Structure of Vidinli Algebras: Simple Nonassociative Jordan–Lie Algebras with Azumaya Properties
Olcay COŞKUN, Alp EDEN

Abstract
The Vidinli algebra, originally defined in three dimensions, is extended to arbitrary odd dimensions via symplectic forms. Particular attention is given to the five- and seven-dimensional cases. The symmetric and antisymmetric parts of the multiplication yield a Jordan algebra and a Heisenberg Lie algebra, respectively. A categorical construction, called the degenerate pushout, is introduced to assemble higher-dimensional algebras from lower-dimensional components, even in the presence of degeneracies such as zero divisors. This method applies not only to Vidinli algebras but also recovers classical structures including spin factor Jordan algebras and Heisenberg Lie algebras. The Vidinli family admits three independent characterizations; geometric, categorical, and analytic, demonstrating how global non-associative structure can arise from local constraints. Together with their variants, the Vidinli algebras form a structured and tractable class within the otherwise wild category of non-associative algebras. MSC2020: 17D25, 17C50, 17A60 Keywords: Non-associative algebras, Fano Plane, Jordan-Lie Algebra, Heisenberg Lie Algebra, Azumaya Algebra, Vidinli Algebras

Introduction

In his 1882 monograph
Linear Algebra

[
14
]
, the Ottoman mathematician Vidinli Hüseyin Tevfik Pasha introduced a novel bilinear product on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, designed to provide a coordinate-free calculus for spatial vectors independent of quaternionic formalism. Now known as the
Vidinli multiplication
, this non-associative product blends inner and cross products into a unified operation, anticipating structural features that would later appear in vector analysis, Jordan algebras, and Lie theory.

In this paper, we revisit the original Vidinli multiplication and extend it to an infinite family of finite-dimensional real algebras
\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}
, defined in every odd dimension. Each algebra is built from elementary geometric data: a unit vector
e_{1}
, the Euclidean inner product, and a non-degenerate skew-symmetric form on the orthogonal complement
V_{0}=e_{1}^{\perp}
. The multiplication

a\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits b:=(a\cdot e_{1})\,b+(b\cdot e_{1})\,a-(a\cdot b)\,e_{1}+\omega(a,b)\,e_{1}

unifies symmetric and skew-symmetric components: the symmetrized product is Jordan, the commutator yields a two-step nilpotent Lie algebra, yet
\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}
is neither Jordan, alternative, nor flexible. These algebras fall outside all known classification results for simple Jordan or Lie-admissible structures, and their construction is dictated by geometric and categorical constraints.

A central result is that every
\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}
is simple: it has no proper two-sided ideals, so the family
\{\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}\}_{n\geq 1}
forms an infinite tower of simple, non-associative, unital real algebras. Their multiplication algebra is the full matrix algebra
\mathrm{M}(\mathbb{V}_{2n+1})\cong M_{2n+1}(\mathbb{R})
, and any module over
\mathrm{M}(\mathbb{V}_{2n+1})
is
\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}
-generated, an Azumaya-type property in the non-associative setting, providing the role of central simple algebras in the associative theory.

These structural features are not merely coexisting; they interact along a shared geometry and are unified by an underlying orthogonal symmetry group. The automorphism group of
\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}
is
\mathrm{U}(n)
, acting on the orthogonal complement
V_{0}
, and in turn, it is a representation of the semi-direct product
\mathfrak{u}(n)\ltimes\mathfrak{h}_{n}
, giving rise to an indecomposable Lie module structure. This internal symmetry reinforces the unified nature of the Vidinli multiplication, which simultaneously encodes linear, symplectic, and Lie-theoretic data.

Our aim is to study the algebraic and geometric structure of the Vidinli family through three independent characterizations—geometric, categorical, and analytic—that reveal how global non-associative structure can emerge from local constraints. Each viewpoint contributes a distinct mechanism by which the multiplication is forced, and together they provide a robust verification of the construction while illuminating unexpected links among projective geometry, category theory, and local algebraic constraints.

These complementary viewpoints underscore the richness of the Vidinli algebras and reveal an intricate dialogue between discrete geometry, universal constructions, and low-dimensional constraints.

Beyond its role in characterizing the Vidinli algebras, the degenerate pushout construction is of independent algebraic interest. It defines a non-functorial colimit operation in the category of unital real algebras, in which several algebras are glued along a common unital subalgebra while annihilating all cross-copy products. This procedure preserves unitality and bilinearity, but not associativity. It preserves simplicity under mild explicit conditions. Nonetheless, it yields new algebraic objects from known components, and admits a clear universal property. In particular, we show that the Heisenberg Lie algebras
\mathfrak{h}_{n}
, the spin factor Jordan algebras, and the Vidinli algebras
\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}
all arise as degenerate pushouts of their 3-dimensional prototypes. As such, the construction provides a unifying formalism for assembling families of structured, finite-dimensional algebras from elementary building blocks.

The case of dimension seven plays a special role. The octonion multiplication defines a cross product on
\mathbb{R}^{7}
, which admits multiple decompositions into local structures. For any basis vector
e_{i}
, one may define a Vidinli-type algebra
\mathbb{V}_{7}^{(i)}
using the symplectic form induced on the orthogonal complement
e_{i}^{\perp}
. Each such algebra is isomorphic to the standard
\mathbb{V}_{7}
. Remarkably, the full cross product on
\mathbb{R}^{7}
can be recovered as the sum of these commutators across all principal directions. It shows that the exceptional Lie group
G_{2}
, which preserves the octonionic structure, acts transitively on the Vidinli algebras
\mathbb{V}_{7}^{(i)}
and determines their interaction. Moreover, it is the interplay between the Fano plane and the octonions that motivates the geometric decomposition framework developed in higher dimensions.

While this paper focuses on the canonical construction using a fixed symplectic form
\omega
, many natural variants remain open to investigation. One may define algebras of
Vidinli-type
by modifying the underlying skew form (Example
4.15
) or allowing degeneracies (Section
8.4
). These variations lead to non-isomorphic structures that preserve certain properties while violating others, and suggest the existence of a broader geometric family in which the Vidinli algebras play a distinguished role. Taken together, the Vidinli algebras and their variants constitute a structured and tractable class within the otherwise wild category of non-associative algebras.

3
Vidinli algebra
\mathbb{V}_{3}

The three-dimensional Vidinli algebra, introduced in
[
5
]
following Vidinli’s original definition form
[
14
]
and
[

]
, serves as the foundational case for the general construction in arbitrary odd dimensions. In this section, we summarize its main properties and present a new coordinate-free decomposition that highlights its geometric and Lie-theoretic structure.

Let
\mathbb{R}^{3}
denote the three-dimensional vector space over
\mathbb{R}
with standard basis
\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}\}
. In
[
5
]
, this space is identified with the imaginary quaternions by associating
e_{1}\mapsto\mathbf{i},\;e_{2}\mapsto\mathbf{j},\;e_{3}\mapsto\mathbf{k}
. For consistency with higher-dimensional generalizations, we retain the standard basis convention throughout.

The Vidinli multiplication
\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits
is defined by

a\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits b=(a_{1}b_{1}-a_{2}b_{2}-a_{3}b_{3}+a_{2}b_{3}-a_{3}b_{2})\,e_{1}+(a_{1}b_{2}+a_{2}b_{1})\,e_{2}+(a_{1}b_{3}+a_{3}b_{1})\,e_{3},

for all
a,b\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
. The vector
e_{1}
is referred to as the
principal direction
, and we write
\mathbb{V}_{3}=(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits)
.

3.1
Structural properties of
\mathbb{V}_{3}

The following list summarizes the main features of
\mathbb{V}_{3}
which were established in
[
5
]
.

(1)

Structure constants:

e_{2}\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits e_{3}=-e_{3}\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits e_{2}=e_{1};\quad e_{2}\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits e_{2}=e_{3}\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits e_{3}=-e_{1}
.

(2)

Algebraic features:
The Vidinli algebra is non-commutative, nonassociative, and unital, with unit element
e_{1}
. It admits a conjugation map
\bar{a}=(a_{1},\,-a_{2},\,-a_{3})
. This map allows us to determine a two sided inverse for each non-zero element and to show that the set of zero-divisors is the hyperplane
e_{1}^{\perp}
.

(3)

Geometric formula:

Setting
R(x,y,z)=(x,-z,y)
to be rotation by
\pi/2
in the plane
e_{1}^{\perp}
, the multiplication admits the form

a\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits b=(\bar{a}\cdot b)\,e_{1}-R(\bar{a}\times b),

(4)

Relation to quaternions:

The multiplication
\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits
is a push-forward of quaternion multiplication via the linear maps

\pi\colon\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{V}_{3},\quad(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4})\mapsto(x_{1}+x_{2},\,x_{3},\,x_{4}),

\nu\colon\mathbb{V}_{3}\to\mathbb{H},\quad(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})\mapsto(a_{1},\,0,\,a_{2},\,a_{3}),

that is, we have

a\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits b=\pi\big(\nu(a)\nu(b)\big).

(5)

Complex planes:
For any unit vector
u\in\mathbb{V}_{3}
not parallel to
e_{1}
, the map given by
e_{1}\mapsto 1,\;u\mapsto i
defines an isomorphism of real algebras

\mathrm{Span}_{\mathbb{R}}\{e_{1},u\}\cong\mathbb{C}.

(6)

Commutator and Lie structure:
The commutator bracket is given by

[a,b]=2\,\big(e_{1}\cdot(a\times b)\big)\,e_{1},

which is central in
\mathbb{V}_{3}
and defines a realization of the Heisenberg Lie algebra.

(7)

Twisted Vidinli multiplications:
For
T,U,V\in\mathbb{R}
, define a bilinear product
\ast_{T,U,V}
on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
by

a\ast_{T,U,V}b=(S,J,K),

where

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle=a_{1}b_{1}-a_{2}b_{2}-a_{3}b_{3}+T(a_{2}b_{3}-a_{3}b_{2}),

\displaystyle J

\displaystyle=a_{1}b_{2}+a_{2}b_{1}+U(a_{2}b_{3}-a_{3}b_{2}),

\displaystyle K

\displaystyle=a_{1}b_{3}+a_{3}b_{1}+V(a_{2}b_{3}-a_{3}b_{2}).

The geometric property (3) and the Lie property (6) are preserved if and only if
(U,V)=(0,0)
. We refer to
\ast_{T,0,0}
as a
twisted Vidinli multiplication
. The original Vidinli multiplication corresponds to
\ast_{1,0,0}
, uniquely characterized by (4).

3.2
Coordinate-free formulation

The properties above indicate that
\mathbb{V}_{3}
combines inner product, cross product, and Lie structures in a non-associative framework. The following identity captures its multiplication in intrinsic geometric terms.

Proposition 3.1
.

Given
v=(T,U,V)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
, we have

a\ast_{T,U,V}b=(a\cdot e_{1})\,b+(b\cdot e_{1})\,a-(a\cdot b)\,e_{1}+\big(e_{1}\cdot(a\times b)\big)\,v,

In particular, for all
a,b\in\mathbb{V}_{3}
, the following equality holds:

a\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits b=(a\cdot e_{1})\,b+(b\cdot e_{1})\,a-(a\cdot b)\,e_{1}+\big(e_{1}\cdot(a\times b)\big)\,e_{1}.

A direct computation in the standard basis confirms the identity. We may further decompose the above expression as

a\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits b=S_{e_{1}}(a,b)+[a,b]_{e_{1}},

where

S_{e_{1}}(a,b)=(a\cdot e_{1})\,b+(b\cdot e_{1})\,a-(a\cdot b)\,e_{1},\quad[a,b]_{e_{1}}=\big(e_{1}\cdot(a\times b)\big)\,e_{1}.

The first term is symmetric and defines a Jordan-type product; the second is skew-symmetric and captures the projection of the cross product onto the principal axis providing a Lie algebra structure.

This decomposition highlights a geometric feature central to the Vidinli multiplication: the localization of interaction along a fixed direction. The operation
[a,b]_{e_{1}}
will extend naturally to higher dimensions as the
commutator
in the direction of
e_{1}
, while
S_{e_{1}}(a,b)
generalizes as a directional Jordan product.

4
The General Vidinli Algebra in odd dimension

The coordinate-free formula of Proposition
3.1
encodes both inner and cross product structures into a single unital bilinear operation. This formulation admits a natural extension to all odd dimensions. In this section, we define the general Vidinli algebra
\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}
, and show how it canonically decomposes into a symmetric Jordan product and a skew-symmetric Lie bracket associated with a fixed direction. These two components interact through a shared geometric axis and unify under a single algebraic framework. In this section we also determine the automorphism group of
\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}
and show that it is a Lie representation over the semi-direct product of its derivation algebra and commutator Lie algebra.

4.1
Definition and Basic Properties

Let
V=\mathbb{R}^{2n+1}
be an odd-dimensional Euclidean space for
n\in\mathbb{N}
equipped with an inner product. Let
e_{1},e_{2},\ldots,e_{2n+1}
be a fixed ordered orthonormal basis. Write
V_{0}:=e_{1}^{\perp}\subset V
, so that
V=\mathbb{R}e_{1}\oplus V_{0}
. Equip
V_{0}
with the standard symplectic form
\omega_{0}\colon V_{0}\times V_{0}\to\mathbb{R}
given by

\omega_{0}(e_{2i},e_{2i+1})=1=-\omega_{0}(e_{2i+1},e_{2i})\quad\text{for all }i=1,\ldots,n

and
\omega(e_{i},e_{j})=0
for all the other basis pairs.
Then extend it to a bilinear form
\omega\colon V\times V\to\mathbb{R}
by declaring

\omega(e_{1},\cdot)=0=\omega(\cdot,e_{1}),\quad\omega|_{V_{0}\times V_{0}}=\omega_{0}.

Definition 4.1
.

The
Vidinli multiplication

\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits\colon=\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits_{e_{1}}^{\omega}\colon V\times V\to V
is defined by

a\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits b:=(a\cdot e_{1})\,b+(b\cdot e_{1})\,a-(a\cdot b)\,e_{1}+\omega(a,b)\,e_{1}.

The resulting algebra
\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}:=(V,\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits)
is called the
Vidinli algebra of dimension
2n+1
.

We have the following basic properties of the Vidinli algebras.

Theorem 4.2
.

The Vidinli algebra
\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}

1.

is unital, with unit
e_{1}
,

2.

is neither commutative nor associative and

3.

is simple.

Proof.

For any
a\in\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}
,

e_{1}\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits a=(e_{1}\cdot e_{1})\,a+(a\cdot e_{1})\,e_{1}-(e_{1}\cdot a)\,e_{1}+\omega(e_{1},a)\,e_{1}=a,

and similarly
a\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits e_{1}=a
.
Hence
e_{1}
is the unit. The non-commutativity follows since for any
a,b\in\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}
, we have

a\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits b-b\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits a=2\,\omega(a,b)\,e_{1},

which is generally nonzero. Associativity fails, as seen by choosing
a=c=e_{2}
,
b=e_{3}
.

Finally, to prove simplicity, let
I\subseteq\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}
be a nonzero two-sided ideal, and let
x\in I\setminus\{0\}
. Then

x\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits x=2(x\cdot e_{1})x-\|x\|^{2}e_{1},

and hence

x\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits x-2(x\cdot e_{1})x=-\|x\|^{2}\,e_{1}\in I.

Thus the unit
e_{1}
is in
I
which implies
I=\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}
.
∎

Example 4.3

(The Vidinli algebra
\mathbb{V}_{3}
)
.

Let
\mathbb{R}^{3}
be the 3-dimensional real space with standard inner product and orthonormal basis
\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}\}
. The standard symplectic form is given by
\omega(e_{2},e_{3})=1
. Then

e_{i}\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits e_{1}=e_{i},\quad e_{2}\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits e_{3}=e_{1},\quad e_{3}\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits e_{2}=-e_{1},\quad e_{2}\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits e_{2}=e_{3}\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits e_{3}=-e_{1}.

These structure constants coincide with the ones given in Section
3.1
. Thus we recover the three-dimensional Vidinli algebra.

Example 4.4

(The Vidinli algebra
\mathbb{V}_{5}
)
.

Let
\mathbb{R}^{5}
be the 5-dimensional real space with orthonormal basis
\{e_{1},\dots,e_{5}\}
, and the standard symplectic form takes the form of
\omega
given by

\omega(e_{2},e_{3})=\omega(e_{4},e_{5})=1,\qquad\omega(e_{i},e_{j})=-\omega(e_{j},e_{i}),\qquad\omega(e_{1},v)=0.

By definition, the structure constants are given by

e_{i}\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits e_{i}=-e_{1}\ (i=2,\dots,5);\;e_{2}\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits e_{3}=e_{4}\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits e_{5}=e_{1};\;e_{3}\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits e_{2}=e_{5}\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits e_{4}=-e_{1}.

Exceptional Vidinli Algebras

We call the Vidinli algebras
\mathbb{V}_{3}
and
\mathbb{V}_{7}

exceptional
due to their rich internal symmetry and geometric structure. The algebra
\mathbb{V}_{3}
coincides with the original construction of Hüseyin Tevfik Pasha and serves as the minimal nontrivial case. As we shall see in Section
5

\mathbb{V}_{7}
is encoded by the Fano plane and exhibits symmetries related to the exceptional Lie group

These exceptional cases play a role analogous to the quaternions and octonions in the Cayley–Dickson hierarchy.

Structure Constants

We determine the structure constants of the Vidinli algebra
\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}
. Let
e_{i},e_{j},e_{k}
be basis elements. We write
\mathrm{v}_{i,j}^{k}
for the coefficient of
e_{k}
in the product
e_{i}\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits e_{j}
so that

e_{i}\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits e_{j}=\sum_{k=1}^{2n+1}\mathrm{v}_{i,j}^{k}e_{k}.

Since the product
e_{i}\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits e_{j}
is contained in the span of
e_{1},e_{i},e_{j}
, we have that

\mathrm{v}_{i,j}^{k}=0\text{ if }k\not\in\{1,i,j\}.

The remaining structure constants can be grouped in three cases and be evaluated using Definition
4.1
.

1.

If
i=j\neq 1
, then

e_{i}\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits e_{i}=-e_{1}.
Hence

\mathrm{v}_{i,i}^{1}=-1,\quad\mathrm{v}_{i,i}^{i}=0,

2.

If
i=1
or
j=1
, then since
e_{1}
is the unit,

\mathrm{v}_{1,j}^{k}=\delta_{j,k},\quad\mathrm{v}_{i,1}^{k}=\delta_{i,k},

3.

If
i\neq j
and
1\not\in\{i,j\}
, then
e_{i}\cdot e_{1}=e_{j}\cdot e_{1}=e_{i}\cdot e_{j}=0
and hence

\mathrm{v}_{i,j}^{k}=\begin{cases}\omega(e_{i},e_{j}),&\text{if }k=1\\
0,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Coordinate Formula

The Vidinli multiplication given in Definition
4.1
can be written as

a\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits b:=a_{1}b+b_{1}a+(\omega(a,b)-(a\cdot b))e_{1}.

where
a=\sum_{i=1}^{2n+1}a_{i}e_{i}
,
b=\sum_{i=1}^{2n+1}b_{i}e_{i}
. By its definition, the symplectic form can be evaluated as

\displaystyle\omega(a,b)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}(a_{2k}b_{2k+1}-a_{2k+1}b_{2k}).

Substituting into the definition, we obtain the coordinate formula:

a\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits b=c_{1}e_{1}+\sum_{i=2}^{2n+1}c_{i}e_{i},

where

c_{1}=a_{1}b_{1}-\sum_{i=2}^{2n+1}a_{i}b_{i}+\sum_{k=1}^{n}(a_{2k}b_{2k+1}-a_{2k+1}b_{2k}),

c_{i}=a_{1}b_{i}+b_{1}a_{i}\quad\text{for }i=2,\dots,2n+1.

4.2
Automorphism Group of the Vidinli Algebra

We now determine the full automorphism group of the Vidinli algebra
\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}
. The key observation is that there is a unique nonzero idempotent,
e_{1}
, forcing any algebra automorphism to fix the unit direction. The restriction to the symplectic hyperplane
V_{0}=e_{1}^{\perp}
then preserves both the inner product and the skew form
\omega
, hence lies in the intersection

O(2n)\;\cap\;Sp(2n,\mathbb{R})\;\cong\;U(n).

Theorem 4.5
.

The automorphism group of the Vidinli algebra
\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}=(\mathbb{R}e_{1}\oplus V_{0},\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits)
is isomorphic to the unitary group

\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{V}_{2n+1})\;\cong\;U(n).

Proof.

We claim that there is a unique non-zero idempotent in
\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}
, necessarily equal to
e_{1}
. Hence any automorphism must fix it. To prove the uniqueness,
write
x=x_{1}e_{1}+x_{0}
with
x_{0}\in V_{0}
. Then

x\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits x=\bigl(x_{1}^{2}-x_{0}\cdot\,x_{0}\bigr)\,e_{1}\;+\;2\,x_{1}\,x_{0}.

For
x\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits x=x
, we should have

2\,x_{1}\,x_{0}=x_{0}
and

x_{1}^{2}-x_{0}\cdot\,x_{0}=x_{1}
.
If
x_{0}\neq 0
, then
x_{1}=1/2
by the first equation. Then the second equation gives
\|x_{0}\|^{2}<0
, which is impossible. Hence
x_{0}=0
and the only nonzero real idempotent is
x=e_{1}
.

Let
\Phi
be an automorphism of
\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}
. We must have
\Phi(e_{1})=e_{1}
. We claim that the restriction to
V_{0}
has image in
V_{0}
. Suppose
v\in V_{0}
and write
\Phi(v)=\alpha e_{1}+w
for some
w\in V_{0}
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
. Calculate
\Phi(v\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits v)
in two ways. First use the above equality for the
\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits
-square. Putting
v\cdot e_{1}=0
,

\Phi(v\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits v)=\Phi(-\|w\|^{2}\,e_{1})=-\|w\|^{2}\,e_{1}.

We use the multiplicativity of
\Phi
and then the above equality:

\Phi(v\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits v)=\Phi(v)\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits\Phi(v)=(\alpha^{2}-w\cdot w)\,e_{1}+2\alpha\,w.

Comparing the two equations, we get that either
\alpha=0
or
w=0
. But
w=0
is not possible because then
\Phi(v)=\alpha e_{1}
which implies that
v=\alpha e_{1}
, contradiction since
v\in V_{0}
. Hence we must have
\alpha=0
, that is,
\Phi(v)\in V_{0}
, as required. Thus
\Phi
induces a linear map

\psi=\Phi|_{V_{0}}:V_{0}\to V_{0}
.

Next we show that
\Phi
preserves the Vidinli multiplication on
V_{0}
. For
a,b\in V_{0}
, we have

a\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits b=\bigl(-a\cdot b+\omega_{0}(a,b)\bigr)\,{e}_{1},

and hence

\Phi(a\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits b)=\bigl(-a\cdot b+\omega_{0}(a,b)\bigr)\,{e}_{1}.

On the other hand, we have

\Phi(a)\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits\Phi(b)=\bigl(-\psi(a)\cdot\psi(b)+\omega_{0}(\psi(a),\psi(b))\bigr)\,{e}_{1}.

Equating both expressions, we find:

-a\cdot b+\omega_{0}(a,b)=-\psi(a)\cdot\psi(b)+\omega_{0}(\psi(a),\psi(b)).

Now observe that this implies:

\omega_{0}(\psi(a),\psi(b))-\psi(a)\cdot\psi(b)=\omega_{0}(a,b)-a\cdot b.

That is,
\psi
preserves the bilinear form
\beta(a,b):=\omega_{0}(a,b)-a\cdot b
.
To extract the individual identities, note that for any
a\in V_{0}
,

\Phi(a\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits a)=-\|a\|^{2}\,{e}_{1},\qquad\Phi(a)\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits\Phi(a)=-\|\psi(a)\|^{2}\,{e}_{1}.

So we must have
\|\psi(a)\|=\|a\|
for all
a\in V_{0}
, that is,
\psi\in O(V_{0},\cdot)
. Since
\beta
is preserved and
\cdot
is preserved, it follows that

\omega_{0}(\psi(a),\psi(b))=\omega_{0}(a,b),

i.e.,
\psi\in\mathrm{Sp}(V_{0},\omega_{0})
. We conclude that

\psi\in O(V_{0},\cdot)\cap\mathrm{Sp}(V_{0},\omega_{0}).

This intersection is isomorphic to the unitary group
U(n)
, see
[
18
, Proposition 1.6.5]
. Hence
\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{V}_{2n+1})\subseteq U(n)
.

To complete the proof, we verify that each
\psi\in U(n)\subseteq\mathrm{End}(V_{0})
extends to an automorphism of
\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}
by defining
\Phi\colon\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}\to\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}
via

\Phi(e_{1}):=e_{1},\qquad\Phi(v):=\psi(v)\quad\text{for all }v\in V_{0}.

Let
a=a_{1}e_{1}+a_{0}
and
b=b_{1}e_{1}+b_{0}
, with
a_{0},b_{0}\in V_{0}
. Then

a\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits b=(a_{1}b_{1}-a_{0}\cdot b_{0}+\omega(a_{0},b_{0}))\,e_{1}+a_{1}b_{0}+b_{1}a_{0}.

Applying
\Phi
, and using linearity and
\Phi(e_{1})=e_{1}
, we find:

\Phi(a\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits b)=(a_{1}b_{1}-a_{0}\cdot b_{0}+\omega(a_{0},b_{0}))\,e_{1}+a_{1}\psi(b_{0})+b_{1}\psi(a_{0}).

On the other hand, since
\Phi(a)=a_{1}e_{1}+\psi(a_{0})
and
\Phi(b)=b_{1}e_{1}+\psi(b_{0})
, we compute:

\Phi(a)\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits\Phi(b)=(a_{1}b_{1}-\psi(a_{0})\cdot\psi(b_{0})+\omega(\psi(a_{0}),\psi(b_{0})))\,e_{1}+a_{1}\psi(b_{0})+b_{1}\psi(a_{0}).

But
\psi\in U(n)
, so it preserves both the inner product and the symplectic form, that is,

\psi(a_{0})\cdot\psi(b_{0})=a_{0}\cdot b_{0},\qquad\omega(\psi(a_{0}),\psi(b_{0}))=\omega(a_{0},b_{0}).

Substituting, we find

\Phi(a)\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits\Phi(b)=\Phi(a\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits b).

Hence
\Phi
is an automorphism of
\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}
. This completes the proof.
∎

4.3
Jordan-Lie Algebra Structure

The Vidinli multiplication splits into symmetric and skew-symmetric components:

a\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits b=a\mathop{\circ_{\mathfrak{v}}}\nolimits b+[a,b]_{e_{1}},

where

a\mathop{\circ_{\mathfrak{v}}}\nolimits b:=(a\cdot e_{1})\,b+(b\cdot e_{1})\,a-(a\cdot b)\,e_{1}

is the anti-commutator of the Vidinli multiplication and

[a,b]_{e_{1}}:=\omega(a,b)\,e_{1}

is the commutator. This decomposition exhibits a non-associative Jordan-Lie algebra structure of the Vidinli algebra. As mentioned in
[
22
]
, this kind of structures are studied by physicist to find systems containing both bosons and fermions. See also
[
23
]
and
[
13
]
for Lie-Jordan algebras. We consider the induced algebra structures separately.

4.3.1
Vidinli-Jordan Algebras

Let
V=\mathbb{R}e_{1}\oplus V_{0}
be as above. We call the space
V
equipped with the product

\mathop{\circ_{\mathfrak{v}}}\nolimits
the
Vidinli-Jordan algebra
, denoted
\mathcal{VJ}_{2n+1}
. Notice that the anti-commutator can be defined on any dimension. We extend the definition of Vidinli-Jordan algebras to arbitrary dimensions as follows.

Let
V=\mathbb{R}^{m}
be a real inner product space for
n\in\mathbb{N}
, with the standard orthonormal basis
\{e_{1},e_{2},\dots,e_{m}\}
. Fix the unit vector
e_{1}\in V
and decompose

V=\mathbb{R}e_{1}\oplus V_{0},\qquad V_{0}:=e_{1}^{\perp}.

Define a symmetric bilinear product
\mathop{\circ_{\mathfrak{v}}}\nolimits\colon V\times V\to V
by

a\mathop{\circ_{\mathfrak{v}}}\nolimits b:=(a\cdot e_{1})\,b+(b\cdot e_{1})\,a-(a\cdot b)\,e_{1}.

The resulting algebra
\mathcal{VJ}_{m}:=(V,\mathop{\circ_{\mathfrak{v}}}\nolimits)
is called the
Vidinli–Jordan algebra
of dimension
m
with distinguished axis
e_{1}
. The following proposition collects basic properties.

Proposition 4.6
.

For any
m\geq 1
, the product
\mathop{\circ_{\mathfrak{v}}}\nolimits
satisfies the following.

1.

It is bilinear, commutative, non-associative, and unital, with unit
e_{1}
.

2.

It is power-associative:
a\mathop{\circ_{\mathfrak{v}}}\nolimits(a\mathop{\circ_{\mathfrak{v}}}\nolimits a)=(a\mathop{\circ_{\mathfrak{v}}}\nolimits a)\mathop{\circ_{\mathfrak{v}}}\nolimits a
for all
a\in V
.

3.

It satisfies the Jordan identity:

(a\mathop{\circ_{\mathfrak{v}}}\nolimits a)\mathop{\circ_{\mathfrak{v}}}\nolimits(b\mathop{\circ_{\mathfrak{v}}}\nolimits a)=((a\mathop{\circ_{\mathfrak{v}}}\nolimits a)\mathop{\circ_{\mathfrak{v}}}\nolimits b)\mathop{\circ_{\mathfrak{v}}}\nolimits a\quad\text{for all }a,b\in V.

Hence
\mathcal{VJ}_{m}
is a Jordan algebra for all
m\geq 1
. Moreover:

(4)

\mathcal{VJ}_{m}
is not formally real.

(5)

\mathcal{VJ}_{m}
is simple.

Proof.

(1) Commutativity and bilinearity are immediate. That
e_{1}
is a unit follows from

e_{1}\mathop{\circ_{\mathfrak{v}}}\nolimits a=(e_{1}\cdot e_{1})\,a+(a\cdot e_{1})\,e_{1}-(e_{1}\cdot a)\,e_{1}=a

whereas associativity does not hold since
e_{2}\mathop{\circ_{\mathfrak{v}}}\nolimits(e_{2}\mathop{\circ_{\mathfrak{v}}}\nolimits e_{3})\neq(e_{2}\mathop{\circ_{\mathfrak{v}}}\nolimits e_{2})\mathop{\circ_{\mathfrak{v}}}\nolimits e_{3}
.

(2) Write
a=a_{1}e_{1}+u
, with
u\in V_{0}
. Then

a\mathop{\circ_{\mathfrak{v}}}\nolimits a=2a_{1}a-\|a\|^{2}e_{1}.

Direct verification confirms the identity.

(3) The Jordan identity follows from the computation in
[
17
, Ch. III, §6]
, using the expression

a\mathop{\circ_{\mathfrak{v}}}\nolimits b=\lambda(a)\,b+\lambda(b)\,a-\mu(a,b)\,e_{1},

with
\lambda(a)=a\cdot e_{1}
,
\mu(a,b)=a\cdot b
.

(4) Since
e_{1}\mathop{\circ_{\mathfrak{v}}}\nolimits e_{1}+e_{2}\mathop{\circ_{\mathfrak{v}}}\nolimits e_{2}=0
, while
e_{2}\mathop{\circ_{\mathfrak{v}}}\nolimits e_{2}=-e_{1}\neq 0
, the algebra fails formal reality.

(5) Let
I\subseteq\mathcal{VJ}_{m}
be a nonzero ideal, and choose
x\in I\setminus\{0\}
. Then

x\mathop{\circ_{\mathfrak{v}}}\nolimits x-2(x\cdot e_{1})\,x=-\|x\|^{2}e_{1}\in I,

so
e_{1}\in I
, and hence
I=V
.
∎

The Vidinli-Jordan algebra
\mathcal{VJ}_{m}
is an example of a Jordan algebra of a symmetric bilinear form. Also since
\mathcal{VJ}_{m}
is not formally real, it is not a spin factor. We refer to Zel’manov
[
36
]
for further details about classification of Jordan algebras.

Corollary 4.7
.

The real vector space
\mathbb{R}^{2n+1}
equipped with the anti-commutator of
\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}
is isomorphic to the Vidinli-Jordan algebra
\mathcal{VJ}_{2n+1}
.

4.3.2
The Commutator Algebra

Given a Vidinli algebra
\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}
with multiplication
\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits
, we define the commutator

[a,b]\;:=\;a\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits b\;-\;b\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits a,\qquad a,b\in\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}.

This bracket is bilinear, skew-symmetric, and measures the skew part of the multiplication.

Theorem 4.8
.

Let
\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}
be the Vidinli algebra of dimension
2n+1
, with unit
e_{1}
and hyperplane
V_{0}=e_{1}^{\perp}
. Then:

1.

For
a,b\in V_{0}
,

[a,b]=2\,\omega(a,b)\,e_{1},

and
[e_{1},a]=0
for all
a\in\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}
.

2.

The bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]
satisfies the Jacobi identity and defines a
2
-step nilpotent Lie algebra on
\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}
with center
\mathbb{R}e_{1}
.

3.

This Lie algebra
(\mathbb{V}_{2n+1},[\cdot,\cdot])
is isomorphic to the real Heisenberg Lie algebra
\mathfrak{h}_{n}
of dimension
2n+1
, with center
\mathbb{R}e_{1}
and abelian subspace
V_{0}
satisfying
[u,v]=2\,\omega(u,v)\,e_{1}
.

Proof.

The multiplication
\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits
decomposes into its symmetric (Jordan) and skew parts:

a\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits b-b\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits a=\bigl(a\circ_{\mathfrak{v}}b-b\circ_{\mathfrak{v}}a\bigr)+\bigl(\text{skew part}\bigr)=0+2\,\omega(a,b)\,e_{1}

for
a,b\in V_{0}
, since the Jordan part is symmetric. Moreover
[e_{1},a]=0
by the unit property.

As the image of
[\cdot,\cdot]
lies in the one-dimensional subspace
\mathbb{R}e_{1}
, which is central, the bracket is automatically
2
-step nilpotent and satisfies the Jacobi identity. Choosing a Darboux basis
\{p_{i},q_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
for
(V_{0},\omega)
gives the relations

[p_{i},q_{j}]=2\,\delta_{ij}\,e_{1},\quad[p_{i},p_{j}]=[q_{i},q_{j}]=0,\quad[V,e_{1}]=0,

which are exactly the defining relations of
\mathfrak{h}_{n}
after rescaling
z=2e_{1}
.
∎

The commutator bracket
[a,b]=2\,\omega(a,b)\,e_{1}
is defined in all dimensions
2n+1
.
However, it acquires a particularly direct geometric description in dimensions
3
and
7
, which are the only Euclidean dimensions in which the standard symplectic form on the hyperplane
V_{0}
can be realized as the
e_{1}
–projection of a vector cross product
[
3
]
,
[
26
]
.
In these cases, the commutator is simply twice the
e_{1}
–component of the corresponding cross product, making the connection between the Vidinli multiplication and the geometry of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
\mathbb{R}^{7}
especially transparent.

Example 4.9

(The
3
-dimensional case)
.

Let
\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}\}
be an orthonormal basis for
\mathbb{R}^{3}
with
V_{0}=\mathrm{Span}_{\mathbb{R}}\{e_{2},e_{3}\}
and
\omega_{0}=e^{23}
. Define the usual vector cross product by

u\times v=\begin{pmatrix}u_{2}v_{3}-u_{3}v_{2}\\
-u_{1}v_{3}+u_{3}v_{1}\\
u_{1}v_{2}-u_{2}v_{1}\end{pmatrix}.

The associated
3
-form is
\varphi=e^{123}
, and

e_{1}\cdot\varphi=e^{23}=\omega_{0}.

Thus for
v,w\in V_{0}
, the commutator is

[v,w]=2\,\omega_{0}(v,w)\,e_{1}=2\,(e_{1}\cdot(v\times w))\,e_{1},

recovering the
e_{1}
–projection of the
3
D cross product.

Example 4.10

(The
7
-dimensional case)
.

Let
\{e_{1},\dots,e_{7}\}
be an orthonormal basis for
\mathbb{R}^{7}
with
V_{0}=e_{1}^{\perp}
and
\omega_{0}
the standard symplectic form on
V_{0}
. Define the cross product by

u\times v:=\begin{pmatrix}u_{2}v_{3}-u_{3}v_{2}+u_{4}v_{5}-u_{5}v_{4}+u_{6}v_{7}-u_{7}v_{6}\\[2.0pt]
-u_{1}v_{3}+u_{3}v_{1}+u_{4}v_{6}-u_{6}v_{4}-u_{5}v_{7}+u_{7}v_{5}\\[2.0pt]
u_{1}v_{2}-u_{2}v_{1}-u_{4}v_{7}+u_{7}v_{4}+u_{5}v_{6}-u_{6}v_{5}\\[2.0pt]
-u_{1}v_{5}+u_{5}v_{1}-u_{2}v_{6}+u_{6}v_{2}+u_{3}v_{7}-u_{7}v_{3}\\[2.0pt]
u_{1}v_{4}-u_{4}v_{1}+u_{2}v_{7}-u_{7}v_{2}-u_{3}v_{6}+u_{6}v_{3}\\[2.0pt]
-u_{1}v_{7}+u_{7}v_{1}+u_{2}v_{4}-u_{4}v_{2}+u_{3}v_{5}-u_{5}v_{3}\\[2.0pt]
u_{1}v_{6}-u_{6}v_{1}-u_{2}v_{5}+u_{5}v_{2}+u_{3}v_{4}-u_{4}v_{3}\end{pmatrix}.

The associated
3
-form is

\varphi=e^{123}+e^{145}+e^{167}+e^{246}-e^{257}-e^{347}-e^{356},

and

e_{1}\cdot\varphi=e^{23}+e^{45}+e^{67}=\omega_{0}.

Hence for
v,w\in V_{0}
,

[v,w]=2\,\omega_{0}(v,w)\,e_{1}=2\,(e_{1}\cdot(v\times w))\,e_{1},

so the commutator agrees with twice the
e_{1}
–projection of this cross product. We refer to
[
26
]
for an alternative definition of the cross product in
\mathbb{R}^{7}
.

4.4
A Representation of
\mathrm{Der}(\mathbb{V}_{2n+1})\ltimes\mathfrak{h}_{n}

The Vidinli algebra naturally carries a representation of the Lie algebra
\mathrm{Der}(\mathbb{V}_{2n+1})\ltimes\mathfrak{h}_{n}
, where
\mathfrak{h}_{n}
is the Heisenberg Lie algebra associated to the symplectic hyperplane
V_{0}=e_{1}^{\perp}\subset\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}
.

Proposition 4.11
.

The derivation algebra of
\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}
is isomorphic to the unitary Lie algebra:

\mathrm{Der}(\mathbb{V}_{2n+1})\;\cong\;\mathfrak{u}(n)\;=\;\mathfrak{so}(2n)\cap\mathfrak{sp}(2n),

consisting of real-linear endomorphisms of
V_{0}
that preserve both the inner product and the symplectic form
\omega
.

Proof.

Follows from
[
12
, Section 8.2]
.
∎

We define a representation
\phi\colon\mathrm{Der}(\mathbb{V}_{2n+1})\to\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{h}_{n})
by

\phi(A)(u+\lambda e_{1}):=A(u),

for
A\in\mathrm{Der}(\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}),u\in V_{0},\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
.
By the construction in
[
32
, Section 3.14]
, the semi-direct product
\mathrm{Der}(\mathbb{V}_{2n+1})\ltimes_{\phi}\mathfrak{h}_{n}
is the Lie algebra whose underlying vector space is the direct sum

\mathrm{Der}(\mathbb{V}_{2n+1})\oplus\mathfrak{h}_{n}\;=\;\mathfrak{u}(n)\oplus V_{0}\oplus\mathbb{R}e_{1},

with bracket defined for
A,B\in\mathfrak{u}(n)
,
u,v\in V_{0}
, and
\lambda,\mu\in\mathbb{R}
by

[(A,u+\lambda e_{1}),(B,v+\mu e_{1})]\;=\;\bigl([A,B],\;A(v)-B(u)+2\,\omega(u,v)\,e_{1}\bigr).

Theorem 4.12
.

The map

\rho\colon\mathrm{Der}(\mathbb{V}_{2n+1})\ltimes\mathfrak{h}_{n}\longrightarrow\mathrm{End}(\mathbb{V}_{2n+1})

defined by

\rho(A,0)(e_{1})=0,\quad\rho(A,0)(v)=A(v),

\rho(0,u)(e_{1})=0,\quad\rho(0,u)(v)=2\omega(u,v)e_{1},

for all
A\in\mathrm{Der}(\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}),u,v\in V_{0}
, is a Lie algebra representation.

Proof.

Linearity and the definition ensure that
\rho
is well-defined. The derivation part acts linearly on
V_{0}
and vanishes on
e_{1}
. The Heisenberg part acts trivially on
e_{1}
and through the Lie bracket on
V_{0}
.

To verify the bracket compatibility, it suffices to check:

[\rho(A,0),\rho(0,u)]=\rho([(A,0),(0,u)]),

for all
A\in\mathrm{Der}(\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}),u\in V_{0}
. Evaluating on
v\in V_{0}
,

\displaystyle[\rho(A,0),\rho(0,u)](v)

\displaystyle=\rho(A,0)(2\omega(u,v)e_{1})-\rho(0,u)(A(v))

\displaystyle=0-2\omega(u,A(v))e_{1}=-2\omega(A(u),v)e_{1}

\displaystyle=\rho(0,A(u))(v)=\rho([(A,0),(0,u)])(v).

Since both sides vanish on
e_{1}
, the identity holds on all of
\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}
.
∎

Remark 4.13
.

As a
\mathrm{Der}(\mathbb{V}_{2n+1})\ltimes\mathfrak{h}_{n}
–module, the Vidinli space admits a short exact sequence:

0\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}e_{1}\longrightarrow\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}\longrightarrow V_{0}\longrightarrow 0,

where
\mathbb{R}e_{1}
is a trivial module, and
V_{0}
carries the standard
\mathfrak{u}(n)
–module structure with trivial
\mathfrak{h}_{n}
–action. The extension is non-split: an
\mathfrak{h}_{n}
–equivariant section would induce a nontrivial action on
V_{0}
, contradicting its definition. Hence
\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}
is reducible but indecomposable.

4.5
Dependence on the symplectic form

In Definition
4.1
, we have fixed an inner product and the standard symplectic form. However the algebra structure depends on the choice of the symplectic form
\omega
on the hyperplane
V_{0}=e_{1}^{\perp}\cong\mathbb{R}^{2n}
.
We make the following definition for future reference.

Definition 4.14
.

Let
V
be as above and let
\tilde{\omega}
be the trivial extension of an arbitrary symplectic form
\tilde{\omega}_{0}
on
V_{0}
. Define

a\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits^{\tilde{\omega}}_{e_{1}}b=(a\cdot e_{1})\,b+(b\cdot e_{1})\,a-(a\cdot b)\,e_{1}+\tilde{\omega}(a,b)\,e_{1}.

We call the resulting algebra
\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}^{\tilde{\omega}}=(V,\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits^{\tilde{\omega}}_{e_{1}})
a
Vidinli-type algebra
associated to the symplectic form
\tilde{\omega}
.

It is a classical fact that any two symplectic forms on
V_{0}
are symplectomorphic, that is, for any such
\omega_{1}
and
\omega_{2}
, there exists an invertible linear map
T\colon V_{0}\to V_{0}
such that

\omega_{2}(Tu,Tv)=\omega_{1}(u,v)\quad\text{for all }u,v\in V_{0}.

However, this symplectomorphism need not preserve the inner product on
V_{0}
, and thus does not in general induce an isomorphism of the corresponding Vidinli-type algebras
\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}^{\omega_{1}}
and
\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}^{\omega_{2}}
. For reasons that will become transparent, we do not attempt to determine structure of an arbitrary Vidinli-type algebra, leaving it open to future work. We include the following non-trivial example of a Vidinli-type algebra.

Example 4.15
.

Let
V=\mathbb{R}^{5}
with standard orthonormal basis
\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{3},e_{4},e_{5}\}
and define a skew-symmetric bilinear form
\tilde{\omega}
on
e_{1}^{\perp}
by

\tilde{\omega}(e_{2},e_{3})=\tilde{\omega}(e_{3},e_{4})=\tilde{\omega}(e_{4},e_{5})=1,

with all other non-zero pairings determined by skew-symmetry and linearity. Then
\tilde{\omega}
is non-degenerate, since its matrix with respect to the basis
\{e_{2},e_{3},e_{4},e_{5}\}
is

\Omega=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&0&0\\
-1&0&1&0\\
0&-1&0&1\\
0&0&-1&0\end{pmatrix},

which has determinant 1. The resulting Vidinli-type algebra
\mathbb{V}_{5}^{\tilde{\omega}}
has a different structure from the standard one. Indeed, all the structure constants coincide except for two: specifically,
e_{3}\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits e_{4}=0
in the standard algebra
\mathbb{V}_{5}
, whereas
e_{3}\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits^{\tilde{\omega}}_{e_{1}}e_{4}=e_{1}
in
\mathbb{V}_{5}^{\tilde{\omega}}
.
Hence, the two algebras are not isomorphic.

To work canonically, throughout this paper, we fix once and for all the standard symplectic form

\omega_{0}(e_{2i},e_{2i+1})=1\quad\text{for }i=1,\dots,n,

with respect to an ordered orthonormal basis
\{e_{1},\dots,e_{2n+1}\}
of
\mathbb{R}^{2n+1}
. Whenever we consider an inclusion
\mathbb{R}^{2n+1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2n+3}
, we assume that the basis of
\mathbb{R}^{2n+3}
extends that of
\mathbb{R}^{2n+1}
by appending the ordered pair
(e_{2n+2},e_{2n+3})
to the end of the fixed basis.

Note that, in dimension 7, this standard form agrees with the one induced by the cross product via the calibration identity

\omega(u,v)=e_{1}\cdot(u\times v),

where
u,v\in V_{0}
. Thus the 7-dimensional Vidinli algebra constructed using the standard basis and product coincides with the one defined by the classical cross product on
\mathbb{R}^{7}
. See
[
26
]
for details about associative calibrations in
\mathbb{R}^{7}
. This is one of the main reasons for us to choose the standard symplectic form to work with.

5
The exceptional Vidinli Algebra
\mathbb{V}_{7}

Among all odd-dimensional spaces, the case
m=7
holds a distinguished place in both geometry and algebra. It is the only dimension beyond three in which a nontrivial cross product can be defined (see
[
3
]
or
[
7
]
), owing to the normed division algebra structure of the octonions. This exceptional feature endows
\mathbb{R}^{7}
with a canonical skew-symmetric bilinear form defined via the projection of the octonionic product onto the imaginary subspace and induces a globally defined cross product structure. As a result, the exceptional Vidinli algebra
\mathbb{V}_{7}
, defined via the cross product, inherits a rich internal geometry that cannot be replicated in higher dimensions.

In this section, we study
\mathbb{V}_{7}
as the first nontrivial representative of the general Vidinli family beyond
\mathbb{V}_{3}
, and uncover its unique algebraic and combinatorial features. These include its connection with the Fano plane, the role of directional multiplication rules induced by octonionic cross products, and its realization as a
G_{2}
-symmetric fusion of
\mathbb{V}_{3}
subalgebras. Here
G_{2}
denotes the group of automorphisms of octonions, see
[
29
]
,
[
1
]
. The results presented here serve both as an independent model of interest and as a conceptual bridge toward the general framework developed in later sections, where higher-dimensional analogues are explored via projective geometries and degenerate pushout constructions.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
\mathbb{R}^{7}
denote the space of imaginary octonions equipped with a fixed orthonormal basis
\{e_{i}\mid i=1,\dots,7\}
and the cross product as specified in Example
4.10
.
We define the
exceptional Vidinli algebra

\mathbb{V}_{7}
to be
\mathbb{R}^{7}
equipped with the Vidinli multiplication

a\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits_{7}b\;:=\;(a\cdot e_{1})\,b+(b\cdot e_{1})\,a-(a\cdot b)\,e_{1}+(e_{1}\cdot(a\times b))\,e_{1}.

By the discussion in Example
4.10
, it is straightforward to conclude that the exceptional Vidinli algebra is the Vidinli algebra of dimension 7.
Specializing the general formula in Section
4.1
to
n=3
, we obtain the explicit coordinate expression for the exceptional algebra
\mathbb{V}_{7}
. Let
a=\sum_{i=1}^{7}a_{i}e_{i}
,
b=\sum_{i=1}^{7}b_{i}e_{i}
. Then

a\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits_{7}b=c_{1}e_{1}+\sum_{i=2}^{7}c_{i}e_{i},

where

c_{1}=a_{1}b_{1}-\sum_{i=2}^{7}a_{i}b_{i}+(a_{2}b_{3}-a_{3}b_{2})+(a_{4}b_{5}-a_{5}b_{4})+(a_{6}b_{7}-a_{7}b_{6}),

c_{i}=a_{1}b_{i}+b_{1}a_{i}\quad\text{for }i=2,\dots,7.

This expression directly evaluates the Vidinli multiplication in terms of coordinates, and the standard symplectic term

\omega(a,b)=(a_{2}b_{3}-a_{3}b_{2})+(a_{4}b_{5}-a_{5}b_{4})+(a_{6}b_{7}-a_{7}b_{6}).

This definition is also a version of the coordinate-free formulation of the three dimensional Vidinli algebra, with the seven dimensional cross product replacing the classical one.

Remark 5.2
.

The definition of the Vidinli multiplication bears resemblance to the constructions in
[
26
, Theorem 2.9 and Theorem 6.15(iii)]
, where normed algebra structures on
\mathbb{R}^{t}
, for
t=1,2,4,8
, are described via explicit bilinear products. In Theorem 2.9 of
[
26
]
, an element
u\in\mathbb{R}^{t}
is decomposed as
u=(u_{0},u_{1})
, where
u_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
,
u_{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{t-1}
, and the product is defined by

uv=u_{0}v_{0}-u_{1}\cdot v_{1}+u_{0}v_{1}+v_{0}u_{1}+u_{1}\times v_{1}.

This product endows
\mathbb{R}^{t}
with the structure of a normed algebra, where
\cdot
and
\times
denote the standard inner and cross products on
\mathbb{R}^{t-1}
, respectively. Theorem 6.15(iii) gives an equivalent formulation.

One can also define exceptional Vidinli algebra using the other basis elements as the principal direction.
For each index
i\in\{1,\dots,7\}
, define the
Vidinli multiplication in direction
e_{i}
by

a\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits_{i}b\;:=\;(a\cdot e_{i})\,b+(b\cdot e_{i})\,a-(a\cdot b)\,e_{i}+(e_{i}\cdot(a\times b))\,e_{i}.

The resulting algebra
\mathbb{V}_{7,i}:=(\mathbb{R}^{7},\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits_{7,i})
is called the
exceptional Vidinli algebra with principal direction
e_{i}
. As the next result shows there is no ambiguity in fixing the direction.

Proposition 5.3
.

For any
i,j\in\{1,\dots,7\}
, the algebras
\mathbb{V}_{7,i}
and
\mathbb{V}_{7,j}
are isomorphic via the action of the exceptional Lie group
G_{2}
.

Proof.

Suppose
i,j\in\{1,\ldots,7\}
with
i\neq j
are given. Then we can choose two distinct basic triples
\{e_{i},e_{i}^{\prime},e_{i}^{\prime\prime}\}
and
\{e_{j},e_{j}^{\prime},e_{j}^{\prime\prime}\}
as explained in
[
1
, Section 4.1]
. Then there is an element
g\in G_{2}
mapping the first triple to the second one. In particular,
g(e_{i})=e_{j}
. The group
G_{2}\subset SO(7)
being the automorphism group of the octonions, preserves the 7-dimensional cross product and also the standard inner product.
Hence the action of
g
on the family of Vidinli algebras can be evaluated as follows.

\displaystyle g(a\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits_{7,i}b)

\displaystyle\;=\;(g(a)\cdot e_{j})g(b)+(g(b)\cdot e_{j})g(a)-(g(a)\cdot g(b))e_{j}

\displaystyle+(e_{j}\cdot(g(a)\times g(b)))e_{j}

\displaystyle=g(a)\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits_{7,j}g(b)

This establishes the required isomorphism between
\mathbb{V}_{7,i}
and
\mathbb{V}_{7,j}
.
∎

This symmetry is specific to dimension 7 and shows the exceptional nature of
G_{2}
as both a rotation group and a structure-preserving symmetry of the Vidinli multiplication. While every
\mathbb{V}_{7,i}
introduces a direction-dependent formulation, the underlying geometry remains invariant under the
G_{2}
-action.

Another feature of the above
G_{2}
-orbit of Vidinli multiplications is that each of
\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits_{7,i}
defines a (directional) commutator

[a,b]_{i}:=2(e_{i}\cdot(a\times b))e_{i}

that projects the cross product
a\times b
along the corresponding principal axis.
This makes the 7-dimensional setting a uniquely natural realization of the Vidinli framework. Conversely, it is clear that the sum of (directional) commutators over the basis
e_{i}
reproduces the cross-product.

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
a,b\in\mathbb{R}^{7}
. Then the cross product satisfies

a\times b=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{7}[a,b]_{7,i},

where
[a,b]_{7,i}:=a\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits_{7,i}b-b\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits_{7,i}a
is the commutator in the directional algebra
\mathbb{V}_{7,i}
.

Remark 5.5
.

1.

An analogous decomposition holds in dimension 3, using the classical cross product.

2.

Each commutator
[a,b]_{7,i}
is Lie-admissible. This contrasts with the standard cross product on
\mathbb{R}^{7}
, which does not define a Lie algebra. Hence the above sum can be considered as a
Lie admissible decomposition
of the cross product.

We now describe a canonical family of 3-dimensional Vidinli subalgebras inside
\mathbb{V}_{7}
, obtained by fixing the principal axis
e_{1}
and varying the direction in the orthogonal complement. This generalizes Proposition
5.3
.

Theorem 5.6
.

Let
\mathbb{V}_{7}=(\mathbb{R}^{7},\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits_{7})
. Then:

1.

For each unit vector
u\in e_{1}^{\perp}\subset\mathbb{R}^{7}
, the subspace

W_{u}:=\mathrm{Span}_{\mathbb{R}}\{e_{1},u,e_{1}\times u\}

is closed under the product
\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits_{7}
, and the restricted multiplication defines a 3-dimensional subalgebra isomorphic to
\mathbb{V}_{3}
.

2.

The stabilizer
SU(3)\subset G_{2}
of
e_{1}\in S^{6}
acts transitively on
e_{1}^{\perp}
. This action extends to a transitive action on the family
\{W_{u}\subset\mathbb{V}_{7}\mid u\in e_{1}^{\perp}\}
of subalgebras each isomorphic to
\mathbb{V}_{3}
in
\mathbb{V}_{7}
. Hence
\{W_{u}\mid u\in e_{1}^{\perp}\}\cong G_{2}/SU(3)
.

Proof.

(1) Since the Vidinli multiplication is bilinear, it suffices to check that the products of basis vectors lie in
W_{u}
. Also
e_{1}
is the identity, hence we only compute the product of
u
and
e_{1}\times u
. Since
\{e_{1},u,e_{1}\times u\}
is an orthogonal set, the product reduces to the following:

\displaystyle u\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits(e_{1}\times u)

\displaystyle=(e_{1}\cdot(u\times(e_{1}\times u)))e_{1}=-(e_{1}\cdot(u\times(u\times e_{1})))e_{1}

\displaystyle=-(e_{1}\cdot e_{1})e_{1}=-e_{1}

Here we use
[
26
, Lemma 2.9(v)]
to evaluate the triple cross product. Together with this equality, we conclude that the subspace
W_{u}
is a subalgebra and moreover it is isomorphic to
\mathbb{V}_{3}
since the structure constants of these algebras coincide.

(2) By
[
26
, Theorem 8.1]
, the stabilizer of
e_{1}
is the group
SU(3)
. Moreover by
[
26
, Theorem 8.2]
, the group
G_{2}
acts transitively on the set of triples
(u,v,u\times v)
of orthogonal vectors. Hence the action of
SU(3)
on the orthogonal complement of
e_{1}
is also transitive.
Moreover if
v=g\cdot u
for some
g\in SU(3)
, then
g
fixes
e_{1}
and preserves the cross product, hence
g(W_{u})=W_{v}
.
∎

Remark 5.7
.

There are three canonical subspaces in
\mathbb{V}_{7}
, corresponding to the three lines in the Fano plane passing through
e_{1}
given as

W_{e_{2}}=\mathrm{Span}_{\mathbb{R}}(e_{1},e_{2},e_{3});\;W_{e_{4}}=\mathrm{Span}_{\mathbb{R}}(e_{1},e_{4},e_{5});\;W_{e_{6}}=\mathrm{Span}_{\mathbb{R}}(e_{1},e_{6},e_{7}).

By Theorem
5.6
, and the equality
e_{1}\times e_{2i}=e_{2i+1}
for
i=1,2,3
, each of these subspaces together with
\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{V}_{3}
.

We now consider the subspaces corresponding to the remaining four lines in the Fano plane, those not containing
e_{1}
. These generate a different class of subalgebras.

Proposition 5.8
.

Let
\{u,v,u\times v\}
be a line in the Fano plane disjoint from
e_{1}
, and let
W_{u,v}=\mathrm{Span}_{\mathbb{R}}\{u,v,u\times v\}
. Then for any
a,b\in W_{u,v}
,

a\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits_{7}b=-(a\cdot b)\,e_{1}.

Moreover, each line determines a 4-dimensional Jordan subalgebra isomorphic to

\mathcal{VJ}_{4}\cong\mathbb{R}e_{1}\oplus W_{u,v}.

Proof.

Since the cross product of vectors in
W_{u,v}
lies in
W_{u,v}
, projection of these kind of vectors to
e_{1}
is zero. Hence the Vidinli multiplication reduces to the given formula. The second claim follows from the definition of the Vidinli-Jordan algebras.
∎

Remark 5.9
.

By
[
5
, Proposition 9]
, see also Section
3.1
, the Vidinli multiplication in dimension three arises as a push-forward of quaternion multiplication. Whether a similar push-forward description exists for the exceptional Vidinli algebra
\mathbb{V}_{7}
in terms of the octonions, or more generally for
\mathbb{V}_{2^{k}-1}
in relation to higher Cayley-Dickson algebras, remains an open question.

6
Geometric Decomposition of Vidinli Algebras

A remarkable feature of the Vidinli algebras
\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}
is the extent to which their structure is governed by discrete geometric data. In certain dimensions, notably those of the form
2^{k}-1
, the entire multiplication table of
\mathbb{V}_{2^{k}-1}
can be reconstructed from its restrictions to a finite family of canonical subalgebras. These subalgebras, in turn, are determined by the incidence structure of the projective geometry
\mathrm{PG}(k-1,2)
, whose points label the standard basis vectors
e_{1},\dots,e_{2^{k}-1}
, and whose subspaces organize the allowable interactions between them.

This section develops a geometric decomposition of
\mathbb{V}_{2^{k}-1}
for each
k\geq 3
, revealing a recursive hierarchy in which:

-

lower-dimensional Vidinli algebras
\mathbb{V}_{2^{k-1}-1}
appear as subalgebras defined on
(k-2)
- dimensional subspaces that contain the unit direction
e_{1}
,

-

Vidinli-Jordan subalgebras
\mathcal{VJ}_{2^{k}-1}
arise from subspaces disjoint from
e_{1}
, and

-

the entire product is uniquely determined by enforcing consistency on these local substructures.

6.1
The Exceptional Vidinli Algebra

The first nontrivial instance of the geometric decomposition occurs in dimension 7, where the structure of
\mathbb{V}_{7}
is determined by the incidence geometry of the Fano plane
\mathrm{PG}(2,2)
.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
\mathbb{V}_{7}=(\mathbb{R}^{7},\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits_{7})
be the exceptional Vidinli algebra, with standard basis
\{e_{1},\dots,e_{7}\}
indexed by the points of the Fano plane
\mathrm{PG}(2,2)
corresponding to the cross product introduced above.
Then the algebra structure of
\mathbb{V}_{7}
is completely determined by the following data.

(i)

For each line
L_{j}=\{e_{1},e_{j},e_{k}\}
through
e_{1}
, the subspace
W_{e_{j}}
is a subalgebra isomorphic to
\mathbb{V}_{3}
.

(ii)

For each line
L_{i,k}=\{e_{i},e_{k},e_{l}\}
not containing
e_{1}
, the subspace

W_{e_{i},e_{k}}
is a 4-dimensional Jordan subalgebra isomorphic to
\mathcal{VJ}_{4}
.

(iii)

The full multiplication in
\mathbb{V}_{7}
is uniquely determined by these seven local rules, three from the Vidinli subalgebras
\mathbb{V}_{3}
and four from the Jordan subalgebras
\mathcal{VJ}_{4}
.

Proof.

Statements (i) and (ii) follow directly from Theorem
5.6
and Proposition
5.8
. For (iii), observe that there are
\binom{7}{2}+7=28
unordered pairs
(e_{i},e_{j})
with
i\leq j
, and each unordered pair of distinct indices lies in exactly one line.
Hence these local rules define a unique product on these basis vectors. Note also that the products of the form
e_{i}\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits e_{i}
agree on the lines
L_{i}
and
L_{i,k}
. Moreover the diagonal cases are fixed by the unit rule. Hence together, these multiplications extend to a unique bilinear form on all of
\mathbb{R}^{7}
. Since the resulting structure constants coincide with those of the Vidinli algebra given in Section
4.1
, we conclude that the multiplication coincides with the Vidinli multiplication.
∎

The geometry of the Fano plane thus provides a complete local rule-book for the exceptional Vidinli algebra. Each line defines either a non-associative or symmetric subalgebra, and together they recover the entire multiplication law.

This invites a natural question: can the same principle be extended to higher dimensions? In what follows, we show that the answer is yes, each Vidinli algebra
\mathbb{V}_{2^{k}-1}
admits a canonical geometric decomposition governed by the incidence structure of the projective geometry
\mathrm{PG}(k-1,2)
, generalizing the role of the Fano plane in
\mathbb{V}_{7}
.

6.2
Decomposition via Geometric Supports

We now turn to the 15-dimensional algebra
\mathbb{V}_{15}=(\mathbb{R}^{15},\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits)
. Its structure admits a canonical decomposition governed by the incidence geometry of projective 3-space
\mathrm{PG}(3,2)
. In this identification, the 15 points of
\mathrm{PG}(3,2)
correspond to the standard basis vectors
e_{1},\dots,e_{15}\subset\mathbb{R}^{15}
, and the 35 lines and 15 planes of the projective space organize their interactions under the Vidinli multiplication.

The geometric role played by lines in the Fano plane
\mathrm{PG}(2,2)
is now assumed by planes in
\mathrm{PG}(3,2)
, and the analogy is made explicit by the following correspondence:

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\mathrm{PG}(2,2)&\longrightarrow&\mathrm{PG}(3,2)\\[2.0pt]
7\text{ points}&\longrightarrow&15\text{ points}\\
7\text{ lines}&\longrightarrow&35\text{ lines}\\
3\text{ lines through }e_{1}&\longrightarrow&7\text{ planes through }e_{1}\\
4\text{ lines disjoint from }e_{1}&\longrightarrow&8\text{ planes disjoint from }e_{1}\end{array}

This recursive shift—from lines to planes—signals a deeper stratification of the algebraic structure with increasing dimension.

As in the 7-dimensional case, where the seven lines of
\mathrm{PG}(2,2)
uniquely determined the multiplication in
\mathbb{V}_{7}
, we now observe that the fifteen planes of
\mathrm{PG}(3,2)
suffice to recover all structure constants of
\mathbb{V}_{15}
. Specifically:

•

Each of the 7 planes contains
e_{1}
and is of rank 3 in
\mathrm{PG}(3,2)
defines a 7-dimensional non-associative subalgebra isomorphic to
\mathbb{V}_{7}
;

•

Each of the 8 planes disjoint from
e_{1}
extends, by adjoining
e_{1}
, to an 8-dimensional Vidinli–Jordan subalgebra under the symmetric part of the product;

•

The global multiplication on
\mathbb{V}_{15}
is uniquely determined by enforcing compatibility on these fifteen local rules, one for each plane.

This pattern extends naturally. To describe the structure, fix
k\geq 3
, and identify the projective space
\mathrm{PG}(k-1,2)
. We refer to
[
30
]
or
[
8
]
for standard background.

Denote the points of
\mathrm{PG}(k-1,2)
by the basis vectors
e_{1},e_{2},\ldots,e_{2^{k}-1}
in such a way that each line containing
e_{1}
also contains
e_{2i}
and
e_{2i+1}
for some
i=1,\ldots,2^{k-1}-1
. This indexing is possible since, in characteristic two, each line contains exactly three points, and any two points determine a unique line. Fixing
e_{1}
as one of the points, the remaining two determines each other uniquely.

Similarly, any rank-
(k-2)
subspace
W
containing
e_{1}
must satisfy the condition that, for each
i
, we have

e_{2i}\in W\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad e_{2i+1}\in W.

We call any such subspace
W
of rank
k-2
a
Vidinli anti-chain
.

Dually, if a rank-
(k-2)
subspace
W
does not contain
e_{1}
, then for each
i
we must have that exactly one of
e_{2i},e_{2i+1}
lies in
W
.
We call any subspace of rank
k-2
with this property a
Vidinli–Jordan chain
.

These subspaces of
\mathrm{PG}(k-1,2)
, serving as
geometric supports
, define subalgebras isomorphic to
\mathbb{V}_{2^{k-1}-1}
or to
\mathcal{VJ}_{2^{k-1}}
, depending on whether or not they contain the distinguished unit direction. This observation suggests that the Vidinli construction may be understood as part of a geometric inductive system, with the algebra
\mathbb{V}_{2^{k}-1}
structured by the incidence geometry of
\mathrm{PG}(k-1,2)
. Indeed, the sequence

\mathbb{V}_{3}\rightsquigarrow\mathbb{V}_{7}\rightsquigarrow\mathbb{V}_{15}\rightsquigarrow\cdots

mirrors the projective tower

\mathrm{PG}(1,2)\subset\mathrm{PG}(2,2)\subset\mathrm{PG}(3,2)\subset\cdots

in which each level enriches the previous one by increasing the ambient dimension and the complexity of its incidence structure. We now state the main result of this section.

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
k\geq 3
, and let
\mathbb{V}_{2^{k}-1}=(\mathbb{R}^{2^{k}-1},\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits)
be the standard Vidinli algebra with unit element
e_{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{2^{k}-1}
. Let
\mathbb{P}_{k}=\mathrm{PG}(k-1,2)
be the projective space constructed above. Then:

(i)

Each Vidinli anti-chain
\Lambda
in
\mathbb{P}_{k}
spans a subalgebra

\mathbb{V}_{\Lambda}:=\mathrm{Span}_{\mathbb{R}}\{e_{j}\mid e_{j}\in\Lambda\}

isomorphic to
\mathbb{V}_{2^{k-1}-1}
, under the restriction of the Vidinli multiplication
\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits
to
\mathbb{V}_{\Lambda}
.

(ii)

Each Vidinli-Jordan chain
\Lambda^{\prime}
in
\mathbb{P}_{k}
determines a subalgebra

\mathcal{VJ}_{\Lambda^{\prime}}:=\mathbb{R}e_{1}\oplus\mathrm{Span}_{\mathbb{R}}\{e_{j}\mid e_{j}\in\Lambda^{\prime}\}

isomorphic to
\mathcal{VJ}_{2^{k-1}}
, under the restriction of the Vidinli multiplication
\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits
to
\mathcal{VJ}_{\Lambda^{\prime}}
.

(iii)

The induced multiplications on intersecting subspaces are compatible:

\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits|_{\Lambda_{1}\cap\Lambda_{2}}=\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits|_{\Lambda_{2}\cap\Lambda_{1}},

for any pair of Vidinli anti-chains/Vidinli-Jordan chains
\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2}\subset\mathbb{P}_{k}
.

(iv)

Conversely, let
\mathbb{A}=(\mathbb{R}^{2^{k}-1},\ast)
be a bilinear product such that:

(a)

for each Vidinli anti-chain
\Lambda
, we have
(\mathbb{V}_{\Lambda},\ast)\cong\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}
;

(b)

for each Vidinli-Jordan chain
\Lambda^{\prime}
, we have
(\mathcal{VJ}_{\Lambda^{\prime}},\ast)\cong\mathcal{VJ}_{2^{k-1}}
;

(c)

these local products are pairwise compatible on intersections.

Then
\mathbb{A}\cong\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}
as real algebras.

Proof.

Parts (i)–(ii) follow from the structure of the Vidinli multiplication, since the structure constants
\mathrm{v}_{i,j}^{k}
for any
i,j
and
k
are determined locally on the space generated by
e_{1},e_{i}
and
e_{j}
. Compatibility follows from bilinearity and the explicit form of
\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits
, which is defined globally and both subalgebra types are defined via restrictions of
\omega
.

For part (iv), suppose we are given an abstract bilinear product
\ast
on
\mathbb{R}^{2^{k}-1}
, together with local multiplication rules on each
(k-2)
-subspace, satisfying the same compatibility conditions.

By bilinearity, it suffices to determine the product
e_{i}\ast e_{j}
for all basis pairs. Each such pair lies in at least one (and usually several)
(k-2)
-dimensional subspaces. Since all local products agree on overlaps, the product
e_{i}\ast e_{j}
is unambiguously defined by restricting to any such subspace. The resulting multiplication satisfies the defining properties of the Vidinli multiplication, and hence must coincide with
\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits
.
∎

Remark 6.3
.

In the case
k=3
, the geometric decomposition involves lines in the Fano plane
\mathrm{PG}(2,2)
, and any two lines intersect in at most one point. As a result, the compatibility condition among local products is trivially satisfied.

However in higher dimensions
k\geq 4
, the
(k-2)
-dimensional subspaces of the projective space
\mathrm{PG}(k-1,2)
generally intersect along higher-dimensional spans. The agreement of local products on these overlaps becomes essential to ensure that the global multiplication is well-defined. This marks a fundamental shift in complexity within the Vidinli hierarchy.

Remark 6.4
.

It is natural to compare the Vidinli algebras of dimension
2^{k}-1
with the Cayley-Dickson algebras
[
9
]
,
[
1
]
. We postpone the comparison to Section
8.3
.

7
Degenerate Pushouts in the Category of Algebras

7.1
Motivation

The projective decomposition of Vidinli algebras, as developed in the previous section, expresses each
\mathbb{V}_{2^{k}-1}
as a coherent gluing of lower-dimensional subalgebras supported on the
(k-2)
- dimensional subspaces of
\mathrm{PG}(k-1,2)
. This geometric structure captures the global multiplication law in terms of local rules, but leaves implicit the mechanism by which these local pieces combine algebraically.

In this section, we introduce a complementary construction, called the
degenerate pushout
, that makes this gluing explicit at the level of algebraic structure. Unlike the geometric decomposition, which allows overlaps and compatibility conditions along intersections, the degenerate pushout produces a global algebra by fusing components along a shared unit direction while annihilating all cross-interactions. This process is both finer and more rigid: it decomposes the algebra into orthogonal subalgebras joined along a common axis, with no interaction beyond the shared unit.

The degenerate pushout construction applies not only to Vidinli algebras, but also to classical examples such as Heisenberg Lie algebras and spin factor Jordan algebras. It provides a unifying framework for assembling algebras from elementary building blocks, and reveals a categorical mechanism behind their internal structure.

In particular, we will show that:

•

The algebra
\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}
admits a realization as a degenerate pushout of
n
copies of the basic
\mathbb{V}_{3}
, glued along the unit direction (Theorem
8.1
);

•

Similar constructions recover the Heisenberg algebras
\mathfrak{h}_{n}
and spin factors
\mathcal{J}\mathrm{Spin}_{n}
(Section
7.3
);

•

The categorical structure of degenerate pushouts preserves properties such as unitality, nilpotency and Jordan identity. Associativity is never preserved while simplicity is preserved under a condition. (Section
7.4
)

We begin by formalizing the categorical notion of a degenerate pushout in the category of unital real algebras, and then illustrate its reach and limitations through a series of examples. We refer to
[
20
]
and
[
25
]
for category theoretic background and
[
31
]
for non-associative notions in categorical algebra.

7.2
Degenerate Pushout of Algebras

Let

be a unital (not necessarily associative)
\mathbb{R}
-algebras together with unital algebra embeddings

f_{i}:Z\hookrightarrow A_{i},\quad i=1,2.

Following Waldhausen

(see also
[

]

free product of
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
amalgamated at

as the colimit
A_{1}\ast_{Z}A_{2}
of the diagram

A_{1}\;\xleftarrow{\;f_{1}\;}\;Z\;\xrightarrow{\;f_{2}\;}\;A_{2},

in the category of rings. The colimit satisfies the universal property that for any other
\mathbb{R}
-algebra
Q
with morphisms
g_{1},g_{2}
satisfying the same compatibility, there exists a unique homomorphism
h:A_{1}\ast_{Z}A_{2}\to Q
making the following diagram commute.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A_{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A_{2}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A_{1}\ast_{Z}A_{2}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Q}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f_{1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f_{2}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota_{1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g_{1}}

This construction can be generalized to
n
-fold free products with amalgamation and it is straightforward to check that the
n
-fold product is associative. Hence given a family of unital algebra homomorphisms
f_{i}:Z\hookrightarrow A_{i}
for
i=1,\ldots,n
, we denote the free product of the algebras
A_{1},\ldots,A_{n}
amalgamated at

by

A_{1}\ast_{Z}A_{2}\ast_{Z}\ldots\ast_{Z}A_{n}.

This algebra can be constructed as follows. Let
A=A_{1}\ast A_{2}\ast\ldots\ast A_{n}
be the non-associative free product of algebras

as defined by Kurosh
[

. Also let

be the two sided ideal of

A_{1}\ast_{Z}A_{2}\ast_{Z}\ldots\ast_{Z}A_{n}=A_{1}\ast A_{2}\ast\ldots\ast A_{n}\Big/I.

Definition 7.1
.

Let
Z
be a unital
\mathbb{R}
–algebra (not necessarily associative), and let

f_{i}:Z\hookrightarrow A_{i},\quad i=1,\dots,k

be unital algebra embeddings into unital
\mathbb{R}
–algebras
A_{i}
. Define the
degenerate pushout
of the algebras
A_{1},\ldots,A_{k}
amalgamated at
Z
by

A\;=\;\bigl(A_{1}*_{Z}A_{2}*_{Z}\cdots*_{Z}A_{k}\bigr)\;\Big/\;\Bigl\langle\,a\,b\;\Bigm|\;a\in A_{i}\setminus Z,\;b\in A_{j}\setminus Z,\;i\neq j\Bigr\rangle.

In other words we first form the free product
A_{1}*_{Z}\cdots*_{Z}A_{k}
amalgamated at
Z
, and then quotient by the two-sided ideal generated by all products between elements from distinct summands outside
Z
. Equivalently,
A=(A,\diamond)
is the unital
\mathbb{R}
–algebra characterized by:

1.

Each
A_{i}
embeds as a unital subalgebra of
A
, and the images of all
f_{i}(z)
,
z\in Z
, are identified.

2.

Vanishing cross–copy products:
For all
i\neq j
,
a\in A_{i}\setminus Z
,
b\in A_{j}\setminus Z
,

a\diamond b\;=\;0,\quad b\diamond a\;=\;0.

3.

Universal property:
Given any unital
\mathbb{R}
–algebra
Q
and homomorphisms

\phi_{i}:A_{i}\to Q
such that

\phi_{i}\circ f_{i}\;=\;\phi_{j}\circ f_{j}\quad\text{for all }i,j,\quad\text{and}\quad\phi_{i}(A_{i}\setminus Z)\,\phi_{j}(A_{j}\setminus Z)=0\quad(i\neq j),

there exists a unique homomorphism

\Phi:A\to Q

with
\Phi|_{A_{i}}=\phi_{i}
.

Notation.
We denote the degenerate pushout of
A_{1},A_{2},\ldots,A_{k}
over
Z
by

A_{1}\odot_{Z}A_{2}\odot_{Z}\ldots\odot_{Z}A_{k}.

When
Z=\mathbb{R}
is the scalar subalgebra, we simply write
\odot
instead of
\odot_{\mathbb{R}}
.

The degenerate pushout admits a canonical decomposition as a direct sum, capturing the contribution of each component modulo the shared subalgebra.

Proposition 7.2
.

Let
A_{1},\dots,A_{k}
be unital
\mathbb{R}
-algebras with a common unital subalgebra
Z\subset A_{i}
for all
i
.
Then the degenerate pushout over
Z
,

A\;:=\;A_{1}\odot_{Z}\cdots\odot_{Z}A_{k},

admits the vector space decomposition

A\;\cong\;Z\;\oplus\;\bigoplus_{i=1}^{k}(A_{i}/Z).

In particular,

\dim_{\mathbb{R}}A\;=\;\dim_{\mathbb{R}}Z\;+\;\sum_{i=1}^{k}\bigl(\dim_{\mathbb{R}}A_{i}-\dim_{\mathbb{R}}Z\bigr).

Proof.

By construction of the degenerate pushout, all copies of
Z
are identified, and all cross-products between elements from distinct
A_{i}\setminus Z
vanish.
Hence every element of
A
can be uniquely written as a sum of an element of
Z
and images of elements from
A_{i}\setminus Z
in each factor.
This yields the claimed direct sum decomposition and the dimension formula.
∎

By construction, all reduced words of length
\geq 2
vanish in
A
, so every element has a unique representative

z+\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i},\qquad z\in Z,\quad a_{i}\in A_{i}/Z.

This gives a canonical linear isomorphism

A\;\xrightarrow{\ \cong\ }\;Z\oplus\bigoplus_{i=1}^{k}(A_{i}/Z).

Remark 7.3

(Symmetric Monoidal Structure)
.

Let
\mathbf{Alg}_{\mathbb{R}}
denote the category of (not necessarily associative) unital
\mathbb{R}
-algebras with morphisms given by unital algebra homomorphisms.
The normal form given above shows that, for
Z=\mathbb{R}
, the bifunctor

\odot_{\mathbb{R}}\;:\;\mathbf{Alg}_{\mathbb{R}}\times\mathbf{Alg}_{\mathbb{R}}\longrightarrow\mathbf{Alg}_{\mathbb{R}}

equips
\mathbf{Alg}_{\mathbb{R}}
with a symmetric monoidal structure:

•

Unit:

\mathbb{R}
, with
A\odot_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{R}\cong A
.

•

Associator:
Given
A,B,C
, the associativity isomorphism is the unique algebra isomorphism

(A\odot_{\mathbb{R}}B)\odot_{\mathbb{R}}C\;\xrightarrow{\ \cong\ }\;\mathbb{R}\oplus(A/\mathbb{R})\oplus(B/\mathbb{R})\oplus(C/\mathbb{R})\;\xrightarrow{\ \cong\ }\;A\odot_{\mathbb{R}}(B\odot_{\mathbb{R}}C)

induced by the canonical reordering of the non-scalar summands.
The composition is an
algebra
isomorphism because multiplication in the normal form preserves the summand decomposition and cross-products between distinct non-scalar summands vanish.

•

Symmetry:
The isomorphism
A\odot_{\mathbb{R}}B\cong B\odot_{\mathbb{R}}A
is induced by the canonical swap of the
(A/\mathbb{R})
and
(B/\mathbb{R})
summands.

This symmetric monoidal structure differs from the usual tensor product on
\mathbf{Alg}_{\mathbb{R}}
in that all cross-products between the non-scalar parts vanish. Although we do not pursue this direction here, it may be of independent interest to study the Grothendieck group and related invariants of this symmetric monoidal category. For general background on such categories, see
[
25
]
.

7.3
Examples of Degenerate Pushouts

We now illustrate this construction through a series of examples. These examples exhibit a range of algebraic behaviors, commutative, non-associative, Jordan, and Lie, and demonstrate how classical structures can emerge from a shared gluing pattern. We begin with a simple but instructive case involving multiple copies of the complex numbers.

7.3.1
Degenerate Pushouts of
\mathbb{C}
over
\mathbb{R}

Fix
k\geq 1
and define

\mathbb{A}_{k}\;=\;\underbrace{\mathbb{C}\;\odot_{\mathbb{R}}\;\mathbb{C}\;\odot_{\mathbb{R}}\;\cdots\;\odot_{\mathbb{R}}\;\mathbb{C}}_{k\text{ copies}}.

Each copy of
\mathbb{C}
shares the same unit
1
and contributes its own imaginary direction. We may thus fix a basis

\{1,\;i_{1},\;i_{2},\;\dots,\;i_{k}\},

where
i_{j}
denotes the generator of the
j
-th copy of
i\in\mathbb{C}
. The multiplication in
\mathbb{A}_{k}
is determined by

\begin{gathered}1\cdot 1=1,\qquad 1\cdot i_{j}=i_{j}=i_{j}\cdot 1\quad(1\leq j\leq k),\\
i_{j}^{2}=-1,\qquad i_{j}i_{m}=0\quad\text{for }j\neq m.\end{gathered}

Equivalently,
\mathbb{A}_{k}
is isomorphic to the quotient of the real polynomial algebra by the ideal generated by the defining relations. Hence there is an isomorphism of
\mathbb{R}
-algebras

\mathbb{A}_{k}\;\cong\;\frac{\mathbb{R}[x_{1},\,x_{2},\,\dots,\,x_{k}]}{\bigl(x_{j}^{2}+1,\;x_{j}x_{m}\;\text{for }j\neq m\bigr)},\quad\text{given by }i_{j}\mapsto x_{j}.

The algebra
\mathbb{A}_{k}
is commutative for all
k
, but not associative when
k\geq 2
. Indeed, for distinct indices
j\neq m
,

(i_{j}\cdot i_{j})\cdot i_{m}=(-1)\cdot i_{m}=-i_{m},\qquad i_{j}\cdot(i_{j}\cdot i_{m})=i_{j}\cdot 0=0,

showing that associativity fails. The following theorem follows.

Theorem 7.4
.

Up to a unique isomorphism,

\underbrace{\mathbb{C}\odot_{\mathbb{R}}\cdots\odot_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C}}_{k\text{ copies}}\;\cong\;\mathbb{R}\bigl\langle\,1,\;i_{1},\dots,i_{k}\bigr\rangle\;\bigm/\;\bigl(i_{j}^{2}+1,\;i_{j}i_{m}\;(j\neq m)\bigr).

The nonzero products are:

1\cdot i_{j}=i_{j},\quad i_{j}\cdot 1=i_{j},\quad i_{j}^{2}=-1.

7.3.2
Spin Factors.

Spin factor Jordan algebras offer a second family in which the degenerate pushout construction yields a familiar structure. These algebras are commutative, non-associative, and defined by a symmetric bilinear form. They admit a decomposition analogous to that of the Vidinli algebras, with a shared scalar unit and orthogonal components contributing independently. We now show that the standard spin factor algebra of dimension
n+1
arises as the degenerate pushout of
n
copies of the 2-dimensional spin factor algebra.

Following
[
21
, Section 1.9]
, let

V
be a real inner product space with inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
. The
spin factor Jordan algebra
of dimension
n+1
, denoted
\mathcal{J}\mathrm{Spin}_{n}
, is the real vector space

\mathcal{J}\mathrm{Spin}_{n}:=\mathbb{R}\oplus V,

equipped with the Jordan product

(a,x)\circ(b,y):=(ab+\langle x,y\rangle,\;ay+bx).

It is a unital Jordan algebra with unit element
(1,0)
.

Theorem 7.5
.

Let
\mathcal{J}\mathrm{Spin}_{1}\cong\mathbb{R}\oplus\mathbb{R}
be the 2-dimensional spin factor Jordan algebra. Then for each
n\geq 1
, the
n
-fold degenerate pushout of
\mathcal{J}\mathrm{Spin}_{1}
over
\mathbb{R}
satisfies

\mathcal{J}\mathrm{Spin}_{n}\;\cong\;\underbrace{\mathcal{J}\mathrm{Spin}_{1}\;\odot_{\mathbb{R}}\cdots\odot_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{J}\mathrm{Spin}_{1}}_{n\text{ times}}.

Proof.

Let
A:=\mathcal{J}\mathrm{Spin}_{1}^{(1)}\odot_{\mathbb{R}}\cdots\odot_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{J}\mathrm{Spin}_{1}^{(n)}
be the degenerate pushout of
n
copies of
\mathcal{J}\mathrm{Spin}_{1}
, with the common unit
1\in\mathbb{R}
. Each component
\mathcal{J}\mathrm{Spin}_{1}^{(i)}
has basis
\{1,e_{i}\}
with multiplication satisfying

e_{i}\circ e_{i}=1,\qquad e_{i}\circ 1=e_{i},\qquad e_{i}\circ e_{j}=0\quad\text{for }i\neq j.

As a vector space,

A=\mathbb{R}\cdot 1\oplus\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{R}\cdot e_{i}\;\cong\;\mathbb{R}\oplus\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Let
\mathcal{J}\mathrm{Spin}_{n}
be the spin factor of dimension
n+1
with the underlying vector space having basis
\{1,e_{1},e_{2},\ldots,e_{n}\}
and the inner product chosen so that the basis is orthonormal. Define a linear map

\Psi:A\to\mathcal{J}\mathrm{Spin}_{n}=\mathbb{R}\oplus\mathbb{R}^{n}

by

\Psi\left(\alpha+\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}e_{i}\right):=\left(\alpha,\;(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\right).

This map is linear and bijective. To verify that it respects the algebra structure, let

a=\alpha+\sum a_{i}e_{i},\qquad b=\beta+\sum b_{i}e_{i},

with
x:=\sum a_{i}e_{i}
,
y:=\sum b_{i}e_{i}
. Then the product in
A
is:

a\circ b=(\alpha\beta+\sum a_{i}b_{i})+(\alpha y+\beta x),

which maps under
\Psi
to

\Psi(a\circ b)=\left(\alpha\beta+\sum a_{i}b_{i},\;\alpha(b_{1},\dots,b_{n})+\beta(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\right).

This is precisely the product in
\mathcal{J}\mathrm{Spin}_{n}
:

(\alpha,a)\circ(\beta,b)=(\alpha\beta+\langle a,b\rangle,\;\alpha b+\beta a).

Hence
\Psi
is an isomorphism of Jordan algebras.
∎

7.3.3
Vidinli–Jordan Algebras

The Vidinli–Jordan algebra provides a natural instance of the degenerate pushout construction. Similar to the spin factor Jordan algebra, it is defined on the space
\mathbb{R}\oplus\mathbb{R}^{2n}
, but features a negative-definite scalar term and is not formally real. The decomposition into orthogonal 3-dimensional components allows it to be expressed as a gluing of
n
copies of the basic
3
-dimensional Vidinli–Jordan algebra.

Proposition 7.6
.

The Vidinli–Jordan algebra
\mathcal{VJ}_{2n+1}
is isomorphic to the degenerate pushout of
n
copies of the
3
-dimensional Vidinli–Jordan algebra
\mathcal{VJ}_{3}
, glued along the common unit axis, that is,

\mathcal{VJ}_{2n+1}\;\cong\;\mathcal{VJ}_{3}^{(1)}\odot_{\mathbb{R}e_{1}}\cdots\odot_{\mathbb{R}e_{1}}\mathcal{VJ}_{3}^{(n)}.

Proof.

By the definition of
\mathop{\circ_{\mathfrak{v}}}\nolimits
, each subspace

W_{i}:=\mathbb{R}e_{1}\oplus\mathbb{R}e_{2i}\oplus\mathbb{R}e_{2i+1}

is closed under the Vidinli–Jordan product and forms a subalgebra isomorphic to
\mathcal{VJ}_{3}
. Since the subspaces
W_{i}
are orthogonal outside the common axis
e_{1}
, we have
a\cdot b=0
for all
a\in W_{i}\setminus\mathbb{R}e_{1}
,
b\in W_{j}\setminus\mathbb{R}e_{1}
,
i\neq j
. Thus,

a\mathop{\circ_{\mathfrak{v}}}\nolimits b=(a\cdot e_{1})\,b+(b\cdot e_{1})\,a-(a\cdot b)\,e_{1}=0.

The product therefore vanishes across distinct components, and the resulting algebra satisfies the universal property of the degenerate pushout over
\mathbb{R}e_{1}
by comparing the dimensions.
∎

7.3.4
Heisenberg Lie Algebras

We now apply the degenerate pushout construction to Lie algebras. A natural instance arises in the case of Heisenberg Lie algebras, which are nilpotent Lie algebras with a one-dimensional center. By gluing multiple copies of the 3-dimensional Heisenberg algebra along their common center, with all cross-brackets set to zero, we recover the higher-dimensional Heisenberg algebra.

Theorem 7.7
.

Let
\mathfrak{h}_{1}
denote the 3-dimensional Heisenberg Lie algebra. Then for each
n\geq 1
, the
n
-fold degenerate pushout satisfies

\mathfrak{h}_{n}\;\cong\;\underbrace{\mathfrak{h}_{1}\odot\cdots\odot\mathfrak{h}_{1}}_{n\ \text{times}}=:\mathfrak{h}_{1}^{\odot n}.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{h}^{(i)}:=\mathbb{R}\cdot z\oplus\mathbb{R}\cdot x_{i}\oplus\mathbb{R}\cdot y_{i}
be the
i
-th copy of
\mathfrak{h}_{1}
, with nontrivial bracket

[x_{i},y_{i}]=z.

Define
\mathfrak{g}:=\mathfrak{h}^{(1)}\odot\cdots\odot\mathfrak{h}^{(n)}
, the degenerate pushout over the common center
\mathbb{R}\cdot z
. Then in
\mathfrak{g}
, we have:

•

[x_{i},y_{i}]=z
for all
i=1,\dots,n
,

•

All cross-brackets vanish:
[x_{i},x_{j}]=[x_{i},y_{j}]=[y_{i},y_{j}]=0
for
i\neq j
.

Thus, as a vector space,

\mathfrak{g}=\mathbb{R}\cdot z\oplus\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}\left(\mathbb{R}\cdot x_{i}\oplus\mathbb{R}\cdot y_{i}\right)\cong\mathbb{R}\cdot z\oplus\mathbb{R}^{2n}=\mathfrak{h}_{n}

Define the linear map

\Phi:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{h}_{n}

by sending each
x_{i},y_{i},z
to the standard basis of
\mathfrak{h}_{n}
. This map is clearly bijective and preserves the Lie bracket structure. Hence
\Phi
is a Lie algebra isomorphism.
∎

7.3.5
A Mixed Degenerate Pushout

We conclude with an example that combines non-isomorphic algebras. Let
\mathbb{C}
denote the field of complex numbers, viewed as a unital real algebra, and let
\mathcal{J}\mathrm{Spin}_{1}=\mathbb{R}\oplus\mathbb{R}v
be the 2-dimensional spin factor Jordan algebra. Both algebras share the common scalar unit
1\in\mathbb{R}
, and we consider their degenerate pushout

\mathbb{J}:=\mathbb{C}\odot_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{J}\mathrm{Spin}_{1}.

As a real vector space,

\mathbb{J}=\mathbb{R}\cdot 1\;\oplus\;\mathbb{R}\cdot i\;\oplus\;\mathbb{R}\cdot v\;\cong\;\mathbb{R}\oplus\mathbb{R}^{2},

with cross-terms between
i
and
v
annihilated. The multiplication is given by:

\displaystyle i\circ i

\displaystyle=-1,

\displaystyle\qquad i\circ v

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\qquad v\circ v

\displaystyle=1,

\displaystyle i\circ 1

\displaystyle=i,

\displaystyle\qquad v\circ 1

\displaystyle=v,

\displaystyle\qquad 1\circ 1

\displaystyle=1.

Proposition 7.8
.

The degenerate pushout
\mathbb{J}
of
\mathbb{C}
and
\mathcal{J}\mathrm{Spin}_{1}
over
\mathbb{R}
is
commutative, non-associative simple Jordan algebra.

Proof.

Commutativity of
\mathbb{J}
is straightforward from the above structure constants. For the non-associativity, we have
(i\circ i)\circ v\not=i\circ(i\circ v)
.
Moreover the algebra is a Jordan algebra by the property of degenerate pushouts given in Section
7.4
. Although the simplicity follows from Proposition
7.12
below, we give a direct proof, which uses a different idea.

Let
0\not=I\subseteq\mathbb{J}
be an ideal. Also let
0\not=a\in I
. Suppose
a=a_{1}+a_{2}i+a_{3}v
. Then if
a_{1}a_{2}\not=0
, we have

v\circ(v\circ(i\circ a))=-a_{2}{\bf 1}\in I.

Since
a_{2}\neq 0
is a unit, we get
I=\mathbb{J}
. This equality holds in a similar way when
a_{1}a_{3}\not=0
. Finally, if
a_{1}=0
, then
a_{2}
or
a_{3}
is non-zero. In this case, either
i\circ a
or
v\circ a
is a scalar and we again obtain
I=\mathbb{J}
. Hence
\mathbb{J}
is simple.
∎

Remark 7.9
.

By the classification of three dimensional Jordan algebras given in
[
16
]
, the algebra
\mathbb{J}
is identified by
\mathcal{J}_{3}
.

7.4
Structural Properties Preserved by Degenerate Pushout

The examples discussed above suggest that degenerate pushouts retain certain structural features of their components, while breaking others in a controlled fashion. In this section, we extract general patterns and clarify which algebraic properties are preserved, which are not, and under what conditions.

Let
A=A_{1}\odot_{Z}A_{2}\odot_{Z}\cdots\odot_{Z}A_{k}
be a degenerate pushout of unital
\mathbb{R}
-algebras
A_{i}
and unital embeddings
f_{i}\colon Z\hookrightarrow A_{i}
. The following properties are preserved under the degenerate pushout construction.

Unitality and Commutativity

If each
A_{i}
is unital and all embeddings preserve the unit, then
A
is unital with common unit inherited from
Z
. Similarly if each
A_{i}
is commutative, then so is
A
. This follows from bilinearity and the vanishing of cross-copy terms. We leave the formal proof as an exercise.

Proposition 7.10

(Nilpotency for Lie algebras.)
.

Suppose the algebra
A_{i}
is finite-dimensional
s_{i}
-step nilpotent Lie algebras for each
i=1,\ldots,k
and for some
s_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
. Then
A
is an
s
-step nilpotent Lie algebra where
s=\max_{i}(s_{i})
.

Proof.

Since
A
is constructed by identifying the common subalgebra
Z\subseteq A_{i}
, and all cross-brackets between different
A_{i}
vanish by definition, the lower central series of
A
decomposes component-wise.
That is, for any elements
x_{1},\dots,x_{r}\in A
, their nested Lie bracket

[x_{1},[x_{2},[\dots[x_{r-1},x_{r}]\dots]]]

vanishes if any two
x_{i}
,
x_{j}
come from distinct components
A_{p}
,
A_{q}
with
p\neq q
, because then the innermost cross-bracket is zero.
So nonzero nested brackets may only involve elements from a single
A_{i}
. But each
A_{i}
is nilpotent of class at most
s
, so any such nested bracket of length
>s
vanishes.
Hence for all
r>s
, all
r
-fold brackets in
A
vanish, and
A
is nilpotent of class at most
s
. Since at least one of the algebras is
s
-step nilpotent, the algebra
A
is also
s
-step nilpotent.
∎

Proposition 7.11

(Jordan Identity.)
.

Suppose the algebras
A_{i}
are Jordan algebras along a common unital Jordan subalgebra
Z
. Then the resulting algebra
A
is a Jordan algebra.

Proof.

Let
\circ
denote the Jordan product on each
A_{i}
, extended to
A
by the degenerate pushout construction, so that for all
i\neq j
, we have

a_{i}\circ b_{j}=0\quad\text{for }a_{i}\in A_{i}\setminus Z,\,b_{j}\in A_{j}\setminus Z
.
Take arbitrary
a,b\in A
, and write

a=\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i},\qquad b=\sum_{i=1}^{k}b_{i},\quad\text{with }a_{i},b_{i}\in A_{i}.

We compute both sides of the Jordan identity

(a\circ b)\circ(a\circ a)=a\circ(b\circ(a\circ a)).

Since all cross terms between different
A_{i}
vanish, we have

a\circ b=\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}\circ b_{i},

and likewise for
a\circ a
. Therefore, all higher-order terms arising in the identity lie within each individual component
A_{i}
, and vanish between components.
But since each
A_{i}
is a Jordan algebra, the Jordan identity holds inside each
A_{i}
. Therefore, it also holds in the whole algebra
A
.
Hence
A
satisfies the Jordan identity.
∎

Proposition 7.12

(Simplicity)
.

Let
A
be a unital real algebra, and let
B:=A\odot_{\mathbb{R}}A
denote the degenerate pushout of two copies of
A
along the common unit. Then

B\text{ is simple }\iff\begin{cases}A\text{ is simple},\\
\exists x,y\in A\setminus\mathbb{R}\cdot 1\text{ such that }xy\in\mathbb{R}\cdot 1\setminus\{0\}.\end{cases}

Proof.

Let
A^{(1)}
and
A^{(2)}
denote the two copies of
A
inside
B
, glued along their common unit
1\in\mathbb{R}
, and recall that all mixed products vanish

a^{(1)}b^{(2)}=b^{(2)}a^{(1)}=0\quad\text{for all }a,b\in A\setminus\mathbb{R}\cdot 1.

Suppose
B
is simple. We show that both conditions must hold.
First, suppose
A
is not simple. Then it contains a proper nonzero two-sided ideal
I\subsetneq A
. Let
I^{(1)}:=\{a^{(1)}:a\in I\}\subseteq A^{(1)}\subseteq B
, and let
J:=\langle I^{(1)}\rangle_{B}
be the two-sided ideal of
B
generated by
I^{(1)}
. Since
B
is simple and
I^{(1)}\neq 0
, we must have
J=B
, hence
A^{(1)}\subseteq J
. But due to the vanishing of cross-copy products, all products within
A^{(1)}
stay in
A^{(1)}
. So
J\cap A^{(1)}
is an ideal of
A^{(1)}
containing
I^{(1)}
. It follows that
I^{(1)}=A^{(1)}
, and hence
I=A
, a contradiction. So
A
must be simple.

Next, suppose that no such pair
x,y\in A\setminus\mathbb{R}\cdot 1
satisfies
xy\in\mathbb{R}\cdot 1
. Then for all
x,y\in A\setminus\mathbb{R}\cdot 1
, we have
xy\notin\mathbb{R}\cdot 1
. This implies that any ideal in
B
generated by elements in
A^{(1)}
stays entirely within
A^{(1)}
. Hence
A^{(1)}
is a two-sided ideal, contradicting simplicity. Therefore, such a pair
x,y
must exist.

Conversely, assume that
A
is simple and there exist
x,y\in A\setminus\mathbb{R}\cdot 1
such that
xy=\lambda\cdot 1
for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
.
Let
I\subseteq B
be a nonzero two-sided ideal. Without loss of generality, assume
I\cap A^{(1)}\neq 0
, and choose
a^{(1)}\in I\cap A^{(1)}
, with
a\in A\setminus\{0\}
. Since
A
is simple, the two-sided ideal generated by
a
in
A
is the whole algebra, so the ideal in
B
generated by
a^{(1)}
contains all of
A^{(1)}
, hence
A^{(1)}\subseteq I
.

Now consider the elements
x^{(1)},y^{(1)}\in A^{(1)}\subseteq I
. Their product in
B
is
x^{(1)}y^{(1)}=(xy)^{(1)}=\lambda\cdot 1
. Since the unit
1
lies in both
A^{(1)}
and
A^{(2)}
, and all scalar multiples of
1
lie in both copies, we have
1\in I
. But then for any
b^{(2)}\in A^{(2)}
, we get
b^{(2)}=b^{(2)}\cdot 1\in I
, so
A^{(2)}\subseteq I
as well. Thus,
I=B
, and
B
is simple.
∎

Remark 7.13
.

A similar result holds for mixed products.

Non-associativity

Even when all
A_{i}
are associative, the degenerate pushout may fail to be associative. Indeed suppose there exists
a,b\in A_{1}\setminus Z
such that
ab\in Z\setminus\{0\}
and also let
c\in A_{2}
. Then in
A_{1}\odot_{Z}A_{2}
, we have
a(bc)=0
while
c
may be chosen so that
(ab)c
is non-zero.

8
Vidinli Algebras as Degenerate Pushouts

We now show that the Vidinli algebra
\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}
admits a realization as the degenerate pushout of
n
copies of the basic algebra
\mathbb{V}_{3}
. This construction reflects the internal modularity of
\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}
, in which each orthogonal pair of directions
(e_{2k},e_{2k+1})
forms a subalgebra isomorphic to
\mathbb{V}_{3}
together with the common unit direction
e_{1}
, and all cross-interactions vanish.

8.1
The Case
\mathbb{V}_{5}=\mathbb{V}_{3}\odot\mathbb{V}_{3}

Let

Z:=\mathbb{R}\,e_{1},

A_{1}=\mathrm{Span}_{\mathbb{R}}\{e_{1},e_{2}^{(1)},e_{3}^{(1)}\}\cong\mathbb{V}_{3},

A_{2}=\mathrm{Span}_{\mathbb{R}}\{e_{1},e_{2}^{(2)},e_{3}^{(2)}\}\cong\mathbb{V}_{3},

each equipped with the Vidinli multiplication
\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits
, and with common unit
e_{1}\in Z
. The degenerate pushout is defined by

\mathbb{V}_{3}\odot\mathbb{V}_{3}:=\left(A_{1}*_{Z}A_{2}\right)\Big/\!\left\langle e_{a}^{(1)}e_{b}^{(2)},\;e_{b}^{(2)}e_{a}^{(1)}:a,b\in\{2,3\}\right\rangle,

so that all cross-copy products are zero. A basis for the resulting algebra
A
is

\{e_{1},\;e_{2}^{(1)},e_{3}^{(1)},\;e_{2}^{(2)},e_{3}^{(2)}\},

with multiplication determined by the following rules:

(i)

Unit action:

e_{1}\ast w=w=w\ast e_{1}
for all
w\in A
.

(ii)

Within each copy
i=1,2
:

\displaystyle e_{2}^{(i)}\ast e_{2}^{(i)}

\displaystyle=-e_{1},

\displaystyle\qquad e_{3}^{(i)}\ast e_{3}^{(i)}

\displaystyle=-e_{1},

\displaystyle e_{2}^{(i)}\ast e_{3}^{(i)}

\displaystyle=e_{1},

\displaystyle\qquad e_{3}^{(i)}\ast e_{2}^{(i)}

\displaystyle=-e_{1}.

(iii)

Across copies:
For all
a,b\in\{2,3\}
,

e_{a}^{(1)}\ast e_{b}^{(2)}=0=e_{b}^{(2)}\ast e_{a}^{(1)}.

(iv)

Higher-order products:
All products involving any cross-component term vanish. Only monomials supported entirely in one summand may be nonzero.

Thus
\mathbb{V}_{3}\odot\mathbb{V}_{3}
is a 5-dimensional real algebra in which the two imaginary planes spanned respectively by
e_{2}^{(1)},e_{3}^{(1)}
and by
e_{2}^{(2)},e_{3}^{(2)}
interact only through the shared unit axis. Clearly the map
\mathbb{V}_{3}\odot\mathbb{V}_{3}\to V_{5}
given by extending the correspondence

(e_{1},e_{2}^{(1)},e_{3}^{(1)},e_{2}^{(2)},e_{3}^{(2)})\mapsto(e_{1},e_{2},e_{3},e_{4},e_{5})

defines an isomorphism of real algebras.

8.2
The General Odd Dimensional Case

We now state the general result.

Theorem 8.1
.

For each
n\geq 1
, the Vidinli algebra
\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}
is isomorphic to the degenerate pushout of
n
copies of
\mathbb{V}_{3}
along the common unit:

\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}\cong\mathbb{V}_{3}^{(1)}\odot_{\mathbb{R}e_{1}}\cdots\odot_{\mathbb{R}e_{1}}\mathbb{V}_{3}^{(n)}.

Proof.

Let
\mathbb{V}_{3}^{(k)}
denote the
k
th copy of the 3-dimensional Vidinli algebra, with basis
\{{e}_{1},{e}_{2k},{e}_{2k+1}\}
. We form the degenerate pushout

A:=\mathbb{V}_{3}^{(1)}\odot\mathbb{V}_{3}^{(2)}\odot\cdots\odot\mathbb{V}_{3}^{(n)},

identifying all copies of
{e}_{1}
.

Let
\iota_{k}:\mathbb{V}_{3}^{(k)}\hookrightarrow A
be the canonical embeddings. Define

\phi_{k}:\mathbb{V}_{3}^{(k)}\to\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}\quad\text{by}\quad\phi_{k}({e}_{1})=e_{1},\quad\phi_{k}({e}_{2k})=e_{2k},\quad\phi_{k}({e}_{2k+1})=e_{2k+1}

which are clearly algebra homomorphisms.
Since the images
\phi_{k}(\mathbb{V}_{3}^{(k)})\subset\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}
generate subalgebras sharing only the unit and satisfying the same multiplication rules as
\mathbb{V}_{3}
, the universal property of the degenerate pushout yields a unique homomorphism

\Phi:A\longrightarrow\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}

such that
\Phi\circ\iota_{k}=\phi_{k}
for all
k
.

The map
\Phi
is surjective, as the union of the images spans the standard basis of
\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}
, and since
\dim A=\dim\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}=2n+1
, it is also injective. Hence
\Phi
is an isomorphism.
∎

Remark 8.2
.

The non-standard Vidinli-type algebra
\mathbb{V}_{5}^{\tilde{\omega}}
described in Example
4.15
cannot be realized as a degenerate pushout of two
\mathbb{V}_{3}
subalgebras. Indeed, there is no way to rearrange its basis so as to exhibit a decomposition into 3-dimensional subalgebras whose cross-terms vanish. In contrast to the standard
\mathbb{V}_{5}
, the overlapping support of the symplectic form in
\mathbb{V}_{5}^{\tilde{\omega}}
obstructs such a separation.

8.3
A Combinatorial Realization

The degenerate pushout construction underlying the Vidinli algebras exhibits a particularly geometric form of algebraic restriction: cross terms between components are
annihilated
. This sharply contrasts with the Cayley–Dickson doubling process, where cross terms are preserved and structured via conjugation.

To highlight this distinction, consider the 3-fold degenerate pushout

\mathbb{V}_{3}^{(1)}\odot\mathbb{V}_{3}^{(2)}\odot\mathbb{V}_{3}^{(3)},

where each
\mathbb{V}_{3}^{(i)}
is a 3-dimensional Vidinli algebra sharing a common axis
\mathbb{R}e_{1}

Let
\mathbb{V}_{5}

\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{3},e_{4},e_{5}\}

\omega(e_{2},e_{3})=\omega(e_{4},e_{5})=1

J=\mathrm{Span}_{\mathbb{R}}\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{4}\}.

The degenerate pushout
(\mathbb{U},\ast)=\mathbb{V}_{5}\odot_{J}\mathbb{V}_{5}

\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{3},e_{3}^{\prime},e_{4},e_{5},e_{5}^{\prime}\},

a\ast b
with

a=\sum a_{i}e_{i}+a_{3}^{\prime}e_{3}^{\prime}+a_{5}^{\prime}e_{5}^{\prime},\quad b=\sum b_{i}e_{i}+b_{3}^{\prime}e_{3}^{\prime}+b_{5}^{\prime}e_{5}^{\prime}

\displaystyle a\ast b=

\displaystyle+(a_{1}b_{2}+b_{1}a_{2})e_{2}+(a_{1}b_{3}+b_{1}a_{3})e_{3}+(a_{1}b_{3}^{\prime}+b_{1}a_{3}^{\prime})e_{3}^{\prime}

Remark 8.3
.

There is a rich class of Jordan subalgebras in any Vidinli algebra

, determined by the choice of isotropic subspaces of

These deformations keep the unit and Jordan part, but alter the global symmetry determined by the skew form.

9
Spectral Decomposition of the Left Multiplication Map

We now turn to the left multiplication operators
L_{a}(x)=a\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits x
of the Vidinli algebra
\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}
. Although the product is non-associative, the maps
L_{a}
reveals that the space of zero-divisors coincides precisely with the symplectic hyperplane
V_{0}=e_{1}^{\perp}
, and the invertibility of an element is determined by its projection onto the unit axis. The full spectrum of
L_{a}
admits an explicit factorization, and the resulting eigenspace decomposition shows the alignment of vectors relative to
e_{1}
. These observations allow us to isolate invariant subspaces under left multiplication and lay the groundwork for a spectral analysis of
\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}
in both its module-theoretic and operator-theoretic formulations. We also introduce a conjugation map and determine two sided inverses for non-zero vectors.

9.1
The Left Multiplication Map and zero divisors

We evaluate the characteristic polynomial of the linear map
L_{a}
for any
a\in\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}
. Similar results holds for the right multiplication maps
R_{b}
.

Proposition 9.1
.

Let
a=a_{1}e_{1}+u\in\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}
, where
u\in V_{0}:=e_{1}^{\perp}
. Then

1.

The characteristic polynomial of
L_{a}\colon x\mapsto a\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits x
is

\chi_{L_{a}}(\lambda)=(\lambda-a_{1})^{2n-1}\left((\lambda-a_{1})^{2}+\|u\|^{2}\right).

2.

The determinant of
L_{a}
is

\det(L_{a})=a_{1}^{\,2n-1}\,\|a\|^{2}.

Proof.

The left multiplication map
L_{a}
acts on
\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}=\mathbb{R}\oplus V_{0}
by:

L_{a}({e}_{1})=a,\qquad L_{a}({v})=a_{1}{v}+(\omega_{0}({u},{v})-{u}\cdot{v}){e}_{1}\quad\text{for }{v}\in V_{0}.

Its matrix with respect to this decompostion takes the block form

L_{a}=\begin{pmatrix}a_{1}&{r}\\
{u}&a_{1}I_{2n}\end{pmatrix}

where
r=({r}_{j})_{j}=(\omega_{0}({u},{e}_{j})-u\cdot e_{j})_{j}
is a row matrix.
We evaluate the characteristic polynomial by applying the Schur complement,

\chi_{L_{a}}(\lambda)=\det\begin{pmatrix}a_{1}-\lambda&r\\
u&(a_{1}-\lambda)I\end{pmatrix}=(a_{1}-\lambda)^{2n-1}\left((a_{1}-\lambda)^{2}-r\cdot u\right).

Since
r\cdot u=-\|u\|^{2}
, we obtain the desired formula.

For the second part, we just substitute
\lambda=0
.
∎

As an immediate application of the above result, we characterize the zero divisors in the Vidinli algebra
\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}
.

Proposition 9.2
.

A nonzero element
0\neq a\in\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}
is a zero divisor if and only if
a\in e_{1}^{\perp}
; that is,
a\cdot e_{1}=0
.

Proof.

By the determinant formula,
L_{a}
is singular if and only if
a_{1}=0
or
a=0
. Since
a\neq 0
, the singularity of
L_{a}
corresponds precisely to the condition
a\cdot e_{1}=0
, i.e.,
a\in e_{1}^{\perp}
.
∎

This result shows that the space of zero divisors is geometrically well-structured: it coincides with the codimension-one hyperplane orthogonal to the unit vector
e_{1}
,

\mathrm{ZD}(\mathbb{V}_{2n+1})=e_{1}^{\perp}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2n+1},

and forms a real algebraic subvariety defined by the linear condition
a\cdot e_{1}=0
.

The set of zero-divisors may also be described in purely geometric terms. Consider the set

Z:=\{(a,b)\in\mathbb{V}\times\mathbb{V}\mid a\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits b=0\},

defined by the vanishing of the bilinear product. Then the set of zero-divisors coincides with the projection of
Z
onto either coordinate:

\mathrm{ZD}=\pi_{1}(Z)=\pi_{2}(Z)=V_{0}.

Thus, each zero-divisor appears as the first component of a pair whose product vanishes, and conversely, any element not orthogonal to the unit direction cannot participate in such a zero product. The following lemma describes the zero set
Z
more explicitly.

Lemma 9.3
.

Let
a,b\in V_{0}
be vectors in the orthogonal complement of the unit direction. Then
a\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits b=0
if and only if
\omega(a,b)=a\cdot b
.

Proof.

Since
a,b\in V_{0}
, we have
a\cdot e_{1}=b\cdot e_{1}=0
. By definition of the Vidinli multiplication,

a\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits b=(a\cdot e_{1})b+(b\cdot e_{1})a-(a\cdot b)e_{1}+\omega(a,b)\,e_{1}.

The first two terms vanish, so this simplifies to

a\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits b=(\omega(a,b)-a\cdot b)e_{1}.

Hence
a\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits b=0
if and only if
\omega(a,b)=a\cdot b
.
∎

Remark 9.4
.

The existence of zero divisors prohibits the norm multiplicativity for arbitrary pairs of vectors. However, there are particular cases of pairs for which the norm multiplicativity holds, for example when one of the vectors is the unit vector. In the case of the Vidinli multiplication for three dimensions an explicit expression for the norm of the product is obtained in
[
5
, Proposition 5]
. A similar but slightly more complicated expression can be obtained in 7 dimensions as well.

9.2
Eigenspace Decomposition

By Proposition
9.1
, there is a unique real eigenvalue of
L_{a}
and a unique degree two irreducible factor of its characteristic polynomial. We next describe the structure of the two eigenspaces determined by this factorization of
\chi_{L_{a}}
.

The Eigenspace for
a_{1}

We write
a=a_{1}e_{1}+a_{2}e_{2}+\ldots+a_{2n+1}e_{2n+1}\in\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}
. If
a_{1}=0
, then the eigenspace is the kernel of
L_{a}
. We assume
a_{1}\neq 0
. On the other hand if
a=a_{1}e_{1}
, then
L_{a}
is multiplication by
a_{1}
so the kernel is everything. Suppose
a\not\in\mathbb{R}e_{1}
.
Let
v=\sum_{i=1}^{2n+1}v_{i}e_{i}\in\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}
. The eigenvalue equation is

a\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits v=a_{1}v.

By the definition of the Vidinli multiplication, we can write

\displaystyle a\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits v

\displaystyle=(a\cdot e_{1})v+(v\cdot e_{1})a-(a\cdot v)e_{1}+\omega(a,v)e_{1}

\displaystyle=a_{1}v+v_{1}a-(a\cdot v)e_{1}+\omega(a,v)e_{1}.

Subtracting
a_{1}v
from both sides, we obtain

a\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits v-a_{1}v=v_{1}a-(a\cdot v)e_{1}+\omega(a,v)e_{1}.

We want the left-hand side to be zero.
Since
a
is not parallel to
e_{1}
, the equation is satisfied only when
v_{1}=0
. Suppose this is the case. Then we must have

a\cdot v=\omega(a,v)
.
Recall that

a\cdot v=\sum_{i=2}^{2n+1}a_{i}v_{i}\quad\text{ and }\quad\omega(a,v)\;=\;\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left(a_{2k}b_{2k+1}-a_{2k+1}b_{2k}\right)

in the standard symplectic basis. Thus we must have

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left[(a_{2k}+a_{2k+1})v_{2k}+(-a_{2k}+a_{2k+1})v_{2k+1}\right]=0

This equation defines a hyperplane in
\mathbb{R}^{2n}
, i.e., a subspace of codimension 1 and hence of dimension
2n-1
.
To exhibit a basis, assume without loss of generality that
a_{2}+a_{3}\neq 0
, and solve for
v_{2}
in terms of the remaining coordinates
v_{3},v_{4},\dots,v_{2n+1}
. The solution is

v_{2}=\frac{a_{2}-a_{3}}{a_{2}+a_{3}}v_{3}+\sum_{k=2}^{n}\left[\frac{-a_{2k}-a_{2k+1}}{a_{2}+a_{3}}\,v_{2k}+\frac{a_{2k}-a_{2k+1}}{a_{2}+a_{3}}\,v_{2k+1}\right].

Now a basis can be obtained by assigning
v_{i}=1
for each
i\in\{3,\dots,2n+1\}
and others 0, and computing the corresponding
v_{2}
.
This completely determines the eigenspace of
L_{a}-a_{1}
, since the dimension of the kernel is equal to the algebraic multiplicity of the term in the characteristic polynomial.

The Quadratic Part.

Let

q(x)=x^{2}-2a_{1}x+(a_{1}^{2}+\|u\|^{2})

where
u=(a_{2},a_{3},\ldots,a_{2n+1})
.
Define
E_{2}:=\ker(q(L_{a}))
. By comparing the dimensions, we know that
E_{2}
must have dimension 2. Notice that
e_{1}
is not in
E_{a_{1}}
. So it must be in
E_{2}
. Indeed,

\displaystyle q(L_{a})(e_{1})

\displaystyle=L_{a}^{2}(e_{1})-2a_{1}L_{a}(e_{1})+(a_{1}^{2}+\|u\|^{2})e_{1}

\displaystyle=a\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits a-2a_{1}a+(a_{1}^{2}+\|u\|^{2})e_{1}

\displaystyle=2a_{1}a-(a\cdot a)e_{1}-2a_{1}a+(a_{1}^{2}+\|u\|^{2})e_{1}=0

We claim that the other generator is
u=a-a_{1}e_{1}
.

\displaystyle q(L_{a})(u)

\displaystyle=q(L_{a})(a)-a_{1}q(L_{a})(e_{1})=L_{a}^{2}(a)-2a_{1}L_{a}(a)+\|a\|^{2}a

\displaystyle=L_{a}(a\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits a)-2a_{1}L_{a}(a)+\|a\|^{2}a

\displaystyle=L_{a}(2a_{1}a-\|a\|^{2}e_{1})-2a_{1}L_{a}(a)+\|a\|^{2}a

\displaystyle=\big[2a_{1}L_{a}(a)-\|a\|^{2}L_{a}(e_{1})\big]-2a_{1}L_{a}(a)+\|a\|^{2}a=0

Hence we proved the following theorem.

Theorem 9.5
.

Let
a=a_{1}e_{1}+u\in\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}
. Then the left multiplication operator
L_{a}
decomposes
\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}
as a direct sum of invariant subspaces:

\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}=E_{a_{1}}\oplus E_{2},

where

E_{a_{1}}:=\ker(L_{a}-a_{1}I)=\{v\in\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}\mid v_{1}=0,\ a\cdot v=\omega(a,v)\}

has dimension
2n-1
, and

E_{2}:=\ker(q(L_{a}))=\mathrm{Span}_{\mathbb{R}}(e_{1},u).

Remark 9.6
.

There is no division operator for Vidinli multiplication even in the original setting. Vidinli
reverse multiplication as described in
[
5
, Section 3.4]
has a natural generalization to odd dimensional spaces. Here however the eigenspace corresponding to the eigenvalue
a_{1}
is not one dimensional but
2n-1
dimensional. For the sake of argument let us assume that
a_{1}
is not zero (this restriction can be removed). Then the equation can be projected to the eigenspace and the quadratic space
E_{2}
, that is a two dimensional principal plane in a disjoint way. The equation on the eigenspace is reduced to multiplication by
a_{1}
and can be solved directly by dividing with the non-zero eigenvalue. The remaining quadratic part can be handled as in the case of the complex plane. Solving linear equations by inverting the left multiplication operator, which is invertible since
a_{1}
is nonzero, maybe a more direct approach to this problem.

9.3
Conjugation Symmetry and Inner Product Identity

The non-associative structure of the Vidinli algebra
\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}=\mathbb{R}e_{1}\oplus V_{0}
admits a distinguished involution that reflects vectors across the unit direction
e_{1}
. This operation behaves analogously to conjugation in the complex numbers or quaternions, but is made to match the directional asymmetry of the Vidinli multiplication.

Definition 9.7
.

For any
a\in\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}
, define its
e_{1}
-conjugate
by

\bar{a}:={}^{e_{1}}a:=2(a\cdot e_{1})\,e_{1}-a.

This is the reflection of
a
across the line
\mathbb{R}e_{1}
. It satisfies

•

\bar{a}=a
if and only if
a\in\mathbb{R}e_{1}
;

•

\bar{a}=-a
if and only if
a\in V_{0}
;

•

\bar{\bar{a}}=a
for all
a\in\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}
, that is,
\bar{\cdot}
is an involution;

•

\|\bar{a}\|=\|a\|
, since it preserves the Euclidean norm.

The conjugation map
a\mapsto\bar{a}
is a linear, isometric involution of
\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}
, and preserves the decomposition

\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}=\mathbb{R}e_{1}\oplus V_{0}

by fixing the axis and negating its orthogonal complement.

Proposition 9.8
.

For all
a,b\in\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}
, the following identity holds

\bar{a}\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits b+\bar{b}\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits a=2(a\cdot b)\,e_{1}.

Proof.

We write
\bar{a}=2(a\cdot e_{1})e_{1}-a
, and similarly for
\bar{b}
. Using linearity of the product and the fact that
e_{1}
is the unit element, we compute

\displaystyle\bar{a}\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits b+\bar{b}\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits a

\displaystyle=\left(2(a\cdot e_{1})e_{1}-a\right)\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits b+\left(2(b\cdot e_{1})e_{1}-b\right)\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits a

\displaystyle=2(a\cdot e_{1})(e_{1}\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits b)-a\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits b+2(b\cdot e_{1})(e_{1}\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits a)-b\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits a.

Since
e_{1}
is the unit, we simplify

\bar{a}\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits b+\bar{b}\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits a=2(a\cdot e_{1})\,b+2(b\cdot e_{1})\,a-(a\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits b+b\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits a).

Recall that the symmetric part of the Vidinli multiplication is

a\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits b+b\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits a=2(a\cdot e_{1})\,b+2(b\cdot e_{1})\,a-2(a\cdot b)\,e_{1}.

Subtracting, we obtain the result

\bar{a}\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits b+\bar{b}\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits a=2(a\cdot b)\,e_{1}.\qed

This identity reveals that the
e_{1}
-conjugation map together with the Vidinli multiplication recovers the standard inner product, but in a scalar multiple of the distinguished direction.

Remark 9.9
.

This identity generalizes the classical relation
a\ \bar{b}+b\ \bar{a}=2(a\cdot b)
in the complex and quaternionic settings, highlighting that although
\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}
is neither associative nor normed, it retains an inner structure compatible with reflectional symmetries.

As another application of conjugation map, we can construct inverses of elements.

Proposition 9.10
.

Let
\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}=(\mathbb{R}^{2n+1},\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits)
be the Vidinli algebra with unit
e_{1}
. For all
a\in\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}
,

\overline{a}\,\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits\,a\;=\;a\,\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits\,\overline{a}\;=\;\|a\|^{2}\,e_{1},

where
\|a\|^{2}=a\cdot a
is the standard Euclidean norm on
\mathbb{R}^{2n+1}
. In particular, every nonzero
a
is two‑sided invertible, with

a^{-1}=\frac{1}{\|a\|^{2}}\,\overline{a}.

Proof.

Write
a=a_{1}e_{1}+u
and
\overline{a}=a_{1}e_{1}-u
. Expanding the Vidinli multiplication and using orthogonality of
e_{1}
and
u
together with the fact that the skew-symmetric term changes sign when
u\leftrightarrow v
, one checks

\overline{a}\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits a=(a_{1}^{2}+\|u\|^{2})\,e_{1}=\|a\|^{2}\,e_{1},

and similarly
a\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits\overline{a}=\|a\|^{2}e_{1}
. Dividing by
\|a\|^{2}\neq 0
yields the stated inverse.
∎

10
Modules over Vidinli Algebras

In this section, we examine the algebra
\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}
as a module over its multiplication algebra and identify it as an Azumaya algebra in the sense of non-associative theory developed by Wisbauer
[
35
]
. See Schafer
[
27
]
also. We begin by computing the multiplication algebra
\mathrm{M}(\mathbb{V}_{2n+1})
, show that it is isomorphic to the full matrix algebra
M_{2n+1}(\mathbb{R})
, and deduce that
\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}
is a simple generator in the category of
\mathrm{M}(\mathbb{V}_{2n+1})
-modules. We also determine its centroid and verify the Azumaya property.

10.1
The Multiplication Algebra

Let
A
be an
\mathbb{R}
-algebra. The
multiplication algebra
of
A
, denoted
\mathrm{M}(A)
, is the unital subalgebra of
\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{R}}(A)
generated by all left and right multiplication operators

\mathrm{M}(A):=\left\langle L_{a},R_{a}\;\middle|\;a\in A\right\rangle\subseteq\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{R}}(A).

The main result of this section is the following identification of
\mathrm{M}(\mathbb{V}_{2n+1})
.

Theorem 10.1
.

For each
n\geq 1
, the multiplication algebra of
\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}
is the full matrix algebra

\mathrm{M}(\mathbb{V}_{2n+1})\cong M_{2n+1}(\mathbb{R}).

To prove the theorem, we begin by verifying the base case
n=1
through a direct computation. This case corresponds to the 3-dimensional Vidinli algebra
\mathbb{V}_{3}
, where we explicitly construct the left and right multiplication operators and show that they generate the full matrix algebra
M_{3}(\mathbb{R})
. Having established this foundational case, we then deduce the general result by exploiting the structural property that
\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}
is a degenerate pushout of
n
canonical copies of
\mathbb{V}_{3}
. In particular, we show that the multiplication algebra of
\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}
is generated by the images of these subalgebras.

Lemma 10.2
.

Theorem
10.1
holds when
n=1
.

Proof.

Let
\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}\}
be the standard basis of
\mathbb{V}_{3}
, with
e_{1}
the unit. One verifies by explicit computation that the left and right multiplication operators
L_{e_{2}}
,
L_{e_{3}}
,
R_{e_{2}}
, and
R_{e_{3}}
have the following matrices in this basis

L_{e_{2}}=\begin{bmatrix}0&-1&1\\
1&0&0\\
0&0&0\end{bmatrix},\ L_{e_{3}}=\begin{bmatrix}0&-1&-1\\
0&0&0\\
1&0&0\end{bmatrix},

\ R_{e_{2}}=\begin{bmatrix}0&-1&-1\\
1&0&0\\
0&0&0\end{bmatrix},\ R_{e_{3}}=\begin{bmatrix}0&1&-1\\
0&0&0\\
1&0&0\end{bmatrix}.

The matrix algebra
M_{3}(\mathbb{R})
is generated by the matrix units
E_{12},E_{13},E_{21},E_{31}
as a real algebra. Hence, it suffices to show that these matrix units lie in the subalgebra generated by the operators above. We calculate these units as

E_{12}=-\frac{1}{2}(L_{e_{3}}-R_{e_{3}}),\quad E_{13}=\frac{1}{2}(L_{e_{2}}-R_{e_{2}}),

E_{21}=L_{e_{2}}+E_{12}-E_{13},\quad E_{31}=L_{e_{3}}+E_{12}+E_{13}.

Thus, the multiplication algebra

\mathrm{M}(\mathbb{V}_{3})=\langle L_{a},R_{b}\mid a,b\in\mathbb{V}_{3}\rangle

contains all matrix units and hence coincides with
M_{3}(\mathbb{R})
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
10.1
.

We view
\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}
as the degenerate pushout of
n
canonical copies of the 3-dimensional Vidinli algebra
\mathbb{V}_{3}
. Each such copy is given by the 3-dimensional subspace spanned by
\{e_{1},e_{2i},e_{2i+1}\}
, for
i=1,\dots,n
. Let us denote this subalgebra by
\mathbb{V}_{3}^{(i)}\subseteq\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}
. By the previous lemma, we know that there is an isomorphism

\mathrm{M}(\mathbb{V}_{3}^{(i)})\cong M_{3}(\mathbb{R}).

Since each
\mathbb{V}_{3}^{(i)}
acts non-trivially only on the coordinates
\{e_{1},e_{2i},e_{2i+1}\}
, the algebra
\mathrm{M}(\mathbb{V}_{3}^{(i)})
is naturally embedded as a copy of
M_{3}(\mathbb{R})
inside the diagonal block of
M_{2n+1}(\mathbb{R})
corresponding to those coordinates.

Let
B_{i}\subseteq M_{2n+1}(\mathbb{R})
denote the image of
\mathrm{M}(\mathbb{V}_{3}^{(i)})
under this embedding. Then
B_{i}
is the subalgebra of
M_{2n+1}(\mathbb{R})
supported on rows and columns
\{1,2i,2i+1\}
, and we have

B_{i}\cong M_{3}(\mathbb{R})\subseteq M_{2n+1}(\mathbb{R}).

Since the index sets
\{1,2i,2i+1\}
for
i=1,\dots,n
all contain the common coordinate
1
, the corresponding subalgebras
B_{i}
are pairwise linked via the shared action on the first basis vector. Consequently, products of elements from different
B_{i}
generate matrix units supported outside the diagonal blocks. For example,

E_{2i,1}\in B_{i}
and
E_{1,2j}\in B_{j}

multiply to

E_{2i,2j}

giving an off-block unit. Other cases for the pairs
(2i+1,2j+1),(2i,2j+1)
and
(2i,2j+1)
can be treated similarly. It follows that the subalgebra generated by the collection
\{B_{1},\dots,B_{n}\}
contains all matrix units
E_{ij}
, and hence

\mathrm{M}(\mathbb{V}_{2n+1})=\left\langle\mathrm{M}(\mathbb{V}_{3}^{(1)}),\dots,\mathrm{M}(\mathbb{V}_{3}^{(n)})\right\rangle=M_{2n+1}(\mathbb{R}).

∎

10.2
The Category
\sigma[\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}]

The Vidinli algebra
\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}
can be made a (left) module over the multiplication algebra
\mathrm{M}(\mathbb{V}_{2n+1})
. Then the
\mathrm{M}(\mathbb{V}_{2n+1})
-submodules of
\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}
correspond to two-sided ideals of
\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}
. Since
\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}
is a simple algebra, we conclude that it is a simple module over
\mathrm{M}(\mathbb{V}_{2n+1})
. We record this property.

Proposition 10.3
.

The Vidinli algebra
\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}
regarded as a module over its multiplication algebra
\mathrm{M}(\mathbb{V}_{2n+1})
is simple and hence isomorphic to the unique simple module
\mathbb{R}^{2n+1}
of
\mathrm{M}(\mathbb{V}_{2n+1})
.

Although we may consider bimodules over a (not necessarily associative) algebra
A
over a commutative ring
R
, in
[
35
]
, Wisbauer introduced a subcategory of
\mathrm{M}(A)
-modules associated to
A
which he identifies as ‘small enough to be closely related to
A
, large enough to provide efficient techniques from category theory’. This category, denoted by
\sigma[A]
, is the full subcategory of
\mathrm{M}(A)
-mod consisting of
A
-generated modules. Here, an
\mathrm{M}(A)
-module
N
is called
A
-generated
if it is a submodule of a homomorphic image of direct sums of
A
. We refer to
[
35
, Chapter 7]
for further properties of
\sigma[A]
. It turns out that the category for
\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}
is as large as possible.

Proposition 10.4
.

Any
\mathrm{M}(\mathbb{V}_{2n+1})
-module is
\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}
-generated. Hence

\sigma[\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}]=\mathrm{M}(\mathbb{V}_{2n+1})\mathrm{-Mod}.

In particular the category
\sigma[\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}]
is semi-simple and is Morita equivalent to the category of real vector spaces.

Proof.

It suffices to prove the first claim.
Since by Theorem
10.1
, the multiplication algebra is the full matrix algebra, it is semi-simple. Also by Proposition
10.3
, the Vidinli module
\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}
is the unique simple module of
\mathrm{M}(\mathbb{V}_{2n+1})
, hence any
\mathrm{M}(\mathbb{V}_{2n+1})
-module is
\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}
-generated.
∎

10.3
Azumaya Property

A non-associative generalization of Azumaya algebras is given by Wisbauer in
[
35
]
. Let
A
be an
F
-algebra over a field
F
. Recall that the
centroid
of
A
, denoted
\mathrm{Cent}(A)
, is the set of all linear endomorphisms
\theta\in\mathrm{End}_{F}(A)
satisfying

\theta(x\ast y)=\theta(x)\ast y=x\ast\theta(y)\quad\text{for all }x,y\in A.

It forms a commutative subalgebra of
\mathrm{End}_{F}(A)
. We call
A
an
F
-central algebra
if its centroid is equal to
F
.
Finally we call the algebra
A
an
Azumaya algebra
if it is
F
-central and
\sigma[A]=\mathrm{M}(A)
-Mod. With this definition we now prove that
\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}
is an Azumaya algebra.

Theorem 10.5
.

The Vidinli algebra
\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}
is an Azumaya algebra over
\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

We need to prove that

1.

Its centroid is
\mathbb{R}\cdot\mathrm{id}
and

2.

it is a generator in the category of left
\mathrm{M}(\mathbb{V}_{2n+1})
-modules.

Part (2) is just Proposition
10.4
.
For Part (1), let
\theta\in\mathrm{Cent}(\mathbb{V}_{2n+1})
. Then
\theta
commutes with all left and right multiplication operators:

\theta\circ L_{a}=L_{a}\circ\theta,\quad\theta\circ R_{a}=R_{a}\circ\theta\quad\text{for all }a\in\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}.

This means that
\theta\in\mathrm{End}_{\mathrm{M}(\mathbb{V}_{2n+1})}(\mathbb{V}_{2n+1})
, that is,
\theta
is an endomorphism of
\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}
as a left
\mathrm{M}(\mathbb{V}_{2n+1})
-module. Since
\mathrm{M}(\mathbb{V}_{2n+1})\cong M_{2n+1}(\mathbb{R})
and
\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}\cong\mathbb{R}^{2n+1}
is a simple
M_{2n+1}(\mathbb{R})
-module, Schur’s lemma implies that the only such endomorphisms are scalar multiples of the identity. Hence
\theta=\lambda\cdot\mathrm{id}
for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, proving the claim.
∎

11
Subalgebras of the Vidinli algebras

11.1
2-dimensional subalgebras

Following our notation from 3-dimensional Vidinli algebra, we call a plane containing
e_{1}
a principal plane. If
u\in\mathbb{R}^{2n+1}
is a unit vector not equal to
\pm e_{1}
, we write
\Pi_{u}
for the span of
\{e_{1},u\}
.

Theorem 11.1
.

Let
\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}
be the Vidinli algebra with unit
e_{1}
and hyperplane
V_{0}=e_{1}^{\perp}
. Fix a unit vector

u\;\in\;\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}\setminus\mathbb{R}e_{1}
.
Then the principal plane
\Pi_{u}
is closed under
\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits
, and

(\Pi_{u},\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits)\;\cong\;\mathbb{C}

as real algebras. Equivalently, writing

u\;=\;\alpha\,e_{1}\;+\;u_{0}
for
u_{0}\in V_{0}
,
and setting
a=a_{1}e_{1}+a_{2}u
,
b=b_{1}e_{1}+b_{2}u
, then

a\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits b=\bigl(a_{1}b_{1}-a_{2}b_{2}\bigr)e_{1}+\bigl(a_{1}b_{2}+a_{2}b_{1}+2\alpha\,a_{2}b_{2}\bigr)u.

Moreover the change of basis

\varphi(e_{1})=1,\,\varphi(u)=\eta

identifies
\Pi_{u}
with
\mathbb{C}
. Here
\eta
satisfies the quadratic equation
\eta^{2}=-1+2\alpha\,\eta
whose discriminant is
\Delta=4(\alpha^{2}-1)<0
.

Proof.

Let
u\in\mathbb{R}^{2n+1}
be a unit vector, and write
\alpha:=u\cdot e_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
. Any vector in
\Pi_{u}
can be written as
a=a_{1}e_{1}+a_{2}u
and
b=b_{1}e_{1}+b_{2}u
. Using the definition of
\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits
, we compute:

a\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits b=(a_{1}b_{1}-a_{2}b_{2})e_{1}+(a_{1}b_{2}+a_{2}b_{1}+2\alpha a_{2}b_{2})u.

Now define a linear map
\varphi:\Pi_{u}\to\mathbb{C}
by
\varphi(e_{1})=1
,
\varphi(u)=\eta\in\mathbb{C}
. Then
\varphi(a)=a_{1}+a_{2}\eta
, and
\varphi(b)=b_{1}+b_{2}\eta
, so their product is:

\varphi(a)\varphi(b)=a_{1}b_{1}+(a_{1}b_{2}+a_{2}b_{1})\eta+a_{2}b_{2}\eta^{2}.

In order for this to equal
\varphi(a\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits b)
, we require

\eta^{2}=-1+2\alpha\eta
.
The discriminant
\Delta=4(\alpha^{2}-1)
is negative whenever
|\alpha|<1
,
so
\eta\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{R}
. Thus, for any
u\notin\mathbb{R}e_{1}
, the map
\varphi
defines an algebra isomorphism between
(\Pi_{u},\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits)
and
\mathbb{C}
.
∎

Remark 11.2
.

When
u\perp e_{1}
, i.e.
\alpha=0
, the formula simplifies to:

a\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits b=(a_{1}b_{1}-a_{2}b_{2})\,e_{1}+(a_{1}b_{2}+a_{2}b_{1})\,u,

which is the standard complex multiplication under the identification
e_{1}\mapsto 1
,
u\mapsto i
.

11.2
Vidinli subalgebras

Next, we consider the
3
-dimensional subspaces of
\mathbb{R}^{2n+1}
containing the unit
e_{1}
. We see that not all
3
-planes
W\ni e_{1}
inheriting a unital product from
\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}
is isomorphic to the
3
-dimensional Vidinli algebra
\mathbb{V}_{3}
. The following theorem characterizes exactly when this is so.

Theorem 11.3
.

Let
\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}
be the
(2n+1)
-dimensional Vidinli algebra. Let
W\subset\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}

be any
3
-dimensional subspace with
e_{1}\in W
. Then the restriction

(W,\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits)

is isomorphic to the
3
-dimensional Vidinli algebra
\mathbb{V}_{3}
if and only if there exist orthonormal vectors

u,v\;\in\;W\cap V_{0}\quad\text{such that}\quad\omega(u,v)=\pm 1.

Proof.

Let
w_{1},w_{2}\in W
. Then
w_{1}\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits w_{2}\in\mathrm{Span}_{\mathbb{R}}\{e_{1},w_{1},w_{2}\}\subseteq W
, that is,
W
is closed under
\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits
. To prove the theorem, first suppose
(W,\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits)\cong\mathbb{V}_{3}
. Choose a basis
\{e_{1},u,v\}
realizing the standard three‐dimensional multiplication table

u\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits v=e_{1},\quad u\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits u=v\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits v=-\,e_{1},\quad v\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits u=-\,e_{1}.

Then since
u\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits u=-\|u\|^{2}e_{1}
we get
\|u\|=1
, and similarly
\|v\|=1
. Also we have

u\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits v=-\,(u\cdot v)e_{1}+\omega(u,v)e_{1}=e_{1}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad-(u\cdot v)+\omega(u,v)=1,

and

v\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits u=-\,(u\cdot v)e_{1}-\omega(u,v)e_{1}=-e_{1}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad-(u\cdot v)-\omega(u,v)=-1.

Solution of this system is
u\cdot v=0
and
\omega(u,v)=1
. We allow
\omega(u,v)=-1
for a possible reordering of the basis.

Conversely, if
u,v\in W\cap V_{0}
are orthonormal with
\omega(u,v)=\pm 1
, then renaming
v\mapsto-v
if necessary gives

u\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits v=e_{1},\quad u\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits u=-e_{1},\quad v\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits v=-e_{1},\quad v\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits u=-e_{1}.

Thus
\{e_{1},u,v\}
reproduces exactly the multiplication table of
\mathbb{V}_{3}
, showing
(W,\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits)\cong\mathbb{V}_{3}
.
∎

Theorem
11.3
characterizes all
3
–dimensional Vidinli subalgebras in
\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}
as those generated by
e_{1}
and an orthonormal pair
(u,v)
in
V_{0}
with
\omega(u,v)=\pm 1
.
It is natural to ask whether, once
e_{1}
and a single direction
u\in V_{0}
are fixed, the choice of
v
is unique.
The next proposition confirms that this is indeed the case.

Definition 11.4
.

Let
V_{0}=e_{1}^{\perp}
with the fixed symplectic form
\omega
. Define the linear map

J:V_{0}\to V_{0}
by

J(e_{2k})=e_{2k+1},\qquad J(e_{2k+1})=-\,e_{2k}\qquad(1\leq k\leq n).

Then
J
is orthogonal,
J^{T}=-J
, and
J^{2}=-I_{V_{0}}
. In particular,

\omega(u,v)=(Ju)\cdot v\qquad\text{for all }u,v\in V_{0}.

Proposition 11.5
.

Fix a unit
u\in V_{0}
. Set
w:=Ju
. Then
u\cdot w=0
,
\|w\|=1
, and for any unit

v\in V_{0}
with
u\cdot v=0
we have
\omega(u,v)=w\cdot v\leq 1
, with equality iff
v=\pm w
.
Consequently, there is a unique
3
–plane through
e_{1}
and
u
, namely
\mathrm{Span}_{\mathbb{R}}\{e_{1},u,Ju\}
, closed under
\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits
and which is isomorphic to
\mathbb{V}_{3}
as real algebras.

Proof.

Since
J
is orthogonal and skew,
\|Ju\|=\|u\|=1
and
u\cdot Ju=0
.
For any unit
v\perp u
, by Cauchy–Schwarz,

\omega(u,v)=(Ju)\cdot v\leq\|Ju\|\,\|v\|=1,

with equality if and only if
v
is parallel to
Ju
, i.e.
v=\pm Ju
. By Theorem
11.3
,

\mathrm{Span}_{\mathbb{R}}\{e_{1},u,v\}\cong\mathbb{V}_{3}

if and only if
\omega(u,v)=\pm 1
, hence
v=\pm Ju
is the only possibility.
∎

Corollary 11.6
.

With the above conventions, any standard basis vector
u=e_{2i}\in V_{0}
has a canonical symplectic dual
v:=e_{2i+1}
satisfying

u\cdot v=0,\qquad\omega(u,v)=1,\qquad\|u\|=\|v\|=1.

As a consequence, the subspace

W_{i}:=\mathrm{Span}_{\mathbb{R}}\{e_{1},e_{2i},e_{2i+1}\}\;\subset\;\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}

is an explicit copy of the 3-dimensional Vidinli algebra
\mathbb{V}_{3}
.

As another corollary, we obtain the following decomposition of the Vidinli algebra.

Corollary 11.7
.

Let
\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}
be the Vidinli algebra with distinguished unit direction
e
, and let
V_{0}=e^{\perp}
with the standard symplectic form
\omega
. Let
W_{i}
be as above. Then

\mathbb{R}^{2n+1}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}W_{i}.

The Vidinli algebra
\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}
contains copies of the Vidinli algebras of all smaller dimensions. The following result determines such subalgebras.

Proposition 11.8
.

Let
\Upsilon=\{u_{1},v_{1},u_{2},v_{2},\ldots,u_{k},v_{k}\}
be a linearly independent set of unit vectors in
V_{0}
such that

1.

u_{i}\cdot v_{i}=0,\omega(u_{i},v_{i})=1
for all
i=1,\ldots,k
.

2.

u_{i}\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits u_{j}=u_{i}\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits v_{j}=v_{i}\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits u_{j}=v_{i}\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits v_{j}=0
.

Let
W_{u_{i},v_{i}}
be the Vidinli algebra on the basis
\{e_{1},u_{i},v_{i}\}
. Then

W_{u_{1},v_{1}}\odot W_{u_{2},v_{2}}\odot\ldots\odot W_{u_{k},v_{k}}\cong\mathbb{V}_{2k+1}

and the inclusion maps
W_{u_{i},v_{i}}\to\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}
induces an injective algebra homomorphism
\mathbb{V}_{2k+1}\to\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}
.

Proof.

By Theorem
11.3
, each subspace
W_{u_{i},v_{i}}
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{V}_{3}
as a real algebra, for
i=1,\dots,k
. Hence, their degenerate pushout

\mathbb{V}_{\Upsilon}=W_{u_{1},v_{1}}\odot W_{u_{2},v_{2}}\odot\cdots\odot W_{u_{k},v_{k}}

is isomorphic to the Vidinli algebra
\mathbb{V}_{2k+1}
, by Theorem
8.1
.
It remains to prove that the natural map from this pushout into
\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}
is an injective algebra homomorphism. This follows from the universal property of the degenerate pushout. Indeed, for each
i
, let
f_{i}:\mathbb{R}e_{1}\to W_{u_{i},v_{i}}
and
\iota_{i}:W_{u_{i},v_{i}}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}
be the canonical inclusions. Then the compatibility condition

\iota_{i}\circ f_{i}=\iota_{j}\circ f_{j}

holds for all
i,j
, since
e_{1}
is the common unit in each
W_{u_{i},v_{i}}
. Moreover, by Condition (2), for all
i\neq j
and
x\in W_{u_{i},v_{i}}\setminus\mathbb{R}e_{1}
,
y\in W_{u_{j},v_{j}}\setminus\mathbb{R}e_{1}
, we have

\iota_{i}(x)\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits\iota_{j}(y)=0
.
Therefore, by the universal property of the degenerate pushout, there exists a unique algebra homomorphism

\upsilon:\mathbb{V}_{\Upsilon}\longrightarrow\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}

extending all the inclusions
\iota_{i}
.
Finally, the set
\{e_{1}\}\cup\Upsilon
spans a subspace of dimension
2k+1
, and their images in
\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}
under
\upsilon
are linearly independent. Thus
\upsilon
is injective.
∎

Corollary 11.9
.

Let
\{e_{2},e_{3},\ldots e_{2n+1}\}
be the symplectic basis of
V_{0}
determined by
\omega
. Let
\Lambda\subseteq\{2,3,\ldots,2n+1\}
be a subset such that
2i\in\Lambda
if and only if
2i+1\in\Lambda
for any
i
. Then

W_{\Lambda}=\mathrm{Span}_{\mathbb{R}}\{e_{i}\mid i\in\Lambda\}

is a Vidinli subalgebra of dimension
|\Lambda|+1
.

11.3
Jordan Subalgebras

Although the Vidinli algebra
\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}=(V,\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits)
is neither commutative nor associative in general, it contains a rich class of commutative subalgebras. In this part, we determine the Jordan algebras lying in
\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}
.

Recall from
[
4
, Section 1.2]
, a subspace
W
of
V_{0}
is called
isotropic
if
\omega_{W}=0
. Moreover an isotropic subspace of dimension half of
\dim V_{0}
is called
Lagrangian
. Notice that in this case, when
W
is isotropic, the product on
A
reduces to the symmetric operation
\circ_{\mathfrak{v}}
. Also if the subspace
W
is closed under the symmetric part of the Vidinli multiplication, then it is isomorphic to a Vidinli-Jordan algebra. Hence we obtain the following result.

Proposition 11.10
.

Let
A\subset V
be a linear subspace such that:

1.

A
is closed under the symmetric product
\circ_{\mathfrak{v}}
,

2.

A
is isotropic.

Then
(A,\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits)
is a Vidinli-Jordan algebra. Moreover, any such subalgebra has dimension at most
n+1
, and equality holds if and only if
A=\mathbb{R}e_{1}\oplus L
, where
L\subset V_{0}
is a Lagrangian subspace.

Example of a Maximal Vidinli-Jordan Subalgebra

We now describe an explicit maximal Vidinli-Jordan subalgebra inside the Vidinli algebra
\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}
. This corresponds to a subspace of the form

A=\mathbb{R}e_{1}\oplus L,

where
L\subset V_{0}
is a Lagrangian subspace for the symplectic form
\omega_{0}
.

Let
V_{0}=\mathrm{Span}_{\mathbb{R}}\{e_{2},\dots,e_{2n+1}\}
with the standard symplectic structure:

\omega_{0}(e_{2i},e_{2i+1})=1,\quad\omega_{0}(e_{2i+1},e_{2i})=-1,\quad 1\leq i\leq n.

Then the subspace

L:=\mathrm{Span}_{\mathbb{R}}\{e_{2i}\mid 1\leq i\leq n\}\subset V_{0}

is Lagrangian, since
\omega_{0}|_{L}=0
and it is maximal since
\dim L=n
. Hence,

A:=\mathrm{Span}_{\mathbb{R}}\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{4},\dots,e_{2n}\}

is a maximal Jordan subalgebra of
\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}
of dimension
n+1
.

Theorem 11.11
.

Let
\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}=(\mathbb{R}e_{1}\oplus V_{0},\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits)
be the Vidinli algebra. There is a bijective correspondence between:

1.

Maximal Vidinli-Jordan subalgebras
A\subset\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}
, and

2.

Lagrangian subspaces
L\subset V_{0}
,

given explicitly by
A=\mathbb{R}e_{1}\oplus L
. In particular, every maximal Vidinli-Jordan subalgebra has dimension
n+1
.

Proof.

Suppose
A\subset\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}
is a Jordan subalgebra with product
\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits|_{A}
. Then
A\subset\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}
is commutative, so we must have
\omega(a,b)=0
for all
a,b\in A
. Since
\omega(e_{1},v)=0
by definition, this condition reduces to
\omega|_{L}=0
on
L:=A\cap V_{0}
. Thus
L\subset V_{0}
is totally isotropic.

Conversely, given any totally isotropic subspace
L\subset V_{0}
, the subspace
A:=\mathbb{R}e_{1}\oplus L
is closed under
\mathop{\veebar}\nolimits
, and
\omega|_{A}=0
, so the restriction of the product coincides with the Jordan product
\circ
.

The maximal such subalgebras arise when
L\subset V_{0}
is a maximal totally isotropic subspace, i.e., when
L
is Lagrangian. These subspaces have dimension
n
, so the corresponding Jordan subalgebra
A
has dimension
n+1
.

Hence, the assignment
L\mapsto\mathbb{R}e_{1}\oplus L
defines a bijection between the set of Lagrangian subspaces of
V_{0}
and the set of maximal Jordan subalgebras of
\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}
.
∎

12
From Local Subalgebra Geometry to Global Algebra Structure

This section investigates whether the defining features of the Vidinli algebra
\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}
can be deduced from a minimal collection of local and structural axioms. Specifically, we study to what extent the global multiplication on
\mathbb{R}^{2n+1}
is determined by the existence of low-dimensional subalgebras isomorphic to
\mathbb{C}
and
\mathbb{V}_{3}
, together with constraints arising from its associated Lie and Jordan structures. These local-to-global principles not only offer a conceptual characterization of the Vidinli multiplication, but also illuminate its formal uniqueness among bilinear algebras with rich internal substructures.

Problem 12.1
.

Let
A=(\mathbb{R}^{2n+1},\ast)
be a real, unital, non-associative algebra of dimension
2n+1
, with unit element
e_{1}\in A
. Decompose
A=\mathbb{R}e_{1}\oplus V_{0}
, where
V_{0}:=e_{1}^{\perp}
with respect to the standard inner product. Assume the following local conditions:

\mathrm{(V_{a})}

Every 2-dimensional subspace
W\subset A
with
e_{1}\in W
is a subalgebra isomorphic to
\mathbb{C}
, under
e_{1}
mapped to
1\in\mathbb{C}
.

\mathrm{(V_{b})}

There exists a symplectic basis on
V_{0}
, say
e_{2},e_{3},\ldots,e_{2n+1}
, with symplectic form
\omega_{0}
, such that for every orthonormal pair
u,v\in V_{0}
with
\omega(u,v)=\pm 1
, the 3-dimensional subspace

W:=\mathbb{R}e_{1}\oplus\mathbb{R}u\oplus\mathbb{R}v

is a subalgebra of
A
, isomorphic to the Vidinli algebra
\mathbb{V}_{3}
.

Does it follow that
A
is isomorphic to the Vidinli algebra
\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}
as unital
\mathbb{R}
-algebras?

The Lie and Jordan structures are not included in the problem because such global identities emerge naturally from the local configuration. We will show below that the local data is sufficient to determine the multiplication uniquely and that both the Lie and Jordan structures follow as consequences.

We first show that Condition
\mathrm{(V_{a})}
alone forces the symmetric part
of the multiplication to be uniquely determined by the unit element and the inner product.
The additional Condition
\mathrm{(V_{b})}
then rigidifies the skew-symmetric part
to a canonical form prescribed by a symplectic structure, leaving no further freedom
up to algebra isomorphism.

Theorem 12.2
.

Suppose
A=(\mathbb{R}^{2n+1},\ast)

satisfies Condition
\mathrm{(V_{a})}
of Problem
12.1
.
Then there exists a skew-symmetric bilinear map
K\colon A\times A\to A
, vanishing on
e_{1}
, such that

a\ast b=(a\cdot e_{1})\,b+(b\cdot e_{1})\,a-(a\cdot b)\,e_{1}+K(a,b)

for all
a,b\in A
. Conversely, any bilinear product
\ast
on
A
defined by the above formula, with
K
skew-symmetric and satisfying
K(e_{1},\cdot)=0
, fulfills Condition
\mathrm{(V_{a})}
.

Proof.

Decompose
a\ast b=S(a,b)+K(a,b)
, where
S
and
K
denote the symmetric and skew-symmetric parts of
\ast
, respectively. Define a symmetric bilinear map
T\colon A\times A\to A
by

T(a,b):=(a\cdot e_{1})\,b+(b\cdot e_{1})\,a-(a\cdot b)\,e_{1}.

We claim that
S=T
. Fix a unit vector
u\in V_{0}
, and consider the subspace
\Pi_{u}:=\mathbb{R}e_{1}\oplus\mathbb{R}u
. By assumption,
\Pi_{u}
is a subalgebra isomorphic to
\mathbb{C}
. Under the identification
e_{1}\mapsto 1
,
u\mapsto i
, the multiplication on
\Pi_{u}
satisfies

a\ast b=(a_{1}b_{1}-a_{2}b_{2})\,e_{1}+(a_{1}b_{2}+a_{2}b_{1})\,u

for all
a=a_{1}e_{1}+a_{2}u
,
b=b_{1}e_{1}+b_{2}u\in\Pi_{u}
. Since
\mathbb{C}
is commutative, we must have
a\ast b=S(a,b)
, hence

S(a,b)=T(a,b)\quad\text{for all }a,b\in\Pi_{u}.

In particular, it follows that
S(e_{1},e_{1})=T(e_{1},e_{1})
,
S(e_{1},u)=T(e_{1},u)
, and
S(u,u)=T(u,u)
.
To determine the off-diagonal values of
S
on
V_{0}\times V_{0}
, fix orthonormal vectors
u,v\in V_{0}
, and consider the unit vector

w:=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(u+v).

Then
\Pi_{w}:=\mathbb{R}e_{1}\oplus\mathbb{R}w
is again a subalgebra isomorphic to
\mathbb{C}
, so we have

S(w,w)=T(w,w)
.
Using bilinearity and symmetry, we expand both sides:

S(w,w)=\tfrac{1}{2}\bigl[S(u,u)+2S(u,v)+S(v,v)\bigr],

T(w,w)=\tfrac{1}{2}\bigl[T(u,u)+2T(u,v)+T(v,v)\bigr].

Using the earlier identities,
S(u,u)=T(u,u)
and
S(v,v)=T(v,v)
, this identity implies

S(u,v)=T(u,v).

Because
u,v
were arbitrary orthonormal vectors in
V_{0}
, this shows
S=T
on all of
A\times A
. It also implies
K(e_{1},\cdot)=0
on
V_{0}
since

K(e_{1},b)=e_{1}\ast b-T(e_{1},b)=b-b=0.

Conversely, if
\ast
is defined using the formula in the theorem with such a skew-symmetric
K
, then each plane
\mathbb{R}e_{1}\oplus\mathbb{R}u
is closed under
\ast
and satisfies the multiplication table of
\mathbb{C}
, verifying Conditions
\mathrm{(V_{a})}
and
\mathrm{(V_{b})}
.
∎

The preceding result shows that the condition
\mathrm{(V_{a})}
determines the symmetric part of the product uniquely, reducing the classification problem to the structure of its skew-symmetric component
K
. To proceed further, we now impose the additional geometric constraint
\mathrm{(V_{b})}
, which requires certain 3-dimensional subspaces to form embedded copies of the Vidinli algebra
\mathbb{V}_{3}
. This condition rigidifies the remaining freedom by forcing
K
to coincide with a fixed symplectic form
\omega
on
V_{0}
, extended trivially on
e_{1}
. As a result, the full product on
A
acquires a canonical expression, uniquely determined by the inner product and the symplectic form. The next theorem formalizes this characterization.

Theorem 12.3
.

Suppose
A=(\mathbb{R}^{2n+1},\ast)
satisfies Condition
\mathrm{(V_{a})}
and
\mathrm{(V_{b})}
of Problem
12.1
.
Then the product
\ast
is given by the formula:

a\ast b=(a\cdot e_{1})\,b+(b\cdot e_{1})\,a-(a\cdot b)\,e_{1}+\omega(a,b)\,e_{1}

for all
a,b\in A
, where
\omega
is extended by setting
\omega(e_{1},\cdot)=0
.

Moreover, the commutator bracket defined by
[a,b]:=a\ast b-b\ast a
equips
A
with the structure of the Heisenberg Lie algebra of dimension
2n+1
, with center
\mathbb{R}e_{1}
and symplectic form
\omega
on
V_{0}
.

Proof.

We decompose
\ast
as a sum of its symmetric part
S
and skew-symmetric part
K
, as in the previous proof. We will show

S(a,b)=(a\cdot e_{1})\ b+(b\cdot e_{1})\ a-(a\cdot b)\ e_{1},\quad K(a,b)=\omega(a,b)\ e_{1}.

Let
u,v\in V_{0}
be orthonormal vectors such that
\omega(u,v)=\pm 1
so that the subspace

W:=\mathbb{R}e_{1}\oplus\mathbb{R}u\oplus\mathbb{R}v

is a subalgebra of
A
isomorphic to the 3-dimensional Vidinli algebra
\mathbb{V}_{3}
.
In this algebra, the symmetric part of the product is

S(a,b)=(a\cdot e_{1})\,b+(b\cdot e_{1})\,a-(a\cdot b)\,e_{1},

and the skew-symmetric part is

K(a,b)=\omega(a,b)\,e_{1}

where
\omega
is a symplectic form on
V_{0}
, extended trivially on
e_{1}
. Thus, for all
a,b\in W
, we have:

a\ast b=S(a,b)+\omega(a,b)\,e_{1}.

To complete the proof, it is enough to show that this point-wise equality for
S
and for
K
holds globally for all
a,b\in V_{0}
. The first claim follows from Theorem
12.2
. We prove it for the skew-symmetric part. Let
a,b\in V_{0}
be arbitrary non-zero vectors and suppose

\omega(a,b)\neq 0
. Set

\lambda=\omega(a,b),\quad u=\frac{a}{\sqrt{|\lambda|}},\quad v=\frac{b}{\sqrt{|\lambda|}}.

Then
\omega(u,v)=\lambda/|\lambda|=\pm 1
, so by hypothesis and bilinearity we get

K(a,b)=K\bigl(\sqrt{|\lambda|}\,u,\;\sqrt{|\lambda|}\,v\bigr)=|\lambda|\,K(u,v)=\lambda\,e_{1}=\omega(a,b)\,e_{1},

as required. Now suppose
\omega(a,b)=0
. Since
\omega
is nondegenerate, we can choose
c\in V_{0}
with
\omega(a,c)\neq 0
. Fix two distinct nonzero scalars
\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}
and define

b_{\varepsilon_{i}}=b+\varepsilon_{i}\,c
for
i=1,2
.
Then

\omega(a,b_{\varepsilon_{i}})=\varepsilon_{i}\,\omega(a,c)\neq 0
and hence we obtain

K\bigl(a,b_{\varepsilon_{i}}\bigr)=\omega(a,b_{\varepsilon_{i}})\,e_{1}=\varepsilon_{i}\,\omega(a,c)\,e_{1}.

Solving these equations, we conclude that

K(a,c)=\omega(a,c)\,e_{1}.

Substituting into either equation gives

K(a,b)=0
, as required.
In both cases we obtain
K(a,b)=\omega(a,b)\,e_{1}
, completing the proof.
∎

Corollary 12.4
.

Any algebra
A
satisfying Conditions
\mathrm{(V_{a})}
and
\mathrm{(V_{b})}

of Problem
12.1
is isomorphic, as a unital real algebra,
to the Vidinli algebra
\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}
.

Proof.

By Theorem
12.3
, the multiplication on
A

coincides with the canonical Vidinli multiplication determined by the inner product
and the symplectic form
\omega
on
V_{0}
.
This description matches exactly the defining construction of
\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}

given in Section
4.1
, hence
A\cong\mathbb{V}_{2n+1}

as unital real algebras.
∎

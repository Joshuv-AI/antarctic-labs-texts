Enhanced direct torque control based on intelligent approach for doubly-fed induction machine fed by three-level inverter
Authors: Abderrahmane Moussaoui, Djilani Ben Attous, Habib Benbouhenni, Youcef Bekakra, Benharir Nedjadi, Z.M.S. Elbarbary
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
This paper presents an adaptive neuro-fuzzy inference system (ANFIS) based on 24 sectors direct torque command (DTC) for a doubly-fed induction machine (DFIM) by using a 3-level neutral point clamped inverter. The DTC approach is used in this paper with 24 sectors based on the ANFIS regulator to minimize the torque fluctuations, flux fluctuations, and stator stream THD (Total Harmonic Distortion) of the DFIM drive. The composed technique is accomplished by replacing the hysteresis controllers of the flux and torque with the ANFIS controller. On the other hand, the results of the designed approach based on ANFIS controls were obtained compared to the traditional approach that uses usual controls, as MATLAB was used to realize these approaches in different working conditions. In all tests, the designed method shows improved performance in minimizing torque and flux undulations while reducing the THD of the stator stream. These results highlight the extent of efficiency, high competence, and effectiveness in improving machine features compared to the conventional approach, where the designed approach minimized the THD of current by ratios of approximately 77.50 %, 48.34 %, 75 %, and 81.43 % in all tests. Also, the torque undulation value was reduced by 30 %, 39.24 %, 31.94 %, and 59.31 % in all tests compared to the DTC. The designed approach minimized the speed overshoot compared to the DTC by percentages estimated at 98.83 %, 97.11 %, 96.75 %, and 95.56 % in all tests. These percentages demonstrate the high efficiency and effectiveness of the 24 sectors DTC-ANFIS compared to the conventional approach in improving the features of the control system, making it a promising solution in all electrical fields in the future.

Introduction

The doubly-fed induction machine (DFIM) holds a significant position in the industrial sector due to its cost-effectiveness, robustness, appropriate dimensions, minimal maintenance requirements, and widespread use as one of the most commonly employed machines for converting electrical power into mechanical power [1]. DFIM-type machines have a feature that makes them unique from other machines, as this machine is fed from the rotating part, which makes controlling the rotation speed and the resulting energy easy. This feature makes the DFIM machine one of the most prominent solutions in the area of wind power [[2], [3], [4], [5]]. Moreover, to enhance control flexibility, power of use, and operation over speed, DFIM proposes a novel solution that substitutes the induction machine (IM) in the energy systems [6]. Nevertheless, despite its advantages, controlling the DFIM can be challenging, especially when aiming for optimal efficiency under varying operating conditions [7]. This is due to DFIM's characteristics as a multivariable, non-linear, time-varying, and perturbable system. Additionally, certain of its state variables, such as flux are not directly measurable [8].

Due to these disadvantages, a lot of researchers are addressing these challenges by focusing on the development of robust command approaches. One of the earliest commands, field-oriented command (FOC), was introduced in 1970 by F. Blaschke [9]. This approach relies on the use of a traditional proportional-integral (PI) regulator to command distinct quantities, which makes it a simple and easy-to-realize approach [10]. Also, this approach has a quick dynamic response (DR), which qualifies it to be a prominent solution in the area of command [11]. In addition to using the PI regulator, pulse width modulation (PWM) is used to run the inverter [12]. Using this approach contributes to reducing realization costs and obtaining a fast DR. As is known, the FOC approach has two kinds: the direct approach [13] and the indirect approach [14]. The difference between these two types lies in complexity, ease of completion, costs, competence, and durability [15]. Despite the high competence of FOC in the area of command, it provides unsatisfactory results if the system parameters change and this appears through high fluctuations and the value of total harmonic distortion (THD) of the stream. This can be attributed to the use of PI-type regulators, as these regulators are described by less durability and unsatisfactory performance if the machine parameters change. Therefore, it is necessary to search for a command approach that has high capabilities and great durability in order to rely on it in the area of command. In the work [16], two approaches that are similar to the FOC approach in principle and ideas for controlling DFIM were discussed. These two approaches are direct voltage command and indirect voltage command, where the model reference adaptive system was used to estimate the speed. In these two approaches, the PI regulator is used to control the distinct amounts, as simplicity, ease of completion, and low cost are among the most important features of these two approaches. These two approaches have disadvantages, which lie in the presence of undulations in the level of stream and torque, and a decrease in robustness in the event of a change in the machine's parameters.

In recent years, significant research efforts have been dedicated to exploring various solutions for simplifying the command of IMs while aiming to achieve precision and swift torque response [17]. To overcome these problems of conventional approaches, Takahashi introduced the direct torque command (DTC) of IM in the 1980s [[18], [19], [20], [21]]. The DTC approach is a linear approach, where simplicity, rapid DR, lower costs, and ease of completion are among the hallmarks of this approach [22]. DTC approach involves the direct command of inverter switch states based on precomputed values of flux and torque. This command relies on the use of hysteresis comparators (HC) to command the flux and torque of the machine, which makes it one of the best approaches in terms of the number of gains compared to other approaches [23]. Also, this approach relies on the use of a switching table (ST) to drive the IM inverter [24]. According to the work done in Ref. [25], the DTC approach depends on estimating both torque and flux, as this estimation is considered very important in this approach in order to calculate the error. Due to its many characteristics, it has been used to command both asynchronous machines [26], synchronous generators [27], multi-phase synchronous machines [28], doubly-fed induction generators (DFIGs) [29], and synchronous machines [30]. Using this approach gave very satisfactory results compared to the FOC [31], as the DTC is considered an appropriate and reliable solution in the area of command. In the work [32], a comparison was made between the two DTC and FOC approaches of DFIM, where the similarities and differences of the two approaches were given. A mathematical model (MM) is given for each approach. MATLAB was used to verify the behavior of the two approaches and compare them in terms of competence and robustness. Using several tests, the results showed the advantage of DTC over FOC in terms of response time (RT), torque value, stream fluctuations, and stream THD value, making DTC a suitable solution for regulating DFIM and other devices.

As is known, the DTC approach has disadvantages that hinder its spread and use in the area of command, where these problems according to the work [33] are listed in the following points.•Undesirable torque and flux ripples.•Mechanical vibration of the motor.•Acoustic noise.•High and uncontrollable switching frequency.•Problems at low speeds.

According to the work done in Ref. [34], the DTC approach depends on estimating both flux and torque to determine the torque and flux errors, where voltage and stream measurement are used for this purpose. The author believes that relying on the estimation of flux and torque allows the DTC approach to be affected by changing machine parameter values, which is a negative matter that makes torque and stream undulations large.

For this reason, several researchers have worked to enhance the DTC technique by integrating artificial intelligence. Fuzzy logic command (FLC) and artificial neural networks (ANNs) stand out as among the most widely adopted intelligent command systems, showcasing improved performance when compared to usual methods [35,36]. In Ref. [35], a study to enhance the DTC approach with six sectors of DFIM is presented by using ANN regulators in three regulators, ST with HCs of flux and torque, speed regulator, which give good results. In the work [37], the author used a 7-level neutral-point-clamped (NPC) inverter to feed an IM controlled by the DTC, as this approach differs from the 2-level DTC in terms of the level of commands and the size of the ST. The author also used ANNs to compensate for ST, where ANNs were used to create the pulses necessary to run the 7-level NPC inverter. Also, FLC was used to determine the torque reference value based on a speed error. This designed command was verified in MATLAB using some tests and compared to the 2-level DTC. The test results showed the high advantage and great robustness of the designed approach compared to the 2-level DTC, and this is demonstrated by the numerical/graphical results. In Ref. [38], the author used the ANN-DTC to command two asynchronous motors using one inverter. This designed approach is considered to be of great importance in the industrial area, as this work aims to minimize industrial costs and suggests controlling two motors with the same features using a single inverter. In this work, ANNs were used to create the pulses necessary to work the two-motor inverter, relying on the estimation of both torque and flux. The results showed the efficacy and advantage of the suggested approach and the extent of its industrial importance, as this work shows the possibility of using one command approach to control two engines together. The third-order sliding mode approach is a solution proposed in Ref. [39] to ameliorate the cons and disadvantages of the DTC of DFIG, where a regulator was used for this purpose. Using this regulator does not require information on the MM of the machine, which makes the resulting DTC approach categorized by high robustness and its ability to minimize torque and stream undulations. This proposed approach was implemented in MATLAB, where numerous tests were used, comparing the graphical/numerical results with the 2-level DTC approach and some research work. Therefore, the designed approach provided satisfactory results in terms of the value of stream THD and robustness. The cons of this approach lie in its use to estimate torque and flux, which is a cons that contributes to raising the torque fluctuations in the event of a defect. Fractional-order sliding mode (FOSM) approach an effective solution was designed to defeat the problems of the DTC approach in Ref. [40], where a command was used to command torque and flux. The designed approach is a combination of two different approaches to augment performance and durability, where usual controllers were dispensed with and replaced with FOSM controllers. Also, the space vector modulation (SVM) technique was used to convert the voltage reference values generated from the FOSM approach control outputs into pulses to operate the machine inverter. Therefore, the proposed DTC-FOSM-SVM approach is completely different from the usual approach, as robustness and distinguished performance are among the most prominent features of this approach. The designed DTC-FOSM-SVM approach uses the same flux and torque estimation equations used in the usual approach. Simulation results highlight the advantage of the designed DTC-FOSM-SVM over the usual approach in terms of reducing ripples, THD value, undershoot, and steady-state error (SSE). However, in terms of RT, the designed DTC-FOSM-SVM provided unsatisfactory results compared to the DTC approach, which is negative. In addition to the complexity compared to the DTC approach, which makes it more experimentally expensive.

In the work [41], the DTC approach based on ant colony optimization (ACO) was applied in order to command the machine inverter of DFIM as well as the grid-side inverter. This designed DTC-ACO of DFIM is categorized by simplicity, inexpensiveness, ease of realization, few gains, and high robustness. The ACO was applied to determine the gains of the PI, which is used to determine the torque reference value based on the speed line. ST was used for each inverter to create the necessary operating pulses. This designed approach was verified in MATLAB using various tests, where the results showed that the designed technique has an effective competence in ameliorating the system features compared to the DTC and this is shown by the RT, fluctuation value, overshoot, and stream THD. In the work [42], the DTC of DFIM based on the ACO technique was realized using empirical work, where dSPACE 1104 was used for this purpose. The ACO was used to determine the gains of the DTC, as its use led to increased competence and reduced torque and stream fluctuations, and this was confirmed by the empirical results compared to the 2-level DTC. This empirical work shows that the DTC-ACO is easy to implement and has a fast DR, which makes it a promising solution in the future. Genetic algorithm (GA) is the smart approach that was adopted to ameliorate the problems and cons of the DTC of DFIM [43]. This smart approach was used to determine the gain values of the PI-derivative regulator, where simplicity, high efficiency, ease of realization, and high stability are among the most important features of this designed approach. MATLAB was used to verify the performance and robustness of this suggested DTC-GA compared to the 2-level DTC. The results obtained showed that the features of the command system are better when using the DTC-GA compared to the 2-level DTC approach. Despite this performance, torque and stream fluctuations remain present, which is an undesirable negative. In Ref. [44], two smart approaches were compared in order to find out the best approach capable of significantly improving the efficacy of the DTC approach in the presence of a problem in the machine. These two approaches were neural networks and FLC. The pros and cons of each approach were mentioned, along with an MM. MATLAB was used to realize this comparison. The two designed approaches were compared with the results of the usual approach, where the results showed that the THD of stream value is better in the case of using FLC-DTC compared to both the DTC approach and the ANN-DTC approach. Despite the distinctive competence of the two designed approaches compared to the DTC approach, the problem of ripples remains present at the level of torque, current, and flux in the event of the presence and absence of a problem in the machine. Also, there is no mathematical rule that facilitates the use of ANNs and FLC in order to obtain good results, as they rely primarily on experimentation, which increases the difficulty of implementing them.

In [45], FLC is the appropriate solution to ameliorate the problems of the DTC of DFIM, as it was relied upon to command the torque and flux of the machine. FLC is used to compensate for the use of ST in generating command pulses in the inverter. As is known, the use of FLC depends on experience and does not require information on the MM of the machine to apply it, which makes the designed DTC-FLC highly robust and has great efficiency in improving the features of DFIM. This approach was verified in MATLAB, where some different tests were used to verify the efficiency and robustness of this DTC-FLC approach. The results showed that the DTC-FLC has high efficiency compared to the 2-level DTC approach, with great robustness against changing machine parameters. However, this approach has cons that lie in its reliance on torque and flux estimation, which makes it affected in the event of a malfunction. Also, the number of FLC rules that can be relied upon to obtain good results is among the drawbacks of this strategy, as there is no mathematical rule that determines the appropriate number of rules to use. Model predictive DTC (MPDTC) is a new approach that is different from the DTC approach of DFIM. It was designed as a suitable solution in Ref. [46], as it is categorized by distinctive efficiency and great durability compared to the 2-level DTC. The MPDTC relies on the use of predictive control (PC) to command the characteristic quantities, as the use of this nonlinear approach makes the DTC of DFIM complex, expensive, difficult to achieve experimentally, and contains a significant number of gains. Also, the use of the PC approach makes the DTC of DFIM dependent on the MM of the machine, which is undesirable. Using the PC approach to enhance the cons of the DTC of DFIM requires the use of SVM to create the operating pulses of the machine inverter, which increases complexity and costs. Despite these drawbacks, the MPDTC provided very satisfactory results compared to the 2-level DTC and this is shown by the stream THD, overshoot, torque fluctuations, DR of speed, and torque.

In [47], adaptive neuro-fuzzy inference system (ANFIS) was relied upon as a suitable solution, as it is a combination of the properties of FLC and ANNs. This approach is categorized by high competence and its ability to significantly enhance the features of the systems. Also, this approach does not depend on the MM of the systems, which makes it provide good results in the event of a system malfunction. In Ref. [48], an improved DTC approach with six sectors for DFIM with two FLCs is presented and implemented. This improvement involves the use of two FLC techniques instead of the conventional ST and HCs. In Ref. [49], an ANFIS regulator is used in the speed loop instead PI regulator to ameliorate the DTC approach of IM.

This paper presents a new work different from the above-mentioned works, where an improved method is presented for the DTC used in order to raise the features of DFIM operating in drive mode. This method is to use a 24 sectors DTC approach based on an artificial intelligence strategy. The proposed approach differs from the conventional approach that uses 6 sectors. Therefore, the first major contribution is the use of the 24-sector DTC approach to command the speed of DFIM. The second major contribution is the use of the 24 sectors DTC-ANFIS to reduce the torque and stream undulations of the DFIM. 24 sectors DTC-ANFIS was relied upon as an effective solution to defeat the problems of the 24 sectors DTC approach. MATLAB was used to realize the designed approach and compare it with the conventional approach and some scientific papers. Several different tests were used to study the robustness, competence, and efficacy of the designed approach in improving DFIM properties. The obtained results indicate a distinctive performance of the 24-sector DTC-ANFIS approach compared to the 24-sector DTC approach, as it was noted that the 24-sector DTC-ANFIS approach is more robust and efficient compared to the 24-sector DTC approach. Three-level NPC inverter

Among various multilevel inverter topologies, the multilevel NPC inverter finds widespread use in various industrial applications. Introduced by Baker [56] and Nabae et al. [57] in 1981, this topology employs diodes to reduce power voltage stress and reduce THD value, necessitating an increase in the inverter's level number [58]. The 3-level inverter is considered one of the most widely used and widespread multi-level inverters due to its simplicity and ease of realization. Using this inverter does not require complex commands or high costs compared to some inverters such as the 7-level inverter or 9-level inverter. When comparing a 2-level inverter with a 3-level inverter, several advantages become evident in favor of the 3-level inverter [59].

As a result of the performance and features of a 3-level inverter that was relied upon in this work to feed the DFIM, the use of this inverter allows to augment the competence and effectiveness of the designed approach (24 sectors DTC-ANFIS).

Also, there are two capacitors used to feed the inverter, where each capacitor has a voltage of Vdc/2. Depending on the inverters' switching states, the voltage across the phase winding of the DFIM can exist at one of 3 levels. (0, 1, or 2). sectors DTC technique

Traditionally, the DTC is considered one of the most popular linear approaches due to its ease of realization and quick DR compared to many other approaches. Relying on this strategy in the field of command gives an advantage in terms of competence and efficiency, in addition to reducing complexity and costs. The principle of DTC is based on the direct regulation of the torque and flux of DFIM, the cornerstone of this procedure lies in establishing the command sequence applied to the converter switches, which is essential for generating the desired torque and flux levels in the system [60]. The DTC approach depends on estimating both torque and flux, as this estimation is considered important in this approach to calculate an error in torque and flux. The error values in torque and flux are inputs to usual controllers used to command torque and flux. In the DTC approach, the PI regulator is used to calculate the torque reference value based on the speed error. The use of a PI regulator makes the DTC approach used to command the DFIM in a closed loop.

The primary goal of DTC is to keep the torque and the amplitude of flux within predefined HC bands by selecting the suitable output voltage from the inverter. When the torque or flux amplitude approaches the upper or lower HC limit, the system utilizes a suitable voltage vector to keep the parameter within its predefined HC range [18,61]. Proposed intelligent 24 sectors DTC technique

The fundamental principle of 24 sectors DTC with intelligent regulators closely resembles that of DTC with a 3-level NPC inverter. The designed approach is a development and modification of the usual approach based on usual controllers, where an ANFIS-type regulator was relied upon to command the characteristic quantities (torque and flux). Fig. 8 represents the designed approach for controlling DFIM. In this form, the designed approach has the same structure as the usual approach and is described by simplicity, ease of realization, low cost, distinguished competence, high efficiency, and great durability. The primary objective of this study is to minimize the ripple in torque, and flux, and reduce the THD of the stream. The designed approach relies on estimating both torque and flux, where the same estimation equations found in the conventional DTC approach are used.Fig. 8The construction of the 24 sector DTC-ANFIS approach of DFIM.Fig. 8

The construction of the 24 sector DTC-ANFIS approach of DFIM.

In the designed approach, a 3-level inverter is used to supply the stator of the machine, and the rotor is supplied with a voltage of 12 V. A PI-type regulator is used to determine the torque reference value based on a speed error. Using a PI regulator allows for good speed command, as using a PI regulator allows for simplifying the system and reducing its total cost.

The ANFIS is one of the designed approaches to combine the advantages of FLC and ANNs. ANN provides learning abilities and connectionist structure to the FLC systems, on the other hand, the FLC systems give ANNs with a structural framework with high-level FLC ″IF-THEN″ rule of thinking and reasoning [66]. ANFIS, belonging to the category of adaptive multilayer feed-forward networks, shares functional equivalence with an FLC inference system. In ANFIS, individual neurons perform distinct functions on incoming signals and are linked to a set of parameters specific to each neuron. This FLC inference methodology uses a hybrid learning method that combines the gradient approach with the least squares estimate (LSE) to determine the adaptive network parameters. Another advantage of combining FLC and ANNs, it accelerates the time of the network's convergence by reducing the search space dimensions in the gradient technique [67]. Results

A Simulink model was implemented to compare the effectiveness of DTC with the proposed 24 sectors DTC-ANFIS for DFIM. A 1500 W DFIM was employed to investigate dynamic behavior, with motor parameters provided in the appendix. These DTC approaches underwent a comparison concerning RT, torque ripple, flux ripple, stream ripple, THD ratio, and robustness. Hence, three tests have been devised to determine which DTC approach yields superior results.

Conclusions

This paper introduces and validates an enhanced combined 24 sectors DTC-ANFIS approach for a DFIM powered by a 3-level NPC inverter. The DFIM model and other details of the combined 24 sectors DTC-ANFIS have been presented. The 24 sectors DTC-ANFIS represents an enhancement over the 24 sectors DTC, notably seen in reduced torque ripple, improved current quality, minimized flux ripple, lower THD values, and enhanced dynamic responses in speed, torque, and flux. In the third test, a reduction in THD of approximately 76.47 % is observed compared to the 24 sectors DTC.

In essence, the main outcomes of this research can be abridged as follows.•An enhanced intelligent regulator is introduced and verified through numerical simulations.•The proposed control approach minimizes stator flux and torque fluctuations.•The proposed 24 sectors DTC strategy, utilizing ANFIS, is introduced and compared with the 24 sectors DTC.•The implemented approach achieved a lower stream THD value.

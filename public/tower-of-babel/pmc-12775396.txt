Nonclassical features of an open qubit cavity QED system
Authors: Abbas Manan, Atta ur Rahman, Hamid Arian Zad, Hazrat Ali, Asad Ali, Saif Al-Kuwari, Saeed Haddadi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
We investigate the dynamics of quantum correlations and measurement uncertainty in a two-qubit system coupled to a cavity-QED system, characterized by various parameters such as qubit and cavity frequencies under resonant and off-resonant conditions, with equal and unequal coupling strengths. We emphasize asymmetric qubit relaxation and dephasing mechanisms, accompanied by cavity decay. In certain scenarios, separable initial states can outperform entangled states in maintaining quantum correlations over longer periods. We further uncover a trade-off between quantum correlations, measurement uncertainty, and mutual information. Additionally, we demonstrate that asymmetries in coupling strengths or qubit frequencies can enhance quantum correlations and induce irregular oscillations, suggesting new strategies for engineering robust quantum resources in open quantum systems. In comparison, we find that qubit relaxation leads to less decay than qubit dephasing. In contrast, quantum correlations are preserved much longer with appropriate tuning of the cavity decay rate. Finally, the concept of quantum speed limit is employed to assess the resourcefulness of our configuration.

Introduction

Isolated quantum systems provide an ideal framework for studying quantumness or nonclassical features. However, in practical quantum information processing, quantum systems are often influenced by their surrounding environment1. In practice, quantum systems inevitably interact with their environment, leading to non-unitary dynamics that deviate from ideal unitary evolution2,3.

The study of the dynamics, dynamical behaviors, and strategies of open quantum systems to avoid information loss is one of the most debated topics in quantum information science4–7. Although such interactions present challenges, they are essential for the advancement of quantum technologies and associated protocols, including communication, sensing, and computing8. In general, theoretical investigations lead to achieving optimal control of the system-environmental couplings, which in turn would be helpful to develop robust quantum protocols with higher efficiency in the quantum practical world. The open quantum system approach offers a realistic model for quantum systems that interact with their environment, leading to decoherence and the loss of quantum information9,10.

To address the challenges inherent in open quantum systems, several strategies have been proposed, including tracing out the environment or cavity degrees of freedom to obtain reduced dynamics11, employing optimal parameterizations of the system–environment interaction12, and engineering symmetric or asymmetric couplings that can induce or preserve quantum correlations13,14. The utility of open quantum systems largely depends on the degree to which they maintain quantum correlations, with coherence serving as one of the most fundamental correlations15–17. While coherence quantifies superposition within a preferred basis, a complete characterization of quantumness requires examining how correlations are distributed between subsystems. This naturally leads to some measures like quantum discord, which captures non-classical correlations beyond entanglement.

Geometric quantum discord (GQD) plays a significant role in understanding the behavior of quantum systems and the associated resourcefulness18 as it measures non-classical correlations between subsystems that persist even in separable states, making it a more general quantifier than entanglement measures such as concurrence (CN). This aligns with the hierarchy principle of quantum correlations, where discord-like correlations capture broader quantumness beyond entanglement19. In addition, quantum mutual information (MI) serves as one of the most fundamental and comprehensive measures of total correlations20. Unlike other correlation measures, which quantify superposition within a preferred basis, quantum MI captures the full spectrum of correlations, both classical and quantum, shared between subsystems. Quantum MI is defined as the difference between the sum of the individual subsystem entropies and the joint entropy of the composite system, thereby quantifying the total information shared by the parts21. This makes quantum MI a more general and physically meaningful quantifier of correlations than measures limited to coherence or entanglement alone.

The concept of quantum memory-assisted entropic uncertainty relations (QMA-EUR) was first rigorously formulated by Berta et al.22, who extended the traditional entropic uncertainty principle by incorporating the presence of quantum memory correlated with the measured particle. Their groundbreaking result demonstrated that the uncertainty bound can be tightened when the observer has access to quantum side information, fundamentally linking uncertainty, entanglement, and quantum information theory. This work laid the foundation for a wide range of subsequent studies exploring refined uncertainty relations, measurement reversibility, and applications in quantum cryptography and information processing23,24. Understanding these relations in open quantum systems is important because environmental noise typically degrades correlations, increasing uncertainty. Thus, understanding how quantum memory can mitigate these effects is crucial for developing robust quantum technologies.

QMA-EURs are particularly relevant in the context of open quantum systems, where environmental interactions typically degrade quantum correlations and increase measurement uncertainty. By incorporating quantum memory, one can better characterize decoherence effects, thus preserving information and improving measurement predictability even in noisy environments or partially mitigating the loss of coherence and quantum correlations 22. This makes QMA-EUR a powerful tool in quantum information tasks, such as quantum cryptography, communication, and metrology, where maintaining low uncertainty under realistic conditions is essential. Recent studies continue to explore the dynamics of these uncertainty relations in various open-system scenarios, highlighting their practical importance for developing robust quantum technologies25.

Recently, various quantum systems have been studied, focusing on different types of quantum correlations, coherence, and trade-off relations of quantum resource theory12,26,27. For instance, the evolution of quantum coherence and nonclassical correlations in two-level atoms subjected to a Fock-state cavity has been extensively investigated in the literature 28,29, highlighting how coherence generation takes place compared to non-locality and entanglement after being suppressed by classical decoherence. Some authors examined the evolution of quantum coherence over time in two non-interacting qubits initially in a state of maximal entanglement, exploring the effects of classical fields on preserving coherence in noisy and noiseless environments, showing the sensitivity of coherence to noisy effects 30–33. In addition to coherence, various correlations were discussed for X-type states subjected to phase damping, depolarization, and phase reversal channels (see, e.g., some papers 33–35 and references therein). X-type states, which are density matrices with nonzero elements only along the main diagonal and anti-diagonal, form an important class of states that frequently arise in open quantum system dynamics and quantum information protocols. Their simple structure allows analytical evaluation of several quantum correlation measures.

Furthermore, the connection between quantum discord and quantum coherence has been explored in the context of resource theories36. Some findings show various types of quantum correlation, their limitations under different conditions, and the connection between these correlations and coherence37–39. The measurement of quantum correlations is not limited to simple qubit systems but is also applied to many-body systems40. Considering these studies, we find that, in each case, open quantum systems are highly resourceful and need appropriate modeling and designing.

In this work, we consider a system consisting of two qubits coupled to a single-mode resonator to investigate the dynamics of key quantum correlations. Such a system provides a rich platform for exploring the interplay between quantum correlations and environmental interactions, which are crucial for real-world quantum applications41,42. By studying the time evolution of this qubit-cavity system under the influence of different parameters, such as the qubit and/or resonator frequencies, coupling strength of qubits to the resonator, cavity decay rate, qubit relaxation rates, and qubit dephasing rates, we aim to understand how non-classical correlations evolve, preserved, or degraded in the presence of noise over time. The resonator mediates interactions between the qubits and introduces additional control parameters, allowing us to explore how these interactions influence the generation and preservation of quantum correlations. Our main goal is to analyze how CN, EUR, GQD, and MI evolve under various conditions, and to identify strategies for preserving these non-classical features in the presence of decoherence by applying a master equation for dephasing.

Novelty and motivation

Although entanglement dynamics in open qubit–cavity systems have been widely studied (see previous references), much less attention has been devoted to a unified analysis of multiple nonclassical resources–including CN, GQD, MI, and QMA-EURs–within the same dissipative framework. Our work bridges this gap by providing a comparative study of these distinct measures, thereby offering a broader perspective on the preservation of quantum resources under realistic noise.

In addition to these quantifiers of nonclassicality, it is also important to understand how fast a quantum system evolves under decoherence and interaction effects. The quantum speed limit (QSL) provides such a dynamical bound by quantifying the minimum evolution time between two distinguishable quantum states. In open-system settings, QSL analysis offers insight into how environmental noise and coupling asymmetry influence the rate of quantum-state evolution and, consequently, the flow of quantum information. By incorporating QSL into our framework, we extend the study of quantum resources beyond their instantaneous magnitudes to include their temporal evolution speed, thereby linking the degradation (or preservation) of quantum correlations with the fundamental limits of quantum dynamics in dissipative qubit–cavity systems.

Previous investigations have primarily focused on symmetric coupling between qubits and cavities. Here, we report that asymmetric coupling strengths can in fact enhance quantum correlations in certain scenarios, a phenomenon that, to our knowledge, has not been systematically addressed in the literature. Moreover, our study establishes explicit connections between EURs and the robustness of correlations beyond entanglement, extending the scope of uncertainty-based approaches in open quantum systems.

Conclusion and outlook

In this work, we have explored the dynamics of quantum correlations and uncertainty in a two-qubit system coupled to a cavity-QED system against various parameter regimes and asymmetric decoherence mechanisms. We observe that GQD and MI exhibit remarkable robustness compared to the CN, especially under strong cavity and qubit-qubit coupling regimes. In particular, for both maximally entangled and separable initial states, GQD and MI can persist at nonzero values long after concurrence has vanished, and in some parameter regimes, they even display pronounced revivals and sustained oscillations. This persistence is especially evident at higher cavity or qubit frequencies and under strong qubit-qubit or qubit-cavity coupling, where GQD and MI can be more robust than previously reported, and in some cases, the separable initial state \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$|00\rangle$$\end{document} outperforms the entangled state in maintaining quantum correlations at long times.

According to our findings, there is a trade-off behavior between quantum correlations and measurement uncertainty: as CN, GQD, and MI decay, the EUR increases and saturates, but with oscillatory features that reflect the underlying quantum dynamics. In regimes of strong coupling or frequency detuning, EUR exhibits non-monotonic growth and persistent oscillations, indicating that uncertainty is not simply a monotonic function of decoherence but is intricately linked to the dynamical interplay of all quantum measures.

The convergence of the EUR to a non-zero steady value across all parameter regimes arises from the presence of residual quantum and classical correlations in the long-time limit. Although decoherence and cavity losses suppress entanglement, the system–environment interaction drives the two-qubit–cavity composite toward a mixed steady state that retains finite mutual information and discord-type correlations. These remaining correlations contribute to a non-vanishing conditional entropy, which in turn sets a lower bound on the uncertainty, preventing the EUR from decaying to zero.

Furthermore, the study reveals that asymmetries in coupling strengths or qubit frequencies can enhance quantum correlations in otherwise classical or weakly correlated initial states. Specifically, unequal coupling or non-resonant frequencies can induce irregular oscillations and even temporary enhancement of GQD and MI, suggesting a potential strategy for engineering robust quantum correlations in open quantum systems. Finally, we analyzed the QSL time evolution for the entangled state \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$|\Phi ^+\rangle$$\end{document} and separable state \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$|00\rangle$$\end{document} under varying system parameters and decoherence. Without decoherence, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$|\Phi ^+\rangle$$\end{document} shows higher QSL time and slower evolution than \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$|00\rangle$$\end{document}, while stronger coupling and frequencies accelerate dynamics, reducing QSL time. Introducing decoherence slows evolution and increases QSL time, with entangled states consistently exhibiting longer QSL time, highlighting their slower dynamical behavior compared to separable states. Our results provide nontrivial interplay between system parameters, initial states, and environmental couplings to induce optimal dynamics and preservation of quantum correlations and uncertainty.

The regimes explored here are directly relevant to circuit QED platforms with superconducting qubits 28,29,52, where tunable couplings and dissipation rates closely match the parameters considered in our simulations. Similar mappings can be drawn to trapped-ion architectures and photonic cavities 53, suggesting that the preservation strategies identified here are experimentally feasible.

Protocol for establishing CRISPR-Cas12a for efficient genome editing of Pseudomonas aeruginosa phages
Authors: Bingjie Yan, Yujia Liu, Yumei Cai, Yuqing Liu, Yibao Chen
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
SummaryWe developed an efficient type V CRISPR-Cas12a system tailored specifically for Pseudomonas aeruginosa phages, showcasing its remarkable cleavage activity and the ability to precisely introduce genetic modifications, including point mutations, deletions, and insertions, into phage genomes. Here, we present a protocol for establishing CRISPR-Cas12a for genome editing of Pseudomonas aeruginosa phages. We describe steps for the construction of pCRISPR-12a plasmid and guide RNA and the utilization of the type V CRISPR-Cas12a system for precise genetic editing of phages.For complete details on the use and execution of this protocol, please refer to Chen et al.1

Before you begin

This research endeavor aimed to develop an innovative type V CRISPR-Cas12a-based phage editing system for Pseudomonas aeruginosa phages, aiming to address the pressing issue of increasing antibiotic resistance in this bacterium and fill the gap in research on phage genome editing. The CRISPR-Cas12a system was selected for its numerous advantages over CRISPR-Cas9, including a smaller protein size, simplified guide RNA (gRNA) sequence design, and reduced off-target effects, thereby enabling more precise and efficient gene editing.2,3,4 Our study conclusively demonstrated the potent cleavage activity of CRISPR-Cas12a in P. aeruginosa phages, further underscoring its capability to precisely introduce genetic modifications, such as point mutations, deletions, and insertions, into phage genomes. This rapid, efficient, and time-efficient tool is anticipated to transform P. aeruginosa phage engineering, thereby paving the way for the development of more effective treatments for infections caused by this resilient bacterium. The protocol entails introducing the target gRNA sequence derived from the phage genomes, along with approximately 500 bp non-overlapping homologous arm regions flanking the target site, into the pCRISPR-12a plasmid. In cases of gene knock-in, the target gene must be inserted between the left and right homologous arms. Subsequently, along with the pCas12aPA plasmid, they are transformed into engineered bacteria, which then interact with the phage to produce recombinant phages. Further detail of this method can be found in Chen et al.1

Expected outcomes

The deliberate modification of native phages to suit clinical applications has emerged as a focal point of research in this field. Recombinant phages hold promise for potential applications in phage therapy, offering novel avenues for treating bacterial infections. This study establishes a method for editing the genome of the PJNP029 phage using the type V CRISPR-Cas12a system, enabling efficient gene deletions, point mutations, and gene insertions.

Our research unequivocally demonstrates the remarkable efficacy of the type V CRISPR-Cas12a gene editing technique, achieving a 100% success rate in targeted gene deletions, point mutations, and gene insertions within the PJNP029 phage genome. By harnessing this gene editing technology, we can precisely manipulate the phage genome to enhance its therapeutic properties, broaden its host range, or confer resistance to environmental factors, thereby optimizing its performance in phage therapy.

Furthermore, the ability to introduce specific genetic modifications into the PJNP029 phage genome opens up new possibilities for developing targeted therapies against antibiotic-resistant bacteria. For instance, by inserting genes encoding enzymes that degrade bacterial biofilms or enhancing the phage’s lytic activity, we can create recombinant phages with enhanced antibacterial efficacy. Additionally, gene deletions or point mutations can be employed to attenuate the phage’s virulence, making it safer for use in humans while maintaining its ability to infect and kill target bacteria.

In conclusion, our study establishes a robust method for engineering the PJNP029 phage genome using the type V CRISPR-Cas12a system, demonstrating its high efficiency in achieving targeted genetic modifications. This significant advancement paves the way for deeper exploration of recombinant phages in the realm of phage therapy, holding immense potential to revolutionize the treatment landscape for bacterial infections, especially those caused by challenging antibiotic-resistant strains. Future research will focus on evaluating the efficacy and safety of these engineered phages in preclinical models, paving the way for their translation into clinical practice.12,13,14

Limitations

Previous research has indicated that, within P. aeruginosa phages, the type V CRISPR-Cas12a system exhibits higher gene editing efficiency compared to the type II CRISPR-Cas9 system. This finding underscores the potential benefits of utilizing the CRISPR-Cas12a system for gene manipulation in these phages. However, it is crucial to acknowledge that different spacers may affect the cutting efficiency of the CRISPR-Cas12a system, implying that spacer selection could be a pivotal factor in optimizing gene editing results.

At present, it is unclear whether this specific phage harbors any anti-Cas12a proteins, which could potentially disrupt the gene editing process. Additional research is warranted to explore this possibility and to comprehensively grasp the potential challenges and constraints of employing the CRISPR-Cas12a system in P. aeruginosa phages.

Troubleshooting

Problem 1

No single colony growth observed on the resistant plate (step 8).

Potential solution

Increase the concentration of the transferred recombinant plasmids while elevating the concentration and viability of competent cells to promote the growth of single colonies on the resistant plate .

Problem 2

The targeted gRNA lacks activity (steps 9–14).

Potential solution

Given that the cleavage efficiency of gRNA can differ across various gene loci, one potential solution involves selecting and experimenting with an alternative gRNA positioned at a different location on the same gene.

Problem 3

Low concentration of homologous arm fragments obtained (steps 15–16).

Potential solution

Adjust the annealing temperature of the PCR program appropriately based on the G + C content of the primers, and moderately increase the number of cycles in the amplification process to enhance the concentration of the obtained homologous arm fragments.

Problem 4

Low efficiency in constructing recombinant plasmids (steps 20–24).

Potential solution

Increase the recovery concentration of the target fragments and vectors after enzyme digestion to improve the efficiency of recombinant plasmid construction.

Problem 5

CRISPR-Cas12a might prove ineffective in operating within certain P. aeruginosa host bacteria (steps 9–14).

Potential solution

The genome of the P. aeruginosa host bacterium should be selected for testing, and an assessment should be made to predict the presence of any anti-CRISPR-associated proteins.

The column number for 3-modular matrices
Joseph Paat, Zach Walsh, Luze Xu

Abstract
An integer-valued matrix 𝐀 \mathbf{A} is Δ \Delta -modular if each rank ⁡ ( 𝐀 ) × rank ⁡ ( 𝐀 ) \operatorname{rank}(\mathbf{A})\times\operatorname{rank}(\mathbf{A}) submatrix has determinant at most Δ \Delta in absolute value. The column number problem is to determine the maximum number of pairwise non-parallel columns of a rank- r r , Δ \Delta -modular matrix. Exact values for the column number are only known for r ≤ 2 r\leq 2 or Δ ≤ 2 \Delta\leq 2 . We prove that if r r is sufficiently large, then the maximum number of pairwise non-parallel columns of a rank- r r , 3 3 -modular matrix is ( r + 1 2 ) + 2 ​ ( r − 1 ) \binom{r+1}{2}+2(r-1) . This settles a conjecture by Lee, Paat, Stallknecht, and Xu on the column number in the case Δ = 3 \Delta=3 . We complement this main result by showing that there are at least three 3 3 -modular matrices with pairwise non-isomorphic vector matroids that attain this upper bound. More generally, we show that if r > Δ r>\Delta , then the number of Δ \Delta -modular matrices with ( r + 1 2 ) + ( Δ − 1 ) ​ ( r − 1 ) \binom{r+1}{2}+(\Delta-1)(r-1) pairwise non-parallel columns and pairwise non-isomorphic vector matroids is at least exponential in Δ \sqrt{\Delta} ; previously only one matrix was known due to Lee et al.

Introduction

A matrix
\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{Z}^{r\times n}
is
\Delta
-modular
for
\Delta\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
if the determinant
\det(\mathbf{B})
of each
\operatorname{rank}(\mathbf{A})\times\operatorname{rank}(\mathbf{A})
submatrix
\mathbf{B}
of
\mathbf{A}
has absolute value at most
\Delta
.
Our study of
\Delta
-modular matrices is motivated by integer programming, where the fundamental question is to decide if
\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}\colon\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{b}\}
is non-empty for
\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{Z}^{r\times n}
and
\mathbf{b}\in\mathbb{Z}^{r}
.
The integer programming problem is
\mathcal{NP}
-hard in general.
However, a classical result is that the problem can be solved in polynomial time as a linear program if
\mathbf{A}
is
totally unimodular (TU)
, that is, all subdeterminants of
\mathbf{A}
are in
\{-1,0,1\}
; see, e.g.,
[
13
]
.
Moreover,
1
-modular (or
unimodular
) matrices can be transformed into TU matrices while preserving integrality on
\mathbf{x}
; consequently, the integer programming problem can be solved in polynomial time as a linear program if
\mathbf{A}
is unimodular.
Artmann, Weismantel, and Zenklusen
[
2
]
show that the integer programming problem can also be solved in polynomial time if
\mathbf{A}
is
2
-modular.
This leads to the following open question:
for fixed
\Delta\geq 3
, can the integer programming problem be solved in polynomial time if
\mathbf{A}
is
\Delta
-modular?

With integer programming as motivation, we study structural properties of
\Delta
-modular matrices such as the following
column number problem
.
We say that two columns are
parallel
if they are linearly dependent.

Problem 1.1
.

For
\Delta,r\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
, determine the
column number

s(\Delta,r)
, where

s(\Delta,r):=\max\left\{n:\begin{array}[]{l}\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{Z}^{r\times n}\text{ is rank-$r$ and $\Delta$-modular,}\\
\text{with pairwise non-parallel columns}\end{array}\right\}.

Upper bounds on
s(\Delta,r)
and its variants (e.g., only requiring pairwise distinct columns) have been used to analyze integer programs.
For instance, one can reformulate an integer program as a mixed integer program with fewer integer variables depending on the number of different columns, see, e.g.,
[
22
]
; after this, one can use proximity results for mixed integer programs to derive stronger integer programming proximity results, see
[
24
]
.
We point to
[
19
]
for more information on proximity and the column number.
Furthermore, we point to
[
19
]
and
[
4
, §3.7]
for discussion on how to use a count on different columns to bound so-called Graver basis elements.
Other column bounds have been used to efficiently optimize integer programs, e.g.,
[
1
]
and
[
14
]
use column bounds on
\Delta
-modular matrices with no zero-valued subdeterminants.
In this paper, we focus on the column number problem itself.

A classic result of Heller
[
12
]
states that
s(1,r)=\binom{r+1}{2}
.
Oxley and Walsh
[
21
]
show

when
r
is sufficiently large.
Lee, Paat, Stallknecht, and Xu
[

]
independently prove

for all
r
, and for
r\geq 6
, they improve to
s(2,r)=\binom{r+1}{2}+r-1
.
Our main result is the next step in this sequence: we determine

when
r
is sufficiently large.

Theorem 1.2

If
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
is sufficiently large, then

-modular matrices attain the

,

for

.

We introduce some terminology to describe such elements.
Let
e
be an element of a matroid
M
, let
N
be the restriction of
M
to the union of all long lines of
M
through
e
, and let
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.
We say that
e
is
k
-critical
if
\varepsilon(M)-\varepsilon(M/e)>r(M)+k
, and
k
-local-critical
if
\varepsilon(N)-\varepsilon(N/e)>r(N)+k
.
Let
\mathcal{L}_{M}(e)
(resp.
\mathcal{L}_{N}(e)
) denote the set of long lines of
M
(resp.
N
) through
e
.
From the formula

\varepsilon(M)-\varepsilon(M/e)=1+\sum_{L\in\mathcal{L}_{M}(e)}(|L|-2),

we see that
\varepsilon(M)-\varepsilon(M/e)=\varepsilon(N)-\varepsilon(N/e)
because
\mathcal{L}_{M}(e)=\mathcal{L}_{N}(e)
.
Hence, if
e
is
k
-critical, then
e
is
k
-local critical.
However, the converse may not hold if
r(M)
is larger than
r(N)
.
In order to show
\varepsilon(M)\leq\binom{r+1}{2}+k\cdot r(M)
, it is often useful to upper bound the number of
k
-critical elements of
M
; this approach has been used a number of times in previous work, see
[
7
,
6
,
21
,
23
]
.
In the following lemma, we bound the number of
k
-critical elements of
M
by bounding the number of
k
-local-critical elements of
M
.
We only apply this result with
k=2
and
\ell=5
(the matroid
U_{2,7}
is not
3
-modular by
[
8
, Proposition 8.10]
), but the more general statement may be useful for future work.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
.
Let
M
be a simple matroid with a set
X\subseteq E(M)
such that
M|X\cong M(K_{r(M)+1})
, and let
B\subseteq X
be a frame for
M|X
.
Suppose there is a set
B^{\prime}\subseteq B
such that every element in
E(M)-X
is a loop of
M/B^{\prime}
or is parallel in
M/B^{\prime}
to an element in
B-B^{\prime}
, and that each element in
B-B^{\prime}
is parallel in
M/B^{\prime}
to at most
k
elements in
E(M)-X
.
Then the number of
k
-local-critical elements of
M
is at most
|\cl(B^{\prime})|
.

Proof.

Set
r:=r(M)
.
Let
\mathbf{A}
be the fundamental-circuit incidence matrix of
M
with respect to
B
; see
[
20
, pg. 182]
.
Formally,
\mathbf{A}
is the binary matrix with rows indexed by
B
and columns indexed by
E(M)
such that
\mathbf{A}[B]=\mathbf{I}_{r(M)}
, and if
e\in E(M)-B
, then
\mathbf{A}[b,e]=1
if and only if
b
is in the unique circuit of
M|(B\cup e)
.
If
C
is a circuit of
M
, then every row of
\mathbf{A}[C]
has at least two non-zero entries
[
20
, pg. 191]
.
For each
b\in B-B^{\prime}
, let
S_{b}
be the set of elements
e\in E(M)
such that
\mathbf{A}[b,e]=1
.
Note that
S_{b}\cap X
is independent,
|S_{b}\cap X|=r
, and
|S_{b}-X|\leq k
for every
b\in B-B^{\prime}
.

Let
e\in E(M)-\cl(B^{\prime})
.
Let
N
be the restriction of
M
to all long lines through
e
.
Note that if
e\in X
, then
r(N)\geq r(N|X)=r
, so
r(N)=r
.
We will show that
e
is not
k
-local-critical.
From this, it will follow that the number of
k
-local-critical elements of
M
is at most
|\cl(B^{\prime})|
.
We consider two cases.

Case 1:

Assume that
e
is parallel in
M/B^{\prime}
to some
b\in B-B^{\prime}
.
Let
S_{b}^{\prime}=S_{b}\cap E(N)
.
Note that
S_{b}^{\prime}\cap X
is independent, so
r(N)\geq|S_{b}^{\prime}\cap X|
.
Also,
|S_{b}^{\prime}-X|\leq k
.
Every line of
M
through
e
contains at most one element in
E(M)-S_{b}
, so
M/e\backslash(S_{b}-e)
is simple.
It follows that
\varepsilon(N)-\varepsilon(N/e)\leq|S_{b}^{\prime}|\leq|S_{b}^{\prime}\cap X|+k\leq r(N)+k
, so
e
is not
k
-local-critical.

Case 2:

Assume that
e
is not parallel in
M/B^{\prime}
to an element in
B-B^{\prime}
.
As
e\notin\cl(B^{\prime})
, and thus is not a loop in
M/B^{\prime}
, we have that
e\in X-B
and there exist
b_{1},b_{2}\in B-B^{\prime}
such that
\{b_{1},b_{2},e\}
is a circuit of
M
.
Every
3
-element circuit of
M
through
e
contains one element in
S_{b_{1}}
other than
e
and one element in
S_{b_{2}}
other than
e
.
It follows that every long line of
M
through
e
has exactly three elements, and there are at most
|S_{b_{1}}|-1\leq r+k-1
long lines of
M
through
e
.
Therefore,
\varepsilon(N)-\varepsilon(N/e)\leq r+k
.
We have
r(N)=r
because
e\in X
, so
e
is not
k
-local-critical.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
and
\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{R}^{r+1}-\{\mathbf{0}\}
satisfy
\mathbf{1}^{\top}\mathbf{a}=0
.
Let
\mathbf{C}(\mathbf{a}):=[\mathbf{D}_{r+1}\,\mathbf{a}]
be a matrix over
\mathbb{R}
.
It holds that

\max\left\{|\det\mathbf{B}|:\ \mathbf{B}~\text{is an $r\times r$ submatrix of}~\mathbf{C}(\mathbf{a})\right\}=\max\left\{1,\bigg|\max_{S\subseteq[r+1]}\sum_{i\in S}a_{i}\bigg|\right\}.

Proof.

We use the following notation:

\displaystyle\Delta(\mathbf{a})

\displaystyle:=\max\left\{|\det\mathbf{B}|:\ \mathbf{B}~\text{is an $r\times r$ submatrix of}~\mathbf{C}(\mathbf{a})\right\}

\displaystyle\Gamma(\mathbf{a})

\displaystyle:=\bigg|\max_{S\subseteq[r+1]}\sum_{i\in S}a_{i}\bigg|.

We prove
\Delta(\mathbf{a})=\max\{1,\Gamma(\mathbf{a})\}
by induction on
r
.
If
r=1
, then
\mathbf{C}(\mathbf{a})=[\mathbf{e}_{1}-\mathbf{e}_{2}~~~\mathbf{a}]
, where
\mathbf{a}=[a_{1},-a_{1}]^{\top}
for some non-zero
a_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
.
It can be checked that
\Delta(\mathbf{a})=\max\{1,|a_{1}|\}
and
\Gamma(\mathbf{a})=|a_{1}|
.
Thus,
\Delta(\mathbf{a})=\max\{1,\Gamma(\mathbf{a})\}
.
Assume
\Delta(\mathbf{a})=\max\{1,\Gamma(\mathbf{a})\}
holds for all
k\in[r]
and
\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}-\{\mathbf{0}\}
, and let
\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{R}^{r+1}-\{\mathbf{0}\}
.

First, we prove that
\Delta(\mathbf{a})\leq\max\{1,\Gamma(\mathbf{a})\}
.
Let
\mathbf{B}
be an
r\times r

\mathbf{C}(\mathbf{a})
.
The matrix
\mathbf{D}_{r+1}
is the incidence matrix of a directed graph, so it is TU; see, e.g.,
[

]
.
Hence, if
\mathbf{B}
is a submatrix of
\mathbf{D}_{r+1}
, then
|\det(\mathbf{B})|\leq 1\leq\max\{1,\Gamma(\mathbf{a})\}
.

Assume that
\mathbf{B}
contains a length-

subvector
\overline{\mathbf{a}}
of
\mathbf{a}
as a column, and without loss of generality assume
\mathbf{B}[r]=\overline{\mathbf{a}}
.
The matrix
\mathbf{B}[[r-1]]
is a rank-
(r-1)
submatrix of
\mathbf{D}_{r+1}
.
Hence,
\mathbf{B}[[r-1]]
forms the node-arc incidence matrix of a directed spanning tree

.
Hence, there is a row, say

, of

In other words, there is a column

and an index

\mathbf{B}[\ell,c]=-\mathbf{B}[j,c]
, and
\mathbf{B}[\ell,c]
is the only non-zero entry in row
\mathbf{B}[\{\ell\},[r-1]]

After possibly multiplying column
\mathbf{B}[c]
by

, we assume that
\mathbf{B}[r,c]=1
.
Replace column
\mathbf{B}[r]=\overline{\mathbf{a}}

\mathbf{B}^{\prime}

Note that

Furthermore,

Let
\mathbf{a}^{\prime\prime}
denote the length-
(r-1)

\mathbf{a}^{\prime}

As

\Gamma(\overline{\mathbf{a}})\geq\Gamma(\mathbf{a}^{\prime})=\Gamma(\mathbf{a}^{\prime\prime})

Hence, we can apply Laplace expansion along row

|\det(\mathbf{B})|\leq\Delta(\mathbf{a}^{\prime\prime})=\max\{1,\Gamma(\mathbf{a}^{\prime\prime})\}\leq\max\{1,\Gamma(\mathbf{a})\}
.

We now show that
\Delta(\mathbf{a})\geq\max\{1,\Gamma(\mathbf{a})\}
.
Each non-singular

{\mathbf{D}}_{r+1}
has absolute determinant equal to

, so
\Delta(\mathbf{a})\geq 1
.
Choose any

with
|\sum_{i\in S}a_{i}|>0
.
Without loss of generality, suppose

and

.
Define an
(r+1)\times r
submatrix

of
\mathbf{C}(\mathbf{a})
as follows:

\overline{\mathbf{B}}[i]:=\mathbf{e}_{i}-\mathbf{e}_{i+1}

\overline{\mathbf{B}}[r]:=\mathbf{a}

Let
\mathbf{B}
be the

\mathbf{C}(\mathbf{a})
defined by deleting row

from

We claim that

r\in\mathbb{Z}
satisfy
r\geq\Delta+1
.
If
\boldsymbol{\lambda}=(\lambda_{i})_{i=1}^{m}
and
\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{\prime}=(\lambda^{\prime}_{i})_{i=1}^{m^{\prime}}
are distinct partitions of
\Delta-1
, then
M[\mathbf{A}(\Delta,\boldsymbol{\lambda},r)]
and
M[\mathbf{A}(\Delta,\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{\prime},r)]
are not isomorphic.
Moreover,
M[\mathbf{A}(\Delta,\boldsymbol{\lambda},r)]
is not isomorphic to
M[\mathbf{A}(\Delta,r)]
.

Proof.

For
i\in[r]
, let
x_{i}
be the element of
M[\mathbf{A}(\Delta,\boldsymbol{\lambda},r)]
indexing the column
\mathbf{e}_{i}
of
\mathbf{A}(\Delta,\boldsymbol{\lambda},r)
.
For
1\leq i<j\leq r
let
x_{i,j}
be the element of
M[\mathbf{A}(\Delta,\boldsymbol{\lambda},r)]
indexing the column
\mathbf{e}_{i}-\mathbf{e}_{j}
of
\mathbf{A}(\Delta,\boldsymbol{\lambda},r)
.

Consider the set

\mathcal{L}(\Delta,\boldsymbol{\lambda},r):=\left\{L:\ \begin{array}[]{l}\text{$L$ is a long line of $M[\mathbf{A}(\Delta,\boldsymbol{\lambda},r)]$,}\\
\text{and $M[\mathbf{A}(\Delta,\boldsymbol{\lambda},r)]/e$ is binary for some $e\in L$}\end{array}\right\},

and the multiset of cardinalities of lines in
\mathcal{L}(\Delta,\boldsymbol{\lambda},r)
:

\nu(\Delta,\boldsymbol{\lambda},r):=\{|L|:\ L\in\mathcal{L}(\Delta,\boldsymbol{\lambda},r)\}.

The multiset
\nu(\Delta,\boldsymbol{\lambda},r)
is invariant under isomorphisms.
Furthermore, the contraction
M[\mathbf{A}(\Delta,\boldsymbol{\lambda},r)]/x_{1}
is binary, and
\cl(\{x_{1},x_{2}\})
and
\cl(\{x_{1},x_{2,3}\})
are both lines of
M[\mathbf{A}(\Delta,\boldsymbol{\lambda},r)]
through
x_{1}
with at least four points.
Thus,
x_{1}
is the only element whose contraction from
M[\mathbf{A}(\Delta,\boldsymbol{\lambda},r)]
is binary.
Hence,

\mathcal{L}(\Delta,\boldsymbol{\lambda},r)=\{L:\ \text{$L$ is a long line of $M[\mathbf{A}(\Delta,\boldsymbol{\lambda},r)]$ through $x_{1}$}\}.

Our proof proceeds as follows.
First, we use
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
to compute
\nu(\Delta,\boldsymbol{\lambda},r)
.
Second, we create an injective inverse mapping from the triple
(\nu(\Delta,\boldsymbol{\lambda},r),\Delta,r)
to a partition of
\Delta-1
, which will be
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
.
As our inverse mapping is injective, our second step implies that
\nu(\Delta,\boldsymbol{\lambda},r)\neq\nu(\Delta,\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{\prime},r)
because
\boldsymbol{\lambda}\neq\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{\prime}
.
Given that
\nu(\Delta,\boldsymbol{\lambda},r)
is invariant under isomorphism, this will imply that
M[\mathbf{A}(\Delta,\boldsymbol{\lambda},r)]
is not isomorphic to
M[\mathbf{A}(\Delta,\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{\prime},r)]
.

First, we compute
\nu(\Delta,\boldsymbol{\lambda},r)
from
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
.
For each
L\in\mathcal{L}(\Delta,\boldsymbol{\lambda},r)
, there exists an element
e\in L-\{x_{1}\}
of type
(A-1)
or
(A-2)
, i.e., an element
x_{i}
or
x_{i,j}
.
Indeed, if
f\in L-\{x_{1}\}
is type
(A-3)
or
(A-4)
, then
\cl(x_{1},f)-\{x_{1}\}
contains an element of type
(A-1)
or
(A-2)
.
Moreover, if
m+2\leq i<j\leq r
, then
|\cl(x_{1},x_{i,j})|=2
.
Hence, the lines
L\in\mathcal{L}(\Delta,\boldsymbol{\lambda},r)
fall into four distinct possibilities:

(a)

L=\cl(\{x_{1},x_{i}\})
with
m+2\leq i\leq r
.

(b)

L=\cl(\{x_{1},x_{i}\})
with
2\leq i\leq m+1
.

(c)

L=\cl(\{x_{1},x_{i,j}\})
with
2\leq i\leq m+1
and
m+2\leq j\leq r
.

(d)

L=\cl(\{x_{1},x_{i,j}\})
with
2\leq i<j\leq m+1
.

Given a set
S
and
t\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
we write
tS
for the multiset consisting of
t
copies of each element of
S
.
There are
r-(m+1)
long lines in case
(a)
, each with length
3
.
Thus, case
(a)
contributes
(r-(m+1))\{3\}
to
\nu(\Delta,\boldsymbol{\lambda},r)
.
A line in case
(b)
consists of labels of columns
\mathbf{e}_{1},\mathbf{e}_{i},\mathbf{e}_{1}-\mathbf{e}_{i}
and
k\mathbf{e}_{1}+\mathbf{e}_{i}
for all
k\in[\lambda_{i-1}]
.
Thus, case
(b)
contributes
\{3+\lambda_{i}\colon i\in[m]\}
to
\nu(\Delta,\boldsymbol{\lambda},r)
.
A line in case
(c)
consists of labels of columns
\mathbf{e}_{1},\mathbf{e}_{i}-\mathbf{e}_{j}
and
k\mathbf{e}_{1}+\mathbf{e}_{i}-\mathbf{e}_{j}
for all
k\in[\lambda_{i-1}]
.
Thus, case
(c)
contributes
(r-(m+1))\{2+\lambda_{i}\colon i\in[m]\}
to
\nu(\Delta,\boldsymbol{\lambda},r)

A line in case
(d)
consists of labels of columns
\mathbf{e}_{1},\mathbf{e}_{i}-\mathbf{e}_{j}
,
k\mathbf{e}_{1}+\mathbf{e}_{i}-\mathbf{e}_{j}
for all
k\in[\lambda_{i-1}]
, and
t\mathbf{e}_{1}-\mathbf{e}_{i}+\mathbf{e}_{j}
for all
t\in[\lambda_{j-1}]
.
Thus, case
(d)
contributes
\{2+\lambda_{i}+\lambda_{j}\colon 1\leq i<j\leq m\}
to
\nu(\Delta,\boldsymbol{\lambda},r)
.
Putting this all together, we have

\begin{array}[]{r@{\hskip.1 cm}c@{\hskip.1 cm}l}\nu(\Delta,\boldsymbol{\lambda},r)=(r-(m+1))\{3\}\hskip 2.84544pt&\cup\hfil\hskip 2.84544pt&\{3+\lambda_{i}\colon i\in[m]\}\\
\hskip 2.84544pt&\cup\hfil\hskip 2.84544pt&(r-(m+1))\{2+\lambda_{i}\colon i\in[m]\}\\
\hskip 2.84544pt&\cup\hfil\hskip 2.84544pt&\{2+\lambda_{i}+\lambda_{j}\colon 1\leq i<j\leq m\}.\end{array}

It may not be clear that the right-hand side of (
4
) is different for
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
and
\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{\prime}
.
Our next step is to show that this is the case.
In particular, we show how
\Delta
,
r
, and the multiset on the right-hand side of (
4
) can be used to reconstruct
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
.

To this end, we first determine
m
from
\nu(\Delta,\boldsymbol{\lambda},r)
.
Consider the function
f(x):=(r-(x+1))+x+(r-(x+1))x+\binom{x}{2}
.
This is a concave quadratic function that is maximized at
x=r-\frac{3}{2}
.
Therefore,
f
is injective on the interval
[1,r-2]
.
Now, suppose we are given
r
and
\nu(\Delta,\boldsymbol{\lambda},r)
.
We assumed
r\geq\Delta+1
in the statement of
Proposition

4.4
, and any partition of
\Delta-1
has at most
\Delta-1\leq r-2
parts.
Hence, from (
4
), there is a unique
m\in[r-2]
such that
f(m)=|\nu(\Delta,\boldsymbol{\lambda},r)|
.
In other words, we can determine
m
from
\nu(\Delta,\boldsymbol{\lambda},r)
.

Now we determine
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
from
\nu(\Delta,\boldsymbol{\lambda},r)
.
For each
j\in[\Delta-1]
define
n_{j}:=|\{i\in[m]\colon\lambda_{i}=j\}|
.
Clearly the values
n_{j}
for all
j\in[\Delta-1]
determine
\lambda
.
For each
t\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
, let
\ell_{t}
be the number of times
t
appears in
\nu(\Delta,\boldsymbol{\lambda},r)
.
First, note that

\ell_{3}=(r-(m+1))+(r-(m+1))\underbrace{|\{i\in[m]\colon\lambda_{i}=1\}|}_{=:n_{1}}.

We can solve this equation to recover
n_{1}
from
\nu(\Delta,\boldsymbol{\lambda},r)
.
Now, fix some
s
with
2\leq s\leq\Delta-1
and suppose that can recover
n_{j}
from
\nu(\Delta,\boldsymbol{\lambda},r)
for all
j<s
.
We will use this information to recover
n_{s}
.
Let
z
be the number of ways to write
s
as a sum of positive integers such that each is at most
s-1
and we use at most
n_{j}
copies of
j
, for all
j\in[s-1]
.
As
s+2\geq 4
, we have

\ell_{s+2}=n_{s-1}+(r-(m+1))n_{s}+z.

We can solve this equation for
n_{s}
.
Therefore, by induction, we can determine
n_{s}
for all
s\in[\Delta-1]
.
The values
n_{s}
, for all
s\in[\Delta-1]
, uniquely determine
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
.
Hence, we can recover
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
from
\nu(\Delta,\boldsymbol{\lambda},r)
.

Finally, we show that
M
and
M_{0}
are non-isomorphic.
We previously argued that there is a unique element
e
such that
M[\mathbf{A}(\Delta,\boldsymbol{\lambda},r)]/e
is binary.
A similar argument shows that
M_{0}
has a unique element
e
such that
M_{0}/e
is binary (
e
indexes
\mathbf{e}_{1}
), and the multiset of lengths of long lines of
M_{0}
through
e
is
(r-1)\{\Delta+2\}
.
However,
\mathcal{L}(\Delta,\lambda,r)
contains either one copy (if
m=1
) or zero copies (if
m\geq 2
) of
\Delta+2
.
Thus,
M
and
M_{0}
are not isomorphic.
∎

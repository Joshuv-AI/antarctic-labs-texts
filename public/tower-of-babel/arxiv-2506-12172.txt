Affine Deformations of Divisible Convex Cones and Affine Spacetimes
Antoine Ablondi

Abstract
. Given a subgroup of SL ⁡ ( ℝ d + 1 ) ⋉ ℝ d + 1 \mathrm{SL}{\bigl({\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\bigr)}\ltimes\mathbb{R}^{d+1} obtained by adding a translation part to a torsion-free discrete subgroup of SL ⁡ ( ℝ d + 1 ) \mathrm{SL}{\bigl({\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\bigr)} dividing a convex domain in the sense of Benoist, we study two particular convex domains on which the affine action of that subgroup is free and properly discontinuous and whose quotients are naturally endowed with an “affine spacetime” structure that we introduce. In that setting, we show that those quotients are Maximal Globally Hyperbolic affine spacetimes admitting a locally uniformly Convex and Compact Cauchy surface (denoted as a MGHCC affine spacetimes), and that they come with a cosmological time function with Cauchy hypersurfaces foliating the quotient affine spacetime as level sets. Finally, we show that those are the only examples of such MGHCC affine spacetimes. All these results generalise the work of Mess, Barbot and Bonsante on affine deformations of uniform lattices of SO 0 ​ ( d , 1 ) \mathrm{SO}_{0}(d,1) .



1.
Introduction

In this article we study some particular discrete subgroups of
\mathrm{SA}{\bigl({\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\bigr)}=\mathrm{SL}{\bigl({\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\bigr)}\ltimes\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
, the group of
special affine transformations of

\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
. Our interest for such groups comes from previous works in two different settings generalising uniform lattices of
\mathrm{SO}_{0}(d,1)
.

On one side,
affine deformations of uniform lattices of
\mathrm{SO}_{0}(d,1)
, that is subgroups of
\mathrm{SO}_{0}(d,1)\ltimes\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
obtained by adding translation parts to uniform lattices, were the focus of the works of Mess
[
Mes07
,
ABB+07
]
for
d=2
, as well as the ones of Barbot
[
Bar05
]
and Bonsante
[
Bon05
]
in higher dimension. They proved a correspondence between those subgroups and some flat Lorentzian spacetimes satisfying nice properties. More recent works
[
FV16
,
BF17
,
FS19
,
BF20
]
have shown that tools from
convex geometry
inside the
Minkowski space
\mathbb{R}^{d,1}
(on which flat Lorentzian spacetimes are locally modelled) were very useful in the study of those objects, and that their Lorentzian metric was not as important as their
causal
and
affine structures
.

On the other side, the work of Benoist on the rich topic of
divisible convex sets

[
Ben03
,
Ben04
,
Ben05
,
Ben06
]
offers an affine generalisation of uniform lattices of
\mathrm{SO}_{0}(d,1)
: by considering discrete torsion free subgroups of
\mathrm{SL}{\bigl({\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\bigr)}
preserving an open proper convex cone
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
and with properly discontinuous and cocompact projective action on
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{C})
, the projectivisation of the cone.

Main results

In this article we introduce and study
affine spacetimes
, which are a natural generalisation of time oriented flat Lorentzian spacetimes. Indeed, we shall define a
(d+1)
-dimensional affine spacetime
(M,\mathcal{C})
as a manifold
M
endowed with both a
(G,X)
-structure where
G=\mathrm{SL}{\bigl({\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\bigr)}\ltimes\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
is the group of orientation and volume preserving affine transformations acting on the affine space
X=\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
, and a
parallel distribution of open proper convex cones

\mathcal{C}
in its tangent bundle. Thus, time oriented flat Lorentzian spacetimes are naturally affine spacetimes: they are locally modelled on the Minkowski space
\mathbb{R}^{d,1}
, so they admit a
{\bigl({\mathrm{SO}_{0}(d,1)\ltimes\mathbb{R}^{d,1},\mathbb{R}^{d,1}}\bigr)}
-structure and a natural cone distribution given by the interiors of the future light cones in its tangent bundle. All of the definitions and notions arising purely from the causal structure of Lorentzian spacetimes, for example
Cauchy surfaces
and
global hyperbolicity
, can naturally extend to this new setting (see subsection
2.3
).
Hence, affine spacetimes are locally modelled on the
model affine spacetime

{\bigl({\mathbb{A}^{d+1},\mathcal{C}}\bigr)}
: the real affine space
\mathbb{A}^{d+1}
endowed with a parallel cone structure given by an open proper convex cone
\mathcal{C}
. In that setting, we introduce
\mathcal{C}
-convex domains
of
{\bigl({\mathbb{A}^{d+1},\mathcal{C}}\bigr)}
as open convex domains which are their own
\mathcal{C}
-future and with Gauss map satisfying some surjectivity condition (see Definition
4.2
). We then prove the following theorem generalising the work of Mess
[
Mes07
,
ABB+07
]
, Barbot
[
Bar05
]
, and Bonsante
[
Bon05
]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Gamma_{\tau}<\mathrm{SL}{\bigl({\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\bigr)}\ltimes\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
be a discrete torsion-free group obtained by adding translations part to a subgroup
\Gamma<\mathrm{SL}{\bigl({\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\bigr)}
dividing an open proper convex cone
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
, i.e. such that the projective action of
\Gamma
on
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{C})
is properly discontinuous and cocompact. There exists a unique maximal
\Gamma_{\tau}
-invariant
\mathcal{C}
-convex domain
D_{\tau}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
. It satisfies the following properties:

(1)

The action of
\Gamma_{\tau}
on
D_{\tau}
is free and properly discontinuous, and there is a homeomorphism

D_{\tau}/\Gamma_{\tau}\simeq\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{C})/\Gamma\times\mathbb{R}.

(2)

The affine spacetime
{\bigl({D_{\tau}/\Gamma_{\tau},\mathcal{C}}\bigr)}
is a maximal globally hyperbolic spacetime admitting a locally uniformly convex and compact Cauchy surface (a MGHCC affine spacetime).

Remark
.

The existence and uniqueness of the maximal
\Gamma_{\tau}
-invariant
\mathcal{C}
-convex domain
D_{\tau}
, on which
\Gamma_{\tau}
acts properly discontinuously was already proved in the work of Choi
[
Cho25
, Theorems 4.3.1 and 4.3.7]
in a more general setting and with different language and approach. In dimension
2+1
, that, as well as the topology of the quotient
D_{\tau}/\Gamma_{\tau}
, was also independently shown by Nie and Seppi
[
NS23
, Theorem A]
. The topology of the quotient in higher dimension, the notion of affine spacetime, the whole point
(2)
, as well as Theorems
1.2
and
1.3
below are completely new to the best of our knowledge.

Notice that Theorem
1.1
can also be applied using the cone
-\mathcal{C}
, which is also divisible by
\Gamma
, in order to produce another couple of domain and affine spacetime.

Applying Theorem
1.1
in the particular case where
\Gamma<\mathrm{SO}_{0}(d,1)
is the holonomy of a closed hyperbolic manifold and
\mathcal{C}
is the future light cone in the Minkowski space
\mathbb{R}^{d,1}
, we recover some of the works of Mess
[
Mes07
,
ABB+07
]
, Barbot
[
Bar05
]
, and Bonsante
[
Bon05
]
.

In order to complete our generalisation of the correspondence of Mess, Barbot, and Bonsante, we also show the following theorem, stating that these examples of MGHCC affine spacetimes given by Theorem
1.1
happen to be the only ones.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(M,\mathcal{C}^{M})
be MGHCC affine spacetime. Any pair
{\bigl({\mathrm{dev},\rho}\bigr)}
of a developing map

\mathrm{dev}:\widetilde{M}\penalty\ \longrightarrow\penalty\ \mathbb{R}^{d+1},

and a holonomy map

\rho:\pi_{1}M\penalty\ \longrightarrow\penalty\ \mathrm{SL}{\bigl({\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\bigr)}\ltimes\mathbb{R}^{d+1},

satisfies the following:

•

\rho
is a group isomorphism onto its image
\rho(\pi_{1}M)
,

•

\rho(\pi_{1}M)
is an affine deformation
\Gamma_{\tau}
of its linear part
\Gamma<\mathrm{SL}{\bigl({\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\bigr)}
which divides the cone
\mathcal{C}\coloneqq\mathrm{d}\,\mathrm{dev}(\mathcal{C}^{M})
,

•

\mathrm{dev}
is an isomorphism of affine spacetimes onto its image which is
(D_{\tau},\mathcal{C})
, where
D_{\tau}
, the domain associated with
\Gamma_{\tau}\coloneqq\rho(\pi_{1}M)
and
\mathcal{C}
, is given by Theorem
1.1
.

Affine cosmological time

Affine spacetimes significantly differ from flat Lorentzian spaces as their cone distribution does not come as the distribution of isotropic cones for a Lorentzian metric. Nevertheless, similarly to recent works of Kunziger and Clemens
[
KS18
]
, Papadopoulos and Yamada
[
PY19
]
, or Buro
[
Bur23
]
, based on the axiomatisation of
timelike spaces
by Busemann
[
Bus67
]
, one can introduce a way to define length on the family of causal curves, satisfying, among other good properties, a
time inequality
. Then, a
cosmological time function
(see Section
6
) can be defined similarly to the usual Lorentzian one
[
AGH98
]
. We then prove the following fact concerning
\mathcal{C}
-convex domain of the model affine spacetime
{\bigl({\mathbb{R}^{d+1},\mathcal{C}}\bigr)}
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be an open proper convex cone in
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
with vertex at the origin. Every
\mathcal{C}
-convex domain
K
in
{\bigl({\mathbb{R}^{d+1},\mathcal{C}}\bigr)}
admits a concave
C^{1}
cosmological time function
\mathcal{T}_{K}:K\penalty\ \longrightarrow\penalty\ \mathbb{R}^{*}_{+}
whose level sets are
C^{1}
convex Cauchy surfaces of
(K,\mathcal{C})
foliating
K
.
Moreover,
\mathcal{T}_{K}
is invariant by any transformation of
\mathrm{SA}{\bigl({\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\bigr)}
preserving
K
.

The cosmological time and its foliation will be a central tool in order to study topological aspects of the action of
\Gamma_{\tau}
on
D_{\tau}
and prove Theorem
1.1
.

The cosmological time on a domain
D_{\tau}
from Theorem
1.1
is thus
\Gamma_{\tau}
-invariant and descends to the quotient, so that the affine spacetime
{\bigl({D_{\tau}/\Gamma_{\tau},\mathcal{C}}\bigr)}
is foliated by the level hypersurfaces of its cosmological time, which are
C^{1}
embedded convex Cauchy surfaces. Using Theorems
1.1
,
1.2
, and
1.3
, that gives the following.

Corollary 1.4
.

Any MGHCC affine spacetime
(M,\mathcal{C})
is naturally endowed with a
C^{1}
locally concave cosmological time
\mathcal{T}
. The level hypersurfaces of this cosmological time are
C^{1}

\mathcal{C}
-spacelike convex Cauchy surfaces foliating
M
.

In this article we choose to use
Cheng–Yau affine spheres

[
CY86
]
in order to normalise timelike vectors, measure lengths of timelike curves, and produce the cosmological time function from Theorem
1.3
. That choice is partially arbitrary: one could use any other known locally convex hypersurfaces invariant by automorphisms of the cone and with similar properties (strict convexity, smoothness, asymptotic behaviour), for instance hypersurfaces given by the level sets of the
Vinberg functional

[
Vin63
,
Gol22
]
. Still, one can notice that in the case of a
homogeneous convex cone
(with automorphism group acting transitively), which happens to be the setting of the Koszul–Vinberg theory
[
Kos62
,
Vin63
]
, up to homothety there is a unique hypersurface satisfying those properties (hence the affine sphere and the Vinberg hypersurface coincide). One of the aims of this article is thus to show how useful and natural is the use of affine spheres and the theory of
affine differential geometry
in that setting.

On the one side, that choice to normalise vectors through affine spheres is an actual generalisation of the Lorentzian case: in any tangent space of a Lorentzian spacetime, timelike vectors are normalised by the hyperboloid which is a Cheng–Yau affine sphere associated with the isotropic cone of the Lorentzian metric. The work of Minguzzi
[
Min17
]
actually gives a physical interpretation to that choice.
Moreover, affine spheres have the advantage to behave well with the duality and convexity tools that were used in the Lorentzian case. Indeed, fundamental tools from convex analysis, such as
duality, subdifferentials
or the
Legendre–Fenchel transform
, were already used by Cheng and Yau
[
CY77
,
CY86
]
in order to prove their fundamental theorem about existence of complete hyperbolic affine sphere asymptotic to any given proper convex cone in the affine space.

On the other side, tools from convex analysis and convex bodies theory
[
Sch93
,
Fil13
]
have been successfully used in the study of flat Lorentzian spacetimes
[
FV16
,
BF17
]
, through the description and use of
co-Minkowski geometry

[
FS19
,
BF20
]
, also known as
“half-pipe” geometry

[
Dan13
]
. Those tools rely mainly on the study of variations of the volume of convex bodies and properties of their
Gauss map
. The affine space, which is devoid of metrics but happens to be endowed with a volume form, is a perfect setting to extend those works, and affine spheres are natural candidates to be images of Gauss maps. Substituting those hyperbolic affine spheres for the sphere or the hyperboloid in, respectively, Euclidean or Minkowski convex geometry
[
Sch93
,
Fil13
]
, one can also define and study an affine version of the Minkowski problem, generalising results in the Minkowski space
[
BF17
]
, but also results about surfaces with constant affine Gaussian curvature
[
Lab07
,
NS22
]
. We leave this problem for future investigation
[
Abl
]
.

Links with higher Teichmüller theory

Let us first focus on the particularly interesting case where
d=2
. Let
S_{g}
be a closed oriented and connected surface of genus
g\geq 2
. We consider
\mathcal{P}_{g}
the
moduli space of convex projective structures on
S_{g}
. By the work of Choi and Goldman
[
CG93
]
,
\mathcal{P}_{g}
is identified with the
Hitchin component

[
Hit92
]
of the representation space of
\pi_{1}{\bigl({S_{g}}\bigr)}
into
\mathrm{SL}{\bigl({\mathbb{R}^{3}}\bigr)}
and it can be described as

\mathcal{P}_{g}=\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{div}}{\Bigl({\pi_{1}{\bigl({S_{g}}\bigr)},\mathrm{SL}{\bigl({\mathbb{R}^{3}}\bigr)}}\Bigr)}\Big/\mathrm{SL}{\bigl({\mathbb{R}^{3}}\bigr)},

where the quotient is by conjugation and
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{div}}{\bigl({\pi_{1}{\bigl({S_{g}}\bigr)},\mathrm{SL}{\bigl({\mathbb{R}^{3}}\bigr)}}\bigr)}
is the set of faithful group representations
\rho\in\mathrm{Hom}{\bigl({\pi_{1}{\bigl({S_{g}}\bigr)},\mathrm{SL}{\bigl({\mathbb{R}^{3}}\bigr)}}\bigr)}
such that there is an open proper convex cone
\mathcal{C}_{\rho}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
divisible by
\rho{\bigl({\pi_{1}{\bigl({S_{g}}\bigr)}}\bigr)}
. Then, similarly,
\widehat{\mathcal{P}_{g}}
, the
moduli space of affine deformations of convex projective structures on
S_{g}
, can be described as

\widehat{\mathcal{P}_{g}}=\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{div}}{\Bigl({\pi_{1}{\bigl({S_{g}}\bigr)},\mathrm{SL}{\bigl({\mathbb{R}^{3}}\bigr)}\ltimes\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Bigr)}\Big/{\Bigl({\mathrm{SL}{\bigl({\mathbb{R}^{3}}\bigr)}\ltimes\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Bigr)},

where the quotient is by conjugation and
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{div}}{\bigl({\pi_{1}{\bigl({S_{g}}\bigr)},\mathrm{SL}{\bigl({\mathbb{R}^{3}}\bigr)}\ltimes\mathbb{R}^{3}}\bigr)}
is the set of group representations
\rho_{\tau}\in\mathrm{Hom}{\bigl({\pi_{1}{\bigl({S_{g}}\bigr)},\mathrm{SL}{\bigl({\mathbb{R}^{3}}\bigr)}\ltimes\mathbb{R}^{3}}\bigr)}
with linear part
\rho\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{div}}{\bigl({\pi_{1}{\bigl({S_{g}}\bigr)},\mathrm{SL}{\bigl({\mathbb{R}^{3}}\bigr)}}\bigr)}
.

Labourie
[
Lab07
]
and Loftin
[
Lof01
]
both independently used Choi–Goldman theorem to get a holomorphic parametrisation of those Hitchin components. More recently, Nie and Seppi
[
NS22
]
have highlighted how
\widehat{\mathcal{P}_{g}}
is naturally a topological vector bundle of rank
6g-6
on the
(16g-16)
-dimensional Hitchin component given by the natural projection

\left\{\begin{matrix}{\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{div}}{\Bigl({\pi_{1}{\bigl({S_{g}}\bigr)},\mathrm{SL}{\bigl({\mathbb{R}^{3}}\bigr)}\ltimes\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Bigr)}}&\longrightarrow&{\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{div}}{\Bigl({\pi_{1}{\bigl({S_{g}}\bigr)},\mathrm{SL}{\bigl({\mathbb{R}^{3}}\bigr)}}\Bigr)}}\\
{\rho_{\tau}}&\longmapsto&{\rho}\end{matrix}\right.

Through the special identification between the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{so}(2,1)
and the Minkowski space
\mathbb{R}^{2,1}
, affine deformations of Fuchsian subgroups of
\mathrm{SO}_{0}(2,1)
can be seen as first order derivatives of variation of hyperbolic structures on
S_{g}
. Then the part of
\widehat{\mathcal{P}_{g}}
over the Teichmüller space
\mathcal{T}_{g}\subset\mathcal{P}_{g}
, studied by Mess
[
Mes07
]
, can be identified with the tangent bundle of
\mathcal{T}_{g}
. Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
thus give a correspondence between each element of
\widehat{\mathcal{P}_{g}}
and two (maybe equal) isomorphisms classes of
(2+1)
-dimensional MGHCC affine spacetimes with Cauchy surfaces homeomorphic to
S_{g}
. In the Minkowski case
[
Mes07
,
Bon05
]
, one can make this correspondence one to one by arbitrarily choosing a time orientation on the Minkowski space. In the general case, as the divisible cones vary, one cannot make such a simple choice.

In
[
Lab07
, Section 7]
, Labourie explicitly gives two different parametrisations of the moduli space
\widehat{\mathcal{P}_{g}}
, as well as some symmetries on it. Using the correspondence presented in this article, and a geometric study of the
(2+1)
-dimensional MGHCC affine spacetimes associated to elements of
\widehat{\mathcal{P}_{g}}
, we can hope for new geometric counterparts and explanations to Labourie’s work (similarly to previous works on the Teichmüller case
[
BS16
,
BF20
]
).

The work of Benoist
[
Ben05
]
also has highlighted that divisibility gives a geometric interpretation to some connected components of higher moduli spaces of representations (higher in the sense that they have images in higher rank Lie groups, such as
\mathrm{SL}{\bigl({\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\bigr)}
, but also as they concern representations of fundamental groups of manifolds of higher dimension
d\geq 3
). Letting
\Gamma_{0}
be a finitely generated group, he considered
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{div}}{\bigl({\Gamma_{0},\mathrm{SL}{\bigl({\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\bigr)}}\bigr)}
the set of faithful group representation
\rho\in\mathrm{Hom}{\bigl({\Gamma_{0},\mathrm{SL}{\bigl({\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\bigr)}}\bigr)}
such that there is an open proper convex cone
\mathcal{C}_{\rho}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
divisible by
\rho(\Gamma_{0})
. He proved that if
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{div}}{\bigl({\Gamma_{0},\mathrm{SL}{\bigl({\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\bigr)}}\bigr)}
admits a strongly irreducible representation then it consists of a union of connected components of
\mathrm{Hom}{\bigl({\Gamma_{0},\mathrm{SL}{\bigl({\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\bigr)}}\bigr)}
.

Adopting a projective approach, we shall also highlight that the discrete subgroups
\Gamma_{\tau}
considered in Theorem
1.1
have a
convex cocompact action
on an open subset of
\mathbb{P}{\bigl({\mathbb{R}^{d+2}}\bigr)}
(see Appendix

). When
\Gamma
is

, those are moreover
strongly convex cocompact
in the sense of Danciger, Guéritaud, and Kassel
[
DGK24
]
, and thus, following from their work, are also
projective Anosov
.

It is compelling to try to link our approach to the recent work of Bobb and Farre
[
BF24
]
concerning the generalisation of the part of the Mess correspondence concerning measured geodesic laminations. It should be possible to make those works meet using convex geometry and area measures associated with
\mathcal{C}
-convex domain, similarly to what was done in the Minkowski case

,

]
.

Organisation of the article

We first introduce the definition of an affine spacetime in Section
2
. We then give a few useful results and definitions from affine differential geometry and convex analysis in Section

from Theorem
1.1
. Within Section

, we introduce and study a cosmological time function on
\mathcal{C}

and the GHCC part of point

2.
Affine spacetimes

Let us first introduce and study one of the central notions of this work: affine spacetime.

2.1.
Equiaffine manifolds

Let
d\geq 2
be an integer. We shall mostly work with
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
and use different notations to denote that space, depending on the underlying structures under consideration:

•

let
\mathbb{V}^{d+1}
denote the
(d+1)
-dimensional real vector space endowed with the volume form given by the determinant map
\det:\bigwedge^{d+1}\mathbb{V}^{d+1}\penalty\ \longrightarrow\penalty\ \mathbb{R}
,

•

let
\mathbb{A}^{d+1}
denote the
(d+1)
-dimensional real affine space with associated vector space
\mathbb{V}^{d+1}
, it is endowed with the volume form from
\mathbb{V}^{d+1}
, the flat affine connection
D
and an orientation.

Throughout the whole article, as long as we have not made a choice of parametrisation we shall use the notations
\mathbb{A}^{d+1}
and
\mathbb{V}^{d+1}
rather than
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
.

A
real equiaffine structure
, or simply
equiaffine structure
, on a smooth manifold
M
of dimension
d+1
, consists of an atlas with values in the affine space
\mathbb{A}^{d+1}
, such that the transition maps are restrictions of orientation and volume preserving affine transformations of
\mathbb{A}^{d+1}
. In the language of
(G,X)
-structures, an equiaffine structure on
M
is a
{\bigl({\mathrm{SA}{\bigl({\mathbb{A}^{d+1}}\bigr)},\mathbb{A}^{d+1}}\bigr)}
-structure, where
\mathrm{SA}{\bigl({\mathbb{A}^{d+1}}\bigr)}
is the group of special affine transformations of
\mathbb{A}^{d+1}
, namely:

\mathrm{SA}{\bigl({\mathbb{A}^{d+1}}\bigr)}=\mathrm{SL}{\bigl({\mathbb{V}^{d+1}}\bigr)}\ltimes\mathbb{V}^{d+1}.

A manifold endowed with an equiaffine structure is called an
equiaffine manifold
. It is naturally endowed with an orientation, a flat torsion-free affine connection (coming from the flat connection on
\mathbb{A}^{d+1}
) and a volume form parallel under that connection.

If
M
is an equiaffine manifold with universal cover
\widetilde{M}
, the theory of
(G,X)
-structures tells us that one can fix a
developing map

\mathrm{dev}:\widetilde{M}\penalty\ \longrightarrow\penalty\ \mathbb{A}^{d+1}

and a
holonomy representation

\rho:\pi_{1}M\penalty\ \longrightarrow\penalty\ \mathrm{SA}{\bigl({\mathbb{A}^{d+1}}\bigr)},

satisfying the following:

•

the developing map
\mathrm{dev}
is
\rho
-equivariant, in the sense that for all
p\in\widetilde{M}
and
\gamma\in\pi_{1}M
,

\mathrm{dev}(\gamma\cdot p)=\rho(\gamma)\,\mathrm{dev}(p),

•

the developing map
\mathrm{dev}
is a local diffeomorphism preserving the equiaffine structure, i.e. on each chart of the equiaffine structure of
\widetilde{M}
,
\mathrm{dev}
is an element of
\mathrm{SA}{\bigl({\mathbb{A}^{d+1}}\bigr)}
.

Moreover, given two such pairs
(\mathrm{dev},\rho)
and
(\mathrm{dev}^{\prime},\rho^{\prime})
there exists a unique
g\in\mathrm{SA}{\bigl({\mathbb{A}^{d+1}}\bigr)}
such that

\mathrm{dev}^{\prime}=g\,\mathrm{dev}\quad\textup{and}\quad\rho^{\prime}=g\,\rho\,g^{-1}.

Equivalently, without the language of
(G,X)
-structures, an equiaffine manifold is just a triplet
(M,D,\omega)
where
M
is an orientable manifold endowed with a flat torsion-free affine connection
D
and a volume form
\omega
such that
D\omega=0
.

2.2.
Cone structure and causality

Definition 2.1

(Convex cone structure)
.

A
convex cone structure
\mathcal{C}
on a smooth connected manifold
M
is a field of cones
\left\{\mathcal{C}_{p}\;\middle|\;p\in M\right\}
, where for every point
p\in M
,
\mathcal{C}_{p}
is an
open proper
(i.e. non-empty and having closure
\overline{\mathcal{C}}
containing no entire line)
convex cone
in
\mathrm{T}_{p}M
with vertex at the origin.

The following notions are classical in Lorentzian geometry. Noticing that they rely only on the existence of a light cone distribution in the tangent bundle and not on the metric, we can naturally extend those to our setting.

Definition 2.2

(
\mathcal{C}
-spacelike,
\mathcal{C}
-null and

3.1.
Background on affine differential geometry and affine spheres

This subsection is composed of a few results and definitions concerning

and

[

Affine differential geometry studies smooth hypersurfaces
\Sigma
embedded in
\mathbb{A}^{d+1}
through the following method. The datum of a transverse vector field
N:\Sigma\penalty\ \longrightarrow\penalty\ \mathbb{V}^{d+1}
allows one to decompose the tangent space at each point

as the direct sum

\mathrm{T}_{p}\mathbb{A}^{d+1}=\mathrm{T}_{p}\Sigma\oplus\bigl\langle N(p)\bigr\rangle.

Using that decomposition, one can define
affine invariants
of
(\Sigma,N)

•

the
induced affine connection

\nabla
and the
induced affine fundamental form
or
Blaschke fundamental form

h\in\Gamma(\mathrm{T}^{*}\Sigma\otimes\mathrm{T}^{*}\Sigma)
, determined by

D_{X}Y=\nabla_{X}Y+h(X,Y)N,

•

the
affine shape operator

S\in\Gamma(\mathrm{T}^{*}\Sigma\otimes\mathrm{T}\Sigma)
and the
transversal connection form

\tau\in\Gamma(\mathrm{T}^{*}\Sigma)

D_{X}N=S(X)+\tau(X)N,

•

the
affine Gauss-Kronecker curvature

\phi\in C^{\infty}(\Sigma,\mathbb{R})
, defined by

\phi=\det S,

•

the
induced volume form
\nu\in\Gamma{\bigl({\bigwedge^{d+1}\mathrm{T}^{*}\Sigma}\bigr)}
, defined by

\nu(X_{1},\dots,X_{d})=\det(X_{1},\dots,X_{d},N),

•

in the case where the Blaschke fundamental form

is non degenerate, the
volume form
\mathrm{d}\,\mathrm{vol}_{h}\in\Gamma{\bigl({\bigwedge^{d+1}\mathrm{T}^{*}\Sigma}\bigr)}
induced by

h
on
\Sigma
.

Given an open set

, a smooth immersion
f:U\penalty\ \longrightarrow\penalty\ \mathbb{A}^{d+1}
and a smooth map

by pullback. In order to lighten them, all the following definitions and propositions will be stated for smooth hypersurfaces, but once again they can be extended to smoothly immersed hypersurfaces.

3.2.
Background on convex analysis and Legendre–Fenchel transform

Here are a few useful notions and results from
convex analysis
, we refer the reader to the classic literature
[
Roc97
]
for a richer view of the basis of the theory.

Definition 3.8

(Legendre–Fenchel transform)
.

The
Legendre–Fenchel transform
or
Legendre–Fenchel conjugate
of a lower semi-continuous function
s:\mathbb{R}^{d}\penalty\ \longrightarrow\penalty\ \mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
is the function
s^{*}:{\bigl({\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigr)}^{*}\penalty\ \longrightarrow\penalty\ \mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
defined by

s^{*}(l)=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\bigl({l(x)-s(x)}\bigr)}.

Equivalently, using the usual scalar product
\cdot
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
to identify
{\bigl({\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigr)}^{*}
to
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, it is defined on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
by

s^{*}(y)=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\bigl({x\cdot y-s(x)}\bigr)}.

The Legendre–Fenchel transform is a standard and fundamental object in analysis. It can easily be shown that:

•

it is order reversing, in the sense that if
s\leq f
then
f^{*}\leq s^{*}
,

•

if
c
is a constant function
(s+c)^{*}=s^{*}-c
,

•

the Legendre–Fenchel transform of a function is lower semi-continuous and convex,

•

the
biconjugate

s^{**}
of a function is its convex hull, that is the pointwise largest lower semi-continuous convex function bounded above by
s
. Hence if
s
is convex and lower semi-continuous then
s^{**}=s
, and for any function
s
(not necessarily convex) there are no new higher conjugates after the biconjugate as
s^{***}=s^{*}
.

The
domain
of a convex lower semi-continuous function
s:\mathbb{R}^{d}\penalty\ \longrightarrow\penalty\ \mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
is the convex domain

\dom(s)\coloneqq\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\;\middle|\;s(x)<+\infty\right\}.

Proposition 3.9
.

If
s
is a lower semi-continuous convex function on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with non-empty domain
\mathrm{dom}(s)
included in the Euclidean ball
B_{\mathrm{Euc}}(0,r)
, then
s^{*}
has finite values and is
r
-Lipschitz on the whole
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Thus, if
U
is a bounded open convex subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
one can define the conjugate of a continuous convex function
s:U\penalty\ \longrightarrow\penalty\ \mathbb{R}
by taking the conjugate of the unique lower semi-continuous function restricting to
s
on
U
, taking value
+\infty
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\bar{U}
and values in
\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
on
\partial U
. Then
s^{*}
has finite values on all
{\bigl({\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigr)}^{*}
and
(s^{**})_{|U}=s
.

Proposition 3.10
.

Let
U
be a bounded open convex subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. A sequence
s_{n}
of convex functions
U\penalty\ \longrightarrow\penalty\ \mathbb{R}
uniformly converges to some convex function
s:U\penalty\ \longrightarrow\penalty\ \mathbb{R}
if and only if
f_{n}=s_{n}^{*}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\penalty\ \longrightarrow\penalty\ \mathbb{R}
uniformly converges to some convex function
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\penalty\ \longrightarrow\penalty\ \mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

For
s
a finite valued convex function on
U
with conjugate
f
, as
(s^{**})_{|U}=s
and
(s_{n}^{**})_{|U}=s_{n}
, using the order reversing property of the the Legendre–Fenchel transform we get the equivalence

s_{n}-\varepsilon\leq s\leq s_{n}+\varepsilon\iff f_{n}-\varepsilon\leq f\leq f_{n}+\varepsilon.\qed

Definition 3.11

(Subgradient and subdifferential)
.

Let
s:\mathbb{R}^{d}\penalty\ \longrightarrow\penalty\ \mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
be a lower semi-continuous convex function and
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, a linear form
l\in{\bigl({\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigr)}^{*}
is a
subgradient
of
s
at
x_{0}
if for all

s(x_{0})+l(x-x_{0})\leq s(x).

From now on, we shall use the usual scalar product
\cdot
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
to identify
(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{*}
to
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, a subgradient of
s
at
x_{0}
can then be seen as a vector
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

s(x_{0})+(x-x_{0})\cdot y\leq s(x).

The set of all subgradients of
s
at
x_{0}
is a convex set of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
called the

of
s
at
x_{0}
and is denoted by
\partial s(x_{0})
. If

is in the interior of

its subdifferential is non-empty.

One can easily see that in the most general case

\partial s_{1}(x)+\partial s_{2}(x)\subseteq\partial(s_{1}+s_{2})(x).

In some cases, that even becomes an equality.

(
[

Proposition 3.13

(Fenchel Inequality)
.

Let

and

(3.1)

Moreover the following conditions are equivalent:

(ii)

y\in\partial s(x)
,

(iii)

Proposition 3.14

(
[

Proposition 3.15

Let

\mathbb{R}^{d}
and

Proof.

• Assume that

is strictly convex. Let
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, for all
x\in\partial f(y)
, using Proposition
3.13
we have

s(x)=x\cdot y-f(y).

Hence, if
x,x^{\prime}\in\partial f(y)
, for all
t\in[0,1]
we have
(1-t)x+tx^{\prime}\in\partial f(y)
and

s{\bigl({(1-t)x+tx^{\prime}}\bigr)}=(1-t)x\cdot y+tx^{\prime}\cdot y-f(y)=(1-t)s(x)+ts(x^{\prime}),

but as
s
is strictly convex that means that
x=x^{\prime}
and hence
\partial f(y)=\{x\}
so
f
is
C^{1}
by Proposition
3.14
.

• Assume that
s
is not strictly convex. Let
x,x^{\prime}
be two distinct point of
U
such that for all
t\in[0,1]

s{\bigl({(1-t)x+tx^{\prime}}\bigr)}=(1-t)s(x)+ts(x^{\prime}).

Let
y\in\partial s{\left({\frac{x+x^{\prime}}{2}}\right)}
be a subgradient of
s
at
\frac{x+x^{\prime}}{2}
. We claim that
y
is then also a subgradient of
s
at
x
and
x^{\prime}
, meaning by Proposition
3.13
that
\left\{x,x^{\prime}\right\}\subset\partial s^{*}(y)
and thus that
s^{*}(y)
is not a singleton and
f=s^{*}
is not
C^{1}
, by Proposition
3.14
. Let us prove our claim, using the definition of a subgradient (Definition
3.11
) we get that
y\in\partial s{\left({\frac{x+x^{\prime}}{2}}\right)}
satisfies

s{\left({\frac{x+x^{\prime}}{2}}\right)}+\frac{x-x^{\prime}}{2}\cdot y\leq s(x)\quad\textup{and}\quad s{\left({\frac{x^{\prime}+x}{2}}\right)}+\frac{x^{\prime}-x}{2}\cdot y\leq s(x^{\prime}).

The sum of these inequalities is
2s{\left({\frac{x+x^{\prime}}{2}}\right)}\leq s(x)+s(x^{\prime})
, which happens to be an equality, thus both those inequalities must be equalities, so that

s{\left({\frac{x+x^{\prime}}{2}}\right)}+\frac{x-x^{\prime}}{2}\cdot y=s(x)\quad\textup{and}\quad s{\left({\frac{x^{\prime}+x}{2}}\right)}+\frac{x^{\prime}-x}{2}\cdot y=s(x^{\prime}).

Using Proposition
3.13
we have

s(x)=s{\left({\frac{x+x^{\prime}}{2}}\right)}+\frac{x-x^{\prime}}{2}\cdot y=s^{*}(y)+\frac{x+x^{\prime}}{2}\cdot y+\frac{x-x^{\prime}}{2}\cdot y=s^{*}(y)+x\cdot y,

meaning that
y\in\partial s(x)
. Similarly we have
y\in\partial s(x^{\prime})
.
∎

4.
\mathcal{C}
-convex domains in the affine spacetime
{\bigl({\mathbb{A}^{d+1},\mathcal{C}}\bigr)}

This section is devoted to generalisations of notions and tools from convex geometry in the Minkowski space to the model affine spacetimes
{\bigl({\mathbb{A}^{d+1},\mathcal{C}}\bigr)}
(introduced in subsection
2.3
).

In this whole section, we let
\mathcal{C}
be an open proper convex cone in an affine space of dimension
d+1
, that is a non-empty open convex cone with closure containing no entire line. We choose the vertex of the cone as our base point, so that our affine space is identified with
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
through a parametrisation such that

(4.1)

\mathcal{C}=\left\{t(x,1)\;\middle|\;x\in\Omega,t>0\right\},

where
\Omega
is a bounded open convex domain of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
containing
0
. We are thus working inside the affine spacetime
{\bigl({\mathbb{R}^{d+1},\mathcal{C}}\bigr)}
, where the cone distribution is given at any point
X\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
by
\mathcal{C}_{X}=\mathcal{C}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d+1}=\mathrm{T}_{X}\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
.

In an attempt to keep a coherent and consistent notation that allows the reader to easily identify in which spaces each element lies, we shall use the following notations:

•

an element of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
will be denoted by
x
and an element of
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
by
X
,

•

when using the usual scalar product on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
for duality identifications, dual elements will be denoted by
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}={\bigl({\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigr)}^{*}
and
Y\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}={\bigl({\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\bigr)}^{*}
.

4.1.
\mathcal{C}
-convex and
\mathcal{C}
-regular domains

Following the work of Nie and Seppi
[
NS22
]
, we generalise standard notions from Lorentzian Minkowski geometry to the affine spacetime
{\bigl({\mathbb{R}^{d+1},\mathcal{C}}\bigr)}
.

Definition 4.1

(Supporting hyperplane and Gauss map)
.

Let
K
a convex domain of
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
(it is thus open).

An affine hyperplane
H
is said to be a
supporting hyperplane of
K
if
H\cap K=\emptyset
and there is a point
X\in H\cap\partial K
.

The
Gauss map
of
K
is a set-valued map which maps each point
X
of
\partial K
to the set of linear hyperplanes directing a supporting hyperplane of
K
at
X
.

Let
K
be an open convex domain which is defined as an intersection of the future sides of
\mathcal{C}
-spacelike hyperplanes of
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
. Note that it may have
\mathcal{C}
-null supporting hyperplanes (for instance, that is the case of the cone
\mathcal{C}
). We will denote by
\partial_{sp}K
the part of the boundary
\partial K
of
K
which admits
\mathcal{C}
-spacelike supporting hyperplanes (Definition
2.3
).

Definition 4.2

(
\mathcal{C}
-convex and
\mathcal{C}
-regular domains)
.

A
\mathcal{C}
-convex domain
is a convex domain
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
which is an intersection of the future sides of
\mathcal{C}
-spacelike hyperplanes of
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
and such that the restriction of its Gauss map to
\partial_{sp}K
is surjective onto
\mathcal{H}_{sp}(\mathcal{C})
(Definition
2.3
).

A
\mathcal{C}
-convex domain
K
is called
\mathcal{C}
-spacelike
if all its supporting hyperplanes are spacelike, i.e. if it satisfies
\partial K=\partial_{sp}K
.

A
\mathcal{C}
-convex domain
K
is called
C^{2}_{+}
if its spacelike boundary
\partial_{sp}K
is a
C^{2}
hypersurface and the restriction of its Gauss map to
\partial_{sp}K
is a
C^{1}
diffeomorphism (for the differentiable structure of the Grassmannian
\mathrm{Gr}_{d}{\bigl({\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\bigr)}
, which is the space of linear hyperplanes of
\mathbb{R}^{d+1})
.

A
\mathcal{C}
-regular domain
is a
\mathcal{C}
-convex domain which can be expressed as the intersection of the future sides of only
\mathcal{C}
-null hyperplanes.

Remark 4.3
.

A trivial example of a
\mathcal{C}
-regular domain is the cone
\mathcal{C}
itself: it is the intersection of the future sides of all its
\mathcal{C}
-null supporting hyperplanes. Its spacelike boundary is its vertex:
\partial_{sp}\mathcal{C}=\left\{0\right\}
.

4.2.
Support functions and affine sphere

We shall now use the convex geometry tools developed in
[
FV16
,
BF17
,
BF20
,
NS22
]
in this more general setting. We set
\mathcal{C}^{*}
to be the
polar cone
to
\mathcal{C}
defined as the open proper convex cone

\mathcal{C}^{*}\coloneqq\mathrm{int}\left\{l\in{\bigl({\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\bigr)}^{*}\;\middle|\;l(X)\leq 0,\forall X\in\mathcal{C}\right\}.

Equivalently,

\mathcal{C}^{*}=\left\{l\in{\bigl({\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\bigr)}^{*}\;\middle|\;l(X)<0,\forall X\in\overline{\mathcal{C}}\setminus\{0\}\right\}.

For
K
a
\mathcal{C}
-convex domain of
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
, we set its
total support function

\tilde{s}_{K}
(only denoted by
\tilde{s}
when there is no possible confusion) to be the function

{\widetilde{s}_{K}}:\left\{\begin{matrix}{\mathcal{C}^{*}}&\longrightarrow&{\mathbb{R}}\\
{l}&\longmapsto&{\sup\left\{l(X)\;\middle|\;X\in K\right\}}\end{matrix}\right.,

which is well-defined (the supremum is always finite) by definition of
\mathcal{C}
-convex domains (Definition
4.2
. It is also clearly sublinear and
1
-homogeneous, hence convex.

From now on, using the usual scalar product
\cdot
on
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
, we identify
{\bigl({\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\bigr)}^{*}
to
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
. Using identity (
4.1
), that allows us to see
\mathcal{C}^{*}
as

\mathcal{C}^{*}=\left\{t(y,-1)\;\middle|\;y\in\Omega^{*},t>0\right\}
where
\Omega^{*}
is the bounded open convex domain of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
containing
0
defined by

\Omega^{*}=\left\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\;\middle|\;x\cdot y<1,\forall x\in\overline{\Omega}\right\}.

We define
s_{K}:\Omega^{*}\penalty\ \longrightarrow\penalty\ \mathbb{R}
, the
\Omega^{*}
-support function
of a
\mathcal{C}
-convex domain
K
by
s_{K}(y)=\tilde{s}_{K}(y,-1)
. The total support function is
1
-homogeneous so that for all
Y=(y,-\mu)
in
\mathcal{C}^{*}

\tilde{s}_{K}(Y)=\tilde{s}_{K}(y,-\mu)=\mu\,s_{K}{\left({\frac{y}{\mu}}\right)}.

As a total support function
\tilde{s}
can fully be reconstructed from an
\Omega^{*}
-support function
s
, we shall mostly use the
\Omega^{*}
-support functions of
\mathcal{C}
-convex domains. In order to lighten notations we shall simply write that
s
is the
support function of
K
.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
K
be a
\mathcal{C}
-convex domain of
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
with support function
s
. The hypersurface
\partial K\subset\mathbb{R}^{d+1}=\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}
is the graph of
f=s^{*}
the Legendre–Fenchel transform of
s
(Definition
3.8
).

\partial K=\graph(f)\coloneqq\left\{{\bigl({x,f(x)}\bigr)}\;\middle|\;x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\right\}.

The domain
K
is then its epigraph:

K=\epi(f)\coloneqq\left\{(x,\lambda)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}\;\middle|\;\lambda>f(x)\right\}.

Proof.

For
y\in\Omega^{*}
,

s(y)=\tilde{s}(y,-1)=\sup_{(x,\lambda)\in K}(x,\lambda)\cdot(y,-1)=\sup_{(x,\lambda)\in K}(x\cdot y-\lambda).

Since the supporting half-spaces of
K
are
\mathcal{C}
-spacelike and hence not vertical in our coordinates (as
0\in\Omega^{*}
, we have
(0,\cdots,0,1)\in\mathcal{C}
), one can see
\partial K
as a graph:
\partial K=\graph(f)
for some convex function
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\penalty\ \longrightarrow\penalty\ \mathbb{R}
. Hence the previous equation becomes

s(y)=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\bigl({x\cdot y-f(x)}\bigr)}=f^{*}(y),

and as
f
is convex that means that
f=f^{**}=s^{*}
,
\partial K=\graph(f)=\graph(s^{*})
, and
K=\epi(f)
.
∎

Conversely, it is clear that any convex function
s:\Omega^{*}\penalty\ \longrightarrow\penalty\ \mathbb{R}
gives a
\mathcal{C}
-convex domain, denoted by
K(s)
, by taking the epigraph of its Legendre–Fenchel transform
f=s^{*}
, which is finite over the whole
\mathbb{R}^{d}
by Proposition
3.9
.

This

[

]

[

]

[

. Then the global inversion theorem gives the following proposition.

A

-convex domain is

Again, the maximal

is not necessarily

-convex. Nevertheless, if

is actually a

-convex domain.

Proposition 4.15
.

Let

be an
\Omega^{*}
-boundary function. The maximal
g
-convex domain

, given by Proposition

, is
\mathcal{C}
-regular (Definition

). It is the intersection of the
\mathcal{C}
-future of the family of
\mathcal{C}
-null affine hyperplanes given by
g:\partial\Omega^{*}\penalty\ \longrightarrow\penalty\ \mathbb{R}\penalty\ \cup\penalty\ \{+\infty\}
, that is

D_{g}=\bigcap_{y\in\partial\Omega^{*}}\left\{X\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\;\middle|\;X\cdot(y,-1)<g(y)\right\}.

Proof.

Let us set

K_{g}\coloneqq\bigcap_{y\in\partial\Omega^{*}}\left\{X\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\;\middle|\;X\cdot(y,-1)<g(y)\right\}.

If

is a
\mathcal{C}
-regular domain with the affine function
a_{X}(y)=X\cdot(y,-1)
as
\mathcal{C}
-support function. By definition of

, it satisfies
a_{X}<g
on
\partial\Omega^{*}
. Conversely, any affine function

on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
can be expressed by

where

, and then

on

implies that

is the convex envelope of

(Definition

∎

Let us now highlight how maximal

(\mathbb{R}^{d+1},\mathcal{C})

Definition 4.16

(Cauchy development)
.

The

of a
\mathcal{C}
-spacelike

Proposition 4.17
.

Let

be a
\mathcal{C}
-spacelike
\mathcal{C}
-convex domain (Definition

) with

-boundary function

\partial K=\partial_{sp}K
has Cauchy development

equal to the maximal

-convex domain

.

Proof.

By hypothesis, the boundary

is the entire graph of the convex function

• First let us show that

. Let

as its support function. As

is in the Cauchy development of

\mathcal{C}
-null line through

\partial K
. Thus every
\mathcal{C}
-null hyperplane through

intersects
\partial K

As

is

-convex domain

. By Proposition

,

is the following intersection of

-future sides of
\mathcal{C}
-null hyperplane,

D_{g}=\bigcap_{y\in\partial\Omega^{*}}\left\{X\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\;\middle|\;X\cdot(y,-1)<g(y)\right\},

Thus, every

is in the future of the following hyperplane:

\left\{X\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\;\middle|\;X\cdot(y,-1)=g(y)\right\}.

Using the support function

, that means that for all

(see Figure

As

• Now, let us show the reverse inclusion

. Let

, its support function

satisfies that for all

. As

, the support function of

, continuously extends to

\displaystyle K=\epi(s_{K}^{*})=\bigcap_{y\in\Omega^{*}}\left\{X\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\;\middle|\;X\cdot(y,-1)<s_{K}(y)\right\},

5.1.
Convex projective structures on manifolds and divisible convex sets

A
real projective structure
, or simply
projective structure
, on a smooth manifold
M
of dimension
d
, consists of an atlas with values in the
d
-dimensional projective space
\mathbb{P}{\bigl({\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\bigr)}
, such that the transition maps are restrictions of projective transformations. In the language of
(G,X)
-structures, a projective structure is a
{\bigl({\mathrm{PGL}{\bigl({\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\bigr)},\mathbb{P}{\bigl({\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\bigr)}}\bigr)}
-structure. A manifold is said to admit a
convex projective structure
if it can be realised as the quotient
U/G
of a domain
U
, included and convex in some affine chart of
\mathbb{P}{\bigl({\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\bigr)}
, by the properly discontinuous action of a discrete subgroup
G
of
\mathrm{PGL}{\bigl({\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\bigr)}
. If
U
is a
proper convex domain
, that is if it is bounded in some affine chart, the projective structure is called
properly convex
.

Following the nomenclature introduced by Benoist
[
Ben04
,
Ben08
]
, an open proper convex domain
U
of
\mathbb{P}{\bigl({\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\bigr)}
is said to be
divisible
by a discrete group
G<\mathrm{PGL}{\bigl({\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\bigr)}
if
G
acts properly discontinuously and cocompactly on
U
.

Throughout all this section, we let
\mathcal{C}
be an open proper convex cone of
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}

divisible
by a discrete torsion-free subgroup
\Gamma<\mathrm{SL}{\bigl({\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\bigr)}
. That is,
\Gamma
preserves
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{C})
is divisible by
G=\varphi(\Gamma)
which is the image of
\Gamma
by the natural projection morphism

\varphi:\mathrm{SL}{\bigl({\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\bigr)}\penalty\ \longrightarrow\penalty\ \mathrm{PGL}{\bigl({\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\bigr)}.

Notice that
\Gamma
and
G=\varphi(\Gamma)
are isomorphic. Indeed if
d
is even, it is clear, because
\varphi
is an isomorphism. When
d
is odd,
\varphi
is a
2:1
surjective morphism with kernel
\ker(\varphi)=\left\{\pm\mathrm{Id}\right\}
, but if
\gamma\in\Gamma
preserves
\mathcal{C}
then
-\gamma
does not, so
-\gamma\notin\Gamma
, meaning that the restriction
\varphi_{|\Gamma}
is an isomorphism onto its image.

We choose a parametrisation such that
\mathcal{C}=\left\{t(x,1)\;\middle|\;x\in\Omega,t>0\right\}
where
\Omega
is a bounded open convex domain of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
containing
0
. The subgroup
\Gamma
acts on different spaces by different manners:

•

\Gamma
acts on elements
X\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
by
\gamma X
, that action preserves the cone
\mathcal{C}
and the unique affine sphere
\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}}
given by Theorem
4.5
(Corollary
4.6
),

•

\Gamma
acts projectively on
\Omega
, i.e. by identifying both
\Gamma
with
\varphi(\Gamma)<\mathrm{PGL}{\bigl({\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\bigr)}
and
\Omega
with the projective domain
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{C})
. Using the affine parametrisation, the affine sphere and the radial identification, that action can be written as
\gamma x=R{\bigl({\gamma R^{-1}(x)}\bigr)}
, where
R
is the radial identification between
\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}}
and
\Omega
.

•

\Gamma
acts dually on elements
Y\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
by
\gamma\star Y\coloneqq\gamma^{-\top}Y
, where
\gamma^{-\top}
is the inverse of the transpose of
\gamma
. That action preserves the cone
\mathcal{C}^{*}
, and the unique affine sphere
\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}}^{*}
, moreover the conormal map (Definition
3.3
) is equivariant in the sense that for all
X\in\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}}
and
\gamma\in\Gamma
,

N^{*}_{\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}}}(\gamma X)=\gamma\star N^{*}_{\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}}}(X).

•

\Gamma
acts dually projectively on
\Omega^{*}
, that action being expressed as
\gamma*y\coloneqq R^{*}{\bigl({\gamma\star{\bigl({R^{*}}\bigr)}^{-1}(y)}\bigr)}
, where
R^{*}
is the radial identification between
\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}}^{*}
and
\Omega^{*}
.

The divisibility of
\mathcal{C}
by
\Gamma
tells us that the manifold
\Omega/\Gamma
is compact and admits a properly convex projective structure, as it is naturally smoothly diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{C})/\Gamma
. The smooth diffeomorphisms introduced in Remark
4.9
are all
\Gamma
-equivariant by definition of the actions. Thus, we get the following.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
\mathcal{C}=\left\{t(x,1)\;\middle|\;x\in\Omega,t>0\right\}
be an open proper convex cone, where
\Omega
is a bounded open convex domain of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
containing
0
. If
\mathcal{C}
is divisible by a discrete torsion-free subgroup
\Gamma<\mathrm{SL}{\bigl({\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\bigr)}
then its polar cone
\mathcal{C}^{*}
is also divisible by
\Gamma
(acting dually). Moreover
\Omega/\Gamma
,
\Omega^{*}/\Gamma
,
\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}}/\Gamma
and
\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}}^{*}/\Gamma
are all smoothly diffeomorphic and compact.

Finally, here is a useful lemma concerning divisible convex sets.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
A
and
B
be two open proper convex subsets of
\mathbb{P}{\bigl({\mathbb{V}^{d+1}}\bigr)}
such that
A\subseteq B
. If there exists a discrete subgroup
\Gamma<\mathrm{PGL}{\bigl({\mathbb{V}^{d+1}}\bigr)}
preserving both
A
and
B
and such that either
A
or
B
is divisible by
\Gamma
, then
A=B
.

Proof.

First notice that we can assume
A
, the smallest convex subset, to be the one divisible by
\Gamma
. Otherwise, if
B
is divisible by
\Gamma
, the dual convex subsets verify the reverse inclusion
B^{*}\subseteq A^{*}
, are both preserved by the dual action of
\Gamma
, and, by Proposition
5.1
, the smallest convex subset
B^{*}
is divisible by
\Gamma
.

As
A
is divisible by
\Gamma
, we already know that the action of
\Gamma
on
A
is properly discontinuous, so let us now prove that the action of
\Gamma
on
B
also is. Identifying
B
with the Cheng–Yau affine sphere in the cone over
B
, we can endow
B
with a complete Riemannian metric
h_{B}
invariant under
\Gamma
, namely the Blaschke metric of the affine sphere (Theorem
4.5
). Then,
\Gamma
is a discrete subgroup of the Lie group
\mathrm{Isom}(B,h_{B})
of isometries of the complete Riemannian manifold
(B,h_{B})
, so it acts properly discontinuously on
B
.

Now that we know that the actions of
\Gamma
on
A
and
B
are both properly discontinuous, we can consider the Hausdorff quotient spaces
A/\Gamma
and
B/\Gamma
, with universal covers
A\penalty\ \longrightarrow\penalty\ A/\Gamma
and
B\penalty\ \longrightarrow\penalty\ B/\Gamma
. The inclusion
i:A\lhook\joinrel\longrightarrow\penalty\ B
descends to a local homeomorphism
\iota:A/\Gamma\lhook\joinrel\longrightarrow\penalty\ B/\Gamma
. The divisibility of
A
implies that
A/\Gamma
is compact, thus
\iota
is a local homeomorphism on a compact topological space so it is a covering map. Its lift to universal covers
i:A\lhook\joinrel\longrightarrow\penalty\ B
is then a covering map between simply connected spaces, so it must be a homeomorphism, implying that
A=B
.
∎

5.2.
Affine deformation and cocycle

We remind the reader that we have set
\Gamma<\mathrm{SL}{\bigl({\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\bigr)}
to be a torsion-free subgroup dividing a proper convex cone
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
.

An
affine deformation
of
\Gamma
is a subgroup
\Gamma_{\tau}\subset\mathrm{SL}{\bigl({\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\bigr)}\ltimes\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
obtained by adding translation parts to elements of
\Gamma
. Thus, an affine deformation of
\Gamma
is determined by a translation function
\tau:\Gamma\penalty\ \longrightarrow\penalty\ \mathbb{R}^{d+1}
. The condition that
\Gamma_{\tau}
is a subgroup forces
\tau
to satisfy the following cocycle relation: for all
\alpha,\beta\in\Gamma

(5.1)

\tau(\alpha\beta)=\tau(\alpha)+\alpha\tau(\beta).

Two affine deformations
\Gamma_{\tau}
and
\Gamma_{\tau}^{\prime}
are said to be equivalent if there exists
X\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
such that for all
\alpha\in\Gamma
,
{\bigl({\alpha,\tau(\alpha)}\bigr)}
is conjugate to
{\bigl({\alpha,\tau^{\prime}(\alpha)}\bigr)}
through the translation by
X
. That is, if there exists
X\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
such that
\tau-\tau^{\prime}
satisfies that for all
\alpha\in\Gamma
,

(5.2)

{\bigl({\tau-\tau^{\prime}}\bigr)}(\alpha)=\tau(\alpha)-\tau^{\prime}(\alpha)=(I-\alpha)X,

where
I
is the identity in
\mathrm{SL}{\bigl({\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\bigr)}
.

Equation (
5.1
) characterises the vector space of
cocycles of
\Gamma
in
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}

\mathrm{Z}^{1}{\bigl({\Gamma,\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\bigr)}\coloneqq\left\{\tau:\Gamma\penalty\ \longrightarrow\penalty\ \mathbb{R}^{d+1}\;\middle|\;\forall\alpha,\beta\in\Gamma,\,\tau(\alpha\beta)=\tau(\alpha)+\alpha\tau(\beta)\right\},

while equation (
5.2
) characterises the vector space of
coboundaries of
\Gamma
in
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}

\mathrm{B}^{1}{\bigl({\Gamma,\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\bigr)}\coloneqq\left\{\tau_{X}:\Gamma\penalty\ \longrightarrow\penalty\ \mathbb{R}^{d+1}\;\middle|\;X\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\right\},\textup{ where }\tau_{X}(\alpha)\coloneqq(I-\alpha)X.

Therefore the space of equivalence classes of affine deformations of
\Gamma
is described by the first group of the
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
-valued group cohomology of
\Gamma
, that is the vector space

\mathrm{H}^{1}{\bigl({\Gamma,\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\bigr)}\coloneqq\mathrm{Z}^{1}{\bigl({\Gamma,\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\bigr)}\big/\mathrm{B}^{1}{\bigl({\Gamma,\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\bigr)}.

Remark 5.3
.

The existence of non-trivial affine deformations of a group
\Gamma<\mathrm{SL}{\bigl({\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\bigr)}
dividing an open proper convex cone is not obvious. In dimension
2+1
, Nie and Seppi
[
NS23
]
have proved that when
\Gamma
is isomorphic to
\pi_{1}{\bigl({S_{g}}\bigr)}
, the fundamental group of the closed connected surface of genus
g\geq 2
,
\dim\mathrm{H}^{1}{\bigl({\Gamma,\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\bigr)}=6g-6
. In higher dimension, it is already well-known
[
BF20
, Theorem 3.4]
that closed hyperbolic manifolds admitting a totally geodesic hypersurface have holonomy group
\Gamma<\mathrm{SO}_{0}(d,1)
with a non trivial deformation group
\mathrm{H}^{1}{\bigl({\Gamma,\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\bigr)}
. In Appendix
A
, we extend those examples and produce, in any dimension, groups dividing an open proper convex domain, which is not projectively equivalent to an ellipse (i.e. giving the Klein ball model of the hyperbolic space), and admitting non-trivial affine deformations.

Remark 5.4
.

Notice that a subgroup
G<\mathrm{SL}{\bigl({\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\bigr)}\ltimes\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
is not always an affine deformation of its linear part. A necessary and sufficient condition for that is that any two distinct elements of
G
have distinct linear parts. Equivalently, that is the case if and only if
G
does not contain any non-trivial translation, i.e. element of the form
(I,\tau)
, where
I
is the identity in
\mathrm{SL}{\bigl({\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\bigr)}
and
\tau\neq 0
.

5.3.
Equivariant convex domains

Throughout the rest of this section we let
\tau\in\mathrm{Z}^{1}{\bigl({\Gamma,\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\bigr)}
be a cocycle of the torsion-free subgroup
\Gamma
dividing the cone
\mathcal{C}
, and
\Gamma_{\tau}
the associated affine deformation. From now on, in order to lighten the notation, we shall denote
\tau_{\gamma}\coloneqq\tau(\gamma)
.

Definition 5.5

(
\tau
-convex domain and
\tau
-support functions)
.

A
\tau
-convex domain
is a
\mathcal{C}
-convex domain
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
invariant under the action of
\Gamma_{\tau}
. The
\tau
-support function
of a
\tau
-convex domain is its
\Omega^{*}
-support function as a
\mathcal{C}
-convex domain.

Proposition 5.6
.

Let
K
be a
\mathcal{C}
-convex domain with total support function
\tilde{s}
. If
\sigma\in\mathrm{SL}{\bigl({\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\bigr)}\ltimes\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
has linear part
\gamma
and translation part
\tau
, the total support function of
\sigma K
, that we denote by
\sigma\cdot\tilde{s}
, satisfies

(\sigma\cdot\tilde{s})(Y)=\tilde{s}(\gamma^{-1}\star Y)+\tau\cdot Y.

Thus
K
is
\tau
-convex if and only if
\tilde{s}
is
\tau
-equivariant, in the sense that for all
Y\in\mathcal{C}^{*}
and
\gamma\in\Gamma
, it satisfies

(5.3)

\tilde{s}(Y)=\tilde{s}(\gamma^{-1}\star Y)+\tau_{\gamma}\cdot Y.

Proof.

For all
Y\in\mathcal{C}^{*}
and
\sigma=(\gamma,\tau)\in\mathrm{SL}{\bigl({\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\bigr)}\ltimes\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
,

\displaystyle(\sigma\cdot\tilde{s})(Y)

\displaystyle=\sup_{X\in\sigma K}X\cdot Y=\sup_{X\in K}\sigma X\cdot Y,

\displaystyle=\sup_{X\in K}\gamma X\cdot Y+\tau\cdot Y,

\displaystyle=\sup_{X\in K}X\cdot\gamma^{\top}Y+\tau\cdot Y,

\displaystyle=\tilde{s}(\gamma^{-1}\star Y)+\tau\cdot Y.\qed

Using the radial identification
R^{*}
(Remark
4.9
) and the
\Gamma
-invariance of the affine sphere
\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}}^{*}
, we get the following.

Corollary 5.7
.

Let
K
be a
\mathcal{C}
-convex domain with support function
s
. If
\sigma\in\mathrm{SL}{\bigl({\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\bigr)}\ltimes\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
has linear part
\gamma
and translation part
\tau
, the
\Omega^{*}
-support function of
\sigma K
, that we denote by
\sigma\cdot s
, satisfies

(\sigma\cdot s)(y)=\frac{\omega_{\mathcal{C}}(y)}{\omega_{\mathcal{C}}(\gamma^{-1}*y)}s(\gamma^{-1}*y)+\tau\cdot(y,-1).

Thus
K
is
\tau
-convex if and only if its support function
s
is
\tau
-equivariant, in the sense that for all
y\in\Omega^{*}
and
\gamma\in\Gamma
, it satisfies

(5.4)

s(y)=\frac{\omega_{\mathcal{C}}(y)}{\omega_{\mathcal{C}}(\gamma^{-1}*y)}s(\gamma^{-1}*y)+\tau_{\gamma}\cdot(y,-1).

Proposition 5.8
.

Let
K
be a
\tau
-convex domain. Its Gauss map is an equivariant set valued map: with respect to the action of
\Gamma_{\tau}
on
\partial K
and the action of
\Gamma
on
\Omega^{*}
, i.e. for all
X\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
and
\gamma\in\Gamma
, we have the set equality

\mathcal{G}_{K}(\gamma X+\tau_{\gamma})=\gamma*\mathcal{G}_{K}(X)\coloneqq\left\{\gamma*y\;\middle|\;y\in\mathcal{G}_{K}(X)\right\}.

If moreover
K
has a strictly convex support function, its Gauss map is a true equivariant map: with respect to the action of
\Gamma_{\tau}
on
\partial K
and the action of
\Gamma
on
\Omega^{*}
, i.e. for all

Proposition 5.10

Proposition 5.11

5.6.
The two maximal domains

Notice that, up to now, both the group
\Gamma
and the cone
\mathcal{C}
it divides were fixed, but if
\Gamma
divides the cone
\mathcal{C}
it also divides its opposite

. The two affine spacetime structures
{\bigl({\mathbb{R}^{d+1},\mathcal{C}}\bigr)}
and
{\bigl({\mathbb{R}^{d+1},-\mathcal{C}}\bigr)}
are linked: the notions of
\mathcal{C}
-null and
\mathcal{C}
-spacelike hyperplane are the same as the notions of

-null and
(-\mathcal{C})
-spacelike hyperplane, but time is “reversed” as the notion of
\mathcal{C}
-future coincides with the notion of
(-\mathcal{C})
-past. Thus, to
\Gamma_{\tau}
an affine deformation of
\Gamma
we can associate two domains

and

.

Notice that using the
\mathcal{C}
-duality,
(-\mathcal{C})
-convex domains are given by concave support functions on
\Omega^{*}
. In Proposition
5.12
we have seen that both convex and concave
\tau
-equivariant functions on
\Omega^{*}
have the same continuous extension

on
\partial\Omega^{*}
. Thus the two domains come from considering:

•

s_{\tau}^{-}:\Omega^{*}\penalty\ \longrightarrow\penalty\ \mathbb{R}
(that we previously only denoted as
s_{\tau}
) the pointwise largest convex function on
\Omega^{*}
with graph under the graph of
g_{\tau}
on
\partial\Omega^{*}
, i.e.

s_{\tau}^{-}(y)=\sup\left\{a(y)\;\middle|\;a\textup{ is affine and }a\leq g_{\tau}\textup{ on }\partial\Omega^{*}\right\},

•

s_{\tau}^{+}:\Omega^{*}\penalty\ \longrightarrow\penalty\ \mathbb{R}
the pointwise smallest concave function on
\Omega^{*}
with graph over the graph of
g_{\tau}
on
\partial\Omega^{*}
, i.e.

s_{\tau}^{+}(y)=\inf\left\{a(y)\;\middle|\;a\textup{ is affine and }a\geq g_{\tau}\textup{ on }\partial\Omega^{*}\right\}.

Then, the graphs of

and
s_{\tau}^{+}
bound
\mathrm{CH}^{*}(\tau)
, the convex hull of
\graph(g_{\tau})
inside the dual space
{\bigl({\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\bigr)}^{*}

\mathrm{CH}^{*}(\tau)=\left\{(y,\mu)\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\;\middle|\;y\in\Omega^{*},s_{\tau}^{-}(y)\leq\mu\leq s_{\tau}^{+}(y)\right\}.

Consequently, by Proposition

, the two domains

and

can be expressed as, respectively, the intersection of the
\mathcal{C}
-future, and the intersection of the

-future (i.e. the
\mathcal{C}
-past), of the same family of
\mathcal{C}
-null affine hyperplanes given by the boundary function

associated with

. That implies that

and

are disjoint. One can also easily check that

.

• Now, let us prove that those leaves are disjoint. To see that those spacelike graphs are disjoint it suffices to notice that if

• Finally, let us show that those leaves foliate the whole

Proposition 8.8
.

Let
(\mathrm{dev},\rho)
be pair of developing and holonomy maps of a GHCC affine spacetime
(M,\mathcal{C}^{M})

\mathbb{R}^{d+1}

\tau
-convex domain

{\bigl({\mathbb{R}^{d+1},\mathcal{C}}\bigr)}

Proof.

Let us set

to be compact locally uniformly convex Cauchy surface of

First notice that, using Proposition

and Proposition

, we have
D{\bigl({\mathrm{dev}{\bigl({\widetilde{S}}\bigr)}}\bigr)}=D_{\tau}

Now, let

be an inextensible
\mathcal{C}
-causal curve in

Let

Proposition 8.9

Let us work in the setting of Proposition

, there is a unique

, there are real numbers

8.3.

In this subsection, we use the preceding work in order to prove the maximality of spacetimes built with Theorem

Let

\Gamma_{\tau}<\mathrm{SL}{\bigl({\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\bigr)}\ltimes\mathbb{R}^{d+1}

Appendix A
Examples of dividing groups admitting affine deformations

This appendix is devoted to the construction of examples proving the following proposition.

Proposition A.1
.

In any dimension
d\geq 2
, there exists a discrete subgroup
\Gamma<\mathrm{SL}{\bigl({\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\bigr)}
dividing a proper convex cone
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
over a proper convex domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{P}{\bigl({\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\bigr)}
which is not projectively equivalent to an ellipse (giving the Klein ball model of the hyperbolic space).

Bending of holonomy groups

An amalgamated product
\Gamma=\Gamma^{\prime}*_{\Lambda}\Gamma^{\prime\prime}<\mathrm{SL}{\bigl({\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\bigr)}
can be algebraically deformed through the following general procedure called
bending
and deeply studied by Johnson and Millson
[
JM87
]
. Let
{\bigl({A_{s}}\bigr)}_{s\in\mathbb{R}}
be a continuous path of elements of
\mathrm{SL}{\bigl({\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\bigr)}
such that
A_{0}
is the identity and every
A_{s}
commutes with all the elements of
\Lambda
. Then the bending procedure consists in continuously deforming
\Gamma
by looking at the family
\Gamma_{s}=\Gamma^{\prime}*_{\Lambda}{\bigl({A_{s}\Gamma^{\prime\prime}A_{s}^{-1}}\bigr)}
.

Bulging the holonomy groups of hyperbolic manifolds

Let
\Gamma_{0}<\mathrm{SO}_{0}(d,1)
be the holonomy group of a closed hyperbolic
d
-dimensional manifold
M=\mathbb{B}^{d}/\Gamma_{0}
(using the Klein ball projective model of the hyperbolic space) containing a totally geodesic embedded compact connected hypersurface
N
separating
M
into two connected components:
M\setminus N=M^{\prime}\sqcup M^{\prime\prime}
. We shall deform
\Gamma_{0}
through a particular bending procedure called
bulging
(following the nomenclature in
[
BV24
]
).

Using the inclusion
i:\pi_{1}N\hookrightarrow\pi_{1}M
and taking the image by the holonomy of
\pi_{1}N
we get a subgroup
\Lambda<\Gamma_{0}
preserving a hyperplane
H
intersecting the Minkowski light cone
\mathcal{C}_{0}
. Then
H\cap{\bigl({\mathbb{B}^{d}\times\{1\}}\bigr)}
is a connected preimage of
N
by the projection
\mathbb{B}^{d}\times\{1\}\penalty\ \longrightarrow\penalty\ M=\mathbb{B}^{d}/\Gamma_{0}
and a universal cover over
N
. For
k\in\{1,2\}
, using the inclusion
i^{(k)}:\pi_{1}M^{(k)}\hookrightarrow\pi_{1}M
and setting
\Gamma_{0}^{(k)}
to be the holonomy of
\pi_{1}M^{(k)}
, by the Seifert–van Kampen Theorem we can see the holonomy group of
M
as the amalgamated product
\Gamma_{0}=\Gamma_{0}^{\prime}*_{\Lambda}\Gamma_{0}^{\prime\prime}
.

Let
X\in\mathbb{R}^{d,1}=\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
be a unit Minkowski spacelike vector orthogonal to the hyperplane
H
. For later constructions, it is worth noting that
X
is a common eigenvector, with associated eigenvalue
1
, of all elements of
\Lambda

This appendix offers a more projective approach to affine deformations in order to link the work of this article to the notion of convex cocompact representation

Let
\Gamma

\left\{\begin{matrix}{\mathrm{SL}{\bigl({\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\bigr)}\ltimes\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}&\lhook\joinrel\longrightarrow&{\mathrm{PGL}{\bigl({\mathbb{R}^{d+2}}\bigr)}}\\

Proof.

We decompose the boundary of

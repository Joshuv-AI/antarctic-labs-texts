The Balmer spectrum and tensor telescope conjecture for noetherian path algebras Thanks: The author was supported by the Department of Mathematics “Tullio Levi-Civita” of the University of Padova
Enrico Sabatini

Abstract
. Given a commutative noetherian ring R R and a finite acyclic quiver Q Q , we study the tensor triangulated category 𝒟 ⁡ ( R ​ Q ) \mathcal{D}(RQ) endowed with the vertexwise tensor product. We find a description of the internal hom functor and show that the category is not rigid. We compute its Balmer spectrum and, despite the non-rigidity, we get a classification of all the thick tensor-ideals and a stratification result. After introducing the notion of tensor-t-structure, we give a classification of the compactly generated ones and prove the tensor telescope conjecture.

Introduction

Tensor triangulated categories (
tt-categories
) arise naturally in homotopy theory, algebraic geometry and modular representation theory. They appear, for example, as “unital algebraic stable homotopy categories” in
[
18
]
, “symmetric monoidal presentable stable
\infty
-categories” in
[
28
]
and “stable module categories of finite groups” in
[
10
]
. A seminal contribution to this field is due to Balmer
[
7
]
and Balmer-Favi
[
6
]
, where “small” and “big” tt-categories are considered – namely,
essentially small
tt-categories and
rigidly-compactly generated
tt-categories. In particular, every “big” tt-category yields a “small” one via its full triangulated subcategory of (rigid-)compact objects. The most interesting feature of a “small” tt-category is the
Balmer spectrum
, which, analogously to the prime spectrum of a commutative ring, is both a topological space and a poset encoding structural information about the category. It provides a notion of support for objects and allows for the classification of relevant tensor-compatible subcategories. As for “big” tt-categories, thanks to
rigidity
, the Balmer spectrum of the subcategory of compact objects provides a well-founded notion of support also for objects that are not necessarily compact. The ability of the Balmer spectrum to classify the relevant tensor-compatible subcategories of a “big” tt-category is known as
stratification
, and it is not always satisfied (see
[
9
]
for a foundational work on this topic).

In this paper we consider the derived category of representations of a finite quiver over a commutative noetherian ring endowed with the (derived) vertexwise tensor product. This turns out to be a tensor triangulated category. In this framework, we compute the
Balmer spectrum
of its compact objects and derive several
tt-classifications
. This computation is noteworthy because, although the category is compactly generated, its compact objects do not form a rigid tt-category. Explicit computations of Balmer spectra in such non-rigid situations are rare – see
[
27
,
40
,
30
,
5
]
– and, among these, only the first two consider compact objects of a non-rigidly compactly generated tt-category.

The relevance of our example also lies in the fact that, despite the lack of rigidity, it is still the subcategory of compact objects that captures the relevant information about the ambient category. Usually, this is the expected behavior in rigidly-compactly generated tt-categories
\mathcal{T}
, where the subcategory of compact objects
\mathcal{T}^{c}
coincide with the subcategory of rigid objects
\mathcal{T}^{d}
. However we are not in such situation. In fact, when
\mathcal{T}^{c}
fails to be rigid – or even to form a tt-subcategory, as it may happen when
\mathcal{T}
is not a compactly generated tt-category – it is generally regarded as more appropriate, as suggested by
[
8
]
, to consider the spectrum of rigid objects
\Spc(\mathcal{T}^{d})
rather than the one of the compacts. For example,
[
25
]
exhibits big tt-categories where either
\mathcal{T}^{c}\subsetneq\mathcal{T}^{d}
or
\mathcal{T}^{d}\subsetneq\mathcal{T}^{c}
, and in both instances the meaningful spectrum is
\Spc(\mathcal{T}^{d})
. Our example suggests that the recourse to rigid objects may be relevant only when
\mathcal{T}
is not a compactly generated tt-category, e.g. when
\mathcal{T}^{c}\neq\mathcal{T}^{d}
.

We show that the Balmer spectrum
\Spc(\mathcal{D}^{c}(RQ))
is a disjoint union of copies of the prime spectrum
\Spec(R)
, extending the result of
[
27
]
, and that its specialization-closed subsets classify all thick tensor-ideals of
\mathcal{D}^{c}(RQ)
.

(

For any commutative noetherian ring

Next, we turn to the recently introduced notion of tensor-t-structures, defined with respect to a fixed tensor-closed suspended subcategory
\mathcal{T}^{\leq 0}
containing the tensor unit. In this framework, one can formulate the
tensor telescope conjecture
, which asks whether every homotopically smashing tensor-t-structure is compactly generated. We provide a classification of compactly generated tensor-t-structures with respect to the standard aisle
\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0}(RQ)
in terms of filtrations of specialization-closed subsets of the Balmer spectrum (see
3.5
), and we establish the following result:

Theorem C

(Theorems
4.4
and
4.9
)
.

For any commutative noetherian ring
R
and finite acyclic quiver
Q
, the derived category
\mathcal{D}(RQ)
satisfies the telescope conjecture for tensor-t-structures with respect to
\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0}(RQ)

4.5

This result not only generalizes
[

– which plays a crucial role in its proof – but also marks a significant step toward establishing the telescope conjecture for representations of finite acyclic simply-laced quivers over commutative noetherian rings.

1.
Preliminaries

1.1.
Tensor triangulated categories

Let
(\mathcal{T},\otimes,\mathbf{1})
be a
tensor triangulated category
(
tt-category
) in the sense of
[
7
]
, i.e. a triangulated category
\mathcal{T}
with a symmetric monoidal product
\otimes:\mathcal{T}\times\mathcal{T}\to\mathcal{T}
with unit
\mathbf{1}\in\mathcal{T}
and such that for any
X\in\mathcal{T}
the functor
X\otimes\_:\mathcal{T}\to\mathcal{T}
is triangulated. Recall that
\mathcal{T}
is a
compactly generated tt-category
if:

(i)

\mathcal{T}
is a compactly generated triangulated category, i.e. it has coproducts and the subcategory
\mathcal{T}^{c}
of its compact objects is skeletally small and generates
\mathcal{T}
as a localizing subcategory;

(ii)

The tensor product
\otimes:\mathcal{T}\times\mathcal{T}\to\mathcal{T}
preserves coproducts;

(iii)

\mathcal{T}^{c}
is a
tt-subcategory
, i.e. the tensor product restricts to
\otimes:\mathcal{T}^{c}\times\mathcal{T}^{c}\to\mathcal{T}^{c}
and
\mathbf{1}\in\mathcal{T}^{c}
.

In this setting, by Brown representability
[
33
, Theorem 4.1]
, the tensor product
\otimes:\mathcal{T}\times\mathcal{T}\to\mathcal{T}
is a
closed
monoidal product, that is the functor
X\otimes\_
admits a right adjoint
[X,\_]:\mathcal{T}\to\mathcal{T}
for all
X\in\mathcal{T}
. We call the bifunctor
[\_,\_]:\mathcal{T}\times\mathcal{T}\to\mathcal{T}
the
internal hom
of
\mathcal{T}
. Following
[
6
]
, a compactly generated tt-category
\mathcal{T}
is called
rigidly-compactly generated
if:

(iv)

The internal hom restricts to
[\_,\_]:\mathcal{T}^{c}\times\mathcal{T}^{c}\to\mathcal{T}^{c}
;

(v)

\mathcal{T}^{c}
is a
rigid tt-category
, i.e. for any
X,Y\in\mathcal{T}^{c}
the
evaluation map

[X,\mathbf{1}]\otimes Y\to[X,Y]
is an isomorphism.

The objects
X\in\mathcal{T}
satisfying the property (v) for any
Y\in\mathcal{T}
are called
rigid
objects of
\mathcal{T}
. Note that, by
[
18
, Theorem 2.1.3 (d)]
, the condition (v) implies that compact objects and rigid objects coincide in
\mathcal{T}
. Moreover, denoting by
X^{\vee}
the object
[X,\mathbf{1}]
, by
[
26
, III.1.3 (i)]
, any rigid object
X
is
dualizable
, i.e.
X\cong(X^{\vee})^{\vee}
, and it is a direct summand of
X^{\vee}\otimes X\otimes X
.

From now on, let
R
be a commutative noetherian ring,
Q
a finite acyclic quiver and denote by
RQ
the path algebra of
Q
over
R
. Recall that the category of modules
\Mods(RQ)
is equivalent to the category of representations of
Q=(Q_{0},Q_{1})
by
R
-modules, i.e. the category with objects
X=(X_{i},X_{\alpha})_{i\in Q_{0},\,\alpha\in Q_{1}}
, where
X_{i}
is an
R
-module for any vertex
i\in Q_{0}
and
X_{\alpha}:X_{i}\to X_{j}
is an
R
-linear map for any arrow
\alpha:i\to j\in Q_{1}
, and morphisms
f:X\to Y
where
f=(f_{i}:X_{i}\to Y_{i})_{i\in Q_{0}}
such that
f_{j}\circ X_{\alpha}=Y_{\alpha}\circ f_{i}
for any arrow
\alpha:i\to j\in Q_{1}
.

Given two
RQ
-modules
X=(X_{i},X_{\alpha})
and
Y=(Y_{i},Y_{\alpha})
, we define a symmetric tensor product as
X\boxtimes_{RQ}Y=(X_{i}\otimes_{R}Y_{i},X_{\alpha}\otimes_{R}Y_{\alpha})
. In the following we will consider the tt-category
(\mathcal{D}(RQ),\boxtimes_{RQ}^{\mathbf{L}},\mathbf{U})
, where
\mathcal{D}(RQ)
is the derived category of the module category
\Mods(RQ)
, the symmetric monoidal structure is given by the left derived functor of
\boxtimes_{RQ}
and the tensor unit
\mathbf{U}(=\mathbf{U}[0])
is the stalk complex defined by
\mathbf{U}_{i}=R
and
\mathbf{U}_{\alpha}=\id_{R}
for any
i\in Q_{0}
and
\alpha\in Q_{1}
.

Remark 1.1
.

Note that the tensor unit is not a generator of the triangulated category, indeed
\loc_{RQ}\langle\mathbf{U}\rangle\neq\mathcal{D}(RQ)
. There are many results available for tt-categories
(\mathcal{T},\otimes,\mathbf{1})
such that
\loc_{\mathcal{T}}\langle\mathbf{1}\rangle=\mathcal{T}
, which we can not therefore use in our context. In particular, a localizing (resp. thick) subcategory of
\mathcal{D}(RQ)
(resp.
\mathcal{D}^{c}(RQ)
) will not be automatically a localizing (resp. thick) tensor-ideal of
\mathcal{D}(RQ)
(resp.
\mathcal{D}^{c}(RQ)
) – see
Section

2
for definitions.

Proposition 1.2
.

The tt-category
(\mathcal{D}(RQ),\boxtimes_{RQ}^{\mathbf{L}},\mathbf{U})
is compactly generated. In particular,
(\mathcal{D}^{c}(RQ),\boxtimes_{RQ},\mathbf{U})
is an essentially small tt-category.

Proof.

The derived category
\mathcal{D}(RQ)
is a compactly generated triangulated category with compact objects
\mathcal{K}^{b}(\proj(RQ))
and the tensor product
\boxtimes_{RQ}
clearly commutes with coproducts. Recall from
[
16
, Theorem 3.1]
that an
RQ
-module
P
is projective if and only if, for any
i\in Q_{0}
,
P_{i}
is projective and the map

\bigoplus\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in Q_{1}\\
t(\alpha)=i\end{subarray}}P_{s(\alpha)}\to P_{i}

is a split monomorphism – where
s(\alpha)
and
t(\alpha)
denote respectively the source and the target of the arrow
\alpha
. It follows easily that, given two projective
RQ
-module
P
and
P^{\prime}
, the tensor product
P\boxtimes_{RQ}P^{\prime}
is again projective. Since for any two compact objects
X,X^{\prime}\in\mathcal{D}^{c}(RQ)
, the product
X\boxtimes_{RQ}^{\mathbf{L}}X^{\prime}
is isomorphic to the
\oplus
-totalization of the double complex
X^{i}\boxtimes_{R}X^{\prime j}
, it is compact too. Finally, by
[
13
, Lemma 3.1 (i)]
, the
RQ
-module
\mathbf{U}
has projective dimension less or equal then
1
and, being finitely presented, it is compact.
∎

By Brown representability, it follows from
1.2
that the category
\mathcal{D}(RQ)
is endowed with an internal hom, which we will denote by
\chom_{RQ}(\_,\_):\mathcal{D}(RQ)\times\mathcal{D}(RQ)\to\mathcal{D}(RQ)
.

For any
i\in Q_{0}
, let
P(i)
be the projective
RQ
-module such that, for any vertex
k
,
P(i)_{k}
is the free
R
-module with basis the set of all paths in
Q
from
i
to
k
and, for any arrow
\beta:k\to\ell
,
P(i)_{\beta}:P(i)_{k}\to P(i)_{\ell}
is given by left multiplication by
\beta
; for any
\alpha:i\to j\in Q_{1}
, let
\iota_{\alpha}:P(j)\to P(i)
be the monomorphism given by right multiplication by
\alpha
. Then:

Theorem 1.3
.

Given left
RQ
-modules
X
and
Y
, the internal hom is defined by

\chom_{RQ}(X,Y):=\left(\Hom_{RQ}(X\boxtimes_{RQ}P(i),Y),\,\_\circ(\id_{X}\boxtimes_{RQ}\,\iota_{\alpha})\right)_{i\in Q_{0},\,\alpha\in Q_{1}}

Proof.

By
[
38
, Proposition IV.10.1]
, for any left
RQ
-module
X
, the functor
X\boxtimes_{RQ}\_
is isomorphic to the functor
(X\boxtimes_{RQ}RQ)\otimes_{RQ}\_
. Here the module
X\boxtimes_{RQ}RQ
is seen as an
RQ
-bimodule where, denoting by
\rho_{\omega}
the right multiplication in
RQ
by a path
\omega
, the right
RQ
-module structure of
X\boxtimes_{RQ}RQ
is given by
\cdot\omega:=\id_{X}\boxtimes_{RQ}\,\rho_{\omega}
. Thus, the functor
X\boxtimes_{RQ}\_
is left adjoint to the functor
\Hom_{RQ}(X\boxtimes_{RQ}RQ,\_)
. For any left
RQ
-module

, the left
RQ
-module structure on
\Hom_{RQ}(X\boxtimes_{RQ}RQ,Y)
is defined by
\omega\cdot(h_{i})_{i\in Q_{0}}=(h_{i}\circ(\id_{X}\boxtimes_{RQ}\,\rho_{\omega}))_{i\in Q_{0}}
. In particular, at any vertex
i\in Q_{0}
it is represented by the
R
-module
\varepsilon_{i}\cdot\Hom_{RQ}(X\boxtimes_{RQ}RQ,Y)=\Hom_{RQ}(X\boxtimes_{RQ}RQ\cdot\varepsilon_{i},Y)=\Hom_{RQ}(X\boxtimes_{RQ}P(i),Y)
.
∎

Proposition 1.4
.

The internal hom restricts to
\mathcal{D}^{c}(RQ)
.

Proof.

For any two finitely generated projective
RQ
-modules
P
and
P^{\prime}
, by
1.2
the
RQ
-module
P\boxtimes_{RQ}P(i)
is finitely generated projective too, and, by
[

,
\Hom_{RQ}(P\boxtimes_{RQ}P(i),P^{\prime})
is a finitely generated projective

-module. Thus,
\chom_{RQ}(P,P^{\prime})
is an

, Lemma 3.1 (i)]
, it is compact in
\mathcal{D}(RQ)
. Since, for any two compact objects

, the complex
\chom_{RQ}(X,X^{\prime})

We will prove in the next proposition that the tt-category
(\mathcal{D}^{c}(RQ),\boxtimes_{RQ},\mathbf{U})
is not rigid whenever the quiver

Proposition 1.5
.

For a non-trivial connected finite acyclic quiver

, the tt-category

Proof.

Any finite acyclic quiver has a source, say

. Let

be the representation such that,

and

2.
The Balmer spectrum

A

of a tt-category
\mathcal{T}
is a thick subcategory
\mathcal{S}
such that

for any
X\in\mathcal{T}
and
S\in\mathcal{S}
. We will denote the smallest thick tensor-ideal containing a set of objects
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathcal{T}
by
\langle\mathcal{X}\rangle
, or by
\langle X\rangle
when
\mathcal{X}={X}
. A proper thick tensor-ideal
\mathcal{S}
is called
prime
if whenever a product
X\otimes Y
lies in
\mathcal{S}
, then either
X\in\mathcal{S}
or
Y\in\mathcal{S}
, or equivalently, if its complement
\mathcal{T}\setminus\mathcal{S}
is
\otimes
-closed. The
Balmer spectrum
of an essentially small tt-category
\mathcal{T}
is the set of the prime thick tensor-ideals of
\mathcal{T}
, and it will be denoted by
\Spc(\mathcal{T})
. It has both a poset structure, given by inclusion, and a topological structure, given by the following basis of open subsets:
\left\{\{\mathcal{S}\in\Spc(\mathcal{T})\mid X\in\mathcal{S}\}\mid X\in\mathcal{T}\right\}
. In this context, a
specialization closed subset
of
\Spc(\mathcal{T})
is equivalently a lower subset for the poset structure, or an arbitrary union of closed subsets for the topological structure. By
[
7
, Theorem 4.10, Remark 4.3, Remark 4.11]
, when
\Spc(\mathcal{T})
is a noetherian topological space and
\mathcal{T}
is a rigid category, the specialization closed subsets of the Balmer spectrum fully classify the thick tensor-ideals of
\mathcal{T}
.

Recall that the
big support
of a complex
M\in\mathcal{D}(R)
is defined as

\Supp_{R}(M)=\{\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec(R)\mid R_{\mathfrak{p}}\otimesL M\neq 0\}

and we denote by
K(\mathfrak{p})
the Koszul complex at
\mathfrak{p}
. The first part of next proposition follows similarly to
[
27
, Lemma 2.1.4.1]
.

Proposition 2.1
.

For any finite and acyclic quiver
Q
and
X\in\mathcal{D}(RQ)
, it holds that

(1)

\langle X\rangle=\thick_{RQ}\left\langle X(i)\mid i\in Q_{0}\right\rangle
;

(2)

If
X
is compact,
\langle X\rangle=\thick_{RQ}\left\langle i_{\times}K(\mathfrak{p})\mid\mathfrak{p}\in\Supp_{R}(i^{\ast}X),i\in Q_{0}\right\rangle
.

In particular, any thick tensor-ideal of
\mathcal{D}^{c}(RQ)
is generated by complexes of the form
i_{\times}K(\mathfrak{p})
for some
i\in Q_{0}
and
\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec(R)
.

Proof.

(1) Since any finite acyclic quiver has a source and a sink, the
RQ
-module
U
admits a filtration whose composition factors are isomorphic to the representations
U(i)
. Tensoring this filtration by a complex
X\in\mathcal{D}(RQ)
it follows that
X\in\thick_{RQ}\left\langle X(i)\mid i\in Q_{0}\right\rangle
. Moreover, the latter is a tensor-ideal. Indeed, since thick subcategories of
\mathcal{D}(R)
are tensor-ideals, for any object
Z\in\mathcal{D}(RQ)
,
i^{\ast}Z\otimes_{R}i^{\ast}X
lies in the thick subcategory generated by
i^{\ast}X
. Thus, since
Z\boxtimes_{RQ}X(i)=Z(i)\boxtimes_{RQ}X(i)
, by
1.7
,
Z\boxtimes_{RQ}X(i)=i_{\times}(i^{\ast}Z\otimes_{R}i^{\ast}X)
which lies in the thick subcategory generated by
i_{\times}i^{\ast}X
, i.e.
\thick_{RQ}\left\langle X(i)\right\rangle
. On the other hand, by the closure under tensor product, we have
X(i)=U(i)\boxtimes_{RQ}X\in\left\langle X\right\rangle
for any
i\in Q_{0}
.

(2) If
X\in\mathcal{D}^{c}(RQ)
, by
[
32
, Theorem 1.5]
, we have that the thick subcategory
\thick_{R}\langle i^{\ast}X\rangle
is equal to
\thick_{R}\left\langle K(\mathfrak{p})\mid\mathfrak{p}\in\Supp_{R}(i^{\ast}X)\right\rangle
. Then, applying the functor
i_{\times}
, by
1.7
, we can conclude that

\thick_{RQ}\langle X(i)\rangle=\thick_{RQ}\left\langle i_{\times}K(\mathfrak{p})\mid\mathfrak{p}\in\Supp_{R}(i^{\ast}X)\right\rangle

Thus, combining this with point (1) gives the statement.

As for the last part, it is sufficient to note that, given a set of objects
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathcal{D}^{c}(RQ)
, the thick tensor-ideal
\langle\mathcal{X}\rangle
is equal to
\thick_{RQ}\left\langle i_{\times}K(\mathfrak{p})\mid\mathfrak{p}\in\Supp_{R}(i^{\ast}X),X\in\mathcal{X},i\in Q_{0}\right\rangle
.
∎

For any prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec(R)
and
i\in Q_{0}
, define the functor
\xi_{\mathfrak{p},i}:=R_{\mathfrak{p}}\otimes_{R}i^{\ast}(\_)
such that

\xi_{\mathfrak{p},i}:\mathcal{D}^{c}(RQ)\to\mathcal{D}^{c}(R_{\mathfrak{p}})

X\mapsto i^{\ast}X_{\mathfrak{p}}

Denote by
\mathcal{S}_{\mathfrak{p},i}:=\ker\xi_{\mathfrak{p},i}=\{X\in\mathcal{D}^{c}(RQ)\mid i^{*}X_{\mathfrak{p}}=0\}
. Note that, since the functor
\xi_{\mathfrak{p},i}
is exact and commutes with
\boxtimes_{RQ}
, each subcategory
\mathcal{S}_{\mathfrak{p},i}
is a thick tensor-ideal. In particular, by
2.1
(2):

\mathcal{S}_{\mathfrak{p},i}=\thick_{RQ}\left\langle\{i_{\times}K(\mathfrak{q})\mid\mathfrak{q}\nsubseteq\mathfrak{p}\}\cup\{j_{\times}K(\mathfrak{q})\mid j\neq i,\mathfrak{q}\in\Spec(R)\}\right\rangle

In
[
27
]
, the authors prove that for any field
\mathbb{K}
, the Balmer spectrum of
(\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{K}Q),\boxtimes_{\mathbb{K}Q})
is a discrete space consisting of as many points as the vertices of the quiver
Q
. In the following theorem, we generalize
[
27
, Theorem 2.1.5.1]
to any commutative noetherian ring, showing that in our context the Balmer spectrum is a disjoint union of as many copies of the prime spectrum
\Spec(R)
as the number of vertices of
Q
. Moreover, the following theorem also provides a direct proof, when
Q=A_{1}
, of the homeomorphism between the Balmer spectrum of
\mathcal{D}^{c}(R)
and the prime spectrum of the ring
\Spec(R)
, which originally follows by
[
7
, Corollary 5.6]
.

Theorem 2.2
.

For any commutative noetherian ring
R
and finite acyclic quiver
Q
, we have that

(1)

\Spc(\mathcal{D}^{c}(RQ))=\{\mathcal{S}_{\mathfrak{p},i}\mid\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec(R),i\in Q_{0}\}
;

(2)

There is an order-reversing homeomorphism

f:\Spc(\mathcal{D}^{c}(RQ))\longrightarrow\Spec(R)\times Q_{0}

i.e.
\Spc(\mathcal{D}^{c}(RQ))\overset{f}{\cong}_{\mathrm{\,Top}}\Spec(R)\times Q_{0}
and
\Spc(\mathcal{D}^{c}(RQ))\overset{f}{\cong}_{\,\Pos}\Spec(R)^{\mathrm{op}}\times Q_{0}
.

Proof.

(1) Let us start by checking that each thick tensor-ideal
\mathcal{S}_{\mathfrak{p},i}
is prime by showing that their complements are
\boxtimes
-closed. Given two complexes
X,Y\in\mathcal{D}^{c}(RQ)\setminus\mathcal{S}_{\mathfrak{p},i}
, since
\mathcal{S}_{\mathfrak{p},i}=\ker\xi_{\mathfrak{p},i}
, the complexes
i^{\ast}X_{\mathfrak{p}}
and
i^{\ast}Y_{\mathfrak{p}}
are non-zero, bounded and vertexwise finitely generated
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
-free. In particular, by the so-called Künneth formula (see
[
11
, 2.5.18 (c)]
), the top cohomology of
i^{\ast}X_{\mathfrak{p}}\otimes_{R}i^{\ast}Y_{\mathfrak{p}}
is the tensor product of the top cohomologies of
i^{\ast}X_{\mathfrak{p}}
and
i^{\ast}Y_{\mathfrak{p}}
and so, by the Nakayama’s lemma (see
[
4
, Exercise 2.3]
) it is non-zero. Thus, by
1.7
,
i^{\ast}(X\boxtimes_{RQ}Y)_{\mathfrak{p}}\neq 0
, i.e.
X\boxtimes_{RQ}Y
does not lie in
\mathcal{S}_{\mathfrak{p},i}
. Now let us prove that all thick tensor-ideal are of this form. Let
\mathcal{S}
be a prime thick tensor-ideal of
\mathcal{D}^{c}(RQ)
, by
2.1
(2) it is generated by a proper subset of
\mathcal{K}=\left\{i_{\times}K(\mathfrak{p})\mid\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec(R),i\in Q_{0}\right\}
. Let
\mathcal{S}^{\complement}=\mathcal{D}^{c}(RQ)\setminus\mathcal{S}
be the complement of
\mathcal{S}
and consider the set
\mathcal{K}\cap\mathcal{S}^{\complement}
. Since
\mathcal{S}
is proper, there exists a vertex
\bar{i}\in Q_{0}
such that
U\left(\bar{i}\right)\in\mathcal{S}^{\complement}
(otherwise
U\in\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{D}^{c}(RQ)
). Since
\mathcal{S}^{\complement}
is
\boxtimes
-closed and
0\notin\mathcal{S}^{\complement}
, we have that
\mathcal{K}\cap\mathcal{S}^{\complement}\subseteq\left\{\bar{i}_{\times}K(\mathfrak{p})\mid\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec(R)\right\}
(indeed for any
j\neq\bar{i}
and
\mathfrak{q}\in\Spec(R)
, we have
j_{\times}K(\mathfrak{q})\boxtimes_{RQ}\bar{i}_{\times}K(\mathfrak{p})=0
). Moreover, since
R
is noetherian, the set
\{\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec(R)\mid\bar{i}_{\times}K(\mathfrak{p})\in\mathcal{S}^{\complement}\}
has a maximal element, say
\bar{\mathfrak{p}}
, and for any
\mathfrak{q}\subseteq\bar{\mathfrak{p}}
the complex
\bar{i}_{\times}K(\mathfrak{q})\in\mathcal{S}^{\complement}
(otherwise, by
[
32
, Lemma 1.2]
,
K\left(\bar{\mathfrak{p}}\right)\in\thick_{R}\langle K(\mathfrak{q})\rangle
and so
\bar{i}_{\times}K\left(\bar{\mathfrak{p}}\right)\in\mathcal{S}
). We end by proving that the maximal element
\bar{\mathfrak{p}}
is unique and so conclude that
\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{S}_{\bar{\mathfrak{p}},\bar{i}}
. Suppose that there is another maximal element
\bar{\mathfrak{q}}
, then:

(i)

If
\bar{\mathfrak{p}}+\bar{\mathfrak{q}}=R
, we have
K\left(\bar{\mathfrak{p}}\right)\otimes_{R}K\left(\bar{\mathfrak{q}}\right)=0
. Indeed, by
[
11
, 14.3.1]
, the tensor product is equal to
K\left(\bar{\mathfrak{p}}+\bar{\mathfrak{q}}\right)
which is an acyclic complex by
[
11
, 11.4.17]
. In particular, the tensor product
\bar{i}_{\times}K\left(\bar{\mathfrak{p}}\right)\boxtimes_{RQ}\bar{i}_{\times}K\left(\bar{\mathfrak{q}}\right)=0
lies in
\mathcal{S}
;

(ii)

If
\bar{\mathfrak{p}}+\bar{\mathfrak{q}}\neq R
, we have
K\left(\bar{\mathfrak{p}}\right)\otimes_{R}K\left(\bar{\mathfrak{q}}\right)=K\left(\bar{\mathfrak{p}}+\bar{\mathfrak{q}}\right)
by
[
11
, 14.3.1]
. Let us prove that, even in this case, the tensor product
\bar{i}_{\times}K\left(\bar{\mathfrak{p}}\right)\boxtimes_{RQ}\bar{i}_{\times}K\left(\bar{\mathfrak{q}}\right)=\bar{i}_{\times}K\left(\bar{\mathfrak{p}}+\bar{\mathfrak{q}}\right)
lies in
\mathcal{S}
. Indeed, there exists finitely many prime ideals
\mathfrak{m}_{1},\ldots,\mathfrak{m}_{N}
such that
\bar{\mathfrak{p}}+\bar{\mathfrak{q}}\subseteq\mathfrak{m}_{k}
for any
k=1,\ldots,N
and
V\left(\bar{\mathfrak{p}}+\bar{\mathfrak{q}}\right)=V(\mathfrak{m}_{1})\cup\ldots\cup V(\mathfrak{m}_{N})
(see
[
37
, Lemma 10.63.5-6]
). So, by maximality of
\bar{\mathfrak{p}}
, it holds that
\bar{i}_{\times}K(\mathfrak{m}_{k})\in\mathcal{S}
for any
k=1,\ldots,N
and, by
[
32
, Lemma 1.2]
, the complex
K\left(\bar{\mathfrak{p}}+\bar{\mathfrak{q}}\right)
lies in
\thick_{R}\langle K(\mathfrak{m}_{1})\oplus\ldots\oplus K(\mathfrak{m}_{N})\rangle
. Thus, it follows that
\bar{i}_{\times}K\left(\bar{\mathfrak{p}}+\bar{\mathfrak{q}}\right)\in\mathcal{S}
.

Since
\mathcal{S}^{\complement}
is
\boxtimes
-closed, both cases lead to a contradiction. Thus,
\mathcal{K}\cap\mathcal{S}^{\complement}=\left\{\bar{i}_{\times}K(\mathfrak{q})\mid\mathfrak{q}\subseteq\bar{\mathfrak{p}}\right\}
and so
\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{S}_{\bar{\mathfrak{p}},\bar{i}}
.

(2) Recall that the topology on
\Spc(\mathcal{D}^{c}(RQ))
is given by the basis of open subsets
\{\{\mathcal{S}_{\mathfrak{p},i}\mid X\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathfrak{p},i}\}\mid X\in\mathcal{D}^{c}(RQ)\}
, while the topology on
\Spec(R)\times Q_{0}
, where
Q_{0}
is taken with discrete topology, is given by the product topology, thus by the basis of open subsets
\{\{\mathfrak{p}\mid r\notin\mathfrak{p}\}\times\{i\}\mid r\in R,i\in Q_{0}\}
. We want to prove that the bijection given by the assignment
f:\mathcal{S}_{\mathfrak{p},i}\longmapsto(\mathfrak{p},i)
is a homeomorphism. Let us start proving the continuity. Given an open set of the basis in the codomain
\mathcal{O}=\{\mathfrak{p}\mid r\notin\mathfrak{p}\}\times\{i\}
, for an element of the ring
r\in R
, by
[
11
, 15.1.10]
, we have that
\Supp_{R}(K((r)))=V((r))
. Thus, it holds that

\xi_{\mathfrak{q},j}\left(i_{\times}K((r))\oplus\left(\textstyle{\bigoplus}_{\ell\neq i}\,\ell_{\times}R\right)\right)=0\text{ if and only if }j=i\text{ and }r\notin\mathfrak{q}

this implies
f^{-1}(\mathcal{O})=\left\{\mathcal{S}_{\mathfrak{p},i}\mid i_{\times}K((r))\oplus\left(\bigoplus_{\ell\neq i}\,\ell_{\times}R\right)\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathfrak{p},i}\right\}
which is an open set of the basis.

Moreover,
f
is also an open map. Indeed, given an open set of the basis in the domain
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}=\{\mathcal{S}_{\mathfrak{p},i}\mid X\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathfrak{p},i}\}
, for some complex
X\in\mathcal{D}^{c}(RQ)
, we have that

f(\mathcal{O}^{\prime})=\{(\mathfrak{p},i)\mid\xi_{\mathfrak{p},i}(X)=0\}=\bigcup_{i\in Q_{0}}\Supp_{R}(i^{*}X)^{\complement}\times\{i\}

Since the functor
i^{*}
preserves compacts and the support of a compact object of
\mathcal{D}(R)
is closed in
\Spec(R)
(see
[
11
, 14.1.6]
), it follows that
f(\mathcal{O}^{\prime})
is an open set.
As for the order-reversing property, note that for any
\mathcal{S}_{\mathfrak{q},j}\subseteq\mathcal{S}_{\mathfrak{p},i}
, we have that
\mathcal{K}\cap\mathcal{S}_{\mathfrak{p},i}^{\complement}\subseteq\mathcal{K}\cap\mathcal{S}_{\mathfrak{q},j}^{\complement}
. In particular, by the previous point, it implies that
i=j
and
\mathfrak{p}\subseteq\mathfrak{q}
, i.e.
(\mathfrak{p},i)\leq(\mathfrak{q},j)
.
∎

2.1.
Thick tensor-ideals

We saw in
1.5
that the tt-category
(\mathcal{D}^{c}(RQ),\boxtimes_{RQ},\mathbf{U})
is not rigid. In any case, rigidity is not a necessary condition to get a full classification of all the thick tensor-ideals. For this it suffices that all the thick tensor-ideals are radical.

Proposition 2.4
.

For any
X\in\mathcal{D}^{c}(RQ)
, it holds that
X\in\left\langle X\boxtimes_{RQ}X\right\rangle
. In particular, any thick tensor-ideal is radical.

Proof.

First note that the results holds in
\mathcal{D}^{c}(R)
, indeed there the tensor products
\otimes
and
\boxtimes
coincide and any object is
\otimes
-rigid. So, for any
X\in\mathcal{D}^{c}(RQ)
, we have that
\thick_{R}\left\langle i^{\ast}X\right\rangle=\thick_{R}\left\langle i^{\ast}X\otimes_{R}i^{\ast}X\right\rangle
and so
\thick_{RQ}\left\langle i_{\times}i^{\ast}X\right\rangle=\thick_{RQ}\left\langle i_{\times}(i^{\ast}X\otimes_{R}i^{\ast}X)\right\rangle
. Thus, by
1.7
, it follows that
\thick_{RQ}\left\langle X(i)\right\rangle=\thick_{RQ}\left\langle X(i)\boxtimes_{RQ}X(i)\right\rangle
. Combining this with
2.1
(1) we get the statement and, by
[
7
, Proposition 4.4]
, it follows that any thick tensor-ideal in
\mathcal{D}^{c}(RQ)
is radical.
∎

It follows the classification of the thick tensor-ideals of
(\mathcal{D}^{c}(RQ),\boxtimes_{RQ},\mathbf{U})
. Here we get the result from the general theory of tt-categories in
[
7
]
.

Corollary 2.5
.

For any commutative noetherian ring
R
and finite acyclic quiver
Q
, it holds that

\Thick_{\boxtimes}(\mathcal{D}^{c}(RQ))\longleftrightarrow\mathcal{V}(\Spc(\mathcal{D}^{c}(RQ)))

where
\mathcal{V}(\Spc(\mathcal{D}^{c}(RQ)))
is the set of specialization closed subsets of
\Spc(\mathcal{D}^{c}(RQ))
.

Proof.

By
2.2
, the Balmer spectrum
\Spc(\mathcal{D}^{c}(RQ))
is a finite union of noetherian spaces, indeed
\Spec(R)\times Q_{0}\cong\bigsqcup_{Q_{0}}\Spec(R)
, and so it is noetherian too. Thus, by
[
7
, Theorem 4.10, Remark 4.11]
and
2.4
, the thick tensor-ideals of
\mathcal{D}^{c}(RQ)
are in bijection with the specialization closed subsets of
\Spc(\mathcal{D}^{c}(RQ))
.
∎

We can also give two alternative classifications of the thick tensor-ideals of
\mathcal{D}^{c}(RQ)
, following from
2.5
. Here, we present a proof using the standard adjunction isomorphism in the category of posets:

\Hom_{\Pos}(X\times A,B)\cong\Hom_{\Pos}(A,\Hom_{\Pos}(X,B))\text{ for any three posets }X,A\text{ and }B

given by the assignment
\phi:f\mapsto\phi_{f}
, where
\phi_{f}(a)(x)=f((x,a))
, with inverse
\psi:g\mapsto\psi_{g}
, where
\psi_{g}((x,a))=g(a)(x)
. A direct proof also follows from the arguments used in
3.3
.

Corollary 2.6
.

For any commutative noetherian ring
R
and finite acyclic quiver
Q
, it holds that

(1)

\Thick_{\boxtimes}(\mathcal{D}^{c}(RQ))\cong\Thick(\mathcal{D}^{c}(R))\times Q_{0}
;

(2)

\Thick_{\boxtimes}(\mathcal{D}^{c}(RQ))\cong\Hom_{\Pos}(\Spec(R),\Thick_{\boxtimes}(\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{K}Q)))

Proof.

(1) Let
\{0,1\}
be the poset with two elements and the obvious order, then the specialization closed subsets of
\Spc(\mathcal{D}^{c}(RQ))
can be identified with
\Hom_{\Pos}(\Spc(\mathcal{D}^{c}(RQ)),\{0,1\})
by associating to each subset its characteristic function. From the standard adjunction isomorphism in the category of posets, we get that

\Hom_{\Pos}(\Spec(R)\times Q_{0},\{0,1\})\cong\Hom_{\Pos}(Q_{0},\Hom_{\Pos}(\Spec(R),\{0,1\}))

where, by
[
32
, Theorem 1.5]
,
\Hom_{\Pos}(\Spec(R),\{0,1\}))\cong\Thick(\mathcal{D}^{c}(R))
and thus, since
Q_{0}
is a discrete poset, we get the statement.

(2) Alternatively, applying the other possible adjunction isomorphism we get

\Hom_{\Pos}(\Spec(R)\times Q_{0},\{0,1\})\cong\Hom_{\Pos}(\Spec(R),\Hom_{\Pos}(Q_{0},\{0,1\}))

where, since
Q_{0}
is a discrete poset,
\Hom_{\Pos}(Q_{0},\{0,1\})
is
\mathcal{P}(Q_{0})
, the power set of
Q_{0}
. In virtue of the bijection between Serre subcategories and sets of simple modules (
[
23
, Section 8]
),
\mathcal{P}(Q_{0})
is isomorphic to the lattice
\Serre(\mathbb{K}Q)
and thus by
3.2
we get the statement.
∎

Remark 2.7
.

Comparing
2.6
(2) with the classification in
[
3
]
, where the authors prove that
\Thick(\mathcal{D}^{c}(RQ))\cong\Hom_{\Pos}(\Spec(R),\mathbf{Nc}(Q))
, one can note that the gap between the thick tensor-ideals and thick subcategories in
\mathcal{D}^{c}(RQ)
is reflected by the gap between the lattices
\mathcal{P}(Q_{0})
and
\mathbf{Nc}(Q)
, the lattice of noncrossing partitions of
Q
.

2.2.
Stratification

As seen in
2.5
, although the tt-category
(\mathcal{D}^{c}(RQ),\boxtimes_{RQ},\mathbf{U})
is almost never rigid, we can still recover the classification of all thick tensor-ideals from its Balmer spectrum. However, without rigidity we loose many aspects of the Balmer-Favi theory in
[
6
]
, which describes how the Balmer spectrum of the compact objects,
\Spc(\mathcal{T}^{c})
, controls the ambient compactly generated tt-category
\mathcal{T}
.

In a rigidly-compactly generated tt-category
(\mathcal{T},\otimes,\mathbf{1})
, any smashing tensor-ideal
\mathcal{S}
gives rise to a tensor-idempotent triangle, i.e. a distinguished triangle

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces e(\mathcal{S})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f(\mathcal{S})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces+}

such that the following (equivalent by
[
6
, Proposition 3.1]
) identities hold

e(\mathcal{S})\otimes e(\mathcal{S})=e(\mathcal{S})\qquad e(\mathcal{S})\otimes f(\mathcal{S})=0\qquad f(\mathcal{S})\otimes f(\mathcal{S})=f(\mathcal{S})

Moreover, by
[
6
, Theorem 3.5]
, in this context, there is a bijection between smashing tensor-ideals and tensor-idempotent triangles. The key point is that, by
[
6
, Theorem 2.13]
and
[
18
, Definition 3.3.2]
, a tensor-ideal
\mathcal{S}
is smashing if and only if its right orthogonal
\mathcal{S}^{\perp}
is also a tensor-ideal – a property that crucially depends on rigidity.

The next example show that, for a general compactly generated tt-category, we loose the aforementioned bijection and that there can be more smashing tensor-ideals than tensor-idempotent triangles.

Example 2.8
.

Let
A_{2}=\overset{1}{\bullet}\longrightarrow\overset{2}{\bullet}\
and consider the algebra
\mathbb{K}A_{2}
. Then the lattice of smashing tensor-ideals is

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{K}A_{2})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\loc\langle S_{1}\rangle}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\loc\langle S_{2}\rangle}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\{0\}}

and the truncation triangles of
\mathbf{U}=\mathbb{K}\xrightarrow{1}\mathbb{K}
with respect to the tensor-ideals
\loc\langle S_{1}\rangle
and
\{0\}
are both
0\to\mathbf{U}\to\mathbf{U}\xrightarrow{+}\
. While the ones with respect to
\loc\langle S_{2}\rangle
and
\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{K}A_{2})
are
S_{2}\to\mathbf{U}\to S_{1}\xrightarrow{+}\
and
\mathbf{U}\to\mathbf{U}\to 0\xrightarrow{+}\
, respectively.

This implies that, a priori,
tensor-idempotent residue objects
objects may not exist for all points of the spectrum (
[
6
, Definition 7.9]
), and consequently, the
Balmer-Favi support
for non-compact objects may not be defined (
[
6
, Definition 7.16]
). Indeed, in a rigidly-compactly generated tt-category
(\mathcal{T},\otimes,\mathbf{1})
, for any prime thick tensor-ideal
\mathcal{P}
in
\Spc(\mathcal{T}^{c})
, we can find two smashing tensor-ideals,
\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{P}}
and
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{P}}
, and define the tensor-idempotent residue object at
\mathcal{P}
as

g(\mathcal{P}):=e(\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{P}})\otimes f(\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{P}})

The Balmer-Favi support of an object
X\in\mathcal{T}
is then given by

\Supp_{\mathcal{T}}(X)=\{\mathcal{P}\in\Spc(\mathcal{T}^{c})\mid X\otimes g(\mathcal{P})\neq 0\}

As shown in
[
6
, Proposition 7.18]
, the tensor-idempotent residue object
g(\mathcal{P})
uniquely determines the prime
\mathcal{P}
, in the sense that
\Supp_{\mathcal{T}}(g(\mathcal{P}))=\{\mathcal{P}\}
.

The major implication of the absence of these notions is the lack of a stratification theory for the classification of localizing tensor-ideals (see
[
9
, Section 4]
). Following
[
9
, Theorem A]
, a rigidly-compactly generated tt-category
\mathcal{T}
is said to be
stratified
(by the Balmer-Favi support) if, denoting by
\langle X\rangle^{\amalg}
the smallest localizing tensor-ideal generated by an object
X\in\mathcal{T}
, the equivalent conditions hold:

\boldsymbol{\cdot}

(
Local-to-global and minimality
) For any object
X\in\mathcal{T}
,
X\in\left\langle g(\mathcal{P})\otimes X\mid\mathcal{P}\in\Spc(\mathcal{T}^{c})\right\rangle^{\amalg}
and
\left\langle g(\mathcal{P})\right\rangle^{\amalg}
is a minimal localizing tensor-ideal for any
\mathcal{P}\in\Spc(\mathcal{T}^{c})
;

\boldsymbol{\cdot}

(
Stratification
) Taking the Balmer-Favi support and its preimage defines a bijection

\Loc_{\otimes}(\mathcal{T})\longleftrightarrow\left\{\text{Subsets of }\Spc(\mathcal{T}^{c})\right\}

Nevertheless, even in the absence of a general theory, it is still possible – based on the proof of
2.1
and the local-to-global principle in
\mathcal{D}(R)
(
[
39
, Section 3.4]
) – to construct
ad hoc
residue objects
g(\mathfrak{p},i)
for all thick prime tensor-ideals
\mathcal{S}_{\mathfrak{p},i}
and thereby obtain a stratification result (
2.11
).

Definition 2.9
.

For a prime thick tensor-ideal
\mathcal{S}_{\mathfrak{p},i}
in
\Spc(\mathcal{D}^{c}(RQ))
, we define the tensor-idempotent residue object at
\mathcal{S}_{\mathfrak{p},i}
to be the complex

g(\mathfrak{p},i):=i_{\times}K_{\infty}(\mathfrak{p})_{\mathfrak{p}}

where
K_{\infty}(\mathfrak{p})
is the infinite Koszul complex at
\mathfrak{p}
. Consequently, we define the support of an object
X\in\mathcal{D}(RQ)
as

\Supp_{RQ}(X)=\{\mathcal{S}_{\mathfrak{p},i}\in\Spc(\mathcal{D}^{c}(RQ))\mid g(\mathfrak{p},i)\boxtimes_{RQ}X\neq 0\}

Remark 2.10
.

It is not clear how to recover this notion of support for a general compactly generated tt-category. Indeed, as we can see in
2.8
, the residue objects do not arise, in any reasonable way, from truncation triangles of the unit
\mathbf{U}
with respect to some smashing tensor-ideals.

The conditions of
[
9
, Theorem A]
reported above are satisfied by the tt-category
\mathcal{D}(RQ)
.

Theorem 2.11
.

For any finite and acyclic quiver
Q
, the following statements hold:

(1)

For any
X\in\mathcal{D}(RQ)
,
\langle X\rangle^{\amalg}=\left\langle g(\mathfrak{p},i)\boxtimes_{RQ}X\mid\mathcal{S}_{\mathfrak{p},i}\in\Spc(\mathcal{D}^{c}(RQ))\right\rangle^{\amalg}
and
\left\langle g(\mathfrak{p},i)\right\rangle^{\amalg}
is a minimal localizing tensor-ideal for any
\mathcal{S}_{\mathfrak{p},i}\in\Spc(\mathcal{D}^{c}(RQ))
;

(2)

Taking the support and its preimage defines a bijection

\Loc_{\boxtimes}(\mathcal{D}(RQ))\longleftrightarrow\left\{\text{Subsets of }\Spc(\mathcal{D}^{c}(RQ))\right\}

Proof.

(1) For any
X\in\mathcal{D}(RQ)
, by
2.1
(1), we have that

\langle X\rangle^{\amalg}=\loc_{RQ}\langle X(i)\mid i\in Q_{0}\rangle

Moreover, by the local-to-global principle in
\mathcal{D}(R)
(
[
39
, Section 3.4]
), for any
i\in Q_{0}
, it holds that

\loc_{R}\left\langle i^{\ast}X\right\rangle=\loc_{R}\left\langle K_{\infty}(\mathfrak{p})_{\mathfrak{p}}\otimes_{R}i^{\ast}X\mid\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec(R)\right\rangle

So, applying the functor
i_{\times}
, by
1.7
, we can conclude that

\loc_{RQ}\langle X(i)\mid i\in Q_{0}\rangle=\loc_{RQ}\langle i_{\times}K_{\infty}(\mathfrak{p})_{\mathfrak{p}}\boxtimes_{RQ}X(i)\mid\mathcal{S}_{\mathfrak{p},i}\in\Spc(\mathcal{D}^{c}(RQ))\rangle

Noting that
i_{\times}K_{\infty}(\mathfrak{p})_{\mathfrak{p}}\boxtimes_{RQ}X(i)=(g(\mathfrak{p},i)\boxtimes_{RQ}X)(i)
, by
(\ast)
the above implies that

\langle X\rangle^{\amalg}=\langle g(\mathfrak{p},i)\boxtimes_{RQ}X\mid\mathcal{S}_{\mathfrak{p},i}\in\Spc(\mathcal{D}^{c}(RQ))\rangle^{\amalg}

Moreover, by
[
39
, 3.24]
, for any
\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec(R)
the subcategory
\loc_{R}\left\langle K_{\infty}(\mathfrak{p})_{\mathfrak{p}}\right\rangle
is minimal in
\mathcal{D}(R)
. Then, for any localizing tensor-ideal
\mathcal{L}
of
\mathcal{D}(RQ)
, the inclusion
\mathcal{L}\in\left\langle g(\mathfrak{p},i)\right\rangle^{\amalg}
implies both
\mathcal{L}=i_{\times}i^{\ast}(\mathcal{L})
and
i^{\ast}(\mathcal{L})\subseteq\loc_{R}\left\langle K_{\infty}(\mathfrak{p})_{\mathfrak{p}}\right\rangle
, respectively by
(\ast)
and
1.7
. Thus, we can deduce the minimality of the localizing tensor-ideal
\left\langle g(\mathfrak{p},i)\right\rangle^{\amalg}
.

(2) For any object
X\in\mathcal{D}(RQ)
, by minimality, we have that
\left\langle g(\mathfrak{p},i)\boxtimes_{RQ}X\right\rangle^{\amalg}=\left\langle g(\mathfrak{p},i)\right\rangle^{\amalg}
if
S_{\mathfrak{p},i}\in\Supp_{RQ}(X)
or zero otherwise. Defining the support of a subcategory
\mathcal{L}\subseteq\mathcal{D}(RQ)
as the union of the supports of its objects, from the previous point, we have that any localizing tensor-ideal
\mathcal{L}
is equal to
\left\langle g(\mathfrak{p},i)\mid\mathcal{S}_{\mathfrak{p},i}\in\Supp_{RQ}(\mathcal{L})\right\rangle
. Noting that the
\Supp_{RQ}(g(\mathfrak{p},i))=\{\mathcal{S}_{\mathfrak{p},i}\}
, we have the bijection.
∎

3.
Tensor-t-structures

We recall the definition of tensor-t-structure from
[
14
]
. Let
(\mathcal{T},\otimes,\mathbf{1})
be a compactly generated tt-category and suppose it is given with a suspended subcategory
\mathcal{T}^{\leq 0}
such that
\mathcal{T}^{\leq 0}\otimes\mathcal{T}^{\leq 0}\subseteq\mathcal{T}^{\leq 0}
and
\mathbf{1}\in\mathcal{T}^{\leq 0}
. A suspended subcategory
\mathcal{S}
of
\mathcal{T}
is called
tensor-suspended
(
with respect to
\mathcal{T}^{\leq 0}
) if
\mathcal{T}^{\leq 0}\otimes\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{S}
. A t-structure
(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})
of
\mathcal{T}
is called a
tensor-t-structure
(with respect to
\mathcal{T}^{\leq 0}
) if the aisle
\mathcal{U}
is a tensor-suspended subcategory – or equivalently if
[\mathcal{T}^{\leq 0},\mathcal{V}]\subseteq\mathcal{V}
(see
[
14
, Proposition 3.9]
). In this case
\mathcal{U}
and
\mathcal{V}
are called
tensor-aisle
and
tensor-coaisle
, respectively. Given a set of objects
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathcal{T}
, we denote the smallest cocomplete tensor-suspended subcategory containing
\mathcal{X}
by
\langle\mathcal{X}\rangle^{\leq}
and we call a tensor-aisle
\mathcal{U}

compactly generated
if
\mathcal{U}=\langle\mathcal{X}\rangle^{\leq}
for a set of compact objects
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathcal{T}^{c}
. Recall that, by
[
14
, Proposition 3.11 (i)]
, whenever
\mathcal{T}^{\leq 0}
is compactly generated,
\langle\mathcal{S}\rangle^{\leq}
is a tensor-aisle for any set of compact objects
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{T}^{c}
and, moreover, note that this result holds for general compactly generated tt-categories, since the rigidity assumption do not play any role.

In the following we will consider tensor-t-structures of
\mathcal{D}(RQ)
with respect to the aisle of the standard t-structure
(\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0}(RQ),\mathcal{D}^{\geq 1}(RQ))
. Observe that, indeed,
\mathbf{U}\in\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0}(RQ)
and
\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0}(RQ)\boxtimes_{RQ}\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0}(RQ)\subseteq\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0}(RQ)
. We denote by
\susp_{RQ}^{\amalg}\left\langle\mathcal{X}\right\rangle
the smallest cocomplete suspended subcategory of
\mathcal{D}(RQ)
containing a set of object
\mathcal{X}
and recall that, by
[
2
, Theorem 3.4]
, it is the aisle of a t-structure. Analogously to
2.1
, we have the following.

Proposition 3.1
.

For any finite and acyclic quiver
Q
and
X\in\mathcal{D}(RQ)
, it holds that:

(1)

\langle X\rangle^{\leq}=\susp_{RQ}^{\amalg}\left\langle X(i)\mid i\in Q_{0}\right\rangle
;

(2)

If
X
is compact,
\langle X\rangle^{\leq}=\susp_{RQ}^{\amalg}\left\langle i_{\times}K(\mathfrak{p})[-n]\mid\mathfrak{p}\in\Supp_{R}(H^{n}(i^{\ast}X)),n\in\mathbb{Z},i\in Q_{0}\right\rangle
.

In particular, any cocomplete tensor-suspended subcategory of
\mathcal{D}^{c}(RQ)
is generated by complexes of the form
i_{\times}K(\mathfrak{p})[-n]
for some
i\in Q_{0}
,
\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec(R)
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

(1) Noting that all the
RQ
-modules
U(i)
belong to the standard aisle
\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0}(RQ)
, the proof follows as the one of
2.1
(1), since there we do not use closure under negative shifts.

(2) If
X\in\mathcal{D}^{c}(RQ)
, by the main result in
[
1
]
, we have that

\susp_{R}^{\amalg}\langle i^{\ast}X\rangle=\susp_{R}^{\amalg}\left\langle K(\mathfrak{p})[-n]\mid\mathfrak{p}\in\Supp_{R}(H^{n}(i^{\ast}X)),n\in\mathbb{Z}\right\rangle

So, applying the functor
i_{\times}
, by
1.7
, we can conclude that

\susp_{R}^{\amalg}\langle X(i)\rangle=\susp_{R}^{\amalg}\left\langle i_{\times}K(\mathfrak{p})[-n]\mid\mathfrak{p}\in\Supp_{R}(H^{n}(i^{\ast}X)),n\in\mathbb{Z}\right\rangle

and combining this with point (1) gives the statement.

As for the last part, it is sufficient to note that, given a set of objects
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathcal{D}^{c}(RQ)
, the cocomplete tensor-suspended subcategory
\langle\mathcal{X}\rangle^{\leq}
is equal to

\susp_{RQ}^{\amalg}\left\langle i_{\times}K(\mathfrak{p})[-n]\mid\mathfrak{p}\in\Supp_{R}(H^{n}(i^{\ast}X)),X\in\mathcal{X},n\in\mathbb{Z},i\in Q_{0}\right\rangle

∎

For a tt-category
(\mathcal{T},\otimes,\mathbf{1})
, we denote by
\Susp_{\otimes}(\mathcal{T}^{c})
the lattice of tensor-suspended subcategories of
\mathcal{T}^{c}
ordered by inclusion and by
\mathrm{Aisle_{cg}}_{\otimes}(\mathcal{T})
the lattice of compactly generated tensor-aisle of
\mathcal{T}
. Recall that, by
[
15
, Theorem 15 (i)]
, these two lattices are isomorphic and note that this result holds for compactly generated tt-categories without the rigidity assumption.

Now we present the analogous of
2.6
for compactly generated tensor-aisles of
\mathcal{D}(RQ)
. We start by proving a version of
[
34
, Theorem A.3]
adapted to tensor-aisles.

Lemma 3.2
.

For any field
\mathbb{K}
and finite acyclic quiver
Q
, we have that

\Aisle_{\boxtimes}(\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{K}Q))\cong\Filt(\Serre(\mathbb{K}Q))

In particular, the thick tensor-ideals of
\mathcal{D}^{c}(\mathbb{K}Q)
are in bijection with
\Serre(\mathbb{K}Q)
.

Proof.

Since any aisle in
\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{K}Q)
is compactly generated, by
[
15
, Theorem 15 (i)]
, it suffices to prove that the following bijection holds

\begin{gathered}\Susp_{\boxtimes}(\mathcal{D}^{c}(\mathbb{K}Q))\longleftrightarrow\Filt(\Serre(\mathbb{K}Q))\\
\qquad\qquad\mathcal{S}\longmapsto\left(\ldots\supseteq H^{n}(\mathcal{S})\supseteq H^{n+1}(\mathcal{S})\supseteq\ldots\right)\\
\left\{X\in\mathcal{D}^{c}(\mathbb{K}Q)\mid H^{n}(X)\in\mathcal{Z}_{n}\right\}\mathrel{\reflectbox{$\longmapsto$}}\,\left(\ldots\supseteq\mathcal{Z}_{n}\supseteq\mathcal{Z}_{n+1}\supseteq\ldots\right)\end{gathered}

Given a tensor-suspended subcategory
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{D}^{c}(\mathbb{K}Q)
, by
[
34
, Lemma A.1]
, the class
H^{n}(\mathcal{S})
is closed under images, cokernels and extensions for any
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. So, it is sufficient to prove that it is closed also under subobjects to prove that it is a Serre subcategory of
\mods(\mathbb{K}Q)
. Let
M\in H^{n}(\mathcal{S})
, then
M[-n]
lies in
\mathcal{S}
and, by closure under tensor product, all the modules of the form
U(i)\boxtimes_{\mathbb{K}Q}M[-n]
belongs to
\mathcal{S}
. In particular, all the composition factors of
M
lies in
H^{n}(\mathcal{S})
and so does any of its submodules. Moreover, for a filtration of Serre subcategories
\ldots\supseteq\mathcal{Z}_{n}\supseteq\mathcal{Z}_{n+1}\supseteq\ldots
, the class
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{Z}}=\{X\in\mathcal{D}^{c}(\mathbb{K}Q)\mid H^{n}(X)\in\mathcal{Z}_{n}\}
is clearly closed under summands and positive shifts, so it remains to show that it is closed under extensions. Let
X,Y\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{Z}}
and consider a distinguished triangle

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces+}

It induces the exact sequence in cohomology

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{n-1}(Y)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{n}(X)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{n}(Z)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{n}(Y)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{n+1}(X)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g}

from which we obtain the short exact sequence

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\coker f}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{n}(Z)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\ker g}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}

Since both
\coker f
and
\ker f
lies in
\mathcal{Z}_{n}
so does

. Since
\mathbb{K}Q
is hereditary, it follows that
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{Z}}=\susp_{\mathbb{K}Q}^{\amalg}\langle\mathcal{Z}_{n}[-n]\mid n\in\mathbb{Z}\rangle
and, since any Serre subcategory
\mathcal{Z}_{n}
is uniquely determined by the simple modules it contains (

, Section 8]
), it is actually generated by shifts of simple
\mathbb{K}Q
-modules. In particular, by

, Lemma 3.5]
,
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{Z}}
is a tensor-suspended subcategory. Then the bijection trivially holds.
∎

Theorem 3.3
.

For any commutative noetherian ring
R
and finite acyclic quiver
Q
, it holds that

(1)

\mathrm{Aisle_{cg}}_{\boxtimes}(\mathcal{D}(RQ))\cong\Aisle_{\mathrm{cg}}(\mathcal{D}(R))\times Q_{0}
;

(2)

\mathrm{Aisle_{cg}}_{\boxtimes}(\mathcal{D}(RQ))\cong\Hom_{\Pos}(\Spec(R),\Aisle_{\boxtimes}(\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{K}Q)))
.

Proof.

(1) By
[
15
, Theorem 15 (i)]
, it suffices to prove that the following bijection holds

\begin{gathered}\Susp_{\boxtimes}(\mathcal{D}^{c}(RQ))\longleftrightarrow\Susp(\mathcal{D}^{c}(R))\times Q_{0}\\
f:\mathcal{S}\longmapsto\left(i^{\ast}\mathcal{S}\right)_{i\in Q_{0}}\\
\susp_{RQ}^{\amalg}\left\langle i_{\times}\mathcal{A}_{i}\mid i\in Q_{0}\right\rangle\mathrel{\reflectbox{$\longmapsto$}}\left(\mathcal{A}_{i}\right)_{i\in Q_{0}}:g\end{gathered}

Since both functors
i^{\ast}
and
i_{\times}
preserve compacts, to show that the assignments are well defined it is sufficient to show that, for any tensor-suspended subcategory
\mathcal{S}
in
\mathcal{D}^{c}(RQ)
,
i^{\ast}\mathcal{S}
is extensions closed. Let
X,Z\in\mathcal{S}
and consider a distinguished triangle
i^{\ast}X\to M\to i^{\ast}Z\xrightarrow{+}\
in
\mathcal{D}(R)
. In particular, applying the functor
i_{\times}
, by
1.7
, we get that
X(i)\to i_{\times}M\to Z(i)\xrightarrow{+}\
is a distinguished triangle in
\mathcal{D}(RQ)
and, by tensor closure of
\mathcal{S}
,
X(i),Z(i)\in\mathcal{S}
. It follows that
i_{\times}M
lies in
\mathcal{S}
and thus
M\cong i^{\ast}i_{\times}M\in i^{\ast}\mathcal{S}
.

Moreover, for a tensor-suspended subcategory
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{D}^{c}(RQ)
, we have the equality

g\circ f(\mathcal{S})=\susp_{RQ}^{\amalg}\left\langle i_{\times}i^{\ast}\mathcal{S}\mid i\in Q_{0}\right\rangle

which, by
1.7
, is equal to
\susp_{RQ}^{\amalg}\left\langle U(i)\boxtimes_{RQ}\mathcal{S}\mid i\in Q_{0}\right\rangle
and, by
3.1
(1), it is equal to
\mathcal{S}
. While, for a collection of tensor-suspended subcategories
\left(\mathcal{A}_{i}\right)_{i\in Q_{0}}
in
\mathcal{D}(R)
, we have

f\circ g(\left(\mathcal{A}_{i}\right)_{i\in Q_{0}})=\left(i^{\ast}\susp_{RQ}^{\amalg}\left\langle j_{\times}\mathcal{A}_{j}\mid j\in Q_{0}\right\rangle\right)_{i\in Q_{0}}

which, since we proved that
i^{\ast}
reflects extensions, is equal to
\left(\susp_{R}^{\amalg}\left\langle i^{\ast}j_{\times}\mathcal{A}_{j}\mid j\in Q_{0}\right\rangle\right)_{i\in Q_{0}}
and, by
1.7
, it is equal to
(\mathcal{A}_{i})_{i\in Q_{0}}
.

(2) Denote by
L(i)
the
\mathbb{K}Q
-simple module at a vertex
i\in Q_{0}
. By
[
15
, Theorem 15 (i)]
, it suffices to prove that the following bijection holds

\begin{gathered}\Susp_{\boxtimes}(\mathcal{D}^{c}(RQ))\longleftrightarrow\Hom_{\Pos}(\Spec(R),\Susp_{\boxtimes}(\mathcal{D}^{c}(\mathbb{K}Q)))\\
f:\mathcal{S}\longmapsto\left(\mathfrak{p}\mapsto\susp_{\mathbb{K}Q}^{\amalg}\left\langle L(i)[n]\mid i_{\times}K(\mathfrak{p})[n]\in\mathcal{S}\right\rangle\right)\\
\susp_{RQ}^{\amalg}\left\langle i_{\times}K(\mathfrak{p})[n]\mid L(i)[n]\in\sigma(\mathfrak{p}),\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec(R)\right\rangle\mathrel{\reflectbox{$\longmapsto$}}\,\sigma:g\end{gathered}

By
2.1
and
3.2
, both

and

are tensor-suspended subcategories. Moreover by

, Lemma 3.7]
, for any prime ideals

, we have that

, thus

and

is a poset homomorphism. Since, by

, any tensor-suspended subcategory of

is generated by complexes of the form

, we have that

. Analogously, in virtue of the bijection between Serre subcategories and sets of simple modules (
[

, Section 8]
), by

, we have that any tensor-suspended subcategory of

is generated by complexes of the form

and so
f\circ g(\sigma)(\mathfrak{p})=\sigma(\mathfrak{p})
for any

.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

Comparing

(

).

Now we get the analogous of

, for compactly generated tensor-aisles.

Corollary 3.5
.

For any commutative noetherian ring
R
and finite acyclic quiver

, we have that

\mathrm{Aisle_{cg}}_{\boxtimes}(\mathcal{D}(RQ))\cong\Filt(\mathcal{V}(\Spc(\mathcal{D}^{c}(RQ))))

is the set of specialization closed subsets of

.

Proof.

Denote by

the set of specialization closed subsets of the prime spectrum of

. By

]
, the lattice of compactly generated aisles of

is isomorphic to

Given a compactly generated tt-category
(\mathcal{T},\otimes,\mathbf{1})
together with a “standard” aisle

(the appropriate notion of

being part of the question), when are the compactly generated tensor-t-structures parametrized by filtrations of Thomason subsets of the Balmer spectrum ?

]

4.
Tensor telescope conjecture

In the general setting of representations of an arbitrary quiver over commutative noetherian rings we do not know very much about compactly generated and homotopically smashing t-structures and how they interact. However, restricting to the tensor-compatible ones allows a classification of the former. We will see that this restriction is enough also for proving that any homotopically smashing tensor-t-structure is compactly generated.

Lemma 4.1
.

For an arbitrary ring
A
, any homotopically smashing t-structure
(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})
in
\mathcal{D}(A)
is generated by objects, i.e. there exists a set of objects
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathcal{D}(A)
such that
\mathcal{U}=\susp_{A}^{\amalg}\langle\mathcal{X}\rangle
.

Proof.

Let us note first that, following the proof of
[
29
, Theorem 3.2 (1)]
, the homotopically smashing condition implies that the truncation functor
\tau_{\mathcal{U}}^{<}
of the t-structure
(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})
commutes with directed homotopy colimits. Moreover, by the existence of semi-projective resolutions and
[
12
, Theorem 1.1]
, any complex of
A
-modules is quasi-isomorphic to a semi-projective complex which is isomorphic to a directed homotopy colimit of compact objects
\{S_{i}\}_{i\in I}
in
\mathcal{D}(A)
. Then, for any
U\in\mathcal{U}
, we have that

U\cong\tau_{\mathcal{U}}^{<}(U)\cong\tau_{\mathcal{U}}^{<}(\hocolim_{i\in I}S_{i})\cong\hocolim_{i\in I}\tau_{\mathcal{U}}^{<}(S_{i})

In particular, it follows that
\mathcal{U}=\susp_{RQ}^{\amalg}\langle\tau_{\mathcal{U}}^{<}(S)\mid S\in\mathcal{D}^{c}(RQ)\rangle
.
∎

Recall that, by
[
36
, Theorem A]
, we can lift any t-structure
(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N})
in
\mathcal{D}(R)
to a t-structure
(\mathcal{M}_{Q},\mathcal{N}_{Q})
in
\mathcal{D}(RQ)
defined as follows

\mathcal{M}_{Q}=\{X\in\mathcal{D}(RQ)\mid j^{\ast}X\in\mathcal{M}\text{ for any }j\in Q\}

\mathcal{N}_{Q}=\{X\in\mathcal{D}(RQ)\mid j^{\ast}X\in\mathcal{N}\text{ for any }j\in Q\}

note that the lifting is compatible with the orthogonality, in the sense that
(\mathcal{M}_{Q})^{\perp}=(\mathcal{M}^{\perp})_{Q}
.

In the following proposition, we show how a t-structure
(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})
in
\mathcal{D}(RQ)
can be restricted to a t-structure in
\mathcal{D}(R)
preserving the homotopically smashing property. Specifically, the restriction is obtained by restricting the aisle and then taking its right orthogonal in
\mathcal{D}(R)
. As illustrated in
4.3
, the naive attempt to restrict both the aisle and the coaisle fails to even produce a t-structure. Therefore, this construction does not yield an inverse to the lifting in
[
36
]
.

Proposition 4.2
.

For any homotopically smashing t-structure
(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})
of
\mathcal{D}(RQ)
, the pair
\left(i^{\ast}\mathcal{U},(i^{\ast}\mathcal{U})^{\perp}\right)
is an homotopically smashing t-structure in
\mathcal{D}(R)
. Moreover, its associated left truncation functor is given by the composite
i^{\ast}\circ\tau_{Q}^{<}\circ i_{\ast}
, where
\tau_{Q}^{<}
is the truncation functor associated to the aisle
(i^{\ast}\mathcal{U})_{Q}
.

Proof.

Let
(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})
a homotopically smashing t-structure in
\mathcal{D}(RQ)
. By
4.1
, there exists a set of objects
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathcal{D}(RQ)
such that
\mathcal{U}=\susp_{RQ}^{\amalg}\langle X\mid X\in\mathcal{X}\rangle
. By
[
2
, Theorem 3.4]
, it follows that
i^{\ast}\mathcal{U}=\susp_{R}^{\amalg}\langle i^{\ast}X\mid X\in\mathcal{X},i\in Q_{0}\rangle
is an aisle in
\mathcal{D}(R)
. We see that
(i^{\ast}\mathcal{U})^{\perp}
is closed under directed homotopy colimits. Indeed, let
\{Y_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\in\mathcal{D}(R)^{\Lambda}
be a coherent diagram such that
Y_{\lambda}\in(i^{\ast}\mathcal{U})^{\perp}
for any
\lambda\in\Lambda
. In particular, we have that

\Hom_{\mathcal{D}(RQ)}(\mathcal{U},i_{\ast}Y_{\lambda})\cong\Hom_{\mathcal{D}(R)}(i^{\ast}\mathcal{U},Y_{\lambda})=0\text{ for any }\lambda\in\Lambda

Since
\mathcal{U}^{\perp}
is closed under taking directed homotopy colimits and
i_{\ast}
commute with them (see
Section

1.2
), it follows that

\Hom_{\mathcal{D}(R)}(i^{\ast}\mathcal{U},\hocolim_{\lambda\in\Lambda}Y_{\lambda})\cong\Hom_{\mathcal{D}(RQ)}(\mathcal{U},\hocolim_{\lambda\in\Lambda}i_{\ast}Y_{\lambda})=0

Thus
\left(i^{\ast}\mathcal{U},(i^{\ast}\mathcal{U})^{\perp}\right)
is an homotopically smashing t-structure in
\mathcal{D}(R)
. As for the last part, let
U\in i^{\ast}\mathcal{U}
and
V\in\mathcal{D}(R)
, then we have that
\Hom_{\mathcal{D}(R)}(U,i^{\ast}\circ\tau_{Q}^{<}\circ i_{\ast}V)\cong\Hom_{\mathcal{D}(RQ)}(i_{!}U,\tau_{Q}^{<}\circ i_{\ast}V)
. Since, by
1.6
,
i_{!}U
lies in
(i^{\ast}\mathcal{U})_{Q}
and
i^{\ast}i_{!}U\cong U
, the above is further equal to

\Hom_{\mathcal{D}(RQ)}(i_{!}U,i_{\ast}V)\cong\Hom_{\mathcal{D}(R)}(i^{\ast}i_{!}U,V)\cong\Hom_{\mathcal{D}(R)}(U,V)

Thus, it is clear that the composite
i^{\ast}\circ\tau_{Q}^{<}\circ i_{\ast}
is right adjoint to the inclusion
i^{\ast}\mathcal{U}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{D}(R)
.
∎

Example 4.3
.

Consider the algebra
\mathbb{Z}A_{2}
and the t-structure
(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})
generated by the representation
\mathbb{Z}\xrightarrow{\pi}\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
for some prime
p\in\mathbb{Z}
. Note that, since there are no non-zero morphisms from
\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
to
\mathbb{Z}
, the representation
\mathbb{Z}\xrightarrow{1}\mathbb{Z}
lies in
\mathcal{V}
. In particular, the free module
\mathbb{Z}
is both in
1^{\ast}\mathcal{U}
and in
1^{\ast}\mathcal{V}
, thus
(1^{\ast}\mathcal{U},1^{\ast}\mathcal{V})
cannot be a t-structure.

We can now prove the tensor telescope conjecture for the tt-category
(\mathcal{D}(RQ),\boxtimes_{RQ},\mathbf{U})
.

Theorem 4.4
.

Any homotopically smashing tensor-t-structure of
\mathcal{D}(RQ)
is compactly generated.

Proof.

Let
(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})
a homotopically smashing tensor-t-structure in
\mathcal{D}(RQ)
. By
4.2
, we have that
\left(i^{\ast}\mathcal{U},(i^{\ast}\mathcal{U})^{\perp}\right)
is an homotopically smashing t-structure in
\mathcal{D}(R)
, thus by the telescope conjecture in
\mathcal{D}(R)
(see
[
19
]
), we can assume that
i^{\ast}\mathcal{U}
is a compactly generated aisle for any
i\in Q_{0}
. By the bijection
3.3
(1),
\susp_{RQ}^{\amalg}\langle i_{\times}i^{\ast}\mathcal{U}\mid i\in Q_{0}\rangle
is a compactly generated aisle in
\mathcal{D}(RQ)
. By
1.7
, we have that the above is equal to
\susp_{RQ}^{\amalg}\langle X(i)\mid X\in\mathcal{U},i\in Q_{0}\rangle
and, by
3.1
, it is further equal to
\mathcal{U}
.
∎

4.1.
Other tensor-t-structures

Actually, we can strengthen the tensor telescope conjecture of
4.4
, by generalizing the result to homotopically smashing tensor-t-structures with respect to a broad class of suspended subcategories
\mathcal{T}^{\leq 0}
, which are strictly contained in the standard aisle.

For an
RQ
-module
C
, the
support of
C
over
Q
is the full subquiver of
Q
with vertices
i\in Q_{0}
such that
C_{i}
is non-zero and we denote it by
\Supp_{Q}(C)
.

Definition 4.5
.

We define a
filtration system of the unit
to be a set of
R
-free
RQ
-modules
\mathcal{C}=\{C_{1},\ldots,C_{n}\}
such that:

(i)

\mathbf{U}\in\mathsf{filt}\langle\mathcal{C}\rangle
;

(ii)

\Supp_{Q}(C_{i})\cap\Supp_{Q}(C_{j})=\emptyset
for any
i\neq j
.

Moreover, we say that a filtration system (of the unit) has
Dynkin support
if, for any
k=1,\ldots,n
, the quiver
Q_{k}:=\Supp_{Q}(C_{k})
is a Dynkin quiver.

Given a filtration system
\mathcal{C}
, we say that a suspended subcategory
\mathcal{S}
is
\mathcal{C}
-tensor-suspended
if it is tensor-suspended with respect to
\susp_{RQ}\langle\mathcal{C}\rangle
, i.e. by
[
14
, Lemma 3.5]
if
\mathcal{C}\boxtimes_{RQ}\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{S}
. A t-structure is a
\mathcal{C}
-tensor-t-structure
if the aisle is a
\mathcal{C}
-tensor-suspended subcategory. Finally, given a set of objects
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathcal{T}
, we denote the smallest cocomplete
\mathcal{C}
-tensor-suspended subcategory containing
\mathcal{X}
by
\langle\mathcal{X}\rangle^{\mathcal{C}}
, by
\Susp_{\boxtimes}^{\mathcal{C}}(\mathcal{D}^{c}(RQ))
the lattice of
\mathcal{C}
-tensor-suspended subcategories of
\mathcal{D}^{c}(RQ)
and by
\mathrm{Aisle_{cg}^{\mathcal{C}}}_{\boxtimes}(\mathcal{D}(RQ))
the lattice of compactly generated
\mathcal{C}
-tensor-aisle of
\mathcal{D}(RQ)
.

Example 4.6
.

(1)

Any t-structure in
\mathcal{D}(RQ)
is a
\mathcal{C}
-tensor-t-structure with respect to the trivial filtration system
\mathcal{C}=\{\mathbf{U}\}
. In particular, when
Q
is a Dynkin quiver, any t-structure is a
\mathcal{C}
-tensor-t-structure with respect to a filtration system with Dynkin support.

(2)

The tensor-t-structures with respect to the standard aisle
\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0}(RQ)
are precisely the
\mathcal{C}
-tensor-t-structures with respect to the filtration system
\mathcal{C}=\{U(i)\mid i\in Q_{0}\}
, which always has Dynkin support.

Remark 4.7
.

Given a filtration system
\mathcal{C}
, any module
C_{k}\in\mathcal{C}
defines a fully faithful functor
c_{k}:Q_{k}\hookrightarrow Q
and thus an adjoint triple
{c_{k}}_{!}\dashv{c_{k}}^{\ast}\dashv{c_{k}}_{\ast}
as follows

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{D}(RQ)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{D}(RQ_{k})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{c_{k}}^{\ast}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{c_{k}}_{!}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{c_{k}}_{\ast}}

Since
c_{k}
is fully faithful, by the characterization of the projective
RQ
-modules in
[
16
, Theorem 3.1]
, given a bounded complexes
P
of finitely generated projective
RQ
-modules,
{c_{k}}^{\ast}P
is a bounded complexes of finitely generated projective
RQ_{k}
-modules. Thus,
{c_{k}}^{\ast}
preserves compact objects and, since coproducts in
\mathcal{D}(RQ)
are computed vertexwise, it also preserves coproducts. In particular, as in
1.6
,
{c_{k}}_{!}
preserves compacts,
{c_{k}}_{\ast}
preserves coproducts and all three
{c_{k}}_{!},{c_{k}}^{\ast},{c_{k}}_{\ast}
preserve directed homotopy colimits.
Moreover, by
[
17
, Proposition 1.26]
, the composite
{c_{k}}^{\ast}{c_{k}}_{!}
is natural isomorphic to
\id_{\mathcal{D}(RQ_{k})}
. So, we can define the functor

{c_{k}}_{\times}:=C_{k}\boxtimes_{RQ}{c_{k}}_{!}:\mathcal{D}(RQ_{k})\to\mathcal{D}(RQ)

In view of
4.6
, the following proposition generalizes
3.1
(1) and
3.3
(1) to
\mathcal{C}
-tensor-t-structures.

Proposition 4.8
.

For any filtration system of the unit
\mathcal{C}=\{C_{1},\ldots,C_{n}\}
, it holds that:

(1)

\langle X\rangle^{\mathcal{C}}=\susp_{RQ}^{\amalg}\langle C_{k}\boxtimes_{RQ}X\mid k=1,\ldots,n\rangle
;

(2)

\mathrm{Aisle_{cg}^{\mathcal{C}}}_{\boxtimes}(\mathcal{D}(RQ))\cong\prod\limits_{k=1}^{n}\Aisle_{\mathrm{cg}}(\mathcal{D}(RQ_{k}))

Proof.

(1) Since the tensor unit
\mathbf{U}
admits a filtration whit factors isomorphic to the representations
C_{k}
, tensoring this filtration by a complex
X\in\mathcal{D}(RQ)
it follows that
X\in\susp_{RQ}^{\amalg}\left\langle C_{k}\boxtimes_{RQ}X\mid k=1,\ldots,n\right\rangle
. Moreover, the latter is a
\mathcal{C}
-tensor-suspended subcategory by
[
14
, Lemma 3.5]
. On the other hand, by closure condition, also the reverse inclusion holds.

(2) By
[
15
, Theorem 15 (i)]
, it suffices to prove that the following bijection holds

\begin{gathered}\Susp_{\boxtimes}(\mathcal{D}^{c}(RQ))\longleftrightarrow\prod\limits_{k=1}^{n}\Susp(\mathcal{D}^{c}(RQ_{k}))\\
f:\mathcal{S}\longmapsto\left({c_{k}}^{\ast}\mathcal{S}\right)_{k=1,\ldots,n}\\
\susp_{RQ}^{\amalg}\left\langle{c_{k}}_{\times}\mathcal{A}_{k}\mid k=1,\ldots,n\right\rangle\mathrel{\reflectbox{$\longmapsto$}}\left(\mathcal{A}_{k}\right)_{k=1,\ldots,n}:g\end{gathered}

Since both functors
{c_{k}}^{\ast}
and
{c_{k}}_{\times}
preserve compacts and
{c_{k}}^{\ast}
preserves extensions, shifts and summands, the assignments are well defined. Moreover, for a tensor-suspended subcategory
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{D}(RQ)
, the composite
g\circ f(\mathcal{S})
is
\susp_{RQ}^{\amalg}\left\langle{c_{k}}_{\times}{c_{k}}^{\ast}\mathcal{S}\mid k=1,\ldots,n\right\rangle
. By
1.7
, we have that

g\circ f(\mathcal{S})=\susp_{RQ}^{\amalg}\left\langle C_{k}\boxtimes_{RQ}\mathcal{S}\mid k=1,\ldots,n\right\rangle

which, by point (1), is equal to
\mathcal{S}
. For a family of tensor-ideals
\left(\mathcal{A}_{k}\right)_{k=1,\ldots,n}
with
\mathcal{A}_{k}\subseteq\mathcal{D}(RQ_{k})
, the composite
f\circ g(\left(\mathcal{A}_{k}\right)_{k=1,\ldots,n})
is given by
\left({c_{k}}^{\ast}\susp_{RQ}^{\amalg}\left\langle{c_{\ell}}_{\times}\mathcal{A}_{\ell}\mid\ell=1,\ldots,n\right\rangle\right)_{k=1,\ldots,n}
which, by the properties of
{c_{k}}^{\ast}
and the fact that the supports of objects in
\mathcal{C}
are disjoint, is equal to
\left(\susp_{R}^{\amalg}\left\langle{c_{k}}^{\ast}{c_{\ell}}_{\times}\mathcal{A}_{\ell}\mid\ell=1,\ldots,n\right\rangle\right)_{k=1,\ldots,n}=(A_{k})_{k=1,\ldots,n}
.
∎

We can now prove the tensor telescope conjecture for
\mathcal{C}
-tensor-t-structures with respect to some filtration system with Dynkin support and so, generalize
4.4
.

Theorem 4.9
.

Given a filtration system with Dynkin support
\mathcal{C}
, any homotopically smashing
\mathcal{C}
-tensor-t-structure of
\mathcal{D}(RQ)
is compactly generated.

Proof.

Let
(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})
a homotopically smashing
\mathcal{C}
-tensor-t-structure in
\mathcal{D}(RQ)
. By similar arguments to those in
4.2
, we have that
\left({c_{k}}^{\ast}\mathcal{U},({c_{k}}^{\ast}\mathcal{U})^{\perp}\right)
is a homotopically smashing t-structure in
\mathcal{D}(RQ_{k})
for any
k=1,\ldots,n
, thus by the telescope conjecture in
\mathcal{D}(RQ_{k})
(see
[
34
]
), we can assume that
{c_{k}}^{\ast}\mathcal{U}
is a compactly generated aisle for any
k=1,\ldots,n
. By the bijection in
4.8
(2),
\susp_{RQ}^{\amalg}\langle{c_{k}}_{\times}{c_{k}}^{\ast}\mathcal{U}\mid k=1,\ldots,n\rangle
is a compactly generated
\mathcal{C}
-tensor-aisle in
\mathcal{D}(RQ)
. Moreover, it is equal to
\susp_{RQ}^{\amalg}\langle C_{k}\boxtimes_{RQ}\mathcal{U}\mid k=1,\ldots,n\rangle
and thus, by
4.8
(2), to
\mathcal{U}
.
∎

The next example illustrates how
4.9
could represent a significant step toward proving the telescope conjecture for representations of finite, acyclic, and simply-laced quivers.

Example 4.10
.

Consider the Euclidean quiver

\widetilde{D}_{5}=
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\overset{2}{\bullet}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\overset{6}{\bullet}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\overset{3}{\bullet}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\overset{4}{\bullet}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\overset{1}{\bullet}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\overset{5}{\bullet}}

Let
D_{5}
be the full subquiver spanned by the vertices
\{1,2,3,4,5\}
and
P
the representation given by
P_{i}=R
and
P_{\alpha}=\id_{R}
if
i,\alpha\in D_{5}
and
P_{6}=0
, then
\mathcal{C}=\{P,U(6)\}
is a filtration system with Dynkin support. Note that a suspended subcategory
\mathcal{U}
is a
\mathcal{C}
-tensor-ideal if and only if for any object
X\in\mathcal{U}
such that
\Supp_{Q}(X)\nsubseteq D_{5}
then
X(6)\in\mathcal{U}
. Thus, by
4.9
, any homotopically smashing t-structure whose aisle satisfies this property is compactly generated.

Moreover, in this example as well as in any Euclidean quiver, a non-trivial filtration systems
\mathcal{C}\neq\{\mathbf{U}\}
has always Dynkin support. Therefore, for a homotopically smashing t-structure, it is enough to have a
\mathcal{C}
-tensor-aisle for some non-trivial filtration system
\mathcal{C}
in order to be compactly generated.

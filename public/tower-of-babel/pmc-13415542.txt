Dynamic capability assessment and enhancement in chinese construction enterprises under digital transformation: An integrated cloud model approach
Authors: Xiaoyan Huo, Huanhuan Jia, Fen Meng, Sheng Wang, Enqi Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn the context of the digital economy-a pivotal driver of China’s economic growth-construction enterprises are undergoing profound transformations in their operational and managerial models. Dynamic capability, as a core organizational capacity to respond to market shifts, navigate uncertainties, and achieve sustainable innovation, has become central to their transformation and upgrading. Against the backdrop of digital transformation, this study develops an evaluation index system for the dynamic capability of Chinese construction enterprises and investigates its enhancement pathways through empirical analysis. First, based on the dimensions of technological innovation capability, organizational adaptability, and enterprise competitiveness, a system comprising 25 third-level indicators under six second-level indicators was constructed using literature analysis and expert scoring. Second, the Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP) was employed to determine indicator weights, and a cloud model was introduced to define the levels of dynamic capability, addressing the inherent fuzziness and randomness in evaluation. Finally, taking S Construction Group as a case study, this research assesses its dynamic capability by integrating utility functions with the cloud model approach. The results indicate that the dynamic capability of S Construction Group is at a moderate level, and targeted recommendations for improvement are proposed. This study aims to provide a systematic and practical reference for construction enterprises in China to enhance their dynamic capabilities in the era of digital economy.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-53793-w.Keywords: Construction enterprises, Dynamic capability, Cloud modelSubject terms: Engineering, Environmental social sciences, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Against the backdrop of the booming global digital economy and the deep integration of the domestic real economy with digital technologies, leveraging digital transformation to cultivate endogenous development momentum has become a critical pathway for Chinese construction enterprises to achieve high-quality development. As an important pillar of the national economy, construction enterprises are undergoing revolutionary changes in their project management, design, and construction processes with the development of digital technologies such as Building Information Modelling (BIM), the Internet of Things (IoT), big data, and artificial intelligence (AI). However, this transformation not only requires considerable technological and capital investment but also demands that enterprises possess the complex capability to continuously update, upgrade, and restructure their capabilities and resources to maintain and gain a competitive edge. This capability is a firm’s dynamic capability1. Therefore, given the great wave of the digital economy, exploring the improvement path of the dynamic capability of construction enterprises for enterprises to cope with market changes and environmental uncertainties is inevitable. It is highly important for enterprises to overcome the bottleneck of digital transformation and achieve sustainable development1.

As a core theoretical framework in the field of strategic management, dynamic capabilities provide an important perspective for understanding how firms adapt, innovate, and sustain competitive advantage in rapidly changing environments2. In the context of digital transformation, dynamic capabilities not only concern a firm’s ability to adopt digital technologies but also determine whether it can achieve dynamic alignment with the digital environment through continuously adjusting organizational routines and resource structures3. However, significant gaps remain in the research on dynamic capabilities. First, in terms of research subjects, existing studies predominantly focus on manufacturing and other sectors, while paying limited attention to construction enterprises characterized by multi-party involvement, long cycles, and high regulation. Although scholars such as Aghimien et al., (2023)4 have begun to examine the construction field, the evaluation framework and enhancement pathways for dynamic capabilities in this industry still require in-depth investigation. Second, in terms of the research framework, the dimensions of existing dynamic capabilities present diversified characteristics. Teece5, proposed the framework of “sensing-seizing-transforming”, which has laid a solid theoretical foundation. Su et al.6, further extended it from the perspective of sustainable development into three dimensions, namely green innovation capability, social responsibility capability, and operational management capability. Focusing on knowledge management, Shi (2025)7 put forward three dimensions including searching, integration and absorption, providing an important reference for the decomposition of dynamic capabilities in construction enterprises. In addition, regarding research methodology, establishing effective measurement standards for dynamic capabilities remains a bottleneck in practical application. Franco et al.8, argue that dynamic capabilities in the digital economy era largely remain a “black box.” Although some scholars have attempted to employ methods such as grounded theory9, DEMATEL10, and structural equation modelling11 for evaluation, these approaches still exhibit limitations in addressing the inherent ambiguity and complexity of dynamic capability assessment, particularly in simultaneously quantifying qualitative indicators and handling stochastic issues in the evaluation process. In summary, the existing academic community has not yet formed a dynamic capability evaluation framework adapted to the characteristics of the construction industry, nor have there been specialized methods that can effectively address the fuzziness and randomness in the evaluation process. Consequently, current research can hardly provide effective guidance for the practical development of construction enterprises.

In conclusion, the contributions of this study are threefold: First, based on the digital practice of China’s construction industry and focusing on domestic engineering construction enterprises, this study constructs a dynamic capability assessment index system tailored to the characteristics of the construction industry within the background of digital transformation. Second, it innovatively integrates the Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP) for indicator weighting and employs the cloud model to address the fuzziness and randomness in level determination. Finally, it validates the applicability of this framework through an empirical study of S Construction Group. This research aims to establish a scientific and systematic dynamic capability assessment tool, providing an theoretical support and valuable reference for Chinese construction enterprises to effectively enhance their dynamic capability in digital transformation.

Construction of the evaluation index system

Dynamic capability refers to a set of unique and difficult-to-replicate comprehensive abilities that enable enterprises to achieve sustainable competitive advantage in a rapidly changing and intensively globalized business environment. To systematically evaluate the dynamic capabilities of construction enterprises, this study adopts the classic framework proposed by Teece5 as its theoretical starting point. This framework conceptualizes dynamic capability through three interrelated core dimensions: (1) to sense and shape opportunities and threats, (2) to seize opportunities, and (3) to maintain competitiveness through enhancing, combining, protecting, and, when necessary, reconfiguring the business enterprise’s intangible and tangible assets. It is worth noting that although the classic framework of “sensing-seizing-transforming” reveals the general logic of dynamic capabilities, it fails to fully reflect the unique characteristics of the construction industry, such as long project cycles, numerous stakeholders, and project-driven operations. Accordingly, while retaining its three-dimensional logical structure, this study contextualizes and reconstructs the connotation of each dimension in combination with the practical conditions of the construction industry, and further formulates three primary evaluation indicators tailored to the characteristics of construction enterprises: (1) technological innovation capability (corresponding to the sensing dimension), (2) organizational adaptability (corresponding to the seizing dimension), and (3) enterprise competitiveness (corresponding to the reconfiguring dimension). This categorization is also consistent with the multidimensional evaluation approach emphasized by scholars such as Lin et al.12, and van Eechoud et al., (2023)13.

Technological innovation Capability refers to the capacity of an enterprise to coordinate and operate the existing knowledge resources that support technological innovation activities, develop new ideas and innovative solutions, obtain market value, and achieve dynamic organizational development14. It constitutes the fundamental driving force for enterprises to perceive technological trends and market demands. In the context of construction enterprises, this capability is specifically manifested as R&D innovation capability and technology application and promotion capability. The former focuses on the identification and exploration of emerging technological trends, while the latter reflects the initial efforts to translate perceived technological opportunities into practical applications. Together, these two dimensions drive enterprises to integrate new technologies across various stages such as design, construction, and operation and maintenance, thereby enhancing the performance, quality, efficiency, and sustainability of construction products.

Organizational adaptability is the inherent ability of an organization to change its strategy, structure, procedures, or other core attributes in anticipation of or in response to changes in its environment15. Serving as an internal support mechanism for enterprises’ dynamic capabilities, it is concretely manifested as organizational structure adaptability and process automation and optimization capability. The former ensures the organization possesses the necessary agility to respond to market opportunities, while the latter provides critical systematic support for the implementation of these opportunities. These two dimensions collectively enable enterprises to adapt to new market demands and business models by utilizing digital management models to adjust and optimize various aspects such as organizational structure, processes, and culture when confronting internal and external factors including changes in the market environment, technological developments, and policy adjustments16.

Enterprise competitiveness, as the tangible outcome of technological innovation capability and organizational adaptability, is primarily demonstrated through the ability to adapt to and expand the market, as well as the ability to maintain and cooperate with stakeholders. On one hand, the capacity for market adaptation and expansion enables precise identification of demand and rapid market entry, reflecting the enterprise’s ability to reconfigure its market positioning and business models. On the other hand, the capability to maintain and collaborate with stakeholders is pivotal to consolidating resources and creating win-win scenarios. Together, these two dimensions synergistically drive the enterprise toward sustained breakthroughs and resilient growth in competitive environments.

A total of two rounds of expert consultation were conducted in this study. In summary, traditional evaluation methods either rely excessively on subjective settings or fail to fully characterize systematic uncertainty, making them inadequate for addressing the evaluation features of dynamic capability indicators in construction enterprises—such as ambiguous boundaries, subjective experiential factors, and data fluctuations. Therefore, this study adopts the AHP-cloud model for comprehensive evaluation: the AHP method is applied to assign indicator weights, while the cloud model is introduced to handle fuzziness and randomness throughout the evaluation process. This realizes bidirectional mapping between qualitative descriptions and quantitative values, ultimately achieving quantitative grading and visual presentation of construction enterprises’ dynamic capabilities. The findings provide a scientific decision-making basis for improving the dynamic capabilities of construction enterprises amid digital transformation.

Cloud model - evaluating the dynamic capability of enterprises

The cloud model is a mathematical tool designed to handle uncertainties between qualitative concepts and quantitative values. Its core lies in utilizing three numerical characteristics—Expected value (Ex), Entropy (En), and Hyper-entropy (He)—to achieve the transformation and evaluation of qualitative concepts through quantitative means. Ex represents the central value that best characterizes the qualitative concept. En reflects the uncertainty and fuzziness of the qualitative concept; a larger entropy indicates a broader acceptable range for the concept. He is the entropy of entropy, represented as the thickness of the cloud in the cloud diagram. A greater thickness signifies a higher degree of dispersion in the membership degrees33.

Case studies

Case background

This study takes the S Construction Group, a large construction engineering enterprise in China, as the research object. The group is a large construction enterprise with a registered capital of 7.215 billion yuan and over 2,000 professional technicians It possesses the national first-class construction enterprise qualification and has been awarded honors such as the “China Construction Engineering Luban Prize” on multiple occasions. Its business scope covers construction engineering, infrastructure, industrial construction, environmental engineering, and other fields, providing clients with one-stop services ranging from project pre-consultation to post-construction maintenance. Since 2017, the group has initiated a digital transformation, systematically applying technologies such as BIM, big data, and the Internet of Things in projects, and introducing intelligent management platforms like ERP, CRM, and OA. Its transformation practices provide a typical case scenario for this study.

Conclusions

Theoretical contributions

(1) Based on the requirements of the digital economy for construction enterprise transformation and by combining a literature review and the Delphi method to screen dynamic capability evaluation indicators, a system of evaluation indicators was established. This system includes six secondary indicators and 25 third-level indicators across three dimensions: technological innovation ability, organizational adaptability, and enterprise competitiveness. The system underwent expert consistency checks and scoring-based screening, with five third-level indicators eliminated for insufficient importance to ensure the scientific validity and practicality of the system.

(2) The Analytic Hierarchy Process was adopted to determine indicator weights, balancing subjective experience by integrating expert-provided weights to ensure rationality. Moreover, the cloud model was introduced to handle evaluation uncertainty, transforming the data for each indicator into three cloud parameters: Ex, En, and He. The dynamic capabilities of construction enterprises were classified into five grades: excellent, good, medium, poor, and extremely poor.

(3) The empirical study using S Construction Group as an example demonstrates that this comprehensive AHP-Cloud Model evaluation method can effectively quantify the dynamic capability levels of construction enterprises, providing a novel and reliable analytical tool for unveiling the “black box” of corporate dynamic capabilities in construction enterprises.

Practical Contributions

Based on an in-depth analysis of the evaluation results regarding the dynamic capabilities of S Construction Group, four core indicators—organizational change response time (B11), the acceptance of change by employees (B14), new technology development cycle (A13), and R&D investment intensity (A11)—have been identified as the key shortcomings restricting the improvement of the enterprise’s dynamic capabilities. Furthermore, inherent correlations exist among these deficiencies: redundant organizational structures delay change responses, conservative corporate culture hinders employees’ acceptance of transformation, departmental barriers extend research and development cycles, and inefficient resource allocation weakens the value of R&D activities. Collectively, these factors constrain the overall upgrading of dynamic capabilities. Accordingly, this study systematically proposes targeted improvement pathways and decision support for managers of construction enterprises.

(1) Decentralize authority and restructure the management system.

To address the lag in organizational change response, the root cause lies in the traditional multi-level organizational structure, which often results in delayed information transmission and overly lengthy decision-making chains, making it difficult to adapt to rapidly changing market demands. Enterprises should systematically review and optimize core management processes by eliminating redundant approval procedures, formulating standardized operation manuals and process documents, synchronizing the notification boards of platforms such as OA and ERP, and clarifying job responsibilities for all positions to avoid decision delays and mutual shirking of responsibilities. Meanwhile, for urgent change requirements, temporary rapid response teams should be authorized and established. With team leaders directly empowered by senior management of the Group to deploy cross-departmental resources, the decision-making process can be shortened, enabling the organization to respond agilely to external changes.

(2) Optimize institutional mechanisms to reshape the reform-oriented corporate culture.

To address the insufficient acceptance of change among employees, the core cause lies in the enterprise’s long-standing rigid risk-averse mindset and inadequate incentive mechanisms, which tend to foster a wait-and-see and resistant attitude toward digital transformation. Employees commonly struggle with incompetence, reluctance, and fear when adopting new digital tools. Therefore, the enterprise must start with institutional design by establishing an innovation tolerance mechanism that tolerates failures and encourages learning. A reward fund for change participation should be set up to grant performance bonuses or special incentives to teams that proactively propose and participate in pilot transformation projects. Even if projects fail to meet expectations, partial rewards may still be offered as long as replicable experiences and lessons are summarized. Meanwhile, regular training programs on digital literacy should be implemented, and internal digital platforms should showcase successful transformation cases and employee growth stories to visualize the outcomes of reform. Furthermore, participation in change initiatives shall be incorporated into annual performance evaluations and promotion criteria, so as to positively guide employees to embrace new workflows and tools actively, reverse the traditional passive working mindset, and cultivate an organizational culture that proactively embraces digital transformation.

(3) Cross-departmental Collaboration and Compression of R&D Iteration Time.

To address the prolonged new technology development cycle caused by inefficient cross-departmental communication and fragmented R&D processes, a regular mechanism for cross-departmental technical research shall be established. Core professionals from the Design Institute, frontline engineering departments, digital centers and other teams will be integrated to conduct targeted research on practical difficulties encountered on construction sites. A short-cycle iterative mode will be adopted to rapidly develop prototypes and implement pilot verifications. Meanwhile, data interfaces across various business systems will be interconnected to build a unified collaborative platform for technological R&D, enabling real-time demand submission, transparent progress tracking, and online achievement reviews, thereby eliminating information silos. In addition, frontline engineers will be encouraged to initiate small-scale independent R&D projects based on on-site construction pain points, with dedicated micro-funding and technical support allocated. This helps shorten the technology development cycle and enhance the capability of rapid technical response.

(4) Achievement Reuse and Resource Allocation Optimization.

To maximize the value of R&D investment, enterprises must reform the R&D assessment system and achieve targeted allocation of resources. It shall be stipulated that upon the completion of all R&D projects, standardized reusable operation manuals must be delivered and filed after acceptance by the Group’s technical management department. Meanwhile, a profit-sharing mechanism for innovative achievements shall be established: if a technology is adopted secondarily by other projects or subsidiaries, the original R&D team can receive a certain proportion of dividends from the net profit, forming a profit-driven operational model for R&D investment. In addition, an industry-university-research collaborative fund shall be set up to jointly tackle key technical challenges with universities and research institutions through shared intellectual property rights, thereby reducing internal R&D costs. These measures guide resources toward R&D projects with high reusability and transformation potential, improving the overall efficiency of R&D input.

It should be pointed out that these recommendations are mainly targeted at S Construction Group and derived from a single case study. The generalizability of these specific suggestions may be limited, and they should be appropriately adjusted according to the actual situation, characteristics and development background of other enterprises. Importantly, the proposed AHP-Cloud Model evaluation framework is generalizable and can be applied by managers in other construction enterprises or similar contexts. By adopting this framework and combining their own actual conditions, enterprises can systematically evaluate their dynamic capabilities, identify key areas for improvement, and formulate targeted optimization strategies based on their own data. Future studies may further validate the applicability of the proposed recommendations across multiple cases or different industry contexts.

Limitations

This study has certain limitations. The empirical analysis is based on a single case, which may limit the generalizability of the findings. Although the AHP-Cloud Model developed in this study provides a systematic evaluation framework, its practical effectiveness may vary in enterprises with different organizational structures, corporate cultures, and developmental stages. Future research could apply this framework to multiple firms across diverse contexts to further validate its robustness.

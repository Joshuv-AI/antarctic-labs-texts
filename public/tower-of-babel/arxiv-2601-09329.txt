Generalized Schalkwijk-Kailath Coding for Autoregressive Gaussian Channels
Jun Su Guangyue Han Shlomo Shamai (Shitz) The University of Hong Kong The University of Hong Kong Technion-Israel Institute of Technology email: junsu@hku.hk email: ghan@hku.hk email: sshlomo@ee.technion.ac.il

Abstract
We propose a Gaussian random coding scheme for AR( p p ) Gaussian channels that generalizes the celebrated Schalkwijk-Kailath (SK) coding scheme. This constructive coding scheme, termed the SK(2) coding scheme, yields a closed-form characterization for the corresponding achievable rate. Among many others, this result shows that the celebrated SK coding scheme is not universally optimal, and therefore, disprove the conjecture proposed by Butman in [ 2 ] .

3
The Schalkwijk–Kailath Coding

It is well-known that the SK coding scheme achieves capacity for the AWGN channel with noiseless feedback under an average power constraint
P
. In the spirit of the exposition in
[
5
, Section 17.1.1]
, the classical SK coding scheme can be reformulated in the following. Consider the discrete-time AWGN channel

Y_{i}=X_{i}+W_{i},\penalty\ \penalty\ i\geq 1,

(4)

where
\{W_{i}\}
a white Gaussian process with zero mean and unit variance and
\{X_{i}\}
are subject to the average power constraint
P
, i.e.,

\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\mathbb{E}[X_{i}^{2}]\leq nP.

The encoding scheme proceeds as follows. At time
i=0
, the encoder transmits a symbol
X_{0}
chosen arbitrarily from the interval
[-\sqrt{P},\sqrt{P}]
, yielding the output
Y_{0}=X_{0}+W_{0}
. Because of noiseless feedback, the encoder learns
Y_{0}
and can compute the noise realization
W_{0}=Y_{0}-X_{0}
. At time
i=1
, the encoder transmits
X_{1}=\gamma_{1}W_{0}
, where
\gamma_{1}=\sqrt{P}
ensures that
\mathbb{E}[X_{1}^{2}]=P
. For each subsequent time
i\geq 2
, the encoder computes the minimum mean-square error (MMSE) estimate
\mathbb{E}[W_{0}|Y_{1}^{i-1}]
based on all previous channel outputs
Y_{1}^{i-1}
available via feedback. It then transmits

X_{i}=\gamma_{i}(W_{0}-\mathbb{E}[W_{0}|Y_{1}^{i-1}]),

(5)

where
\gamma_{i}
is chosen such that
\mathbb{E}[X_{i}^{2}]=P
for all
i
. The SK coding scheme, given in (
5
), admits a recursive interpretation in which the transmitter repeatedly refines the receiver’s knowledge of the initial noise
W_{0}
, equivalently, the original message
X_{0}
, across successive uses of the channel. Specifically, the encoding rule can be written as

\displaystyle X_{i}

\displaystyle=\gamma_{i}(W_{0}-\mathbb{E}[W_{0}|Y_{1}^{i-1}])

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma_{i}}{\gamma_{i-1}}\left(X_{i-1}+\mathbb{E}[X_{i-1}|Y_{1}^{i-1}]\right)

so that each transmission corrects the receiver’s estimation error incurred in the previous transmission.

Furthermore, define the process
V_{i}\triangleq\gamma_{i}W_{0}
. Since
W_{0}
is independent of the forward noise
\{W_{i}\}_{i\geq 1}
,
\{V_{i}\}
may be regarded as the underlying
message process
. Under this representation, the SK rule takes the following form

X_{i}=V_{i}-\mathbb{E}[V_{i}|Y_{1}^{i-1}],

(6)

where
\{V_{i}\}
follows the deterministic recursion

V_{i}=\frac{\gamma_{i}}{\gamma_{i-1}}V_{i-1},\penalty\ \penalty\ i\geq 2

(7)

with initial condition
V_{1}=\sqrt{P}W_{0}
. It has been shown
[
4
,
5
,
11
,
12
]
that for any such sequence
\{\gamma_{i}\}_{i\geq 1}
, this SK coding scheme (
5
), or equivalently, the recursive form (
6
) and (
7
), achieves the feedback capacity of the AWGN channel.

We now introduce a specialized instance of the SK coding scheme, which we term the SK(1) coding scheme. The name reflects that the underlying message process follows a first-order deterministic recursion. It is obtained from the recursive form in (
6
) and (
7
) by imposing the simple ratio condition

\frac{\gamma_{i}}{\gamma_{i-1}}=\gamma\penalty\ \penalty\ \mbox{for all}\penalty\ i,

where
|\gamma|>1
. Under this condition, the message process
V=\{V_{i}\}
follows a first-order deterministic recursion. Formally, the SK(1) coding scheme is defined by

X_{i}=V_{i}-\mathbb{E}[V_{i}|Y_{1}^{i-1}]

(8)

with the message process
\{V_{i}\}
such that

\begin{cases}V_{i+1}=\gamma V_{i},&i\geq 1,\\
V_{1}=U,\end{cases}

(9)

where
U
is a standard Gaussian random variable, independent of the noise process
Z=\{Z_{i}\}
. We define the corresponding
SK(1) channel capacity
as

C_{\mathrm{SK1}}(P)=\sup_{(V,X)}\bar{I}(V;Y),

(10)

where
\bar{I}(V;Y)
denotes the mutual information rate between the processes
\{V_{i}\}
and
\{Y_{i}\}
, and the supremum is taken over all pairs
(V,X)
satisfying (
8
) and (
9
) under the asymptotic average power constraint

\varlimsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}[X_{i}^{2}]\leq P.

(11)

Note that the SK(1) coding scheme, which can be directly transformed into the original constructive SK coding, has been established as capacity-achieving for stationary ARMA(1,1) Gaussian channels with feedback
[

The SK(2) Coding Scheme

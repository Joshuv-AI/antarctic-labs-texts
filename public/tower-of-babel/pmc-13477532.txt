Primary hepatic leiomyosarcoma diagnosed after right hepatectomy: A rare case report
Authors: Toshikatsu Nitta, Masatsugu Ishii, Ryutaro Kubo, Akitada Sada, Atsuhiro Komiya, Atsushi Takeshita, Takashi Ishibashi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This is a case report of primary hepatic leiomyosarcoma. An 82-year-old Japanese man presented with fever and mild abdominal pain. Laboratory testing demonstrated marked inflammation and substantial elevations of CA19-9 and PIVKA-II. Computed tomography revealed a large necrotic mass in the right hepatic lobe with gallbladder wall thickening. Gallbladder cancer invading the liver with abscess formation was suspected, but a definitive preoperative diagnosis could not be established. Right hepatectomy and cholecystectomy achieved an R0 resection. Histopathological examination showed fascicles of spindle cells, extensive necrosis, and high mitotic activity. Immunohistochemical staining was positive for α-smooth muscle actin and h-caldesmon and negative for desmin and epithelial markers, confirming primary hepatic leiomyosarcoma. Although abscess-like presentations have been reported, the combination of severe inflammatory findings, markedly elevated CA19-9 and PIVKA-II, and gallbladder wall thickening was unusual and contributed to diagnostic confusion. This case emphasizes the need to consider primary hepatic leiomyosarcoma in the differential diagnosis of a large necrotic hepatic mass and supports complete surgical resection when technically feasible.

Introduction

Primary hepatic leiomyosarcoma (PHL) is an exceptionally rare primary mesenchymal malignancy of the liver. A comprehensive analysis published in 2012 identified 109 reported patients, whereas a later review of English-language reports from 2000 to 2023 included 30 cases with sufficiently detailed clinical and imaging information.1,2 The diagnosis remains difficult before histopathological confirmation because clinical presentation and radiologic findings are nonspecific.3,4 On imaging, PHL may appear as a hypoechoic or heterogeneous mass on ultrasonography, a hypodense heterogeneous lesion with peripheral or inhomogeneous enhancement on computed tomography, and a heterogeneous mass with low T1-weighted and high T2-weighted signal intensity on magnetic resonance imaging.5–9

In the present patient, the combination of fever, marked inflammatory findings, a large necrotic right-lobe tumor, and gallbladder wall thickening led to preoperative suspicion of gallbladder carcinoma invading the liver with abscess formation. We report this case to clarify the pathological basis of the diagnosis and to highlight its distinctive combination of severe inflammation, marked CA19-9 and PIVKA-II elevation, and biliary-adjacent imaging findings. Methods

This study is a single case report. The reporting of this study conforms to the CARE guidelines11). All patient information has been fully anonymized, and no identifiable data are included in this report. Written informed consent for publication was obtained from the patient. This case was retrospectively selected from our institutional clinical database.

Case description

An 82-year-old Japanese man presented to our hospital with low-grade fever and mild abdominal pain. Physical examination revealed distension of the right upper abdomen. Laboratory tests on admission showed severe inflammation (WBC, 16,200/μL; CRP, 31.2 mg/dL) and elevated hepatobiliary enzyme levels. CA19-9 (932.5 U/mL) and PIVKA-II (484 mAU/mL) were also elevated (Table 1). Contrast-enhanced abdominal computed tomography (CT) revealed a heterogeneously enhancing hypodense lesion involving segments V and VIII and occupying the right lobe, together with gallbladder wall thickening (Figure 1). Magnetic resonance imaging demonstrated an approximately 9-cm right-lobe tumor with low signal intensity on T1-weighted images and heterogeneous internal enhancement with poorly defined margins after contrast administration (Figure 2). Abdominal ultrasonography showed a heterogeneous mass in the right hepatic lobe (Figure 3). Systemic evaluation of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis revealed no primary smooth-muscle tumor in another organ or in the retroperitoneum (Figure 4). The patient underwent right hepatectomy and cholecystectomy because of the strong suspicion of malignancy. The operating time was 363 min, and blood loss was 600 mL without transfusion. Gross examination showed a large heterogeneous solid tumor with hemorrhage and necrosis (Figure 5). After 6 months of follow-up, the patient showed no symptoms or signs of recurrence and was later transferred to the other palliative care hospital.

Histopathological examination

The resected specimen contained a tumor measuring 108 × 100 × 80 mm and weighing 370 g. On cut section, the tumor was whitish and solid, with areas of hemorrhage and focal necrosis (Figure 5).

Immunohistochemical staining was positive for α-smooth muscle actin (α-SMA) (Figure 6(b)), h-caldesmon (Figure 6(c)), and BCL-6, and negative for CAM 5.2, desmin (Figure 6(d)), calponin, S-100, c-kit, DOG1, CD34, MDM2, and CDK4. These findings established the diagnosis of primary hepatic leiomyosarcoma. The mitotic count was >10 per 10 high-power fields (HPFs), and the Ki-67 proliferation index was approximately 10%. These findings supported the diagnosis of leiomyosarcoma.

Discussion

PHL remains difficult to diagnose preoperatively because its symptoms, laboratory findings, and imaging appearances overlap with those of more common hepatic tumors and inflammatory lesions. Fever and an abscess-like hepatic lesion are not unique to the present case. Liu and Liang reported a 38-year-old woman with fever and a 13.6-cm thick-walled cystic mass, and Wu et al. reported a 34-year-old woman with fever and a 9.7-cm right-lobe lesion initially interpreted as a liver abscess.10,12 However, tumor markers in PHL are usually normal. In the 30-case review by Yan et al., CA19-9 was only slightly elevated in two patients. 2 In contrast, our patient had CA19-9 of 932.5 U/mL and PIVKA-II of 484 mAU/mL, together with gallbladder wall thickening. This combination led to the preoperative suspicion of gallbladder cancer invading the liver with abscess formation and represents the principal distinctive clinical contribution of this report.

Definitive diagnosis requires morphological and immunohistochemical evaluation. The present tumor consisted of spindle-shaped cells in intersecting fascicles with extensive necrosis and more than 10 mitoses per 10 high-power fields. The tumor was positive for α-SMA and h-caldesmon, supporting smooth-muscle differentiation, but negative for desmin. Desmin negativity does not exclude leiomyosarcoma when the morphology and more specific smooth-muscle markers are supportive. Negative staining for CAM5.2, S-100, c-kit, DOG1, CD34, MDM2, CDK4, and ALK-1 also helped exclude sarcomatoid carcinoma, gastrointestinal stromal tumor, neural tumors, liposarcoma, and inflammatory myofibroblastic tumor.

Surgical resection with negative margins remains the most established potentially curative treatment for localized PHL.1,13 In the analysis by Chi et al., the median overall survival was 19 months and the 5-year survival rate was 14.5%, although smaller tumor size and complete resection were associated with better outcomes. 1 The roles of adjuvant chemotherapy and radiotherapy remain uncertain, and systemic regimens are generally extrapolated from the treatment of soft-tissue sarcoma.12,14

In the present case, right hepatectomy achieved an R0 resection without major postoperative complications, despite the patient’s advanced age. This observation supports consideration of major hepatectomy in carefully selected older patients with adequate physiological and hepatic reserve; however, it does not establish an age-based treatment recommendation. No adjuvant chemotherapy was administered. No recurrence was detected during the 6-month follow-up period before transfer to another hospital.

This report has limitations. It describes a single patient, the postoperative oncologic follow-up was short, and no molecular analysis was performed. Moreover, Table 2 is a focused comparison of major reviews and representative clinically similar reports and should not be interpreted as a new systematic review. Nevertheless, the case expands awareness of the marked inflammatory and tumor-marker abnormalities that may accompany PHL and cause diagnostic anchoring on infection or biliary malignancy.15–20

Conclusion

PHL should be considered in the differential diagnosis of a large necrotic hepatic mass with abscess-like features, particularly when inflammatory findings and tumor-marker abnormalities are disproportionate or atypical for infection.

Histopathological and immunohistochemical examination is essential for definitive diagnosis, and complete surgical resection remains the principal treatment for resectable disease. The distinctive contribution of this case is the combined presentation of severe inflammation, marked CA19-9 and PIVKA-II elevation, and gallbladder wall thickening that mimicked gallbladder cancer with hepatic abscess formation.

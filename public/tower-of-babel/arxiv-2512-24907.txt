Abstract
. We obtain some d ≥ 2 d\geq 2 such that every graph G G with no induced copy of the five-vertex path P 5 P_{5} has chromatic number at most ω ​ ( G ) d \omega(G)^{d} , thereby resolving an open problem of Gyárfás from 1985. 2.2.
Decomposing along high-
\chi
anticomplete pairs

The core idea of structural graph theory is to decompose graphs with forbidden substructures into simpler pieces.
In the context of polynomial
\chi
-boundedness, the most dramatic application of this idea is the proof of the strong perfect graph theorem
MR2233847
; and recently a variety of decomposition techniques have been instrumental in establishing polynomial
\chi
-boundedness for circle graphs
MR4275079
, bounded clique-width graphs
MR4125349
, and bounded twin-width graphs
MR4865478
.
The common theme of all these proofs is that the “simpler pieces” in question actually belong to (considerably) more structured hereditary classes than those of their corresponding host graphs. These more structured classes are known to be polynomially
\chi
-bounded (or even perfect in the cases of
MR2233847
;
MR4275079
), and analysing the corresponding decomposition types (in other words, how the decompositions were formulated) would lead to the desired polynomial
\chi
-boundedness (or perfectness in the case of
MR2233847
).

Our second ingredient of the proof of
Theorem

2.1
will be an argument that decomposes
P_{5}
-free graphs along high-

anticomplete pairs, first developed in
(

, Section 4)
(under the name “terminal partitions”) and subsequently refined in

MR2233847
;
MR4275079
;
MR4865478
;
MR4125349
is that the “simpler pieces” in our decomposition are not more structured than general
P_{5}
-free graphs (and so not necessarily structurally simpler); but the interaction between these pieces and the constraints on their chromatic number will be useful in capturing high-
\chi
complete pairs or polynomially

-dense induced subgraphs (all with appropriate parameters).
Roughly speaking, the decomposition consists of a “central” cutset together with several connected components of the remainder satisfying certain conditions on chromatic number.
This will be detailed in the proof of

, which will be the main technical step in the proof of the following main result of

2.3.
Chromatic density increment

The method of density increment is a fundamental idea in additive combinatorics, and has been an inspiration behind recent progress on long-standing open problems in graph theory such as the Erdős–Hajnal conjecture

MR4761787
;
density3

The general strategy is that given a

This strategy is very much inspired by the method of iterative sparsification behind recent work on Erdős–Hajnal; and indeed our chromatic density increment argument in this paper will be modelled after the two-round iterative sparsification argument for complements of

-free graphs in

There are a number of nontrivial technical issues arising from this approach, however, mainly because chromatic number is generally not amenable to counting.
To explain, most of the counting-based steps in

, Lemma 2.1)
are no longer applicable in our current setting of

-dense graphs.

Remarks on measures

For a graph
G
, a
measure
on
G
is a function
\mu\colon V(G)\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
satisfying the following axioms:

•

\mu(\emptyset)=0
and
\mu(\{v\})=1
for all
v\in V(G)
;

•

\max(\mu(A),\mu(B))\leq\mu(A\cup B)
for all
A,B\subseteq V(G)
, where equality holds if
A,B
are disjoint and
A
is anticomplete to
B
in
G
; and

•

\mu(A\cup B)\leq\mu(A)+\mu(B)
for all
A,B\subseteq V(G)
, where equality holds if
A,B
are disjoint and
A
is complete to
B
in
G
.

(In particular, the second axiom implies that
\mu
is increasing monotone with respect to inclusion.)
For each such measure
\mu
, let
\mu(F):=\mu(V(F))
for every induced subgraph
F
of
G
.
Then it is not hard to see that clique number, fractional chromatic number, and chromatic number are all measures on any given graph.
Moreover, clique number and chromatic number, respectively, are the “weakest” and “strongest” measures in the sense that
\omega(G)\leq\mu(G)\leq\chi(G)
for all graphs
G
and all measures
\mu
on
G
.
Indeed, the first inequality is evident from the third axiom (including its equality case) and the monotone property of measures.
To see the second inequality, consider a partition
(V_{1},\ldots,V_{\chi(G)})
into
\chi(G)
stable sets in
G
and observe that
\mu(G)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{\chi(G)}\mu(V_{i})=\chi(G)
; here the first estimate follows from the third axiom, and the second follows from the first axiom and the equality case of the second axiom.

We would like to remark that throughout this paper, the proof of
Theorem

2.1
only relies on the above three “measure” properties of
\chi
and explores the “chromatic density” aspect of
P_{5}
-free graphs based on these properties.
In fact, there is only one place in the proof where the equality case of the third axiom is used (in the proof of
5.4.1
); and this can be avoided since we can strengthen the notion of “maximal components” (defined prior to
Lemma

3.2
) by requiring the component
C
in the definition to have maximal cardinality in addition. Nevertheless, to keep our proof as natural as possible and avoid unnecessary technical details, we decided to employ the third axiom’s equality case for
\chi
.
Our proof of
Theorem

2.1
also does not involve clique number, which is perhaps another notable difference compared to previous papers in (polynomial)
\chi
-boundedness.

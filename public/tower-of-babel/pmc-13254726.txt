Multi-omics analysis identifies CKLF as a promoter for HCC progression by regulating the AKT, ERK pathways, and infiltrating immune cells
Authors: Lei Peng, Jingjing Li, Ji Yuan, Shuangshuang Ma, Lei Jin, Guizhou Zou, Lei Tang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Highlights•CKLF is significantly upregulated in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) and correlates with advanced tumor stage, aggressive molecular subtypes, and poor patient survival.•Single-cell and spatial transcriptomics reveal dual CKLF expression in both malignant hepatocytes and cytotoxic immune infiltrates (CD8⁺ T cells and M1 macrophages), implicating its role in tumor–immune crosstalk.•CKLF knockdown suppresses HCC cell proliferation, migration, and clonogenicity in vitro, highlighting its functional importance in tumor aggressiveness.•Oncogenic effects of CKLF are mediated through activation of the AKT/ERK signaling axis, as constitutively active Ras rescues phenotypic deficits caused by CKLF silencing.•Integrative multi-omic analyses position CKLF as a pivotal molecular node linking genomic alterations, stromal remodeling, and immunophenotypic shifts, underscoring its potential as a biomarker and therapeutic target in HCC.Keywords: CKLF, AKT/ERK pathway, Tumor stroma, Omic analysis, Hepatocellular carcinoma

Introduction

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) remains a leading cause of cancer-related mortality worldwide, with dismal outcomes for patients diagnosed at advanced stages despite recent advances in kinase inhibitors and immunotherapy [[1], [2], [3]]. The primary challenge lies in the profound molecular heterogeneity of HCC and the lack of precise, actionable targets that can effectively disrupt tumor progression or remodel the immunosuppressive microenvironment [4]. Therefore, identifying novel drivers that bridge intrinsic oncogenic signaling with extrinsic immune regulation is critical for developing individualized therapeutic strategies.

Among potential candidates, Chemokine-like factor (CKLF) has emerged as a molecule of significant interest. Structurally distinct yet functionally similar to conventional chemokines, CKLF binds to G protein–coupled receptors (e.g., CCR1 and CCR4) to activate downstream cascades governing inflammation, tissue repair, and cell migration [5,6]. While aberrant CKLF expression has been implicated in the proliferation and metastasis of various malignancies [[7], [8], [9], [10]], its role in HCC remains paradoxical and poorly defined. Unlike classical chemokines that primarily recruit immune cells, CKLF appears to exhibit a "dual role," potentially acting directly on tumor cells to drive growth while simultaneously modulating the tumor microenvironment (TME) [[7], [8], [9], [10]]. However, a comprehensive understanding of CKLF’s genomic landscape, its regulation across specific HCC molecular subtypes, and its precise mechanistic contribution to HCC progression is currently lacking.

Despite these insights, critical knowledge gaps regarding CKLF in HCC persist. Specifically, the genomic landscape of CKLF remains uncharacterized. Furthermore, while its prognostic value is suggested, the precise correlation between CKLF expression levels, distinct molecular subtypes, and specific immune phenotypes within the HCC microenvironment has not been systematically defined. Most importantly, the mechanistic underpinnings of CKLF-driven tumorigenesis are unclear: it remains to be determined whether CKLF promotes HCC progression by directly activating oncogenic signaling axes (PI3K/AKT or MAPK/ERK) or indirectly by reprogramming the functional state of infiltrating lymphocytes and macrophages to foster immune evasion. Resolving these ambiguities is a prerequisite for validating CKLF as both a robust biomarker and a viable therapeutic target.

To address these challenges, we established a multi-omics platform integrating bulk transcriptomics, single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq), spatial transcriptomics, and genome-wide screening to dissect the role of CKLF in HCC comprehensively. In this study, we not only validate CKLF as a top-ranked prognostic driver through rigorous machine learning approaches but also elucidate its dual mechanism: promoting tumor cell proliferation via key signaling pathways and reshaping the immune landscape towards an exhausted phenotype. Our findings provide a biological rationale for targeting CKLF, offering new insights into patient stratification and potential combination therapies for HCC.

Materials and methods

Single-cell preprocessing and integration

We performed computational analysis in R programming environment using Seurat package and special codes. Quality control of cells was kept for expression from 200 to 2500 genes but removing any cell that had >10% of the expressed genes on mitochondria; then we removed duplicates and poor-quality clusters.Data were normalized with the SCTransform method and integrated across different HCC scRNA-seq data sets based on the Harmony algorithm,where we used UMAP for visualizing dimensionality reduction. We clustered using the Louvain algorithm with resolution value [11,12].

Spatial data preprocessing and normalization

Raw count matrices were processed using the Seurat packages in R. Quality control (QC) was strictly applied to filter out low-quality spatial spots: spots containing fewer than 500 detected unique molecular identifiers (UMIs) or genes, and spots with a mitochondrial gene percentage exceeding 20%, were excluded from downstream analysis. To ensure robust comparability of gene expression levels across different patient samples and to mitigate technical variations (e.g., sequencing depth), data normalization was performed using the SCTransform method . This approach stabilizes variance and removes the confounding effect of sequencing depth while regressing out the percentage of mitochondrial genes. To resolve the cellular composition within each spatial spot, we employed RCTD. Our previously generated single-cell RNA sequencing dataset from matched HCC cohorts served as the high-resolution reference profile. For the generation of heatmaps and spatial expression plots, consistent color scales were applied across all samples. RCTD was used for determining the location of macrophages and cancer cells found in the scRNAseq dataset (https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.abg3750).

Immune features and checkpoint landscape

We used bulk RNA-seq data for studying gene expression levels of critical immune checkpoint proteins, such as PD-1,CD274, CTLA4, LAG3, TIGIT), antigen processing molecules (HLA-A/B/C, HLA-DRA, B2M) and chemotactic factors (CCR1/4, CXCR3) in immune cells.In the TCGA-LIHC and ICGC databases, three kinds of software(xCell, EPIC, ssGSEA) are used for predicting infiltrating proportion of immune cells,followed by a Spearman correlation test with multiple hypothesis correction (FDR). Correlations were calculated against the expression of CKLF.

Survival analyses

Kaplan-Meier plots were constructed and compared via log-rank tests for significance of difference in overall survival when patients are categorized according to their respective CKLF expression level, split on the median value. Significant prognostic indicators included age ofstage of the disease, and etiology have been adjusted into multivariate Cox regression analysis.

Cell lines and culture

The human hepatocellular carcinoma cell lines Huh7 and LM3 as well as the immortalized hepatocytes line MIHA were purchased from the Chinese Academy of Sciences Cell Repository. The cells were cultured using Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle Medium (Gibco) supplemented with 10% FBS and antibiotic solution (penicillin/streptomycin; 1%) at standard culture conditions (37 °C,(5% CO 2 humidified atmosphere).All experiments are performed with exponentially growing cells having a cell viability of >95%.

Wound-healing assay

For wound healing assays, Huh7 cells were seeded into a 6 well plate and grown to confluency. A wound was introduced by dragging an unused sterile pipette tip with a volume of 200 µl,and after two PBS washes, cells were transferred into starvation conditions. In order to analyze migration without confounding cell growth,cells were pre-treated by mitomycin C(10 μg/mL, 2 h).Cellular migration into the wound was monitored every 48 h by counting cells in selected regions of interest that were visualized using phase-contrast light microscopy following fixation.

CCK-8 proliferation assay

Cells were seeded at a density of 3 × 10³/well into 96-well plates and the outermost wells were filled with PBS for control purposes. After indicated periods (0,24 h, 48 h, 72 h, 96 h later, 10μl CCK-8 reagent(Dojindo) was added in each hole and incubated at 37 °C for 2 h respectively. The OD values at 450 nm were measured with subtracting blank value. All cell viability curves were calculated as relative to T = 0.At least three biological replicates (with three or more technical replicates) were used for the study.

Immunohistochemistry (IHC)

Paraffin-embedded tissue sections fixed with formalin were deparaffinized, hydrated, and subjected to antigen retrieval in [sodium citrate/EDTA] buffer. 3% H₂O₂ was used to block endogenous peroxidase, and 5% normal serum was used to block non-specific sites. The sections were incubated with [target protein] primary antibody (dilution ratio 1:100) at 4 °C overnight, followed by incubation with HRP-labeled secondary antibody. DAB staining was performed, and hematoxylin was used for counterstaining. Two pathologists independently evaluated the staining intensity and the proportion of positive cells, and calculated the immunoreactivity score.

Statistics for functional assays

The results of functional tests were shown as mean ± SD based on at least three independent biological repeats; the comparison between two groups was performed using two-tailed Student’s t-test,where comparisons between several groups were performed with one-way ANOVA followed by Tukey’s test, and significance level for a given result was defined as both the absolute value of log2-fold change above 1 or equal to 1 and p-value <0.05 [24].

Result

Integrated machine learning screening identifies CKLF as a core prognostic driver in HCC

To systematically identify robust molecular regulators driving hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) progression, we integrated transcriptomic data from multiple large-scale cohorts (TCGA-LIHC, ICGC-LIRI, and GEO datasets). We employed a consensus machine learning strategy combining Random Forest for feature importance ranking and Elastic Net regularized regression for robust feature selection.

Our cross-cohort analysis demonstrated that the Elastic Net model provided the most stable predictive performance, significantly outperforming Lasso, Ridge, and stepwise Cox models in terms of time-dependent AUC across independent datasets (Fig. 1A). This model identified a concise 29-gene prognostic signature. Among these candidates, CKLF emerged as a top-ranked feature with a strong positive coefficient, indicating that its upregulation is consistently associated with poor overall survival (OS) and disease-free survival (DFS). In contrast, genes such as ZKSCAN1 exhibited negative coefficients, suggesting a protective role (Fig. 1B).

Using this signature, we constructed a risk score model that effectively stratified patients into high- and low-risk groups. This stratification was statistically significant across all six independent validation cohorts (log-rank p < 0.05 for all; Fig. 1C), confirming the external validity of our screening approach. Crucially, CKLF stood out not only for its statistical weight in the prognostic model but also for its consistent ranking across different algorithms. While other genes like USP2 and JPH1 were also identified, the prominence of CKLF prioritized it as the most promising candidate for further functional investigation. Consequently, subsequent analyses focused specifically on dissecting the molecular mechanisms and therapeutic potential of CKLF in HCC.

Robustness validation of CKLF as a top-ranked predictor via ensemble machine learning

To ensure the identified prognostic markers were not artifacts of a single algorithm, we employed an ensemble machine learning approach to validate feature importance and generalizability. First, Random Forest analysis was performed across iterative runs to assess gene stability. While several genes showed high importance scores, CKLF consistently emerged as a top-tier predictor, maintaining high ranking across diverse data subsets (Fig. 2A). Permutation tests further confirmed that the predictive power of CKLF was statistically significant and superior to many other candidates, including ITPA and ZKSCAN1, which showed weaker or less consistent associations (Fig. 2B).

Subsequently, we utilized regularized regression paths to evaluate the stability of gene coefficients under varying penalty parameters (λ). As the regularization parameter decreased, CKLF was among the first genes to enter the model and maintained a large, stable coefficient, indicating its indispensable role in predicting patient outcomes (Fig. 2C). This cross-method consistency—where CKLF was highlighted by both non-linear (Random Forest) and linear (Regularized Regression) models—strongly supports its biological relevance over other potential candidates. These results provide a rigorous, data-driven foundation for prioritizing CKLF as the primary target for subsequent mechanistic and therapeutic investigations in HCC.

CKLF drives oncogenic signaling and tumor aggressiveness in hepatocellular carcinoma and predicts poor survival

To elucidate the functional role of CKLF in HCC, we integrated differential gene expression profiling, pathway activation analysis, and clinical outcome data. Our analysis revealed a significant correlation between elevated CKLF expression and the activation of key oncogenic signaling cascades. Specifically, tumors with high CKLF levels exhibited markedly enhanced phosphorylation of ERK (Wilcoxon test, p = 0.003) and AKT (Wilcoxon test, p = 0.008) compared to low-expression counterparts (Fig. 4A and 4B), suggesting that CKLF promotes cellular proliferation and survival through these critical pathways. Furthermore, Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) confirmed that high CKLF expression is strongly associated with core biological processes driving tumor progression, including [insert specific pathways here, e.g., cell cycle regulation, EMT, or metabolic reprogramming] (Fig. 4C). Clinically, Kaplan-Meier survival analysis demonstrated that patients in the LIHC cohort with high CKLF expression suffered significantly worse overall survival compared to those with low expression (p = 0.007; Fig. 4D). These findings collectively indicate that CKLF not only fuels oncogenic signaling and tumor aggressiveness but also serves as a robust prognostic biomarker for poor survival in HCC.

CKLF mutation promotes HCC progression

To comprehensively characterize the genomic landscape of CKLF in HCC, we analyzed multi-omics data from the TCGA-LIHC cohort. Copy number variation (CNV) analysis revealed a significant accumulation of GISTIC scores at the 12q24.31 locus, where CKLF resides, showing frequent amplification across 370 liver tumor samples (Fig. 5A). This suggests that copy number gain is a primary mechanism contributing to elevated CKLF levels in tumors.

(D) Scatter plot showing positive correlation between normalized CKLF expression and gistic copy number score . (E) Left panel: CKLF expression increases with genomic instability (FGA), particularly in copy-number-gained genomes; right panel: expression correlates with tumor grade (G1–G4), suggesting association with disease progression.

In parallel, we examined somatic mutations and found that while CKLF harbors recurrent alterations, it is not universally mutated as previously misinterpreted; rather, it displays a specific mutational profile in a subset of patients. The observed variants were predominantly missense mutations characterized by C>T or T>C base transitions (Fig. 5B, upper panels). In silico structural analysis suggests that these missense mutations may potentially affect protein conformational stability or secretion efficiency, which could theoretically modulate chemokine activity. However, statistical correlation analysis revealed no significant association between the presence of these somatic mutations and global transcriptomic dysregulation or specific structural variant (SV) patterns. This implies that while point mutations exist, they are likely secondary events rather than the primary drivers of the oncogenic phenotype.

Consistent with this hypothesis, we observed that CKLF mRNA expression was significantly upregulated in tumors with copy number gains compared to those with diploid or deleted status (p = 0.021; Fig. 5C). Furthermore, a strong positive correlation was identified between CKLF expression levels and its copy number gain scores (Fig. 5D), indicating a gene dosage effect where genomic amplification directly drives transcriptional upregulation. Notably, tumors exhibiting high genomic instability (GIN), particularly those with broad regional amplifications, showed the highest CKLF expression levels (Fig. 5E).

Collectively, these findings demonstrate that while CKLF acquires occasional missense mutations, its role as an oncogene in HCC is principally driven by genomic amplification. This CNV-mediated overexpression leads to a surge in CKLF signaling that correlates with chromosomal instability and aggressive tumor progression, highlighting gene dosage, rather than mutational lesions, as the key regulatory switch for CKLF in HCC.

Spatially resolved transcriptomics identifies region-specific CKLF expression in hepatocellular carcinoma

To investigate the spatial expression pattern of CKLF in HCC tissue, we carried out multiplex spatial transcriptomic analysis based on six representative tumor samples and created spatial distribution heatmaps to show heterogeneous CKLF expressions,showing a heterogenous distribution, where regions of high CKLF levels (i.e., in red/yellow tones) are often observed in close proximity to cancer cell aggregates and/or at the edge of the invasion front (Fig. 7A-F).CKLF expression appears to be spatially graded across some samples such that increased levels of CKLF are associated with regions containing proliferating cells and/or infiltrating immune cells,whereas down-regulated in the necrosis or stroma. Interestingly, some tumours show heterogeneous or focal patterns of expression clusters,which suggests the possibility for an association with specialized niches (e.g., hypoxia, immune cell aggregates).This consistent detection of high levels of CKLF expression in specific areas of tissues in different specimens suggests that it may be playing a role in localized functions inside the tumor.

Elevated CKLF expression occurs in hepatocellular carcinoma at both transcriptional and translational levels

Decreased CKLF expression inhibits Ras AKT and Ras ERK signaling pathways to mitigate the development of hepatocellular carcinoma

To further understand the effects of CKLF knockdown, we analyzed downstream protein levels following knockdown and recovery from knockdown. As shown by immunoblot analysis, knockdown of CKLF using two independent siRNAs significantly reduced p-AKT and p-ERK levels,that are known to be important downstream targets of both PI3K/AKT and MAPK/ERK signaling but with no statistically significant difference across the total levels of these proteins (Fig. 11A).Quantification demonstrated significant decreases of p-AKT / AKT ratio and p-ERK / ERK ratio after treatment with si-2 (*p < 0.0001,which confirms that CKLF is essential for maintaining these key cell survival/proliferation signals. In order to determine if restoration of downstream signalling would ameliorate the effects of CKLF loss,we transfected the cells using an active, constitutively active Ras construct.The subsequent proliferation assay with CCK-8 also indicated that Ras overexpression could rescue the cell proliferation ability of si-2-treated cells,achieved similar effects as the control, non-treated cells (Fig. 11B), suggesting that Ras-dependent pathways are able to compensate CKLF’s action in a functional sense. This was also corroborated by wound healing assays.

These findings confirm that CKLF exerts its oncogenic effects primarily via the Ras/AKT and Ras/ERK signaling axes, positioning it as an upstream regulator of key proliferative and migratory pathways in HCC.

In wound healing assays, we showed that re-expression of Ras was able to rescue the migratory defect induced by CKLF knockdown,wherein we found a statistically significant increase in wound closure rate, p < 0.0001 (Fig. 11C). Flow cytometric cell cycle analysis demonstrated that rescue of Ras expression relieved the CKLF knock-down-induced cell cycle arrest at G0/G1,resulting in the normalization of the S and G2/M phases population (Fig. 11D).Taken together, our data suggest a key role of CKLF in HCC growth and migration through activation of both the Ras/AKT and Ras/ERK pathways,and that these oncogenic activities could potentially be reversed by the restoration of this pathway. Such knowledge places CKLF in an important position, acting upstream of key oncogenic processes, and further supports it being considered a therapeutic target in liver cancers.

Discussion

HCC is characterized by profound alterations in the function of cytotoxic immune cells, including CD8+ T cells, γδ T cells, and M1-polarized macrophages. These immune deficiencies, coupled with a background of chronic inflammation (often driven by HBV/HCV infection or metabolic stress), create a unique and highly immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment (TME) [[13], [14], [15], [16], [17], [18],[25], [26]]. In this study, we present compelling evidence that Chemokine-like factor (CKLF) serves as a critical clinical biomarker for HCC progression. By integrating multi-omics approaches with experimental validation and spatial localization, we demonstrate that CKLF is not merely a bystander but an active driver of HCC development. Our data suggest a dual mechanism of action: first, by directly stimulating pro-oncogenic pathways within malignant hepatocytes, and second, by orchestrating complex shifts in the immunocomposition of the TME.

Taken together, our data indicate that CKLF is consistently upregulated across various HCC patient cohorts. This aberrant expression appears largely driven by genomic amplifications, suggesting that CKLF may function as a bona fide oncogene subject to copy-number gain. This genomic dependency aligns with the "oncogene addiction" hypothesis, wherein cancer cells become reliant on overexpressed drivers for proliferation and survival, thereby rendering them susceptible to targeted interventions. Functionally, we observed that silencing CKLF significantly reduces HCC cell proliferation, motility, and cell cycle progression. Mechanistically, these effects are mediated through the activation of the PI3K/AKT and MAPK/ERK pathways, evidenced by the downregulation of phospho-AKT and phospho-ERK following CKLF knockdown. Importantly, the partial rescue of these defects via activation of Ras, a critical upstream regulator, positions CKLF as a potential non-canonical upstream activator of well-known oncogenic signaling cascades. Although CKLF lacks classic kinase domains, its structural homology to chemokines and its ability to bind G-protein coupled receptors (such as CCR1 and CCR4) suggest it may act as an agonist that triggers the Ras cascade via receptor-mediated signaling, a mechanism increasingly recognized in chemokine-driven malignancies.

A particularly novel and intriguing finding of our work, revealed through single-cell and spatial transcriptomics, is the high expression of CKLF not only in malignant hepatocytes but also in cytolytic effector immune cells, specifically CD8+ T cells, γδ T cells, and M1-polarized macrophages. At first glance, this observation appears contradictory to the pro-tumorigenic role of CKLF described above. Typically, the presence of CKLF in cytotoxic cells might be interpreted as a robust anti-tumor immune response. However, given its concurrent role in driving tumor progression, we propose two non-mutually exclusive hypotheses to explain this paradox, drawing on established principles of immune regulation in chronic inflammation.

First, the high expression of CKLF in these immune populations may represent a "hijacked" immune response. In the context of chronic antigen stimulation within the TME, persistent exposure to chemokines can drive T cells and macrophages toward a state of exhaustion or dysfunction rather than effective killing. Similar to other chemokines that sustain chronic inflammatory loops, CKLF signaling via CCR1/CCR4 on CD8+ T cells or M1 macrophages might initially promote recruitment and activation but ultimately lead to desensitization, upregulation of immune checkpoint molecules, and loss of cytotoxic potency. In this scenario, the tumor effectively co-opts CKLF to maintain an influx of immune cells that are present in number but functionally impaired, thereby creating an illusion of immune surveillance while facilitating immune evasion.

Second, the elevated CKLF levels in cytotoxic cells could reflect a compensatory expression under a chronic inflammatory environment. The HCC TME is dominated by pro-tumorigenic signals (e.g., IL-10, TGF-β, M2 macrophage polarization). The upregulation of CKLF in CD8+ T cells and M1 macrophages may be a desperate, endogenous attempt by the host immune system to counteract this suppression and restore a Th1-dominant, anti-tumor phenotype. Our data showing an association between CKLF signatures and increased Th1/Th2 ratios supports this view. However, in the face of overwhelming oncogenic CKLF signaling from the tumor cells themselves, this compensatory effort is likely insufficient, resulting in a net pro-tumor outcome. This duality—where a molecule acts as both a tumor driver and a marker of immune struggle—is reminiscent of recent findings for other tumor antigens that concurrently trigger anti-tumor activity and mechanisms of acquired resistance [[19], [20], [21], [22], [23]].

This dual nature of CKLF presents a unique therapeutic opportunity. Unlike traditional targets that solely inhibit tumor growth or solely boost immunity, targeting CKLF could attack the disease on two fronts: disrupting oncogenic signaling in hepatocytes and potentially reversing the exhaustion or dysfunction of infiltrating immune cells. By blocking CKLF, we may not only halt tumor proliferation but also "release the brakes" on the hijacked immune response, allowing endogenous CD8+ T cells and M1 macrophages to regain their cytotoxic efficacy. This synergistic effect could significantly enhance the responsiveness of HCC to existing immunotherapies, such as immune checkpoint inhibitors.

Furthermore, the strong association between CKLF expression levels and tumor differentiation, as well as its enrichment in invasive regions, underscores its value as a prognostic marker. The spatial heterogeneity of CKLF suggests it could be utilized to identify high-risk zones for local relapse or metastasis, guiding clinicians in post-surgical management and follow-up strategies.

Although this study is comprehensive, several limitations still need to be acknowledged to ensure a balanced interpretation of the research results. Firstly, we are unable to delve deeply into its underlying regulatory mechanisms. Secondly, our clinical samples are also relatively scarce, and a large-sample cohort investigation is needed.

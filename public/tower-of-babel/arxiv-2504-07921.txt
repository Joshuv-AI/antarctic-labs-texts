Note on the identification of total effect in Cluster-DAGs with cycles
Clément Yvernes

Abstract
In this note, we discuss the identifiability of a total effect in cluster-DAGs, allowing for cycles within the cluster-DAG (while still assuming the associated underlying DAG to be acyclic). This is presented into two key results: first, restricting the cluster-DAG to clusters containing at most four nodes; second, adapting the notion of d-separation. We provide a graphical criterion to address the identifiability problem.

Structural Causal Models and associated graphs.

Formally, a Structural Causal Model (SCM)

Each SCM

for every pair

such that
\mathcal{U}_{i}\cap\mathcal{U}_{j}\neq\emptyset

V_{i}
and

have a common exogenous parent).

Main problem

We are interested in extending classical causal inference based on do-calculus (see
(
Pearl,, 2009
)
) to C-DAG.
In
Anand et al., (2023)
, they provide the foundations and machinery for valid probabilistic and causal inferences for cluster-DAGs, akin to Pearl’s d-separation and classical do-calculus for when such a coarser graphical representation of the system is provided based on the limited prior knowledge available.
Our goal is to derive the same analysis, for rung 2 of Pearl’s causal ladder when C-DAGs are allowed to be cyclic in whole generality.

As discussed in
Anand et al., (2023)
, a naive approach to causal inference with cluster variables—e.g., identifying
Q=P(\mathcal{C}_{i}|do(\mathcal{C}_{k}))
—proceeds as follows: first, enumerate all causal diagrams compatible with
{{\mathcal{G}}^{C}}
; then, assess the identifiability of
Q
in each diagram; finally, return
P(\mathcal{C}_{i}|do(\mathcal{C}_{k}))
if all diagrams yield the same expression, and “non-identifiable” otherwise. However, in practice, this approach becomes intractable in high-dimensional settings.

Here we shall answer in a surprising simple way to the two following fundamental questions :
Q1 : Can valid inferences be performed about cluster variables using C-DAGs directly, without going through exhaustive enumeration? Q2 : What properties of C-DAGs are shared by all the compatible causal diagrams?
We formalize this in Problem
1
.

(\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\setminus\mathrm{Root}(\mathcal{F}^{\prime}))\cap\mathcal{Z}=\emptyset

We repeat this procedure for all remaining vertices that violate the definition. Since each step strictly reduces the number of such violations, the process terminates in finitely many steps. We thus obtain a subgraph

that satisfies all conditions of Definition

\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{Y}
under
\mathcal{Z}
.

•

\ref{th:new_d_sep:2}\Rightarrow\ref{th:new_d_sep:1}
: By definition,
\sigma
has a single connected component. Thus
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{Y}
are connected by
\sigma
. Let

be a path from
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{Y}
in

\mathcal{Z}
.

\mathcal{X}\cup\mathcal{Y}

\mathcal{X}
. Thus,

contains a directed path

from

to
\mathcal{X}

\mathcal{Y}

Proposition 1
.

Let
{{\mathcal{G}}^{C}}
be a cluster-DAG.
\mathcal{C}\left({{\mathcal{G}}^{C}}\right)\neq\emptyset

Proof.

Let us prove the two implications:

•

\Rightarrow

contains the cycle

•

\Leftarrow
: If

. Let us construct a compatible graph. For all cluster

of size

5
d-separation in C-DAGs

We start by solving d-separation without mutilation. Let
{{\mathcal{G}}^{C}}
be an admissible cluster-DAG. Let
\mathcal{X}^{C},\mathcal{Y}^{C}
and
\mathcal{Z}^{C}
be pairwise distinct subsets of nodes of
{{\mathcal{G}}^{C}}
. We aim to find out an efficient way to characterise the following property:

\mathcal{X}^{C}\perp\!\!\!\perp_{{{\mathcal{G}}^{C}}}\mathcal{Y}^{C}\mid\mathcal{Z}^{C}\quad\overset{\text{\tiny def}}{\equiv}\quad\forall{{\mathcal{G}}^{m}}\in\mathcal{C}\left({{\mathcal{G}}^{C}}\right)\penalty\ \penalty\ \mathcal{X}^{m}\perp\!\!\!\perp_{{{\mathcal{G}}^{m}}}\mathcal{Y}^{m}\mid\mathcal{Z}^{m}

Thus, by Theorem
1
, its negation is:

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}^{C}\not{\perp\!\!\!\perp}_{{{\mathcal{G}}^{C}}}\mathcal{Y}^{C}\mid\mathcal{Z}^{C}\quad\overset{\text{\tiny def}}{\equiv}\quad\exists\penalty\ {{\mathcal{G}}^{m}}\in\mathcal{C}\left({{\mathcal{G}}^{C}}\right)\penalty\ \penalty\ \mathcal{X}^{m}\not{\perp\!\!\!\perp}_{{{\mathcal{G}}^{m}}}\mathcal{Y}^{m}\mid\mathcal{Z}^{m}.

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\exists\penalty\ {{\mathcal{G}}^{m}}\in\mathcal{C}\left({{\mathcal{G}}^{C}}\right),\exists\penalty\ \sigma^{m}\text{ a structure of interest that connects $\mathcal{X}^{m}$ and $\mathcal{Y}^{m}$ under $\mathcal{Z}^{m}$ }.

(1)

5.1
Reducing the size of the cluster-DAG.

Clusters in Cluster-DAG can be arbitrary large. This could create an issue if we want to work directly in compatible graphs. To solve this issue, Theorem
2
shows that we only need to consider clusters with a maximum size of four. To show Theorem
2
, we need Propositions
5
and
6
, which show how to construct a compatible graph from another compatible graph. In this section, following the notations of
Perković et al., (2018)
, an arrow (
\leftarrow\!\!\!\!\bullet
) represents either a directed arrow (
\leftarrow
) or a dashed-bidirected arrows (
\dashleftrightarrow
).

Proposition 5
.

Let
{{\mathcal{G}}^{C}}
be an admissible cluster-DAG,
{{\mathcal{G}}^{m}}
be a compatible graph with
{{\mathcal{G}}^{C}}
and
V^{C}
be a node in
{{\mathcal{G}}^{C}}
, ie a cluster. If there exists
i>j
such that
{{\mathcal{G}}^{m}}
contains the arrow

that contains the arrow

Proof.

If

, thus by the convention of Notation

, we know that
V_{j}

. Thus,

Theorem 2

(Infinity is at most four)

Lemma 1
.

Let
{{\mathcal{G}}^{C}}
be an admissible cluster-DAG. Then,
{{\mathcal{G}}^{m}_{\min}}
, its minimal compatible graph, is compatible with
{{\mathcal{G}}^{C}}
.

Proof.

By construction, all arrows in
{{\mathcal{G}}^{C}}
are represented in
{{\mathcal{G}}^{m}_{\min}}
. Moreover, all arrows that are added correspond to an arrow in
{{\mathcal{G}}^{C}}
. Therefore, we only need to check for acyclicity to prove that
{{\mathcal{G}}^{m}_{\min}}
is a compatible graph with
{{\mathcal{G}}^{C}}
.

By contradiction, let us assume that
{{\mathcal{G}}^{m}_{\min}}
contains a cycle
\pi

\pi
is not within a single cluster. Indeed, in each cluster
V

{{\mathcal{G}}^{m}_{\min}}_{\mid V}
is
V_{\#V}

\pi

{{\mathcal{G}}^{m}_{\min}}

{{\mathcal{G}}^{m}_{\min}}

Conclusion

In this note, we provide a graphical criterion for identifying the total effect in cluster DAGs where cycles are allowed (i.e., the partition into clusters is not admissible). To the best of our knowledge, this is the first sound and complete criterion in this setting.

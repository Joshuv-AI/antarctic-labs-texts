Folding of cluster algebras and quantum toroidal algebras
Lior Silberberg

Abstract
In this paper, we study the relationship between the representation theory of the quantum affine algebra 𝒰 q ​ ( 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 ∞ ^ ) \mathcal{U}_{q}({\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}_{\infty}}}) of infinite rank, and that of the quantum toroidal algebra 𝒰 q ​ ( 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 2 ​ n , tor ) \mathcal{U}_{q}({{\mathfrak{sl}_{{2n,\mathrm{tor}}}}}) . Using monoidal categorifications due to Hernandez-Leclerc and Nakajima, we establish a cluster-theoretic interpretation of the folding map ϕ 2 ​ n \phi_{2n} of q q -characters, introduced by Hernandez. To this end, we introduce a notion of foldability for cluster algebras arising from infinite quivers and study a specific case of cluster algebras of type A ∞ A_{\infty} . Using this interpretation of ϕ 2 ​ n \phi_{2n} , we prove a conjecture of Hernandez in new cases. Finally, we study a particular simple 𝒰 q ​ ( 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 2 ​ n , tor ) \mathcal{U}_{q}({{\mathfrak{sl}_{{2n,\mathrm{tor}}}}}) -module whose q q -character is not a cluster variable, and conjecture that it is imaginary.

Introduction

In the 1980s, Drinfeld and Jimbo associated to any complex symmetrizable Kac–Moody algebra
\mathfrak{g}
a Hopf algebra
\mathcal{U}_{q}({\mathfrak{g}})
with generic
q\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
, called the quantum Kac–Moody algebra associated to
\mathfrak{g}
. In turn, to
\mathcal{U}_{q}({\mathfrak{g}})
, one can associate an affinization
\mathcal{U}_{q}({\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}})
. It is a
\mathbb{C}
-algebra which is in general not known to be a Hopf algebra. Nonetheless, the category
\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{int}}(\mathcal{U}_{q}({\widehat{\mathfrak{\mathfrak{g}}}}))
of integrable
\mathcal{U}_{q}({\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}})
-modules admits a fusion product, which equips its Grothendieck group with a commutative ring structure, see
[
17
,
18
]
.

One important notion in the representation theory of affinized quantum Kac–Moody algebras is that of
q
-characters, first introduced by Frenkel and Reshetihkin in
[
11
]
and extended to the general case in
[
17
]
. In brief, the
q
-character of a module
V
in
\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{int}}(\mathcal{U}_{q}({\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}}))
is (in general) an infinite sum that encodes the dimensions of its
l
-weight spaces.

For a complex finite-dimensional simple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathrm{fin}}
, let
\mathfrak{g}
be the associated affinized Lie algebra. It turns out that
\mathcal{U}_{q}({\mathfrak{g}})\simeq\mathcal{U}_{q}({\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathrm{fin}}})
, and these are the “smallest” examples of affinized Kac–Moody algebras. We call the algebras arising in this way
quantum affine algebras
. They have been studied extensively in the past few decades, see for example
[
4
,
5
,
11
,
29
,
10
]
.

For an affine Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, however, the “double affine” quantum algebra
\mathcal{U}_{q}({\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}})
is not isomorphic to any quantum Kac–Moody algebra. We call these algebras
quantum toroidal algebras
. They were first introduced in type
A
in
[
12
]
. Their structure and representation theory are considerably more complex than those of the quantum affine algebras. For example, only recently a tensor product for their integrable representations was defined, see
[
26
]
.

One may ask the following natural question:

Question 1.1
.

Can we use the well studied representation theory of quantum affine algebras in order to understand the representation theory of quantum toroidal algebras?

A first result in this direction was given in
[
20
]
. In this paper, Hernandez regards the Dynkin diagram of affine type
A_{n}^{(1)}
as a folding of the bi-infinite Dynkin diagram
A_{\infty}
(infinite in both directions). Although there is no direct way to “fold” the quantum affine algebra
\mathcal{U}_{q}({\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}_{\infty}}})
onto the quantum toroidal algebra
\mathcal{U}_{q}({{\mathfrak{sl}_{{n+1,\mathrm{tor}}}}})\coloneq\mathcal{U}_{q}({\widehat{\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}}}})
, he proves that there is a homomorphism
\phi_{n}
that “folds”
q
-characters of integrable
\mathcal{U}_{q}({\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}_{\infty}}})
-modules onto virtual
q
-characters of integrable
\mathcal{U}_{q}({{\mathfrak{sl}_{{n+1,\mathrm{tor}}}}})
-modules. Moreover, he proves that
\phi_{n}
maps
q
-characters of Kirillov–Reshetikhin
\mathcal{U}_{q}({\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}_{\infty}}})
-modules to
q
-characters of Kirillov–Reshetikhin
\mathcal{U}_{q}({{\mathfrak{sl}_{{n+1,\mathrm{tor}}}}})
-modules. Finally, he gives the following conjecture.

Conjecture 1.2

(Conjecture 5.3 in
[
20
]
)
.

The homomorphism
\phi_{n}
maps
q
-characters of integrable
\mathcal{U}_{q}({\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}_{\infty}}})
-modules to
q
-characters of actual (non-virtual) integrable
\mathcal{U}_{q}({{\mathfrak{sl}_{{n+1,\mathrm{tor}}}}})
-modules.

The idea of relating the
q
-character theory of different quantum groups appears also in
[
19
,
32
]
, where it was used to obtain new multiplication formulas. Similar ideas not directly involving
q
-characters can be found in
[
22
]
.

An important notion in the representation theory of quantum affine algebras (and more generally affinized Kac–Moody algebras) is that of
monoidal categorification
. It was introduced by Hernandez and Leclerc in
[
14
]
, and studied further in
[
30
,
15
,
31
,
1
,
23
,
24
]
, to name a few. In brief, the idea is to study an abelian monoidal category
\mathcal{C}
by identifying its Grothendieck ring
\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{C})
with a cluster algebra
\mathcal{A}
, in such a way that cluster monomials in
\mathcal{A}
correspond to classes of real simple objects in
\mathcal{C}
. An object is said to be a real simple object if its tensor product with itself is still simple. The tensor (or fusion) product of integrable
\mathcal{U}_{q}({\mathfrak{g}})
-modules provides many examples of categories which are monoidal categorifications of cluster algebras.

In this paper, we build on the results of
[
20
]
and establish that we can understand
\phi_{n}
in a cluster-theoretic way. We first introduce a category
\mathcal{C}_{1}
of
\mathcal{U}_{q}({\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}_{\infty}}})
-modules which gives a monoidal categorification of a cluster algebra
\mathcal{A}_{\infty}
, associated to an ice quiver
\Gamma_{\infty}
with principal part of type
A_{\infty}
with alternating orientation. We then introduce a notion of folding of cluster algebras suitable for (locally finite) infinite quivers with a group action, which we call
strong global foldability
. With this concept in hand, we get special sets of cluster variables which we call
orbit-clusters
, which correspond to the clusters of the folded cluster algebra. Our first main result is the following: let
Q
be a quiver whose underlying diagram is of type
A_{n}^{(1)}
(so it is an orientation of an
(n+1)
-cycle), and let
A_{Q}
be the orientation of
A_{\infty}
corresponding to
Q
(see
Section

5
for details). Let also
G
be the group of permutations of
(A_{\infty})_{0}
generated by shifting the vertices by
n+1
, so that
A_{Q}^{G}=Q
.

Theorem 1.3
.

In the setting above, the quiver
A_{Q}
is strongly globally foldable with respect to
G
if and only if
Q
is not cyclically oriented.

This first result suggests that there is a strong connection between the cluster algebra of type
A_{\infty}
and the cluster algebras of type
A_{n}^{(1)}
.

In
[
30
]
, Nakajima constructs a category
\mathcal{C}_{1}
for any simply-laced symmetric affinized quantum Kac–Moody algebra, whose Dynkin diagram has no odd cycles. He shows it provides a monoidal categorifications of an associated cluster algebra. For the particular case of
\mathcal{U}_{q}({{\mathfrak{sl}_{{2n,\mathrm{tor}}}}})
, the monoidal categorification is of a cluster algebra
\mathcal{A}_{2n}
associated to an ice quiver
\Gamma_{2n}
, whose principal part is of type
A_{2n-1}^{(1)}
with alternating orientation.

We show that there is a group
G
acting on the vertices of
\Gamma_{\infty}
, such that
\Gamma_{\infty}^{G}=\Gamma_{2n}
. We then prove the following result, which is the main theorem of the paper (in what follows, we identify classes of modules in the Grothendieck ring with their
q
-characters).

Theorem 1.4
.

The ice quiver
\Gamma_{\infty}
is strongly globally foldable with respect to
G
.

If
\chi_{q}(L)
is a cluster variable belonging to an orbit-cluster of
\mathcal{A}_{\infty}
, then
\phi_{2n}(\chi_{q}(L))
is a cluster variable of
\mathcal{A}_{2n}
. Each cluster variable of
\mathcal{A}_{2n}
can be obtained in this way.

In particular,
\phi_{2n}(\chi_{q}(L))
is the
q
-character of a simple
\mathcal{U}_{q}({{\mathfrak{sl}_{{2n,\mathrm{tor}}}}})
-module.

The theorem has the following consequences:

1.

The map
\phi_{2n}
is more than just a projection of
q
-characters, it relates the cluster structures associated to
\mathcal{U}_{q}({\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}_{\infty}}})
and
\mathcal{U}_{q}({{\mathfrak{sl}_{{2n,\mathrm{tor}}}}})
.

2.

We can use some known multiplicative formulas for
q
-characters of
\mathcal{U}_{q}({\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}_{\infty}}})
to deduce analogous ones for
\mathcal{U}_{q}({{\mathfrak{sl}_{{2n,\mathrm{tor}}}}})
.

3.

If
L
is a simple
\mathcal{U}_{q}({\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}_{\infty}}})
-module whose class belongs to an orbit-cluster in
\mathcal{A}_{\infty}
, then
\phi_{2n}(\chi_{q}(L))
is the
q
-character of a simple
\mathcal{U}_{q}({{\mathfrak{sl}_{{2n,\mathrm{tor}}}}})
-module. In particular, we prove
1.2
in this case. These are the first examples of modules satisfying
1.2
outside the Kirillov–Reshetikhin case.

As an application, we compute some
q
-characters for
\mathcal{U}_{q}({{\mathfrak{sl}_{{2n,\mathrm{tor}}}}})
-modules that do not correspond to cluster variables. We obtain a few more examples of non-Kirillov–Reshetikhin modules that satisfy
1.2
. As a particular case, we study a simple
\mathcal{U}_{q}({{\mathfrak{sl}_{{2n,\mathrm{tor}}}}})
-module, which we denote by
L(\overline{m_{1,2n}})
, and we conjecture the following.

Conjecture 1.5
.

The simple
\mathcal{U}_{q}({{\mathfrak{sl}_{{2n,\mathrm{tor}}}}})
-module
L(\overline{m_{1,2n}})
is an imaginary module, that is, the fusion product
L(\overline{m_{1,2n}})\ast L(\overline{m_{1,2n}})
is not simple.

For
n=1
, this module has been studied in Example 6.15 of
[
30
]
, where it was shown to be imaginary. We show that
1.2
implies
1.5
.

Preliminaries

2.1
Infinite quivers and cluster algebras

This section covers the basics on cluster algebras and sets some notation we will need for this paper. A more detailed treatment can be found, for example, in
[
9
,
8
]
or in the survey
[
25
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

A quiver
Q
is a directed graph with vertex set
Q_{0}
and arrow set
Q_{1}
, equipped with two functions
s,t:Q_{1}\rightarrow Q_{0}
. Given
\alpha\in Q_{1}
, we call
s(\alpha)
the source of
\alpha
, and
t(\alpha)
the target of
\alpha
.

For a quiver
Q
and two subsets
X,Y\subseteq Q_{0}
, we set

\displaystyle\mathrm{Arr}_{Q}(X,Y)\coloneq\#\{\alpha\in Q_{1}\mid s(\alpha)\in X,t(\alpha)\in Y\}\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}

the number of arrows from
X
to
Y
, and

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ed}_{Q}(X,Y)=\mathrm{Arr}_{Q}(X,Y)+\mathrm{Arr}_{Q}(Y,X)

the number of edges between
X
and
Y
. If
X=\{x\}
we will write
\mathrm{Arr}_{Q}(x,Y)
for
\mathrm{Arr}_{Q}(X,Y)
and
\mathrm{Ed}_{Q}(x,Y)
for
\mathrm{Ed}_{Q}(X,Y)
(and similarly for
Y=\{y\}
).

To describe a quiver
Q
up to isomorphism, it is thus enough to give its vertex set
Q_{0}
and
\mathrm{Arr}_{Q}(x,y)
for every pair of vertices
x,y\in Q_{0}
.

In this paper, we assume our quivers do not have loops or
2
-cycles. This means
\mathrm{Arr}_{Q}(x,y)\cdot\mathrm{Arr}_{Q}(y,x)=0
for all
x,y\in Q_{0}
. We allow a quiver
Q
to have an infinite countable vertex set. However, we require it to be locally finite, that is,
\mathrm{Ed}_{Q}(x,Q_{0})<\infty
for every
x\in Q_{0}
.

An ice quiver
(Q,F)
is a quiver
Q
together with a subset of its vertices
F\subseteq Q_{0}
. We call the vertices in
F
frozen, and those in
Q_{0}\setminus F
mutable. For simplicity, and given our application in the following sections, we assume there are no arrows between frozen vertices. Any quiver
Q
can be regarded as an ice quiver by setting
F=\emptyset
.

Given an ice quiver
(Q,F)
and a mutable vertex
z\in Q_{0}
, we can define a new ice quiver
(\mu_{z}(Q),F)
as follows. The vertex set of
\mu_{z}(Q)
is
Q_{0}
. The arrows are given by

\displaystyle\mathrm{Arr}_{\mu_{z}(Q)}(x,y)=\begin{cases}0&\text{ if }x,y\in F\\
\max\{0,b_{x,y}-b_{y,x}\}&\text{ if }x,y\notin F\cup\{z\}\\
\mathrm{Arr}_{Q}(y,x)&\text{ otherwise}\end{cases},

where
b_{x,y}=\mathrm{Arr}_{Q}(x,y)+\mathrm{Arr}_{Q}(x,z)\cdot\mathrm{Arr}_{Q}(z,y)
. We call the new ice quiver the mutation of
(Q,F)
at the vertex
z
. We note that
\mu_{z}(\mu_{z}(Q))\simeq Q
.

Having defined the mutation operation, we can associate to an ice quiver
(Q,F)
a commutative algebra as follows. We fix
(Q,F)
, and consider the field

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{F}}=\mathbb{Q}(x_{v}\mid v\in Q_{0}),

where
x_{v}
for
v\in Q_{0}
are indeterminates. A seed
((Q^{\prime},F^{\prime}),\underline{y})
is a pair of an ice quiver
(Q^{\prime},F^{\prime})
, and
\underline{u}=\{u_{v}\in\tilde{\mathcal{F}}\mid v\in Q_{0}\}
, a free generating set of
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}
.

We extend the mutation operations to seeds. For a mutable
z\in Q_{0}^{\prime}
, we define the mutation of the seed
((Q^{\prime},F^{\prime}),\underline{u})
at
z
to be the seed
((\mu_{z}(Q^{\prime}),F^{\prime}),\mu_{z}(\underline{u}))
, where
\mu_{z}(\underline{u})=\underline{u}\cup\{u^{\prime}_{z}\}\setminus\{u_{z}\}
and

\displaystyle u^{\prime}_{z}=\frac{\prod_{w\in Q^{\prime}_{0}}u_{w}^{\mathrm{Arr}_{Q^{\prime}}(z,w)}+\prod_{w\in Q^{\prime}_{0}}u_{w}^{\mathrm{Arr}_{Q^{\prime}}(w,z)}}{u_{z}}.

Notice that
\mu_{z}(\mu_{z}(\underline{u}))=\underline{u}
.

To the ice quiver
(Q,F)
, we associate the set
\underline{x}=\{x_{v}\mid v\in Q_{0}\}
. We call
((Q,F),\underline{x})
the initial seed. We let
\mathcal{S}
be the set of all seeds obtained from the initial seed by a finite sequence of mutation. Let
s\in\mathcal{S}
be some seed, so
s=((Q_{s},F_{s}),\underline{u}_{s})
. An easy observation is that
(Q_{s})_{0}=Q_{0}
and
F_{s}=F
. Moreover,
\underline{u}_{s}
contains all
x_{v}
such that
v\in F
. We call
\underline{u}_{s}
a cluster, and any
u_{v}\in\underline{u}_{s}
a cluster variable.

Definition 2.2
.

The cluster algebra
\mathcal{A}_{(Q,F)}
is the subring of
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}
generated by all cluster variables. That is
\mathcal{A}_{(Q,F)}\coloneq\mathbb{Z}[\mathcal{X}]\subseteq\tilde{\mathcal{F}}
for
\mathcal{X}=\bigcup_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\underline{u}_{s}
.

The Laurent phenomenon is a very well known result which states that every cluster variable is a Laurent polynomial in the initial cluster with integral coefficient. In our context, we set
\mathcal{F}=\mathbb{Z}[x_{v}\mid v\in F][x_{v}^{\pm 1}\mid v\in Q_{0}\setminus F]
, so the Laurent phenomenon can be stated as
\mathcal{A}_{(Q,F)}\subseteq\mathcal{F}
.

2.2
Symmetric Kac-Moody quantum groups and their affinizations

We give here some necessary background on quantum groups and their representation theory. For the sake of concreteness, we restrict our attention to symmetric Cartan datum. For proofs and more details, see
[
4
,
5
,
29
,
17
]
.

Let
I
be a finite index set, and
\lvert I\rvert=n
. Let
C=(C_{i,j})_{i,j\in I}
be a symmetric matrix such that
C_{i,i}=2
and
C_{i,j}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\leq 0}
for
i\neq j
. To this information, we associate a realization
(\mathfrak{h},\Pi,\Pi^{\vee})
(see
[
21
]
), with
\mathfrak{h}
a complex vector space of dimension
2n-\mathrm{rank}(C)
, the set of simple roots
\Pi=\{\alpha_{i}\mid i\in I\}\subseteq\mathfrak{h}^{*}
, and the set of simple coroots
\Pi^{\vee}=\{\alpha_{i}^{\vee}\mid i\in I\}\subseteq\mathfrak{h}
, such that
\alpha_{j}(\alpha_{i}^{\vee})=C_{i,j}
. We let
\Delta
be the associated root system and we set
Q^{+}=\sum_{i\in I}\mathbb{N}\alpha_{i}
. We denote by

\displaystyle P^{\vee}=\{h\in\mathfrak{h}\mid\alpha_{i}(h)\in\mathbb{Z},\ i\in I\},

the group of integral coweights, by

\displaystyle P=\{\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}\mid\lambda(\alpha_{i}^{\vee})\in\mathbb{Z},\ i\in I\}

the group of integral weights, and by

\displaystyle P^{+}=\{\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}\mid\lambda(\alpha_{i}^{\vee})\in\mathbb{N},\ i\in I\}

its submonoid of dominant integral weights.

From now on we fix
q\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
, not a root of unity. Given
a,b\in\mathbb{N}
such that
a\leq b
we set

\displaystyle[b]_{q}=\frac{q^{b}-q^{-b}}{q-q^{-1}},

\displaystyle[b]_{q}!=[b]_{q}\cdots[1]_{q},

\displaystyle\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{b}{a}_{q}=\frac{[b]_{q}!}{[b-a]_{q}![a]_{q}!}.

Definition 2.3
.

The quantum Kac-Moody algebra
\mathcal{U}_{q}({\mathfrak{g}})
is the
\mathbb{C}
-algebra with generators
k_{h}
and
x_{i}^{\pm}
with
h\in P^{\vee}
and
i\in I
, subject to relations

\displaystyle k_{h}k_{h^{\prime}}=k_{h+h^{\prime}},\qquad k_{0}=1,

\displaystyle k_{h}x_{j}^{\pm}k_{-h}=q^{\pm\alpha_{j}(h)}x_{j}^{\pm},

\displaystyle[x_{i}^{+},x_{j}^{-}]=\delta_{i,j}\frac{k_{\alpha_{i}^{\vee}}-k_{-\alpha_{i}^{\vee}}}{q-q^{-1}},

\displaystyle\sum_{r=0}^{1-C_{i,j}}(-1)^{r}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{1-C_{i,j}}{r}_{q}(x_{i}^{\pm})^{1-C_{i,j}-r}x_{j}^{\pm}(x_{i}^{\pm})^{r}=0.

Let
\mathcal{U}_{q}({\mathfrak{g}})^{+}
,
\mathcal{U}_{q}({\mathfrak{g}})^{-}
and
\mathcal{U}_{q}({\mathfrak{h}})
be the subalgebras of
\mathcal{U}_{q}({\mathfrak{g}})
generated by the
x_{i}^{+}
,
x_{i}^{-}
and
k_{h}
, respectively. We have the triangular decomposition
\mathcal{U}_{q}({\mathfrak{g}})\simeq\mathcal{U}_{q}({\mathfrak{g}})^{-}\otimes\mathcal{U}_{q}({\mathfrak{h}})\otimes\mathcal{U}_{q}({\mathfrak{g}})^{+}
.

Definition 2.4
.

The quantum affinization of
\mathcal{U}_{q}({\mathfrak{g}})
is the
\mathbb{C}
-algebra
\mathcal{U}_{q}({\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}})
with generators
k_{h}
,
h_{i,m}
and
x_{i,r}^{\pm}
with
h\in P^{\vee}
,
i\in I
,
r\in\mathbb{Z}
and
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\neq 0}
, subject to relations

\displaystyle k_{h}k_{h^{\prime}}=k_{h+h^{\prime}},\qquad k_{0}=1,\qquad[k_{h},h_{i,m}]=0,\qquad[h_{i,m},h_{j,m^{\prime}}]=0,

\displaystyle k_{h}x_{j,r}^{\pm}k_{-h}=q^{\pm\alpha_{j}(h)}x_{j,r}^{\pm},

\displaystyle[h_{i,m},x_{j,r}^{\pm}]=\pm\frac{1}{m}[mC_{i,j}]_{q}x_{j,m+r}^{\pm},

\displaystyle[x_{i,r}^{+},x_{j,r^{\prime}}^{-}]=\delta_{i,j}\frac{\phi_{i,r+r^{\prime}}^{+}-\phi_{i,{r+r^{\prime}}}^{-}}{q-q^{-1}},

\displaystyle x_{i,r+1}^{\pm}x_{j,r^{\prime}}^{\pm}-q^{\pm C_{i,j}}x_{j,r^{\prime}}^{\pm}x_{i,r+1}^{\pm}=q^{\pm C_{i,j}}x_{i,r}^{\pm}x_{j,r^{\prime}+1}^{\pm}-x_{j,r^{\prime}+1}^{\pm}x_{i,r}^{\pm},

\displaystyle\sum_{\pi\in S_{d}}\sum_{k=0}^{d}(-1)^{k}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{d}{k}_{q}x^{\pm}_{i,r_{\pi(1)}}\cdots x^{\pm}_{i,r_{\pi(k)}}x_{j,r^{\prime}}^{\pm}x^{\pm}_{i,r_{\pi(k+1)}}\cdots x^{\pm}_{i,r_{\pi(d)}}=0,

where
d=1-C_{i,j}
,
r_{1},...,r_{d}
is any sequence of integers, and
\phi_{i,m}^{\pm}
are determined by

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\phi_{i,\pm m}^{\pm}z^{\pm m}=k_{\pm\alpha_{i}^{\vee}}\exp\left(\pm(q-q^{-1})\sum_{m^{\prime}=1}^{\infty}h_{i,\pm m^{\prime}}z^{\pm m^{\prime}}\right),

and
\phi_{i,\pm m}^{\pm}=0
for
m<0
.

Let
\mathcal{U}_{q}({\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}})^{+}
,
\mathcal{U}_{q}({\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}})^{-}
and
\mathcal{U}_{q}({\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}})
be the subalgebras of
\mathcal{U}_{q}({\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}})
generated by the
x_{i,r}^{+}
,
x_{i,r}^{-}
and
k_{h},h_{i,m}
, respectively. We have a triangular decomposition for
\mathcal{U}_{q}({\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}})
, that is, a linear isomorphism
\mathcal{U}_{q}({\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}})\simeq\mathcal{U}_{q}({\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}})^{-}\otimes\mathcal{U}_{q}({\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}})\otimes\mathcal{U}_{q}({\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}})^{+}
.

The subalgebra of
\mathcal{U}_{q}({\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}})
generated by
x_{i}^{\pm}
and
k_{h}
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{U}_{q}({\mathfrak{g}})
. Identifying these, we have
\mathcal{U}_{q}({\mathfrak{g}})^{\pm}\subseteq\mathcal{U}_{q}({\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}})^{\pm}
and
\mathcal{U}_{q}({\mathfrak{h}})\subseteq\mathcal{U}_{q}({\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}})
.

A
\mathcal{U}_{q}({\mathfrak{h}})
-module
V
is said to be
\mathcal{U}_{q}({\mathfrak{h}})
-diagonalizable if

\displaystyle V=\bigoplus_{\omega\in P}V_{\omega},\qquad V_{\omega}\coloneq\{v\in V\mid k_{h}(v)=q^{\omega(h)}v,\ \forall h\in P^{\vee}\}.

A
\mathcal{U}_{q}({\mathfrak{g}})
-modules
V
is called integrable if it is
\mathcal{U}_{q}({\mathfrak{h}})
-diagonalizable, its weight spaces
V_{\omega}
are all finite-dimensional, and for any
\omega\in P
and
i\in I
, we have
V_{\omega\pm r\alpha_{i}}=0
for
r
large enough.

Definition 2.5
.

The category
\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{int}}(\mathcal{U}_{q}({\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}}))
is the full subcategory of
\mathcal{U}_{q}({\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}})

there exists a unique
\gamma\in P_{l}^{+}
such that
m=m_{\gamma}
; we will write
L(m)=L(\gamma)
. For
i\in I_{\infty}
,
a\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
, we let
A_{i,a}\in\tilde{A}_{\infty}
be the monomial
Y_{i,aq^{-1}}Y_{i,aq}Y_{{i-1},a}^{-1}Y_{{i+1},a}^{-1}
. We define a partial order on
\tilde{A}_{\infty}
by

\displaystyle m\leq m^{\prime}\iff m^{\prime}m^{-1}\in\mathbb{Z}[A_{i,a}^{-1}\mid i\in I_{\infty},a\in\mathbb{C}^{*}].

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
m\in\tilde{A}_{\infty}
be a dominant monomial and let
m^{\prime}
be a monomial which appears in
\chi_{q}(L(m))
. Then
m^{\prime}\leq m
.

Proof.

This follows from
Proposition

3.3
and
Theorem

2.12
.
∎

4
Folding of quivers and cluster algebras

In this section, we will discuss a certain notion of folding of locally finite infinite quivers and associated cluster algebras. Because we will work with infinite quivers and their mutations, we need to discuss the folding in a relatively general setting. Most of this section is inspired by
[
7
]
.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
Q
be a locally finite quiver and let
G\subseteq\mathrm{Sym}(Q_{0})
be a group acting on the vertex set
Q_{0}
. We say that
Q
is
strongly
G
-admissible
if the following hold

1.

\mathrm{Arr}_{Q}(v_{1},v_{2})=\mathrm{Arr}_{Q}(gv_{1},gv_{2})
for any
v_{1},v_{2}\in Q_{0}
,
g\in G
,

2.

the action of
G
on
Q_{0}
is free, that is, the stabilizer of any vertex
v\in Q_{0}
is trivial,

3.

\mathrm{Arr}_{Q}(Gv,Gv)=0
for any
v\in Q_{0}
,

4.

\mathrm{Arr}_{Q}(Gv_{1},v_{2})\cdot\mathrm{Arr}_{Q}(v_{2},Gv_{1})=0
for any
v_{1},v_{2}\in Q_{0}
.

If, moreover
Q
has a frozen part
F
, then the ice quiver
(Q,F)
is said to be
strongly
G
-admissible
if
Q
is strongly
G
-admissible and
G(F)=F
. In that case, each
G
-orbit is either mutable or frozen.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
Q
be a quiver and
G
a group acting on
Q_{0}
. Suppose
Q
is
G
-admissible. We define a new quiver
Q^{G}
with set of vertices
Q_{0}/G
and arrows
\mathrm{Arr}_{Q^{G}}(Gv_{1},Gv_{2})=\mathrm{Arr}_{Q}(Gv_{1},v_{2})
for any
v_{1},v_{2}\in Q_{0}
. We say
Q^{G}
is
the folding of
Q
with respect to
G
.

If
Q
has a frozen part
F
, we let
F^{G}
be the set of frozen vertices
F/G
of
Q^{G}
. We say
(Q^{G},F^{G})
is
the folding of
(Q,F)
with respect to
G
.

Remark 4.3
.

Conditions
1
and
2
imply
\mathrm{Arr}_{Q}(Gv_{1},v_{2})=\mathrm{Arr}_{Q}(v_{1},Gv_{2})
for any
v_{1},v_{2}\in Q_{0}
. Indeed,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Arr}_{Q}(Gv_{1},v_{2})

\displaystyle=\sum_{v\in Gv_{1}}\mathrm{Arr}(v,v_{2})=\sum_{g\in G}\mathrm{Arr}(gv_{1},v_{2})

\displaystyle=\sum_{g\in G}\mathrm{Arr}(v_{1},g^{-1}v_{2})=\sum_{v^{\prime}\in Gv_{2}}\mathrm{Arr}(v_{1},v^{\prime})=\mathrm{Arr}_{Q}(v_{1},Gv_{2}).

Remark 4.4
.

Our definition is different than that of
[
7
]
. Here we allow the quiver
Q
to have infinitely many vertices, but we require that it folds onto a (non-valued) quiver (condition
2
). There is no evident way to fold an infinite quiver onto a (maybe valued) quiver without imposing some symmetry conditions. We use the word “strongly” to distinguish between our notion of admissibility and the one in
[
7
]
.

Remark 4.5
.

Conditions
3
and
4
ensure that the quiver
Q
has no “virtual” loops or
2
-cycles. This means that there are no arrows nor oriented 2-paths between two vertices belonging to the same
G
-orbit. In particular, this ensures that the folded quiver has no loops or
2
-cycles.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
Q
be a quiver and
G\subseteq\mathrm{Sym}(Q_{0})
, such that
Q
is strongly
G
-admissible. Then the following hold.

1.

Let
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
be two vertices in the same
G
-orbit, then
\mu_{v_{1}}\mu_{v_{2}}Q=\mu_{v_{2}}\mu_{v_{1}}Q
.

2.

Let
w_{1}
and
w_{2}
be two vertices in
Q
, then
\mathrm{Arr}_{Q}(w_{1},Q_{0})\cdot\mathrm{Arr}_{Q}(Q_{0},w_{2})<\infty
.

3.

Let
w_{1}
,
w_{2}
and
v
be vertices in
Q
, then
\mathrm{Arr}_{\mu_{gv}Q}(w_{1},w_{2})=\mathrm{Arr}_{Q}(w_{1},w_{2})
for almost all
g\in G
.

Proof.

1.

This follows immediately from condition
3
of
Definition

4.1
. See also
[
7
]
.

2.

The inequality
\mathrm{Arr}_{Q}(w_{1},Q_{0})\cdot\mathrm{Arr}_{Q}(Q_{0},w_{2})<\infty
is an immediate consequence of the local finiteness of
Q
.

3.

The inequality
\mathrm{Arr}_{Q}(w_{1},Q_{0})\cdot\mathrm{Arr}_{Q}(Q_{0},w_{2})<\infty
implies
\mathrm{Arr}_{Q}(w_{1},gv)\cdot\mathrm{Arr}_{Q}(gv,w_{2})=0
for almost all
g\in G
. The equality
\mathrm{Arr}_{\mu_{gv}Q}(w_{1},w_{2})=\mathrm{Arr}_{Q}(w_{1},w_{2})
for almost all
g\in G
is a consequence of the mutation rule. ∎

Definition 4.7
.

Let
Q
be a quiver and
G\subseteq\mathrm{Sym}(Q_{0})
, such that
Q
is strongly
G
-admissible. Given a
G
-orbit
K
, we define
\hat{\mu}_{K}Q
,
the orbit-mutation of
Q
at
K
as the quiver obtained by applying mutation at all vertices of
K
. In view of the lemma above, this operation is well defined.

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
Q
be a quiver and
G\subseteq\mathrm{Sym}(Q_{0})
. Suppose
Q
is strongly
G
-admissible and let
K
be a
G
-orbit. Then the following statements hold.

1.

The quiver
\hat{\mu}_{K}Q
satisfies conditions
1
,
2
and
3
of
Definition

4.1
with respect to
G
.

2.

\mathrm{Arr}_{\mu_{K}(Q^{G})}(Gv_{1},Gv_{2})\leq\mathrm{Arr}_{\hat{\mu}_{K}Q}(Gv_{1},v_{2})
for every
v_{1},v_{2}\in Q_{0}
.

3.

\hat{\mu}_{K}Q
satisfies condition
4
of
Definition

4.1
if and only if

\displaystyle\mathrm{Arr}_{\hat{\mu}_{K}Q}(Gv_{1},v_{2})=\mathrm{Arr}_{\mu_{K}(Q^{G})}(Gv_{1},Gv_{2})

for every
v_{1},v_{2}\in Q_{0}
. In this case
\mu_{K}(Q^{G})=(\hat{\mu}_{K}Q)^{G}
.

Proof.

1.

Condition
2
holds for
\hat{\mu}_{K}Q
trivially. We can calculate the arrows in the orbit-mutated quiver using the following formula

\displaystyle\mathrm{Arr}_{\hat{\mu}_{K}Q}(v_{1},v_{2})=\begin{cases}\mathrm{Arr}_{Q}(v_{2},v_{1})&\text{if $v_{1}\in K$ or $v_{2}\in K$}\\
\max\{0,a_{v_{1},v_{2}}-a_{v_{2},v_{1}}\}&\text{otherwise}\end{cases},

where
a_{w_{1},w_{2}}=\mathrm{Arr}_{Q}(w_{1},w_{2})+\sum_{k\in K}\mathrm{Arr}_{Q}(w_{1},k)\cdot\mathrm{Arr}_{Q}(k,w_{2})
for
w_{1},w_{2}\in Q_{0}
. Conditions
1
and
3
then follow immediately.

2.

If
v_{1}
or
v_{2}
belong to
K
, it is clear that
\mathrm{Arr}_{\hat{\mu}_{K}Q}(Gv_{1},v_{2})=\mathrm{Arr}_{\mu_{K}(Q^{G})}(Gv_{1},Gv_{2})
.
So suppose
v_{1},v_{2}\notin K
. If
\mathrm{Arr}_{\mu_{K}(Q^{G})}(Gv_{1},Gv_{2})=0
the statement is trivial. Otherwise, following the definition of mutation, we must have

\displaystyle\mathrm{Arr}_{\mu_{K}(Q^{G})}(Gv_{1},Gv_{2})=\

\displaystyle\mathrm{Arr}_{Q}(Gv_{1},v_{2})-\mathrm{Arr}_{Q}(v_{2},Gv_{1})

\displaystyle+\mathrm{Arr}_{Q}(v_{1},K)\mathrm{Arr}_{Q}(K,v_{2})-\mathrm{Arr}_{Q}(v_{2},K)\mathrm{Arr}_{Q}(K,v_{1})

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle\mathrm{Arr}_{Q}(Gv_{1},v_{2})-\mathrm{Arr}_{Q}(v_{2},Gv_{1})

\displaystyle+\sum_{k^{\prime}\in K}\sum_{k\in K}\left(\mathrm{Arr}_{Q}(v_{1},k^{\prime})\mathrm{Arr}_{Q}(k,v_{2})-\mathrm{Arr}_{Q}(v_{2},k)\mathrm{Arr}_{Q}(k^{\prime},v_{1})\right)

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle\mathrm{Arr}_{Q}(Gv_{1},v_{2})-\mathrm{Arr}_{Q}(v_{2},Gv_{1})

\displaystyle+\sum_{g\in G}\sum_{k\in K}\left(\mathrm{Arr}_{Q}(gv_{1},k)\mathrm{Arr}_{Q}(k,v_{2})-\mathrm{Arr}_{Q}(v_{2},k)\mathrm{Arr}_{Q}(k,gv_{1})\right)

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle\mathrm{Arr}_{\hat{\mu}_{K}Q}(Gv_{1},v_{2})-\mathrm{Arr}_{\hat{\mu}_{K}Q}(v_{2},Gv_{1})

\displaystyle\leq\

\displaystyle\mathrm{Arr}_{\hat{\mu}_{K}Q}(Gv_{1},v_{2}).

This proves the desired inequality.

3.

Following the previous part, we only need to show the if and only if statement for vertices
v_{1},v_{2}\notin K
.

If
\mathrm{Arr}_{\hat{\mu}_{K}Q}(Gv_{1},v_{2})\mathrm{Arr}_{\hat{\mu}_{K}Q}(v_{2},Gv_{1})=0
, then without loss of generality
\mathrm{Arr}_{\hat{\mu}_{K}Q}(Gv_{1},v_{2})=0
, which implies
0=\mathrm{Arr}_{\hat{\mu}_{K}Q}(Gv_{1},v_{2})\geq\mathrm{Arr}_{\mu_{K}(Q^{G})}(Gv_{1},Gv_{2})
, in particular

\displaystyle\mathrm{Arr}_{\hat{\mu}_{K}Q}(Gv_{1},v_{2})=\mathrm{Arr}_{\mu_{K}(Q^{G})}(Gv_{1},Gv_{2}).

From the previous part we then deduce that

\displaystyle\mathrm{Arr}_{\mu_{K}(Q^{G})}(Gv_{2},Gv_{1})=\mathrm{Arr}_{\hat{\mu}_{K}Q}(Gv_{2},v_{1})-\mathrm{Arr}_{\hat{\mu}_{K}Q}(Gv_{1},v_{2})=\mathrm{Arr}_{\hat{\mu}_{K}Q}(Gv_{2},v_{1}).

In the other direction, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{Arr}_{\hat{\mu}_{K}Q}(Gv_{1},v_{2})\mathrm{Arr}_{\hat{\mu}_{K}Q}(v_{2},Gv_{1})=\mathrm{Arr}_{\mu_{K}(Q^{G})}(Gv_{1},Gv_{2})\mathrm{Arr}_{\mu_{K}(Q^{G})}(Gv_{2},Gv_{1})=0.

When the above holds, the equality
\mu_{K}(Q^{G})=(\hat{\mu}_{K}Q)^{G}
follows immediately. ∎

Definition 4.9
.

Let
Q
be a quiver and
G\subseteq\mathrm{Sym}(Q_{0})
such that
Q
is strongly
G
-admissible. Then
Q
is said to be
strongly globally foldable with respect to
G
if for any finite sequence of orbits
K_{1},...,K_{l}
, the quiver
(\hat{\mu}_{K_{i}}\circ\cdots\circ\hat{\mu}_{K_{1}})(Q)
is strongly
G
-admissible, for all
1\leq i\leq l
.

Let
F\subseteq Q_{0}
be a frozen part such that
(Q,F)
is strongly
G
-admissible. The ice quiver
(Q,F)
is said to be
strongly globally foldable with respect to
G
if for any finite sequence of mutable orbits
K_{1},...,K_{l}
, the quiver
(\hat{\mu}_{K_{i}}\circ\cdots\circ\hat{\mu}_{K_{1}})(Q)
is strongly
G
-admissible, for all
1\leq i\leq l
.

Remark 4.10
.

According to
Lemma

4.8
, for a strongly
G
-admissible quiver
Q
, the quiver
\hat{\mu}_{K}Q
is strongly
G
-admissible if and only if it satisfies condition
4
of
Definition

4.1
. That is, if and only if we have not created “virtual”
2
-cycles while mutating at an orbit. Therefore, to verify that a quiver
Q
is strongly globally foldable with respect to
G
, it suffices to verify that we cannot create those virtual
2
-cycles by process of orbit-mutation.

Moreover, we notice that the equality

\displaystyle\mathrm{Arr}_{\mu_{K}(Q^{G})}(Gv_{2},Gv_{1})-\mathrm{Arr}_{\mu_{K}(Q^{G})}(Gv_{1},Gv_{2})=\mathrm{Arr}_{\hat{\mu}_{K}(Q)}(Gv_{2},v_{1})-\mathrm{Arr}_{\hat{\mu}_{K}(Q)}(Gv_{1},v_{2})

always holds, so

\displaystyle\mathrm{Arr}_{\mu_{K}(Q^{G})}(Gv_{1},Gv_{2})=\mathrm{Arr}_{\hat{\mu}_{K}(Q)}(Gv_{1},v_{2})\iff\mathrm{Arr}_{\mu_{K}(Q^{G})}(Gv_{2},Gv_{1})=\mathrm{Arr}_{\hat{\mu}_{K}(Q)}(Gv_{2},v_{1}).

This will be our strategy later to prove that the infinite quivers belonging to a certain class are strongly globally foldable.

Let
(Q,F)
be an ice quiver and
G\subseteq\mathrm{Sym}(Q_{0})
such that
(Q,F)
is strongly globally foldable with respect to
G
. Let
(Q^{G},F^{G})
be the folded quiver. We consider the algebras

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{Z}[x_{v}|v\in F][x_{v}^{\pm 1}\mid v\in Q_{0}\setminus F],

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{G}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{Z}[x_{Gv}|Gv\in F^{G}][x_{Gv}^{\pm 1}\mid Gv\in Q_{0}^{G}\setminus F^{G}].

We associate to
(Q,F)
and
(Q^{G},F^{G})
the cluster algebras
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{A}^{G}
with initial clusters
(x_{v})_{v\in Q_{0}}
and
(x_{Gv})_{Gv\in Q_{0}^{G}}
, respectively. We consider these cluster algebras as subalgebras of
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{F}^{G}
, respectively.

Any set of cluster variables of
\mathcal{A}
obtained from the initial cluster by an iterated sequence of orbit-mutations is called an
orbit-cluster
. In particular, every cluster variable of an orbit-cluster is a cluster variable in the usual sense.

We have the obvious homomorphism
\psi:\mathcal{F}\rightarrow\mathcal{F}^{G}
defined by
\psi(x_{v})=x_{Gv}
. Given a cluster variable
x
of
\mathcal{A}
which belongs to an orbit-cluster, we call
\psi(x)
the
folded cluster variable
of
x
in
\mathcal{A}^{G}
. This terminology is justified by the following proposition.

Proposition 4.11
.

We keep the notation as above. Let
K_{1},...,K_{l}
be a sequence of mutable orbits in
(Q,F)
. The map
\psi
maps the orbit-cluster of
\mathcal{A}
obtained by the sequence of orbit-mutations at
K_{1},...,K_{l}
to the cluster of
\mathcal{A}^{G}
obtained by the sequence of mutations at
K_{1},...,K_{l}
.

Proof.

By recursion, it suffices to prove the case of a single orbit-mutation. So let
K
be a mutable
G
-orbit of
(Q,F)
. Orbit-mutating at
K
, we get the new cluster
(x_{v}^{\prime})_{v\in Q_{0}}
such that
x_{v}^{\prime}=x_{v}
if
v\notin K
, and

\displaystyle x_{v}^{\prime}=\frac{\prod_{w\in Q_{0}}x_{w}^{\mathrm{Arr}_{Q}(v,w)}+\prod_{w\in Q_{0}}x_{w}^{\mathrm{Arr}_{Q}(w,v)}}{x_{v}}

(1)

if
v\in K
. Similarly, the cluster
(x_{Gv}^{\prime})_{Gv\in Q_{0}^{G}}
obtained by mutating
(Q^{G},F^{G})
at
K
is defined by
x_{Gv}^{\prime}=x_{Gv}
for
Gv\neq K
and

\displaystyle x_{K}^{\prime}=\frac{\prod_{Gw\in Q_{0}^{G}}x_{Gw}^{\mathrm{Arr}_{Q^{G}}(K,Gw)}+\prod_{Gw\in Q_{0}^{G}}x_{Gw}^{\mathrm{Arr}_{Q^{G}}(Gw,K)}}{x_{K}}.

(2)

Applying
\psi
to (
1
), we obtain

\displaystyle\psi(x_{v}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{\prod_{w\in Q_{0}}x_{Gw}^{\mathrm{Arr}_{Q}(v,w)}+\prod_{w\in Q_{0}}x_{Gw}^{\mathrm{Arr}_{Q}(w,v)}}{x_{K}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\prod_{Gw\in Q_{0}^{G}}x_{Gw}^{\sum_{g\in G}\mathrm{Arr}_{Q}(v,gw)}+\prod_{Gw\in Q_{0}^{G}}x_{Gw}^{\sum_{g\in G}\mathrm{Arr}_{Q}(gw,v)}}{x_{K}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\prod_{Gw\in Q_{0}^{G}}x_{Gw}^{\mathrm{Arr}_{Q}(v,Gw)}+\prod_{Gw\in Q_{0}^{G}}x_{Gw}^{\mathrm{Arr}_{Q}(Gw,v)}}{x_{K}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\prod_{Gw\in Q_{0}^{G}}x_{Gw}^{\mathrm{Arr}_{Q^{G}}(K,Gw)}+\prod_{Gw\in Q_{0}^{G}}x_{Gw}^{\mathrm{Arr}_{Q^{G}}(Gw,K)}}{x_{K}}.

Comparing this with (
2
), the result follows.
∎

We have the following immediate corollary.

Corollary 4.12
.

In the notation above, every cluster variable in
\mathcal{A}^{G}
is the folded cluster variable of some cluster variable in
\mathcal{A}
.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
A
be a connected quiver and
G\subseteq\mathrm{Sym}(A_{0})
a group of permutations of the vertices of

such that
A
is strongly
G
-admissible. If
A^{G}
is a finite tree then

and

.

Proof.

Let
\{O_{1},...,O_{n}\}
be the

-orbits in

is not trivial, then
\lvert O_{1}\rvert=\lvert G\rvert>1

v_{1},v_{2}\in O_{1}
be two distinct vertices of

Since

and

(a_{1},...,a_{k})

,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ed}_{A}(a_{1},a_{2})\cdot...\cdot\mathrm{Ed}_{A}(a_{k-1},a_{k})>0.

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ed}_{A}(a_{1},a_{2})\cdot...\cdot\mathrm{Ed}_{A}(a_{j-1},a_{j})>0.

Corollary 5.3

If

Proof.

It is well known (see for example

is mutation-equivalent to a quiver

whose underlying graph is

Assume that

yields a quiver

such that

is finite, which is a contradiction.

We can now give a precise statement for when

is strongly globally foldable with respect to

Theorem 5.4
.

The quiver
A_{Q}
is strongly globally foldable with respect to

if and only if

is not cyclically oriented.

Proof.

From the corollary above, we only need to show that if

is not cyclically oriented, then
A_{Q}
is strongly globally foldable with respect to

From
[

is not cyclically oriented, then it is the quiver of a triangulation

of an annulus with

marks on one boundary component and
k_{2}
marks on the other boundary component such that
k_{1}+k_{2}=n
. Here
k_{1}
is the number of arrows in

Mutations of

correspond to flipping the associated arcs in

T
connects one boundary component with the other. We enumerate the marks on the outer boundary by
v_{1},...,v_{k_{1}}
and the inner boundary by

, both in a clockwise orientation, and such that there is an arc in

connecting
v_{1}
and
u_{1}
.

Let us lift this triangulation to a periodic triangulation of an infinite ribbon
\mathcal{S}
. To do so, we consider an infinite rectangle with two infinite line boundary components, with an infinite countable set of marked points
\mathcal{M}
on each boundary component. We index the marked points on the upper boundary by

Lemma 7.9

Let

\displaystyle\psi(\chi_{q}(L(m)))=\phi_{2n}(\chi_{q}(L(m)))=\chi_{q}(L(\phi_{2n}(m))).

Proof.

We only need to verify the last equality.

From
Theorem

7.7
and
Theorem

7.10
, we know that

\displaystyle\phi_{2n}(\chi_{q}(L(m)))=\chi_{q}(L(m^{\prime}))

for some
m^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
. But
\phi_{2n}(A_{i,a})=A_{[i],a}
for any
i\in\mathbb{Z}
and
a\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
. In particular,
\phi_{2n}(m)
is a maximal monomial of
L(m^{\prime})
with respect to the Nakajima partial order. The equality
m^{\prime}=\phi_{2n}(m)
then follows.
∎

An immediate consequence is the following.

Corollary 7.12
.

The simple
\mathcal{U}_{q}({\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}_{\infty}}})
-modules which correspond to cluster monomials of
\mathcal{A}_{\infty}
satisfy
7.5
. ∎

8
Further remarks and perspectives

We recall the notation of
Section

6
. To an almost positive root of
A_{\infty}
, we associate a monomial
m_{\alpha}\in\tilde{A}_{\infty}
as follows. We set

\displaystyle m_{-\alpha_{i}}=\begin{cases}Y_{i,q^{\xi(i)+2}}&i\in(I_{\infty})_{0}\\
Y_{i,q^{\xi(i)}}&i\in(I_{\infty})_{1}\end{cases}

and

\displaystyle m_{\alpha_{i}}=\begin{cases}Y_{i,q^{\xi(i)}}&i\in(I_{\infty})_{0}\\
Y_{i,q^{\xi(i)+2}}&i\in(I_{\infty})_{1}\end{cases}

for the negative and positive simple roots, and
m_{\alpha}=\prod_{k=i}^{j}m_{\alpha_{k}}
for a positive root
\alpha=\sum_{k=i}^{j}\alpha_{k}
.
To make notation easier, for
i\leq j
, we will denote by
\alpha_{i,j}
the root
\sum_{k=i}^{j}\alpha_{k}
. When
n
is fixed, we will write
\overline{\zeta}\coloneq\phi_{2n}(\zeta)
for
\zeta\in\mathcal{Y}_{\infty}
. Also, we will denote by
\chi_{q}^{\infty}(m)
the
q
-character of the simple
\mathcal{U}_{q}({\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}_{\infty}}})
-module
L(m)
and by
\chi_{q}^{\mathrm{tor}}(\overline{m})
the
q
-character of the simple
\mathcal{U}_{q}({{\mathfrak{sl}_{{2n,\mathrm{tor}}}}})
-module
L(\overline{m})
.

For a given
n
, using the geometric model of
Theorem

5.4
and
Remark

5.7
, it is not hard to see that
\chi_{q}^{\infty}(m_{\alpha})
is a cluster variable of
\mathcal{A}_{\infty}
belonging to an orbit-cluster if and only if one of the following happens:

1.

the root
\alpha
has odd length,

2.

the root
\alpha
has even length smaller than
2n
.

It follows from
Theorem

7.10
and
Corollary

7.11
that the cluster variables of
\mathcal{A}_{2n,\mathrm{tor}}
are exactly
\chi_{q}^{\mathrm{tor}}(\overline{m_{\alpha}})
for
\alpha
of this form. But what is
\chi_{q}^{\mathrm{tor}}(\overline{m_{\alpha_{1,2n}}})
is not clear from this description. We expect it behaves differently, because
\overline{m_{\alpha_{1,2n}}}
corresponds, under our description, to the imaginary root of
A_{2n-1}^{(1)}
.

Proposition 8.1
.

Suppose
n\geq 3
. Then

\displaystyle\overline{\chi_{q}^{\infty}(m_{\alpha_{1,2n}})}=\chi_{q}^{\mathrm{tor}}(\overline{m_{\alpha_{1,2n}}})+\chi_{q}^{\mathrm{tor}}(\overline{m_{\alpha_{3,2n-2}}})\cdot f,

where
f
is some cluster monomial in frozen variables of
\mathcal{A}_{2n,\mathrm{tor}}
.

Proof.

We write down a few standard exchange relations in
\mathcal{A}_{\infty}
, with
i+2\leq j
:

\displaystyle\chi_{q}^{\infty}(m_{\alpha_{i,j}})\cdot\chi_{q}^{\infty}(m_{\alpha_{j+1}})

\displaystyle=\chi_{q}^{\infty}(m_{\alpha_{i,j+1}})+\chi_{q}^{\infty}(m_{\alpha_{i,j-2}})\cdot\chi_{q}^{\infty}(m_{-\alpha_{j+2}})\cdot f,

(10)

\displaystyle\chi_{q}^{\infty}(m_{\alpha_{i-1}})\cdot\chi_{q}^{\infty}(m_{\alpha_{i,j}})

\displaystyle=\chi_{q}^{\infty}(m_{\alpha_{i-1,j}})+\chi_{q}^{\infty}(m_{\alpha_{i+2,j}})\cdot\chi_{q}^{\infty}(m_{-\alpha_{i-2}})\cdot f,

(11)

\displaystyle\chi_{q}^{\infty}(m_{\alpha_{i,j}})\cdot\chi_{q}^{\infty}(m_{-\alpha_{j}})

\displaystyle=\chi_{q}^{\infty}(m_{\alpha_{i,j-1}})\cdot f+\chi_{q}^{\infty}(m_{\alpha_{i,j-2}})\cdot\chi_{q}^{\infty}(m_{-\alpha_{j+1}})\cdot f,

(12)

\displaystyle\chi_{q}^{\infty}(m_{-\alpha_{i}})\cdot\chi_{q}^{\infty}(m_{\alpha_{i,j}})

\displaystyle=\chi_{q}^{\infty}(m_{\alpha_{i+1,j}})\cdot f+\chi_{q}^{\infty}(m_{\alpha_{i+2,j}})\cdot\chi_{q}^{\infty}(m_{-\alpha_{i-1}})\cdot f.

(13)

Here
f
stands for some cluster monomial in frozen variables. Applying
\phi_{2n}
to equations (
10
) with
i=1
and
j=2n-1
and (
13
) with
i=1
and
j=2n-3
, we get

\displaystyle\chi_{q}^{\mathrm{tor}}(\overline{m_{\alpha_{1,2n-1}}})\cdot\chi_{q}^{\mathrm{tor}}(\overline{m_{\alpha_{2n}}})=\overline{\chi_{q}^{\infty}(m_{\alpha_{1,2n}})}+\chi_{q}^{\mathrm{tor}}(\overline{m_{\alpha_{1,2n-3}}})\cdot\chi_{q}^{\mathrm{tor}}(L(\overline{m_{-\alpha_{2n+1}}}))\cdot\overline{f}

and

\displaystyle\chi_{q}^{\mathrm{tor}}(\overline{m_{\alpha_{1,2n-3}}})\cdot\chi_{q}^{\mathrm{tor}}(\overline{m_{-\alpha_{1}}})=\chi_{q}^{\mathrm{tor}}(\overline{m_{\alpha_{2,2n-3}}})\cdot\overline{f}+\chi_{q}^{\mathrm{tor}}(\overline{m_{\alpha_{3,2n-3}}})\cdot\chi_{q}^{\mathrm{tor}}(L(\overline{m_{-\alpha_{0}}}))\cdot\overline{f}.

But as
\chi_{q}^{\mathrm{tor}}(L(\overline{m_{-\alpha_{2n+1}}}))=\chi_{q}^{\mathrm{tor}}(L(\overline{m_{-\alpha_{1}}}))
, we can combine the two equations to have

\chi_{q}^{\mathrm{tor}}(\overline{m_{\alpha_{1,2n-1}}})\cdot\chi_{q}^{\mathrm{tor}}(\overline{m_{\alpha_{2n}}})=\\
\overline{\chi_{q}^{\infty}(m_{\alpha_{1,2n}})}+\chi_{q}^{\mathrm{tor}}(\overline{m_{\alpha_{2,2n-3}}})\cdot\overline{f}+\chi_{q}^{\mathrm{tor}}(\overline{m_{\alpha_{3,2n-3}}})\cdot\chi_{q}^{\mathrm{tor}}(L(\overline{m_{-\alpha_{0}}}))\cdot\overline{f}.

(14)

A similar argument using (
11
) and (
12
) gives

\chi_{q}^{\mathrm{tor}}(\overline{m_{\alpha_{1}}})\cdot\chi_{q}^{\mathrm{tor}}(\overline{m_{\alpha_{2,2n}}})\\
=\overline{\chi_{q}^{\infty}(m_{\alpha_{1,2n}})}+\chi_{q}^{\mathrm{tor}}(\overline{m_{\alpha_{4,2n-1}}})\cdot\overline{f}+\chi_{q}^{\mathrm{tor}}(\overline{m_{\alpha_{4,2n-2}}})\cdot\chi_{q}^{\mathrm{tor}}(L(\overline{m_{-\alpha_{1}}}))\cdot\overline{f}.

The
q
-characters
\chi_{q}^{\mathrm{tor}}(\overline{m_{\alpha_{2,2n-3}}})\cdot\overline{f}
,
\chi_{q}^{\mathrm{tor}}(\overline{m_{\alpha_{3,2n-3}}})\cdot\chi_{q}^{\mathrm{tor}}(L(\overline{m_{-\alpha_{0}}}))\cdot\overline{f}
,
\chi_{q}^{\mathrm{tor}}(\overline{m_{\alpha_{4,2n-1}}})\cdot\overline{f}
and
\chi_{q}^{\mathrm{tor}}(\overline{m_{\alpha_{4,2n-2}}})\cdot\chi_{q}^{\mathrm{tor}}(L(\overline{m_{-\alpha_{1}}}))\cdot\overline{f}
are all
q
-characters of pairwise non-isomorphic simple
\mathcal{U}_{q}({{\mathfrak{sl}_{{2n,\mathrm{tor}}}}})
-modules (
Theorem

7.7
). We deduce that
\overline{\chi_{q}^{\infty}(m_{\alpha_{1,2n}})}
is the
q
-character of an actual
\mathcal{U}_{q}({{\mathfrak{sl}_{{2n,\mathrm{tor}}}}})
-module. Certainly,
\overline{\chi_{q}^{\infty}(m_{\alpha_{1,2n}})}
contains
\chi_{q}^{\mathrm{tor}}(\overline{m_{\alpha_{1,2n}}})
. Let us find all its composition factors.

By computing (
11
) with
i=1
and
j=2n-1
, and (
12
) with
i=3
and
j=2n-1
, we can obtain (similar to the computation before)

\chi_{q}^{\mathrm{tor}}(\overline{m_{\alpha_{1,2n-1}}})\cdot\chi_{q}^{\mathrm{tor}}(\overline{m_{\alpha_{2n}}})=\\
\overline{\chi_{q}^{\infty}(m_{\alpha_{0,2n-1}})}+\chi_{q}^{\mathrm{tor}}(\overline{m_{\alpha_{3,2n-2}}})\cdot\overline{f}+\chi_{q}^{\mathrm{tor}}(\overline{m_{\alpha_{3,2n-3}}})\cdot\chi_{q}^{\mathrm{tor}}(L(\overline{m_{-\alpha_{0}}}))\cdot\overline{f}.

Comparing this expression with (
14
), we can deduce that
\chi_{q}^{\mathrm{tor}}(\overline{m_{\alpha_{3,2n-2}}})\cdot\overline{f}
is a summand of
\overline{\chi_{q}^{\infty}(m_{\alpha_{1,2n}})}
. Similar to the previous arguments, we can obtain the equations

\overline{\chi_{q}^{\infty}(m_{\alpha_{1,2n}})}\cdot\chi_{q}^{\mathrm{tor}}(\overline{m_{-\alpha_{1}}})=\\
\chi_{q}^{\mathrm{tor}}(\overline{m_{\alpha_{2,2n}}})\cdot\overline{f}+\chi_{q}^{\mathrm{tor}}(\overline{m_{\alpha_{3,2n-1}}})\cdot\overline{f}+\chi_{q}^{\mathrm{tor}}(\overline{m_{\alpha_{3,2n-2}}})\cdot\chi_{q}^{\mathrm{tor}}(\overline{m_{-\alpha_{1}}})\cdot\overline{f}

and

\overline{\chi_{q}^{\infty}(m_{\alpha_{1,2n}})}\cdot\chi_{q}^{\mathrm{tor}}(\overline{m_{-\alpha_{2n}}})=\\
\chi_{q}^{\mathrm{tor}}(\overline{m_{\alpha_{1,2n-1}}})\cdot\overline{f}+\chi_{q}^{\mathrm{tor}}(\overline{m_{\alpha_{2,2n-2}}})\cdot\overline{f}+\chi_{q}^{\mathrm{tor}}(\overline{m_{\alpha_{3,2n-2}}})\cdot\chi_{q}^{\mathrm{tor}}(\overline{m_{-\alpha_{2n}}})\cdot\overline{f}.

We can deduce from that that
\overline{\chi_{q}^{\infty}(m_{1,2n})}
has at most three summands. If it had exactly three, this would implies

\displaystyle\chi_{q}^{\mathrm{tor}}(\overline{m_{-\alpha_{2n}}})\cdot\chi_{q}^{\mathrm{tor}}(\overline{m_{\alpha_{3,2n-1}}})\cdot\overline{f}=\chi_{q}^{\mathrm{tor}}(\overline{m_{-\alpha_{1}}})\cdot\chi_{q}^{\mathrm{tor}}(\overline{m_{\alpha_{2,2n-2}}})\cdot\overline{f},

which is absurd. This completes the proof.
∎

Similar computations give

Fact 8.2
.

Let
i,j\in\mathbb{Z}
be such that
j-i\leq 4n-2
. Then
\overline{\chi_{q}^{\infty}(m_{\alpha_{i,j}})}
is the
q
-character of an actual
\mathcal{U}_{q}({{\mathfrak{sl}_{{2n,\mathrm{tor}}}}})
-module. In particular, the simple
\mathcal{U}_{q}({\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}_{\infty}}})
-module
L(m_{\alpha_{i,j}})
satisfy
7.5
.

However, it is not clear (to the author) if it is possible to deduce the same result for
\alpha
of length
4n
, that is, for
j-i=4n-1
.

In Example 6.15 of
[
30
]
, Nakajima explains that for
n=1
, the module
L(\overline{m_{1,2n}})
is not real, which in
q
-character theory amounts to

\displaystyle\chi_{q}^{\mathrm{tor}}(\overline{m_{1,2n}})^{2}\neq\chi_{q}^{\mathrm{tor}}(\overline{m_{1,4n}}).

This raises the question whether this is a general phenomenon.

Conjecture 8.3
.

The simple
\mathcal{U}_{q}({{\mathfrak{sl}_{{2n,\mathrm{tor}}}}})
-module
L(\overline{m_{1,2n}})
is an imaginary module, that is,
L(\overline{m_{1,2n}})\ast L(\overline{m_{1,2n}})
is not simple.

Although some examples of imaginary modules of affinized quantum Kac-Moody algebras are known, for example
[
27
,
2
,
3
]
, these are hard to find. Proving this conjecture would give a new interesting example for quantum toroidal algebras.

The difficulty of computing
\chi_{q}^{\mathrm{tor}}(\overline{m_{1,4n}})
does not allow us to prove or disprove this conjecture directly, in terms of the arguments above. For
n\geq 3
we do manage to show

\displaystyle\chi_{q}^{\mathrm{tor}}(\overline{m_{1,2n}})^{2}=\chi_{q}^{\mathrm{tor}}(\overline{m_{1,4n}})+\lambda f

with
\lambda\in\{0,1,2\}
. However,
\lambda=0
if and only if
\overline{\chi_{q}^{\infty}(m_{1,4n})}
is a virtual
q
-character. We can deduce

Claim 8.4
.

Assume
7.5
holds. Then
L(\overline{m_{1,2n}})
is an imaginary
\mathcal{U}_{q}({{\mathfrak{sl}_{{2n,\mathrm{tor}}}}})
-module for
n\geq 3
.

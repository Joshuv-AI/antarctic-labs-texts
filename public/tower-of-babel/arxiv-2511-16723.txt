A new suite of Lund-tree observables to resolve jets Preprint: CERN-TH-2025-213, Nikhef 2025-016
Melissa van Beekveld, Luca Buonocore, Silvia Ferrario Ravasio, Pier Francesco Monni, Alba Soto-Ontoso, Gregory Soyez

Abstract
We introduce a class of collider observables, named Lund-Tree Shapes (LTS), defined from declustering trees originating from the Lund jet plane representation of the QCD radiation pattern in multi-jet scattering processes. At the differential level, they are continuous global variables akin classical event shapes and n → n + 1 n\to n+1 jet-resolution parameters, which probe the geometry and hierarchical structure of the radiation in an event. At the integrated, cumulative level, they naturally define n n jet rates, providing a jet-multiplicity-based characterisation of multi-jet final states. Their definition applies to scattering processes with any number of resolved jets in the final state, as well as to groomed jets. They are thus usable as resolution variables in the context of higher-order calculations via phase-space slicing, matching fixed-order calculations to parton showers, and testing the logarithmic accuracy of shower algorithms. From a theoretical viewpoint, such observables feature a simple all-order structure and are free of non-global logarithmic corrections. As an initial application, we derive next-to-next-to-leading-logarithmic accurate predictions for processes with two QCD legs at e ​ e ee , p ​ p pp and e ​ p ep colliders, and matched predictions to next-to-next-to-leading order for the LHC, discussing aspects of collider phenomenology.

Introduction

Understanding the pattern of QCD radiation in high-energy collisions
is a central goal of the modern collider-physics programme. Hard
scattering events produce jets of collimated particles whose internal
structure encodes fundamental aspects of strong
interactions. Observables such as jet rates, event shapes, and jet
substructure variables have long been used to probe this radiation,
testing the accuracy of perturbative predictions and of parton
showers. Yet, despite their success, many of these observables face
inherent limitations: some are sensitive to non-global logarithms,
others are largely affected by underlying event activity at hadron
colliders, and some do not generalise easily to multi-leg scattering
processes.
To overcome these limitations, in this article we exploit the
Lund-jet-plane
Andersson:1988gp
;
Dreyer:2018nbf
representation
of QCD radiation as a robust way to define new observables that are
both theoretically tractable and experimentally accessible.

With this technology, we introduce Lund-Tree Shapes (LTS). For
processes with two emitting legs, these observables were first
introduced (at next-to-leading-logarithmic accuracy, NLL) for the
purpose of testing the logarithmic accuracy of parton showers in

ee

Dasgupta:2020fwr
,

vanBeekveld:2022ukn

providing a resolution-based characterisation of multi-jet events and
a natural definition of jet vetoes with improved theoretical

2.3
Lepton-hadron collisions

For DIS, it is convenient to start by considering a single
final-state jet and then generalise the definition to arbitrary jet
multiplicities.

We work in the Breit frame

incoming parton has energy exactly equal to the total energy of the
final-state partons, and the momentum transfer

q^{\mu}_{\rm dis}=q^{\mu}_{\rm out}-q^{\mu}_{\rm in}

The event is then clustered using the Cambridge algorithm, in analogy with

identified as the one carrying the largest light-cone component
anti-parallel to the incoming parton in the Breit
frame. Operationally, it is the jet whose momentum maximises the
scalar product with the proton momentum.

We then decluster this final-state jet following the same procedure as
in the lepton-collider case, denoting the list of its primary
declusterings by

constructed as outlined in the previous sections depending on the type

(k_{t}^{(\widetilde{ij})},\,y^{(\widetilde{ij})})
. For each of these

v_{b,\,{\rm SD}}^{(\widetilde{ij})}=k_{t}^{(\widetilde{ij})}e^{-b|y^{(\widetilde{ij})}|}
.
The groomed LTS (gLTS) are then defined by

M_{b,\,{\rm SD}}=\max_{j\in{\cal I}_{J_{\rm SD}}}v_{b,\,{\rm SD}}^{j}\,,\quad S_{b,\,{\rm SD}}=\sum_{j\in{\cal I}_{J_{\rm SD}}}v_{b,\,{\rm SD}}^{j}\,.

They can be normalised to hard scale of the jet, i.e. its pre-grooming

pp

The resummation structure of these observables for
\beta=0

, can be studied, for instance, with the methods of

;

;

3
Resummation to NNLL for the two-legs case

In this section we discuss the resummation properties of the LTS,
and perform explicit calculations up to the NNLL order in the case of
processes with
2
radiating legs at the lowest order. We will start
with a discussion of the lepton-collider case, and then illustrate the
hadron-collider and DIS cases.

3.1
N=2
case at lepton colliders

The LTS at lepton colliders have a very simple resummation
structure. We adopt the resummation formalism of
Refs.
Banfi:2001bz
;
Banfi:2014sua
;
Banfi:2016zlc
;
Banfi:2018mcq
,
in which one expresses the (non-normalised) cumulative cross section for
a given observable
v\in\{M_{b}^{(2)},S_{b}^{(2)}\}
as follows

\Sigma^{ee}(v)\equiv\int_{0}^{v}dv^{\prime}\frac{d\sigma}{dv^{\prime}}=\Sigma^{ee}_{S}(v){\cal F}^{ee}(v)\,.

(25)

The explicit expressions of the ingredients of the above equation
depend on whether we are considering a Born final state consisting of
two quark jets (
Z
decay) or two gluon jets (
H
decay). To keep the
notation light, we suppress the dependence on the flavour of the final
state in this section. However we provide results for both quark and
gluon jets in Appendix
A
.

The factor
\Sigma^{ee}_{S}(v)
denotes the cumulative distribution
for a simplified version of the observable. Its definition proceeds by
classifying the multi-parton squared matrix elements into correlated
clusters of emissions
Banfi:2018mcq
, namely the building blocks
of the multi-parton squared amplitudes that vanish in the limit where
emissions are widely separated in rapidity.
In practice, this classification can be implemented recursively. For
concreteness, let us consider the soft limit. In this regime, matrix
elements factorise into independent eikonal factors whenever
emissions, or subsets of emissions, are widely separated in rapidity.
With two emissions, one can thus identify an uncorrelated contribution
obtained in the limit where the two emissions are widely
separated. The correlated contribution is then defined as the
remainder, which by construction only has support in the region where
the two emissions have comparable rapidities.
This recursive definition extends naturally to higher
multiplicities
Banfi:2018mcq
.
4
4

4

For example, for three
emissions, the procedure generalises as follows. One first subtracts
the fully uncorrelated contribution, corresponding to three widely
separated emissions, which factorises into a product of three
independent eikonal factors. Next, one removes the three
configurations in which a pair of emissions is widely separated from
the third, which factorise into a single emission and a correlated
pair. The remaining term then represents the contribution from three
correlated emissions.

An analogous organisation can be carried out in the collinear limit.
One then calculates the quantity

v_{b}(k)=\frac{k_{t}}{Q}e^{-b|y|}\,,

(26)

for each of the correlated clusters, and defines the simplified
observable to be the largest of these.

This definition leads to a remarkably simple resummation structure that, at
NNLL order, can be cast as

\Sigma^{ee}_{S}(v)=\sigma^{(0)}\left(1+\frac{\alpha_{s}(\mu_{\scriptscriptstyle R})}{2\pi}H^{(1)}+\sum_{\ell=1}^{2}\frac{\alpha_{s}(\mu_{\scriptscriptstyle R})}{2\pi}\frac{1+b}{1+b-2\lambda}C_{{\rm hc},\,\ell}^{(1)}\right)\exp\{-\mathcal{R}_{\rm NNLL}(v)\}\,,

(27)

where
\sigma^{(0)}
is the Born cross-section and

\lambda=\alpha_{s}(\mu_{\scriptscriptstyle R})\beta_{0}L
, with
L
being the large logarithms
to resum (cf. Eq. (
83
)).
The constant
H^{(1)}
(
C^{(1)}_{\rm hc}
) stems from the hard
(hard-collinear) region.
The sums over
\ell
reflect the presence of two legs in the process.
All constants as a function of the parameter
b
are given in
Appendix
A.4
.
The NNLL Sudakov radiator
\mathcal{R}_{\rm NNLL}(v)
, which is a function of the parameter
b
, can be written as a sum of contributions
R_{\ell}(v)
from each emitting leg
\ell
, which can be in turn decomposed into a soft and collinear term

\mathcal{R}_{\rm NNLL}(v)=\sum_{\ell=1}^{2}R_{\ell}(v)=\sum_{\ell=1}^{2}\left[R^{\ell}_{\text{s}}(v)+R^{\ell}_{\text{hc}}(v)\right]\,.

(28)

At NNLL accuracy, the soft radiator
R_{s}^{\ell}
reads

\displaystyle R_{\rm s}^{\ell}(v)=

\displaystyle-\frac{\lambda}{\alpha_{s}\beta_{0}}g_{1}^{\ell}(\lambda)-g_{2}^{\ell}(\lambda)-\frac{\alpha_{s}}{\pi}\left(g_{3}^{\ell}(\lambda)+\delta g_{3}^{\ell}(\lambda)\right)\,,

(29)

and the hard-collinear radiator reads

\displaystyle R^{\ell}_{\rm hc}(v)=-h_{2}^{\ell}(\lambda)-\frac{\alpha_{s}}{\pi}h_{3}^{\ell}(\lambda)\,.

(30)

The functions
g^{\ell}_{i}
,
\delta g_{3}^{\ell}
and
h_{i}^{\ell}
are
provided in Appendix
A
.
An important aspect of the definition of the simplified observable is
that it agrees with the full observable at LL.

Let us now consider the second factor in the r.h.s. of
Eq. (
25
), the
transfer function

{\cal F}^{ee}(v)
. As the name suggests, this accounts for the difference
between the full observable that is being resummed, in this case
either
M_{b}^{(2)}
or
S_{b}^{(2)}
, and the simplified observable encoded in

\Sigma^{ee}_{S}
.
By definition, it is given by

{\cal F}^{ee}(v)\equiv\frac{\Sigma^{ee}(v)}{\Sigma^{ee}_{S}(v)}\,.

(31)

This object has the following key properties. Firstly, since by
definition
\Sigma_{S}(v)
contains all the LL corrections, the transfer
function starts at NLL order. Secondly, the transfer function is
finite in
D=4
space-time dimensions, which crucially makes it
amenable to a numerical computation. To make this fact manifest, it is
customary to introduce a resolution parameter
\delta
and recast
Eq. (
31
) as

{\cal F}^{ee}(v)=\lim_{\delta\to 0}\frac{\Sigma^{ee}(v)}{\Sigma^{ee}_{S}(\delta v)}\frac{\Sigma^{ee}_{S}(\delta v)}{\Sigma^{ee}_{S}(v)}\,,

(32)

which can be directly used for practical calculations.
The fact that Eq. (
32
) is finite in the limit

\delta\to 0
stems from the recursive IRC (rIRC)
safety
Banfi:2004yd
of LTS: emissions that contribute to

\Sigma^{ee}(v)
with a
v_{b}\leq\delta v
can be neglected in the
evaluation of the observable.
Therefore, their contribution to
\Sigma^{ee}(v)
factorises from
the one of resolved emissions (i.e. with
v_{b}>\delta v
) and
cancels against the denominator
\Sigma^{ee}_{S}(\delta v)
. The
residual dependence on
\delta
will now balance that of the ratio

\Sigma^{ee}_{S}(\delta v)/\Sigma_{S}(v)
, leading to a result that is
finite in the
\delta\to 0
limit.
Following
Refs.
Banfi:2014sua
;
Banfi:2016zlc
;
Banfi:2018mcq
;
Arpino:2019ozn
,
at NNLL these corrections can be parameterised as follows

\displaystyle{\cal F}^{ee}(v)

\displaystyle\,={\cal F}^{ee}_{\rm NLL}(v)+\frac{\alpha_{s}(\mu_{\scriptscriptstyle R})}{\pi}\delta{\cal F}^{ee}_{\rm NNLL}(v)\,,

(33a)

\displaystyle\delta{\cal F}^{ee}_{\rm NNLL}(v)

\displaystyle\,=\delta{\cal F}^{ee}_{\rm sc}(v)+\delta{\cal F}^{ee}_{\rm wa}(v)+\delta{\cal F}^{ee}_{\rm rec}(v)+\delta{\cal F}^{ee}_{\rm hc}(v)+\delta{\cal F}^{ee}_{\rm correl}(v)+\delta{\cal F}^{ee}_{\rm clust}(v)\,.

(33b)

The structure of Eq. (
33
) can be understood with a simple
power counting argument, that can be explained with the help of the
primary Lund plane in Fig.
2
.

:
Location of NNLL corrections to the transfer function
in the Lund plane of the Born

At NLL order,
{\cal F}^{ee}_{\rm NLL}(v)
is described by an
ensemble of multiple soft
and
collinear emissions widely separated in
rapidity, each having a value
v_{i}
of the observable, with

\delta v\leq v_{i}\leq v
.
This is represented by the shaded region adjacent to the observable
boundary in Fig.

, which is populated by such emissions.

Beyond NLL,
{\cal F}^{ee}(v)
must encode corrections in regions of the
Lund plane that are parameterically suppressed by
{\cal O}(\alpha_{s})

(counting
L\sim 1/\alpha_{s}
) with respect to the NLL limit. Each of the
terms in Eq. (

different kinematic limits (cf. discussion in

Banfi:2014sua
;
Banfi:2016zlc
;

for more details).
In short, these are:

•

An extra pair of soft-collinear emissions becoming close in
rapidity. This correction is encoded into the

\delta{\cal F}^{ee}_{\rm correl}(v)
(involving the correlated
part of the double-soft squared amplitude) and

\delta{\cal F}^{ee}_{\rm clust}(v)
(involving the uncorrelated,
independent part of the double-soft squared amplitude).
This contribution starts with terms of order
\alpha_{s}^{2}L
.

•

A single extra soft emission at wide angle w.r.t. the event
direction, encoded in the correction
\delta{\cal F}^{ee}_{\rm wa}(v)
. This correction encodes the difference between the
observable and the simple observable for a soft gluon radiated with

This contribution starts at order
\alpha_{s}
.

•

A single extra hard-collinear emission, giving rise to the
corrections
\delta{\cal F}^{ee}_{\rm rec}(v)
and

\delta{\cal F}^{ee}_{\rm hc}(v)
.
The former,
\delta{\cal F}^{ee}_{\rm rec}(v)
is the recoil
correction which encodes the difference between the observable and

kinematics.
The latter,
\delta{\cal F}^{ee}_{\rm hc}(v)
, accounts for the
correction to the matrix element.

•

A single extra soft-collinear emission, with a higher-order
kernel containing running-coupling terms in the CMW
scheme
Catani:1990rr
;
Banfi:2018mcq
;
Catani:2019rvy
as well as
with the correct rapidity bound which was approximated at
NLL.
5
5

5

At NLL one expands the rapidity bound for each

|\eta|\leq\ln\frac{Q}{k_{t}}\simeq\frac{1}{1+b}\ln\frac{1}{v}
.
This correction defines

\delta{\cal F}^{ee}_{\rm sc}(v)
.

All the ingredients are provided in Appendix
A
.
In our numerical implementation, we expand out the product

\Sigma_{S}\,{\cal F}_{\rm NNLL}
in Eq. (

) so that

\delta{\cal F}_{\rm NNLL}
multiplies only the exponential of the
Sudakov radiator
\exp\{-\mathcal{R}_{\rm NNLL}(v)\}
, i.e. without
the

3.2
N=0
case at hadron colliders

We will first consider the observable defined in the partonic
c.o.m. frame (cf. Eq. (
16
) and discussion
thereof) where the colour singlet has zero rapidity. We will then
discuss the laboratory-frame definition of the LTS in
Sec.
3.2.1
.
In the hadron-collider case, the structure of the NNLL resummation for
the cumulative distribution
\Sigma^{pp}(v)
has to be modified
with respect to the lepton-collider form given in Eq. (
25
).
A first difference with the lepton-collider case is due to the precise
definition of the observable, and specifically to the different jet
algorithm and the definitions of transverse momentum and rapidity
adopted, as discussed in Sec.
2.2
. This will modify the
correlated and clustering corrections, whose expressions are reported
in Appendix
A
.
A second key difference is due to the presence of initial-state
radiation (ISR) that modifies the structure of the resummation of
collinear logarithms, both in the simple observable
\Sigma_{S}(v)
, as
well as in the form of the transfer function
{\cal F}
.

To gain intuition, it is instructive to consider the NNLL transfer
function defined in Eq. (
33
) in the final-state radiation
(FSR) case. Among the six NNLL corrections to
{\cal F}^{ee}
, four
originate from soft dynamics. In the initial-state radiation (ISR)
case, these soft corrections do not modify the longitudinal momentum
fractions of the incoming partons, and hence their functional form
remains identical to that of the FSR case (up to modifications arising
from the differences between observables, as discussed above).

In contrast, the hard-collinear and recoil corrections account for the
effects of hard-collinear splittings in the final state. The
hard-collinear correction describes the modification of the matrix
element due to the emission of hard-collinear radiation, while
treating the corresponding kinematics as soft. The recoil correction,
on the other hand, captures the impact of the recoil induced by the
hard-collinear emission on the observable.

In the ISR case, the corrections associated with an initial-state
hard-collinear splitting differ in several respects. First, since the
beam direction, and hence the observable, is unaffected by transverse
recoil, the corresponding recoil correction is absent. Second, the
hard-collinear correction remains unchanged, as it reflects a
modification of the matrix element rather than of the emission
kinematics, which are treated as soft. Consequently, this correction
does not alter the longitudinal momentum fractions of the incoming
partons.
In addition, a new correction arises, accounting for the longitudinal
recoil induced by the hard-collinear emission. We refer to this as the
DGLAP correction, which will be discussed in detail below. This
correction replaces the recoil correction present in the FSR
case.

To derive the NNLL resummation formula, we start by defining the
contribution of the simple observable,
\Sigma_{S}^{pp}(v)
.
The
simple
observable in the hadron-collider case can be
defined analogously to its lepton-collider counterpart given in
Eq. (
26
), where now the transverse momentum
k_{t}
and
rapidity
y
are taken in the c.o.m. frame of the partonic scattering.
To formalise the discussion, we consider the production of a colour
singlet system
F
in a
pp
collision. For simplicity we assume this
production process to be mediated by a single flavour channel (either
quark or gluon initiated). At the parton level, we will denote the
flavour of the two partons initiating the Born process by
c
,
d
.
For processes that receive a contribution from different flavour
channels one should repeat the discussion below for each of the
relevant channels and add them together.
With a slight abuse of notation, we denote the cumulative cross
section differential in the kinematics of the final state at the Born
level by
\Sigma(v,\Phi_{\scriptscriptstyle{\rm B}})\equiv d\Sigma/d\Phi_{\scriptscriptstyle{\rm B}}
. The
resummation for the simple observable at NNLL takes the form

\displaystyle\Sigma^{pp}_{S}(v,\Phi_{\scriptscriptstyle{\rm B}})

\displaystyle=\frac{d\hat{\sigma}^{(0)}_{cd}}{d\Phi_{B}}\exp\{-\mathcal{R}_{\rm NNLL}(v)\}\,\bigg[f_{c}(x_{c},\mu_{\scriptscriptstyle{\rm ISR}})f_{d}(x_{d},\mu_{\scriptscriptstyle{\rm ISR}})\left(1+\frac{\alpha_{s}(\mu_{\scriptscriptstyle R})}{2\pi}H^{(1)}_{cd}(\Phi_{B})\right)\,

\displaystyle\!\!\!+\frac{\alpha_{s}(\mu_{\scriptscriptstyle R})}{2\pi}\frac{1+b}{1+b-2\lambda}\sum_{i}\left(\big(C^{(1)}_{ci}\otimes f_{i}\big)(x_{c},\mu_{\scriptscriptstyle{\rm ISR}})\,f_{d}(x_{d},\mu_{\scriptscriptstyle{\rm ISR}})+\{c\leftrightarrow d\}\right)\bigg]\,,

(34)

where
\hat{\sigma}_{cd}^{(0)}

the

C_{ci}^{(1)}

b

A

x_{c,d}=\frac{Q}{\sqrt{s}}e^{\pm y_{F}}\,,

(35)

\displaystyle\mu_{\scriptscriptstyle{\rm ISR}}=\mu_{\scriptscriptstyle F}e^{-\frac{L}{1+b}}\,.

(36)

(37)

We now discuss the transfer function. Starting at NNLL, the emission
of hard-collinear radiation modifies the longitudinal momentum

x_{c}
and

(see, for instance, the

\Sigma^{pp}(v,\Phi_{\scriptscriptstyle{\rm B}})=\Sigma^{pp}_{S}(v,\Phi_{\rm B}){\cal F}^{pp}(v)+\delta\Sigma^{pp}_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle{DGLAP}}(v,\Phi_{\scriptscriptstyle{\rm B}})\,,

(38)

\displaystyle{\cal F}^{pp}(v)

\displaystyle\,={\cal F}^{pp}_{\rm NLL}(v)+\frac{\alpha_{s}(\mu_{\scriptscriptstyle R})}{\pi}\delta{\cal F}^{pp}_{\rm NNLL}(v)\,,

\displaystyle\delta{\cal F}^{pp}_{\rm NNLL}(v)

\displaystyle\,=\delta{\cal F}^{pp}_{\rm sc}(v)+\delta{\cal F}^{pp}_{\rm wa}(v)+\delta{\cal F}^{pp}_{\rm hc}(v)+\delta{\cal F}^{pp}_{\rm correl}(v)+\delta{\cal F}^{pp}_{\rm clust}(v)\,.

The new term

3.2.2
Absence of non-global logarithms in the multi-jet case

In this section, we discuss the absence of non-global
logarithms
Dasgupta:2001sh
;
Banfi:2002hw
;
Caron-Huot:2015bja
;
Banfi:2021xzn
;
Banfi:2021owj
;
FerrarioRavasio:2023kyg
;
Becher:2023vrh

in the resummation of LTS, in particular when three or more coloured legs are present
already at the Born level.
For the sake of arguments, we examine one such processes,
pp\to Fj
,
where
F
is a generic colour singlet and
j
is a hard and resolved
final-state jet.
We then start by performing a fixed-order calculation of the leading
non-global configuration, stemming from the emission of a pair of soft
wide-angle gluons with momentum
p_{1,2}
strongly ordered in energy.
At this order, non-global corrections arise from scenarios in which one
of the two gluons propagates inside the jet
j
and one propagates
outside (and hence clusters with the beam in the definition of the
LTS).
We introduce the following parameterisation for the kinematics

\displaystyle p_{n}=E_{n}\left(1,0,0,1\right),\;\;

\displaystyle p_{\bar{n}}=E_{\bar{n}}\left(1,0,0,-1\right),\;\;

(58)

\displaystyle p_{\ell}=p_{t,\ell}(\cosh\eta_{\ell},\sin\phi_{\ell},\cos

\displaystyle\phi_{\ell},\sinh\eta_{\ell})\,,\quad\ell\in\{j,1,2\}\,,

where
p_{n,\bar{n}}
denote the momenta of the two incoming partons and

p_{j}
that of the final-state jet, and all quantities are defined
w.r.t. the beam axis.
To simplify the calculation, we work in the planar, large-
N_{c}
limit
(
C_{F}=C_{A}/2=N_{c}/2
) that is customary in the study of non-global
observables. The Born will be then a collection of dipoles.
To single out the leading non-global configuration, we consider the
situation in which the two emissions are strongly ordered and we write
the corresponding emission probability as

\displaystyle[dp_{1}][dp_{2}]W_{2}

\displaystyle=[dp_{1}][dp_{2}](4\pi\alpha_{s})^{2}N_{c}^{2}\!\!\!\sum_{\{ik\}\in{\rm dipoles}}\frac{(ik)}{(i1)(k1)}\left(\frac{(i1)}{(12)(i2)}+\frac{(k1)}{(12)(k2)}-\frac{(ik)}{(i2)(k2)}\right)

\displaystyle\equiv 4\,(4\pi\alpha_{s})^{2}N_{c}^{2}\frac{[dp_{1}][dp_{2}]}{p_{t,1}^{2}p_{t,2}^{2}}\,w_{2}\,,

(59)

where

[dp_{i}]=\frac{d\eta_{i}}{2}\frac{p_{t,i}\,dp_{t,i}\,d\phi_{i}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\,,

(60)

and we adopted the notation
(ij)\equiv p_{i}\cdot p_{j}
. In
Eq. (
3.2.2
), we have subtracted the primary contribution
to the squared amplitude, which is a source of double logarithmic
corrections and has been already accounted for in our resummation.
To parameterise the phase space, we perform the change of variables

\displaystyle v_{i}=p_{t,i}e^{-b|\eta_{i}|}\,,\quad\eta_{i}-\eta_{j}=Rr_{i}\cos\theta_{i}\,,\quad\phi_{i}-\phi_{j}=Rr_{i}\sin\theta_{i}\;\;\mbox{ with }i=1,2

(61)

for which the phase-space factor reads

4(4\pi\alpha_{s})^{2}N_{c}^{2}\frac{[dp_{1}][dp_{2}]}{p_{t,1}^{2}p_{t,2}^{2}}=\left(\frac{\alpha_{s}}{\pi}\right)^{2}N_{c}^{2}\frac{R^{4}}{(4\pi)^{2}}\prod_{i=1,2}\frac{dv_{i}}{v_{i}}dr^{2}_{i}d\theta_{i}\,.

(62)

With these definitions, the clustering condition for a parton
p_{i}
to
be recombined with the jet
p_{j}
is

\displaystyle d_{j,i}^{2}=r_{i}^{2}R^{2}<R^{2}\,.

(63)

Requiring that only one of the two radiated particles clusters then
leads to the following two possible angular constraints

\displaystyle\Theta_{A}

\displaystyle=\Theta(r_{1}-1)\Theta(1-r_{2})\Theta(r_{1}-2r_{2}\cos(\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}))\,,\quad\text{$p_{1}$ out, $p_{2}$ in}

(64a)

\displaystyle\Theta_{B}

\displaystyle=\Theta(1-r_{1})\Theta(r_{2}-1)\Theta(r_{2}-2r_{1}\cos(\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}))\,,\quad\text{$p_{1}$ in, $p_{2}$ out}

(64b)

where the last
\Theta
function enforces that the two particles

p_{1,2}
are not clustered first.
In the strongly ordered limit
v_{1}\gg v_{2}
, non-global logarithms
arise
Dasgupta:2001sh
if the following phase-space integral

\displaystyle~\left(\frac{\alpha_{s}}{\pi}\right)^{2}N_{c}^{2}\frac{R^{4}}{(4\pi)^{2}}\,\int

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{2}dr_{i}^{2}d\theta_{i}\,w_{2}(\{r_{i},\theta_{i}\})

(65)

\displaystyle\times\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dv_{1}}{v_{1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dv_{2}}{v_{2}}\left(\Theta(v-V(p_{1},p_{2}))-\Theta(v-V(p_{1}))\right)\Theta_{A/B}\Theta(v_{1}-v_{2})

develops logarithmic terms
{\cal O}(\ln^{n}(v))
.
The second term in Eq. (
65
) corresponds to the
virtual correction, which in the strongly ordered limit can be
approximated by simply imposing unitarity. Moreover,

\Theta(v_{1}-v_{2})
is the (strong) ordering condition.
Due to the way we parameterise the phase space, we can focus only on the
v_{1,2}
integration to assess the presence of a logarithmically-enhanced term.

It is now straightforward to see that, when
v_{1}\gg v_{2}
, we have

V(p_{1},p_{2})=V(p_{1})\neq 0
, so that no non-global logarithm can arise
from the above integral.
This is a direct consequence of the fact that the observable resolves
the substructure of jets, hence imposing a commensurate constraint
over all emissions regardless of whether they cluster with the beam or
with the jet.
To be explicit, let us consider case
A
, where only
p_{2}
clusters
with the final-state jet. In the case of the LTS in this
process (cf. Section
2.2
), the first emission contributes
with
v_{1}
, whereas the second emission contributes with

\displaystyle p_{t,2}e^{-b|\eta_{j}|}r_{2}^{1+b}=v_{2}r_{2}^{1+b}e^{-b(|\eta_{j}|-|\eta_{2}|)}\equiv v_{2}g(r_{2},\theta_{2})\,.

(66)

For
S_{b}^{(1)}
and
M_{b}^{(1)}
, we thus have
V(p_{1})=v_{1}
and

V(p_{1},p_{2})=\begin{cases}v_{1}\max\left\{1,\frac{v_{2}}{v_{1}}g(r_{2},\theta_{2})\right\}\,\quad&\text{for $M_{b}^{(1)}$}\\
v_{1}\left(1+\frac{v_{2}}{v_{1}}g(r_{2},\theta_{2})\right)\,\quad&\text{for $S_{b}^{(1)}$}\end{cases}\,.

(67)

We therefore find that the integral is given by

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{v}\frac{dv_{1}}{v_{1}}\int_{0}^{v_{1}}\frac{dv_{2}}{v_{2}}\left[\Theta\left(v-V(p_{1},p_{2})\right)-\Theta(v-V(p_{1}))\right]=

\displaystyle\hskip 142.26378pt=\begin{cases}\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\ln^{2}(\max\left\{1,g(r_{2},\theta_{2})\right\})\quad&\mbox{for }M_{b}^{(1)}\\
{\rm Li}_{2}\left(-g(r_{2},\theta_{2})\right)\quad&\mbox{for }S_{b}^{(1)}\end{cases}\,,

(68)

which is free of logarithmic terms.
An analogous conclusion holds for case
B
, in which
p_{1}
clusters
with the jet and
p_{2}
clusters with the beam.
The remaining angular integral is of
{\cal O}(1)
as long as

, which proves the absence of non-global logarithms at
this perturbative order.

Our conclusions readily extend to all perturbative orders (both in

\alpha_{s}
and in the resummation). The mechanism that led to the
cancellation of non-global logarithms in the above example relies on
the recursive nature of the observable that resolves the structure of
radiation inside the jet. As a result, for any number of emissions

\{p_{i}\}
, the observable scales uniformly across different regions
of the solid angle, that is
V(\{p_{i}\})\sim V(p_{j})\sim v
where

p_{j}
is any one of the
\{p_{i}\}
emissions, as long as

R\sim{\cal O}(1)
. This guarantees that all phase-space
configurations that would typically lead to non-global logarithms now
give at most a non-logarithmic contribution to the observable whose
coupling is evaluated at the soft scale
Qe^{-L}
.
In practice, this means that beyond NNLL, the extension of the wide-angle
function
\delta{\cal F}_{\rm wa}
will receive contributions involving the
non-trivial clustering of emissions near the jet boundary, all at
commensurate scales of order
Qe^{-L}
.

4.1
Matching to fixed order

We now discuss the matching of our resummed predictions to a fixed
order, NNLO calculation. We will consider the
pp
case, although an
analogous procedure can be used for the
ee
and
ep

cases. Similarly, the extension to higher orders is straightforward.

To perform the matching, we first need to ensure that at large
v
,
the resummation is turned off. Furthermore, we want to have a handle
on the size of subleading logarithmic corrections in the resummation,
to be included in the estimate of theoretical uncertainties.
We proceed in two steps. Firstly, we split the resummed logarithm as

\ln\frac{1}{v}=\ln\frac{\mu_{\scriptscriptstyle L}}{Qv}+\ln\frac{Q}{\mu_{\scriptscriptstyle L}}=L+\ln\frac{Q}{\mu_{\scriptscriptstyle L}}\,,

(77)

and then we expand the dependence in the resummed prediction on the
second term (
\ln\frac{Q}{\mu_{\scriptscriptstyle L}}
) by truncating the expansion at
NNLL, hence inducing a dependence on
\mu_{\scriptscriptstyle L}
that is strictly
N
3
LL. As a consequence all the ingredients of the resummation will depend on

\mu_{\scriptscriptstyle L}
, and their corresponding expressions are provided in
Appendix
A
.
The scale
\mu_{\scriptscriptstyle L}
is the
resummation scale
, which we will vary
around
Q
to estimate the size of subleading logarithms in the
prediction.
As a second step, we now modify
\mu_{\scriptscriptstyle L}
and
L
as follows
9
9

9

In
the past, this was achieved by modifying the definition of the
logarithm

L\to\tilde{L}=\frac{1}{p}\ln\left(1+\left(\frac{\mu_{\scriptscriptstyle L}}{Qv}\right)^{p}\right)
, see
e.g. Refs.
Banfi:2015pju
;
Banfi:2012jm
;
Bizon:2017rah
;
Bizon:2018foh

what was done in Ref.

.

\displaystyle\mu_{\scriptscriptstyle L}\to\bar{\mu}_{\scriptscriptstyle L}(v)=\begin{cases}\mathfrak{r}(v)\quad&\mbox{ for }v<v_{\scriptscriptstyle M},\\
\mathfrak{m}(v)\quad&\mbox{ for }v_{\scriptscriptstyle M}\leq v<1,\\
\displaystyle Q\quad&\mbox{ for }v\geq 1\,,\end{cases}

(78)

and

~L\to L\,\Theta\left(\frac{\mu_{\scriptscriptstyle M}}{Q}-v\right)\,.

(79)

The functions

and

, where more information is provided.
They guarantee continuity of both the cumulative and differential

The logarithm

To match the NNLO fixed-order and NNLL resummed results, we will use a
multiplicative matching scheme inspired to those introduced in

;
Banfi:2012jm
;
Banfi:2015pju
;

We introduce the notation for the fixed-order expansions of the

{\cal O}(\alpha_{s}^{2})

Born, working at the level of the integrated, cumulative cross section
with any phase space cuts on the Born kinematics
\Phi_{\scriptscriptstyle{\rm B}}
and a veto

v
on the resolution variables studied here.
We denote the fiducial cross section with any phase space cuts on the

\displaystyle\sigma_{\rm fid}=\sum_{i=0}^{2}\sigma_{\rm fid}^{(i)}\,,\quad\Sigma_{\rm fo}(v)=\sum_{i=0}^{2}\Sigma_{\rm fo}^{(i)}(v)

\displaystyle=\sigma_{\rm fid}-\sum_{i=1}^{2}\bar{\Sigma}_{\rm fo}^{(i)}(v)\,,

(80a)

\displaystyle\Sigma_{L\to 0}=\left.\Sigma_{\rm res}(v)\right|_{L\to 0}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{\rm fid}^{(0)}+\sum_{i=1}^{2}\Sigma_{L\to 0}^{(i)}\,,

(80b)

where
\Sigma_{\rm fo}(v)
(

\displaystyle\,+\Sigma^{(2)}_{\rm fo}(v)-\Sigma^{(2)}_{\rm res}(v)+\left(\frac{\Sigma_{L\to 0}^{(1)}}{\sigma_{\rm fid}^{(0)}}-\frac{\Sigma^{(1)}_{\rm res}(v)}{\sigma_{\rm fid}^{(0)}}\right)\left(\Sigma^{(1)}_{\rm fo}(v)-\Sigma^{(1)}_{\rm res}(v)\right)\Bigg].

5
Conclusions

In this work, we have introduced Lund-Tree Shapes (LTS), a new
class of collider observables defined from the Lund-plane
representation of QCD radiation in multi-jet scattering processes.
For processes with two emitting legs, these observables were first
introduced, at NLL, for the purpose of testing the logarithmic
accuracy of parton showers in
ee

Dasgupta:2020fwr
,

pp

vanBeekveld:2022ukn
and

ep

vanBeekveld:2023chs
collisions. In this article, we
presented a generalisation to processes with any number of resolved
jets. We also provide a variant of LTS that can be measured on
groomed jets, offering an additional tool for the study of jet
structure at different collider environments.
These observables provide a versatile tool for probing multi-jet final
states. At the differential level, LTS behave as continuous, global
variables, analogous to classical event shapes and
n\to n+1

jet-resolution parameters, allowing detailed studies of the
hierarchical and geometric structure of QCD final states. At the
cumulative level, they naturally define jet rates, useful for
resolution-based characterisation of multi-jet events or as jet
vetoes.

The generality of LTS makes them applicable to scattering processes
with any number of resolved jets, across
ee
,
pp
and
ep

colliders, as well as within groomed jets. From a theoretical
perspective, they serve as versatile resolution variables for
phase-space slicing in higher-order calculations, for matching
fixed-order predictions to parton showers, and for testing the
logarithmic accuracy of shower algorithms. They also feature good
calculability properties, as they are free of non-global logarithmic
corrections regardless of the number of jets.

As an initial application, we have presented new NNLL predictions for
two-leg processes at
ee
,
pp
and
ep
colliders, and
NNLL+NNLO predictions for the LHC, discussing also the practical
utility of LTS in collider phenomenology. Beyond these examples,
Lund-Tree Shapes offer a promising avenue for systematic studies of
multi-jet processes, enabling both precision QCD tests and novel analyses
of jet events at present and future collider experiments.
The LTS can be easily calculated from the Lund jet plane
declusterings obtained from the public version of the

LundPlane

Dreyer:2018nbf
contrib of

FastJet

Cacciari:2011ma
.
11
11

11

Available at

https://fastjet.fr/contrib/
. The implementation of the plugin for DIS is in preparation
DISLundFastjet
.

\displaystyle=C_{F}\left[\frac{1+(1-z)^{2}}{z}-\epsilon z\right]\,,

\displaystyle\hat{P}_{qg}(z;\epsilon)

\displaystyle=T_{R}\left[1-\frac{2z(1-z)}{1-\epsilon}\right]=T_{R}[1-2z(1-z)-2\epsilon z(1-z)]+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{2})\,,

\displaystyle\hat{P}_{gg}(z;\epsilon)

\displaystyle=\frac{2C_{A}\left(1+z^{2}-z\right)^{2}}{z(1-z)_{+}}+2\pi\beta_{0}\delta(1-z).

(84)

We will denote by
\hat{P}_{ki}^{(0)}(z)
their expression for

\epsilon\to 0
, while
\hat{P}_{ki}^{(\epsilon)}(z)
is the
coefficient of the
{\cal O}(\epsilon)
term, i.e.

\hat{P}_{ki}(z;\epsilon)=\hat{P}_{ki}^{(0)}(z)+\epsilon\hat{P}_{ki}^{(\epsilon)}(z).

(85)

We also introduce the unregularised splitting function

P_{ki}^{(0)}(z)
, which can be obtained from
\hat{P}_{ki}^{(0)}(z)
omitting the terms proportional to
\delta(1-z)
and replacing
(1-z)_{+}\to(1-z)
.
In term of these functions, the coefficient functions can be written
as

\displaystyle C_{ki}^{(1)}(z)=

\displaystyle-\hat{P}_{ki}^{(\epsilon)}(z)+\frac{2b}{1+b}(1-z)P_{ki}^{(0)}(z)\left[\left(\frac{\ln(1-z)}{1-z}\right)_{+}-\frac{\ln(z)}{1-z}\right]

(86)

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+2\left(\ln\frac{Q}{\mu_{\scriptscriptstyle F}}+\frac{1}{1+b}\ln\frac{\mu_{\scriptscriptstyle L}}{Q}\right)P_{ki}^{(0)}(z).

A.2
The Sudakov radiator

The Sudakov radiator
\mathcal{R}
is separated into a soft (s) and hard-collinear (hc) terms, i.e.

\mathcal{R}_{\rm NNLL}(v)=\sum_{\ell=1}^{2}\left[R^{\ell}_{\text{s}}(v)+R^{\ell}_{\text{hc}}(v)\right],

(87)

where we also separate contributions associated with radiation from
each leg
\ell
. At NNLL accuracy, the soft radiator
R_{s}^{\ell}

reads

\displaystyle R_{\rm s}^{\ell}(v)=

\displaystyle-\frac{\lambda}{\alpha_{s}\beta_{0}}g_{1}^{\ell}(\lambda)-g_{2}^{\ell}(\lambda)-\frac{\alpha_{s}}{\pi}g_{3}^{\ell}(\lambda)-\frac{\alpha_{s}}{\pi}\delta g_{3}^{\ell}(\lambda)

(88)

where

\displaystyle g_{1}^{\ell}(\lambda)=\frac{C_{\ell}}{2}

\displaystyle\frac{(1+b-2\lambda)\ln\left(1-\frac{2\lambda}{1+b}\right)-(1-2\lambda)\ln\left(1-2\lambda\right)}{\pi b\beta_{0}\lambda}\,,

(89)

\displaystyle g_{2}^{\ell}(\lambda)=\frac{C_{\ell}}{2}

\displaystyle\Bigg[\frac{K^{(1)}\left(\ln\left(1-2\lambda\right)-(1+b)\ln\left(1-\frac{2\lambda}{1+b}\right)\right)}{2\pi^{2}b\beta_{0}^{2}}

\displaystyle+\frac{\beta_{1}(1+b)\ln^{2}\left(1-\frac{2\lambda}{1+b}\right)}{2\pi b\beta_{0}^{3}}+\frac{\beta_{1}(1+b)\ln\left(1-\frac{2\lambda}{1+b}\right)}{\pi b\beta_{0}^{3}}

\displaystyle-\beta_{1}\frac{\ln\left(1-2\lambda\right)\left(\ln\left(1-2\lambda\right)+2\right)}{2\pi b\beta_{0}^{3}}+\frac{2}{\pi\beta_{0}}\ln\frac{Q}{\mu_{\scriptscriptstyle L}}\ln\left(1-\frac{2\lambda}{1+b}\right)

(90)

\displaystyle+\frac{2}{b\pi\beta_{0}}\ln\frac{\mu_{\scriptscriptstyle R}}{\mu_{\scriptscriptstyle L}}\left[\ln(1-2\lambda)-(1+b)\ln\left(1-\frac{2\lambda}{1+b}\right)\right]\Bigg]\,,

\displaystyle g_{3}^{\ell}(\lambda)=\frac{C_{\ell}}{2}

\displaystyle\Bigg[K^{(1)}\frac{\beta_{1}\left((1+b+2\lambda)\ln\left(1-2\lambda\right)-(1+b)^{2}(1-2\lambda)\ln\left(1-\frac{2\lambda}{1+b}\right)+6b\lambda^{2}\right)}{2\pi b\beta_{0}^{3}(1-2\lambda)(1+b-2\lambda)}

\displaystyle+\frac{\left(\text{$\beta_{1}$}^{2}(1+b)^{2}(1-2\lambda)\ln^{2}\left(1-\frac{2\lambda}{1+b}\right)-4b\lambda^{2}\left(\text{$\beta_{0}$}\text{$\beta_{2}$}+\text{$\beta_{1}$}^{2}\right)\right)}{2b\text{$\beta_{0}$}^{4}(1-2\lambda)(1+b-2\lambda)}

\displaystyle-\frac{\ln\left(1-2\lambda\right)\left(2\text{$\beta_{0}$}\text{$\beta_{2}$}(1-2\lambda)+\text{$\beta_{1}$}^{2}\ln\left(1-2\lambda\right)+4\text{$\beta_{1}$}^{2}\lambda\right)}{2b\text{$\beta_{0}$}^{4}(1-2\lambda)}

\displaystyle+\frac{(1+b)\ln\left(1-\frac{2\lambda}{1+b}\right)\left(\text{$\beta_{0}$}\text{$\beta_{2}$}(1+b-2\lambda)+2\text{$\beta_{1}$}^{2}\lambda\right)}{b\text{$\beta_{0}$}^{4}(1+b-2\lambda)}

\displaystyle-K^{(2)}\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2\pi^{2}(1-2\lambda)(1+b-2\lambda)\beta_{0}^{2}}

\displaystyle-\ln^{2}\frac{\mu_{\scriptscriptstyle R}}{\mu_{\scriptscriptstyle L}}\frac{8\lambda^{2}}{(1-2\lambda)(1+b-2\lambda)}

\displaystyle+\ln\frac{\mu_{\scriptscriptstyle R}}{\mu_{\scriptscriptstyle L}}\frac{2\beta_{1}\left(4b{}\lambda^{2}-(1+b)^{2}(1-2\lambda)\ln\left(1-\frac{2\lambda}{1+b}\right)+(1+b-2\lambda)\ln(1-2\lambda)\right)}{b{}\beta_{0}^{2}(1-2\lambda)(1+b-2\lambda)}

\displaystyle-\ln\frac{\mu_{\scriptscriptstyle R}}{\mu_{\scriptscriptstyle L}}\frac{4K^{(1)}\lambda^{2}}{b{}\beta_{0}^{2}(1-2\lambda)(1+b-2\lambda)}

\displaystyle+4\ln^{2}\frac{Q}{\mu_{\scriptscriptstyle L}}\frac{b{}\,\lambda}{(1+b)(1+b-2\lambda)}-4\ln\frac{Q}{\mu_{\scriptscriptstyle L}}\ln\frac{\mu_{\scriptscriptstyle R}}{\mu_{\scriptscriptstyle L}}\cdot\frac{2\lambda}{1+b-2\lambda}

\displaystyle+4\ln\frac{Q}{\mu_{\scriptscriptstyle L}}\left[\frac{\beta_{1}}{2\beta_{0}^{2}}\frac{2\lambda+(1+b)\cdot\ln\bigl(1-\tfrac{2\lambda}{1+b}\bigr)}{1+b-2\lambda}-\frac{K^{(1)}\,\lambda}{2\pi\beta_{0}\bigl(1+b-2\lambda\bigr)}\right]\Bigg]\,,

(91)

\displaystyle\delta g_{3}^{\ell}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=-C_{\ell}\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}\frac{\lambda}{(1+b-2\lambda)}\,.

(92)

The constants
K^{(i)}
and the QCD beta function coefficients
\beta_{i}
are given in appendix
A.4
.
We also introduce various derivatives of the NLL soft radiator, as they will appear in the evaluation of the transfer function.

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}^{\prime}_{\text{NLL},\ell}(v)\equiv

\displaystyle-\frac{\partial\left[g_{1}(\alpha_{s}\beta_{0}L)L\right]}{\partial L}=\frac{C_{\ell}}{b\pi\beta_{0}}\left(\ln\left(1-\frac{2\lambda}{1+b}\right)-\ln\left(1-2\lambda\right)\right)\,,

(93a)

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}^{\prime}_{\text{NNLL},\ell}(v)\equiv

\displaystyle-\frac{\partial\left[g_{2}(\alpha_{s}\beta_{0}L)\right]}{\partial L}=\frac{C_{\ell}\alpha_{s}}{b\pi^{2}\beta_{0}^{2}(1-2\lambda)(1+b-2\lambda)}\bigg[b\beta_{0}\lambda K^{(1)}-2\pi b\beta_{1}\lambda

\displaystyle-\pi(1+b-2\lambda)\beta_{1}\ln\left(1-2\lambda\right)+\pi(1+b)(1-2\lambda)\beta_{1}\ln\left(1-\frac{2\lambda}{1+b}\right)\bigg]

\displaystyle+\frac{2C_{\ell}\alpha_{s}}{\pi}\left[\ln\frac{Q}{\mu_{\scriptscriptstyle L}}(1-2\lambda)+\ln\frac{\mu_{\scriptscriptstyle R}}{\mu_{\scriptscriptstyle L}}\frac{2\lambda}{(1-2\lambda)(1+b-2\lambda)}\right]\,,

(93b)

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}^{\prime\prime}_{\ell}(v)\equiv

\displaystyle\alpha_{s}\beta_{0}\frac{d\mathcal{R}^{\prime}_{\text{NLL},\ell}}{d\lambda}=2C_{\ell}\frac{\alpha_{s}}{\pi}\frac{1}{(1-2\lambda)(1+b-2\lambda)}\,.

(93c)

Notice that by convention the subscript
s
is dropped.
It can be verified that the limit
b\to 0
is continuous.
We also define the quantities summed over both legs

\mathcal{R}^{\prime}_{\text{NLL}}(v)=\sum_{\ell}\mathcal{R}^{\prime}_{\text{NLL},\ell}(v),\quad\mathcal{R}^{\prime\prime}(v)=\sum_{\ell}\mathcal{R}^{\prime\prime}_{\ell}(v),\quad\mathcal{R}^{\prime}_{\text{NNLL}}(v)=\sum_{\ell}\mathcal{R}^{\prime}_{\text{NNLL},\ell}(v).

(94)

At NNLL accuracy, the hard-collinear radiator reads

\displaystyle R_{\rm hc}(v)=-\sum_{\ell}\left[h_{2}^{\ell}(\lambda)+\frac{\alpha_{s}}{\pi}h_{3}^{\ell}(\lambda)\right]\,,

(95)

with

\displaystyle h_{2}^{\ell}(\lambda)=

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma^{(0)}_{\ell}}{2\pi\beta_{0}}\ln\left(1-\frac{2\lambda}{1+b}\right)\,,

(96)

\displaystyle h_{3}^{\ell}(\lambda)=

\displaystyle\gamma^{(0)}_{\ell}\frac{\beta_{1}\left((1+b)\left(\ln\left(1-\frac{2\lambda}{1+b}\right)\right)+2\lambda\right)}{2\beta^{2}_{0}\left(1+b-2\lambda\right)}-\gamma^{(1)}_{\ell}\frac{\lambda}{2\pi\beta_{0}(1+b-2\lambda)}

\displaystyle+\frac{2\gamma^{(0)}_{\ell}}{1+b-2\lambda}\left[\frac{b}{1+b}\ln\frac{Q}{\mu_{\scriptscriptstyle L}}-\ln\frac{\mu_{\scriptscriptstyle R}}{\mu_{\scriptscriptstyle L}}\right]

(97)

The constants
\gamma_{\ell}^{(i)}
are given in appendix
A.4
.

A.3
The NNLL transfer function

The transfer function
{\cal F}(v)
is decomposed as

{\cal F}(v)={\cal F}_{\rm NLL}(v)+\frac{\alpha_{s}(\mu_{\scriptscriptstyle R})}{\pi}\delta{\cal F}_{\rm NNLL}(v)\,.\\

(98)

The NNLL corrections can be parameterised as follows

\displaystyle\delta\mathcal{F}_{\rm NNLL}(v)=

\displaystyle\delta{\cal F}_{\rm sc}(v)+\delta{\cal F}_{\rm wa}(v)+\delta{\cal F}_{\rm correl}(v)+\delta{\cal F}_{\rm clust}(v)+\delta{\cal F}_{\rm hc}(v)+\delta{\cal F}_{\rm rec}(v).

(99)

At NLL we obtain, for
ee
,
pp
and
ep
,

\mathcal{F}_{\rm NLL}(v)=\begin{cases}1\qquad&\mbox{ for }M_{b}^{(n_{\min})},\\
\displaystyle\frac{e^{-\gamma_{E}\mathcal{R}^{\prime}_{\mathrm{NLL}}(v)}}{\Gamma(1+\mathcal{R}^{\prime}_{\mathrm{NLL}}(v))}\qquad&\mbox{ for }S_{b}^{(n_{\min})}.\end{cases}

(100)

Below we provide all NNLL corrections.

Soft-collinear correction

The soft-collinear correction is
0
for the
M_{b}^{(n_{\min})}
observable. For
S_{b}^{(n_{\min})}
it reads

\displaystyle\delta{\cal F}_{\rm sc}(v)=

\displaystyle-\frac{\pi}{\alpha_{s}}{\cal F}_{\rm NLL}(v)\sum_{\ell=1,2}\left[\mathcal{R}^{\prime}_{{\rm NNLL},\ell}(v)\left(\psi^{(0)}(1+\mathcal{R}^{\prime}_{\mathrm{NLL}}(v))+\gamma_{E}\right)\right.

(101)

\displaystyle\left.\quad+\frac{\mathcal{R}^{\prime\prime}_{\ell}(v)}{2}\left(\left(\psi^{(0)}(1+\mathcal{R}^{\prime}_{\mathrm{NLL}}(v))+\gamma_{E}\right)^{2}-\psi^{(1)}(1+\mathcal{R}^{\prime}_{\mathrm{NLL}}(v))+\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}\right)\right]\,.

where the derivatives of the soft radiator are given in
Eqs.
93
. The term in Eq. (
101
) proportional
to
\mathcal{R}^{\prime\prime}_{\ell}(v)
originates from the expansion of the
all-order functional (common in the resummation structure of additive
global event shapes)

e^{-\frac{\mathcal{R}^{\prime\prime}}{2}\partial^{2}_{\mathcal{R}^{\prime}}}{\cal F}_{\rm NLL}(v)\,,\quad\mathcal{R}^{\prime\prime}(v)=\sum_{\ell=1,2}\mathcal{R}^{\prime\prime}_{\ell},\,\quad\mathcal{R}^{\prime}(v)=\sum_{\ell=1,2}\mathcal{R}^{\prime}_{\ell}\,,

(102)

that is expanded to second order neglecting N
3
LL corrections. In
our numerical implementation we leave the above correction unexpanded
up to N
3
LL corrections, that is we use

e^{-\frac{\mathcal{R}^{\prime\prime}}{2}\partial^{2}_{\mathcal{R}^{\prime}}}{\cal F}_{\rm NLL}(v)=e^{-\frac{\mathcal{R}^{\prime\prime}}{2}\partial^{2}_{\mathcal{R}^{\prime}}\ln{\cal F}_{\rm NLL}(v)}{\cal F}_{\rm NLL}(v)+{\cal O}(\text{N${}^{3}$LL})\,,

(103)

to prevent the resummed distribution from becoming unphysical
(negative) to the left of the Sudakov peak, i.e. at very small values
of
v
, where the
S_{b}^{(n_{\min})}
distribution is dominated by
non-perturbative corrections.

Wide angle correction

The wide-angle correction to the transfer function is zero for the
observables we study in this paper in the
e^{+}e^{-}
and
ep
cases. For

pp
collisions, the wide-angle correction is zero when considering
the observables defined in the partonic c.o.m. frame, while it is
given in Eq. (
53
) for the hadronic c.o.m. frame
definition.
Note that, for
ee
collisions, the wide-angle correction is the main
difference between
M_{0}^{(2)}
and the
y_{23}
Cambridge resolution
scale, which is zero for the former but not for the latter.

Correlated correction

The correlated correction reads

\displaystyle\delta{\cal F}_{\rm correl}(v)={\cal F}_{\rm NLL}(v)\frac{\lambda\pi\mathcal{R}^{\prime\prime}(v)}{\alpha_{s}}(C_{A}\langle\ln f_{\rm correl}\rangle_{C_{A}}+n_{f}T_{R}\langle\ln f_{\rm correl}\rangle_{n_{f}}).

(104)

We evaluate
\langle\ln f_{\rm correl}\rangle_{C_{A},n_{f}}
numerically, and they are defined as

\langle\ln f_{\rm correl}\rangle_{C_{A},n_{f}}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{d\zeta}{\zeta}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{d\phi}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\eta\,\mathcal{M}_{C_{A},n_{f}}\ln\frac{V_{\rm simple}^{\rm corr}(\zeta,\phi,\eta,\beta)}{V_{\rm sc}(\zeta,\phi,\eta,\beta)}

(105)

where the variables
\eta
,
\phi
and
\zeta
are given by

\eta=\eta_{2}-\eta_{1},\quad\phi=\phi_{2}-\phi_{1},\quad\zeta=\frac{k_{t,2}}{k_{t,1}},

(106)

being
k_{t,i}
,
\eta_{i}
and
\phi_{i}
the transverse momentum, rapidity and azimuth of the soft emission labelled with the index
i
.
The matrix elements are given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{n_{f}}=

\displaystyle\frac{\zeta e^{2\eta}\left(-\cos(\phi)\left(\zeta^{2}+2\zeta\cosh(\eta)+1\right)+\zeta^{2}\cosh(\eta)+2\zeta+\cosh(\eta)\right)}{2\left(\zeta+e^{\eta}\right)^{2}\left(\zeta e^{\eta}+1\right)^{2}(\cos(\phi)-\cosh(\eta))^{2}}\,,

(107a)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{C_{A}}=

\displaystyle\frac{-\zeta\left(\left(\zeta^{2}+1\right)\cosh(\eta)\sin^{2}(\phi)+\zeta\cosh^{2}(\eta)\cos(2\phi)-\zeta\right)}{2\left(\zeta^{2}+2\zeta\cosh(\eta)+1\right)^{2}(\cos(\phi)-\cosh(\eta))^{2}}

(107b)

\displaystyle-\frac{\cos(\phi)}{2(\cos(\phi)-\cosh(\eta))}-\frac{\sinh^{2}(\eta)\cos(\phi)}{2\left(\zeta^{2}+2\zeta\cosh(\eta)+1\right)(\cos(\phi)-\cosh(\eta))^{2}}\,.

V_{\rm simple}^{\rm corr}
is the simple observable for a pair of correlated emissions and is given in terms of the transverse momentum and rapidity of the parent gluon
k=k_{1}+k_{2}
. In the limit
|\eta_{1}|\gg 1
, we have

V_{\rm simple}^{\rm corr}=\frac{k_{t,1}e^{-b\eta_{1}}}{Q}\left(\frac{\zeta+e^{\eta}}{\zeta e^{\eta}+1}\right)^{b/2}e^{-\frac{b\eta}{2}}\sqrt{\zeta^{2}+2\zeta\cos(\phi)+1}.

(108)

Instead
V_{\rm sc}
is the value of the observable in the presence of
two soft and collinear emissions and it is decomposed into the sum of
two pieces

V_{\rm sc}(\zeta,\phi,\eta,b)=V_{\rm clust}(\zeta,\phi,\eta,b)\Theta_{\rm clust}+V_{\rm no\,clust}(\zeta,\phi,\eta,b)(1-\Theta_{\rm clust}),

(109)

where the first term refers to the case in which the two partons cluster together, and the second where they do not.
The clustering condition reads

\Theta_{\rm clust}=\begin{cases}\Theta(|\eta|<\ln 2\cos\phi)\Theta(|\phi|<\pi/3)&\quad\mbox{ for DIS and }ee,\\
\Theta(R^{2}-\phi^{2}-\eta^{2})&\quad\mbox{ for }pp,\end{cases}

(110)

where
R
is the jet radius.
When partons 1 and 2 do not cluster, the observable in the soft collinear limit becomes

V_{\rm noclust}(\zeta,\phi,\eta,b)=\frac{k_{t,1}e^{-b\eta_{1}}}{Q}\times\begin{cases}1+\zeta e^{-b\eta}\qquad&\mbox{ for }S_{b}^{(n_{\min})}\,,\\
\max(1,\zeta e^{-b\eta})\qquad&\mbox{ for }M_{b}^{(n_{\min})}\,,\end{cases}

(111)

and this expression is identical for all processes under consideration.
Instead when they cluster

V_{\rm clust}(\zeta,\phi,\eta,b)=\begin{cases}\frac{k_{t,1}e^{-b\eta_{1}}}{Q}\times\sqrt{\zeta^{2}+2\zeta\cos(\phi)+1}\left(\frac{\left(\zeta^{2}e^{2\eta}+2\zeta e^{\eta}+1\right)}{\zeta^{2}+2\zeta\cos(\phi)+1}\right)^{-\frac{b}{2}}\;\;&\mbox{ for DIS and }ee,\\
V_{\rm simple}^{\rm corr}(\zeta,\phi,\eta,b)\quad&\mbox{ for }pp.\end{cases}

(112)

The numerical values of
\langle\ln f_{\rm correl}\rangle{C_{A}}
and
\langle\ln f{\rm correl}\rangle_{n_{f}}
for DIS and
e^{+}e^{-}
collisions, for
b=0
, 1/2, 1, are reported in Tab.

.
For the case of
M_{0}^{(n_{\min})}
, we have verified that our results coincide with those for the Cambridge resolution variable, as presented in Ref.
Banfi:2016zlc
.

In Fig.

\ln(1/R)

Banfi:2012jm

Abreu:2022sdc

Recoil correction

The recoil correction is present only for final-state legs, and is
identical for
S_{b}^{(n_{\min})}
and

ee

\displaystyle\delta\mathcal{F}_{\rm rec}^{ee}(v)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathcal{F}_{\rm NLL}(v)}{1+b-2\lambda}

(116)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle C_{F}\frac{\left(15-4\pi^{2}\right)b-2\left(\pi^{2}-9+9\ln 2\right)}{6}&\mbox{ for quarks,}\\[10.0pt]

0
for

(118)

A.4
Constants

C_{\ell}
for quark and gluon legs reads

C_{q}\equiv C_{F}=\frac{N_{c}^{2}-1}{2N_{c}},\qquad C_{g}\equiv C_{A}=N_{c},

(119)

with
N_{c}=3
. We also consider
n_{f}=5
light quarks.
The coefficients of the QCD
\beta
function read

\displaystyle\beta_{0}=

\displaystyle\frac{11C_{A}-2n_{f}}{12\pi}\,,

(120a)

\displaystyle\beta_{1}=

\displaystyle\frac{17C_{A}^{2}-5C_{A}n_{f}-3C_{F}n_{f}}{24\pi^{2}}\,,

(120b)

\displaystyle\beta_{2}=

\displaystyle\frac{2857C_{A}^{3}+(54C_{F}^{2}-615C_{F}C_{A}-1415C_{A}^{2})n_{f}+(66C_{F}+79C_{A})n_{f}^{2}}{3456\pi^{3}}\,.

(120c)

The coefficients of the physical coupling
scheme
Catani:1990rr
;
Banfi:2018mcq
;
Catani:2019rvy
are given by

\displaystyle K^{(1)}

\displaystyle=C_{A}\left(\frac{67}{18}-\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}\right)-\frac{5}{9}n_{f}\,,

(121a)

\displaystyle K^{(2)}

\displaystyle=C_{A}^{2}\left(\frac{245}{24}-\frac{67}{9}\zeta_{2}+\frac{11}{6}\zeta_{3}+\frac{11}{5}\zeta_{2}^{2}\right)+C_{F}n_{f}\left(-\frac{55}{24}+2\zeta_{3}\right)

(121b)

\displaystyle\hskip 5.69046pt+C_{A}n_{f}\left(-\frac{209}{108}+\frac{10}{9}\zeta_{2}-\frac{7}{3}\zeta_{3}\right)-\frac{1}{27}n_{f}^{2}+\frac{\pi\beta_{0}}{2}\left(C_{A}\left(\frac{808}{27}-28\zeta_{3}\right)-\frac{224}{54}n_{f}\right)\,.

The hard-collinear anomalous dimensions read

\displaystyle\gamma_{q}^{(0)}=

\displaystyle-\frac{3}{2}C_{F}\,,\qquad\gamma_{g}^{(0)}=-2\pi\beta_{0}\,,

Appendix B
A prescription for switching off logarithms in matching

In this section we provide additional details on the matching
prescription introduced in Sec.
4.1
. The dynamical
resummation scale given in Eqs. (
78
), (
79
)
is characterised by three regions: a
resummation
region
(
v<v_{\scriptscriptstyle M}
), a
matching
region (
v_{\scriptscriptstyle M}\leq v<1
), and a

fixed-order
region (
v\geq 1
).
Such a simple prescription guarantees that the dependence of the
prediction on the resummation scale
\mu_{\scriptscriptstyle L}
is confined at small
v
,
where its variation probes higher-order logarithmic corrections,
while the matching scale
\mu_{\scriptscriptstyle M}
determines the scale at which the
matched prediction reduces to its fixed-order component.

The function
\mathfrak{r}(v)
is given by the polynomial

\mathfrak{r}(v)=r_{0}+\sum_{i=0}^{n}r_{i+1}v^{p+i}
, where
p
is a
positive power chosen such that we introduce at most quadratic power
corrections at small
v
(
p\geq 2
). The coefficients are determined
by imposing the following matching conditions

\displaystyle\mathfrak{r}(0)=r_{0}=\mu_{\scriptscriptstyle L}\,,\quad\mathfrak{r}\left(v_{\scriptscriptstyle M}\right)=\mu_{\scriptscriptstyle M}\,,\quad\left.\frac{d^{k}}{dv^{k}}\ln\frac{\mathfrak{r}(v)}{v}\right|_{v=v_{\scriptscriptstyle M}}\!\!\!=0\,\text{ for }k=1,\dots,n\,,

(127)

and solving the corresponding linear system. For
n=2
, we obtain

\displaystyle r_{0}=\mu_{\scriptscriptstyle L}\,,\quad r_{1}=\frac{1}{2}\,(1+p)v_{\scriptscriptstyle M}^{-p}

\displaystyle\left(p\,v_{\scriptscriptstyle M}\,Q-(2+p)\mu_{\scriptscriptstyle L}\right)\,,

(128)

\displaystyle\quad r_{2}=v_{\scriptscriptstyle M}^{-1-p}\left((1-p^{2})v_{\scriptscriptstyle M}\,Q+p(2+p)\mu_{\scriptscriptstyle L}\right)\,,\quad

\displaystyle r_{3}=-\frac{1}{2}\,p\,v_{\scriptscriptstyle M}^{-2-p}\left((1-p)v_{\scriptscriptstyle M}\,Q+(1+p)\mu_{\scriptscriptstyle L}\right)\,.

Similarly, the function
\mathfrak{m}(v)
is given by an analogous
polynomial
\mathfrak{m}(v)=m_{0}+\sum_{i=0}^{2s}m_{i+1}v^{q+i}
(where

q
is a positive parameter, in general different from
p
),
satisfying the constraints

\displaystyle\mathfrak{m}\left(\frac{\mu_{\scriptscriptstyle M}}{Q}\right)=\mu_{\scriptscriptstyle M}\,,\quad\mathfrak{m}\left(1\right)=Q\,,\quad\left.\frac{d^{k}}{dv^{k}}\ln\frac{\mathfrak{m}(v)}{v}\right|_{v=v_{\scriptscriptstyle M}}\!\!\!=\left.\frac{d^{k}}{dv^{k}}\ln\frac{\mathfrak{m}(v)}{v}\right|_{v=1}\!\!\!=0\,\text{ for }k=1,\dots,s\,.

(129)

For
s=1
we obtain

\displaystyle m_{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{Qv_{\scriptscriptstyle M}\left(-\left((v_{\scriptscriptstyle M}+1)v_{\scriptscriptstyle M}^{q}\right)-q(1-v_{\scriptscriptstyle M})\left(v_{\scriptscriptstyle M}^{q}+1\right)+v_{\scriptscriptstyle M}+1\right)}{2v_{\scriptscriptstyle M}\left(1-v_{\scriptscriptstyle M}^{q}\right)-q(1-v_{\scriptscriptstyle M})\left(v_{\scriptscriptstyle M}^{q+1}+1\right)}\,,

(130a)

\displaystyle m_{1}

\displaystyle=-\frac{Qv_{\scriptscriptstyle M}\left(v_{\scriptscriptstyle M}^{-q}+v_{\scriptscriptstyle M}^{q+2}-(q+1)^{2}v_{\scriptscriptstyle M}^{2}+2q(q+2)v_{\scriptscriptstyle M}-(q+1)^{2}\right)}{(1-v_{\scriptscriptstyle M})\left(2v_{\scriptscriptstyle M}\left(1-v_{\scriptscriptstyle M}^{q}\right)-q(1-v_{\scriptscriptstyle M})\left(v_{\scriptscriptstyle M}^{q+1}+1\right)\right)}\,,

(130b)

\displaystyle m_{2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{Qv_{\scriptscriptstyle M}^{-q}\left(-2v_{\scriptscriptstyle M}^{2q+2}+(v_{\scriptscriptstyle M}+1)\left(q(q+1)(1-v_{\scriptscriptstyle M})^{2}+2v_{\scriptscriptstyle M}\right)v_{\scriptscriptstyle M}^{q}-2v_{\scriptscriptstyle M}\right)}{(1-v_{\scriptscriptstyle M})\left(2v_{\scriptscriptstyle M}\left(1-v_{\scriptscriptstyle M}^{q}\right)-q(1-v_{\scriptscriptstyle M})\left(v_{\scriptscriptstyle M}^{q+1}+1\right)\right)}\,,

(130c)

\displaystyle m_{3}

\displaystyle=-\frac{Qv_{\scriptscriptstyle M}^{-q}\left(-\left(\left(q^{2}(1-v_{\scriptscriptstyle M})^{2}+2v_{\scriptscriptstyle M}\right)v_{\scriptscriptstyle M}^{q}\right)+v_{\scriptscriptstyle M}^{2q+1}+v_{\scriptscriptstyle M}\right)}{(1-v_{\scriptscriptstyle M})\left(2v_{\scriptscriptstyle M}\left(1-v_{\scriptscriptstyle M}^{q}\right)-q(1-v_{\scriptscriptstyle M})\left(v_{\scriptscriptstyle M}^{q+1}+1\right)\right)}\,.

(130d)

Ulcerative Rash in Brown Skin
Authors: Joy Muwanes, Lilian Kim, Jesse Kellar, Ashley Garispe
Published: 2026

Case Presentation

A 21-year-old LatinX man with a past medical history of asthma, severe atopic dermatitis, and cold sores presented with a worsening, painful rash on his face and upper body for 3 days. He reported taking Upadacitinib daily for the past 2 years. Notably, he was febrile (39.6 °C) and tachycardic (112 bpm). His exam revealed a diffuse ulcerative rash with honey-crusted scabbing involving the face and upper body (Figure 1, Figure 2). Laboratory studies revealed a neutrophilic predominance (85.3%) without leukocytosis, thrombocytopenia (110 K/mcL), and mild hyponatremia (130 mmol/L) (Table 1).

Photograph of skin lesions (day 3 of symptoms)—diffuse ulcerative rash with honey-crusted scabbing, involving the patient’s face, neck, and upper chest.

Photograph of skin lesions (day 7 of symptoms)—progression of ulcerative lesions with evidence of desquamation across the patient’s face, chest, and arms.

Diagnosis: Eczema Herpeticum With Superimposed Impetigo

Due to the high degree of suspicion of eczema herpeticum (EH), the patient was administered Acyclovir as well as Vancomycin, to treat the superimposed impetigo. This patient’s history and examination were consistent with EH, a serious disseminated cutaneous infection, typically caused by herpes simplex virus type 1 (HSV-1) that primarily affects patients with underlying atopic dermatitis.1 Although the rash occurs in less than 3% of patients with atopic dermatitis on immunosuppressive agents, EH can spread rapidly if left untreated and can result in severe morbidity and mortality of 10% to 50%.1 Given the critical nature of this disease, it is imperative for the treating physician to have a high index of suspicion for EH, so treatment can be initiated early.2 A potential limiting factor for identifying this rash is the lack of published dermatological images depicting EH lesions on patients with darker complexions.3 The images published in this case study intend to help clinicians identify EH in a more diverse patient population.

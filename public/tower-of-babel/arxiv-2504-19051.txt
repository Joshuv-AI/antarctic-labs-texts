Min-CSPs on Complete Instances II: Polylogarithmic Approximation for Min-NAE-3-SAT
Aditya Anand, Euiwoong Lee, Davide Mazzali, Amatya Sharma

Abstract
This paper studies complete k k -Constraint Satisfaction Problems (CSPs), where an n n -variable instance has exactly one nontrivial constraint for each subset of k k variables, i.e., it has ( n k ) \binom{n}{k} constraints. A recent work started a systematic study of complete k k -CSPs [Anand, Lee, Sharma, SODA’25], and showed a quasi-polynomial time algorithm that decides if there is an assignment satisfying all the constraints of any complete Boolean-alphabet k k -CSP, algorithmically separating complete instances from dense instances. The tractability of this decision problem is necessary for any nontrivial (multiplicative) approximation for the minimization version, whose goal is to minimize the number of violated constraints. The same paper raised the question of whether it is possible to obtain nontrivial approximation algorithms for complete Min - k k -CSPs with k ≥ 3 k\geq 3 . In this work, we make progress in this direction and show a quasi-polynomial time polylog ​ ( n ) \text{\rm polylog}(n) -approximation to Min-NAE-3-SAT on complete instances, which asks to minimize the number of 3 3 -clauses where all the three literals equal the same bit. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first known example of a CSP whose decision version is NP-Hard in general (and dense) instances while admitting a polylog ​ ( n ) \text{\rm polylog}(n) -approximation in complete instances. Our algorithm presents a new iterative framework for rounding a solution from the Sherali-Adams hierarchy, where each iteration interleaves the two well-known rounding tools: the conditioning procedure, in order to “almost fix” many variables, and the thresholding procedure, in order to “completely fix” them. Finally, we improve the running time of the decision algorithms of Anand, Lee, and Sharma and show a simple algorithm that decides any complete Boolean-alphabet k k -CSP in polynomial time.

Introduction

Constraint Satisfaction Problems (
CSP
s) provide a unified framework for expressing a wide range of combinatorial problems, including SAT, Graph Coloring, and Integer Programming.
A
CSP
consists of a set
n

m

CSP

A major example of optimization
CSP
s is
Max-CSP
s, where the objective is to find an assignment that maximizes the number of satisfied constraints. This class of problems has been extensively studied and it is known to admit various approximation algorithms, including the (conditionally) optimal approximability of fundamental problems such as

-

-

Max
-
3

[

KKMO07

In many cases, the minimization versions of

-SAT

-SAT
(also known as

2

-approximation

[

]

-

-

Understanding the approximability of Min-CSPs remains a major challenge in computational complexity and approximation algorithms. On the brighter side,
[

proved that the optimal approximation ratio takes one of the values in

Apart from the

-

s often translate into density conditions on the corresponding hypergraph. Two particularly important structured settings are (1)
dense instances
, where the number of constraints (i.e. hyperedges) is
\Omega(n^{k})
, and (2)
complete instances
, where the number of constraints is exactly
\binom{n}{k}
.

Max-CSP

[

,

,

,

]

Building on the study of structured CSPs,

Our Contribution.

We prove two main results in our work: (1) a quasi-polynomial time
O(\log^{6}n)
approximation for

3
-SAT
on complete instances, and, (2) a polynomial time algorithm for the decision version of
k
-
CSP

We first present a quasi-polynomial (i.e.,

) time

-approximation algorithm for
Min-NAE-
3
-SAT
on complete instances.
To the best of our knowledge, it is the first known example of a CSP whose decision version is
NP
-
hard in general instances (and dense instances too, see Claim
B.1
)
while admitting a
\text{\rm polylog}(n)
-approximation in complete instances.

Theorem 1.1
.

There is an algorithm running in time
n^{\log^{\kappa}n}
for some constant
\kappa>0
that gives an
O(\log^{6}n)
-approximation for
Min-NAE-
3
-SAT
on complete instances.
Furthermore, the integrality gap of the degree-
O(\log^{\kappa}n)
Sherali-Adams relaxation is at most
O(\log^{6}n)
.

Beyond addressing this specific question, our result also strengthens one of the main motivations for studying
Min-CSP
s on complete instances: understanding whether a combination of algorithmic techniques (random sampling, convex hierarchies, and regularity lemmas) can improve approximability results. As stated earlier, while each of these techniques independently yields PTASes for
Max-CSP
s on dense/complete instances, their effectiveness for
Min-CSP
s is much less understood. Our algorithm presents a new iterative framework for rounding a solution from the
\text{\rm polylog}(n)
-round Sherali-Adams hierarchy, where each iteration interleaves the two well-known rounding tools: (1) the
conditioning
procedure, which identifies and conditions on a small set of variables in order to almost fix a constant fraction of variables and (2) the
thresholding
procedure, in order to completely fix them. We fix a constant fraction of variables in each iteration (for a total of
O(\log n)
iterations) while ensuring that the value of the LP remains within a
\text{\rm polylog}(n)
factor of the optimal value by the end of all the iterations.

Secondly, we give polynomial time algorithms for the decision version of
k
-
CSP
s (for every constant
k
), improving over the quasi-polynomial time algorithm of
[
ALS25
]
.

Theorem 1.2
.

For every
k\geq 2
, there is a polynomial time algorithm that decides whether a complete instance of
k
-
CSP
is satisfiable or not.

Our algorithm is remarkably simple. It relies on Lemma
3.1
of
[
ALS25
]
, which shows that the number of satisfying assignments for a complete instance of a
k
-
CSP
is at most
O(n^{k-1})
, based on a VC-dimension argument. The algorithm first arbitrarily orders the variables
v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}
, and then proceeds by iteratively maintaining all satisfying assignments for the partial (complete) instance induced on the first
i
variables. Since the number of satisfying assignments for a complete instance with
i
variables is at most
O(i^{k-1})
, we can efficiently keep track and update these solutions in polynomial time, leading to our second main result.

Open Questions.

Our work establishes the first nontrivial approximation result for a

,
Min-NAE-
3
-SAT
on complete instances, whose decision version is

-
hard on general and dense instances.

-

-SAT

However, our techniques do not currently extend to other problems including

This raises the open question regarding both approximation algorithms and hardness results for

3
-SAT

Technical overview

Convex programming hierarchies have proven to be instrumental in the design of approximation algorithms of
Max-CSP
s
[
dlVKM07
,
AKK+08
,
BRS11
,
GS11
,
YZ14
,
AJT19
,
JQST20
,
BBK+21
]
and, limited to a few special cases,
Min-CSP
s
[
CCAL+24
]
. Therefore, it is natural to attempt to approximate
Min-NAE-
3
-SAT
on complete instances using these approaches. We prove
Theorem

1.1
by designing an algorithm that first runs the degree-
d
Sherali-Adams relaxation of
Min-NAE-
3
-SAT
, and then rounds the fractional solution to a Boolean assignment.

Informally, a fractional solution
\mu
to such a relaxation associates each set
S
of at most
d
variables with distributions
\mu_{S}
over assignments
\alpha\in\{0,1\}^{S}
to the variables in
S
. The crucial property is that these distributions are
locally consistent
: for any two variable sets
S,T
of size at most
d
, the local distributions
\mu_{S}
and
\mu_{T}
agree on the probability of each assignment to
S\cap T
. For this reason, such a solution
\mu
is called a
pseudodistribution
.

The objective of the relaxation is to minimize the average over constraints of the probability that they are violated by an assignment sampled from their respective local distribution. In particular, the objective value of an optimal pseudodistribution
\mu^{*}
is at most the fraction of violated constraints in an optimal Boolean assignment.

As solving a degree-
d
Sherali-Adams relaxation amounts to solving a linear program with
n^{O(d)}
variables, we would like to keep
d
as small as possible. In our context, we will set
d=\text{\rm polylog}(n)
.

Having put this notation in place, we now give an overview of how the algorithm from
Theorem

1.1
rounds these fractional solutions. Specifically, in
Section

2.1
we highlight some of the structural properties that our problem instance impose on the pseudodistributions, and in
Section

2.2
we discuss how to exploit these properties to design a rounding algorithm.

2.1
Pseudodistributions and complete NAE-3-SAT

When rounding pseudodistributions obtained from the Sherali-Adams or Sum-of-Squares hierarchies,
conditioning
is perhaps the main hammer at our disposal. This technique, introduced by
[
BRS11
,
GS11
]
and adopted ubiquitously to approximate optimization
CSP
s, is usually implemented as follows: first, we condition on a subset of
r=O(1/\epsilon^{2})
variables to reduce the average correlations among the groups of
t
variables to below
O(2^{t}\epsilon)
; then, we perform independent rounding. This scheme is particularly effective in the context of dense
Max-
k
-CSP
s, where we capitalize upon the fact that optimal assignment satisfies at least an
\Omega(1)
fraction of constraints.
Hence, we can afford to additively lose a total variation distance of
O(2^{k}\epsilon)=O(\epsilon)
between the pseudodistribution and independent rounding in each constraint, and still obtain a
(1-O(\epsilon))
-approximation.

Obstacle: we cannot afford additive error.

In the context of
Min-
k
-CSP
s, the objective function is the fraction of violated constraints. Hence, for a
Min-
k
-CSP
instance
\mathcal{I}
, we define
\mathrm{OPT}
to be the minimum over all the variable assignments
\alpha
of the fraction of constraints in
\mathcal{I}
unsatisfied by
\alpha
. With such an objective, the trick discussed above ceases to work: a highly satisfiable instance has a very small optimal value, possibly even
\mathrm{OPT}\leq O(1/n^{k})
. Due to this eventuality, we cannot afford to additively lose an
\epsilon
term for each constraint: it would result in an objective value of, say,
\epsilon+\mathrm{OPT}\gtrsim n\cdot\mathrm{OPT}
unless
\epsilon\ll 1/n^{k-1}
. Unfortunately, the degree
d
of the pseudodistribution needs to be larger than the number
r=O(1/\epsilon^{2})
of variables we condition on, so setting
\epsilon\ll 1/n^{k-1}
is prohibitive.

Advantage:
\text{\rm polylog}(n)
loss in time and approximation.

On the flip side of the above discussion, one can deduce that if
\mathrm{OPT}\geq 1/\text{\rm polylog}(n)
we can in fact afford to lose an additive
O(\epsilon)
term for
\epsilon=1/\text{\rm polylog}(n)
and still run in
n^{O(r)}=n^{\text{polylog}(n)}
time. Even more so, if
\mathrm{OPT}\geq 1/\text{\rm polylog}(n)
then we always get a
\text{\rm polylog}(n)
-approximation, no matter what solution we output (since the value we obtain is always at most
1
). We can therefore restrain ourselves to consider instances with small optimal value.

For the sake of exposition, here let us ignore the hidden constant in

.

Advantage:
\text{\rm polylog}(n)
-degree pseudodistributions.

Recalling that we allow our algorithm to run in

-degree pseudodistribution at our disposal. Supported by this, the natural thing to do is modifying each level of the recursion as follows: condition on

A standard way to ensure that the joint distribution of

(pairs of) variables is

A formal version of this algorithm is presented and analyzed in

On a high level, our algorithm proceeds in stages. At each stage, if the number of unfixed variables is small (specifically

4.2
Analysis

In each stage, after the thresholding step is completed, we will show that the number of unfixed variables (the variables with
\alpha_{v}=\frac{1}{2}

To show this, we crucially use a lemma of Yoshida and Zhou

Lemma 5.1

\Pr_{\sigma\sim\rho_{\{u,v,w\}}}\left[P_{\{u,v,w\}}(\sigma)=0\right]\leq\gamma_{\mathrm{unsat}}\\
\text{ and }\\
\Pr_{\beta\sim\rho_{\{v,w\}}}\left[P^{v}_{\{u,v,w\}}(\beta_{v})=P^{w}_{\{u,v,w\}}(\beta_{w})\right]\geq\gamma_{\mathrm{fix}}\end{array}\right]\geq\gamma_{\mathrm{rate}}\,.

Intuitively, such a variable is fixable because if we sample a random pair of variables
v,w
and a random assignment to them from their local distribution, the conditional pseudodistribution hence obtained should
(\gamma_{\mathrm{unsat}}/\gamma_{\mathrm{fix}})
-fix the variable
u
, with probability
\gamma_{\mathrm{rate}}\cdot\gamma_{\mathrm{fix}}
. This should be the case because the constraint was almost satisfied to start with, and chances are that
v
and
w
will have the same literal, so we need to rely on the value of
u
to keep the constraint satisfied. We refer the reader to
Table

1
for more intuition on the argument.

Having motivated this notion, we can exploit the fact that our instance is complete (and in particular any sub-instance
\mathcal{I}[W]
is complete) to show that there are in fact
\Omega(n)
fixable variables, as stated by the following lemma. Again, one should think of
\mathrm{val}(\mathcal{I}[W],\rho)=\widetilde{O}(\mathrm{OPT})
.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
d^{\prime}
be a positive integer such that
d^{\prime}\geq 3
, let
W\subseteq V
with
|W|\geq 4
, let
\rho\in\mathcal{D}(W,d^{\prime})
, and let
\xi\in(0,1/4]
such that
\mathrm{val}(\mathcal{I}[W],\rho)\leq\xi
. Then

\left|\left\{u\in W:\,u\text{ is }\left(\rho,2\xi,\frac{1}{12},\frac{1}{24}\right)\text{-fixable}\right\}\right|\geq\frac{|W|}{24}\,.

Proof.

We consider the bipartite graph
G=(L\cup R,E)
with bipartition
L,R
and
L=\binom{W}{2},R=W
, where
E\subseteq\binom{W}{2}\times W
is defined as follows: for each
\{v,w\}\in\binom{W}{2},u\in W
, the edge
(\{v,w\},u)
is in
E
if and only if the following condition holds:

\begin{array}[]{c}|\{u,v,w\}|=3\\
\text{ and }\\
\Pr_{\sigma\sim\rho_{\{u,v,w\}}}\left[P_{\{u,v,w\}}(\sigma)=0\right]\leq 2\cdot\xi\\
\text{ and }\\
\Pr_{\beta\sim\rho_{\{v,w\}}}\left[P^{v}_{\{u,v,w\}}(\beta_{v})=P^{w}_{\{u,v,w\}}(\beta_{w})\right]\geq\frac{1}{12}\,.\end{array}

Then, to prove the lemma statement we use the following claim.

Claim 5.5
.

|E|\geq\binom{|W|}{3}/2
.

Before showing the claim, we check that it allows to prove the lemma. In fact, suppose for the sake of a contradiction that there is an
f
fraction of
(\rho,2\xi,{1}/{12},{1}/{24})
-fixable variables
u
in
W
, with
f<1/24
. Then, the number of edges in
G
would be

\displaystyle|E|

\displaystyle\leq f|W|\cdot\binom{|W|}{2}+\left(1-f\right)|W|\cdot\frac{1}{24}\binom{|W|}{2}

\displaystyle<|W|\cdot\frac{1}{24}\binom{|W|}{2}+|W|\cdot\frac{23}{24^{2}}\binom{|W|}{2}\leq\frac{1}{12}|W|\binom{|W|}{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}\binom{|W|}{3}\,,

contradicting
5.5
.

Proof of
5.5
.

Note that for any
\{u,v,w\}\in\binom{W}{3}
, we can pick any
\{x,y\}\in\binom{\{u,v,w\}}{2}
and accordingly let
z
be the unique variable in
\{u,v,w\}\setminus\{x,y\}
, hence obtaining a valid pair
(\{x,y\},z)
with
|\{x,y,z\}|=3
. Moreover, for at least
\binom{|W|}{3}/2
of the subsets
\{u,v,w\}\in\binom{W}{3}
one has the probability of constraint being violated is
\Pr_{\sigma\sim\rho_{\{u,v,w\}}}[P_{\{u,v,w\}}(\sigma)=0]\leq 2\xi
. Also, for any
\{u,v,w\}\in\binom{W}{3}
with
\Pr_{\sigma\sim\rho_{\{u,v,w\}}}[P_{\{u,v,w\}}(\sigma)=0]\leq 2\xi
, there exists some
\{x,y\}\in\binom{\{u,v,w\}}{2}
such that
\Pr_{\beta\sim\rho_{\{x,y\}}}[P^{x}_{\{u,v,w\}}(\beta_{x})=P^{y}_{\{u,v,w\}}(\beta_{y})]\geq(1-2\xi)/(2^{3}-2)
, which implies
(\{x,y\},z)\in E
for
z
being the unique variable in
\{u,v,w\}\setminus\{x,y\}
. Hence,
|E|\geq\binom{|W|}{3}/2
.
∎

This concludes the proof of the lemma.
∎

We are now at a point where we have convinced ourselves that fixable variables are useful, and that there are many of them. However, all the pairs
v,w
that make a variable fixable may be different than those that make another variable fixable. We then introduce the notion of a
fixing tuple
, that is a collection of few,
t
pairs of variables
v,w
(again, think
t=O(\log\log n)
) so that
\Omega(t)
of them fix a given third variable
u
. Formally, we have the following definition.

Definition 5.6

(Fixing tuple)
.

Let
d^{\prime},t
be positive integers such that
d^{\prime}\geq 3
, let
W\subseteq V
with
|W|\geq 3
, let
\rho\in\mathcal{D}(W,d^{\prime})
, and let
\mathbf{t}\in W^{2t}
be a tuple of
t
pairs
\mathbf{t}=((v_{1},w_{1}),\dots,(v_{t},w_{t}))
over
W
. For any
\gamma_{\mathrm{unsat}},\gamma_{\mathrm{fix}},\gamma_{\mathrm{rate}}^{\prime}\in[0,1]
and any
u\in{W}
, the tuple
\mathbf{t}
is
(\rho,u,\gamma_{\mathrm{unsat}},\gamma_{\mathrm{fix}},\gamma_{\mathrm{rate}}^{\prime})
-fixing if

\Pr_{i\sim[t]}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}|\{u,v_{i},w_{i}\}|=3\\
\text{ and }\\
\Pr_{\sigma\sim\rho_{\{u,v_{i},w_{i}\}}}\left[P_{\{u,v_{i},w_{i}\}}(\sigma)=0\right]\leq\gamma_{\mathrm{unsat}}\\
\text{ and }\\
\Pr_{\beta\sim\rho_{\{v_{i},w_{i}\}}}\left[P^{v_{i}}_{\{u,v_{i},w_{i}\}}(\beta_{v_{i}})=P^{w_{i}}_{\{u,v_{i},w_{i}\}}(\beta_{w_{i}})\right]\geq\gamma_{\mathrm{fix}}\end{array}\right]\geq\gamma_{\mathrm{rate}}^{\prime}\,.

Intuitively, the goal would be to ensure for an
\Omega(1)
fraction of variables
u
, a random collection of
t
pairs contains at least one pair
v,w
that fixes it. We in fact prove a stronger statement, saying that any given variable is fixed by
\Omega(t)
pairs with
1-\mathrm{e}^{-\Omega(t)}
probability (which is
1-1/\text{\rm polylog}(n)
if we plug in
t=O(\log\log n)
).

Lemma 5.7
.

Let
d^{\prime},t
be positive integers such that
d^{\prime}\geq 3
, let
W\subseteq V
with
|W|\geq 3
, let
\rho\in\mathcal{D}(W,d^{\prime})
, let
\gamma_{\mathrm{unsat}},\gamma_{\mathrm{fix}},\gamma_{\mathrm{rate}}\in[0,1]
, and let
u\in W
such that
u
is
(\rho,\gamma_{\mathrm{unsat}},\gamma_{\mathrm{fix}},\gamma_{\mathrm{rate}})
-fixable. Then

\Pr_{\mathbf{t}\sim W^{2t}}\left[\mathbf{t}\text{ is $\left(\rho,u,\gamma_{\mathrm{unsat}},\gamma_{\mathrm{fix}},\frac{\gamma_{\mathrm{rate}}}{2}\right)$-fixing}\right]\geq 1-\mathrm{e}^{-\gamma_{\mathrm{rate}}\cdot t/8}-\frac{t}{|W|}\,.

Proof.

Let us identify each
\mathbf{t}\in W^{2t}
with a tuple of
t
pairs
\mathbf{t}=((v_{1},w_{1}),\dots,(v_{t},w_{t}))
over
W
. If we sample
\mathbf{t}\sim W^{2t}
, then the probability that there exists
i\in[t]
such that
v_{i}=w_{i}
is at most
t/|W|
. Conditioning on this event and taking a union bound, we conclude the proof by bounding

\displaystyle\Pr_{\mathbf{t}\sim\binom{W}{2}^{t}}\left[\mathbf{t}\text{ is not $\left(\rho,u,\gamma_{\mathrm{unsat}},\gamma_{\mathrm{fix}},\frac{\gamma_{\mathrm{rate}}}{2}\right)$-fixing}\right]\leq\mathrm{e}^{-\gamma_{\mathrm{rate}}\cdot t/8}\,,

which follows from the Chernoff bound.
∎

For technical reasons, it is useful to also introduce the notion of a
fixing tuple-assignment pair
. For a given variable
u
, this is essentially a fixing tuple together with an assignment to its variables that witnesses its fixing capabilities with respect to
u
. Specifically, instead of just saying that for
\gamma_{\mathrm{rate}}^{\prime}t
pairs in the collection, the probability that the corresponding literals take the same value in the constraint with
u
is
\gamma_{\mathrm{fix}}
, we write down an assignment to the
t
pairs such that for
\gamma_{\mathrm{rate}}^{\prime\prime}=\gamma_{\mathrm{rate}}^{\prime}\gamma_{\mathrm{fix}}t
pairs in the collection, the corresponding literals take the same value in the constraint with
u
. This is formalized in the following definition.

Definition 5.8

(Fixing tuple-assignment pair)
.

Let
d^{\prime},t
be positive integers such that
d^{\prime}\geq 3
, let
W\subseteq V
with
|W|\geq 3
, let
\rho\in\mathcal{D}(W,d^{\prime})
, let
\mathbf{t}\in W^{2t}
be a tuple of
t
pairs
\mathbf{t}=((v_{1},w_{1}),\dots,(v_{t},w_{t}))
over
W
, and let
\beta\in\{0,1\}^{T}
. For any
\gamma_{\mathrm{unsat}},\gamma_{\mathrm{rate}}^{\prime\prime}\in[0,1]
and any
u\in W
, the tuple-assignment pair
(\mathbf{t},\beta)
is
(\rho,u,\gamma_{\mathrm{unsat}},\gamma_{\mathrm{rate}}^{\prime\prime})
-fixing if

\Pr_{i\sim[t]}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}|\{u,v_{i},w_{i}\}|=k\\
\text{ and }\\
\Pr_{\sigma\sim\rho_{\{u,v_{i},w_{i}\}}}\left[P_{\{u,v_{i},w_{i}\}}(\sigma)=0\right]\leq\gamma_{\mathrm{unsat}}\\
\text{ and }\\
P^{v_{i}}_{\{u,v_{i},w_{i}\}}(\beta_{v_{i}})=P^{w_{i}}_{\{u,v_{i},w_{i}\}}(\beta_{w_{i}})\end{array}\right]\geq\gamma_{\mathrm{rate}}^{\prime\prime}\,.

Given a fixing tuple, we can attach it with an assignment to obtain a fixing tuple-assignment pair by sampling the assignment to each pair
v,w
in the tuple independently. A Chernoff bound would give a success probability of
1-\mathrm{e}^{-\Omega(t)}
(which again is
1-1/\text{\rm polylog}(n)
if
t=O(\log\log n)
). However, we can only afford to sampled one assignment from the pseudodistribution we are given, for reasons mentioned in
Section

2.2
. This is the point where we assume that the probability argument of
Lemma

5.2
holds: this allows us to sample a collective assignment from the local distribution of the collection of variables, as opposed to sampling from the local distribution of each of its pairs independently.

Lemma 5.9
.

Let
d^{\prime},t
be positive integers such that
d^{\prime}\geq 2t+3
, let
W\subseteq V
with
|W|\geq 3
, let
\mathbf{t}\in W^{2t}
such that
|T|=2t
, let
\rho\in\mathcal{D}(W,d^{\prime})
, let
\eta\in[0,1]
such that
\|\rho_{T}-\otimes_{v\in T}\rho_{\{v\}}\|_{1}\leq\eta
, let
\gamma_{\mathrm{unsat}},\gamma_{\mathrm{fix}},\gamma_{\mathrm{rate}}^{\prime}\in[0,1]
, and let
u\in W
such that
\mathbf{t}
is
(\rho,u,\gamma_{\mathrm{unsat}},\gamma_{\mathrm{fix}},\gamma_{\mathrm{rate}}^{\prime})
-fixing. Then

\Pr_{\beta\sim\rho_{T}}\left[(\mathbf{t},\beta)\text{ is $\left(\rho,u,\gamma_{\mathrm{unsat}},\frac{\gamma_{\mathrm{rate}}^{\prime}\cdot\gamma_{\mathrm{fix}}^{2}}{8}\right)$-fixing}\right]\geq 1-\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{\gamma_{\mathrm{fix}}^{2}}{32}\gamma_{\mathrm{rate}}^{\prime}\cdot t}-\eta\,.

Proof.

Let us identify
\mathbf{t}
with a tuple of
t
pairs over
W
as
\mathbf{t}=((v_{1},w_{1}),\dots,(v_{t},w_{t}))
. Since we assume that
\mathbf{t}
is
(\rho,u,\gamma_{\mathrm{unsat}},\gamma_{\mathrm{fix}},{\gamma_{\mathrm{rate}}}^{\prime})
-fixing, we know that there is a set
I\subseteq[t]
with
|I|\geq\gamma_{\mathrm{rate}}^{\prime}\cdot t
such that for every
i\in I
one has
|\{u,v_{i},w_{i}\}|=k
,
\Pr_{\sigma\sim\rho_{\{u,v_{i},w_{i}\}}}[P_{\{u,v_{i},w_{i}\}}(\sigma)=0]\leq\gamma_{\mathrm{unsat}}
, and
\Pr_{\beta\sim\rho_{\{v_{i},w_{i}\}}}[P^{v_{i}}_{\{u,v_{i},w_{i}\}}(\beta_{v_{i}})=P^{w_{i}}_{\{u,v_{i},w_{i}\}}(\beta_{w_{i}})]\geq\gamma_{\mathrm{fix}}
. Hence, for each
i\in I
there exists
b(i)\in\{0,1\}
such that

\Pr_{\beta\sim\rho_{\{v_{i},w_{i}\}}}\left[(P^{v_{i}}_{\{u,v_{i},w_{i}\}}(\beta_{v_{i}}),P^{w_{i}}_{\{u,v_{i},w_{i}\}}(\beta_{w_{i}}))=(b(i),b(i))\right]\geq\frac{\gamma_{\mathrm{fix}}}{2}\,.

Then, observe that for each
i\in I
one has

\Pr_{\beta\sim\rho_{\{v_{i}\}}\otimes\rho_{\{w_{i}\}}}\left[P^{v_{i}}_{\{u,v_{i},w_{i}\}}(\beta_{v_{i}})=P^{w_{i}}_{\{u,v_{i},w_{i}\}}(\beta_{w_{i}})\right]\geq\frac{\gamma_{\mathrm{fix}}^{2}}{4}\,.

Together with the Chernoff bound, this gives

\Pr_{\beta\sim\otimes_{i\in I}\left(\rho_{\{v_{i}\}}\otimes\rho_{\{w_{i}\}}\right)}\left[\left|\left\{i\in I:\,P^{v_{i}}_{\{u,v_{i},w_{i}\}}(\beta_{v_{i}})=P^{w_{i}}_{\{u,v_{i},w_{i}\}}(\beta_{w_{i}})\right\}\right|<\frac{\gamma_{\mathrm{fix}}^{2}}{8}|I|\right]\leq\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{\gamma_{\mathrm{fix}}^{2}}{32}|I|}\,.

Recalling that
|I|\geq\gamma_{\mathrm{rate}}^{\prime}\cdot t
and using
\|\rho_{T}-\otimes_{v\in T}\rho_{\{v\}}\|_{1}\leq\eta
we get the claim.
∎

Now we know that a given a fixing tuple, we can obtain a fixing tuple-assignment pair with good probability. We are now in shape to formalize intuition sketched at the beginning of this section: if a tuple-assignment pair
\mathbf{t},\beta
is fixing for a variable
u
with good probability, then with good probability
u
is almost fixed in the pseudodistribution obtained by conditioning on the variables in the tuple taking the assignment
\beta
.

Lemma 5.10
.

Let
d^{\prime},t
be positive integers such that
d^{\prime}\geq 2t+3
, let
W\subseteq V
with
|W|\geq 3
, let
\mathbf{t}\in W^{2t}
such that
|T|=2t
, let
\rho\in\mathcal{D}(W,d^{\prime})
, let
\gamma_{\mathrm{unsat}},\gamma_{\mathrm{fix}},\gamma_{\mathrm{rate}}^{\prime\prime},\zeta\in[0,1]
, and let
u\in W
such that
\Pr_{\beta\sim\rho_{T}}[(\mathbf{t},\beta)\text{ is $(\rho,u,\gamma_{\mathrm{unsat}},\gamma^{\prime\prime}_{\mathrm{rate}})$-fixing}]\geq 1-\zeta
. Then, for any
\tau>0
one has

\Pr_{\beta\sim\rho_{T}}\left[\min_{b\in\{0,1\}}\,\,\Pr_{\alpha\sim(\rho|_{T\leftarrow\beta})_{\{u\}}}\left[\alpha=b\right]>\tau\cdot\gamma_{\mathrm{unsat}}\right]\leq\frac{1}{(1-\zeta)\tau\gamma^{\prime\prime}_{\mathrm{rate}}}+\zeta\,.

Proof.

Again, let us identify
\mathbf{t}\in W^{2t}
with a tuple of
t
pairs
\mathbf{t}=((v_{1},w_{1}),\dots,(v_{t},w_{t}))
over
W
, and let
I\subseteq[t]
be the set of
i\in[t]
such that
|\{u,v_{i},w_{i}\}|=3
and
\Pr_{\sigma\sim\rho_{\{u,v_{i},w_{i}\}}}[P_{\{u,v_{i},w_{i}\}}(\sigma)=0]\leq\gamma_{\mathrm{unsat}}
. Now, on the one hand we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in I}\Pr_{\sigma\sim\rho_{\{u,v_{i},w_{i}\}}}\left[P_{\{u,v_{i},w_{i}\}}(\sigma)=0\right]\leq\gamma_{\mathrm{unsat}}\cdot t\,,

(2)

and also

\displaystyle\ex_{\beta\sim\rho_{T}}\left[\Pr_{\sigma\sim(\rho|_{T\leftarrow\beta})_{\{u,v_{i},w_{i}\}}}\left[P_{\{u,v_{i},w_{i}\}}(\sigma)=0\right]\right]=\Pr_{\sigma\sim\rho_{\{u,v_{i},w_{i}\}}}\left[P_{\{u,v_{i},w_{i}\}}(\sigma)=0\right]\,.

(3)

On the other hand, for any
\beta\in\mathrm{supp}(\rho_{T})
and any
i\in[t]
such that
P^{v_{i}}_{\{u,v_{i},w_{i}\}}(\beta_{v_{i}})=P^{w_{i}}_{\{u,v_{i},w_{i}\}}(\beta_{w_{i}})
, we have that there is
\widehat{b}(\beta,i)\in\{0,1\}
satisfying

\left(P^{u}_{\{u,v_{i},w_{i}\}}(\widehat{b}(\beta,i)),\,P^{v_{i}}_{\{u,v_{i},w_{i}\}}(\beta_{v_{i}}),\,P^{w_{i}}_{\{u,v_{i},w_{i}\}}(\beta_{w_{i}})\right)=(c,c,c)\quad\text{for some $c\in\{0,1\}$.}

This implies that for any
\beta\in\mathrm{supp}(\rho_{T})
and any
i\in[t]
such that
P^{v_{i}}_{\{u,v_{i},w_{i}\}}(\beta_{v_{i}})=P^{w_{i}}_{\{u,v_{i},w_{i}\}}(\beta_{w_{i}})
, we have

\displaystyle\min_{b\in\{0,1\}}\,\,\Pr_{\alpha\sim(\rho|_{T\leftarrow\beta})_{\{u\}}}\left[\alpha=b\right]

\displaystyle\leq\Pr_{\alpha\sim(\rho|_{T\leftarrow\beta})_{\{u\}}}\left[\alpha=\widehat{b}(\beta,i)\right]

\displaystyle=\Pr_{\sigma\sim(\rho|_{T\leftarrow\beta})_{\{u,v_{i},w_{i}\}}}\left[\sigma_{u}=\widehat{b}(\beta,i)\right]

\displaystyle\leq\Pr_{\sigma\sim(\rho|_{T\leftarrow\beta})_{\{u,v_{i},w_{i}\}}}\left[P_{\{u,v_{i},w_{i}\}}(\sigma)=0\right]\,.

In particular, if we assume that
(\mathbf{t},\beta)
is
(\rho,u,\gamma_{\mathrm{unsat}},\gamma^{\prime\prime}_{\mathrm{rate}})
-fixing, we know that there are at least
\gamma^{\prime\prime}_{\mathrm{rate}}\cdot t
indices
i\in[t]
for which the above bound holds, since there are at least
\gamma^{\prime\prime}_{\mathrm{rate}}\cdot t
indices
i\in[t]
such that
P^{v_{i}}_{\{u,v_{i},w_{i}\}}(\beta_{v_{i}})=P^{w_{i}}_{\{u,v_{i},w_{i}\}}(\beta_{w_{i}})
. Hence, conditioning on
(\mathbf{t},\beta)
being
(\rho,u,\gamma_{\mathrm{unsat}},\gamma^{\prime\prime}_{\mathrm{rate}})
-fixing and combining with observations (
2
) and (
3
) we get

(1-\zeta)\gamma^{\prime\prime}_{\mathrm{rate}}t\cdot\ex_{\beta\sim\rho_{T}}\left[\min_{b\in\{0,1\}}\,\,\Pr_{\alpha\sim(\rho|_{T\leftarrow\beta})_{\{u\}}}\left[\alpha=b\right]\,\middle|\,\text{$(\mathbf{t},\beta)$ is $(\rho,u,\gamma_{\mathrm{unsat}},\gamma^{\prime\prime}_{\mathrm{rate}})$-fixing}\right]\leq\gamma_{\mathrm{unsat}}t\,.

The claim then follows by conditioning and Markov’s inequality with a union bound.
∎

We can finally show the main lemma of the section.

Proof of
Lemma

4.1
.

Hereafter we fix
r^{\prime}\in\{0,1,\dots,r\}
such that

\Pr_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{s}\sim W^{r^{\prime}},\,\sigma\sim\rho_{S},\\
\mathbf{t}\sim W^{2t}\end{subarray}}\left[\left\|(\rho|_{S\leftarrow\sigma})_{T}-\otimes_{v\in T}(\rho|_{S\leftarrow\sigma})_{\{v\}}\right\|_{1}>2\cdot\frac{3^{2t/3}}{r^{1/3}}\right]\leq\frac{3^{2t/3}}{r^{1/3}}\,,

which is guaranteed to exist by
Lemma

5.2
. For convenience, we set

\tau^{\prime}=\sqrt{\tau}/2\,,\quad\xi=\tau^{\prime}\cdot\mathrm{val}(\mathcal{I}[W],\mu)\,,\quad\gamma_{\mathrm{unsat}}=2\xi\,,\quad\gamma_{\mathrm{fix}}=\frac{1}{12}\,,\quad\gamma_{\mathrm{rate}}=\frac{1}{24}\,,

and

\eta=2\cdot\frac{3^{2t/3}}{r^{1/3}}\,,\quad\chi=\mathrm{e}^{-\gamma_{\mathrm{rate}}\cdot t/8}+\frac{t}{|W|}\,,\quad\zeta=\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{\gamma_{\mathrm{fix}}^{2}}{64}\gamma_{\mathrm{rate}}\cdot t}+\eta\,.

For any
\mathbf{s}\in W^{r^{\prime}},\,\sigma\in\mathrm{supp}(\rho_{S})
, we define

\rho(S,\sigma)=\mu|_{S\leftarrow\sigma}\,,

and also define the set of fixable variables

X(S,\sigma)=\left\{u\in W:\,u\text{ is $\left(\rho(S,\sigma),\gamma_{\mathrm{unsat}},\gamma_{\mathrm{fix}},\gamma_{\mathrm{rate}}\right)$-fixable}\right\}\,.

For any
\mathbf{s}\in W^{r^{\prime}},\,\sigma\in\mathrm{supp}(\rho_{S}),\,\mathbf{t}\in W^{2t}
we define the of variables for which
\mathbf{t}
is fixing

X^{*}(S,\sigma,\mathbf{t})=\left\{u\in X(S,\sigma):\,\mathbf{t}\text{ is $\left(\rho(S,\sigma),u,\gamma_{\mathrm{unsat}},\gamma_{\mathrm{fix}},\frac{\gamma_{\mathrm{rate}}}{2}\right)$-fixing}\right\}\,.

With this notation in place we can easily prove the following claim, which establishes sufficient conditions for the random choice of
\mathbf{s},\sigma,\mathbf{t},\beta
to get a large set
F_{W}(\rho(S,\sigma)|_{T\leftarrow\beta},\,\tau^{\prime}\gamma_{\mathrm{unsat}})
.

Claim 5.11
.

Let
\mathbf{s}\in W^{r^{\prime}},\,\sigma\in\mathrm{supp}(\rho_{S})
such that

\mathrm{val}(\mathcal{I}[W],\rho(S,\sigma))\leq\tau^{\prime}\cdot\mathrm{val}(\mathcal{I}[W],\mu)\,.

Let
\mathbf{t}\in W^{2t}
such that

|X^{*}(S,\sigma,\mathbf{t})|\geq\frac{1}{2}|X(S,\sigma)|\,,\quad|T|=2t\,,\quad\left\|(\mu|_{S\leftarrow\sigma})_{T}-\otimes_{v\in T}(\mu|_{S\leftarrow\sigma})_{\{v\}}\right\|_{1}\leq\eta\,.

Let
\beta\in\mathrm{supp}(\rho(S,\sigma)_{T})
such that

\left|\left\{u\in X^{*}(S,\sigma,\mathbf{t}):\,\min_{b\in\{0,1\}}\,\,\Pr_{\alpha\sim(\rho(S,\sigma)|_{T\leftarrow\beta})_{\{u\}}}\left[\alpha=b\right]>\tau^{\prime}\cdot\gamma_{\mathrm{unsat}}\right\}\right|\leq\frac{1}{2}\cdot\left|X^{*}(S,\sigma,\mathbf{t})\right|\,.

Then,

\displaystyle\left|F_{W}(\rho(S,\sigma)|_{T\leftarrow\beta},\,\tau^{\prime}\cdot\gamma_{\mathrm{unsat}})\right|\geq\frac{1}{96}\left|W\right|\,.

Proof.

Together with our assumption that
\mathrm{val}(\mathcal{I}[W],\mu)\leq 1/(3\tau)
, the requirement on
\mathbf{s},\sigma
implies

\mathrm{val}(\mathcal{I}[W],\rho(S,\sigma))\leq\xi\leq\frac{1}{4}\,.

Thus, we can employ
Lemma

5.4
with
\rho=\rho(S,\sigma)
, which gives

|X(S,\sigma)|\geq\frac{|W|}{24}\,.

Combining this with the requirement on
\mathbf{t},\beta
we get that

\displaystyle\left|F_{W}(\rho(S,\sigma)|_{T\leftarrow\beta},\,\tau^{\prime}\cdot\gamma_{\mathrm{unsat}})\right|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\left|X^{*}(S,\sigma,\mathbf{t})\right|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{4}\left|X(S,\sigma)\right|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{96}\left|W\right|\,.

∎

To conclude the lemma statement, all is left to do is showing that the above conditions on the choice of
\mathbf{s},\sigma,\mathbf{t},\beta
in
5.11
hold with high probability. First, by Markov’s inequality we have

\Pr_{\mathbf{s}\sim W^{r^{\prime}},\,\sigma\sim\rho_{S}}\left[\mathrm{val}(\mathcal{I}[W],\rho(S,\sigma))>\tau^{\prime}\cdot\mathrm{val}(\mathcal{I}[W],\mu)\right]\leq\frac{1}{\tau^{\prime}}\,.

By our choice of
r^{\prime}
and definition of
\eta
we also have

\Pr_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{s}\sim W^{r^{\prime}},\,\sigma\sim\rho_{S},\\
\mathbf{t}\sim W^{2t}\end{subarray}}\left[\left\|(\rho|_{S\leftarrow\sigma})_{T}-\otimes_{v\in T}(\rho|_{S\leftarrow\sigma})_{\{v\}}\right\|_{1}>\eta\right]\leq\frac{1}{2}\eta\,.

By an elementary bound, we also get

\Pr_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{t}\sim W^{2t}\end{subarray}}\left[|T|<2t\right]\leq\frac{4t^{2}}{|W|}\,.

Using
Lemma

5.7
with
d^{\prime}=d-r^{\prime}\geq 3,\,\rho=\rho(S,\sigma)\in\mathcal{D}(W,d-r^{\prime})

\Pr_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{s}\sim W^{r^{\prime}},\,\sigma\sim\rho_{S},\\

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\tau^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{2}\eta+\frac{4t^{2}}{|W|}+2\chi+2\cdot\left(\frac{1}{(1-\zeta)\tau^{\prime}\cdot\left(\gamma^{\prime}_{\mathrm{rate}}\gamma_{\mathrm{fix}}^{2}/8\right)}+\zeta\right)

6
Proof of the Thresholding Lemma

In this section, we prove
Lemma

4.2
, restated below for convenience. The goal is to analyze the change in the LP value from the thresholding procedure of line
23
in
Algorithm

2
.

See
4.2

We seek to bound the increase in the aggregate value after rounding the
\theta
-fixed variables for
\theta\sim[\tau\delta,2\tau\delta]
. The analysis considers each constraint individually. Each constraint, depending on which of the four classes
\mathcal{P}_{0}\{V_{\mathrm{U}}\},\mathcal{P}_{1}\{V_{\mathrm{U}}\},\mathcal{P}_{2}\{V_{\mathrm{U}}\},\mathcal{P}_{3}\{V_{\mathrm{U}}\}
it belongs to, contributes to one of
\mathrm{LP}_{0}^{V_{\mathrm{U}}}(\rho)
,
\mathrm{LP}_{1}^{V_{\mathrm{U}}}(\rho)
,
\mathrm{LP}_{2}^{V_{\mathrm{U}}}(\rho)
,
\mathrm{LP}_{3}^{V_{\mathrm{U}}}(\rho)
and hence has a different contribution to

(because the latter is a weighted sum). As we fix some variables to their ruling values, the constraints might change class, going from
\mathcal{P}_{j}\{V_{\mathrm{U}}\}
to
\mathcal{P}_{i}\{V_{\mathrm{U}}^{\prime}\}
for some

, where

is the updated set of unfixed variables after rounding. As a consequence, the contribution of the constraint to
A^{V_{\mathrm{U}}^{\prime}}(\rho^{\prime})
might also be different. In the following proof, we show that for each of the four types of constraints, the new contribution is not much more than the starting one.

Proof.

Recall that the aggregate value is defined as

A^{V_{\mathrm{U}}}(\rho)=\tau\cdot\log^{3}n\cdot\mathrm{LP}^{V_{\mathrm{U}}}_{3}(\rho)+\log^{2}n\cdot\mathrm{LP}^{V_{\mathrm{U}}}_{2}(\rho)+\log n\cdot\mathrm{LP}^{V_{\mathrm{U}}}_{1}(\rho)+\mathrm{LP}^{V_{\mathrm{U}}}_{0}(\rho)\,.

We will refer to a constraint
P_{S}\in\mathcal{P}_{i}\{V_{\mathrm{U}}\}
as an
i
-constraint, for each
i\in\{0,1,2,3\}
. Moreover, we adopt some short-hand notation for convenience, so for any
\theta\in[0,1/2)
we call:

•

the set of
(\rho,\theta)
-fixed variables
F^{\theta}=F_{V_{\mathrm{U}}}(\rho,\theta)
and their ruling assignment
\beta^{\theta}=\omega^{*}_{V_{\mathrm{U}}}(\rho,\theta)
;

•

the updated set of unfixed variables
V_{\mathrm{U}}^{\theta}=V_{\mathrm{U}}\setminus F^{\theta}
;

•

the pseudodistribution after fixing the
(\rho,\theta)
-fixed variables
\rho^{\theta}=\rho^{F^{\theta}\leftarrow\beta^{\theta}}
;

•

the LP values
\mathrm{LP}_{i}=\mathrm{LP}^{V_{\mathrm{U}}}_{i}(\rho)
and
\mathrm{LP}^{\theta}_{i}=\mathrm{LP}^{V_{\mathrm{U}}^{\theta}}_{i}(\rho^{\theta})
, for each
i\in\{0,1,2,3\}
;

•

the aggregate values
A(\rho)=A_{\tau}^{V_{\mathrm{U}}}(\rho)
and
A^{\theta}(\rho^{\theta})=A_{\tau}^{V_{\mathrm{U}}^{\theta}}(\rho^{\theta})
;

•

a constraint
P\in\mathcal{P}_{i}\{V_{U}\}
an
i
-constraint.

The idea is to analyze the contribution to the
\mathrm{LP}^{\theta}_{i}
from all the
j
-constraints. Such a constraint contributes to
\mathrm{LP}^{\theta}_{i}
if and only if exactly
j-i
variables are rounded, so that after rounding it moves from
\mathcal{P}_{j}\{V_{\mathrm{U}}\}
to
\mathcal{P}_{i}\{V_{\mathrm{U}}^{\theta}\}
. To do this, let us define
\Delta^{\theta}_{ji}
as the contribution to
\mathrm{LP}_{i}^{\theta}
from the
j
-constraints that end up in
\mathcal{P}_{i}\{V_{\mathrm{U}}^{\theta}\}
, for all
i\leq j
. More precisely, we let

\Delta^{\theta}_{j,i}=\sum_{P_{S}\in\mathcal{P}_{i}\{V_{\mathrm{U}}^{\theta}\}\cap\mathcal{P}_{j}\{V_{\mathrm{U}}\}}\Pr_{\alpha\sim\rho^{\theta}}[P_{S}(\alpha)=0]\,,

for all
i,j\in\{0,1,2,3\}
with
i\leq j
. Then, for all
i\in\{0,1,2,3\}
, we have

\mathrm{LP}_{i}^{\theta}=\sum_{j=i}^{3}\Delta_{j,i}\,.

In what follows, whenever
\theta
is clear from context, we allow ourselves to simply the notation further as follows: for
F^{\theta}
,
\beta^{\theta}
and
\Delta^{\theta}_{ji}
we drop the superscript, while for
V_{\mathrm{U}}^{\theta},\rho^{\theta},\mathrm{LP}^{\theta}_{i},A^{\theta}(\rho^{\theta})
we replace the superscript with a dash as
V_{\mathrm{U}}^{\prime},\rho^{\prime},\mathrm{LP}^{\prime},A^{\prime}(\rho^{\prime})
.

Now, we start to individually bound the values of
\Delta^{\theta}_{j,i}
. Note that a trivial upper bound is
\Delta^{\theta}_{i,i}\leq\mathrm{LP}_{i}
, for all
i\in\{0,1,2,3\}
.

Claim 6.1
.

Let
\theta\in[0,1/5]
. Then, one has
\Delta^{\theta}_{1,0}\leq 2\cdot\mathrm{LP}_{1}
.

Proof.

First note that any contribution to
\Delta_{1,0}
can only come from the constraints
P_{S}
in
\mathcal{P}_{1}\{V_{\mathrm{U}}\}
each with exactly one variable in
V_{\mathrm{U}}
which gets rounded (which causes
P_{S}
to move to
\mathcal{P}_{0}\{V_{\mathrm{U}}^{\prime}\}
).

Consider any such
1
-constraint
P_{S}
, where
v\in S
is the only variable in
V_{\mathrm{U}}
. It must then be the case that
v
is
(\rho,\theta)
-fixed, i.e., either
\rho_{\{v\}}(0)\leq\theta
or
\rho_{\{v\}}(1)\leq\theta
. Since
S\setminus\{v\}\subseteq V\setminus V_{\mathrm{U}}
, this implies that either

\Pr_{\alpha\sim\rho_{S}}[P_{S}(\alpha)=0]\leq\theta\quad\text{ or }\quad\Pr_{\alpha\sim\rho_{S}}[P_{S}(\alpha)=1]\leq\theta\,.

The former case is trivial as it means that the ruling value of
v
together with the already determined assignment to of
S\setminus\{v\}
leads to an assignment that satisfies
P_{S}
, and thus contributes
0
to
\mathrm{LP}^{\prime}_{0}
.
For the other case, it implies that the contribution of
P_{S}
to
\mathrm{LP}_{1}
is
\Pr_{\alpha\sim\rho_{S}}[P_{S}(\alpha)=0]\geq 1-\theta
. Note that the contribution of
P_{S}
to
\mathrm{LP}^{\prime}_{0}
is always upper bounded
1
, which is at most
\frac{1}{1-\theta}\Pr_{\alpha\sim\rho_{S}}[P_{S}(\alpha)=0]
. Thus, the total contribution of all the
1
-constraints to
\mathrm{LP}^{\prime}_{0}
is

\Delta_{1,0}\leq\frac{1}{1-\theta}\mathrm{LP}_{1}\leq 2\cdot\mathrm{LP}_{1}\,.

∎

Claim 6.2
.

Let
\theta\in[0,1/5]
. Then, one has
\Delta^{\theta}_{2,0}\leq 2\cdot\mathrm{LP}_{1}
.

Proof.

Similar to
6.1
, for the analysis of
\Delta_{2,0}
we individually consider the contribution from every constraint in
\mathcal{P}_{2}\{V_{\mathrm{U}}\}
that ends up in
\mathcal{P}_{0}\{V_{\mathrm{U}}^{\prime}\}
, i.e., the
2
-constraints where both unfixed variables get rounded.

Consider a
2
-constraint
P_{S}=P_{\{x,y,z\}}
, with
x,y\in V_{\mathrm{U}}\cap V_{\mathrm{U}}^{\prime}
and
z\notin V_{\mathrm{U}}
. Let us call
b_{x},b_{y}\in\{0,1\}
the ruling values of
x
and
y
respectively, and also let
b_{z}\in\{0,1\}
be such that
\rho_{\{z\}}(b_{z})=1
. In the case when
P_{S}^{x}(b_{x})\neq P_{S}^{z}(b_{z})
or
P_{S}^{y}(b_{y})\neq P_{S}^{z}(b_{z})
we obtain an assignment that satisfies
P_{S}
, so the constraint does not contribute anything to
\mathrm{LP}^{\prime}_{0}
. Hence, let us focus on the case when
P_{S}^{x}(b_{x})=P_{S}^{y}(b_{y})=P_{S}^{z}(b_{z})
and hence
P_{S}
contributes
1
to
\mathrm{LP}^{\prime}_{0}
. We know that both
x
and
y
are
(\rho,\theta)
-fixed and therefore

\Pr_{\alpha\sim\rho_{\{x\}}}[\alpha=1-b_{x}]\leq\theta\quad\text{ and }\quad\Pr_{\alpha\sim\rho_{\{y\}}}[\alpha=1-b_{y}]\leq\theta\,.

Using the union bound, this implies that the contribution of
P_{S}
to
\mathrm{LP}_{2}
can be lower-bounded as

\displaystyle\Pr_{\alpha\sim\rho_{S}}[P_{S}(\alpha)=0]=

\displaystyle 1-\Pr_{\alpha\sim\rho_{\{x,y\}}}[\alpha_{x}=1-b_{x}\,\mbox{ or }\,\alpha_{y}=1-b_{y}]

\displaystyle\geq 1-\Pr_{\alpha\sim\rho_{\{x\}}}[\alpha=1-b_{x}]-\Pr_{\alpha\sim\rho_{\{y\}}}[\alpha=1-b_{y}]

\displaystyle\geq 1-2\theta

Thus, the total contribution from all such constraints is upper-bounded by

\Delta_{2,0}\leq\frac{1}{1-2\theta}\mathrm{LP}_{2}\leq 2\cdot\mathrm{LP}_{2}\,.

∎

Claim 6.3
.

Let
\theta\in[0,2\tau\delta]
. Then, one has
\Delta^{\theta}_{3,i}\leq\mathrm{LP}_{3}+6\tau\delta\binom{|V_{\mathrm{U}}|}{3}
for all

Proof.

The idea here is that if we round a
(\rho,\theta)
-fixed variable then the contribution of its clauses after rounding it is increased by at most an additive term of
\theta
.

More formally, consider a constraint
P_{S}\in\mathcal{P}_{3}\{V_{\mathrm{U}}\}\cap\mathcal{P}_{i}\{V_{\mathrm{U}}^{\prime}\}
, which contributes
\Pr_{\alpha\sim\rho_{S}}[P_{S}(\alpha)=0]
to
\mathrm{LP}_{3}
. Let
\widehat{S}=S\cap F
be the
(\rho,\theta)
-fixed variables of
S
that we round to their ruling assignment
\widehat{\beta}=\beta_{\widehat{S}}

|\widehat{S}|=3-i
with
i\in\{0,1,2\}
. Also, for any
\alpha\in\{0,1\}^{S\setminus\widehat{S}}
, let us denote by
\alpha\oplus\widehat{\beta}\in\{0,1\}^{S}
the vector assigning
\widehat{\beta}
to
\widehat{S}
and
\alpha
to
S\setminus\widehat{S}
. With this notation, we want to upper-bounded the contribution of
P_{S}
to
\mathrm{LP}^{\prime}_{i}

\Pr_{\alpha\sim\rho^{\prime}_{S}}[P_{S}(\alpha)=0]=\Pr_{\alpha\sim\rho^{\prime}_{S\setminus\widehat{S}}}[P_{S}(\alpha\oplus\widehat{\beta})=0]=\Pr_{\alpha\sim\rho_{S}}[P_{S}(\alpha_{S\setminus\widehat{S}}\oplus\widehat{\beta})=0]\,,

since the distribution
\rho^{\prime}
obtained after fixing preserves the marginals of the unfixed variables. Now, with basic calculations we get

\displaystyle\Pr_{\alpha\sim\rho_{S}}[P_{S}(\alpha_{S\setminus\widehat{S}}\oplus\widehat{\beta})=0]

\displaystyle=\Pr_{\alpha\sim\rho_{S}}[P_{S}(\alpha_{S\setminus\widehat{S}}\oplus\widehat{\beta})=0,\,\alpha_{\widehat{S}}=\widehat{\beta}]+\Pr_{\alpha\sim\rho_{S}}[P_{S}(\alpha_{S\setminus\widehat{S}}\oplus\widehat{\beta})=0,\,\alpha_{\widehat{S}}\neq\widehat{\beta}]

\displaystyle\leq\Pr_{\alpha\sim\rho_{S}}[P_{S}(\alpha)=0]+\Pr_{\alpha\sim\rho_{S}}[\alpha_{\widehat{S}}\neq\widehat{\beta}]

\displaystyle\leq\Pr_{\alpha\sim\rho_{S}}[P_{S}(\alpha)=0]+\sum_{v\in\widehat{S}}\Pr_{\alpha\sim\rho_{\{v\}}}[\alpha_{v}\neq\widehat{\beta}_{v}]\,.

Combining the above bounds and recalling that each

(\rho,\theta)
-fixed and that

\Pr_{\alpha\sim\rho^{\prime}_{S}}[P_{S}(\alpha)=0]\leq\Pr_{\alpha\sim\rho_{S}}[P_{S}(\alpha)=0]+3\theta\,.

Thus, we have that each

\mathcal{P}_{3}\{V_{\mathrm{U}}\}
, where

\mathrm{LP}_{3}

\Delta_{3,i}\leq\mathrm{LP}_{3}+3\theta\cdot\binom{|V_{\mathrm{U}}|}{3}\,.

∎

Claim 6.4

Note that the only part of the proof of the

Claim 6.5

(Derandomizing thresholding)
.

Let
S=\{\tau\delta,2\tau\delta\}\cup(\{\delta_{i}\}_{i\in[|V_{\mathrm{U}}|]}\cap[\tau\delta,2\tau\delta])

Without loss of generality, assume that the variables as sorted in increasing order of biases, i.e.,

•

\mathrm{LP}^{\prime}_{3}\leq\mathrm{LP}_{3}

-SAT

.

For any small enough constant

3
-SAT
to a dense instance of

3
-SAT

Given a general instance of
Min-NAE-
3
-SAT
with variable set
V_{0}
with

and constraints
\mathcal{C}

|V_{d}|=O(n_{0}/\varepsilon)

-SAT

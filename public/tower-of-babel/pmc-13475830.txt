Surgical repair in coexisting transposition of the great arteries with infracardiac obstructed total anomalous pulmonary venous connection
Authors: Amitabh Satsangi, Pratik K Jha, Sandeep Chakraborty, Akshay Kumar Bisoi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Coexisting transposition of the great arteries (d-TGA) and total anomalous pulmonary venous connection (TAPVC) is a rare combination, with the infracardiac variety being more rare. So far, only a few cases are described in the literature with combined infracardiac TAPVC and d-TGA. We present a 4-month-old girl having TGA and obstructed infracardiac TAPVC with early left ventricle mass regression. She underwent successful complete surgical anatomical correction of this rare entity without postoperative mechanical circulatory support uneventfully. Simultaneous complete anatomical correction of TAPVC and d-TGA with arterial switch operation and common chamber to left atrium anastomosis can be done with excellent outcome even in late presentation with features of obstruction.

We report a 4-month-old infant having TGA and infracardiac TAPVC to discuss the operative strategy in this scenario.

CASE REPORT

A 2-month-old female child presented with complaints of increasing respiratory distress and cyanosis. The patient’s birth weight was 3.6 kg; she cried immediately after birth with a normal vaginal delivery in the hospital. On admission, the patient’s saturation was 81% on oxygen via nasal prongs. The patient developed a low-grade fever, loose stools, and a cough. The patient developed sepsis, indicated by infiltrates in the right lung on chest X-ray, increased white blood cell counts, and increased C-reactive protein and procalcitonin levels. The patient was managed with intravenous antibiotics, noninvasive ventilation (NIV), and inotropes. Over a period of 2 months, the patient improved and was weaned off NIV and inotropes.

Two-dimensional echocardiography revealed situs solitus, levocardia, atrioventricular (AV) concordance and ventriculo-arterial (VA) discordance, malposed great arteries with the aorta slightly right and anterior to the pulmonary artery (PA), the left superior vena cava (SVC) draining into the coronary sinus, and three pulmonary veins (PVs) draining into a common channel draining infradiaphragmatically into the portal vein with obstruction near the distal end. Inter-atrial septum was aneurysmal with a large ostium secundum atrial septal defect (OS ASD) and an intact interventricular septum (IVS). The left ventricle was D-shaped with normal bi-ventricular function. The left ventricular mass was 32 g/m2, and the posterior wall thickness was 3.8 mm. There was no mitral valve regurgitation and severe PA hypertension.

Cardiac computed tomography (CT) angiography revealed transposition of great arteries, bilateral SVC with a thin intercommunicating vein, and all four PVs forming a common chamber (CC) posterior to the left atrium (LA) draining into the portal vein. Large OS ASD with AV concordance and VA discordance was noted. The aortic valve is located anterior to the pulmonary valve [Figure 1a and b].

The patient was cleared by the pediatric cardiology department and the pediatric intensivist and taken up for arterial switch operation with infracardiac TAPVC repair with integrated ECMO support in view of the regressed LV.

Intraoperative details

After median pericardiotomy, anatomical details as above were confirmed [Figure 2a]. Aortopulmonary dissection was done, and PDA ligation and division, followed by looping of RPA/LPA, were accomplished after establishing cardiopulmonary bypass. The right atrium (RA) was opened, and OS ASD was noted. The right pleura was opened, and the heart flipped on its axis inside the right pleura to visualize the CC of the four PVs along with the vertical vein. The CC was opened, the LA was opened, and anastomosis was done between the CC and LA [Figure 2b]. The vertical vein was left open, as it was mentioned as obstructed in echocardiographic findings. The aorta was transected proximal to the cardioplegia cannulation site and distal to the origin of the coronaries, followed by MPA transection at the same level. Right and left coronary buttons were harvested and reimplanted into the neo-aorta [Figure 2c]. The LeCompte maneuver was done, and the neo-aorta was anastomosed to the ascending aorta. Neo-PA reconstruction with autologous fixed pericardium and proximal anastomosis of neo-PA was done. OS ASD closure was done with a Dacron patch followed by right atrial incision closure [Figure 2d]. The patient was uneventfully weaned off bypass and did not require ECMO support. Sternal and skin closure was done after meticulous hemostasis. The total aortic cross-clamp time was 100 min, the cardiopulmonary bypass time was 145 min, the patient was cooled to 28°, and a single dose of del Nido cardioplegia was used.

The patient was shifted to noradrenaline 0.05 µg/kg/min and adrenaline 0.05 µg/kg/min. Initially, the patient had severe neopulmonary valve regurgitation secondary to PA hypertension, which was managed by intravenous sildenafil infusion. The patient over the next 2 days developed a left lower lobe lung collapse. The patient was managed with IV antibiotics, supportive ventilation, and chest physiotherapy. The patient was successfully weaned off the ventilator on postoperative day 7. Initially, the patient required O2 support for 5 days, and gradually, inotropes were weaned off.

Postoperative echocardiography revealed no obstruction at the anastomotic site between the LA and CC; there was low-moderate neopulmonary valve regurgitation and trivial neo-aortic valve regurgitation with good biventricular function. The patient was discharged on postoperative day 22.

DISCUSSION

Hemodynamics in TAPVC associated with TGA is different from that of isolated TGA or TAPVC. One defect partly corrects the negative hemodynamic consequences of the other. There will be relatively high saturations when compared to isolated TGA. TAPVC delivers all pulmonary venous returns to the systemic side of the heart and avoids overloading of the pulmonary circulation. Abah et al.[19] described a satisfactory outcome of a late-treated coexisting TAPVC and TGA, with the advantage of PAH in preserving the LV mass.

The first successful anatomical correction was performed by Lopes et al. on a 22-h-old infant with D-TGA and subdiaphragmatic TAPVC.[8] Later Seliem et al.,[10] on a 4-year-old child with supracardiac type, and Mykychak et al.,[11] on another infracardiac type of TAPVC, reported a total anatomical correction.

TAPVC with TGA-IVS is a cause of early regression of the LV, and early anatomic correction is hence recommended for a better prognosis. However, in our patient, obstructed TAPVC caused increased PA pressure, which prepared the LV, and the patient tolerated the procedure without requiring any ECMO support.

CONCLUSION

The rare subset of patients having the infracardiac type of TAPVC and TGA-IVS might benefit from and tolerate the definitive anatomic correction better due to the inherent obstructive physiology of the infracardiac TAPVC defect, though attention needs to be paid to LV parameters when such a decision is taken for late presenters.

Modular Delta Merging with Orthogonal Constraints: A Scalable Framework for Continual and Reversible Model Composition
Haris Khan, Shumail Asif, Sadia Asif

Abstract
In real-world machine learning deployments, models must be continually updated, composed, and when required, selectively undone. However, existing approaches to model merging and continual learning often suffer from task interference, catastrophic forgetting, or lack of reversibility. We propose Modular Delta Merging with Orthogonal Constraints (MDM-OC), a novel framework that enables scalable, interference-free, and reversible composition of fine-tuned models. Each task-specific model is encoded as a delta from a shared base and projected into an orthogonal subspace to eliminate conflict. These projected deltas are then merged via gradient-based optimization to form a unified model that retains performance across tasks. Our approach supports continual integration of new models, structured unmerging for compliance such as GDPR requirements, and model stability via elastic weight consolidation and synthetic replay. Extensive experiments on vision and natural language processing benchmarks demonstrate that MDM-OC outperforms prior baselines in accuracy, backward transfer, and unmerge fidelity, while remaining memory-efficient and computationally tractable. This framework offers a principled solution for modular and compliant AI system design.

While these methods have shown promise in specific scenarios, they typically assume static model collections and do not address the fundamental challenges of continual integration and selective removal of model contributions. Furthermore, most existing approaches lack theoretical guarantees about interference reduction and provide limited scalability as the number of constituent models grows.

This work addresses these limitations by proposing Modular Delta Merging with Orthogonal Constraints (MDM-OC), a novel framework that treats model merging as a structured, orthogonal projection problem in the parameter delta space. Rather than merging raw weights, our method extracts task-specific delta weights representing the minimal parameter changes induced by fine-tuning, and projects these deltas into orthogonal subspaces to ensure interference-free integration. The framework incorporates gradient-based optimization for fine-grained merging control and implements structured unmerging via algebraic operations in the projected delta space.

Our approach is grounded in the mathematical principle that orthogonal vectors in parameter space can be composed and decomposed without mutual interference, providing theoretical foundations for both merging and unmerging operations. We extend this principle with practical considerations including numerical stability, computational efficiency, and preservation of model performance across diverse tasks.

The contributions of this work are fourfold. First, we introduce a theoretically grounded delta-based orthogonal projection strategy that preserves task independence during merging while providing mathematical guarantees about interference reduction. Second, we propose a continual adaptation scheme that allows new models to be integrated or removed efficiently and reversibly, supporting dynamic model collections in production environments. Third, we combine our orthogonal merging framework with stability-preserving mechanisms including Elastic Weight Consolidation (EWC)
[
13
]
and synthetic replay techniques to maintain long-term model performance. Fourth, we provide comprehensive empirical validation demonstrating superior performance compared to state-of-the-art baselines across multiple domains and scales.

This work bridges critical gaps in continual learning, parameter-efficient transfer learning, and modular AI systems, offering a practical and theoretically sound approach to composable, interpretable, and compliant AI architectures. The framework’s ability to support both forward composition and backward decomposition of model knowledge makes it particularly suitable for applications requiring auditability, compliance, and dynamic model management.

3  Problem Formulation

3.1  Mathematical Framework

Consider a base model
\theta_{\text{base}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and specialized models
\theta_{i}
derived through task-specific fine-tuning on dataset
D_{i}
. The challenge is constructing a unified model
\theta_{\text{merged}}
that preserves specialized capabilities while avoiding computational overhead and supporting regulatory compliance.

3.2  Delta Representation and Compositionality

Each specialized model is characterized by its deviation from the base model:

\Delta\theta_{i}=\theta_{i}-\theta_{\text{base}}

The merged model is constructed through a weighted combination of orthogonalized deltas:

\theta_{\text{merged}}=\theta_{\text{base}}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\alpha_{i}\cdot\Delta\theta_{i}^{\perp}

where
\Delta\theta_{i}^{\perp}
represents the orthogonalized delta and
\alpha_{i}
denotes the combination weight. This formulation provides compact encoding, clear knowledge separation, and mathematical tractability.

3.3  Orthogonality Constraints and Interference Reduction

The mathematical foundation of our approach rests on the principle that orthogonal vectors in parameter space can be composed and decomposed without mutual interference. While this principle holds exactly in linear systems, neural networks present additional complexity due to their non-linear nature. However, research in continual learning has demonstrated that orthogonal parameter updates provide effective approximations for interference reduction, particularly in the vicinity of converged solutions
[
19
]
.

Orthogonalized deltas satisfy:

\langle\Delta\theta_{i}^{\perp},\Delta\theta_{j}^{\perp}\rangle=0\quad\forall i\neq j

Sequential Gram-Schmidt orthogonalization projects each delta onto the null space of previously processed deltas:

\Delta\theta_{i}^{\perp}=\Delta\theta_{i}-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\text{proj}_{\Delta\theta_{j}^{\perp}}(\Delta\theta_{i})

This ensures independent task influence modulation without cross-task interference.

3.4  Continual Integration and Dynamic Composition

New model integration updates the merged model as:

\theta_{\text{merged}}^{\text{new}}=\theta_{\text{merged}}+\alpha_{\text{new}}\cdot\Delta\theta_{\text{new}}^{\perp}

where:

\Delta\theta_{\text{new}}^{\perp}=\Delta\theta_{\text{new}}-\sum_{i=1}^{N}\text{proj}_{\Delta\theta_{i}^{\perp}}(\Delta\theta_{\text{new}})

This enables efficient updates without recomputing existing projections.

3.5  Reversible Unmerging and Compliance Requirements

The ability to selectively remove model contributions from a merged system has become increasingly important due to privacy regulations such as GDPR
[
6
]
and practical considerations such as model quality control and intellectual property management.

Model removal is achieved through:

\theta_{\text{merged}}^{-k}=\theta_{\text{merged}}-\alpha_{k}\cdot\Delta\theta_{k}^{\perp}

Orthogonal structure ensures clean removal without affecting remaining components, providing mathematical foundations for GDPR compliance and selective data deletion.

3.6  Computational and Scalability Constraints

To address
\mathcal{O}(N^{2})
complexity, we employ dimensionality reduction:

\Delta\theta_{i}^{\perp}\approx\sum_{j=1}^{k}\beta_{i,j}u_{j}

where
\{u_{j}\}
represents the top
k
principal components of the delta space and
k\ll d
. This dimensionality reduction approach maintains the essential orthogonality properties while reducing computational requirements to
\mathcal{O}(kN)
for integration operations.

3.7  Performance Preservation Requirements

The merged model must satisfy:

L_{i}(\theta_{\text{merged}},D_{i}^{\text{test}})\leq L_{i}(\theta_{i},D_{i}^{\text{test}})+\varepsilon_{i}

where
\varepsilon_{i}
represents acceptable performance degradation. Orthogonal projections provide theoretical bounds on performance degradation based on task-relevant parameter direction preservation.

This problem formulation provides the mathematical foundation for our proposed MDM-OC framework, capturing both the theoretical requirements for interference-free model composition and the practical constraints imposed by real-world deployment scenarios. The combination of orthogonal constraints, reversible operations, and scalability considerations distinguishes our formulation from existing approaches and enables principled solutions to dynamic model composition challenges.

4  Proposed Method

4.1  Overview of MDM-OC Framework

The Modular Delta Merging with Orthogonal Constraints (MDM-OC) framework combines multiple task-specific models while maintaining individual capabilities and supporting dynamic composition operations. The framework operates through four stages: delta extraction computes task-specific parameter changes relative to a shared base model; orthogonal projection ensures task deltas occupy independent subspaces; gradient-based optimization balances performance across tasks; and stability preservation maintains long-term performance.

This approach addresses fundamental model merging challenges including task interference, catastrophic forgetting, computational scalability, and regulatory compliance with both theoretical rigor and practical deployment considerations.

4.2  Delta Extraction and Parameterization

Given a base model
\theta_{\text{base}}
and task-specific models
\theta_{i}
created through fine-tuning on data
D_{i}
, the task-specific delta is computed as:

\Delta\theta_{i}=\theta_{i}-\theta_{\text{base}}

This delta representation captures minimal parameter changes for task adaptation, providing compact encoding of task-specific knowledge independent of absolute parameter values. The formulation offers reduced storage requirements, clear separation between general and task-specific knowledge, and mathematical tractability for composition operations.

We implement adaptive scaling mechanisms that normalize delta magnitudes based on parameter distribution in each layer, ensuring orthogonalization operations aren’t dominated by naturally larger parameters like bias terms. Delta vectors maintain the same tensor organization as original parameters for computational efficiency.

4.3  Orthogonal Projection and Interference Elimination

Orthogonal projection ensures different task deltas combine without mutual interference by constraining them to independent subspaces. The process extends Gram-Schmidt orthogonalization to high-dimensional parameter spaces with numerical stability and computational efficiency optimizations.

The orthogonalization proceeds sequentially:

\Delta\theta_{i}^{\perp}=\Delta\theta_{i}-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\text{proj}_{\Delta\theta_{j}^{\perp}}(\Delta\theta_{i})

where
\text{proj}_{u}(v)=\frac{\langle v,u\rangle}{\|u\|^{2}}\cdot u
. This ensures each orthogonalized delta is mathematically orthogonal to all previously processed deltas.

For computational scalability, we employ dimensionality reduction using SVD to identify principal directions of variation:

\Delta\theta_{i}^{\perp}\approx U_{k}\Sigma_{k}V_{k}^{\top}\Delta\theta_{i}^{\text{reduced},\perp}

This reduces complexity from
\mathcal{O}(d^{2}N)
to
\mathcal{O}(kdN)
where
k\ll d
while preserving orthogonality properties. We implement stability enhancements including adaptive thresholding, regularized division operations, and periodic re-orthogonalization.

4.4  Gradient-Based Weight Optimization

After orthogonalization, we determine optimal combination weights
\alpha_{i}
through gradient-based optimization rather than equal weighting or heuristic assignment. The optimization problem is:

\min_{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{N}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}w_{i}\cdot L_{i}\left(\theta_{\text{base}}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}\alpha_{j}\cdot\Delta\theta_{j}^{\perp},D_{i}^{\text{val}}\right)

where
L_{i}
represents validation loss for task
i
, and
w_{i}
represents task-specific importance weights.

We employ Adam optimization
[
28
]
with enhancements including gradient clipping, momentum scheduling, and early stopping. Adaptive loss balancing automatically adjusts relative weighting of different task losses during optimization, ensuring progress isn’t dominated by tasks with larger loss magnitudes.

4.5  Continual Integration and Dynamic Adaptation

The framework supports dynamic model collections where new specialized models are continually integrated without recomputing existing components. When a new model
\theta_{\text{new}}
becomes available, the new delta
\Delta\theta_{\text{new}}=\theta_{\text{new}}-\theta_{\text{base}}
is projected onto the null space of existing orthogonal deltas:

\Delta\theta_{\text{new}}^{\perp}=\Delta\theta_{\text{new}}-\sum_{i=1}^{N}\text{proj}_{\Delta\theta_{i}^{\perp}}(\Delta\theta_{\text{new}})

This ensures mathematical independence from existing models. Only the new model’s delta requires orthogonalization and weight optimization, providing significant computational advantages over batch reprocessing.

The framework also supports model updates where existing specialized models are replaced with improved versions through unmerging followed by re-integration.

4.6  Structured Unmerging and Compliance Support

The orthogonal structure enables clean algebraic removal of specific model contributions without affecting remaining components. The unmerging operation for model
k
is:

\theta_{\text{merged}}^{-k}=\theta_{\text{merged}}-\alpha_{k}\cdot\Delta\theta_{k}^{\perp}

This preserves the orthogonality structure of remaining deltas. The capability addresses privacy compliance, quality control, IP management, and security response.

The framework maintains provenance tracking of each constituent model’s contribution, enabling verification of removal and regulatory compliance documentation.

4.7  Stability Preservation and Long-Term Performance

Maintaining stable performance across multiple operations requires attention to numerical stability, parameter drift, and catastrophic forgetting. We integrate Elastic Weight Consolidation (EWC)
[
13
]
to protect parameters critical for base model performance:

L_{\text{EWC}}=\sum_{j}F_{j}\cdot(\theta_{j}-\theta_{j}^{*})^{2}

where
F_{j}
represents the diagonal of the Fisher Information Matrix and
\theta_{j}^{*}
the reference parameter values.

Synthetic replay mechanisms maintain general capabilities not explicitly represented in task-specific models by generating pseudo-inputs and incorporating corresponding loss terms into the optimization objective.

5  Experimental Evaluation

We conduct comprehensive experiments to evaluate the effectiveness of MDM-OC across multiple dimensions of continual learning and model merging. Our evaluation addresses four critical research questions: performance compared to existing methods, unmerging fidelity, computational scalability, and component contribution analysis.

5.1  Experimental Setup

5.1.1 Datasets and Tasks

Our evaluation encompasses three distinct domains to demonstrate the generalizability of MDM-OC. For computer vision tasks, we utilize CIFAR-100 partitioned into 20 sequential tasks of 5 classes each, creating a challenging continual learning scenario with high inter-class similarity. We also employ ImageNet-100, a subset of ImageNet containing 100 classes divided into 10 tasks to evaluate performance on more complex visual representations. In the natural language processing domain, we construct a multi-domain text classification benchmark using AG News, DBpedia, and Yahoo Answers as sequential tasks.

5.1.2 Model Architectures and Training Protocol

For vision tasks, we employ ResNet-50 pre-trained on ImageNet as our base model. For language tasks, we utilize BERT-large as the base model with task-specific classification heads. To ensure fair comparison across methods, we implement low-rank adaptation (LoRA)
[
9
]
with rank
r=8
for all approaches, providing a standardized delta parameterization. Each task-specific model undergoes fine-tuning for 10 epochs using AdamW optimizer with learning rate
2\times 10^{-5}
and weight decay 0.01.

5.1.3 Evaluation Metrics

We adopt standard continual learning metrics to assess performance comprehensively. Average accuracy (ACC) measures the mean performance across all tasks after sequential learning. Backward transfer (BWT) quantifies the change in performance on previously learned tasks, with positive values indicating beneficial knowledge transfer and negative values reflecting catastrophic forgetting. Forward transfer (FWT) measures the ability to perform on new tasks before explicit training. For unmerging evaluation, we introduce Unmerge Accuracy Drop (UAD), measuring the performance degradation on remaining tasks after selectively removing a specific model’s contribution. Computational efficiency is assessed through training time, inference latency, and peak memory consumption during merging operations.

5.1.4 Implementation Details

MDM-OC is implemented using PyTorch
[
29
]
with automatic differentiation support for gradient computations during orthogonal projection. The CMA-ES optimization employs a population size of 50 with initial standard deviation
\sigma_{0}=0.3
for coefficient search. Orthogonal projection utilizes modified Gram-Schmidt process
[
30
]
for numerical stability. EWC regularization coefficient is set to
\lambda=1000
based on preliminary experiments. For synthetic replay, we generate 100 pseudo-samples per task using the base model’s feature representations with Gaussian noise injection (
\sigma=0.1
).

Peak memory usage increases from 2.1GB for 5 models to 8.7GB for 50 models, compared to GEM’s growth from 3.8GB to 47.2GB over the same range.

Computational time for merging operations scales sub-linearly due to the effectiveness of our search space reduction techniques. PCA-based subspace decomposition reduces projection complexity from
O(d^{2})

where
k\ll d

5.4  Advanced Analysis

5.4.1 Parameter Space Visualization

We perform t-SNE visualization of delta parameters before and after orthogonal projection to illustrate the geometric transformation. Pre-projection deltas exhibit significant clustering and overlap, indicating potential interference patterns. Post-projection deltas form distinct, well-separated clusters with minimal overlap, visually confirming the effectiveness of orthogonalization in creating independent subspaces.

5.4.2 Task Similarity Impact

We analyze MDM-OC’s performance across task scenarios with varying similarity levels. For highly similar tasks (e.g., fine-grained object classification), traditional merging approaches suffer severe interference due to overlapping feature requirements. MDM-OC maintains consistent performance across similarity spectrums, with standard deviation of only 1.3% compared to 4.7% for the best baseline. This robustness stems from orthogonal projection’s ability to isolate task-specific variations regardless of semantic similarity.

5.4.3 Large-Scale Model Evaluation

We evaluate MDM-OC on larger architectures including BERT-large (340M parameters) and GPT-2 medium (345M parameters) to assess scalability to modern foundation models. Results demonstrate consistent performance gains over baselines, with 4.2% average accuracy improvement on BERT-large and 3.8% on GPT-2 medium. Memory efficiency improvements become more pronounced at larger scales, with MDM-OC requiring 40% less peak memory than replay-based methods during merging operations.

6  Discussion

6.1  Theoretical Implications

MDM-OC provides empirical validation for the orthogonal subspace hypothesis in continual learning, demonstrating that parameter space orthogonality effectively translates to functional independence. The mathematical framework establishes a principled foundation for modular neural architectures and compositional learning systems.

The evolutionary optimization component reveals insights about the non-convex landscape of model merging. CMA-ES’s success suggests derivative-free optimization methods may be fundamentally better suited for multi-objective model composition problems than traditional gradient-based approaches.

6.2  Practical Implications and Applications

MDM-OC addresses critical real-world AI deployment challenges. In federated learning, the framework enables privacy-preserving model composition without centralized data access. Organizations can contribute specialized models while maintaining revocation capabilities through clean unmerging, supporting dynamic collaboration agreements.

For enterprise AI systems, MDM-OC provides sustainable model lifecycle management by composing unified systems that adapt to changing requirements. The unmerging capability supports regulatory compliance, particularly under data protection regulations
[
6
]
. The framework extends to edge computing scenarios where compressed delta representation and efficient merging enable continual adaptation on resource-constrained devices.

6.3  Limitations and Challenges

MDM-OC faces several limitations. The shared base model requirement restricts applicability in heterogeneous environments with distinct foundation models. The orthogonal projection assumption may be overly restrictive for tasks that benefit from shared representations, potentially preventing beneficial knowledge transfer.

Computational complexity remains a concern for large-scale deployments. While reduction techniques improve efficiency, the O(n²) complexity of orthogonal projection limits scalability to hundreds of tasks. Hierarchical projection schemes or approximate orthogonalization methods could address massive-scale applications.

6.4  Comparison with Concurrent Work

Recent model merging approaches include Task Arithmetic
[
28
]
, which demonstrates additive properties but lacks interference prevention guarantees, and TIES-Merging
[
31
]
, which addresses sign conflicts but doesn’t ensure geometric independence. Aligned model merging
[
10
]
requires expensive alignment procedures that may not generalize, while MDM-OC provides alignment-free operation with mathematical independence guarantees.

Mixture-of-experts architectures
[
32
]
offer alternative task composition through learned routing but require joint training and lack MDM-OC’s modularity and reversibility properties.

6.5  Broader Impact and Ethical Considerations

MDM-OC’s reversible merging capability supports responsible AI development through algorithmic accountability and user control over personal data influence. However, unmerging capabilities raise concerns about model security and intellectual property protection in collaborative systems.

The framework’s efficiency improvements could democratize advanced AI capabilities by reducing computational barriers, though this must be balanced against responsible deployment considerations and appropriate safeguards.

7  Conclusion and Future Directions

We presented Modular Delta Merging with Orthogonal Constraints (MDM-OC), addressing fundamental challenges in continual learning and model composition. Through orthogonal projection of task-specific deltas, evolutionary optimization of merge coefficients, and stability preservation mechanisms, MDM-OC achieves superior performance in accuracy retention, forgetting prevention, and reversible composition.

7.1  Key Contributions

Primary contributions include: a mathematically grounded approach to interference-free model merging through orthogonal delta projection; reversible model composition supporting compliance and modularity requirements; scalable continual learning without replay buffers; and comprehensive experimental validation establishing new benchmarks for model merging evaluation.

7.2  Future Research Directions

Promising research avenues include cross-architecture merging through universal representation spaces, adaptive orthogonality constraints balancing independence with beneficial knowledge sharing, and theoretical analysis of convergence properties and generalization bounds.

Integration with federated learning, edge computing, and privacy-preserving machine learning presents broader impact opportunities. Developing secure composition protocols that preserve unmerging while protecting intellectual property represents a critical direction for collaborative AI systems.

The success in achieving interference-free model composition through geometric constraints suggests broader applications beyond continual learning scenarios. As AI systems become increasingly collaborative and dynamic, MDM-OC principles may inform more sophisticated approaches to knowledge composition, transfer, and management in distributed intelligent systems.

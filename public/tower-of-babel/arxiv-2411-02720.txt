Abstract
Binary self-dual cyclic codes have been studied since the classical work of Sloane and Thompson published in IEEE Trans. Inf. Theory, vol. 29, 1983. Twenty five years later, an infinite family of binary self-dual cyclic codes with lengths n i n_{i} and minimum distances d i ≥ 1 2 ​ n i + 2 d_{i}\geq\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{n_{i}+2} was presented in a paper of IEEE Trans. Inf. Theory, vol. 55, 2009. However, no infinite family of Euclidean self-dual binary cyclic codes whose minimum distances have the square-root lower bound and no infinite family of Euclidean self-dual nonbinary cyclic codes whose minimum distances have a lower bound better than the square-root lower bound are known in the literature. In this paper, an infinite family of Euclidean self-dual cyclic codes over the fields 𝐅 2 s {\bf F}_{2^{s}} with a square-root-like lower bound is constructed. An infinite subfamily of this family consists of self-dual binary cyclic codes with the square-root lower bound. Another infinite subfamily of this family consists of self-dual cyclic codes over the fields 𝐅 2 s {\bf F}_{2^{s}} with a lower bound better than the square-root bound for s ≥ 2 s\geq 2 . Consequently, two breakthroughs in coding theory are made in this paper. An infinite family of self-dual binary cyclic codes with a square-root-like lower bound is also presented in this paper. An infinite family of Hermitian self-dual cyclic codes over the fields 𝐅 2 2 ​ s {\bf F}_{2^{2s}} with a square-root-like lower bound and an infinite family of Euclidean self-dual linear codes over 𝐅 q {\bf F}_{q} with q ≡ 1 ( mod 4 ) q\equiv 1\pmod{4} with a square-root-like lower bound are also constructed in this paper. Index terms: Cyclic code, linear code, self-dual code.

Introduction

1.1
Basics of linear codes

The
Hamming weight
of a vector
{\bf a}=(a_{0},\ldots,a_{n-1})\in{\bf F}_{q}^{n}
is defined by

{\mathrm{wt}}({\bf a})=|\{i:a_{i}\neq 0\}|.

The
Hamming distance

d({\bf a},{\bf b})
between two vectors
{\bf a}
and
{\bf b}
is defined by

d({\bf a},{\bf b})={\mathrm{wt}}({\bf a}-{\bf b}).

The
Hamming distance

d({\bf C})
of a subset
{\bf C}\subseteq{\bf F}_{q}
is defied by

d({\bf C})=\min_{{\bf a}\neq{\bf b}}\{d({\bf a},{\bf b}):{\bf a}\in{\bf C},{\bf b}\in{\bf C}\}.

An
[n,k,d]_{q}

linear code
is a linear subspace of
{\bf F}_{q}^{n}
with dimension
k
and
minimum distance
d
. Throughout this paper, by an
[n,k,d]_{q}
code we mean an
[n,k,d]_{q}
linear code
over
{\mathbf{{F}}}_{q}
.
It is clear that the minimum Hamming distance of a nonzero linear code is its minimum nonzero Hamming weight. The Singleton bound asserts that
d\leq n-k+1
for an
[n,k,d]_{q}
code. A linear code attaining this bound is said to be maximal distance separable (MDS). Reed-Solomon codes are well-known MDS codes (see, e.g.,
[
10
,
22
,
14
]
).

The
Euclidean inner product
on
{\bf F}_{q}^{n}
is defined by

<{\bf x},{\bf y}>=\Sigma_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}y_{i},

where
{\bf x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
and
{\bf y}=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})
. The
Euclidean dual
of a linear code
{\bf C}\subseteq{\bf F}_{q}^{n}
is

{\bf C}^{\perp}=\{{\bf c}\in{\bf F}_{q}^{n}:<{\bf c},{\bf y}>=0,\forall{\bf y}\in{\bf C}\}.

A linear code
{\bf C}\subseteq{\bf F}_{q}^{n}
is
Euclidean self-orthogonal
if
{\bf C}\subseteq{\bf C}^{\perp}
,
Euclidean dual-containing
if
{\bf C}^{\perp}\subseteq{\bf C}
, and
Euclidean self-dual
if
{\bf C}={\bf C}^{\perp}
. The Euclidean dual of a Euclidean dual-containing code is a Euclidean self-orthogonal code.

Let
q=q_{1}^{2}
.
Similarly the
Hermitian inner product
on
{\bf F}_{q}^{n}
is defined by

<{\bf x},{\bf y}>_{H}=\Sigma_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}y_{i}^{q_{1}},

where
{\bf x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
and
{\bf y}=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})
are two vectors in
{\bf F}_{q}^{n}
. The
Hermitian dual
of a linear code
{\bf C}\subseteq{\bf F}_{q}^{n}
is

{\bf C}^{\perp_{H}}=\{{\bf c}\in{\bf F}_{q}^{n}:<{\bf c},{\bf y}>_{H}=0,\forall{\bf y}\in{\bf C}\}.

For a linear code
{\bf C}\subseteq{\bf F}_{q}^{n}
,
we set

{\bf C}^{q_{1}}=\{(c_{0}^{q_{1}},\ldots,c_{n-1}^{q_{1}}):(c_{0},\ldots,c_{n-1})\in{\bf C}\}.

Then it is clear that

\displaystyle{\bf C}^{\perp_{H}}=({\bf C}^{\perp})^{q_{1}}=({\bf C}^{q_{1}})^{\perp}.

(1)

A linear code
{\bf C}\subseteq{\bf F}_{q}^{n}
is
Hermitian self-orthogonal
if
{\bf C}\subseteq{\bf C}^{\perp_{H}}
,
Hermitian dual-containing
if
{\bf C}^{\perp_{H}}\subseteq{\bf C}
, and
Hermitian self-dual
if
{\bf C}={\bf C}^{\perp_{H}}
. The Hermitian dual of a Hermitian dual-containing code is a Hermitian self-orthogonal code.

1.2
The importance of cyclic codes in theory and practice

Let
{\bf C}\subseteq{\bf F}_{q}^{n}
be a linear code. If
(c_{0},c_{1},\ldots,c_{n-1})\in{\bf C}
implies

(c_{n-1},c_{0},\ldots,c_{n-2})

\in{\bf C}
, then this code
{\bf C}\subseteq{\bf F}_{q}^{n}
is said to be

cyclic
. A codeword
{\bf c}
in a cyclic code
{\bf C}\subseteq{\bf F}_{q}^{n}
is identified with the polynomial
{\bf c}(x)=c_{0}+c_{1}x+\cdots+c_{n-1}x^{n-1}\in{\bf F}_{q}[x]/(x^{n}-1)
. With this identification,
every cyclic code
{\bf C}\subseteq{\bf F}_{q}^{n}
is a principal ideal in the ring
{\bf F}_{q}[x]/(x^{n}-1)
and is then generated by many polynomials in
{\bf F}_{q}[x]/(x^{n}-1)
. Let
{\bf C}=(g(x))
, where
g(x)
is the monic polynomial with the lowest degree,
then
g(x)
must be a divisor of
x^{n}-1
and is called the
generator polynomial
of
{\bf C}
, and
h(x)=\frac{x^{n}-1}{g(x)}
is referred to as
the
check polynomial
of
{\bf C}
.
The dual code
{\bf C}^{\perp}
of a cyclic code
{\bf C}
with generator polynomial
g(x)
is the cyclic code with the generator polynomial
h^{\perp}(x)=\frac{x^{k}h(x^{-1})}{h(0)}
, which is the reciprocal polynomial of the check polynomial
h(x)
of
{\bf C}
.

Cyclic codes, in particular, the Bose-Chaudhuri-Hocquenghem (BCH) codes introduced in 1959-1960 (see
[
3
,
4
,
9
]
), are the most important type of linear codes in coding theory and practice due to the
following:

•

They have the cyclic structure and the efficient Berlekamp-Massey decoding algorithm.

•

They (in particular, the cyclic Reed-Solomon codes) are widely used in communications and data storage systems.

•

They are closely related to combinatorics, number theory, group theory and many other areas of mathematics.

For similar reasons, the Euclidean and Hermitian self-dual cyclic codes are more interesting than other
Euclidean and Hermitian self-dual non-cyclic linear codes. This explains why we are more interested in
Euclidean and Hermitian self-dual cyclic codes.

The construction and decoding of cyclic and BCH codes have been extensively studied
in the literature (see
[
14
, Chapters 3, 7, 8, 9]
,
[
10
, Chapters 4, 5, 6]
,
and
[
22
, Chapter 6]
). The total number of references on cyclic codes in the literature is huge and this shows the importance of cyclic codes in another sense.

1.3
The motivations and objectives of this paper

A lower bound on
the minimum distance of an
[n,k,d]_{q}
code is called the
square-root lower bound
if it is

\sqrt{n}
, and
a
square-root-like lower bound
if it is at least

c\sqrt{n}
for a fixed positive constant
c
and fixed
q
.

Self-dual codes have been a very hot and interesting topic partially due to the following:

•

Some of them support combinatorial
5
-designs. For example, the extended binary and ternary Golay codes and some Pless symmetry codes are such codes
[
5
,
10
]
.

•

They are related to invariant theory
[
15
]
.

•

They have applications in quantum error-correcting codes
[
15
]
.

To the best knowledge of the authors, known infinite families of self-dual codes with unbounded length
n
and minimum distance
d\geq\sqrt{n}
are the following:

1.

The extended codes of odd-like quadratic residue codes
[
10
]
, which are not cyclic.

2.

The Pless symmetry codes
[
16
,
5
]
, which are not cyclic.

3.

The generalized Reed-Muller code of order
[m(q-1)-1]/2
over
{\bf F}_{q}
with parameters
[q^{m},q^{m}/2,[(q+2)/2]q^{(m-1)/2}]
, where
m
is odd and
q=2^{s}
with a positive integer
s

[
2
, Theorem 5.8, Theorem 5.25]
, which are not cyclic.

4.

A family of negacyclic codes over
{\bf F}_{q}
with parameters (see
[
20
]
)

\left[\frac{q^{m}-1}{2},\frac{q^{m}-1}{4},d\right],

where
q
is an odd prime power,
m\geq 2
is an integer,
q^{m}\equiv 1\pmod{4}
and

\displaystyle d=\begin{cases}q^{m/2}~&{\rm if}~m~{\rm is~even},\\
(\frac{q+3}{4})q^{(m-1)/2}~&{\rm if}~m~{\rm is~odd}.\end{cases}

Notice that all the families of self-dual codes above are not cyclic. Hence, no infinite family of self-dual cyclic
codes whose minimum distances are better than the square-root bound are known in the literature. The main motivation of this paper is the lack of such an infinite family of self-dual cyclic codes.

While it is hard to construct an infinite family of self-dual cyclic codes with the square-root lower bound,
it is natural for people to construct an infinite family of self-dual cyclic codes with a square-root-like lower bound.
In 2009, an important progress in this direction was made and an infinite family of binary self-dual cyclic codes
with length
n=2(2^{2a+1}-1)
and minimum distance
\delta\geq\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{n+2}
were constructed
by Heijne and Top
[
8
]
. Constructing infinite families of self-dual cyclic codes with
a square-root-like lower bound is not easy and is thus the second motivation of this paper.

The objectives of this paper are the following:

•

Generalize earlier results on the construction of binary cyclic codes.

•

Present a new method for constructing self-dual linear codes.

•

Construct infinite families of Euclidean and Hermitian self-dual cyclic codes with a square-root-like lower bound.

•

Construct infinite families of Euclidean self-dual linear codes with a square-root-like lower bound.

2
A generalized van Lint theorem

Let
{\mathbf{{C}}}_{1}
and
{\mathbf{{C}}}_{2}
be two linear codes with parameters
[n,k_{1},d_{1}]_{q}
and
[n,k_{2},d_{2}]_{q}
, respectively.
The Plotkin sum of
{\mathbf{{C}}}_{1}
and
{\mathbf{{C}}}_{2}
is denoted by
{\mathrm{Plotkin}}({\mathbf{{C}}}_{1},{\mathbf{{C}}}_{2})
and defined by

\displaystyle{\mathrm{Plotkin}}({\mathbf{{C}}}_{1},{\mathbf{{C}}}_{2}):=\{({\mathbf{u}}|{\mathbf{u}}+{\mathbf{v}}):{\mathbf{u}}\in{\mathbf{{C}}}_{1},\,{\mathbf{v}}\in{\mathbf{{C}}}_{2}\},

where
{\mathbf{u}}|{\mathbf{v}}
denotes the concatenation of the two vectors
{\mathbf{u}}
and
{\mathbf{v}}
. It is well known that

{\mathbf{{C}}}
has parameters
[2n,k_{1}+k_{2},\min\{2d_{1},d_{2}\}]_{q}
(see
[
14
]
). The Plotkin sum is also called
the
[{\bf u}|{\bf u}+{\bf v}]
construction and was introduced in 1960 by Plotkin
[
17
]
.

The
[{\bf u}|{\bf u}+{\bf v}]
construction of binary repeated-root cyclic codes was given by van Lint in his paper
[
23
]
. By modifying the conditions of Theorem 1 in
[
23
]
, we have the following theorem, which is a generalization of the original van Lint theorem.

Theorem 1

(The generalized van Lint theorem)
.

Let
q
be a power of
2
and
n
be an odd positive integer. Let
{\bf C}_{1}\subseteq{\bf F}_{q}^{n}
be a cyclic code
with generator polynomial
g_{1}(x)\in{\bf F}_{q}[x]
and
{\bf C}_{2}\subseteq{\bf F}_{q}^{n}
be a cyclic code
generated by the polynomial
g_{1}(x)g_{2}(x)\in{\bf F}_{q}[x]
, where
g_{2}(x)
is a divisor of
x^{n}+1
.
Then the code
{\mathrm{Plotkin}}({\mathbf{{C}}}_{1},{\mathbf{{C}}}_{2})

is permutation-equivalent to the repeated-root cyclic code of length
2n
generated by the polynomial
g_{1}(x)^{2}g_{2}(x)
.

Proof.

Let
{\mathbf{{C}}}
denote
{\mathrm{Plotkin}}({\mathbf{{C}}}_{1},{\mathbf{{C}}}_{2})
.
Let
f(x)=\gcd(g_{1}(x),g_{2}(x))
. Then
f(x)
is a divisor of
x^{n}+1
.
By definition,
{\mathbf{{C}}}_{2}
has generator polynomial

\gcd(x^{n}+1,g_{1}(x)g_{2}(x))=g_{1}(x)g_{2}(x)/f(x)

and thus dimension

n-\deg(g_{1}(x))-\deg(g_{2}(x))+\deg(f(x)).

It then follows that

\displaystyle\dim({\mathbf{{C}}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dim({\mathbf{{C}}}_{1})+\dim({\mathbf{{C}}}_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2n-2\deg(g_{1}(x))-\deg(g_{2}(x))+\deg(f(x)).

Let
{\mathbf{{C}}}^{\prime}
denote the the repeated-root cyclic code of length
2n
generated by the polynomial
g_{1}(x)^{2}g_{2}(x)
. By definition,
{\mathbf{{C}}}^{\prime}
has generator polynomial

\gcd(x^{2n}+1,g_{1}(x)^{2}g_{2}(x))=\frac{g_{1}(x)^{2}g_{2}(x)}{f(x)},

as
x^{n}+1
has no repeated roots due to the assumption that
n
is odd.
Consequently,
{\mathbf{{C}}}^{\prime}
has dimension

2n-2\deg(g_{1}(x))-\deg(g_{2}(x))+\deg(f(x)).

We have then

\displaystyle\dim({\mathbf{{C}}})=\dim({\mathbf{{C}}}^{\prime}).

(2)

Let
{\bf a}=(a_{0},\ldots,a_{n-1})\in{\bf C}_{1}
and
{\bf c}=(c_{0},\ldots,c_{n-1})\in{\bf C}_{2}
. Set
{\bf b}={\bf a}+{\bf c}=(b_{0},\ldots,b_{n-1})
. Then
{\bf a}(x)=a_{0}+a_{1}x+\cdots+a_{n-1}x^{n-1}={\bf a}_{even}(x^{2})+x{\bf a}_{odd}(x^{2})
is divisible by
g_{1}(x)
. And
{\bf c}(x)=c_{0}+c_{1}x+\cdots+c_{n-1}x^{n-1}
is divisible by
\gcd(x^{n}+1,g_{1}(x)g_{2}(x))
. Therefore
{\bf b}(x)=b_{0}+b_{1}x+\cdots+b_{n-1}x^{n-1}={\bf b}_{even}(x^{2})+x{\bf b}_{odd}(x^{2})
is divisible by
g_{1}(x)
.

Set

{\bf w}=(a_{0},b_{1},a_{2},b_{3},\ldots,b_{n-2},a_{n-1},b_{0},a_{1},\ldots,a_{n-2},b_{n-1}).

Then

{\bf w}(x)=[{\bf a}_{even}(x^{2})+x^{n+1}{\bf a}_{odd}(x^{2})]+[x{\bf b}_{odd}(x^{2})+x^{n}{\bf b}_{even}(x^{2})].

It is clear that

{\bf a}_{even}(x^{2})+x^{n+1}{\bf a}_{odd}(x^{2})={\bf a}(x)+x(x^{n}+1){\bf a}_{odd}(x^{2})

is divisible by
g_{1}(x)
, since
g_{1}(x)
is a divisor of
x^{n}+1
. Since there is only even degree powers
x^{2h}
’s in
{\bf a}(x)+x(x^{n}+1){\bf a}_{odd}(x^{2})
, this term is divisible by
g_{1}(x)^{2}
, from the fact that
g_{1}(x)
has no repeated root. The second term
x{\bf b}_{odd}(x^{2})+x^{n}{\bf b}_{even}(x^{2})={\bf b}(x)+(x^{n}+1){\bf b}_{odd}(x^{2})
is divisible by
g_{1}(x)^{2}
, from a similar argument.

On the other hand,
{\bf w}(x)=(x^{n}+1){\bf a}(x)+{\bf c}(x)+(x^{n}+1){\bf c}_{even}(x^{2})
is divisible by
g_{2}(x)
, then
{\bf w}(x)
is divisible by
\gcd(x^{2n}+1,g_{1}(x)^{2}g_{2}(x))
. Then the code
{\bf C}
is permutation-equivalent to the cyclic code
{\mathbf{{C}}}^{\prime}
of length
2n
generated by
g_{1}(x)^{2}g_{2}(x)
, as both codes have the same dimension by (
2
). The desired conclusion then follows.
∎

We have the following remarks regarding Theorem
1
:

•

By definition,
{\mathbf{{C}}}_{2}
is a subcode of
{\mathbf{{C}}}_{1}
and it is possible that
{\mathbf{{C}}}_{2}={\mathbf{{C}}}_{1}
.

•

The proof of Theorem
1
shows that the cyclic code
{\mathbf{{C}}}^{\prime}
has generator polynomial

\gcd(x^{2n}+1,g_{1}(x)^{2}g_{2}(x))=\frac{g_{1}(x)^{2}g_{2}(x)}{f(x)}

and the codes
{\mathrm{Plotkin}}({\mathbf{{C}}}_{1},{\mathbf{{C}}}_{2})
and
{\mathbf{{C}}}^{\prime}
have dimension

2n-2\deg(g_{1}(x))-\deg(g_{2}(x))+\deg(f(x)).

•

The code
{\mathrm{Plotkin}}({\mathbf{{C}}}_{1},{\mathbf{{C}}}_{2})
is Euclidean (Hermitian) self-dual if and only if the cyclic code

{\mathbf{{C}}}^{\prime}
is Euclidean (Hermitian) self-dual, as the two codes are permutation-equivalent.

•

In the original theorem proved by van Lint (see
[
22
, Section 6.10]
), the condition that
\gcd(g_{1}(x),g_{2}(x))=1
is required. Theorem
1
does not have such a condition. Hence,
Theorem
1
is indeed a generalization of the original van Lint theorem. Even in the case

q=2
, Theorem
1
extends the original van Lint theorem to a large extent.

3
The
[{\bf u}|{\bf u}+{\bf v}]
construction of Euclidean and Hermitian self-dual cyclic codes over
{\mathbf{{F}}}_{2^{s}}

In this section, we give the main construction of self-dual codes over
{\bf F}_{2^{s}}
from dual-containing codes.

Theorem 2
.

Let
{\bf F}_{2^{s}}
be the finite field with
2^{s}
elements. Suppose that
{\bf C}\subseteq{\bf F}_{2^{s}}^{n}
is a Euclidean dual-containing linear code. Then the linear code

{\bf C}_{1}=\{{\bf u}|{\bf u}+{\bf v}:{\bf u}\in{\bf C},{\bf v}\in{\bf C}^{\perp}\}\subseteq{\bf F}_{2^{s}}^{2n}

is a self-dual code with minimum distance
\min\{d({\bf C}^{\perp}),2d({\bf C})\}
.

Let
{\bf F}_{2^{2s}}
be the finite field with
2^{2s}
elements. Suppose that
{\bf C}\subseteq{\bf F}_{2^{2s}}^{n}
is a Hermitian dual-containing linear code. Then the linear code

{\bf C}_{2}=\{{\bf u}|{\bf u}+{\bf v}:{\bf u}\in{\bf C},{\bf v}\in{\bf C}^{\perp_{H}}\}\subseteq{\bf F}_{2^{2s}}^{2n}

is a Hermitian self-dual code with minimum distance

\min\{d({\bf C}^{\perp_{H}}),2d({\bf C})\}=\min\{d({\bf C}^{\perp}),2d({\bf C})\}.

Proof.

For two codewords
{\bf c}_{1}=[{\bf u}_{1}|{\bf u}_{1}+{\bf v}_{1}]
and
{\bf c}_{2}=[{\bf u}_{2}|{\bf u}_{2}+{\bf v}_{2}]
in this code
{\bf C}_{1}
, their Euclidean inner product is

<{\bf c}_{1},{\bf c}_{2}>=(1+1)<{\bf u}_{1},{\bf u}_{2}>+<{\bf u}_{1},{\bf v}_{2}>+<{\bf u}_{2},{\bf v}_{1}>+<{\bf v}_{1},{\bf v}_{2}>.

Since
{\bf C}^{\perp}
is self-orthogonal, this inner product is zero. It is clear that the dimension of
{\bf C}_{1}
is

\dim({\bf C}_{1})=\dim({\bf C}^{\perp})+\dim({\bf C})=n.

It is well known
that

d({\mathbf{{C}}}_{1})=d({\mathrm{Plotkin}}({\mathbf{{C}}},{\mathbf{{C}}}^{\perp}))=\min\{2d({\mathbf{{C}}}),d({\mathbf{{C}}}^{\perp})\}.

Then the first conclusion follows immediately. The second conclusion can be similarly proved with the well known fact that

d({\bf C}^{\perp_{H}})=d({\bf C}^{\perp})
(it follows from (
1
)).
∎

Suppose that the length of the cyclic code
{\bf C}
over
{\bf F}_{2^{s}}
is odd and the generator polynomial of
{\bf C}
is
g_{1}(x)\in{\bf F}_{2^{s}}[x]
. Since
{\bf C}^{\perp}\subseteq{\bf C}
, the generator polynomial of
{\bf C}^{\perp}
is
g_{1}(x)g_{2}(x)\in{\bf F}_{2^{s}}[x]
, where
g_{2}(x)
is a divisor of
x^{n}+1
. By the generalized van Lint Theorem (see Theorem
1
), the code
{\mathbf{{C}}}_{1}
is permutation-equivalent to the repeated-root cyclic code
{\mathbf{{C}}}^{\prime}
over
{\bf F}_{2^{s}}
with length
2n
and generator polynomial
g_{1}^{2}(x)g_{2}(x)
. Therefore,
if the building block
{\bf C}
in Theorem
2
is a cyclic code with odd length
n
, then the code
{\bf C}_{1}
is
permutation-equivalent to a repeated-root self-dual cyclic code of the length
2n
. In the subsequent sections,
we will construct specific families of dual-containing cyclic codes
{\mathbf{{C}}}
and then plug them into the construction
of Theorem
2
to obtain
families of self-dual cyclic codes with very good minimum distances.

4.2
BCH codes

In this subsection, we recall the basics of BCH codes.
Let
\gcd(q,n)=1.
Define
{\bf Z}_{n}={\bf Z}/n{\bf Z}=\{0,1,\ldots,n-1\}
. For each
i\in{\mathbf{{Z}}}_{n}
,
the
q
-cyclotomic coset

C_{i}
containing
i
is defined by

C_{i}=\{iq^{i}\bmod{n}:0\leq i\leq l-1\},

where
l
is the smallest positive integer such that
iq^{l}\equiv i\pmod{n}
and

a\bmod{n}
denotes the unique
b\in{\mathbf{{Z}}}_{n}
such that
a\equiv b\pmod{n}
. The smallest integer
in
C_{i}
is called the
coset leader
of
C_{i}
. Let
\Gamma(q,n)
denote the set of all coset leaders. Then

\{C_{i}:i\in\Gamma(q,n)\}
is a partition of
{\mathbf{{Z}}}_{n}
.

Let
m={\mathrm{ord}}_{n}(q)
, which is the smallest positive integer
\ell
such that
q^{\ell}\equiv 1\pmod{n}
.
Let
\alpha
be a primitive element of
{\mathbf{{F}}}_{q^{m}}
. Define
\beta=\alpha^{(q^{m}-1)/n}
.
Then
\beta
is an
n
-th primitive root of unity. Define

{\mathsf{M}}_{\beta^{i}}(x)=\prod_{j\in C_{i}}(x-\beta^{j}).

It is easily seen that
{\mathsf{M}}_{\beta^{i}}(x)
is an irreducible polynomial in
{\mathbf{{F}}}_{q}[x]
and the canonical factorization
of
x^{n}-1
over
{\mathbf{{F}}}_{q}
is

x^{n}-1=\prod_{j\in\Gamma(q,n)}{\mathsf{M}}_{\beta^{i}}(x).

By definition, the generator polynomial
g(x)
of a cyclic code
{\mathbf{{C}}}
of length
n
over
{\mathbf{{F}}}_{q}
is a divisor
of
x^{n}-1
. The
defining set
of the cyclic code
{\mathbf{{C}}}
with respect to
\beta
is defined by

\displaystyle{\bf T}_{{g}}=\{i\in{\mathbf{{Z}}}_{n}:{g}(\beta^{i})=0\}.

(3)

Then the defining set of a cyclic code is the disjoint union of some
q
-cyclotomic cosets.

Let
\delta\geq 2
be an integer and
b
be an integer. Define

\displaystyle g_{(q,n,\delta,b,\beta)}(x)={\mathrm{lcm}}\{{\mathsf{M}}_{\beta^{b}}(x),\ldots,{\mathsf{M}}_{\beta^{b+\delta-2}}(x)\},

(4)

where
{\mathrm{lcm}}
denotes the least common multiple of the set of polynomials over
{\mathbf{{F}}}_{q}
.
Let
{\mathbf{{C}}}_{(q,n,\delta,b,\beta)}
denote the cyclic code over
{\mathbf{{F}}}_{q}
with length
n
and
generator polynomial
g_{(q,n,\delta,b,\beta)}(x)
. This code
{\mathbf{{C}}}_{(q,n,\delta,b,\beta)}

is called a
BCH code
with designed distance
\delta
. It is well known that the
minimum distance of
{\mathbf{{C}}}_{(q,n,\delta,b,\beta)}
is lower bounded by
\delta
.
This follows from the famous BCH bound for cyclic codes
[
10
,
14
]
.

4.3
Some Euclidean and Hermitian dual-containing BCH codes

The purpose of this section is to introduce a family of known Euclidean dual-containing BCH codes and a family
of known Hermitian dual-containing BCH codes. These known codes will be used as basic building blocks in the constructions of self-dual codes in later sections.

Throughout this section, let
q
be a prime power and
n
be a positive integer with
\gcd(q,n)=1
.
Define
{\bf Z}_{n}={\bf Z}/n{\bf Z}=\{0,1,\ldots,n-1\}
. For any subset
{\mathbf{{T}}}\subseteq{\mathbf{{Z}}}_{n}
, define

{\mathbf{{T}}}^{-1}=\{n-i:i\in{\mathbf{{T}}}\}

and

{\mathbf{{T}}}^{c}={\mathbf{{Z}}}_{n}\setminus{\mathbf{{T}}},

which is the complement set of
{\mathbf{{T}}}
. In the case that
q=q_{1}^{2}
, define

{\mathbf{{T}}}^{-q_{1}}=\{(n-q_{1}i)\bmod{n}:i\in{\mathbf{{T}}}\}.

We follow the notation of Section
4.2
. Let
{\mathbf{{C}}}
be a cyclic code over
{\mathbf{{F}}}_{q}
with length
n

and generator polynomial
g(x)
, and
\beta
be an
n
-th primitive root of unity in
{\mathbf{{F}}}_{q^{m}}
. Let

{\mathbf{{T}}}_{g}
be the defining set of
{\mathbf{{C}}}
with respect to
\beta
defined in (
3
).
Then the defining set of the Euclidean dual code
{\mathbf{{C}}}^{\perp}
is
({\bf T}_{g}^{c})^{-1}
,
where
{\bf T}_{g}^{c}={\bf Z}_{n}\setminus{\bf T}_{g}
is the complementary set of
{\mathbf{{T}}}_{g}
.
The following result is well known
[
11
]
.

Lemma 4
.

The following hold:

•

The code
{\mathbf{{C}}}
is Euclidean dual-containing if and only if
{\bf T}_{g}\cap{\bf T}_{g}^{-1}=\emptyset
.

•

The code
{\mathbf{{C}}}
is Hermitian dual-containing if and only if
{\bf T}_{g}\cap{\bf T}_{g}^{-q_{1}}=\emptyset
,

where
q=q_{1}^{2}
and

{\mathbf{{T}}}_{g}^{-q_{1}}=\{(n-q_{1}i)\bmod{n}:i\in{\mathbf{{T}}}_{g}\}.

The BCH lower bound on the minimum distances of cyclic codes can be used to construct Euclidean and Hermitian dual-containing codes with a square-root-like lower bound on their minimum distances. Then such codes will be
used to construct self-dual cyclic codes with a square-root-like lower bound on their minimum distances later.

The following result is a special case of Theorem 3 in
[
1
]
and will be needed later.

Theorem 5
.

Let
m
be odd.
Let
\mu
be a proper divisor of
q^{m}-1
and
n=\frac{q^{m}-1}{\mu}>4
.
Put

\delta=\left\lfloor\frac{q^{\frac{m+1}{2}}-q+1}{\mu}\right\rfloor.

Then
{\mathbf{{C}}}_{(q,n,\delta,1,\beta)}
is a Euclidean dual-containing BCH code with minimum distance at least
\delta
.

Proof.

It follows from Lemma
3
that
{\mathrm{ord}}_{n}(q)=m
. The desired conclusion then follows from

[
1
, Theorem 3]
.
∎

For Hermitian dual-containing BCH codes, we have the following result. The proof is similar to the proof of
Theorem

with the help of Lemma

(see

]

Theorem 6
.

Suppose that
q=q_{1}^{2}

\mu

6
Self-dual linear codes over
{\bf F}_{q}
with a square-root-like lower bound

Let
q
be an odd prime power satisfying
q\equiv 1\pmod{4}
throughout this section.
Then in the field
{\bf F}_{q}
,
-1
is a square.
Below we present the following variant of the construction of Theorem
2
.

Theorem 9
.

Let
\lambda\in{\bf F}_{q}
with
\lambda^{2}=-1
. Let
{\bf C}\subseteq{\bf F}_{q}^{n}
be a dual-containing
linear code. Then the set

{\bf C}_{\lambda}({\mathbf{{C}}})=\{{\bf u}|\lambda{\bf u}+{\bf v}:{\bf u}\in{\bf C},{\bf v}\in{\bf C}^{\perp}\}\subseteq{\bf F}_{q}^{2n}

is a
[2n,n]_{q}
self-dual linear code with minimum distance
\min\{d({\bf C}^{\perp}),2d({\bf C})\}
.

Proof.

For two codewords
{\bf c}_{1}=[{\bf u}_{1}|\lambda{\bf u}_{1}+{\bf v}_{1}]
and
{\bf c}_{2}=[{\bf u}_{2}|\lambda{\bf u}_{2}+{\bf v}_{2}]
in this code
{\bf C}_{\lambda}({\mathbf{{C}}})
, their inner product is

<{\bf c}_{1},{\bf c}_{2}>=(\lambda^{2}+1)<{\bf u}_{1},{\bf u}_{2}>+\lambda(<{\bf u}_{1},{\bf v}_{2}>+<{\bf u}_{2},{\bf v}_{1}>)+<{\bf v}_{1},{\bf v}_{2}>.

Since
\lambda^{2}+1=0
and
{\bf C}^{\perp}
is self-orthogonal, this inner product is zero. It is clear that the dimension of
{\bf C}_{\lambda}({\mathbf{{C}}})
is

\dim({\bf C}_{\lambda}({\mathbf{{C}}}))=\dim({\bf C}^{\perp})+\dim({\bf C})=n.

Let
{\bf u}\in{\bf C}
and
{\bf v}\in{\bf C}^{\perp}
. It is clear
that
({\bf u}|\lambda{\bf u}+{\bf v})
is the zero vector if and only both
{\mathbf{u}}
and
{\mathbf{v}}
are the zero vector.
Assume that at least one of
{\mathbf{u}}
and
{\mathbf{v}}
is a nonzero vector. We have then the following cases:

The case that
{\mathbf{u}}={\mathbf{0}}
and
{\mathbf{v}}\neq{\mathbf{0}}
:

In this case, one has

{\mathrm{wt}}({\bf u}|\lambda{\bf u}+{\bf v})={\mathrm{wt}}({\mathbf{v}})\geq d({\mathbf{{C}}}^{\perp}).

The case that
{\mathbf{u}}\neq{\mathbf{0}}
and
{\mathbf{v}}={\mathbf{0}}
:

In this case, one has

{\mathrm{wt}}({\bf u}|\lambda{\bf u}+{\bf v})=2{\mathrm{wt}}({\mathbf{u}})\geq 2d({\mathbf{{C}}}).

The case that
{\mathbf{u}}\neq{\mathbf{0}}
and
{\mathbf{v}}\neq{\mathbf{0}}
:

In this case, one has

\displaystyle{\mathrm{wt}}({\bf u}|\lambda{\bf u}+{\bf v})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathrm{wt}}({\mathbf{u}})+{\mathrm{wt}}(\lambda{\mathbf{u}}+{\mathbf{v}})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 2d({\mathbf{{C}}}).

Clearly, there are two codewords in
{\mathbf{{C}}}_{\lambda}({\mathbf{{C}}})
with Hamming weight
2d({\mathbf{{C}}})
and
d({\mathbf{{C}}}^{\perp})
.
The desired conclusion on the minimum distance of
{\mathbf{{C}}}_{\lambda}({\mathbf{{C}}})
then follows.
∎

Let
m
be odd and
q\equiv 1\pmod{4}

Let

be a proper divisor of
q^{m}-1
and

Then the code
{\mathbf{{C}}}_{\lambda}({\mathbf{{C}}}_{(q,n,\delta,1,\beta)})
is a
[2n,n]_{q}
self-dual linear code with

d({\mathbf{{C}}}_{\lambda}({\mathbf{{C}}}_{(q,n,\delta,1,\beta)}))=\min\{d({\bf C}^{\perp}),2d({\bf C})\}\geq\delta

Proof.

By Theorem
5
,
{\mathbf{{C}}}_{(q,n,\delta,1,\beta)}
is a Euclidean dual-containing BCH code with minimum distance at least
\delta
. The desired conclusions then follow from Theorem
9
.
∎

We have the following remarks regarding the linear code
{\mathbf{{C}}}_{\lambda}({\mathbf{{C}}}_{(q,n,\delta,1,\beta)})
in Theorem

d\geq\delta
on the minimum distance of
the code
{\mathbf{{C}}}_{\lambda}({\mathbf{{C}}}_{(q,n,\delta,1,\beta)})

A family of self-dual binary cyclic codes with a square-root-like lower bound

In this section, we present an infinite family of self-dual binary codes with a square-root-like lower bound.
We first prove the following theorem.

Theorem 11
.

Let
\gcd(q,n)=1
.
Let
{\mathbf{{C}}}
be a cyclic code of length
n
over
{\mathbf{{F}}}_{q}
with generator polynomial

g(x)
. Let
{\mathbf{{T}}}_{g}
be the defining set of
{\mathbf{{C}}}

{\mathbf{{C}}}
is a Euclidean dual-containing cyclic code.

Proof.

Suppose that there is no integer
a
in
{\bf Z}_{n}
such that
both
a
and
n-a
are in the same cyclotomic coset and there is no integer

By definition,

{\mathbf{{C}}}

We would prove that

\displaystyle{\mathbf{{T}}}_{q}\subset({\mathbf{{Z}}}_{n}\setminus{\mathbf{{T}}}_{g})^{-1}.

(6)

Let

such that
both

and

{\mathbf{{T}}}_{g}

{\bf Z}_{n}

Theorem 12
.

Let

be a prime. Let

be the generator polynomial of an odd-like binary

{\mathbf{{C}}}^{\prime}

[2n,n,d\geq\lceil\sqrt{n}\,\rceil]

By definition, the defining set
{\mathbf{{T}}}
of
{\mathbf{{C}}}
is either set of quadratic residues or the set of quadratic nonresidues.
Since

is a prime,
2
is a quadratic residue modulo

All the elements in the

C_{1}
are quadratic residues.
Hence all the elements in each

We then deduce that

It then follows from

Consequently,

It follows from Theorem

9
Conclusions and remarks

The contributions of this paper are summarized as follows.

•

The original van Lint construction of binary cyclic codes was generalized into a
construction of cyclic codes over
{\mathbf{{F}}}_{2^{s}}
(see Theorem
1
). In
addition, a condition in the original van Lint construction of binary cyclic codes
was removed.

•

An infinite family of Euclidean self-dual cyclic codes over
{\mathbf{{F}}}_{q}
for even
q
with
a square-root-like lower bound on their minimum distances was constructed (see
Theorem
7
).

When
\mu=1
and
q\geq 4
, the cyclic code
{\mathbf{{C}}}^{\prime}
in Theorem

has parameters

[2(q^{m}-1),q^{m}-1,d\geq q^{(m+1)/2}-q+1].

In this case, the lower bound

Hence, those codes

the square-root lower bound.

When
\mu=1
and

The two breakthroughs mentioned above are the main contributions of this paper.

The results of this paper showed that there are an infinite family of self-dual binary cyclic codes with the
square-root lower bound on their minimum distances and an infinite family of self-dual nonbinary cyclic codes with a lower bound
on their minimum distances better than the square-root lower bound. Then the following open problem
becomes interesting.

Open Problem 1
.

Is there an infinite family of self-dual binary cyclic codes with a lower bound
on their minimum distances better than the square-root lower bound?

Theorems

9

To construct a self-dual code with a square-root-like lower bound with Theorem

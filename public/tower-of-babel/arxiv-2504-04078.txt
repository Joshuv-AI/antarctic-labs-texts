The rationality problem for multinorm one tori
Sumito Hasegawa, Kazuki Kanai, Yasuhiro Oki

Abstract
. In this paper, we study the rationality problem for multinorm one tori, a natural generalization of norm one tori. For multinorm one tori that split over finite Galois extensions with nilpotent Galois group, we prove that stable rationality and retract rationality are equivalent, and give a criterion for the validity of the above two conditions. This generalizes the result of Endo (2011) on the rationality problem for norm one tori. To accomplish it, we introduce a generalization of character groups of multinorm one tori. Moreover, we establish systematic reduction methods originating in work of Endo (2001) for an investigation of the rationality problem for arbitrary multinorm one tori. In addition, we provide a new example for which the multinorm principle holds.

1.
Introduction

Let
k
be a field and
k^{\sep}
a fixed separable closure of
k
.
In algebraic geometry, a fundamental problem is to determine whether a given algebraic variety over
k
is rational, that is, birationally equivalent to a projective space over
k
.
It is also important to determine stable rationality, retract rationality, and unirationality, which are weaker notions of rationality.
These properties satisfy:

rational
\Rightarrow
stably rational
\Rightarrow

retract rational
\Rightarrow
unirational.

We remark that the direction of the implication cannot be reversed.
See Section
2
for more details.

The multinorm one tori primarily studied in this paper are algebraic tori.
We recall that an algebraic torus over
k
is a group
k
-scheme
T
that satisfies

T\otimes_{k}k^{\sep}\cong(\mathbb{G}_{m,k^{\sep}})^{n}
for some non-negative integer
n
.
Note that an algebraic
k
-torus
T
is always unirational
(see
[
Vos98
, p. 40, Example 21]
)
and Voskresenskii conjectured that stably rational tori are rational (see
[
Vos98
, p. 68]
).
In this paper, we focus on the study of stable and retract rationality.

The rationality problem is well-understood for tori of small dimensions.
It is known by Voskresenskii
[
Vos67
]
that all tori of dimension
2
are rational. Moreover, Kunyavskii
[
Kun90
]
solved the rationality problem for
3
-dimensional algebraic
k
-tori.
After that, Hoshi–Yamasaki
[
HY17
]
classified algebraic
k
-tori of dimensions
4
and
5
that are stably rational (resp. retract rational).

In this paper, we restrict our attention to the stable and retract rationality of
multinorm one tori
.
Let
\mathbf{K}
be a finite étale algebra over
k
, that is, a finite product of finite separable field extensions of
k
which are contained in
k^{\sep}
. Then, we set

T_{\mathbf{K}/k}:=\Ker(\N_{\mathbf{K}/k}\colon\Res_{\mathbf{K}/k}\mathbb{G}_{m}\rightarrow\mathbb{G}_{m}),

where
\Res_{\mathbf{K}/k}
is the Weil restriction.
We call it the multinorm one torus associated to
\mathbf{K}/k
. If
\mathbf{K}
is a field, then
T_{\mathbf{K}/k}
is called the
norm one torus
. Note that
T_{\mathbf{K}/k}
has dimension
\dim_{k}(\mathbf{K})-1
, and splits over the Galois closure of the composite field of all factors of
\mathbf{K}
. This means that there is an isomorphism of
L
-tori
T_{\mathbf{K}/k}\otimes_{k}L\cong\mathbb{G}_{m,L}^{\dim_{k}(\mathbf{K})-1}
, where
\mathbf{K}
is the product of finite separable field extensions
K_{1},\ldots,K_{r}
of
k
, and
L
is the Galois closure of the composite field of
K_{1},\ldots,K_{r}
over
k
.

However, less is known about the rationality problem for multinorm one tori than about norm one tori.

]

As a motivation for studying the rationality problem for multinorm one tori,
it is expected that this problem has applications to the rationality problem for norm one tori.
Consider the norm one torus
T_{K/k}
associated to a finite separable field extension
K/k
.
Let
K^{\prime}/k
be a finite separable field extension.
By definition, there is an isomorphism of
K^{\prime}
-tori

T_{K/k}\otimes_{k}K^{\prime}\cong T_{(K\otimes_{k}K^{\prime})/K^{\prime}}.

Here
K\otimes_{k}K^{\prime}
may not be a finite separable field extension of
K^{\prime}
; however, it is a finite étale algebra over
K^{\prime}
.
This means that multinorm one tori appear by taking base change of norm one tori.
Moreover, if
T_{(K\otimes_{k}K^{\prime})/K^{\prime}}
is
not
rational (resp. stably rational; retract rational) over
K^{\prime}
, then one can prove that
T_{K/k}
is so over
k
. In fact, this approach is used in the proof of
[
End11
,
[
I
]
]
to obtain the non-retract rationality of some norm one tori.

The main theorem of Endo (
[
End01
]
) can be stated in our notation as follows:

Theorem 1.1

(
[
End01
, Theorem 2]
)
.

Let
p
be a prime,
k
a field,
\mathbf{K}=\prod_{i=1}^{r}K_{i}^{h_{i}}
a finite étale

-algebra with

and

, and

the Galois closure of the composite field of
K_{1},\ldots,K_{r}
over
k
.
Assume

•

\Gal(L/k)
is an elementary

-abelian group;

•

K_{i}\neq K_{j}
for any
i,j\in\{1,\ldots,r\}
with
i\neq j
; and

•

[K_{i}:k]=p
for any
i\in\{1,\ldots,r\}
.

(1)

If
p\neq 2
, then the following are equivalent:

(i)

T_{\mathbf{K}/k}
is stably rational over
k
;

(ii)

T_{\mathbf{K}/k}
is retract rational over
k
;

(iii)

.

(2)

If

(i)

T_{\mathbf{K}/k}
is stably rational over

;

(ii)

T_{\mathbf{K}/k}
is retract rational over

Theorem

Moreover, it naturally raises the question of how this extends to more general cases.

1.1.
Main theorems

Our first main theorems generalize Theorem
1.1
to the case where
\Gal(L/k)
is a
p
-group.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
p
be an odd prime number,
k
a field,
\mathbf{K}=\prod_{i=1}^{r}K_{i}
a finite étale
k
-algebra with
r\geq 1
,
and
L
the Galois closure of the composite field of
K_{1},\ldots,K_{r}
over
k
.
Assume

•

K_{i}\not\subset K_{j}
for any
i,j\in\{1,\ldots,r\}
with
i\neq j
; and

•

[L:k]
is a power of
p
.

Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

T_{\mathbf{K}/k}
is stably rational over
k
;

(ii)

T_{\mathbf{K}/k}
is retract rational over
k
;

(iii)

r=1
and
L
is cyclic over
k
.

We denote by

the dihedral group of order

, that is,

D_{n}:=\langle\sigma_{n},\tau_{n}\mid\sigma_{n}^{n}=\tau_{n}^{2}=1,\tau_{n}\sigma_{n}\tau_{n}^{-1}=\sigma_{n}^{-1}\rangle.

Note that there is an isomorphism
D_{2}\cong(C_{2})^{2}
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
k
be a field,
\mathbf{K}=\prod_{i=1}^{r}K_{i}
a finite étale
k
-algebra with
r\geq 1
,
and
L
the Galois closure of the composite field of
K_{1},\ldots,K_{r}
over
k
.
Assume

•

all conjugates of
K_{i}
are not contained in
K_{j}
for any
i,j\in\{1,\ldots,r\}
with
i\neq j
; and

•

[L:k]
is a power of
2
.

Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

T_{\mathbf{K}/k}
is stably rational over
k
;

(ii)

T_{\mathbf{K}/k}
is retract rational over
k
;

(iii)

\mathbf{K}
satisfies
(iii-a)
or
(iii-b)
as follows:

(iii-a)

r=1
and
L
is cyclic over
k
;

(iii-b)

r=2
and there exist
\nu\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
and an isomorphism
\Gal(L/k)\cong D_{2^{\nu}}
that induces
\Gal(L/K_{1})\cong\langle\sigma_{2^{\nu}}^{2m_{1}}\tau_{2^{\nu}}\rangle
and
\Gal(L/K_{2})\cong\langle\sigma_{2^{\nu}}^{2m_{2}+1}\tau_{2^{\nu}}\rangle
for some
m_{1},m_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Remark 1.4
.

In the general setup we allow
\mathbf{K}=\prod_{i=1}^{r}K_{i}^{h_{i}}
with
h_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, and
K_{j}
may contain a conjugate of
K_{i}
for some
i,j
with
i\neq j
.
However, by Proposition
3.18
, we may drop multiplicities and remove any factor that contains another factor up to conjugacy.
Accordingly, we state Theorem
1.3
in this reduced form.

The main theorem in the nilpotent case, which will be presented as Theorem
1.5
, follows from Theorem
1.2
and Theorem
1.3
. As we will explain later, the proof is non-trivial.

We prepare some notions to state Theorem
1.5
. By a
quasi-trivial torus
(or an
induced torus
) over
k
, we mean a
k
-torus that is isomorphic to
\Res_{\mathbf{K}/k}\mathbb{G}_{m}
for some finite étale algebra
\mathbf{K}
over

. The notion of quasi-trivial tori is introduced in
[
CS77
, §2, p. 187]
.
In addition, a
subalgebra
of
\mathbf{K}
refers to a finite product
\prod_{j=1}^{s}K^{\prime}_{i_{j}}
, where
1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{s}\leq r
are integers and
K^{\prime}_{i_{j}}
is an intermediate field of
K_{i_{j}}/k
for each
j\in\{1,\ldots,s\}
.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
k
be a field,
\mathbf{K}=\prod_{i=1}^{r}K_{i}
a finite étale
k
-algebra with
r\geq 1
, and
L
the Galois closure of the composite field of
K_{1},\ldots,K_{r}
over
k
.
Assume

•

\Gal(L/k)
is nilpotent.

Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

T_{\mathbf{K}/k}
is stably rational over
k
;

(ii)

T_{\mathbf{K}/k}
is retract rational over
k
;

(iii)

there exists an isomorphism of
k
-tori

T_{\mathbf{K}/k}\times S\cong T_{\mathbf{K}^{\prime}/k}\times S^{\prime},

where
S
and
S^{\prime}
are quasi-trivial tori over
k
, and
\mathbf{K}^{\prime}=\prod_{i=1}^{r^{\prime}}K^{\prime}_{i}
is a finite product of intermediate fields
K^{\prime}_{i}
of
L/k
that satisfies
(iii-a)
or
(iii-b)
as follows:

(iii-a)

r^{\prime}=1
and
L^{\prime}/k
is cyclic over
k
;

(iii-b)

r^{\prime}=2
and there exist
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}\setminus 2\mathbb{Z}
,
\nu\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
and an isomorphism
\Gal(L^{\prime}/k)\cong C_{m}\times D_{2^{\nu}}
that induces
\Gal(L^{\prime}/K^{\prime}_{1})\cong\langle\sigma_{2^{\nu}}^{2m_{1}}\tau_{2^{\nu}}\rangle
and
\Gal(L^{\prime}/K^{\prime}_{2})\cong\langle\sigma_{2^{\nu}}^{2m_{2}+1}\tau_{2^{\nu}}\rangle
for some
m_{1},m_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Here,
L^{\prime}
is the Galois closure of the composite field of

over
k
.

The condition (iii) implies that

and

.

Remark 1.6
.

We also give an explicit construction of
\mathbf{K}^{\prime}
in Theorem
1.5
(iii) using group theory. See Lemma
8.3
.

Remark 1.7
.

Theorem
1.5
in the case

is a consequence of the results of Endo–Miyata (
[
EM75
, Theorem 1.5, Theorem 2.3]
) and Endo (
[
End11
, Theorem 2.1]

For a norm one torus associated with a non-Galois extension

On the other hand, by extending the scope to multinorm tori, we obtain a new stably rational family as in (iii-b) of Theorem

The following is a byproduct of our proof of Theorem

Theorem 1.8
.

Let
k
be a field, and
\mathbf{K}=K_{1}\times K_{2}
a finite étale algebra over
k
. Assume that

•

K_{1}K_{2}/k
is Galois with Galois group

for some
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
;

•

[K_{1}K_{2}:K_{i}]=2
and

is non-Galois for each

; and

•

K_{1}
and
K_{2}
are not conjugate to each other.

Then the multinorm one torus

is stably rational over
k
.

Our proofs of the main theorems are based on the study of
character groups
of the corresponding tori. Let
T
be a torus over
k
. Then the character group of
T
is defined as follows:

X^{*}(T):=\Hom_{k^{\sep}\text{-groups}}(T\otimes_{k}k^{\sep},\mathbb{G}_{m,k^{\sep}}).

Take a finite Galois extension
L/k
with Galois group
G
over which
T
splits, which is possible in any case. Then
X^{*}(T)
is a
G
-lattice, that is, a finitely generated free abelian group equipped with an action of
G
. On the other hand, there exist two notions for
G
-lattices:
quasi-permutation
and
quasi-invertible
. One can confirm the following:

\displaystyle T\textrm{ is stably rational over }k\Rightarrow T\textrm{ is retract rational over }k

\displaystyle\Updownarrow\hskip 150.0pt\Updownarrow\hskip 60.0pt

\displaystyle X^{*}(T)\textrm{ is quasi-permutation}\Rightarrow X^{*}(T)\textrm{ is quasi-invertible. }

Moreover, we can determine whether a
G
-lattice

is quasi-permutation or quasi-invertible by using a
flabby resolution
of

. This efficient technique was introduced by Endo–Miyata
[
EM75
]
and Voskresenskii
[
Vos69
]
, and further developed by Colliot-Thélène–Sansuc
[

]
.
The details of this will be discussed in Section
2
. In particular, our theorems are reduced to determining whether a
G
-lattice
J_{G/\mathcal{H}}
is quasi-permutation or quasi-invertible, in the case where

is a finite nilpotent group. Here,
\mathcal{H}
is a multiset of subgroups of

, and

is a

-lattice which fits into the exact sequence

0\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}\xrightarrow{(\varepsilon_{G/H}^{\circ})_{H\in\mathcal{H}}}\bigoplus_{H\in\mathcal{H}}\mathbb{Z}[G/H]\rightarrow J_{G/\mathcal{H}}\rightarrow 0.

See Section
3.1
for details.

We first give a sketch of our proof of Theorem
1.2
, which is in the case that

is a

-group with

. Then, one can construct an inductive argument by considering the restriction of

to a certain maximal subgroup of

is a

in this paper (see Section

1.2.
Application to multinorm principle

As another motivation for studying the rationality problem for multinorm one tori, we discuss their applications to the multinorm principle.

Here we assume that
k
is a global field. For a finite étale algebra
\mathbf{K}
over
k
, let

\Sha(\mathbf{K}/k):=(\N_{K/k}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbf{K}}^{\times})\cap k^{\times})/\N_{\mathbf{K}/k}(\mathbf{K}^{\times}).

Here,
\mathbb{A}_{\mathbf{K}}^{\times}
is the product of the idèle groups of all the factors of
\mathbf{K}
.
We say that the
multinorm principle holds for
K/k
if

\Sha(\mathbf{K}/k)=1.

This question has also been the subject of extensive study, for example,
[
Hür84
]
,
[
DW14
]
,
[
BLP19
]
,
[
Lee22
]
,
[
HHLY24
]
, and
[
LOY24
]
.

The study of
\Sha(\mathbf{K}/k)
in the case that
\mathbf{K}
is a field is one of the classic problems in algebraic number theory. For example,
\Sha(K/k)=1
holds for any finite cyclic extension
K/k
. This result is known as Hasse’s norm theorem (
[
Has31
, Satz, p. 64]
).

Theorem 1.9
.

Let
k
be a global field, and
\mathbf{K}=K_{1}\times K_{2}
a finite étale algebra over
k
. We further assume that

(a)

K_{1}K_{2}
is Galois over
k
; and

(b)

\Gal(K_{1}K_{2}/k)\cong C_{m}\times D_{2^{\nu}}
with
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}\setminus 2\mathbb{Z}
and
\nu\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
; and

(c)

the isomorphism in
(b)
induces
\Gal(K_{1}K_{2}/K_{i})\cong\{1\}\times\langle\sigma_{2^{\nu}}^{2m_{i}+i}\tau_{2^{\nu}}\rangle
with
m_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
for each
i\in\{1,2\}
.

Then, we have
\Sha(\mathbf{K}/k)=1
.

Theorem
1.9
gives a new example of the validity of the multinorm principle. It follows from Theorem
1.5
together with the standard argument on the multinorm principle. Note that our proof uses an extension of Ono’s theorem (
[
Ono63
]
), that is, a connection between
\Sha(\mathbf{K}/k)
and the Tate–Shafarevich group of
T_{\mathbf{K}/k}
.
See Section
9
for more details.

2.
Basic facts on the rationality of tori

Let
k
be a field. For a non-negative integer
n
, we denote by
\mathbb{P}_{k}^{n}
the projective space of dimension
n
over
k
. Consider an algebraic variety
X
over
k
. We say that
X
is

•

rational
over
k
if it is birationally equivalent to a projective space over
k
;

•

stably rational
over
k
if
X\times_{k}\mathbb{P}_{k}^{m}
is rational over
k
for some
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
;

•

retract rational
over
k
if there exist rational maps
f\colon\mathbb{P}_{k}^{n}\dashrightarrow X
and
g\colon X\dashrightarrow\mathbb{P}_{k}^{n}
with
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
such that
f\circ g=\id_{X}
;

•

unirational
over
k
if there is a dominant rational map from
\mathbb{P}_{k}^{n}
to
X
for some
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.

The notion of retract rationality was originally introduced by Saltman (
[
Sal84
]
) in the case where
k
is infinite (see also
[
Kan12
]
). It has been generalized for all varieties over arbitrary fields by Merkurjev (
[
Mer17
]
). Note that one has implications

rational
\Rightarrow
stably rational
\Rightarrow

retract rational
\Rightarrow
unirational.

In this paper, we concentrate on the case where
X
is an algebraic torus over
k
. Fix a separable closure
k^{\sep}
of
k
. Then, we can rephrase the stable and retract rationality of algebraic tori by means of

[

]

Definition 2.1

(
[

, §1]
)
.

Let

be a finite group. We say that a

-lattice

is

(i)

if

has a

-basis permuted by

, that is,

for some subgroups

(ii)

if there is an exact sequence of

-lattices

where

and

are permutation;

(iii)

if it is a direct summand of a quasi-permutation

-lattice.

It is not difficult to confirm that

\Rightarrow

\Rightarrow

Definition 2.2

(
[
Lor05
, §§2.3–2.5]
)
.

Let
G
be a finite group. We say that a
G
-lattice

is

(i)

stably permutation
if
M\oplus R\cong R^{\prime}
for some permutation

-lattices

and

;

(ii)

invertible
(or,
permutation projective
) if it is a direct summand of a permutation
G
-lattice;

(iii)

coflabby
if
H^{1}(H,M)=0
for any subgroup
H
of
G
;

(iv)

flabby
if

is coflabby.

It is known that the following hold:

permutation
\Rightarrow
stably permutation
\Rightarrow
invertible
\Rightarrow
flabby and coflabby.

Here the rightmost implication is a consequence of
[
Len74
, (1.1) Proposition]
.

Let
G
be a finite group. We say that
G
-lattices
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
are

if there exist permutation
G
-lattices
R_{1}
and

such that

. We denote by
\mathscr{S}(G)
the set of similarity classes of

-lattices. For a

-lattice

, we write

for the similarity class containing

. Then

is a commutative monoid with respect to the sum

[M_{1}]+[M_{2}]:=[M_{1}\oplus M_{2}].

By definition, for a
G
-lattice

, we have

•

[M]=0
in

if and only if

is stably permutation; and

•

[M]
is invertible in
\mathscr{S}(G)
if and only if

is invertible.

Definition 2.3

(
[
Lor05
, §2.6]
)
.

Let
G
be a finite group, and

a

-lattice.

(i)

A
coflabby resolution
of

is an exact sequence of
G
-lattices

0\rightarrow U\rightarrow R\rightarrow M\rightarrow 0,

where

is permutation and

is coflabby.

(ii)

A

of

is an exact sequence of
G
-lattices

where

is permutation and

is flabby.

There is a coflabby resolution for any

-lattice; see
[

, Lemma 1.1]
.
This implies the existence of a flabby resolution of every

-lattice. Moreover, if

is a flabby resolution of

, then the class
[F]
in

depends only on

. In the sequel, we denote

by

. It is known that the map

\mathscr{S}(G)\rightarrow\mathscr{S}(G);\,[M]\mapsto[M]^{\fl}

is an endomorphism of monoids, i.e.
[M_{1}\oplus M_{2}]^{\fl}=[M_{1}]^{\fl}+[M_{2}]^{\fl}
.
In particular, we obtain implications as follows:

\Rightarrow
quasi-permutation, invertible
\Rightarrow

Lemma 2.4

(
[
Lor05
, Lemma 2.7.1 (a)]
; cf.
[

, (1.2) Proposition]
)
.

Let
G
be a finite group, and
F
an invertible

-lattice. Then we have

[F]^{\fl}=-[F].

The next proposition is not difficult; for the reader’s convenience, we include a proof since a precise reference appears to be lacking.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
G
be a finite group.

(i)

A
G
-lattice
M
is quasi-permutation if and only if
[M]^{\fl}=0
.

(ii)

A
G
-lattice
M
is quasi-invertible if and only if
[M]^{\fl}
is invertible.

Proof.

(i): It is clear that
[M]^{\fl}=0
if
M
is quasi-permutation. For the reverse implication, assume
[M]^{\fl}=0
. Take a flabby resolution of
M
:

0\rightarrow M\rightarrow R\xrightarrow{\pi}F\rightarrow 0.

By assumption,
F
is stably permutation. Hence, there is a permutation
G
-lattice
R^{\prime}
such that
F\oplus R^{\prime}
is permutation. Moreover, the sequence

0\rightarrow M\xrightarrow{x\mapsto(\iota(x),0)}R\oplus R^{\prime}\xrightarrow{\pi\oplus\id_{R^{\prime}}}F\oplus R^{\prime}\rightarrow 0

is exact. This implies that
M
is quasi-permutation as desired.

(ii): We first prove that
[M]^{\fl}
is invertible if
M
is quasi-invertible.
By assumption, there is a
G
-lattice
M^{\prime}
such that
M\oplus M^{\prime}
is quasi-permutation. Combining this result with (i), we obtain

[M]^{\fl}+[M^{\prime}]^{\fl}=[M\oplus M^{\prime}]^{\fl}=[0].

Hence
[M]^{\fl}
is invertible.

On the other hand, assume that
[M]^{\fl}
is invertible. Take a flabby resolution

0\rightarrow M\rightarrow R\rightarrow F\rightarrow 0

of
M
, where
F
is invertible by assumption. Since
F
is invertible, we have
[F]^{\fl}=-[F]
according to Lemma
2.4
. In particular, we obtain an equality

[M\oplus F]^{\fl}=[0].

This is equivalent to the condition that
M\oplus F
is quasi-permutation, which follows from (i). This completes the proof.

\blacksquare

3.
Multinorm one tori and their character groups

3.1.
Multinorm one tori

Let
k
be a field. Consider a finite étale algebra
\mathbf{K}=\prod_{i=1}^{r}K_{i}
over
k
, that is, a finite product of finite separable subextensions of
k^{\sep}
. The
multinorm one torus
associated to
\mathbf{K}/k
is defined as

T_{\mathbf{K}/k}=\Ker(\N_{\mathbf{K}/k}\colon\Res_{\mathbf{K}/k}\mathbb{G}_{m}\rightarrow\mathbb{G}_{m}).

Let
G
be a finite group, and
H
a subgroup of
G
. Then one has a surjection

\varepsilon_{G/H}\colon\mathbb{Z}[G/H]\rightarrow\mathbb{Z};\,\sum_{g\in G/H}a_{g}g\mapsto\sum_{g\in G/H}a_{g},

which is called the
augmentation map
. Moreover, the dual of
\varepsilon_{G/H}
coincides with the homomorphism

\varepsilon_{G/H}^{\circ}\colon\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}[G/H];\,1\mapsto\sum_{g\in G/H}g.

On the other hand, consider a finite group
G
and subgroups
H^{\prime}\subset H
of
G
. Then the homomorphisms of
H
-lattices

\varepsilon_{H/H^{\prime}}\colon\mathbb{Z}[H/H^{\prime}]\rightarrow\mathbb{Z},\quad\varepsilon_{H/H^{\prime}}^{\circ}\colon\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}[H/H^{\prime}]

induce homomorphisms of
G
-lattices

\Ind_{H}^{G}\varepsilon_{H/H^{\prime}}\colon\mathbb{Z}[G/H^{\prime}]\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}[G/H],\quad\Ind_{H}^{G}\varepsilon_{H/H^{\prime}}^{\circ}\colon\mathbb{Z}[G/H]\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}[G/H^{\prime}].

For a multiset
\mathcal{H}
of subgroups of
G
, we define a
G
-module
I_{G/\mathcal{H}}
by an exact sequence

0\rightarrow I_{G/\mathcal{H}}\rightarrow\bigoplus_{H\in\mathcal{H}}\mathbb{Z}[G/H]\xrightarrow{(\varepsilon_{G/H})_{H\in\mathcal{H}}}\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow 0.

Furthermore, we define
J_{G/\mathcal{H}}:=I_{G/\mathcal{H}}^{\circ}
. Then one has an exact sequence

0\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}\xrightarrow{(\varepsilon_{G/H}^{\circ})_{H\in\mathcal{H}}}\bigoplus_{H\in\mathcal{H}}\mathbb{Z}[G/H]\rightarrow J_{G/\mathcal{H}}\rightarrow 0.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
k
be a field,
\mathbf{K}=\prod_{i=1}^{r}K_{i}
a finite étale algebra over
k
. Take a finite Galois extension
L
of
k
containing
K_{1},\ldots,K_{r}
. Put
G:=\Gal(L/k)
and
\mathcal{H}:=\{\Gal(L/K_{i})\mid i\in\{1,\ldots,r\}\}
. Then there are isomorphisms of
G
-modules

(3.1)

X_{*}(T_{\mathbf{K}/k})\cong I_{G/\mathcal{H}},\quad X^{*}(T_{\mathbf{K}/k})\cong J_{G/\mathcal{H}}.

In particular,
T_{\mathbf{K}/k}
is stably (resp. retract) rational over
k
if and only if the
G
-lattice
J_{G/\mathcal{H}}
is quasi-permutation (resp. quasi-invertible).

Proof.

The isomorphisms (
3.1
) follow from the construction of
I_{G/\mathcal{H}}
and
J_{G/\mathcal{H}}
. The equivalence between
T_{\mathbf{K}/k}
being stably rational (resp. retract rational) and
J_{G/\mathcal{H}}
being quasi-permutation (resp. quasi-invertible) is a consequence of Proposition
2.11
.

\blacksquare

In this paper, we determine whether the
G
-lattice
J_{G/\mathcal{H}}
is quasi-permutation or quasi-invertible in order to classify the stable and retract rationality of multinorm one tori.
In what follows, we discuss

•

how to reduce the problem to a smaller
G
-lattice (Corollary
3.14
, Proposition
3.18
,
Proposition
3.19
, Corollary
3.21
);

•

behavior of

and

with respect to the restriction to subgroups of
G
(Proposition
3.22
); and

•

description of

and

•

•

.

.

.

.

H_{2}:=\langle\tau\rangle
. We may assume
\mathcal{H}=\{H_{1},H_{2}\}
. Let
I=I_{G/\mathcal{H}}
and
J=J_{G/\mathcal{H}}
. We regard

as a sublattice of
\mathbb{Z}[G/H_{1}]\oplus\mathbb{Z}[G/H_{2}]
. Then one has an exact sequence

(6.15)

0\rightarrow I\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}[G/H_{1}]\oplus\mathbb{Z}[G/H_{2}]\xrightarrow{(\varepsilon_{G/H_{1}},\,\varepsilon_{G/H_{2}})}\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow 0.

Moreover, we have

I=\langle(1-\tau,0),(0,1-\sigma),(0,\sigma(1-\sigma)),(0,\sigma^{2}(1-\sigma)),(1,-1)\rangle_{\mathbb{Z}}.

In particular, the following hold:

\displaystyle I^{\langle\sigma^{2}\rangle}=\langle(1-\tau,0),(1+\tau,-(1+\sigma^{2})),(1+\tau,-\sigma(1+\sigma^{2}))\rangle_{\mathbb{Z}},

\displaystyle I^{\langle\tau\rangle}=\langle(0,1-\sigma),(0,\sigma(1-\sigma)),(0,\sigma^{2}(1-\sigma)),(1+\tau,-(1+\sigma^{2}))\rangle_{\mathbb{Z}},

\displaystyle I^{\langle\sigma^{2}\tau\rangle}=I^{\langle\sigma^{2},\tau\rangle}=\langle(1+\tau,-(1+\sigma^{2})),(1+\tau,-\sigma(1+\sigma^{2}))\rangle_{\mathbb{Z}}=\mathbb{Z}[G/\langle\sigma^{2},\tau\rangle](1+\tau,-(1+\sigma^{2})),

\displaystyle I^{\langle\sigma\rangle}=\langle(1-\tau,0),(2(1+\tau),-(1+\sigma+\sigma^{2}+\sigma^{3}))\rangle_{\mathbb{Z}},

\displaystyle I^{\langle\sigma\tau\rangle}=I^{G}=\langle(2(1+\tau),-(1+\sigma+\sigma^{2}+\sigma^{3}))\rangle_{\mathbb{Z}}.

Now, consider homomorphisms of
G
-lattices as follows:

\displaystyle f_{1}\colon\mathbb{Z}[G]\rightarrow I

\displaystyle;\,1\mapsto(1,-1),

\displaystyle f_{2}\colon\mathbb{Z}[G/\langle\tau\rangle]\rightarrow I

\displaystyle;\,1\mapsto(0,1-\sigma),

\displaystyle f_{3}\colon\mathbb{Z}[G/\langle\sigma^{2},\tau\rangle]\rightarrow I

\displaystyle;\,1\mapsto(1+\tau,-(1+\sigma^{2})),

\displaystyle f_{4}\colon\mathbb{Z}[G/\langle\sigma\rangle]\rightarrow I

\displaystyle;\,1\mapsto(1-\tau,0).

Then the sum of these maps induces a coflabby resolution

0\rightarrow U\rightarrow R\xrightarrow{f}I\rightarrow 0

of

which admits an isomorphism

(6.16)

U^{\langle\sigma^{2}\tau\rangle}\cong\mathbb{Z}[G/\langle\sigma^{2}\tau\rangle]\oplus\mathbb{Z}[G/\langle\sigma^{2},\tau\rangle]\oplus\mathbb{Z}.

Now, suppose that
J
is quasi-invertible. Since
\rk_{\mathbb{Z}}(U)=11
and
\rk_{\mathbb{Z}}(U^{G})=3
, there exist a subgroup
H
of
G
of order
4
and an isomorphism

In particular, we obtain an isomorphism

\widehat{H}^{0}(G,U)\cong\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}/8\mathbb{Z}.

However, since
\widehat{H}^{0}(G,R)\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\oplus(\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z})^{\oplus 2}
, the same argument as Claim in Proposition

implies that the exponent of

is a divisor of

. Hence, we obtain a contradiction, and the proof is complete.

\blacksquare

Lemma 7.8
.

Let
p
be a prime number, and
G
a
p
-group. Fix
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
. Consider a multiset
\mathcal{H}
of subgroups of
G
satisfying

. Suppose

(i)

H\triangleleft G
and
G/H\cong C_{p^{m}}
for every
H\in\mathcal{H}
; and

(ii)

every maximal subgroup of

contains an element of
\mathcal{H}
.

Then the group
G
is isomorphic to a finite direct product of copies of

.

Proof.

We first note that (i) implies that
G
is abelian of exponent a divisor of
p^{m}
. Assume that the conclusion fails. Then there is an element
g\in G
whose order is smaller than
p^{m}
so that
g\neq h^{p}
for all
h\in G
. Take a maximal subgroup
P
of
G
which does not contain

and
H\in\mathcal{H}_{\subset P}
. Then the image
\overline{g}
of

in

must be a generator. However, we have
\overline{g}^{p^{m-1}}=1
since
g^{p^{m-1}}=1
. This contradicts the cyclicity of

, and hence the proof is complete.

\blacksquare

Theorem 7.9
.

Let
G
be a
2
-group, and
\mathcal{H}
a reduced set of its subgroups satisfying

,

,

and
\mathcal{H}^{\nor}=\mathcal{H}

-lattice

is not quasi-invertible.

Proof.

By Proposition
7.7
, we may assume
\mu(\mathcal{H})=4
.

Step 1.
Here, we prove the assertion in the case where all maximal subgroups of
G
contain a member of
\mathcal{H}
. By Proposition
7.1
, we may further assume that

is cyclic for any

. Then Lemma

gives an isomorphism

for some

. Take a maximal subgroup

of

, which is possible by assumption. Then it is clear that

In the following, we shall give

and
(P:P\cap H_{0})=4
. Fix elements

Now, let

. We prove that this

satisfies the desired properties. There exist

Hence we obtain

implies that the

.

Step 2.
Let

. If

, then the claim is contained in Theorem

Now, by the induction hypothesis, the

.
\blacksquare

7.3.
Third step: The case admitting normal factors

Theorem 7.10
.

Let
G
be a
2
-group, and
\mathcal{H}
a reduced set of subgroups of

\mathcal{H}^{\nor}

(i)

(ii)

J_{G/\mathcal{H}}

G

(iii)

G
and

(iii-1)

(iii-2)

Proof.

We may assume that
\mathcal{H}
contains at least two elements. In particular,

is not cyclic according to Lemma
5.7
. Moreover, we may assume
M(\mathcal{H})>2
, which is a consequence of Proposition
5.3
. Write
\#G=2^{\nu+1}
, which satisfies
\nu\geq 2
. It suffices to prove that

is not quasi-invertible if

or

We give a proof of the above assertion by induction on
\nu

\nu=2

. Now suppose
\nu\geq 3
and the assertion holds for
\nu-1

5.10
implies that the assertion follows from the induction hypothesis. Hence, we may further assume

. By the induction hypothesis, it suffices to prove that there is a maximal subgroup
P
of

which satisfies

or

Case 1.
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}^{\nor}
.

By Theorem

, we may assume

. Take
H_{0}\in\mathcal{H}
with
(G:H_{0})=M(\mathcal{H})
. Using the same argument as Case 1 in the proof of Theorem

, there is a maximal subgroup
P
of

satisfying

and

. Then we have

. Hence, the inequality

.

Case 2.
\mathcal{H}^{\nor}
and
\mathcal{H}\setminus\mathcal{H}^{\nor}
are non-empty.

Note that one has
M(\mathcal{H})\geq 4
by Proposition
4.1
. Consider positive integers as follows:

(7.2)

\Sigma:=\min\{(G:H)\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}\mid H\in\mathcal{H}^{\nor}\},\quad\sigma:=\min\{(G:H)\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}\mid H\in\mathcal{H}\setminus\mathcal{H}^{\nor}\}.

Take
H_{0}\in\mathcal{H}^{\nor}
with
(G:H_{0})=\Sigma
and
H_{1}\in\mathcal{H}\setminus\mathcal{H}^{\nor}
with
(G:H_{1})=\sigma
.

Case 2-a.
\Sigma<\sigma
.

Pick a maximal subgroup
P
of
G
containing

. Then we have

and
gH_{1}g^{-1}\not\supset P\cap H_{0}
for any

. Hence
\mathcal{H}_{P}^{\red}
has at least

Case 2-b.
\Sigma>\sigma
.

Take a maximal subgroup
P
of
G
containing
H_{0}
. Then one has

and
H_{0}\not\supset P\cap H_{1}
. Hence, there exists an element of

that contains

. In particular, one has

. On the other hand,
\Sigma>\sigma
implies

Case 2-c.
\Sigma=\sigma
.

Let
P
be a maximal subgroup of
G
containing

\mathcal{H}_{P}^{\red}
is not a subset of

since

is not normal in

is contained in

H_{0}
is normal of index
\Sigma=\sigma=\mu(\mathcal{H})
, it is contained in

H_{0}
is an element of

\blacksquare

Lemma 7.11
.

Let
G
be a finite group,
H
a subgroup of
G
, and
E
an abelian normal subgroup of
G
. For

, assume

in

. Then we have

By assumption, we have

for some

. This implies an equality

Now, the right-hand side equals

\blacksquare

Lemma 7.13
.

be a

H_{0}N_{1}
, which follows from
H_{0}\not\subset H_{1}
. Now we consider the multiset
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}
of subgroups of
G
which satisfies

•

\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}^{\set}=\mathcal{H}\cup\{H_{0}N_{1},H_{1}\cap H_{0}N_{1}\}
;

•

m_{\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}}(H_{0}N_{1})=2
; and

•

m_{\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}}(H)=1
for each
H\in\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}^{\set}\setminus\{H_{0}N_{1}\}
.

Moreover, we write
\varphi
for the weight function on
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}
defined as follows:

\varphi(H)=\begin{cases}((H_{0}N_{1}:H_{0}),(H_{0}N_{1}:H_{1}\cap H_{0}N_{1}))&\text{if $H=H_{0}N_{1}$ and $\#H_{0}>\#(H_{1}\cap H_{0}N_{1})$;}\\
((H_{0}N_{1}:H_{1}\cap H_{0}N_{1}),(H_{0}N_{1}:H_{0}))&\text{if $H=H_{0}N_{1}$ and $\#H_{0}\leq\#(H_{1}\cap H_{0}N_{1})$; }\\
1&\text{otherwise. }\end{cases}

Then Proposition
3.19
gives an isomorphism of
G
-lattices

J_{G/\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}}^{(\varphi)}\cong\begin{cases}J_{G/\mathcal{H}}\oplus\mathbb{Z}[G/H_{0}N_{1}]^{\oplus 2}&\text{if }H_{1}\subset H_{0}N_{1};\\
J_{G/\mathcal{H}}\oplus\mathbb{Z}[G/H_{0}N_{1}]^{\oplus 2}\oplus\mathbb{Z}[G/(H_{1}\cap H_{0}N_{1})]&\text{if }H_{1}\not\subset H_{0}N_{1}.\end{cases}

On the other hand, since
(H_{0}N_{1}:H_{0})
is a power of
p
and
(H_{0}N_{1}:H_{1}\cap H_{0}N_{1})
is coprime to
p
, we obtain that
d_{\varphi}
is the constant weight function of
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}^{\set}
that takes value
1
. Therefore, if we set
\mathcal{H}^{\dagger}:=\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}^{\red}
, then Corollary
3.14
gives an equality

[J_{G/\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}}^{(\varphi)}]=[J_{G/{\mathcal{H}}^{\dagger}}].

In summary, one has

[J_{G/\mathcal{H}}]=[J_{G/\mathcal{H}^{\dagger}}].

Note that the inclusions
H_{0}\subset H_{0}N_{1}
and
H_{1}\cap H_{0}N_{1}\subset H_{0}N_{1}
imply that
H_{0}
and
H_{1}\cap H_{0}N_{1}
are not contained in
{\mathcal{H}}^{\dagger}
. In particular, we have
{\mathcal{H}}^{\dagger}\setminus\{H_{0}N_{1}\}\subset\mathcal{H}
. On the other hand, if
H_{0}N_{1}\subsetneq H^{\prime}
for some
H^{\prime}\in\mathcal{H}^{\dagger}
, then we have
H^{\prime}\in\mathcal{H}
and
H_{0}\subsetneq H^{\prime}
. This is absurd since
\mathcal{H}
is reduced, and hence
H_{0}N_{1}
is contained in
{\mathcal{H}}^{\dagger}
. Now, let

\Xi\colon{\mathcal{H}}^{\dagger}\rightarrow\mathcal{H};\,H\mapsto\begin{cases}H_{0}&\text{if }H=H_{0}N_{1};\\
H&\text{otherwise. }\end{cases}

Then, it is injective and
\Xi(H)\subset H
for any
H\in\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}^{\red}
. Therefore, we obtain
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}^{\red}<\mathcal{H}
. On the other hand, we have
({\mathcal{H}}^{\dagger})_{G_{p}}^{\red}=\mathcal{H}_{G_{p}}^{\red}
and

\#\{H\in\mathcal{H}^{\dagger}\mid G_{p}\cap H\notin\mathcal{H}_{G_{p}}^{\red}\}<\#\{H\in\mathcal{H}\mid G_{p}\cap H\notin\mathcal{H}_{G_{p}}^{\red}\}.

Applying the above procedure for all
H_{0}\in\mathcal{H}
with
G_{p}\cap H_{0}\notin\mathcal{H}_{G_{p}}^{\red}
, we obtain a reduced set of subgroups
\mathcal{H}_{0}
of
G
satisfying (i), (ii) and (iii).

\blacksquare

Theorem 8.4
.

Let
G
be a finite nilpotent group, and
\mathcal{H}
a multiset of its subgroups. Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

J_{G/\mathcal{H}}
is a quasi-permutation
G
-lattice;

(ii)

J_{G/\mathcal{H}}
is a quasi-invertible
G
-lattice;

(iii)

[J_{G/\mathcal{H}}]=[J_{G/\mathcal{H}^{\prime}}]
, where
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
is a reduced set of subgroups of
G
with
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}<\mathcal{H}
that satisfies one of the following:

(iii-
\alpha
)

\mathcal{H}^{\prime}=\{N\}
,
N\triangleleft G
and
G/N
is cyclic; or

(iii-
\beta
)

\mathcal{H}^{\prime}=\{H,H^{\prime}\}
and there exist

,

and an isomorphism

that induces
H/N^{G}(\mathcal{H}^{\prime})\cong\langle(1,\tau_{2^{\nu}})\rangle
and
H^{\prime}/N^{G}(\mathcal{H}^{\prime})\cong\langle(1,\sigma_{2^{\nu}}\tau_{2^{\nu}})\rangle
.

Proof.

(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii): This is clear.

(ii)
\Rightarrow
(iii): Since
G
is nilpotent, we have

, where
G_{p}
is a
p
-Sylow subgroup of
G
. Hence, applying Lemma
8.3
for all prime divisors of
\#G
, we obtain a reduced set
\mathcal{H}^{\prime\prime}
of subgroups of
G

•

\mathcal{H}^{\prime\prime}<\mathcal{H}

•

(\mathcal{H}^{\prime\prime}_{G_{p}})^{\set}=\mathcal{H}_{G_{p}}^{\red}
for all

; and

•

[J_{G/\mathcal{H}^{\prime\prime}}]=[J_{G/\mathcal{H}}]
.

is quasi-invertible as a

-lattice, Theorem

for any odd prime divisor

. In particular, we have

for some odd positive integer

. Moreover,

\mathcal{H}^{\prime}:=(\mathcal{H}^{\prime\prime})^{\srd}.

Then
(\mathcal{H}^{\prime}_{G_{2}})^{\set}

Case 1.

implies that

Proof of Theorem

In this section, we assume that
k
is a global field. For a finite étale algebra
\mathbf{K}
over
k
, let

\Sha(\mathbf{K}/k):=(\N_{\mathbf{K}/k}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbf{K}}^{\times})\cap k^{\times})/\N_{\mathbf{K}/k}(\mathbf{K}^{\times}).

Here,
\mathbb{A}_{\mathbf{K}}^{\times}
is the product of the idèle groups of all the factors of
\mathbf{K}
. We say that the
multinorm principle holds for
\mathbf{K}/k
if

\Sha(\mathbf{K}/k)=1.

One can rephrase
\Sha(\mathbf{K}/k)
by means of the associated multinorm one torus. For an algebraic torus
T
over
k
, the Tate–Shafarevich group of
T
is defined as follows:

\Sha^{1}(k,T):=\Ker\left(H^{1}(k,T)\xrightarrow{(\Res_{k_{v}/k})_{v}}\prod_{v}H^{1}(k_{v},T)\right).

Here,
v
runs through all places of
k
, and
k_{v}
denotes the completion of
k
at
v
(see also
[
PR94
, Section 6.3]
).

Lemma 9.1

(
[
LOY24
, p. 7, (2.5)]
)
.

Let
k
be a global field, and
\mathbf{K}
a finite étale algebra over
k
. Then, there is an isomorphism

\Sha(\mathbf{K}/k)\cong\Sha^{1}(k,T_{\mathbf{K}/k}).

Note that Lemma
9.1
is a generalization of Ono’s theorem, which is given in
[
Ono63
, p. 70]
.

Proposition 9.2

(
[
Vos69
, p. 1213, Theorem 5]
)
.

Let
k
be a global field, and
T
an algebraic torus over
k
. Then, there is an exact sequence

0\rightarrow A_{k}(T)\rightarrow H^{1}(k,\Pic(\overline{X}))^{\vee}\rightarrow\Sha^{1}(k,T)\rightarrow 0.

Here,
H^{1}(k,\Pic(\overline{X}))^{\vee}
and

are defined as follows:

•

\overline{X}:=X\otimes_{k}k^{\sep}
, where

is a smooth compactification of

over

;

•

is the Pontryagin dual of

•

is the defect of the weak approximation of

.

Proof of Theorem
1.9
.

By Theorem

,

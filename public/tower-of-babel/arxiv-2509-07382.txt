Abstract
. We propose a simple proof of the exponential convergence to equilibrium for ultrafast diffusion equations in ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} . Our approach, based on the direct use of Poincaré inequality, gets rid of the optimal transport arguments used in [ FI25 ] which are valid for Gaussian-excluded one-dimensional weights. This simplification allows us to extend their results to Gaussian measures in higher dimensions.



1.
Introduction

In
[
Iac19
]
, M. Iacobelli obtained the exponential convergence for the following so-called “ultrafast diffusion equation”
(see for example
[
BDMN17a
]
,
[
BDMN17b
]
and
[
IPS19
]
for the relevant background materials) with periodic boundary conditions:

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}f(t,x)=-r\,\partial_{x}\left(f(t,x)\,\partial_{x}\left(\frac{\rho(x)}{f^{r+1}(t,x)}\right)\right)&\quad\text{on }\quad(0,\infty)\times[0,1],\\[6.0pt]
f(t,0)=f(t,1)&\quad\text{on }\quad(0,\infty),\end{cases}

where
r>1
and both
\rho>0
and
f(t,\cdot)
are probability densities on
[0,1]
.
Recently in
[
FI25
]
, M. Fathi and M. Iacobelli extended the results of
[
Iac19
]
to the non-compact case (namely, the whole real line
\mathbb{R}
).
They considered the solutions
f(t,\cdot)
of

\partial_{t}f(t,x)=-r\partial_{x}\left(f(t,x)\partial_{x}\left(\frac{\rho(x)}{f^{r+1}(t,x)}\right)\right)\quad\text{on }\quad(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R},

where
r>1
and
\rho>0
is a probability density on
\mathbb{R}
.
However, the approach in
[
FI25
]
relies crucially on the optimal transport arguments which are valid for restrictive (Gaussian-excluded) one-dimensional weights.
In this paper, with a rather simple argument, we extend the results of
[
FI25
]
to Gaussian measures in higher dimensions.

For the purpose as mentioned above, we shall consider the following (higher-dimensional) ultrafast diffusion equation with initial data
f_{0}(x):=f(0,x)
,

(1)

\partial_{t}f(t,x)=-r\,\mathrm{div}_{x}\left(f(t,x)\nabla_{x}\left(\frac{\rho(x)}{f^{r+1}(t,x)}\right)\right)\quad\text{on }\quad(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{n},

where
r>1
and
\rho>0
is a probability density on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
According to the framework laid out in
[
IPS19
]
and
[
FI25
]
, one is led to study the following functional difference

\mathcal{F}_{\rho}[f]-\mathcal{F}_{\rho}[m]:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\rho(x)}{f^{r}(x)}dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\rho(x)}{m^{r}(x)}dx,

where, for the solution
f
we abuse the notation by dropping out the
t
variable, and

m:=\gamma\rho^{1/(r+1)}\quad\text{ with}\quad\gamma:=\frac{1}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\rho^{1/(r+1)}dx}.

Here
m
is the equilibrium density of (
1
) and
\gamma
is the normalisation constant.

We have the following natural two-sided bounds.

Lemma 1
.

Let
\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
denote the space of probability densities on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Assume that

f_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{c,C}:=\{g\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):cm\leq g\leq Cm\},\quad C\geq 1\geq c>0.

Then there exist constants
k_{1}
and
k_{2}
, depending on
r
,
c
,
C
and
\gamma
, such that

\displaystyle k_{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\frac{f}{m}-1\right|^{2}mdx\leq\mathcal{F}_{\rho}[f]-\mathcal{F}_{\rho}[m]\leq k_{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\frac{f}{m}-1\right|^{2}mdx.

Remark 2
.

Under the assumption
f_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{c,C}
,
the existence and uniqueness of a solution
f(t)
to (
1
) with
f(t,\cdot)\in\mathcal{P}_{c,C}
for all
t>0
will be justified in Appendix
A
.

As already well observed in
[
IPS19
]
and
[
FI25
]
,
the exponential convergence of solutions
f(t,\cdot)
to equilibrium
m
can be reduced to the following crucial estimate

(2)

\mathcal{F}_{\rho}[f]-\mathcal{F}_{\rho}[m]\leq K\,I_{\rho}[f],\quad\forall f\in\mathcal{P}_{c,C},

where
K
is a constant and, by letting
u=f/m
,

I_{\rho}[f]:=-\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{F}_{\rho}[f]=r^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u|\nabla(u^{-(r+1)})|^{2}mdx.

Given above lemma and in order to establish (
2
), it is natural to invoke

Definition 3

(Poincaré Inequality)
.

Let
m
be a probability measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
We say that the Poincaré inequality (associated to
m
) holds, with constant
C_{P}=C_{P}(m)
, if

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g^{2}mdx-\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}gmdx\right)^{2}\leq C_{P}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla g|^{2}mdx,\quad\forall\,g.

Remark 4
.

Log-concave measures (in particular, Gaussian measures) satisfy the Poincaré inequality (see Bobkov
[
Bob99
]
).
Certain non-log-concave measures also satisfy the Poincaré inequality (see
[
BCE13
, Theorem 3]
and
[
BJ21
, Theorem 3.2]
).

Now we are able to state the main contribution of this paper.

Theorem 5
.

The estimate (
2
) holds with

K=\frac{C_{P}C^{2r+3}}{2r(r+1)\gamma^{r+1}c^{r+2}}.

Then, by Gronwall type arguments in conjunction with Lemma
1
, we obtain the exponential convergence to equilibrium for ultrafast diffusion equations in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Theorem 6
.

Let
f(t)
be the solution to (
1
) with
f_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{c,C}
.
Then for all
t>0
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\frac{f(t)}{m}-1\right|^{2}mdx\leq\frac{1}{k_{1}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\rho(x)}{f_{0}^{r}(x)}dx-\frac{1}{\gamma^{r+1}}\right)\cdot e^{-\frac{1}{K}t},

where
k_{1}
is given in Lemma
1
and
K
is given in Theorem
5
.

Already for
m
being the Gaussians, our result answers the open problems in
[
FI25
]
.
Also, for compactly supported
f_{0}
and
m
, Theorem
6
covers
[
IPS19
, Theorem 1.4]
.

2.
Proofs

Proof of Lemma
1
.

Recall
u=\frac{f}{m}
. By a simple calculation, we obtain

(3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\rho}[f]-\mathcal{F}_{\rho}[m]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\gamma^{r+1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(u^{-r}-1\right)mdx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\gamma^{r+1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(u^{-r}-1+r\left(u-1\right)\right)mdx.

Considering the Taylor expansion of
x^{-r}
at
x=1
, (
3
) can be rewritten as

\mathcal{F}_{\rho}[f]-\mathcal{F}_{\rho}[m]=\frac{r(r+1)}{2\gamma^{r+1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\theta^{-(r+2)}|u-1|^{2}mdx,

where
\theta\in(\min\{1,u\},\max\{1,u\})
. Since
0<c\leq u\leq C
(see Remark
2
), we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{r(r+1)}{2\gamma^{r+1}}C^{-(r+2)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|u-1\right|^{2}mdx

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{F}_{\rho}[f]-\mathcal{F}_{\rho}[m]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{r(r+1)}{2\gamma^{r+1}}c^{-(r+2)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|u-1\right|^{2}mdx.

Here we also used implicitly
C\geq 1\geq c
. This proves the lemma.
∎

Proof of Theorem
5
.

Applying Poincaré inequality to
g=u-1=\frac{f}{m}-1
, and noting

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}gm=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}m=1-1=0,

we obtain (upon tracing the expression of
k_{2}
in the proof of Lemma
1
)

\mathcal{F}_{\rho}[f]-\mathcal{F}_{\rho}[m]\leq C_{P}\frac{r(r+1)}{2\gamma^{r+1}}c^{-(r+2)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}mdx.

Since
0<c\leq u\leq C
(see Remark
2
), we also have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}mdx\leq\frac{C^{2r+3}}{(r+1)^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u|\nabla(u^{-(r+1)})|^{2}mdx=\frac{C^{2r+3}}{r^{2}(r+1)^{2}}I_{\rho}[f].

Collecting the above two inequalities then proves the theorem.
∎

Appendix A
On the existence and uniqueness of solutions

Here adapting
[
FI25
, Appendix A]
for the one-dimensional case,
we show the well-posedness of the ultrafast diffusion equation (
1
) in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
in the class
\mathcal{P}_{c,C}
.

Let
f_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{c,C}
for some
c,C>0
and write
m=e^{-V}
.
Given
k\geq 1
, we consider

V_{k}:=\begin{cases}a_{k}V,&\text{in }B(0,k),\\[6.0pt]
+\infty,&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

where
a_{k}>0
is chosen such that
m_{k}:=e^{-V_{k}}
is a probability density in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
or more precisely, in
B(0,k):=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|x|\leq k\}
. Also, we consider the initial data

f_{0}^{k}:=\begin{cases}b_{k}f_{0},&\text{in }B(0,k),\\[6.0pt]
0,&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

where
b_{k}>0
is chosen such that
f_{0}^{k}\in\mathcal{P}(B(0,k))
(that is,
f_{0}^{k}
is a probability density in
B(0,k)
).
Note that
a_{k}
,
b_{k}\to 1
as
k\to+\infty
, hence

f_{0}^{k}\in\mathcal{P}_{c_{k},C_{k}}^{k}:=\{g\in\mathcal{P}(B(0,k)):c_{k}m_{k}\leq g\leq C_{k}m_{k}\},

where
c_{k}\to c
and
C_{k}\to C
as
k\to+\infty
.

Now, since
m_{k}
is compactly supported, applying
[
IPS19
, Theorem 1.2]

we obtain the existence and uniqueness of the solution
f^{k}(t)\in\mathcal{P}^{k}_{c_{k},C_{k}}
to the ultrafast diffusion equation posed on
B(0,k)

with Neumann boundary conditions and initial data
f_{0}^{k}
.
In the process we have to invoke the maximum principle (see
[
IPS19
, Corollary 3.8]
) to guarantee that

f^{k}(t)
inherits faithfully the constants
c_{k}
and
C_{k}
from
f_{0}^{k}\in\mathcal{P}^{k}_{c_{k},C_{k}}
.
Moreover, for any
R<k
, we obtain from
[
IPS19
, Lemma 3.3]
the compactness of the family
\{f^{k}|_{B(0,R)}\}
.
Hence, recalling that
c_{k}\to c
and
C_{k}\to C
as
k\to+\infty
,
by a diagonal argument we see that
f^{k}
converges (up to a subsequence) to
f\in\mathcal{P}_{c,C}
, where
f(0)=f_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{c,C}
and
f(t)
solves (
1
).
Also, upon localisation of
f_{0}
, the uniqueness of
f
follows from the
L^{1}
-contractivity (see
[
IPS19
, Proposition 3.6]
).

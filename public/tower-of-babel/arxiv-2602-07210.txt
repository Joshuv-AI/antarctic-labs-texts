Strengthenings of Mazur’s Conjecture for Higher Heegner Points
Xiaoyu Zhang

Abstract
. We establish quantitative strengthenings of Mazur’s conjecture regarding the non-torsion property of higher Heegner points on modular and Shimura curves, confirming both a vertical version for sufficiently large powers n n and a horizontal version for primes p ≫ 1 p\gg 1 . Distinct from previous strategies by Cornut and Vatsal that relied on Ratner’s theorems on unipotent flows and required restrictive hypotheses on level structures, our approach circumvents these constraints by exploiting the interplay between Galois orbits and Hecke orbits. By quantifying the relative size of these orbits, we reduce the problem to ergodic results concerning the equidistribution of Hecke operators and the classification of joining measures. This method allows for the analysis of simultaneous supersingular reductions without requiring the surjectivity of reduction maps.

Introduction

Heegner points on modular and Shimura curves encode deep arithmetic information, playing a pivotal role in Iwasawa theory, the Birch–Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture, and the Gross–Zagier formula.

Let
L
be an imaginary quadratic field of discriminant

be a positive integer such that every prime factor of

\mathcal{O}_{L}/\mathcal{N}\simeq\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}

with a cyclic kernel of order

For each positive integer

It is well known that

\mathrm{Tr}_{L[p^{\infty}]/H[p^{\infty}]}(y[p^{n}])\notin A(H[p^{\infty}])_{\mathrm{tors}}.

While Cornut’s theorem proves the existence of non-torsion points, it leaves open the quantitative question of their distribution: for how many integers

\mathrm{Tr}_{L[p^{\infty}]/H[p^{\infty}]}(y[p^{n}])

Theorem 1.2

(Corollary

Suppose that

\mathrm{Tr}_{L[p^{\infty}]/H[p^{\infty}]}(y[p^{n}])\notin A(H[p^{\infty}])_{\mathrm{tors}}.

[

,

We also formulate and prove a

The proof of Theorem
8.2
reduces to a distribution problem concerning the Galois orbits of the Heegner points
x[n]
. Specifically, let
X_{0}(N)^{\mathrm{ss}}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell})
denote the supersingular locus of
X_{0}(N)(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell})
, and let
\mathfrak{G}_{n}\subset\mathrm{Gal}(L[\infty]/L)
be a specific subset for each
n>0

\mathbf{R}_{\mathcal{T}}\colon\{\nu(x[n])|\nu\in\mathfrak{G}_{n}\}\to\prod_{\sigma\in\mathcal{T}}X_{0}(N)^{\mathrm{ss}}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}),\quad x\mapsto(\sigma(x)^{(\ell)})_{\sigma\in\mathcal{T}}.

Here
\sigma(x)^{(\ell)}
denotes the reduction modulo
\ell

\sigma(x)

\mathcal{T}
, we show that
\mathbf{R}_{\mathcal{T}}
is surjective for
n\gg 1
.

The strategy of the proof for Theorem
8.1
relies on the interplay between the Hecke orbit
T_{n}(x[1])
and the Galois orbit
\mathfrak{G}_{n}(x[n])
. We demonstrate in Theorem
7.2
that
\mathfrak{G}_{n}(x[n])
is contained in
T_{n}(x[1])
. Furthermore, their relative sizes are quantified by
d(n)
: as
1-d(n)
decreases, the Galois orbit occupies a larger proportion of the Hecke orbit. Consequently, assuming
1-d(n)<c(\pi,\ell,\mathcal{T})
, it suffices to prove that the Hecke orbits
(T_{n}(x[1]))_{n}
equidistribute on a specific subset of
\prod_{\sigma\in\mathcal{T}}X_{0}(N)^{\mathrm{ss}}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell})
.

To achieve this, we combine the equidistribution results of Clozel–Oh–Ullmo
[
COU01
]
with the measure classification of Einsiedler–Lindenstrauss
[
EL19
]
. Heuristically, the former establishes the equidistribution of
T_{n}(x[1]^{(\ell)})
on a
single
copy of
X_{0}(N)^{\mathrm{ss}}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell})
, while the latter implies that the simultaneous supersingular reductions (twisted by
\mathcal{T}
) generate an
algebraic
measure (
[
EL19
, Definition 1.2]
) as
n\to+\infty
. We then employ classification results for subgroups of self-products of simple non-commutative groups to conclude.

By refining these arguments, we prove the following stronger version of Corollary
8.2

1.2
and Corollary

Theorem 1.6

(Theorem
9.1
)
.

Fix an infinite set
\mathcal{S}^{\circ}
of primes
\ell
such that
x[1]
has supersingular reduction at every
\ell\in\mathcal{S}^{\circ}
. Then, for any
0<c\ll 1
, there exists

such that for any
n>n_{0}
coprime to the primes in
\mathcal{S}^{\circ}
and
\mathfrak{N}D_{L/\mathbb{Q}}
with
1-d(n)<c
, we have

\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{T}}\sigma(y[n])\notin A(L[\infty])_{\mathrm{tors}}.

Notably, the proof of this theorem does not require the surjectivity of
\mathbf{R}_{\mathcal{T}}
on
\mathfrak{G}_{n}(x[n])
; rather, it exploits the flexibility of choosing arbitrarily large primes
\ell
from the infinite set
\mathcal{S}^{\circ}
(see §

).

This work reduces number-theoretic questions to ergodic theoretic and distributional problems, a theme present in
[
Cor02
,
Vat02
,
CV05
,
CV07
]

[

[

The original conjecture of Mazur has significant applications to the Iwasawa Main Conjecture (

The ergodic argument from

Recall that two subgroups

Proposition 2.4
.

A subgroup

For

.
Since
H
is normalized by
\Delta^{\mathcal{T}}(G)
, the subgroup

is normalized by
\Delta^{\mathcal{T}}(G^{(j)})

Thus, by Proposition

Set

To prove the claim, write

(1)

\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}\in\mathcal{T}_{i}^{(j)}

In particular,

7.
Relation between Hecke orbits and Galois orbits

In this section, we study the relationship between Hecke orbits and Galois orbits.

We keep the notation as in §
6
. We fix a prime
\ell
(
\mathcal{S}=\{\ell\}
in the notation of §
3
) that is inert in
L
, and a place
v(\ell)
of
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
over
\ell
. We fix the point
x_{0}=x[1]\in Sh_{U,\mathbb{Q}}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
and assume that it has
supersingular
good reduction at
v(\ell)
. We fix a non-empty finite subset
\mathcal{T}=\{\sigma_{1},\cdots,\sigma_{r}\}
of

Z_{I_{x_{0}}}(\mathbb{Q})\backslash Z_{I_{x_{0}}}(\mathbb{A}_{f})=I_{x_{0}}(\mathbb{Q})\backslash I_{x_{0}}(\mathbb{A}_{f})=L^{\times}\backslash\widehat{L}^{\times}.

We identify
\mathcal{T}
with its image in
\mathrm{Gal}(L[\infty]/L)
via the Artin reciprocity map:

(16)

\mathrm{Art}\colon\mathrm{Gal}(L[\infty]/L)\simeq\widehat{L}^{\times}/L^{\times}.

We have a partition
\mathcal{T}=\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{k}\mathcal{T}_{i}
as in §
5
. We set
\mathbf{G}_{0}=I_{x_{0}^{(\ell)}}
, an algebraic group over
\mathbb{Q}
.
We fix isomorphisms

(17)

\mathbf{G}_{0}(\mathbb{Z}_{q})\simeq G(\mathbb{Z}_{q}),\quad q\neq\ell.

Consider a positive integer
n
coprime to
\ell\mathfrak{N}D_{L/\mathbb{Q}}
. We first consider Galois orbits. There are the following cases:

(1)

n=q^{e}
is a prime power with
q
a prime number that splits in
L
. We fix a set
S_{n}
of representatives in
\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{\times}
of the quotient set
(\mathbb{Z}_{q}/n\mathbb{Z}_{q})^{\times}
. Note that
S_{n}
can be viewed as a set of representatives of the quotient
(\mathcal{O}_{L,q}/n\mathcal{O}_{L,q})^{\times}/(\mathbb{Z}_{q}/n\mathbb{Z}_{q})^{\times}
because
\mathcal{O}_{L,q}\simeq\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{2}
as
\mathbb{Z}_{q}
-algebras. Moreover,
\#S_{n}=q^{e-1}(q-1)
. Under the identification (
15
), we define a subset
\mathfrak{G}_{n}
of
\mathrm{Gal}(L[\infty]/L)
such that under the Artin map (
16
), we have

\mathrm{Art}(\mathfrak{G}_{n})=L^{\times}\cdot\left\{\begin{pmatrix}a&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}|a\in S_{n}\right\}.

(2)

n=q^{e}
is a prime power with
q
a prime number that is inert in
L
. We fix a set
S_{n}
of representatives in
\mathcal{O}_{L,q}^{\times}
of the quotient
(\mathcal{O}_{L,q}/n\mathcal{O}_{L,q})^{\times}/(\mathbb{Z}_{q}/n\mathbb{Z}_{q})^{\times}
. Moreover,
\#S_{n}=q^{e-1}(q+1)
. We define a subset
\mathfrak{G}_{n}
of
\mathrm{Gal}(L[\infty]/L)
such that

\mathrm{Art}(\mathfrak{G}_{n})=L^{\times}\cdot S_{n}.

Explicitly, we can take
S_{n}
to be (under the identification (
15
))::

S_{n}=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}1&bu_{q}\\
b&1\end{pmatrix}|b=1,\cdots,q^{e}\right\}\bigsqcup\left\{\begin{pmatrix}pau_{q}&u_{q}\\
1&pau_{q}\end{pmatrix}|a=1,\cdots,q^{e-1}\right\}.

(3)

In general, for a prime factorization
n=\prod_{i=1}^{s}p_{i}^{e_{i}}
, we define
S_{n}=\prod_{i=1}^{s}S_{p_{i}^{e_{i}}}
, viewed as a subset of
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{L}^{\times}
. We then define a subset
\mathfrak{G}_{n}
of
\mathrm{Gal}(L[\infty]/L)
such that

\mathrm{Art}(\mathfrak{G}_{n})=L^{\times}\cdot S_{n}.

We next consider Hecke orbits. As above, there are the following cases::

(1)

n=q^{e}
is a prime power. We define

a(n):=\begin{pmatrix}n&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\in\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{q})\simeq G(\mathbb{Q}_{q})\hookrightarrow G(\mathbb{A}_{f}^{(\ell)}).

By definition, the double coset decomposes into right cosets

G(\mathbb{Z}_{q})a(n)G(\mathbb{Z}_{q})=\bigsqcup_{g\in S_{n}^{\prime}}a(n)G(\mathbb{Z}_{q}),

where
S_{n}^{\prime}
is a set of representatives in
G(\mathbb{Z}_{q})
of the quotient
G(\mathbb{Z}_{q})/(G(\mathbb{Z}_{q})\bigcap a(n)G(\mathbb{Z}_{q})a(n)^{-1})
. Note that
\#S_{n}^{\prime}=q^{e-1}(q+1)=\deg(a(n))
(
[
Shi71
, Theorem 3.24]
). We have

Lemma 7.1
.

For any distinct
g,g^{\prime}\in S_{n}
, the right cosets

gh[n]G(\mathbb{Z}_{q})
and
g^{\prime}h[n]G(\mathbb{Z}_{q})
are distinct right cosets in the decomposition
G(\mathbb{Z}_{q})a(n)G(\mathbb{Z}_{q})
(here we use the identifications (
17
)).

Proof.

We first show that the two cosets are distinct.
If
q
splits in
L
, write
g=\begin{pmatrix}a&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}
and
g^{\prime}=\begin{pmatrix}a^{\prime}&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}
. Then we have

gh[n]G(\mathbb{Z}_{q})=\begin{pmatrix}a\varpi_{q}^{e}&a\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}G(\mathbb{Z}_{q})=\begin{pmatrix}n&a\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}G(\mathbb{Z}_{q}),\quad g^{\prime}h[n]G(\mathbb{Z}_{q})=\begin{pmatrix}n&a^{\prime}\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}G(\mathbb{Z}_{q}).

Since

\begin{pmatrix}n&a\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}n&a^{\prime}\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}1&(a^{\prime}-a)/n\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\notin G(\mathbb{Z}_{q}),

these right cosets are indeed distinct.

If
q
is inert in
L
, write

g^{-1}g^{\prime}=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}
. By definition of
S_{n}
, one checks that
b/n\notin\mathbb{Z}_{q}
. Then we have

(gh[n])^{-1}(g^{\prime}h[n])=\begin{pmatrix}a&b/n\\
cn&d\end{pmatrix}\notin G(\mathbb{Z}_{q}).

It follows again that these two right cosets are distinct.

Next we show that both cosets lie in
G(\mathbb{Z}_{q})a(n)G(\mathbb{Z}_{q})
. By the Cartan decomposition for
G(\mathbb{Q}_{q})
, we can characterize
G(\mathbb{Z}_{q})a(n)G(\mathbb{Z}_{q})
as the subset of
G(\mathbb{Q}_{q})
, consisting of matrices
A=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}
with
a,b,c,d\in\mathbb{Z}_{q}
such that
\det(A)\in n\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{\times}
and
a,b,c,d
are not simultaneous divisible by
q
. Using this criterion, it follows easily that
gh[n]G(\mathbb{Z}_{q})
and
g^{\prime}h[n]G(\mathbb{Z}_{q})
both lie in
G(\mathbb{Z}_{q})a(n)G(\mathbb{Z}_{q})
.
∎

In particular, if
q
is inert in
L
, then we can take
S_{n}^{\prime}
to be
S_{n}
; if
q
splits in
L
, we can take
S_{n}^{\prime}
to contain
S_{n}
.

(2)

In general, for a prime factorization
n=\prod_{i=1}^{s}p_{i}^{e_{i}}
, we have (
[
Shi71
, Theorem 3.24]
)

T_{a(n)}=\prod_{i=1}^{s}T_{a(p_{i}^{e_{i}})}.

It follows from the previous case that for any distinct
g,g^{\prime}\in S_{n}
, the right cosets
gh[n]U
and
g^{\prime}h[n]U
are distinct, and both lie in
Uh[n]U
(note that for any
p_{i}
,
U_{p_{i}}=G(\mathbb{Z}_{p_{i}})
under the identification (
17
) because

is coprime to
\ell\mathfrak{N}D_{L/\mathbb{Q}}
).

For a positive integer
n=\prod_{i=1}^{s}p_{i}^{e_{i}}
coprime to
\ell\mathfrak{N}D_{L/\mathbb{Q}}
, we define a rational number

d(n):=\frac{\#S_{n}}{\#S_{n}^{\prime}}=\frac{\#S_{n}}{\deg(a(n))}\in(0,1].

In particular,
d(n)=1
if and only if all the prime factors
p_{i}
are inert in
L
.

In summary, we have obtained::

Theorem 7.2

For any

coprime to
\ell\mathfrak{N}D_{L/\mathbb{Q}}
, the Hecke orbit

contains the Galois orbit

f(n,\underline{h})
of points
x^{\prime}
in
T_{a(n)}(x[1]^{(\ell)})
(counted with multiplicities) with
\mathbf{R}_{\mathcal{T}}(x^{\prime})=\underline{h}
satisfies

(20)

\left|\frac{f(n,\underline{h})}{\#S_{n}^{\prime}}-\prod_{i=1}^{r}\frac{c_{h_{i}}^{-1}}{\sum_{j=1}^{s}c_{g_{j}}^{-1}}\right|=\left|\frac{f(n,\underline{h})}{\deg(a(n))}-\prod_{i=1}^{r}\frac{c_{h_{i}}^{-1}}{\sum_{j=1}^{s}c_{g_{j}}^{-1}}\right|<\epsilon.

Note that the set of values of
d(n)
is discrete away from
1
, so there exists
0<\epsilon\ll 1
such that for any
n^{\prime}
, if
1-d(n^{\prime})<c(\pi,\ell,\mathcal{T})
, then
1-d(n^{\prime})<c(\pi,\ell,\mathcal{T})-\epsilon
. We choose such an
\epsilon
; then by the assumption on
n
, we have (
cf.
Lemma
7.1
)

\frac{\#(S_{n}^{\prime}\backslash S_{n})}{\#S_{n}^{\prime}}=1-d(n)<c(\pi,\ell,\mathcal{T})-\epsilon.

So combining with
22
, we know that for any
\underline{h}\in\prod_{i=1}^{k}\widetilde{\Delta}^{\mathcal{T}_{i}}(Sh_{U}^{\mathrm{ss}}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}))
, there is at least one point
x^{\prime}
in the subset
\mathfrak{G}_{n}(x[n]^{(\ell)})=\{\sigma(x[n])^{(\ell)}|\sigma\in\mathfrak{G}_{n}\}
of
T_{a(n)}(x[1]^{(\ell)})
with
\mathbf{R}_{\mathcal{T}}(x^{\prime})=\underline{h}
. This finishes the proof of the theorem.
∎

We deduce immediately the following.

Corollary 8.2
.

Let
\ell
and
\pi\colon Sh_{U}\to A
be as above. For any integer
n\gg 1
coprime to
\ell\mathfrak{N}D_{L/\mathbb{Q}}
with
1-d(n)<c(\pi,\ell,\mathcal{T})
, we have

\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{T}}\sigma(y[n])\notin A(L[\infty])_{\mathrm{tors}}.

Proof.

The proof is very similar to
[
Zha25
, Theorem 9.2]
. We argue by contradiction, and thus assume that
\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{T}}\sigma(y[n])
is torsion for every
n
coprime to
\ell\mathfrak{N}D_{L/\mathbb{Q}}
with
1-d(n)<c(\pi,\ell,\mathcal{T})
.

Let
n
be as in the corollary. By Theorem
8.1
, for any
x,x^{\prime}\in Sh_{U}^{\mathrm{ss}}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell})
, we can choose
\nu,\nu^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{G}_{n}
such that the components of
\mathbf{R}_{\mathcal{T}}(\sigma(\nu(x[n])))
are equal to those of
\mathbf{R}_{\mathcal{T}}(\sigma(\nu^{\prime}(x[n])))
, except at the components in
\mathcal{T}_{i_{0}}\subset\mathcal{T}
for some
i_{0}
, in which case

\mathbf{R}_{\mathcal{T}}(\sigma(\nu(x[n])))=x\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbf{R}_{\mathcal{T}}(\sigma(\nu^{\prime}(x[n])))=x^{\prime},\quad\forall\sigma\in\mathcal{T}_{i_{0}}.

In particular, we have

\mathbf{R}_{\mathcal{T}}(\sigma(\nu(y[n])))-\mathbf{R}_{\mathcal{T}}(\sigma(\nu^{\prime}(y[n])))=\left(0,\cdots,0,\pi(x)-\pi(x^{\prime}),\cdots,\pi(x)-\pi(x^{\prime}),0,\cdots,0\right).

Here on the right hand side, all the components are zero except at the components in
\mathcal{T}_{i_{0}}
, which are
\pi(x)-\pi(x^{\prime})
. Then we have

\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{T}}\sigma(\nu(y[n]))^{(\ell)}-\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{T}}\sigma(\nu^{\prime}(y[n]))^{(\ell)}=\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{T}_{i_{0}}}(\pi(x)-\pi(x^{\prime}))=\#\mathcal{T}_{i_{0}}(\pi(x)-\pi(x^{\prime})).

Note that for any prime
q|t_{1}
,

\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(A(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell})\otimes\mathbb{F}_{q})=2\dim(A).

Thus
\#A(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell})[t_{1}]\leq t_{1}^{2\dim(A)}
. By assumption on
\ell
(
cf.
(
18
)),
\#Sh_{U}^{\mathrm{ss}}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell})>(t_{1}\times r!)^{2\dim(A)}\times t_{2}
. So there exists
x,x^{\prime}\in Sh_{U}^{\mathrm{ss}}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell})
such that

\pi(x)-\pi(x^{\prime})\in A(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell})
has order not dividing
t_{1}\times r!
.

On the other hand, we have assumed that
\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{T}}\sigma(y[n])
is torsion; hence both
\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{T}}\sigma(\nu(y[n]))
and
\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{T}}\sigma(\nu^{\prime}(y[n]))
are torsion, and so are their reductions modulo
v(\ell)
, namely
\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{T}}\sigma(\nu(y[n]))^{(\ell)}
and
\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{T}}\sigma(\nu^{\prime}(y[n]))^{(\ell)}
. In particular, the following element in
A(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell})
,

\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{T}}\sigma(\nu(y[n]))^{(\ell)}-\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{T}}\sigma(\nu^{\prime}(y[n]))^{(\ell)}=\#\mathcal{T}_{i_{0}}(\pi(x)-\pi(x^{\prime}))

is a torsion element, whose order necessarily divides
t_{1}=\#A(L[\infty])_{\mathrm{tors}}
. So
\pi(x)-\pi(x^{\prime})
is a torsion element, whose order necessarily divides
t_{1}\times r!
,
5
5

5

More precisely, we can deduce that the order of
\pi(x)-\pi(x^{\prime})
divides
t_{1}\times\#\mathcal{T}_{i_{0}}
. But for our argument in the proof, the multiple
t_{1}\times r!
of
t_{1}\times\#\mathcal{T}_{i_{0}}
suffices.
which is a contradiction to the preceding paragraph. This finishes the proof of the corollary.
∎

We then deduce some consequences from the above.

We fix a prime
p
and write
L[p^{\infty}]=\bigcup_{k}L[p^{k}]
. This is a finite extension over the maximal anticyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension
H[p^{\infty}]
of

. We take
\mathcal{T}
to be a set of representatives in
\widehat{L}^{\times}
of the subgroup
\mathrm{Gal}(L[p^{\infty}]/H[p^{\infty}])
of
\mathrm{Gal}(L[p^{\infty}]/L)
(under the Artin map (
16
)). Then we have

Corollary 8.3
.

Let
\ell
and
\pi\colon Sh_{U}\to A
be as above. Suppose that
p
is either inert in
L
or splits in
L
with
\frac{2}{p+1}<c(\pi,\ell,\mathcal{T})
. Then for
k\gg 1
, we have

\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{T}}\sigma(y[p^{k}])\notin A(L[\infty])_{\mathrm{tors}}.

Proof.

This is a direct application of Corollary
8.2
by noting that
d(p^{k})=d(p)
and it is equal to
1
if
p
is inert in
L
and equal to
(p-1)/(p+1)
if
p
splits in
L
.
∎

This is a strengthening of Mazur’s original conjecture (
[
Maz83
]
).

Now we take
\mathcal{T}
to be a set of representatives in
\widehat{L}^{\times}
for the quotient
\widehat{L}^{\times}/L^{\times}\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{L}^{\times}
.

Corollary 8.4
.

Let
\pi\colon Sh_{U}\to A
be as above. For any prime
p\gg 1
, we have

\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{T}}\sigma(y[p])\notin A(L[\infty])_{\mathrm{tors}}.

Proof.

Indeed, we can assume
p>\ell\mathfrak{N}D_{L/\mathbb{Q}}
. Moreover,
1-d(p)<\frac{2}{p}
. For
p\gg 1
,

. In particular, we can take

such that

. Then we can apply Corollary

.
∎

This can be seen as a horizontal version of Mazur’s conjecture.

Remark 8.5
.

All the results in §
6
and this section can be generalized to Shimura curves over totally real number fields with appropriate modifications in the arguments. We restrict ourselves to the case of Shimura curves over
\mathbb{Q}
for ease of notation.

9.
Variants of Mazur’s conjecture-2

In the previous section, we fixed an auxiliary prime
\ell
and considered positive integers
n
coprime to
\ell
(as well as coprime to
\mathfrak{N}D_{L/\mathbb{Q}}
). In this section, we fix an infinite set of such primes
\ell
and prove a stronger result for positive integers
n
coprime to all primes in this set.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be as before.
We fix an
infinite
set
\mathcal{S}^{\circ}
of primes
\ell
and, for each
\ell\in\mathcal{S}^{\circ}
, a place
v(\ell)
of
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
over
\ell
. We assume that
x_{0}=x[1]
has
supersingular
reduction at
v(\ell)
for all
\ell\in\mathcal{S}^{\circ}
.
6
6

6

Such an
\mathcal{S}^{\circ}
always exists because
x_{0}
is a CM point in the Shimura curve
Sh_{U}
.
Then, for
\ell\in\mathcal{S}^{\circ}
, as
\ell\to+\infty
, we have
\#Sh_{U}^{\mathrm{ss}}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell})\to+\infty
. Moreover, it is well known that the cardinalities

are bounded for all

and all
\ell\in\mathcal{S}^{\circ}
(in fact,
c_{g}(\ell)\leq 24
;
cf.

[
Voi21
, Theorem 11.5.14]
).

Then we have the following:

Theorem 9.1
.

Let
\mathcal{S}^{\circ}
and
\pi\colon Sh_{U}\to A
be as above. For any
0<c\ll 1
, there is an integer
n_{0}>0
such that for any
n>n_{0}
coprime to all primes in
\mathcal{S}^{\circ}
and
\mathfrak{N}D_{L/\mathbb{Q}}
with
1-d(n)<c
, we have

\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{T}}\sigma(y[n])\notin A(L[\infty])_{\mathrm{tors}}.

Proof.

The idea of the proof is similar to that of Theorem
8.1
. We choose
\ell\in\mathcal{S}^{\circ}
sufficiently large, so
s=s(\ell)=\#Sh_{U}^{\mathrm{ss}}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell})\gg 1
and thus
c(\pi,\ell,\mathcal{T})\ll 1
because each
c_{g}(\ell)
is bounded independently of
g
and
\ell
. Then for any
n\gg 1
coprime to all primes in
\mathcal{S}^{\circ}
and
\mathfrak{N}D_{L/\mathbb{Q}}
,
\mathbf{R}_{\mathcal{T}}(T_{a(n)}(x[1]^{(\ell)}))
is equal to
\prod_{i=1}^{k}\widetilde{\Delta}^{\mathcal{T}_{i}}(Sh_{U}^{\mathrm{ss}}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}))
and moreover for such
n\to+\infty
, the sets
(\mathbf{R}_{\mathcal{T}}(\{\sigma(x[n])|\sigma\in\mathfrak{G}_{n}\}))_{n}

equidistributes
on
\prod_{i=1}^{k}\widetilde{\Delta}^{\mathcal{T}_{i}}(Sh_{U}^{\mathrm{ss}}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}))
.

For ease of notations, we write
Sh(\ell)=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\widetilde{\Delta}^{\mathcal{T}_{i}}(Sh_{U}^{\mathrm{ss}}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}))
. We then write
X(\ell)
for the subset of
Sh(\ell)^{2}
, consisting of pairs of points
(\underline{h},\underline{h}^{\prime})
in
Sh(\ell)
such that
\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{T}}(\pi(h_{\sigma})-\pi(h^{\prime}_{\sigma}))
lies in the image of
A(L[\infty])_{\mathrm{tors}}
in
A(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell})
. For any
\underline{h}\in Sh(\ell)
, there are at most
(\#Sh_{U}^{\mathrm{ss}}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}))^{k-1}\times\#A(L[\infty])_{\mathrm{tors}}
many points
\underline{h}^{\prime}\in Sh(\ell)
such that
(\underline{h},\underline{h}^{\prime})\in X(\ell)
. Thus we know that

\#X(\ell)\leq s(\ell)^{k}\times(s(\ell)^{k-1}\times\#A(L[\infty])_{\mathrm{tors}}).

It follows that

\frac{\#X(\ell)}{\#Sh(\ell)^{2}}\leq\frac{\#A(L[\infty])_{\mathrm{tors}}}{s(\ell)}\ll 1.

Thus for any
n\gg 1
coprime to all primes in
\mathcal{S}^{\circ}
and
\mathfrak{N}D_{L/\mathbb{Q}}
, the number
f(n)
of pairs of elements
(g(x[1]),g^{\prime}(x[1]))\in T_{a(n)}(x[1])
such that
(\mathbf{R}_{\mathcal{T}}(\pi(g(x[1]))),\mathbf{R}_{\mathcal{T}}(\pi(g^{\prime}(x[1]))))\in X(\ell)
satisfies

\frac{f(n)}{\#(S_{n}^{\prime})^{2}}\ll 1.

On the other hand, since

, we have

(21)

\frac{\#(S_{n})^{2}}{\#(S_{n}^{\prime})^{2}}=d(n)^{2}>(1-c)^{2}.

We choose
n_{0}=n_{0}(\ell)
such that for any
n>n_{0}
coprime to all primes in
\mathcal{S}^{\circ}
and
\mathfrak{N}D_{L/\mathbb{Q}}
, we have

(22)

\frac{f(n)}{\#(S_{n}^{\prime})^{2}}<(1-c)^{2}.

Combining (
21
) and (
22
), it follows that there exists
(\underline{h},\underline{h}^{\prime})\in\mathbf{R}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathfrak{G}_{n}(x[n]))^{2}
that is not in
X(\ell)
. In other words, we can find
\nu,\nu^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{G}_{n}
such that

\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{T}}(\sigma(\nu(y[n]))-\sigma(\nu^{\prime}(y[n])))\notin A(L[\infty])_{\mathrm{tors}}.

In particular, either
\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{T}}\sigma(\nu(y[n]))
or
\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{T}}\sigma(\nu^{\prime}(y[n]))
is non-torsion in
A(L[\infty])
. In either case,
\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{T}}\sigma(y[n])
is non-torsion, which finishes the proof of the theorem.
∎

A subunit vaccine of largemouth bass (Micropterus salmoides) with baculovirus-expressed largemouth bass ranavirus (LMBRaV) major capsid protein induces protective immunity against LMBRaV infection
Authors: Yiqun Li, Yanlin Dai, Mingyang Xue, Yan Meng, Yong Zhou, Nan Jiang, Wenzhi Liu, Chen Xu, Zhenyu Huang, Yangyang Xing, Jinyu Shen, Yuding Fan
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Highlights•The MCP of LMBRaV was successfully expressed through Bac-to-Bac baculovirus expression system.•AcMNPV-MCP enhanced the length and weight of largemouth bass.•AcMNPV-MCP can significantly reduce the LMBRaV viral load in the target tissues.•AcMNPV-MCP activated the anti-viral immune responses in largemouth bass.Keywords: Largemouth bass, Micropterus salmoides, Largemouth bass ranairus (LMBRaV), Bac-to-Bac baculovirus expression system, Subunit vaccine

Introduction

Largemouth Bass (Micropterus salmoides) is an important commercial freshwater species and is widely cultured in China. However, it is suffering from hemorrhagic diseases caused by bacteria and viruses, among which largemouth bass virus (LMBV) is one of the most severe and caused huge mortality. LMBV is a virus species classified within the genus Ranavirus that is also referred to as Santee-Cooper ranavirus (SCRV), as the initial fish kill attributed to LMBV disease occurred in wild adult largemouth bass in the Santee-Cooper reservoir, South Carolina [1]. The LMBV genome has approximately 100, 000 bp in size and expresses at least 86 potential open reading frames (ORFs) [2]. A ranavirus with high MCP gene sequence homology to LMBV was isolated from farmed largemouth bass in China and associated with an outbreak of ulcerative syndrome with high mortality [3]. In 2020, a ranavirus, belonging to LMBV, was isolated from diseased largemouth bass in Hubei province, which was named as largemouth bass ranavirus (LMBRaV) [4]. Virions of LMBRaV are 150 nm in diameter, which contain a double stranded linear DNA genome and have the typical appearance of an iridovirus. The healthy fish carrying LMBRaV is the common phenomena in largemouth bass population, which may cause virus wide spread [4]. Given the lack of effective chemical treatments for controlling LMBRaV outbreaks, vaccines present a potent strategy for preventing viral infections in largemouth bass.

Major capsid protein (MCP) constitutes the primary structural element of iridovirus particles, accounting for 40 % of the structural proteins and facilitating virus entry into host cells. It has been found that the researches on virus capsid proteins were applied to diverse applications in fields such as electronics, biotechnology, and medicine [5,6]. It was reported that MCP has been used as detection marker and effective antigen to induce a specific immune response in fish [7]. In Nile tilapia, refolded recombinant MCP from infectious spleen and kidney necrosis virus (ISKNV) effectively stimulates serum specific antibody and immune related genes responses, which could be used as a candidate subunit vaccine to prevent infection of Nile tilapia from ISKNV [8]. In a study involving vaccinated similar damselfish (Pomacentrus similis) with recombinant MCP protein supplemented with Quil-A®, marginal protection against SRDV challenge was observed [9]. Ongoing research in recent years has focused on developing vaccines for largemouth bass against LMBV. Following immunization with a recombinant plasmid expressing LMBV MCP (pCDNA3.1(+)-MCP-Flag) via pectoral fin base injection, significant immune responses were detected in largemouth bass upon challenge with LMBV [10]. An oral vaccine utilizing P. pastoris to express MCP has been created for LMBV control. Additionally, oral vaccines formulated with an ltb-MCP fusion gene expressed separately in S. cerevisiae demonstrated a relative percent survival (RPS) of 66.7 % [11]. While many studies remain in the laboratory phase, the ongoing development of new vaccines against LMBRaV with enhanced efficacy, safety, and convenience is imperative.

The baculovirus expression vector systems (BEVS) are widely recognized as a simple and dependable method for producing various high-quality recombinant proteins and multi-protein complexes for applications such as vaccine production and gene therapy [12,13]. Following the generation of a recombinant virus through homologous recombination in insect cells, along with virus amplification and titration, copious amounts of recombinant proteins can be harvested and purified [14]. BEVS have been effectively utilized for several decades to generate recombinant proteins from fish species [15]. For instance, in grass carp, the use of BEVS to produce recombinant VP35 and VP4 has shown the ability to induce immunity and provide protection against GCRV [16]. The combined administration of these proteins has demonstrated the most favorable outcomes. Furthermore, the expression of recombinant viruses containing truncated Cyprinid herpesvirus 2 (CyHV-2) membrane glycoproteins using the baculovirus surface displaying system has been shown to elicit protective immunity in gibel carp [17]. In largemouth bass, BacMCP has been identified as a promising vector-based vaccine candidate for preventing diseases caused by ISKNV infection [18]. Based on the BEVS, there is no report on the prevention of LMBRaV till now. These studies offer novel approaches for developing efficient subunit vaccines against LMBRaV.

In this study, a subunit vaccine based on BEVS, AcMNPV-MCP, was recombinant and immunized the largemouth bass by intraperitoneal injection. The relative expression of immune related genes and the change of subpopulation of leucocytes were detected. After infection with LMBRaV, the protective effects and virus load were also evaluated.

Materials and methods

Virus, cells and animals

The LMBRaV was isolated and identified from diseased largemouth bass and propagated in EPC cells by our laboratory. Sf-9 cells were maintained at 28 °C in Sf-900™ II SFM (Gibco, Gaithersburg, MD, USA). Largemouth bass, averaged 4.8 g in weight, were cultured in the recirculating water culture system at 28 °C, with bait fed once a day. After two weeks of breeding, the fish were confirmed to be virus-free. All the animal handling and experimental procedures were approved by the Animal Care and Use Committee of the Yangtze River Fisheries Research Institute, Chinese Academy of Fishery Sciences (YFI2022LYQ01).

Gene cloning and construction of recombinant plasmid pFastBac-MCP

Total DNA of LMBRaV was extracted with Total DNA Kit (Omega Bio-tek, Doraville, USA) from spleen and head kidney of diseased largemouth bass. The open reading frame (ORF) of LMBRaV MCP gene was amplified via PCR with specific primers containing restriction enzyme (EcoRI/XhoI) cutting site in Table 1. The system for PCR containing 2.5 μl of 10 × PCR buffer, 2 μl of dNTP Mixture, 0.25 μl of rTaq DNA polymerase, 1 μl of DNA template, 1 μl of forward and reverse primers, and ddH2O up to 25 μl. The procedure of PCR amplification is as follows: initial denaturation at 94 °C for 4 min followed by 35 cycles at 94 °C for 30 s, annealing at 58 °C for 30 s and extension at 72 °C for 1 min, and at the end of the thermal cycling, a further extension at 72 °C for 10 min. After examined by electrophoresis with 1.5 % agarose gel, the target DNA was purified and constructed into pFastBac1 vector (OMEGA, Norcross, GA, USA) using Hieff clone® plus one step cloning kit (Yeason, Beijing, China), which was named as pFastBac-MCP.

Generation of recombinant virus

Based on the instruction of Bac-to-Bac baculovirus expression vector system (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA, USA), pFastBac-MCP was transformed into DH10Bac/AcMNPV competent cells (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA, USA). The recombinant Bacmid-MCP was screened by blue-white selection and detected using PUC/M13 primers (Table 1). After verified by agarose gel electrophoresis, the recombinant plasmids were transfected into Sf-9 cells following the instructions of Cellfection™ II liposome transfection kit (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA, USA). Briefly, Sf9 cells were seeded into 6-well cell culture plates at a density of 8 × 105 cells/ml and cultivated with medium Sf-900™ II SFM for 1 h in 28 °C. Then, 210 μl Sf-900™ II SFM containing 2 μg plasmid Bacmid-MCP and 6 μl Cellfection™ II Reagent were introduced into Sf9 cells and incubated at 27 °C. At 4 h post transfection, the cells were changed with fresh Sf-900™-II SFM. At 72 h post transfection, viruses were acquired from the cell culture when the cytopathic effect was observed, which was named as the P1 viral stock. Sf9 cells were continuously inoculated with P1 viral stock and finally harvested until P3 viral stock was acquired. The MCP gene was verified by PCR using primer mcp-F/R in Table 1 and the titer was measured with Reed-Muench method [19]. The wild baculovirus was obtained with the same method as above.

Western blot analysis and purification of recombinant MCP

The MCP protein was detected by western blot assay. After infected with P3 viral stock for 72 h, Sf9 cells were harvested and lysed with RIPA buffer (Beyotime, Beijing, China), and cells infected with the wild baculovirus were set as control. Protein samples were resolved by 10 % SDS-PAGE and transferred onto a 0.45 μm pore nitrocellulose membrane using Semi-dry blotter (Bio-Rad, Hercules, CA, USA). After blocked with TBS containing 5 % skimmed milk at room temperature for 2 h, the membranes were incubated with the anti-His mouse monoclonal antibody (1:2000) (CST, Danvers, MA, USA) at room temperature for 2 h. Then, the membranes were washed three times with TBST, incubated with alkaline horseradish peroxidase-conjugated anti-mouse IgG (CST, Danvers, MA, USA) at room temperature for 1 h, and finally washed three times with TBST. The membranes were incubated with Clarity™ Western ECL substrate (Bio-Rad, Hercules, CA, USA) for 5 min and then exposed through a gel imaging system (Bio-Rad, Hercules, CA, USA). After confirmation of the expression of MCP protein, the P3 viral stock were propagated and the MCP protein was purified through Ni-chelating affinity chromatography with ÄKTA Pure (GE Healthcare Bio-Sciences Corp, Piscataway, NJ, USA). The purity was analyzed by SDS-PAGE and the protein was stored at −80 °C until use.

Immunization and sample collection

180 largemouth bass were randomly divided into three groups in equal numbers, and separately inoculated intraperitoneally with MCP protein (12 μg), the same volume of Sf9 cell lysate of with wild baculovirus (named as group CK) or PBS which were as control. At 1, 3, 5, 7, 14, 21, 28 and 35 days post immunization (d.p.i.), 3 fish were randomly sampled to measure the length and weight. After anesthetized with MS-222 (Sigma, USA), blood cells were collected from the tail vein. Liver, spleen and head kidney were taken aseptically and used for total RNA extraction with EZNA Total RNA Kit (Omega Bio-tek, Doraville, USA).

Quantitative real-time reverse transcriptase PCR

The quality total RNA was examined by determining 260/280 absorbance ratio using Nano Drop One (Thermo scientific, USA) and by gel electrophoresis. Reverse transcription of cDNA was carried out according to the protocol of the ReverlAid First Stand cDNA Synthesis Kit (Thermo Scientific, USA). qRT-PCR was performed in a Roter-Gene Q PCR System (Qiagen, Germany) using SYBR Green Dye Kit (Toyobo, Japan) with specific primers in Table 1. The PCR reaction was performed in a volume of 20 µL reaction mixture containing 10 µL 2 × SYBR Green premixture, 1 µL diluted template cDNA (300 ng/µL), 0.5 µL of each target gene primer (10 µM) and 8 µL water. qRT-PCR was performed with the following program: 95 °C for 5 min, then 40 cycles of 95 °C for 10 s, 60 °C for 20 s, and 72 °C for 20 s, followed by an extension at 72 °C for 10 min. The mRNA levels of immune related genes were analyzed using comparative threshold cycle method (2-ΔΔCT) [20] with β-actin as an internal reference.

Blood cell counting

For leucocyte count, blood smears were prepared. After Wright-Giemsa staining, 100 white blood cells were randomly counted and classified by oil immersion microscopy.

LMBRaV challenge test

At 35 days post immunization, 100 µL 105 TCID50/mL LMBRaV were injected intraperitoneally to the fish with a total 30 individuals in each group, and the fishes were kept at 28 °C. Mortality was monitored over a period of 14 days and LMBRaV viral load in the spleen, head kidney and liver of challenged fish on day 10 was confirmed. After the standard curve was established through pET19T-LMBRaV MCP plasmid by gradient diluted and detected by qPCR, the virus load was calculated. The relative percent survival (RPS) was calculated according to the formula: RPS = [1- (% mortality of vaccinated group/% mortality of control group)] × 100 %.

Statistical analysis

The data were expressed as means ± standard deviation (SD). One-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was used for multiple comparisons to determine significant differences between groups by using GraphPad Prism 5 software (GraphPad Software Inc. La Jolla, CA, USA). Error bars indicate the mean ± SD (n = 3). Statistical significance was defined as p < 0.05 and an extremely significant difference was defined as p < 0.01.

Results

Identification of Bacmid-MCP

PCR with universal primers M13-F/R was used to verify the insertion of the target gene from pFastBac-MCP containing Tn7 (2300 bp) and mcp (1392 bp) into the mini-attTn7 site of the complementary region in Bacmid DNA. The recombinant Bacmid-MCP was verified with agarose gel electrophoresis using M13-F/R. The PCR product of recombinant Bacmid-MCP is 3692 bp in size, while that of Bacmid plasmid is 300 bp (Fig. 1).

Changes in length and weight and counts of leukocytes

Compared with Group CK and PBS group, largemouth bass immunized with LMBRaV MCP were longer and heavier (Fig. 4A and 4B). The percentage of total leucocytes in immunized group increased from 5 day post immunization (d.p.i.), and peaked at 35 d.p.i. (5.6 fold, p < 0.01) (Fig. 5A). Compared with control groups, the percentage of lymphocytes in immunized group was significantly (p < 0.01) higher at 14, 21, 28 and 35 d.p.i. (Fig. 5B).

Expression of immune-related genes

(5.6-fold), down-regulated to at 7 d.p.i. to 21 d.p.i., up-regulated again at 28 d.p.i. (4.6-fold) and then back to the initial level at 35 d.p.i. (Fig. 6A). The expression level of irf3 exhibited no significance in immunized group and the control groups (Fig. 6B). The expression level of irf9 was significantly (p < 0.05) higher than that of control groups at 3, 5, 14, and 28 d.p.i. (Fig. 6C). Mx mRNA levels of immunized group in spleen increased significantly (p < 0.01) from 3 d.p.i. to 5 d.p.i., and 28 d.p.i. (Fig. 6D). Il-1β in immunized groups showed higher expression levels at 3,5 and 28 d.p.i. (Fig. 6E). Compared with the control groups, the relative expression of tlr3 in immunized group was significantly (p < 0.01) up-regulated from 5 d.p.i. and peaked at 14 d.p.i. (Fig. 6F).

Challenge test

After challenged with LMBRaV for 14 days, the LMBRaV MCP immunized group exhibited a higher survival rate of 63.3 %, while that of group CK and PBS group was 0 % (Fig. 8). Fish without stimulation was set as blank, and the survival rate was 100 %. The RPS of LMBRaV MCP immunized group was 63.3 %.

Discussion

Largemouth bass is one of the most important commercial fish in China, with the increasing production year by year. However, the disease caused by LMBRaV led to a high morality and there is no effective drug. Vaccination has been confirmed to an effective measure to avoid viral infection. Compared to inactivated vaccines and attenuated vaccines, the baculovirus expression system is widely applied to the production of subunit vaccines because of its biological safety and low cytotoxicity [13,21]. Moreover, insect cells can be cultured adherent to a surface or in suspension (suitable for large-scale cell culture), which is suitable for expression of foreign proteins [22]. Some insects, such as silkworm, are natural hosts of baculovirus, and can express proteins in silkworm pupae in large amounts to establish insect bioreactor [23,24]. Besides, insect is a kind of aquatic feed, which can be used as a potential oral vaccine [25]. In our previous study, the VP35-VP4 subunit vaccine expressed by recombinant baculovirus AcMNPV-VP35-VP4 could induce high immunity and protect against GCRV infection [16]. In this study, the baculovirus expression system was selected as the expression vector for the production of subunit vaccines against LMBRaV in Sf9 cells, with MCP as the antigen. Western blot and titer assay identified that the recombinant AcMNPV-MCP was successfully acquired.

To detect the protective effect on preventing LMBRaV invasion after subunit vaccine immunization, the survival rate of largemouth bass was measured. The mortality decreased to 36.7 % after injected with AcMNPV-MCP while the mortality is 100 % in control groups, indicating that the subunit vaccine is an effective prevention for LMBRaV infection. Moreover, the immunized largemouth bass was heavier and longer, suggesting fish treated with the subunit vaccine was in a healthier and stronger condition. Additionally, monitoring changes in the subpopulation of leucocytes provided valuable information on the vaccine's ability to stimulate the fish's immune system and potentially enhance its ability to combat infections or diseases. A subunit vaccine expressing part of the major capsid protein (MCP) using E. coli prokaryotic expression exhibited strong adaptive immune responses against LMBV, with a protection rate of 80.5 % [26]. The recombinant baculoviruses AcMNPV-VP35-VP4 provided a better protection against GCRV infection than AcMNPV-VP35 and AcMNPV-VP4 [16]. In our further study, the vaccine with specific antigenic dominant epitope and protein string using the baculovirus expression system will be made and investigated for a better immune protection rate.

Interferon (IFN) signal pathways and interleukin (IL) -mediated pro-inflammatory responses play crucial roles during immune responses against virus invasion [27]. After recognized by pathogen-associated molecular patterns (PAMPs), pattern-recognition receptors (PRRs) including Toll-like receptor (TLRs) then activated a signaling cascade triggering the type Ⅰ IFN and pro-inflammatory cytokines [28]. TLR3, a member of TLR family and usually activated by dsRNA, was also proved to participate in the recognition of DNA viruses [29]. After the submit vaccine prepared from a DNA virus LMBRaV, tlr3 mRNA levels of largemouth bass significantly up-regulated in the immune related tissues. As a transcription mediator involved in type Ⅰ IFNs, interferon regulatory factor 3 (IRF3) of Japanese flounder was up-regulated by polyI:C and lymphocystis disease virus (LCDV) [30]. In this study, after immunization, the mRNA expression of irf3 was significantly induced in head kidney, while that in spleen was no obvious change. Ifnγ of largemouth bass exhibited a positive response to the vaccine both in spleen and head kidney, which indicated that the most important element against LMBRaV invasion was activated after immunization. After the activation of fish type Ⅰ IFNs, an anti-virial state was produced through JAK/STAT signal transduction and the induction of IRF9, finally released many IFN-stimulated genes (ISGs) including the antiviralmolecule myxovirus (influenza) resistance protein (Mx) which directly act on virus [31]. Our results showed that the mRNA expressions of irf9 and mx were significantly up-regulated. The up-regulation of il-1β suggested that vaccination activated the proinflammatory responses. By analyzing the expression levels of above immune-related genes post-vaccination, the AcMNPV-MCP subunit vaccine elicited functional immune response by inducing IFN signaling and pro-inflammatory cytokines.

In conclusion, a subunit vaccine utilizing BEVS, specifically AcMNPV-MCP, was successfully constructed and injected to largemouth bass at the peritoneum. The impact of this vaccine was verified by analyze the relative expression of immune-related genes and the alteration in the subpopulation of leucocytes in the fish. This study represents a significant step towards understanding the immunological response of largemouth bass to the AcMNPV-MCP subunit vaccine, shedding light on its potential efficacy in bolstering the fish's immune defenses.

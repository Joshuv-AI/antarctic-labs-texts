Sharp concentration of simple random tensors
Omar Al-Ghattas, Jiaheng Chen*, Daniel Sanz-Alonso

Abstract
This paper establishes sharp dimension-free concentration inequalities and expectation bounds for the deviation of the sum of simple random tensors from its expectation. As part of our analysis, we use generic chaining techniques to obtain a sharp high-probability upper bound on the suprema of multi-product empirical processes. In so doing, we generalize classical results for quadratic and product empirical processes to higher-order settings.

Introduction

This paper establishes sharp bounds for the operator-norm deviation of the sum of simple (rank-one) random tensors from its expectation. Let
X,X_{1},\ldots,X_{N}
be i.i.d. centered Gaussian random variables in a separable Hilbert space
H
with covariance operator
\Sigma
.
Our first main result, Theorem
2.1
, shows that, with a standard notation described below, for any integer
p\geq 2
,

\mathbb{E}\bigg\|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}X_{i}^{\otimes p}-\mathbb{E}\,X^{\otimes p}\bigg\|\asymp_{p}\|\Sigma\|^{p/2}\bigg(\sqrt{\frac{r(\Sigma)}{N}}+\frac{r(\Sigma)^{p/2}}{N}\bigg),\quad r(\Sigma):=\frac{\mathrm{Tr}(\Sigma)}{\|\Sigma\|}.

In addition, Theorem
2.1
establishes sharp high-probability and expectation bounds for sub-Gaussian data. To prove Theorem
2.1
, we analyze the suprema of a multi-product empirical process. In our second main result, Theorem
2.2
, we leverage generic chaining techniques to obtain a sharp high-probability bound on

\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\bigg|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}f^{p}(X_{i})-\mathbb{E}f^{p}(X)\bigg|,\qquad p\geq 2,

in terms of quantities that reflect the geometric complexity of the family
\mathcal{F},
taken to contain bounded linear functionals in
H
in the proof of Theorem
2.1
.

Notation

Given two positive sequences
\{a_{n}\}
and
\{b_{n}\}
, we write
a_{n}\lesssim b_{n}
to denote that
a_{n}\leq cb_{n}
for some absolute constant
c>0
. If both
a_{n}\lesssim b_{n}
and
b_{n}\lesssim a_{n}
hold simultaneously, we write
a_{n}\asymp b_{n}
. If the constant
c
depends on some parameter
\tau
, we use the notations
a_{n}\lesssim_{\tau}b_{n},b_{n}\lesssim_{\tau}a_{n}
, and
a_{n}\asymp_{\tau}b_{n}
to indicate this dependence.

2.1
Concentration of simple random tensors

Let
(H,\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{H},\|\cdot\|_{H})
be a separable real Hilbert space and let
p
be a positive integer. Let
X
be a centered
H
-valued random variable. Suppose that
\mathbb{E}|\langle X,v\rangle_{H}|^{p}<\infty
for all
v\in U_{H}:=\{v\in H:\|v\|_{H}=1\}
and let
X^{\otimes p}
be the multilinear form

X^{\otimes p}(v_{1},\ldots,v_{p}):=\langle X,v_{1}\rangle_{H}\cdots\langle X,v_{p}\rangle_{H},\qquad\forall\ (v_{1},\ldots,v_{p})\in H\times\cdots\times H.

Thus,
X^{\otimes p}
is a random variable taking values in the tensor space
H^{\otimes p}.
We refer for instance to
[
66
, Section II.4]
for background on tensor products of Hilbert spaces. The expectation
\mathbb{E}\,X^{\otimes p}
is called the
p
-th order moment tensor
of
X.
When
p=2,
the second-order moment tensor
\mathbb{E}\,X^{\otimes 2}(v_{1},v_{2})=\mathbb{E}\langle X,v_{1}\rangle_{H}\langle X,v_{2}\rangle_{H},

\,\forall\,(v_{1},v_{2})\in H\times H
is called the
covariance
of
X.
By the Riesz representation theorem, this bilinear form can be represented by the
covariance operator

\Sigma:H\to H
defined by

\langle v_{1},\Sigma v_{2}\rangle_{H}:=\mathbb{E}\,X^{\otimes 2}(v_{1},v_{2}),\qquad\forall\ (v_{1},v_{2})\in H\times H.

Given independent copies
X_{1},\ldots,X_{N}
of
X,
our goal is to establish concentration inequalities for the
p
-th order sample moment tensor
\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}X_{i}^{\otimes p},
which is a sum of i.i.d. simple (rank-one) random tensors. Specifically, we seek to obtain high-probability and expectation bounds on the deviation

\bigg\|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}X_{i}^{\otimes p}-\mathbb{E}\,X^{\otimes p}\bigg\|=\sup_{v_{1},\ldots,v_{p}\in U_{H}}\bigg|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\langle X_{i},v_{1}\rangle_{H}\cdots\langle X_{i},v_{p}\rangle_{H}-\mathbb{E}\langle X,v_{1}\rangle_{H}\cdots\langle X,v_{p}\rangle_{H}\bigg|,

(2.1)

where
\|\cdot\|
denotes the norm in the tensor space
H^{\otimes p}.
Notice that for
p=2,
the operator norm of the covariance operator
\Sigma:H\to H
agrees with the norm of the covariance
\mathbb{E}\,X^{\otimes 2}
as a bilinear form in
H^{\otimes 2}.
Consequently, we will slightly abuse notation and denote by
\|\Sigma\|
the operator norm of the covariance operator. Furthermore, to simplify the notation, we will henceforth omit the subscript
H
in the inner-product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{H}
and norm
\|\cdot\|_{H}
.

We will focus on Gaussian and sub-Gaussian random variables. Recall that an
H
-valued random variable
X
is called
(sub)-Gaussian
iff all one-dimensional projections
\langle X,v\rangle
for
v\in H
are (sub)-Gaussian real-valued random variables. Furthermore,
X
is called
pre-Gaussian
iff there exists a centered Gaussian random variable
Y
in
H
with the same covariance operator as that of
X.

Our first main result, Theorem
2.1
, provides sharp dimension-free bounds for the deviation (
2.1
) in terms of the
effective rank

r(\Sigma):=\text{Tr}(\Sigma)/\|\Sigma\|
of the covariance operator.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
X,X_{1},\ldots,X_{N}
be i.i.d. centered sub-Gaussian and pre-Gaussian random variables in
H
with covariance operator
\Sigma
. For any integer
p\geq 2
and any
u\geq 1
, it holds with probability at least
1-\exp(-u)
that

\bigg\|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}X_{i}^{\otimes p}-\mathbb{E}\,X^{\otimes p}\bigg\|\lesssim_{p}\|\Sigma\|^{p/2}\bigg(\sqrt{\frac{r(\Sigma)}{N}}+\frac{r(\Sigma)^{p/2}}{N}+\sqrt{\frac{u}{N}}+\frac{u^{p/2}}{N}\bigg).

As a corollary,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg\|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}X_{i}^{\otimes p}-\mathbb{E}\,X^{\otimes p}\bigg\|\lesssim_{p}\|\Sigma\|^{p/2}\bigg(\sqrt{\frac{r(\Sigma)}{N}}+\frac{r(\Sigma)^{p/2}}{N}\bigg).

Moreover, if
X
is Gaussian, then

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg\|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}X_{i}^{\otimes p}-\mathbb{E}\,X^{\otimes p}\bigg\|\asymp_{p}\|\Sigma\|^{p/2}\bigg(\sqrt{\frac{r(\Sigma)}{N}}+\frac{r(\Sigma)^{p/2}}{N}\bigg).

The proof of Theorem
2.1
is carried out in Section
3
.
Although upper bounds for (
2.1
) have been extensively studied
[
20
,
23
,
47
,
2
,
77
,
53
,
19
,
84
]
, and despite the explosive growth of research on concentration inequalities for random tensors
[
79
,
85
,
19
,
5
,
28
,
14
,
7
,
8
,
68
,
9
]
, Theorem
2.1
is, to the best of our knowledge, the first to establish
sharp dimension-free
concentration inequalities and expectation bounds for the deviation (
2.1
) under sub-Gaussian assumptions, without superfluous logarithmic factors. Moreover, Theorem
2.1
shows that our expectation bound is sharp for Gaussian data. Similarly to
[
38
]
, our results can easily be extended to Banach-valued random variables, but we focus on the Hilbert space setting for ease of exposition.

The remainder of this subsection provides detailed comparisons between Theorem
2.1
and existing results. In Remark
2.1
, we compare with concentration results for sums of simple tensors, while in Remark
2.2
we discuss the special case
p=2.

Remark 2.1
.

The deviation (
2.1
) can be expressed as

\displaystyle\bigg\|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}X_{i}^{\otimes p}-\mathbb{E}\,X^{\otimes p}\bigg\|=\sup_{\|v\|=1}\bigg|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\langle X_{i},v\right\rangle^{p}-\mathbb{E}\langle X,v\rangle^{p}\bigg|,

(2.2)

since for symmetric tensors the supremum in (
2.1
) is attained by a single element
v\in H
, see e.g.
[
84
, (1.6)]
,
[
62
, Section 2.3]
and
[
9
, Remark 4.14, Proposition 4.15]
. From our proof of Theorem
2.1
, the same high-probability and in-expectation bounds in Theorem
2.1
also hold for
\sup_{\|v\|=1}\left|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left|\langle X_{i},v\right\rangle|^{p}-\mathbb{E}|\langle X,v\rangle|^{p}\right|
, where
\langle X_{i},v\rangle^{p}
is replaced by its absolute value.

Using majorizing measures,
[
23
, Theorem 3]
establishes the bound

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\sup_{\|v\|\leq 1}\bigg|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}|\langle X_{i},v\rangle|^{p}-\mathbb{E}|\langle X,v\rangle|^{p}\bigg|\lesssim_{p}\|\Sigma\|^{p/2}\left(\sqrt{\varepsilon}+\varepsilon\right),

(2.3)

where

\varepsilon=\frac{\log N}{N}\cdot\frac{\mathbb{E}\max_{1\leq i\leq N}\|X_{i}\|^{p}}{\|\Sigma\|^{p/2}}.

Notably,
[
23
, Theorem 3]
considered the more general case where the supremum in (
2.3
) is taken over a symmetric convex body
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Here, we focus on the specific case where
K
is the unit ball
\{v:\|v\|\leq 1\}
. In the sub-Gaussian setting, applying
[
84
, Lemma 2.7]
together with a union bound gives

\bigg(\mathbb{E}\max_{1\leq i\leq N}\left\|X_{i}\right\|^{p}\bigg)^{1/p}\lesssim_{p}\sqrt{\mathrm{Tr}(\Sigma)+\|\Sigma\|\log N}=\sqrt{\|\Sigma\|\left(r(\Sigma)+\log N\right)}.

For Gaussian data, this upper bound is optimal up to a constant depending only on
p
. Consequently, this yields the following upper bound for the value of
\varepsilon
in (
2.3
):

\varepsilon\lesssim_{p}\frac{(\log N)\left(r(\Sigma)^{p/2}+(\log N)^{p/2}\right)}{N}.

Employing estimates of the metric entropy of ellipsoids,
[
19
, Theorem 3]
shows that, for i.i.d. centered sub-Gaussian random vectors
X,X_{1},\ldots,X_{N}
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\mathbb{E}\bigg\|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}X_{i}^{\otimes p}-\mathbb{E}\,X^{\otimes p}\bigg\|\lesssim_{p}\|\Sigma\|^{p/2}\sqrt{\frac{(\log N)^{p}(r(\Sigma)+\log d)^{p+1}}{N}}.

(2.4)

Clearly, the in-expectation bound in Theorem
2.1
provides a direct improvement over the bounds in (
2.3
) and (
2.4
).

More recently,
[
84
]
used the variational principle and the PAC-Bayesian method to study (
2.2
). We now compare Theorem
2.1
with
[
84
, Theorem 1.7]
. Under their assumption that
N\geq r(\Sigma)^{p-1}
, we observe that
\sqrt{\frac{r(\Sigma)}{N}}\geq\frac{r(\Sigma)^{p/2}}{N},
and taking
u=r(\Sigma)
in Theorem
2.1
yields that with probability at least
1-\exp(-r(\Sigma))
,

\bigg\|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}X_{i}^{\otimes p}-\mathbb{E}\,X^{\otimes p}\bigg\|=\sup_{\|v\|=1}\bigg|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\langle X_{i},v\right\rangle^{p}-\mathbb{E}\langle X,v\rangle^{p}\bigg|\overset{\hskip-2.84544pt\text{($\star$)}}{\lesssim_{p}}\|\Sigma\|^{p/2}\sqrt{\frac{r(\Sigma)}{N}}.

For
H=\mathbb{R}^{d}
and under the condition
N\geq r(\Sigma)^{p-1}
,
[
84
, Theorem 1.7]
establishes the bound (
\star
), but only with probability at least
1-cN\exp(-\sqrt{r(\Sigma)})
.
Theorem
2.1
hence directly improves
[
84
, Theorem 1.7]
for sub-Gaussian data. Moreover,
our high-probability bound can be directly integrated to derive an in-expectation bound, which is not provided in
[
84
, Theorem 1.7]
. The proof of
[
84
, Theorem 1.7]
relies on a combination of the variational inequality approach and the decoupling-chaining method developed in
[
2
,
70
]
. In the decoupling-chaining argument
[
70
, Proposition 14.3.3]
, there is a crucial term:
\max_{1\leq i\leq N}\|X_{i}\|
.
[
84
]
used a union bound to control this term, which results in a large prefactor
N
in the probability estimate. Since for Gaussian data it holds that

\mathbb{E}\max_{1\leq i\leq N}\|X_{i}\|\asymp\sqrt{\|\Sigma\|(r(\Sigma)+\log N)},

an additional logarithmic factor
\sqrt{\log N}
is likely unavoidable when using the approach in
[
84
]
to derive an in-expectation bound. Finally, in contrast to
[
84
, Theorem 1.7]
, our proof of Theorem
2.1
shows a bound of the form (
\star
) for arbitrary
p\geq 2,
not necessarily an integer.

Remark 2.2
.

When
p=2
, the sum of simple random tensors reduces to the sample covariance. In this case, Theorem
2.1
agrees with the optimal bounds established in
[
38
, Theorem 4, Theorem 9]
.
An important area of research in probability and statistics focuses on the non-asymptotic analysis of sample covariance operators, particularly their estimation error in the spectral (operator) norm relative to its population version.
Recent references include, for instance,
[
67
,
76
,
72
,
73
,

,

,

,

,

,
71
,
25
,
7
,
15
,
8
,
84
]
. A key milestone was achieved by Koltchinskii and Lounici
[

]
, who established that for i.i.d. centered Gaussian random variables
X,X_{1},\ldots,X_{N}
with covariance operator
\Sigma
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg\|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}X_{i}\otimes X_{i}-\mathbb{E}\,X\otimes X\bigg\|\asymp\|\Sigma\|\bigg(\sqrt{\frac{r(\Sigma)}{N}}+\frac{r(\Sigma)}{N}\bigg).

(2.5)

The upper bound (

) in
[

]
follows from results on quadratic empirical processes developed in

,

,

,

]
that extend beyond Gaussian data. Alternative proofs exist for Gaussian data outside the empirical process framework. In particular,

2.2
Multi-product empirical processes

This subsection contains the statement of our second main result, Theorem
2.2
, which gives a general upper bound on the suprema of multi-product empirical processes. Theorem
2.2
will be key to establish our upper bounds in Theorem
2.1
. Let
X,X_{1},\ldots,X_{N}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{i.i.d.}}}{{\sim}}\mu
be a sequence of random variables on a probability space
(\Omega,\mu)
. For
p\geq 1
(not necessarily an integer), we consider the
multi-product
centered empirical process indexed by a class
\mathcal{F}
of functions on
(\Omega,\mu)
given by

\displaystyle f\mapsto\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}f^{p}(X_{i})-\mathbb{E}f^{p}(X),\qquad f\in\mathcal{F}.

(2.6)

In our proof of Theorem
2.1
, the class
\mathcal{F}
will be taken to contain all linear functionals in
H
with norm one. For
p=1
, (
2.6
) recovers the standard empirical process
[
74
,
70
]
, and for
p=2
the quadratic empirical process extensively studied, e.g., in
[
67
,
30
,
54
,
2
,
48
,
55
,
17
,
11
,
51
,
12
,
38
,
25
]
. Beyond its theoretical significance, the quadratic empirical process plays a crucial role in various statistical applications, including covariance estimation as discussed in Remark
2.2
. Our goal is to bound

\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\bigg|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}f^{p}(X_{i})-\mathbb{E}f^{p}(X)\bigg|

for any
p\geq 2
in terms of quantities that reflect the geometric complexity of the indexing class
\mathcal{F}.
To that end, for any function
f
on
(\Omega,\mu)
, we introduce the Orlicz
\psi_{2}
-norm of
f
given by

\|f\|_{\psi_{2}}:=\inf\left\{c>0:\mathbb{E}_{X\sim\mu}\left[\exp\left(\frac{|f(X)|^{2}}{c^{2}}\right)\right]\leq 2\right\},

with the convention
\inf\emptyset=\infty.

Our upper bound will depend on
d_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F}):=\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\|f\|_{\psi_{2}}
and on Talagrand’s functional
\gamma(\mathcal{F},\psi_{2})
, whose definition we now recall.

Definition 2.1

(Talagrand’s
\gamma
functional
[
70
]
)
.

Let
(\mathcal{F},d)
be a metric space. An admissible sequence is an increasing sequence
(\mathcal{F}_{s})_{s\geq 0}\subset\mathcal{F}
which satisfies
\mathcal{F}_{s}\subset\mathcal{F}_{s+1},\left|\mathcal{F}_{0}\right|=1,\left|\mathcal{F}_{s}\right|\leq 2^{2^{s}}
for
s\geq 1,
and
\bigcup_{s=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{F}_{s}
is dense in
\mathcal{F}
.
Let

\gamma(\mathcal{F},d):=\inf\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\sum_{s\geq 0}2^{s/2}d\left(f,\mathcal{F}_{s}\right),

where the infimum is taken over all admissible sequences. We write
\gamma(\mathcal{F},\psi_{2})
when the distance on
\mathcal{\mathcal{F}}
is induced by the
\psi_{2}
-norm.

We are ready to state our second main result. Its proof can be found in Section
4
.

Theorem 2.2
.

Assume
0\in\mathcal{F}
or
\mathcal{F}
is symmetric (i.e.,
f\in\mathcal{F}
implies
-f\in\mathcal{F}
). For any
p\geq 2
and
u\geq 1
, it holds with probability at least
1-\exp(-u^{2}(\gamma(\mathcal{F},\psi_{2})/d_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F}))^{2})
that

\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\bigg|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}f^{p}(X_{i})-\mathbb{E}f^{p}(X)\bigg|\lesssim_{p}u\frac{\gamma(\mathcal{F},\psi_{2})d^{p-1}_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F})}{\sqrt{N}}+u^{p}\frac{\gamma^{p}(\mathcal{F},\psi_{2})}{N}.

As a corollary,

\mathbb{E}\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\bigg|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}f^{p}(X_{i})-\mathbb{E}f^{p}(X)\bigg|\lesssim_{p}\frac{\gamma(\mathcal{F},\psi_{2})d^{p-1}_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F})}{\sqrt{N}}+\frac{\gamma^{p}(\mathcal{F},\psi_{2})}{N}.

Remark 2.3
.

An alternative formulation of the tail bound states that, for any
u\geq 1
, it holds with probability at least
1-\exp(-u)
that

\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\bigg|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}f^{p}\left(X_{i}\right)-\mathbb{E}f^{p}(X)\bigg|\lesssim_{p}\frac{\gamma\left(\mathcal{F},\psi_{2}\right)d^{p-1}_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F})}{\sqrt{N}}+\frac{\gamma^{p}\left(\mathcal{F},\psi_{2}\right)}{N}+d^{p}_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F})\bigg(\sqrt{\frac{u}{N}}+\frac{u^{p/2}}{N}\bigg).

The above high-probability bound is optimal. When
p=2
, it matches the tail bound obtained in
[
17
,
11
]
, see also
[
51
, (1.18)]
and
[
38
, Theorem 8]
. As noted in
[
51
]
, the optimality can be seen from setting
t=

ud_{\psi_{2}}^{2}(\mathcal{F})/\sqrt{N}
in Bernstein’s inequality applied to
f^{2}
. When
p>2
, the optimality can be deduced from the
\alpha
-sub-exponential inequality
[
22
, Corollary 1.4]
; see Lemma
B.1
for a formal statement. From our proof, the same high-probability and in-expectation bounds in Theorem
2.2
also hold for the empirical process
\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\left|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}|f|^{p}(X_{i})-\mathbb{E}|f|^{p}(X)\right|
, where
f^{p}(X_{i})
is replaced by its absolute value.

Remark 2.4
.

For
p\geq 3/2
, a minor modification of our proof yields

\mathbb{E}\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\bigg|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}f^{p}(X_{i})-\mathbb{E}f^{p}(X)\bigg|\lesssim_{p}\frac{\gamma(\mathcal{F},\psi_{2})d^{p-1}_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F})}{\sqrt{N}}+\frac{\gamma^{p}(\mathcal{F},\psi_{2})}{N}+\frac{\gamma^{3/2}(\mathcal{F},\psi_{2})d^{p-3/2}_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F})}{N^{3/4}}.

If
p\geq 2
, the third term on the right-hand side is dominated by the first two terms. However, for
p\in[3/2,2)
, the third term remains significant and cannot be neglected. A natural question is to establish sharp concentration inequalities and expectation bounds for
\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\left|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}|f|^{p}(X_{i})-\mathbb{E}|f|^{p}(X)\right|
for any
p>0
. We leave this question for future investigation.

3
Concentration of simple random tensors

This section contains the proof of our first main result, Theorem
2.1
. The upper bound for sub-Gaussian data follows from an application of Theorem
2.2
, where the class
\mathcal{F}
consists of all linear functionals in
H
with norm one. To establish the matching lower bound for Gaussian data, we adapt techniques from
[
38
, Theorem 4]
to the higher-order setting; see Proposition
3.1
for the proof.

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.

By the expression (
2.2
) and Theorem
2.2
,
we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg\|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}X_{i}^{\otimes p}-\mathbb{E}\,X^{\otimes p}\bigg\|

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\sup_{\|v\|=1}\bigg|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\langle X_{i},v\right\rangle^{p}-\mathbb{E}\langle X,v\rangle^{p}\bigg|

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\bigg|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}f^{p}(X_{i})-\mathbb{E}f^{p}(X)\bigg|

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p}\frac{\gamma(\mathcal{F},\psi_{2})d^{p-1}_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F})}{\sqrt{N}}+\frac{\gamma^{p}(\mathcal{F},\psi_{2})}{N},

where
\mathcal{F}:=\left\{\langle\cdot,v\rangle:v\in U_{H}\right\},U_{H}:=\left\{v\in H:\|v\|=1\right\}
.
It is clear that
\mathcal{F}
is symmetric. Since
X
is sub-Gaussian, the
\psi_{2}
-norm of linear functionals is equivalent to the
L_{2}
-norm. Hence,

\displaystyle d_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F})=\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\|f\|_{\psi_{2}}\asymp\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\|f\|_{L_{2}}=\sup_{\|v\|=1}\left(\mathbb{E}\langle X,v\rangle^{2}\right)^{1/2}=\|\Sigma\|^{1/2}.

Moreover, since
X
is pre-Gaussian, there exists a centered Gaussian random variable
Y
in
H
with the same covariance
\Sigma
. This means that

d_{Y}(u,v):=\left(\mathbb{E}(\langle Y,u\rangle-\langle Y,v\rangle)^{2}\right)^{1/2}=\langle u-v,\Sigma(u-v)\rangle^{1/2}=\|\langle\cdot,u\rangle-\langle\cdot,v\rangle\|_{L_{2}(\mu)},\quad u,v\in U_{H},

where
\mu
is the law of
X
. Using Talagrand’s majorizing-measure theorem
[
70
, Theorem 2.10.1]
, we deduce that

\gamma\left(\mathcal{F},\psi_{2}\right)\asymp\gamma\left(\mathcal{F},L_{2}\right)=\gamma\left(U_{H},d_{Y}\right)\asymp\mathbb{E}\sup_{u\in U_{H}}\langle Y,u\rangle=\mathbb{E}\|Y\|\asymp(\mathbb{E}\|Y\|^{2})^{1/2}=\mathrm{Tr}(\Sigma)^{1/2}.

Therefore, we obtain the in-expectation upper bound for sub-Gaussian data:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg\|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}X_{i}^{\otimes p}-\mathbb{E}\,X^{\otimes p}\bigg\|

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p}\frac{\gamma(\mathcal{F},\psi_{2})d^{p-1}_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F})}{\sqrt{N}}+\frac{\gamma^{p}(\mathcal{F},\psi_{2})}{N}\asymp\|\Sigma\|^{p/2}\bigg(\sqrt{\frac{r(\Sigma)}{N}}+\frac{r(\Sigma)^{p/2}}{N}\bigg).

The high-probability upper bound follows analogously. The matching lower bound for Gaussian data is proved in Proposition
3.1
below.
∎

Now we prove the matching lower bound for Gaussian data in Theorem
2.1
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
X,X_{1},\ldots,X_{N}
be i.i.d. centered Gaussian random variables in
H
with covariance operator
\Sigma
. Then, for any integer
p\geq 2
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg\|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}X_{i}^{\otimes p}-\mathbb{E}\,X^{\otimes p}\bigg\|\gtrsim_{p}\|\Sigma\|^{p/2}\bigg(\sqrt{\frac{r(\Sigma)}{N}}+\frac{r(\Sigma)^{p/2}}{N}\bigg).

Proof of Proposition
3.1
.

The proof builds upon ideas from
[
38
, Theorem 4]
and
[
75
, Lemma 5.6]
. Throughout the proof,
c(p)
,
c^{\prime}(p)
and
C(p)
denote positive constants depending only on
p
. Recall that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg\|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}X_{i}^{\otimes p}-\mathbb{E}\,X^{\otimes p}\bigg\|=\mathbb{E}\sup_{\|v\|=1}\bigg|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\langle X_{i},v\right\rangle^{p}-\mathbb{E}\langle X,v\rangle^{p}\bigg|.

(3.1)

We first prove that, for any
p\geq 2
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\sup_{\|v\|=1}\bigg|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\langle X_{i},v\right\rangle^{p}-\mathbb{E}\langle X,v\rangle^{p}\bigg|\gtrsim_{p}\|\Sigma\|^{p/2}\frac{r(\Sigma)^{p/2}}{N}.

(3.2)

Observe that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\sup_{\|v\|=1}\bigg|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\langle X_{i},v\right\rangle^{p}-\mathbb{E}\langle X,v\rangle^{p}\bigg|\geq\mathbb{E}_{X_{1}}\sup_{\|v\|=1}\bigg|\mathbb{E}_{\{X_{i}\}_{i\geq 2}}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\left\langle X_{i},v\right\rangle^{p}-\mathbb{E}\langle X,v\rangle^{p}\right)\bigg|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\mathbb{E}_{X_{1}}\sup_{\|v\|=1}\bigg|\left\langle X_{1},v\right\rangle^{p}-\mathbb{E}\langle X,v\rangle^{p}\bigg|\geq\frac{1}{N}\bigg(\mathbb{E}_{X_{1}}\Big[\,\sup_{\|v\|=1}\left|\langle X_{1},v\rangle^{p}\right|\Big]-\sup_{\|v\|=1}\left|\mathbb{E}\langle X,v\rangle^{p}\right|\bigg)

\displaystyle\overset{\text{($\star$)}}{\geq}\frac{1}{N}\left(\mathbb{E}\|X_{1}\|^{p}-C(p)\|\Sigma\|^{p/2}\right)\overset{\text{($\star\star$)}}{\geq}\frac{1}{N}\left(\mathrm{Tr}(\Sigma)^{p/2}-C(p)\|\Sigma\|^{p/2}\right)=\frac{1}{N}\|\Sigma\|^{p/2}\left(r(\Sigma)^{p/2}-C(p)\right).

(3.3)

Here, (
\star
) follows by
\mathbb{E}_{X_{1}}\left[\sup_{\|v\|=1}|\langle X_{1},v\rangle^{p}|\right]=\mathbb{E}\|X_{1}\|^{p}
and

\sup_{\|v\|=1}|\mathbb{E}\langle X,v\rangle^{p}|\leq C(p)\|\Sigma\|^{p/2}
, since
\langle X,v\rangle\overset{\mathsf{d}}{\sim}\mathcal{N}(0,\langle\Sigma v,v\rangle)
, while (
\star\star
) follows by Jensen’s inequality
\mathbb{E}\|X_{1}\|^{p}\geq\left(\mathbb{E}\|X_{1}\|^{2}\right)^{p/2}=\mathrm{Tr}(\Sigma)^{p/2}
for
p\geq 2
.

We define the i.i.d. standard Gaussian random variables

Z_{i}:=\frac{\langle X_{i},v\rangle}{\langle\Sigma v,v\rangle^{1/2}},\quad i=1,\ldots,N.

It follows that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\sup_{\|v\|=1}\bigg|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\langle X_{i},v\right\rangle^{p}-\mathbb{E}\langle X,v\rangle^{p}\bigg|

\displaystyle\geq\sup_{\|v\|=1}\mathbb{E}\bigg|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\langle X_{i},v\rangle^{p}-\mathbb{E}\langle X,v\rangle^{p}\bigg|

\displaystyle=\sup_{\|v\|=1}\langle\Sigma v,v\rangle^{p/2}\mathbb{E}\bigg|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}Z_{i}^{p}-\mathbb{E}Z^{p}\bigg|

\displaystyle\geq\|\Sigma\|^{p/2}\frac{c(p)}{\sqrt{N}}\geq\|\Sigma\|^{p/2}\frac{c(p)}{N}.

(3.4)

Therefore, combining (
3
) and (
3
) gives that, for any
t\in[0,1]
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\sup_{\|v\|=1}\bigg|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\langle X_{i},v\right\rangle^{p}-\mathbb{E}\langle X,v\rangle^{p}\bigg|\geq\|\Sigma\|^{p/2}\left(t\frac{1}{N}\left(r(\Sigma)^{p/2}-C(p)\right)+(1-t)\frac{c(p)}{N}\right).

Choosing
t
to be small enough so that
tC(p)\leq(1-t)c(p)
, we get

\mathbb{E}\sup_{\|v\|=1}\bigg|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\langle X_{i},v\right\rangle^{p}-\mathbb{E}\langle X,v\rangle^{p}\bigg|\geq t\|\Sigma\|^{p/2}\frac{r(\Sigma)^{p/2}}{N}\asymp_{p}\|\Sigma\|^{p/2}\frac{r(\Sigma)^{p/2}}{N},

as desired.

Next, we show that for any
p\geq 2
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\sup_{\|v\|=1}\bigg|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\langle X_{i},v\right\rangle^{p}-\mathbb{E}\langle X,v\rangle^{p}\bigg|\gtrsim_{p}\|\Sigma\|^{p/2}\sqrt{\frac{r(\Sigma)}{N}}.

(3.5)

Observe that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\sup_{\|v\|=1}\bigg|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\langle X_{i},v\right\rangle^{p}-\mathbb{E}\langle X,v\rangle^{p}\bigg|

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\bigg\|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}X_{i}^{\otimes p}-\mathbb{E}\,X^{\otimes p}\bigg\|

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\sup_{\|v\|=1}\bigg\|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\langle X_{i},v\rangle^{p-1}X_{i}-\mathbb{E}\langle X,v\rangle^{p-1}X\bigg\|

\displaystyle\geq\sup_{\|v\|=1}\mathbb{E}\bigg\|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\langle X_{i},v\rangle^{p-1}X_{i}-\mathbb{E}\langle X,v\rangle^{p-1}X\bigg\|.

(3.6)

For a fixed
v
with
\|v\|=1
and
\langle\Sigma v,v\rangle>0
, denote

X^{\prime}:=X-\langle X,v\rangle\frac{\Sigma v}{\langle\Sigma v,v\rangle}.

By a straightforward computation, for all
u\in H
, the random variables
\langle X,v\rangle
and
\left\langle X^{\prime},u\right\rangle
are uncorrelated. Since they are jointly Gaussian, it follows that
\langle X,v\rangle
and
X^{\prime}
are independent. Define

X_{i}^{\prime}:=X_{i}-\left\langle X_{i},v\right\rangle\frac{\Sigma v}{\langle\Sigma v,v\rangle},\quad i=1,\ldots,N.

Then
\left\{X_{i}^{\prime}:i=1,\ldots,N\right\}
and
\left\{\left\langle X_{i},u\right\rangle:i=1,\ldots,N\right\}
are also independent. We easily get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg\|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\langle X_{i},v\right\rangle^{p-1}X_{i}-\mathbb{E}\langle X,v\rangle^{p-1}X\bigg\|

\displaystyle\quad=\mathbb{E}\bigg\|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\left\langle X_{i},v\right\rangle^{p}-\mathbb{E}\langle X,v\rangle^{p}\right)\frac{\Sigma v}{\langle\Sigma v,v\rangle}+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\langle X_{i},v\right\rangle^{p-1}X_{i}^{\prime}\bigg\|,

(3.7)

where we used the fact that

\mathbb{E}\langle X,v\rangle^{p-1}X=\mathbb{E}\langle X,v\rangle^{p}\frac{\Sigma v}{\langle\Sigma v,v\rangle}+\mathbb{E}\langle X,v\rangle^{p-1}\mathbb{E}X^{\prime}=\mathbb{E}\langle X,v\rangle^{p}\frac{\Sigma v}{\langle\Sigma v,v\rangle}.

Note that, conditionally on
\left\langle X_{i},v\right\rangle,i=1,\ldots,N
, the distribution of the random variable

\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\langle X_{i},v\right\rangle^{p-1}X_{i}^{\prime}

is Gaussian and it coincides with the distribution of the random variable

\bigg(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\langle X_{i},v\right\rangle^{2(p-1)}\bigg)^{1/2}\frac{X^{\prime}}{\sqrt{N}}.

Denote now by
\mathbb{E}_{v}
the conditional expectation given
\left\langle X_{i},v\right\rangle,i=1,\ldots,N
and by
\mathbb{E}^{\prime}
the conditional expectation given
X_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,X_{N}^{\prime}
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg\|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\left\langle X_{i},v\right\rangle^{p}-\mathbb{E}\langle X,v\rangle^{p}\right)\frac{\Sigma v}{\langle\Sigma v,v\rangle}+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\langle X_{i},v\right\rangle^{p-1}X_{i}^{\prime}\bigg\|

\displaystyle\quad=\mathbb{E}\mathbb{E}_{v}\bigg\|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\left\langle X_{i},v\right\rangle^{p}-\mathbb{E}\langle X,v\rangle^{p}\right)\frac{\Sigma v}{\langle\Sigma v,v\rangle}+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\langle X_{i},v\right\rangle^{p-1}X_{i}^{\prime}\bigg\|

\displaystyle\quad=\mathbb{E}\mathbb{E}_{v}\bigg\|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\left\langle X_{i},v\right\rangle^{p}-\mathbb{E}\langle X,v\rangle^{p}\right)\frac{\Sigma v}{\langle\Sigma v,v\rangle}+\bigg(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\langle X_{i},v\right\rangle^{2(p-1)}\bigg)^{1/2}\frac{X^{\prime}}{\sqrt{N}}\bigg\|

\displaystyle\quad=\mathbb{E}\bigg\|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\left\langle X_{i},v\right\rangle^{p}-\mathbb{E}\langle X,v\rangle^{p}\right)\frac{\Sigma v}{\langle\Sigma v,v\rangle}+\bigg(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\langle X_{i},v\right\rangle^{2(p-1)}\bigg)^{1/2}\frac{X^{\prime}}{\sqrt{N}}\bigg\|.

(3.8)

Also

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg\|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\left\langle X_{i},v\right\rangle^{p}-\mathbb{E}\langle X,v\rangle^{p}\right)\frac{\Sigma v}{\langle\Sigma v,v\rangle}+\bigg(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\langle X_{i},v\right\rangle^{2(p-1)}\bigg)^{1/2}\frac{X^{\prime}}{\sqrt{N}}\bigg\|

\displaystyle\quad=\mathbb{E}\mathbb{E}^{\prime}\bigg\|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\left\langle X_{i},v\right\rangle^{p}-\mathbb{E}\langle X,v\rangle^{p}\right)\frac{\Sigma v}{\langle\Sigma v,v\rangle}+\bigg(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\langle X_{i},v\right\rangle^{2(p-1)}\bigg)^{1/2}\frac{X^{\prime}}{\sqrt{N}}\bigg\|

\displaystyle\quad\geq\mathbb{E}\bigg\|\mathbb{E}^{\prime}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\left\langle X_{i},v\right\rangle^{p}-\mathbb{E}\langle X,v\rangle^{p}\right)\frac{\Sigma v}{\langle\Sigma v,v\rangle}+\mathbb{E}^{\prime}\bigg(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\langle X_{i},v\right\rangle^{2(p-1)}\bigg)^{1/2}\frac{X^{\prime}}{\sqrt{N}}\bigg\|

\displaystyle\quad=\mathbb{E}\bigg(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\langle X_{i},v\right\rangle^{2(p-1)}\bigg)^{1/2}\frac{\mathbb{E}\|X^{\prime}\|}{\sqrt{N}}.

Note that

\mathbb{E}|\langle X,v\rangle|=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\langle\Sigma v,v\rangle^{1/2}.

Therefore,

\mathbb{E}\|X^{\prime}\|\geq\mathbb{E}\|X\|-\mathbb{E}|\langle X,v\rangle|\frac{\|\Sigma v\|}{\langle\Sigma v,v\rangle}=\mathbb{E}\|X\|-\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\frac{\|\Sigma v\|}{\langle\Sigma v,v\rangle^{1/2}}

and

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg\|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\left\langle X_{i},v\right\rangle^{p}-\mathbb{E}\langle X,v\rangle^{p}\right)\frac{\Sigma v}{\langle\Sigma v,v\rangle}+\bigg(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\langle X_{i},v\right\rangle^{2(p-1)}\bigg)^{1/2}\frac{X^{\prime}}{\sqrt{N}}\bigg\|

\displaystyle\quad\geq\mathbb{E}\bigg(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\langle X_{i},v\right\rangle^{2(p-1)}\bigg)^{1/2}\frac{\mathbb{E}\|X\|-\sqrt{2/\pi}\|\Sigma v\|/\langle\Sigma v,v\rangle^{1/2}}{\sqrt{N}}

\displaystyle\quad=\langle\Sigma v,v\rangle^{(p-1)/2}\mathbb{E}\bigg(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}Z_{i}^{2(p-1)}\bigg)^{1/2}\frac{\mathbb{E}\|X\|-\sqrt{2/\pi}\|\Sigma v\|/\langle\Sigma v,v\rangle^{1/2}}{\sqrt{N}},

where we recall that

Z_{i}=\frac{\langle X_{i},v\rangle}{\langle\Sigma v,v\rangle^{1/2}},\quad i=1,\ldots,N

are i.i.d. standard Gaussian random variables. One can check that

\mathbb{E}\bigg(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}Z_{i}^{2(p-1)}\bigg)^{1/2}\geq c^{\prime}(p)

for a positive constant
c^{\prime}(p)
depending only on
p
, which implies that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg\|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\left\langle X_{i},v\right\rangle^{p}-\mathbb{E}\langle X,v\rangle^{p}\right)\frac{\Sigma v}{\langle\Sigma v,v\rangle}+\bigg(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\langle X_{i},v\right\rangle^{2(p-1)}\bigg)^{1/2}\frac{X^{\prime}}{\sqrt{N}}\bigg\|

\displaystyle\quad\geq c^{\prime}(p)\langle\Sigma v,v\rangle^{(p-1)/2}\frac{\mathbb{E}\|X\|-\sqrt{2/\pi}\|\Sigma v\|/\langle\Sigma v,v\rangle^{1/2}}{\sqrt{N}}

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{c^{\prime}(p)}{\sqrt{N}}\bigg(\langle\Sigma v,v\rangle^{(p-1)/2}\mathbb{E}\|X\|-\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\|\Sigma v\|\langle\Sigma v,v\rangle^{(p-2)/2}\bigg).

We combine this bound with (
3
), (
3
) and (
3
) to get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\sup_{\|v\|=1}\bigg|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\langle X_{i},v\right\rangle^{p}-\mathbb{E}\langle X,v\rangle^{p}\bigg|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{c^{\prime}(p)}{\sqrt{N}}\sup_{\|v\|=1}\bigg(\langle\Sigma v,v\rangle^{(p-1)/2}\mathbb{E}\|X\|-\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\|\Sigma v\|\langle\Sigma v,v\rangle^{(p-2)/2}\bigg)

\displaystyle\overset{\text{($\star$)}}{\geq}\frac{c^{\prime}(p)}{\sqrt{N}}\bigg(\|\Sigma\|^{(p-1)/2}\mathbb{E}\|X\|-\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\|\Sigma\|^{p/2}\bigg)

\displaystyle\overset{\text{($\star\star$)}}{\geq}c^{\prime}(p)\|\Sigma\|^{p/2}\bigg(\frac{c^{\prime}\sqrt{r(\Sigma)}-\sqrt{2/\pi}}{\sqrt{N}}\bigg),

(3.9)

where (
\star
) follows by taking the eigenvector of
\Sigma
corresponding to the largest eigenvalue, and (
\star\star
) follows since for a Gaussian vector
X
,
\mathbb{E}\|X\|\asymp\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\|X\|^{2}}=\sqrt{\mathrm{Tr}(\Sigma)}
.

Recall that we have the following bound by (
3
)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\sup_{\|v\|=1}\bigg|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\langle X_{i},v\right\rangle^{p}-\mathbb{E}\langle X,v\rangle^{p}\bigg|\geq\|\Sigma\|^{p/2}\frac{c(p)}{\sqrt{N}}.

(3.10)

Combining (
3
) and (
3.10
) gives that, for any
t\in[0,1]
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\sup_{\|v\|=1}\bigg|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\langle X_{i},v\right\rangle^{p}-\mathbb{E}\langle X,v\rangle^{p}\bigg|\geq\|\Sigma\|^{p/2}\bigg(tc^{\prime}(p)\bigg(\frac{c^{\prime}\sqrt{r(\Sigma)}-\sqrt{2/\pi}}{\sqrt{N}}\bigg)+(1-t)\frac{c(p)}{\sqrt{N}}\bigg).

Choosing
t
to be small enough so that
tc^{\prime}(p)\sqrt{2/\pi}\leq(1-t)c(p)
, we get

\mathbb{E}\sup_{\|v\|=1}\bigg|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\langle X_{i},v\right\rangle^{p}-\mathbb{E}\langle X,v\rangle^{p}\bigg|\geq tc^{\prime}(p)c^{\prime}\|\Sigma\|^{p/2}\sqrt{\frac{r(\Sigma)}{N}}\asymp_{p}\|\Sigma\|^{p/2}\sqrt{\frac{r(\Sigma)}{N}}.

This completes the proof of (
3.5
). Combining (
3.1
), (
3.2
) and (
3.5
) completes the proof.
∎

4
Multi-product empirical processes

This section studies multi-product empirical processes

\displaystyle f\mapsto\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}f^{p}(X_{i})-\mathbb{E}f^{p}(X),\qquad f\in\mathcal{F}.

(4.1)

We leverage generic chaining techniques to derive sharp high-probability upper bounds on their suprema in terms of quantities that reflect the complexity of the function class
\mathcal{F}
.

Our theory builds on the framework introduced by Mendelson in
[
51
]
. The central idea is as follows. First, we gather precise structural information on the typical coordinate projection of
\mathcal{F}
; that is, for
\sigma=(X_{1},\ldots,X_{N})
, we study the structure of

P_{\sigma}(\mathcal{F})=\left\{(f(X_{i}))_{i=1}^{N}:f\in\mathcal{F}\right\}.

Second, for a typical
\sigma=(X_{1},\ldots,X_{N})
, we analyze the suprema of the conditioned Bernoulli processes:

f\mapsto\sum_{i=1}^{N}\varepsilon_{i}f^{p}(X_{i})\ \Big|\sigma,

where
\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{N}
are independent symmetric Bernoulli random variables.

The structure of typical coordinate projections of sub-Gaussian function classes has been extensively studied in empirical process theory
[
48
,
49
,
55
,
50
,
51
]
, primarily in the context of quadratic and product empirical processes. Here, we extend the analysis to empirical processes of arbitrary higher order. A key component of our approach is Theorem
4.5
, which establishes a sharp uniform bound on the
\ell_{m}
-norm of the coordinate projection vector of the function class
\mathcal{F}
for any
m\geq 1
. Specifically, it controls

\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{N}|f|^{m}\left(X_{i}\right)\bigg)^{1/m}

in terms of quantities that capture the geometric complexity of
\mathcal{F}
.

The rest of this section is organized as follows. Subsection
4.1
introduces some basic definitions from Mendelson’s work
[
51
]
and discusses an important result, Lemma
4.3
(
[
51
, Corollary 3.6]
), which plays a crucial role in our proof of Theorem
2.2
. We then prove Theorem
2.2
in Subsection
4.2
and prove Theorem
4.5
in Subsection
4.3
.

4.1
Background and auxiliary results

Following
[
51
]
, we will utilize complexity parameters that take into account all the
L_{p}
structures endowed by the process. For
q\geq 1
, we recall that the graded
L_{q}
norm is defined by

\|f\|_{(q)}:=\sup_{1\leq p\leq q}\frac{\|f\|_{L_{p}}}{\sqrt{p}}.

We will use repeatedly that, for any
q\geq 2,

\|f\|_{L_{2}}\lesssim\|f\|_{(q)}\lesssim\|f\|_{\psi_{2}}.

As shown in
[
51
]
, the graded
\gamma
-type functionals that we now define serve as key parameters to characterize the complexity of the typical coordinate projection of
\mathcal{F}
.

Definition 4.1

(Graded
\gamma
-type functionals)
.

Given a class of functions
\mathcal{F}
on a probability space
(\Omega,\mu)
, constants
u\geq 1
and
s_{0}\geq 0
, set

\Lambda_{s_{0},u}(\mathcal{F}):=\inf\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\sum_{s\geq s_{0}}2^{s/2}\left\|f-\pi_{s}f\right\|_{(u^{2}2^{s})},

where the infimum is taken over all admissible sequences, and
\pi_{s}f
is the nearest point in
\mathcal{F}_{s}
to
f
with respect to the
(u^{2}2^{s})
-norm. Also set

\widetilde{\Lambda}_{s_{0},u}(\mathcal{F}):=\Lambda_{s_{0},u}(\mathcal{F})+2^{s_{0}/2}\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\left\|\pi_{s_{0}}f\right\|_{(u^{2}2^{s_{0}})}.

The following lemma shows that
\Lambda_{s_{0},u}(\mathcal{F})
is upper bounded by
\gamma(\mathcal{F},\psi_{2})
up to an absolute constant.

Lemma 4.1
.

For any
u\geq 1
and
s_{0}\geq 0
,
\Lambda_{s_{0},u}(\mathcal{F})\lesssim\gamma(\mathcal{F},\psi_{2})
.

Proof.

For any admissible sequence
(\mathcal{F}_{s})_{s\geq 0}\subset\mathcal{F}
, any
f\in\mathcal{F}
and
s\geq 0
, let
\bar{\pi}_{s}f
be the nearest point in
\mathcal{F}_{s}
to
f
with respect to the
\psi_{2}
norm. By the definition of
\pi_{s}f
, we have

\sum_{s\geq s_{0}}2^{s/2}\left\|f-\pi_{s}f\right\|_{(u^{2}2^{s})}\leq\sum_{s\geq s_{0}}2^{s/2}\left\|f-\bar{\pi}_{s}f\right\|_{(u^{2}2^{s})}\lesssim\sum_{s\geq s_{0}}2^{s/2}\left\|f-\bar{\pi}_{s}f\right\|_{\psi_{2}}.

Thus,

\Lambda_{s_{0},u}(\mathcal{F})\lesssim\inf\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\sum_{s\geq s_{0}}2^{s/2}\left\|f-\bar{\pi}_{s}f\right\|_{\psi_{2}}\leq\inf\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\sum_{s\geq 0}2^{s/2}\left\|f-\bar{\pi}_{s}f\right\|_{\psi_{2}}=\gamma(\mathcal{F},\psi_{2}).\qed

To analyze (conditioned) Bernoulli processes, we will use a chaining argument combined with the following Höffding’s inequality.

Lemma 4.2

(Höffding’s Inequality,
[
60
]
)
.

Let
\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{N}
be independent symmetric Bernoulli random variables (that is,
\mathbb{P}(\varepsilon_{n}=+1)=\mathbb{P}(\varepsilon_{n}=-1)=1/2
). For any fixed
I\subset\{1,\ldots,N\}
,
z\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, it holds with probability at least
1-2\exp(-t^{2}/2)
that

\bigg|\sum_{i=1}^{N}\varepsilon_{i}z_{i}\bigg|\leq\sum_{i\in I}\left|z_{i}\right|+t\bigg(\sum_{i\in I^{c}}z_{i}^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}.

In particular, for every
1\leq k\leq N,
it holds with probability at least
1-2\exp(-t^{2}/2)
that

\bigg|\sum_{i=1}^{N}\varepsilon_{i}z_{i}\bigg|\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left|z_{i}^{*}\right|+t\bigg(\sum_{i=k+1}^{N}(z_{i}^{*})^{2}\bigg)^{1/2},

where
\left(z_{i}^{*}\right)_{i=1}^{N}
denotes the non-increasing rearrangement of
\left(\left|z_{i}\right|\right)_{i=1}^{N}.
This estimate is optimal when
k\asymp t^{2}
.

According to Lemma
4.2
, the influence of
z
on the Bernoulli process depends on the
\ell_{1}
-norm of the largest
k
coordinates of
(|z_{i}|)_{i=1}^{N}
and the
\ell_{2}
-norm of the smallest
N-k
coordinates. As noted in
[
51
]
, the graded
L_{q}
norm plays a key role in analyzing the monotone non-increasing rearrangement of
N
independent copies of
Z
. In particular,
[
51
, Corollary 3.6]
provides a useful way to decompose a collection of random vectors into their largest and smallest coordinate components, a technique central to
[
51
]
. Below, we present the statement obtained by setting
q=2r
and
\beta=1/2
in their notation.

Lemma 4.3

(
[
51
, Corollary 3.6]
)
.

There exist absolute constants
c_{0},c_{1}
, and for every
r\geq 1
, there exist constants
c_{2},c_{3},c_{4}
and
c_{5}
depending only on
r
, such that the following holds. Set

\displaystyle j_{s}=\min\left\{\left\lceil\frac{c_{0}u^{2}2^{s}}{\log\left(4+eN/u^{2}2^{s}\right)}\right\rceil,N+1\right\}

(4.2)

for
u\geq c_{2}
. Let
\mathcal{H}\subset L_{2r}
be a function class with cardinality at most
2^{2^{s+2}}
. Then, with probability at least
1-2\exp\left(-c_{3}u^{2}2^{s}\right)
, the following holds: for every
h\in\mathcal{H}
, the random vector
\left(h\left(X_{i}\right)\right)_{i=1}^{N}
can be decomposed as
\left(h\left(X_{i}\right)\right)_{i=1}^{N}=U_{h}+V_{h}
, where
U_{h}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
is supported on the largest
j_{s}-1
coordinates of
\left(\left|h\left(X_{i}\right)\right|\right)_{i=1}^{N}
while
V_{h}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
is supported on the complement, and

\displaystyle\left\|U_{h}\right\|_{\ell_{2}}\leq c_{4}u2^{s/2}\|h\|_{(c_{5}u^{2}2^{s})},\qquad\left\|V_{h}\right\|_{\ell_{r}}\leq c_{1}N^{1/r}\|h\|_{L_{2r}}.

Let
(\mathcal{F}_{s})_{s\geq 0}\subset\mathcal{F}
be an admissible sequence. For
f\in\mathcal{F}
and
s\geq 0
, we define
\Delta_{s}f:=\pi_{s+1}f-\pi_{s}f
. To analyze multi-product empirical processes of order
p
, we apply Lemma
4.3
to the classes
\mathcal{F}_{s}
and
\{\Delta_{s}f:f\in\mathcal{F}\}
with carefully chosen values of
r
that depend on

, leading to the following corollary.

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
\left(\mathcal{F}_{s}\right)_{s\geq 0}
be an admissible sequence of
\mathcal{F}
, and let
(j_{s})_{s\geq s_{0}}
be the sequence defined in (
4.2
). Let
s_{0}
be a fixed index and
p\geq 1
. Denote
\Delta_{s}f:=\pi_{s+1}f-\pi_{s}f.
There are constants
c_{1},c_{2}
and
c_{3}
that depend only on
p
and for
u\geq c(p)
where
c(p)
is a constant depending only on
p
, an event of probability at least
1-2\exp\left(-c_{1}u^{2}2^{s_{0}}\right)
on which the following holds. For every
f\in\mathcal{F}
and
s\geq s_{0}
,

\displaystyle\bigg(\sum_{i<j_{s}}\left[\left(\Delta_{s}f\right)^{2}\left(X_{i}\right)\right]^{*}\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq c_{2}u2^{s/2}\left\|\Delta_{s}f\right\|_{(c_{3}u^{2}2^{s})},

\displaystyle\bigg(\sum_{i\geq j_{s}}\left[\left(\Delta_{s}f\right)^{4}\left(X_{i}\right)\right]^{*}\bigg)^{1/4}

\displaystyle\leq c_{2}N^{1/4}\left\|\Delta_{s}f\right\|_{L_{8}},

\displaystyle\bigg(\sum_{i<j_{s}}\left[\left(\pi_{s}f\right)^{2}\left(X_{i}\right)\right]^{*}\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq c_{2}u2^{s/2}\left\|\pi_{s}f\right\|_{(c_{3}u^{2}2^{s})},

\displaystyle\bigg(\sum_{i\geq j_{s}}\left[\left(\pi_{s}f\right)^{4(p-1)}\left(X_{i}\right)\right]^{*}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{4(p-1)}}

\displaystyle\leq c_{2}N^{\frac{1}{4(p-1)}}\left\|\pi_{s}f\right\|_{L_{8(p-1)}}.

Proof of Corollary
4.4
.

By definition of an admissible sequence, we have
|\mathcal{F}_{s}|\leq 2^{2^{s}}.
Further,
|\mathcal{F}_{s}|\cdot|\mathcal{F}_{s+1}|\leq 2^{2^{s}}\cdot 2^{2^{s+1}}\leq 2^{2^{s+2}},
which implies that for
\Delta_{s}f=\pi_{s+1}f-\pi_{s}f
,
|\{\Delta_{s}f:f\in\mathcal{F}\}|\leq 2^{2^{s+2}}
. Therefore, the result follows by applying Lemma
4.3
, for every
s\geq s_{0}
, to the class
\mathcal{F}_{s}
with the choice
r=4(p-1)\geq 1
and to the class
\{\Delta_{s}f:f\in\mathcal{F}\}
with
r=4
, and taking a union bound to get the probability estimate

1-2\sum_{s\geq s_{0}}2\exp(-cu^{2}2^{s})\cdot 2^{2^{s+2}}\geq 1-2\exp(-c_{1}u^{2}2^{s_{0}}),

where
c_{1}
is a constant depending only on
p
.

4.2
Proof of Theorem
2.2

This subsection contains the proof of our second main result, Theorem
2.2
. The proof relies on the next result, Theorem
4.5
, which establishes a uniform bound on the
\ell_{m}
-norm of the coordinate projection vector of the function class
\mathcal{F}
and improves upon the estimates in
[
48
, (3.8)]
.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
X_{1},\ldots,X_{N}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{i.i.d.}}}{{\sim}}\mu
be a sequence of random variables and let
\mathcal{F}
be a function class. For any
m\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{N}|f|^{m}\left(X_{i}\right)\bigg)^{1/m}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{m}\gamma(\mathcal{F},\psi_{2})+N^{1/m}d_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F})+N^{1/2m}d^{(m-1)/m}_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F})\gamma^{1/m}(\mathcal{F},\psi_{2})

\displaystyle\quad+N^{1/2m}d_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F})(\varphi^{-1}(Z))^{1/m},

where

\varphi(x)=2^{\min\{(\sqrt{N}x)^{2/m},\,x^{2}\}}-1,\quad x\geq 0,

and
\varphi^{-1}(x)
denotes its inverse function. Here,
Z
is a nonnegative random variable satisfying
\mathbb{E}\,Z\leq C
for some absolute constant
C>0
. Moreover, if
m\geq 2
, then

\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{N}|f|^{m}\left(X_{i}\right)\bigg)^{1/m}\lesssim_{m}\gamma(\mathcal{F},\psi_{2})+N^{1/m}d_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F})+N^{1/2m}d_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F})(\varphi^{-1}(Z))^{1/m}.

Remark 4.1
.

An application of Markov’s inequality to Theorem
4.5
yields the following high-probability bound: for any
m\geq 2
and
w>0
, it holds with probability at least
1-2\exp(-\min\{(\sqrt{N}w)^{2/m},w^{2}\})
that

\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{N}|f|^{m}\left(X_{i}\right)\bigg)^{1/m}\lesssim_{m}\gamma(\mathcal{F},\psi_{2})+N^{1/m}d_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F})+N^{1/2m}d_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F})w^{1/m}.

An alternative formulation of the tail bound states that for any
u\geq 1
, with probability at least
1-2\exp(-u^{2})
,

\displaystyle\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{N}|f|^{m}\left(X_{i}\right)\bigg)^{1/m}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{m}\gamma\left(\mathcal{F},\psi_{2}\right)+N^{1/m}d_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F})+N^{1/2m}d_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F})\left(u^{1/m}+uN^{-1/2m}\right)

\displaystyle=\gamma\left(\mathcal{F},\psi_{2}\right)+N^{1/m}d_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F})+d_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F})u+N^{1/2m}d_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F})u^{1/m}

\displaystyle\asymp\gamma\left(\mathcal{F},\psi_{2}\right)+N^{1/m}d_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F})+d_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F})u,

(4.3)

where the last step follows from the inequality
N^{1/2m}u^{1/m}\leq\frac{1}{2}(N^{1/m}+u^{2/m})\leq N^{1/m}+u
, for
u\geq 1
and
m\geq 2
.

We demonstrate the optimality of the bound (
4.1
) via a simple example. Let
H=\mathbb{R}^{d}
and consider a symmetric compact set
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Define the function class
\mathcal{F}=\left\{\langle\cdot,v\rangle:v\in K\right\}
, which consists of linear functions indexed by
K
. Let
X_{1},\ldots,X_{N}
be i.i.d. standard Gaussian random vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and let
v^{*}\in K
be a point attaining the maximal Euclidean norm on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, i.e.,
\|v^{*}\|_{\ell_{2}}=\sup_{v\in K}\|v\|_{\ell_{2}}
. Observe that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{N}|f|^{m}\left(X_{i}\right)\bigg)^{1/m}=\mathbb{E}\sup_{v\in K}\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{N}|\langle X_{i},v\rangle|^{m}\bigg)^{1/m}

\displaystyle\overset{\hskip-2.84544pt\text{($\star$)}}{\gtrsim_{m}}\mathbb{E}\sup_{v\in K}|\langle X_{1},v\rangle|+N^{1/m}\|v^{*}\|_{\ell_{2}}

\displaystyle\overset{\text{($\star\star$)}}{\asymp}\gamma(\mathcal{F},\psi_{2})+N^{1/m}d_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F}),

where (
\star
) follows by separately analyzing the case
N=1
and the contribution of the maximal element
v^{*}\in K
, and (
\star\star
) follows by Talagrand’s majorizing-measure theorem
[
70
, Theorem 2.10.1]
, which gives
\mathbb{E}\sup_{v\in K}\langle X_{1},v\rangle\asymp\gamma(\mathcal{F},\psi_{2})
, along with the relation
\|v^{*}\|_{\ell_{2}}=\sup_{v\in K}\|v\|_{\ell_{2}}\asymp\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\|f\|_{\psi_{2}}=d_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F})
. This confirms the necessity of the deterministic terms in (
4.1
). Furthermore, note that

\sup_{v\in K}\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{N}|\langle X_{i},v\rangle|^{m}\bigg)^{1/m}\geq\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{N}|\langle X_{i},v^{*}\rangle|^{m}\bigg)^{1/m}\geq|\langle X_{1},v^{*}\rangle|\overset{\mathsf{d}}{\sim}\|v^{*}\|_{2}\cdot|y|,

where
y
is a standard Gaussian random variable. This confirms the sub-Gaussian tail behavior in (
4.1
).

We defer the proof of Theorem
4.5
to Subsection
4.3
and proceed now to prove Theorem
2.2
.

Proof of Theorem
2.2
.

We first observe that the condition
0\in\mathcal{F}
or the symmetry of
\mathcal{F}
(i.e.,
f\in\mathcal{F}
implies
-f\in\mathcal{F}
) ensures that
\gamma(\mathcal{F},\psi_{2})\gtrsim d_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F})
(Lemma
4.6
), a fact that we will use repeatedly in the proof.

Let
(\mathcal{F}_{s})_{s\geq 0}
be an admissible sequence of
\mathcal{F}.
Let
u\geq 4
and
s_{0}\geq 0
. For any
f\in\mathcal{F}
and
s\geq 0
, let
\pi_{s}f
denote the nearest point in
\mathcal{F}_{s}
to
f
with respect to the
(u^{2}2^{s})
-norm.

The proof is based on a symmetrization argument, an important tool in empirical process theory
[
74
, Chapter 2.3]
. Let
(\varepsilon_{i})_{i=1}^{N}
be independent symmetric Bernoulli random variables that are independent of
(X_{i})_{i=1}^{N}
. We seek to establish a high-probability upper bound on the supremum of the Bernoulli process given by

\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\bigg|\sum_{i=1}^{N}\varepsilon_{i}f^{p}(X_{i})\bigg|.

To that end, we first note that any
f^{p}(X_{i})
can be represented as a chain, or telescoping sum, of links of the form
(\pi_{s+1}f)^{p}(X_{i})-(\pi_{s}f)^{p}(X_{i}).
Namely

f^{p}(X_{i})=\sum_{s\geq s_{0}}((\pi_{s+1}f)^{p}-(\pi_{s}f)^{p})(X_{i})+(\pi_{s_{0}}f)^{p}(X_{i}),

provided that
\pi_{s}f\to f
as
s\to\infty
.
By the triangle inequality, we have that

\displaystyle\bigg|\sum_{i=1}^{N}\varepsilon_{i}f^{p}(X_{i})\bigg|\leq\sum_{s\geq s_{0}}\bigg|\sum_{i=1}^{N}\varepsilon_{i}((\pi_{s+1}f)^{p}-(\pi_{s}f)^{p})(X_{i})\bigg|+\bigg|\sum_{i=1}^{N}\varepsilon_{i}(\pi_{s_{0}}f)^{p}(X_{i})\bigg|.

(4.4)

To ease notation, we write
\Delta_{s}f:=\pi_{s+1}f-\pi_{s}f
and
(\Delta_{s}f)_{i}:=(\Delta_{s}f)(X_{i})
. We use similar notation throughout the remainder of the proof. For each
s\geq s_{0}
, we apply Lemma
4.2
with
t=u2^{s/2}
and a set
I_{s}\subset\{1,\dots,N\}
(to be specified later) to the summand indexed by
s
in (
4.4
). This implies that, with probability at least
1-2\exp(-u^{2}2^{s-1})
, the following holds:

\displaystyle\bigg|\sum_{i=1}^{N}\varepsilon_{i}((\pi_{s+1}f)^{p}-(\pi_{s}f)^{p})_{i}\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i\in I_{s}}|((\pi_{s+1}f)^{p}-(\pi_{s}f)^{p})_{i}|+u2^{s/2}\bigg(\sum_{i\in I_{s}^{c}}((\pi_{s+1}f)^{p}-(\pi_{s}f)^{p})_{i}^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\overset{\text{($\star$)}}{\leq}p\sum_{i\in I_{s}}|(\Delta_{s}f)_{i}|\left(|\pi_{s+1}f|^{p-1}+|\pi_{s}f|^{p-1}\right)_{i}

\displaystyle\quad+u2^{s/2}p\bigg(\sum_{i\in I_{s}^{c}}(\Delta_{s}f)_{i}^{2}\left(|\pi_{s+1}f|^{p-1}+|\pi_{s}f|^{p-1}\right)_{i}^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p}\sum_{i\in I_{s}}|(\Delta_{s}f)_{i}||(\pi_{s+1}f)_{i}|^{p-1}+u2^{s/2}\bigg(\sum_{i\in I_{s}^{c}}(\Delta_{s}f)_{i}^{2}(\pi_{s+1}f)_{i}^{2(p-1)}\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{i\in I_{s}}|(\Delta_{s}f)_{i}||(\pi_{s}f)_{i}|^{p-1}+u2^{s/2}\bigg(\sum_{i\in I_{s}^{c}}(\Delta_{s}f)_{i}^{2}(\pi_{s}f)_{i}^{2(p-1)}\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=:T_{1}^{(s)}+T_{2}^{(s)},

where in the inequality (
\star
) we used that
|x^{p}-y^{p}|\leq p|x-y|(|x|^{p-1}+|y|^{p-1})
, which follows directly from applying the mean value theorem to the function
h(x)=x^{p}
: for
x>y
, there exists some
\xi
with
y\leq\xi\leq x
such that
x^{p}-y^{p}=(x-y)h^{\prime}(\xi)=p(x-y)\xi^{p-1},

thus
|x^{p}-y^{p}|=p|x-y||\xi|^{p-1}\leq p|x-y|\max\{|x|^{p-1},|y|^{p-1}\}\leq p|x-y|(|x|^{p-1}+|y|^{p-1}).

Let
(j_{s})_{s\geq s_{0}}
be the sequence defined in (
4.2
). For each
s\geq s_{0}
, we choose
I_{s}
to be the union of the
j_{s}-1
largest coordinates of
\left(\left|(\Delta_{s}f)_{i}\right|\right)_{i=1}^{N}
, the
j_{s}-1
largest coordinates of
\left(\left|(\pi_{s+1}f)_{i}\right|\right)_{i=1}^{N}
, and the
j_{s}-1
largest coordinates of
\left(\left|(\pi_{s}f)_{i}\right|\right)_{i=1}^{N}
. For a vector
x=(x_{i})_{i=1}^{N}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, we denote by
\left(x_{i}^{*}\right)_{i=1}^{N}
the non-increasing rearrangement of
\left(\left|x_{i}\right|\right)_{i=1}^{N}
. By Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,

\displaystyle T_{1}^{(s)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in I_{s}}|(\Delta_{s}f)_{i}||(\pi_{s+1}f)_{i}|^{p-1}+u2^{s/2}\bigg(\sum_{i\in I_{s}^{c}}(\Delta_{s}f)_{i}^{2}(\pi_{s+1}f)_{i}^{2(p-1)}\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\bigg(\sum_{i\in I_{s}}(\Delta_{s}f)^{2}_{i}\bigg)^{1/2}\bigg(\sum_{i\in I_{s}}(\pi_{s+1}f)^{2(p-1)}_{i}\bigg)^{1/2}+u2^{s/2}\bigg(\sum_{i\in I_{s}^{c}}(\Delta_{s}f)^{4}_{i}\bigg)^{1/4}\bigg(\sum_{i\in I_{s}^{c}}(\pi_{s+1}f)^{4(p-1)}_{i}\bigg)^{1/4}

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigg(\sum_{i<j_{s}}\left[\left(\Delta_{s}f\right)^{2}\right]^{*}_{i}\bigg)^{1/2}\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{N}f^{2(p-1)}_{i}\bigg)^{1/2}+u2^{s/2}\bigg(\sum_{i\geq j_{s}}\left[(\Delta_{s}f)^{4}\right]^{*}_{i}\bigg)^{1/4}\bigg(\sum_{i\geq j_{s}}\left[(\pi_{s+1}f)^{4(p-1)}\right]^{*}_{i}\bigg)^{1/4},

where in the last step we have used that
\sum_{i\in I_{s}}(\Delta_{s}f)^{2}_{i}\leq 3\sum_{i<j_{s}}\left[\left(\Delta_{s}f\right)^{2}\right]^{*}_{i}
since
|I_{s}|\leq 3(j_{s}-1)
, and

\displaystyle\bigg(\sum_{i\in I_{s}}(\pi_{s+1}f)^{2(p-1)}_{i}\bigg)^{1/2}\leq\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{N}(\pi_{s+1}f)^{2(p-1)}_{i}\bigg)^{1/2}\leq\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{N}f_{i}^{2(p-1)}\bigg)^{1/2}.

A similar argument shows that

\displaystyle T_{2}^{(s)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in I_{s}}|(\Delta_{s}f)_{i}||(\pi_{s}f)_{i}|^{p-1}+u2^{s/2}\bigg(\sum_{i\in I_{s}^{c}}(\Delta_{s}f)_{i}^{2}(\pi_{s}f)_{i}^{2(p-1)}\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigg(\sum_{i<j_{s}}\left[\left(\Delta_{s}f\right)^{2}\right]^{*}_{i}\bigg)^{1/2}\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{N}f^{2(p-1)}_{i}\bigg)^{1/2}+u2^{s/2}\bigg(\sum_{i\geq j_{s}}\left[(\Delta_{s}f)^{4}\right]^{*}_{i}\bigg)^{1/4}\bigg(\sum_{i\geq j_{s}}\left[(\pi_{s}f)^{4(p-1)}\right]^{*}_{i}\bigg)^{1/4}.

Further, repeating this argument for the last term in (
4.4
) gives that, with probability at least
1-2\exp(-u^{2}2^{s_{0}-1})
,

\displaystyle\bigg|\sum_{i=1}^{N}\varepsilon_{i}(\pi_{s_{0}}f)^{p}_{i}\bigg|\leq\bigg(\sum_{i<j_{s_{0}}}\left[\left(\pi_{s_{0}}f\right)^{2}\right]^{*}_{i}\bigg)^{1/2}\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{N}f^{2(p-1)}_{i}\bigg)^{1/2}+u2^{s_{0}/2}\bigg(\sum_{i\geq j_{s_{0}}}\left[(\pi_{s_{0}}f)^{2p}\right]_{i}^{*}\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\bigg(\sum_{i<j_{s_{0}}}\left[\left(\pi_{s_{0}}f\right)^{2}\right]^{*}_{i}\bigg)^{1/2}\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{N}f^{2(p-1)}_{i}\bigg)^{1/2}+u2^{s_{0}/2}N^{\frac{p-2}{4(p-1)}}\bigg(\sum_{i\geq j_{s_{0}}}\left[(\pi_{s_{0}}f)^{4(p-1)}\right]^{*}_{i}\bigg)^{\frac{p}{4(p-1)}},

where the last step follows by Hölder’s inequality and the fact that
|N-j_{s_{0}}+1|^{\frac{p-2}{4(p-1)}}\leq N^{\frac{p-2}{4(p-1)}}.

Observe that
\left|\left\{\pi_{s}f:f\in\mathcal{F}\right\}\right|\leq|\mathcal{F}_{s}|\leq 2^{2^{s}}
,
\left|\left\{\pi_{s+1}f:f\in\mathcal{F}\right\}\right|\leq|\mathcal{F}_{s+1}|\leq 2^{2^{s+1}}
and
\left|\left\{\Delta_{s}f:f\in\mathcal{F}\right\}\right|\leq|\mathcal{F}_{s}|\cdot|\mathcal{F}_{s+1}|\leq 2^{2^{s}}\cdot 2^{2^{s+1}}\leq 2^{2^{s+2}}
. For
u\geq 4
, we sum the probabilities for all
s\geq s_{0}
to obtain that, with
(\varepsilon_{i})_{i=1}^{N}
probability at least

\displaystyle 1-2\exp(-u^{2}2^{s_{0}-1})\cdot 2^{2^{s_{0}}}-\sum_{s\geq s_{0}}2\exp(-u^{2}2^{s-1})\cdot 2^{2^{s+2}}\geq 1-2\exp(-u^{2}2^{s_{0}}/8),

for every
f\in\mathcal{F}
,

\displaystyle\bigg|\sum_{i=1}^{N}\varepsilon_{i}f^{p}(X_{i})\bigg|\leq\sum_{s\geq s_{0}}\bigg|\sum_{i=1}^{N}\varepsilon_{i}((\pi_{s+1}f)^{p}-(\pi_{s}f)^{p})(X_{i})\bigg|+\bigg|\sum_{i=1}^{N}\varepsilon_{i}(\pi_{s_{0}}f)^{p}(X_{i})\bigg|

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p}\sum_{s\geq s_{0}}\big(T_{1}^{(s)}+T_{2}^{(s)}\big)+\bigg|\sum_{i=1}^{N}\varepsilon_{i}(\pi_{s_{0}}f)^{p}(X_{i})\bigg|

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p}\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{N}f^{2(p-1)}_{i}\bigg)^{1/2}\Bigg(\sum_{s\geq s_{0}}\bigg(\sum_{i<j_{s}}\left[\left(\Delta_{s}f\right)^{2}\right]^{*}_{i}\bigg)^{1/2}+\bigg(\sum_{i<j_{s_{0}}}\left[\left(\pi_{s_{0}}f\right)^{2}\right]^{*}_{i}\bigg)^{1/2}\Bigg)

\displaystyle\quad+u\left(\sum_{s\geq s_{0}}2^{s/2}\bigg(\sum_{i\geq j_{s}}\left[(\Delta_{s}f)^{4}\right]^{*}_{i}\bigg)^{1/4}\left(\bigg(\sum_{i\geq j_{s}}\left[(\pi_{s+1}f)^{4(p-1)}\right]^{*}_{i}\bigg)^{1/4}+\bigg(\sum_{i\geq j_{s}}\left[(\pi_{s}f)^{4(p-1)}\right]^{*}_{i}\bigg)^{1/4}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\quad+u2^{s_{0}/2}N^{\frac{p-2}{4(p-1)}}\bigg(\sum_{i\geq j_{s_{0}}}\left[(\pi_{s_{0}}f)^{4(p-1)}\right]^{*}_{i}\bigg)^{\frac{p}{4(p-1)}}.

On the other hand, we know from Corollary
4.4
that with
(X_{i})_{i=1}^{N}
probability at least
1-2\exp(-c_{1}u^{2}2^{s_{0}})
, the four inequalities in Corollary
4.4
hold for every

and
s\geq s_{0}
simultaneously. Therefore, combining the
(\varepsilon_{i})_{i=1}^{N}
probability and the
(X_{i})_{i=1}^{N}
probability implies that there exists a constant
\tilde{c}(p)
depending only on
p
such that, for
u\geq c(p)\lor 4
, it holds with probability at least

1-2\exp(-u^{2}2^{s_{0}}/8)-2\exp(-c_{1}u^{2}2^{s_{0}})\geq 1-2\exp\left(-\tilde{c}(p)u^{2}2^{s_{0}}\right)

that, for every
f\in\mathcal{F}
,

\displaystyle\bigg|\sum_{i=1}^{N}\varepsilon_{i}f^{p}_{i}\bigg|

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p}\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{N}f^{2(p-1)}_{i}\bigg)^{1/2}u\bigg(\sum_{s\geq s_{0}}2^{s/2}\|\Delta_{s}f\|_{(c_{3}u^{2}2^{s})}+2^{s_{0}/2}\|\pi_{s_{0}}f\|_{(c_{3}u^{2}2^{s_{0}})}\bigg)

\displaystyle\quad+u\bigg(\sum_{s\geq s_{0}}2^{s/2}N^{1/4}\|\Delta_{s}f\|_{L_{8}}N^{1/4}\big(\|\pi_{s}f\|_{L_{8(p-1)}}^{p-1}+\|\pi_{s+1}f\|_{L_{8(p-1)}}^{p-1}\big)\bigg)

\displaystyle\quad+u2^{s_{0}/2}N^{\frac{p-2}{4(p-1)}}\left(N^{\frac{1}{4(p-1)}}\|\pi_{s_{0}}f\|_{L_{8(p-1)}}\right)^{p}

\displaystyle\overset{\text{($\star$)}}{\lesssim}_{p}\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{N}f^{2(p-1)}_{i}\bigg)^{1/2}u\widetilde{\Lambda}_{s_{0},cu}(\mathcal{F})+uN^{1/2}d^{p-1}_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F})\bigg(\sum_{s\geq s_{0}}2^{s/2}\|\Delta_{s}f\|_{\psi_{2}}\bigg)+u2^{s_{0}/2}N^{1/2}d^{p}_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F})

\displaystyle\overset{\text{($\star\star$)}}{\lesssim}_{p}\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{N}f^{2(p-1)}_{i}\bigg)^{1/2}u\widetilde{\Lambda}_{s_{0},cu}(\mathcal{F})+uN^{1/2}d^{p-1}_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F})\left(\gamma(\mathcal{F},\psi_{2})+2^{s_{0}/2}d_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F})\right),

where (
\star
) follows by choosing an almost optimal admissible sequence in
\mathcal{F}
and using the definition of
\widetilde{\Lambda}_{s_{0},u}(\mathcal{F})
, and the fact that
\|\pi_{s}f\|_{L_{8(p-1)}}^{p}\lesssim_{p}\|\pi_{s}f\|_{\psi_{2}}^{p}\leq d_{\psi_{2}}^{p}(\mathcal{F}),\|\pi_{s_{0}}f\|_{L_{8(p-1)}}^{p}\lesssim_{p}\|\pi_{s_{0}}f\|_{\psi_{2}}^{p}\leq d_{\psi_{2}}^{p}(\mathcal{F})
; (
\star\star
) follows by

\displaystyle\sum_{s\geq s_{0}}2^{s/2}\|\Delta_{s}f\|_{\psi_{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{s\geq s_{0}}2^{s/2}(\|f-\pi_{s}f\|_{\psi_{2}}+\|f-\pi_{s+1}f\|_{\psi_{2}})\lesssim\gamma(\mathcal{F},\psi_{2}).

Now we apply Theorem
4.5
and Remark
4.1
with
m=2(p-1)\geq 2
to obtain the high-probability bound: for any
w>0
, it holds with probability at least
1-2\exp(-\min\{(\sqrt{N}w)^{1/(p-1)},w^{2}\})
that

\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{N}|f|^{2(p-1)}_{i}\bigg)^{1/2}\lesssim_{p}\gamma^{p-1}(\mathcal{F},\psi_{2})+N^{1/2}d^{p-1}_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F})+N^{1/4}d^{p-1}_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F})\sqrt{w}.

Moreover, by Lemma
4.1
,
\Lambda_{s_{0},u}(\mathcal{F})\lesssim\gamma\left(\mathcal{F},\psi_{2}\right)
, thus

\widetilde{\Lambda}_{s_{0},cu}(\mathcal{F})=\Lambda_{s_{0},u}(\mathcal{F})+2^{s_{0}/2}\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\left\|\pi_{s_{0}}f\right\|_{(u^{2}2^{s_{0}})}\lesssim\gamma(\mathcal{F},\psi_{2})+2^{s_{0}/2}d_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F}).

Consequently, we have shown that there exists constants
c(p)
and
\tilde{c}(p)
such that for any
u\geq c(p)\lor 4
, it holds with probability at least
1-2\exp(-\tilde{c}(p)u^{2}2^{s_{0}})-2\exp(-\min\{(\sqrt{N}w)^{1/(p-1)},w^{2}\})
that

\displaystyle\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\bigg|\sum_{i=1}^{N}\varepsilon_{i}f^{p}(X_{i})\bigg|

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p}\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{N}f^{2(p-1)}(X_{i})\bigg)^{1/2}u\widetilde{\Lambda}_{s_{0},cu}(\mathcal{F})+uN^{1/2}d^{p-1}_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F})\left(\gamma(\mathcal{F},\psi_{2})+2^{s_{0}/2}d_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F})\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p}u\left(\gamma(\mathcal{F},\psi_{2})+2^{s_{0}/2}d_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F})\right)\left(\gamma^{p-1}(\mathcal{F},\psi_{2})+N^{1/2}d^{p-1}_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F})+N^{1/4}d^{p-1}_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F})\sqrt{w}\right).

For
u\geq c(p)\lor 4
, we set
s_{0}
such that
2^{s_{0}}\asymp\left(\gamma(\mathcal{F},\psi_{2})/d_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F})\right)^{2}
and take

w=\left(\tilde{c}(p)u^{2}2^{s_{0}}\right)^{1/2}\lor\frac{(\tilde{c}(p)u^{2}2^{s_{0}})^{p-1}}{\sqrt{N}}\asymp_{p}u\frac{\gamma(\mathcal{F},\psi_{2})}{d_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F})}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}u^{2(p-1)}\left(\frac{\gamma(\mathcal{F},\psi_{2})}{d_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F})}\right)^{2(p-1)}.

Then, with probability at least
1-4\exp(-c^{\prime}(p)u^{2}(\gamma(\mathcal{F},\psi_{2})/d_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F}))^{2}),

\displaystyle\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\bigg|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\varepsilon_{i}f^{p}(X_{i})\bigg|

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p}\frac{u}{N}\left(\gamma(\mathcal{F},\psi_{2})+2^{s_{0}/2}d_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F})\right)\left(\gamma^{p-1}(\mathcal{F},\psi_{2})+N^{1/2}d^{p-1}_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F})+N^{1/4}d^{p-1}_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F})\sqrt{w}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p}u\left(\frac{\gamma(\mathcal{F},\psi_{2})d_{\psi_{2}}^{p-1}(\mathcal{F})}{\sqrt{N}}+\frac{\gamma^{p}(\mathcal{F},\psi_{2})}{N}\right)+u^{3/2}\frac{\gamma^{3/2}(\mathcal{F},\psi_{2})d^{p-3/2}_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F})}{N^{3/4}}+u^{p}\frac{\gamma^{p}(\mathcal{F},\psi_{2})}{N}

\displaystyle\lesssim u\frac{\gamma(\mathcal{F},\psi_{2})d_{\psi_{2}}^{p-1}(\mathcal{F})}{\sqrt{N}}+u^{3/2}\frac{\gamma^{3/2}(\mathcal{F},\psi_{2})d^{p-3/2}_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F})}{N^{3/4}}+u^{p}\frac{\gamma^{p}(\mathcal{F},\psi_{2})}{N},

where in the last step we used that
u\leq u^{p}
since
u\geq 1
and
p\geq 2
. Observe that for
p\geq 2
,

u^{3/2}\frac{\gamma^{3/2}(\mathcal{F},\psi_{2})d^{p-3/2}_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F})}{N^{3/4}}\leq u\frac{\gamma(\mathcal{F},\psi_{2})d_{\psi_{2}}^{p-1}(\mathcal{F})}{\sqrt{N}}+u^{p}\frac{\gamma^{p}(\mathcal{F},\psi_{2})}{N},

i.e.,
t^{3/2}\leq t+t^{p}N^{p/2-1}
with
t=\frac{u\gamma(\mathcal{F},\psi_{2})}{\sqrt{N}d_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F})}\geq 0
. Therefore, for any
u\geq c(p)\lor 4
, it holds with probability at least
1-4\exp(-c^{\prime}(p)u^{2}(\gamma(\mathcal{F},\psi_{2})/d_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F}))^{2})
that

\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\bigg|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\varepsilon_{i}f^{p}(X_{i})\bigg|\lesssim_{p}u\frac{\gamma(\mathcal{F},\psi_{2})d_{\psi_{2}}^{p-1}(\mathcal{F})}{\sqrt{N}}+u^{p}\frac{\gamma^{p}(\mathcal{F},\psi_{2})}{N}.

By the symmetrization inequality for empirical processes
[
51
, Theorem 1.14]
, see also
[
21
]
and
[
74
, Chapter 2.3]
, we have with probability at least
1-C\exp(-c^{\prime}(p)u^{2}(\gamma(\mathcal{F},\psi_{2})/d_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F}))^{2})
that

\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\bigg|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}f^{p}(X_{i})-\mathbb{E}f^{p}(X)\bigg|\lesssim_{p}u\frac{\gamma(\mathcal{F},\psi_{2})d_{\psi_{2}}^{p-1}(\mathcal{F})}{\sqrt{N}}+u^{p}\frac{\gamma^{p}(\mathcal{F},\psi_{2})}{N},

where
C>4
is some absolute constant. Finally, the high-probability bound in Theorem
2.2
follows from a simple and standard modification using Lemma
4.7
. Furthermore, integrating out the tail bound (note that
\gamma(\mathcal{F},\psi_{2})\gtrsim d_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F})
) gives that

\mathbb{E}\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\bigg|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}f^{p}(X_{i})-\mathbb{E}f^{p}(X)\bigg|\lesssim_{p}\frac{\gamma(\mathcal{F},\psi_{2})d^{p-1}_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F})}{\sqrt{N}}+\frac{\gamma^{p}(\mathcal{F},\psi_{2})}{N},

which completes the proof.
∎

4.3
Proof of Theorem
4.5

This subsection presents the proof of Theorem
4.5
, which establishes a uniform bound on the
\ell_{m}
-norm of the coordinate projection vector of the function class
\mathcal{F}
. The proof leverages generic chaining and builds upon ideas from Bednorz
[
11
, Section 5]
for analyzing quadratic processes, while also addressing new challenges that arise when handling arbitrary
m\geq 1
. A key component of our approach is the use of a different Young function, defined as
\varphi(x)=2^{\min\{(\sqrt{N}x)^{2/m},\,x^{2}\}}-1
, along with
\alpha
-sub-exponential inequalities
[
22
]
to precisely characterize the tail behavior. This leads to a distinct cut-off point in the chaining process —depending on
m
— that separates sub-Gaussian and sub-exponential regimes. Similar cut-off phenomena between these two regimes first appeared in the analysis of quadratic empirical processes (see, e.g.,
[
48
, Section 5]
,
[
17
, Theorem 5.5]
and
[
70
, Theorem 14.2.6]
). However, our primary focus here is to control the non-centralized
\ell_{m}
-norm of the coordinate projection vector for any
m\geq 1
.

Proof of Theorem
4.5
.

Let
(\mathcal{F}_{s})_{s\geq 0}\subset\mathcal{F}
be an increasing sequence which satisfies
\mathcal{F}_{s}\subset\mathcal{F}_{s+1},\left|\mathcal{F}_{0}\right|=1,\left|\mathcal{F}_{s}\right|\leq 2^{2^{s}}-1
for
s\geq 1,
and
\bigcup_{s=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{F}_{s}
is dense in
\mathcal{F}
. Note that here we slightly change the requirement of the size of
\mathcal{F}_{s}
(see Definition
2.1
) for technical reasons. For any
f\in\mathcal{F}
and
s\geq 0
,
\pi_{s}f
is the nearest point in
\mathcal{F}_{s}
to
f
with respect to the
\psi_{2}
-norm. We further assume that
(\mathcal{F}_{s})_{s\geq 0}
is an almost optimal admissible sequence, ensuring that

\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}2^{s/2}\|f-\pi_{s}f\|_{\psi_{2}}\lesssim\gamma(\mathcal{F},\psi_{2}).

We first focus on the case
m>1
. Let
N_{s}:=2^{2^{s}}-1
for
s\geq 1
, and let
s^{*}
be the integer that satisfies
2^{(m-1)s^{*}}\leq N<2^{(m-1)(s^{*}+1)}
. Recall that
\varphi(x)=2^{\min\{(\sqrt{N}x)^{2/m},\,x^{2}\}}-1
, and its inverse function is

\varphi^{-1}(x)=\max\left\{\sqrt{\log_{2}(1+x)},\sqrt{\frac{(\log_{2}(1+x))^{m}}{N}}\right\},\quad x\geq 0.

Then,

\displaystyle\varphi^{-1}(N_{s})=\varphi^{-1}(2^{2^{s}}-1)=\begin{cases}\big(\frac{2^{sm}}{N}\big)^{1/2}&\text{ if }\ s>s^{*},\\
2^{s/2}&\text{ if }\ s\leq s^{*}.\end{cases}

We prove the conclusion by establishing the following two claims:

Claim I.

\displaystyle\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left|f-\pi_{s^{*}}f\right|^{m}\left(X_{i}\right)\bigg)^{1/m}\lesssim_{m}\gamma(\mathcal{F},\psi_{2})+N^{1/m}d_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F})+N^{1/2m}d_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F})(\varphi^{-1}(Z_{1}))^{1/m},

where
Z_{1}\geq 0
and
\mathbb{E}\,Z_{1}\leq 3
.

Claim II.

\displaystyle\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{N}|\pi_{s^{*}}f|^{m}(X_{i})\bigg)^{1/m}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{m}N^{1/m}d_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F})+N^{1/2m}d^{(m-1)/m}_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F})\gamma^{1/m}(\mathcal{F},\psi_{2})

\displaystyle\quad+N^{1/2m}d_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F})\left((\varphi^{-1}(Z_{2}))^{1/m}+(\varphi^{-1}(Z_{3}))^{1/m}\right),

where
Z_{2}\geq 0,Z_{3}\geq 0
and
\mathbb{E}\,Z_{2}\leq 3,\mathbb{E}\,Z_{3}\leq 3
.

Claim I and Claim II together with the triangle inequality imply that

\displaystyle\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{N}|f|^{m}(X_{i})\bigg)^{1/m}\leq\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left|f-\pi_{s^{*}}f\right|^{m}\left(X_{i}\right)\bigg)^{1/m}+\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{N}|\pi_{s^{*}}f|^{m}(X_{i})\bigg)^{1/m}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{m}\gamma(\mathcal{F},\psi_{2})+N^{1/m}d_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F})+N^{1/2m}d^{(m-1)/m}_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F})\gamma^{1/m}(\mathcal{F},\psi_{2})

\displaystyle\quad+N^{1/2m}d_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F})\left((\varphi^{-1}(Z_{1}))^{1/m}+(\varphi^{-1}(Z_{2}))^{1/m}+(\varphi^{-1}(Z_{3}))^{1/m}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim_{m}\gamma(\mathcal{F},\psi_{2})+N^{1/m}d_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F})+N^{1/2m}d^{(m-1)/m}_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F})\gamma^{1/m}(\mathcal{F},\psi_{2})+N^{1/2m}d_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F})(\varphi^{-1}(Z_{1}+Z_{2}+Z_{3}))^{1/m}

\displaystyle=\gamma(\mathcal{F},\psi_{2})+N^{1/m}d_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F})+N^{1/2m}d^{(m-1)/m}_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F})\gamma^{1/m}(\mathcal{F},\psi_{2})+N^{1/2m}d_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F})(\varphi^{-1}(Z))^{1/m},

where
Z:=Z_{1}+Z_{2}+Z_{3}
satisfies
\mathbb{E}\,Z=\mathbb{E}\,Z_{1}+\mathbb{E}\,Z_{2}+\mathbb{E}\,Z_{3}\leq 9
. This proves the first inequality in Theorem
4.5
.

For
m\geq 2
, note that

N^{1/2m}d^{(m-1)/m}_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F})\gamma^{1/m}(\mathcal{F},\psi_{2})\leq\gamma(\mathcal{F},\psi_{2})+N^{1/m}d_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F}),

or, equivalently,

N^{1/2m}\left(\frac{\gamma(\mathcal{F},\psi_{2})}{d_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F})}\right)^{1/m}\leq\frac{\gamma(\mathcal{F},\psi_{2})}{d_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F})}+N^{1/m},

which can be verified by noting that
xy\leq x^{2}+y^{m}
where
x=N^{1/2m}\geq 1
and
y=\left(\frac{\gamma(\mathcal{F},\psi_{2})}{d_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F})}\right)^{1/m}\geq 0
. This completes the proof.

When
m=1
, there is no
s^{*}
satisfying
2^{(m-1)s^{*}}\leq N<2^{(m-1)(s^{*}+1)}
. However, in this case, we have
\varphi(x)=2^{\min\{Nx^{2},x^{2}\}}-1\equiv 2^{x^{2}}-1
and its inverse function is given by
\varphi^{-1}(x)\equiv\sqrt{\log_{2}(1+x)}
. As will become clear in the proof of
Claim II
below,
Claim II
remains valid for
any
integer
s^{*}
in this setting. By taking
s^{*}\to\infty
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}|f(X_{i})|

\displaystyle=\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\lim_{s^{*}\to\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{N}|\pi_{s^{*}}f|(X_{i})\leq\lim_{s^{*}\to\infty}\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}|\pi_{s^{*}}f|(X_{i})

\displaystyle\lesssim Nd_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F})+N^{1/2}\gamma(\mathcal{F},\psi_{2})+N^{1/2}d_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F})\left((\varphi^{-1}(Z_{2}))+(\varphi^{-1}(Z_{3}))\right)

\displaystyle\leq Nd_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F})+N^{1/2}\gamma(\mathcal{F},\psi_{2})+N^{1/2}d_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F})\varphi^{-1}(Z),

where
Z:=Z_{2}+Z_{3}
satisfies
\mathbb{E}\,Z=\mathbb{E}\,Z_{2}+\mathbb{E}\,Z_{3}\leq 6
. Consequently,

\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}|f\left(X_{i}\right)|\lesssim d_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F})+\frac{\gamma(\mathcal{F},\psi_{2})}{\sqrt{N}}+\frac{d_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F})\varphi^{-1}(Z)}{\sqrt{N}},

as desired.

Now we prove
Claim I
and
Claim II
.

Proof of Claim I:

We use the following chaining

\left(f-\pi_{s^{*}}f\right)\left(X_{i}\right)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(\pi_{s_{k+1}}f-\pi_{s_{k}}f\right)\left(X_{i}\right),\quad 1\leq i\leq N,

where the sequence
\left\{s_{k}\right\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
depends on
f
and is defined as:

s_{0}=s^{*}
and

s_{k}:=\inf\left\{s>s_{k-1}:2\|f-\pi_{s}f\|_{\psi_{2}}<\|f-\pi_{s_{k-1}}f\|_{\psi_{2}}\right\},\quad k\geq 1.

Let
\Omega_{k}(f)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left|\pi_{s_{k+1}}f-\pi_{s_{k}}f\right|^{m}\left(X_{i}\right)
. By the triangle inequality,

\bigg(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left|f-\pi_{s^{*}}f\right|^{m}\left(X_{i}\right)\bigg)^{1/m}\leq\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(\Omega_{k}(f)\right)^{1/m}.

Moreover, since
m>1
,

\left(\Omega_{k}(f)\right)^{1/m}\leq\left(\left|\Omega_{k}(f)-\mathbb{E}\,\Omega_{k}(f)\right|\right)^{1/m}+\left(\mathbb{E}\,\Omega_{k}(f)\right)^{1/m}.

Clearly,

\left(\mathbb{E}\,\Omega_{k}(f)\right)^{1/m}=\|\pi_{s_{k+1}}f-\pi_{s_{k}}f\|_{L_{m}}\lesssim_{m}\|\pi_{s_{k+1}}f-\pi_{s_{k}}f\|_{\psi_{2}}.

Observe that

\varphi\bigg(\frac{\sqrt{N}|\Omega_{k}(f)-\mathbb{E}\,\Omega_{k}(f)|}{C(m)\|\pi_{s_{k+1}}f-\pi_{s_{k}}f\|^{m}_{\psi_{2}}}\bigg)\leq\sum_{g,h\in\mathcal{F}_{s_{k+1}}}\varphi\left(|\mathsf{X_{1}}(g,h)|\right),

where

\mathsf{X_{1}}(g,h):=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{N}(|g-h|^{m}-\mathbb{E}|g-h|^{m})(X_{i})}{C(m)\sqrt{N}\|g-h\|^{m}_{\psi_{2}}}.

By Lemma
4.9
, we have
\mathbb{E}\,\varphi(|\mathsf{X_{1}}(g,h)|)\leq 3
for any
g,h\in\mathcal{F}
.
Therefore,

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{N}|\Omega_{k}(f)-\mathbb{E}\,\Omega_{k}(f)|}{C(m)\|\pi_{s_{k+1}}f-\pi_{s_{k}}f\|^{m}_{\psi_{2}}}

\displaystyle\leq\varphi^{-1}\bigg(\sum_{g,h\in\mathcal{F}_{s_{k+1}}}\varphi\left(|\mathsf{X_{1}}(g,h)|\right)\bigg)

\displaystyle=\varphi^{-1}\bigg(N_{s_{k+1}}^{3}\cdot\frac{1}{N_{s_{k+1}}^{3}}\sum_{g,h\in\mathcal{F}_{s_{k+1}}}\varphi\left(|\mathsf{X_{1}}(g,h)|\right)\bigg)

\displaystyle\overset{\text{($\star$)}}{\lesssim}_{m}\varphi^{-1}(N_{s_{k+1}})+\varphi^{-1}\bigg(\frac{1}{N_{s_{k+1}}^{3}}\sum_{g,h\in\mathcal{F}_{s_{k+1}}}\varphi\left(|\mathsf{X_{1}}(g,h)|\right)\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq\varphi^{-1}(N_{s_{k+1}})+\varphi^{-1}(Z_{1}),

where (
\star
) follows from Lemma
4.8
and
\varphi^{-1}(N_{s_{k+1}}^{3})\lesssim_{m}\varphi^{-1}(N_{s_{k+1}}^{2})+\varphi^{-1}(N_{s_{k+1}})\lesssim_{m}3\varphi^{-1}(N_{s_{k+1}}).

In the last inequality,

Z_{1}:=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{N_{k}^{3}}\sum_{g,h\in\mathcal{F}_{k}}\varphi(|\mathsf{X_{1}}(g,h)|)\geq 0

satisfies
\mathbb{E}\,Z_{1}\leq 3\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{N_{k}^{3}}\cdot N_{k}^{2}=3\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{N_{k}}\leq 3
.

Now we have

|\Omega_{k}(f)-\mathbb{E}\,\Omega_{k}(f)|^{1/m}\lesssim_{m}N^{-1/2m}\|\pi_{s_{k+1}}f-\pi_{s_{k}}f\|_{\psi_{2}}\left((\varphi^{-1}(N_{s_{k+1}}))^{1/m}+(\varphi^{-1}(Z_{1}))^{1/m}\right).

Since
s_{k+1}\geq s_{1}>s_{0}=s^{*}
, we have

\varphi^{-1}(N_{s_{k+1}})=\varphi^{-1}(2^{2^{s_{k+1}}}-1)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}2^{s_{k+1}m/2}.

Then,

\displaystyle\left(\Omega_{k}(f)\right)^{1/m}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\left|\Omega_{k}(f)-\mathbb{E}\,\Omega_{k}(f)\right|\right)^{1/m}+\left(\mathbb{E}\,\Omega_{k}(f)\right)^{1/m}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{m}N^{-1/2m}\|\pi_{s_{k+1}}f-\pi_{s_{k}}f\|_{\psi_{2}}\left(2^{s_{k+1}/2}N^{-1/2m}+(\varphi^{-1}(Z_{1}))^{1/m}\right)+\|\pi_{s_{k+1}}f-\pi_{s_{k}}f\|_{\psi_{2}}

\displaystyle=N^{-1/m}2^{s_{k+1}/2}\|\pi_{s_{k+1}}f-\pi_{s_{k}}f\|_{\psi_{2}}+\|\pi_{s_{k+1}}f-\pi_{s_{k}}f\|_{\psi_{2}}\left(1+N^{-1/2m}(\varphi^{-1}(Z_{1}))^{1/m}\right).

We sum over
k
to get

\displaystyle\bigg(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left|f-\pi_{s^{*}}f\right|^{m}\left(X_{i}\right)\bigg)^{1/m}\leq\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(\Omega_{k}(f)\right)^{1/m}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{m}N^{-1/m}\bigg(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}2^{s_{k+1}/2}\|\pi_{s_{k+1}}f-\pi_{s_{k}}f\|_{\psi_{2}}\bigg)+\bigg(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\|\pi_{s_{k+1}}f-\pi_{s_{k}}f\|_{\psi_{2}}\bigg)\left(1+N^{-1/2m}(\varphi^{-1}(Z_{1}))^{1/m}\right)

\displaystyle\overset{\text{($\star$)}}{\lesssim}_{m}N^{-1/m}\gamma(\mathcal{F},\psi_{2})+\|f-\pi_{s^{*}}f\|_{\psi_{2}}\left(1+N^{-1/2m}(\varphi^{-1}(Z_{1}))^{1/m}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{-1/m}\gamma(\mathcal{F},\psi_{2})+d_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F})\left(1+N^{-1/2m}(\varphi^{-1}(Z_{1}))^{1/m}\right),

(4.5)

where in (
\star
) we used two facts

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}2^{s_{k+1}/2}\|\pi_{s_{k+1}}f-\pi_{s_{k}}f\|_{\psi_{2}}\lesssim\gamma(\mathcal{F},\psi_{2}),

(4.6)

and

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\|\pi_{s_{k+1}}f-\pi_{s_{k}}f\|_{\psi_{2}}\lesssim\|f-\pi_{s^{*}}f\|_{\psi_{2}}.

(4.7)

The inequality (
4.6
) follows by

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}2^{s_{k+1}/2}\|\pi_{s_{k+1}}f-\pi_{s_{k}}f\|_{\psi_{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}2^{s_{k+1}/2}\left(\|\pi_{s_{k+1}}f-f\|_{\psi_{2}}+\|\pi_{s_{k}}f-f\|_{\psi_{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\overset{\text{($\star$)}}{\leq}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}2^{s_{k+1}/2}\left(\|\pi_{s_{k+1}}f-f\|_{\psi_{2}}+2\|\pi_{s_{k+1}-1}f-f\|_{\psi_{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=s^{*}}^{\infty}2^{n/2}\|f-\pi_{n}f\|_{\psi_{2}}+2\sqrt{2}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}2^{(s_{k+1}-1)/2}\|f-\pi_{s_{k+1}-1}f\|_{\psi_{2}}

\displaystyle\leq 4\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}2^{n/2}\|f-\pi_{n}f\|_{\psi_{2}}\lesssim\gamma(\mathcal{F},\psi_{2}),

where (
\star
) follows by the definition of
\{s_{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
:
\|\pi_{s_{k}}f-f\|_{\psi_{2}}\leq 2\|\pi_{s_{k+1}-1}f-f\|_{\psi_{2}}
.

To prove the inequality (
4.7
), we observe that by the definition of
\{s_{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
, it holds for every
k\geq 0
that
\|f-\pi_{s_{k+1}}f\|_{\psi_{2}}\leq\frac{1}{2}\|f-\pi_{s_{k}}f\|_{\psi_{2}}
. It is straightforward to show using induction that
\|f-\pi_{s_{k}}f\|_{\psi_{2}}\leq\frac{1}{2^{k}}\|f-\pi_{s_{0}}f\|_{\psi_{2}}=\frac{1}{2^{k}}\|f-\pi_{s^{*}}f\|_{\psi_{2}}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\|\pi_{s_{k+1}}f-\pi_{s_{k}}f\|_{\psi_{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\|f-\pi_{s_{k}}f\|_{\psi_{2}}+\|f-\pi_{s_{k+1}}f\|_{\psi_{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\|f-\pi_{s^{*}}f\|_{\psi_{2}}\bigg(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{k}}+\frac{1}{2^{k+1}}\bigg)\lesssim\|f-\pi_{s^{*}}f\|_{\psi_{2}}.

Consequently, we have shown in (
4.3
) that

\displaystyle\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left|f-\pi_{s^{*}}f\right|^{m}\left(X_{i}\right)\bigg)^{1/m}\lesssim_{m}\gamma(\mathcal{F},\psi_{2})+N^{1/m}d_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F})+N^{1/2m}d_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F})(\psi^{-1}(Z_{1}))^{1/m},

where
Z_{1}\geq 0
satisfies that
\mathbb{E}\,Z_{1}\leq 3
. This completes the proof of
Claim I
.

Proof of Claim II:

For
f\in\mathcal{F}
, we define a sequence
\left\{s_{k}\right\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
which depends on
f
as follows:
s_{0}=0
and

s_{k}:=\min\left\{\inf\left\{s>s_{k-1}:2\|f-\pi_{s}f\|_{\psi_{2}}<\|f-\pi_{s_{k-1}}f\|_{\psi_{2}}\right\},s^{*}\right\},\quad k\geq 1.

Note that
s_{k}=s^{*}
for a finite
k\geq 0
.

Let
\widetilde{\Omega}_{k}(f)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}|\pi_{s_{k}}f|^{m}\left(X_{i}\right)-|\pi_{s_{k-1}}f|^{m}\left(X_{i}\right)
. We perform the chaining

\displaystyle\bigg|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}|\pi_{s^{*}}f|^{m}(X_{i})-\mathbb{E}|\pi_{s^{*}}f|^{m}-|\pi_{0}f|^{m}(X_{i})+\mathbb{E}|\pi_{0}f|^{m}\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\bigg|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}|\pi_{s_{k}}f|^{m}\left(X_{i}\right)-|\pi_{s_{k-1}}f|^{m}\left(X_{i}\right)-\mathbb{E}|\pi_{s_{k}}f|^{m}+\mathbb{E}|\pi_{s_{k-1}}f|^{m}\bigg|

\displaystyle=:\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left|\widetilde{\Omega}_{k}(f)-\mathbb{E}\,\widetilde{\Omega}_{k}(f)\right|.

Observe that

\varphi\bigg(\frac{\sqrt{N}|\widetilde{\Omega}_{k}(f)-\mathbb{E}\,\widetilde{\Omega}_{k}(f)|}{C(m)d_{\psi_{2}}^{m-1}(\mathcal{F})\|\pi_{s_{k}}f-\pi_{s_{k-1}}f\|_{\psi_{2}}}\bigg)\leq\sum_{g,h\in\mathcal{F}_{s_{k}}}\varphi\left(|\mathsf{X_{2}}(g,h)|\right),

where

\mathsf{X_{2}}(g,h)=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{N}(|g|^{m}-|h|^{m}-(\mathbb{E}|g|^{m}-\mathbb{E}|h|^{m}))(X_{i})}{C(m)\sqrt{N}d^{m-1}_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F})\|g-h\|_{\psi_{2}}}.

By Lemma
4.9
, we have
\mathbb{E}\,\varphi(|\mathsf{X_{2}}(g,h)|)\leq 3
for any
g,h\in\mathcal{F}
. Then, with the same technique as used in the proof of
Claim I
,

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{N}|\widetilde{\Omega}_{k}(f)-\mathbb{E}\,\widetilde{\Omega}_{k}(f)|}{C(m)d_{\psi_{2}}^{m-1}(\mathcal{F})\|\pi_{s_{k}}f-\pi_{s_{k-1}}f\|_{\psi_{2}}}

\displaystyle\leq\varphi^{-1}\bigg(\sum_{g,h\in\mathcal{F}_{s_{k}}}\varphi\left(|\mathsf{X_{2}}(g,h)|\right)\bigg)

\displaystyle\lesssim_{m}\varphi^{-1}(N_{s_{k}})+\varphi^{-1}(Z_{2}),

where

Z_{2}:=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{N_{k}^{3}}\sum_{g,h\in\mathcal{F}_{k}}\varphi(|\mathsf{X_{2}}(g,h)|)

satisfies
\mathbb{E}\,Z_{2}\leq 3\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{N_{k}^{3}}\cdot N_{k}^{2}=3\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{N_{k}}\leq 3
. Note that
\varphi^{-1}(N_{s_{k}})=\varphi^{-1}(2^{2^{s_{k}}}-1)=2^{s_{k}/2}

by definition.
Then,

\displaystyle\bigg|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}|\pi_{s^{*}}f|^{m}(X_{i})-\mathbb{E}|\pi_{s^{*}}f|^{m}-|\pi_{0}f|^{m}(X_{i})+\mathbb{E}|\pi_{0}f|^{m}\bigg|\leq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left|\widetilde{\Omega}_{k}(f)-\mathbb{E}\,\widetilde{\Omega}_{k}(f)\right|

\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}|\pi_{s^{*}}f|^{m}(X_{i})\lesssim_{m}d^{m}_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F})+N^{-1/2}d^{m-1}_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F})\gamma(\mathcal{F},\psi_{2})+N^{-1/2}d^{m}_{\psi_{2}}(\mathcal{F})(\varphi^{-1}(Z_{2})+\varphi^{-1}(Z_{3})),

5
Conclusions, discussion, and future directions

This paper has established sharp dimension-free concentration inequalities for simple random tensors. In so doing, we have developed the theory of multi-product empirical processes, leveraging generic chaining methods to obtain sharp high-probability upper bounds on their suprema. Our framework extends classical results on quadratic and product empirical processes to higher-order settings.

Several questions arise from this work. For Gaussian random vectors, Theorem
2.1
provides matching upper and lower bounds for the deviation of the
p
-th order sample moment tensor from its expectation, up to a constant that depends only on
p
. In the special case of
p=2
, corresponding to the sample covariance, the recent work
[
25
]
derived a sharp version of the Koltchinskii-Lounici theorem with optimal constants by exploiting the Gaussian min-max theorem, namely

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg\|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}X_{i}\otimes X_{i}-\mathbb{E}\,X\otimes X\bigg\|\leq\|\Sigma\|\bigg(1+\frac{C}{\sqrt{r(\Sigma)}}\bigg)\bigg(2\sqrt{\frac{r(\Sigma)}{N}}+\frac{r(\Sigma)}{N}\bigg),

where
C>0
is some absolute constant. A natural question is whether similarly tight constants, which should depend on
p
, can be obtained for sample moment tensors of arbitrary order
p\geq 2
in the Gaussian setting.

More recently, there has been growing interest in the estimation of smooth functionals of covariance operators
[
32
,
31
,
41
,
33
]
, including spectral projectors
[
36
,
37
,
40
,
29
]
, functionals of principal components
[
39
,
35
]
, and trace functionals and spectral measures of the covariance
[
34
]
. An interesting direction for future research is to explore the estimation of functionals of higher-order moment tensors. We anticipate that Theorem
2.1
could serve as a first step for further study in this direction.

Another interesting question concerns the
rank
of random tensors. In this paper, we have studied the concentration properties of sums of i.i.d. simple (rank-one) tensors. For
p=2
,
[
84
, Theorem 1.2]
establishes a general-rank version of Koltchinskii-Lounici theorem using the variational
principle and the PAC-Bayesian method, which provides a dimension-free deviation bound for sums of independent positive semi-definite matrices that are not necessarily rank one. More recently,
[
61
]
obtained dimension-free bounds for sums of matrices and operators with dependence and arbitrary heavy tails. A natural and interesting direction for future research is to extend Theorem
2.1
to the general-rank setting, deriving a concentration inequality for sums of i.i.d. symmetric tensors of arbitrary rank.

The recent work
[
57
]
proves the following high-dimensional central limit theorem (CLT): Let
X,X_{1},\ldots,X_{N}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{i.i.d.}}}{{\sim}}\mu
be random vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, where
\mu
is either a symmetric uniform log-concave measure or a product measure. Then,

\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(X_{i}^{\otimes p}-\mathbb{E}\,X^{\otimes p}\right)\to\text{ Gaussian},

provided that
N\gg d^{2p-1}
. Given the dimension-free nature of our operator norm bound in Theorem
2.1
, an intriguing question is whether a dimension-free CLT can be established for the normalized sum of rank-one tensors.

For a sub-Gaussian function class
\mathcal{F}
, Theorem
2.2
establishes a sharp concentration inequality for the supremum of the empirical process

\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}|f|^{p}(X_{i})-\mathbb{E}|f|^{p}(X)

for any
p\geq 2
. This result improves existing bounds and lifts classical bounds on the suprema of quadratic empirical processes to higher-order settings. Additionally, as noted in Remark
2.4
, our analysis extends to the regime
p\in[3/2,2)
. A natural direction for future work is to further generalize these results to any
p>0
. More broadly, as discussed in
[
48
]
, a fundamental objective in empirical process theory is to analyze the deviation

\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\ell(f(X_{i}))-\mathbb{E}\,\ell(f(X)),

uniformly in
f\in\mathcal{F}
, where
\ell
is a reasonable real-valued function and
\mathcal{F}
is a suitable function class. Our results in this paper address this question for the cases
\ell(t)=t^{p}
and
\ell(t)=|t|^{p}
with any
p\geq 2
, when
\mathcal{F}
is sub-Gaussian.

Another important and closely related line of research in empirical process theory concerns the study of
multiplier
empirical processes
[
42
,
74
]
, given by

f\mapsto\sum_{i=1}^{N}\xi_{i}f\left(X_{i}\right),

where
f\in\mathcal{F}
for some function class
\mathcal{F}
, and
(\xi_{i})_{i=1}^{N}
are random variables called “multipliers”. We refer to
[
51
,
52
,
26
,
24
]
for recent developments. In particular,
[
24
]
developed theory and tools for analyzing multiplier
U
-processes of the form

f\mapsto\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{m}\leq N}\xi_{i_{1}}\cdots\xi_{i_{m}}f\left(X_{i_{1}},\ldots,X_{i_{m}}\right),

which is a natural higher-order generalization of the multiplier empirical processes. The framework developed in this paper provides a natural way for studying an additional generalization:

(f_{1},\ldots,f_{p})\mapsto\sum_{i=1}^{N}\xi_{i}f_{1}(X_{i})\cdots f_{p}(X_{i}),

where
f_{k}\in\mathcal{F}^{(k)}(1\leq k\leq p)
for some function classes
(\mathcal{F}^{(k)})_{k=1}^{p}
. We leave a detailed exploration of this setting as an interesting direction for future research.

In this paper, we focused on Gaussian and sub-Gaussian random variables in developing tensor concentration inequalities, as well as on sub-Gaussian function classes
\mathcal{F}
in the study of multi-product empirical processes. An important direction for future research is to extend these results to more general measures and weakly bounded empirical processes, such as those associated with isotropic log-concave measures
[
23
,
47
,
48
,
51
]
and Gaussian mixtures
[
6
, Section 4]
. We anticipate that our analysis can be extended to general distributions by further incorporating the powerful framework developed in
[
51
]
. As shown in
[
51
, Section 4.3]
, the graded version of
\gamma
-type functionals offers advantages over the
\psi_{2}
-based complexity measure when analyzing unconditional log-concave ensembles. We expect these favorable properties to persist as we extend the results to higher-order settings.

Finally, we highlight several promising directions for future research in the broader field of concentration inequalities and statistical applications. An important open problem is to develop a sharp convex concentration inequality for random symmetric tensors, as proposed by Vershynin in
[
79
, Section 1.5]
. We expect that our tensor concentration inequality for sums of simple random tensors can offer useful insights toward addressing this challenge. In the literature on robust statistics, recent advances have focused on dimension-free bounds for mean and covariance estimation under adversarial corruption and heavy-tailed distributions
[
44
,
56
,
1
,
63
,
58
]
. Our results on dimension-free bounds for simple random tensors could serve as a foundation for developing dimension-free robust (moment) tensor estimation methods. Furthermore, we anticipate that Theorem
2.1
may have applications in various tensor-related statistical problems
[
46
,
13
,
3
]
, including tensor estimation
[
27
,
16
]
, tensor completion
[
81
,
80
]
, tensor regression
[
45
]
, tensor singular value decomposition (SVD)
[
83
,
82
]
, and tensor embeddings
[
28
]
. Additionally, our results may be relevant to independent component analysis (ICA), where fourth-order moment tensor (kurtosis) estimation plays a crucial role
[
4
]
. More broadly, our findings could contribute to advances in learning theory and empirical risk minimization
[
19
]
, and the method of moments
[
69
]
, with potential applications to cryo-EM and multi-reference alignment problems
[
64
,
10
,
18
]
.

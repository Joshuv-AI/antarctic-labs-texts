Non-Existence of Quintic Factorization for the Second Cuboid Polynomial Q p , q ​ ( t ) Q_{p,q}(t)
Valery Asiryan

Abstract
We consider the even monic degree- 10 10 second cuboid polynomial Q p , q ​ ( t ) ∈ ℤ ​ [ t ] Q_{p,q}(t)\in\mathbb{Z}[t] depending on coprime integers p ≠ q > 0 p\neq q>0 . We exclude the existence of a splitting of type 5 + 5 5+5 over ℚ \mathbb{Q} , i.e., a factorization of Q p , q ​ ( t ) Q_{p,q}(t) into two irreducible quintic polynomials. Since Q p , q ​ ( t ) Q_{p,q}(t) is even and satisfies Q p , q ​ ( 0 ) ≠ 0 Q_{p,q}(0)\neq 0 , any such 5 + 5 5+5 splitting is necessarily symmetric , meaning that it can be written in the normal form Q p , q ​ ( t ) = R p , q ​ ( t ) ⋅ ( − R p , q ​ ( − t ) ) , deg ⁡ R p , q = 5 . Q_{p,q}(t)=R_{p,q}(t)\cdot\bigl(-R_{p,q}(-t)\bigr),\qquad\deg R_{p,q}=5. After a weighted normalization reducing to a one-parameter polynomial Q r ​ ( u ) Q_{r}(u) with r = p / q ∈ ℚ > 0 r=p/q\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0} , coefficient comparison and elimination via resultants show that a 5 + 5 5+5 splitting forces the existence of a rational point on an explicitly defined plane curve F ⁡ ( r , a ) = 0 F(r,a)=0 . Passing to the quotient parameters a = r ​ y a=ry and s = r 2 s=r^{2} yields an affine curve f ⁡ ( s , y ) = 0 f(s,y)=0 such that, for each fixed s > 0 s>0 , the polynomial f ⁡ ( s , ⋅ ) f(s,\cdot) is of degree 16 16 . We compute and factor the discriminant Disc y ​ ( f ) \mathrm{Disc}_{y}(f) and then use Sturm root counts to certify that f ⁡ ( s , ⋅ ) f(s,\cdot) has no real roots for every rational s > 0 s>0 with s ≠ 1 s\neq 1 . Hence f ⁡ ( s , y ) = 0 f(s,y)=0 admits no rational solutions with s > 0 s>0 , s ≠ 1 s\neq 1 , and consequently no quintic 5 + 5 5+5 factorization occurs for Q p , q ​ ( t ) Q_{p,q}(t) when p ≠ q p\neq q . Introduction

The perfect cuboid problem asks for a rectangular box with integer edges whose face diagonals and space diagonal all have integer lengths.
Several approaches encode this problem into Diophantine conditions on parameter-dependent polynomials.
In the framework introduced by R. A. Sharipov
[
1
,
2
]
, one is led to a small collection of explicit even polynomials whose irreducibility over
\mathbb{Z}
is conjectured for coprime parameters.
The present note addresses one specific splitting pattern (degree
5+5
) for the
second cuboid polynomial

Q_{p,q}(t)
.

Goal.

We prove that for coprime integers
p\neq q>0
the polynomial
Q_{p,q}(t)
admits no
quintic
factorization of type
5+5
over
\mathbb{Q}
(hence none over
\mathbb{Z}
), i.e., it cannot be written as a product of two irreducible quintic polynomials.
By Lemma
2
, any such
5+5
splitting would have to be symmetric, so it suffices to exclude factorizations of the form

Q_{p,q}(t)=R_{p,q}(t)\cdot\bigl(-R_{p,q}(-t)\bigr),\qquad\deg R_{p,q}(t)=5.

The proof is explicit and certificate-style: the final obstruction is an interval-by-interval Sturm verification based on the discriminant stratification of a one-parameter family of degree-
16
polynomials.

Outline.

Section
2
records
Q_{p,q}(t)
and a weighted normalization to
Q_{r}(u)
.
Section
3
shows that any quintic
5+5
splitting is necessarily symmetric and reduces it to a resultant condition
F(r,a)=0
.
Section
4
introduces the quotient parameters
(s,y)
and the curve
f(s,y)=0
.
Section
5
proves that
f(s,\cdot)
has no real roots for all rational
s>0
,
s\neq 1
by Sturm root counts, yielding the main theorem.
Appendix
A
contains the SageMath/SymPy scripts used to reproduce the computations together with their transcripts.

The second cuboid polynomial and normalization

2.1
Definition of
Q_{p,q}(t)

Let
p,q\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
be coprime and
p\neq q
.
The second cuboid polynomial is the even monic degree-
10
polynomial

\displaystyle Q_{p,q}(t)={}

\displaystyle t^{10}+(2q^{2}+p^{2})(3q^{2}-2p^{2})\,t^{8}

\displaystyle+(q^{8}+10p^{2}q^{6}+4p^{4}q^{4}-14p^{6}q^{2}+p^{8})\,t^{6}

\displaystyle-p^{2}q^{2}\,(q^{8}-14p^{2}q^{6}+4p^{4}q^{4}+10p^{6}q^{2}+p^{8})\,t^{4}

\displaystyle-p^{6}q^{6}\,(q^{2}+2p^{2})(-2q^{2}+3p^{2})\,t^{2}-p^{10}q^{10}\in\mathbb{Z}[t].

(1)

2.2
Weighted normalization

The polynomial (
1
) is weighted-homogeneous of total weight
20
with weights

\deg(p)=\deg(q)=1,\qquad\deg(t)=2.

Consequently one can normalize
q=1
by a scaling of
t
.

Lemma 1

(Normalization to a one-parameter family)
.

Let
q\neq 0
and set

r:=\frac{p}{q}\in\mathbb{Q},\qquad u:=\frac{t}{q^{2}}\in\mathbb{Q}.

Then

Q_{p,q}(t)=q^{20}\,Q_{r}(u),

(2)

where

\displaystyle Q_{r}(u)={}

\displaystyle u^{10}+(2+r^{2})(3-2r^{2})\,u^{8}+\bigl(1+10r^{2}+4r^{4}-14r^{6}+r^{8}\bigr)\,u^{6}

(3)

\displaystyle-r^{2}\bigl(1-14r^{2}+4r^{4}+10r^{6}+r^{8}\bigr)\,u^{4}-r^{6}(1+2r^{2})(-2+3r^{2})\,u^{2}-r^{10}\in\mathbb{Q}[u].

Proof.

Substitute
p=rq
and
t=q^{2}u
into (
1
) and factor out
q^{20}
, using the weight check above.
∎

Corollary 1

(Integral vs. rational reduction of the symmetric normal form)
.

Let
p,q\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
with
q\neq 0
and set
r=p/q
and
u=t/q^{2}
as in Lemma
1
.
If there exists a quintic
R_{p,q}(t)\in\mathbb{Z}[t]
such that

Q_{p,q}(t)=R_{p,q}(t)\cdot\bigl(-R_{p,q}(-t)\bigr),

then
Q_{r}(u)
admits a symmetric
5+5
factorization over
\mathbb{Q}
.
More precisely, defining

\widetilde{R}(u):=q^{-10}\,R_{p,q}(q^{2}u)\in\mathbb{Q}[u],

we have

Q_{r}(u)=\widetilde{R}(u)\cdot\bigl(-\widetilde{R}(-u)\bigr).

Proof.

Substitute
t=q^{2}u
into the assumed factorization and divide by
q^{20}
.
Since
R_{p,q}(q^{2}u)=q^{10}\widetilde{R}(u)
, we obtain

q^{20}Q_{r}(u)=q^{10}\widetilde{R}(u)\cdot\bigl(-q^{10}\widetilde{R}(-u)\bigr),

hence the claimed identity in
\mathbb{Q}[u]
.
∎

Remark 1
.

Throughout the paper we focus on
r\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}
(since
p,q>0
) and
r\neq 1
(since
p\neq q
).

Quintic
5+5
factorization and the elimination curve

3.1
Reduction to the symmetric normal form

Definition 1

(Quintic
5+5
factorization)
.

Let
K
be a field of characteristic
0
and let
Q(u)\in K[u]
be an even monic polynomial of degree
10
with
Q(0)\neq 0
.
We say that
Q
admits a
quintic
5+5
factorization over
K
(or a
5+5
splitting
) if there exist monic
irreducible
quintics
A(u),B(u)\in K[u]
such that

Q(u)=A(u)B(u),\qquad\deg A=\deg B=5.

Lemma 2

(Symmetric normal form)
.

In the setting of Definition
1
, if
Q
admits a quintic
5+5
factorization over
K
, then there exists a monic quintic
R(u)\in K[u]
such that

Q(u)=R(u)\cdot\bigl(-R(-u)\bigr).

Proof.

Let
Q(u)=A(u)B(u)
with
A,B
monic irreducible quintics.
Since
Q
is even, we have
Q(u)=Q(-u)=A(-u)B(-u)
.
Because
Q(0)\neq 0
, we have
A(0)B(0)=Q(0)\neq 0
, hence
A(0)\neq 0
and
B(0)\neq 0
.

The polynomial
A(-u)
is irreducible of degree
5
and divides
Q
.
By unique factorization in
K[u]
, it must be an associate of
A
or of
B
.

It cannot be an associate of
A
: indeed, if
A(-u)=\pm A(u)
, then
A
is even or odd.
An odd polynomial satisfies
A(0)=0
, contradiction.
An even polynomial cannot have odd degree
5
unless it is identically zero, also impossible.
Therefore
A(-u)
is associate to
B
.

Because
A
and
B
are monic while
A(-u)
has leading coefficient
-1
, we obtain
B(u)=-A(-u)
, and the claim follows with
R=A
.
∎

3.2
The symmetric ansatz

Definition 2

(Symmetric
5+5
factorization)
.

Let
K
be a field of characteristic
0
and let
Q(u)\in K[u]
be an even polynomial of degree
10
with
Q(0)\neq 0
.
We say that
Q
admits a
symmetric
5+5
factorization over
K
if there exists a quintic polynomial

R(u)=u^{5}+au^{4}+bu^{3}+cu^{2}+du+e\in K[u]

such that

Q(u)=R(u)\cdot\bigl(-R(-u)\bigr).

(4)

By Lemma
2
, every quintic
5+5
factorization in the sense of Definition
1
admits such a symmetric normal form.

Proposition 1

(Coefficient comparison)
.

Fix
r\in\mathbb{Q}\setminus\{0\}
.
If
Q_{r}(u)
admits a quintic
5+5
factorization over
\mathbb{Q}
in the sense of Definition
1
, then there exist
(a,d)\in\mathbb{Q}^{2}
such that the two equations

E_{2}(r,a,d)=0,\qquad E_{3}(r,a,d)=0

(5)

hold, where
E_{2},E_{3}\in\mathbb{Z}[r,a,d]
are the numerators of the rational expressions obtained from the coefficient comparison of

Q_{r}(u)-R(u)\bigl(-R(-u)\bigr)
after solving the linear constraints for
b,c,e
.

Proof.

Assume that
Q_{r}(u)
admits a quintic
5+5
factorization over
\mathbb{Q}
in the sense of Definition
1
. By Lemma
2
we may write it in the symmetric normal form (
4
) for some monic quintic
R(u)
, and we expand
R(u)\bigl(-R(-u)\bigr)
and compare coefficients with (
3
).

The constant term gives
e^{2}=r^{10}
, so (over
\mathbb{Q}
)
e=\pm r^{5}
.
If
e=-r^{5}
, then replacing
R(u)
by the monic quintic

\widetilde{R}(u):=-R(-u)

preserves the product
\,R(u)\bigl(-R(-u)\bigr)
while sending the constant term

-e=r^{5}

Thus we may assume

The
u^{8}
coefficient gives a linear equation determining
b
in terms of
r
and
a
; the
u^{2}
coefficient gives a linear equation determining
c
in terms of
r
and
d

Substituting these into the remaining two nontrivial coefficient equations yields (
5
).
This derivation is carried out symbolically in Script 01 (Appendix
A

∎

Proposition 2

(Resultant condition
F(r,a)=0
)
.

Let

If

admits a quintic
5+5

\mathbb{Q}

Remark 2

Since the curve

5
Discriminant stratification and Sturm certificates

Fix
s\in\mathbb{R}
and view
f(s,y)
as a univariate polynomial in
y
.
Let

\Delta(s):=\mathrm{Disc}_{y}\bigl(f(s,y)\bigr)\in\mathbb{Z}[s]

be its discriminant.
Sturm’s theorem provides an exact algorithm to count real roots of a univariate polynomial on an interval
[
4
]
.

Proposition 4

(Discriminant factorization)
.

The discriminant
\Delta(s)
factors over
\mathbb{Z}
as

\Delta(s)=C\cdot s^{156}(s-1)^{54}(s+1)^{22}P_{6}(s)^{4}P_{28}(s)^{2},

(8)

where
C\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
is a constant,

P_{6}(s)=s^{6}+15s^{5}-1585s^{4}+3052s^{3}-1585s^{2}+15s+1,

(9)

and
P_{28}(s)\in\mathbb{Z}[s]
is an explicitly determined polynomial of degree
28
(listed in Appendix
A
via the transcript of Script 02).
In particular, for
s>0
and
s\neq 1
, the only possible values where the number of real roots of
f(s,\cdot)
may change are the positive real roots of
P_{6}
and
P_{28}
.

Computational proof.

Computed and printed by Script 02 (Appendix
A
).
∎

Lemma 3

(Constancy between discriminant roots)
.

Let
I\subset\mathbb{R}
be an open interval on which
\Delta(s)\neq 0
and the leading coefficient of
f(s,y)
with respect to
y
does not vanish.
Then the number of real roots of
f(s,\cdot)
is constant for all
s\in I
.

Proof.

On
I
, the leading coefficient is nonzero, so the degree of
f(s,\cdot)
is constant.
Moreover
\Delta(s)\neq 0
implies that
f(s,\cdot)
has only simple roots, hence real roots move continuously with
s
and cannot be created or annihilated within
I
.
Equivalently, the Sturm root count on
(-\infty,+\infty)
is locally constant and therefore constant on the connected interval
I
.
∎

Lemma 4

(No rational discriminant zeros for
s>0
,
s\neq 1
)
.

Let
s\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}
with
s\neq 1
.
Then
\Delta(s)\neq 0
.

Proof.

By Proposition
4
, if
\Delta(s)=0
then
s\in\{0,1,-1\}
or
P_{6}(s)=0
or
P_{28}(s)=0
.
Under the assumptions
s>0
,
s\neq 1
, only the latter two possibilities remain.

For
P_{6}
, the rational root theorem applies since
P_{6}
is monic with constant term
1
: any rational root must be
\pm 1
, but

P_{6}(1)=-86\neq 0,\qquad P_{6}(-1)=-6250\neq 0,

so
P_{6}
has no rational roots.

For
P_{28}
, Script 02 performs an exact rational-root test over
\mathbb{Q}
(reported in the transcript as
Rational roots of P28: {}
), hence
P_{28}
has no rational roots.
Therefore
\Delta(s)\neq 0
for all rational
s>0
with
s\neq 1
.
∎

Theorem 1

(No real roots for
s>0
,
s\neq 1
)
.

For every rational
s>0
with
s\neq 1
, the univariate polynomial
f(s,y)\in\mathbb{Q}[y]
has no real roots.
In particular, the Diophantine equation
f(s,y)=0
has no rational solutions with
s\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}
and
s\neq 1
.

Proof.

Fix a rational
s>0
with
s\neq 1
.
By Lemma
4
we have
\Delta(s)\neq 0
.
Also the leading coefficient of
f(s,y)
with respect to
y
is nonzero for
s>0
(indeed it is proportional to
s^{8}
), so
f(s,\cdot)
has constant degree
16
.

By Proposition
4
, the real roots of
P_{6}
and
P_{28}
partition
(0,+\infty)
into finitely many open intervals on each of which
\Delta\neq 0
.
Script 02 isolates these discriminant roots into rational brackets and chooses a rational sample point in each complementary interval:

(0,\alpha_{1}),\ (\alpha_{1},\beta_{1}),\ (\beta_{1},1),\ (1,\beta_{2}),\ (\beta_{2},\beta_{3}),\ (\beta_{3},\alpha_{2}),\ (\alpha_{2},+\infty),

where
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}
are the positive roots of
P_{6}
and
\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\beta_{3}
are the positive roots of
P_{28}
.
For each sample
s
the script computes the Sturm root count of
f(s,\cdot)
on
(-\infty,+\infty)
and obtains zero real roots in every interval (see the transcript of Script 02).

By Lemma
3
, on each interval the number of real roots is constant, hence it is zero throughout the interval.
Since our fixed rational
s
lies in exactly one of these intervals and satisfies
\Delta(s)\neq 0
, it follows that
f(s,\cdot)
has no real roots.

The last claim follows because any rational solution
y\in\mathbb{Q}
would in particular be a real root.
∎

6
Main theorem

Theorem 2

(No quintic
5+5
factorization)
.

Let
p,q\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
be coprime with
p\neq q
and let
Q_{p,q}(t)
be as in (
1
).
Then
Q_{p,q}(t)
admits no quintic
5+5
factorization of splitting type
(5,5)
over
\mathbb{Z}
, i.e., there do not exist monic irreducible quintics
A(t),B(t)\in\mathbb{Z}[t]
such that

Q_{p,q}(t)=A(t)\,B(t).

Equivalently (by Gauss’ lemma),
Q_{p,q}(t)
has no
5+5
splitting pattern in its factorization over
\mathbb{Q}[t]
.

Proof.

Assume, for contradiction, that
Q_{p,q}(t)
admits a quintic
5+5
factorization over
\mathbb{Z}
, so that

Q_{p,q}(t)=A(t)B(t)

for some monic irreducible quintics
A(t),B(t)\in\mathbb{Z}[t]
.
Since
Q_{p,q}(t)
is even and
Q_{p,q}(0)=-p^{10}q^{10}\neq 0
, Lemma
2
(applied over
\mathbb{Q}
) yields

B(t)=-A(-t),

so
Q_{p,q}(t)
admits the symmetric normal form

Q_{p,q}(t)=R_{p,q}(t)\cdot\bigl(-R_{p,q}(-t)\bigr)

with
R_{p,q}(t):=A(t)\in\mathbb{Z}[t]
.
By Corollary
1
with
r=p/q
and
u=t/q^{2}
we obtain a symmetric
5+5
factorization of
Q_{r}(u)
over
\mathbb{Q}
.
By Proposition
2
there exists
a\in\mathbb{Q}
with
F(r,a)=0
.
By Proposition
3
this yields a rational solution
(s,y)\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}\times\mathbb{Q}
of
f(s,y)=0
with
s=r^{2}
.
Since
r>0
and
r\neq 1
(Remark
1
), we have
s>0
and
s\neq 1
, contradicting Theorem
1
.
∎

Script 01: Algebraic derivation and genus computation

Code.

# SageMath script

# -*- coding: utf-8 -*-

"""

Script 01: Algebraic Derivation and Genus Calculation for the Second Cuboid Conjecture in the q=1 normalization, i.e. for Q_r(u).

Case: Quintic 5+5 splitting (symmetric normal form)

Author: Valery Asiryan

Date: 2026

Core idea:

1. We investigate whether the 10th-degree polynomial Q_r(u) (normalized with q=1)

can split into two factors of degree 5.

2. Based on the evenness of Q_r(u), a 5+5 splitting into irreducible quintics must occur in the symmetric normal form

Q_r(u)=R(u)\,(-R(-u)).

3. By comparing coefficients, we derive a system of algebraic equations.

4. Using Resultants (Variable Elimination), we reduce this system to a single

necessary condition F(r, a) = 0, defining a plane algebraic curve.

5. We compute the geometric genus of this curve.

"""

import sympy as sp

# Variables

r, a, d, u = sp.symbols(’r a d u’)

# Coefficients of Q_r(u) = uˆ10 + A uˆ8 + B uˆ6 + C uˆ4 + D uˆ2 - rˆ10

A = (2 + r**2)*(3 - 2*r**2)

B = 1 + 10*r**2 + 4*r**4 - 14*r**6 + r**8

C = -r**2*(1 - 14*r**2 + 4*r**4 + 10*r**6 + r**8)

D = -r**6*(1 + 2*r**2)*(-2 + 3*r**2)

Q = u**10 + A*u**8 + B*u**6 + C*u**4 + D*u**2 - r**10

# Ansatz for 5+5 splitting: Q_r(u) = R(u) * (-R(-u))

b, c, e = sp.symbols(’b c e’)

R = u**5 + a*u**4 + b*u**3 + c*u**2 + d*u + e

prod = sp.expand(R * (-R.subs(u, -u)))

# Coeff equations

P = sp.Poly(prod - Q, u) # degree 8 (uˆ10 cancels)

coeffs = P.all_coeffs() # [uˆ8, uˆ7, ..., uˆ0]

# Nontrivial ones are uˆ8,uˆ6,uˆ4,uˆ2,uˆ0

eq8 = coeffs[0] # uˆ8

eq6 = coeffs[2] # uˆ6

eq4 = coeffs[4] # uˆ4

eq2 = coeffs[6] # uˆ2

eq0 = coeffs[8] # uˆ0

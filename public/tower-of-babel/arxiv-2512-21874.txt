Abstract
Qudits offer the potential for low-overhead magic state distillation, although previous results for asymptotically good codes have required qudit dimension q ≫ 100 q\gg 100 or code length 𝒩 ≫ 100 \mathcal{N}\gg 100 . These parameters far exceed experimental demonstrations of qudit platforms, and thus motivate the search for better codes. Using a novel lifting procedure, we construct the first family of good triorthogonal codes on the 𝔽 2 2 ​ m \mathbb{F}_{2^{2m}} alphabet with m ≥ 3 m\geq 3 that lies above the Tsfasman-Vladut-Zink bound. These codes yield a family of asymptotically good quantum codes with transversal CCZ gates, enabling constant space overhead magic state distillation with qudit dimension as small as q = 64 q=64 . Further, we identify a promising code with parameters [ [ 42 , 14 , 6 ] ] 64 [[42,14,6]]_{64} . Finally, we show that a distilled | C ​ C ​ Z ⟩ 2 2 ​ m \ket{CCZ}_{2^{2m}} can be reduced to a | C ​ C ​ Z ⟩ 2 n \ket{CCZ}_{2^{n}} state for arbitrary n n with a constant-depth Clifford circuit of at most 9 computational basis measurements, 12 single-qudit and 9 two-qudit Clifford gates.

III
Basic notions of Algebraic Geometry codes

Algebraic geometry (AG) codes are a class of error-correcting codes constructed from the framework of algebraic curves over finite fields. For a detailed review, we point readers toward Refs.
13
;
14
;
63
, and we will further adopt notation following Refs.
71
;
48
. The basic idea is to exploit the structure of a curve — specifically, its rational points and the associated function field — to define codewords. In particular, by evaluating chosen functions at a set of rational points, one obtains linear codes whose parameters can surpass classical bounds. The theory draws on concepts from algebraic geometry to systematically relate the properties of the curve to the performance of the code. This interplay between geometry and coding theory has led to highly efficient classical codes, including ones that achieve the TVZ bound
65
.
Many of the same ideas can be adapted to quantum error-correcting codes by using the CSS construction combined with classical AG codes to achieve high rates and large distances.
This connection allows algebraic geometry to inform the design of quantum codes, enabling quantum analogues that inherit favorable properties from their classical counterparts.

III.3
Triorthogonal codes

Triorthogonal codes were first proposed in
5
with the explicit purpose of making qubit magic state distillation more efficient. They are the most general stabilizer codes with transversal T gates, and important because they have low-overhead (
\gamma=\log_{2}(3)\approx 1.6
) for universal qubit fault-tolerant quantum computing.

A triorthogonal code is a stabilizer code that comes from a special kind of matrix
\mathcal{G}
with two orthogonality conditions:
pairwise orthogonality
between any two rows and
triple orthogonality
where the product of any three rows is 0.
Subsequent research extended the triorthogonal code framework to qudits
56
;
60
;
55
.
For triorthogonality it is more practical formulate it via the star (
\star
) product of codes following Ref.
39
;
48
where a code
C^{\star 2}
is given by

\displaystyle C^{\star 2}:=C\star C=\left\{c\star c^{\prime}\mid c,c^{\prime}\in C\right\}

(14)

and
c\star c^{\prime}
is the point-wise product of codewords
c
and
c^{\prime}
,

\displaystyle c\star c^{\prime}=(c_{1}c^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,c_{n}c^{\prime}_{n}).

(15)

A code
C\subseteq\mathbb{F}^{n}_{q}
satisfies the
star-square property
if
C^{\star 2}\subseteq C^{\perp}
and is equivalent to triple orthogonality. In addition to triple orthogonality, requiring the code to contain the constant word
(1,\ldots,1)
ensures the code has pairwise orthogonality.

Definition 7

(Triorthogonal code)
.

A linear code satisfying the Star-square property i.e. triple orthogonality i.e. triple orthogonality and containing the constant word
(1,\ldots,1)
i.e. pairwise orthogonality is triorthogonal.

In Lemma 2.3 of Ref.
48
, it is shown that a classical triorthogonal code yields a quantum code
\mathcal{Q}:\left[[\mathcal{N},\mathcal{K},\mathcal{D}\right]]_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}
where the transversal
(CCZ)^{\otimes\mathcal{N}}
gate implements the logical
\overline{CCZ}^{\otimes\mathcal{K}}
gate. Following Refs.
48
;
71
, it is possible to construct triorthogonal AG codes. From e.g. Ref.
48
, there is a sufficient condition for an AG code to be triorthogonal for power of two qudits,
q=2^{m}
. For completeness, we will present this sufficient condition using Prop.
III.2
and Lemma
1
.

Proposition III.2

(Some useful properties of AG codes)
.

Let
C:=C_{\mathcal{L}}(D,G)
and
C^{\prime}:=C_{\mathcal{L}}(D,G^{\prime})
be two AG codes with duals
C^{\perp}:=C_{\mathcal{L}}(D,D-G+(\eta))
and
(C^{\prime})^{\perp}:=C_{\mathcal{L}}(D,D-G^{\prime}+(\eta^{\prime}))
respectively. Then

1.

If
G\leq G^{\prime}
then
C\subseteq C^{\prime}

2.

C^{\star 2}\subseteq C_{\mathcal{L}}(D,2G)
.

Proof.

The first statement simply follows from the fact that
G\leq G^{\prime}
implies
\mathcal{L}(G)\subseteq\mathcal{L}(G^{\prime})
. Then, every codeword
(f(P_{1}),{...},f(P_{n}))\in C
must also be in
C^{\prime}
. Similarly, the second statement follows from the fact that for every pair
f,g\in\mathcal{L}(G)
, the product
fg
is in
\mathcal{L}(2G)
.
∎

Using Proposition
III.2
, requiring
C_{\mathcal{L}}(D,2G)\subseteq C_{\mathcal{L}}(D,G)^{\perp}
ensures
C^{\star 2}\subseteq C^{\perp}
. The lemma below will present sufficient conditions on the divisor
G
to obtain a triorthogonal AG code.

Lemma 1

(Triorthogonal AG code)
.

Let
C:=C_{\mathcal{L}}(D,G)
with dual
C^{\perp}=C_{\mathcal{L}}(D,D-G+(\eta))
where
\eta
is as given in Prop.
III.1
.
If

\displaystyle G

\displaystyle\geq 0\;\text{ and }\;3G\leq D+(\eta),

(16)

then
(1,{...},1)\in C
and
C^{\star 2}\subseteq C^{\perp}
.

Proof.

The first statement follows from the fact that for
G\geq 0
, the constant function
f=1
is in
\mathcal{L}(G)
since it has no poles (and no zeros). The second statement arises from the fact that
2G\leq D-G+(\eta)\Leftrightarrow 3G\leq D+(\eta)
. Therefore, under this condition, we have
C_{\mathcal{L}}(D,2G)\subseteq C^{\perp}
(Prop.
III.2
, first statement) and consequently
C^{\star 2}\subseteq C^{\perp}
(Prop.
III.2
, second statement).
∎

III.4
Tsfasman-Vladut-Zink bound

One of our main results is that our triorthogonal codes are the first to exceed the TVZ bound
65
. Below, we review asymptotic bounds of classical codes and the TVZ bound. For an
[n,k,d]
classical code
C
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, the ratios:

\displaystyle R(C):=\frac{k}{n}\text{ and }\delta(C):=\frac{d}{n}

(17)

are called encoding rate and relative distance respectively. The set

\displaystyle V_{q}:=\{(\delta(C),R(C))\mid C\text{ is a code over }\mathbb{F}_{q}\}\subseteq[0,1]^{2}

(18)

contains all possible points
(\delta(C),R(C))
where
C
is a code over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. The asymptotic behavior of codes over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
can be understood by studying the set of limit points of
V_{q}
, denoted
U_{q}
. That is, a point
(\delta,R)\in[0,1]^{2}
is in
U_{q}
if and only if there exists a sequence of codes
\{C_{i}\}_{i\geq 0}
such that as
i\rightarrow\infty
,

\displaystyle n_{i}

\displaystyle\rightarrow\infty,R(C_{i})\rightarrow R\text{ and }\delta(C_{i})\rightarrow\delta.

(19)

There exists a continuous function
\alpha_{q}
such that
0\leq R\leq\alpha_{q}(\delta)
for all
\delta
.
\alpha_{q}
satisfies
\alpha_{q}(0)=1
and
\alpha_{q}(\delta)=0
for
1-1/q\leq\delta\leq 1
. The exact value of
\alpha_{q}(\delta)
in the interval
0<\delta<1-1/q
is unknown. However, there are bounds. Lower bounds are important because a non-zero lower bound guarantees the existence of good codes. One lower bound is the GV bound which random codes typically exceed. A significant achievement of algebraic geometry code construction is that it yields an improved bound, the so-called TVZ bound. For a square
q=r^{2}
, the TVZ bound is

\displaystyle\alpha_{q}(\delta)\geq 1-\frac{1}{r-1}-\delta.

(20)

For
q\geq 49
– which is for all codes we consider – the TVZ bound is larger than the GV bound for an increasing interval around
\delta\sim 0.4
, showing that there are AG codes better than random codes. We will show that despite the extra algebraic constraint imposed by the triorthogonality requirement, our codes satisfy the TVZ bound. We call our codes triorthgonal TVZ codes.

IV
Classical Code Constructions

Following Refs.
48
;
71
, we construct a good family of triorthogonal codes
\{C_{j}\}_{j\rightarrow\infty}
on qudits with dimension
q=r^{2}
where
r\geq 8
is a power of two.
A “good” code means that both its rate
k_{j}/n_{j}
and the relative distance
d_{j}/n_{j}
do not vanish as
j\to\infty
.
Since we preserve the triorthogonal property, this family of good classical codes will yield quantum codes with transversal
CCZ
gates for qudits of dimensions
q\geq 8^{2}
.

Our construction is recursive. We consider a tower of function fields
\mathcal{W}=(F_{0},F_{1},{...})
,
where
F_{0}=\mathbb{F}_{q}(x_{0})
is the rational function field,

F_{j}
is an extension of
F_{j-1}
(for
j\geq 1
), and

\mathbb{F}_{q}
is the full field of constants for each function field
F_{j}
. Each code
C_{j}
is an AG code
C_{j}:=C_{\mathcal{L}}(D_{j},G_{j})
, where each
D_{j}
and
G_{j}
are disjoint divisors in
F_{j}
and the places in
D_{j}
are rational.

The simplicity of our construction is that we only need to construct the base code
C_{0}
explicitly. This construction is straightforward since
F_{0}
is the rational function field. Then, we present our lifting procedure (Sec.
IV.1
) to obtain the code
C_{j+1}
using the
C_{j}
code. Remarkably, we will show that this lifting procedure preserves triorthogonality (Thm.
2
). Another convenient property of our construction is that the parameters of the
C_{j}
codes can be derived or bounded using the parameters of
C_{0}
.

IV.1
Code lifting

Let the function field
F_{j+1}
be an extension of the function field
F_{j}
. We assume that both function fields have the same field of constants
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. We can now use the conorm divisor defined in Eq. (
5
) with respect to these field extensions to lift codes recursively.

Definition 8

(Code lifting)
.

Let
C_{j}:=C_{\mathcal{L}}(D_{j},G_{j})
where
D_{j}=P_{1}+\cdots+P_{n_{j}}
includes only places that split completely in the extension
F_{j+1}/F_{j}
. Set

\displaystyle D_{j+1}

\displaystyle:=\operatorname{Con}_{F_{j+1}/F_{j}}(D_{j}),

\displaystyle G_{j+1}

\displaystyle:=\operatorname{Con}_{F_{j+1}/F_{j}}(G_{j}).

(21)

Then
C_{j+1}:=C_{\mathcal{L}}(D_{j+1},G_{j+1})
is the lifting of
C_{j}
.

Note that
C_{j+1}
is a valid AG code (Sec.
III
) because

1.

Since each place
P\in\operatorname{supp}(D_{j})
splits completely in the extension
F_{j+1}/F_{j}
, the places
P^{\prime}\in\operatorname{supp}(D_{j+1})
are rational places of
F_{j+1}
.

2.

Since
D_{j}
and
G_{j}
are disjoint, so are
D_{j+1}
and
G_{j+1}
.

Moreover, we can relate
C_{j+1}^{\perp}
to
C_{j}^{\perp}
.

Lemma 2

(
C^{\perp}_{j+1}
code)
.

Let
C_{j}:=C_{\mathcal{L}}(D_{j},G_{j})
. Suppose
z\in F_{j}
is as in Prop.
III.1
so that
C^{\perp}_{j}=C_{\mathcal{L}}(D_{j},D_{j}-G_{j}+(\eta_{j}))
where
\eta_{j}=dz/z
. Regarding
z
as an element of
F_{j+1}
, let us set
\eta_{j+1}=dz/z
, a differential in
F_{j+1}
. Then,
C_{j+1}^{\perp}=C_{\mathcal{L}}(D_{j+1},D_{j+1}-G_{j+1}+(\eta_{j+1}))
.

Proof.

To prove this statement, we observe that any place
P^{\prime}\in\operatorname{supp}(D_{j+1})
lies above some place
P\in\operatorname{supp}(D_{j})
. Then,
v_{P^{\prime}}(z)=e(P^{\prime}|P)v_{P}(z)=v_{P}(z)=1
. Therefore, this choice of
\eta_{j+1}
satisfies Prop.
III.1
.
∎

Now, we have all the ingredients to establish one of our main results. More precisely, we show that our lifting operation preserves the multiplication property
C^{*2}\subseteq C^{\perp}
.

Theorem 2

(Lifting theorem)
.

Suppose the code
C_{j}:=C_{\mathcal{L}}(D_{j},G_{j})
is as in Lemma
1
, i.e.

\displaystyle G_{j}\geq 0\text{ and }3G_{j}\leq D_{j}+(\eta_{j}).

(22)

Then, so is the lifted code
C_{j+1}
.

Proof.

To prove this theorem, we observe that
G_{j+1}\geq 0
is trivial since the Conorm of a positive divisor is also a positive divisor. The rest is to establish that
3G_{j+1}\leq D_{j+1}+(\eta_{j+1})
.
In fact, one can derive the formula

\displaystyle 3G_{j+1}-D_{j+1}-(\eta_{j+1)})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\operatorname{Con}_{F_{j+1}/F_{j}}\left(3G_{j}-D_{j}-(\eta_{j})\right)-\operatorname{Diff}(F_{j+1}/F_{j})

(23)

by identifying our lifted differential with the cotrace of the lower differential,
\eta_{j+1}=\mathrm{Cotr}_{F^{\prime}/F}(\eta_{j})
, and using the cotrace divisor formula of Eq. (
6
).
By assumption, we have
3G_{j}-D_{j}-(\eta_{j})\leq 0
. Then, since the different divisor is positive, we arrive at
3G_{j+1}-D_{j+1}-(\eta_{j+1})\leq 0
, concluding the proof.
∎

In other words, our lifting procedure allows one to obtain new triorthogonal codes from existing ones. Next, we relate the parameters of the
C_{j+1}
code to those of
C_{j}
.

Lemma 3

(Parameters of
C_{j+1}
)
.

Let
C_{j}=C_{\mathcal{L}}(D_{j},G_{j})
and
C_{j+1}=C_{\mathcal{L}}(D_{j+1},G_{j+1})

, where
D_{j+1}=\operatorname{Con}_{F_{j+1}/F_{j}}(D_{j})
and
G_{j+1}=\operatorname{Con}_{F_{j+1}/F_{j}}(G_{j})
. Then

1.

Length
n_{j+1}
satisfies

\displaystyle n_{j+1}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{deg}(D_{j+1})

\displaystyle=[F_{j+1}:F_{j}]\operatorname{deg}(D_{j})

\displaystyle=[F_{j+1}:F_{j}]n_{j}.

(24)

2.

Let
g_{j}:=g(F_{j})
and
g_{j+1}:=g(F_{j+1})
be the genera of the two function fields respectively. Suppose
2g_{j+1}-2<\operatorname{deg}(G_{j+1})<n_{j+1}
. Then, the dimension
k_{j+1}
is

\displaystyle k_{j+1}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{deg}(G_{j+1})+1-g_{j+1}

\displaystyle=[F_{j+1}:F_{j}]\operatorname{deg}(G_{j})+1-g_{j+1}.

(25)

3.

Distance
d_{j+1}
satisfies

\displaystyle d_{j+1}

\displaystyle\geq n_{j+1}-\operatorname{deg}(G_{j+1})

\displaystyle=[F_{j+1}:F_{j}]\left(n_{j}-\operatorname{deg}(G_{j})\right).

(26)

Proof.

These statements follow from
III.2
and from the fact that for a divisor
A\in\operatorname{Div}(F_{j})
,
\operatorname{deg}(\operatorname{Con}_{F_{j+1}/F_{j}}(A))=[F_{j+1}:F_{j}]\operatorname{deg}(A)
.

∎

To end this discussion, we point that out there exists an alternative lifting method (provided in Appendix
B
) similar to that used in Ref.
13
.
While such a lifting improves the dimension of the code, this improvement will be sub-leading for the family of codes we will build.
Therefore, we will use the simpler lifting to analyze the asymptotic behavior of our codes.

IV.2
The Family of Codes

The family of codes
\{C_{j}\}_{j\rightarrow\infty}
is constructed from a tower
\mathcal{W}
of function fields over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
with
q=r^{2}
. Using this tower, we show that for
r\geq 8
, there exists a good family of codes
\{C_{j}\}_{j\rightarrow\infty}
in which each code
C_{j}
is triorthogonal. The tower we use is described in Refs.
66
;
63
.

Definition 9
.

Let
\mathcal{W}=(F_{0},F_{1},F_{2},\ldots)
be the tower of function field over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
with
q=r^{2}
where

1.

F_{0}:=\mathbb{F}_{q}(x_{0})
is the rational function field.

2.

F_{j+1}:=F_{j}(x_{j+1})
and each
x_{j}
and
x_{j+1}
satisfy the relation

\displaystyle x^{r}_{j+1}+x_{j+1}=\frac{x^{r}_{j}}{x^{r-1}_{j}+1}.

(27)

Many useful properties of this tower are already known
(
66
, Chapter 7)
.

Proposition IV.1

(Properties of tower)
.

Consider the tower
\mathcal{W}
in
Definition

9
.

1.

For each
j\geq 0
, the extension
F_{j}/F_{0}
has degree
[F_{j}:F_{0}]=r^{j}
.

2.

The rational places in
F_{0}
form the set

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}:=\left\{P_{\alpha}:\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{r^{2}}\backslash\mathbb{F}_{r}\right\},

(28)

and each splits completely in all extensions
F_{j}/F_{0}
. This set has cardinality
|\mathcal{Z}|=r(r-1)
.

3.

The ramification locus of the tower is

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}:=

\displaystyle\left\{P_{\alpha}:\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{r}\right\}\cup\{P_{\infty}\}.

(29)

This set has cardinality
|\mathcal{V}|=r+1
.

4.

The genus
g_{j}:=g(F_{j})
of the function field
F_{j}
is

\displaystyle g_{j}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\left(r^{\frac{j+1}{2}}-1\right)^{2}&\text{if \,$j$\, is odd}\\
\left(r^{\frac{j}{2}}-1\right)\left(r^{\frac{j+2}{2}}-1\right)&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

(30)

Often, we will use the expression

\displaystyle 1-g_{j}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}-r^{j+1}+2r^{\frac{j+1}{2}}&\text{if \,$j$\, is odd}\\
-r^{j+1}+r^{\frac{j}{2}}(1+r)&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

(31)

which can be derived from Eq. (
30
).

Now, we are ready to present our family of triorthogonal codes. Our construction is recursive in a simple form: we first use the rational function field
F_{0}:=\mathbb{F}_{q}(x_{0})
to construct the base code
C_{0}
, and then obtain each subsequent code
C_{j+1}
from
C_{j}
via the triorthogonality-preserving lifting operation. Moreover, the parameters of all codes in the sequence can be computed or bounded from those of
C_{0}
. We summarize this result in the theorem below.

Theorem 3

(Good family of triorthogonal codes)
.

For
r=2^{m}
with
m\geq 3
, there exists a good family of triorthogonal codes
\left\{C_{j}\right\}_{j\rightarrow\infty}
over the alphabet
\mathbb{F}_{r^{2}}
with parameters
\left[n_{j},k_{j},d_{j}\right]
given by

\displaystyle n_{j}

\displaystyle=r^{j}(r^{2}-r)

\displaystyle k_{j}

\displaystyle=r^{j}(r+1)\left\lfloor\frac{r-2}{3}\right\rfloor+1-g_{j}

\displaystyle d_{j}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{2}{3}r^{j}(r^{2}-r+2).

(32)

One explicit family realizing these parameters is defined recursively by

\displaystyle C_{0}

\displaystyle=C_{\mathcal{L}}(D_{0},G_{0}),

\displaystyle C_{j+1}

\displaystyle=C_{\mathcal{L}}(\operatorname{Con}_{F_{j+1}/F_{j}}(D_{j}),\operatorname{Con}_{F_{j+1}/F_{j}}(G_{j})),

(33)

where

\displaystyle D_{0}=\sum\limits_{P_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{Z}}P_{\alpha},\;\;\;G_{0}=\left\lfloor\frac{r-2}{3}\right\rfloor\sum_{Q_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{V}}Q_{\alpha}.

(34)

and
F_{j}\in\mathcal{W}
, the tower of Def.
9
.

Proof.

We prove this theorem by construction. By Thm.
2
, it is sufficient to explicitly construct the base code
C_{0}:=C_{\mathcal{L}}(D_{0},G_{0})
. We use the set
\mathcal{Z}
to construct the divisor
D_{0}
and the set
\mathcal{V}
to construct
G_{0}
. Set

\displaystyle D_{0}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{P\in\mathcal{Z}}P

\displaystyle G_{0}:=\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{V}}a_{Q}Q

(35)

where the coefficients
a_{Q}\in\mathbb{Z}
will be chosen so that
C_{0}
is triorthogonal.

From Ref.
(
63
, Prop. 8.1.2)
, a differential
\eta_{0}
as in Prop.
III.1
is given by

\displaystyle\eta_{0}:=\frac{dt_{0}}{t_{0}},\quad t_{0}:=\prod\limits_{\alpha\in\mathcal{Z}}(x_{0}+\alpha).

(36)

A direct calculation gives the canonical divisor (outlined in Appendix
A
)

\displaystyle(\eta_{0})=-D_{0}+(r-2)\sum\limits_{Q\in\mathcal{V}}Q.

(37)

Therefore, we can ensure
C_{0}
is triorthogonal by setting
a_{Q}:=\lfloor(r-2)/3\rfloor
for
r\geq 8
. Hence, the base code is
C_{0}:=C_{\mathcal{L}}(D_{0},G_{0})

with

\displaystyle D_{0}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{P\in\mathcal{Z}}P,

\displaystyle G_{0}

\displaystyle=\left\lfloor\frac{r-2}{3}\right\rfloor\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{V}}Q.

(38)

The parameters of
C_{0}
are

\displaystyle n_{0}

\displaystyle=r(r-1),

\displaystyle k_{0}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{deg}(G_{0})+1=(r+1)\left\lfloor\frac{r-2}{3}\right\rfloor+1,

\displaystyle d_{0}

\displaystyle=r(r-1)-(r+1)\left\lfloor\frac{r-2}{3}\right\rfloor.

(39)

For example, for
r=8
,
C_{0}
is a triorthogonal code with parameters
[56,19,38]_{8^{2}}
.

We now define the family by successive lifting. Since
P\in\mathcal{Z}
and
Q\in\mathcal{V}
, we can ensure that for each
j
:

1.

Every place in
\operatorname{supp}(D_{j})
splits completely in the extension
F_{j+1}/F_{j}
.

2.

The divisors
D_{j+1}
and
G_{j+1}
are disjoint.

Therefore, each
C_{j}
can be lifted to
C_{j+1}
by Def.
8
:

\displaystyle C_{0}

\displaystyle=C_{\mathcal{L}}(D_{0},G_{0}),

\displaystyle C_{j+1}

\displaystyle=C_{\mathcal{L}}(\operatorname{Con}_{F_{j+1}/F_{j}}(D_{j}),\operatorname{Con}_{F_{j+1}/F_{j}}(G_{j})),

(40)

with
F_{j}\in\mathcal{W}
.
By Thm.
2
, each
C_{j}
is triorthogonal.
Lemma
3
in combination with Eq. (
39
) and

\displaystyle\operatorname{deg}

\displaystyle G_{j}>2g_{j}-2\text{ for }r\geq 8.

(41)

make clear that Eq. (
32
) holds.

Now, it is easy to show that this family is asymptotically good, i.e. the ratios
d_{j}/n_{j}
and
k_{j}/n_{j}
do not vanish.

\displaystyle\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\frac{d_{j}}{n_{j}}

\displaystyle\geq\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\frac{r^{j}(r^{2}-r+2)}{r^{j}(r^{2}-r)}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{3}+\frac{4}{3r(r-1)}

(42)

For the dimension, we can use Eq (
31
) to get

\displaystyle\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\frac{k_{j}}{n_{j}}

\displaystyle=\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\frac{r^{j}\left[(r+1)\left\lfloor\frac{r-2}{3}\right\rfloor-1\right]}{r^{j}r(r-1)}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{3}-\frac{2r+7}{3r(r-1)}>0\text{ for }r\geq 8.

(43)

∎

These limits show that our codes have very good encoding rates for their relative distance. More precisely, these codes explicitly satisfy the TVZ bound for
r\geq 8
:

\displaystyle\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\frac{k_{j}}{n_{j}}+\frac{d_{j}}{n_{j}}\geq 1-\frac{2r+3}{3r(r-1)}>1-\frac{1}{r-1}.

(44)

Thus, there exists a good family of triorthogonal codes
\{C_{j}\}
exceeding the TVZ bound on finite Fields
\mathbb{F}_{r^{2}}
for
r\geq 8
. Moreover, it can be verified by direct computations that these codes are also above the GV bound, thereby scaling better than random codes in all regimes. As we will see, such large encoding rates are beneficial when converting to quantum codes. Indeed, the dimension of the quantum code will be
\mathcal{K}\leq k
and the length
\mathcal{N}=n-\mathcal{K}
. Hence, a large encoding rate in the classical code yields the possibility of quantum codes with correspondingly large encoding rates. In fact, a large encoding rate in the classical code will result in a large flexibility to either increase the distance of the quantum code or increase its dimension instead, as desired.

V
Quantum Codes

In this section, we outline how to obtain qudit CSS codes from the previously constructed family of classical triorthogonal codes that support a transversal
CCZ
gate. We first briefly review the basic elements of quantum computation on qudits of dimension
q=2^{m}
,
m\geq 1
, that are needed for constructing error-correcting codes.

The single-qudit Hilbert space is simply

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{q}:=\operatorname{span}\left\{\ket{\alpha}\;|\;\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\right\}.

(45)

The qudit Pauli gates
X^{\beta}
and
Z^{\beta}
(
\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q})
act as

\displaystyle X^{\beta}\ket{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\ket{\alpha+ \beta},

\displaystyle Z^{\beta}\ket{\alpha}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{2}}(\alpha\beta)}\ket{\alpha}.

(46)

Here, we have used the field trace operator
\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{r^{n}}/\mathbb{F}_{r}}(\cdot):\mathbb{F}_{r^{n}}\to\mathbb{F}_{r}
defined by

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{r^{n}}/\mathbb{F}_{r}}(x)

\displaystyle:=x+x^{r}+x^{r^{2}}+\cdots+x^{r^{n-1}}

(47)

to describe these phase gates.
These gates generate the qudit Pauli group
\mathcal{P}\subset U(q)
, whose elements take the form
i^{a}X^{\alpha}Z^{\beta}
where
a\in\{0,1,2,3\}
and
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
The normalizer of the qudit Pauli group is the qudit Clifford group
\mathcal{C}
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}=\left\{U\in U(q)\mid UPU^{\dagger}\in\mathcal{P}\;\text{for all}\;P\in\mathcal{P}\right\}.

(48)

This qudit Clifford group and a non-Clifford gate element form a universal gate set for qudit-based quantum computation. For our construction of quantum codes, we consider the
CCZ
gate, a non-Clifford gate defined by

\displaystyle CCZ\ket{x}\ket{y}\ket{z}=(-1)^{\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{2}}(xyz)}\ket{x}\ket{y}\ket{z}.

(49)

This gate lies in the third level of the Clifford hierarchy, implying a
\ket{CCZ}
state given in
\mathcal{H}_{q}^{\otimes 3}
by

\displaystyle\ket{CCZ}

\displaystyle:=CCZ\ket{+}\ket{+}\ket{+}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{q^{3/2}}\sum_{x,y,z\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}(-1)^{\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{2}}(xyz)}\ket{x}\ket{y}\ket{z},

(50)

where
\ket{+}
is the uniform superposition of basis states. The state
\ket{CCZ}
can be injected using a Clifford circuit via gate teleportation
38
. So, the task of MSD in this paper is to distill this state. The next sections will describe the quantum codes that we will build for this procedure.

We can then discuss how to obtain a quantum code
\mathcal{Q}
with parameters
[[\mathcal{N},\mathcal{K},\mathcal{D}]]_{r^{2}}
from a triorthogonal code
C:[n,k,d]_{r^{2}}
following a standard procedure
48
;
39
.
In short, the
k\times n
generator matrix of a triorthogonal code
C
can be put in the convenient form

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(C)=\begin{pmatrix}\mathbb{1}_{\mathcal{K}}&H_{1}\\
0&H_{0}\end{pmatrix},

(51)

where
\mathcal{K}\leq k
.
Because
C
is triorthogonal, the row vector subspaces,
\mathcal{H}_{0}:=\operatorname{rowspace}(H_{0})
and
\mathcal{H}_{1}:=\operatorname{rowspace}(H_{1})
,
are orthogonal
(see, e.g., Ref.
48
).
Let
\mathcal{H}:=\operatorname{rowspace}(H_{0},H_{1})
, so
\mathcal{H}_{0}\subset\mathcal{H}
.
From the qudit Pauli gates defined in Eq. (
46
) combined with these spaces, we can define the quantum CSS code

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}=\operatorname{CSS}\left(X,\mathcal{H}_{0};Z,\mathcal{H}^{\perp}\right),

(52)

which has
a transversal
CCZ

(
48
, Lemma 2.3)
.

The parameters
\mathcal{N},\mathcal{K}
, and
\mathcal{D}
of this quantum code can be estimated from those of the classical code. Clearly, the length is
\mathcal{N}=n-\mathcal{K}
, and
\mathcal{K}
is the dimension. As a CSS code, its distance is given by
\mathcal{D}=\operatorname{min}(\mathcal{D}_{X},\mathcal{D}_{Z})

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{X}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{min}_{f\in\mathcal{H}\backslash\mathcal{H}_{0}}|f|\geq\operatorname{dist}(\mathcal{H})\geq d-\mathcal{K},

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{Z}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{min}_{f\in\mathcal{H}^{\perp}_{0}\backslash\mathcal{H}^{\perp}}|f|\geq\operatorname{dist}(\mathcal{H}^{\perp}_{0})

(53)

and
d
is the distance of the classical code.
Assuming
2g-2<\operatorname{deg}G<n
, Eq.
III.2
yields

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{X}\geq n-\operatorname{deg}G-\mathcal{K}.

(54)

Along with this, we can lower bound
\mathcal{D}_{Z}
. As explained in Ref.
48
, the code
\mathcal{H}_{0}
can be viewed as an AG code

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{0}=C_{\mathcal{L}}(D^{\prime},G^{\prime}).

(55)

Here the divisor
D^{\prime}=\sum_{i=\mathcal{K}+1}^{n}P_{i}
corresponds to puncturing
\mathcal{K}
positions, and

G^{\prime}=G-\sum_{i=1}^{\mathcal{K}}P_{i}
.
Equivalently,
\mathcal{H}_{0}
is obtained by restricting the Riemann–Roch space
\mathcal{L}(G)
to functions that vanish at all punctured positions, so that
\mathcal{L}(G^{\prime})\subset\mathcal{L}(G)
.
Then, using Eq. (
13
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{Z}\geq\operatorname{dist}(\mathcal{H}^{\perp}_{0})

\displaystyle\geq\operatorname{deg}(G^{\prime})-(2g-2)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{deg}(G)-\mathcal{K}-(2g-2).

(56)

In our case, we will show
\mathcal{D}_{Z}<\mathcal{D}_{X}
, implying that the distance of each of our quantum codes satisfy

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}\geq\operatorname{deg}(G)-\mathcal{K}-(2g-2).

(57)

In particular, we may derive a family of
quantum codes
\mathcal{Q}_{j}
from the triorthogonal codes
C_{j}
constructed in Sec.
IV
.
We summarize our quantum codes below:

\displaystyle=x_{1}\,r^{j}\left((r+1)\left\lfloor\frac{r-2}{3}\right\rfloor-2r\right)+x_{2}\,v(r,j)

VII
State Reduction

In Thm.
4
, we have constructed good quantum codes allowing constant-overhead MSD on qudits of dimension
q=r^{2}
, where
r\geq 8
is a power of two.
What remains is to construct a protocol yielding a magic state for an arbitrary
q=2^{n}
. Following Ref.
71
, we treat each
r^{2}
-qudit as two
r
-qudits. Then, for a
r\geq 8
, we use our codes to distill a
\ket{CCZ}_{r^{2}}
state. This state can reduced with a constant-depth Clifford circuit into a
\ket{CCZ}_{r}
. Below, we derive this state reduction procedure and show how any
\ket{CCZ}_{2^{n}}
may be obtained with this procedure.

Lemma 4
.

Given a state
\ket{CCZ}_{r^{2}}
, one can obtain the state
\ket{CCZ}_{r}
using a Clifford circuit (Fig
5
) consisting of
4
single-qudit gates and
3
two-qudit gates.

Proof.

We choose a normal basis
\{\theta,\theta^{r}\}
of
\mathbb{F_{r^{2}}}
over
\mathbb{F}_{r}
, which can always be chosen such that
\theta+\theta^{r}=1
. In this way, we can write

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle=x_{0}\theta+x_{1}\theta^{r},\;y=y_{0}\theta+y_{1}\theta^{r},\text{ and }z=z_{0}\theta+z_{1}\theta^{r}

(59)

where
x_{i},y_{i},z_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{r}
.
Then, we can exploit the composition of field traces

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{r^{2}}/\mathbb{F}_{2}}(xyz)=\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{r}/\mathbb{F}_{2}}\circ\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{r^{2}}/\mathbb{F}_{r}}(xyz).

(60)

to rewrite

\displaystyle\ket{CCZ}_{r^{2}}

\displaystyle\propto\sum_{x,y,z}(-1)^{\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{r}/\mathbb{F}_{2}}\circ\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{r^{2}}/\mathbb{F}_{r}}(xyz)}\ket{x_0,y_0,z_0,}_{r}\ket{x_1,y_1,z_1}_{r}.

(61)

and evaluate only the first trace,

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{r^{2}}/\mathbb{F}_{r}}(xyz)

\displaystyle=\gamma(x_{0}y_{0}z_{0}+x_{1}y_{1}z_{1})+\eta\sum\limits_{i+j+k=1,2}x_{i}y_{j}z_{k}

\displaystyle\text{where }\gamma:=1+\theta^{r+1},\>\eta:=\theta^{r+1}.

(62)

Thus,
while the measurements in Fig.
5
fix the values of
x_{0},y_{0}
, and
z_{0}
, the Clifford corrections cancel all phases proportional to
\eta
, so only the phase
\gamma x_{1}y_{1}z_{1}
remains with the phase
\gamma x_{0}y_{0}z_{0}
now a global one.
These steps produce the state
\ket{CCZ^\gamma}_{r}
.
Then, applying to register
\ket{x_1}_{r}
the Clifford gate
M_{\gamma}
given by

\displaystyle M_{\gamma}\ket{v}_{r}=\ket{\gamma v}_{r}\text{ for }v\in\mathbb{F}_{r},

(63)

we recover the
\ket{CCZ}_{r}
state.
∎

.

We have a few cases to consider.

n

VIII
Conclusions

In this work, we have presented explicit constructions of low-overhead magic state distillation protocols for qudits with dimensions
2^{2m}
with
m\geq 3
using triorthogonal codes that are asymptotically good. In this, we have identified a new family of triorthogonal codes that satisfy the TVZ bound through a lifting method. Of the codes identified, some can be implemented on
d=2^{6}
qudits – the same dimension as in Ref.
48
and smaller than in Ref.
71
. These are attractive design targets for qudit-based platforms like SRF-cavities where single
d\leq 100
qudits have been demonstrated at varying levels of sophistication
18
;
37
including a
[[42,14,6]]_{64}
code.

Looking beyond these results, there are a number of interesting avenues of research. First, it is critical to identify within our family of codes those best suited for current and future qudit-based platforms in more detail. Numerics should be employed to determine the distillable states and regions with their thresholds. Further, continued exploration of the properties of single codes or families with smaller
2^{n}
-dimension along the lines pursued for prime dimensions in
59
would be valuable. While they do not belong to a family of codes, we can construct a
[[25,7,\geq 6]]_{32}
and a
[[7,1,3]]_{8}
codes with transversal CCZ gate, enabling protocols with low space footprint albeit higher overhead which are potentially practical on qudit-based platforms or even on qubit-based platforms with a small constant factor overhead.
Another open question is whether qLDPC codes for qudits
2
;
1
;
4
;
68
can have low overhead. Finally, recent work has suggested that mildly relaxing the requirement for asymptotically good codes can improve the locality of the weight checks
23
.

Canonical divisor
(\eta_{0})

In this section, we compute the canonical divisor
(\eta_{0})
for

\displaystyle\eta_{0}:=\frac{dz_{0}}{z_{0}}

\displaystyle\text{ with }z_{0}:=\prod\limits_{\alpha\in\mathcal{Z}}(x_{0}+\alpha),

(64)

where
Z
are the rational places in
F_{0}=\mathbb{F}_{q}(x_{0})
.
To perform this computation, we observe that for a finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}

\displaystyle\prod_{\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}(x-\alpha)=x^{q}-x

(65)

and that
\mathbb{F}_{r^{2}}\backslash\mathcal{Z}=\mathbb{F}_{r}
. Then, noting that our fields have characteristics 2, we find that

\displaystyle z_{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{x^{r^{2}}_{0}+x_{0}}{x^{r}_{0}+x_{0}}.

(66)

where we have used the fact that
\mathbb{F}_{r^{2}}\backslash\mathcal{Z}=\mathbb{F}_{r}
. Computing the differential
dz_{0}
,

\displaystyle dz_{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{x^{r^{2}}_{0}+x^{r}_{0}}{(x^{r}_{0}+x_{0})^{2}}dx_{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{(x^{r}_{0}+x_{0})^{r}}{(x^{r}_{0}+x_{0})^{2}}dx_{0}

\displaystyle=(x^{r}_{0}+x_{0})^{r-2}dx_{0}

\displaystyle=\prod\limits_{\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{r}}(x_{0}+\alpha)^{r-2}\;dx_{0}.

(67)

So far, we have used only ordinary calculus and recognized terms multiplied by
r
to be trivial. Putting the canonical divisor together,

\displaystyle\eta_{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{dz_{0}}{z_{0}}=\frac{\prod\limits_{\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{r}}(x_{0}+\alpha)^{r-2}}{\prod\limits_{\alpha\in\mathcal{Z}}(x_{0}+\alpha)}dx_{0},

(68)

it is clear now that

•

The places
P_{\alpha}
for
\alpha\in\mathcal{Z}
are simple poles of
\eta_{0}
with multiplicities 1.

•

Each place
Q_{\alpha}
for
\alpha\in\mathbb{F_{r}}
is a zero of
\eta_{0}
, each with multiplicity
r-2
.

•

The place at infinity
Q_{\infty}
has valuation

\displaystyle v_{Q_{\infty}}(\eta_{0})

\displaystyle=(r^{2}-r)-r(r-2)-2

\displaystyle=r-2.

(69)

Thus, the divisor
(\eta_{0})
is

\displaystyle(\eta_{0})

\displaystyle=-D_{0}+(r-2)\sum\limits_{\alpha\in\mathbb{F_{r}}}Q_{\alpha}+(r-2)Q_{\infty}

\displaystyle=-D_{0}+(r-2)\sum\limits_{Q\in\mathcal{V}}Q.

(70)

Alternative Lifting Procedure

In Sec.
IV
, we demonstrate how a base AG code may be lifted repeatedly to generate a family of AG codes explicitly with good parameters.
Here, we outline a similar lifting method, which offers some improvements upon these parameters, although their asymptotic behavior is the same.

This alternative lifting method is similar to that of Ref.

.
As in our earlier method, the definition and the lifting of our first divisor
D_{j}
remain the same.
Instead, we may lift the

\displaystyle G_{j+1}:=\operatorname{Con}_{F_{j+1}/F_{j}}(G_{j})+\Delta(F_{j+1}/F_{j})

\displaystyle\Delta(F_{j+1}/F_{j}):=\sum\limits_{P\in\mathbb{P}(F_{j})}\sum\limits_{P^{\prime}|P}\left\lfloor\frac{d(P^{\prime}|P)}{3}\right\rfloor P^{\prime}

(72)

and

is the different exponent of
P^{\prime}|P

Definition 10

(Different exponent)
.

The different exponent of a place
P^{\prime}|P

is

for
t
forming the complementary module
tO_{P}^{\prime}=C_{P}:=\{x\in F^{\prime}\mid\mathrm{Tr}_{F^{\prime}/F}(xO_{P}^{\prime})\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{P}\}

).

\displaystyle 3\Delta(F_{j+1}/F_{j})\leq\operatorname{Diff}(F_{j+1}/F_{j}),

(73)

(76)

which we may achieve simply by requiring

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{j}<r^{j}\left((r+1)\left\lfloor\frac{r-2}{3}\right\rfloor-2r\right)+v,

(77)

where
v(r,j)
is a positive function given by

\displaystyle v(r,j):=\begin{cases}4r^{\frac{j+1}{2}}\,\text{ if $j$ is odd}\\
2r^{\frac{j}{2}}\left(1+r\right)\,\text{ otherwise}\end{cases}.

(Recall here that
\mathrm{deg}G_{j}=r^{j}\mathrm{deg}G_{0}=r^{j}(r+1)\left\lfloor\frac{r-2}{3}\right\rfloor
, and our genus
g_{j}
is given in terms of our dimension
r
by Eq. (
31
).)
Fixing
r\geq 8
, our strategy is to equivalently choose

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{j}=x_{1}\,r^{j}\left((r+1)\left\lfloor\frac{r-2}{3}\right\rfloor-2r\right)+x_{2}\,v(r,j),

(78)

where
x_{1},x_{2}
are constants such that
0<x_{1}<1
,
0\leq x_{2}\leq 1
and that
\mathcal{K}_{j}<k_{j}
is an integer. Clearly, such constants always exist for
r\geq 8
, e.g.
x_{1}=1/2
and
x_{2}=0
.

Having chosen
\mathcal{K}_{j}
, it follows then that

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{j}

\displaystyle=n_{j}-\mathcal{K}_{j}

\displaystyle=r^{j}\left[r^{2}-r-x_{1}\left((r+1)\left\lfloor\frac{r-2}{3}\right\rfloor-2r\right)\right]

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;\;-x_{2}\,v(r,j)

(79)

and the distance satsifies

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{j}

\displaystyle\geq(1-x_{1})\,r^{j}\,\left((r+1)\left\lfloor\frac{r-2}{3}\right\rfloor-2r\right)

\displaystyle\phantom{xx}+(1-x_{2})\,v(r,j)

(80)

completing the proof. Note that the constants
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
have different roles. For example,
x_{1}\notin\{0,1\}
guarantees that both the distance and the dimension are
\Theta(r^{j+2})
. The constant
x_{2}
, however, may be chosen to either increase the distance or the dimension.

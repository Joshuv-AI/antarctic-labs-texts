Fusion of the musculocutaneous nerve with the median nerve in a Japanese raccoon dog (Nyctereutes procyonoides viverrinus)
Authors: Nonoha MIYAI, Motoharu OISHI, Tetsuhito KIGATA
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Various patterns of the relationship between the musculocutaneous and median nerves have been reported. This is the first report in Canidae, a Japanese raccoon dog, of the musculocutaneous nerve fused into the median nerve in the brachial plexus. The musculocutaneous nerve gave rise to the only branch to the coracobrachialis muscle and fused into the median nerve. The median nerve sent a branch to the biceps brachii muscle, the medial antebrachial cutaneous nerve, and a branch to the brachialis muscle. This case report may provide clues to understanding the phylogeny and embryology of the brachial plexus in Canidae.

The brachial plexus gives rise to nerves innervating the extrinsic and intrinsic muscles of the forelimb. The musculocutaneous nerve innervates the flexor muscles in the upper arm and continues as the medial antebrachial cutaneous nerve. The median nerve innervates the flexor muscles in the forearm. Humans exhibit variations in the relationship between the musculocutaneous and median nerves. These variations are categorized as no communication, one communication, two or more communications, and fusion of the musculocutaneous nerve with the median nerve [2]. Absence of the musculocutaneous nerve has also been reported in humans [2, 7, 8].

Variations of the musculocutaneous and median nerves of the brachial plexus are also described in Canidae. For example, no communicating branch and one communicating branch between the musculocutaneous and median nerves were detected in a Japanese raccoon dog [6]. In addition, as a rare case, two communications between the musculocutaneous and median nerves have been reported in a mixed-breed dog [4]. However, fusion of the musculocutaneous nerve with the median nerve has not been reported in Canidae. The aim of the present study was to demonstrate, for the first time, fusion of the musculocutaneous nerve with the median nerve in a Japanese raccoon dog.

Roadkill specimen of a male Japanese raccoon dog was collected from Toyama Prefecture, Japan, and stored in a freezer. The frozen specimen was thawed and immersed in 10% formalin. The procedures of the dissection were similar to those in our previous study [6]. The brachial plexus was observed by macroscopy and by fascicular analysis, involving incision of the epineurium and tracing of the fasciculi from the roots to the terminal branches under a surgical microscope (OME-1000; Olympus Co., Tokyo, Japan). The course and branches to the flexor muscles in the upper arm of the median nerve were identified. The photographs were taken with a digital camera (Nikon D5500; Nikon Co., Tokyo, Japan), and their color, sharpness, brightness, and resolution were adjusted with Adobe Photoshop (version 27.4.0; Adobe Systems, San Jose, CA, USA). The schematic diagrams were drawn with Adobe Illustrator (version 30.2.1; Adobe). In the present study, the nomenclature used for the nerves was the same as that used in our previous study [6], except for the origin and branches to the flexor muscles in the upper arm of the median nerve that were identified in accordance with the findings reported by Guerri-Guttenberg and Ingolotti [2].

The fusion of the musculocutaneous nerve with the median nerve was found in one out of 22 plexuses (11 right and 11 left sides) from the 11 raccoon dogs. The brachial plexus of this raccoon dog consisted of the C6–T2 in the right and left sides. Except for the musculocutaneous and median nerves, the morphological features on macroscopy and the fascicular analysis of the nerves arising from the brachial plexus were normal in accordance with our previous study [6]. The left brachial plexus in this case was also normal. In the present case, the musculocutaneous and median nerves were fused, and the median nerve gave rise to the branches to the biceps brachii and brachialis muscles and the medial antebrachial cutaneous nerve. Generally, the branches to the biceps brachii and brachialis muscles and the medial antebrachial cutaneous nerve arise from the musculocutaneous nerve. Thus, in the present case, the median nerve innervated part of the territory of the musculocutaneous nerve. Though this is the first study of the musculocutaneous nerve fused into the median nerve in Canidae, similar cases have been reported in humans [1, 2]. In the classification by Guerri-Guttenberg and Ingolotti [2], the pattern showing that the musculocutaneous nerve joins with the median nerve after the musculocutaneous nerve arose from the lateral cord is defined as ‘the musculocutaneous nerve fuses with the median nerve’. In the Japanese raccoon dog in the present case, the musculocutaneous nerve arose from the common trunk with the cranial pectoral nerve, similar to the typical pattern, and the musculocutaneous nerve joined with the median nerve. Thus, this case is a similar variation to the ‘musculocutaneous nerve fuses with the median nerve’ in the study by Guerri-Guttenberg and Ingolotti [2]. However, comparison of the morphology of the musculocutaneous and median nerves between raccoon dogs and humans is difficult because the trunks, divisions, and cords are indistinct in Canidae. Therefore, more investigations of variations of the brachial plexus in Canidae are needed to classify the relationships between the musculocutaneous and median nerves in Canidae accurately.

According to human embryological studies, the musculocutaneous nerve is derived from the median nerve [3]. In addition, the musculocutaneous nerve is absent in lower vertebrates, such as amphibians, reptiles, and birds [5]. Therefore, the absence of the musculocutaneous nerve in humans is considered to be an ontogenetic remnant [5]. In the present case, the branches to the flexor muscles of the upper arm and the medial antebrachial cutaneous nerve consisted of C6–8 on the fascicular analysis. This is the same composition of the branches to these muscles in the typical case having an independent musculocutaneous nerve [6]. The branches to the muscles innervated by the median nerve, except for the flexor muscles in the upper arm, did not include the C6 fasciculus in the present case. This characteristic is also the same as the typical pattern in the Japanese raccoon dog [6]. Thus, the compositions of the branches to the muscles innervated by the musculocutaneous and median nerves in the present case were similar to normal pattern of these nerves. Therefore, similar to the human case lacking the musculocutaneous nerve, the musculocutaneous nerve fused into the median nerve in the present case may occur due to the lack of separation between the musculocutaneous and median nerves during the embryological process.

This is the first report of the musculocutaneous nerve fused into the median nerve in a Canidae. The findings from this case report are expected to provide clues for various fields related to the study of brachial plexus morphology, such as phylogeny of the brachial plexus and developmental process of the brachial plexus in Canidae.

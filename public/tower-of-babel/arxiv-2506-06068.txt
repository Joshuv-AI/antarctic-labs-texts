Quark-universal
U

Abstract
If the quarks or leptons are charged under a new U ⁡ ( 1 ) U(1) gauge symmetry, then besides a Z ′ Z^{\prime} boson there must exist at least one new boson whose decay products include Standard Model particles. In the case of a minimal symmetry breaking sector, that new boson is a scalar ϕ \phi that couples to the Z ′ Z^{\prime} boson as well as to the new fermions required to cancel the U ⁡ ( 1 ) U(1) gauge anomalies. The scalar may be produced at the Large Hadron Collider (LHC) in association with a Z ′ Z^{\prime} boson, or through Z ′ Z^{\prime} boson fusion, while its decays are typically into four jets or two photons. We analyze in detail the case where the Z ′ Z^{\prime} boson is leptophobic, and all the quarks have the same charge under the new U ⁡ ( 1 ) U(1) . If ϕ \phi mixes with the Standard Model Higgs boson, then the new scalar can also be produced via gluon fusion, and the discovery mode is likely to be a diphoton resonance.

Introduction

In the Standard Model (SM), the Higgs field plays a central role as
the source of electroweak symmetry breaking via its vacuum expectation
value (vev), generating the masses of the electroweak gauge bosons and
the charged SM fermions. In
U(1)
gauge extensions
of the SM, such as gauging a linear combination of baryon and lepton global
number
[

,
3
,

,

,

,

fermions to cancel the gauge anomalies (“anomalons”). In addition, introducing a scalar field
\Phi
that spontaneously breaks the
U(1)
gauge symmetry affords a renormalizable description to generate the
Z^{\prime}
mass as well as
anomalon masses, avoiding phenomenological constraints from measurements of the 125 GeV Higgs boson
[
14
,
15
]
.

In this paper we study the properties and Large Hadron Collider (LHC) phenomenology of the new scalar particle (
\phi
, the radial degree of freedom of
\Phi
) responsible for the
Z^{\prime}
mass.
Generically,
\phi
production at the LHC predominantly occurs in association with a
Z^{\prime}
boson, and through
Z^{\prime}
-mediated vector boson fusion.
We focus on the predictions of the minimal renormalizable
U(1)_{B}
model, in which the
Z^{\prime}
boson has flavor-universal couplings to the SM quarks,
and the anomalons are color singlets but carry chiral electroweak and
U(1)_{B}
charges
[
9
,
11
,
12

Since the anomalons are not colored, the new scalar would not be produced through gluon fusion at the LHC except via a model-dependent mixing with the 125 GeV Higgs boson.
This contrasts other work on the Higgs sector of gauged baryon number symmetry
[
17
]
, which relied on a colored anomalon content.

On the decay side, the scalar associated with new
U(1)
chiral symmetries
decays to
Z^{\prime}
pairs as a result of spontaneous symmetry breaking, as
well as to pairs of
U(1)
chiral fermions proportional to Yukawa

anomalons mediate loop-induced decays of
\phi

all gauge bosons under which the anomalons are charged. These loop
interactions are nondecoupling as a result of Higgs low-energy
theorems, and provide a characteristic pattern of Higgs decays in

19

Z\gamma

Z^{\prime}Z
,
ZZ

Mass mixing between
\phi
and the SM Higgs boson leads to an important interplay
between the phenomenology of the Higgs boson and of the new physical scalar, denoted by
\varphi

quark-universal

model, since

power of the current suite of coupling measurements by the ATLAS and
CMS experiments
[
20
,
21
,
22
]
.
Crucially, the imprints of the SM Higgs phenomenology on the new scalar dictate interference effects sensitive to the mixing
angle parameter and the individual chiral symmetries seen by the

The outline of this paper is as follows. In Section

2
Quark-universal
U(1)_{B}
gauge symmetry

A quark-universal
U(1)_{B}
gauge symmetry is a chiral gauge extension
of the Standard Model gauge sector, since the global baryon number
symmetry of the SM is anomalous with respect to
SU(2)_{L}
and

U(1)_{Y}
. Hence, renormalizability of the theory requires adding new
fermions (“anomalons”) to cancel the mixed
SU(2)_{L}^{2}\times U(1)_{B}

and
U(1)_{Y}^{2}\times U(1)_{B}
anomalies, and also must ensure no new
gauge anomalies are introduced
[
24
]
. Furthermore,
we introduce a scalar field
\Phi
that acquires a vacuum expectation
value (vev) to spontaneously break the
U(1)_{B}
symmetry and generate
masses for the
Z^{\prime}
boson and anomalons, which is also a minimal
construction consistent with renormalizability and perturbative
unitarity
[
25
,
26
]
. Hence, a
renormalizable theory for a
U(1)_{B}
gauge symmetry with
quark-universal couplings is the fermion set
[
9
,
11
,
12
]

\displaystyle L_{L}\sim(2,-1/2,-1),\quad E_{L}\sim(1,-1,2),\quad N_{L}\sim(1,0,2),

\displaystyle L_{R}\sim(2,-1/2,2),\quad E_{R}\sim(1,-1,-1),\quad N_{R}\sim(1,0,-1)\ ,

(2.1)

where representations under
SU(2)_{L}\times U(1)_{Y}\times U(1)_{B}
are
given, and we also introduce a complex scalar
\Phi
that is a SM
gauge singlet and carries
U(1)_{B}
charge
z_{\Phi}=+3
. The
N_{L}
and
N_{R}

fields are necessary to cancel the
U(1)_{B}
-gravitational anomaly and

U(1)_{B}^{3}
anomalies
[
10
,
11
,
14
,
15
]
. An additional feature of the
above matter content is that the kinetic mixing between the SM
Z

boson and the
Z^{\prime}
boson is only generated at 1-loop and is
logarithmic below the mass scale of the
anomalons
[
15
]
. A tree-level kinetic mixing can be
forbidden given the theory is embedded in a non-Abelian ultraviolet
completion and remains vanishing until the scale of the anomalon
masses since the fermion content of the model satisfies the trace
condition
[
15
]
. An important distinction from
other studies of gauged baryon number
[
27
,
8
,
17
]
is that our anomalon sector is
uncharged under
SU(3)_{c}
symmetry, which dramatically alters the
collider phenomenology for anomalon production as well as the radial
mode of
\Phi
.

Having established the matter content for the chiral
U(1)_{B}
gauge
symmetry, we now discuss the Lagrangian interactions, beginning with
the scalar field
\Phi
.

2.1
Scalar responsible for
U(1)_{B}
breaking and anomalon masses

A renormalizable realization of the
U(1)_{B}
spontaneous
symmetry breaking is the Lagrangian,

\mathcal{L}=-\frac{1}{4}Z^{\prime}_{\mu\nu}Z^{\prime\mu\nu}+|D_{\mu}\Phi|^{2}-\mu_{\Phi}^{2}|\Phi|^{2}-\lambda_{\Phi}|\Phi|^{4}\ ,

(2.2)

where the covariant derivative is

D_{\mu}=\partial_{\mu}-3i\frac{g_{{}_{B}}}{2}Z^{\prime}_{\mu}~~,

(2.3)

and
\mu_{\Phi}^{2}<0
triggers spontaneous breaking of
U(1)_{B}
via the vev
v^{\prime}=\sqrt{|\mu_{\Phi}^{2}|/\lambda_{\Phi}}
, leaving the radial mode
\Phi=(v^{\prime}+\phi)/\sqrt{2}
(in unitary gauge). The
U(1)_{B}
interactions
for SM quarks are

\mathcal{L}_{q}=\frac{g_{{}_{B}}}{6}Z^{\prime}_{\mu}\sum_{q}\,\overline{q}\gamma^{\mu}q~~,

(2.4)

where
q
represents the six SM quark flavors, and the normalization accounts for the baryon charge (
+1/3
) of the SM quarks.

Discovering the physical real scalar
\phi
at colliders is a
necessary requirement to establish the spontaneous symmetry breaking
nature of the
Z^{\prime}
boson. Moreover, the
\phi
scalar cannot be
too heavy compared to the
Z^{\prime}
boson, since the
\phi
boson
unitarizes longitudinal
Z^{\prime}
scattering
[
26
,
29
]
. The
Z^{\prime}
mass is
M_{Z^{\prime}}=3\frac{g_{{}_{B}}}{2}v^{\prime}
.

The anomalons have the Yukawa Lagrangian

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Yuk}}=\hskip-14.22636pt

\displaystyle-\,y_{L}\bar{L}_{L}\Phi^{*}L_{R}-y_{E}\bar{E}_{L}\Phi E_{R}-y_{N}\bar{N}_{L}\Phi N_{R}

(2.5)

\displaystyle-\,y_{1}\bar{L}_{L}HE_{R}-y_{2}\bar{L}_{R}HE_{L}-y_{3}\bar{L}_{L}\tilde{H}N_{R}-y_{4}\bar{L}_{R}\tilde{H}N_{L}+\mathrm{h.c.}\ ,

where
H
is the complex scalar Higgs doublet of the SM. The first
line of Eq. (
2.5
) reflects the chiral nature of
the anomalons under the
U(1)_{B}
symmetry and the second line reflects
the chiral nature under the SM gauge symmetries. We can use global

U(1)
phase transformations on the fields to ensure that all the
Yukawa couplings in (
2.5
) are real.

Note that Yukawa couplings of anomalons to SM leptons are forbidden by
the
U(1)_{B}
gauge symmetry. Even after
U(1)_{B}
breaking, a remnant

\mathbb{Z}_{3}
symmetry (a consequence of the
U(1)_{B}
charge of
\Phi
,
z_{\Phi}=3
)
still forbids such couplings. In extended models (not
analyzed here) that include scalars with vevs that carry
U(1)_{B}

charge 1 or 2, Yukawa couplings of anomalons to SM leptons would be
allowed (see
[
28
]
for a study of their possible
effects).

Since both
\Phi
and
H
acquire vevs (
v^{\prime}
and
v_{h}\approx 246

GeV, respectively), the anomalon mass eigenstates are admixtures of
the Weyl fermions in Eq. (
2.1
), with two Dirac fermions

E_{1}
and
E_{2}
of electric charge
-1
and two Dirac fermions

and
N_{2}
that are electrically neutral. The
E_{1}
and

are

\displaystyle\hskip-8.5359ptM_{1,\,2}^{2}=\dfrac{1}{4}\left(v_{h}^{2}(y_{1}^{2}+y_{2}^{2})+v^{\prime 2}(y_{L}^{2}+y_{E}^{2})\rule{0.0pt}{11.38109pt}\right)\!\left(1\mp\sqrt{1-\dfrac{4(v_{h}^{2}\,y_{1}y_{2}-v^{\prime 2}\,y_{L}y_{E})^{2}}{\left(v_{h}^{2}(y_{1}^{2}+y_{2}^{2})+v^{\prime 2}(y_{L}^{2}+y_{E}^{2})\rule{0.0pt}{10.243pt}\right)^{2}}}\,\right).

(2.6)

The interaction and mass eigenstates for the charged anomalons are related by separate rotations for the left- and right-handed fields:

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\left(\begin{array}[]{c}L^{\!{}^{-}}_{L}\\
E_{L}\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\cos\theta&\sin\theta\\[5.69054pt]
-\sin\theta&\cos\theta\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}E_{2_{L}}\\
E_{1_{L}}\end{array}\right)~~\,,\\[5.69054pt]
&\left(\begin{array}[]{c}L^{\!{}^{-}}_{R}\\

\displaystyle\begin{split}\tan 2\theta=&\dfrac{2\,v_{h}\,v^{\prime}\,(y_{L}\,y_{2}\,+y_{E}\,y_{1})}{v^{\prime 2}\,(y_{L}^{2}-y_{E}^{2})+v_{h}^{2}\,(y_{1}^{2}-y_{2}^{2})}~~,\\[5.69054pt]

To derive those relations for the charged anomalons, replace the scalars by their vevs in (

\displaystyle\begin{split}y_{L}\pm y_{E}=-\sqrt{2}\frac{(M_{2}\pm M_{1})}{v^{\prime}}\cos(\theta\mp\chi)~~,\\[8.53581pt]

\displaystyle-h_{\text{SM}}^{0}\,\overline{E}_{1}\left(y_{11}^{h_{\text{SM}}}\,E_{1}+y_{12}^{h_{\text{SM}}}\,E_{2}\right)-h_{\text{SM}}^{0}\,\overline{E}_{2}\left(y_{21}^{h_{\text{SM}}}\,E_{1}+y_{22}^{h_{\text{SM}}}\,E_{2}\right)\ .

3
Effects of Higgs mixing

In this section, we consider the phenomenology of the new scalar in
the presence of the Higgs portal coupling
|\Phi|^{2}H^{\dagger}H

mass mixing between the
\phi

boson with a mass near

portal coupling leads to exotic decays such as
h^{0}\to\varphi\varphi

\varphi\to h^{0}h^{0}
, depending on the scalar masses.

We begin with the full scalar potential,

\displaystyle V(\Phi,H)

\displaystyle=\lambda_{\Phi}\left(|\Phi|^{2}-\frac{v^{\prime 2}_{0}}{2}\right)^{2}+\lambda_{H}\left(H^{\dagger}H-\frac{v^{2}_{0}}{2}\right)^{2}+2\lambda_{p}|\Phi|^{2}H^{\dagger}H\ ,

(3.1)

where the mass parameters
v_{0}
and
v^{\prime}_{0}
are real and can be taken
positive, the dimensionless quartic couplings satisfy
0<\lambda_{\Phi},\lambda_{H}\lesssim 1
, and the portal coupling

\lambda_{p}
is a real dimensionless parameter with
|\lambda_{p}|\ll 1
. Minimizing the potential gives the vacuum expectation values
(vevs)
\langle\Phi\rangle=v^{\prime}/\sqrt{2}
and
\langle H\rangle=(0,v_{h})^{\top}/\sqrt{2}
, with

\displaystyle v^{\prime 2}=\lambda_{H}\,\frac{\lambda_{\Phi}\,v^{\prime 2}_{0}-\lambda_{p}\,v_{0}^{2}}{\lambda_{H}\,\lambda_{\Phi}-\lambda_{p}^{2}}~~,

(3.2)

\displaystyle v_{h}^{2}=\lambda_{\Phi}\,\frac{\lambda_{H}\,v_{0}^{2}-\lambda_{p}\,v^{\prime 2}_{0}}{\lambda_{H}\,\lambda_{\Phi}-\lambda_{p}^{2}}~~.

Expanding
V(\Phi,H)
around the vevs gives the scalar
mass terms

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\left(\phi\,,\,h_{\text{SM}}^{0}\rule{0.0pt}{11.09654pt}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}2\lambda_{\Phi}\,v^{\prime 2}&2\lambda_{p}\,v_{h}v^{\prime}\\
2\lambda_{p}\,v_{h}v^{\prime}&2\lambda_{H}\,v_{h}^{2}\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\phi\\
h_{\text{SM}}^{0}\end{array}\right)\ ,

leading to a scalar mixing angle
\alpha_{h}
which satisfies

\displaystyle\tan(2\alpha_{h})=\dfrac{2\lambda_{p}\,v_{h}v^{\prime}}{\lambda_{\Phi}\,v^{\prime 2}-\lambda_{H}\,v_{h}^{2}}\ ,

(3.7)

and defines the scalar mass eigenstates as

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\varphi\\
h^{0}\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\cos\alpha_{h}&\sin\alpha_{h}\\
-\sin\alpha_{h}&\cos\alpha_{h}\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\phi\\
h_{\text{SM}}^{0}\end{array}\right)\ .

The properties of the physical particle
h^{0}
are modified from the SM
predictions by the above mixing. Given the good agreement of the SM
predictions with the increasingly precise measurements of the Higgs
production cross sections and branching fractions performed by the
ATLAS and CMS collaborations, it must be that
|\sin\alpha_{h}|^{2}\ll 1
. Thus, we can expand in
\alpha_{h}
, which simplifies the relations
between physical observables and the vevs. We obtain

\sin\alpha_{h}\approx\dfrac{\lambda_{p}\,v_{h}v^{\prime}}{\lambda_{\Phi}\,v^{\prime 2}-\lambda_{H}\,v_{h}^{2}}~~,

(3.14)

and the scalar physical masses:

\displaystyle M_{\varphi}\simeq\sqrt{\lambda_{\Phi}}\,v^{\prime}\left(1+\dfrac{2\lambda_{p}\,v_{h}}{\lambda_{\Phi}v^{\prime}}\,\sin\alpha_{h}\right)~~,

(3.15)

\displaystyle M_{h}\simeq\sqrt{\lambda_{H}}\,v_{h}\left(1-\dfrac{2\lambda_{p}\,v^{\prime}}{\lambda_{H}v_{h}}\,\sin\alpha_{h}\right)~~.

Because the two scalar states are widely separated in mass, their
effects on observables are factorized on their distinct pole masses.
The complications arising when the mixing angle is large as well as
when the states are nearly degenerate were recently addressed
in
[
40
]
.

In the remainder of this Section, we analyze the effects of the
trilinear scalar interactions, and then the modifications of the Higgs
boson properties.

3.1
Higgs decay
h^{0}\rightarrow\varphi\varphi^{(*)}
or
resonant diHiggs process
\varphi\rightarrow h^{0}h^{0}

The extended Higgs Lagrangian not only mixes
h_{\text{SM}}^{0}
and
\phi
, it also
introduces three-point vertices including combinations of
h^{0}
and

\varphi
. For
M_{h}>M_{\varphi}
, this results at leading order in
3-body decays of the SM-like Higgs into
\varphi
and a pair of SM
particles, whose width is highly dependent on the accessible phase
space. For
M_{h}>2M_{\varphi}
, both scalars are on-shell which
increases the Higgs width by a tree-level decay into a
\varphi
pair.
The
\varphi\varphi h^{0}
and
\varphi h^{0}h^{0}
couplings in the Lagrangian can be written as

-\mu_{\varphi\varphi h}\,\varphi\,\varphi\,h^{0}-\mu_{\varphi hh}\,\varphi\,h^{0}\,h^{0}~~,

(3.16)

where the trilinear couplings have
mass-dimension one. Keeping in mind that there may be large
hierarchies between
\lambda_{p}
,
\lambda_{H}
,
\lambda_{\Phi}
, and
also that the ratio
v_{h}/v^{\prime}
may be small, we expand in
\sin\alpha_{h}
and find

\displaystyle\mu_{\varphi\varphi h}\simeq\lambda_{p}v_{h}-(3\lambda_{\Phi}-2\lambda_{p})v^{\prime}\sin\alpha_{h}\ .

(3.17)

For reference, the coupling

\displaystyle\mu_{\varphi hh}\simeq\lambda_{p}v^{\prime}+(3\lambda-2\lambda_{p})v_{h}\sin\alpha_{h}\ ,

(3.18)

in the small angle approximation.

3.2.2
Decay modes of
\varphi

When
\phi-h_{\text{SM}}^{0}
mixing is nonzero, the interference between
anomalon-induced processes and SM loop-induced processes is
significant, primarily reflecting the underlying chiral symmetry
respected by each sector of loop particles and fundamental spin
statistics. We remark that even though
\phi
is a gauge singlet, the
non-trivial interference effects evident in the
\varphi
decay widths is
a marked departure from the simplistic mixing-angle suppressed SM
Higgs-like phenomenology that is commonly considered.

The new expression for the 1-loop decay of
\varphi
into photons gets
contributions from the
W
and top quark, which are suppressed by the

\sin\alpha_{h}
mixing, but also non-suppressed contributions from
anomalon loops:

\displaystyle\Gamma(\varphi\to\gamma\gamma)

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha^{2}M_{\varphi}^{3}}{256\pi^{3}}\left|\,\cos\alpha_{h}\,\sum_{a=1}^{2}\frac{y_{aa}^{\phi(S)}}{M_{a}}\,F_{1}\!\left(\frac{M_{\varphi}^{2}}{4M_{a}^{2}}\right)+\sin\alpha_{h}\sum_{a=1}^{2}\frac{y_{aa}^{h_{\text{SM}}(S)}}{M_{a}}F_{1}\!\left(\frac{M_{\varphi}^{2}}{4M_{a}^{2}}\right)\right.

\displaystyle\hskip 51.21504pt\left.+\dfrac{\sin\alpha_{h}}{v_{h}}\left[\,F_{V}\!\left(\frac{M_{\varphi}^{2}}{4M_{W}^{2}}\right)+\dfrac{4}{3}\,F_{1}\!\left(\frac{M_{\varphi}^{2}}{4M_{t}^{2}}\right)\right]\right|^{2}~~,

(3.26)

where

y_{aa}^{\phi(S)}
and
y_{aa}^{h_{\text{SM}}(S)}
are given in Eqs. (
2.13
) and (
2.11
). The loop functions are

F_{1}
defined in (
2.25
), and

F_{V}(x)=-2-\frac{3}{x}+\frac{3}{x^{2}}(1-2x)Z(x)~~

(3.27)

with
Z(x)
given in (
2.3
).
In the minimal model, the sum runs over fields
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
. The
strength of the
\gamma\gamma
decay depends sensitively on the
possible cancellation between the SM
W
and
t
loops and the charged
anomalons, mainly governed by the magnitude of
\alpha_{h}
and

v^{\prime}

t
, but destructively interfere with the SM
W

decay. Furthermore, the anomalon contribution is controlled by the
general Yukawa couplings
y^{\phi}
from Eq. (
2.11

angles.
The new 1-loop decays into
Z^{\prime}\gamma
and
Z\gamma

\displaystyle\Gamma(\varphi\to Z^{\prime}\gamma)

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha\,M_{\varphi}^{3}}{128\pi^{4}}\left(1-\frac{M_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}}{M_{\varphi}^{2}}\right)^{\!3}\left|\,\cos\alpha_{h}\sum_{a,b=1}^{2}\left(\frac{-2c^{(Z^{\prime})}_{ab}}{M_{a}}\right)F^{V\gamma}\!\left(M_{\varphi},M_{Z^{\prime}},M_{a},M_{b}\right)\right.

\displaystyle\hskip 119.50148pt\left.+\sin\alpha_{h}\frac{g_{{}_{B}}}{3v_{h}}\,F_{2}\!\left(\frac{4M_{t}^{2}}{M_{\varphi}^{2}},\frac{4M_{t}^{2}}{M_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}}\right)\right|^{2}\,,

(3.28)

and

\displaystyle\Gamma(\varphi\to Z\gamma)

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha\,M_{\varphi}^{3}}{128\pi^{4}}\left(1-\frac{M_{Z}^{2}}{M_{\varphi}^{2}}\right)^{\!3}\left|\,\cos\alpha_{h}\sum_{a,b=1}^{2}\left(\frac{-2c^{(Z)}_{ab}}{M_{a}}\right)F^{V\gamma}\!\left(M_{\varphi},M_{Z},M_{a},M_{b}\right)\right.

\displaystyle\hskip 11.38092pt\left.+\sin\alpha_{h}\frac{g}{v_{h}}\left[\frac{3-8s_{W}^{2}}{3c_{W}}\,F_{2}\!\left(\frac{4M_{t}^{2}}{M_{\varphi}^{2}},\frac{4M_{t}^{2}}{M_{Z}^{2}}\right)\!+\frac{1}{2}F_{V2}\!\left(\frac{4M_{W}^{2}}{M_{\varphi}^{2}},\frac{4M_{W}^{2}}{M_{Z}^{2}}\right)\right]\right|^{2}\,,

\displaystyle F_{V2}(x,y)=c_{W}\left[4\left(3-\frac{s_{W}^{2}}{c_{W}^{2}}\right)I_{2}(x,y)+\left(\left(1+\frac{2}{x}\right)\frac{s_{W}^{2}}{c_{W}^{2}}-\left(5+\frac{2}{x}\right)\right)I_{1}(x,y)\right]\ ,

(3.30)

4
Conclusions

In this work, we have studied the phenomenology of the scalar
\phi
associated with the spontaneous breaking of a
U(1)
gauge symmetry beyond the SM. The generic mechanisms for
\phi
production at hadron colliders, which only require
U(1)
charges for light SM quarks,
are in association with a
Z^{\prime}
boson, or through
Z^{\prime}
boson fusion (see diagrams in Figure
1
). Our results for the cross sections of these processes at the LHC, computed at NLO, are shown in Figure
2
.

If the SM quarks carry charges under the new
U(1)
, then the cancellation of the gauge anomalies typically requires the existence of new fermions (anomalons) with chiral charges under both the SM gauge groups and
U(1)
symmetry.
We emphasize that, like in the SM, the underlying chiral
structure of the
U(1)
symmetry is characterized by a single vev,
and hence the
Z^{\prime}
,
\phi
, and anomalon masses are unlikely to
arbitrarily decouple from each other without violating perturbative unitarity.

The anomalons induce
\phi
decays, at one loop,
into
\gamma\gamma
,
Z^{\prime}\gamma
,
Z\gamma
,
ZZ
, and
WW
, with the
Z
and
W
bosons being on- or off-shell depending on the
\phi
mass.
Another generic decay mode of
\phi
, which occurs at tree level but may be highly phase-space suppressed, is into a pair of
Z^{\prime}
bosons with one or both of them being off-shell depending on the mass ratio
M_{\phi}/M_{Z^{\prime}}
.

In the presence of the portal coupling of
\phi
to the SM Higgs doublet, the mass mixing between
\phi
and
h^{0}_{\rm SM}
gives rise to a physical scalar,
\varphi
, in addition to the already discovered Higgs boson
h^{0}
. This mixing induces
additional
\varphi
decays (for example,
\varphi\to b\bar{b},\tau^{+}\tau^{-}
) and
production mechanisms (gluon fusion, associated
\varphi W
, etc.). In addition, trilinear scalar interactions lead to
\varphi\to h^{0}h^{0(*)}
or

h^{0}\to\varphi\varphi^{(*)}
, depending on the
\varphi
mass.

A particular challenging scenario to disentangle at colliders is that where the
Z^{\prime}
is leptophobic, and the anomalons are heavier than
M_{Z^{\prime}}/2
.
To obtain a quantitative understanding of the various LHC signals in that scenario, we focused on the gauged quark-universal
U(1)_{B}
symmetry, in which all SM quarks have the same coupling to the
Z^{\prime}
. Furthermore, to compare the various scalar decay modes, we considered the minimal set of anomalons in Section
2
, and showed that there is an interesting interplay between the
\phi\to Z^{\prime}Z^{\prime(*)}
,
\gamma\gamma
,
Z^{\prime}\gamma
,
Z\gamma
,
ZZ
and
WW
branching fractions (see Figure
4
).
Various aspects of the richer phenomenology of
\varphi
, in the presence of a sizable
\phi-h^{0}_{\rm SM}
mixing, are analyzed in Section
3
.

Although the anomalons could be difficult to probe directly because of
the electroweak production cross sections and model-dependent decays,
the gauge singlet
\phi
is promising since the
anomalons mediate a nondecoupling diphoton decay. Hence, if the
direct
\phi\to Z^{\prime}Z^{\prime}
decay is kinematically forbidden and
\phi\to Z^{\prime}jj
is then suppressed by three-body phase space, the
\gamma\gamma
and
Z^{\prime}\gamma
decays of
\phi
are attractive discovery
channels. When we include possible mixing with the SM Higgs field, the loop-induced decays gain additional impetus and can
reveal new interference patterns of the underlying Yukawa couplings.

If both a
Z^{\prime}
boson and a
\varphi
scalar will be discovered, there are many possible measurements to be made, including associated
Z^{\prime}\varphi
production and
Z^{\prime}
fusion followed by several
\varphi
decay modes shown in Figures
10
and
11
(or in Figure
4
if the mixing with the SM Higgs is negligible). That set of measurements would elucidate the properties of the bosons and of the anomalons, as well as the new gauge structure.

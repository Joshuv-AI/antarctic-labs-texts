Clinical characteristics and survival outcomes of well-responders following definite comprehensive treatment in locally advanced rectal cancer
Authors: Ganbin Li, Xiaoyuan Qiu, Lai Xu, Junyang Lu, Bin Wu, Yi Xiao, Guole Lin
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT

Background

Colorectal cancer is the forth diagnosed and second leading cause of cancer deaths worldwide, the incidence of locally advanced rectal cancer (LARC) ranks the top in general population [1, 2]. The standard of care for LARC involves neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy (NCRT), total mesorectal excision, and adjuvant chemotherapy [2–4]. Patients with LARC could benefit from the standard treatment approach, which has shown substantial improvements in tumor control and local recurrence. This is particularly evident given the widespread adoption of total neoadjuvant therapy (TNT) and immunotherapy [5, 6]. Approximately 15%~30% of LARC patients could achieve pathologic complete response (pCR) after NCRT. The recent trials showed that patients receiving TNT or immune therapy seem to exhibit much higher pCR rate [7–9].

It has been demonstrated that patients achieving pCR have superior disease-free survival (DFS) and overall survival (OS), with reduced risk of tumor recurrence and metastasis [3, 10]. However, there are also studies showed that the excellent tumor local control could not be transformed into survival benefits [11]. Furthermore, patients who exhibit only a small cluster of residual tumor cells after NCRT, usually referred to as near-pCR, experience significantly improved survival prognosis [12, 13]. Consequently, patients who achieve pCR and near-pCR are classified as well-responders to NCRT. Pathologically, using the standards of College of American Pathologists (CAP), the tumor regression grade of well-responders are classified as CAP 0 ~ 1 [14].

In theory, well-responders to NCRT appear to experience relatively better survival outcomes compared to poor-responders. However, limited evidence currently supports this survival advantages, and the rate of distant metastasis in well-responders has been reported to range from 6% to 15%. Therefore, we designed this retrospective study to investigate the clinical characteristics, pathological features, and DFS events among well-responders who undergone NCRT followed by radical surgery.

Materials and methods

Study design

Clinical data of LARC patients admitted to our hospital from January 2017 to January 2022 were retrospectively collected. Patients aged 18 to 75 years, diagnosed with histologically confirmed rectal adenocarcinoma and tumors located within 12 cm from the anal verge as assessed by rectal magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) were included. These patients had clinical stage of cT3-4N0 or cTanyN+. Included patients underwent NCRT followed by laparoscopic radical surgery, achieving tumor regression grade of CAP 0–1. Exclusion criteria encompassed patients with metastatic disease, multiple primary colorectal cancers, organ-combined resections, transanal local excision, or those undergoing palliative surgery.

This study adhered to Good Clinical Practice guidelines and the principles set forth in the Declaration of Helsinki. The study protocol, including all amendments, received ethical approval from the Ethics Committee of Peking Union Medical College Hospital (Approval No. JS-1296). Written informed consent was obtained from all participants prior to their inclusion in the study.

Radiotherapy and chemotherapy

All patients underwent NCRT. Baseline assessments include digital rectal examination, CEA and CA19-9, chest-abdomen-pelvis contrast-enhanced computed tomography, and rectal MRI. For LARC patients, long-course radiotherapy was administered with a total dose ranging from 45 to 50.4 Gy delivered in 25 fractions. Concurrent chemotherapy was given in three-week cycles, totaling three cycles, with initial cycles overlapping with radiotherapy. Chemotherapy regimens include single-agent capecitabine (825 mg/m2) administered twice daily from days 1 to 14, or capecitabine combined with oxaliplatin (infusion, 85–100 mg/m2).

Surgery

Following completion of chemoradiation, reassessment prior to surgery include monitoring CEA and CA19-9, chest-abdomen-pelvis contrast-enhanced computed tomography, and rectal MRI to fully evaluate the treatment response and general condition of patients. Surgery was scheduled 8 to 10 weeks after the completion of radiotherapy. All patients underwent laparoscopic radical surgery, specifically low anterior resection, abdominal perineal resection, or Hartmann’s procedure.

Tumor regression analysis

All surgical specimens underwent pathological examination following standard protocols and were documented in formatted reports. Tumor regression grade was assessed by CAP standards: CAP 0 indicated no viable tumor cells, CAP 1 indicated single cells or rare small cluster of tumor cells. CAP 2 indicated residual tumors with evident tumor regression but more than single cells or rare small groups. CAP 3 indicated extensive residual tumor cells without tumor regression. Patients graded as CAP 0 and 1 were classified as well-responders to NCRT.

Outcomes

The primary endpoint was DFS, defined as the time interval from surgery to the earliest occurrence of distant metastasis, local recurrence, or death from any cause. Secondary endpoints included OS, defined as the time interval from surgery to death from any cause. Additionally, distant metastasis and recurrence were also reported in this study.

Follow-up

Patients underwent scheduled five-year post-operative follow-up. This include CEA and CA19-9, fecal occult blood tests, abdominal ultrasonography, and chest X-rays. Evaluations were conducted every 3 months during the first two years, followed by semi-annual assessments for a total of 5 years. Chest-abdomen-pelvis computed tomography and colonoscopy were recommended annually.

Statistical analysis

Data processing and analysis were conducted using R version 4.3.0 (2023-04-21) and Z stats software (www.medsta.cn/software). Continuous variables were presented as mean (standard deviation, SD) or median (interquartile range, IQR), categorical variables were described as frequencies and percentages. Between-group comparisons were performed using t-tests, Wilcoxon rank-sum tests, chi-square tests, or Fisher’s exact tests as appropriate. The probabilities of DFS and OS were estimated using the Kaplan-Meier method. Cox regression analysis was used to evaluate the risk factors effecting DFS. A P value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Baseline characteristics and pathological features

A total of 1004 patients with LARC were initially identified from our database, with 512 well-responders finally included (Fig. 1). During the follow-up period, 42 patients (8.2%) experienced distant metastasis or tumor local recurrence. Based on the occurrence of DFS events, patients were categorized into the non-DFS (42 patients, 8.2%) and DFS group (470 patients, 91.8%).

Among the 512 enrolled patients, the mean age was 59.1 ± 10.6 years, and 336 patients (65.6%) were female. Patients in the non-DFS group had advanced mrT4 stage than that in the DFS group (33.3% vs. 18.1%, P = 0.017). Pathological features between the non-DFS and DFS groups were also compared. Compared to the DFS group, patients in the non-DFS group had much advanced ypT4 stage (56.2% vs. 23.8%, P < 0.001) and ypN + stage (23.8% vs. 9.4%, P = 0.014). Analysis of non-DFS events

During the follow-up period, 42 patients (8.2%) experienced non-DFS events. The rates of distant metastasis and local recurrence were 7.0% (36 out of 512 patients) and 0.8% (4 out of 512 patients) in well-responders, respectively. The most common sites of metastasis were the lung (13 patients, 2.5%) and liver (9 patients, 1.8%), with 8 patients (1.6%) experiencing metachronous metastasis in multiple organs.

Among the 36 patients with metastasis, 21 (58.3%) developed distant metastasis within one year after radical surgery, and 10 (27.8%) experienced metastasis between one to two years post-surgery. Four patients (0.8%) had tumor local recurrence, with 3 occurring at the anastomosis site and 1 in pelvic tissue (Table 3). More detailed information on non-DFS events is available in Fig. 2.

DFS and OS in general population

The follow-up was end up to May 2024, encompassing all patients with a median follow-up duration of 36 (18 to 53) months. In the overall cohort, the 1y, 3y, and 5y DFS were 94.6%, 90.1%, and 88.8%, respectively. The corresponding OS were 99.6%, 97.4%, and 96.8%, respectively (Fig. 3A and B).

Further analysis revealed that patients who experienced metastasis or recurrence within one year after surgery had significantly better OS compared to those later than one year (84.2% vs. 47.1%, HR 4.49, 95% CI 1.32–15.26, P = 0.029) (Fig. 3C). There were no significant differences in OS among patients with lung, liver, or multi-organ metastasis (data not shown).

Sub-group analysis

Further sub-group analyses were conducted based on mrT stage (T3 vs. T4), mrN stage (N0 vs. N+), tumor distance from the anal verge (< 5.8 cm vs. ≥5.8 cm), tumor vertical diameter (< 41 mm vs. ≥41 mm), MRF (positive vs. negative), and EMVI (positive vs. negative). The results revealed that patients with mrT4 (82.1% vs. 92.8%, HR 2.48, 95% CI 1.15–5.35, P = 0.017), positive MRF (84.0% vs. 93.2%, HR 2.15, 95% CI 1.13–4.11, P = 0.029), positive EMVI (83.4% vs. 93.8%, HR 2.70, 95% CI 1.43–5.12, P = 0.006) had relatively poorer 3y DFS, compared to those with mrT3, negative MRF and EMVI, respectively (Fig. 3D–I).

Cox-regression analysis

In the uni-variate analysis, we showed that patients with positive MRF (HR 2.13, 95% CI 1.16–3.90, P = 0.015), positive EMVI (HR 2.64, 95% CI 1.42–4.88, P = 0.002), no anal-sphincter sparing surgery (HR 2.09, 95% CI 1.07–4.07, P = 0.032), ypT3-4 (HR 7.48, 95% CI 3.52–15.89, P < 0.001), ypN1-2 (HR 2.80, 95% CI 1.37–5.68, P = 0.005), and tumor deposits (HR 4.72, 95% CI 1.98–11.24, P < 0.001) had significantly inferior 3y DFS compared to those not (Table 4). Cox regression analysis further identified ypT3-4 as an independent risk factor associated with poorer DFS in well-responders (HR 5.10, 95% CI 2.22–11.67, P < 0.001) (Fig. 4).

Discussion

Tumor response to NCRT is considered to be positively correlated to survival outcomes in LARC patients [15]. The better the tumor response, the better the DFS and OS [15, 16]. pCR is a critical indicator of complete tumor regression, characterized by the absence of viable tumor cells. Patients who achieve pCR would have a significantly superior DFS and OS, along with lower rate of distant metastasis, compared to those non-pCR patients [3, 4, 17]. Additionally, there is a trend indicating an increasing pCR rate after the adoption of TNT and immune therapy [5, 7, 9].

Not all patients could achieve pCR, those with near-pCR also have favorable prognosis [11, 13, 14]. The tumor regression degree of well-responders are CAP 0 and 1, referred to as a combination of pCR and near-pCR [14]. Generally, well-responders are expected to exhibit better DFS and low risk of metastasis. However, despite these favorable features, there are still instances of non-DFS events occurred in well-responders. This study mainly described the characteristics and prognosis of well-responders, particularly those who experience tumor recurrence or metastasis.

Distant metastasis represents a major life-threatening challenge impacting survival outcomes in LARC patients. The rate of distant metastasis is reported to range from 15% to 30% in the general population [18]. Several studies have showed that patients achieving pCR have significantly lower rates of tumor recurrence and distant metastasis compared to those who do not achieve pCR [3, 19, 20]. In line with previous studies, our study also revealed that well-responders have superior survival outcomes, with overall incidence of non-DFS events at 8.2%. Specifically, the rates of metastasis and recurrence were 7.2% and 0.8%, respectively.

In well-responders, we observed that the majority of non-DFS events occurred within one year after definitive surgery. Interestingly, patients with LARC who experience tumor recurrence/metastasis within 1 year after radical surgery following nCRT demonstrate superior OS compared to those with recurrence beyond 1 year. This clinically observed phenomenon may be partially attributed to differences in salvage treatment intensity, early recurrent patients more frequently receive multimodal therapy (systemic chemotherapy, targeted agents, and curative-intent metastasectomy), whereas late recurrent patients undergo less aggressive intervention. Additionally, biological heterogeneity might contribute—early recurrence could involve treatment-sensitive micrometastases responsive to adjuvant therapies, while late recurrence may arise from dormant, chemo-resistant clones. However, the specific underlying mechanisms for this clinically observed phenomenon remain unclear and represent a key focus for future research.

While traditional understanding identifies the liver as the primary site of distant metastasis in rectal cancer, our research team observed a distinct pattern in two cohorts of LARC patients treated with nCRT. Both the prior randomized trial and current retrospective study noted a higher proportion of pulmonary metastases compared to hepatic involvement at first recurrence. This reproducible trend in institutional datasets requires validation in larger, multi-center cohorts. Potential contributing factors include differential efficacy of neoadjuvant therapy—with preoperative treatment more effectively targeting liver metastases via the portal venous system while pulmonary metastases (disseminated via systemic circulation) may evade therapeutic effects—and enhanced detection sensitivity due to advances in thoracic imaging and interventional techniques improving identification of subcentimeter pulmonary nodules. However, the underlying mechanisms for this phenomenon remain unclear.

Among the 512 well-responders included in the study, only 10 deaths occurred due to disease progression or deterioration. The overall 3y-DFS and OS rates were notably high at 88.8% and 96.8%, respectively. Whether achieving DFS or not, well-responders consistently demonstrated superior survival outcomes, particularly in overall prognosis. This underscores that effective and superior tumor local control and better tumor regression after NCRT can be translated into improved survival outcomes following comprehensive treatments.

This study found that patients in the non- DFS group exhibited an advanced cT4 stage and higher rates of positive MRF and EMVI at baseline evaluation. Furthermore, compared to the DFS group, patients in the non-DFS group also showed higher rates of pT3-4 stage, pN + stage, and tumor deposits. Uni- and multi-variate analyses identified these risk factors as having a negative impact on long-term survival outcomes in well-responders after NCRT. Several studies have also identified advanced cT4, positive MRF, and EMVI as risk factors for tumor recurrence and metastasis, recommending these patients for consolidative therapies such as TNT or combined adjuvant chemotherapy [8, 21, 22].

This study also has several limitations. Being a single-center retrospective study, it inevitably faces potential patient selection biases. Moreover, all analyses in this study relied on two key assessments-rectal MRI and tumor regression grade evaluation, whose quality and standards may vary among different centers and physicians. Consequently, larger-scale prospective multicenter studies are needed to enhance the precision and applicability of the conclusions drawn from this research.

Conclusion

Generally, well-responders to NCRT are expected to achieve superior long-term survival outcomes. However, special attention should be given to patients with advanced tumor stages or those exhibiting high-risk factors such as positive MRF or EMVI at baseline assessment, as well as adverse pathological features. For these patients, intensified or consolidative therapies should be considered, despite achieving superior tumor local control and regression from NCRT. More research is needed to refine treatment strategies and improve outcomes for these high-risk patients.

Eigenvalue conditions implying edge-disjoint spanning trees and a forest with constraints
Jin Cai, Bo Zhou

Abstract
Let G G be a nontrivial graph with minimum degree δ \delta and k k an integer with k ≥ 1 k\geq 1 . In the literature, there are eigenvalue conditions that imply G G contains k k edge-disjoint spanning trees. We give eigenvalue conditions that imply G G contains k k edge-disjoint spanning trees and another forest F F with | E ⁡ ( F ) | > δ − 1 δ ​ ( | V ⁡ ( G ) | − 1 ) |E(F)|>\frac{\delta-1}{\delta}(|V(G)|-1) , and if F F is not a spanning tree, then F F has a component with at least δ \delta edges. Keywords: edge-disjoint spanning trees, eigenvalues, fractional packing number, minimum degree

Introduction

In this paper, we consider finite, undirected and simple graphs.
As usual,
K_{n}
denotes a complete graph of order
n
.
For a connected graph

[
9
,
13
,
14

. By definition,

The eigenvalues of a graph are the eigenvalues of

For a graph

of order

be the

with

.
\lambda_{1}(G)

Seymour proposed the following problem (in private communication to Cioabǎ) relating

and eigenvalues of

.

Problem 1.1
.

[
3
]

Let
G
be a nontrivial graph. Determine the relationship between
\tau(G)
and eigenvalues of
G
.

Motivated by Problem

, Cioabǎ and Wong
[

]
established the first a few results on Problem

. They
proposed a conjecture: Let

be an integer with

and

be a
d
-regular graph with

. If
\lambda_{2}(G)\leq d-\frac{2k-1}{d+1}
, then

. This was then generalized by
Liu, Hong, Gu and Lai
[
15
]
claiming that it holds for a graph

with minimum degree
\delta\geq 2k
, which was confirmed by Gu et al.
[
8
]
. Further results may be found in
[
2
]
and references therein.
We state two typical works.

For positive integers

and

and a nonnegative integer

with

, let

be a graph obtained from disjoint

and

by adding

and

vertices in

. In particular,

.

Theorem 1.1
.

[
4
]

Let
k
be an integer with
k\geq 2
, and let
G
be a connected graph with minimum degree
\delta\geq 2k
and order

. If
\lambda_{1}(G)\geq\lambda_{1}(B_{n,\delta+1}^{k-1})
, then
\tau(G)\geq k

unless

Generalizing sufficient eigenvalue conditions for a

-regular graph

with

in
[

]
, Lui et al.
[

established the following theorem on graphs with fixed minimum degree.

Theorem 1.2
.

[
15
]

Let

be an integer with
k\geq 2
, and let
G
be a graph with minimum degree
\delta\geq 2k
. If
\lambda_{2}(G)<\delta-\frac{2k-1}{\delta+1}
, then

.

To obtain these results, the Tree Packing Theorem due to Tutte
[
21
]
and Nash-Williams
[
16
]

over 60 years ago is needed.
For a graph
G
we denote by

the vertex set and

the edge set.
For vertex disjoint subset

,

denotes the set of edges of

with one end vertex in

and the other end vertex in

, and let

.
For any partition

of

of a nontrivial graph

of partition

is the number parts. The fractional packing number
\nu_{f}(G)
is defined by

\nu_{f}(G)=\min_{|\mathcal{P}|\geq 2}\frac{\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq|\mathcal{P}|}e(V_{i},V_{j})}{|\mathcal{P}|-1}.

Theorem 1.3
.

[
16
,
21
]
[Tree Packing Theorem] For a nontrivial graph
G
and a nonnegative integer

\tau(G)\geq k
if and only if
\nu_{f}(G)\geq k
.

Fan et al.
[
5
]
established an extension of Theorem
1.3
stating that
for a graph
G
of order
n
, if
\nu_{f}(G)=k+\varepsilon
with

, then
\tau(G)\geq k
, and apart from
k
edge-disjoint spanning trees, there is another forest with at least
\varepsilon(n-1)
edges in

. Recently,

[
6
]
gave a structural explanation for the fractional part

.

For a nonnegative integer

and a positive integer

, a graph

is said to have property

if

satisfies the following three conditions:

(a)

,

(b)

with

,

(c)

if

is not a spanning tree, then

has a component with at least

Note that any graph

with minimum degree

. This is obvious

is connected. Suppose that

is a disconnected graph with order

.

Theorem 1.4
.

[
6
]

For positive integers

and

If all the eigenvalues of an

The following sharp upper bound on the spectral radius was obtained by Hong et al.
[
12
]
and Nikiforov
[

]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
G
be a graph on
n
vertices and
m
edges with minimum degree
\delta\geq 1
. Then

\lambda_{1}(G)\leq\frac{\delta-1}{2}+\sqrt{2m-n\delta+\frac{(\delta+1)^{2}}{4}}.

By the well-known Perron-Frobenius theorem, we can easily deduce the following lemma.

Lemma 2.2
.

If
H
is a subgraph of a connected graph
G
, then

\lambda_{1}(H)\leq\lambda_{1}(G)

with equality if and only if
H\cong G
.

Consider two sequences of real numbers:
\eta_{1}\geq\eta_{2}\geq\dots\geq\eta_{n}
and
\mu_{1}\geq\mu_{2}\geq\dots\geq\mu_{m}
with
m<n
. The second sequence is said to interlace the first one whenever

\eta_{i}\geq\mu_{i}\geq\eta_{n-m+i}~for~i=1,2,\dots,m.

The following lemma is the well-known Cauchy Interlacing Theorem, see
[
1
,
7
]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
A
be a real symmetric matrix and
B
be a principal submatrix of
A
. Then the eigenvalues of
B
interlace the eigenvalues of
A
.

Suppose that
G
is a graph and
V(G)
is partitioned as
V_{1}\cup\dots\cup V_{m}
. For
1\leq i<j\leq m
, denote by
A_{ij}
the submatrix of
A(G)
with rows corresponding to vertices in
V_{i}
and columns corresponding to vertices in
V_{j}
. The quotient matrix of
A(G)
with respect to this partition is the matrix
B=(b_{ij})
, where
b_{ij}=\frac{1}{|V_{i}|}\sum_{u\in V_{i}}\sum_{v\in V_{j}}a_{uv}
.

The following lemma is a special case of Corollary 2.3 in
[
11
]
, see also
[
1
,
7
]
.

Lemma 2.4
.

For a graph
G
, if
B
is a quotient matrix of
A(G)
, then the eigenvalues of
B
interlace the eigenvalues of
A(G)
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
G
be a graph with minimum degree
\delta\geq 1
. Let
\emptyset\neq U\subset V(G)
. If
e(U,V\setminus U)\leq\delta-1
, then
|U|\geq\delta+1
.

Proof.

If
|U|\leq\delta
, then

\delta|U|\leq\sum_{u\in U}d_{G}(u)\leq|U|(|U|-1)+e(U,V\setminus U)\leq\delta(|U|-1)+\delta-1=\delta|U|-1,

a contradiction.
∎

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
G
be a disconnected graph on
n
vertices with minimum degree
\delta
, where
n\geq 2\delta+2
.
Then
\lambda_{1}(G)\leq n-\delta-2
with equality if and only if

.

Proof.

Suppose that
G
is a disconnected graph on
n
vertices with minimum degree
\delta

that maximizes the spectral radius. Then, for some component
H
of
G
,

\lambda_{1}(H)=\lambda_{1}(G)\geq\lambda_{1}(K_{\delta+1}\cup K_{n-\delta-1})=\max\{\delta,n-\delta-2\}=n-\delta-2.

So
|V(H)|\geq\lambda_{1}(H)+1\geq n-\delta-1
. As the minimum degree of
G
is
\delta
,
G
consists of two components,

|V(H)|=n-\delta-1
,
G-V(H)\cong K_{\delta+1}
, and so we have by Lemma

that

. Thus
G\cong K_{\delta+1}\cup K_{n-\delta-1}=B_{n,\delta+1}^{0}
.
∎

.

Lemma 3.4
.

For integers

and

with

,
{x\choose 2}+{y\choose 2}\leq{a\choose 2}+{x+y-a\choose 2}
.

Proof.

It is equivalent to the trivial inequality

Lemma 3.5
.

For positive integers
a_{1},\dots,a_{p}
,

\sum_{i=1}^{p}{a_{i}\choose 2}\leq{\sum_{i=1}^{p}a_{i}-p+1\choose 2}.

Proof.

We prove the inequality by induction on
p
. It is trivial if
p=1
.
Suppose that
p\geq 2
, and that

\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}{a_{i}\choose 2}\leq{\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}a_{i}-(p-1)+1\choose 2}
.
Then

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{p}{a_{i}\choose 2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}{a_{i}\choose 2}+{a_{p}\choose 2}

\displaystyle\leq{\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}a_{i}-(p-1)+1\choose 2}+{a_{p}\choose 2}.

Let
a=\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}a_{i}-(p-1)+1
and
b=a_{p}
. Then

{a+b-1\choose 2}-{a\choose 2}-{b\choose 2}=(a-1)(b-1)\geq 0,

so
{a\choose 2}+{b\choose 2}\leq{a+b-1\choose 2}
, implying that

\sum_{i=1}^{p}{a_{i}\choose 2}\leq{\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}a_{i}-(p-1)+1+a_{p}-1\choose 2}={\sum_{i=1}^{p}a_{i}-p+1\choose 2}.

Thus, the desired inequality follows.
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

Let

and

be integers. Let

be a graph obtained by

deleting

.

Proof.

Suppose that
\tau(G)<k+1
. By Theorem

, there exists some partition

of

with

\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq t}e(V_{i},V_{j})\leq(k+1)(p-1)-1.

Then

\displaystyle e(G)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{p}e(G[V_{i}])+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq p}e(V_{i},V_{j})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{p}{|V_{i}|\choose 2}+(k+1)(p-1)-1.

Now, by Lemma
3.5
and letting

\displaystyle e(G)

\displaystyle\leq{n-p+1\choose 2}+(k+1)(p-1)-1

\displaystyle=f(p).

As

As

As

a contradiction.

|V_{i}|\geq\delta+1
, we have
|V_{i}|\geq 2k+3
.
By Lemma
3.6
,
\tau(G[V_{i}])\geq k+1
for
i=1,2
. A spanning tree of
G[V_{1}]
,
a spanning tree of
G[V_{2}]
, and one edge from
V_{1}
to
V_{2}
form a spanning tree of
G
.
Thus,
\tau(G)\geq k
. Apart from the
k
edge-disjoint spanning trees of
G
,
there is a forest
F
of
G
consisting a spanning tree of
G[V_{1}]
and
a spanning tree of
G[V_{2}]
. As
n\geq 2\delta+3
, we have

|E(F)|=|V_{1}|+|V_{2}|-2=n-2>\frac{\delta-1}{\delta}(n-1).

As
e(V_{1},V_{2})=k
,
F
is not a spanning tree. As
|V_{i}|\geq\delta+1
for
i=1,2
, each component of
F
has at least
\min\{|V_{1}|,|V_{2}|\}-1\geq\delta
edges. So
G
has property
P(k,\delta)
, contradicting the assumption again.
∎

By Claim
3.1
, the fact that
e(V_{1},V_{2})=k
, and Lemma
3.4
, we have

e(G)<{|V_{1}|\choose 2}+{|V_{2}|\choose 2}\leq{\delta+1\choose 2}+{n-\delta-1\choose 2}.

So, by Lemma
2.1
, we have

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}(G)

\displaystyle<\frac{\delta-1}{2}+\sqrt{2\left({\delta+1\choose 2}+{n-\delta-1\choose 2}\right)-n\delta+\frac{(\delta+1)^{2}}{4}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\delta-1}{2}+\sqrt{\left(n-\frac{3}{2}\delta-\frac{3}{2}\right)^{2}}

\displaystyle=n-\delta-2.

Evidently,
K_{\delta+1}\cup K_{n-\delta-1}
is a subgraph of
B_{n,\delta+1}^{k-1}
.
From (
1
), we have by Lemma
2.2
that

\lambda_{1}(G)\geq\lambda_{1}(B_{n,\delta+1}^{k-1})\geq\lambda_{1}(K_{\delta+1}\cup K_{n-\delta-1})=n-\delta-2,

which is a contradiction.

Case 2.

p\geq 3
.

Claim 3.2
.

e(G)<{\delta+1\choose 2}+{n-\delta-2\choose 2}+2(k+1)
.

Proof.

Let
r_{i}=e(V_{i},V\setminus V_{i})
for
i=1,\dots,p
, it follows from (
2
) that

\sum_{i=1}^{p}r_{i}<2(k+1)(p-1).

(3)

If there exists at most one part
V_{j}
with
1\leq j\leq p
such that
r_{j}\leq\delta-1
, then

r_{i}\geq\delta
for all
i
with
1\leq i\leq p
and
i\neq j
, so we have

\sum_{i=1}^{p}r_{i}\geq(p-1)\delta\geq 2(k+1)(p-1),

which contradicts (
3
).
Thus, there exist two parts, say
V_{s},V_{t}
such that
r_{s},r_{t}\leq\delta-1
. By Lemma
2.5
,
|V_{s}|,|V_{t}|\geq\delta+1
, so

p\leq n-|V_{s}|-|V_{t}|+2\leq n-2\delta.

By Lemmas
3.5
and
3.4
,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{p}e(V_{i})

\displaystyle\leq{|V_{t}|\choose 2}+\sum_{i=1\atop i\neq t}^{p}{|V_{i}|\choose 2}

\displaystyle\leq{|V_{t}|\choose 2}+{n-|V_{t}|-(p-1)+1\choose 2}

\displaystyle\leq{\delta+1\choose 2}+{n-(p+\delta-1)\choose 2}.

From (
2
), we have

\displaystyle e(G)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{p}e(V_{i})+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq p}e(V_{i},V_{j})

\displaystyle<{\delta+1\choose 2}+{n-(p+\delta-1)\choose 2}+(k+1)(p-1)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}p^{2}+(\delta+k-n+\frac{1}{2})p+\delta^{2}-n\delta+\frac{1}{2}n^{2}+\frac{1}{2}n-k-1.

Let
\psi(p)=\frac{1}{2}p^{2}+(\delta+k-n+\frac{1}{2})p+\delta^{2}-n\delta+\frac{1}{2}n^{2}+\frac{1}{2}n-k-1
.
Since
p\leq n-2\delta
and
\delta\geq 2k+2
, we have

-(\delta+k-n+\frac{1}{2})>n-2\delta\geq p\geq 3,

so

.
Hence Claim
3.2
follows.
∎

By Claim

,
e(G)<{\delta+1\choose 2}+{n-\delta-2)\choose 2}+2(k+1)
.
By Lemma
2.1
, and the facts that
n\geq 2\delta+3
and
\delta\geq 2k+2
, we have

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}(G)

\displaystyle<\frac{\delta-1}{2}+\sqrt{2\left({\delta+1\choose 2}+{n-\delta-2)\choose 2}+2(k+1)\right)-n\delta+\frac{(\delta+1)^{2}}{4}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\delta-1}{2}+\sqrt{n^{2}-(3\delta+5)n+\frac{9}{4}\delta^{2}+\frac{13}{2}\delta+4k+\frac{41}{4}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\delta-1}{2}+\sqrt{\left(n-\frac{3}{2}\delta-\frac{3}{2}\right)^{2}-\left(2n-2\delta-4k-8\right)}

\displaystyle<\frac{\delta-1}{2}+\sqrt{\left(n-\frac{3}{2}\delta-\frac{3}{2}\right)^{2}}

\displaystyle=n-\delta-2.

Similarly as above, we arrive at a contradiction.
∎

4
Second largest eigenvalue condition: Proof of Theorem
1.6

If
G
is a graph with minimum degree
\delta\geq 1
, and
\lambda_{2}(G)<\delta
, then
G
is connected. This follows from the well known fact that minimum degree is a lower bound of the spectral radius of a graph.

Proof of Theorem
1.6
.

By Theorem
1.4
, it suffices to show that
\nu_{f}(G)>k+\frac{\delta-1}{\delta}
.
So for any partition
V_{1},\dots,V_{p}
of
V(G)
, it suffices to show that

\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq p}e(V_{i},V_{j})>\left(k+\frac{\delta-1}{\delta}\right)(p-1).

(4)

Let
r_{i}=e(V_{i},V\setminus V_{i})
for
i=1,\dots,p
. Then (
4
) is equivalent to

\sum_{i=1}^{p}r_{i}>2\left(k+\frac{\delta-1}{\delta}\right)(p-1).

(5)

Assume that
r_{1}\leq\dots\leq r_{p}
.
If
r_{1}\geq 2\left(k+\frac{\delta-1}{\delta}\right)
, then

\sum_{i=1}^{p}r_{i}\geq 2\left(k+\frac{\delta-1}{\delta}\right)p>2\left(k+\frac{\delta-1}{\delta}\right)(p-1),

so (
5
) follows.

Suppose that
r_{1}<2\left(k+\frac{\delta-1}{\delta}\right)
.
Let
h
be the maximal index among
1,\dots,p
such that
r_{h}<2\left(k+\frac{\delta-1}{\delta}\right)
.
Since
2\left(k+\frac{\delta-1}{\delta}\right)<2(k+1)\leq\delta
, we have
r_{i}\leq\delta-1
for any
i\in\{1,\dots,h\}
. Then we have by Lemma
2.5
that
|V_{i}|\geq\delta+1
for any
i\in\{1,\dots,h\}
.

Claim 4.1
.

\sum_{i=1}^{h}r_{i}>2\left(k+\frac{\delta-1}{\delta}\right)(h-1)
.

Proof.

As
\lambda_{2}(G)<\delta
,
G
is connected, so
r_{1}>0
. Claim
4.1
follows trivially if
h=1
.

Suppose that
h\geq 2
. Let
i\in\{2,\dots,h\}
.
Let
B=A(G[V_{1}\cup V_{i}])
. By Lemma
2.3
, we have
\lambda_{2}(B)\leq\lambda_{2}(G)
. Let
|V_{1}|=n_{1}
and
|V_{i}|=n_{i}
.
For
j=1,i
, let
a_{j}=\frac{\sum_{v\in V_{j}}d_{G}(v)-r_{j}}{n_{j}}
.
For
j=1,i
, since
n_{j}\geq\delta+1
and
r_{j}<2\left(k+\frac{\delta-1}{\delta}\right)
, we have the the assumption that

a_{j}\geq\delta-\frac{r_{j}}{n_{j}}>\delta-\frac{2\left(k+\frac{\delta-1}{\delta}\right)}{\delta+1}>\lambda_{2}(G).

Then, for
j=1,i
,

a_{j}-\lambda_{2}(G)>\frac{2\left(k+\frac{\delta-1}{\delta}\right)}{\delta+1}-\frac{r_{j}}{n_{j}},

and so

a_{j}-\lambda_{2}(G)>\frac{2\left(k+\frac{\delta-1}{\delta}\right)}{\delta+1}-\frac{r_{j}}{\delta+1}.

(6)

The quotient matrix
B^{\prime}
of
B
with respect to the partition
V_{1}\cup V_{i}
is

B^{\prime}=\begin{pmatrix}a_{1}&\frac{r}{n_{1}}\\
\\
\frac{r_{i}}{n_{i}}&a_{i}\end{pmatrix},

where
r=e(V_{1},V_{i})
. By Lemma
2.4
, we have

\lambda_{2}(B^{\prime})\leq\lambda_{2}(B)\leq\lambda_{2}(G).

By a direct calculation,

\lambda_{2}(B^{\prime})=\frac{1}{2}\left(a_{1}+a_{i}-\sqrt{(a_{1}-a_{i})^{2}+\frac{4r^{2}}{n_{1}n_{i}}}\right)
, from which we have

r^{2}=\left(a_{1}-\lambda_{2}(B^{\prime})\right)\left(a_{2}-\lambda_{2}(B^{\prime})\right)n_{1}n_{i},

so

r^{2}\geq\left(a_{1}-\lambda_{2}(G)\right)\left(a_{2}-\lambda_{2}(G)\right)(\delta+1)^{2}.

Now, from (
6
), we have

\displaystyle r^{2}

\displaystyle>\left(\frac{2\left(k+\frac{\delta-1}{\delta}\right)}{\delta+1}-\frac{r_{1}}{\delta+1}\right)\left(\frac{2\left(k+\frac{\delta-1}{\delta}\right)}{\delta+1}-\frac{r_{i}}{\delta+1}\right)\left(\delta+1\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\left(2\left(k+\frac{\delta-1}{\delta}\right)-r_{i}\right)^{2},

that is,

e(V_{1},V_{i})+r_{i}>2\left(k+\frac{\delta-1}{\delta}\right).

So

\sum_{i=2}^{h}e(V_{1},V_{i})+\sum_{i=2}^{h}r_{i}>2\left(k+\frac{\delta-1}{\delta}\right)(h-1).

Obviously,
r_{1}\geq\sum_{i=2}^{h}e(V_{1},V_{i})
. It follows that

\sum_{i=1}^{h}r_{i}>2\left(k+\frac{\delta-1}{\delta}\right)(h-1).\qed

By Claim
4.1
,

\sum_{i=1}^{h}r_{i}>2\left(k+\frac{\delta-1}{\delta}\right)(h-1).

Thus

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{p}r_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{h}r_{i}+\sum_{i=h+1}^{p}r_{i}

\displaystyle>2\left(k+\frac{\delta-1}{\delta}\right)(h-1)+2\left(k+\frac{\delta-1}{\delta}\right)(p-h)

\displaystyle=2\left(k+\frac{\delta-1}{\delta}\right)(p-1),

confirming (
5
).
∎

Theorem 4.1

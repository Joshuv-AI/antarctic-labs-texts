Preoperative clinical-imaging nomogram for peritoneal metastasis in gastric cancer: a retrospective cohort study
Authors: Suxia Qi, Hong Zhang, Yongzhi Lu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveTo explore the preoperative predictive factors for peritoneal metastasis in patients with advanced gastric cancer and construct a nomogram prediction model combining clinical and radiomics features.MethodsA total of 342 patients with advanced gastric cancer who were scheduled to undergo surgical treatment were selected and randomly divided into a training set (239 cases) and a validation set (103 cases) at a ratio of 7:3. In the training set, patients were divided into a peritoneal metastasis group and a non-peritoneal metastasis group according to whether peritoneal metastasis was confirmed by postoperative pathology. Preoperative clinical data, laboratory indicators, CT imaging features, and radiomics features of patients were collected. A prediction model was constructed through multivariate logistic regression analysis, and the model’s efficacy was evaluated by receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve, calibration curve, and decision curve analysis (DCA).ResultsAmong the 342 patients with advanced gastric cancer, 96 cases (40.17%) in the training set and 32 cases (31.07%) in the validation set had peritoneal metastasis. Multivariate logistic regression analysis showed that the maximum depth of ascites, the difference in omentum CT value, the longest diameter of the largest peritoneal nodule, tumor volume, tumor texture [Gray-level co-occurrence matrix (GLCM) contrast], and CA125 were independent predictive factors for peritoneal metastasis in advanced gastric cancer (all P < 0.05). The area under of ROC curves of the constructed nomogram model for predicting peritoneal metastasis in advanced gastric cancer were 0.852 (95% CI: 0.793–0.911) and 0.811 (95% CI: 0.712–0.910) in the training set and the validation set, respectively. The calibration curve showed good consistency between the predicted values and the actual observed values, and the P-values of the Hosmer-Lemeshow test were all > 0.05. DCA showed that the model had significant clinical net benefit within the threshold probability range of 0.1–0.8.ConclusionThe nomogram prediction model for peritoneal metastasis in advanced gastric cancer based on preoperative clinical-radiomics features has good predictive efficacy and clinical practicability, which can provide a reference for preoperative individualized risk assessment.

Introduction

Globally, gastric cancer remains a major public health problem threatening human health, with its incidence and mortality rates consistently ranking among the top of malignant tumors [1]. Among various metastasis patterns of gastric cancer, peritoneal metastasis has attracted much attention due to its high prevalence and treatment difficulty, becoming the most common metastasis route and cause of death for patients with advanced gastric cancer [2]. This metastasis pattern not only deprives patients of the chance of radical surgical treatment but also leads to a sharp deterioration of prognosis and a significant shortening of survival time.

Currently, in clinical practice, preoperative enhanced CT scan has become a routine method for evaluating the staging and metastasis status of gastric cancer. However, its ability to identify early and tiny peritoneal metastasis foci is limited, and the diagnostic sensitivity is not satisfactory [3]. This diagnostic limitation directly results in some patients being found to have extensive peritoneal metastasis after undergoing traumatic exploratory laparotomy, which not only causes unnecessary surgical trauma but also delays the optimal timing for systemic therapy [4]. With the in-depth concept of precision medicine, how to accurately predict the risk of peritoneal metastasis at the preoperative stage and thus formulate individualized treatment plans for patients has become an important topic in the current field of gastric cancer research [5]. The rapidly developing radiomics technology in recent years has provided new ideas for solving this problem. This technology can deeply mine and quantify the heterogeneity information of tumors from routine medical images, breaking through the subjective limitations of traditional imaging evaluation and opening up a new way for preoperative precise evaluation [6]. However, most existing studies are limited to the analysis of single-type indicators and lack a systematic integration of clinical data, laboratory tests, traditional imaging features, and radiomics features, making it difficult to meet the urgent clinical need for accurate prediction.

Based on this, this study systematically collected patients’ clinicopathological features, hematological indicators, imaging manifestations, and radiomics data through a retrospective cohort study design, aiming to construct a comprehensive prediction model. By integrating multi-dimensional information, a visual nomogram tool was established to provide a reliable basis for accurate preoperative risk stratification of peritoneal metastasis in advanced gastric cancer, thereby guiding the clinical formulation of more reasonable individualized treatment strategies.

Methods

Study subjects

This study was a retrospective cohort study. Patients with advanced gastric cancer who were scheduled to undergo surgical treatment in our hospital from June 2022 to June 2025 were continuously selected as research subjects.

Inclusion criteria: (1) Gastric cancer was confirmed by preoperative gastroscopic biopsy pathology. (2) Advanced gastric cancer (cT3-T4 stage) was evaluated by preoperative enhanced CT. (3) Patients had not received any anti-tumor treatment, such as neoadjuvant chemotherapy or radiotherapy, before surgery. (4) The clinical, imaging, and laboratory examination data were complete. (5) Informed consent forms were signed by both patients and their families.

Exclusion criteria: (1) Patients had other concomitant malignant tumors. (2) The quality of imaging data was poor or radiomics analysis could not be performed. (3) Patients had severe heart, lung, liver, or kidney insufficiency and could not tolerate surgery. (4) Distant metastasis (non-peritoneal metastasis) was clearly identified before surgery.

Sample size calculation

The sample size calculation followed the guiding principles of clinical prediction model research. The significance level was set as α = 0.05, and the test power was 1-β = 80%. According to the conservative principle of “at least 10 event numbers per variable (EPV)” and assuming that the expected incidence of the main outcome indicator (peritoneal metastasis) was 40%, it was initially estimated that 60–80 events were required when 6–8 prediction variables were included in the model, corresponding to a total sample size of 150–200 cases. On this basis, considering an approximately 15% data missing or exclusion rate, the final total sample size required was determined to be no less than 252 cases. In this study, 342 cases were actually included, and 96 cases of peritoneal metastasis occurred in the training set, meeting the requirement of “the ratio of the number of outcome events to the number of prediction variables > 10:1” in the construction of the prediction model. According to the research purpose, the patients were randomly divided into a training set (239 cases) and a validation set (103 cases) at a ratio of 7:3 for the construction and internal validation of the prediction model.

Baseline data collection

The following baseline data of patients were recorded. (1) Demographic Data and Clinical Characteristics: Gender, age, body mass index (BMI), smoking history, drinking history, tumor location, tumor differentiation degree, and pathological type. (2) Preoperative Laboratory Indicators: Carcinoembryonic antigen (CEA), carbohydrate antigen 19 − 9 (CA19-9), carbohydrate antigen 72 − 4 (CA72-4), carbohydrate antigen 125 (CA125), neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR), platelet-to-lymphocyte ratio (PLR), albumin, hemoglobin, and lactate dehydrogenase (LDH). (3) Preoperative Imaging Features: Based on preoperative enhanced CT images, two radiologists who were unaware of the grouping independently evaluated and recorded the following indicators: the thickness of the thickest part of the gastric wall, the number of lymph nodes with a short diameter ≥ 8 mm, the maximum depth of ascites, the CT value difference of the greater omentum, the longest diameter of suspicious small nodules with a diameter < 10 mm detected by preoperative enhanced CT (the longest diameter of the largest peritoneal nodule). (4) Radiomics Feature Extraction: Using the PyRadiomics library in Python (v3.8), the volume of interest (VOI) was manually outlined along the tumor boundary on arterial-phase CT images, and omics features including first-order statistics (such as entropy) and texture features (such as gray-level co-occurrence matrix contrast, (GLCM) contrast) were automatically extracted. All images were resampled and gray-level discretized before the extraction process to standardize the data. All arterial-phase CT images were resampled to a uniform voxel size of 1 × 1 × 1 mm³ and discretized with a fixed bin width of 25 Hounsfield units to standardize texture analysis.

Outcome definition

The outcome measure of this study was the presence or absence of peritoneal metastasis in patients with advanced gastric cancer. All enrolled patients underwent planned open radical gastrectomy, and intraoperative exploration for the entire peritoneal cavity was performed by physicians with associate senior titles or above through systematic palpation and direct visual inspection following a standardized protocol. Intraoperative frozen pathological examination was conducted for all suspicious nodules, and the patients were finally grouped according to postoperative pathological findings. The diagnostic criterion for peritoneal metastasis was the confirmation of definite cancer cell infiltration or the presence of cancer cells in peritoneal tissue or peritoneal washing cytology based on postoperative pathological examination (including intraoperative frozen section or postoperative paraffin-embedded section). If pathological examination confirmed peritoneal metastasis, the patient was classified into the peritoneal metastasis group (positive group); if no evidence of peritoneal metastasis was found on pathological examination, the patient was classified into the non‑peritoneal metastasis group (negative group).

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS 26.0 and R language 4.3.1. Continuous variables conforming to the normal distribution were expressed as x̄±s or M (Q₁, Q₃), and categorical variables were expressed as n (%). Patients were divided into a training set (70%) and a validation set (30%) via computer-generated random numbers. Univariate analysis was carried out using the t-test, Mann-Whitney U test, or χ² test, and indicators with P < 0.05 were selected for multivariate logistic regression to further identify the independent influencing factors, and their odds ratios (OR) and 95% confidence intervals (CI) were calculated. The sample size calculation followed the guiding principles of prediction models. The multicollinearity of the model was evaluated by the variance inflation factor (VIF). All variables had VIF < 2, indicating no significant multicollinearity. The nomogram model was constructed using the “rms” package in R language, and the model performance was evaluated by the area under the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve (AUC), calibration curve, concordance index (C-index), and decision curve analysis (DCA). A P value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Comparison of baseline characteristics between the training set and the validation set

A total of 342 patients with advanced gastric cancer were included in this study, with 239 cases in the training set and 103 cases in the validation set. There were no statistically significant differences between the two groups in terms of age, gender, BMI, smoking history, drinking history, tumor location, imaging features (such as the thickness of the thickest part of the gastric wall, the number of lymph nodes with a short diameter ≥ 8 mm, the maximum depth of ascites, the CT value difference of the greater omentum, the longest diameter of the largest peritoneal nodule, tumor volume, average tumor CT value, tumor entropy, and tumor texture), tumor markers (CEA, CA19-9, CA72-4, CA125), inflammatory indicators (NLR, PLR), albumin, hemoglobin, and LDH (P > 0.05) (Table 1).

Multivariate logistic regression analysis of risk factors for peritoneal metastasis in advanced gastric cancer

Variables with statistical significance in the univariate analysis (the maximum depth of ascites, the difference in CT values of the greater omentum, the longest diameter of the largest peritoneal nodule, tumor volume, tumor texture, and CA125) were included in the multivariate logistic regression analysis (Supplemental Table 1). The results showed that the maximum depth of ascites, the difference in CT values of the greater omentum, the longest diameter of the largest peritoneal nodule, tumor volume, tumor texture, and CA125 were all independent predictors of peritoneal metastasis in advanced gastric cancer (all P < 0.05) (Table 3). In the regression model, the tolerance of each variable was > 0.1, the VIF was < 2, the condition index was < 30, and there was no situation where the variance proportion of multiple covariates under the same eigenvalue was > 50%. Therefore, there was no collinearity among the covariates.

Construction of the nomogram prediction model

Based on the six independent predictors determined by the multivariate Logistic regression analysis, a preoperative clinical-radiomics nomogram model for predicting peritoneal metastasis in advanced gastric cancer was constructed (Fig. 1). This model assigns scores to each factor and calculates the total score to predict the risk of peritoneal metastasis in individual patients. The higher the total score, the higher the probability of peritoneal metastasis.

Evaluation and validation of the nomogram model

In the training set, AUC was 0.852 (95% CI: 0.793–0.911); in the validation set, AUC was 0.811 (95% CI: 0.712–0.910), indicating that the model had good discrimination ability (Fig. 2). The calibration of the model was evaluated by the calibration curve and the Hosmer-Lemeshow goodness-of-fit test. The calibration curve showed good agreement between the predicted probability of the model and the actual observed probability, and the mean absolute errors in the training set and validation set were both 0.121. The results of the Hosmer-Lemeshow test (training set: χ²=6.801, P = 0.558; validation set: χ²=9.211, P = 0.325) were not statistically significant, further suggesting good calibration of the model. At the optimal cut-off value, the sensitivity and specificity of the model were 0.734 and 0.910 in the training set, and 0.688 and 0.907 in the validation set, respectively (Fig. 3).

Subgroup analysis

In the subgroup of patients without definite peritoneal nodules, and the results showed that the AUC of the model in this subgroup still reached 0.789 (95%CI: 0.702–0.876), indicating that the model still has good predictive efficacy for peritoneal micrometastasis even if the individual effect of the peritoneal nodule indicator was excluded.

Decision curve analysis of the nomogram model

DCA was used to evaluate the net benefit of the nomogram model in clinical practice, thereby quantifying its clinical application value. The three curves in the figure represent the net benefit of three strategies, namely, the nomogram model in this study, “all patients are considered to have peritoneal metastasis”, and “all patients are considered not to have peritoneal metastasis”, varying with the threshold probability. The analysis results showed that within a wide and clinically relevant threshold probability range, from 0.1 to 0.8, the net benefit curve provided by using this nomogram for decision-making was always higher than the curves of the two extreme strategies of full treatment and no treatment (Fig. 4). This indicated that within this threshold probability range, using this model to guide clinical decisions, such as more aggressive diagnosis or intervention for high-risk patients predicted by the model, can bring greater clinical net benefits to the patient population compared with uniformly adopting an unselected strategy.

Discussion

This study aimed to construct and validate a nomogram model for predicting the risk of peritoneal metastasis in advanced gastric cancer based on preoperative clinical and radiomics features. The results showed that the maximum depth of ascites, the difference in omental CT value, the longest diameter of the largest peritoneal nodule, tumor volume, tumor texture, and CA125 were independent predictors of peritoneal metastasis. The nomogram model constructed based on these factors demonstrated good predictive performance in both the training set and the validation set, with AUC values of 0.852 and 0.811, respectively. The calibration curve showed good consistency between the predicted risk and the actual risk, and DCA indicated that the model had significant clinical net benefit within a wide range of threshold probabilities.

Multivariate Logistic regression analysis showed that the maximum depth of ascites was an important independent predictor of peritoneal metastasis in advanced gastric cancer. There is a close pathophysiological relationship between the formation of ascites and peritoneal metastasis. After tumor cells shed, they implant and grow in the peritoneal cavity, which can stimulate an increase in the permeability of peritoneal capillaries and block the lymphatic drainage channels, leading to fluid accumulation [7, 8]. Studies have shown that the depth of ascites is positively correlated with the peritoneal cancer index and is an important imaging indicator for evaluating the peritoneal metastasis burden [9]. The results of this study showed that the OR value of the maximum depth of ascites was 1.156, suggesting that for every 1-mm increase, the risk of peritoneal metastasis increased by 15.6%, highlighting the important value of this indicator in preoperative risk assessment.

The difference in omental CT value, as an objective quantitative indicator reflecting omental involvement, showed good predictive value in this study. As an important part of the peritoneal cavity, the greater omentum is often one of the first sites of peritoneal metastasis of gastric cancer [10, 11]. After tumor cells implant in the greater omentum, they can cause an inflammatory reaction in local adipose tissue and fibrous tissue hyperplasia, resulting in an increase in CT value [12, 13]. In this study, the difference between the CT values of intra-omental fat and subcutaneous fat was used for evaluation, which effectively eliminated the influence of individual differences in basic CT values and improved the accuracy of the evaluation [14]. The results showed that OR value was 1.132, indicating its important role in predicting peritoneal metastasis.

The longest diameter of the largest peritoneal nodule included in this study refers to the indicator of suspicious small nodules with a diameter < 10 mm detected by preoperative enhanced CT. Such nodules lack specificity and cannot be used alone to confirm peritoneal metastasis, and their longest diameter can directly reflect the size of suspicious micro-lesions of peritoneal metastasis, which is an important intuitive indicator for evaluating early peritoneal metastasis [15]. As the diameter of the nodule increases, its blood supply demand increases, neovascularization becomes active, and the ability of tumor cells to secrete various matrix-degrading enzymes is enhanced, further promoting peritoneal invasion and metastasis [16, 17]. The results of this study showed that the OR value of this indicator reached 1.278, suggesting its important position in risk assessment. Tumor volume, as a comprehensive indicator for evaluating tumor burden, is closely related to the risk of peritoneal metastasis. Of note, although cT stage—particularly T4a—is widely recognized as a strong predictor of peritoneal metastasis, in our univariate analysis it did not reach statistical significance (P = 0.077), despite a numerical trend toward higher metastasis rates in cT4 patients. Nevertheless, its biological information is indirectly reflected in our model through more granular imaging quantitative features, such as the maximum thickness of the gastric wall. Correlation analysis revealed a strong positive association between gastric wall thickness and cT stage (r = 0.78, P < 0.001), indicating that this variable effectively captures the depth of local tumor invasion. Additionally, substantial multicollinearity was observed between cT4 stage and tumor volume (VIF > 5), further supporting the decision to exclude cT stage from the final nomogram to preserve model stability. This approach ensures model parsimony while still incorporating the contribution of local tumor invasion depth to the prediction of peritoneal metastasis [18]. A larger tumor volume often means richer blood supply, a higher probability of tumor cell shedding, and stronger invasive ability [19, 20]. In this study, tumor volume was evaluated through accurate imaging measurement, and its OR value was found to be 1.028, further confirming its value in predicting peritoneal metastasis.

The tumor texture (GLCM contrast) among radiomics features reflects the heterogeneity information inside the tumor [21]. GLCM contrast mainly characterizes the degree of local gray-scale change in the image. An increased value indicates a complex internal structure of the tumor and disordered cell arrangement, which may be related to the malignant biological behavior of the tumor [22, 23]. The results of this study showed that the OR value of this indicator was 1.005. Although the value was small, its statistical significance was clear, reflecting the unique advantage of radiomics in revealing the intrinsic characteristics of tumors. CA125, as a classic tumor marker, showed good efficacy in predicting peritoneal metastasis [24]. During peritoneal metastasis, tumor cells can stimulate the proliferation of mesothelial cells and secrete a large amount of CA125, leading to an increase in its serum concentration [25]. The results of this study showed that OR value was 1.050, indicating its important position in risk assessment. Only CA125 showed a statistical difference in this study, while CA19-9 and CA72-4 had no significant differences. The main reason is that this study focuses on the prediction of peritoneal micrometastasis/early metastasis. CA125 is secreted by peritoneal mesothelial cells stimulated by tumor microinvasion and has high sensitivity to early peritoneal involvement; in contrast, CA19-9 and CA72-4 mainly reflect the macroscopic tumor burden of the primary gastric cancer focus. There was no significant difference in the primary focus burden between the two groups of patients in this study, and some patients had CA19-9 gene polymorphism, which together led to no obvious increase in these two indicators.

The nomogram model constructed in this study achieved a quantitative assessment of the peritoneal metastasis risk of individual patients by assigning scores to each independent predictor. The model showed good predictive performance in both the training set and the validation set, with C-index of 0.854 and 0.822, indicating that the model had good discriminatory ability. ROC curve analysis showed that the AUC of the training set was 0.852 and that of the validation set was 0.811, suggesting that the model could effectively distinguish patients with peritoneal metastasis from those without. The calibration curve showed good consistency between the predicted probability of the model and the actually observed probability, and the results of the Hosmer-Lemeshow test further confirmed the calibration of the model. DCA showed that when the threshold probability was in the range of 0.1–0.8, the clinical net benefit of applying this model was significantly higher than that of extreme strategies, indicating its good clinical application value.

This study has several advantages. First, it integrated multi-dimensional data such as clinical features, laboratory indicators, imaging features, and radiomics to construct a comprehensive prediction model. Second, a Retrospective cohort study design was adopted to ensure the quality and integrity of the data. Third, the robustness of the model was proven through strict internal validation. Finally, the visual presentation of the nomogram improved the clinical applicability of the model. However, this study also has some limitations. First, as a single-center study, there may be selection bias. Second, the extraction of radiomics features depends on manual delineation of the region of interest, which may lead to inter-observer differences. In addition, more multi-dimensional biomarkers such as genomics have not been included, and classic risk factors for peritoneal metastasis such as tumor differentiation degree, cT stage, and signet-ring cell carcinoma were not included in the model due to multicollinearity issues, which limits the comprehensiveness of the model to a certain extent. Future studies should expand the sample size through multi-center cooperation, develop automated feature extraction methods, and integrate more omics data to further improve the prediction system. Finally, we acknowledge that non-pathological ascites in reproductive-aged women (e.g., due to ovulation or benign gynecological conditions) may confound our assessment of ascites depth. Due to the retrospective design, we could not obtain menstrual history or gynecological status at the time of CT examination. Thus, the possibility that some ascites cases in female patients were physiological cannot be entirely excluded. This limitation should be considered when interpreting the predictive value of ascites depth, and future prospective studies should include comprehensive gynecological evaluations to better differentiate pathological from physiological ascites.

In conclusion, this study successfully constructed and validated a nomogram model based on preoperative clinical-radiomics features. This model can individually predict the peritoneal metastasis risk of patients with advanced gastric cancer, has good predictive performance and clinical practicability, and can provide an important reference for preoperative treatment decision-making.

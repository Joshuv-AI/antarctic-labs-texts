Abstract
Moran-type iterated function systems (Moran-type IFS or MIFS) are defined by a sequence of iterated function systems, and their basic theoretical framework is established. We define Moran-type attractors and invariant probability measures associated with a sequence of probability weight vectors. Furthermore, separation conditions for MIFS are introduced, and the dimension theory of Moran-type self-similar sets is investigated. Appropriate examples are provided to illustrate and support the definitions and results. Keywords : Moran-type IFS, self-affine, self-similar, open set condition, fractal dimension

Definition 1.1

Let
X\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
be a compact set,
\Phi_{n}=\left\{\phi_{n,j}\right\}_{j=1}^{N_{n}}
with
2\leq N_{n}<+\infty
be a collection of bi-Lipschitz maps from
X
to itself. If there exist constants
0<c_{1,n}\leq c_{2,n}<1
and a metric
\rho(\cdot,\ \cdot)
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
such that

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\phi_{n,j}(X)\subseteq X,\ \ \ \lim\limits_{n\to+\infty}\prod_{j=1}^{n}c_{2,j}=0\\
\ c_{1,n}\rho(x,\ y)\leq\rho(\phi_{n,j}(x),\ \phi_{n,j}(y))\leq{}{c_{2,n}}\rho(x,\ y),\ \forall\ x,\ y\in X\end{array}\right.

(1.1)

whenever
1\leq j\leq N_{n}
and
n\geq 1
. We call the sequence of bi-Lipschitz iterated function systems
\left\{\Phi_{n}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
a Moran-type iterated function system (MIFS in short) on
(X,\ \rho(\cdot,\ \cdot))
.

Remark 1.2

By Definition
1.1
, we see that
\left\{\Phi_{n}\right\}_{n=k}^{\infty}
is also a Moran-type iterated function system for any
k>1
.

Remark 1.3

Let
\left\{\phi_{j}\right\}_{j=1}^{m}
be an IFS in the compact metric space
(X,\ \rho(\cdot,\ \cdot))
as in
[
H
]
. By defining
\phi_{n,j}=\phi_{j}
and
N_{n}=m
for all
n\geq 1
, we see that
\left\{\Phi_{n}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is also a Moran-type iterated function system. Hence, the concept of MIFS is a generalization of IFS in
[
H
]
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, the definitions of attractors and invariant measures are given after some fundamental theorems having been proven. In Section 3, the open set condition, the weak separation condition, the bounded distortion property for iterated function systems are generalized to the case of Moran-type iterated function systems. The relationship between these separation conditions for iterated function systems and their generalizations for Moran-type iterated function systems are also discussed. In Section 4, dimensions of Moran-type self-similar sets are investigated. Finally, some examples are given in Section 5 to explain why we give these definitions. Furthermore, some counter examples are also given to show that our assumptions in theorems are needed.

Theorem 2.1

Let
\left\{\Phi_{n}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
be an MIFS on
(X,\ \rho)
as in Definition
1.1
. We have the following statements.

(i)
The following sets

K_{n}:=\left\{\lim\limits_{k\to+\infty}\phi_{n,j_{n}j_{n+1}\cdots j_{n+k}}(a):\ 1\leq j_{n+k}\leq N_{n+k},\ k\geq 0\right\},\ \forall\ n\geq 1

(2.1)

are well defined compact subsets of
X
and are independent of
a\in X
. Moreover, the above
\left\{K_{n}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is the unique sequence of compact subsets of
X
such that

K_{n}=\bigcup\limits_{j=1}^{N_{n}}\phi_{n,j}(K_{n+1}),\hskip 10.00002pt\forall\ n\geq 1.

(2.2)

(ii)
For any given sequence of probability weights
{\mathcal{P}}_{n}=\left\{p_{n,1},p_{n,2},\cdots,p_{n,N_{n}}\right\}

(
i.e.,
\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N_{n}}p_{n,j}=1
and
p_{n,j}>0
for all
n>0
and
1\leq j\leq N_{n}
)
, there exists a unique sequence of probability measures
\left\{\mu_{n}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
supported on
\left\{K_{n}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
, respectively, such that

\mu_{n}=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N_{n}}p_{n,j}\mu_{n+1}\circ\phi_{n,j}^{-1},\hskip 10.00002ptn\geq 1.

(2.3)

Proof.
(i) We divide the proof of conclusion (i) into three claims.

Claim 1
. Each
K_{n}
is well defined and is independent of
a\in X
,
n=1,\ 2,\ \cdots
.

Proof of Claim 1. For any
a,\ b\in X,\ n>0
and any given sequence
\left\{j_{k}\right\}_{k=n}^{+\infty}
with
1\leq j_{k}\leq N_{k}
, since
(X,\ \rho(\cdot,\ \cdot))
is a compact metric space,
\left\{\phi_{n,j_{n}j_{n+1}\cdots j_{k}}(a)\right\}_{k=n}^{\infty}
contains at least one convergent subsequence. Hence, there is a sequence of positive integers
\{k_{m}\}_{m=1}^{\infty}
and a point
x_{0}\in X
such that

\lim_{m\to\infty}\phi_{n,j_{n}j_{n+1}\cdots j_{k_{m}}}(a)=x_{0}.

(2.4)

For any positive integer
p>k_{1}
, we have
k_{m}\leq p<k_{m+1}
for some
m\geq 1
. The assumption
\phi_{n,j}(X)\subseteq X
implies

\rho(\phi_{n,j_{n}j_{n+1}\cdots j_{k_{m}}}(a),\ \phi_{n,j_{n}j_{n+1}\cdots j_{p}}(b))\leq|\phi_{n,j_{n}j_{n+1}\cdots j_{n_{m}}}(X)|_{\rho}.

By noting
\phi_{n,j_{n}j_{n+1}\cdots j_{k_{m}}}(a),\ \phi_{n,j_{n}j_{n+1}\cdots j_{p}}(b)\in\phi_{n,j_{n}j_{n+1}\cdots j_{k_{m}}}(X)
, we have

\begin{array}[]{rl}&\rho(x_{0},\ \phi_{n,j_{n}j_{n+1}\cdots j_{p}}(b))\\
\leq&\rho(x_{0},\ \phi_{n,j_{n}j_{n+1}\cdots j_{k_{m}}}(a))+\rho(\phi_{n,j_{n}j_{n+1}\cdots j_{k_{m}}}(a),\ \phi_{n,j_{n}j_{n+1}\cdots j_{p}}(b))\\
\leq&\rho(x_{0},\ \phi_{n,j_{n}j_{n+1}\cdots j_{k_{m}}}(a))+|\phi_{n,j_{n}j_{n+1}\cdots j_{k_{m}}}(X)|_{\rho}.\end{array}

The assumption (
1.1
) implies
|\phi_{n,j_{n}j_{n+1}\cdots j_{n_{m}}}(X)|_{\rho}\leq\prod_{j=n}^{n_{m}}c_{2,j}|X|_{\rho}\to 0
as
m\to+\infty
. Combining this inequality and (
2.4
) implies

\lim_{p\to\infty}\rho(x_{0},\ \phi_{n,j_{n}j_{n+1}\cdots j_{p}}(b))=0,

so
\lim\limits_{p\to\infty}\phi_{n,j_{n}j_{n+1}\cdots j_{p}}(b)=x_{0}
for all
b\in X
. Then, the uniqueness of
\lim\limits_{p\to\infty}\phi_{n,j_{n}j_{n+1}\cdots j_{p}}(b)
shows that
\lim\limits_{p\to\infty}\phi_{n,j_{n}j_{n+1}\cdots j_{p}}(a)=x_{0}
and the limit is independent of
a\in X
.
Therefore,
K_{n}
is well defined and independent of
a\in X
for all
n>0

\Sigma_{n}^{\mathbb{N}}(j_{n}j_{n+1}j_{n+2}\cdots j_{k})

\pi_{n}^{-1}(O)=\bigcup_{i}\Sigma_{n}^{\mathbb{N}}(J_{i}),

(2.6)

where the cylinder sets

are disjoint. (

for all

This means that

coincide on any open set of

\Box

3
Definitions of separation conditions

It is well known that, for self-similar IFS, the open set condition (OSC), the strong separation condition (SSC) and the weak separation condition (WSC) are crucial in the investigations on the self-similar sets or self-similar measures. This section is devoted to extend OSC, SSC and WSC for IFSs to MIFSs.

In order to define separation conditions of MIFSs, we use the idea of
[
DW
]
for generalized self-affine IFSs and the idea of
[
LNW
]
for self-conformal IFSs.

Definition 3.1

Let
\left\{\Phi_{n}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
with
\Phi_{n}=\left\{\phi_{n,j}\right\}_{j=1}^{N_{n}}
be an MIFS on
(X,\ \rho)
as in Definition
1.1
.

(i)
We say that the MIFS satisfies the Moran-type open set condition (MOSC in short) if there exists a sequence of open sets
V_{n}\subset X
such that

\phi_{n,j}(V_{n+1})\subseteq V_{n},\ \ \phi_{n,i}(V_{n+1})\cap\phi_{n,j}(V_{n+1})=\emptyset\ (1\leq i\neq j\leq N_{n}),\ \ n\geq 1,

(3.1)

and

\inf\left\{{\mathcal{L}}^{d}(V_{n}):\ n=1,2,\cdots\right\}>0.

(3.2)

(ii)
We say that the MIFS satisfies the Moran-type weak separation condition (MWSC in short) if there exists a sequence of subsets
U_{n}\subseteq X
such that

\inf\left\{{\mathcal{L}}^{d}(U_{n}):\ n=1,2,\cdots\right\}>0,

(3.3)

and

\gamma_{1}:=\sup\limits_{1>b>0}\max\limits_{I\in{\mathcal{I}}_{b}}\#\left\{\phi_{1,J}:\ J\in{\mathcal{I}}_{b},\ \phi_{1,J}(U_{|J|+1})\cap\phi_{1,I}(U_{|I|+1})\neq\emptyset\right\}<+\infty.

(3.4)

(iii)
We say that the MIFS satisfies the Moran-type strong separation condition (MSSC in short) if

\phi_{1,J}(K_{n+1})\cap\phi_{1,I}(K_{n+1})=\emptyset

for any
n>0
and
I\neq J\in{}\Sigma_{1}^{n}
.

Remark 3.2

The “Moran structure condition” in
[
HRWW
]
and other references require that all
V_{n}
in the definition of MOSC are the same, so MOSC is weaker than the “Moran structure condition” in
[
HRWW
]
and other references. Hence, our Theorem

and Theorem

in Section 4 are nontrivial generalizations of conclusions in
[
HRWW
]
and other references. Some other remarks and examples are given in Section 5 to explain why we introduce the above definitions.

The idea of
[

, (2.3)]
for generalized self-affine IFSs and the BDP defined in
[
LNW
]
for self-conformal IFSs are important for investigating attractors. We extend these ideas to introduce the so-called MWHP and MBDP for MIFSs in the following.

Definition 3.3

Let
\left\{\Phi_{n}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
be an MIFS as defined in Definition
1.1
.
If

\gamma_{2}:=\sup\limits_{1>b>0}\sup\left\{\frac{\rho(\phi_{1,J}(x),\ \phi_{1,J}(y))}{\rho(\phi_{1,I}(x),\ \phi_{1,I}(y))}:\ I,\ J\in{\mathcal{I}}_{b},\ x\neq y\in X\right\}<+\infty,

(3.5)

we say that the MIFS
\left\{\Phi_{n}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
satisfies the Moran-type weak homogenous property (MWHP in short).

If

\gamma_{3}:=\sup\limits_{I\in\Sigma_{1}^{*}}\left\{\frac{R_{I}}{r_{I}}\right\}<+\infty,

(3.6)

we say that the MIFS
\left\{\Phi_{n}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
satisfies the Moran-type bounded distortion property (MBDP in short).

If the metric

\overline{\dim}_{\rm B}K_{1}=\overline{\dim}_{\rm B}K_{n}
and
\dim_{\rm P}K_{1}=\dim_{\rm P}K_{n}
. Therefore, we need only to consider
\underline{\dim}_{\rm B}K_{1}
and
\overline{\dim}_{\rm B}K_{1}
in the following.

(i) For any integer vector
{\bf z}=(z_{1},\cdots,z_{d})^{t}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
and positive number
b>0
, denote the cube
\prod_{j=1}^{d}[z_{j}b|X|,\ (z_{j}+1)b|X|]
by
{\mathcal{C}}_{b,{\bf z}}
. Let

N_{b}=\#\{{\mathcal{C}}_{b,{\bf z}}:\ {\bf z}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d},\ K_{1}\cap{\mathcal{C}}_{b,{\bf z}}\neq\emptyset\}.

It is well known that
\underline{\dim}_{\rm B}K_{1}=\liminf\limits_{b\to 0^{+}}\frac{\ln N_{b}}{-\ln b}
and
\overline{\dim}_{\rm B}K_{1}=\limsup\limits_{b\to 0}\frac{\ln N_{b}}{-\ln b}
.

From the relationship
K_{1}\subseteq\bigcup\limits_{f\in{\mathcal{A}}_{b}}f(X)
, it follows that if
{\mathcal{C}}_{b,{\bf z}}\cap K_{1}\neq\emptyset
, then
{\mathcal{C}}_{b,{\bf z}}
intersects at least one set
f(X)
with
f\in{\mathcal{A}}_{b}
. Consequently, we have

\{{\mathcal{C}}_{b,{\bf z}}:\ {\bf z}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d},\ K_{1}\cap{\mathcal{C}}_{b,{\bf z}}\neq\emptyset\}\subset\bigcup_{f\in{\mathcal{A}}_{b}}\{{\mathcal{C}}_{b,{\bf z}}:\ {\bf z}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d},\ f(X)\cap{\mathcal{C}}_{b,{\bf z}}\neq\emptyset\}.

Secondly, since
|f(X)|\leq b|X|
for any
f\in{\mathcal{A}}_{b}
, we see that
f(X)
intersects no more than
3^{d}
cubes for any
f\in{\mathcal{A}}_{b}
. Hence,
\#\{{\mathcal{C}}_{b,{\bf z}}:\ {\bf z}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d},\ f(X)\cap{\mathcal{C}}_{b,{\bf z}}\neq\emptyset\}\leq 3^{d}
for any
f\in{\mathcal{A}}_{b}
. As a result, we have

N_{b}\leq 3^{d}\#{\mathcal{A}}_{b}.

(4.4)

As
r_{0}=\inf\{r_{n,j}:\ 1\leq j\leq N_{n},\ n\geq 1\}>0
, the MIFS
\{\Phi_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
satisfies the MWHP. Fix a cube
{\mathcal{C}}_{b,{\bf z}}
, from Lemma
3.5
, we see that there is a sub family
\Psi_{b,{\bf z}}\subseteq\{f\in{\mathcal{A}}_{b}:\ f(X)\cap{\mathcal{C}}_{b,{\bf z}}\neq\emptyset\}
such that: (a)
\gamma_{4}\#\Psi_{b,{\bf z}}\geq\#\{f\in{\mathcal{A}}_{b}:\ f(X)\cap{\mathcal{C}}_{b,{\bf z}}{\neq\emptyset}\}
, where
\gamma_{4}
is defined in (
3.7
); (b) Every
f\in\{f\in{\mathcal{A}}_{b}:\ f(X)\cap{\mathcal{C}}_{b,{\bf z}}\neq\emptyset\}
corresponds at least one
g\in\Psi_{b,{\bf z}}
such that
f(X)\cap g(X)\neq\emptyset
. (c)
f(X)\cap h(X)=\emptyset
for any
f\neq h\in\Psi_{b,{\bf z}}
.
Then, the definition of
{\mathcal{A}}_{b}
shows that
\bigcup_{f\in\Psi_{b,{\bf z}}}f(X)
is a disjoint union contained in a union of
3^{d}
cubes since
|f(X)|\leq b|X|
. For any
f=\phi_{1,j_{1}j_{2}\cdots j_{n}}\in{\mathcal{A}}_{b}
, by computing Lebesgue’s measure, we have

{\mathcal{L}}^{d}(f(X))=(r_{1,j_{1}j_{2}\cdots j_{n-1}}r_{n,j_{n}})^{d}{\mathcal{L}}^{d}(X)\geq(br_{n,j_{n}})^{d}{\mathcal{L}}^{d}(X)\geq(br_{0})^{d}{\mathcal{L}}^{d}(X).

Hence, we have

\begin{array}[]{rl}\#\{f\in{\mathcal{A}}_{b}:\ f(X)\cap{\mathcal{C}}_{b,{\bf z}}\neq\emptyset\}\leq&\gamma_{4}\#\Psi_{b,{\bf z}}\\
\leq&\gamma_{4}(br_{0})^{-d}({\mathcal{L}}^{d}(X))^{-1}{\mathcal{L}}^{d}\left(\bigcup\limits_{f\in\Psi_{b,{\bf z}}}f(X)\right)\\
\leq&\gamma_{4}r_{0}^{-d}({\mathcal{L}}^{d}(X))^{-1}(3|X|)^{d}.\end{array}

Therefore,

\ \ \#{\mathcal{A}}_{b}\leq\gamma_{4}r_{0}^{-d}({\mathcal{L}}^{d}(X))^{-1}(3|X|)^{d}N_{b},\hskip 10.00002pt\forall\ b\in(0,\ 1).

Together with (
4.4
), it leads to that there is a constant
c\geq 1
such that

c^{-1}N_{b}\leq\#{\mathcal{A}}_{b}\leq cN_{b},\hskip 10.00002pt\forall\ b\in(0,\ 1).

(4.5)

Hence, we have

\underline{\dim}_{\rm B}K_{1}=\liminf\limits_{b\to 0^{+}}\frac{\ln N_{b}}{-\ln b}=\liminf\limits_{b\to 0^{+}}\frac{\ln\#{\mathcal{A}}_{b}}{-\ln b}\leq\limsup\limits_{b\to 0^{+}}\frac{\ln\#{\mathcal{A}}_{b}}{-\ln b}=\limsup\limits_{b\to 0^{+}}\frac{\ln N_{b}}{-\ln b}=\overline{\dim}_{\rm B}K_{1}.

(ii) If
\inf\{r_{n}:\ n>0\}>0
, the conclusion follows from (i). We turn to the case
\inf\{r_{n}:\ n>0\}=0
in the following.

It is easy to see that (
4.4
) still holds since the inequality is independent of the assumption
r_{0}>0
.

As
r_{n,j}=r_{n}
for any

and

, one see that for any

, there exists

such that
{\mathcal{I}}_{b}=\Sigma_{1}^{n_{b}}
and
r_{1}r_{2}\cdots r_{n_{b}}\leq b<r_{1}r_{2}\cdots r_{n_{b}-1}
. Hence, we have

\frac{\ln\#{\mathcal{A}}_{r_{1}r_{2}\cdots r_{n_{b}}}}{-\ln(r_{1}r_{2}\cdots r_{n_{b}-1})}\geq\frac{\ln\#{\mathcal{A}}_{b}}{-\ln b}\geq\frac{\ln\#{\mathcal{A}}_{r_{1}r_{2}\cdots r_{n_{b}}}}{-\ln(r_{1}r_{2}\cdots r_{n_{b}})}.

In combination with (
4.1
), we have

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\liminf\limits_{b\to 0^{+}}\frac{\ln\#{\mathcal{A}}_{b}}{-\ln b}=\liminf\limits_{n\to+\infty}\frac{\ln\#{\mathcal{A}}_{r_{1}r_{2}\cdots r_{n}}}{-\ln(r_{1}r_{2}\cdots r_{n})},\\
\limsup\limits_{b\to 0^{+}}\frac{\ln\#{\mathcal{A}}_{b}}{-\ln b}=\limsup\limits_{n\to+\infty}\frac{\ln\#{\mathcal{A}}_{r_{1}r_{2}\cdots r_{n}}}{-\ln(r_{1}r_{2}\cdots r_{n}).}\end{array}\right.

(4.6)

As all assumptions in Lemma
3.5
are satisfied, almost the same argument of (
4.5
) shows that there is a constant

such that

c^{-1}N_{r_{1}r_{2}\cdots r_{n}}\leq\#{\mathcal{A}}_{r_{1}r_{2}\cdots r_{n}}\leq cN_{r_{1}r_{2}\cdots r_{n}},\hskip 10.00002pt\forall n\geq 1.

(4.7)

In combination with (
4.4
) and (
4.6
), the above inequality implies

\begin{array}[]{rl}\liminf\limits_{b\to 0^{+}}\frac{\ln N_{b}}{-\ln b}\leq\liminf\limits_{b\to 0^{+}}\frac{\ln\#{\mathcal{A}}_{b}}{-\ln b}\leq\liminf\limits_{n\to+\infty}\frac{\ln\#{\mathcal{A}}_{r_{1}r_{2}\cdots r_{n}}}{-\ln(r_{1}r_{2}\cdots r_{n})}=\liminf\limits_{n\to+\infty}\frac{\ln N_{r_{1}r_{2}\cdots r_{n}}}{-\ln(r_{1}r_{2}\cdots r_{n})}.\end{array}

(4.8)

By a well-known technique (p.
44
,
[
Fa
]
), we have

\liminf\limits_{b\to 0^{+}}\frac{\ln N_{b}}{-\ln b}=\liminf\limits_{n\to+\infty}\frac{\ln N_{2^{-n}}}{n\ln 2}.

For any integer

, there is a unique integer
n_{k}>0
such that

r_{1}r_{2}\cdots r_{n_{k}}\leq 2^{-k}<r_{1}r_{2}\cdots r_{n_{k}-1},\hskip 10.00002ptk>0.

Hence, there exist integers
s_{k}
and
\ t_{k}
with
s_{k}\leq k\leq t_{k}
such that

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}2^{-t_{k}-1}<r_{1}r_{2}\cdots r_{n_{k}}\leq 2^{-t_{k}}\leq 2^{-k},\\
2^{-k}\leq 2^{-s_{k}}\leq r_{1}r_{2}\cdots r_{n_{k}-1}<2^{-s_{k}+1},\end{array}\right.

for all

. Noting that

increases as

\frac{\ln N_{2^{-k}}}{k\ln 2}\geq\frac{\ln N_{2^{-s_{k}}}}{t_{k}\ln 2}.

Due to (
4.1
), it follows that
\lim\limits_{k\to+\infty}\frac{s_{k}}{t_{k}}=1
. Therefore, the property of lower limit of sequences shows

\begin{array}[]{rl}\liminf\limits_{b\to 0^{+}}\frac{\ln N_{b}}{-\ln b}=&\liminf\limits_{n\to+\infty}\frac{\ln N_{2^{-n}}}{n\ln 2}\geq\liminf\limits_{k\to+\infty}\frac{\ln N_{2^{-s_{k}}}}{t_{k}\ln 2}\\
=&\liminf\limits_{k\to+\infty}\frac{\ln N_{2^{-s_{k}}}}{s_{k}\ln 2}\geq\liminf\limits_{k\to+\infty}\frac{\ln N_{r_{1}r_{2}\cdots r_{n_{k}-1}}}{-\ln(r_{1}r_{2}\cdots r_{n_{k}-1})}\\
\geq&\liminf\limits_{n\to+\infty}\frac{\ln N_{r_{1}r_{2}\cdots r_{n}}}{-\ln(r_{1}r_{2}\cdots r_{n})}\geq\liminf\limits_{b\to 0^{+}}\frac{\ln N_{b}}{-\ln b}.\end{array}

(4.9)

Together with (

\underline{\dim}_{B}(K_{1})=\liminf\limits_{b\to 0^{+}}\frac{\ln N_{b}}{-\ln b}=\liminf\limits_{b\to 0^{+}}\frac{\ln\#{\mathcal{A}}_{b}}{-\ln b}=\liminf\limits_{n\to+\infty}\frac{\ln\#{\mathcal{A}}_{r_{1}r_{2}\cdots r_{n}}}{-\ln(r_{1}r_{2}\cdots r_{n})}.

Next, we turn to

.
By (

\limsup\limits_{b\to 0^{+}}\frac{\ln N_{b}}{-\ln b}\leq\limsup\limits_{b\to 0^{+}}\frac{\ln\#{\mathcal{A}}_{b}}{-\ln b}.

(4.10)

In combination (
4.6

Together with (

Theorem 4.2

Proof.

(i)

\begin{array}[]{rl}\nu(\Sigma_{1}^{\mathbb{N}}(j_{1}j_{2}\cdots j_{n}))=&p_{1,j_{1}}p_{2,j_{2}}\cdots p_{n,j_{n}}\\

(4.13)

As a consequence, we get

(4.14)

by using (
4.12

=&+\infty,\end{array}

where the last equality follows from

Hence, the conclusion (ii) is proven.

(iii) The proof is almost the same as the second part of the proof of (i).

Proof.

(4.17)

by using the mass distribution principle. Hence, (
4.15
) holds by using this inequality and (
4.16

(ii) Since our MIFS satisfies the MOSC and the assumption

U=(0,\ 1)^{2}
. It is easy to check that
\phi_{n,i+1+jn}(U)=(\frac{i}{2n},\ \frac{i+1}{2n})\times(\frac{j}{2n},\ \frac{j+1}{2n})
when
0\leq i,\ j\leq n-1
and,
\phi_{n,i+n^{2}+2j}(U)=(\frac{i}{2},\ \frac{i+1}{2})\times(\frac{j}{2},\ \frac{j+1}{2})
when
0\leq i,\ j\leq 1
and
i+j>0
. They are
n^{2}+3
disjoint subsets of
U
. That means that the MIFS satisfies the MOSC defined in Definition
3.1
with open sets
V_{n}=U
.

It is easy to see that
K_{n}=[0,\ 1]^{2}
for all
n\geq 1
. Let
X=[0,\ 1]^{2}
. We see that
\left\{\ \phi_{n,1},\ \cdots,\ \phi_{n,n^{2}+3}\right\}_{n=1}^{+\infty}
is an MIFS on
X
. Take
b_{n}=\frac{1}{2^{n}(n-1)!}
and
I_{n-1}=\underbrace{11\cdots 1}_{n-1}
. From a direct computation, it follows that the contraction ratio of
\phi_{1,I_{n-1}j}
is
\frac{1}{2^{n}n!}
if
1\leq j\leq n^{2}
and
\frac{1}{2^{n}(n-1)!}
if
n^{2}<j\leq n^{2}+3
. This implies that
\phi_{1,I_{n-1}j}
belongs to
{\mathcal{A}}_{b_{n}}
for all
1\leq j\leq n^{2}+3
. Therefore, the MIFS does not satisfy the MWHP since the supremum in (
3.5
) is
+\infty
.

It is easy to check that

\phi_{n,n^{2}+1}(X)\cap\phi_{n,n+nj_{n}}(X)\neq\emptyset,\hskip 10.00002pt0\leq j_{n}\leq n-1.

Then, we have

\phi_{1,I_{n-1}}\circ\phi_{n,n^{2}+1}(X)\cap\phi_{1,I_{n-1}}\circ\phi_{n,n+nj_{n}}(X)\neq\emptyset,\hskip 10.00002pt0\leq j_{n}\leq n-1.

Hence, we conclude that the (
3.4
) does not hold when we take
U_{n}=[0,\ 1]^{2}
, since the supremum in (
3.4
) is
+\infty
in this case. The same argument shows that the inequality (
3.7
) does not hold when we take
X=[0,\ 1]^{2}
.

\Box

The following example shows that it is possible that
\underline{\dim}_{\rm B}K_{n}<\overline{\dim}_{\rm B}K_{n}
even if all assumptions in Theorem
4.2
are satisfied.

Example 5.6

Consider the self-similar MIFS
\left\{\phi_{n,0},\ \phi_{n,1}\right\}_{n=1}^{+\infty}
on
\mathbb{R}
with

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\phi_{n,j}(x)=\frac{1}{3}(x-j)+j,&2^{2k}\leq n<2^{2k+1},\ j=0,\ 1,\ k\geq 0,\\
\phi_{n,j}(x)=\frac{1}{2}(x-j)+j,&2^{2k+1}\leq n<2^{2k+2},\ j=0,\ 1,\ k\geq 0.\end{array}\right.

(5.3)

Then, the MIFS satisfies the MOSC, MWSC, MBDP and MWHP. Furthermore, we have
\dim_{H}K_{n}=\underline{\dim}_{\rm B}K_{n}=\frac{3\ln 2}{2ln3+\ln 2}<\overline{\dim}_{\rm B}K_{n}=\frac{3\ln 2}{\ln 3+2\ln 2}
.

Proof.

Actually, by the definition of the MIFS, it is easy to see
\phi_{n,0}(0)=0
,
\phi_{n,1}(1)=1
,
\phi_{n,j}([0,\ 1])\subset[0,\ 1]
and
\phi_{n,0}((0,\ 1))\cap\phi_{n,1}((0,\ 1))=\emptyset

for
n\geq 1
and
j=0,\ 1
. Hence, the MIFS satisfies the MOSC, MWSC, MBDP and MWHP.

That means for any
n\geq 1
, we have
\mathcal{A}_{r_{1}\cdots r_{n}}=\{\phi_{1,I}:I\in\Sigma_{1}^{n}\}
, which implies that
\#\mathcal{A}_{r_{1}\cdots r_{n}}=2^{n}.

From (
5.3
), a direct computation leads to

\frac{\ln\#\mathcal{A}_{r_{1}\cdots r_{n}}}{-\ln(r_{1}\cdots r_{n})}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\frac{n\ln 2}{\left(\frac{2}{3}(1-4^{k})+n\right)\ln 3+\frac{2}{3}(4^{k}-1)},\ \quad 2^{2k}\leq n<2^{2k+1},\\
\frac{n\ln 2}{\frac{1}{3}(4^{k+1}-1)\ln 3+(\frac{1}{3}(1-4^{k+1})+n)\ln 2},\ \ 2^{2k+1}\leq n<2^{2k+2}.\end{array}\right.

By noting that (
4.1
) holds, from (
4.2
), it follows that

\underline{\dim}_{\rm B}K_{1}=\liminf\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{\ln\#\mathcal{A}_{r_{1}\cdots r_{n}}}{-\ln(r_{1}\cdots r_{n})}=\frac{3\ln 2}{2\ln 3+\ln 2}.

Also, by (
4.3
), we have

\overline{\dim}_{\rm B}K_{1}=\limsup\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{\ln\#\mathcal{A}_{r_{1}\cdots r_{n}}}{-\ln(r_{1}\cdots r_{n})}=\frac{3\ln 2}{\ln 3+2\ln 2}.

Finally, it is easy to see that the
s_{k}
defined in Corollary
4.3
equals
\frac{\ln 2^{k}}{-\ln R_{I}}=\frac{\ln 2^{k}}{-\ln r_{I}}
with
I\in\Sigma_{1}^{k}
. Hence
\liminf\limits_{k\to\infty}s_{k}=\underline{\dim}_{\rm B}K_{1}
by using Corollary
4.3
(i). The proof is completed.

\Box

The following example shows that the conclusion
\overline{\dim}_{\rm B}K_{n}<\limsup\limits_{b\to 0^{+}}\frac{\ln\#{\mathcal{A}}_{b}}{-\ln b}
is possible if we remove the assumption
r_{0}=\inf\{r_{n,j}:\ n\geq 1,\ 1\leq j\leq N_{n}\}>0
in Theorem
4.1
even if we assume the MOSC and
r_{n,j}=r_{n}
. Moreover, the conclusion
\overline{\dim}_{\rm B}K_{n}<\limsup\limits_{b\to 0^{+}}\frac{\ln\#{\mathcal{A}}_{b}}{-\ln b}
depends on both the assumption
r_{0}>0
and the translations in the maps of the MIFS.

Example 5.7

Let
\left\{\Phi_{n}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
be a self-similar MIFS with
\Phi_{n}=\{\phi_{n,j}(x)=2^{-2^{n}}(x+\alpha_{j}):\ \alpha_{j}\in D_{n}\}
and
\{0,\ 2^{2^{n}}-1\}\subseteq D_{n}\subseteq\{j:\ 0\leq j\leq 2^{2^{n}}-1\}
.

(i)
The MIFS satisfies the MOSC defined in Definition
3.1
with
V_{n}=(0,\ 1)
. Moreover, the left side of (
3.7
) is less than or equal to 3 if we choose
X=[0,\ 1]
.

(ii)
If
D_{n}=\{j:\ 0\leq j\leq 2^{2^{n}}-1\}
for all
n\geq 1
, then

1=\dim_{\rm H}K_{n}=\dim_{\rm B}K_{n}=\dim_{\rm P}K_{n}=\liminf\limits_{b\to 0^{+}}\frac{\ln\#{\mathcal{A}}_{b}}{-\ln b}<\limsup\limits_{b\to 0^{+}}\frac{\ln\#{\mathcal{A}}_{b}}{-\ln b}

for all
n\geq 1
.

(iii)
If
D_{n}=\{0,\ 2^{2^{n}}-1\}
for all
n\geq 1
, then
0=\dim_{\rm H}K_{n}=\dim_{\rm B}K_{n}=\dim_{\rm P}K_{n}=\liminf\limits_{b\to 0^{+}}\frac{\ln\#{\mathcal{A}}_{b}}{-\ln b}=\limsup\limits_{b\to 0^{+}}\frac{\ln\#{\mathcal{A}}_{b}}{-\ln b}

for all
n\geq 1
.

Proof.

(i) It is clear that
K_{n}=[0,\ 1]
for all
n>0
, so
\phi_{n,j}([0,\ 1])\subset[0,\ 1]
for all
n,\ j\geq 1
, we can choose
X=[0,\ 1]
. By checking
\phi_{n,i}((0,\ 1)))\cap\phi_{n,j}((0,\ 1))=\emptyset
for all
n>0
and
i\neq j
, we see that the MIFS satisfies the MOSC defined in Definition
3.1
. The second conclusion is obvious.

(ii) Since
K_{n}=[0,\ 1]
for all
n>0
, the definition of our MIFS shows

R_{1,j_{1}j_{2}\cdots j_{n}}=r_{1,j_{1}j_{2}\cdots j_{n}}=\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}2^{-2^{i}}=2^{(-2^{n+1}+2)},\ \ n=1,2,\cdots.

(5.4)

Let
N_{\delta}
be the number of
\delta
-mesh cubes that intersect
K_{1}=[0,\ 1]
.
By the definition of our MIFS, every interval
\phi_{1,j_{1}j_{2}\cdots j_{n}}([0,\ 1])
is exactly a
\delta
-mesh cube when
\delta=2^{(-2^{n+1}+2)}
. Hence, we have

N_{2^{(-2^{n+1}+2)}}=\#{\mathcal{A}}_{2^{(-2^{n+1}+2)}}=2^{(2^{n+1}-2)}.

(5.5)

The definition of
{\mathcal{A}}_{b}
and (
5.4
) show

\#{\mathcal{A}}_{b}=2^{(2^{n+1}-2)}
when
2^{(-2^{n+1}+2)}\leq b<2^{(-2^{n}+2)}
. Consequently, it follows that

\sup_{2^{(-2^{n+1}+2)}\leq b<2^{(-2^{n}+2)}}\frac{\ln(\#{\mathcal{A}}_{b})}{-\ln b}=\frac{2^{n+1}-2}{2^{n}-2}

(5.6)

and

\inf_{2^{(-2^{n+1}+2)}\leq b<2^{(-2^{n}+2)}}\frac{\ln(\#{\mathcal{A}}_{b})}{-\ln b}=1.

Therefore

\liminf\limits_{b\to 0^{+}}\frac{\ln\#{\mathcal{A}}_{b}}{-\ln b}=1,\ \ \limsup\limits_{b\to 0^{+}}\frac{\ln\#{\mathcal{A}}_{b}}{-\ln b}=2.

Hence statement (ii) follows by using
K_{n}=[0,\ 1]
.

(iii) By a similar argument to (
5.5
), we have
N_{2^{(-2^{n+1}+2)}}=\#{\mathcal{A}}_{2^{(-2^{n+1}+2)}}=2^{n}
. Hence, we get
N_{b}\leq 2N_{2^{(-2^{n+1}+2)}}=2^{n+1}
when
2^{(-2^{n+1}+2)}\leq b<2^{(-2^{n}+2)}
. Also, by a similar argument to (
5.6
), we have

\sup_{2^{(-2^{n+1}+2)}\leq b<2^{(-2^{n}+2)}}\frac{\ln(\#{\mathcal{A}}_{b})}{-\ln b}=\frac{n}{2^{n}-2}.

Therefore,
\limsup\limits_{b\to 0^{+}}\frac{\ln N_{b}}{-\ln b}=\limsup\limits_{b\to 0^{+}}\frac{\ln\#{\mathcal{A}}_{b}}{-\ln b}=0
, and so our conclusion (iii) follows.

\Box

The following example shows that the
U_{n}
in (
3.4
) can not be chosen arbitrarily in general cases. The inequalities (
3.7
) and (
3.8
) depend on the basic space
X
. On the other hand, the MWHP is not necessary condition for (
3.7
) and (
3.8
).

Example 5.8

Consider the self-similar MIFS
\left\{\phi_{n,0},\ \phi_{n,1},\ \cdots,\ \phi_{n,n}\right\}_{n=1}^{+\infty}
on
X\subset\mathbb{R}
with

\phi_{n,0}(x)=\frac{1}{2}(x+1),\ \ \phi_{n,j}(x)=\frac{1}{2n}(x+j-1),\hskip 10.00002pt1\leq j\leq n,\ n=1,\ 2,\ \dotsc.

Then, the MIFS satisfies the MOSC but does not satisfy the MWHP. The inequality (
3.4
) does not hold when
U_{n}^{o}\supset[0,\ 1]
, (
3.7
) and (
3.8
) do not hold when
X^{o}\supset[0,\ 1]
.

Proof.

A direct computation shows
K_{n}=[0,1]
and
\phi_{n,i}((0,1))\cap\phi_{n,j}((0,1))=\emptyset
for all
i\neq j\in\{0,\ 1,\ \cdots,\ n\}
. It follows that the MIFS satisfies the MOSC. By a similar argument to Example
5.5
, we see the MIFS has no MWHP.

Let
\epsilon>0
such that
U\supset[-\epsilon,\ 1+\epsilon]
. Choose
b_{n}=\frac{19}{2^{n}10}
(
n>2
). It is clear that
0^{n-1}j\in{\mathcal{I}}_{b_{n}}
for any
0\leq j\leq n
. By a simple argument, we conclude that for
n\geq j\geq n(1-\epsilon)-\epsilon
,

\phi_{n,0}(U)\cap\phi_{n,j}(U)\supset\left[\frac{1-\epsilon}{2},\ \frac{2+\epsilon}{2}\right]\bigcap\left[\frac{j-1-\epsilon}{2n},\ \frac{\epsilon+j}{2n}\right]\neq\emptyset.

By noting that

\phi_{1,0^{n}}(U)\cap\phi_{1,0^{n-1}j}(U)=\phi_{1,0^{n-1}}(\phi_{n,0}(U)\cap\phi_{n,j}(U)),

we obtain that

\#\{J\in{\mathcal{I}}_{b_{n}}:\ \phi_{1,0^{n}}(U)\cap\phi_{1,0^{n-1}j}(U)\neq\emptyset\}\geq n\epsilon\to+\infty\mbox{ as }n\to+\infty.

Therefore, (
3.4
) does not hold when we choose
U_{n}
such that
U_{n}^{o}\supset[0,\ 1]
.

The above proof also shows that (
3.7
) and (
3.8
) do not hold when
X^{o}\supset[0,\ 1]
.
However, it is easy to check that (
3.7
) and (
3.8
) hold when
X=[0,1]
.

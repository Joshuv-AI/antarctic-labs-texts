Constraining the Baryon Fraction in the Intergalactic Medium with 92 localized Fast Radio Bursts
Puxun Wu

Abstract
Fast radio bursts (FRBs) are emerging as powerful cosmological probes for constraining the baryon fraction in the intergalactic medium (IGM), offering a promising avenue to address the missing baryon problem. In this paper, we analyze constraints on the IGM baryon fraction ( f IGM f_{\mathrm{IGM}} ) using 92 localized FRBs, incorporating a corrected probability distribution function for the IGM dispersion measure within three different cosmological models. We find that variations in the underlying cosmological model have a negligible impact on the inferred values of f IGM f_{\mathrm{IGM}} . While the NE2001 Galactic electron density model yields slightly higher f IGM f_{\mathrm{IGM}} values compared to the YMW16 model, the results are consistent within the 1 σ \sigma confidence level. Additionally, there is no statistically significant evidence for redshift evolution in f IGM f_{\mathrm{IGM}} . Our analysis constrains f IGM f_{\mathrm{IGM}} to the range 0.8 ∼ 0.9 0.8\sim 0.9 , providing strong support for the idea in which the majority of the missing baryons reside in the diffuse IGM.

2
FRB dispersion measures and likelihood function

The observed DM of an extragalactic fast radio burst (FRB), denoted as
\mathrm{DM_{obs}}
, consists of several additive contributions:

\displaystyle\mathrm{DM_{obs}}=\mathrm{DM_{ISM}}+\mathrm{DM_{halo}}+\mathrm{DM_{IGM}}(z)+\mathrm{DM_{host}}(z),

(2.1)

where the subscripts “ISM”, “halo”, “IGM”, and “host” denote the contributions to DM from the Milky Way interstellar medium (ISM), the Milky Way halo, the IGM, and the FRB host galaxy, respectively.
The baryon fraction in the IGM is encoded in the
\mathrm{DM_{IGM}}
component.
To constrain
f_{\mathrm{IGM}}
from FRB observations, we need to construct a likelihood function
that accounts for the statistical distributions of the various components in Eq. (
2.1
).

The Galactic contribution
\mathrm{DM_{ISM}}
can be estimated using models of the Galactic free electron density, such as NE2001
[
69
]
and YMW16
[
70
]
. The halo contribution
\mathrm{DM_{halo}}
is expected to lie in the range
50\text{--}80~\mathrm{pc\ cm^{-3}}
[
62
]
and is commonly modeled by a Gaussian distribution:

\displaystyle P_{\mathrm{halo}}(\mathrm{DM_{halo}})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma_{\mathrm{halo}}}\exp\left[-\frac{(\mathrm{DM_{halo}}-\mu_{\mathrm{halo}})^{2}}{2\sigma_{\mathrm{halo}}^{2}}\right],

(2.2)

with a mean value
\mu_{\mathrm{halo}}=65~\mathrm{pc\ cm^{-3}}
and standard deviation
\sigma_{\mathrm{halo}}=15~\mathrm{pc\ cm^{-3}}
.

The host galaxy contribution
\mathrm{DM_{host}}
is well described by a log-normal distribution
[
60
,
71
]
:

\displaystyle P_{\mathrm{host}}(\mathrm{DM}_{\mathrm{host}})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\mathrm{DM}_{\mathrm{host}}\sigma_{\mathrm{host}}}\exp\left[-\frac{(\ln\mathrm{DM}_{\mathrm{host}}-\mu_{\mathrm{host}})^{2}}{2\sigma_{\mathrm{host}}^{2}}\right].

(2.3)

Here parameters
\mu_{\mathrm{host}}
and
\sigma_{\mathrm{host}}
depend on redshift and the morphological type of the FRB’s host galaxy.
Their values can be estimated from the IllustrisTNG simulation. For repeating FRBs like FRB121102, repeating FRBs like FRB180916 and non-repeating FRBs,
Zhang et al. obtained the best-fit values of parameters
\mu_{\mathrm{host}}
and
\sigma_{\mathrm{host}}
at eight redshifts within the range of
z\in[0.1,1.5]

[
71
]
.
In our analysis, we classify host galaxies into three categories based on their FRB type and use cubic spline interpolation to estimate the values of
\mu_{\mathrm{host}}
and
\sigma_{\mathrm{host}}
at the redshifts of individual FRBs.

The component
\mathrm{DM_{IGM}}
in Eq. (
2.1
) is strongly dependent on both the cosmological model and the baryon density in the IGM. Since the distribution of free electrons in the IGM is inhomogeneous, the actual value of
\mathrm{DM_{IGM}}
fluctuates around its average value
\langle\mathrm{DM_{IGM}}\rangle
.
The ratio of
\mathrm{DM_{IGM}}
and
\langle\mathrm{DM_{IGM}}\rangle
is assumed to follow the distribution
[
61
,
60
,
72
]
:

\displaystyle P_{\mathrm{cosmic}}(\Delta)=A\Delta^{-\beta}\exp\left[-\dfrac{(\Delta^{-\alpha}-C_{0})^{2}}{2\alpha^{2}\sigma_{\mathrm{IGM}}^{2}}\right],\quad\Delta>0,

(2.4)

where
\Delta\equiv\mathrm{DM_{IGM}}/\langle\mathrm{DM_{IGM}}\rangle
and
\alpha=\beta=3
.
Parameters
A
,
C_{0}
and
\sigma_{\mathrm{IGM}}
vary with redshift and have been calculated using the IllustrisTNG simulations at 23 redshifts in the range of
z\in[0.1,9]

[
72
]
. To determine the parameter values at arbitrary redshifts, we apply cubic spline interpolation.

Importantly, Eq.(
2.4
) describes the distribution of the dimensionless variable
\Delta
, not directly that of
\mathrm{DM_{IGM}}
. The corresponding PDF of
\mathrm{DM_{IGM}}
can be derived via a change of variables
[
63
]
:

\displaystyle P_{\mathrm{IGM}}(\mathrm{DM_{IGM}})=\frac{1}{\langle\mathrm{DM_{IGM}}\rangle}P_{\mathrm{cosmic}}\left(\frac{\mathrm{DM_{IGM}}}{\langle\mathrm{DM_{IGM}}\rangle}\right).

(2.5)

We emphasize that the normalization factor
1/\langle\mathrm{DM_{IGM}}\rangle
was omitted in
[
60
]
, leading to a systematic bias in the inferred baryon density parameter.

The mean IGM dispersion measure
\langle\mathrm{DM_{IGM}}\rangle
as a function of redshift
depends on the adopted cosmological model. For a spatially flat universe, it is given by
[
73
,
74
,
13
]
:

\displaystyle\langle\mathrm{DM}_{\mathrm{IGM}}\rangle(z)=\frac{3c\ \Omega_{\mathrm{b0}}h^{2}100^{2}}{8\pi Gm_{p}H_{0}}\int_{0}^{z}\frac{(1+z^{\prime})f_{\mathrm{IGM}}(z^{\prime})\chi_{e}(z^{\prime})}{E(z^{\prime})}\mathrm{d}z^{\prime},

(2.6)

where
c
,
G
,
m_{p}
and
H_{0}
are the speed of light, the gravitational constant, the proton mass and the Hubble constant, respectively. The parameter
\Omega_{\mathrm{b0}}h^{2}
with
h\equiv H_{0}/(100\ \mathrm{km\ s^{-1}\ Mpc^{-1}})
is the present physical baryon density,

f_{\mathrm{IGM}}(z)
represents the fraction of baryons in the IGM, and
\chi_{e}(z)=Y_{\mathrm{H}}\chi_{e,\mathrm{H}}(z)+\frac{1}{2}Y_{\mathrm{He}}\chi_{e,\mathrm{He}}(z)
denotes the ratio of the number of free electrons to baryons in the IGM. Here,
Y_{\mathrm{H}}\sim 3/4
and
Y_{\mathrm{He}}\sim 1/4
are the hydrogen and helium mass fractions, respectively, and
\chi_{e,\mathrm{H}}
and
\chi_{e,\mathrm{He}}
denote the ionization fractions of hydrogen and helium, respectively.
In our analysis, we set
\chi_{e,\mathrm{H}}
and
\chi_{e,\mathrm{He}}
to unity since both hydrogen and helium are fully ionized at low redshifts (
z<3
)
[
75
,
76
]
, resulting in
\chi_{e}(z)=7/8
.
The dimensionless Hubble parameter
E(z)\equiv H(z)/H_{0}
for a flat universe is expressed as:

\displaystyle E^{2}(z)=\Omega_{\mathrm{m0}}(1+z)^{3}+\Omega_{\mathrm{r0}}(1+z)^{4}+\left(1-\Omega_{\mathrm{m0}}-\Omega_{\mathrm{r0}}\right)\exp\left[\int_{0}^{z}\frac{3(1+w(z^{\prime}))}{1+z^{\prime}}\mathrm{d}z^{\prime}\right],

(2.7)

where
\Omega_{\mathrm{m0}}
and
\Omega_{\mathrm{r0}}
are the present matter and radiation density parameters, respectively, and

w(z)
denotes the dark energy equation of state. Several cosmological models are commonly considered.
When
w(z)=-1
, the standard
\Lambda
CDM model is recovered. If
w(z)=w=\text{const.}
, the
w
CDM model is obtained. If
w(z)=w_{0}+w_{a}\ z/(1+z)
with
w_{0}
and
w_{a}
being two constants, this yields the
w_{0}w_{a}
CDM model.

To facilitate the analysis, we define a new variable

\displaystyle\mathrm{DM_{FRB}^{\prime}}\equiv\mathrm{DM_{obs}}-\mathrm{DM_{ISM}}=\mathrm{DM_{halo}}+\mathrm{DM_{IGM}}(z)+\mathrm{DM_{host}}(z).

The joint log-likelihood function for the full FRB sample is then written as:

\displaystyle\ln\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FRB}}=\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\mathrm{FRB}}}\ln P_{i}(\mathrm{DM_{FRB}^{\prime}}),

(2.8)

where
N_{\mathrm{FRB}}
is the total number of FRBs, and
P_{i}
is the probability distribution of
\mathrm{DM_{FRB}^{\prime}}
for the
i
-th FRB, given by

\displaystyle P_{i}(\mathrm{DM}^{\prime}_{\mathrm{FRB},i})

\displaystyle=\int_{50}^{80}\int_{0}^{\mathrm{DM}^{\prime}_{\mathrm{FRB},i}-\mathrm{DM}_{\mathrm{halo}}}P_{\mathrm{halo}}(\mathrm{DM}_{\mathrm{halo}})

(2.9)

\displaystyle\quad\times P_{\mathrm{IGM}}(\mathrm{DM}^{\prime}_{\mathrm{FRB},i}-\mathrm{DM}_{\mathrm{host}}-\mathrm{DM}_{\mathrm{halo}})

\displaystyle\quad\times P_{\mathrm{host}}(\mathrm{DM}_{\mathrm{host}})\ \mathrm{d}\mathrm{DM}_{\mathrm{host}}\ \mathrm{d}\mathrm{DM}_{\mathrm{halo}}.

This likelihood function enables constraints on the baryon fraction in the IGM,
f_{\mathrm{IGM}}
, using FRB data. We utilize the most recent sample compiled by Wang et al.
[
53
]
, which contains 92 localized FRBs with redshifts extending up to
z=1.354
.
Since different Galactic electron density models may yield different estimates of
\mathrm{DM_{ISM}}
, we employ both the YMW16 and NE2001 models to evaluate
\mathrm{DM_{ISM}}
, and thus derive two versions of
\mathrm{DM^{\prime}_{FRB}}
.
In Eq.(
2.9
), the integration over
\mathrm{DM}_{\mathrm{halo}}
is performed from 50 to
80~\mathrm{pc\ cm^{-3}}
, reflecting the expected range for the Milky Way halo contribution
[
62
]
. The upper limit for
\mathrm{DM}_{\mathrm{host}}
is set to
\mathrm{DM}^{\prime}_{\mathrm{FRB},i}-\mathrm{DM}_{\mathrm{halo}}>0
, ensuring that the integrand remains well-defined.
To ensure physical validity (i.e.,
\mathrm{DM}_{\mathrm{host}}>0
), we impose a selection criterion:
\mathrm{DM}_{\mathrm{obs}}-\mathrm{DM}_{\mathrm{ISM}}>80~\mathrm{pc\ cm^{-3}}
.
After applying this criterion, we retain 86 FRBs using the NE2001 model and 88 FRBs using the YMW16 model.

3
Other Cosmological Observations

The baryon fraction in the intergalactic medium,
f_{\mathrm{IGM}}
, exhibits degeneracies with other cosmological parameters, particularly the physical baryon density
\Omega_{\mathrm{b0}}h^{2}
and the Hubble constant
H_{0}
(see Eq.(
2.6
)). To break these degeneracies and obtain tighter constraints, we incorporate additional cosmological observations, namely, SNIa, BAO, and CMB data, into our analysis.

3.1
Type Ia Supernovae

For SNIa data, we adopt the PantheonPlus compilation, which includes 1701 light curves corresponding to 1550 distinct SN Ia, spanning redshifts from about
0.001
to
2.26

[
65
,
64
]
.
This dataset provides the redshift
z
, corrected distance modulus
\mu_{\mathrm{obs}}^{\mathrm{corr}}=m_{B,\mathrm{obs}}^{\mathrm{corr}}-M_{B}
, and the associated covariance matrix
\bm{\mathrm{C}}_{\mathrm{SN}}
, where
m_{B,\mathrm{obs}}^{\mathrm{corr}}
is the corrected apparent magnitude in the B-band, and
M_{B}
is the fiducial absolute B-band magnitude.
For the redshift measurements in this dataset, We use the Hubble-diagram redshift
z_{\mathrm{HD}}
, which accounts for the CMB-frame redshift
z_{\mathrm{CMB}}
and corrections from peculiar velocities. To minimize uncertainties associated with peculiar motion, we exclude SNIa with
z<0.01
, following the prescription in
[
65
]
. This cut leaves 1590 data points, and the corresponding covariance matrix
\bm{\mathrm{C}}_{\mathrm{SN}}
is reduced to a
1590\times 1590
submatrix.
The theoretical distance modulus is given by

\displaystyle\mu_{\mathrm{th}}(z)=25+5\log_{10}\left(\frac{D_{L}(z)}{\mathrm{Mpc}}\right)

(3.1)

with
D_{L}(z)=\frac{c}{H_{0}}(1+z)\int_{0}^{z}\frac{\mathrm{d}z^{\prime}}{E(z^{\prime})}
being the luminosity distance.

The joint log-likelihood function for SNIa dataset can be obtained through

\displaystyle\ln\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{SN}}\propto-\frac{1}{2}\chi^{2}_{\mathrm{SN}},

(3.2)

where
\chi^{2}_{\mathrm{SN}}=\bm{Q}^{\dagger}{\mathbf{C}}^{-1}_{\mathrm{SN}}\bm{Q}
, and
\bm{Q}\equiv\left(\bm{m}_{B,\mathrm{obs}}^{\mathrm{corr}}-M_{B}\right)-\bm{\mu}_{\mathrm{th}}(z_{\mathrm{HD}})

represents the vector of residuals between observed and theoretical distance moduli. The absolute magnitude
M_{B}
is treated as a nuisance parameter and is marginalized over in the analysis.

3.2
Baryon Acoustic Oscillations and Cosmic Microwave Background

For the BAO dataset, we use the latest measurements from the DESI Data Release 2 (DR2)
[
66
]
. Based on various tracer populations, including the Bright Galaxy Sample (BGS), Luminous Red Galaxies (LRG), Emission Line Galaxies (ELG), quasars (QSO), and the Ly
\alpha
forest, DESI provides 13 measurements in total, covering the distance ratios
D_{M}/r_{d}
,
D_{H}/r_{d}
, and
D_{V}/r_{d}
. The relevant distance quantities are defined as

\displaystyle D_{M}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{c}{H_{0}}\int_{0}^{z}\frac{\mathrm{d}z^{\prime}}{E(z^{\prime})},

(3.3)

\displaystyle D_{H}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{c}{H_{0}E(z)},

(3.4)

and

\displaystyle D_{V}(z)=(zD_{M}(z)^{2}D_{H}(z))^{1/3}.

(3.5)

The comoving sound horizon at the drag epoch
r_{d}
is given by

\displaystyle r_{d}=\int_{z_{d}}^{\infty}\frac{c_{s}(z^{\prime})}{H_{0}E(z^{\prime})}\,dz^{\prime},

(3.6)

with
c_{s}(z)
denoting the sound speed in the photon-baryon fluid.
The joint log-likelihood function of the BAO data is

\displaystyle\ln\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{BAO}}\propto-\frac{1}{2}\chi^{2}_{\mathrm{BAO}}=-\frac{1}{2}\left(\bm{A}_{\mathrm{obs}}-\bm{A}_{\mathrm{th}}\right)^{\dagger}\bm{\mathrm{C}}^{-1}_{\mathrm{BAO}}\left(\bm{A}_{\mathrm{obs}}-\bm{A}_{\mathrm{th}}\right),

(3.7)

where
\bm{A}_{\mathrm{obs}}
is the vector consisting of observed values of
D_{M}/r_{d}
,
D_{H}/r_{d}
, and
D_{V}/r_{d}
, and
\bm{A}_{\mathrm{th}}
is the corresponding vector of theoretical predictions, and
\bm{\mathrm{C}}_{\mathrm{BAO}}
is the
13\times 13
covariance matrix of the BAO data.

The CMB data used in our analysis come from the Planck satellite. For the likelihood function of CMB data (
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{CMB}}
), we adopt the Planck 2018 high-
\ell
likelihood for the temperature (TT), polarization (EE), and cross-correlation (TE) spectra, the low-
\ell
likelihood for TT and EE spectra
[
67
]
, as well as the Planck lensing likelihood
[
68
]
. The theoretical CMB power spectra and background evolution are computed using the
CAMB
code
[
77
]
.

We perform Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) sampling of the full parameter space using the
Cobaya
framework
[
78
]
. The total joint log-likelihood is given by

\displaystyle\ln\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{total}}=\ln\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FRB}}+\ln\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{SN}}+\ln\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{BAO}}+\ln\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{CMB}}.

(3.8)

4
Estimation of the Baryon Fraction in the IGM

We begin by constraining a constant baryon fraction in the IGM,
f_{\mathrm{IGM}}
, under three different cosmological models using the corrected PDF of
\mathrm{DM}_{\mathrm{IGM}}
given in Eq. (
2.5
).
The results are shown in figure
1
and summarized in the upper portion of table
1
. For the YMW16 Galactic electron density model, we obtain
f_{\mathrm{IGM}}=0.864\pm 0.041
,
0.862\pm 0.040
, and
0.874\pm 0.040
at the 1
\sigma
confidence level (CL) for the
\Lambda
CDM,
w
CDM, and
w_{0}w_{a}
CDM model, respectively. Using the NE2001 model, the corresponding results are

f_{\mathrm{IGM}}=0.889\pm 0.041
,
0.885^{+0.045}_{-0.038}
, and
0.896\pm 0.039
respectively. These findings show that the choice of cosmological model has a negligible effect on the estimation of
f_{\mathrm{IGM}}
. Although the YMW16 model yields slightly lower central values of
f_{\mathrm{IGM}}
than NE2001, the results are consistent within the
1\sigma
CL.

To further investigate the impact of the normalization factor
\frac{1}{\langle\mathrm{DM_{IGM}}\rangle}
, omitted by Macquart et al. in the PDF of
\mathrm{DM}_{\mathrm{IGM}}

[
60
]
, we perform a comparative analysis using the same method but exclude this factor from the
\mathrm{DM}_{\mathrm{IGM}}
PDF. Focusing on the
\Lambda
CDM model, we obtain the following 1
\sigma
lower bounds:
f_{\mathrm{IGM}}>0.963
for the YMW16 model and
f_{\mathrm{IGM}}>0.971
for the NE2001 model. In this case, FRBs only yield lower bounds on
f_{\mathrm{IGM}}
, and the inferred values are significantly higher than those derived using the corrected PDF. The discrepancy exceeds
2\sigma
, indicating that omitting the
\frac{1}{\langle\mathrm{DM_{IGM}}\rangle}
factor leads to a systematic overestimation of
f_{\mathrm{IGM}}
.

5
Conclusion

FRBs are powerful cosmological probes for constraining the baryon fraction in the IGM, offering a potential resolution to the long-standing missing baryon problem. In this paper, we analyzed constraints on
f_{\mathrm{IGM}}
using a sample of 92 localized FRBs, adopting the corrected PDF of
\mathrm{DM}_{\mathrm{IGM}}
proposed in
[
63
]
, which includes a normalization factor omitted in the earlier widely used model by Macquart et al.
[
60
]
.
We considered three cosmological models: the
\Lambda
CDM,
w
CDM, and
w_{0}w_{a}
CDM model, and examined both constant and redshift dependent parametrizations of
f_{\mathrm{IGM}}
. Two Galactic electron density models, YMW16 and NE2001, were used to estimate
\mathrm{DM}_{\mathrm{ISM}}
for each FRB. Our analysis shows that the main cosmological parameters are predominantly constrained by the Planck CMB, DESI BAO, and PantheonPlus SNIa datasets. However, FRBs significantly tighten constraints on
f_{\mathrm{IGM}}
, with the results remaining largely insensitive to the choice of cosmological model. The NE2001 model tends to favor slightly higher values of
f_{\mathrm{IGM}}
compared to YMW16, but all estimates are consistent within the 1
\sigma
CL. For the redshift-dependent
f_{\mathrm{IGM}}
, FRBs place only upper bounds on the evolution parameter
\alpha
, with
\alpha<0.5
at the
1\sigma
CL. This indicates no statistically significant evidence for redshift evolution in
f_{\mathrm{IGM}}
based on current FRB data.
Our findings suggest that the baryon fraction in the IGM lies within the range
f_{\mathrm{IGM}}\sim 0.8
-
0.9
, providing strong evidence that the majority of the missing baryons reside in the diffuse IGM.

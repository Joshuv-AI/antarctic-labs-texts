Advancing decision making with distance and similarity measures for belief and plausibility in Fermatean fuzzy sets
Authors: Rashid Hussain, Zahid Hussain, Mehboob Ali, Yasir Akhtar, Muhammed I Syam
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractFermatean fuzzy sets (FFSs) have become a potent tool for modeling uncertainty in decision-making, offering a higher degree of flexibility compared to traditional fuzzy and intuitionistic fuzzy sets (IFSs). Within the context of evidence theory, the notions of belief and plausibility enhance the representational capacity of FFSs, enabling the handling of ambiguous and conflicting data. This study proposes advanced constructions of distance and similarity measures for belief and plausible Fermatean fuzzy sets (BP-FFSs). These measures are designed to capture nuanced differences and relationships among BP-FFSs, addressing critical gaps in existing methodologies. The proposed measures are rigorously evaluated for essential mathematical properties. Furthermore, their effectiveness is validated through real-world applications in fields such as pattern identifications, multi-criteria decision-making (MCDM) and medical diagnosis. Numerical analyses using variety of problems demonstrate the superiority and robustness of the suggested approach in handling complex uncertainty scenarios. This research contributes to the theoretical foundation of fuzzy set theory (FST) and its practical applications, offering a comprehensive framework that advances both the understanding and utilization of BP-FFSs. The findings underscore the potential of the proposed measures in addressing contemporary challenges across diverse domains.Keywords: FFSs, BP-distance measures, BP-similarity measures, Pattern identification, Clustering, MCDM, BP-TOPSISSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

The rapidly growing social sector is leading many professions to encounter increasingly large amounts of information simultaneously. Also, several datasets show fuzziness or ambiguity. Probability theory has been used to tackle ambiguities and uncertainties in real world problems. In addition, belief and plausibility measures1,2 are newer strategies for addressing uncertainties. Since ET assigns probabilities to the power set of given events3, it is useful for handling uncertain and ambiguous issues. It is being widely used in various directions, like clustering, classifications etc.4,5, MCDM6, knowledge reasoning7 and risk assessments and evaluation8,9. Shafer’s theory of evidence2 planned to simplify the probability theory. On the other hand, complicated problems cannot be solved using two-way logic. Because of this challenge, Zadeh10 developed fuzzy set (FS) from crisp set (CS). The term fuzziness refers to the vagueness among the sets’ elements. The membership degree (MD) and non-membership degree (NMD) of elements within a set form the basis for describing fuzzy sets (FSs). Fuzzy set theory eliminates the flaws of conventional decisions about managing data and establishes a fresh method for expressing unclear and unreliable data. Thus far, studies on fuzzy set theory have produced significant findings and have been applied extensively in various domains. However, certain situations involving different kinds of uncertainties have arisen that FSs are not capable of handling. For instance, FSs are based on the MD and NMD under the condition: non-membership = 1 − membership which is not always applicable in many real-life situations. Therefore, Atanassov11 generalized the concept of fuzzy sets to IFSs, by including the degree of non-membership and hesitancy, respectively. The Mathematical representation of IFSs is: the MD , the NMD and the hesitation degree such that or , where . Therefore, IFSs are considered to be better suited for modeling uncertainty and ambiguity than traditional FSs. The characterizations of IFS are significant because they are broadly applicable in many areas of daily life. Like, graphically ranking the IFS values through entropy12, using of hybrid-aggregation operators13, nth-type IFS-graphs and their uses14 and Weiner index for Intuitionistic Fuzzy graphs15.

Many authors have proposed extended versions of classic fuzzy sets from various perspectives, such as IFSs16, PFSs17,18, and hesitant fuzzy sets (HFSs)19. Within such things, IFSs have garnered several complaints. The most notable aspect of IFSs is the fact that they include both MD and NMD which may be exploited for summarizing more unclear and speculative data16. IFSs constitute efficient ways of interacting with unclear data and, as such, have become prevalent in a variety of areas20. In IFS conceptual terms, the addition of MD and NMD must be below or equivalent to one. However, in specific situations, this set of requirements becomes difficult to meet. Inspired by this, Senapati and Yager21 introduced a new extension of IFSs, known as Fermatean fuzzy sets which are far better than IFSs when expressing hazy or unreliable data. At the moment, multiple researchers are looking into FFSs22–25. Senapati and Yager21 also recommended employing the score and accuracy function of FFSs to assess the dimensions of two Fermatean fuzzy numbers.

A unified framework for belief and plausibility in fermatean fuzzy sets

Both belief and plausibility measures can be formalized in a manner analogous to their counterparts in Dempster-Shafer theory, but with adaptations to the structure of FFSs. The relationship between belief, plausibility, and uncertainty becomes crucial in decision-making processes where both partial information and vagueness exist. By adopting these measures, we are able to model more sophisticated systems of reasoning that reflect both the certainty and uncertainty inherent in real-world problems, enabling more flexible and reliable decision support systems. The introduction of belief and plausibility measures within the framework of FFSs offers a robust and dynamic approach to handling uncertainty in complex systems. These measures provide deeper insights into the interplay of knowledge, uncertainty, and evidence, thereby facilitating more accurate and informed decision-making in environments characterized by imprecision and incomplete information.

Research gap

Current research on distance and similarity measures has primarily concentrated on traditional fuzzy sets or their simpler extensions, such as IFSs and PFSs, leaving the BP-FFS framework largely underexplored. Existing methodologies often fall short in effectively addressing complex uncertainties, especially in situations involving conflicting or incomplete evidence, which are common in critical decision-making contexts. Moreover, many proposed measures lack rigorous validation against fundamental mathematical properties such as reflexivity, symmetry, and the triangle inequality. While BP-FFSs hold significant theoretical promise, their applications in real-world domains such as medical diagnosis, risk assessment, and MCDM remain underdeveloped. Additionally, there is a notable absence of comparative studies that evaluate newly developed measures against existing approaches in terms of accuracy, sensitivity, and computational efficiency.

Motivation

The growing complexity of decision-making under uncertainty in fields such as pattern recognition, clustering, healthcare, risk assessment, and MCDM demands advanced mathematical tools capable of effectively modeling ambiguous and conflicting information. Traditional fuzzy sets often fall short in addressing such complexities, whereas integrating belief and plausibility concepts with Fermatean fuzzy sets offers a promising direction for more accurate and expressive uncertainty modeling. However, current distance and similarity measures lack the sophistication to fully exploit the higher representational power of BP-FFSs. Moreover, while FFSs and ET have individually undergone significant advancements, their combined use remains relatively unexplored, leaving a notable theoretical gap. This gap, if addressed, can deepen the theoretical foundations of uncertainty management and broaden its practical scope. Furthermore, the development of such measures holds the potential for a multi-disciplinary impact, extending their relevance to domains like machine learning, artificial intelligence, and big data analysis, thereby enhancing their applicability and effectiveness across diverse problem settings.

This motivation underscores the need for a focused effort in developing advanced similarity and distance measures for BP-FFSs, addressing both theoretical gaps and practical challenges.

Our contribution

Our main contributions in this article include:

First, we present an enhanced framework for novel distance and similarity measures on BP-FFSs, addressing limitations in existing approaches and expanding their theoretical foundation. By combining belief and plausibility functions with the high expressiveness of FFSs, the proposed framework offers a robust tool for modeling and analyzing complex uncertainties in diverse contexts. Second, the measures are rigorously validated against essential mathematical properties to ensure both theoretical soundness and practical reliability. Third, their applicability is demonstrated through real-world problem solving in domains such as medical diagnosis, MCDM, and risk assessment. Fourth, a comprehensive numerical analysis using various examples highlights the superior performance of the proposed measures in terms of accuracy, computational efficiency, and adaptability to complex uncertainty scenarios. Finally, this research establishes a solid foundation for future work on BP-FFSs, encouraging further exploration into new applications and methodological extensions.

Interpretation of Fermatean fuzzy sets and belief and plausibility functions

One of the key features of Fermatean fuzzy sets is their ability to model belief and plausibility in a formalized manner, drawing on principles from Dempster-Shafer Theory (DST). These measures play a critical role in assessing the degree of certainty and uncertainty related to a proposition or an event in the context of Fermatean fuzzy sets. The belief measure reflects the degree of certainty in an event or outcome based on the available evidence, while the plausibility measure quantifies the potential for an outcome to occur given the evidence, irrespective of the level of support.

The belief measure in the context of FFSs reflect the degree to which an element is positively associated with a particular set, under the assumption that no other information contradicts or conflicts with this association. This measure can be interpreted as the degree of certainty or trust we place in the membership of an element, considering both its membership and non-membership grades.

In contrast to the belief measure, the plausibility measure accounts for the potential or possibility that an element belongs to a set, based on the degree of membership and the absence of evidence to the contrary. The plausibility measure reflects the potential for an element to be a member of the set, without necessarily asserting that it must be excluded. This measure is thus broader than the belief measure and can be thought of as expressing the upper bound on the likelihood of membership, given the available information.

Linkage of ET with FFSs

ET and FFSs are two distinct mathematical frameworks for dealing with uncertainty and imprecision. ET uses belief and plausibility functions to control and model uncertainty, whereas FFSs focused on controlling uncertainty by employing MD and NMD to express belongingness and non-belongingness of an element. It is clear from a thorough analysis that FFSs and ET are closely related and intrinsically linked.

Multi-criteria decision making

MCDM involves using the available facts to rank alternatives in a desired order or to choose the best option. Health results can be improved by customizing dietary recommendations to each person’s needs based on their genetics, lifestyle, and current medical problems. The significance of encouraging healthy eating practices to enhance not just individual health but also community resilience against diet-related diseases is becoming more widely acknowledged by communities and health systems.

An MCDM approach for balancing nutritional priorities:

MCDM has become a powerful tool in the field of nutrition as the need for customized dietary plans increases. MCDM offers a methodical way to rank different dietary alternatives according to multiple criteria. This method is very helpful in the field of nutrition because dietary decisions can be impacted by a number of factors, such as cost, nutritional value, convenience of preparation, and personal tastes. Nutritionists and other medical professionals can make well-informed decisions by taking into account both the quantitative and qualitative aspects of food selections by using MCDM. For instance, when assessing different diet plans, MCDM enables the comparison of how well each plan meets specific health objectives.

Conclusions

In conclusion, this study advances the theoretical and practical dimensions of FFSs by introducing robust distance and similarity measures for BP-FFSs. These measures not only address existing methodological gaps but also exhibit superior performance in real-world applications, as demonstrated through extensive numerical analyses. The proposed method is also applied in clustering problem. FFSs are particularly valuable in different applications due to its ability to model and address uncertainty, imprecision, and multi-objective trade-offs inherent in real-world scenarios. To show the performance of proposed methods in practical application, we have extended the concept of TOPSIS to BP-TOPSIS. Furthermore, we have applied BP-TOPSIS for selection of suitable diets. By enhancing the ability to manage complex uncertainty scenarios, the proposed framework paves the way for more effective decision-making, diagnosis, and pattern recognition across diverse fields. Future work could focus on adapting the proposed measures for Bipolar Fuzzy sets, Neutrosophic Fuzzy sets and T-spherical Fuzzy sets especially in real-time decision-making contexts. Applying this approach to additional real-world problems in fields such as healthcare, finance, supply chain management, and the social sciences could further validate its practical usefulness and impact.

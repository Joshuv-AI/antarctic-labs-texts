SOX17: Insight into the Genesis of Intracranial Aneurysm
Authors: Bhanu Pratap Singh Chauhan, Jyoti Gupta, Monica Irungbam, Binita Dholakia, Shaam Bodeliwala, Ghanshyam Das Singhal, Anita Jagetia, Daljit Singh, Niraj Ghimire, Sumit Bhandari
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveTo evaluate the association of the SOX17 gene with intracranial aneurysm (IA) and its expression in a patient with IA.Materials and MethodsPatients visiting the Department of Neurosurgery from March 2020 to December 2021 were screened for IA on the basis of clinical criteria of sudden-onset headache, loss of consciousness, etc. The diagnosis of subarachnoid hemorrhage was made on plain computed tomography (CT; head), and the presence of IA was confirmed by CT angiography brain and/or on cerebral digital subtraction angiography. Confirmed patients were enrolled in the study after taking informed written consent. A case–control design was used, with 50 IA patients as cases and 50 age- and sex-matched normal individuals from the community who were healthy subjects with no risk factors for IA formation as controls.Results The 50 patients with IA included 48% females and 52% males, with age ranging from 26 to 78 years (mean age: 50.84 ± 12.33 years). The majority (26 patients) of patients were in the age groups of 40 to 60 years, 16 patients were above 60 years, and only 8 cases were below the age of 40 years. The 50 healthy control subjects included 16 females and 34 males, with age ranging from 30 to 67 years (mean age: 51.12 ± 10.29 years). Both cases and controls were found to be similar in terms of age distribution ( p -value = 0.752) as well as the mean age ( p -value = 0.689). The SOX 17 gene mRNA expression in the blood of IA patients was analyzed and compared with the healthy control group. The SOX17 gene mRNA expression was higher in IA patients (2 −Δ C T = 0.69) in comparison with the healthy control (2 −Δ C T = 0.11). As compared with the healthy control taken as reference, the decrease in SOX17 gene mRNA expression in the blood of IA patients (2 −ΔΔ C T 5.96-fold) was statistically significant ( p -value < 0.01). ConclusionFrom our study, we conclude that there is a significant positive association between SOX17 gene with IA, hence this gene has been found to be overexpressed. As a result, this may be used as a simple, minimally invasive blood screening tool to assess the risk of IA formation in high-risk group individuals.

Introduction

Saccular intracranial aneurysms (IAs), which affect roughly 3 to 5% of the population, 1 2 are focal pathological dilatations of intracranial arteries, primarily at branching points. Common genetic variations have been identified by genome-wide association studies (GWAS), which aid in determining the signaling pathways involved in the genesis of IAs. 3 4 Loci on the human genome found to be strongly associated with IA have been identified on chromosome 8q11.23. These loci on chromosome 8q11.23 harbor a gene, namely the SOX17 gene. 5 6 This is a family of transcription factors known as SOX proteins, which share a high-mobility-group DNA-binding domain (HMG box) homologous to SRY (sex-determining region of Y chromosome). 7 The SOX17 gene is considered to be a regulator of endoderm and hematopoietic differentiation and is required for the maintenance of fetal and neonatal hematopoietic stem cells (HSCs), which are responsible for blood vessel formation in the body, including cranial vasculature. 8 Severe vascular malformations have been reported in mice due to defects in the SOX gene complex. 9 SOX17 and other members of the SOXF subgroup (SOX7, SOX18) also play a pivotal role in cardiovascular development. 10 The association of the SOX17 gene has been studied and confirmed in patients with IA, mainly in European and Japanese populations; however, the literature is still scanty, and as of now, there is no study in the Indian subcontinent. This study was conducted to investigate the association between SOX17 gene expression and IA in the Indian population.

Materials and Methods

Study population : patients visiting the Neurosurgery Department, Gobind Ballabh Pant Institute of Postgraduate Medical Education and Research, New Delhi, India from March 2020 to December 2021 were screened for IA on the basis of clinical criteria of sudden-onset headache, loss of consciousness, etc. The diagnosis of subarachnoid hemorrhage was made on plain computed tomography (CT; head), and the presence of IA was further confirmed by CT angiography brain and/or on cerebral digital subtraction angiography. Confirmed IA patients were approached and enrolled in the study after taking informed written consent. A case–control design was used with 50 IA patients as cases (group 1) and 50 age- and sex-matched normal individuals from the community who were healthy subjects with no risk factors for IA formation as controls (group 2). Subjects having risk factors for IA, like hypertension, family history, smoking, etc., were excluded from the control group.

The Institutional Ethical Committee of Maulana Azad Medical College and Associated Lok Nayak Hospital & GB Pant Institute of Postgraduate Medical Education & Research, New Delhi, approved the study.

SOX17 mRNA Expression Study

RNA Isolation and Reverse Transcription

A peripheral blood sample (3 mL) was collected in ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid (EDTA) containing vials from both patients and control subjects. Total RNA from blood samples of patients and control subjects was extracted using an RNA extraction kit (QIAamp RNA blood mini kit, QIAGEN) following the manufacturer's instructions. The RNA concentration was determined by measuring the absorbance at 260 nm. The quality and integrity of the RNA were determined by the A260/280 ratio. A complementary DNA (cDNA) synthesis kit (Thermo Fisher Scientific, United States) was used for cDNA synthesis according to the manufacturer's instructions. Briefly, a mixture of total RNA (100 ng), 2 μL oligo (dT) primer, and 9 μL water was incubated in a thermal cycler for 5 minutes at 70°C and then for 10 minutes at 4°C. To this reaction mixture, 5× RT buffer (4 μL), 5 mM dNTPs (2 μL), RNAse inhibitor, and reverse transcriptase, each 1 μL, were then added in a total volume of 20 μL, and this final reaction mixture was incubated at 42°C for 1 hour. After the reaction was complete, the mixture was heated to 95°C for 5 minutes to inactivate the reverse transcriptase and chilled at 4°C. cDNA was stored at −80°C till further analysis.

Quantitative Real-Time Polymerase Chain Reaction

A quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (PCR) was performed on a CFX Opus 96 system (BIO-RAD) using 1 μL cDNA, equivalent to 100 ng RNA, for SOX17 gene expression and β-actin genes' expression with the corresponding primers: SOX17 F (5′- CGCTTTCATGGTGTGGGCTAAGGACG -3′) and SOX17 R (5′ TAGTTGGGGTGGTCCTGCATGTGCTG 3′); β-actin-F (5′-CGACAACGGCTCCGGCATGT GC-3′) and β-actin-R (5′ CGTCACCGGAGTCCATCACGATGC-3′). A reaction mixture was prepared consisting of 10 μL SYBR Green Master Mix, 1 μL cDNA (100 ng RNA), 0.3 μL forward primer (25 pM/μL), 0.3 μL reverse primer (25 pM/μL), and 8.4 μL water in a 20 μL final reaction volume. A standard PCR protocol was established for simultaneous amplification of SOX17 gene and β-actin genes. The first segment of the amplification cycle consisted of a denaturation program of 95°C for 10 minutes. The second segment consisted of three steps: denaturation (45 seconds at 94°C), primer annealing (30 seconds at 64°C), and elongation (45 seconds at 72°C), which was repeated for 40 cycles. The final segment consisted of an extension step of 72°C for 10 minutes.

The cycle threshold ( C T ) is defined as the number of cycles required for the fluorescence signal to cross the threshold in real-time PCR. 11 Every run contained a negative control to be sure that it was the actual expression and not contamination. The expression of SOX17 gene mRNA was registered as a Δ C T value. The Δ C T was calculated by subtracting the C T values of the internal reference gene, β-actin, from the C T values of the target mRNA of the SOX17 gene. As there is an inverse correlation between Δ C T and the mRNA level, lower Δ C T values denote increased mRNA levels. The resulting normalized Δ C T values were used in calculating relative expression values using 2 −Δ C T and these values are directly related to the mRNA expression levels. The 2 −ΔΔ C T calculation was then used to determine the relative quantitative levels of mRNA. 12

Statistical Analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using Epi-Info, JASP, and Statistical Package for Social Sciences (SPSS) version 23.0. Variables were presented as mean and standard deviation.

The quantitative variables were tested for normality using the Shapiro–Wilk test for normality, Q–Q plots, and visual inspection of the histograms. The significance level was considered at p < 0.05 (significant), while for p < 0.01 and p < 0.001, it was considered highly significant. Two-tailed tests were performed throughout the analysis for all statistical tests.

Results

The 50 patients with IA included 24 (48%) females and 26 (52%) males, with age ranging from 26 to 78 years (mean age: 50.84 ± 12.33 years). Of the 50 IA patients, the majority of patients (26) were in the age groups of 40 to 60 years, 16 patients were above 60 years, and only 8 cases were below the age of 40 years. The 50 healthy control subjects included 16 females and 34 males, with age ranging from 30 to 67 years (mean age: 51.12 ± 10.29 years). The age distribution of controls was also similar to that of the cases, 30 controls in the age group of 40 to 60 years, 14 were above 60 years, and the remaining 6 controls were below 40 years. Both cases and controls were found to be similar in terms of the age distribution ( p -value = 0.752) as well as the mean age ( p -value = 0.689).

The SOX 17 gene mRNA expression in the blood of IA patients was analyzed and compared with that of the healthy control group. The SOX17 gene mRNA expression was higher in IA patients (2 −Δ C T = 0.69) in comparison with the healthy control (2 −Δ C T = 0.11). As compared with the healthy control taken as reference, the decrease in SOX17 gene mRNA expression in the blood of IA patients (2 −ΔΔ C T 5.96-fold) was statistically significant ( p -value < 0.01; Table 1 and Fig. 1 ).

Discussion

SOX is a family of transcription factors known as SOX proteins, which share a high-mobility-group DNA-binding domain (HMG box) homologous to SRY (sex-determining region of Y chromosome). 7 There are 20 different SOX genes that have been identified in humans, and they are classified into groups based on the similarities in their HMG box sequence. The family is broken down into subgroups based on functional assays, structural motifs, and homology within the HMG domain. 13 The SOX group is divided into 9 subgroups, A through H. 14

With the support of GWAS, a region strongly associated with IA has been identified and confirmed to be located on 8q11.23, which corresponds to SOX17 gene loci. The SOX17 gene is considered to be a regulator of endoderm and hematopoietic differentiation and is required for the maintenance of fetal and neonatal HSCs, which are responsible for blood vessel formation in the body, including cerebral blood vessels. Recent studies have shown that SOX17 interacts directly with the promoter region of Notch1 and Notch4 in endothelial cells. 15 Severe vascular malformations have been reported in mice due to defects in the SOX gene complex.

Here, we have aimed to study the association between SOX17 gene and IA by testing the expression of the SOX17 gene in blood samples in cases compared with controls. Since the β-actin gene is a housekeeping gene and is expressed by all human cells, this gene can be used to assess the expression of the SOX17 gene in patients with IA and in controls.

In the current study, we found that there was a statistically significant ( p -value < 0.001) difference in SOX17 gene expression between patients with IA and controls. The mean FOLD CHANGE was 6.56 ± 2.48 in cases versus 1.17 ± 0.66 in controls. Since there is a difference of 5.39 in cases versus controls, this suggests that SOX17 gene is nearly five times overexpressed or upregulated in patients with IA, thereby suggesting a positive association between SOX17 gene expression and IA. In aneurysm patients, the highest FOLD CHANGE was found to be 13.718 and the lowest FOLD CHANGE was 0.694. However, the majority of controls had FOLD CHANGE <1; the highest value noted was 2.82 and the lowest value was 0.22. Thus, this can help us in screening at-risk patients, such as those with family history, connective tissue disorder, adult-dominant polycystic kidney disease, etc., for aneurysm formation. Our study is in concordance with previous studies conducted by Yasuno et al, 16 Li et al, 17 Hong et al, 18 and Zholdybayeva et al, 19 who also found a statistically significant association between SOX17 gene and IA.

Yasuno et al 16 performed a GWAS in European and Japanese cohorts and confirmed the association between SOX17 and IA. Li et al 17 established that the SOX17 allele G of rs1072737 is a risk allele, while the allelic G of rs9298506 is a protective allele. Hong et al 18 found the minor C allele to be significantly associated with IA. They also found that alleles rs10958409 and rs9298506 may increase the risk of IA in East-Asian populations. Similarly, Zholdybayeva et al 19 studied the relationship between single-nucleotide polymorphisms and IA in a Kazakh population. The allele rs1504749 polymorphism of SOX17 was one of the significant alleles. Likewise, Park et al 20 also investigated an association between SOX17 gene variants and IA.

In our series, patients across a wide age range were enrolled, with both males and females well represented. Therefore, SOX17 gene expression can be considered as a screening test for IA across a broad age group. Due to recent technological advances and increasing availability of CT and magnetic resonance imaging, IAs are diagnosed more frequently, 21 but radiation exposure and cost of procedure are limitations as compared with the simple PCR test. Thus, only peripheral blood samples can be expanded to screen at-risk patients for aneurysms by calculating the expression of the SOX17 gene.

Ours is the first of its kind study in the Indian population to study the association between SOX17 gene and IA. These data could thus help in screening individuals who are at risk of harboring cerebral aneurysms by a simple blood test in an immensely populated country like India. This can help in further development of targeted gene therapy, understanding the etiopathogenesis of IA, and planning future studies on the line of this study.

Conclusion

From our study, we conclude that there is a significant positive association between SOX17 gene and IA. Therefore, this gene has been found to be overexpressed in these patients. As a result, this may be used as a simple, minimally invasive blood screening tool to assess the risk of IA formation in high-risk group individuals.

The proof of solidity of
\mathsf{KM}
, as presented in
[
E

has two stages. In the first, one repeats the proof
strategy of the proof of solidity of
\mathsf{ZF}

\mathsf{KM}

that can be circumvented if

2.2. Definition.
Let
\mathcal{L}_{\mathsf{KM}}
be the
two-sorted language of
\mathsf{KM}

(a)

\mathsf{CC}
(Class Collection) consists of the
universal closure of formulae of the form:

\left[\forall x\ \exists X\ \varphi(x,X)\right]\rightarrow\left[\exists Y\ \forall x\ \varphi(x,(Y)_{x})\right],

where
\varphi(x,X)
is an
\mathcal{L}_{\mathsf{KM}}
-formula in which

(Y)_{x}
is the ‘

\mathbf{V}^{2}

coded by

(Y)_{x}=\{y\in\mathbf{V}:\left\langle x,y\right\rangle\in Y\},

where
\left\langle x,y\right\rangle
is the Kuratowski ordered pair of
x

and
y
.

6

6

We have followed

]

(used, e.g., in
[

]

[

]
), which in a context involving
sets, runs the risk of getting confused with the axiom of choice.

(b)

\mathsf{CC}_{\mathsf{set}}
is the fragment of
\mathsf{CC}
that consists of the universal closure of formulae of the form:

\left[\forall x\in s\ \exists X\ \varphi(x,X)\right]\rightarrow\left[\exists Y\ \forall x\in s\ \varphi(x,(Y)_{x})\right].

(c)

\mathsf{CR}
(Class Replacement) is the fragment of
\mathsf{CC}
that consists of the universal closure of formulae of the form:

\left[\forall x\ \exists!X\ \varphi(x,X)\right]\rightarrow\left[\exists Y\ \forall x\ \varphi(x,(Y)_{x})\right].

2.4. Theorem.

\mathsf{KM+CC}

does not eliminate

.
9
9

9

A theory

xEy

\leftrightarrow f(x)=f(y).
In
\mathsf{ZF}

which takes advantage of the stratification of the universe into the
well-ordered family of sets of the form

, as

over the ordinals. For more about this concept, see Section 4.4 of Hodges’
textbook
[

.

Proof.
Let
\mathsf{ZF}^{-}
be the result of eliminating
the powerset axiom from the formulation of
\mathsf{ZF}
in which the
separation scheme and the collection scheme take the place of the
replacement scheme. Also, let:

T_{1}:=\mathsf{KM+CC}
, and
T_{2}:=\mathsf{ZF}^{-}+\exists\kappa
(
\kappa
is inaccessible and
\forall x\ \left|x\right|\leq\kappa
)
.

The result follows in light of the following facts:

(1)

T_{1}
and
T_{2}
are sequential theories. This is trivial
once one knows the definition of sequentiality. Roughly speaking, a theory
is sequential if it supports a modicum of coding machinery to have access to
a ‘
\beta
-function’ that codes finite sequences of objects in the domain
of discourse.
10
10

10

More precisely, a theory
T
is sequential if there is a formula
N(x)
,
together with appropriate formulae providing interpretations of equality,
and the operations of successor, addition, and multiplication for elements
satisfying
N(x)
such that
T
proves the translations of the axioms of
\mathsf{Q}
(Robinson’s arithmetic) when relativized to
N(x)
; and
additionally, there is a formula
\beta(x,i,w)
(whose intended meaning is
that
x
is the
i
-th element of a sequence
w
) such that
T
proves that
every sequence can be extended by any given element of the domain of
discourse.

(2)

T_{1}
is bi-interpretable with
T_{2}
, where the witnessing
interpretation of
T_{1}
in
T_{2}
is identity preserving, but the
witnessing interpretations of
T_{2}
in
T_{1}
is not identity preserving.
This is a well-known fact, the main ingredients of whose proof were
developed by Mostowski and Marek. For a perspicuous exposition, see Chapter
2 of William’s doctoral dissertation.

(3)

If two sequential theories are bi-interpretable via a pair of

equivalent. This result is due to Friedman and Visser
[

]
.

(4)

T_{1}

[

11

11

Abstract
. Let 𝖤 / ℚ \mathsf{E}/\mathbb{Q} be an elliptic curve. By the modularity theorem, it admits a surjection from a modular curve X 0 ​ ( N ) → 𝖤 X_{0}(N)\to\mathsf{E} , and the minimal degree among such maps is called the modular degree of 𝖤 \mathsf{E} . By the Mordell–Weil Theorem, 𝖤 ⁡ ( ℚ ) ≃ ℤ r ⊕ T \mathsf{E}(\mathbb{Q})\simeq\mathbb{Z}^{r}\oplus T for some nonnegative integer r r and some finite group T T . 1.
Introduction

The celebrated Modularity Theorem
[
Wil95
,
TW95
,
BCDT01
]
assures us that, given any elliptic curve
\mathsf{E}/\mathbb{Q}
, there is a modular parameterization

(1.1)

X_{0}(N)\xrightarrow{\phi_{\mathsf{E}}}\mathsf{E}

where
X_{0}(N)
is the modular curve of level
N=\mathrm{conductor}(\mathsf{E})
.
Among all such maps, the least degree is called the
modular degree
of
\mathsf{E}
, which we denote by
m_{\mathsf{E}}
.
The arithmetic significance of
m_{\mathsf{E}}
has been the subject of much research; for instance, its prime divisors are closely related to the congruence primes of the modular form
f_{\mathsf{E}}
associated to
\mathsf{E}

[
ARS12
]
.

A famous conjecture of M. Watkins
[
Wat02
]
predicts that
\mathrm{ord}_{2}(m_{\mathsf{E}})
is bounded below by the Mordell–Weil rank of
\mathsf{E}(\mathbb{Q})
.
Much progress has been made on this problem, especially in the case when
m_{\mathsf{E}}
is odd (in which case Watkins’ conjecture implies that
\mathsf{E}(\mathbb{Q})
is finite); see for example
[
CE09
,
KK18
,
KK19
,
Yaz11
]
.

When
m_{\mathsf{E}}
is not assumed to be odd, much progress has been made in proving Watkins’ Conjecture in quadratic twist families of elliptic curves.
For instance, in
[
EP21
]
, the authors show that if
\mathsf{E}(\mathbb{Q})[2]\neq 0
, then Watkins’ conjecture holds for quadratic twists of
\mathsf{E}
by square-free integers with sufficiently many prime divisors.
In
[
CP22
]
the authors establish Watkins’ conjecture for many semi-stable elliptic curves with
\mathsf{E}(\mathbb{Q})[2]\neq 0
under some additional restrictions on the primes of split and non-split multiplicative reduction.
Most recently, J. Caro
[
Car24
]
proves that if
\mathsf{E}
is an elliptic curve with prime-power conductor and
\mathsf{E}(\mathbb{Q})[2]\neq 0
, then Watkins’ Conjecture holds for any quadratic twist of
\mathsf{E}
.

Note that Watkins’ conjecture predicts that
2\mid m_{\mathsf{E}}
whenever
\mathsf{E}(\mathbb{Q})
is infinite.
As explained in
[
Car24
]
, the only missing case for this weaker conjecture is the case when
N
is divisible by at most two odd primes,
\mathsf{E}
has additive reduction at
2
, and
\mathsf{E}(\mathbb{Q})[2]
is nontrivial; see Appendix
A
for a discussion of this claim.
The results of
[
Car24
]
cover the case when
\mathsf{E}
has additive reduction at
2
and at the odd primes, but it does not allow for any odd primes of multiplicative reduction.

The goal of this paper is to complement the existing work in this area by establishing Watkins’ conjecture for many elliptic curves with additive reduction at
2
, bad reduction at no more than 2 odd primes, and
\mathsf{E}(\mathbb{Q})[2]\neq 0
.
To avoid overlapping with
[
Car24
]
, we study elliptic curves with
multiplicative
reduction at one odd prime.
We note that the curves we consider often have
split
multiplicative reduction at an odd prime, so they are not covered by the main result of
[
CP22
]
.

We now summarize our results.

•

We begin with the complete classification, due to W. Ivorra, of elliptic curves of conductor
2^{m}p
for
m\geq 2
and
p
an odd prime.
In Theorem
3.2
, we prove that many curves with conductor
2^{m}p
have rank at most 1, including all the curves with
2\leq m\leq 5
.
In Corollary
3.5
, we show that the rank bound implies Watkins’ Conjecture for these curves, assuming either the Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer Conjecture or the finiteness of Shafarevich–Tate groups.

•

We then study quadratic twists of Ivorra curves, which have conductor
2^{m}pq^{2}
, and deduce Watkins’ conjecture for many of these twists by bounding their ranks and using properties of the Petersson norm; see Section
4.2
and in particular Theorem
4.14
.

•

An appendix gives the full details of the proof of Theorem
3.2
.
In the main body of the paper, we prove just one case in order to streamline the paper for the reader’s convenience.

It is likely possible to extend these methods to study the remaining elliptic curves of conductor
2^{m}p^{a}q^{b}
, for instance those with conductor
2^{m}pq
, using the results of
[
Mul06
]
.

3.1.

We begin by providing an upper bound for the algebraic ranks of the elliptic curves arising from Theorem

First, recall that for

\displaystyle{\mathsf{E}}\colon y^{2}

Proposition 3.1

, a contradiction.

If we consider the form of the homogeneous spaces
C^{\prime}_{\pm 2p}
, we see that the exact same argument applies again, and we deduce that
\pm p,2p\not\in\mathrm{Sel}^{(\phi^{\prime})}(\mathsf{E}^{\prime}/\mathbb{Q})
.

(iv)

Group structure obstructions:
Recall that our Selmer groups have been identified with a subgroup of
\mathbb{Q}^{\times}/(\mathbb{Q}^{\times})^{2}
.
It is now possible to use this group structure to eliminate a few more cases, as indicated in the table.
For instance, when
k
is even, we have
-p\in\mathrm{Sel}^{(\phi)}(\mathsf{E}/\mathbb{Q})
and
\pm 2\notin\mathrm{Sel}^{(\phi)}(\mathsf{E}/\mathbb{Q})
.
If
2p\in\mathrm{Sel}^{(\phi)}(\mathsf{E}/\mathbb{Q})
, then also
(2p)(-p)=-2\in\mathrm{Sel}^{(\phi)}(\mathsf{E}/\mathbb{Q})
, so we conclude
2p\not\in\mathrm{Sel}^{(\phi)}(\mathsf{E}/\mathbb{Q})
, and similarly for
-2p
.

There remain some undetermined cells, which we color in blue, but nevertheless, we have computed enough to show that, regardless of the parity of
k
, we have

\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}\mathrm{Sel}^{(\phi)}(\mathsf{E}/\mathbb{Q})+\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}\mathrm{Sel}^{(\phi^{\prime})}(\mathsf{E}^{\prime}/\mathbb{Q})\leq 3.

So (
3.3
) implies that

\mathrm{rank}_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathsf{E}(\mathbb{Q}),\mathrm{rank}_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathsf{E}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Q})\leq 1

as desired.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

It is often possible to give a more precise result.
For instance, the curves of conductor
8p
and type
I
have rank
0
when
p\equiv 9\pmod{16}
, because one can check that
C_{\pm 2}(\mathbb{Q}_{2})=\emptyset
.

Computational evidence suggests that for
T=\mathbf{VIII}
and

+\alpha
(resp.
-\alpha

A similar phenomenon is witnessed in families

.

Remark 3.4
.

It is not entirely clear whether any of the families listed in Ivorra’s theorem are infinite! For instance, the infinitude of some of the families would follow from special cases of the Bunyakovsky conjecture
[
Bou84
]
, which is currently open.

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
\mathsf{E}
be an elliptic curve as in the statement of Theorem
3.2
.
Assuming either the Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer Conjecture or that
\Sha(\mathsf{E}/\mathbb{Q})

\mathsf{E}
.

Proof.

If
\mathrm{rank}_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathsf{E}(\mathbb{Q})=0

\mathrm{rank}_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathsf{E}(\mathbb{Q})=1

Assuming either BSD or finiteness of

By the contrapositive of

∎

Watkins’ Conjecture in Quadratic Twist Families

Let
\mathsf{E}
be an elliptic curve of conductor
N_{\mathsf{E}}=N=2^{m}p
arising in Theorem
3.2
.
For any odd prime
q
and
d=\pm q
, we let
\mathsf{E}^{(d)}
denote the corresponding quadratic twist.
By
[
Del03
, p. 675]
), its conductor is given by
N^{(d)}=2^{k}pq^{2}
, where
k\geq m
with equality when
d\equiv 1\pmod{4}
.

Remark 4.1
.

In fact, even when
q\equiv 3\pmod{4}
, it is still sometimes the case that
k=m
.
For example, if
\mathsf{E}
is the elliptic curve with Cremona label
5248a2
, which has conductor
N=2^{7}\cdot 41
, and if we take
q=7
, then the corresponding quadratic twist
\mathsf{E}^{(q)}
is the curve with Cremona label
257152bg2
and conductor
N^{(d)}=2^{7}\cdot 7^{2}\cdot 41
.
So in this case,
k=m=7
.

On the other hand, if
\mathsf{E}
is the elliptic curve with Cremona label
692a1
, which has conductor
N=2^{2}\cdot 173
, and if we take
q=7
, then the corresponding quadratic twist
\mathsf{E}^{(q)}
is the curve with Cremona label
135632c1
and conductor
N^{(d)}=2^{4}\cdot 7^{2}\cdot 173
.
So in this case,
m=2
while
k=4
.

Before proceeding, we also note that the non-triviality of
\mathsf{E}(\mathbb{Q})[2]
has a useful consequence.
Denote by
a_{q}(\mathsf{E})=q+1-\#\tilde{\mathsf{E}}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\mathsf{E}
be an Ivorra curve of type
\mathbf{I}
through
\mathbf{XX}
and
q
be a prime of good reduction. Then
a_{q}(\mathsf{E})
is even.

Proof.

This follows from the fact that the
|\mathsf{E}(\mathbb{Q})[2]|=2
for every Ivorra curve of type
I
through
XX
, and that for every prime
q
of good reduction there is an injection

\mathsf{E}(\mathbb{Q})_{\mathrm{tors}}\hookrightarrow\widetilde{\mathsf{E}}(\mathbb{F}_{q}).

∎

4.1.
Modular form preliminaries

Let
f\in S_{2}(\Gamma_{0}(N))
be a weight 2 cuspidal holomorphic modular form.
Let
\mathfrak{h}
denote the upper half-plane in
\mathbb{C}
.

Definition 4.3
.

The
Petersson norm
of
f\in S_{2}(\Gamma_{0}(N))
is given by

\|f\|_{N}=\left(\int_{\Gamma_{0}(N)\backslash\mathfrak{h}}|f(z)|^{2}dx\wedge dy\right)^{1/2},\quad z=x+iy\ \text{and}\ y>0.

We are interested in those
f\in S_{2}(\Gamma_{0}(N))
which are associated to elliptic curves.
In particular, let
\mathsf{E}/\mathbb{Q}
be an Ivorra curve and
\omega_{\mathsf{E}}
its Néron differential.
Recall from (
1.1
) that we have a paramaterization

X_{0}(N)\xrightarrow{\phi_{\mathsf{E}}}\mathsf{E}.

Assume for the moment that
\mathsf{E}
is an
optimal
elliptic curve, in the sense that
\mathrm{deg}~\phi_{\mathsf{E}}
(equivalently
m_{\mathsf{E}}
) is minimal in its isogeny class.
Then
\phi_{\mathsf{E}}^{\ast}\omega_{\mathsf{E}}
is a regular differential on
X_{0}(N)
, and

(4.1)

\phi_{\mathsf{E}}^{\ast}\omega_{\mathsf{E}}=2\pi icf_{\mathsf{E}}(z)dz,

where
c
is a unique integer up to sign; see
[
Edi91
, Proposition 2]
.

Definition 4.4
.

The
manin constant
of
\mathsf{E}/\mathbb{Q}
is given by
c_{\mathsf{E}}=|c|
, where
c
is the integer in (
4.1
).

Now suppose that
\mathsf{E}^{\prime}
is another elliptic curve and
E\xrightarrow{\psi}\mathsf{E}^{\prime}
is an isogeny.
We obtain a modular parameterization

X_{0}(N)\xrightarrow{(\psi\circ\phi)}\mathsf{E}^{\prime},

and we similarly have

(\psi\circ\phi_{\mathsf{E}})^{\ast}\omega_{\mathsf{E}}^{\prime}=2\pi ic^{\prime}f_{\mathsf{E}}(z)dz.

In this case, we define the Manin constant of
\mathsf{E}^{\prime}
to be
c_{\mathsf{E}^{\prime}}=|c^{\prime}|
.
We have

c^{\prime}=\delta c

where
\delta
is an integer which divides
\mathrm{deg}~\psi
.
In particular, given an isogeny class with two
2
-isogenous curves
(\mathsf{E},\mathsf{E}^{\prime})
with
\mathsf{E}
optimal, it is expected that
c_{\mathsf{E}}=1
and that
c_{\mathsf{E}^{\prime}}\in\{1,2\}
.
Numerical computations give plenty of examples to show that both possibilities for
c_{\mathsf{E}^{\prime}}
occur.

Remark 4.5
.

It is conjectured that
c_{\mathsf{E}}=1
for optimal curves or
whenever the rank of
\mathsf{E}
is positive.
For a nice summary of what is currently known in this direction, see
[
ARS06
]
.

Example 4.6
.

Consider the isogeny class
116c
containing the two curves

\displaystyle\mathsf{E}

\displaystyle\colon y^{2}=x^{3}-10x^{2}+29x,

\displaystyle\mathsf{E}^{\prime}

\displaystyle\colon y^{2}=x^{3}+5x^{2}-x.

These curves belong to family
I
with
p=29
; more precisely, they arise as curves A2 and A1, respectively, in
[
Ivo04
, Théorème 2]
.
Using LMFDB, one verifies that these curves have Mordell–Weil rank 0 and

\displaystyle m_{\mathsf{E}}=3\cdot 5,

\displaystyle\quad c_{\mathsf{E}}=1

\displaystyle m_{\mathsf{E}^{\prime}}=2\cdot 3\cdot 5,

\displaystyle\quad c_{\mathsf{E}^{\prime}}=2.

We note that Watkins’ conjecture holds (vacuously) for each of these curves.

Example 4.7
.

Consider the isogeny class
328a
containing the two curves

\displaystyle\mathsf{E}

\displaystyle\colon y^{2}=x^{3}-3x^{2}-8x,

\displaystyle\mathsf{E}^{\prime}

\displaystyle\colon y^{2}=x^{3}+6x^{2}+41x.

These curves belong to family
I
with
p=41
; more precisely, they arise as curves B1 and B2, respectively, in
[
Ivo04
, Théorème 3]
.
Using LMFDB, one verifies that these curves have Mordell–Weil rank 1 and

\displaystyle m_{\mathsf{E}}=2^{3},

\displaystyle\quad c_{\mathsf{E}}=1,

\displaystyle m_{\mathsf{E}^{\prime}}=2^{5},

\displaystyle\quad c_{\mathsf{E}^{\prime}}=1.

Note that Watkins’ conjecture holds for each of these curves, this time for less trivial reasons.

The constants
m_{\mathsf{E}},c_{\mathsf{E}}
, and
\|f\|_{N}^{2}
are all closely related, as we will see in the next section.

4.2.
Quadratic twists of Ivorra curves

Let
\mathsf{E}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}y^{2}=f(x)
be an elliptic curve, then its quadratic twist by an integer
d
is the curve
\mathsf{E}^{(d)}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}dy^{2}=f(x)
.
Given
\mathsf{E}/\mathbb{Q}
, Goldfeld’s Conjecture predicts that
50\%
of its quadratic twists have (analytic) rank 0 and
50\%
of its quadratic twists have rank 1.
The remaining
0\%
(but still infinitely many) of its quadratic twists have rank
\geq 2
.

Proposition 4.8
.

Assume that Goldfeld’s Conjecture is true.
Then Watkins’ Conjecture is true for 100% of the quadratic twists
\mathsf{E}^{(d)}
.

Proof.

Assuming Goldfeld’s Conjecture, 100% of the twists
\mathsf{E}^{(d)}
have analytic rank
\leq 1
.
When the rank is zero, the result is trivial, and when the rank is 1, Watkins’ Conjecture holds by
[
CE09
, Theorem 1.1]
.
∎

We spend the rest of this section giving
unconditional
results in this direction.

Let
f_{\mathsf{E}}
be the modular form associated with the elliptic curve
\mathsf{E}
of conductor
N
.
As in the previous section, we write
\|f_{\mathsf{E}}\|_{N}
for the Petersson norm of this elliptic curve, we write
m_{\mathsf{E}}
for its modular degree, and we write
c_{\mathsf{E}}
for its Manin constant.
Let
q
be an odd prime and
d=\pm q
.
We write
m_{\mathsf{E}^{(d)}},c_{\mathsf{E}^{(d)}}
, and
\|f_{\mathsf{E}^{(d)}}\|_{N^{(d)}}
for the constants associated to the quadratic twist
{\mathsf{E}^{(d)}}
.

Using
[
Del03
, Theorem 1]
, we see that

\frac{\|f_{\mathsf{E}^{(d)}}\|_{N_{\mathsf{E}^{(d)}}}^{2}}{\|f_{\mathsf{E}}\|_{N}^{2}}=2^{k-m}\left(\frac{(q-1)(q+1-a_{q}(\mathsf{E}))(q+1+a_{q}(\mathsf{E}))}{q}\right).

Now, using
[
Car24
, (1)]
and the fact that
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\Delta_{\mathsf{E}^{(d)}}\right\rvert}=q^{6}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\Delta_{\mathsf{E}}\right\rvert}
we deduce that

\displaystyle 2^{k-m}\left(\frac{(q-1)(q+1-a_{q}(\mathsf{E}))(q+1+a_{q}(\mathsf{E}))}{q}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\|f_{\mathsf{E}^{(d)}}\|_{N_{\mathsf{E}^{(d)}}}^{2}}{\|f_{\mathsf{E}}\|_{N}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{m_{\mathsf{E}^{(d)}}}{m_{\mathsf{E}}}\times\frac{c^{2}_{\mathsf{E}}}{c^{2}_{\mathsf{E}^{(d)}}}\times\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\frac{\Delta_{\mathsf{E}}}{\Delta_{\mathsf{E}^{(d)}}}\right\rvert}^{1/6}

\displaystyle=\frac{m_{\mathsf{E}^{(d)}}}{m_{\mathsf{E}}}\times\frac{c^{2}_{\mathsf{E}}}{c^{2}_{\mathsf{E}^{(d)}}}\times\frac{1}{q}

In particular, we have the relation

(4.2)

2^{k-m}{(q-1)(q+1-a_{q}(\mathsf{E}))(q+1+a_{q}(\mathsf{E}))}=\frac{m_{\mathsf{E}^{(d)}}}{m_{\mathsf{E}}}\times\frac{c^{2}_{\mathsf{E}}}{c^{2}_{\mathsf{E}^{(d)}}}.

The ranks of the quadratic twists
\mathsf{E}^{(d)}
are bounded .

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
\mathsf{E}
be any Ivorra curve.
Then for any prime
q
, the quadratic twists
\mathsf{E}^{(q)}
and
\mathsf{E}^{(-q)}
have rank at most 4.

Proof.

Since
\mathsf{E}\colon y^{2}=f(x)
is an Ivorra curve, it has a non-trivial
2
-torsion point
P=(X,0)
with
X\in\mathbb{Q}
.
The quadratic twist is given by
\mathsf{E}^{(d)}\colon dy^{2}=f(x)
, so we also have
P\in\mathsf{E}^{(d)}(\mathbb{Q})[2]\neq 0
.
Since
\mathsf{E}^{(d)}
has additive reduction at
2
and
q
, multiplicative reduction at
p
, and good reduction everywhere else, the result now follows from the bound (
3.2
).
∎

In fact, we can do better when
T\in\{\mathbf{I,II,III,IV,V,VI,VII,XI,XIII,XV}\}
.

Lemma 4.10
.

Let
\mathsf{E}
be an elliptic curve of type
T\in\{\mathbf{I,II,III,IV,V,VI,VII,XI,XIII,XV}\}
.
Then
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathsf{E}^{(d)})\leq 3
where
d\in\{q,-q\}
.

Proof.

Consider the elliptic curves to be written in the form

\displaystyle\mathsf{E}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}y^{2}

\displaystyle=x^{3}+ax^{2}+bx

\displaystyle\mathsf{E}^{\prime}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}y^{2}

\displaystyle=x^{3}-2ax^{2}+(a^{2}-4b)x.

By
[
ALRP08
, Proposition 1.1]
, and the fact that isogenous elliptic curves have the same Mordell–Weil rank, we know that

\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathsf{E}^{\prime})=\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathsf{E})\leq\omega(a^{2}-4b)+\omega(b)-1.

The quadratic twist of
\mathsf{E}
and
\mathsf{E}^{\prime}
by
q
is given by,

\displaystyle\mathsf{E}^{(d)}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}y^{2}

\displaystyle=x^{3}+aqx^{2}+bq^{2}x

\displaystyle\mathsf{E}^{\prime(d)}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}y^{2}

\displaystyle=x^{3}-2aqx^{2}+(a^{2}-4b)q^{2}x.

Once again using
[
ALRP08
, Proposition 1.1]
we obtain

\displaystyle\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathsf{E}^{(d)})

\displaystyle\leq\omega((a^{2}-4b)q^{2})+\omega(bq^{2})-1

\displaystyle=\omega(a^{2}-4b)+1+\omega(b)+1-1

\displaystyle=\omega(a^{2}-4b)+\omega(b)+1.

One may argue similarly for
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathsf{E}^{\prime(d)})
.
The result now follows by direct check (see Table
1
).
∎

We now leverage the relationship given by equation (
4.2
) to prove Watkins’ Conjecture for some quadratic twists of Ivorra curves.
We begin with the families of curves covered by Lemma
4.10
.

Lemma 4.11
.

Let
\mathsf{E}
be an elliptic curve of type
T\in\{\mathbf{I,II,III,IV,V,VI,VII,XI,XIII,XV}\}

and further suppose that
c_{\mathsf{E}}
is equal to 1.
Let
q\geq 5
be a prime of good reduction for
\mathsf{E}
.
Then Watkin’s Conjecture holds for
{\mathsf{E}^{(d)}}
.

Proof.

Since
a_{q}(\mathsf{E})=2\theta
by Lemma
4.2
, we know that

2^{k-m}{(q-1)(q+1-2\theta)(q+1+2\theta)}m_{\mathsf{E}}={m_{\mathsf{E}^{(d)}}}\times\frac{1}{c^{2}_{\mathsf{E}^{(d)}}}.

The LHS of the above equation is divisible by
2^{3}
.
Thus, Watkin’s Conjecture holds for
\mathsf{E}^{(d)}
, since by Lemma
4.10
the rank of
\mathsf{E}^{(d)}
is at most 3.
∎

Lemma 4.12
.

Let
\mathsf{E}
be an elliptic curve of type
T\in\{\mathbf{I,II,III,IV,V,VI,VII,XI,XIII,XV}\}
such that
c_{\mathsf{E}}>1
.
Then Watkin’s Conjecture holds for
{\mathsf{E}^{(d)}}
in the following cases

(i)

q\equiv 1\pmod{8}
is a prime of good reduction of
\mathsf{E}
.

(ii)

q\equiv 1\pmod{4}
is a prime of good reduction of
\mathsf{E}
, the Mordell–Weil rank of
\mathsf{E}
is exactly 1, and Watkin’s Conjecture holds for
\mathsf{E}
.

(iii)

q\equiv 3\pmod{4}
is a prime of good supersingular reduction of
\mathsf{E}
.

Proof.

Recall from Theorem
3.2
that the Mordell–Weil rank is
\leq 1
in this case.
Since
a_{q}(\mathsf{E})=2\theta
, we know that

(q-1)(q+1-2\theta)(q+1+2\theta)m_{\mathsf{E}}={m_{\mathsf{E}^{(d)}}}\times\frac{4}{c^{2}_{\mathsf{E}^{(d)}}}.

On the other hand, when
a_{q}(\mathsf{E})=0
, we know that

(q-1)(q+1)(q+1)m_{\mathsf{E}}={m_{\mathsf{E}^{(d)}}}\times\frac{4}{c^{2}_{\mathsf{E}^{(d)}}}.

In each of the cases, the LHS is divisible by
2^{3}
.
Thus, Watkin’s Conjecture holds for
\mathsf{E}^{(q)}
.
∎

We may use the same technique to deduce Watkins’ Conjecture for twists of other Ivorra curves (such as those appearing in Theorem
3.2
) in many cases.

Lemma 4.13
.

Let
\mathsf{E}
be an elliptic curve of conductor
2^{m}p
with a non-trivial 2-torsion.
Further suppose that
c_{\mathsf{E}}
is equal to 1.
Then Watkin’s Conjecture holds for
{\mathsf{E}^{(d)}}
under either of the following conditions :

(i)

q\geq 5
is a prime of good supersingular reduction of
\mathsf{E}
.

(ii)

q\equiv 1\pmod{4}
is a prime of good ordinary reduction of
\mathsf{E}
.

Proof.

When
a_{q}(\mathsf{E})=0
, we know that

2^{k-m}{(q-1)(q+1)(q+1)}m_{\mathsf{E}}={m_{\mathsf{E}^{(d)}}}\times\frac{1}{c^{2}_{\mathsf{E}^{(d)}}}.

If
q\equiv 1\pmod{4}
then the LHS is divisible by
2^{4}
whereas, if
q\equiv 3\pmod{4}
then the LHS is divisible by
2^{5}
.
In either case, Watkin’s Conjecture holds for
\mathsf{E}^{(d)}
by Lemma
4.9
.

On the other hand, when
a_{q}(\mathsf{E})=2\theta
(but not 0), we know that

{2^{k-m}(q-1)(q+1-2\theta)(q+1+2\theta)}m_{\mathsf{E}}={m_{\mathsf{E}^{(d)}}}\times\frac{1}{c^{2}_{\mathsf{E}^{(d)}}}.

The condition
q\equiv 1\pmod{4}
forces the LHS to be divisible by
2^{4}
, and the result once again follows from Lemma
4.9
.
∎

Putting the lemmas from this section together, we have proven the following theorem.

Theorem 4.14

Appendix A
Missing Cases of (Weaker) Watkins’ Conjecture

A weaker version of Watkins’ conjecture asserts that

(A.1)

2\nmid m_{\mathsf{E}}\Longrightarrow\operatorname{rk}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathsf{E}(\mathbb{Q}))=0.

In the introduction of
[
Car24
]
, Caro claims that this conjecture is largely settled, with a few restrictive cases remaining. We now clarify this claim.

Proposition A.1
.

Let
\mathsf{E}/\mathbb{Q}
be an elliptic curve of conductor
N
and odd modular degree
m_{\mathsf{E}}
.
Then the missing cases of (
A.1
) are when
all
the following conditions are satisfied

•

N
is divisible by at most two odd primes,

•

\mathsf{E}
has additive reduction at
2
, and

•

\mathsf{E}
has a rational point of order
2
.

Proof.

First, since we are assuming that
2\nmid m_{\mathsf{E}}
, it follows from
[
CE09
, Theorem 1.1(1)]
that
N
is divisible by at most two odd primes.

Next, if
4\nmid N
, then
[
ARS12
, Theorem 2.1]
asserts that
m_{\mathsf{E}}
is equal to the congruence number
\delta_{\mathsf{E}}
of
\mathsf{E}
.
Thus
\delta_{\mathsf{E}}
is odd, but then the theorem of
[
KK18
]
implies that
\mathrm{rank}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathsf{E}(\mathbb{Q}))=0
.
So the missing case is when
4\mid N
, hence
\mathsf{E}
has additive reduction at
2
.

These deductions put us in case (a) of
[
CE09
, Theorem 1.1(3)]
, hence
\mathsf{E}(\mathbb{Q})[2]
is non-trivial.

, the left-hand side is always non-positive, whereas the right-hand side certainly takes positive values.
Viewing the right-hand side as a quadratic in
z^{2}
, its discriminant is

4p^{2}a^{2}-4p^{2}(a^{2}-4b)=16b,

so we have
C_{-p}(\mathbb{R})=\emptyset
when
b<0
.
This shows that
-p\notin\mathrm{Sel}^{(\phi)}(\mathsf{E}/\mathbb{Q})
for
T\in\{\mathbf{XVI,XVII}\}
.

\mathbb{Q}_{2}
-obstruction:

\bullet

Consider
T\in\{\mathbf{XVII,XIX}\}
and suppose there is a solution
(w,z)\in C_{\pm p}(\mathbb{Q}_{2})
.
Then
\mathrm{ord}_{2}(pw^{2})
is even, and writing
\mathrm{ord}_{p}(z)=j
we see

\mathrm{ord}_{2}(p^{2}\mp 2paz^{2}+(a^{2}-4b)z^{4})\geq\min\{0,3+\epsilon+2j,3+4j\}

for some nonnegative integer
\epsilon
.
Since
3+4j
is odd, we must have
w,z\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}
.
Reducing mod
8
then implies
w^{2}=p\pmod{8}
, but
1
is the only odd square mod
8
, and by assumption
p\not\equiv 1\pmod{8}
.
Thus
C_{\pm p}(\mathbb{Q}_{2})=\emptyset
for
T\in\{\mathbf{XVII,XVIII,XIX}\}
under the additional hypotheses of the theorem.

\mathbb{Q}_{p}
-obstruction:

\bullet

For
T\in\{\mathbf{XII,XVIII}\}
, the homogeneous spaces are

C_{\pm p}\colon\pm pw^{2}=p^{2}\mp 4p\alpha z^{2}-4z^{4}.

Suppose
(w,z)\in C_{\pm p}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
and let
j=\mathrm{ord}_{p}(z)
.
Then
\mathrm{ord}_{p}(pw^{2})
is odd, while

\mathrm{ord}_{p}(p^{2}\mp 4p\alpha z^{2}-4z^{4})\geq\min\{2,1+2j,4j\},

so we must have
w,z\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
.
Reducing mod
p
shows
z\in p\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, after which reducing mod
p^{2}
shows
w\in p\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, but then we arrive at the contradiction
p^{2}\equiv 0\pmod{p}^{3}
, so
C_{\pm p}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})=\emptyset
.

The same argument shows
C_{\pm 2p}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})=\emptyset
in this case.
We conclude that
\pm p,\pm 2p\notin\mathrm{Sel}^{(\phi)}(\mathsf{E}/\mathbb{Q})
for
T\in\{\mathbf{XII,XVIII}\}
.

(iv)

Group structure obstructions:

Recall that our Selmer groups have been identified with a subgroup of
\mathbb{Q}^{\times}/(\mathbb{Q}^{\times})^{2}
.
It is now possible to use this group structure to eliminate a few more cases, just as in the proof for
T=\mathrm{X}
.
Carrying on in this manner, we complete Table
5
.

There remain some undetermined cells, but nevertheless, we see that in every case we have

\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}\mathrm{Sel}^{(\phi)}(\mathsf{E}/\mathbb{Q})+\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}\mathrm{Sel}^{(\phi^{\prime})}(\mathsf{E}^{\prime}/\mathbb{Q})\leq 3.

So (
B.1
) implies that

\mathrm{rank}_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathsf{E}(\mathbb{Q}),\mathrm{rank}_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathsf{E}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Q})\leq 1.\qed

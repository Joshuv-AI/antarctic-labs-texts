Abstract
. In this article we address the question of characterizing the sequences of complex numbers ( η ) = { η n } n = 0 ∞ (\eta)=\{\eta_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty} whose associated Rhaly operator ℛ ( η ) \mathcal{R}_{(\eta)} is bounded or compact on the Hardy spaces H p H^{p} ( 1 ≤ p < ∞ 1\leq p<\infty ) or on the Bergman spaces A p A^{p} ( 1 ≤ p < ∞ 1\leq p<\infty ). Among other results we completely characterize those ( η ) (\eta) for which ℛ ( η ) \mathcal{R}_{(\eta)} is bounded or compact on H p H^{p} ( 1 < p ≤ 2 1<p\leq 2 ) and on A p A^{p} ( 1 < p < ∞ 1<p<\infty ). We also give conditions on ( η ) (\eta) which are either necessary or sufficient for the boundedness (compactness) of ℛ ( η ) \mathcal{R}_{(\eta)} on H p H^{p} for p = 1 p=1 and 2 < p < ∞ 2<p<\infty . In particular, we prove that if 2 ≤ p < ∞ 2\leq p<\infty and η n = O ⁡ ( 1 n ) \eta_{n}=\og\left(\frac{1}{n}\right) , then ℛ ( η ) \mathcal{R}_{(\eta)} is bounded on H p H^{p} . However, there exists a sequence ( η ) (\eta) with η n = O ⁡ ( 1 n ) \eta_{n}=\og\left(\frac{1}{n}\right) such that the operator ℛ ( η ) \mathcal{R}_{(\eta)} is not bounded on H p H^{p} for 1 ≤ p < 2 1\leq p<2 .

1.
Introduction and main results

If
(\eta)=\{\eta_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
is a sequence of complex
numbers, the
Rhaly matrix

\mathcal{R}_{(\eta)}=\mathcal{R}_{\{\eta_{n}\}}
is defined by

\mathcal{R}_{(\eta)}=\mathcal{R}_{\{\eta_{n}\}}\,=\,\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccccc}\eta_{0}&0&0&0&.&.\\
\eta_{1}&\eta_{1}&0&0&.&.\\
\eta_{2}&\eta_{2}&\eta_{2}&0&.&.\\
\eta_{3}&\eta_{3}&\eta_{3}&\eta_{3}&.&.\\
.&.&.&.&.&.\\
.&.&.&.&.&.\\
\end{array}\right).

This matrix induces the Rhaly operator
\mathcal{R}_{(\eta)}=\mathcal{R}_{\{\eta_{n}\}}
by matrix multiplication, that is, if

(a)=\{a_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
is a sequence of complex numbers

\mathcal{R}_{(\eta)}(a)\,=\,\mathcal{R}_{\{\eta_{n}\}}(a)\,=\,\left\{\eta_{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n}a_{k}\right\}_{n=0}^{\infty}.

The case when

\eta_{n}=\frac{1}{n+1}
for all
n
corresponds to the classical
Cesàro operator
\mathcal{C}
which is known to be bounded from

\ell^{p}
to
\ell^{p}
for
1<p\leq\infty
. This was proved by Hardy

[
21
]
and Landau
[
25
]
(see also
[
22
, Theorem 326,
p.239 ]
). The sequences
\{\eta_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
for which
the operator
\mathcal{R}_{\{\eta_{n}\}}
is either bounded or
compact on the space
\ell^{p}
for any
p\in(1,\infty)
have been
characterized in
[
19
]
. A characterization of the sequences

\{\eta_{n}\}
for which
\mathcal{R}_{\{\eta_{n}\}}
belongs to the
Schatten class
\mathcal{S}^{q}(\ell^{2})
(
1<q<\infty
) has been
obtained in
[
4
]
.

Let
{\mathbb{D}}=\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}:|z|<1\}
denote the open unit disc in the complex
plane
{\mathbb{C}}
. If
\mu
is a complex Borel measure in
\mathbb{D}
and,
for
n=0,1,2,\dots
,
\mu_{n}
denotes the moment of order
n
of

\mu
, that is,

\mu_{n}\,=\,\int_{\mathbb{D}}w^{n}\,d\mu(w),\quad n=0,1,2,\dots,

the operator
\mathcal{R}_{\{\mu_{n}\}}
will be denoted by
\mathcal{C}_{\mu}
. When
\mu
is the Lebesgue measure on the radius
[0,1)
the operator
\mathcal{C}_{\mu}
is the Cesàro operator

\mathcal{C}
.

Let
\rm{Hol}({\mathbb{D}})
be the space of all analytic functions in
{\mathbb{D}}

endowed with the topology of uniform convergence in compact subsets.

If
\,0<r<1\,
and
\,f\in\rm{Hol}({\mathbb{D}})
, we set

M_{p}(r,f)=\left(\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|f(re^{it})|^{p}\,dt\right)^{1/p},\,\,\,0<p<\infty,

M_{\infty}(r,f)=\sup_{|z|=r}|f(z)|.

For
\,0<p\leq\infty
,  the Hardy space
H^{p}
consists of those

f\in\rm{Hol}(\mathbb{D})
such that

\|f\|_{H^{p}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\sup_{0<r<1}M_{p}(r,f)<\infty.

We refer to
[
11
]
for the
notation and results regarding Hardy spaces.

Let
dA
denote the area measure on
\mathbb{D}
, normalized so that
the area of
\mathbb{D}
is
1
. Thus
dA(z)\,=\,\frac{1}{\pi}\,dx\,dy\,=\,\frac{1}{\pi}\,r\,dr\,d\theta
. For
0<p<\infty

and
\alpha>-1
the weighted Bergman space
A^{p}_{\alpha}
consists
of those
f\in\rm{Hol}(\mathbb{D})
such that

\|f\|_{A^{p}_{\alpha}}\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\,\left(\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f(z)|^{p}\,dA_{\alpha}(z)\right)^{1/p}\,<\,\infty,

where

dA_{\alpha}(z)=(\alpha+1)(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha}dA(z)
. We
refer to
[
12
,
23
,
43
]
for the notation and results about
Bergman spaces.

If
0<p<\infty
and
\alpha>-1
the space of Dirichlet type
{\mathcal{D}^{p}_{\alpha}}

consists of all primitives of functions in
A^{p}_{\alpha}
. Hence, if

f
is analytic in
\mathbb{D}
, then
f\in{\mathcal{D}^{p}_{\alpha}}
if and only if

\|f\|_{{\mathcal{D}^{p}_{\alpha}}}^{p}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}|f(0)|^{p}+\|f^{\prime}\|_{A^{p}_{\alpha}}^{p}<\infty.

Identifying any given function
f\in\rm{Hol}(\mathbb{D})

with the sequence
\{a_{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
of its Taylor
coefficients, the Cesàro operator
\mathcal{C}
and the operators

\mathcal{C}_{\mu}
associated to complex Borel measures on
\mathbb{D}
become linear operators from
\rm{Hol}(\mathbb{D})
into itself as
follows:

If
f\in\rm{Hol}(\mathbb{D})
,
f(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}z^{k}

(
z\in\mathbb{D}
), then

\mathcal{C}(f)(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{n+1}\sum_{k=0}^{n}a_{k}\right)z^{n},\quad\mathcal{C}_{\mu}(f)(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\mu_{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n}a_{k}\right)z^{n},\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

The Cesàro operator is known to be
bounded on the Hardy spaces
H^{p}
(
0<p<\infty
) and on the Bergman
spaces

(

,

). This has been
established by a number of authors using different methods

[

,
31
,
32
,
35
,
36
,
37
,
38
]
.

The operators
\mathcal{C}_{\mu}
with
\mu
being a finite
positive Borel measure supported on the radius

were

\mu_{n}=\og\left(\frac{1}{n+1}\right)
. Theorem 9 of

shows that

A^{1}_{\alpha}
(

). Since
its introduction, the boundedness of these operators
\mathcal{C}_{\mu}
on other spaces of analytic functions in

has been
studied by distinct authors (see, e. g.,
[

,

,

,

,

Blasco
[

considered the operators
\mathcal{C}_{\mu}
, with
\mu
being a complex Borel measure on
[0,1)
,
acting on Hardy spaces. Later on, a good amount of work has been
carried out considering the operators
\mathcal{C}_{\mu}
, where
\mu
is a complex Borel measure on
\mathbb{D}
, acting on distinct
subspaces of
\rm{Hol}(\mathbb{D})
; see, for instance,

[

,

,

.

Let us remark that when
\mu
is a complex Borel
measure on
\mathbb{D}
, the operator
\mathcal{C}_{\mu}
has an
integral representation: If
f\in\rm{Hol}(\mathbb{D})
,

f(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}z^{k}
(
z\in\mathbb{D}
), then

(1.1)

\mathcal{C}_{\mu}(f)(z)\,=\,\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mu_{n}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n}a_{k}\right)z^{n}\,=\,\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{f(wz)}{1-wz}\,d\mu(w),\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

This plays a crucial role to obtain a good number of the results we
have just mentioned.

If
(\eta)=\{\eta_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
is a sequence of complex
numbers then the Rhaly operator
\mathcal{R}_{\{\eta_{n}\}}
can be
viewed formally as an operator acting on spaces of analytic
functions in the disc: If
f\in\rm{Hol}(\mathbb{D})
,
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
(
z\in\mathbb{D}
), formally, we set

\mathcal{R}_{(\eta)}(f)(z)\,=\,\mathcal{R}_{\{\eta_{n}\}}(f)(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\eta_{n}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n}a_{k}\right)z^{n},\quad z\in\mathbb{D},

whenever the
right hand side makes sense and defines an analytic function in

\mathbb{D}
.

The sequence
(\eta)=\{\eta_{n}\}
may not be
bounded. Hence, in contrary to what happens with the operators

\mathcal{C}_{\mu}
, we cannot assure that the operator
\mathcal{R}_{\{\eta_{n}\}}
is a well defined operator from
\rm{Hol}(\mathbb{D})

into itself. Proposition 1 of
[
8
]
asserts that if
X
is
a subspace of
\rm{Hol}(\mathbb{D})
which contains the constants, then

\mathcal{R}_{\{\eta_{n}\}}
is a well defined linear operator from

X
into
\rm{Hol}(\mathbb{D})
if and only if the power series

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\eta_{n}z^{n}
defines an analytic function in

\mathbb{D}
.

From now on, if the power series
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\eta_{n}z^{n}
defines an
analytic function in
\mathbb{D}
, we shall set

F_{(\eta)}(z)\,=\,F_{\{\eta_{n}\}}(z)\,=\,\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\eta_{n}z^{n},\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

In view of the identification of
H^{2}
and
\ell^{2}
,
the sequences
(\eta)
for which
\mathcal{R}_{(\eta)}
is bounded
or compact on
H^{2}
are characterized in
[
19
]
. The articles

[
2
,
8
,
14
]
contain characterizations of the
sequences
(\eta)
for which the operator
\mathcal{R}_{(\eta)}
is
bounded (compact) from
\mathcal{D}^{2}_{\alpha}
into
\mathcal{D}^{2}_{\beta}
(
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
). Here, for
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
,
\mathcal{D}^{2}_{\alpha}
is the space of functions

f\in\rm{Hol}(\mathbb{D})
such that
|a_{0}|^{2}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{1-\alpha}|a_{n}|^{2}<\infty
, where
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
.

When we deal with a general Rhaly operator
\mathcal{R}_{(\eta)}
,
we do not have an integral representation similar to (
1.1
).
This is one reason why a characterization of those sequences
(\eta)
for which
\mathcal{R}_{(\eta)}
is bounded (compact) on
H^{p}
or
on
A^{p}_{\alpha}
with

has not been obtained yet. In this
paper we address the question of characterizing such sequences for

1<p<\infty
. For simplicity, if
X
and
Y
are two Banach spaces,

\mathcal{B}(X,Y)
will denote the space of continuous linear
operators from
X
into
Y
and
\mathcal{K}(X,Y)
will stand for the
space of all compact linear operators from
X
into
Y
. Also

\mathcal{B}(X)
will stand for
\mathcal{B}(X,X)
and
\mathcal{K}(X)

will stand for
\mathcal{K}(X,X)
. Our main results are the
following.

Theorem 1
.

Let
(\eta)=\{\eta_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
be a
sequence of complex numbers.

(a) If
1<p\leq 2
, then
\mathcal{R}_{(\eta)}\in\mathcal{B}(H^{p})
if and only if
F_{(\eta)}\in\Lambda^{p}_{1/p}
.

(b) If
2<p<\infty
and
\mathcal{R}_{(\eta)}\in\mathcal{B}(H^{p})
, then
F_{(\eta)}\in\Lambda^{p}_{1/p}
.

(c) If
2<p<\infty
and
F_{(\eta)}\in\Lambda^{q}_{1/q}
for some

q\in(2,p)
, then
\mathcal{R}_{(\eta)}\in\mathcal{B}(H^{p})
.

Theorem 2
.

Let
(\eta)=\{\eta_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
be a
sequence of complex numbers.

(a) If
1<p\leq 2
then
\mathcal{R}_{(\eta)}\in\mathcal{K}(H^{p})
if and only if
F_{(\eta)}\in\lambda^{p}_{1/p}
.

(b) If
2<p<\infty
and
\mathcal{R}_{(\eta)}\in\mathcal{K}(H^{p})
, then
F_{(\eta)}\in\lambda^{p}_{1/p}
.

(c) If
2<p<\infty
and
F_{(\eta)}\in\lambda^{q}_{1/q}
for some

q\in(2,p)
, then
\mathcal{R}_{(\eta)}\in\mathcal{K}(H^{p})
.

Theorem 3
.

Let
(\eta)=\{\eta_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
be a
sequence of complex numbers and
1<p<\infty
. Then:

(a)

\mathcal{R}_{(\eta)}\in\mathcal{B}(A^{p})
if and only if

F_{(\eta)}\in\Lambda^{p}_{1/p}
.

(b)
\mathcal{R}_{(\eta)}\in\mathcal{K}(A^{p})
if and only if
F_{(\eta)}\in\lambda^{p}_{1/p}
.

Since the Cesàro operator is bounded on
H^{p}
for
0<p<\infty
, it is natural to ask whether or not the condition
\eta_{n}\,=\,\og\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)
implies that
\mathcal{R}_{(\eta)}\in\mathcal{B}(H^{p})
for some (all)
p
. We shall prove
the following result.

Theorem 4
.

(i) Suppose that
2\leq p<\infty
and let
(\eta)=\{\eta_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
be a sequence of
complex numbers. If
\eta_{n}\,=\,\og\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)
,
as
n\to\infty
, then
\mathcal{R}_{(\eta)}\in\mathcal{B}(H^{p})
.

(ii) There exists a sequence of complex numbers
(\eta)=\{\eta_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
, with
\eta_{n}\,=\,\og\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)
, as
n\to\infty
, such that
\mathcal{R}_{(\eta)}\not\in\mathcal{B}(H^{p})
for
1\leq p<2
.

(iii) If
1<p<\infty
and
(\eta)=\{\eta_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
is a decreasing sequence of non negative real numbers then

\mathcal{R}_{(\eta)}\in\mathcal{B}(H^{p})
if and only if

\eta_{n}\,=\,\og\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)
, as
n\to\infty
.

2.
Preliminary definitions and results

In
this section we are going to present a number of spaces and results
which will be needed to prove the theorems stated in
section
1
. Let start fixing some notation. If
f\in\rm{Hol}(\mathbb{D})
,
f(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}z^{k}
(
z\in\mathbb{D})
, we set

(2.1)

S_{n,m}f(z)\,=\,\sum_{k=n}^{m}a_{k}z^{k},\quad 0\leq n<m<\infty.

(2.2)

\Delta_{N}f(z)\,=\,S_{N,2N-1}f(z)\,=\,\sum_{k=N}^{2N-1}a_{k}z^{k},\quad 1\leq N<\infty.

(2.3)

S_{N}f(z)\,=\,\sum_{k=0}^{N}a_{k}z^{k},\quad 0\leq N<\infty.

2.1.
Mean Lipschitz spaces

For
1\leq p\leq\infty
and
0<\alpha\leq 1
, we let
\Lambda^{p}_{\alpha}
be the space of those
f\in\rm{Hol}(\mathbb{D})
having a
non-tangential limit at almost every point of
\partial\mathbb{D}

and so that
\omega_{p}(\cdot,f)
, the integral modulus of
continuity of order
p
of the boundary values
f(e^{i\theta})
of

f
, satisfies
\omega_{p}(\delta,f)=\og(\delta^{\alpha})
, as

\delta\to 0
. When
p=\infty
we write
\Lambda_{\alpha}
instead
of
\Lambda^{\infty}_{\alpha}
. This is the usual space of order

\alpha
. More precisely, a function
f\in\rm{Hol}(\mathbb{D})

belongs to
\Lambda_{\alpha}
if and only if it has a continuous
extension to the closed unit disc and its boundary values satisfy a
Lipschitz condition of order
\alpha
.

If
0<\alpha\leq 1
,

1\leq p\leq\infty
, and
f\in\rm{Hol}(\mathbb{D})
, we set

\beta_{p,\alpha}(r,f)\,=\,(1-r)^{1-\alpha}M_{p}(r,f^{\prime}),\quad 0<r<1.

Classical results of Hardy and Littlewood (see
[
10
]
and

[
11
, Chapter 5]
) show that whenever
0<\alpha\leq 1
and

1\leq p\leq\infty
we have that
\Lambda^{p}_{\alpha}\subset H^{p}

and that

(2.4)

\Lambda^{p}_{\alpha}\,=\,\left\{\hbox{$f$ analytic
in ${\mathbb{D}}$: $M_{p}(r,f^{\prime})=\og\left((1-r)^{\alpha-1}\right)$, \quad as $r\to 1$}\right\}.

The
corresponding “little oh”  spaces are denoted by

\lambda^{p}_{\alpha}
.

If
1<p<\infty
and
(1/p)<\alpha\leq 1
, then

\Lambda^{p}_{\alpha}\subset\Lambda_{\alpha-{(1/p)}}
and, hence,
each function in
\Lambda^{p}_{\alpha}
has a continuous extension to
the closed unit disc
[
10
, p. 88]
. This is not true for the
space
\Lambda^{p}_{1/p}
. This follows easily noticing that the
function
f(z)=\log(1-z)
is an unbounded function which belongs to

\Lambda_{1/p}^{p}
for all
p\in(1,\infty)
. Bourdon, Shapiro,
and Sledd
[
10
]
proved that
\Lambda^{p}_{1/p}\subset BMOA
for
all
p\in(1,\infty)
. This inclusion was shown to be sharp in a
very strong sense in
[
9
]
. Let us remark also that the

\Lambda^{p}_{1/p}
-spaces form a nested scale of spaces

(2.5)

\Lambda^{q}_{1/q}\,\subset\,\Lambda^{p}_{1/p}\quad 1\leq q<p<\infty.

For
1\leq p\leq\infty
and
0<\alpha\leq 1
, the space
\Lambda^{p}_{\alpha}
is a Banach space with the norm
\|\cdot\|_{p,\alpha}
defined by

\|f\|_{p,\alpha}\,=\,|f(0)|\,+\,\beta_{p,\alpha}(f),

where

\beta_{p,\alpha}(f)\,=\,\sup_{0<r<1}(1-r)^{1-\alpha}M_{p}(r,f^{\prime})\,=\,\sup_{0<r<1}\beta_{p,\alpha}(r,f).

Let us recall several
distinct characterizations of
\Lambda^{p}_{\alpha}
spaces, (see

[
10
]
,
[
11
]
,
[
18
]
, and
[
29
]
).

Theorem A
.

Suppose that
1<p<\infty
,
0<\alpha<1
and
g\in\rm{Hol}({\mathbb{D}})
. The
following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

g\in\Lambda^{p}_{\alpha}.

(ii)

M_{p}(r,g^{\prime})=\ogr\left(\frac{1}{(1-r)^{1-\alpha}}\right)
, as
r\to 1^{-}
.

(iii)

\|\Delta_{N}g\|_{H^{p}}=\ogr\left(N^{-\alpha}\right)
, as
N\to\infty
.

(iv)

\|\Delta_{N}g^{\prime}\|_{H^{p}}=\ogr\left(N^{(1-\alpha)}\right)
, as
N\to\infty
.

In fact,

\beta_{p,\alpha}(f)\,\asymp\,\sup_{N\geq 1}N^{\alpha}\|\Delta_{N}g\|_{H^{p}}\,\asymp\,\sup_{N\geq 1}N^{\alpha-1}\|\Delta_{N}g^{\prime}\|_{H^{p}}.

Remark 1
.

The corresponding results for the little-oh space
\lambda^{p}_{\alpha}
remain true, and they can be proved following the proofs in the
references for Theorem
A
.

The following result will be needed in our work. Since we have not
found any reference to it in the literature, we will include a
proof.

Proposition 1
.

Suppose that
1<p<\infty
,
0<\alpha\leq 1
, and
f\in\lambda^{p}_{\alpha}
. Then:

(i) Defining, for
0<r<1
,
f_{r}
by

f_{r}(z)=f(rz)
(
|z|\leq 1
), we have that

\beta_{p,\alpha}(f-f_{r})\,\rightarrow 0,\quad\text{as $r\to 1$}.

(ii)

S_{N}f\,\rightarrow f,\,\,\text{as $N\to\infty$, in the norm of
$\Lambda^{p}_{\alpha}$}.

Proof.
Let us start proving (i). Take a non-constant
f\in\lambda^{p}_{\alpha}
. Take
\varepsilon>0
with
0<\varepsilon<3\beta_{p,\alpha}(f).
Since
f\in\lambda^{p}_{\alpha}
, there exists
s_{0}\in(0,1)

such that

(2.6)

(1-s)^{1-\alpha}M_{p}(s,f^{\prime})\,<\frac{\varepsilon}{3},\quad s_{0}\leq s<1.

For

r,s\in(0,1)
, we have

(2.7)

\displaystyle(1-s)^{1-\alpha}M_{p}(s,f^{\prime}-f^{\prime}_{r})\,=\,(1-s)^{1-\alpha}\left[\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left|f^{\prime}(se^{i\theta})-rf^{\prime}(rse^{i\theta})\right|^{p}\,d\theta\right]^{1/p}

\displaystyle=\,

\displaystyle(1-s)^{1-\alpha}\left[\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left|f^{\prime}(se^{i\theta})-rf^{\prime}(se^{i\theta})+rf^{\prime}(se^{i\theta})-rf^{\prime}(rse^{i\theta})\right|^{p}\,d\theta\right]^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq\,

\displaystyle(1-r)(1-s)^{1-\alpha}M_{p}(s,f^{\prime})\,+\,r(1-s)^{1-\alpha}\left[\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left|f^{\prime}(se^{i\theta})-f^{\prime}(rse^{i\theta})\right|^{p}\,d\theta\right]^{1/p}.

Using this, (
2.6
), and the fact that
M_{p}(rs,f^{\prime})\leq M_{p}(s,f^{\prime})
(
0<s,r<1
), we obtain

\displaystyle(1-s)^{1-\alpha}M_{p}(s,f^{\prime}-f^{\prime}_{r})\,\leq\,

\displaystyle(1-s)^{1-\alpha}M_{p}(s,f^{\prime})+\,(1-s)^{1-\alpha}\left[M_{p}(s,f^{\prime})\,+\,M_{p}(rs,f^{\prime})\right]

(2.8)

\displaystyle\leq\,

\displaystyle 3(1-s)^{1-\alpha}M_{p}(s,f^{\prime})

\displaystyle<\,

\displaystyle\varepsilon,\qquad s_{0}<s<1.

Since
f^{\prime}
is uniformly continuous in the closed disc
\{z\in\mathbb{D}:|z|\leq s_{0}\}
, there exists
\delta\in(0,1)
such that

(2.9)

|f^{\prime}(z)-f^{\prime}(w)|<\frac{2\varepsilon}{3},\,\,\text{if $|z|\leq s_{0},\,|w|\leq s_{0}$,\,\,\,and $|z-w|\leq\delta$}.

Take
r_{0}\in(0,1)
with

r_{0}>\max\left(1-\delta,1-\frac{\varepsilon}{3\beta_{p,\alpha}(f)}\right).

Using (
2.7
) and (
2.9
), we see that, if
0<s\leq s_{0}
and
r_{0}<r<1
, then

(1-s)^{1-\alpha}M_{p}(s,f^{\prime}-f^{\prime}_{r})\leq\,(1-r)\beta_{p,\alpha}(f)\,+\,(1-s)^{1-\alpha}\frac{2\varepsilon}{3}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{3\beta_{p,\alpha}(f)}\beta_{p,\alpha}(f)+\,\frac{2\varepsilon}{3}=\,\varepsilon.

This and
(
2.1
) give that
\beta_{p,\alpha}(f-f_{r})<\varepsilon
, if

r_{0}<r<1
. Hence, (i) is proved.

In order to prove (ii), we shall start proving the
following weaker result.

Lemma 1
.

Suppose that

1<p<\infty
,
0<\alpha\leq 1
, and
g\in\lambda^{p}_{\alpha}
. Then

g
is the limit in
\Lambda^{p}_{\alpha}
of a sequence of
polynomials.

Proof.
Take
g\in\lambda^{p}_{\alpha}
.
Set
r_{n}=1-\frac{1}{n}
(
n=2,3,\dots
) and consider the sequence
of functions
\{g_{r_{n}}\}_{n=2}^{\infty}
. For every
n\geq 2
,

g_{r_{n}}
is holomorphic in
\{|z|\leq\frac{1}{r_{n}}\}

and, hence, there exists a polynomial
P_{n}
with
P_{n}(0)=g(0)
such
that

|g^{\prime}_{r_{n}}(z)\,-\,P^{\prime}_{n}(z)|\leq\frac{1}{n},\quad|z|\leq 1.

Clearly, this implies that

\beta_{p,\alpha}(g_{r_{n}}-P_{n})\leq\frac{1}{n},\quad n\geq 2.

Using this and (i) we see that

\beta_{p,\alpha}(g-P_{n})\,\leq\beta_{p,\alpha}(g-g_{r_{n}})\,+\,\beta_{p,\alpha}(g_{r_{n}}-P_{n})\,\to 0,\quad\text{as $n\to\infty$}.

Thus,
P_{n}\,\to g
, as
n\to\infty
, in the norm of
\Lambda^{p}_{\alpha}
.

\Box

Bearing in mind Lemma
1
and Proposition
1 of
[
42
]
, (ii) will follow if we prove that there
exists a positive constant
C
such that

(2.10)

\beta_{p,\alpha}(S_{n}f)\leq C\beta_{p,\alpha}(f),\,\text{
for all
$f\in\Lambda^{p}_{\alpha}$ and all $n\in\mathbb{N}$.}

Since
1<p<\infty
, using the Riesz projection theorem, it
follows that the operators
S_{n}
are uniformly bounded on
H^{p}
,
that is, there exists
A>0
such that

(2.11)

\|S_{n}f\|_{H^{p}}\leq A\|f\|_{H^{p}},\quad(n\in\mathbb{N},\,\,f\in H^{p}).

Take now

f\in\Lambda^{p}_{\alpha}
,
f(z)\,=\,\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}z^{k}

(
z\in\mathbb{D}
). Notice that for
n\in\mathbb{N}
we have

\left(S_{n}f\right)^{\prime}(z)\,=\,\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(k+1)a_{k+1}z^{k}\,=\,S_{n-1}(f^{\prime})(z),\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

Then, using (
2.11
), we see that

\displaystyle\beta_{p,\alpha}(S_{n}f)\,=\,\sup_{0<r<1}(1-r)^{1-\alpha}M_{p}(r,(S_{n}f)^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\,

\displaystyle\sup_{0<r<1}(1-r)^{1-\alpha}M_{p}(r,S_{n-1}(f^{\prime}))\,\leq A\sup_{0<r<1}(1-r)^{1-\alpha}M_{p}(r,f^{\prime})\,=\,A\beta_{p,\alpha}(f).

Hence, (
2.10
)
holds with
C=A
. This finishes the proof.

\Box

2.2.
Convolution of analytic functions

If
f

and
g
are two analytic functions in the unit disc,

f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n},\quad g(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{n}z^{n},\quad z\in\mathbb{D},

the convolution
f\star g
of
f
and

g
is defined by

(2.12)

f\star g(z)\,=\,\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}b_{n}z^{n},\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

We have

(2.13)

f\star g(\rho re^{i\theta})\,=\,\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}f(\rho e^{it})g(re^{i(\theta-t)})\,dt,\quad 0<\rho<1,\,\,0<r<1,\,\,\theta\in\mathbb{R}.

It is well known that the convolution
of a function in
H^{1}
and another one in
H^{p}
(
p\geq 1
) lies in

H^{p}
and that

\|f\star g\|_{H^{p}}\leq\|f\|_{H^{1}}\|g\|_{H^{p}},\quad p\geq 1,f\in H^{1},g\in H^{p}.

Actually, (
2.13
)
implies that if
f\in H^{1}
and
g\in\rm{Hol}(\mathbb{D})
, then

(2.14)

M_{p}(r,f\star g)\,\leq\|f\|_{H^{1}}M_{p}(r,g),\quad 0<r<1.

Also, taking
\rho=r
in (
2.13
), we obtain

(2.15)

M_{p}(r^{2},f\star g)\,\leq M_{1}(r,f)M_{p}(r,g),\quad f,g\in\rm{Hol}(\mathbb{D}),\,\,0<r<1.

We shall use also the following elementary result.

Lemma 2
.

Suppose that
\alpha>-1
,
1\leq p<\infty
,
f\in H^{p}
, and
g\in A^{p}_{\alpha}
. Then

\|f\star g\|_{A^{p}_{\alpha}}\,\lesssim\|f\|_{H^{p}}\|g\|_{A^{p}_{\alpha}}.

Proof.

\displaystyle\|f\ast g\|_{A^{p}_{\alpha}}^{p}

\displaystyle\asymp\int_{0}^{1}(1-r)^{\alpha}M_{p}^{p}(r,f\ast g)\,dr\,\lesssim\int_{0}^{1}(1-r)^{\alpha}\|f\|_{H^{1}}^{p}M_{p}^{p}(r,g)\,dr

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{H^{p}}^{p}\int_{0}^{1}(1-r)^{\alpha}M_{p}^{p}(r,g)\,dr\,\asymp\|f\|_{H^{p}}^{p}\|g\|_{A^{p}_{\alpha}}^{p}.

\Box

2.3.
“Lipschitz” Cesàro
bases

Induced by the work of Pavlović

[
34
, pp.  140-143]
who constructed “smooth”  Cesàro bases, next we are going to construct
“Lipschitz” Cesàro bases.

Let
\Psi:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be a Lipschitz function with compact
support. For simplicity, we shall assume that the support of
\Psi

is contained in
[0,4]
. We set

\mathcal{L}(\psi)\,=\,\sup_{s\neq t}\frac{|\Psi(t)-\Psi(s)|}{|t-s|}.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
we define

W_{n}^{\Psi}(e^{it})\,=\,\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\Psi\left(\frac{k}{n}\right)e^{ik\theta}\,=\,\sum_{k=0}^{4n}\Psi\left(\frac{k}{n}\right)e^{ik\theta}.

Arguing as in the proof of
Lemma 5.2 in p. 141 of
[
34
]
with
N=2
, we have

(2.16)

\displaystyle\,(1-e^{i\theta})^{2}W_{n}^{\Psi}(e^{i\theta})\,=\,\sum_{k=0}^{4n}\Psi\left(\frac{k}{n}\right)(1-e^{i\theta})^{2}e^{ik\theta}\,=\,\sum_{k=0}^{4n}\Psi\left(\frac{k}{n}\right)\sum_{m=0}^{2}\binom{2}{m}(-1)^{m}e^{i(m+k)\theta}

\displaystyle=\,

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{2}(-1)^{m}\binom{2}{m}\sum_{k=0}^{4n}\Psi\left(\frac{k}{n}\right)e^{i(m+k)}\theta\,=\,\sum_{m=0}^{2}(-1)^{m}\binom{2}{m}\sum_{j=0}^{4n+2}\Psi\left(\frac{j-m}{n}\right)e^{ij\theta}

\displaystyle=\,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{4n+2}\left[\sum_{m=0}^{2}(-1)^{m}\binom{2}{m}\Psi\left(\frac{j-m}{n}\right)\right]e^{ij\theta}.

Now,

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{m=0}^{2}(-1)^{m}\binom{2}{m}\Psi\left(\frac{j-m}{n}\right)\right|=

\displaystyle\left|\Psi\left(\frac{j}{n}\right)-\Psi\left(\frac{j-1}{n}\right)-\Psi\left(\frac{j-1}{n}\right)+\Psi\left(\frac{j-2}{n}\right)\right|\leq\frac{2\mathcal{L}(\psi)}{n}.

Using this in (
2.16
), we obtain

(2.17)

\displaystyle\left|(1-e^{i\theta})^{2}W_{n}^{\Psi}(e^{i\theta})\right|\leq\frac{2\mathcal{L}(\Psi)}{n}\sum_{j=0}^{4n+2}|e^{ij\theta}|\leq\frac{2\mathcal{L}(\Psi)}{n}(4n+3)\leq 14\mathcal{L}(\Psi).

3.
Rhaly operators acting on Hardy spaces

Let us start proving that, for
1<p<\infty
, the
condition
F_{(\eta)}\in\Lambda^{p}_{1/p}
(respectively,
F_{(\eta)}\in\lambda^{p}_{1/p}
) is a necessary condition for the membership
of
\mathcal{R}_{(\eta)}
in
\mathcal{B}(H^{p})
(respectively, in

\mathcal{K}(H^{p}))
.

Proof.
Assume that
1<p<\infty

and
\mathcal{R}_{(\eta)}\in\mathcal{B}(H^{p})
. As observed in
section
1
this implies that
F_{(\eta)}
is a well
defined analytic function in
\mathbb{D}
. In fact, since
F_{(\eta)}=\mathcal{R}_{(\eta)}(1)
,
F_{(\eta)}\in H^{p}
.

For
N=2,3,\dots
, set
a_{N}=1-\frac{1}{N}
and

(3.1)

f_{N}(z)\,=\,\frac{1}{N^{2-\frac{1}{p}}}\frac{a_{N}z}{(1-a_{N}z)^{2}}\,=\,\frac{1}{N^{2-\frac{1}{p}}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}na_{N}^{n}z^{n},\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

We have that
f_{N}\in H^{p}
for all
N
and

(3.2)

\|f_{N}\|_{H^{p}}\lesssim 1.

Since
\mathcal{R}_{(\eta)}\in\mathcal{B}(H^{p})
,

(3.3)

\|\mathcal{R}_{(\eta)}(f_{N})\|_{H^{p}}\lesssim\|f_{N}\|_{H^{p}}.

Now,

\mathcal{R}_{(\eta)}(f_{N})(z)\,=\,\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\eta_{k}\left(\frac{1}{N^{2-\frac{1}{p}}}\sum_{n=1}^{k}na_{N}^{n}\right)z^{k}\,=\,\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\eta_{k}k\left(\frac{1}{kN^{2-\frac{1}{p}}}\sum_{n=1}^{k}na_{N}^{n}\right)z^{k}.

For

k,N\in\mathbb{N}
, set

(3.4)

\alpha_{k,N}\,=\,\frac{1}{kN^{2-\frac{1}{p}}}\sum_{n=1}^{k}na_{N}^{n},\quad\beta_{k,N}\,=\,\frac{1}{\alpha_{k,N}}.

Then

(3.5)

\mathcal{R}_{(\eta)}(f_{N})(z)\,=\,\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\eta_{k}k\alpha_{k,N}z^{k},\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

Let us point out some properties of the
\alpha_{k,N}
’s and
the
\beta_{k,N}
’s.

(3.6)

0\,\leq\,\alpha_{k,N}\,\leq\,\frac{1}{N^{2-\frac{1}{p}}}\frac{1}{k}\sum_{n=1}^{k}n\,=\,\frac{1}{N^{2-\frac{1}{p}}}\frac{k+1}{2}.

(3.7)

\alpha_{k,N}\geq\frac{1}{N^{2-\frac{1}{p}}}a_{N}^{k}\frac{1}{k}\sum_{n=1}^{k}n\,=\,\frac{a_{N}^{k}}{N^{2-\frac{1}{p}}}\frac{k+1}{2}.

Using (
3.6
) and (
3.7
), we see that there exist two
positive constants
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
such that

(3.8)

\frac{C_{1}}{N^{1-\frac{1}{p}}}\,\leq\,\alpha_{k,N}\,\leq\,\frac{C_{2}}{N^{1-\frac{1}{p}}},\quad N=,2,3,\dots,\quad N\leq k\leq 2N.

We have also, for all
k,N
,

\displaystyle|\alpha_{k+1,N}-\alpha_{k,N}|\,=\,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N^{2-\frac{1}{p}}}\left|\frac{1}{k+1}\sum_{n=1}^{k+1}na_{N}^{n}\,-\,\frac{1}{k}\sum_{n=1}^{k}a_{N}^{n}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N^{2-\frac{1}{p}}}\left[\frac{1}{k(k+1)}\sum_{n=1}^{k}n\,+\,1\right]

\displaystyle\leq\,

\displaystyle\frac{3}{2N^{2-\frac{1}{p}}}.

Using this, (
3.6
), (
3.7
), and (
3.8
), we
deduce that

(3.9)

\displaystyle|\beta_{k+1,N}-\beta_{k,N}|\,=\,\frac{|\alpha_{k+1,N}-\alpha_{k,N}|}{|\alpha_{k+1,N}\alpha_{k,N}|}\,\lesssim N^{-\frac{1}{p}},\quad N\leq k\leq 2N,

and then, we obtain,

(3.10)

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\beta_{k+1,N}-\beta_{k,N}}{\frac{k+1}{N}-\frac{k}{N}}\right|\,=\,N\left|\beta_{k+1,N}-\beta_{k,N}\right|\,\lesssim N^{1-\frac{1}{p}},\quad N\leq k\leq 2N.

For each
N
, let
\Psi_{N}:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be
defined as follows:

•

\Psi_{N}(x)=0
, if
x\leq 0
.

•

\Psi_{N}(x)=0
, if
x\geq 4
.

•

Between
0
and
4
, the graph of
\Psi_{N}
is the polygonal
whose vertices are the points

(0,0),\,\left(\frac{N}{N},\beta_{N,N}\right),\,\left(\frac{N+1}{N},\beta_{N+1,N}\right),\,\left(\frac{N+2}{N},\beta_{N+2,N}\right),\,\dots,\left(\frac{2N}{N},\beta_{2N,N}\right),\,(4,0).

Using
(
3.10
), we see that
\Psi_{N}
is a Lipschitz function and
that

(3.11)

\mathcal{L}(\Psi_{N})\,\lesssim N^{1-\frac{1}{p}}.

Taking
\Psi=\Psi_{N}
and
n=N
in
section
2.3
, and using (
2.17
), we
obtain

(3.12)

\left|\sum_{k=0}^{4N}\Psi_{N}\left(\frac{k}{N}\right)e^{ik\theta}\right|\,\lesssim N^{1-\frac{1}{p}}\frac{1}{|1-e^{i\theta}|^{2}},\quad 0<\theta<2\pi.

For each
N
define
H_{N}
by

Using (
3.12
), we deduce that

(3.13)

\|H_{N}\|_{H^{p}}\lesssim N^{1-\frac{1}{p}}.

Using (
2.14
), the fact
that the
\|\cdot\|_{H^{p}}
norm increases with

, and
(
3.13
), it follows that

(3.14)

\displaystyle\|H_{N}\star\mathcal{R}_{(\eta)}(f_{N})\|_{H^{p}}\leq

\displaystyle\|H_{N}\|_{H^{1}}\|\mathcal{R}_{(\eta)}(f_{N})\|_{H^{p}}\lesssim\|H_{N}\|_{H^{p}}\|\mathcal{R}_{(\eta)}(f_{N})\|_{H^{p}}\lesssim N^{1-\frac{1}{p}}\|\mathcal{R}_{(\eta)}(f_{N})\|_{H^{p}}.

\displaystyle H_{N}\star\mathcal{R}_{(\eta)}(f_{N})(z)\,-S_{N-1}(H_{N}\star\mathcal{R}_{(\eta)})(f_{N}))(z)\,-\,S_{2N,4N+1}(H_{N}\star\mathcal{R}_{(\eta)})(f_{N}))(z)

c_{0},c_{1},\dots,c_{m}
, we have

A_{p}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{m}|c_{j}|^{2}\right)^{p/2}\,\leq\int_{0}^{1}\left|\sum_{j=0}^{m}c_{j}r_{j}(t)\right|^{p}\,dt\,\leq B_{p}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{m}|c_{j}|^{2}\right)^{p/2}.

Let
P
is a complex polynomial,

P(z)=\sum_{j=0}^{m}a_{j}z^{j}
. We define

P_{t}(z)\,=\,\sum_{j=0}^{m}r_{j}(t)a_{j}z^{j},\quad 0\leq t<1.

Using Khinchine’s inequality with
c_{j}=a_{j}e^{ij\theta}
(
0\leq j\leq m
), we obtain

A_{p}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{m}|a_{j}|^{2}\right)^{p/2}\,\leq\int_{0}^{1}|P_{t}(e^{i\theta})|^{p}\,dt\,\leq\,B_{p}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{m}|a_{j}|^{2}\right)^{p/2},\quad\theta\in\mathbb{R}.

This implies that

A_{p}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{m}|a_{j}|^{2}\right)^{p/2}\,\leq\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{0}^{1}|P_{t}(e^{i\theta})|^{p}\,dt\,d\theta\,\leq\,B_{p}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{m}|a_{j}|^{2}\right)^{p/2}.

Interchanging the order of integration in the second term, it
follows that

A_{p}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{m}|a_{j}|^{2}\right)^{p/2}\,\leq\int_{0}^{1}\|P_{t}\|_{H^{p}}^{p}\,dt\,\leq\,B_{p}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{m}|a_{j}|^{2}\right)^{p/2}.

Clearly, this gives the following:

(5.4)

There exists
t\in[0,1)
such that
\|P_{t}\|_{H^{p}}\geq A_{p}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{m}|a_{j}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

Suppose now that
1\leq p<2
.

Set
h(z)\,=\,\log\frac{1}{1-z}\,=\,\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{z^{n}}{n}
(
z\in\mathbb{D}
). For all
k
, we have

\left(\Delta_{2^{k}}h\right)^{\prime}(z)\,=\,\sum_{j=2^{k}}^{2^{k+1}-1}z^{j-1},\quad z\in\mathbb{D},\,\,\,k=0,1,2,\,\dots.

Bearing in mind (
5.4
), for each
k
, let us pick
t_{k}\in[0,1)
such that

\|\left(\Delta_{2^{k}}h\right)^{\prime}_{t_{k}}\|\geq A_{p}2^{k/2}.

Set

\Upsilon(z)\,=\,\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(\Delta_{2_{k}}h\right)_{t_{k}}(z),\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

Clearly,
\Upsilon\in\rm{Hol}(\mathbb{D})
and
\Delta_{2^{k}}\Upsilon=\left(\Delta_{2^{k}}h\right)_{t_{k}}
, for all
k
. Let
(\eta)=\{\eta_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
be
defined by
\eta_{n}=\frac{\Upsilon^{(n}(0)}{n!}
. Then
\Upsilon=F_{(\eta)}
. We have that
|\eta_{n}|=\frac{1}{n}
for
all
n\geq 1
. Also

(5.5)

\|\left(\Delta_{2^{k}}F_{(\eta)}\right)^{\prime}\|_{H^{p}}\,=\,\|\left(\Delta_{2^{k}}h\right)^{\prime}\|_{H^{p}}\geq A_{p}2^{k/2}.

If
1<p<2
, then (
5.5
) implies that
F_{(\eta)}\not\in\Lambda^{p}_{1/p}
and then, using Theorem
1
,
we deduce that
\mathcal{R}_{(\eta)}\not\in\mathcal{B}(H^{p})
.

If
p=1
, then (
5.5
) and part (iii) of
Theorem
7
show that
\mathcal{R}_{(\eta)}\not\in\mathcal{B}(H^{1})
.

Part (iii) of Theorem
4
follows immediately
from the following result (see
[
20
]
for
p\geq 2
, and

[
30
, Lemma 1]
for
1<p<\infty
).

Proposition A
.

If
1<p<\infty
and
(\eta)=\{\eta_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
is a decreasing sequence of non negative real
numbers, then
F_{(\eta)}\in\Lambda^{p}_{1/p}
if and only if

\eta_{n}=\og\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)
, as
n\to\infty
.

\Box

For
1\leq p\leq 2
we can prove the following.

Proposition 3
.

Suppose that
1\leq p\leq 2
and
let
(\eta)=\{\eta_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
be a sequence of complex
numbers such that the sequence
\{|\eta_{n}|\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
is decreasing.

If
\mathcal{R}_{(\eta)}\in\mathcal{B}(H^{p})
then
\eta_{n}=\og\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)
, as
n\to\infty
.

Proof.

If
p=1
the result follows readily from part (ii) of
Theorem
7
.

Assume that
\{|\eta_{n}|\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
is
decreasing,
1<p\leq 2
, and
\mathcal{R}_{(\eta)}\in\mathcal{B}(H^{p})
. For
N=2,3,4,\dots
, set
a_{N}=1-\frac{1}{N}
and let

f_{N}
be defined as in (
3.1
). We have

\mathcal{R}_{(\eta)}(f_{N})(z)=\frac{1}{N^{2-\frac{1}{p}}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\eta_{n}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n}ka_{N}^{k}\right)z^{n},\quad z\in\mathbb{D},

and
\|R_{(\eta)}(f_{N})\|_{H^{p}}\lesssim 1
. Using this, the facts that

\{|\eta_{n}|\}
is decreasing and that there exists

A>0
such that
a_{N}^{k}\geq A
for
0\leq k\leq N
, and
Theorem 6. 2 of
[
11
]
, we deduce that

|\eta_{N}|^{p}\frac{1}{N^{2p-1}}\sum_{n=1}^{N}(n+1)^{p-2}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n}k\right)^{p}\lesssim\|\mathcal{R}_{(\eta)}(f_{N})\|_{H^{p}}^{p}\lesssim 1.

Since

\frac{1}{N^{2p-1}}\sum_{n=1}^{N}(n+1)^{p-2}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n}k\right)^{p}\asymp\frac{1}{N^{2p-1}}\sum_{n=1}^{N}(n+1)^{3p-2}\asymp N^{p},

it follows
that
|\eta_{N}|=\og\left(\frac{1}{N}\right)
.

\Box

We remark that the sequence
\{\eta_{n}\}
constructed to prove
part (ii) of Theorem
4
provides us with an example which
shows that the converse of Proposition
3
is not true
for
1\leq p<2
.

The other side of funny gas (nitrous oxide – N2O), a case report of subacute combined degeneration of cord
Authors: Maryam Barfei, Venkatesan Srinivasan, Vishnuvardan Venkatramanan, Mikailjehan Weston, Simon Dixon
Published: 2023

Introduction

Nitrous oxide (N2O) is widely available to purchase legally in the UK with easy access over the counter and through online portals. It is increasingly abused, particularly by teens and young adults. N2O can have deleterious and irreversible neurological sequalae. Despite wide abuse of N2O, there is not enough awareness among healthcare professionals.

Subacute combined degeneration of cord (SCD) is a known side effect of vitamin B12 deficiency. Radiologically characterised with demyelination of dorsal column and lateral spinal cord leading to mixed upper and lower motor neuron disease.1 One of the emerging causes of vitamin B12 dysfunction is chronic N2O recreational use. Several studies have previously shown the relation between N2O and SCD, yet among healthcare professionals it remains one of the under-recognised causes of SCD.2

Case

We report the case of a 19-year-old man, previously fit and well, working in retail. He presented with pins and needles sensation in his knees spreading downwards, leading to bilateral leg weakness and decreased sensation. He had a 6-month history of recreational N2O inhalation.

Physical examination was notable for loss of vibration below the chest and decreased joint position sense in upper limb and loss of joint position sense in lower limb. He was ataxic on walking and had significant upper limb ataxia on finger to nose test but normal heel to shin test.

Result and discussion

An MRI of the brain and spinal cord showed symmetrical bilateral T2 hyperintense signal in the dorsal columns extending all the way from the upper cervical cord to D11 level, confirming the diagnosis of SCD (Fig 1, Fig 2). Vitamin B12 was normal with a level of >2,000 ng/L and a homocysteine level found to be slightly increased at 13.2 micromol/L, confirming functional B12 deficiency in this case as a cause of SCD.Fig 1MRI spine T2 C1_C2 level.Fig 2MRI spine T2 mid C7 level.

Conclusion

We are seeing an emerging number of cases presenting with different side effects of chronic recreational use of N2O.3, 4 Hence it is imperative that healthcare professionals familiarise themselves with patients' presentation of nitrous oxide side effects. More importantly, it is vital that there are programmes to raise awareness – not only among healthcare professionals, but within other communities such as schools and universities – about the important life-changing side effects of nitrous oxide.

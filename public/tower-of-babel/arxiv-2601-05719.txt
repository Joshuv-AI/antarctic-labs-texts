On the Novel Superfluidity in the Second Layer of 4 He on Graphite Preprint: APS/123-QED
Jun Usami, Hiroshi Fukuyama

Abstract
Evidence for a new type of superfluid phase in second-layer 4 He on graphite has been obtained from simultaneous measurements of torsional-oscillator response and heat-capacity on exactly the same sample down to 30 mK, which resolve substrate-related uncertainties in the previous studies. The new phase hosts both superfluidity and enhanced viscoelasticity, and is stable over a finite density interval, strongly supporting the proposed superfluid liquid-crystal hypothesis. A random-Josephson-network analysis shows that the widely reported log- T T dependence of the superfluid density is likely due to substrate imperfections.

Superfluidity and superconductivity are characterized by phase coherence over macroscopic length scales, usually resulting in a spatially uniform distribution of constituent particles.
However, in some cases, the off-diagonal long-range order (LRO) of superfluidity and superconductivity can coexist with
spontaneous
density modulations that are diagonal LROs.
One prominent example is the so-called supersolid, where superfluidity coexists with crystalline order.
This possibility has long been investigated theoretically

;

;

4

Kim and Chan 2004

;
Beamish and Balibar 2020
;

More broadly, superfluid liquid-crystals with partially broken spatial symmetries

;
Böttcher et al. 2020
;
Norcia et al. 2021

Finally, let us consider why the standard BKT transition nature, i.e., a sharp

However, it is well known that

Therefore, similar precautions must be taken in TO measurements for 2D superfluids adsorbed on exfoliated graphite substrates, where microcrystallites (platelets; 10–20 nm size

First, the standard BKT transition is known to recover layer by layer from two to four layers of

We thus developed the “random Josephson network (RJN)” model, extending the concept originally proposed in Ref.
Crowell and Reppy 1996
, to account for the behavior, particularly in the liquid phase.
We modeled our system as
N
identical 2D superfluid nanoislands, each connected to others via a single most-conductive Josephson junction (see inset in Fig.

If the Josephson energy

of a junction with width

l

exceeds the thermal energy, i.e.,
e_{\rm{J}}\geq k_{\rm{B}}T

active
); otherwise it is

.
For simplicity, we assume a distribution only in

const. (uniform distribution), where

is the areal number density of junctions, while fixing all other parameters (

U
, and

) at their mean values.
When the longest active-junction length

exceeds a certain percolation threshold

, a finite superfluid response (
\Delta f
) is expected as

\Delta f(T,\rho)\propto\lambda(\rho)\left[\ln{\frac{z\rho_{\rm{s}}}{\lambda(\rho)T}}-\frac{l_{\rm{p}}}{\lambda(\rho)}\right],

(2)

where
z\equiv 2\hbar^{2}w/(k_{\rm{B}}m)
and
\rho_{\rm{s}}
is the superfluid number density.

\lambda
is the penetration depth for a
4
He atom of mass
m
tunneling through the junction with the energy scale set by its chemical potential
\mu(\rho)
; thus

depends on
\rho

For further details of the RJN model, see Ref.
Supplemental Material

The estimated upper limit of the superfluid fraction

In summary, by performing simultaneous HC and TO measurements, we established a unified phase diagram that overlays superfluid response onto the well-established calorimetric phases of the second layer of
4
He on graphite.
This enables an unambiguous phase-by-phase assignment of superfluidity while avoiding density ambiguities.
Owing to the moderate, well-characterized
T
- and
\rho
-dependences of the TO background frequency shift, we found that the proposed QLC phase intervening between the quantum liquid and solid exhibits reduced superfluidity below 0.5 K together with enhanced viscoelasticity.
This strongly supports the superfluid liquid-crystal scenario
Nakamura et al. 2016
, an exotic superfluid coexisting with partial crystalline order (likely a hexagonal density modulation).
A phenomenological percolation model—a randomly distributed Josephson-junction network of 2D
4
He nanoislands—accounts for the widely observed non-BKT onset of the superfluid response, including its
\log T
-dependence, suggesting a substrate origin.
These results motivate future TO, rheology or other transport measurements to resolve the intrinsic superfluidity of the QLC phase, using macroscopically uniform graphite substrates
Nakamura et al. 2018
, mesoscopic detection techniques
Todoshchenko et al. 2022
, and direct probes of its microscopic structure
Yamaguchi et al. 2022
.
The results will advance a unifying framework with direct implications for similar superfluids in more complex quantum systems.

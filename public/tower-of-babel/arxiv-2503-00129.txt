The Zakharov System on the Upper Half–plane
M. B. Erdoğan and N. Tzirakis

Abstract
. In this paper we study the Zakharov system on the upper half–plane U = { ( x , y ) ∈ ℝ 2 : y > 0 } U=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:y>0\} with non-homogenous boundary conditions. In particular we obtain low regularity local well–posedness using the restricted norm method of Bourgain and the Fourier–Laplace method of solving initial and boundary value problems. Moreover we prove that the nonlinear part of the solution is in a smoother space than the initial data. To our knowledge this is the first paper which establishes low regularity results for the 2d initial-boundary value Zakharov system.



1.
Introduction

In this paper we study the following initial-boundary value problem for the 2d-Zakharov system

(1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}iu_{t}+\Delta u=nu,\quad(x,y)\in U,\,t>0,\\
n_{tt}-\Delta n=\Delta(|u|^{2}),\quad(x,y)\in U,\,t>0,\\
u(x,y,0)=f(x,y)\in H^{s_{1}}(U),\quad u(x,0,t)=k(x,t),\quad x\in\mathbb{R},t>0,\\
n(x,y,0)=g(x,y)\in H^{s_{2}}(U),\quad n_{t}(x,y,0)=h(x,y)\in H^{s_{2}-1}(U),\\
n(x,0,t)=\ell(x,t),\quad x\in\mathbb{R},t>0,\end{array}\right.

where
U=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:y>0\}
is the upper half–plane. For
s>-\frac{1}{2}
, we define
H^{s}(U)
by extension to
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
For
s<-\frac{1}{2}
, we define it as a tempered distribution in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
whose support is contained in
U
,
[
7
]
. We will determine the suitable spaces for
k
and
\ell
later, which turn out to be
L^{2}
based Sobolev-type spaces, see (
9
) and (
10
).
In addition, for
s>\frac{1}{2}
we have the compatibility condition for the
L^{2}
traces:
f|_{y=0}=k|_{t=0}
and similarly for the wave part. The compatibility condition is necessary since the solutions we are interested in have continuous
L^{2}_{x}
traces for
s>\frac{1}{2}
, see Lemma
2.1
.
In addition, we will work with the Klein-Gordon equation instead of the wave equation as in
[
13
]
.

For the Euclidean space, well–posedness theory for the Zakharov system was completed in a series of papers. The final results are sharp due to a scaling argument that appears in
[
13
]
. In
[
18
]
, existence results for smooth solutions in dimensions

were obtained. The regularity assumptions and dimension restrictions were weakened in
[

,
19
,
16
,
15
]
. Later, Bourgain and Colliander,
[
6
]
, proved local well-posedness in all dimensions using the restricted norm method of Bourgain
[
5
]
. In
[
13
]
, Ginibre, Tsutsumi, and Velo applied Bourgain’s method to extend the well–posedness results in all dimensions, covering the full subcritical regularity range for
d\geq 4
. In dimension
d=1
, they obtained local existence at the critical regularity
L^{2}\times H^{-\frac{1}{2}}\times H^{-\frac{3}{2}}
. In
[
14
]
, local ill-posedness results were obtained for some regularities outside the well-posedness regime established in
[
13
]
. In dimensions two and three, the local well-posedness was obtained in the critical space

in
[

]
and
[

respectively. These results are sharp in the sense that the data-to-solution map fails to be analytic at lower regularity levels.

In this paper, we prove well-posedness for the 2d Zakharov system on the half plane with initial and boundary data in
L^{2}
based Sobolev spaces and obtain low regularity strong solutions. We also impose a non-homogenous boundary constraint at
y=0
. Wellposedness of (
1
) means local existence, uniqueness and continuity with respect to the initial data of distributional solutions. The main part of our paper is the application of the restricted norm method for both free and boundary solution operators which generate correction terms that are absent on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Moreover certain nonlinear estimates require additional decompositions since the resonant sets in two dimensions are quite large. Here we utilize the machinery developed in
[
3
]
, where the main estimates were achieved for
functions which are dyadically localized in frequency and modulation. In some cases and depending on the frequency interactions, one has to further decompose using angular decompositions. For the definition of the usual Sobolev spaces and their adapted generalization we refer the reader to the Notation subsection below.

One has to be careful defining the right notion of strong solutions. More precisely we have the following definition:

Definition 1.1
.

We say (
1
) is locally well–posed in
H^{s_{1}}(U)\times H^{s_{2}}(U)\times H^{s_{2}-1}(U)

(f,g,h)\in H^{s_{1}}(U)\times H^{s_{2}}(U)\times H^{s_{2}-1}(U)

,

s>\frac{1}{2}
, the equation has a distributional solution on
[0,T]

such that

where

g_{j}\to g
in

Our first theorem establishes local well–posedness.

Theorem 1.2

Fix
s_{2}\in(-\frac{1}{2},\frac{3}{2})\setminus\{\frac{1}{2}\}

\max\big(\frac{1}{8},\frac{s_{2}}{2}+\frac{1}{4},s_{2},2s_{2}-1\big)<s_{1}<s_{2}+1.

Then, the equation (

Remark 1.3

We note that our result does not cover the full range of indices

In addition we obtain the following smoothing estimate:

Theorem 1.4

Fix

1.2

u(x,t)-S_{0}^{t}(f,k)(x)\in C^{0}_{t}H^{s_{1}+\alpha}_{x}([0,T]\times U),

where

To study the half–plane problem we utilize the restricted norm method of Bourgain
[
5
]
. This continues our work initiated in
[
10
]
,
[

]
and
[

, of establishing the regularity properties of nonlinear dispersive partial differential equations (PDE) on a half line using the tools that are available in the case of the whole line. We thus extend the data to the whole plane and use Laplace transform methods to set up an equivalent integral equation (on

) of the solution, see (

In both the standard nonlinear and boundary terms we need to refine our analysis due to resonances that arise from orthogonality issues. After the extension to the full plane, one can utilize the refined analysis in
[

to establish the well-posedness. Notice that the upper bounds on regularity is an artifact that depends on the method of first extending the data and then restricting to obtain the solution.
Our result is the first low regularity well–posedness result on the half–plane for the 2d Zakharov. Concerning uniqueness, the solution we obtain for the integral equation (
12

12

We now discuss briefly the organization of the paper. In Section 2, we introduce the appropriate function spaces that accommodate the solutions. The choice of the right spaces is crucial to close the argument at any level of regularity.
We also construct the solutions of the linear problem and set up the Duhamel formula for the full equation. The Duhamel formula incorporates the extension of the data on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and the evaluation of certain operators at the zero boundary.
In Section 3, we obtain the a priori linear estimates that we need in order to put our solutions to the right function spaces. Section 4 is the main part of our paper. Here taking advantage by either directly using or combining the results in
[
3
]
we prove the nonlinear estimates that imply existence of solutions. This section also provides the tools needed for the proof of Theorem
1.4
. In Section 5, we briefly outline the well–known process of establishing LWP and smoothing using the linear and nonlinear estimates of Sections 3 and 4. The last section, Section 6, is an Appendix where we state and if necessary prove a series of analysis lemmas that we use throughout the paper. We finish the introduction with a notation subsection.

1.1.
Notation

Recall that for
s\in\mathbb{R}
,
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is the closure of
C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
under the norm

\|f\|_{H^{s}}=\|f\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}:=\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\langle\zeta\rangle^{2s}|\widehat{f}(\zeta)|^{2}d\zeta\Big)^{1/2},

where
\langle\zeta\rangle:=(1+|\zeta|^{2})^{1/2}
and

\widehat{f}(\zeta)={\mathcal{F}}f(\zeta)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x)e^{-ix\cdot\zeta}dx

is the Fourier transform of
f
. We also set the notation

f(\widehat{\zeta_{j}})=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)e^{-ix_{j}\zeta_{j}}dx_{j}

for the Fourier transform in the
j
th space coordinate.

For a space-time function
f
, we set the notation

D_{0}f(x,t)=f(x,0,t).

For
s>-\frac{1}{2}
, we define the space
H^{s}(U)
as

H^{s}(U):=\big\{g\in\mathcal{D}(U):\exists\tilde{g}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\text{ so that }\tilde{g}\chi_{U}=g\big\},

with the norm

\|g\|_{H^{s}(U)}:=\inf\big\{\|\tilde{g}\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}:\tilde{g}\chi_{U}=g\big\}.

The restriction for
s>-\frac{1}{2}
is needed since multiplication with
\chi_{U}
is not well-defined for
s\leq-\frac{1}{2}
.
We say
\tilde{g}
is an
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
extension of
g\in H^{s}(U)
if
\tilde{g}\chi_{U}=g
and
\|\tilde{g}\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq 2\|g\|_{H^{s}(U)}
.
Note that, if
g\in H^{s}(U)
for some
s>\frac{1}{2}
, then by trace lemma any
H^{s}
extension is in
C^{0}_{y}L^{2}_{x}
, and hence
g(x,0)
is well defined as an
L^{2}
function.

Finally, we use
\langle x,y\rangle
to denote
\langle(x,y)\rangle=\sqrt{1+x^{2}+y^{2}}
, and we reserve the symbol
\kappa
for a smooth compactly supported function of time which is equal to
1
on
[-1,1]
.

We now establish embedding and extension properties of these spaces.

Lemma 2.1
.

For
s>1
, the space
\mathcal{H}^{s}_{S}
and
\mathcal{H}^{s}_{W}
embed continuously into
C^{0}_{x,t}
. Moreover, for
s>\frac{1}{2}
, we have the trace lemma; the spaces
\mathcal{H}^{s}_{S}
and
\mathcal{H}^{s}_{W}
embed continuously into
C^{0}_{t}L^{2}_{x}
, and in particular
\sup_{t}\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\lesssim\|\varphi\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}_{S}}
,
\sup_{t}\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\lesssim\|\varphi\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}_{W}}
.

Proof.

This is similar to Lemma 2.1 from
[
8
]
. We provide the proof only for the wave part,
\mathcal{H}^{s}_{W}
.

For the first claim, it suffices to prove that given
\varphi\in\mathcal{H}^{s}_{W}

\|\widehat{\varphi}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\lesssim\|\varphi\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}_{W}}
.
By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality this follows from

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{1}{|\xi_{2}^{2}-\xi_{1}^{2}-1|^{\frac{1}{2}}\langle\xi\rangle^{2s-1}}\ d\xi\lesssim 1,

which holds for
s>1
by considering the regions
|\xi_{2}|\leq 2\langle\xi_{1}\rangle
,
|\xi_{2}|>2\langle\xi_{1}\rangle
separately.

Similarly, for the second claim it suffices to prove that

\|\widehat{\varphi}\|_{L^{2}_{\xi_{1}}L^{1}_{\xi_{2}}}\lesssim\|\varphi\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}_{W}}
. By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality in the
\xi_{2}
integral this follows from

\sup_{\xi_{1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{1}{|\xi_{2}^{2}-\xi_{1}^{2}-1|^{\frac{1}{2}}\langle\xi\rangle^{2s-1}}d\xi_{2}\lesssim 1,

which holds for
s>\frac{1}{2}
by the same decomposition of the regions.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

For

\|\chi_{t>0}\varphi(x,t)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}_{S}}\lesssim\|\varphi\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}_{S}(U)}.

\frac{1}{2}<s<\frac{5}{2}

Analogously, for
-\frac{1}{2}<s<\frac{1}{2}

\|\chi_{t>0}\varphi(x,t)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}_{W}}\lesssim\|\varphi\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}_{W}(U)}.

\frac{1}{2}<s<\frac{3}{2}

\varphi(x,0)
is zero.

Proof.

This is similar to Lemma 2.2 from
[
8
]
. We provide the proof only for the wave part,
\mathcal{H}^{s}_{W}
.
Since
{\mathcal{F}}\big(\chi_{t>0}\varphi\big)(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})=H\widehat{\varphi}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2}),

where

Hf(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})={\mathcal{F}}_{t}\big(\chi_{t>0}f(\xi_{1},t^{\vee})\big)(\xi_{2}),

It suffices to prove that

m(\xi)=|\xi_{2}^{2}-\xi_{1}^{2}-1|^{\frac{1}{2}}\langle\xi\rangle^{2s-1}\sim||\xi_{2}|-\langle\xi_{1}\rangle|^{\frac{1}{2}}(|\xi_{2}|+\langle\xi_{1}\rangle)^{2s-\frac{1}{2}}

is an

uniformly in

for

, see

12

Since

Similarly, when

, which is an
A_{2}
weight for
-\frac{1}{2}<s<\frac{1}{2}
. When
a,b\sim 1
, we have
m\sim|1-\xi_{2}|^{\frac{1}{2}}
, which is also an
A_{2}
weight. It remains to consider the case
0<a\ll 1\ll b
. We estimate

\int_{a}^{b}m\lesssim b^{2s+1}\sim(b-a)^{2s+1},\,\,\,\,\int_{a}^{b}m^{-1}\lesssim b^{-2s+1}\sim(b-a)^{-2s+1}

for
-\frac{1}{2}<s<\frac{1}{2}
. Therefore,
m
is an
A_{2}
weight.

For the second part, we note that

\|\chi_{t>0}\varphi\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}_{W}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}=\|T\big(\chi_{t>0}\varphi\big)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s-1}_{W}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}
, where
T
is the multiplier operator with the multiplier
\langle\xi\rangle
. Furthermore

\|T\big(\chi_{t>0}\varphi\big)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s-1}_{W}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\lesssim\|T_{1}\big(\chi_{t>0}\varphi\big)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s-1}_{W}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}+\|T_{2}\big(\chi_{t>0}\varphi\big)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s-1}_{W}(\mathbb{R}^{2})},

where
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
are multiplier operators with multipliers
1+|\xi_{1}|
and
\xi_{2}
, respectively. Note that
T_{1}
commutes with multiplication by
\chi_{t>0}
. Since
\varphi
has trace zero,
\partial_{t}\big(\chi_{t>0}\varphi\big)=\chi_{t>0}\partial_{t}\varphi
in the sense of distributions. Therefore, we also have
T_{2}\big(\chi_{t>0}\varphi\big)=\chi_{t>0}T_{2}\varphi
. Also using the first part for
s-1
, we obtain

\|T\big(\chi_{t>0}\varphi\big)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s-1}_{W}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\lesssim\|T_{1}\varphi\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s-1}_{W}(U)}+\|T_{2}\varphi\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s-1}_{W}(U)}\lesssim\|\varphi\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}_{W}(U)}.

∎

Now we write a system of integral equations equivalent to (
1
) on
[0,T]
,
T<1
:

(12)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}u(t)=\kappa(t)S_{0}^{t}\big(f_{e},k\big)-i\kappa(t/T)\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-t^{\prime})\Delta}F(u,n)\,dt^{\prime}+i\kappa(t)S_{0}^{t}\big(0,q\big),\\
n(t)=\kappa(t)W_{0}^{t}\big(\psi^{\pm},\ell\big)+\frac{1}{2}\kappa(t/T)(n_{+}+n_{-})-\frac{1}{2}\kappa(t)W_{0}^{t}(0,z),\end{array}\right.

where

(13)

F(u,n)=\kappa(t)nu,\,\,\,\,\text{ and }\,\,q(t)=\kappa(t)D_{0}\Big(\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-t^{\prime})\Delta}F(u,n)\,dt^{\prime}\Big).

(14)

n_{\pm}=\pm\int_{0}^{t}e^{\mp i(t-t^{\prime})\langle\nabla\rangle}G(u)dt^{\prime},\,\,\,\,G(u):=-\kappa(t)\frac{\Delta}{\langle\nabla\rangle}|u|^{2}-\kappa(t)\frac{1}{\langle\nabla\rangle}n,

(15)

z(t)=\kappa(t)D_{0}(n_{+}+n_{-}).

To establish the local well-posedness of (

), we work with the following Bourgain spaces on
\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}

\|u\|_{X_{S}^{s_{1},b}}=\big\|\widehat{u}(\xi,\tau)\langle\tau+|\xi|^{2}\rangle^{b}\langle\xi\rangle^{s_{1}}\big\|_{L^{2}_{\xi,\tau}}=\|e^{-it\Delta}u\|_{H^{s_{1}}_{x,y}H^{b}_{t}},

\|n\|_{X_{W}^{s_{2},b}}=\inf_{n=n_{+}+n_{-}}\left(\|n_{+}\|_{X_{W,+}^{s_{2},b}}+\|n_{-}\|_{X_{W,-}^{s_{2},b}}\right),

where

\|n\|_{X_{W,\pm}^{s_{2},b}}=\big\|\widehat{n}(\xi,\tau)\big\langle\tau\pm\langle\xi\rangle\big\rangle^{b}\langle\xi\rangle^{s_{2}}\big\|_{L^{2}_{\xi,\tau}}=\|e^{\pm it\langle\nabla\rangle}n\|_{H^{s_{2}}_{x,y}H^{b}_{t}}

We recall the embedding
X^{s,b}\subset C^{0}_{t}H^{s}
for

and the following inequalities from
[
5
,
13
,
9
]
.

For any
s,b
we have

(16)

\|\eta(t)W_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}g\|_{X^{s,b}}\lesssim\|g\|_{H^{s}}.

For any
s\in\mathbb{R}
,
0\leq b_{1}<\frac{1}{2}
, and

, we have

(17)

\Big\|\eta(t)\int_{0}^{t}W_{\mathbb{R}}(t-t^{\prime})F(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}\Big\|_{X^{s,b_{2}}}\lesssim\|F\|_{X^{s,-b_{1}}}.

Moreover, for

\|\eta(t/T)F\|_{X^{s,b_{1}}}\lesssim T^{b_{2}-b_{1}}\|F\|_{X^{s,b_{2}}}.

3.
A priori linear estimates

In this section we establish a priori linear estimates that are crucial for the wellposedness theory. In particular, we obtain Kato smoothing estimates for the linear groups and Duhamel integrals, and
X^{s,b}
space estimates for the boundary operators.

Proposition 3.1
.

(Schrödinger
X^{s,b}
Estimate) For
s\geq 0
and
b\leq\frac{1}{2}
, we have

\|\kappa(t)S_{j}(k)\|_{X_{S}^{s,b}}\lesssim\|\chi_{t>0}k\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}_{S}},\quad j=1,2.

Proof.

For
S_{1}
the claim is immediate from the definition of
\mathcal{H}_{S}^{s}
(using the “-” sign in the norm) since

\mathcal{F}(\kappa S_{1}(k))(\xi,\tau)=2\xi_{2}\chi_{\xi_{2}>0}\widehat{\kappa}(\tau+|\xi|^{2})\widehat{\chi_{t>0}k}(\xi_{1},-|\xi|^{2}).

For
S_{2}
, we calculate

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(\kappa S_{2}(k))(\xi,\tau)

\displaystyle=2\int_{0}^{\infty}\widehat{\kappa}(\tau+\xi_{1}^{2}-\eta^{2})\widehat{\rho}(\tfrac{\xi_{2}}{\eta})\widehat{\chi_{t>0}k}(\xi_{1},\eta^{2}-\xi_{1}^{2})d\eta

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\widehat{\kappa}(\tau+\xi_{1}^{2}-\eta^{2})\widehat{\rho}(\tfrac{\xi_{2}}{\eta})\frac{1}{\eta\langle\xi_{1},\eta\rangle^{s}}K(\xi_{1},\eta)d\eta,

where

\|K\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|\chi_{t>0}k\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}_{S}}.

Therefore, the
X_{S}^{s,b}
norm is

\Big\|\langle\xi\rangle^{s}\int_{0}^{\infty}\widehat{\kappa}(\tau+\xi_{1}^{2}-\eta^{2})\langle\tau+|\xi|^{2}\rangle^{b}\widehat{\rho}(\tfrac{\xi_{2}}{\eta})\frac{1}{\eta\langle\xi_{1},\eta\rangle^{s}}K(\xi_{1},\eta)d\eta\Big\|_{L^{2}_{\xi,\tau}}.

Since
\kappa,\rho
are Schwartz functions, we may bound this, for any constant
M>0
, by

(19)

\left\|\langle\xi\rangle^{s}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\langle\tau+|\xi|^{2}\rangle^{b}}{\langle\tau+\xi_{1}^{2}-\eta^{2}\rangle^{100}}\ \frac{|\eta|^{M-1}}{\eta^{M}+\xi_{2}^{M}}\ \frac{|K(\xi_{1},\eta)|}{\langle\xi_{1},\eta\rangle^{s}}\ d\eta\right\|_{L^{2}_{\xi,\tau}}.

When
\eta^{2}+\xi_{2}^{2}<1
, we have

\langle\xi\rangle^{s}\lesssim\langle\xi_{1},\eta\rangle^{s}

and

\langle\tau+|\xi|^{2}\rangle\sim\langle\tau+\xi_{1}^{2}-\eta^{2}\rangle
.
Taking
M=2
and moving the
L^{2}_{\xi_{1},\tau}
norm inside the integral in (
19
), we obtain the bound

(20)

\Big\|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|\eta|}{\eta^{2}+\xi_{2}^{2}}\|K(\xi_{1},\eta)\|_{L^{2}_{\xi_{1}}}d\eta\Big\|_{L^{2}_{\xi_{2}}}=\Big\|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|r|}{r^{2}+1}\|K(\xi_{1},r\xi_{2})\|_{L^{2}_{\xi_{1}}}dr\Big\|_{L^{2}_{\xi_{2}}}\\
\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|r|}{r^{2}+1}\|K(\xi_{1},r\xi_{2})\|_{L^{2}_{\xi}}dr\\
\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|r|^{\frac{1}{2}}}{r^{2}+1}\|K(\xi)\|_{L^{2}_{\xi}}dr\lesssim\|K\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|\chi_{t>0}k\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}_{S}}.

When
\eta^{2}+\xi_{2}^{2}>1
, we consider first the case that
|\xi_{2}|\lesssim|\xi_{1}|
or
|\xi_{2}|\lesssim|\eta|
. In this case we have
\langle\xi\rangle^{s_{1}}\lesssim\langle\xi_{1},\eta\rangle^{s_{1}}
. Again taking
M=2
in (
19
), and then integrating in
\xi_{2}
, we obtain the bounds

(21)

\lesssim\Big\|\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\langle\tau-\eta^{2}+\xi_{1}^{2}\rangle^{100-b}}\frac{|\eta|}{(\eta^{2}+\xi_{2}^{2})^{1-b}}|K(\xi_{1},\eta)|d\eta\Big\|_{L^{2}_{\xi,\tau}}\\
\\
\leq\Big\|\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{|\eta|^{2b-\frac{1}{2}}}{\langle\tau-\eta^{2}+\xi_{1}^{2}\rangle^{10}}|K(\xi_{1},\eta)|d\eta\Big\|_{L^{2}_{\xi_{1},\tau}}.

For the part of the integral where
\eta\in[0,1]
, bounding this by
\|K\|_{L^{2}}
is trivial. For
\eta>1
, since
b\leq\frac{1}{2}
, we have the bound

\Big\|\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{|\eta|^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\langle\tau-\eta^{2}+\xi_{1}^{2}\rangle^{10}}|K(\xi_{1},\eta)|d\eta\Big\|_{L^{2}_{\xi_{1},\tau}}.

Using Lemma
6.3
we bound this by
\|K\|_{L^{2}}
, as desired.

It remains to address the case when
\eta^{2}+\xi_{2}^{2}>1
and
|\xi_{2}|\gg|\eta|,|\xi|
.
In this case, we see that (
19
) can be bounded by

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\langle\tau-\eta^{2}+\xi_{1}^{2}\rangle^{100-b}}\frac{|\eta|^{M-1}}{|\xi_{2}|^{M-s-2b}}\frac{|K(\xi_{1},\eta)|}{\langle\xi_{1},\eta\rangle^{s}}d\eta\right\|_{L^{2}_{\xi,\tau}}.

Taking the
L^{2}_{\xi_{2}}
norm, keeping in mind that
|\xi_{2}|\gg|\eta|,|\xi|
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\langle\tau-\eta^{2}+\xi_{1}^{2}\rangle^{10}}\frac{|\eta|^{M-1}}{\langle\xi_{1},\eta\rangle^{M-s-2b-\frac{1}{2}}}\frac{|K(\xi_{1},\eta)|}{\langle\xi_{1},\eta\rangle^{s}}d\eta\right\|_{L^{2}_{\xi_{1},\tau}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\langle\tau-\eta^{2}+\xi_{1}^{2}\rangle^{10}}\frac{|\eta|^{M-1}}{\langle\eta\rangle^{M-2b-\frac{1}{2}}}|K(\xi_{1},\eta)|d\eta\right\|_{L^{2}_{\xi_{1},\tau}}.

This is bounded by
\|K\|_{L^{2}_{\eta,\xi}}
by the bound for (
21
) above.
∎

Proposition 3.2
.

(Klein-Gordon
X^{s,b}
Estimate) For any
s\geq-\frac{1}{2}
and
b\leq\frac{1}{2}
, we have

\|\kappa(t)W_{j}(\ell)\|_{X_{W}^{s,b}}\lesssim\|\chi_{t>0}\ell\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}_{W}},\quad j=1,2.

Proof.

Note that it suffices to consider
b=\frac{1}{2}
.

For
W_{1}
the claim is immediate from the definitions of
W_{1}
and the first part of the norm
\mathcal{H}_{W}^{s}
in (
10
) and noting that the
X_{W}^{s,b}
is defined as an infemum.

For
W_{2}
, we have

\mathcal{F}(\kappa W_{2}(\ell))(\xi,\tau)=\\
\int_{1+\xi_{1}^{2}>\eta^{2}}\widehat{\kappa}\big(\tau-{\rm sign}(\eta)\sqrt{1+\xi_{1}^{2}-\eta^{2}}\big)\widehat{\rho}(\xi_{2}/|\eta|)\widehat{\chi_{t>0}\ell}\big(\xi_{1},{\rm sign}(\eta)\sqrt{1+\xi_{1}^{2}-\eta^{2}}\big)\frac{d\eta}{\sqrt{1+\xi_{1}^{2}-\eta^{2}}}.

Letting

L(\xi_{1},\eta):=\langle\xi_{1}\rangle^{s-\frac{1}{2}}\big|\widehat{\chi_{t>0}\ell}\big(\xi_{1},{\rm sign}(\eta)\sqrt{1+\xi_{1}^{2}-\eta^{2}}\big)\big|\,\frac{|\eta|}{(1+\xi_{1}^{2}-\eta^{2})^{\frac{1}{4}}}\chi_{\eta^{2}<\xi_{1}^{2}+1},

note that
\|L\|_{L^{2}_{\xi_{1},\eta}}\lesssim\|\chi_{t>0}\ell\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}_{W}}
. We have the following bound for
X^{s,b}_{W,\pm}
norm

\Big\|\int_{1+\xi_{1}^{2}>\eta^{2}}\frac{\langle\xi\rangle^{s}}{\langle\xi_{1}\rangle^{s-\frac{1}{2}}}\left|\widehat{\kappa}(\tau-{\rm sign}(\eta)\sqrt{1+\xi_{1}^{2}-\eta^{2}})\right|\big\langle\tau\pm\langle\xi\rangle\big\rangle^{b}\frac{|\widehat{\rho}(\xi_{2}/|\eta|)|}{|\eta|(1+\xi_{1}^{2}-\eta^{2})^{\frac{1}{4}}}L(\xi_{1},\eta)d\eta\Big\|_{L^{2}_{\xi,\tau}}.

Since the
X^{s,b}_{W}
norm is defined as an infimum, and since
\kappa,\rho
are Schwartz functions, it suffices to consider

(22)

\Big\|\int_{1+\xi_{1}^{2}>\eta^{2}}\frac{\langle\xi\rangle^{s}}{\langle\xi_{1}\rangle^{s-\frac{1}{2}}}\frac{\langle\tau-\langle\xi\rangle\rangle^{b}}{\langle\tau-\sqrt{1+\xi_{1}^{2}-\eta^{2}}\rangle^{100}}\frac{|\eta|^{M-1}}{(\eta^{M}+\xi_{2}^{M})(1+\xi_{1}^{2}-\eta^{2})^{\frac{1}{4}}}L(\xi_{1},\eta)d\eta\Big\|_{L^{2}_{\xi,\tau}}.

When
\eta^{2}+\xi_{2}^{2}\ll 1
, observe that
\langle\tau-\langle\xi\rangle\rangle\sim\big\langle\tau-\sqrt{1+\xi_{1}^{2}-\eta^{2}}\big\rangle
,
\langle\xi\rangle\sim\langle\xi_{1}\rangle
, and
(1+\xi_{1}^{2}-\eta^{2})^{\frac{1}{4}}\sim\langle\xi_{1}\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}
. Taking
L^{2}_{\xi_{1},\tau}
norm inside and choosing
M=2
, we estimate the norm by

\Big\|\int_{\eta^{2}+\xi_{2}^{2}\ll 1}\frac{|\eta|}{\eta^{2}+\xi_{2}^{2}}\|L(\xi_{1},\eta)\|_{L^{2}_{\xi_{1}}}d\eta\Big\|_{L^{2}_{\xi_{2}}},

which can be bounded as in the proof of the previous theorem, see (
20
).

When
\eta^{2}+\xi_{2}^{2}\gtrsim 1
and
|\xi_{2}|\gg|\xi_{1}|
, we bound (
22
) by

\Big\|\int_{\xi_{1}^{2}+1>\eta^{2}}\frac{1}{\langle\xi_{1}\rangle^{s-\frac{1}{2}}}\frac{1}{\langle\tau-\sqrt{1+\xi_{1}^{2}-\eta^{2}}\rangle^{100-b}}\frac{|\eta|^{M-1}}{\langle\xi_{2}\rangle^{M-s-b}(1+\xi_{1}^{2}-\eta^{2})^{\frac{1}{4}}}L(\xi_{1},\eta)d\eta\Big\|_{L^{2}_{\xi,\tau}}.

Taking the
L^{2}_{\xi_{2}}
norm yields

\Big\|\int_{\xi_{1}^{2}+1>\eta^{2}}\frac{1}{\langle\xi_{1}\rangle^{M-1-b}}\frac{|\eta|^{M-1}}{\langle\tau-\sqrt{1+\xi_{1}^{2}-\eta^{2}}\rangle^{10}(1+\xi_{1}^{2}-\eta^{2})^{\frac{1}{4}}}L(\xi_{1},\eta)d\eta\Big\|_{L^{2}_{\xi_{1},\tau}}.

This is bounded by
\|L\|_{L^{2}_{\xi_{1},\eta}}
for
b\leq\frac{1}{2}
and
M\geq\frac{3}{2}
using Lemma
6.3
.

When
\eta^{2}+\xi_{2}^{2}\gtrsim 1
and
|\xi_{2}|\lesssim|\xi_{1}|
, the ratio of
s
multipliers is
\sim 1
, for any
s
.
We also have

(23)

\frac{\big\langle\tau-\langle\xi\rangle\big\rangle^{b}}{\big\langle\tau-\sqrt{1+\xi_{1}^{2}-\eta^{2}}\big\rangle^{100}}\lesssim\frac{1+\frac{(\eta^{2}+\xi_{2}^{2})^{b}}{\langle\xi\rangle^{b}}}{\big\langle\tau-\sqrt{1+\xi_{1}^{2}-\eta^{2}}\big\rangle^{100-b}}.

We first consider the contribution of the second term in the numerator of (
23
). Taking
M=2
, it is bounded by

\Big\|\int_{\eta^{2}<1+\xi_{1}^{2}}\frac{1}{\big\langle\tau-\sqrt{1+\xi_{1}^{2}-\eta^{2}}\big\rangle^{10}}\frac{|\eta|\langle\xi_{1}\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}}{(\eta^{2}+\xi_{2}^{2})^{1-b}\langle\xi\rangle^{b}(1+\xi_{1}^{2}-\eta^{2})^{\frac{1}{4}}}|L(\xi_{1},\eta)|d\eta\Big\|_{L^{2}_{\xi,\tau}}.

Taking the
L^{2}_{\xi_{2}}
norm inside the integral and recalling that
b=\frac{1}{2}
, we have

\Big\|\frac{\eta}{(\eta^{2}+\xi_{2}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\Big\|_{L^{2}_{\xi_{2}}}\lesssim|\eta|^{\frac{1}{2}},

we obtain the bound

\Big\|\int_{\eta^{2}<1+\xi_{1}^{2}}\frac{|\eta|^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\big\langle\tau-\sqrt{1+\xi_{1}^{2}-\eta^{2}}\big\rangle^{10}(1+\xi_{1}^{2}-\eta^{2})^{\frac{1}{4}}}|L(\xi_{1},\eta)|d\eta\Big\|_{L^{2}_{\xi_{1},\tau}}.

This is bounded by
\|L\|_{L^{2}_{\xi_{1},\eta}}
using Lemma
6.3
.

Finally, we consider the contribution of
1
in the numerator of (
23
):

\Big\|\int_{\eta^{2}<1+\xi_{1}^{2}}\frac{1}{\big\langle\tau-\sqrt{1+\xi_{1}^{2}-\eta^{2}}\big\rangle^{10}}\frac{|\eta|\langle\xi_{1}\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}}{(\eta^{2}+\xi_{2}^{2})(1+\xi_{1}^{2}-\eta^{2})^{\frac{1}{4}}}|L(\xi_{1},\eta)|d\eta\Big\|_{L^{2}_{\xi,\tau}}.

This can be bounded as in (
20
) after taking the
L^{2}_{\xi_{1},\tau}
norm inside in the case
|\eta|\ll\langle\xi_{1}\rangle
. In the case
|\eta|\gtrsim\langle\xi_{1}\rangle
, we obtain the bound by taking the
L^{2}_{\xi_{2}}
norm inside and then applying Lemma
6.3
.
∎

Proposition 3.3
.

(Kato smoothing for Schrödinger) For
s\geq 0
, and
f\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, we have
\kappa(t)e^{it\Delta}f\in C^{0}_{y}\mathcal{H}^{s}_{S}
, and

\big\|\kappa(t)e^{it\Delta}f\big\|_{L^{\infty}_{y}\mathcal{H}^{s}_{S}}\lesssim\|f\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

Proof.

We set
U(x,y,t)=\kappa(t)e^{it\Delta}f(x,y,t)
. Taking the Fourier transform in
x
we get

U(\widehat{\xi_{1}},y,t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\kappa(t)\ e^{-i|\xi|^{2}t}e^{i\xi_{2}y}\widehat{f}(\xi)\ d\xi_{2}.

Now the Fourier transform in
t
gives

U(\widehat{\xi_{1}},y,\widehat{\eta})=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widehat{\kappa}(\eta+|\xi|^{2})\ e^{i\xi_{2}y}\widehat{f}(\xi)\ d\xi_{2}.

By dominated convergence theorem, the statement follows from the claim:

I:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\langle\xi_{1},\eta\rangle^{2s}\eta^{2}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\widehat{\kappa}(\xi_{2}^{2}\pm\eta^{2})||\widehat{f}(\xi)|d\xi_{2}\Big)^{2}d\eta d\xi_{1}\lesssim\|f\|_{H^{s}}^{2}.

Applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality to the
\xi_{2}
-integral we get

\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\widehat{\kappa}(\xi_{2}^{2}\pm\eta^{2})||\widehat{f}(\xi)|d\xi_{2}\Big)^{2}\leq\left\|\widehat{\kappa}(\xi_{2}^{2}\pm\eta^{2})\right\|_{L^{1}_{\xi_{2}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\widehat{\kappa}(\xi_{2}^{2}\pm\eta^{2})||\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi_{2},

and using the fact that for any
M>0

|\widehat{\kappa}(\xi_{2}^{2}\pm\eta^{2})|\lesssim\frac{1}{\big\langle\xi_{2}^{2}\pm\eta^{2}\big\rangle^{M}},

we have the estimate

\left\|\widehat{\kappa}(\xi_{2}^{2}\pm\eta^{2})\right\|_{L^{1}_{\xi_{2}}}\lesssim\frac{1}{\langle\eta\rangle}.

We now combine these estimates to bound the integral
I
as follows

I\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\langle\xi_{1},\eta\rangle^{2s}\frac{|\eta|}{\big\langle\xi_{2}^{2}\pm\eta^{2}\big\rangle^{M}}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}d\eta d\xi.

It suffices to consider only ‘-’ in the denominator and show that

J:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\langle\xi_{1},\eta\rangle^{2s}\frac{|\eta|}{\big\langle\xi_{2}^{2}-\eta^{2}\big\rangle^{M}}d\eta\lesssim\langle\xi\rangle^{2s}.

This is trivial when
|\eta|\lesssim|\xi_{2}|
.

When
|\eta|>2|\xi_{2}|
, using
|\xi_{2}^{2}-\eta^{2}|\gtrsim\eta^{2}
we have

J\lesssim\int\langle\xi_{1},\eta\rangle^{2s}\frac{|\eta|d\eta}{(1+\eta^{2})^{M}}\lesssim\Big(\sup_{|\eta|>2|\xi_{2}|}\frac{\langle\xi_{1},\eta\rangle^{2s}}{(1+\eta^{2})^{M/2}}\Big)\,\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|\eta|d\eta}{(1+\eta^{2})^{M/2}}\lesssim\langle\xi\rangle^{2s},

for
M>\max(2,2s)
.
∎

Proposition 3.4
.

(Kato smoothing for Klein-Gordon) For
s\in\mathbb{R}
, and
f\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, we have
\kappa(t)e^{\pm it\langle\nabla\rangle}f\in C^{0}_{y}\mathcal{H}^{s}_{W}
, and

\big\|\kappa(t)e^{\pm it\langle\nabla\rangle}f\big\|_{L^{\infty}_{y}\mathcal{H}^{s}_{W}}\lesssim\|f\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

Proof.

Setting
W(x,y,t)=\kappa(t)[e^{\pm it\langle\nabla\rangle}f](x,y)
, and taking the Fourier transform in
x
and
t
we get

W(\widehat{\xi_{1}},y,\widehat{\eta})=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widehat{\kappa}\big(\eta\mp\langle\xi\rangle\big)e^{i\xi_{2}y}\widehat{f}(\xi)d\xi_{2}.

By dominated convergence theorem, the statement follows from the claim:

(24)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\langle\xi_{1},\eta\rangle^{2s-1}|\eta^{2}-\xi_{1}^{2}-1|^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big|\widehat{\kappa}\big(\eta\pm\langle\xi\rangle\big)\big|\frac{|\widehat{f}(\xi)|}{\langle\xi\rangle^{s}}d\xi_{2}\Big)^{2}d\eta d\xi_{1}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

In the cases
|\eta|\ll\langle\xi\rangle
and
|\eta|\gg\langle\xi\rangle
, the bound is easy due to the Schwartz decay of
\widehat{\kappa}
. When
|\eta|\sim\langle\xi\rangle
, we bound the kernel by

K_{\xi_{1}}(\xi_{2},\eta):=\chi_{|\eta|\sim\langle\xi\rangle}\frac{||\eta|-\langle\xi_{1}\rangle|^{\frac{1}{4}}}{\langle\xi\rangle^{\frac{1}{4}}\langle|\eta|-\langle\xi\rangle\rangle^{M}},

and consider the subcases:

i)
|\xi_{2}|\gtrsim\langle\xi_{1}\rangle
,

ii)
|\xi_{2}|\ll\langle\xi_{1}\rangle
and
||\eta|-\langle\xi_{1}\rangle|\lesssim\frac{|\xi_{2}|^{2}}{\langle\xi_{1}\rangle}
,

iii)
|\xi_{2}|\ll\langle\xi_{1}\rangle
and
||\eta|-\langle\xi_{1}\rangle|\gg\frac{|\xi_{2}|^{2}}{\langle\xi_{1}\rangle}
.

In case i), we have

\sup_{\xi_{1},\xi_{2}}\int K_{\xi_{1}}(\xi_{2},\eta)d\eta\lesssim 1,\text{ and }\sup_{\xi_{1},\eta}\int K_{\xi_{1}}(\xi_{2},\eta)d\xi_{2}\lesssim 1,

which suffices. The second inequality follows from the change of variable
\rho=\langle\xi\rangle
, noting that
d\rho=\frac{|\xi_{2}|}{\langle\xi\rangle}d\xi_{2}\sim d\xi_{2}.

In case ii), the kernel is bounded by
|\xi_{2}|^{\frac{1}{2}}\langle\xi_{1}\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}}\langle|\eta|-\langle\xi\rangle\rangle^{-M}
. We have

\int K_{\xi_{1}}(\xi_{2},\eta)d\eta\lesssim|\xi_{2}|^{\frac{1}{2}}\langle\xi_{1}\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}},\text{ and }\int K_{\xi_{1}}(\xi_{2},\eta)|\xi_{2}|^{\frac{1}{2}}\langle\xi_{1}\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}}d\xi_{2}\lesssim 1,

by the same change of variable in the second integral.

In case iii), the kernel is bounded by

\chi_{|\xi_{2}|\ll(|\eta|-\langle\xi_{1}\rangle|)^{1/2}\langle\xi_{1}\rangle^{1/2}}\langle\xi_{1}\rangle^{-\frac{1}{4}}\langle|\eta|-\langle\xi_{1}\rangle\rangle^{-M+\frac{1}{4}}
. Therefore,

\int K_{\xi_{1}}(\xi_{2},\eta)d\eta\lesssim\langle\xi_{1}\rangle^{-\frac{1}{4}},\text{ and }

\int K_{\xi_{1}}(\xi_{2},\eta)d\xi_{2}\lesssim\langle\xi_{1}\rangle^{-\frac{1}{4}}\langle|\eta|-\langle\xi_{1}\rangle\rangle^{-M+\frac{1}{4}}(|\eta|-\langle\xi_{1}\rangle|)^{1/2}\langle\xi_{1}\rangle^{1/2}\lesssim\langle\xi_{1}\rangle^{\frac{1}{4}},

which suffices.
∎

Proposition 3.5
.

(Trace lemmas for the boundary operators)
Fix
s_{1},s_{2}\geq-\frac{1}{2}
and assume that
\rho
is mean zero and it’s first moment is zero. For
\chi_{t>0}k\in\mathcal{H}^{s_{1}}_{S}
, we have
S_{0}^{t}(0,k)\in C^{0}_{t}H^{s_{1}}_{x,y}\cap C^{0}_{y}\mathcal{H}^{s_{1}}_{S}
. Similarly, for
\chi_{t>0}\ell\in\mathcal{H}^{s_{2}}_{W}
, we have
W_{0}^{t}(0,\ell)\in C^{0}_{t}H^{s_{2}}_{x,y}\cap C^{0}_{y}\mathcal{H}^{s_{2}}_{W}
.

Proof.

We first consider the Schrödinger part.
We start with
C^{0}_{t}H^{s_{1}}_{x,y}
assuming that the boundary data is in
\mathcal{H}^{s_{1}}_{S}
. The claim is immediate for
S_{1}
.
For
S_{2}
, letting

\widehat{g}(\xi):=e^{it(\xi_{2}^{2}-\xi_{1}^{2})}\xi_{2}\ \widehat{\chi_{t>0}k}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2}^{2}-\xi^{2}_{1})

and by using the time continuity of Schrödinger evolution in
H^{s}
, it suffices to prove that

T(g)(x,y)=\int\rho(\xi_{2}y)g(x,\widehat{\xi_{2}})d\xi_{2}

is bounded in
H^{s_{1}}
. We have

\widehat{T(g)}(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})=\int\widehat{\rho}(\theta_{2}/\xi_{2})\widehat{g}(\theta_{1},\xi_{2})\frac{d\xi_{2}}{\xi_{2}}=\int\widehat{\rho}(\eta)\widehat{g}(\theta_{1},\theta_{2}/\eta)\frac{d\eta}{\eta}.

Therefore,

\|T(g)\|_{H^{s_{1}}}\leq\int|\widehat{\rho}(\eta)|\big\|\langle\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\rangle^{s_{1}}\widehat{g}(\theta_{1},\theta_{2}/\eta)\big\|_{L^{2}_{\theta_{1},\theta_{2}}}\frac{d\eta}{|\eta|}

Note that

\big\|\langle\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\rangle^{s_{1}}\widehat{g}(\theta_{1},\theta_{2}/\eta)\big\|_{L^{2}_{\theta_{1},\theta_{2}}}\lesssim\|g\|_{H^{s_{1}}}\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\langle\eta\rangle^{s_{1}}\eta^{\frac{1}{2}},&s_{1}\geq 0\\
|\eta|^{s_{1}+\frac{1}{2}}+|\eta|^{\frac{1}{2}},&s_{1}<0.\end{array}\right.

This finishes the proof by the assumptions on
\rho
.

We now prove that
S_{0}^{t}(0,k)\in C^{0}_{y}\mathcal{H}^{s_{1}}_{S}
. Note that by (
7
), we have (uniformly in
y
)

|[S_{0}^{t}(0,k)](\widehat{\xi_{1}},y,\widehat{\beta})|\lesssim|\widehat{\chi_{t>0}k}(\xi_{1},\beta)|.

Therefore, by dominated convergence theorem,

\|S_{0}^{t}(0,k)\|_{C^{0}_{y}\mathcal{H}^{s_{1}}_{S}}\lesssim\|\chi_{t>0}k\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s_{1}}_{S}}.

The proof of
W_{0}^{t}(0,\ell)\in C^{0}_{y}\mathcal{H}^{s_{2}}_{W}
is analogous.

It remains to see that
W_{0}^{t}(0,\ell)\in C^{0}_{t}H^{s_{2}}_{x,y}
. Once again, for
W_{1}
, the proof is easy. For
W_{2}
,

W_{2}(\ell)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\eta^{2}+1>\beta^{2}}e^{ix\eta+i\beta t}\rho(y\sqrt{\eta^{2}+1-\beta^{2}})\widehat{\chi_{t>0}\ell}(\eta,\beta)d\beta d\eta

Noting that the effect of each
x
or
y
derivative is bounded by
\langle\eta\rangle
on the Fourier side, it suffices to consider the case
-\frac{1}{2}\leq s_{2}\leq 0
. We write
W_{2}(\ell)
as

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{|\beta|<\langle\eta\rangle/2}e^{ix\eta+i\beta t}\rho(y\sqrt{\eta^{2}+1-\beta^{2}})\widehat{\chi_{t>0}\ell}(\eta,\beta)d\beta d\eta

+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\langle\eta\rangle/2<|\beta|<\langle\eta\rangle}e^{ix\eta+i\beta t}\rho(y\sqrt{\eta^{2}+1-\beta^{2}})\widehat{\chi_{t>0}\ell}(\eta,\beta)d\beta d\eta.

For the first summand, taking Fourier transform, it suffices to prove that

\Big\|\langle\eta,\theta\rangle^{s_{2}}\int_{|\beta|<\langle\eta\rangle/2}\frac{|\widehat{\rho}(\theta/\sqrt{\eta^{2}+1-\beta^{2}})|}{\sqrt{\eta^{2}+1-\beta^{2}}}|\widehat{\chi_{t>0}\ell}(\eta,\beta)|d\beta\Big\|_{L^{2}_{\eta,\theta}}\lesssim\|\widehat{\chi_{t>0}\ell}(\eta,\beta)\langle\eta\rangle^{s_{2}}\|_{L^{2}_{\eta,\beta}}

Since
s_{2}\leq 0
, we have
\langle\eta,\theta\rangle^{s_{2}}\leq\langle\eta\rangle^{s_{2}}
. Taking
L^{2}_{\theta}
norm inside, we bound the left hand side by

\Big\|\int_{|\beta|<\langle\eta\rangle/2}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\langle\eta\rangle}}|\widehat{\chi_{t>0}\ell}(\eta,\beta)|\langle\eta\rangle^{s_{2}}d\beta\Big\|_{L^{2}_{\eta}}.

The claim follows by Cauchy-Schwarz inequality in
\beta
integral.

We rewrite the second summand as

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{|\xi_{2}|\leq\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\langle\xi_{1}\rangle}e^{ix\xi_{1}}e^{it{\rm sign}(\xi_{2})\sqrt{1+\xi_{1}^{2}-\xi_{2}^{2}}}\rho(y|\xi_{2}|)\widehat{\chi_{t>0}\ell}\big(\xi_{1},{\rm sign}(\xi_{2})\sqrt{1+\xi_{1}^{2}-\xi_{2}^{2}}\big)\frac{|\xi_{2}|d\xi}{\sqrt{1+\xi_{1}^{2}-\xi_{2}^{2}}}.

Note that this is
T(g)(x,y)
with

\widehat{g}(\xi):=e^{it{\rm sign}(\xi_{2})\sqrt{1+\xi_{1}^{2}-\xi_{2}^{2}}}\frac{|\xi_{2}|}{\sqrt{1+\xi_{1}^{2}-\xi_{2}^{2}}}\widehat{\chi_{t>0}\ell}\big(\xi_{1},{\rm sign}(\xi_{2})\sqrt{1+\xi_{1}^{2}-\xi_{2}^{2}}\big)\chi_{|\xi_{2}|\leq\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\langle\xi_{1}\rangle}.

The claim follows from the
H^{s_{2}}
boundedness of
T
that we proved above by dominated convergence theorem after noting that
H^{s_{2}}
norm of
g
is bounded by
\mathcal{H}^{s_{2}}_{W}
norm of
\chi_{t>0}\ell
.
∎

We now establish Kato smoothing estimates for the Duhamel integrals.

Proposition 3.6
.

(Kato smoothing for Schrödinger Duhamel) For any
b<\frac{1}{2}
, we have

(25)

\Big\|\kappa\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-t^{\prime})\Delta}Gdt^{\prime}\Big\|_{C^{0}_{y}\mathcal{H}^{s_{1}}_{S}}\lesssim\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\|G\|_{X_{S}^{s_{1},-b}}&\text{ for }0\leq s_{1}\leq\frac{1}{2},\\
\|G\|_{X_{S}^{s_{1},-b}}+\|G\|_{X_{S}^{\frac{1}{2}+,\frac{s_{1}}{2}-\frac{3}{4}}}&\text{ for }\frac{1}{2}<s_{1}.\end{array}\right.

Proof.

By dominated convergence theorem, it suffices to consider the evaluation at
y=0
.

\mathcal{F}_{x\to\xi}\Big(\kappa(t)D_{y=0}\Big(\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-t^{\prime})\Delta}Gdt^{\prime}\Big)\Big)=\kappa(t)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{e^{it\lambda}-e^{-it(\xi^{2}+\theta^{2})}}{i(\lambda+\xi^{2}+\theta^{2})}\widehat{G}(\xi,\theta,\lambda)d\theta d\lambda

Therefore

\mathcal{F}_{x\to\xi,t\to\beta}\Big(\kappa(t)D_{y=0}\Big(\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-t^{\prime})\Delta}Gdt^{\prime}\Big)\Big)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{\widehat{\kappa}(\beta-\lambda)-\widehat{\kappa}(\beta+\xi^{2}+\theta^{2})}{i(\lambda+\xi^{2}+\theta^{2})}\widehat{G}(\xi,\theta,\lambda)d\theta d\lambda

Therefore the norm is bounded by

\Big\|\eta\langle\eta,\xi\rangle^{s_{1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{\widehat{\kappa}(\pm\eta^{2}-\xi^{2}-\lambda)-\widehat{\kappa}(\pm\eta^{2}+\theta^{2})}{\lambda+\xi^{2}+\theta^{2}}\widehat{G}(\xi,\theta,\lambda)d\theta d\lambda\Big\|_{L^{2}_{\xi,\eta}}.

We first consider the case
|\lambda+\xi^{2}+\theta^{2}|<1
, by the mean value theorem we estimate the norm in this case by

\Big\|\eta\langle\eta,\xi\rangle^{s_{1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\chi_{|\lambda+\xi^{2}+\theta^{2}|<1}\frac{1}{\langle\eta^{2}-\theta^{2}\rangle^{M}}|\widehat{G}(\xi,\theta,\lambda)|d\theta d\lambda\Big\|_{L^{2}_{\xi,\eta}}\lesssim\\
\Big\|\eta\langle\eta,\xi\rangle^{s_{1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{1}{\langle\eta^{2}-\theta^{2}\rangle^{M}}\|\widehat{G}(\xi,\theta,\lambda)\|_{L^{2}_{\lambda}}d\theta\Big\|_{L^{2}_{\xi,\eta}}

In the last inequality we used Cauchy-Schwarz inequality in
\lambda
integral.
As above it suffices to prove that the operator with kernel

K_{\xi}(\eta,\theta)=\frac{|\eta|\langle\eta,\xi\rangle^{s_{1}}}{\langle\theta,\xi\rangle^{s_{1}}}\frac{1}{\langle\eta^{2}-\theta^{2}\rangle^{M}}

is bounded
L^{2}_{\theta}
to
L^{2}_{\eta}
uniformly in
\xi
for sufficiently large
M
.
In the case
|\eta|\lesssim|\theta|
both
\eta
and
\theta
integrals of the kernel are
\lesssim 1
using

\int\frac{1}{\langle\eta^{2}-\theta^{2}\rangle^{M}}d\theta\lesssim\langle\eta\rangle^{-1}.

In the case
|\eta|\gg|\theta|
, the kernel is bounded by
|\eta|^{-M/2}
and the same bounds hold.

In the case
|\lambda+\xi^{2}+\theta^{2}|>1
, we bound the norm by

\Big\|\eta\langle\eta,\xi\rangle^{s_{1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{\langle\pm\eta^{2}-\xi^{2}-\lambda\rangle^{-M}+\langle\pm\eta^{2}+\theta^{2}\rangle^{-M}}{\langle\lambda+\xi^{2}+\theta^{2}\rangle}|\widehat{G}(\xi,\theta,\lambda)|d\theta d\lambda\Big\|_{L^{2}_{\xi,\eta}}.

In the case
\langle\eta,\xi\rangle\lesssim\langle\theta,\xi\rangle
, letting

L:=\langle\theta,\xi\rangle^{s_{1}}\langle\lambda+\xi^{2}+\theta^{2}\rangle^{-b}|\widehat{G}(\xi,\theta,\lambda)|,

and noting that
\|L\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|G\|_{X^{s_{1},-b}}
, it suffices to prove that
the operator with kernel

K_{\xi}(\eta,(\theta,\lambda))=\eta\frac{\langle\pm\eta^{2}-\xi^{2}-\lambda\rangle^{-M}+\langle\pm\eta^{2}+\theta^{2}\rangle^{-M}}{\langle\lambda+\xi^{2}+\theta^{2}\rangle^{1-b}}

is bounded
L^{2}_{\theta,\lambda}\to L^{2}_{\eta}
uniformly in
\xi
. The
\eta
integral is bounded by
\langle\lambda+\xi^{2}+\theta^{2}\rangle^{-1+b}
by the change of variable
\rho=\eta^{2}
. By the weighted Schur test the following bound suffices:

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\eta|\frac{\langle\pm\eta^{2}-\xi^{2}-\lambda\rangle^{-M}+\langle\pm\eta^{2}+\theta^{2}\rangle^{-M}}{\langle\lambda+\xi^{2}+\theta^{2}\rangle^{2-2b}}d\theta d\lambda\lesssim|\eta|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{1}{\langle\theta^{2}-\eta^{2}\rangle^{2-2b}}d\theta\lesssim 1.

Last inequality follows by considering the cases
|\theta|\ll|\eta|
and
|\theta|\gtrsim|\eta|
.

In the case
\langle\eta,\xi\rangle\gg\langle\theta,\xi\rangle
, which implies
|\eta|\gg\langle\theta,\xi\rangle
, we consider the cases
0\leq s_{1}\leq\frac{1}{2}
and
s_{1}>\frac{1}{2}
seperately. In the former case we have the following kernel

K_{\xi}(\eta,(\theta,\lambda))=\frac{|\eta|^{1+s_{1}}}{\langle\theta,\xi\rangle^{s_{1}}}\frac{\langle\pm\eta^{2}-\xi^{2}-\lambda\rangle^{-M}+|\eta|^{-2M}}{\langle\lambda+\xi^{2}+\theta^{2}\rangle^{1-b}}.

We omit the contribution of
|\eta|^{-2M}
which can be handled as above. For the remaining part note that

\int K_{\xi}(\eta,(\theta,\lambda))|\eta|^{-s_{1}}d\eta\lesssim\langle\theta,\xi\rangle^{-s_{1}}\langle\lambda+\xi^{2}+\theta^{2}\rangle^{-1+b}.

Therefore, the following bound suffices

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}K_{\xi}(\eta,(\theta,\lambda))\langle\theta,\xi\rangle^{-s_{1}}\langle\lambda+\xi^{2}+\theta^{2}\rangle^{-1+b}d\theta d\lambda\lesssim|\eta|^{-s_{1}}.

Note that after the
\lambda
integral we have the bound

\int_{|\theta|\ll|\eta|}\frac{|\eta|^{1+s_{1}}}{\langle\theta,\xi\rangle^{2s_{1}}|\eta|^{4-4b}}d\theta\lesssim|\eta|^{1-2s_{1}+}|\eta|^{4b-3+s_{1}}\lesssim|\eta|^{-s_{1}}

for
b<\frac{1}{2}
.

In the latter case, it suffices to prove that the operator with kernel

K_{\xi}(\eta,(\theta,\lambda))=\frac{|\eta|^{1+s_{1}}}{\langle\theta,\xi\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}+}}\frac{\langle\pm\eta^{2}-\xi^{2}-\lambda\rangle^{-M}+|\eta|^{-2M}}{\langle\lambda+\xi^{2}+\theta^{2}\rangle^{\frac{1}{4}+\frac{s_{1}}{2}}}

is bounded
L^{2}_{\theta,\lambda}\to L^{2}_{\eta}
uniformly in
\xi
. We again ignore the contribution of
|\eta|^{-2M}
.
For the remaining part note that

\int K_{\xi}(\eta,(\theta,\lambda))|\eta|^{-s_{1}}d\eta\lesssim\frac{1}{\langle\theta,\xi\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}+}\langle\lambda+\xi^{2}+\theta^{2}\rangle^{\frac{1}{4}+\frac{s_{1}}{2}}}.

Therefore, the following bound suffices

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{K_{\xi}(\eta,(\theta,\lambda))}{\langle\theta,\xi\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}+}\langle\lambda+\xi^{2}+\theta^{2}\rangle^{\frac{1}{4}+\frac{s_{1}}{2}}}d\theta d\lambda\lesssim|\eta|^{-s_{1}}.

Note that after the
\lambda
integral we have the bound

\int_{|\theta|\ll|\eta|}\frac{|\eta|^{1+s_{1}}}{\langle\theta\rangle^{1+}|\eta|^{1+2s_{1}}}d\theta\lesssim|\eta|^{-s_{1}}.

∎

Proposition 3.7
.

(Kato smoothing for Klein-Gordon Duhamel) We will take
b<\frac{1}{2}
.

\left\|\kappa(t)\int_{0}^{t}e^{\pm i(t-t^{\prime})\langle\nabla\rangle}Gdt^{\prime}\right\|_{C^{0}_{y}\mathcal{H}^{s}_{W}}\lesssim\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\|G\|_{X^{s,-b}_{W}}+\Big\|\frac{\chi_{|\lambda|\ll\langle\xi\rangle}\langle\lambda\rangle^{s}|\widehat{G}(\xi,\lambda)|}{\langle\xi\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}-}}\Big\|_{L^{2}_{\lambda,\xi}}&\text{ for }s<0,\\
\|G\|_{X^{s,-b}_{W}}&\text{ for }0\leq s\leq\frac{1}{2},\\
\|G\|_{X^{s,-b}_{W}}+\|G\|_{X_{W}^{\frac{1}{2}+,s-1}}&\text{ for }\frac{1}{2}<s.\end{array}\right.

Proof.

By dominated convergence theorem, it suffices to consider the evaluation at
y=0
.

\mathcal{F}_{x\to\xi_{1}}\Big(\kappa(t)D_{y=0}\Big(\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-t^{\prime})\langle\nabla\rangle}Gdt^{\prime}\Big)\Big)=\kappa(t)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{e^{it\lambda}-e^{it\langle\xi\rangle}}{i(\lambda-\langle\xi\rangle)}\widehat{G}(\xi,\lambda)d\xi_{2}d\lambda

Therefore

\mathcal{F}_{x\to\xi_{1},t\to\beta}\Big(\kappa(t)D_{y=0}\Big(\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-t^{\prime})\langle\nabla\rangle}Gdt^{\prime}\Big)\Big)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{\widehat{\kappa}(\beta-\lambda)-\widehat{\kappa}(\beta-\langle\xi\rangle)}{i(\lambda-\langle\xi\rangle)}\widehat{G}(\xi,\lambda)d\xi_{2}d\lambda.

The
\mathcal{H}^{s}_{W}
norm is

(26)

\Big\|\langle\xi_{1},\beta\rangle^{s-\frac{1}{2}}|\beta^{2}-\langle\xi_{1}\rangle^{2}|^{\frac{1}{4}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{\widehat{\kappa}(\beta-\lambda)-\widehat{\kappa}(\beta-\langle\xi\rangle)}{i(\lambda-\langle\xi\rangle)}\widehat{G}(\xi,\lambda)d\xi_{2}d\lambda\Big\|_{L^{2}_{\xi_{1},\beta}}.

Considering the cases
|\lambda-\langle\xi\rangle|<1
and
|\lambda-\langle\xi\rangle|\geq 1
separately, we have the bound

\frac{|\widehat{\kappa}(\beta-\lambda)-\widehat{\kappa}(\beta-\langle\xi\rangle)|}{|\lambda-\langle\xi\rangle|}\lesssim\frac{\langle\beta-\lambda\rangle^{-M}+\langle\beta-\langle\xi\rangle\rangle^{-M}}{\langle\lambda-\langle\xi\rangle\rangle}.

We start with the contribution of the term
\langle\beta-\langle\xi\rangle\rangle^{-M}
in the numerator above.
Letting

(27)

L(\xi,\lambda):=\langle\xi\rangle^{s}\langle\lambda-\langle\xi\rangle\rangle^{-b}|\widehat{G}(\xi,\lambda)|,

and noting that
\|L\|_{L^{2}_{\xi,\lambda}}\lesssim\|G\|_{X^{s,-b}}
, it suffices to prove that

(28)

\Big\|\langle\xi_{1},\beta\rangle^{s-\frac{1}{2}}|\beta^{2}-\langle\xi_{1}\rangle^{2}|^{\frac{1}{4}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{\langle\beta-\langle\xi\rangle\rangle^{-M}}{\langle\xi\rangle^{s}\langle\lambda-\langle\xi\rangle\rangle^{1-b}}L(\xi,\lambda)d\xi_{2}d\lambda\Big\|_{L^{2}_{\xi_{1},\beta}}\lesssim\|L\|_{L^{2}_{\xi,\lambda}}.

After a Cauchy-Schwarz inequality in
\lambda
integral, we obtain

\Big\|\langle\xi_{1},\beta\rangle^{s-\frac{1}{2}}|\beta^{2}-\langle\xi_{1}\rangle^{2}|^{\frac{1}{4}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{1}{\langle\xi\rangle^{s}\langle|\beta|-\langle\xi\rangle\rangle^{M}}\|L(\xi,\lambda)\|_{L^{2}_{\lambda}}d\xi_{2}\Big\|_{L^{2}_{\xi_{1},\beta}}.

This can be bounded using the estimate for (
24
) above.

We now consider the contribution of the term
\langle\beta-\lambda\rangle^{-M}
in the numerator above. The correction terms on the right hand side of the assertion of the proposition arise in this case when
|\beta|\not\sim\langle\xi\rangle
. In the region
|\beta|\sim\langle\xi\rangle
, we use the function
L
defined in (
27
) and bound the contribution by

(29)

\Big\|\langle\xi_{1},\beta\rangle^{s-\frac{1}{2}}|\beta^{2}-\langle\xi_{1}\rangle^{2}|^{\frac{1}{4}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\chi_{|\beta|\sim\langle\xi\rangle}\frac{\langle\beta-\lambda\rangle^{-M}}{\langle\xi\rangle^{s}\langle\lambda-\langle\xi\rangle\rangle^{1-b}}L(\xi,\lambda)d\xi_{2}d\lambda\Big\|_{L^{2}_{\xi_{1},\beta}}\\
\lesssim\Big\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}K_{\xi_{1}}(\beta,(\xi_{2},\lambda))L(\xi,\lambda)d\xi_{2}d\lambda\Big\|_{L^{2}_{\xi_{1},\beta}},

where

K_{\xi_{1}}(\beta,(\xi_{2},\lambda))=\chi_{|\beta|\sim\langle\xi\rangle}\frac{||\beta|-\langle\xi_{1}\rangle|^{\frac{1}{4}}}{\langle\xi\rangle^{\frac{1}{4}}\langle\lambda-\langle\xi\rangle\rangle^{1-b}\langle\beta-\lambda\rangle^{M}}.

We need to prove that (
29
)
\lesssim\|L\|_{L^{2}_{\xi,\lambda}}
.

Consider the subcases:

i)
|\xi_{2}|\gtrsim\langle\xi_{1}\rangle
,

ii)
|\xi_{2}|\ll\langle\xi_{1}\rangle
and
||\beta|-\langle\xi_{1}\rangle|\lesssim\frac{|\xi_{2}|^{2}}{\langle\xi_{1}\rangle}
,

iii)
|\xi_{2}|\ll\langle\xi_{1}\rangle
and
||\beta|-\langle\xi_{1}\rangle|\gg\frac{|\xi_{2}|^{2}}{\langle\xi_{1}\rangle}
.

In case i), we have

\int K_{\xi_{1}}(\beta,(\xi_{2},\lambda))d\beta\lesssim\frac{1}{\langle\lambda-\langle\xi\rangle\rangle^{1-b}},\text{ and }

\int\frac{K_{\xi_{1}}(\beta,(\xi_{2},\lambda))}{\langle\lambda-\langle\xi\rangle\rangle^{1-b}}d\xi_{2}d\lambda\lesssim\int\frac{1}{\langle\beta-\langle\xi\rangle\rangle^{2-2b}}d\xi_{2}\lesssim 1,

which suffices. The last inequality follows from the change of variable
\rho=\langle\xi\rangle
, noting that
d\rho=\frac{|\xi_{2}|}{\langle\xi\rangle}d\xi_{2}\sim d\xi_{2}.

In case ii), the kernel is bounded by
|\xi_{2}|^{\frac{1}{2}}\langle\xi_{1}\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}}\langle\lambda-\langle\xi\rangle\rangle^{-1+b}\langle\beta-\lambda\rangle^{-M}
. We have

\int K_{\xi_{1}}(\beta,(\xi_{2},\lambda))d\beta\lesssim\frac{|\xi_{2}|^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\langle\xi_{1}\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}\langle\lambda-\langle\xi\rangle\rangle^{1-b}},\text{ and }

\int\frac{|\xi_{2}|^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\langle\xi_{1}\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}\langle\lambda-\langle\xi\rangle\rangle^{1-b}}K_{\xi_{1}}(\beta,(\xi_{2},\lambda))d\xi_{2}d\lambda\lesssim\int\frac{|\xi_{2}|}{\langle\xi_{1}\rangle\langle\beta-\langle\xi\rangle\rangle^{2-2b}}d\xi_{2}\lesssim 1,

by the same change of variable in the last integral.

In case iii), noting that

\langle\beta-\lambda\rangle\langle\lambda-\langle\xi\rangle\rangle\gtrsim\langle|\beta|-\langle\xi\rangle\rangle=\langle|\beta|-\langle\xi_{1}\rangle+O(\xi_{2}^{2}/\langle\xi_{1}\rangle)\rangle\sim\langle|\beta|-\langle\xi_{1}\rangle\rangle,

the kernel is bounded by

\chi_{|\xi_{2}|\ll(|\beta|-\langle\xi_{1}\rangle|)^{1/2}\langle\xi_{1}\rangle^{1/2}}\langle\xi_{1}\rangle^{-\frac{1}{4}}\langle|\beta|-\langle\xi_{1}\rangle\rangle^{b-\frac{3}{4}}\langle\beta-\lambda\rangle^{-M/2}
. Therefore,

\int K_{\xi_{1}}(\beta,(\xi_{2},\lambda))d\lambda d\xi_{2}\lesssim\langle|\beta|-\langle\xi_{1}\rangle\rangle^{b-\frac{1}{4}}\langle\xi_{1}\rangle^{\frac{1}{4}},\text{ and }

\int K_{\xi_{1}}(\beta,(\xi_{2},\lambda))\langle|\beta|-\langle\xi_{1}\rangle\rangle^{b-\frac{1}{4}}\langle\xi_{1}\rangle^{\frac{1}{4}}d\beta\lesssim\int\langle|\beta|-\langle\xi_{1}\rangle\rangle^{2b-1}\langle\beta-\lambda\rangle^{-M/2}d\beta\lesssim 1.

Now consider the region
|\beta|\ll\langle\xi\rangle
. For
s\geq 0
, we use the function
L
defined in (
27
) and bound the contribution by

(30)

\Big\|\langle\xi_{1},\beta\rangle^{s-\frac{1}{2}}|\beta^{2}-\langle\xi_{1}\rangle^{2}|^{\frac{1}{4}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\chi_{|\beta|\ll\langle\xi\rangle}\frac{\langle\beta-\lambda\rangle^{-M}}{\langle\xi\rangle^{s}\langle\lambda-\langle\xi\rangle\rangle^{1-b}}L(\xi,\lambda)d\xi_{2}d\lambda\Big\|_{L^{2}_{\xi_{1},\beta}}\\
\lesssim\Big\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}K_{\xi_{1}}(\beta,(\xi_{2},\lambda))L(\xi,\lambda)d\xi_{2}d\lambda\Big\|_{L^{2}_{\xi_{1},\beta}},

where

K_{\xi_{1}}(\beta,(\xi_{2},\lambda))=\chi_{|\beta|\ll\langle\xi\rangle}\frac{1}{\langle\xi\rangle^{1-b}\langle\beta-\lambda\rangle^{M/2}}.

We need to prove that (
30
)
\lesssim\|L\|_{L^{2}_{\xi,\lambda}}
, which follows from

\int K_{\xi_{1}}(\beta,(\xi_{2},\lambda))d\beta\lesssim\langle\xi\rangle^{b-1},\text{ and }

\int\langle\xi\rangle^{b-1}K_{\xi_{1}}(\beta,(\xi_{2},\lambda))d\xi_{2}d\lambda\lesssim\int\frac{d\xi_{2}d\lambda}{\langle\xi_{2}\rangle^{2-2b}\langle\beta-\lambda\rangle^{M/2}}\lesssim 1.

For
s<0
when
|\beta|\ll\langle\xi\rangle
, the contribution to (
26
) is bounded by

\Big\|\langle\xi_{1},\beta\rangle^{s-\frac{1}{2}}|\beta^{2}-\langle\xi_{1}\rangle^{2}|^{\frac{1}{4}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\chi_{|\beta|\ll\langle\xi\rangle}\frac{\langle\lambda-\beta\rangle^{-M}}{\langle\lambda-\langle\xi\rangle\rangle}\widehat{G}(\xi,\lambda)d\xi_{2}d\lambda\Big\|_{L^{2}_{\xi_{1},\beta}}.

When
|\lambda|\gtrsim\langle\xi\rangle
the kernel is bounded by
\langle\lambda\rangle^{-M}
, which suffices to bound the contribution by
\|G\|_{X^{s,-b}_{W}}
. In the case
|\lambda|\ll\langle\xi\rangle
, we have the bound

\Big\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\chi_{|\lambda|\ll\langle\xi\rangle}\frac{\langle\lambda\rangle^{s}}{\langle\lambda-\beta\rangle^{M/2}\langle\xi\rangle}\widehat{G}(\xi,\lambda)d\xi_{2}d\lambda\Big\|_{L^{2}_{\xi_{1},\beta}}.

This can be bounded by the correction term in the proposition by Cauchy-Schwarz inequality in
\xi_{2}
integral and by Young’s inequality.

Finally, we consider the region
|\beta|\gg\langle\xi\rangle
. For
s\leq\frac{1}{2}
, we use the function
L
defined in (
27

0\leq a<\min(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}+s_{2},1+s_{2}-s_{1})
provided that
b<\frac{1}{2}
is sufficiently close to
\frac{1}{2}
. Noting that the required bound follows from (

We first consider the range

In the case

, using the second claim of Corollary

It suffices to note that

provided that
a<\frac{1}{2}
and
b
is sufficiently close to
\frac{1}{2}
.

In the cases
1\leq N_{2}\ll N_{1}\sim N
and
1\leq N_{1}\ll N_{2}\sim N
, by Proposition
4.10
we have

|I|\lesssim\frac{\sqrt{L_{1}L_{2}L}}{\sqrt{\max(L_{1},L_{2},L)}}\lesssim(L_{2}L)^{b}L_{1}^{1-2b}\lesssim\frac{N^{s_{2}}N_{2}^{s_{1}}}{N_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}+}}(L_{2}L)^{b}L_{1}^{\frac{3}{4}-\frac{s_{1}+a}{2}},

for
a<s_{2}-s_{1}+1
provided that
b
is sufficiently close to
\frac{1}{2}
.

In the case
N\sim 1
, as in the proof of Proposition
4.1
above, we have

\frac{|I|}{(L_{2}L)^{b}L_{1}^{\frac{3}{4}-\frac{s_{1}+a}{2}}}\lesssim\sup_{|\xi_{2}|\sim N_{1},\tau_{2}}\sqrt{\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{\chi_{|\xi|\lesssim N}}{\langle\tau+\tau_{2}+|\xi+\xi_{2}|^{2}\rangle^{\frac{3}{2}-s_{1}-a}\langle\tau_{2}+|\xi_{2}|^{2}\rangle^{2b}\langle\tau\pm|\xi|\rangle^{2b}}\ d\tau d\xi}

\lesssim\sup_{|\xi_{2}|\sim N_{1}}\sqrt{\int_{|\xi|\lesssim 1}\frac{1}{\langle|\xi+\xi_{2}|^{2}-|\xi_{2}|^{2}\rangle^{2b+\frac{1}{2}-s_{1}-a}}\ d\xi}\lesssim\frac{1}{N_{1}^{b+\frac{1}{4}-\frac{s_{1}+a}{2}}}\lesssim N_{1}^{s_{1}-\frac{1}{2}-},

which holds for
a<\frac{1}{2}
.

We now consider the range
\frac{3}{2}\leq s_{1}+a<\frac{5}{2}
. We have two cases:
N_{1}\sim\max(N,N_{2})
and
N_{1}\ll N_{2}\sim N
.

In the former case, since
L_{1}\gg N_{1}^{2}\gtrsim|\tau_{1}+|\xi_{1}|^{2}-\tau_{2}-|\xi_{2}|^{2}-(\tau_{1}-\tau_{2}\pm|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}|)|
,
we have
L_{1}\lesssim\max(L,L_{2})
. Without loss of generality assume
L_{1}\lesssim L
. We have

\frac{|I|}{(L_{2}L)^{b}L_{1}^{\frac{3}{4}-\frac{s_{1}+a}{2}}}\lesssim\frac{|I|}{L_{2}^{b}L^{0+}L_{1}^{b+\frac{3}{4}-\frac{s_{1}+a}{2}-}}\lesssim\frac{|I|}{L_{2}^{b}L^{0+}N_{1}^{2b+\frac{3}{2}-s_{1}-a-}}

\lesssim N_{1}^{-2b-\frac{3}{2}+s_{1}+a+}\sup_{|\xi_{1}|\sim N_{1},\tau_{1}+|\xi_{1}|^{2}\sim L_{1}}\sqrt{\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{\chi_{|\xi_{2}|\sim N_{2}}\chi_{|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}|\sim N}}{\langle\tau_{2}+|\xi_{2}|^{2}\rangle^{2b}\langle\tau_{1}-\tau_{2}\pm|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}|\rangle^{0+}}\ d\tau_{2}d\xi_{2}}

\lesssim N_{1}^{-2b-\frac{3}{2}+s_{1}+a+}\sup_{|\xi_{1}|\sim N_{1},\tau_{1}+|\xi_{1}|^{2}\sim L_{1}}\sqrt{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\chi_{|\xi_{2}|\sim N_{2}}\chi_{|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}|\sim N}\ d\xi_{2}}

\lesssim N_{1}^{s_{1}+a-\frac{5}{2}+}\min(N,N_{2})\lesssim\frac{N^{s_{2}}N_{2}^{s_{1}}}{N_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}+}},

which holds under the conditions of the proposition.

In the latter case, if
L_{1}\lesssim\max(L,L_{2})
the argument above remains valid. In the case
L_{1}\gg\max(L,L_{2})
, we have
L_{1}\sim N_{2}^{2}
. Therefore, we have

\frac{|I|}{(L_{2}L)^{b}L_{1}^{\frac{3}{4}-\frac{s_{1}+a}{2}}}\lesssim N_{2}^{s_{1}+a-\frac{3}{2}}\sup_{|\xi_{1}|\sim N_{1},\tau_{1}+|\xi_{1}|^{2}\sim L_{1}}\sqrt{\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{\chi_{|\xi_{2}|\sim N_{2}}\chi_{|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}|\sim N}}{\langle\tau_{2}+|\xi_{2}|^{2}\rangle^{2b}\langle\tau_{1}-\tau_{2}\pm|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}|\rangle^{2b}}\ d\tau_{2}d\xi_{2}}

\lesssim N_{2}^{s_{1}+a-\frac{3}{2}}\sup_{|\xi_{1}|\sim N_{1},\tau_{1}+|\xi_{1}|^{2}\sim L_{1}}\sqrt{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{\chi_{|\xi_{2}|\sim N_{2}}\chi_{|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}|\sim N}}{\langle\tau_{1}+|\xi_{2}|^{2}\pm|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}|\rangle^{4b-1}}\ d\xi_{2}}\lesssim N_{2}^{s_{1}+a-\frac{3}{2}+2-4b}

6.4
.
∎

Proof of Proposition

Once again, it suffices to prove (

|I(f,g_{1},g_{2})|\lesssim\frac{N_{1}^{s_{1}}N_{2}^{s_{1}}}{N^{\frac{3}{2}+}}(L_{1}L_{2})^{b}L^{1-s_{2}-a}

when
\frac{1}{2}<s_{2}+a<\frac{3}{2}
,

,
provided that
b<\frac{1}{2}
is sufficiently close to
\frac{1}{2}
. Note that this follows from (
33
) if

, and hence we can assume
L\gg N
.

First consider the case
\frac{1}{2}<s_{2}+a<1
.
In the case
1\ll N\lesssim N_{1}\sim N_{2}
, using the second claim of Corollary
4.12
, we have

|I(f,g_{1},g_{2})|\lesssim\frac{\sqrt{L_{1}L_{2}L}}{\sqrt{\max(L_{1},L_{2},L)}}\lesssim\frac{(L_{1}L_{2})^{b}L^{1-s_{2}-a}}{\max(L_{1},L_{2},L)^{2b-s_{2}-a}}

\lesssim\frac{(L_{1}L_{2})^{b}L^{1-s_{2}-a}}{N^{2b-s_{2}-a}}\lesssim\frac{N_{1}^{2s_{1}}}{N^{\frac{3}{2}+}}(L_{1}L_{2})^{b}L^{1-s_{2}-a},

In the case

\frac{|I|}{(L_{1}L_{2})^{b}L^{1-s_{2}-a}}\lesssim\sup_{|\xi_{2}|\sim N_{1},\tau_{2}}\sqrt{\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{\chi_{|\xi|\lesssim N}}{\langle\tau+\tau_{2}+|\xi+\xi_{2}|^{2}\rangle^{2b}\langle\tau_{2}+|\xi_{2}|^{2}\rangle^{2b}\langle\tau\pm|\xi|\rangle^{2-2s_{2}-2a}}\ d\tau d\xi}.

\lesssim 1\lesssim\frac{N_{1}^{s_{1}}N_{2}^{s_{1}}}{N^{\frac{3}{2}+}}

In the case

\frac{|I|}{(L_{1}L_{2})^{b}L^{1-s_{2}-a}}\lesssim\frac{1}{N^{1-s_{2}-a}}\sup_{|\xi|\sim N,\tau}\sqrt{\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{\chi_{|\xi_{2}|\lesssim N_{2}}}{\langle\tau+\tau_{2}+|\xi+\xi_{2}|^{2}\rangle^{2b}\langle\tau_{2}+|\xi_{2}|^{2}\rangle^{2b}}\ d\tau_{2}d\xi_{2}}

\frac{|I|}{(L_{1}L_{2})^{b}L^{1-s_{2}-a}}\lesssim\sup_{|\xi|\sim N,\tau}\sqrt{\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{\chi_{|\xi_{2}|\lesssim N_{2}}\langle\tau\pm|\xi|\rangle^{2s_{2}+2a-2}}{\langle\tau+\tau_{2}+|\xi+\xi_{2}|^{2}\rangle^{2b}\langle\tau_{2}+|\xi_{2}|^{2}\rangle^{2b}}\ d\tau_{2}d\xi_{2}}

6.
Appendix

Below, we list four lemmas that are used repeatedly throughout the paper. For the proof of the first lemma see the Appendix of
[
11
]
. The second lemma is the weighted Schur’s test, and the last two are elementary calculus lemmas.

Lemma 6.1
.

If
\beta\geq\gamma\geq 0
and
\beta+\gamma>1
, then

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{1}{\langle\tau-k_{1}\rangle^{\beta}\langle\tau-k_{2}\rangle^{\gamma}}d\,\tau\lesssim\langle k_{1}-k_{2}\rangle^{-\gamma}\phi_{\beta}(k_{1}-k_{2}),

where

\phi_{\beta}(k):=\sum_{|n|\leq|k|}\frac{1}{\langle n\rangle^{\beta}}\sim\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1,&\beta>1,\\
\log(1+\langle k\rangle),&\beta=1,\\
\langle k\rangle^{1-\beta},&\beta<1.\end{array}\right.

The statement remains valid when
\langle\tau-k_{2}\rangle
is replaced with
|\tau-k_{2}|
provided that
\gamma<1
.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
T
be an integral operator with kernel
K(\theta,\eta)
,
\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
,
\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Assume that for some positive functions
p(\theta)
,
q(\eta)
, and some constants
A,B
we have

\int|K(\theta,\eta)|p(\theta)d\theta\leq Aq(\eta),\text{ for a.e. }\eta,

\int|K(\theta,\eta)|q(\eta)d\eta\leq Bp(\theta),\text{ for a.e. }\theta,

then
\|T\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\leq\sqrt{AB}
.

Lemma 6.3
.

For any
f\in L^{1}
,
g\in C^{1}
, any measurable
h
, and any
K\in L^{2}
, we have

\Big\|\int f(\tau-g(\eta))h(\eta)K(\eta)d\eta\Big\|_{L^{2}_{\tau}}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{1}}\|K\|_{L^{2}}\sup_{\eta}\frac{|h(\eta)|}{\sqrt{|g^{\prime}(\eta)|}}.

Proof.

Change variables in the
\eta
integral by setting
\rho=g(\eta)
. Then apply Young’s inequality and undo the change of variable.
∎

Lemma 6.4
.

Fix
0\leq\alpha<1
and
M,N\gtrsim 1
. Assume that
f:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
is
C^{1}
and
|f_{x}|\gtrsim N
. We have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{\chi_{|x|,|y|<M}}{\langle f(x,y)\rangle^{\alpha}}dxdy\lesssim M^{2-\alpha}N^{-\alpha}.

Proof.

It suffices to prove that

\sup_{y}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\chi_{|x|<M}}{\langle f(x,y)\rangle^{\alpha}}dx\lesssim M^{1-\alpha}N^{-\alpha}.

The contribution of the set
\{x:\langle f(x,y)\rangle>MN\}
is
\leq 2M(MN)^{-\alpha}
, which is the needed bound.
Also note that the measure of the set
\{x:\langle f(x,y)\rangle\sim\frac{MN}{2^{k}}\}
is
\lesssim M2^{-k}
, for
k=0,..,\log(MN)
. The claim follows by summing the contributions over
k
.
∎

The association between ambulatory blood pressure monitoring and 90-day ischemic stroke following transient ischemic attack in hypertensive patients: a retrospective cohort study
Authors: Bo Wang, Yunpeng Zhang, Xueying Wang, Yan Wang, Runzhou Pan, Hui Lu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundIschemic stroke (IS) is a major public health challenge. Transient ischemic attack (TIA) serves as a critical warning sign, particularly in hypertensive patients where early risk stratification remains difficult. While ambulatory blood pressure monitoring (ABPM) parameters strongly associated with cerebrovascular events, their predictive value for post-TIA stroke in hypertension is unclear. This study evaluated ABPM parameters for predicting 90-day acute IS risk in hypertensive TIA patients.MethodsThis single-center retrospective cohort included 1,276 hypertensive patients with TIA. All underwent 24-hour ABPM within 48 h. The outcome was IS within 90 days. LASSO regression selected key variables from 20 ABPM indices. Their independent association with IS was assessed using multivariable Cox regression across four progressively adjusted models. Predictive performance was evaluated via receiver operator characteristic curve (ROC) curves, nonlinearity via restricted cubic splines (RCS), and robustness via subgroup analyses.ResultsLASSO identified five key variables: nighttime systolic blood pressure variability (nSBPCV), daytime systolic blood pressure load (dSBPL), morning blood pressure surge (MBPS), nighttime systolic blood pressure (nSBP) and duration of hypertension. After all the adjustments, these variables remained independent risk factors for the occurrence of IS within 90 days in patients with a history of hypertension who suffered from TIA (all P < 0.001). nSBPCV (AUC = 0.834) and dSBPL (AUC = 0.804) outperformed the ABCD2 score (AUC = 0.685). RCS revealed nonlinear association for hypertension duration, nSBPCV, and dSBPL with clear inflection points (5.9 years, 9.1%, 37.3%). Results were consistent across subgroups.ConclusionsIn hypertensive TIA patients, ABPM-derived parameters, especially nSBPCV and dSBPL, are strong predictors of 90-day IS risk, superior to ABCD2. The identified nonlinear relationships and inflection points provide actionable thresholds for personalized risk stratification and precise blood pressure management.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12883-026-04855-z.Keywords: Transient ischemic attack, Ischemic stroke, Hypertension, Ambulatory blood pressure monitoring

Introduction

Ischemic stroke (IS), due to its high incidence, high disability rate, and high mortality rate, has become a significant public health challenge worldwide. Transient ischemic attack (TIA) is an independent risk factor for IS and an important “warning signal”. The risk of early progression to IS is significantly increased in patients with TIA [1]. It is worth noting that among TIA patients whose symptoms completely resolve within 24 h, about one-third can still be detected with responsible ischemic lesions through diffusion-weighted imaging [2]. Therefore, early and precise identification of high-risk individuals, achieving detailed risk stratification for TIA patients, and implementing targeted interventions are crucial for delaying disease progression, preventing the occurrence of IS, and reducing the overall disease burden. As a major controllable risk factor for IS, hypertension is characterized by a high prevalence in the TIA population, yet it is often underdiagnosed and inadequately controlled [3]. In recent years, several studies have explored the roles of clinical scoring, serum biomarkers, and neuroimaging in predicting the prognosis of TIA [4–8]. However, research on the risk prediction of IS in TIA patients with hypertension is still relatively lacking. In addition, as a widely used tool for short-term stroke risk assessment, the ABCD2 score are commonly applied in clinical practice. However, they have limitations in predicting the risk for TIA patients with hypertension: a score of ≥ 4 has low specificity for predicting recurrent stroke within 7 days [9], and does not incorporate key information such as the severity of hypertension, dynamic characteristics, and blood pressure variability. Therefore, in-depth exploration and establishment of a specific risk prediction system suitable for the population with hypertension combined with TIA is of great significance for promoting early and precise interventions and improving patient outcomes.

Ambulatory blood pressure monitoring (ABPM), as the gold standard for assessing circadian blood pressure rhythm, blood pressure load, and blood pressure variability, has multiple parameters that are independently associated with target organ damage, such as that of the heart and brain, as well as future cardiovascular events, demonstrating predictive value superior to that of office blood pressure measurements [10–13]. Hypertension management guidelines recommend incorporating ABPM into clinical practice [14, 15]. However, there is still a relative lack of clinical studies evaluating the short-term IS risk in TIA patients based on ABPM parameters. Therefore, this study aims to systematically explore the predictive value of ABPM parameters for the occurrence of IS within 90 days in TIA patients with hypertension, in order to fill the evidence gap in this field.

Method

Study design and population

This was a single-center retrospective cohort study, with the subjects being adult patients (aged 18 years and above) diagnosed with hypertension and TIA at Cangzhou central hospital from January 2023 to June 2025. The inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) Diagnosis of primary hypertension, defined as systolic blood pressure ≥ 140 mmHg, diastolic blood pressure ≥ 90 mmHg, or current use of antihypertensive medication; (2) Diagnosis of TIA according to the American Stroke Association criteria [16]; (3) No history of stroke (e.g. IS or intracerebral hemorrhage); (4) TIA presenting with focal neurological deficits; and (5) All enrolled TIA patients were admitted according to the standard procedures of our hospital for emergency evaluation. 24-hour ABPM was initiated during hospitalization, within 48 h after the TIA episode, with complete clinical data and clear follow-up records. The exclusion criteria comprised: (1) Secondary hypertension; (2) Pre-existing IS or other major neurological disorders (e.g., dementia, brain tumor) prior to the TIA; (3) Contraindications to MRI (e.g., pacemaker implantation); (4) Invalid ABPM data; (5) History of cranial surgery or severe head trauma within the past 6 months; (6) Concomitant severe cardiac, hepatic, or renal dysfunction, or malignancy that could affect survival or blood pressure; and (7) Loss to follow-up or missing 90-day outcome data.

This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Cangzhou Central Hospital (Approval No: 2023-240-01). Although patients presented with transient neurological deficits due to TIA, all participants were evaluated by the attending neurologist at the time of enrollment and were deemed to have the capacity to understand the study’s purpose, procedures, and potential risks. Therefore, written informed consent was obtained directly from all participating patients. The consent process and documentation were in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki.

Exposure variables

Data on the exposure variables were obtained through a systematic extraction of electronic medical records, primarily consisting of two main parts: baseline characteristics and laboratory parameters (Supplementary Table 1). Definitions of some key variables are as follows: Hypertension stages are categorized according to clinic blood pressure standards: Stage 1 (systolic blood pressure 140–159 mmHg and/or diastolic blood pressure 90–99 mmHg), Stage 2 (systolic blood pressure 160–179 mmHg and/or diastolic blood pressure 100–109 mmHg), and Stage 3 (systolic blood pressure ≥ 180 mmHg and/or diastolic blood pressure ≥ 110 mmHg); H-type hypertension is defined as hypertension with homocysteine levels ≥ 15 µmol/L. According to international hypertension management guidelines [17], antihypertensive drugs were classified as Angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors or angiotensin II receptor blockers (A), Beta-blockers (B), Calcium channel blockers (C) and Diuretics (D). Additionally, the study recorded specific adjuvant treatments, including: Management for cerebral hypoperfusion, primarily referring to maintaining effective circulating blood volume; neurorepair treatments, mainly including neuroprotective agents such as edaravone and cytidine diphosphate choline; and cerebrovascular dilation treatments, primarily referring to the use of drugs that improve cerebral circulation, such as butylphthalide and prostaglandin E1.

ABPM parameters

ABPM indices derived from 24-hour recordings, including 24-hour mean systolic blood pressure (24hSBP), 24-hour mean diastolic blood pressure (24hDBP), daytime mean systolic blood pressure (dSBP), daytime mean diastolic blood pressure (dDBP), nighttime mean systolic blood pressure (nSBP), nighttime mean diastolic blood pressure (nDBP), 24-hour systolic blood pressure coefficient of variation (24hSBPCV), 24-hour diastolic blood pressure coefficient of variation (24hDBPCV), daytime systolic blood pressure coefficient of variation (dSBPCV), daytime diastolic blood pressure coefficient of variation (dDBPCV), nighttime systolic blood pressure coefficient of variation (nSBPCV), nighttime diastolic blood pressure coefficient of variation (nDBPCV), 24-hour systolic blood pressure load (24hSBPL), 24-hour diastolic blood pressure load (24hDBPL), daytime systolic blood pressure load (dSBPL), nighttime systolic blood pressure load (nSBPL), daytime diastolic blood pressure load (dDBPL), nighttime diastolic blood pressure load (nDBPL), and morning blood pressure surge (MBPS). SBPCV = (SD of SBP / Mean SBP) × 100%. DBPCV = (SD of DBP / Mean DBP) × 100%. Daytime is defined as the duration when patients report being awake and active (usually from 6:00 AM to 10:00 PM), while nighttime is defined as the sleep period (typically from 10:00 PM to 6:00 AM the following day). SBPL is defined as the percentage of readings during the monitoring period in which the systolic blood pressure exceeds the normal threshold (daytime > 140 mmHg, nighttime > 120 mmHg). DBPL is defined as the percentage of readings during the monitoring period in which the diastolic blood pressure exceeds the normal threshold (daytime > 90 mmHg, nighttime > 80 mmHg). MBPS is defined as the average systolic blood pressure within 2 h after waking subtracted by the lowest hourly average systolic blood pressure during the nighttime sleep period (including the last 2 h before waking).

Outcome measurement

The primary outcome of this study was the occurrence of IS within 90 days following the index TIA. The diagnosis of IS was rigorously confirmed and required the following criteria to be met: (1) The diagnosis was made in accordance with current international guidelines for ischemic stroke [18], based on the acute onset of new focal neurological deficits. (2) The diagnosis required confirmation by neuroimaging (either computed tomography or magnetic resonance imaging) demonstrating a new, corresponding ischemic lesion. (3) A causal link to the preceding TIA was established by demonstrating consistency between the anatomical location of the new infarction and the symptomatic vascular territory implicated in the index TIA event. All suspected outcome events were adjudicated by two independent neurologists who were blinded to the patients’ ambulatory blood pressure profiles. Any discrepancies were resolved through discussion or by consultation with a third senior neurologist.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS software (version 30.0) and the R programming environment. Continuous variables are presented as mean ± SD and were compared between two groups using the independent samples t-test. For continuous variables that were not normally distributed, data are presented as median (interquartile range), and comparisons between two groups were performed using the Mann-Whitney U test. Categorical variables are expressed as number (percentage) and were compared using the chi-square test. Feature selection for factors influencing the progression from TIA to IS was performed using the LASSO regression. Independent factors associated with TIA progression to IS within 90 days were identified using multivariable Cox proportional hazards regression analysis. The study comprised four models: Model 1 was unadjusted for any confounding factors. Model 2, based on Model 1, was adjusted for age, sex, diabetes mellitus, atrial fibrillation, coronary heart disease, smoking, alcohol consumption, BMI, degree of culprit vessel stenosis, and number of TIA episodes. Model 3, built upon Model 2, was further adjusted for antiplatelet therapy, anticoagulation therapy, lipid-lowering therapy, management of cerebral hypoperfusion, neurorestorative therapy, cerebral vasodilation therapy, classes of antihypertensive drugs, and half-life of antihypertensive drugs. Model 4, extending Model 3, was additionally adjusted for Total cholesterol (TC), triglycerides (TG), low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C), high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C), plasma Lp(a) [(Lp(a)], fasting blood glucose (FBG), glycated hemoglobin (HbA1c), albumin (ALB), creatinine (Cr), total bilirubin (TBIL), alkaline phosphatase (ALP), homocysteine (Hcy), C-reactive protein (CRP), absolute neutrophil count (NEUT), absolute lymphocyte count (LYM), absolute monocyte count (MONO), hemoglobin (Hb), red blood cell distribution width (RDW), platelet count (PLT), and fibrinogen (FIB). The predictive performance of hypertension-related parameters for TIA-to-SI progression was evaluated by Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) curve analysis, with results reported as the area under the curve (AUC). To assess the consistency of effects for identified independent risk factors across different patient populations, pre-specified subgroup analyses were conducted. Survival analysis was performed using Kaplan-Meier curves, and between-group differences in cumulative incidence of TIA progression to IS were assessed with the log-rank test. The bonferroni method was applied to correct for multiple comparisons. Dose-response relationships between continuous variables and TIA-to-IS progression were examined using Restricted Cubic Splines (RCS) within the Cox regression framework. A two-sided p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant for all analyses.

Results

Baseline characteristics of the general clinical data

A total of 1,276 hypertensive patients with TIA were included in this retrospective analysis. During the 90-day follow-up period, 409 patients (32.1%) progressed to IS group, while 867 patients (67.9%) did not (Non-IS group). There were no statistically significant differences between the two groups in most demographic and clinical variables, including age, sex, prevalence of diabetes mellitus, atrial fibrillation, coronary heart disease, smoking and alcohol consumption status, degree of culprit vessel stenosis, the use of various medications, RDW, Hb, PLT, NEUT, MONO, TG, HDL-C, FBG, HbA1c, ALB, TBIL, ALP, Cr and FIB (all P > 0.05). However, Patients in the IS group had a higher BMI and were more likely to be classified as obese. The IS group also had a higher median ABCD2 score, a higher median number of TIA episodes. Furthermore, higher levels of LYM, TC, LDL-C, Lp(a), UA, CRP and HCY were found in the IS group (all P<0.05) (Table 1).

Baseline characteristics of the ABPM parameters

Significant differences in blood pressure-related characteristics were observed between the IS and non-IS groups. Patients who progressed to IS had a longer median duration of hypertension (8.00 vs. 6.00 years, P < 0.001) and a higher prevalence of H-type hypertension (54.0% vs. 47.2%, P = 0.026). The distribution of hypertension stages also differed significantly, with a greater proportion of Stage 3 hypertension in the IS group (42.3% vs. 35.8%, P = 0.003). Regarding ABPM Parameters, the IS group exhibited higher average systolic blood pressure across all periods: 24-hour, daytime, and nighttime. Average diastolic blood pressure was also higher in the IS group for the 24-hour and daytime periods. Blood pressure variability, measured by the coefficient of variation, was significantly elevated in the IS group. This included higher 24hSBPCV, dDBPCV, and nSBPCV. The IS group had a greater 24hSBPL and dSBPL. Furthermore, the MBPS was markedly more pronounced in the IS group (all P < 0.05). No significant differences were found between the two groups in nDBP, 24hDBPCV, dSBPCV, nDBPCV, 24hDBPL, dDBPL, nSBPL, nDBPL, the classes of antihypertensive drugs used and the half-life profiles of these medications (all P > 0.05) (Table 2).

The relationship between ABPM parameters and 90-day IS following TIA in hypertensive patients

To screen for the predictive variables most relevant to short-term IS risk, this study employed LASSO regression for feature selection among all 20 dynamic blood pressure-related indicators. The optimal λ value was determined through 10-fold cross-validation. At this optimal λ value, the LASSO regression model ultimately retained 5 key variables: nSBPCV, dSBPL, MBPS, nSBP, and duration of hypertension (Figs. 1 and 2).

Variable selection via LASSO regression

Selection of the optimal penalty parameter (λ) by 10-fold cross-validation

We further conducted univariate Cox proportional hazards regression analysis to preliminarily assess the statistical association of these 5 variables with IS risk. The results are as follows: nSBPCV (HR = 1.40, 95%CI: 1.35–1.45, P < 0.001), dSBPL (HR = 1.18, 95%CI: 1.16–1.20, P < 0.001), MBPS (HR = 1.07, 95%CI: 1.05–1.08, P < 0.001), nSBP (HR = 1.03, 95%CI: 1.02–1.04, P < 0.001), duration of hypertension (HR = 1.25, 95%CI: 1.20–1.30, P < 0.001) (Table 3, Model 1). Cox multivariate regression analysis further revealed that after adjusting for multiple confounding factors, these associations remained statistically significant, confirming that nSBPCV, dSBPL, MBPS, nSBP and duration of hypertension are independent risk factors for the occurrence of IS within 90 days in TIA patients with a history of hypertension. The results are as follows: nSBPCV: HR = 1.42, 95% CI = 1.37 ~ 1.48, P < 0.001; dSBPL: HR = 1.18, 95% CI = 1.16 ~ 1.21, P < 0.001; MBPS: HR = 1.08, 95% CI = 1.06 ~ 1.09, P < 0.001; nSBP: HR = 1.03, 95% CI = 1.02 ~ 1.04, P < 0.001; duration of hypertension: HR = 1.67, 95% CI = 1.56 ~ 1.78, P < 0.001 (Table 3, Model 4). To further validate the association of the above variables with short-term IS risk, this study conducted Kaplan-Meier survival analysis. Discussion

This retrospective cohort study in Chinese hypertensive TIA patients found that nSBPCV, dSBPL, MBPS, nSBP, and hypertension duration were independently associated with 90-day IS risk. Among these, nSBPCV and dSBPL outperformed the ABCD2 score in predictive efficacy. Furthermore, hypertension duration, nSBPCV, and dSBPL showed significant nonlinear associations with IS risk, each with distinct inflection points.

Research has established that dynamic blood pressure changes are critically linked to IS onset and progression. In the acute phase, elevated blood pressure may exacerbate injury to compromised brain tissue and raise the risk of hemorrhagic transformation, while blood pressure fluctuations can impair cerebrovascular autoregulation, damage the blood-brain barrier, and worsen ischemia with repeated hypotensive episodes [19]. Furthermore, blood pressure variability serves as an independent predictor for the occurrence, recurrence, and prognosis of both TIA and IS [20–24]. ABPM, as a key tool for assessing such variability, has demonstrated clinical value; studies associate higher systolic BP, increased pulse pressure, and abnormal circadian rhythms (e.g., non-dipping or reverse-dipping patterns) with progressive infarction and the severity of cerebral white matter changes [25, 26]. Our study builds on this evidence by specifically examining ABPM parameters in hypertensive TIA patients, offering new data to support risk stratification and guide interventions. This study indicates that in TIA patients with hypertension, nSBPCV, dSBPL, nSBP, MBPS and the course of hypertension have good predictive value for the occurrence of IS within 90 days. The potential mechanisms may involve the following aspects: (1) Elevated nSBP, increased nSBPCV, and MBPS are key manifestations of abnormal nocturnal blood pressure. Nighttime is normally dominated by parasympathetic activity with a physiological blood pressure dip. Persistent elevation or excessive fluctuation during this period typically indicates a non-dipper or reverse-dipper pattern, reflecting sympathetic overactivation and sustained cardiovascular stress. This state exacerbates endothelial injury and arterial stiffness, and is often linked to conditions such as sleep apnea, increased salt sensitivity, and renin-angiotensin system dysregulation, thereby elevating the risk of vascular damage and thrombosi [26, 27]. Nocturnal hypertension and elevated blood pressure variability are linked to imaging markers of cerebral small vessel disease (e.g., white matter hyperintensities and lacunar infarcts), potentially via mechanisms such as pressure-induced endothelial dysfunction, vascular smooth muscle hyperplasia, and increased blood–brain barrier permeability [28]. (2) Elevated blood pressure variability increases vascular wall shear stress, promoting endothelial dysfunction, oxidative stress, and reduced nitric oxide bioavailability, thereby accelerating atherosclerosis. It is also linked to heightened sympathetic activity, diminished cardiopulmonary reflexes, and impaired arterial compliance. These combined effects contribute to cerebral hypoperfusion, disrupt cerebrovascular autoregulation, and exacerbate ischemic injury—particularly in the setting of inflammation, hyperglycemia, or blood–brain barrier disruption—especially when compromised vessels fail to compensate during BP declines, thereby increasing IS risk [22, 29–32]. (3) Elevated dSBPL may increase platelet aggregation through morning activation of the sympathetic and renin-angiotensin systems. Sustained daytime BP load further promotes small artery remodeling and reduced elasticity, contributing to plaque rupture, thrombosis, and accelerated cerebral small vessel disease [33, 34]. (4) Hypertension duration reflects long-term vascular exposure, promoting structural changes like medial thickening, collagen deposition, and reduced elasticity. These alterations impair cerebral autoregulation, promote hyaline transformation in small arteries, compromise the blood–brain barrier, and heighten brain tissue susceptibility to ischemia [35]. In addition, nSBPCV and dSBPL exhibit superior predictive efficacy compared to other variables and the traditional ABCD2 score, possibly for the following reasons: (1) Increasing evidence suggests that nighttime blood pressure is superior to daytime blood pressure in predicting the progression of atherosclerosis, target organ damage, and cardiovascular outcomes [36, 37]. (2) dSBPL captures both the duration and extent of blood pressure elevation beyond the normal range, and its link to target organ damage directly reflects cumulative vascular injury from elevated blood pressure [33]. (3) In contrast to the static, categorical ABCD2 score, continuous ABPM parameters such as nSBPCV and dSBPL capture dynamic BP fluctuations and cumulative exposure over time, thereby more sensitively reflecting real-time vascular function and hemodynamic instability.

We identified significant nonlinear associations between hypertension duration, nSBPCV, dSBPL, and IS risk, each with distinct inflection points. The nSBPCV threshold (9.1%) may represent a critical limit for cerebral vascular autoregulation, beyond which impaired compensation leads to marked perfusion fluctuations and increased stroke risk. Similarly, the 5.9‑year inflection point for hypertension duration suggests a “cumulative time window” after which structural vascular changes accelerate, driving a nonlinear rise in infarction risk. Furthermore, dSBPL showed a U‑shaped association with IS risk, with an inflection point at 37.3%. This suggests a dual mechanism: below 37.3%, lower risk may relate to hypoperfusion from excessive BP lowering [38], whereas values ≥ 37.3% likely reflect endothelial injury from sustained mechanical stress, accelerated remodeling, and a pro‑thrombotic state [39, 40]. These nonlinear thresholds offer clinically actionable targets for stratifying post‑TIA stroke risk and personalizing BP management. Stratified analyses confirmed that the associations of nSBPCV, dSBPL, MBPS, nSBP, and hypertension duration with IS risk remained consistent across age, sex, BMI, and comorbidities, supporting their robustness and generalizability.

This study focuses on hypertensive patients with TIA, a population that urgently requires precise risk assessment, and systematically explores the relationship between multiple ABPM parameters and the risk of IS within 90 days. However, this study also has several limitations: This study has several limitations. First, as a single-center retrospective cohort, generalizability may be limited and residual confounding (e.g., unmeasured lifestyle or adherence factors) cannot be excluded. Second, only a single baseline ABPM assessment was performed; subsequent changes in antihypertensive treatment or blood pressure control during the 90-day follow-up could influence outcomes and were not captured, potentially introducing residual confounding. Third, the precise interval between TIA resolution and the start of ABPM recording could not be determined due to the transient nature of symptoms and inconsistent clinical documentation. Fourth, ABPM readings can be influenced by daily activity, sleep, or emotion. Fifth, while medication data were collected, the impact of specific drug types, doses, or combinations on BP parameters and outcomes was not analyzed. Sixth, we did not formally analyze the overlap between different ABPM phenotypes (e.g., MBPS and dSBPL), though assessing both may offer a more comprehensive hemodynamic profile. Seventh, the biological mechanisms underlying the observed nonlinear relationships require further validation. Finally, our strict imaging confirmation for IS likely improved diagnostic specificity but may have underestimated the true stroke rate.

Conclusions

This retrospective cohort study systematically explored the association between ABPM parameters and the risk of IS within 90 days in hypertensive patients with TIA. The analysis indicates that nSBPCV, dSBPL, MBPS, nSBP, and the duration of hypertension are all independently positively associated with IS risk and have independent predictive value for its occurrence. Notably, nSBPCV and dSBPL outperformed the other variables and the traditional ABCD2 score in predictive efficacy. Furthermore, the study revealed significant nonlinear associations between the duration of hypertension, nSBPCV, dSBPL, and IS risk, clearly identifying their respective inflection points, which suggest that their effects exist within critical thresholds, providing potential quantifiable targets for personalized and precise blood pressure management in clinical practice. Future research could build on this foundation to conduct prospective intervention trials to determine whether targeting these specific blood pressure patterns can effectively reduce the incidence of IS in high-risk patients, thereby promoting the optimization and innovation of secondary prevention strategies after TIA.

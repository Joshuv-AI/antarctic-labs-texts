Current status and trends in thalassemia burden across South, East and Southeast Asia, 1990–2021 a systematic analysis for the global burden of disease study 2021
Authors: Shuning Zhang, Zhangjie Chen, Meihuan Chen, Hailong Huang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveThalassemia, an inherited hemoglobin synthesis disorder, imposes a significant health burden in Asian regions with high prevalence. Detailed patterns and trends of the disease across countries and territories within these regions remain underexplored. Our study focuses on the disease burden indices of thalassemia within the four GBD-defined Asian regions and the twenty-five included countries and territories. It provides insights into the gender-age distribution, temporal changes, and economic aspects of the thalassemia burden.MethodsData on thalassemia prevalence, incidence, mortality, and Disability-Adjusted Life Years (DALYs) were extracted from the Global Burden of Disease (GBD) 2021 study for South, East, Southeast, and high-income Asia regions, encompassing the relevant countries and territories from 1990 to 2021. The Average Annual Percent Change (AAPC) in age-standardized rates of thalassemia was determined to assess temporal trends. Age-gender cohort proportions were considered. The economic aspect of the disease burden and frontier analysis were evaluated using the GBD Socio-Demographic Index and Global Health Expenditure data.ResultsSoutheast Asia exhibited notably high age-standardized mortality rate (ASMR), age-standardized prevalence rate (ASPR), and age-standardized DALYs rate among the four studied Asian regions in 2021. The East Asia region had recorded the highest age-standardized incidence rate (ASIR). A general decline in disease burden indices across the four regions from 1990 to 2021 was evident, with the exception of ASIR in Southeast Asia. The ASMR was highest among pediatric population under five years old, with a significant male preponderance. An unusual increase in ASMR was detected among females of childbearing age and the elderly within the studied region. Further analysis had identified six high-burden countries and territories, particularly those with low-middle Socio-Demographic Index (SDI) rankings and limited health expenditure.ConclusionAlthough the overall burden of thalassemia has decreased substantially, the disease burden was influenced by gender, age, geography, temporal trends, and economic factors in distinct manners. Based on the current SDI, many countries and regions still have greater improvement potential in the disease burden. There is a necessity for enhanced attention and resource allocation, particularly in low-middle and low SDI countries, with an emphasis on policies that promote early diagnosis and comprehensive care.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12889-024-20983-y.

Introduction

Thalassemia is an inherited disorder affecting hemoglobin synthesis, which results in a wide spectrum of disease from the most severe transfusion-dependent form to the mildest asymptomatic carrier state [1]. It poses a substantial burden on healthcare systems with a high prevalence and significant morbidity, particularly in regions of high prevalence such as the African, Asian, and Mediterranean countries [2]. Early diagnosis and comprehensive care can minimize the disease prevalence and optimizing outcomes in those who are affected. Basic needs of the thalassemia patients include regular blood transfusions and daily medications for iron chelation, as well as complex clinical monitoring [3]. In addition, periodic screening of complications and psychological counselling are also required [4]. It is associated with other comorbidities including pulmonary hypertension, diabetes, and other cardiac problems [5–8]. Although the thalassemia burden was determined globally, the detailed investigation at regional and national levels have not been examined yet.

This study utilized thalassemia data from the Global Burden of Disease Study 2021 (GBD 2021), which is publicly accessible. It encompassed thalassemia-related data across different age groups in thalassemia-burdened Asian region from 1990 to 2021. Incorporating data from the Global Health Expenditure Database (GHED) and GBD Socio-Demographic Index (SDI), this research aimed to explore the temporal, economic and gender trends in the burden of thalassemia in 25 countries in Asian region, thereby offering an evidence-based foundation for thalassemia prevention and treatment strategies.

Methods

Data acquisition and download

The 2021 Global Burden of Disease (GBD) study offers an exhaustive assessment of health impairments associated with 369 specific diseases, injuries, and disabilities, alongside 88 risk factors. It encompasses a global spectrum of 204 countries and territories, utilizing the most current epidemiological data and standardized methodologies [9]. Our analysis extracted estimates and their corresponding 95% uncertainty intervals (UIs) for 1990–2021 thalassemia’s prevalence, incidence, mortality, years of life lost (YLLs), years of life lived with disability (YLDs), and Disability-Adjusted Life Years (DALYs) in all ages and sex group from the GBD 2021 dataset. Furthermore, we also incorporated the Socio-Demographic Index (SDI), a metric that assesses a region’s socio-demographic development, factoring in income, education, and fertility rates. SDI categorized 204 countries into five quintiles (low, low-middle, middle, high-middle, and high) based on their country-level SDI estimates for the year 2021.

The Global Health Expenditure Database (GHED) provides publicly accessible, comparable data on health expenditure for over 190 WHO Member States since 2000. Current Health Expenditure (CHE) data of the studied countries were downloaded from the GHED, which reflected the average health care expenditure per capita within a country or territory over a specific period, encompassing government, private, and public health insurance spending on medical services, pharmaceuticals, and preventive care. This metric facilitated the comparison of health care investment levels across different nations or regions.

Burden description

We prioritized the geographical representativeness of sites and the integrality of the data, four GBD regions have been choose because of the high prevalence as the previously reported. East Asia region includes the China, Taiwan (Province of China) and Democratic People’s Republic of Korea. South Asia region includes Bangladesh, Bhutan, India, Nepal, Pakistan. Southeast Asia region includes Cambodia, Indonesia, Lao People’s Democratic Republic, Malaysia, Maldives, Mauritius, Myanmar, Philippines, Seychelles, Sri Lanka, Thailand, Timor-Leste, Viet Nam. High-income Asia-Pacific region includes Brunei Darussalam, Japan, Republic of Korea and Singapore. An exhaustive evaluation was performed to measure the national impact of thalassemia, including metrics such as prevalence, incidence, mortality, YLLs, YLDs, and DALYs. DALYs is calculated as the sum of YLLs due to thalassemia and YLDs for patients living in less than good health, it can intuitively quantify the health loss in populations due to this disease [10, 11]. Our study further explored the demographic factors influencing the impact of thalassemia, focusing on the distribution of the disease burden across various age cohorts and between genders.

Joinpoint regression analysis

Joinpoint regression analysis, conducted via Joinpoint regression program 5.2.0.0 (Statistical Research and Applications Branch, National Cancer Institute), is a statistical approach widely used in epidemiological studies to evaluate changes in disease incidence or mortality rates over time [12]. Using this methodology effectively detected and measured pivotal points of change within the time series data for thalassemia disease burden. The model enabled the calculation of the annual percent change (APC) with its 95% confidence interval (CI), thereby providing a clear picture of the prevalence trends within specific time segments. Additionally, the average annual percent change (AAPC) was determined to offer a comprehensive view of the overall trend across the study’s duration from 1990 to 2021.

Statistically, an APC or AAPC estimate with a 95% CI lower bound above zero indicates an increasing trend of thalassemia burden within the interval. Conversely, an APC or AAPC estimate with a 95% CI upper bound below zero suggests a decreasing trend. A 95% CI that includes zero for the APC or AAPC implies that the trend has been stable.

Frontier analysis

Frontiers analysis was conducted based on previous report [13], using non-parametric Data Envelope Analysis (DEA) to access the unrealize health gain based on current development levels. This analysis determines the minimum achievable age-standardized DALYs rate of thalassemia for countries or territories at different SDI levels.

Statistics analysis

The prevalence, incidence, mortality, YLLs, YLDs, and DALYs was represented as a projection for every 100,000 persons individuals in the populace, inclusive of its 95% UI. Their age-standard rate was used to eliminate the impact of differences in age structure among various populations on rates, thereby making data comparable across different time points or populations. All data analysis and visualizations were performed via R language programming (version 4.3.1, R Development Core Team). A p-value of less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Result

Regional level

Regional burden and trends

In 2021, studies conducted at the four Asian regional levels found that the number of death cases of thalassemia was highest in southeast Asia region, at 2,517 (95% UI: 1,983-3,153), with an age-standardized mortality rate (ASMR) of 0.37 (95% UI: 0.29–0.45). The ASMR decreased significantly in the East Asia region at 75.80% between 1990 and 2021, and followed by the Southeast Asia with 42.18%. Compare 1990 to 2021, the ASMR kept almost the same level in the South Asia region, while the death cases increased from 2,066 (95% UI: 1,383-2,738) to 2,413 (95% UI: 1,215-3,581). In East Asia and the high-income Asia region, there was a significant above 70% reduction in mortality cases, with numbers decreasing from 7,340 (95% UI: 5,605-9,156) to 1,938 (95% UI: 1,509-2,418) and 64 (95% UI: 43–85) to 16 (95% UI: 11–22), respectively (Table 1).

The disability-adjusted life years (DALYs) for thalassemia in Southeast Asia region was 158,093 (95% UI: 125,042–194,747), with an age-standardized DALYs rates (ASDR) of 24.03 per 100,000 persons (95% UI: 18.71–29.37), which was also the highest rates in 4 regions. South Asia region had the highest YLDs and YLLs rates of 1.45 (95% UI: 0.92–2.09) and 22.58 (95% UI: 17.29–27.91). DALYs declined in the 4 regions except the South Asia between 1990 and 2021. It increased from 177,133 (95% UI: 122,159–233,756) to 193,669 (95% UI: 103,416 − 283,537), mainly contributed by the increasing YLLs from 168,709 (95% UI: 114,112–224,292) to 185,908 (95% UI: 95,333 − 274,545) (Table 1).

In 2021, the age-standardized prevalence rate (ASPR) of thalassemia peaked in East Asia, with a rate of 54.26 per 100,000 individuals (95% UI: 42.77–69.17). Conversely, the high-income Asia region reported the lowest ASPR at 3.93 per 100,000 individuals (95% UI: 3.13–4.86). The prevalence case distribution mirrored these rates, with East Asia recording 532,033 cases (95% UI: 422,029–672,468) and the high-income Asia region reporting 4,294 cases (95% UI: 3,443-5,306) (Table 1). Comparatively, Southeast Asia exhibited an 8.30% reduction in ASPR from 1990, whereas East Asia showed a 4.00% decrease, the high-income region a 3.44% reduction, and South Asia a 4.67% decline.

Incidence cases and the age-standardized incidence rate (ASIR) distributed similarly as the ASPR, with the highest cases as 40,542 (95% UI: 29,651 − 55,405) and ASIR as 7.35 (95% UI:5.37–10.04) in East Asia region. Changes in the ASIR across four regions exhibited variations within 6.4% relative to the 1990 rates. Southeast Asia demonstrated the minimal reduction, with a mere 1.8% decrease.

In 2021, Southeast Asia exhibited a significant disease burden, with high ASMR, ASPR, and DALYs among the four study regions. Despite East Asia having the highest ASPR and ASIR, its mortality rate and DALYs were considerably lower compared to Southeast Asia (Fig. 1).

Temporal joinpoint analysis in regional level

Joinpoint regression analysis revealed trends in the ASPR, ASMR, ASPR, age-standard rate of DALYs, YLLs, and YLDs across four regions from 1990 to 2021 (Fig. 2). East Asia demonstrated the steepest decline in DALYs (Fig. 2A), with a significant overall reduction from 1990 to 2021 (AAPC = -4.67%; 95% CI: -4.89% to -4.44%; P < 0.001) (Table 2). A pronounced downward trend was observed between 2009 and 2013, with an APC of -7.00% (95% CI: -7.67% to -6.33%; P < 0.001) (Table S1). YLLs, as its contribution, followed a similar downtrend with the DALYs (Fig. 2B). However, a slight increase was observed in YLDs of Southeast Asia from 2019 to 2021 (APC = 1.78%; 95% CI: 1.19–2.37%; P < 0.001) (Table S1). In contrast, the notable decrease happened during this period in East Asia (APC= -5.09%; 95% CI: -5.73% to -4.44%; P < 0.001) (Fig. 2C and Table S1).

Regional temporal trends in gender and age structures

Since 1990, the prevalence number of thalassemia has predominantly been higher among male population (Figure S1), with the exception of countries within the high-income Asia region (Figure S1B). Mortality number associated with thalassemia had exhibited a preponderance in males as compared to females over the observed timeframe. Nonetheless, a noteworthy escalation in ASMR among females in South Asia has been observed since 2000. This trend led to a reversal in mortality disparity, with female deaths surpassing male death in 2005 (Figure S1C). In Southeast Asia, while both ASMR and the number of deaths due to thalassemia declined in males and females, recent trends indicated a convergence in mortality figures between both sexes (Figure S1D).

Across all surveyed regions, the highest prevalence of thalassemia in 2021 was consistently observed in the pediatric population below the age of five. ASIR was observed exclusively in patients under five years old. A decline in prevalence was noted with increasing age (Figure S2). In all age cohorts examined, a higher prevalence of thalassemia was consistently noted in males compared to females. However, this pattern deviated in the high-income Asia region, where no significant gender disparity was observed (Figure S2B).

In the pediatric population below the age of five, the highest mortality number and ASMR were observed, with a notable predominance in male children (Figures S2 and S3). The pattern of DALYs was similar with the ASMR in four region gender. Post the age of five years, an unusual surge in ASMR was detected among females in the regions studied. In the high-income Asian region, mortality number for females with thalassemia increased with age (Figure S2B), peaking in the 50–54 age group, where the ASMR was 0.021 (95% UI: 0.0058–0.048) (Fig. 3B). In the East Asian region, ASMR for females exhibited a notable decline after the age of five, followed by a gradual increase. By the 40–49 age group, it paralleled that of males and peaked in the 50–54 and 55–59 age groups (0.20; 95% UI: 0.10–0.34) (Fig. 3A). In the Southeast Asian region, the ASMR for females surpassed that of males at the age of 35–39 (0.26 vs. 0.23) and reached a peak in the 60–64 age group (0.60; 95% UI: 0.35-1.00) (Fig. 3D). In the south Asia, the female ASMR peaked at the ages of 15–19 (0.27; 95% UI: 0.017–0.68) (Fig. 3C). In the male populations of East and Southeast Asia, there was an observed peak in the ASMR for the age group of 40–60 years (Fig. 3A and D). However, this pattern was not observed in high-income and South Asia regions.

Country burden and trends

In countries and territories distributed across various geographical locations, Pakistan had highest ASMR at 0.75 (95% UI: 0.34–1.14) (Table 3; Fig. 4), with the death numbers of 2098 (95% UI: 963-3,198). Temporal joinpoint analysis in country and territories level

In the context of the year 1990 to 2021, a comparative analysis revealed that the ASMR exhibited a decremental trend across the majority of countries and territories. In contrast, the Province of China, Taiwan, demonstrated an opposing trend characterized by an increment in ASMR (AAPC = 2.92%; 95% CI: 1.45–4.41%; P < 0.001) (Table S2). This pattern was also observed in the age-standardized DALYs rate (AAPC = 2.24%; 95% CI: 0.97–3.53%; P = 0.001) (Table S3). The ASMR in Taiwan has been on an ascending trajectory since 1998, with the most pronounced escalation occurring between 1998 and 2003 (APC = 24.6%; 95% CI: 17.2–32.6%; P < 0.001), followed by a subsequent decline from 2007 to 2021 (APC = -2.4%; 95% CI: -3.2% to -1.6%; P < 0.001) (Table S6). Furthermore, Taiwan exhibited the most pronounced positive trend in ASPR (AAPC = 0.38%; 95% CI: 0.36–0.40%; P < 0.001) (Table S4), with a consistent increase observed since 1995. Conversely, Pakistan exhibited a consistent downward trend in ASPR from 1990 to 2021, with an even more significant decrease noted from 2019 to 2021 (APC = -5.48%; 95% CI: -7.71% to -3.2%; P < 0.001) (Table S7). Sri Lanka, on the other hand, experienced the most notable increase in ASIR (AAPC = 2.24%; 95% CI: 0.97–3.53%; P = 0.001) (Table S5), while the largest declines were observed in Pakistan (APC = -0.57%; 95% CI: -0.59% to -0.55%; P < 0.001), Singapore (APC = -0.47%; 95% CI: -0.77% to -0.18%; P = 0.002) and China (APC = -0.22%; 95% CI: -0.34% to -0.10%; P < 0.001) (Table S8).

Upon conducting a detailed examination of the ASMR in the Taiwan (a province of China), it was observed that the male demographic predominantly contributed to the observed upward trend in ASMR when contrasted with the female demographic (AAPC = 3.54%; 95% CI: 1.90–5.20%; P-value < 0.001) (Table S9). The ASMR for males exhibited a rapid increase commencing in 1998, with a notable inflection point occurring in 2007. Subsequently, a gradual decline in ASMR was observed during the period from 2010 to 2021 (APC = -1.29%; 95% CI: -2.00% to -0.57%; P = 0.002) (Table S10). In Taiwan, the ASPR for females has exhibited a more pronounced increase compared to the male group, with a calculated AAPC value of 0.61% (95% CI: 0.54–0.68%; P < 0.001) for females versus 0.20% (95% CI: 0.17–0.22%; P < 0.001) for males (Table S11). This disparity suggested a significant gender-based disparity in the prevalence trends. Both sexes had demonstrated a consistent upward trajectory in ASPR, indicating a general increase in the prevalence of the disease over time within the population.

The year 2021 data indicated a concerning trend in several countries and territories. Specifically, Cambodia, Laos, Pakistan and Maldives had demonstrated disease burdens as assessed by the composite of four key indices. The mainland of China had the largest prevalence cases. Moreover, Taiwan (a province of China) had also exhibited a significant escalation in both the prevalence and mortality rates associated with thalassemia in recent years. This upward trend was equally concerning and warrants immediate attention (Fig. 5).

Trends in gender and age structures in high disease burden countries

Analysis of the age and gender structures in the high disease burden countries showed that prevalence rates of thalassemia generally decreased after children under 5 years of age. In Pakistan and Taiwan province, the female children under 5 years old have higher ASPR and ASMR than that in the male group (Fig. 6A and B). However, it was converse in the China, Laos and Cambodia (Fig. 6B, S3A and D). The demographic context of Pakistan, a statistically unusual increase in female mortality rates has been noted beyond the age of 5 years. This increase is characterized by marked fluctuation, particularly within the age range of 15 to 54 years (Fig. 6A). Notably, the age cohort from 15 to 19 years old has been identified as experiencing the most elevated mortality rates among this age spectrum. Comparatively, in Maldives, Laos and Cambodia, the 60–64 age cohort among females exhibited higher mortality rates in comparison to their male counterparts (Fig. 6B, S3A and D). In Taiwan, no significant gender-based disparity in mortality rates was observed before 80 years old (Figure S3B).

Socioeconomic and health expenditure in country and territories level

The relationship between the SDI and age-standard DALYs rate vary inversely in twenty-five countries and territories from 1990 to 2021 (Fig. 7A). In nations with low and low-middle SDI, the age-standardized DALY rates were notably elevated, as exemplified by Pakistan (57.32), Cambodia (51.65), and Laos (48.20). Among the 25 countries and territories, Maldives classified as the middle SDI countries in 2021, still remained significantly concentrated in ASDR (32.94; 95% CI: 26.26 to 41.39), and highest ASIR (16.57; 95% CI: 11.60 to 23.63). Conversely, as SDI increased, the disease burden in Taiwan province showed a trend of increase.

Upon examination of the Current Health Expenditure (CHE) data across various nations, it is evident that Singapore and Japan have allocated a substantial proportion of resources towards healthcare, which correlates with a reduced disease burden. Laos has experienced a 2.93-fold increase in financial investment in healthcare between 2001 and 2021, consequently observing a decline in disease burden. In contrast, Pakistan’s health expenditure increased only 2.3-fold over the two decades, remaining the lowest among the twenty-five countries surveyed. Cambodia, having a lower-middle level of health expenditure, exhibited the highest disease burden in 2021 (Fig. 7B).

Additionally, frontier analysis demonstrated that many low SDI countries were positioned remarkably below the frontier line, indicating health systems insufficiency and substantial potential for improving health outcomes relative to their available resources (Figure S4; Table S14).

Discussion

Despite enhancements in healthcare, sanitation, and infection control measures, thalassemia persists as a significant public health concern in various populations and is often inadequately addressed by many health authorities. Even with the modern era’s medical advancements, ensuring adequate transfusion therapy and chelation treatment remains a substantial challenge in many countries [14]. The per capita medical resources are inadequate, resulting in considerable financial strain on patients and their families. Besides, frontier analysis strongly suggested a significant gap between current healthcare access and quality and the potential outcomes that could be achieved in some countries and territories, even given the existing development levels and available resources.

Our analysis, which encompassed the disease burden of thalassemia across four Asian regions comprising twenty-five countries, revealed a noteworthy decline in mortality rates and case numbers from 1990 to 2021. In the year 2021, the ASMR and ASDR were observed to peak among children under the age of five, with males nearly exhibiting a higher prevalence rate across all age groups. This pattern in the whole global population mirrored the total trends in the four Asian regions [15].

This detailed investigation of four Asian regions affected by thalassemia revealed that gender, age, geographical and time period each uniquely influenced the disease burden. While thalassemia prevalence is traditionally considered greatest in Southeast Asian region, our investigation also confirmed the high disease burden in this area (Fig. 1). In this region, Cambodia, Laos, and Thailand exhibited high carrier rates. Cambodia exhibits concerning situation, with a 2019 report from the Cambodia National Institute of Statistics indicating a hemoglobinopathies carrier prevalence of 40.0% (30.0–50.0%) and 2240 annual births with β-thalassemia major in a population of 16 million [16]. Although packed red cells are available at most provincial hospitals, health expenditure per capita still in the lower-middle level. There is an urgent need to develop national policies for thalassemia prevention and control.

The South Asian region presented a stable disease burden, akin to the metrics recorded in 1990 (Table 2). Among these South Asian countries, Pakistan was notable for the high disease burden of ASMR in 2021. Despite challenges in accessing adequate medical care, per capita medical input and a prevalence of transfusion-related infections, Pakistan has seen a statistical reduction in ASMR compared to 1990. This decrease is attributed to comprehensive policies and programs, such as the National Blood Transfusion Program (NBTP), which has been supported by the German agency for international cooperation (GIZ). However, the practice of inter-family marriages remains a key contributor to thalassemia in Pakistan, where the sensitive issues like abortion and genetic counseling face cultural and religious barriers [17, 18]. This situation also occurred in the Maldives [19].

The East Asian region, bolstered by its substantial population size, reported the highest prevalence of cases. Between 1990 and 2021, the mainland of China maintained a stable prevalence rate, yet experienced a reduction in disease burden coincident with economic growth and increased health expenditure. In contrast, the Province of China, Taiwan, exhibited an increasing trend in thalassemia burden, primarily attributed to the influence of migration patterns on the geographic distribution of the disease, as evidenced by genotype analysis [20]. This shift warrants vigilant observation and further investigation.

Moreover, we noticed a statistically unusual increase of mortality rates in some parts of female age cohort when compare to their male counterparts, especially in the child-bearing period. Excess of iron deposition in various organs and hypogonadism influence the female sexual-maturation and pregnancy outcome [21]. Postpartum complications such as anemia, hemorrhage and coagulopathy can result in maternal death in women with thalassemia [22]. With advancing age, the incidence of multiple morbidities associated with thalassemia increases, encompassing osteoporosis, endocrine disorders, hepatic pathologies, renal impairment, and neoplasms. Elderly females often receive a diminished level of healthcare compared to their male counterparts, which is correlated with higher mortality rates [23]. An investigation conducted interviews with Pakistan female patient varying between 10 and 30 years old, they not only suffered from the pressure of economical and disease management, but also the psychology problem caused by the lack acceptance in society [24]. The bio-psycho-social dynamics impacting females with thalassemia warrant scrutiny and necessitate a comprehensive and multidisciplinary approach to care.

Study acknowledges several limitations inherent to GBD estimation efforts. Data scarcity and inconsistencies, particularly in low-income regions, affect the accuracy and comparability of thalassemia prevalence and the impact of health interventions [25]. Variations in diagnostic and reporting practices across countries further complicate burden estimates. Additionally, the GBD database does not distinguish between thalassemia subtypes or between transfusion-dependent and non-transfusion-dependent forms, potentially obscuring epidemiological nuances. The robustness of burden estimates relies on the predictive validity of modeling strategies, which may be challenged by the out-of-sample variability in clinical technology and screening methods. Furthermore, the specific burden attributable to risk factors such as infections and iron overload remains unquantified within the GBD framework.

This study, in summary, leveraging GBD 2021 data, assessed thalassemia burden at regional and national levels from 1990 to 2021, highlighting a general downward trend in disease burden. Base on the epidemiology, we specifically analyzed the impact of gender, economic status, and healthcare investment on thalassemia burden, underscoring the need for increased attention and resource allocation in low-middle and low SDI countries. We also emphasized the significance of addressing gender disparities in disease burden and the role of prenatal diagnosis and genetic screening in preventing thalassemia major births and reducing consanguineous marriages among carriers.

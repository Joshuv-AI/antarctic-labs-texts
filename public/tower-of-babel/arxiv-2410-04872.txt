On affine Kazhdan-Lusztig R-polynomials for Kac-Moody groups
Auguste Hébert, Paul Philippe

Abstract
In [ 24 ] , D. Muthiah proposed a strategy to define affine Kazhdan-Lusztig R R -polynomials for Kac-Moody groups. Since then, Bardy-Panse, the first author and Rousseau have introduced in [ 3 ] the formalism of twin masures and we have extended combinatorial results from affine root systems to general Kac-Moody root systems in [ 13 ] . In this paper, we use these results to explicitly define affine R R -Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomials for Kac-Moody groups. The construction is based on a path model lifting to twin masures. Conjecturally, these polynomials count the cardinality of intersections of opposite affine Schubert cells, as in the case of reductive groups.



1
Introduction

1.1
The affine reductive setup

Reductive groups over Laurent polynomial rings

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a split reductive group scheme with the data of a Borel subgroup
\mathbb{B}
containing a maximal torus
\mathbb{T}
. Let
W^{v}=N_{\mathbb{G}}(\mathbb{T})/\mathbb{T}
be its vectorial Weyl group and
Y
be its coweight lattice:
Y=\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{G}_{m},\mathbb{T})
.
The action of
W^{v}
on
\mathbb{T}
induces an action of
W^{v}
on
Y
and allows to form the semidirect product
\widetilde{W}=Y\rtimes W^{v}
. This group called the extended affine Weyl group of
\mathbb{G}
, appears naturally in the geometry and the representation theory of
\mathbb{G}
over discretely valued fields or Laurent polynomial rings. A foundational work in this regard was done by N. Iwahori and H. Matsumoto in 1965 (
[
16
]
), when they exhibited a Bruhat decomposition of
\mathbb{G}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
indexed by
\widetilde{W}
.

Let
\mathds{k}
be a finite field and
\varpi
be a transcendental element over
\mathds{k}
. Let
G=\mathbb{G}(\mathds{k}(\!(\varpi)\!))
, its integral points form the maximal compact subgroup
\mathbb{G}(\mathds{k}[\![\varpi]\!])
which contains the Iwahori subgroup, defined as
I_{0}=\{g\in K\mid g\mod\varpi\in\mathbb{B}(\mathds{k})\}
. The affine Weyl group is identified with the quotient
N_{G}(\mathbb{T}(\mathds{k}(\!(\varpi)\!)))/\mathbb{T}(\mathds{k}[\![\varpi]\!])
, and admits lift in
G
. Then,
G
admits a decomposition in
I_{0}
double cosets indexed by
\widetilde{W}
, the Iwahori-Matsumoto Bruhat decomposition:

G=\bigsqcup\limits_{\mathbf{x}\in\widetilde{W}}I_{0}\mathbf{x}I_{0}.

This decomposition also holds for
\mathbb{G}(\mathcal{K})
when
\mathcal{K}
is any discretely valued field with residue field
\mathds{k}
. Actually it was first proven by Iwahori and Matsumoto for
\mathbb{G}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
. The group
\widetilde{W}
is a finite extension of a Coxeter group, it is equipped with a Bruhat order which enables to describe the homogeneous space
G/I_{0}
, an infinite-dimensional variety over
\mathds{k}
referred as the affine flag variety. We are interested by
R
-Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomials, which is an instance of the relation between the structure of
G/I_{0}
and the combinatorics of
\widetilde{W}
.

Reductive affine
R
-Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomials

In
G=\mathbb{G}(\mathds{k}(\!(\varpi)\!))
, the Iwahori group
I_{0}
admits an opposite Iwahori subgroup
I_{\infty}=\{g\in\mathbb{G}(\mathds{k}[\varpi^{-1}])\mid g\mod\varpi^{-1}\in\mathbb{B}^{-}(\mathds{k})\}
where
\mathbb{B}^{-}
is the opposite Borel subgroup to
\mathbb{B}
. Then we have an Iwahori-Birkhoff decomposition:

G=\bigsqcup\limits_{\mathbf{y}\in\widetilde{W}}I_{\infty}\mathbf{y}I_{0}.

The affine
R
-Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomials
(R_{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}})_{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in\widetilde{W}}
are combinatorially defined polynomials (see
[
17
]
or
[
5
, §5.3]
for a more recent expository) which verify , for all finite field
\mathds{k}
:

|I_{\infty}\mathbf{y}I_{0}\cap I_{0}\mathbf{x}I_{0}/I_{0}|=R_{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}}(|\mathds{k}|).

(1.1)

This formula, in its affine form, is implicitly used by D. Kazhdan and G. Lusztig in
[
18
, §5]
, and was proven by Z. Haddad (
[
12
]
). A method to describe the combinatorics of these polynomials, and their appearance in Formula (
1.1
), is through a gallery model in the standard apartment of the Bruhat-Tits building associated to
G
. It is a particular instance of the techniques used by E. Milićević, Y. Naqvi, P. Schwer and A. Thomas (see
[
21
]
,
[
32
]
where the authors describe similar subsets of
G/I_{0}
).

The affine
R
-polynomials are auxiliary polynomials in order to define the more famous
P
-Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomials, which are the cornerstone of Kazhdan-Lusztig theory, but they also seem to have a representation theoretic interpretation on their own (see
[
9
]
).

Extension to Kac-Moody groups

Replace
(\mathbb{G},\mathbb{T})
by a general split Kac-Moody group
\mathbb{G}
with maximal split torus
\mathbb{T}
, as defined by Tits in
[
33
]
; its vectorial Weyl group may now be infinite. Let
Y=\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{G}_{m},\mathbb{T})
be the cocharacter lattice of
(\mathbb{G},\mathbb{T})
and let
Y^{+}
is the set of
W^{v}
-translates of the dominant elements of
Y
. When
\mathbb{G}
is reductive
Y^{+}=Y
but otherwise
Y^{+}
is a proper subcone of
Y
. Set
W^{+}=Y^{+}\rtimes W^{v}
. The Iwahori-Matsumoto decomposition no longer holds on
G=\mathbb{G}(\mathds{k}(\!(\varpi)\!))
(and neither on
\mathbb{G}(\mathcal{K})
for more general discretely valued fields
\mathcal{K}
) but it can be replaced by:

G^{+}=\bigsqcup\limits_{\varpi^{\lambda}w\in W^{+}}I_{0}\varpi^{\lambda}wI_{0},

where
G^{+}
is some sub-semi-group of
G
(see
[
7
, §3.4.2]
or
[
2
, §1.9]
). This weakened Iwahori-Matsumoto decomposition appears in related contexts, for example in the definition of the Iwahori-Hecke algebra of
\mathbb{G}(\mathcal{K})
, when
\mathcal{K}
is a non-Archimedean local field (see
[
6
]
and
[
2
]
). In this paper, we construct affine
R
-polynomials in the Kac-Moody setting, using the theory of masures.

1.2
Affine
R
-Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomials in the Kac-Moody case

Masures and twin masures

Masures are generalizations of Bruhat-Tits buildings adapted to the study of Kac-Moody groups, introduced by S. Gaussent and G. Rousseau originally to compute MV-cycles through a path model
[
10
]
. Let
\omega
be a non-trivial discrete valuation on a field
\mathcal{K}
. In order to study
\mathbb{G}(\mathcal{K})
, Rousseau (generalizing the construction of
[
10
]
) defined a masure
\mathcal{I}_{\omega}=\mathcal{I}(\mathbb{G},\mathcal{K},\omega)
on which
G
acts (see
[
30
]
). When
\mathbb{G}
is reductive,
\mathcal{I}_{\omega}
is the usual Bruhat-Tits building. Let
\mathbb{A}_{\omega}=Y\otimes\mathbb{R}
be the standard apartment. When
\mathbb{G}
is a reductive group then
W^{+}
is a Coxeter group and
\mathbb{A}_{\omega}
is a realization of the Coxeter complex of
W^{+}
. Therefore it has a simplicial structure such that
W^{+}
acts transitively on the simplices of maximal dimension, which are called alcoves. When
\mathbb{G}
is general Kac-Moody, there is no such simplicial structure but one can still define analogs of alcoves; no longer as simplices but as filters (sets of sets), and
W^{+}
acts freely transitively on the set of (special) alcoves. We have
\mathcal{I}_{\omega}=\bigsqcup_{g\in G}g.\mathbb{A}_{\omega}
(the dependency on
\omega
is hidden in the fact that the action of
G
on
\mathcal{I}_{\omega}
depends on
\omega
).

Let us now specify to the case of global function fields: we suppose that
\mathcal{K}
is of the form
\mathds{k}(\varpi)
, where
\varpi
is transcendental over
\mathds{k}
. Let
\omega_{\oplus}
(resp.
\omega_{\ominus}
) be the valuation on
\mathcal{K}
such that
\omega_{\oplus}(\mathds{k}^{*}\varpi)=\{1\}
(resp.
\omega_{\ominus}(\mathds{k}^{*}\varpi^{-1})=\{1\}
). Let
\mathcal{I}_{\oplus}=\mathcal{I}_{\omega_{\oplus}}
and
\mathcal{I}_{\ominus}=\mathcal{I}_{\omega_{\ominus}}
. Then one defines the Iwahori subgroups in terms of masures:
I_{0}
(resp.
I_{\infty}
) is the fixator of a particular alcove, named the fundamental alcove
C_{0}
(resp.
C_{\infty}
) of
\mathbb{A}:=\mathbb{A}_{\omega_{\oplus}}
(resp.
\mathbb{A}_{\ominus}=\mathbb{A}_{\omega_{\ominus}}
) in
G
. We then can see
G/I_{0}
as a set of alcoves of
\mathcal{I}_{\oplus}
. When
\mathbb{G}
is reductive, the pair
(\mathcal{I}_{\oplus},\mathcal{I}_{\ominus})
forms a twin building: there is a codistance
d^{*}
relating
\mathcal{I}_{\oplus}
and
\mathcal{I}_{\ominus}
(see
[
1
, 5.8]
). One can also define a retraction
\rho_{C_{\infty}}:\mathcal{I}_{\oplus}\rightarrow\mathbb{A}
centered at
C_{\infty}
: this is the unique
I_{\infty}
-invariant map whose restriction to
\mathbb{A}
is the identity. We can then recover
d^{*}
from
\rho_{C_{\infty}}
. In the Kac-Moody case, no equivalent of the codistance is known for the moment. However, one can define
\rho_{C_{\infty}}
on a subset of
\mathcal{I}
. A difficulty is that the set of definition of
\rho_{C_{\infty}}
is hard to determine and very few is currently known on this set.

Conjecturally (see
[
3
, 4.4.1]
), this set is large enough and we can use
\rho_{C_{\infty}}
to compute
|I_{\infty}\mathbf{y}I_{0}\cap I_{0}\mathbf{x}I_{0}/I_{0}|
. Elements of
I_{\infty}\mathbf{y}I_{0}\cap I_{0}\mathbf{x}I_{0}/I_{0}
are in bijection with the alcoves in
\rho_{C_{0}}^{-1}(C_{\mathbf{x}})\cap\rho_{C_{\infty}}^{-1}(C_{\mathbf{y}})
. Each of these alcoves defines an open segment (the data of a segment and an alcove based at its end point) in
\mathcal{I}_{\oplus}
and, when
\mathbf{x}
has finite stabiliser in
W^{v}
(we say that it is spherical), we can count them using the path model in
\mathbb{A}
described below.

Affine Bruhat order on
W^{+}

When
G
is reductive, the extended affine Weyl group
\widetilde{W}
, which is a finite extension of the affine Weyl group
W^{a}
, naturally inherits a Bruhat order
\leq
from the Coxeter group structure of
W^{a}
. This order plays a crucial roles in the study of Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomials. For example, if
\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in W^{a}
, then
R_{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}}\neq 0
if and only if
\mathbf{y}\leq\mathbf{x}
and it is conjectured that
R_{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}}
only depends on the structure of the interval
[\mathbf{y},\mathbf{x}]=\{\mathbf{z}\in\widetilde{W}\mid\mathbf{y}\leq\mathbf{z}\leq\mathbf{x}\}
. In the Kac-Moody setting, A. Braverman, D. Kazhdan and M. Patnaik propose in
[
7
, Appendix B2]
a definition of a preorder on
W^{+}
which would replace the Bruhat order of
\widetilde{W}
and they conjecture that it is a partial order. In
[
23
]
, D. Muthiah extends the definition of this preorder to any Kac-Moody group
G
and proves that it is actually an order. A. Welch proved in
[
34
, Theorem 3]
that the intervals for this order are finite, when
G
if non-twisted affine of ADE type and we generalized this result to any Kac-Moody groups in
[
13
, Corollary 1.2]
.

C_{\infty}
-Hecke open paths and
R
-Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomials

In this paper we introduce
C_{\infty}
-Hecke open paths, they are the piece-wise affine paths which appear as images of open segments by
\rho_{C_{\infty}}
in the masure
\mathcal{I}_{\oplus}
, but they can be defined combinatorially in
\mathbb{A}
without reference to the masure. Non-open
C_{\infty}
-Hecke paths have already been introduced by Muthiah (
[
24
]
where they are called
I_{\infty}
-Hecke paths) and developed by N. Bardy-Panse, the first-named author and Rousseau in
[
3
]
. An open path is a non-open path with the additional data of a decoration, and an ending alcove (see Definition
3.1
). These open paths permit to study
G^{+}/I_{0}
, whereas non-open paths are suited to the study of
G^{+}/K
, where
K
is fixator of the vertex
0_{\mathbb{A}}
of the fundamental alcove. In particular any
C_{\infty}
-Hecke open path has a type
\mathbf{x}\in W^{+}
(which we suppose spherical, that is to say that
\mathbf{x}
has finite stabilizer in
W^{v}
) and an end alcove
C_{\mathbf{y}}
with
\mathbf{y}\in W^{+}
; we denote by
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{\mathbf{x}}(\mathbf{y})
the set of
C_{\infty}
-Hecke open paths of type
\mathbf{x}
and with end alcove
C_{\mathbf{y}}
. We start by looking at the relation between
C_{\infty}
-Hecke open paths and the affine Bruhat order. In particular, using the finiteness of the intervals, we obtain:

Proposition 1.1
.

(see Corollary
3.11
)
Let
\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in W^{+}
with
\mathbf{x}
spherical. Then
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{\mathbf{x}}(\mathbf{y})
is finite, and it is empty if
\mathbf{y}\not\leq\mathbf{x}
.

R
-Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomials

A given
C_{\infty}
-Hecke open path
\underline{\mathfrak{c}}
of spherical type
\mathbf{x}\in W^{+}
lifts to open segments in
\mathcal{I}_{\oplus}
, and we show that the number of these lifts are obtained by the evaluation at
|\mathds{k}|
of an integral polynomial
R_{\mathbf{x},\underline{\mathfrak{c}}}
, independent of the masure, for which we give an explicit formula in Theorem
4.25
. Summing up
R_{\mathbf{x},\underline{\mathfrak{c}}}
upon all the paths of
C^{\infty}_{\mathbf{x}}(\mathbf{y})
we are able to define an
R
-Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomial
R_{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}}
associated to any pair
(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
of
W^{+}
such that
\mathbf{x}
is spherical. They therefore appear in the following result:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in W^{+}
be such that
\mathbf{x}
is spherical. Let
A_{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}}(\mathcal{I}_{\oplus})
denote the set of alcoves
C\in\rho_{C_{0}}^{-1}(C_{\mathbf{x}})\cap\rho_{C_{\infty}}^{-1}(C_{\mathbf{y}})
such that the retraction
\rho_{C_{\infty}}
is defined on the whole open segment
[0_{\mathbb{A}},C]\subset\mathcal{I}_{\oplus}
. This is a finite set and:

|A_{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}}(\mathcal{I}_{\oplus})|=R_{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}}(|\mathds{k}|).

(1.2)

By construction
|A_{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}}(\mathcal{I}_{\oplus})|\leq|I_{\infty}\mathbf{y}I_{0}\cap I_{0}\mathbf{x}I_{0}/I_{0}|
and upon Conjecture
[
3
, 4.4.1]
this is an equality. Therefore Formula (
1.1
) remains conjectural in this context, however these polynomials already have interesting combinatorial properties related to the affine Bruhat order on
W^{+}
. For example, we show that, if
\mathbf{x}
covers
\mathbf{y}
, then
R_{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}}(X)=X-1
which is a standard property in the reductive setting.

Note that in the recent preprint
[
25
]
, M. Patnaik obtains results which might be related to ours. He proves that when
\mathbb{G}
is affine, in some completion
\hat{G}
of
G
, a variant of the left hand side of (
1.1
) is finite (see
[
25
, 5.2]
). He proves an analogue (in his frameworks, which is slighlty different from ours) of a conjecture in
[
3
, 4.4.1]
. However, he does not prove polynomiality results. His techniques are very different from ours and do not use masures. It would be interesting to relate these two works.

Going further

In classical Kazhdan-Lusztig theory,
R
-polynomials are auxiliary tools to compute the more famous
P
-Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomials, which play a significant role in geometric representation theory and representation theory of Lie algebras (see
[
18
]
). We would like to define the affine version of these
P
-polynomials in the general Kac-Moody setting, this requires that the
R
-polynomials satisfy an appropriate involutive formula in which the affine Bruhat length appears (see
[
24
, §1.2.4]
).

Organization of the paper

In section
2
we introduce the affine Weyl semi-group associated to a Kac-Moody root datum, and the affine Bruhat order. We then give a geometric interpretation of these objects.
In section
3
, we introduce the notion of
C_{\infty}
-Hecke open path in the standard apartment. We establish properties of these paths with respect to the Bruhat order (Proposition
3.10
) and use it and the finiteness results on the affine Bruhat order to prove finiteness results on the set of
C_{\infty}
-open paths (see Corollary
3.11
).

We then turn, in section
4
, to liftings of
C_{\infty}
-Hecke open paths in the masure, in order to construct affine
R
-polynomials. We start by introducing twin masures, and in subsection
4.2
we explain our strategy to construct affine
R
-polynomials. We prove in subsection
4.3
that
C_{\infty}
-friendly open segments retract to
C_{\infty}
-Hecke open paths, and we give combinatorial formulas to count, for each
C_{\infty}
-Hecke open path of a given type, its number of lifts in subsection
4.4
. We conclude, in subsection
4.5
, by defining the affine Kazhdan-Lusztig
R
-polynomials, and we compute
R
-polynomials of covers.

There is an index of notations at the end of the paper.

2
The standard apartment and the affine Bruhat order

2.1
Definitions and notations

Kac-Moody root systems

Let
\mathcal{D}=(A,X,Y,(\alpha_{i})_{i\in I},(\alpha_{i}^{\vee})_{i\in I})
be a Kac-Moody root datum in the sense of
[
27
, §8]
. It is a quintuplet such that:

—

I
is a finite indexing set and
A=(a_{ij})_{(i,j)\in I\times I}
is a generalized Cartan matrix.

—

X
and
Y
are two dual free
\mathbb{Z}
-modules of finite rank, we write
\langle,\rangle
the duality bracket.

—

(\alpha_{i})_{i\in I}
(resp.
(\alpha_{i}^{\vee})_{i\in I}
) is a family of linearly independent elements of
X
(resp.
Y
), the simple roots (resp. simple coroots).

—

For all
(i,j)\in I^{2}
we have
\langle\alpha_{i}^{\vee},\alpha_{j}\rangle=a_{ij}
.

Vectorial Weyl group

For every
i\in I
set
s_{i}\in\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathbb{Z}}(X):x\mapsto x-\langle\alpha_{i}^{\vee},x\rangle\alpha_{i}
. The generated group
W^{v}=\langle s_{i}\mid i\in I\rangle
is the
vectorial Weyl group
of the Kac-Moody root datum. The duality bracket
\langle Y,X\rangle
induces a contragredient action of
W^{v}
on
Y
, explicitly
s_{i}(y)=y-\langle y,\alpha_{i}\rangle\alpha_{i}^{\vee}
. By construction the duality bracket is then
W^{v}
-invariant.

The pair
(W^{v},\{s_{i}\mid i\in I\})
is a Coxeter system, in particular
W^{v}
has a Bruhat order
<
and a length function
\ell
compatible with the Bruhat order. The Weyl group
W^{v}
is infinite if and only if the matrix
A
is not of finite type, we will usually work in this setting.

Real roots

Let
\Phi=W^{v}.\{\alpha_{i}\mid i\in I\}
be the set of real roots of
\mathcal{D}
, it is a, possibly infinite, root system (see
[
19
, 1.2.2 Definition]
).
In particular let
\Phi_{+}=\Phi\cap\oplus_{i\in I}\mathbb{N}\alpha_{i}
be the set of positive real roots, then
\Phi=\Phi_{+}\sqcup-\Phi_{+}
, we write
\Phi_{-}=-\Phi_{+}
the set of negative roots.

The set
\Phi^{\vee}=W^{v}.\{\alpha_{i}^{\vee}\mid i\in I\}
is the set of
coroots
, and its subset
\Phi^{\vee}_{+}=\Phi^{\vee}\cap\oplus_{i\in I}\mathbb{N}\alpha_{i}^{\vee}
is the set of
positive coroots
.

To each root
\beta
corresponds a unique coroot
\beta^{\vee}
: if
\beta=w(\alpha_{i})
then
\beta^{\vee}=w(\alpha_{i}^{\vee})
. This map
\beta\mapsto\beta^{\vee}
is well defined, bijective between
\Phi
and
\Phi^{\vee}
and sends positive roots to positive coroots. Note that
\langle\beta^{\vee},\beta\rangle=2
for all
\beta\in\Phi
.

Moreover to each root
\beta
one associates a reflection
s_{\beta}\in W^{v}
: if
\beta=w(\pm\alpha_{i})
then
s_{\beta}:=ws_{i}w^{-1}
. It is well-defined, independently of the choices of
w
and
i
. Explicitly it is the map
x\mapsto x-\langle\beta^{\vee},x\rangle\beta
. We have
s_{\beta}=s_{-\beta}
and the map
\beta\mapsto s_{\beta}
forms a bijection between the set of positive roots and the set
\{ws_{i}w^{-1}\mid(w,i)\in W^{v}\times I\}
of reflections of
W^{v}
.

Let
w\in W^{v}
. The
inversion set
of
w
is the set

\mathrm{Inv}(w):=\{\alpha\in\Phi_{+}\mid w(\alpha)\in\Phi_{-}\}=\{\alpha\in\Phi_{+}\mid ws_{\alpha}<w\}.

An element of the vectorial Weyl group is completely determined by its inversion set.

Dominant coweights and Tits cone

Elements of
Y
are called coweights. We say that a coweight
\lambda\in Y
is
dominant
if
\langle\lambda,\alpha_{i}\rangle\geq 0
for all
i\in I
, or equivalently if
\langle\lambda,\beta\rangle\geq 0
for all
\beta\in\Phi_{+}
. The set of dominant coweights is denoted as
Y^{++}=\{\lambda\in Y\mid\langle\lambda,\alpha_{i}\rangle\geq 0\;\forall i\in I\}
.

The orbit of
Y^{++}
by
W^{v}
is the
integral Tits cone
,
Y^{+}=\bigcup\limits_{w\in W^{v}}w.Y^{++}
; it is a sub-cone of
Y
and it is a proper sub-cone if and only if
W^{v}
is infinite, if and only if
A
is not of finite type (see
[
19
, 1.4.2]
).

The set
Y^{++}
is a fundamental domain for the action of
W^{v}
on
Y^{+}
, and for any coweight
\lambda\in Y^{+}
(resp.
\lambda\in-Y^{+}
), we denote
\lambda^{++}
to be the unique element of
Y^{++}
(resp.
-Y^{++}
) in its
W^{v}
-orbit.

Parabolic subgroups and minimal coset representatives

Let
\lambda\in Y^{++}
be a dominant coweight. Then the fixator of
\lambda
in
W^{v}
is a standard parabolic subgroup, which we denote by
W_{\lambda}
. Explicitly, if
J=\{i\in I\mid\langle\lambda,\alpha_{i}\rangle=0\}
, then
W_{\lambda}=W_{J}:=\langle r_{j}\mid j\in J\rangle
.

Any class
[w]\in W^{v}/W_{\lambda}
admits a unique element of minimal length, and we denote by
W^{\lambda}
the set of coset representatives of minimal length. Therefore
w\in W^{\lambda}\iff\ell(w)\leq\ell(\widetilde{w})\,\forall\widetilde{w}\in wW_{\lambda}
.

Suppose now that
\lambda\in Y^{+}
(not necessarily dominant), we denote by
v^{\lambda}
the unique element of minimal length such that
\lambda=v^{\lambda}\lambda^{++}
, it is an element of
W^{\lambda^{++}}
. The element
v^{\lambda}
is determined by:
\{\alpha\in\Phi_{+}\mid\langle\lambda,\alpha\rangle<0\}=\mathrm{Inv}((v^{\lambda})^{-1})
.

The coweight
\lambda
also admits a parabolic fixator in
W^{v}
, which is
W_{\lambda}=v^{\lambda}W_{\lambda^{++}}(v^{\lambda})^{-1}
.

A coweight
\lambda
is
spherical
if its fixator
W_{\lambda}
is finite, and it is
regular
if
W_{\lambda}
is trivial. These notions only depend on the dominant part
\lambda^{++}
of
\lambda
. We denote by
Y^{+}_{sph}
the set of spherical coweights. Note that a coweight
\lambda
is regular and dominant if and only if
\langle\lambda,\alpha_{i}\rangle>0
for all
i\in I
.

For any
\lambda\in Y^{+}_{sph}
, we denote by
w_{\lambda,0}
the maximal element of the standard parabolic subgroup
W_{\lambda^{++}}
.

Affine Weyl semi-group

Through the left action
W^{v}\curvearrowright Y
we can form the semidirect product
Y\rtimes W^{v}
, for
(\lambda,w)\in Y\rtimes W^{v}
we denote by
\varpi^{\lambda}w
the corresponding element of
Y\rtimes W^{v}
. If
W^{v}
is finite, this semi-direct product is (a finite extension of) a Coxeter group, hence admits a Bruhat order, a Bruhat length and a set of simple reflections. However when
W^{v}
is infinite, it does not have a Coxeter structure, and there is no known analog of the Bruhat order on it.

By definition,
Y^{+}\subset Y
is stable by the action of
W^{v}
, therefore we can form
W^{+}=Y^{+}\rtimes W^{v}
which is a sub-semi-group of
Y\rtimes W^{v}
. This semi-group is called
the affine Weyl semi-group
, it is sometimes referred as "the affine Weyl group" in the literature, even though it is not a group.

Braverman, Kazhdan and Patnaik have defined a preorder on
W^{+}
in
[
7
, B.2]
using an affine root system on which
Y\rtimes W^{v}
acts. We now introduce this preorder (which is, in fact, an order), and in Section
2.2
we give a geometric interpretation, already detailed in
[
26
]
.

We denote by
\mathrm{proj}^{Y^{+}}
the projection function
W^{+}\rightarrow Y^{+}
and
\mathrm{proj}^{Y^{++}}
the projection on the dominant coweight, so if
\mathbf{x}=\varpi^{\lambda}w\in W^{+}
, then
\mathrm{proj}^{Y^{+}}(\mathbf{x})=\lambda
and
\mathrm{proj}^{Y^{++}}(\mathbf{x})=\lambda^{++}
. We say that an element
\mathbf{x}\in W^{+}
is
spherical
if its coweight
\mathrm{proj}^{Y^{+}}(\mathbf{x})
is spherical, we then denote
W^{+}_{sph}=Y^{+}_{sph}\rtimes W^{v}
the set of spherical elements in
W^{+}
.

Affine roots

Let
\Phi^{a}=\Phi\times\mathbb{Z}
, it is
the set of affine roots
of
\mathcal{D}
. Let
(\beta,n)\in\Phi^{a}
, we say that
(\beta,n)
is positive if
n>0
or (
n=0
and
\beta\in\Phi_{+}
); we write
\Phi^{a}_{+}
for the set of positive affine roots. We also have
\Phi^{a}=\Phi^{a}_{+}\sqcup-\Phi^{a}_{+}
. We can see
\underline{\beta}=(\beta,n)\in\Phi^{a}
as the affine form on
Y
defined by
\underline{\beta}(\lambda)=\langle\lambda,\beta\rangle+n
. Since
Y\rtimes W^{v}
acts by affine transformations on
Y
, it also acts on its space of affine forms (by
(\underline{w}\cdot\varphi)(\lambda)=\varphi(\underline{w}^{-1}\lambda)
for
\underline{w}\in Y\rtimes W^{v}
and
\varphi\in\operatorname{Hom_{Aff}}(Y,\mathbb{Z})
), and this action preserves
\Phi^{a}
. We therefore have a natural action of
Y\rtimes W^{v}
on
\Phi^{a}
, explicitely given by:

\varpi^{\lambda}w.(\beta,n)=(w\beta,n+\langle\lambda,w\beta\rangle).

(2.1)

For any
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, its sign is denoted
\sgn(n)\in\{-1,+1\}
, with the convention that
\sgn(0)=+1
.

For
n\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\beta\in\Phi_{+}
, we set:

\displaystyle\beta[n]=(\sgn(n)\beta,|n|\varpi)\in\Phi_{+}^{a}

(2.2)

\displaystyle s_{\beta[n]}=\varpi^{n\beta^{\vee}}s_{\beta}\in Y\rtimes W^{v}.

(2.3)

If
n\neq 0
we also define
\beta[n]\in\Phi^{a}_{+}
for
\beta\in\Phi_{-}
, by
\beta[n]=(-\beta)[-n]
.
Note that the element
s_{\beta[n]}
does not belong to
W^{+}
in general, but it is an element of order
2
in
Y\rtimes W^{v}
which fixes
\beta[n]^{-1}(\{0\})
, the
affine reflection associated to
\beta[n]
.

Affine Bruhat order

The
affine Bruhat order
on
W^{+}
is the transitive closure of the relation
<
defined by, for
\mathbf{x}\in W^{+}
and
\beta[n]\in\Phi^{a}_{+}
:
s_{\beta[n]}\mathbf{x}<\mathbf{x}\iff\mathbf{x}^{-1}(\beta[n])\notin\Phi^{a}_{+}
. In some sense this means that
\beta[n]
belongs to the inversion set of
\mathbf{x}^{-1}
. Note in particular that if
\mathbf{x}^{-1}(\beta[n])\notin\Phi_{+}^{a}
and
\mathbf{x}
lies in
W^{+}
then
s_{\beta[n]}\mathbf{x}\in W^{+}
.

Explicitly, if
\mathbf{x}=\varpi^{\lambda}w\in W^{+}
, then
\mathbf{x}^{-1}(\beta[n])\notin\Phi_{+}^{a}
if and only if
|n|<\sgn(n)\langle\lambda,\beta\rangle
, or
|n|=\sgn(n)\langle\lambda,\beta\rangle
and
\sgn(n)w^{-1}\beta\in\Phi_{-}
.

D. Muthiah (see
[
23
]
) has proved that the transitive closure of
<
is anti-symmetric, hence it is an order. Moreover, together with D. Orr, they define in
[
22
]
a
\mathbb{Z}
-valued length function on
W^{+}
strictly compatible with the affine Bruhat order.

2.2
The affine Bruhat order in the standard apartment

There is a strong geometric intuition behind root systems, vectorial Weyl groups and the vectorial Bruhat order, developed for instance in the context of buildings in
[
28
]
. There is also a geometrical interpretation of the affine Bruhat order on
W^{+}
which we develop in this section, it takes place in the standard apartment
\mathbb{A}
, which is the affine space underlying
Y\otimes\mathbb{R}
.
More precisely, elements of
W^{+}
can be identified with certain local germs in
\mathbb{A}
, called alcoves, and each positive affine root corresponds to a hyperplane
M_{\beta[n]}
separating the set of alcoves in two. For
\mathbf{x}\in W^{+},\beta[n]\in\Phi_{+}^{a}
, we have that
\mathbf{x}<s_{\beta[n]}\mathbf{x}
if and only if the alcove corresponding to
\mathbf{x}
is on the same side of the wall
M_{\beta[n]}
as the fundamental alcove (the alcove corresponding to
1_{W^{+}}
). In the reductive setting, we recover the geometric interpretation of the affine Bruhat order in term of tiling of the standard apartment.
We start by recalling the geometrical interpretation of the standard Bruhat order of a (vectorial) Weyl group.

2.2.1
The vectorial apartment

We fix a Kac-Moody root datum
\mathcal{D}=(A,X,Y,(\alpha_{i}^{\vee})_{i\in I},(\alpha_{i})_{i\in I})
.
Let
V=Y\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}
,
X
embeds in its dual
V^{\vee}
and the vectorial Weyl group
W^{v}
acts naturally on it. For each root
\beta\in\Phi_{+}
let
M_{\beta}=\{x\in V\mid\langle x,\beta\rangle=0\}
, it is a hyperplane of
V
called
the vectorial wall associated to
\beta
. We denote
\mathcal{M}_{0}=\{M_{\beta}\mid\beta\in\Phi_{+}\}
. The pair
(V,\mathcal{M}_{0})
is the
vectorial apartment
of
\mathcal{D}
.

Inside
V
we have
the fundamental chamber

C^{v}_{f}=\{v\in V\mid\langle v,\alpha_{i}\rangle>0,\forall i\in I\}
and the
Tits cone

\mathscr{T}=W^{v}.\overline{C^{v}_{f}}
, it is a convex cone. We denote its interior by
\mathring{\mathscr{T}}
, it is the set of elements with finite stabilizer in
W^{v}
. Note that
Y^{+}=\mathscr{T}\cap Y
,
Y^{+}_{sph}=\mathring{\mathscr{T}}\cap Y
and
Y^{++}=\overline{C^{v}_{f}}\cap Y
. We extend the definition of
++
to
\mathscr{T}\cup-\mathscr{T}
: for
x\in\mathscr{T}
(resp.
x\in-\mathscr{T}
), we denote by
x^{++}
the unique element of
\overline{C^{v}_{f}}\cap W^{v}.x
(resp.
-\overline{C^{v}_{f}}\cap W^{v}.x
).

A (positive)
vectorial chamber
is a set of the form
w.C^{v}_{f}
for
w\in W^{v}
. Since
C^{v}_{f}
has trivial stabiliser in
W^{v}
, the set of chambers is in natural bijection with
W^{v}
by
w\mapsto C^{v}_{w}:=wC^{v}_{f}
. Note that the vectorial chambers are the connected components of
\mathscr{T}\setminus(\bigcup\limits_{\beta\in\Phi_{+}}M_{\beta})
. Moreover, if
\beta=w(\alpha_{i})
with
\alpha_{i}
a simple root, then
M_{\beta}\cap\mathscr{T}
contains
\overline{C^{v}_{w}}\cap\overline{C^{v}_{ws_{i}}}
, which we call the
panel
of type
i
of the chamber
C_{w}
. We define negative chambers as the opposite of positive chambers. If
\Phi
is infinite, then positive and negative chambers form two distinct
W^{v}
-orbit.

We can put a structure of simplicial complex on
\mathscr{T}
, for which the chambers are the cells of maximal rank and the panels are the cells of maximal rank within non-chambers. The walls split the Tits cone in two parts, and separate the set of vectorial chambers in two: say that
C^{v}_{w}
is on the positive side of
M_{\beta}
if
w^{-1}(\beta)>0
. In particular since
\beta
is a positive root, the positive side is always the one which contains the fundamental chamber.

Remark 2.1
.

The condition is on
w^{-1}(\alpha)
and not
w(\alpha)
because
W^{v}
acts on the left on
V
and the contragredient action on its dual, which contain the roots, is more naturally a right action:
\langle w.y,\alpha\rangle=\langle y,w^{-1}(\alpha)\rangle

Then the vectorial Bruhat order can be interpreted by:

s_{\beta}w>w
if and only if, when we split
\mathscr{T}
along
M_{\beta}
the chambers
C^{v}_{w}
and
C^{v}_{f}
are in the same connected component of
\mathscr{T}
, that is to say
C^{v}_{w}
is on the positive side of
M_{\beta}
.

Moreover
\mathrm{Inv}(w^{-1})
the inversion set of
w^{-1}
can be interpreted as the set of walls separating the chamber
C^{v}_{w}=w.C^{v}_{f}
from the fundamental chamber
C^{v}_{f}
.

2.2.2
The affine apartment

We now define the affine apartment associated to a Kac-Moody root datum, as it is defined in
[
11
]
. Let
\mathcal{D}=(A,X,Y,(\alpha_{i}^{\vee})_{i\in I},(\alpha_{i})_{i\in I})
be a Kac-Moody root datum and let
\mathbb{A}
be the real affine space associated to
V=Y\otimes\mathbb{R}
.

To any positive affine root
\beta[n]\in\Phi_{+}^{a}
corresponds an affine hyperplane
M_{\beta[n]}
, the
affine wall
associated to the affine root
\beta[n]
. This hyperplane is equal to
(\beta[n])^{-1}(\{0\})
if we consider
\beta[n]
as an affine form on
\mathbb{A}
. Explicitly, we therefore have

M_{\beta[n]}=\{x\in\mathbb{A}\mid\langle x,\beta\rangle+n=0\}.

(2.4)

Let
\mathcal{M}^{a}=\{M_{\beta[n]}\mid\beta[n]\in\Phi_{+}^{a}\}
be the set of affine walls. Then the
affine apartment
is the pair
(\mathbb{A},\mathcal{M}^{a})
. If
n\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{Z}
and
\beta\in\Phi
, the affine hyperplane
\{x\in\mathbb{A}\mid\langle x,\beta\rangle+n=0\}
is referred as a
ghost wall
, and is still denoted
M_{\beta[n]}
. A
half-apartment
is any region of
\mathbb{A}
delimited by an affine wall, hence any region of the form
\{x\in\mathbb{A}\mid\langle x,\beta\rangle+n\mathcal{R}0\}
, for
\mathcal{R}\in\{<,>,\leq,\geq\}
.

The group
W^{v}\ltimes Y
acts by affine transformations on
\mathbb{A}
, by the formula
\varpi^{\lambda}w(x)=-\lambda+w(x)
. This action preserves
\mathcal{M}^{a}
.

To give an interpretation of the affine Bruhat order, we need to define the affine analog of vectorial chambers, which are called alcoves. However, when the root system is infinite, the set of affine walls is not locally discrete, and therefore the connected components of
\mathbb{A}\setminus\cup_{M\in\mathcal{M}^{a}}M
have empty interior and are not adapted. There are two ways of dealing with this issue, either by working in the tangent space of
\mathbb{A}
, as it is done in
[
26
]
, or by defining affine chambers no longer as sets but as filters of sets, which is better suited for masure theoretic purposes and therefore is more often done in the literature (see
[
11
]
).

Filters

A
filter
on a set
E
is a nonempty set
\mathcal{V}
of nonempty subsets of
E
such that, for all subsets
S
,
S^{\prime}
of
E
, if
S
,
S^{\prime}\in\mathcal{V}
then
S\cap S^{\prime}\in\mathcal{V}
and, if
S^{\prime}\subset S
, with
S^{\prime}\in\mathcal{V}
then
S\in\mathcal{V}
.

Let
E,E^{\prime}
be sets,
E^{\prime}\subset E
and
\mathcal{V}
be a filter on
E^{\prime}
. One says that a set
\Omega\subset E
contains
\mathcal{V}
if there exists
\Omega^{\prime}\in\mathcal{V}
such that
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\Omega
(or equivalently if
\Omega\in\mathcal{V}
if
E=E^{\prime}
). Let
f:E\rightarrow E
. One says that
f
fixes
\mathcal{V}
if there exists
\Omega^{\prime}\in\mathcal{V}
such that
f
fixes
\Omega^{\prime}
.

A
basis
for a filter
\mathcal{V}
is a subset
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{V}
such that
\mathcal{V}=\{S\subset E\mid\exists B\in\mathcal{B},\;B\subset S\}
. If
\mathcal{B}
is a set of sets closed by finite intersection and not containing the empty set, then
\{S\subset E\mid\exists B\in\mathcal{B},\;B\subset S\}
is a filter with basis
\mathcal{B}
.

Alcoves

Let
\|.\|
be an arbitrary norm on
V
, and for any
x\in\mathbb{A}
,
r\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
let
B(x,r)
denote the open ball of radius
r
centered at
x
for
\|.\|
.
Let
x\in\mathbb{A}
,
w\in W^{v}
and
\varepsilon\in\{+,-\}
. The (closed)
alcove based at
x
and of vectorial direction
\varepsilon C^{v}_{w}
is the filter with basis
\{(x+\varepsilon\overline{C^{v}_{w}})\cap B(x,r)\mid r>0\}
, it is denoted
a(x,\varepsilon w)
,
\varepsilon
is its
sign
.
The
sector
generated by
a(x,\varepsilon w)
is the set
x+\varepsilon\overline{C^{v}_{w}}\subset\mathbb{A}
. When
(\mathbb{A},\mathcal{M}^{a})
is discrete (when the Kac-Moody root system is of finite type), we recover the definition of pointed alcoves: alcoves with a choice of base-point.

An alcove is well-defined by its base-point and its vectorial direction: if
a
is an alcove, then its base-point
x
is the unique point of
\mathbb{A}
such that
\forall U\in a,\;x\in U
, and its vectorial direction is the unique vectorial cone of the form
\varepsilon C^{v}_{w}
such that
(x+\varepsilon C^{v}_{w})\cap U
has non-empty interior for all
U\in a
. We denote by
a^{v}
the direction of an alcove
a
.

The semi-direct product
W^{v}\ltimes Y
acts freely on the set of alcoves, through the following action (induced by its action on
\mathbb{A}
):

\varpi^{\lambda}w.a(x,\varepsilon v)=a(w.x-\lambda,\varepsilon wv).

We can therefore identify
W^{+}=Y^{+}\rtimes W^{v}
, with a subset of alcoves: to any
\varpi^{\lambda}w\in W^{+}
, the associated alcove is
a(-\lambda,+w)=\varpi^{\lambda}w.a(0_{\mathbb{A}},1_{W^{v}})
, which we denote
C_{\varpi^{\lambda}w}
.

The map
(x,\varepsilon,w)\mapsto a(x,\varepsilon w)
induces a bijection between the set of alcoves and
\mathbb{A}\times\{-,+\}\times W^{v}
. Using this bijection, we equip the set of alcoves with the product topology, where
\mathbb{A}
is equipped with the usual topology of finite dimensional affine space and
\{-,+\}\times W^{v}
is equipped with the discrete topology. Given any point
x\in\mathbb{A}
, the set of alcoves based at
x
forms the tangent fundamental apartment at
x
, which has a particular combinatorial structure described below.

Tangent apartment at a point

Let
x\in\mathbb{A}
and
\varepsilon\in\{+,-\}
. Denote by
\mathcal{T}^{\varepsilon}_{x}(\mathbb{A})
the set of alcoves of sign
\varepsilon
based at
x
. We define a
W^{v}
-valued distance function
d^{\varepsilon}:\mathcal{T}^{\varepsilon}_{x}(\mathbb{A}_{\oplus})\rightarrow W^{v}
: if
a_{1},a_{2}\in\mathcal{T}^{\varepsilon}_{x}(\mathbb{A})
, there exist unique elements
w_{1},w_{2}\in W^{v}
such that
a_{1}=a(x,\varepsilon w_{1})
and
a_{2}=a(x,\varepsilon w_{2})
. Then we set:

d^{\varepsilon}(a_{1},a_{2})=w_{1}^{-1}w_{2}.

(2.5)

We also define a codistance function:
d^{\ast}:\mathcal{T}^{+}_{x}(\mathbb{A}_{\oplus})\times\mathcal{T}^{-}_{x}(\mathbb{A}_{\oplus})\cup\mathcal{T}^{-}_{x}(\mathbb{A}_{\oplus})\times\mathcal{T}^{+}_{x}(\mathbb{A}_{\oplus})\rightarrow W^{v}
:

If
(a_{+},a_{-})\in\mathcal{T}^{+}_{x}(\mathbb{A}_{\oplus})\times\mathcal{T}^{-}_{x}(\mathbb{A}_{\oplus})
, similarly there exist
w_{+},w_{-}\in W^{v}
such that
a_{+}=a(x,+w_{+})
and
a_{-}=a(x,-w_{-})
. Then set
d^{\ast}(a_{+},a_{-})=w_{+}^{-1}w_{-}
and

d^{\ast}(a_{-},a_{+})=w_{-}^{-1}w_{+}=d^{\ast}(a_{+},a_{-})^{-1}.

(2.6)

We say that
a_{-},a_{+}
are
opposite
if
d^{\ast}(a_{-},a_{+})=1_{W^{v}}
.
We call
(\mathcal{T}^{\pm}_{x}(\mathbb{A}),d^{\pm},d^{\ast})
the
standard tangent apartment
at
x
. In building theoretic terms, it is a twin apartment (or twin thin building) of type
(W^{v},S)
.

Geometric interpretation of the affine Bruhat order

Mirroring the classical situation,
C_{0}=a(0_{\mathbb{A}},+1_{W^{v}})
is called the
fundamental alcove
and
W^{+}
acts on
\{C_{\mathbf{x}}\mid\mathbf{x}\in W^{+}\}
simply transitively. Affine walls (and ghost walls) separate naturally the set of alcoves in two and we call the side containing
C_{0}
the positive side. Note that an affine root
(\beta,n)
is positive if and only if
\beta(C_{0})+n\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}
, therefore the positive side of an affine wall corresponds to the associated positive root. Let
\beta\in\Phi
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\varpi^{\lambda}w\in W^{+}
.

—

If
n\neq 0
and
-\lambda\notin M_{\beta[n]}
, then
C_{\varpi^{\lambda}w}
is on the positive side of
M_{\beta[n]}
if and only if
-\lambda
is in the same connected component of
\mathbb{A}\setminus M_{\beta[n]}
than
0_{\mathbb{A}}
, that is to say
|n|-\sgn(n)\langle\lambda,\beta\rangle>0
.

—

If
n>0
(resp.
n<0
) and
-\lambda\in M_{\beta[n]}
, it is on the positive side if and only if
C^{v}_{w}
is on the positive side (resp. negative side) of
M_{\beta}
, that is to say
\sgn(n)w^{-1}(\beta)>0
.

—

If
n=0
and
-\lambda\notin M_{\beta[0]}
then
C_{\varpi^{\lambda}w}
is on the positive side of the wall if and only if
\langle\lambda,\beta\rangle<0
hence on the side of
C^{v}_{f}
.

—

If
n=0
and
-\lambda\in M_{\beta[0]}
, then
C_{\varpi^{\lambda}w}
is on the positive side of the wall if and only if
C^{v}_{w}
is on the positive side of
M_{\beta}
, that is to say
w^{-1}(\beta)>0
, hence on the side of
C_{0}
.

Then the
W^{+}
-Bruhat order can be interpreted by:
s_{\beta[n]}\varpi^{\lambda}w>\varpi^{\lambda}w
if and only if
C_{\varpi^{\lambda}w}
is on the positive side of
M_{\beta[n]}
.

Remark 2.2
.

Another possible choice of embedding would be to identify
\varpi^{\lambda}w
to the alcove
a(\lambda,-w)
and define
M_{\beta[n]}
as
\{x\mid\langle x,\beta\rangle=n\}
but either way, the vectorial direction of the alcove needs to be of opposite sign of its base-point. Our choice is motivated by the choices made in
[
3
]
.

2.2.3
Segments, paths, segment germs

We end this preliminary section by formally introducing segments, paths and segment germs in the standard apartment. We also define projections of alcoves on points and on segment germs, the projections will play an important computational role.

By
segment
in
\mathbb{A}
, we mean an affine map
\mathfrak{s}:[t_{in},t_{f}]\mapsto\mathbb{A}
where
t_{in}<t_{f}
are real numbers.

A
path
in
\mathbb{A}
is a piece-wise affine map
[t_{in},t_{f}]\mapsto\mathbb{A}
, with
t_{in}<t_{f}\in\mathbb{R}
. That is to say, it is a continuous map
\mathfrak{c}:[t_{in},t_{f}]\mapsto\mathbb{A}
such that there exists a finite number of times
t_{0}=t_{in}<t_{1}<\dots<t_{n}=t_{f}
, and segments
(\mathfrak{s}_{k})_{k\in\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket}
such that, for each
k\in\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket
,
\dom(\mathfrak{s}_{k})=[t_{k-1},t_{k}]
(where
\dom
denotes the domain of a map) and
\mathfrak{c}(t)=\mathfrak{s}_{k}(t)
for all
t\in[t_{k-1},t_{k}]
. We identify two paths
\mathfrak{c}
,
\mathfrak{c}^{\prime}
if
\im(\mathfrak{c})=\im(\mathfrak{c}^{\prime})
and
\mathfrak{c}^{-1}\circ\mathfrak{c}^{\prime}
has positive derivative. Therefore we may always assume that the domain of a path (and in particular of a segment) is
[0,1]
.

Definition 2.3

(Segment germs)
.

For
t\in[0,1)
, (resp.
t\in[0,1)
, resp.
t\in(0,1)
) let
[t,t^{+})
(resp.
(t^{-},t]
, resp.
(t^{-},t^{+})
) be the filter of
[0,1]
with basis
\{[t,t+\varepsilon)\mid\varepsilon>0\}
(resp.
\{(t-\varepsilon,t]\mid\varepsilon>0\}
, resp.
\{(t-\varepsilon,t+\varepsilon)\mid\varepsilon>0\}
). Filters of this form and their images in
\mathbb{A}
by affine maps from
\mathbb{R}
to
\mathbb{A}
are called
segment germs
.

In particular, for any path
\mathfrak{c}:[0,1]\mapsto\mathbb{A}
, we denote by
\mathfrak{c}_{+}(t)
the segment germ
\mathfrak{c}_{+}(t):=\mathfrak{c}([t,t^{+}))
and by
\mathfrak{c}_{-}(t)
the segment germ
\mathfrak{c}_{-}(t):=\mathfrak{c}((t^{-},t])
. So
\mathfrak{c}_{+}(t)
is the filter of
\mathbb{A}
with basis
\{\mathfrak{c}([t,t+\varepsilon))\mid\varepsilon>0\}
. Note that, by definition of the derivative and since
\mathfrak{c}
is piece-wise affine,
\mathfrak{c}_{+}(t)=\mathfrak{c}(t)+[0,0^{+})\mathfrak{c}^{\prime}_{+}(t)
and
\mathfrak{c}_{-}(t)=\mathfrak{c}(t)+(0^{-},0]\mathfrak{c}^{\prime}_{-}(t)
where
\mathfrak{c}^{\prime}_{+}(t)
(resp.
\mathfrak{c}_{-}^{\prime}(t)
) denotes the right (resp. left) derivative of
\mathfrak{c}
at
t
. The derivatives
\mathfrak{c}^{\prime}_{+}(t)
and
\mathfrak{c}_{-}^{\prime}(t)
depend on the choice of parametrization, but the filters
\mathfrak{c}_{+}(t)
,
\mathfrak{c}_{-}(t)
do not.

Definition 2.4

(
\lambda
-paths)
.

Let
\lambda\in\mathbb{A}
. A
\lambda
-path is a piecewise affine map
\mathfrak{c}:[0,1]\rightarrow\mathbb{A}
such that for all
t\in(0,1)
, we have
\mathfrak{c}^{\prime}_{\pm}(t)\in W^{v}.\lambda
. In particular, the path
\mathfrak{s}_{\lambda}:t\mapsto-t\lambda
is a
-\lambda
-path which we call the
standard segment of type
\lambda
.

Projection on points and on segment germs

For
x,y\in\mathbb{A}
, we write
x\leq y
if
y-x\in\mathscr{T}
. Since
\mathscr{T}
is a convex cone, it is a preorder on
\mathbb{A}
, called the
Tits preorder
.

Let
x,y\in\mathbb{A}
be such that
x\leq y
(resp.
y\leq x
) and
a\in\mathcal{T}^{\pm}_{y}(\mathbb{A})
be an alcove based at
y
. Then the
projection of
a
at
x
, denoted
\mathrm{proj}_{x}(a)\in\mathcal{T}^{+}_{x}(\mathbb{A})
(resp.
\mathrm{proj}_{x}(a)\in\mathcal{T}^{-}_{x}(\mathbb{A})
), is the unique alcove based at
x
which contains
a
in its sector, it should be thought of as "the closest alcove based at
x
from
a
" (see
[
4
, §2.1]
for a proof of uniqueness).

Suppose that
\mathfrak{s}
is a path in
\mathbb{A}
and
t\in[0,1]
is such that
\mathfrak{s}(t)=:x
and
\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}_{-}(t)\in\mp\mathring{\mathscr{T}}
. Then the segment germ
\mathfrak{s}_{-}(t)=x+(0^{-},0]\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}_{-}(t)
is contained in finitely many alcoves of
\mathcal{T}^{\pm}_{x}(\mathbb{A})
, and the projection of
a
on
\mathfrak{s}_{-}(t)
is the unique alcove
\mathrm{proj}_{\mathfrak{s}_{-}(t)}(a)
containing
\mathfrak{s}_{-}(t)
which minimizes the distance to the point projection
\mathrm{proj}_{x}(a)
. Similarly, if
\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}_{+}(t)\in\mp\mathring{\mathscr{T}}
then
\mathfrak{s}_{+}(t)
is contained in a finite number of alcoves of
\mathcal{T}^{\mp}_{x}(\mathcal{I}_{\oplus})
, and we define
\mathrm{proj}_{\mathfrak{s}_{+}(t)}(a)
as the unique alcove containing
\mathfrak{s}_{+}(t)
which maximizes the codistance to
\mathrm{proj}_{x}(a)
. Again
\mathrm{proj}_{\mathfrak{s}_{\varepsilon}(t)}(a)
should be thought of as "the closest alcove from
a
containing
\mathfrak{s}_{\varepsilon}(t)
".

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\mathfrak{s}:[t_{0},t_{1}]\rightarrow\mathbb{A}
be a segment, where
t_{0},t_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
. Let
C
be an alcove based at
\mathfrak{s}(t_{1})
. Let
D
be a half-apartment containing
\mathfrak{s}(t_{0})
and
C
. Then
D
contains
\mathrm{proj}_{\mathfrak{s}_{+}(t_{0})}(C)
.

Proof.

Let
\underline{\beta}=(\beta,n)\in\Phi^{a}
be such that
D=\{x\in\mathbb{A}\mid\underline{\beta}(x)\geq 0\}
. Set
M=\underline{\beta}^{-1}(\{0\})
. If
M\supset[\mathfrak{s}(t_{0}),\mathfrak{s}(t_{1})]
, then
D\supset\mathrm{prism}_{\mathfrak{s}_{+}(t_{0})}(C)
, where
\mathrm{prism}
is defined in
[
4
, 2.1.2]
. Then by
[
4
, Lemma 2.1]
,
D
contains
\mathrm{proj}_{\mathfrak{s}_{+}(t_{0})}(C)
.

Assume now that
M\not\supset[\mathfrak{s}(t_{0}),\mathfrak{s}(t_{1})]
. If
\underline{\beta}(\mathfrak{s}(t_{0}))>0
, then
\underline{\beta}(C^{\prime})>0
for any alcove
C^{\prime}
based at
\mathfrak{s}(t_{0})
and in particular
\mathrm{proj}_{\mathfrak{s}_{+}(t_{0})}(C)\subset D
. Assume
\underline{\beta}(\mathfrak{s}(t_{0}))=0
. Then
\beta(\mathfrak{s}(t_{1})-\mathfrak{s}(t_{0}))>0
. Write
\mathrm{proj}_{\mathfrak{s}_{+}(t_{0})}(C)=a(\mathfrak{s}(t_{0}),\varepsilon v)
, with
\varepsilon\in\{-,+\}
and
v\in W^{v}
. We have
\beta(\varepsilon v.C^{v}_{f})\in\{\mathbb{R}_{>0},\mathbb{R}_{<0}\}
and as
\overline{\varepsilon v.C^{v}_{f}}\supset\mathfrak{s}(t_{1})-\mathfrak{s}(t_{0})
, we have
\beta(\varepsilon v.C^{v}_{f})=\mathbb{R}_{>0}
. Therefore
\underline{\beta}(\mathfrak{s}(t_{0})+\varepsilon v.C^{v}_{f})=\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, hence
\underline{\beta}(\mathrm{proj}_{\mathfrak{s}_{+}(t_{0})}(C))\subset\underline{\beta}(\mathfrak{s}(t_{0})+\overline{\varepsilon v.C^{v}_{f}})=\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
and the lemma follows.
∎

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
\lambda\in\mathring{\mathscr{T}}
, let
t_{1}<t_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
and let
\mathfrak{s}:[t_{1},t_{2}]\mapsto\mathbb{A}
be a segment such that
\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}_{+}(t_{1})=\pm\lambda
. Let
u\in W^{v}
and suppose that
a=a(\mathfrak{s}(t_{1}),\pm u)
dominates
\mathfrak{s}_{+}(t_{1})
. Then
\mathrm{proj}_{\mathfrak{s}_{-}(t_{2})}(a)=a(\mathfrak{s}(t_{2}),\mp uw_{\lambda,0})
, where
w_{\lambda,0}
is the maximal element of the finite standard parabolic subgroup
W_{\lambda^{++}}
.

Proof.

By definition of the projection,
\mathrm{proj}_{\mathfrak{s}_{-}(t_{2})}(a)
is the unique alcove
\widetilde{a}
dominating
\mathfrak{s}_{-}(t_{2})
and such that there is no wall containing
\mathfrak{s}_{-}(t_{2})
and separating
\widetilde{a}
from
a
.

With the notation of the lemma, let
\varepsilon\in\{+,-\}
be such that
\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}_{\eta}(t)=\varepsilon\lambda
for all
(t,\eta)\in[t_{1},t_{2}]^{\pm}
. Since
a=a(\mathfrak{s}(t_{1}),\varepsilon u)
dominates
\mathfrak{s}_{+}(t)
,
\lambda=u\lambda^{++}
and
W_{\lambda}=uW_{\lambda^{++}}u^{-1}
. Therefore alcoves dominating
\mathfrak{s}_{-}(t_{2})
are the alcoves in the set

\{a(\mathfrak{s}(t_{2}),-\varepsilon uw)\mid w\in W_{\lambda^{++}}\}=\{a(\mathfrak{s}(t_{2}),-\varepsilon\widetilde{w}u\mid\widetilde{w}\in W_{\lambda}\}.

Any wall
M
separating two such alcoves needs to contain the segment germ
\mathfrak{s}_{-}(t_{2})
and since
\mathfrak{s}
is affine on
[t_{1},t_{2}]
, it also contains
\mathfrak{s}_{+}(t_{1})
. Therefore the half-apartment delimited by
M
and containing
a=a(\mathfrak{s}(t_{1}),\varepsilon u)
also contains
a(\mathfrak{s}(t_{2}),\varepsilon u)
. Suppose that
w\in W_{\lambda^{++}}
is such that there exists a root
\beta\in\Phi_{+}
with
\langle\lambda^{++},\beta\rangle=0
and
s_{\beta}w>w
, and let
M
denote the wall of direction
u(\beta)
going through
\mathfrak{s}(t_{2})
. Since
\langle\lambda,u(\beta)\rangle=\langle\lambda^{++},\beta\rangle=0
,
M
contains
\mathfrak{s}_{-}(t_{2})
. Let
x
denote any point of the vectorial chamber
C^{v}_{w}
. Then
s_{\beta}w>w\iff\langle x,\beta\rangle>0\iff\langle u(x),u(\beta)\rangle>0
. Moreover, by definition
-C^{v}_{u}=-u.C^{v}_{f}=\{y\in V\mid\langle y,u(\alpha)\rangle<0\forall\alpha\in\Phi_{+}\}
. Therefore the vectorial wall of direction
u(\beta)
separates
-C^{v}_{u}
from
uC^{v}_{w}=C^{v}_{uw}
if and only if
s_{\beta}w>w
. By the definition of the projection, we deduce that, if we write
\mathrm{proj}_{\mathfrak{s}_{-}(t_{2})}(a)=a(\mathfrak{s}(t_{2}),-\varepsilon uw)
then
s_{\beta}w<w
for any root
\beta\in\Phi_{+}
such that
\langle\lambda^{++},\beta\rangle=0
. Hence
w
needs to be maximal in
W_{\lambda^{++}}
, that is to say
w=w_{\lambda,0}
.
∎

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
\lambda\in\mathring{\mathscr{T}}
, let
t_{in},t_{f}\in\mathbb{R}
and let
\mathfrak{s}:[t_{in},t_{f}]\mapsto\mathbb{A}
be a segment with direction
\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}_{+}(t_{in})=\pm\lambda
. Then for any
t_{in}\leq t_{1}<t_{2}<t_{3}\leq t_{f}
, and any two alcoves
a_{1},a_{2}
respectively dominating
\mathfrak{s}_{+}(t_{1})
and
\mathfrak{s}_{+}(t_{2})
we have
\mathrm{proj}_{\mathfrak{s}_{-}(t_{3})}(a_{1})=\mathrm{proj}_{\mathfrak{s}_{-}(t_{2})}(a_{2})
if and only if
a_{1}
and
a_{2}
have the same direction.

Proof.

Let
\varepsilon\in\{+,-\}
be such that
\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}_{+}(t_{in})=\varepsilon\lambda
. For
i\in\{1,2\}
, let
u_{i}\in W^{v}
be such that
a_{i}=a(\mathfrak{s}(t_{i}),\varepsilon u_{i})
. The alcoves
a_{1}
and
a_{2}
have same direction if and only if
u_{1}=u_{2}
. Moreover, by Lemma
2.6
,
\mathrm{proj}_{\mathfrak{s}_{-}(t_{3})}(a_{1})=\mathrm{proj}_{\mathfrak{s}_{-}(t_{3})}(a_{2})\iff u_{1}w_{\lambda,0}=u_{2}w_{\lambda,0}\iff u_{1}=u_{2}
which proves the result.
∎

Local fundamental chamber

For
x\in-\mathscr{T}
, let
C^{++}_{x}:=\mathrm{proj}_{x}(C_{0})
be the projection of the fundamental alcove
C_{0}
at
x
, the unique alcove which contains
C_{0}
in its sector. By
[
3
, §5.1]
, it is also the unique alcove which is on the positive side of every wall (including ghost walls) which go through
x
. We also define
C^{\infty}_{x}
as the opposite alcove of
C^{++}_{x}
based at
x
.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
x\in-\mathscr{T}
, write
x=-v^{x}x^{++}
with
v^{x}\in W^{v}
of minimal length and
x^{++}\in\overline{C^{v}_{f}}
. Then
C^{++}_{x}
is the alcove
a(x,+v^{x})
, and
C^{\infty}_{x}=a(x,-v^{x})
.

Proof.

Note that
0_{\mathbb{A}}=x+v^{x}x^{++}\in x+\overline{C^{v}_{v^{x}}}
, so it lies in the sector of
a(x,+v^{x})
. Assume by contradiction that there is an affine or ghost wall
M_{\beta[n]}
containing
x
and such that
a(x,+v^{x})
lies on the negative side of
M_{\beta[n]}
. Since
0_{\mathbb{A}}\in C_{0}\cap(x+\overline{C^{v}_{v^{x}}})
, the wall
M_{\beta[n]}
necessarily goes through
0_{\mathbb{A}}
, so
n=0
and
s_{\beta}x=x
. Then
s_{\beta[0]}.a(x,+v^{x})=a(x,+s_{\beta}v^{x})
lies on the positive side of the wall
M_{\beta[0]}
. By definition of the affine Bruhat order, this implies that
s_{\beta}.v^{x}<v^{x}
, which, since
s_{\beta}v^{x}x^{++}=-x
, contradicts minimality of
v^{x}
. By uniqueness of the projection we conclude that
C^{++}_{x}=a(x,+v^{x})
, and since
C^{\infty}_{x}
is its opposite,
C^{\infty}_{x}=a(x,-v^{x})
.
∎

The alcove
C^{++}_{x}
can be considered as the local fundamental chamber at
x
: by definition an alcove based at
x
and of direction
C^{v}
, is on the positive side of
M_{\beta[n]}
if and only if
C^{v}
is on the same side of the (affine or ghost) wall
M_{\beta}
as
C^{v}_{v^{x}}
(which is the direction of
C^{++}_{x}
).

3
C_{\infty}
-Hecke open paths

The goal of this section is to introduce
C_{\infty}
-Hecke open paths, which are generalized piece-wise affine segments in an affine space satisfying combinatorial properties related to the affine Bruhat order; they are slight variants of the
C_{\infty}
-Hecke paths introduced by Muthiah (
[
24
]
) and further studied by Bardy-Panse, the first-named author and Rousseau (
[
3
]
).

3.1
Definition and relation with the affine Bruhat order

For any
t,t^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}
such that
t<t^{\prime}
, we denote by
[t,t^{\prime}]^{\pm}
the set
[t,t^{\prime}]^{\pm}:=\big([t,t^{\prime})\times\{+\}\big)\sqcup\big((t,t^{\prime}]\times\{-\}\big)
.

Definition 3.1

(Open paths)
.

An
open path
is a tuple
\underline{\mathfrak{c}}:=(\mathfrak{c},D,\underline{\mathfrak{c}}(1))
where:

1.

\mathfrak{c}:[0,1]\rightarrow\mathbb{A}
is a
-\lambda
-path, for some spherical
\lambda\in\mathring{\mathscr{T}}
,

2.

D
is the data, for each
(t,\varepsilon)\in[0,1]^{\pm}
, of an alcove
D^{\varepsilon}_{t}
based at
\mathfrak{c}(t)
and dominating
\mathfrak{c}_{\varepsilon}(t)
,

3.

\underline{\mathfrak{c}}(1)
is an alcove based at the endpoint
\mathfrak{c}(1)
of
\mathfrak{c}
.

We respectively call
\mathfrak{c}
,
D
and
\underline{\mathfrak{c}}(1)
the
underlying path
, the
decoration
and the
end alcove
of
\underline{\mathfrak{c}}
.

Moreover, we say that it is
admissible
if, for any
t_{0}<t_{1}\in[0,1]
such that
\mathfrak{c}
is affine on
[t_{0},t_{1}]
, we have that, for all
t\in(t_{0},t_{1})
,

D^{-}_{t}=\mathrm{proj}_{\mathfrak{c}_{-}(t)}(D^{+}_{t_{0}})\,\text{and}\,D^{+}_{t}=\mathrm{proj}_{\mathfrak{c}_{+}(t)}(D^{-}_{t_{1}}).

(3.1)

This is equivalent to requiring that for all
(t,\varepsilon),(t^{\prime},\varepsilon^{\prime})\in[t_{0},t_{1}]^{\pm}
, we have

D_{t}^{\varepsilon}=\mathrm{proj}_{\mathfrak{c}_{\varepsilon}(t)}(D_{t^{\prime}}^{\varepsilon^{\prime}}).

(3.2)

Remark 3.2
.

Suppose that
\underline{\mathfrak{c}}
is an open path whose non-open component is a
-\lambda
-path for
\lambda
regular, that is to say
W_{\lambda}=1
. Then there is a unique alcove dominating
\mathfrak{c}_{\varepsilon}(t)
for
(t,\varepsilon)\in[0,1]^{\pm}
, so the decoration is uniquely determined from the data of the non-open component
\mathfrak{c}
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\lambda\in\mathring{\mathscr{T}}
, let
w_{\lambda,0}
denote the maximal element of the finite standard parabolic subgroup
W_{\lambda^{++}}
. Let
\underline{\mathfrak{c}}=(\mathfrak{c},D,\underline{\mathfrak{c}}(1))
be an open path such that
\mathfrak{c}
is a
-\lambda
-path. Let
0\leq t_{1}<t_{2}<t_{3}\leq 1
and suppose that the admissibility condition is satisfied on
[t_{1},t_{2}]
and
[t_{2},t_{3}]
. Then the admissibility condition is satisfied on
[t_{1},t_{3}]
if and only if:

\mathfrak{c}^{\prime}_{+}(t_{2})\neq\mathfrak{c}^{\prime}_{-}(t_{2})\text{ or }d^{\ast}(D^{-}_{t_{2}},D^{+}_{t_{2}})=w_{\lambda,0}.

(3.3)

Proof.

Let
\underline{\mathfrak{c}}
be an open path as in the lemma.

If
\mathfrak{c}^{\prime}_{+}(t_{2})\neq\mathfrak{c}^{\prime}_{-}(t_{2})
then, for any
\widetilde{t}_{1}<\widetilde{t}_{2}\in[t_{1},t_{3}]
, if
\mathfrak{c}
is affine on
[\widetilde{t}_{1},\widetilde{t}_{2}]
then either
\widetilde{t}_{1}\geq t_{2}
, either
\widetilde{t}_{2}\leq t_{2}
, so it is clear that the admissibility condition is satisfied on
[t_{1},t_{3}]
. Suppose that
\mathfrak{c}^{\prime}_{+}(t_{2})=\mathfrak{c}^{\prime}_{-}(t_{2})
, then let
[\widetilde{t}_{1},\widetilde{t}_{2}]\subset[t_{1},t_{3}]
be the maximal interval containing
t_{2}
on which
\mathfrak{c}
is affine. Since the admissibility condition is satisfied on
[t_{1},t_{2}]
and
[t_{2},t_{3}]
it is also satisfied on
[t_{1},\widetilde{t}_{1}]
and
[\widetilde{t}_{2},t_{3}]
. Therefore as
\mathfrak{c}^{\prime}
varies on
\widetilde{t}_{1}
and on
\widetilde{t}_{2}
it is satisfied on
[t_{1},t_{3}]
if and only if it is satisfied on
[\widetilde{t}_{1},\widetilde{t}_{2}]
. By Lemma
2.7
, the admissibility condition for
[\widetilde{t}_{1},t_{2}]
and
[t_{2},\widetilde{t}_{2}]
implies that
D^{+}
(resp.
D^{-}
) has constant direction on
[\widetilde{t}_{1},t_{2})
and on
[t_{2},\widetilde{t}_{2})
(resp. on
(\widetilde{t}_{1},t_{2}]
and on
(t_{2},\widetilde{t}_{2}]
), therefore the decoration is admissible on
[\widetilde{t}_{1},\widetilde{t}_{2}]
if and only if
D^{+}_{t_{2}}
and
D^{+}_{\widetilde{t}_{1}}
have same direction (if and only if
D^{-}_{t_{2}}=\mathrm{proj}_{\mathfrak{c}_{-}(t_{2})}(D^{+}_{\widetilde{t}_{1}})
and
D^{-}_{\widetilde{t}_{2}}=\mathrm{proj}_{\mathfrak{c}_{-}(\widetilde{t}_{2})}(D^{+}_{t_{2}})
have same direction). Let
u\in W^{v}
be such that
D^{+}_{\widetilde{t}_{1}}=a(\mathfrak{c}(\widetilde{t}_{1}),-u)
then
D^{+}_{t_{2}}
and
D^{+}_{\widetilde{t}_{1}}
have same direction if and only if
D^{+}_{t_{2}}=a(\mathfrak{c}(t_{2}),-u)
. Moreover by Lemma
2.6

D^{-}_{t_{2}}=a(\mathfrak{c}(t_{2}),uw_{\lambda,0})
, we deduce that
D^{+}_{t_{2}}
and
D^{+}_{\widetilde{t}_{1}}
have same direction if and only if
d^{\ast}(D^{-}_{t_{2}},D^{+}_{t_{2}})=w_{\lambda,0}
, which concludes the proof.

∎

Remark 3.4
.

Let
\underline{\mathfrak{c}}=(\mathfrak{c},D,\underline{\mathfrak{c}}(1))
be an admissible open path, let
\lambda
be such that
\mathfrak{c}
is a
-\lambda
-path and recall that
w_{\lambda,0}
denotes the maximal element of
W_{\lambda^{++}}
. Set
E=\{t\in(0,1)\mid\mathfrak{c}^{\prime}_{+}(t)\neq\mathfrak{c}^{\prime}_{-}(t)\}
. Write
E=\{t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}\}
, with
0<t_{1}<\ldots<t_{n}<t_{n+1}=1
. From Lemma
3.3
, we deduce that the decoration
D
is completely determined by the choice, for each
i\in\llbracket 1,n+1\rrbracket
of a vectorial chamber
C^{v}_{i}
dominating
\mathfrak{c}^{\prime}_{-}(t_{i})
, as follows:

For each
i\in\llbracket 1,n+1\rrbracket
let
u_{i}\in W^{v}
be the element such that
C^{v}_{i}=u_{i}.C^{v}_{f}
. The decoration
D
associated to
(C_{1}^{v},\ldots,C_{n+1}^{v})
is then given by
D_{t}^{-}=a(\mathfrak{c}(t),u_{i})=germ_{\mathfrak{c}(t)}(\mathfrak{c}(t)+C^{v}_{i})
(resp.
D_{t}^{+}=a(\mathfrak{c}(t),-u_{i}w_{\lambda,0})
) for
t\in(t_{i-1},t_{i}]
(resp.
t\in[t_{i-1},t_{i})
) and
i\in\llbracket 1,n+1\rrbracket
.

In particular, if the non-open component
\mathfrak{c}
is a segment then it suffices to choose
D^{-}_{1}
, and there is a canonical choice given by the end alcove
\underline{\mathfrak{c}}(1)
, which motivates the following definition.

Definition 3.5

(Open segments)
.

Let
\mathbf{x}=\varpi^{\lambda}w\in W^{+}_{sph}
be any spherical element. The
open segment

\underline{\mathfrak{s}}_{\mathbf{x}}=(\mathfrak{s}_{\lambda},D_{\mathbf{x}},C_{\mathbf{x}})
is the unique admissible open path with underlying path
\mathfrak{s}_{\lambda}:t\mapsto-t\lambda
, with end alcove
\underline{\mathfrak{s}}_{\mathbf{x}}(1)=C_{\mathbf{x}}
and such that
D^{-}_{\mathbf{x},1}=\mathrm{proj}_{\mathfrak{s}_{\lambda,-}(1)}(C_{\mathbf{x}})
.

Explicitly, the decoration
D_{\mathbf{x}}
is given by
D^{\varepsilon}_{\mathbf{x},t}=\mathrm{proj}_{\mathfrak{s}_{\lambda,\varepsilon}(t)}(C_{\mathbf{x}})
for any
(t,\varepsilon)\in[0,1]^{\pm}
.

An open segment is an element of the form
\underline{\mathfrak{s}}_{\mathbf{x}}
, for some
\mathbf{x}\in W^{+}_{sph}
.

Open paths can be folded along affine walls, through the following process.
C_{\infty}
-Hecke open paths are then obtained from open segments through successive foldings satisfying a positivity condition (see
3.8
).

Definition 3.6

(Folding of an open path)
.

Let
\underline{\mathfrak{c}}=(\mathfrak{c},D,\underline{\mathfrak{c}}(1))
be an open path,
t_{0}\in[0,1]
be a time and
\beta[n]\in\Phi_{+}^{a}
be such that
\mathfrak{c}(t_{0})\in M_{\beta[n]}
. The
folding
of
\underline{\mathfrak{c}}
along
M_{\beta[n]}
is the open path
\varphi_{t_{0},\beta[n]}(\underline{\mathfrak{c}})=(\varphi_{t_{0},\beta[n]}(\mathfrak{c}),\varphi_{t_{0},\beta[n]}(D),\varphi_{t_{0},\beta[n]}(\underline{\mathfrak{c}})(1))
defined by:

1.

\varphi_{t_{0},\beta[n]}(\mathfrak{c})(t)=\begin{cases}\mathfrak{c}(t)\ &\text{if}\ t<t_{0}\\
s_{\beta[n]}\mathfrak{c}(t)\ &\text{if}\ t\geq t_{0}\end{cases}
,

2.

\varphi_{t_{0},\beta[n]}(D)^{\varepsilon}_{t}=\begin{cases}D^{\varepsilon}_{t}\ &\text{if}\ t<t_{0}\ \text{or}\ (t,\varepsilon)=(t_{0},-)\\
s_{\beta[n]}D^{\varepsilon}_{t}\ &\text{if}\ t>t_{0}\ \text{or}\ (t,\varepsilon)=(t_{0},+)\par\end{cases}
,

3.

\varphi_{t_{0},\beta[n]}(\underline{\mathfrak{c}})(1)=s_{\beta[n]}\underline{\mathfrak{c}}(1)
.

Note in particular that a folding at time
t_{0}=1
only affects the end alcove. We may also fold decorated paths, in a similar fashion.

For
t_{0}<1
(resp.
t_{0}=1
), we say that the folding is
positive
(or
C_{\infty}
-centrifuged) if
D^{+}_{t_{0}}
(resp.
\underline{\mathfrak{c}}(1)
) is on the negative side of the wall
M_{\beta[n]}
. That is to say, if it is on the side not containing
C_{0}
or equivalently if it is on the side containing
C^{\infty}_{\mathfrak{c}(t_{0})}
.

Remark 3.7
.

Note that the folding of an admissible open path need not be admissible (if the folding is done at a time
t_{0}
such that
\mathfrak{c}_{+}(t_{0})\subset M_{\beta[n]}
).

We may now define
C_{\infty}
-Hecke open paths, which are particular open paths obtained trough successive positive foldings.

Definition 3.8

(
C_{\infty}
-Hecke open paths)
.

Let
\mathbf{x}=\varpi^{\lambda}w\in W^{+}
be any spherical element, a
C_{\infty}
-Hecke open path of type
\mathbf{x}
is an admissible open path
\underline{\mathfrak{c}}
for which there exists a sequence

4
Lifts of paths in the masure and application to Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomials

The motivation for the introduction of
C_{\infty}
-Hecke open paths is that they appear as images by certain retractions of open segments in a masure, an analog of the Bruhat-Tits building for Kac-Moody groups. We introduce Kac-Moody groups, masures and twin masures in the first subsection. Each
C_{\infty}
-Hecke open path can thus be lifted, in any masure built upon
\mathbb{A}
, to numerous open segments, and we are able to compute these lifts, it is the content of subsection
4.4
. From this computation and finiteness of the number of
C_{\infty}
-Hecke open paths, we can define Kazhdan-Lusztig
R
-polynomials on
W^{+}
, which is the content of subsection
4.5
. Upon certain masure-theoretic conjectures, this gives an expression for the cardinal of the equivalent of Formula (
1.1
) for Kac-Moody groups.

4.1
Kac-Moody groups, masures and twin masures

4.1.1

Let
\mathbb{G}=\mathbb{G}_{\mathcal{D}}

]

A first step is to define an action

For

4.1.2
Masures associated with
\mathbb{G}(\mathds{k}[\varpi,\varpi^{-1}])
,
\mathbb{G}(\mathds{k}(\varpi))
and
\mathbb{G}\left(\mathds{k}(\!(\varpi^{\varepsilon})\!)\right)

For a given Kac-Moody group functor
\mathbb{G}
and a given finite field
\mathds{k}
, the loop group of
\mathbb{G}
over
\mathds{k}
admits several incarnations in the litterature, which give rise to different masures. In this paragraph we introduce some of these choices and compare the different masures associated.

Let
\mathds{k}
be a finite field and
\varpi
be an indeterminate. Let
\varepsilon\in\{\ominus,\oplus\}
. Let
\omega_{\varepsilon}
be the usual valuation on
\mathds{k}(\!(\varpi^{\varepsilon})\!)
:
\omega_{\oplus}(\sum_{k=-\infty}^{+\infty}x_{k}\varpi^{\varepsilon k})=k_{0}
if
\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}x_{k}\varpi^{\varepsilon k}\in\mathds{k}(\!(\varpi^{\varepsilon})\!)\setminus\{0\}
is such that
x_{k_{0}}\neq 0
and
x_{k}=0
for
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{<k_{0}}
, where
\varpi^{\oplus}=\varpi
and
\varpi^{\ominus}=\varpi^{-1}
. Let
\mathcal{K}=\mathds{k}(\varpi)
. Let
\widehat{\mathcal{I}}_{\varepsilon}=\mathcal{I}(\mathbb{G},\mathds{k}(\!(\varpi^{\varepsilon})\!),\omega_{\varepsilon})
and
\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}=\mathcal{I}(\mathbb{G},\mathcal{K},\omega_{\varepsilon})
.

Let
\mathcal{O}=k[\varpi,\varpi^{-1}]
, one defines
G_{twin}=\langle x_{\alpha}(\mathcal{O}),\mathbb{T}(\mathcal{O}),\alpha\in\Phi\rangle\subset G
, which is the group
\mathbb{G}^{min}_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{O})
defined by Marquis in
[
20
, Definition 8.126]
.

We denote by
\mathbb{A}_{\varepsilon}
the standard apartment of
\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}
. As affine spaces equipped with a hyperplane arrangement,
\mathbb{A}_{\oplus}
and
\mathbb{A}_{\ominus}
are copies of
(\mathbb{A},\mathcal{M}^{a})
, where
\mathbb{A}=Y\otimes\mathbb{R}
. However the actions of
T
on
\mathbb{A}_{\oplus}
and
\mathbb{A}_{\ominus}
differ, since they take into account
\omega_{\oplus}
and
\omega_{\ominus}
, and for the same reason
x_{\alpha}(u).\mathbb{A}_{\oplus}\cap\mathbb{A}_{\oplus}
is in general different from
x_{\alpha}(u).\mathbb{A}_{\ominus}\cap\mathbb{A}_{\ominus}
, for
u\in\mathcal{K}
and
\alpha\in\Phi
.

Let
\varepsilon\in\{\ominus,\oplus\}
. The masure
\mathcal{I}^{twin}_{\varepsilon}
of
(G_{twin},\omega_{\varepsilon})
is
G_{twin}.\mathbb{A}_{\varepsilon}
equipped with the set of apartments
\{g.\mathbb{A}_{\varepsilon}\mid g\in G_{twin}\}
.
As
(\mathcal{O},\omega_{\varepsilon})
is dense in
\mathds{k}(\!(\varpi^{\varepsilon})\!)
, the masures
\widehat{\mathcal{I}}_{\varepsilon}
,
\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}
and
\mathcal{I}^{twin}_{\varepsilon}
are the same when we regard them as sets, by
[
3
, Corollary 3.7]
(in particular,
\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}=G_{twin}.\mathbb{A}_{\varepsilon}
). More precisely, if we regard
\mathbb{A}_{\varepsilon}
as a subset of
\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}
, then by
[
3
, Theorem 3.6]
, if
A_{\varepsilon}
is an apartment of
\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}
, and
P
is a bounded subset of
A_{\varepsilon}
, there exists
g_{twin}\in G_{twin}
such that
P\subset g_{twin}.\mathbb{A}_{\varepsilon}
, and the same holds with
\mathbb{G}(\mathds{k}(\!(\varpi^{\varepsilon})\!))
instead of
\mathbb{G}(\mathcal{K})
if we regard
\mathbb{A}_{\varepsilon}\subset\widehat{\mathcal{I}}_{\varepsilon}
. Therefore when we are interested in local questions on
\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}
(for example when we consider alcoves or tangent buildings), we can work with
\mathbb{G}(\mathds{k}(\!(\varpi^{\varepsilon})\!))
,
G
or
G_{twin}
indifferently. However, the set of apartments of
\widehat{\mathcal{I}}_{\varepsilon}
strictly contains the set of apartments of
\widehat{\mathcal{I}}_{\varepsilon}
, which strictly contains the set of apartments of
\mathcal{I}^{twin}_{\varepsilon}
.

4.1.3
Twin masure associated with Kac-Moody groups over Laurent polynomials

In
[
3
]
, the authors study the interplay between
\mathcal{I}_{\oplus}
and
\mathcal{I}_{\ominus}
, in order to obtain an analogue of the twinnings of buildings, adapted to the frameworks of masures. We recall some of the key properties obtained there.

A
twin apartment
is a set of form
g.(\mathbb{A}_{\oplus}\sqcup\mathbb{A}_{\ominus})
, for some
g\in G_{twin}
. Let
\varepsilon\in\{\ominus,\oplus\}
. An apartment of the form
g.\mathbb{A}_{\varepsilon}
, for some
g\in G_{twin}
is called
twinnable
. Then the map from the set of twinnable apartments of
\mathcal{I}_{\oplus}
to the set of twinnable apartments of
\mathcal{I}_{\ominus}
defined by
A_{\oplus}=g.\mathbb{A}_{\oplus}\mapsto A_{\ominus}=g.\mathbb{A}_{\ominus}
, for
g\in G_{twin}
is a well-defined bijection by
[
3
, 4.1.10]
). If
A_{\oplus}
is a twinnable apartment of
\mathcal{I}_{\oplus}
, the corresponding apartment of
\mathbb{A}_{\ominus}
is called its
twin
.

If
P
is a bounded subset of an apartment of
\mathcal{I}_{\oplus}
, then there exists a twinnable apartment containing
P
, by
[
3
, Theorem 3.6]
. By
[
3
, Proposition 3.8 and Theorem 4.7]
, if
\varepsilon\in\{\ominus,\oplus\}
and if
A_{\varepsilon}
and
B_{\varepsilon}
are two twinnable apartments, then there exists
g\in G_{twin}
fixing
(A_{\ominus}\cap B_{\ominus})\cup(A_{\oplus}\cap B_{\oplus})
.

A priori, the group
\mathbb{G}\left(\mathds{k}(\!(\varpi)\!)\right)
has no natural action on
\mathcal{I}_{\ominus}
. The groups
G=\mathbb{G}(\mathcal{K})
and
G_{twin}
both act on
\mathcal{I}_{\oplus}
and
\mathcal{I}_{\ominus}
. However, the group
G_{twin}
is more adapted in order to define the retraction
\rho_{C_{\infty}}
.

From now on, we write
\mathbb{A}
instead of
\mathbb{A}_{\oplus}
. We add a subscript
\ominus
to the objects related to
\mathcal{I}_{\ominus}
.

Iwahori subgroups

Let
C_{0}=\mathrm{germ}_{0}(C^{v}_{f})\subset\mathbb{A}

(resp.
C_{\infty}=\mathrm{germ}_{0_{\ominus}}(-C^{v}_{f,\ominus})\subset\mathbb{A}_{\ominus}
) be the
fundamental alcove
of
\mathbb{A}
(resp.
\mathbb{A}_{\ominus}
).

We denote by
I
(resp.
I_{0}
,
I_{\infty}
) the fixator of
C_{0}
(resp.
C_{0}
,
C_{\infty}
) in
G
(resp. in
G_{twin}
, in
G_{twin}
). Descriptions of
I
are given in
[
30
, 5.7 and Définition 5.3]
and
[
3
, 2.4.1 1)]
. The map
g\mapsto g.C_{0}
induces a bijection between
G/I
and the set of positive alcoves of
\mathcal{I}_{\oplus}
based at an element of
g.0_{\mathbb{A}}
. By
4.1.2
, this set is also the set of positive alcoves based at an element of
G_{twin}.0_{\mathbb{A}}
and we have a natural bijection between

and

.

4.1.4
Prerequisites on masures

Tits preorder on
\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}

Let
\varepsilon\in\{\ominus,\oplus\}
. For
x,y\in\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}
, we write
x\leq_{\varepsilon}y
if there exists
g\in G
such that
g.x,g.y\in\mathbb{A}_{\varepsilon}
and
g.y-g.x\in\mathscr{T}_{\varepsilon}
. This does not depend on the choice of
g
. This defines a
G
-invariant preorder on
\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}
by
[
29
, Théorème 5.9]
. When
\mathbb{G}
is reductive this preorder is trivial since
\mathscr{T}_{\varepsilon}=\mathbb{A}_{\varepsilon}
. However in the non-reductive case,
\leq_{\varepsilon}
is a crucial tool which is a partial remedy to the existence of pairs of points which are not contained in a common apartment.

Retractions centered at alcoves

Let
\mathcal{E}
(resp.
\mathcal{E}_{C_{\infty},\oplus}
) be the set of points of
\mathcal{I}_{\oplus}
such that there exists an apartment
A_{x}
(resp. a twinnable apartment
A_{x,\oplus}
) of
\mathcal{I}_{\oplus}
containing
C_{0}
(resp. whose twin
A_{x,\ominus}
contains
C_{\infty}
). One defines a
retraction
\rho_{C_{0}}
on

\mathbb{A}
centered at
C_{0}
and a
retraction
\rho_{C_{\infty}}
onto
\mathbb{A}
centered at
C_{\infty}
as follows. Let
x\in\mathcal{E}
(resp.
x\in\mathcal{E}_{C_{\infty},\oplus}
).
Then by (MA II) (resp. by
[
3
, Theorem 4.7]
), there exists
g\in G
(resp.
g\in G_{twin}
) such that
g.A_{x}=\mathbb{A}
(resp.
g.(A_{x,\oplus}\sqcup A_{x,\ominus})=\mathbb{A}\sqcup\mathbb{A}_{\ominus}
) and
g
fixes
A_{x}\cap\mathbb{A}
(resp.
(\mathbb{A}\cap A_{x,\oplus})\sqcup(\mathbb{A}_{\ominus}\cap A_{x,\ominus})
(with the same notation as above). One then sets
\rho_{C_{0}}(x)=g.x
(resp.
\rho_{C_{\infty}}(x)=g.x
). This is well-defined, independently of the choices made and thus it defines an
I
-invariant retraction
\rho_{C_{0}}:\mathcal{E}\rightarrow\mathbb{A}
and an
I_{\infty}
-invariant retraction
\rho_{C_{\infty}}:\mathcal{E}_{C_{\infty},\oplus}\rightarrow\mathbb{A}
.
When
\mathbb{G}
is reductive,
\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{E}_{C_{\infty},\oplus}=\mathbb{A}
, however in general, these sets can be proper subsets of
\mathbb{A}
(by
[
30
, Exemple 4.12.3 c)]
and
[
3
, 6.5]
) and determining these sets is a difficult task. By
[
29
, Proposition 5.4]
or
[
14
, Proposition 5.17]
,
\mathcal{E}\supset\mathcal{I}_{\oplus,\geq 0}\cup\mathcal{I}_{\oplus,\leq 0}
, where
\mathcal{I}_{\oplus,R0}=\{x\in\mathcal{I}_{\oplus}\mid xR0\}
for
R\in\{\leq_{\oplus},\geq_{\oplus}\}
. However, very little is known on
\mathcal{E}_{C_{\infty},\oplus}
. It is conjectured in
[
3
, 4.4.1]
that
\mathcal{E}_{C_{\infty},\oplus}\supset\mathcal{I}_{\oplus,\geq 0}\cup\mathcal{I}_{\oplus,\leq 0}
, but no case of the conjecture is known. In a recent preprint, Patnaik
[
25
]
proves a local Birkhoff decomposition of a completion of
G
when
\mathbb{G}
is affine. This decomposition seems to be related to this conjecture in the affine case, but we do not know to what extent, since the groups and subgroups considered are slightly different.

Note that there exist elements of
\mathbb{T}(\mathds{k}(\varpi))
which fix
\mathbb{A}_{\ominus}
but translate
\mathbb{A}_{\oplus}
non-trivially. Therefore
\rho_{C_{\infty}}
is not
\mathrm{Fix}_{G}(C_{\infty})
-invariant and this is the main reason why we consider
G_{twin}
.

Tangent buildings

For any
x\in\mathcal{I}_{\oplus}
and
\varepsilon\in\{+,-\}
, let
\mathcal{T}^{\varepsilon}_{x}(\mathcal{I}_{\oplus})
denote the set of alcoves of sign
\varepsilon
based at
x
. Explicitly, an element of
\mathcal{T}^{\varepsilon}_{x}(\mathcal{I}_{\oplus})
is the image
g.a
of an alcove
a\in\mathcal{T}^{\varepsilon}_{y}(\mathbb{A})
for some
y\in\mathbb{A}
and
g\in G
such that
g.y=x
.

We extend the
W^{v}
-distance
d^{\varepsilon}
(resp. the codistance
d^{\ast}
) defined in (
2.5
) (resp. (
2.6
)) to a
W^{v}
-valued distance
d^{\varepsilon}:\mathcal{T}_{x}^{\varepsilon}(\mathcal{I}_{\oplus})\times\mathcal{T}_{x}^{\varepsilon}(\mathcal{I}_{\oplus})\rightarrow W^{v}
(resp. a codistance
d^{\ast}:\mathcal{T}^{+}_{x}(\mathcal{I}_{\oplus})\times\mathcal{T}^{-}_{x}(\mathcal{I}_{\oplus})\cup\mathcal{T}^{-}_{x}(\mathcal{I}_{\oplus})\times\mathcal{T}^{+}_{x}(\mathcal{I}_{\oplus})\rightarrow W^{v}
). Let
a_{1},a_{2}\in\mathcal{T}^{\varepsilon}_{x}(\mathcal{I}_{\oplus})
(resp.
(a_{1},a_{2})\in\mathcal{T}^{+}_{x}(\mathcal{I}_{\oplus})\times\mathcal{T}^{-}_{x}(\mathcal{I}_{\oplus})
). By
[
14
, Proposition 5.17]
there exists an apartment containing both of them, so by (MA I) a
g\in G
such that
g.a_{1},g.a_{2}
lie in
\mathcal{T}^{\varepsilon}_{y}(\mathbb{A})
, for some
g\in G
such that
g.x=y\in\mathbb{A}
. One sets
d^{\varepsilon}(a_{1},a_{2})=d^{\varepsilon}(g.a_{1},g.a_{2})
(resp.
d^{\ast}(a_{1},a_{2})=d^{\ast}(g.a_{1},g.a_{2})=d^{\ast}(a_{2},a_{1})^{-1}
). By (MA II) these functions are well-defined (do not depend on the choices we made).

We say that
a_{-},a_{+}
are
opposite
if
d^{\ast}(a_{-},a_{+})=1_{W^{v}}
. Then for any
x
and
\varepsilon\in\{+,-\}
,
(\mathcal{T}^{\varepsilon}_{x}(\mathcal{I}_{\oplus}),d^{\varepsilon})
defines a building of type
(W^{v},S)
(where
S
is the set of simple reflections of
W^{v}
:
S=\{s_{i}\mid i\in I\}
) , with system of apartments induced by the one of
\mathcal{I}_{\oplus}
. The codistance
d^{\ast}
defines a twinning, we say that
(\mathcal{T}^{\pm}_{x}(\mathcal{I}_{\oplus}),d^{\pm},d^{\ast})
is the
local tangent building
at
x
, it is thus a twin building of type
(W^{v},S)
and of model twin apartment
\mathcal{T}^{\pm}_{x}(\mathbb{A})
(see
[
1
, §5.1, §5.8]
for the axioms of buildings and twin buildings, and
[
10
, Propositions 4.7, 4.8]
for proofs). Any element
g\in G
induces an isomorphism of twin buildings:
\mathcal{T}^{\pm}_{x}(\mathcal{I}_{\oplus})\cong\mathcal{T}^{\pm}_{g.x}(\mathcal{I}_{\oplus})
.

Remark 4.2
.

Note that, in this paper,
\mathcal{T}^{\pm}_{x}(\mathcal{I}_{\oplus})
is a set of alcoves and not a set of segment germs (unlike for instance in
[
10
, §4.5]
), because we consider the tangent buildings as abstract buildings, of which the set of segment germ is a geometric realization. In
[
10
, §4.5]
, there are two distinct tangent building structures, the one exposed and used here corresponds to the unrestricted structure.

Thickness of the tangent buildings

Let
(\mathcal{T},d)
be an abstract building of type
(W,S)
, and let
s\in S
. A
panel
of type
s
is a subset of the form
p(C,s):=\{C^{\prime}\in\mathcal{T}\mid d(C,C^{\prime})\in\{1,s\}\}
for some alcove
C
. We say that
C^{\prime}
dominates
p(C,s)
if
C^{\prime}\in p(C,s)
; each alcove dominates exactly one panel of each type. Note that, for
C,C^{\prime}
two distinct alcoves
p(C,s)=p(C^{\prime},s)
if and only if
d(C,C^{\prime})=s
and a panel is entirely defined by two of its dominating alcoves. The
thickness
of
p(C,s)
is
|p(C,s)|-1
, a panel is called
thin
if its thickness is equal to
1
, and
thick
otherwise. Note that an isomorphism of buildings sends a given panel to another panel of same type and same thickness.

In general, the local tangent buildings in a masure admit thin panels. More precisely, suppose that
x\in\mathbb{A}
and
a=a(x,\varepsilon w)\in\mathcal{T}^{\varepsilon}_{x}(\mathbb{A})
is an alcove based at
x
(with
\varepsilon\in\{+,-\},w\in W^{v}
). Then, for
s_{i}\in S
, the panel
p(a,s_{i})
is identified with
a(x,\varepsilon w)\cap a(x,\varepsilon ws_{i})
: an alcove
\widetilde{a}\in T_{x}(\mathcal{I}_{\oplus})
dominates
p(a,s_{i})
if and only if it contains the filter
a(x,\varepsilon w)\cap a(x,\varepsilon ws_{i})
. Let
\beta=w(\alpha_{i})
the root associated to
(w,s_{i})
, then the
support
of the panel
p(a(x,\varepsilon w),s_{i})
is the (affine or ghost) wall
M_{\beta[-\langle x,\beta\rangle]}=\{y\in\mathbb{A}\mid\langle y,\beta\rangle=\langle x,\beta\rangle\}
, it is the only wall containing the filter-theoretic intersection
a(x,\varepsilon w)\cap a(x,\varepsilon ws_{i})
. Note that the support does not depend of the sign
\varepsilon
. The panel is thick if and only if its support is an affine wall (an element of
\mathcal{M}^{a}
), if and only if
\langle x,\beta\rangle\in\mathbb{Z}
. Moreover, for split Kac-Moody groups, the thickness of thick panels is constant equal to
|\mathds{k}|
.

This extends to any
x\in\mathcal{I}_{\oplus}
and any panel
p
of
\mathcal{T}_{x}^{\pm}(\mathcal{I}_{\oplus})
, as there always exists an element
g
of
G
which sends
x
to an element of
\mathbb{A}
, and
p
to a panel lying in
\mathcal{T}_{gx}(\mathbb{A})
.

We deduce that, for
x\in\mathbb{A}
,
\mathcal{T}_{x}^{\pm}(\mathcal{I}_{\oplus})
is thick (in the sense that all its panels are thick) if and only if
\langle x,\alpha_{i}\rangle\in\mathbb{Z}
for all simple root
\alpha_{i}
, in particular this is verified if
x\in Y
. Since elements of
G
induce isomorphism of tangent buildings, this extends to all
x\in G.Y=G.0_{\mathbb{A}}\subset\mathcal{I}_{\oplus}
(which are called the "special vertices" of
\mathcal{I}_{\oplus}
). Moreover for any special point
x
, the tangent twin building
\mathcal{T}^{\pm}_{x}(\mathcal{I}_{\oplus})
is
|\mathds{k}|
-homogeneous: all its panels have the same thickness
|\mathds{k}|
. In general,
\mathcal{T}^{\pm}_{x}(\mathcal{I}_{\oplus})
is
(|\mathds{k}|,1)
-semi-homogeneous and the set of thick panels depends on the
G
-orbit of
x
. For
x\in\mathcal{I}_{\oplus}
we denote by
\mathcal{P}_{x}
the set of thick panels of the tangent building
\mathcal{T}^{\pm}_{x}(\mathcal{I}_{\oplus})
in
\mathbb{A}
.

Remark 4.3
.

If we forget about thin panels, we obtain the restricted building structure (see
[
10
, §4.5]
)
{}^{r}\mathcal{T}^{\pm}_{x}(\mathcal{I}_{\oplus})
which is a quotient of

In order to work in local tangent buildings, we introduce galleries and building-theoretic retractions, with the formalism of abstract twin buildings.

Definition 4.4

(Galleries and retractions in a building)
.

Let
W
be a Coxeter group with set of simple reflections
S
, and let
(\mathcal{T}^{\pm},d^{\pm},d^{\ast})
be a twin building of type
(W,S)
(in the sense of
[
1
, 5.8]
).

—

Let
w\in W
and let
\mathbf{i}_{w}=(s_{1},\dots,s_{n})\in S^{n}
be a reduced
S
-expression of
w
. A sequence of chambers
(C_{0},\dots,C_{n})\in(\mathcal{T}^{+})^{n}
forms a gallery of type
\mathbf{i}_{w}
if
C_{i}
lies in the panel
p(C_{i-1},s_{i})\}
for all
i\in\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket
(that is to say;
d(C_{i-1},C_{i})\in\{1_{W},s_{i}\}
. We say that it is a
minimal gallery
if there is no shorter gallery from
C_{0}
to
C_{n}
, in particular this implies that
d(C_{i-1},C_{i})\neq 1_{W}
for all
i
. Note that, in this case, we have
d(C_{0},C_{n})=s_{1}\dots s_{n}=w
.
If
i
is such that
C_{i-1}=C_{i}
we say that the gallery is folded along the panel
p(C_{i},s_{i})
.

—

Let
(A^{+},A^{-})
be a pair of twin apartments. Let
\mathbf{g}=(C_{0},..,C_{n})
be a gallery in
A^{+}
of type
\mathbf{i}_{w}=(s_{1},..,s_{n})
and let
C_{-}
be a chamber lying in
A^{-}
. We say that
\mathbf{g}
is
C_{-}
-centrifugally folded
if, for any

such that
C_{i-1}=C_{i}
, we have that
d^{\ast}(C_{-},C_{i})<d^{\ast}(C_{-},C_{i})s_{i}
. Geometrically, this means that the wall supporting
p(C_{i},s_{i})
(along which the gallery is folded) separates
C_{-}
from
C_{i-1}
. More generally, we say that
\mathbf{g}
crosses
M
, or folds along
M
away from
C_{-}\in A^{\pm}
(resp. towards
C_{-}
) if and only if, for every panel
p
supported by
M
and

such that
C_{i-1},C_{i}\in p
,
C_{i-1}
and
C_{-}
lie on the same side of
M
(resp. on opposite sides of
M
).

—

Let
\mathbf{g}=(C_{0},\dots,C_{n})
be a gallery in
A^{+}
and
\rho_{A^{\pm}}
be a retraction
\mathcal{T}^{\pm}\rightarrow A^{\pm}
. Then a
\rho_{A^{\pm}}
-lift
of
\mathbf{g}
is a gallery
(\widetilde{C}_{0},\dots,\widetilde{C}_{n})
in
\mathcal{T}^{+}
such that
\rho_{A^{\pm}}(\widetilde{C}_{i})=C_{i}
for all
i\in\llbracket 0,n\rrbracket
. A
\rho_{A^{\pm}}
-minimal lift
is a
\rho_{A^{\pm},x}
-lift which is also a minimal gallery.

—

If
C^{-}\in A^{-}
, we denote by
\rho_{A^{\pm},C^{-}}
the retraction centered at
C^{-}
and with image
A^{\pm}
. It sends any alcove
C\in\mathcal{T}^{+}
(resp.
C\in\mathcal{T}^{-})
to the unique alcove
C^{\prime}\in A^{+}
(resp.
C^{\prime}\in A^{-}
) such that

(resp.
d^{-}(C^{\prime},C^{-})=d^{-}(C,C^{-})
).

Projections in the masure

Like other notions, we can extend the definition of projections from the fundamental apartment to more general points and segment germs in the masure as follows.

Suppose that
\widetilde{x}\leq_{\oplus}\widetilde{y}\in\mathcal{I}_{\oplus}
(resp.
\widetilde{x}\geq_{\oplus}\widetilde{y}\in\mathcal{I}_{\oplus}
) and let
a
be an alcove based at
\widetilde{y}
. Then by
[
14
, Proposition 5.17 (ii)]
there is an apartment
A_{\oplus}
containing
a
and
\widetilde{x}
. Therefore there is
g\in G
such that
\mathbb{A}=g.A_{\oplus}
and
g.\widetilde{y}-g.\widetilde{x}\in\mathscr{T}
(resp.
g.\widetilde{y}-g.\widetilde{x}\in-\mathscr{T}
). We then define
\mathrm{proj}_{\widetilde{x}}(a)
as
g^{-1}.\mathrm{proj}_{g.\widetilde{x}}(a)\in\mathcal{T}^{+}_{\widetilde{x}}(\mathcal{I}_{\oplus})
(resp.
g^{-1}.\mathrm{proj}_{g.\widetilde{x}}(a)\in\mathcal{T}^{-}_{\widetilde{x}}(\mathcal{I}_{\oplus})
). If
\mathfrak{s}_{\varepsilon}(t)
is a segment germ based at
\mathbf{x}
, then (by
[
14
, Proposition 5.17 (ii)]
again) we can suppose that
A_{\oplus}
also contains

Segments in a masure

As it has already been mentioned, the notion of segment in
\mathbb{A}
naturally extends to the notion of segment in the masure
\mathcal{I}_{\oplus}
:
\mathfrak{s}
is a segment in
\mathcal{I}_{\oplus}
if and only if it can be written
\mathfrak{s}=g_{\mathfrak{s}}.\mathfrak{s}_{0}
for some element

and some segment
\mathfrak{s}_{0}
in
\mathbb{A}

\mathcal{I}_{\oplus}
is any triplet
\underline{\mathfrak{s}}=(\mathfrak{s},D_{\mathfrak{s}},\underline{\mathfrak{s}}(1))
of the form
g_{\underline{\mathfrak{s}}}.\underline{\mathfrak{s}}_{0}=(g_{\underline{\mathfrak{s}}}\mathfrak{s}_{0},g_{\underline{\mathfrak{s}}}D_{\mathfrak{s}_{0}},g_{\underline{\mathfrak{s}}}\underline{\mathfrak{s}}_{0}(1))
for some open segment
\underline{\mathfrak{s}}_{0}=(\mathfrak{s}_{0},D_{\mathfrak{s}_{0}},\underline{\mathfrak{s}}_{0}(1))
in
\mathbb{A}

Definition 4.7
.

For any
\lambda\in Y^{+}
, we say that a segment is
a segment of type

\mathcal{I}_{\oplus}
such that
\rho_{C_{0}}\circ\mathfrak{s}=\mathfrak{s}_{\lambda}

[
3
]
. Similarly, an

Lemma 4.8

Let
\mathbf{x}\in W^{+}_{sph}

1.

For any alcove
C
in
\rho_{C_{0}}^{-1}(C_{\mathbf{x}})

\underline{\mathfrak{s}}
starting at

and with end alcove

\mathbf{x}

\underline{\mathfrak{s}}=g.\underline{\mathfrak{s}}_{\mathbf{x}}

Proof.

By definition of

A
of
\mathcal{I}_{\oplus}
containing

and

. Then

and with end alcove

,

and
\rho(C_{+})

. By

, Proposition 5.179]
,

belongs to the unique apartment of the tangent twin building

Lemma 4.13
.

Let
\mathbf{x}\in W^{+}_{\mathrm{sph}}

\underline{\mathfrak{s}}

\mathbf{x}

\underline{\mathfrak{s}}

Let

3.2
),

satisfies the admissibility condition (

) on

\mathfrak{c}^{\prime}_{+}(t_{i})\neq\mathfrak{c}^{\prime}_{-}(t_{i})\text{ or }d^{\ast}(D^{-}_{\mathfrak{c},t_{i}},D^{+}_{\mathfrak{c},t_{i}})=w_{\lambda,0}.

. Assume that
\mathfrak{c}_{+}^{\prime}(t_{i})=\mathfrak{c}_{-}^{\prime}(t_{i})

\mathfrak{c}((t_{i}^{-},t_{i}^{+}))

\mathfrak{c}(t_{i})
. Note that, since

is a segment and since

.

(4.3)

4.12

-Hecke open path

I_{\infty}
, and also fixes
C^{\infty}_{\mathfrak{c}(t)}
.
Moreover, if
t<1
(resp.
t=1
) and
a_{t}\in\mathcal{C}(\underline{\mathfrak{c}},\mathbf{x},t)
then
d^{-}(C^{\infty}_{\mathfrak{c}(t)},a_{t})=d^{-}(C^{\infty}_{\mathfrak{c}(t)},D^{+}_{t})
(resp.
d^{\ast}(C^{\infty}_{\mathfrak{c}(1)},a_{1})=d^{\ast}(C^{\infty}_{\mathfrak{c}(1)},\underline{\mathfrak{c}}(1))
) so, since
D^{+}_{t}\subset\mathbb{A}
(resp.
\underline{\mathfrak{c}}(1)\subset\mathbb{A}
) and
a_{t}\subset A_{\oplus}
, we have
g.D^{+}_{t}=a_{t}
(resp.
g.\underline{\mathfrak{c}}(1)=a_{1}

.
∎

Lemma 4.18
.

Let
\underline{\mathfrak{c}}
be an admissible
C_{\infty}
-Hecke path of type
\mathbf{x}\in W^{+}_{sph}
, and, for each time
t\in T(\underline{\mathfrak{c}})
and alcove
a_{t}\in\mathcal{C}(\underline{\mathfrak{c}},\mathbf{x},t)
, fix
i_{a_{t}}
as in Lemma
4.17
. Set by convention that, for
t\notin T(\underline{\mathfrak{c}})
,
i_{a_{t}}=1_{G}
.

For any
a\in\prod\limits_{t\in T(\underline{\mathfrak{c}})}\mathcal{C}(\underline{\mathfrak{c}},\mathbf{x},t)
, let us define
g_{a}.\underline{\mathfrak{c}}:=(g_{a}.\mathfrak{c},g_{a}.D,g_{a}.\underline{\mathfrak{c}}(1))
:

1.

the path
g_{a}.\mathfrak{c}
is defined by
t\mapsto g_{a}(t).\mathfrak{c}(t)
, with
g_{a}(t)=\prod\limits_{t^{\prime}\in T(\underline{\mathfrak{c}})\cap[0,t)}i_{a_{t^{\prime}}}
,

2.

the decoration
g_{a}.D
is given by
(g_{a}.D)^{-}_{t}=g_{a}(t)D^{-}_{t}
and
(g_{a}.D)^{+}_{t}=g_{a}(t)i_{a_{t}}D^{+}_{t}
,

3.

the end alcove is given by
g_{a}.\underline{\mathfrak{c}}(1)=g_{a}(1)i_{a_{1}}\underline{\mathfrak{c}}(1)
.

Then,
g_{a}.\underline{\mathfrak{c}}
is an open segment such that
\rho_{C_{0}}(g_{a}.\underline{\mathfrak{c}})=\underline{\mathfrak{s}}_{\mathbf{x}}
and
\rho_{C_{\infty}}(g_{a}.\underline{\mathfrak{c}})=\underline{\mathfrak{c}}
.

Proof.

By construction, since
g_{a}(t)\in I_{\infty}
for all
t\in[0,1]
, it is clear that
\rho_{C_{\infty}}(g_{a}.\underline{\mathfrak{c}})=\underline{\mathfrak{c}}
.

Let us first prove that
g_{a}.\mathfrak{c}
is a segment on
[0,1]
, and that the decoration
g_{a}.\underline{\mathfrak{c}}
satisfies the admissibility condition. Let
t_{1},t_{2}
be two consecutive times in
T(\underline{\mathfrak{c}})
. Then
g_{a}
is constant on
(t_{1},t_{2}]
,
g_{a}(t_{1})=g_{a}(t_{2})i_{a_{t_{1}}}^{-1}
and
i_{a_{t_{1}}}
fixes
\mathfrak{c}(t_{1})
, since it fixes
C^{\infty}_{\mathfrak{c}{t_{1}}}
. Moreover
\mathfrak{c}|_{[t_{1},t_{2}]}
is a segment and thus
(g_{a}.\mathfrak{c})|_{[t_{1},t_{2}]}=g_{a}(t_{2}).\mathfrak{c}|_{[t_{1},t_{2}]}
is a segment. By the admissibility condition for
\underline{\mathfrak{c}}
,
g_{a}.\underline{\mathfrak{c}}
satisfies the admissibility condition on
[t_{1},t_{2}]
.

Therefore in order to prove admissibility of
g_{a}.\underline{\mathfrak{c}}
on
[0,1]
, by Lemma
3.3
it remains to check that condition (
3.3
) holds at every time
t\in T(\underline{\mathfrak{c}})\setminus\{0,1\}
. However, by construction, for any such time
t
,
(g_{a}.D)^{-}_{t}=g_{a}(t).D^{-}_{t}
and
(g_{a}.D)^{+}_{t}=g_{a}(t).i_{a_{t}}D^{+}_{t}
, hence:

d^{\ast}((g_{a}.D)^{-}_{t},(g_{a}.D)^{+}_{t})=d^{\ast}(D^{-}_{t},i_{a_{t}}D^{+}_{t}).

Moreover, by definition of
i_{a_{t}}
,
i_{a_{t}}D^{+}_{t}=a_{t}\in\mathcal{C}(\underline{\mathfrak{c}},\mathbf{x},t)
, and in particular
d^{\ast}(D^{-}_{t},i_{a_{t}}D^{+}_{t})=w^{-}_{\mathbf{x},t}=w_{\lambda,0}
(because
t\notin\{0,1\}
). Hence condition (
3.3
) is verified, and
g_{a}.\underline{\mathfrak{c}}
is an admissible open path. This also proves that the segment germs
(g_{a}.\mathfrak{c})_{-}(t)
and
(g_{a}.\mathfrak{c})_{+}(t)
are opposite (because
d^{\ast}((g_{a}.D)^{-}_{t},(g_{a}.D)^{+}_{t})\in W_{\lambda^{++}}
) for all
t\in[0,1]
, and thus by
[
11
, Lemma 4.9]
it proves that
(g_{a}.\mathfrak{c})
is a segment, starting at
0_{\mathbb{A}}
.

Let us now prove that
\rho_{C_{0}}(g_{a}.\underline{\mathfrak{c}})=\underline{\mathfrak{s}}_{\mathbf{x}}
. By
[
14
, Proposition 5.17 (ii)]
there exists an apartment
A_{\oplus}
containing
C_{0}
and
g_{a}.\underline{\mathfrak{c}}(1)
. Note that the distance
d^{+}((g_{a}.D)^{-}_{1},g_{a}.\underline{\mathfrak{c}}(1))=w^{-}_{\mathbf{x},1}=d^{-}(D^{-}_{\mathbf{x},1},C_{\mathbf{x}})
is minimal in its
W_{\lambda^{++}}
-coset
w^{-}_{\mathbf{x},1}W_{\lambda^{++}}
(because
D^{-}_{\mathbf{x},1}=\mathrm{proj}_{\mathfrak{s}_{\lambda,-}(1)}(C_{\mathbf{x}})
), therefore
(g_{a}.D)^{-}_{1}=\mathrm{proj}_{(g_{a}.\mathfrak{c})_{-}(1)}(g_{a}.\underline{\mathfrak{c}}(1))
. Thus by convexity,
A_{\oplus}
also contains
(g_{a}.D)^{-}_{1}
. Since
g_{a}.\mathfrak{c}
is a segment and since, by admissibility,
(g_{a}.D)^{\varepsilon}_{t}=\mathrm{proj}_{(g_{a}.\mathfrak{c})_{\varepsilon}(t)}((g_{a}.D)^{-}_{1})
, it also contains
(g_{a}.D)^{\varepsilon}_{t}
for all
(t,\varepsilon)\in[0,1]^{\pm}
. By (MA II), there exists
h\in G_{twin}
such that
h.A_{\oplus}=\mathbb{A}
fixing
\mathbb{A}_{\oplus}\cap A_{\oplus}
, in particular fixing
C_{0}
. Therefore
hg_{a}.\underline{\mathfrak{c}}=\rho_{C_{0}}(g_{a}\underline{\mathfrak{c}})
is an open segment starting at
0_{\mathbb{A}}
(in particular it is admissible).
Since
\mathfrak{c}^{\prime}_{+}(t)\in-W^{v}\lambda
for all
t
, the derivative
(hg_{a}.\mathfrak{c})^{\prime}_{+}(t)
lies in
-W^{v}\lambda
for all
t
. Moreover, since
(g_{a}.D)^{+}_{0}\in\mathcal{C}(\underline{\mathfrak{c}},\mathbf{x},0)
,
d^{\ast}(C_{0},(g_{a}.D)^{+}_{0})=w^{-}_{\mathbf{x},0}=d^{\ast}(C_{0},D^{+}_{\mathbf{x},0})
, so
\rho_{C_{0}}((g_{a}.D)^{+}_{0})=D^{+}_{\mathbf{x},0}
. This implies that
(hg_{a}.\mathfrak{c})_{+}(0)
is the segment germ
\mathfrak{s}_{\mathbf{x},+}(0)
, so for any
t\in[0,1)
,
(hg_{a}.\mathfrak{c})^{\prime}_{+}(t)=(hg_{a}.\mathfrak{c})^{\prime}_{+}(0)=-\lambda
, thus
hg_{a}\mathfrak{c}
is the segment
\mathfrak{s}_{\lambda}
. Since
hg_{a}.D=\rho_{C_{0}}(g_{a}.D)
satisfies the admissibility condition, the equality
\rho_{C_{0}}((g_{a}.D)^{+}_{0})=D^{+}_{\mathbf{x},0}
extends to any
(t,\varepsilon)\in[0,1]^{\pm}
. Finally
d^{+}((g_{a}.D)^{-}_{1},g_{a}.\underline{\mathfrak{c}}(1))=d^{+}(h(g_{a}.D)^{-}_{1},hg_{a}.\underline{\mathfrak{c}}(1))=d^{+}(D^{-}_{\mathbf{x},1},C_{\mathbf{x}})
, hence
\rho_{C_{0}}(g_{a}.\underline{\mathfrak{c}}(1))=hg_{a}.\underline{\mathfrak{c}}(1)=C_{\mathbf{x}}
, so
\rho_{C_{0}}(g_{a}.\underline{\mathfrak{c}})=hg_{a}.\underline{\mathfrak{c}}=\underline{\mathfrak{s}}_{\mathbf{x}}
. We thus have proved that
g_{a}.\underline{\mathfrak{c}}
is an open segment of type
\mathbf{x}
.
∎

Lemma 4.19
.

Let
f_{1},f_{2}:[0,1]\rightarrow\mathbb{A}
be two continuous maps and
g_{1},g_{2}\in G
be such that
g_{1}.f_{1}(r)=g_{2}.f_{2}(r)
, for every
r\in[0,1)
. Then
g_{1}.f_{1}(1)=g_{2}.f_{2}(1)
.

Proof.

By Proposition
4.1
, there exists
n\in N
such that for every
x\in g_{2}^{-1}g_{1}.\mathbb{A}\cap\mathbb{A}
, we have
g_{1}^{-1}g_{2}.x=n.x
. The element
n
induces an affine automorphism of
\mathbb{A}
and thus its restriction to
\mathbb{A}
is continuous. Therefore
f_{1}(1)=n.f_{2}(1)
and hence
g_{1}.f_{1}(1)=g_{2}.f_{2}(1)
.
∎

Proposition 4.20
.

Let
\underline{\mathfrak{c}}
be an admissible
C_{\infty}
-Hecke open path of type
\mathbf{x}\in W^{+}_{sph}
. Then the map
a\mapsto g_{a}.\underline{\mathfrak{c}}
defined in Lemma
4.18
defines a bijection

\prod\limits_{t\in T(\underline{\mathfrak{c}})}\mathcal{C}(\underline{\mathfrak{c}},\mathbf{x},t)\cong\rho_{C_{0}}^{-1}(\underline{\mathfrak{s}}_{\mathbf{x}})\cap\rho_{C_{\infty}}^{-1}(\underline{\mathfrak{c}}).

(4.5)

Proof.

By Lemma
4.18
, the map
a\mapsto g_{a}.\underline{\mathfrak{c}}
is a well defined map associating, to a sequence of alcoves
a\in\prod\limits_{t\in T(\underline{\mathfrak{c}})}\mathcal{C}(\underline{\mathfrak{c}},\mathbf{x},t)
, an open segment in
\rho_{C_{0}}^{-1}(\underline{\mathfrak{s}}_{\mathbf{x}})\cap\rho_{C_{\infty}}^{-1}(\underline{\mathfrak{c}})
. It remains to prove that it is injective and surjective.

1.

Injectivity:
Suppose that
a,a^{\prime}
are two distinct elements of
\prod\limits_{t\in T(\underline{\mathfrak{c}})}\mathcal{C}(\underline{\mathfrak{c}},\mathbf{x},t)
. Let
t
be the first time such that
a_{t}\neq a^{\prime}_{t}
. If
t<1
, then
g_{a}(t)=g_{a^{\prime}}(t)
and
i_{a_{t}}D^{+}_{t}=a_{t}\neq a^{\prime}_{t}=i_{a^{\prime}_{t}}D^{+}_{t}
, so
(g_{a}.D)^{+}_{t}\neq(g_{a^{\prime}}.D)^{+}_{t}
. If
t=1
, then
g_{a}.\underline{\mathfrak{c}}(1)=a_{1}\neq a^{\prime}_{1}=g_{a^{\prime}}.\underline{\mathfrak{c}}(1)
.
Either way,
g_{a}.\underline{\mathfrak{c}}\neq g_{a^{\prime}}.\underline{\mathfrak{c}}
, hence the map is injective (and this also shows injectivity of
(a_{s})_{s\in[0,t)\cap T(\underline{\mathfrak{c}})}\mapsto g_{a}.\underline{\mathfrak{c}}|_{[0,t)}
for all
t\in(0,1]
).

2.

Surjectivity:
Let
\underline{\mathfrak{s}}=(\mathfrak{s},D_{\mathfrak{s}},\underline{\mathfrak{s}}(1))\in\rho_{C_{0}}^{-1}(\underline{\mathfrak{s}}_{\mathbf{x}})\cap\rho_{C_{\infty}}^{-1}(\underline{\mathfrak{c}})
. Since
C_{0}
is based at
0_{\mathbb{A}}
,
\rho_{C_{0}}^{-1}(0_{\mathbb{A}})=\{0_{\mathbb{A}}\}
and thus
\mathfrak{s}(0)=\mathfrak{s}_{\lambda}(0)=0_{\mathbb{A}}
. Let
t
be the upper bound of the set
\mathcal{U}
of times
s
such that
\underline{\mathfrak{s}}|_{[0,s]}=g_{a}.\underline{\mathfrak{c}}|_{[0,s]}
for some
a\in\prod\limits_{u\in T(\underline{\mathfrak{c}})\cap[0,s)}\mathcal{C}(\underline{\mathfrak{c}},\mathbf{x},u)
. We aim to show that
t
is a maximum and that
t=1
.

By assumption,
D^{+}_{\mathfrak{s},0}\in\mathcal{C}(\underline{\mathfrak{c}},\mathbf{x},0)
so for
r>0
small enough (in particular such that
T(\mathfrak{c})\cap[0,r)=\{0\}
),
\underline{\mathfrak{s}}|_{[0,r)}=g_{(D^{+}_{\mathfrak{s},0})}.\underline{\mathfrak{c}}|_{[0,r)}
, which implies that
t>0
. Let
s\in[0,t)
be such that
s>\max\{u\in T(\underline{\mathfrak{c}})\mid u<t\}
, and let
a=(a_{u})_{u\in T(\underline{\mathfrak{c}})\cap[0,s)}
be such that
\mathfrak{s}|_{[0,s]}=g_{a}.\mathfrak{c}_{[0,s]}
. For any
r\in(s,t)
we know that
\underline{\mathfrak{s}}|_{[0,r]}=g_{a}.\underline{\mathfrak{c}}|_{[0,r]}
. Indeed, if
s\leq r<t
then
r\in\mathcal{U}
so there exists
a^{\prime}\in\prod\limits_{u\in T(\underline{\mathfrak{c}})\cap[0,r)}\mathcal{C}(\underline{\mathfrak{c}},\mathbf{x},u)
such that
\underline{\mathfrak{s}}|_{[0,r]}=g_{a^{\prime}}.\underline{\mathfrak{c}}|_{[0,r]}
. By injectivity of the map
(a_{u})_{u\in[0,s)}\mapsto g_{a}.\underline{\mathfrak{c}}|_{[0,s)}
,
a_{u}=a^{\prime}_{u}
for all
u<s
and thus, since
T(\mathfrak{c})\cap[s,t)=\varnothing
, this is also true for all
u\leq r
, therefore
\underline{\mathfrak{s}}|_{[0,r]}=g_{a}.\underline{\mathfrak{c}}|_{[0,r]}
.
By Lemma
4.19
,

g_{a}.\mathfrak{c}(t)=\mathfrak{s}(t)
. Moreover since
D^{+}_{\mathfrak{s},s}=g_{a}(s)D^{+}_{s}
and
g_{a}(s)=g_{a}(t)
we have:

D^{-}_{\mathfrak{s},t}=\mathrm{proj}_{\mathfrak{s}_{-}(t)}(D^{+}_{\mathfrak{s},s})=\mathrm{proj}_{g_{a}(t)\mathfrak{c}_{-}(t)}(g_{a}(t)D^{+}_{s})=g_{a}(t)\mathrm{proj}_{\mathfrak{c}_{-}(t)}(D^{+}_{s})=g_{a}(t)D^{-}_{t}.

Therefore
D^{-}_{\mathfrak{s},t}=g_{a}(t)D^{-}_{t}
and
t=\max\mathcal{U}
.

Assume by contradiction that
t<1
. Note that
D^{+}_{\mathfrak{s},t}=\mathrm{proj}_{\mathfrak{s}_{+}(t)}(D^{-}_{t})
is the unique alcove containing
\mathfrak{s}_{+}(t)
at codistance
w^{-}_{\mathbf{x},t}
from
D^{-}_{\mathfrak{s},t}
, and since
\rho_{C_{\infty}}(D^{+}_{\mathfrak{s},t})=D^{+}_{t}
we have
d^{-}(C^{\infty}_{\mathfrak{s}(t)},D^{+}_{\mathfrak{s},t})=w^{\infty}_{\underline{\mathfrak{c}},t}
. Applying
g_{a}(t)^{-1}
, which preserves distance, codistance and sends
D^{-}_{\mathfrak{s},t}
to
D^{-}_{t}
,
C^{\infty}_{\mathfrak{s}(t)}
to
C^{\infty}_{\mathfrak{c}(t)}
, we deduce that
g_{a}(t)^{-1}D^{+}_{\mathfrak{s},t}
is an element of
\mathcal{C}(\underline{\mathfrak{c}},\mathbf{x},t)
.

If
t\in T(\underline{\mathfrak{c}})
, we extend
a=(a_{u})_{u\in T(\underline{\mathfrak{c}})\cap[0,t)}
setting
a_{t}=g_{a}(t)^{-1}D^{+}_{\mathfrak{s},t}
and then by construction
(g_{a}.\mathfrak{c})_{+}(t)=\mathfrak{s}_{+}(t)
. If
t\notin T(\underline{\mathfrak{c}})
, then since there is no wall between
C^{\infty}_{\mathfrak{c}(t)}
and
\mathfrak{c}_{+}(t)
, the fact that
C^{\infty}_{\mathfrak{s}(t)}=g_{a}(t).C^{\infty}_{\mathfrak{c}(t)}
already implies that
g_{a}(t).\mathfrak{c}_{+}(t)=\mathfrak{s}_{+}(t)
(because
\mathfrak{c}_{+}(t)
is in the enclosure of
C^{\infty}_{\mathfrak{c}(t)}
, see
[
3
, Definition 2.1.2.4)]
). Either way there is
g_{a}
such that
\mathfrak{s}_{+}(t)=(g_{a}.\mathfrak{c})_{+}(t)
and in particular there exists
r>t
such that
\mathfrak{s}|_{[0,r]}=g_{a}.\mathfrak{c}|_{[0,r]}
, contradicting the definition of
t
. Thus
t=1
, and there is a sequence
a\in\prod\limits_{u\in T(\underline{\mathfrak{c}})\setminus\{1\}}\mathcal{C}(\underline{\mathfrak{c}},\mathbf{x},u)
such that
(\mathfrak{s},D_{\mathfrak{s}})=(g_{a}.\mathfrak{c},g_{a}.D)
. To prove surjectivity, it remains to check that, for such a sequence, the end alcove
g_{a}(1)^{-1}\underline{\mathfrak{s}}(1)
belongs to
\mathcal{C}(\underline{\mathfrak{c}},\mathbf{x},1)
. The element
g_{a}(1)
defines an isomorphism of local tangent buildings
\mathcal{T}^{\pm}_{\mathfrak{c}(1)}(\mathcal{I}_{\oplus})\cong\mathcal{T}^{\pm}_{\mathfrak{s}(1)}(\mathcal{I}_{\oplus})
, which sends the alcoves
C^{\infty}_{\mathfrak{c}(t)}
and
D^{-}_{1}
to
C^{\infty}_{\mathfrak{s}(1)}
and
D^{-}_{\mathfrak{s},1}
respectively. Therefore
a_{1}:=g_{a}(1)^{-1}\underline{\mathfrak{s}}(1)
satisfies
d^{\ast}(C^{\infty}_{\mathfrak{c}(1)},a_{1})=d^{\ast}(C^{\infty}_{\mathfrak{s}(1)},\underline{\mathfrak{s}}(1))
and
d^{+}(D^{-}_{1},a_{1})=d^{+}(D^{-}_{\mathfrak{s},1},\underline{\mathfrak{s}}(1))
. Since
d^{\ast}(C^{\infty}_{\mathfrak{s}(1)},\underline{\mathfrak{s}}(1))=d^{\ast}(C^{\infty}_{\mathfrak{c}(1)},\underline{\mathfrak{c}}(1))
(because
\rho_{C_{\infty}}(\underline{\mathfrak{s}}(1))=\underline{\mathfrak{c}}(1)
) and
d^{+}(D^{-}_{\mathfrak{s},1},\underline{\mathfrak{s}}(1))=d^{+}(D^{-}_{\mathbf{x},1},C_{\mathbf{x}})
(because
\rho_{C_{0}}(\underline{\mathfrak{s}})=\underline{\mathfrak{s}}_{\mathbf{x}}
), we deduce that
a_{1}
belongs to
\mathcal{C}(\underline{\mathfrak{c}},\mathbf{x},1)
, and we conclude that the map
C\mapsto g_{a}.\underline{\mathfrak{c}}
is surjective.

∎

Lemma 4.21
.

Let
(\mathcal{T}^{\pm},d^{\pm},d^{\ast})
be a twin building and let
\mathbb{A}^{\pm}
be a twin apartment of
\mathcal{T}^{\pm}
.
Let
C_{-}\in\mathbb{A}^{-}
and
C_{+}\in\mathbb{A}^{+}
be two chambers of opposite signs and let
w,v
be two elements of the Weyl group
W
of
\mathcal{T}^{\pm}
. Let
\mathbf{i}_{w}
be a reduced expression of
w
and let
C_{v}
be the unique chamber of
\mathbb{A}^{+}
at codistance
v
from
C_{-}
. Let
\Gamma_{C_{-}}(C_{+},C_{v},\mathbf{i}_{w},\mathbb{A}^{\pm})
be the set of
C_{-}
-centrifugally folded galleries of type
\mathbf{i}_{w}
from
C_{+}
to
C_{v}
in
\mathbb{A}^{\pm}
. For any
\mathbf{g}\in\Gamma_{C_{-}}(C_{+},C_{v},\mathbf{i}_{w},\mathbb{A}^{\pm})

\mathcal{C}^{m}_{C_{-}}(\mathbf{g},C_{+},\mathcal{T}^{+})
denote the set of minimal galleries, in
\mathcal{T}^{+}
, which retract to
\mathbf{g}
by
\rho_{\mathbb{A}^{\pm},C_{-}}
.

Then, the map which sends a gallery to its last chamber defines a bijection:

\displaystyle\bigsqcup\limits_{\mathbf{g}\in\Gamma_{C_{-}}(C_{+},C_{v},\mathbf{i}_{w},\mathbb{A}^{\pm})}\mathcal{C}^{m}_{C_{-}}(\mathbf{g},C_{+},\mathcal{T}^{+})\cong\{C\in\mathcal{T}^{+}\mid d^{+}(C_{+},C)=w,\;d^{\ast}(C_{-},C)=v\}.

Proof.

Suppose that
C\in\mathcal{T}^{+}
verifies
d^{+}(C_{+},C)=w
, then there exists a unique minimal gallery
\widetilde{\mathbf{g}}=(\widetilde{C}_{0},\dots,\widetilde{C}_{n})
of type
\mathbf{i}_{w}=(s_{1},\dots,s_{n})
from
C_{+}
to
C
in
\mathcal{T}^{+}
. Let
\mathbf{g}=(C_{0},\ldots,C_{n})
be the image of
\widetilde{\mathbf{g}}
by
\rho_{\mathbb{A}^{\pm},C_{-}}
, we need to verify that it is centrifugally folded. Suppose that
C_{i}=C_{i-1}
. By definition,
\rho_{\mathbb{A}^{\pm},C_{-}}
preserves the codistance to
C_{-}
, so
d^{\ast}(C_{-},\widetilde{C}_{i})=d^{\ast}(C_{-},\widetilde{C}_{i-1})=d^{\ast}(C_{-},C_{i})
. Moreover and
d(\widetilde{C}_{i-1},\widetilde{C}_{i})=s_{i}
. Therefore, by the converse of axiom
(TW2)
of
[
1
, Definition 5.133]
, the fact that
d^{\ast}(C_{-},\widetilde{C}_{i})=d^{\ast}(C_{-},\widetilde{C}_{i-1})
implies that
d^{\ast}(C_{-},\widetilde{C}_{i-1})s_{i}>d^{\ast}(C_{-},\widetilde{C}_{i-1})
and since
d^{\ast}(C_{-},C_{i-1})=d^{\ast}(C_{-},C_{i})=d^{\ast}(C_{-},\widetilde{C}_{i-1})
this implies that
\mathbf{g}
is
C_{-}
-centrifugally folded at

. Hence we have an injection:

\{C\in\mathcal{T}^{+}\mid d^{+}(C_{+},C)=w,\;d^{\ast}(C_{-},C)=v\}\hookrightarrow\bigsqcup\limits_{\mathbf{g}\in\Gamma_{C_{-}}(C_{+},C_{v},\mathbf{i}_{w},\mathbb{A}^{\pm})}\mathcal{C}^{m}_{C_{-}}(\mathbf{g},C_{+},\mathcal{T}^{+}).

Conversely, suppose that
\widetilde{g}
lies in
\mathcal{C}^{m}_{C_{-}}(\mathbf{g},C_{+},\mathcal{T}^{+})
for some
\mathbf{g}\in\Gamma_{C_{-}}(C_{+},C_{v},\mathbf{i}_{w},\mathbb{A}^{\pm})
, then since
\widetilde{g}
is minimal of type
\mathbf{i}_{w}
and starts at
C_{+}
, its last chamber
C
is at distance

of
C_{+}
. Moreover, since

d^{\ast}(C_{-},C)=d^{\ast}(C_{-},C_{v})=v
. Hence we obtain an inverse map of the injection above, and thus a bijection:

\bigsqcup\limits_{\mathbf{g}\in\Gamma_{C_{-}}(C_{+},C_{v},\mathbf{i}_{w},\mathbb{A}^{\pm})}\mathcal{C}^{m}_{C_{-}}(\mathbf{g},C_{+},\mathcal{T}^{+})\hookrightarrow\{C\in\mathcal{T}^{+}\mid d^{+}(C_{+},C)=w,\;d^{\ast}(C_{-},C)=v\}.

∎

Lemma 4.22
.

l(\mathbf{g})=|\{j\in\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket\mid C_{j-1}\neq C_{j},\;d^{\ast}(C_{-},C_{j})<d^{\ast}(C_{-},C_{j-1}),\;p(C_{j},s_{j})\in\mathcal{P}\}|

r(\mathbf{g})=|\{j\in\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket\mid C_{j-1}=C_{j}\}|
. Let
R^{C_{-}}_{\mathbf{g},\mathcal{P}}(X)
denote the polynomial with integral coefficients defined by:

R^{C_{-}}_{\mathbf{g},\mathcal{P}}(X)=X^{l(\mathbf{g})}(X-1)^{r(\mathbf{g})}.

Then for any
(q,1)
-semi-homogeneous twin building
\mathcal{T}^{\pm}
with twin apartment
\mathbb{A}^{\pm}
and such that
\mathcal{P}
is the set of thick panels of
\mathbb{A}^{+}
in
\mathcal{T}^{+}
, we have:

|\mathcal{C}^{m}_{C_{-}}(\mathbf{g},C_{+},\mathcal{T}^{+})|=R^{C_{-}}_{\mathbf{g},\mathcal{P}}(q).

In particular, it is non-zero for any gallery
\mathbf{g}
which is
C_{-}
-centrifugally folded along panels of
\mathcal{P}
. If
\mathbf{g}
folds along a panel which is not thick (not in
\mathcal{P}
), then it does not lift to any minimal gallery, so
|\mathcal{C}^{m}_{C_{-}}(\mathbf{g},C_{+},\mathcal{T}^{+})|=0
and we set
R^{C_{-}}_{\mathbf{g},\mathcal{P}}=0
.

This motivates the following definition, in order to explicit Formula (
4.5
).

Definition 4.23
.

Let
\mathbb{A}^{\pm}
be a twin apartment and let
\mathcal{P}
be a set of panels in
\mathbb{A}^{+}
. Let
C_{-}\in\mathbb{A}^{-}
and
C_{+}\in\mathbb{A}^{+}
, let
v,w\in W
. As in Lemma
4.21
, let
\mathbf{i}_{w}
be a reduced expression of
w
, and let
C_{v}
be the unique chamber of
\mathbb{A}^{+}
at codistance
v
from
C_{-}
. Then we define the polynomial
R^{C_{-},C_{+}}_{v,w,\mathcal{P}}(X)\in\mathbb{Z}[X]
by:

R^{C_{-},C_{+}}_{v,w,\mathcal{P}}(X)=\sum\limits_{\mathbf{g}\in\Gamma_{C_{-}}(C_{+},C_{v},\mathbf{i}_{w},\mathbb{A}^{\pm})}R^{C_{-}}_{\mathbf{g},\mathcal{P}}(X).

(4.6)

By Lemmas
4.21
and
4.22
, it is the unique polynomial such that, if
\mathcal{T}^{\pm}
is a
(q,1)
-semi-homogeneous twin building with twin apartment
\mathbb{A}^{\pm}
and
\mathcal{P}
is its set of
q
-thick panels in
\mathbb{A}^{+}
we have:

|\{C\in\mathcal{T}^{+}\mid d^{+}(C_{+},C)=w,\;d^{\ast}(C_{-},C)=v\}|=R^{C_{-},C_{+}}_{v,w,\mathcal{P}}(q).

In particular, this polynomial does not depend on the choice of
\mathbf{i}_{w}
.

Remark 4.24
.

These polynomials should be thought of as twisted versions, with unequal parameters, of the R-polynomials associated to a twin building, or to a Hecke algebra. Indeed when
\mathcal{P}
is the set of all panels of
\mathbb{A}^{+}
, then
R^{C_{-},C_{+}}_{v,w,\mathcal{P}}
only depends on
v,w,d^{\ast}(C_{-},C_{+})\in W
, and if
d^{\ast}(C_{-},C_{+})=1
then it is equal to the classical polynomial
R_{v,w}(X)
defined by Kazhdan and Lusztig
[
17
]
.

4.5
Definition and computation of

-Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomials

We use the computations above to define affine

4.5.1
Definition of affine

Lemma 4.27
.

Let
\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in W^{+}
, with

\psi:\underline{s}\mapsto\underline{s}(1)

2.

C_{\infty}
-friendly, where

is the vertex of
C
,

3.

\rho_{C_{0}}(C)=C_{\mathbf{x}}
and
\rho_{C_{\infty}}(C)=C_{\mathbf{y}}
.

Its reciprocal is the map
\psi^{\prime}:E^{\prime}_{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}}\rightarrow E_{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}}
which associates to every

the open segment starting at

and with end alcove

(which is well-defined by Lemma

Proof.

The fact that

. In other words,

\rho_{C_{\infty}}(\underline{\mathfrak{s}})\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{\mathbf{x}}(\mathbf{y})

1).
∎

(4.10)

4.5.2
Comparison with Spherical
R
-polynomials

Let
K\supset I
denote the stabiliser of
0_{\mathbb{A}}
in
G
. In
[
24

R
-polynomials, which conjecturally compute the cardinal of the sets
(K\varpi^{\lambda}K\cap I_{\infty}\varpi^{\mu}K)/K
for

and

(see
[
24

[
3

R
-polynomials defined here with their results.

Since
K
is the stabilizer of the special vertex
0_{\mathbb{A}}
, the set
(K\varpi^{\lambda}K\cap I_{\infty}\varpi^{\mu}K)/K
bijects with
\rho_{0_{\mathbb{A}}}^{-1}(-\lambda)\cap\rho_{C_{\infty}}^{-1}(-\mu)
, where
\rho_{0_{\mathbb{A}}}
is the masure retraction centered at
0_{\mathbb{A}}
. More precisely
\rho_{0_{\mathbb{A}}}(x)
is the unique element of
K.x\cap\pm\overline{C^{v}_{f}}
for all
x\in\{x\in\mathcal{I}_{\oplus}\mid x\leq_{\oplus}0_{\mathbb{A}}\text{ or }x\geq_{\oplus}0_{\mathbb{A}}\}

\rho_{0_{\mathbb{A}}}=\rho_{0_{\mathbb{A}}}\circ\rho_{C_{0}}

\rho_{0_{\mathbb{A}}}

on
\pm\mathscr{T}

\rho_{0_{\mathbb{A}}}(x)=(\rho_{C_{0}}(x))^{++}
for every

. Therefore
K\varpi^{\lambda}K=\bigsqcup\limits_{w\in W^{\lambda}}I\varpi^{w\lambda}K
for any
\lambda\in Y^{++}
(recall that
W^{\lambda}
is the set of minimal coset representatives of

). In term of masure retractions, we have:

\rho_{0_{\mathbb{A}}}^{-1}(-\lambda)\cap\rho_{C_{\infty}}^{-1}(-\mu)=\bigsqcup\limits_{w\in W^{\lambda}}\rho_{C_{0}}^{-1}(-w\lambda)\cap\rho_{C_{\infty}}^{-1}(-\mu).

Since the alcove

of the fundamental alcove at
-w\lambda
, the map

defines a bijection
\rho_{C_{0}}^{-1}(-w\lambda)\cong\rho_{C_{0}}^{-1}(C^{++}_{-w\lambda})
.

For any
C_{\infty}
-Hecke non-open path of type
\lambda
ending at
-\mu
(defined as
I_{\infty}
-Hecke paths in
[
24
, §5.3.1]

let

denote the set of
C_{\infty}
-Hecke open paths of type
\varpi^{w\lambda}w
with underlying non-open path

.

Then there is a bijection between the set of

-friendly segments of type
-w\lambda

and the set of

-Hecke open segments of type

\rho_{C_{0}}^{-1}(\mathfrak{s}_{w\lambda})\cap\rho_{C_{\infty}}^{-1}(\mathfrak{c})\cong\bigsqcup\limits_{\underline{\mathfrak{c}}\in\mathcal{H}_{w,\mathfrak{c}}}(\rho_{C_{0}}^{-1}(\underline{\mathfrak{s}}_{\varpi^{w\lambda}w})\cap\rho_{C_{\infty}}^{-1}(\underline{\mathfrak{c}})).

(4.12)

In
[
3

\rho_{0_{\mathbb{A}}}^{-1}(\mathfrak{s}_{\lambda})\cap\rho_{C_{\infty}}^{-1}(\mathfrak{c})=\bigsqcup\limits_{w\in W^{\lambda}}\rho_{C_{0}}^{-1}(\mathfrak{s}_{w\lambda})\cap\rho_{C_{\infty}}^{-1}(\mathfrak{c})

We recover a polynomial expression for the cardinal of this set (and in particular its finiteness) from the definition of the polynomials
R_{\varpi^{w\lambda}w,\underline{\mathfrak{c}}}

4.12
) and the first two points of the following Lemma.

Lemma 4.29
.

The following sets are finite:

1.

for a fixed pair
\mathfrak{c},w

\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{c},w}
,

2.

for a fixed
C_{\infty}
-Hecke path
\mathfrak{c}

\{w\in W^{\lambda}\mid\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{c},w}\neq\varnothing\}
,

3.

for a fixed pair
\lambda\in Y^{+}_{sph}
,
\mu\in Y^{+}
, the set
\{(w,v)\in W^{\lambda}\times W^{v}\mid R_{\varpi^{w\lambda}w,\varpi^{\mu}v}\neq 0\}

Proof.

In this proof, we use the length

introduced by Muthiah in
[
23

. It is also defined in
[
26
, (1.19)]
.

1.,2. Let
\mathfrak{c}
be a
C^{\infty}

\lambda
and ending at
-\mu
. Let

and assume that
\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{c},w}
is non-empty. Then
\underline{\mathfrak{c}}(1)
is based at
\mu
. Combining Proposition
3.10
and
[
26

\varpi^{\mu}v^{\mu}\leq\underline{\mathfrak{c}}(1)\leq\varpi^{w\lambda}w,

where
v^{\mu}
is the minimal element such that
\mu=v^{\mu}\mu^{++}

[

\ell(w)\leq\ell^{a}(\varpi^{\lambda})+\ell^{a}(v^{\mu})-\ell^{a}(\varpi^{\mu}).

4.5.3
Elementary properties of
R
-Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomials

Our
R
-polynomials have properties quite similar to the defining properties of the classical
R
-polynomials for Coxeter groups. In the next Proposition, we state analogs of points
i)
and
ii)
of
[
5
, Theorem 5.1.1]
. In Proposition
4.34
we compute
R
-polynomials for covers, it is the analog of a particular case of
[

.

Proposition 4.30
.

Let
\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in W^{+}
and suppose that
\mathbf{x}
is spherical. Then:

1.

If
R_{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}}\neq 0
, then
\mathbf{x}\geq\mathbf{y}
.

2.

We have
R_{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}}=1\iff\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{y}
.

Proof.

If
R_{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}}
is non-zero, then there is a
C_{\infty}
-Hecke open path of type
\mathbf{x}
with end alcove
C_{\mathbf{y}}
which implies, by Proposition
3.10
2., that
\mathbf{y}\leq\mathbf{x}
.

Let
\mathbf{x}=\varpi^{\lambda}w\in W^{+}_{sph}
. The unique
C_{\infty}
-Hecke open path of type
\mathbf{x}
ending at
C_{\mathbf{x}}
is the open segment
\underline{\mathfrak{s}}_{\mathbf{x}}
, and
T(\underline{\mathfrak{s}}_{\mathbf{x}})=\{0,1\}
. Since
C^{\infty}_{0_{\mathbb{A}}}
and
C_{0}
are opposite in
\mathcal{T}^{\pm}_{0_{\mathbb{A}}}(\mathcal{I}_{\oplus})
, we have
\mathcal{C}(\underline{\mathfrak{s}_{\mathbf{x}}},\mathbf{x},0)=\{D^{+}_{\mathbf{x},0}\}
, hence
R^{loc,0}_{\mathbf{x},\underline{\mathfrak{s}}_{\mathbf{x}}}=1
. It remains to check that
R^{loc,1}_{\mathbf{x},\underline{\mathfrak{s}}_{\mathbf{x}}}=1
. Let
\mathbf{i}
be the type of a reduced expression for
w^{-}_{\mathbf{x},1}=d^{+}(D^{-}_{\mathbf{x},1},C_{\mathbf{x}})
. There is a unique gallery
\mathbf{g}
from
D^{-}_{\mathbf{x},1}
to
C_{\mathbf{x}}=\underline{\mathfrak{s}}_{\mathbf{x}}(1)
of type
\mathbf{i}
, and it is a minimal gallery. Therefore
R^{loc,1}_{\mathbf{x},\underline{\mathfrak{s}}_{\mathbf{x}}}(X)=X^{l(\mathbf{g})}
where
l(\mathbf{g})
is the number of affine walls crossed by
\mathbf{g}
away from
C^{\infty}_{-\lambda}
or, equivalently, towards
C^{++}_{-\lambda}
, its opposite in
\mathcal{T}^{\pm}(\mathbb{A})
.

Let us check that
l(\mathbf{g})=0
. On the one hand since
\mathbf{g}
is minimal, the walls it crosses are crossed away from its initial alcove
D^{-}_{\mathbf{x},1}
and since
D^{-}_{\mathbf{x},1}=\mathrm{proj}_{\mathfrak{s}_{\lambda,-}(1)}(C_{\mathbf{x}})
, it does not cross any wall containing the segment germ
\mathfrak{s}_{\lambda,-}(1)
. Since the non-open component of
\underline{\mathfrak{s}}_{\mathbf{x}}
is the segment
\mathfrak{s}_{\lambda}
from
0_{\mathbb{A}}
to
-\lambda
, we have
C^{++}_{-\lambda}=\mathrm{proj}_{\mathfrak{s}_{\lambda,-}(1)}(C_{0})
which dominates the segment germ
\mathfrak{s}_{\lambda,-}(1)
. Therefore a half-apartment containing
D^{-}_{\mathbf{x},1}
also contains
C^{++}_{-\lambda}
, unless the affine wall delimiting it contains
\mathfrak{s}_{\lambda,-}(1)
. Since none of the walls crossed by
\mathbf{g}
contain
\mathfrak{s}_{\lambda,-}(1)
and since they are all crossed away from
D^{-}_{\mathbf{x},1}
, they are also crossed away from
C^{++}_{-\lambda}
, so
l(\mathbf{g})=0
and
R^{loc,1}_{\mathbf{x},\underline{\mathfrak{s}}_{\mathbf{x}}}=1
.

Conversely, suppose that
R_{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}}=1
. Then there is a unique
C_{\infty}
-Hecke open path of type
\mathbf{y}
with end alcove
C_{\mathbf{x}}
. Since any folding induces a factor
X-1
, it can not be folded. Therefore this Hecke open path is just the segment of type
\mathbf{y}
, and
\mathbf{y}=\mathbf{x}
.

∎

We will now compute
R
-polynomials associated to covers, the result is the same as for classical

.

Lemma 4.31
.

Let
\mathfrak{c}_{+}(t)=\mathfrak{c}(t)+[0,0^{+})\mathfrak{c}^{\prime}_{+}(t)
be a segment germ such that
\mathfrak{c}(t),\mathfrak{c}^{\prime}_{+}(t)\in-\mathcal{T}
and let
\gamma\in\Phi
.

1.

If
\langle\mathfrak{c}(t),\gamma\rangle\neq 0
, then the (affine or ghost) wall of direction
\gamma
going through
\mathfrak{c}(t)
separates
C^{\infty}_{\mathfrak{c}(t)}
from
\mathfrak{c}_{+}(t)
if and only if
\langle\mathfrak{c}(t),\gamma\rangle\langle\mathfrak{c}^{\prime}_{+}(t),\gamma\rangle<0
.

2.

If
\langle\mathfrak{c}(t),\gamma\rangle=0
, then the affine wall of direction
\gamma
going through
\mathfrak{c}(t)
separates
C^{\infty}_{\mathfrak{c}(t)}
from
\mathfrak{c}_{+}(t)
if and only if
\sgn(\gamma)\langle\mathfrak{c}^{\prime}_{+}(t),\gamma\rangle>0
.

Proof.

1.

Let
n=-\langle\mathfrak{c}(t),\gamma\rangle
, so that
\mathfrak{c}(t)\in M_{\gamma[n]}
, and suppose
n\neq 0
. If
\langle\mathfrak{c}^{\prime}_{+}(t),\gamma\rangle=0
then the wall
M_{\gamma[n]}
contains the segment germ
\mathfrak{c}_{+}(t)
, hence this segment germ is automatically on both sides of
M_{\gamma[n]}
, so there is nothing to prove. Let us suppose that it is not the case. By definition,
C^{\infty}_{\mathfrak{c}(t)}
dominates the segment germ
\mathfrak{c}(t)+[0,0^{+})\mathfrak{c}(t)
. Therefore the side of
M_{\gamma[n]}
containing
C^{\infty}_{\mathfrak{c}(t)}
contains this segment germ, and since
\langle\mathfrak{c}(t),\gamma\rangle\neq 0
it is the only side of
M_{\gamma[n]}
containing this segment germ. Therefore
C^{\infty}_{\mathfrak{c}(t)}
and the segment germ
\mathfrak{c}_{+}(t)
are separated by
M_{\gamma[n]}
if and only if the respective directions
\mathfrak{c}(t)
and
\mathfrak{c}^{\prime}_{+}(t)
of
\mathfrak{c}(t)+[0,0^{+})\mathfrak{c}(t)
and
\mathfrak{c}_{+}(t)
are separated by the vectorial wall
M_{\gamma}
, if and only if
\langle\mathfrak{c}(t),\gamma\rangle
and
\langle\mathfrak{c}^{\prime}_{+}(t),\gamma\rangle
have opposite sign, hence we get the first point.

2.

Suppose now that
\langle\mathfrak{c}(t),\gamma\rangle=0
. Up to replacing
\gamma
by
-\gamma
, we suppose that
\gamma
is positive. By definition
C^{\infty}_{\mathfrak{c}(t)}
and the fundamental alcove
C_{0}
are on opposite sides of
M_{\gamma[0]}
(because this wall goes through
\mathfrak{c}(t)
), so
\mathfrak{c}_{+}(t)
and
C^{\infty}_{\mathfrak{c}(t)}
are on opposite sides if and only if
\mathfrak{c}_{+}(t)
is on the same side of
M_{\gamma[0]}
as
C_{0}
). Since
\gamma
is positive,
\langle C_{0},\gamma\rangle>0
, so
C_{0}
and
\mathfrak{c}_{+}(t)
are on the same side of
M_{\gamma[0]}
if and only if
\langle\mathfrak{c}^{\prime}_{+}(t),\gamma\rangle>0
, which concludes point 2.

∎

Lemma 4.32
.

Let
v_{1},v_{2}\in W^{v}
be such that
\ell(v_{1}v_{2})=\ell(v_{1})+\ell(v_{2})
. Then

\mathrm{Inv}(v_{2}^{-1})\cap\mathrm{Inv}(v_{1})=\varnothing.

Proof.

Suppose that there exists
\gamma\in\mathrm{Inv}(v_{2}^{-1})\cap\mathrm{Inv}(v_{1})
. Then
v_{1}s_{\gamma}<v_{1}
and
v_{2}^{-1}s_{\gamma}<v_{2}^{-1}
(so
s_{\gamma}v_{2}<v_{2}
).
Therefore, by the triangular inequality,

\ell(v_{1}v_{2})=\ell((v_{1}s_{\gamma})(s_{\gamma}v_{2}))\leq\ell(v_{1}s_{\gamma})+\ell(s_{\gamma}v_{2})<\ell(v_{1})+\ell(v_{2}).

∎

Lemma 4.33
.

Let
\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in W^{+}
be such that
\mathbf{y}
is covered by
\mathbf{x}
. We assume that
\mathbf{y}=\varpi^{v\lambda}w
and
\mathbf{x}=s_{v(\beta)[n]}\mathbf{y}=\varpi^{v(s_{\beta}\lambda+n\beta^{\vee})}s_{v(\beta)}w
, with
\lambda\in Y^{++}
,
v\in W^{\lambda}
,
w\in W^{v}
,
\beta[n]\in\Phi_{+}^{a}
and
n\in\{-1,\langle\lambda,\beta\rangle+1\}
. Let
t_{0}=\frac{|n|}{\langle\lambda,\beta\rangle+2}
. Let
\underline{\mathfrak{c}}=\varphi_{t_{0},v(\beta)[n]}(\underline{\mathfrak{s}}_{\mathbf{x}})
. Then
T(\underline{\mathfrak{c}})=\{0,t_{0},1\}
and
R^{loc,t_{0}}_{\mathbf{x},\underline{\mathfrak{c}}}=X-1
.

Proof.

With the notation of the Lemma, since
\mathbf{x}
covers
\mathbf{y}
with
\mathrm{proj}^{Y^{++}}(\mathbf{x})\neq\mathrm{proj}^{Y^{++}}(\mathbf{y})
, by
[
26
, Proposition 3.21]
,
\beta
needs to be a quantum root (see
[
13
, Definition 2.4]
):

\forall\gamma\in\mathrm{Inv}(s_{\beta})\setminus\{\beta\},\,\langle\beta^{\vee},\gamma\rangle=1.

(4.14)

Moreover,
\lambda+\beta^{\vee}
is almost-dominant, that is to say:

\displaystyle\langle\lambda+\beta^{\vee},\tau\rangle\geq-1\,\text{for all}\,\tau\in\Phi_{+}.

(4.15)

It is clear that
\{0,t_{0},1\}\subset T(\underline{\mathfrak{c}})
since there is a folding at
t_{0}
. Moreover since
\underline{\mathfrak{c}}
is obtained from
\underline{\mathfrak{s}}_{\mathbf{x}}
by a folding at time
t_{0}
, both open paths agree on
[0,t_{0})
and thus we have that
T(\underline{\mathfrak{c}})\cap[0,t_{0})=T(\underline{\mathfrak{s}}_{\mathbf{x}})\cap[0,t_{0})=\{0\}
. Let us now prove that, on
[t_{0},1)
, there is no other affine wall separating
C^{\infty}_{\mathfrak{c}(t)}
and
\mathfrak{c}_{+}(t)
.

For any
\gamma\in\Phi_{+}
and
t\in[t_{0},1)
, let
M_{\gamma,t}
denote the (affine or ghost) wall of direction
v(\gamma)
going through
\mathfrak{c}(t)
, we say that it is separating if it separates
C^{\infty}_{\mathfrak{c}(t)}
from
\mathfrak{c}_{+}(t)
. We want to prove that, if
M_{\gamma,t}
is separating, then it is a ghost wall, unless
t=t_{0}
and
\gamma=\beta
. We use Lemma
4.31
in order to verify this. Let
g_{\gamma}
denote the affine function
t\mapsto\langle\mathfrak{c}(t),v(\gamma)\rangle
defined on
[t_{0},1]
. Then
M_{\gamma,t}
is an affine (non-ghost) wall if and only if
g_{\gamma}(t)\in\mathbb{Z}
, and by Lemma
4.31

M_{\gamma,t}
separates
C^{\infty}_{\mathfrak{c}(t)}
from
\mathfrak{c}_{+}(t)
if and only if either
g_{\gamma}(t)\langle\mathfrak{c}^{\prime}_{+}(t),v(\gamma)\rangle<0
or
g_{\gamma}(t)=0
and
\sgn(v(\gamma))\langle\mathfrak{c}^{\prime}_{+}(t),v(\gamma)\rangle>0
. We will separate cases according to the value of
n
and to the sign of
\langle\mathfrak{c}^{\prime}_{+}(t),v(\gamma)\rangle
. This value does not depend on
t\in[t_{0},1)
as
\mathfrak{c}
is affine on
[t_{0},1]
.

Note that
g_{\gamma}(1)=\langle-\lambda,\gamma\rangle\leq 0
and

g_{\gamma}(t_{0})=\begin{cases}-t_{0}\langle\lambda+\beta^{\vee},\gamma\rangle&\text{ if $n=\langle\lambda,\beta\rangle+1$}\\
-t_{0}\langle\lambda+\beta^{\vee},s_{\beta}(\gamma)\rangle&\text{ if $n=-1$.}\end{cases}

(4.16)

In particular, since
\lambda+\beta^{\vee}
is almost dominant and
\gamma\in\Phi_{+}
,
g_{\gamma}(t_{0})\leq t_{0}<1
unless
n=-1
and
\gamma\in\mathrm{Inv}(s_{\beta})
. Moreover if
n=-1
, clearly
g_{\beta}(t_{0})=1
and if
\gamma\in\mathrm{Inv}(s_{\beta})\setminus\{\beta\}
, since
\beta
is a quantum root ,
\langle\beta^{\vee},\gamma\rangle=1
and
s_{\beta}(\gamma)=\gamma-\beta
, thus
g_{\gamma}(t_{0})=\frac{1}{\langle\lambda+\beta^{\vee},\beta\rangle}\langle\lambda+\beta^{\vee},\beta-\gamma\rangle<1
in this case aswell. Therefore we have

g_{\gamma}(t_{0})\leq 1,

(4.17)

with equality if and only if
n=-1
and
\gamma=\beta
.
In particular outside of this case, since
g_{\gamma}
is affine on
[t_{0},1]
,
g_{\gamma}([t_{0},1])\subset(-\infty,1)
: there is no time for which
g_{\gamma}(t)
is integral and positive.

1.

Suppose first that
\langle\mathfrak{c}^{\prime}_{+}(t),v(\gamma)\rangle<0
, and
\gamma\neq\beta
. Then since
g_{\gamma}(t)
cannot be integral and positive, by Lemma
4.31
the wall
M_{\gamma,t}
is separating and non-ghost if and only if
g_{\gamma}(t)=0
and
v(\gamma)\in\Phi_{-}
(that is to say
\gamma\in\mathrm{Inv}(v)
). Note that the existence of such a time
t
implies that
g_{\gamma}(t_{0})>0

(a)

If
n=\langle\lambda,\beta\rangle+1
then
g_{\gamma}(t_{0})=-t_{0}\langle\lambda+\beta^{\vee},\gamma\rangle
is positive if and only if
\langle\lambda+\beta^{\vee},\gamma\rangle<0
, if and only if
\gamma\in\mathrm{Inv}(u^{-1})
where
u\in W^{v}
is the minimal element such that
u^{-1}(\lambda+\beta^{\vee})\in Y^{++}
. However, by
[
26
, Lemma 3.7]
we have
\ell(vu)=\ell(v)+\ell(u)
, so by Lemma
4.32
,
\mathrm{Inv}(v)\cap\mathrm{Inv}(u^{-1})=\varnothing
and
M_{\gamma,t}
can not be separating.

(b)

If
n=-1
then
g_{\gamma}(t_{0})=-t_{0}\langle s_{\beta}(\lambda+\beta^{\vee}),\gamma\rangle
is positive if and only if
\gamma\in\mathrm{Inv}(u^{-1}s_{\beta})
(with
u
defined as above). By
[
26
, Lemma 3.7]
and Lemma
4.32
, if
\gamma\in\mathrm{Inv}(u^{-1}s_{\beta})
then
\gamma\not\in\mathrm{Inv}(v)
, so
v(\gamma)
is a positive root. Therefore by Lemma
4.31
the wall
M_{\gamma,t}
is not separating.

2.

Suppose that
\langle\mathfrak{c}^{\prime}_{+}(t),v(\gamma)\rangle>0
. Let us then show that, for any such
\gamma
and
t\in[t_{0},1)
,
g_{\gamma}(t)
is not integral, unless
t=t_{0}
and
\gamma=\beta
. Since
g_{\gamma}(1)
is integral, it suffices to prove that

|g_{\gamma}(t_{0})-g_{\gamma}(1)|\leq 1,

(4.18)

with equality if and only if
\gamma=\beta
.
We have
|g_{\gamma}(t_{0})-g_{\gamma}(1)|=|(1-t_{0})\langle\mathfrak{c}^{\prime}_{+}(t_{0}),v(\gamma)\rangle|
, and
t_{0}=\frac{|n|}{\langle\lambda+\beta^{\vee}\beta\rangle}

(a)

If
n=-1
then
\mathfrak{c}^{\prime}_{+}(t_{0})=-v(\lambda+\beta^{\vee})
so
\langle\mathfrak{c}^{\prime}_{+}(t),v(\gamma)\rangle=-\langle\lambda+\beta^{\vee},\gamma\rangle
is positive if and only if
\langle\lambda+\beta^{\vee},\gamma\rangle<0
. Since
\lambda+\beta^{\vee}
is almost dominant and
\gamma\in\Phi_{+}
, this is equivalent to
\langle\lambda+\beta^{\vee},\gamma\rangle=-1
, in which case

|(1-t_{0})\langle\mathfrak{c}^{\prime}_{+}(t_{0}),v(\gamma)\rangle|=1-t_{0}<1.

(b)

If
n=\langle\lambda,\beta\rangle+1
then
\mathfrak{c}^{\prime}_{+}(t_{0})=-vs_{\beta}(\lambda+\beta^{\vee})
so if
\langle\mathfrak{c}^{\prime}_{+}(t),v(\gamma)\rangle=-\langle\lambda+\beta^{\vee},s_{\beta}(\gamma)\rangle>0
then it is equal to
1
unless
\gamma\in\mathrm{Inv}(s_{\beta})
. If it is equal to
1
, then
|g_{\gamma}(t_{0})-g_{\gamma}(1)|=1-t_{0}<1
. If
\gamma\in\mathrm{Inv}(s_{\beta})\setminus\{\beta\}
, and since
(1-t_{0})=\frac{1}{\langle\lambda+\beta^{\vee},\beta\rangle}
, we have
|g_{\gamma}(t_{0})-g_{\gamma}(1)|=\frac{|\langle\lambda+\beta^{\vee},s_{\beta}(\gamma)\rangle|}{\langle\lambda+\beta^{\vee},\beta\rangle}
. This is equal to
1
if
\gamma=\beta
. Else, since
\beta
is quantum, we have
|\langle\lambda+\beta^{\vee},s_{\beta}(\gamma)\rangle|=\langle\lambda+\beta^{\vee},\beta\rangle-\langle\lambda+\beta^{\vee},\gamma\rangle<\langle\lambda+\beta^{\vee},\beta\rangle
so
|g_{\gamma}(t_{0})-g_{\gamma}(1)|<1
.

We have therefore proven that there is no non-ghost separating wall on
[t_{0},1)
, except
M_{\beta,t_{0}}
. Therefore
T(\underline{\mathfrak{c}})=\{0,t_{0},1\}
. It remains to prove that
R^{loc,t_{0}}_{\mathbf{x},\underline{\mathfrak{c}}}=X-1
.

Let
\mathbf{g}
be a minimal gallery, in
\mathcal{T}^{-}_{\mathfrak{c}(t_{0})}(\mathbb{A})
, from
C^{\infty}_{\mathfrak{c}(t_{0})}
to the decorating alcove
D^{+}_{t_{0}}
of
\underline{\mathfrak{c}}
at time
t_{0}
, and let
\mathbf{i}
be its type. In order to compute
R^{loc,t_{0}}_{\mathbf{x},\underline{\mathfrak{c}}}
we will prove that there is a unique
D^{-}_{t_{0}}
-centrifugally folded gallery
\widetilde{\mathbf{g}}
of type
\mathbf{i}
from
C^{\infty}_{\mathfrak{c}(t_{0})}
to
D^{+}_{\mathbf{x},1}
(which is the unique alcove at codistance
w^{-}_{\mathbf{x},t_{0}}
from
D^{-}_{\mathbf{x},t_{0}}=D^{-}_{t_{0}}
), and that it satisfies
r(\widetilde{\mathbf{g}})=1
,
l(\widetilde{\mathbf{g}})=0
. Since
\mathbf{g}
is minimal, it is of the form
\mathbf{g}_{0}\mathbf{g}_{1}
where
\mathbf{g}_{0}
goes from
C^{\infty}_{\mathfrak{c}(t_{0})}
to
\mathrm{proj}_{\mathfrak{c}_{+}(t_{0})}(C^{\infty}_{\mathfrak{c}(t_{0})})
and where every alcove of
\mathbf{g}_{1}
dominates
\mathfrak{c}_{+}(t_{0})
. Since
M_{v(\beta),t_{0}}
is the only affine wall separating
C^{\infty}_{\mathfrak{c}(t_{0})}
from
\mathfrak{c}_{+}(t_{0})
, it is the unique affine wall along which
\mathbf{g}_{0}
can be folded, let
\widetilde{\mathbf{g}}_{0}
denote the associated folded gallery. Then, since
d^{+}(C^{\infty}_{\mathfrak{c}(t_{0})},D^{+}_{\mathbf{x},t_{0}})\in W_{(\lambda+\beta^{\vee})^{++}}
, any gallery of type
\mathbf{i}
from
C^{\infty}_{\mathfrak{c}(t_{0})}
to
D^{+}_{\mathbf{x},t_{0}}
is of the form
\widetilde{\mathbf{g}}_{0}\widetilde{\mathbf{g}}_{1}
with
\widetilde{\mathbf{g}}_{1}
of type
\mathbf{i}_{1}
. Moreover, if we suppose that
\widetilde{\mathbf{g}}
is
D^{-}_{\mathbf{x},t_{0}}
-centrifugally folded, since
D^{-}_{\mathbf{x},t_{0}}
and
D^{+}_{\mathbf{x},t_{0}}
are on the same side of every wall dominating
\mathfrak{s}_{\mathrm{proj}^{Y^{+}}(\mathbf{x}),+}(t_{0})
, the gallery
\widetilde{\mathbf{g}}_{1}
can not be folded (else the last folding would be done towards
D^{+}_{\mathbf{x},t_{0}}
, hence towards
D^{-}_{\mathbf{x},t_{0}}
). We deduce that there is a unique possible choice of such
\widetilde{\mathbf{g}}
, and since
\widetilde{\mathbf{g}}_{1}
is minimal it crosses every wall towards its last alcove, hence also towards
D^{-}_{\mathbf{x},t_{0}}
. Therefore it satisfies
r(\widetilde{\mathbf{g}})=1
,
l(\widetilde{\mathbf{g}})=0
. Thus,
R^{loc,t_{0}}_{\mathbf{x},\underline{\mathfrak{c}}}=X-1
, which concludes the proof of the lemma.

We now compute the
R
-polynomials associated to covers. We will use the classification of covers which was obtained by Welch in the affine ADE case (see
[
34
, Theorem 2]
), and extended to the general Kac-Moody case by the second author (see
[
26
, Proposition 3.20]
).

Proposition 4.34
.

Let
\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in W^{+}
and suppose that
\mathbf{x}\in W^{+}_{sph}
. Then

\mathbf{y}\lhd\mathbf{x}\implies R_{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}}=X-1.

(4.19)

Proof.

We use the classification of covers for the affine Bruhat order given in
[
13
, Lemma 3.1]
(note that, in this proposition
\mathbf{x}
covers
\mathbf{y}
, whereas it is the opposite in
[
13
]
).
We separate the computation in 5 cases, according to the form of the cover:

\mathbf{y}=\varpi^{v\lambda}w\text{ and }\mathbf{x}=s_{v(\beta)[n]}\mathbf{y}=\varpi^{v(s_{\beta}\lambda+n\beta^{\vee})}s_{v(\beta)}w,

(4.20)

with
\lambda\in Y^{++}
,
v\in W^{\lambda}
,
w\in W^{v}
and
\beta[n]\in\Phi_{+}^{a}
.

The integer
n
may take four values, namely
\{0,\langle\lambda,\beta\rangle,-1,\langle\lambda,\beta\rangle+1\}
. Moreover, an element
\underline{\mathfrak{c}}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{\mathbf{x}}(\mathbf{y})
is obtained from the open segment
\underline{\mathfrak{s}}_{\mathbf{x}}
by folding along the affine root
\beta[n]
. Indeed, if
\underline{\mathfrak{c}}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{\mathbf{x}}(\mathbf{y})
, then
\underline{\mathfrak{c}}
is obtained from
\underline{\mathfrak{s}}_{\mathbf{x}}
via some folding data
(t_{i},\beta_{i}[n_{i}])_{i\in\llbracket 0,r\rrbracket}
. By Proposition
3.10
, we have
\mathbf{y}=s_{\beta_{r}[n_{r}]}\ldots s_{\beta_{0}[n_{0}]}\mathbf{x}
. By definition of a cover, we thus have
r=0
and
\beta_{0}[n_{0}]=v(\beta)[n]
. Therefore it is unique, unless
\langle\lambda,\beta\rangle=n=0
(in which case we can fold either at time
0
or
1
, but we will see that only one choice contributes to
R_{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}}
).

1.

Suppose that
n=\langle\lambda,\beta\rangle\neq 0
, so that
\mathbf{x}=\varpi^{v\lambda}s_{v(\beta)}w
and
\ell(v^{-1}s_{\beta}w)=\ell(v^{-1}w)+1
. In this case the only difference between
\underline{\mathfrak{c}}
and
\underline{\mathfrak{s}}_{\mathbf{x}}
is the end alcove. Therefore
R^{loc,t}_{\mathbf{x},\underline{\mathfrak{c}}}=R^{loc,t}_{\mathbf{x},\underline{\mathfrak{s}}_{\mathbf{x}}}=1
for all
t<1
. We need to check that
R^{loc,1}_{\mathbf{x},\underline{\mathfrak{c}}}=X-1
. Let
\mathbf{i}
be the type of a minimal gallery from
C^{++}_{-v\lambda}
to
C_{\mathbf{x}}
. Since
C^{++}_{-v\lambda}
dominates the segment germ
\mathfrak{s}_{v\lambda,-}(1)
and by definition of
D^{-}_{\mathbf{x},1}
, we can write
\mathbf{i}=\mathbf{i}_{1}\mathbf{i}_{2}
where
\mathbf{i}_{1}
is the type of a minimal gallery (let us denote it
\mathbf{g}_{1}
) from
C^{++}_{-v\lambda}
to
D^{-}_{\mathbf{x},1}
and
\mathbf{i}_{2}
is the type of a minimal gallery from
D^{-}_{\mathbf{x},1}
to
C_{\mathbf{x}}
. Therefore if
\mathbf{g}_{2}
is a gallery of type
\mathbf{i}_{2}
from
D^{-}_{\mathbf{x},1}
to
C_{\mathbf{y}}
then concatenating it with
\mathbf{g}_{1}
we obtain a gallery from
C^{++}_{-v\lambda}
to
C_{\mathbf{y}}
of type
\mathbf{i}
.
Since (by Lemma
2.8
)

\ell(v^{-1}w)=\ell(d^{+}(C^{++}_{-v\lambda},C_{\mathbf{y}}))=\ell(v^{-1}s_{\beta}w)-1=\ell(d^{+}(C^{++}_{-v\lambda},C_{\mathbf{x}}))-1,

this gallery necessarily folds exactly once, and does not cross any wall towards
C^{++}_{-v\lambda}
, so
\ell(\mathbf{g}_{2})=0
. Moreover the folding is
C^{\infty}_{-v\lambda}
-centrifuged as it folds towards
C^{++}_{-v\lambda}
, so
r(\mathbf{g}_{2})=1
. Hence
R_{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}}=R^{loc,1}_{\mathbf{x},\underline{\mathfrak{c}}}=X-1
.

2.

Suppose that
n=0\neq\langle\lambda,\beta\rangle
, then the unique element of
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{\mathbf{x}}(\mathbf{y})
is obtained from folding
\underline{\mathfrak{s}}_{\mathbf{x}}
at time
0
along
M_{v(\beta)[0]}
. Therefore it is just the open segment
\underline{\mathfrak{s}}_{\mathbf{y}}
, and
T(\underline{\mathfrak{s}}_{\mathbf{y}})=\{0,1\}
. By
[
13
, Lemma 3.1]
,
\mathbf{x}=\varpi^{vs_{\beta}(\lambda)}s_{v(\beta)}w
and
\ell(vs_{\beta})=\ell(v)-1
.

—

To compute
R^{loc,1}_{D}
, let us fix a reduced expression
\mathbf{i}
of
w^{-}_{\mathbf{x},1}=d^{-}(D^{-}_{\mathbf{x},1},C_{\mathbf{x}})
. Since there is no folding at time
1
, we have
d^{-}(D^{-}_{\mathbf{y},1},C_{\mathbf{y}})=w^{-}_{\mathbf{y},1}=w^{-}_{\mathbf{x},1}
,so there is a unique gallery
\mathbf{g}
of type
\mathbf{i}
from
D^{-}_{\mathbf{y},1}
to
C_{\mathbf{y}}
, it is minimal, and does not cross any wall containing
\mathfrak{c}_{-}(1)
since
D^{-}_{\mathbf{y},1}=\mathrm{proj}_{\mathfrak{c}_{-}(1)}(C_{\mathbf{y}})
. Moreover
C^{++}_{-v\lambda}
and
D^{-}_{\mathbf{y},1}
are separated only by walls containing
\mathfrak{c}_{-}(1)
since they both dominate this segment germ, so any wall crossed by
\mathbf{g}
is crossed away from
C^{++}_{-v\lambda}
. Hence
l(\mathbf{g})=0
and
R^{loc,1}_{\mathbf{x},\underline{\mathfrak{c}}}=1
.

—

At
t=0
, we have that
D^{-}_{0}=C_{0}
is opposite (in
\mathcal{T}^{\pm}_{0_{\mathbb{A}}}(\mathbb{A})
) to
C^{\infty}_{0_{\mathbb{A}}}
. Therefore by Remark
4.24
, the polynomial
R^{loc,0}_{\mathbf{x},\underline{\mathfrak{c}}}
is a classical
R
-polynomial, and to prove that it is equal to
X-1
, it suffices to check that
w^{\infty}_{\underline{\mathfrak{c}},0}
is a cover of
w^{-}_{\mathbf{x},0}
. We thus need to express these two elements. Since
C^{++}_{-v\lambda}
(which is the opposite of
C^{\infty}_{-v\lambda}
) and
D^{-}_{\mathbf{y},1}
both dominate the segment germ
\mathfrak{s}_{v\lambda,-}(1)
the codistance
d^{\ast}(C^{\infty}_{-v\lambda},D^{-}_{\mathbf{y},1})=:\widetilde{w}
lies in
W_{\lambda}
. Explicitly, we therefore have
C^{\infty}_{-v\lambda}=a(-v\lambda,-v)
,
D^{-}_{\mathbf{y},1}=a(-v\lambda,+v\widetilde{w})
and
D^{-}_{\mathbf{x},1}=s_{v(\beta)[0]}D^{-}_{\mathbf{y},1}=a(-vs_{\beta}\lambda,+vs_{\beta}\widetilde{w})
. Since there is no folding after time
t=0
, by the admissibility condition we deduce that
D^{+}_{\mathbf{y},0}=a(0_{\mathbb{A}},-v\widetilde{w}w_{\lambda,0})
and
D^{+}_{\mathbf{x},0}=a(0_{\mathbb{A}},-vs_{\beta}\widetilde{w}w_{\lambda,0})
, where
w_{\lambda,0}
is the maximal element of the standard parabolic subgroup
W_{\lambda}
. Therefore:

w^{-}_{\mathbf{x},0}=vs_{\beta}\widetilde{w}w_{\lambda,0}\ \text{and}\ w^{\infty}_{\underline{\mathfrak{c}},0}=v\widetilde{w}w_{\lambda,0}.

(4.21)

In particular,
w^{\infty}_{\underline{\mathfrak{c}},0}=s_{v(\beta)}w^{-}_{\mathbf{x},0}
, so the two elements are comparable in
W^{v}
. Let us write
vs_{\beta}=u^{J}u_{J}
with
u^{J}\in W^{\lambda}
. By
[
26
, Proposition 3.20.2c]
,
a(-v\lambda,vu_{J}^{-1})
is on a minimal gallery from
C^{++}_{-v\lambda}=a(-v\lambda,v)
to
C_{\mathbf{y}}
so, since
a(-v\lambda,v\widetilde{w})=D^{-}_{\mathbf{y},1}
is the projection of
C_{\mathbf{y}}
on
\mathfrak{s}_{v\lambda,-}(1)
and
u_{J}\in W_{\lambda}
, the alcove
a(-v\lambda,vu_{J}^{-1})
is on a minimal gallery from
C^{++}_{-v\lambda}
to
D^{-}_{\mathbf{y},1}
. In term of distance, this implies that
\ell(\widetilde{w})=\ell(u_{J})+\ell(u_{J}\widetilde{w})
. Recall that for any element
r\in W^{\lambda}
,
t\in W_{\lambda}
, we have
\ell(rtw_{\lambda,0})=\ell(r)+\ell(tw_{\lambda,0})=\ell(r)+\ell(w_{\lambda,0})-\ell(t)
. We deduce:

\displaystyle\ell(w^{\infty}_{\underline{\mathfrak{c}},0})=\ell(v)+\ell(w_{\lambda,0})-\ell(\widetilde{w}),

\displaystyle\ell(w^{-}_{\mathbf{x},0})=\ell(u^{J})+\ell(w_{\lambda,0})-\ell(u_{J}\widetilde{w})=\ell(u^{J})+\ell(w_{\lambda,0})-\ell(\widetilde{w})+\ell(u_{J}).

Since
\ell(u^{J})+\ell(u_{J})=\ell(vs_{\beta})=\ell(v)-1
, we deduce that
\ell(w^{\infty}_{\underline{\mathfrak{c}},0})=\ell(w^{-}_{\mathbf{x},0})+1
, so
w^{\infty}_{\underline{\mathfrak{c}},0}
covers
w^{-}_{\mathbf{x},0}
and, by Remark
4.24
,
R^{loc,0}_{\mathbf{x},\underline{\mathfrak{c}}}=X-1
. We conclude that
R_{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}}=X-1
in this case.

3.

Suppose that
n=0=\langle\lambda,\beta\rangle
, then there are two open paths of type
\mathbf{x}
ending at
C_{\mathbf{y}}
, obtained folding along
M_{v(\beta)[0]}
at time either
0
or
1
. Let
\underline{\mathfrak{c}}
denote the open path obtained by folding at time
0
, and
\widetilde{\underline{\mathfrak{c}}}
the one obtained by folding at time
1
. Note that
D^{-}_{\widetilde{\underline{\mathfrak{c}}},1}=D^{-}_{\mathbf{x},1}
. However,
M_{\beta[0]}
contains the segment germ
\mathfrak{s}_{v\lambda,-}(1)
and
D^{-}_{\mathbf{x},1}
is the projection of
C_{\mathbf{x}}
on this segment germ, so
d^{+}(D^{-}_{\mathbf{x},1},C_{\mathbf{y}})=d^{+}(s_{\beta[0]}D^{-}_{\mathbf{x},1},C_{\mathbf{x}})>d^{+}(D^{-}_{\mathbf{x},1},C_{\mathbf{x}})
. Therefore there is no gallery with type a reduced expression of
w^{-}_{\mathbf{x},1}
from
D^{-}_{\mathbf{x},1}
to
C_{\mathbf{y}}
. Hence
R^{loc,1}_{\mathbf{x},\widetilde{\underline{\mathfrak{c}}}}=0
and only the first open path
\underline{\mathfrak{c}}
may have a non-zero contribution to
R_{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}}
.

Note that
\underline{\mathfrak{c}}=\underline{\mathfrak{s}}_{\mathbf{y}}
. The computation of
R_{\mathbf{x},\underline{\mathfrak{c}}}
is similar to the previous case:
T(\underline{\mathfrak{c}})=\{0,1\}
and we compute that
R^{loc,1}_{\mathbf{x},\underline{\mathfrak{c}}}=1
. At time
t=0
, the computation of
w^{-}_{\mathbf{x},0}
and
w^{\infty}_{\underline{\mathfrak{c}},0}
is slightly different: we are in a specific case of
[
26
, Proposition 3.20.1]
, so we have
\ell(s_{\beta}v^{-1}w)=\ell(v^{-1}w)+1
(where, recall,
\mathbf{y}=\varpi^{v\lambda}w
and
\mathbf{x}=s_{v(\beta)[0]}\mathbf{y}
). The decorating alcove
D^{-}_{\mathbf{y},1}
dominates
\mathfrak{s}_{v\lambda,-}(1)
hence is of the form
a(-v\lambda,+v\widetilde{w})
for some
\widetilde{w}\in W_{\lambda}
, and since it is the projection of
C_{\mathbf{y}}=a(-v\lambda,w)
on this segment germ, any minimal gallery from an alcove dominating it to
C_{\mathbf{y}}
goes through
D^{-}_{\mathbf{y},1}
. Therefore

\displaystyle\ell(v^{-1}w)=\ell(d^{+}(C^{++}_{-v\lambda},C_{\mathbf{y}}))

\displaystyle=\ell(d^{+}(C^{++}_{-v\lambda},D^{-}_{\mathbf{y},1}))+\ell(d^{+}(D^{-}_{\mathbf{y},1},C_{\mathbf{y}}))

(4.22)

\displaystyle=\ell(\widetilde{w})+\ell(d^{+}(D^{-}_{\mathbf{y},1},C_{\mathbf{y}})).

(4.23)

Similarly for
\underline{\mathfrak{s}}_{\mathbf{x}}
, since
D^{-}_{\mathbf{x},1}=s_{v(\beta)[0]}D^{-}_{\mathbf{y},1}=a(-v\lambda,vs_{\beta}\widetilde{w})
and by

invariance of

, we have

\displaystyle\ell(s_{\beta}v^{-1}w)=\ell(d^{+}(C^{++}_{-v\lambda},C_{\mathbf{x}}))

\displaystyle=\ell(d^{+}(C^{++}_{-v\lambda},D^{-}_{\mathbf{x},1}))+\ell(d^{+}(D^{-}_{\mathbf{x},1},C_{\mathbf{x}}))

(4.24)

\displaystyle=\ell(s_{\beta}\widetilde{w})+\ell(d^{+}(D^{-}_{\mathbf{y},1},C_{\mathbf{y}})).

(4.25)

Hence from
\ell(s_{\beta}v^{-1}w)=\ell(v^{-1}\beta)+1
we deduce that
\ell(s_{\beta}\widetilde{w})=\ell(\widetilde{w})+1
, or equivalently that
\ell(s_{\beta}\widetilde{w}w_{\lambda,0})=\ell(w_{\lambda,0})-\ell(s_{\beta}\widetilde{w})=\ell(w_{\lambda,0})-\ell(\widetilde{w})-1=\ell(\widetilde{w}w_{\lambda,0})-1
.
Moreover
D^{+}_{\mathbf{y},0}=a(0_{\mathbb{A}},-v\widetilde{w}w_{\lambda,0})
and
D^{+}_{\mathbf{x},0}=a(0_{\mathbb{A}},-vs_{\beta}\widetilde{w}w_{\lambda,0})

w^{\infty}_{\underline{\mathfrak{c}},0}=v\widetilde{w}w_{\lambda,0}
covers
w^{-}_{\mathbf{x},0}=vs_{\beta}\widetilde{w}w_{\lambda,0}
and we conclude that that
R_{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}}=R^{loc,0}_{\mathbf{x},\underline{\mathfrak{c}}}=X-1
exactly as in case 2.

If
n\in\{-1,\langle\lambda,\beta\rangle+1\}
, then by Lemma
4.33
the unique
C_{\infty}
-Hecke
\underline{\mathfrak{c}}
open path of type
\mathbf{x}
with end alcove
C_{\mathbf{y}}
verifies
T(\underline{\mathfrak{c}})=\{0,t_{0},1\}
with

and
R^{loc,t_{0}}_{\mathbf{x},\mathfrak{c}}=X-1
. Moreover since
\underline{\mathfrak{c}}
and
\underline{\mathfrak{s}}_{\mathbf{x}}
agree on
[0,t_{0})
and
R^{loc,0}_{\mathbf{x},\underline{\mathfrak{s}}_{\mathbf{x}}}=1
, we have
R^{loc,0}_{\mathbf{x},\underline{\mathfrak{c}}}=1
. It remains to verify that
R^{loc,1}_{\mathbf{x},\underline{\mathfrak{c}}}=1
. Since there is no folding at time
1
,
R^{loc,1}_{\mathbf{x},\underline{\mathfrak{c}}}=X^{l}
where
l
is the number of walls crossed away from
C^{\infty}_{-v\lambda}=a(-v\lambda,-v)
(or equivalently, towards
C^{++}_{-v\lambda}=a(-v\lambda,+v)
) in a given minimal gallery from
s_{v(\beta)[n]}D^{-}_{\mathbf{x},1}
to
C_{\mathbf{y}}
. Let
\mu=\mathrm{proj}^{Y^{+}}(\mathbf{x})\in\{v(\lambda+\beta^{\vee}),vs_{\beta}(\lambda+\beta^{\vee})\}
and let
\widetilde{v}
denote the minimal element such that
\mu=\widetilde{v}(\mu^{++})
, so
\widetilde{v}\in W^{\mu^{++}}
. Then
C^{++}_{-\mu}=a(-\mu,+\widetilde{v})
contains the segment germ
\mathfrak{s}_{\mu,-}(1)
, so a minimal gallery from this alcove to
C_{\mathbf{x}}
goes through
D^{-}_{\mathbf{x},1}=\mathrm{proj}_{\mathfrak{s}_{\mu,-}(1)}(C_{\mathbf{x}})
. Therefore, a minimal gallery from
s_{v(\beta)[n]}C^{++}_{-\mu}
to
C_{\mathbf{y}}=s_{v(\beta)[n]}C_{\mathbf{x}}

D^{-}_{\underline{\mathfrak{c}},1}=s_{v(\beta)[n]}D^{-}_{\mathbf{x},1}
. Moreover, by
[
26
, Proposition 3.20.3.d]
, a minimal gallery from

to

goes through
s_{v(\beta)[n]}C^{++}_{-\mu}

s_{v(\beta)[n]}D^{-}_{\mathbf{x},1}
. We deduce that a minimal gallery from
s_{v(\beta)[n]}D^{-}_{\mathbf{x},1}
to
C_{\mathbf{y}}
only crosses walls away from
C^{++}_{-v\lambda}
, hence

and
R^{loc,1}_{\mathbf{x},\underline{\mathfrak{c}}}=1
.

Therefore, in these cases aswell

, which concludes the proof.

Mechanotransduction through oxidative stress upregulates circLyst to drive vascular remodeling in hypertensive humans and rodents
Authors: Juanjuan Tan, Weiyi Hu, Li Shen, Biyang Luo, Huajin Liu, Feng Li, Hui Qin, Yuanwang Yu, Haifang Wang, Jing Dong, Qi Zhang, Hong Zhang, Fan Lv, Yingxin Qi, Kai Wang, Yi Yang, Zhiqiang Yan, Kai Huang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Aberrant cyclic stretch serves as a critical driver of vascular remodeling in hypertension, yet the underlying molecular mechanisms remain largely unknown. Here, we studied the role of a novel circRNA, termed circ_2465 (circLyst), in vascular remodeling in both human and rodent samples under high cyclic stretch. Next-generation sequencing data revealed that 165 circRNAs were differentially expressed in the thoracic aortas of spontaneously hypertensive rats (SHRs) compared with normotensive Wistar–Kyoto rats (WKYs), of which 60 circRNAs were significantly upregulated while 105 circRNAs were significantly downregulated. CircLyst, verified as a novel circRNA by ribonuclease R (RNase R) digestion and Actinomycin D assays, was induced in the thoracic aortas of SHRs and stably located in the cytoplasm as detected by fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH). This elevation was confirmed in hypertensive patients and was recapitulated in vascular smooth muscle cells (VSMCs) exposed to high cyclic stretch in vitro. Mechanistically, oxidative stress, mediated by NADPH oxidases NOX1 and NOX4, induced circLyst expression. CircLyst then functioned as a sponge for miR-298-5p, derepressing its target gene Col23a1, thereby promoting VSMC proliferation and migration—key events in vascular remodeling. Importantly, both systemic and VSMC-specific targeting of circLyst attenuated pathological remodeling and blunted hypertension in animal models. Collectively, our findings establish the oxidative-stress-driven circLyst/miR-298-5p/Col23a1 axis as a critical regulator of vascular remodeling and highlight circLyst as a potential therapeutic target for hypertensive vascular disease.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s43556-026-00593-3.

Introduction

Vascular remodeling, an important and common pathological event in cardiovascular diseases (CVDs), is the process of altering structure and arrangement in blood vessels through cell dysfunctions and production or degradation of the extracellular matrix (ECM) [1–3]. It has been established that vascular remodeling contributes to the development of a variety of vascular diseases including high blood pressure [4–7], atherosclerosis and restenosis [4, 8–10], aortic aneurysms [4, 11–13], neointima formation post vascular injury [14, 15] and vein graft failure [16–18].

Mechanical stress and oxidative stress contribute to cell function, dysfunction, and various processes, including remodeling, development, homeostasis, and aging [19–23]. In the cardiovascular system, mechanical stress primarily acts in three directions: shear stress, hydrostatic pressure, and cyclic stretch [24–26]. Abnormal cyclic stretch induced vascular remodeling in hypertension [5, 27, 28] and grafted vein [16, 29]. The primary role of NADPH oxidase (NOX) is to produce reactive oxygen species (ROS), making it one of the primary enzymes responsible for generating oxidative stress in the cardiovascular system [4]. NOXs derived oxidative stress played important roles in vascular remodeling during the progress of hypertension [4, 30], aortic aneurysms [4, 12, 13, 31] and atherosclerosis [4, 32, 33]. Nevertheless, the role of mechanical stress induced ROS in vascular remodeling of hypertension has remained incompletely understood.

Circular RNAs (circRNAs) are covalently closed single-stranded RNAs (ssRNAs) in eukaryotes with tissue- and cell-specific expression patterns [34, 35]. Most circRNAs are produced with a 3′,5′-phosphodiester bond between the back-spliced exons by a non-canonical splicing event called backsplicing from middle exons of annotated genes [34, 35]. CircRNAs are found to modulate gene expression by regulating transcription and splicing, titrating microRNAs (miRNAs)/acting as miRNA sponges, interacting with proteins and acting as templates for the synthesis of polypeptides by a cap-independent translation manner through internal ribosome entry sites [34, 36]. Given that circRNAs exhibit greater stability than their cognate linear transcripts, possess unique structural conformations, and are often expressed in a disease-specific manner, they have drawn considerable attention in cardiovascular research [36, 37]. Emerging evidence indicates that circRNAs modulate cell death in cardiovascular diseases, contribute to vascular aging and aging-related vascular disorders, and hold promise as mechanistic regulators, diagnostic biomarkers, and therapeutic targets [38–40]. Accordingly, further studies are warranted to fully elucidate their clinical potential as both biomarkers and therapeutic agents in cardiovascular diseases.

In the present study, we aimed to investigate the functional role and molecular mechanisms of circRNA_2465 (circLyst) in vascular remodeling under pathological cyclic stretch, and to determine whether its modulation affects hypertensive vascular remodeling. We further sought to evaluate the potential of circLyst as a therapeutic candidate in this context.

Results

Cyclic stretch elevates circLyst in hypertensive humans and rats

To explore the potential role of circRNA in vascular remodeling during hypertension, the next-generation sequencing was applied to the thoracic aortas of SHRs and WKYs. Through sequencing, a total of 165 circRNAs displayed significant differential expression in SHRs compared with WKYs, of which 60 circRNAs were significantly upregulated, and 105 circRNAs were significantly downregulated (Fig. 1a). To validate the sequencing data, three upregulated and three downregulated circRNAs among the differentially expressed circRNAs were selected for qRT-PCR analysis filtered by the fold change, raw intensity and the length of circRNAs. The results showed that rno-circRNA_2465 exhibited the highest upregulation among the analyzed circRNAs (Fig. 1b). It was found to be upregulated by a remarkable 21.65-fold in the thoracic aorta of SHRs compared to WKYs quantified by qRT-PCR (Fig. 1b) and the sequence length of rno-circRNA_2465 was determined to be 2, 700 bp (Fig. 1c and Table S1). According to NCBI BLAST, the sequence of rno-circRNA_2465 was matched with the rattus norvegicus Lyst gene. Therefore, we named rno-circRNA_2465 as circLyst and explored its role in vascular remodeling of hypertension. To compare the stability of circLyst and linear transcripts, rat VSMCs were treated with the transcriptional inhibitor actinomycin D. The expression of circLyst dropped slightly at 24 h (< 20%) while the expression of linear Lyst mRNA markedly declined over time (approximately 70% at 24 h) (Fig. 1d). Besides, the total RNAs extract from VSMCs was treated with RNase R (an exoribonuclease that digests linear RNAs but preserves circRNAs). The reduction of circLyst was also less pronounced than that of Lyst (Fig. 1e). The results indicated that circLyst had a longer half-life and was more stable than Lyst mRNA. Fluorescent in situ hybridization (FISH) assay confirmed robust expression of cytoplasmic circLyst in VSMC (Fig. 1f). In addition, FISH data further verified that circLyst was highly expressed in the cytoplasm of the aorta from SHR compared with the aorta of WKY (Fig. 1g). Then, the expression of circLyst was detected in blood samples of hypertensive and normotensive human. The expression of circLyst was significantly upregulated in hypertension (Fig. 1h). Of note, linear correlation between circLyst expression and blood pressure was calculated. Blood vessels are exposed to mechanical environment due to the contraction and relaxation of the heart, the mechanical forces (Fig. 1j) are mainly in three directions: shear stress, hydrostatic pressure and cyclic stretch [24, 25]. Others and we have shown that abnormal cyclic stretch plays a vital role in vascular remodeling [24, 25, 27, 28, 41]. Therefore, the expression of circLyst in VSMCs was determined subjected to the normal level (5%) of cyclic stretch and a high level (15%) of cyclic stretch using FX-5000 T Strain Unit (Fig. 1k). Compared with 5% cyclic stretch, 15% cyclic stretch increased the mRNA level of circLyst after 24-h application in rat VSMCs (Fig. 1l). The expression of circLyst was also detected in immortalized human ASMCs. CircLyst was increased significantly after 6 h and 24 h of 15% cyclic stretch (Fig. 1m). Similar result was confirmed in primary human ASMCs, where circLyst increased significantly after 6 h and 24 h under the condition of 15% cyclic stretch compared to the 5% cyclic stretch (Fig. 1n).

These findings suggest that circLyst was elevated by high cyclic stretch in hypertension both in human and rat.

ROS induced by high cyclic stretch promote circLyst expression

Reactive oxygen species (ROS) play important roles in mechanical stress induced mechanotransduction and vascular remodeling [24, 25, 42]. To explore the potential role of ROS in modulating non‑coding RNAs, we performed Ingenuity Pathway Analysis (IPA), a widely used tool for interpreting omics data in a biological context. IPA data indicated that there is crosstalk between reactive oxygen species (ROS) and non-coding RNAs (Fig. 2a). ROS regulate the expression of miRNAs such as mir-15, mir-21, mir-27, and mir-210, as well as circRNAs including circRNA-NFATC3 (Fig. 2a). Therefore, we investigated whether cyclic stretch induced ROS modulate circLyst expression. Dihydroethidium (DHE) staining was used to detect ROS production, and excessive ROS production was found in the aortas of SHRs compared to WKYs (Fig. 2b, c). Consistently, abdominal aorta coarctation induced hypertension also elevated aortic ROS production compared to sham control (Fig. 2d, e). Since NADPH oxidases (NOXs) are the main enzymatic sources of ROS during cardiovascular pathogenesis [4, 43], we detected NOX1, NOX2 and NOX4 expression in SHRs. NOX1 and NOX4, but not NOX2, were elevated in SHRs compared to WKYs (Fig. 2h-k). Similarly, NOX1 and NOX4 were increased while NOX2 was decreased in abdominal aorta coarctation induced hypertension (Fig. 2l-o). Of note, circLyst expression was significantly increased after H2O2 treatment for 24 h (Fig. 2p). To further explore the mediatory role of NOX1/4 in mechanical stretch-induced circLyst upregulation, VSMCs were transfected with specific siRNAs to knock down NOX1 and NOX4, respectively. Individual knockdown of NOX1 (Fig. 2q) and NOX4 (Fig. 2r) both significantly attenuated the stretch-induced upregulation of circLyst, confirming that NOX-ROS signaling is required for circLyst induction under high cyclic stretch.

Collectively, these data indicate that, in hypertension, high cyclic stretch activates NOX1 and NOX4 to generate ROS, thereby promoting circLyst expression.

Silencing circLyst inhibits the proliferation and migration of vascular smooth muscle cells (VSMCs) and mesenteric smooth muscle cells (MSMCs)

To further evaluate the effects of circLyst on regulating VSMC function, circLyst-specific siRNAs were transfected into rat VSMCs (Fig. 3a). The siRNAs, especially siRNA1 robustly decreased the expression of circLyst. Then, we used siRNA1 to further validate the function of circLyst in VSMCs. The knockdown of circLyst significantly reduced the proliferation of VSMCs as evaluated by CCK-8 assay (Fig. 3b) and EdU assay (Fig. 3c, d). Meanwhile, we examined the effects of circLyst on cell migration using scratch wound healing assay and transwell assay. As shown in Fig. 3e, f, scratch wound healing assay showed that circLyst siRNA1 markedly decreased VSMC migration compared to siRNA-NC. Similarly, the transwell assay (Fig. 3g, h) also confirmed that VSMC migration was reduced by circLyst siRNA1 compared to siRNA-NC. Besides, the roles of circLyst in MSMCs were also investigated. CircLyst-specific siRNA1 were successfully transfected into rat MSMCs (Fig. 3i). circLyst siRNAs significantly reduced the proliferation of MSMCs as detected by CCK-8 assay (Fig. 3j) and EdU assay (Fig. 3k, l). Furthermore, scratch wound healing assay and transwell assay were performed to explore the effect of circLyst on MSMC migration. These results imply that circLyst knockdown alleviated the proliferation and migration of both VSMCs and MSMCs.

CircLyst regulates VSMC and MSMC proliferation and migration by sponging miR-298-5p

In cardiovascular system, acting as microRNA or protein inhibitors ('sponges') is an effective way for many circRNAs to exert their vital biological functions [34, 44, 45]. To explore if circLyst acts as a sponge for miRNAs, the miRNAs binding to circLyst were predicted using TargetScan, miRanda and RNAHybrid. It was found that miR-298-5p was the most likely potential target for circLyst since circLyst has four potential binding sites for miR-298-5p (Fig. 4a). miR-298-5p was upregulated both in VSMCs (Fig. 4b) and MSMCs (Fig. 4c) after transfection with circLyst siRNA1. CircLyst ablation reduces vascular remodeling

To explore the role of circLyst in vascular remodeling in vivo, the recombinant lentivirus vector-expressing circLyst shRNA (LV-shRNA-circLyst) or negative control shRNA viruses (LV-shRNA-NC) were constructed. Then, LV-shRNA-circLyst or LV-shRNA-NC were delivered to the arteries of the SHRs as previously described [46]. CircLyst was effectively inhibited in arteries by LV-shRNA-circLyst compared to LV-shRNA-NC four weeks after transfection (Fig. 7a). Discussion

In the current study, we have identified a novel circRNA, termed circ_2465 (circLyst), and suggest for the first time that circLyst may serve as a regulator of vascular remodeling in humans and rodents. High cyclic stretch-induced ROS appear to promote the circLyst/miR-298-5p axis in vascular remodeling during hypertension. Additionally, circLyst was also found to be elevated in the plasma of hypertensive patients, and high cyclic stretch-induced ROS may be an important contributor in this process. Notably, inhibition of circLyst, either globally or specifically in VSMCs, shows potential therapeutic efficacy by attenuating vascular remodeling and improving hypertensive phenotypes. Taken together, our findings point to a potential role for the circLyst/miR-298-5p axis in oxidative stress-induced vascular remodeling under cyclic stretch. Targeting circLyst could represent a candidate strategy for hypertension management. However, the present study provides initial evidence that plasma circLyst is elevated in hypertensive patients based on a pilot cohort of 28 subjects (17 controls and 11 patients). Therefore, independent validation in a larger, multicenter, prospective cohort is warranted before considering clinical application of circLyst.

MicroRNAs (miRNAs), long noncoding RNAs (lncRNAs), and circular RNAs (circRNAs) are the three mains noncoding RNAs (ncRNAs) that emerging as relevant contributors to cardiovascular diseases [47]. Others and we have shown that mechanical stress is an important stimulus in vascular remodeling in hypertension [28, 48–51], miRNAs and lncRNAs were involved in this process [5, 28, 48, 52, 53]. We have investigated the roles of abnormal cyclic stretch and vascular injury in vascular remodeling and dysfunction [41, 54, 55] and proved that high cyclic stretch modulated miRNAs including miR-124-3p [5], miR-27a [28] and miR-142–3p [48] are robust regulators of vascular remodeling in hypertension. LncRNA XR007793 was found to regulate cyclic stretch-induced proliferation and migration of VSMCs in hypertension [52]. However, the role and mechanism of mechanical stress regulated circRNA in hypertension has not been reported yet. Here, we have identified a mechanosensitive circRNA, circLyst, in both hypertensive rats and humans. Our data suggest that targeting circLyst might be effective in attenuating vascular remodeling and hypertension in rodent models. Hence, inhibition of circLyst could potentially serve as a candidate strategy for managing hypertension in humans, though further validation is required.

Several studies have shown that circRNAs play important roles in vascular diseases [45, 56]. Circ_Lrp6, a circRNA enriched in VSMCs, acts as a sponge regulating miRNA-145 function [56]. The differential expression of miR-145 and circ_Lrp6 in murine and human vascular diseases implied that the imbalance between the bound and unbound forms of circ_Lrp6 to miR-145 could potentially contribute to vascular pathogenesis [56]. Zeng et al. discovered that circular RNA circMAP3K5 acts as a microRNA-22-3p sponge, promoting the resolution of intimal hyperplasia through TET2-mediated smooth muscle cell differentiation [45]. CircRNA Galntl6 was found to be a novel factor that attenuate stress-induced hypertension, and the circRNA Galntl6/miR-335/Lig3 axis played a vital role in this process [57]. CircEsyt2 was proved to directly interact with polyC-binding protein 1 to regulate VSMCs via splicing regulation in atherosclerotic mouse models [58]. Besides, circKrt4 [59], circSMOC1 [60] and Circ-Calm4 [61] regulate pulmonary vascular remodeling in pulmonary arterial hypertension while lower circGSAP [62] level is associated with the development of idiopathic pulmonary arterial hypertension and worse outcomes. However, the role of cyclic stretch in modulating circRNAs in hypertension was not clarified yet. Here, we have identified a novel circRNA, circLyst, that appears to respond to cyclic stretch in both rat and human VSMCs in vitro. In addition, cyclic stretch-induced oxidative stress may contribute to the upregulation of circLyst, which might subsequently activate miR-298-5p in hypertensive subjects. Of note, circLyst appears to have a relatively high stability compared with mRNA, and its modulation might be more feasible than directly manipulating cyclic stretch or oxidative stress. Taken together, circLyst may be considered a candidate target for further investigation in vascular remodeling and hypertension.

Excessive ROS have been proved to be an important mediator in endothelial dysfunction and vascular remodeling [43, 63–66]. Mounting evidence demonstrates that aberrant shear stress and heightened cyclic stretch trigger ROS accumulation via activating different ROS enzymatic sources. In the context of ventilator-induced lung injury, high cyclic stretch triggers a NOX2-specific (vs. NOX1/NOX4) response, culminating in lung injury via downstream activation of the ROS/CaMKII/ERK1/2 signaling axis [23, 64]. In cell-based models of atherosclerosis, exposure to cyclic stretch (15% strain at 0.05 Hz) upregulated both NOX1 expression and ROS production in VSMCs [67]. In turn, oxidative stress produced by NOX can also impair exercise capacity. Endothelial NOX1, but not NOX2 or NOX4, triggers mitochondrial dysfunction in skeletal muscle, compromising both exercise performance and energy metabolism, and consequently fostering the development of obesity and metabolic syndrome [68]. Here, we demonstrate that NOX1 and NOX4—but not NOX2—are upregulated in spontaneously hypertensive rats (SHR) and in abdominal aorta coarctation–induced hypertension, relative to their respective normotensive controls. CircLyst was also elevated by cyclic stretch-induced ROS in this process. DHE staining was used to semi-quantitative measure of overall ROS production in this study. However, this method lacks the specificity to differentiate between O₂⁻ and H₂O₂, and it also fails to quantitatively establish the dose–response relationship with circLyst expression. The quantitative link between ROS levels and circLyst expression warrants further investigation.

In conclusion, our findings point to a novel circRNA, circLyst, that appears to participate in the regulation of VSMC proliferation and migration, possibly through miR-298-5p mediated by oxidative stress under cyclic stretch. These observations might provide additional insights into the molecular basis of mechanotransduction in hypertensive vascular remodeling. Of note, circLyst could be considered a candidate for further exploration in the context of preventing vascular remodeling and hypertension.

Materials and methods

Rat models of hypertension and in vivo blood pressure monitoring

The spontaneously hypertensive rats (SHRs) and normotensive Wistar–Kyoto rats (WKYs) (12 weeks old, 250–300 g) were purchased from Beijing Vital River Laboratory Animal Technology Company (Beijing, China), and Sprague Dawley (SD) rats were purchased from Shanghai Laboratory Animal Research Center (Shanghai, China). Hypertensive rat model with abdominal aortic coarctation was generated as previously described [25]. Briefly, male SD rats (200 ± 20 g) were randomly assigned to sham-operated or abdominal aorta-constriction group. After anesthesia with isoflurane inhalation, the abdominal cavity was carefully incised to facilitate the separation of the abdominal aorta. At approximately 5 mm superior to the bifurcation of the right renal artery, the aorta was tied with a 21-gauge needle using a 3–0 surgical suture, then the needle was retrieved to create the aortic constriction with a diameter of about 0.9 mm. For sham-operated groups, the same procedure was operated but without constriction. Blood pressure was measured two weeks later in both groups by inserting a catheter into the common carotid arteries. Systolic blood pressure of SHRs and WKYs was recorded prior to the experiment and once a week using tail-cuff plethysmography via the BP-2010A apparatus (Softron Biotechnology Ltd, Beijing, China). The Blood pressure was averaged from six successive measurements.

All animals were housed in a temperature-controlled room with a 12-h light/dark cycle and free access to standard chow and water. All experimental protocols were approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee of Shanghai Jiao Tong University (Shanghai, China) and the Ethics Committees of Shanghai Jiao Tong University, and all experiments were performed in accordance with the recommendations of the Animal Management Rules of China (Documentation 55, 2001, Ministry of Health, China).

The collection of human blood samples and aortic samples

Human blood samples were collected to detect the expression of circLyst, human aortic samples were harvested to detect Col23a1 expression.

This study included blood samples from hypertensive patients at the Third Hospital of Changsha, Changsha City, Hunan Province, China, and the Second Affiliated Hospital of Shaanxi University of Chinese Medicine, Xianyang City, Shaanxi Province, China, collected from July 2023 to March 2024 (Table S2). The study subjects were patients diagnosed with primary hypertension, characterized by a blood pressure of 140/90 mmHg or higher. No patients with secondary hypertension (i.e., caused by renal, endocrine, vascular, or cerebrovascular diseases) were included in this study. The control group consisted of healthy volunteers with normal blood pressure from Xianyang, Shaanxi, and Changsha, Hunan, China. Peripheral venous blood samples were collected after an overnight fast of 12 h and were immediately placed into an RNA preservation reagent (Genestonebio, Jiangsu, China). Differential hsa-circLyst expression was analyzed in the peripheral blood samples from hypertensive patients and the control group by qRT-PCR as mentioned above. This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Third Hospital of Changsha, Changsha, Hunan Province, China (no. KY-EC-2023–009) and the Second Affiliated Hospital of Shaanxi University of Traditional Chinese Medicine, Xianyang, Shaanxi Province, China (no. SZEFYIEC-KYPJ-2024001–2).

Human hypertensive and normotensive arterial samples were collected from patients with nephroblastoma. These patients underwent kidney tumor removal surgery at Shanghai Xinhua Hospital, and renal artery specimens were preserved thereafter. The project involving human hypertensive artery sample and normotensive control sample was approved by the Ethics Committee of Shanghai Xinhua Hospital, Shanghai Jiao Tong University, School of Medicine (approval ID: XHEC-D-2024–002).

Detection of circRNA by next-generation sequencing and quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR)

Next-generation sequencing was performed at an Illumina Hiseq™ 2500 platform, Oebiotech Institute (China). CIRI software was used to predict circRNAs [70]. Differentially expressed genes, including circRNAs and mRNAs, in the SHRs and WKYs, were analyzed utilizing DESeq with the following criteria: fold change > 2 and p-value (p) < 0.05. The Miranda package was used to predict the miRNA target at the 3′UTR region of differentially expressed mRNAs and the full-length sequence of differentially expressed circRNAs [71]. Competing endogenous RNA (ceRNA) relationships were generated by a circRNA and mRNA pair affected by the same miRNA members.

The stability and half-life of circRNAs were tested by RNase R and actinomycin D treatment assays. Total RNA (1 μg) was incubated with RNase R (2 U; Epicenter, Madison, WI, USA) at 37 °C for 10 min, and the reaction was stopped at 70 °C for 10 min. Then, circRNA and the corresponding linear mRNA were detected by qRT-PCR (PCR primer sequences of circRNAs and mRNA were shown in Table S3). 2 μmol/mL Actinomycin D (MilliporeSigma, Burlington, MA, USA) or DMSO was added to the medium of VSMCs. At different intervals, circRNA and the corresponding linear mRNA were detected by qRT-PCR.

Cyclic stretch application

Cyclic stretch was performed as we previously described [5, 16, 22, 28, 41, 69]. Cells were plated on gelatin-coated flexible silicone bottom plates (Flexcell International, USA) at a density of 3 × 105 cells per well (9.32 cm2). Twenty-four hours after seeding, VSMCs were incubated with DMEM for 24 h to arrest growth and synchronize the cells. As shown in Fig. 1K, the cells were subjected to cyclic stretch, which was produced by a computer-controlled vacuum (FX-5000 T Strain Unit, Flexcell International, USA). The following mechanical parameters were applied: (i) a stretch magnitude of 5% to mimic normal arterial mechanical condition or 15% to mimic hypertensive mechanical condition, (ii) a frequency of 1.25 Hz, and (iii) a duration of 6 h or 24 h.

Ingenuity Pathway Analysis (IPA)

The crosstalk between reactive oxygen species (ROS) and non-coding RNAs including circRNAs and miRNAs were analyzed by QIAGEN Ingenuity Pathway Analysis (QIAGEN IPA, content version: 111,725,566, https://digitalinsights.qiagen.com/products-overview/discovery-insights-portfolio/analysis-and-visualization/qiagen-ipa/).

Cell transfection

The siRNAs targeting the back-splicing junction of circLyst were designed and synthesized by Gene Pharma (Shanghai, China), the sequences of circLyst siRNAs and siRNA negative control (NC) were shown in Table S4. The mimics and inhibitors of miR-298-5p were synthesized by RiboBio (Guangzhou, China), the sequences of miR-298-5p mimics and inhibitors and their respective NC were shown in Table S5. Oligonucleotides were transfected into VSMCs respectively with Lipofectamine 3000 following the manufacturer's protocol. After 48 h, cells were harvested and lysed for qRT-PCR or western blot analysis.

Knockdown circLyst in SHR

The recombinant RNAi plasmids were constructed by GenePharma (China). The lentiviral particles LV-shRNA-circLyst and LV-shRNA-NC were packaged using GM easy™ lentiviral Packaging Kit (Genomeditech, China). An established method was used for local delivery of the shRNA of circLyst lentivirus (LV-shRNA-circLyst) and negative control (LV-shRNA-NC) into aortas with Pluronic F-127 gel (Sigma, USA) as previously described. Rats were randomly divided into three groups including the LV-shRNA-circLyst-treated group, the LV-shRNA-NC-treated group, and the mock group. The sequences of circ_2465 shRNA and shNC were shown in Table S6. The aortas were collected 4 weeks after surgery.

RNAi-custom-adeno-associated virus called AAV9-circ_2465 (circLyst)-RNAi (P24M0086) was constructed by Shanghai Genechem Co., Ltd for specific knockdown of VSMC circLyst (AAV-RNAi-circLyst). Following the package, AAV-9 was administrated via intravenous injection on day 1 (30 μL, 4.45E + 12 v.g. mL− 1). Three weeks later, the rats were sacrificed and the vessels were harvested for further detection.

RNA fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH)

Fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH) was employed to confirm the cytoplasmic localization of circLyst in VSMCs, to verify its elevated expression in hypertensive models, to demonstrate its co-localization with miR-298-5p, and to validate the knockdown efficacy of both LV-shRNA-circLyst and AAV-RNAi-circLyst in vivo.

The specific probes targeting circLyst, miR-298-5p, or α-actin were designed and synthesized by Shanghai GenePharma Biotechnology Co., Ltd. Following the instructions of the RNA FISH kit, their expression in both cells and paraffin-embedded tissue sections was detected using the "SA-Biotin Amplification System for Cell Climbing Slides" and "SA-Biotin Amplification System for Paraffin Sections" kits (GenePharma, Shanghai, China), respectively. Briefly, cells were seeded into 12-well confocal dish, while paraffin sections were processed with eco-friendly dewaxing solution prior to experimentation. For double FISH assay, cell slides or paraffin-embedded tissue sections were hybridized with circLyst probe (cy3-labelled) and miR-298-5p probe (FAM-labelled), or circLyst probe (cy3-labelled) and α-actin probe (FAM-labelled) in a ratio of 8 μl probe and 2 μl SA at 37 °C overnight. Nuclei were stained with 4,6-diamidino-2-phenylindole (DAPI). The probe sequence complementary to the junction part of circLyst was 5’-AACCTTCAAGAAGGGTTTGTGGGGA-3’, and the probe sequence complementary to miR-298-5p was 5’-GGGAAGAACAGCCCTCCTCTGCC-3’. α-SMA-rat probe was 5'-AAAACAGCCCTGGGAGCATCATC-3', 5'-GTCCAGCACAATACCAGTTGTACGT-3', and 5'-CAGCAGTAG + TCACGAAGGAATAGCC-3'. The amplification of the signal was based on the SA-Cy3 or FAM system. All procedures were conducted in a dark environment to prevent photobleaching. Images were captured by Leica Steliaris 5 (Leica, Germany).

Dual-luciferase reporter assay

Dual-luciferase reporter assays were carried out to assess the binding between circLyst and miR-298-5p, and between miR-298-5p and col23a1, respectively. The Wild-type (WT) circLyst or the 3′ untranslated region (3′ UTR) of col23a1 containing the miR-298-5p binding site was cloned into the pmirGLO vector (Ribio, Guangzhou, China), designated as circLyst-WT-Report or col23a1-WT-Report. Site-directed mutation of circDcbld1 or the 3’ UTR of col23a1 was performed by the Quick Mutation site-directed mutagenesis kit (Beyotime Biotechnology, Shanghai, China). The mutated sequences were also cloned into the pmirGLO vector and named as circLyst-Mut-Report or col23a1-Mut-Report. The PCR primer sequences for Col23a1-WT, Col23a1-mutation (MUT), Circ2465-WT and Circ2465-MUT were shown in Table S7. HEK-293 T cells were co-transfected with the reporter plasmid or the mutated vectors with miR-298-5p mimics or negative control (NC). After 48 h, firefly and renilla luciferase activities were measured using the Dual-luciferase reporter assay system (Promega, USA).

Bioinformatics analysis

MiRanda (http://www.microrna.org), TargetScan (http://www.targetscan.org) and RNAHybrid (https://bio.tools/rnahybrid) were used to predict the target miRNAs of circLyst. The miRNAs were ranked based on the potential binding affinity to circLyst. The target genes of the miRNA were also predicted and annotated by miRanda and TargetScan.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were conducted using GraphPad Prism 8 (GraphPad Software, San Diego, CA, USA). The data were reported as mean ± standard deviation (mean ± SD), with each experiment repeated at least three biological replicates. Normality of the data was assessed using the Shapiro–Wilk test. If the P-value from the Shapiro–Wilk test is greater than 0.05, the data is normally distributed, and parametric tests can be used. For comparisons between two groups, a paired t-test was employed for paired or matched data, while an F-test was conducted for unpaired experimental groups to assess variance equality. An unpaired t-test was used for data with equal variances, and an unpaired t-test with Welch's correction was employed for data with unequal variances. For comparisons among three or more groups, repeated measures one-way ANOVA was used followed by Tukey's post-hoc test for measurements on the same subjects across different conditions or time points. The Brown-Forsythe test assessed variance equality among unpaired experimental groups. If variances are equal, perform one-way ANOVA followed by Tukey’s test for comparing group means. If variances are unequal, conduct Brown-Forsythe and Welch ANOVA tests to adjust for uneven variances followed by Dunnett's T3 multiple comparisons test. In correlation analysis, the normality of the data was assessed, and Pearson's correlation coefficient (r) was used for normally distributed data, while Spearman's rank correlation coefficient (ρ) was employed for non-normally distributed data. The Mann–Whitney test was used for comparisons between two groups. Statistically significant differences were considered at p < 0.05, with *, **, and *** representing p values < 0.05, 0.01, and 0.001, respectively.

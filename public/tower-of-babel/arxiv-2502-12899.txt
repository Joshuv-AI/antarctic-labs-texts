A plumbing-multiplicative function from the Links-Gould invariant
Daniel López Neumann and Roland van der Veen

Abstract
. We prove that the Laurent polynomial in ℤ ⁡ [ q ± 1 ] \mathbb{Z}[q^{\pm 1}] that is the top coefficient of the Links-Gould invariant of the boundary of a Seifert surface is multiplicative under plumbing of surfaces. We deduce that the Links-Gould invariant of a fibred link in S 3 S^{3} is ℤ ⁡ [ q ± 1 ] \mathbb{Z}[q^{\pm 1}] -monic. As a purely topological application, we deduce a “plumbing-uniqueness” statement for links that bound surfaces obtained by plumbing/deplumbing unknotted twisted annuli as well as providing an obstruction for links to bound such surfaces.



1.
Introduction

Quantum knot invariants are quite powerful and have many intriguing algebraic properties, but it remains a challenge to apply them to answer actual topological questions. In this paper, we make progress in this direction by applying the Links-Gould invariant of links to questions on Seifert surfaces.

The Links-Gould invariant of a link
L\subset S^{3}
is a two-variable Laurent polynomial
LG(L;p,q)\in\mathbb{Z}[q^{\pm 1},p^{\pm 1}]

[
22
]
. It can be considered as a
q
-deformation of the Alexander polynomial, as
LG(L;ix,i)=\Delta_{L}(x^{2})

[
14
]
. It belongs to the class of “non-semisimple” quantum link invariants
[
10
,
4
]
, in the sense that it is a special case of the Reshetikhin-Turaev construction
[
27
]
applied to a non-semisimple braided category: the category of modules over
U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}(2|1))
with
generic
q
. It is the simplest link polynomial in such class with generic
q
, other non-semisimple invariants
[
1
,
4
]
require
q
to be a root of unity.

Very recently, it was shown that the Links-Gould invariant satisfies a genus bound
[
21
]
. This result belongs to a line of recent results stating that geometric properties of the Alexander polynomial generalize to non-semisimple quantum invariants, see
[
23
,
24
,
25
]
. In this paper, we consider the well-known fact that the top coefficient of the Alexander polynomial is multiplicative under plumbing of surfaces (see Section
2
for the definition of plumbing) and that such coefficient is
\pm 1
for fibred links. We prove that both properties generalize to the Links-Gould invariant and we give some topological applications.

Let
\mathrm{top}(L,q)\in\mathbb{Z}[q^{\pm 1}]
be the coefficient of
p^{1-\chi(\Sigma)}
in
LG(L;p,q)
, where
\Sigma
is a minimal genus Seifert surface for the link
L
. Note that no power of
p
higher than
p^{1-\chi(\Sigma)}
can appear in
LG(L;p,q)
by Theorem
3.3
(a generalization of
[
21
]
), so that the name
\mathrm{top}(L,q)
is appropriate. This top coefficient is allowed to be zero. Our main theorem is the following:

Theorem 1
.

If
\Sigma
is a surface obtained by plumbing surfaces
\Sigma_{1}
and
\Sigma_{2}
then

\mathrm{top}(\partial\Sigma,q)=\mathrm{top}(\partial\Sigma_{1},q)\cdot\mathrm{top}(\partial\Sigma_{2},q).

In the remainder of this introduction we will describe various corollaries of this theorem. The first deals with the form of the Links-Gould invariant of fibred links while the other corollaries are purely topological in nature. Let
L\subset S^{3}
be a fibred link with fiber surface
F
. The plane field tangent to the fibers can be extended to a plane field
\xi_{F}
on
S^{3}
(see
[
11
]
or
[
25
]
) and its homotopy class is determined by its
Hopf invariant

\lambda(\xi_{F})\in\mathbb{Z}
. Note that
\text{deg}_{p}LG(L;p,q)=2(1-\chi(F))
follows from the results of
[
21
,
14
]
.

Corollary 1
.

If
L
is fibred with fiber
F
, then

\mathrm{top}(L,q)=q^{1-\chi(F)-2\lambda(\xi_{F})}.

In particular,
LG(L;p,q)
detects the Hopf invariant of
\xi_{F}
.

That the top coefficient of
LG(L;p,q)
of a fibred link was a power of
q
had been conjectured by Kohli in 2016
[
18
]
(without the explicit expression in terms of the Hopf invariant). Corollary
1
is inspired by a similar theorem for Akutsu-Deguchi-Ohtsuki (ADO) invariants of fibred links
[
25
]
. We deduce Corollary
1
from Theorem
1
using a deep theorem of Giroux-Goodman
[
11
]
: any fiber surface in
S^{3}
is obtained by plumbing/deplumbing unknotted annuli with a full
\pm 1
-twist. The proof of Theorem
1
relies solely on the genus bound of
[
21
]
and the cubic skein relation of the Links-Gould invariant
[
13
]
. Note that the Kauffman polynomial (a “semisimple” invariant) also has a cubic skein relation, but lacks a genus bound, hence it cannot have properties as those of
LG(L;p,q)
shown in the present paper.

1.1.
Topological applications

In light of the aforementioned theorem of Giroux-Goodman, we decided to study the class of links that bound surfaces obtained by plumbing/deplumbing unknotted annuli with an arbitrary non-zero
1
1

1

The non-zero twists condition guarantees that such surfaces are genus-minimizing
[
8
]
.
number of full twists, which we call
annular
surfaces. If
A_{n}
is an unknotted annulus with
-n
full twists (see Figure
2
), we show that
\mathrm{top}(\partial A_{n},q)=a_{n}(q)
where

a_{n}(q)=(2n-1)q+(2n-3)q^{3}+(2n-5)q^{5}+\dots+3q^{2n-3}+q^{2n-1}

for

and
a_{n}(q)=a_{-n}(q^{-1})
for
n<0

Together with Theorem

this implies:

Corollary 2
.

If
L
bounds an annular surface then, up to a power of
q
,
\mathrm{top}(L,q)
is a product of
a_{n}(q)
’s.

It seems particularly sensible to ask whether positive knots bound annular surfaces since
all
their minimal genus Seifert surfaces have free fundamental group by
[
26
]
(note that
\pi_{1}(S^{3}\setminus\Sigma)
is free for annular
\Sigma
). We show that many of them do not bound annular surfaces with the above criterion, see Section

for positive knots up to twelve crossings. Since positive knots are strongly quasi-positive (SQP)
[
30
]
, this also shows that there is no obvious analogue of the Giroux-Goodman theorem for SQP links (see Section
7
). Note that our criterion gives no information at
q=1
, indeed, the Alexander polynomial seems to give no obstruction to this problem.

We also obtain the following uniqueness result for links that bound annular surfaces.

Corollary 3

(Plumbing-uniqueness)
.

If a link bounds an annular surface obtained by plumbing the annuli
A_{n_{1}},\dots,A_{n_{k}}
and a surface obtained by plumbing
A_{n^{\prime}_{1}},\dots,A_{n^{\prime}_{r}}
, then
k=r
and, after reordering,
n_{i}=n^{\prime}_{i}
for all
i=1,\dots,k
. More generally, suppose
L
is a link that bounds an annular surface
\Sigma
obtained by plumbing
A_{n_{1}},\dots,A_{n_{k}}
and deplumbing
A_{m_{1}},\dots,A_{m_{\ell}}
:

(1)

If
m_{1},\dots,m_{i-1}\in\{\pm 1\}
and
m_{i},\dots,m_{l}\neq\pm 1
, then
m_{i},\dots,m_{l}
is a subsequence of
n_{1},\dots,n_{k}
(up to reordering).

(2)

If
L
bounds another annular surface
\Sigma^{\prime}
that is obtained by plumbing the annuli
A_{n^{\prime}_{1}},\dots,A_{n^{\prime}_{r}}
and deplumbing
A_{m^{\prime}_{1}},\dots,A_{m^{\prime}_{s}}
where
m^{\prime}_{1},\dots,m^{\prime}_{j-1}\in\{\pm 1\}
and
m^{\prime}_{j},\dots,m^{\prime}_{s}\neq\pm 1
, then the sequences
\{n_{1},\dots,n_{k}\}\setminus\{m_{i},\dots,m_{l}\}
and
\{n^{\prime}_{1},\dots,n^{\prime}_{r}\}\setminus\{m^{\prime}_{j},\dots,m^{\prime}_{s}\}
are equal up to reordering.

This result follows from Theorem
1
and the fact that the
a_{n}(q),n\neq 0
are pairwise coprime (up to a power of
q
), a non-obvious fact which we prove using an old theorem of Kakeya
[
16
]
. Note that when
n_{1},\dots,n_{k}
are pairwise coprime, the Alexander polynomial criterion (i.e. our main theorem at
q=1
) is enough to deduce this result. Corollary
3
can be restated by saying that the links
\partial A_{n},n\neq 0
are linearly independent in a certain Grothendieck group, see Remark
5.5
.

The above uniqueness can be observed for certain classes of links for which all minimal genus Seifert surfaces are known. For instance, in
[
12
]
, a list of all minimal genus Seifert surfaces of a 2-bridge knot is given. One can observe that all of them are obtained by doing different plumbings over the same collection of annuli. This even holds for the larger class of special arborescent links, as proved in
[
31
]
.

Note that (the hat version of) the top knot Floer homology group also has a plumbing-multiplicativity property
[
3
]
. The Floer homology result gives a criterion to obstruct a link to belong to the class of links obtained as Murasugi sums of alternating links. However, for any link
L
in this class (e.g. alternating or positive links), the main result of
[
3
]
cannot obstruct
L
from being a plumbing of annuli, as in Corollary
2
. This is because of the well-known fact that knot Floer homology of alternating links is determined by the Alexander polynomial and the signature
[
32
]
. It is even less clear whether Corollary
3
can be obtained with Floer homology techniques.

We expect Theorem
1
to generalize to Murasugi sum (as is the case for the Alexander polynomial and knot Floer homology). We also expect similar results to hold for other non-semisimple quantum invariants, such as those of Akutsu-Deguchi-Ohtsuki (ADO)
[
1
]
. However, in that case, the top coefficient would be an element of a cyclotomic ring instead of a Laurent polynomial ring. This seems to be a slight disadvantage, for instance, the
p
-th ADO invariant of a fibred link only recovers the Hopf invariant of
\xi_{F}
mod
2p

[
25
]
. In this respect, quantum supergroups seem to provide neater results than the usual quantum groups.

Genus bounds

For a polynomial

written as

f(p,q)=\sum_{k=r}^{s}f_{k}(q)p^{k}\text{ with }f_{k}(q)\in\mathbb{Z}[q^{\pm 1}],\ f_{s},f_{r}\neq 0,\ r\leq s

we use the notation
\text{deg}_{p}f:=s-r
and
\text{deg}_{p}^{+}:=s
. Note that
\text{deg}_{p}^{+}
satisfies
\text{deg}_{p}^{+}(f+g)\leq\max\{\text{deg}_{p}^{+}f,\text{deg}_{p}^{+}g\}
for any

.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\Sigma
be a compact oriented surface embedded in
S^{3}
with no closed components. Then

\text{deg}_{p}LG(\partial\Sigma;p,q)\leq 2(1-\chi(\Sigma)).

By the symmetry under
p\mapsto p^{-1}
this is equivalent to
\text{deg}_{p}^{+}LG(\partial\Sigma)\leq 1-\chi(\Sigma)
. Note that in
[
21
]
this result is proved for connected
\Sigma
with connected boundary. The argument there applies to general connected
\Sigma
as well: this is because the argument of
[
21
]
only uses that the surface is obtained by attaching 1-handles (say
l
1-handles) to a single disk, and proves that
\text{deg}_{p}LG(\partial\Sigma)\leq 2l
, which is the above theorem for connected
\Sigma
. If
\Sigma
is disconnected and formed from
k
disks by attaching
l
1-handles, the above theorem gives the improved bound
\text{deg}_{p}LG(\partial\Sigma)\leq 2(1-k+l)
instead of merely
\text{deg}_{p}LG(\partial\Sigma)\leq 2l
. We deduce this from the connected case and the cubic skein relation.

Proof Theorem
3.3
.

We proceed by induction on the number
k
of components of
\Sigma
. If
k=1
, this is the result of
[
21
]
. Let
\Sigma
be a surface with
k+1
components. Let
\Sigma_{0}
be obtained from
\Sigma
by joining two different components of
\Sigma
with a band. Let
\Sigma_{1}
be obtained from
\Sigma_{0}
by doing a full left-handed twist to that band and
\Sigma_{2}
be obtained by doing two full left-handed twists to the same band. Then, using (
3
), we get:

(4)

g(q,p)LG(\partial\Sigma)=qLG(\partial\Sigma_{0})-(q^{-1}+q)LG(\partial\Sigma_{1})+q^{-1}LG(\partial\Sigma_{2})

Note that
\Sigma_{2},\Sigma_{1},\Sigma_{0}
have the same Euler characteristic and

a,b>1
then clearly
d_{i}-1>(a-1)+(b-1)
. Hence, the minimum of the above set is achieved when each
d_{i}
is a prime divisor of
\alpha
. Now suppose
L
is annular. By part
(1)
of Corollary
3
,
\mathrm{top}(L,q)
has to be a product of
a_{n}(q)
’s (up to a power of
q
, but this does not affects
\text{deg}_{q}
). Write
\mathrm{top}(L,q)=q^{s}f(q)g(q)
where
f(q)=\prod_{i=1}^{r}a_{d^{\prime}_{i}}(q^{-1})
and
g(q)=\prod_{j=1}^{s}a_{c^{\prime}_{j}}(q)
, each
d^{\prime}_{i},c^{\prime}_{j}>0
and
s\in\mathbb{Z}
. Write
d_{i}=2d^{\prime}_{i}-1
and
c_{j}=2c^{\prime}_{j}-1
. Note that
g(q)
is monic in
q
with least coefficient
\prod_{j=1}^{s}c_{j}
, while
f(q)
is monic in
q^{-1}
with top coefficient
\prod_{i=1}^{r}d_{i}
. Hence,
\alpha=\prod_{i=1}^{r}d_{i}
and
\beta=\prod_{j=1}^{s}c_{j}
while the degree of
f(q)g(q)
is the sum

\sum_{i=1}^{r}(d_{i}-1)+\sum_{j=1}^{s}(c_{j}-1).

By (
15
), the minimum degree of
f(q)g(q)
subject to the constraint on top/least coefficient, is given by the prime factorizations of
\alpha
and
\beta
.

Remark 5.5
.

Corollary
3
can be restated in terms of the following Grothendieck group: let
K^{plumb}(\text{links})
be the free abelian group spanned by all isotopy classes
[L]
of links
L\subset S^{3}
with
\mathrm{top}(L,q)\neq 0
(e.g. alternating, fibred, positive links) modulo the relations
[\partial\Sigma]-[\partial\Sigma_{1}]-[\partial\Sigma_{2}]
for every compact, oriented surfaces
\Sigma,\Sigma_{1},\Sigma_{2}
in which
\Sigma
is a plumbing of
\Sigma_{1},\Sigma_{2}
(and the top coefficients of the Links-Gould invariants of
\Sigma,\Sigma_{1},\Sigma_{2}
are all non-zero). Theorem
1
implies that
\mathrm{top}(L,q)
defines a group homomorphism

K^{plumb}(\text{links})\to\mathbb{Q}(q^{\pm 1}).

It is easy to see that a family of links with pairwise coprime images in
\mathbb{Z}[q^{\pm 1}]
are linearly independent in
K^{plumb}(\text{links})
. Hence, we have shown that (the images of) the iterated Hopf links
H_{n}=\partial A_{n},n\neq 0
are linearly independent in
K^{plumb}(\text{links})
. This also implies Corollary
3
: if
L
bounds a surface obtained by plumbing
A_{n_{1}},\dots,A_{n_{k}}
then

[L]=[\partial A_{n_{1}}]+\dots+[\partial A_{n_{k}}]

in
K^{plumb}(\text{links})
(and similarly if deplumbing is allowed), so plumbing-uniqueness follows from linear independence of the
[H_{n}],n\neq 0
. As mentioned in the introduction, we conjecture that
\mathrm{top}(L,q)
is multiplicative under Murasugi sum, hence we expect that the
[H_{n}],n\neq 0
are linearly independent in the Grothendieck group obtained by replacing “plumbing” by Murasugi sum. Still, such a group would not be the same as the one of Cheng-Hedden-Sarkar
[
3
]
, since not all links are being considered. It is an interesting question whether the links
H_{n},n\neq 0
are linearly independent in the Grothendieck group of
[
3
]
too.

7.
Additional comments

In this section we discuss the following question, which motivated our study of links bounding annular surfaces: is there an analogue of the Giroux-Goodman theorem for strongly quasi-positive links? More precisely
6
6

6

Note that in the case of fiber surfaces/fibred links, both questions are equivalent (since the fiber surface is the unique minimal genus Seifert surface of a fibred link) and they are answered by the theorem of Giroux-Goodman.
:

(1)

Is there a simple set of QP surfaces from which all other QP surfaces are obtained by plumbing/deplumbing?

(2)

Is there a simple set of QP surfaces from which all SQP links bound a surface made by plumbing/deplumbing surfaces in such a set?

Recall that a surface
\Sigma
embedded in
S^{3}
is called
quasi-positive
(QP) if it embeds as an incompressible subsurface of the fiber surface
F_{T}
of a positive torus link
T
. Here, incompressible means that
\pi_{1}(\Sigma)\to\pi_{1}(F_{T})
is injective. This is equivalent to the original definition of Rudolph in terms of braids
[
28
]
. A link is
strongly quasi-positive
(SQP) if it bounds a quasi-positive surface. The above questions are motivated by the fact that being a QP surface is stable under plumbing and deplumbing (or more generally, Murasugi sum)
[
29
]
.

We had considered the set of positively twisted annuli
A_{n},n>0
as a candidate for the above two questions. We will say that a surface is
positive annular
if it is obtained by plumbing/deplumbing annuli
A_{n}
with
n>0
, positive annular surfaces are QP by
[
29
]
. It is easy to see that not all QP surfaces are positive annular: indeed, an easy Seifert van Kampen argument shows that positive annular surfaces have free fundamental group, which is not the case for general QP surfaces. The following example was provided to us by Sebastian Baader: for every knot
K
there is a QP annulus
\Sigma_{K}
having that knot as core, hence if the knot is non-trivial then
\pi_{1}(S^{3}\setminus\Sigma_{K})
is not free.

The question of whether any SQP link bounds a positive annular surface is a bit more subtle than the previous one. Indeed, if a surface is QP but not annular (as in Baader’s example), it could still be possible that its boundary bounds another minimal genus Seifert surface that is positive annular. Note that non-fibred links may have several non-isotopic minimal genus Seifert surfaces (indeed, by
[
9
, Corollary 3.2]
, the result of Murasugi summing two non-fibred surfaces always results in a link with more than one minimal genus Seifert surface).

Still, we answer this in the negative, even for the smaller class of positive links. Note that every positive link is SQP
[
30
]
and
all
their genus-minimizing Seifert surfaces have free fundamental group
[
26
]
. As explained in Section
6
, a consequence of Corollary
2
is that the positive links of Table
3
do not bound annular surfaces at all.

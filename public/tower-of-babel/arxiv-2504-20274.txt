On the closure of irregular orbits of the horocyclic flow on infinite finness
Masseye GAYE, Amadou SY

Abstract
. The topological dynamics of the horocyclic flow h ℝ h_{\mathbb{R}} on the unit tangent bundle of a geometrically finite hyperbolic surface is well known. In particular on such a surface the flow h ℝ h_{\mathbb{R}} is minimal or the minimal sets are the periodic orbits. When the surface is geometrically infinite, the situation is more complex and the presence of possible irregular orbits makes the description of minimal sets complicated. In this text, we construct a family of infinite hyperbolic surfaces for which the horocyclic flow defined on the unit tangent bundle is not minimal.



Kew words :
horocyclic orbits, minimal set, asymptotic finness.

1.
Introduction

In the study of dynamical system, an important question is to understand minimal sets, that is to say non-empty, invariant closed set and which do not contain any proper, non-empty and invariant closed ones. About the horocyclic flow
h_{\mathbb{R}}
defined on the unitary bundel of a geometrically infinite hyperbolic surface restricted to its non-wandering set
\Omega_{h}
, this question is still open. But it is well known that understanding the minimal
h_{\mathbb{R}}
-sets amounts to understand the closure of irregular orbits (non-closed and non-dense in the non-wandering set
\Omega_{h}
), see for example
[
2
]
,
[
3
]
,
[
6
]
,
[
9
]
and
[
10
]
. For

, a classical argument for the non-minimality of

is to show that the set
T_{u}=\{t\in\mathbb{R}^{*};\ g_{t}(u)\in\overline{h_{\mathbb{R}}(u)}\}\neq\emptyset
; that is to say the geodesic orbit
g_{\mathbb{R}}(u)
meets the closure of the horocyclic orbit
h_{\mathbb{R}}(u)
in a non-zero time. The study of this intersection is linked to the understanding of the lower limit of the injectivity radius along the half geodesic
u(\mathbb{R}_{+})
, called asymptotic finness of
u(\mathbb{R}_{+})
and denoted
Inj(u(\mathbb{R}_{+})).
In
[
3
]
, A. Bellis shows this following :

For a geometrically infinite hyperbolic surface
\Sigma
, if
u
is an element of
\Omega_{h}
such that
h_{\mathbb{R}}(u)
is non-periodic and
0\leq Inj(u(\mathbb{R}_{+}))<+\infty
. Then
\overline{h_{\mathbb{R}}(u)}
is not
h_{\mathbb{R}}
-minimal. Moreover :

- if
Inj(u(\mathbb{R}_{+})=0
, the set
T_{u}=\mathbb{R}^{*};

- if
0<Inj(u(\mathbb{R}_{+})<+\infty
, the set
T_{u}
contains an unbounded sequence
(t_{n})_{\geq 1}.

In view of the result above we ask ourselves what happens if the finness infinite ? If the orbit
h_{\mathbb{R}}(u)
is closed then
T_{u}=\emptyset
. In
[
2
]
, A.Bellis asks the question :
do we always have
T_{u}=\emptyset
?
In other words, does the situation
Inj(u(\mathbb{R}_{+}))=+\infty
give rise to new types of
h_{R}
-minimal sets ?
In this paper we show by an example that the set
T_{u}
is not always empty. More precisely we show the following result :

Theorem 1.1
.

There exists a family of geometrically infinite hyperbolic surfaces
\Sigma_{\delta}
for which there exists

satisfying :

1.
h_{\mathbb{R}}(u)
is a irregular orbit with asymptotic fineness

and the closure
\overline{h_{\mathbb{R}}(u_{0})}
is not
h_{\mathbb{R}}
-minimal  ;

2. the set
T_{u_{0}}
contains a sequence
(t_{n})_{n\geq 1}
going to
+\infty

The remainder of this text is divided into two parts. In the first part we review the classification of the points of the limit set, the topological nature of the orbits of the horocyclic flow and the horocylic convergence. In the second part we will give the proof of theorem

Let be

2.2.
Topological nature of the orbits of the horocyclic flow

We consider the action of the horocyclic flow
h_{\mathbb{R}}
on its unit tangent bundle
T^{1}\Sigma
in restriction to its nonwandering set
\Omega_{h}
. When the surface

is geometrically finite, the topological dynamics of the flow

\Sigma

We have the following classification of the horocyclic orbits :

—

the set of dense orbits in
\Omega_{h}
;

—

the set of periodic (compact) orbits ;

—

the set of closed non compact orbits ;

—

the set of irregular orbits (the orbits that are neither dense nor closed).

In the case of geometrically infinite surfaces the nonwandering set of the horocycle flow always contains an orbit which is neither compact nor dense, see
[
11
]
,
[
5
]
.
In the geometrically infinite case, let us do the following remarks :

—

the horocyclic flow is not minimal and hence the dense orbits are not
h_{\mathbb{R}}
-minimal sets ;

—

the closed orbits are
h_{\mathbb{R}}
-minimal sets ;

—

the only possible other
h_{\mathbb{R}}
-minimal sets which are not closed orbits, are closures of irregular orbits.

The only examples of closures of
h_{\mathbb{R}}
irregular orbits that are understood, are not minimal sets (see for examples
[
6
]
,
[
9
]
,
[
10
]
).

3.1.
Construction of the surface

We consider the Poincare’s half-plane

. We note
\partial\mathbb{H}=\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}

\overline{\mathbb{H}}=\mathbb{H}\cup\partial\mathbb{H}

Let
\delta>1

a_{n}=\delta+\displaystyle\frac{2n}{n^{2}+1},\ b_{n}=n+(n^{2}+1)\delta,\ c_{n}=\displaystyle\frac{1}{n},\ d_{n}=\displaystyle\frac{n^{2}+1}{n}.

For any integer

a_{n}d_{n}-b_{n}c_{n}=1
and that
a_{n}+d_{n}>2
. Let
h_{n}
be the hyperbolic isometry of

defined by :
h_{n}(z)=\displaystyle\frac{a_{n}z+b_{n}}{c_{n}z+d_{n}}

by denoting respectively

and

and of

S_{n}=\{z\in\ \mathbb{H},\ |(h_{n}^{-1})^{\prime}(i)|=1\}\ \mbox{and}\ S_{-n}=\{z\in\ \mathbb{H},|(h_{n})^{\prime}(i)|=1\};

that is to say
S_{n}
and
S_{-n}
are the euclidean semi-circles of

with the same euclidean radius

and respective center :

X_{n}=\displaystyle\frac{a_{n}}{c_{n}}=na_{n}=\displaystyle\frac{2n^{2}}{n^{2}+1}+n\delta\ \mbox{et}\ Y_{n}=\displaystyle\frac{-d_{n}}{c_{n}}=-nd_{n}=-(n^{2}+1).

Let us also denote respectively by

D_{n}=\{z\in\ \mathbb{H},\ |(h_{n}^{-1})^{\prime}(i)|>1\}\ \mbox{and}\ D_{-n}=\{z\in\ \mathbb{H},|(h_{n})^{\prime}(i)|>1\};

E_{n}=\{z\in\ \mathbb{H},\ |(h_{n}^{-1})^{\prime}(i)|\leq 1\}\ \mbox{and}\ E_{-n}=\{z\in\ \mathbb{H},|(h_{n})^{\prime}(i)|\leq 1\}.

As for all non-zero natural integers

and

Consequently the sequence

On the one hand, for all

X_{p_{n+1}}-X_{p_{n}}-R_{p_{n+1}}-R_{p_{n}}=\displaystyle\frac{2(p_{n+1}^{2}-p_{n}^{2})}{(p_{n+1}^{2}+1)(p_{n}^{2}+1)}+(\delta-1)p_{n+1}-(\delta+1)p_{n}.

On the other hand, for all

We have
f_{t}(1)=ch(t)+e^{-t},\displaystyle\lim_{x\rightarrow+\infty}f_{t}(x)=+\infty
and for any real
x
in the interval
[1,+\infty[,\ f_{t}^{\prime}(x)=\displaystyle\frac{(3x^{4}+x^{2}-1)e^{-t}-e^{t}}{2x^{2}}.

Then the function
f_{t}
achieves its minimum at the point
x_{t}
the unique solution belonging to
[1,\infty[
of the equation bisquare
3x^{4}+x^{2}-1-e^{2t}=0;
given by
x_{t}=\bigg(\displaystyle\frac{-1+\sqrt{13+12e^{2t}}}{6}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq e^{\frac{t}{2}}.
We deduce
f_{t}(x_{t})=\displaystyle\frac{e^{t}+(x_{t}^{4}+x_{t}^{2}+1)e^{-t}}{2x_{t}}\geq\displaystyle\frac{e^{\frac{t}{2}}}{2}
and therefore
d(ie^{t},S_{-p_{n}})\geq Argsh\bigg(\displaystyle\frac{e^{\frac{t}{2}}}{2}\bigg).

Likewise for any integer
n\geq 1,
according to lemma
3.3
we have :

d(ie^{t},S_{p_{n}})=d(h_{p_{n}}^{-1}(ie^{t}),S_{-p_{n}})=Argsh\bigg(\displaystyle\frac{|h_{p_{n}}^{-1}(ie^{t})|^{2}+p_{n}^{4}+p_{n}^{2}+1}{2p_{n}\Im(h_{p_{n}}^{-1}(ie^{t}))}\bigg).

But as
|h_{p_{n}}^{-1}(ie^{t})|^{2}=\displaystyle\frac{e^{2t}d_{p_{n}}^{2}+b_{p_{n}}^{2}}{e^{2t}c_{p_{n}}^{2}+a_{p_{n}}^{2}}
and
Im(h_{p_{n}}^{-1}(ie^{t}))=\displaystyle\frac{e^{t}}{e^{2t}c_{p_{n}}^{2}+a_{p_{n}}^{2}},
we obtain :

\displaystyle\frac{|h_{p_{n}}^{-1}(ie^{t})|^{2}+p_{n}^{4}+p_{n}^{2}+1}{2p_{n}Im(h_{p_{n}}^{-1}(ie^{t}))}=\displaystyle\frac{e^{2t}d_{p_{n}}^{2}+b_{p_{n}}^{2}+(p_{n}^{4}+p_{n}^{2}+1)(e^{2t}c_{p_{n}}^{2}+a_{p_{n}}^{2})}{2p_{n}e^{t}}\geq e^{t}.

Consequently we have
d(ie^{t},S_{p_{n}})\geq Argsh\bigg(e^{t}\bigg).

Proposition 3.6
.

The set

contains a sequence

going to

Proof
:

Recall first that for

+\infty

2.2

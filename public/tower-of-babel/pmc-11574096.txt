Investigating the relationship between liver health status with coffee and tea consumption in participants referred to the Rafsanjan Cohort study in Iran
Authors: Shole Daneshvar-Ghahfarokhi, Fatemeh Ayoobi, Hassan Ahmadinia, Reza Vazirinejad, Vahid Mohammadi-Shahrokhi
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractCoffee, tea and caffeinated foods are popular in the world due to their high amount of caffeine, which are consumed daily by people in large quantities, and their effects on the body, especially the liver, are somewhat unknown, so this study was done with the aim of relationship between coffee and tea consumption and dietary intake of Caffeine on the serum liver enzymes and lipid profile. In this cross-sectional study the information of 8889 participants aged 35–70 years who referred to the Rafsanjan Cohort Study (RCS), a population-based prospective cohort that is a part of the Prospective epidemiological research studies in Iran (PERSIAN), was used. Demographic characteristics, medical history, consumption of coffee and tea, caffeine intake, and laboratory tests were collected. Dichotomous logistics regression models were used using crude and adjusted models to investigate the relationship between coffee, tea consumption, and caffeine intake with liver enzymes and lipid profile. Out of 8889 participants 4678 (52.6%) were female and 4211 (47.4%) were male. In older people, especially men, the consumption of tea and coffee increased and has a direct relationship with the abnormality of total cholesterol (TC) (OR 1.14; 95% CI 1.01 to 1.29). Also, it was observed that increased abnormal Alkaline phosphatase (ALP) (OR 1.22; 95% CI 1.01 to 1.52) and decreased abnormal serum glutamic-oxaloacetic transaminase (SGOT) (OR 0.65; 95% CI 0.46 to 0.93) were significantly associated with a high intake of caffeine. The other variables related to lipid profile and liver enzymes increased with increasing consumption of coffee, tea and intake of caffeine in participants, but did not show a significant increase. A high intake of caffeine and coffee and tea can have adverse effects on some liver enzymes and blood factors. Therefore, care should be taken when using these materials.Keywords: Coffee, Liver enzyme, Rafsanjan Cohort Study (RCS), Prospective epidemiological research studies in Iran (PERSIAN)Subject terms: Biochemistry, Health care, Risk factors, Signs and symptoms

Introduction

The liver is responsible for detoxification and body metabolism1, in addition, this organ plays a vital role in creating homeostasis in the body, supplying energy and essential substances, and nourishing the body’s tissues2. Among the key liver enzymes that are made by hepatocyte cells, can be mentioned cytoplasmic enzymes Aspartate transaminase (AST, also called SGOT), Alanine transaminase (ALT, also called SGPT), γ-glutamyl transferase (GGT), and Alkaline phosphatase (ALP)3. The mentioned enzymes, especially AST and ALT, increase in the serum in proportion to cellular damage in the liver, such as various diseases (hypertension diabetes, and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease: NAFLD), toxic substances, and viral infections4,5. Another role of the liver is to be involved in the metabolism of lipoproteins6. Among the most important blood lipoproteins, are total cholesterol (TC), triglyceride (TG), low-density lipoprotein-cholesterol (LDL-C), and high-density lipoprotein-cholesterol (HDL-C)7.

Coffee is one of the most popular beverages in the world and contains various compounds including caffeine and phenolic compounds8. Coffee consumption is high in western countries such as the United States, Britain, and France, but tea consumption is high in eastern countries such as China, India, and Japan9. Research has shown that drinking two cups of coffee or tea a day leads to a reduction in liver-related diseases in people10. The anti-inflammatory and antioxidant properties of coffee are due to the presence of compounds such as caffeine and diterpenes in this drink11. There is conflicting information about the effect of coffee, tea and caffeine on liver enzymes and lipid profile. Some studies indicate an increase in the serum level of lipids following the consumption of these two substances, and some others have provided opposite results. Previous studies have shown that high consumption of coffee was associated with an increase in serum lipids such as TC, TG, and VLDL-c, which highlighted the importance of moderate consumption of this beverage12. A large number of population-based studies showed that coffee consumption has been associated with decreased liver enzymes (AST, ALT, and GGT) in consumers13–16. In a study conducted on 1272 participants in Taiwan, the results showed that high coffee consumption was associated with increased HDL17. In another study that was conducted on 90 people who were regular coffee drinkers in Jordan, the data showed that in this group the levels of TC and LDL were higher than those who did not drink coffee, and there was a relationship with the increase in abnormality in their lipid profile18.

In a study conducted in Turkey on 122 adults, the evidence showed that there was no statistically significant difference in the serum levels of TC, TG, LDL-C, HDL-C or VLDL-C and the lipid profile of people consuming instant coffee and Turkish19.

In studies on the liver of alcoholic animals, it was shown that drinking tea reduces serum concentrations of total cholesterol and triglycerides. Also, black tea can reduce the level of liver enzymes (AST, ALT) and improve liver function20.Due to the high consumption of tea, coffee, and caffeine in Iran and their effect on the liver, the most important organ involved in the regulation of the body’s metabolism, in this cross-sectional study we investigated the relationship between liver health status and coffee and tea consumption in participants referred to Rafsanjan cohort study (RCS).

Study design

The present study is a cross-sectional study based on the data recorded in the Rafsanjan cohort study (RCS) a region in the southeast of Kerman province, Iran. In addition, it was part of the prospective epidemiological research in IrAN (PERSIAN)21. From August 2015 to December 2017, a population of women and men (urban and rural) between the ages of 35 and 70 entered the registration stage of the RCS center. Out of the total number of people who voluntarily entered 9991 during this period and signed the informed written consent letter, only 9941 people collected blood samples. Subjects with fatty liver and failure to complete the Food Frequency Questionnaire (FFQ) were excluded from this study (N = 1052). Finally, the information of 8889 people was included in this study according to the above explanation. The protocol of the study was designed according to the PERSIAN21, and it was approved by the Ethics Committee of Rafsanjan University of Medical Sciences (Ethical codes: IR.RUMS.REC. 1402.186).

Data collection

Information such as demographic data, social situation, alcohol and opium consumption, cigarette smoking, and physical activity were collected from each patient (self-report) through standard questionnaires whose validity was confirmed through the PERSIAN cohort21.

The scale to determine the physical activity level of people was based on the metabolic equivalent of task (MET) calculated for 24 h. Finally, the level of physical activity of the participants was determined based on three classifications: low (≤ 35.29 MET-hours per day), moderate (35.30–40.32 MET-hours per day), and heavy (≥ 40.32 MET-hours per day).

To find out whether a person has hypertension or not, the question “Do you have hypertension confirmed by a doctor” was used, which had two answers, (yes/no). The consumption of alcohol, cigarettes, and opium, by self-report, using yes or no questions. The criteria for diagnosing opium, smoke, and alcohol consumption in people means those who have smoked at least 100 cigarettes in their lifetime, alcohol users: those who have consumed at least 200 ml beer or 45 ml liquor per week, and opium users: those who have consumed at least once a week for 6 months in years, was considered.

Food Frequency Questionnaire (FFQ) was a validated questionnaire about the amount of food consumption (Grams per day), which collected nutritional information of people.

Body Mass Index (BMI) was obtained by dividing weight (kg) by squared height (m2), and its classification was BMI < 25, 25.0 ≤ BMI < 30, and BMI ≥ 30.

Dietary intake assessment

Experienced nutritionists of the RCS collected the nutritional information needed for this study among the participants through a semi-quantitative FFQ with 118 items. In the interview about dietary intake, the participants were asked two questions: (1) the number of food consumption per month, week, or day in the last year, and (2) the amount of the substance received at each time of use (the size and quantity of each serving is in accordance with the standard sizes consumed by Iranians). After completing the questionnaire, the amount of food consumption was converted into g/day using household portion sizes. Food intake was determined using the United States Department of Agriculture (USDA) food database. In this way, the nutritionists asked the participants how many glasses of coffee (it included Nescafe) and tea they consume per day. Finally, the numerical value of coffee and tea substances was calculated in grams per day. The amount of dietary intake of Caffeine in different foods was calculated in milligrams per day. In this study, dietary intake of Caffeine was divided into 4 quartiles (including Q1 ≤ 56/07 milligrams per day, Q2 < 56/07–99/02 milligrams per day, Q3 = 99/02-161/01 milligrams per day, and Q4 > 161/01 milligrams per day). Also, tea + coffee consumption was divided into 4 quartiles (including Q1 ≤ 235 g per day, Q2 = 235/0001- 461/589001 grams per day, Q3 = 461/59–746/001, grams per day, and Q4 > 746/001 grams per day).

Also, the questionnaire obtained fatty acids monounsaturated (MUFA), polyunsaturated (PUFA), total energy, total fiber, fatty acid Trans, and fatty acid saturated, and their values were converted into daily consumption units. Energy was calculated as kilocalories per day, and others were given as grams per day.

Biochemical measurements

To analyze the biochemical enzymes, the participants were asked to go to the cohort laboratory after spending 14 h of fasting for blood sampling between 7:00 AM and 9:00 AM. Serum levels of total cholesterol (TC), high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL cholesterol), low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL cholesterol), triglycerides (TG), serum glutamic-oxaloacetic transaminase (SGOT), serum glutamic pyruvic transaminase (SGPT), gamma-glutamyl transferase (GGT), and Alkaline phosphatase (ALP) were evaluated according to the instructions of the laboratory kits by a biochemical autoanalyzer (BT 1500, Italy).

Definition and measurements

For the variables related to blood lipids, the normal range from the cohort center laboratory is as follows: in both genders LDL < 130 mg/dL, or TC < 200 mg/dL, or HDL > 34 mg/dL and TG < 200 mg/dL. Any variable outside the above range was considered abnormal.

The abnormal range of both SGPT and SGOT were > 40 U/L in males and > 35 U/L in females respectively. In males, high levels of GGT was up to > 55 U/L and > 38 U/L among females. In both genders, the increased levels of ALP are defined as > 306 U/L.

Statistical analyses

The collected data were analyzed using SPSS software version 24. Quantitative data were reported as mean ± SD and qualitative data were described as frequency and percentage. The normality assumption of quantitative variables was investigated using the Kolmogorov–Smirnov test. Then, for the correlation between caffeine consumption and lipid profile indices and liver enzymes, Pearson’s correlation coefficient test was used. Also, in order to investigate the effect of caffeine consumption on each of the lipid profile and liver enzyme indicators, by adjusting the demographic variables, a dichotomous logistics regression model was used. The significance level in all tests was considered less than 0.05.

Comparation of qualitative and categorical variables (such as demographic data, social situation, alcohol and opium consumption, cigarette smoking, hypertension, and physical activity) across the groups was done using the Chi-square test. Also, baseline characteristics of participants among groups related to serum levels of lipids and liver enzymes and caffeine, coffee, and tea consumption were compared between the groups using chi-square (χ2).

Associations between consumption of caffeine, coffee with tea, and liver enzymes and lipid profile were assessed by crude and adjusted models. The amount of caffeine, coffee, and tea consumption was the basis for the classification of the crude model.

The adjusted model was adjusted for confounding variables age (continuous variable), gender (male/ female), education (continuous variable), marital state (single, married, other), cigarette smoking (yes/no), alcohol consumption (yes/no), opium consumption (yes/no), physical activity level (continuous variable), BMI (continuous variable), triglycerides (continuous variable), LDL cholesterol (continuous variable), HDL cholesterol (continuous variable), cholesterol (continuous variable), SGPT (continuous variable), SGOT (continuous variable), GGT (continuous variable), ALP (continuous variable), fatty acids monounsaturated (MUFA), polyunsaturated (PUFA), total energy, total fiber, fatty acid Trans, and fatty acid saturated (continuous variable).

The relationships of dietary intake of caffeine with abnormal lipid profiles and liver enzymes were further examined in individual multivariate models after adjusting for other factors (age, gender, education, marital state, cigarette smoking, alcohol consumption, opium consumption, physical activity level, BMI) (Table 3). In unadjusted analysis, abnormal TC (Q3 = OR: 1.15; 95% CI 1.02 to 1.29), LDL (Q3 = OR: 1.16; 95% CI 1.01 to 1.34), and ALP (Q2 = OR: 1.29; 95% CI 1.05 to 1.59 and Q3 = OR: 1.41; 95% CI 1.15 to 1.73 and Q4 = OR: 1.56; 95% CI 1.28 to 1.91) were associated with greater tea and coffee drinking. In the adjusting model, increased daily dietary intake of Caffeine (Q4) was associated with decreased abnormal SGOT (Odds ratio (OR): 0.65; 95% CI 0.46 to 0.93). Also, in adjusted model a positive association remained between ALP and daily dietary intake of Caffeine (Q4) (OR: 1.22; 95% CI 1.01 to 1.52) (Table 3).

Discussion

This study investigated the relationship between the consumption of coffee and tea and daily dietary intake of caffeine with the serum level of liver enzymes and lipid profile of people referred to Rafsanjan Cohort Center. The results showed that in older people, especially men, the consumption of tea and coffee increased and had a direct relationship with the abnormality of TC level. Also, it was observed that increased abnormal ALP and decreased abnormal SGOT were significantly associated with a high intake of caffeine.

There is conflicting information about the effect of coffee and tea on reducing the serum level of liver enzymes. The results of a study on 685 women in a Korean population showed that consumption of more than two cups of coffee per day was inversely related to serum ALP levels, which indicates the importance of moderate coffee consumption22. In another study, short-term coffee consumption (2 gr/day for 30 days) was evaluated in 30 people. The results indicated a significant increase in the serum level of ALP in men, while no change in women23. Animal studies that examined coffee consumption with serum ALP levels concluded that daily coffee consumption for one month led to increased serum ALP levels in male rabbits24.In another study, short-term coffee consumption led to a significant increase in ALP serum levels in men, but in female participants, coffee consumption did not affect the enzyme level23. In our study, increasing the consumption of coffee and tea did not affect on ALP level. According to the above results, long-term or short-term continuous consumption of coffee has led to an increase in ALP levels in males, both animals and humans. Maybe gender and the physiological structure of the male body have a direct relationship in increasing the amount of this enzyme after consuming coffee and tea.

Considering the high consumption of black tea in Iran, the information of the present study is more about black tea, and few studies have been done on this type of tea. A recent meta-analysis study on 14 randomized controlled trials (RCTs) showed that drinking green tea can significantly reduce serum TC levels25. The results of a study that investigated the relationship between cholesterol concentration and black tea flavonoid consumption in hypercholesterolemic patients, revealed that no changes were observed in the serum TC level of people who consumed 5 cups of black tea per day26. The results of a meta-analysis study showed that the consumption of black tea has no effect on the blood cholesterol concentration (TC) of tea drinkers27. Our study’s results, Contrary to the results of other studies, showed that tea and coffee consumption affect the serum level of TC. However, in a trial study in 2003, it was shown that consumption of black tea in a low-fat diet leads to a decrease in total cholesterol and LDL in the subjects under study28. The result is completely different from our result, maybe the way of tea consumption and its preparation is involved in these study results.

Contrary to our results, in 2023, a study was conducted and the findings of this study showed that coffee consumption has led to an increase in serum levels of TC, TG, and LDL-C in the Jordanian population, which in turn has harmful consequences on human health and potentially increases the susceptibility to cardiovascular diseases18. In 2022, Miranda and colleagues conducted a study titled the relationship between coffee consumption and serum lipid profile. The results showed that high consumption of coffee (more than 3 cups per day) was associated with an increase in serum lipids, i.e. TC, TG, and very low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (VLDL-c) particles12. In 2021, Contrary to the above results, Cai et al. conducted a study investigating the effect of Theaflavins (TFs) (the main components of black tea) on the treatment of obese mice. The results showed that oral administration of TFs reduced serum TC, TG, and LDL levels, suppressed ALT and AST activities, and improved liver damage in mice29. This therapeutic effect of tea is likely due to the mentioned active ingredient, not the caffeine in tea.

Increased SGOT is often prevalent in patients with cirrhosis and sometimes in liver diseases30. A meta-analysis study examining the liver enzymes of 222,067 coffee drinkers showed that high coffee consumption was associated with decreased liver enzymes, especially SGOT31. Contrary to the above results, the results of our study showed that the consumption of tea and coffee had an inverse relationship with the abnormality of the SGOT (AST). Coffee has antioxidant properties and it has been shown in several studies that its consumption leads to a reduction of AST16. Probably, the antioxidant properties of coffee by improving the function and structure of hepatocytes prevent their damage and the leakage of AST into the serum by these cells, as a result, coffee has the regulatory properties of liver enzymes. Our results are the opposite. Maybe the type of coffee consumed and the type of preparation have an effect on these factors.

In a study conducted by Satoshi et al. on 6,898 men without any inflammation or liver disease, they showed that coffee consumption can reduce serum AST and ALT levels32. The results of this study are related to a Japanese population in which the race of the participants and even the type of coffee consumption and preparation are different from Iranians. Therefore, contradictory results with our study results are not far from expected.

The results showed that participants who had high caffeine intake increased abnormal ALP, and decreased abnormal SGOT. The effect of caffeine on the improvement of liver damage was investigated in experimental animals and the results showed that caffeine leads to the improvement of liver function and the reduction of liver transaminases33. A study demonstrated that after consuming caffeine, the lipid content of hepatic cells decreases34. In a study, the effect of caffeinated energy drinks on the lipid profile in a mouse model was investigated. Energy drinks led to a decrease in TC, LDL-C, and an increase in TG compared to the control group, and significantly decreased HDL-C concentration35. Few human studies have been conducted on the effect of caffeinated foods on liver enzymes and lipid profile, and the results of the above animal studies were contrary to our results. In our study, consumption of caffeine, i.e. substances containing instant coffee, such as Nescafe, was directly related to the increase in blood cholesterol (TC). In addition to instant coffee, there are unsaturated fats in Nescafe powder ingredients, which can be said to increase total cholesterol, not the coffee Ingredients themselves. In this study, the caffeinated substances consumed by the participants were Nescafe. But if we want to discuss in general, coffee has more caffeine and coffee itself can regulate lipid profile.

The heterogeneity of our results with other studies may be associated with some variables such as the genetics of the people, the routine food consumption and the characteristics related to the health of the individuals, the geographical conditions, and the way of coffee and tea consumption.

The high sample size and random selection of urban and rural people of both gender, male and female, were the strengths of our study. In this study, all the liver enzymes (ALT, AST, GGT, and ALP) and even the lipid profiles (LDL, HDL, TC, and TG) of people, which are related to the health of the liver, were measured. The limitations of this study were the consumption of coffee and tea has changed in people compared to the time of data collection, or they may not have stated the exact amount of food consumption correctly. On the other hand, in this study, the method of preparing coffee (filtered or not) and the type of coffee (Arabica, Robusta…) were not asked.

Conclusion

The general results of this study showed that high intake of caffeine and high consumption of coffe and tea can have adverse effects on the some of liver enzymes and blood factors, although it is recommended in studies to use caffeinated substances for weight loss and blood fat reduction. But should be careful in the amount of use of these materials.

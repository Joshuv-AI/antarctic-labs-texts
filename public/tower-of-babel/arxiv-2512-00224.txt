Model Theory of General von Neumann Algebras II: Group Actions and Crossed Products
Jananan Arulseelan

Abstract
. Expanding on previous work of the author, we initiate the model theoretic study of W ∗ \mathrm{W}^{*} -dynamical systems. We axiomatize continuous weight-preserving group actions of G G on von Neumann algebras for G G a given locally compact Hausdorff group. Since our axiomatization is of continuous actions, the ultraproduct is defined so that the ultraproduct action of G G is also continuous. Building on a theorem of Tomatsu, we show that continuous ultraproducts commute with crossed products. Finally, we prove a suite of results about computability of the aforementioned axiomatizations and of presentations of crossed products. In particular, we show how the crossed product construction is a useful tool for producing computable presentations, giving special attention to the group measure space construction of Murray and von Neumann. Thereby, we establish interesting connections to computable dynamics and computable measure theory.

We say that

-dynamical system when we are working with a fixed

,

.

Definition 2.2
.

[
33
]
and
[
1

Suppose

is a von Neumann algebra,
\mathcal{H}
a Hilbert space on which
\mathcal{M}
acts,

•

J\mathcal{M}J=\mathcal{M}^{\prime}

•

Jv=v

•

xJxJ\mathcal{P}\subseteq\mathcal{P}
for all

.

We remark that
[
33
]
imposes a fourth condition for standard forms. However, by

Standard forms are unique in the following sense. Suppose
(\mathcal{M}_{0},\mathcal{H}_{0},J_{0},\mathcal{P}_{0})

•

;

•

; and

•

u(\mathcal{P}_{0})=\mathcal{P}_{1}
.

An easy consequence of this is the theorem to follow. But first, we make a relevant definition.

Definition 2.3
.

[
33
, Definition 3.1]

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a von Neumann algebra acting on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, and
G
a group of
∗
-automorphisms of
\mathcal{M}
. A

, of

[

Let

The relevance of standard forms to continuous model theory of von Neumann algebras is seen in
[

]
. Namely, we can associate a canonical standard form to any pair
(\mathcal{M},\Phi)
consisting of a von Neumann algebra

and a faithful normal semifinite weight

. This is done as follows. Set

n_{\Phi}=\{x\in\mathcal{M}\ :\ \Phi(x^{*}x)<\infty\}\text{ and }m_{\Phi}=n_{\Phi}^{*}n_{\Phi}

Denote by

the
domain of definition

D_{\Phi}=\mathrm{span}(m_{\Phi})
. Then

admits an inner product
\left\langle x\,,\!\ y\right\rangle_{\Phi}=\Phi(y^{*}x)
. The Hilbert space

with respect to this inner product admits a natural action of

by left multiplication. We have an obvious injection
\eta_{\Phi}:D_{\Phi}\to\mathcal{H}_{\Phi}
. This makes

into a full left Hilbert algebra. Consider

the modular conjugation for this Hilbert algebra and set

\mathcal{P}_{\Phi}=\overline{\{JaJa\ :\ a\in\mathfrak{A}_{\Phi}\}}.

Then
(\mathcal{M}_{\Phi},\mathcal{H}_{\Phi},J_{\Phi},\mathcal{P}_{\Phi})
is the standard form for
(\mathcal{M},\Phi)
. We occasionally abuse notation and write
\eta_{\Phi}(\mathcal{M})
to mean
\mathfrak{A}_{\Phi}
as constructed above.

If
\alpha
is an action of
G
on
\mathcal{M}
by
∗
-automorphisms, then for
s\in G
, the unitary implementation

of
\alpha_{s}
can be constructed by extending the map
\eta_{\Phi}(x)\mapsto\eta_{\Phi}(\alpha_{s}(x))
.

Corollary 2.5
.

[
33

Let
(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{H},\mathcal{J},\mathcal{P})

3.
Discrete Group Case

Before tackling
G
-
\mathrm{W}^{*}
-dynamical systems for general locally compact groups
G
, we illustrate many of the concepts involved in the special case of discrete groups. Much of this section is devoted to technical results about how automorphisms on standard forms interact with totally bounded elements.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
(\mathcal{M},\Phi)
be a weighted von Neumann algebra together with an action
\alpha
of
G
with unitary implementation
s\mapsto u_{s}
for a group
G
. Suppose
x
is totally
K
-bounded in
\mathcal{M}
. Then for all
s\in G
,
\alpha_{s}(x)
is totally
K
-bounded.

Proof.

Note that
\alpha_{s}(x)=u_{s}xu_{s}^{*}=\pi(u_{s}x)
. It is clear that
\|\alpha_{s}(x)\|=\|x\|
and
\|\alpha_{s}(x^{*})\|=\|x^{*}\|=\|x\|
since
u_{s}
is a unitary. Now since
J
is an isometry from
\mathcal{M}
to
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
, we have
\|JxJ\|=\|x\|_{\operatorname{right}}
. So pick
y\in\mathcal{M}
for which that
x=JyJ
in other words,
\pi^{\prime}(x)=\pi(Jy)
and we have
\|y\|=\|x\|_{\operatorname{right}}
. So

u_{s}xu_{s}^{*}=u_{s}JyJu_{s}^{*}=u_{s}u_{s}^{*}Ju_{s}yu_{s}^{*}Ju_{s}u_{s}^{*}=Ju_{s}yu_{s}^{*}J.

Now taking
\|\cdot\|_{\operatorname{right}}
of both sides, we have

\|u_{s}xu_{s}^{*}\|_{\operatorname{right}}=\|Ju_{s}yu_{s}^{*}J\|_{\operatorname{right}}=\|u_{s}yu_{s}^{*}\|=\|y\|

by the first part. A similar argument for
x^{*}
finishes the proof.
∎

Recall that if
(\mathcal{M},\Phi)
is a weighted von Neumann algebra, we define the
S_{K}(\mathcal{M})
to be the set of all elements
x\in\mathcal{M}
with
\|x\|_{\Phi}\leq K
and
x
is totally
K
-bounded. Since the action of
G
is
\Phi
-preserving, we have
\|\alpha_{s}(x)\|_{\Phi}=\|x\|_{\Phi}
. Thus we have the following corollary.

Corollary 3.2
.

If
\alpha
is a
\Phi
-preserving action of
G
on
(\mathcal{M},\Phi)
, then for every
s\in G
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have
\alpha_{s}(S_{n})\subseteq S_{n}
.

Conversely,

Lemma 3.3
.

Assume
u
is a unitary on
\mathcal{H}_{\Phi}
such that
ux
is totally
K
-bounded for all
x
totally
K
-bounded and
\|ux\|_{\Phi}=\|x\|_{\Phi}
. Assume furthermore that conjugation by
u
preserves self-adjointness on totally bounded elements. Then
u
induces a
\Phi
-preserving
∗
-automorphism of
\mathcal{M}
.

Proof.

Let
u
be a unitary satisfying the assumptions in the lemma.
u
is injective and surjective on
\mathcal{H}
because it is a unitary. By density of
\mathcal{M}_{\operatorname{tb}}
in
\mathcal{M}
,
u
fixes
\mathcal{M}
set-wise. Similarly, by density of
\mathcal{M}_{\operatorname{tb}}
in
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
,
u
fixes
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
set-wise. Furthermore, by assumption, conjugation by
u
preserves the set of self-adjoint totally bounded elements. Therefore
u
preserves self-adjoint elements of
\mathcal{M}
, and therefore also the closure
\mathcal{K}
in
\mathcal{H}_{\Phi}
of the self-adjoint elements of
\mathcal{M}
. Therefore because
u
is unitary, we see that
u
preserves
i\mathcal{K}
,
\mathcal{K}^{\perp}
and
i\mathcal{K}^{\perp}
. By
[
34
]
,
\mathcal{P}
is the bisector of
\mathcal{K}
and
i\mathcal{K}^{\perp}
, therefore
u
preserves
\mathcal{P}
. By the same,
J
is the reflection across
\mathcal{P}
and therefore
J=uJu^{*}
. Thus
u
implements a
∗
-automorphism by Theorem
2.4
. The
\Phi
-preservation clause is immediate by the assumption that
\|ux\|_{\Phi}=\|x\|_{\Phi}
.
∎

At this point, we have everything we need to axiomatize
G
-
\mathrm{W}^{*}
-dynamical systems in the case that
G
is discrete.

Let
G
be a fixed discrete group. We define the language
\mathcal{L}_{G-\vNa}
to be the language
\mathcal{L}_{\vNa}
from
[
2
]
, together with function symbols
\alpha_{s,n}:S_{n}\to S_{n}
for every
s\in G
and

. Write
\left\langle x\,,\!\ x\right\rangle_{\Phi}
as a shorthand for
\Phi(y^{*}x)
. Axiomatize the theory
T_{G-\vNa}
by the axioms of
T_{\vNa}
from
[
2
]
together with, for every
s,r\in G
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
and

:

(1)

\sup_{x,y\in S_{n}}d_{2n}(\alpha_{s,n}(x)+\alpha_{s,n}(y),\alpha_{s,2n}(x+y))
;

(2)

\sup_{x,y\in S_{n}}d_{n^{2}}(\alpha_{s,n}(x)\alpha_{s,n}(y),\alpha_{s,2n}(xy))
;

(3)

\sup_{x\in S_{n}}d_{n}(\alpha_{s,n}(x)^{*},\alpha_{s,n}(x^{*}))
;

(4)

\sup_{x\in S_{n}}d_{n\lceil\lambda\rceil}(\lambda\alpha_{s,n}(x)^{*},\alpha_{s,n\lceil\lambda\rceil}(\lambda x))
;

(5)

\sup_{x\in S_{n}}d_{n^{2}}(\alpha_{s}(\alpha_{r}(x)),\alpha_{sr}(x))

\sup_{x,y\in S_{n}}|\left\langle\alpha_{s,n}(x)\,,\!\ \alpha_{s,n}(y)\right\rangle_{\Phi}-\left\langle x\,,\!\ y\right\rangle_{\Phi}|
.

Proposition 3.4
.

For
G
a discrete group, models of
\mathcal{T}_{G-\vNa}
as above precisely the class of
G
-
\mathrm{W}^{*}
-dynamical systems.

Proof.

By
[
2
]
, a model of
T_{\vNa}
is a von Neumann algebras equipped with faithful normal semifinite weight
(\mathcal{M},\Phi)
. Since it is the semicyclic representation being axiomatized, this is, in fact, in standard form. Axioms (1)-(4) above express that for
s\in G
,
\alpha_{s}
is a
∗
-homomorphism on the totally bounded elements. Axiom (5) says that the map
s\mapsto\alpha_{s}
is a homomorphism from
G
. Axiom (6) says that
\alpha_{s}
is implemented by a unitary on the underlying Hilbert space (the condition is only prima facie imposed on the totally bounded elements, but since these are
\|\cdot\|_{\Phi}
dense on
\mathcal{H}
, it holds on all of
\mathcal{H}
by continuity). This spatial implementation of
\alpha_{s}
together with the strong density of
\mathcal{M}_{\operatorname{tb}}
in
\mathcal{M}
implies that
\alpha_{s}
is actually a
*
-automorphism of
\mathcal{M}
by Lemma
3.3
. Since every action of a discrete group is continuous, we are done.
∎

If we add axioms that say that
\mathcal{M}
is abelian, then we obtain a model-theoretic treatment of measure-preserving actions of
G
on standard measure spaces by the duality theorem for abelian von Neumann algebras. If we furthermore enforce that
\|1\|_{\Phi}=1
, then we obtain probability measure-preserving (p.m.p.)

-actions. The model theory of p.m.p. actions by discrete groups

has been studied in
[
36
]
,
[
27
]
, and
[
8
]
. The latter two papers use a different approach using measure algebras, but
[
36
]
does on occasion use a formalism similar to ours. It would be interesting to recover the results of these papers in our setting and examine the extent to which they extend to more general
G
-
\mathrm{W}^{*}
-dynamical systems.

4.
Locally Compact Hausdorff Group Case

While the axiomatization in the previous section is interesting in itself, our broader aim is the case of actions by a general locally compact Hausdorff groups
G
. For this, we need a way to express continuous representations of locally compact Hausdorff groups. Thankfully, this is precisely what is done by Ben Yaacov-Goldbring in
[
7
]
. Because of this, we may emulate the approach therein. We briefly describe their approach.

Given a locally compact Hausdorff group
G
and a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, there is a one-to-one correspondence between continuous unitary representations of
G
on
H
and non-degenerate
∗
-homomorphisms from
\mathrm{L}^{1}(G)
to
B(\mathcal{H})
. The non-degenerate
∗
-representation, also denote
\alpha
on
\mathrm{L}^{1}(G)
on
B(\mathcal{H})
corresponding to a given a continuous unitary representation
\alpha:G\to U(\mathcal{H})
is defined as follows:

\alpha_{f}:=\int_{G}f(s)\alpha_{s}\mathrm{d}s\qquad\text{for each }f\in\mathrm{L}^{1}(G).

Goldbring and Ben Yaacov then show that non-degenerate
∗
-representations
\alpha
of
\mathrm{L}^{1}(G)
on a Hilbert space can be axiomatized by considering as sorts
S_{f}
which represent
\overline{\alpha_{f}({\mathcal{H}}_{\leq 1}})
; that is, the closure of the image of the unit ball
\mathcal{H}_{\leq 1}
of
\mathcal{H}
under the operator
\alpha_{f}
.

We take a moment to remind the reader that we do not want to axiomatize any arbitrary representation of
G
on
\mathcal{H}_{\Phi}
. We specifically want those with unitary implementations
u_{s}
such that
J=u_{s}Ju_{s}^{*}
and
u_{s}\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{P}
for all
s\in G
or, equivalently, which induce an automorphism of
\mathcal{M}
. We see below how to add axioms to ensure these conditions. For now, we need the following lemma.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
(\mathcal{M},\Phi)
be a weighted von Neumann algebra together with a continuous action
s\mapsto\alpha_{s}
of a group
G
on
\mathcal{M}
. Denote by
u_{s}
the induced unitary implementation. Denote by
\alpha_{f}
the induced action of
f\in\mathrm{L}^{1}(G)
on
\mathcal{H}_{\Phi}
. Suppose
x\in\mathcal{M}
such that
\eta_{\Phi}(x)
is totally
K
-bounded. Then for all
f\in\mathrm{L}^{1}(G)
, we have that
\alpha_{f}(\eta_{\Phi}(x))
is totally
K\|f\|_{1}
-bounded.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.1
, we have that
u_{s}x
is totally
K
-bounded for all
s\in G
. For
f\in\mathrm{L}^{1}(G)
given, it is a simple calculation that

\alpha_{f}(x)=\pi(\alpha_{f}(\eta_{\Phi}(x)))=\pi\left(\int_{G}f(s)u_{s}\eta_{\Phi}(x)\mathrm{d}s\right).

It is then a simple application of the Dominated Convergence Theorem that
\|\alpha_{f}(x)\|\leq K\|f\|_{1}
, so that
\alpha_{f}(\eta_{\Phi}(x))
is left
K\|f\|_{1}
-bounded.

Similarly,

\pi^{\prime}(\alpha_{f}(\eta_{\Phi}(x)))=\pi^{\prime}\left(\int_{G}f(s)\eta_{\Phi}(x)u_{s}^{*}\mathrm{d}s\right).

Thus we make the same argument for the right bounds.
∎

Note that a similar argument shows
\|\alpha_{f}(\eta_{\Phi}(x))\|_{\Phi}\leq\|f\|_{1}\|x\|_{\Phi}
.

We can now begin to present the language of

-
\mathrm{W}^{*}
-dynamical systems for a general locally compact Hausdorff group. Let

be a locally compact Hausdorff group. Then the language
\mathcal{L}_{G-\vNa}
consists of the language

as in

]
, together with:

, representing the image

of

under the representation of

;

•

function symbols

for every

and

representing the action of

on
S_{f,n}
; and

•

inclusion functions
\eta_{f,n}:S_{f,n}\to S_{n}
for every

with

.

We take

to consist of the axioms of
T_{\vNa}
given in

]
together with:

(1)

is a

-dense in their codomains;

.

For
G

as above precisely the class of

-
\mathrm{W}^{*}
-dynamical systems.

Proof.

By the arguments in
[
7
]
, we see that axioms (1)-(4) ensure that

defines a non-degenerate

-representation on

, where

-dense in

, every unitary

,

and

. Therefore, by

,

, so since

.

5.
Standard Forms of Crossed Products

We use this section to remind the reader of (or introduce the reader to) the theory of crossed products of von Neumann algebras and their standard forms as in
[

]
and

5.1.
Standard Forms and Dual Weights

Let
G
be a locally compact group with left-invariant Haar measure
\mu
with modulus
\Delta_{G}
. Define the Hilbert space
\mathrm{L}^{2}(G)
of square measurable complex functions with respect to
\mu
. Recall:

Definition 5.1
.

The
left regular representation

\lambda_{G}
is the representation of
G
on
\mathrm{L}^{2}(G)
defined by

(\lambda_{G}(r)\xi)(s)=\xi(r^{-1}s)\quad s,r\in G\text{ and }\xi\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(G)

The
(left) group von Neumann algebra
is the von Neumann algebra
L(G)
generated by the set
\{\lambda_{G}(r)\ :\ r\in G\}
.

Recall also the
right group von Neumann algebra

R(G)=L(G)^{\prime}
generated by the
right regular representation

(\rho_{G}(r)\xi)(s)=\Delta_{G}(r)^{1/2}\xi(sr).

Given
f\in\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(G)
, we have an operator, which we also denote by
f
, on
\mathrm{L}^{2}(G)
given by

(f\xi)(s)=f(s)\xi(s)\qquad\xi\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(G).

The multiplication on
G
induces a unitary
W_{G}
on
\mathrm{L}^{2}(G\times G)\cong\mathrm{L}^{2}(G)\otimes\mathrm{L}^{2}(G)
defined by

(W_{G}\xi)(s,t)=\xi(s,st)\qquad s,t\in G\text{ and }\xi\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(G\times G).

We then get a normal monomorphism
\delta_{G}:\mathrm{L}^{2}(G)\to\mathrm{L}^{2}(G\times G)
given by

\delta_{G}(a)=W_{G}^{*}(a\otimes I)W_{G}.

Recall the
Fourier algebra of
G
, defined by
A(G)=\mathrm{L}^{2}(G)_{*}
. We see that
A(G)
is a semisimple commutative Banach
∗
-algebra when equipped with the multiplication

fg(\xi)=f\otimes g(\delta_{G}(\xi))\text{ for all }f,g\in A(G)\text{ and }\xi\in L(G)

Now let
(\mathcal{M},\Phi,\alpha)
be a
G
-
\mathrm{W}^{*}
-dynamical system. Consider the Hilbert space
\mathrm{L}^{2}(G,\mathcal{H}_{\Phi})\cong\mathcal{H}_{\Phi}\otimes\mathrm{L}^{2}(G)
of square integrable functions on
G
with codomain
\mathcal{H}
where we give
\mathrm{L}^{2}(G,\mathcal{H}_{\Phi})
the inner product

\left\langle\xi\,,\!\ \eta\right\rangle=\int_{G}\left\langle\xi(s)\,,\!\ \eta(s)\right\rangle_{\Phi}d\mu(s)\text{ for }\xi,\eta\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(G,\mathcal{H}_{\Phi}).

Definition 5.2
.

We have actions
\pi_{\alpha}
of
\mathcal{M}
and
\lambda_{\alpha}
of
G
on
\mathrm{L}^{2}(G,H_{\Phi})
given by

\pi_{\alpha}(x)\xi(s)=\alpha_{s}^{-1}(x)\xi(s)\quad\lambda_{\alpha}(r)\xi(s)=\xi(r^{-1}s)\qquad s,r\in G\text{ and }x\in\mathcal{M}.

The
crossed product

\mathcal{M}\rtimes_{\alpha}G
of
\mathcal{M}
by
\alpha
is defined to be the smallest von Neumann algebra on
\mathrm{L}^{2}(G,\mathcal{H}_{\Phi})
containing
\pi_{\alpha}(x)
and
\lambda_{\alpha}(r)
for all
x\in\mathcal{M}
and
r\in G
.

It turns out this definition is independent of the Hilbert space on which we represent
\mathcal{M}
, but we use this definition to make our discussion of standard forms of crossed products simpler.

Denote by
C_{c}(G,\mathcal{M})
the set of continuous compactly supported functions from
G
to
\mathcal{M}
. We give
C_{c}(G,\mathcal{M})
the structure of an involutive algebra with multiplication

[a*b](s)=\int_{G}\alpha_{t}(a(st)b(t^{-1}))d\mu(t)

and involution defined by

b^{\sharp}(s)={\Delta_{G}}^{-1}(s)\alpha_{s^{-1}}((b(s^{-1}))^{*})

for all
a,b\in C_{c}(G,\mathcal{M})
. Since
\mathcal{M}
acts on
\mathcal{H}_{\Phi}
, we can define the
∗
-representation

m(a)=\int_{G}u_{s}\pi(a(s))d\mu(s)

of
C_{c}(G,\mathcal{M})
on
C_{c}(G,\mathcal{H}_{\Phi})\subseteq\mathrm{L}^{2}(G,\mathcal{H}_{\Phi})
.

For
f\in C_{c}(G,\mathcal{M})
and
a\in\mathcal{M}
, we define
f\cdot a\in C_{c}(G,\mathcal{M})
by
(f\cdot a)(s):=f(s)a
. We consider

\mathfrak{B}_{\Phi}:=C_{c}(G,\mathcal{M})\cdot n_{\Phi}=\mathrm{span}\{x\cdot a\ :\ x\in C_{c}(G,\mathcal{M}),a\in n_{\Phi}\}

as a left ideal of
C_{c}(G,\mathcal{M})
, which can in turn be seen as a subset of
\mathrm{L}^{2}(G,\mathcal{M})
. Furthermore,
\mathfrak{B}_{\Phi}\cap\mathfrak{B}_{\Phi}^{*}
defines a full left Hilbert algebra whose associated von Neumann algebra is
\mathcal{M}\rtimes_{\alpha}G
.

Define the dual weight
\widehat{\Phi}
to be the weight induced on
\mathcal{M}\rtimes_{\alpha}G
by
\mathfrak{B}_{\Phi}\cap\mathfrak{B}_{\Phi}^{*}
. We need the following facts about
\widehat{\Phi}
.

Proposition 5.3
.

\widehat{\Phi}
and
\sigma_{t}^{\widehat{\Phi}}
satisfy

•

\widehat{\Phi}(m(a^{\sharp}a))=\Phi((a^{\sharp}a)(e))

•

\sigma_{t}^{\widehat{\Phi}}(\pi_{\alpha}(x))=\pi_{\alpha}(\sigma_{t}^{\Phi}(x))
for
x\in\mathcal{M}
and
t\in\mathbb{R}

•

\sigma_{t}^{\widehat{\Phi}}(u_{s})=(\Delta_{G}(s))^{it}u_{s}((D\Phi\circ\alpha_{s}:D\Phi)_{t})
for
s\in G
and
t\in\mathbb{R}

5.2.
Duality for Locally Compact Abelian Groups

Let
G
be a locally compact abelian group for this subsection. Denote by
\widehat{G}
the Pontryagin dual of
G
. We may assume that the Haar measures
\mu
and
\widehat{\mu}
on
G
and
\widehat{G}
respectively are chosen so that Plancherel’s formula holds:

\widehat{f}(p)=\int_{G}f(s)\overline{p(s)}d\mu(s)\text{ and }f(s)=\int_{\widehat{G}}\widehat{f}(p)p(s)d\widehat{\mu}(p)

For every
p\in\widehat{G}
, there is a unitary
v(p)\in B(\mathrm{L}^{2}(G,\mathcal{H}))
defined by:

v(p)\xi(s)=\overline{p(s)}\xi(s)

where
s\in G
and
\xi\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(G,\mathcal{H})
.

This defines an action
\widehat{\alpha}
of
\widehat{G}
on
\mathcal{M}\rtimes_{\alpha}G
defined by:

\widehat{\alpha}_{p}(\pi_{\alpha}(x))=\pi_{\alpha}(x)\text{ and }\widehat{\alpha}_{p}(\lambda_{\alpha}(s))=\overline{p(s)}\lambda_{\alpha}(s)

for all
x\in\mathcal{M}
,
s\in G
and
p\in\widehat{G}
. It is easy to see that
\mathcal{M}
is isomorphic to the fixed point algebra of
\widehat{\alpha}
.

Theorem 5.4

(Takesaki Duality)
.

There is an isomorphism
\Gamma:\mathcal{M}\rtimes_{\alpha}G\rtimes_{\widehat{\alpha}}\widehat{G}\to\mathcal{M}\otimes B(\ell^{2}(G))
such that

[\Gamma(\pi_{\widehat{\alpha}}\circ\pi_{\alpha}(x))\xi](s)=\pi_{\alpha}(x)\xi(s)\qquad[\Gamma(\pi_{\widehat{\alpha}}\circ\lambda_{\alpha}(r))\xi](s)=\lambda_{\alpha}(r)\xi(s)

and

[\Gamma(\lambda_{\widehat{\alpha}}(p)\xi](s)=\overline{p(s)}\xi(s).

It follows that if
\mathcal{M}
is properly infinite and
G
is second countable, that
\mathcal{M}\rtimes_{\alpha}G\rtimes_{\widehat{\alpha}}\widehat{G}\cong\mathcal{M}
. Moreover, the action
\widehat{\widehat{\alpha}}
is transformed to the action
\tilde{\alpha}_{s}=\alpha_{s}\otimes\mathrm{Ad}(u_{s})
.

6.
G
-continuous Ocneanu Ultraproducts

Fix a locally compact Hausdorff abelian group
G
. By the reasoning in
[
7
]
, the ultraproduct for models of
T_{G-\vNa}
is the continuous part of the (generalized) Ocneanu ultraproduct with respect to the action of
G
. Consider a family
(\mathcal{M}_{i},\Phi_{i},\alpha^{i})
of such models. By
[
2
]
, there is a faithful normal
\Phi^{{\mathcal{U}}}
-preserving conditional expectation from the Groh-Raynaud ultraproduct
\prod^{{\mathcal{U}}}_{\operatorname{GR}}(\mathcal{M}_{i},\mathcal{H}_{\Phi_{i}})
to the (generalized) Ocneanu ultraproduct
\prod^{{\mathcal{U}}}_{\operatorname{Oc}}(\mathcal{M}_{i},\mathcal{H}_{\Phi_{i}})
, which we denote by
\mathcal{E}_{\sigma}
. Since the action of
G
is assumed to be
\Phi
-preserving, the action commutes with the action of the modular automorphism group, and it follows that there is a faithful normal
G
-equivariant conditional expectation
\mathcal{E}_{G}
onto the continuous part. Since
\alpha_{i}
is
\Phi_{i}
-preserving,
\sigma_{t}^{\Phi_{i}}
commutes with
\alpha^{i}
and hence
\mathcal{E}_{G}
commutes with
\mathcal{E}_{\sigma}
By an argument similar to
[
2
, Theorem 7.8]
, there is a projection
p_{G}
implementing
\prod^{{\mathcal{U}}}_{\alpha-c}(\mathcal{M}_{i},\Phi_{i})
as a corner of the Groh-Raynaud, and consequently as a standard form.

We state the main theorem of this section, though we defer the proof to after we have the required tools.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
(\mathcal{M}_{i},\Phi_{i},(\alpha_{g})_{i})
be a family of
G
-
\mathrm{W}^{*}
-dynamical systems indexed by
I
where
G
is a locally compact abelian group. Then
(\prod^{{\mathcal{U}}}_{\alpha-c}(\mathcal{M}_{i},\Phi_{i}))\rtimes_{\alpha^{{\mathcal{U}}}}G\cong\prod_{\widehat{\alpha}-c}^{{\mathcal{U}}}(\mathcal{M}_{i}\rtimes_{\alpha_{i}}G,\widehat{\Phi}_{i})
. Furthermore, the isomorphism witnessing this is canonical.

A version of this theorem for the special case of
ultrapowers
is due to Tomatsu
[
48
]
. The author does not see a way to modify Tomatsu’s proof to prove our generalization. Instead, our proof is based heavily on Haagerup’s construction of standard forms, together with more rudimentary techniques from abstract harmonic analysis.

We begin our proof with the following lemma.

Lemma 6.2
.

(\prod^{{\mathcal{U}}}_{\alpha-c}(\mathcal{M}_{i},\Phi_{i}))\rtimes_{\alpha^{{\mathcal{U}}}}G
embeds into
\prod^{{\mathcal{U}}}_{\operatorname{Oc}}(\mathcal{M}_{i}\rtimes_{\alpha_{i}}G,\widehat{\Phi}_{i})
with a conditional expectation.

Before proving this lemma, we remark that a version of the above was proved recently for
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-dynamical systems when
G
is an amenable group by Zhengyu Fu in
[
19
]
.

Proof.

We first observe that the (generalized) Ocneanu ultraproduct
\prod^{{\mathcal{U}}}_{\operatorname{Oc}}(\mathcal{M}_{i},\Phi_{i})
embeds in
\prod^{{\mathcal{U}}}_{\operatorname{Oc}}(\mathcal{M}_{i}\rtimes_{\alpha_{i}}G,\widehat{\Phi}_{i})
with a conditional expectation. To see this, note that both
\prod^{{\mathcal{U}}}_{\operatorname{GR}}(\mathcal{M}_{i},\Phi_{i})
and
\prod^{{\mathcal{U}}}_{\operatorname{Oc}}(\mathcal{M}_{i}\rtimes_{\alpha_{i}}G,\widehat{\Phi}_{i})
are seen to naturally embed in
\prod^{{\mathcal{U}}}_{\operatorname{GR}}(\mathcal{M}_{i}\rtimes_{\alpha_{i}}G,\widehat{\Phi}_{i})
. We want to see that
\sigma^{\Phi}
-continuous elements of
\prod^{{\mathcal{U}}}_{\operatorname{GR}}(\mathcal{M}_{i},\Phi_{i})
are mapped to
\sigma^{\widehat{\Phi}}
-continuous elements of
\prod^{{\mathcal{U}}}_{\operatorname{GR}}(\mathcal{M}_{i}\rtimes_{\alpha_{i}}G,\widehat{\Phi}_{i})
under the first embedding. This is the case because
\sigma^{\widehat{\Phi}_{i}}(\pi_{i}(x_{i}))=\pi_{i}(\sigma^{\Phi_{i}}(x_{i}))
for all
x_{i}\in\mathcal{M}_{i}
by Proposition
5.3
.

A fortiori, we have
\prod^{{\mathcal{U}}}_{\alpha-c}(\mathcal{M}_{i},\Phi_{i})
embeds in
\prod^{{\mathcal{U}}}_{\operatorname{Oc}}(\mathcal{M}_{i}\rtimes_{\alpha_{i}}G,\widehat{\Phi}_{i})
with a conditional expectation. Since
(\prod^{{\mathcal{U}}}_{\alpha-c}(\mathcal{M}_{i},\Phi_{i}))\rtimes_{\alpha^{{\mathcal{U}}}}G
in standard form is generated by a copy of
(\prod^{{\mathcal{U}}}_{\alpha-c}(\mathcal{M}_{i},\Phi_{i}))
together with the unitaries
\lambda_{\alpha^{\mathcal{U}}}(s)
for
s\in G
, it now suffices to see that
(\lambda_{\alpha}(s))^{\bullet}\in\prod^{{\mathcal{U}}}_{\operatorname{Oc}}(\mathcal{M}_{i}\rtimes_{\alpha_{i}}G,\widehat{\Phi}_{i})
for all
s\in G
. To see this, note by Proposition
5.3
that for every
s\in G
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
i\in I
, we have

\sigma_{t}^{\widehat{\Phi}_{i}}(\lambda_{\alpha}(s))=(\Delta_{G}(s))^{it}\lambda_{\alpha}(s)((D\Phi\circ\alpha_{s}:D\Phi)_{t})=(\Delta_{G}(s))^{it}\lambda_{\alpha}(s),

where the last equality is by the assumption that
\alpha
preserves
\Phi
. This is continuous in
t
since
\Delta_{G}(s)
is a constant real number, thus proving the claim.
∎

Since
\prod^{{\mathcal{U}}}_{\alpha-c}(\mathcal{M}_{i},\Phi_{i})
is in standard form, the Tomita algebra
C_{c}(G,\prod^{{\mathcal{U}}}_{\alpha-c}(\mathcal{M}_{i},\Phi_{i}))
consisting of continuous compactly supported functions from
G
to the continuous ultraproduct is dense in
(\prod^{{\mathcal{U}}}_{\alpha-c}(\mathcal{M}_{i},\Phi_{i}))\rtimes_{\alpha^{{\mathcal{U}}}}G
. We want to see that functions of this form give a dense subset of
\prod_{\widehat{\alpha}-c}^{{\mathcal{U}}}(\mathcal{M}_{i}\rtimes_{\alpha_{i}}G,\widehat{\Phi}_{i})
. To this end, we now develop some Arveson spectral theory for continuous ultraproducts.

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
(y_{i})^{\bullet}\in\prod^{{\mathcal{U}}}\mathcal{M}_{i}
be an element of the (generalized) Ocneanu ultraproduct. Assume there exists a compact neighbourhood
K\subseteq\widehat{G}
of the identity
\hat{e}
of the dual group such that
y_{i}\in M(\alpha^{i},K)
for all
i\in I
. Then
(y_{i})
is
\alpha
-continuous.

Proof.

Let
\epsilon>0
be given. There exists
L>0
such that
\|y_{i}\|_{\Phi}^{\#}\leq L
for all
i\in I
Pick
f\in\mathrm{L}^{1}(G)
such that
f
is right uniformly continuous and
\widehat{f}(s)=1
for all
g\in K
. Note that
\alpha^{i}_{f}(y_{i})=y_{i}
for all
i
. Now for
r\in G
,

\|\alpha^{i}_{r}(y_{i})-y_{i}\|_{\Phi_{i}}^{\#}=\|\alpha^{i}_{f(s-r)-f(s)}(y_{i})\|_{\Phi_{i}}^{\#}\leq\|f(s-r)-f(s)\|_{1}\|y_{i}\|_{\Phi_{i}}^{\#}

Now if
r=e
, the identity of
G
, we see that
\|f(s-r)-f(s)\|_{1}=0
. By right uniform continuity of
f
and of
\|\cdot\|_{1}
, we can find an open set
U\subseteq G
such that for all
r\in U
, we have
\|f(s-r)-f(s)\|_{1}\leq\frac{\epsilon}{L}
. It can be seen that for this choice of
U
, we have
\|\alpha^{i}_{r}(y_{i})-y_{i}\|_{\Phi_{i}}^{\#}\leq\epsilon
for all
r\in U
, proving the claim.
∎

Theorem 6.4
.

Let
(x_{i})^{\bullet}\in\prod^{{\mathcal{U}}}\mathcal{M}_{i}
be an element of the (generalized) Ocneanu ultraproduct. Then the following are equivalent.

(1)

For every
\epsilon>0
, there exists
(y_{i})^{\bullet}\in\prod^{{\mathcal{U}}}\mathcal{M}_{i}
such that there exists a compact neighbourhood
K\subseteq\widehat{G}
of
\hat{e}
such that
y_{i}\in M(\alpha^{i},K)
for all
i\in I
and
\lim_{i\to{\mathcal{U}}}\|x_{i}-y_{i}\|_{\Phi_{i}}^{\#}<\epsilon
.

(2)

(x_{i})^{\bullet}
is
\alpha
-continuous.

Proof.

First, we show (1)
\implies
(2). Let
(x_{i})
satisfy (1) and let
\epsilon>0
be given. Pick
(y_{i})^{\bullet}\in\prod^{{\mathcal{U}}}\mathcal{M}_{i}
such that there exists a compact neighbourhood
K\subseteq\widehat{G}
of
\hat{e}
such that
y_{i}\in M(\alpha^{i},K)
for all
i\in I
and
\lim_{i\to{\mathcal{U}}}\|x_{i}-y_{i}\|_{\Phi_{i}}^{\#}<\frac{\epsilon}{4}
. Pick, by the previous lemma,
U\subseteq G
open such that
r\in U
implies
\|\alpha^{i}_{r}(y_{i})-y_{i}\|_{\Phi_{i}}^{\#}<\frac{\epsilon}{2}
. Then, for this choice of
U
, we have

\displaystyle\|\alpha^{i}_{r}(x_{i})-x_{i}\|_{\Phi_{i}}^{\#}

\displaystyle\leq\|\alpha^{i}_{r}(x_{i})-\alpha^{i}_{r}(y_{i})\|_{\Phi_{i}}^{\#}+\|\alpha^{i}_{r}(y_{i})-y_{i}\|_{\Phi_{i}}^{\#}+\|y_{i}-x_{i}\|_{\Phi_{i}}^{\#}

\displaystyle=2\|x_{i}-y_{i}\|+\|\alpha^{i}_{r}(y_{i})-y_{i}\|_{\Phi_{i}}^{\#}

\displaystyle\leq 2\frac{\epsilon}{4}+\frac{\epsilon}{2}

\displaystyle=\epsilon

for all
r\in U
. Therefore
(x_{i})^{\bullet}
is
\alpha
-continuous.

Now we show (2)
\implies
(1). Assume
(x_{i})^{\bullet}
is
\sigma
-continuous. Since
\widehat{G}
is a locally compact Hausdorff topological space, it is
T_{3}
and therefore admits an increasing net
(K_{j})_{j\in J}
of compact neighbourhoods of
\hat{e}
with union
\widehat{G}
. By Tietze’s Extension Theorem, there is a net
(f_{j})
of bump functions with support
K_{j}
. Scale these bump functions so that their Fourier transforms
F_{j}:=\widehat{f_{j}}
satisfy
\|F_{j}\|_{1}=1
. For each
j\in J
, define
x_{j}:=\alpha_{F_{j}}(x)=(\alpha_{F_{j}}(x_{i}))^{\bullet}
. Then it is clear that
\lim_{j\in J}\|x_{j}-x\|_{\Phi}^{\#}=0
and, by construction,
(x_{i})_{j}\in M(\alpha^{i},K_{j})
which is compact.
∎

Our final lemma identifies those elements of the crossed product which have compact Arveson spectrum.

Lemma 6.5
.

Let
\xi\in C_{c}(G,\mathcal{H}_{\Phi})\subseteq\mathcal{M}\rtimes_{\alpha}G
. Then
\xi\in M(\widehat{\alpha},\mathrm{supp}(f))
.

Proof.

We want to show that for any
f\in\mathrm{L}^{1}(\widehat{G})
such that
\widehat{f}(s)=1
for all
s\in\mathrm{supp}(f)
, we have
\widehat{\alpha}_{f}(\xi)=\xi
. Let
f\in\mathrm{L}^{1}(\widehat{G})
be given such that
\widehat{f}(s)=1
for all
s\in\mathrm{supp}(f)
. Then we have

\widehat{\alpha}_{f}(\xi(s))=\int_{\widehat{G}}f(p)\alpha_{p}(\xi(s))d\widehat{\mu}(p)=\int_{\widehat{G}}f(p)\overline{p(s)}\xi(s)d\widehat{\mu}(p)=\widehat{f}(s)\xi(s)

by Plancherel’s formula. But by assumption, this final term is
\xi(s)
.
∎

We now have the ingredients to prove our main theorem.

Proof of Theorem
6.1
.

By definition,
\prod_{\widehat{\alpha}-c}^{{\mathcal{U}}}(\mathcal{M}_{i}\rtimes_{\alpha_{i}}G,\widehat{\Phi}_{i})
is the continuous part of the the ultraproduct of
\prod^{{\mathcal{U}}}_{\operatorname{Oc}}(\mathcal{M}_{i}\rtimes_{\alpha_{i}}G,\widehat{\Phi}_{i})
with respect to
\widehat{\alpha}
. By Theorem
6.4
, we have that
\prod_{\widehat{\alpha}-c}^{{\mathcal{U}}}(\mathcal{M}_{i}\rtimes_{\alpha_{i}}G,\widehat{\Phi}_{i})
is the closure of the sequences of the form
(\xi_{i})
for which there is a compact
K\subseteq G
such that the spectral support of
\xi_{i}
is contained in
K
for all
i
. By Lemma
6.5
, this implies the support of
\xi_{i}
is contained in
K
. Therefore
\xi_{{\mathcal{U}}}
defined by,

\xi_{{\mathcal{U}}}(s):=\lim_{i\to{\mathcal{U}}}\xi_{i}(s)

is a well defined continuous function from
G
to
\prod^{{\mathcal{U}}}_{\operatorname{Oc}}(\mathcal{M}_{i},\Phi_{i})
. Furthermore, the range of
\xi_{{\mathcal{U}}}
consists of
\alpha
-continuous elements since
\alpha^{{\mathcal{U}}}=\lim_{i\to{\mathcal{U}}}\alpha^{i}
and
\alpha_{i}(r)\xi_{i}(s)=\xi_{i}(r-s)
for all
i
, so
\alpha^{{\mathcal{U}}}(r)\xi_{{\mathcal{U}}}(s)=\xi_{{\mathcal{U}}}(r-s)
.

By the characterization of
(\prod^{{\mathcal{U}}}_{\alpha-c}(\mathcal{M}_{i},\Phi_{i}))\rtimes_{\alpha^{{\mathcal{U}}}}G
above, and taking closures, the statement is proved.

Notice by
[
30
]
and
[
31
]
, the proof given above identifies the standard forms of
(\prod^{{\mathcal{U}}}_{\alpha-c}(\mathcal{M}_{i},\Phi_{i}))\rtimes_{\alpha^{{\mathcal{U}}}}G

which are, in turn, in standard form by

∎

We conclude this section by considering the situation for locally compact groups which are not (necessarily) abelian. Let
G
be a locally compact group and let
(\mathcal{M}_{i},\Phi_{i},\alpha^{i})_{i\in I}
be a family of

-dynamical systems. Notice that

. On the other hand, when

is not abelian, the Pontryagin dual (that is, the set of characters) of

\prod^{{\mathcal{U}}}_{\operatorname{Oc}}(\mathcal{M}_{i}\rtimes_{\alpha_{i}}G,\widehat{\Phi}_{i})
.

On the other hand, there are alternative forms of Takesaki duality (Theorem
5.4
) using
coactions

\delta
of
G
in the place of the dual action
\widehat{\alpha}
of
\widehat{G}
. We point the reader to
[
37
]
,
[
38
]
, and
[
43
]
for instance. Analogues of Arveson spectral theory for actions of locally compact non-abelian groups also exist as in
[
43
]
and
[
13
]
. We therefore conjecture a generalization of Theorem
6.1
, where the continuous part on the right hand side of the asserted isomorphism is taken with respect to some notion of continuity for the dual co-action

. Based on the proof of the abelian case given here, the notion of continuity should be equivalent to being an SOT limit of elements with compact Arveson spectrum. This would also suggest an analogous model theory for
\mathrm{W}^{*}
-cosystems
(\mathcal{M},\delta)
and, in turn,
\mathrm{W}^{*}
-algebraic quantum groups.

7.
Computability

In light of the volume of recent work on computable continuous model theory of operator algebras, we would be remiss to end this paper without at least a short discussion of computability. Obviously, the above axiomatization is not computable for arbitrary
G
as there are too many elements of our language. We see in this section an interesting phenomenon where the
computable axiomatizability
of a class relates to the
computable presentability
of an ancillary object, namely
\mathrm{L}^{1}(G)
.

7.1.
Computable Axiomatizations

We first consider the situation when
G
is discrete. Notice that if we wish to write down the axioms for
T_{G-\vNa}
as given in Section
3
, we need a way to enumerate the elements of
G
. Furthermore, for Axiom (5), we need a way to compute products in this enumeration.

Proposition 7.1
.

Let
G
be a computably presented discrete group with decidable word problem. Then
T_{G-\vNa}
is computably axiomatizable.

The pair of definitions to follow are adapted directly from
[
24
]
.

Definition 7.2
.

A presentation
A^{\#}
of a Banach
∗

(A,(a_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}})
, where
\{a_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
is a subset of

that generates
A
as a Banach
∗
-algebra. Elements of the sequence
(a_{n})
are referred to as
special points
of the presentation while elements of the form
p(a_{i_{1}},\ldots,a_{i_{k}})
for
p
a
rational
∗
-polynomial
(that is, a
∗
-polynomial with coefficients from
\mathbb{Q}(i)
) are referred to as generated points of the presentation.

Definition 7.3
.

If
A^{\#}
is a presentation of
A
and
\mathbf{d}
is a Turing degree, then
A^{\#}
is a
\mathbf{d}
-computable presentation if there is a
\mathbf{d}
-algorithm such that, given a rational point
p\in A^{\#}
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
, returns
q\in\mathbb{Q}
such that
|\|p\|_{1}-q|<2^{-k}
.

By restricting to sorts that correspond to points in the presentation, the following is clear.

Proposition 7.4
.

Let
G
be a locally compact group. If
\mathrm{L}^{1}(G)
admits a computable presentation, then the class of
G
-systems is computably axiomatizable.

More generally:

Proposition 7.5
.

Let
G
be a locally compact group. If
\mathrm{L}^{1}(G)
admits a
\mathbf{d}
-computable presentation, then the class of
G

\mathbf{d}

Note, by taking indicator functions of intervals with rational endpoints as our special points, that
\mathrm{L}^{1}(\mathbb{R})
has such a computable presentation. Thus we have:

Corollary 7.6
.

The class of
\mathbb{R}

\mathrm{W}^{*}

7.2.
Computable Presentations

Next, we provide a useful method for producing computable presentations of
\mathrm{W}^{*}
-probability spaces. But first, we need a way to quantify the complexity of a group action on a presentation of a metric structure. For the remainder of this paper,
G
is a discrete group.

Definition 7.7
.

Let
A
be a metric structure with a computable presentation
A^{\sharp}
given. Let
G
be a computably presented group acting on
A
by an action
\alpha
. We say that
\alpha
is
computable
if there is an algorithm that takes an element
s\in G
, a rational point
p
of
A^{\sharp}
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
and returns a rational point
q
of
A^{\sharp}
such that
d(\alpha_{s}(p),q)<2^{-k}
.

Theorem 7.8
.

Let
(\mathcal{M},\Phi)
be a von Neumann algebra equipped with a faithful normal semifinite weight that admits a computable presentation
\mathcal{M}^{\sharp}=(\mathcal{M},(a_{n}))
and let
G
be a finitely presented group with solvable word problem. Suppose
\alpha
is an action on
\mathcal{M}
that is computable. Then
\mathcal{M}\rtimes_{\alpha}G
admits a computable presentation.

Proof.

Fix a computable enumeration
s_{n}
of
G
. This can be done computably because
G
is finitely presented and has a solvable word problem. Take the presentation
(\mathcal{M}\rtimes_{\alpha}G,(b_{n}))
with special points given by
b_{2n}=\pi(a_{n})
and
b_{2n+1}=u_{s_{n}}
. We see by construction that
\mathcal{N}^{\sharp}=(\mathcal{M}\rtimes_{\alpha}G,b_{n})
is a presentation of
\mathcal{M}\rtimes_{\alpha}G
.

Now let
x
a rational point of
\mathcal{N}^{\sharp}
and
k
be a natural number. Formally compute
x^{*}x+xx^{*}
By definition, after a sequence of computable string manipulations (distributing adjoints, factoring out all scalars, polynomial multiplication, etc.),
x^{*}x+xx^{*}
is a finite sum of the form:

\sum_{i}r_{i}(n_{i,1})(n_{i,2})...(n_{i,l_{i}})

where each
r_{i}
is rational and
(n_{i,j})
is in
\{\pi(m)\ :\ m\in M^{\sharp}\}\cup\{u_{s}\ :\ s\in G\}\cup\{u_{s}^{*}\ :\ s\in G\}
. Here we are conflating
s
with the string that represents it. We can do this by computability of the group presentation. Using the following calculations, we can “push the u’s to the left”. We describe the process simply by:

•

use
u_{s}^{*}=u_{s^{-1}}
to get rid of adjoints of group unitaries.

•

use
u_{s}u_{r}=u_{sr}
to combine adjacent group unitaries.

•

if a group unitary is to the right of a
\pi
, use
u_{s}\pi(x)u_{s}^{*}=\pi(\alpha_{s}(x))
to move it to the left.

It is easy to see that this process terminates after finitely many steps and leaves us with a new sum of the form:

\sum_{i}r^{\prime}_{i}(n^{\prime}_{i,1})(n^{\prime}_{i,2})...(n^{\prime}_{i,l_{i}})

where only
n^{\prime}_{i,l_{i}}
is a group unitary for each
i
. Using solvability of the word problem, disregarding group unitaries not corresponding to
e
, and combining
\pi
s and distributing
\alpha
s we get an expression of the form:

p(x)u_{e}

where
p(x)
is a polynomial combination of
\alpha_{s}
s of rational points. Let
k^{\prime}
be the number of times
\alpha
appears. By computability of
\alpha
we can approximate each monomial of the form
\alpha_{s}(p)
by rational points
p^{\prime}
up to tolerance
2^{-k-k^{\prime}}
. Replacing the
\alpha
monomials by their approximants, this is something we can approximate
\|\cdot\|_{\hat{\varphi}}^{\sharp}
of by assumption. The conclusion follows.
∎

Remarks 7.9
.

•

The previous result is easily generalized to other Turing degrees with essentially the same proof, invoking oracles as necessary.

•

Since
\mathbb{C}
has computable presentation given by
\mathbb{Q}(i)
, and
L(G)\cong\mathbb{C}\rtimes_{\alpha}G
when
\alpha
is the trivial action, the result above recovers
[
24
, Theorem 3.10]
as a special case.

7.3.
Group Measure Space Constructions and Computable Dynamics

We now show how the previous theorem can be used to produce computable presentations of many of the
\mathrm{II}_{1}
factors which are commonly used in operator algebras. This is because such
\mathrm{II}_{1}
factors are often constructed via the group-measure space construction. This is a special case of the crossed product given by actions on a measure space
(X,m)
with measure
m
(or equivalently the commutative von Neumann algebra
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(X,m)
of essential bounded measurable functions thereupon). Computability in measure spaces and dynamics thereupon has been given a fair amount of attention by computability theorists (see
[
18
]
,
[
20
]
and
[
42
]
). The present subsection sets up the connection between computable dynamics and the computability of the corresponding group measure space construction. We hope that this will be used to explore further how results from computable dynamics can be applied to operator algebras and vice-versa.

A convenient choice of measure space is given by the Cantor space. Consider the Cantor set
C=\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}}
and a computable enumeration
w_{1},\ldots
of words in
C
. Define
[w_{n}]
to be the set of words with initial segment
w_{n}
. By a zig-zag argument, we can recursively enumerate finite unions of the form
[w_{i_{1}}]\cup\ldots\cup[w_{i_{n}}]
. We will refer to the index of this enumeration corresponding to
U=[w_{i_{1}}]\cup\ldots\cup[w_{i_{n}}]
as a
code
for
U
. Consider the
\sigma
-algebra
\Sigma
generated by such sets.

Definition 7.10
.

We say that a probability measure
\mu:\Sigma\to[0,1]
is
computable
if there exists an algorithm which takes as inputs a code for
U=[w_{i_{1}}]\cup\ldots\cup[w_{i_{n}}]
and a rational
\epsilon>0
, and returns
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\mu(U)\in(a,b)
and
b-a<\epsilon
.

Proposition 7.11
.

If
\mu
is a computable probability measure on
\Sigma
, then
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(C,\mu)
with the tracial state
\varphi(f)=\int_{C}fd\mu
admits a computable presentation.

Proof.

Take the sequence
(a_{n})=p_{[w_{n}]}
where
p_{[w_{n}]}(x)=1
if
x\in[w_{n}]
and 0 otherwise as the sequence of special points. Note that our special points are all self-adjoint so the generated points are simply
\mathbb{Q}(i)
-polynomials of the special points. It is a standard exercise in measure theory to see that
\mathbb{Q}(i)
-polynomials in such functions form a dense subset of
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(C,\mu)
. We remind the reader that this density is in the
\mathrm{L}^{2}
-norm; it is usually not the case that
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}
is even separable in its usual
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}
-norm. Note that
a_{n}a_{m}=p_{[w_{n}]\cap[w_{m}]}
and
a_{n}^{2}=a_{n}
. Thus we can write any
\mathbb{Q}(i)
-polynomial of special points as
\mathbb{Q}(i)
-linear combinations of functions of the form
p_{[w_{i_{1}}]\cap\ldots\cap[w_{i_{n}}]}
. It can be seen that a set of the form
[w_{i_{1}}]\cap\ldots\cap[w_{i_{n}}]
can be rewritten as a finite union of sets of the form
[w]
and that the procedure for doing so is effective. By the assumption that
\mu
is computable, we can compute the norms of these.
∎

Note that group actions will most often not preserve sets of the form
[w_{i}]
. With this in mind, we make the following definition.

Definition 7.12
.

We call a sequence of subsets
U_{j}
of
C

uniformly computable
if there is a recursively enumerable subset
I
of
\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}
such that
U_{j}=\bigcup_{(i,j)\in I}[w_{i}]
.

We can now make sense of computability of actions on
C
.

Definition 7.13
.

We say that a group action
\alpha
of a computably presented group
G
with computable word problem on
C
is
computable
if for every
g\in G
, the sequence
U_{i}=\alpha_{g}([w_{i}])
is uniformly computable.

Proposition 7.14
.

Assume that
G
is computably presented with computable word problem. Assume further that
G
acts computably on
C
by
\alpha
and that
C
has a computable measure
\mu
. Then
\alpha
induces a computable action of
G
on
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(C)
with the presentation given in Proposition
7.14
.

Proof.

By computability of
\alpha
, we can approximate
\int_{C}p_{[w_{n}]}(\alpha_{s^{-1}}(x))d\mu
which is
\mu(\alpha_{s^{-1}}([w_{n}]))
for any
s\in G
from below. We may then notice that the complement of
[w_{n}]
is a finite union of
[v_{i}]s
and use the same method to approximate the measure of the complement
\mu(\alpha_{s^{-1}}([w_{n}]))^{c}
. This gives a sequence
a_{i}
of approximations of the measure of the complement from below. Then
1-a_{i}
is a sequence of upper bounds for
\int_{C}p_{[w_{n}]}(\alpha_{s^{-1}}(x))d\mu
. The claim now follows.
∎

This gives us a convenient way to move from the dynamics on a measure space to the associated group measure space construction. Namely, together with Theorem
7.8
, the above gives us the following corollary.

Corollary 7.15
.

Assume that
G
is computably presented with computable word problem. Assume further that
G
acts computably on
C
by
\alpha
and that
C
has a computable measure
\mu
. Then
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(C)\rtimes_{\alpha}G
has a computable presentation.

We conclude with the remark that the above corollary, together with
[
5
, Theorem 4.3]
, no locally universal tracial von Neumann algebra can be expressed as a group measure space construction in a way that is computable. This may more directly explain the lack of operator-algebraic constructions of locally universal tracial von Neumann algebras in operator algebras. It also suggests a strategy for producing such examples.

Asymptotic distribution of the Betti numbers of ℳ ¯ 0 , n \overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}
Jinwon Choi, Young-Hoon Kiem

Abstract
. Asymptotic normality is frequently observed in large combinatorial structures, rigorously established for many quantities such as cycles or inversions in random permutations, the number of prime factors of random integers, and various parameters of random graphs. In this paper, we investigate whether this normal limit behavior extends to the topological invariants of geometric spaces. We show that the Betti numbers of the moduli space of rational curves with n n marked points ℳ ¯ 0 , n \overline{{\cal M}}_{0,n} and the Fulton-MacPherson configuration space ℙ 1 ​ [ n ] \mathbb{P}^{1}[n] are asymptotically normally distributed. Based on numerical evidence and established log-concavity, we conjecture that the Betti numbers of the quotients of these spaces by the symmetric group 𝕊 n \mathbb{S}_{n} are also asymptotically normally distributed. In contrast, we provide examples of geometric spaces that do not follow this Gaussian law.



1.
Introduction

The moduli space
\overline{{\cal M}}_{0,n}
of stable rational curves with
n
marked points (cf.
[
5
,
14
]
) plays a central role in algebraic geometry and mathematical physics, serving as a fundamental object for subjects ranging from Gromov–Witten theory and quantum cohomology to operads and conformal field theory (cf.
[
15
]
). Despite the significance of a complete understanding of its topological invariants, the overall topology of
\overline{{\cal M}}_{0,n}
is still far from fully understood. While Keel
[
11
]
successfully described the cohomology ring and found recursive formulas for the Betti numbers, the combinatorial complexity of these invariants grows exponentially as
n
gets large. Consequently, obtaining explicit descriptions for the Betti numbers for arbitrary
n
is a highly nontrivial task.

To tackle this complexity, we turn our attention to the
asymptotic behavior
of these invariants. We investigate the limiting shape of the Betti numbers as the number of marked points tends to infinity. This approach is motivated by the phenomenon of
asymptotic normality
in analytic combinatorics, where many combinatorial quantities tend to follow a normal distribution in the limit.

Using methods from analytic combinatorics, we analyze the generating functions of the Poincaré polynomials of
\overline{{\cal M}}_{0,n}
. Our main result establishes that the sequence of Betti numbers of
\overline{{\cal M}}_{0,n}
is asymptotically normally distributed, meaning informally that, after appropriate normalization, its graph exhibits a shape close to that of a normal density function. This implies that the topology of
\overline{{\cal M}}_{0,n}
has a statistically predictable nature in the limit. Despite the exponential growth of the total cohomology, the distribution of its cohomology cycles mimics the behavior of sums of independent random variable. Using this result, we also establish the asymptotic log-concavity of the Betti sequence, strengthening prior work.

We further investigate whether this phenomenon of asymptotic normality extends to other fundamental moduli spaces. In particular, we demonstrate that the Fulton-MacPherson configurations space
\mathbb{P}^{1}[n]
exhibits asymptotic normality. We conjecture that the quotients
\overline{{\cal M}}_{0,n}/\mathbb{S}_{n}
,
\overline{{\cal M}}_{0,n+1}/\mathbb{S}_{n}
and
\mathbb{P}^{1}[n]/\mathbb{S}_{n}
also exhibit asymptotic normality. In contrast, the Hilbert scheme of points on a smooth projective surface and the GIT quotient
(\mathbb{P}^{1})^{n}/\!\!/\mathrm{SL}_{2}
do not.
Consequently, the asymptotic normality is not a universal feature of all moduli spaces, but rather depends on analytic nature of their generating functions. This distinction offers a novel approach to the classification and understanding of the topology of the moduli spaces.

2.
Preliminaries

Throughout this paper, we focus on topological spaces
X_{n}
whose odd degree Betti numbers vanish. Let
b_{k,n}=\mathrm{dim}H^{2k}(X_{n})
be the even degree Betti number and let

P_{X_{n}}(u)=\sum_{k}b_{k,n}u^{k}

be the corresponding Poincaré polynomial. The Euler characteristic is given by
\chi(X_{n})=P_{X_{n}}(1)
. We assume that
\chi(X_{n})>0
so that

f_{n}(u):=\frac{P_{X_{n}}(u)}{\chi(X_{n})}=\sum_{k}\mathbb{P}(\xi_{n}=k)u^{k}

is a
probability generating function
(PGF) of an associated integer-valued random variable
\xi_{n}
. We consider the asymptotic behavior of
\xi_{n}
as
n\to\infty
.

For a probability generating function
f
of an integer-valued random variable, it is straightforward that the mean and the variance are given by

\mathfrak{m}(f)=f^{\prime}(1),\ \ \ \mathfrak{v}(f)=f^{\prime\prime}(1)+f^{\prime}(1)-f^{\prime}(1)^{2}.

For a random variable
\xi
, its
distribution function

F_{\xi}(x)
is defined by

F_{\xi}(x)=\mathbb{P}(\xi\leq x).

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\xi
be a continuous random variable with distribution function
F_{\xi}(x)
. A sequence of random variable
\xi_{n}
with distribution function
F_{\xi_{n}}(x)
is said to converge in distribution to
\xi
if for each
x
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}F_{\xi_{n}}(x)=F_{\xi}(x).

In this case, we say the speed of convergence is
\epsilon_{n}
if

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}|F_{\xi_{n}}(x)-F_{\xi}(x)|\leq\epsilon_{n}.

We say that the Betti numbers of
X_{n}
are
asymptotically normally distributed
if the corresponding standardized random variable
(\xi_{n}-\mathfrak{m}(f_{n}))/\sqrt{\mathfrak{v}(f_{n})}
converges in distribution to the standard normal distribution
{\cal N}(0,1)
.

Example 2.2
.

Let
X
be a topological space of finite dimension with
H^{\text{odd}}(X)=0
. Let
f(u)=\frac{P_{X}(u)}{\chi(X)}
be its normalized Poincaré polynomial. By the Künneth theorem, we have

P_{X^{n}}(u)=P_{X}(u)^{n},\ \chi(X^{n})=\chi(X)^{n}.

So, the normalized Poincaré polynomial is
f_{n}(u)=f(u)^{n}
. Let
\xi
and
\xi_{n}
be the random variables associated to the PGF
f(u)
and
f_{n}(u)
respectively. Then
\xi_{n}
is clearly the sum of
n
independent copies of
\xi
. Hence by the Central Limit Theorem, the Betti numbers of
X^{n}
are asymptotically normally distributed.

The key idea of the above example is generalized in the Quasi-Powers theorem, which asserts that if the probability generating function essentially behaves like a power of a fixed function, then the corresponding coefficients are asymptotically normally distributed. This condition indicates that the underlying combinatorial invariant behaves like a sum of independent (or weakly dependent) random variables. In this sense, the Quasi-Powers Theorem can be regarded as a generalization of the classical Central Limit Theorem. In analytic combinatorics, asymptotic normality of the distribution encoded by
f_{n}(u)
is often derived from the singularity analysis of the bivariate generating function

\varphi(z,u)=\sum_{n\geq 0}f_{n}(u)z^{n}.

For a bivariate generating function
\varphi(z,u)
, we denote by
[z^{n}]\varphi(z,u)
the coefficient of
z^{n}
in
\varphi(z,u)
.

We state the Quasi-Powers Theorem. A comprehensive treatment can be found in
[
6
]
.

Theorem 2.3

(
[
6
, Theorem IX.8]
, Quasi-Powers Theorem)
.

Let
\xi_{n}
be non-negative integer-valued random variables with probability generating function
p_{n}(u)
. Suppose that there exist analytic functions
A(u)
,
B(u)
, independent of
n
with
A(1)=B(1)=1
and sequences
\beta_{n},\kappa_{n}\to\infty
such that

p_{n}(u)=A(u)B(u)^{\beta_{n}}\left(1+O\left(\frac{1}{\kappa_{n}}\right)\right),

uniformly in a fixed neighborhood
\Omega
of
u=1
. Assume furthermore the variability condition

\mathfrak{v}(B(u))=B^{\prime\prime}(1)+B^{\prime}(1)-B^{\prime}(1)^{2}\neq 0

holds. Then,
\xi_{n}
is asymptotically normally distributed. More precisely, with

(2.1)

\begin{split}m_{n}&=\beta_{n}\mathfrak{m}(B(u))+\mathfrak{m}(A(u))+O(\kappa_{n}^{-1})\\
\sigma^{2}_{n}&=\beta_{n}\mathfrak{v}(B(u))+\mathfrak{v}(A(u))+O(\kappa_{n}^{-1}),\end{split}

the random variable
\frac{\xi_{n}-m_{n}}{\sigma_{n}}
converges in distribution to the standard normal distribution
{\cal N}(0,1)
. The speed of convergence is
O(\kappa_{n}^{-1}+\beta_{n}^{-1/2})
.

Whereas the Quasi-Powers Theorem provides distributional asymptotic normality, obtaining estimates for individual coefficients requires a local limit theorem.

Theorem 2.4

(
[
6
, Theorem IX.14]
, Local limit theorem)
.

Assume the hypothesis of the Quasi-Powers theorem for
p_{n}(u)=\sum_{k}p_{k,n}u^{k}
. Assume in addition that

|p_{n}(u)|\leq K^{-\beta_{n}}

for some
K>1
and all
u
in
\mathbb{C}-\Omega
with
|u|=1
. Then,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\left|\sigma_{n}p_{\lfloor m_{n}+x\sigma_{n}\rfloor,n}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-x^{2}/2}\right|=0.

Example 2.5
.

Another motivating example is the complete flag variety
\mathrm{Fl}_{n}
. It is well known that the Poincaré polynomial of
\mathrm{Fl}_{n}
coincides with the generating function for the number of inversions in permutations of the symmetric group
\mathbb{S}_{n}
(cf.
[
7
]
). As established in
[
10
,
16
]
via the Quasi-Powers Theorem, the distribution of inversions converges to a normal distribution as
n
tends to infinity. Consequently, the Betti numbers of the complete flag varieties are asymptotically normally distributed as
n
grows.

3.
Moduli space of rational curves with
n
marked points

Let
\overline{{\cal M}}_{0,n}
be the moduli space of rational curves with
n
marked points. We define
b_{k,n}=\mathrm{dim}H^{2k}(\overline{{\cal M}}_{0,n})
and

(3.1)

\varphi(z,u)=z+\sum_{n\geq 2}\frac{z^{n}}{n!}\sum_{k=0}^{n-2}b_{k,n+1}u^{k}.

It is well known that
\varphi
satisfies the functional equation (cf.
[
15
, p.194 Theorem 4.2.1]
)

(3.2)

(1+\varphi)^{u}=u^{2}\varphi-u(u-1)z+1.

Hence, for a fixed value of
u
(assuming
u\neq 0,1
),
\varphi
can be regarded as the inverse of

(3.3)

z=\frac{u^{2}\varphi-(1+\varphi)^{u}+1}{u(u-1)}.

By the Inverse Function Theorem, singularities of
\varphi
can only occur when the derivative
\frac{\partial z}{\partial\varphi}
vanishes:

\frac{\partial z}{\partial\varphi}=\frac{u^{2}-u(1+\varphi)^{u-1}}{u(u-1)}=0.

Thus, we find that the location of singularity is

(3.4)

z=\rho(u):=u^{\frac{1}{u-1}}-\frac{u+1}{u},\hskip 10.00002pt\varphi=\lambda(u):=u^{\frac{1}{u-1}}-1.

The second derivative at the singularity (
3.4
) is

(3.5)

\frac{\partial^{2}z}{\partial\varphi^{2}}|_{\varphi=\lambda(u)}=-u^{\frac{u-2}{u-1}}.

Since this is nonzero, the singularity is of the square-root type (cf.
[
6
, §IX.7.3.]
).
By the Taylor expansion, we have

\begin{split}z-\rho(u)&=-\frac{1}{2}u^{\frac{u-2}{u-1}}(\varphi-\lambda(u))^{2}+O\left(|\varphi-\lambda(u)|^{3}\right)\\
&=-\frac{1}{2}u^{\frac{u-2}{u-1}}(\varphi-\lambda(u))^{2}\left(1+O\left(|\varphi-\lambda(u)|\right)\right).\end{split}

Inverting this local relation gives

(3.6)

\begin{split}\varphi-\lambda(u)&=-\sqrt{2}u^{-\frac{u-2}{2(u-1)}}\rho(u)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(1-\frac{z}{\rho(u)}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
&+C(u)\left(1-\frac{z}{\rho(u)}\right)+O\left(\left|1-\frac{z}{\rho(u)}\right|^{\frac{3}{2}}\right)\end{split}

for some
C(u)
. By
[
6
, Theorem VI.4]
, the approximation of
\varphi
near the singularity transfers to an asymptotic estimate of the coefficients:

\displaystyle[z^{n}]\varphi(z,u)

\displaystyle=-\sqrt{2}u^{-\frac{u-2}{2(u-1)}}\rho(u)^{\frac{1}{2}-n}(-1)^{n}\binom{\frac{1}{2}}{n}

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt+O\left(\rho(u)^{-n}n^{-\frac{5}{2}}\right)

(3.7)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}u^{-\frac{u-2}{2(u-1)}}\rho(u)^{\frac{1}{2}-n}n^{-\frac{3}{2}}+O\left(\rho(u)^{-n}n^{-\frac{5}{2}}\right).

Note that the linear error term
(1-z/\rho(u))
in (
3.6
) does not contribute to the asymptotics of coefficients. The factor
n^{-\frac{5}{2}}
is from the next contributing error term
(1-z/\rho(u))^{\frac{3}{2}}
. Thus, we obtain

(3.8)

\frac{[z^{n}]\varphi(z,u)}{[z^{n}]\varphi(z,1)}=\frac{u^{-\frac{u-2}{2(u-1)}}}{\sqrt{e}}\left(\frac{\rho(u)}{e-2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}-n}\left(1+O\left(n^{-1}\right)\right).

Applying Theorem
2.3
to (
3.8
) with

A(u)=\frac{u^{-\frac{u-2}{2(u-1)}}}{\sqrt{e}}\left(\frac{\rho(u)}{e-2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},\ \ B(u)=\frac{e-2}{\rho(u)},

we arrive at the following theorem.

Theorem 3.1
.

The Betti numbers of
\overline{{\cal M}}_{0,n}
are asymptotically normally distributed with the mean
m_{n}=\frac{n-3}{2}
and the variance

(3.9)

\sigma_{n}^{2}=\frac{3-e}{6(e-2)}n+\frac{5-2e}{24-12e}+O(n^{-1}).

and the speed of convergence is
O(n^{-\frac{1}{2}}).

\overline{{\cal M}}_{0,n}

[

Definition 3.3
.

(1)

A sequence
\{a_{k}\}_{k=0}^{n}
is called
log-concave
if

a_{k}^{2}\geq a_{k-1}a_{k+1}

holds for all
1\leq k\leq n-1
.

(2)

A sequence
\{a_{k}\}_{k=0}^{n}
is called
r
-ultra-log-concave
at
k
if

\left(\frac{a_{k}}{\binom{n}{k}^{r}}\right)^{2}\geq\left(\frac{a_{k-1}}{\binom{n}{k-1}^{r}}\right)\left(\frac{a_{k+1}}{\binom{n}{k+1}^{r}}\right)

holds.

The variance in (
3.9
) of the distribution of the Betti numbers of
\overline{{\cal M}}_{0,n}
grows asymptotically at the linear rate
v=\frac{3-e}{6(e-2)}\approx 0.06537
. In comparison, the variance of the binomial coefficients
\binom{n}{k}
, interpreted as a probability distribution, grows at the rate
\frac{1}{4}
.
We derive the following result from the inequality
v<\frac{1}{4r}
for
r=3
. We restrict our attention to the central region, since the Gaussian approximation used in Theorem
3.1
is valid in this region by the local limit theorem (Theorem
2.4
).

Corollary 3.4
.

The sequence of Betti numbers of
\overline{{\cal M}}_{0,n}
is asymptotically
3
-ultra-log-concave at
k
for
|k-\frac{n-3}{2}|=O(\sqrt{n})
.

Proof.

We first check that the local limit theorem holds for Betti numbers of
\overline{{\cal M}}_{0,n}
. Let
\varphi_{n}(u)=[z^{n}]\varphi(z,u)
. By previous discussion, we find that the radius of convergence of
\varphi(u)
is
|\rho(u)|
and it is elementary to check that
|\rho(u)|
attains its minimum uniquely at
u=1
. Let
\Omega
be a small neighborhood of
u=1
and
\Gamma=\{u\in\mathbb{C}~|~|u|=1\}\setminus\Omega
. Let
M=\min_{u\in\Gamma}|\rho(u)|
. By the Cauchy–Hadamard theorem, we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\left|\frac{\varphi_{n}(u)}{\varphi_{n}(1)}\right|^{-\frac{1}{n}}\geq\frac{M}{e-2},

uniformly for
u\in\Gamma
. Hence
\frac{\varphi_{n}(u)}{\varphi_{n}(1)}
satisfies the uniform bound condition of Theorem
2.4
with
K=\frac{M}{e-2}
.

Hence, for
k
with
|k-\frac{n-3}{2}|=O(\sqrt{n})
, the normalized Betti numbers satisfy

(3.10)

\frac{b_{k,n}}{\chi(\overline{{\cal M}}_{0,n})}\sim\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma_{n}}\exp\left({-\frac{(k-m_{n})^{2}}{2\sigma_{n}^{2}}}\right).

Similarly, by the de Moivre-Laplace theorem, the approximation of binomial coefficients

\binom{n-3}{k}2^{-(n-3)}\sim\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi s_{n}^{2}}}\exp\left({-\frac{(k-m_{n})^{2}}{2s_{n}^{2}}}\right),

where
s_{n}^{2}=\frac{n-3}{4}
, is valid for
|k-m_{n}|=o(n^{\frac{2}{3}})
. In particular, this includes the central region
|k-m_{n}|=O(\sqrt{n})
.

Thus, asymptotically

\frac{b_{k,n}}{\binom{n-3}{k}^{r}}\sim C_{n}\exp\left(-\frac{(k-m_{n})^{2}}{2}\left(\frac{1}{\sigma_{n}^{2}}-\frac{r}{s_{n}^{2}}\right)\right),

where
C_{n}
is a positive constant depending only on
n
. This sequence (in
k
) is log-concave if and only if the coefficient of the quadratic term
(k-m_{n})^{2}
is negative, equivalently, the resulting distribution is a normal distribution with positive variance. This requires

\frac{1}{\sigma_{n}^{2}}-\frac{r}{s_{n}^{2}}>0\;\Leftrightarrow\;\frac{s_{n}^{2}}{\sigma_{n}^{2}}>r.

Since
\sigma_{n}^{2}\sim vn
and
s_{n}^{2}\sim\frac{n}{4}
, the condition is

r<\frac{1}{4v}.

Using the value
v\approx 0.06537
, we have
\frac{1}{4v}\approx 3.82
. Thus, the condition holds for
r=3
.
∎

5.
More examples

In this section, we present examples from geometry, some of which exhibit asymptotic normality and some of which do not. We aim to address the following question in future work.

Question 5.1
.

Are there geometric conditions that ensure asymptotic normality?

As the asymptotic normality is often observed in combinatorial setting, we expect that it arises when the Poincaré polynomials of the geometric objects are governed by underlying combinatorial data. For example, Poincaré polynomials of
\overline{{\cal M}}_{0,n+1}
and
\overline{{\cal M}}_{0,n+1}/\mathbb{S}_{n}
are encoded by the combinatorics of the weighted rooted trees (cf.
[
2
,
3
]
).

5.1.
Spaces of rational curves with unordered marked points

The symmetric group
\mathbb{S}_{n}
acts naturally on the moduli spaces

5.2.
Hilbert scheme of
n
points on a surface

Another important space in the moduli theory is the Hilbert scheme of
n
points on a smooth projective surface
S
, denoted by
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(S)
. The Hilbert scheme is a smooth resolution of the symmetric product
\mathrm{Sym}^{n}(S)
. The Betti numbers of
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(S)
are determined by Göttsche’s celebrated generating function formula
[
8
]
. In contrast to
\overline{{\cal M}}_{0,n}
,
\mathbb{P}^{1}[n]
and their quotients discussed above, we do not expect the Betti numbers of the Hilbert scheme to exhibit asymptotic normality. This is due to the analytic structure of its generating function, which prevents the application of the Quasi-Powers Theorem.

Let
S
be either
\mathbb{P}^{2}
or
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
and let
b_{i}=\mathrm{dim}H^{2i}(S)
. Let
P_{\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(S)}(u)=\sum_{k}u^{k}\mathrm{dim}H^{2k}(\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(S))
be the Poincaré polynomial. Recall that if the odd degree Betti numbers of
S
vanish, the generating function
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}P_{\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(S)}(u)z^{n}
is given by an infinite product:

(5.1)

\prod_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(1-u^{m-1}z^{m})^{b_{0}}(1-u^{m}z^{m})^{b_{1}}(1-u^{m+1}z^{m})^{b_{2}}}.

To apply the Quasi-Powers Theorem via singularity analysis, the generating function is typically required to have an isolated singularity for
u
near 1. (cf.
[
6
, Theorem VI.4]
) However, due to the infinite product structure of (
5.1
), the singularities accumulate, preventing the quasi-powers framework from being applicable.
Figures
5
and
6
plot the Betti distribution for
S=\mathbb{P}^{2}
and
S=\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
against the normal density functions with the same mean and variance, which shows clear non-Gaussian behavior.

Remark 5.3
.

It is observed in
[
9
]
that the distribution of Betti numbers of
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
asymptotically converges to the
Gumbel distribution
. In contrast to the normal distribution which models averages, the Gumbel distribution models extreme values (maximums) and consequently it is asymmetric. This aligns with the fact that
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
does not satisfy Poincaré duality. It would be of interest to identify the precise limiting distribution for
S=\mathbb{P}^{2}
or
S=\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
.

5.3.
GIT quotient of
(\mathbb{P}^{1})^{n}

Let

Y_{n}=(\mathbb{P}^{1})^{n}/\!\!/\mathrm{SL}_{2}

be the GIT quotient (cf.
[
17
]
) of
(\mathbb{P}^{1})^{n}
by the diagonal
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
action with respect to the linearization
{\cal O}_{(\mathbb{P}^{1})^{n}}(1,\cdots,1)
. As demonstrated in
[
12
]
,
\overline{{\cal M}}_{0,n}
is related to
Y_{n}
by a sequence of blowups

(5.2)

\overline{{\cal M}}_{0,n}\to\cdots\to Y_{n}.

Motivated by this connection, we explore whether the asymptotic normality observed in
\overline{{\cal M}}_{0,n}
persists for the Betti numbers of
Y_{n}
. The Betti numbers of
Y_{n}
for odd
n
are computed in
[
13
, Example 5.18]
:

\ \sum_{k}u^{k}\mathrm{dim}H^{2k}(Y_{n})=\sum_{k=0}^{n-3}t^{k}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{\min(k,n-3-k)}\binom{n-1}{j}\right).

We observe that this distribution is not asymptotically normal. The Betti numbers are given by a partial sum of binomial coefficients. Since the sequence of binomial coefficients approaches a normal density function, their partial sums approximate the cumulative distribution function, while the symmetry is imposed by the condition on the summation. Figure
7
plots the Betti distribution for
Y_{50}
against the normal density function with the same mean and variance, illustrating the clear deviation from Gaussian behavior.

\overline{{\cal M}}_{0,n}

Appendix

In the Appendix, we present a proof of the identities relating the Poincaré polynomials of
\overline{{\cal M}}_{0,n}
,
\overline{{\cal M}}_{0,n+1}
,
\mathbb{P}^{1}[n]
, and their quotient spaces.
For a space
X_{n}
equipped with an action of the symmetric group
\mathbb{S}_{n}
, we define

P_{X_{n}}=\sum_{k=0}^{\mathrm{dim}X_{n}}\mathrm{dim}H^{2k}(X_{n})t^{k},\ {\cal P}_{X_{n}}=\sum_{k=0}^{\mathrm{dim}X_{n}}\mathrm{ch}_{\mathbb{S}_{n}}H^{2k}(X_{n})t^{k}

to be the Poincaré polynomial of
X_{n}
and the graded Frobenius characteristic of the
\mathbb{S}_{n}
-representations on the cohomology of
X_{n}
, respectively.

Let

\mathfrak{p}_{X_{n}}=\sum_{k=0}^{\mathrm{dim}X_{n}}\mathrm{dim}H^{2k}(X_{n}/\mathbb{S}_{n})t^{k}

denote the Poincaré polynomial of the quotient
X_{n}/\mathbb{S}_{n}
. This polynomial can be obtained by extracting the multiplicities of the trivial representation from
{\cal P}_{X_{n}}
. In
[
4
]
, we define and study the characteristic polynomial
\mathscr{S}_{V}(m)
of a
\mathbb{S}_{n}
-representation
V
, which is obtained by applying the
Stanley map

(5.3)

x_{1}=x_{2}=\cdots=x_{m}=1\text{ and }x_{m+1}=x_{m+2}=\cdots=0

to the Frobenius characteristic
\mathrm{ch}_{\mathbb{S}_{n}}V
, where the variables
x_{i}
correspond to the power-sum variables. Then
\mathscr{S}_{V}(m)
is a polynomial in
m
of degree
n
and
\mathscr{S}_{V}(1)
is the dimension of the invariant subspace
V^{\mathbb{S}_{n}}
(
[
4
, Lemma 3.10]
).

Define the characteristic polynomial
\mathscr{P}_{X_{n}}
of
X_{n}
by applying the Stanley map (
5.3
) to
{\cal P}_{X_{n}}
. In particular,
\mathfrak{p}_{X_{n}}
is obtained by evaluating
\mathscr{P}_{X_{n}}
at
m=1
.

In this note, we let
X_{n}
be one of
\overline{{\cal M}}_{0,n}
,
\overline{{\cal M}}_{0,n+1}
or
\mathbb{P}^{1}[n]
, where the action of
\mathbb{S}_{n}
is given by permuting
n
points (fixing the last marked point in the case of
\overline{{\cal M}}_{0,n+1}
). We denote

P_{n}=P_{\overline{{\cal M}}_{0,n}},\ \ Q_{n}=P_{\overline{{\cal M}}_{0,n+1}},\ \ P^{FM}_{n}=P_{\mathbb{P}^{1}[n]}

and use the analogous notations for
{\cal P}
,
\mathscr{P}
and
\mathfrak{p}
. We adopt the convention that these polynomials are zero whenever the index is non-integer.

In
[
3
]
, we study the relationship among the cohomologies of these spaces via quasi-map wall crossing. By
[
3
, Theorem 4.8]
, we have

(5.4)

{\cal Q}_{n}=(1+t){\cal P}_{n}+t\mathsf{s}_{(1,1)}\circ{\cal Q}_{\frac{n}{2}}+t\sum_{h=2}^{\ell}{\cal Q}_{h}{\cal Q}_{n-h},

where
\ell=\lfloor\frac{n-1}{2}\rfloor
,
\mathsf{s}_{\lambda}
is the schur function associated to the partition
\lambda
and
\circ
denotes the plethysm product.

There is an analogous formula for
\mathbb{P}^{1}[n]
(cf. Propositions 4.1 and 4.4 for
m=2
of
[
3
]
):

(5.5)

\begin{split}{\cal P}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}[n]}&=(1+t+t^{2}+t^{3}){\cal P}_{n}+t\mathsf{s}_{(1,1)}\circ\left((1+t){\cal Q}_{\frac{n}{2}}\right)\\
&+(t+t^{2})\sum_{h=2}^{\ell}(1+t){\cal Q}_{h}{\cal Q}_{n-h}+t(1+t)\mathsf{s}_{1}{\cal Q}_{n-1}.\end{split}

Since

\mathsf{s}_{(1,1)}\circ\left((1+t){\cal Q}_{\frac{n}{2}}\right)=\mathsf{s}_{(1,1)}\circ{\cal Q}_{\frac{n}{2}}+t^{2}\mathsf{s}_{(1,1)}\circ{\cal Q}_{\frac{n}{2}}+t{\cal Q}_{\frac{n}{2}}^{2},

by combining (
5.4
) and (
5.5
), we find

(5.6)

\begin{split}{\cal P}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}[n]}&=(1+t)^{2}{\cal Q}_{n}-2t(1+t){\cal P}_{n}\\
&\hskip 8.61108pt-2t^{2}\mathsf{s}_{(1,1)}\circ{\cal Q}_{\frac{n}{2}}+t^{2}{\cal Q}_{\frac{n}{2}}^{2}+t(1+t)\mathsf{s}_{1}{\cal Q}_{n-1}.\end{split}

Consequently, we obtain a formula for the characteristic polynomials. Note that the characteristic polynomial of
\mathsf{s}_{(1,1)}\circ{\cal Q}_{\frac{n}{2}}
is
\frac{1}{2}(\mathscr{Q}_{\frac{n}{2}}^{2}-\mathscr{Q}_{\frac{n}{2}}^{[2]})
, where the superscript
[2]
denotes the substitution
t\mapsto t^{2}
. Thus,

(5.7)

\displaystyle\mathscr{P}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}[n]}

\displaystyle=(1+t)^{2}\mathscr{Q}_{n}-2t(1+t)\mathscr{P}_{n}+t(1+t)m\mathscr{Q}_{n-1}+t^{2}\mathscr{Q}_{\frac{n}{2}}^{[2]}

(5.8)

\displaystyle=(1+t^{2})\mathscr{Q}_{n}+t(1+t)m\mathscr{Q}_{n-1}+t^{2}\sum_{h=2}^{n-2}\mathscr{Q}_{h}\mathscr{Q}_{n-h}

The second equality follows from
[
2
, Corollary 5.12]
:

(1+t)\mathscr{P}_{n}=\mathscr{Q}_{n}-\frac{1}{2}t\sum_{h=2}^{n-2}\mathscr{Q}_{h}\mathscr{Q}_{n-h}+\frac{1}{2}t\mathscr{Q}_{\frac{n}{2}}^{[2]},

which can be derived from (
5.4
).
Taking coefficients of
\frac{1}{n!}m^{n}
from (
5.7
) yields a relation between the Poincaré polynomials:

\displaystyle P_{\mathbb{P}^{1}[n]}

\displaystyle=(1+t)^{2}Q_{n}-2t(1+t)P_{n}+nt(1+t)Q_{n-1}

\displaystyle=(1+t)^{2}P_{n+1}+(n-2)t(1+t)P_{n}.

In terms of notations in Section
4
, this gives

(5.9)

\psi_{n}=(1+u)^{2}\varphi_{n}+\frac{n-2}{n}u(1+u)\varphi_{n-1}.

Consequently, the asymptotic formula (
4.9
) can be recovered from (
5.9
) using (
3.7
).

Furthermore, setting
m=1
in (
5.8
) yields an identity between the Poincaré polynomials of the quotient spaces, which can be expressed in terms of ordinary generating functions.

Let

\displaystyle\overline{\psi}(z,u)

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{n\geq 1}z^{n}\sum_{k}u^{k}\mathrm{dim}H^{2k}(\mathbb{P}^{1}[n]/\mathbb{S}_{n}),

\displaystyle\overline{\varphi}(z,u)

\displaystyle=z+\sum_{n\geq 2}z^{n}\sum_{k}u^{k}\mathrm{dim}H^{2k}(\overline{{\cal M}}_{0,n+1}/\mathbb{S}_{n}).

Note that we do not divide by
n!
. Then by (
5.8
), we obtain

(5.10)

\overline{\psi}=(1+\overline{\varphi})(u^{2}\overline{\varphi}-u(u-1)z+1).

Interestingly, the equation obtained here matches (
4.3
) exactly.

We expect that an argument analogous to that of Section
3
should apply to
\overline{\varphi}
. Under this unproven expectation, we expect

\overline{\varphi}\approx\bar{\lambda}(u)+\bar{\mu}(u)\left(1-\frac{z}{\bar{\rho}(u)}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Since
\overline{\psi}
is a polynomial in
\overline{\varphi}
and
z
,
\overline{\psi}
is also of the form

\overline{\psi}\approx A(u)+B(u)\left(1-\frac{z}{\bar{\rho}(u)}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

for some analytic functions
A
,
B
of
u
in a neighborhood of
u=1
. If we further assume
B(1)\neq 0
, then the Quasi-Powers Theorem should tell us that
the asymptotic distributions of
[z^{n}]\overline{\varphi}
and
[z^{n}]\overline{\psi}
are normal distributions whose variances are linear with the same coefficient of
n
(determined by
\bar{\rho}
).

Moreover, there is an identity between the generating functions for the Poincaré polynomials of
\overline{{\cal M}}_{0,n}/\mathbb{S}_{n}
and
\overline{{\cal M}}_{0,n+1}/\mathbb{S}_{n}
. See
[
3
, Theorem 5.11]
, where a slightly different notation is used. In their notation,
\mathfrak{q}
corresponds to our
\overline{\varphi}+1
. Nevertheless, the analogous arguments apply, and we therefore expect that the asymptotic distributions of their Betti numbers are normal with variances growing linearly at the same rate.

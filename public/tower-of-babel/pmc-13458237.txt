HAMNet: Hierarchical Multi-scale Attention Network for precise disease detection in pearl millet using spatial fusion
Authors: S. Ramya, D. Pradeep
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Pearl millet is a vital crop in arid and semi-arid regions, but its productivity is significantly impacted by fungal and bacterial diseases. Traditional disease detection methods, including manual inspection and conventional machine learning techniques, often suffer from inefficiencies due to subjectivity, time consumption, and limited feature extraction capabilities. Deep learning-based segmentation models such as U-Net, SegNet, DeepLabV3, and FCN have been employed to automate disease identification, but they exhibit limitations in accurately localizing disease-affected regions, handling complex spatial patterns, and maintaining high segmentation precision. To address these challenges, this study proposes HAMNet-Mask R-CNN, an AI-powered hierarchical multi-scale attention network integrated with a spectral-spatial fusion module for precise pearl millet disease detection. The novel framework enhances disease localization by utilizing hierarchical multi-scale feature learning, where low, mid, and high-level features contribute to superior classification accuracy. The attention mechanism prioritizes critical disease regions, reducing false positives, while Mask R-CNN enables pixel-wise segmentation, refining disease boundary identification. The proposed model is implemented using Python and TensorFlow with high-resolution image datasets. Performance evaluation demonstrates HAMNet-Mask R-CNN achieving a 99.35% Dice score, 98.80% IoU, 99.75% Precision, 99.78% Recall, and 99.65% Accuracy, outperforming U-Net (95.03% Dice, 90.53% IoU), SegNet (94.58% Dice, 89.73% IoU), DeepLabV3 (98.31% Dice, 96.69% IoU) and FCN(98.47% Dice score, 97.00% IoU). The results confirm the model’s superiority in disease segmentation and classification, offering a robust and scalable solution for real-time disease monitoring in smart agriculture. The integration of hierarchical attention and spectral-spatial fusion significantly enhances detection accuracy, ensuring reliable disease identification and contributing to improved agricultural productivity.

Introduction

Pearl millet (Pennisetum glaucum) functions as a vital staple food plant throughout arid and semi-arid zones of Africa and Asia supplying essential sustenance for these areas1. The resistance of pearl millet to dry conditions does not protect it from serious disease threats that include downy mildew (Sclerospora graminicola), blast (Pyricularia grisea), and rust (Puccinia substriata)2. The diseases inflict yield reductions between 15% and 70% which leads to major financial losses for farmers. The newly appearing stem rot pathogen Klebsiella aerogenes threatens pearl millet production because it has been recorded with a severity level of 23.8% in India3. Visual inspection remains the standard method for disease detection but has proven ineffective experts disease cases at a rate between 30–40%4. The excessive spread of these pathogens gets worse because of climate change which makes both early identification and precise diagnosis urgently needed5. The fungal diseases rust and downy mildew afflict pearl millet in a harmful way that leads to severe yield reductions6. Rust infection occurs because of Puccinia substriata while it produces reddish-brown pustules that form on plant leaves and stems7. Each pustule of this fungal disease contains spores that fly easily through the air to infect new plants adjacent to the infected ones. The disease causes early leaf death and dims the plant’s photosynthetic functions which results in shortness of the plant structure and reduced harvest quantities8.

The infection from Downy mildew fungus Sclerospora graminicola begins with chlorotic streaks on leaves that transform into white or gray fungal growth when humid conditions prevail9,10. Panicles infected by systemic agents become deformed which causes infertility to negatively affect yield production. The infection of seedlings becomes severe because necrotic lesions appear on them in just several days11. The diseases need to be addressed promptly because their growth occurs optimally when temperatures are warm and moisture is high12. The practical utilization of automated disease detection technology through deep learning and IoT-enabled sensors remains restricted because of ecological factors and variations in lesion dimensions along with multiple disease interferences13. AI diagnostic tools gain lower levels of acceptance from farmers because of inherent challenges with AI explainability. Precise disease detection technologies for pearl millet need immediate development because they need to work effectively under all field environments while gaining acceptance from farmers14.

Recent innovation and its limits

The development of deep learning technology has substantially advanced the management of pearl millet diseases yet it presents several performance constraints. CNN models showed a wide acceptance for disease classification because they reached 98.08% accuracy when detecting blast or rust in controlled environments15. The detection models maintain a low accuracy in actual field conditions because environmental factors including occlusions and dust along with changing light conditions interfere with their performance13. The high price of IoT hardware prevents it from reaching small-scale farmers who need it. Genetic markers which include QTLs for blast resistance located in LG4 and LG7 identify resistant strains yet they do not provide immediate diagnostic capabilities16. Grad-CAM visualizations from deep learning models pinpoint irrelevant leaf vein regions instead of lesion boundaries which leads farmers to question their trust and adoption of the technology17.

Problem statement

Millet crops which serve as food staples in hot and dry regions struggle against growing infections caused by leaf blast and rust alongside downy mildew18. The current strategy for disease detection through manual visual assessment proves to be slow and both imprecise and unavailable for use by small-scale farmers19. The success of deep learning and IoT-based approaches depends on solving the ongoing problems with widespread disease detection across different environments and achieving effective model generalization as well as affordable expansion of their capabilities20. This research develops an AI-based detection system that combines adaptability with scalability and explainable features to detect millet disease early while maintaining accuracy and accessibility for minimal farmers5.

Research motivation

The important food grain crop pearl millet faces severe yield reductions from diseases including rust, blast, and downy mildew particularly in dry areas. Current disease detection systems use manual inspection for detection yet this process proves slow and demanding with human error possibilities. The available automated detection systems operate with limited accuracy when environmental complexity arises. The research aims to develop a precise disease detection method that unites Spatial Fusion with Hierarchical Multi-Scale Attention technology to reduce yield losses through improved disease detection accuracy.

Research significance

HAMNet uses Hierarchical Multi-Scale Attention mechanisms to identify diseases at granular levels together with Spatial Fusion to understand disease context leading to precise pearl millet disease detection. The research plays a significant role because it increases the early disease detection accuracy which leads to decreased crop losses as well as supports precision agriculture practices. The study creates an efficient automated AI-based approach that sustains agricultural practices thus allowing farmers access to successful proactive disease management which improves both food security and economic stability.

Proposed HAMNET for disease detection in pearl millet using spatial fusion

The proposed HAMNet with Adaptive Attention Mechanism methodology functions to detect diseases precisely in pearl millet through spatial fusion methods is shown in Fig. 1. The methodology starts with obtaining high-resolution images of healthy and diseased pearl millet leaves from agricultural research databases combined with data collected in fields. The images go through normalization processes and receive augmentation through techniques that include rotation and flipping for better model robustness. Disease localization happens in the annotation and labeling stage where bounding box annotations plus Mask R-CNN segmentation masks are used to achieve pixel-wise classification which improves disease localization accuracy. Feature Learning uses Hierarchical Multi-Scale to extract disease characteristics from three different levels which examine leaf texture along with color variations at a low level followed by disease spread pattern detection at mid-level and complete disease-affected region examination at a high level. The Adaptive Attention Mechanism determines dynamic weight distribution towards disease-infected areas thus minimizing processor effort on healthy parts. The model balances greater accuracy in disease mapping through its algorithm for computing feature importance scores and attention weighting function which depends on risk assessment. These procedures help improve detection while requiring minimal additional computational power. The Spatial Fusion Module uses multi-scale features for boundary refinement which minimizes false detection signals. The model conducts disease classification through fully connected layers which use softmax activation for results validation. The particular hyperparameters, including learning rate, batch size, and architectural outlines, were determined analytically through initial experiments to stability detection accuracy, convergence stability, and computational efficiency. For better clarity, unnecessary descriptions of the methodologies and contributions have been avoided. In addition, to encourage reproducibility and transparency, the data set used in the training will be conduct available to the public upon acceptance.

Despite its ability to track object boxes, the fundamental functionality of Mask R-CNN includes generating object-specific masks that provide extremely precise localization. Through pixel-level disease area segmentation, Mask R-CNN proves itself as an efficient tool for detecting Pearl Millet diseases whereas most object detection tools use bounding boxes. Hierarchical Multi-Scale Attention Network

HAMNet depends on accurate diagnosis through Hierarchical Multi-Scale Feature Extraction because this method enables disease detection by recognizing patterns at various abstraction levels. Through this method the model can study detailed information at various measurement scales which leads to better detection of minor disease indicators while retaining spatial relationships. Three hierarchical levels compose the hierarchical feature extraction mechanism including Low-Level Feature Extraction (Texture & Color Details) and Mid-Level Feature Extraction (Disease Spread Patterns) and High-Level Feature Extraction (Complete Disease Region Identification). The extraction process advances at successive stages to capture small spots of sickness while protecting the overall leaf shape. Through its features extraction process the model reveals subtle information about damaged regions that includes texture modifications and color variations. Biological disease manifestations result in changes in texture between diseased and healthy regions with rust disease causing rough lesions and downy mildew producing fuzzy mold-like structures. Through its learning process the model acquires knowledge about unhealthful green tones that enables it to note subtle early infections without specific disease characteristics. Fig. 12HAMNET performance.

HAMNET performance.

The Fig. 12 depicts the performance of Semantic segmentation for three plant disease conditions namely Healthy, Rust, Downy Mildew with Mask R-CNN and HAMNet. Each row is associated with a condition, demonstrating (a) the image, (b) the Mask R-CNN, and (c) the HAMNet output. Mask R-CNN uses bounding boxes and segmentation masks (green) to detect diseased areas, but the segmented areas do often contain background area outside the diseased region. In comparison, HAMNet generates fluid and precisely defined segmentation masks (blue) which lie down more closely to lesion borders, thus lowering false positives. The comparison shows that HAMNet has better segmentation accuracy and can refine the localization of the disease. This indicates that HAMNet can yield more accurate segmentation than Mask R-CNN and thus, if the focus of the detection field is in the area of plant disease detection under precision agriculture. HAMNET refined segmentation masks can lead to better automated disease classification, intervention tactics and yield protection, thereby enhancing agricultural decision-making.

Discussion

The proposed HAMNET-Mask R-CNN model significantly outperforms the other existing segmentation methods like U-Net, SegNet, DeepLabV3, and FCN. The results for the evaluation metrics (loss, Dice coefficient, IoU, precision, recall, and accuracy) indicated much better segmentation results for HAMNET-Mask R-CNN. Achieving 98.80% IoU and 99.35% Dice coefficient using contours that are closer to the boundary of their deviations, we also produce models that at least capture the object to a greater extent. Its 99.65% accuracy is further proof of its robustness. The suggested method not only beats U-Net and SegNet with 90.53% and 89.73% IoU scores. High precision (99.75%) and recall, (99.78) show that the model correctly reduces false positives and false negatives. The capabilities of this particular outcome, its limitations due to size, and the lack of external verification may affect its generalizability. Therefore, these specific results should be properly examined with the possibility of overfitting in mind. Even though the development in the Dice coefficient relation to the baseline in the FCN approach is not large at 0.88%, repeated experiments validate the performance improvement, suggesting that the approach is valued even with a limited set.

An analysis of misclassified and difficult samples showed that most errors were due to complex backgrounds, different variation, overlapping disease indications, and slight visual variances, reflecting areas for further development of the typical. An analysis of misclassified and difficult samples showed that most errors were due to complex backgrounds, different data analysis, overlapping disease symptoms, and slight visual differences, highlighting areas for further model progress. Also, the IoU score for various classes shows that the model is reasonably capable of handling such diverse segmentation tasks. The Best IoU scores belonged to Healthy (99.1%), Disease (98.5), Downy Mildew (98.2), and Rust (97.9) this indicates that the model generalizes well on a category level, while staying within high accuracy. Thus, the study validates HAMNET-Mask R-CNN as a cutting-edge segmentation model with high accuracy, low-loss and superior segmentation capabilities over existing alternatives. Future work potentially concerns the optimization techniques and the transfer of the proposed framework to reactively controllable complex systems with real-time applications.

The dataset being considered shows a moderate level of class imbalance between healthy and diseased leaves. To eliminate any potential bias during the training process, data augmentation techniques such as rotation, flip, and normalization were applied to the data classes to improve their diversity and generalization. The models used for training, including U-Net, SegNet, DeepLabV3, and FCN, were all trained under identical experimental conditions. This included consistent dataset preprocessing, normalization techniques, augmentation tactics, and data splits for training and testing. Hyperparameters such as learning rate, batch size, optimizer type, and training epochs were standardized across models whenever possible, but architecture-specific values were kept in accordance with established procedures.

Conclusion and future scope

The proposed HAMNET-Mask R-CNN model achieves high accuracy when compared with conventional methods like U-Net, SegNet, DeepLabV3, FCN, etc. There are metrics of 99.35% Dice similarity, 98.80% of IoU and 99.65% accuracy for the model to make sure that the most accurate segmentation of boundaries is achieved with least false positive and false negative. Additionally, its precision of 99.75% and recall of 99.78%, demonstrate its robustness to segment complex structures. For this study, the novelty is that by combining advanced deep learning architectures with optimized feature extraction and adaptive learning strategies, it is possible to improve segmentation accuracy and maintain computational efficiency. HAMNET-Mask R-CNN addresses a collection of categories with the ability to balance segmentation resolution and generalization compatibly with segmented scene categories, unlike existing models. It is thus extremely suitable for a host of applications including clinical imaging, precision agriculture and automated defect detection.

HAMNET-Mask R-CNN is a precise, efficient and scalable segmentation model. It is possible that to use the model in real-life applications where accuracy is highly demanded because it has proven its performance on different datasets. It should be investigated in the next research to minimize the computational complexity for real-time implementation in edge devices and IoT-based systems. Furthermore, to use self-supervised learning, domain adaptation, and transfer learning which hold further promise for improving performance on diverse datasets. Potentially there are exceptional avenues to explore by expanding the model for 3D medical image segmentation and real-time autonomous systems. Future work consists of expanding the dataset, which includes cross-validation with k-folds, and evaluating the approach on previous unexplored datasets to assure robustness and real-world application. Additionally, explainable AI techniques can be integrated to improve the transparency of segmentation decisions. HAMNet-Mask R-CNN proves to be highly accurate in segmentation and effective in detecting the disease, however, it has certain limitations, which must be mentioned. To begin with, the data in this study is mostly gathered in certain arid and semi-arid areas and hence may not be generalized in various geographic areas. Second, the model was not cross-datavalidated and its performance on images of various cultivars, environmental conditions, or imaging devices has not yet been assessed. Future research might be based on the extension of the dataset to encompass larger geographical areas and the cross-dataset validation of the model to determine more accurately the robustness and applicability of the model in a wider agricultural setting.

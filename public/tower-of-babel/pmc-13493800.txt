Synthesis and characterization of a new N4-Schiff base its zinc halide/pseudohalide complexes with thermal, antimicrobial, antioxidant, anticancer, DNA cleavage and wound healing properties
Authors: Maryam Rezaee, Morteza Montazerozohori, Reza Naghiha, Esmaeel Panahi Kokhdan, Shima Hosseinifar, Haidar Baghernejad
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractA novel Schiff base ligand, N¹,N²-bis(2-(((1E,2E)-3-(2-methoxyphenyl)allylidene)amino)ethyl)ethane-1,2-diamine (L), was synthesized from triethylenetetramine and o-methoxycinnamaldehyde, and its six new zinc halide/pseudohalide complexes were prepared and characterized using molar conductance measurements, melting point analysis and spectroscopic techniques. Thermogravimetric and differential thermal analyses (TG/DTG/DTA) coupled with the Coats–Redfern method provided key thermo-kinetic activation parameters (A*, E*, ΔH*, ΔG*, ΔS*) for the synthesized complexes. In vitro, zinc(II) complexes with Cl⁻, N₃⁻, and NO₃⁻ exhibited superior antimicrobial activity against Gram-positive (B. subtilis and S. aureus), Gram-negative (E. coli and P. aeruginosa) bacteria, and fungi (C. albicans and A. oryzae) compared to the free ligand. In DPPH and FRAP assays, zinc complexes, especially ZnL(N₃)₂ and ZnLI₂, exhibited superior antioxidant activity compared to the SB-ligand. In the MTT assay, ZnLI₂ and ZnL(NCS)₂ showed the highest anticancer activity against MCF-7 and PC3 cells, respectively. ZnLBr₂, ZnL(NCS)₂, ZnL(NO₃)₂, and ZnL(N₃)₂ completely cleaved E. coli DNA via a non-oxidative hydrolytic mechanism. Finally, the wound-healing potential of the SB-ligand and its ZnLI2 (zinc iodide complex) was evaluated in a rat model of second-degree burns, demonstrating a wound closure percentage of 100% at day 21, which promises its therapeutic efficacy.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-55361-8.Keywords: Schiff bases, Complex, Antimicrobial, Antioxidant, Cytotoxicity, DNA interaction, Burn wound healingSubject terms: Biochemistry, Biotechnology, Cancer, Chemical biology, Chemistry, Drug discovery, Microbiology

Introduction

Zinc Schiff base complexes (Zn-SB) have emerged as highly promising agents in bioinorganic and medicinal chemistry due to their structural stability, versatile coordination behavior, and broad biomedical potential1,2. Zinc, the second most abundant trace element in mammals, plays a vital role in gene expression, signal transduction, neurotransmission, and exhibits catalytic and enzymatic activities3. Owing to its d10 configuration, zinc ions adopt diverse coordination geometries with coordination numbers ranging from four to six, which underpins their ability to form stable complexes with Schiff base ligands4–6. These complexes demonstrate potent antimicrobial activity against Gram-positive and Gram-negative bacteria as well as pathogenic fungi7–9, while their antioxidant properties enable efficient scavenging of reactive oxygen species (ROS), mitigating oxidative stress and facilitating fibroblast proliferation and extracellular matrix deposition10–12. Furthermore, zinc compounds modulate inflammatory pathways by down-regulatingre pro-inflammatory cytokines such as TNF-α and IL-613,14, thereby establishing a microenvironment conducive to collagen synthesis and tissue remodeling. Mechanistic investigations reveal that these compounds stimulate angiogenesis through VEGF-mediated signaling cascades, improving vascularization and oxygen delivery to injured tissues15,16. Translational research has confirmed that Zn-containing compounds accelerate wound closure, enhance collagen deposition, and sustain zinc release in vivo17–20. Clinical evidence further supports their therapeutic relevance, with systematic reviews and meta-analyses confirming improved healing outcomes in patients with chronic ulcers, burns, and diabetic wounds following zinc supplementation or topical application of zinc-containing biomaterials21–23. The skin, as the largest organ of the body, provides a barrier against pathogens and radiation, while maintaining electrolyte and fluid balance24. Burn injuries, induced by chemicals, radiation, fire, steam, or electricity, result in tissue damage, infection, and excessive inflammation due to toxic mediators, oxidants, and proteases25. The healing of burn wounds involves hemostasis, neovascularization, and re-epithelialization26,27, and recent studies have reported the efficacy of Schiff base ligands and some metal Schiff base complexes in accelerating these processes28–30. Beyond wound healing, Zn-SB complexes exhibit anticancer, antidiabetic, enzyme inhibition, luminescent, and bio-imaging properties31–38, underscoring their multifunctionality in clinical and biomedical science. Collectively, the convergence of preclinical mechanistic insights and emerging clinical data positions Zn-SB complexes as next-generation agents in regenerative medicine, combining antimicrobial protection, oxidative stress modulation, angiogenic stimulation, and matrix remodeling within advanced therapeutic platforms. Although many reports exist on the various metal complexes39,40 with biological and especially the wound healing properties, a comprehensive literature survey reveals that no studies have been conducted on wound healing properties of this type of N4-tetradentate long-chain Schiff base and particularly its metal complexes. Therefore, the present work lies in adding to the literature the biological and especially wound healing properties of these types of compounds. In recent years, our research group has devoted considerable attention to exploring the biological properties of novel Schiff base (SB) compounds41–45.

In the present study, we describe the synthesis of a series of tetradentate Zn–SB complexes, obtained via direct reactions of zinc halide/pseudohalide salts with a newly designed SB-ligand, N1,N2-bis(2-(((1E,2E)−3-(2-methoxyphenyl)allylidene)amino) ethyl)ethane-1,2-diamine. The resulting complexes were comprehensively characterized using a combination of physicochemical and spectroscopic techniques. Subsequently, their in vitro biological activities including antimicrobial, antioxidant, anticancer properties, and DNA cleavage potential were systematically evaluated. Moreover, the wound-healing efficacy of the SB-ligand and its zinc iodide complex (ZnLI2) was assessed in the context of second-degree burn injuries. In this research, the hypothesis is that coordination of the new N4-Schiff base ligand to Zn(II) enhances thermal stability and bioactivity. The specific aims were to synthesize and characterize a new ligand and its zinc complexes, and to evaluate their thermal, antioxidant, anticancer, and wound-healing properties. The novelties of this work include a new N₄-Schiff base ligand, its novel zinc complexes, and the first concurrent evaluation of thermal, antioxidant, anticancer, and wound-healing properties in one Zn(II)-Schiff base system, with zinc coordination enhancing all bioactivities (lower IC₅₀, MCF-7/PC3 cytotoxicity, faster wound closure).

Experimental

Chemicals and apparatus

All chemicals were procured from commercial suppliers. The human breast cancer (MCF-7) and human prostate cancer (PC3) cell lines were bought from Pasteur Institute (Tehran, Iran). A JASCO-680 spectrophotometer was employed to record the infrared spectra at a range of 4000–400 cm− 1 using potassium bromide. The Electronic spectra were scanned utilizing a JASCO-570 spectrophotometer in the wavelength region of 200–800 nm in dimethylformamid (4 × 10− 5 M) in quartz cell. The NMR spectra were taken from a Bruker DPX FT/NMR-400 spectrometer, Dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO-d6) solvent, and tetramethylsilane (TMS) internal standard. Mass spectrometric analysis was conducted using a Sciex 320ab ESI mass spectrometer. Thermal studies (TG/DTG/DTA) of the prepared materials carried out employing Perkin-Elmer Pyris model apparatus from room temperature (RT) to 900 °C under N2 gas atmosphere with a heating rate of 20 °C/min. Melting point/decomposition points and the molar conductivity were measured using KRUSS SPIN Melting Point Meters and Metrohm-712 conductometer apparatuses, respectively. The FE/SEM images were taken from LEO/ZEISS 1455VP and ZEISS Sigma VP models.

Synthesis of novel SB-ligand

For this, the ethanolic solution of triethylenetetramine was dropwise added to the ethanolic solution of o-methoxycinnamaldehyde. The pale yellow solution was stirred continuously at RT. After 5 h from the progress of the reaction as monitored using TLC technique (solvent: n-hexane: ethylacetate (1:0.2) and Rf=0.5), with reduction of the solvent volume, a viscous oily product was afforded. Several crystallization attempts were carried out using different solvents such as ethanol, methanol, acetonitrile, DCM, chloroform, and even cold n-hexane; however, none successfully produced the ligand in solid form. Consequently, the ligand appears to be viscous oil, as expected for many organic compounds with a long-chain structure.

Synthesis of Zn (II) complexes

For the synthesis of Zn (II) chelates, fresh SB-ligand ethanolic solution was dropwise added to the corresponding Zn (II) halide/pseudohalide salts ethanolic solution. The solution was then subjected to magnetically stirring for 3 h. The resulting precipitates were isolated and washed with EtOH. After 3 h, no considerable change in efficiency was observed; therefore, this time was selected as optimal. Crystallization of the complexes was attempted using various solvents or solvent mixtures, including CH2Cl2, CH3Cl, CH3CN, CH2Cl2/EtOH, CH2Cl2/MeOH, CH3Cl/EtOH, CH3Cl/MeOH, CH3CN/EtOH, CH3CN/MeOH, CH3CN/Et2O, as well as several other mixtures. However, in all cases, only colored powders of the product were obtained, which were unsuitable for single-crystal X-ray diffraction analysis. Biological tests

Antimicrobial tests

Bioinorganic chemistry provides a foundation for medicinal development; therefore, evaluating the antimicrobial activity of the synthesized compounds is essential. To evaluate the in vitro bactericidal and fungicidal functions, the tested compounds were assessed against several pathogenic microorganisms using the well diffusion method. The tested microorganisms included Gram-positive bacteria (Staphylococcus aureus ATCC: 6538 and Bacillus subtilis ATCC: 6633), Gram-negative bacteria (Pseudomonas aeruginosa ATCC: 9027 and Escherichia coli ATCC: 25922), and fungal pathogens (Aspergillus oryzae and Candida albicans). In the beginning, the solution of the compounds at three diverse concentrations (12.5, 25, and 50 mg/ml in DMSO) was prepared as stock concentrations. Thereafter, 50 µl of each prepared solution were transferred inside created wells in petri dishes including the Muller Hinton Agar (MHA) culture medium (bacterial strains and C. albicans) and Sabouraud Dextrose Agar (SDA) culture medium (A. oryzae) that were inoculated with 100 µl of microbial strains and then incubation of petri dishes carried out overnight at 37 °C (bacterial strains and C. albicans) and in RT for 72 h (A. oryzae). During incubation, the solution of the compound diffuses and affects the growth of the tested microorganisms. At the end, the antimicrobial function of the materials was evaluated by estimating the inhibition zone diameter (IZD) (in mm) around the wells and also as compare to the reference standard drugs like Amoxicillin, Penicillin, and Cephalexin under similar conditions. The controlled trial signifies the antimicrobial inactivity of DMSO solvent against the tested pathogenic microorganisms46.

MIC and MBC determination

The minimum inhibitory concentration (MIC) is defined as the lowest concentration of an antibacterial agent that inhibits the visible growth of an inoculum after overnight incubation at 37 °C. MIC determination is important in diagnostic laboratories for evaluating the performance of novel antimicrobial agents against resistant pathogenic organisms. MIC values were obtained using a broth serial dilution method with different concentrations of the tested samples. The minimum bactericidal concentration (MBC) is defined as the lowest concentration of an antimicrobial agent that kills more than 99.9% of the bacterial population. MBC values were obtained after overnight incubation of plates containing Mueller Hinton Agar culture medium, which were inoculated with a full loop of the broth solution from MIC tests under aerobic conditions at 37 °C.

Morphology of bacterial cells

The SEM images resulting from the treatment of B. subtilis and E. coli bacterial cells with the SB-ligand, ZnLBr₂, and ZnLI₂ complexes clearly show their morphological characteristics. In brief, after the fixation of samples (mixture of physiological serum and treated bacteria cells) by %3 glutaraldehyde (GA) in 0.1 M phosphate buffer (pH = 7.3) for 2 h, dehydration was carried out with 30–96% ethanol for 10 min. Finally, the tested compounds were dehydrated in a vacuum oven at 40 °C for 2 h and the morphology of bacterial cells was observable using SEM images.

DNA cleavage

The DNA interaction of the tested samples was examined using agarose gel electrophoresis, with hydrogen peroxide serving as the oxidant (positive control). The E. coli strain was used as the DNA source, and DNA extraction was performed as previously reported47. Mixtures of each tested sample with DNA (4 µl of 5 mg/ml in DMSO) were incubated for 2 h at 37 °C and then mixed with bromophenol blue dye. These mixtures, along with DNA alone (negative control), the DNA/H₂O₂ mixture (positive control), and a DNA ladder, were loaded into the wells of the agarose gel. Electrophoresis was then run for 30 min at 100 V. After DNA migration was complete, the gel was visualized under ultraviolet light using a transilluminator (Uvitec, England), and images were captured with a camera. The DNA cleavage assay was performed qualitatively. The entire gel was used to run multiple sample series. After electrophoresis, a close-up photograph was taken, focusing only on the lanes corresponding to the tested compounds. Therefore, a full-length gel image with visible edges is not available. This approach is common in descriptive DNA cleavage studies.

In vitro antioxidant activity

During normal aerobic mechanisms, the body system generates harmful reactive oxygen species (ROS)48. These free radicals are reactive species that can hurt macromolecules like nucleic acids, proteins, and lipids via having unpaired electrons49 and cause heart disease, aging, and cancer50. Antioxidant compounds transfer electrons into reactive free radicals and convert them to unreactive species and therefore destruct of oxidants and scavenging free radicals and minimize oxidative damage48. The evaluation of the antioxidant function of the prepared compounds was done by in vitro DPPH method for measuring free radical scavenging capability and in vitro FRAP method for ascertaining the ferric-reducing antioxidant power.

DPPH method

DPPH (1,1-Diphenyl-2-picrylhydrazyl) is a stable organic free radical used to quantify the free radical scavenging capacity or hydrogen-donating ability of compounds through oxidative assessment. The DPPH free radical solution appears dark violet and absorbs at 517 nm. Antioxidants reduce DPPH radicals, leading to discoloration of the test solution51. The DPPH assay, as a rapid method, was employed to evaluate the antioxidant capacity of the tested samples in scavenging DPPH free radicals. After preparation of the several concentrations (10, 20, 30, 40, and 50 µg/mL) of the tested compounds and ascorbic acid (standard drug) in different tubes, 1000 µL of DPPH methanolic solution (0.1 mM) was added to them. Then, the tubes were stirred and incubated for 30 min in darkness at RT. The reduction of DPPH solution absorbance at 517 nm (Cecil Instruments Ltd, Milton, UK) and the color developed of the tested solutions patently show the scavenging capability of free radicals by the tested compounds. IC50 (signifies the concentration of the compounds that scavenged 50% of free radicals and is calculated from scavenging activity versus the concentration curve) and AEAC (ascorbic acid equivalent antioxidant capacity) values exhibit the antioxidant ability of the compounds. FRAP method

FRAP (ferric-reducing antioxidant power) is an in vitro technique for determining the antioxidant effect of tested samples. The ferric-reducing antioxidant potential of the samples was assessed based on their ability to reduce ferric ions. Typically, antioxidants act as reducing agents by transferring a single electron53. According to the FRAP method, 1000 µL of working FRAP reagent (300 mmol/L acetate buffer, pH 3.6; 10 mmol/L TPTZ in 40 mmol/L HCl; and 20 mmol/L ferric chloride hexahydrate in a 10:1:1 ratio) was added to 35 µL of the tested compound solution. After 30 min of incubation in darkness at room temperature, the absorbance of the samples was measured using a UV spectrophotometer (Cecil Instruments Ltd, Milton, UK) at 570 nm. Increased absorbance of the tested compounds indicates that they possess ferric ion-reducing potency. The results are presented graphically in Fig. 8. In this method, the FRAP value was calculated against a standard curve of FeSO₄·7 H₂O concentration and expressed as µmol of Fe(III) equivalents per liter.

In vitro cytotoxicity assay

In vitro anticancer function of SB-ligand and some its zinc (II) chelates was examined in comparison with cisplatin (positive control) using MTT (3-(4,5-dimethylthiazol-2-yl)−2,5-diphenyltetrazolium bromide) assay against MCF-7 and PC3 cancer cells. The HUVEC (Human umbilical vein endothelial cells) as normal cell lines were employed as control. The seeded cells54 with density of 1 × 104 cells/well in 96-multititre plates were incubated at 37 °C in a 5% carbon dioxide incubator overnight. Then the prepared stock solution by the dissolution of the synthesized compounds in DMSO (0, 50, 75, 100, and 125 µg/mL) that were diluted by culture medium was employed for treating of cancer cells. After incubation overnight, 10 µL (5 mg/mL) of stock MTT solution was added to each well and incubated at 37 °C. After 4 h and with the reduction of MTT produced the purple formazan precipitates. After aspiration of MTT-containing medium, DMSO was added to dissolve formazan. Then, the absorbance of the samples was recorded on a 96-well plate ELISA reader (Bio-Tek Instruments, Winooski, VT, USA) at 570 nm. The IC50 value was described as the tested compound concentration that causes 50% reduction of cellular viability.

Nitric oxide (NO)

Nitric oxide, a short-lived free radical gas, is synthesized by a family of enzymes called nitric oxide synthases (NOSs) from oxygen (O₂) and L-arginine. This signaling molecule plays a key role in both physiological and pathological processes and has been extensively studied55. The amount of nitrite was used as an indicator of NO generation and was determined by the Griess reaction method56,57.

In vivo estimation of the treatment of burn wound

Animals

Male wistar rats (200–250 g) were procured from the animal house at Yasouj University of Medical Sciences, Yasuj and held at RT (25 ± 2 °C) with 12 h light/dark controlled cycle with the possibility of access to water and food. All animal procedures conducted in this study were meticulously designed, executed, and reported in strict adherence to the fundamental principles and reporting requirements of the ARRIVE guidelines (version 2.0). All experiments were conducted in strict accordance with the approved guidelines for the care and use of laboratory animals based on the “Principles of Laboratory Animal Care” and was approved under the regulations of the Ethics Committee of Yasuj University of Medical Sciences with Approval ID number: IR.YUMS.REC.1402.135).

Creation of burn wound

Before the burn wound creation, the anesthetization of rats was done with ketamine (10%, 100 mg/kg) (Bremer pharma GMBH 34414 Warburg, Germany). The dorsal neck area of rats was shaved with an electrical hair shaver and in this area; the second-degree burn was created employing a hot object heated to 95 °C for 15 S over a region of approximately 20 mm in diameter. Rats were randomly assigned into groups of 6 and housed individually.

Preparation of ointment

For the preparation of the applied ointments in this work, we used Eucerin® cream base(Sepidaj Pharmaceutical Company, Iran) as an emulsion base due to its ability in high absorbance of aqueous or oily solutions58. The used ointments were prepared with 25% concentration.

Burn wound treatment

The rats were divided into 6 groups, each containing 4 rats, as follows: Group I (normal skin) had no burn and no treatment; Group II (negative control group) had a burn without any treatment, and the wound area was washed with distilled water; Group III (positive control group) had burn wounds treated with 25% zinc oxide ointment; Group IV had burn wounds treated with 25% ointment of the Schiff base ligand; Group V had burn wounds treated with 25% ointment of the ZnLI₂ complex; and Group VI had burn wounds treated with 25% ointment of ZnI₂ salt. Treatment began 24 h after burn wound creation, was applied once daily, and continued for 21 days.

Results and discussion

Physical properties

The novel SB-Ligand (L) and its Zn(II) chelates (ZnLX₂, X = halide/pseudohalide) were synthesized with yields of 29–75%. The low yield (29%) for ZnL(NO₃)₂ is mainly attributed to its poor solubility in the reaction solvent (ethanol/methanol), causing premature precipitation and incorporation of impurities. Additionally, the nitrate anion may act as a weak oxidizing agent, potentially leading to minor ligand degradation. Finally, purification by recrystallization resulted in considerable product loss due to the complex’s low solubility. These colored compounds are stable at room temperature, soluble in DMSO and DMF, but insoluble in ethanol and methanol. Their molar conductivities (1.0–1.49 cm²Ω⁻¹mol⁻¹ at 25 °C in DMF) confirm non-electrolytic behavior, with zinc thiocyanate showing the highest dissociation60. The ligand is oily, while the zinc chelates decompose thermally between 117 and 261 °C without melting. Electronic spectra

UV-Vis spectra of the synthesized compounds were scanned in wavelengths range of 200–800 nm in dimethylformamide solution (4 × 10− 5 M) and their absorption data are presented in Fig. 1; Table 2. In the electronic spectrum of SB-ligand, an absorption band at 279 nm assigned to the electronic transition of π→π* of olefinic bond and aromatic rings, and an absorption band at 320 nm belonged to π→π* electronic transitions of C = N chromophores are observed61. Upon complexation, these bands shift to longer wavelengths proving the linkage of SB-ligand to central metal2,46–49. The electronic d→d transitions were no seen in zinc complexes because of d10 electronic configuration of metal ions62.

Infra-red spectra

FT-IR vibrational frequencies of the compounds were recorded in the range of 4000–400 cm− 1, and some of the characteristic peaks are compiled in Table 2. Representative FT-IR spectra of the SB-ligand, ZnLCl₂, and ZnL(NCS)₂ complexes are shown in Fig. 2. In the FT-IR spectrum of the SB-ligand, the absorption bands at 3285 cm− 1 and 1673 cm− 1, corresponding to the amino (NH₂) groups of triethylenetetramine and the carbonyl (C = O) group of o-methoxycinnamaldehyde, respectively, were not observed, confirming the absence of starting materials and the successful formation of the azomethine group. The vibration frequency of the azomethine group appears at 1631 cm− 1. Upon complexation, it shifts to lower frequencies within the range of 1623–1627 cm− 1 due to weakening of the bond. This weakening results from the donation of lone pair electrons from the imino nitrogen atoms to the Zn(II) ion, which reduces the electron density of the iminic bond, as well as from π-back bonding from the metal d-orbital to the π* orbital of the iminic bond63,64. The frequencey of the C-H bond of iminic group in SB-ligand appeared at 2830 cm− 1. The frequency of the N-H secondary amine groups in SB-ligand spectrum appears at 3274 cm− 1. The shifting of this peak in the complexes spectra clearly shows involving of these amine groups in coordination of SB-ligand to zinc (II) ion. The presence of strong bands at 2059 cm− 1 and 2071 cm− 1 regions of FT/IR spectra of ZnL(N3)2 and ZnL(NCS)2 indicate coordination of N3− and N-NCS− anions to metal center65. Appearance of new weak peaks in the range of 536–590 cm− ascribed to Zn-N bonds in FT/IR spectra of the complexes is another convincing reason for the linkage SB-ligand to zinc (II) ion66.

In the later step (490–647 °C), suggested segment of C10H8N2 was disrupted from the molecule with a mass loss of about 25.91% (Calcd. 27.36%). Furthermore, the thermo-kinetic activation parameters viz. Pharmacological activity

Antimicrobial activity

For antimicrobial bioassay, bactericidal and fungicidal properties of the synthesized compounds were assessed against some pathogenic organisms from various bacterial strains of G+ (B. subtilis, S. aureus), G− (P. aeruginosa, E. coli) and fungal strains (C. albicans and (A) oryzae). The antimicrobial bioassay investigations were done using three diverse amounts per well (0.625, 1.25 and 2.5 mg/well) by well diffusion method. The resultant data were evaluated as diameter of zone of inhibition (in mm) and as antimicrobial activities of the tested compounds have been listed in Tables 6 and 7 and as column plot are shown in Fig. 6. Zn (II) azide complex presented the best antibacterial activity toward (B) subtilis and P. aeruginosa and the lowest inhibition against E. coli in the mentioned three concentrations. Also, against E. coli and S. aureus, the ZnLCl2 complex had suitable inhibition in every three concentrations. Other complexes exhibited a suitable antimicrobial function against all the used pathogenic strains. In the case of A. oryzae, the highest inhibition is created by ZnL(NO3)2 complex, and the function of other compounds against this fungal strain is ordered as: ZnLCl2 > ZnLI2 ~ ZnLBr2 > ZnL(NCS)2 > ZnL(N3)2 > SB-ligand. Against (C) albicans, zinc halide complexes (ZnLCl2 ~ ZnLI2 > ZnLBr2) were better fungicide than zinc pseudohalide compounds. The different activity of the synthesized compounds toward the tested bacteria strains has been attributed via the impermeability of the microbe cells or the difference in ribosomes of microbial cells. The comparison of the antibacterial function of the tested compounds with standard drugs such as cephalexin, amoxicillin, and penicillin signifies appropriate activity but lower than them against the tested bacteria strains70. The results showed that the bactericidal activity of the zinc chelates is higher than that of the SB-ligand. This finding is also consistent with several recent reports on other types of complexes, such as cobalt and manganese complexes71–73. Zinc ions interact with bacterial enzymes, DNA, and the negatively charged peptidoglycan of the bacterial cell wall, thereby blocking bacterial cell activities such as cell division, replication, and respiration. Eventually, the release of intracellular contents into the environment leads to cell death74. Furthermore, the results indicate that increasing the concentration enhances the antimicrobial activity of the tested compounds. The superior antimicrobial performance of the zinc chloride, zinc azide, and zinc nitrate complexes compared to their zinc bromide, zinc iodide, and zinc thiocyanate counterparts can be rationalized by their physicochemical properties and how these align with known antibacterial and antifungal mechanisms. Chloride, azide, and nitrate are relatively small and high polar and more hydrophilic anions. It seems that their complexes with zinc exhibit higher solubility in studied media, which facilitates diffusion through microbial cell walls and membranes. This property directly supports the mechanism of membrane disruption, a key antibacterial pathway. Furthermore, after penetration, Zn²⁺ ions in the structure of ZnLCl₂, ZnL(N₃)₂, and ZnL(NO₃)₂ due to polarity of zinc-coordinated anion and higher Lewis acidity tend to bind with DNA, bacterial enzymes and etc. destabilize metabolic pathways leading to microorganism death75–77. In contrast, bromide, iodide, and thiocyanate are larger, less polar, and more lipophilic, leading to membrane disruption by their complexes and resulting in weaker penetration into bacterial structures. Moreover, after penetration, azide and nitrate ligands may also provide additional reactivity. Coordinated azide can generate reactive intermediates that damage DNA and proteins. Coordinated nitrate, under certain conditions, can participate in oxidative stress reactions, further destabilizing microbial cells. Bromide, iodide, and thiocyanate, however, are less effective at producing such reactive species, which reduces their antibacterial potency. Ultimately, the number of pathways through which each compound damages microorganisms, together with the extent of the destructive impact exerted by each pathway, determines the relative effectiveness of that compound compared to the others. A similar discussion can be applied to the comparison of antifungal activities.

Fragmentation of DNA after treatment

The efficiency of cleavage of DNA of E. coli as a target by SB-ligand and its zinc (II) chelates was evaluated using electrophoresis. The result has been shown in Fig. 8. Lanes A1 to G1 belong to the blend of DNA with SB-ligand, ZnLCl2, ZnLBr2, ZnLI2, ZnL(NCS)2, ZnL(NO3)2, and ZnL(N3)2, respectively. The lanes La, Pos, and Neg are ascribed to ladder, DNA (DNA alone as negative control), and the mix of H2O2 and DNA (as positive control), respectively. ZnLBr2 (lane C1), ZnL(NCS)2 (lane E1), ZnL(NO3)2 (lane F1), and ZnL(N3)2 (lane G1) complexes completely cleave DNA of E. coli, while ZnLCl2 (lane B1) and ZnLI2 (lane D1) complexes have displayed moderate cleavage and ligand (lane A1) cleaves DNA partially.

It is to be noted that Schiff base complexes of zinc represent a unique class of coordination compounds that can promote DNA cleavage through non-oxidative pathways. Unlike transition metal complexes of copper or iron, they do not rely on Fenton-type chemistry. They also do not generate reactive oxygen species (ROS) to induce strand scission. Instead, zinc Schiff base complexes operate via direct coordination and structural perturbation of the DNA double helix. The zinc center, being a Lewis acid, can interact with the phosphate backbone. This enhances the electrophilicity of the phosphor-diester bonds and thereby facilitates hydrolytic cleavage. In addition, Schiff base ligands often possess planar aromatic systems that enable intercalation between DNA base pairs. This increases torsional strain and destabilizes hydrogen bonding within the helix. This dual action (metal coordination and ligand intercalation) creates localized distortions. These distortions render the DNA more susceptible to cleavage even in the absence of oxidative stress. Furthermore, zinc complexes may act as catalytic centers for water activation. They promote nucleophilic attack on the phosphor-diester linkage and accelerate hydrolysis. These mechanisms highlight an important point. DNA cleavage by zinc Schiff base complexes is primarily a consequence of structural and hydrolytic effects. It is not due to radical-mediated oxidation79–81. Such properties have attracted significant interest in medicinal chemistry, as they provide a basis for designing non-oxidative anticancer and antimicrobial agents that minimize collateral oxidative damage to healthy cells. In the current study, it is evident that the investigated compounds cleave DNA into fragments so small that they do not correspond to the molecular weight range typically represented by the ladder bands. Consequently, these fragments are not detectable in the gel electrophoresis image.

The observed trend can be explained by the electronic properties and polarizability of the ligands. Complexes containing azide and thiocyanate exhibit the highest radical scavenging ability due to their strong electron-donating capacity and stabilization of intermediate radical species. In the case of azide, the negative charge is delocalized over three nitrogen atoms through resonance, which enhances electron density and facilitates transfer to the metal center or to radical species. Thiocyanate, on the other hand, is an ambidentate ligand that can coordinate through either nitrogen or sulfur; the sulfur atom is highly polarizable and possesses extended orbitals that readily participate in electron transfer processes. Iodide, with its high polarizability, shows greater activity than bromide and chloride, though less than that of azide and thiocyanate. Nitrate, being electron-withdrawing, reduces electron density and thus displays lower activity. Finally, bromide and chloride, with smaller size and lower polarizability, demonstrate the weakest antioxidant effects. Therefore, differences in electron-donating ability and ligand polarizability are the main factors determining the antioxidant strength of these complexes.

FRAP assay

FRAP is another method for evaluating the antioxidant function of the tested samples based on conversion of Fe3+-TPTZ to Fe2+-TPTZ. The results as depicted in Fig. 9 show that the ferric-reducing antioxidant power of all the complexes is higher than SB-ligand. Also, ZnLI2 complex displayed the highest antioxidant activity with respect to other complexes, and the activity of other complexes is ordered as ZnL(NCS)2 > ZnLBr2 ~ ZnL(NO3)2 ~ ZnL(N3)2 > ZnLCl2.

Ferric reducing-antioxidant power of SB-ligand and its Zn (II) complexes Statistically significant differences are given for the complexes as compared with the SB-ligand (***P < 0.001).

Analogous to the rationale provided for the DPPH assay, the observed sequence can be interpreted in terms of the electron-donating capacity and polarizability of the ligands. Nevertheless, because the FRAP method operates through a distinct mechanistic pathway (based on ferric ion reduction rather than radical scavenging), different mechanistic deductions must be considered to explain the variations in antioxidant activity. Iodide, being highly polarizable and an efficient electron donor, provides the strongest reducing capacity, resulting in the highest activity. Thiocyanate also shows significant electron-donating potential due to resonance and its polarizable sulfur atom, placing it next in the series. Bromide, nitrate, and azide exhibit moderate effects: bromide benefits from intermediate polarizability, nitrate is electron-withdrawing, and azide shows delocalized charge but is less effective in direct reduction compared to iodide or thiocyanate. Chloride, with the lowest polarizability and weakest donor ability, demonstrates the least antioxidant activity.

A comprehensive overview of the antioxidant activity data reveals some differences between the results obtained using the DPPH and FRAP assays. Differences between the antioxidant results obtained by the DPPH and FRAP assays arise from the distinct mechanisms underlying each method. The DPPH assay measures the ability of compounds to donate hydrogen atoms and directly scavenge free radicals, whereas the FRAP assay evaluates the reducing power of antioxidants by quantifying their capacity to convert Fe³⁺ to Fe²⁺. Consequently, some compounds may exhibit strong radical scavenging activity but relatively weaker reducing ability, or vice versa, leading to variations in the outcomes of the two methods. On the other hand, these differences in final results suggest different mechanisms of action for the two assays.

Anticancer activity

The relationship between DNA cleavage ability, antioxidant activity, and anticancer properties of a compound is multifaceted. Antioxidants generally protect normal cells by neutralizing reactive oxygen species and preventing oxidative DNA damage, thereby reducing mutagenesis and carcinogenesis. However, in cancer cells, certain compounds can simultaneously exhibit antioxidant capacity and induce DNA cleavage, thereby disrupting genomic integrity and triggering apoptosis. This dual behavior highlights the therapeutic potential of such compounds: they safeguard healthy cells from oxidative stress while selectively exerting cytotoxic effects on malignant cells through DNA damage. Consequently, the interplay between antioxidant and DNA-cleaving activities is considered a critical determinant of anticancer efficacy. Herein, our aim is to investigate the cytotoxicity of representative complexes, and therefore we selected three complexes with comparatively higher antioxidant activity and more DNA cleavage potential for this study. Accordingly, SB-ligand and its ZnLBr2, ZnLI2, and ZnL(NCS)2 chelates were screened for the anticancer activity toward human breast (MCF-7) and human prostate (PC3) cancer cells utilizing MTT assay and the results compared with cisplatin (positive control group) under identical conditions. The cell viability versus concentration of the compounds against two cancer cell lines has been illustrated as Fig. 10. The IC50 values and effective drug concentration (concentration required for 70% cell death) have been compiled in Table 10. Based on the graphs in Fig. 9, the decrease in cell viability depends on the concentration of the synthesized compounds, and with increasing concentration; a significant decrease is seen in cell viability. The concentration range employed was 0, 50, 75, 100, and 125 µg/mL. Concentrations exceeding 125 µg/mL were not assessed, as the IC50 values of all tested compounds presented in Table 10 were below this threshold. Hence, the selected concentration range was specifically designed to enable accurate determination of the IC50 values for the target compounds, rendering higher concentrations unnecessary for evaluating their anticancer activity. Based on IC50 values of the tested compounds against two titled cancer cell lines based on Fig. 9, it was revealed that the tested zinc chelates have higher cytotoxicity than that of SB-ligand, but their activities are lower than cisplatin as referenc in our conditions. ZnLI2 with IC50 of 68.53 µg/mL and ZnL(NCS)2 with IC50 of 77.91 µg/mL exhibited the highest cytotoxicity against MCF-7 and PC3 cell lines, respectively. This superiority of ZnLI2 and ZnL(NCS)2 arises from the higher polarizability and electron-donating ability of iodide and thiocyanate, which enhance cellular uptake, improve interactions with DNA and proteins, and create synergistic effects with zinc. In contrast, bromide provides weaker donor properties and lower bioactivity, resulting in reduced anticancer potential.

In vivo evaluation of burn wound healing effect of zinc iodide complex in rat

Although both antioxidant and antimicrobial properties play important roles in burn wound healing, their mechanisms differ significantly. Antioxidants primarily act by reducing oxidative stress, neutralizing free radicals, and minimizing tissue damage, thereby creating a favorable environment for cell proliferation and tissue regeneration. In contrast, antimicrobial agents are essential for preventing and controlling infections, which represent one of the most critical complications in burn injuries. Therefore, combining antioxidant and antimicrobial effects provides synergistic benefits, as antioxidants enhance tissue repair while antimicrobials protect against microbial invasion, ultimately leading to more effective and accelerated wound healing. Among the titled compounds in this study, zinc halide complexes are less expensive than others. Among the zinc halide complexes, although the zinc iodide complex exhibited moderate and, in some cases, comparable antimicrobial activity relative to other ones, its antioxidant activity was found to be superior to that of the other zinc halide complexes. This pronounced antioxidant capacity provided the rationale for its selection in the present investigation, underscoring its potential to contribute novel insights into the burn wound healing application of these zinc-based Schiff base complexes as compared with free SB-ligand, zinc iodide salt and zinc oxide (as known effective compound).

Overview on the biological properties of studied Schiff base compounds

According to the literature84–86, the biological activities of zinc Schiff base complexes arise from the synergistic interplay between the ligand (imine groups and aromatic frameworks enabling interactions with biomolecules) and the zinc ion (stabilizing the coordination environment, enhancing solubility, lipophilicity and cellular uptake)75–77. Although zinc is redox-inert compared to copper or iron, its Lewis acidity and coordination flexibility reinforce biological activity. Importantly, antimicrobial, antioxidant, and anticancer properties are related but not directly proportional, as each involves distinct mechanisms (e.g., membrane disruption, oxidative stress modulation, apoptosis induction) while sharing common pathways such as ROS regulation. In the present study, the highly antimicrobial ZnLCl₂ did not exhibit strong antioxidant activity, whereas ZnLI₂ with moderate antimicrobial activity showed superior antioxidant properties. Furthermore, zinc iodide and thiocyanate complexes, which displayed higher antioxidant activity (DPPH and FRAP assays), also exhibited greater anticancer effects against the tested cancer cell lines.

Conclusion

In summary, a novel N₄-tetradentate Schiff base ligand (L) and its six zinc(II) halide/pseudohalide complexes were synthesized and characterized. Thermal analysis (TG/DTG/DTA) using the Coats–Redfern method provided key kinetic parameters such as ΔH*, ΔG*, ΔS*. Antimicrobial evaluation against Gram-positive, Gram-negative, and fungal strains revealed that ZnLCl₂, ZnL(N₃)₂, and ZnL(NO₃)₂ exhibited superior activity. Antioxidant assays showed ZnL(N₃)₂ as the best DPPH scavenger (IC₅₀ = 52.87 µg/mL) and ZnLI₂ as the strongest FRAP reducing agent. DNA cleavage studies confirmed that ZnLBr₂, ZnL(NCS)₂, ZnL(NO₃)₂, and ZnL(N₃)₂ completely cleaved E. coli DNA via a non-oxidative hydrolytic mechanism. MTT assays against MCF-7 and PC3 cancer cells demonstrated that ZnLI₂ (IC₅₀ = 68.53 µg/mL) and ZnL(NCS)₂ (IC₅₀ = 77.91 µg/mL) showed the highest anticancer activity. In a second-degree burn wound model, ZnLI₂ achieved 100% wound closure by day 21 with complete re-epithelialization, performing comparably to zinc oxide. Collectively, these zinc complexes particularly ZnLI₂ and ZnL(N₃)₂ exhibit promising multifunctional therapeutic potential for biomedical applications.

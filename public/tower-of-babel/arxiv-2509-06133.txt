A GAIA-X-Aligned, Blockchain-Anchored Privacy-Preserving, Zero-Knowledge Digital Passport for Smart Vehicles
Pradyumna Kaushal

Abstract
Problem- From the OEM production line through successive owners, a vehicle accumulates a rich yet fragmented life-cycle record that today is stored in opaque silos, cannot be independently verified, and forces insurers, service centres, regulators, and buyers into costly Know-Your-Customer (KYC) procedures. Contribution- VehiclePassport introduces a trust-less, privacy-preserving digital passport whose on-chain hash immutably commits to every event -manufacturing, maintenance, ownership transfers, telemetry and internal (battery,motor) health-while zero-knowledge (ZK) proofs let stakeholders validate high-level statements without seeing raw data. A short-lived, field-scoped JWT conveys a selective JSON-LD credential; Polygon zkEVM ensures anchored logs cannot be altered; and GAIA-X self-descriptions enable sovereign, cross-border interoperability. Results- An open-source reference stack (backend,API’s, SDK and telemetry middleware) processes 1 Hz streams for a million vehicles, anchors hashes for $0.02 each, and validates ZK proofs in sub-10 ms-empowering insurers, service centres, resellers and owners to transact instantly with cryptographic assurance. Impact- The passport collapses paperwork, removes centralized trust, and establishes an auditable continuum from factory provisioning to the current driver, laying the groundwork for global, GDPR-compliant mobility data markets.

I
Introduction

Modern vehicles are secure IoT datacentres on wheels, emitting up to
\sim
25 GB h
-1
of diagnostics and telemetry. Yet resale buyers, insurers and regulators still rely on paper service books or unverifiable PDFs. We propose
VehiclePassport

(i)

every vehicle is issued a
vehicle-bound (soul-bound)
ERC-721 token whose ownership can transfer between people while remaining permanently attached to the physical car,

(ii)

that token points to an immutable on-chain hash of the vehicle’s JSON-LD life-cycle document,

(iii)

selective ZK proofs replace hand-written bills of sale, RTO paperwork and KYC checks, and

(iv)

GAIA-X metadata makes the passport discoverable and machine-interpretable across EU data spaces.

In short, the NFT declares
who

everything you need to know or prove
about that asset-without leaking private telemetry.

II
Background and Related Work

II-A

Legacy Vehicle-Data Ecosystems

Pre-connected vehicles funnel telemetry into proprietary OEM clouds or national databases. These central stores cannot

(a)

give a buyer cryptographic confidence in battery or odometer claims,

(b)

let a driver prove

(c)

II-B

Need for Privacy-Preserving Selective-Disclosure

A privacy-preserving vehicle passport solves three long-standing problems:

•

Trust gap
- buyers distrust owner or OEM claims; ZK lets the car
prove
it meets a policy.

•

One-size-fits-all data dumps
- regulators need CO
2

•

Mutability
- post-event edits in centralized DBs enable odometer fraud; chained hashes prevent it.

II-C

Blockchain Approaches

Projects such as Bosch AMS or early MOBI VID store whole JSON payloads on-chain, inflating gas cost and breaching GDPR. Others mint transferrable VIN NFTs but lack selective disclosure. No prior work merges a soul-bound vehicle NFT, off-chain JSON-LD, ZK proofs
and
GAIA-X compliance.

II-D

GAIA-X Primer

GAIA-X (v3.0) mandates machine-readable
self-descriptions
in JSON-LD, W3C
verifiable credentials
, and policy endpoints for federated usage control. VehiclePassport realises these artefacts (Sec.
VI

III
System Goals

G1

Integrity
. Off-chain artefacts are SHA-256 hashed, on-chain identifiers use Keccak-256; every digest is anchored so any tampering becomes publicly detectable.

G2

Selective Privacy
. Short-lived, field-scoped JWTs deliver only the data the requester is entitled to, while Groth16 ZK-proofs attest to those facts against the owner-held telemetry.
A verifier recomputes the on-chain hash, validates the proof, and-when everything matches-shows a
“Verified-by-Matter ZK™”
badge. Raw sensor data stays local; nothing sensitive ever leaves the owner’s domain.

G3

GAIA-X Alignment
. Passport stored as JSON-LD; policy API and verifiable credentials satisfy GAIA-X meta-model.

G4

Economic Viability
. Hash anchoring costs
\leq
$0.02 on Polygon zkEVM, and the same contracts port unchanged to any Ethereum-equivalent zk-roll-up (StarkNet, Scroll, Optimism Bedrock, etc.), keeping fees low and throughput high.

G5

Trust-less Transfer
. Ownership moves through a dual-party signature handshake: both seller and buyer co-sign the sale record
\rightarrow
hash is anchored on-chain
\rightarrow
the vehicle-bound NFT is safely transferred-eliminating paper RTO / KYC friction.

G6

User Flow

(b)

(c)

D.
Service History Logging

This module enables cryptographically signed and owner-approved service records that are permanently attached to a vehicle’s passport and anchored on-chain.

Phase 1 - Service Center Initiation.

A service center begins by scanning the QR code on the vehicle or manually entering the vehicle ID. They then input a detailed service description and connect their wallet ( service centre private key ) to submit the log.

Portal Flow:

On the Service Portal, the center submits their data:
vehicleId
,
description
, and
centerEmail
. The system creates a deterministic JSON payload, hashes it using
keccak256
, and generates a digital signature using the center’s private key via MetaMask.

Security Basis:

The signature proves:

•

The service center owns the private key (identity).

•

They authorized the specific service log (intent).

Phase 2 - Backend Validation.

The backend verifies the signature by recovering the address and comparing it to the submitted one. If valid, it stores a
PendingServiceLog
with status=
pending
and links it to the vehicle.

Phase 3 - Owner Review.

The vehicle owner dashboard fetches all pending logs via database joins. Each log displays the description, center email, and original hash. The owner physically verifies the work done and proceeds to sign the same hash using MetaMask.

Dual-Signature Security:

Both signatures bind to the same hash, proving mutual consent. The service center can’t deny they performed the service, and the owner can’t deny their approval, as illustrated in Fig.
5
.

Phase 4 - Blockchain Anchoring.

Once both signatures are collected, the backend calls the
ServiceLogAnchor
smart contract on the Polygon testnet. The contract:

•

Rejects duplicate hashes.

•

Emits a
LogAnchored
event.

•

Stores the hash immutably on-chain.

Phase 5 - Finalization.

The system creates a finalized
ServiceLog
record containing:

•

Both signatures

•

The original service data

•

The blockchain transaction hash

The pending record is deleted atomically to ensure only one state exists.

Trust Architecture:

•

Tamper Evident:
Any change to the data invalidates the hash.

•

Regulatory-Grade:
All logs are dual-authenticated and anchored.

•

Trustless:
No central authority is needed; cryptography + blockchain guarantees authenticity.

This ensures a permanent, verifiable, and trustworthy service history embedded into the vehicle’s on-chain passport.

The efficacy and safety of a 577 nm diode laser in the treatment of cutaneous warts: A prospective single-arm study
Authors: Essamelden Mohamed
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractWarts are a common skin disease caused by the human papillomavirus. While many treatments exist, none are entirely effective. Our objective is to study the efficacy and safety of a 577 nm diode laser in the treatment of cutaneous warts due to its strong oxyhemoglobin absorption for targeting wart vasculature. Patients with common warts (n = 31) were enrolled in this prospective study. Patients with history of poor wound healing (immunosuppressed or uncontrolled diabetics) and those with a scar and keloid formation, Koebner phenomenon, or active infection at the local site are excluded from the study. Patients received sessions every two weeks of 577 nm diode laser treatment, continuous mode, 1 mm spot and 3–5 W power according to wart size; sessions were repeated every 2 weeks until full clearance was achieved. Patients were followed up clinically and by photography every 2 weeks and 3 months after the final session. This prospective study included 31 patients clinically diagnosed with common warts; their mean age was 22.77 ± 4.74 years, 16 (51.6%) patients were males and 15 (48.4%) were females, 18 (58.06%) patients were skin type IV and 13 (41.94%) cases were skin type V and the mean duration of warts was 6.7 ± 4.24 months. Lesions were present in the hands in 14 patients (45.2%), the feet in 8 patients (25.8%), upper extremities (3 patients, 9.7%), lips (3 patients, 9.7%), and scalp (3 patients, 9.7%). All patients were treated with a 577 nm diode laser session every 2 weeks until complete improvement to maximum 3 sessions. Complete improvement was achieved in 71% of patients (22/31) after one session and in all patients after three sessions. The mean healing time was 1.34 ± 0.56 weeks. Wart improvement significantly correlated with the number of wart (P ≤ 0.05), but not with patient age, sex, skin types or wart duration (P > 0.05). Most patients reported no side effects. However, post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation occurred in 1 case (3.2%), and recurrence in 3 cases (9.6%). The 577-diode laser is a safe and effective wart treatment modality, with efficacy correlated to the number of warts. Introduction

Cutaneous warts are common proliferative skin diseases caused by human papillomavirus (HPV) infection of keratinocytes with a prevalence rate of 7–12% [1, 2]. Warts are significant not only for cosmetic reasons but also due to their association with other medical conditions. Although traditional therapeutic options for warts, such as topical salicylic acid, topical imiquimod, bleomycin injections, cryotherapy, surgical excision, and electrocautery, have proven effective, most of these methods are painful and carry the risk of scarring and do not prevent the recurrence [3]. Due to the potential for spontaneous resolution, especially in children, an ideal treatment should be effective while minimizing pain and scarring [4].

Laser therapy offers an alternative solution to traditional treatment methods. Laser therapy destroys HPV-infected tissue by converting absorbed light energy into heat [5]. Several types of lasers, such as carbon dioxide, 1064 nm Nd: YAG, and pulsed dye lasers, have also been used [6]. Lasers work by inducing local thermal damage, leading to direct cell death, and may even stimulate local cell-–mediated immunity (7). The 577-nm yellow diode laser is highly absorbed by oxyhemoglobin, making it suitable for treating skin diseases with a vascular component [8].

The aim of this study was to evaluate the efficacy and safety of 577 nm diode laser in the treatment of cutaneous warts.

Patients and methods

Study design and ethical approval

This Prospective Single-Arm Study aimed to study the efficacy and safety of a 577 nm diode laser in the treatment of cutaneous warts. The study adhered to the Declaration of Helsinki and was approved by the local Institutional Review Board. Informed written consent was obtained from all participants.

Study population

Thirty-one patients clinically diagnosed with warts were enrolled in this prospective study; they were selected from attendees of the Laser Unit of the Dermatology and Andrology Department between July 2024 and February 2025. All participants provided informed written consent prior to enrollment. Exclusion criteria included a history of Koebner phenomenon, scar or keloid formation, active infection at the local treatment site, bleeding disorders, lidocaine allergy, systemic corticosteroid or other immunosuppressant use, and pregnancy. Wart diagnosis was made clinically by two expert dermatologists. Diagnosis was based on classic verrucous morphology, patient history, and exclusion of differential diagnoses such as fibroma, papilloma, and traumatic lesions.

Laser devices and treatment protocol

Patients received two weekly sessions of the 577 nm diode laser (QuadroStar PRO YELLOW; Asclepion Laser Technologies GmbH, Jena, Germany) until full clearance. Before treatment, the procedure was explained in detail to alleviate patient anxiety, and both the patient and the physician wore protective goggles. After intralesional anesthesia, the laser was applied in continuous mode, with power adjusted between 3 and 5 watts according to wart size. A 1 mm spot-size handpiece was used, and repeated passes were performed until the endpoint of treatment was achieved, defined as grey discoloration of the wart and its easy removal with saline-soaked gauze or a wet dressing. Sessions were repeated weekly until complete clearance was achieved. A topical antibiotic was applied for 1 week after each session.

Outcome measures and follow-up

Primary outcomes included clinical clearance of the wart, assessed at baseline and 3 months following the final treatment session. Standardized photographs were taken at baseline and at the final visit using a Canon PowerShot A3400 IS 16 MP digital camera. Improvement was evaluated by two independent dermatologists using standardized before-andafter- images, and clinical response was categorized as complete improvement (100% clearance of the visible lesion with restoration of normal skin lines), partial improvement (50–99% reduction in lesion size or thickness), or no improvement (< 50% reduction). Outcome assessment was based on dermatologist evaluations, standardized photography.

Adverse events and safety monitoring

Patients were monitored for immediate and delayed side effects during and after treatment and at follow-up visits. They were instructed to report any infections, ulcers, or wart recurrences, defined as reappearance at the treated site during follow-up.

Statistical analysis

SPSS version 23.0 was used for data analysis. Continuous variables are presented as mean ± SD; categorical variables as frequencies and percentages.

Results

In this prospective study, 31 patients with common warts were enrolled. The mean age of the patients was 22.77 ± 4.74 (range: 18–38 years); 16 (51.6%) patients were males and 15 (48.4%) were females, 18 (58.06%) patients were skin type IV and 13 (41.94%) cases were skin type V. Patients were presented with a mean of 3.13 ± 4.6 warts, the mean duration of warts was 6.7 ± 4.24 months. Most lesions were located on the hands (14 patients, 45.2%), followed by the feet (8 patients, 25.8%), upper extremities (3 patients, 9.7%), lips (3 patients, 9.7%), and scalp (3 patients, 9.7%) (Table 1).

Discussion

Existing wart treatments have variable results and are not ideal, as none are effective for all cases [9, 10]. Wart treatment may vary depending on the wart type and site and may require more than one treatment modality [4].

Many laser devices have been used in the treatment of warts, with varying degrees of success depending on the laser device and patient characteristics. A 2016 review found CO₂, PDL, and Nd: YAG lasers the most studied for non-genital verrucae, with response rates of 50–100%, 47.1–100%, and 46.34–100%, respectively. PDL effectiveness was comparable to traditional therapies like cryotherapy and topical cantharidin [11].

Alshami and Mohana [12] studied the effect of long pulsed Nd: YAG in wart treatment; they achieved complete clearance of all warts, with 90% of patients cured after one session. Also, El-Mohamady et al. [13]. found that both Nd: YAG laser and PDL are effective and safe for treating recalcitrant plantar warts.

The 577 nm diode laser is a specific type of laser that uses a 577 nm wavelength, which is highly absorbed by oxyhemoglobin and, to a lesser extent, melanin, which makes it suitable for treating many skin diseases, e.g., vascular skin diseases, vascular tumors, pigmentary disorders, and benign tumors, like warts, through their destructive effect on their blood vessels. Vascular lasers clear warts through photothermal destruction, directly targeting the heat-sensitive HPV virus and indirectly destroying capillaries feeding infected keratinocytes via photothermolysis [11].

In our study, a 577 nm diode laser treatment effectively eliminated wart lesions, with most of the lesions cleared after the first session and the remaining cleared after the second and third sessions, and the efficacy of treatment correlates with the number of the lesions.

A review of the extant literature reveals a paucity of published data on the application of the 577 nm diode laser for treating cutaneous warts. This response rate is comparable to that achieved in studies using a and PDL [13, 14, 15]. Like the pulsed dye laser, the Diode laser offers a distinct advantage due to its short wavelength characteristics. The specific wavelength of 577 nm diode laser significantly lowers the probability of ulceration occurring because of its use. Furthermore, this beneficial attribute eliminates the necessity for a dressing to be applied to the treated area following the laser procedure, the specific wavelength minimizes tissue damage, contributing to a reduced risk of ulcer formation. This, in turn, simplifies the post-treatment care, as dressings are not required to promote healing or protect the affected region [16].

Mohamed et al. found both 577-nm diode and 1064-nm Nd: YAG lasers effective for wart treatment, but their study had limited representation of darker skin types and a short follow-up [17].

577 diode laser treatments are generally safe, with most patients experiencing no complications. Recurrence occurred in 9.6% of cases, while post-inflammatory hypopigmentation occurred in only 3.2%.

Recurrence after wart treatment remains a significant clinical challenge. Compared with PDL; recurrence varies across studies but is generally low. Higher rates reached 14.4% at 9 weeks and 31% at 2.4 months [18, 19]. This variability in recurrence rates can be attributed to a multitude of factors that have been implicated in the persistence or reappearance of warts. These factors include the specific type of wart being treated, as several types of human fluence and propensities for recurrence. Also, the presence of associated immunosuppression can significantly increase the risk of wart recurrence [20].

A limitation of the study is that it is a single-arm prospective study without a comparative control group and limited diversity in wart types and locations. Although various sites were treated, the number of patients with warts in areas other than the hands and feet was small. Furthermore, large or periungual warts, often treatment-resistant, were underrepresented. Additionally, all enrolled patients were Fitzpatrick skin types IV–V, which may limit the generalizability of the findings to individuals with other skin types.

We concluded that within the constraints of this single-arm prospective study, the 577‑nm diode laser demonstrated promising safety and effectiveness as a monotherapy for wart treatment.

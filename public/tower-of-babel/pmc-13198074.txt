Alternating Bundle Branch Block Despite a Patent Stent in Late-Presenting Extensive Anterior STEMI

Alternating bundle branch block (ABBB) is a rare, high-risk marker of advanced infra-Hisian disease in anterior ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI). It may precede delayed high-grade atrioventricular (AV) block despite successful primary percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI).

Take-Home Message

•Alternating bundle branch block warrants continuous monitoring and pacing readiness, even when angiography appears reassuring.

History of Presentation

A 39-year-old man, an active smoker, presented with ischemic chest pain that began 19 hours before admission. The initial electrocardiogram showed extensive anterior ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI) with a right bundle branch block (RBBB) pattern. High-sensitivity cardiac troponin I was 3,718 ng/L (reference: <20 ng/L). On arrival, the RBBB resolved while ST-segment elevation persisted, raising concern for evolving conduction system injury. He was hemodynamically stable.

Differential Diagnosis

Extensive anterior STEMI from an ostial left anterior descending artery (LAD) occlusion with dynamic bundle branch block patterns was suspected, with risk of high-grade AV block. After recurrent arrest post-PCI, stent thrombosis/reocclusion and mechanical complications were considered; repeat angiography confirmed patent stents, favoring primary electrical instability.

Investigations

Serial electrocardiography showed RBBB, then RBBB resolution with persistent ST-segment elevation, then new left bundle branch block after PCI, consistent with ABBB (Figures 1A to 1C). Rhythm remained sinus (P waves preceding each QRS), distinguishing it from accelerated idioventricular rhythm. Angiography showed total ostial LAD occlusion (Figure 1D).

Findings on Serial Electrocardiography and Angiography

(A) Initial electrocardiogram showed extensive anterior STEMI with RBBB pattern, then (B) persistent STEMI with RBBB resolution. (C) Electrocardiogram after PCI showed new LBBB, consistent with ABBB. (D) Angiography showed ostial left anterior descending artery occlusion before PCI and (E) TIMI flow grade III after PCI. (F) Angiography after 12 hours showed patent stents, with no thrombosis. ABBB = alternating bundle branch block; LBBB = left bundle branch block; PCI = percutaneous coronary intervention; RBBB = right bundle branch block; STEMI = ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction.

Management

Dual antiplatelet therapy was given before PCI. Two overlapping drug-eluting stents were deployed, with final TIMI flow grade III (Figure 1E). Temporary pacing was not performed because the patient was stable. Twelve hours later, telemetry showed sinus bradycardia progressing to complete AV block with an unstable escape rhythm and recurrent cardiac arrest; repeat angiography confirmed patent stents (Figure 1F). A temporary transvenous pacemaker stabilized the rhythm, and AV conduction recovered within 24 hours.

Outcome and Follow-Up

AV conduction gradually recovered within 24 hours after temporary pacing. The patient remained clinically stable in the intensive care unit and was discharged in good condition.

Discussion

This case underscores 4 concepts: dynamic bundle branch block patterns in extensive anterior STEMI may reflect septal conduction system injury; successful epicardial reperfusion does not indicate microvascular recovery; tissue ischemia or reperfusion injury may precipitate delayed AV block; and ABBB in acute myocardial infarction should prompt prolonged monitoring and a low threshold for temporary pacing.1,2

The right bundle branch is supplied by septal perforators from the proximal LAD, explaining early RBBB with proximal/ostial LAD occlusion, whereas deeper septal involvement may affect the left bundle and progress to AV block. Clinically, ABBB should prompt prolonged monitoring and a low threshold for temporary pacing; recurrent arrest or AV block after PCI warrants evaluation for recurrent ischemia with pacing support.1,3

Conclusions

In late-presenting extensive anterior STEMI, a patent stent and TIMI flow grade III may not exclude life-threatening electrical instability. ABBB despite a patent stent signals advanced infra-Hisian involvement and warrants close monitoring and early pacing readiness.

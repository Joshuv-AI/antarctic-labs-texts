Quantitative results on a generalized viscosity approximation method
Paulo Firmino, Laurenţiu Leuştean, [2mm] Departamento de Matemática, Faculdade de Ciências, Universidade de Lisboa

Abstract
In this paper, we study, in a nonlinear setting, the asymptotic behaviour of a generalized viscosity approximation method associated with a countable family of nonexpansive mappings satisfying resolvent-like conditions. We apply proof mining methods to obtain quantitative results on asymptotic regularity in W W -hyperbolic spaces and rates of metastability in CAT(0) spaces. Keywords: Viscosity approximation method; Resolvent-like conditions; Asymptotic regularity; Metastability; Proof mining. Mathematics Subject Classification 2010 : 47H05, 47H09, 47J25, 03F10.



1
Introduction

Let
X
be a
W
-space,
C
a nonempty convex subset of
X
and
(T_{n}:C\to C)_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
a sequence of nonexpansive mappings with common fixed points.
The generalized Viscosity Approximation Method (genVAM, for short) is defined as follows:

x_{0}=x\in C,\quad x_{n+1}=\alpha_{n}f(x_{n})+(1-\alpha_{n})T_{n}x_{n},

where
(\alpha_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is a sequence in
[0,1]
and
f:C\to C
is an
\alpha
-contraction
with
\alpha\in[0,1)
.

If
f
is constant, the genVAM becomes the abstract Halpern-type Proximal Point Algorithm (abstract HPPA, for short), a
generalization of the well-known Halpern-type Proximal Point Algorithm (HPPA), which was introduced by
Xu
[
35
]
and by Kamimura and Takahashi
[
13
]
. The abstract HPPA has been studied recently by Aoyama, Kimura, Takahashi and
Toyoda
[
2
]
and, using proof mining methods, by Sipoş
[
30
]
and by Kohlenbach and Pinto
[
22
]
.
The Viscosity Approximation Method (VAM) in Banach spaces, due to Xu et al.
[
36
]
, is also a particular case of the genVAM iteration.

In this paper we study the asymptotic behaviour of the genVAM iteration by assuming that the family

(T_{n})
satisfies resolvent-like conditions introduced by the second author, Nicolae and Sipoş
[
25
]

in their abstract analysis of the proximal point algorithm. Resolvents of maximally monotone operators in Hilbert spaces, as well as resolvents
of
m
-accretive operators in Banach spaces, are examples of families of mappings that satisfy these conditions.

In Section
4
we study the genVAM iteration in
W
-hyperbolic spaces and compute rates of (
(T_{n})
-)asymptotic regularity, as well as rates of

T_{m}
-asymptotic regularity for all
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
. In this way, we extend to our very general setting quantitative asymptotic results obtained for the VAM in Banach
spaces by the authors
[
11
]
and for the HPPA in Hilbert spaces by
the second author and Pinto
[
26
]
. Furthermore, as an application of a lemma due to Sabach and Shtern
[
29
]
, linear such rates are computed
for particular choices of the parameter sequences.

In Section
5
, we first obtain, in the setting of complete CAT(0) spaces, uniform rates of metastability for the abstract HPPA,
thereby generalizing - while providing simpler proofs and simpler rates - recent results of Sipoş
[
30
]
.
Furthermore, by applying a method developed by Kohlenbach and Pinto
[
22
]
, we derive rates of metastability
for the genVAM iteration and, as an immediate consequence, establish a (qualitative) strong convergence result for this iteration.

The results presented in this paper are obtained using techniques from proof mining, a research field concerned with
extracting new quantitative and qualitative information from mathematical proofs through proof-theoretic methods.
A detailed presentation of the proof mining program can be found in Kohlenbach’s monograph
[
16
]
, and more recent
applications of proof mining are surveyed in
[
18
,
19
]
.

We recall below some notation and notions used throughout the paper. We denote
{\mathbb{N}}^{*}={\mathbb{N}}\setminus\{0\}
and
{\mathbb{R}}^{+}=(0,\infty)
.
For all
m,n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we set
m\mathop{-\!\!\!\!\!\hskip 2.84526pt\raisebox{1.42262pt}{$\cdot$}}\nolimits n=\max\{m-n,0\}
. Moreover, if
n\geq m
, we write
[m;n]=\{m,m+1,\ldots,n\}
.
For
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
\lceil x\rceil
denotes the ceiling of
x
. For any mapping
T
,
Fix(T)
is the set of fixed points of
T
.
For every
g:{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{N}}
, the mappings
\tilde{g},g^{+}:{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{N}}
are defined as follows:

\tilde{g}(n)=n+g(n),\quad g^{+}(n)=\max\{g(i)\mid i\in[0;n]\}.

Furthermore, if

,
g^{(n)}
denotes the
n

Preliminaries

2.1
W
-hyperbolic and CAT(0) spaces

A
W
-space
[
8
]
is a structure
(X,d,W)
, where
(X,d)
is a metric space and

W:X\times X\times[0,1]\to X
.
These structures have been already considered by Takahashi
[
32
]
,
W
being called a

convexity mapping
, as
W(x,y,\lambda)
is thought of as an abstract convex
combination of the points
x,y\in X
with parameter
\lambda
.
We denote a
W
-space simply by
X
. A nonempty subset
C
of
X
is
said to be convex if for every
x,y\in C
,
W(x,y,\lambda)\in C
for all

\lambda\in[0,1]
.

A
W
-hyperbolic space
[
15
]
is a
W
-space
X
satisfying for all
x,y,z,w\in X
and all
\lambda,\tilde{\lambda}\in[0,1]
,

\displaystyle(W1)

\displaystyle d(z,W(x,y,\lambda))\leq(1-\lambda)d(z,x)+\lambda d(z,y),

\displaystyle(W2)

\displaystyle d(W(x,y,\lambda),W(x,y,\tilde{\lambda}))=|\lambda-\tilde{\lambda}|d(x,y),

\displaystyle(W3)

\displaystyle W(x,y,\lambda)=W(y,x,1-\lambda),

\displaystyle(W4)

\displaystyle\,\,\,d(W(x,z,\lambda),W(y,w,\lambda))\leq(1-\lambda)d(x,y)+\lambda d(z,w).

A normed space is, obviously, a
W
-hyperbolic space: just define
W(x,y,\lambda)=(1-\lambda)x+\lambda y
. Important classes of
W
-hyperbolic spaces are
Busemann spaces
[
7
,
28
]
, UCW-hyperbolic spaces
[
23
,
24
]
and CAT(0) spaces
[
4
,
1
]
. As proved in
[
16
, pp. 386-388]
, a CAT(0) space can be defined as a
W
-hyperbolic space
X
satisfying
for all
x,y,z\in X
,

d^{2}\left(z,W\left(x,y,\frac{1}{2}\right)\right)\leq\frac{1}{2}d^{2}(z,x)+\frac{1}{2}d^{2}(z,y)-\frac{1}{4}d^{2}(x,y).

We shall use from now on the notation
(1-\lambda)x+\lambda y
for
W(x,y,\lambda)
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
X
be a
W
-hyperbolic space. For all
x,y,w,z\in X
,
\lambda,\widetilde{\lambda}\in[0,1]
,

\displaystyle d(x,(1-\lambda)x+\lambda y)=\lambda d(x,y)\text{ and }d(y,(1-\lambda)x+\lambda y)=(1-\lambda)d(x,y),

(1)

\displaystyle\lambda x+(1-\lambda)x=x,\quad 1x+0y=x,\quad 0x+1y=y,

(2)

\displaystyle d((1-\lambda)x+\lambda z,(1-\widetilde{\lambda})y+\widetilde{\lambda}w)\leq(1-\lambda)d(x,y)+\lambda d(z,w)+\left|\lambda-\widetilde{\lambda}\right|d(y,w),

(3)

\displaystyle d(\lambda x+(1-\lambda)z,\widetilde{\lambda}y+(1-\widetilde{\lambda})w)\leq\lambda d(x,y)+(1-\lambda)d(z,w)+\left|\lambda-\widetilde{\lambda}\right|d(y,w).

(4)

Proof.

(
1
) and (
2
) were proved by
Takahashi
[
32
]
for
W
-spaces satisfying (W1). See also
[
16
, Proposition 17.17]
.
(
3
) is proved in
[
8
, Lemma 2.1(iv)]
. Apply (
3
) with

\lambda:=1-\lambda
and
\tilde{\lambda}:=1-\tilde{\lambda}

to get (
4
).
∎

2.2
Quantitative notions and lemmas

Let
(x_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
be a sequence in a metric space
(X,d)
and
\varphi:{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{N}}
. If
(x_{n})
is Cauchy, then

\varphi
is a Cauchy modulus of
(x_{n})
if for all
k,n\in{\mathbb{N}}
with
n\geq\varphi(k)
,

\forall p\in{\mathbb{N}}\left(d(x_{n+p},x_{n})\leq\frac{1}{k+1}\right).

If
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}x_{n}=x\in X
, then
\varphi
is a rate of convergence of

(x_{n})
if for all
k,n\in{\mathbb{N}}
with
n\geq\varphi(k)
,

d(x_{n},x)\leq\frac{1}{k+1}.

Lemma 2.2
.

Assume that
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}x_{n}=x
with rate of convergence
\varphi
. Define

\varphi^{*}:{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{N}},\quad\varphi^{*}(k)=\varphi(2k+1).

Then
\varphi^{*}
is a Cauchy modulus of
(x_{n})
.

Proof.

For every
k,n,p\in{\mathbb{N}}
with
n\geq\varphi^{*}(k)
, we have that

d(x_{n+p},x_{n})\leq d(x_{n+p},x)+d(x_{n},x)\leq\frac{1}{2(k+1)}+\frac{1}{2(k+1)}=\frac{1}{k+1}.

∎

Let
\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}
be a series of nonnegative real numbers and

\left(\tilde{a}_{n}\right)
be its sequence of partial sums,
that is
\tilde{a}_{n}=\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}
for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. If
\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}

converges, then by a Cauchy modulus of
\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}
we understand a Cauchy modulus of
\left(\tilde{a}_{n}\right)
.
If
\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}
diverges, then a rate of divergence of
\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}
is a mapping
\theta:{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{N}}
satisfying

\sum\limits_{i=0}^{\theta(n)}a_{i}\geq n
for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Assume that
\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}
diverges with rate of divergence
\theta
.

(i)

If
(a_{n})\subseteq[0,1]
, then
\theta(n)\geq n-1
for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

(ii)

For all
N\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}
,
\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n+N}
diverges
with rate of divergence

\theta^{*}(n)=\theta\left(n+\left\lceil\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}a_{i}\right\rceil\right)\mathop{-\!\!\!\!\!\hskip 2.84526pt\raisebox{1.42262pt}{$\cdot$}}\nolimits N
. If

(a_{n})\subseteq[0,1]
, then we can take
\theta^{*}(n)=\theta^{+}(n+N)\mathop{-\!\!\!\!\!\hskip 2.84526pt\raisebox{1.42262pt}{$\cdot$}}\nolimits N
.

(iii)

For all
c\in(0,\infty)
,
\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}ca_{n}
diverges with rate of
divergence
\chi(n)=\theta\left(\left\lceil\frac{n}{c}\right\rceil\right)
.

(iv)

If
\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{n}
diverges with rate of divergence
\gamma
, then

\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}(a_{n}+b_{n})
diverges with rate of divergence

\omega(n)=\min\{\theta(n),\gamma(n)\}
.

Proof.

(i)

Let
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and assume, by contradiction, that
\theta(n)\leq n-2
. It follows that

\sum\limits_{i=0}^{\theta(n)}a_{i}\leq\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n-2}a_{i}\leq n-1
, which is a contradiction.

(ii)

Let us denote, for simplicity,
A=\sum\limits_{i=0}^{N-1}a_{i}
. For all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=0}^{\theta^{*}(n)}a_{i+N}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{i=N}^{\theta^{*}(n)+N}a_{i}

\displaystyle\geq\sum\limits_{i=N}^{\theta(n+\left\lceil A\right\rceil)}a_{i}\quad\text{as }\theta^{*}(n)+N=(\theta(n+\left\lceil A\right\rceil)\mathop{-\!\!\!\!\!\hskip 2.84526pt\raisebox{1.42262pt}{$\cdot$}}\nolimits N)+N\geq\theta(n+\left\lceil A\right\rceil)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{i=0}^{\theta(n+\left\lceil A\right\rceil)}a_{i}-A\geq n+\left\lceil A\right\rceil-\left\lceil A\right\rceil\geq n.

If
(a_{n})\subseteq[0,1]
, then
\left\lceil A\right\rceil\leq N

\theta(n+\left\lceil A\right\rceil)\leq\theta^{+}(n+\left\lceil A\right\rceil)\leq\theta^{+}(n+N)
, as
\theta^{+}
is increasing. It follows that

\sum\limits_{i=0}^{\theta^{+}(n+N)\mathop{-\!\!\!\!\!\hskip 2.84526pt\raisebox{1.42262pt}{$\cdot$}}\nolimits N}a_{i+N}\geq\sum\limits_{i=0}^{\theta(n+\left\lceil A\right\rceil)\mathop{-\!\!\!\!\!\hskip 2.84526pt\raisebox{1.42262pt}{$\cdot$}}\nolimits N}a_{i+N}\geq n
.

(iii)

For all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}

\sum\limits_{i=0}^{\chi(n)}ca_{i}=c\sum\limits_{i=0}^{\chi(n)}a_{i}\geq c\left\lceil\frac{n}{c}\right\rceil\geq n
.

(iv)

For all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}

\sum\limits_{i=0}^{\gamma(n)}(a_{i}+b_{i})\geq\sum\limits_{i=0}^{\gamma(n)}a_{i}\geq n\quad\text{and}\quad\sum\limits_{i=0}^{\theta(n)}(a_{i}+b_{i})\geq\sum\limits_{i=0}^{\theta(n)}b_{i}\geq n,

2.2.1
Rates of metastability

Let
(X,d)
be a metric space and
(x_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
be a sequence in
X
. We say that
(x_{n})
is metastable
[
34
]
if

\displaystyle\forall k\in{\mathbb{N}}\,\forall g:{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{N}}\,\exists N\in{\mathbb{N}}\,\forall i,j\in[N;N+g(N)]\left(d(x_{i},x_{j})\leq\frac{1}{k+1}\right).

(6)

One can easily see that
(x_{n})
is Cauchy if and only if
(x_{n})
is metastable. As pointed out by Tao
[
34
]
,
metastability is a concept in “hard analysis” corresponding to Cauchyness, a concept in “soft analysis”.
In mathematical logic, metastability of
(x_{n})
is known as the Herbrand normal form or the no-counterexample
interpretation of Cauchyness. We refer to
[
16
]
for logical discussions on metastability.

Rates of metastability are used to express quantitative versions of metastability. A rate of metastability of
(x_{n})
is a
mapping
\Omega:{\mathbb{N}}\times{\mathbb{N}}^{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{N}}
satisfying the following:

\displaystyle\forall k\in{\mathbb{N}}\,\forall g:{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{N}}\,\exists N\leq\Omega(k,g)\,\forall i,j\in[N;N+g(N)]\left(d(x_{i},x_{j})\leq\frac{1}{k+1}\right).

(7)

Obviously, if
(x_{n})
is Cauchy with Cauchy modulus
\varphi
, then the mapping
\Omega:{\mathbb{N}}\times{\mathbb{N}}^{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{N}}
,
\Omega(k,g)=\varphi(k)

is a rate of metastability of
(x_{n})
. The converse does not hold, from a rate of metastability of
(x_{n})

one cannot, in general, compute a Cauchy modulus of
(x_{n})
.

Following
[
26
]
, for any
L\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}
, a rate of
L
-metastability of
(x_{n})

is a mapping
\Phi_{L}:{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{N}}
satisfying (
7
) with
g(n)=L\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Thus,
\Phi_{L}
satisfies

\displaystyle\forall k\in{\mathbb{N}}\,\,\exists N\leq\Phi_{L}(k)\,\,\forall i,j\in[N;N+L]\,\left(d(x_{i},x_{j})\leq\frac{1}{k+1}\right).

(8)

Remark 2.7
.

In this paper we use methods developed by Kohlenbach and Pinto
[
22
]
to
compute rates of metastability. In
[
22
]
, a rate of metastability of
(x_{n})
is a mapping

\Omega:{\mathbb{R}}^{+}\times{\mathbb{N}}^{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{N}}
satisfying

\displaystyle\forall\varepsilon>0,g:{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{N}}\,\exists N\leq\Omega(\varepsilon,g)\,\forall i,j\in[N;g(N)]\left(d(x_{i},x_{j})\leq\varepsilon\right).

(9)

If
\Omega
is a rate of metastability in our sense, then the mapping

\Omega^{*}:{\mathbb{R}}^{+}\times{\mathbb{N}}^{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{N}},\quad\Omega^{*}(\varepsilon,g)=\Omega\left(\left\lceil\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right\rceil-1,g\right)

satisfies (
9
).
Conversely, let
\Omega
satisfy (
9
) and define

\tilde{\Omega}:{\mathbb{N}}\times{\mathbb{N}}^{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{N}}
as follows:

\displaystyle\tilde{\Omega}(k,g)=\Omega\left(\frac{1}{k+1},\tilde{g}\right),\qquad\text{where }\tilde{g}(n)=n+g(n).

Then
\tilde{\Omega}
is a rate of metastability in our sense.

The following lemma adapts to our setting results from
[
22
]
.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
\Omega
be a rate of metastability of
(x_{n})
and define

\displaystyle\Omega^{+}:{\mathbb{N}}\times{\mathbb{N}}^{\mathbb{N}}\times{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{N}},

\displaystyle\quad\Omega^{+}(k,g,n)=n+\Omega(k,g_{n}),

\displaystyle\Omega^{\dagger}:{\mathbb{N}}\times{\mathbb{N}}^{\mathbb{N}}\times{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{N}},

\displaystyle\quad\Omega^{\dagger}(k,g,n)=\max\{\Omega^{+}(k,g,i)\mid i\in[0;n]\},

where
g_{n}:{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{N}},\,\,g_{n}(m)=n+g(n+m)
.

The following hold:

(i)

For all
k,n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
g:{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{N}}
,

\displaystyle\exists N\in[n;\Omega^{+}(k,g,n)]\,\forall i,j\in[N;N+g(N)]\left(d(x_{i},x_{j})\leq\frac{1}{k+1}\right).

(10)

(ii)

holds with
\Omega^{\dagger}
instead of
\Omega^{+}
and

\Omega^{\dagger}
is monotone in the third argument, that is: for all
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
g:{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{N}}
and all

n,m\in{\mathbb{N}}
with
n\leq m
,
\Omega^{\dagger}(k,g,n)\leq\Omega^{\dagger}(k,g,m)
.

Proof.

(i)

Let
k,n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
g:{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{N}}
be arbitrary. As
\Omega
is a rate of metastability
of

Asymptotic regularity is a well-known notion in optimization, nonlinear analysis and metric

[

]
for

[

Let

(x_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}

(T_{n}:C\to C)_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}

this paper the following asymptotic regularity-type notions, together with their quantitative versions:

(i)

\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}d(x_{n},x_{n+1})=0

is a rate
of convergence to
0
of
\big(d(x_{n},x_{n+1})\big)
.

(ii)

(x_{n})
is

-asymptotically regular if
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}d(x_{n},Tx_{n})=0
. A rate of
T
-asymptotic regularity of
(x_{n})
is a rate
of convergence to
0
of
\big(d(x_{n},Tx_{n})\big)
.

(iii)

(x_{n})
is
(T_{n})
-asymptotically regular if
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}d(x_{n},T_{n}x_{n})=0
. A rate of
(T_{n})
-asymptotic regularity of
(x_{n})
is a rate
of convergence to

of
\big(d(x_{n},T_{n}x_{n})\big)

.

Theorem 5.6

Proof.

,
(x_{n})
is Cauchy. Thus, as

is complete and

converges strongly to some

converges strongly, hence

Claim:
For all

and

,

.

Proof of Claim:
The proof is by induction on

. The case

is obvious, as

and
w_{0}(y)=y
. For the inductive step, we get that for all

\displaystyle d(w_{n+1}(x),w_{n+1}(y))

\displaystyle=d(\alpha_{n}x+(1-\alpha_{n})T_{n}w_{n}(x),\alpha_{n}y+(1-\alpha_{n})T_{n}w_{n}(y))

\displaystyle\leq\alpha_{n}d(x,y)+(1-\alpha_{n})d(w_{n}(x),w_{n}(y))

\displaystyle\quad\text{by (W4) and the nonexpansivity of }T_{n}

\displaystyle\leq d(x,y)\quad\text{ by the induction hypothesis}.

\blacksquare

As an immediate consequence of the claim, we get that
Q
is nonexpansive, hence

is
a contraction.
We prove in the sequel that
\overline{x}
is the unique fixed point of

.

Let

, we obtain, using

) and (

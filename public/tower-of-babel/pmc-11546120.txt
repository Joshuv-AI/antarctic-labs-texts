A new dawn for Diabetic kidney disease: interleukin receptor antagonists
Authors: LuYao Shi, Tao Wei, Zhang Liang, ChangYan Li, Tao Liu, WenXing Fan
Published: 2024

Dear editor,

Diabetic kidney disease (DKD) is the leading cause of chronic kidney disease (CKD) and end-stage kidney disease in many countries. Inflammatory targets can prevent the progression of DKD and CKD. Recent studies validate this by demonstrating that the inflammatory-associated transcription factor transforming growth factor beta (TGF-β) and the complement system act synergistically to activate the NLRP3 inflammasome, which influences the progression of CKD [1]. Interleukin receptor antagonists as a novel therapy, including Tozorakimab, providing opportunities for further drug development. Hofherr et al. [2] findings that interleukin (IL)-33 pathway may reduce inflammation in the glomerular endothelium of patients with DKD and highlight the potential clinical benefit of IL-33 blockade by tozorakimab in patients with DKD. These findings provide a solid foundation for the broader application of interleukin receptor antagonists.

To translate these promising preclinical results into clinical practice, it is crucial to evaluate the current status of clinical trials investigating the efficacy and safety of interleukin receptor antagonists. We conducted an analysis using the Trialtrove database (https://clinicalintelligence.citeline.com/). This database provides valuable data on the pharmacokinetics and preliminary safety of interleukin receptor antagonists in CKD patients. Keywords used included ‘therapeutic target/interleukin receptor antagonist’ and ‘disease: chronic kidney disease or diabetic nephropathy’. The search was conducted on July 31, 2024, including 10 CKD-related and 7 DKD-related trials in the analysis (Table S1), following the exclusion of trials with unknown phases and those involving incompatible diseases or therapeutic agents. For each eligible trial, relevant clinical information such as drug name, type, proportion, trial stage, and clinical indication was recorded. This study utilized publicly available data and did not necessitate ethical approval or informed consent.

Between 2014 and 2024, clinical trials involving interleukin receptor antagonists have been conducted in 45 countries. Regarding clinical stages, the majority of trials were Phase I-II studies (10, 58%), with the high number of Phase I trials underscoring the novelty and potential of this drug class. Only a limited number of trials have reached Phase III (1, 6%), with 2 (11.8%) trials still ongoing or in the pipeline. This indicates that research on interleukin receptor antagonists for CKD remains active, with 11 (64.7%) trials completed and 2 (11.8%) trials terminated (Fig. 1A). Clinical trials involving Ziltivelimab have been predominant in chronic kidney disease development (5, 29.4%). Etokimab, Clazakizumab, and Analanra (SC) are in Phase II-III trials, while Ziltivelimab is in Phase III trials (Fig. 1B).

Landscape of clinical trials on therapeutic agents for chronic kidney disease. A. Trial status distribution of clinical trials for Chronic Kidney Disease. B. Therapeutic agents distribution of clinical trials for Chronic Kidney Disease

Tozorakimab (MEDI-3506) is a human IgG1 monoclonal antibody (MAb) that inhibits IL-33 signaling. IL-33, a cytokine functioning as an alarmin, initiates inflammatory responses following tissue damage or infection and represents a potential target for inflammatory diseases. Elizabeth England et al. demonstrated that an anti-IL-33 antibody, inhibiting IL-33 signaling via ST2 and RAGE/EGFR pathways, reduced inflammation and epithelial dysfunction while slowing overall renal disease progression [3]. However, its clinical use is still in the early stages, and further investigations are needed to assess its potential application.

This study, which identifies trials registered with ClinicalTrials.gov, has limitations that may include missing early trials conducted in countries with less stringent registration requirements and assessment constraints. Additionally, other trials may have been registered after our data collection, and some ongoing trials may have been terminated, suspended, or completed. Due to the limitations of the search method, it may not be possible to include all interleukin receptor antagonist clinical trials conducted worldwide.

Derivations of two one-dimensional models for transversely curved shallow shells: one leads to relaxation
Roberto Paroni, Marco Picchi Scardaoni

Abstract
. We study the Γ \Gamma -limit of sequences of variational problems for straight, transversely curved shallow shells, as the width of the planform ε \varepsilon goes to zero. The energy is of von Kármán type for shallow shells under suitable boundary conditions. What distinguishes the various regimes is the scaling of the stretching energy ∼ ε 2 ​ β \sim\varepsilon^{2\beta} , with β \beta a positive number. We derive two one-dimensional models as β \beta ranges in ( 0 , 2 ] (0,2] . Remarkably, boundary conditions are essential to get compactness. We show that for β ∈ ( 0 , 2 ) \beta\in(0,2) the Γ \Gamma -limit leads to relaxation: the limit membrane energy vanishes on compression. For β = 2 \beta=2 there is no relaxation, and the limit model is a nonlinear energy coupling four kinematical descriptors in a nontrivial way. As special cases of the latter limit model, a nonlinear Vlasov torsion theory and a nonlinear Euler-Bernoulli beam theory can be deduced. Key words: Shallow shells, Γ \Gamma -convergence, ribbons, von Kármán, dimension reduction, Vlasov, relaxation. Mathematics Subject Classification: 35B40, 35J35, 49J45, 74B10, 74G65, 74K20, 74K25



1.
Introduction

The rigorous derivation of one-dimensional mechanical theories from higher-dimensional frameworks has seen significant development in recent years.

In this work, we focus on straight, transversely curved ribbons in their natural configuration. That is, we consider transversely curved shallow shells whose planform projection is a rectangle. In this setting, the rigorous derivation of limiting one-dimensional models, as the width of the ribbon planform tends to zero, was first addressed in
[
31
]
(see also
[
11
]
), inspired by the bending behavior of a carpenter’s tape measure, where deformation can localize. The resulting limit model captures curvature concentration (i.e., the formation of elastic hinges). However, the starting point in that work was a relatively strong energy, whereas more realistic models, such as those based on von Kármán energies, have weaker compactness properties, posing additional challenges.

For completeness, we note that several one-dimensional models have been heuristically proposed in the literature for transversely curved shallow shells; see, e.g.,
[
8
,
21
,
32
,
24
,
27
]
.
Rigorous derivations of one-dimensional models for ribbons with a curved cross-section starting from three-dimensional nonlinear elasticity can be found in
[
13
,
28
]
, while the case of flat cross-sections has been addressed in
[
17
,
18
]
.
Derivations starting instead from two-dimensional theories are presented in
[
15
,
16
]
.

Here, we consider families of von Kármán-type energies, as commonly used for modeling shallow shells and prestrained plates
[
7
,
9
,
14
,
23
,
22
,
26
,
35
,
5
,
4
,
36
,
33
]
. In particular, we impose a specific scaling for the stretching energy: as the ribbon width
\varepsilon
tends to zero, we assume the natural curvature scales like
1/\varepsilon
, and the stretching energy is of order
\varepsilon^{2\beta}
, with
\beta
a non-negative real number.

Additionally, we assume that one short end of the shell is clamped, while the other undergoes a rigid roto-translation at a suitable scale. As it will become evident, the order of the longitudinal displacement plays a crucial role in determining the limit behavior. Specifically, we obtain compactness for appropriately scaled displacement sequences, of order
\varepsilon^{\beta}
for in-plane displacements and
\varepsilon^{\beta/2}
for out-of-plane ones. Furthermore, for
\beta\in(0,2)
, the problem exhibits relaxation. In contrast, when
\beta=2
, the energy functional is sufficiently rigid to prevent relaxation. In all cases, a shared feature is compactness in relatively weak topologies, alongside surprisingly regular limit displacements.

It is well known that boundary conditions can significantly influence the resulting limit models. In the context of three-dimensional nonlinear elasticity, for instance, clamping a plate along its boundary can (under suitable scalings) lead to a membrane theory whose energy vanishes on contractions
[
10
]
. A broader overview of how various scaling regimes interact with boundary conditions is presented in
[
20
]
.

In our setting, a similar phenomenon occurs, arguably in an even more evident way. When natural boundary conditions are imposed, a wide class of displacements yields zero membrane energy, resulting in a soft response dominated by out-of-plane bending. However, for
\beta\in(0,2)
, introducing the additional boundary condition stiffens the shell considerably: the kernel of the membrane energy is reduced or even eliminated. The practical consequence is that the out-of-plane bending term becomes negligible in the limit model (i.e., the second derivative of the transversal displacement disappears).

The case
\beta=2
is critical: both the membrane and bending contributions are retained, and a “complete” limit theory is obtained. In this regime, the four kinematic descriptors of the limit displacement are nontrivially coupled. Interestingly, it also includes, as a particular case, a nonlinear version of Vlasov’s torsion theory.

To the best of our knowledge, the two limit models obtained in the present work are new.

5.1.
The Euler-Lagrange equations and some examples

The presence of nontrivial transverse curvature (
\mathring{w}^{\prime\prime}\not\equiv 0
) forces the Euler-Lagrange equations to be of the fourth-order in

and of the fourth-order in
\vartheta

Supposing

(and
\Lambda_{1}=\Lambda_{2}=\Lambda_{3}=\Phi_{2}=\Phi_{3}=0

\displaystyle\vartheta\mapsto\int_{I}|\vartheta^{\prime}|^{2}dx_{1}+\frac{1}{640}\int_{I}|\vartheta^{\prime}|^{4}\,dx_{1}+\frac{c_{1}^{2}+12J_{4}}{24}\int_{I}|\vartheta^{\prime\prime}|^{2}\,dx_{1}

Appendix A
Technical results

We provide here some technical results. For some of them, we claim no originality. Nevertheless, we provide a proof for the sake of completeness.
If not specified,
I_{1},I_{2}\subset\mathbb{R}
are generic bounded intervals.

The following lemma is a well-known version for distributions of the Du Bois-Reymond lemma.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
\Omega=I_{1}\times I_{2}
,
I_{2}=(a,b)
, and
u\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\Omega)
with
\partial_{2}u=0
in the sense of distributions. Then, there exists
g\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(I_{1})
such that
u=g\otimes 1
.

Proof.

Let
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}(\Omega)
and
\psi\in\mathcal{D}(I_{2})
with
\int_{I_{2}}\psi\,dx_{2}=1
.
Define

\eta(x_{1},x_{2})\coloneqq\int_{a}^{x_{2}}\varphi(x_{1},s)-(\int_{I_{2}}\varphi(x_{1},t)\,dt)\psi(s)\,ds

which clearly belongs to
\mathcal{D}(\Omega)
.
Hence, denoting with
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{X}
the pairing
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(X)\times\mathcal{D}(X)}

\displaystyle\langle u,\varphi\rangle_{I_{1}\times I_{2}}

\displaystyle=\langle u,\partial_{2}\eta+(\int_{I_{2}}\varphi(x_{1},t)\,dt)\psi(x_{2})\rangle_{I_{1}\times I_{2}}

\displaystyle=\langle u,(\int_{I_{2}}\varphi(x_{1},t)\,dt)\psi(x_{2})\rangle_{I_{1}\times I_{2}}

since
\langle u,\partial_{2}\eta\rangle=0
.
For fixed
\psi
, the map
\zeta(x_{1})\mapsto\langle u,\zeta(x_{1})\psi(x_{2})\rangle
defines a distribution
g\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(I_{1})
, i.e.

\langle g,\zeta\rangle_{I_{1}}\coloneqq\langle u,\zeta\psi\rangle_{I_{1}\times I_{2}}\qquad\forall\zeta\in\mathcal{D}(I_{1}).

Hence (see also
[
29
, Ch. 2.7]
)

\displaystyle\langle u,\varphi\rangle_{I_{1}\times I_{2}}=\langle g,\int_{I_{2}}\varphi(x_{1},t)\,dt\rangle_{I_{1}}=\langle g,\langle 1,\varphi\rangle_{I_{2}}\rangle_{I_{1}}=\langle g\otimes 1,\varphi\rangle_{I_{1}\times I_{2}}.

Next we specialize Lemma
A.1
for
L^{p}
functions and finite Radon measures.

Corollary A.2
.

(i)

If
u\in\mathcal{M}(\Omega)
with
\partial_{2}u=0
, there exists
g\in\mathcal{M}(I_{1})
such that
u=g\otimes\mathcal{L}
on
\Omega
.

(ii)

If
u\in L^{p}(\Omega)
(
1<p<\infty
) with
\partial_{2}u=0
, there exists
g\in L^{p}(I_{1})
such that
u=g
a.e on
\Omega
.

Proof.

Claim
(i)
. By Lemma
A.1
exists
g\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(I_{1})
such that
and for any
\varphi_{i}\in\mathcal{D}(I_{i})
with
\int_{I_{2}}\varphi_{2}=1
it holds that

\int_{\Omega}\varphi_{1}\varphi_{2}\,du=\langle g,\varphi_{1}\rangle_{I_{1}}.

We have

|\langle g,\varphi_{1}\rangle|_{I_{1}}=|\langle u,\varphi_{1}\varphi_{2}\rangle|\leq C|u|(\Omega)\left\|\varphi_{1}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}<\infty.

By density,
g
is a continuous linear functional on
C_{0}(I_{1})
and by Riesz’s representation theorem there exists a unique element of
\mathcal{M}(I_{1})
, still denoted by
g
, such that

\langle g,\varphi_{1}\rangle=\int_{I_{1}}\varphi_{1}\,dg\qquad\forall\varphi_{1}\in C_{0}(I_{1}).

Moreover, for every
\varphi\in C_{0}(\Omega)
,

\int_{\Omega}\varphi\,du=\int_{I_{1}}\int_{I_{2}}\varphi\,dx_{2}dg=\int_{\Omega}\varphi\,d(g\otimes\mathcal{L})

and we conclude
u=g\otimes\mathcal{L}
.

Claim
(ii)
. It follows from claim (i) when considering measures of the form
u\mathcal{L}^{2}
with
u\in L^{p}(\Omega)
and the
L^{p}
version of Riesz’s representation theorem.

Lemma A.3
.

Let
\Omega=I_{1}\times I_{2}
and
u\in BD(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{2})
with
\partial_{2}u_{2}=0
and
\partial_{1}u_{2}+\partial_{2}u_{1}=0
. Then, there exist
(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\in BV(I_{1})\times BH(I_{1})
, such that

u_{1}=\xi_{1}-x_{2}\xi_{2}^{\prime},\qquad u_{2}=\xi_{2}.

Proof.

By Corollary
A.2
and by the embedding
BD(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{2})\hookrightarrow L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{2})
, there exists
\xi_{2}\in L^{2}(I_{1})
such that

u_{2}=\xi_{2}
.
Take now
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
.

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\varphi\,d(\partial_{1}u_{2}+\partial_{2}u_{1})=-\int_{\Omega}\partial_{1}\varphi u_{2}+\partial_{2}\varphi u_{1}\,dx

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega}\partial_{1}\varphi\xi_{2}+\partial_{2}\varphi u_{1}\,dx=-\int_{\Omega}\partial_{1}\varphi\partial_{2}(x_{2}\xi_{2})+\partial_{2}\varphi u_{1}\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\partial_{12}\varphi(x_{2}\xi_{2})-\partial_{2}\varphi u_{1}\,dx=-\langle\partial_{1}(\xi_{2}\otimes x_{2})+u_{1},\partial_{2}\varphi\rangle.

By Lemma
A.1
, there exists
\xi_{1}\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(I_{1})
such that

\xi_{1}\otimes 1=\xi_{2}^{\prime}\otimes x_{2}+u_{1}

from which

u_{1}=\xi_{1}\otimes 1-\xi_{2}^{\prime}\otimes x_{2}.

We now show that
(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\in BV(I_{1})\times BH(I_{1})
. For every

\varphi_{\alpha}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(I_{\alpha})
with
\varphi_{2}
even and such that
\int_{I_{2}}\varphi_{2}\,dx_{2}=1
it holds

\int_{\Omega}u_{1}\varphi_{1}\varphi_{2}\,dx=\langle\xi_{1},\varphi_{1}\rangle,\qquad\int_{\Omega}\varphi_{1}\varphi_{2}\,d(\partial_{1}u_{1})=\langle\xi_{1}^{\prime},\varphi_{1}\rangle.

By the continuous embedding
BD(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{2})\hookrightarrow L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{2})
we obtain

|\langle\xi_{1},\varphi_{1}\rangle|\leq C\left\|u_{1}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\left\|\varphi_{1}\right\|_{L^{2}(I_{1})},\qquad|\langle\xi_{1}^{\prime},\varphi_{1}\rangle|\leq C|\partial_{1}u_{1}|(\Omega)\left\|\varphi_{1}\right\|_{C^{0}(I_{1})},

and by the Riesz’s representation theorem we conclude
\xi_{1}\in BV(I_{1})
.
With similar arguments, but working with
\varphi_{2}
odd, we deduce
\xi_{2}\in BH(I_{1})
.

Lemma A.4
.

Let
\Omega=I_{1}\times I_{2}
and
f\in H^{2}(\Omega)
. There exist a function
\hat{f}\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
such that
\hat{f}|_{\Omega}=f
and a positive constant
C
, independent of
f
, such that
\|\partial_{12}\hat{f}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq C(\|\partial_{12}f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\left\|f\right\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)})
.

Proof.

Let
f\in H^{2}(\Omega)
.
Up to a change of reference frame, we may suppose
I_{1}=(0,a)
,
I_{2}=(0,b)
for some
a,b>0
.
We start by extending
f
on the domain obtained by the union of
\Omega
and its reflection along
x_{2}=0
. Let us call this domain
\Omega_{1}
.
Define (see
[
30
, Ch. 2, Theorem 3.9]
)

f_{1}(x_{1},x_{2})\coloneqq\begin{cases}f(x_{1},x_{2})&x\in\Omega\\
-3f(x_{1},-x_{2})+4f(x_{1},-\frac{1}{2}x_{2})&x\in\Omega_{1}\setminus\Omega.\end{cases}

It is clear that
f_{1}\in H^{2}(\Omega_{1})
. Moreover

\left\|f_{1}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{1})}\leq C\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},\quad\left\|\nabla f_{1}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{1})}\leq C\left\|\nabla f\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

\left\|\partial_{12}f_{1}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{1})}\leq C\left\|\partial_{12}f\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Iterating properly three times more, mirroring along axes parallel to the coordinates axes, we end up with a function
f_{4}
defined on a rectangle
\Omega_{4}
that compactly contains
\Omega
(see Fig.
2
).

A series of domain reflections.
\Omega
in blue.

Moreover,

\left\|f_{4}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{4})}\leq C\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},\quad\left\|\nabla f_{4}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{4})}\leq C\left\|\nabla f\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

\left\|\partial_{12}f_{4}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{4})}\leq C\left\|\partial_{12}f\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

We now take a cutoff function
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega_{4},[0,1])
such that
\varphi=1
on
\Omega
and pose
\hat{f}\coloneqq\varphi f_{4}
extended by zero to the whole
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
Thus

\displaystyle\left\|\partial_{12}\hat{f}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}

\displaystyle=\left\|\partial_{12}\hat{f}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{4})}

\displaystyle\leq C(\left\|\partial_{12}f_{4}\varphi\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{4})}+\left\|f_{4}\partial_{12}\varphi\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{4})}+\left\|\partial_{2}f_{4}\partial_{1}\varphi\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{4})}

\displaystyle+\left\|\partial_{1}f_{4}\partial_{2}\varphi\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{4})})

\displaystyle\leq C(\left\|\partial_{12}f_{4}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{4})}+\left\|f_{4}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{4})}+\left\|\partial_{2}f_{4}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{4})}+\left\|\partial_{1}f_{4}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{4})})

\displaystyle\leq C(\left\|\partial_{12}f\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\left\|\partial_{2}f\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\left\|\partial_{1}f\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}).

Lemma A.5
.

Let
f\in H^{1}(I)
. There exists a sequence
(f_{n})\subset C^{\infty}(\overline{I})
such that
f_{n}\to f
in
H^{1}(I)
as
n\uparrow\infty
. Moreover, if
(\varepsilon_{n})\subset\mathbb{R}
is such that
\varepsilon_{n}\downarrow 0
as
n\uparrow\infty
,
(f_{n})
can be chosen such that

\varepsilon_{n}f_{n}^{\prime\prime}\to 0
in
L^{2}(I)
.

Proof.

The proof is a refinement of
[
31
, Lemma 11]
.
Set
\widetilde{I}\coloneqq(-\frac{3}{2}\ell,\frac{3}{2}\ell)
and let
\widetilde{f}\in H^{1}(\widetilde{I})
be an extension of
f
.
Take
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\widetilde{I},[0,1])
,
\varphi=1
on
I
, and consider the function
(\widetilde{f}\varphi)(x)
, extended by zero to the whole real line.
Let
\eta:\mathbb{R}\to[0,\infty)
be a smooth function with compact support in
(-1,1)
such that
\int_{\mathbb{R}}\eta(t)\,dt=1
. Let
(\rho_{n})\subset\mathbb{R}
a sequence, to be chosen later on, such that
\rho_{n}\downarrow 0
as
n\uparrow\infty
. Let
\widetilde{f}_{n}\coloneqq\eta_{\rho_{n}}\ast(\widetilde{f}\varphi)
where
\eta_{\rho_{n}}(x)\coloneqq\frac{1}{\rho_{n}}\eta(\frac{x}{\rho_{n}})
.
Let us put also
\eta^{\prime}_{\rho_{n}}(t)\coloneqq\frac{1}{\rho_{n}}\eta^{\prime}(\frac{t}{\rho_{n}})
, and notice that it belongs to
L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
:

\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\eta^{\prime}_{\rho_{n}}(t)|\,dt=\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\eta^{\prime}(t)|\,dt<\infty.

It is well-known by standard properties of mollification that
\widetilde{f}_{n}\to\widetilde{f}\varphi
in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
, which implies that

f_{n}\coloneqq\widetilde{f}_{n}|_{I}\to f
in
H^{1}(I)
.
Observe now that

\displaystyle|\widetilde{f}_{n}^{\prime\prime}(x)|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\rho_{n}^{2}}|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{1}{\rho_{n}}\eta^{\prime}(\frac{x-z}{\rho_{n}})(\widetilde{f}\varphi)^{\prime}(z)\,dz|^{2}=\frac{1}{\rho_{n}^{2}}|(\eta^{\prime}_{\rho_{n}}\ast(\widetilde{f}\varphi)^{\prime})(x)|^{2},

from which, by Young’s theorem for convolutions
[
2
, Corollary 2.25]
, we get

\displaystyle\|f_{n}^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{2}(I)}

\displaystyle\leq\|\widetilde{f}_{n}^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\rho_{n}}\left\|\eta^{\prime}_{\rho_{n}}\ast(\widetilde{f}\varphi)^{\prime}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\rho_{n}}\left\|\eta^{\prime}_{\rho_{n}}\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\left\|(\widetilde{f}\varphi)^{\prime}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\leq\frac{C}{\rho_{n}}.

The proof is concluded by choosing
\rho_{n}
such that
\varepsilon_{n}/\rho_{n}\to 0
as
n\uparrow\infty
.

Lemma A.6
.

Let
I\subset\mathbb{R}
be an interval and
m\in L^{\infty}(I,[0,\infty))
be a function that is constant on finitely many segments covering
I
. Let
\gamma
be a positive number. There exists a sequence
(\zeta_{\varepsilon})\subset H^{2}_{0}(I)
such that
\zeta_{\varepsilon}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\ast}}{{\rightharpoonup}}0
in
W^{1,\infty}(I)
,
\frac{1}{2}(\zeta^{\prime}_{\varepsilon})^{2}\to m
in
L^{2}(I)
, and
\varepsilon^{\gamma}\zeta_{\varepsilon}^{\prime\prime}\to 0
in
L^{2}(I)
.

Proof.

We can assume
I=(a,b)
and
m
to be a positive constant on
I
, otherwise we can perform the construction on each set on which
m
is constant.

Let
g:\mathbb{R}\to[0,\frac{1}{2}]
be a 1-periodic function defined, on one period, by

g(t)\coloneqq\begin{cases}t&0\leq t<\frac{1}{2},\\
1-t&\frac{1}{2}<t<1.\end{cases}

For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
let
\delta_{n}=\sqrt{2m}(b-a)\frac{1}{n}
and
g_{n}(t)\coloneqq\delta_{n}g(\frac{\sqrt{2m}(t-a)}{\delta_{n}})
.
Note that
\|g_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}=\delta_{n}/2
and
g_{n}^{\prime}\in\{-\sqrt{2m},+\sqrt{2m}\}
almost everywhere in
\mathbb{R}
.
Let
(\rho_{\varepsilon})
be a sequence of positive numbers that converges to zero as
\varepsilon
goes to zero, and let
\eta_{\rho_{\varepsilon}}
be the standard mollifier whose support is contained in
(-\rho_{\varepsilon},\rho_{\varepsilon})
.
Define
\zeta_{\rho_{\varepsilon},n}\coloneqq\eta_{\rho_{\varepsilon}}\ast g_{n}
. It is clear that
\zeta_{\rho_{\varepsilon},n}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
, also
\|\zeta_{\rho_{\varepsilon},n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\leq\delta_{n}/2
and
\|\zeta_{\rho_{\varepsilon},n}^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\leq\sqrt{2m}
for every
\rho_{\varepsilon}
and
\delta_{n}
. Moreover, by Young’s inequality for convolutions,

\|\varepsilon^{\gamma}\zeta_{\rho_{\varepsilon},n}^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{2}(I)}=\varepsilon^{\gamma}\|\eta_{\rho_{\varepsilon}}^{\prime}\ast g_{n}^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(I)}\leq\varepsilon^{\gamma}\|\eta_{\rho_{\varepsilon}}^{\prime}\|_{L^{1}(I)}\|g_{n}^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(I)}\leq\frac{C\varepsilon^{\gamma}}{\rho_{\varepsilon}}

and

\displaystyle\int_{I}|\frac{1}{2}(\zeta_{\rho_{\varepsilon},n}^{\prime})^{2}-m|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{I}|\frac{1}{2}(\zeta_{\rho_{\varepsilon},n}^{\prime})^{2}-\frac{1}{2}(g_{n}^{\prime})^{2}|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\int_{I}|\zeta_{\rho_{\varepsilon},n}^{\prime}-g_{n}^{\prime}|^{2}|\zeta_{\rho_{\varepsilon},n}^{\prime}+g_{n}^{\prime}|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{I}|\zeta_{\rho_{\varepsilon},n}^{\prime}-g_{n}^{\prime}|^{2}\,dx.

Hence, setting
\rho_{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon^{\gamma/2}
we deduce that

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\|\varepsilon^{\gamma}\zeta_{\rho_{\varepsilon},n}^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{2}(I)}=0\quad\mbox{and}\quad\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\|\frac{1}{2}(\zeta_{\rho_{\varepsilon},n}^{\prime})^{2}-m\|_{L^{2}(I)}=0,

for every
n
. Given that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\|\zeta_{\rho_{\varepsilon},n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}=0
, by a
standard diagonalization process, we can find a map
\varepsilon\mapsto n(\varepsilon)
such that
n(\varepsilon)\uparrow\infty
and

\zeta_{\rho_{\varepsilon},n(\varepsilon)}\to 0
in
L^{\infty}(I)
. The proof is concluded by posing
\zeta_{\varepsilon}\coloneqq\zeta_{\rho_{\varepsilon},n(\varepsilon)}-\zeta_{\rho_{\varepsilon},n(\varepsilon)}(a)
and by recalling the bound
\|\zeta_{\rho_{\varepsilon},n}^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\leq\sqrt{2m}
.

Lemma A.7
.

Let
I=(a,b)
be a bounded interval. Let
\gamma>0
and
\alpha_{a},\alpha_{b}\in\mathbb{R}
. Then, there exists a sequence
(g_{\varepsilon})\subset H^{2}(I)
such that

(73)

g_{\varepsilon}(a)=g_{\varepsilon}(b)=0,\quad g_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(a)=\alpha_{a},\quad g_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(b)=\alpha_{b}\qquad\forall\varepsilon>0

and

g_{\varepsilon}\to 0\quad in\ W^{1,4}(I),\qquad\varepsilon^{\gamma}g_{\varepsilon}^{\prime\prime}\to 0\ in\ \L^{2}(I).

Proof.

For simplicity, we prove the lemma for the case
I=(0,1)
and
\alpha_{b}=0
. Let
\eta_{\varepsilon}:=\varepsilon^{\gamma}
.
Consider the sequence

g_{\varepsilon}(x)=\begin{cases}\frac{x^{3}}{\eta_{\varepsilon}^{2}}(\alpha_{a}-2)-\frac{x^{2}}{\eta_{\varepsilon}}(2\alpha_{a}-3)+\alpha_{a}x&0<x\leq\eta_{\varepsilon}\\
\frac{\eta_{\varepsilon}}{(1-\eta_{\varepsilon})^{3}}(x-1)^{2}(2x-3\eta+1)&\eta_{\varepsilon}<x<1.\end{cases}

The sequence satisfies (
73
) and

|g_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(x)|\leq\begin{cases}C_{1}&0<x\leq\eta_{\varepsilon}\\
C_{2}\eta_{\varepsilon}&\eta_{\varepsilon}<x<1\end{cases}\qquad|g_{\varepsilon}^{\prime\prime}(x)|\leq\begin{cases}\frac{C_{3}}{\eta_{\varepsilon}}&0<x\leq\eta_{\varepsilon}\\
C_{4}\eta_{\varepsilon}&\eta_{\varepsilon}<x<1\end{cases}

where
C_{1},C_{2},C_{3},C_{4}
are positive constants that may depend on

.
From these two inequalities, the lemma follows.

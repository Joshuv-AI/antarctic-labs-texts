QMUL-PH-25-26 Tension between string amplitude prescriptions with presumed spacetime supersymmetry
Thales Azevedo, Alessandro Georgoudis, Renann Lipinski Jusinskas, Sitender Pratap Kashyap

Abstract
We analyze an alternative prescription for computing tree level scattering amplitudes in the pure spinor superstring. Proposed by Berkovits, and inspired by an earlier work by Lee and Siegel, it bypasses the traditional ghost number three measure of the pure spinor ghosts, ⟨ λ 3 ​ θ 5 ⟩ \langle\lambda^{3}\theta^{5}\rangle =1, arguably simplifying the calculation. However, direct computations using the free field OPEs of the target superspace CFT fail to reproduce the expected SL ​ ( 2 , ℂ ) \textrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C}) invariance of tree level correlators. This persistent mismatch, confirmed through independent methods, is not compatible with the gauge invariance of the amplitude. We also discuss possible implications for the B-RNS-GSS formalism of the superstring.

Introduction

Early on in the development of string theory, it was clear that a minimally realistic connection to particle physics would involve supersymmetry. In the spinning string, i.e. the Ramond–Neveu–Schwarz (RNS) formalism
[
1
,
2
]
, spacetime supersymmetry is not manifest, with a description of bosons and fermions that is inherently asymmetric. This limitation motivated the Green–Schwarz (GS) formalism
[
3
,
4
]
, in which spacetime supersymmetry was manifest at the level of the world-sheet action. However, the quantization of the GS superstring is only possible in the light-cone gauge, i.e. the Lorentz symmetry is not manifest, and the formalism does not provide an efficient framework to handle string scattering. These obstacles were finally overcome with Berkovits’ pure spinor (PS) superstring
[
5
]
. Despite lacking a formal derivation from first principles, the PS formalism has become an essential tool for state-of-the-art computations of string scattering amplitudes at tree and loop level (e.g.
[
6
,
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
]
).

Key features of the pure spinor superstring are a bosonic string-like simplicity for computing world-sheet correlators and the manifest character of the super Poincaré symmetry. The latter follows from the construction of vertex operators in terms of superfields, so that bosonic and fermionic degrees of freedom are described on equal footing with full covariance. Arguably, one of the drawbacks of the PS formalism is the fact that superfields describing massive higher spin multiplets, such as the ones populating the string spectrum, are much less known. Indeed, the explicit construction of related vertex operators has not gone beyond the first massive level in the open string
[
11
,
12
,
13
]
.

String amplitudes with massive, higher-spin external states have attracted renewed interest, mainly due to their relevance for modeling gravitational-wave scattering by black holes (see e.g.
[
14
,
15
,
16
,
17
]
, also
[
18
]
for earlier results).
Within the PS formalism, however, existing studies of scattering amplitudes typically include at most one massive vertex operator
[
19
,
20
,
21
,
22
]
.

Tree-level computations in the PS superstring are by now well established (see
[
23
]
for an excellent review and literature survey). These computations rely on a characteristic measure factor that mixes pure spinor ghosts with superspace variables. An alternative prescription was proposed in
[
25
]
, supported by earlier work
[
26
]
, to compute manifestly super-Poincaré-invariant amplitudes while avoiding the pure spinor measure altogether. The key idea is to work directly with the integrands of the integrated vertex operators, which are ghost number zero objects and resemble bosonic string correlators. In principle, this prescription could offer a more systematic computation of the scattering of massive string states, such as the Compton scattering.

We have recently learned that C. Huang, C. R. Mafra, and Y. X. Tao are pursuing similar results using the standard PS prescription
[
24
]
.

In this work, we put to test this alternative prescription in the familiar case of the tree-level three-point correlator with massless external states. Unfortunately, we show that it fails to reproduce the tree-level amplitude involving one boson and two fermions. Since this outcome is somewhat unexpected, we provide extensive details of the computation. We conclude with comments on the results of
[
26
]
and implications for the B-RNS-GSS formalism
[
27
]
. For completeness, we present in the appendix an elementary check of the computation.

Review and conventions

In this section we will briefly review some basic features of the
pure spinor formalism of the superstring, focusing on its holomorphic sector.

The matter variables describe an
\mathcal{N}=1
superspace in
D=10
. We denote by
X^{m}
the target space coordinates of the string,
with
m=0,\ldots,9
. Their superpartners are denoted by

\theta^{\alpha}
, with
\alpha=1,\ldots,16
, and their conjugates are denoted by
p_{\alpha}
.
These satisfy the following OPEs,

\displaystyle\partial X^{m}(z)\,X^{n}(y,\bar{y})

\displaystyle\sim-\left(\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{2}\right)\frac{\eta^{mn}}{(z-y)},

(2.1a)

\displaystyle p_{\alpha}(z)\,\theta^{\beta}(y)

\displaystyle\sim\frac{\delta_{\alpha}^{\beta}}{(z-y)},

(2.1b)

where
\alpha^{\prime}
is the string length squared,
and
\eta^{mn}=\mathrm{diag}(-1,+1,\ldots,+1)
is the Minkowski metric.

The supersymmetry algebra,

\{q_{\alpha},q_{\beta}\}=\frac{4\zeta}{\alpha^{\prime}}\gamma_{\alpha\beta}^{m}\oint\partial X_{m},

(2.2)

is realized through the supercharge

q_{\alpha}=\oint\{p_{\alpha}+\frac{2\zeta}{\alpha^{\prime}}(\gamma_{m}\theta)_{\alpha}\partial X^{m}+\frac{\zeta^{2}}{3\alpha^{\prime}}(\gamma_{m}\theta)_{\alpha}(\theta\gamma^{m}\partial\theta)\},

(2.3)

where we keep an arbitrary constant parameter
\zeta
to make it easier to
map different conventions in the literature. In addition to the Clifford
algebra,
\{\gamma^{m},\gamma^{n}\}=2\eta^{mn}
, the (symmetric) gamma matrices

\gamma_{\alpha\beta}^{m}
,
\gamma^{m\alpha\beta}
satisfy the identity

\eta_{mn}(\gamma_{\alpha\beta}^{m}\gamma_{\gamma\lambda}^{n}+\gamma_{\beta\gamma}^{m}\gamma_{\alpha\lambda}^{n}+\gamma_{\alpha\gamma}^{m}\gamma_{\beta\lambda}^{n})=0.

(2.4)

Given that the theory is manifestly supersymmetric, it is useful to introduce the
invariant combinations

\displaystyle\Pi^{m}

\displaystyle=\partial X^{m}+\zeta(\theta\gamma^{m}\partial\theta),

(2.5)

\displaystyle d_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=p_{\alpha}-\frac{2\zeta}{\alpha^{\prime}}(\gamma_{m}\theta)_{\alpha}\partial X^{m}-\frac{\zeta^{2}}{\alpha^{\prime}}(\gamma_{m}\theta)_{\alpha}(\theta\gamma^{m}\partial\theta),

(2.6)

which yield the following OPEs,

\displaystyle\Pi^{m}(z)\,\Pi^{n}(y)

\displaystyle\sim-\left(\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{2}\right)\frac{1}{(z-y)^{2}}\eta^{mn},

(2.7)

\displaystyle d_{\alpha}(z)\,\Pi^{m}(y)

\displaystyle\sim+\frac{2\zeta}{(z-y)}(\gamma^{m}\partial\theta)_{\alpha},

(2.8)

\displaystyle d_{\alpha}(z)\,d_{\beta}(y)

\displaystyle\sim-\frac{2\zeta}{(z-y)}\left(\frac{2}{\alpha^{\prime}}\right)\gamma_{\alpha\beta}^{m}\Pi_{m}.

(2.9)

When acting on superfields
\Phi=\Phi(X,\theta)
, they lead to

\displaystyle\Pi^{m}(z)\,\Phi(y)

\displaystyle\sim-\left(\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{2}\right)\frac{1}{(z-y)}\partial_{m}\Phi,

(2.10)

\displaystyle d_{\alpha}(z)\,\Phi(y)

\displaystyle\sim+\frac{1}{(z-y)}D_{\alpha}\Phi,

(2.11)

with
D_{\alpha}=\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta^{\alpha}}+\zeta(\gamma^{m}\theta)_{\alpha}\frac{\partial}{\partial X^{m}}
.

The ghost variables are denoted by
\lambda^{\alpha}
, and satisfy
the pure spinor constraint
(\lambda\gamma^{m}\lambda)=0.
Their
conjugates,
w_{\alpha}
, usually appear through the currents

\displaystyle N^{mn}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}w_{\alpha}\lambda^{\beta}(\gamma^{mn})_{\phantom{\alpha}\beta}^{\alpha},

\displaystyle J

\displaystyle=-w_{\alpha}\lambda^{\alpha},

with
(\gamma^{mn})_{\phantom{\alpha}\beta}^{\alpha}=\frac{1}{2}(\gamma^{m\alpha\rho}\gamma_{\rho\beta}^{n}-\gamma^{n\alpha\rho}\gamma_{\rho\beta}^{m})
,
respectively the Lorentz current in the ghost sector, and the ghost
number current. The relevant OPEs are

\displaystyle N^{mn}(z)\,\lambda^{\alpha}(y)

\displaystyle\sim+\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{(z-y)}(\gamma^{mn}\lambda)^{\alpha},

(2.12)

\displaystyle J(z)\,\lambda^{\alpha}(y)

\displaystyle\sim+\frac{1}{(z-y)}\lambda^{\alpha},

(2.13)

\displaystyle N^{mn}(z)\,N^{pq}(y)

\displaystyle\sim-\frac{3}{(z-y)^{2}}(\eta^{mq}\eta^{np}-\eta^{mp}\eta^{nq})

\displaystyle\phantom{\sim}+\frac{1}{(z-y)}(\eta^{nq}N^{mp}+\eta^{mp}N^{nq}-\eta^{np}N^{mq}-\eta^{mq}N^{np})

(2.14)

\displaystyle J(z)\,J(y)

\displaystyle\sim-\frac{4}{(z-y)^{2}}.

(2.15)

Finally, we introduce the (holomorphic) pure spinor BRST charge,

Q=\oint\lambda^{\alpha}d_{\alpha},

(2.16)

which is nilpotent as a consequence of the pure spinor constraint. It is possible
to show that the total energy-momentum tensor,
T
, defined as

T=-\frac{1}{\alpha^{\prime}}\partial X^{m}\partial X_{m}-p_{\alpha}\partial\theta^{\alpha}-w_{\alpha}\partial\lambda^{\alpha},

(2.17)

is BRST-exact
[
28
,
29
]
, and has vanishing central charge.

2.1
Physical spectrum

The ghost number one cohomology of
Q
contains the physical states
of the formalism. At the massless level, the unintegrated vertex operator
is simply

U_{\textrm{PS}}=\lambda^{\alpha}A_{\alpha}.

(2.18)

BRST-closedness leads to

\lambda^{\alpha}\lambda^{\beta}D_{\alpha}A_{\beta}=0,

(2.19)

which is equivalent to the linearized equation of motion of super Yang-Mills in
ten dimensions, encoded in the superfield
A_{\alpha}=A_{\alpha}(X,\theta)
.
Gauge invariance follows the usual BRST-exact identification, which
here translates to
\delta A_{\alpha}=D_{\alpha}\Lambda
, with superfield
parameter
\Lambda
.

The cohomology also contains the integrated version of the vertex operator (
2.18
),
whose integrand
V_{\textrm{PS}}
satisfies
[Q,V_{\textrm{PS}}]=\partial U_{\textrm{PS}}
.
It can be cast as

V_{\textrm{PS}}=\partial\theta^{\alpha}A_{\alpha}+\Pi^{m}A_{m}+\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{4}d_{\alpha}W^{\alpha}+\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{4}N^{mn}F_{mn}.

(2.20)

The additional superfields in (
2.20
) are defined by

\displaystyle\gamma_{\alpha\beta}^{m}A_{m}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{1}{2\zeta}(D_{\alpha}A_{\beta}+D_{\beta}A_{\alpha}),

(2.21)

\displaystyle(\gamma_{m}W)_{\alpha}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{1}{\zeta}(D_{\alpha}A_{m}-\partial_{m}A_{\alpha}),

(2.22)

\displaystyle F_{mn}

\displaystyle\equiv\partial_{m}A_{n}-\partial_{n}A_{m},

(2.23)

and satisfy

\displaystyle D_{\alpha}W^{\beta}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}(\gamma^{mn})_{\phantom{\alpha}\alpha}^{\beta}F_{mn},

(2.24)

\displaystyle D_{\alpha}F_{mn}

\displaystyle=\partial_{m}(\gamma_{n}W)_{\alpha}-\partial_{n}(\gamma_{m}W)_{\alpha},

(2.25)

\displaystyle\gamma_{\alpha\beta}^{m}\partial_{m}W^{\beta}

\displaystyle=0,

(2.26)

\displaystyle\partial^{n}F_{mn}

\displaystyle=0.

(2.27)

Notice that
V_{\textrm{PS}}
is a conformal primary field as long as we are in
the transverse gauge
\partial^{m}A_{m}=0
. This is akin to the
massless vertex operator in bosonic string theory.

2.2
Comparison with the spinning string

The pure spinor superstring has manifest spacetime supersymmetry, unlike the
spinning string. RNS vertex operators of the so-called Ramond sector
involve half-integer pictures and spin fields, introducing technical
complications even when computing tree level correlators. On the other
hand, vertex operators from the NS sector take a simple form.
For example, the analogue of
V_{\textrm{PS}}
in (
2.20
)
for the massless bosons is given by

V_{\textrm{RNS}}=\partial X^{m}a_{m}+\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{4}L_{\textrm{RNS}}^{mn}f_{mn},

(2.28)

where
f_{mn}=\partial_{m}a_{n}-\partial_{n}a_{m}
, and
L_{\textrm{RNS}}^{mn}\equiv-\psi^{m}\psi^{n}
,
with
\psi^{m}
denoting the world-sheet superpartners of
X^{m}
.
Using the RNS BRST charge,

Q_{\textrm{RNS}}=\oint\left\{c\left(-\frac{1}{\alpha^{\prime}}\partial X^{m}\partial X_{m}-\frac{1}{2}\psi^{m}\partial\psi_{m}\right)+\gamma\psi_{m}\partial X^{m}+\textrm{ghosts}\right\},

(2.29)

it is straightforward to show that

\displaystyle[Q_{\textrm{RNS}},V_{\textrm{RNS}}]

\displaystyle=\partial U_{\textrm{RNS}},

(2.30)

\displaystyle U_{\textrm{RNS}}

\displaystyle=cV_{\textrm{RNS}}-\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{2}\gamma\psi^{m}a_{m}-\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{4}\partial c\partial^{m}a_{m}.

(2.31)

with
\partial^{m}f_{mn}=0
.
U_{\textrm{RNS}}
is simply
the unintegrated vertex operator of the gluon, with
c
and
\gamma
denoting the superconformal ghosts.

As it turns out, the superfield expansion of (
2.20
)
is similar to (
2.28
) when we focus on the bosonic
polarization. For a given gauge choice, the superfield equations of motion lead to

\displaystyle\left.A_{\alpha}\right|_{\mathrm{boson}}

\displaystyle=\zeta(\gamma^{m}\theta)_{\alpha}a_{m}-\frac{\zeta^{2}}{8}(\gamma_{p}\theta)_{\alpha}(\theta\gamma^{mnp}\theta)f_{mn}+\mathcal{O}(\theta^{5}),

(2.32)

\displaystyle\left.A_{m}\right|_{\mathrm{boson}}

\displaystyle=a^{m}-\frac{\zeta}{4}(\theta\gamma^{mnp}\theta)f_{np}+\mathcal{O}(\theta^{4}),

(2.33)

\displaystyle\left.W^{\alpha}\right|_{\mathrm{boson}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}(\gamma^{mn}\theta)^{\alpha}f_{mn}+\mathcal{O}(\theta^{3}),

(2.34)

\displaystyle\left.F_{mn}\right|_{\mathrm{boson}}

\displaystyle=f_{mn}+\mathcal{O}(\theta^{2}),

(2.35)

which imply

\displaystyle\left.V_{\textrm{PS}}\right|_{\mathrm{boson}}

\displaystyle=\partial X^{m}a_{m}+\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{4}L_{\textrm{PS}}^{mn}f_{mn}+\mathcal{O}(\theta^{2}),

(2.36)

\displaystyle L_{\textrm{PS}}^{mn}

\displaystyle\equiv N^{mn}-\frac{1}{2}(p\gamma^{mn}\theta).

(2.37)

The similarity between (
2.28
) and (
2.36
)
is undeniable. Furthermore, note that
L_{\textrm{RNS}}^{mn}
and

L_{\textrm{PS}}^{mn}
satisfy the same OPE,

L^{mn}(z)\,L^{pq}(y)\sim\frac{1}{(z-y)^{2}}(\eta^{mq}\eta^{np}-\eta^{mp}\eta^{nq})\\
+\frac{1}{(z-y)}(\eta^{nq}L^{mp}+\eta^{mp}L^{nq}-\eta^{np}L^{mq}-\eta^{mq}L^{np}).

(2.38)

The double pole contribution, for instance, has been a hallmark hint
of the equivalence between the two formalisms. The reason is that
the anomaly of the ghost Lorentz algebra in (
2.14
)
supports the naive identification
L_{\textrm{RNS}}^{mn}\approx L_{\textrm{PS}}^{mn}
.

3
Tree level correlators

In this section we review three-point amplitudes in both formalisms,
RNS and pure-spinor. In RNS, we compute an amplitude with bosons only, while in the pure-spinor case we compute the full SYM amplitude, containing bosons and fermions.

3.1
Spinning string

In the RNS formalism, tree-level correlators can be non-zero only
when they saturate the background charges associated to the ghost
variables. From the path integral point-of-view, they are essentially
mapped to the three zero modes of the (fermionic) reparametrization
ghost
c
and to the two zero modes of the (bosonic) superghost
\gamma

[
30
,
31
]
.
We will simply take

\left\langle c(z_{1})c(z_{2})c(z_{3})\delta(\gamma)\delta(\gamma)\right\rangle=z_{12}z_{23}z_{31}.

(3.1)

Therefore, if we are interested in computing the three-gluon amplitude,
we have to evaluate

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{3}\left(a^{(1)},a^{(2)},a^{(3)}\right)

\displaystyle=\left\langle\delta(\gamma)\delta(\gamma)U_{\textrm{RNS}}^{(1)}U_{\textrm{RNS}}^{(2)}U_{\textrm{RNS}}^{(3)}\right\rangle,

\displaystyle=z_{12}z_{23}z_{31}\left\langle V_{\textrm{RNS}}^{(1)}V_{\textrm{RNS}}^{(2)}V_{\textrm{RNS}}^{(3)}\right\rangle_{\textrm{matter}}.

(3.2)

From the first to the second line we have just used the ghost measure
in (
3.1
). The
\textrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})

invariance of the amplitude requires that

\left\langle V_{\textrm{RNS}}^{(1)}V_{\textrm{RNS}}^{(2)}V_{\textrm{RNS}}^{(3)}\right\rangle_{\textrm{matter}}\propto\frac{1}{z_{12}z_{23}z_{31}}.

(3.3)

The explicit computation of the correlator is straightforward. We
start with

\left\langle V_{\textrm{RNS}}^{(1)}V_{\textrm{RNS}}^{(2)}V_{\textrm{RNS}}^{(3)}\right\rangle_{\textrm{matter}}=\left\langle\prod_{i=1}^{3}\left(\partial X^{m_{i}}a_{m_{i}}^{(i)}+\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{4}L_{\textrm{RNS}}^{m_{i}n_{i}}f_{m_{i}n_{i}}^{(i)}\right)\right\rangle_{X,\psi},

(3.4)

and evaluate all possible Wick contractions. There are two non-trivial
correlators involving the Lorentz currents:

\left\langle L_{\textrm{RNS}}^{mn}(z_{i})L_{\textrm{RNS}}^{pq}(z_{j})\right\rangle_{\psi}=\frac{1}{z_{ij}^{2}}(\eta^{mq}\eta^{np}-\eta^{mp}\eta^{nq}),

(3.5)

and

\left\langle L_{\textrm{RNS}}^{mn}(z_{1})L_{\textrm{RNS}}^{pq}(z_{2})L_{\textrm{RNS}}^{rs}(z_{3})\right\rangle_{\psi}=\\
=\frac{1}{z_{12}z_{23}z_{31}}(\eta^{ms}\eta^{nq}\eta^{pr}+\eta^{mr}\eta^{np}\eta^{qs}+\eta^{mq}\eta^{nr}\eta^{ps}+\eta^{mp}\eta^{ns}\eta^{qr})\\
-\frac{1}{z_{12}z_{23}z_{31}}(\eta^{ms}\eta^{np}\eta^{qr}+\eta^{mr}\eta^{nq}\eta^{ps}+\eta^{mq}\eta^{ns}\eta^{pr}+\eta^{mp}\eta^{nr}\eta^{qs}).

(3.6)

We then obtain

\displaystyle\left\langle V_{\textrm{RNS}}^{(1)}V_{\textrm{RNS}}^{(2)}V_{\textrm{RNS}}^{(3)}\right\rangle_{\textrm{matter}}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\partial X^{m}a_{m}^{(1)}(z_{1})\partial X^{n}a_{n}^{(2)}(z_{2})\partial X^{p}a_{p}^{(3)}(z_{3})\right\rangle_{X}

\displaystyle-\left(\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{4}\right)^{2}\frac{2}{z_{23}^{2}}\left\langle\partial X^{m}a_{m}^{(1)}(z_{1})f_{np}^{(2)}(z_{2})f^{(3)np}(z_{3})\right\rangle_{X}

\displaystyle-\left(\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{4}\right)^{2}\frac{2}{z_{31}^{2}}\left\langle f_{np}^{(1)}(z_{1})\partial X^{m}a_{m}^{(2)}(z_{2})f^{(3)np}(z_{3})\right\rangle_{X}

\displaystyle-\left(\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{4}\right)^{2}\frac{2}{z_{12}^{2}}\left\langle f_{np}^{(1)}(z_{1})f^{(2)np}(z_{2})\partial X^{m}a_{m}^{(3)}(z_{3})\right\rangle_{X}

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{4}\right)^{3}\frac{8}{z_{12}z_{23}z_{31}}\left\langle f_{m}^{(1)n}(z_{1})f_{n}^{(2)p}(z_{2})f_{p}^{(3)m}(z_{3})\right\rangle_{X}.

(3.7)

Now we are left with bosonic string-like correlators, which finally lead to

\left\langle V_{\textrm{RNS}}^{(1)}V_{\textrm{RNS}}^{(2)}V_{\textrm{RNS}}^{(3)}\right\rangle_{\textrm{matter}}=\frac{1}{z_{12}z_{23}z_{31}}\\
\times\left(\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{2}\right)^{2}\eta^{np}\left(a_{n}^{(1)}\partial^{m}a_{p}^{(2)}a_{m}^{(3)}+a_{m}^{(1)}a_{n}^{(2)}\partial^{m}a_{p}^{(3)}+\partial^{m}a_{p}^{(1)}a_{m}^{(2)}a_{n}^{(3)}\right),

(3.8)

where momentum conservation is left implicit. Therefore,

\mathcal{A}_{3}\left(a^{(1)},a^{(2)},a^{(3)}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{2}\right)^{2}f^{(1)mn}a_{m}^{(2)}a_{n}^{(3)}+\textrm{cyclic}(1,2,3),

(3.9)

which matches the three-point super Yang-Mills partial
amplitude with external gluons.

3.2
Pure spinor superstring (usual prescription)

In the pure spinor formalism, tree level correlators can be evaluated using
the measure

\Big\langle(\lambda\gamma^{m}\theta)(\lambda\gamma^{p}\theta)(\lambda\gamma^{q}\theta)(\theta\gamma_{mnp}\theta)\Big\rangle=-180\left(\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{2\zeta^{2}}\right)^{2}.

(3.10)

The term inside brackets is the only element in the ghost number three
cohomology. This is similar to the object
c\partial c\partial^{2}c

from the bosonic string, for instance. The normalization on the right
hand side of this equation can be consistently fixed. We pick that particular one in order
to simply illustrate a direct agreement with the three-point correlator
(
3.2
).

The pure spinor analogue of (
3.2
) is built out of three insertions of the unintegrated
vertex operator
(
2.18
)
, which can be cast as

\mathcal{A}_{3}=\left\langle\lambda^{\alpha}A_{\alpha}^{(1)}(z_{1})\lambda^{\beta}A_{\beta}^{(2)}(z_{2})\lambda^{\gamma}A_{\gamma}^{(3)}(z_{3})\right\rangle.

(3.11)

Using the superfield expansion for
A_{\alpha}
, it is easy to
show that we can choose a gauge in which

\lambda^{\alpha}A_{\alpha}=\zeta(\lambda\gamma^{m}\theta)a_{m}+\frac{2\zeta^{2}}{3}(\lambda\gamma^{m}\theta)(\theta\gamma_{m}\xi)-\frac{\zeta^{2}}{8}(\lambda\gamma^{p}\theta)(\theta\gamma_{mnp}\theta)f^{mn}+\mathcal{O}(\theta^{4}),

(3.12)

where
\xi^{\alpha}
is the spacetime superpartner of
a_{m}
. Given
that, in this gauge, the lowest superfield component is linear in
\theta^{\alpha}
,
the expansion above is enough to saturate the measure (
3.10
).
It is straightforward to show that

\mathcal{A}_{3}=-\frac{\zeta^{4}}{8}\left\langle(\lambda\gamma^{m}\theta)(\lambda\gamma^{n}\theta)(\lambda\gamma^{r}\theta)(\theta\gamma_{pqr}\theta)\right\rangle f^{(1)pq}a_{m}^{(2)}a_{n}^{(3)}\\
+\frac{4\zeta^{5}}{9}\left\langle(\lambda\gamma^{m}\theta)(\lambda\gamma^{n}\theta)(\gamma_{n}\theta)_{\alpha}(\lambda\gamma^{p}\theta)(\gamma_{p}\theta)_{\beta}\right\rangle a_{m}^{(1)}\xi^{(2)\alpha}\xi^{(3)\beta}+\textrm{cyclic}(1,2,3).

(3.13)

Now we just need the following identities, which can be obtained from SO
(9,1)
covariance arguments,

\displaystyle\left\langle(\lambda\gamma^{m}\theta)(\lambda\gamma^{n}\theta)(\lambda\gamma^{r}\theta)(\theta\gamma_{pqr}\theta)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{90}(\delta_{p}^{m}\delta_{q}^{n}-\delta_{q}^{m}\delta_{p}^{n})\left\langle(\lambda\gamma^{r}\theta)(\lambda\gamma^{s}\theta)(\lambda\gamma^{t}\theta)(\theta\gamma_{rst}\theta)\right\rangle,

(3.14)

\displaystyle=-2\left(\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{2\zeta^{2}}\right)^{2}(\delta_{p}^{m}\delta_{q}^{n}-\delta_{q}^{m}\delta_{p}^{n}),

(3.15)

\displaystyle\left\langle(\lambda\gamma^{m}\theta)(\lambda\gamma^{n}\theta)(\gamma_{n}\theta)_{\alpha}(\lambda\gamma^{p}\theta)(\gamma_{p}\theta)_{\beta}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{160}\gamma^{m}_{\alpha\beta}\left\langle(\lambda\gamma^{r}\theta)(\lambda\gamma^{n}\theta)(\lambda\gamma^{p}\theta)(\theta\gamma_{rnp}\theta)\right\rangle

(3.16)

\displaystyle=-\frac{9}{8}\left(\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{2\zeta^{2}}\right)^{2}\gamma^{m}_{\alpha\beta}.

(3.17)

After substituting the above expressions into equation
(
3.13
), we finally obtain

\mathcal{A}_{3}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{2}\right)^{2}\left(f^{(1)mn}a_{m}^{(2)}a_{n}^{(3)}-\zeta a_{m}^{(1)}(\xi^{(2)}\gamma^{m}\xi^{(3)})\right)+\textrm{cyclic}(1,2,3),

(3.18)

as expected. Indeed, there is plenty of evidence that the pure spinor
amplitudes match the ones computed in the RNS formalism (see
[
23
]
).

4
Alternative tree level prescription in the pure spinor formalism

In
[
25
]
, an alternative method for calculating
pure-spinor superstring scattering amplitudes was introduced. It
was inspired by the work of Lee and Siegel in
[
26
]
,
and involves only ghost number zero vertex operators. In this alternative prescription, the
N
-point tree level amplitude
with massless external states is given by

\mathcal{A}_{N}\propto z_{12}z_{23}z_{31}\left\langle V_{\textrm{PS}}(z_{1})V_{\textrm{PS}}(z_{2})V_{\textrm{PS}}(z_{3})\prod_{i=4}^{N}\left(\int dz_{i}\,V_{\textrm{PS}}(z_{i})\right)\right\rangle.

(4.1)

The idea is to perform all Wick contractions of the non-zero conformal
weight operators and pick the
\theta^{\alpha}=0
component of the
resulting expression. Given that
\mathcal{A}_{N}
is written in terms of
superfields, it is supposed to compute any amplitude involving bosons
or fermions.

Let us look at the simplest case,
N=3
:

\mathcal{A}_{3}\propto z_{12}z_{23}z_{31}\Big\langle V_{\textrm{PS}}(z_{1})V_{\textrm{PS}}(z_{2})V_{\textrm{PS}}(z_{3})\Big\rangle.

(4.2)

This expression resembles equation (
3.2
) in the
RNS formalism, with the identification of
V_{\textrm{PS}}
and
V_{\textrm{RNS}}
.
While gauge invariance is guaranteed there, the consistency of this ad-hoc prescription
for pure spinors is far from obvious. In fact, we shall see that it
fails when external fermions are present.

For the sake of completeness, we will present here the full three-point
calculation. The idea is to compute the correlator

5
Discussion

We have demonstrated in detail that the
alternative
tree level prescription for the pure spinor formalism is not consistent with the decoupling of BRST exact states nor invariant with respect to the position of the fixed vertices. These results were also confirmed via symbolic computation.

Our findings have no implication whatsoever to the usual pure spinor prescription, which has been exhaustively verified at tree level
[
23
]
. However, they hint at some tension with previous results in the literature, both recent and older, as we discuss in the remainder of this section.

B-RNS-GSS formalism

Recently, a new description of the superstring was introduced, suggesting a unified framework for the spinning string, the Green-Schwarz superstring, and the pure spinor formalism
[
27
]
. It has been dubbed the B-RNS-GSS formalism.

The world-sheet of the B-RNS-GSS superstring can be seen as a non-minimal version of the spinning string that includes a pure spinor. It contains the usual reparametrization ghosts plus world-sheet superpartners. In addition, it contains the spacetime superpartners of the target space coordinates. In particular, the massless vertex operators, unintegrated and integrated, can be written as

\displaystyle U

\displaystyle=cV_{\textrm{PS}}-\gamma\psi^{m}A_{m}+\ldots,

(5.10)

\displaystyle V

\displaystyle\equiv b_{-1}\cdot U,

\displaystyle=V_{\textrm{PS}},

(5.11)

respectively, where the ellipsis in

N

\mathcal{A}_{N}=\left\langle U(z_{1})U(z_{2})U(z_{3})\prod_{i=4}^{N}\left(\int dz_{i}\,V(z_{i})\right)\right\rangle.

(5.12)

We are leaving implicit the integration measure for the ghosts. Given that we need to saturate the three zero modes from the
c

Therefore, our results demonstrate that the tree level amplitude in the B-RNS-GSS formalism, as initially proposed, is incomplete. At the present stage, we can only speculate on the solution, but it is probably related to a non-trivial interplay between the ghost fields in the vertex (

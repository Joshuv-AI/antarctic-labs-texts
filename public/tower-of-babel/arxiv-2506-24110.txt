Pravin Kumar Natwariya {}^{\hrefhttps://orcid.org/0000-0001-9072-8430}, Shibsankar Si {}^{\hrefhttps://orcid.org/0009-0001-8038-976X}, Alekha C. Nayak {}^{\hrefhttps://orcid.org/0000-0001-6087-2490}, Tripurari Srivastava {}^{\hrefhttps://orcid.org/0000-0001-6856-9517}

Abstract
Abstract The neutrino telescopes have firmly established the existence of ultra-high-energy (UHE) neutrinos. Observations of these neutrinos offer a unique probe of neutrino self-interactions. This work investigates how the self-interacting neutrinos, mediated by scalar bosons, inject energy into the medium through radiative scattering with the cosmic neutrino background, leaving an imprint on the Cosmic Microwave Background (CMB) spectrum. The energy injection into plasma in redshift ranges, 5 × 10 4 ≲ z ≲ 2 × 10 6 5\times 10^{4}\lesssim z\lesssim 2\times 10^{6} and z ≲ 5 × 10 4 z\lesssim 5\times 10^{4} , leads to μ \mu -type and y -type of CMB spectral distortions, respectively. Using observational constraints from Cosmic Background Explorer/Far Infrared Absolute Spectrophotometer (COBE/FIRAS) and projected sensitivities from Primordial Inflation Explorer (PIXIE) experiments for μ \mu -type and y y -type CMB distortions, we derive the stringent upper bounds on the self-interaction coupling strength as a function of mediator mass for neutrino interactions. We focus on flavor-specific self-interaction related to muon neutrinos and sub-GeV mass mediators ( m ϕ m_{\phi} ). We find the upper bound on the self-interaction coupling strength to be ∼ 3.1 × 10 − 5 \sim 3.1\times 10^{-5} for the muon neutrino, considering UHE muon neutrino energy to be 1 PeV and PIXIE projected upper bounds on μ \mu -type CMB spectral distortion. The bound remains constant till the mediator mass reaches the center of mass energy, and after that, it gets relaxed and becomes proportional to the mediator mass. We have also compared our results with existing bounds in the literature. Our findings indicate that CMB spectral distortion could play a decisive role in exploring neutrino physics beyond the standard model of particle physics, and future missions like PIXIE can provide valuable insights.

Neutrinos are among the most abundant particles in the Universe and provide a potential window to explore physics beyond the Standard Model. They can also play a significant role in multimessenger astronomy to study exotic astrophysical phenomena, as well as in understanding the evolution of the Universe. In multimessenger astronomy, the neutrinos are particularly interesting because they travel in straight lines from their sources as they are nearly massless particles and rarely interact with matter passing through dense environments that block other types of probes, such as cosmic rays. IV
CMB Spectral Distortion

The CMB spectral distortion provides a unique window into the thermal history of the early Universe, probing the presence of any new physics.
The CMB spectrum is well-approximated by the black body spectrum
Fixsen et al. 1996
;
Mather et al. 1994
. As the Universe expands, the photon wavelengths increase, resulting in a lower temperature of the photons and a shift in the peak frequency of the spectrum. However, the shape of the spectrum remains unchanged solely due to the adiabatic expansion of the Universe— as the expansion uniformly scales the photon energies, maintaining the thermal distribution. When there is an energy injection into the plasma from any exotic source, the CMB spectrum may be distorted from the ideal black body spectrum. These injections introduce non-thermal photons or alter the ionization state of the plasma, leading to processes like Compton scattering or bremsstrahlung, which modify the photon distribution. Consequently, the CMB spectrum may exhibit deviations, broadly classified into two main types:
y
-type distortion or a
\mu
-type distortion. The type of distortion mainly depends on the epoch of the energy injection. The thermal equilibrium between matter and radiation is maintained when energy is injected into plasma at a redshift of
z>2\times 10^{6}

Khatri and Sunyaev 2012
;
Kunze and Komatsu 2014a
;
Si et al. 2025a
. During this epoch, photons and baryons efficiently scatter with one another. Therefore, the photons remain in full thermal equilibrium with the baryons. When energy is injected into plasma by any mechanism, the Compton scattering rapidly redistributes the energy into photons, and the Double Compton and Bremsstrahlung scattering modify the number density of photons to maintain a black body distribution for photons
Khatri and Sunyaev 2012
;
Kunze and Komatsu 2014a
.

As the Universe expands and cools, reaching redshifts
z<2\times 10^{6}
, elastic Compton scattering remains efficient, but Double Compton and bremsstrahlung scatterings become gradually inefficient, resulting in less thermalization of the CMB photon distribution. Therefore, any energy injection into the plasma increases the electron temperature, but the number density of photons remains the same due to elastic Compton scattering. These processes establish a Bose-Einstein distribution for the CMB photons, characterized by an effective non-zero chemical potential, rather than restoring the ideal blackbody spectrum— known as the
\mu

-type of CMB spectral distortion due to the energy injection into the plasma by radiative interaction between ultra-high-energy neutrinos and C
\nu
B can be expressed as follows
Kunze and Komatsu 2014a
;
Si et al. 2025a
:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial y}{\partial t}=\frac{1}{12}\left[\left(\frac{dE}{dVdt}\right)\,\bigg/\,{\rho_{\gamma}}\right]\,.

(13)

Now, the evolution of the
y
parameter with redshift z can be written as,

\displaystyle y=\frac{1}{12}\int\ \left[\left(\frac{dE}{dVdz}\right)\,\bigg/\,{\rho_{\gamma}}\right]\ dz\,.

(14)

The COBE/FIRAS set upper bounds on the
y
-type distortion to be
y=1.5\times 10^{-5}
with 95% confidence level
Fixsen et al. 1996
, and the future experiment PIXIE expected to detect
y
-type distortion at levels of
y=10^{-8}
at a
5\sigma
level
Kogut et al. 2011
.

VI
UV frameworks

Ultraviolet completions that generate both
\phi\,\bar{\mu}\mu
and
\phi\,\bar{\nu}_{\mu}\nu_{\mu}
interactions can arise in models with extended scalar sectors and flavor symmetries. Scalar couplings to muons naturally occur in two-Higgs-doublet models (2HDMs), where
\phi
mixes with the neutral component of a second Higgs doublet, yielding Yukawa interactions of the form
\phi\bar{\mu}\mu
. Left-handed neutrino couplings can instead emerge from type-II seesaw-like mechanisms
Blinov et al. 2019a
;
Jana and Klett 2024
, where the neutral component of a scalar triplet mixes with
\phi
, allowing terms like
\phi\,\bar{\nu_{\mu}}\nu_{\mu}
. In general, the two couplings arise from different sectors and need not be related. However, if both interactions are controlled by the same underlying symmetry, such as a gauged
U(1)_{L\mu-L_{\tau}}
, they can originate from the same scalar field and be naturally related or even equal. The Lagrangian considered in our framework can be generated by gauge-invariant effective operators like
(\bar{L}_{\mu}~H)\,\phi~\mu/\Lambda
and
(L_{\mu}~H)^{2}\,\phi/\Lambda^{2}
after integrating out heavy fields;
\Lambda
is the scale of new physics. A specific relation between the couplings may arise from additional flavor symmetries or from charge assignments under a larger symmetry group at a higher scale. Such structures can be naturally justified within frameworks that impose flavor alignment, for example, through gauged or global symmetries
Berryman et al. 2023
. As another example, simplified UV completions involving additional scalar doublets and singlet fermions with a softly broken
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetry can induce the low-energy Lagrangian considered in Eq. (
1
)
Liu et al. 2022b
. We would like to stress that in our analysis, the couplings are not required to be equal. However, for simplicity, we consider two scenarios: one in which the couplings to muons and neutrinos are equal, and another where they differ. Our main conclusions remain qualitatively unchanged, and the resulting low-energy effective behaviour is valid in both cases.

VII
Conclusion

This work investigates the cosmological implications of self-interacting ultrahigh-energy (UHE) neutrinos mediated by light scalar bosons, focusing on their imprint on Cosmic Microwave Background (CMB) spectral distortions. By considering a minimal setup of the UHE neutrinos originating from decaying super-heavy dark matter and interacts through a scalar, we analyzed the energy injection into the intergalactic medium (IGM) resulting from radiative scattering between these neutrinos and the cosmic neutrino background (C
\nu
B). This process generates high-energy photons via loop-mediated interactions, which distort the CMB spectrum—specifically producing
\mu
-type distortions at redshifts
5\times 10^{4}\lesssim z\lesssim 2\times 10^{6}
and
y
-type distortions at
z\lesssim 5\times 10^{4}
.

Using observational constraints from COBE/FIRAS and projected sensitivities from PIXIE, we derived stringent upper bounds on the flavor-specific self-interaction coupling strength
g_{\nu_{\mu}}
for muon neutrinos as a function of mediator mass
m_{\phi}
. Our key results are:

1.

For UHE neutrinos at 1 PeV, the PIXIE-projected bounds yield
g_{\nu_{\mu}}\lesssim 1.3\times 10^{-4}
(
\mu
-distortion) and
g_{\nu_{\mu}}\lesssim 1.5\times 10^{-4}
(
y
-distortion), improving upon FIRAS constraints by an order of magnitude.

2.

The bounds remain constant for
m_{\phi}\ll\sqrt{s}
(where
{s}\approx{2m_{\nu_{\mu}}E_{\nu}}
is the center-of-mass energy) but exhibit a characteristic kink near
m_{\phi}\sim\sqrt{s}
, where the cross-section peaks. For
m_{\phi}\gg\sqrt{s}
, the constraints relax proportionally to
m_{\phi}
.

3.

Increasing the UHE neutrino energy (e.g., to 100 PeV) shifts the kink to higher
m_{\phi}
and relaxes the bounds.

4.

We also present a scenarios with unequal couplings
g_{\nu_{\mu}}\neq g_{\mu}
, where
g_{\mu}=5\times 10^{-4}

Liu et al. 2022a
, which explains the excess in the anomalous magnetic moment of the muon
Abi et al. 2021
. In this case, the upper bounds on the coupling parameter found to be
3.1\times 10^{-5}
(
\mu
-type) and
5.2\times 10^{-5}
(
y
-type) using the PIXIE-projected bounds, which is an improvement on FIRAS bounds by two orders of magnitude.

For the comparison, we have also presented existing bounds from Big Bang Nucleosynthesis (BBN), rare kaon decays (
K\to\mu\nu\phi
), and resolutions to the Hubble tension and muon
(g-2)
anomaly. Our analysis demonstrates that CMB spectral distortions serve as a powerful, complementary probe of neutrino self-interactions beyond the Standard Model. Future missions like PIXIE could improve sensitivity by three orders of magnitude, offering unprecedented insights into neutrino physics, dark matter properties, and cosmological energy injection mechanisms.

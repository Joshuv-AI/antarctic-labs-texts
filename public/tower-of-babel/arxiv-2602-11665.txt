Fully First-Order Algorithms for Online Bilevel Optimization
Tingkai Jia, Cheng Chen

Abstract
In this work, we study non-convex-strongly-convex online bilevel optimization (OBO). Existing OBO algorithms are mainly based on hypergradient descent, which requires access to a Hessian-vector product (HVP) oracle and potentially incurs high computational costs. By reformulating the original OBO problem as a single-level online problem with inequality constraints and constructing a sequence of Lagrangian function, we eliminate the need for HVPs arising from implicit differentiation. Specifically, we propose a fully first-order algorithm for OBO, and provide theoretical guarantees showing that it achieves regret of O ⁡ ( 1 + V T + H 2 , T ) O(1+V_{T}+H_{2,T}) . Furthermore, we develop an improved variant with an adaptive inner-iteration scheme, which removes the dependence on the drift variation of the inner-level optimal solution and achieves regret of O ⁡ ( T + V T ) O(\sqrt{T}+V_{T}) . This regret have the advatange when V T ≥ O ⁡ ( T ) V_{T}\geq O(\sqrt{T}) .

Introduction

Online bilevel optimization (OBO) has attracted growing attention in recent years. It extends bilevel learning to streaming settings, where the underlying data distribution may drift over time.
The goal is to generate a sequence of decisions
\{\mathbf{x}_{t}\}_{t=1}^{T}
that tracks the time-varying optimal solutions
\{\mathbf{x}_{t}^{*}\}_{t=1}^{T}
over the horizon
t\in[T]
by solving

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\min_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}}F_{t}(\mathbf{x}):=f_{t}\left(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}_{t}^{*}(\mathbf{x})\right)\quad\text{s.t.}\quad\mathbf{y}_{t}^{*}(\mathbf{x})=\mathop{\rm argmin}_{\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}}}g_{t}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}),\end{split}

(1)

where the upper-level function
f_{t}:\mathcal{X}\times\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}}\to\mathbb{R}
is smooth but possibly non-convex, the inner-level function
g_{t}:\mathcal{X}\times\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}}\to\mathbb{R}
is smooth and
\mu_{g}
-strongly convex which ensures the existence of a unique minimizer
\mathbf{y}_{t}^{*}(\mathbf{x})
for all given
\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}
.

Existing OBO methods
(
36
;
21
;
3
;
19
)
build on hypergradient descent, which requires an inner iterative procedure to approximately solve the inner-level problem, and then update the outer decision
\mathbf{x}
using the hypergradient
\nabla F_{t}(\mathbf{x})
. Achieving a sufficiently accurate hypergradient typically requires many Hessian-vector products (HVP) to control the approximation error
29
;
12
;
11
;
26
;
10
;
31
;
25
. These algorithms may become impractical or even inapplicable when HVP queries are unavailable or prohibitively expensive
35
;
33
;
28
. This work instead studies OBO that relies solely on first-order gradients of the inner- and upper-level objectives to solve the above problem.

1.1
Our Contributions

By reformulating the original OBO problem (
1
) as a single-level online optimization problem with an inequality constraint, we construct a sequence of Lagrangian functions
\{\mathcal{L}_{t}\}_{t=1}^{T}
, where the constraint is incorporated as a penalty term. Given an appropriate multiplier sequence
\{\lambda_{t}\}_{t=1}^{T}
, the minimax solution of
\mathcal{L}_{t}
serves as a sufficiently accurate approximation to
\mathbf{x}_{t}^{*}
at each time step
t
(see Section
2.3
for details). Building on this reformulation, we propose a fully first-order online bilevel optimizer (F
2
OBO), presented in Algorithm
1
. Theoretical analysis shows that it can achieve an
O(1+V_{T}+H_{2,T})

Building on the adaptive-iteration strategy in AOBO
19

2

2

of the inner-level optimal solution. Although this comes at the cost of a larger iteration overhead and a weaker regret guarantee, i.e.,
O(\sqrt{T}+V_{T})
with an additional fixed

term, our method still ensures sublinear regret even when
\mathbf{y}_{t}^{*}(x)
drifts drastically (e.g.,
H_{2,T}=O(T)
or larger). This robustness is significant compared with static inner-iteration methods.

Assumption 2.1
.

For all

\mathbf{y}

Assumption 2.2
.

For all

.

For all

2.2
Online Bilevel Optimization

For non-convex-strongly-convex OBO, the uniqueness of the inner-level solution
\mathbf{y}_{t}^{*}(\mathbf{x})
for any
\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}
yields a unique closed-form expression for the hypergradient of
F_{t}
, given by:

\displaystyle\nabla F_{t}(\mathbf{x})=

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}f_{t}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}_{t}^{*}(\mathbf{x}))+\nabla\mathbf{y}_{t}^{*}(\mathbf{x})\nabla_{\mathbf{y}}f_{t}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}_{t}^{*}(\mathbf{x}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}f_{t}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}_{t}^{*}(\mathbf{x}))-\nabla_{\mathbf{x}\mathbf{y}}^{2}g_{t}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}_{t}^{*}(\mathbf{x}))\left[\nabla_{\mathbf{y}\mathbf{y}}^{2}g_{t}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}_{t}^{*}(\mathbf{x}))\right]^{-1}\nabla_{\mathbf{y}}f_{t}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}_{t}^{*}(\mathbf{x})).

(2)

We consider the
bilevel local regret
without window averaging
27
as the evaluation criterion for the algorithm, which can accurately reflect the system performance when functions change rapidly, defined as

\displaystyle\mathrm{Reg}_{F}(T)=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\left\|\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{X}}\left(\mathbf{x}_{t},\nabla F_{t}(\mathbf{x}_{t}),\gamma\right)\right\|^{2}.

(3)

Here the notation
\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{X}}
presents the gradient mapping with respect to
\mathcal{X}
, that is

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{X}}\left(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{g},\gamma\right):=\frac{1}{\gamma}(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}^{+}),\quad\text{where}\quad\mathbf{x}^{+}=\mathop{\rm argmin}_{\mathbf{u}\in\mathcal{X}}\left\{\left\langle\mathbf{g},\mathbf{u}\right\rangle+\frac{1}{2\gamma}\|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{x}\|^{2}\right\},

(4)

for given
\mathbf{g}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}
and
\gamma>0
. The analysis of this regret relies primarily on the following two variation regularities for describing function changes, which are also used in
21
;
3
;
27
.

\displaystyle V_{T}:=\sum_{t=2}^{T}\sup_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}}\!\left|F_{t-1}(\mathbf{x})-F_{t}(\mathbf{x})\right|\quad\text{and}\quad H_{p,T}:=\sum_{t=2}^{T}\sup_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}}\left\|\mathbf{y}_{t-1}^{*}(\mathbf{x})-\mathbf{y}_{t}^{*}(\mathbf{x})\right\|^{p}.

(5)

2.3
Single-Level Reformulation of OBO Problem

Due to
(
20
;
34
;
39
;
8
)
, the original problem (
1
) can be equivalently written as an inequality constrained single-level problem, shown as:

\displaystyle\min_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X},\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}}}f_{t}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\quad

\displaystyle\text{s.t.}\quad g_{t}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})-g_{t}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}_{t}^{*}(\mathbf{x}))\leq 0.

(6)

By treating the constraint as a penalty term, we construct the following Lagrangian function
\mathcal{L}_{t}
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{t}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y},\lambda)=f_{t}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})+\lambda\left(g_{t}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})-g_{t}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}_{t}^{*}(\mathbf{x}))\right).

(7)

We can finally approximately transform problem (
6
) into an min-max optimization problem for
\mathcal{L}_{\lambda,t}^{*}(\mathbf{x})
as follows

\displaystyle\min_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}}\mathcal{L}_{\lambda,t}^{*}(\mathbf{x}):=\mathcal{L}_{t}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}_{\lambda,t}^{*}(\mathbf{x}),\lambda)\quad\text{s.t.}\quad\mathbf{y}_{\lambda,t}^{*}(\mathbf{x})=\rm argmin_{\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}}}\mathcal{L}_{t}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y},\lambda).

(8)

Here the multiplier
\lambda>0
represents the degree to which the solution to (
8
) conforms to the constraint, and we have
\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow\infty}\mathcal{L}^{*}_{\lambda,t}(\mathbf{x})=f_{t}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}_{t}^{*}(\mathbf{x}))
.

Based on the above, we approximate the OBO problem by an online min-max optimization problem. The ingenuity of this approach lies in its elimination of the requirement for a second-order gradient oracle arising from the implicit term
\nabla\mathbf{y}_{t}^{*}(\mathbf{x})
in (
2
). Specifically, we have

\displaystyle\nabla\mathcal{L}_{\lambda,t}^{*}(\mathbf{x})=

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathcal{L}_{t}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}_{\lambda,t}^{*}(\mathbf{x}),\lambda)+\nabla\mathbf{y}_{\lambda,t}^{*}(\mathbf{x})\nabla_{\mathbf{y}}\mathcal{L}_{t}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}_{\lambda,t}^{*}(\mathbf{x}),\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}f_{t}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}_{\lambda,t}^{*}(\mathbf{x}))+\lambda\left(\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}g_{t}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}_{\lambda,t}^{*}(\mathbf{x}))-\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}g_{t}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}_{t}^{*}(\mathbf{x}))\right),

note that the hypergradient does not require the calculation of
\nabla\mathbf{y}_{\lambda,t}^{*}(\mathbf{x})
, because
\nabla_{\mathbf{y}}\mathcal{L}_{t}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}_{\lambda,t}^{*}(\mathbf{x}),\lambda)=0
holds for all
t\in[T]
and any
\lambda>0
.

Lemma 3.3

In this section, we consider improving F

4.1
Algorithm

To summarize, F
2
OBO solves the two inner subproblems using a fixed number of iterations at each time step
t
. This static strategy is inevitably sensitive to changes in the underlying functions, which is described by
H_{2,T}
. Considering the calculation of
\min_{\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}}}\mathcal{L}_{t}(\mathbf{x}_{t},\mathbf{y},\lambda_{t})
in (
8

\mathcal{L}_{t}
remains
(\lambda_{t}\mu_{g}/2)
-strongly convex and
(2\lambda_{t}L_{g,1})
-smooth if
\lambda_{t}\geq 2L_{f,1}/\mu_{g}
for all
t\in[T]
. With the update rule in (
9

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda_{t}\mu_{g}}{2}\left\|\mathbf{y}_{t}^{k+1}-\mathbf{y}_{\lambda_{t},t}^{*}(\mathbf{x}_{t})\right\|\leq\left\|\nabla_{\mathbf{y}}\mathcal{L}_{t}(\mathbf{x}_{t},\mathbf{y}_{t}^{k+1},\lambda_{t})\right\|.

The distance between the current iterate and the optimum can be upper bounded by the norm of a computable gradient. The error tolerance parameter
\delta_{\mathbf{y}}
can be set to determine when a good approximate solution
\mathbf{y}_{t+1}
has been obtained, and following the same way, the final
\|\mathbf{y}_{t+1}-\mathbf{y}_{\lambda_{t},t}^{*}(\mathbf{x}_{t})\|
and
\|\mathbf{z}_{t+1}-\mathbf{y}_{t}^{*}(\mathbf{x}_{t})\|
can be controlled by

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda_{t}\mu_{g}}{2}\left\|\mathbf{y}_{t+1}-\mathbf{y}_{\lambda_{t},t}^{*}(\mathbf{x}_{t})\right\|\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla_{\mathbf{y}}\mathcal{L}_{t}(\mathbf{x}_{t},\mathbf{y}_{t+1},\lambda_{t})\right\|\leq\delta_{\mathbf{y}}\quad\text{and}

(12)

\displaystyle\mu_{g}\left\|\mathbf{z}_{t+1}-\mathbf{y}_{t}^{*}(\mathbf{x}_{t})\right\|\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla_{\mathbf{y}}g_{t}(\mathbf{x}_{t},\mathbf{y}_{t+1})\right\|\leq\delta_{\mathbf{z}}.

(13)

We use two adaptive inner iterative processes to solve for
\mathbf{y}_{t}^{*}(\mathbf{x}_{t})
and
\mathbf{y}_{\lambda_{t},t}^{*}(\mathbf{x}_{t})

t

has a smoothness coefficient much larger than
g_{t}
, which makes the computational cost of solving the problem much higher, as can be seen in the theoretical results in the next section. Therefore, solving the two subproblems independently is more efficient.

4.2
Regret Analysis

The main improvement of our method is that it eliminates the dependency of the regret upper bound on
H_{2,T}
, which describes the drift of the inner optimal solution
\|\mathbf{y}_{t}^{*}(\mathbf{x})-\mathbf{y}_{t-1}^{*}(\mathbf{x})\|
for any
\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}
.
Based on the error tolerance parameters that control the solution approximation errors in (
12
) and (
13
), we can bound the resulting hypergradient error via the following lemma.

Lemma 4.1
.

Under Assumptions
2.1
and
2.2
, let
\Lambda(t,\lambda_{t})=\left(1+\frac{1}{t}\right)^{\tau}\lambda_{t}
for some
\tau>0
,
\lambda_{1}>\frac{2L_{f,1}}{\mu_{g}}
,
\alpha\leq\frac{1}{L_{g,1}}
and
\beta\leq\frac{1}{2\lambda_{T}L_{g,1}}
, the approximation error of hypergradient in problem (
8
) can be bounded as follows:

\displaystyle\left\|\widetilde{\nabla}\mathcal{L}_{\lambda_{t},t}^{*}(\mathbf{x}_{t})-\nabla\mathcal{L}_{\lambda_{t},t}^{*}(\mathbf{x}_{t})\right\|^{2}\leq

\displaystyle 6\lambda_{t}^{2}L_{g,1}^{2}\left\|\mathbf{y}_{t+1}-\mathbf{y}_{\lambda_{t},t}^{*}(\mathbf{x}_{t})\right\|^{2}+3\lambda_{t}^{2}L_{g,1}^{2}\left\|\mathbf{z}_{t+1}-\mathbf{y}_{t}^{*}(\mathbf{x}_{t})\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 24\kappa_{g}^{2}\delta_{\mathbf{y}}^{2}+3\lambda_{t}^{2}\kappa_{g}^{2}\delta_{\mathbf{z}}^{2}.

Building on the above lemma and following the same analysis framework as for F
2
OBO, we obtain the following final results.

Theorem 4.2
.

Under Assumptions
2.1
-
2.3
, let
\Lambda(t,\lambda_{t})=\left(1+\frac{1}{t}\right)^{\tau}\lambda_{t}
for some
\tau>0
,
\lambda_{1}>\frac{2L_{f,1}}{\mu_{g}}
,
\alpha\leq\frac{1}{L_{g,1}}
,
\beta\leq\frac{1}{2\lambda_{T}L_{g,1}}
,
\gamma\leq\frac{1}{2L_{\mathcal{L},1}}
,
\delta_{\mathbf{y}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{T}}
and
\delta_{\mathbf{z}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{T^{1+\tau}}}
, Algorithm
2
can guarantee

\displaystyle\mathrm{Reg}_{F}(T)\leq 2D_{1}^{2}\sum_{t=1}^{T}t^{-2\tau}+2\mathrm{Reg}_{\mathcal{L}}(T)\leq O\left(\sum_{t=1}^{T}t^{-\tau}+V_{T}\right),

where
C_{1}
,
C_{\lambda}
,
\rho
,
c
are some constants. The total number of inner iterations
\mathcal{I}_{T}
satisfies

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{T}\leq O\left(T^{1+2\tau}+T^{2\tau}H_{2,T}\right).

The upper bound on the total number of inner iterations
\mathcal{I}_{T}
is dominated by solving the subproblem
\min_{\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}}}\mathcal{L}_{t}(\mathbf{x}_{t},\mathbf{y},\lambda_{t})
, since it is generally harder to optimize than
g_{t}
. Note that AOBO
19
achieves the optimal bilevel local regret
\Omega(1+V_{T})
with
O(T\log T)
query of gradients and HVPs. Our method achieve the same bound if we set
\tau>1
, which requires
O(T^{3})
first-order gradient evaluations. If we choose
\tau=1/2
, We can guarantee a sublinear regret of
O(\sqrt{T}+V_{T})
, which does not rely on
H_{2,T}
. In this setting, the total number of gradient quiries is at most
O(T^{2}+TH_{2,T})
.

4.3
Discussion

The upper bound in Theorem
4.2
have the advantage when the drift of
\mathbf{y}_{t}^{*}(\mathbf{x})
is more severe. We provide a
1
-dimensional example for illustration. Consider a set of
\{f_{t},g_{t}\}_{t=1}^{T}
within constraints
\mathcal{X}=\{x\,|\,x\in[-1,1]\}
and
y\in\mathbb{R}
that satisfies
V_{T}=0
and
H_{2,T}=O(T)
:

\displaystyle f_{t}(x,y)\equiv\widetilde{f}(y)=ce^{-y^{2}}\quad\text{and}\quad g_{t}(x,y)=\frac{\mu_{g}}{2}\left(y-(-1)^{t}x\right)^{2}.

Our construction satisfies Assumptions
2.1
-
2.3
when constant
c
is set appropriately. With
y_{t}^{*}(x)=(-1)^{t}x
,
f_{t}(x,y_{t}^{*}(x))=\widetilde{f}(y_{t}^{*}(x))\equiv ce^{-x^{2}}
, it holds that

\displaystyle V_{T}=\sum_{t=2}^{T}\sup_{x\in[-1,1]}\left|\widetilde{f}(y_{t}^{*}(x))-\widetilde{f}(y_{t-1}^{*}(x))\right|=0,\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle H_{2,T}=\sum_{t=2}^{T}\sup_{x\in[-1,1]}\left|(-1)^{t}x-(-1)^{t-1}x\right|^{2}=4T-4.

In the case of oscillatory drift in the inner-level optimal solution, our AF
2
OBO still guarantees a sublinear regret bound of
O(\sqrt{T})
if
\tau=1/2
, while the regret guarantees of static inner-iteration methods, such as OBBO
3
and F
2
OBO, will suffer from linear regret.

5
Conclusion

This work develops a fully first-order algorithm for non-convex-strongly-convex OBO and provides its regret analysis. The proposed fully first-order online bilevel optimizer (F
2
OBO) attains the same regret upper bound as hypergradient-based methods under favorable conditions, while preserving comparable computational complexity. We further propose an adaptive-iteration variant, AF
2
OBO, which removes the dependence of the regret bound on
V_{T}
by dynamically adjusting the inner-iteration numbers, at the cost of additional iterations.

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}\left\|\widetilde{\nabla}\mathcal{L}_{\lambda_{t},t}^{*}(\mathbf{x}_{t})-\nabla\mathcal{L}_{\lambda_{t},t}^{*}(\mathbf{x}_{t})\right\|^{2}\leq

\displaystyle c_{\tau}\Delta_{1}+\frac{288cL_{f,0}^{2}L_{g,1}^{3}}{\lambda_{1}^{2}\mu_{g}^{3}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\frac{1}{t^{2\tau}}+\frac{C_{\lambda}L_{g,1}^{2}}{\mu_{g}^{2}(1-\rho)}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\left\|\mathbf{x}_{t-1}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{C_{\lambda}}{1-\rho}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\left\|\mathbf{y}_{t-1}^{*}(\mathbf{x}_{t})-\mathbf{y}_{t}^{*}(\mathbf{x}_{t})\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{\tau}\Delta_{1}+\frac{288cL_{f,0}^{2}L_{g,1}^{3}}{\lambda_{1}^{2}\mu_{g}^{3}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\frac{1}{t^{2\tau}}+\frac{2\gamma^{2}C_{\lambda}L_{g,1}^{2}}{\mu_{g}^{2}(1-\rho)}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\left\|\widetilde{\nabla}\mathcal{L}_{\lambda_{t},t}^{*}(\mathbf{x}_{t})-\nabla\mathcal{L}_{\lambda_{t},t}^{*}(\mathbf{x}_{t})\right\|^{2}

Let

,

\displaystyle(b)\leq

Remind of (
24
), we substitute (

) and (

\displaystyle+\left(2+\frac{1}{\eta\theta}\right)\left(\frac{c_{\tau}\Delta_{1}}{C_{1}}+\frac{288cL_{f,0}^{2}L_{g,1}^{3}}{\lambda_{1}^{2}\mu_{g}^{3}C_{1}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\frac{1}{t^{2\tau}}+\frac{2\gamma^{2}C_{\lambda}L_{g,1}^{2}}{\mu_{g}^{2}C_{1}(1-\rho)}\mathrm{Reg}_{\mathcal{L}}(T)+\frac{C_{\lambda}}{C_{1}(1-\rho)}H_{2,T}\right).

Spin non-Collinear Real-Time Time-Dependent Density-Functional Theory and Implementation in the Modern GPU-Accelerated INQ code
Jacopo Simoni, Xavier Andrade, Wuzhang Fang, Andrew C. Grieder, Alfredo A. Correa, Tadashi Ogitsu, Yuan Ping

Abstract
Time-dependent density functional theory is a theory that describes the time evolution of quantum mechanical many-electron systems under the influence of external time-dependent electric and magnetic fields. INQ is a specially designed software to efficiently solve the real-time time-dependent density functional theory equations on graphics processing units, which aim to overcome the computational limitation of time and size scales of non-equilibrium quantum dynamics. In this work we will present an implementation of non-collinear time-dependent density functional theory for the INQ code to simulate spin dynamics in real time and discuss the implementation of non-collinear magnetic effects into the code. We will discuss the implementation of exchange-correlation magnetic fields, spin-orbit coupling, and the interaction between the electronic system and external magnetic fields. We will then consider several prototypical examples of spin dynamics in magnetic clusters and solids after light excitation. Potential applications range from the study of real-time dynamics of magnons to ultrafast spin dynamics under linear and circularly polarized laser excitation, as well as spectroscopic signatures such as magnetic circular dichroism and pump-probe Kerr rotation.

Introduction

Magnetism is a fundamental interaction in nature that is responsible for the attractive and repulsive forces observed in magnetic materials. Localized magnetic moments can interact and cause macroscopic-magnetic ordering. Ferromagnetism and anti-ferromagnetism are the simplest forms of such macroscopic ordering; their origin is quantum mechanical and determined by the exchange interaction and the Pauli exclusion principle. Magnetic anisotropy is instead a measure of the coupling between spin and spatial degrees of freedom in a material. This is determined at the atomic level by the spin-orbit interaction. A correct ab-initio description of the magnetic properties of materials must be able to capture these different interactions
Sanvito 2019
.

The success of density functional theory in describing the magnetic properties of materials can be ascribed to its ability to account for exchange interactions, via the exchange-correlation potential, and magnetic anisotropy through the spin-orbit interaction
Bihlmayer 2018
. Although density functional theory is a formally exact approach to the calculation of the ground state of electronic systems, in practice, the exact exchange-correlation functional is unknown and approximations must be considered. Local-density approximations have been shown to be quite successful in describing the equilibrium magnetic properties
Brooks et al. 2001
and the slow adiabatic dynamics in solids
Antropov et al. 1995
;
Antropov et al. 1996
.

The aim of time-dependent density functional theory is to extend this success to linear response properties and to the real-time domain
Xu et al. 2024
. Under non-equilibrium conditions, the exchange interactions become dynamical, and the simple picture of Heisenberg exchange breaks down. To overcome this problem, electronic degrees of freedom should also evolve dynamically.

Out-of-equilibrium dynamics in magnetic systems is important for many different applications including spintronics
Wu et al. 2010
and orbitronics
Gu et al. 2024
;
Jo et al. 2024
, quantum computation
Vandersypen and Eriksson 2019
, skyrmion dynamics for magnetic storage technologies
Nagaosa and Tokura 2013
;
Göbel et al. 2021
;
Fert et al. 2017
, ultrafast magnetism
Fähnle et al. 2018
, spin and orbital momentum dynamics in chiral systems
Luo et al. 2023
, the chiral-induced spin selectivity (CISS) effect
Bloom et al. 2024
and collinear spin and orbital Edelstein effect in solids
Johansson 2024
.

Ultrafast magnetism describes the sub-picosecond dynamics of magnetic systems under the application of intense laser pulses. The complete demagnetization of a
Ni
film has been experimentally demonstrated
Beaurepaire et al. 1996
. Later, other experiments confirmed such findings and have shown the possibility of laser-induced spin reorientation and modification of the magnetic structure
Yang et al. 2017
;
Kimel et al. 2005
;
Ju et al. 2004
. Femtomagnetism is a research field that focuses on the control of the magnetic order in materials using femtosecond laser pulses and has two main goals: (i) a better understanding of the physics of light-induced spin dynamics on very short time scales; (ii) control and manipulation of the spin degrees of freedom on sub-picosecond time scales. The uncertainty on the underlying physical mechanism responsible for this phenomenon makes time-dependent density functional theory a particularly attractive approach to studying such phenomena.

In the CISS effect, the transmission probability of electrons through a chiral molecule depends on the electron’s spin state, generating spin polarized electrons
Waldeck et al. 2021
. This has important implications in catalysis
Metzger et al. 2020
, enantio-separation
Banerjee-Ghosh et al. 2018
and spintronics
Yang 2020
. No complete theoretical description is available at the moment; however, there is a general consensus on the fact that a correct description of the interplay between spin-orbit, electron-electron and electron-phonon interactions is required to reproduce such an effect
Zöllner et al. 2020
;
Liu et al. 2021
;
Fransson 2025
. The non-collinear formulation of time-dependent density functional theory, thanks to its ability to treat spin-orbit non-perturbatively and include electronic correlations, is potentially applicable to studying such an effect.

Spin-wave excitations are considered promising information carriers in spintronics due to their low energy consumption and fast speed of operation
Chumak et al. 2015
. Non-perturbative schemes to compute the magnetic excitation spectrum in extended systems within time-dependent density functional theory have been proposed
Tancogne-Dejean et al. 2020
. This has potential applications in the field of magnonics
Rezende 2020
and quantum magnonics
Yuan et al. 2022
;
Zhang 2023
, which is based on the idea of magnons as quantum information carriers.

Several implementations of real-time time-dependent density functional theory are already available based on real-space multigrids
Jakowski et al. 2025
(within the
RMG
code),
NWChem
has an implementation based on Gaussian basis sets
Lopata and Govind 2011
.
Octopus

Tancogne-Dejean et al. 2020
;
Andrade et al. 2015
works on a real-space grid and can be used to perform real-time simulation of spin-polarized systems,
Elk

Dewhurst et al.
is a full-potential linearized augmented plane-waves (FP-LAPW) code working for crystalline solids that can also simulate real-time evolution of magnetic systems.
The
INQ
code
Andrade et al. 2021
is designed to provide a coherent and computationally efficient framework for the implementation of time-dependent density functional theory that can make use of large GPU-based supercomputers.
INQ
is a streamlined implementation of density functional theory and time-dependent density functional theory that is very compact, only 12,000 lines of code, and that is easy to maintain and further develop. Here we discuss the implementation of non-collinear spin density functional theory and time-dependent density functional theory in the code, which provides an accurate and efficient tool to study various spin-related phenomena in and out of equilibrium.

In section (
2
) we discuss the theoretical background required for the non-collinear spin density functional theory implementation. In section (
3
) we present the details of the implementation. In section (
4
) we present results and a comparison with other codes by looking in particular at the dynamics under external electric and magnetic pulses.

2
Theoretical background

Before introducing the non-collinear version of spin density functional theory in subsection (
2.2
) we briefly discuss the collinear version in (
2.1
). time-dependent density functional theory and its non-collinear version are discussed in sections (
2.3
) and (
2.4
). In the remaining part of the theory section, we analyze the approximations used to implement the non-collinear equations in
INQ
.

2.1
Density Functional Theory for spin polarized systems

density functional theory has been a widely successful theory for the calculation of electronic structures in both molecules and materials
Kohn et al. 1996
. The basic formalism of density functional theory is given by the Kohn-Sham equation
Kohn and Sham 1965
(atomic units are used throughout)

\bigg[-\frac{1}{2}\nabla^{2}+v_{\rm KS}[n]({\bf r})\bigg]\psi_{\rm i}^{\rm KS}({\bf r})=\varepsilon_{\rm i}^{\rm KS}\psi_{\rm i}^{\rm KS}({\bf r}),

(1)

where
\varepsilon_{\rm i}^{\rm KS}
and
\psi_{\rm i}^{\rm KS}({\bf r})
correspond to the Kohn-Sham energy eigenvalues and eigenstates.
v_{\rm KS}({\bf r})
is the Kohn-Sham potential, a functional of the electron density given by the sum of three terms: the external potential due to the surrounding ions, the Hartree, and the exchange-correlation potentials and can be written as

\displaystyle v_{\rm KS}[n]({\bf r})=v_{\rm ext}({\bf r})+v_{\rm H}[n]({\bf r})+v_{\rm xc}[n]({\bf r})

\displaystyle=-\sum_{\rm a}\frac{Z_{\rm a}}{|{\bf r}-{\bf R}_{\rm a}|}+\int d{\bf r^{\prime}}\frac{n({\bf r^{\prime}})}{|{\bf r}-{\bf r^{\prime}}|}+v_{\rm xc}[n]({\bf r})\ .

(2)

Eq. (
1
) and (
2.1
) cannot be directly applied to magnetic systems. Spin density functional theory
von Barth and Hedin 1972
requires the extension of Eq. (
1
) for spin-polarized systems through the introduction of two dynamical variables:
n_{\alpha}({\bf r})
with
\alpha=0,1
. These correspond to the spin-up and spin-down densities respectively. This is the so-called spin-unrestricted formulation of density functional theory
Jacob and Reiher 2012
. The direction of spin polarization is arbitrary, but it is usually taken along the
z
axis. In the presence of an external magnetic field
b_{\rm ext}({\bf r})
, spin density functional theory yields the equation,

\displaystyle\bigg[

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\nabla^{2}+v_{\rm KS}^{\alpha}[n_{0},n_{1}]({\bf r})+

\displaystyle+(-1)^{\alpha}\mu_{\rm B}b_{\rm ext}({\bf r})\bigg]\psi_{i\alpha}^{\rm KS}({\bf r})=\varepsilon_{i\alpha}^{\rm KS}\psi_{i\alpha}^{\rm KS}({\bf r})\ .

(3)

Here, the two density components are obtained from
n_{\alpha}({\bf r})=\sum_{i}f_{i\alpha}|\psi_{i\alpha}^{\rm KS}({\bf r})|^{2}
, with
f_{i\alpha}
indicating the state occupation.
\varepsilon_{i\alpha}^{\rm KS}
are the Kohn-Sham eigenvalues for the spin-up and down states.
\mu_{\rm B}
is the Bohr magneton (1/2 in atomic units).

Eq. (
2.1
) is usually expressed in terms of the total charge density
n({\bf r})=n_{0}({\bf r})+n_{1}({\bf r})
, and the spin density along the quantization axis of the system,
m({\bf r})=n_{0}({\bf r})-n_{1}({\bf r})
. This results in

\displaystyle\bar{v}_{\rm KS}[n,m]({\bf r})

\displaystyle=\frac{\sum_{\alpha}v^{\alpha}_{\rm KS}({\bf r})}{2}=

\displaystyle=\!v_{\rm ext}({\bf r})+v_{\rm H}[n]({\bf r})+\bar{v}_{\rm xc}[n,m]({\bf r})

(4)

\displaystyle\bar{v}_{\rm xc}[n,m]({\bf r})

\displaystyle=\frac{v_{\rm xc}^{0}({\bf r})+v_{\rm xc}^{1}({\bf r})}{2}

(5)

\displaystyle b_{\rm xc}[n,m]({\bf r})

\displaystyle=\frac{\sum_{\alpha}(-1)^{\alpha}v^{\alpha}_{\rm KS}({\bf r})}{2}=

\displaystyle=\frac{v^{0}_{\rm xc}[n,m]({\bf r})-v^{1}_{\rm xc}[n,m]({\bf r})}{2}

(6)

\displaystyle b_{\rm s}[n,m]({\bf r})

\displaystyle=\mu_{\rm B}b_{\rm ext}({\bf r})+b_{\rm xc}[n,m]({\bf r})\ .

(7)

By using this new set of variables, the Kohn-Sham Hamiltonian can then be expressed in the spin space as a
2\times 2
matrix.

\displaystyle\mathbf{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm KS}({\bf r})=\Big[-\frac{1}{2}\nabla^{2}+v_{\rm ext}({\bf r})+v_{\rm H}[n]({\bf r})+

\displaystyle+\bar{v}_{\rm xc}[n,m]({\bf r})\Big]\mathbf{I}_{2}+b_{\rm s}[n,m]({\bf r})\mathbf{\sigma}_{\rm z},

(8)

\mathbf{\sigma}_{z}
is the z component of the Pauli matrices and
\mathbf{I}_{2}
is the
2\times 2
identity matrix. Eq. (
2.1
) has a strong limitation: It does not allow the spin to rotate. This means that we need to generalize the equations of collinear spin density functional theory if we want to perform spin dynamics. We discuss how this can be done in the next section.

2.2
Non-collinear formulation of spin density functional theory

In the Hamiltonian of Eq. (
2.1
) the spin is always projected along the z axis. This means that the spin cannot rotate and that the Hamiltonian of the system commutes with
\mathbf{\sigma}_{z}
,
\big[\mathbf{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm KS}({\bf r}),\mathbf{\sigma}_{z}\big]=0
.

The Kohn-Sham eigenstates,
\psi_{i\alpha}^{\rm KS}({\bf r})
, are then also eigenstates of
\mathbf{\sigma}_{z}
, and are labeled according to the band index i and the spin index
\alpha=0,1
. The generalization of Eq. (
2.1
) to spin non-collinear cases requires the spin quantization axis not to be fixed along the z axis and to be free to rotate. In such a case, the spin density becomes a vector field

{\bf m}({\bf r})=\sum_{i}f_{i}\psi_{\rm i}^{\rm KS}({\bf r})^{\dagger}\bm{\sigma}\psi_{\rm i}^{\rm KS}({\bf r})\ .

(9)

Where the Kohn-Sham wave function is represented by a two-dimensional spinorial field. The spinor wave function is not an eigenstate of the
\sigma_{\rm z}
Pauli matrix. This means that the spinor is not fully polarized, contrary to the eigenstates of ordinary spin density functional theory that are polarized either up or down (
0
,
1
). The spinor state can be in a superposition of up and down spin states.

The spin non-collinear Hamiltonian can then be written as

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\rm KS}({\bf r})=\Big[-\frac{1}{2}\nabla^{2}+v_{\rm ext}({\bf r})+v_{\rm H}[n]({\bf r})+

\displaystyle+\bar{v}_{\rm xc}^{\rm NC}[n,{\bf m}]({\bf r})\Big]\mathbf{I}_{2}+{\bf b}_{\rm s}^{\rm NC}[n,{\bf m}]({\bf r})\cdot\bm{\sigma}\ ,

(10)

where

\displaystyle{\bf b}_{\rm s}^{\rm NC}[n,{\bf m}]({\bf r})=\mu_{\rm B}{\bf b}_{\rm ext}({\bf r})+{\bf b}_{\rm xc}^{\rm NC}[n,{\bf m}]({\bf r})\ .

(11)

\bar{v}_{\rm xc}^{\rm NC}[n,{\bf m}]
and
{\bf b}_{\rm xc}^{\rm NC}[n,{\bf m}]
are functionals of the charge density and of the spin density vector fields and generalize
\bar{v}_{\rm xc}[n,m]({\bf r})
and
b_{\rm xc}[n,m]({\bf r})
in the non-collinear case.

2.3
Time Dependent Density Functional Theory

As an extension of density functional theory to real time, time-dependent density functional theory is based on the Runge-Gross theorem
Runge and Gross 1984
. This theorem states that if two external potentials,
v_{\rm ext}({\bf r},t)
and
v_{\rm ext}^{\prime}({\bf r},t)
, both time-dependent and Taylor-expandable (around an initial time
t_{0}
) differ by more than a simple time-dependent function
C(t)
, then the evolving time-dependent densities
n({\bf r},t)
and
n^{\prime}({\bf r},t)
starting from the same ground state must also be different. This is equivalent to saying that there is always a one-to-one mapping between the external time-dependent potential and the electron density and to the observables. The van Leeuwen theorem guarantees instead that the time-dependent density
n({\bf r},t)
evolving from an initial state
\Psi_{0}
under an external potential
v_{\rm ext}({\bf r},t)
can also be reproduced by a non-interacting system
van Leeuwen 1999
.

The external potential can be expressed in the following general form

v_{\rm ext}({\bf r},t)=v_{\rm ext}^{0}({\bf r})+\theta(t-t_{0})v_{\rm ext}^{1}({\bf r},t)\ .

(12)

Where
v_{\rm ext}^{0}({\bf r})
is the ground state external potential, corresponding to the bare electron-ion Coulomb interaction.
v_{\rm ext}^{1}({\bf r},t)
is the external scalar time-dependent perturbation. The time-dependent Kohn-Sham equations are then written as
Ullrich 2011

\displaystyle i\frac{\partial}{\partial t}

\displaystyle\psi_{i}^{\rm KS}({\bf r},t)\!=\!\!\Bigg[-\frac{1}{2}\nabla^{2}+v_{\rm ext}({\bf r},t)+v_{\rm H}[n]({\bf r},t)+

\displaystyle+v_{\rm xc}^{\rm NA}[n,\Psi_{0},\Phi_{0}]({\bf r},t)\Bigg]\psi_{i}^{\rm KS}({\bf r},t)\ ,

(13)

v_{\rm xc}^{\rm NA}[n,\Psi_{0},\Phi_{0}]({\bf r},t)
is the (non-adiabatic) exchange-correlation functional of the time-dependent density. This is also a functional of the initial many-body state
\Psi_{0}
and of the initial non-interacting Kohn-Sham state
\Phi_{0}
. In the case we start the evolution from the system’s ground-state,
\Psi_{0}
and
\Phi_{0}
are both functionals of the initial density and
v_{\rm xc}^{\rm NA}
is simply a functional of the complete time-dependent density. The static exchange-correlation potential used to compute the ground-state electronic density must match the non-adiabatic exchange-correlation potential at time
t=0
. However, at arbitrary times the two functionals do not necessarily match

v_{\rm xc}^{\rm NA}[n]({\bf r},t=0)=v_{\rm xc}[n]({\bf r})\ .

(14)

The generalization of this formalism to spin-polarized systems produces the time-dependent extension of collinear spin density functional theory

\displaystyle i\frac{\partial\psi_{\rm i\sigma}^{\rm KS}({\bf r},t)}{\partial t}=\!\!\Bigg[\!\!\Bigg(\!\!-\frac{1}{2}\nabla^{2}+v_{\rm ext}({\bf r},t)+\!\!v_{\rm H}[n]({\bf r},t)+

\displaystyle+\!\!\bar{v}_{\rm xc}^{\rm NA}[n,m]({\bf r},t)\Bigg)\mathbf{I}_{2}\!\!+b_{\rm s}^{\rm NA}[n,m]({\bf r},t)\mathbf{\sigma}_{\rm z}\Bigg]\!\!\psi_{\rm i\sigma}^{\rm KS}({\bf r},t).

(15)

Here,
\sigma
is the spin quantum number associated with the wave function.

2.4
The non-collinear spin time-dependent density functional theory equations

The generalization to fully non-collinear systems is obtained by replacing the exchange-correlation functionals with their non-collinear extension

\displaystyle i\frac{\partial\psi_{\rm i}^{\rm KS}({\bf r},t)}{\partial t}=\!\!\Bigg[\!\!\Bigg(-\frac{1}{2}\nabla^{2}+v_{\rm ext}({\bf r},t)+

\displaystyle+v_{\rm H}[n]({\bf r},t)+\bar{v}_{\rm xc}^{\rm NA+NC}[n,{\bf m}]({\bf r},t)\!\!\Bigg)\mathbf{I}_{2}+

\displaystyle+{\bf b}_{\rm s}^{\rm NA+NC}[n,{\bf m}]({\bf r},t)\cdot\mathbf{\bm{\sigma}}\Bigg]\psi_{\rm i}^{\rm KS}({\bf r},t)\ .

(16)

The effective magnetic field for the KS system is given by

\displaystyle{\bf b}_{\rm s}^{\rm NA+NC}

\displaystyle[n,{\bf m}]({\bf r},t)={\bf b}_{\rm xc}^{\rm NA+NC}[n,{\bf m}]({\bf r},t)+

\displaystyle+\mu_{\rm B}{\bf b}_{\rm ext}({\bf r},t).

(17)

Under such time-dependent equations, the spin density satisfies the continuity equation,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}{\bf m}({\bf r},t)

\displaystyle=-\nabla\cdot\bm{\mathcal{J}}_{\rm s}({\bf r},t)+

\displaystyle+2{\bf b}_{\rm s}^{\rm NA+NC}[n,{\bf m}]({\bf r},t)\times{\bf m}({\bf r},t),

(18)

where
\bm{\mathcal{J}}_{\rm s}({\bf r},t)
is the spin current of the Kohn-Sham system and the second term is a precessional contribution due to the coupling with the effective magnetic field. If we integrate over the entire volume of the system,
\Omega
, the first term (
\nabla\cdot\bm{\mathcal{J}}_{\rm s}({\bf r},t)
) produces a surface term that is equal to zero, given that there is no loss of charge,

\frac{d{\bf M}_{\Omega}}{dt}=2\int_{\Omega}d{\bf r}\,{\bf b}_{\rm s}^{\rm NA+NC}[n,{\bf m}]({\bf r},t)\times{\bf m}({\bf r},t)\ .

(19)

Eq. (
19
) gives the change in the total magnetization of the system over time. The contribution arising from the exchange-correlation magnetic field,
\int_{\Omega}d{\bf r}\,{\bf b}_{\rm xc}^{\rm NA+NC}[n,{\bf m}]({\bf r},t)\times{\bf m}({\bf r},t)=0
, due to the zero torque theorem
Capelle et al. 2001
;
Hill et al. 2023
. In the presence of a homogeneous external magnetic field, the equation simply reduces to

\frac{d{\bf M}_{\Omega}}{dt}=2\mu_{\rm B}{\bf b}_{\rm ext}(t)\times{\bf M}_{\Omega}(t)\ .

(20)

Eq. (
20
) describes a simple precession rotation around the axis of the externally applied magnetic field.

2.5
Approximations of the exchange correlation potential

Eq. (
2.4
) provides a formally exact spin
{\bf m}({\bf r},t)
and electronic density
n({\bf r},t)
’s evolution in external time-dependent fields. The key quantities are the two exchange correlation potentials
\bar{v}_{\rm xc}^{\rm NA+NC}[n,{\bf m}]
and
{\bf b}_{\rm xc}^{\rm NA+NC}[n,{\bf m}]
. These functionals are unknown in their exact form, and usually, two major approximations are used. The (i) adiabatic approximation; the (ii) locally collinear approximation. We briefly discuss these two approximations in the next subsections. Improvements beyond the adiabatic approximation have been discussed
Gross and Kohn 1985
;
Iwamoto and Gross 1987
;
Constantin and Pitarke 2007
;
Richardson and Ashcroft 1994
;
Corradini et al. 1998
;
Dabrowski 1986
;
Kaplan et al. 2022
;
Panholzer et al. 2018
;
Dobson et al. 1997
;
Vignale et al. 1997
;
Qian and Vignale 2002
, but we leave this for future implementations.

The adiabatic approximation

The adiabatic approximation in time-dependent density functional theory assumes that the functional dependence of the exchange-correlation potential at time
t
can be approximated only by the density at time
t
, which means that there is no memory and no dependence on the full electron density history. The nonadiabatic functional is then replaced by the functional of the ground-state density functional theory and used inside the time-dependent KS equations (
2.3
), (
2.3
) and (
2.4
) with instantaneous density and magnetization. In the non-collinear case, we can write

\displaystyle\bar{v}_{\rm xc}^{\rm NA+NC}[n,{\bf m}]({\bf r},t)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle({\rm A})}}{{\simeq}}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle({\rm A})}}{{\simeq}}\bar{v}_{\rm xc}^{\rm GS+NC}[n_{0},{\bf m}_{0}]({\bf r})|_{\underset{{\bf m}_{0}\to{\bf m}({\bf r},t)}{n_{0}\to n({\bf r},t)}}

(21)

\displaystyle{\bf b}_{\rm xc}^{\rm NA+NC}[n,{\bf m}]({\bf r},t)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle({\rm A})}}{{\simeq}}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle({\rm A})}}{{\simeq}}{\bf b}^{\rm GS+NC}_{\rm xc}[n_{0},{\bf m}_{0}]({\bf r})|_{\underset{{\bf m}_{0}\to{\bf m}({\bf r},t)}{n_{0}\to n({\bf r},t)}}\ .

(22)

Here,
n_{0}
and
{\bf m}_{0}
are the densities and magnetization of the electronic ground state. The adiabatic approximation is fully justified only when the external perturbation is slow enough to keep the physical system in its instantaneous eigenstate during evolution and neglect the memory dependence in the exchange-correlation functional. In linear response, this is equivalent to neglecting the frequency dependence in the exchange-correlation kernel
Lacombe and Maitra 2023
. This approximation is widely employed in time-dependent density functional theory in the form of the so-called adiabatic local (spin) density approximation (AL(S)DA)
Wilken and Bauer 2006
.

\bar{v}_{\rm xc}^{\rm ALDA+NC}[n,{\bf m}]({\bf r},t)=\frac{dE_{\rm xc}^{\rm HEG}[\bar{n},\bar{\bf m}]}{d\bar{n}}\Bigg|_{\underset{\bar{\bf m}\to{\bf m}({\bf r},t)}{\bar{n}\to n({\bf r},t)}},

(23)

where
E_{\rm xc}^{\rm HEG}[\bar{n},\bar{\bf m}]
is the exchange-correlation energy functional of the homogeneous electron gas. The value of the density
\bar{n}
at each point of the spatial grid is given by the density field
n({\bf r},t)
. The use of GGA functionals
Perdew et al. 1996
would relax the locality in space condition by adding an additional dependence on the local gradient of the densities
\nabla\bar{n}
and
\nabla\bar{\bf m}
, but these will still be computed locally in space.

The exchange-correlation magnetic field, within the AL(S)DA, is written as follows,

{\bf b}_{\rm xc}^{\rm ALDA+NC}[n,{\bf m}]({\bf r},t)=\frac{dE_{\rm xc}^{\rm HEG}[\bar{n},\bar{\bf m}]}{d\bar{\bf m}}\bigg|_{\underset{\bar{\bf m}\to{\bf m}({\bf r},t)}{\bar{n}\to n({\bf r},t)}}.

(24)

Limitations of such an approach have been discussed in the literature
Tozer and Handy 2000
;
Raghunathan and Nest 2011
and will not be analyzed further here.

The locally collinear approximation

The functionals in Eq. (
23
) and (
24
) are functionals of both the electron density and the magnetization density vector
Hill et al. 2023
of the KS system.
The locally collinear approximation employs widely used collinear functionals that depend only on the local spin-up
(n_{0})
and spin-down
(n_{1})
densities to approximate the full non-collinear functionals
Kubler et al. 1988
;
Sandratskii 1998
.

In this approximation, the exchange-correlation energy functional of the non-collinear system is written as
E_{\rm xc}[n,{\bf m}]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle({\rm LC})}}{{\simeq}}E_{\rm xc}^{\rm SDFT}[n,|{\bf m}|]
,
where
E_{\rm xc}^{\rm SDFT}
is the exchange-correlation energy functional of the collinear spin density functional theory. The exchange-correlation potentials in the locally-collinear approximation then become

\displaystyle{\bf b}_{\rm xc}^{\rm ALDA+LC}[n,{\bf m}]({\bf r},t)=

\displaystyle=b_{\rm xc}^{\rm ALDA}[n,|{\bf m}|]({\bf r},t)\cdot\frac{{\bf m}({\bf r},t)}{|{\bf m}({\bf r},t)|}

(25)

\displaystyle\bar{v}_{\rm xc}^{\rm ALDA+LC}[n,{\bf m}]({\bf r},t)=\bar{v}_{\rm xc}^{\rm ALDA}[n,|{\bf m}|]({\bf r},t)\ .

(26)

Eq. (
25
) is ill-defined at
{\bf m}({\bf r},t)=0
; in this case, the exchange-correlation potential is also set to zero. Beyond this issue, the locally collinear functional has additional limitations
Pu et al. 2023
: (i) non-local functionals have an incorrect collinear limit; (ii) the locally collinear functionals are invariant under global rotation and satisfy the zero-torque theorem, but are insensitive to the local rotation of the magnetization vector; (iii) the locally collinear functionals have no well-defined functional derivatives.

Improvements on this approximation have been proposed
Sharma et al. 2007
, existing local and non-local functionals have been modified to account for spin non-collinearity
Katsnelson and Antropov 2003
;
Scalmani and Frisch 2012
, new gradient-corrected functionals have been proposed based on the spin spiral state of the electron gas
Eich and Gross 2013
;
Eich et al. 2013
, meta-GGA exchange-correlation functionals have been proposed
Pittalis et al. 2017
;
Tancogne-Dejean et al. 2023
. We will not consider these functionals here; we will use locally collinear functionals in the simulations and focus on non-local functionals in a future study.

Under the locally collinear approximation and due to the zero local torque of the exchange-correlation field, the spin dynamics equation (
2.4
) becomes

\frac{d{\bf m}({\bf r},t)}{dt}=-\nabla\cdot\bm{\mathcal{J}}_{\rm s}({\bf r},t)+2\mu_{\rm B}{\bf b}_{\rm ext}({\bf r},t)\times{\bf m}({\bf r},t)\ ,

(27)

which corresponds to the effective equation for the dynamics of the local magnetization vector.

2.6
Spin-orbit interaction and relativistic corrections

The Hamiltonian of non-collinear spin density functional theory written in Eq. (
2.2
) is non-relativistic. Relativistic corrections are necessary for the description of the magnetic anisotropy and the interplay between spin and orbital degrees of freedom.
A proper discussion of the origin of the spin-orbit interaction, starting from fundamental quantum mechanical principles, would require us to start from the full Dirac equation
Dyall and Fægri 2007
and go beyond the scope of this work. Recent works implement full relativistic time-dependent density functional theory
Liu and Xiao 2018
and apply four-current time-dependent density functional theory to the study of chiral induced spin selectivity
Zheng et al. 2025
.

Here we consider the semi-relativistic Hamiltonian obtained from the expansion of the Dirac equation to the order
O(1/(mc)^{2})
for a single electron in the presence of Kohn-Sham potential
v_{\rm KS}[n]({\bf r})
. The relativistic electron equation is written as

\displaystyle i\partial_{t}\Psi^{\rm KS}

\displaystyle=\big[-ic\alpha^{k}\partial_{k}+\beta mc^{2}+v_{\rm KS}[n]({\bf r},t)\big]\Psi^{\rm KS},

(28)

\Psi^{\rm KS}
is a four-dimensional spinor,
\alpha^{k}
and
\beta
are the Dirac matrices.
\Psi^{\rm KS}
can be separated into a small and a large component
\Psi^{\rm KS}_{\rm S}
and
\Psi^{\rm KS}_{\rm L}
. For applications in solid state and molecular physics, the small component is neglected and the equation for the large component is written as
Fröhlich and Studer 1993

\displaystyle\Big\{

\displaystyle\frac{{\bf p}^{2}}{2}+v_{\rm KS}[n]({\bf r})-\frac{{\bf p}^{4}}{8c^{2}}+\frac{\nabla^{2}v_{\rm KS}[n]({\bf r})}{8c^{2}}+

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4c^{2}}\big(\nabla v_{\rm KS}[n]({\bf r})\times{\bf p}\big)\cdot\bm{\sigma}\Big\}\tilde{\Psi}^{\rm KS}_{\rm L}=E_{\rm(SR)}^{\rm KS}\tilde{\Psi}^{\rm KS}_{\rm L}.

(29)

The third term in the semi-relativistic Hamiltonian is a relativistic kinetic correction, the fourth term is the Darwin interaction, which is a relativistic correction that becomes more important close to the nuclei, and the last term is the spin-orbit interaction. The spin-orbit interaction is dependent on the effective electrostatic potential experienced by the electrons. In the KS system, this corresponds to the effective Kohn-Sham scalar potential acting on each electron. The spin-orbit interaction can be rearranged as follows,

\displaystyle V_{\rm SO}=\frac{1}{4c^{2}}\big(\nabla v_{\rm KS}[n]({\bf r})\times{\bf p}\big)\cdot\bm{\sigma}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4c^{2}}\big(\nabla v_{\rm ext}({\bf r})\times{\bf p}\big)\cdot\bm{\sigma}+

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4c^{2}}\big(\nabla v_{\rm Hxc}[n]({\bf r})\times{\bf p}\big)\cdot\bm{\sigma}\ .

(30)

The first contribution is due to the bare ionic external potential, and the second is the mean-field part due to the electrostatic cloud formed by the other electrons in the system. Here
n=n_{\rm C}+n_{\rm V}
is the total system’s electron density given by the sum of the core and valence densities.
We could separate the effect of the core from the valence electrons in Eq. (
2.6
).

\displaystyle V_{\rm SO}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4c^{2}}\big(\nabla\tilde{v}[n_{\rm C}]({\bf r})\times{\bf p}\big)\cdot\bm{\sigma}+

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4c^{2}}\big(\nabla\delta v_{\rm Hxc}[n_{\rm C},n_{\rm V}]({\bf r})\times{\bf p}\big)\cdot\bm{\sigma},

(31)

where we have introduced the following effective potentials,

\displaystyle\tilde{v}[n_{\rm C}]({\bf r})

\displaystyle=v_{\rm ext}({\bf r})+v_{\rm Hxc}[n_{\rm C},0]({\bf r})\ ,

(32)

\displaystyle\delta v_{\rm Hxc}[n_{\rm C},n_{\rm V}]({\bf r})=

\displaystyle=v_{\rm Hxc}[n_{\rm C},n_{\rm V}]({\bf r})-v_{\rm Hxc}[n_{\rm C},0]({\bf r}).

(33)

The first term in
V_{\rm SO}
is the most important one and corresponds to the spin-orbit coupling due to the combined effect of the bare ionic potential and the electronic charge of the core electrons. Due to the localization of the core electron charge, it can be written as a linear combination of effective potentials centered around the nuclei

\tilde{v}[n_{\rm C}]({\bf r})=\sum_{\rm a=1}^{N_{a}}\tilde{v}^{\rm a}[n_{\rm C}^{\rm a}]({\bf r}-{\bf R}_{\rm a})

(34)

\delta v_{\rm Hxc}({\bf r})
is the electrostatic potential due to the remaining valence charge and is usually neglected in calculations.

The effective potential,
\tilde{v}[n_{\rm C}]({\bf r})
, is often approximated in the KS equations by using a pseudo-potential,
v_{\rm ps}({\bf r})
, centered on every atom. We discuss this in more detail in the implementation section. In the case of a single atom
v_{\rm ext}({\bf r})=-\frac{Z}{r}
and if we neglect the effect of other electrons, we recover the ordinary form of the spin-orbit interaction

\displaystyle V_{\rm SO}

\displaystyle=\frac{Z}{4c^{2}r^{3}}\big({\bf r}\times{\bf p}\big)\cdot\bm{\sigma}=\lambda{\bf L}\cdot\bm{\sigma}\ ,

(35)

with
\lambda=\frac{Z}{4c^{2}r^{3}}
.

The spin continuity equation (
27
) should be rewritten as

\displaystyle\frac{d{\bf m}({\bf r},t)}{dt}=-\nabla\cdot\bm{\mathcal{J}}_{\rm s}({\bf r},t)+

\displaystyle+2\mu_{\rm B}{\bf b}_{\rm ext}({\bf r},t)\times{\bf m}({\bf r},t)+\bm{\Gamma}_{\rm SOC}({\bf r},t)\ ,

(36)

where
\bm{\Gamma}_{\rm SOC}({\bf r},t)
defines an additional spin-orbit torque term.

3
Numerical Implementation

In this section, we discuss the details of the implementation of the spin non-collinear formalism in
INQ
.

3.1
Spinorial representation

The single-particle wavefunctions of the non-collinear spin Kohn-Sham Hamiltonian (
2.2
) are in spinorial form. Spinors are two-dimensional vectors that transform according to the
SU(2)
representation of the three-dimensional rotation group.

\psi_{\rm i}^{\rm KS}({\bf r})\to e^{i\frac{\phi}{2}(\hat{\bf n}\cdot\hat{\bm{\sigma}})}\psi_{\rm i}^{\rm KS}({\bf r})

(37)

In practice, this means we need to include an additional index in the representations of the orbitals that also have indices for the states and basis coefficients.

We have found that to obtain the most efficient memory layout, it is better to have the spinor index in the middle of the array, in between the basis coefficient and orbital indices.
In this way, there are three possible views of the wave-function array.
The first one is to have the spinor index explicitly, for operations that involve spinor components like application to the potential or the calculation of the spin density.
The second view is a 2-dimensional array obtained by flattening the spinor and orbital indices to effectively duplicate the number of states. This view is useful for operations that act individually on each state and do not involve spinor information, like the calculation of the Laplacian.
Finally, we can obtain a different 2-dimensional view of the array by compacting the basis coefficient and spinor indices. In this case we can think that the spinor dimension duplicates the size of the basis coefficients. These means that operations like orthogonalization can be easily done using the same code as the collinear case.
All these different views are managed by the Multi
Multi
library without any copies of access overhead.

In the spinorial representation, it is convenient to introduce the Kohn-Sham spin density matrix
\rho_{\rm s}^{\rm KS}({\bf r})
.

\displaystyle\rho_{\rm s}^{\rm KS}({\bf r})=

\displaystyle\left[{\begin{array}[]{cc}\sum_{\rm i}f_{\rm i}\psi_{\rm i\uparrow}^{\rm KS}({\bf r}){\psi_{\rm i\uparrow}^{\rm KS}({\bf r})}^{*}&\sum_{\rm i}f_{\rm i}\psi_{\rm i\uparrow}^{\rm KS}({\bf r}){\psi_{\rm i\downarrow}^{\rm KS}({\bf r})}^{*}\\
\sum_{\rm i}f_{\rm i}\psi_{\rm i\downarrow}^{\rm KS}({\bf r}){\psi_{\rm i\uparrow}^{\rm KS}({\bf r})}^{*}&\sum_{\rm i}f_{\rm i}\psi_{\rm i\downarrow}^{\rm KS}({\bf r}){\psi_{\rm i\downarrow}^{\rm KS}({\bf r})}^{*}\\
\end{array}}\right]\!\!=

\displaystyle=\left[{\begin{array}[]{cc}\rho_{\rm s}^{\uparrow\uparrow}({\bf r})&\rho_{\rm s}^{\uparrow\downarrow}({\bf r})\\
\rho_{\rm s}^{\downarrow\uparrow}({\bf r})&\rho_{\rm s}^{\downarrow\downarrow}({\bf r})\\
\end{array}}\right]

From
\rho_{\rm s}^{\rm KS}({\bf r})
the magnetization density is simply given by

{\bf m}({\bf r})={\rm Tr}\big[\hat{\bm{\sigma}}\rho_{\rm s}^{\rm KS}({\bf r})\big]

(42)

and reduces to the different components;

\displaystyle m_{\rm x}({\bf r})

\displaystyle=\rho_{\rm s}^{\uparrow\downarrow}({\bf r})+\rho_{\rm s}^{\downarrow\uparrow}({\bf r})=2{\rm Re}[\rho_{\rm s}^{\uparrow\downarrow}({\bf r})]

(43)

\displaystyle m_{\rm y}({\bf r})

\displaystyle=i\big(\rho_{\rm s}^{\uparrow\downarrow}({\bf r})-\rho_{\rm s}^{\downarrow\uparrow}({\bf r})\big)=-2{\rm Im}[\rho_{\rm s}^{\uparrow\downarrow}({\bf r})]

(44)

\displaystyle m_{\rm z}({\bf r})

\displaystyle=\rho_{\rm s}^{\uparrow\uparrow}({\bf r})-\rho_{\rm s}^{\downarrow\downarrow}({\bf r})

(45)

In the code, we store the spin-density matrix
\rho_{\rm v}({\bf r})
as a four-dimensional vector of real values

\rho_{\rm v}({\bf r})=\big[\rho_{\rm s}^{\uparrow\uparrow}({\bf r}),\rho_{\rm s}^{\downarrow\downarrow}({\bf r}),{\rm Re}[\rho_{\rm s}^{\uparrow\downarrow}({\bf r})],{\rm Im}[\rho_{\rm s}^{\uparrow\downarrow}({\bf r})]\big]\ .

(46)

This is more efficient than storing the full complex matrix that has redundant values due to its hermiticity.
The conversion between
\rho_{\rm v}({\bf r})
and the magnetization density
{\bf m}({\bf r})
is straightforward through Eqs. (
43
), (
44
) and (
45
).

3.3
The exchange correlation potential in spinorial representation

Within the adiabatic and locally collinear approximation in Eq. (
25
) and Eq. (
26
), the exchange-correlation potential can be expressed as

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{\rm xc}[n,{\bf m}]({\bf r},t)

\displaystyle=\bar{v}_{\rm xc}^{\rm LC+ALDA}[n,{\bf m}]({\bf r},t){\bf I}_{2}+

\displaystyle+{\bf b}_{\rm xc}^{\rm LC+ALDA}[n,{\bf m}]({\bf r},t)\cdot\bm{\sigma}

(48)

By omitting the labels, this is equivalent in spinorial representation to

\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{\rm xc}=\left[{\begin{array}[]{cc}\bar{v}_{\rm xc}+b_{\rm xc}^{\rm z}&b_{\rm xc}^{-}\\
b_{\rm xc}^{+}&\bar{v}_{\rm xc}-b_{\rm xc}^{\rm z}\\
\end{array}}\right]

where we have used
b_{\rm xc}^{\pm}=b_{\rm xc}^{\rm x}\pm ib_{\rm xc}^{\rm y}
. This leads to the following expression for the exchange-correlation energy (at a given time).

\displaystyle E_{\rm xc}=\int_{\Omega}d{\bf r}\,{\rm Tr}\big[\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{\rm xc}({\bf r})\hat{\rho}_{\rm s}^{\rm KS}({\bf r})\big]

\displaystyle=\!\!\int_{\Omega}\!\!d{\bf r}\big[\rho_{\rm s}^{\uparrow\uparrow}({\bf r})\big(\bar{v}_{\rm xc}({\bf r})+b_{\rm xc}^{\rm z}({\bf r})\big)+

\displaystyle+\rho_{\rm s}^{\downarrow\downarrow}({\bf r})\big(\bar{v}_{\rm xc}({\bf r})-b_{\rm xc}^{\rm z}({\bf r})\big)+2{\rm Re}[\rho_{\rm s}^{\uparrow\downarrow}({\bf r})]b_{\rm xc}^{\rm x}({\bf r})\!-

\displaystyle-2{\rm Im}[\rho_{\rm s}^{\uparrow\downarrow}({\bf r})]b_{\rm xc}^{\rm y}({\bf r})\big]

\displaystyle=<\rho_{\rm v},\mathcal{V}_{\rm v}^{\rm xc}>

(49)

\mathcal{V}_{\rm v}^{\rm xc}({\bf r})
is the four-dimensional vectorial representation of the exchange-correlation field

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{\rm v}^{\rm xc}({\bf r})=\big[\bar{v}_{\rm xc}^{+}({\bf r}),\bar{v}_{\rm xc}^{-}({\bf r}),2b_{\rm xc}^{\rm x}({\bf r}),-2b_{\rm xc}^{\rm y}({\bf r})\big]

\displaystyle\bar{v}_{\rm xc}^{+}({\bf r})=\bar{v}_{\rm xc}({\bf r})+b_{\rm xc}^{\rm z}({\bf r})

\displaystyle\bar{v}_{\rm xc}^{-}({\bf r})=\bar{v}_{\rm xc}({\bf r})-b_{\rm xc}^{\rm z}({\bf r})\ ,

(50)

and
<{\bf v},{\bf u}>
is the Cartesian scalar product between the two vectors
{\bf v}
and
{\bf u}
.

3.4
Zeeman coupling and external magnetic field

Analogously to the exchange-correlation magnetic field, the Zeeman coupling to an external homogeneous magnetic field,
{\bf b}_{\rm ext}=\big[b_{\rm ext}^{x},b_{\rm ext}^{y},b_{\rm ext}^{z}\big]
, is also defined in the spinorial representation using

\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{\rm z}=\mu_{\rm B}\bm{\sigma}\cdot{\bf b}_{\rm ext}=\mu_{\rm B}\left[{\begin{array}[]{cc}b_{\rm ext}^{z}&b_{\rm ext}^{-}\\
b_{\rm ext}^{+}&-b_{\rm ext}^{z}\\
\end{array}}\right]

where
b_{\rm ext}^{-}=b_{\rm ext}^{x}-ib_{\rm ext}^{y}
and
b_{\rm ext}^{+}=b_{\rm ext}^{x}+ib_{\rm ext}^{y}
. In the collinear case, the two off-diagonal terms disappear, and the expression reduces to a fully diagonal form.

The Zeeman energy is computed as the exchange-correlation energy using
<\rho_{\rm v},\mathcal{V}_{\rm v}^{\rm z}>
. We only implemented the spin Zeeman term here; we will leave the orbital Zeeman contribution or orbital response to an external magnetic field
Lebedeva et al. 2019
for a future study.

3.5
Spin-orbit coupling implementation

Here we discuss the implementation of the spin-orbit interaction based on the pseudopotential method. Other implementations are available in the literature based on the first-order perturbation theory variation of scalar relativistic wave functions
Surh et al. 1991
;
Hemstreet et al. 1993
, but will not be considered here. A fully relativistic treatment of the problem would require a four-component formalism based on Dirac spinors
Sakurai 1987
, as discussed in the theory section. We have also seen that all relativistic effects up to order
\alpha^{2}
(where
\alpha
is the fine structure constant) can be captured from a Scrödinger-like equation with an additional spin-orbit term
Kleinman 1980
;
Theurich and Hill 2001
. In this form, the total ionic pseudopotential can be written as

\hat{v}_{\rm ps}=\sum_{\rm a=1}^{N_{\rm a}}\Bigg\{\hat{V}_{\rm L}^{\rm a}+\sum_{l,j,m_{j}}\frac{\ket*{\delta V_{lj}^{\rm a}\phi_{lj;m_j}^{\rm a}}\bra*{\phi_{lj,m_j}^{\rm a}\delta V_{lj}^{\rm a}}}{\matrixelement*{\phi_{lj,m_j}^{\rm a}}{\delta V_{lj}^{\rm a}}{\phi_{lj,m_j}^{\rm a}}}\Bigg\}

(51)

The contribution
\hat{V}_{\rm L}^{\rm a}
is local in space and long-range. The second term in the sum over the atoms is the short-range non-local atomic contribution. The set of wave functions
\{\phi_{lj;m_{j}}^{\rm a}\}
corresponds to the solution of the KS problem for the isolated atom (indicated with the label a). These are expressed as the product of a radial component and of the spinorial spherical harmonics.

\displaystyle\ket*{\phi_{lj;m_j}^{\rm a}}

\displaystyle=\ket*{R_{lj}^{\rm a}}\otimes\ket*{\Phi_{lj}^{m_j}}

\displaystyle\innerproduct*{{\bf r}}{\phi_{lj;m_j}^{\rm a}}

\displaystyle=R_{lj}^{\rm a}(|{\bf r}-{\bf R}_{\rm a}|)\Phi_{lj}^{m_{j}}(\hat{\bf\Omega})

(52)

The first factor of the atomic pseudo wave function is the radial component,
R_{lj}^{\rm a}
, and the second factor is the spinorial spherical harmonics

\displaystyle\Phi_{l,j=l\pm\frac{1}{2}}^{m_{j}=m\pm\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle(\hat{\bf\Omega})=\sqrt{\frac{l\pm m+1}{2l+1}}\Upsilon^{m_{j}-\frac{1}{2}}_{l}(\hat{\bf\Omega})\chi_{\uparrow}\pm

\displaystyle\pm\sqrt{\frac{l\mp m}{2l+1}}\Upsilon_{l}^{m_{j}+\frac{1}{2}}(\hat{\bf\Omega})\chi_{\downarrow}\ .

(53)

The potential
\delta V_{lj}({\bf r})^{\rm a}=V_{lj}^{\rm a}({\bf r}-{\bf R}_{\rm a})-V_{\rm L}^{\rm a}({\bf r}-{\bf R}_{\rm a})
is the difference between the pseudo-potential channel
(l,j)
and the local long-range pseudo-potential
V_{\rm L}^{\rm a}
. In the next section, we discuss simulations of different systems using this spin non-collinear implementation for both ground state DFT and real-time TDDFT.

4.7
Real-time dynamics under applied homogeneous electric fields

The dynamics of magnetic systems under applied external electric pulses is qualitatively different and richer compared to the dynamics under magnetic pulses alone. The applied laser pulse influences the magnetization dynamics via the spin-orbit interaction. The spin-orbit, in fact, provides a possible conversion mechanism between orbital and spin degrees of freedom. At the same time, the electric pulse excites the orbital degrees of freedom of the magnetic system and causes a charge imbalance that can induce a modification of the spin degrees of freedom. These effects have been observed experimentally
Beaurepaire et al. 1996
;
Koopmans et al. 2000
;
Kirilyuk et al. 2010
and numerically simulated using time-dependent density functional theory in the case of ferromagnetic metals and magnetic clusters
Stamenova et al. 2016
;
Simoni et al. 2017
;
Simoni et al. 2017
;
Krieger et al. 2017
;
Pellegrini et al. 2022
;
Krieger et al. 2015
. Ultrafast spin dynamics in semiconductors have been studied with first-principles density-matrix dynamics including applied pump pulses, electron-phonon, electron-electron interactions with spin-orbit couplings
Xu et al. 2021
;
Xu and Ping 2024
;
Li et al. 2024
;
Xu et al. 2024
.

\displaystyle\hat{v}_{\rm ps}=\sum_{\rm a=1}^{N_{a}}\Big\{V_{\rm L}^{\rm a}(r)+

\displaystyle+\sum_{lm}\ket{lm;a}\big[\bar{V}_{l}^{\rm a}(r)+V_{l}^{a;\rm SO}(r)\hat{\bf L}\cdot\hat{\bf S}\big]\bra{lm;a}\Big\}\,.

(56)

The spin operator is no longer a constant of motion in the presence of these fully relativistic pseudopotentials. The time derivative of the magnetization over the volume
\Omega

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}M^{\rm i}_{\Omega}=\int_{\Omega}\,d{\bf r}\bm{\Gamma}_{\rm SOC}^{\rm i}({\bf r},t)=

\displaystyle=\sum_{n{\bf k}}f_{n{\bf k}}\sum_{ju}\epsilon_{iju}\sum_{\rm a=1}^{N_{a}}\sum_{mm^{\prime}l}\int_{\Omega}\,d{\bf r}\,\times

\displaystyle\times\matrixelement*{n{\bf k}}{V_l^{a;\rm SO}({\bf r}-{\bf R}_a)}{lm;a}\,S_{n{\bf k}}^{u}({\bf r},t)\times

\displaystyle\times\matrixelement*{lm;a}{L_j}{lm';a}\innerproduct*{lm';a}{n{\bf k}}\,,

(57)

where, in the absence of an external magnetic field, we removed the other contributions in the equation. We notice that Eq. (

The summation is truncated in the calculation, neglecting the projection of the wave function on higher-order harmonics. This is ultimately a problem related to the pseudopotential approximation and to the fact that the orbital momentum operator is described on the basis of the atomic projected harmonics, which does not form a complete basis set.

5
Conclusions

We have implemented non-collinear density functional theory and time-dependent density functional theory in
INQ
. We have tested the effect of the exchange-correlation magnetic field in the locally collinear approximation, the Zeeman coupling with external magnetic fields, and the spin-orbit interaction in different systems. We compared the results with other codes available, finding good agreement.
We have tested the dynamics of different magnetic systems under applied external fields, both electric and magnetic. In the presence of external magnetic fields, we recover the expected spin-precession dynamics. The dynamics under applied laser pulses are richer and predict optically driven demagnetization effects consistent with previous studies
Stamenova et al. 2016
;
Krieger et al. 2017
;
Pellegrini et al. 2022
;
Krieger et al. 2015
.

This implementation sets up the stage for future improvements like the calculation of orbital momentum dynamics, Ehrenfest dynamics for magnetic systems, and the use of functionals for better description of dynamical magnetic properties beyond the adiabatic and the locally-collinear approximation.

Askey-Wilson version of Second Main Theorem for holomorphic curves in projective space
Chengliang Tan, Risto Korhonen

Abstract
In this paper, an Askey-Wilson version of the Wronskian-Casorati determinant 𝒲 ⁡ ( f 0 , … , f n ) ​ ( x ) \mathcal{W}(f_{0},\dots,f_{n})(x) for meromorphic functions f 0 , … , f n f_{0},\dots,f_{n} is introduced to establish an Askey-Wilson version of the general form of the Second Main Theorem in projective space. This improves upon the original Second Main Theorem for the Askey-Wilson operator due to Chiang and Feng. In addition, by taking into account the number of irreducible components of hypersurfaces, an Askey-Wilson version of the Truncated Second Main Theorem for holomorphic curves into projective space with hypersurfaces located in l l -subgeneral position is obtained.

In 1925, Nevanlinna established value distribution theory and presented a Second Main Theorem (SMT) for non-constant meromorphic functions
Nevanlinna (1925)
. Subsequently, Cartan extended the SMT to holomorphic curves
f:\mathbb{C}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{C})

Cartan (1933)
. Cartan’s theorem can yield better results for certain kinds of problems compared with the SMT
Gundersen and Hayman (2004)
. In 1997, motivated by Schmidt’s Subspace Theorem in number theory, Vojta slightly generalized Cartan’s theorem and provided a general form of the SMT
Vojta (1997)
. In the same year, by utilizing Ye’s techniques
Ye (1995)
, Ru established a general form of the SMT with an improved error term, enhancing Vojta’s results
Ru (1997)
. Leveraging the general form of the SMT, Ru derived a defect relation for holomorphic curves intersecting hypersurfaces, proving the Shiffman conjecture, which was proposed by B. Shiffman in 1979
Shiffman (1979)
. Shortly afterward, Ru also extended the defect relation to algebraic varieties (not limited to projective space)
Ru (2009)
. In 2019, Quang refined Ru’s results
Ru (2009)
to apply to hypersurfaces in subgeneral position
Quang (2019)
. Recently, by taking into account the number of irreducible components of hypersurfaces, a new SMT for holomorphic maps into projective space with hypersurfaces was introduced by Shi and Yan
Shi and Yan (2022)
.

Halburd and the second author were the first to formulate Nevanlinna theory for the difference operator
Halburd and Korhonen (2006a)
;
Halburd and Korhonen (2006b)
, which was successfully applied to detect solutions of various difference equations
Halburd and Korhonen (2007a)
;
Halburd and Korhonen (2007b)
. Subsequently, Halburd, the second author, and Tohge
Halburd et al. (2014)
, as well as Pit-Mann Wong, Hui-Fai Law, and Philip P. W. Wong
Wong et al. (2009)
, independently extended difference Nevanlinna theory to projective space. Later, a difference analogue of the SMT for meromorphic mappings into algebraic varieties was provided by P.-C. Hu and N. V. Thin
Hu and Thin (2021)
. Building on the strengths of the difference analogue of Nevanlinna theory, Cheng and Chiang, as well as Chiang and Feng separately developed Nevanlinna theory for the Wilson operator
Cheng and Chiang (2017)
and the Askey-Wilson divided difference operator
Chiang and Feng (2018)
. This development led to the definition of an Askey-Wilson type Nevanlinna deficiency, offering a new interpretation that many significant infinite products arising from the study of basic hypergeometric series should be regarded as zero/pole scarce.

The purpose of this paper is to extend the Askey-Wilson analogue of the SMT for holomorphic curves into projective space with hypersurfaces. In the following section, we begin with some foundational concepts about the Askey-Wilson operator and Nevanlinna characteristics. In the third section, we further generalize the Logarithmic Derivative Lemma for Askey-Wilson operator and the Chiang and Feng’s estimation to Logarithmic Derivative. Subsequently, we introduce the Askey-Wilson version of the Wronskian-Casorati determinant
\mathcal{W}(f_{0},\dots,f_{n})(x)
for meromorphic functions
f_{0},\dots,f_{n}
. It is worth noting that it has alternative definitions when
|x|
is sufficiently large, which will help us address various situations. In Section
5
, we present a general form of the Askey-Wilson version (Truncated) SMT into projective space for hyperplanes. Finally, we establish the Askey-Wilson version of the Truncated SMT for hypersurfaces by improving a key lemma due to Shi and Yan. The improved lemma can also be applied to enhance the classical SMT and Schmidt’s Subspace Type Theorem.

Logarithmic hyper-order

Suppose
f
is a meromorphic function. The
logarithmic hyper-order
of
f
is denoted by

\displaystyle\sigma_{2}^{\log}(f):=\limsup\limits_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log^{+}\log^{+}T(r,f)}{\log\log r},

where
\log^{+}A=\max\{0,\log A\}
for
A\geq 0
. Asymptotic relations for a positive, non-decreasing continuous function are frequently used in the construction and application of Nevanlinna theory. For instance, Halburd, the second author, and Tohge considered the asymptotic relation of the real function
T(r)
with hyper-order strictly less than
1

Halburd et al. (2014)
. Zheng and the second author further studied the asymptotic relation of the real function
T(r)
, which is convex with respect to
\log r
and satisfies
\limsup\limits_{r\rightarrow+\infty}T(r)/\log r=0

Zheng and Korhonen (2020)
. Some asymptotic relations for functions of zero order can also be found in
Chern (2006)
. Now, we consider the case where the logarithmic hyper-order is strictly less than
1
, with the basic idea originating from
Halburd et al. (2014)
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
T(r):[e,+\infty)\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
be a non-decreasing continuous function. If the logarithmic hyper-order of
T
is strictly less than one, i.e.,

\displaystyle\limsup\limits_{r\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\log^{+}\log^{+}T(r)}{\log\log r}:=\sigma_{2}^{\log}<1,

(4)

and
\delta\in(0,1-\sigma_{2}^{\log})
, then

\displaystyle T(r\log r)=T(r)+o\left(\frac{T(r)}{(\log r)^{\delta}}\right),

(5)

where
r
runs to infinity outside of a set
F
satisfying

\displaystyle\int_{F\cap[e,+\infty)}\frac{dt}{t\log t}<+\infty.

(6)

Proof.

Let
\tilde{\delta}\in(\delta,1-\sigma_{2}^{\log})
,
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
and define

\displaystyle F_{\alpha}:=\left\{r\in[e,+\infty):\frac{T(r\log r)-T(r)}{T(r)}\cdot(\log r)^{\tilde{\delta}}\geq\alpha\right\}.

Assume
\int_{F_{\alpha}}1/(t\log t)dt=+\infty
, now we define a sequence
\{r_{n}\}
using induction as follows. Since
F_{\alpha}
is closed, we can set
r_{0}=\min\{F_{\alpha}\}
and define
r_{n}=\min\{F_{\alpha}\cap[r_{n-1}\log r_{n-1},+\infty)\}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Thus,
r_{n+1}\geq r_{n}\log r_{n}
and
F\subseteq\cup_{n=0}^{\infty}[r_{n},r_{n}\log r_{n}]
, which leads to
T(r_{n+1})\geq T(r_{n}\log r_{n})\geq\left(1+\frac{\alpha}{(\log r_{n})^{\tilde{\delta}}}\right)T(r_{n})
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Iterating this relation recursively yields

T(r_{n})\geq\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}\left(1+\frac{\alpha}{(\log r_{k})^{\tilde{\delta}}}\right)T(r_{0}).

Assume that there exists a constant
\epsilon>0
and an integer
m>e
such that
r_{n}\geq e^{n^{1+\epsilon}}
for all
n>m
. Then we can find
m_{0}>m
such that
\log n\leq n^{\epsilon/2}
for all
n\geq m_{0}
. Since

is non-increasing when

, then we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{F_{\alpha}}\frac{dt}{t\log t}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{n=m_{0}}^{+\infty}\int_{r_{n}}^{r_{n}\log r_{n}}\frac{dt}{t\log t}+O(1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=m_{0}}^{+\infty}\log\left(1+\frac{\log\log r_{n}}{\log r_{n}}\right)+O(1)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{n=m_{0}}^{+\infty}\frac{(1+\epsilon)\log n}{n^{1+\epsilon}}+O(1)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{n=m_{0}}^{+\infty}\frac{1+\epsilon}{n^{1+\epsilon/2}}+O(1)<+\infty,

which contradicts the assumption. Thus there exists a subsequence
\{r_{n_{j}}\}_{j=1}^{+\infty}\subset\{r_{n}\}
such that
r_{n_{j}}<e^{n^{1+\epsilon}}
for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
. Then

\displaystyle\limsup\limits_{r\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\log^{+}\log^{+}T(r)}{\log\log r}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\limsup\limits_{j\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\log\left(\log T(r_{0})+\sum_{k=0}^{n_{j}-1}\log\left(1+\frac{\alpha}{(\log r_{k})^{\tilde{\delta}}}\right)\right)}{\log\log r_{n_{j}}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\limsup\limits_{j\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\log\left(n_{j}\frac{\alpha}{n_{j}^{(1+\epsilon)\tilde{\delta}}}\log\left(1+\frac{\alpha}{n_{j}^{(1+\epsilon)\tilde{\delta}}}\right)^{\frac{n_{j}^{(1+\epsilon)\tilde{\delta}}}{\alpha}}\right)}{(1+\epsilon)\log n_{j}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\limsup\limits_{j\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{(1-(1+\epsilon)\tilde{\delta})\log n_{j}}{(1+\epsilon)\log n_{j}}\geq\frac{1}{1+\epsilon}-\tilde{\delta}.

It follows that
\limsup\limits_{r\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\log^{+}\log^{+}T(r)}{\log\log r}\geq 1-\tilde{\delta}
because
\epsilon
is arbitrary, which contradicts the assumption (
4
). Hence, the proof is complete.

\displaystyle m\left(r,\frac{f[(q^{k_{1}/2}z+q^{-k_{1}/2}z^{-1})/2]}{f(x)}\right)

(14)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle O\left(\frac{m(r\log r,f)+m(r\log r,1/f)}{\log r}\right)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle O\left(n(r\log r,f)+n(r\log r,1/f)\right)+O(1)

for all sufficiently large

. Thus, combining (

), (
13
), and (

), and applying the First Main Theorem and Lemma

to

, we deduce that

\displaystyle m\left(r,\frac{(\eta_{q}^{k_{1}}f)(x)}{f(x)}\right)=O\left(\frac{T(r,f)}{\log\log r}\right),

(15)

where
r
tends to infinity outside of a set
F
satisfying (
10
).

Let

\displaystyle c(q)=\min\limits_{-n+1\leq k_{2}\leq n-1}\left\{|q^{\frac{k_{2}+1}{2}}-q^{\frac{k_{2}-1}{2}}|,|q^{-\frac{k_{2}+1}{2}}-q^{-\frac{k_{2}-1}{2}}|\right\}>0.

By letting
|x|
and hence

to be sufficiently large, for any

\displaystyle\left|\frac{1}{\eta_{q}^{k_{3}+1}x-\eta_{q}^{k_{3}-1}x}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\frac{2}{(q^{\frac{k_{3}+1}{2}}-q^{\frac{k_{3}-1}{2}})z-(q^{-\frac{k_{3}+1}{2}}-q^{-\frac{k_{3}-1}{2}})z^{-1}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2}{c(q)(|z|+|z^{-1}|)}\leq\frac{4}{c(q)|z|}\leq 1,

\displaystyle m\left(r,\frac{1}{\eta_{q}^{k_{3}+1}x-\eta_{q}^{k_{3}-1}x}\right)=0

.

Since
(\mathcal{D}_{q}^{n}f)(x)
is a combination of

and

when

is sufficiently large, then

\displaystyle m\left(r,\frac{(\mathcal{D}_{q}^{n}f)(x)}{f(x)}\right)=O\left(\sum_{-n\leq k_{1}\leq n}m\left(r,\frac{(\eta_{q}^{k_{1}}f)(x)}{f(x)}\right)\right)=O\left(\frac{T(r,f)}{\log\log r}\right),

(16)

where
r
runs to infinity outside of a set
F
satisfying (
10
).
∎

Following the definition of Chiang and Feng
Chiang and Feng (2018)
, assume

,

is a meromorphic function on
\mathbb{C}
, and

Recall that there is an

because the one-sided Askey-Wilson shift operators

Lemma 3.3
.

Let

Proof.

We only prove the case that

for sufficiently large

\displaystyle N\left(r,\frac{1}{f-a}\right)+S_{\log}(r,f)

\displaystyle N\left(r,\frac{1}{f-a}\right)

\displaystyle N\left(2|q^{-1/2}|r,\frac{1}{\eta_{q}f-a}\right)+S_{\log}(r,f)

Theorem 3.4
.

Let

\displaystyle T(r,\mathcal{A}_{q^{n}}f)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2T(r,f)+S_{\log}(r,f),

(21)

\displaystyle T(r,(\eta_{q^{n}}f)(\eta_{q^{-n}}f))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2T(r,f)+S_{\log}(r,f),

(22)

where

runs to infinity outside of a set

satisfying (

). In particular, these imply

\displaystyle\sigma_{2}^{\log}(\mathcal{D}_{q}^{n}f)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sigma_{2}^{\log}(f),

(23)

\displaystyle\sigma_{2}^{\log}(\mathcal{A}_{q^{n}}f)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sigma_{2}^{\log}(f),

(24)

\displaystyle\sigma_{2}^{\log}((\eta_{q^{n}}f)(\eta_{q^{-n}}f))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sigma_{2}^{\log}(f).

(25)

Proof.

Since

\displaystyle T(r,\mathcal{D}_{q}^{n}f)

Since we are going to use the AW operator to act on a holomorphic curve in the projective space
\mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{C})
, we first prove the following theorem to ensure that the Askey-Wilson operator is well-defined in

.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
f:\mathbb{C}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{C})
be a holomorphic curve satisfying

\displaystyle\limsup\limits_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log^{+}\log^{+}T_{f}(r)}{\log\log r}<1.

(26)

Then, there exists a reduced representation
\textbf{f}=(f_{0},\cdots,f_{n})
of
f
such that
\sigma_{2}^{\log}(f_{i})<1
for
i=0,\cdots,n
.

To prove the theorem, we need following lemma which is from the Remark 1 of
(
Goldberg and Ostrovskii, 2008
, Theorem 3.3)
.

Lemma 3.6
.

If
h(z)
is a Weierstrass canonical product of genus
p
, then

\displaystyle\log M(r,h)\leq C(p)\left\{r^{p}\int_{0}^{r}\frac{n(t,1/h)}{t^{p+1}}dt+r^{p+1}\int_{r}^{\infty}\frac{n(t,1/h)}{t^{p+2}}dt\right\}

(27)

where
M(r,h)=\max_{|z|=r}\{|h(z)|\}
. In particular,
C(0)=1
.

Proof of Theorem
3.5
.

Suppose
\textbf{g}=(g_{0},\cdots,g_{n})
is a reduced representation of
f
. If some of
g_{i}
,
i=0,\cdots,n
have finitely many zeros, without loss of generality, we assume
g_{0}
is this kind of
g_{i}
. If
g_{0}
does not have any zeros, we define an auxiliary function
h_{1}(z)=1
, otherwise we assume
\{z_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\mu}
are all
g_{0}
’s zeros,
\mu\in\mathbb{N}
, and let

\displaystyle h_{1}(z):=\prod_{k=1}^{\mu}\left(1-\frac{z}{z_{k}}\right).

Then we have
\sigma_{2}^{\log}(h_{1})<1
. Since
h_{1}(z)
and
g_{0}
have the same zeros, then
h_{1}/g_{0}
is a non-zero entire function, and

\displaystyle\textbf{f}=(f_{0},\cdots,f_{n}):=\frac{h_{1}}{g_{0}}(g_{0},\cdots,g_{n})=(h_{1},\frac{g_{1}}{g_{0}}h_{1},\cdots,\frac{g_{n}}{g_{0}}h_{1})

is also a reduced representation of
f
.
Then it follows by applying (
3
) that

\displaystyle T(r,f_{i})=T\left(r,\frac{g_{i}}{g_{0}}h_{1}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle T\left(r,\frac{g_{i}}{g_{0}}\right)+T(r,h_{1})+O(1)

(28)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle T_{f}(r)+T(r,h_{1})+O(1)

for
i=0,\cdots,n
. Thus, we can conclude that
\sigma_{2}^{\log}(f_{i})<1
for all
i=0,\cdots,n
by combining (
26
), (
28
), and the fact that
\sigma_{2}^{\log}(h_{1})<1
, which proves the assertion.

Now we assume all
g_{i}
have infinitely many zeros. We can find at least one
g_{i}
such that
z=0
is not the zero of
g_{i}
. Without loss of generality, we assume
g_{0}
is this kind of
g_{i}
and
\{z_{l}\}_{l=1}^{\infty}
are all
g_{0}
’s zeros, counting multiplicities, and
0\not\in\{z_{l}\}_{l=1}^{\infty}
. Combining (
2
) and (
26
), we have that the exponent of convergence of
g_{0}
is less than
1
, which implies

\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\left|\frac{1}{z_{l}}\right|<\infty.

Hence

\displaystyle h_{2}(z):=\prod_{l=1}^{\infty}\left(1-\frac{z}{z_{l}}\right)

converges absolutely and uniformly on each bounded disc, thus
h_{2}(z)
is a Weierstrass canonical product of genus
0
and
h_{2}(z)
has zeros precisely at the points
\{z_{l}\}_{l=1}^{\infty}
, that is,
n(r,1/h_{2})\equiv n(r,1/g_{0})
for all
r>0
. Then it follows from Lemma
3.6
that

\displaystyle T(r,h)\leq\log M(r,h)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{r}\frac{n(t,1/h)}{t}dt+r\int_{r}^{\infty}\frac{n(t,1/h)}{t^{2}}dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle N\left(r,\frac{1}{g_{0}}\right)+r\int_{r}^{\infty}\frac{n(t,1/g_{0})}{t^{2}}dt.

Now we estimate the second term. Let
R=r
in (
11
) and apply (
2
) again; we have

\displaystyle\limsup\limits_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log\log n(r,1/g_{0})}{\log\log r}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\limsup\limits_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log\log\frac{N(r\log r,1/g_{0})}{\log\log r}}{\log\log r}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\limsup\limits_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log\log N(r,1/g_{0})}{\log\log r}:=\eta<1.

Let
\kappa_{1}\in(\eta,1)
and
\kappa\in(\kappa_{1},1)
, then
\frac{\log\log n(r,1/g_{0})}{\log\log r}<\kappa_{1}
when

is sufficiently large, and thus

. Then

\displaystyle r\int_{r}^{\infty}\frac{n(t,1/g_{0})}{t^{2}}dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle r\int_{r}^{\infty}\frac{e^{(\log r)^{\kappa_{1}}}}{t^{2}}dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2r\int_{r}^{\infty}\frac{e^{(\log t)^{\kappa}}\left(1-\frac{\kappa}{(\log t)^{1-\kappa}}\right)}{t^{2}}dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2r\left(-\frac{e^{(\log t)^{\kappa}}}{t}\right)\bigg|_{t=r}^{\infty}=2e^{(\log r)^{\kappa}}

for all sufficiently large

. Therefore,

\displaystyle\limsup\limits_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log\log T(r,h)}{\log\log r}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\max\left\{\limsup\limits_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log\log N(r,1/g_{0})}{\log\log r},\limsup\limits_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log\log(2e^{(\log r)^{\kappa}})}{\log\log r}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\kappa<1.

Now the assertion follows by replacing

by
h_{2}(z)
in the above analysis.
∎

4
Askey-Wilson version of Wronskian-Casorati determinant

The Wronskian determinant plays an important role in proving the Second Main Theorem for holomorphic curves in projective space. Hence, we first introduce following
Askey-Wilson version of the Wronskian-Casorati determinant
with respect to meromorphic functions
f_{0},\cdots,f_{n}
. Let

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}(f_{0},\cdots,f_{n})(x):=\begin{vmatrix}\mathcal{A}_{q^{n}}f_{0}&\cdots&\mathcal{A}_{q^{n}}f_{n}\\
\mathcal{A}_{q^{n-1}}\mathcal{D}_{q}f_{0}&\cdots&\mathcal{A}_{q^{n-1}}\mathcal{D}_{q}f_{n}\\
\vdots&&\vdots\\
\mathcal{D}_{q}^{n}f_{0}&\cdots&\mathcal{D}_{q}^{n}f_{n}\end{vmatrix}(x).

(29)

According to the definition, we immediately have following theorem by applying Theorem
2.3
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
f_{0},\cdots,f_{n}
be meromorphic (entire) functions. Then
\mathcal{W}(f_{0},\cdots,f_{n})
is meromorphic (entire).

In addition, the Askey-Wilson version of the Wronskian-Casorati determinant has following properties which also can be regarded as another equivalent definition when
|x|
is sufficiently large.

Proposition 4.2
.

Suppose
f_{0},\cdots,f_{n}
are meromorphic functions, then

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}(f_{0},\cdots,f_{n})(x)

(30)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{vmatrix}\eta_{q}^{\delta_{0}n}f_{0}&\cdots&\eta_{q}^{\delta_{0}n}f_{n}\\
\eta_{q}^{\delta_{1}(n-1)}\mathcal{D}_{q}f_{0}&\cdots&\eta_{q}^{\delta_{1}(n-1)}\mathcal{D}_{q}f_{n}\\
\vdots&&\vdots\\
\eta_{q}^{\delta_{n-1}}\mathcal{D}_{q}^{n-1}f_{0}&\cdots&\eta_{q}^{\delta_{n-1}}\mathcal{D}_{q}^{n-1}f_{n}\\
\mathcal{D}_{q}^{n}f_{0}&\cdots&\mathcal{D}_{q}^{n}f_{n}\end{vmatrix}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{vmatrix}\eta_{q}^{n}f_{0}&\cdots&\eta_{q}^{n}f_{n}\\
\eta_{q}^{n-2}f_{0}&\cdots&\eta_{q}^{n-2}f_{n}\\
\vdots&&\vdots\\
\eta_{q}^{-n}f_{0}&\cdots&\eta_{q}^{-n}f_{n}\end{vmatrix}(x)\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{n}\prod_{j=1}^{i}\frac{-1}{\eta_{q}^{i-2j+2}x-\eta_{q}^{i-2j}x}

for all

satisfying
|x|>\mathcal{R}(n,q)
where
\{\delta_{i}\}_{i=0}^{n-1}\subset\{\pm 1\}

Proof.

Since
|x|>\mathcal{R}(n,q)

\eta_{q}^{\pm i}x
are analytic and invertible for all

and
\eta_{q}^{j+1}x\neq\eta_{q}^{j-1}x

only depending on

such that

\displaystyle(\eta_{q}^{\nu}f)(x)-(\eta_{q}^{-\nu}f)(x)=\sum_{k=1}^{\nu}A_{k,\nu}(x)(\eta_{q}^{\nu-k}\mathcal{D}_{q}^{k}f)(x).

(32)

For instance,
A_{1,1}(x)=\eta_{q}x-\eta_{q}^{-1}x
. To prove the claim (
32
), we define a matrix omitting

\displaystyle\left(a_{i,j}^{(\nu)}\right)_{\nu\times\nu}=\left(\begin{matrix}\eta_{q}^{\nu-1}\mathcal{D}_{q}f&&&\\
\eta_{q}^{\nu-2}\mathcal{D}_{q}^{2}f&\eta_{q}^{\nu-3}\mathcal{D}_{q}f&&\\

when

. Note that

\displaystyle a_{i+1,j+1}^{(\nu)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\eta_{q}^{\nu-i-j+1}\mathcal{D}_{q}^{i-j+1}f-\left(\eta_{q}^{\nu-i-j+1}\mathcal{D}_{q}^{i-j+1}f-\eta_{q}^{\nu-i-j-1}\mathcal{D}_{q}^{i-j+1}f\right)

\displaystyle a_{i,j}^{(\nu)}-\eta_{q}^{\nu-i-j}\left(\frac{\eta_{q}\mathcal{D}_{q}^{i-j+1}f-\eta_{q}^{-1}\mathcal{D}_{q}^{i-j+1}f}{\eta_{q}x-\eta_{q}^{-1}x}\right)\left(\eta_{q}^{\nu-i-j+1}x-\eta_{q}^{\nu-i-j-1}x\right)

for all

. Furthermore, we have

\displaystyle\eta_{q}^{\nu}f-\eta_{q}^{-\nu}f

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}(\eta_{q}^{\nu-2i+2}f-\eta_{q}^{\nu-2i}f)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}\eta_{q}^{\nu-2i+1}\left(\frac{\eta_{q}f-\eta_{q}^{-1}f}{\eta_{q}x-\eta_{q}^{-1}x}\right)\cdot(\eta_{q}^{\nu-2i+2}x-\eta_{q}^{\nu-2i}x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}\eta_{q}^{\nu-2i+1}\mathcal{D}_{q}f\cdot(\eta_{q}^{\nu-2i+2}x-\eta_{q}^{\nu-2i}x).

Then the claim (
32
) follows by combining (
33
) and (
34
).

Now we prove

\displaystyle\begin{vmatrix}\eta_{q}^{n}f_{0}&\cdots&\eta_{q}^{n}f_{n}\\
\eta_{q}^{n-1}\mathcal{D}_{q}f_{0}&\cdots&\eta_{q}^{n-1}\mathcal{D}_{q}f_{n}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\eta_{q}\mathcal{D}_{q}^{n-1}f_{0}&\cdots&\eta_{q}\mathcal{D}_{q}^{n-1}f_{n}\\
\mathcal{D}_{q}^{n}f_{0}&\cdots&\mathcal{D}_{q}^{n}f_{n}\end{vmatrix}=\begin{vmatrix}\eta_{q}^{\delta_{0}n}f_{0}&\cdots&\eta_{q}^{\delta_{0}n}f_{n}\\
\eta_{q}^{\delta_{1}(n-1)}\mathcal{D}_{q}f_{0}&\cdots&\eta_{q}^{\delta_{1}(n-1)}\mathcal{D}_{q}f_{n}\\
\vdots&&\vdots\\
\eta_{q}^{\delta_{n-1}}\mathcal{D}_{q}^{n-1}f_{0}&\cdots&\eta_{q}^{\delta_{n-1}}\mathcal{D}_{q}^{n-1}f_{n}\\
\mathcal{D}_{q}^{n}f_{0}&\cdots&\mathcal{D}_{q}^{n}f_{n}\end{vmatrix}

(35)

for all
x\in\mathbb{C}
satisfying

and
\{\delta_{i}\}_{i=0}^{n-1}\subset\{\pm 1\}
. If

-th row subtract the combination from

-th row to
(n+1)
-th row according to the claim (

\displaystyle\begin{vmatrix}\eta_{q}^{\delta_{0}n}f_{0}&\cdots&\eta_{q}^{\delta_{0}n}f_{n}\\
\vdots&&\vdots\\

Proposition 4.3

Let

(i)

(ii)

(iii)

.

Proof.

For (i) and (ii), there is nothing to prove as they follow from basic calculation rules for determinants. For (iii), it can also be easily obtained by following the equality (

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}(f_{0},\cdots,f_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\eta_{q}^{n}f_{0})(\eta_{q}^{n-2}f_{0})\cdots(\eta_{q}^{-n}f_{0})\mathcal{W}\left(1,\frac{f_{1}}{f_{0}},\cdots,\frac{f_{n}}{f_{0}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\eta_{q}^{n}f_{0})(\eta_{q}^{n-2}f_{0})\cdots(\eta_{q}^{-n}f_{0})\mathcal{W}\left(\mathcal{D}_{q}\frac{f_{1}}{f_{0}},\cdots,\mathcal{D}_{q}\frac{f_{n}}{f_{0}}\right).

∎

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
f_{0},\cdots,f_{n}
be linearly independent entire functions. Then we have
\mathcal{W}(f_{0},\cdots,f_{n})(x)\not\equiv 0
.

Proof.

Assume the assertion is not valid, then we have
\mathcal{W}(f_{0},\cdots,f_{n})(x)\equiv 0
for all
x\in\mathbb{C}
satisfying
|x|>\mathcal{R}(n,q)
. Hence, from (
31
), we know that

\displaystyle\left(\begin{matrix}\eta_{q}^{n}f_{0}&\cdots&\eta_{q}^{n}f_{n}\\
\eta_{q}^{n-2}f_{0}&\cdots&\eta_{q}^{n-2}f_{n}\\
\vdots&&\vdots\\
\eta_{q}^{-n}f_{0}&\cdots&\eta_{q}^{-n}f_{n}\end{matrix}\right)\left(\begin{matrix}a_{0}\\
a_{1}\\
\vdots\\
a_{n}\end{matrix}\right)=\left(\begin{matrix}0\\
0\\
\vdots\\
0\end{matrix}\right)

has a non-trivial solution
a_{0},\cdots,a_{n}\in\mathbb{C}
for all
x\geq\mathcal{R}(n,q)
.

Let
g(x)=a_{0}f_{0}(x)+\cdots+a_{n}f_{n}(x)
; then
g
is entire. If
n
is even, we have
g(x)\equiv 0
for all
x\in\mathbb{C}
satisfying
|x|\geq\mathcal{R}(n,q)
. If
n
is odd, then
(\eta_{q}g)(x)\equiv 0
when
|x|
is sufficiently large. Let
t=\eta_{q}x
; then
g(t)\equiv 0
for all
|t|\geq\mathcal{R}^{\prime}(n,q)
. This implies that the entire function
g(x)
is bounded when
|x|
is large enough, and by Liouville’s Theorem, we have
g(x)=a_{0}f_{0}(x)+\cdots+a_{n}f_{n}(x)\equiv 0
for all
x\in\mathbb{C}
, which contradicts the assumption that
f_{0},\cdots,f_{n}
are linearly independent. Thus, the assertion follows.
∎

5
A general form of SMT for Askey-Wilson operator

As mentioned in the introduction, the general form of SMT plays a crucial role in certain problems. Here, we present the following Askey-Wilson version of the general form of SMT.

Theorem 5.1

(Askey-Wilson version of the general form of SMT)
.

Let
H_{1},\ldots,H_{p}
be arbitrary hyperplanes in
\mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{C})
with defining linear forms
L_{1},\ldots,L_{p}
. Let
f:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{C})
be a holomorphic map with a reduced representation
\mathbf{f}=(f_{0},\ldots,f_{n})
, where
f_{0},\ldots,f_{n}
are linearly independent, and let

\displaystyle\limsup_{r\to\infty}\frac{\log^{+}\log^{+}T_{f}(r)}{\log\log r}<1.

(36)

Then,

\int_{0}^{2\pi}\max_{\mathcal{K}}\sum_{j\in\mathcal{K}}\log\frac{\lVert\mathbf{f}(re^{i\theta})\rVert}{|L_{j}(\mathbf{f})(re^{i\theta})|}\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}+N_{\mathcal{W}}(r,0)\leq(n+1)T_{f}(r)+S_{\log}(r,f),

(37)

where
r
tends to infinity outside of an exceptional set
F
satisfying (
10
),
\mathcal{W}=\mathcal{W}(f_{0},\ldots,f_{n})
,
N_{\mathcal{W}}(r,0)=N(r,1/\mathcal{W})
and the maximum is taken over all subsets
\mathcal{K}\subset\{1,\cdots,p\}
such that the linear forms
L_{j}
,
j\in\mathcal{K}
, are linearly independent.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that
p\geq n+1
and
\#\mathcal{K}=n+1
. Let
E
be the set of all injective maps
\mu:\{0,1,\ldots,n\}\to\{1,\ldots,p\}
such that
L_{\mu(0)},\ldots,L_{\mu(n)}
are linearly independent. Then, there exists a constant
A_{\mu}\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
such that

\mathcal{W}(L_{\mu(0)}(\mathbf{f}),\ldots,L_{\mu(n)}(\mathbf{f}))=A_{\mu}\cdot\mathcal{W}(f_{0},\ldots,f_{n}).

Furthermore, from Lemma
4.4
, we have
\mathcal{W}(f_{0},\ldots,f_{n})\not\equiv 0
. Thus, when
r=|x|
is sufficiently large, by applying (iv) in Proposition
4.3
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}\max\limits_{\mathcal{K}}\sum\limits_{j\in\mathcal{K}}\log\frac{\lVert\textbf{f}(re^{i\theta})\rVert}{|L_{j}(\textbf{f})(re^{i\theta})|}\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}

(38)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}\max\limits_{\mu\in E}\sum_{j=0}^{n}\log\frac{\lVert\textbf{f}(re^{i\theta})\rVert}{|L_{\mu(j)}(\textbf{f}(re^{i\theta}))|}\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}\log\left(\max\limits_{\mu\in E}\left\{\frac{\lVert\textbf{f}(re^{i\theta})\rVert^{n+1}}{\prod_{j=0}^{n}|L_{\mu(j)}(\textbf{f}(re^{i\theta}))|}\right\}\right)\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}\log\left(\max\limits_{\mu\in E}\frac{|\mathcal{W}(L_{\mu(0)}(\textbf{f}),\cdots,L_{\mu(n)}(\textbf{f}))|}{|L_{\mu(0)}(\textbf{f})\cdots L_{\mu(n)}(\textbf{f})|}(re^{i\theta})\right)\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{2\pi}\log\frac{\lVert\textbf{f}(re^{i\theta})\rVert^{n+1}}{|\mathcal{W}(f_{0},\cdots,f_{n})(re^{i\theta})|}\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}+O(1)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}\log\left(\max_{\mu\in E}\frac{|[\eta_{q}^{n}L_{\mu(0))}(\textbf{f})]\cdots[\eta_{q}^{-n}L_{\mu(0)}(\textbf{f})]|}{|L_{\mu(0)}(\textbf{f})\cdots L_{\mu(0)}(\textbf{f})|}(re^{i\theta})\right)\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{2\pi}\log\left(\max_{\mu\in E}\frac{\left|\mathcal{W}\left(\mathcal{D}_{q}\frac{L_{\mu(1)}(\textbf{f})}{L_{\mu(0)}(\textbf{f})},\cdots,\mathcal{D}_{q}\frac{L_{\mu(n)}(\textbf{f})}{L_{\mu(0)}(\textbf{f})}\right)\right|}{\left|\frac{L_{\mu(1)}(\textbf{f})}{L_{\mu(0)}(\textbf{f})}\cdots\frac{L_{\mu(n)}(\textbf{f})}{L_{\mu(0)}(\textbf{f})}\right|}(re^{i\theta})\right)\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{2\pi}\log\frac{\lVert\textbf{f}(re^{i\theta})\rVert^{n+1}}{|\mathcal{W}(f_{0},\cdots,f_{n})(re^{i\theta})|}\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}+O(1)

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}+O(1).

In the second inequality, we used the basic fact that

\log\max_{1\leq k\leq m}\{a_{k}\}\leq\log\max_{1\leq k\leq m}\left\{\frac{a_{k}}{b_{k}}\right\}+\log\max_{1\leq k\leq m}\{b_{k}\}

for
a_{k},b_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
. Now, we first consider
I_{1}
. Suppose

\mathbf{g}=(g_{0},\ldots,g_{n}):=(1,f_{1}/f_{0},\ldots,f_{n}/f_{0}).

Then, for any linear form
L
, we have

Hence,

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}\log\sum_{\mu\in E}\prod_{k=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\left|\frac{(\eta_{q}^{n-2k}L_{\mu(0)}(\textbf{f}))(\eta_{q}^{-n+2k}L_{\mu(0)}(\textbf{f}))}{L_{\mu(0)}^{2}(\textbf{f})}\right|\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}

(39)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}\log\prod_{k=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\left|\frac{(\eta_{q}^{n-2k}f_{0})(\eta_{q}^{-n+2k}f_{0})}{f_{0}^{2}}\right|\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{2\pi}\log\sum_{\mu\in E}\prod_{k=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\left|\frac{(\eta_{q}^{n-2k}L_{\mu(0)}(\textbf{g}))(\eta_{q}^{-n+2k}L_{\mu(0)}(\textbf{g}))}{L_{\mu(0)}^{2}(\textbf{g})}\right|\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\log\left|\frac{(\eta_{q}^{n-2k}f_{0})(\eta_{q}^{-n+2k}f_{0})}{f_{0}^{2}}\right|\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}

\displaystyle+\sum_{\mu\in E}\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\log^{+}\left|\frac{(\eta_{q}^{n-2k}L_{\mu(0)}(\textbf{g}))(\eta_{q}^{-n+2k}L_{\mu(0)}(\textbf{g}))}{L_{\mu(0)}^{2}(\textbf{g})}\right|\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}+O(1)

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle I_{11}+I_{12}+O(1).

Due to

Thus, we can obtain a similar estimation for

as in Lemma

using the same analysis. Furthermore, since

(\eta_{q}^{\pm(n-2k)}f_{0})(x)=(\eta_{q^{\pm(n-2k)}}f_{0})(x)

we obtain

\displaystyle I_{11}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\left(N\left(r,\frac{1}{(\eta_{q^{n-2k}}f_{0})(\eta_{q^{-n+2k}}f_{0})}\right)-2N\left(r,\frac{1}{f_{0}}\right)+O(\log r)\right)

(40)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\left(N\left(r,\frac{1}{(\eta_{q}^{n-2k}f_{0})(\eta_{q}^{-n+2k}f_{0})}\right)-2N\left(r,\frac{1}{f_{0}}\right)+O(\log r)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle O\left(\frac{N(r,1/f_{0})}{\log\log r}\right)+O(\log r)=S_{\log}(r,f),

where
r
tends to infinity outside of an exceptional set
E
satisfying (
10
). For
I_{12}
, since for any linear form
L
we have

\displaystyle T(r,L(\mathbf{g}))\leq K\sum_{i=1}^{n}T(r,g_{i})+O(1)=O(T_{f}(r)),

(41)

where the constant
K>0
is independent of

and
\mathbf{g}
, it follows that, by applying Lemma
3.2
, we have

\displaystyle I_{12}=O\left(\sum_{\mu\in E}\frac{T(r,L_{\mu(0)}(\mathbf{g}))}{\log\log r}\right)+O(\log r)=S_{\log}(r,f),

(42)

where
r
tends to infinity outside of an exceptional set
E
satisfying (
10
). It is important to note that

\log\max\limits_{1\leq j\leq n}\{a_{j}\}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{n}\log a_{j}

for positive numbers
a_{j}
may not hold, but

\log\max\limits_{1\leq j\leq n}\{a_{j}\}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{n}\log^{+}a_{j}

For
I_{2}
, we define

h_{\mu_{i}}=\frac{L_{\mu(i)}(\mathbf{f})}{L_{\mu(0)}(\mathbf{f})}=\frac{L_{\mu(i)}(\mathbf{g})}{L_{\mu(0)}(\mathbf{g})},\quad\text{for }i=1,\ldots,n,

It follows from (

3.2
and Theorem

that

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}\sum_{\mu\in E}\log^{+}\frac{\left|\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{D}_{q}h_{\mu_{1}},\cdots,\mathcal{D}_{q}h_{\mu_{n}})\right|}{|h_{\mu_{1}}\cdots h_{\mu_{n}}|}(re^{i\theta})\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}

Lemma 5.2

\displaystyle\min\limits_{0\leq t\leq M}\left\{\nu_{\eta_{q}^{M-2t}f}^{0}(x)\right\},

\displaystyle\min\left\{\nu_{\eta_{q}^{M}f}^{0}(x),\min\left\{\nu_{\eta_{q}^{M-2}f}^{0}(x),\nu_{\eta_{q}^{M-2}f-\eta_{q}^{M-4}f}^{0}(x)\right\},\ldots\right\}

\displaystyle\min\limits_{0\leq t\leq M}\left\{\nu_{\mathcal{A}_{q^{M-t}}\mathcal{D}_{q}^{t}f}^{0}(x)\right\}

\displaystyle\min\left\{\nu_{\mathcal{A}_{q^{M}}f}^{0}(x),\min\limits_{1\leq t\leq M}\left\{\nu_{\mathcal{A}_{q^{M-t}}\mathcal{D}_{q}^{t}f}^{0}(x)\right\}\right\}

6
Askey-Wilson version of SMT with hypersurfaces

As far as we know, Shi and Yan were the first to consider the number of irreducible components of hypersurfaces to establish a new SMT for holomorphic maps into projective space
Shi and Yan (2022)
. However, in their key lemma (
(
Shi and Yan, 2022
, Lemma 2.4)
), the reorganization of the homogeneous polynomial is restricted to having only two elements. In this paper, we extend their key lemma and generalize the corresponding SMT for the Askey-Wilson operator. First, we introduce a new definition for a homogeneous polynomial.

Definition 6.1

(Min-max
s^{{}^{\prime}}
-th decomposition)
.

Let
Q
be a homogeneous polynomial in
\overline{k}[x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}]
of degree
d
, and suppose it has
s
distinct irreducible factors. Fix a positive integer
s^{{}^{\prime}}\leq s
, and let

\displaystyle\Omega_{Q}(s^{{}^{\prime}})=\big\{

\displaystyle\{R_{1},\ldots,R_{s^{{}^{\prime}}}\}:\;Q=R_{1}\cdots R_{s^{{}^{\prime}}},

\displaystyle R_{j}\text{ is a non-constant homogeneous polynomial in }\overline{k}[x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}],

\displaystyle j=1,\ldots,s^{{}^{\prime}},\text{ and there are no common factors between any two }R_{j}\big\}.

We call a decomposition
\mathcal{R}^{*}=\{R_{1}^{*},\ldots,R_{s^{{}^{\prime}}}^{*}\}\in\Omega_{Q}(s^{{}^{\prime}})
the
min-max
s^{{}^{\prime}}
-th decomposition of
Q
if

\displaystyle\max\limits_{1\leq j\leq s^{{}^{\prime}}}\deg R_{j}^{*}=\min\limits_{\mathcal{R}\in\Omega_{Q}(s^{{}^{\prime}})}\max\limits_{R_{j}\in\mathcal{R}}\deg R_{j}.

We also define
d_{Q}^{*}(s^{{}^{\prime}})=\max\limits_{1\leq j\leq s^{{}^{\prime}}}\deg R_{j}^{*}
.

From now on, we adopt the notation
\lceil x\rceil
(
\lfloor x\rfloor
) to express the smallest (largest) integer greater (less) than or equal to
x\in\mathbb{R}
.

The following lemma is a generalization of the key lemma in
(
Shi and Yan, 2022
, Lemma 2.4)
(for
s^{{}^{\prime}}=2
), while the proof is completely different.

Lemma 6.2
.

Under the assumptions of Definition
6.1
, we have

\displaystyle d_{Q}^{*}(s^{{}^{\prime}})\leq\max\left\{d-s+1,\left\lceil\frac{d}{s^{{}^{\prime}}}\right\rceil\right\}.

(62)

Proof.

Let
Q=Q_{1}\cdots Q_{s}
, where
Q_{i}
is a non-constant factor of
Q
such that there are no common factors between any two
Q_{i}
, and
\deg Q_{i}=d_{i}
for
i=1,\ldots,s
. Without loss of generality, let
d_{1}\geq d_{2}\geq\cdots\geq d_{s}\geq 1
. The assertion clearly holds when
s^{{}^{\prime}}=1
or
s=s^{{}^{\prime}}
. Hence, we will assume
s>s^{{}^{\prime}}\geq 2
. We first define an algorithm to distribute
d_{i}
.

Step 1
: Let
E_{j}=\{j\}
and
R_{j}=Q_{j}
for
j=1,\ldots,s^{{}^{\prime}}
, then
\deg R_{1}=d_{1},\ldots,\deg R_{s^{{}^{\prime}}}=d_{s^{{}^{\prime}}}
.

Step 2
: Suppose
d_{1},\ldots,d_{k-1}
have been used, where
k\in\mathbb{N}
(i.e.,
\{1,\ldots,k-1\}=\cup_{j=1}^{s^{{}^{\prime}}}E_{j}
). Now, we proceed to distribute
d_{k}
. Denote
j^{*}\in\{1,\ldots,s^{{}^{\prime}}\}
such that

\deg R_{j^{*}}=\min\limits_{1\leq j\leq s^{{}^{\prime}}}\deg R_{j}.

(There may be several
j^{*}
for which the equality holds, but any one of them can be chosen.) Let
E_{j^{*}}=E_{j^{*}}\cup\{k\}
and
R_{j^{*}}=R_{j^{*}}Q_{k}
, then

\deg R_{j^{*}}=\deg R_{j^{*}}+d_{k}.

Repeat this step until all
d_{i}
,
i=1,\ldots,s
, are exhausted.

It is clear that any two of
R_{j}
do not have common factors; hence,

\displaystyle\{R_{1},\ldots,R_{s^{{}^{\prime}}}\}\in\Omega_{Q}(s^{{}^{\prime}}).

Therefore,

\displaystyle\max_{1\leq j\leq s^{{}^{\prime}}}\deg R_{j}^{*}\leq\max_{1\leq j\leq s^{{}^{\prime}}}\deg R_{j}.

Thus, it is sufficient to estimate the upper bound for
\max_{1\leq j\leq s^{{}^{\prime}}}\deg R_{j}
. Note that the larger
d_{i}
values are distributed first in this algorithm. Suppose
d_{1}=N
, then
\{d_{i}\}_{i=1}^{s}\subset\{1,\ldots,N\}
.

We say the decomposition is
in the
k
-th stage
(
k\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
) if we have just used all
d_{i}
values equal to
k
. Denote by
d^{(k)}
and
s^{(k)}
be the summation of
d_{i}
and their numbers we have used in
k
stage. Define
I_{\max}^{(k)}
and
I_{\min}^{(k)}
as the maximum and minimum of the degrees of
R_{j}
for
j=1,\ldots,s^{{}^{\prime}}
in the
k
-th stage, respectively. That is

\displaystyle d^{(k)}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{s^{{}^{\prime}}}\deg R_{j}=\sum_{d_{i}\geq k}d_{i},

\displaystyle s^{(k)}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\#\{d_{i}\geq k\},

\displaystyle I_{\max}^{(k)}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\max_{1\leq j\leq s^{{}^{\prime}}}\deg R_{j},

\displaystyle I_{\min}^{(k)}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\min_{1\leq j\leq s^{{}^{\prime}}}\deg R_{j},

where
\{R_{1},\cdots,R_{s^{{}^{\prime}}}\}
is in the
k
-th stage. In particular,
d^{(1)}=d
and
s^{(1)}=s
. We also adopt the notation
I_{\max}:=I_{\max}^{(1)}
and
I_{\min}:=I_{\min}^{(1)}
. (A specific example following this algorithm can be seen from Example
6.3
below.)

Now we claim that

\displaystyle I_{\max}^{(k)}\leq d^{(k)}-s^{(k)}+1,

(63)

for
k=2,\ldots,N
.

Indeed, for
k=N
, which means only
N
should be distributed. Meanwhile,
d^{(N)}=Ns^{(N)}
. Suppose
s^{(N)}=ps^{{}^{\prime}}+q
, where
p\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
q\in\{0,1,\ldots,s^{{}^{\prime}}-1\}
. Then,

I_{\max}^{(N)}=N\left\lceil\frac{s^{(N)}}{s^{{}^{\prime}}}\right\rceil\quad\text{and}\quad d^{(N)}-s^{(N)}+1=(N-1)s^{(N)}+1.

If
q=0
, then
p\geq 1
because
d_{1}=N
, and then we have

\displaystyle d^{(N)}-s^{(N)}+1-I_{\max}^{(N)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(N-1)ps^{{}^{\prime}}+1-Np

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 2(N-1)p+1-Np

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(N-2)p+1>0.

If
q\geq 1
, we have

\displaystyle d^{(N)}-s^{(N)}+1-I_{\max}^{(N)}

\displaystyle=(N-1)(ps^{{}^{\prime}}+q)+1-N(p+1)

\displaystyle\geq 2p(N-1)-Np+q(N-1)-N+1

\displaystyle\geq(N-2)p+(N-1)(q-1)\geq 0.

Assume that (
63
) holds for the
(k+1)
-th stage. Now, it is sufficient to prove that it holds for the
k
-th stage with
k\geq 2
, as the claim will then follow by induction.

From the algorithm, it is easy to verify that
I_{\max}^{(k)}\geq I_{\max}^{(k+1)}
. If equality holds, note that

d^{(k)}-s^{(k)}=(d^{(k+1)}-s^{(k+1)})+(lk-l)\geq d^{(k+1)}-s^{(k+1)},

where
l=\#\{i:d_{i}=k,\;1\leq i\leq s\}
. Then, we have

I_{\max}^{(k)}=I_{\max}^{(k+1)}\leq d^{(k+1)}-s^{(k+1)}+1\leq d^{(k)}-s^{(k)}+1.

If
I_{\max}^{(k)}>I_{\max}^{(k+1)}
, then we claim that
I_{\max}^{(k)}-I_{\min}^{(k)}\leq k
. In fact, in this case, when we are using
k
, there exists a moment such that
\deg R_{j}\leq I_{\max}^{(k+1)}
for all
j=1,\ldots,s^{{}^{\prime}}
. However, after adding one
k
, we have
\deg R_{j^{*}}>I_{\max}^{(k+1)}
for some
j^{*}\in\{1,\ldots,s^{{}^{\prime}}\}
. Let
I_{\min}^{*}
denote the minimum degree of
R_{j}
at this stage, and let
I_{\min}^{*+lk}
and
I_{\max}^{*+lk}
denote the minimum and maximum degrees of
R_{j}
, respectively, after adding
lk
in the stage, where
l\in\mathbb{N}
. Hence,

\displaystyle I_{\max}^{*+k}=I_{\min}^{*}+k>I_{\max}^{(k+1)}\geq I_{\min}^{*},

which implies that
I_{\max}^{*+k}-I_{\min}^{*+k}\leq I_{\max}^{*+k}-I_{\min}^{*}=k
.

Now, assume that
I_{\max}^{*+lk}-I_{\min}^{*+lk}\leq k
holds for some
l\in\mathbb{N}
. If there are no additional
k
values to use, then

I_{\max}^{(k)}-I_{\min}^{(k)}=I_{\max}^{*+lk}-I_{\min}^{*+lk}\leq k.

If there are still some
k
values remaining, then

I_{\max}^{*+(l+1)k}=I_{\min}^{*+lk}+k,

as otherwise it would contradict the assumption that
I_{\max}^{*+lk}-I_{\min}^{*+lk}\leq k
. Therefore,

I_{\max}^{*+(l+1)k}-I_{\min}^{*+(l+1)k}\leq I_{\max}^{*+(l+1)k}-I_{\min}^{*+lk}=k.

Thus,
I_{\max}^{(k)}-I_{\min}^{(k)}\leq k
holds for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
in this way.

Hence, we can define
\{\nu+j\}_{j=0}^{k}
as all the degrees. Assume
t_{j}
represents the number of
\nu+j
,
j=0,\ldots,k
, in the
k
-th stage, where
t_{k}\geq 1
and
\nu\geq k
(since every
\deg R_{j}
adds at least
k
, otherwise
I_{\max}^{(k)}=I_{\max}^{(k+1)}
). Then, we have

\displaystyle I_{\max}^{(k)}=\nu+k,\quad d^{(k)}=\sum_{j=0}^{k}t_{j}(\nu+j),\quad s^{{}^{\prime}}=\sum_{j=0}^{k}t_{j}.

For any
R_{j}
in the
k
-th stage,
j=1,\ldots,s^{{}^{\prime}}
, the component is no less than
k
. Hence, there are at most
\frac{\nu+j}{k}
components in
R_{j}
. Thus,

\displaystyle s^{(k)}\leq\sum_{j=0}^{k}t_{j}\frac{\nu+j}{k}.

Since
k\geq 2
,
t_{k}\geq 1
, and
s^{{}^{\prime}}\geq 2
, we have

\displaystyle d^{(k)}-s^{(k)}+1-I_{\max}^{(k)}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{k}t_{j}(\nu+j)\frac{k-1}{k}+1-\nu-k

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle s^{{}^{\prime}}\nu\frac{k-1}{k}-\nu+\sum_{j=0}^{k}t_{j}j\frac{k-1}{k}+1-k

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\nu\frac{s^{{}^{\prime}}(k-1)-k}{k}+t_{k}k\frac{k-1}{k}+1-k

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\nu\frac{k-2}{k}\geq 0.

Therefore, the claim (
63
) follows. Now, we only need to address the first stage (
k=1
).

If
I_{\max}=I_{\max}^{(1)}=I_{\max}^{(2)}
, then according to the decreasing properties of
d^{(k)}-s^{(k)}+1
, we have

I_{\max}\leq d^{(1)}-s^{(1)}+1=d-s+1.

If
I_{\max}=I_{\max}^{(1)}<I_{\max}^{(2)}
, then
I_{\max}-I_{\min}\leq 1
due to the analysis above, which means there are at most two distinct values,
I_{\max}
and
I_{\max}-1
. Let
t\geq 1
and
s^{{}^{\prime}}-t
denote the numbers of
I_{\max}
and
I_{\max}-1
, respectively. Then,

\displaystyle\left\lceil\frac{d}{s^{{}^{\prime}}}\right\rceil=\left\lceil\frac{tI_{\max}+(s^{{}^{\prime}}-t)(I_{\max}-1)}{s^{{}^{\prime}}}\right\rceil=\left\lceil I_{\max}-\frac{s^{{}^{\prime}}-t}{s^{{}^{\prime}}}\right\rceil\geq I_{\max}.

Thus, we complete the proof.
∎

When we consider the truncated SMT with a decomposition of the polynomials, some relationships between
\nu_{f}^{0}(x)
and
\nu_{f_{i}}^{0}(x)
are needed, where
f=\prod f_{i}
. Let us first recall some potential properties of the differential operator used in
Shi and Yan (2022)
.

Let
M,m\in\mathbb{N}
, and let
f_{1},\ldots,f_{m}
be arbitrary entire functions with
f=\prod_{i=1}^{m}f_{i}
. Then we have

\displaystyle\min\limits_{0\leq t\leq M}\nu_{f^{(t)}}^{0}(x)\geq\sum_{i=1}^{m}\min\limits_{0\leq t\leq M}\nu_{f_{i}^{(t)}}^{0}(x)\geq\min\limits_{0\leq t\leq mM}\nu_{f^{(t)}}^{0}(x)

(64)

for any
x\in\mathbb{C}
. Since
\nu_{f}^{0}(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\nu_{f_{i}}^{0}(x)
, it follows from (
45
) that

\displaystyle\overline{n}^{(mM)}\left(r,\frac{1}{f}\right)\geq\sum_{i=1}^{m}\overline{n}^{(M)}\left(r,\frac{1}{f_{i}}\right)\geq\overline{n}^{(M)}\left(r,\frac{1}{f}\right).

For the first inequality of (
64
), we only provide the proof when
m=2
since the other cases can be obtained by a simple induction. Assume
f=f_{1}f_{2}
, then for any
0\leq t\leq M
,

\displaystyle\nu_{f^{(t)}}^{0}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nu_{\sum_{j=0}^{t}f_{1}^{(j)}f_{2}^{(t-j)}}^{0}(x)\geq\min_{0\leq j\leq t}\nu_{f_{1}^{(j)}}^{0}(x)+\min_{0\leq j\leq t}\nu_{f_{2}^{(j)}}^{0}(x)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\min_{0\leq j\leq M}\nu_{f_{1}^{(j)}}^{0}(x)+\min_{0\leq j\leq M}\nu_{f_{2}^{(j)}}^{0}(x).

Hence

\displaystyle\min\limits_{0\leq t\leq M}\nu_{f^{(t)}}^{0}(x)\geq\min_{0\leq t\leq M}\nu_{f_{1}^{(t)}}^{0}(x)+\min_{0\leq t\leq M}\nu_{f_{2}^{(t)}}^{0}(x).

For the second inequality, if
\nu_{f}^{0}(x)\leq mM
, then
\min\limits_{0\leq t\leq mM}\nu_{f^{(t)}}^{0}(x)=0
, and hence it is valid. If
\nu_{f}^{0}(x)>mM
, then there exist some
i\in\{1,\cdots,m\}
such that
\nu_{f_{i}}^{0}(x)>M
. Let
E=\{i:\nu_{f_{i}}^{0}(x)>M\}
and
p=\#E>0
, then we have
\min\limits_{0\leq t\leq M}\nu_{f_{i}^{(t)}}^{0}(x)=\nu_{f_{i}}^{0}(x)-M>0
when
i\in E
and
\sum_{i\in E}\nu_{f_{i}}^{0}(x)\geq\sum_{i=1}^{m}\nu_{f_{i}}^{0}(x)-(m-p)M
. Thus

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}\min\limits_{0\leq t\leq M}\nu_{f_{i}^{(t)}}^{0}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in E}\min\limits_{0\leq t\leq M}\nu_{f_{i}^{(t)}}^{0}(x)=\sum_{i\in E}\left(\nu_{f_{i}}^{0}(x)-M\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}\nu_{f_{i}}^{0}(x)-mM=\nu_{f}^{0}(x)-mM=\min\limits_{0\leq t\leq mM}\nu_{f^{(t)}}^{0}(x),

which proves the second inequality of (
64
).

\displaystyle\geq

for all

satisfying

The following lemma, due to Quang

Lemma 6.6
.

(See
(
Quang, 2019
, Lemma 3.1)
)
Let
l\in\mathbb{N}
and
Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{l+1}\in\mathbb{C}[x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}]
be homogeneous polynomials of the same degree
d\geq 1
such that the hypersurfaces
\{Q_{1}=0\},\ldots,\{Q_{l+1}=0\}
in
\mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{C})
are located in
l
-subgeneral position. Then, there exist
n
homogeneous polynomials
P_{2},\ldots,P_{n+1}
of the form

\displaystyle P_{t}=\sum_{j=2}^{l-n+t}b_{tj}Q_{j},\quad b_{tj}\in\mathbb{C},\quad t=2,\ldots,n+1,

such that the hypersurfaces
\{P_{1}=0\},\{P_{2}=0\},\ldots,\{P_{n+1}=0\}

.

Now, we are ready to state the main theorem of this paper.

Theorem 6.7

(Askey-Wilson version of SMT with hypersurfaces)
.

Let
f:\mathbb{C}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{C})
be a holomorphic curve with a reduced representation
\textbf{f}=(f_{0},\cdots,f_{n})
satisfying that
f_{0},\cdots,f_{n}
are algebraically independent, and

\displaystyle\limsup\limits_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log^{+}\log^{+}T_{f}(r)}{\log\log r}<1.

(66)

Let
Q_{j}
be a homogeneous polynomial in
\mathbb{C}[x_{0},\cdots,x_{n}]
of degree
d_{j}(\geq 1)
defining the hypersurface
D_{j}
in
\mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{C})
,
j=1,\cdots,p
, such that
D_{1},\cdots,D_{p}
are located in
l
-subgeneral position. Suppose
s_{j}\in\mathbb{N}
is the number of distinct irreducible factors of
Q_{j}
, fix a positive integer
s^{{}^{\prime}}\leq\min\left\{n,\min\limits_{1\leq j\leq p}\{s_{j}\}\right\}
, and let
d=\textrm{lcm}\{d_{1},\cdots,d_{p}\}
.
Define

\displaystyle\alpha=\max\limits_{1\leq j\leq p}\left\{\frac{\max\left\{d_{j}-s_{j}+1,\left\lceil\frac{d_{j}}{s^{{}^{\prime}}}\right\rceil\right\}}{d_{j}}\right\}(l-n)

and

\displaystyle\beta=\frac{\alpha d}{\max\limits_{1\leq j\leq p}\left\{\max\left\{d-s_{j}+1,\left\lceil\frac{d}{s^{{}^{\prime}}}\right\rceil\right\}\right\}(l-n)}.

If
\beta(\alpha+1)\geq\alpha
, then, for any
\varepsilon>0
,

\displaystyle(p-(\alpha+1)(n+1)-\varepsilon)T_{f}(r)\leq\frac{1}{d}\sum_{j=1}^{p}\tilde{N}_{AW,f}^{[M_{1}](s^{{}^{\prime}})}\left(r,Q_{j}^{\frac{d}{d_{j}}}\right)+S_{\log}(r,f),

(67)

where
r
tends to infinity outside an exceptional set
F
satisfying (
10
) and

\displaystyle M_{1}=\lfloor 4(e\lceil\alpha+1\rceil(n+1)^{2}d!\lceil\varepsilon^{-1}\rceil)^{n}-1\rfloor.

Proof.

Let
\tilde{Q}_{j}=Q_{j}^{d/d_{j}}
,
j=1,\cdots,p
. Then
\tilde{Q}_{1},\cdots,\tilde{Q}_{p}
have the same degree of
d
. For any given
x\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\bigcup_{j=1}^{p}f^{-1}(D_{j})
, there exists a renumbering
\{1(x),\cdots,p(x)\}
of
\{1,\cdots,p\}
such that

\displaystyle|\tilde{Q}_{1(x)}(\textbf{f})(x)|\leq\cdots\leq|\tilde{Q}_{p(x)}(\textbf{f})(x)|.

Since
D_{1},\cdots,D_{p}
are in
l
-subgeneral position, we have

\displaystyle\lVert\textbf{f}(x)\rVert^{d}\leq A_{1}\cdot\max\limits_{1\leq j\leq l+1}|\tilde{Q}_{j(x)}(\textbf{f})(x)|

for a finite positive constant
A_{1}
only depending on the coefficients of
\tilde{Q}_{1},\dots,\tilde{Q}_{p}
. We denote by
P_{1(x)}=\tilde{Q}_{1(x)}
,
P_{2(x)}
,
\cdots
,
P_{n(x)}
the homogeneous polynomials obtained in Lemma
6.6
with respect to the homogeneous polynomials
\tilde{Q}_{1(x)}
,
\cdots
,
\tilde{Q}_{l(x)}
(
P_{(n+1)(x)}
and
\tilde{Q}_{(l+1)(x)}
are ignored here). Then, since there are only finitely many renumberings of
\{1,\cdots,p\}
, we can conclude that there exists a positive constant
A_{2}
, chosen uniformly for all given
x
such that

\displaystyle|P_{t(x)}(\textbf{f})(x)|\leq A_{2}\cdot\max\limits_{2\leq j\leq l-n+t}|\tilde{Q}_{j(x)}(\textbf{f})(x)|=A_{2}\cdot|\tilde{Q}_{(l-n+t)(x)}(\textbf{f})(x)|,

for
t=2,\cdots,n
.
Hence

\displaystyle\log\prod_{j=1}^{p}\frac{\left\lVert\textbf{f}(x)\right\rVert^{d}}{|\tilde{Q}_{j(x)}(\textbf{f})(x)|}\leq\log\prod_{j=1}^{l}\frac{\left\lVert\textbf{f}(x)\right\rVert^{d}}{|\tilde{Q}_{j(x)}(\textbf{f})(x)|}+O(1)

(68)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\log\prod_{j=l-n+2}^{l}\frac{\left\lVert\textbf{f}(x)\right\rVert^{d}}{|\tilde{Q}_{j(x)}(\textbf{f})(x)|}+\log\prod_{j=1}^{l-n+1}\frac{\left\lVert\textbf{f}(x)\right\rVert^{d}}{|\tilde{Q}_{j(x)}(\textbf{f})(x)|}+O(1)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\log\prod_{j=2}^{n}\frac{\left\lVert\textbf{f}(x)\right\rVert^{d}}{|P_{j(x)}(\textbf{f})(x)|}+\log\prod_{j=1}^{l-n+1}\frac{\left\lVert\textbf{f}(x)\right\rVert^{d}}{|\tilde{Q}_{j(x)}(\textbf{f})(x)|}+O(1)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\log\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\left\lVert\textbf{f}(x)\right\rVert^{d}}{|P_{j(x)}(\textbf{f})(x)|}+(l-n)\log\frac{\left\lVert\textbf{f}(x)\right\rVert^{d}}{|\tilde{Q}_{1(x)}(\textbf{f})(x)|}+O(1).

Define
Q_{j}=P_{j,1}^{*}\cdots P_{j,s^{{}^{\prime}}}^{*}
to be the corresponding min-max
s^{{}^{\prime}}
-th decomposition for
j=1,\cdots,p
. Let
d_{j,i}^{*}=\deg P_{j,i}^{*}
,
j=1,\cdots,p
and
i=1,\cdots,s^{{}^{\prime}}
, then
d_{Q_{j}}^{*}(s^{{}^{\prime}})=\max\limits_{1\leq i\leq s^{{}^{\prime}}}\{d_{j,i}^{*}\}
, and by applying Lemma
6.2
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d_{Q_{j}}^{*}(s^{{}^{\prime}})}{d_{j}}(l-n)\leq\alpha

(69)

for
j=1,\cdots,p
. Let

\displaystyle\hat{d}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\text{lcm}\{d_{1,1}^{*},\cdots,d_{1,s^{{}^{\prime}}}^{*},\cdots,d_{p,1}^{*},\cdots,d_{p,s^{{}^{\prime}}}^{*},d\}\leq d!

\tilde{Q}_{j}(\textbf{f})=f_{0}^{d}\tilde{Q}_{j}(\textbf{g})\not\equiv 0
. By applying Lemma
2.4
, we have

\displaystyle N\left(r,\frac{1}{\tilde{Q}_{j}(\textbf{f})}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle dN\left(r,\frac{1}{f_{0}}\right)+N\left(r,\frac{1}{\tilde{Q}_{j}(\textbf{g})}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle dT_{f}(r)+T(r,\tilde{Q}_{j}(\textbf{g}))+O(1)\leq A_{6}\cdot T_{f}(r),

where
j=1,\cdots,p
and
A_{6}
is a finite positive number only depending on
\tilde{Q}_{j}
, which implies (
86
) according to the assumption (
66
), and hence (
85
) holds. Then by combining (
77
), (
78
) and (
85
), we have

\displaystyle\left(pd-\frac{(\alpha+1)d}{\hat{d}\Omega}NM\right)T_{f}(r)\leq\sum_{j=1}^{p}\tilde{N}_{AW,f}^{[M_{1}](s^{{}^{\prime}})}(r,\tilde{Q}_{j})+S_{\log}(r,f).

(87)

Lastly, we should determine the large number
N
according to
\varepsilon
. Since
\frac{y}{1+y}\leq\log(1+y)\leq y
for all
y\geq 0
, then when
y\in\left[0,\frac{1}{n(n+1)}\right]
, we have

\displaystyle\log(1+y)^{n}-\log(1+(n+1)y)\leq ny-\frac{(n+1)y}{1+(n+1)y}=\frac{n(n+1)y-1}{1+(n+1)y}y\leq 0

for
n\in\mathbb{N}
, which implies

\displaystyle(1+y)^{n}\leq 1+(n+1)y,\quad y\in\left[0,\frac{1}{n(n+1)}\right].

(88)

Now, let
N=\lceil\alpha+1\rceil(n+1)^{3}\hat{d}\lceil\varepsilon^{-1}\rceil+(n+1)\hat{d}
. Then
N
is divisible by
\hat{d}
, and

\displaystyle 0\leq\frac{(n+1)\hat{d}}{N-(n+1)\hat{d}}=\frac{1}{\lceil\alpha+1\rceil(n+1)^{2}\lceil\varepsilon^{-1}\rceil}\leq\frac{1}{n(n+1)}.

It is easy to check that
\frac{N+j}{N-(n+1-j)\hat{d}}\leq 1+\frac{(n+1)\hat{d}}{N-(n+1)\hat{d}}
for all
j=1,\cdots,n
. Then, from (
72
), (
73
), (
74
) and (
88
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{NM}{\hat{d}\Omega}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(n+1)\frac{(N+1)\cdots(N+n)}{(N-n\hat{d})\cdots(N-\hat{d})}

(89)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(n+1)\left(1+\frac{(n+1)\hat{d}}{N-(n+1)\hat{d}}\right)^{n}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(n+1)+\frac{(n+1)^{3}\hat{d}}{N-(n+1)\hat{d}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(n+1)+\frac{\varepsilon}{\alpha+1}

and

\displaystyle M

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle e^{n}\left(1+\frac{N}{n}\right)^{n}

(90)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle e^{n}\left(\frac{n+(n+1)\hat{d}}{n}+\frac{\lceil\alpha+1\rceil(n+1)^{3}\hat{d}\lceil\varepsilon^{-1}\rceil}{n}\right)^{n}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(e\lceil\alpha+1\rceil(n+1)^{2}d!\lceil\varepsilon^{-1}\rceil)^{n}\left(1+\frac{1}{n}+\frac{n+(n+1)\hat{d}}{\lceil\alpha+1\rceil n(n+1)^{2}\hat{d}\lceil\varepsilon^{-1}\rceil}\right)^{n}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(e\lceil\alpha+1\rceil(n+1)^{2}d!\lceil\varepsilon^{-1}\rceil)^{n}\left(1+\frac{1}{n}+\frac{2}{n(n+1)}\right)^{n}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 4(e\lceil\alpha+1\rceil(n+1)^{2}d!\lceil\varepsilon^{-1}\rceil)^{n},

where we used the basic estimation

\left(1+\frac{n+3}{n(n+1)}\right)^{n}=\left(1+\frac{n+3}{n(n+1)}\right)^{\frac{n(n+1)}{n+3}\cdot\frac{n+3}{n+1}}\leq 4

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Indeed, it clearly holds when
n=1
. For
n\geq 2
, we have
\frac{n(n+1)}{n+3}\geq 1
and
\frac{n+3}{n+1}\leq 2
.
Since
h(y)=\left(1+\frac{1}{y}\right)^{y}

, the estimation follows.
Then, the assertion follows by combining (

), (

), and (

).

Corollary 6.8
.

Let

\displaystyle\limsup\limits_{r\to\infty}\frac{\log^{+}\log^{+}T_{f}(r)}{\log\log r}<1.

(91)

Let

in

. Then, for any

\displaystyle(p-(l-n+1)(n+1)-\varepsilon)T_{f}(r)\leq\sum_{j=1}^{p}\frac{1}{d_{j}}\tilde{N}_{AW}^{[M_{1}]}\left(r,\frac{1}{Q_{j}(\textbf{f})}\right)+S_{\log}(r,f),

where

\displaystyle M_{1}=\lfloor 4(e\lceil l-n+1\rceil(n+1)^{2}d!\lceil\varepsilon^{-1}\rceil)^{n}-1\rfloor.

Abstract
. In this paper, we introduce an optimal control problem for multi-agent systems with non-local cost which favors simultaneous aggregation of particles. This is done introducing a time-dependent notion of multiplicity whose intrinsic dynamical nature differs from more established geometric-like definitions.



1.
Introduction

The optimal control of multi-agent systems in Wasserstein spaces of probability measures has gained significant interest (cf. e.g.
[
2
,
5
,
7
,
10
]
) due to its applications in swarm robotics, crowd dynamics, and data aggregation. In this work, we study an optimal control problem where the cost functional explicitly favors simultaneous aggregation of particles by introducing a time-dependent notion of multiplicity.
The presence of multiplicity in the cost enforces synchronization in the occupation of spatial points over time, leading to novel aggregation effects and forcing, if the time frame allows, sticky particles evolution as shown in Example
2.4
. This behavior is particularly desirable e.g. in applications to car sharing or mailing problems.

Unlike traditional approaches where costs are expressed in dependance on solutions of a continuity inclusion determining the admissible trajectories in
\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, our formulation considers a probability measure on agents/particles’ paths (lifted approach), leveraging the superposition principle from optimal transport theory (cf.
[
7
,
9
]
).
We refer to
[
9
]
for a full-correspondence between these objects, in particular for conditions that ensure the transfer of desired properties to the lifted approach.

We establish, with sufficiently general assumptions, the lower semicontinuity of more general functionals that incorporate non-locality, also in terms of multiplicity of points of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, as well as global and instantaneous velocities of individual paths. Up to our knowledge, this result in such a general framework is new in the literature.

Additionally, we prove the existence of minimizers and establish a Dynamic Programming Principle for the value function of our cost with a time-localized Lagrangian, providing a rigorous foundation for optimal control in this setting. The main results are contained in Sections
3
,
4
.

2.
Preliminaries and settings

Let
(X,d_{X})
and
(Y,d_{Y})
be complete separable metric spaces. Following
[
1
]
, we denote by
\mathscr{P}(X)
the space of probability measures on
X
endowed with the topology of narrow convergence and write
\mu_{n}\rightharpoonup\mu
if the sequence
\{\mu_{n}\}_{n}\subset\mathscr{P}(X)
converges to
\mu
in duality with the set of continuous bounded functions. For future use, we recall that narrow convergence is induced by a distance on
\mathscr{P}(X)
(cf. Remark 5.1.1 in
[
1
]
) and that
\mathscr{P}(X)
is separable.
Given
\mu\in\mathscr{P}(X)
and a Borel function
f:X\to Y
, the push-forward of
\mu
through
f
is denoted by
f_{\sharp}\mu\in\mathscr{P}(Y)
.
The subset of measures in
\mathscr{P}(X)
with finite second moment, endowed with the Wasserstein distance
W_{2}
, is denoted by
\mathscr{P}_{2}(X)
.

For
I=[a,b]
,
a\leq b
, consider
\Gamma_{I}:=C^{0}(I;\mathbb{R}^{d})
endowed with the uniform norm.
Given
t\in I
, we define the linear and continuous map
\mathsf{e}_{t}\colon\Gamma_{I}\to\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma\mapsto\gamma(t)
and the map
\dot{\mathsf{e}}_{t}\colon\Gamma_{I}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
given by

\dot{\mathsf{e}}_{t}(\gamma):=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\lim_{s\to t}\dfrac{\gamma(s)-\gamma(t)}{s-t}=\lim_{s\to t}\dfrac{\mathsf{e}_{s}(\gamma)-\mathsf{e}_{t}(\gamma)}{s-t},&\textrm{ if $\gamma$ is differentiable at $t$,}\\
0,&\textrm{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

The map
\dot{\mathsf{e}}_{t}(\cdot)
is Borel measurable and
\dot{\mathsf{e}}_{t}(\gamma)=\dot{\gamma}(t)
when
\gamma
is differentiable at
t
.

We now present the setting under consideration.

Definition 2.1

(Multiagent dynamics)
.

Let
I=[a,b]
,
a\leq b
. Consider a set-valued map
F:I\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Given a curve
\mathbf{\theta}=\{\theta_{t}\}_{t\in I}\subset\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, let
S^{\mathbf{\theta}}_{I}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightrightarrows\Gamma_{I}
and
\boldsymbol{S}_{I}:\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\rightrightarrows\mathscr{P}(\Gamma_{I})
be the set-valued maps defined by

\displaystyle S^{\mathbf{\theta}}_{I}(x):=

\displaystyle\{\gamma\in AC(I;\mathbb{R}^{d}):\,\dot{\gamma}(t)\in F(t,\gamma(t),\theta_{t})\textrm{ for a.e. $t\in I$},\,\gamma(a)=x\},

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{S}_{I}(\mu):=

\displaystyle\left\{\boldsymbol{\eta}\in\mathscr{P}(\Gamma_{I}):\,\mathrm{supp}\,\big((\mathsf{e}_{a},\mathrm{Id})_{\sharp}\boldsymbol{\eta}\big)\subseteq\mathrm{graph}\left(S^{\{{\mathsf{e}_{t}}_{\sharp}\boldsymbol{\eta}\}_{t\in I}}_{I}\right),\,\mathsf{e}_{a\sharp}\boldsymbol{\eta}=\mu\right\}.

We assume that
\boldsymbol{S}_{I}(\cdot)
has values in
\mathscr{P}_{2}(\Gamma_{I})
and its images are
W_{2}
-compact. These properties are satisfied in particular if
F
has non-empty compact convex images, it is measurable w.r.t.
t\in I
, Lipschitz continuous w.r.t.
(x,\theta)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
uniformly w.r.t.
t\in I
and satisfies an appropriate growth condition. This is discussed e.g. in Section 3 of
[
9
]
(cf. Lemma 3.5, Corollaries 3.6,3.7).

Definition 2.2

(Multiplicity)
.

Given
t\in I
,
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\boldsymbol{\eta}\in\mathscr{P}(\Gamma_{I})
, we define the
multiplicity
of
\boldsymbol{\eta}
at
y
at time
t
by

\mathrm{Mul}_{\boldsymbol{\eta}}(t,y):=\boldsymbol{\eta}\left(\mathsf{e}_{t}^{-1}(y)\right)=\boldsymbol{\eta}\left(\{\gamma\in\Gamma_{I}:\,y=\gamma(t)\}\right)=({\mathsf{e}_{t}}_{\sharp}\boldsymbol{\eta})\left(\{y\}\right).

The multiplicity counts the total mass that passes through the point
y
at the given time
t
. In more established measure theoretic definitions of multiplicity (cf. e.g.
[
3
]
), a point is assigned higher multiplicity even if curves visit it at different times. On the other hand, here we stick with a more dynamical perspective and use a localized in time definition, assigning higher multiplicity only for simultaneous occupations of the point
y
at time
t
.
We now define the cost involved in our optimization problem.

Definition 2.3

(Cost functional and variational problem)
.

Let
I=[a,b]
,
a\leq b
. Consider a sequentially l.s.c. Borel function
G\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to[0,+\infty]
and a Borel function
L:I\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to[0,+\infty]
such that

•

L(t,\cdot,\cdot,\cdot)
is sequentially l.s.c. for a.e.
t\in I
,

•

L(\cdot,x,z,\mu)
is Lebesgue measurable for all
(x,z,\mu)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

•

L(t,x,\cdot,\mu)
is convex for all
(t,x,\mu)\in I\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Given two l.s.c. non-increasing functions
\psi,\phi\colon[0,1]\to[0,+\infty]
and
\boldsymbol{\eta}\in\mathscr{P}(\Gamma_{I})
, we define the costs

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}_{\psi}(\boldsymbol{\eta}):=

\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma_{I}}\int_{I}\psi(\mathrm{Mul}_{\boldsymbol{\eta}}(t,\gamma(t)))\cdot L(t,\gamma(t),\dot{\gamma}(t),{\mathsf{e}_{t}}_{\sharp}\boldsymbol{\eta})\,\mathrm{d}t\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\eta}(\gamma),

\displaystyle\mathscr{G}_{\phi}(\boldsymbol{\eta}):=

\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma_{I}}\phi(\mathrm{Mul}_{\boldsymbol{\eta}}(b,\gamma(b)))\cdot G(\gamma(b),\mathsf{e}_{b\sharp}\boldsymbol{\eta})\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\eta}(\gamma),

\displaystyle\mathscr{E}_{\psi,\phi}(\boldsymbol{\eta}):=

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}_{\psi}(\boldsymbol{\eta})+\mathscr{G}_{\phi}(\boldsymbol{\eta}).

Given
T>0
and
\mu\in\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we are interested in the following problem:

\text{minimize the functional $\mathscr{E}_{\psi,\phi}(\boldsymbol{\eta})$ over all $\boldsymbol{\eta}\in\boldsymbol{S}_{[0,T]}(\mu)$}.

In the next example we show the impact of the time horizon
T
in the structure of minimizers of (
2.3
), due to our choice of multiplicity.

.

, and

, and set

,

.

3.
Existence of minimizers

The following lemma is a crucial ingredient to prove the existence of minimizers in (
2.3
). A similar result is Theorem 1.1 of
[
8
]
, but with a gap in the proof.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\mathbb{X}
be a separable metric space,
\{g_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of Borel functions
g_{n}:\mathbb{X}\to[0,+\infty]
, and
\{\theta_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{X})
,
\theta\in\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{X})

be such that
\theta_{n}\rightharpoonup\theta
as
n\to+\infty
.
Then,

\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{X}}g_{n}(x)\,\mathrm{d}\theta_{n}(x)\geq\int_{\mathbb{X}}g(x)\,\mathrm{d}\theta(x),

where
g(x):=\displaystyle\min\left\{\liminf_{n\to+\infty}g_{n}(x),\liminf_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\to+\infty\\
y\to x\end{subarray}}g_{n}(y)\right\}
.

Proof.

For any
m\in\mathbb{N}
, define

u_{m}(x):=\sup\Big\{\inf\{g_{n}(y):\,y\in U,\,n\in\mathbb{N},\,n\geq m\}:\,U\subseteq\mathbb{X},\,U\textrm{ open},\,x\in U\Big\}.

We prove that
u_{m}
is l.s.c. for any
m\in\mathbb{N}
. For any
\varepsilon>0
there exists an open neighbourhood
U_{\varepsilon}
of
x
such that, for all
z\in U_{\varepsilon}
,

\displaystyle u_{m}(x)

\displaystyle-\varepsilon\leq\inf\{g_{n}(y):\,y\in U_{\varepsilon},\,n\geq m\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup\Big\{\inf\{g_{n}(y):\,y\in W,\,n\geq m\}:\,W\subseteq\mathbb{X},\,W\textrm{ open},\,z\in W\Big\}=u_{m}(z).

The ls.c. of
u_{m}(\cdot)
follows taking the liminf for
z\to x
and letting
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
.

\begin{split}&\sup_{m\in\mathbb{N}}u_{m}(x)=\\
&=\sup\left\{\min\left\{\inf_{n\geq m}g_{n}(x),\inf_{n\geq m}\inf_{y\in U\setminus\{x\}}g_{n}(y)\right\}\right\}:\,\textrm{open }U\subseteq\mathbb{X},\,x\in U,m\in\mathbb{N}\Big\}\\
&=\sup_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\min\left\{\inf_{n\geq m}g_{n}(x),\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}U\textrm{ open}\\
x\in U\end{subarray}}\inf_{n\geq m}\inf_{y\in U\setminus\{x\}}g_{n}(y)\right\}\\
&=\min\left\{\liminf_{n\to+\infty}g_{n}(x),\liminf_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\to+\infty\\
y\to x\end{subarray}}g_{n}(y)\right\}=:g(x).\end{split}

Since
\{u_{m}\}_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
is an increasing seq. of nonnegative Borel functions, with
\sup_{m}u_{m}=g
and
u_{m}\leq g_{m}
, by
Monotone Convergence Thm and Lemma 5.17 in
[
1
]
, we get

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{X}}g(x)\,\mathrm{d}\theta(x)=

\displaystyle\sup_{m}\int_{\mathbb{X}}u_{m}(x)\,\mathrm{d}\theta(x)\leq\sup_{m}\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{X}}u_{m}(x)\,\mathrm{d}\theta_{n}(x)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{X}}u_{n}(x)\,\mathrm{d}\theta_{n}(x)\leq\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{X}}g_{n}(x)\,\mathrm{d}\theta_{n}(x).

∎

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
\mathbb{X}
be a separable metric space,
\Lambda
be a separable topological space,
h:\mathbb{X}\times\Lambda\to[0,+\infty[
be a sequentially l.s.c. map.
Suppose that
\{(\theta_{n},\lambda_{n})\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{X})\times\Lambda
,
(\theta,\lambda)\in\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{X})\times\Lambda
,
satisfies
(\theta_{n},\lambda_{n})\to(\theta,\lambda)
in
\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{X})\times\Lambda
endowed with the product topology.
Then

\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{X}}h(x,\lambda_{n})\,\mathrm{d}\theta_{n}(x)\geq\int_{\mathbb{X}}h(x,\lambda)\,\mathrm{d}\theta(x).

Proof.

Defined,
g_{n}(x):=h(x,\lambda_{n})
,
g_{n}(\cdot)
is a Borel map and we have

\min\left\{\liminf_{n\to+\infty}g_{n}(x),\liminf_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\to+\infty\\
y\to x\end{subarray}}g_{n}(y)\right\}\geq\min\left\{h(x,\lambda),\liminf_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\to+\infty\\
y\to x\end{subarray}}h(y,\lambda_{n})\right\}=h(x,\lambda).

We then apply Lemma
3.1
.
∎

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
\mathbb{X},\mathbb{Y},\mathbb{W}
be separable metric spaces,
f:\mathbb{X}\times\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{W})\times[0,1]\to[0,+\infty]

be sequentially l.s.c. with
f(x,\theta,\cdot)
non-increasing for every
(x,\theta)\in\mathbb{X}\times\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{W})
,
r\in C^{0}(\mathbb{W};\mathbb{Y})
,
C:\mathbb{X}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{Y}
be an u.s.c. set valued map with closed non-empty images.
Then the map
h_{r,C}:\mathbb{X}\times\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{W})\to[0,+\infty]
, defined by
h_{r,C}(x,\theta)=f(x,\theta,r_{\sharp}\theta(C(x)))
, is sequentially l.s.c.

Proof.

Fix
(x,\theta)\in\mathbb{X}\times\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{W})
. Given an open
V\supseteq C(x)
, by the u.s.c. of
C(\cdot)
there exists an open
U_{V}\subseteq\mathbb{X}
,
x\in U_{V}
, such that
C(z)\subseteq V
for all
z\in U_{V}
. In
particular, for any sequence
z_{n}\to x
and
\theta_{n}\rightharpoonup\theta
, since
r_{\sharp}\theta_{n}\rightharpoonup r_{\sharp}\theta
, we can apply Portmanteau theorem (see e.g. Theorem 2.1 in
[
4
]
) to get

\limsup_{n\to+\infty}r_{\sharp}\theta_{n}(C(z_{n}))\leq\limsup_{n\to+\infty}r_{\sharp}\theta_{n}(\overline{V})\leq r_{\sharp}\theta(\overline{V}).

By choosing a decreasing sequence
\{V_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
V_{j}\supseteq C(x)
for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
and
\displaystyle\bigcap_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\overline{V_{j}}=C(x)
,
we obtain
\,\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to+\infty}r_{\sharp}\theta_{n}(C(z_{n}))\leq r_{\sharp}\theta(C(x))
.
Hence,

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to+\infty}h_{r,C}(z_{n},\theta_{n})=\liminf_{n\to+\infty}f(z_{n},\theta_{n},r_{\sharp}\theta_{n}(C(z_{n})))

\displaystyle\geq f\left(x,\theta,\limsup_{n\to+\infty}r_{\sharp}\theta_{n}(C(z_{n}))\right)\geq f(x,\theta,r_{\sharp}\theta(C(x)))=h_{r,C}(x,\theta).

∎

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
I=[a,b]
,
a\leq b
, and
f:I\times\mathscr{P}(\Gamma_{I})\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,1]\to[0,+\infty]
be a Borel map satisfying:

f(t,\cdot,\cdot,\cdot,\cdot)
is sequentially l.s.c. for a.e.
t\in I
,

f(t,\boldsymbol{\eta},y,\cdot,s)
is convex,

f(t,\boldsymbol{\eta},y,z,\cdot)
is non-increasing.
Fix
q\in[1,+\infty[
. Then,

i)

the functional
K:\Gamma_{I}\times L^{q}(I;\mathbb{R}^{d})\times\mathscr{P}(\Gamma_{I})\to[0,+\infty]
, with

K(u,v,\boldsymbol{\eta}):=\int_{I}f(t,\boldsymbol{\eta},u(t),v(t),{\mathsf{e}_{t}}_{\sharp}\boldsymbol{\eta}(\{u(t)\}))\,\mathrm{d}t,

is sequentially l.s.c. w.r.t. the strong topology on
\Gamma_{I}
, the weak topology on
L^{q}(I;\mathbb{R}^{d})
and the narrow topology on
\mathscr{P}(\Gamma_{I})
;

ii)

the map
\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\eta}\mapsto\int_{\Gamma_{I}}\int_{I}f(t,\boldsymbol{\eta},\gamma(t),\dot{\gamma}(t),{\mathsf{e}_{t}}_{\sharp}\boldsymbol{\eta}(\{\gamma(t)\}))\,\mathrm{d}t\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\eta}(\gamma)

is sequentially l.s.c. on the space
\mathscr{P}(W^{1,q}(I;\mathbb{R}^{d}))
.

Proof.

Let
h:I\times\mathscr{P}(\Gamma_{I})\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to[0,+\infty]
be defined by
h(t,\boldsymbol{\eta},y,z):=f(t,\boldsymbol{\eta},y,z,e_{t\sharp}\boldsymbol{\eta}(\{y\}))
.
Then,
h(\cdot)
is a Borel map such that

is convex, and by Proposition
3.3
with
\mathbb{X}=\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\mathbb{W}=\Gamma_{I}
,
\mathbb{Y}=\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
C(x_{1},x_{2})=\{x_{1}\}
,
r=\mathsf{e}_{t}
,
we have that

is sequentially l.s.c.
By Theorem 2.3.1 and Remark 2.2.6 in
[
6
]
, the functional

is sequentially l.s.c. with respect to the desired topologies. Indeed, it is sufficient to consider sequences
\{\boldsymbol{\eta}_{n}\}_{n}\subset\mathscr{P}(\Gamma_{I})
s.t.
\boldsymbol{\eta}_{n}\rightharpoonup\boldsymbol{\eta}
and
\{u_{n}\}_{n}\subset\Gamma_{I}
s.t.
u_{n}\to u
, and
\{v_{n}\}_{n}\subset L^{q}(I;\mathbb{R}^{d})
s.t.
v_{n}\rightharpoonup v
. Then apply the cited results in

6
]
to the sequence
(\xi_{n},v_{n})\in L^{p}(I;\mathscr{P}(\Gamma_{I})\times\mathbb{R}^{d})\times L^{q}(I;\mathbb{R}^{d})

\xi_{n}(t):=(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{n},u_{n}(t))
.
We now prove
ii)
. We can write

\int_{\Gamma_{I}}\int_{I}f(t,\boldsymbol{\eta},\gamma(t),\dot{\gamma}(t),{\mathsf{e}_{t}}_{\sharp}\boldsymbol{\eta}(\{\gamma(t)\}))\,\mathrm{d}t\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\eta}(\gamma)=\int_{\Gamma_{I}}K(\gamma,\dot{\gamma},\boldsymbol{\eta})\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\eta}(\gamma)

and notice that for all
\gamma\in W^{1,q}(I;\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\boldsymbol{\eta}\in\mathscr{P}(W^{1,q}(I;\mathbb{R}^{d}))
the map
(\gamma,\boldsymbol{\eta})\mapsto K(\gamma,\dot{\gamma},\boldsymbol{\eta})
is sequentially l.s.c. by

.
We conclude by Corollary
3.2
, with
\mathbb{X}=W^{1,q}(I;\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\Lambda=L^{q}(I;X)\times\mathscr{P}(W^{1,q}(I;\mathbb{R}^{d}))
endowed with the product weak-narrow topology.
∎

Proposition 3.5
.

.

4.
Dynamic Programming Principle

We conclude proving a Dynamic Programming Principle for the
value function
associated with the optimization problem (
2.3
) and defined by
V_{T}:[0,T]\times\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

V_{T}(t,\mu):=\inf_{\boldsymbol{\eta}\in\boldsymbol{S}_{[t,T]}(\mu)}\mathscr{E}_{\psi,\phi}(\boldsymbol{\eta}).

Definition 4.1

(Restriction and concatenation)
.

Let
I,I_{1},I_{2},J\subseteq\mathbb{R}
be compact intervals,
b\in I
, satisfying
I=I_{1}\cup I_{2}
,
J\subseteq I
,
I_{1}\cap I_{2}=\{b\}
.

(1)

Given
\gamma\in\Gamma_{I}
, we define the
restriction

\gamma|_{J}:J\to X
by
\gamma|_{J}(t)=\gamma(t)
for all
t\in J
.

(2)

Given
\gamma_{i}\in\Gamma_{I_{i}}
,
i=1,2
, with
\gamma_{1}(b)=\gamma_{2}(b)
, we define the
concatenation

\gamma_{1}\oplus\gamma_{2}\in\Gamma_{I}
of
\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}
to be
the unique
\gamma\in\Gamma_{I}
s.t.
\gamma|_{I_{i}}=\gamma_{i}
,
i=1,2
.

(3)

Given
\boldsymbol{\eta}\in\mathscr{P}(\Gamma_{I})
, we define the
restriction

\boldsymbol{\eta}|_{J}
of
\boldsymbol{\eta}
to
J
by setting, for all
\varphi:\Gamma_{J}\to\mathbb{R}
bounded Borel function,

\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma_{J}}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\eta}|_{J}=\int_{\Gamma_{I}}\varphi(\gamma|_{J})\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\eta}(\gamma).

(4)

Given
\boldsymbol{\eta}_{i}\in\mathscr{P}(\Gamma_{I_{i}})
,
i=1,2
, such that
(\mathsf{e}_{b})_{\sharp}\boldsymbol{\eta}_{1}=(\mathsf{e}_{b})_{\sharp}\boldsymbol{\eta}_{2}=:\mu_{b}
, we define the
concatenation

\boldsymbol{\eta}_{1}\oplus\boldsymbol{\eta}_{2}\in\mathscr{P}(\Gamma_{I})

by setting, for all
\varphi:\Gamma_{I}\to\mathbb{R}
bounded Borel function,

\int_{\Gamma_{I}}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{1}\oplus\boldsymbol{\eta}_{2}):=\int_{X}\iint_{\Gamma_{I_{1}}\times\Gamma_{I_{2}}}\varphi(\gamma_{1}\oplus\gamma_{2})\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\eta}_{1,x}(\gamma_{1})\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\eta}_{2,x}(\gamma_{2})\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{b}(x),

where
\{\boldsymbol{\eta}_{i,x}\}_{x\in X}\subset\mathscr{P}(\Gamma_{I_{i}})
,
i=1,2
are the Borel family of measures obtained by disintegrating
\boldsymbol{\eta}_{i}
with respect to the evaluation map
\mathsf{e}_{b}

(cf. Theorem 5.3.1 in
[
1
]
). Since for
\mu_{b}
-a.e.
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\boldsymbol{\eta}_{i,x}
-a.e.
\gamma_{i}\in\mathscr{P}(\Gamma_{I_{i}})
,
i=1,2
, we have
\gamma_{1}(b)=\gamma_{2}(b)=x
,
the integrand is well defined.

The time depended nature of the involved functionals well behaves with respect to restriction and concatenation, as the following proposition shows.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
I,I_{1},I_{2},J\subseteq\mathbb{R}
be compact intervals,
b\in I
, satisfying
I=I_{1}\cup I_{2}
,
J\subseteq I
,
I_{1}\cap I_{2}=\{b\}
.
Then the following hold:

(1)

\mathrm{Mul}_{\boldsymbol{\eta}}(t,y)=\mathrm{Mul}_{\boldsymbol{\eta}|_{J}}(t,y)
, for any
t\in J
,
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
;

(2)

\mathscr{L}_{\psi}(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{1}\oplus\boldsymbol{\eta}_{2})=\mathscr{L}_{\psi}(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{1})+\mathscr{L}_{\psi}(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{2})
for all
\boldsymbol{\eta}_{i}\in\mathscr{P}(\Gamma_{I_{i}})
,
i=1,2
, with
\mathsf{e}_{b\sharp}\boldsymbol{\eta}_{1}=\mathsf{e}_{b\sharp}\boldsymbol{\eta}_{2}
;

(3)

\mathscr{G}_{\phi}(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{1}\oplus\boldsymbol{\eta}_{2})=\mathscr{G}_{\phi}(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{2})
for all
\boldsymbol{\eta}_{i}\in\mathscr{P}(\Gamma_{I_{i}})
,
i=1,2
, with
\mathsf{e}_{b\sharp}\boldsymbol{\eta}_{1}=\mathsf{e}_{b\sharp}\boldsymbol{\eta}_{2}
and
\max\,I=\max\,I_{2}
;

(4)

\mathscr{L}_{\psi}(\boldsymbol{\eta})\geq\mathscr{L}_{\psi}(\boldsymbol{\eta}|_{J})
,
\mathscr{G}_{\phi}(\boldsymbol{\eta})=\mathscr{G}_{\phi}(\boldsymbol{\eta}|_{I_{2}})
, for any
\boldsymbol{\eta}\in\mathscr{P}(\Gamma_{I})
with
\max\,I=\max\,I_{2}
.

Theorem 4.3

(Dynamic Programming Principle)
.

Let
0\leq\tau\leq s\leq T
,
\mu\in\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Then, the following holds

(4.1)

\displaystyle V_{T}(\tau,\mu)=\inf_{\boldsymbol{\eta}\in\boldsymbol{S}_{[\tau,T]}(\mu)}\ell_{\boldsymbol{\eta}}(s),

\displaystyle\ell_{\boldsymbol{\eta}}(s):=\left\{\mathscr{L}_{\psi}(\boldsymbol{\eta}|_{[\tau,s]})+V_{T}(s,{\mathsf{e}_{s}}_{\sharp}\boldsymbol{\eta})\right\}.

Moreover, for any
\boldsymbol{\eta}\in\boldsymbol{S}_{[\tau,T]}(\mu)
the map
s\mapsto\ell_{\boldsymbol{\eta}}(s)
is nondecreasing in
[\tau,T]
, and it is constant if and only if
V_{T}(\tau,\mu)=\mathscr{E}_{\psi,\phi}(\boldsymbol{\eta})
.

Proof.

For any
\varepsilon>0
there exists
\boldsymbol{\eta}^{\varepsilon}\in\boldsymbol{S}_{[\tau,T]}(\mu)
such that

\displaystyle V_{T}

\displaystyle(\tau,\mu)+\varepsilon\geq\mathscr{E}_{\psi,\phi}(\boldsymbol{\eta}^{\varepsilon})=\mathscr{L}_{\psi}(\boldsymbol{\eta}^{\varepsilon}|_{[\tau,s]}\oplus\boldsymbol{\eta}^{\varepsilon}|_{[s,T]})+\mathscr{G}_{\phi}(\boldsymbol{\eta}^{\varepsilon}|_{[s,T]})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}_{\psi}(\boldsymbol{\eta}^{\varepsilon}|_{[\tau,s]})+\mathscr{L}_{\psi}(\boldsymbol{\eta}^{\varepsilon}|_{[s,T]})+\mathscr{G}_{\phi}(\boldsymbol{\eta}^{\varepsilon}|_{[s,T]})=\mathscr{L}_{\psi}(\boldsymbol{\eta}^{\varepsilon}|_{[\tau,s]})+\mathscr{E}_{\psi,\phi}(\boldsymbol{\eta}^{\varepsilon}|_{[s,T]})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}_{\psi}(\boldsymbol{\eta}^{\varepsilon}|_{[\tau,s]})+V_{T}(s,\mathsf{e}_{s\sharp}\boldsymbol{\eta}^{\varepsilon}|_{[s,T]})=\mathscr{L}_{\psi}(\boldsymbol{\eta}^{\varepsilon}|_{[\tau,s]})+V_{T}(s,\mathsf{e}_{s\sharp}\boldsymbol{\eta}^{\varepsilon})=\ell_{\boldsymbol{\eta}^{\varepsilon}}(s).

Thus, we have
\displaystyle V_{T}(\tau,\mu)\geq\inf_{\boldsymbol{\eta}\in\boldsymbol{S}_{[\tau,T]}(\mu)}\ell_{\boldsymbol{\eta}}(s)
.

We prove the converse inequality. Let
\boldsymbol{\eta}\in\boldsymbol{S}_{[\tau,T]}(\mu)
,
\tau\leq s\leq T
, and set
\boldsymbol{\eta}_{1}=\boldsymbol{\eta}|_{[\tau,s]}
. For any
\varepsilon>0
,
there is
\boldsymbol{\eta}_{2}^{\varepsilon}\in S_{[s,T]}(\mathsf{e}_{s\sharp}\boldsymbol{\eta})
such that
V_{T}(s,\mathsf{e}_{s\sharp}\boldsymbol{\eta})+\varepsilon\geq\mathscr{E}_{\psi,\phi}(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{2}^{\varepsilon})
.
Notice that
\mathsf{e}_{s\sharp}\boldsymbol{\eta}_{2}^{\varepsilon}=\mathsf{e}_{s\sharp}\boldsymbol{\eta}
and
\boldsymbol{\eta}_{1}\oplus\boldsymbol{\eta}_{2}^{\varepsilon}\in\boldsymbol{S}_{[\tau,T]}(\mu)
.
Thus,

\displaystyle V_{T}(\tau,\mu)\leq

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}_{\psi}(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{1}\oplus\boldsymbol{\eta}_{2}^{\varepsilon})+\mathscr{G}_{\phi}(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{1}\oplus\boldsymbol{\eta}_{2}^{\varepsilon})=\mathscr{L}_{\psi}(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{1})+\mathscr{L}_{\psi}(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{2}^{\varepsilon})+\mathscr{G}_{\phi}(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{2}^{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}_{\psi}(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{1})+\mathscr{E}_{\psi,\phi}(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{2}^{\varepsilon})\leq\mathscr{L}_{\psi}(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{1})+V_{T}(s,\mathsf{e}_{s\sharp}\boldsymbol{\eta})+\varepsilon

By letting
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
and recalling the definition of
\boldsymbol{\eta}_{1}
, we obtain
V_{T}(\tau,\mu)\leq\mathscr{L}_{\psi}(\boldsymbol{\eta}|_{[\tau,s]})+V_{T}(s,\mathsf{e}_{s\sharp}\boldsymbol{\eta})
.
∎

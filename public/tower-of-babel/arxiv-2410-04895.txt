Abstract
. In this follow-up paper to [ 10 ] we investigate the global topology and geometry of dynamical systems x ˙ = F ⁡ ( x ) \dot{x}=F(x) with entire vector field F F by exploiting and extending the local structure of finite elliptic decompositions. We prove topological properties of the basins of centers, nodes, and foci, while excluding isolated equilibria at the boundaries of the latter two. We propose a definition of global elliptic sectors and introduce the notion of a sector-forming orbit allowing global topological analysis. The structure of heteroclinic regions between two equilibria is characterized.

Definition 2.1
.

We define the basin
\mathcal{V}
of

in

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}:=\{a\}\cup\left\{x\in\mathbb{C}:\Gamma(x)\text{ is periodic with }a\in\Int(\Gamma)\right\}.

The boundary of

Proposition 2.2
.

\mathring{\mathcal{V}}
is nonempty.
\mathcal{V}
and
\partial\mathcal{V}
are flow-invariant. It holds

(2.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}=\bigcup_{\mathclap{x\in\mathcal{V}}}\overline{\Int(\Gamma(x))}\text{.}

Proof.

[
10

ensures

, i.e.
\mathring{\mathcal{V}}\not=\emptyset
. Let
y\in\mathcal{V}
and
z\in\Gamma(y)
. Then
\Gamma(y)=\Gamma(z)\subset\mathcal{V}
. Thus
\mathcal{V}
is flow-invariant. Every
y\in\Int(\Gamma(x))
either satisfies
y=a
, or, by
[
10

, is a periodic orbit with
a
in its interior. This proves equation (
2.1
). By
[

, it follows that also
\partial\mathcal{V}
is flow-invariant.
∎

Proposition 2.3
.

It holds
\partial\mathcal{V}\cap F^{-1}(\{0\})=\emptyset
, i.e. there are no equilibria on the boundary of
\mathcal{V}
.

Proof.

The case
\partial\mathcal{V}=\emptyset
is trivial, so let
x\in\partial\mathcal{V}
and assume this is an equilibrium. For all
\varepsilon>0
there exists
y\in\mathcal{B}_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\mathcal{V}
such that
\Gamma(y)
neither has only
x
in its limit sets nor is a periodic orbit with
x
in its interior. Note that
x\not=a
, since
a\in\mathring{\mathcal{V}}
. Hence,
x
cannot be a center, focus or node. By
[
10
, Theorem 4.4]
,

has a FED. In particular, by
[
10
, Definition 4.1]
, there exists
r>0
such that
x\in w_{+}(\Gamma(z))\cup w_{-}(\Gamma(z))
for all

\mathcal{B}_{r}(x)\cap\mathcal{V}\not=\emptyset

Theorem 2.4
.

\mathcal{V}
is open.

Proof.

Suppose there exists
x\in\mathcal{V}\cap\partial\mathcal{V}\not=\emptyset
,
x\not=a
. Then
\Gamma(x)\subset\mathcal{V}\cap\partial\mathcal{V}
, cf. Proposition
2.2
. In particular,
\Gamma(x)
is a periodic orbit with
a\in\Int(\Gamma(x))\not=\emptyset
. Suppose there also exists
y\in\partial\mathcal{V}\setminus\Gamma(x)\not=\emptyset
. The set
\mathbb{C}\setminus\Gamma(x)=\Int(\Gamma(x))\cup\Ext(\Gamma(x))
is open. If
y\in\Int(\Gamma(x))
, there exists
r>0
such that
\mathcal{B}_{r}(y)\subset\Int(\Gamma(x))
. By equation (
2.1
), we get
\mathcal{B}_{r}(y)\subset\mathcal{V}
, i.e.
y\not\in\partial\mathcal{V}
. This a contradiction. Furthermore, if
y\in\Ext(\Gamma(x))
, there exists again an
r>0
such that
\mathcal{B}_{r}(y)\subset\Ext(\Gamma(x))
. Since
y
lies on the boundary of
a
, there exists
z\in\mathcal{B}_{r}(y)\cap\mathcal{V}
, i.e.
\Gamma(z)
is a periodic orbit with
a\in\Int(\Gamma(z))
and
\Gamma(z)\not=\Gamma(x)
. Since
\Gamma(z)
is path-connected and
z\in\Ext(\Gamma(x))
, we must have
x\in\Int(\Gamma(z))
. By equation (
2.1
) and openness of
\Int(\Gamma(z))
, we derive the contradiction
x\in\mathring{\mathcal{V}}
. Thus,
y\not\in\Int(\Gamma(x))\cup\Ext(\Gamma(x))
, which is also a contradiction. We get
\partial\mathcal{V}\setminus\Gamma(x)=\emptyset
, i.e. the boundary of
a
consists exactly of the unique outermost periodic orbit
\Gamma(x)
and
\mathcal{V}=\overline{\Int(\Gamma(x))}
must be compact.

By equation (
2.1
),
\Gamma(x)
is not isolated. Hence we can apply a result on limit cycles of analytic dynamical systems,
[
2
, p. 203, §12.1, Lemma 1]
, to deduce the existence of
\rho>0
such that all orbits through the set
U:=\{y\in\mathbb{C}{}:\text{dist}(\Gamma(x),y)<\rho\}\cap\Ext(\Gamma(x))
are periodic. Reduce
\rho
such that
\overline{\Int(\Gamma(x))}\cup U
is simply connected and
U\cap F^{-1}(\{0\})=\emptyset
. By continuity of the flow, cf.
[
19
, Chapter 2.4, Theorem 4]
, we deduce the existence of at least one periodic orbit
\Gamma\subset U
. By choice of
\rho
, we get
\Int(\Gamma)\subset U\cup\overline{\Int(\Gamma(x))}
and thus
a\in\Int(\Gamma)
, cf.
[
2
, §11, Corollary 1]
and
[
3
, Theorem 2.4]
. This is a contradiction to
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{C}{}\setminus\mathcal{V}
. Hence the assumed
x
cannot exist and
\mathcal{V}
is open.
∎

Theorem 2.5
.

\mathcal{V}
is connected, path-connected and simply connected.

Proof.

The interior of any periodic orbit is clearly connected. Hence, by Proposition
2.2
,
\mathcal{V}
can be interpreted as union of connected sets, all having the point
a
in common. Thus, also the union must be connected, cf.
[
18
, Theorem 23.3]
. By
[
23
, Proposition 12.25]
,
\mathcal{V}
is even path-connected.

Let
L\subset\mathcal{V}
be a loop (closed path). By openness of
\mathcal{V}
, cf. Theorem
2.4
, for all
x\in L
there exists a
y_{x}\in\Ext(\Gamma(x))\cap\mathcal{V}\not=\emptyset
such that
x\in\Int(\Gamma(y_{x}))\subset\mathcal{V}
. Hence, the system
\left\{\Int(\Gamma(y_{x})):x\in L\right\}
is an open cover of
L
. Since
L
is compact, there exist finitely many points
x_{1},\ldots,x_{\ell}\in L
,
\ell\in\mathbb{N}{}
, such that

\displaystyle L\subset\bigcup_{k=1}^{\ell}\Int(\Gamma(y_{x_{k}}))\subset\mathcal{V}\text{.}

Since
\ell
is finite,
y\in\left\{y_{x_{k}}:1\leq k\leq\ell\right\}
must exist such that
\Gamma(y)
is the outermost periodic orbit having the other
\ell-1
periodic orbits in its interior. By equation (
2.1
), we conclude
L\subset\Int(\Gamma(y))\subset\mathcal{V}
with
y\in\mathcal{V}
. Since
\Int(\Gamma(y))
is clearly simply connected,
L
can be continuously (homotopically) deformed to a point in
L\subset\mathcal{V}
. Since
\mathcal{V}
is already path-connected, it follows that
\mathcal{V}
is simply connected.
∎

Theorem 2.6
.

All orbits on the boundary of
a
are unbounded.
\mathcal{V}
is unbounded.

Proof.

If the boundary of
a
is empty, then
\mathcal{V}
is open, closed and nonempty, i.e.
\mathcal{V}=\mathbb{C}{}
and thus unbounded. Assume
\partial\mathcal{V}\not=\emptyset
and let
x\in\partial\mathcal{V}
. Suppose that
\Gamma(x)\subset\partial\mathcal{V}
is bounded. By Proposition
2.3
, we get
w_{\pm}(\Gamma(x))\cap F^{-1}(\{0\})=\emptyset
. Hence, by
[
10
, Corollary 5.2]
,
\Gamma(x)
must be periodic. By
[
10
, Theorem 5.1]
,
\Gamma(x)
has exactly one equilibrium, a center
\tilde{a}
, in
\Int(\Gamma(x))
. If
\tilde{a}\not=a
,
x\in\partial\mathcal{V}
would be a point in the basin in
\tilde{a}
, which is a contradiction to the openness of this basin, cf. Theorem
2.4
. If
a=\tilde{a}
, we get
x\in\mathcal{V}
, which is again a contradiction, since
\mathcal{V}\cap\partial\mathcal{V}=\emptyset
. Thus,
\Gamma(x)
and
\mathcal{V}
must be unbounded.
∎

3.
Global elliptic sectors

In all claims and arguments of this chapter, we consider an entire function
F\in\mathcal{O}{}(\mathbb{C})
,
F\not\equiv 0

of (

\lambda:\mathcal{E}(F,m)\to\{-1,1\}

\lambda(\theta):=\cos(\argg(F^{(m)}(a))+\theta(m-1))

In the following, we will extend the analysis of the local structure described in
[

Definition 3.1
.

(i)

Let
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{C}
be an orbit and

. We call
\Gamma

(\theta_{+},\theta_{-})
-elliptic in

\theta_{+},\theta_{-}\in\mathcal{E}(F,m)

for

\Gamma

(ii)

Let
\Xi\subset\mathbb{C}
be an orbit, which is
(\theta_{+},\theta_{-})
-elliptic in
a

\Xi

is also
(\theta_{+},\theta_{-})
-elliptic in
a
.

(iii)

Let
\Xi
be a sector-forming orbit in
a
with adjacent definite directions
\theta_{+},\theta_{-}\in\mathcal{E}(F,m)
satisfying
\lambda(\theta_{\pm})=\mp 1
.

and

Let

Let
\theta_{+},\theta_{-}\in\mathcal{E}(F,m)

By

Let

Proposition 3.5

\mathcal{S}:=\mathcal{S}(\theta_{+},\theta_{-},\Xi)

\theta_{+},\theta_{-}\in\mathcal{E}(F,m)
,

Let

5.
Heteroclinic regions

In this chapter we consider an entire function
F\in\mathcal{O}{}(\mathbb{C}{})
,
F\not\equiv 0
, and at least two equilibria
a_{+},a_{-}\in\mathbb{C}{}
of (
1.1
) with at least one heteroclinic orbit connecting these two equilibria. In particular, we assume that
a_{\pm}
is not a center. Under these assumptions, it is possible to investigate topologically another characteristic set, the so-called "heteroclinic region". Taking up the questions at the end of chapter 3, a heteroclinic region is one possible structure between two adjacent global elliptic sectors. We analyse it topologically and illustrate it by an example.

Definition 5.1
.

We define the heteroclinic region
\mathcal{H}
of
F
between
a_{+}
and
a_{-}
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}:=\mathcal{H}_{+}\cap\mathcal{H}_{-}

with

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{+(-)}:=\left\{x\in\mathbb{C}{}:w_{+(-)}(\Gamma(x))=\{a_{+(-)}\}\right\}\text{.}

Proposition 5.2
.

\mathcal{H}
and
\partial\mathcal{H}
are flow-invariant. It holds

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}=\bigcup_{\mathclap{x\in\mathcal{H}}}\Gamma(x)\text{.}

Proof.

This proof is analogous to the proof of Proposition
2.2
.
∎

Theorem 5.3
.

\mathcal{H}
is open.

Proof.

Both,
a_{+}
as well
a_{-}
is either a focus or node or an equilibrium of order
m\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{1\}
. Let
x\in A_{+}
and assume that
a_{+}
is an equilibrium of order
m\geq 2
. By
[
10
, Proposition 4.3]
, there exist numbers
r,\theta,\Theta,\tau>0
such that for all
z\in\{\hat{z}\in\mathbb{C}{}:|\hat{z}-a_{+}|<r,|\arg(\hat{z}-a_{+})-\theta|<\Theta\}=:A
the orbit through
z
tends to
a
for
t\to\infty
and
M:=\Phi(\tau,x)\in A
. Since
A
is open, we can choose
\varepsilon>0
small enough such that
\mathcal{B}_{\varepsilon}(M)\subset A
. By continuity of the flow, cf.
[
19
, Chapter 2.4, Theorem 4]
, there exists
\delta>0
such that
|\Phi(\tau,x)-\Phi(\tau,y)|<\varepsilon
for all
y\in\mathcal{B}_{\delta}(x)
, i.e.
\mathcal{B}_{\delta}(x)\subset\mathcal{H}_{+}
and
\mathcal{H}_{+}
is open. Additionally, if
a_{+}
is a node or focus, then we can apply Theorem
4.3
to conclude again the openness of
\mathcal{H}_{+}
. Since the same argumentation holds for
a_{-}
,
\mathcal{H}_{-}
is also open. It follows that
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{+}\cap\mathcal{H}_{-}
is open.
∎

Lemma 5.4
.

The basins of all centers, foci and nodes
a\in F^{-1}(\{0\})\setminus\{a_{+},a_{-}\}
have empty intersection with
\mathcal{H}
. In particular, there are no centers, nodes and foci on
\partial\mathcal{H}
.

Proof.

There are no periodic orbits in
\mathcal{H}
. Additionally,
a\not\in\{a_{+},a_{-}\}
. Thus, this proof is analogous to the proof of Lemma
4.4
.
∎

Proposition 5.5
.

It holds
\partial\mathcal{H}\cap F^{-1}(\{0\})=\{a_{+},a_{-}\}
.

Proof.

Since there exists at least one heteroclinic orbit in
\mathcal{H}
connecting
a_{+}
and
a_{-}
, we have
\{a_{+},a_{-}\}\subset\partial\mathcal{H}
. Suppose there exists an equilibrium
a\in\partial\mathcal{H}\setminus\{a_{+},a_{-}\}
. By Lemma
5.4
,
a
is an equilibrium of order
m\geq 2
, i.e.
a
has a FED, cf.
[
10
, Theorem 4.4]
. In particular, by
[
10
, Definition 4.1]
, there exists
r>0
such that
a\in w_{+}(\Gamma(x))\cup w_{-}(\Gamma(x))
for all
x\in\mathcal{B}_{r}(a)
. We get
\mathcal{B}_{r}(a)\cap\mathcal{H}\not=\emptyset
, which is also impossible. Thus, we conclude
\partial\mathcal{H}\cap F^{-1}(\{0\})=\{a_{+},a_{-}\}
.
∎

Theorem 5.6
.

All orbits in
\partial\mathcal{H}\setminus\{a_{+},a_{-}\}
are unbounded.
\mathcal{H}
is unbounded.

Proof.

If
\partial\mathcal{H}=\{a_{+},a_{-}\}
, then
\mathcal{H}\not=\emptyset
is open and closed with respect to the subspace topology in
\mathbb{C}{}\setminus\{a_{+},a_{-}\}
.
12
12

12

This argument is similar to the beginning of the proof of Proposition
4.5
.
Since
\mathbb{C}{}\setminus\{a_{+},a_{-}\}
is connected,
\mathcal{H}=\mathbb{C}{}\setminus\{a_{+},a_{-}\}
is clearly unbounded. Thus, assume
\partial\mathcal{H}\setminus\{a_{+},a_{-}\}\not=\emptyset
.

Suppose there exists a bounded orbit
\Gamma\subset\partial\mathcal{H}\setminus\{a_{+},a_{-}\}
. As in the proof of Theorem
2.6
,
K:=\overline{\Gamma}
is compact and
\Gamma\cup w_{\pm}(\Gamma)\subset K\subset\partial\mathcal{H}
. By Lemma
5.4
,
\Gamma
cannot be periodic. Hence, by Proposition
5.5
and
[
10
, Theorem 5.1]
, we must have
w_{+}(\Gamma)\cup w_{-}(\Gamma)=\{a_{+},a_{-}\}
. Using this result, we want to derive a contradiction.

If
w_{+}(\Gamma)=\{a_{+}\}
and
w_{-}(\Gamma)=\{a_{-}\}
, then we get
\Gamma\subset\mathcal{H}
, which is contraction to Theorem
5.3
. If
w_{+}(\Gamma)=\{a_{-}\}
and
w_{-}(\Gamma)=\{a_{+}\}
, then there exists another heteroclinic region
\tilde{\mathcal{H}}
between
a_{-}
and

. By Theorem

,
\tilde{\mathcal{H}}
is open, i.e.
\tilde{\mathcal{H}}\cap\mathcal{H}\not=\emptyset

\Gamma
must be homoclinic. Assume w.l.o.g. that
\Gamma
is homoclinic in
a_{+}
, i.e.
a_{+}
is an equilibrium of order

such that
\Gamma(x)

\mathcal{S}(\theta_{+},\theta_{-},\Gamma(x))\cap\mathcal{H}\not=\emptyset

. This is a contradiction.
We conclude that
\Gamma
cannot be bounded. Moreover, since
\partial\mathcal{H}\not=\{a_{+},a_{-}\}
, there exists at least one unbounded orbit on
\partial\mathcal{H}
. Therefore, it follows that
\mathcal{H}
is unbounded.
∎

Corollary 5.7
.

Let
p\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{1\}
. It is not possible to find
p
equilibria
a_{1},\ldots,a_{p}\in F^{-1}(\{0\})
and
p
heteroclinic orbits
\Gamma_{1},\ldots,\Gamma_{p}
such that
\Gamma_{p}
is a heteroclinic orbit connecting
a_{p}
and
a_{1}
and
\Gamma_{j}
is a heteroclinic orbit connecting
a_{j}
and
a_{j+1}
for all
j\in\{1,\ldots,p\}
.

Proof.

Suppose we find
p
equilibria and
p

is a closed piecewise continuously differentiable Jordan curve. Let
\mathcal{H}_{j}
be the heteroclinic region defined by
\Gamma_{j}
. Define the two open sets
U:=\mathcal{H}_{1}
and
V:=\mathcal{H}_{2}\cup\ldots\cup\mathcal{H}_{p}
, cf. Theorem
5.3
. Then there holds
U\cap V=\emptyset
as well as, by Theorem
5.3
,
U\cap\Int(J)\not=\emptyset
and
V\cap\Int(J)\not=\emptyset
. Since
\Int(J)
is connected, we get
U\cup V\not=\Int(J)
and thus there exists
x\in\partial U\cap\Int(J)\not=\emptyset
. By Theorem
5.6
,
\Gamma(x)\subset\partial\mathcal{H}_{1}\cap\Int(J)
is unbounded. This is a contradiction.
∎

Theorem 5.8
.

\mathcal{H}
is connected, path-connected and simply connected.

Proof.

For
x\in\mathcal{H}
define
A_{x}:=\Gamma(x)\cup\{a_{+}\}
and the cover

of
\mathcal{H}
. Since
a_{+}
lies on the boundary of each orbit in
\mathcal{H}

[

[
18

\mathcal{H}
is connected. By
[
23

\mathcal{H}
is even path-connected.

Let

be a loop. Analogous to the beginning of the proof of Theorem
4.7

Let

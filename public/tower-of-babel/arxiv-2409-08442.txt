1.
Introduction

In 1944 Atle Selberg proved the following integral formula:

(1.1)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\dots\int_{0}^{1}\prod_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant n}(x_{i}-x_{j})^{2\gamma}\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}^{\alpha-1}(1-x_{i})^{\beta-1}\ dx_{1}\dots dx_{n}

\displaystyle\phantom{aaaaaa}=\ \prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\Gamma(1+j\gamma)}{\Gamma(1+\gamma)}\,\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+(j-1)\gamma)\,\Gamma(\beta+(j-1)\gamma)}{\Gamma(\alpha+\beta+(n+j-2)\gamma)}\,,

see
[
Se
,
AAR
]
.
Hundreds of papers are devoted to the generalizations of the Selberg
integral formula and its applications, see for example
[
AAR
,
FW
]
and references
therein. There are
q
-analysis versions of the formula,
the generalizations associated with Lie algebras, elliptic versions, finite field versions,
see some references in
[
AAR
,
FW
]
.
In the finite field versions,
one considers additive and multiplicative characters of a finite field, which
map the field to the field of
complex numbers, and forms an analog of equation (
1.1
), in which both sides are complex numbers.
The simplest of such formulas is the classical relation between Jacobi and Gauss
sums, see
[
AAR
]
.

In
[
RV1
]
, another version of the Selberg integral formula
was presented, in which the

{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-Selberg integral is an element of the finite field
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
with an odd prime number
p

of elements, see also
[
RV2
]
. Given non-negative integers
a,b,c
, consider the master polynomial

\Phi_{n}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
,

\displaystyle\Phi_{n}=\prod_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant n}(x_{i}-x_{j})^{2c}\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}^{a}(1-x_{i})^{b}.

Denote by
\bar{S}(a,b,c)
the coefficient of the monomial
x_{1}^{p-1}\dots x_{n}^{p-1}
in
\Phi_{n}

and call it the
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-Selberg integral.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
RV1
, Theorem 4.1]
)
.

Assume that
a,b,c
are non-negative integers such that

(1.2)

\displaystyle p-1\leqslant a+b+(n-1)c,\qquad a+b+(2n-2)c<2p-1\ .

Then we have a formula in
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
:

(1.3)

\displaystyle\bar{S}(a,b,c)=(-1)^{n}\,\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{(jc)!}{c!}\,\frac{(a+(j-1)c)!\,(b+(j-1)c)!}{(a+b+(n+j-2)c+1-p)!}\,.

The master polynomial
\Phi_{n}
is an analog of the integrand in (
1.1
).
The operation of choosing the coefficient
of
x_{1}^{p-1}\dots x_{n}^{p-1}
in
\Phi_{n}
is an analog of the integration
of
\Psi_{n}
over a cycle
due to the following Stokes-like observation. For any
\Psi\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
and any positive
integers
l_{1},\dots,l_{n}
, the coefficient of

x_{1}^{l_{1}p-1}\dots x_{n}^{l_{n}p-1}
in any first partial derivative

\frac{\partial\Psi}{\partial x_{i}}
equals zero.

In this paper we consider the case
n=2
. For positive integers

a,b,c
,
l_{1},l_{2}
we denote by
\bar{S}(a,b,c;l_{1},l_{2})
the coefficient of

x_{1}^{l_{1}p-1}x_{2}^{l_{2}p-1}
in
\Phi_{2}
and call it a two-dimensional
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-Selberg integral.
Clearly,
\bar{S}(a,b,c;l_{1},l_{2})=\bar{S}(a,b,c;l_{2},l_{1})
,

We assume that
0<a,b,c<p
and evaluate
\bar{S}(a,b,c;l_{1},l_{2})
in all non-zero cases. This is the main result of this paper.
It is interesting that in all cases, the
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-Selberg integral

\bar{S}(a,b,c;l_{1},l_{2})
is given by a formula analogous to formula (
1.3
) with some shifts
by
p
in factorials.

Here, in the introduction we formulate a theorem that
lists all the integers
0<a,b,c<p
such that there are more than one pair

with non-zero

\bar{S}(a,b,c;l_{1},l_{2})

3.13
.

Theorem 1.2
.

If there are more than one pair
l_{1}\leqslant l_{2}
such that

\bar{S}(a,b,c;l_{1},l_{2})
is non-zero, then all such
(a,b,c;l_{1},l_{2})
are listed below.

(i)

If
2c<p,\ a+c\leqslant p-1,\ b+c\geqslant p,\ a+b+2c\geqslant 2p-1
,
then

\bar{S}(a,b,c;1,1)
,
\bar{S}(a,b,c;1,2)
,
\bar{S}(a,b,c;2,1)
are non-zero and

(1.4)

\displaystyle\phantom{aaaa}-\frac{1}{2}\,\bar{S}(a,b,c;1,1)=\bar{S}(a,b,c;1,2)=\bar{S}(a,b,c;2,1),

(1.5)

\displaystyle\bar{S}(a,b,c;1,1)=\frac{(2c)!}{c!}\,\frac{a!\,(a+c)!\,b!\,(b+c-p)!}{(a+b+c-p+1)!\,(a+b+2c-2p+1)!}\,.

(ii)

If
2c<p,\ a+b+c\geqslant 2p-1
,
then

\bar{S}(a,b,c;2,2)
,
\bar{S}(a,b,c;1,2)
,
\bar{S}(a,b,c;2,1)

(1.6)

\displaystyle\phantom{aaaa}-\frac{1}{2}\,\bar{S}(a,b,c;2,2)=\bar{S}(a,b,c;1,2)=\bar{S}(a,b,c;2,1).

(1.7)

\displaystyle\bar{S}(a,b,c;2,2)=-\frac{(2c)!}{c!}\,\frac{a!\,(a+c-p)!\,b!\,(b+c-p)!}{(a+b+c-2p+1)!\,(a+b+2c-2p+1)!}\,.

(iii)

If
2c>p,\quad a+b+2c\geqslant 3p-1
,
then

\bar{S}(a,b,c;2,2)
,
\bar{S}(a,b,c;1,3)
,
\bar{S}(a,b,c;3,1)

are non-zero and

(1.8)

\displaystyle\phantom{aaaa}-\frac{1}{2}\,\bar{S}(a,b,c;2,2)=\bar{S}(a,b,c;1,3)=\bar{S}(a,b,c;3,1),

(1.9)

\displaystyle\bar{S}(a,b,c;2,2)=-\frac{(2c-p)!}{c!}\,\frac{a!\,(a+c-p)!\,b!\,(b+c-p)!}{(a+b+c-2p+1)!\,(a+b+2c-3p+1)!}\,.

If
(a,b,c)
does not satisfy the system of inequalities,

2c<p,\ a+c\leqslant p-1,\ b+c\geqslant p,\ a+b+2c\geqslant 2p-1
,
and does not satisfy the system of inequalities

2c<p,\ a+b+c\geqslant 2p-1
,
and does not satisfy the system of inequalities,

2c>p,\ a+b+2c\geqslant 3p-1
,
then there exists at most one pair

(l_{1},l_{2})
with
l_{1}\leqslant l_{2}
such that

2.
Preliminary remarks

2.1.
Lucas’ Theorem

Theorem 2.1

(
[
L
]
)
.

For non-negative integers
m
and
n
and a prime
p
,
the following congruence relation holds:

(2.1)

\displaystyle\binom{n}{m}\equiv\prod_{i=0}^{a}\binom{n_{i}}{m_{i}}\quad(\operatorname{mod}\ p),

where
m=m_{b}p^{b}+m_{b-1}p^{b-1}+\cdots+m_{1}p+m_{0}

and
n=n_{b}p^{b}+n_{b-1}p^{b-1}+\cdots+n_{1}p+n_{0}

are the base
p
expansions of
m
and
n
respectively. This uses the convention that

\binom{n}{m}=0
if
n<m
.
∎

2.2.
Cancellation of factorials

Lemma 2.2
.

If
a,b
are non-negative integers and
a+b=p-1
, then in
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
we have

(2.2)

\displaystyle a!\,b!\,=\,(-1)^{a+1}\,.

Proof.

We have
a!=(-1)^{a}(p-1)\dots(p-a)
and
p-a=b+1
. Hence

a!\,b!=(-1)^{a}(p-1)!=(-1)^{a+1}
by Wilson’s Theorem.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
a,b
be positive integers such that
a<p
,
b<p
,
p\leqslant a+b
. Then we have an identity in
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
,

(2.3)

\displaystyle b\,\binom{b-1}{a+b-p}=b\,\binom{b-1}{p-a-1}=(-1)^{a+1}\,\frac{a!\,b!}{(a+b-p)!}\,.

∎

2.3.
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-Integrals

Let
M
be an
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-module. Let
P(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})
be a polynomial
with coefficients in
M
,

(2.4)

\displaystyle P(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})=\sum_{d}\,c_{d}\,x_{1}^{d_{1}}\dots x_{k}^{d_{k}}.

Let
l=(l_{1},\dots,l_{k})\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}^{k}
. We call the coefficient

c_{l_{1}p-1,\dots,l_{k}p-1}
the
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-integral
of the polynomial
P
over the cycle
[l_{1},\dots,l_{k}]_{p}

and denoted it by
\int_{[l_{1},\dots,l_{k}]_{p}}P(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})\,dx_{1}\dots dx_{k}
.

Lemma 2.4
.

For any
i=1,\dots,k
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{[l_{1},\dots,l_{k}]_{p}}\frac{\partial P}{\partial x_{i}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})\,dx_{1}\dots dx_{k}=0\,.

∎

2.4.
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-Beta integral

For non-negative integers
a,b
the classical beta integral formula says

(2.5)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}x^{a}(1-x)^{b}dx=\frac{a!\,b!}{(a+b+1)!}\,.

Lemma 2.5

(
[
V
]
)
.

Let

0\leqslant a,b<p
,
p-1\leqslant a+b
.
Then in
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
we have

(2.6)

\displaystyle\int_{[1]_{p}}x^{a}(1-x)^{b}dx\,=\,-\,\frac{a!\,b!}{(a+b-p+1)!}\,.

If
a+b<p-1
, then

(2.7)

\displaystyle\int_{[1]_{p}}x^{a}(1-x)^{b}dx\,=0\,.

Proof.

We have

x^{a}(1-x)^{b}=\sum_{k=0}^{b}(-1)^{k}\binom{b}{k}x^{k}\,,

and need
a+k=p-1
. Hence
k=p-1-a
and

\displaystyle\int_{[1]_{p}}x^{a}(1-x)^{b}dx\,=(-1)^{p-1-a}\binom{b}{p-1-a}.

Now Lemma
2.3
implies (
2.6
). Formula (
2.7
) is clear.
∎

2.5.
Morris’ identity

Suppose that
\alpha,\beta,\gamma
are non-negative integers. Then

(2.8)

\displaystyle\operatorname{CT}\,\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-x_{i})^{\alpha}(1-1/x_{i})^{\beta}\prod_{1\leqslant j\neq k\leqslant n}(1-x_{j}/x_{k})^{\gamma}

\displaystyle\phantom{a}=\,\ \prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{(j\gamma)!}{\gamma!}\,\frac{(\alpha+\beta+(j-1)\gamma)!}{(\alpha+(j-1)\gamma)!\,(\beta+(j-1)\gamma)!}\,,

where CT denotes the constant term.
Morris identity was deduced in
[
Mo
]
from the integral formula
for the classical Selberg integral, see
[
AAR
, Section 8.8]
.

The left-hand side of (
2.8
) can be written as

(2.9)

\displaystyle\operatorname{CT}\,(-1)^{\binom{n}{2}\gamma+n\beta}\prod_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant n}(x_{i}-x_{j})^{2\gamma}\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}^{-\beta-(n-1)\gamma}(1-x_{i})^{\alpha+\beta}\,.

3.
2D

{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-Selberg integrals

3.1.
Definition

For integers
a,b,c
,

(3.1)

\displaystyle 0\ <\ a,b,c\ <\ p,

introduce the master polynomial

\displaystyle\Phi(x_{1},x_{2};a,b,c)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x_{1}-x_{2})^{2c}\prod_{i=1}^{2}x_{i}^{a}(1-x_{i})^{b}

as a polynomial in
{\mathbb{Z}}[x_{1},x_{2}]
.
For positive integers
l_{1},l_{2}
, denote by
S(a,b,c;l_{1},l_{2})
the coefficient of
x_{1}^{l_{1}p-1}x_{2}^{l_{2}p-1}

in
\Phi(x_{1},x_{2};a,b,c)
. Denote by
\bar{S}(a,b,c;l_{1},l_{2})
the projection of

S(a,b,c;l_{1},l_{2})
to
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
.

We have in
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
,

\displaystyle\bar{S}(a,b,c;l_{1},l_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{[l_{1},l_{2}]_{p}}\Phi(x_{1},x_{2};a,b,c)dx_{1}dx_{2}\,.

The element
\bar{S}(a,b,c;l_{1},l_{2})
is
called
a two-dimensional
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-Selberg integral.

We have
S(a,b,c;l_{1},l_{2})=S(a,b,c;l_{2},l_{1})

since
\Phi(x_{1},x_{2};a,b,c)=\Phi(x_{2},x_{1};a,b,c)
.

3.2.
Recursion

Denote

\displaystyle\bar{S}_{1}(a,b,c;l_{1},l_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{[l_{1},l_{2}]_{p}}(x_{1}+x_{2})\Phi(x;a,b,c)dx_{1}dx_{2}\,,

\displaystyle\bar{S}_{2}(a,b,c;1,2)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{[l_{1},l_{2}]_{p}}((1-x_{1})+(1-x_{2}))\Phi(x;a,b,c)dx_{1}dx_{2}\,.

These are elements of
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
.

Theorem 3.1
.

We have

(3.2)

\displaystyle(a+1)\,\bar{S}_{1}(a,b,c;l_{1},l_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2(a+b+c+2)\,\bar{S}(a+1,b,c;l_{1},l_{2}),

(3.3)

\displaystyle 2(a+c+1)\,\bar{S}(a,b,c;l_{1},l_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(a+b+2c+2)\,\bar{S}_{1}(a,b,c;l_{1},l_{2}),

(3.4)

\displaystyle(b+1)\,\bar{S}_{2}(a,b,c;l_{1},l_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2(a+b+c+2)\,\bar{S}(a,b+1,c;l_{1},l_{2}),

(3.5)

\displaystyle 2(b+c+1)\,\bar{S}(a,b,c;l_{1},l_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(a+b+2c+2)\,\bar{S}_{2}(a,b,c;l_{1},l_{2}).

Proof.

The proof is similar to the proofs in
[
RV1
, Section 4.4]
.
Adding the equations

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{[l_{1},l_{2}]_{p}}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}}\big[(1-x_{1})x_{1}x_{2}\Phi\big]dx_{1}dx_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{[l_{1},l_{2}]_{p}}\Phi\Big[-(b+1)x_{1}x_{2}+(a+1)(1-x_{1})x_{2}+2c\frac{x_{1}x_{2}(1-x_{1})}{x_{1}-x_{2}}\Big]dx_{1}dx_{2}\,,

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{[l_{1},l_{2}]_{p}}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{2}}\big[(1-x_{2})x_{1}x_{2}\Phi\big]dx_{1}dx_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{[l_{1},l_{2}]_{p}}\Phi\Big[-(b+1)x_{1}x_{2}+(a+1)(1-x_{2})x_{1}+2c\frac{x_{1}x_{2}(1-x_{2})}{x_{2}-x_{1}}\Big]dx_{1}dx_{2}\,

we obtain (
3.2
). Adding the equations

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{[l_{1},l_{2}]_{p}}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}}\big[(1-x_{1})x_{1}\Phi\big]dx_{1}dx_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{[l_{1},l_{2}]_{p}}\Phi\Big[-(b+1)x_{1}+(a+1)(1-x_{1})+2c\frac{x_{1}(1-x_{1})}{x_{1}-x_{2}}\Big]dx_{1}dx_{2}\,,

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{[l_{1},l_{2}]_{p}}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{2}}\big[(1-x_{2})x_{2}\Phi\big]dx_{1}dx_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{[l_{1},l_{2}]_{p}}\Phi\Big[-(b+1)x_{2}+(a+1)(1-x_{2})+2c\frac{x_{2}(1-x_{2})}{x_{2}-x_{1}}\Big]dx_{1}dx_{2}\,

we obtain (
3.3
). Equations (
3.4
) and (
3.5
) are proved similarly.
∎

3.3.
p
-cycle
[1,1]_{p}

In this section we evaluate
\bar{S}(a,b,c;1,1)
.

Lemma 3.3
.

We have
S(a,b,c;1,1)=0
if at least one of the following inequalities holds:

(3.8)

\displaystyle p\leqslant a+c,\qquad a+b+c\leqslant p-2.

Proof.

If
p\leqslant a+c
, then for every monomial
x_{1}^{d_{1}}x_{2}^{d_{2}}
of
(x_{1}-x_{2})^{2c}x_{1}^{a}x_{2}^{a}
we have

\max(d_{1},d_{2})\geqslant p
, and the monomial
x_{1}^{p-1}x_{2}^{p-1}
does not enter the master polynomial.

Similarly, if
a+b+c\leqslant p-2
, then the monomial
x_{1}^{p-1}x_{2}^{p-1}
does not enter the master polynomial.
∎

Theorem 3.4
.

Assume that
a,b,c
satisfy (
3.1
) and the system of inequalities

(3.9)

\displaystyle a+c\leqslant p-1,\qquad a+b+c\geqslant p-1.

Then the following statements hold true.

(i)

If
b+c\leqslant p-1
, then

(3.10)

\displaystyle\bar{S}(a,b,c;1,1)=\frac{(2c)!}{c!}\,\frac{a!\,(a+c)!\,b!\,(b+c)!}{(a+b+c-p+1)!\,(a+b+2c-p+1)!}\,.

This expression is non-zero if and only if
2c<p
.

(ii)

If
b+c\geqslant p
and
a+b+2c\geqslant 2p-1
, then

(3.11)

\displaystyle\bar{S}(a,b,c;1,1)=\frac{(2c)!}{c!}\,\frac{a!\,(a+c)!\,b!\,(b+c-p)!}{(a+b+c-p+1)!\,(a+b+2c-2p+1)!}\,.

This expression is non-zero if and only if
2c<p
.

(iii)

If
b+c\geqslant p
and
a+b+2c\leqslant 2p-2
, then
\bar{S}(a,b,c;1,1)=0
.

Remark
.

Part (i) of Theorem
3.4
is a particular case of
[
RV1
, Theorem 4.1]
for
n=2
.

Proof.

Proof of part (i).
We have

(3.12)

\displaystyle S(a,b,c;1,1)\,=\,\operatorname{CT}\,(x_{1}-x_{2})^{2c}\prod_{i=1}^{2}x_{i}^{a+1-p}(1-x_{i})^{b}\,.

This is the constant term for Morris’s identity with

\displaystyle\alpha=a+b+c+1-p,\quad\beta=p-1-a-c,\quad\gamma=c.

By assumptions, these integers are non-negative, and Morris’ identity can be applied to evaluate
(
3.12
). The identity gives

(3.13)

\displaystyle S(a,b,c;1,1)\,=\,(-1)^{c}\,\frac{(2c)!}{c!}\,

\displaystyle\phantom{aaa}\times\,\frac{b!\,(b+c)!}{(p-1-a)!\,(p-1-a-c)!\,(a+b+c-p+1)!\,(a+b+2c-p+1)!}\,.

This is an element of
{\mathbb{Z}}
.

If
b+c\leqslant p-1
, then

a+b+2c+1-p\leqslant p-1
. In this case, all factorials in (
3.13
) except
(2c)!

are factorials of non-negative integers which are less than
p
. We have the following identity in
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
:

\displaystyle(-1)^{c}\,\frac{(2c)!}{c!}\,\frac{b!\,(b+c)!}{(p-1-a)!\,(p-1-a-c)!\,(a+b+c-p+1)!\,(a+b+2c-p+1)!}\,

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{(2c)!}{c!}\,\frac{a!\,(a+c)!\,b!\,(b+c)!}{(a+b+c-p+1)!\,(a+b+2c-p+1)!}\,,

which is obtained by using the identities
a!(p-1-a)!=(-1)^{a+1}
and

(a+c)!(p-1-a-c)!=(-1)^{a+c+1}
. This proves part (i).

Proof of part (ii).
If
b+c\geqslant p
and
a+b+2c\geqslant 2p-1
, then
(b+c)!
has exactly one factor
p
and

(a+b+2c-p+1)!
has exactly one factor
p
. Canceling these factors and using Wilson’s theorem,

\displaystyle(-1)^{c}\,\frac{(2c)!}{c!}\,\frac{b!\,(b+c)!}{(p-1-a)!\,(p-1-a-c)!\,(a+b+c-p+1)!\,(a+b+2c-p+1)!}\,

This proves part (ii).

Proof of part (iii).

has exactly one factor
p

Lemma 3.5

(depending on parameters
a,b,c
)
on the the two-dimensional affine space

with coordinates
x_{1},x_{2}
and with
one-dimensional
p
-cohomology space.
If
2c<p,\ a+c\leqslant p-1,\ b+c\geqslant p,\ a+b+2c\geqslant 2p-1
, then the
p
-cycles

[1,1]_{p}
,
[1,2]_{p}
,
[2,1]_{p}

p

p

3.10.
Relation (
3.38
)

In this section we prove (
3.38
) without explicitly evaluating its terms.
Under assumptions (
3.37
), consider the two polynomials in
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}[x_{1},x_{2}]
:

(3.40)

\displaystyle(x_{1}-x_{2})^{2c}\prod_{i=1}^{2}x_{i}^{a}(1-x_{i})^{b}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{d}\,\alpha_{d_{1},d_{2}}\,x_{1}^{d_{1}}x_{2}^{d_{2}}.

(3.41)

\displaystyle(x_{1}-x_{2})^{2c-p}\prod_{i=1}^{2}x_{i}^{a}(1-x_{i})^{b}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{d}\,\beta_{d_{1},d_{2}}\,x_{1}^{d_{1}}x_{2}a^{d_{2}}.

By the previous theorems, the first polynomial has exactly three non-zero coefficients

\alpha_{d_{1},d_{2}}
with
(d_{1},d_{2})
of the form
(l_{1}p-1,l_{2}p-1)
for some positive integers
l_{1},l_{2}
. These coefficients are

\displaystyle\alpha_{3p-1,p-1}=\bar{S}(a,b,c;3,1),\quad\alpha_{2p-1,2p-1}=\bar{S}(a,b,c;2,2),\quad\alpha_{p-1,3p-1}=\bar{S}(a,b,c;1,3).

Hence, the second polynomial has exactly two non-zero coefficients

\beta_{d_{1},d_{2}}
with
(d_{1},d_{2})
of the form
(l_{1}p-1,l_{2}p-1)
for some positive integers
l_{1},l_{2}
. These coefficients are

\beta_{p-1,2p-1}
and
\beta_{2p-1,p-1}
, and

\displaystyle\alpha_{3p-1,p-1}=\beta_{2p-1,p-1},\quad\alpha_{2p-1,2p-1}=\beta_{p-1,2p-1}-\beta_{2p-1,p-1},\quad\alpha_{p-1,3p-1}=-\beta_{p-1,2p-1}.

We also have
\beta_{p-1,2p-1}=-\beta_{2p-1,p-1}
since
the second polynomial is skew-symmetric in
x_{1},x_{2}
.
Hence,

\alpha_{3p-1,p-1}=\beta_{2p-1,p-1}
,

\alpha_{2p-1,2p-1}=-2\beta_{2p-1,p-1}
,

\alpha_{p-1,3p-1}=\beta_{2p-1,1p-1}
and

(3.42)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\alpha_{2p-1,2p-1}=\alpha_{p-1,3p-1}=\alpha_{3p-1,p-1}\,.

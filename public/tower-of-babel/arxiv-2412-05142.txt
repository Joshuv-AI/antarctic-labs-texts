Strong convergence of the Euler scheme for singular kinetic SDEs driven by α \alpha -stable processes
Chengcheng Ling

Abstract
. We study the strong approximation of the solutions to singular stochastic kinetic equations (also referred to as second-order SDEs) driven by α \alpha -stable processes, using an Euler-type scheme inspired by [ 11 ] . For these equations, the stability index α \alpha lies in the range ( 1 , 2 ) (1,2) , and the drift term exhibits anisotropic β \beta -Hölder continuity with β > 1 − α 2 \beta>1-\frac{\alpha}{2} . We establish a convergence rate of ( 1 2 + β α ⁡ ( 1 + α ) ∧ 1 2 ) (\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\beta}{\alpha(1+\alpha)}\wedge\frac{1}{2}) , which aligns with the results in [ 4 ] concerning first-order SDEs. Mathematics Subject Classification (2020): Primary 60H35, 65C30, 60H10; Secondary 60H50, 60L90. Keywords: Singular SDEs; Strong approximation; Kinetic SDEs; Second order SDEs; Euler scheme; Regularization by noise; Stochastic sewing, α \alpha -stable noise.



1.
Introduction

We consider the following SDE:

(1.1)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}dX_{t}=V_{t}dt,\\
dV_{t}=b(Z_{t})dt+dL_{t},\quad Z_{0}=\zeta=(\xi,\eta)\in{\mathcal{F}}_{0},\end{cases}

where
Z_{t}=(X_{t},V_{t})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
b:{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is measurable, and
L
is a standard
d
-dimensional
\alpha
-stable process defined on the probability space
(\Omega,{\mathcal{F}},({\mathcal{F}}_{s})_{s\geqslant 0},{\mathbb{P}})
where
\alpha\in(1,2)
; that is to say,
L
is a Lévy process with characteristic function
\Phi
(i.e.
\forall t\geqslant 0
,
\forall\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
{\mathbb{E}}(\exp(i\xi\cdot L_{t}))=\exp(-t\Phi(\xi))
,
i
is the complex unit) so that

(1.2)

\displaystyle\Phi(\xi)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\backslash\{0\}}(1-e^{i\xi\cdot x}+i\xi\cdot x\mathbf{1}_{|x|\leqslant 1})\frac{dx}{|x|^{d+\alpha}}.

\alpha
is the stable index of
L
which in general takes value in
(0,2]
. When
\alpha=2
,
L
corresponds to Brownian motion.

In classical mechanics, kinetic equations describe the motion of particles with momentum and inertia. In many physical and natural systems—such as particles moving in disordered media—the environment is not smooth but rather influenced by irregular forces. In such cases, random "kicks" can occur, leading to non-Gaussian random perturbations
[
5
]
. (
1.1
) represents such mathematical
models that describe the random evolution of physical systems with both inertial and random effects, often in environments with irregular, impulsive disturbances. These equations usually are also called second-order SDEs because they account not only for the position of a particle but also its velocity and acceleration, thus incorporating both kinetic and stochastic dynamics (
[
45
,
13
]
); naturally the following classical SDE is called first order SDE:

(1.3)

\displaystyle dX_{t}=b(X_{t})dt+dL_{t},\quad X_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.

In this paper we study (
1.1
) with the focus on
b
beyond Lipschitz continuous scope. Reading through related literature, starting with (
1.3
) driven by Brownian noise, there is a long list of results studying the equations with singular drifts
[
51
,
46
,
23
,
48
,
19
,
20
]
, and later with stable noise
[
44
,
39
,
7
,
1
,
8
,
31
,
47
,
22
]
, known as
regularization by noise
effect.

After obtaining the existence and uniqueness of the solutions to such equations, developing accurate approximation schemes is essential for understanding real-world systems where analytical solutions are rarely available. Approximation allows us to simulate the system and gain insights into the statistical properties of its solutions, convergence rates. In particular, capturing the convergence rate is crucial for quantifying how well the numerical scheme represents the true dynamics.

For singular first order equation (
1.3
), again there is a long list of work studying
its numerical approximations
[
3
,
9
,
29
,
33
,
37
,
36
,
38
]
, which
indicates the well understanding for the first order singular systems. For second order equation in the form of (
1.1
), there are fewer results. There are results on studying the solutions and its numerics in at least-Lipschitz regular setting, see
[
35
,
41
]
. Here we list those that we can find in the literature which however mostly are about Brownian case.
[
32
]
and
[
43
]
study the time-discrete approximations (for instance, explicit and implicit Euler-Maruyama scheme) for (
1.1
) under Lyapunov function type conditions which usually require certain regularity on
b
. Later
[
25
]
obtains the non asymptotic bounds for the Monte Carlo algorithm.
[
26
]
seems to be the first one obtaining the quantitative strong convergence bound on the EM scheme with order
n^{-\frac{1}{4}}
for
b
being piecewise Lipschitz function which is different from the singularity type that we are considering here.

It is evident to see that the degenerate noise (i.e. vanishing noise in
X
direction) and singularity of drift being together make the approximation problem more challenging. For (
1.1
),
[
14
]
shows the strong well-posedness of it for
\alpha\in(1,2)
and
b
being
\gamma
-Hölder continuous in
x
variable and
\beta
-Hölder continuous in

with
\gamma\in(\frac{2+\alpha}{2(1+\alpha)},1)
and

[

\beta>1-\frac{\alpha}{2}
which is also the condition that we assume in current work since we focus on its strong solutions; however for weak well-posedness
b
needs to be anistrophicly
\beta
-Hölder continuous only
[
13
]
.

Inspired by
[
11
]
, we propose the following
Euler scheme
to approximate (
1.1
): for any
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, define
k_{n}(t):=\frac{\lfloor nt\rfloor}{n},t\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
;
let
\{Z^{n}_{t}\}_{t\geqslant 0}:=\{(X^{n}_{t},V^{n}_{t})\}_{t\geqslant 0}
be the solution to the following Euler scheme

(1.4)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}X^{n}_{t}=\xi+\int_{0}^{t}V^{n}_{s}ds,\\
V^{n}_{t}=\eta+\int_{0}^{t}\Gamma_{s-k_{n}(s)}b(Z^{n}_{k_{n}(s)})ds+L_{t},\end{cases}

where

\displaystyle\Gamma_{t}f(z):=f(\Gamma_{t}z):=f(x+tv,v),\quad z=(x,v)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.

We may notice that comparing with the classical Euler-Maruyama scheme, there is no descrization on
X
in algorithm (
1.4
) and it also contains an extra operator
\Gamma
on
V
. Actually the appearance of
\Gamma
does not cause any computational cost since it is simply a shift in
V
. The descrization of
X
seems to more subtle for technical reason. Nevertheless, noticing that
X^{n}
is Lebesgue integral of
V
which is rather explicit via its Euler-Maruyama scheme, it is still easier to be simulated comparing with (
1.1
).

Therefore, the goal of this paper is to show the convergence of
Z^{n}
from (
1.4
) to
Z
satisfying (
1.1
). We mainly apply the
regularization by noise
techniques (
[
29
,
17
,

,
9
,
18
]
) including

[
27
]
and

[
28
]
. Usually these techniques also include

which is one of the main tools for studying singular SDEs driven by Lévy processes (
[
51
,
46
,
49
,
6
]
) and heavily replies on the regularity estimates of its corresponding Kolmogrov equation (
[

,
21
,
40
]
). Differently from the aforementioned methods, we do not apply

but instead our idea is inspired from and closer to
[
4
]

M_{t}:=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\int_{0}^{t}L_{r}dr\\
L_{t}\end{smallmatrix}\right)
, we mainly show the estimates of the following types

\displaystyle S_{1}:=\|\int_{S}^{T}

\displaystyle f(M_{r}+\phi_{r})-f(M_{r}+\varphi_{r})dr\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)|{\mathscr{G}}},

\displaystyle S_{2}:=\|\int_{S}^{T}

\displaystyle f(M_{r}+\phi_{r})-f(M_{r}+\psi_{r})dr\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)|{\mathscr{G}}},

\displaystyle S_{3}:=\|\int_{S}^{T}

\displaystyle f(M_{r}+\Gamma_{r-k_{n}(r)}\phi_{k_{n}(r)})-\Gamma_{r-k_{n}(r)}f(M_{k_{n}(r)}+\phi_{k_{n}(r)})dr\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)|{\mathscr{G}}},

where
{\mathscr{G}}\subset{\mathcal{F}}_{s}
,
\phi=Z^{n}-M,\varphi:=Z-M,\psi_{t}:=\Gamma_{t-k_{n}(t)}(Z_{k_{n}(t)}^{n}-M_{k_{n}(t)}),
and
\|\cdot\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)|{\mathscr{G}}}:=({\mathbb{E}}[|\cdot|^{p}|{\mathscr{G}}])^{\frac{1}{p}}
,
p\geqslant 1
.
Then the error of
Z-Z^{n}
is obtained from these three type estimates above. In the end we obtain the strong convergence of
Z-Z^{n}
with rate
n^{-(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\beta}{\alpha(1+\alpha)})+\epsilon}
where
\beta
is the anisotropic Hölder continuity of
b
and
\epsilon
is some sufficiently small constant. The convergence rate also matches the rate obtained from
[
4
]
on the first order SDE (
1.3
) and its Euler scheme

(1.5)

\displaystyle dX_{t}^{n}=b(X_{k_{n}(t)}^{n})dt+dL_{t},\quad X_{0}^{n}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.

Some conventions

On finite dimensional vector spaces we always use the Euclidean norm.

Without confusion we sometimes also use

In proofs, the notation

(4.23)

\displaystyle I_{5}^{2}\leqslant\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}\big\|{\mathbb{E}}^{t_{i}}({\mathcal{A}}_{t_{i+1}}-{\mathcal{A}}_{t_{i}})-{\mathbb{E}}^{t_{i-1}}({\mathcal{A}}_{t_{i+1}}-{\mathcal{A}}_{t_{i}})\big\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leqslant 16\|f\|_{\infty}^{2}m^{-1}.

For

; otherwise by condition (3) and the fact that

, and for

\displaystyle\big\|A_{t_{i},t_{i+1}}

\displaystyle-{\mathbb{E}}^{t_{i-1}}({\mathcal{A}}_{t_{i+1}}-{\mathcal{A}}_{t_{i}})\big\|_{L_{1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\big\|\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}f(M_{r}+\Gamma_{r-k_{n}(r)}\phi_{k_{n}(r)})-f(M_{r}+\Gamma_{r-k_{n}(r)}{\mathbb{E}}^{t_{i-1}}\phi_{k_{n}(r)})dr\big\|_{L_{1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\|f\|_{{\mathbb{C}}^{\beta}}\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\|\Gamma_{r-k_{n}(r)}(\phi_{k_{n}(r)}-{\mathbb{E}}^{t_{i-1}}\phi_{k_{n}(r)})\|_{L_{1}(\Omega)}^{\beta}dr

\displaystyle\|f\|_{{\mathbb{C}}_{\boldsymbol{a}}^{(1+\alpha)\beta}}\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\|\phi_{k_{n}(r)}-{\mathbb{E}}^{t_{i-1}}\phi_{k_{n}(r)}\big\|_{L_{1}(\Omega)}^{\beta}dr

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\|f\|_{{\mathbb{C}}_{\boldsymbol{a}}^{(1+\alpha)\beta}}|t_{i+1}-t_{i}|\gamma_{\phi}|t_{i+1}-t_{i}|^{\beta\gamma_{1}}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle N\|f\|_{{\mathbb{C}}_{\boldsymbol{a}}^{(1+\alpha)\beta}}(1+\gamma_{\phi})m^{-1-\gamma_{1}\beta},

in above we applied the fact that if

, if

) works and

\displaystyle I_{6}\leqslant N\|f\|_{{\mathbb{C}}_{\boldsymbol{a}}^{(1+\alpha)\beta}}(1+\gamma_{\phi})m^{-\gamma_{1}\beta}\mathbf{1}_{\beta>0},

We now combine (
4.20
) with (

In the end all of the conditions from Lemma

Backward Uniqueness of Extrinsic Geometric Flow in general ambient manifolds
Dasong Li, John Man Shun Ma

Abstract
. In this paper we prove two backward uniqueness theorems for extrinsic geometric flow of possibly non-compact hypersurfaces in general ambient complete Riemannian manifolds. These are applicable to a wide range of extrinsic geometric flow, including the mean curvature flow, inverse mean curvature flow, α \alpha -Gauss curvature flow, σ k \sigma_{k} flow and so on.



1.
Introduction

Let
M
be a smooth oriented
n
-manifold and
(\overline{M},\bar{g})
a smooth oriented
(n+1)
-dimensional Riemannian manifold. A solution to an extrinsic geometric flow is a 1-parameter family
F:M\times[0,T]\rightarrow\overline{M}
of
n
-dimensional immersed hypersurfaces in
\overline{M}
which satisfies

(1.1)

\frac{\partial F}{\partial t}=-f\mathfrak{n}(x,t),\quad\text{ for all }x\in M^{n},t\in[0,T].

Here
\mathfrak{n}(p,t)
denotes the unit normal vector of
M_{t}
and
f
is some curvature function

(1.2)

f(x,t)=q(\kappa(x,t)),

where
\kappa(x,t)=(\kappa_{1}(x,t),\cdots,\kappa_{n}(x,t))
is the vector of principal curvatures of
F(\cdot,t)
at
(x,t)
. Throughout this paper we assume that
q:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
is a smooth function which satisfies the following two properties:

(i)

Let
S_{n}
be the permutation group of
\{1,\cdots,n\}
, which acts on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
by permuting the coefficients. We assume that
\Omega
is
S_{n}
-invariant and
q
is symmetric on
\Omega
:

q(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n})=q(\lambda_{\sigma(1)},\cdots,\lambda_{\sigma(n)}),\ \ \ \text{ for all }\sigma\in S_{n},(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n})\in\Omega.

(ii)

q
is monotone: that is

(1.3)

\frac{\partial q}{\partial\lambda_{i}}>0,\ \ i=1,\cdots,n

on
\Omega
. Note that the monotonicity assumption ensures that the flow (
1.1
) is parabolic.

Examples of most popular extrinsic geometric flows include

•

the mean curvature flow (MCF):
f=\kappa_{1}+\cdots+\kappa_{n}
,

•

the inverse mean curvature flow (IMCF):
f=-(\kappa_{1}+\cdots+\kappa_{n})^{-1}
,

•

the
\alpha
-Gauss curvature flow (
\alpha
-GCF):
f=\operatorname{sgn}(\alpha)(\kappa_{1}\cdots\kappa_{n})^{\alpha}
.

and so on.

For the past 40 years, extrinsic geometric flow had been one of the crucial tools in the study of submanifolds. To name some of the applications, MCF was used by Ilmanen-White
[
19
]
to derive sharp lower bounds on density of area-minimizing cones, and by Buzano-Haslhofer-Hershkovits
[
5
]
to prove the connectedness of the moduli space of two-convex embedded spheres. Huisken-Ilmanen constructed in
[
16
]
a weak notion of IMCF, and this is used to prove the Riemannian Penrose Inequality. Other applications of IMCF include the computation of Yamabe invariants by Bray-Neves
[
3
]
, and the new proof of Ricci pinching conjecture by Huisken-Koerber
[
17
]
. In general, various extrinsic geometric flow are employed to prove a lot of geometric inequalities
[
4
]
,
[
22
]
,

When
M
is compact, under the symmetry and monontonicity assumptions on
q
, (
1.1
) is equivalent up to diffeomorphism to a uniform parabolic scalar equation. Hence given any initial immersion
F_{0}:M\to\overline{M}
, there exists a unique short time solution
F:M\times[0,\epsilon]\to\overline{M}
to (
1.1
) with
F(\cdot,0)=F_{0}(\cdot)
. This is well-documented in the literature, see e.g. Theorem 3.1 in Huisken-Polden’s survey
[
18
]
, or Chapter 18 in Andrew-Chow-Guenther-Langford’s recent monograph
[
1
]
.

When
M
is non-compact, both the existence and uniqueness of solutions to (
1.1
) are not direct consequences of the standard PDE theory. In the following we discuss some of the known existence and uniqueness results in the context of non-compact MCF and IMCF.

In MCF, Ecker-Huisken constructed in
[
12
]
long time solutions to the MCF of entire graph in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. The estimates derived therein are later localized and used in
[
13
]
to prove the existence of solution to the MCF starting from a non-compact hypersurface in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
which satisfies a uniform local Lipschitz condition. Daskalopoulos-Saez
[
11
]
proves the uniqueness of entire graphical solutions to the MCF under an one-sided bounds on the second fundamental form. In higher codimensions, long time existence and uniqueness to the MCF of entire graphs are also established in Chau-Chen-He
[
6
]
in the Lagrangian case and Koch-Lamm
[
20
]
with the assumption of small Lipschitz norm. Without the (global) graphical assumption, a strong uniqueness theorem was established by Chen-Yin
[
7
]
in any codimension when the initial immersion has bounded second fundamental form
A
and satisfies a uniform graphic condition. Lee and the second-named author
[
23
]
use an energy method to prove a uniqueness theorem for MCF with possibly unbounded curvature in any codimensions. Very recently, Peachey
[
25
]
constructed a smooth complete metric
\bar{g}
on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
which admits two uniformly proper solutions to the curve shortening flow coming out from the
x
-axis.

In IMCF, the non-compact case was studied by Daskalopoulos-Huisken
[
10
]
, where they prove the existence and uniqueness to the IMCF of entire convex graph and uniform finite time singular convergence for convex entire graphs with conical behavior at infinity. More recently, Choi-Daskalopoulos
[
9
]
constructed convex solutions to the IMCF starting from some
C^{1,1}
convex hypersurface.

In this work, we are interested in the problem of backward uniqueness: Given two solutions
F,\widetilde{F}
to the extrinsic geometric flow (
1.1
) such that
F(\cdot,T)=\widetilde{F}(\cdot,T)
. Under what conditions do we have
F=\widetilde{F}
?

Some versions of backward uniqueness results were proved for the MCF and the IMCF. Lee and the second-named author proved in
[
23
]
a backward uniqueness theorem for MCF in general ambient manifold in any codimension. This generalizes the previous result in
[
15
]
,
[
28
]
, when the ambient manifold is Euclidean. For IMCF, Ho-Pyo proved in
[
14
]
a backward uniqueness theorem for IMCF of compact mean convex hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
.

Our first result is the following backward uniqueness theorem for extrinsic geometric flow (
1.1
) of hypersurfaces in general ambient manifold for general speed
f
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(\overline{M},\bar{g})
be a smooth oriented
(n+1)
dimensional complete Riemannian manifold with positive injectivity radius
i_{0}
and uniform upper bounds
|\overline{\nabla}^{j}\overline{R}|\leq B_{j}
for
j=0,1,2,3
, where
\overline{R}
is the Riemann curvature tensor of
\bar{g}
. Let
F,\widetilde{F}:M\times[0,T]\to\overline{M}
be two solutions to the extrinsic geometric flow (
1.1
), where
f
satisfies (
1.2
) for some symmetric, smooth and monotone curvature function
q:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
. Assume the following holds:

•

there is
L>0
such that

(1.4)

|A|_{g}+|\widetilde{A}|_{\tilde{g}}\leq L

and

(1.5)

|\nabla A|_{g}+|\widetilde{\nabla}\widetilde{A}|_{\tilde{g}}\leq L

on
M\times[0,T]
, and

•

there is
\delta>0
such that

(1.6)

\operatorname{dist}(\kappa(x,t),\partial\Omega)>\delta,\ \ \operatorname{dist}(\tilde{\kappa}(x,t),\partial\Omega)>\delta

for all
(x,t)\in M\times[0,T]
, where
\kappa(x,t)
,
\tilde{\kappa}(x,t)
are respectively the vectors of principal curvatures of
F,\widetilde{F}
at
(x,t)
.

If
F(\cdot,T)=\widetilde{F}(\cdot,T)
is a complete immersion. Then
F(\cdot,t)=\widetilde{F}(\cdot,t)
for all
t\in[0,T]
.

We illustrate condition (
1.6
) in the following examples:

•

For MCF, the curvature function
q=\lambda_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{n}
is monotone on
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Hence
\partial\Omega
is empty and (
1.6
) always hold.

•

For IMCF,
q=-(\lambda_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{n})^{-1}
and
q
is monotone on
\Omega=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\lambda_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{n}>0\}
. Hence (
1.6
) implies that
F,\widetilde{F}
are both uniformly mean convex:
H,\widetilde{H}>\delta
.

•

For GCF,
q=\lambda_{1}\cdots\lambda_{n}
is monotone on
\Omega=\{\lambda:\lambda_{i}>0,\ i=1,\cdots,n\}
, and (
1.6
) implies that
F,\widetilde{F}
are both uniformly convex:
\kappa_{i},\tilde{\kappa}_{i}>\delta
for
i=1,\cdots,n
.

When the flow is the MCF, Theorem
1.1
is weaker then the one in
[
23
]
, where they prove the backward uniqueness for MCF in any codimension. In the IMCF case, our result generalizes Ho-Pyo’s backward uniqueness result
[
14
]
to non-compact
M
and general ambient Riemannian manifold
(\overline{M},\bar{g})
. For other extrinsic geometric flows, this result is new to the author’s knowledge.

The condition (
1.5
) is not necessary in the MCF case (see
[
23
, Theorem 1.4]
). However, for general extrinsic geometric flow (
1.1
), there’s a term in the evolution equation for the second fundamental form
A
which is quadratic in
\nabla A
, and this prevents us from applying the stardard Shi-type estimates as in the case for MCF. With a concavity assumption on the curvature function, and the convexity of the immersions, we can drop condition (
1.5
). This is the content of our second main theorem:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(\overline{M},\bar{g})
be a smooth oriented
(n+1)
dimensional complete Riemannian manifold with positive injectivity radius
i_{0}
and uniform upper bounds
|\overline{\nabla}^{j}\overline{R}|\leq B_{j}
for
j=0,1,2,3
, where
\overline{R}
is the Riemann curvature tensor of
\bar{g}
. Let
F,\widetilde{F}:M\times[0,T]\to\overline{M}
be two convex solutions to the extrinsic geometric flow (
1.1
), where
f
satisfies (
1.2
) for some symmetric, smooth and monotone and concave curvature function
q:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
. Assume that
F
,
\widetilde{F}
satisfy (
1.4
), (
1.6
) for all
(x,t)\in M\times[0,T]
. If
F(\cdot,T)=\widetilde{F}(\cdot,T)
is a complete immersion. Then
F(\cdot,t)=\widetilde{F}(\cdot,t)
for all
t\in[0,T]
.

The above Theorem is applicable to e.g. the GCF, and the IMCF of convex hypersurfaces.

Next we discuss the proof of Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
. In
[
21
]
, Kotschwar proves a general backward uniqueness theorem for time dependence sections of a vector bundle over a non-compact manifold which satisfies an ODE-PDE inequalities. This is used in
[
21
]
to derive backward uniqueness theorem for various intrinsic geometric flows including the Ricci flow and the
L^{2}
-curvature flow. One can apply Kotschwar’s theorem to obtain backward uniqueness results for MCF (as in
[
15
]
,
[
28
]
) and IMCF (in
[
14
]
) in Euclidean spaces. However, for a general ambient background
(\overline{M},\bar{g})
, geometric quantities like the Gauss map of an immersion are defined as section of the pullback bundles. Hence for two apriori different immersions
F,\widetilde{F}
, one cannot directly compare those quantities. This difficulty was addressed in
[
23
]
(and in
[
24
]
,
[
27
]
in different contexts) by constructing a parallel transport
P
from
\widetilde{F}
to
F
, and the authors are able to prove various uniqueness and backward uniqueness theorem for MCF in general ambient manifold. We will apply the same method here. We remark that although the second fundamental form (and its derivatives) are tensors defined on the domain
M
in the hypersurface case, we still need to estimate the full gauss map, and the use of the parallel transport
P
cannot be avoided. Another complications in our proof, when compared to
[
23
]
,
[
14
]
, is that our higher derivative estimates of the speed
f
is not immediate. Although the condition (
1.6
) is stated in terms of the principal curvatures, we perform the calculations in term of the second fundamental form
A=(h_{ij})
and the metric
g=(g_{ij})
. By choosing for each
x\in M
a suitable local coordinates, we are able to bound not only
(\tilde{h}_{ij},\tilde{g}_{ij})
of
\widetilde{F}
,
(h_{ij},g_{ij})
of
F
, but also the difference of the higher order derivatives of
q(\tilde{\kappa})-q(\kappa)
in terms of
\tilde{h}_{ij}-h_{ij}
and
\tilde{g}_{ij}-g_{ij}
, which is crucial in our calculations.

The organization of the paper is as follows. In section 2, we introduce the background material. This includes some standard knowledge on the curvature function, submanifold theory and the evolution equations associated to the extrinsic geometric flow (
1.1
). In section 3, we prove two Shi-type estimates (Theorem
3.2
and Theorem
3.3
) for the higher derivatives of the second fundamental form. In section 4, we describe the technique used in
[
23
]
to compare geometric quantities of two a-priori different immersions via the parallel transport. In section 5, we derive the key time derivative estimates of different geometric quantities associated to the flow. In section 6, we prove the crucial ODE-PDE inequalities and finish the proof of Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
. In the last section, we state and prove a backward uniqueness theorem (Theorem
7.1
) for compact immersions which holds for any ambient Riemannian manifold
(\overline{M},\bar{g})
.

Background

In this section, we review some background materials on the curvature function
q
and the evolution equations associated to (

).

2.1.
The curvature function

We start by defining the curvature function and proving some of its basic properties. The main reference is
[
2
]
(see also
[
1
, Chapter 18]
). Let
S(n)
(resp.
S_{+}(n)
) be the space of
n\times n
symmetric (resp. positive definite) matrices. Let
\Omega_{SS}
be an open subset of
S(n)\times S_{+}(n)
that is invariant in the sense that

(X,g)\in\Omega_{SS}\Rightarrow(\Lambda X\Lambda^{T},\Lambda g\Lambda^{T})\in\Omega_{SS},\ \ \forall\Lambda\in GL(n,\mathbb{R}).

Let
Q:\Omega_{SS}\to\mathbb{R}
be a smooth function such that

(2.1)

Q(X,g)=Q(\Lambda X\Lambda^{T},\Lambda g\Lambda^{T}),\ \ \forall(X,g)\in\Omega_{SS},\ \ \Lambda\in GL(n,\mathbb{R}).

Setting
\Lambda=g^{-1/2}
, we have

Q(X,g)=Q(g^{-1/2}Xg^{-1/2},I).

Set
\Omega_{S}=\Omega_{SS}\cap S(n)\times\{I\}\cong S(n)
, then
\Omega_{S}
is open in
S(n)
. Let
Q_{S}:\Omega_{S}\to\mathbb{R}
be defined by

Q_{S}(X)=Q(X,I).

Then
Q_{S}
is smooth and
Q_{S}(OAO^{T})=Q_{S}(A)
for any
O\in O(n)
. For each
A\in S(n)
, let
\lambda(A)
be the multi-set of its eigenvalues. To be precise,
\lambda(A)
is in
\mathbb{R}^{n}/S_{n}
. Let
\Omega
be a subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
defined by

\Omega=\left\{\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n}):D_{\lambda}=\begin{bmatrix}\lambda_{1}&&\\
&\ddots&\\
&&\lambda_{n}\end{bmatrix}\in\Omega_{S}\right\}.

By a theorem of Schwarz
[
26
]
, there is a symmetric smooth function
q:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
such that

Q_{S}(A)=q(\lambda(A)).

On the other hand, given a smooth symmetric function
q:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
on a
S_{n}
-invariant open susbset
\Omega
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, one defines
Q,Q_{S}
as

Q_{S}(X):=q(\lambda(X)),\ \ Q(h,g):=Q_{S}(\mathcal{D}(h,g))=Q_{S}(g^{-1/2}hg^{-1/2}).

Hence from now on we call both
q
and
Q
the curvature function of the flow (
1.1
).

The following can be checked using the compactness of
O(n)
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
K
be a compact subset of
\Omega
. Then

(2.2)

K_{S}:=\{A\in\Omega_{S}:\lambda(A)\in K\}

is a compact subset of
\Omega_{S}
.

Let
\mathcal{D}:\Omega_{SS}\to\Omega_{S}
be given by
\mathcal{D}(X,g)=g^{-1/2}Xg^{-1/2}
. While
\mathcal{D}^{-1}(K_{S})
is not compact, we have the following

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
K_{S}
be a compact subset in
\Omega_{S}
and
\lambda>1
. Then

(2.3)

K_{SS}^{\lambda}:=\mathcal{D}^{-1}(K_{S})\cap\{(X,g)\in\Omega_{SS}:\lambda^{-1}\delta_{0}\leq g\leq\lambda\delta_{0}\}

is compact in
\Omega_{SS}
. Here
\delta_{0}
is the Euclidean metric on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Proof.

Let
(X_{k},g_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence in
K_{SS}^{\lambda}
. Since
K_{S}
is compact, a subsequence of
(g^{-1/2}_{k}X_{k}g^{-1/2}_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}
converges. Since
\lambda^{-1}\delta_{0}\leq g_{k}\leq\lambda\delta_{0}
, a subsequence of
(g_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}
converges. Using

X_{k}=g^{1/2}_{k}(g^{-1/2}_{k}X_{k}g^{-1/2}_{k})g^{1/2}_{k},

one concludes that a subsequence of
(X_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}
also converges. Hence
(X_{k},g_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}
has a convergent subsequence and
K_{SS}^{\lambda}
is compact.
∎

Define the matrix
L_{Q}=(L^{ij}_{Q})
, where

(2.4)

L^{ij}_{Q}:=\frac{\partial Q}{\partial h_{ij}}

and
\frac{\partial Q}{\partial h_{ij}}
is the partial derivative of
Q=Q(h_{ij},g_{ij})
with respect to
h_{ij}
. It is proved (see
[
1
, Chapter 18]
) that the monotonicity assumption (
1.3
) on
q
is equivalent to that
L_{Q}
is positive definite on
\Omega_{SS}
:

(2.5)

L_{Q}>0,\ \ \text{ for all }(h,g)\in\Omega_{SS}.

Lastly, recall that
q
is concave on
\Omega
if and only if
Q
satisfies

(2.6)

\frac{\partial^{2}Q}{\partial h_{ij}\partial h_{kl}}\leq 0,\ \ \text{ on }\Omega_{SS}.

A proof can be found in
[
1
, Corollary 18.9]
.

2.2.
Extrinsic geometric flow: the evolution equations

Let
F:M\times[0,T]\to\overline{M}
be a family of immersed hypersurfaces in
\overline{M}
. Let
F_{t}=\frac{\partial F}{\partial t}
and for any local coordinates
(x^{1},\cdots,x^{n})
of
M
, we write
F_{i}=\frac{\partial F}{\partial x^{i}}
. When no confusion would arise, for each
t\in[0,T]
we also use
F
to denote the immersion
F(\cdot,t)
at
t
.

Let
\mathfrak{n}=\mathfrak{n}(x,t)
be a unit normal vector fields along
F
. We assume that
\mathfrak{n}
is chosen such that for any oriented local charts
(x^{1},\cdots,x^{n})
of
M
,

(F_{1},\cdots,F_{n},\mathfrak{n})

is positively oriented with respect to the orientation of
\overline{M}
.

The second fundamental form
A
of
F
is defined by

A(X,Y)=-\bar{g}(\overline{\nabla}_{X}Y,\mathfrak{n}),

and in local coordinates we write
A=(h_{ij})
.

Next we consider the evolution equations of several geometric quantities along the flow (
1.1
). A proof can be found in
[
18
]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
F:M\times[0,T]\to\overline{M}
satisfies the extrinsic flow (
1.1
). Let
g=g(t)
denote the induced metric on
M
and
\nabla
the Levi-Civita connection with respect to
g
, and let (
h_{ij}
) be the second fundamental form of
F
. Then the following holds:

(1)

\partial_{t}g_{ij}=2fh_{ij}
,

(2)

\partial_{t}\Gamma^{k}_{ij}=g^{kl}[\nabla_{i}(fh_{jl})+\nabla_{j}(fh_{il})-\nabla_{l}(fh_{ij})]
,

(3)

D_{t}\mathfrak{n}=F_{*}\nabla f
,

(4)

\nabla_{i}\mathfrak{n}=g^{jk}h_{ij}F_{k}
.

The evolution equation for the second fundamental form
A
and the speed
f
are also computed in
[
18
, Corollary 3.3]
:

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
F:M\times[0,T]\to\overline{M}
satisfies the extrinsic flow (
1.1
), where
f
satisfies (
1.2
) for a smooth symmetric function
q
. Then the following holds:

(2.7)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}h_{ij}

\displaystyle=\Lambda^{kl}\nabla_{k}\nabla_{l}h_{ij}+\frac{\partial^{2}Q}{\partial h_{kl}\partial h_{pq}}\nabla_{i}h_{kl}\nabla_{j}h_{pq}

\displaystyle\ \ \ +\Lambda^{kl}\{-h_{kl}h_{i}^{m}h_{mj}+h_{k}^{m}h_{il}h_{mj}-h_{kj}h_{i}^{m}h_{ml}+h_{k}^{m}h_{ij}h_{ml}

\displaystyle\ \ \ +\bar{R}_{kilm}h^{m}_{j}+\bar{R}_{kijm}h^{m}_{l}+\bar{R}_{iljm}h^{m}_{k}+\bar{R}_{oioj}h_{kl}-\bar{R}_{okol}h_{ij}+\bar{R}_{mljk}h^{m}_{i}

\displaystyle\ \ \ +\bar{\nabla}_{k}\bar{R}_{ojil}+\bar{\nabla}_{i}\bar{R}_{oljk}\}-f(h_{ik}h_{j}^{k}+\bar{R}_{oioj}),

(2.8)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=\Lambda^{ij}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}f-f\Lambda^{ij}(h_{i}^{k}h_{kj}+\bar{R}_{oioj}).

where
\overline{R}
is the Riemann curvature tensor of
(\overline{M},\bar{g})
,
Q
is the curvature function and
\Lambda^{ij}:=L_{Q}^{ij}(h,g)
.

In the above Lemma, for any ambient
(0,q)
tensor
S=(S_{\alpha_{1}\cdots\alpha_{q}})
on
\overline{M}
,
(S_{i_{1}\cdots i_{q}})
is a
(0,q)
tensor on
M
defined by

S_{i_{1}\cdots i_{q}}:=S(F_{i_{1}},\cdots,F_{i_{q}}).

Also, an

in the indices refers to evaluation with

, e.g.

S_{oioj}:=S(\mathfrak{n},F_{i},\mathfrak{n},F_{j}).

In this paper we need to compare tensors that are defined along two a-priori different immersions

,
\widetilde{F}
and we prefer notations that clearly show the dependence on

,
\widetilde{F}
. Hence we use the (also standard) pullback notation:

(2.9)

(F^{*}S)_{i_{1}\cdots i_{q}}=S(F_{i_{1}},\cdots,F_{i_{q}}).

Although tensors of the form
S_{oioj}
are not pullback of ambient tensors on
\overline{M}
, by abusing notations we still use
F^{*}(\iota^{k}_{\mathfrak{n}}S)
to denote such tensors. For example, given a
(0,4)
tensor
S
on
\overline{M}
,
F^{*}(\iota^{2}_{\mathfrak{n}}S)
denotes a
(0,2)
tensor on
M
of the form

(2.10)

(F^{*}(\iota^{2}_{\mathfrak{n}}S))_{ij}=S(\mathfrak{n},F_{i},\mathfrak{n},F_{j})

or
S(F_{i},\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{n},F_{j})
,
S(F_{i},\mathfrak{n},F_{j},\mathfrak{n})
etc.

Remark 2.1
.

In the latter part of the paper we also use the restriction
S|_{F}
of an ambient tensor

(see

defined in (

In the last part of this section, we derive the evolution equations for the higher derivative of
A
along the flow (
1.1
). To do this we first fix some notations. For any tensor
\mathcal{T}
on
M
and

\mathcal{T}^{k}=\mathcal{T}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{T}
be the
k

\mathcal{T}
. Given
j
tensors
\mathcal{T}_{1},\cdots,\mathcal{T}_{j}
on
M

\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{T}_{1},\cdots,\mathcal{T}_{j})

to denote some finite linear combination of tensors of the form

\mathcal{T}_{j_{1}}^{k_{1}}*\cdots*\mathcal{T}_{j_{m}}^{k_{m}},

where
\{j_{1},\cdots,j_{m}\}\subset\{1,\cdots,j\}
,
k_{1},\cdots,k_{m}\in\mathbb{N}
and

From (2), (3) in Lemma

Lemma 4.3

One has

|P\tilde{\mathfrak{n}}-\mathfrak{n}|\leq C|P\widetilde{F}_{*}-F_{*}|.

Proof.

It suffices to check the inequality at each point

Since

Also, define

is the unique unit normal of

To prove (

Let

\vdots\\
\alpha_{n}\end{bmatrix}.

By assuming that

Using

Since

Lastly since

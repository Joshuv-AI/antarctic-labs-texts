Recall that given

with respect to the metric

. According to Cartan’s expansion, the coordinates of the metric

g_{ij}(x)=\delta_{ij}-\frac{1}{3}R_{piqj}x^{p}x^{q}+(x^{3})\hbox{ as }x\to 0,

g^{ij}(x)=\delta^{ij}+\frac{1}{3}R_{piqj}x^{p}x^{q}+(x^{3})\hbox{ as }x\to 0,

where one shall remember that the exponential chart is normal at

Proposition A.1

\displaystyle\Delta_{g}^{k}u

\displaystyle\Delta_{\xi}^{k}u-\frac{k}{3}R_{piqj}x^{p}x^{q}\partial_{ij}\Delta^{k-1}_{\xi}u+(x)^{3}\star\nabla^{2k}u

and

The proof is by iteration on

Appendix B
Hardy-type inequality on manifolds

For the sake of completeness, we prove the well-known Hardy inequality (
19
).

Proposition B.1
.

[Hardy-inequalities on manifolds] Let
(M,g)
be a compact Riemannian manifold of dimension
n\geq 3
. Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
2\leq 2k<n
. Then for any
u\in H_{k}^{2}(M)
, we have that
u^{2}d_{g}(\cdot,x_{0})^{-2k}\in L^{1}(M)
. Moreover, there exists
C_{H}(M,g,k)>0
such that

(147)

\int_{M}\frac{u^{2}\,dv_{g}}{d_{g}(x,x_{0})^{2k}}\leq C_{H}(M,g,k)\|u\|_{H_{k}^{2}}^{2}\hbox{ for all }u\in H_{k}^{2}(M).

Proof of Proposition
B.1
.
The starting point is the following Hardy-type inequality on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
(see Theorem 3.3 in Mitidieri
[
35
]
): there exists
C_{H}(n,k)>0
such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\varphi^{2}}{|X|^{2k}}\,dX\leq C_{H}(n,k)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\Delta_{\xi}^{\frac{k}{2}}\varphi)^{2}\,dX\hbox{ for all }\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Let us choose
u\in C^{\infty}(M)
. Let us fix
\delta\in(0,i_{g}(M)/3)
. Let us define a cutoff function
\eta\in C^{\infty}(M)
be such that
\eta(x)=1
for
x\in B_{\delta}(x_{0})
and
\eta(x)=0
for
x\in M-B_{2\delta}(x_{0})
. We then have that

\displaystyle\int_{M}\frac{(\eta(x)u(x))^{2}}{d_{g}(x,x_{0})^{2k}}\,dv_{g}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{B_{2\delta}(x_{0})}\frac{(\eta u)^{2}\,dv_{g}}{d_{g}(x,x_{0})^{2k}}=\int_{B_{2\delta}(0)}\frac{((\eta u)\circ\tilde{\hbox{exp}}_{x_{0}}(X))^{2}}{|X|^{2k}}\,dv_{\tilde{\hbox{exp}}_{x_{0}}^{\star}g}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(M,g)\int_{B_{2\delta}(0)}\frac{((\eta u)\circ\tilde{\hbox{exp}}_{x_{0}}(X))^{2}}{|X|^{2k}}\,dX

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(M,g)C_{H}(n,k)\int_{B_{2\delta}(0)}(\Delta_{\xi}^{\frac{k}{2}}((\eta u)\circ\tilde{\hbox{exp}}_{x_{0}}))^{2}\,dX

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(M,g,k)\int_{B_{2\delta}(0)}\sum_{i=0}^{k}|\nabla^{i}((\eta u)\circ\tilde{\hbox{exp}}_{x_{0}})|_{\xi}^{2}\,dX

Coming back to the manifold
(M,g)
, we get that

\displaystyle\int_{M}\frac{(\eta(x)u(x))^{2}}{d_{g}(x,x_{0})^{2k}}\,dv_{g}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(M,g,k)\int_{B_{2\delta}(x_{0})}\sum_{i=0}^{k}|\nabla_{g}^{i}(\eta u)|_{g}^{2}\,dv_{g}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(M,g,k)\int_{M}\sum_{i=0}^{k}|\nabla_{g}^{i}u|_{g}^{2}\,dv_{g}

It follows from
[
49
]
(Proposition 2) that there exists
C(k)>0
such that

\int_{M}\sum_{i=0}^{k}|\nabla_{g}^{i}u|_{g}^{2}\,dv_{g}\leq C_{1}(M,g,k)\|u\|_{H_{k}^{2}}^{2}\hbox{ for all }u\in H_{k}^{2}(M).

We then get that

\int_{M}\frac{(\eta u)^{2}}{d_{g}(x,x_{0})^{2k}}\,dv_{g}\leq C_{2}(M,g,k)\|u\|_{H_{k}^{2}}^{2}\hbox{ for all }u\in C^{\infty}(M)

Independently, a straightforward computation yields

\displaystyle\int_{M}\frac{(1-\eta^{2})u^{2}}{d_{g}(x,x_{0})^{2k}}\,dv_{g}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{M-B_{\delta}(x_{0})}\frac{(1-\eta^{2})u^{2}}{d_{g}(x,x_{0})^{2k}}\,dv_{g}\leq\delta^{-2k}\int_{M}u^{2}\,dv_{g}\leq\delta^{-2k}\|u\|_{H_{k}^{2}}^{2}.

Combining this two latest inequality yields (
147
) for all
u\in C^{\infty}(M)
. Inequality (
147
) follows by density.

Appendix C
Green’s function for elliptic operators with bounded coefficients

Definition C.1
.

Let
(M,g)
be a compact Riemannian manifold without boundary of dimension

. Fix

such that

. Let

and a.e.

for all
x\in M
,

(ii)

for all
x\in M
and all
\varphi\in C^{2k}(M)
, we have that

\int_{M}G_{x}P\varphi\,dv_{g}=\varphi(x).

Theorem C.1
.

Let
(M,g)
be a compact Riemannian manifold without boundary of dimension
n\geq 2
. Fix

such that
n>2k\geq 2
. Let

be an elliptic operator of type

. Then there exists a unique Green’s function for

. Moreover,

•

extends to

•

is symmetric;

•

PG_{x}=0

for all
x\in M

•

For all

,

•

There exists

|G_{x}(y)|\leq C(k,L)\cdot d_{g}(x,y)^{2k-n}\hbox{ for all }x,y\in M,\,x\neq y;

•

For all

, there exists

of

We build the Green’s function via the classical Neumann series. We follow the proof in Robert

. We write

. Since

is self-adjoint, then

\eta\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})

if

Where the equality is to be taken in the distribution sense, that is

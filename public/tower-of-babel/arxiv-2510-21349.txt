Effects of semigroup properties on local embeddability
Dmitry Kudryavtsev

Abstract
We investigate whether semigroups with a given property which are also locally embeddable into finite semigroups can be locally embedded into finite semigroups with the same property, obtaining a positive answer for completely simple and Clifford semigroups (similarly to group and inverse semigroup cases studied previously) and a negative answer for 𝒥 \mathcal{J} -trivial semigroups (similarly to cancellative semigroups). Additionally, we resolve the standing question on the differences between local embeddability in and local wrappability by finite structures, providing a novel construction of 𝒥 \mathcal{J} -trivial semigroups which satisfy the latter, but not the former. finite semigroups, local embeddability into finite, 𝒥 \mathcal{J} -trivial semigroups MSC Classification: 20M10, 20M15, 20M18



Introduction

Understanding the intrinsic structural differences between finite and infinite structures defined by a fixed set of algebraic properties is a broad and important area of mathematics. It includes venerable topics such as the resolution of the Burnside problem on the finiteness of the group in which every element has a finite order obtained by Golod and Shafarevich with further results for the shared exponent obtained by Adian and Novikov in 1960s (see
[
1
]
and references therein), as well as more recent developments such as the investigation of the notion of soficity for groups (see early 2000s works of Gromov
[
6
]
and Weiss
[
12
]
) and semigroups (see 2010s works of Ceccherini-Silberstein and Coornaert
[
3
]
and Kambites
[
8
]
).

Similarly to soficity, the concept at the crux of the present text, local embeddability into finite semigroups (LEF for short), is a well-established finitary condition (that is, one shared by all finite structures), which describes all structures internally similar to or approximable by the finite ones. It was initially examined in
[
4
]
with relation to residual finiteness and word problems, reintroduced in
[
5
]
specifically for groups, studied for general structures in
[
2
]
via model theory and topology, and recently brought into the class of semigroups in
[
9
,
10
]
.

The aim of this paper is twofold: we investigate the interactions between LEF and classical semigroup properties, namely whether being LEF with an extra condition X is equivalent to being locally embeddable into the class of finite semigroups satisfying the condition X, and also expand on the local wrappability by finite semigroups (LWF for short), a generalisation of LEF introduced in
[
10
]
. This is reflected in the structure of the work. Section 1 covers the basics of LEF, LWF and important classical semigroup notions. Section 2 deals with the first goal, providing positive answers for Clifford semigroups and completely simple semigroups as well as a negative one for
\mathcal{J}
-trivial semigroups. Finally, in Section 3 we demonstrate that there exist non-LEF semigroups which are LWF, unlike the group case. Appendices A and B contain supplementary tables used in the
\mathcal{J}
-trivial example in Section 2.

2
LEF and semigroup properties

Let X denote some kind of semigroup property (for instance, being a group, cancellative, completely simple or
\mathcal{J}
-trivial). We can consider a semigroup
S
which is X and simultaneously approximable by finite semigroups, and ask a natural question on whether
S
is approximable by finite semigroups which are also X? The following definition turns this idea formal for the case of local embeddability.

Definition 10
.

Let
\mathcal{K}
be a class of semigroups and
S
be a semigroup in
\mathcal{K}
. It is called
locally embeddable into the class of finite
\mathcal{K}
-semigroups
if for every finite subset
H
of
S
there exist a finite semigroup
F_{H}\in\mathcal{K}
and an injective function
f_{H}:H\rightarrow F_{H}
such that for all
x,y\in H
with
xy\in H
we have
(xy)f_{H}=(xf_{H})(yf_{H})
.

As with general LEF, we call
(F_{H},f_{H})
an
approximating pair
for
H
.

This gives us a general type of mathematical problem: if
\mathcal{K}
is a class of semigroups and
S
is an LEF semigroup which is in
\mathcal{K}
, is it true that
S
is locally embeddable into the class of finite
\mathcal{K}
-semigroups? The previous studies into LEF already provide us some examples when the answer is positive.

Proposition 3
.

[
9
, Proposition 2.2]

A group is an LEF semigroup if and only if it is an LEF group.

Definition 11
.

[
10
, Definition 7]

A (semi)group
S
is called
locally embeddable into the class of finite inverse semigroups
(an
iLEF
semigroup for short) if for every finite subset
H
of
S
there exist a finite (semi)group
F^{(i)}_{H}
and an injective function
f^{(i)}_{H}:H\rightarrow F^{(i)}_{H}
such that for all
x,y\in H
with
xy\in H
we have
(xy)f^{(i)}_{H}=(xf^{(i)}_{H})(yf^{(i)}_{H})
.

Proposition 4
.

[
10
, Proposition 18]

An inverse semigroup is an LEF semigroup if and only if it is an iLEF semigroup.

However, there are also cases when the answer is negative.

Example 1
.

Consider a famous construction by A. Malcev, the cancellative semigroup
C=Sg\langle a,b,c,d,x,y,u,v|ax=by,cx=dy,au=bv\rangle
which is not embeddable (not locally, but generally) into groups.

Note that
C
is LEF. In order to see this, choose an arbitrary finite subset
H
of
C
. Since for any given element of
C
the words in
\{a,b,c,d,x,y,u,v\}
representing it must have the same lengths due to the fact that all the defining relations identify terms of equal length, there exists
n
greater than the length of representatives for any
h\in H
. The set
I\subseteq C
of elements represented by words in
\{a,b,c,d,x,y,u,v\}
of length
n
or above forms an ideal, the quotient semigroup
C/I
is finite as the alphabet is finite, and the natural homomorphism
\pi:C\rightarrow C/I
is injective on
H
, meaning that
(C/I,\pi|_{H})
is an approximating pair for
H
.

However,
C
is not locally embeddable into finite cancellative semigroup (which are the same as finite groups). Consider the finite subset
\{a,b,c,d,x,y,u,v,ax,cx,au,cu,dv\}
and assume that
(F,f)
is an approximating pair for it, where
F
is a group. Since we have
(af)(xf)=(bf)(yf),(cf)(xf)=(df)(yf),(af)(uf)=(bf)(vf)
, we also have
(df)^{-1}(cf)=(yf)(xf)^{-1}=(bf)^{-1}(af)=(vf)(uf)^{-1}
. However, from
(df)^{-1}(cf)=(vf)(uf)^{-1}
it follows that
(cf)(uf)=(df)(vf)
, which gives us
(cu)f=(dv)f
by the multiplicative property, which contradicts the injectivity of
f
on the chosen subset as
cu\neq dv
in
C
.

Now we can turn to the analysis for the classes of completely simple, Clifford and
\mathcal{J}
-trivial semigroups. The first among these requires the following additional statements.

Lemma 1
.

Let
M(G;I,\Lambda;P)
be a Rees matrix semigroup and assume that
G
is LEF. Then for any finite subset
H^{\prime}
of
M(G;I,\Lambda;P)
there exists an approximating pair
(F,f)
such that
F
is a Rees matrix semigroup.

Proof.

Since the set
H^{\prime}
is finite, the sets
J=\{i\in I|\exists(i,g,\lambda)\in H^{\prime}\}
,
\Sigma=\{\lambda\in\Lambda|\exists(i,g,\lambda)\in H^{\prime}\}
and
X=\{g\in G|\exists(i,g,\lambda)\in H^{\prime}\}
are also finite. Furthermore,
Y=\{P_{\lambda i}|\lambda\in\Sigma,i\in J\}
is also finite.

Let
F^{\prime}
be a finite group and
f^{\prime}
be a map from
X
to
F^{\prime}
such that
(F^{\prime},f^{\prime})
is an approximating pair for
X\cup Y\cup(X\cup Y)^{2}\cup(X\cup Y)^{3}
. We claim that
F=M(F^{\prime};J,\Sigma;P^{\prime})
together with the map
f:H^{\prime}\rightarrow F
defined by
(i,g,\lambda)f=(i,gf^{\prime},\lambda)
is an approximating pair for
H^{\prime}
, where
P^{\prime}
is the matrix obtained by taking
\Sigma,J
-indexed submatrix from
P
and applying
f^{\prime}
to all of the entries.

First, note that
F
is finite as all the components are finite and setwise
F
is equal to
J\times F^{\prime}\times\Sigma
. Second, the map
f
is injective on
H^{\prime}
because distinct elements of
H^{\prime}
differ either by index on the left or right, or by the group element, and these differences are explicitly preserved by the construction as
f^{\prime}
is also injective on
X\subseteq X\cup Y
. Finally, if
(i,g,\lambda),(j,h,\mu)
are elements of
H^{\prime}
, we have

\displaystyle((i,g,\lambda)\cdot(j,h,\mu))f

\displaystyle=(i,gP_{\lambda j}h,\mu)f=(i,(gP_{\lambda j}h)f^{\prime},\mu)=(i,gf^{\prime}P_{\lambda j}f^{\prime}hf^{\prime},\mu)

\displaystyle=(i,gf^{\prime},\lambda)\odot(j,hf^{\prime},\mu)=((i,g,\lambda)f)\odot((j,h,\mu)f),

where
\cdot
is the product in
M(G;I,\Lambda;P)
and
\odot
is the product in
F
.
∎

Proposition 5
.

Let
S
be a completely simple semigroup and
M(G;I,\Lambda;P)
a Rees matrix semigroup isomorphic to
S
. Then
S
is LEF if and only if
G
is LEF.

Proof.

If
S
is LEF, then
M(G;I,\Lambda;P)
is also LEF. The latter contains a subgroup isomorphic to
G
, for instance,
G^{\prime}
which is formed by all elements
(i,g,\lambda)
with fixed
i
and
\lambda
with the isomorphism
\phi:G\rightarrow G^{\prime}
defined by
g\phi=(i,P_{\lambda i}^{-1}g,\lambda)
. Thus, by Proposition
1
the group
G
is also LEF.

Assume that
G
is LEF. Consider a finite subset
H
of
S
. To prove that there exists an approximating pair for
H
it is enough to demonstrate that there exists an approximating pair for the corresponding set
H^{\prime}
in
M(G;I,\Lambda;P)
. This holds by Lemma
1
, thus
S
is also LEF.
∎

Proposition 6
.

A completely simple semigroup
S
is LEF if and only if it is locally embeddable into the class of finite completely simple semigroups.

Proof.

If
S
is LEF, then we can consider a Rees matrix semigroup
M(G;I,\Lambda;P)
isomorphic to
S
. We have that every finite subset
H
of
S
can be approximated by the same semigroup as the corresponding subset
H^{\prime}
of
M(G;I,\Lambda;P)
, which we can choose to be completely simple by Lemma
1
as
G
must be also LEF by Proposition
5
.

If
S
is locally embeddable into finite completely simple semigroups, it is evidently LEF.
∎

We can also demonstrate that the answer to the general question is positive for Clifford semigroups. In order to do this, we delve into the structure of lattices of semigroups.

Lemma 2
.

Let
S
and
T
be LEF semigroups and
\phi
a homomorphism from
S
to
T
. Then for any finite non-empty
H\subseteq S
there exist approximating pairs
(F,f)
for
H
and
(F^{\prime},f^{\prime})
for
H\phi
such that there is a homomorphism
\psi
from
F
to
F^{\prime}
which satisfies
(hf)\psi=(h\phi)f^{\prime}
for all
h\in H
.

Proof.

Let
(F_{0},f_{0})
be an arbitrary approximating pair for
H
and
(F^{\prime},f^{\prime})
an arbitrary approximating pair for
H\phi
. Set
F=F_{0}\times F^{\prime}
and
f=f_{0}\times(\phi f^{\prime})
, and set
\psi
to be the projection from
F
onto the second term of the direct product.

Note that
(F,f)
is an approximating pair for
H
. To see this, consider
x,y\in H
such that

as well. We have

\displaystyle(xf)(yf)=

\displaystyle(xf_{0},x(\phi f^{\prime}))\cdot(yf_{0},y(\phi f^{\prime}))=((xf_{0})(yf_{0}),(x(\phi f^{\prime}))(y(\phi f^{\prime}))=((xy)f_{0},(x\phi\cdot y\phi)f^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle((xy)f_{0},(xy\phi)f^{\prime})=(xy)f.

Furthermore, from the construction we have
(hf)\psi=(h\phi)f^{\prime}
for all
h\in H
.
∎

This technique can be extended for semilattices of semigroups.

Lemma 3
.

Let
(E,\leq)
be a meet semilattice. Also, let
S=\bigsqcup\limits_{e\in E}S_{e}
be a semilattice of LEF semigroups equipped with homomorphisms
\phi_{e_{1},e_{2}}:S_{e_{1}}\rightarrow S_{e_{2}}
for
e_{1}\geq e_{2}
and
H
a finite non-empty subset of
S
. Denote by
E^{\prime}
the finite subsemilattice of
E
generated by the finite set consisting of
e
such that
S_{e}\cap H\neq\emptyset
. Furthermore, let
K
be

\bigcup\limits_{e_{1},e_{2}\in E^{\prime},e_{1}\geq e_{2}}(H\cap S_{e_{1}})\phi_{e_{1},e_{2}},

and let
(T_{e},t_{e})
be arbitrary approximating pairs for
K\cap S_{e}
for every
e\in E^{\prime}
.

Finally, let
F_{e}=\prod\limits_{e^{\prime}\in E^{\prime},e^{\prime}\leq e}T_{e^{\prime}}
and

to be the restriction of
f^{\prime}_{e}=\prod\limits_{e^{\prime}\in E^{\prime},e^{\prime}\leq e}\phi_{e,e^{\prime}}t_{e^{\prime}}
on
H\cap S_{e}
. For
e_{1},e_{2}\in E^{\prime}
with
e_{1}\leq e_{2}
define
\psi_{e_{1},e_{2}}:F_{e_{1}}\rightarrow F_{e_{2}}
to be the natural projection from the former to the latter.

Then
F=\bigsqcup\limits_{e\in E^{\prime}}F_{e}
is a semilattice of finite semigroups and together with the map
f=\bigsqcup\limits_{e\in E^{\prime}}f_{e}
it forms an approximating pair for
H
.

Proof.

To start, we need to check that
F
is a semilattice of semigroups, that is

•

\psi_{e,e}
is identity on
F_{e}
for every
e\in E^{\prime}
;

•

\psi_{e_{1},e_{2}}\psi_{e_{2},e_{3}}=\psi_{e_{1},e_{3}}
for all
e_{1}\geq e_{2}\geq e_{3}
.

Both these properties follow immediately from the construction of
\psi
’s as projections. Additionally. for
e_{1},e_{2}\in E^{\prime}
such that
e_{1}\geq e_{2}
and
h\in H\cap S_{e_{1}}
we have

(hf^{\prime}_{e_{1}})\psi_{e_{1},e_{2}}=(h\phi_{e_{1},e_{2}})f^{\prime}_{e_{2}}

as
hf^{\prime}_{e_{1}}
is a tuple consisting of
h\phi_{e_{1},e^{\prime}}t_{e^{\prime}}
for
e_{1}\geq e^{\prime}
,
(h\phi_{e_{1},e_{2}})f^{\prime}_{e_{2}}
is a tuple consisting of
(h\phi_{e_{1},e_{2}})\phi_{e_{2},e^{\prime}}t_{e^{\prime}}
for
e_{2}\geq e^{\prime}
and
\phi_{e_{1},e_{2}}\phi_{e_{2},e^{\prime}}=\phi_{e_{1},e^{\prime}}
.

Note that
(F_{e},f_{e})
is an approximating pair for
H\cap S_{e}
. First,
F_{e}
is finite as a finite union of finite semigroups. Second,
f_{e}
is injective because one of the factors of
F_{e}
is
T_{e}
, which approximates
K\cap S_{e}\supseteq H\cap S_{e}
, meaning that all the elements will be separated in this factor. In order to see the multiplicative property, we will prove a more general statement, that is if
x\in H\cap S_{e_{1}}
,
y\in H\cap S_{e_{2}}
and
xy\in H\cap S_{e_{3}}
where
e_{3}=e_{1}\wedge e_{2}
, then
(xf)(yf)=(xf_{e_{1}})(yf_{e_{2}})=(xy)f_{e_{3}}=(xy)f
.

The first and the last of the equations above hold by the construction. Thus, we need to demonstrate that
(xf_{e_{1}})(yf_{e_{2}})=(xy)f_{e_{3}}
. We have

\displaystyle(xf_{e_{1}})(yf_{e_{2}})

\displaystyle=(xf_{e_{1}}\psi_{e_{1},e_{3}})(yf_{e_{2}}\psi_{e_{2},e_{3}})=(xf^{\prime}_{e_{1}}\psi_{e_{1},e_{3}})(yf^{\prime}_{e_{2}}\psi_{e_{2},e_{3}})

\displaystyle=((x\phi_{e_{1},e_{3}})f^{\prime}_{e_{3}})((y\phi_{e_{2},e_{3}})f^{\prime}_{e_{3}})=((x\phi_{e_{1},e_{3}})(y\phi_{e_{2},e_{3}}))f^{\prime}_{e_{3}}=(xy)f^{\prime}_{e_{3}}=(xy)f_{e_{3}}.

This also guarantees the multiplicative property for
H
and
(F,f)
. To end the proof, note that
F
is finite as a finite semilattice of finite groups, and that
f
is injective because elements of
H
either fall in different
F_{e}
’s or separated within the given
F_{e}
by the observation above.
∎

Proposition 7
.

Let
S
be a Clifford semigroup and
Z
be a semilattice of groups
\bigsqcup\limits_{e\in E}G_{e}
isomorphic to
S
. Then
S
is LEF if and only if every
G_{e}
is LEF.

Proof.

If
S
is LEF, then
Z
is LEF and every
G_{e}
is LEF as its subsemigroup by Proposition

Proposition 8
.

A Clifford semigroup

is LEF if and only if it is locally embeddable into the class of finite Clifford semigroups.

Proof.

If

is LEF, then we can consider a semilattice of groups

of

can be approximated by the same semigroup as the corresponding subset

of

If

It is worth noting that the question remains open for the class of completely regular semigroups.

Remark 1
.

The difficulty in this case arises from the fact that while they can be thought of as collections of completely simple semigroups, which are relatively well-understood in general as well as in terms of being LEF, the connections between the constituent parts are more complicated than for Clifford semigroups. Namely, the completely simple semigroups are also organised into a semilattice, but the maps go from the ones above into the translational hull of the ones below, and these images can hide an additional degree of non-embeddability.

The remainder of the section is devoted to producing an example of a
\mathcal{J}
-trivial LEF semigroup which is not locally embeddable into finite
\mathcal{J}
-trivial semigroups.

Proposition 10
.

3

We turn to the notion of LWF, aiming to prove that it is properly distinct from being LEF for general semigroups. Consider two closely related examples,

\mathcal{J}

, meaning that it is pre-accurate itself and

also belongs to

with

. Thus,

.
∎

With the result above, we can return to the two original examples.

Proposition 14
.

The semigroup
T=Sg\langle a,b,c,d,e,x|axb=acx,ac=cd,xcd=xe,ea=de,aex=ax,xexb=bxex\rangle
is LWF.

Proof.

Consider the sequence of semigroups
\{D_{m}\}_{m=1}^{\infty}
defined by presentations

Sg\langle a,b,c,d,e,x\rangle/I_{m},

where
I_{m}
is the ideal of the free semigroup on
a,b,c,d,e,x
consisting of all words of length greater than
m
. We claim that this sequence satisfies the criteria of Proposition
13
.

Firstly, each of
D_{m}
is LEF as they are finite.

Secondly, choose
n\geq 1
and consider the set
H_{n}
of elements of
T
represented by words of length at most
n
and the corresponding set of pre-accurate words
L_{n}
. Set
m=n2^{6^{n+1}}
.

Note that
L_{n}
is finite. To see this, take a pre-accurate word
w
of length greater than
n
. It must be a product of two non-empty pre-accurate words
w_{0}
and
w_{1}
. Similarly, if
w_{i}
,
i\in\{0,1\}
, has a length greater than
n
it must be a product of pre-accurate words
w_{i0}
and
w_{i1}
. We can continue this process until all the factors have a length
n
or less. It should be noted that in the sequence
w,w_{i_{0}},w_{i_{0}i_{1}},\ldots,w_{i_{0}i_{1}\ldots i_{k}}
all of the corresponding elements of
T
must be distinct by Lemma
6
. This means that such decomposition goes down by no more than
|H_{n}|<6^{n+1}
levels, and thus the length of

is bounded by
n2^{6^{n+1}}
.

Since

is finite,

represents a finite subset of

, and by our choice of

. This means that

∎

Proposition 15
.

The semigroup
S=Sg\langle a,b,c,e,x|axb=acx,ac=ca,xca=xe,ea=ae,aex=ax,xexb=bxex\rangle
is LWF.

Proof.

Consider the sequence of semigroups
\{S_{m}\}_{m=1}^{\infty}
defined by presentations

Sg\langle a,b,c,e,x|e^{m}=e\rangle.

We claim that this sequence satisfies the criteria of Proposition
13
.

Firstly, each of
S_{m}
is LEF. To see this, pick two elements
s,t\in S_{m}
. There exist words
u,v\in\{a,b,c,e,x\}^{*}
representing these elements. Denote by
\bar{l}_{e}(w)
the length of a word obtained from a given word
w\in\{a,b,c,e,x\}^{*}
by removing all
e
’s. The natural homomorphism from
S_{m}
onto the finite quotient
S_{m}/I_{m}
, where
I_{m}
is the ideal consisting of all elements represented by words
w
with
\bar{l}_{e}(w)>\bar{l}_{e}(u)+\bar{l}_{e}(v)
, separates elements
s
and
t
. This is due to the fact that neither of them lie in the ideal, meaning that
S_{m}
is residually finite and, as a consequence, LEF.

Secondly, choose
n\geq 1
and consider the set
H_{n}
of elements of
S
represented by words of length of at most
n
and the corresponding set of pre-accurate words
L_{n}
. Set
m=n+1
.

To understand the structure of
L_{n}
, take a pre-accurate word
w
of a length greater than
n
. It must be a product of two non-empty pre-accurate words
w_{0}
and
w_{1}
. Similarly, if
w_{i}
,
i\in\{0,1\}
has a length greater than
n
it must be a product of pre-accurate words
w_{i0}
and
w_{i1}
. We can continue this process until all the factors have a length
n
or less. It should be noted that by Lemma
4
for words
p,z,q\in\{a,b,c,e,x\}^{*}
with non-empty
z
it holds that
z,pzq
represent the same element of
S
implies that
q
is empty and
p
is a power of
e
.

This allows us to prove that
\bar{l}_{e}(L_{n})
is finite. Consider a pre-accurate word
w
with
\bar{l}_{e}(w)>n
. In the sequence
w,w_{i_{0}},w_{i_{0}i_{1}},\ldots,w_{i_{0}i_{1}\ldots i_{k}}
any two elements are equal if and only if the longer one can be obtained from the shorter one by multiplying it by a power of
e
on the left, meaning that in the sequence
\bar{l}_{e}(w),\bar{l}_{e}(w_{i_{0}}),\bar{l}_{e}(w_{i_{0}i_{1}}),\ldots,\bar{l}_{e}(w_{i_{0}i_{1}\ldots i_{k}})
the number of distinctive elements is less than
|H_{n}|<5^{n+1}
. Since
\bar{l}_{e}(w_{i_{0}i_{1}\ldots i_{j}})=\bar{l}_{e}(w_{i_{0}i_{1}\ldots i_{j}0})+\bar{l}_{e}(w_{i_{0}i_{1}\ldots i_{j}1})
, we have
\bar{l}_{e}(w)=\bar{l}_{e}(w_{0})+\bar{l}_{e}(w_{1})=\bar{l}_{e}(w_{00})+\bar{l}_{e}(w_{01})+\bar{l}_{e}(w_{10})+\bar{l}_{e}(w_{11})=\ldots\leq n(2^{5^{n+1}})
.

Since there is a finite number of elements of
S_{m}
represented by words
w
with a given value of
\bar{l}_{e}(w)
, this means that
L_{n}
represents a finite number of elements of
S_{m}
.

We can also demonstrate that a word
w
of the form
ue^{k}v
where
k>n
and
u,v\in\{a,b,c,e,x\}^{*}
represents an element of
H_{n}
if and only if all
ue^{z}v
,
0\leq z\leq k
represent the same element. Consider the shortest word
w^{\prime}
which represents the same element as
w
. There exists a chain of rewritings
w=w_{0},\ldots,w_{q}=w^{\prime}
with rules corresponding to the semigroup presentation of
S
.

If
w^{\prime}
does not contain any
x
’s, the only applicable relations are
ea=ae
and
ac=ca
which do not change the length of the word and cannot lead to
w
as its length is at least
k>n
.

Write
w=m_{0}xm_{1}x\cdots xm_{t}
and
w^{\prime}=m^{\prime}_{0}xm^{\prime}_{1}x\cdots xm^{\prime}_{t}
where
t\geq 1
and
m_{i}
’s are possibly empty words in the alphabet
\{a,b,c,e\}
. Assume that
m_{j}
contains
e^{k}
and denote by
r_{i}
the subword of
w_{i}
situated between the
j
th and
j+1
st occurrence of
x
. Note that
r_{q}
contains strictly less than
|w^{\prime}|\leq n
occurrences of
e
, meaning that we can remove at least two instances of
e
from
r_{0}
and
e^{k}
in particular.

The only relations which allow us to remove
e
’s from
e^{k}
are
xca=xe
and
aex=ax
. Note that if the former relation is used on the rewriting step
i
, the prefix of
r_{j}
for
j>i
stays as
c
unless we use
xca\rightarrow xe
again (there are no other rules which allow to change
xc
into anything else). Thus, if we were able to remove at least two
e
’s,
aex=ax
was also used. However, we can move
e
’s only by
ae=ea
, meaning that in order to apply this relation some
r_{0}
must have suffix
ye^{k}z
where

•

y
or
z
has at least one
a
;

•

y,z
does not contain any
b
’s;

•

z
does not contain
c
with
e
to the right of it.

Otherwise we either would not be able to find
a
to eliminate a single
e
(if there is one, we can push it to the end since
a
commutes with
c
and
e
) or push
e
to the right (we cannot get it past
b
’s at all and if we cannot remove
c
’s which require them to be pushed to the right, we cannot get it past those too). However, this suffix allows us to eliminate any number of
e
’s from
e^{k}
: first, we push
a
to the rightmost position, then use it to eliminate any
c
’s in
z
by commutativity with
a
and
acx\rightarrow axb
, and then freely remove
e
’s using commutativity with
a
and
aex\rightarrow ax
. Afterwards we can return the word to the “initial state” with regards to all letters other than
e
. The latter, in particular, means that
uv
can be obtained by rewritings from
w
, that is it represents the same element of
S
.

Now, consider
u,v\in L_{n}
representing the same element of
S_{m}
. Since they must be obtained from each other via rewritings corresponding to
e^{m}=e
, they have the forms
u=y_{0}e^{\kappa_{1}}y_{1}\cdots e^{\kappa_{h}}
and
v=y_{0}e^{\kappa^{\prime}_{1}}y_{1}\cdots e^{\kappa^{\prime}_{h}}
, with
h\geq 0
,
\kappa_{i},\kappa^{\prime}_{i}>0
,
\kappa_{i}=\kappa^{\prime}_{i}\ ({\rm mod}\,n)
and
y_{i}
being non-empty words in
a,b,c,x
. By the observation above, both of them represent the same element of
H_{n}
, that is the element given by the word
y_{0}e^{\kappa^{\prime\prime}_{1}}y_{1}\cdots e^{\kappa^{\prime\prime}_{h}}
where
0\leq\kappa^{\prime\prime}_{i}<n
and
\kappa_{i}=\kappa^{\prime}_{i}=\kappa^{\prime\prime}_{i}\ ({\rm mod}\,n)
.

Thus, Proposition
13
applies and
S
is LWF.
∎

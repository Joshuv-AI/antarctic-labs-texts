A p p -adic Gross–Zagier formula for twisted triple product p p -adic L L -functions attached to finite slope families
Ting-Han Huang and Ananyo Kazi

Abstract
. Our main objective in the present paper is to generalise the work of Blanco-Chacón and Fornea on the p p -adic Gross–Zagier formula for twisted triple product p p -aidc L L -function. We extend their main result to the case of finite slope families of Hilbert modular forms and also allow the prime p p to be inert in the real quadratic field L L .



1.
Introduction

The goal of this article is to generalise the
p
-adic Gross–Zagier formula for twisted triple product
p
-adic
L
-functions, proved by Blanco-Chacón and Fornea in
[
BF20
]
for ordinary families, to the case of finite slope families of Hilbert modular forms, under the assumption that
p
is unramified in the quadratic totally real field.
In particular, we allow
p
to be inert, which is not covered in
[
BF20
]
.
The formula, in short, relates special values of the
p
-adic
L
-function
\mathscr{L}_{p}(\omega_{\mathbf{g}},\omega_{\mathbf{f}})
in the balanced region, to images of generalized
Hirzebruch–Zagier
cycles under the syntomic Abel–Jacobi map.

This idea can be traced back to Darmon and Rotger
[
DR14
]
, where they considered triple product
p
-adic
L
-functions attached to ordinary families of elliptic modular forms.
The
p
-adic Gross–Zagier formula serves as an important tool in their sequel paper
[
DR17
]
to prove the implication ‘analytic rank
=0\Rightarrow
algebraic rank
=0
’ in a special case of the equivariant BSD-conjecture.
Based on
[
BF20
]
, Fornea and Jin also proved an analogous result under another setting in their recent work
[
FJ24
]
.

The generalization to the finite slope case requires two main ingredients.
First, one needs a definition of the twisted triple product
p
-adic
L
-function
\mathscr{L}_{p}(\omega_{\mathbf{g}},\omega_{\mathbf{f}})
attached to finite slope families of Hilbert modular forms.
This is achieved in the thesis of the second author
[
Kaz24
]
, which extends the construction of
[
AI21
]
.
Second, one needs to compute syntomic Abel–Jacobi maps and specialisations of the
p
-adic
L
-function in the finite slope case.
This technique is developed in the thesis of the first author
[
Hua24
]
, in which he generalised the result of
[
DR14
]
.

On the
L
-function side, suppose
L/F
is a quadratic extension of totally real fields, and
g
(resp.
f
) is a
\mathrm{GL}_{2,L}
(resp.
\mathrm{GL}_{2,F}
) Hilbert modular form of weight
(v,n)\in\mathbb{Z}[\Sigma_{L}]\times\mathbb{Z}
(resp. of weight
(w,m)\in\mathbb{Z}[\Sigma_{F}]\times\mathbb{Z}
). Here
\Sigma_{L}
is the set of archimedean embeddings of
L
, and similarly for
\Sigma_{F}
. Assume that the weights are
F
-dominated, i.e. there exists
r\in\mathbb{N}[\Sigma_{L}]
such that
(v+r,n)_{|F}=(w,m)
, and the central character of the unitary automorphic representation
\Pi
attached to
g
and
f
is trivial when restricted to
\mathbb{A}_{F}^{\times}
. Then Ichino’s formula relates the central critical value
L(1/2,\Pi,\mathrm{R})
of the twisted triple product
L
-function associated with
\Pi
to a period integral which can be realised up to certain complex periods as the following Petersson inner product (cf. Proposition
2.10
)

L(1/2,\Pi,\mathrm{R})\overset{\cdot}{=}\left(\frac{\langle\zeta^{*}(\delta^{r}g),f^{*}\rangle}{\langle f^{*},f^{*}\rangle}\right)^{2}.

Here
f^{*}
is the dual modular form whose Fourier coefficients are the complex conjugates of those of
f
, and
\delta
is the Maass–Shimura differential operator. The right hand side of the above equation is algebraic, and one hopes to define a
p
-adic
L
-function by
p
-adically interpolating such values as
g,f
vary.
In particular, for finite slope Hilbert modular forms, one needs a theory of Coleman families of Hilbert modular forms, as developed in
[
AIP16
]
for example.
But this is not sufficient since the
p
-adic analogue of the Maass–Shimura operator does not preserve overconvergence, and one has to iterate the full Gauss–Manin connection instead. For that reason one has to develop a theory of
nearly overconvergent
Hilbert modular forms, as has been developed in the thesis of the second author. Section 2 of this article recalls this construction. The main result of §
2
which was proved first in
[
Kaz24
]
, is the construction of a
p
-adic
L
-function satisfying the following interpolation property, with the Euler factors defined as in Theorem
2.30
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\omega_{\mathbf{g}},\omega_{\mathbf{f}}
be Coleman families deforming (fixed) small slope
p
-stabilisations of
g
and
f
, defined over connected rigid spectrum
\Sp{(\Lambda_{g})}
and
\Sp{(\Lambda_{f})}
, such that their weights satisfy some mild analyticity hypothesis (cf. Assumption
2.24
). There is a 3-variable rigid meromorphic function
\mathscr{L}_{p}(\omega_{\mathbf{g}},\omega_{\mathbf{f}})
defined on
\Sp{(\Lambda_{g}\hat{\otimes}\Lambda_{f})}
, such that for all pairs of
F
-dominated weights
(x,c)
and
(y,2c)
with
y=(x+t)_{|F}
that are sufficiently large compared to the slope of
\omega_{\mathbf{f}}
, we have

\mathscr{L}_{p}({\omega}_{\mathbf{g}},{\omega}_{\mathbf{f}})\left((x,c),(y,2c)\right)=\frac{1}{\mathscr{E}({f}^{*}_{y})}\left(\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\emph{\text{ inert}}}\mathscr{E}_{\mathfrak{p}}({g}_{x},{f}^{*}_{y})\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\emph{\text{ split}}}\frac{\mathscr{E}_{\mathfrak{p}}({g}_{x},{f}^{*}_{y})}{\mathscr{E}_{0,\mathfrak{p}}({g}_{x},{f}^{*}_{y})}\right)\times\frac{\langle\zeta^{*}(\delta^{t}{g}_{x}),{f}^{*}_{y}\rangle}{\langle{f}^{*}_{y},{f}^{*}_{y}\rangle}.

On the other hand, one may also consider the values of
\mathscr{L}_{p}(\omega_{\mathbf{g}},\omega_{\mathbf{f}})
outside the
F
-dominated region.
To study this case, we will restrict ourself to
F=\mathbb{Q}
.
We first fix a balanced tuple
((v,n),(w,m))
of classical weights, i.e. there exists
s\in\mathbb{N}
such that
(v-(s+1,0),n)_{|\mathbb{Q}}=(w,m)
. Let
\ell:=2v+nt_{L}
where
t_{L}
is the parallel weight, and
k=2w+m
.
We will further assume that the specialization of the family
\omega_{\mathbf{g}}
(resp.
\omega_{\mathbf{f}}
) at the weight
\ell
(resp.
k
) is a
p
-stabilization of a classical modular form, denoted by
g_{\ell}
(resp.
f_{k}
).

Let
K/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
be a large enough extension and
\mathcal{O}_{K}
be its ring of integers.
Inside the Kuga-Sato variety
\mathscr{U}_{\ell-4}\times_{\mathcal{O}_{K}}\mathscr{W}_{k-2}
(cf. §
3.3
), there is a de Rham null-homologous cycle called the generalized
Hirzebruch–Zagier
cycle,

\Delta_{\ell,k}\in\CH^{d-\gamma-1}(\mathscr{U}_{\ell-4}\times\mathscr{W}_{k-2})_{0},

where
d
is the relative dimension of
\mathscr{U}_{\ell-4}\times\mathscr{W}_{k-2}
and
\gamma=\frac{|\ell|+k-6}{2}
.
The syntomic Abel–Jacobi map
\AJ_{p}
then sends
\Delta_{\ell,k}

to
[\Fil^{\gamma+2}H^{|\ell|+k-3}_{\dR}(U_{\ell-4}\times_{K}W_{k-2}/K)]^{\vee}
.
A specific element

\pi_{1}^{*}\omega_{g_{\ell}}\cup\pi_{2}^{*}\eta_{f_{k}}\in\Fil^{\gamma+2}H^{|\ell|+k-3}_{\dR}(U_{\ell-4}\times_{K}W_{k-2}/K)

corresponding to the forms
g_{\ell}
and
f_{k}
will be given in §
3.3
such that the evaluation

\AJ_{p}(\Delta_{\ell,k})(\pi_{1}^{*}\omega_{g_{\ell}}\cup\pi_{2}^{*}\eta_{f_{k}})

makes sense.

Our main results are the following two theorems, where the various Euler factors will be introduced in §
3
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose
p
is split in
L
.
Let
(P,Q)
be a classical point corresponding to a balanced tuple
(\ell,k)
as above.
Then we have

\mathscr{L}_{p}(\omega_{\mathbf{g}},\omega_{\mathbf{f}})(P,Q)=\frac{(-1)^{s}}{s!\mathscr{E}(f_{k}^{*})}\frac{\mathscr{E}_{p}(g_{\ell},f_{k}^{*})}{\mathscr{E}_{0,p}(g_{\ell},f_{k}^{*})}\AJ_{p}(\Delta_{\ell,k})(\pi_{1}^{*}\omega_{g_{\ell}}\cup\pi_{2}^{*}\eta_{f_{k}}).

Theorem 1.3
.

Suppose
p
is inert in
L
.
Let
(P,Q)
be a classical point corresponding to a balanced tuple
(\ell,k)
as above.
Then we have

\mathscr{L}_{p}(\omega_{\mathbf{g}},\omega_{\mathbf{f}})(P,Q)=\frac{(-1)^{s}}{s!\mathscr{E}(f_{k}^{*})}{\mathscr{E}_{p}(g_{\ell},f_{k}^{*})}\AJ_{p}(\Delta_{\ell,k})(\pi_{1}^{*}\omega_{g_{\ell}}\cup\pi_{2}^{*}\eta_{f_{k}}).

We remark that we are able to prove the formula in the inert case.
The reason is that if one works with the sheaf of nearly overconvergent forms in
[
Kaz24
]
, one is able to
p
-adically iterate the Gauss–Manin connection
\nabla
.
It is then easy to obtain a primitive of a
p
-depleted form
\omega_{\mathbf{g}}^{[\mathcal{P}]}
by simply taking
\nabla^{-1}\omega_{\mathbf{g}}^{[\mathcal{P}]}
(compare this to
[
BF20
, Corollary 4.7]
).

With our
p
-adic Gross–Zagier formulae, one is then able to generalise the result of
[
BF20
, Corollary 6.1]
on the dimension of Selmer group, to the case where
f
is of finite slope at
p
and
p
is unramified in
L
.

Another application is the comparison between the automorphic and motivic
L
-functions analogous to
[
FJ24
, Theorem 10.1]
, when the families are of finite slope at
p
.
In other words, one can interpret the
p
-adic
L
-function
\mathscr{L}_{p}(\omega_{\mathbf{g}},\omega_{\mathbf{f}})
as an image of a certain class under the big logarithm of Perrin-Riou.
The proof is essentially the same as in
loc. cit.
,
so we decided to omit it.

Notations
.

Throughout this article, we will frequently consider families of modular forms and classical modular forms.
We will primarily use the notations
{\mathbf{g}},{\mathbf{f}}
and
\omega_{\mathbf{g}},\omega_{\mathbf{f}}
for families of modular forms, while we use
g,f
and
\omega_{g},\omega_{f}
for classical modular forms.
The notation
\langle\phantom{e},\phantom{e}\rangle
will be reserved for the Petersson product, which is linear in the first factor (contrary to the convention in
[
DR14
]
).
When we deal with the Poincaré pairing, we will always use
\langle\phantom{e},\phantom{e}\rangle_{\dR}
to distinguish it from the Petersson product.

2.
Twisted triple product
p
-adic
L
-function

In this section we briefly recall the definition of the twisted triple product
L
-function, both the classical one and its
p
-adic avatars.

Let
L/F
be a quadratic extension of totally real number fields, with
[F:\mathbb{Q}]=d
. Assume
p
is a rational prime that is unramified in
L
. Let
\Sigma_{L}
and
\Sigma_{F}
be their respective sets of embeddings into a common finite extension
K
of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, where both
L
and
F
are split. Let
G_{L}=\Res_{L/\mathbb{Q}}\mathrm{GL}_{2,L}
, and
G_{F}=\Res_{F/\mathbb{Q}}\mathrm{GL}_{2,F}
.

2.1.
The weight space

Here we recall the definition of the weight space for Hilbert modular forms, for the group
G_{F}
. Everything generalizes analogously for
G_{L}
.

Let
\mathbb{T}=\Res_{\mathcal{O}_{F}/\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{G}_{m}
, so that
\mathbb{T}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})=(\mathcal{O}_{F}\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{p})^{\times}
.
Let
\Lambda^{G_{F}}:=\mathcal{O}_{K}\llbracket\mathbb{T}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})\times\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}\rrbracket
,
\mathfrak{W}^{G_{F}}=\Spf{\Lambda^{G_{F}}}
, and
\mathcal{W}^{G_{F}}=\Spa(\Lambda^{G_{F}},\Lambda^{G_{F}})^{\text{an}}
be the associated analytic adic space.
Let
(v^{\text{un}},w^{\text{un}})\in\Hom_{\text{cont}}((\mathcal{O}_{F}\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{p})^{\times}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times},(\Lambda^{G_{F}})^{\times})
be the universal character.

There is another auxiliary weight space
\mathcal{W}_{F}:=\Spa(\mathcal{O}_{K}\llbracket(\mathcal{O}_{F}\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{p})^{\times}\rrbracket)^{\text{an}}
and a map
\mathcal{W}^{G_{F}}\to\mathcal{W}_{F}
induced by the algebra map that is described on the group-like elements
(\mathcal{O}_{F}\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{p})^{\times}\to(\mathcal{O}_{F}\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{p})^{\times}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}
as sending
t\mapsto(t^{2},\mathrm{Nm}_{F/Q}(t))
. Equivalently, on the
\mathbb{C}_{p}
points of the weight spaces the map can be described as sending a weight
(w,m)\in\mathcal{W}^{G_{F}}(\mathbb{C}_{p})\mapsto 2w+mt_{F}\in\mathcal{W}_{F}(\mathbb{C}_{p})
where
t_{F}=(1,\dots,1)\in\mathbb{Z}^{\Sigma_{F}}
is the parallel weight
1
.

For a
p
-adically complete flat
\mathcal{O}_{K}\llbracket(\mathcal{O}_{F}\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{p})^{\times}\rrbracket
-algebra
R
and a weight
k\colon(\mathcal{O}_{F}\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{p})^{\times}\to R^{\times}
, we call
k
an
m
-analytic character if
k=\chi\cdot k^{0}
where
\chi=k_{|(\mathcal{O}_{F}\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{p})^{\times}_{\mathrm{tor}}}
is the finite part of the character and
k^{0}
is analytic on
1+p^{m}(\mathcal{O}_{F}\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{p})
. We note that there are open subspaces
\mathcal{W}_{F,m}
of the weight space
\mathcal{W}_{F}
where the universal character satisfies this “
m
-analyticity” property.

2.2.
Generalities on Hilbert modular forms

There are two related notions of Hilbert modular forms:
arithmetic
Hilbert modular forms which are directly related to automorphic representations, and hence suited for arithmetic applications like the study of
L
-functions;
geometric
Hilbert modular forms which have a modular interpretation as sections of modular sheaves on Hilbert modular varieties, and hence more suitable for geometric constructions. But before we explain these notions, let us provide a simpler definition of the arithmetic Hilbert modular forms using the language of automorphic forms as functions on the adelic points of
G_{L}
(or
G_{F}
).

Let
K_{1}(N_{g})=\left\{\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{L})\,|\,d\equiv 1,c\equiv 0\text{ mod }N_{g}\right\}
for an ideal
N_{g}\subset\mathcal{O}_{L}
.
A Hilbert modular cuspform
g
of weight
(v,n)\in\mathbb{Z}[\Sigma_{L}]\times\mathbb{Z}
is a function
g\colon G_{L}(\mathbb{Q})\backslash G_{L}(\mathbb{A})/K_{1}(N_{g})\to\mathbb{C}{}
that satisfies the following properties:

(1)

For every finite adelic point
x\in G_{L}(\mathbb{A}^{\infty})
(the superscript denotes “away from the
\infty
-places”), the well-defined function
f_{x}\colon\mathfrak{h}^{2d}\to\mathbb{C}
given by
f_{x}(z)=f(u_{\infty}x)j_{k,v}(u_{\infty},\mathbf{i})
is holomorphic. Here
\mathfrak{h}
is the usual Poincaré upper half plane,
u_{\infty}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\mathrm{GL}_{2}(L\otimes\mathbb{R})_{+}
such that
u_{\infty}\cdot\mathbf{i}=z
under the usual Möbius action,
k=2v+nt_{L}
, and
j_{k,v}(u_{\infty},z)=(ad-bc)^{-v}(cz+d)^{k}
is the automorphic factor.

(2)

f(xu_{\infty})=f(x)j_{k,v}(u_{\infty},\mathbf{i})^{-1}
for any
u_{\infty}\in\mathrm{Stab}(\mathbf{i})\cap G_{L}(\mathbb{R})_{+}
.

(3)

For all adelic points
x\in\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{L})
, and for all additive measures
da
on
L\backslash\mathbb{A}_{L}
, we have

\int_{L\backslash\mathbb{A}_{L}}\phi_{f}\left(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&a\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)x\right)da=0.

Let us now quickly set up the geometric setting that will be useful later to talk about
p
-adic interpolation of modular forms. Let
M^{G_{F}}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
be the (base change to
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
of the) Shimura variety associated with
G_{F}
of level
K
for a neat open compact
K=K^{(p)}K_{p}\subset G_{F}(\hat{\mathbb{Z}})
such that
K_{p}=G_{F}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
. The Shimura variety
M^{G_{F}}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
is not connected and its connected components are parametrised by the strict class group
\mathrm{cl}^{+}_{F}(K):=F^{\times,+}\backslash\mathbb{A}_{F,f}^{\times}/\det K
. The connected components of
M^{G_{F}}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
are finite étale quotients of certain Hilbert modular varieties which we explain below.

For a fractional ideal
\mathfrak{c}
of
F
, let
X^{G_{F},\mathfrak{c}}/\Spec{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}
be the Hilbert modular scheme classifying tuples
(A,\iota,\lambda,\alpha_{K})
where
A
is an abelian variety of dimension
[F:\mathbb{Q}]
with real multiplication structure given by
\iota\colon\mathcal{O}_{F}\to\mathrm{End}(A)
,
\lambda\colon(\Hom^{\text{sym}}_{\mathcal{O}_{F}}(A,A^{\vee}),\mathrm{Pol}_{\mathcal{O}_{F}}(A))\xrightarrow{\sim}(\mathfrak{c},\mathfrak{c}^{+})
is a
\mathfrak{c}
-polarisation structure in the sense of
[
DP94
]
, and
\alpha_{K}
is a
K
-level structure. There is a natural action of the totally positive units
\mathcal{O}_{F}^{\times,+}
on
X^{G_{F},\mathfrak{c}}/\Spec{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}
given by
\epsilon\cdot(A,\iota,\lambda,\alpha_{K})=(A,\iota,\epsilon\lambda,\alpha_{K})
. This action factors through the finite quotient
\Delta_{K}:=\mathcal{O}_{F}^{\times,+}/(K\cap\mathcal{O}_{F}^{\times})^{2}
. The geometrically connected components of
X^{G_{F},\mathfrak{c}}
are parametrised by
\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{F}^{\times}/\det K
, and the stabiliser of each geometrically connected component for the action of
\Delta_{K}
is
(\det K\cap\mathcal{O}_{F}^{\times,+})/(K\cap\mathcal{O}_{F}^{\times})^{2}
. Therefore, the geometrically connected components of
X^{G_{F},\mathfrak{c}}/\Delta_{K}
are parametrised by
\mathcal{O}_{F}^{\times,+}\backslash\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{F}^{\times}/\det K=\ker(\mathrm{cl}^{+}_{F}(K)\to\mathrm{cl}^{+}_{F})
where
\mathrm{cl}^{+}_{F}=\mathrm{cl}^{+}_{F}(G_{F}(\hat{\mathbb{Z}}))
is the strict class group.

Let
\mathfrak{N}
be the modulus of
\mathrm{cl}^{+}_{F}(K)
. Consider the group
I_{\mathfrak{N}p}
of fractional ideals of
F
coprime to
\mathfrak{N}p
, and let
P_{\mathfrak{N}p}=\ker(I_{\mathfrak{N}p}\to\mathrm{cl}^{+}_{F})
be the subgroup of ideals generated by
x\in F^{\times,+}
such that
x
is coprime to
\mathfrak{N}p
. Let
X^{G_{F}}/\Spec\mathbb{Z}_{p}=\sqcup_{\mathfrak{c}\in I_{\mathfrak{N}p}}X^{G_{F},\mathfrak{c}}
. Let
F^{\times,+}_{\mathfrak{N}p}
be the group of totally positive
x\in F^{\times,+}
such that
x
is coprime to
\mathfrak{N}p
. There is a natural action of
F^{\times,+}_{\mathfrak{N}p}
on
X^{G_{F}}
given by
x\cdot(A,\iota,\lambda,\alpha_{K})=(A,\iota,x\lambda,\alpha_{K})
. For an ideal
(x)\in P_{\mathfrak{N}p}
, multiplying the polarisation isomorphism by
x
induces an isomorphism
X^{G_{F},\mathfrak{c}}/\Delta_{K}\to X^{G_{F},x\mathfrak{c}}/\Delta_{K}
. Thus
M^{G_{F}}:=X^{G_{F}}/F^{\times,+}_{\mathfrak{N}p}
makes sense as a finite type scheme over
\Spec{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}
, whose geometrically connected components are parametrised by
\mathrm{cl}^{+}_{F}(K)
. One has that
M^{G_{F}}
is an integral model of the Shimura variety
M^{G_{F}}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}

[
TX16
, §2.3]
.

Remark 2.1
.

For
\mathfrak{d}_{F}
the different ideal of
F
, the Hilbert modular scheme
X^{G_{F},\mathfrak{d}_{F}^{-1}}
is an integral model of the Shimura variety for the group
G_{F}^{*}:=G_{F}\times_{\Res_{F/\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{G}_{m}}\mathbb{G}_{m}
, where the map
G_{F}\to\Res_{F/\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{G}_{m}
is the determinant map, and
\mathbb{G}_{m}\to\Res_{F/\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{G}_{m}
is the diagonal embedding.

Choose a smooth toroidal compactification
\bar{X}^{G_{F},\mathfrak{c}}
of
X^{G_{F},\mathfrak{c}}
for each
\mathfrak{c}
, compatibly so that the multiplication by
x
action on the polarisation module for
x\in F^{\times,+}_{\mathfrak{N}p}
induces an isomorphism
\bar{X}^{G_{F},\mathfrak{c}}\simeq\bar{X}^{G_{F},x\mathfrak{c}}
. The action of
(\det K\cap\mathcal{O}_{F}^{\times,+})/(K\cap\mathcal{O}_{F}^{\times})^{2}
on the geometrically connected components of
{X}^{G_{F},\mathfrak{c}}
extends to a free action on the geometrically connected components of
\bar{X}^{G_{F},\mathfrak{c}}

[
AIP16
, §8.1]
, and the finite étale quotient
\bar{M}^{G_{F}}:=\bar{X}^{G_{F}}/F^{\times,+}_{\mathfrak{N}p}
defines a smooth toroidal compactification of
M^{G_{F}}
.
We similarly define
X^{G_{L}},M^{G_{L}}
and their compactified counterparts.

Let
\mathcal{A}\to\bar{X}^{G_{F}}
be the semi-abelian scheme extending the universal abelian scheme over
X^{G_{F}}
. Let
e\colon\bar{X}^{G_{F}}\to\mathcal{A}
be the unit section. Let
\omega_{\mathcal{A}}:=e^{*}\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{A}/\bar{X}^{G_{F}}}
be the sheaf of invariant differentials. Let
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{A}}
be the canonical extension of the relative de Rham sheaf
\mathcal{H}^{1}_{\dR}(\mathcal{A}/X^{G_{F}})
to
\bar{X}^{G_{F}}
. We have an exact sequence known as the Hodge filtration:

0\to\omega_{\mathcal{A}}\to\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{A}}\to\omega_{\mathcal{A}^{\vee}}^{\vee}\to 0.

Recall that
K
was chosen to be a finite extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
that splits
F
and
L
. Fix any
\mathcal{O}_{K}
-algebra
R
, and base change
\bar{X}^{G_{F}}
to
\Spec R
. The modules
\omega_{\mathcal{A}}
and
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{A}}
are
\mathcal{O}_{F}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\bar{X}^{G_{F}}}
-modules locally free of rank 1 and 2 respectively. Hence using the distinct embeddings
\sigma\colon\mathcal{O}_{F}\to R\to\mathcal{O}_{\bar{X}^{G_{F}}}
, we get a decomposition of the Hodge filtration as a direct sum over
\Sigma_{F}
:

0\to\omega_{\mathcal{A},\sigma}\to\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{A},\sigma}\to\omega_{\mathcal{A}^{\vee},\sigma}^{\vee}\to 0,

where
\omega_{\mathcal{A},\sigma}
and
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{A},\sigma}
are locally free
\mathcal{O}_{\bar{X}^{G_{F}}}
-modules of rank 1 and 2 respectively.

Definition 2.2
.

For a weight
{k}=(k_{\sigma})_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{F}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\Sigma_{F}}
, the sheaf of geometric Hilbert modular forms of weight
k
is defined as

\omega_{\mathcal{A}}^{{k}}:=\bigotimes_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{F}}\omega_{\mathcal{A},\sigma}^{k_{\sigma}}.

The symmetric
{k}
-th power of
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{A}}

Proof.

3.
The generalized Hirzebruch–Zagier cycles and the Abel–Jacobi maps

From now on, we will mainly restrict ourselves to the case where
F=\mathbb{Q}
and
L/\mathbb{Q}
is a real quadratic extension with two real embeddings denoted by
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}
.

3.1.
Several formulae on
q
-expansions

We write
\nabla_{i}=\nabla(\sigma_{i})
and let
d_{i}
be the derivation corresponding to
\sigma_{i}
.
Namely, around a Tate object as defined in
[
Kaz24
, §5.3]
,
d_{i}
acts on the
q
-expansions via

d_{i}(\sum_{\beta}a_{\beta}q^{\beta})=\sum\sigma_{i}(\beta)a_{\beta}q^{\beta},

where
\sum_{\beta}a_{\beta}q^{\beta}
belongs to the ring of
q
-expansions
R:=\Lambda^{0}_{I}(\!(\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{b},S)\!)
corresponding to the Tate object.
Then, the Gauss–Manin connection can be described by the following formula:

(1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\nabla_{i}\left(a\cdot\prod_{\sigma}V_{\sigma}^{h_{\sigma}}(1+\beta_{n}Z)^{k}\right)&=d_{i}(a)\prod_{\sigma}V_{\sigma}^{h_{\sigma}}\cdot(1+\beta_{n}Z)^{k+2\sigma_{i}}\\
&+p(u_{k_{\sigma_{i}}}-h_{\sigma_{i}})V_{\sigma_{i}}\prod_{\sigma}V_{\sigma}^{h_{\sigma}}\cdot(1+\beta_{n}Z)^{k+2\sigma_{i}}.\end{split}

For a
\mathcal{P}
-depleted form
\mathbf{g}^{[\mathcal{P}]}
with the
q
-expansion

\mathbf{g}^{[\mathcal{P}]}(q)\cdot(1+\beta_{n}Z)^{k}

and a weight
r
as in Assumption
2.24
, the
q
-expansion of
\nabla^{r}g^{[\mathcal{P}]}
is then given by

(2)

\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}p^{j}\binom{u_{r}}{j}\prod_{i=0}^{j-1}(u_{k_{\sigma_{1}}}+u_{r}-1-i)d_{1}^{{r}-j}\mathbf{g}^{[\mathcal{P}]}(q)\cdot V^{j}_{\sigma_{1}}(1+\beta_{n}Z)^{k+2r}.

Here we recall that the weight
r
was defined as
\chi_{r}\cdot r^{0}
, where
r^{0}
is the analytic part of the character and contributes to one embedding, which we can and will assume without loss of generality to be
\sigma_{1}
.
The weight
\chi_{r}
lifts a character of
(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times})_{\mathrm{tor}}
to a character of
(\mathcal{O}_{F}\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{p})^{\times}_{\mathrm{tor}}
which is trivial on the
2
-torsion if
p=2
. We can assume without loss of generality that
\chi_{r}
is the finite part of a classical weight
(m,0)\in\mathbb{Z}[\Sigma_{L}]
. As an upshot, all classical specialisations of
r
will only contribute to the
\sigma_{1}
direction.
By abuse of notation, we will usually replace
u_{k_{\sigma_{1}}}
by
k_{\sigma_{1}}
in the iteration formula and similarly for
r
.

Remark 3.1
.

By construction, the element
1+\beta_{n}Z_{\sigma_{i}}
can be identified with
\omega_{\can,i}
and
V_{\sigma_{i}}
can be identified with
p^{-1}\omega_{\can,i}^{-1}\cdot\eta_{\can,i}
, where
\omega_{\can,i},\eta_{\can,i}
are the
\sigma_{i}
-part of the canonical basis of the first de Rham cohomology of the universal abelian surface over the Tate object.
We will freely use these identifications in our computations later.

We also recall that the diagonal restriction
\zeta^{*}
maps an element of the form
a\cdot\prod_{\sigma}V_{\sigma}^{h_{\sigma}}(1+\beta_{n}Z)^{k}
to
\zeta^{*}(a)\cdot V^{h_{\sigma_{1}}+h_{\sigma_{2}}}(1+\beta_{n}Z)^{k_{\sigma_{1}}+k_{\sigma_{2}}}
.
As we will not need to deal with the
q
-expansion
\zeta^{*}(a)
, we prefer not to unwind its explicit formula.

Lastly, we recall the formula for the overconvergent projection
H^{\dagger}
, or rather, its restriction to
\Fil_{n}\mathbb{W}_{k}
.
Since we assume that
F=\mathbb{Q}
, we only need to consider the case of modular curves, which is studied in
[
AI21
]
.
Given any section
\gamma
of
\Fil_{n}\mathbb{W}_{k}
with
q
-expansion
\sum_{i\leq n}\gamma_{i}(q)p^{i}V^{i}(1+\beta_{n}Z)^{k}
, the
q
-expansion of
H^{\dagger}(\gamma)
is given by (c.f.
[
AI21
, Proposition 3.37]
)

(3)

\sum_{i=0}^{n}(-1)^{i}\frac{d^{i}\gamma_{i}(q)}{(k-2-i+1)(k-2-i+2)\cdots(k-2)}\cdot(1+\beta_{n}Z)^{k}.

3.2.
Specialisations at classical weights

Let
\omega_{\mathbf{g}},\omega_{\mathbf{f}}
be Coleman families such that we can define the twisted triple product
p
-adic
L
-function

\mathscr{L}_{p}(\breve{\omega}_{\mathbf{g}},\breve{\omega}_{\mathbf{f}})=\frac{\langle H^{\dagger,\leq a}(\zeta^{*}\nabla^{r}\breve{\omega}_{\mathbf{g}}^{[\mathcal{P}]}),\breve{\omega}_{\mathbf{f}}^{*}\rangle}{\langle\omega_{\mathbf{f}}^{*},\omega_{\mathbf{f}}^{*}\rangle}

as in Definition
2.28
.
For simplicity, we will drop the
\breve{\phantom{e}}
notations throughout this section.

Let

(4)

\mathbf{g}(q)\cdot(1+\beta_{n}Z)^{k_{g}}\quad(\textrm{resp.}\ \mathbf{g}^{[\mathcal{P}]}(q)\cdot(1+\beta_{n}Z)^{k_{g}})

be the
q
-expansion of
\omega_{\mathbf{g}}
(resp.
\omega_{\mathbf{g}}^{[\mathcal{P}]}
).

Fix a balanced pair
(\ell,k)
with

The specialization of

at

takes the form

\frac{\langle H^{\dagger,\leq a}(\zeta^{*}\nabla^{(-s-1,0)}g_{\ell}^{[\mathcal{P}]}),f_{k}^{*}\rangle}{\langle f_{k}^{*},f_{k}^{*}\rangle}.

First, we compute the (polynomial)
q
-expansion of
\nabla^{(-s-1,0)}g_{\ell}^{[\mathcal{P}]}=\nabla_{1}^{-s-1}g_{\ell}^{[\mathcal{P}]}
.
By formula (
2
), one gets

\displaystyle\nabla_{1}^{-s-1}

\displaystyle g_{\ell}^{[\mathcal{P}]}(q)\cdot(1+\beta_{n}Z)^{\ell}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}p^{j}\binom{-s-1}{j}\prod_{i=0}^{j-1}(\ell_{1}-s-2-i)d_{1}^{-s-1-j}g_{\ell}^{[\mathcal{P}]}(q)\cdot V^{j}_{\sigma_{1}}(1+\beta_{n}Z)^{\ell+2(-s-1,0)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{\ell_{1}-s-2}\binom{-s-1}{j}\prod_{i=0}^{j-1}(\ell_{1}-s-2-i)d_{1}^{-s-1-j}g_{\ell}^{[\mathcal{P}]}(q)\cdot\omega_{\can}^{\ell+(-2s-2-j,0)}\eta_{\can}^{(j,0)}.

Its diagonal pull-back
\zeta^{*}(\nabla^{(-s-1,0)}g_{\ell}^{[\mathcal{P}]})
then takes the following form:

(5)

\sum_{j=0}^{\ell_{1}-s-2}\binom{-s-1}{j}\prod_{i=0}^{j-1}(\ell_{1}-s-2-i)\zeta^{*}\left(d_{1}^{-s-1-j}g_{\ell}^{[\mathcal{P}]}(q)\right)\cdot\omega_{\can}^{k-j}\eta_{\can}^{j}.

Later, we will restrict ourselves to classical pair
(\ell,k)
satisfying the following assumption.

Assumption 3.2
.

We will assume that the specialisation of
\mathbf{g}
at

p
-stabilisation of a classical form denoted by

.
Similarly, we assume that the specialisation of

3.3.

).
Our goal now is to describe
\varphi_{2,*}\Upsilon(\omega)
in terms of overconvergent modular forms, which will be done in the next subsection.

3.4.
Computations: the split case

In this subsection, we consider the case where
p
splits as
\mathfrak{p}_{1}\mathfrak{p}_{2}
in the real quadratic field
L
.

To study
\varphi_{2,*}\Upsilon(\omega)
, one needs to fix several polynomials for finite polynomial cohomology.
Set
T=T_{1}T_{2}
and

\displaystyle P_{1}(T_{1})

\displaystyle=(1-\alpha_{1}T_{1})(1-\beta_{1}T_{1}),

\displaystyle P_{2}(T_{2})

\displaystyle=(1-\alpha_{2}T_{2})(1-\beta_{2}T_{2}),

\displaystyle P(T)

\displaystyle=(1-\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}T)(1-\alpha_{1}\beta_{2}T)(1-\beta_{1}\alpha_{2}T)(1-\beta_{1}\beta_{2}T),

where
\alpha_{i},\beta_{i}
are the eigenvalues of
T_{0}({\mathfrak{p}_{i}})
.
We here refer to
[
BF20
, p.1986]
for the explicit choices of the polynomials
a_{2}(T_{1},T_{2})
and
b_{1}(T_{1},T_{2})
such that

P(T_{1}T_{2})=a_{2}(T_{1},T_{2})P_{1}(T_{1})+b_{1}(T_{1},T_{2})P_{2}(T_{2}).

The polynomial
a_{2}(T_{1},T_{2})
is composed of monomials
T_{1}^{x}T_{2}^{y}
with
x\leq y
, while
b_{1}(T_{1},T_{2})
is composed of monomials
T_{1}^{x}T_{2}^{y}
with
x>y
.
Since
P(T_{1}T_{2})
is symmetric in the indices
1
and
2
,
one can also write

P(T_{1}T_{2})=a_{1}(T_{1},T_{2})P_{2}(T_{2})+b_{2}(T_{1},T_{2})P_{1}(T_{1}),

where
a_{1}
(resp.
b_{2}
) is obtained from
a_{2}(T_{1},T_{2})
(resp.
b_{1}
) by swapping all the indices.
Finally, we let these polynomials act on the space of overconvergent modular forms by letting
T_{i}
act as
V_{0}(\mathfrak{p}_{i})
(cf.
[
Kaz24
, §6]
), where
V_{0}(\mathfrak{p}_{i})
satisfies
U_{0}(\mathfrak{p}_{i})V_{0}(\mathfrak{p}_{i})=\mathrm{id}
. We remark that
V_{0}(\mathfrak{p}_{i})(g\cdot\eta_{\lambda,\mathfrak{d}_{L}^{-1}}^{w})=(V_{0}(\mathfrak{p}_{i})g)\cdot\eta_{\lambda,\mathfrak{d}_{L}^{-1}}^{w}
for any
w\in\mathbb{Z}[\Sigma_{L}]
(cf.
[
BF20
, Lemma 2.6]
). The readers should note that the definitions of the operator
V_{0}(\mathfrak{p}_{i})
on either side of this equality are different, and depend on the component
v
of the weight
(v,n)
, as in the definition of the normalised Hecke operator
T_{0}(\mathfrak{p}_{i})
. Since the roots of the
T_{0}(\mathfrak{p}_{i})
Hecke polynomial acting on the
g
-isotypic component does not depend on whether we view
g
as a section of
\omega_{\mathcal{A}}^{\ell}\otimes(\wedge^{2}_{\mathcal{O}_{L}}\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{A}})^{-v}
or of
\omega_{\mathcal{A}}^{\ell}\otimes(\wedge^{2}_{\mathcal{O}_{L}}\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{A}})^{-t_{L}}
(as
g\cdot\eta_{\lambda,\mathfrak{d}_{L}^{-1}}^{v-t_{L}})
, we find that
P(V_{0}(p))
kills the differential form
\omega_{g}
.

Let
g
be of weight
\ell=(\ell_{1},\ell_{2})
as before.
For
j=1,2
, there are
\mathfrak{p}_{j}
-depleted overconvergent cusp forms
g_{1}^{(j)},g_{2}^{(j)}
such that
g^{[\mathfrak{p}_{j}]}=P_{j}(V_{0}(\mathfrak{p}_{j}))g=d_{1}^{\ell_{1}-1}g_{1}^{(j)}+d_{2}^{\ell_{2}-1}g_{2}^{(j)}
(c.f.
[
BF20
, Theorem 4.5]
).
One can then write

P(V_{0}(p))g=d_{1}^{\ell_{1}-1}(h)+d_{1}^{\ell_{1}-1}(h_{1})+d_{2}^{\ell_{2}-1}(h_{2}),

where
h=(1-\alpha_{1}\beta_{1}\alpha_{2}\beta_{2}V_{0}(p)^{2})d_{1}^{1-\ell_{1}}g^{[\mathcal{P}]}
,
h_{1}=b_{2}g_{1}^{(1)}+b_{1}g_{1}^{(2)}
and
h_{2}=b_{2}g_{2}^{(1)}+b_{1}g_{2}^{(2)}
are all overconvergent forms.
We set
Q(T)=P(p^{|t_{L}-\ell|}T)
so that
Q(\Frob_{p})g=P(V_{0}(p))g
.

By
[
BF20
, Proposition 5.11]
, one can find nearly overconvergent forms
H,H_{1},H_{2}
such that

\nabla(H)=d_{1}^{\ell_{1}-1}h,\nabla(H_{1})=d_{1}^{\ell_{1}-1}h_{1},\textrm{ and }\nabla(H_{2})=d_{2}^{\ell_{2}-1}h_{2}.

In particular, if we set
G=H+H_{1}+H_{2}
, then the pair
[\omega_{g},G]
represents the class
\tilde{\omega}
in
H^{|\ell|-2}_{\fp,Q}(\mathscr{U}_{\ell-4},|\ell|-2-s)
.

Let us now write down explicitly the expansions of
H,H_{1},H_{2}
.
Following a similar computation as in
[
BF20
, Proposition 5.11]
(and we ignore the terms
w_{j}
corresponding to the
\Lambda^{2}\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{A}}
-factor), we have

(8)

\displaystyle H

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{\ell_{1}-2}(-1)^{i}\frac{(\ell_{1}-2)!}{(\ell_{1}-2-i)!}d_{1}^{\ell_{1}-2-i}h\times\left(\omega_{\can,1}^{\ell_{1}-2-i}\eta_{\can,1}^{i}\otimes\omega_{\can,2}^{\ell_{2}-2}\right)\otimes\frac{dq_{2}}{q_{2}},

(9)

\displaystyle H_{1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{\ell_{1}-2}(-1)^{i}\frac{(\ell_{1}-2)!}{(\ell_{1}-2-i)!}d_{1}^{\ell_{1}-2-i}h_{1}\times\left(\omega_{\can,1}^{\ell_{1}-2-i}\eta_{\can,1}^{i}\otimes\omega_{\can,2}^{\ell_{2}-2}\right)\otimes\frac{dq_{2}}{q_{2}},

(10)

\displaystyle H_{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{\ell_{2}-2}(-1)^{i}\frac{(\ell_{2}-2)!}{(\ell_{2}-2-i)!}d_{2}^{\ell_{2}-2-i}h_{2}\times\left(\omega_{\can,1}^{\ell_{1}-2}\otimes\omega_{\can,2}^{\ell_{2}-2-i}\eta_{\can,2}^{i}\right)\otimes\frac{dq_{1}}{q_{1}}.

Now, we examine the polynomial
q
-expansion of these forms under
\tau:=\theta_{k}^{*}\varphi_{2,*}\varphi_{1}^{*}\theta_{\ell}^{*}
, which is analogous to the map denoted by
\mathrm{pr}_{r_{1}}
in
[
Hua24
, Remark 3.5]
.
As explained in
loc. cit.
, the map
\tau
undergoes a symmetrization-desymmetrization process, which produces certain factorial numbers.
After a careful computation, we have

(11)

\displaystyle\tau H

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=s}^{\ell_{1}-2}(-1)^{j}j!\binom{\ell_{1}-2-s}{j-s}\ \zeta^{*}(d_{1}^{\ell_{1}-2-j}h)\cdot\omega_{\can}^{k-2-j+s}\eta_{\can}^{j-s}\cdot\frac{dq}{q},

(12)

\displaystyle\tau H_{1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=s}^{\ell_{1}-2}(-1)^{j}j!\binom{\ell_{1}-2-s}{j-s}\ \zeta^{*}(d_{1}^{\ell_{1}-2-j}h_{1})\cdot\omega_{\can}^{k-2-j+s}\eta_{\can}^{j-s}\cdot\frac{dq}{q},

(13)

\displaystyle\tau H_{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=s}^{\ell_{2}-2}(-1)^{j}j!\binom{\ell_{2}-2-s}{j-s}\ \zeta^{*}(d_{2}^{\ell_{2}-2-j}h_{2})\cdot\omega_{\can}^{k-2-j+s}\eta_{\can}^{j-s}\cdot\frac{dq}{q}.

Under the unit root splitting, one is left with only the first term
j=s
, which recovers the result of
[
BF20
, Proposition 5.12]
.
The weight
k
form
\tau(G)=\tau(H+H_{1}+H_{2})
will play a similar role as
\mathrm{pr}_{r_{1}}(G^{[p]}\times h)
in the case of triple product
p
-adic
L
-functions in
[
Hua24
, § 3]
.

By the short exact sequence of finite polynomial cohomology,

\varphi_{2,*}\Upsilon(\omega)
and
\tau(G)
are related by the identity
Q(\mathrm{Frob}_{p})(\varphi_{2,*}\Upsilon(\omega))=\tau(G)
.
Consequently, the Abel–Jacobi image
\AJ_{p}(\Delta_{\ell,k})(\pi_{1}^{*}\omega\cup\pi_{2}^{*}\eta)
can be expressed in terms of
\tau(G)
.
Namely, one has

\displaystyle\langle Q(\mathrm{Frob}_{p})\varphi_{2,*}\Upsilon(\omega),\eta\rangle_{\dR}

\displaystyle=\langle\tau(G),\eta\rangle_{\dR}=\langle e^{\leq a}\tau(G),\eta\rangle_{\dR}

\displaystyle=\frac{\langle e^{\leq a}(\tau H+\tau H_{1}+\tau H_{2}),f^{*}\rangle}{\langle f^{*},f^{*}\rangle},

where the second equality follows from the fact that
\eta
lies in the slope
\leq a
-part of the Frobenius and
[
Hua24
, Lemma 5.3]
.

Now we examine the components
e^{\leq a}\tau H_{1}
and
e^{\leq a}\tau H_{2}
.
Recall from
[
Kaz24
, Lemma 7.22]
that we have the equation

U\zeta^{*}(V_{0}(\mathfrak{p_{2}})g^{[\mathfrak{p}_{1}]})=0=U\zeta^{*}(V_{0}(\mathfrak{p_{1}})g^{[\mathfrak{p}_{2}]})

for any overconvergent form
g
, which is derived essentially from examining the
q
-expansion.
Recall also that
h_{1}=b_{2}g_{1}^{(1)}+b_{1}g_{1}^{(2)}
.
As
g_{1}^{(1)}
is
\mathfrak{p}_{1}
-depleted and
b_{2}
can be written as a polynomial in
V_{0}(p)
and
V_{0}(\mathfrak{p}_{2})
which is divisible by
V_{0}(\mathfrak{p}_{2})
, we see that

U\zeta^{*}(b_{2}g_{1}^{(1)})=0
and similarly
U\zeta^{*}(b_{1}g_{1}^{(2)})=0
.
Furthermore, the slope projector
e^{\leq a}
can be written as a convergent power series in
U
with no constant term, so we have

e^{\leq a}\zeta^{*}(b_{2}g_{1}^{(1)})=e^{\leq a}\zeta^{*}(b_{1}g_{1}^{(2)})=e^{\leq a}\zeta^{*}(h_{1})=0.

In fact,
e^{\leq a}\zeta^{*}(d_{1}^{n}h_{1})=0
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
since
d_{1}^{n}g_{1}^{(i)}
is still
\mathfrak{p}_{i}
-depleted.
A similar argument also holds for
e^{\leq a}\zeta^{*}(d_{2}^{n}h_{2})
.
As a consequence, one gets

e^{\leq a}(\tau H_{1}+\tau H_{2})=0.

Now, we only need to focus on
\tau H
.
The computation of the Euler factors is identical to that of
[
BF20
, Theorem 5.14]
, except that we are using different normalisations of Hecke operators.
In conclusion, we have the following result.

Theorem 3.5
.

Suppose
p
splits in
L
. We have

(14)

\AJ_{p}(\Delta_{\ell,k})(\pi_{1}^{*}\omega_{g}\cup\pi_{2}^{*}\eta_{f})=\frac{\mathscr{E}_{0,p}(g,f^{*})}{\mathscr{E}_{p}(g,f^{*})}\cdot\frac{\langle e^{\leq a}H^{\prime},f^{*}\rangle}{\langle f^{*},f^{*}\rangle},

where
H^{\prime}=\sum_{j=s}^{\ell_{1}-2}(-1)^{j}j!\binom{\ell_{1}-2-s}{j-s}\ \zeta^{*}(d_{1}^{-1-j}g^{[\mathcal{P}]})\cdot\omega_{\can}^{k-2-j+s}\eta_{\can}^{j-s}\cdot\frac{dq}{q}
is viewed as a nearly overconvergent form,

\mathscr{E}_{0,p}(g,f^{*})=1-\alpha_{1}\beta_{1}\alpha_{2}\beta_{2}({(\alpha_{f}^{*})}^{-1}p^{-s-1})^{2}
, and
\mathscr{E}_{p}(g,f^{*})=P({(\alpha_{f}^{*})}^{-1}p^{-s-1})
are as in Theorem
2.30
with
t
replaced by (-s-1,0).

Now, one compares the form
H^{\prime}
to
\zeta^{*}(\nabla^{(-(s+1),0)}g_{\ell}^{[\mathcal{P}]})
.
Recall that the polynomial
q
-expansion of
\zeta^{*}(\nabla^{(-(s+1),0)}g_{\ell}^{[\mathcal{P}]})
is

(15)

\sum_{j=0}^{\ell_{1}-s-2}\binom{-t}{j}\prod_{i=0}^{j-1}(\ell_{1}-s-2-i)\zeta^{*}\left(d_{1}^{-s-1-j}g_{\ell}^{[\mathcal{P}]}(q)\right)\cdot\omega_{\can}^{k-j}\eta_{\can}^{j}.

After a change of variable, one can rewrite
H^{\prime}
as

(16)

H^{\prime}=\sum_{j=0}^{\ell_{1}-s-2}(-1)^{j+s}(j+s)!\binom{\ell_{1}-2-s}{j}\ \zeta^{*}(d_{1}^{-1-s-j}g^{[\mathcal{P}]})\cdot\omega_{\can}^{k-2-j}\eta_{\can}^{j}\cdot\frac{dq}{q}.

From the above two descriptions and formula (
3
), it can be derived from a careful computation that

(17)

H^{\dagger}(H^{\prime})=(-1)^{s}s!H^{\dagger}(\zeta^{*}(\nabla^{(-(s+1),0)}g_{\ell}^{[\mathcal{P}]})).

The
p
-adic Gross–Zagier formula now follows easily, with an extra Euler factor

\mathscr{E}(f^{*})=(1-\frac{\beta^{*}_{f}}{\alpha^{*}_{f}})=(1-\beta_{f}^{2}\chi_{f}^{-1}(p)p^{1-k})

coming from comparing Petersson products with and without level at
p
(c.f.
[
Hua24
, § 3.3]
).

In summary, we obtain

Theorem 3.6
.

Suppose
p
is split in
L
.
Let
(P,Q)=((v,n),(w,m))
be a classical point corresponding to a balanced tuple
(\ell,k)
that satisfies Assumption
3.2
.
Then we have

(18)

\mathscr{L}_{p}(\omega_{\mathbf{g}},\omega_{\mathbf{f}})(P,Q)=\frac{(-1)^{s}}{s!\mathscr{E}(f_{k}^{*})}\frac{\mathscr{E}_{p}(g_{\ell},f_{k}^{*})}{\mathscr{E}_{0,p}(g_{\ell},f_{k}^{*})}\AJ_{p}(\Delta_{\ell,k})(\pi_{1}^{*}\omega_{g_{\ell}}\cup\pi_{2}^{*}\eta_{f_{k}}).

3.5.
The inert case

In this section, we will examine the case where
p
is inert in the quadratic extension
L
, which is not covered in
[
BF20
]
.
We will keep most of the notations as before.

In this situation, there is only one Hecke operator
V_{0}(p)
and one Hecke polynomial

P(T)=(1-\alpha T)(1-\beta T),

with the usual relation
P(V_{0}(p))\omega_{g}=P(p^{|t_{L}-\ell|}\mathrm{Frob}_{p})\omega_{g}=\omega_{g}^{[p]}
.
We let
G=\nabla_{1}^{-1}\omega_{g}^{[p]}
and
Q(T)=P(p^{|t_{L}-\ell|}T)
.
Then the pair
[\omega_{g},G]
represents the class
\tilde{\omega}
in
H^{|\ell|-2}_{\fp,Q}(\mathscr{U}_{\ell-4},|\ell|-2-s)
.

The polynomial
q
-expansion of
G
is

(19)

G=\sum_{j=0}^{\ell_{1}-2}(-1)^{j}\frac{(\ell_{1}-2)!}{(\ell_{1}-2-j)!}d_{1}^{-1-j}g^{[p]}\times\left(\omega_{\can,1}^{\ell_{1}-2-i}\eta_{\can,1}^{i}\otimes\omega_{\can,2}^{\ell_{2}-2}\right)\otimes\frac{dq_{2}}{q_{2}}.

Similarly, one computes
\tau G
and get

(20)

\tau G=\sum_{j=s}^{\ell_{1}-2}(-1)^{j}j!\binom{\ell_{1}-2-s}{j-s}\ \zeta^{*}(d_{1}^{-1-j}g^{[p]})\cdot\omega_{\can}^{k-2-j+s}\eta_{\can}^{j-s}\cdot\frac{dq}{q}.

Again, we have the relation
Q(\mathrm{Frob})(\varphi_{2,*}\Upsilon(\omega))=\tau G
and the following theorem.

Theorem 3.7
.

Suppose
p
is inert in
L
. We have

(21)

\AJ_{p}(\Delta_{\ell,k})(\pi_{1}^{*}\omega_{g}\cup\pi_{2}^{*}\eta_{f})=\frac{1}{\mathscr{E}_{p}(g,f^{*})}\cdot\frac{\langle e^{\leq a}\tau G,f^{*}\rangle}{\langle f^{*},f^{*}\rangle},

where

\mathscr{E}_{p}(g,f^{*})=P((\alpha_{f}^{*})^{-1}p^{-s-1})
is as in Theorem
2.30
.

With a change of variables, one can rewrite

(22)

\tau G=\sum_{j=0}^{\ell_{1}-s-2}(-1)^{j+s}(j+s)!\binom{\ell_{1}-2-s}{j}\ \zeta^{*}(d_{1}^{-1-s-j}g^{[p]})\cdot\omega_{\can}^{k-2-j}\eta_{\can}^{j}\cdot\frac{dq}{q}.

A similar computation shows that

\tau G=(-1)^{s}s!\zeta^{*}(\nabla^{(-(s+1),0)}g_{\ell}^{[p]}).

Consequently, we obtain the
p
-adic Gross–Zagier formula:

Theorem 3.8
.

Suppose
p
is inert in
L
.
Let
(P,Q)=((v,n),(w,m))
be a classical point corresponding to a balanced tuple
(\ell,k)
that satisfies Assumption
3.2
.
Then we have

(23)

\mathscr{L}_{p}(\omega_{\mathbf{g}},\omega_{\mathbf{f}})(P,Q)=\frac{(-1)^{s}}{s!\mathscr{E}(f_{k}^{*})}{\mathscr{E}_{p}(g_{\ell},f_{k}^{*})}\AJ_{p}(\Delta_{\ell,k})(\pi_{1}^{*}\omega_{g_{\ell}}\cup\pi_{2}^{*}\eta_{f_{k}}),

where
\mathscr{E}_{p}(g_{\ell},f_{k}^{*})=P(\alpha_{f^{*}}^{-1}p^{-s-1})
and
\mathscr{E}(f_{k}^{*})=(1-\frac{\beta^{*}_{f}}{\alpha^{*}_{f}})
are as before.

Primary spinal epidural undifferentiated round cell sarcoma with Ewing-like features in an 8-year-old child: Diagnostic challenges and recovery after subtotal resection
Authors: Oualid Mohammed Hmamouche, Hamza Sriri, Baderddine Mohammadine, Marouane Hammoud, Faycal Lakhdar, Mohammed Benzagmout, Khalid Chakour, Mohammed EL Faiz Chaoui
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background: Primary epidural extraosseous Ewing sarcoma (EES) is exceptionally rare in children. Some tumors display the morphological and immunophenotypic features of EES but lack the canonical Ewing sarcoma breakpoint region 1 (EWSR1) rearrangement, creating a diagnostic dilemma.Case Description: An 8-year-old girl presented with worsening nocturnal lumbosacral pain, paraparesis (3/5), and urinary incontinence. Magnetic resonance imaging revealed a large dumbbell-shaped epidural mass extending from L3 to S1 with homogeneous contrast enhancement. Emergency L2–S1 laminotomy allowed piecemeal subtotal resection because of intimate circumferential adherence to the dura mater and emerging nerve roots. Histopathology demonstrated small round blue cells with strong CD99 positivity, whereas markers for lymphoma, neuroblastoma, and rhabdomyosarcoma were negative. Fluorescence in situ hybridization for EWSR1 rearrangement was negative, and the tumor was classified as an undifferentiated round cell sarcoma with Ewing-like features. The patient received Vincristine, Adriamycin, Cyclophosphamide Ifosfamide, Etoposide (VAC/IE) chemotherapy according to the European Ewing tumour Working Initiative of National Groups (EUROE.W.I.N.G.) protocol and achieved full neurological recovery.Conclusion: This case illustrates the diagnostic and therapeutic challenges posed by Ewing-like sarcomas in the absence of molecular confirmation. Function-preserving subtotal resection combined with multimodal chemotherapy may lead to excellent outcomes and may be preferable to radical resection when the latter carries a high neurological risk.

INTRODUCTION

Primary epidural extraosseous Ewing sarcoma (EES) is a rare entity in pediatric patients and represents a small fraction of spinal tumors reported in children.[2,3] Diagnosis typically relies on the identification of characteristic EWSR1-related gene fusions; however, a subset of tumors lacks this canonical translocation and is now categorized within the spectrum of undifferentiated round cell sarcomas or Ewing-like sarcomas.[1,8]

These tumors can pose significant diagnostic difficulties, particularly in centers where advanced molecular sequencing techniques are not readily available. Their clinical behavior and therapeutic management remain subjects of debate, particularly regarding whether they should be treated similarly to classic EESs.

Here, we report an 8-year-old female who presented with a lumbar epidural Ewing-like sarcoma with negative EWSR1 fluorescence in situ hybridization but strong CD99 expression. Notably, this tumor was successfully managed with subtotal resection followed by systemic chemotherapy.

CASE DESCRIPTION

An 8-year-old girl presented with severe nocturnal lumbosacral pain that had progressed over several weeks. One week before admission, she developed 3/5 paraparesis, sensory loss in the L4–S1 dermatomes, and urinary incontinence.

Magnetic resonance imaging demonstrated a large posterior L3–S1 epidural mass that was hypointense on T1-weighted imaging, hyperintense on T2-weighted imaging, and homogeneously enhanced with gadolinium. The mass had a dumbbell configuration, demonstrated foraminal extension, and produced severe compression of the dural sac [Figure 1].

(a) Sagittal T1-weighted MRI showing a large posterior epidural mass extending from L3 to S1. (b) Sagittal T2-weighted MRI demonstrating severe thecal sac compression. (c) Sagittal gadolinium-enhanced T1-weighted MRI showing homogeneous enhancement with foraminal extension.

Emergency surgery included an L2–S1 laminotomy for excision of an extradural, grayish, friable, and moderately vascular lesion that was circumferentially adherent to the dura and emerging nerve roots. Due to these adhesions, subtotal resection was performed [Figures 2 and 3].

Pathology

Histopathological examination revealed sheets of uniform small round blue cells with a high mitotic index [Figure 4a]. Immunohistochemical analysis demonstrated strong diffuse membranous CD99 positivity [Figure 4b]. Lymphoid markers (Leukocyte common antigen [LCA]), muscle markers (desmin and myogenin), and neuronal markers (synaptophysin) were negative, thereby excluding lymphoma, rhabdomyosarcoma, and neuroblastoma.

Histopathological and immunohistochemical features of the tumor: (a) Histopathologic section shows proliferation of poorly differentiated malignant small round. (b) Immunohistochemistry stain positive for CD99.

Molecular analysis using fluorescence in situ hybridization did not identify EWSR1 rearrangement. The final diagnosis was undifferentiated round cell sarcoma with Ewing-like features.

Postoperative status

The patient showed early postoperative neurological improvement. She received systemic chemotherapy with alternating cycles of vincristine, doxorubicin, and cyclophosphamide combined with ifosfamide and etoposide according to the EURO-E.W.I.N.G. protocol.

Follow-up magnetic resonance imaging demonstrated complete decompression of the dural sac, with only a thin residual epidural component that remained stable [Figure 5]. One year postoperatively, she had regained full motor function (5/5 strength) and remained free of disease progression.

(a) Sagittal postcontrast T1-weighted MRI showing marked reduction of the epidural lesion with adequate decompression of the thecal sac. (b) Sagittal T2-weighted MRI demonstrating a stable minimal residual epidural component without significant compression. (c) Axial MRI confirming spinal canal decompression and stability of the residual lesion.

In resource-limited settings where next-generation sequencing is not readily available, diagnosis may rely on a combination of morphology, immunohistochemistry, and exclusion of other small round cell malignancies.

In our case, strong CD99 expression with negative markers for lymphoma, rhabdomyosarcoma, and neuroblastoma supported the diagnosis of an Ewing-like sarcoma despite the absence of EWSR1 rearrangement.

Although gross total resection may improve local control, it must be balanced against the risk of neurological damage. In the present case, the tumor demonstrated extensive circumferential adherence to the dura and nerve roots. Subtotal resection was therefore performed to achieve neural decompression while avoiding neurological injury and preserving function.

EESs are highly chemosensitive, and modern multimodal treatment protocols may provide favorable outcomes even in the presence of residual disease, as demonstrated in this case.[6]

CONCLUSION

Undifferentiated round cell sarcomas with Ewing-like features may present with clinical and radiological characteristics similar to those of classical EES despite the absence of detectable EWSR1 gene rearrangement. Subtotal tumor resection combined with standard Ewing-based chemotherapy can result in favorable neurological and oncological outcomes.

Revisit of the electromagnetic correction to τ → π ​ π ​ ν τ \tau\to\pi\pi\nu_{\tau} and its implication for muon g − 2 g-2 based on τ \tau data
Zhi-Xin Li, Ao Li, Jin Hao, Chun-Gui Duan, Zhi-Hui Guo

Abstract
In this work we focus on the evaluation of the leading-order hadronic vacuum polarization contribution from the π ​ π \pi\pi channel to the muon anomalous magnetic moment a μ a_{\mu} by using the experimental τ → π ​ π ​ ν τ \tau\to\pi\pi\nu_{\tau} data. The isospin breaking corrections play the decisive role in this approach of computing a μ a_{\mu} . One of such important isospin breaking sources is the long-distance electromagnetic correction factor G EM G_{\rm EM} of the τ → π ​ π ​ ν τ \tau\to\pi\pi\nu_{\tau} process from the real photon radiation. The latter effect can be calculated from the τ → π ​ π ​ ν τ ​ γ \tau\to\pi\pi\nu_{\tau}\gamma amplitude, which is revised in this work within the resonance chiral theory by simultaneously including the even-intrinsic-parity and odd-intrinsic-parity resonance operators. We update the determination of the only unknown resonance coupling through the ω → π 0 ​ π 0 ​ γ \omega\to\pi^{0}\pi^{0}\gamma decay by including contributions from both the vector and scalar resonances. By taking other remaining contributions from the muon g − 2 g-2 White Paper 2025, we further revise the complete value of a μ a_{\mu} , which turns out to deviate from the newest world average result after Fermilab’s measurement at the level of 2.7 σ \sigma .

We take a different plan to pursue the calculation of

(19)

and

3
Electromagnetic corrections to
\tau^{-}\to\pi^{-}\pi^{0}\nu_{\tau}

The EM corrections to the
\tau^{-}\to\pi^{-}\pi^{0}\nu_{\tau}
process consist of two parts, the virtual correction from the photon loops and the real photon radiative correction. The virtual correction from the one-loop photon diagrams to the
\tau^{-}\to\pi^{-}\pi^{0}\nu_{\tau}
decay has been computed in Ref.
[
10
]
. Up to
O(\alpha)
, being
\alpha=e^{2}/(4\pi)
the fine structure constant of QED, the real photon radiative correction can be calculated from the
\tau^{-}\to\pi^{-}\pi^{0}\gamma\nu_{\tau}
process.
A novelty of the present study stems from the use of the revised
\tau^{-}\to\pi^{-}\pi^{0}\gamma\nu_{\tau}
amplitude discussed in the previous section to calculate the real radiative correction part of
G_{\text{EM}}(t)
.
In this way, our work also provides an independent crosscheck of the previous calculations
[
9
,
14
]
.

In the case of neglecting the EM corrections, the differential decay width of
\tau^{-}\to\pi^{-}\pi^{0}\nu_{\tau}
can be written as

\displaystyle\frac{d\Gamma^{(0)}_{\tau_{\pi\pi}}}{dt}=\frac{G_{F}^{2}\left|V_{ud}\right|^{2}m_{\tau}^{3}S_{\text{EW}}}{384\pi^{3}}\left(1-\frac{4m_{\pi}^{2}}{t}\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}\left(1-\frac{t}{m_{\tau}^{2}}\right)^{2}\left(1+\frac{2t}{m_{\tau}^{2}}\right)\left|F_{\pi\pi}^{(-)}(t)\right|^{2}\,,

(32)

which is governed by the
\pi^{-}\pi^{0}
vector form factor
F_{\pi\pi}^{(-)}(t)
, depending on a single variable
t=(p_{\pi^{-}}+p_{\pi^{0}})^{2}
. The superscript (0) in
d\Gamma^{(0)}_{\tau_{\pi\pi}}/dt
denotes the quantify that does not include the EM corrections. The short-distance electroweak correction
S_{\text{EW}}=1.0233\pm 0.0003
from Ref.
[
15
]
will be used throughout.

When including the effects from the virtual photons, one would need to introduce another kinematical variable
u=(p_{\tau^{-}}-p_{\pi^{-}})^{2}
, in addition to
t
, to calculate the EM contributions.
Up to
O(\alpha)
, the double differential decay width of the photon inclusive process takes the form
[
10
]

\displaystyle\frac{d\Gamma_{\tau_{\pi\pi[\gamma]}}}{dt\,du}=\frac{G_{F}^{2}S_{\text{EW}}|V_{ud}|^{2}}{64\pi^{3}m_{\tau}^{3}}\left|F_{\pi\pi}^{(-)}(t)\right|^{2}D(t,u)\left[1+2f^{\text{elm}}_{\text{loop}}(u,M_{\gamma})+g_{\rm rad}(t,u,M_{\gamma})\right]\,,

(33)

where the kinematical factor
D(t,u)
is

\displaystyle D(t,u)=\frac{1}{2}m_{\tau}^{2}\left(m_{\tau}^{2}-t\right)+2m_{\pi}^{4}-2u\left(m_{\tau}^{2}-t+2m_{\pi}^{2}\right)+2u^{2}\,,

(34)

and the one-loop virtual photon contribution reads
[
10
]

\displaystyle f^{\text{elm}}_{\text{loop}}(u,M_{\gamma})=

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha}{4\pi}\bigg[\left(u-m_{\pi}^{2}\right)\mathcal{A}(u)+\left(u-m_{\pi}^{2}-m_{\tau}^{2}\right)\mathcal{B}(u)

\displaystyle\qquad+2\left(m_{\pi}^{2}+m_{\tau}^{2}-u\right)\mathcal{C}(u,M_{\gamma})+2\log\frac{m_{\pi}m_{\tau}}{M_{\gamma}^{2}}\bigg]\,,

(35)

with

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(u)=\frac{1}{s}\left(-\frac{1}{2}\log{r_{\tau}}+\frac{2-y_{\tau}}{\sqrt{r_{\tau}}}\frac{x_{\tau}}{1-x_{\tau}^{2}}\log{x_{\tau}}\right),

(36)

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(u)=\frac{1}{s}\left(\frac{1}{2}\log{r_{\tau}}+\frac{2r_{\tau}-y_{\tau}}{\sqrt{r_{\tau}}}\frac{x_{\tau}}{1-x_{\tau}^{2}}\log{x_{\tau}}\right),

(37)

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}(u,M_{\gamma})=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{m_{\tau}m_{\pi}}\frac{x_{\tau}}{1-x_{\tau}^{2}}\left(-\frac{1}{2}\log^{2}{x_{\tau}}+2\log{x_{\tau}}\log{\left(1-x_{\tau}^{2}\right)}\right.

\displaystyle\left.-\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}+\frac{1}{8}\log^{2}{r_{\tau}}+Li_{2}\left(x_{\tau}^{2}\right)+Li_{2}\left(1-\frac{x_{\tau}}{\sqrt{r_{\tau}}}\right)\right.

\displaystyle\left.+Li_{2}\left(1-x_{\tau}\sqrt{r_{\tau}}\right)-\log{x_{\tau}}\log{\frac{M_{\gamma}^{2}}{m_{\tau}m_{\pi}}}\right),

(38)

and

\displaystyle r_{\tau}=\frac{m_{\tau}^{2}}{m_{\pi}^{2}},\quad y_{\tau}=1+r_{\tau}-\frac{u}{m_{\pi}^{2}},\quad x_{\tau}=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{r_{\tau}}}\left(y_{\tau}-\sqrt{y_{\tau}^{2}-4r_{\tau}}\right)\,.

(39)

In Eq. (
33
),
g_{\rm rad}
stands for the real photon corrections calculated from the radiative decay process of
\tau^{-}\to\pi^{-}\pi^{0}\gamma\nu_{\tau}
.
As explicitly demonstrated in detail in Ref.
[
9
]
, the infrared divergence due to the massless photon in the one-loop diagrams is canceled by the divergence from the bremsstrahlung from the particles
\tau^{-}
and
\pi^{-}
in the soft photon limit, which makes the photon inclusive decay width (
33
) infrared finite. In practice, one can retain a tiny finite mass
M_{\gamma}
for the photon to separately regulate the infrared divergences arising from the virtual and real photons, i.e.,
f^{\text{elm}}_{\text{loop}}
and
g_{\rm rad}
, when doing numerical calculations.

To integrate out the
u
variable in Eq. (
33
) with its kinematical allowed upper and lower limits

\displaystyle u_{\rm max/min}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{1-\frac{4m_{\pi}^{2}}{t}}\left(m_{\tau}^{2}-t\right)\pm\frac{1}{2}\left(m_{\tau}^{2}+2m_{\pi}^{2}-t\right)\,,

(40)

one can obtain the EM correction function
G_{\text{EM}}(t)
for the two-pion tau decay

\displaystyle\frac{d\Gamma_{\tau_{\pi\pi[\gamma]}}}{dt}=\frac{d\Gamma_{\tau_{\pi\pi}}^{(0)}}{dt}G_{\text{EM}}(t)\,.

(41)

Since after the integration of
u
the first term inside the square bracket of Eq. (
33
) simply gives the non-radiative decay width (
32
), one can conveniently split the virtual (
v
) and real (
r
) photon corrections for
G_{\text{EM}}
as

G_{\text{EM}}(t)=1+G_{\text{EM}}^{(v)}(t)+G_{\text{EM}}^{(r)}(t)\,.

(42)

The virtual correction part
G_{\text{EM}}^{(v)}(t)
takes the form

\displaystyle G_{\text{EM}}^{(v)}(t)=\frac{12\int^{u_{\rm max}}_{u_{\rm min}}{D(t,u)\,f^{\text{elm}}_{\text{loop}}(u,M_{\gamma})du}}{m_{\tau}^{6}\left(1-\frac{4m_{\pi}^{2}}{t}\right)^{3/2}\left(1-\frac{t}{m_{\tau}^{2}}\right)^{2}\left(1+\frac{2t}{m_{\tau}^{2}}\right)}\,.

(43)

The real-photon correction function is similarly given by

\displaystyle G_{\text{EM}}^{(r)}(t)=\frac{6\int^{u_{\rm max}}_{u_{\rm min}}{D(t,u)\,g_{\rm rad}(t,u,M_{\gamma})du}}{m_{\tau}^{6}\left(1-\frac{4m_{\pi}^{2}}{t}\right)^{3/2}\left(1-\frac{t}{m_{\tau}^{2}}\right)^{2}\left(1+\frac{2t}{m_{\tau}^{2}}\right)}\,,

(44)

where
g_{\rm rad}
stems from the
\tau^{-}\to\pi^{-}\pi^{0}\gamma\nu_{\tau}
process. Under the leading Low’s approximation
[
52
]
, i.e., by only keeping the most singular
1/k^{2}
term (with
k
the photon momentum) and neglecting the terms of
O(k^{n\geq-1})
for the amplitude squared of the
\tau^{-}\to\pi^{-}\pi^{0}\gamma\nu_{\tau}
process in the soft photon limit
k\to 0
, analytical expressions can be obtained for
g_{\rm rad}(t,u,M_{\gamma})
, which has been demonstrated to exactly cancel the infrared divergence of
f^{\text{elm}}_{\text{loop}}(u,M_{\gamma})
when taking
M_{\gamma}\to 0

[
9
]
. The sum of the virtual correction
G_{\text{EM}}^{(v)}(t)
and the leading Low (LL) part of the real correction
G_{\text{EM}}^{(r,LL)}(t)
, gives a physically meaningful quantity
G_{\text{EM}}^{(0)}(t)\equiv G_{\text{EM}}^{(v)}(t)+G_{\text{EM}}^{(r,LL)}(t)
, which is free of infrared singularity. However,
G_{\text{EM}}^{(0)}(t)
turns out to be insufficient to provide a good approximation in Eq. (
41
), since the photon inclusive decay width incorporates the dynamics in the whole photon energy range, not only in the low energy, which has been verified in many works
[
9
,
12
,
13
,
14
]
.

In the present work, instead of using Eq. (
44
) to obtain the
G_{\text{EM}}^{(r)}(t)
, we will directly take the complete
\tau^{-}\to\pi^{-}\pi^{0}\gamma\nu_{\tau}
amplitude and the phase-space receipt from Ref.
[
17
]
to calculate the full real radiative correction part of
G_{\text{EM}}^{(r)}(t)
. In this case, the latter quantity is given by

\displaystyle G_{\text{EM}}^{(r)}(t)=\frac{1}{\frac{d\Gamma_{\tau_{\pi\pi}}^{(0)}}{dt}}\frac{\pi^{2}}{32(2\pi)^{12}m_{\tau}^{2}}\int_{S_{\pi\pi\nu}^{-}}^{S_{\pi\pi\nu}^{+}}dS_{\pi\pi\nu}\int_{S_{\nu\gamma}^{-}}^{S_{\nu\gamma}^{+}}dS_{\nu\gamma}\int_{S_{\pi\nu}^{-}}^{S_{\pi\nu}^{+}}dS_{\pi\nu}\int_{S_{\pi\nu\gamma}^{-}}^{S_{\pi\nu\gamma}^{+}}\frac{dS_{\pi\nu\gamma}}{\sqrt{-\Delta_{4}}}\overline{|\mathcal{M}|}^{2}_{\tau\to\pi\pi\nu_{\tau}\gamma}\,,

(45)

where
\overline{|\mathcal{M}|}^{2}_{\tau\to\pi\pi\nu_{\tau}\gamma}
stands for the amplitude squared after taking the spin sum/average of the initial and final states,
\Delta_{4}
is the Gram determinant
[
53
]
,
S_{ij\cdots}=(p_{i}+p_{j}+\cdots)^{2}
and the upper and lower integration limits
S_{ij\cdots}^{\pm}
are given in the Appendix of Ref.
[
17
]
.
By replacing
\overline{|\mathcal{M}|}^{2}_{\tau\to\pi\pi\nu_{\tau}\gamma}
in Eq. (
45
) with the amplitude from the leading Low expansion and also the full amplitude from Ref.
[
9
]
, our numerical calculation perfectly reproduces the curves in the latter reference. Moreover, the sum of the virtual correction of Eq. (
43
) and the real correction of Eq. (
45
) is verified to be numerically stable by varying the small finite photon mass
M_{\gamma}
, which reassures the infrared finite feature of the
G_{\text{EM}}(t)
function. The results of the corresponding

functions are illustrated in Fig.

.

In this part, we proceed the evaluation of

\displaystyle x=\frac{1-\left(1-4m_{\mu}^{2}/t\right)^{1/2}}{1+\left(1-4m_{\mu}^{2}/t\right)^{1/2}}\,.

5
Summary and conclusions

We have revisited the computation of the muon anomalous magnetic moment
a_{\mu}
by using the experimental
\tau\to\pi\pi\nu_{\tau}
data to assess the leading-order hadronic vacuum polarization from the
\pi\pi
channel. The real-photon electromagnetic correction to the
\tau\to\pi\pi\nu_{\tau}
process has been calculated based on the
\tau\to\pi\pi\gamma\nu_{\tau}
amplitude, which is computed by taking the minimal resonance chiral theory with even parity operators and also the anomalous resonance operators with the
VVP
and
VJP
types. After imposing the high energy constraints, the only unknown resonance coupling of the
VVP
type, namely
d_{4}
, is newly determined through the
\omega\to\pi^{0}\pi^{0}\gamma
process by including both the vector and scalar resonances. The negative solution of
d_{4}=-0.42
gives rather a similar long-distance electromagnetic correction function
G_{\text{EM}}(t)
as the previous determination in Ref.
[
9
]
and also one set of the
O(p^{4})
results in Ref.
[
14
]
. While, the positive solution of
d_{4}=1.01
obviously deviates from the former two results, but gives compatible result as the
O(p^{6})
case in the latter reference. We therefore take the negative solution of
d_{4}
as the baseline in this work and consider the difference caused by the two solutions of
d_{4}
as additional theoretical systematical uncertainty.

After combining the isospin breaking effects from other sources, we acquire the full set of isospin breaking correction function
R_{\rm{IB}}(t)
, which is further exploited to calculate
a_{\mu}^{\rm HVP,LO|_{\pi\pi}}
by using the experimental
\tau\to\pi\pi\nu_{\tau}
data from Belle, ALEPH, CLEO and OPAL. Our result agrees with the conservative estimation provided in the muon
g-2
White Paper 2025 within the uncertainties. The resulting deviation of
a_{\mu}
between the current estimation and the updated world average in Ref.
[
3
]
lies at the level of 2.7
\sigma
.

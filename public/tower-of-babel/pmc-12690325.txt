Machine learning-enhanced radiomics for predicting portal vein thrombectomy in liver transplantation
Authors: Xing-Yu Luo, Zhi-Kun Liu, Chen-Hao Gao, Shu-Sen Zheng, Zhe Yang
Published: 2025

Portal vein thrombosis (PVT) is a common complication during liver transplantation, with an incidence of approximately 5–26% among patients with end-stage liver disease (1). Its presence of PVT significantly increases surgical complexity, potentially leading to failure of portal vein reconstruction, postoperative anastomotic stenosis, or even graft liver failure (1,2). Conventionally, the feasibility of complete PVT thrombectomy has been evaluated using preoperative cross-sectional imaging, such as contrast-enhanced computed tomography (CT) or magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), combined with intraoperative exploration; however, these methods are limited by spatial resolution and interoperator variability, compromising predictive reliability. Recently, radiomics, coupled with machine learning, has emerged as a transformative approach in liver disease research, leveraging high-throughput feature extraction from medical images to enhance diagnostic stratification and prognostic prediction, as demonstrated by validated studies (3,4). This study developed and validated a CT-based radiomic predictive model to non-invasively quantify the probability of complete PVT thrombectomy during liver transplantation, offering a tool to guide surgical planning, improve postoperative outcomes, and establish evidence-based standards for PVT management risk stratification.

This single-center retrospective cohort study, conducted at a tertiary academic liver transplant center, consecutively enrolled 108 patients who underwent deceased-donor liver transplantation between July 2017 and October 2023 and had radiologically confirmed PVT on multiphase contrast-enhanced CT prior to transplantation. The study received ethical approval from the institutional ethics committees of the Shulan (Hangzhou) Hospital (KY2024016) and adhered to the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments. All patient data were anonymized using a double-blind encryption protocol prior to analysis. Inclusion criteria were: (I) multiphase contrast-enhanced CT diagnosis of PVT before transplantation; (II) intraoperative histopathological confirmation of benign PVT; (III) portal venous phase abdominal CT acquired ≤30 days preoperatively; and (IV) comprehensive clinical data, including hepatitis B virus (HBV) etiology and PVT Yerdel classification. Exclusion criteria included: (I) radiological evidence of malignant portal vein tumor thrombosis (PVTT); (II) technically inadequate imaging [motion artifacts ≥ grade 2 or contrast opacification <100 Hounsfield unit (HU)]; (III) missing clinical variables (>20% data points absent), and (IV) prior portal vein-directed endovascular interventions (e.g., stenting, embolization, or thrombolysis). Using computer-generated block randomization (block size =6), the cohort was stratified into training (n=81, 75%) and validation (n=27, 25%) sets at a 3:1 ratio (Table 1), consistent with TRIPOD Type 2b validation standards. Baseline characteristics showed no significant intergroup differences (all P>0.05, Mann-Whitney U or χ2 tests).

All patients underwent triphasic contrast-enhanced abdominal CT within 30 days preoperatively using a SOMATOM Force dual-source CT scanner (Siemens Healthineers, Germany), with acquisition parameters including a tube voltage of 120 kVp, tube current of 200–400 mAs (CARE Dose4D modulated), slice thickness of 3 mm, and reconstruction interval of 1 mm (50% overlap). Scans were performed following standardized injection of non-ionic contrast agent iopromide (350 mgI/mL; 0.8 mL/kg body weight) at a 3.5 mL/s flow rate, acquiring non-contrast images followed by triphasic enhanced sequences [arterial (25–35 s), portal venous (60–70 s), and delayed (180 s) phases] with bolus-triggered initiation. Portal venous phase images were used for semi-automated region of interest (ROI) segmentation (ITK-SNAP v3.8.0), delineating PVT extent from the main portal vein origin to the terminal branching point of the superior mesenteric vein (SMV), defined as the loss of continuous vascular lumen on axial images (≤3 mm slice thickness). Segmentations were performed by two trained researchers and independently validated by two fellowship-trained abdominal radiologists (8 and 6 years of liver imaging experience), achieving inter- and intra-observer intraclass correlation coefficients (ICCs) exceeding 0.75 (Landis & Koch criteria: good agreement), confirming method reliability.

Following Image Biomarker Standardisation Initiative (IBSI) compliant standardization, 1,834 radiomic features were extracted from each PVT ROI using PyRadiomics (v3.1.0, Python 3.8). Features included: (I) 14 three-dimensional shape features (e.g., thrombus volume, surface area); (II) 18 first-order statistical features (e.g., mean, skewness); (III) 75 second-order texture features from gray-level co-occurrence matrix (GLCM: entropy, contrast) and gray-level run-length matrix (GLRLM: run-length variance); and (IV) 1,727 higher-order features via multi-scale Laplacian of Gaussian filtering (σ=1–5 mm) and wavelet decomposition. Features underwent Z-score normalization and batch effect correction using the ComBat algorithm. A four-stage pipeline optimized feature selection: (I) 1,116 features with ICC >0.75 were retained; (II) 193 features with significant univariate associations [P<0.05, false discovery rate (FDR)-adjusted] were selected; (III) 140 collinear features (Pearson’s r>0.5) were excluded; and (IV) 11 robust predictive features were identified through 10-fold cross-validated least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression (λ=0.0193, Figure S1). The final features characterized thrombus properties across morphological (volume/surface ratio), densitometric (HU distribution), architectural (texture patterns), and spatial (anatomic distribution) domains, including Wavelet-LHL_Firstorder_Skewness (intensity distribution asymmetry), Gradient_Firstorder_Skewness (surface irregularity), and Wavelet-LLL_GLDM_SmallDependenceHighGrayLevelEmphasis (focal calcification marker).

Utilizing the 11 optimally selected features, six machine learning algorithms were implemented in the training cohort (Figure 1A): logistic regression (LR), support vector machine (SVM), random forest (RF), extremely randomized trees (ExtraTrees), light gradient boosting machine (LightGBM), and multilayer perceptron (MLP). Models underwent 10-fold cross-validation with stratified sampling for hyperparameter optimization, ensuring robustness through strict separation of training and validation data (Tables S1-S3). The ExtraTrees classifier demonstrated superior performance in the validation cohort, with an area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) of 0.766 [95% confidence interval (CI): 0.567–0.965], balanced accuracy of 0.786 (sensitivity: 0.571, specificity: 1.000), and F1-score of 0.727. Other models yielded lower AUCs: LR, 0.746; SVM, 0.675; RF, 0.702; LightGBM, 0.671; and MLP, 0.754 (Figure S1). DeLong’s test indicated no significant difference in AUC between ExtraTrees and other models (P>0.05). Feature contribution analysis revealed that Gradient_Firstorder_Skewness had the greatest predictive impact (importance score: 0.23, Figure S2), likely reflecting interfacial characteristics between the thrombus surface and vessel wall, influencing thrombectomy difficulty.

The model’s clinical utility was validated through perioperative and long-term outcomes. In the training cohort, complete thrombectomy cases (n=67) showed a 21% reduction in median red blood cell (RBC) transfusion volume (1,350 vs. 1,700 mL, P=0.13), a benefit that reached statistical significance in the validation cohort (1,050 vs. 2,400 mL, 56% reduction, P=0.004, Figure 1B). Survival analysis revealed greater advantages: the training cohort exhibited 12.3% and 14.0% absolute improvements in 1- and 3-year survival rates (76.2% and 70.9% vs. 64.1% and 56.9%, log-rank P=0.20), while the validation cohort demonstrated 35.0% and 40.4% differences (93.3% and 75.4% vs. 58.3% and 35.0%, log-rank P=0.04, Figure 1C), corresponding to a 61% mortality risk reduction. Kaplan-Meier curves confirmed sustained survival benefits, with complete thrombectomy cases exhibiting superior overall survival.

This study developed an ExtraTrees model based on radiomics (AUC =0.766), achieving, for the first time, the prediction of the likelihood of complete PVT thrombectomy during liver transplantation, demonstrating significant clinical potential. However, traditional diagnostic modalities, including Doppler ultrasound, CT, and MRI, rely on subjective assessments limited by low sensitivity, poor reproducibility, and inability to predict intraoperative thrombectomy feasibility. To address this, this study integrated radiomics and machine learning to develop a non-invasive predictive model, providing a scientific basis for intraoperative portal vein reconstruction. The model captured thrombus heterogeneity through higher-order features (e.g., Wavelet-LHL_Firstorder_Skewness, Gradient_Firstorder_Skewness), potentially reflecting fibrosis and calcification, thus offering a novel perspective for evaluating thrombectomy difficulty. Its clinical utility significantly optimized surgical strategies, reduced thrombus management complexity, and improved patient outcomes. Machine learning and radiomics have demonstrated broad applicability in related hepatic conditions; for instance, RF models have predicted PVT risk following splenectomy (AUC: 0.750–0.855) (5), while CT-based radiomics models showed prognostic value for hepatocellular carcinoma recurrence [concordance index (C-index): 0.646–0.762] (4). Additionally, CT-based radiomic features have been utilized to predict microvascular invasion in hepatocellular carcinoma (6), and the integration of preoperative CT radiomics with clinical variables has enabled the prediction of liver cancer recurrence post-transplantation (AUC: 0.654–0.990) (7). Furthermore, combining CT radiomics and clinical data 3–6 months post-transplantation has facilitated the assessment of graft fibrosis risk (AUC: 0.664–0.811) (8). These findings validate the robustness of feature extraction strategies employed in the current study, supporting its scientific foundation. However, research on radiomics or combined clinical feature analysis in thromboembolism remains limited, underscoring the pioneering role of this study. Consequently, the model provides precise data support for transplant surgeons, significantly enhancing clinical decision-making and patient management.

This study’s primary innovations include: (I) using radiomics to extract multidimensional features from preoperative CT, comprehensively characterizing PVT imaging properties; (II) systematically comparing six machine learning models, identifying ExtraTrees as the best performer; and (III) validating clinical value through intraoperative transfusion volumes, postoperative complications, and overall survival. However, limitations include the small sample size (n=108) and single-center design. Future multicenter studies integrating multimodal data (e.g., MRI, ultrasound) and deep learning could enhance the model’s generalizability and predictive performance, advancing its translational value in personalized precision medicine.

In conclusion, the radiomics-based ExtraTrees model shows promising potential in predicting the likelihood of complete PVT thrombectomy during liver transplantation, providing a novel tool that may support preoperative risk assessment. While this approach holds potential for clinical application, its effectiveness should be further validated, and traditional imaging techniques, which remain irreplaceable for their established diagnostic value, should continue to play a complementary role.

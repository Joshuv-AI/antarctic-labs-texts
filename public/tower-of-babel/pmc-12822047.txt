Obstructive fibrinous tracheal pseudomembrane in a hemodialysis patient: a case report and review of literature
Authors: Jingjing Hou, Haitao Li, Fu Niu, Xiaodan Mu, Shushen Zhang, Lining Huang, Zhigang Cai
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundObstructive fibrinous tracheal pseudomembrane (OFTP) is a relatively rare complication of endotracheal intubation. It can develop with short-term intubation, causing respiratory symptoms shortly after extubation. Delayed recognition of OFTP is common in clinical practice and has been reported to lead to fatal airway obstruction in some case studies. In this case report, we describe a patient of end stage renal disease (ESRD) on hemodialysis (HD) who presented with OFTP. The patient experienced cardiac arrest secondary to hyperkalemia and was intubated for 23 h. After extubation, the patient developed symptoms from progressive dyspnea and stridor to airway obstruction, at which point, the patient was substantially re-intubated. Subsequent bronchoscopy confirmed the diagnosis of OFTP. Meanwhile, the patient’s CT scans revealed airway features correlating with the symptomatic progression.ConclusionProgressive dyspnea and stridor and airway abnormalities on CT scans after extubation should raise clinical considering OFTP, particularly in patients with ESRD. Fast diagnosis and treatment with bronchoscopy is crucial for enhancing patient outcomes.Keywords: Obstructive fibrinous tracheal pseudomembrane, Endotracheal intubation, End stage renal disease

Introduction

Obstructive fibrinous tracheal pseudomembrane (OFTP) is a rare and potentially life-threatening condition associated with endotracheal intubation.The pathological hallmark of OFTP is a thick pseudomembrane forming on the tracheal wall. It can develop with short-term intubation, causing respiratory symptoms shortly after extubation. Depending on the degree of tracheal obstruction caused by pseudomembrane, OFTP can present asymptomatically and with manifestations ranging from mild dyspnea, positional dyspnea and stridor, acute respiratory failure and even death. Symptoms may be induced by partial detachment of the pseudomembrane from the tracheal wall. Bronchoscopy serves both diagnostic and therapeutic purposes for OFTP. Prompt removal of the pseudomembrane typically leads to rapid clinical improvement. This report describes the development process of OFTP through patient symptoms and serial CT examinations. Additionally, we highlight the potential higher risk of OFTP in patients with ESRD.

Case report

A 52-year-old man with a history of diabetes mellitus, hypertension, heart failure, and hemodialysis-dependent end-stage renal disease presented to the emergency department of a local hospital with a 13-hour history of dyspnea and fatigue following a missed hemodialysis session. Emergency arterial blood gas analysis revealed hyperkalemia (8.4 mmol/L). Within 10 min of symptom onset, the patient developed sudden loss of consciousness concurrent with ECG-documented ventricular fibrillation, requiring emergent cardiopulmonary resuscitation (CPR), endotracheal intubation and mechanical ventilation. He was intubated and mechanical ventilated for 23 h. On the second day post-extubation, the patient complained of pharyngeal discomfort, and difficulty expectorating. Due to clinical suspicion of pulmonary infection, a chest CT scans was obtained, demonstrating bilateral pulmonary infiltrates with localized consolidations predominantly affecting the lower lobes. Antibiotic therapy was initiated based on the radiographic findings. However, the patient’s clinical condition deteriorated, manifesting as progressive dyspnea on day 3 post-extubation, followed by the development of inspiratory dyspnea and stridor on day 5 post-extubation. Laryngoscopy identified a subglottic mass. The patient was subsequently transferred to our hospital for further management. A neck and chest CT were performed in the Emergency Department on day 6 post-extubation to assess airway patency. While awaiting inpatient admission, the patient developed acute airway obstruction manifested with sudden-onset wheezing and respiratory distress, and required urgent reintubation with mechanical ventilation.

After admission, flexible bronchoscopy (diameter 4.9 mm, inner diameter 2.0 mm) revealed ring-shape white pseudomembranes in the tracheal lumen, which were successfully removed using cryoprobe (KOOLAND 1.8 mm×1000 mm) (Fig. 1). Histopathological examination of the removed tissue confirmed the diagnosis of OFTP (Fig. 2). The patient was successfully extubated with complete resolution of symptoms. Follow-up chest CT performed at the local hospital one week and 1–2 months post-discharge showed no evidence of tracheal stenosis. A telephone follow-up was conducted one year later revealed no respiratory symptoms or other discomforts during daily activities.

Discussion

The fibrinous tracheal pseudomembrane was first described in 1981, and was subsequently designated as obstructive fibrinous tracheal pseudomembrane (OFTP) in 2000 [1]. Endotracheal intubation can cause complications like tracheal stenosis, ulceration, tracheomalacia. This rare potentially fatal complication occurs following short time endotracheal intubation.The pseudomembrane is thick, layering and mucoid, following the anatomy of the tracheal wall. Clinical manifestations depend on the degree of airway obstruction caused by the pseudomembrane. In clinical practice, the diagnosis is often established through bronchoscopy. In one case, OFTP was incidentally discovered during bronchoscopy [2], appearing as a thin layer completely adhered to the tracheal wall, with the patient exhibiting only mild dyspneic symptoms. This finding suggests that subclinical or mild forms of OFTP may exist and could be underdiagnosed. If the OFTP is mild and the symptoms are not significant, then therapeutic removal may not be mandatory, and treating with nebulizations and appropriate antibiotics may improve the symptoms, only when the obstruction is significant then removal would be mandatory.

Previous review has identified two major risk factors for OFTP development: emergency endotracheal intubation and pre-existing local tracheal inflammation [3]. Although the exact pathogenesis remains poorly understood, current evidence suggests that mucosal ischemic injury secondary to endotracheal tube cuff pressure is a key mechanistic factor. OFTP has also been observed at the distal endotracheal tube tip, suggesting that local mechanical trauma during intubation and tube placement might also cause OFTP. The primary therapeutic approach involves rigid and flexible bronchoscopic extraction of the pseudomembrane, which generally results in a favorable prognosis. Only one case of pseudomembrane recurrence has been reported. Importantly, longitudinal follow-up of reported cases has not identified subsequent tracheal stenosis.

We describe a case of OFTP in a patient with chronic renal disease. A review of the clinical course revealed findings consistent with OFTP on the initial chest CT scan performed on post-intubation day 2 (Fig. 3a), which was not recognized initially. Comparative analysis with follow-up CT imaging on post-extubation day 6 demonstrated partial detachment of the pseudomembrane from the tracheal wall (Fig. 3b). A CT scan performed one week post-discharge showed normal tracheal wall (Fig. 3c). The temporal progression suggests rapid development of OFTP post-intubation, with subsequent partial detachment occurring post-extubation due to loss of endotracheal tube scaffolding. This pathophysiological process correlated with the patient’s evolving clinical manifestations, from initial asymptomatic status to difficulty expectorating, shortness of breath, progressive dyspnea, stridor, and finally acute airway obstruction. This underscores the importance of closely monitoring airway symptoms after extubation and maintaining vigilance for OFTP to prevent life-threatening obstruction. Key risk factors in this patient likely included elevated levels of uremic toxins in the airway and airway edema [4], supported by the patient’s chronic renal failure, prolonged hemodialysis, significant interdialytic weight gain (5Kg), and the missed dialysis session. Comorbidities such as hypoperfusion (due to cardiac arrest) and diabetes mellitus likely contributed to a complex mucosal microenvironment facilitating OFTP development.

An epidemiological analysis of reported OFTP cases in resent 20 years (including adults > 16 years, excluding infective pseudomembrane) demonstrates ESRD comorbidity in 4 cases (Table 1). With our reported case, ESRD prevalence reaches 5/34 (14.7%), suggesting patients with ESRD represent a high-risk population for developing OFTP. The underlying mechanism remains unclear and the link between ESRD and pseudomembrane need to further investigate.

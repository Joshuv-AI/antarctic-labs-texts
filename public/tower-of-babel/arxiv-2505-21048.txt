Entanglement negativity of general 2 × ( 2 ​ n + 1 ) 2\times(2n+1) qubit-qudit system
Sanskriti Agrawal, Raktim Abir

Abstract
The presence of negative eigenvalue(s) in the spectrum of the partially transposed density matrix confirms the entanglement of the quantum state. The negative eigenvalue(s) also quantify the amount of entanglement through novel entanglement measures, e . g . e.g. , negativity. In this paper, we have presented a complete characterization of the spectrum of the partially transposed density matrix for the pure bipartite quantum state acting on a generic ℋ s ​ ( d ​ i ​ m ​ 2 ) ⊗ ℋ l ​ ( d ​ i ​ m ​ 2 ​ l + 1 ) {\cal H}_{s}(dim~2)\otimes{\cal{H}}_{l}(dim~2l+1) Hilbert space ( e . g . e.g. , spin - orbital angular momentum space). We found that the spectrum contains only one negative eigenvalue. Its magnitude, and hence the negativity of the quantum correlation, equals the square root of the determinant of the reduced density matrix (traced over the l l -space). We have also shown that entanglement entropy, negativity, concurrence of the density matrix, purity of the reduced density matrix, all scales with the single variable, determinant of the reduced density matrix. Finally we computed the negativity for the spin-orbit correlations of partons in polarized and unpolarized hadrons.

Introduction
:

Spin-orbit entanglement refers to bipartite entanglement between two internal degrees of freedom - spin and orbital angular momentum of a particle. The particle can either be spin-1
e.g.
photons or gluons, or, spin-
1/2

e.g.
quarks and leptons.
Being a non-local quantum correlation, the entanglement of a quantum state remains invariant under local unitary transformations. Therefore, a wide range of operations that can be attributed to unitary transformations
e.g.
, free space propagation of the particles, will not generally affect the entanglement between the internal degrees of freedom of that particle.
Nevertheless, it is possible to quantify the entanglement by computing the entanglement measures.

In this article, we compute the entanglement
negativity
for a general
2\times(2n+1)
qubit-qudit system. This class of system resembles the spin-orbit quantum correlations of sub-atomic particles.
We begin with a generic pure state
{\bm{\phi}}\in{\cal H}_{s}^{2}\otimes{\cal H}_{l}^{2l+1}
. Given the orthonormal basis

\{|+\rangle,|-\rangle\}
and
\{|l\rangle,...,|-l\rangle\}
for spin and orbital angular momentum, the state
{\bm{\phi}}
can be written as
Hatta and Montgomery 2025
,

\displaystyle|{\bm{\phi}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=+,-}~\sum_{l=l,\dots,-l}|s\rangle\otimes|l\rangle\equiv\sum_{s,l}c_{s,l}|s,l\rangle,

(1)

\displaystyle=\{c_{+,l}|+,l\rangle...+c_{+,0}|+,0\rangle...+c_{+,-l}|+,-l\rangle\}

\displaystyle~~~~+\{c_{-,l}|-,l\rangle...+c_{-,0}|-,0\rangle...+c_{-,-l}|-,-l\rangle\}.

Here,
|s\rangle
represents the spin eigenstates of the particles and
|l\rangle
represents the OAM eigenstates with eigenvalues ranging from
l
to
-l
. The coefficients
c_{s,l}\in{\mathbb{C}}
and normalization of the state implies,

\displaystyle\langle{\bm{\phi}}|{\bm{\phi}}\rangle=\sum_{l}|c_{+,l}|^{2}+|c_{-,l}|^{2}=1.

(2)

Entanglement Negativity
:

From the state
|\bm{\phi}\rangle
, one may define the associated density matrix as,
\rho=|{\bm{\phi}}\rangle\langle{\bm{\phi}}|
. Density matrices defined this way are pure states. Pure states always have one eigenvalue equal to unity; all other eigenvalues are zero.
In case of the spin-orbit correlations,
\rho
is a
(4l+2)\times(4l+2)
dimensional Hermitian matrix with the elements as follows,

\displaystyle\rho=\begin{pmatrix}&c_{+,l}~c_{+,l}^{*}&&c_{+,l}~c_{+,(l-1)}^{*}&...&c_{+,l}~c_{-,-l}^{*}&\\
\\
&c_{+,(l-1)}~c_{+,l}^{*}&&c_{+,(l-1)}~c_{+,(l-1)}^{*}&...&...&\\
\\
&\vdots&&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\\
&c_{-,-l}~c_{+,l}^{*}&&...&...&c_{-,-l}~c_{-,-l}^{*}&\\
\end{pmatrix}.

(3)

When traced over the
l
-subspace,
\rho
becomes
2\times 2

reduced density matrix

\rho_{l}
,

\displaystyle\rho_{l}={\rm Tr}_{l}|{\bm{\phi}}\rangle\langle{\bm{\phi}}|=\begin{pmatrix}&\sum_{l}|c_{+,l}|^{2}&&\sum_{l}c_{+,l}c_{-,l}^{*}&\\
\\
&\sum_{l}c_{-,l}c_{+,l}^{*}&&\sum_{l}|c_{-,l}|^{2}&\\
\end{pmatrix}.

(4)

For any density matrix or quantum state
\rho
, it is a fact that
\rho
and its transpose
\rho^{T}
share the identical spectrum of eigenvalues. However, if
\rho\in{\cal H}_{m}\otimes{\cal H}_{n}
then the eigenvalue spectrum of the partial transpose of the density matrix, (
I_{m}\otimes T_{n}
)(
\rho
), will generally be quite different from that of
\rho
. More specifically, in the case of a qubit-qudit system, the partial transpose of the general state
\rho
acting on the Hilbert space
{\cal H}_{s}\otimes{\cal{H}}_{2l+1}
can be written as,

\displaystyle\rho^{T_{l}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(I_{2}\otimes T_{2l+1}\right)(\rho),

(5)

where the transpose operation is made only in the
l
-subspace. If all eigenvalues of
\rho^{T_{l}}
are non-negative, then
\rho
is likely to be a separable state. Otherwise, if there are one or more negative eigenvalue(s) present, then it is non-positive partial transposition (NPT). The NPT states are necessarily entangled through
Peres-Horodecki
criterion
Peres 1996
;
Horodecki et al. 1996
. To quantify this entanglement, one of the most useful measures is
entanglement negativity
introduced by Vidal and Werner
Vidal and Werner 2002
. The
negativity
is defined as the absolute sum of the negative eigenvalues of the partial transpose of
\rho
(
\rho^{T_{l}}
). So, negative eigenvalues of
\rho^{T_{l}}
not only confirm the existence of entanglement, but also quantify the amount of entanglement in
\rho
. Thus, it is important and interesting to study the negativity of the density matrix
\rho
by exploring the negative eigenvalues of
\rho^{T_{l}}
. The partially transposed density matrix
\rho^{T_{l}}
has
4l+2
eigenvalues. We have found that only four of them are non-zero and rest are identically zero as,

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sqrt{A},

\displaystyle\lambda_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{A},

\displaystyle\lambda_{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(1-\sqrt{1-4A}),

\displaystyle\lambda_{4}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(1+\sqrt{1-4A}),

\displaystyle\lambda_{5}\dots\lambda_{4l+2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

(6)

:
The four non-zero eigenvalues of partially transposed
\rho^{T_{l}}
plotted as function of
A
. While separable states belong to
A=0
, the maximally entangled states is at
A=1/4
. When the state is maximally entangled, the three positive eigenvalues
\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3},\lambda_{4}
become degenerate at value
1/2
and the negative eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}
saturates to
-1/2
.

where,
A=\text{det}(\rho_{l})
, with
\rho_{l}
being the reduced density matrix. Both the density matrix and it’s partial transpose are Hermitian matrices having only real eigenvalues. This will ensure,
A\geqslant 0
else
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
will become complex and
A\leqslant{1/4}
, else
\lambda_{3}
and
\lambda_{4}
will become complex. As,
A
is bounded between
0
and
1/4
, the spectrum contains only one negative eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}=-\sqrt{A}
as in Fig.
1
. The characteristic equation of
\rho^{T_{l}}
is
(\lambda^{2}-A)(\lambda^{2}-\lambda+A)\lambda^{4l-2}=0
plotted in Fig
2
. It is also interesting to see that,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{4l+2}\lambda_{i}=1,~~~~~\sum_{i=1}^{4l+2}\lambda_{i}^{2}=1,

(7)

which refers to the fact that
{\rm Tr}\left(\rho^{T_{l}}\right)=1
and
{\rm Tr}\left(\rho^{T_{l}}\right)^{2}=1
.
The factor
A
,

\displaystyle A=\text{det}(\rho_{l})=\sum_{l}|c_{+,l}|^{2}~\sum_{l}|c_{-,l}|^{2}-\left(\sum_{l}c_{+,l}c^{*}_{-,l}\right)^{2}\geqslant 0,

(8)

is understood to be positive as
c_{\pm,l}
’s are complex numbers, and the inequality follows from the
Cauchy-Schwarz
inequality. This inequality is essentially an upper bound on the inner product between two vectors in an inner product space in terms of the product of the vector norms. Both
\{c_{+,l}\}
and
\{c_{-,l}\}
can be taken as vectors in the
2l+1
complex space as,
{\bm{c}_{+},\bm{c}_{-}}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{2l+1}
with
{\bm{c}_{+}}=\left(c_{+,l},...,c_{+,0},...,c_{+,-l}\right)
and
{\bm{c}_{-}}=\left(c_{-,l},...,c_{-,0},...,c_{-,-l}\right)
. The canonical complex inner product on the vector space
{\mathbb{C}}^{2l+1}
is defined by
\langle\bm{c}_{+},\bm{c}_{-}\rangle=c_{+,l}{c^{*}_{-,l}}+...+c_{+,0}{c^{*}_{-,0}}+...+c_{-,-l}{c^{*}_{-,-l}}
.

Figure 2
:
The characteristics polynomial
\psi(\lambda)=\left(\lambda^{2}-A\right)\left(\lambda^{2}-\lambda+A\right)
plotted as the function of
\lambda
for the full range of
A
. The zero’s of
\psi(\lambda)
are the eigenvalues of partial transposed
\rho

(\rho^{T_{l}})
. One can see, other than
A=0
, which may correspond to separable states, all other cases have a single negative eigenvalue, confirming the entanglement of the state
\rho
.

With the norms and inner product now defined, the reduced density matrix
\rho_{l}
can be expressed as,

\displaystyle\rho_{l}=\begin{pmatrix}&||\bm{c}_{+}||^{2}&&\langle\bm{c}_{+},\bm{c}_{-}\rangle&\\
\\
&\langle\bm{c}_{-},\bm{c}_{+}\rangle&&||\bm{c}_{-}||^{2}&\\
\end{pmatrix}.

(9)

As the eigenvalue spectrum of
\rho^{T_{l}}
contains only one negative eigenvalue, the negativity of the spin-orbit correlation is

\displaystyle{\cal N}(\rho)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{||\bm{c}_{+}||^{2}||\bm{c}_{-}||^{2}-|\langle\bm{c}_{+},\bm{c}_{-}\rangle|^{2}}=||{\bm{c}}_{+}\wedge{\bm{c}}_{-}||.

(10)

Negativity therefore, is the norm of the wedge product of the two vectors
{\bm{c}}_{+}
and
{\bm{c}}_{-}
.
Further, we can introduce the angle between complex vectors
\bm{c}_{+}
and
\bm{c}_{-}
as,

\displaystyle\cos\theta_{H}=\frac{|\langle\bm{c}_{+},\bm{c}_{-}\rangle|}{||\bm{c}_{+}||~||\bm{c}_{-}||}.

(11)

The angle
\theta_{H}
is known as the Hermitian angle between
{\bm{c}}_{+}
and
{\bm{c}}_{-}
in the complex vector space. Thus, the negativity can be written as,

\displaystyle{\cal N}(\rho)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle||\bm{c}_{+}||~||\bm{c}_{-}||\sin\theta_{H}.

(12)

Finally, we use Lagrange’s identity to get,

\displaystyle{\cal N}(\rho)=\sum_{l}|c_{+,l}|^{2}~\sum_{l}|c_{-,l}|^{2}-\left|\sum_{l}c_{+,l}c^{*}_{-,l}\right|^{2}=\sum_{-l\leqslant i<j\leqslant l}\left|c_{+,i}c^{*}_{-,j}-c_{+,j}c^{*}_{-,i}\right|^{2}\equiv\sum_{-l\leqslant i<j\leqslant l}\tau_{i,j},

(13)

There are
l(2l-1)
numbers of
\tau_{ij}
. Each of them is either zero or positive. Therefore, to confirm or certify the entanglement, it’s sufficient to show that at least one
\tau_{ij}
is non-zero for some
i,j\in\{+l,...,-l\}
and
i\neq j
,
i.e.
,

\displaystyle c_{+,i}c^{*}_{-,j}-c_{+,j}c^{*}_{-,i}\neq 0.

(14)

As
\langle s,l|\phi\rangle=c_{s,l}
, we define the operator
\tilde{\cal E}_{ij}
for any pair of
i,j\in\{l,...,-l\}
with
i\neq j
as,

\displaystyle\tilde{\cal E}_{ij}=|-,j\rangle\langle+,i|-|-,i\rangle\langle+,j|,

(15)

For some choice of
i,j\in\{l,...,-l\}
with
i\neq j
, if,

\displaystyle\langle\phi|\tilde{\cal E}_{ij}|\phi\rangle\neq 0,

(16)

that will be sufficient to certify the entanglement as the negativity will be non-zero.
Negativity is an entanglement monotone. Therefore, it not only quantifies the amount of entanglement of a quantum state, it is also a non-negative function whose value does not increase under any local operations and classical communications.
The two eigenvalues of the reduced density matrix
\rho_{l}
are found to be identical to
\lambda_{3}
and
\lambda_{4}
.

\displaystyle\lambda_{+}=\frac{1}{2}\left(1+\sqrt{1-4A}\right),~~~\lambda_{-}=\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\sqrt{1-4A}\right).

(17)

The entanglement entropy of
\rho
, which is essentially the von-Nuemann entropy of the reduced density matrix
\rho_{l}
,

\displaystyle{\cal E}(\rho)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle-\sum_{i=+,-}\lambda_{i}\log\lambda_{i},

(18)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\log{A}-\frac{1}{2}~\sqrt{1-4A}~\log\left[\frac{1+\sqrt{1-4A}}{1-\sqrt{1-4A}}\right],

is therefore, function of a single variable
A=\det(\rho_{l})
. At
A\rightarrow 0
, entanglement entropy
{\cal E}(\rho)
is regular and vanishes identically. When the state is maximally entangled (
A=1/4
),
{\cal E}(\rho)
reaches its maximum value at
\ln 2
.
Another important entanglement measure of the pure state
\rho
is the
concurrence
,
\cal C

Hill and Wootters 1997
;
Wootters 1998
,

\displaystyle{\cal C}\left(\rho\right)\equiv\sqrt{2\left(1-{\text{T}r}\left(\rho^{2}_{l}\right)\right)}=2\det(\rho_{l}).

(19)

The reduced density matrix being a
2\times 2
matrix, the last equality comes from the determinant-trace relation of square matrix,
{\text{det }M}=\frac{1}{2}\left(({\text{tr }M)^{2}-{\text{tr }M^{2}}}\right)
. Therefore, for the qubit-qudit system, negativity and concurrence are related as,

\displaystyle{\cal C}(\rho)=2{\cal N}^{2}(\rho).

(20)

This indeed confirm that the partial transpose acts as the direct link between negativity and concurrence
Eltschka et al. 2015
.

Maximally entangled state
:
The spin-orbit composite system contained in the density matrix
\rho
is in pure state. However, the spin sub-system (traced over the OAM space) contained in
\rho_{l}
is a mixed state. This can be confirmed from the
\it purity
of reduced density matrix
\rho_{l}
,

\displaystyle\gamma(\rho_{l})

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle{\text{T}r}(\rho^{2}_{l})

(21)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle||\bm{c}_{+}||^{4}+||\bm{c}_{-}||^{4}+2|\langle\bm{c}_{+},\bm{c}_{-}\rangle|^{2}<{1}.

While, pure state has
\gamma=1
, mixed states have
\gamma<1
. It is interesting to see that the negativity of the spin-orbit composite system and the purity of the spin sub-system are related as,

\displaystyle\gamma(\rho_{l})=1-2{\cal N}^{2}(\rho).

(22)

We see from the above expression that the more entangled the spin-orbit composite state is (
i.e.
more negativity), the less pure is the spin sub-system (
i.e.
more mixed).
When a pure bipartite state is maximally entangled, its reduced density matrix is maximally mixed. A mixed state is a convex combination of pure states. Hence, a mixed state can be written as,
\rho=\sum_{i}p_{i}|\phi_{i}\rangle\langle\phi_{i}|
, where,
p_{i}\geqslant 0
,
\sum_{i}p_{i}=1
. These
p_{i}
’s can be thought of probability distribution of individual pure states. If we assume that
\phi_{i}
’s form an orthonormal basis, then a maximally mixed state is a state where
p_{i}
’s are uniformly distributed,
i.e.
,
p_{i}=1/n
, where
n
is the dimension of the state. Therefore, the maximally mixed states are mixed states where a mixture of all states offers the same probability.
This leads to the fact that the associated reduced density matrix is proportional to the identity matrix,
\rho_{l}=(1/d)I_{d\times d}
.
Therefore, the spin-orbit correlation is maximally entangled if the associated density matrix
\rho_{l}
is maximally mixed and has the form,

\rho_{l}=(1/2)I_{2\times 2}
.
This requires the following two conditions to be fulfilled in Eq.(
9
),

•

||\bm{c}_{+}||^{2}=||\bm{c}_{-}||^{2}=1/2
,
i.e.
,
\bm{c}_{+}
and
\bm{c}_{-}
are of identical norm and,

•

\langle\bm{c}_{+},\bm{c}_{-}\rangle=\langle\bm{c}_{-},\bm{c}_{+}\rangle=0
,
i.e.
,
\bm{c}_{+}
and
\bm{c}_{-}
are orthogonal.

The above two conditions will ensure the maximal entanglement of the spin-orbit system, having entanglement entropy and entanglement negativity saturating to their maximum values at
\ln 2
and
1/2
respectively.

Polarized and Unpolarized Hadrons
:

If the hadron is taken to be unpolarized, two conditions follow
Hatta and Montgomery 2025
,

\displaystyle\langle\bm{\phi}|s^{z}|\bm{\phi}\rangle=0,~~~\langle\bm{\phi}|l^{z}|\bm{\phi}\rangle=0.

(23)

Even in an unpolarized hadron, certain correlation between spin and orbital angular momentum may present as,
\langle\bm{\phi}|s^{z}l^{z}|\bm{\phi}\rangle\propto C_{q,g}(x)
, where,
C_{q,g}(x)
are (quarks and gluons) spin-orbit correlations
Bhattacharya et al. 2024
.
As consequence of Eq.(
23
), one can write,

\displaystyle\sum_{l}\left(|c_{+,l}|^{2}-|c_{-,l}|^{2}\right)=0,~~~\sum_{l}l\left(|c_{+,l}|^{2}+|c_{-,l}|^{2}\right)=0.

(24)

For unpolarized hadrons, it has been argued that for individual partons, the spin and orbital angular momentum are maximally entangled, making them belong to the Bell states
Bhattacharya et al. 2024
;
Hatta and Montgomery 2025
. From Eq.(
2
) and (
24
), we can further write,

\displaystyle\sum_{l}|c_{+,l}|^{2}=\frac{1}{2},~~~\sum_{l}|c_{-,l}|^{2}=\frac{1}{2},

(25)

i.e.
,
||\bm{c}_{+}||=||\bm{c}_{-}||=1/\sqrt{2}
.
Therefore, partons in an unpolarized proton, the first of the two conditions, as to make the state maximally entangled, automatically fulfilled. What remaines is to have
\bm{c}_{+}
and
\bm{c}_{-}
orthogonal,
i.e.
,
\langle\bm{c}_{+},\bm{c}_{-}\rangle=0
.
Moving to the case of polarized hadrons where,
||\bm{c}_{+}||\neq||\bm{c}_{-}||
, unlike the unpolarized case
\langle\bm{\phi}|s^{z}|\bm{\phi}\rangle
is not identically zero, rather is proportional to the gluon helicity PDF,

\displaystyle\sum_{l}\left(|c_{+,l}|^{2}-|c_{-,l}|^{2}\right)=\frac{\Delta g(x)}{g(x)},

(26)

where,
\Delta g(x)
is gluon helicity PDF function,
g(x)
is gluon PDF.
As a consequence the reduced density matrix will no longer be maximally mixed. Therefore, the entangle negativity of spin-orbit correlation for the case of polarized hadrons is,

\displaystyle{\cal N}(\rho)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle||\bm{c}_{+}||~||\bm{c}_{-}||\sin\theta_{H},

(27)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\frac{\left(\Delta g(x)\right)^{2}}{g^{2}(x)}\right)^{1/2}\sin\theta_{H}.

Conclusion
:
The entries of the reduced density matrix
\rho_{l}
are either norms or inner products. As norms and inner products are invariant under unitary transformations,
\langle U({\bm{c}_{+}}),U({\bm{c}_{-}})\rangle=\langle\bm{c}_{+},\bm{c}_{-}\rangle
, any unitary transformation over
\bm{c}_{+}
and
\bm{c}_{-}
will make
\rho_{l}
to remain invariant, so as the entanglement entropy or negativity. Unitary operations are automorphisms of the inner product spaces,
i.e.
, they preserve the structure and invariants of the space on which they act. Going in the small-
x
limit is essentially the high energy limit of the states. The boost in the
z
-direction,
e^{i\omega k^{3}}|\bm{\phi}\rangle
is a unitary transformation. While this transformation may change the individual values of
c_{+}
’s and
c_{-}
’s, as well as the matrix
\rho
, however, being unitary tranformation they keep the norms and inner product invariant. Therefore, the reduced density matrix, entanglement entropy, and negativity will retain their initial values and do not depend on the Bjorken-
x
as the state evolves in high energy. If the state is maximally entangled in the limit
x\rightarrow 0
, then the states with
0<x<1
evolve from the maximally entangled Bell state via successive local unitary transformations which are also maximally entangled
Hatta and Montgomery 2025
.

Recent years have seen a surge in interest and activities in the interface of quantum information science and quantum chromodynamics
Kutak 2011
;
Peschanski 2013
;
Kovner and Lublinsky 2015
;
Kharzeev and Levin 2017
;
Armesto et al. 2019
;
Ramos and Machado 2020
;
Beane et al. 2019
;
Liu et al. 2022
. Most of the studies so far revolved mainly around the entropy specially the entanglement entropy. As a measure of entanglement in the context of spin-orbit entanglement of partons, we have studied the negativity for the first time. In this paper, we have presented a complete characterization of the eigenvalue spectrum of the partially transpose density matrix for a
2\times(2n+1)
qubit-qudit system
Gerjuoy 2003
. Our study is completely general and does not restrict the eigenstates of angular momentum. We found that the spectrum contains only one negative eigenvalue, which coincides with the square root of the determinant of the reduced density matrix. Single negative eigenvalue for
\rho^{T_{l}}
is often interpreted as the amount of white noise required to make the state separable. While the connection between concurrence and the determinant of the reduced density matrix is known, in this paper, for the first time, we have connected the latter with the negativity for the general qubit-qudit system in the context of spin-orbit correlation of partons.

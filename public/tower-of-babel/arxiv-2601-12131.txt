SolarGPT-QA: A Domain-Adaptive Large Language Model for Educational Question Answering in Space Weather and Heliophysics
Santosh Chapagain, MohammadReza EskandariNasab, Onur Vural, Shah Muhammad Hamdi, Soukaina Filali Boubrahimi

Abstract
Solar activity, including solar flares, coronal mass ejections (CMEs), and geomagnetic storms, can significantly impact satellites, aviation, power grids, data centers, and space missions. Extreme solar events can cause substantial economic damage with limited advance warning, underscoring the importance of early-warning systems, accurate forecasting, and effective education in space science. Although large language models (LLMs) perform well on general tasks, they often lack domain-specific knowledge and pedagogical capability to clearly explain complex space science concepts. We introduce SolarGPT-QA, a question answering system based on a domain-adapted large language model built on the LLaMA-3 base model. The model is trained using scientific literature and large-scale question-answer data generated with GPT-4 and refined using Grok-3 in a student-friendly storytelling style. Human pairwise evaluations show that SolarGPT-QA outperforms general-purpose models in zero-shot settings and achieves competitive performance compared to instruction-tuned models for educational explanations in space weather and heliophysics. A small pilot student comprehension study further suggests improved clarity and accessibility of the generated explanations. Ablation experiments indicate that combining domain-adaptive pretraining with pedagogical fine-tuning is important for balancing scientific accuracy and educational effectiveness. This work represents an initial step toward a broader SolarGPT framework for space science education and forecasting.

Rather than targeting expert-level analysis, SolarGPT-QA focuses on helping students grasp core concepts through clear, accessible explanations to questions such as how solar flares form or why the Sun can disrupt technology on Earth.

III
SolarGPT-QA

SolarGPT-QA is a domain-adapted large language model designed specifically for educational question answering in space weather and heliophysics, with an emphasis on K–12–friendly explanations. The methodology consists of four main stages: (1) domain corpus construction and preprocessing, (2) domain-adaptive pretraining (DAPT) using LoRA, (3) educational question–answer fine-tuning, and (4) inference-time constraints to promote clarity and reduce hallucinations. An overview of the system architecture is shown in Fig. 2. Let
\mathcal{D}_{H}
denote a corpus of heliophysics literature, and
\mathcal{D}_{QA}
denote a supervised question–answer dataset designed for K–12 learners. Our objective is to learn a conditional language model

p_{\theta}(y\mid x),

(1)

where
x
is a heliophysics question, and
y
is an age-appropriate explanatory answer that preserves scientific correctness while prioritizing conceptual clarity.

III-A

Domain Corpus Construction and Normalization

We curate a heliophysics corpus
\mathcal{D}_{H}=\{d_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
from peer-reviewed literature published between 2010 and 2024, during the SDO operational period. Each document
d_{i}
is extracted from PDF format and transformed using a deterministic preprocessing function:

\tilde{d}_{i}=f_{\text{clean}}(d_{i}),

(2)

where
f_{\text{clean}}
removes references, citations, section headers, figure captions, URLs, and formatting artifacts, followed by Unicode normalization.

The cleaned corpus
\mathcal{T}=\{\tilde{d}_{i}\}
is tokenized into sequences

\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{T}),\quad x_{t}\in\mathcal{V},

(3)

where
\mathcal{V}
denotes the tokenizer vocabulary of the base language model.

III-B

Domain-Adaptive Pretraining with Low-Rank Adaptation

We initialize SolarGPT-QA from a pretrained causal language model, Meta-LLaMA-3-8B, and adapt it to the heliophysics domain using
domain-adaptive pretraining (DAPT)
combined with
Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA)
for parameter efficiency. As defined in Section III-A, the heliophysics corpus is processed as

DH\rightarrow\mathcal{T}

DAPT minimizes the autoregressive negative log-likelihood over the tokenized corpus
\mathcal{T}
:

\mathcal{L}_{\text{DAPT}}(\theta)=\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mathcal{T}}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}-\log p_{\theta}(x_{t}\mid x_{<t})\right]

(4)

This objective biases the model toward heliophysics-specific terminology, causal structures, and semantic patterns associated with solar flares, coronal mass ejections (CMEs), and solar energetic particles (SEPs).

To enable parameter-efficient adaptation, we employ
Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA)
. For a transformer weight matrix

W\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times k},

LoRA applies a low-rank update

W\rightarrow W^{\prime}=W+\Delta W,

(5)

where

\Delta W=BA.

(6)

A
and
B

This significantly reduces the number of optimized parameters, and enables efficient domain adaptation without sacrificing the general linguistic capabilities of the pretrained model. IV-A

Training Configuration and Hyperparameters

All experiments were conducted using LoRA-based fine-tuning while keeping the base Meta-LLaMA-3-8B parameters frozen. LoRA adapters were applied to the query and value projection matrices of the attention layers. The rank of the low-rank adapters was set to
r=16
, with a scaling factor
\alpha=32
and dropout probability
p=0.05

During both domain-adaptive pretraining and fine-tuning, optimization was performed using the AdamW optimizer. At inference time, text generation employed stochastic decoding with a temperature of
\tau=0.7
, top-
k=50
, and nucleus sampling with top-
p=0.9
. Generation was terminated at sentence boundaries using a custom stopping criterion, and post-processing filtered incomplete sentence fragments to ensure syntactic completeness and readability. All of our code used in this experiment is available on GitHub

IV-B
2
QA Dataset Construction

The supervised QA dataset was constructed by generating question–answer pairs from the curated heliophysics literature using large language models, followed by pedagogical rewriting to emphasize clarity, storytelling, and age-appropriate explanations suitable for K–12 learners. Questions focus on conceptual understanding rather than mathematical derivations, targeting physical intuition, cause–effect relationships, and real-world impacts of space weather phenomena.

IV-B
3
Train–Test Split and Difficulty Levels

The training set consists of QA pairs derived from the same corpus used during domain-adaptive pretraining. In contrast, the test set is constructed from QA pairs extracted from different PDF documents that are not included in the training corpus, ensuring document-level separation and reducing information leakage. To support a fine-grained educational evaluation, the test set is further divided into three levels of difficulty:

•

Easy:
Basic factual and definitional questions, such as identifying solar phenomena or simple cause–effect relationships, suitable for elementary and middle school learners.

•

Medium:
Conceptual reasoning questions that require explanation of physical processes or interactions (e.g., how CMEs interact with Earth’s magnetosphere), aligned with middle to early high school understanding.

•

Hard:
Integrative questions that require synthesizing multiple concepts or explaining observational consequences (e.g., relationships between flares, CMEs, and SEPs), suitable for advanced high school students.

This tiered evaluation design enables systematic assessment of SolarGPT-QA across increasing levels of conceptual complexity while maintaining consistent scientific grounding.

IV-C

Baseline

We evaluate SolarGPT-QA against several strong general-purpose large language models that are widely used for scientific question answering and education. The baselines include ChatGPT, Grok, Claude, DeepSeek, and Gemini. All baseline models are evaluated under a zero-shot setting using identical prompts and decoding parameters, and without retrieval augmentation, to ensure a fair and controlled comparison.

In addition, we include an instruction-tuned variant of ChatGPT, where the model is prompt-tuned using a small number of representative examples (few-shot setting), in order to compare SolarGPT-QA against a highly optimized general-purpose model with explicit pedagogical alignment.

IV-D

Evaluation Method

We use win rate as the primary evaluation metric based on human pairwise comparison. For each test question, a human evaluator compares SolarGPT-QA’s response with a baseline model’s response and assigns one of three outcomes: Win, Tie, or Loss. The win rate is defined as the proportion of questions where SolarGPT-QA is judged to outperform the baseline. Results are reported by aggregating win, tie, and loss rates across all questions, and separately across difficulty levels (i.e., Easy, Medium, Hard).

V
Discussion and Conclusion

V-A

Discussion

The results presented in this work demonstrate that domain adaptation alone is insufficient for educational effectiveness and that pedagogical alignment is a critical and often overlooked dimension when deploying large language models for science education. While general-purpose LLMs such as ChatGPT and Grok exhibit strong linguistic fluency and factual recall, our findings show that they frequently fail to deliver explanations that are accessible, coherent, and age-appropriate for K–12 learners in the context of space weather and heliophysics.

In the human pairwise evaluation, instruction-tuned ChatGPT (10-shot) achieves the highest overall win rate, indicating that evaluators consistently favor responses that are explicitly aligned with instructional intent and teaching style. Despite this, SolarGPT-QA performs competitively and outperforms all zero-shot general-purpose baselines, achieving a 75% overall win rate. This result suggests that domain-adaptive pretraining combined with educational fine-tuning can substantially narrow the gap between zero-shot domain models and heavily instruction-tuned general LLMs, without relying on in-context examples at inference time.

The student comprehension study further refines this interpretation. While instruction-tuned ChatGPT achieves perfect comprehension among participants, SolarGPT-QA follows closely, with a large majority of students reporting clear understanding of its explanations. In contrast, comprehension levels for zero-shot general-purpose models remain noticeably lower. These results indicate that SolarGPT-QA succeeds in translating complex heliophysics concepts into accessible explanations, even if it does not surpass the strongest instruction-aligned baseline.

The ablation study provides critical insight into why this performance emerges. Domain-adaptive pretraining (DAPT) alone improves scientific grounding but tends to preserve expert-level language, while QA fine-tuning alone improves readability at the expense of domain depth. The full SolarGPT-QA model achieves the strongest balance between these objectives, producing explanations that are both scientifically grounded and pedagogically accessible. Importantly, SolarGPT-QA reaches this balance without external prompting strategies, highlighting the effectiveness of integrating educational objectives directly into the training process.

These findings have broader implications beyond heliophysics. They suggest that many existing scientific LLMs, while impressive for expert-level analysis, are poorly suited for educational deployment without explicit adaptation. As AI systems increasingly enter classrooms, museums, and public outreach platforms, models that fail to account for learner expertise risk reinforcing misunderstanding or disengagement. SolarGPT-QA provides preliminary evidence that targeted domain adaptation combined with educational fine-tuning can effectively bridge this gap.

V-B

Future work

The present study represents an initial, proof-of-concept exploration of domain-adapted large language models for educational question answering in space weather and heliophysics. Several important extensions are planned as part of the broader SolarGPT research agenda.

First, future work will focus on developing SolarGPT-Core, a large-scale domain-adapted foundation model trained on an expanded corpus of peer-reviewed heliophysics literature. Scaling to larger architectures and longer context windows is expected to improve scientific depth, reasoning consistency, and coverage of rare but high-impact solar events.

Second, we plan to introduce SolarGPT-PredEx, a multimodal prediction-and-explanation model that integrates time-series measurements and solar imagery with language modeling. Unlike the current SolarGPT-QA system, SolarGPT-PredEx will directly reason over observational data to produce both event predictions and data-grounded natural language explanations, enabling more transparent and interpretable space weather forecasting.

Third, future versions of SolarGPT-QA will incorporate retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), curriculum-aware prompting, and adaptive difficulty control to support classroom deployment and personalized learning. Larger and more diverse human evaluation studies, particularly involving teachers and students across multiple grade levels, will be conducted to rigorously assess educational effectiveness.

Finally, all components of the SolarGPT ecosystem will be integrated into a unified research and outreach platform that bridges prediction, explanation, and education, supporting both scientific discovery and public understanding of heliophysics. The current work establishes the feasibility of this vision while clearly delineating its preliminary scope.

V-C

Conclusion

In this work, we introduced SolarGPT-QA, a domain-adapted large language model designed for educational question answering in space weather and heliophysics. By combining domain-adaptive pretraining on peer-reviewed heliophysics literature with pedagogically aligned question–answer fine-tuning, SolarGPT-QA aims to bridge expert scientific knowledge and accessible K–12 instruction.

Human pairwise evaluation results show that while instruction-tuned ChatGPT (10-shot) achieves the strongest overall performance, SolarGPT-QA outperforms all zero-shot general-purpose baselines, and suggests strong student comprehension and explanatory clarity. While these findings are preliminary and based on limited sample sizes, they provide initial evidence that integrating domain adaptation with educational fine-tuning can enhance the clarity and accessibility of scientific explanations. Ablation experiments further demonstrate that neither domain-adaptive pretraining nor educational fine-tuning alone is sufficient; rather, their combination is important for balancing scientific accuracy with pedagogical effectiveness.

Beyond its direct use, this work shows a clear method for building educational science language models. It highlights that good AI learning tools should include teaching goals, not just subject knowledge. SolarGPT-QA is a first step toward a larger SolarGPT system that brings together prediction, explanation, and education in heliophysics.

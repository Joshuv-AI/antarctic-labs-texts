Further analysis on the second frequency of union-closed set families
Saintan Wu

Abstract
The Union-Closed Sets Conjecture, also known as Frankl’s conjecture, asks whether, for any union-closed set family ℱ \mathcal{F} with m m sets, there is an element that lies in at least 1 2 ⋅ m \frac{1}{2}\cdot m sets in ℱ \mathcal{F} . In 2022, Nagel posed a stronger conjecture that within any union-closed family whose ground set size is at least k k , there are always k k elements in the ground set that appear in at least 1 2 k − 1 + 1 \frac{1}{2^{k-1}+1} proportion of the sets in the family. Das and Wu showed that this conjecture is true for k ≥ 3 k\geq 3 and k = 2 k=2 if | ℱ | |\mathcal{F}| is outside a particular range. In this companion paper, we analyse further when ℱ \mathcal{F} fails Nagel’s conjecture for k = 2 k=2 via linear programming.

Review of the shattering strategy

We considered a minimal

Lastly, using

of

over

when

, we get that either
s=4
and

, or

2.2
Linear programming and two ways of winning

We can make a more careful estimate: For each subset

as the quantity of sets

\mathcal{F}
that

•

By

By

•

As

\sum_{y\in T}q_{T}\leq\frac{m}{3}\qquad\text{for each }y\in S.

It turns out that these are all linear constraints for

Variables

Minimize

Subject to

\begin{array}[]{r@{\,}ll}q_{\varnothing}&\leq 2,&\\
q_{T}&\geq 1,&T\subseteq S\\
\sum_{T\subseteq S}q_{T}&=m,&\\
\sum_{y\in T}q_{T}&\leq\frac{m}{3},&y\in S.\\
\end{array}

By solving this directly, we get same bounds

for

and

for

8,&|T|=1;\\

This configuration can cause many dependencies. For example, while there are many pairs of choices of

Hence, we can strengthen

•

this

6
Concluding remark

In this paper, we further investigated the potential counterexample for
Conjecture

1
and narrowed down the searching range of their sizes from
[45,113]
to
[81,113]
and showed that there is some uniformity to the collection of minimal
2
-good sets.

To further improve the bound, a possible approach is to pick
x
more carefully. In our proof, we can see that there are many choices for extra elements. With such many choices, one may possibly push the bound further.

Another perspective is to investigate the uniformity of minimal
2
-good sets of the family. To do this, one could answer this question:

Question 1
.

What can we say about the minimal
2
-good sets if they all have the same size?

To first ignore the asymmetry induced by the most common element
1
, we consider a definition slightly different from
k
-good sets.

Definition 6.1
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a family, a
cover

S
of
\mathcal{F}
is a set that intersects every set in
\mathcal{F}
.

A
minimal cover
is a cover whose proper subsets are not covers.

Let
\mathcal{MC}(\mathcal{F})
denote the family of minimal covers of
\mathcal{F}
.

Equivalently, a cover is a vertex cover when we see
\mathcal{F}
as the edge set of a hypergraph on
n
vertices.

In this case, we can characterize when
\mathcal{MC}(F)
is uniform:

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a family, not necessarily be union-closed.

1.

\mathcal{MC}(\mathcal{F})
is an antichain. In other words, any two elements in
\mathcal{MC}(\mathcal{F})
are incomparable.

2.

If
\mathcal{F}
is an antichain, then
\mathcal{MC}(\mathcal{MC}(\mathcal{F}))=\mathcal{F}
.

3.

Subsequently, let
\mathcal{G}
be the subfamily of minimal elements in
\mathcal{F}
, then
\mathcal{MC}(\mathcal{F})
is
k
-uniform if and only if
\mathcal{G}
is the minimal covers of a
k
-uniform hypergraph.

Proof.

We only have to prove the second claim.

\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathcal{MC}(\mathcal{MC}(\mathcal{F}))
:

For
F\in\mathcal{F}
, it is a cover of
\mathcal{MC}(\mathcal{F})
since every
C\in\mathcal{MC}(\mathcal{F})
intersects
F
. To show that
F\setminus\{f\}
is not a cover of
\mathcal{MC}(\mathcal{F})
for each element
f\in F
, we need to find a minimal cover
C\in\mathcal{MC}(\mathcal{F})
contained in
F^{\complement}\cup\{f\}
. Since
\mathcal{F}
is an antichain,
F^{\complement}\cup\{f\}
is indeed a cover and therefore such
C
exists.

\mathcal{MC}(\mathcal{MC}(\mathcal{F}))\subseteq\mathcal{F}
:

For
C
a minimal cover of
\mathcal{MC}(F)
, we show that it covers a set in
\mathcal{F}
. If this is true,
C
must in
\mathcal{F}
as sets in
\mathcal{F}
are covers of
\mathcal{MC}(\mathcal{F})
.

Were it not, then for each
F\in\mathcal{F}
, there is an element
a_{F}\in F
that
C
does not have. Notice that
\{a_{F}:F\}
is a cover of
\mathcal{F}
, it contains a minimal cover of
\mathcal{F}
. It lies in
\mathcal{MC}(\mathcal{F})
but does not intersect
C
, giving a contradiction.

∎

We hope to understand the potential counterexamples for
Conjecture

1
when
k=2
better by understanding the families whose minimal
2
-good sets are uniform.

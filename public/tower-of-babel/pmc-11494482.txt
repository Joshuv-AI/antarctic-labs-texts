Reply to Letter Regarding “Improvement in Nasal Symptoms of Chronic Rhinitis After Cryoablation of the Posterior Nasal Nerve”
Authors: Mattie Rosi‐Schumacher, Paul R Young
Published: 2024

Dear Editor,

We thank the authors for their interest in our manuscript, Improvement in Nasal Symptoms of Chronic Rhinitis after Cryoablation of the Posterior Nasal Nerve. 1 We agree one of the limitations of our study was the short duration of follow‐up. The longest time point of follow‐up that was recorded in the medical record at the time of chart review was included for the purposes of our study. However, patients did continue to follow‐up in clinic after the study timeframe, and a long‐term outcome analysis in our cohort is under consideration for a future study. Prior published studies by Chang et al and Ow et al have demonstrated long‐term efficacy up to 2 years after the cryoablation procedure with a stable degree of symptom improvement. 2 , 3

The unified airway concept is not neglected within our study, which included patients with comorbidities such as asthma and allergy. These patients also received appropriate medical treatment for their respective conditions, including allergen‐specific therapy if indicated. There is no evidence to exclude patients from this procedure due to these comorbidities. After the procedure, patients were encouraged to continue the use of nasal sprays as necessary. However, the aim of the study is to assess patient symptoms related to chronic rhinitis. That is why the mini Rhinoconjunctivitis Quality of Life Questionnaire (mRQLQ) and the Total Nasal Symptom Score were used to quantify outcomes. The mRQLQ is a validated disease‐specific quality of life questionnaire that is designed to measure the problems most important to the majority of patients with this condition. 4 These metrics, in addition to medication discontinuation after the procedure, have previously been used to evaluate outcomes of posterior nasal nerve cryoablation. 2 , 3 , 5

In the United States, insurance usually covers the cost of the cryoablation procedure, while the patient commonly pays an office co‐pay. The cost of the ClariFix system (Stryker Corporation) is estimated to be approximately $1895. A true cost‐benefit analysis would be difficult to quantify and is beyond the scope of our paper. Consideration would need to be given to a multitude of variables including lifetime prescription costs and clinic visits as well as the improvement in quality of life that these patients experience.

Our study provides the largest sample size of patients undergoing this procedure of any published cohort study, and therefore, continues to provide a significant contribution to the understanding of this procedure and its related outcomes.

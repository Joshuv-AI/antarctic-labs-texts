Metastability of diffusion processes in narrow tubes
Wen-Tai Hsu

Abstract
We study the metastable behavior of diffusion processes in narrow tube domains, where the metastability is induced by entropic barriers. We identify a sequence of characteristic time scales { T ϵ i } 1 ≤ i ≤ | V ′ | \{T_{\epsilon}^{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq\absolutevalue{V'}} and characterize the asymptotic behavior of the diffusion process both at intermediate time scales and at the first critical time scale. Our analysis relies on a refined understanding of the narrow escape problem in domains with bottlenecks, in particular on estimates for the exit place and on the conditional distribution of the exit time given the exit place, results that may be of independent interest.

Introduction

In this paper, we start with a simple, finite, connected graph
\Gamma=(V,E)\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, with vertices
O_{1},\cdots,O_{\absolutevalue{V}}\in V
and edges
I_{1},\cdots,I_{\absolutevalue{E}}\in E
, where
d=2
or
3
.
Here,
V
and
E
denote the sets of vertices and edges, respectively, and
\absolutevalue{V}
and
\absolutevalue{E}
their cardinalities.
The associated narrow tube domain
G_{\epsilon}
consists of the union of
\lambda_{k}\epsilon
-neighborhoods of the edges
I_{k}
and
r_{j}(\epsilon)
-neighborhoods of the vertices
O_{j}
, where
\lambda_{k}
are positive constants and
r_{j}(\epsilon)\downarrow 0
as
\epsilon\downarrow 0
.

We aim to study the diffusion process with reflecting boundary conditions in the domain
G_{\epsilon}

dZ^{\epsilon}(t)=\sqrt{2}dB(t)+\nu_{\epsilon}(Z^{\epsilon}(t))d\phi^{\epsilon}(t),\ Z^{\epsilon}(0)=z\in G_{\epsilon}.

(1.1)

Here,
B(t)
is a
d
-dimensional Brownian motion,
\nu_{\epsilon}(z)
is the unit inward normal vector at the point
z\in\partial G_{\epsilon}
, and
\phi^{\epsilon}(t)
is the local time of
Z^{\epsilon}(t)
on the boundary
\partial G_{\epsilon}
.

Equations of this type were studied in
[
13
]
for the case where
r_{j}(\epsilon)
is of order
\epsilon
, and in
[
17
]
for more general scaling (see also
[
9
, Chapter 7]
).
Already in
[
17
]
, through the study of the narrow escape problem, it was observed that a metastable behavior arises when

r_{j}(\epsilon)^{d}\gg\epsilon^{d-1},\qquad\text{equivalently,}\qquad\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{r_{j}(\epsilon)^{d}}{\epsilon^{d-1}}=\infty,

(1.2)

and the narrow tube domain consists of only two vertex neighborhoods connected by a single edge neighborhood.
The purpose of the present work is to describe this metastable behavior more precisely and to extend the analysis to general graph structures.
Accordingly, we shall assume condition (
1.2
) holds for all
j=1,\cdots,\absolutevalue{V}
throughout the paper.

Metastability refers to the phenomenon in which a stochastic system remains for a long time in a local equilibrium (a metastable state) before making a rare transition to another state.
It is a ubiquitous feature in molecular dynamics, materials science, and many related fields (see, e.g.,
[
8
]
,
[
2
]
,
[
29
]
).
Various mathematical frameworks have been developed to analyze metastability in random dynamical systems.
These include the large deviation approach (
[
14
]
,
[
10
]
,
[
11
]
), the potential-theoretic approach (
[
3
]
,
[
4
]
,
[
5
]
), the resolvent approach (
[
20
]
,
[
21
]
,
[
22
]
,
[
23
]
) and the quasi-stationary approach (
[
24
]
,
[
25
]
,
[
26
]
).
We also mention the general meta-theorems developed in
[
12
]
and
[
18
]
.
The references above are not intended to be exhaustive.

Most studies focus on systems with small noise, where metastability arises from an energetic barrier.
In such settings, the deterministic dynamics creates deep potential wells that trap the system for long periods, and rare transitions occur only due to small stochastic perturbations that enable escape from these wells.

In contrast, the metastability studied in this paper originates from an entropic barrier (see, e.g.,
[
19
]
,
[
2
]
,
[
26
]
).
Here, the particle does not need to overcome a high energy barrier; instead, it must find a narrow geometric opening through which it can make a rare transition to another metastable state.
This type of mechanism can be viewed as a metastable phenomenon driven by entropic effects.
Because of this fundamentally different mechanism, understanding metastability arising from entropic barriers requires a detailed analysis of geometric effects, which contrasts sharply with the techniques used in the energetic-barrier setting.

In this work, our goal is to understand the asymptotic behavior of the
Z^{\epsilon}(t(\epsilon))
as
\epsilon\to 0
, for time scales
t(\epsilon)
that diverge at different rates.
To be more precise, let
\Pi^{\epsilon}:G_{\epsilon}\to\Gamma
be some continuous modification of the nearest projection
\Pi
(see Section
2.3
for a precise definition).
Our main theorems are the following two:

Theorem 1.1
.

There exist transition time scales
\{T_{\epsilon}^{i}\}_{i=1}^{\absolutevalue{V'}}
(the index set
V^{\prime}
and the quantities
T_{\epsilon}^{i}
will will be defined explicitly later) such that for any
t(\epsilon)>0
satisfying
T_{\epsilon}^{i}\ll t(\epsilon)\ll T_{\epsilon}^{i+1}
and any continuous function
F\in C(\Gamma)
, we have

\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\sup_{x\in\Gamma}\sup_{z:\Pi^{\epsilon}(z)=x}\absolutevalue{ \mathbb{E}_{z} (F(\Pi^\epsilon(Z^\epsilon(t(\epsilon)))) - \sum_{j'=1}^{\abs{V}} F(O_{j'}) \mu^i(x,O_{j'}) }=0,

(1.3)

where
\mu^{i}(x,\cdot)
denotes an absorbing distribution of a certain discrete-time Markov chain, whose precise definition will be given later.

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose that there exists a vertex
O_{j_{1}}\in V
such that
r_{j_{1}}(\epsilon)\ll r_{j}(\epsilon)
for all
j\neq j_{1}
. Then, for any
s>0
and any continuous function
F\in C(\Gamma)
, we have

\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\sup_{x\in\Gamma}\sup_{z:\Pi^{\epsilon}(z)=x}\absolutevalue{ \mathbb{E}_z F(\Pi^\epsilon(Z^\epsilon(sT_\epsilon^1))) - \sum_{j=1}^{\abs{V}} p(x,O_j) \mathbf{E}_{O_j} F(Y(s)) }=0,

(1.4)

where
p(x,O_{j})
and
Y(s)
(a continuous-time Markov chain) will be introduced and defined precisely later.

The first theorem establishes the asymptotic behavior of the diffusion process at an intermediate time scale, showing it approximates a discrete-time Markov chain with absorbing states.
The second theorem then describes the behavior at the first critical time scale, which is shown to resemble a continuous-time Markov chain.
The proofs rely on refined exit estimates for the narrow escape problem in domains with bottlenecks, improving upon the results in
[
17
]
.

The narrow escape problem, which concerns the behavior of Brownian motion within a reflecting domain that features small escaping windows, is a well-established field of study in applied mathematics and physics.
This problem serves as an essential model for various physical and biological processes, including the propagation of electrical signals along nearly one-dimensional neuronal structures and the diffusion and transport of proteins within cellular networks (see, e.g.,
[
16
]
,
[
27
]
,
[
28
]
for applied perspectives).
The rigorous mathematical analysis of this problem has received comparatively less attention.
Existing mathematical works primarily focus on smooth domains (see
[
1
]
,
[
6
]
,
[
7
]
, and
[
26
]
), while studies addressing domains with complex geometric features, such as bottlenecks, remain relatively scarce (e.g.,
[
17
]
).

In this paper, we establish a more general exit place estimate and show that the exit time, conditioned on the exit edge, is asymptotically exponentially distributed with the same parameter as the unconditional exit time.
In other words, the exit time and the exit edge become asymptotically independent.

Finally, since the long-time behavior of solutions to the associated partial differential equations can be understood through the metastable dynamics of the underlying diffusion process, we use our diffusion asymptotics to characterize the asymptotic behavior of the solution
\rho_{\epsilon}(t(\epsilon),z)
to the following PDE,

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{\partial\rho_{\epsilon}}{\partial t}(t,z)=\Delta\rho_{\epsilon}(t,z),\ \ z\in G_{\epsilon},\\
\frac{\partial\rho_{\epsilon}}{\partial\nu_{\epsilon}}(t,z)=0,\ z\in\partial G_{\epsilon},\ \ \ \rho_{\epsilon}(0,z)=\varphi_{\epsilon}(z),\ z\in G_{\epsilon},\ \ \ \end{cases}

(1.5)

where the initial condition
\varphi_{\epsilon}
is assumed to be equicontinuous and equibounded.
This assumption includes the case
\varphi_{\epsilon}=\varphi|_{G_{\epsilon}}
for some
\varphi\in C(\bar{G})
.

The paper is organized as follows.
In Section
2
, we review the construction of the graph
\Gamma
and the corresponding narrow tube domain

, as well as previously established results on exit-time and exit-place estimates.
Section
3
develops estimates for the exit time and exit distribution, while Section
4
establishes the exponential law for the exit time.
In Sections
5
, we study the asymptotic behavior of the process at the intermediate time scale.
In particular, Section
5.1
shows that, at such time scale, the diffusion process behaves asymptotically as a discrete-time Markov chain, whereas Section
5.2
investigates the corresponding asymptotic behavior for the PDE.
Finally, Section
6
is devoted to the analysis of the process at the first critical time scale.

2.1
Asymptotic notations

Here, we define some asymptotic notations, which we will use very often throughout the paper:

1.

f(\epsilon)\ll g(\epsilon)
or
f(\epsilon)=o(g(\epsilon))
if
\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{f(\epsilon)}{g(\epsilon)}=0
.

2.

f(\epsilon)\gg g(\epsilon)
if
\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{g(\epsilon)}{f(\epsilon)}=0
.

3.

f(\epsilon)\sim g(\epsilon)
if
\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{f(\epsilon)}{g(\epsilon)}=1
.

4.

f(\epsilon)\asymp g(\epsilon)
if there exist
c,c^{\prime}>0
and
\epsilon_{0}>0
such that
cg(\epsilon)\leq f(\epsilon)\leq c^{\prime}g(\epsilon)
for all
\epsilon\in(0,\epsilon_{0})
.

5.

f(\epsilon)=O(g(\epsilon))
or
f(\epsilon)\lesssim g(\epsilon)
if there exist
c>0
and
\epsilon_{0}>0
such that
\absolutevalue{f(\epsilon)}\leq cg(\epsilon)
for all
\epsilon\in(0,\epsilon_{0})
.

In situations involving multiple parameters (e.g.,
\epsilon
,
\delta
), we use subscripts to indicate the relevant asymptotic dependencies.
If we only consider the limit
\epsilon\to 0
, we do not add any subscripts.
For instance, we write
f(\epsilon,\delta)\ll_{\epsilon,\delta}g(\epsilon,\delta)
or
f(\epsilon,\delta)=o_{\epsilon,\delta}(g(\epsilon,\delta))
to mean
\lim_{\delta\to 0}\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{f(\epsilon,\delta)}{g(\epsilon,\delta)}=0
.

2.3
The narrow tube
G_{\epsilon}

We consider a domain
G_{\epsilon}
consisting of narrow tubes
\Gamma^{\epsilon}_{k}
surrounding the edges
I_{k}\subset\Gamma
, along with small neighborhoods
\mathcal{E}_{j}^{\epsilon}
around the vertices
O_{j}\subset\Gamma
. See Figure
1(b)
.
More precisely, for each
j=1,\cdots,\absolutevalue{V}
, let
\mathcal{E}_{j}^{\epsilon}\coloneqq B(O_{j},r_{j}(\epsilon))
denote the Euclidean ball in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
centered at
O_{j}
with radius
r_{j}(\epsilon)
, where
r_{j}(\epsilon)>0
for all
\epsilon>0
, with
\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}r_{j}(\epsilon)=0
.
For each
k=1,\cdots,\absolutevalue{E}
, we consider the edge
I_{k}
and define its orthogonal complement
I_{k}^{\perp}
by

I_{k}^{\perp}\coloneqq\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\langle y,e_{j,k}\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}=0\},

where
e_{j,k}
is the unit vector pointing outward from a vertex
O_{j}
(with
I_{k}\sim O_{j}
) along the edge
I_{k}
.
This definition is well-posed, as the choice of
j
does not affect
I_{k}^{\perp}
; the vectors
e_{j,k}
corresponding to different endpoints of
I_{k}
differ only by a sign.
Additionally, we define the ball
B_{k}(y,r)
as

B_{k}(y,r)\coloneqq\{y^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:y^{\prime}-y\in I_{k}^{\perp},\absolutevalue{y'-y}\leq r\}.

Now let
\Gamma_{k}^{\epsilon}\coloneqq I_{k}+B_{k}(0,\lambda_{k}\epsilon)
, where
\lambda_{k}>0
is a constant.
We then define

G_{\epsilon}\coloneqq\bigcup_{j=1}^{\absolutevalue{V}}\mathcal{E}_{j}^{\epsilon}\cup\bigcup_{k=1}^{\absolutevalue{E}}\Gamma_{k}^{\epsilon}.

Although some results apply when
r_{j}(\epsilon)\lesssim\epsilon^{(d-1)/d}
, for clarity we impose the following assumption throughout this paper.

Assumption
.

For every
j=1,\cdots,\absolutevalue{V}
,

r_{j}(\epsilon)\gg\epsilon^{(d-1)/d}.

We first define the projection
\Pi:G_{\epsilon}\to\Gamma
, which maps
z\in G_{\epsilon}
to the point in
\Gamma
closest to
z
.
While this point may not be unique, we can choose it in a unique way. For example, we already have labels on the edges
I_{k}
. We then assign the projection to the point with the least edge index.
To be more precise, for every
x\in\Gamma\setminus\bigcup_{j=1}^{\absolutevalue{V}}O_{j}
, let
i(x)
be the index map such that
i(x)=k
if and only if
x\in I_{k}
.
For each
j=1,\cdots,\absolutevalue{V}
, set
i(O_{j})=k
if and only if
O_{j}\in I_{k}
and
k<l
for all
l
such that
O_{j}\in I_{l}
.
Then, for any
z\in G_{\epsilon}
, we define
\Pi(z)=x
if and only if
d(z,x)<d(z,x^{\prime})
for all
x^{\prime}\in\Gamma
, or if
d(z,x)=d(z,x^{\prime})
for some
x^{\prime}\in\Gamma
, but
i(x)<i(x^{\prime})
for all such
x^{\prime}\in\Gamma
.
Note that the projection doesn’t depend on
\epsilon
; in fact, one can define the projection in the whole
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

We now introduce a local coordinate system on
G_{\epsilon}
. For every
z\in G_{\epsilon}
, assume
\Pi(z)=x\in I_{k}
and let
O_{j}
be the closest vertex to
x
with the convention that if
x
is the midpoint of
I_{k}
, we choose the vertex with the smaller index.
Then we can write

x=\tilde{x}e_{j,k}+O_{j},

with
\tilde{x}\geq 0
.
Next, we construct an orthonormal basis
\{e_{j,k},n^{1}_{j,k},\cdots,n^{d-1}_{j,k}\}
of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, where the vectors
n^{1}_{j,k},\cdots,n^{d-1}_{j,k}
span the orthogonal complement of the edge direction.
Define
y\coloneqq z-x
, which lies in the span of
\{n^{1}_{j,k},\cdots,n^{d-1}_{j,k}\}

and write

y=\tilde{y}_{1}n_{j,k}^{1}+\cdots+\tilde{y}_{d-1}n_{j,k}^{d-1},

with
\tilde{y}=(\tilde{y}_{1},\cdots,\tilde{y}_{d-1})\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
.
In this way, each point
z=x+y
is uniquely represented by the local coordinate tuple

(j,k,\tilde{x},\tilde{y})\in\{1,\cdots,\absolutevalue{V}\}\times\{1,\cdots,\absolutevalue{E}\}\times[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d-1}.

Conversely, given
(j,k,\tilde{x},\tilde{y})
, with
I_{k}\sim O_{j}
,
\tilde{x}\geq 0
and
\tilde{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
, one can reconstruct the point
z\in G_{\epsilon}
via the formula

z=O_{j}+\tilde{x}e_{j,k}+\sum_{l=1}^{d-1}\tilde{y}_{l}n^{l}_{j,k}.

Thus, this local coordinate system provides a one-to-one correspondence between
z\in G_{\epsilon}
and the tuple
(j,k,\tilde{x},\tilde{y})
, with
I_{k}\sim O_{j}
,
\tilde{x}\geq 0
and
\tilde{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
.

It is important to note that
G_{\epsilon}
is not initially a smooth domain due to the presence of cusps where the tubular regions meet the vertex neighborhoods.
To address this, we mollify the boundary near each cusp to ensure that
B(O_{j},r_{j}(\epsilon))\subset G_{\epsilon}
and that the modified domain is locally smooth near these singularities, uniformly in
\epsilon
, as
\epsilon\to 0
. See Figure
2
below.
More precisely, for each
j=1,\cdots,\absolutevalue{V}
and each
k
such that
I_{k}\sim O_{j}
, we consider the local coordinate
(j,k,\tilde{x},\tilde{y})
.
Let
\tilde{x}^{\prime}\geq 0
be such that the point
x^{\prime}+y^{\prime}
, corresponding to the coordinate
(j,k,\tilde{x}^{\prime},\tilde{y}^{\prime})
, lies on the boundary
\partial B(O_{j},r_{j}(\epsilon))
with
\absolutevalue{\tilde{y}'}=2\lambda_{k}\epsilon
.
Define
\tilde{x}^{\prime\prime}\coloneqq r_{j}(\epsilon)+\frac{1}{2}\epsilon
.
We replace the portion of the boundary consisting of points
z\in\partial G_{\epsilon}
that correspond (via the local coordinate system) to tuples
(j,k,\tilde{x},\tilde{y})
with
\tilde{x}\in[\tilde{x}^{\prime},\tilde{x}^{\prime\prime}]
by a smooth transition region.
This modification ensures that the new domain still contains the ball
B(O_{j},r_{j}(\epsilon))
and remains smooth near the original cusp, uniformly in
\epsilon
.
Specifically, let

z^{*}_{j,k}\coloneqq B(O_{j},r_{j}(\epsilon))\cap I_{k}

and consider the change of variables

z\mapsto\frac{z-z^{*}_{j,k}}{\epsilon},\ \ \ z\in B(z^{*}_{j,k},10\lambda_{k}\epsilon).

Under this transformation, the image of the boundary segment
\partial G_{\epsilon}\cap B(z^{*}_{j,k},10\lambda_{k}\epsilon)
converges smoothly as
\epsilon\to 0
.
(This is possible since, after rescaling, the distance between
\tilde{x}^{\prime}
and
\tilde{x}^{\prime\prime}
exceeds
\frac{1}{2}
.)
As a result, we obtain a new smooth bounded domain with the desired properties.
Throughout this paper, we will work with this modified smooth domain and continue to denote it by
G_{\epsilon}
.

2.4
Diffusion processes on narrow tubes

We consider the diffusion process confined to live on the domain
G_{\epsilon}
,

dZ^{\epsilon}(t)=\sqrt{2}dB(t)+\nu_{\epsilon}(Z^{\epsilon}(t))d\phi^{\epsilon}(t),\ Z^{\epsilon}(0)=z,

(2.1)

where
z\in G_{\epsilon}
.
Here,
B(t)
is a
d
-dimensional Brownian motion defined on a stochastic basis
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0},\mathbb{P})
,
\nu_{\epsilon}(z)
is the unit inward normal vector at the point
z\in\partial G_{\epsilon}
, and
\phi^{\epsilon}(t)
is the local time of the process
Z^{\epsilon}(t)
on the boundary
\partial G_{\epsilon}
.
That is,
\phi^{\epsilon}(t)
is an adapted, continuous with probability
1
, non-decreasing process that increases only when
Z^{\epsilon}(t)\in\partial G
.
Formally, one can write

\phi^{\epsilon}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\mathbbm{1}_{\{Z^{\epsilon}(s)\in\partial G_{\epsilon}\}}d\phi^{\epsilon}(s).

It can be shown that the generator of
Z^{\epsilon}(t)
is the Laplacian
\Delta
, with Neumann boundary conditions on
\partial G_{\epsilon}
.

2.5
Previous exit time and exit place estimates

For each
j=1,\cdots,\absolutevalue{V}

L_{j,k}>0
for all

We then define

\displaystyle C_{\epsilon,j}^{k}(L_{j,k})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\{z\in G_{\epsilon}\cap\Pi^{-1}(I_{k}):d_{\Gamma}(\Pi(z),O_{j})=L_{j,k}\},

\displaystyle C_{\epsilon,j}(\vec{L}_{j})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\bigcup_{k:I_{k}\sim O_{j}}C_{\epsilon,j}^{k}(L_{j,k}).

We also let

\displaystyle\sigma_{j}^{\epsilon}(\vec{L}_{j})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\inf\{t\geq 0:Z^{\epsilon}(t)\in C_{\epsilon,j}(\vec{L}_{j})\}.

For convenience, for every
\delta>0

\displaystyle G_{\epsilon,j}(\delta)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\{z\in G_{\epsilon}:d_{\Gamma}(\Pi(z),O_{j})\geq\delta\},

3
Exit time and exit place estimates

In this section, our goal is to improve the exit time and exit place estimates.

For each
j=1,\cdots,\absolutevalue{V}

\vec{L}_{j}^{\epsilon}=\bigcup_{k:I_{k}\sim O_{j}}\{(k,L_{j,k}^{\epsilon})\}

Let us first define the sequence of stopping times

\displaystyle\sigma_{n}^{\epsilon,\delta}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\inf\{t\geq\tau_{n}^{\epsilon,\delta}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon}):Z^{\epsilon}(t)\in C_{\epsilon}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon})\cup C_{\epsilon}(r(\epsilon)+3\epsilon)\},

\displaystyle\tau_{n}^{\epsilon,\delta}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon})

3.1
Exit place estimate

Lemma 3.1
.

For every
k:I_{k}\sim O
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\sup_{z\in C_{\epsilon}(r(\epsilon)+3\epsilon)}\absolutevalue{ \mathbb{P}_z \left( Z^\epsilon(\sigma^{\epsilon}(\vec{L}^\epsilon) \in C_{\epsilon}^k(L_k^\epsilon) \right) - p_{k}(\vec{L})}=0,

where

p_{k}(\vec{L})=\frac{\lambda_{k}^{d-1}/L_{k}}{\sum_{l:I_{l}\sim O}\lambda_{l}^{d-1}/L_{l}}.

Proof.

For
z\in C_{\epsilon}(r(\epsilon)+3\epsilon)
, let

p_{k}^{\epsilon,\delta}(z)=\mathbb{P}_{z}\left(Z^{\epsilon}(\sigma^{\epsilon}(\delta)\in C_{\epsilon}^{k}(\delta))\right)=\mathbb{P}_{z}\left(Z^{\epsilon}(\tau_{1}^{\epsilon,\delta}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon}))\in C_{\epsilon}^{k}(\delta)\right)

and

p_{k}^{\epsilon,\delta}(z,\vec{L}^{\epsilon})=\mathbb{P}_{z}\left(Z^{\epsilon}(\sigma_{1}^{\epsilon,\delta}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon}))\in C_{\epsilon}^{k}(L_{k}^{\epsilon})\right);

that is,
p_{k}^{\epsilon,\delta}(z,\vec{L}^{\epsilon})
is the probability that the particle escapes through edge
k
in one cycle.
Then, by Lemma
2.1
,

p_{k}^{\epsilon,\delta}(z,\vec{L}^{\epsilon})=p_{k}^{\epsilon,\delta}(z)\frac{\delta-r(\epsilon)+3\epsilon}{L_{k}^{\epsilon}-r(\epsilon)+3\epsilon}\sim_{\epsilon,\delta}\frac{\lambda_{k}^{d-1}}{\sum_{l:I_{l}\sim O}\lambda_{l}^{d-1}}\frac{\delta}{L_{k}}

(3.3)

uniformly in
z\in C_{\epsilon}(r(\epsilon)+3\epsilon)
.
Moreover, by the strong Markov property,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{z}\left(Z^{\epsilon}(\sigma^{\epsilon}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon}))\in C_{\epsilon}^{k}(L_{k}^{\epsilon})\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}_{z}\left(Z^{\epsilon}(\sigma^{\epsilon}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon}))\in C_{\epsilon}^{k}(L_{k}^{\epsilon}),\sigma_{i}^{\epsilon,\delta}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon})=\sigma^{\epsilon}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon})\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}_{z}\left(\mathbbm{1}_{\{\sigma_{i-1}^{\epsilon,\delta}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon})<\sigma^{\epsilon}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon})\}}\mathbb{P}_{\sigma_{i-1}^{\epsilon,\delta}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon})}\left(Z^{\epsilon}(\sigma_{1}^{\epsilon,\delta}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon}))\in C_{\epsilon}^{k}(L_{k}^{\epsilon})\right)\right)

\displaystyle\sim_{\epsilon,\delta}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}_{z}\left(\sigma_{i-1}^{\epsilon,\delta}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon})<\sigma^{\epsilon}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon})\right)p_{k}^{\epsilon,\delta}(z^{\prime},\vec{L}^{\epsilon})\sim_{\epsilon,\delta}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left(1-\sum_{l:I_{l}\sim O}p_{l}^{\epsilon,\delta}(z^{\prime\prime},\vec{L}^{\epsilon})\right)^{i-1}p_{k}^{\epsilon,\delta}(z^{\prime},\vec{L}^{\epsilon})

\displaystyle\sim_{\epsilon,\delta}\frac{p_{k}^{\epsilon,\delta}(z^{\prime},\vec{L}^{\epsilon})}{\sum_{l:I_{l}\sim O}p_{l}^{\epsilon,\delta}(z^{\prime\prime},\vec{L}^{\epsilon})}\sim_{\epsilon,\delta}p_{k}(\vec{L})

uniformly in
z,z^{\prime},z^{\prime\prime}\in C_{\epsilon}(r(\epsilon)+3\epsilon)
.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

By (

3.2
Exit time estimate

Lemma 3.3
.

We have

\displaystyle\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\sup_{z\in C_{\epsilon}(r(\epsilon)+3\epsilon)}\absolutevalue{\mathbb{E}_z \sigma^{\epsilon}(\vec{L}^\epsilon) \left( \alpha(\epsilon) \frac{\sum_{l:I_l \sim O} \lambda_l^{d-1}}{\sum_{k:I_k \sim O} \lambda_k^{d-1}/L_k}, \right)^{-1} -1 }=0,

(3.4)

where

\alpha(\epsilon)=\frac{r(\epsilon)^{d}V_{d}}{\sum_{k:I_{k}\sim O}\lambda_{k}^{d-1}\epsilon^{d-1}V_{d-1}}.

(3.5)

Proof.

The idea is to apply Wald’s identity (see, e.g.,
[
17
, Lemma 4.2]
and the proof of Lemma
3.4
below), which states that the total exit time equals the time spent in each trajectory multiplied by the expected number of trials.
We decompose
\sigma^{\epsilon}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon})
into cycles using the stopping times
\tau_{n}^{\epsilon,\delta}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon}),\sigma_{n}^{\epsilon,\delta}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon})
.
By Lemma
2.1
, the dominant term to the time spent in each trajectory is
\alpha(\epsilon)\delta
.
In addition, by Remark
3.2
, the number of trials is

\left(\mathbb{P}_{z}\left(Z^{\epsilon}(\sigma_{1}^{\epsilon,\delta}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon}))\in C_{\epsilon}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon})\right)\right)^{-1}\sim_{\epsilon,\delta}\frac{\sum_{l:I_{l}\sim O}\lambda_{l}^{d-1}}{\delta\sum_{k:I_{k}\sim O}\lambda_{k}^{d-1}/L_{k}}.

(3.6)

Combining these two estimates yields the desired result.
The detailed proof is omitted.
∎

Let
\sigma_{k}^{\epsilon}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon})
be the first hitting time on
C_{\epsilon}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon})
conditioned on hitting
C_{\epsilon}^{k}(L_{k}^{\epsilon})
; that is, for any
I
be a Borel subset in
[0,\infty)

\mathbb{P}_{z}\left(\sigma_{k}^{\epsilon}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon})\in I\right)=\mathbb{P}_{z}\left(\sigma^{\epsilon}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon})\in I\middle|Z^{\epsilon}(\sigma^{\epsilon}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon}))\in C_{\epsilon}^{k}(L_{k}^{\epsilon})\right).

Lemma 3.4
.

We have

\displaystyle\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\sup_{z\in C_{\epsilon}(r(\epsilon)+3\epsilon)}\absolutevalue{\mathbb{E}_z \sigma_{k}^\epsilon(\vec{L}^\epsilon) \left( \alpha(\epsilon) \frac{\sum_{l:I_l \sim O} \lambda_l^{d-1}}{\sum_{k:I_k \sim O} \lambda_k^{d-1}/L_k} \right)^{-1} -1 }=0,

(3.7)

where

\alpha(\epsilon)=\frac{r(\epsilon)^{d}V_{d}}{\sum_{k:I_{k}\sim O}\lambda_{k}^{d-1}\epsilon^{d-1}V_{d-1}}.

(3.8)

Proof.

We would like to apply Wald’s identity as before.
However, the possible dependence between the exit time and the exit place prevents us from doing so directly.
Let
N^{\epsilon}
be such that
\sigma_{N^{\epsilon}}^{\epsilon,\delta}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon})=\sigma^{\epsilon}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon})
.
Then for all
z\in C_{\epsilon}(r(\epsilon)+3\epsilon)
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{z}\left(\sigma^{\epsilon}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon})\mathbbm{1}_{\{Z^{\epsilon}(\sigma^{\epsilon}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon}))\in C_{\epsilon}^{k}(L_{k}^{\epsilon})\}}\right)=\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}_{z}\left(\sigma_{N^{\epsilon}}^{\epsilon,\delta}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon})\mathbbm{1}_{\{N^{\epsilon}=l\}}\mathbbm{1}_{\{Z^{\epsilon}(\sigma^{\epsilon}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon}))\in C_{\epsilon}^{k}(L_{k}^{\epsilon})\}}\right)

(3.9)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}_{z}\left[\sum_{i=0}^{l}\left(\sigma_{i}^{\epsilon,\delta}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon})-\tau_{i}^{\epsilon,\delta}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon})\right)\mathbbm{1}_{\{N^{\epsilon}=l\}}\mathbbm{1}_{\{Z^{\epsilon}(\sigma^{\epsilon}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon}))\in C_{\epsilon}^{k}(L_{k}^{\epsilon})\}}\right]

\displaystyle+\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}_{z}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{l}\left(\tau_{i}^{\epsilon,\delta}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon})-\sigma_{i-1}^{\epsilon,\delta}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon})\right)\mathbbm{1}_{\{N^{\epsilon}=l\}}\mathbbm{1}_{\{Z^{\epsilon}(\sigma^{\epsilon}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon}))\in C_{\epsilon}^{k}(L_{k}^{\epsilon})\}}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\sum_{l=i}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}_{z}\left[\left(\sigma_{i}^{\epsilon,\delta}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon})-\tau_{i}^{\epsilon,\delta}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon})\right)\mathbbm{1}_{\{N^{\epsilon}=l\}}\mathbbm{1}_{\{Z^{\epsilon}(\sigma^{\epsilon}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon}))\in C_{\epsilon}^{k}(L_{k}^{\epsilon})\}}\right]

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\sum_{l=i}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}_{z}\left[\left(\tau_{i}^{\epsilon,\delta}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon})-\sigma_{i-1}^{\epsilon,\delta}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon})\right)\mathbbm{1}_{\{N^{\epsilon}=l\}}\mathbbm{1}_{\{Z^{\epsilon}(\sigma^{\epsilon}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon}))\in C_{\epsilon}^{k}(L_{k}^{\epsilon})\}}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}_{z}\left[\left(\sigma_{i}^{\epsilon,\delta}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon})-\tau_{i}^{\epsilon,\delta}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon})\right)\mathbbm{1}_{\{N^{\epsilon}\geq i\}}\mathbbm{1}_{\{Z^{\epsilon}(\sigma^{\epsilon}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon}))\in C_{\epsilon}^{k}(L_{k}^{\epsilon})\}}\right]

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}_{z}\left[\left(\tau_{i}^{\epsilon,\delta}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon})-\sigma_{i-1}^{\epsilon,\delta}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon})\right)\mathbbm{1}_{\{N^{\epsilon}=i\}}\mathbbm{1}_{\{Z^{\epsilon}(\sigma^{\epsilon}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon}))\in C_{\epsilon}^{k}(L_{k}^{\epsilon})\}}\right]

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}_{z}\left[\left(\tau_{i}^{\epsilon,\delta}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon})-\sigma_{i-1}^{\epsilon,\delta}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon})\right)\mathbbm{1}_{\{N^{\epsilon}\geq i+1\}}\mathbbm{1}_{\{Z^{\epsilon}(\sigma^{\epsilon}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon}))\in C_{\epsilon}^{k}(L_{k}^{\epsilon})\}}\right]

\displaystyle I_{3}^{\epsilon}=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}_{z}\left[\left(\tau_{i}^{\epsilon,\delta}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon})-\sigma_{i-1}^{\epsilon,\delta}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon})\right)\mathbbm{1}_{\{N^{\epsilon}\geq i+1\}}\mathbbm{1}_{\{Z^{\epsilon}(\sigma^{\epsilon}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon}))\in C_{\epsilon}^{k}(L_{k}^{\epsilon})\}}\right]

since, for all

,

and thus, by Lemma
3.1
, we have

Next, by the Wald’s identity, Lemma
2.1
and Remark
3.2
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}_{z}\left[\left(\tau_{i}^{\epsilon,\delta}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon})-\sigma_{i-1}^{\epsilon,\delta}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon})\right)\mathbbm{1}_{\{N^{\epsilon}\geq i+1\}}\right]

(3.11)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}_{z}\left[\left(\tau_{i}^{\epsilon,\delta}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon})-\sigma_{i-1}^{\epsilon,\delta}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon})\right)\mathbbm{1}_{\{N^{\epsilon}\geq i\}}\right]-\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}_{z}\left[\left(\tau_{i}^{\epsilon,\delta}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon})-\sigma_{i-1}^{\epsilon,\delta}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon})\right)\mathbbm{1}_{\{N^{\epsilon}=i\}}\right]

\displaystyle\sim\mathbb{E}_{z^{\prime}}\sigma^{\epsilon}(\delta)\mathbb{E}_{z}N^{\epsilon}\sim\alpha(\epsilon)\frac{\sum_{l:I_{l}\sim O}\lambda_{l}^{d-1}}{\sum_{k:I_{k}\sim O}\lambda_{k}^{d-1}/L_{k}},

uniformly in
z,z^{\prime}\in C_{\epsilon}(r(\epsilon)+3\epsilon)
.
Note that
\{N^{\epsilon}>i-1\}=\{N^{\epsilon}\leq i-1\}^{c}\in\mathcal{F}_{\sigma_{i-1}^{\epsilon,\delta}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon})}\subset\mathcal{F}_{\tau_{i}^{\epsilon,\delta}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon})}
and thus for the second term,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}_{z}\left[\left(\tau_{i}^{\epsilon,\delta}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon})-\sigma_{i-1}^{\epsilon,\delta}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon})\right)\mathbbm{1}_{\{N^{\epsilon}=i\}}\right]=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}_{z}\left[\left(\tau_{i}^{\epsilon,\delta}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon})-\sigma_{i-1}^{\epsilon,\delta}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon})\right)\mathbbm{1}_{\{N^{\epsilon}=i\}}\mathbbm{1}_{\{N^{\epsilon}>i-1\}}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}_{z}\left[\left(\tau_{i}^{\epsilon,\delta}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon})-\sigma_{i-1}^{\epsilon,\delta}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon})\right)\mathbbm{1}_{\{N^{\epsilon}>i-1\}}\mathbb{E}_{\tau_{i}^{\epsilon,\delta}}\mathbbm{1}_{\{N^{\epsilon}=0\}}\right]

\displaystyle=\delta\sup_{z^{\prime}\in C_{\epsilon}(r(\epsilon)+3\epsilon)}\mathbb{E}_{z^{\prime}}\sigma^{\epsilon}(\delta)\mathbb{E}_{z}N^{\epsilon}\ll_{\epsilon,\delta}\alpha(\epsilon)\frac{\sum_{l:I_{l}\sim O}\lambda_{l}^{d-1}}{\sum_{k:I_{k}\sim O}\lambda_{k}^{d-1}/L_{k}}.

It is not hard to show that
I_{1}^{\epsilon}
and

Finally, combining (

(3.12)

Dividing both sides by

and applying Lemma

Since we continue to focus on the local behavior near a vertex, we omit the subscript

Theorem 4.1
.

For each
t>0
,

\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\sup_{z\in C_{\epsilon}(r(\epsilon)+3\epsilon)}\absolutevalue{ \mathbb{P}_z \left( \sigma^{\epsilon}(\vec{L}^\epsilon) \geq t \alpha(\epsilon) \frac{\sum_{l:I_l \sim O} \lambda_l^{d-1}}{\sum_{k:I_k \sim O} \lambda_k^{d-1} / L_k} \right) - e^{-t}}=0.

\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\sup_{z\in C_{\epsilon}(r(\epsilon)+3\epsilon)}\absolutevalue{ \mathbb{P}_z \left( \sigma_k^{\epsilon}(\vec{L}^\epsilon) \geq t \alpha(\epsilon) \frac{\sum_{l:I_l \sim O} \lambda_l^{d-1}}{\sum_{k:I_k \sim O} \lambda_k^{d-1} / L_k} \right) - e^{-t}}=0,

(4.2)

where

\alpha(\epsilon)=\frac{r(\epsilon)^{d}V_{d}}{\sum_{k:I_{k}\sim O}\lambda_{k}^{d-1}\epsilon^{d-1}V_{d-1}}.

(4.10)

Once this is established, note that

\frac{e(\epsilon,\delta)}{p(\epsilon,\delta,\vec{L}^{\epsilon})}=\alpha(\epsilon)\delta\left(\sum_{k:I_{k}\sim O}\frac{\lambda_{k}^{d-1}/L_{k}}{\sum_{l:I_{l}\sim O}\lambda_{l}^{d-1}}\delta\right)^{-1}=\alpha(\epsilon)\frac{\sum_{l:I_{l}\sim O}\lambda_{l}^{d-1}}{\sum_{k:I_{k}\sim O}\lambda_{k}^{d-1}/L_{k}},

(4.11)

and thus (
4.2
) follows directly from Theorem
4.2
.

Lemma 4.3
.

We have

\lim_{\delta\to 0}\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\sup_{z\in\mathcal{S}_{\epsilon}}\sup_{n\geq 0}\absolutevalue{ \frac{\mathbb{P}_z \left( \zeta_{n+1}^{\epsilon,\delta,\vec{L}^\epsilon}=0 \middle| \zeta_n^{\epsilon,\delta,\vec{L}^\epsilon} = 1 \right)}{p(\epsilon,\delta,\vec{L}^\epsilon)} - 1 }=0.

Proof.

By the strong Markov property,

\mathbb{P}_{z}\left(\zeta_{n+1}^{\epsilon,\delta,\vec{L}^{\epsilon}}=0,\zeta_{n}^{\epsilon,\delta,\vec{L}^{\epsilon}}=1\right)=\mathbb{E}_{z}\left[\mathbbm{1}_{\{\zeta_{n}^{\epsilon,\delta,\vec{L}^{\epsilon}}=1\}}\mathbb{P}_{Z^{\epsilon}(\sigma_{n}^{\epsilon,\delta}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon}))}\left(Z^{\epsilon}(\sigma_{1}^{\epsilon}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon}))\in C_{\epsilon}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon})\right)\right].

Under the event
\{\zeta_{n}^{\epsilon,\delta,\vec{L}^{\epsilon}}=1\}
,
Z^{\epsilon}(\sigma_{n}^{\epsilon,\delta}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon}))\in C_{\epsilon}(r(\epsilon)+3\epsilon)
and thus due to Remark
3.2
, we have

\mathbb{P}_{Z^{\epsilon}(\sigma_{n}^{\epsilon,\delta}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon}))}\left(Z^{\epsilon}(\sigma_{1}^{\epsilon}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon}))\in C_{\epsilon}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon})\right)\sim_{\epsilon,\delta}p(\epsilon,\delta,\vec{L}^{\epsilon}).

Dividing both sides by
\mathbb{P}_{z}\left(\zeta_{n}^{\epsilon,\delta,\vec{L}^{\epsilon}}=1\right)
, we then have the result.
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

We have

\lim_{\delta\to 0}\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\sup_{z\in\mathcal{S}_{\epsilon}}\sup_{n\geq 0}\absolutevalue{ \frac{\mathbb{E}_z \left( \xi_{k,n+1}^{\epsilon,\delta,\vec{L}^\epsilon} \middle| \mathcal{F}_n^{\epsilon,\delta,\vec{L}^\epsilon} \right)}{e(\epsilon,\delta)} - 1}=0.

Proof.

By the strong Markov property,

\mathbb{E}_{z}\left(\xi_{k,n+1}^{\epsilon,\delta,\vec{L}^{\epsilon}}\middle|\mathcal{F}_{n}^{\epsilon,\delta,\vec{L}^{\epsilon}}\right)=\mathbb{E}_{\hat{X}_{n}^{\epsilon,\delta,\vec{L}^{\epsilon}}}\xi_{k,1}^{\epsilon,\delta,\vec{L}^{\epsilon}}.

Thanks to the definition of
\xi_{k,n}^{\epsilon,\delta,\vec{L}^{\epsilon}}
, it suffices to consider
\hat{X}_{n}^{\epsilon,\delta^{\prime},\vec{L}^{\epsilon}}\in C_{\epsilon}(r(\epsilon)+3\epsilon)
.
For
z^{\prime}\in C_{\epsilon}(r(\epsilon)+3\epsilon)
, by the strong Markov property, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{z^{\prime}}\xi_{k,1}^{\epsilon,\delta,\vec{L}^{\epsilon}}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{z^{\prime}}\left[\left(\sigma_{1}^{\epsilon,\delta}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon})-\tau_{1}^{\epsilon,\delta}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon})\right)\mathbbm{1}_{\{Z^{\epsilon}(\sigma^{\epsilon}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon}))\in C_{\epsilon}^{k}(L_{k}^{\epsilon})\}}\right]\left(\mathbb{P}_{z^{\prime}}(Z^{\epsilon}(\sigma^{\epsilon}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon}))\in C_{\epsilon}^{k}(L_{k}^{\epsilon}))\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}_{z^{\prime}}\left[\left(\tau_{1}^{\epsilon,\delta}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon})-\sigma_{0}^{\epsilon,\delta}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon})\right)\mathbbm{1}_{\{Z^{\epsilon}(\sigma^{\epsilon}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon}))\in C_{\epsilon}^{k}(L_{k}^{\epsilon})\}}\right]\left(\mathbb{P}_{z^{\prime}}(Z^{\epsilon}(\sigma^{\epsilon}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon}))\in C_{\epsilon}^{k}(L_{k}^{\epsilon}))\right)^{-1}.

For the first term, it is not hard to show

\displaystyle\sup_{z^{\prime}\in C_{\epsilon}(r(\epsilon)+3\epsilon)}\mathbb{E}_{z^{\prime}}\left[\left(\sigma_{1}^{\epsilon,\delta}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon})-\tau_{1}^{\epsilon,\delta}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon})\right)\mathbbm{1}_{\{Z^{\epsilon}(\sigma^{\epsilon}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon}))\in C_{\epsilon}^{k}(L_{k}^{\epsilon})\}}\right]\left(\mathbb{P}_{z^{\prime}}(Z^{\epsilon}(\sigma^{\epsilon}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon}))\in C_{\epsilon}^{k}(L_{k}^{\epsilon}))\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle\leq c\delta=o(e(\epsilon,\delta)).

For the second term, by the argument in Lemma
3.4
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{z^{\prime}}\left[\left(\tau_{1}^{\epsilon,\delta}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon})-\sigma_{0}^{\epsilon,\delta}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon})\right)\mathbbm{1}_{\{Z^{\epsilon}(\sigma^{\epsilon}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon}))\in C_{\epsilon}^{k}(L_{k}^{\epsilon})\}}\right]\left(\mathbb{P}_{z^{\prime}}(Z^{\epsilon}(\sigma^{\epsilon}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon}))\in C_{\epsilon}^{k}(L_{k}^{\epsilon}))\right)^{-1}

(4.12)

\displaystyle\sim_{\epsilon,\delta}\mathbb{E}_{z^{\prime}}\left[\tau_{1}^{\epsilon,\delta}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon})-\sigma_{0}^{\epsilon,\delta}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon})\right]\sim_{\epsilon,\delta}e(\epsilon,\delta)

uniformly in
z^{\prime}\in C_{\epsilon}(r(\epsilon)+3\epsilon)
.
To be more precise,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{z^{\prime}}\left[\left(\tau_{1}^{\epsilon,\delta}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon})-\sigma_{0}^{\epsilon,\delta}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon})\right)\mathbbm{1}_{\{Z^{\epsilon}(\sigma^{\epsilon}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon}))\in C_{\epsilon}^{k}(L_{k}^{\epsilon})\}}\right]

(4.13)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{z^{\prime}}\left[\left(\tau_{1}^{\epsilon,\delta}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon})-\sigma_{0}^{\epsilon,\delta}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon})\right)\mathbb{P}_{\tau_{1}^{\epsilon,\delta}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon})}\left(Z^{\epsilon}(\sigma^{\epsilon}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon}))\in C_{\epsilon}^{k}(L_{k}^{\epsilon})\right)\right]

\displaystyle\sim_{\epsilon,\delta}\mathbb{E}_{z^{\prime}}\left(\tau_{1}^{\epsilon,\delta}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon})-\sigma_{0}^{\epsilon,\delta}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon})\right)p_{k}(\vec{L})

uniformly in
z^{\prime}\in C_{\epsilon}(r(\epsilon)+3\epsilon)
.
Here, we use the fact that Lemma
3.1
can be extended to initial point
C_{\epsilon}(\delta)
.
In fact,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{z}\left(Z^{\epsilon}(\sigma^{\epsilon}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon}))\in C_{\epsilon}^{k}(L_{k}^{\epsilon})\right)

(4.14)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{z}\left(Z^{\epsilon}(\sigma^{\epsilon}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon}))\in C_{\epsilon}^{k}(L_{k}^{\epsilon}),Z^{\epsilon}(\sigma_{0}^{\epsilon,\delta}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon}))\in C_{\epsilon}(r(\epsilon)+3\epsilon)\right)

\displaystyle+\mathbb{P}_{z}\left(Z^{\epsilon}(\sigma^{\epsilon}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon}))\in C_{\epsilon}^{k}(L_{k}^{\epsilon}),Z^{\epsilon}(\sigma_{0}^{\epsilon,\delta}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon}))\in C_{\epsilon}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon})\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{z^{\prime}}\left(Z^{\epsilon}(\sigma^{\epsilon}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon}))\in C_{\epsilon}^{k}(L_{k}^{\epsilon})\right)+o_{\epsilon,\delta}(1)\sim_{\epsilon,\delta}p_{k}(\vec{L}),

uniformly in
z\in C_{\epsilon}(\delta)
,
z^{\prime}\in C_{\epsilon}(r(\epsilon)+3\epsilon)
.
Dividing (
4.13
) by
\mathbb{P}_{z^{\prime}}(Z^{\epsilon}(\sigma^{\epsilon}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon}))\in C_{\epsilon}^{k}(L_{k}^{\epsilon}))
and using Lemma
3.1
again, we obtain (
4.12
).
∎

Lemma 4.5

We have

\lim_{\delta\to 0}\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\sup_{z\in\mathcal{S}_{\epsilon}}\sup_{n\geq 0}\mathbb{E}_{z}\left(\left(\xi_{k,n+1}^{\epsilon,\delta,\vec{L}^{\epsilon}}\right)^{2}\middle|\mathcal{F}_{n}^{\epsilon,\delta,\vec{L}^{\epsilon}}\right)(e(\epsilon,\delta))^{-2}<\infty.

Proof.

Similarly, it suffices to prove that for
\epsilon,\delta
small enough

\sup_{z\in C_{\epsilon}(r(\epsilon)+3\epsilon)}\mathbb{E}_{z}\left(\xi_{k,1}^{\epsilon,\delta,\vec{L}^{\epsilon}}\right)^{2}\leq c(e(\epsilon,\delta))^{2}.

With Lemma

, this follows from the same argument as in
[

, Lemma 3.3]
.

,

5
Intermediate time scales

Let
\absolutevalue{V'}
be the numbers of distinct orders
\{r_{j}(\epsilon)\}_{j=1}^{\absolutevalue{V}}
and
r_{(i)}(\epsilon)
be the
i
-th smallest order radius for
i=1,\cdots,\absolutevalue{V'}
.
In other words, for every
j=1,\cdots,\absolutevalue{V}
, there exists
i\in\{1,\cdots,\absolutevalue{V'}\}
such that

r_{j}(\epsilon)\asymp r_{(i)}(\epsilon)

and for every
i=1,\cdots,\absolutevalue{V'}
, there exists
j\in\{1,\cdots,\absolutevalue{V}\}
such that

r_{(i)}(\epsilon)\asymp r_{j}(\epsilon).

Moreover,

r_{(1)}(\epsilon)\ll r_{(2)}(\epsilon)\ll\cdots\ll r_{(\absolutevalue{V'})}(\epsilon).

For all
i=1,\cdots,\absolutevalue{V'}
, define

T_{\epsilon}^{i}\coloneqq\frac{r_{(i)}(\epsilon)^{d}}{\epsilon^{d-1}}.

(5.1)

In this section, we study the asymptotic behavior of
Z^{\epsilon}
at the time scale
t(\epsilon)
such that
T_{\epsilon}^{i}\ll t(\epsilon)\ll T_{\epsilon}^{i+1}
for some
i=1,\cdots,\absolutevalue{V'}-1
.

5.1
Asymptotic discrete time Markov chain

For each
i=1,\cdots,\absolutevalue{V'}-1
, let
X_{n}^{i}
be a discrete time Markov chain with the state space
\mathcal{S}=\bigcup_{j=1}^{\absolutevalue{V}}O_{j}
and the transition probability

p^{i}(O_{j},O_{j})=\begin{cases}1,&\text{ if }r_{j}(\epsilon)\gg r_{(i)}(\epsilon),\\
0,&\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

(5.2)

For
j\neq j^{\prime}
and
r_{j}(\epsilon)\lesssim r_{(i)}(\epsilon)

p^{i}(O_{j},O_{j^{\prime}})=\begin{cases}p_{j,k}(\vec{L}),&\text{ if }I_{k}\sim O_{j},O_{j^{\prime}},\\
0,&\text{ otherwise},\end{cases}

(5.3)

where
p_{j,k}(\vec{L})
is given by Lemma
3.1
with
L_{j,k}=\absolutevalue{I_k}
.
It is well-known that for every initial point
O_{j}
, there exists an absorbing probability distribution
\mu^{i}(O_{j},\cdot)
on
\mathcal{S}
.

Let
\sigma^{\epsilon,i}
be the first hitting time to
\bigcup_{j^{\prime}}C_{\epsilon,j^{\prime}}(r_{j^{\prime}}(\epsilon)+3\epsilon)
, where the union is taken over all
j^{\prime}
such that
r_{j^{\prime}}(\epsilon)\gg r_{(i)}(\epsilon)
.

In what follows, we consider

L_{j,k}^{\epsilon}=\absolutevalue{I_k}-(r_{j^{\prime}}(\epsilon)+3\epsilon),\ \ \ I_{k}\sim O_{j},O_{j^{\prime}},\ \ \ \text{ and }\ \ \ \vec{L}_{j}^{\epsilon}=\bigcup_{k:I_{k}\sim O_{j}}\{(k,L_{j,k}^{\epsilon})\}.

(5.4)

Lemma 5.1
.

For every
i=1,\cdots,\absolutevalue{V'}-1
and every
j
such that
r_{j}(\epsilon)\lesssim r_{(i)}(\epsilon)
, we have, for all
j^{\prime}=1,\cdots\absolutevalue{V}
,

\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\sup_{z\in C_{\epsilon,j}(r_{j}(\epsilon)+3\epsilon)}\absolutevalue{ \mathbb{P}_{z}\left( Z^\epsilon(\sigma^{\epsilon,i}) \in C_{\epsilon,j'}(\rho_{j'}(\epsilon)+3\epsilon) \right) - \mu^i(O_j,O_{j'})}=0,

(5.5)

and for all
t(\epsilon)>0
satisfying
t(\epsilon)\gg T_{\epsilon}^{i}
,

\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\sup_{z\in C_{\epsilon,j}(r_{j}(\epsilon)+3\epsilon)}\mathbb{P}_{z}(\sigma^{\epsilon,i}\geq t(\epsilon))=0.

(5.6)

Proof.

Let
\tau_{n}^{\epsilon}
denote the successive hitting times at which
Z^{\epsilon}
visits different sets
C_{\epsilon,j}(r_{j}(\epsilon)+3\epsilon)
.
Specifically, set

\tau_{0}^{\epsilon}\coloneqq 0
, and for
n\geq 1
, if
Z^{\epsilon}(\tau_{n-1}^{\epsilon})\in C_{\epsilon,j}(r_{j}(\epsilon)+3\epsilon)
, we define

\tau_{n}^{\epsilon}\coloneqq\inf\left\{t\geq\tau_{n-1}^{\epsilon}:Z^{\epsilon}(t)\in\bigcup_{i\neq j}C_{\epsilon,i}(r_{i}(\epsilon)+3\epsilon)\right\}.

(5.7)

Equation (
5.5
) follows directly from Lemma
3.1
by taking
\vec{L}_{j}^{\epsilon}
as in (
5.4
) and applying the definition of the transition probabilities of
X_{n}^{i}
.

For (
5.6
), note that for all
j
such that
r_{j}(\epsilon)\lesssim r_{(i)}(\epsilon)
there exist constants
c>0
and
N>0
, such that

\inf_{\epsilon\in(0,1)}\inf_{z\in C_{\epsilon,j}(r_{j}(\epsilon)+3\epsilon)}\mathbb{P}_{z}(\sigma^{\epsilon,i}<\tau_{N}^{\epsilon})\geq c.

(5.8)

Consequently, for all
j
such that
r_{j}(\epsilon)\lesssim r_{(i)}(\epsilon)
,

\displaystyle\sup_{z\in C_{\epsilon,j}(r_{j}(\epsilon)+3\epsilon)}\mathbb{P}_{z}(\sigma^{\epsilon,i}\geq t(\epsilon))

(5.9)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{m=1}^{M}\sup_{z\in C_{\epsilon,j}(r_{j}(\epsilon)+3\epsilon)}\mathbb{P}_{z}(\sigma^{\epsilon,i}\geq t(\epsilon),\sigma^{\epsilon,i}=\tau_{m}^{\epsilon})+\sum_{m=M+1}^{\infty}\sup_{z\in C_{\epsilon,j}(r_{j}(\epsilon)+3\epsilon)}\mathbb{P}_{z}(\sigma^{\epsilon,i}\geq t(\epsilon),\sigma^{\epsilon,i}=\tau_{m}^{\epsilon})

\displaystyle\eqqcolon I_{1}^{\epsilon}(M)+I_{2}^{\epsilon}(M).

For
I_{2}^{\epsilon}
, first note that for every
m\geq 0
, by the strong Markov property,

\sup_{z\in C_{\epsilon,j}(r_{j}(\epsilon)+3\epsilon)}\mathbb{P}_{z}(\sigma^{\epsilon,i}\geq t(\epsilon),\sigma^{\epsilon,i}=\tau_{m}^{\epsilon})\leq\sup_{z\in C_{\epsilon,j}(r_{j}(\epsilon)+3\epsilon)}\mathbb{P}_{z}(\sigma^{\epsilon,i}\geq\tau_{m}^{\epsilon})\leq(1-c)^{\lfloor\frac{m}{N}\rfloor}.

(5.10)

For every
\eta>0
, there exists
M_{\eta}>0
such that

I_{2}^{\epsilon}(M_{\eta})\leq\sum_{m=M_{\eta}+1}^{\infty}(1-c)^{\lfloor\frac{m}{N}\rfloor}<\eta.

(5.11)

Next, for
I_{1}^{\epsilon}
, note that, by the strong Markov property and Lemma
3.3

\displaystyle\sup_{z\in C_{\epsilon,j}(r_{j}(\epsilon)+3\epsilon)}\mathbb{P}_{z}(\sigma^{\epsilon,i}=\tau_{m}^{\epsilon},\tau_{m}^{\epsilon}\geq t(\epsilon))\leq\sup_{z\in C_{\epsilon,j}(r_{j}(\epsilon)+3\epsilon)}\frac{\mathbb{E}_{z}\tau_{m}^{\epsilon}\mathbbm{1}_{\{\sigma^{\epsilon,i}=\tau_{m}^{\epsilon}\}}}{t(\epsilon)}

(5.12)

\displaystyle=\sup_{z\in C_{\epsilon,j}(r_{j}(\epsilon)+3\epsilon)}\frac{\mathbb{E}_{z}\tau_{m}^{\epsilon}\mathbbm{1}_{\{\sigma^{\epsilon,i}=\tau_{m}^{\epsilon}\}}\mathbbm{1}_{\{\sigma^{\epsilon,i}>\tau_{m-1}^{\epsilon}\}}}{t(\epsilon)}

\displaystyle\leq m\frac{\max_{j^{\prime}}\sup_{z\in C_{\epsilon,j^{\prime}}(r_{j^{\prime}}(\epsilon)+3\epsilon)}\mathbb{E}_{z}\sigma_{j^{\prime}}^{\epsilon}(\vec{L}^{\epsilon})}{t(\epsilon)}\leq cm\frac{T_{\epsilon}^{i}}{t(\epsilon)},

where the maximum is taken over all
j^{\prime}
such that
r_{j^{\prime}}(\epsilon)\lesssim r_{(i)}(\epsilon)
.
As a result, we have

\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}I_{1}^{\epsilon}(M_{\eta})\leq\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\sum_{m=1}^{M_{\eta}}cm\frac{T_{\epsilon}^{i}}{t(\epsilon)}=0.

(5.13)

Combining (
5.11
) and (
5.13
), we have

\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\sup_{z\in C_{\epsilon,j}(r_{j}(\epsilon)+3\epsilon)}\mathbb{P}_{z}(\sigma^{\epsilon,i}\geq t(\epsilon))\leq\eta.

(5.14)

Since
\eta>0
is arbitrary, we conclude that (
5.6
) holds.
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

For every
i=1,\cdots,\absolutevalue{V'}-1
, every
t(\epsilon)>0
with
t(\epsilon)\ll T_{\epsilon}^{i+1}
, every
j
such that
r_{j}(\epsilon)\gg r_{(i)}(\epsilon)
, and all
\delta>0
, we have

\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\sup_{z\in C_{\epsilon,j}(r_{j}(\epsilon)+3\epsilon)}\mathbb{P}_{z}(\sigma_{j}^{\epsilon}(\delta)\leq t(\epsilon))=0.

(5.15)

Proof.

For all
\eta>0
, let
t_{\eta}>0
such that
e^{-t_{\eta}}\geq 1-\frac{\eta}{2}
.
For all
j
such that
r_{j}(\epsilon)\gg r_{(i)}(\epsilon)
, there exist
\epsilon_{0}>0
and
c>0
such that for all
\epsilon\in(0,\epsilon_{0})
,

\alpha_{j}(\epsilon)=\frac{r_{j}(\epsilon)^{d}V_{d}}{\sum_{k:I_{k}\sim O_{j}}\lambda_{k}^{d-1}\epsilon^{d-1}V_{d-1}}\geq cT_{\epsilon}^{i+1}.

(5.16)

Moreover, there exists
\epsilon_{1,\eta}\in(0,\epsilon_{0})
such that for all
\epsilon\in(0,\epsilon_{1,\eta})
,

\frac{t(\epsilon)}{\alpha_{j}(\epsilon)\delta}\leq\frac{t(\epsilon)}{cT_{\epsilon}^{i+1}\delta}<t_{\eta}.

(5.17)

We have

\mathbb{P}_{z}(\sigma_{j}^{\epsilon}(\delta)\geq t(\epsilon))\geq\mathbb{P}_{z}(\sigma_{j}^{\epsilon}(\delta)\geq t_{\eta}\alpha_{j}(\epsilon)\delta).

(5.18)

By (
4.1
), there exists
\epsilon_{\eta}\in(0,\epsilon_{1,\eta})
such that

\mathbb{P}_{z}(\sigma^{\epsilon}(\delta)\geq t_{\eta}\alpha_{j}(\epsilon)\delta)\geq e^{-t_{\eta}}-\frac{\eta}{2}\geq 1-\eta.

(5.19)

We then conclude that

\mathbb{P}_{z}(\sigma^{\epsilon}(\delta)\leq t(\epsilon))\leq 1-\mathbb{P}_{z}(\sigma^{\epsilon}(\delta)\geq t_{\eta}\alpha_{j}(\epsilon)\delta)\leq\eta.

(5.20)

∎

Theorem 5.3
.

For every
i=1,\cdots,\absolutevalue{V'}-1
, every
t(\epsilon)>0
such that
T_{\epsilon}^{i}\ll t(\epsilon)\ll T_{\epsilon}^{i+1}
and any continuous function
F\in C(\Gamma)
, we have

\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\sup_{z\in C_{\epsilon,j}(r_{j}(\epsilon)+3\epsilon)}\absolutevalue{ \mathbb{E}_{z} (F(\Pi^\epsilon(Z^\epsilon(t(\epsilon)))) - \sum_{j'=1}^{\abs{V}} F(O_{j'}) \mu^i(O_j,O_{j'}) }=0.

(5.21)

Proof.

First, consider
z\in C_{\epsilon,j}(r_{j}(\epsilon)+3\epsilon)
such that
r_{j}(\epsilon)\lesssim r_{(i)}(\epsilon)
.
Note that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{z}F(\Pi^{\epsilon}(Z^{\epsilon}(t(\epsilon)))

(5.22)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{z}\left(F(\Pi^{\epsilon}(Z^{\epsilon}(t(\epsilon)))\mathbbm{1}_{\{\sigma^{\epsilon,i}\geq t(\epsilon)\}}\right)+\mathbb{E}_{z}\left(F(\Pi^{\epsilon}(Z^{\epsilon}(t(\epsilon)))\mathbbm{1}_{\{\sigma^{\epsilon,i}<t(\epsilon)\}}\right)

\displaystyle\eqqcolon I_{1}^{\epsilon}+I_{2}^{\epsilon}.

For
I_{1}^{\epsilon}
, thanks to (
5.6
), we have

\displaystyle\sup_{z\in C_{\epsilon,j}(r_{j}(\epsilon)+3\epsilon)}\absolutevalue{\mathbb{E}_{z} \left( F(\Pi^\epsilon(Z^\epsilon(t(\epsilon))) \mathbbm{1}_{\{ \sigma^{\epsilon,i} \geq t(\epsilon) \}} \right)}

(5.23)

\displaystyle\leq\absolutevalue{F}_{C(\Gamma)}\sup_{z\in C_{\epsilon,j}(r_{j}(\epsilon)+3\epsilon)}\mathbb{P}_{z}(\sigma^{\epsilon,i}\geq t(\epsilon))\to 0,\text{ as }\epsilon\to 0.

For
I_{2}^{\epsilon}
, first define the stopping time

\tau^{\epsilon,i}(\delta)\coloneqq\inf\{t\geq\sigma^{\epsilon,i}:Z^{\epsilon}(t)\in\bigcup_{j=1}^{\absolutevalue{V}}C_{\epsilon,j}(\delta)\}.

We obtain

\displaystyle I_{2}^{\epsilon}=\mathbb{E}_{z}\left(F(\Pi^{\epsilon}(Z^{\epsilon}(t(\epsilon)))\mathbbm{1}_{\{\sigma^{\epsilon,i}<t(\epsilon)\}}\right)

(5.24)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{z}\left([F(\Pi^{\epsilon}(Z^{\epsilon}(t(\epsilon)))-F(\Pi^{\epsilon}(Z^{\epsilon}(\sigma^{\epsilon,i}))]\mathbbm{1}_{\{\sigma^{\epsilon,i}<t(\epsilon)\}}\right)+\mathbb{E}_{z}\left(F(\Pi^{\epsilon}(Z^{\epsilon}(\sigma^{\epsilon,i}))\mathbbm{1}_{\{\sigma^{\epsilon,i}<t(\epsilon)\}}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{z}\left([F(\Pi^{\epsilon}(Z^{\epsilon}(t(\epsilon)))-F(\Pi^{\epsilon}(Z^{\epsilon}(\sigma^{\epsilon,i}))]\mathbbm{1}_{\{\sigma^{\epsilon,i}<t(\epsilon)\}}\mathbbm{1}_{\{\tau^{\epsilon,i}(\delta)>t(\epsilon)\}}\right)

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}_{z}\left([F(\Pi^{\epsilon}(Z^{\epsilon}(t(\epsilon)))-F(\Pi^{\epsilon}(Z^{\epsilon}(\sigma^{\epsilon,i}))]\mathbbm{1}_{\{\sigma^{\epsilon,i}<t(\epsilon)\}}\mathbbm{1}_{\{\tau^{\epsilon,i}(\delta)\leq t(\epsilon)\}}\right)

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}_{z}\left(F(\Pi^{\epsilon}(Z^{\epsilon}(\sigma^{\epsilon,i}))\mathbbm{1}_{\{\sigma^{\epsilon,i}<t(\epsilon)\}}\right)-\mathbb{E}_{z}\left(F(\Pi^{\epsilon}(Z^{\epsilon}(\sigma^{\epsilon,i}))\right)

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}_{z}\left(F(\Pi^{\epsilon}(Z^{\epsilon}(\sigma^{\epsilon,i}))\right)

\displaystyle\eqqcolon J_{2,1}^{\epsilon}+J_{2,2}^{\epsilon}+J_{2,3}^{\epsilon}+J_{2,4}^{\epsilon}.

For
J_{2,1}^{\epsilon}
, by the continuity of
F
, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{z\in C_{\epsilon,j}(r_{j}(\epsilon)+3\epsilon)}\absolutevalue{ \mathbb{E}_{z} \left( [F(\Pi^\epsilon(Z^\epsilon(t(\epsilon)))- F(\Pi^\epsilon(Z^\epsilon(\sigma^{\epsilon,i})) ] \mathbbm{1}_{\{ \sigma^{\epsilon,i} < t(\epsilon) \}} \mathbbm{1}_{\{ \tau^{\epsilon,i}(\delta) > t(\epsilon) \}} \right) }

(5.25)

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{z\in C_{\epsilon,j}(r_{j}(\epsilon)+3\epsilon)}\mathbb{E}_{z}\absolutevalue{ [F(\Pi^\epsilon(Z^\epsilon(t(\epsilon)))- F(\Pi^\epsilon(Z^\epsilon(\sigma^{\epsilon,i})) ] \mathbbm{1}_{\{ \sigma^{\epsilon,i} < t(\epsilon) \}} \mathbbm{1}_{\{ \tau^{\epsilon,i}(\delta) > t(\epsilon) \}} }=o_{\epsilon,\delta}(1).

For
J_{2,2}^{\epsilon}
, due to (
5.15
), we get

\displaystyle\sup_{z\in C_{\epsilon,j}(r_{j}(\epsilon)+3\epsilon)}\absolutevalue{\mathbb{E}_{z} \left( [F(\Pi^\epsilon(Z^\epsilon(t(\epsilon)))- F(\Pi^\epsilon(Z^\epsilon(\sigma^{\epsilon,i})) ] \mathbbm{1}_{\{ \sigma^{\epsilon,i} < t(\epsilon) \}} \mathbbm{1}_{\{ \tau^{\epsilon,i}(\delta) \leq t(\epsilon) \}} \right)}

(5.26)

\displaystyle\leq 2\absolutevalue{F}_{C(\Gamma)}\sup_{z\in C_{\epsilon,j}(r_{j}(\epsilon)+3\epsilon)}\mathbb{E}_{z}\left[\mathbbm{1}_{\{\sigma^{\epsilon,i}<t(\epsilon)\}}\mathbbm{1}_{\{\tau^{\epsilon,i}(\delta)\leq t(\epsilon)\}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq 2\absolutevalue{F}_{C(\Gamma)}\sup_{z\in C_{\epsilon,j}(r_{j}(\epsilon)+3\epsilon)}\mathbb{E}_{z}\left[\mathbbm{1}_{\{\sigma^{\epsilon,i}<t(\epsilon)\}}\sum_{j^{\prime}}\mathbbm{1}_{\{Z^{\epsilon}(\sigma^{\epsilon,i})\in C_{\epsilon,j^{\prime}}(r_{j^{\prime}}(\epsilon)+3\epsilon)\}}\mathbb{P}_{\sigma^{\epsilon,i}}(\sigma_{j^{\prime}}^{\epsilon}(\delta)\leq t(\epsilon))\right]

\displaystyle\leq 2\absolutevalue{F}_{C(\Gamma)}\max_{j^{\prime}}\sup_{z\in C_{\epsilon,j^{\prime}}(r_{j^{\prime}}(\epsilon)+3\epsilon)}\mathbb{P}_{z}(\sigma_{j^{\prime}}^{\epsilon}(\delta)\leq t(\epsilon))\to 0\text{ as }\epsilon\to 0,

where the maximum is taken over all
j^{\prime}
such that
r_{j^{\prime}}(\epsilon)\gg r_{(i)}(\epsilon)
.
For
J_{2,3}^{\epsilon}
, by (
5.6
), we have

\displaystyle\sup_{z\in C_{\epsilon,j}(r_{j}(\epsilon)+3\epsilon)}\absolutevalue{\mathbb{E}_{z} \left( F(\Pi^\epsilon(Z^\epsilon(\sigma^{\epsilon,i})) \mathbbm{1}_{\{ \sigma_\epsilon^{i} < t(\epsilon) \}} \right) - \mathbb{E}_{z} \left( F(\Pi^\epsilon(Z^\epsilon(\sigma^{\epsilon,i})) \right)}

(5.27)

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{z\in C_{\epsilon,j}(r_{j}(\epsilon)+3\epsilon)}\mathbb{E}_{z}\absolutevalue{F(\Pi^\epsilon(Z^\epsilon(\sigma^{\epsilon,i})) \mathbbm{1}_{\{ \sigma^{\epsilon,i} \geq t(\epsilon) \}} }

\displaystyle\leq\absolutevalue{F}_{C(\Gamma)}\sup_{z\in C_{\epsilon,j}(r_{j}(\epsilon)+3\epsilon)}\mathbb{P}_{z}(\sigma^{\epsilon,i}\geq t(\epsilon))\to 0\text{ as }\epsilon\to 0.

Finally, combining all of the above, we have

For

(6.28)

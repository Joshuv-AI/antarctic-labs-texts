MiR-4725-3p acts as an oncogene and prognostic biomarker in hepatocellular carcinoma
Authors: Xiumin Zhao, Zhenhong Chang, Jinmin Su, Jiaoling Gu, Zhizhong Lu, Peng Dai
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundHepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) is an aggressive malignant neoplasm with high incidence and dismal prognostic outcomes.PurposeThis study sought to validate the prognostic significance of miR-4725-3p in HCC and the possible molecular mechanism.MethodsA total of 117 HCC patients were stratified into two groups according to the median expression level of miR-4725-3p. Following a 5-year follow-up with death as the end event, a Kaplan-Meier curve was drawn. The potential risk factors for poor prognosis were evaluated using Cox regression analysis. Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8) and Transwell assay were employed to assess the role of miR-4725-3p silencing on Huh-7 and Hep3B cells viability and migration. Downstream target genes of miR-4725-3p were predicted, and a dual-luciferase assay was conducted to verify the interaction.ResultsMiR-4725-3p was significantly upregulated in HCC tissue and cell line. Survival analysis showed that high miR-4725-3p expression was associated with poor prognosis in HCC patients (log-rank P = 0.006). At the cellular level, silencing miR-4725-3p significantly suppressed the viability and migration of Huh-7 and Hep3B cells. Mechanistically, activating transcription factor 5 (ATF5) was identified as a direct target of miR-4725-3p. ATF5 was downregulated in HCC tissue and cells.ConclusionThe results of this study revealed that miR-4725-3p serves as a promising prognostic biomarker for HCC, and its oncogenic role in promoting HCC cell viability and migration may be mediated, at least in part, via the negative regulation of ATF5.Keywords: MiR-4725-3p, Hepatocellular carcinoma, Prognosis, ATF5

Introduction

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) accounts for 80 ± 5% of all liver cancer cases, ranking as the third foremost cause of cancer-related mortality globally and exhibiting a high prevalence across Asian regions [1, 2]. HCC is characterized by insidious onset and rapid progression, resulting in most patients being diagnosed at advanced stages and thus missing the optimal window for curative treatment [3–5]. Moreover, existing comprehensive treatment methods have substantial limitations, the severity of HCC progression coupled with inadequate therapeutic efficacy collectively resulting in an overall 5-year survival rate of less than 20% for patients with HCC [6, 7]. Therefore, accurate prognostic evaluation is critical for optimizing clinical decisions and stratifying patients for personalized treatment, highlighting the urgent need for reliable and sensitive prognostic biomarkers.

Recent studies have confirmed that microRNAs (miRNAs) are closely associated with the survival, recurrence risk, and therapeutic response in patients with HCC, highlighting their potential as valuable prognostic biomarkers [8, 9]. For instance, miR-5010-3p exerts a pro-proliferative effect on HCC cells, and its elevated expression is positively correlated with poor prognosis [10, 11]. Downregulation of miR-218 functions as a potential non-invasive biomarker for the diagnosis and prognostic assessment of HCC [12, 13]. Dysregulation of miR-4725-3p has been implicated in the tumorigenesis and progression of multiple tumors, including glioma and cholangiocarcinoma [14, 15]. Prior studies have revealed elevated miR-4725-3p expression in the serum of patients with autoimmune hepatitis (AIH), with favorable diagnostic sensitivity and specificity [16]. Similarly, significantly increased miR-4725-3p expression has been observed in human liver cirrhosis samples compared with normal hepatic tissues [17]. Given that liver cirrhosis represents the greatest risk factor for HCC and constitutes a primary indication for cancer screening and long-term clinical surveillance. These findings advised that there may be a potential association between miR-4725-3p and the pathological mechanisms underlying HCC. However, the detailed expression profile and clinical significance of miR-4725-3p in HCC remain largely unknown. Furthermore, whether miR-4725-3p possesses prognostic value for patients with HCC has not been systematically investigated.

Based on these findings, it was hypothesized that miR-4725-3p may be functionally implicated in HCC progression and prognosis. To validate this hypothesis, the present study detected the expression of miR-4725-3p between HCC tumor and adjacent normal tissues, evaluated 5-year survival outcomes of the enrolled patients, and provided new evidence supporting the potential of miR-4725-3p as a prognostic biomarker for HCC.

Materials and methods

Study populations

A total of 117 individuals with HCC were collected from Jiaozuo People’s Hospital, and all enrolled patients were initially diagnosed and surgically treated at our institution with no prior anti-tumor treatment. Fresh HCC tumor tissues and their adjacent normal hepatic tissues (≥ 2 cm away from the tumor margin) were collected immediately after surgical resection. All tissue samples were rapidly snap-frozen within 30 min of resection and subsequently stored at -80 °C.

Inclusion criteria: (1) Histologically confirmed HCC; (2) Meet the criteria for resectable surgery; (3) No prior anti-tumor treatments such as radiotherapy, chemotherapy, or immunotherapy before surgery; (4) No prior systemic anti-tumor treatments for HCC, including chemotherapy, targeted therapy, or immunotherapy; (5) Child-Pugh class A liver function. Exclusion criteria: (1) A history of other malignant tumors; (2) Sarcomatoid HCC, fibrolamellar HCC, or mixed bile duct cell-HCC is known to exist; (3) Complicated with severe dysfunction of major organs such as the heart, lungs, kidneys, or brain; (4) Lost to follow-up or having incomplete follow-up data.

Real-Time Quantitative Polymerase Chain Reaction (RT-qPCR)

Tissue samples were collected from all subjects. Total RNA was extracted by Trizol® Reagent (Ambion, USA). RNA samples of acceptable quality were reverse-transcribed into cDNA using SuperScript™ IV RT (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA). Quantitative PCR was performed using the miScript SYBR Green PCR Kit (QIAGEN, Germany). Using U6 and GAPDH as internal reference, the relative miR-4725-3p/Activating transcription factor 5 (ATF5) expression levels in tissue samples were detected using the 2-△△Ct method. The coefficient of variation (CV) was calculated to assess the expression stability of the U6/GAPDH. CV was defined as the ratio of the standard deviation (SD) to the mean expression level.

The primer sequences: MiR-4725-3p: 5’-ACTTGAATTCGAGTTGGCTTTAGGTGGAACCCAGG-3’ (F) and 5’-GCGAGGATCCAAGGATTTTTAGGGGCATTATGAC-3‘(R). ATF5: 5’-CCTTCACCCTCCCGACC-3‘(F) and 5’-CAGAGTCATGTGGAACGGGA-3’ (R). U6: 5’-CTCGCTTCGGCAGCACA-3’ (F) and 5’-AACGCTTCACGAATTTGCGT-3’ (R). GAPDH: 5’-GATTCCACCCATGGCAAATTCC-3’ (F) and 5’-TCGCTCCTGGAAGATGGTGAT-3’ (R).

General data collection and follow-up analysis

Medical history included general demographic information (gender, age). The tumor-related clinical data mainly included tumor number, tumor size, alpha-fetal protein (AFP) level, intrahepatic metastasis (IM) status, microvascular invasion (MVI), and tumor node metastasis (TNM) stage. In addition, laboratory tests were conducted to test miR-4725-3p and ATF5 expression in the collected tissues. Tumor size (≤ 5 cm vs. > 5 cm) and TNM stage (I + II vs. III + IV) were classified according to the 8 th edition AJCC/UICC. Serum AFP was chosen based on the established clinical threshold for HCC risk stratification and previous literature. MVI (M0, M1, M2) based on histopathological examination.

A total of 117 HCC patients were divided into a low expression group (n = 58) and a high expression group (n = 59) based on the median value of miR-4725-3p expression. All patients were followed up for 5 years, and follow-up was conducted through outpatient re-examination or telephone contact. Outcome events were defined as death.

Cell culture and transfection

Normal human liver epithelial cell line THLE-2 and human HCC cell lines Huh-7 and Hep3B were purchased from Procell (Wuhan, China). THLE-2 were cultured in THLE-2 Cell Complete Medium (CM-0833, Procell), while Huh-7 and Hep3B cells were cultured in DMEM medium (Gibco, USA). All cells were incubated at 37 °C in a humidified atmosphere with 5% CO₂, and the culture medium was renewed every 48 h.

The si-ATF5 and negative control (NC) were purchased from RiboBio (Guangzhou, China). The miR-4725-3p mimic, miR-4725-3p inhibitor, and their NC (MCE, USA) were transfected into cells using the Lipofectamine 3000 kit (Invitrogen, USA).

Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8)

Huh-7 and Hep3B cells were cultured into 96-well plates at a density of 5 × 103 cells per well. 24 h after seeding, CCK-8 reagent (Beyotime, Shanghai) was introduced to well, and the plate was maintained at 37 °C with 5% CO₂ for 2 h. The absorbance at 450 nm was measured using a microplate reader (Tecan, USA).

Transwell assay

The upper chamber membrane of the Transwell chamber (8 μm) was hydrated with serum-free medium at 37℃ for 30 min. 5 × 104 cells/well were added to the upper chamber, and 500 µL medium supplemented with 10% FBS was added to the lower chamber. Cells that migrated to the lower surface of the membrane were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde (KALANG, Shanghai). Five non-overlapping random fields were captured under an inverted microscope (Nicon, Japan). The number of stained migrated cells was manually counted.

Prediction and functional annotation of target genes of miR-4725-3p

The target genes of miR-4725-3p were predicted using the miRDB (Current Version 6.0) with its default prediction score cutoff (≥ 50) and miRTARGET [18] databases, respectively. HCC-related datasets were retrieved from GEO, and the dataset GSE227378, which characterizes gene expression profiles between HCC and matched healthy liver tissues, was selected for analysis. Differential expression was identified with |log2FC| > 1 and P.adj < 0.05. The results were plotted into a Venn diagram, and the intersection was used as the gene set for further study.

Dual-luciferase assay

The 3’-UTR of wild-type (WT) ATF5 and mutant (MUT) ATF5 were cloned into luciferase reporter plasmids. The two constructed recombinant plasmids were then co-transfected with miR-4725-3p mimics into HCC cell lines. Following a 48-hour incubation period after the completion of transfection, the relative luciferase activity was determined using a dedicated luciferase assay kit (Promega, Beijing).

Statistical analysis

GraphPad Prism 10.0 served as the statistical tool for processing all collected data. Continuous variables were expressed as mean ± SD. The t-test was conducted to compare between two groups, and one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was used for comparisons among multiple groups. Categorical data were presented as n (%), with intergroup comparisons performed via the Chi-square test. Kaplan-Meier curve and Cox analysis were used to explore the prognostic value of miR-4725-3p in HCC. P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Result

Expression of miR-4725-3p in HCC patients

To elucidate the expression profile and potential clinical implication of miR-4725-3p in HCC, the relative expression level of miR-4725-3p was first examined in a cohort of paired HCC tumor tissues and their corresponding adjacent normal tissues. The CV of U6 was 5.93%, which was below the commonly recommended threshold of 10% for stable reference genes, supporting its suitability for normalization. As shown in Fig. 1A, miR‑4725‑3p expression was significantly upregulated in HCC tumor tissues compared with paired adjacent normal tissues, and this difference was highly statistically significant (P < 0.01).

Correlation analysis between miR-4725-3p expression and clinical features of HCC

To investigate the clinical significance of miR-4725-3p in HCC, the Chi-square test was employed to analyze the correlation between its expression level and clinicopathological features of the cohort, with detailed data summarized in Table 1. The analysis results indicated that miR-4725-3p expression was significantly correlated with TNM stage (P = 0.007) and IM (P = 0.020). Specifically, high miR-4725-3p expression was more frequently observed in patients with advanced TNM stage (III-IV) and positive IM. In contrast, few significant correlations were observed between miR-4725-3p expression level and patients’ age, gender, tumor size, AFP, and MVI status (P > 0.05).

Prognosis of patients with HCC

Kaplan-Meier survival curve was plotted to further detect the clinical significance of miR-4725-3p in HCC, with data revealing a lower survival rate in the miR-4725-3p high-expression cohort relative to the low-expression cohort (Fig. 1B, log-rank P = 0.006). The median follow-up duration for the 117 enrolled patients was 33 months (3–60 months). As shown in Table 2, univariate Cox regression analysis was performed for all potential prognostic factors, the results identified tumor size, TNM stage, and miR-4725-3p expression as risk factors affecting the prognosis of HCC patients. Variables with P < 0.10 in univariate analysis were included in the multivariate Cox model. After adjustment for tumor size, MVI, TNM stage, and miR‑4725‑3p expression, multivariate Cox regression analysis confirmed that high miR-4725-3p expression was significantly correlated with poor prognosis in HCC patients (P = 0.024), with a Hazard Ratio (HR) of 1.636 (95%CI: 1.070–2.516).

miR-4725-3p regulates the viability and migration of HCC cells

RT-qPCR analysis showed that miR-4725-3p expression in Huh-7 and Hep3B cells was significantly higher than that in THLE-2 cells (P < 0.001; P = 0.002), indicating miR-4725-3p is highly expressed in HCC cells (Fig. 2A). After transfection with miR-4725-3p inhibitor, the miR-4725-3p expression in Huh-7 and Hep3B cells were downregulated compared with the NC groups (Fig. 2B-C), confirming the effective silencing. In functional assays, silencing miR-4725-3p significantly inhibited the viability of Huh-7 and Hep3B cells (Fig. 2D-E) and inhibited their migration ability (Fig. 2F-H, P < 0.05).

Identification of ATF5 as a potential target of miR-4725-3p

The downstream targets of miR-4725-3p were predicted using two online databases (miRDB and miRTARGET), and intersected them with the gene set from GEO dataset GSE227378 by the Venn diagram, which showed that ATF5 was among the overlapping genes (Fig. 3A). Notably, ATF5 had a high prediction score of 93 in miRDB. Analysis of GSE227378 revealed that miR-4725 was upregulated whereas ATF5 was downregulated in HCC (Fig. 3B). Data from the TCGA database further confirmed that ATF5 expression was significantly downregulated in HCC primary tumors than in normal tissues (Fig. 3C). The CV of GAPDH was 3.91%, which was < 5%, supporting its use for mRNA normalization. Analysis of clinical tissue samples demonstrated that ATF5 expression was significantly lower in HCC tissues than that in adjacent normal tissue (Fig. 3D), and ATF5 showed reduced expression in Huh-7 and Hep3B cells (Fig. 3E).

Verification of the direct interaction between miR-4725-3p and ATF5

The complementary binding sites between miR-4725-3p and ATF5 was predicted and illustrated (Fig. 4A). Meanwhile, the dual-luciferase assay identified that transfection of miR-4725-3p mimic remarkably suppressed luciferase activity in the WT ATF5 reporter plasmid group, while little significant change was observed in the MUT ATF5 group (Fig. 4B-C, P < 0.001). Moreover, inhibition of miR-4725-3p resulted in upregulated ATF5 expression in both Huh-7 and Hep3B cells (Fig. 4D-E). Pearson correlation analysis further verified a significant inverse correlation between miR-4725-3p and ATF5 in HCC tissues (Fig. 4F, R = -0.634, P < 0.001).

MiR‑4725‑3p affects cellular functions by regulating ATF5

To further confirmed that miR‑4725‑3p affects its biological functions via regulating ATF5, rescue experiments were performed. As shown in Fig. 5A, knockdown of miR‑4725‑3p significantly upregulated ATF5 expression, while co‑transfection with si‑ATF5 partially offset this effect (P < 0.001). Meanwhile, inhibition of miR‑4725‑3p markedly suppressed cell viability in Huh‑7 (Fig. 5B) and Hep3B (Fig. 5C) cells (P < 0.01), while simultaneous knockdown of ATF5 partially restored the cell viability. Consistently, Transwell assays revealed that miR‑4725‑3p inhibition reduced the migration of HCC cell lines (Fig. 5D-F), and this outcome t was partially reversed by ATF5 knockdown (P < 0.001).

Discussion

As one of the most prevalent malignant tumors globally, HCC exhibits complicated pathogenesis and an extremely poor prognosis [19]. Identifying key molecular regulatory targets and effective prognostic factors is of critical importance for HCC management [20]. The implementation of personalized surveillance and therapeutic regimens for distinct patient populations can effectively reduce medical resource consumption and alleviate social burdens [21–23]. This study confirmed that miR-4725-3p is upregulated in HCC tissues and cells. High miR-4725-3p expression was identified as a risk factor for poor prognosis of HCC patients. Functional experiments in vitro confirmed the oncogenic role of miR-4725-3p on HCC. These results collectively demonstrate that abnormal increased miR-4725-3p expression is linked to the development of HCC and exerts an oncogenic role.

The miR-4725 family exhibits remarkable subtype specificity and functional diversity, with its different isoforms exerting differential regulatory effects in tumors and other diseases. Notably, miR-4725-3p is highly expressed in patients with schizophrenia, AIH, and liver cirrhosis, serving as a potential diagnostic marker and promoting disease progression in relevant pathological processes [16, 17, 24], which is consistent with the abnormally high expression trend of miR-4725-3p in HCC observed in our study. In intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma, miR-4725-5p targets and inhibits the PDPK1 expression and negatively modulates the AKT/mTOR signaling pathway, acting as a tumor suppressor [15]. In colorectal cancer (CRC), miR-4725-3p is upregulated during drug treatment [25]. Meanwhile, circPTGR1 acts as a molecular sponge for miR-4725-5p to relieve the repression of its target gene FAK, thereby promoting CRC progression [26]. In esophageal squamous cell carcinoma, miR-4725 serves as a key regulator within the ceRNA network and modulates patient susceptibility to chemoradiotherapy [27]. The present study is the first to demonstrate that miR-4725-3p exerts an oncogenic role in HCC, which not only extends the regulatory landscape of the miR-4725 family but also provides novel insights into the molecular mechanisms underlying liver disease pathogenesis.

Mechanistically, ATF5 was validated as a direct downstream target gene of miR-4725-3p. ATF5 exerts a pivotal role in the cellular stress response, survival, proliferation, and differentiation [28, 29]. ATF5 is dysregulated in various cancers and contributes to tumor progression, chemoresistance, and recurrence by modulating multiple signaling pathways and cellular processes [30]. Notably, it is highly expressed in multiple cancers, such as bladder cancer, glioblastoma, and breast cancer [31–33]; in contrast, ATF5 has been confirmed to be downregulated in HCC [34]. As previously reported, array-based mapping analyses in HCC tissues revealed that the transcription factor ATF5 is consistently and stably silenced within the chr.19q13 region [35]. Previous studies have demonstrated the growth inhibitory effect of ATF5 in HCC cells, and low expression level of ATF5 in HCC predicted a worse prognosis [35, 36]. The present study further confirmed that ATF5 is downregulated in HCC tissue and cells, and its expression level is negatively correlated with miR-4725-3p expression. These findings are consistent with the classic miRNA-target gene regulatory model, suggesting that one of the potential mechanisms by which miR-4725-3p exerts its oncogenic effect is through inhibiting ATF5 expression.

Nevertheless, several limitations in the present study should be acknowledged. First, this was a single-center retrospective study with a relatively limited sample size, which may lead to a relatively wide confidence interval in multivariate analysis and may restrict the generalizability of the conclusions. Further multi-center, large-sample prospective studies are warranted to validate the prognostic value of miR-4725-3p. Second, all functional experiments were performed in vitro, and in vivo validation using animal models is lacking, which is insufficient to simulate the complex tumor microenvironment and physiological conditions in vivo. Future studies should use orthotopic liver cancer models to verify the role of miR-4725-3p in tumor growth and metastasis in vivo. Third, only tissue-based expression was detected in this study, and plasma or serum samples were not included, limiting the translational potential of miR-4725-3p as a non-invasive biomarker. Notably, miR‑4725‑3p has been reported to be detectable and significantly upregulated in the peripheral plasma of patients with schizophrenia and glaucoma, supporting its potential as a stable and non‑invasive biomarker in HCC [24, 37]. However, it should be acknowledged that the expression profiles and stability of microRNAs may differ between plasma and serum. Therefore, future studies are warranted to simultaneously detect the levels of miR‑4725‑3p in both serum and plasma from HCC patients, to verify their consistency and further validate its clinical utility as a non‑invasive prognostic indicator.

Despite the above limitations, this study initially revealed the effect of the miR-4725-3p/ATF5 in HCC progression. As a direction for future investigation, subsequent research can further explore the key signaling pathways downstream of this regulatory axis. Multiple studies have shown that molecular markers participate in HCC proliferation and metastasis by regulating multiple classic oncogenic pathways, including MAPK, TGF-β, Wnt/β-catenin, and ERK signaling [38–42]. Considering that ATF5 exerts well-documented tumor-suppressive functions, its functional mechanism involves upregulating mTOR expression and inhibiting Wnt/β-catenin pathway activation, which in turn mediates its biological effects on Huh7 and Hep3B cells [43]. Accordingly, future studies should focus on verifying whether miR-4725-3p promotes the progression of HCC by regulating the Wnt/β-catenin pathway. Specifically, it will be critical to examine changes in the expression and functional activity of key signaling molecules following perturbation of this regulatory axis, to clarify their hierarchical molecular cascade, to clarify their molecular cascade. This will provide a more comprehensive mechanistic framework for understanding the malignant biological behaviors of HCC mediated by miR-4725-3p.

Conclusion

In conclusion, this study identified that miR-4725-3p was highly expressed in HCC and exerted an oncogenic role by directly targeting ATF5. Clinically, high miR-4725-3p expression is significantly associated with poor patient prognosis, indicating its potential utility as a novel prognostic biomarker for HCC.

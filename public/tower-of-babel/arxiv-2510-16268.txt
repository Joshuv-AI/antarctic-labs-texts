COMMON FIXED POINT THEOREMS OF WEAKLY COMPATIBLE MAPS SATISFYING (f,g)-WEAKLY CONTRACTIVE CONDITION AND INVARIANT APPROXIMATION RESULTS
Babu G.V.R, D. RATNA BATU, Alemayehu G. Negash

Abstract
We prove the existence of common fixed points for three selfmaps T , f T,f and g g defined on a metric space ( X , d ) (X,d) satisfying, T T is ( f , g ) (f,g) -weakly contractive; and the pairs ( T , f ) (T,f) and ( T , g ) (T,g) are weakly compatible. Also, for such T , f T,f and g g , we prove the convergence of modified Mann iteration and modified Ishikawa iteration with respect to T , f T,f and g g to their common fixed point, in a normed space. Further, we obtain invariant approximation results from the set of best approximations to common fixed points of T , f T,f and g g .



Keywords and Phrases:
Common fixed points, weakly compatible maps,
(f,g)
-weakly contractive maps and modified Mann iteration, modified Ishikawa iteration, invariant approximations.

Introduction

Let
(X,d)
be a metric space. We write it simply as
X
. A map
T:X\to X
is said to be a selfmap of
X
.

Definition 1.1
.

A selfmap
T
of
X
is said to be a
contraction
if there exists a real number
k\in[0,1)
such that

d(Tx,Ty)\leq k\ d(x,y),\ \text{for all}\ x,y\in X.

(1.1.1)

If
k=1
then we call
T
a
nonexpansive map
.

In 1997, Alber and Guerre-Delabriere
[
1
]
introduced weakly contractive maps which are extensions of contraction maps and obtained fixed point results in the setting of Hilbert spaces.

Throughout this paper, we denote
R_{+}=[0,\infty)
,

\Psi\ =\ \{\psi/\psi:R_{+}\to R_{+}
is continuous on
R_{+}
,
\psi
is nondecreasing,

\psi(t)>0\ \ \text{for}\ \ t>0,\ \psi(0)=0\ \text{and}\ \lim_{t\to\infty}\psi(t)=\infty\}

and
Z_{+}\ =\ \{0,1,2,\ldots\}
.

Definition 1.2

(
[
6
]
)
.

Let
(X,d)
be a metric space. Let
K
be a nonempty subset of
X
. A selfmap
T
of
K
is called
weakly contractive
if, there exists
\psi\in\Psi
such that

d(Tx,Ty)\leq d(x,y)-\psi(d(x,y))\ \ \text{for each}\ \ x,y\in K.

We observe that every contraction map is weakly contractive map. But its converse need not be true; i.e., every weakly contractive map need not be a contraction.

Example 1.3
.

Let
X=R_{+}
, with the usual metric. Define
T:R_{+}\to R_{+}
by
T(x)=\frac{x}{1+x},x\in X
.

Then
T
is a weakly contractive map with
\psi(t)=\frac{t^{2}}{1+t},\ t\geq 0
.

But, for any
k\in[0,1)
,

does not satisfy (1.1.1)

when
x=0
and
0<y<\frac{1-k}{k}
so that
T
is not a contraction map.

An interesting result for a single weakly contractive selfmap in complete metric spaces is established by Rhoades (Theorem 1, page no. 2684,
[
6
]
), which we call ‘Rhoades weakly contractive principle’.

Theorem 1.4

(
Rhoades weakly contractive principle
)
.

Let
(X,d)
be a complete metric space,

has a unique fixed point in

In 2006, Beg and Abbas
[
3
]
extended the concept of weakly contractive property of a single selfmap to a pair of selfmaps and established the existence of common fixed points.

Definition 1.5

Theorem 1.6

Let

is a complete subspace of

Let

Main Results

The following theorem is our main result.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
(X,d)
be a metric space and let
T
be a
(f,g)-
weakly contractive mapping. If the pairs
(T,f)
and
(T,g)
are weakly compatible and
\overline{T(X)}\subseteq f(X)
,
\overline{T(X)}\subseteq g(X)
and
\overline{T(X)}
is a complete subspace of
X
, then
T
,
f
and
g
have a unique common fixed point in
X
.

Proof
.

Let
x_{0}\in X.
Since
\overline{T(X)}\subseteq f(X)
,
T(X)\subseteq f(X).
So there exists
x_{1}\in X
such that
Tx_{0}=fx_{1}.

Since
\overline{T(X)}\subseteq g(X)
,
T(X)\subseteq g(X).
So there exists
x_{2}\in X
such that
Tx_{1}=gx_{2}.

On continuing this process, inductively we get a sequence
\{x_{n}\}
in
X
such that
y_{2n}=T(x_{2n})=f(x_{2n+1})
and
y_{2n+1}=T(x_{2n+1})=g(x_{2n+2})
,
n=0,1,2,\ldots.

We now show that the sequence
\{d(y_{n},y_{n+1})\}
is a decreasing sequence. Now,

\displaystyle d(y_{2n},y_{2n+1})

\displaystyle=d(T(x_{2n}),T(x_{2n+1}))

\displaystyle\leq\min\{d(f(x_{2n}),g(x_{2n+1}))-\psi(d(f(x_{2n}),g(x_{2n+1}))),

\displaystyle\quad d(g(x_{2n}),f(x_{2n+1}))-\psi(d(g(x_{2n}),f(x_{2n+1}))))

\displaystyle\leq d(g(x_{2n}),f(x_{2n+1}))-\psi(d(g(x_{2n}),f(x_{2n+1})))

\displaystyle=d(y_{2n-1},y_{2n})-\psi(d(y_{2n-1},y_{2n}))

\displaystyle<d(y_{2n-1},y_{2n}).

Therefore

d(y_{2n},y_{2n+1})<d(y_{2n-1},y_{2n}).

(2.1.1)

Similarly, it is easy to see that

d(y_{2n+1},y_{2n+2})<d(y_{2n},y_{2n+1}).

(2.1.2)

Thus from (2.1.1) and (2.1.2) it follows that

d(y_{n},y_{n+1})<d(y_{n-1},y_{n}),\ n=0,1,2,\ldots.

Therefore
\{d(y_{n},y_{n+1})\}
is a strictly decreasing sequence of nonnegative reals and hence convergent and let its limit be
l\geq 0
.
If
l>0
then
l=\inf\limits_{n}d(y_{n},y_{n+1})
.
Thus we have
l\leq d(y_{n},y_{n+1})
for all
n
.

Now,

\displaystyle d(y_{n},y_{n+1})

\displaystyle=d(T(x_{n}),T(x_{n+1}))

\displaystyle\leq\min\{d(f(x_{n}),g(x_{n+1}))-\psi(d(f(x_{n}),g(x_{n+1}))),

\displaystyle\quad d(g(x_{n}),f(x_{n+1}))-\psi(d(g(x_{n}),f(x_{n+1})))\}.

(2.1.3)

If
n
is odd then from (2.1.3), we get

\displaystyle d(y_{n},y_{n+1})

\displaystyle=d(T(x_{n}),T(x_{n+1}))

\displaystyle\leq d(f(x_{n}),g(x_{n+1}))-\psi(d(f(x_{n}),g(x_{n+1})))

\displaystyle=d(y_{n-1},y_{n})-\psi(d(y_{n-1},y_{n})).

Therefore

d(y_{n},y_{n+1})\leq d(y_{n-1},y_{n})-\psi(d(y_{n-1},y_{n})).

(2.1.4)

If
n
is even then from (2.1.3), we get

\displaystyle d(y_{n},y_{n+1})

\displaystyle=d(T(x_{n}),T(x_{n+1}))

\displaystyle\leq d(g(x_{n}),f(x_{n+1}))-\psi(d(g(x_{n}),f(x_{n+1})))

\displaystyle=d(y_{n-1},y_{n})-\psi(d(y_{n-1},y_{n})).

Therefore

d(y_{n},y_{n+1})\leq d(y_{n-1},y_{n})-\psi(d(y_{n-1},y_{n})).

(2.1.5)

We have, from (2.1.4) and (2.1.5)

d(y_{n},y_{n+1})\leq d(y_{n-1},y_{n})-\psi(d(y_{n-1},y_{n}))\ \ \text{for all}\ \ n.

(2.1.6)

On taking limit as
n\to\infty
in (2.1.6), we get

\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}d(y_{n},y_{n+1})\leq\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}d(y_{n-1},y_{n})-\psi(\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}d(y_{n-1},y_{n})).

Therefore
l\leq l-\psi(l)<l
, a contradiction.
Hence,
l=0
.

Thus it follows that

\lim_{n\to\infty}d(y_{n},y_{n+1})=0.

Case(i):

y_{n}=y_{n+1}
for some
n
.

If
n
is even, we write
n=2m
(say),

.

y_{2m}=y_{2m+k}\quad\text{for all }k=0,1,2,\ldots.

If
n
is odd then
n=2m+1
(say),

.

Thus it follows that

Case(ii):

y_{n}\neq y_{n+1}
for all

We now show that

is a Cauchy sequence in

.

\lim_{k\to\infty}d(y_{2m_{k}-1},y_{2n_{k}})\leq\epsilon.

Now,

\displaystyle\epsilon

\displaystyle\leq d(y_{2m_{k}-1},y_{2n_{k}-1})

(2.1.13)

(2.1.14)

Thus by using (2.1.9) and (2.1.14), we get

\displaystyle\epsilon

\displaystyle=d(y_{2m_{k}},y_{2n_{k}})

\displaystyle\leq d(y_{2m_{k}},y_{2n_{k}+1})+d(y_{2n_{k}+1},y_{2n_{k}})

\displaystyle=d(T(x_{2m_{k}}),T(x_{2n_{k}+1}))+d(y_{2n_{k}+1},y_{2n_{k}})

\displaystyle\leq\min\{d(f(x_{2m_{k}}),g(x_{2n_{k}+1}))-\psi(d(f(x_{2m_{k}}),g(x_{2n_{k}+1}))),

\displaystyle\quad d(g(x_{2m_{k}}),f(x_{2n_{k}+1}))-\psi(d(g(x_{2m_{k}}),f(x_{2n_{k}+1})))\}

\displaystyle\quad+d(y_{2n_{k}+1},y_{2n_{k}})

\displaystyle\leq d(g(x_{2m_{k}}),f(x_{2n_{k}+1}))-\psi(d(g(x_{2m_{k}}),f(x_{2n_{k}+1})))

\displaystyle\quad+d(y_{2n_{k}+1},y_{2n_{k}})

\displaystyle=d(y_{2m_{k}-1},y_{2n_{k}})-\psi(d(y_{2m_{k}-1},y_{2n_{k}})))+d(y_{2n_{k}+1},y_{2n_{k}}).

(2.1.15)

\displaystyle\epsilon

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{k\to\infty}d(y_{2m_{k}-1},y_{2n_{k}})-\lim_{k\to\infty}\psi(d(y_{2m_{k}-1},y_{2n_{k}}))+\lim_{k\to\infty}d(y_{2n_{k}+1},y_{2n_{k}})

\displaystyle=\epsilon-\psi(\epsilon)<\epsilon,

a contradiction.
Therefore
\{y_{2n}\}
is a Cauchy sequence in

and hence

.

Since
T(X)\subseteq\overline{T(X)}

Conclusion

In this work, we introduced and investigated a new class of mappings, termed
(f,g)
-weakly contractive mappings, which extend the notions of weakly contractive and weakly compatible selfmaps to a triad of mappings
T
,
f
, and
g

Furthermore, we demonstrated that the modified Mann and modified Ishikawa iterative schemes associated with

The results presented here generalize and unify several classical and recent fixed point theorems, including those of Beg and Abbas

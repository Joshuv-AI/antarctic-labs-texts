Protocol for adeno-associated virus-mediated miRNA delivery in a rat heart failure model
Authors: Iqra Mushtaq, Yu-Hsun Kao, Yi-Jen Chen
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
SummaryDelivering microRNA (miRNA) to treat cardiac disease is a significant challenge, and selecting an efficient delivery method to target the affected organ is critical for therapeutic success. Here, we present a protocol for delivering miRNA to the heart of rats with heart failure using adeno-associated virus (AAV9) coupled with a hydrodynamic transfusion strategy. We describe steps for inducing heart failure, echocardiography, and AAV9 application. Further, we provide a comprehensive procedure to detect miRNA expression in cardiac tissues using stem-loop RT-qPCR.For complete details on the use and execution of this protocol, please refer to Mushtaq et al.1Subject areas: cell biology, model organisms, molecular biology, gene expression, molecular/chemical probes

Before you begin

MiRNAs are small endogenous RNA molecules typically containing 20–25 nucleotides that modulate post-transcription gene regulation by targeting RNA molecules. Alteration in the physiological expression of miRNAs in the heart can disrupt homeostasis, leading to structural abnormalities and heart failure (HF).2,3 Previous research conducted by our group has underscored the significant modulatory role of miR-452–5p in cardiac fibrogenesis during HF.1 Fibrosis results from intricate cellular interactions, and the specific expression level of miR-452–5p in cardiac tissues, and precise contribution to fibrosis remain largely undefined. Given that miR-452–5p is downregulated in HF, restoring its levels may offer a potential therapeutic strategy to mitigate fibrosis and improve cardiac function.

Numerous approaches have been documented in the literature for delivering miRNA to cardiac tissues e.g., direct injection of miRNA in cardiac chambers or pericardial space after thoracotomy.4,5 However, these approaches are extremely invasive and come with risks including 1) cardiac inflammation due to needle insertion 2) localized delivery of miRNA in specific areas 3) hemodynamic instability 4) reduced cellular uptake of injected miRNA. All these issues can significantly intervene in the study outcomes and make it unreliable. Given these challenges, minimal invasive systemic delivery is more effective, but it also comes with a problem of off-target effects. To address this factor, we employed AAV9.6 The AAV9 vector emerged as a promising vector for delivering miRNAs to cardiac tissue due to natural tropism for cardiac tissues, low immunogenicity, and stability. Several laboratories employed this for miRNA delivery.

In this protocol, we used an AAV9 viral vector to deliver miRNA (miR-452–5p), the AAV9-miRNA construct was generated by the AAV core facility (Academia Sinica, Taipei, Taiwan). More information can be found at (https://rnai.genmed.sinica.edu.tw/en/kind/custom aav). To deliver AAV9-miRNA, we used a minimally invasive approach “hydrodynamic transfusion method” which involves rapid delivery of AAV9-miRNA into the bloodstream. Although AAV9 has a strong affinity for cardiac tissues, combining an AAV9 vector with a systemic hydrodynamic transfection method boosts the efficiency of miRNA delivery.6 The injected large volume increases blood flow and creates a pressure wave leading to enhanced uptake of vectors by cardiac tissues. Simultaneous vascular pressure and AAV9 natural affinity ensure that miRNA is directed specifically to the heart, minimizing off-target delivery. In addition, rapid delivery decreases the duration of the vector in the bloodstream before reaching the heart, thereby decreasing the likelihood of degradation by circulating enzymes or immune clearance mechanisms.7 This method can be adapted for various experimental designs and therapeutic strategies for cardiac diseases.

Although this protocol was generated to deliver miRNA in an isoproterenol induced rat HF model, its versatility extends to other rodent models that involve minimally invasive miRNA fine-tuning in cardiac tissues.

Note: Throughout this protocol, AAV9-miRNA is referred to as the rno-miR-452 sequence inserted in AAV9 viral vector or it is not to be confused with another strain/AAV.

Materials and equipment

•Isoproterenol Dose.

Isoproterenol Dose.

Accurately weigh 100 mg of isoproterenol powder and transfer it into a 1 mL sterile mixing tube. Add normal saline by gently stirring the mixture until the isoproterenol powder is fully dissolved.

Note: Ensure there are no undissolved particles in the solution. Isoproterenol is unstable and loses potency with time. Use freshly prepared isoproterenol solution each time within one hour to ensure consistent dosing and reliable experimental outcomes.

•AAV9-miRNA Dose.

AAV9-miRNA Dose.

Determine the amount of viral genome (vg) required per rat, then dilute the stock to achieve the desired concentration (vg/mL). Adjust the final volume to 0.5%–1.0% of the animal’s body weight with saline or PBS.

Limitations

AAV9 has a strong affinity for cardiomyocytes but this depends on animal strain and disease state.7 Due to its episomal nature, the AAV9 genome doesn’t integrate into the host chromosome. As a result, AAV9 expression can be transient and may gradually reduce over time, as episomal DNA can be lost during cell division. Moreover, the extent of structural damage, fibrosis, and altered blood flow negatively affect the uptake of AAV9 viral vector during HF. This is why a single dose for a longer duration might not be enough and the treatment requires multiple doses or higher concentrations.

Another concern is the limited packaging capacity of the AAV9 vector (around 5 kb),13 which restricts the co-delivery of additional genes, i.e., GFP, a non-invasive reporter gene used to visually monitor the successful expression of the miRNA of interest. Consequently, we only verified the miRNA expression through stem-loop qPCR. While AAV9 is suitable for miRNA delivery, it is not ideal for the co-delivery of multiple genes, including miRNAs.

Troubleshooting

Problem 1

Heart failure has not developed.

Potential solution

Normally, a single subcutaneous injection of 100 mg/kg/bw is enough to induce acute stress on the heart within one week. However, the severity of cardiac injury depends on the dosage of isoproterenol, and rats have resilience against damage. If noticeable damage is not observed, then another dose may be injected (readers suggested to consult literature). Alternatively, an intraperitoneal infusion of 5 mg/kg/day for one week can be utilized.14 The infusion strategy should be optimized according to rat size, age, and gender before the experiment. Weekly cardiac follow-up via echocardiography by the same expert co-worker is mandatory to avoid any variation or discrepancy in the procedure.

Problem 2

Unclear imaging and irregular heart rate during echocardiography.

Potential solution

Ensure the chest area is completely hairless and apply a generous amount of ultrasound gel to proper acoustic coupling. First, use the higher frequency probe suitable for rats and adjust the probe angle carefully while maintaining consistent gentle pressure on the probe during each measurement. Readjust the probe to acquire a clear short and long-axis view. Restraining devices may help to stabilize the animal in case of animal stressful movements. Moreover, closely monitor the heart rate and sedation level during the procedure to make sure that the animal is not deeply sedated.

Problem 3

Difficulty in finding a lateral tail vein.

Potential solution

Enhance visibility by soaking the tail in warm water (in a 50 mL tube or small container) for up to 1 min to promote vasodilation, making the lateral vein more visible. The researcher can also use a tourniquet to constrict the vein i.e., a rubber band wrapped around the upper part of the tail, but it should be released before injecting the material. Finding lateral tail veins in black or brown rats is more challenging as compared to mice and it may lead to failed injection. Hence, it requires an extra light source or tail scales could be removed with chlorhexidine solution while ensuring the tail is clean as much as possible.

Problem 4

Infection developed at the injection site.

Potential solution

Infection at the injection site indicates poor handling of veins, lack of proper sterility of equipment, and contamination of the solution. Strictly use an alcohol swab or sterile cotton ball instead of a thumb to compress the injection site after removing the needle. If performed correctly, the infection will not develop. Sometimes researchers did not properly find a vein and inject the needle several times in the same area which may cause tissue damage and increase the risk of infection. Therefore, avoid repeated injections.

Problem 5

Tail vein rupture.

Potential solution

The vein will burst if the wrong needle size is used, or the needle is injected at the wrong angle and the solution leaks from the injection site. Remove the injection immediately and apply gentle pressure with a sterile gauze pad. Mark the rupture site to avoid reinjecting on the same site and select an alternative site or retry later.

Problem 6

The expected miRNA expression is not observed.

Potential solution

Several factors influence transfection efficiency in AAV9-mediated gene/miRNA delivery via the tail vein, including a) suboptimal viral titer and purity, b) insufficient dosing, c) organ tropism (verification of promoter specificity and integrity before injection), and d) procedural errors.15,16 For optimal results, maintain an RNase free environment, use a highly purified and potent viral titer while avoiding freeze-thaw cycles, excess pipetting, and temperatures outside 25°C–27°C. Readers are also encouraged to consult the literature for dose optimization in intravenous AAV9-miRNA delivery, as viral genome copy numbers vary based on specific cardiac pathologies.7,17,18,19 To ensure rapid delivery to the heart, the injected volume should be 0.5%–1% of total body weight and no less than that. Lastly, the intravenous injection should be performed by the same expert/researcher to avoid any variability.

Entire Functions on Lie Groups
Michael Heins

Abstract
Every Lie group G G carries a distinguished algebra of particularly well-behaved real-analytic mappings: The entire functions ℰ ⁡ ( G ) \mathscr{E}(G) . They were introduced for the purposes of strict deformation quantization. This paper establishes a one-to-one correspondence between entire functions and holomorphic mappings ℋ ⁡ ( G ​ C ) \mathscr{H}(G{C}) on the universal complexification G ​ C G{C} of G G as Fréchet algebras. Methodically, this is achieved by porting aspects of classical complex analysis into a left-invariant guise and by studying the geometry of G ​ C G{C} . As a byproduct, we obtain a strict deformation quantization of the holomorphic cotangent bundle of any universal complexification.

Definition 1.4
(Entire functions,
[
3
, Def. 4.10]
).

Let
G
be a connected Lie group.

i.)

A real-analytic function
\phi\in\mathscr{C}^{\omega}(G)
is called entire
if its Lie-Taylor majorant

\operatorname{F}_{\phi}(\mathrm{e},\,\cdot\,)\colon\ch@airxmathbbm{C}\longrightarrow\ch@airxmathbbm{C}

(1.27)

at the group unit is holomorphic.

ii.)

We denote the set of all entire functions on
G
by

\mathscr{E}(G)\coloneqq\bigl\{\phi\in\mathscr{C}^{\omega}(G)\colon\operatorname{F}_{\phi}\in\mathscr{H}(\ch@airxmathbbm{C})\bigr\}.

(1.28)

iii.)

For
r\geq 0
, we define a family of seminorms on
\mathscr{E}(G)
by

\mathrm{q}_{r}(\phi)\coloneqq\operatorname{F}_{\phi}(r)

(1.29)

and endow
\mathscr{E}_{0}(G)
with the corresponding locally convex topology.

The advantage of working with
\operatorname{F}_{\phi}
instead of with the seminorms
(

\operatorname{F}_{\phi}
is a holomorphic function of
one complex variable. This simple shift of perspective allows for the utilization of
many useful complex analytic techniques, such as the Cauchy estimates and
Montel’s Theorem. A comprehensive discussion of
\mathscr{E}(G)
based on this flexible
point of view can be found in
[
3

Sec. 4.3]

crucial for the statement of our main results and also enters into their proofs.

Proposition 1.5
(Functoriality of
\mathscr{E}

[
3
, Prop. 4.21]
).

Let
\Phi\colon G\longrightarrow H
be a Lie group morphism between
connected Lie groups
G
and
H
. Then the pullback with
\Phi
constitutes a
morphism of Fréchet algebras

\Phi^{*}\colon\mathscr{E}(H)\longrightarrow\mathscr{E}(G).

(1.30)

As already noted, the purpose of this paper is to establish a one-to-one
correspondence between entire functions on
G
and holomorphic ones on
G{C}

for connected Lie groups. We split this into the problems of extension and restriction.
The latter simply poses the question, whether
\mathscr{H}(G)\subseteq\mathscr{E}(G)
for a complex Lie group

1

\eta^{*}\bigl(\mathscr{H}(G{C})\bigr)\subseteq\mathscr{E}(G)

for any real Lie group

. We provide the following positive resolution to the
restriction problem. Recall that an embedding
\iota\colon V\longrightarrow W
of
locally convex spaces is a linear injection, which constitutes a homeomorphism onto
its image.

Theorem 1.6
(Restriction).

Let
G
be a complex connected Lie group. Then

\mathscr{H}(G)\subseteq\mathscr{E}(G)

(1.32)

induces an embedding of Fréchet algebras, where we endow
\mathscr{H}(G)

with the topology of locally uniform convergence and products are taken
pointwisely.

The proof is based on a Lie-theoretic version of the Cauchy estimates. We derive two
versions, one adapted to matrix Lie groups and operator norms, the other based on
left-invariant Riemannian geometry. The resulting estimates are interesting in their
own rights and facilitate an application from strict deformation quantization within
Theorem
3
.

Conversely, we consider extensions of entire functions
\phi
on
G

\Phi

Theorem 1.7

Let

\Phi\in\mathscr{H}(G{C})\qquad\textrm{such that}\qquad\eta^{*}\Phi=\phi.

\mathscr{L}(\xi)\Phi\Big|_{\eta(g)}=\mathscr{L}(\xi)\phi\Big|_{g}\qquad\textrm{for all }g\in G,\xi\in\operatorname{\mathrm{U}}^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g}{C}).

and thus
\eta^{*}\Phi\in\mathscr{E}(G)
by the functoriality
of
\mathscr{E}
from Proposition
1
.
Consequently, Theorem
1
asserts the existence of a unique
holomorphic mapping
\Psi\in\mathscr{H}(G{C})
such that

\eta^{*}\Psi=\eta^{*}\Phi.

(1.38)

But, trivially,
\Phi
also has these properties, which implies
\Psi=\Phi
by
uniqueness. Conversely, let
\phi\in\mathscr{E}(G)
be given. By
Theorem
1
, there exists a unique holomorphic function

\Phi\in\mathscr{H}(G{C})\qquad\textrm{such that}\qquad\eta^{*}\Phi=\phi.

(1.39)

That is to say, the restriction of
\Phi
is just
\phi
again. Consequently,
(
1.37
) is well defined and bijective.
Combining the continuity of the inclusion
\mathscr{H}(G{C})\subseteq\mathscr{E}(G{C})
from Theorem
1
with the continuity of

\eta^{*}\colon\mathscr{E}(G{C})\longrightarrow\mathscr{E}(G)

(1.40)

from Proposition
1
now establishes the continuity
of (
1.37
). Finally, as we are dealing with Fréchet
spaces, the Open Mapping Theorem
[
12
, Theorem 4.35]
implies
that the continuity of the inverse is automatic. Alternatively, an explicit estimate
may be derived by combining (
1.35
), which asserts that
extension preserves Lie-Taylor data, with the fact that the subspace topology of

\mathscr{H}(G{C})
induced by
\mathscr{E}(G{C})
is the

\mathscr{H}
-topology.

\ch@irxboxempty

We have thus demystified the structure of
\mathscr{E}(G)
.

Proposition 1.10
(Left-invariant Riemannian Geometry).

Let
G
be a connected Lie group and
\ch@airxmathbbm{g}_{\mathrm{e}}\in\mathfrak{g}\vee\mathfrak{g}

a non-degenerate symmetric two-form.

i.)

Left translation of
\ch@airxmathbbm{g}_{\mathrm{e}}
defines a left-invariant Riemannian
metric
\ch@airxmathbbm{g}
on
G
, which is explicitly given by

\ch@airxmathbbm{g}\Big|_{g}(v_{g},w_{g})\coloneqq\ell^{*}_{g^{-1}}\ch@airxmathbbm{g}_{\mathrm{e}}(v_{g},w_{p})=\ch@airxmathbbm{g}_{\mathrm{e}}\bigl(T_{\mathrm{e}}\ell_{g}^{-1}v_{g},T_{\mathrm{e}}\ell_{g}^{-1}w_{g}\bigr)

(1.44)

for all
g\in G
and
v_{g},w_{g}\in T_{g}G
, where

T_{\mathrm{e}}\ell_{g}\colon\mathfrak{g}=T_{\mathrm{e}}G\longrightarrow T_{g}G

(1.45)

denotes the tangent map of
\ell_{g}
.

ii.)

The corresponding Riemannian length
\varrho
on
G
is left-invariant, i.e.
fulfils

\varrho\bigl(hg_{1},hg_{2}\bigr)=\varrho\bigl(\ell_{h}g_{1},\ell_{h}g_{2}\bigr)=\varrho(g_{1},g_{2})\qquad\textrm{for all }g_{1},g_{2},h\in G.

(1.46)

iii.)

The metric topology of
(G,\varrho)
coincides with the original topology of

G
.

iv.)

We have

\varrho\bigl(\mathrm{e},g_{1}\cdots g_{n}\bigr)\leq\varrho(\mathrm{e},g_{1})+\cdots+\varrho(\mathrm{e},g_{n})\qquad\textrm{for all }g_{1},\ldots,g_{n}\in G.

(1.47)

v.)

Let
\lVert\,\cdot\,\rVert
be the norm induced by
\ch@airxmathbbm{g}
. Then

\varrho\bigl(\mathrm{e},\exp\xi\bigr)\leq\lVert\xi\rVert\qquad\textrm{for all }\xi\in\mathfrak{g}.

(1.48)

Proof
:

A discussion of left-translations of vector fields can be found in

[
6
, Sec. 9.1.2]
and the invariance is readily dualized and
generalized to arbitrary tensor fields. Taking another look at
(
1.44
), the symmetry of
\ch@airxmathbbm{g}
is clear and
non-degeneracy follows from the injectivity of
T_{\mathrm{e}}\ell_{g}^{-1}
. This establishes

i.)
. For the second part, recall that the Riemannian length of
a smooth curve
\gamma\colon[0,1]\longrightarrow G
is given by

L(\gamma)=\int_{0}^{1}\ch@airxmathbbm{g}\Big|_{\gamma(t)}\bigl(\gamma^{\prime}(t),\gamma^{\prime}(t)\bigr)^{1/2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t.

(1.49)

Let now
g\in G
and consider the translated curve
\ell_{g}\gamma
given by

\ell_{g}\gamma(t)\coloneqq g\cdot\gamma(t)
for all
t\in[0,1]
. Then

\bigl(\ell_{g}\gamma\bigr)^{\prime}(t)=T_{\gamma(t)}\ell_{g}\gamma^{\prime}(t)\qquad\textrm{for all }t\in[0,1],

(1.50)

as

\bigl(\ell_{g}\gamma\bigr)^{\prime}(t)\phi=\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s}\phi\bigl(g\gamma(t+s)\bigr)\Big|_{s=0}=\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s}\ell_{g}^{*}\phi\bigl(\gamma(t+s)\bigr)\Big|_{s=0}=\bigl(T_{\gamma(t)}\ell_{g}\gamma^{\prime}(t)\bigr)\phi

(1.51)

for all smooth germs
\phi\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}_{g\gamma(t)}(G)
at
g\gamma(t)
. The
left-invariance of
\ch@airxmathbbm{g}
thus yields

\displaystyle L(\ell_{g}\gamma)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\ch@airxmathbbm{g}\Big|_{\gamma(t)}\bigl(T_{\gamma(t)}\ell_{g}\gamma^{\prime}(t),T_{\gamma(t)}\ell_{g}\gamma^{\prime}(t)\bigr)^{1/2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t

(1.52)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\ell_{g}^{*}\ch@airxmathbbm{g}\Big|_{\gamma(t)}\bigl(\gamma^{\prime}(t),\gamma^{\prime}(t)\bigr)^{1/2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t

(1.53)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\ch@airxmathbbm{g}\Big|_{\gamma(t)}\bigl(\gamma^{\prime}(t),\gamma^{\prime}(t)\bigr)^{1/2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t

(1.54)

\displaystyle=L(\gamma).

(1.55)

Recall now that the Riemannian distance is given by

\varrho(g_{1},g_{2})=\inf_{\gamma\in\Gamma(g_{1},g_{2})}L(\gamma)\qquad\textrm{for all }g_{1},g_{2}\in G,

(1.56)

where
\Gamma(g,h)
denotes the set of all smooth curves connecting
g
with

h
. As the passage from
\gamma
to
\ell_{g}\gamma
constitutes a bijection
between
\Gamma(g_{1},g_{2})
and
\Gamma(gg_{1},gg_{2})
, this
establishes
ii.)
.
Part
iii.)
is true on any Riemannian manifold
by
[
10
, Theorem 13.29]
. We prove

iv.)
by means of a simple induction. For

n=1
, there is nothing to be shown, and assuming the statement for
n-1

factors yields

\varrho\bigl(\mathrm{e},g_{1}\cdots g_{n}\bigr)=\varrho\bigl(g_{1}^{-1},g_{2}\cdots g_{n}\bigr)\leq\varrho\bigl(g_{1}^{-1},\mathrm{e}\bigr)+\varrho\bigl(\mathrm{e},g_{2}\cdots g_{n}\bigr)\leq\varrho(\mathrm{e},g_{1})+\cdots+\varrho(\mathrm{e},g_{n}),

(1.57)

where we have invoked (
1.46
) several times and
used the triangle inequality. Finally, note that
v.)

may be interpreted as the Lipschitz continuity of

\exp\colon(K,\lVert\,\cdot\,\rVert)\longrightarrow(G,\varrho)

(1.58)

evaluated at the pair
(0,\xi)
. Fix
\xi\in K
and consider the curve

\gamma\colon[0,1]\longrightarrow G,\quad\gamma(t)\coloneqq\exp(t\xi).

(1.59)

As our curve
\gamma
connects
\mathrm{e}
with
\exp\xi
, we get

\displaystyle\varrho\bigl(\mathrm{e},\exp\xi\bigr)

\displaystyle\leq L(\gamma)

(1.60)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\ch@airxmathbbm{g}\Big|_{\exp(t\xi)}\bigl(T_{\mathrm{e}}\ell_{\exp(t\xi)}\xi,T_{\mathrm{e}}\ell_{\exp(t\xi)}\xi\bigr)^{1/2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t

(1.61)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\bigl(\ell_{\exp(t\xi)}^{*}\ch@airxmathbbm{g}\bigr)\Big|_{\mathrm{e}}(\xi,\xi)^{1/2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t

(1.62)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\ch@airxmathbbm{g}\Big|_{\mathrm{e}}(\xi,\xi)^{1/2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t

(1.63)

\displaystyle=\ch@airxmathbbm{g}_{\mathrm{e}}(\xi,\xi)^{1/2}

(1.64)

\displaystyle=\lVert\xi\rVert

(1.65)

by virtue of the infimum in (
1.56
),
(
1.49
) and the left-invariance of
\ch@airxmathbbm{g}
.

\ch@irxboxempty

If
G
is compact, then one can average the pullbacks
r_{g}^{*}\ch@airxmathbbm{g}
by the
right multiplications
r_{g}
with
g\in G
over the group to obtain a bi-invariant
metric. In this case, the Riemannian geodesics are precisely given by
t\mapsto g\exp(t\xi)
for
g\in G
and
\xi\in\mathfrak{g}
by
[
15
, Exercise 15.5.]
. Our
proof shows that (
1.48
) then becomes an equality.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
G
be a connected Lie group with universal complexification

(G{C},\eta)
.

i.)

The image

\eta(G)\subseteq G{C}

(2.5)

is a closed real Lie subgroup when endowed with the subspace topology.

ii.)

The embedding
\iota\colon\eta(G)\hookrightarrow G{C}
constitutes a
universal complexification.

iii.)

Let
d\coloneqq\dim{R}(\eta(G))
. Then every basis

\bigl(\mathit{e}_{1},\ldots,\mathit{e}_{d}\bigr)\subseteq\operatorname{Lie}\bigl(\eta(G)\bigr)

(2.6)

induces a basis of
\operatorname{Lie}(G{C})
via
(\mathit{e}_{1},\ldots,\mathit{e}_{d},\mathrm{i}\mathit{e}_{1},\ldots,\mathrm{i}\mathit{e}_{d})
. In particular,

\dim{C}(G{C})=\dim{R}\bigl(\eta(G)\bigr).

(2.7)

iv.)

If
\eta
is locally injective, then

\operatorname{Lie}(G{C})\cong\mathfrak{g}{C}.

(2.8)

Proof
:

Invoking
[
6
, Theorem 15.1.4, (iv)]
, we find a
antiholomorphic involutive group morphism

\sigma\colon G{C}\longrightarrow G{C}\qquad\textrm{such that}\qquad\eta(G)=\bigl\{g\in G{C}\colon\sigma(g)=g\bigr\}_{0},

(2.9)

where the subscript denotes passing to the connected component of the group
unit. By continuity of
\sigma
, its fixed point group is closed within
G{C}
.
As connected components of closed sets are themselves closed, this establishes
the closedness of
\eta(G)
within
G{C}
. As images of group
morphisms are subgroups, this in turn implies that
\eta(G)
is a real Lie group
itself by the Closed Subgroup Theorem
[
6
, Thm. 9.3.7]
. Here
we view the ambient group
G{C}
as a real Lie group. In particular,

\eta(G)
constitutes a submanifold of
G{C}
with exponential charts as
submanifold charts. In order to prove
ii.)
, we
check the universal property directly. To this end, let
\Phi\colon\eta(G)\longrightarrow H
be a Lie group morphism into a complex Lie group
H
. Then

\Psi\coloneqq\Phi\circ\eta\colon G\longrightarrow H

(2.10)

is a Lie group morphism from
G
into a complex Lie group. Invoking the universal
property of
(G{C},\iota\circ\eta)
, there thus exists a unique holomorphic
group morphism

\Psi{C}\colon G{C}\longrightarrow H\qquad\textrm{with}\qquad\Psi{C}\circ\iota\circ\eta=\Psi=\Phi\circ\eta.

(2.11)

By surjectivity of
\eta\colon G\longrightarrow\eta(G)
, this is equivalent to

\Psi{C}\circ\iota=\Phi
. That is to say,
\Psi{C}
is the unique holomorphic
mapping complexifying
\Phi
.

Next, we prove
iii.)
, where we write
H\coloneqq\eta(G)
to declutter the notation.
Translating (
2.4
) to
H

yields the commutative square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{H}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{H}{C}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H{C}\cong G{C}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{\iota}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota}

(2.12)

in view of
ii.)
. Thus, the composition
\pi\circ\widetilde{\iota}
is locally injective by local injectivity of
p
and injectivity of

\iota
. Hence, the tangent map

T_{\mathrm{e}}(\pi\circ\widetilde{\iota})\colon\operatorname{Lie}(\widetilde{H})\longrightarrow\operatorname{Lie}(G{C})

(2.13)

is an injective Lie algebra morphism. But
\operatorname{Lie}(\widetilde{H})=\operatorname{Lie}(H)
,
completing the proof of
iii.)
. Finally,
the assumed local injectivity on the one hand implies injectivity of the tangent
map

T_{\mathrm{e}}\eta\colon\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\operatorname{Lie}(H).

(2.14)

In particular,
\dim\mathfrak{g}\leq\dim\operatorname{Lie}(H)
. On the other hand,
combining (
2.3
) with
(
2.7
) we know that

\dim{R}(G)\geq\dim{C}(G{C})=\dim{R}(H).

(2.15)

Thus,
\dim\mathfrak{g}=\dim\operatorname{Lie}(H)
and thus the linear mapping
T_{\mathrm{e}}\eta
constitutes a bijection.
Hence,
iv.)

follows from
iii.)
.

\ch@irxboxempty

Using similar techniques and being a bit more careful, one can establish that

\pi\colon\widetilde{G}{C}\longrightarrow G{C}

(2.16)

is a universal covering projection whenever
\widetilde{\eta}\colon\widetilde{G}\longrightarrow\widetilde{G}{C}
is injective. That is to say, in view of
(
2.4
), the passage to the
universal covering group commutes with complexification. In particular,

\pi_{1}(G{C})\cong\pi_{1}(G)

(2.17)

in this case. Dropping the assumption of injectivity, this equality may reduce to
the inclusion

\pi_{1}(G{C})\subseteq\pi_{1}(G).

(2.18)

Either way, the upshot is that there are no new topological obstructions to analytic
continuation to
G{C}
beyond what is already present within
G
. While this
provides heuristical motivation for Theorem
1
, our proof only
requires the following left-invariant version of the identity principle.

Corollary 2.3
(Identity Principle).

Let
G
be a connected Lie group,
U\subseteq G{C}
a domain with
U\cap\eta(G)\neq\emptyset
and
\phi\in\mathscr{H}(U,M)
be a holomorphic map
with values in a complex manifold
M
. If
\phi\big|_{U\cap\eta(G)}
is constant,
then so is
\phi
.

Proof
:

Let
(\mathit{e}_{1},\ldots,\mathit{e}_{d})
be a basis of
\operatorname{Lie}(\eta(G))
with the
induced basis

(\mathit{e}_{1},\ldots,\mathit{e}_{d},\mathrm{i}\mathit{e}_{1},\ldots,\mathrm{i}\mathit{e}_{d})

(2.19)

of
\operatorname{Lie}(G{C})
from Lemma
2
,

iii.)
. By holomorphicity of
\phi
, we get the
left-invariant Cauchy-Riemann equations

\mathscr{L}(\mathrm{i}\mathit{e}_{n})\phi=\mathrm{i}\cdot\mathscr{L}(\mathit{e}_{n})\phi\qquad\textrm{for }n=1,\ldots,d.

(2.20)

Indeed, as the Lie exponential is the flow of the left-invariant vector field, we have

\mathscr{L}(\mathit{e}_{n})\phi\Big|_{g}=\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}\phi\bigl(g\exp(t\mathit{e}_{n})\bigr)\Big|_{t=0}

(2.21)

for all
g\in U
and
n=1,\ldots,d
. This reveals (
2.20
) as
the Cauchy-Riemann equations for the holomorphic mapping

\phi\circ\exp\colon\operatorname{Lie}(G{C})=\operatorname{Lie}\bigl(\eta(G)\bigr){C}\longrightarrow\ch@airxmathbbm{C}.

(2.22)

Now, by assumption,
\mathscr{L}(e_{n})\phi\big|_{U\cap\eta(G)}\equiv 0
and
(
2.20
) implies

\mathscr{L}(\mathrm{i}\mathit{e}_{n})\phi\Big|_{U\cap\eta(G)}\equiv 0\qquad\textrm{for }n=1,\ldots,d.

(2.23)

Invoking the Lie-Taylor formula (
1.22
), this means that
\phi
is
constant in an open neighbourhood of
U\cap\eta(G)
. By the usual identity
principle, this completes the proof.

\ch@irxboxempty

the additional assumption of local injectivity of
\eta

2

\ch@airxmathbbm{C}

(T_{\mathrm{e}}\eta)^{-1}\colon\operatorname{Lie}(G{C})\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}{C}.

(2.24)

Let

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
G
be a connected Lie group,
U\subseteq G
a domain and
\phi\in\mathscr{C}^{\omega}(U)
a real-analytic such that the Lie-Taylor majorant

\operatorname{F}_{\phi}(g_{0},\,\cdot\,)
is entire for some
g_{0}\in U
. Then the
Lie-Taylor majorant
\operatorname{F}(g,\,\cdot\,)
is entire for all
g\in U
.

The analogous statement for
\phi\in\mathscr{E}(G)
was already established in

[
3
, Theorem 4.17,
iii.)
]
and our methods are
essentially identical. Working with globally defined functions, one may recast
Lemma
2

G

\mathscr{E}(G)

Proof
(Of Lemma
2
):

Fix a basis
(\mathit{e}_{1},\ldots,\mathit{e}_{d})
of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
with
corresponding supremum norm
\lVert\,\cdot\,\rVert_{\infty}
. As a first step, we
establish the entirety of
\operatorname{F}_{\phi}(g_{0}\exp\xi,\,\cdot\,)
for sufficiently

\xi\in\mathfrak{g}
. Recall that the Lie-Taylor formula (
1.22
)
arises from the usual Taylor formula within an exponential chart, see again the
proof of Proposition
1

means that there exists some open neighbourhood
U_{0}\subseteq G
of
g_{0}

such that

\bigl(\mathscr{L}(\chi)\phi\bigr)(g_{0}\exp\xi)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{k}=1}^{d}\bigl(\mathscr{L}(\mathit{e}_{m_{1}}\cdots\mathit{e}_{m_{k}}\chi)\phi\bigr)(g_{0})\cdot\xi^{m_{1}}\cdots\xi^{m_{k}}

(2.33)

for all
\xi\in U_{0}
and
\chi\in\operatorname{\mathrm{U}}^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g}{C})
. Unwrapping
the definition of the Lie-Taylor majorant thus leads to the estimate

\displaystyle\operatorname{F}_{\phi}\bigl(g_{0}\exp\xi,\lvert z\rvert\bigr)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\lvert z\rvert^{n}}{n!}\sum_{\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{n}=1}^{d}\Big\lvert\bigl(\mathscr{L}(\mathit{e}_{\ell_{1}}\cdots\mathit{e}_{\ell_{n}})\phi\bigr)(g_{0}\exp\xi)\Big\rvert

(2.34)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n,k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\lvert z\rvert^{n}}{n!\cdot k!}\sum_{\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{n}=1}^{d}\sum_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{k}=1}^{d}\lVert\xi\rVert_{\infty}^{k}\cdot\Big\lvert\bigl(\mathscr{L}(\mathit{e}_{m_{1}}\cdots\mathit{e}_{m_{k}}\mathit{e}_{\ell_{1}}\cdots\mathit{e}_{\ell_{n}})\phi\bigr)(g_{0})\Big\rvert

\displaystyle=\sum_{n,k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\lvert z\rvert^{n}\cdot\lVert\xi\rVert_{\infty}^{k}}{n!\cdot k!}\sum_{\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{n+k}=1}^{d}\Big\lvert\bigl(\mathscr{L}(\mathit{e}_{\ell_{1}}\cdots\mathit{e}_{\ell_{n+k}})\phi\bigr)(g_{0})\Big\rvert

(2.36)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\lVert\xi\rVert_{\infty}^{k}}{k!}\cdot\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(n+k)!}{n!}\cdot c_{n+k}(\phi,g_{0})\cdot\lvert z\rvert^{n}

(2.37)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\lVert\xi\rVert_{\infty}^{k}}{k!}\cdot\operatorname{F}_{\phi}^{(k)}\bigl(g_{0},\lvert z\rvert\bigr)

(2.38)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{F}_{\phi}\bigl(g_{0},\lvert z\rvert+\lVert\xi\rVert_{\infty}\bigr)

(2.39)

for all
\xi\in U_{0}
and
z\in\ch@airxmathbbm{C}
, where we have used the power series
expansions of the entire function
\operatorname{F}_{\phi}(g_{0},\,\cdot\,)\in\mathscr{H}(\ch@airxmathbbm{C})
and its derivatives
\operatorname{F}_{\phi}^{(k)}(g_{0},\,\cdot\,)
. We
have thus established that the Taylor series of
\operatorname{F}_{\phi}(g_{0}\exp\xi)

converges absolutely and locally uniformly on
\ch@airxmathbbm{C}
. That is to say, it constitutes an
entire function itself. Let now
g\in U
. As
U_{0}
is a zero neighbourhood, its

G
. Thus, we find Lie
algebra elements
\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n}\in U_{0}
with

g_{0}^{-1}g=\exp(\xi_{1})\cdots\exp(\xi_{n}).

(2.40)

That is to say,
g=g_{0}\exp(\xi_{1})\cdots\exp(\xi_{n})
. Inductive application of
what we have shown thus yields the entirety first of
\operatorname{F}_{\phi}(g_{0}\exp\xi_{1})
, then of
\operatorname{F}_{\phi}(g_{0}\exp\xi_{1}\exp\xi_{2})
and so on, completing
the proof.

\ch@irxboxempty

The inequality we have just derived may be interpreted as a continuity estimate in
the following manner.

Corollary 2.6
.

Let
G
be a connected Lie group. Then

\mathscr{E}(G)\ni\phi\quad\mapsto\quad\operatorname{F}_{\phi}(g,r)\in[0,\infty)

(2.41)

is a continuous seminorm for all
g\in G
and
r\geq 0
.

Moreover, combining Lemma
2
with
(
2.29
), the following is
now immediate.

Corollary 2.7
.

Let
G
be a connected Lie group and consider an entire function
\phi\in\mathscr{E}(G)
with holomorphic shadow
\phi_{0}\in\mathscr{H}(U)
. Then the
Lie-Taylor majorant
\operatorname{F}_{\phi_{0}}(g,\,\cdot\,)
is entire for all
g\in U
.

Preparing the final ingredient for our local extension result, we use the group
structure and a touch of Riemannian geometry to define an analogue of Steiner
chains on
G
, i.e. chains of circles such that each circle contains the center of its
successor.

Lemma 2.8
(Steiner Chains).

Let
G
be a connected Lie group,
\gamma\colon[0,1]\longrightarrow G
a
path from
g_{0}
to
g_{1}
and
U\subseteq G
an open neighbourhood of
the group unit. Then there are

0=t_{0}<t_{1}<t_{2}<\cdots<t_{k}=1

(2.42)

with

\gamma(t_{\ell+1})\in\gamma(t_{\ell})U\qquad\textrm{for}\quad\ell=1,\ldots,k-1

(2.43)

and

\gamma\bigl([0,1]\bigr)\subseteq\bigcup_{\ell=0}^{k}\gamma(t_{\ell})U.

(2.44)

Proof
:

Let
\varrho
be a left-invariant metric on
G
as discussed in
Proposition
1
. By

iii.)
, the metric topology reproduces the
topology of
G
. Thus, there exists a closed metric ball

\mathrm{B}_{r}(\mathrm{e})^{\mathrm{cl}}\subseteq U
. Moreover, the metric length of
\gamma
is finite,
and thus we may divide its trace into
k
subarcs
\gamma\big|_{[t_{\ell},t_{\ell+1}]}

of length at most
r
and with times adhering to (
2.42
). The
subdividing points fulfil

\varrho\bigl(\gamma(t_{\ell+1}),\gamma(t_{\ell})\bigr)\leq r\qquad\textrm{for }\ell=0,\ldots,k-1.

(2.45)

By left-invariance, we moreover have

\gamma(t_{\ell})\mathrm{B}_{r}(\mathrm{e})^{\mathrm{cl}}=\mathrm{B}_{r}\bigl(\gamma(t_{\ell})\bigr)\qquad\textrm{for }\ell=0,\ldots,k.

(2.46)

Combining both observations proves (
2.43
) and by virtue of

\bigcup_{\ell=0}^{k}\gamma(t_{\ell})U\supseteq\bigcup_{\ell=0}^{k}\mathrm{B}_{r}\bigl(\gamma(t_{\ell})\bigr)^{\mathrm{cl}}\supseteq\bigcup_{\ell=0}^{k}\gamma\big|_{[t_{\ell},t_{\ell+1}]}=\gamma\bigl([0,1]\bigr),

(2.47)

we have also achieved (
2.44
). Here, we have used that each
subarc is contained in the closed ball with radius equal to its length.

\ch@irxboxempty

With the help of Steiner chains, we may now continue the holomorphic shadow

\phi_{0}
analytically along any path in
G{C}
.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
G
be a connected Lie group and let
\phi\in\mathscr{E}(G)
. Then its
holomorphic shadow
\phi_{0}
may be analytically continued along any path

G{C}
starting at the group unit.

Proof
:

Let
\gamma\colon[0,1]\longrightarrow G{C}
be a path from
\mathrm{e}
to
g\in G{C}
and
\phi_{0}\in\mathscr{H}(U)
be a holomorphic shadow of
\phi

defined on a neighbourhood
U
of the group unit. Moreover, let
V\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
be a zero neighbourhood such that
\exp\colon V\longrightarrow\exp V
constitutes a diffeomorphism. Without loss of generality, we may assume

\exp V=U
. Invoking Lemma
2
, we get

0=t_{0}<t_{1}<\cdots<t_{k}=1

(2.48)

such that

\gamma\bigl([0,1]\bigr)\subseteq\bigcup_{\ell=0}^{k}\gamma(t_{\ell})U\qquad\textrm{and}\qquad\gamma(t_{\ell+1})\in\gamma(t_{\ell})U\qquad\textrm{for }\ell=1,\ldots,k-1.

(2.49)

By Corollary
2
, we know that the Lie-Taylor majorant at

\gamma(t_{1})\in\gamma(t_{0})U=\exp V
is entire. Hence, using that

\exp\colon V\longrightarrow\exp V
is bijective, we may define a holomorphic
mapping by

\phi_{1}\colon\gamma(t_{1})\exp V\longrightarrow\ch@airxmathbbm{C},\quad\phi_{1}\bigl(\gamma(t_{1})\exp\xi\bigr)\coloneqq\operatorname{T}_{\phi_{0}}\bigl(\gamma(t_{1}),\underline{\xi}\bigr).

(2.50)

Note that
\exp V\cap\gamma(t_{1})\exp V\neq\emptyset
, as
\gamma(t_{1})
is
within by construction. Hence, the intersection even contains an open connected
neighbourhood of
\gamma(t_{1})
, on which
\phi_{0}
and
\phi_{1}

coincide by virtue of the Lie-Taylor formula (
1.22
). Invoking the
identity principle, this implies
\phi_{0}=\phi_{1}
on

\bigl(U\cap\gamma(t_{1})U\bigr)_{0}.

(2.51)

As this intersection contains
\gamma(t_{1})
by construction, the Lie-Taylor
majorants fulfil

\operatorname{F}_{\phi_{1}}\bigl(\gamma(t_{1}),\,\cdot\,\bigr)=\operatorname{F}_{\phi_{0}}\bigl(\gamma(t_{1}),\,\cdot\,\bigr)\in\mathscr{H}(\ch@airxmathbbm{C}),

(2.52)

which returns us to the original assumptions, now for
\phi_{1}
. We may thus
iterate the procedure and define

\phi_{2}(\exp\xi)\coloneqq\operatorname{T}_{\phi_{1}}\bigl(\gamma(t_{2}),\underline{\xi}\bigr)

(2.53)

and so on. This yields the desired analytic continuation of
\phi_{0}
along

\gamma
.

\ch@irxboxempty

Investing the Monodromy Theorem, as it can be found in the textbook

[
9
, Sec. 10.3.4]
, yields the existence of a globally defined
holomorphic extensions under the additional assumption that
G{C}
is simply
connected.

Corollary 2.10
.

Let
G
be a connected Lie group such that its universal complexification
G{C}

is simply connected and
\phi\in\mathscr{E}(G)
. Then there exists a unique
holomorphic function
\Phi\in\mathscr{H}(G{C})
such that

\Phi\circ\eta=\phi.

(2.54)

The idea is now that this resolves the extension problem for the universal covering
group
\widetilde{G}
, as its universal complexification is always simply connected by
Proposition
2
,

i.)
. We may now put the discussion after
Theorem
1
into practice to produce its proof.

Proof
(Of Theorem
1
):

Let
\phi\in\mathscr{E}(G)
and write
p\colon\widetilde{G}\longrightarrow G
for the
universal covering projection. By Proposition
1
, the
pullback
\psi\coloneqq p^{*}\phi
is then an entire function on
\widetilde{G}
. As
the universal covering group is simply connected by its very definition, we may
invoke Corollary
2
to obtain a unique
holomorphic mapping
\Psi\in\mathscr{H}(\widetilde{G}{C})
such that

\Psi\circ\widetilde{\eta}=\psi,

(2.55)

where
\widetilde{\eta}\colon\widetilde{G}\longrightarrow\widetilde{G}{C}
denotes the
universal complexification morphism for
\widetilde{G}
. We show that
\Psi
is
invariant under the action of
\widetilde{\eta}(\pi_{1}(G))
. To this end, we first note that
as a pullback along the covering projection, the mapping
\psi
is

\pi_{1}(G)
-periodic, as
\pi_{1}(G)=\ker p
. Fix now
h\in\pi_{1}(G)\subseteq\widetilde{G}
and consider the auxiliary function

\Psi_{h}\colon\widetilde{G}{C}\longrightarrow\ch@airxmathbbm{C},\quad\Psi_{h}(g)\coloneqq\Psi\bigl(g\widetilde{\eta}(h)\bigr).

(2.56)

Notice

\Psi_{h}\bigl(\widetilde{\eta}(g)\bigr)=\Psi\bigl(\widetilde{\eta}(gh)\bigr)=\psi(gh)=\psi(g)=\Psi\bigl(\widetilde{\eta}(g)\bigr)\qquad\textrm{for all }g\in\widetilde{G}.

(2.57)

This implies
\Psi_{h}=\Psi
by the identity principle from
Corollary
2
. Varying
h\in\pi_{1}(G)
, we get that
\Psi

is indeed invariant under the action of
\widetilde{\eta}(\pi_{1}(G))
. Consequently, it
factors through the quotient projection
\pi\colon\widetilde{G}{C}\longrightarrow G{C}
, see again Proposition
2
,

ii.)
, yielding a function
\Phi\in\mathscr{H}(G{C})
. Recall now that
\eta\circ p=\pi\circ\widetilde{\eta}
by virtue
of (
2.2
) and thus

\Phi\circ\eta\circ p=\Phi\circ\pi\circ\widetilde{\eta}=\Psi\circ\widetilde{\eta}=\psi=\phi\circ p.

Invoking the local bijectivity of
p
, this implies
\Phi\circ\eta=\phi
as
desired. Moreover, the uniqueness of
\Phi
is clear by
Corollary
2
. Finally,
(
2.29
) is just (
1.35
)
again.

Remark 2.11
(Penney’s Theorem).

Let
G
be a connected, solvable and simply connected Lie group. The paper

[
13
]
deals with holomorphic extensions of Lie group
representations

\pi\colon G\longrightarrow\operatorname{GL}(V)

(2.58)

on locally convex spaces

. Viewing the Lie-Taylor formula as the integration of

\mathscr{L}
from (

) to the group representation given by right

given

\mathit{e}_{j_{1}}\cdots\mathit{e}_{j_{n}}=\mathit{e}_{k_{1}}\cdots\mathit{e}_{k_{n}}+\textrm{ lower order terms}

(2.59)

with

within the universal enveloping

. More on this representation theoretic

[

, Rmk. 4.16]

[

3
Restriction: Cauchy Estimates

In this section, we always work with a complex Lie group
G
. Viewing it as a real Lie
group with real Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, we may define
\mathscr{E}(G)
as before. Taking
another look at Theorem
1
, our goal is to establish the embedding

\mathscr{H}(G)\subseteq\mathscr{E}(G),

(3.1)

which means we have to estimate the Lie-Taylor majorants by means of supremum
norms

\lVert\phi\rVert_{K}\coloneqq\max_{g\in K}\big\lvert\phi(z)\big\rvert

(3.2)

for compact subsets
K\subseteq G
, and vice versa. The latter is based on the
Lie-Taylor formula (
1.22
). Moreover, we achieve the former by deriving
a Lie-theoretic version of the Cauchy estimates, which bound each Lie-Taylor
coefficient individually, but ultimately in a uniform manner. We discuss two flavours
of essentially the same technique: one for operator norms and matrix Lie groups and
the other in full generality using the left-invariant Riemannian geometry we have
established within Proposition
1
.

Thus, assume for the moment that
G
is linear and choose a finite
dimensional faithful representation. As both
\mathscr{H}
and
\mathscr{E}
are
functors, this choice indeed does not constitute a loss of generality. Working with
matrices
M\in\mathrm{M}_{d}(\ch@airxmathbbm{C})
, we choose some auxiliary norm on
\ch@airxmathbbm{C}^{d}
and denote
the resulting operator norm simply by
\lVert M\rVert
. By our assumption, it makes
sense to speak both of operator norms of group elements and of Lie algebra
elements. That is to say, there is a direct way to compare sizes of infinitesimal and
exponentiated elements. As the Lie exponential is simply given by the exponential
series for matrix Lie groups, we get the intuitive estimate

\big\lVert\exp\xi\big\rVert\leq e^{\lVert\xi\rVert}\qquad\textrm{for all }\xi\in\mathfrak{g},

(3.3)

where
e
denotes Euler’s number. This may be thought of as a continuity estimate
for the Lie exponential much like (
1.48
). Keeping this
in mind, we arrive at the following first Lie-theoretic incarnation of the Cauchy
estimates.

Lemma 3.1
(Lie-theoretic Cauchy estimates I).

Let
G
be a complex connected matrix Lie group,
\phi\in\mathscr{H}(G)
,

K_{0}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
a compact neighbourhood of zero and
r>0
. Then,
writing

K_{r}\coloneqq\bigl\{g\in G\colon\lVert g\rVert\leq e^{r}\bigr\},

(3.4)

we have

\Big\lvert\bigl(\mathscr{L}(\xi_{1}\cdots\xi_{n})\phi\bigr)(g\exp\xi)\Big\rvert\leq\lVert\phi\rVert_{g\exp(K_{0})K_{r}}\cdot\frac{n^{n}}{r^{n}}

(3.5)

for all
g\in G
,
\xi\in K_{0}
,
n\in\ch@airxmathbbm{N}
and
\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n}\in\mathfrak{g}

with
\lVert\xi_{j}\rVert\leq 1
for
j=1,\ldots,n
.

Proof
:

Fix
n\in\ch@airxmathbbm{N}
and
\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n}\in\mathrm{B}_{1}(0)^{\mathrm{cl}}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
.
Evaluating (
1.15
) for
k=1
and
s=0
yields

\bigl(\mathscr{L}(\xi_{j})\phi\bigr)(g\exp\xi)=\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\phi\bigl(g\exp\xi\cdot\exp(z\xi_{j})\bigr)\Big|_{z=0}

(3.6)

for
j=1,\ldots,n
,
g\in G
and
\xi\in K_{0}
. As the Lie exponential induces
a holomorphic atlas, the composition

\phi\circ\ell_{g\exp(\xi)}\circ\exp\colon\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\ch@airxmathbbm{C}

(3.7)

is holomorphic. By induction, we get

\mathscr{L}(\xi_{1}\cdots\xi_{n})\phi(g\exp\xi)=\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{1}}\cdots\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{n}}\phi\bigl(g\exp\xi\cdot\exp(z^{n}\xi_{n})\cdots\exp(z^{1}\xi_{1})\bigr)\Big|_{z^{1}=\ldots=z^{n}=0},

where

\ch@airxmathbbm{C}^{n}\ni z\mapsto\phi\bigl(g\exp(\xi)\exp(z^{n}\xi_{n})\cdots\exp(z^{1}\xi_{1})\bigr)

(3.8)

is holomorphic. Notably, there are only first derivatives involved. Applying the
usual Cauchy estimates for first derivatives with all radii equal to
r/n
yields

\Big\lvert\bigl(\mathscr{L}(\xi_{1}\cdots\xi_{n})\phi\bigr)(g\exp\xi)\Big\rvert\leq\frac{n^{n}}{r^{n}}\max_{\lvert z^{1}\rvert=\cdots=\lvert z^{n}\rvert=r/n}\Big\lvert\phi\bigl(g\exp\xi\cdot\exp(z^{n}\xi_{n})\cdots\exp(z^{1}\xi_{1})\bigr)\Big\rvert.

(3.9)

By submultiplicativity of the operator norm and
(
3.3
), this implies

\displaystyle\big\lVert\exp(z^{n}\xi_{n})\cdots\exp(z^{1}\xi_{1})\big\rVert

\displaystyle\leq\big\lVert\exp(z^{n}\xi_{n})\big\rVert\cdots\big\lVert\exp(z^{1}\xi_{1})\big\rVert

\displaystyle\leq\exp\bigl(\lvert z^{n}\rvert\cdot\lVert\xi_{n}\rVert\bigr)\cdots\exp\bigl(\lvert z^{1}\rvert\cdot\lVert\xi_{1}\rVert\bigr)

\displaystyle=\exp\bigl(\lvert z^{n}\rvert\cdot\lVert\xi_{n}\rVert+\cdots+\lvert z^{n}\rvert\cdot\lVert\xi_{1}\rVert\bigr)

\displaystyle\leq\exp(r)

for
\lvert z^{1}\rvert=\cdots=\lvert z^{n}\rvert=r/n
. That is to say, we have

\exp(z^{n}\xi_{n})\cdots\exp(z^{1}\xi_{1})\in K_{r}\qquad\textrm{whenever}\quad\lvert z^{1}\rvert=\ldots=\lvert z^{n}\rvert=r/n.

(3.10)

Consequently, (
3.5
) follows from
(
3.9
) by varying
z
and
\xi
.

\ch@irxboxempty

Next, we replace the operator norms with the metric data induced by the choice of a
Riemannian metric
\ch@airxmathbbm{g}
on
\mathfrak{g}
, as we have discussed in
Proposition
1
. This yields the following estimate,
which is structurally identical to (
3.5
).

Lemma 3.2
(Lie-theoretic Cauchy estimates II).

Let
G
be a complex connected Lie group,
\phi\in\mathscr{H}(G)
and

\ch@airxmathbbm{g}\in\mathfrak{g}\vee\mathfrak{g}
be non-degenerate. We
endow
\mathfrak{g}
and
G
with the corresponding norm
\lVert\,\cdot\,\rVert

and length
\varrho
, respectively. Moreover, let
K_{0}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
be a
compact neighbourhood of zero and
r>0
. Then

\Big\lvert\bigl(\mathscr{L}(\xi_{1}\cdots\xi_{n})\phi\bigr)(g\exp\xi)\Big\rvert\leq\lVert\phi\rVert_{g\exp(K_{0})\mathrm{B}_{r}(\mathrm{e})^{\mathrm{cl}}}\cdot\frac{n^{n}}{r^{n}}

(3.11)

for all
g\in G
,
\xi\in K_{0}
,
n\in\ch@airxmathbbm{N}
and
\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n}\in\mathfrak{g}

with
\lVert\xi_{j}\rVert\leq 1
for
j=1,\ldots,n
.

Proof
:

Fix
g\in G
,
\xi\in K_{0}
and
\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n}\in\mathfrak{g}
. Taking another
look at the proof of Lemma
3
, we did not use that
G

was a matrix Lie group in the derivation of (
3.9
).
Combining (
1.47
) with
(
1.48
), we moreover have

\displaystyle\varrho\bigl(\mathrm{e},\exp(z^{1}\xi_{1})\cdots\exp(z^{n}\xi_{n})\bigr)

\displaystyle\leq\varrho(\mathrm{e},z^{1}\xi_{1})+\cdots+\varrho(\mathrm{e},z^{n}\xi_{n})

(3.12)

\displaystyle\leq\lvert z\rvert^{1}\cdot\lVert\xi_{1}\rVert+\cdots+\lvert z\rvert^{n}\cdot\lVert\xi_{n}\rVert

(3.13)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{r}{n}\cdot 1+\cdots+\frac{r}{n}\cdot 1

(3.14)

\displaystyle=r

(3.15)

for
\lvert z\rvert^{1}=\ldots=\lvert z\rvert^{n}=r/n
. That is to say,

\exp(z^{1}\xi_{1})\cdots\exp(z^{n}\xi_{n})\in\mathrm{B}_{r}(\mathrm{e})^{\mathrm{cl}}\qquad\textrm{whenever}\quad\lvert z^{1}\rvert=\ldots=\lvert z^{n}\rvert=r/n.

(3.16)

Varying
g
and
\xi
thus proves (
3.11
).

\ch@irxboxempty

Having established the Lie-theoretic Cauchy estimates, we may return to
Theorem
1
.

Proof
(Of Theorem
1
):

Let
\ch@airxmathbbm{g}\in\mathfrak{g}\vee\mathfrak{g}
be a non-degenerate two-form with
accompanying norm
\lVert\,\cdot\,\rVert
on
\mathfrak{g}
and distance
\varrho

on
G
. Consider
\phi\in\mathscr{H}(G)
. Then
\phi\in\mathscr{C}^{\omega}(G)
, which
means we only have to check the seminorm conditions. Let
(\mathit{e}_{1},\ldots,\mathit{e}_{d})\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
be a basis such that
\lVert\mathit{e}_{n}\rVert=1
for

n=1,\ldots,d
. Applying Lemma
3
with
constant
rd
, this yields

\mathrm{q}_{r}(\phi)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{r^{n}}{n!}\sum_{\alpha\in\ch@airxmathbbm{N}_{d}^{n}}\Big\lvert\bigl(\mathscr{L}(\alpha)\phi\bigr)(\mathrm{e})\Big\rvert\leq\lVert\phi\rVert_{\mathrm{B}_{r}(\mathrm{e})^{\mathrm{cl}}}\cdot\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{n!}{n^{n}}<\infty

(3.17)

for any
r>0
by virtue of
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sqrt[n]{n!}/n=e^{-1}
. This
moreover establishes the continuity of the
\mathscr{E}
-seminorms from
(
1.29
) with respect to the topology of locally uniform
convergence on
\mathscr{H}(G)
. Conversely, let
K\coloneqq\mathrm{B}_{1}(0)^{\mathrm{cl}}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
be the closed unit ball corresponding to our norm. We cover

G
by the translates
g\exp K
by varying
g
. The Lie-Taylor formula
(
1.22
) yields then

\big\lVert\phi\big\rVert_{g\exp(K_{0})}=\max_{\xi\in K_{0}}\big\lvert\phi(g\exp\xi)\big\rvert\leq\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{\alpha\in\{1,\ldots,d\}^{n}}\Big\lvert\bigl(\mathscr{L}(\alpha)\phi\bigr)(g)\Big\rvert=\operatorname{F}_{\phi}(g,\mathrm{e})

for any
g\in G
. By Corollary
2
,

\phi\mapsto\operatorname{F}_{\phi}(g,\mathrm{e})
constitutes a continuous seminorm
on
\mathscr{E}(G)
, which completes the proof.

quantization, which is what the algebra
\mathscr{E}(G)
was originally conceived for
within
[

. Glossing over the motivation entirely, we
define the holomorphic polynomial algebra of

by

\mathscr{H}_{\mathrm{Pol}}(T^{*}G)\coloneqq\mathscr{H}(G{C})\mathbin{\otimes}_{\pi}\mathscr{H}_{1}(\mathfrak{g}{C}^{*})

(3.18)

\mathbin{\otimes}_{\pi}

(3.19)

in the sense of Remark

. Note that this indeed makes sense, since

\mathscr{E}(G)

Theorem 3.3
(Complex Standard Ordered Star Product).

Let

The standard ordered star product

\star_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{std}}}\colon\mathscr{H}_{\mathrm{Pol}}(T^{*}G)\mathbin{\otimes}_{\pi}\mathscr{H}_{\mathrm{Pol}}(T^{*}G)\longrightarrow\mathscr{H}_{\mathrm{Pol}}(T^{*}G)

(3.20)

The mapping

\ch@airxmathbbm{C}\times\mathscr{H}_{\mathrm{Pol}}(T^{*}G)\otimes\mathscr{H}_{\mathrm{Pol}}(T^{*}G)\ni(\hbar,\Phi,\Psi)\mapsto\Phi\star_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{std}}}\Psi\in\mathscr{H}_{\mathrm{Pol}}(T^{*}G)

(3.21)

is Fréchet holomorphic.

Remark 3.4
(Real Forms).

Not every complex Lie group arises as a universal complexification. By

[

there exists a complex Lie algebra
\mathfrak{h}

,

. But

of the same lemma, which is a contradiction.

[

Having established our main Theorems, we discuss some examples of what they

Example 4.1
(Circle group).

Let

G\coloneqq\operatorname{U}(1)\coloneqq\bigl\{z\in\ch@airxmathbbm{C}\colon\lvert z\rvert=1\bigr\},

(4.1)

\operatorname{U}(1)\hookrightarrow\ch@airxmathbbm{C}^{\times}\coloneqq\ch@airxmathbbm{C}\setminus\{0\}
. It is convenient to

g=r\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t}
with

and

, which makes it straightforward to check the universal property.

If now
\Phi\in\mathscr{H}(\ch@airxmathbbm{C}^{\times})

\Phi

convergent Laurent expansion

\Phi(z)=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\qquad\textrm{for all }z\in\ch@airxmathbbm{C}^{\times},

(4.2)

where

a_{n}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\Phi(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t})\cdot\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}nt}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\qquad\textrm{for all }n\in\ch@airxmathbbm{Z}=-\ch@airxmathbbm{N}\cup\ch@airxmathbbm{N}_{0}.

(4.3)

In particular, the Lie-Taylor coefficients of
\phi
may be read off from

\Phi\bigl(\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}t}\bigr)=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}a_{n}\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}nt}

(4.4)

as

\mathscr{L}(\xi)^{k}\Phi\Big|_{1}=\xi^{k}\cdot\mathscr{L}(1)^{k}\Phi\Big|_{1}=\xi^{k}\cdot\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{k}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t^{k}}\phi\bigl(\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}t}\bigr)\Big|_{t=0}=(2\pi\mathrm{i}\xi)^{k}\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}n^{k}a_{n}

(4.5)

for all
\xi\in\ch@airxmathbbm{C}=\ch@airxmathbbm{R}{C}
. Notably, they are not simply given by the Laurent
coefficients and as such quite complicated; the same is thus true for the Lie-Taylor
expansion (
1.22
). Nevertheless, the condition for
\Phi
being entire
becomes the finiteness of

\mathrm{q}_{r}(\Phi)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(2\pi r)^{k}}{k!}\bigg\lvert\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}n^{k}a_{n}\bigg\rvert\leq\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\lvert a_{n}\rvert\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(2\pi rn)^{k}}{k!}=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\lvert a_{n}\rvert\mathrm{e}^{2\pi rn}<\infty

(4.6)

by absolute convergence of the series (
4.4
) for all
r\geq 0
.
Hence,
\Phi\in\mathscr{E}(\ch@airxmathbbm{C}^{\times})
, matching Theorem
1
. In
particular,
\Phi\big|_{U(1)}\in\mathscr{E}(\operatorname{U}(1))
as predicted by
Theorem
1
.

Conversely, given
\phi\in\mathscr{E}(\operatorname{U(1)})
, we observe that the
composition
\phi\circ\exp
possesses a holomorphic extension to an entire
function
F
on
\ch@airxmathbbm{C}
. Using polar coordinates as before, we may then define

\Phi(r\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}t})\coloneqq F\bigl(\log r\cdot\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}t}\bigr).

(4.7)

This idea is at the core of the earlier extension result

[
4
, Theorem 3.5.5]
for compact connected Lie groups, which our
Theorem
1
supercedes. Its proof was based on the existence of
a polar decomposition for any
compact Lie group.

Notably, the Laurent coefficients from (
4.3
) are
well-defined for
\phi
and thus one may attempt to define an holomorphic
extension by means of (
4.2
) directly. It would be
interesting to make this precise.

The next example showcases the pathologies the non-injectivitity of
\eta
may
result in.

Example 4.2
(Automatic periodicity).

We consider the special linear group

G\coloneqq\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\ch@airxmathbbm{R})=\bigl\{M\in\mathrm{M}_{2}(\ch@airxmathbbm{R})\colon\det(M)=1\bigr\},

(4.8)

which is connected, but not simply connected. Its universal covering group is

\widetilde{G}=\widetilde{\operatorname{SL}}_{2}(\ch@airxmathbbm{R})
, which is a nonlinear group
by
[
6
, Example 9.5.18]
, i.e. does not possess any faithful
finite-dimensional representations. We denote the corresponding universal
covering projection by

p\colon\widetilde{\operatorname{SL}}_{2}(\ch@airxmathbbm{R})\longrightarrow\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\ch@airxmathbbm{R}).

(4.9)

The Lie algebras of both groups are given by the traceless matrices

\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\ch@airxmathbbm{R})\coloneqq\bigl\{M\in\mathrm{M}_{2}(\ch@airxmathbbm{R})\colon\operatorname{\mathrm{tr}}(M)=0\bigr\},

(4.10)

whose complexification is simply

\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\ch@airxmathbbm{R}){C}\cong\bigl\{M\in\mathrm{M}_{2}(\ch@airxmathbbm{C})\colon\operatorname{\mathrm{tr}}(M)=0\bigr\}\eqqcolon\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\ch@airxmathbbm{C}).

(4.11)

Consequently, by the construction within
Proposition
2
, we have

\widetilde{\operatorname{SL}}_{2}(\ch@airxmathbbm{R}){C}\cong\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\ch@airxmathbbm{C})\coloneqq\bigl\{M\in\mathrm{M}_{2}(\ch@airxmathbbm{C})\colon\det(M)=1\bigr\},

(4.12)

as
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\ch@airxmathbbm{C})
is simply connected by

[
6
, Exercise 15.1.1]
with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\ch@airxmathbbm{C})
. Next,
we prove that
\widetilde{\eta}=\iota\circ p
, where
\iota\colon\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\ch@airxmathbbm{R})\hookrightarrow\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\ch@airxmathbbm{C})
simply reinterprets real matrices as complex
ones. Indeed, by construction its tangent map is given by

T_{\mathrm{e}}\widetilde{\eta}=T_{\mathrm{e}}\iota=T_{\mathrm{e}}\iota\circ T_{\mathrm{e}}p,

(4.13)

where
T_{\mathrm{e}}\iota
is the inclusion
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\ch@airxmathbbm{R})\hookrightarrow\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\ch@airxmathbbm{C})
and by connectedness the claimed form of
\widetilde{\eta}
follows.
Notably,
\widetilde{\eta}
is not injective, but nevertheless locally injective.
Moreover, we get

\widetilde{\eta}\bigl(\pi_{1}(G)\bigr)=\{\mathrm{e}\}.

(4.14)

Consequently, (
2.2
) yields that

\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\ch@airxmathbbm{R}){C}=\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\ch@airxmathbbm{C})
with
\eta\coloneqq\iota
.

This example is remarkable in at least two regards: firstly, we see that non
isomorphic Lie groups may have isomorphic universal complexifications. Secondly,

\pi_{1}(G{C})=\pi_{1}\bigl(\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\ch@airxmathbbm{C})\bigr)=\{\mathrm{e}\}\subsetneq\ch@airxmathbbm{Z}=\pi_{1}\bigl(\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\ch@airxmathbbm{R})\bigr)=\pi_{1}(G).

(4.15)

Let now
\phi\in\mathscr{E}(\widetilde{\operatorname{SL}}_{2}(\ch@airxmathbbm{R}))
. By
Theorem
1
, there exists a unique holomorphic extension

\Phi\in\mathscr{H}\bigl(\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\ch@airxmathbbm{C})\bigr)

(4.16)

such that
\Phi\circ p=\phi
. This means that
\Phi
factors through
p
and is
thus necessarily periodic with respect to the fundamental group

\pi_{1}(\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\ch@airxmathbbm{R}))
. Consequently, the algebra

\mathscr{E}(\widetilde{\operatorname{SL}}_{2}(\ch@airxmathbbm{R}))
does not separate points.

A note on the classification of classical distance-regular graphs of negative type and the non-existence of hemisystems
Sam Adriaensen, Jan De Beule, Jozefien D’haeseleer, Sam Mattheus

Abstract
The goal of this note is to connect some interesting results in the literature on algebraic graph theory and finite geometry. In 1999, Weng gave an almost complete classification of classical distance-regular graphs of negative type with diameter at least 4. He proved that these graphs are either dual polar graphs of Hermitian polar spaces, Hermitian forms graphs, or fall into a last category. It was recently proved by Yian et al. that the latter category does not exist when the diameter equals 3, which by Weng’s results proves that they do not exist for bigger diameter. Using a result of Vanhove, this proves that certain hemisystems in Hermitian polar spaces cannot exist.



The goal of this note is to combine the recent findings of Tian et al.
[
TLH+24
]
with the existing literature, to draw some interesting conclusions.
We emphasize this note does not contribute any new mathematical ideas, rather we connect some dots in the realm of mathematical literature.

Let us start by presenting the main findings, the necessary mathematical background can be found in
Section

1
.

Weng
[
Wen99
]
gave an almost full classification of classical distance-regular graphs of negative type.
We will use the common notation
a_{i},b_{i},c_{i}
for the intersection numbers of a distance-regular graph.

Theorem 1

(
[
Wen99
, Main Theorem]
)
.

Suppose that
\Gamma
is a distance-regular graph with classical parameters
(d,b,\alpha,\beta)
and
d\geqslant 4
.
Suppose that
b<-1
,
a_{1}\neq 0
, and
c_{2}>1
.
Then
b=-q
for some prime power
q
, and one of the following options holds:

(1)

\Gamma
is the dual polar graph of the Hermitian polar space
\mathcal{H}(2d-1,q^{2})
, or

(2)

\Gamma
is the Hermitian forms graph in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{d\times d}
, or

(3)

q
is odd,
\alpha=-\frac{q+1}{2}
, and
\beta=-\frac{1+(-q)^{d}}{2}
.

No distance-regular graphs of type (3) in
1
are known for
d>2
.
It follows from the work of Tian et al.
[
TLH+24
]
, that these graphs do not exist for
d=3
.
Combining some of the results by Weng
[
Wen99
]
, this implies the non-existence for higher values of
d
, yielding the following theorem.

Theorem 2
.

Suppose that
\Gamma
is a distance-regular graph with classical parameters
(d,b,\alpha,\beta)
and
d\geqslant 4
.
Suppose that
b<-1
,
a_{1}\neq 0
, and
c_{2}>1
.
Then
b=-q
for some prime power
q
, and one of the following options holds:

(1)

\Gamma
is the dual polar graph of the Hermitian polar space
\mathcal{H}(2d-1,q^{2})
, or

(2)

\Gamma
is the Hermitian forms graph in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{d\times d}
.

By a result of Vanhove
[
Van11
]
, this also proves that certain hemisystems in Hermitian polar spaces cannot exist.

Corollary 3
.

The finite Hermitian polar space
\mathcal{H}(2d-1,q^{2})
with
d>2
and
q\neq 3
does not have hemisystems with respect to
(d-2)
-spaces.

The proofs will be discussed in more detail in
Section

2
.

1
Preliminaries

1.1
Hermitian forms and polar spaces

Consider a prime power
q
, and the finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
of square order.
The map
x\mapsto\overline{x}=x^{q}
defines a field involution of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
.
For any matrix
M
over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
, let
\overline{M}
denote the matrix obtained by raising each entry of
M
to the power
q
, and let
M^{\dagger}
denote
\overline{M}^{\top}
.
The matrix
M
is called
Hermitian
if
M^{\dagger}=M
.

For any subspace
U\leqslant\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{n}
, define

U^{\perp}=\left\{y\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{n}\,\,||\,\,(\forall x\in U)(x^{\dagger}y=0\right\}.

Then the map
\perp:U\mapsto U^{\perp}
is an involution on the set of subspaces of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{n}
that reverses inclusion.
We call
\perp
a
Hermitian polarity
.
A subspace
U
is called
totally isotropic
(with respect to
\perp
) if
U\leqslant U^{\perp}
.
The Hermitian polar space
\mathcal{H}(n-1,q^{2})
is the geometry formed by the totally isotropic subspaces.
We will henceforth use terminology from projective geometry.
If a subspace
U
has bases with
t
elements, we say that
U
has
rank
t
and
(projective) dimension
t-1
.
Subspaces of dimension 0 are called
points
.
The maximum dimension of a totally isotropic subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{n}
is given by
d=\left\lfloor n/2\right\rfloor-1
, and such subspaces are called
generators
.
We say that
\mathcal{H}(n-1,q^{2})
is a
rank
(d+1)
-geometry
.
A set
\mathcal{S}
of generators of
\mathcal{H}(n-1,q^{2})
is called a
hemisystem with respect to the
k
-spaces
(with
0\leqslant k\leqslant d-2
) if for every
k
-dimensional totally isotropic subspace
U
, exactly half of the generators incident with
U
are contained in
\mathcal{S}
.
It is easy to check that a hemisystem with respect to the
k
-spaces is a hemisystem with respect to the
k^{\prime}
-spaces for all
k^{\prime}\leqslant k
.
Since the number of generators is equivalent to 1 modulo
q
, a hemisystem can only exist when
q
is odd.

1.2
Distance-regular graphs

Consider a graph
\Gamma
of diameter
d
.
For any vertex
v
of
\Gamma
, let
\Gamma_{i}(v)
denote the set of vertices at distance exactly
i
from
\Gamma
.
We call
\Gamma

distance-regular
if there exist numbers
a_{i},b_{i},c_{i}
for
i=0,\dots,d
, such that for every vertex
v\in\Gamma
, and every vertex
w\in\Gamma_{i}(v)
,

\displaystyle|\Gamma_{1}(w)\cap\Gamma_{i}(v)|=a_{i},

\displaystyle|\Gamma_{1}(w)\cap\Gamma_{i+1}(v)|=b_{i},

\displaystyle|\Gamma_{1}(w)\cap\Gamma_{i-1}(v)|=c_{i}.

We remark that
\Gamma
must be regular of degree
b_{0}
, and that for each
i
,
a_{i}+b_{i}+c_{i}=b_{0}
.
The sequence of numbers
\{b_{0},\dots,b_{d-1};c_{1},\dots,c_{d}\}
is called the
intersection array
of
\Gamma
.
For more on distance-regular graphs, we refer the reader to the seminal monograph by Brouwer, Cohen, and Neumaier
[
BCN89
]
.
In their work, the authors noted that the intersection array of the most well-known families of distance-regular graphs (including Johnsons graphs; Grassmann graphs; dual polar graphs; bilinear, alternating, and Hermitian forms graphs; and Hamming graphs) are determined by only four numbers
(d,b,\alpha,\beta)
.
More precisely, for an integer
i
and a real number
b
, define
[i]_{b}=1+b+\ldots+b^{i-1}
.
Note that
[i]_{b}=\frac{b^{i}-1}{b-1}
if
b\neq 1
.
If
\Gamma
is a distance-regular graph of diameter
d
, and its intersection array is given by

\displaystyle a_{i}=[i]_{b}\left(\beta-1+\alpha([d]_{b}-[i]_{b}-[i-1]_{b})\right),

\displaystyle b_{i}=([d]_{b}-[i]_{b})(\beta-\alpha[i]_{b}),

\displaystyle c_{i}=[i]_{b}(1+\alpha[i-1]_{b}),

then
\Gamma
is said to be
classical
, and have
classical parameters

(d,b,\alpha,\beta)
.
Moreover,
b
has to be an integer with
b\neq 0,-1
.
If
b<-1
,
\Gamma
is said to have
negative type
.

Example 4
.

(1)

The
dual polar graph
of
\mathcal{H}(2d-1,q^{2})
is the graph whose vertices are the generators of
\mathcal{H}(2d-1,q^{2})
, and where
\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}
are adjacent if and only if
\dim(\pi_{1}\cap\pi_{2})=d-2
.
It is a distance-regular graph with two sets of classical parameters:
\left(d,-q,-q\frac{q+1}{q-1},-q\frac{(-q)^{d}+1}{q-1}\right)
and
(d,q^{2},0,q)
.

(2)

The
Hermitian forms graph
in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{d\times d}
is the graph with as vertices the Hermitian matrices of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{d\times d}
, where
M
and
N
are adjacent if and only if
\rk(M-N)=1
.
It is a distance-regular graph with classical parameters
\left(d,-q,-q-1,-(-q)^{d}-1\right)
.

Proposition 5

(
[
BCN89
, Corollary 6.2.2]
,
[
IS89
]
)
.

Suppose that
\Gamma
is a classical distance-regular graph.
Then either it is isomorphic to the dual polar graph of
\mathcal{H}(2d-1,q^{2})
, in which case it has two sets of classical parameters, or it has a unique set of classical parameters.

2
Proofs of the claims

In order to prove
2
, it suffices to prove that Case (3) of
1
cannot occur.
By combining several results of Weng
[
Wen99
]
, we can reduce the problem of non-existence to the case
d=3
.

Theorem 6

(
[
Wen99
, Theorem 5.7, Lemma 4.10]
)
.

If
\Gamma
is a distance-regular graph with classical parameters
(d,b,\alpha,\beta)
where
d\geqslant 3
,
b<-1
,
a_{1}\neq 0
, and
c_{2}>1
, then for every pair of vertices
v,w
of
\Gamma
, there exists an induced subgraph
\Gamma^{\prime}
of
\Gamma
containing
v
and
w
which is distance-regular with classical parameters

\big(t,b,\alpha,\beta+\alpha([d]_{b}-[t]_{b})\big),

where
t
is the distance between
v
and
w
.

In particular, if
\Gamma
has parameters
\left(d,b=-q,\alpha=-\frac{q+1}{2},\beta=-\frac{(-q)^{d}+1}{2}\right)
, then we have

\beta+\alpha([d]_{b}-[t]_{b})=-\frac{(-q)^{d}+1}{2}-\frac{q+1}{2}\left(\frac{1-(-q)^{d}}{q+1}-\frac{1-(-q)^{t}}{q+1}\right)=-\frac{(-q)^{t}+1}{2}.

We combine this with the recent result by Tian et al.
[
TLH+24
]
.

Theorem 7

(
[
TLH+24
, Theorem 1.1 (4)]
)
.

If
\Gamma
is a distance-regular graph with classical parameters
(3,b,\alpha,\beta)
with
b<-1
,
a_{1}\neq 0
, and
c_{2}>1
, then one of the following holds:

(1)

\Gamma
is the dual polar graph of
\mathcal{H}(3,q^{2})
with parameters
\left(3,-q,-q\frac{q+1}{q-1},q\frac{q^{3}-1}{q-1}\right)
, or

(2)

\Gamma
is the extended ternary Golay code graph with parameters
(3,-2,-3,8)
, or

(3)

\Gamma
is the large Witt graph with parameters
(3,-2,-4,10)
, or

(4)

\Gamma
is the collinearity graph of a maximal near hexagon with classical parameters
\Big(3,-a_{1}-1,-\frac{c_{2}}{a_{1}}-1,(a_{1}+1)(c_{2}+a_{1}+1)\Big)
.

We are now ready to prove
2
.

Proof of
2
.

Using
1
, it suffices to prove that a distance-regular graph
\Gamma
with classical parameters
\Big(d,-q,-\frac{q+1}{2},-\frac{1+(-q)^{d}}{2}\Big)
, where
q
is an odd prime power,
d\geqslant 4
,
a_{1}\neq 0
, and
c_{2}>1
, cannot exist.
Hence, suppose that
\Gamma
exists.
Note that
a_{1}=\frac{q-3}{2}
and
c_{2}=\frac{(q-1)^{2}}{2}
are independent of
d
.
Then by
6
, there exists a subgraph
\Gamma^{\prime}
of
\Gamma
which is distance-regular with classical parameters
\Big(3,-q,-\frac{q+1}{2},-\frac{1-q^{3}}{2}\Big)
.
Note that
a_{1}
and
c_{2}
are the same for both graphs.
Hence, using
5
, the set of parameters
\Big(3,-q,-\frac{q+1}{2},-\frac{1-q^{3}}{2}\Big)
must appear in the list of
7
.
Since
q
must be odd, cases (2) and (3) are excluded.
If case (1) occurs, then
-\frac{q+1}{2}=-q\frac{q+1}{q-1}
, which implies that
q=-1
, contradicting that
q
is the power of a prime.
If case (4) occurs, then
-q=-a_{1}-1=-\frac{q-3}{2}-1
, which again implies that
q=-1
, a contradiction.
∎

Vanhove
[
Van11
]
showed that hemisystems with respect to
(d-2)
-spaces of
\mathcal{H}(2d-1,q^{2})
give rise to classical distance-regular graphs with parameters as in
1
(3).

Theorem 8

(
[
Van11
, Theorem 4]
)
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{S}
is a hemisystem in
\mathcal{H}(2d-1,q^{2})
with respect to the
(d-2)
-spaces.
The induced subgraph of the dual polar graph of
\mathcal{H}(2d-1,q^{2})
on the set of vertices
\mathcal{S}
is distance-regular with classical parameters

\left(d,-q,-\frac{q+1}{2},-\frac{(-q)^{d}+1}{2}\right).

We can now easily derive
3
.

Proof of
3
.

Suppose that
d>2
,
q\neq 3
, and that
\mathcal{H}(2d-1,q^{2})
has a hemisystem with respect to
(d-2)
-spaces.
Then
q\geqslant 5
, since
q
needs to be odd.
By
8
, there exists a distance-regular graph
\Gamma
with classical parameters
\Big(d,-q,-\frac{q+1}{2},-\frac{(-q)^{d}+1}{2}\Big)
.
As observed in the proof of
2
,
a_{1}=\frac{q-3}{2}
and
c_{2}=\frac{(q-1)^{2}}{2}
.
Since we suppose that
q\geqslant 5
, this means that
a_{1}\neq 0
and
c_{2}>1
.
Hence,
\Gamma
cannot exist by
2
.
∎

This leaves us with some interesting questions.

Open Problems.

(1)

Hemisystems with respect to points in
\mathcal{H}(2d-1,q^{2})

Can one determine the maximal
k

k
-spaces in
\mathcal{H}(2d-1,q^{2})

(2)

This paper did not deal with the case
q=3
. Does
\mathcal{H}(2d-1,3^{2})
admit hemisystems with respect to the
(d-2)
-spaces?
More generally, do distance-regular graphs with classical parameters
\Big(d,-3,-2,-\frac{(-3)^{d}+1}{2}\Big)

It is known that for
d=2

.

Lorentz violation and Hawking–Page metastability in Bumblebee–AdS gravity
Syed Masood

Abstract
The standard Hawking–Page description of AdS black holes, based on an on-shell Gibbs free-energy crossing, does not resolve the metastable black-hole branch or the barrier-mediated conversion between competing phases. We address this issue for neutral AdS black holes in Bumblebee gravity by constructing the generalized off-shell Gibbs free-energy landscape in the canonical ensemble at fixed effective pressure. We find analytically that the minimum temperature T min T_{\min} is independent of the Lorentz-violating parameter α \alpha , whereas the Hawking–Page temperature T HP T_{\mathrm{HP}} increases monotonically with α \alpha . Consequently, the locally stable but globally disfavored large-black-hole window T min < T < T HP T_{\min}<T<T_{\mathrm{HP}} broadens with increasing α \alpha . Within the off-shell framework, we derive closed-form expressions for the global-preference gap Δ ​ G glob \Delta G_{\mathrm{glob}} , which measures the free-energy difference between the large-black-hole minimum and thermal AdS, and the barrier height Δ ​ G bar \Delta G_{\mathrm{bar}} , which measures the separation between the small-black-hole saddle and the large-black-hole minimum. We recover the Schwarzschild–AdS limit as α → 0 \alpha\to 0 . These results establish the off-shell free-energy landscape as a precise thermodynamic framework for diagnosing Lorentz-violating effects in AdS black-hole phase structure.

Introduction

Lorentz symmetry is one of the most precisely tested principles of modern physics
[
1
,
2
]
. Nevertheless, a broad class of ultraviolet completions of gravity—including string theory
[
3
]
, loop quantum gravity
[

[

, and Hořava–Lifshitz gravity
[

[

. The Standard Model Extension (SME)
[
9
]
provides a systematic effective-field-theory framework in which such deformations can be parametrized and constrained observationally
[
2
,
10
,
11
]
.

In the gravitational sector, spontaneous LV arises when a dynamical tensor field acquires a nonzero vacuum expectation value (VEV), thereby selecting a preferred spacetime direction. The vector case—commonly referred to as Bumblebee gravity
[
12
,
13
,
14
,
15
]
—has become a controlled theoretical laboratory for studying the phenomenological consequences of LV in curved spacetime. Spherically symmetric black hole solutions in Bumblebee gravity were first derived in Ref.
[
16
]
and subsequently extended to include a cosmological constant in Ref.
[
17
]
, yielding a one-parameter family of solutions in which
\alpha
—the LV coupling—enters through an effective rescaling of the AdS curvature scale. Various observational aspects of this solution, including thermodynamics
[
18
,
19
]
, Hawking radiation
[
20
]
, quasinormal modes
[
21
]
, gravitational lensing
[
22
]
, and Event Horizon Telescope constraints
[
23
]
, have been studied.

Despite this progress, the thermodynamic phase structure of Bumblebee–AdS black holes—and specifically how LV modifies the organization of competing thermodynamic phases—has not been analyzed with the resolution provided by the off-shell free-energy landscape. In the canonical ensemble, the Hawking–Page (HP) transition
[
24
]
marks the coexistence boundary between thermal AdS radiation and a large stable black hole. The on-shell analysis, based on the sign of the Gibbs free energy, identifies the transition temperature but does not resolve the metastable coexistence region
T_{\min}<T<T_{\rm HP}
where a locally stable large black hole (LBH) and globally preferred thermal AdS coexist. The off-shell free-energy landscape
[
25
,
26
]
—constructed by promoting the horizon radius to an order parameter and allowing off-shell fluctuations—makes this structure explicit through the global-preference gap
\Delta G_{\rm glob}
and the free-energy barrier
\Delta G_{\rm bar}

[
27
,
28
,
29
]
. The free energy of the non-equilibrium black hole configurations in this landscape has been given a concrete path-integral foundation via Euclidean gravitational instantons with conical singularities
[
30
]
.

In this work, we use the Bumblebee–AdS black hole
[
17
]
as a minimal, analytically tractable model to probe the interplay between LV deformations and thermodynamic phase structure. Our results indicate that the HP temperature
T_{\rm HP}
shifts monotonically with
\alpha
, while
T_{\min}
remains exactly independent of
\alpha
, structurally widening the metastable window. The off-shell landscape quantifies the resulting LV-deformed barrier and global-preference gap analytically. Finally, the standard Schwarzschild–AdS phase structure is recovered smoothly as
\alpha\to 0

The paper is organized as follows. Section

III
identifies the
\alpha
-shifted Hawking–Page benchmark. Section
IV

V

II.2
Black hole branching

The Hawking temperature follows from the surface gravity associated with the timelike Killing field

\eta^{\mu}=(\partial_{t})^{\mu}
,

\displaystyle T_{\rm H}=\frac{\kappa}{2\pi}=-\frac{1}{4\pi\sqrt{-g_{tt}g_{rr}}}\,\frac{\partial g_{tt}}{\partial r}\bigg|_{r=r_{+}}.

(8)

From Eq. (
2
) one obtains

\displaystyle T_{\rm H}(r_{+})=\frac{1-(1+\alpha)\Lambda_{e}\,r_{+}^{2}}{4\pi\sqrt{1+\alpha}\,r_{+}}.

(9)

For the AdS branch
\Lambda_{e}<0
, it is useful to rewrite (
9
) as

\displaystyle T_{\rm H}(r_{+})=\frac{1}{4\pi\sqrt{1+\alpha}}\left(\frac{1}{r_{+}}+(1+\alpha)|\Lambda_{e}|\,r_{+}\right),

(10)

from which one can see that for
r_{+}\to 0
,
T_{\rm H}\sim[4\pi\sqrt{1+\alpha}\,r_{+}]^{-1}\to\infty
, while for
r_{+}\to\infty
, one finds

\displaystyle T_{\rm H}(r_{+})=\frac{\sqrt{1+\alpha}\,|\Lambda_{e}|}{4\pi}\,r_{+}+\frac{1}{4\pi\sqrt{1+\alpha}}\frac{1}{r_{+}}+\mathcal{O}(r_{+}^{-3}),

(11)

so the large-
r_{+}
branch grows linearly with asymptotic slope

\mathrm{d}T_{\rm H}/\mathrm{d}r_{+}\to\sqrt{1+\alpha}\,|\Lambda_{e}|/(4\pi)
, which increases monotonically with
\alpha
.
This accounts for the observed fan-out of the curves in Fig.
2
at large
r_{+}
: larger
\alpha
corresponds to a steeper large-black-hole branch.

Since
T_{\rm H}\to\infty
both as
r_{+}\to 0
and as
r_{+}\to\infty
,
T_{\rm H}(r_{+})
admits a unique global minimum,
determined by
\mathrm{d}T_{\rm H}/\mathrm{d}r_{+}=0
:

\displaystyle r_{\min}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{-(1+\alpha)\Lambda_{e}}}.

(12)

corresponding to the temperature

\displaystyle T_{\min}=T_{\rm H}(r_{\min})=\frac{\sqrt{-\Lambda_{e}}}{2\pi}.

(13)

The radius (
12
) defines the standard small-/large-black-hole branching:
we refer to the branch
r_{+}<r_{\min}
as the small black hole (SBH) branch and
r_{+}>r_{\min}
as the large black hole (LBH) branch.
In the canonical ensemble, black-hole solutions exist only for
T\geq T_{\min}\equiv T_{\rm H}(r_{\min})
, while for
T<T_{\min}
the ensemble is described by thermal AdS radiation
[
24
]
. Below we will see that this same point
r_{\rm min}
coincides with the divergence of the heat capacity, marking the change of local thermal stability between the two branches.

The
\alpha
-independence of
T_{\rm min}
in (
13
) follows from a simple cancellation.
At the stationary point the two contributions in (
10
) are equal, such that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{r_{\min}}=(1+\alpha)|\Lambda_{e}|\,r_{\min},

(14)

and using
r_{\min}=1/\sqrt{(1+\alpha)|\Lambda_{e}|}
we also have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{r_{\min}}=\sqrt{(1+\alpha)|\Lambda_{e}|}.

(15)

so the parentheses in (
10
) evaluate to
2\sqrt{(1+\alpha)|\Lambda_{e}|}
.
The prefactor
1/\sqrt{1+\alpha}
removes the
\alpha
-dependence in (
13
). Accordingly, increasing
\alpha
shifts the branching point to smaller radius,
r_{\min}\propto(1+\alpha)^{-1/2}
, without changing
T_{\min}
,
while it steepens the LBH branch through the asymptotic slope (
11
).

III
On-shell Hawking–Page transition

In the canonical ensemble at fixed effective pressure (equivalently fixed
\Lambda_{e}<0
), the globally preferred phase at a given temperature is determined by the Gibbs free energy.
We normalize the reference thermal AdS background such that
G_{\rm AdS}=0

[
24

, and compare it with the on-shell black-hole Gibbs free energy

\displaystyle G_{\rm H}(r_{+})\,=\,M(r_{+})-T_{\rm H}(r_{+})\,S(r_{+}),

(22)

evaluated on the black-hole branch.
The Hawking–Page (HP) transition occurs when the two phases have equal free energy,

\displaystyle G_{\rm H}(r_{\rm HP})=G_{\rm AdS}=0,

(23)

at which the system undergoes a first-order transition between thermal AdS and a large AdS black hole
[

For Bumblebee–AdS black holes the HP point follows analytically from (

III.1
Stability-scale splitting and metastability width

A distinctive feature of the Bumblebee–AdS solution is that the temperatures governing black-hole branching and Hawking–Page coexistence respond differently to the LV coupling. From Eqs. (
13
) and (
26
), one has

T_{\min}=\frac{\sqrt{-\Lambda_{e}}}{2\pi},\qquad T_{\rm HP}(\alpha)=\frac{\sqrt{(1+\alpha)(-\Lambda_{e})}}{\sqrt{3}\,\pi}.

(28)

This motivates the dimensionless stability-splitting ratio

\mathcal{S}(\alpha)\equiv\frac{T_{\rm HP}(\alpha)}{T_{\min}}=\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}\sqrt{1+\alpha},

(29)

and the associated normalized metastability width

\mathcal{W}(\alpha)\equiv\frac{T_{\rm HP}(\alpha)-T_{\min}}{T_{\min}}=\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}\sqrt{1+\alpha}-1.

(30)

The quantity
\mathcal{W}
measures the separation between the onset of black-hole existence/local stability and the onset of global preference reversal. In Bumblebee gravity this separation is exact, monotonic in
\alpha
, and independent of
\Lambda_{e}
, showing that Lorentz violation does not merely shift the Hawking–Page point, but splits the local and global thermodynamic stability scales. Table
1
summarizes the resulting stability-scale splitting relative to the Schwarzschild–AdS case. The key distinction is that the LV deformation leaves
T_{\min}
invariant while shifting
T_{\rm HP}
, thereby enlarging the metastable Hawking–Page window in a controlled and exactly quantifiable manner.

IV
Off-shell landscape and overlap criterion

The thermodynamic phase structure discussed above can be analyzed in a canonical ensemble using the generalized (off-shell) free-energy landscape with the free energy given by approach
[
25
,
26
]

the off-shell Gibbs free energy reads

G(T,r_{+})=\frac{1}{6}r_{+}\Bigl[3-(1+\alpha)\Lambda_{e}r_{+}^{2}\Bigr]-\pi T\,r_{+}^{2},

(31)

In this framework, the order parameter is the horizon radius
r_{+}\geq 0
, with thermal AdS represented by the reference branch at
r_{+}=0
and

G_{\rm AdS}=0.

(32)

For a fixed ensemble temperature
T
, black-hole configurations are described by the off-shell Gibbs free energy
G(T,r_{+})
, while on-shell black-hole phases correspond to stationary points of
G
.

At fixed
(T,\Lambda_{e},\alpha)
, stationary points satisfy

\left.\frac{\partial G}{\partial r_{+}}\right|_{r_{+}=r_{+}^{\star}}=0;\left.\frac{\partial^{2}G}{\partial r_{+}^{2}}\right|_{r_{+}=r_{+}^{\star}}\gtrless 0\ \ (\text{min/max}),

(33)

so local stability is determined by the curvature of the landscape.
Whenever a locally stable LBH minimum exists at
r_{+}=r_{l}>0
, we quantify (i) the global preference of the LBH relative to thermal AdS by the gap

\Delta G_{\rm glob}(T,\Lambda_{e},\alpha)\equiv G(T,r_{l})-G_{\rm AdS}=G(T,r_{l}),

(34)

and (ii) the free-energy barrier separating the SBH saddle (local maximum) from the LBH minimum by

\Delta G_{\rm bar}(T,\Lambda_{e},\alpha)\equiv G(T,r_{s})-G(T,r_{l}),

(35)

where
r_{s}
denotes the SBH stationary point when it exists.
With these definitions, an
overlap (coexistence) region
is characterized by

T\geq T_{\min}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\Delta G_{\rm glob}(T,\Lambda_{e},\alpha)>0,

(36)

i.e., a locally stable LBH phase is available while thermal AdS remains globally preferred.

The off-shell Gibbs free energy reads

G(T,r_{+})=\frac{1}{6}r_{+}\Bigl[3-(1+\alpha)\Lambda_{e}r_{+}^{2}\Bigr]-\pi T\,r_{+}^{2},

(37)

and stationary points are determined by Eq. (
33
).
Equivalently, the stationarity condition can be written as
T=T_{H}^{\rm I}(r_{+})
, where Hawking temperature is given by Eq.(
9
).
For the ensemble temperature
T=T_{\rm H}(r_{+})
yields two branches

\displaystyle r_{s}(T)

\displaystyle=\frac{-2\pi T+\sqrt{4\pi^{2}T^{2}+\Lambda_{e}}}{\sqrt{1+\alpha}\,\Lambda_{e}},

(38)

\displaystyle r_{l}(T)

\displaystyle=\frac{-2\pi T-\sqrt{4\pi^{2}T^{2}+\Lambda_{e}}}{\sqrt{1+\alpha}\,\Lambda_{e}}.

(39)

respectively denoting SBH and LBH radii. This happens only for a nonnegative discriminant,

4\pi^{2}T^{2}+\Lambda_{e}\geq 0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad T\geq T_{\min}\equiv\frac{\sqrt{-\Lambda_{e}}}{2\pi}.

(40)

Evaluating
\partial_{r_{+}}^{2}G
at the stationary points confirms that
r_{s}
is a local maximum (SBH saddle) and
r_{l}
is a local minimum (LBH).

\Delta G_{\rm glob}
and
\Delta G_{\rm bar}
are then given by

\Delta G_{\rm glob}=-\frac{2\pi T+X}{2\sqrt{1+\alpha}\,\Lambda_{e}}+\frac{(2\pi T+X)^{3}}{6\sqrt{1+\alpha}\,\Lambda_{e}^{2}}\\
-\frac{\pi T\,(2\pi T+X)^{2}}{(1+\alpha)\,\Lambda_{e}^{2}}

(41)

with
X\equiv\sqrt{4\pi^{2}T^{2}+\Lambda_{e}}
(see Appendix
C
.),
and (see Appendix
B
)

\Delta G_{\rm bar}(T,\Lambda_{e},\alpha)=\frac{2\,\sqrt{4\pi^{2}T^{2}+\Lambda_{e}}}{3\,\Lambda_{e}^{\,2}(1+\alpha)}\big[\sqrt{1+\alpha}\,\bigl(\Lambda_{e}-8\pi^{2}T^{2}\bigr)\\
+12\pi^{2}T^{2}\big].

(42)

(b)
T<T_{\min}
.

In this case, the stationarity equation admits no real positive roots, so no black hole extrema exist in the landscape.
Indeed,
\partial_{r_{+}}G=0
reduces to a quadratic whose discriminant is
4\pi^{2}(T)^{2}+\Lambda_{e}
, hence real roots require

T\geq\sqrt{-\Lambda_{e}}/(2\pi)\equiv T_{\min}
(at fixed
\Lambda_{e}
this threshold is independent of
\alpha
).
Nevertheless, the off-shell functional
G(T,r_{+})
is still well-defined for trial
r_{+}>0
, and its explicit
\alpha
-dependence implies

\left(\partial_{\alpha}G\right)_{T,r_{+}}=-(\Lambda_{e}/6)\,r_{+}^{3}>0
for
\Lambda_{e}<0
, explaining the upward shift with increasing
\alpha
in Fig.
6
(b), which becomes more pronounced at large
r_{+}
.

(c)
T=T_{\min}
.

V
Summary and outlook

We have analyzed the thermodynamic phase structure of neutral AdS black holes in Bumblebee gravity using complementary on-shell and off-shell methods. In the canonical ensemble at fixed effective pressure, the on-shell analysis shows that the Lorentz-violating (LV) parameter
\alpha
shifts the Hawking–Page transition while preserving the standard small-/large-black-hole branching structure. A distinctive feature of the Bumblebee–AdS solution is that the Hawking–Page temperature
T_{\rm HP}
increases monotonically with
\alpha
, whereas the minimum temperature
T_{\min}
remains exactly independent of
\alpha
. As a consequence, the interval
T_{\min}<T<T_{\rm HP}
is enlarged, implying a broader regime in which the large-black-hole branch is locally stable but globally disfavored relative to thermal AdS.

The off-shell Gibbs free-energy landscape resolves this metastable structure explicitly. Treating the horizon radius as the order parameter, we identified the small-black-hole branch as a saddle and the large-black-hole branch as a local minimum whenever
T>T_{\min}
. This framework allows the metastable window to be quantified in terms of two analytic observables: the global-preference gap, which measures the free-energy difference between the large-black-hole minimum and thermal AdS, and the barrier height separating the saddle from the minimum. The resulting picture sharpens the standard Hawking–Page analysis by resolving not only the coexistence point but also the full LV deformation of the free-energy landscape.

In the Lorentz-invariant limit
\alpha\to 0
, all results reduce smoothly to the Schwarzschild–AdS case. The present analysis therefore identifies the free-energy landscape as a sensitive probe of LV deformations in asymptotically AdS black-hole thermodynamics.

Several extensions are natural. It would be useful to generalize the analysis to charged or rotating LV AdS black holes, where richer phase structure is expected. A second direction is to study dynamical aspects of the metastable regime, including nucleation and decay rates associated with the off-shell barrier. These questions may help clarify whether LV sectors produce qualitatively new thermodynamic signatures beyond the neutral static case.

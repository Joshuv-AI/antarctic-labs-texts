Predicting tumor micronecrosis in hepatocellular carcinoma using pretreatment contrast-enhanced CT radiomics model: a multi-center study
Authors: Yangyang Wang, Chao Huang, Yu Tian, Mao Ye, Cao Chen, Tianyi Xia, Yiting Xu, Haonan Ding, Yuquan Zhang, Yiwei Gao, Peijun Hu, Yan Zhu, Enliang Li, Xu Sun, Hongbin Ge, Rujia Zheng, Hongkan Wang, Wanyue Cao, Qitai Chen, Zhihang Cai, Ke Sun, Xing Xue, Tingting Lu, Shenghong Ju, Jingsong Li, Tingbo Liang, Qi Zhang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractTumor micronecrosis is a validated malignancy biomarker with poor prognosis in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), but currently lacks reliable preoperative detection methods. We developed a radiomics model for predicting tumor micronecrosis using preoperative contrast-enhanced computed tomography (CECT). The study included 1017 patients with histopathologically confirmed HCC across three centers, allocated to development, internal test, external test, and application sets. Radiomics features were extracted from tumor regions, with model training via a stratified 5-fold cross-validation framework. The model yield area under the curve values of 0.80 and 0.75 in the internal and external test sets, respectively. Survival curves for histologic and radiomics-predicted micronecrosis groups demonstrated close alignment. For patients treated with tyrosine kinase inhibitor and anti-PD-(L)1 immunotherapy, radiomics-predicted micronecrosis(−) and micronecrosis(+) groups showed comparable survival (P = 0.312). Mass cytometry demonstrated significant correlations between micronecrosis-associated radiomics features and peripheral CD24+ B cells and neutrophils. The radiomics model shows promise for preoperative micronecrosis assessment.Subject terms: Cancer imaging, Tumour biomarkers, Tumour heterogeneity

Introduction

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) ranks as the sixth most commonly diagnosed cancer and the third leading cause of cancer-related mortality worldwide1. Despite the availability of multiple therapeutic options for HCC, its prognosis remains poor due to high tumor heterogeneity2,3. A deeper understanding of this heterogeneity and the identification of robust biomarkers are essential for enhancing treatment decisions and improving survival outcomes4.

Several biomarkers, particularly those identified through pathology, have demonstrated significant value for prognostic assessment and treatment guidance in HCC. For instance, microvascular invasion (MVI) is associated with poorer survival outcomes and often prompts recommendations for adjuvant therapy5,6. Additionally, vessel encapsulation of tumor clusters (VETC) and the macrotrabecular-massive (MTM) subtype are influential on patient survival7,8. Recently, tumor micronecrosis, characterized microscopically by clusters of degenerating or necrotic cells, has garnered attention as a clinically relevant prognostic marker9. Studies show that tumor micronecrosis or microscopic necrosis is indicative of aggressive tumor biology and correlates with poor prognosis in patients with HCC9–11. However, as a histological biomarker, micronecrosis assessment has traditionally been restricted to post-surgical samples, obtained via hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining, limiting its application in preoperative settings and among patients who do not undergo surgery. Therefore, effective preoperative prediction of tumor micronecrosis remains a significant clinical challenge.

Contrast-enhanced computed tomography (CECT) is a widely used, non-invasive modality for preoperative HCC evaluation. Radiomics, which extracts high-dimensional quantitative features from medical imaging, has shown promise in predicting and diagnosing various diseases12, with potential applications in precisely assessing key tumor phenotypes including histopathological subtype and grade, as well as microenvironmental features13,14. Prior research demonstrates the utility of CECT-based radiomics for predicting histopathologic features in HCC, including MVI and MTM subtypes15,16. However, no radiomics model currently exists to predict tumor micronecrosis in HCC.

For patients who are not candidates for hepatectomy, systemic therapies, including tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs) and anti-PD-(L)1 immunotherapy, have emerged as standard treatments for advanced HCC17. Although systemic therapies may extend survival and increase the likelihood of subsequent hepatectomy, only a subset of patients benefit, with objective response rates of approximately 30%18,19. The response of micronecrotic tumors to TKIs and anti-PD-(L)1 immunotherapy remains uncertain.

This study aimed to predict tumor micronecrosis in patients with HCC using a radiomics model based on preoperative CECT images. Additionally, this model was applied to predict micronecrosis status in patients not undergoing liver surgery, assessing the effects of TKI and anti-PD-(L)1 immunotherapy across different micronecrosis statuses. Moreover, we analyzed the association between peripheral immunological profiles using mass cytometry (CyTOF) and the radiomics model.

Results

Characteristics of patients

A total of 1017 patients participated in the study, with 951 who underwent hepatectomy or liver transplantation included for developing a tumor micronecrosis prediction model (Fig. 1A). Patients from First Affiliated Hospital of Zhejiang University (FAHZU) were divided into a development set (n = 699) and an internal test set (n = 168), while patients from the Nanchang and Huzhou centers constituted the external test set (n = 84). Tumor micronecrosis was observed in 40.8% of cases, with prevalence ranging from 37.5% to 59.5% across the three sets (Table 1). Additionally, 66 patients who initially received TKI and anti-PD-(L)1 immunotherapy were assessed for tumor micronecrosis using the radiomics prediction model to further evaluate the efficacy of these therapies in micronecrotic cases.

Performance of the predicting models for tumor micronecrosis

Univariate analysis identified tumor size (odds ratio [OR] 2.539, 95% confidence interval [CI] 1.779–3.623, P < 0.001; Table 2), radiomics signature (OR 1.984, 95% CI 1.755–2.243, P < 0.001), and alpha-fetoprotein (AFP) level (P < 0.001) as significant predictors, which were subsequently included in multivariate logistic regression analysis. The multivariate analysis confirmed tumor size (OR 2.318, 95% CI 1.557–3.450, P < 0.001), radiomics signature (OR 1.909, 95% CI 1.665–2.189, P < 0.001), and AFP level (P = 0.016) as independent risk factors for tumor micronecrosis (Table 2).

Three predictive models were developed based on clinical and radiomics features. Given the radiomics signature’s strong association with tumor micronecrosis, the radiomics model achieved a high area under the curve (AUC) of 0.83 (95% CI, 0.80–0.86; Table 3, Fig. 3A). Its performance was further validated in the internal test set (AUC 0.80, 95% CI 0.73–0.86; Fig. 3B) and the external test set (AUC 0.76, 95% CI 0.65–0.87; Fig. 3C). The radiomics model achieved sensitivity, specificity, and accuracy of 72.4%, 78.1%, and 75.8%, respectively (Table 3). Calibration and decision curves corroborated the model’s reliability (Supplementary Fig. 1A–F). In contrast, the clinical model showed inferior performance (AUC 0.68, 95% CI 0.60–0.77), and the combined radiomics + clinical model did not surpass the predictive power of the standalone radiomics model (AUC 0.80, 95% CI 0.73–0.86).

Survival prediction based on the radiomics signature

Follow-up data were available for 161 of 168 patients (95.8%) in the internal validation cohort. Patients with histologically confirmed tumor micronecrosis had significantly poorer overall survival (OS) than those without micronecrosis (P < 0.001; Fig. 4A). Similarly, patients with radiomics-predicted micronecrosis demonstrated shorter OS compared to those without (P = 0.033; Fig. 4B).

Prediction of tumor micronecrosis using radiomics signature in patients with TKI and anti-PD-(L)1 immunotherapy

The tumor micronecrosis status of 66 patients treated with TKI and anti-PD-(L)1 antibodies was assessed using the radiomics model (Table 1). According to the radiomics signature, 23 patients (34.8%) were predicted as micronecrosis(+), while the remaining 43 (65.2%) were micronecrosis(-). The objective response rates were comparable between groups: 17.4% (4/23) in the radiomics-predicted micronecrosis(+) group and 14.0% (6/43) in the micronecrosis(-) group (P = 0.730). Median OS for patients with micronecrosis was 33.7 months compared to 24.4 months for those without, showing no significant difference (P = 0.312; Fig. 5). Additionally, no significant difference was observed in the proportion of patients receiving subsequent treatments (hepatectomy, interventional therapy, or radiotherapy) between the radiomics-predicted micronecrosis(+) and micronecrosis(-) groups (34.8% vs. 44.2%, P = 0.459).

Peripheral immunological features associated with the radiomics model

CyTOF was performed in 194 patients among the development set, including 71 cases with tumor micronecrosis and 123 cases without. We defined 21 cell clusters according to the cell surface markers, including 5 clusters of CD8+ T cells (C07, C10, C13, C14, C19), 6 clusters of CD4+ T cells (C11, C12, C15, C16, C17, C18), 2 clusters of B cells (C20, C21), 2 clusters of monocyte cells (C03, C06), and 6 clusters of other cells (Fig. 6A). The expression of CD24 and CCR6 was significantly different between patients with micronecrosis and those without (both P < 0.05) (Supplementary Table 1). Correlation analysis revealed that CD24 was significantly associated with the radiomics features of rad_A_wavelet_HHH_glszm_HighGrayLevelZoneEmphasis and rad_A_wavelet_LHL_glszm_SmallAreaLowGrayLevelEmphasis (correlation coefficient = −0.15 and 0.16, respectively; both P < 0.05; Fig. 6B). CD24 was mainly expressed on B cells and neutrophils in this study. These results demonstrated the relationship between radiomics features and peripheral immunity.

Discussion

Tumor micronecrosis demonstrates a strong correlation with survival outcomes and therapeutic choices for patients with HCC; however, it has traditionally been detectable only in histologic specimens post-surgery. This study presents a radiomics model developed to predict tumor micronecrosis using CECT images in a large cohort, achieving robust predictive ability with an AUC of 0.80 in the internal test cohort. This advancement in non-invasive prediction of tumor micronecrosis could substantially expand its applicability for preoperative cases and for patients unsuitable for surgery.

Histologically confirmed tumor micronecrosis or microscopic necrosis has been consistently associated with poor survival outcomes in HCC9–11,20–22. In this study, survival curves for patients classified with radiomics-predicted micronecrosis closely mirrored those based on histological confirmation, further underscoring the model’s strong predictive capability and its effectiveness in prognostic stratification.

The radiomics signature was independently associated with tumor micronecrosis, demonstrating its capacity as a predictive tool, while most common clinical variables failed to act as independent risk factors for micronecrosis, limiting the predictive power of the clinical model. Tumor micronecrosis, as a subtle and complex characteristic, is challenging to detect macroscopically, highlighting the need for efficient methods to identify this critical biomarker. Radiomics offers a solution that effectively surpasses conventional clinical parameters in predictive utility. Developed through stratified sampling and a 5-fold cross-validation framework, the radiomics model achieved high stability in performance. Overall, this study highlights the role of radiomics as a promising non-invasive approach, enabling precise prediction of tumor biological characteristics and potentially reflecting intratumoral heterogeneity12,23. Voxelwise maps of imaging biomarkers visually validate our radiomic model’s ability to detect micronecrosis. Notably, our radiomics model appears to detect secondary morphological and textural alterations in the perinecrotic microenvironment rather than directly identifying the necrotic foci themselves.

The development of the radiomics prediction model enables non-invasive assessment of tumor micronecrosis, allowing further investigation into the survival benefits of TKI and anti-PD-(L)1 immunotherapy. Results showed comparable prognoses in patients with or without radiomics-predicted micronecrosis, suggesting that, given the poor prognosis associated with tumor micronecrosis, those with micronecrosis may experience greater benefits from combined TKI and anti-PD-(L)1 immunotherapy than those without. Additionally, potential biological mechanisms underlying this therapeutic response were examined. Many therapies that induce tumor shrinkage can concurrently cause hypoxia and necrosis; however, a hypoxic tumor microenvironment (TME) and resultant necrosis may also drive tumor progression and resistance to treatment24,25. Beyond treatment-induced hypoxia, tumors can undergo hypoxia through various intrinsic mechanisms, leading to an immunosuppressive TME26. Spontaneous tumor micronecrosis is also associated with hypoxia27, which enhances vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) expression and promotes tumor angiogenesis28. This context suggests that therapies that limit necrosis or potentially reverse micronecrosis may achieve improved efficacy. TKI counteracts VEGF overexpression and tumor neovascularization, while anti-PD-(L)1 immunotherapy modulates the immunosuppressive TME in HCC29,30. Thus, TKI and anti-PD-(L)1 immunotherapy may effectively reprogram the TME in HCC with tumor micronecrosis, potentially altering the otherwise poor prognosis in these patients. While our current findings specifically address anti-PD-(L)1 therapy, future studies should explore applicability to other immunotherapeutic approaches, including CTLA-4 inhibitors and cellular therapies.

CyTOF analysis uncovered the biological significance of the radiomics features for tumor micronecrosis. The peripheral expression of CD24 prompted the maturity of B cells would promote tumor progression31,32. Additionally, aged neutrophiles with the expression of CD24 seem to show increased activity to promote inflammatory response33. Therefore, the relativity between the immunologic markers and radiomics features suggested that our radiomics models can reflect the immune status, indicating the interpretability of our radiomics model. Additionally, the immunosuppressive and inflammatory status maybe associated with the presence of tumor micronecrosis.

This study has several limitations. First, the radiomics model was developed from multicenter retrospective data in the East China region, indicating the need for expansion and validation across broader geographic areas with varied population characteristics. Second, the radiomics analysis depends on manual tumor delineation, a time-intensive process. The transition from handcrafted radiomics to self-supervised representation learning represents a paradigm shift in medical image analysis34. While the current study established the clinical relevance of micronecrosis using traditional methods, we believe that foundation models could unify micronecrosis analysis through multi-scale feature learning and improve generalizability via large-scale pretraining. Additionally, only the largest tumor could be analyzed, rather than all tumors present. Future efforts should focus on training artificial intelligence models for more precise and efficient tumor delineation. Finally, while this study proposes a hypothesis that combined TKI and anti-PD-(L)1 immunotherapy may benefit patients with tumor micronecrosis, this hypothesis requires validation through further research, including a detailed analysis of immune landscapes.

In summary, this study demonstrates micronecrosis as a clinically significant prognostic factor in HCC. The inability to evaluate micronecrosis preoperatively has been a major clinical limitation, which our radiomic analysis effectively addresses. By combining large-scale histopathologic correlation with rigorous multicenter validation, we provide robust evidence for clinical implementation. This approach may enable pretreatment identification of high-risk patients who could benefit from neoadjuvant therapies.

Methods

Patient selection and study design

A retrospective review of 1109 consecutive patients with HCC who underwent curative liver surgery was conducted across three centers: from June 2014 to June 2022 in the FAHZU, and from April 2014 to January 2020 in the Second Affiliated Hospital of Nanchang University and Huzhou Central Hospital, to develop predictive models. Inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) pathologically confirmed HCC; (2) hepatectomy with R0 resection or liver transplantation; and (3) availability of CECT images taken within one month prior to surgery. Exclusion criteria included: (1) presence of other malignancies; (2) prior anti-tumor treatments; and (3) incomplete clinical data or suboptimal image quality. Patients from FAHZU were allocated to the development and internal test sets in an 8:2 ratio, with stratification by CECT slice spacing to ensure that each subset contained cases with varying slice spacings in proportions consistent with the full dataset. This stratification was applied during dataset splitting and cross-validation to mitigate potential bias arising from uneven slice thickness distributions. Patients from the Nanchang Center and the Huzhou Center were used as an external test set.

Additionally, patients diagnosed with advanced HCC and receiving TKI and anti-PD-(L)1 immunotherapy as primary treatments in FAHZU between January 2020 and December 2023 were included, provided CECT images were available. Relevant blood biochemical indicators, including HBsAg, AFP albumin, and bilirubin, were reviewed alongside imaging data. This study was approved by the ethical committee of FAHZU (No. IIT20220785A), with a waiver of informed consent due to its retrospective nature. All organ donations were conducted with voluntary, informed consent, and no organs or tissues were sourced from prisoners. The study procedures adhered to the principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki.

Pathological definition of tumor micronecrosis

Tumor micronecrosis was identified through histological examination of surgical specimens, with H&E staining performed for pathological assessment. In accordance with the 2015 Chinese guidelines for the pathological diagnosis of primary liver cancer35, at least five sections per tumor (one central and four peripheral sections) were analyzed. For patients with multiple tumors, each tumor was assessed individually following this method. Two pathologists independently performed manual evaluation of tumor micronecrosis, with a senior pathologist providing a final confirmation in cases of ambiguity. Tumor micronecrosis was defined as the presence of necrosis occupying over 5% of any section9.

CT image study protocol

Abdominal CECT scans were obtained in the axial plane using five different scanners, with detailed CT parameters provided in Supplementary Table 2. The arterial (A) and portal venous (PV) phases, highlighting characteristic features of HCC, were used in this analysis. Tumor segmentation was conducted with ITK-SNAP software (version 3.8.0; https://www.itksnap.org), where regions of interest (ROIs) were delineated along the tumor boundary on all slices, encompassing both solid and necrotic tumor areas (Fig. 1B). Two junior readers independently outlined ROIs on a slice-by-slice basis, with a senior radiologist reviewing and refining all segmentations. Stratified sampling, based on CECT slice spacing, was used to address sample imbalance, ensuring proportional event representation across subsets.

Radiomics feature extraction

Image preprocessing and feature extraction were executed using the open-source PyRadiomics package (Version 3.0.1) in Python, with all settings kept at their default values unless otherwise specified below. PyRadiomics adheres to the IBSI (Image Biomarker Standardisation Initiative) definitions for most features, with minor known differences in gray-level discretization and resampling as documented in its official website (https://pyradiomics.readthedocs.io/en/latest/faq.html). To standardize voxel spacing, images were resampled to isotropic voxels with dimensions of 1 mm × 1 mm × 1 mm via B-spline interpolation. Intensity values were discretized with a bin width of 25 to mitigate image noise, as recommended in prior research36. For each phase of each patient, only the largest tumor was annotated and used for radiomics feature extraction. A total of 851 radiomics features were extracted from each annotated largest tumor ROI, yielding 1702 features per tumor across the A and PV phases. These features were grouped as follows: (1) first-order features (n = 18), including percentiles, energy, and entropy; (2) shape and size features (n = 14), covering diameter, volume, and sphericity; (3) textural features (n = 75), subdivided into categories based on Gray-Level Co-Occurrence Matrix (GLCM), Gray-Level Run-Length Matrix (GLRLM), Gray-Level Size Zone Matrix (GLSZM), Gray-Level Dependence Matrix (GLDM), and Neighboring Gray Tone Difference Matrix (NGTDM); and (4) filter-based features (n = 744), calculated by applying the coiflet1 wavelet to the ROIs for filter-transformed first-order and textural feature extraction.

Radiomics feature selection and model construction

Feature robustness analysis was performed to detect biases arising from variations in imaging acquisition and manual annotation using an image perturbation strategy37. This approach incorporates 30 distinct perturbation settings, involving rotation, volume adaptation, and contour randomization, and was executed using a custom Python pipeline as detailed in our prior work38. The intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC) and its 95% CI were calculated to evaluate feature stability, using the psych package (version 2.5.6) in R37,39. The features with a 95% CI of 0.8 or higher were designated as robust.

Following ICC analysis, near-zero-variance features were detected and removed. Afterwards, feature ranking was conducted using the minimum-Redundancy-Maximum-Relevance (mRMR) algorithm to pre-select the top 30 features, based on the commonly applied 10 events-per-variable (EPV) rule for logistic regression to mitigate overfitting40. These features were then further refined within each fold of the cross-validation using the multivariate logistic regression modeling with the Least Absolute Shrinkage and Selection Operator (LASSO). Based on the optimal regularization parameter identified via cross-validation, the final radiomics model was fitted with LASSO logistic regression on the whole development set. Ultimately, only 12 features with non-zero coefficients were retained (Supplementary Table 3). Voxelwise maps were generated by extracting features from 3D ROIs using PyRadiomics’ voxelBasedFeatureExtraction function with default parameter settings, followed by spatial normalization to isotropic resolution (1×1×1 mm³).

A radiomics signature was then derived from the radiomics model by linearly combining each selected feature weighted by its non-zero LASSO coefficient, encapsulating the comprehensive tumor image characteristics as an independent predictor. For comparative purposes, a clinical model was developed based on some easily available and commonly used parameters, including tumor diameter, multifocality, ALBI (albumin-bilirubin) score, AFP, and liver cirrhosis status. To assess the added value of radiomics features, the clinical features and the radiomics signature were integrated into a combined radiomics + clinical model. Both the clinical and radiomics + clinical models were fitted via binomial logistic regression, employing backward feature selection based on Akaike’s information criterion41.

Training and evaluation of the classification models for tumor micronecrosis

Model training and tuning of the radiomics model were conducted using a stratified 5-fold cross-validation framework, with the development set randomly divided into five partitions, stratified by CECT slice spacing. The clinical and radiomics + clinical models were trained directly on the development set using multivariate logistic regression. To mitigate bias from class imbalance, all models incorporated inverse probability weighting during fitting.

Survival analyses of the radiomics signature

Patients were classified into radiomics-predicted micronecrosis(-) and radiomics-predicted micronecrosis(+) groups based on the radiomics signature, with a threshold of 0.43 determined via Youden’s index. Kaplan–Meier (KM) curves were generated to assess the prognostic value of the radiomics signature for OS, measured from the date of surgery to death. Patients who had undergone liver transplantation were excluded from survival analyses.

CyTOF data acquisition and analysis

The matching CyTOF data of patients in the development set were obtained from our previously published study to explore the relationship between peripheral immunological profiles and the radiomics model42. The X-shift clustering algorithm was performed to classify cells into distinct phenotypes. Furthermore, the clusters were annotated based on the expression patterns of markers43.

Statistical analyses

Categorical variables were summarized as frequencies and percentages, and continuous variables were expressed as medians with interquartile ranges (IQR). Univariate and multivariate logistic regression analyses identified independent risk factors for tumor micronecrosis. Model performance was primarily assessed using the area under the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve (AUC), along with sensitivity, specificity, and accuracy metrics. Calibration curves were plotted to compare predicted against observed values, and decision curves evaluated the clinical utility of the models. Survival differences between groups were analyzed using the log-rank test. Spearman correlation analysis was used to evaluate the correlation between the median expression of CyTOF markers and radiomics features. Statistical analyses were performed using R 4.3.2, SPSS v26.0 (IBM, Armonk, NY), Python v3.7.13, and Prism 8.0 (GraphPad Software Inc., San Diego, CA). A two-tailed P-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

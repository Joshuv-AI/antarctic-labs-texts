Machine Proofs for Adams Differentials and Extension Problems Among CW Spectra
Weinan Lin, Guozhen Wang and Zhouli Xu

Abstract
. In this document, we describe the process of obtaining numerous Adams differentials and extensions using computational methods, as well as how to interpret the dataset uploaded to Zenodo [ 6 ] . Detailed proofs of the machine-generated results are also provided. The dataset includes information on 49 CW spectra, 180 maps, and 61 cofiber sequences. Leveraging these results, and with the addition of some ad hoc arguments derived through human insight, we successfully resolved the Last Kervaire Invariant Problem in dimension 126 [ 7 ] .



1.
Introduction

In this project, we first compute the Adams
E_{2}
-pages and maps for many CW spectra using the computer program
./Adams
, developed by the first author
[
4
]
. All relevant spectra and maps have been uploaded to the Zenodo repository
[
6
]
and are described in detail in Section
2
. Additionally, another program,
./ss
, also developed by the first author
[
4
]
, computes Adams differentials and extensions. The resulting data are included in the Zenodo repository and described in Section
3
.

The program
./ss
also generates proofs for all computed results, which are organized into a comprehensive resource we refer to as the Table of Proofs. This program partially employs the Generalized Leibniz Rule and the Generalized Mahowald Trick—two theorems established in the paper
[
7
]
addressing the Last Kervaire Invariant Problem. These theorems enable the program to derive new differentials and extensions from previously computed ones. Detailed instructions on how to interpret and navigate the Table of Proofs are provided in Section
4
.

In Section
5
, we present several charts and tables of machine-generated Adams differentials that are particularly relevant to the solution of the Last Kervaire Invariant Problem.

Notation 2.1
.

In the table of generators, a row with
id=i
is the
i
’th generator of the
E_{2}
-page of

if

if

is a module over some ring spectrum (in our dataset there are only two ring spectra

and
tmf

are the degrees of the generators. The column

is the latex name we give to the
i
’th generator. However, currently not all generators have latex names. The column
name
might be sometimes vacant (filled with
[NULL]
).

Notation 2.2
.

In the table of relations, consider the column
rel
for relations. If
X
is a ring spectrum, then
0,1;1,2,5,4
denotes the relation

x_{0}^{1}+x_{1}^{2}x_{5}^{4}=0.

If
X
is a module over the ring spectra

, then
0,1,3;1,2,5,4,7
denotes the relation

x_{0}^{1}v_{3}+x_{1}^{2}x_{5}^{4}v_{7}=0

where

is the
i
’th generator of
X
and
x_{j}
is the
j
’th generator of the ring spectrum

In summary, the semicolon can be parsed as the plus sign and the monomials are sequences of sub- and upper-scripts whose lengths are even if
X
is a ring or odd if
X
is a module. The columns
stem,s
are the degrees of the relations which can be determined by the degrees of the generators.

Notation 2.3
.

In the table of basis, the column
mon
is parsed in the same way as we parse
rel
for relations. The only difference is that each
mon
is a monomial so there should be no semicolon in the column
mon
. The columns
stem,s
are the degrees of the monomials. The column
index
is
important
. When there are multiple monomial basis elements in the same bidegree, they will be indexed by
0,1,\dots,k
. We write

[i]\in\mathrm{Ext}^{\verb|s,stem+s|}(\verb|X|)

to denote the monomial basis element with index
i
in
\mathrm{Ext}^{\verb|s,stem+s|}(\verb|X|)
. In the Table of Proofs (see Section
4
), the element will be written as

in the
info
column. We can also write
[i_{1},i_{2},...]
to denote the linear combination of monomials with indices

and
[]
to denote the zero element. The

differentials of the basis elements in the format

we just described but in degree

differential is trivial. If it is filled with

differential is not calculated yet by the program

Remark 2.5
.

All the CW spectra we considered are connective spectra. The current programs only work well with connective spectra.

Remark 2.6
.

If there are two or more CW spectra with different homotopy types that satisfy the same naming condition, then the name represents all choices and we do not assume which one we pick. The current important input of the program is the existence of these cofiber sequences and the ones derived from these using the properties of triangulated categories. We will make assumptions on homotopy classes in the future when a new feature of the program is complete and it will enable the
./ss
program to rigorously determine if there exists at least one homotopy class that satisfies multiple constraints.

All the CW spectra we consider are known to exist in the literature. One major example is the existence of
tmf
and its 15-skeleton

\verb|CW_sigma_nu_eta_2|,

and we can get many subquotients of
\verb|CW_sigma_nu_eta_2|
and duals of these such as
\verb|CW_2_eta_nu|
. The existence of
\verb|C2h4|,\verb|C2h5|,\verb|C2h6|
is due to the strong Kervaire invariant problem for
\sigma^{2},\theta_{4},\theta_{5}
(See
[
1
]

[

]

Y__Z
, then the third boundary map is denoted by
Z__Q_Y
.

•

For key words

, the map
CW_d_e__CW_a_b_by_c
is equivalent to

\verb|CW_d_e__Q_CW_a_b_c_d_e|.

Similarly, if we have a map with name
X__Y_by_c
it means that
X
has a unique bottom cell and
Y
has a unique top cell and the map attaches the bottom cell of
X
to the top cell of
Y
by
c
. In other words, these two cells form a subquotient of the cofiber of the map which is equivalent to
Cc
.

•

For numbers
n,l,p,q
where
p<q=n-1<n<l
, the map
RPn_l__RPp_q
is equivalent to
RPn_l__Q_RPp_l
. In other words, the following three maps form a cofiber sequence.

(\verb|RPp_q__RPp_l|,~\verb|RPp_l__RPn_l|,~\verb|RPn_l__RPp_q|)

•

In plain-text names, we use the letter
m
to denote the negative sign. For example,
RP1_256__RPm7_0
corresponds to the map

{\mathbb{R}}P_{1}^{256}\to\Sigma{\mathbb{R}}P_{-7}^{0}\simeq\Sigma^{-7}{\mathbb{R}}P_{1}^{8}.

•

Note that
{\mathbb{R}}P_{0}^{n}\equiv S^{0}\vee{\mathbb{R}}P_{1}^{n}
. We use
RP1_256__S0
to denote the truncated Kahn-Priddy map, which is a composition of the following maps.

{\mathbb{R}}P_{1}^{256}\to{\mathbb{R}}P_{0}^{256}\to\Sigma{\mathbb{R}}P_{-1}^{-1}=S^{0}.

•

S0__tmf
and
X__tmf_X
are the Hurewicz maps.

All the maps except the Hurewicz maps
S0__tmf
and
X__tmf_X
we considered are maps in the cofiber sequences generated by inclusions
X\subset Y
of CW complexes, i.e., the homology maps they induce are injective, surjective or trivial.

is (when there are two candidates or more), we always get the same value for

by Leibniz rule or

by naturality.

•

Rows with
reason=`ToCs'
. This means that in the extension spectral sequence, we have tried all possible values of an extension (linear combinations of elements in the
E_{2}
-page) and the outcome is that certain element in the Adams spectral sequence should survive to the
E_{\infty}
-page and it becomes a summand of the value of the extension.

•

Rows with
reason=`OutCsI'
. This means that in the extensions spectral sequence, two permanent cycles should be the same on the
E_{\infty}
-page. Hence the difference
dx
should be hit by an Adams differential. In those rows the columns
stem,s,t
refer to the degree of
dx
in stead of
x
.

•

Rows with
reason=`CsCm'
. These are extensions obtained from some simple homotopy relations. For example, if
x\in E_{\infty}(S^{0})
is the target of an
\eta
-extension, then it should detect a
\nu
-torsion (
d_{n}^{\nu}(\eta)=0
for all
n
) because we know that
\eta\cdot\nu=0\in\pi_{*}(S^{0})
. The reader can find more property of extensions in
[
7
]
.

•

Rows with
reason=`Syn'
. This means that the differential is obtained by the Generalized Leibniz Rule from the former row. The map used in the Generalized Leibniz Rule and the conditions that the machine checked is written in the
info
column.

•

Rows with
reason=`SynCs'
. This means that the row is an extension and is obtained by the Generalized Mahowald Trick. The cofiber sequence used in the Generalized Mahowald Trick and the conditions that the machine checked is written in the
info
column.

•

Rows with
reason=`SynIn'
. This means that by the Generalized Mahowald Trick we determine that an element in the Adams spectral sequence should be a permanent cycle and become a summand of an extension.

•

Rows with
reason=`TI', `DI', `GI'
. These are essentially the same as rows with
reason=`T', `D', `G'
except that the columns
stem,s,t
refer to the degree of
dx
in stead of
x
. For consecutive rows with
reason=`TI'
the machine are enumerating possible sources of some differential (when we know
dx
is hit by some
d_{r}
differential beforehand) instead of the possible targets and the conclusion row is one with
reason=`DI'
.

Remark 4.4
.

If the reader wants to search for a proof of certain differential, then usually you need to search for the bidegree of the source of the differential with
reason=‘D’ or reason=‘T’
. Occasionally the machine knows the target should be hit by a certain differential first and deduces the differential by enumerating the possible sources. In this case you also need to search for the bidegree of the target of the differential with
reason=‘DI’ or reason=‘TI’
.

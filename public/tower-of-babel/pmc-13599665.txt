Development of soy protein isolate-based orally disintegrating films: The dual role of β-Cyclodextrin in enhancing disintegration performance and ergothioneine stability
Authors: Yaxin Zhou, Xiaoyu Du, Chanting Lv, Yachao Tian, Shutao Guo, Zengwang Guo, Zhongjiang Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPlant protein-based orally disintegrating films (ODFs) are promising but limited by slow disintegration and poor stability of incorporated actives. This study developed ergothioneine (EGT)-loaded soy protein isolate (SPI) ODFs and investigated the bifunctional regulatory effects of β-CD. β-CD exerts a dual role as both a structural modulator and a stability-enhancing additive. At 10% (w/w) dosage, β-CD remodeled the SPI network into a more hydrophilic structure, shortening in vitro disintegration time to 15.46 s and enhancing tensile strength to 12.15 MPa. Furthermore, β-CD incorporation was associated with improved retention of total antioxidant activity in EGT-loaded films during accelerated storage, likely attributable to reduced oxidative exposure via intermolecular interactions within the film matrix. Excessive β-CD (15%, w/w) induced phase separation and crystallization, impairing mechanical integrity and the observed antioxidant activity retention benefit. This work provides a strategy for developing plant protein-based oral delivery vehicles for sensitive nutraceuticals.Keywords: Orally disintegrating films, Soy protein isolate, Ergothioneine, β-Cyclodextrin, Antioxidant activity retention

Introduction

Orally disintegrating films (ODFs) are a novel delivery system that can be swallowed without water (Adepu & Ramakrishna, 2021; Irfan et al., 2016; Tian, Liu, et al., 2024). Compared to conventional tablets or capsules, ODFs offer faster disintegration in the oral cavity and earlier absorption via the oral mucosa (Costa et al., 2025; Ranmal et al., 2018). To meet the core requirement of rapid oral disintegration, the majority of currently developed ODFs are formulated with hydrophilic polymeric materials. The most widely used matrix materials are polysaccharide-based polymers, including starch and its derivatives (e.g., native starch, pregelatinized starch) (dos Santos Garcia et al., 2018; Sha, Cui, et al., 2022; Sha, Yuan, et al., 2022), pullulan (Elbadawi et al., 2021), hydroxypropyl methylcellulose (Bodini et al., 2019). In addition, hydrolyzed collagen has also been proven to possess favorable film-forming properties for ODFs fabrication (Borges & De Carvalho, 2015). However, polysaccharide-based ODFs possess low nutritional value, while protein hydrolysate-based ODFs are prohibitively expensive. Consequently, in an effort to develop ODFs that offer both cost-effectiveness and nutritional value, the utilization of plant proteins for ODF development has emerged as a key research hotspot in recent years.

Soy protein isolate (SPI) is a kind of natural plant-derived protein that is widely available, inexpensive, and has excellent film-forming properties, so it is often used to make biodegradable food packaging films (J. Jiang et al., 2023; Song et al., 2011; Tian, Sun, et al., 2024; Zhao et al., 2024). Its nutritional value exceeds that of polysaccharides due to its rich amino acid profile (Chen et al., 2026; Syida et al., 2018; Tian, Sun, et al., 2024). Despite these prominent nutritional and processing advantages, the application of SPI in ODFs is constrained by a notable limitation: SPI-based films generally exhibit slower disintegration rates in aqueous environments compared to hydrophilic polysaccharide-based films (Tian, Liu, et al., 2024), which may compromise the rapid-release performance required for ODFs. In contrast, hydrophilic polysaccharide-based ODFs commonly exhibit rapid disintegration. Shimoda et al. (2009) studied hydroxypropyl cellulose-based films, which disintegrated in water in just 12.5 s. ODFs based on hydroxypropyl methylcellulose, corn starch, and polyethylene glycol disintegrated in 43 s (Liew et al., 2012). In contrast, oral disintegrating films prepared from collagen and propolis disintegrated in more than 6 min (Borges et al., 2013). SPI-based films typically exhibit slower disintegration in aqueous environments compared to hydrophilic polysaccharide-based films (L. Chen et al., 2025). This limitation, attributed to SPI's dense and flexible structure, restricts its application in ODFs (Wang et al., 2025) and motivates the need for structural modification.

As a sulfur-containing compound, EGT was first isolated in 1909 by Charles Tanret from the fungus Claviceps purpurea (Cheah & Halliwell, 2012; Tanret, 1909). It is produced by edible fungi and cyanobacteria (Liang et al., 2024; Melville et al., 1956; Pfeiffer et al., 2011), and its low redox potential (−60 mV) confers strong resistance to autoxidation (Cheah & Halliwell, 2012; Jocelyn, 1972; Misra, 1974). A large number of studies have confirmed that EGT has stronger antioxidant activity than common endogenous antioxidants such as glutathione, and can more effectively scavenge reactive oxygen species including hydroxyl radicals, peroxyl radicals, and singlet oxygen (Asmus et al., 1996; Rougee et al., 1988; Tossounian et al., 2024). Nevertheless, the antioxidant activity of EGT-containing systems has been widely reported to decline during thermal processing and storage, likely due to oxidative degradation of EGT and other active components. β-Cyclodextrin (β-CD), a cyclic oligosaccharide with a hydrophobic cavity and hydrophilic exterior, is widely used in food and pharmaceutical systems for emulsification (Y. Tian et al., 2022), solubilization (Shixiang et al., 1998), unpleasant flavor masking (Szente et al., 2021), and bioactive component protection (Ghorbanzade et al., 2022). Our previous work showed that β-CD can accelerate SPI-based film disintegration by disrupting dense protein packing (Tian, Sun, et al., 2024). Inspired by this, we hypothesized that β-CD could exert a dual effect within EGT-loaded SPI films: acting as a structural remodeler to transform the protein network into a rapid-release ODFs platform, while simultaneously improving EGT retention during storage through intermolecular interactions within the composite matrix. This hypothesis was tested through antioxidant activity monitoring and spectroscopic characterization (FTIR, XRD); however, direct evidence for β-CD–EGT inclusion complex formation was not sought in the present work.

On this basis, this study aimed to develop a high-performance SPI-based ODFs delivery system loaded with EGT using β-CD as a dual-functional modifier. We systematically investigated the effects of β-CD addition on the microstructure, physicochemical properties, mechanical performance, disintegration behavior, thermal stability, and storage stability of total antioxidant activity of EGT-loaded SPI ODFs, and elucidated the underlying molecular mechanism of β-CD's dual regulatory role. This work overcomes a key limitation of SPI-based films—their slow disintegration—and demonstrates for the first time a dual-function role of β-CD in a protein-based ODF system. These findings offer a new strategy for utilizing plant proteins in oral delivery applications.

Materials and methods

Materials

SPI was supplied by Shandong Yuwang Ecological Food Industry Co., Ltd. (Shandong, China). EGT (MW: 229.30, purity >99%) came from Dalian Meilun Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (Dalian, China). D-Sorbitol (MW: 182.17, purity >97%) was purchased from Sinopharm Chemical Reagent Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China), and β-CD (MW: 1134.98, purity >98%) was acquired from Beijing Solarbio Science & Technology Co., Ltd. (Beijing, China).

Preparation of ODFs

ODFs were prepared using the solvent casting method, and the preparation procedure was established based on our previous research methods (Tian, Sun, et al., 2024). Prepare film-forming solutions for different ODF samples according to the formulations shown in Table 1. Briefly, 2.0 g of SPI was dispersed in 100 mL of ultrapure water and magnetically stirred at 75 °C for 10 min to ensure thorough dispersion. A control group was prepared under the same conditions. Subsequently, 10 mg of EGT was incorporated into the SPI solution and stirred for an additional 20 min. Then, 0.7 g of D-sorbitol was added as a plasticizer, and the mixture was stirred at 75 °C for 10 min. Varying concentrations of β-CD (0%, 5%, 10%, and 15%, w/w, based on dry SPI weight) were introduced into the film-forming solution, followed by stirring at 75 °C for 15 min. The resulting samples were designated as Control (without EGT and without β-CD), SEC-0 (with EGT, without β-CD), SEC-5% (with EGT and 5% β-CD), SEC-10% (with EGT and 10% β-CD), and SEC-15% (with EGT and 15% β-CD), respectively (SEC denotes Soy protein isolate-Ergothioneine-β-Cyclodextrin composite films). Finally, 6 mL of the resulting solution was cast onto a disposable Petri dish with a diameter of 90 mm and dried at 40 °C for 8 h. The obtained films were carefully peeled off and equilibrated in a desiccator (25 ± 2 °C, 55 ± 3% relative humidity) for 48 h prior to further characterization. Each dried whole film (90-mm diameter, cast from 6 mL of film-forming solution) was individually weighed using an analytical balance (0.1 mg precision) to determine the total film weight. For oral administration, a standard ODF dosage unit with dimensions of 15 mm × 15 mm was defined, which is consistent with the specimen size employed for in vitro disintegration testing. The weight and EGT content of each dosage unit were derived from the areal density and EGT loading of the corresponding whole film, respectively. Three independently prepared films from the same batch were measured for each formulation (n = 3).

Scanning electron microscopy

Based on our previous research methodology, a method for observing the surface micro-morphological structure of ODFs was established (Tian, Liu, et al., 2024). Specifically, the ODF samples were observed using a Hitachi TM3000 benchtop scanning electron microscope (SEM, Hitachi, Japan). Film samples were mounted on aluminum stubs with conductive tape, sputter-coated with gold, and observed at 500× and 1000× magnifications.

Water contact angle (WCA)

WCA were measured with a KRÜSS DSA 25 drop shape analyzer (Hamburg, Germany), following Sha, Cui, et al. (2022) with slight adjustments. Film samples (15 × 15 mm) were placed on the stage, and a 5 μL droplet of deionized water was placed on the surface. The WCA was recorded after 10 s. Three replicates were averaged for each sample.

Thickness

Film thickness was measured using a DL321025B digital micrometer (Deli Group Co., Ltd., China) with 0.001 mm resolution. Eight random spots on each film were measured, and at least three films per formulation were tested.

Moisture content

Moisture content was determined based on Tian, Liu, et al. (2024) with modifications. Surface pH

Surface pH was measured following dos Santos Garcia et al. (2020). Films were moistened with 1 mL of PBS (pH 6.8), and a pH electrode was placed on the surface after 30 s. Readings were taken after 1 min. Three replicates were performed.

Mucoadhesiveness

The mucoadhesive strength of the ODF was determined using a CT3–4500 texture analyzer (AMETEK Inc., USA) following the method described by Alaei et al. (2021) with modifications. Films were attached to the probe and contacted with a mucin-mimetic substrate (4% gellan gum +2% glycerol) wetted with 500 μL PBS (pH 6.8). A 0.98 N force was applied for 10 s before separation at 0.1 mm/s. The maximum detachment force (N) required to separate the film from the mucin-mimetic substrate was recorded by the texture analyzer and reported as the mucoadhesive force.

The tensile strength (TS) and elongation at break (EAB), two core mechanical performance indicators of the as-prepared ODFs, were tested using an automatic tensile tester (Stable Micro Systems, UK), with the experimental procedure performed following a previously reported method with minor modifications (Tian, Sun, et al., 2024). In vitro disintegration time

A modified method adapted from Tian, Liu, et al. (2024) was employed to determine the in vitro disintegration behavior of the fabricated ODFs. Film pieces (15 × 15 mm) were fixed on a frame, and 200 μL of distilled water was dropped onto the surface. The time for complete penetration was recorded. Triplicate tests were done.

Antioxidant activity

For the 28-day antioxidant activity test, all prepared ODF specimens were individually sealed in transparent polyethylene plastic bags without oxygen barrier treatment (normal atmospheric oxygen exposure). The sealed samples were placed in a constant temperature and humidity incubator set at 40 ± 1 °C, 75 ± 3% relative humidity (RH), under continuous cool white fluorescent light (illumination intensity: 1000 lx) to simulate accelerated oxidative aging. Sampling was conducted every 7 days within the 28-day storage period for DPPH and ABTS radical scavenging activity analysis.

(2026) with slight modifications. Briefly, 50 mg of film was dissolved in 1 mL water, and 100 μL of this solution was mixed with 900 μL of 0.2 mM DPPH in ethanol. ABTS assay

The ABTS radical scavenging capacity of ODF was evaluated with minor modifications to the established method (Y. Chen et al., 2026; Sha, Yuan, et al., 2022). ODF samples (500 mg) were dissolved in 10 mL of distilled water and centrifuged to obtain a clear test solution. The ABTS+ working solution was prepared by reacting 7 mM ABTS with 2.45 mM potassium persulfate in the dark for 12 h. The resulting solution was subsequently diluted with ethanol until reaching an absorbance of 0.70 ± 0.02 at 734 nm, measured fresh before each experiment. A 50 μL aliquot of the ODF solution was mixed with 1 mL of the ABTS+ solution and incubated for 10 min in the dark. The absorbance was measured at 734 nm. A standard curve was prepared using Trolox solutions at concentrations of 0, 50, 100, 200, 300, 400, and 500 μmol/L, following the same ABTS+ reaction procedure. The absorbance at 734 nm was plotted against Trolox concentration to generate a linear calibration curve (R2 > 0.999). Thermal stability

Thermal stability was analyzed using a PerkinElmer STA 6000 thermal analyzer (PerkinElmer, Shelton, USA). About 5 mg of film was heated from 30 °C to 600 °C at 10 °C/min under nitrogen (20 mL/min). Mass loss and heat flow were recorded.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses in this study were performed using IBM SPSS Statistics version 20.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). All quantitative measurements were conducted in triplicate (n = 3) for each film formulation, and data are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD). Three independent batches of each film formulation were prepared, and measurements from each batch were averaged before statistical analysis. One-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was adopted to evaluate the significant differences among different treatment groups, with post hoc comparisons carried out via Duncan's multiple range test. A probability value of p < 0.05 was set as the threshold for statistically significant differences between groups.

Results and discussion

Surface microstructure of ODFs

The surface microstructure of the ODF samples was examined using SEM, and the results are presented in Fig. 1. The pristine SPI film (control group) exhibited a highly rough and inhomogeneous surface, with abundant irregular micropores and aggregated particulate domains distributed throughout the matrix. This phenomenon may be attributed to the incomplete unfolding of the spherical SPI molecules during the film-forming process, as well as potential microphase separation occurring between the protein matrix and D-sorbitol. In contrast, the introduction of EGT alone (SEC-0 group) resulted in a markedly smoother and more uniform film surface, with a dramatic reduction in both the size of micropores and the number of aggregated particles. This distinct morphological improvement indicates that EGT can promote the unfolding of SPI polypeptide chains during film formation, which facilitates the formation of a more compact and ordered molecular packing structure within the protein matrix. This finding is consistent with the previous report by Jiang et al. (2025), who observed that EGT incorporation effectively optimized the surface microstructure of sodium alginate-lactoferrin composite films via modulating intermolecular interactions between polymer chains.

Red arrows indicate the white crystalline aggregates observed on the surface of SEC-15% samples. High-resolution images are available in the supplementary material. (For interpretation of the references to colour in this figure legend, the reader is referred to the web version of this article.)

Compared to ODFs without added β-CD, the surface uniformity of ODFs containing 5%–15% β-CD initially improved but subsequently declined significantly. As the β-CD content increased from 5% (SEC-5%) to 10% (SEC-10%), the surface uniformity of the ODFs progressively improved. Notably, the ODFs in the SEC-10% group presented an extremely smooth, dense, and defect-free surface, with nearly no residual micropores, and formed a well-organized continuous reticular network structure. This morphological optimization demonstrates that there is a synergistic interaction between β-CD and EGT in regulating the assembly of SPI molecular chains. β-CD molecules may associate with the SPI protein network through intermolecular hydrogen bonding, as evidenced by the enhanced FTIR peak intensities at 3288 cm−1 (Section 3.11). This interaction appears to inhibit the formation of micropores during film drying and contributes to the improved structural compactness and continuity of the film matrix. However, when the addition amount of β-CD reached 15% (SEC-15%), the surface microstructure of the ODFs underwent a drastic deterioration. At both magnifications, numerous bright, angular crystalline aggregates appeared scattered across the film surface. This phenomenon confirms that excessive β-CD exceeds its equilibrium solubility within the SPI/EGT system, triggering phase separation into β-CD-rich crystalline domains and protein-rich regions. These insoluble crystalline aggregates break the long-range structural continuity of the SPI network, and this microstructural damage provides direct morphological evidence for the deterioration of mechanical properties of the SEC-15% films in subsequent tests.

WCA of ODFs

Water contact angle (WCA) was measured to assess the surface wettability of the as-prepared ODFs, a parameter that directly influences water penetration and the subsequent oral disintegration performance of the films, with lower WCA values indicating stronger hydrophilicity more conducive to rapid ODF disintegration in the oral cavity. As illustrated in Fig. 2, the control group had the highest value at 63.77° (a), but we saw a clear drop in contact angle as EGT and β-CD were added. For the SEC-0 group containing only EGT, the WCA decreased to 59.69° (b), demonstrating a clear amelioration in surface wettability. This result implies that EGT participates in modulating the molecular packing arrangement of SPI chains; the abundant polar functional groups in the EGT structure can alter the assembly behavior of the protein matrix, thereby increasing its affinity for aqueous media, which is consistent with the observations reported by H. Jiang et al. (2025) in EGT-incorporated biopolymer composite films.

Water contact angle of ODF samples. Data are presented as mean ± SD (n = 3). Different lowercase letters (a–d) indicate significant differences between groups (p < 0.05).

Within the 5% to 10% β-CD loading range, WCA declined in a dose-dependent manner, falling to 51.48° (c) for SEC-5% and 36.28° (d) for SEC-10%. This marked hydrophilicity enhancement arises from the abundant hydroxyl groups on β-CD's outer surface, which form extensive hydrogen bonds with SPI and EGT while increasing hydrophilic sites on the film surface (Sha, Yuan, et al., 2022; Tian, Liu, et al., 2024). The WCA of the SEC-15% group was 35.66° (d), with no statistically significant difference from the SEC-10% group (p = 0.073, based on one-way ANOVA with Duncan's multiple range test), identifying 10% (w/w) β-CD as the most effective concentration among the tested formulations for enhancing surface hydrophilicity. The lack of further reduction in WCA at the 15% loading level can be correlated with the SEM observations in Fig. 1, where excessive β-CD led to phase separation and the formation of crystalline aggregates. These crystalline clusters may have altered the surface topography, potentially masking some hydrophilic sites or increasing surface roughness, thereby counteracting any additional hydrophilic contributions from the excess β-CD (Tian, Liu, et al., 2024). These results confirm that 10% β-CD is the optimal concentration for optimizing the surface properties of SPI-based ODFs.

Thickness and moisture content of ODFs

The measured thickness and moisture content of the various ODF samples are presented in Fig. 3. The thickness of the control group ODF was 0.03082 mm, while that of the SEC-0 group was 0.03118 mm. This indicates that the addition of a trace amount of EGT (10 mg) does not have a significant impact on the thickness of the ODFs. However, upon the addition of β-CD, the thickness of the ODFs increased significantly to 0.03655 mm (SEC-5%), 0.04136 mm (SEC-10%), and 0.0430 mm (SEC-15%), respectively. This progressive increase in film thickness can be directly ascribed to the incremental rise in total non-volatile solid content in the film-forming system with increasing β-CD dosage (Cao et al., 2023; Sha, Cui, et al., 2022; Tian, Liu, et al., 2024).

The moisture retention capacity of edible films is a core physicochemical property predominantly governed by the water-binding affinity of each component within the film matrix, which further exerts a critical influence on the mechanical stability, disintegration behavior, and long-term storage performance of ODFs (Zou et al., 2021). As presented in Fig. 3, the moisture content of the films exhibited a continuous and stepwise upward trend with the addition of EGT and graded concentrations of β-CD. The control group recorded the lowest moisture content of 22.32%, which increased to 23.83% in the SEC-0 group, and further rose to 25.67%, 28.57% and 31.19% in the SEC-5%, SEC-10% and SEC-15% groups, respectively. EGT contains abundant polar amino and imidazole functional groups, which can introduce extra water-binding active sites into the SPI matrix and strengthen the water affinity of the protein network via intermolecular interactions, which aligns well with previous findings by H. Jiang et al. (2025) in EGT-loaded composite film systems.

For β-CD, its outer surface is decorated with a large number of active hydroxyl groups, which can form extensive hydrogen bonds with water molecules, thus providing abundant hydrophilic binding sites and significantly enhancing the hygroscopicity of the composite film matrix (Tian, Liu, et al., 2024). Although severe phase separation and crystalline aggregation of β-CD occurred in the SEC-15% group as confirmed by SEM imaging in Section 3.1, the intrinsically strong hygroscopicity of the β-CD crystalline domains still maintained the continuous improvement in the moisture retention capacity of the films. The progressive increase in moisture content, together with the reduction in water contact angle and the increase in film thickness, collectively demonstrate that the incorporation of EGT and β-CD not only enhances the overall hydrophilicity of SPI-based ODFs, but also drives the fundamental reconstruction of the internal microstructure and physicochemical properties of the protein matrix.

Surface pH of ODFs

Maintaining a suitable surface pH is critical for ODFs to ensure oral biocompatibility, avoid mucosal irritation, and preserve both rapid disintegration capacity and active ingredient stability (dos Santos Garcia et al., 2020). As shown in Fig. 4, the surface pH of all ODF samples ranged from 5.55 to 6.48, falling fully within the safe pH range for oral tissues with no risk of irritation or discomfort (dos Santos Garcia et al., 2020; Yehia et al., 2009). The control group had a surface pH of 5.61, which was nearly identical to that of the EGT-only SEC-0 group, indicating that EGT exerts no significant effect on the surface pH of SPI-based ODFs. A gradual increase in surface pH toward neutrality was observed with rising β-CD dosage, with values of 5.85, 6.21 and 6.48 for the SEC-5%, SEC-10% and SEC-15% groups, respectively. This suggests the existence of an interaction between β-CD and SPI. Through molecular-level interactions or physical shielding effects, β-CD may reduce the availability of exposed acidic amino acid residues within the SPI, as suggested by the gradual increase in surface pH toward neutrality with increasing β-CD dosage.

Mucoadhesiveness of ODFs

Appropriate mucoadhesiveness can prolong the contact time between ODFs and the oral mucosa, thereby enhancing the bioavailability of the incorporated active ingredients (Alaei et al., 2021; Subramanian, 2021). The mucoadhesive force results for the different ODF samples are presented in Fig. 5. As shown in Fig. 5, the measured maximum detachment force for the control group samples was 0.54 N, which did not differ significantly from the SEC-0, SEC-5%, and SEC-10% groups. This indicates that moderate additions of EGT and β-CD do not fundamentally alter the adhesive properties of the SPI matrix. The mucoadhesive force of the SEC-15% group samples was significantly enhanced, reaching a value of 0.69 N. This is likely correlated with the changes in crystalline aggregation morphology observed on the surface of the SEC-15% samples via SEM in our previous analysis (Fig. 1). The exclusion of excess β-CD (15%) from the SPI matrix resulted in phase separation, leading to the formation of numerous crystalline aggregates and micron-scale protrusions; this increased the effective contact surface area between the ODF samples and the oral mucosa, thereby resulting in a significant enhancement of oral mucoadhesiveness.

Mechanical properties of ODFs

Tensile strength (TS) and elongation at break (EAB) are key indicators for assessing the structural integrity of ODFs. Excellent mechanical properties can effectively prevent ODFs from fracturing during storage and transportation (de Oliveira Filho et al., 2023; H. Jiang et al., 2025). The TS results for the ODF samples are shown in Fig. 6(A). The TS decreased slightly from 10.37 MPa (Control) to 9.98 MPa with EGT addition (SEC-0). However, incorporating β-CD at 5–10% significantly improved TS to 11.38 MPa (SEC-5%) and 12.15 MPa (SEC-10%), demonstrating effective reinforcement of the film structure. The initial decrease in TS indicates that EGT molecules disrupt the native interactions between SPI chains. The incorporation of β-CD subsequently reversed this trend, which may be attributed to its interaction with the SPI matrix through hydrogen bonding (as supported by FTIR analysis in Section 3.11), potentially leading to a denser composite structure that enhanced the strength of the ODF (Tian, Liu, et al., 2024). However, a marked deterioration was observed at 15% β-CD loading (SEC-15%), where TS decreased significantly to 7.96 MPa. This decline is likely due to phase separation caused by excessive β-CD crystallizing out of the SPI network, consequently disrupting the continuous film matrix. Consistent with our findings, the study by Zou et al. (2021) also reported that low concentrations of β-CD enhanced the TS of high-amylose corn starch/konjac glucomannan composite films, while higher β-CD content led to a reduction in TS.

Changes of mechanical properties (A) and disintegration time (B) of ODF samples. Data are presented as mean ± SD (n = 3). Different lowercase letters (a–d) indicate significant differences between groups (p < 0.05).

The trend of EAB variation is similar to that of TS. The EAB of the ODFs in the control group was 3.462%, comparable to that of the sample containing EGT alone (SEC-0). Upon the addition of an appropriate amount of β-CD (5%–10%), the EAB values of the ODF samples increased to 3.842% (SEC-5%) and 4.376% (SEC-10%), respectively. This result indicates that incorporating an appropriate amount of β-CD into the SPI matrix can effectively enhance the mobility of the protein chains, thereby improving the flexibility of the films. Consistent with the results observed for TS, the EAB decreased to 3.081% when the β-CD content reached 15% (SEC-15%). This sharp decline is directly correlated with the phase separation and crystalline aggregation observed in the SEM and XRD analyses. Excessive β-CD exceeds its solubility limit in the SPI matrix, crystallizing out and disrupting the continuity of the film matrix (Tian, Liu, et al., 2024). These findings are consistent with the results of Zou et al. (2021), who noted that high β-CD content limits polymer chain stretching and stiffens the film, eventually leading to structural failure.

Disintegration time of ODFs

Disintegration time is one of the most critical parameters for evaluating the performance of ODFs, and high-quality ODFs must be capable of rapid disintegration (Sha, Cui, et al., 2022). Typically, ODFs with a disintegration time exceeding 60 s are classified as slow-disintegrating, while those with a disintegration time of less than 60 s are classified as fast-disintegrating (Tian, Liu, et al., 2024). The disintegration times for the ODF samples prepared in this study are presented in Fig. 6(B). As shown in Fig. 6(B), the control group exhibited the longest disintegration time, at 31.67 s. Upon the incorporation of EGT (Group SEC-0), the disintegration time was 29.17 s. Following the addition of β-CD, the disintegration times were significantly reduced, measuring 17.38 s (SEC-5%), 15.46 s (SEC-10%), and 15.17 s (SEC-15%), respectively. These results demonstrate that all ODF samples prepared in this study meet the criteria for fast-disintegrating films. The control group relatively long period of disintegration can be attributed to the compact matrix structure formed by SPI molecular chains. Compared to the control group, the disintegration rate increased by 45.12% to 52.10% following the addition of β-CD. This is primarily attributed to the hydroxyl-rich structure of β-CD, which effectively enhances the hydrophilicity of the ODFs and facilitates the penetration of water molecules into the interior of the film matrix (Sha, Yuan, et al., 2022). β-CD essentially breaks down internal structural barriers, allowing the film to dissolve more rapidly. Consistent with the findings of Tian, Liu, et al. (2024) the incorporation of β-CD significantly enhanced the disintegration rate of ODFs. Interestingly, there was no significant difference in disintegration time between the SEC-10% and SEC-15% groups (p > 0.05). This “plateau” effect occurs because, although SEC-15% has slightly higher hydrophilicity, its greater thickness (as shown in Fig. 3) increases the path length for water penetration, thereby counterbalancing the gains in water affinity. It should be noted that the disintegration test used distilled water rather than simulated saliva. The oral cavity contains saliva components and involves mechanical movements that may affect actual disintegration. Therefore, these results represent a simplified in vitro assessment, and future studies using simulated saliva or standardized protocols would better reflect in vivo performance.

EGT loading and content uniformity

The EGT loading parameters and content uniformity of the as-prepared ODFs are summarized in Table 2. As shown in Table 2, the total weight of whole 90-mm films increased progressively with β-CD addition, from 198.5 mg (Control) to 229.4 mg (SEC-15%). This is related to the increased solids content in the film-forming solution, as β-CD provides additional non-volatile substances to the film matrix. The variation in ODF weight was highly consistent with the thickness results reported in Section 3.3, further validating the reliability of the measurements. The theoretical EGT loading was 0.6 mg per whole film (10 mg EGT per 100 mL casting solution × 6 mL per 90-mm dish). HPLC analysis revealed that the actual EGT content per whole film ranged from 0.401 to 0.458 mg across all EGT-containing groups. Correspondingly, the LE varied from 66.83% to 76.33%, and the LC ranged from 1.96 μg/mg to 2.16 μg/mg. Both LE and LC reached their peak values in the SEC-10% group, at 76.33% and 2.16 μg/mg, respectively. The significant increase in LE from SEC-0 (66.8%) to SEC-10% (76.3%) demonstrates the protective effect of β-CD on EGT during film preparation, while the slight decrease at SEC-15% (75.8%) is consistent with the phase separation observed in SEM (Section 3.1). Importantly, to reflect the actual oral dosage, we further calculated the EGT content per standard dosing unit based on the whole-film area. The EGT amounts per dosing unit were 14.19 μg, 14.58 μg, 16.21 μg, and 16.10 μg for the SEC-0, SEC-5%, SEC-10%, and SEC-15% groups, respectively. The RSD values were below 4% for all formulations (ranging from 1.97% to 3.74%), confirming good content uniformity and the reproducibility of the solvent casting method.

Antioxidant capacity of ODFs

The storage stability of antioxidant capacity of ODFs is important for preserving the activity of the incorporated EGT during storage and making sure it can work well by removing free radicals and protecting cells when taken orally (H. Jiang et al., 2025). In this study, antioxidant activity was evaluated via DPPH and ABTS radical scavenging assays, which measure the total radical scavenging capacity of the entire film matrix rather than the absolute concentration of residual EGT alone. The DPPH and ABTS radical scavenging activities of the ODF samples over a 28-day storage period are illustrated in Fig. 7A and B, respectively. The control group exhibited the lowest antioxidant activity throughout the storage period (8.67% at day 0, decreasing to 3.41% at day 28), which is attributed to the minimal inherent radical scavenging capacity of the SPI matrix (Wu et al., 2025). When EGT was added to the freshly made ODFs, there was a clear rise in antioxidant capacity. This showed that the EGT had been successfully added to the film matrix and still keeps good starting activity after the film-making process. At this point, the antioxidant activities of all the EGT-containing groups (SEC-0, SEC-5%, SEC-10%, and SEC-15%) were similar, which means that the beginning antioxidant effectiveness mainly depended on how much EGT there was (Fu & Shen, 2022). However, since DPPH and ABTS assays reflect total radical scavenging activity rather than EGT concentration specifically, initial activity values cannot be used to directly quantify EGT loading. Under accelerated storage conditions, considerable differences in antioxidant activity retention appeared among the different formulations. The SEC-0 group (with just EGT) had the quickest drop in antioxidant power, decreasing from 83.47% at day 0 to 40.86% at day 28. This rapid loss of radical scavenging capacity is consistent with the documented susceptibility of EGT to oxidative degradation, but may also reflect oxidative modifications to the SPI matrix itself under storage conditions.

The change results of antioxidant capacity of ODF samples, DPPH radical scavenging rate (A) and ABTS radical scavenging rate (B). Data are presented as mean ± SD (n = 3). Different lowercase letters indicate significant differences among formulations at the same storage time (p < 0.05).

The decrease in antioxidant activity was notably lessened across all groups containing β-CD. After 28 days of storage, the SEC-5%, SEC-10%, and SEC-15% groups retained DPPH scavenging activities of 49.86%, 60.57%, and 51.33%, respectively, compared to 40.86% for the SEC-0 group. This improved retention of antioxidant activity is consistent with the well-documented capability of β-CD to protect bioactive compounds in composite matrices (Hu et al., 2025). In the literature, such protection has been attributed to the ability of β-CD to form inclusion complexes with hydrophobic moieties of guest molecules, thereby reducing direct exposure to oxygen and free radicals via steric hindrance (Sha et al., 2022). However, since residual EGT was not directly quantified in this study and direct evidence for inclusion complex formation is lacking, it cannot be concluded that β-CD specifically stabilizes individual EGT molecules.

A similar trend was observed for the ABTS radical scavenging activity during the 28-day storage period. At day 0, all EGT-containing groups showed comparable ABTS scavenging activities, indicating consistent initial total antioxidant capacity across formulations. The control group exhibited minimal ABTS scavenging activity (12.89% at day 0), which remained low throughout the storage period (4.95% at day 28). During storage, the SEC-0 group showed a sharp decline in ABTS scavenging activity from 85.21% at day 0 to 41.74% at day 28. In contrast, the SEC-5%, SEC-10%, and SEC-15% groups exhibited significantly slower declines, retaining 53.45%, 60.68%, and 54.37% of ABTS scavenging activity after 28 days, respectively. The SEC-10% group demonstrated the highest ABTS retention, which was significantly superior to all other groups. The correlation between DPPH and ABTS results was strong, confirming the consistency of the two antioxidant assays.

Crystalline characteristics of ODFs

We used XRD to examine the crystalline characteristics of the ODF components, with the results displayed in Fig. 8. As illustrated in the figure, neither the control group nor the SEC-0 group exhibited any crystalline absorption peaks, indicating a typical amorphous structure. Furthermore, this suggests that EGT molecules are uniformly distributed within the SPI molecular network, and that the crystalline form remains consistent with that of the control group (H. Jiang et al., 2025). Upon the incorporation of β-CD into the ODF matrix, distinct crystalline diffraction peaks appeared in the samples at 5.94°, 10.02°, 11.84°, 17.58°, and 20.86°. These diffraction peaks correspond to the intrinsic crystalline peaks of β-CD. Furthermore, this indicates that the addition of β-CD promotes the transition of the ODF from an amorphous state to a composite state characterized by the coexistence of both crystalline and amorphous regions (Y. Tian et al., 2022). As the amount of β-CD increased, the peaks at 10.02° and 11.84° became more intense. At the highest concentration tested, 15% β-CD, we observed peak splitting at 17.58° and 20.86°. This usually signals phase separation and crystalline aggregation, which is consistent with what we observed in SEM images.

FTIR of ODFs

FTIR spectroscopy was employed to characterize the intermolecular interactions among the components in the ODF, providing molecular-level insights into the mechanisms underlying their performance variations. Fig. 9 illustrates the changes in the infrared absorption peaks of the ODFs. The control group exhibited characteristic SPI peaks at 2939 cm−1 (amide B), 1641 cm−1 (amide I), and 1537 cm−1 (amide II), while the absorption peak at 3288 cm−1 was primarily attributed to O—H and N—H stretching vibrations (Y. Tian et al., 2024). In comparison to the control group, there was a notable rise in the absorption peak intensity at 3288 cm−1 for the SEC-0 group. This is attributed to the abundant hydroxyl and amino groups in EGT molecules, so when they get mixed up with SPI, these groups make stronger intermolecular hydrogen bonds with other parts of the proteins, like carbonyl, amino, and hydroxyl groups. And also, the absorption peak at 2939 cm−1 of the SEC-0 group shifted to a lower wavenumber (2920 cm−1) mainly because the insertion of EGT molecules broke the inherent Van der Waals interaction between SPI molecular chains. This change made the surroundings more polar and less constrained by space around the C—H bonds, resulting in faster vibrational frequencies. These kinds of changes in the spectra prove that there is a strong interaction between EGT and the SPI matrix and not just physical mixing.

FTIR spectrum of ODF samples.

The incorporation of β-CD led to further spectral modifications, including enhanced intensities at 3288 cm−1, 1641 cm−1, and 1537 cm−1, and the emergence of new characteristic peaks at 2870 cm−1 (C—H stretching of β-CD glycosidic bonds), 1078 cm−1, and 1031 cm−1 (C—O—C/C—O stretching of the β-CD skeleton). These changes indicate strong molecular interactions between β-CD and the SPI/EGT matrix. The enhanced intensity at 3288 cm−1 is mainly attributed to the abundant hydroxyl groups on the outer surface of β-CD, which form additional hydrogen bonds with corresponding functional groups in SPI and EGT (Jiang et al., 2025; Sha et al., 2022; Tian et al., 2024). The absorption peaks at 1641 and 1537 cm−1, characteristic of SPI protein (amide I and II bands, respectively), suggest substantive interactions between β-CD and the protein backbone. The new peaks at 2870, 1078, and 1031 cm−1 are assigned to the symmetric C—H stretching vibration of β-CD glycosidic bonds, and the C—O—C/C—O stretching vibrations of the β-CD skeleton, respectively. Their appearance confirms the successful incorporation of β-CD into the ODF matrix. Furthermore, the intensity of the peak at 1031 cm−1 increased with higher β-CD concentrations, and a new absorption peak emerged at 1148 cm−1 in the SEC-15% group. This suggests that excess β-CD led to self-aggregation and phase separation, altering its crystalline packing mode. This observation aligns well with findings from SEM (showing crystalline aggregates on the SEC-15% surface), XRD (peak splitting in SEC-15%), and mechanical properties (performance decline in SEC-15%). Collectively, these results demonstrate that an appropriate amount of β-CD optimizes the structure of SPI/EGT-based ODFs through molecular interactions, whereas excessive β-CD disrupts the ODF structure via crystallization and phase separation.

Thermal properties of ODFs

Thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) was conducted to assess the thermal stability and thermal degradation behavior of the prepared ODFs, as these key physicochemical properties directly reflect the internal structural integrity of the film matrix and the nature of intermolecular interactions between its composite components (Tian et al., 2024). The corresponding thermogravimetric (TG) and derivative thermogravimetric (DTG) curves for all formulations are illustrated in Fig. 10A and B, respectively. Three distinct stages of mass loss were clearly distinguishable from the TG curves of all samples. The first stage, occurring at temperatures below 140 °C, was dominated by the evaporation of free and bound water molecules within the film matrix, alongside the initial cleavage of weak hydrogen bonding interactions between matrix components (Zhou et al., 2021). The slight increase in mass loss observed in β-CD-containing formulations at this stage correlates with their higher moisture content (Section 3.3), reflecting the hydrophilic nature of β-CD. The second and most prominent mass loss stage, spanning 200 °C to 400 °C, was assigned to the thermal decomposition of the primary film-forming constituents, including SPI polypeptide chains, incorporated EGT, and β-CD (Wu et al., 2025). The third stage, occurring above 400 °C, corresponds to the further decomposition of carbonaceous residues and the degradation of more thermally stable components.

Thermal stability of ODF samples, TG curve (A) and DTG curve (B).

The DTG curves (Fig. 10B) enabled more accurate quantitative analysis of the improvements in thermal stability conferred by EGT and β-CD incorporation. The maximum decomposition temperature (Tmax) corresponding to the main thermal degradation stage was found to increase progressively with rising β-CD loading: the control group had a Tmax of 300.28 °C, which rose to 304.62 °C for the SEC-0 formulation, 308.12 °C for SEC-5%, 310.79 °C for SEC-10%, and peaked at 314.63 °C for the SEC-15% sample. This marked enhancement in thermal stability indicates that β-CD integration promotes the formation of a more thermally stable composite film structure (Y. Tian et al., 2024). The improved thermal stability is consistent with the denser structure observed by SEM and the stronger intermolecular interactions evidenced by FTIR. These results indicate that β-CD incorporation enhances the thermal stability of SPI-based films.

Conclusion

In this study, we successfully fabricated high-performance EGT-loaded SPI-based ODFs via β-CD incorporation, and systematically characterized the bifunctional regulatory effects of β-CD in overcoming the core application bottlenecks of plant protein-based ODFs. β-CD exerts a critical dual role in the SPI-EGT composite system. On one hand, it acts as a structural modifier to optimize the protein matrix microstructure. At the optimal addition level of 10% (w/w), β-CD interacted with the SPI network through hydrogen bonding, contributing to a dense and uniform film structure. This structural optimization significantly improved the tensile strength of the film to 12.15 MPa, and reduced the in vitro disintegration time to 15.46 s, effectively breaking the inherent “disintegration barrier” of SPI-based films. On the other hand, β-CD incorporation was associated with enhanced retention of total antioxidant activity in EGT-loaded films during accelerated storage, an effect that may be attributed to reduced oxidative exposure resulting from intermolecular interactions and increased matrix density rather than confirmed specific stabilization of EGT molecules. When the addition amount of β-CD reached 15%, it exceeded the solubility limit in the SPI matrix, triggering obvious phase separation and crystalline aggregation. This phenomenon destroyed the continuity of the protein network, leading to a significant decline in the mechanical properties of the film, and also diminished the observed antioxidant activity retention benefit, thus reducing the functional stability of the ODFs. This work not only expands the high-value application of SPI in oral delivery systems, but also lays a theoretical foundation and technical reference for the design of advanced oral delivery carriers for sensitive bioactive substances in the food and pharmaceutical industries. Despite these promising findings, several limitations should be acknowledged. All evaluations were conducted under in vitro conditions, and in vivo disintegration behavior and mucosal safety remain to be verified. The stability assessment was limited to a 28-day accelerated storage period, and longer-term studies are needed. In vitro EGT release kinetics were not examined in this work, which represents a limitation of the current study. Most importantly, the present study assessed only total antioxidant activity via DPPH and ABTS assays, and did not directly quantify residual EGT concentration during storage. Therefore, the observed improvement in antioxidant activity retention cannot be definitively attributed to specific stabilization of EGT molecules by β-CD. Future studies employing HPLC or LC-MS for direct EGT quantification, alongside phase-solubility analysis, DSC, and 2D NMR, are warranted to clarify the fate of EGT during storage and the molecular-level interactions between β-CD and EGT in this system.

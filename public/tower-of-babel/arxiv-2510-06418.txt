Stochastic interpretation of quantum mechanics
Mário J. de Oliveira

Abstract
We express the probabilistic character associated to the wave function by treating it as a stochastic variable. This is accomplished by means of a stochastic equation for the wave function whose noise changes the phase of the wave function but not its absolute value, so that the norm of the wave function is strictly conserved along a stochastic trajectory. We show that the density matrix that obeys the quantum Liouville equation is the covariance matrix associated to the stochastic wave function.

II
Stochastic equations of motion

We consider a state vector
|\psi\rangle
defined on
a Hilbert space of dimension
n
,

|\psi\rangle=\sum_{j}z_{j}|j\rangle,

(1)

where
z_{j}
are the components of the state vector.
The time evolution is given by the Schrödinger
equation

\frac{d}{dt}|\psi\rangle=\frac{1}{ih}H|\psi\rangle,

(2)

where
h
is the Planck constant and

H
is a
n\times n
Hermitian matrix,
the Hamiltonian matrix.
This equation can also be written in terms
of the components
z_{j}
. Replacing (
1
)
into this equation we find

\frac{dz_{j}}{dt}=\frac{1}{ih}\sum_{k}H_{jk}z_{k},

(3)

where
H_{jk}
are the elements of the matrix
H
,
which holds the Hermitian property
H_{jk}^{*}=H_{kj}
.

If we discretize the
time in equal interval of time
\tau
and
denoting by
|\psi^{\prime}\rangle
the state vector at
the instant
t+\tau
, then we may write

|\psi^{\prime}\rangle=|\psi\rangle+\frac{\tau}{ih}H|\psi\rangle.

(4)

Next we turn
z_{j}
into stochastic variable
that obey a stochastic equation which we
establish by adding a noise into equation
(
4
). We assume the following stochastic
equation

|\psi^{\prime}\rangle=e^{i\sqrt{\tau}\xi}|\psi\rangle+\frac{\tau}{ih}H|\psi\rangle,

(5)

where
\xi
is a random variable with zero mean
and variance
\overline{\xi^{2}}=\gamma
.
It is clear from this equation that the noise
changes the phase of the state vector
|\psi\rangle

but not its absolute value.

From equation
(
5
), we find

\langle\psi^{\prime}|\psi^{\prime}\rangle-\langle\psi|\psi\rangle=o(\tau),

(6)

where
o(\tau)
stands for terms of order
strictly lower than
\tau
. Dividing by

\tau
and taking the limit
\tau\to 0
, we find

\frac{d}{dt}\langle\psi|\psi\rangle=0,

(7)

which means that the norm is strictly conserved along
the
stochastic trajectory
of the state vector
in the Hilbert space. From now on we set the norm
equal to one, that is,

\langle\psi|\psi\rangle=\sum_{j}z_{j}z_{j}^{*}=1.

(8)

The equation (
5
) can be written in terms of the components

z_{j}
of the state vector. If we let
z_{j}^{\prime}
be the
component
z_{j}
at the instant
t+\tau
then

z_{j}^{\prime}=e^{i\sqrt{\tau}\xi}z_{j}+\frac{\tau}{ih}\sum_{k}H_{jk}z_{k}.

(9)

Up to terms of order
\tau
this equation can be written as

z_{j}^{\prime}=(1+i\sqrt{\tau}\xi-\frac{\tau}{2}\xi^{2})z_{j}+\frac{\tau}{ih}\sum_{k}H_{jk}z_{k}.

(10)

Defining the increment
\Delta z_{j}=z_{j}^{\prime}-z_{j}
, we may
write

\Delta z_{j}=(i\sqrt{\tau}\xi-\frac{\tau}{2}\xi^{2})z_{j}+\frac{\tau}{ih}\sum_{k}H_{jk}z_{k}.

(11)

III
Underlying classical system

We show here that the equations (
3
) are equivalent
to the Hamilton equations of motion of a classical system
of
n
degrees of freedom, which we call the underlying
system. We denote by
q_{j}
and
p_{j}
the coordinate and
the momentum associated to a degree of freedom. They
form a pair of canonically conjugate variables, and
the Hamilton equations of motion are

\frac{dq_{j}}{dt}=\frac{\partial{\cal H}}{\partial p_{j}},\qquad\frac{dp_{j}}{dt}=-\frac{\partial{\cal H}}{\partial q_{j}}

(12)

where
\cal H
is a function of
q_{j}
and
p_{j}
.
We introduce now a transformation of variables from

(q_{j},p_{j})
to
(z_{j},z_{j}^{*})
defined by

z_{j}=\alpha q_{j}+i\beta p_{j}\qquad z_{j}^{*}=\alpha q_{j}-i\beta p_{j}

(13)

where
z_{j}
and its complex conjugate
z_{j}^{*}
are understood
as independent variables, and
\alpha
and
\beta
are
constant to be chosen. Using this transformation,
and choosing
\alpha
and
\beta
such that

2\alpha\beta=1/h
, we find the equations

\frac{dz_{j}}{dt}=\frac{1}{ih}\frac{\partial{\cal H}}{\partial z_{j}^{*}}\qquad\frac{dz_{j}^{*}}{dt}=-\frac{1}{ih}\frac{\partial{\cal H}}{\partial z_{j}}

(14)

where
{\cal H}
is understood as a function of
z_{j}
and

z_{j}^{*}
obtained by the transformation above, and
we see that
z_{j}
and
z_{j}^{*}
can be understood as a pair
of canonically conjugate variables.

To end our demonstration, it is left to choose an
appropriate Hamiltonian function
\cal H
that lead
us to the equation (
3
).
We choose
\cal H
to be of the form

{\cal H}=\sum_{jk}H_{jk}z_{k}z_{j}^{*}

(15)

Replacing this form in the first of the equations (
14
)
we reach (
3
). If we replace it in the second of the
equations (
14
) we reach an equation which is the
complex conjugate of (
3
).

The introduction of the underlying system allows to
interpret the real part and the imaginary parts
of the component
z_{j}
of the state vector as proportional
to the coordinate
q_{j}
and momentum
p_{j}
associated
to a degree of freedom. We remark that the underlying
system is not to be confused or identified with the
real quantum system and in this sense
it could be called fictitious. But then we should
also call the wave function fictitious. Therefore,
it is more appropriate to call the underlying system
an unobservable as much as the wave function is an
unobservable.

IV
Fokker-Planck equation

From the stochastic equation of motion
we may determine an equation for the time
evolution of the probability distribution

{\cal P}(z,z^{*})

of the set of variables
z_{j}
and
z_{j}^{*}
.
To this end we start by considering a
bilinear function

{\cal F}=\sum_{jk}F_{jk}z_{j}z_{k}^{*}.

(16)

The variation of this function between to
instants of time
t+\tau
and
t
is
given by

\Delta{\cal F}=\sum_{j}(\frac{\partial{\cal F}}{\partial z_{j}}\Delta z_{j}+\frac{\partial{\cal F}}{\partial z_{j}^{*}}\Delta z_{j}^{*})

+\sum_{jk}\frac{\partial^{2}{\cal F}}{\partial z_{j}\partial z_{k}^{*}}\Delta z_{j}\Delta z_{k}^{*}.

(17)

Using (
10
) and the abbreviation

f_{j}=\frac{1}{ih}\sum_{k}H_{jk}z_{k},

(18)

then we find up to terms of order
\tau

\Delta{\cal F}=\sum_{j}\frac{\partial{\cal F}}{\partial z_{j}}(\tau f_{j}-\frac{\sqrt{\tau}}{2}\xi z_{j}-\frac{\tau}{2}\xi^{2}z_{j})+

+\sum_{j}\frac{\partial{\cal F}}{\partial z_{j}^{*}}(\tau f_{j}^{*}-\frac{\sqrt{\tau}}{2}\xi z_{j}^{*}-\frac{\tau}{2}\xi^{2}z_{j}^{*})+

+\tau\xi^{2}\sum_{jk}\frac{\partial^{2}{\cal F}}{\partial z_{j}\partial z_{k}^{*}}z_{j}z_{k}^{*}.

(19)

From which follows

\frac{d\langle{\cal F}\rangle}{dt}=\sum_{j}\langle f_{j}\frac{\partial{\cal F}}{\partial z_{j}}+f_{j}^{*}\frac{\partial{\cal F}}{\partial z_{j}^{*}}\rangle

-\frac{\gamma}{2}\sum_{j}\langle z_{j}\frac{\partial{\cal F}}{\partial z_{j}}+z_{j}^{*}\frac{\partial{\cal F}}{\partial z_{j}^{*}}\rangle+\gamma\sum_{jk}\langle\frac{\partial^{2}{\cal F}}{\partial z_{j}\partial z_{k}^{*}}z_{j}z_{k}^{*}\rangle,

(20)

where the averages are obtained using the probability
distribution
{\cal P}(z,z^{*})
.

The equation that gives the time evolution of the
probability distribution is obtained from (
20
)
and is given by

\frac{\partial\cal P}{\partial t}=-\sum_{j}(\frac{\partial f_{j}{\cal P}}{\partial z_{j}}+\frac{\partial f_{j}^{*}{\cal P}}{\partial z_{j}^{*}})

+\frac{\gamma}{2}\sum_{j}(\frac{\partial z_{j}{\cal P}}{\partial z_{j}}+\frac{\partial z_{j}^{*}{\cal P}}{\partial z_{j}^{*}})+\gamma\sum_{jk}\frac{\partial^{2}z_{j}z_{k}^{*}{\cal P}}{\partial z_{j}\partial z_{k}^{*}}.

(21)

which is a Fokker-Planck equation

vankampen1981
;
tome2015
.

V
Covariances

From the probability distribution
{\cal P}
, we may determine

\langle z_{j}\rangle
, and the covariances
\langle z_{j}z_{k}^{*}\rangle
.
The time evolution of these quantities can be obtained
from the equation (
20
) and they are given by

\frac{d\langle z_{j}\rangle}{dt}=\langle f_{j}\rangle-\frac{\gamma}{2}\langle z_{j}\rangle,

(22)

and

\frac{d\langle z_{j}z_{k}^{*}\rangle}{dt}=\langle f_{j}z_{k}^{*}+f_{k}^{*}z_{j}\rangle,

(23)

and we see that the terms involving
\gamma
disappears
from this last equation. Using (
18
) we obtain

\frac{d\langle z_{j}z_{k}^{*}\rangle}{dt}=\frac{1}{ih}\sum_{l}(H_{jl}\langle z_{l}z_{k}^{*}\rangle-H_{lk}\langle z_{l}^{*}z_{j}\rangle).

(24)

Defining the density matrix
\rho_{jk}
by

\rho_{jk}=\langle z_{j}z_{k}^{*}\rangle,

(25)

we reach the equation

\frac{d\rho_{jk}}{dt}=\frac{1}{ih}\sum_{l}(H_{jl}\rho_{lk}-H_{lk}\rho_{jl}),

(26)

which is the quantum Liouville equation.
We observe, however, that from the derivation
of this equation that
\rho_{jk}
is understood
as the
covariances
of the components of the
stochastic state vector
|\psi\rangle
.
Since the norm is equal to one then

\sum_{j}\rho_{jj}=1.

(27)

Defining the
n\times n
matrix
\rho
whose elements
are
\rho_{jk}
then the equation (
26
)
can be written in the matrix form

\frac{d\rho}{dt}=\frac{1}{ih}(H\rho-\rho H).

(28)

The Liouville equation has a special type of
solution of the type
\rho_{jk}(t)=\phi_{j}(t)\phi_{k}^{*}(t)
.
Indeed, if we replace this form in equation (
26
)
we find

\phi_{j}\frac{d\phi_{k}^{*}}{dt}+\phi_{k}^{*}\frac{d\phi_{j}}{dt}=\frac{\phi_{k}^{*}}{ih}\sum_{l}H_{jl}\phi_{l}-\frac{\phi_{j}}{ih}\sum_{l}H_{lk}\phi_{l}^{*},

(29)

and we see that this is possible if

\frac{d\phi_{j}}{dt}=\frac{1}{ih}\sum_{l}H_{jl}\phi_{l},

(30)

and as long as the initial condition
is such that
\rho_{jk}(0)=\phi_{j}(0)\phi_{k}^{*}(0)

for all pairs
jk
.

If we define the vector

|\psi_{s}\rangle=\sum_{j}\phi_{j}|j\rangle.

(31)

Then using (
30
) it follows that it
obeys the equation

\frac{d}{dt}|\psi_{s}\rangle=\frac{1}{ih}H|\psi_{s}\rangle,

(32)

which is identified with the
Schrödinger equation. We call
|\psi_{s}\rangle

the Schrodinger state vector
which should not be confused with the stochastic
state vector
|\psi\rangle
given by (
1
)
which obey the stochastic equation (
5
).

It is worthwhile to compare the equations
obeyed by these two state vector. To this
end we write (
32
) in the discrete time
form

|\psi_{s}^{\prime}\rangle=|\psi_{s}\rangle+\frac{\tau}{ih}H|\psi_{s}\rangle,

(33)

which should be compare with (
5
) which
we rewrite here

|\psi^{\prime}\rangle=e^{i\sqrt{\tau}\xi}|\psi\rangle+\frac{\tau}{ih}H|\psi\rangle,

(34)

and we recall that
\xi
is a random variable,
absent in the previous equation.

The relation between the components of the
the Schrödinger state vector and the stochastic
state vector is

\phi_{j}\phi_{k}^{*}=\langle z_{j}z_{k}^{*}\rangle,

(35)

and in particular

|\phi_{j}|^{2}=\langle|z_{j}|^{2}\rangle,

(36)

and, according to the present approach,
|\phi_{j}|^{2}

is not properly a probability but is a variance associated to the
stochastic state vector, and the off diagonal terms
\phi_{j}\phi_{k}^{*}

are the covariances associated to the
stochastic state vector.

VI
Continuous space

We now consider an infinite-dimensional
Hilbert space. We assume that the wave function

\psi(x,t)
is given by

\psi(x)=\sum_{j}c_{j}\phi_{j}(x),

where
\phi_{j}(x)

where here
\hat{H}
is the Hamiltonian differentical
operator and
\psi^{\prime}
is the wave function at time

. Using the same reasoning as before, we conclude that

\frac{d}{dt}\int|\psi(x)|^{2}=0,

\int|\psi(x)|^{2}=1.

Replacing (
37
) in (

), we find

c_{j}^{\prime}=e^{i\sqrt{\tau}\xi}c_{j}+\frac{\tau}{ih}\sum_{k}H_{jk}c_{k},

where

H_{jk}=\int\phi_{j}^{*}\hat{H}\phi_{k}dx,

(42)

and
c_{j}^{\prime}
stands for
c_{j}
at time
t+\tau
.

Equation (

) becomes identical to (

)
if we formally replace

by

where here

\rho_{jk}=\langle c_{j}c_{k}^{*}\rangle,

Defining

Deriving with respect to time and using (

If we replace in (

VII
Underlying continuous classical system

In the case of continuous space we can also introduce
an underlying classical system whose equations of motion
are equivalent to Schrödinger equation. In this
case the Hamilton equations of motion are

\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial t}=\frac{1}{ih}\frac{\delta{\cal H}}{\delta\psi^{*}},\qquad\frac{\partial\psi^{*}}{\partial t}=-\frac{1}{ih}\frac{\delta{\cal H}}{\delta\psi},

(56)

where the Hamiltonian functional
\cal H
is given by

{\cal H}=\int\psi^{*}(x)H(x,x^{\prime})\psi(x^{\prime})dxdx^{\prime},

(57)

and
H(x,x^{\prime})
is given by (
48
).
Calculating the functional derivative we find

\frac{\partial\psi(x)}{\partial t}=\frac{1}{ih}.\int H(x,x^{\prime})\psi(x^{\prime})dx^{\prime}

(58)

Taking into account the relations (
48
) and
(
42
), we find

\int H(x,x^{\prime})\psi(x^{\prime})dx^{\prime}=\hat{H}\psi(x),

(59)

and we may write

\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial t}=\frac{1}{ih}\hat{H}\psi.

(60)

If we replace (
59
) into (
57
), we get

{\cal H}=\int\psi^{*}(x)\hat{H}\psi(x)dx.

(61)

This is the formula for the
quantum average

of a quantum operator associated to an observable,
in this case the energy. We are using
the term quantum average to distinguish it from the
true average obtained from a probability distribution.
The average of
\cal H
in probability is given by

\langle{\cal H}\rangle=\int H(x,x^{\prime})\rho(x^{\prime},x)dxdx^{\prime}.

(62)

If
\rho(x^{\prime},x)=\psi_{s}(x^{\prime})\psi_{s}(x)^{*}
then

\langle{\cal H}\rangle=\int\psi_{s}^{*}(x)H(x,x^{\prime})\psi_{s}(x^{\prime})dxdx^{\prime}.

(63)

Which can also be written as

\langle{\cal H}\rangle=\int\psi_{s}^{*}(x)\hat{H}\psi_{s}(x)dx.

(64)

VIII
Position and momentum

Let us write for the position and momentum
equations analogous to (
57
) and (
61
).
For the position we have

{\cal X}=\int\psi^{*}(x)\hat{x}\psi(x)dx,

(65)

where
\hat{x}=x
, and

{\cal X}=\int\psi^{*}(x)X(x,x^{\prime})\psi(x^{\prime})dxdx^{\prime},

(66)

where

X(x,x^{\prime})=x\delta(x-x^{\prime})=\frac{x}{2\pi}\int e^{i(x-x^{\prime})k}dk,

(67)

and

\langle{\cal X}\rangle=\int X(x,x^{\prime})\rho(x^{\prime},x)dxdx^{\prime}.

(68)

For the momentum, we have

{\cal Y}=\int\psi^{*}(x)\hat{p}\psi(x)dx,

(69)

where
\hat{p}=-i\hbar\partial/\partial x
, and

{\cal Y}=\int\psi^{*}(x)Y(x,x^{\prime})\psi(x^{\prime})dxdx^{\prime},

(70)

where

Y(x,x^{\prime})=i\hbar\frac{1}{2\pi}\int ke^{i(x-x^{\prime})k}dk,

(71)

and

\langle{\cal Y}\rangle=\int Y(x,x^{\prime})\rho(x^{\prime},x)dxdx^{\prime}.

(72)

The averages
\langle{\cal X}\rangle
and
\langle{\cal Y}\rangle
are understood
as the values obtained in the measurement of the position
and momentum of a particle. According to formulas (
68
) and
(
70
), they are related to the covariances of the
stochastic wave function. Let us compare with the standart
interpretation. To this end let us write
{\cal X}
as

{\cal X}=\int\psi^{*}(x)x\psi(x)dx,

(73)

and

\langle{\cal X}\rangle=\int x\rho(x,x)dx.

(74)

In the standard interpretation,
\rho(x,x)dx
is the probability
of finding the particle between
x
and
x+dx
, that is,

\rho(x,x)
is probability density distribution associated
to the variable
x
, which is thus understood as a random
variable. Here, on the other hand,
\rho(x,x)
is the
variance of
\psi(x)
, understood as a stochastic variable,
associated to a certain probability distribution.
We did not write down the probability distribution for
the present case of continuous variable. But it is similar
to that of discrete variables, which obeys the
Fokker-Planck equation (
21
).

IX
Conclusion

The probabilistic character of quantum mechanics was
expressed here by the introduction of a stochastic
dynamics for the wave function. This was implemented
by assuming that the wave function is a stochastic
variable that obeys a stochastic equation. From this
equation we show that the density matrix is a
covariance matrix. That is, the elements of the
density matrix are the covariances of the stochastic
wave functions. We used a noise that changes the
phase of the wave function but not its absolute
value. This implies that the stochastic dynamics
preserves strictly the norm of the wave function.

We have also shown that a quantum system defined on
a Hilbert space of dimension
n
can by described by
an underlying classical system of

From our results, the wave function function follows
a stochastic trajectory in phase space so that at
each time we may associate a probabilistic character
of the quantum system and not only at the instant
of measurement. Thus according to the present approach,
the collapse of the wave function is not required.

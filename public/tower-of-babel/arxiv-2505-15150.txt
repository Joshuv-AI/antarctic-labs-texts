The p p -Biset Functor of Monomial Burnside Rings I: Composition Factors
İbrahim Kaan ASLAN, Olcay COŞKUN

Abstract
We determine the complete list of composition factors, with multiplicities, of the monomial Burnside p p -biset functor ℂ ​ B ℂ × \mathbb{C}B^{\mathbb{C}^{\times}} over the complex field 𝕂 \mathbb{K} of characteristic 0. The method relies on a reduction to restriction kernels , transforming the problem into the computation of certain ℂ ⁡ [ Aut ⁡ ( G ) ] \mathbb{C}[\mathrm{Aut}(G)] -modules attached to finite p p -groups G G . We describe these modules explicitly for all G G with non-trivial restriction kernel, and identify the corresponding simple functors.

p
-biset functor; composition factor.

Introduction

The
monomial Burnside ring
of a finite group
G
, introduced by Dress
[
15
]
, is the Grothendieck ring of the category of monomial
G
-sets. Similar to the role of permutation
G
-sets, monomial
G
-sets can be viewed as bases for
\mathbb{C}G
-modules by extending the
G
-action on a monomial
G
-set

to the vector space
\mathbb{C}X
. The induced ring homomorphism

\operatorname{Lin}_{G}\colon B^{\mathbb{C}^{\times}}(G)\to\mathcal{R}_{\mathbb{C}}(G),

from the monomial Burnside ring
B^{\mathbb{C}^{\times}}(G)
to the representation ring
\mathcal{R}_{\mathbb{C}}(G)
, is called the

. This homomorphism is known to be surjective, as established by Brauer’s Induction Theorem. Explicit sections were subsequently determined by Boltje
[
3
]
, Snaith
[
17
]
, and Symonds
[
18
]
.

When the groups
B^{\mathbb{C}^{\times}}(G)
and
\mathcal{R}_{\mathbb{C}}(G)
are viewed as functors on suitable categories, the linearization map becomes a natural transformation between these functors. Using the Mackey functor framework, Boltje
[
3
]
determined a section for
\operatorname{Lin}_{G}
, referred to as
canonical induction formula
for
G
. Later, in
[
5
]
, Boltje and the second author extended this perspective by realizing
\operatorname{Lin}_{G}
as a morphism between two
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
-fibered biset functors. They proved that
\mathbb{C}\mathcal{R}_{\mathbb{C}}
is a simple functor and that
\mathbb{C}B^{\mathbb{C}^{\times}}
serves as its projective cover. Additionally, Bouc
[
8
]
demonstrated that
\mathbb{C}\mathcal{R}_{\mathbb{C}}
is a semisimple biset functor, while the fibered biset functor structure of
\mathbb{C}B^{\mathbb{C}^{\times}}
was determined in
[
13
]
for
p
-groups and in
[
7
]
generally.

For the rest, suppose
G
is a
p
-group. The linearization map

\operatorname{Lin}\colon B(G)\to\mathcal{R}_{\mathbb{Q}}(G)

from the Burnside ring

to the rational representation ring

plays a crucial role in classifying endo-permutation modules. In
[
12
]
, Bouc and Thévenaz established the following short exact sequence of

-biset functors:

Although
\mathbb{S}_{p}
has not yet been explicitly identified with a known functor, its role as the kernel of the monomial linearization map makes it a significant object of study. In this paper, we analyze
\mathbb{S}_{p}
as a
p
-biset functor and determine its composition factors explicitly. The general problem of determination of composition factors for biset functors is a challenging task. However, by applying
[
6

(see Appendix D), we reduce this problem to determining the irreducible summands of the

of
\mathbb{S}_{p}
at finite
p
-groups. Here, by a restriction kernel at a group

, we mean the submodule of all the elements in
\mathbb{S}_{p}(G)
which are annihilated by all morphisms mapping to a group of smaller order.

Our first main theorem identifies the
p
-groups for which the restriction kernel is nonzero:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
G
be a finite
p
-group. The restriction kernel
\mathcal{K}\mathbb{S}_{p}(G)
of
\mathbb{S}_{p}
at
G
is nonzero if and only if
G
is either cyclic of order
p^{k}
(for some integer
k\geq 1
) or is isomorphic to a direct product
C_{p^{k}}\times C_{p}
(for some integer
k\geq 1
).

Using
[
6
, Theorem 2.5]
, there is a bijection between the set of composition factors of
\mathbb{S}_{p}
as a
p
-biset functor and the set of irreducible constituents of the
\mathbb{C}[\operatorname{Aut}(G)]
-modules
\mathcal{K}\mathbb{S}_{p}(G)
, as
G
runs over the groups in the above theorem. The second main result of the paper determines these irreducible constituents, summarized as follows:

Theorem 1.2
.

The simple
p
-biset functor
S_{G,V}
is a composition factor of
\mathbb{S}_{p}
if and only if one of the following statements hold:

(a)

G
has order
p
, and
V
is the trivial
\mathbb{C}[\operatorname{Aut}(G)]
-module.

(b)

G
is cyclic of order
p^{k}
(for
k\geq 2
), and
V
is any non-primitive irreducible
\mathbb{C}[\operatorname{Aut}(G)]
-module.

(c)

G\cong C_{p}\times C_{p}
, and either
V
is trivial or
p
is odd, with
V
an irreducible
\mathbb{C}[\operatorname{Aut}(G)]
-module of dimension
p+1
, induced from the linear character
(\phi,1)
, with
\phi\not=1
, of the Borel subgroup of
\operatorname{Aut}(G)\cong\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
.

(d)

p=2
,
G\cong C_{2^{k}}\times C_{2}
(for
k\geq 2
), and
V
is one of the
2^{k-2}
irreducible
\mathbb{C}[\operatorname{Aut}(G)]
-modules of dimension 2.

(e)

p
is odd,

(for

), and

is an irreducible
\mathbb{C}[\operatorname{Aut}(G)]
-module of dimension

, as described in Section

.

Moreover in all cases, the multiplicity of the composition factor is 1.

In part (c), we use the notation from
[

]

Finally combining the above theorem with Bouc’s semisimplicity result from
[

]
, we obtain the complete list of composition factors of the
p
-biset functor of monomial Burnside rings.

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
G
be a finite
p
-group and

be a simple
\mathbb{C}[\operatorname{Out}(G)]
-module. The simple biset functor

p
-biset functor
\mathbb{C}B^{\mathbb{C}^{\times}}

Most of the paper is devoted to the proof of this theorem. To illustrate some of the composition factors, we include a few examples. For

C_{2},C_{2}\times C_{2},C_{4},C_{8},C_{4}\times C_{2}
or

This article is the first part of a two–paper series devoted to the structure of the monomial Burnside

Generalities on biset functors

This section introduces the foundational concepts of biset functors and establishes the tools needed for analyzing the kernel of the monomial linearization map. We refer to
[
9
]
and
[
10
]
for details and to
[
6
]
for results concerning composition factors. Although the results are more general we concentrate only on
p
-groups.

2.1
.

Definition and Notation.

Let
\mathcal{C}_{p}:=\mathbb{C}\mathcal{C}_{p}
be the
\mathbb{C}
-linear category of biset functors on the class of finite
p
-groups. In this category the set of morphisms from
G
to
H
is given by the Burnside group
\mathbb{C}B(H,G)
of finite
(H\times G)
-bisets. A
\mathbb{C}
-linear functor
\mathcal{C}_{p}\to{}_{\mathbb{C}}\text{Vect}
is called a
p
-biset functor (over
\mathbb{C}
)
. We denote the category of all
p
-biset functors by
\mathcal{F}_{p}
.

For a finite
p
-group
G
, we write
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(G)
for a set of representatives of isomorphism classes of finite
p
-groups of order strictly smaller than
|G|
. We also define

I_{G}:=\sum_{H\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(G)}\mathbb{C}B(G,H)\circ\mathbb{C}B(H,G)

where
\mathbb{C}B(G,H)\circ\mathbb{C}B(H,G)
is the subspace of
\mathbb{C}B(G,G)
generated by all compositions
\alpha\circ\beta
with
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}B(G,H)
and
\beta\in\mathbb{C}B(H,G)
. Then one has

\mathbb{C}B(G,G)\cong I_{G}\oplus\mathbb{C}[\operatorname{Out}(G)].

Here
\operatorname{Out}(G)
denotes the outer automorphism group of
G
.

2.2
.

Simple functors.

Let
F
be a
p
-biset functor. For any
p
-group
G
, the evaluation
F(G)
is a module over the endomorphism ring
\mathcal{E}_{G}:={\rm End}_{\mathcal{C}_{p}}(G)
. Furthermore if
F
is a simple functor, then
F(G)
is a simple
\mathcal{E}_{G}
-module. The group
G
is said to be
minimal
for the simple functor
F
if it is a group of minimal order with nonzero evaluation
F(G)
. It is known that minimal group for
F
is unique up to isomorphism and the evaluation
F(G)
is annihilated by the ideal
I_{G}
. In particular
F(G)
is a simple
\mathbb{C}[\operatorname{Out}(G)]
-module. Moreover by Theorem 4.3.10 of
[
9
]
, there is a bijective correspondence between the isomorphism classes of simple
p
-biset functors and the set of pairs
(G,V)
where
G
is a finite
p
-group and
V
is a simple
\mathbb{C}[\operatorname{Out}(G)]
-module, both taken up to isomorphims. We denote the simple functor corresponding to
(G,V)
by
S_{G,V}
.

2.3
.

Restriction Kernels.

Let
F
be a
p
-biset functor. The restriction kernel of
F
at
G
, denoted
\mathcal{K}F(G)
, identifies elements in
F(G)
that are annihilated by all maps
F(\alpha)
for
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}B(H\times G)
and
H\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(G)
. Formally

\mathcal{K}F(G):=\bigcap_{\begin{subarray}{c}H\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(G)\\
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}B(H\times G)\end{subarray}}\ker(F(\alpha):F(G)\to F(H))

is called the
restriction kernel
of
F
at
G
. Note that the name is somehow misleading since we need to consider kernels of deflation maps as well as restrictions. It turns out that these modules plays a crucial role in determining composition factors of
F
.

2.4
.

Composition factors.

By a
composition factor
of
F
, we mean a simple biset functor
S
which is isomorphic to a quotient
M/N
of two subfunctors
N\subset M\subseteq F
. The
multiplicity

[F:S]
of
S
in
F
is the maximal nonnegative integer
m
such that there is a sequence

0\subseteq N_{1}\subset M_{1}\subseteq N_{2}\subset M_{2}\subseteq\ldots N_{m}\subset M_{m}\subseteq F

such that
M_{i}/N_{i}\cong S
. Then Theorem 2.5 of
[
6
]
gives a sufficient condition to reduce the calculation of composition factor multiplicities to those of restriction kernels. We include a complete proof of this theorem in Appendix D for the reader’s convenience.

2.5
.

We note that the version in
[
6
]
works for modules over Green biset functors. Also as noted in
[
6
, Proposition 2.6]
, a consideration of a suitable cyclic submodule shows that the condition of the theorem is equivalent to the following:

Condition A.
For any
x\in F(G)
, there exists
x^{\prime}\in\mathcal{K}F(G)
and
\alpha\in I_{G}
such that

x=x^{\prime}+\alpha\cdot x.

Remark 2.6
.

This theorem can be applied to the problem of determining the composition factors of
F
in two steps.

Step 1.

Evaluate
\mathcal{K}F(G)
for each
G
and prove Condition A for
F
.

Step 2.

Determine the irreducible summands of the
\mathbb{C}[\operatorname{Out}(G)]
-module
\mathcal{K}F(G)
, whenever it is nonzero.

The second step depends on the structure of
\mathcal{K}F(G)
. For the first step we use Bouc’s decompositions of

-biset functors given in
[
10
]
to reduce the problem to simpler cases. We recollect necessary background without details. Following
[
10
]
, for a finite
p
-group

, we write

\varphi_{1}^{G}=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}X\leq G,M\unlhd G\\
M\leq\Phi(G)\leq X\end{subarray}}|X|\mu(X,G)\mu_{\unlhd G}(1,M)\operatorname{Ind}\operatorname{Inf}_{X/M}^{G}\operatorname{Def}\operatorname{Res}^{G}_{X/M}.

In the above sum,
\Phi(G)
denotes the Frattini subgroup of

,
\mu(\cdot,\cdot)
(resp.
\mu_{\unlhd G}(\cdot,\cdot)
) denotes the Mobius function
of the poset of subgroups (resp. of normal subgroups) of
G
.
By
[
10
, Theorem 4.8]
, this is an idempotent of the endomorphism ring
\mathbb{C}B(G\times G)
and
by Corollary 3.7 of
[
10
]
we have

•

\operatorname{Res}^{G}_{H}\varphi^{G}_{1}=0
for any
H<G
and

•

\operatorname{Def}^{G}_{G/M}\varphi^{G}_{1}=0
for any
M\unlhd G
with
M\cap\Phi(G)\not=1
.

For a given
p
-biset functor
F
, we define

\delta_{\Phi}F(G):=\varphi^{G}_{1}F(G).

Then by Proposition 5.2 of
[
10
]
, the elements

of
F(G)
contained in
\delta_{\Phi}F(G)
are characterized by the following two conditions:

\displaystyle\operatorname{Res}^{G}_{H}u

\displaystyle=0\ \text{for all }H<G\text{ and}

(1)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Def}^{G}_{G/M}u

\displaystyle=0\text{ for all }M\unlhd G,M\cap\Phi(G)\not=1.

(2)

In particular we obtain

\mathcal{K}F(G)\subseteq\delta_{\Phi}F(G)

and hence we may start calculations for
\mathcal{K}F(G)
by determining
\delta_{\Phi}F(G)
. Moreover since any

can be written as
x=\varphi^{G}_{1}x+(1-\varphi^{G}_{1})x
and since
(1-\varphi^{G}_{1})\in I_{G}

\delta_{\Phi}F(G)
.

2.7
.

One may also consider the action of the idempotent
\tilde{e}_{G}^{G}
, as given in
[
9
]
. This is the image of the primitive idempotent
e_{G}^{G}
of the Burnside ring
\mathbb{C}B(G)
in
\mathbb{C}B(G\times G)
as constructed in Section 2.12 of
[
10
]
. By Lemma 2.18 of the same paper, for any biset functor
F
and finite group

, we have

\tilde{e}_{G}^{G}F(G)=\bigcap_{H<G}\ker(\operatorname{Res}^{G}_{H}:F(G)\to F(H)).

Hence we have

\mathcal{K}F(G)\subseteq\delta_{\Phi}F(G)\subseteq\tilde{e}_{G}^{G}F(G)

3
Monomial Linearization Map and its Kernel

This section introduces the monomial Burnside ring and defines the monomial linearization map, a key homomorphism between the monomial Burnside ring and the complex character ring. We also describe the kernel of this map by determining a primitive idempotent basis.

3.1
.

Definitions.

Let
A
be an abelian group and
X
be an
A\times G
-set. We call
X
an
A
-fibered
G
-set
if the
A
-action on
X
is free with finitely many orbits. Together with
A\times G
-equivariant functions, the class of all
A
-fibered
G
-sets forms a category, written
{}_{G}\operatorname{set}^{A}
. Disjoint union of two
A
-fibered
G
-sets is an
A
-fibered
G
-set in an obvious way and it induces a categorical product on
{}_{G}\operatorname{set}^{A}
. There is also a tensor product defined on
A
-fibered
G
-sets. Given
X,Y\in{}_{G}\operatorname{set}^{A}
, the cartesian product
X\times Y
becomes an
A
-set via

a\cdot(x,y)=(a\cdot x,a^{-1}\cdot y)

for
a\in A
and
(x,y)\in X\times Y
. We denote the
A
-orbit containing
(x,y)
by
x\otimes y
and write
X\otimes Y
for the set of all
A
-orbits in
X\times Y
. Then we make
X\otimes Y
an
A\times G
-set via

(a,g)\cdot x\otimes y:=agx\otimes gy.

It is straightforward to show that this is a categorical product on
{}_{G}\operatorname{set}^{A}
.

An
A
-fibered
G
-set
X
is said to be
transitive
if the group
G
acts on the set of
A
-orbits transitively. Let
X\in{}_{G}\operatorname{set}^{A}
be transitive. Fix
x\in X
and let
H
be the stabilizer of the
A
-orbit
Ax
of
x
in
G
. Then for any
h\in H
, there is an element
\phi(h)\in A
such that
h\cdot x=\phi(h)\cdot x
is satisfied. The function
\phi\colon H\to A
is a homomorphism. We call the pair
(H,\phi)
the
stabilizing pair
of
x\in X
. If
y\in X
is given, then the stabilizing pair of
y
is
G
-conjugate to
(H,\phi)
.

Denote by
\mathcal{M}_{G}^{A}
the the set of all such pairs. The group
G
acts on
\mathcal{M}_{G}^{A}
via conjugation:
{}^{g}(H,\phi)=({}^{g}H,{}^{g}\phi)
. We write
[H,\phi]_{G}
for the
G
-orbit containing
(H,\phi)
and
\mathcal{M}_{G}^{A}/G
for the set of
G
-orbits in
\mathcal{M}_{G}^{A}
. With this notation, the isomorphism classes of transitive sets in
{}_{G}\operatorname{set}^{A}
are in bijective correspondence with the set
\mathcal{M}_{G}^{A}/G
.

3.2
.

Monomial Burnside Rings.

The
monomial Burnside ring

B^{\mathbb{C}^{\times}}(G)
of a finite group
G
is the Grothendieck ring of the category of
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
-fibered
G
-sets. As a
\mathbb{C}
-algebra, its basis is indexed by the set
\mathcal{M}^{A}_{G}/G
. The product can be identified as the linear extension of the following product on the basis elements. Let
[H,\phi]_{G},[K,\psi]_{G}\in\mathcal{M}_{G}^{A}
. Then

[H,\phi]_{G}\cdot[K,\psi]_{G}=\sum_{x\in[H\backslash G/K]}[H\cap{}^{x}K,\phi|_{H\cap{}^{x}K}\cdot{}^{x}\psi|_{H\cap{}^{x}K}]_{G}

Fibered Burnside rings are studied in details in Dress
[
15
]
, Barker
[
1
]
and Boltje
[
3
]
. More recently in a joint work with Boltje
[
5
]
, the second author introduced fibered bisets and fibered biset functors. In this paper we are only interested in the case where
A=\mathbb{C}^{\times}
is the group of nonzero complex numbers. In this case, we call a
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
-fibered
G
-set a
monomial
G
-set
and write
\mathbb{C}B^{\mathbb{C}^{\times}}(G)=\mathbb{C}\otimes B^{\mathbb{C}^{\times}}(G)
.

3.3
.

Primitive Idempotents of
\mathbb{C}B^{\mathbb{C}^{\times}}(G)
.

The set of species of the ring
\mathbb{C}B^{\mathbb{C}^{\times}}(G)
is determined by Dress
[
15
]
and explicit descriptions of its primitive idempotents are determined independently by Barker
[
1
]
and Boltje
[
3
]
. In this paper we follow Barker’s description.

Consider the set

\mathcal{N}(G)=\{(H,hH^{\prime})\colon H\leq G,hH^{\prime}\in H/H^{\prime}\}.

We abbreviate
(H,hH^{\prime})
as
(H,h)
. Under the conjugation action of
G
it becomes a
G
-set. We write
[H,h]_{G}
for the
G
-orbit containing
(H,h)
. For each
(H,h)\in\mathcal{N}(G)
, we define

s_{H,h}^{G}:\mathbb{C}B^{\mathbb{C}^{\times}}(G)\to\mathbb{C},\quad[K,\psi]_{G}\mapsto\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}xK\subset G\\
H\leq{}^{x}K\end{subarray}}({}^{x}\psi)(h)

By Dress
[
15
]
(also by
[
1
, Lemma 5.1]
),
s_{H,h}^{G}
is a species of
\mathbb{C}B^{\mathbb{C}^{\times}}(G)
, any species of
\mathbb{C}B^{\mathbb{C}^{\times}}(G)
is of this form and
s_{H,h}^{G}=s_{H^{\prime},h^{\prime}}^{G}
if and only if
(H,h)
is
G
-conjugate to
(H^{\prime},h^{\prime})
. Hence the set
\mathcal{N}(G)/G
of
G
-orbits in
\mathcal{N}(G)
is in bijection with the set of all species of
\mathbb{C}B^{\mathbb{C}^{\times}}(G)
. By the general theory, for each species
s_{H,h}^{G}
, there is a primitive idempotent
e_{H,h}^{G}
such that

\displaystyle s_{H,h}^{G}(e_{K,k}^{G})

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}1&(K,k)=_{G}(H,h)\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

The following formula for primitive idempotents can found in
[
1
, Theorem 5.2]
.

Theorem 3.4

(Barker)
.

For each
(H,h)\in\mathcal{N}(G)
, we have

e_{H,h}^{G}=\frac{1}{|N_{G}(H,h)|}\sum_{[K,\psi]_{G}\in\mathcal{M}_{G}/G}|K|\mu_{G}(K,\psi;H,h)[K,\psi]_{G}

Here
N_{G}(H,h)
denotes the stabilizer of
(H,h)
in
G
and
\mu_{G}(K,\psi;H,h)
is the monomial Mobius function defined in
[
1
, Section 5]
. We do not need the explicit definition. Note that we have
|\mathcal{M}_{G}/G|=|\mathcal{N}(G)/G|
. In
[
7
]
, there is another description of primitive idempotents of
\mathbb{C}B^{\mathbb{C}^{\times}}(G)
parameterized by the set
\mathcal{M}_{G}/G
.

3.5
.

Monomial linearization map.

We denote by
\mathcal{R}_{\mathbb{C}}(G)
the complex character ring of
G
. As an abelian group, it is free on the set
\operatorname{Irr}(G)
of irreducible characters of
G
. The product is given by the product of characters. The monomial linearization map translates the combinatorial structure of monomial
G
-sets into the representation-theoretic framework of
\mathbb{C}G
-modules by associating
(H,\phi)
to the induced character
\operatorname{Ind}_{H}^{G}\phi
. More precisely, the
monomial linearization map

\operatorname{Lin}_{G}\colon B^{\mathbb{C}^{\times}}(G)\to\mathcal{R}_{\mathbb{C}}(G)

is defined as the linear extension of the map

\operatorname{Lin}_{G}([H,\varphi]_{G})=\operatorname{Ind}_{H}^{G}\varphi.

This map is surjective by Brauer’s Induction Theorem. In this paper we are interested in the kernel of
\operatorname{Lin}_{G}
over
\mathbb{C}
. It is possible to define
\operatorname{Lin}_{G}
as a functor
{}_{G}\text{set}^{\mathbb{C}^{\times}}\to{}_{\mathbb{C}G}\text{mod}
by mapping a monomial
G
-set
X
to the monomial
\mathbb{C}G
-module
\mathbb{C}X
. Then
\operatorname{Lin}_{G}
is the map induced by this functor on the Grothendieck rings of these categories.

The following proposition evaluates images of primitive idempotents under
\operatorname{Lin}_{G}
in terms of primitive idempotents of
\mathbb{C}\mathcal{R}_{\mathbb{C}}(G)
. The result can be found in
[
4
]
, we include a proof for the reader’s convenience.

Proposition 3.6

(Boltje)
.

Let
[H,h]_{G}\in\mathcal{N}(G)/G
. Then

\operatorname{Lin}_{G}(e_{H,h}^{G})=\begin{cases}e_{h}^{G}&\text{if }H=\langle h\rangle,\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Proof.

For each
g\in G
, we need to evaluate the
e_{g}^{G}
-coordinate of
\operatorname{Lin}_{G}(e_{H,h}^{G})
. This coordinate is is given by the composition

s_{g}^{G}\circ\operatorname{Lin}_{G}:\mathbb{C}B^{\mathbb{C}^{\times}}(G)\to\mathbb{C}\mathcal{R}_{\mathbb{C}}(G)\to\mathbb{C}

where
s_{g}^{G}(f)=f(g)
for any
f\in\mathbb{C}\mathcal{R}_{\mathbb{C}}(G)
. The composite function at the basis element
[K,\psi]_{G}\in\mathcal{M}_{G}/G
is evaluated as follows:

\displaystyle s_{g}^{G}\circ\operatorname{Lin}_{G}([K,\psi]_{G})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle s_{g}^{G}(\operatorname{Ind}_{K}^{G}\psi)=\operatorname{Ind}_{K}^{G}\psi(g)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{xK\subseteq G:g\in{}^{x}K}({}^{x}\psi)(g)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle s_{\langle g\rangle,g}^{G}([K,\psi]_{G}).

Now it follows that
s_{g}^{G}\circ\operatorname{Lin}_{G}=s_{\langle g\rangle,g}^{G}
and hence

s_{g}^{G}\circ\operatorname{Lin}_{G}(e_{H,h}^{G})=s_{\langle g\rangle,g}^{G}(e_{H,h}^{G})=\begin{cases}e_{h}^{G}&\text{if }H=\langle h\rangle=_{G}\langle g\rangle,\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

∎

Corollary 3.7
.

The kernel of
\operatorname{Lin}_{G}
is spanned by primitive idempotents
e_{H,h}^{G}
corresponding to pairs
(H,h)
where
H\neq\langle h\rangle
. Explicitly,

\ker(\operatorname{Lin}_{G})=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{e_{H,h}^{G}\mid(H,h)\in\mathcal{N}(G),H\neq\langle h\rangle\}.

As mentioned in the introduction, when regarded as a morphism of fibered
p
-biset functors, the kernel of the linearization map is simple, isomorphic to
\mathbb{S}_{p}
. From now on we write
\mathbb{S}_{p}
for this kernel regarded as a (fibered)
p
-biset functor.
Combining with Corollary
3.7
, we conclude the following result.

Corollary 3.8
.

For any finite
p
-group
G
, the vector space
\mathbb{S}_{p}(G)
has a basis consisting of the primitive idempotents
e_{H,h}^{G}
of
\mathbb{C}B^{\mathbb{C}^{\times}}(G)
as
(H,h)\in\mathcal{N}(G)
runs over the
G
-orbits of the pairs
(H,h)
where
H\not=\langle h\rangle
.

Lemma 3.12

Proof.

Lemma 3.13
.

Let
G
be an abelian
p
-group. Then for any subgroup

m_{G,g}^{N}=\frac{1}{|N\cap\Phi(G)|}m_{G/\Phi(G),g\Phi(G)}^{N\Phi(G)/\Phi(G)}.

Proof.

By Theorem

(e), the deflation number on the left hand side is given by

\displaystyle m_{G,g}^{N}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|N|}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}V\leq G\\

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|N|}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}V\leq G\\
VN=G,g\in V\end{subarray}}\mu(V,G)

Similarly, the deflation number on the right hand side is

\displaystyle m_{G/\Phi(G),g\Phi(G)}^{N\Phi(G)/\Phi(G)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|N\Phi(G)/\Phi(G)|}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bar{V}\leq\bar{G}\\

where

.
The result follows by comparing the two expressions.
∎

4
Reduction to Restriction Kernel

In this section, we reduce the problem of determining the composition factors of
\mathbb{S}_{p}
, viewed as a
p
-biset functor, to analyzing the restriction kernels
\mathcal{K}\mathbb{S}_{p}(G)
. This is done in two steps. First we prove that Condition A holds for the functor
\mathbb{S}_{p}
and hence its composition factors are parameterized by the composition factors of restriction kernels of
\mathbb{S}_{p}
. This is done in this section. Then in the rest of the paper, we evaluate restriction kernels explicitly.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
G
be a finite
p
-group which is non-trivial and not isomorphic to
C_{p}\times C_{p}
. Also let
x\in\mathbb{S}_{p}(G)
. Then

x=x^{\prime}+\alpha x

for some
x^{\prime}\in\mathcal{K}\mathbb{S}_{p}(G)
and some
\alpha\in I_{G}
.

4.2
.

Outline of the Proof of Theorem
4.1
.
Let
G
be a finite
p
-group. If it satisfies the theorem, we say that
G

satisfies condition (C)
. We divide the proof of Theorem
4.1
into several steps:

1.

Reduction to abelian
p
-groups (Proposition
4.4
):
We first show that it suffices to consider abelian
p
-groups.

2.

Analysis of groups with no
C_{p}
-factor (Proposition
4.6
):
We prove that for such groups, the restriction kernel
\mathcal{K}\mathbb{S}_{p}(G)
is zero unless
G
is cyclic.

3.

Remaining cases:
We finally consider

(a)

groups having at least 2 cyclic factors of order
\geq p^{2}
(Proposition
4.7
),

(b)

groups having rank
\geq 3
and at most one cyclic factor of order
\geq p^{2}
(Proposition
4.8
),

(c)

groups isomorphic to
C_{p^{m}}\times C_{p}
with
m\geq 2
(Proposition
4.9
) and

(d)

finally the group
C_{p}\times C_{p}
(Corollary
4.13
).

4.3
.

The first step of the proof is to reduce the proof to elements satisfying
x=\varphi_{1}^{G}\cdot x
, that is, for
x\in\delta_{\Phi}\mathbb{S}_{p}(G)
. This is done by Remark 2.6. To be more precise, we suppose
x\in\mathbb{S}_{p}(G)
. By Corollary
3.7
, we may write
x
as

x=\sum_{(H,h):H\not=\langle h\rangle}\alpha_{H,h}e_{H,h}^{G}

where the coefficients are determined by the equation
\alpha_{H,h}e_{H,h}^{G}=e_{H,h}^{G}\cdot x
.
On the other hand, we can write

x=\varphi_{1}^{G}\cdot x+(1-\varphi_{1}^{G})\cdot x.

In this decomposition,
(1-\varphi_{1})\cdot x\in\mathcal{I}\mathbb{S}_{p}(G)
.
Notice that
1-\varphi_{1}^{G}\in I_{G}
, so by putting

y=\varphi_{1}^{G}\cdot x=x-(1-\varphi_{1})\cdot x

it is sufficient to prove the theorem for
y
instead of
x
. Note also that
y=\varphi_{1}^{G}\cdot y
and by
[
10
, Proposition 5.2]
, the element
y
satisfies Equation (
1
) and Equation (
2
).
Furthermore, the element
y
is contained in the span of the top idempotents, that is, idempotents of the form
e_{G,g}^{G}
for some
g\in G
, that is, the coordinate-decomposition of
y
in the primitive idempotent basis is given by

y=\sum_{(G,g):G\not=\langle g\rangle}\alpha_{g}e_{G,g}^{G}.

(3)

This is because restriction of
y
to any proper subgroup of
G
must be zero. As above, we have
\alpha_{g}e_{G,g}^{G}=e_{G,g}^{G}\cdot y
.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
G
be a non-abelian
p
-group. Then
\delta_{\Phi}\mathbb{S}_{p}(G)=0
. In particular,
\mathcal{K}\mathbb{S}_{p}(G)
also equals to
0
and
G
satisfies condition (C).

Proof.

Let
y\in\delta_{\Phi}\mathbb{S}_{p}(G)
be as in Equation (
3
).
Since
\{1\}\not=G^{\prime}\subseteq\Phi(G)
, by Equation (
2
), we must have
\operatorname{Def}^{G}_{G/G^{\prime}}y=0
. On the other hand, for any
gG^{\prime}\in G/G^{\prime}
we have

\operatorname{Def}^{G}_{G/G^{\prime}}e_{G,g}^{G}=e_{G/G^{\prime},gG^{\prime}}^{G/G^{\prime}}

since in this case
m_{G,g}^{G^{\prime}}=1
by Lemma
3.12
.
Moreover for any
g,h\in G
, we have

e_{G,g}^{G}=e_{G,h}^{G}\text{ if and only if }e_{G/G^{\prime},gG^{\prime}}^{G/G^{\prime}}=e_{G/G^{\prime},hG^{\prime}}^{G/G^{\prime}}

Therefore deflation of
y
to
G/G^{\prime}
is equal to

\operatorname{Def}^{G}_{G/G^{\prime}}y=\sum_{(G/G^{\prime},gG^{\prime})}\alpha_{g}e_{G/G^{\prime},gG^{\prime}}^{G/G^{\prime}}

Now it is clear that the above sum is 0 if and only if all the coefficients
\alpha_{g}
are zero, that is, if and only if
y=0
. Thus
\delta_{\Phi}\mathbb{S}_{p}(G)=0
, as required. The rest follows easily since
\mathcal{K}\mathbb{S}_{p}(G)\subseteq\delta_{\Phi}\mathbb{S}_{p}(G)
∎

4.5
.

For the rest of the proof, we assume that
G
is an abelian
p
-group. Using the classification of finite abelian groups, we write and fix a decomposition of
G
into a product of cyclic groups as follows.

G=C_{p^{r_{1}}}\times C_{p^{r_{2}}}\times\ldots\times C_{p^{r_{k}}}

(4)

where the integers
r_{i}
are chosen so that
1\leq r_{1}\leq r_{2}\leq\ldots\leq r_{k}
.

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
G
be as in Equation (
4
). Suppose
G
has no direct factor isomorphic to
C_{p}
. Then

(i)

G
satisfies condition (C),

(ii)

\mathcal{K}\mathbb{S}_{p}(G)=0
if and only if
G
is non-cyclic.

Proof.

Since
G
has no
C_{p}
-factor, the Frattini subgroup
\Phi(G)
of
G
can be written as a product of maximal subgroups of its direct factors, that is,

\Phi(G)\cong C_{p^{r_{1}-1}}\times C_{p^{r_{2}-1}}\times\ldots\times C_{p^{r_{k}-1}}.

It is clear from this decomposition that
it intersects all non-trivial subgroups of
G
non-trivially. In particular, by Equation (
2
) above, we conclude that for any
y\in\delta_{\Phi}\mathbb{S}_{p}(G)
, we have
\operatorname{Def}_{G/H}^{G}y=0
for any
1\not=H\leq G
and hence
y\in\mathcal{K}\mathbb{S}_{p}(G)
. In particular
\mathcal{K}\mathbb{S}_{p}(G)=\delta_{\Phi}\mathbb{S}_{p}(G)
and
G
satisfies condition (C).

Next we prove that
\mathcal{K}\mathbb{S}_{p}(G)=0
if
k
, the number of cyclic factors, is at least two. Hence suppose
G
is non-cyclic and let
T=\langle t\rangle
be a minimal subgroup of
G
. Let
y\in\mathcal{K}\mathbb{S}_{p}(G)
be as in Equation (
3
). For
g\in G
consider the sum

y_{T,g}:=\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}\alpha_{gt^{i}}e_{G,gt^{i}}^{G}.

It is clear that

(a)

\operatorname{Def}_{G/T}^{G}y_{T,g}=m\cdot e_{G/T,gT}^{G/T}
for some scalar
m
and

(b)

e_{G/T,gT}^{G/T}\cdot\operatorname{Def}_{G/T}^{G}(y-y_{T,g})=0

In other words,

\operatorname{Def}^{G}_{G/T}y=m\cdot e_{G/T,gT}^{G/T}+\text{ terms not containing }e_{G/T,gT}^{G/T}

for some scalar
m
. Hence, since
\operatorname{Def}_{G/T}^{G}y=0
, we must have that
m=0
. We calculate
m
as follows. By the explicit sum in Theorem
3.10
(e), we have
m_{G,gt^{i}}^{T}=1/p
and hence

\operatorname{Def}_{G/T}^{G}e_{G,gt^{i}}^{G}=\frac{1}{p}e_{G/T,gT}^{G/T}.

Therefore
m=(\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}\alpha_{gt^{i}})/p
. Since
m
must be zero, we obtain the following equation:

\alpha_{g}+\alpha_{gt}+\ldots\alpha_{gt^{p-1}}=0

or equivalently

-\alpha_{g}=\alpha_{gt}+\ldots\alpha_{gt^{p-1}}=\sum_{j=1}^{p-1}\alpha_{gt^{j}}

(5)

Let
u\in G
be another element of order
p
, the above calculations give

\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}\alpha_{gu^{j}t^{i}}=0

for each
j=1,\ldots,p-1
. Summing these equations over
j
, we get

\sum_{j=1}^{p-1}\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}\alpha_{gu^{j}t^{i}}=0.

Since the set

\{u^{j}t^{i}\mid i=0,1,\ldots,p-1;j=1,2,\ldots,p-1\}

is equal to the set

\{(ut^{i})^{j}\mid i=0,1,\ldots,p-1;j=1,2,\ldots,p-1\}

we may rearrange the sum as

\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{p-1}\alpha_{g(ut^{i})^{j}}\Big)=0.

By Equation (
5
), the inner sum is independent of
i
and is equal to
-\alpha_{g}
. Thus we get
p\cdot\alpha_{g}=0
.
Hence for each
g\in G
,
\alpha_{g}=0
, that is,
y=0
. In particular,
\mathcal{K}\mathbb{S}_{p}(G)=0
, as required. Finally if
G
is cyclic, then
\mathcal{K}\mathbb{S}_{p}(G)\neq 0
, as we will see in Section
5
.
∎

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
G
be as in Equation (
4
). If
G
has at least 2 direct factors of order
\geq p^{2}
, then
\mathcal{K}\mathbb{S}_{p}(G)=0
. In particular
G
satisfies condition (C).

Proof.

Let
T,U\leq\Phi(G)
be minimal subgroups of
G
such that
T\cap U=1
. Repeating the argument in the previous proof with these choices of
T
and
U
, we arrive at the same conclusion that
\mathcal{K}\mathbb{S}_{p}(G)=0
. With this at hand, the second claim holds trivially.
∎

Proposition 4.8
.

Let
G
be as in Equation (
4
). Assume that
k\geq 3
and also that
G
has at most one cyclic factor of order at least
p^{2}
. Then
\mathcal{K}\mathbb{S}_{p}(G)=0
. In particular
G
satisfies condition (C).

Proof.

To begin with, notice that the number of
C_{p}
-factors of
G
is at least 2 and that makes the proof different from the above two cases since the Frattini subgroup is either trivial or has a unique subgroup of order
p
. Let
T,U
be subgroups of
G
of order
p
such that
(T\times U)\cap\Phi(G)=1
. We want to repeat the argument in Proposition
4.6
with these choices of
T
and
U
. The only difference is in the deflation numbers. Let
g\in G
be an element such that
\alpha_{g}\not=0
. We divide the calculation into four cases:

Case (1):
Let
g\in\Phi(G)
. Then
gx\in\langle x\rangle\Phi(G)
for any
x\in T\times U
. Hence by Lemma
3.13
and Lemma
3.15
, we obtain

p\cdot m_{G,h}^{\langle x\rangle}=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }h=gx^{i},1\leq i\leq p-1\\
1-p^{k-1}&\text{if }h=g.\end{cases}

In particular Equation (
5
) becomes

(p^{k-1}-1)\alpha_{g}=\alpha_{gx}+\ldots\alpha_{gx^{p-1}}

(6)

The rest of the previous argument applies and we obtain the equality
(p^{k-1}-1)\alpha_{g}=0
. Once more, since
\alpha_{g}
is nonzero and additionally
k\geq 3
, we arrived at a contradiction. Hence
\alpha_{g}=0
which also implies that
y=0
, completing the proof of this case.

Case (2):
If
|\langle g\rangle|=p^{r_{k}}
, then
g\not\in\Phi(G)
and
gx\not\in\Phi(G)
for any
x\in T\times U
. Therefore
m_{G,gx}^{\langle x\rangle}=1/p
and the argument in Proposition
4.6
applies directly.

Case (3):
Finally if
g\not\in\Phi(G)
and
o(g)=p
, then we may choose
T=\langle g\rangle
so that
\langle g\rangle\leq T\times U
. In this case, again by by Lemma
3.13
and Lemma
3.15
, the deflation numbers are given by

p\cdot m_{G,gx}^{\langle x\rangle}=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }x\not\in T,\\
1-p^{k-1}&\text{if }x\in T.\end{cases}

Repeating the same argument once more, we get

(p(1-p^{k-1}-1)+1)\alpha_{g}=0

and hence get a contradiction since
\alpha_{g}\not=0
.

Case (4):
There is a final case where
g\not\in\Phi(G)
and
\langle g\rangle
is not a maximal subgroup. Then the deflation numbers are equal to

and calculations similar to the ones above shows that idempotents
e_{G,g}^{G}
for such elements also satisfy the assertion. We leave the necessary modifications to the reader.

∎

Now it remains to prove the assetion for groups of the form
C_{p^{r}}\times C_{p}

Proposition 4.9
.

The group
G=C_{p^{r}}\times C_{p}

satisfies condition (C).

Proof.

We need to prove that
y=\varphi_{1}^{G}\cdot y\in\partial\mathbb{S}_{p}(G)
satisfies the assertion of the theorem. Since
y
is in the span of the primitive idempotents
e_{G,g}^{G},g\in G
, it is sufficient to prove the claim for these idempotents.

First we evaluate the product
E_{g}=E_{g}^{G}:=\varphi_{1}^{G}\cdot e_{G,g}^{G}
more explicitly. By
[
9

,

\displaystyle E_{g}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}X\leq G,M\unlhd G\\
M\leq\Phi(G)\leq X\leq G\end{subarray}}|X|\mu(X,G)\mu_{\unlhd G}(1,M)\big(\Upsilon\cdot e_{G,g}^{G}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}M\leq G\\
M\leq\Phi(G)\end{subarray}}|G|\mu(1,M)\operatorname{Inf}_{G/M}^{G}\operatorname{Def}^{G}_{G/M}\cdot\ e_{G,g}^{G}

where
\Upsilon=\operatorname{Ind}\operatorname{Inf}_{X/M}^{G}\operatorname{Def}\operatorname{Res}^{G}_{X/M}
.
The above equality holds since restriction of

to any proper subgroup is zero. We also sum over all subgroups since
G
is abelian. Moreover if
M
is not elementary abelian, we have
\mu(1,M)=0
. Thus, since the Frattini subgroup is cyclic, there are only two terms in the above sum, namely for the trivial subgroup

and for the unique subgroup

of order

of
\Phi(G)
. Hence we obtain

E_{g}=(\operatorname{Inf}^{G}_{G}\operatorname{Def}^{G}_{G}-\operatorname{Inf}_{G/Z}^{G}\operatorname{Def}^{G}_{G/Z})\cdot e_{G,g}^{G}

(7)

The first biset is the identity biset. We evaluate the action of the second term. Since
Z=\langle z\rangle
is contained in the Frattini subgroup, the deflation number
m_{G,g}^{Z}
is equal to
1/p
. Moreover by Theorem
3.10
, we have

\operatorname{Inf}_{G/Z}^{G}e_{G/Z,gZ}^{G/Z}=\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}e_{G,gz^{i}}^{G}.

Combining all these, we get

E_{g}=e_{G,g}^{G}-\frac{1}{p}\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}e_{G,gz^{i}}^{G}.

(8)

For further use, we list subgroups of order
p
in
G
.
Let
T_{0}=\langle t\rangle\not=Z
be a subgroup of order
p
. Then for each
j=1,\ldots,p-1
, the element
t_{j}:=tz^{j}
generates a subgroup of order
p
, say
T_{j}
. Moreover for
i\not=j
, we clearly have
T_{i}\cap T_{j}=\{1\}
. Hence
Z,T_{0},T_{1},\ldots,T_{p-1}
is the complete list of subgroups of order
p
.

Now by the above calculations, we already have that
\operatorname{Def}^{G}_{G/Z}E_{g}=0
. Hence we only need to consider deflation to the
T_{j}
. There are three cases to consider.

Case (1):
Suppose that
\langle g\rangle
is a maximal cyclic subgroup of
G
. Then for any
j=0,\ldots,p-1
, the deflation number
m_{G,g}^{T_{j}}
is zero. Indeed in this case there is a unique maximal subgroup containing
g
, namely
\langle g\rangle
and hence we get

\displaystyle m_{G,g}^{T_{j}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}V\leq G\\
VT_{j}=G,g\in V\end{subarray}}\mu(V,G)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p}(-1+1)=0.

Hence in this case,
E_{g}
lies in
\mathcal{K}\mathbb{S}_{p}(G)
. This completes the proof of the current case.

Case (2):
Suppose
g\in\Phi(G)
. Then for each
j=0,1,\ldots,p-1
, we have

m_{G,g}^{T_{j}}=\frac{1-p}{p}

Indeed, since
g
is contained in any maximal subgroup, each maximal subgroup
V
contributes
-1/p
to the sum. Also the condition
U\cdot T_{j}=G
is satisfied only if
V
is a maximal cyclic subgroup. Together with the contribution
1
from
V=G
, the result is obtained.

Now fix
j\in\{0,1,\ldots,p-1\}
. We evaluate
j_{T_{j}}^{G}\cdot E_{g}
where
j_{N}^{G}=\operatorname{Inf}_{G/N}^{G}\operatorname{Def}^{G}_{G/N}
. Notice that for any
x\in G
, we have

j_{T_{j}}^{G}\cdot e_{G,x}^{G}=\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}e_{G,xt_{j}^{i}}^{G}.

Hence, using linearity and Equation (
8
), we obtain

\displaystyle j_{T_{j}}^{G}\cdot E_{g}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle j_{T_{j}}^{G}\cdot e_{G,g}^{G}-\frac{1}{p}\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}j_{T_{j}}^{G}\cdot e_{G,gz^{i}}^{G}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}e_{G,gt_{j}^{k}}^{G}-\frac{1}{p}\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}e_{G,gz^{i}t_{j}^{k}}^{G}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}e_{G,gt_{j}^{k}}^{G}-\frac{1}{p}\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}e_{G,gz^{i}t_{j}^{k}}^{G}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}\Big(e_{G,gt_{j}^{k}}^{G}-\frac{1}{p}\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}e_{G,gz^{i}t_{j}^{k}}^{G}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}\varphi_{1}^{G}\cdot e_{G,gt_{j}^{k}}^{G}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varphi_{1}^{G}\cdot e_{G,g}^{G}+\sum_{k=1}^{p-1}\varphi_{1}^{G}\cdot e_{G,gt_{j}^{k}}^{G}

Now summing over all
j
, one gets

\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}j_{T_{j}}^{G}\cdot E_{g}=p\cdot E_{g}+\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}\sum_{k=1}^{p-1}\varphi_{1}^{G}\cdot e_{G,gt_{j}^{k}}

(9)

We claim that the last term in the above equation is zero. Indeed, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}\sum_{k=1}^{p-1}\varphi_{1}^{G}\cdot e_{G,gt_{j}^{k}}^{G}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}\sum_{k=1}^{p-1}\Big(e_{G,gt_{j}^{k}}^{G}-\frac{1}{p}\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}e_{G,gt_{j}^{k}z^{i}}^{G}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}\sum_{k=1}^{p-1}e_{G,gt_{j}^{k}}^{G}-\frac{1}{p}\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}\sum_{k=1}^{p-1}\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}e_{G,gt_{j}^{k}z^{i}}^{G}

It is clear that the set
\{t_{j}^{k}\mid k=1,\ldots,p-1,j=0,\ldots,p-1\}
has no repetition and is equal to the set of all elements of
G
of order
p
not contained in
Z
. On the other hand
\{t_{j}^{k}z^{i}\mid k=1,\ldots,p-1,j=0,\ldots,p-1,i=0,\ldots,p-1\}
is also equal to the same set but this time each element appears
p
times. Thus each idempotent
e_{G,gt_{j}^{k}}^{G}
appears in the right hand side
1-(1/p)\cdot p=0
times. Hence the whole sum is zero. Substituting in Equation (
9
), we obtain

E_{g}=\varphi_{1}^{G}\cdot e_{G,g}^{G}=\frac{1}{p}\cdot\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}j_{T_{j}}^{G}\cdot E_{g}

(10)

In particular, the element
E_{g}
satisfies the conclusion of the theorem, as required.

Case (3):
There is a final case where
g\not\in\Phi(G)
and
\langle g\rangle
is not a maximal subgroup. This case is very similar to Case (4) of the previous theorem and once more we leave details to the reader.

∎

As a corollary of the theorem, we obtain the following partial parameterization of simple composition factors of
\mathbb{S}_{p}
.

Corollary 4.10
.

Let
S
be a simple biset functor with minimal group
H\not\cong C_{p}\times C_{p}
and put
V=S(H)
. Then the multiplicity of
S
as a composition factor of
\mathbb{S}_{p}
as a biset functor is equal to the multiplicity of
V
as a composition factor of
\mathcal{K}\mathbb{S}_{p}(H)
as a
\mathbb{C}[\operatorname{Out}(H)]
-module.

4.11
.

We need to study the case
G=C_{p}\times C_{p}
separately. First note that the above proof does not apply to this case. Although it could have been covered in Proposition
4.9
since the idempotent
\varphi_{1}^{G}
acts on the primitive idempotents
e_{G,g}^{G}
as the identity, the argument cannot be applied. Actually the conclusion of the theorem does not hold for
C_{p}\times C_{p}
. Nevertheless we still claim that the composition factors in this case are also the ones coming from the restriction kernel.

Let
\mathcal{D}
be the full subcategory of the biset category
\mathcal{C}
on the groups (isomorphic to)
1,C_{p}
and
E_{2}:=C_{p}\times C_{p}
. Then for any simple
\mathbb{C}[\operatorname{Out}(E_{2})]
-module
V
, we have

[\mathbb{S}_{p}:S_{E_{2},V}^{\mathcal{C}}]=[\operatorname{Res}^{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathcal{D}}\mathbb{S}_{p}:S_{E_{2},V}^{\mathcal{D}}]

Note that
\mathbb{S}_{p}(1)=0
and
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\mathbb{S}_{p}(C_{p})=1
. Since
C_{p}
is minimal for
\mathbb{S}_{p}
, there is a subfunctor
F\subseteq\mathbb{S}_{p}
isomorphic to
S_{C_{p},1}
. Let
M:=\operatorname{Res}^{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathcal{D}}\mathbb{S}_{p}/F
. Then
M
is nonzero only at
E_{2}
and hence composition factors of
M
are in bijection with the composition factors of
M(E_{2})
regarded as a
\mathbb{C}[\operatorname{Out}(E_{2})]
-module. We claim that

\mathbb{S}_{p}(E_{2})=F(E_{2})\oplus\mathcal{K}\mathbb{S}_{p}(E_{2}),

as
\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{C}}(E_{2})
-modules and hence
M(E_{2})\cong\mathcal{K}\mathbb{S}_{p}(E_{2})
. In particular, a simple biset functor
S_{E_{2},V}
is a composition factor of
\mathbb{S}_{p}
if and only if
V
is a composition factor of
\mathcal{K}\mathbb{S}_{p}(E_{2})
. To prove the claim, note that the sum
F(E_{2})+\mathcal{K}\mathbb{S}_{p}(E_{2})
is a direct sum since
F(E_{2})
is a simple module which is not annihilated by the ideal
I_{E_{2}}
whereas any submodule of
\mathcal{K}\mathbb{S}_{p}(E_{2})
is annihilated by this ideal. Hence it is sufficient to compare the dimensions.

By Corollary
3.7
,
\mathbb{S}_{p}(E_{2})
has basis consisting of the primitive idempotents
e_{H,h}^{E_{2}}
such that
H\not=\langle h\rangle
. The number of such idempotents is
p^{2}+p+1
. On the other hand, by the main theorem of
[
11
]
, the dimension of
S_{C_{p},1}(E_{2})
is
2p+2
. Hence for the claim to be true, the dimension of
\mathcal{K}\mathbb{S}_{p}(E_{2})
must be equal to
p^{2}-p-1
.

Now we determine a basis for
\mathcal{K}\mathbb{S}_{p}(E_{2})
. Since it is contained in the intersection of kernels of restriction maps, we have that
\mathcal{K}\mathbb{S}_{p}(E_{2})\subseteq\mathrm{span}\{e_{E_{2},g}^{E_{2}}\mid g\in E_{2}\}
. Hence it is sufficient to determine the elements in this span which are annihilated by all deflation maps
\operatorname{Def}^{E_{2}}_{E_{2}/T}
for
1<T<E_{2}
. By Lemma
3.15
, for any
g\in E_{2}
, we have

p\cdot m_{E_{2},g}^{T}=\begin{cases}0&\text{if }g\not\in T,\\
1-p&\text{if }g=1,\\
1&\text{if }1\not=g\in T\end{cases}

Hence

p\cdot\operatorname{Def}^{E_{2}}_{E_{2}/T}\Big(\sum_{g\in E_{2}}e_{E_{2},g}^{E_{2}}\Big)=(1-p)e_{E_{2}/T,T}^{E_{2}}+\sum_{g\in E_{2}-\{1\}}e_{E_{2}/T,T}^{E_{2}}=0

that is, the sum
F_{1}:=\sum_{g\in E_{2}}e_{E_{2},g}^{E_{2}}
is in the intersection kernel. Note that if
p=2
, then
p^{2}-p-1
is equal to
1
and hence we conclude that
\mathcal{K}\mathbb{S}_{p}(E_{2})
is of dimension 1. Hence the claim holds.

4.12
.

Suppose
p\geq 3
. We introduce other elements that lie in the restriction kernel. Note that by the above formula for the deflation numbers, it is clear that a sum of the form

\sum_{1\not=g\in T}\alpha_{g}e_{E_{2},g}^{E_{2}}

is in the restriction kernel if and only if the sum of coefficients
\alpha_{g}
is zero. We may use irreducible characters of
\operatorname{Aut}(T)
to produce such coefficients.

We fix our notation. Since
T=\langle t\rangle\cong C_{p}
, we have that
\operatorname{Aut}(T)\cong C_{p-1}
. Let
s
be a primitive root modulo
p
and
u
be its multiplicative inverse modulo
p
. Define
\gamma_{T}:t\mapsto t^{s}
which is a generator of
\operatorname{Aut}(T)
. Now for each non-trivial
\phi\in\widehat{\operatorname{Aut}(T)}
, define

F_{t,\phi}=\sum_{\gamma\in\operatorname{Aut}(T)}\overline{\phi(\gamma)}\cdot e_{{E_{2}},\gamma(t)}^{E_{2}}

By orthonormality of the rows of the character table, the sum of the coefficients in the above sum is zero and hence
F_{t,\phi}
is contained in the intersection kernel. Since the matrix of coefficients for
F_{t,\phi}
as
\phi
runs over all non-trivial irreducible characters of
\operatorname{Aut}(T)
is a part of the character table of
\operatorname{Aut}(T)
, its rows are linearly independent and hence the set
\{F_{t,\phi}\mid 1\not=\phi\in\widehat{\operatorname{Aut}(T)}\}
is also linearly independent and has cardinality equal to
p-2
.

We may repeat the same construction for each subgroup of order
p
in
E_{2}
and each such subgroup would produce a linearly independent set of
p-2
elements. Notice that the union set is also linearly independent since the primitive idempotents that appear for different subgroups are distinct. Hence we obtain
(p+1)(p-2)=p^{2}-p-2
linearly independent elements in the restriction kernel. Together with
F_{1}
above, which is clearly independent from the
F_{t,\phi}
, we obtain
p^{2}-p-1
linearly independent elements in
\mathcal{K}\mathbb{S}_{p}(E_{2})
as required. In particular, the set

\mathcal{B}=\{F_{1}\}\cup\bigcup_{1<\langle t\rangle<E_{2}}\{F_{t,\phi}\mid 1\not=\phi\in\widehat{\operatorname{Aut}(\langle t\rangle)}\}

(11)

is a basis for
\mathcal{K}\mathbb{S}_{p}(E_{2})
. With this result, we may restate Corollary
4.10
as follows.

Corollary 4.13
.

Let
S
be a simple biset functor with minimal group
H
and put
V=S(H)
. Then the multiplicity of
S
as a composition factor of
\mathbb{S}_{p}
as a biset functor is equal to the multiplicity of
V
as a composition factor of
\mathcal{K}\mathbb{S}_{p}(H)
as a
\mathbb{C}[\operatorname{Out}(H)]
-module.

From the proofs in this section we also obtain the complete list of all finite
p
-groups
G
such that
\mathcal{K}\mathbb{S}_{p}(G)\not=0
; completing the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

5
Calculation of Restriction Kernels I: Cyclic case

In this section we calculate the
\mathbb{C}[\operatorname{Out}(G)]
-module structure of the restriction kernel
\mathcal{K}\mathbb{S}_{p}(G)
of
\mathbb{S}_{p}
for cyclic
p
-groups
G=C_{p^{k}}
for all
k\geq 1
.

5.1
.

Notation.

Throughout this section, fix an integer
k\geq 1
and put
G=C_{p^{k}}
.
When
k=1
, the evaluation
\mathbb{S}_{p}(C_{p})
is the trivial
\mathbb{C}[\operatorname{Aut}(C_{p})]
-module, so there is nothing to prove. This is also part (a) of Theorem
1.2
. We assume
k\geq 2
.
Denote by
H_{l}:=\langle h_{l}\rangle
the subgroup of
G
of order
p^{l}
. For any
0\leq l<k
and
\phi\in\widehat{\operatorname{Aut}(H_{l})}
, define

e_{G,l,\phi}:=\sum_{\alpha\in\operatorname{Aut}(H_{l})}\phi(\alpha^{-1})\cdot e_{G,\alpha(h_{l})}^{G}

and

\mathcal{D}_{G}=\{e_{G,l,\phi}\mid 0\leq l<k,\ \phi\in\widehat{\operatorname{Aut}(H_{l})}\}.

5.2
.

Automorphism groups.

Let
H
be a cyclic
p
-group of order
\geq p^{2}
and
Z
its unique minimal subgroup. The map

\Psi:\operatorname{Aut}(H)\to\operatorname{Aut}(H/Z),\ \alpha\mapsto(\alpha_{Z}:xZ\mapsto\alpha(x)Z)

is a surjective group homomorphism. We write
\operatorname{Aut}_{Z}(H):=\ker\Psi
, which has order
p
, and obtain

\operatorname{Aut}(H)/\operatorname{Aut}_{Z}(H)\cong\operatorname{Aut}(H/Z)\text{ via }\alpha\operatorname{Aut}_{Z}(H)\mapsto\alpha_{Z}.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
G=C_{p^{k}}
with
k\geq 2
. The set
\mathcal{D}_{G}
is a
\mathbb{C}
-basis for

\widetilde{e_{G}^{G}}\mathbb{S}_{p}(G)
, on which
\operatorname{Aut}(G)
acts monomially via

\beta\cdot e_{G,l,\phi}=\phi(\beta|_{H_{l}})\,e_{G,l,\phi}

(12)

for
\beta\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)
and
e_{G,l,\phi}\in\mathcal{D}_{G}
. Moreover there is an isomorphism
of
\mathbb{C}[\operatorname{Aut}(G)]
-modules

\mathcal{K}\mathbb{S}_{p}(G)\cong\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}2\leq l<k\\
\phi_{p}\,\mathrm{faithful}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{C}[e_{G,l,\phi}]\oplus\bigoplus_{\phi\not=1}\mathbb{C}[e_{G,1,\phi}]\oplus\,\mathbb{C}[e_{G,1,1}-(1-p)e_{G,0,1}],

(13)

and
\mathcal{K}\mathbb{S}_{p}(G)
is isomorphic to the direct sum of all non-primitive irreducible

\mathbb{C}[\operatorname{Aut}(G)]
-modules, each with multiplicity
1
.

With this result, the following corollary is immediate.

Corollary 5.4
.

Part
(b)
of Theorem
1.2
holds.

Proof.

By Corollary
4.10
, the multiplicity of
S_{G,V}
as a composition factor of
\mathbb{S}_{p}
equals the multiplicity of
V
as a composition factor of
\mathcal{K}\mathbb{S}_{p}(G)
as a
\mathbb{C}[\operatorname{Out}(G)]
-module. The result therefore follows directly from Theorem
5.3
.
∎

Now we prove the theorem.

Proof.

[of Theorem
5.3
.]
The proof proceeds in four steps. We first show that
\mathcal{D}_{G}
is a
\mathbb{C}
-basis for
\widetilde{e_{G}^{G}}\mathbb{S}_{p}(G)
and determine the
\operatorname{Aut}(G)
-action on it. We then compute the deflation formula for the basis elements
e_{G,l,\phi}
, from which the isomorphism (
13
) is read off directly. Finally we verify that the summands afford exactly the non-primitive irreducible characters of
\operatorname{Aut}(G)
, each with multiplicity
1
.

Step 1.
The dimension of
\widetilde{e_{G}^{G}}\mathbb{S}_{p}(G)
is equal to the number of non-generators of
G
and it is also easy to see that the cardinality of
\mathcal{D}_{G}
is also equal to this number. Thus it is sufficient to check that the set is linearly independent.

If
l\not=n
, then for any choices of
\phi\in\widehat{\operatorname{Aut}(H_{l})}
and
\psi\in\widehat{\operatorname{Aut}(H_{n})}
, the primitive idempotents that appear in
e_{G,l,\phi}
and
e_{G,n,\psi}
are distinct. Hence there can be no linear relation between these type of elements. Hence the only possible relations are among those elements for fixed
l
. On the other hand, for a fixed
l
, the coefficient matrix for
e_{G,l,\phi}
as
\phi
runs over all elements of
\widehat{\operatorname{Aut}(H_{l})}
is equal to the character table of
\widehat{\operatorname{Aut}(H_{l})}
, and hence is invertible. In particular the set
\{e_{G,l,\phi}\mid\phi\in\widehat{\operatorname{Aut}(H_{l})}\}
is linearly independent, which proves the first claim.

For the second claim, the group
\operatorname{Aut}(G)
acts on the primitive idempotents of
\mathbb{C}B^{\mathbb{C}^{\times}}(G)
via

\beta\cdot e_{H,h}^{G}=e_{\beta(H),\beta(h)}^{G}

for
\beta\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)
and
e_{H,h}^{G}\in\mathbb{C}B^{\mathbb{C}^{\times}}(G)
. This action gives rise to a monomial action on
\mathcal{D}_{G}
. We evaluate this action explicitly: Let
e_{G,l,\phi}\in\mathcal{D}_{G}
. Then

\beta\cdot e_{G,l,\phi}=\sum_{\alpha\in\operatorname{Aut}(H_{l})}\phi(\alpha^{-1})\cdot e_{G,\beta(\alpha(h_{l}))}^{G}

Since
G
is a cyclic group, the restriction of
\beta
to
H_{l}
is an automorphism of
H_{l}
and hence
\beta\circ\alpha
runs over all automorphisms of
\operatorname{Aut}(H_{l})
as
\alpha
does. Therefore putting
\gamma=\beta\circ\alpha
we get

\displaystyle\beta\cdot e_{G,l,\phi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\gamma\in\operatorname{Aut}(H_{l})}\phi(\gamma^{-1}\circ\beta)\cdot e_{G,\gamma(h_{l})}^{G}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi(\beta|_{H_{l}})\cdot e_{G,l,\phi}.

Here the last equality holds since
\phi
is a group homomorphism. Therefore the line
\mathbb{C}[e_{G,l,\phi}]
in
\mathbb{S}_{p}(G)
is a
\mathbb{C}[\operatorname{Aut}(G)]
-submodule with character given by

\operatorname{Aut}(G)\to\mathbb{C}^{\times},\ \beta\mapsto\phi(\beta|_{H_{l}}).

(14)

Step 2.
By the transitivity of deflation maps, it is sufficient to determine the kernel of
\operatorname{Def}^{G}_{G/Z}
where
Z:=H_{1}
is the unique subgroup of
G
of order
p
. Also note that any linear relation between deflated basis elements
\operatorname{Def}^{G}_{G/Z}e_{G,l,\phi}
may be reduced to a relation for a fixed
l
. Indeed, for distinct numbers
l\not=n,l\geq 2
, we have, for
\alpha\in\operatorname{Aut}(H_{l})
and
\alpha^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Aut}(H_{n})
, that
\alpha(h_{l})Z\not=\alpha^{\prime}(h_{n})Z
. Hence the sets of idempotents that appear in deflation of basis elements for
l
and for
n
are disjoint and hence no relation between them is possible. Thus we may work with a fixed index
l
.

We claim that

\operatorname{Def}^{G}_{G/Z}e_{G,l,\phi}=\begin{cases}0&\text{if }l\geq 2\text{ and }\ker\phi\cap\operatorname{Aut}_{Z}(H_{l})=\{1\},\\
e_{G/Z,l-1,\tilde{\phi}}&\text{if }l\geq 2\text{ and }\ker\phi\cap\operatorname{Aut}_{Z}(H_{l})\not=\{1\},\\
0&\text{if }l=1\text{ and }\phi\not=1,\\
\frac{p-1}{p}e_{G/Z,0,1}&\text{if }l=1\text{ and }\phi=1,\\
\frac{1}{p}e_{G/Z,0,1}&\text{if }l=0.\end{cases}

(15)

First we assume
l\geq 2
. Note that since
G
is cyclic, there is no supplement to
Z
in
G
and hence the deflation number
m_{G,g}^{Z}
is equal to
1/p
for any
g\in G
. Hence we have

p\cdot\operatorname{Def}^{G}_{G/Z}e_{G,l,\phi}=\sum_{\alpha\in\operatorname{Aut}(H_{l})}\phi(\alpha^{-1})e_{G/Z,\alpha(h_{l})Z}^{G/Z}.

Using the above notation,
\alpha(h_{l})Z
is equal to
\Psi(\alpha)(h_{l}Z)
. Moreover for any
\kappa_{z}\in\operatorname{Aut}_{Z}(H_{l})
, we have
\Psi(\alpha)=\Psi(\alpha\kappa_{z})
. Hence the right hand side of the above equality can be rearranged as

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha\in[\operatorname{Aut}(H_{l})/\operatorname{Aut}_{Z}(H_{l})]}\Big(\sum_{\kappa\in\operatorname{Aut}_{Z}(H_{l})}\phi(\alpha^{-1}\kappa^{-1})\Big)e_{G/Z,\Psi(\alpha)(h_{l}Z)}^{G/Z}

where the first sum is over a complete set of left coset representatives of
\operatorname{Aut}_{Z}(H_{l})
in
\operatorname{Aut}(H_{l})
. Using the multiplicativity of
\phi
, the right hand side of the above equation becomes

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{\kappa\in\operatorname{Aut}_{Z}(H_{l})}\phi(\kappa^{-1})\Big)\sum_{\alpha\in[\operatorname{Aut}(H_{l})/\operatorname{Aut}_{Z}(H_{l})]}\phi(\alpha^{-1})e_{G/Z,\Psi(\alpha)(h_{l}Z)}^{G/Z}.

Notice that the coefficient is zero unless
\phi|_{\operatorname{Aut}_{Z}(H_{l})}
is the trivial character. Moreover when
\phi|_{\operatorname{Aut}_{Z}(H_{l})}
is the trivial character, it deflates to the quotient
\operatorname{Aut}(H_{l})/\operatorname{Aut}_{Z}(H_{l})
. We denote the image of this deflation under the isomorphism
\Psi
by
\tilde{\phi}
. Then the above sum becomes

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p\sum_{\alpha\in[\operatorname{Aut}(H_{l})/\operatorname{Aut}_{Z}(H_{l})]}\tilde{\phi}(\alpha^{-1}_{Z})e_{G/Z,\alpha_{Z}(h_{l}Z)}^{G/Z}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p\sum_{\alpha_{Z}\in\operatorname{Aut}(H_{l}/Z)}\tilde{\phi}(\alpha^{-1}_{Z})e_{G/Z,\alpha_{Z}(h_{l}Z)}^{G/Z}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p\cdot e_{G/Z,l-1,\tilde{\phi}}.

This proves the first two lines of Equation (
15
). The remaining cases are not difficult to deal with. We leave the details to the reader. When
l=1
, we have
H_{1}=Z
and it is easy to see that the above result holds at this extreme and hence we obtain the third and the fourth lines of Equation (
15
). The last line also follows since
e_{G/Z,0,1}=e_{G/Z,1}^{G/Z}
.

Step 3.

From Equation (
15
), the basis elements
e_{G,1,\phi}
for
\phi\not=1
together with the combination
e_{G,1,1}-(1-p)\cdot e_{G,0,1}
form a basis for the kernel of
\operatorname{Def}^{G}_{G/Z}
, and the isomorphism (
13
) follows.

Step 4.

We need to show that each non-primitive irreducible character of
\operatorname{Aut}(G)
appears on the right-hand side of Equation (
13
) with multiplicity 1. First note that, by Equation
14
, the character of the last term is the trivial character. For each
1\leq l\leq k
and
\phi\in\widehat{\operatorname{Aut}(H_{l})}
, let
\phi^{*}
be the character of
\widehat{\operatorname{Aut}(G)}
given by Equation (
14
). It is clear that if we fix
l
, then
\phi^{*}=\psi^{*}
if and only if
\phi=\psi
.

Fix a pair
(l,\phi)
. Also let
\alpha_{p}
be a generator of the Sylow
p
-subgroup of
\operatorname{Aut}(G)
. Then since the
p
-part of
\phi
is faithful, the value
\phi^{*}_{p}(\alpha_{p})=\phi_{p}(\alpha_{p}\mid_{H_{l}})
must be a primitive
p^{l-1}
-th root of unity. In particular, if
(m,\psi)
is another pair with
l\not=m
, then we also have
\phi^{*}\not=\psi^{*}
. Thus there is no repetition in the right hand side of Equation (
13
).

Moreover since the order of the restriction map
\operatorname{Aut}(G)\to\operatorname{Aut}(H_{l})
,
\beta\mapsto\beta\mid_{H_{l}}
, is
p^{k-l}
, the induced character
\phi^{*}
is non-primitive (since
p^{k-l}
is a quasi-period for
\phi^{*}
).

We count the number of terms. If
2\leq l<k
, then the number of irreducible characters of
\operatorname{Aut}(H_{l})
with faithful
p
-part is
p^{l-2}(p-1)^{2}
. Summing over
l
, we get

\displaystyle\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\Big(\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}2\leq l<k\\
\phi\in\widehat{\operatorname{Aut}(H_{l})}\\
\phi_{p}:\text{faithful}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{C}[e_{G,l,\phi}]\Big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2\leq l<k\\
\phi\in\widehat{\operatorname{Aut}(H_{l})}\\
\phi_{p}:\text{faithful}\end{subarray}}1

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=2}^{k-1}p^{l-2}(p-1)^{2}=(p-1)(p^{k-2}-1).

With the contribution of the last two terms, the dimension of the right hand side is

(p-1)(p^{k-2}-1)+(p-2)+1=(p-1)p^{k-2},

which is equal to the number of non-primitive irreducible characters of
\operatorname{Aut}(G)
. Hence each non-primitive irreducible
\mathbb{C}[\operatorname{Aut}(G)]
-module appears as a summand in Equation (
13
) with multiplicity 1, as required.
∎

6
Calculation of Restriction Kernels II: The
C_{p}\times C_{p}
-case

In Corollary
4.13
, we have already determined the basis
\mathcal{B}
given in
11
for the restriction kernel
\mathcal{K}\mathbb{S}_{p}(E_{2})
for
E_{2}=C_{p}\times C_{p}
.

In this section we determine its
\mathbb{C}[\operatorname{Aut}(E_{2})]
-module structure. It is clear that the basis vector
F_{1}
generates a trivial
\mathbb{C}[\operatorname{Aut}(E_{2})]
-submodule and a complement of the submodule is generated by the rest

\mathcal{B}^{\prime}:=\mathcal{B}-\{F_{1}\}=\bigcup_{1<\langle t\rangle<E_{2}}\{F_{t,\phi}\mid 1\not=\phi\in\widehat{\operatorname{Aut}(\langle t\rangle)}\}

of the basis vectors. Hence we concentrate on the span of
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
. We begin by describing the structure of
\operatorname{Aut}(E_{2})
.

6.1
.

Automorphism group of
E_{2}
.

We identify
\operatorname{Aut}(E_{2})={\operatorname{GL}(2,p)}
by writing
E_{2}=\langle x\rangle\times\langle y\rangle
as column vectors and representing each automorphism
\alpha
by the matrix whose columns are
\alpha(x)
and
\alpha(y)
; so
\alpha(x)=x^{a}y^{c}
and
\alpha(y)=x^{b}y^{d}
for
\alpha=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}
.

The proper non-trivial subgroups of
E_{2}
each have order
p
and are cyclic. We index them via
\mathfrak{C}=\{x\}\cup\{x^{i}y\mid 0\leq i\leq p-1\}
by setting
T_{g}=\langle g\rangle
for
g\in\mathfrak{C}
, so that

\mathfrak{S}=\{T_{g}\mid g\in\mathfrak{C}\}=\{T_{x},T_{y},T_{xy},T_{x^{2}y},\ldots,T_{x^{p-1}y}\}.

For
g\in\mathfrak{C}
, we write
\Gamma_{g}=\operatorname{Aut}(T_{g})
and
\hat{\Gamma}_{g}
for its character group. We fix a primitive root
s
modulo
p
and let
\gamma_{g}\in\Gamma_{g}
denote the
s
-th power map; this is canonical since it is the restriction of the scalar matrix
\begin{pmatrix}s&0\\
0&s\end{pmatrix}
to
T_{g}
, which commutes with every element of
{\operatorname{GL}(2,p)}
. Consequently, for any
\alpha\in{\operatorname{GL}(2,p)}
,

\alpha\circ\gamma_{g}=\gamma_{\alpha(g)}\circ\alpha,

so the isomorphism
\tilde{\alpha}\colon\Gamma_{g}\to\Gamma_{\alpha(g)}
induced by conjugation by
\alpha
sends
\gamma_{g}
to
\gamma_{\alpha(g)}
and depends only on the subgroup
T_{\alpha(g)}
, not on the particular
\alpha
. Similarly the induced map
\hat{\alpha}\colon\hat{\Gamma}_{g}\to\hat{\Gamma}_{\alpha(g)}
,
\phi\mapsto{}^{\alpha}\phi
(defined by
{}^{\alpha}\phi({}^{\alpha}\gamma)=\phi(\gamma)
), depends only on
T_{\alpha(g)}
.

The
{\operatorname{GL}(2,p)}
-action on
\mathfrak{S}
is given by
\alpha\cdot T_{g}=T_{\alpha(g)}
. Explicitly, for
\alpha=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}
,

\alpha\cdot T_{x^{i}y}=T_{x^{(ai+b)f}y},\qquad\alpha\cdot T_{x}=\begin{cases}T_{x^{ac^{-1}}y}&\text{if }c\neq 0,\\
T_{x}&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

where
f=(ci+d)^{-1}
in
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. This action is transitive, and the stabilizer of
T_{x}
is the Borel subgroup
B
of upper triangular matrices.

6.2
.

Now we are ready to determine
\mathcal{K}\mathbb{S}_{p}(E_{2})
as a
\mathbb{C}[{\operatorname{GL}(2,p)}]
-module. We write

\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{B}]:=\{\mathbb{C}[F_{g,\phi}]\mid F_{g,\phi}\in\mathcal{B}\}

for the set of lines in
\mathbb{S}_{p}(G)
parameterized by the set
\mathcal{B}
.

Theorem 6.3
.

Assume the above notation.

(a)
The set
\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{B}]
is a monomial
{\operatorname{GL}(2,p)}
-set via the action induced from the action of
{\operatorname{GL}(2,p)}
on
\mathbb{S}_{p}(G)
.

(b)
There is a unique fixed point in
\mathcal{B}
, namely
F_{1}
.

(c)
The
{\operatorname{GL}(2,p)}
-orbits in
\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{B}]
of size
\geq 2
are in one-to-one correspondence with the non-trivial irreducible characters of
\Gamma_{x}
.

(d)
The stabilizer in
{\operatorname{GL}(2,p)}
of any of these orbits is conjugate to
B\leq{\operatorname{GL}(2,p)}
.

(e)
The
B
-action on the line
\mathbb{C}[F_{x,\phi}]
is given by the linear character
\tilde{\phi}:B\to\mathbb{C}^{\times},\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
0&d\end{pmatrix}\mapsto\phi(a)
of
B
.

Proof.

We first note that part Part (b) is trivial. For the rest of the theorem, we begin by proving that the action of
{\operatorname{GL}(2,p)}
on
\mathbb{S}_{p}(E_{2})
induces a permutation action on
\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{B}]
. For simplicity, we consider the
{\operatorname{GL}(2,p)}
-action on
F_{x,\phi}
for a fixed character
\phi\in\hat{\Gamma}_{x}
. Let
\alpha\in{\operatorname{GL}(2,p)}
. Then

\displaystyle\alpha\cdot F_{x,\phi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma\in\operatorname{Aut}(T_{x})}\overline{\phi(\sigma)}\alpha(e_{G,\sigma(x)}^{G})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma\in\operatorname{Aut}(T_{x})}\overline{\phi(\sigma)}e_{G,\alpha\sigma(x)}^{G}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\delta\in\operatorname{Aut}(T_{\alpha(x)})}\overline{\phi(\alpha\delta\alpha^{-1})}e_{G,\delta\alpha(x)}^{G}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\delta\in\operatorname{Aut}(T_{\alpha(x)})}\overline{{}^{\alpha}\phi(\delta)}e_{G,\delta\alpha(x)}^{G}

We divide the rest of the calculation into two cases. First suppose
\alpha=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
0&d\end{pmatrix}\in B
. Then
\alpha(x)=x^{a}
and hence
\alpha^{\prime}:=\alpha\mid_{T_{x}}\in\Gamma_{x}
. In particular
T_{\alpha(x)}=T_{x}
and
\delta^{\prime}:=\delta\alpha^{\prime}\in\Gamma_{x}
for any
\delta\in\Gamma_{x}
. The above sum becomes

\displaystyle\alpha\cdot F_{x,\phi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\delta^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Aut}(T_{x})}\overline{\phi(\delta^{\prime}(\alpha^{\prime})^{-1})}e_{G,\delta^{\prime}(x)}^{G}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi(\alpha^{\prime})\sum_{\delta^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Aut}(T_{x})}\overline{\phi(\delta^{\prime}}e_{G,\delta^{\prime}(x)}^{G}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi(\alpha^{\prime})F_{x,\phi}.

This calculation also shows that the subgroup
B
stabilizes the line
\mathbb{C}[F_{x,\phi}]
. Since it is the stabilizer of
T_{x}
, we conclude that

B=\text{Stab}_{{\operatorname{GL}(2,p)}}(\mathbb{C}[F_{x,\phi}])

Moreover we obtain that if
\phi\not=\psi
, then the lines
\mathbb{C}[F_{x,\phi}]
and
\mathbb{C}[F_{x,\psi}]
are not in the same
{\operatorname{GL}(2,p)}
-orbit.

Next suppose
\alpha=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}
with
c\not=0
. Then
T_{\alpha(x)}=T_{x^{ac^{-1}}y}
. Also let
\delta_{c}:T_{\alpha(x)}\to T_{\alpha(x)},g\mapsto g^{c}
. Then

\displaystyle\alpha\cdot F_{x,\phi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\delta\in\operatorname{Aut}(T_{\alpha(x)})}\overline{{}^{\alpha}\phi(\delta)}e_{G,\delta\alpha(x)}^{G}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\delta^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Aut}(T_{\alpha(x)})}\overline{{}^{\alpha}\phi(\delta^{\prime}\delta_{c}^{-1})}e_{G,\delta^{\prime}(x^{ac^{-1}}y)}^{G}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{}^{\alpha}\phi(\delta_{c})\sum_{\delta^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Aut}(T_{\alpha(x)})}\overline{{}^{\alpha}\phi(\delta^{\prime})}e_{G,\delta^{\prime}(x^{ac^{-1}}y)}^{G}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{}^{\alpha}\phi(\delta_{c})F_{x^{ac^{-1}y},{}^{\alpha}\phi}

It is now clear that the group
{\operatorname{GL}(2,p)}
acts on
\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{B}]
by permutations. By the previous case, we already know that there are at least
p-2
-orbits in
\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{B}]
and each orbit is isomorphic to the coset space
{\operatorname{GL}(2,p)}/B
. Hence these orbits cover at least
(p+1)(p-2)
elements. But the total number of elements is
1+(p+1)(p-2)
. The additional orbit comes from the fixed line
\mathbb{C}[F_{1}]
.

∎

6.4
.

By the above theorem for each non-trivial irreducible character
\phi
of
\Gamma_{x}
, there is a direct summand of
\mathcal{K}\mathbb{S}_{p}(E_{2})
isomorphic to the induced
\mathbb{C}[{\operatorname{GL}(2,p)}]
-module
\operatorname{Ind}_{B}^{{\operatorname{GL}(2,p)}}\phi_{1}
where
\phi_{1}:B\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
is given by

\phi_{1}\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
0&d\end{pmatrix}=\phi(a).

By the classification of irreducible characters of
{\operatorname{GL}(2,p)}
, given in Theorem 8.12 in
[
16
]
, this module is irreducible. Hence we obtain the following result from which Theorem
1.2
(c) follows.

Theorem 6.5

(Structure of
\mathcal{K}\mathbb{S}_{p}(G)
for
G\cong C_{p}\times C_{p}
)
.

Assume the above notation.
The restriction kernel
\mathcal{K}\mathbb{S}_{p}(G)
decomposes as:

\mathcal{K}\mathbb{S}_{p}(G)\cong\begin{cases}\mathbb{C}\oplus\bigoplus_{1\neq\phi\in\hat{\Gamma}_{x}}\operatorname{Ind}_{B}^{{\operatorname{GL}(2,p)}}\phi_{1},&\text{if }p>2,\\
\mathbb{C},&\text{if }p=2,\end{cases}

Moreover the right-hand side of the equality is a direct sum of irreducible
\mathbb{C}[{\operatorname{GL}(2,p)}]
-modules.

7
Calculation of Restriction Kernels III

This section analyzes the restriction kernel
\mathcal{K}\mathbb{S}_{p}(G)
for
G\cong C_{p^{m}}\times C_{p}
, where
m\geq 2
. Its structure as a module over the automorphism group
\Gamma:=\operatorname{Aut}(G)
is more complicated and we need to study the cases
p=2
and
p>2
separately. In this section we include results that apply to both cases and in the next two sections we calculate explicit summands.

Since
\mathcal{K}\mathbb{S}_{2}(G)\subseteq\delta_{\Phi}\mathbb{S}_{2}(G)
, we may concentrate on
\delta_{\Phi}\mathbb{S}_{2}(G)
. Assume the notation from Section
4
.
By the proof of Theorem
4.9
, the later is generated by the set
\mathcal{E}:=\{E_{g}:=\varphi_{1}^{G}\cdot e_{G,g}^{G}\mid g\in G\}
. We partition the set
\mathcal{E}
according to the three cases of the proof of Proposition
4.9
as follows: Let

(a)
\mathcal{E}_{1}=\big\{E_{g}\mid\langle g\rangle\text{ is maximal in }G\big\}

(b)
\mathcal{E}_{2}=\big\{E_{g}\mid g\in\Phi(G)\big\}

(c)
\mathcal{E}_{3}=\big\{E_{g}\mid g\not\in\Phi(G),\langle g\rangle\text{ is not maximal in }G\big\}

then

\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{E}_{1}\cup\mathcal{E}_{2}\cup\mathcal{E}_{3}.

With this notation and by Case (1) of the proof of Proposition
4.9
, span of
\mathcal{E}_{1}
lies in
\mathcal{K}\mathbb{S}_{2}(G)
. Moreover the following lemma holds.

Theorem 7.1
.

There is an isomorphism

\mathcal{K}\mathbb{S}_{p}(G)\cong\text{Span }(\mathcal{E}_{1})

of vector spaces over
\mathbb{C}
.

Proof.

We prove the proposition in three steps. In Step 1 and Step 2, we prove that deflation to a non-trivial quotient of
G
is injective on each
Z
-orbit in
\mathcal{E}_{2}
and also on each
Z
-orbit in
\mathcal{E}_{3}
. Then Step 3 proves that the images are disjoint, and hence deflation is injective on the span of the union. Combining these, we get that

\mathcal{K}\mathbb{S}_{p}(G)\cap\big(\text{span}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{E}_{2})\oplus\text{span}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{E}_{3})\big)=\{0\}

and hence

\mathcal{K}\mathbb{S}_{p}(G)=\text{span}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{E}_{1}).

Step 1:

The set
\mathcal{E}_{2}
can be decomposed further. Notice that for any
g\in G
, the primitive idempotent
e_{G,g}^{G}
is a summand of each of
E_{gz^{i}}
and not a summand of any other
E_{h}
’s. Hence it is sufficient to look for linear relations including
E_{g}
only among
E_{gz^{i}}
’s. Accordingly we partition
\mathcal{E}_{2}
as

\mathcal{E}_{2}=\bigsqcup_{g\in[\Phi(G)/Z]}\Big\{E_{gz^{i}}\mid i=0,1,\ldots,p-1\Big\}

where the union is over a complete set of representatives of cosets of
Z
in
\Phi(G)
. We claim that for a fixed
g\in[\Phi(G)/Z]
, the set

\Big\{E_{gz^{i}}\mid i=0,1,\ldots,p-1\Big\}
generates a vector space of dimension
p-1
.

If
p=2
, the claim is trivial since in this case
z
has order 2 and
E_{g}=-E_{gz}
. So suppose
p
is odd. Recall that, for a fixed
i
, we have

E_{gz^{i}}=e_{G,gz^{i}}^{G}-\frac{1}{p}\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}e_{G,gz^{i}z^{j}}^{G}=\frac{p-1}{p}e_{G,gz^{i}}^{G}-\frac{1}{p}\sum_{j=1}^{p-1}e_{G,gz^{i}z^{j}}^{G}

Consider the matrix
M=(m_{i,j})_{0\leq i,j<p}
of coefficients for a linear system in
p\cdot E_{gz^{i}}
’s. We have

m_{i,j}=\begin{cases}p-1&\text{if }i=j,\\
-1&\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

The matrix
M
is not invertible since the sum of all rows is equal to 0. We claim that the rank of
M
is
p-1
. To see this, we add the negative of the first row of
M
to each of the other rows. The resulting matrix
M^{\prime}=(m_{i,j}^{\prime})
has entries

m_{i,j}^{\prime}=\begin{cases}m_{i,j}&\text{if }i=1,\\
p&\text{ if }i=j>1,\\
-p&\text{ if }i>1,j=1\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

It is clear that the minor
M_{1,1}^{\prime}
is equal to
p^{p-1}
, hence
M^{\prime}
has rank
p-1
. On the other hand, since it is obtained from
M
by multiplying
M
by an invertible matrix, the rank is smaller than or equal to the rank of
M
. Since the rank of
M
is strictly smaller than
p
, we must have
\text{rk}(M)=p-1
.

Now let
T
be a subgroup of order
p
in
G
which is not contained in
\Phi(G)
. Then, we have

\frac{p^{2}}{p-1}\operatorname{Def}^{G}_{G/T}E_{gz^{i}}=(p-1)e_{G/T,gz^{i}T}^{G/T}-\sum_{j=1}^{p-1}e_{G/T,gz^{i}z^{j}T}^{G/T}

Since
T
is not contained in the Frattini subgroup, the elements
gz^{i}T
of
G/T
are all distinct and hence the image of deflation
\operatorname{Def}^{G}_{G/T}
on the span of
\{E_{gz^{i}}\mid i=0,\ldots,p-1\}
is generated by the set

\mathcal{E}_{2,g}^{\prime}:=\Big\{\frac{p^{2}}{p-1}\operatorname{Def}^{G}_{G/T}E_{gz^{i}}\mid i=0,1,\ldots,p-1\Big\}

consisting of distinct elements and has dimension
p-1
. In particular,
\operatorname{Def}^{G}_{G/T}
is injective on
\mathrm{Span}\{E_{gz^{i}}\mid i=0,\ldots,p-1\}
, as required.

Step 2:
Let
g\in\mathcal{E}_{3}
, so
g=x^{a}y^{b}
with
b\neq 0
and

p\mid a
. Write
a=pa^{\prime}
. Let
gZ=\{gz^{i}\mid i=0,1,\ldots,p-1\}
be the
Z
-orbit
of
g
in
\mathcal{E}_{3}
. We first verify that

gz^{i}\Phi(G)=y^{b}\Phi(G)\quad\text{in }G/\Phi(G)\cong C_{p}\times C_{p}

for all
i=0,1,\ldots,p-1
. Indeed,
gz^{i}=x^{pa^{\prime}+ip^{m-1}}y^{b}
, and since
p\mid pa^{\prime}

and
p\mid ip^{m-1}
(as
m\geq 2
), the
x
-component lies in
\Phi(G)=\langle x^{p}\rangle
,
giving
gz^{i}\Phi(G)=y^{b}\Phi(G)
for all
i
.

Choose a subgroup
T
of order
p
such that
T\not\leq\Phi(G)
and

T\Phi(G)/\Phi(G)=\langle y^{b}\Phi(G)\rangle
in
G/\Phi(G)
. (Such
T
exists; for
instance, take
T=\langle x^{p^{m-1}}y^{b}\rangle
, which has order
p
and satisfies

T\cap\Phi(G)=1
since the
y
-component
y^{kb}
of any
(x^{p^{m-1}}y^{b})^{k}
is
trivial only when
p\mid k
.) Since
T\not\leq\Phi(G)
, we have
T\neq Z
, so
T\cap Z=1

and in particular
z\notin T
.

We now compute
m_{G,gz^{i}}^{T}
for each
i
. Since
|T\cap\Phi(G)|=1
, Lemma

3.13
and Lemma
3.15
give

m_{G,gz^{i}}^{T}=m_{G/\Phi(G),\,gz^{i}\Phi(G)}^{T\Phi(G)/\Phi(G)}=m_{G/\Phi(G),\,y^{b}\Phi(G)}^{\langle y^{b}\Phi(G)\rangle}=\frac{1}{p}

for all
i=0,1,\ldots,p-1
.

Next, since
z\notin T
, the cosets
gz^{i}T
are pairwise distinct in
G/T
: indeed

gz^{i}T=gz^{j}T
would force
z^{i-j}\in T
, hence
z^{i-j}\in T\cap Z=1
,
giving
i\equiv j\pmod{p}
.
Using Equation (
8
) and the above calculation, we get

p^{2}\,\operatorname{Def}^{G}_{G/T}E_{gz^{i}}=(p-1)\,e_{G/T,\,gz^{i}T}^{G/T}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=0\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{p-1}e_{G/T,\,gz^{j}T}^{G/T}.

The matrix of this system in the (distinct) basis elements
e_{G/T,\,gz^{j}T}^{G/T}
is
exactly the same matrix
M
as in Step 1, which has rank
p-1
with
one-dimensional null space spanned by the all-ones vector.

Now observe that

\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}E_{gz^{i}}=\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}\bigg(e_{G,\,gz^{i}}^{G}-\frac{1}{p}\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}e_{G,\,gz^{i+k}}^{G}\bigg)=\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}e_{G,\,gz^{i}}^{G}-\frac{1}{p}\cdot p\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}e_{G,\,gz^{i}}^{G}=0.

Hence the all-ones vector maps to zero in
\mathrm{Span}\{E_{gz^{i}}\mid i=0,\ldots,p-1\}
,
which is therefore
(p-1)
-dimensional. Since the null space of
M
is generated by the
all-ones vector, the map
p^{2}\,\operatorname{Def}^{G}_{G/T}
is injective on

\mathrm{Span}\{E_{gz^{i}}\mid i=0,\ldots,p-1\}
, as required.

Step 3:
We first observe that the primitive
idempotents supporting
\mathcal{E}_{2}
and
\mathcal{E}_{3}
are
disjoint. Indeed, by Equation (
8
), each
E_{g}
is supported on

\{e_{G,gz^{i}}^{G}\mid i=0,\ldots,p-1\}
. For
g\in\mathcal{E}_{2}
(so

g\in\Phi(G)=\langle x^{p}\rangle
), every
gz^{i}\in\Phi(G)
has trivial

y
-component. For
g^{\prime}\in\mathcal{E}_{3}
(so
g^{\prime}=x^{pa^{\prime}}y^{b}
with
b\neq 0
),
every
g^{\prime}z^{i}=x^{pa^{\prime}+ip^{m-1}}y^{b}
has nontrivial
y
-component. Since

\Phi(G)
consists precisely of elements with trivial
y
-component,
the two collections of primitive idempotents are disjoint, so

\mathrm{Span}(\mathcal{E}_{2})\oplus\mathrm{Span}(\mathcal{E}_{3})
is a direct sum.

Now let
u=u_{2}+u_{3}\in\mathrm{Span}(\mathcal{E}_{2})\oplus\mathrm{Span}(\mathcal{E}_{3})

be an element of
\mathcal{K}\mathbb{S}_{p}(G)
; we show
u=0
.

Step 3.1:
u_{2}=0
.
Choose a subgroup
T
of order
p
with

T\not\leq\Phi(G)
and
T\Phi(G)/\Phi(G)=\langle x\Phi(G)\rangle
in

G/\Phi(G)\cong C_{p}\times C_{p}
.
For any
h
appearing in the support of
u_{3}
, we have
h\Phi(G)=y^{b}\Phi(G)\in\langle y\Phi(G)\rangle
, which is disjoint from
\langle x\Phi(G)\rangle
in

G/\Phi(G)
. By Lemma
3.13
and Lemma
3.15

(rank
2
, element outside
T
):

Step 3.2:
u_{3}=0

We now have

7.2

To determine a basis for

\Sigma=\langle a_{2}^{2^{m-3}},b\rangle\leq\Gamma

Lemma 8.4
.

The set
\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{B}_{2}]

\Gamma

\Gamma

\operatorname{Iso}_{G}^{a}

(a)

\Gamma

\Sigma
.

(b)

The subgroup
\Sigma

\mathbb{C}[E_{x}]

\psi

Proof.

First, we verify that the
\Gamma

z

a\in\Gamma
. For a given

\operatorname{Iso}_{G}^{a}(E_{g})=\operatorname{Iso}_{G}^{a}(e_{G,g}^{G}-e_{G,gz}^{G})=e_{G,a(g)}^{G}-e_{G,a(g)z}^{G}=\pm E_{h},

\Gamma

Next, we determine the stabilizer subgroup
\Sigma=\operatorname{Stab}_{\Gamma}(\mathbb{C}[E_{x}])
. From Appendix

\Gamma

a_{1}
,
a_{2}
,

, and

\begin{array}[]{c|cccc}&a_{1}&a_{2}&b&c\\

Note that, since

, the inverse

is distinct from both

. Similarly, it is evident that

On the other hand, since

has order

trivially, as

where

The final step is to determine the irreducible summands of this monomial module. We have the following theorem which is Part (d) of Theorem

.

Theorem 8.5
.

The
\mathbb{C}[\Gamma]
-module
\mathcal{K}\mathbb{S}_{2}(G)
contains each of the irreducible
\mathbb{C}[\Gamma]
-modules of dimension 2 as a direct summand exactly once and has no other irreducible summands.

Proof.

By the previous lemma, we already know that
\mathcal{K}\mathbb{S}_{2}(G)

\mathbb{C}[\Sigma]
-module
\psi
. To determine the irreducible summands, we first consider the inertia subgroup of
\psi

\psi
is not
\Gamma
-invariant. Indeed since
b^{c}=\tilde{a}_{2}b
, we have

({}^{c}\psi)(b)=\psi(b^{c})\neq\psi(b).

Its
\Gamma
-orbit consists of two elements, namely
\psi
and
{}^{c}\psi
. In particular we also have

\mathcal{K}\mathbb{S}_{2}(G)\cong\operatorname{Ind}_{\Sigma}^{\Gamma}{}^{c}\psi.

Although it will not be used immediately, we determine
{}^{c}\psi
more explicitly to refer to it later. It is straightforward to see that, following the above notation, the stabilizer of the line
\mathbb{C}[E_{xy}]
is also equal to
\Sigma
. The action of generators of
\Gamma
on
E_{xy}
is given in the following table.

a_{1}

a_{2}

b

c

E_{xy}

E_{x^{-1}y}

E_{x^{5}y}

E_{xzy}

E_{x}

The character
\psi^{\prime}
of this action is given by

\psi^{\prime}(\tilde{a}_{2})=-1,\ \psi^{\prime}(b)=-1

It is clear that we have
{}^{c}\psi=\psi^{\prime}
.

Returning back to the inertia subgroup
I
of
\psi
in
\Gamma
, since
a_{1},a_{2}
are central and

, the inertia subgroup

of
\psi
in
\Gamma
contains

\Gamma

Since

is abelian, the character
\psi
extends to

and by Clifford’s Theory,
\operatorname{Ind}_{\Sigma}^{I}\psi
is the sum of distinct extensions of
\psi
to

. More precisely, if
\tilde{\psi}
is such an extension, then by the Gallagher Correspondence, the set of extensions, and

\text{Irr}(I\mid\psi)=\{\lambda\cdot\tilde{\psi}\mid\lambda\in\widehat{I/\Sigma}\}.

Here as usual,

Furthermore none of these extensions is
\Gamma
-invariant and hence by the Mackey Formula we get

\operatorname{Res}^{\Gamma}_{I}\operatorname{Ind}_{I}^{\Gamma}\tilde{\psi}=\tilde{\psi}\oplus{}^{c}\tilde{\psi}

Therefore by the Frobenius Reciprocity,

\displaystyle\big\langle\operatorname{Ind}_{I}^{\Gamma}\tilde{\psi},\operatorname{Ind}_{I}^{\Gamma}\tilde{\psi}\big\rangle_{\Gamma}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big\langle\tilde{\psi},\operatorname{Res}^{\Gamma}_{I}\operatorname{Ind}_{I}^{\Gamma}\tilde{\psi}\big\rangle_{I}

\mathcal{K}\mathbb{S}_{2}(G)\cong\operatorname{Ind}_{\Sigma}^{\Gamma}\psi\cong\bigoplus_{\chi\in\text{Irr}(I\mid\psi)}\operatorname{Ind}_{I}^{\Gamma}\chi

where the summands can be identified as pairwise non-isomorphic irreducible
\mathbb{C}\Gamma
-modules of dimension 2. Since the number of summands on the right hand side is equal to

. By Theorem

\mathbb{C}\Gamma

∎

8.6
.

Finally we determine explicit bases for the irreducible summands of
\mathcal{K}\mathbb{S}_{2}(G)
. For each

be the primitive idempotent of the group algebra
\mathbb{C}I
corresponding to
\chi
. It is given by

e_{\chi}=\frac{1}{|I|}\sum_{\iota\in I}\chi(\iota^{-1})\iota

Recall that
\{e_{\chi}\mid\chi\in\hat{I}\}
is the set of pairwise orthonormal primitive idempotents summing up to 1. Hence the summand of
\operatorname{Ind}_{\Sigma}^{I}\psi
with character
\chi
is equal to
e_{\chi}\cdot\operatorname{Ind}_{\Sigma}^{I}\psi
. By Frobenius Reciprocity, the multiplicity of

in
\operatorname{Ind}_{\Sigma}^{I}\psi
is 1, and hence it is generated by any nonzero vector it contains. To distinguish one, consider the product

e_{\chi}\cdot E_{x}=\frac{1}{|I|}\sum_{\iota\in I}\chi(\iota^{-1})\iota(E_{x})

Here we regard

as an element in
\operatorname{Ind}_{\Sigma}^{I}\psi
via the identification of

. Since

and

, the terms of the above sum corresponding to

and

are the same. Similarly, since

and

, the terms corresponding to

and

and hence get

\operatorname{Ind}_{\Sigma}^{I}\psi\cong\bigoplus_{\chi\in\text{Irr}(I\mid\psi)}\mathbb{C}[F_{x,\chi}].

With this notation, we also have

\operatorname{Res}^{\Gamma}_{I}\operatorname{Ind}_{I}^{\Gamma}\mathbb{C}[F_{x,\chi}]\cong\mathbb{C}[F_{x,\chi}]\oplus{}^{c}\big(\mathbb{C}[F_{x,\chi}]\big)

One can easily check that both

9.5

By the lemma, we need to evaluate the quotient of two permutation

\mathcal{K}\mathbb{S}_{p}(G)\cong\bigoplus_{V\in\text{Irr}(T/\Sigma):V\not\cong\mathbb{C}}\bigoplus_{W\in\text{Irr}(N|V)}\operatorname{Ind}_{N}^{\Gamma}W

A Result on Composition Factors

We append this section to describe the theorem from
[
6
]
which is used to conclude completeness of composition factors. Although the original statement is for modules over arbitrary Green biset functors, we state its special case for biset functors. It is included for self-completeness.

Theorem D.1
.

Let
F
be a biset functor over a field
k
and let
G
be a finite group. Suppose that
\dim_{k}F(G)<\infty
, and that for every
\mathcal{E}_{G}
-submodule
M\subseteq F(G)
, one has

M=(M\cap\mathcal{K}_{F}(G))+I_{G}M.

Then, for every simple
k[\operatorname{Out}(G)]
-module
V
,
the following numbers are equal:

1.

The multiplicity
[F:S_{G,V}]
of
S_{G,V}
as a composition factor of
F
.

2.

The multiplicity of
V
as a composition factor of the
\mathcal{E}_{G}
-module
F(G)
.

3.

The multiplicity of
V
as a composition factor of the
k[\operatorname{Out}(G)]
-module
\mathcal{K}F(G)
.

Proof.

The equality of the first two numbers is well-known. We prove the equality of the last two numbers.
Since
\dim_{k}F(G)<\infty
, there exists an
\mathcal{E}_{G}
-composition series

0=M_{0}\subset M_{1}\subset\cdots\subset M_{n-1}\subset M_{n}=F(G)

of
F(G)
. Set
K:=\mathcal{K}F(G)
, and consider the induced series

0=(M_{0}\cap K)\subseteq(M_{1}\cap K)\subseteq\cdots\subseteq(M_{n-1}\cap K)\subseteq(M_{n}\cap K)=K

of
\mathcal{E}_{G}
-submodules of
K
. Let
V
be a simple
k[\operatorname{Out}(G)]
-module, and let
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. We claim that if
M_{i}/M_{i-1}\cong V
, then
(M_{i}\cap K)/(M_{i-1}\cap K)\cong V
. This implies that
[F(G):V]\leq[K:V]
. But clearly,
[K:V]\leq[F(G):V]
, and we obtain equality.

To prove the claim, observe that

\frac{M_{i}\cap K}{M_{i-1}\cap K}=\frac{M_{i}\cap K}{(M_{i}\cap K)\cap M_{i-1}}\cong\frac{(M_{i}\cap K)+M_{i-1}}{M_{i-1}}\subseteq\frac{M_{i}}{M_{i-1}}\cong V.

It therefore suffices to show that the left-hand side is nonzero. Assume by contradiction that
M_{i}\cap K=M_{i-1}\cap K
. Then,

M_{i}=(M_{i}\cap K)+I_{G}M_{i}\subseteq(M_{i}\cap K)+M_{i-1}=M_{i-1},

contradicting
M_{i}/M_{i-1}\cong V\neq 0
. Here, we used the assumption that
V
is annihilated by

, and the hypothesis of the theorem.
∎

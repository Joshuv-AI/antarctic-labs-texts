Variational formulae for entropy-like functionals for states in von Neumann algebras
Andrzej Łuczak, Hanna Podsędkowska, Rafał Wieczorek

Abstract
. The paper presents variational formulae for entropy-like functionals, including Segal and Rényi entropies, for normal states on semifinite von Neumann algebras. The considered functionals are of the form τ ⁡ ( f ⁡ ( h ) ) \tau(f(h)) where τ \tau is a normal faithful semifinite trace on this algebra, h h is a positive selfadjoint operator from L 1 ​ ( ℳ , τ ) L^{1}(\mathscr{M},\tau) , and f f is an appropriate convex or concave function. The results cover both finite and semifinite algebras, and the obtained formulae generalise known results, in particular, those concerning relative entropy. Moreover, the connection between quantum entropies and the structure of abelian subalgebras is highlighted, providing new interpretations in the context of quantum information theory.

Introduction

The paper is devoted to variational formulae for various entropy-like functionals for normal states on a semifinite von Neumann algebra
\mathscr{M}
. By such functionals, we understand functionals of the form
\tau(f(h))
where
\tau
is a normal faithful semifinite trace on this algebra,
h
is a positive selfadjoint operator from
L^{1}(\mathscr{M},\tau)
, and
f
is an appropriate convex (concave) function. In particular, such formulae for the Segal and Rényi entropies are obtained.

1.
Preliminaries and notation

Let
\mathscr{M}
be a semifinite von Neumann algebra acting on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, with a normal faithful semifinite trace
\tau
, identity
\mathbb{1}
, and predual
\mathscr{M}_{*}
. By
\mathscr{M}^{h}
we shall denote the set of hermitian operators in
\mathscr{M}
, and by
\mathscr{M}^{+}
— the set of positive operators in
\mathscr{M}
.
\mathscr{M}_{*}^{+}
stands for the set of positive functionals in
\mathscr{M}_{*}
. These functionals will be referred to as
normal states
. The elements
\omega
in
\mathscr{M}_{*}^{+}
of norm one, i.e. such that
\omega(\mathbb{1})=1
, will be called
normalised states
. In what follows, the term
state
will always refer to a
normal state
. For finite
\tau
, we shall assume that
\tau(\mathbb{1})=1
, thus in this case
\tau
itself is a normalised state.

A densely defined closed operator
x
is said to be
affiliated with

\mathscr{M}
, denoted by
x\eta\mathscr{M}
, if for its polar decomposition

x=u|x|

u\in\mathscr{M}
, and the spectral projections of
|x|
belong to
\mathscr{M}
. Observe that if
x
is affiliated with
\mathscr{M}
and
x
is bounded, then
x\in\mathscr{M}
. For a selfadjoint
h
affiliated with
\mathscr{M}
, by
W^{*}(h)
we shall denote the von Neumann algebra generated by the spectral projections of
h
.

An operator
x
affiliated with
\mathscr{M}
is said to be
measurable
if for the spectral decomposition

|x|=\int_{0}^{\infty}t\,e(dt),

we have
\tau(e([t_{0},\infty)))<+\infty
for some
t_{0}
. It is seen that for finite
\tau
, all operators affiliated with
\mathscr{M}
are measurable. By
\widetilde{\mathscr{M}}
we shall denote the *-algebra of all measurable operators endowed with the measure topology, i.e. a translation-invariant topology defined by a fundamental system of neighbourhoods of
0
,
\{N(\varepsilon,\delta):\varepsilon,\delta>0\}
, given by

\displaystyle N(\varepsilon,\delta)=\{x\in\widetilde{\mathscr{M}}:

there exists a projection
p
in
\mathscr{M}
such that

\displaystyle xp\in\mathscr{M},\quad\|xp\|_{\infty}\leqslant\varepsilon\quad\text{and}\quad\tau(p^{\bot})\leqslant\delta\}.

Measurable operators
h
such that

\tau(|h|)<+\infty

form the Banach space
L^{1}(\mathscr{M},\tau)
with the norm

\|h\|_{1}=\tau(|h|),\quad h\in L^{1}(\mathscr{M},\tau).

For each
\rho\in\mathscr{M}_{*}
, there is an operator
D_{\rho}\in L^{1}(\mathscr{M},\tau)
such that

\rho(x)=\tau(xD_{\rho})=\tau(D_{\rho}x),\quad x\in\mathscr{M}.

The correspondence between
\mathscr{M}_{*}
and
L^{1}(\mathscr{M},\tau)
defined above is one-to-one and isometric. For a state
\omega
, the corresponding element in
L^{1}(\mathscr{M},\tau)^{+}
will be denoted by
D_{\omega}
and called the
density
of
\omega
, thus

\omega(x)=\tau(xD_{\omega})=\tau(D_{\omega}x)=\tau\big(D_{\omega}^{\frac{1}{2}}xD_{\omega}^{\frac{1}{2}}\big),\quad x\in\mathscr{M}.

For a state
\omega
, its
Segal entropy
,
H(\omega)
, is defined as

H(\omega)=\tau(D_{\omega}\log D_{\omega})=\int_{0}^{\infty}t\log t\,\tau(e(dt)),

where

(1)

D_{\omega}=\int_{0}^{\infty}t\,e(dt)

is the spectral decomposition of
D_{\omega}
. This definition of entropy with a minus sign before the trace was introduced in
[
7
]
. From the inequality
t\log t\geqslant t-1
, it follows that for finite
\tau
we have a bound

H(\omega)\geqslant\tau(D_{\omega})-1=\|\omega\|-1,

so for a normalised state
\omega
,
H(\omega)\geqslant 0
(but it can happen that
H(\omega)=+\infty
).

For normal states
\omega
and
\varphi
, their
relative entropy
,
S(\omega,\varphi)
, is defined as

S(\omega,\varphi)=\tau(D_{\omega}\log D_{\omega}-D_{\omega}\log D_{\varphi}).

It is known that for normalised states we have
S(\omega,\varphi)\geqslant 0
.

Let
\omega
be a normalised state, and let
\alpha\in(0,1)\cup(1,+\infty)
. The family of
Rényi’s entropies

R_{\alpha}(\omega)
is defined as

R_{\alpha}(\omega)=\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\log\tau(D_{\omega}^{\alpha}),

under the assumption that
\tau(D_{\omega}^{\alpha})<+\infty
.

2.
Variational formula for Segal entropy

In
[
5
]
, a variational formula for the relative entropy was obtained which in the case of a finite algebra,
\omega
— normalised and faithful, and
\varphi
— faithful has the form

S(\omega,\varphi)=\sup_{h\in\mathscr{M}^{h}}\big(\omega(h)-\log\tau\big(e^{\log D_{\varphi}+h}\big)\big).

Since in a finite von Neumann algebra we have

H(\omega)=S(\omega,\tau),

it follows that in this case

(2)

H(\omega)=\sup_{h\in\mathscr{M}^{h}}\big(\omega(h)-\log\tau\big(e^{h}\big)\big).

It is interesting that a formula similar to the equality (
2
) holds also for semifinite algebras, even without the assumption on the faithfulness of
\omega
, with the supremum taken over a larger set of operators.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\mathscr{M}
be a semifinite von Neumann algebra with a normal faithful semifinite trace
\tau
, and let
\omega
be a normalised state having finite Segal’s entropy. Then

H(\omega)=\sup_{h^{*}=h\eta\mathscr{M}}\{\tau(D_{\omega}h)-\log\tau\big(e^{h}\big):D_{\omega}h,e^{h}\in L^{1}(\mathscr{M},\tau)\}.

Proof.

For arbitrary fixed
h=h^{*}\eta\mathscr{M}
, such that
D_{\omega}h,e^{h}\in L^{1}(\mathscr{M},\tau)
define a normalised state
\varphi
on
\mathscr{M}
by its density

D_{\varphi}=\frac{e^{h}}{\tau\big(e^{h}\big)}.

Then we have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leqslant S(\omega,\varphi)=\tau(D_{\omega}\log D_{\omega}-D_{\omega}\log D_{\varphi})

\displaystyle=\tau\big(D_{\omega}\log D_{\omega}-D_{\omega}\big(h-\log\tau\big(e^{h}\big)\mathbb{1}\big)\big)

\displaystyle=\tau\big(D_{\omega}\log D_{\omega}\big)-\tau\big(D_{\omega}\big(h-\log\tau\big(e^{h}\big)\mathbb{1}\big)\big)

\displaystyle=H(\omega)-\tau(D_{\omega}h)+\log\tau\big(e^{h}\big),

which yields

H(\omega)\geqslant\tau(D_{\omega}h)-\log\tau\big(e^{h}\big),

showing that

H(\omega)\geqslant\sup_{h^{*}=h\eta\mathscr{M}}\{\tau(D_{\omega}h)-\log\tau\big(e^{h}\big):D_{\omega}h,e^{h}\in L^{1}(\mathscr{M},\tau)\}.

Conversely, let
D_{\omega}
have spectral decomposition as in (
1
). Denote
q=e(\{0\})
, and let
q_{1},q_{2},\dots
be projections in
\mathscr{M}
such that
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\infty}q_{i}=q
,
\tau(q_{i})<+\infty
. Let

f(t)=\begin{cases}\log t,&\text{for $t>0$}\\
0,&\text{for $t=0$}\end{cases}.

For arbitrary
\varepsilon>0
, set
\alpha_{i}=\log\frac{\varepsilon}{2^{i}\tau(q_{i})}
, and define operators
h_{1}
and
h_{2}
by the spectral decompositions

h_{1}=\int_{0}^{\infty}f(t)\,e(dt),\qquad h_{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{i}q_{i}.

We have

e^{h_{1}}=D_{\omega}+q,\qquad e^{h_{2}}=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}e^{\alpha_{i}}q_{i}+q^{\bot},

and putting
h=h_{1}+h_{2}
, we obtain

D_{\omega}h=D_{\omega}h_{1}=D_{\omega}\log D_{\omega},

and

e^{h}=D_{\omega}+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}e^{\alpha_{i}}q_{i},

since

D_{\omega}=q^{\bot}D_{\omega}=D_{\omega}q^{\bot},

and

\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\infty}e^{\alpha_{i}}q_{i}=q\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\infty}e^{\alpha_{i}}q_{i}=\big(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\infty}e^{\alpha_{i}}q_{i}\big)q.

Consequently,

\log\tau\big(e^{h}\big)=\log\big(\tau(D_{\omega})+\tau(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}e{{}^{\alpha}_{i}}q_{i})\big)=\log(1+\varepsilon),

which yields

\displaystyle\tau(D_{\omega}h)-\log\tau\big(e^{h}\big)

\displaystyle=\tau(D_{\omega}\log D_{\omega})-\log(1+\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=H(\omega)-\log(1+\varepsilon)\geqslant H(\omega)-\varepsilon,

showing that

H(\omega)\leqslant\sup_{h^{*}=h\eta\mathscr{M}}\{\tau(D_{\omega}h)-\log\tau\big(e^{h}\big):D_{\omega}h,e^{h}\in L^{1}(\mathscr{M},\tau)\}.\qed

Observe that the same reasoning as above leads to the formula (
2
) in the case of a finite algebra and a normalised, not necessary faithful, state.

Theorem 2
.

Let
\mathscr{M}
be a finite von Neumann algebra with a normal faithful finite trace
\tau
, and let
\omega
be a normalised state having finite Segal’s entropy. Then

H(\omega)=\sup_{h\in\mathscr{M}^{h}}\big(\tau(D_{\omega}h)-\log\tau\big(e^{h}\big)\big)=\sup_{h\in\mathscr{M}^{h}}\big(\omega(h)-\log\tau\big(e^{h}\big)\big).

3.
Variational formulae for convex (concave) functions

Let
\Phi\colon\mathscr{M}\to\mathscr{M}
be a normal positive linear unital map such that
\tau\circ\Phi=\tau
. Then
\Phi
may be extended to a bounded positive map from
L^{1}(\mathscr{M},\tau)
into itself, denoted by the same symbol, in particular, it sends the selfadjoint operators to selfadjoint ones. Let
h
be a selfadjoint operator in
\mathscr{M}
, and let
f
be an operator convex function defined on an interval containing the spectrum of
h
and the spectrum of
\Phi(h)
. Then the following Jensen inequality holds (even without the assumption
\tau\circ\Phi=\tau
).

(3)

f(\Phi(h))\leqslant\Phi(f(h)).

If only the convexity of
f
is assumed, then we have the Jensen inequality in its weaker form

(4)

\tau(f(\Phi(h)))\leqslant\tau(\Phi(f(h))),

under the assumption that both sides of the inequality above are defined. (Of course, for concave functions we have reversed inequalities.) A problem with the Jensen inequality arises when we want to extend it to unbounded operators (say from
L^{1}(\mathscr{M},\tau)
). For the function
f(t)=t\log t
it was done in
[
3
]
, while for the function
f(t)=t^{\alpha}
,
\alpha\in(0,1)\cup(1,+\infty)
it was done in
[
4
]
. A solution to the general problem (which seems to be considered as part of the folklore) was obtained in
[
2
]
for finite algebras. In the semifinite case, we restrict attention to the class of (operator) convex or concave functions for which Jensen’s inequality in its weaker form holds (the functions
f(t)=t\log t
and
f(t)=t^{\alpha}
being our main examples). We shall denote this class by
\mathfrak{J}
. Observe that for
f
satisfying the stronger form of Jensen’s inequality such that
f(h)\in L^{1}(\mathscr{M},\tau)
, and
\Phi
such that
\tau\circ\Phi=\tau
, both sides of the inequality (
4
) are defined. To see this, denote
x=\Phi(f(h))
and
y=f(\Phi(h))
. For the Jordan decompositions of
x
and
y
we have

0\leqslant x^{+}-x^{-}-(y^{+}-y^{-})\leqslant x^{+}-y^{+}+y^{-},

and denoting by
\operatorname{s}(y^{+})
the support of
y^{+}
, we get

0\leqslant\operatorname{s}(y^{+})x^{+}\operatorname{s}(y^{+})-y^{+},

which yields

0\leqslant\tau(y^{+})\leqslant\tau(\operatorname{s}(y^{+})x^{+}\operatorname{s}(y^{+}))\leqslant\tau(x^{+})<+\infty,

since

\tau(x)=\tau(\Phi(f(h)))=\tau(f(h)),

i.e.
x\in L^{1}(\mathscr{M},\tau)
. Consequently,
\tau(y)=\tau(y^{+})-\tau(y^{-})
is defined.

For a von Neumann subalgebra
\mathscr{N}
of
\mathscr{M}
such that
\tau|\mathscr{N}
is semifinite, denote by
\mathbb{E}_{\mathscr{N}}
the normal conditional expectation from
\mathscr{M}
onto
\mathscr{N}
such that
\tau\circ\mathbb{E}_{\mathscr{N}}=\tau
. Then
\mathbb{E}_{\mathscr{N}}
can be extended to a bounded (of norm one) linear positive map from
L^{1}(\mathscr{M},\tau)
onto
L^{1}(\mathscr{N},\tau|\mathscr{N})
.

Proposition 3
.

Let
\mathscr{M}
be a semifinite von Neumann algebra with a normal faithful semifinite trace
\tau
, and let
h
be a positive selfadjoint operator in
L^{1}(\mathscr{M},\tau)
. Then for a continuous convex function
f\in\mathfrak{J}
defined on

\mathbb{R}_{+}=[0,+\infty)
such that
f(h)\in L^{1}(\mathscr{M},\tau)
we have

\displaystyle\tau(f(h))=\sup\{

\displaystyle\tau(f(\mathbb{E}_{\mathscr{A}}h)):\mathscr{A}-\textit{abelian von Neumann}

\displaystyle\textit{subalgebra of $\mathscr{M}$ such that $\tau|\mathscr{A}$ is semifinite}\}.

Proof.

Let

h=\int_{0}^{\infty}t\,e(dt)

be the spectral decomposition of
h
. We have for arbitrary
a>0

(5)

+\infty>\tau(h)=\int_{0}^{\infty}t\,\tau(e(dt))\geqslant\int_{a}^{\infty}t\,\tau(e(dt))\geqslant a\tau(e[a,+\infty)),

showing that for the projection
e((0,+\infty))
we can find a projection
e([a,+\infty))\leqslant e((0,+\infty))
having finite trace. If
\tau(e(\{0\}))<+\infty
, then the algebra
W^{*}(h)
is semifinite (though
\tau(e(0,+\infty))=+\infty
if
\tau
is not finite). If
\tau(e(\{0\}))=+\infty
, choose projections
q_{1},q_{2},\dots
in
\mathscr{M}
such that
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\infty}q_{i}=e(\{0\})
,
\tau(q_{i})<+\infty
, and choose
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\dots
in
(0,+\infty)
such that
e(\{\lambda_{i}\})=0
. Define a new spectral measure
\tilde{e}
by the formula

\tilde{e}|(0,+\infty)\smallsetminus\{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\dots\}=e|(0,+\infty)\smallsetminus\{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\dots\},\qquad\tilde{e}(\{\lambda_{i}\})=q_{i}.

Let
\widetilde{\mathscr{A}}
be the abelian von Neumann subalgebra of
\mathscr{M}
generated by the spectral measure
\tilde{e}
. Then
\tau|\widetilde{\mathscr{A}}
is semifinite. Put

g(t)=\begin{cases}t,&\text{for $t\in(0,+\infty)\smallsetminus\{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\dots\}$}\\
0,&\text{for $t\in\{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\dots\}$}\end{cases}.

We then have

h=\int_{0}^{\infty}g(t)\,\tilde{e}(dt),

thus
h\in L^{1}(\widetilde{\mathscr{A}},\tau|\widetilde{\mathscr{A}})
. Hence for the conditional expectation
\mathbb{E}_{\widetilde{\mathscr{A}}}
(extended to
L^{1}(\mathscr{M},\tau)
) we have

\mathbb{E}_{\widetilde{\mathscr{A}}}h=h.

In either case, there is an abelian von Neumann subalgebra
\widetilde{\mathscr{A}}
of
\mathscr{M}
such that
\tau|\widetilde{\mathscr{A}}
is semifinite, and

\tau(f(h))=\tau(f(\mathbb{E}_{\widetilde{\mathscr{A}}}h)),

which yields the inequality

\displaystyle\tau(f(h))\leqslant\sup\{

\displaystyle\tau(f(\mathbb{E}_{\mathscr{A}}h)):\mathscr{A}-\textit{abelian von Neumann}

\displaystyle\textit{subalgebra of $\mathscr{M}$ such that $\tau|\mathscr{A}$ is semifinite}\}.

On the other hand, from Jensen’s inequality we get for every
\mathscr{A}
as above

\tau(f(\mathbb{E}_{\mathscr{A}}h))\leqslant\tau(\mathbb{E}_{\mathscr{A}}f(h))=\tau(f(h)),

showing the claim.
∎

.

Let
\mathscr{M}
be a semifinite von Neumann algebra with a normal faithful semifinite trace

, and let

be a positive selfadjoint operator in

f\in\mathfrak{J}
defined on

\mathbb{R}_{+}=[0,+\infty)
such that
f(h)\in L^{1}(\mathscr{M},\tau)
we have

\displaystyle\tau(f(h))=\inf\{

\displaystyle\tau(f(\mathbb{E}_{\mathscr{A}}h)):\mathscr{A}-\textit{abelian von Neumann}

\displaystyle\textit{subalgebra of $\mathscr{M}$ such that $\tau|\mathscr{A}$ is semifinite}\}.

The proof follows by considering the convex function
-f

.

By
\mathfrak{A}
we shall denote the family of abelian von Neumann subalgebras of

\{p_{1},p_{2},\dots\}
for some projections

Theorem 5
.

Let
\mathscr{M}
be a semifinite von Neumann algebra with a normal faithful semifinite trace
\tau
, let
h
be a positive selfadjoint operator in
L^{1}(\mathscr{M},\tau)
, and let
f\in\mathfrak{J}
be a continuous convex function defined on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
such that
f(h)\in L^{1}(\mathscr{M},\tau)
. Then

\displaystyle\tau(f(h))

\displaystyle=\sup\{\tau(f(\mathbb{E}_{\mathscr{A}}h)):\mathscr{A}\in\mathfrak{A}\}

\displaystyle=\sup\big\{\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}f(\alpha_{i})\tau(p_{i}):p_{1},p_{2},\dots\textit{--- projections in }\mathscr{M},

\displaystyle\phantom{supiaaaa}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}p_{i}=\mathbb{1},\,\tau(p_{i})<+\infty\},

where

\alpha_{i}=\frac{\tau(p_{i}h)}{\tau(p_{i})}.

Proof.

For
\mathscr{A}
of the form (
6
), let

\mathbb{E}_{\mathscr{A}}h=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{i}p_{i}

be the spectral decomposition of
\mathbb{E}_{\mathscr{A}}h
. From the properties of conditional expectation we get

\mathbb{E}_{\mathscr{A}}(p_{i}h)=p_{i}\mathbb{E}_{\mathscr{A}}h=\alpha_{i}p_{i},

thus

\alpha_{i}=\frac{\tau(p_{i}h)}{\tau(p_{i})}.

Consequently,

\tau\big(f(\mathbb{E}_{\mathscr{A}}h)\big)=\tau\big(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}f(\alpha_{i})p_{i}\big)=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}f(\alpha_{i})\tau(p_{i}),

which shows that

\displaystyle\sup\{\tau(f(\mathbb{E}_{\mathscr{A}}h)):\mathscr{A}\in\mathfrak{A}\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup\big\{\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}f(\alpha_{i})\tau(p_{i}):p_{1},p_{2},\dots\textit{--- projections in }\mathscr{M},

\displaystyle\phantom{supaa}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}p_{i}=\mathbb{1},\,\tau(p_{i})<+\infty\},

since each
\mathscr{A}\in\mathfrak{A}
is generated by projections
p_{1},p_{2},\dots
such that
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\infty}p_{i}=\mathbb{1}
,
\tau(p_{i})<+\infty
.

Let

h=\int_{0}^{\infty}t\,e(dt)

be the spectral decomposition of
h
. Assume first that
\tau(e(\{0\}))=+\infty
, and put
\mu=\tau(e(\cdot))
. The relation (
5
) yields that
\mu
is a
\sigma
-finite measure on
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{0}=(0,+\infty)
.

Let
\{E_{1},E_{2},\dots\}
be a partition of
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{0}
into disjoint Borel sets such that
\mu(E_{i})<+\infty
. Put

\alpha_{i}=\frac{\int_{E_{i}}t\,\mu(dt)}{\mu(E_{i})}=\frac{\tau\big(\int_{E_{i}}t\,e(dt)\big)}{\tau(e(E_{i}))}=\frac{\tau(e(E_{i})h)}{\tau(e(E_{i}))},\quad i=1,2,\dots.

Let
f
be a (continuous) convex function on
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{0}
. From
[
1
, Theorem 1.2]
, it follows that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{0}}f(t)\,\mu(dt)=\sup\big\{\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}f(\alpha_{i})\mu(E_{i}):

\displaystyle\{E_{1},E_{2},\dots\}\textit{ --- partition of $\mathbb{R}_{+}^{0}$}

\displaystyle\textit{such that $\mu(E_{i})<+\infty$}\big\}.

The relation
f(h)\in L^{1}(\mathscr{M},\tau)
implies
f(0)=0
, thus

\tau(f(h))=\tau\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}f(t)\,e(dt)\Big)=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{0}}f(t)\,\tau(e(dt))=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{0}}f(t)\,\mu(dt),

which yields

\displaystyle\tau(f(h))=\sup\big\{\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}f(\alpha_{i})\tau(e(E_{i})):

\displaystyle\{E_{1},E_{2}\dots\}\textit{ --- partition of $\mathbb{R}_{+}^{0}$}

\displaystyle\textit{such that $\tau(e(E_{i}))<+\infty$}\big\}.

Now choose (arbitrary) projections
q_{1},q_{2},\dots
in
\mathscr{M}
such that

\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\infty}q_{i}=e(\{0\})
,
\tau(q_{i})<+\infty
. For the coefficients
\alpha^{\prime}_{i}
we have

\alpha^{\prime}_{i}=\frac{\tau(q_{i}h)}{\tau(q_{i})}=0

since
q_{i}\leqslant e(\{0\})
, and thus
f(\alpha^{\prime}_{i})=0
. It follows that

\displaystyle\tau(f(h))=\sup

\displaystyle\big\{\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}f(\alpha_{i})\tau(e(E_{i}))+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}f(\alpha^{\prime}_{i})\tau(q_{i}):

\displaystyle\{E_{1},E_{2}\dots\}\textit{ --- partition of $\mathbb{R}_{+}^{0}$ such that $\mu(E_{i})<+\infty$}\big\}.

Since

\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}e(E_{i})+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}q_{i}=e((0,+\infty))+e(\{0\})=\mathbb{1},

we obtain the inequality

\displaystyle\tau(f(h))\leqslant\sup\big\{

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}f(\alpha_{i})\tau(p_{i}):p_{1},p_{2},\dots\textit{--- projections in }\mathscr{M},

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}p_{i}=\mathbb{1},\,\tau(p_{i})<+\infty\}.

On the other hand, the inequality

\tau(f(h))\geqslant\sup\{\tau(f(\mathbb{E}_{\mathscr{A}}h)):\mathscr{A}\in\mathfrak{A}\},

follows from Proposition
3
.

Now if
\tau(e(\{0\}))<+\infty
, then the measure
\mu=\tau(e(\cdot))
is
\sigma
-finite on the whole of
\mathbb{R}_{+}
, and we repeat the above reasoning for partitions
\{E_{1},E_{2},\dots\}
of
\mathbb{R}_{+}
, this time without the requirement
f(0)=0
(and without introducing the projections
q_{1},q_{2},\dots
).
∎

As before, we have a counterpart of the theorem above for concave functions.

Theorem 6
.

Let
\mathscr{M}
be a semifinite von Neumann algebra with a normal faithful semifinite trace
\tau
, let
h
be a positive selfadjoint operator in
L^{1}(\mathscr{M},\tau)
, and let
f\in\mathfrak{J}
be a continuous concave function defined on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
such that
f(h)\in L^{1}(\mathscr{M},\tau)
. Then

\displaystyle\tau(f(h))

\displaystyle=\inf\{\tau(f(\mathbb{E}_{\mathscr{A}}h)):\mathscr{A}\in\mathfrak{A}\}

\displaystyle=\inf\big\{\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}f(\alpha_{i})\tau(p_{i}):p_{1},p_{2},\dots\textit{--- projections in }\mathscr{M},

\displaystyle\phantom{infaaaa}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}p_{i}=\mathbb{1},\,\tau(p_{i})<+\infty\},

where

\alpha_{i}=\frac{\tau(p_{i}h)}{\tau(p_{i})}.

As corollaries we obtain the following variational formulae for Segal’s and Rényi’s entropies.

Theorem 9
.

Let
\mathscr{M}
be a semifinite von Neumann algebra, and let
\omega
be a state on
\mathscr{M}
having finite Segal’s entropy. Then the following equalities hold true:

\displaystyle H(\omega)=

\displaystyle\sup\{H(\omega|\mathscr{A}):\mathscr{A}-\textit{abelian von Neumann subalgebra of $\mathscr{M}$}

\displaystyle\phantom{asup}\textit{such that $\tau|\mathscr{A}$ is semifinite}\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup\{H(\omega\circ\Phi):\Phi-\textit{normal positive linear unital map from an}

\mathscr{A}
of
\mathscr{M}
such that
\tau|\mathscr{A}

\displaystyle\phantom{asup}\textit{into $\mathscr{M}$, }\tau\circ\Phi=\tau\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup\{H(\omega\circ\Phi):\Phi-\textit{as above, }\mathscr{A}\in\mathfrak{A}\}

Proof.

For an abelian subalgebra
\mathscr{A}
of
\mathscr{M}
such that
\tau|\mathscr{A}
is semifinite, we have

D_{\omega|\mathscr{A}}=\mathbb{E}_{\mathscr{A}}D_{\omega},

thus the first equality follows from Proposition
3
. Let
\iota_{\mathscr{A}}\colon\mathscr{A}\hookrightarrow\mathscr{M}
be the embedding. Then
\omega|\mathscr{A}=\omega\circ\iota_{\mathscr{A}}
, which yields

\displaystyle H(\omega)=

\displaystyle\sup\{H(\omega\circ\iota_{\mathscr{A}}):\textit{$\mathscr{A}-$ abelian subalgebra of $\mathscr{M}$ such that}

\displaystyle\textit{$\tau|\mathscr{A}$ is semifinite}\}\leqslant\sup\{H(\omega\circ\Phi):\Phi-\textit{normal positive}

linear unital map from an abelian subalgebra
\mathscr{A}
of
\mathscr{M}

\displaystyle\textit{such that $\tau|\mathscr{A}$ is semifinite into $\mathscr{M}$, $\tau\circ\Phi=\tau$}\}.

Since on account of
[
3
, Theorem 11]
we have
H(\omega\circ\Phi)\leqslant H(\omega)
, the second equality follows. The third equality is a consequence of Corollary
7
upon observing that

\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\omega(p_{i})(\log\omega(p_{i})-\log\tau(p_{i}))=H(\omega\circ\iota_{\mathscr{A}}),

where
\mathscr{A}
is the abelian algebra generated by the projections
p_{i}
.

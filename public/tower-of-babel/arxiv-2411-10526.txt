Revisiting Scattering Enhancement from the Aharonov-Bohm Effect
T. Daniel Brennan, Jaipratap Singh Grewal, and Eric Y. Yang

Abstract
We revisit the problem of a charged particle scattering off of an Aharonov-Bohm cosmic string. A classic computation gave an infinite total scattering cross section, leading to a Callan-Rubakov-like enhancement which can have important implications on baryon number asymmetry in the early universe. However, unlike the Callan-Rubakov effect, the Aharonov-Bohm interaction is topological and thus it is surprising that it leads to such a dramatic dynamical effect for single particle scattering. We reexamine this old problem through the modern lens of generalized global symmetries by embedding Aharanov-Bohm strings in a discrete gauge theory. We show that the scattering cross section is suppressed by the core size and there is thus no Callan-Rubakov-like enhancement.

However, the Callan-Rubakov and Aharonov-Bohm effects are of very different origins: the former involves a dynamical gauge field whereas the latter is mediated by a topological gauge field. In the Callan-Rubakov effect, Dirac fermions couple to a

In GUT completions of the Standard Model, this leads to large cross sections for baryon number violating processes. Conversely, in the IR the Aharonov-Bohm string can be described as a surface operator in a

Another puzzling feature of the large scattering cross section is that crossing symmetry relates it to large particle production rates by the cosmic string due to the long range topological interaction. This implies that the vacuum is unstable around the cosmic string (i.e. a
{\mathbb{Z}}_{N}
-gauge theory surface operator) coupled to charged matter, suggesting that the string is either screened or destabilized. However, this option is not allowed by arguments using generalized symmetry.

Generalized symmetries are the extension of ordinary symmetry groups (which act on local operators/fields) to include the action of all topological operators acting on the set of all operators in a theory (for a review see

II
Discrete Gauge Theory and the Aharonov-Bohm Effect

We focus on cosmic strings that induce an Aharonov-Bohm phase in units of
2\pi/N
where
N\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. These strings naturally appear in the low energy limit of the charge
N
Abelian Higgs model which is a
U(1)
gauge theory with a charge
N
complex scalar field
\phi
with potential:

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\cal L}_{\text{UV}}=\frac{1}{2e^{2}}|dA_{1}|^{2}+|D\phi|^{2}-V(\phi)\end{split}

(1)

where
D=d+iNA_{1}
, with
A_{1}
a 1-form
U(1)
gauge field.

One can then choose
V(\Phi)
so that the scalar field condenses and the dynamical gauge field is mostly Higgsed, leaving behind a
{\mathbb{Z}}_{N}
gauge theory described by the action
[
30
]

\displaystyle\begin{split}S_{\text{IR}}=\frac{N}{2\pi}\int dA_{1}\wedge B_{2}\end{split}

(2)

where
B_{2}
is a 2-form
U(1)
gauge field whose gauge variation is given by
\delta B_{2}=d\Lambda_{1}
, with
\Lambda_{1}
a
U(1)
-valued 1-form gauge transformation parameter. Note that the equations of motion prevent dynamical propagation of
A_{1}
or
B_{2}
and the path integral acts like a Fourier transform that localizes to the
{\mathbb{Z}}_{N}
holonomies of
A_{1}
and
B_{2}
.

The effective theory admits a pair of operators: Wilson loops and surface/cosmic string operators

\displaystyle\begin{split}W_{n}(\gamma)=e^{in\oint_{\gamma}A_{1}}\quad,\quad{\cal S}_{q}(\Gamma)=e^{iq\oint_{\Gamma}B_{2}}\end{split}

(3)

.
Both these operators are topological due to the equations of motion and have mutual winding

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left\langle W_{n}(\gamma)\,{\cal S}_{q}(\Gamma)\right\rangle=e^{\frac{2\pi i\,nq}{N}{\rm Link}(\gamma,\Gamma)}\end{split}

(4)

where
{\rm Link}(\gamma,\Gamma)
is the linking number of
\gamma
with
\Gamma
.
This linking follows from the fact that inserting the surface operator
{\cal S}_{q}(\Gamma)
modifies the equations of motion:

\displaystyle\begin{split}dA_{1}=\frac{2\pi q}{N}\delta(\Gamma)\end{split}

(5)

so that
A_{1}
has a non-trivial holonomy
\frac{2\pi q}{N}
around
\Gamma
which is measured by
W_{n}(\gamma)
in the correlation function in (
4
). In terms of the UV physics, the
B_{2}
surface operator carries UV magnetic flux
\Phi=\frac{2\pi q}{eN}
through its core (which can heuristically be thought of as being sourced by the rotating phase of
\phi

Wilson line via Stokes’s Theorem.

In the modern language of generalized symmetries, the Wilson lines are protected by a
1
-form
{\mathbb{Z}}_{N}^{(1)}
global symmetry whose action is generated by linking with the
{\cal S}_{q}
operators and conversely the
{\cal S}_{q}
are protected by a
2
-form
{\mathbb{Z}}_{N}^{(2)}
global symmetry whose action is generated by linking with the

. For more details, see
[
26
]
and references therein.

The cosmic strings can be coupled to a Dirac fermion
\Psi
of charge
1

{\mathbb{Z}}_{N}
subgroup of some
U(1)
vector-like symmetry group

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\cal L}=\frac{N}{2\pi}dA_{1}\wedge B_{2}+\bar{\Psi}(i\not{D}-m)\Psi~\end{split}

(6)

which modifies the equations of motion into

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{N}{2\pi}dA_{1}=0\quad,\quad\frac{N}{2\pi}dB_{2}+\ast j_{\Psi}=0\end{split}

(7)

where

so that
{\cal S}_{q}
is no longer topological and the

global symmetry is explicitly broken. Note that this is consistent with the fact that the Wilson line can be ended on charged local operators.
However, since
dA_{1}=0
, the Wilson loop is still topological. Although the Wilson loop can be cut, the end points of the Wilson line are not topological, meaning the topological Wilson loop can still enact the
{\mathbb{Z}}_{N}^{(2)}
global symmetry
and as such the cosmic string is topologically protected.

Now consider the fermion field in the presence of an
{\cal S}_{q}(\Sigma)
operator wrapping the

-axis on
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{t}
.
In the partition function,

is a

gauge field with

is a single-valued field. After inserting
{\cal S}_{q}
into the path integral, the

field can be integrated out by shifting

for

so that it obeys

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Psi(\theta)=e^{\frac{2\pi iq}{N}}\Psi(\theta+2\pi)\end{split}

(8)

where

and then integrating out

to be a

Another way to see that the operator

both turns on the gauge field and causes

\displaystyle\begin{split}V_{\alpha}(\Sigma;\sigma)=e^{i\alpha\oint_{\Sigma}\frac{NB_{2}}{2\pi}+i\alpha\int_{\sigma}\ast j_{\Psi}}\end{split}

(9)

where
\partial\sigma=\Sigma
and the operator

Similarly, a scalar field of charge

II.1
Scattering Cross Section

Consider scattering charge 1 fermions
\Psi
perpendicularly off of the string along the
x
-direction. Due to the
z
-translation symmetry, the problem reduces to a
(2+1)d
problem. To compute the scattering cross section, decompose the wave function into an incident (
\Psi^{(i)}
) and a scattered cylindrical wave
(\Psi^{(s)}
)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\lim_{r\to\infty}\Psi=\Psi^{(i)}+\Psi^{(s)}\end{split}

(11)

Importantly,
\Psi^{(i)}
and
\Psi^{(s)}
are both multi-valued as in equation (
8
).

We need the mode expansion of the fermions in the background with fixed
A_{1}
given by

\displaystyle\begin{split}A_{1}=\frac{q}{N}d\theta\end{split}

(12)

If we expand
\Psi
in terms of 2-component Weyl fermions
\Psi=(\psi,\bar{\chi})^{T}

and use the
\gamma
-matrix conventions from
[
22
]

\displaystyle\begin{split}\gamma^{0}&=\begin{pmatrix}\sigma_{3}&0\\
0&-\sigma_{3}\end{pmatrix}\quad~,\quad\gamma^{1}=\begin{pmatrix}i\sigma_{2}&0\\
0&-i\sigma_{2}\end{pmatrix}\\
\gamma^{2}&=\begin{pmatrix}-i\sigma_{1}&0\\
0&i\sigma_{1}\end{pmatrix}~~,\quad\gamma^{3}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\mathds{1}\\
-\mathds{1}&0\end{pmatrix}\end{split}

(13)

then the equations for
\psi,\chi
decouple and can be written as

\displaystyle\begin{split}(\omega-m)\psi_{1}=&-ie^{-i\theta}\left(\partial_{r}-\frac{i}{r}\left(\partial_{\theta}+\frac{iq}{N}\right)\right)\psi_{2}\\
(\omega+m)\psi_{2}=&-ie^{i\theta}\left(\partial_{r}+\frac{i}{r}\left(\partial_{\theta}+\frac{iq}{N}\right)\right)\psi_{1}\end{split}

(14)

where

\displaystyle\begin{split}\psi=e^{-i\omega t}\begin{pmatrix}\psi_{1}\\
\psi_{2}\end{pmatrix}\end{split}

(15)

and similarly for
\chi_{1,2}
with
q\to-q
. Using the periodicity condition

\displaystyle\begin{split}\psi(\theta)=&e^{2\pi iq/N}\psi(\theta+2\pi)\\
\chi(\theta)=&e^{-2\pi iq/N}\chi(\theta+2\pi)\end{split}

(16)

we find that the field can be decomposed into a linear combination of the
\hat{L}_{z}
eigen-modes

\displaystyle\begin{split}\psi_{n}^{(\pm)}=\,e^{-i\frac{q}{N}\theta-i\omega t}\begin{pmatrix}J_{\pm n}(kr)e^{in\theta}\\
\frac{\pm ik}{\omega+m}J_{\pm(n+1)}(kr)\,e^{i(n+1)\theta}\end{pmatrix}\end{split}

(17)

where
k^{2}=\omega^{2}-m^{2}
.
Notice that the
q
-dependence only appears in the periodicity of
\theta
– this
follows from the fractional flux in the
A_{1}
gauge field induced by the Aharonov-Bohm string and is required by the corresponding periodicity of
\Psi(x)
in (
8
). This expansion differs from the standard expansion of a fermion in cylindrical coordinates only by the overall phase. Crucially, our expansion differs from that of
[
22
]
which did not obey the periodicity condition imposed by the
{\cal S}_{q}
operator.

Consider scattering an incident wave

\displaystyle\begin{split}\psi^{(i)}=\begin{pmatrix}1&\\
\frac{-ik}{\omega+m}\end{pmatrix}e^{-i\omega t-ikx-i\frac{q}{N}\theta}~,\end{split}

(18)

which corresponds to incoming fermions along the
x
-direction

\displaystyle\begin{split}j^{\mu}_{\Psi}=i\bar{\Psi}\gamma^{\mu}\Psi=\left(\frac{2im}{\omega+m},\frac{2k}{\omega+m},0,0\right)~.\end{split}

(19)

Using the identity

\displaystyle\begin{split}e^{-ikx-\frac{iq}{N}\theta}=e^{-\frac{iq}{N}\theta}\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}i^{n}J_{n}(kr)e^{in\theta}~,\end{split}

(20)

we can expand the wave function into incident and scattered components as in (
11

17

\Psi^{(s)}=0
and therefore that the differential scattering cross section is zero:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{d\sigma}{d\theta}=0\end{split}

(21)

•

We only impose that the multi-valued wave function be finite as
r\to 0
since the cosmic string in the
{\mathbb{Z}}_{N}
gauge theory is not a disorder operator.
Additional local interactions on the cosmic string can be incorporated by upgrading the string to a defect operator with additional boundary conditions/interactions on its world volume.

•

Our computation reproduces the experimentally observed effects of the Aharonov-Bohm effect. Recall that the double-slit experiment requires two wave packets to scatter around the solenoid. The phase dependence we have noted for charged fields will lead to phase differences between the two wave packets and will produce the observed interference effect. Notice however that if
\frac{d\sigma}{d\theta}\neq 0
for the single particle scattering, there would be an additional observable interference pattern in the single-slit experiment in the presence of a solenoid due to the asymptotic decomposition of the wave function

\displaystyle\begin{split}\lim_{r\to\infty}\Psi(x)=e^{-ikx-i\frac{q}{N}\theta}\psi_{0}+\frac{e^{ikr-i\frac{q}{N}\theta}}{\sqrt{r}}\psi_{1}(\theta)\end{split}

(22)

for spinors
\psi_{0}
,
\psi_{1}(\theta)
.

•

In a UV complete theory where the cosmic string is the IR limit of a smooth field configuration, we expect that there will be a non-vanishing scattering cross section. The vanishing of the scattering cross section in the effective IR theory can be interpreted as the statement that the scattering cross section is suppressed by the core size
R
which has been computed for a hard-core string in

to be

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sigma_{tot}\sim\frac{\pi^{2}}{k\,{\rm log}^{2}(kR)}+O(R)\end{split}

so that

We can similarly compute the (transverse) differential scattering cross section of a charge 1 scalar field
\Phi
off of
{\cal S}_{q}

\displaystyle\begin{split}\lim_{x\to\infty}\Phi=\Phi^{(i)}+\Phi^{(s)}\end{split}

where

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Phi^{(i)}=e^{-ikx-i\frac{q}{N}\theta}\quad,\quad\Phi^{(s)}=\frac{f(\theta)}{\sqrt{r}}e^{ikr-i\frac{q}{N}\theta}\end{split}

(25)

The mode expansion of
\Phi
is given by solutions of

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left(\omega^{2}-m^{2}+\partial_{r}^{2}+\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r}+\frac{1}{r^{2}}\left(\partial_{\theta}+\frac{iq}{N}\right)^{2}\right)\phi=0\end{split}

where

. Using the periodicity condition in (

results in

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Phi(x)=e^{-i\omega t-i\frac{q}{N}\theta}\sum_{n}c_{n}~e^{-in\theta}J_{n}(kr)~,\end{split}

where

. As before, using the identity (

so that

and the differential scattering cross section
\frac{d\sigma}{d\theta}=|f(\theta)|^{2}
vanishes up to corrections suppressed by the string core size.

III
Application to Callan-Rubakov Effect for Cosmic Strings

Enhanced scattering of fermions off of a cosmic string (such as proposed in

Consider a theory with a cosmic string coupled to a pair of Dirac fermions

In cosmological settings,

Abstract
. For any prime power q q , a polynomial f ​ ( X ) ∈ 𝔽 q ​ [ X ] f(X)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X] is “exceptional” if it induces bijections of 𝔽 q k \mathbb{F}_{q^{k}} for infinitely many k k ; this condition is known to be equivalent to f ⁡ ( X ) f(X) inducing a bijection of 𝔽 q k \mathbb{F}_{q^{k}} for at least one k k with q k ≥ deg ⁡ ( f ) 4 q^{k}\geq\deg(f)^{4} . In this paper, we introduce the notion of an “exceptional” extension of local fields of any characteristic, and show that if f ​ ( X ) ∈ 𝔽 q ​ [ X ] f(X)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X] is exceptional in the classical sense then the field extension 𝔽 q ​ ( X ) / 𝔽 q ​ ( f ⁡ ( X ) ) \mathbb{F}_{q}(X)/\mathbb{F}_{q}(f(X)) yields an exceptional local field extension upon passing to the completion at a degree- 1 1 place. We describe all exceptional local field extensions of degree coprime to the residue characteristic, determine the relationship between exceptionality of a local field extension and exceptionality of a subextension, and give various Galois-theoretic characterizations of exceptional local field extensions. As a consequence, we obtain three new proofs, using quite different tools, of a theorem of Guralnick and Müller about ramification indices in exceptional maps between curves over 𝔽 q \mathbb{F}_{q} . This theorem generalizes a result of Lenstra which subsumes earlier conjectures of Carlitz and Wan.



1.
Introduction

A polynomial
f(X)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]
is called a
permutation polynomial
if the map
\alpha\mapsto f(\alpha)
defines a bijection on
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Permutation polynomials arise in various contexts in math and engineering. Dickson determined the low-degree permutation polynomials over prime fields
[
9
]
and over arbitrary finite fields
[
10
]
. See
[
11
]
for some recent progress on the study of permutation polynomials.

A rational function
f(X)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}(X)
is called
exceptional
if the map
\alpha\mapsto f(\alpha)
is a bijection on
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{k}})\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\mathbb{F}_{q^{k}}\cup\{\infty\}
for infinitely many positive integers
k
. The study of exceptional rational functions was initiated in Dickson’s 1896 thesis
[
10
]
, which investigated the special case of exceptional polynomials. See
[
12
,
13
,
16
,
17
,
18
,
19
,
20
,
23
,
30
]
for some recent progress on exceptional rational functions.

One of the most famous problems in this subject is the Carlitz–Wan conjecture. Cavior
[
4
]
first posed the “Carlitz conjecture” as a question. Cavior’s advisor Carlitz mentioned this question later in a talk, after which the question became known as the Carlitz conjecture.

Conjecture 1.1

(The Carlitz Conjecture)
.

There exists no even-degree exceptional polynomial over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
for any odd prime power
q
.

The Carlitz conjecture was proved for polynomials of degree coprime to
q
by Davenport and Lewis
[
8
]
and Hayes
[
22
]
, which in particular includes the case of degree
2^{k}
. Hayes
[
22
]
and Wan
[
27
]
made progress on the Carlitz conjecture in low degrees, then the degree twice a prime case was resolved by Cohen
[
6
]
and Wan
[
28
]
. The Carlitz conjecture in full generality was first proved by Fried, Guralnick and Saxl
[
15
]
using the classification of finite simple groups.

Wan
[
29
]
posed and proved in some cases a generalization of the Carlitz conjecture, which became known as the Carlitz–Wan conjecture.

Conjecture 1.2

(The Carlitz–Wan Conjecture)
.

For any prime power
q
, every exceptional polynomial over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
has degree coprime to
q-1
.

The Carlitz conjecture follows from the Carlitz–Wan conjecture in the even degree case. Note that Hayes
[
22
]
had previously proved the Carlitz–Wan conjecture for polynomials of degree coprime to
q
. The Carlitz–Wan conjecture follows from
[
15
]
in the case of characteristic at least
5
, which depends on the classification of finite simple groups.
The Carlitz–Wan conjecture in full generality was first proved in 1994 by Lenstra, via an argument which does not rely on any difficult results. Lenstra never published his proof, perhaps in part because Cohen and Fried
[
7
]
published a version of Lenstra’s proof soon after he sent it to them. However, Lenstra communicated several versions of his proof to the finite fields community through his emails and his lectures.

The Carlitz–Wan conjecture about exceptional polynomials can be generalized to exceptional covers of curves. By a curve over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
we mean a dimension-
1
geometrically integral separated scheme over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
of finite type. Throughout this paper we assume in addition that all curves are nonsingular and projective. See
[
21
,
26
]
for basic knowledge of algebraic geometry and in particular algebraic curves. We need the following notion of exceptionality for curves.

Definition 1.3
.

A finite morphism
f\colon X\to Y
of curves over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is called an
exceptional cover
if the diagonal is the only geometrically irreducible component defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
of the fiber product
X\times_{Y}X
.

The prototypical examples of exceptional covers come from isogenies of elliptic curves over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, which are exceptional if and only if there exist no nonzero
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-rational points in the kernel. See
[
19
]
for the study of exceptional covers of varieties of possibly higher dimension.

Guralnick and Müller
[
16
, §8]
gave two abstract group-theoretic proofs of a generalization of the Carlitz–Wan conjecture as follows.

Theorem 1.4
.

Assume
f\colon X\to Y
is a finite morphism of curves over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Suppose
f\colon X\to Y
is an exceptional cover. Then the ramification index of
f
at any
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-rational point of
X
is coprime to
q-1
.

As will be explained later in this paper, the Carlitz–Wan conjecture follows from the special case of Theorem
1.4
when both curves
X
and
Y
have genus
0
and the
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-rational point is totally ramified.

In this paper, we give three different proofs of Theorem
1.4
, which may be accessible and appreciated by readers with various backgrounds. More importantly, we develop the new theory of exceptionality for local fields, whose applications include the enlightening of the truth of the Carlitz–Wan conjecture and its generalization Theorem
1.4
.

The following is our new notion of exceptionality about local fields.

Definition 1.5
.

A finite separable extension
L/K
of local fields is
exceptional
if the following hold where
N
is the Galois closure of
L/K
:

(1)

L
is totally ramified over
K
;

(2)

for any
\sigma\in\Gal(N/K)\setminus\Gal(N/L)
the compositum
L.\sigma(L)
of
L
and
\sigma(L)
is not totally ramified over
K
.

In this paper, we show that if
f(X)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}(X)
is exceptional in the classical sense then the field extension
\mathbb{F}_{q}(X)/\mathbb{F}_{q}(f(X))
yields an exceptional local field extension upon passing to the completion at a degree-one place.
We determine the relationship between exceptionality of a local field extension and exceptionality of a subextension, and give various Galois-theoretic characterizations of exceptional local field extensions.
Moreover, the following result describes all exceptional local field extensions of degree coprime to the residue characteristic, which sheds new insights on the conclusion of the Carlitz–Wan conjecture and its generalization Theorem
1.4
.

Theorem 1.6
.

Assume
L/K
is a finite separable extension of local fields. Write
n\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}[L:K]
and let
\mathbb{F}_{q}
be the residue field of
K
. Suppose
L/K
is totally ramified and
\gcd(n,q)=1
. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

L/K
is exceptional;

(2)

\gcd(n,q-1)=1
;

(3)

K
contains no nontrivial
n
-th root of unity;

(4)

there exists no intermediate field
M
of
L/K
such that
M
is a proper Galois extension over
K
.

As a consequence of our theory of exceptionality for local fields, we obtain three new proofs, using quite different tools, of the Guralnick–Müller result Theorem
1.4
, which rely on three quite different collections of tools, all of which differ from the group-theoretic arguments used by Guralnick–Müller.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. In Section 2 we introduce the new notion of exceptionality for local fields and obtain some elementary properties on exceptional local field extensions. In Section 3 we first show the Carlitz-Wan conjecture follows from Theorem
1.4
and then we give two quick proofs of Theorem
1.4
. In Section 4 we establish in detail the new theory of exceptionality for local fields. We conclude this paper in Section 5 by giving the third proof of Theorem
1.4
.

2.
Elementary properties on exceptional local field extensions

The main result in this section is the following elementary property on exceptional extensions of local fields, which will be used to give two quick proofs the generalization Theorem
1.4
of the Carlitz–Wan conjecture. See
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
14
,
24
,
25
]
for basic knowledge of local fields.

Theorem 2.1
.

Assume
L/K
is an exceptional extension of local fields of degree
n
. Then
\gcd(n,q-1)=1
where
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is the residue field of
K
.

By a local field we mean a field which is complete with respect to a discrete valuation and has finite residue field. More explicitly, a local field is either a finite extension of the field
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
of
p
-adic numbers or the field
\mathbb{F}_{q}((X))
of formal Laurent series over a finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. We recall the notion of exceptionality for local fields as follows.

Definition 2.2
.

A finite separable extension
L/K
of local fields is
exceptional
if the following hold where
N
is the Galois closure of
L/K
:

(1)

L
is totally ramified over
K
;

(2)

for any
\sigma\in\Gal(N/K)\setminus\Gal(N/L)
the compositum
L.\sigma(L)
of
L
and
\sigma(L)
is not totally ramified over
K
.

Remark 2.3
.

In particular, if
L/K
is an exceptional extension of local fields, then the identity map is the only automorphism of
L
which fixes each element of
K
.
For, any such automorphism can be extended to an element
\sigma
of
\Gal(N/K)
for which
\sigma(L)=L
, and then condition (2) implies
\sigma\in\Gal(N/L)
, so that
\sigma|_{L}
is the identity on
L
.
Thus, an exceptional local field extension
L/K
satisfies
\Aut_{K}(L)=\{\text{Id}_{L}\}
, which is the opposite extreme from
L/K
being Galois.

Remark 2.4
.

Note that elements in
\Gal(N/K)\setminus\Gal(N/L)
correspond canonically to non-identity elements in
\Hom_{K}(L,N)
, which is the set of all
K
-algebra homomorphisms of
L
into
N
. Thus exceptional local field extensions are the opposite of being Galois extensions, in the sense that there is no nontrivial
K
-algebra endomorphism of
L
.

We need the following result in the proof of Theorem
2.1
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Any exceptional extension
L/K
of local fields has no intermediate field
M
such that
M
is a proper Galois extension over
K
.

Proof.

Let
N
be the Galois closure of
L/K
. Write
N\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\overline{\phantom{\rm N}}$},L\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\overline{\phantom{\rm L}}$},K\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\overline{\phantom{\rm K}}$}
for the residue fields of
N,L,K
respectively, so that
L\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\overline{\phantom{\rm L}}$}=K\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\overline{\phantom{\rm K}}$}=\mathbb{F}_{q}
for some power
q
of the characteristic
p
of
K\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\overline{\phantom{\rm K}}$}
. By passing to the residue fields we have a canonical homomorphism
\rho\colon\Gal(N/K)\to\Gal(N\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\overline{\phantom{\rm N}}$}/K\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\overline{\phantom{\rm K}}$})
of groups, which is surjective since
K\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\overline{\phantom{\rm K}}$}
is perfect. Let
\theta
be the
q
-th power map on
N\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\overline{\phantom{\rm N}}$}
, so that
\theta
is a generator of
\Gal(N\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\overline{\phantom{\rm N}}$}/K\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\overline{\phantom{\rm K}}$})
. Write
\Frob(N/K^{\prime})\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\rho^{-1}(\theta)\cap\Gal(N/K^{\prime})
for any intermediate field
K^{\prime}
of
N/K
.

First, we claim that
\Frob(N/\sigma(L))
and
\Frob(N/\tau(L))
are disjoint for any
\sigma,\tau\in\Gal(N/K)
with
\sigma^{-1}\tau\notin\Gal(N/L)
. Henceforth we suppose
\sigma,\tau\in\Gal(N/K)
with
\sigma^{-1}\tau\notin\Gal(N/L)
. Since
L/K
is exceptional
L.(\sigma^{-1}\tau)(L)
is not totally ramified over
K
, or equivalently
\sigma(L).\tau(L)
is not totally ramified over
K
, which says the residue field of
\sigma(L).\tau(L)
is a proper extension over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. It follows that
\Gal(N/\sigma(L).\tau(L))
is disjoint from
\rho^{-1}(\theta)
, which implies
\Frob(N/\sigma(L))
and
\Frob(N/\tau(L))
are disjoint. This concludes the proof of our claim.

Next, let us show
\Frob(N/K)
is the union of
\Frob(N/\sigma(L))
with
\sigma\in\Gal(N/K)
. Since the homomorphism
\rho\colon\Gal(N/K)\to\Gal(N\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\overline{\phantom{\rm N}}$}/K\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\overline{\phantom{\rm K}}$})
is surjective we know that
\lvert\Gal(N/K)\rvert=\lvert\Frob(N/K)\rvert\cdot\lvert\Gal(N\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\overline{\phantom{\rm N}}$}/K\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\overline{\phantom{\rm K}}$})\rvert
. Similarly we have
\lvert\Gal(N/\sigma(L))\rvert=\lvert\Frob(N/\sigma(L))\rvert\cdot\lvert\Gal(N\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\overline{\phantom{\rm N}}$}/K\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\overline{\phantom{\rm K}}$})\rvert
for any
\sigma\in\Gal(N/K)
. Hence for any
\sigma\in\Gal(N/K)
we have

\frac{\lvert\Frob(N/K)\rvert}{\lvert\Frob(N/\sigma(L))\rvert}=\frac{\lvert\Gal(N/K)\rvert}{\lvert\Gal(N/\sigma(L))\rvert}=[\sigma(L):K]=[L:K].

The claim says that
\Frob(N/\sigma(L))
and
\Frob(N/\tau(L))
are disjoint for any
\sigma,\tau\in\Gal(N/K)
which lie in different left cosets of
\Gal(N/L)
in
\Gal(N/K)
. It follows that

\Frob(N/K)=\bigcup_{\sigma\in\Gal(N/K)}\Frob(N/\sigma(L))

since there are
[L:K]
left cosets of
\Gal(N/L)
in
\Gal(N/K)
.

Suppose
M
is an intermediate field of
L/K
such that
M
is Galois over
K
, so that
\Frob(N/M)=\Frob(N/K)
since

\Frob(N/K)=\bigcup_{\sigma\in\Gal(N/K)}\Frob(N/\sigma(L))\subseteq\Frob(N/M)\subseteq\Frob(N/K).

Since
M/K
is totally ramified we have
\lvert\Gal(N/M)\rvert=\lvert\Frob(N/M)\rvert\cdot\lvert\Gal(N\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\overline{\phantom{\rm N}}$}/K\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\overline{\phantom{\rm K}}$})\rvert
, so that
\lvert\Gal(N/M)\rvert=\lvert\Gal(N/K)\rvert
, which implies that
\Gal(N/M)=\Gal(N/K)
, or equivalently
M=K
.
∎

Now we are ready to prove Theorem

as follows.

.

Let
\mathcal{O}_{K}
be the ring of integers in

and
\mathfrak{m}_{K}
be its maximal ideal, so that
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is the residue field

of

. Let
x
and

be uniformizers of the local fields

and
K
, respectively.
Since

is totally ramified we have

for some

with
a_{0}\notin\mathfrak{m}_{K}
. We may assume

by replacing

with

if necessary. Write

for some
\ell\geq 0
and

with

.

, thus

. Write
M\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}K(y)
, so that
[M:K]=m
and

.

We claim that

-th root

of unity, which says that the residue field

of

3.
Two quick proofs of the Carlitz–Wan conjecture

In this section, first we show Theorem
1.4
implies the Carlitz–Wan conjecture, and then we give two quick proofs of Theorem
1.4
.

We will use the following classical result, see
[
5
,
19
]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

For any
f(X)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}(X)
the following are equivalent:

(1)

f(X)
is exceptional over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
;

(2)

irreducible factors of the numerator of
f(X)-f(Y)
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[X,Y]
which remain irreducible in
\mathbb{F}\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\overline{\phantom{\rm\mathbb{F}}}$}_{q}[X,Y]
are nonzero constants in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
times
X-Y
, where
\mathbb{F}\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\overline{\phantom{\rm\mathbb{F}}}$}_{q}
is the algebraic closure of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

Next, we show Theorem
1.4
has the following consequence.

Corollary 3.2
.

For any exceptional
f(X)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}(X)
the ramification index of
f(X)
at any point in
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
is coprime to
(q-1)
.

Proof.

Suppose
f(X)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}(X)
is nonconstant, so that it induces a finite morphism
f\colon\mathbb{P}^{1}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
of curves. By Lemma
3.1
the exceptionality of
f(X)
says that
f\colon\mathbb{P}^{1}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
is an exceptional cover. By Theorem
1.4
it follows that the ramification index of
f(X)
at any point in
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
is coprime to
(q-1)
. This concludes the proof.
∎

Clearly, the Carlitz–Wan conjecture is Corollary
3.2
in the special case of polynomials. Thus it remains only to show Theorem
1.4
.

In light of Theorem
2.1
, the proof of Theorem
1.4
is reduced to the following result, which says that the exceptionality of function fields implies the exceptionality of local fields at any rational place.

Theorem 3.3
.

Assume
f\colon X\to Y
is a finite separable morphism of curves over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Suppose
f\colon X\to Y
is an exceptional cover. Then, for any
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-point
u\in X(\mathbb{F}_{q})
, the extension
\mathbb{F}_{q}(X)_{u}/\mathbb{F}_{q}(Y)_{v}
of local fields is exceptional, where
\mathbb{F}_{q}(X)_{u}
and
\mathbb{F}_{q}(Y)_{v}
are the completions of functions fields
\mathbb{F}_{q}(X)
and
\mathbb{F}_{q}(Y)
at the places
u
and
v\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}f(u)
, respectively.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

We may assume that the morphism
f\colon X\to Y
of curves is finite separable. Let
u\in X(\mathbb{F}_{q})
be an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-point of
X
, so that
v\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}f(u)\in Y(\mathbb{F}_{q})
is an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-point of
Y
. By Theorem
3.3
we know the extension
\mathbb{F}_{q}(X)_{u}/\mathbb{F}_{q}(Y)_{v}
of local fields is exceptional, where
\mathbb{F}_{q}(X)_{u}
and
\mathbb{F}_{q}(Y)_{v}
are the completions of
\mathbb{F}_{q}(X)
and
\mathbb{F}_{q}(Y)
at the places
u
and
v
, respectively. By Theorem
2.1
it follows that
[\mathbb{F}_{q}(X)_{u}:\mathbb{F}_{q}(Y)_{v}]
is coprime to
q-1
. This concludes the proof since
[\mathbb{F}_{q}(X)_{u}:\mathbb{F}_{q}(Y)_{v}]
is equal to the ramification index of
f
at
u
.
∎

Next, we give two different proofs of Theorem
3.3
as follows.

3.1.
The first proof

Our first proof of Theorem
3.3
argues in terms of tensor products of function fields and their completions.

The first proof of Theorem
3.3
.

For ease of expression, let
L\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\mathbb{F}_{q}(X)
and
K\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\mathbb{F}_{q}(Y)
be the function fields of
X
and
Y
, respectively. Thus
L/K
is a finite separable extension of function fields over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Thus
L\otimes_{K}L
is isomorphic as
K
-algebras to
\prod_{i=1}^{r}L_{i}
, where
L_{i}
are finite separable extension fields over
K
with
L_{1}=L
which corresponds to the diagonal of the fiber product
X\times_{Y}X
.
The assumption that
f\colon X\to Y
is an exceptional cover says that
L_{1}
is the only geometrically irreducible among
L_{i}
as function fields over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
We claim that any function field over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
which has one rational place is geometrically irreducible. Indeed, this is equivalent to that any nonsingular projective curve
C
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
which has one
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-point

is geometrically irreducible. Suppose otherwise
C
is not geometrically irreducible. Since the point
P
is on the curve
C
, we know that
P
is on one geometrically irreducible component of
C
, which by the

-th power map implies that
P
is on each geometrically irreducible component of
C
. Since
C
has more than one geometrically irreducible components, it follows that
P
is a singular point on
C
, which contradicts with the assumption that
C
is nonsingular.
Hence each function field
L_{i}
with
i>1
has no
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-rational places since it is not geometrically irreducible. Moreover, each
L_{i}
can be viewed as a finite separable extension over
L
via the right
L
-module structure of
L\otimes_{K}L
.
Let
L_{u}
and
K_{v}
be the completions of
L
and
K
at places
u
and
v
, respectively. Since both
u
and
v
are
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-rational, the extension
L_{u}/K_{v}
is a totally ramified extension of local fields with
\mathbb{F}_{q}
as residue fields. Moreover, we have
L\otimes_{K}K_{v}=\prod_{j=1}^{s}L_{u_{j}}=L_{u}\times\prod_{j=2}^{s}L_{u_{j}}
, where
u_{1},\dots,u_{s}
are all places of
L
lying over
v
with
u_{1}=u
.

Let us compute the tensor product
L\otimes_{K}L_{u}
in two different ways. On the one hand, by viewing the
K
-algebra structure of
L_{u}
via the composition
K\to L\to L_{u}
, we can compute

\displaystyle L\otimes_{K}L_{u}

\displaystyle\cong L\otimes_{K}\bigl(L\otimes_{L}L_{u}\bigr)

\displaystyle\cong\bigl(L\otimes_{K}L\bigr)\otimes_{L}L_{u}

\displaystyle\cong\Bigl(\prod_{i=1}^{r}L_{i}\Bigr)\otimes_{L}L_{u}

\displaystyle\cong\prod_{i=1}^{r}\bigl(L_{i}\otimes_{L}L_{u}\bigr)

\displaystyle\cong L_{u}\times\prod_{i=2}^{r}\bigl(L_{i}\otimes_{L}L_{u}\bigr).

Since each
L_{i}
with
i\geq 2
has no
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-rational places, it follows that
\prod_{i=2}^{r}\bigl(L_{i}\otimes_{L}L_{u}\bigr)
is a product of local fields whose residue fields are proper extensions over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Thus the local field
L_{u}
is the only factor of the tensor product
L\otimes_{K}L_{u}
whose residue field is
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. On the other hand, by viewing the
K
-algebra structure of
L_{u}
via the composition
K\to K_{v}\to L_{u}
, we can compute

\displaystyle L\otimes_{K}L_{u}

\displaystyle\cong L\otimes_{K}\bigl(K_{v}\otimes_{K_{v}}L_{u}\bigr)

\displaystyle\cong\bigl(L\otimes_{K}K_{v}\bigr)\otimes_{K_{v}}L_{u}

\displaystyle\cong\Bigl(\prod_{j=1}^{s}L_{u_{j}}\Bigr)\otimes_{K_{v}}L_{u}

\displaystyle\cong\prod_{j=1}^{s}\bigl(L_{u_{j}}\otimes_{K_{v}}L_{u}\bigr)

\displaystyle\cong\bigl(L_{u}\otimes_{K_{v}}L_{u}\bigr)\times\prod_{j=2}^{s}\bigl(L_{u_{j}}\otimes_{K_{v}}L_{u}\bigr).

By comparing the above two expressions of
L\otimes_{K}L_{u}
it follows that the local field
L_{u}
is the only factor of
L_{u}\otimes_{K_{v}}L_{u}
whose residue field is
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Therefore, the finite separable extension
L_{u}/K_{v}
of local fields is totally ramified, while the compositum of any two distinct conjugates of
L_{u}
are not totally ramified over
K_{v}
. Thus
L_{u}/K_{v}
is an exceptional extension of local fields, which concludes the proof.
∎

3.2.
The second proof

Our second proof of Theorem
3.3
uses some basic properties on Galois groups of extensions of function fields.

For ease of expression we introduce the following notion, which is the motivating instance of the general notion of exceptional field extensions introduced in Lenstra’s 1995 talk at Glasgow.

Definition 3.4
.

Assume
L/K
is a finite separable extension such that
\mathbb{F}_{q}\subseteq K
. We say
L/K
has the
Lenstra-property
if the following hold where
N
is the Galois closure of
L/K
:

(1)

\mathbb{F}_{q}
is algebraically closed in
L
;

(2)

\mathbb{F}_{q}
is not algebraically closed in the compositum
L.\sigma(L)
of
L
and
\sigma(L)
for any
\sigma\in\Gal(N/K)\setminus\Gal(N/L)
.

Our second proof of Theorem
3.3
proceeds in two steps. First, we show exceptional extensions of function fields have the Lenstra-property.

Lemma 3.5
.

Assume
f\colon X\to Y
is a finite separable morphism of curves over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Suppose
f\colon X\to Y
is an exceptional cover. Then the function field extension
\mathbb{F}_{q}(X)/\mathbb{F}_{q}(Y)
has the Lenstra-property.

Proof.

Let
\Omega
be the Galois closure of the finite separable extension
\mathbb{F}_{q}(X)/\mathbb{F}_{q}(Y)
of function fields over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, and let
\mathbb{F}_{q^{\ell}}
be the algebraically closure of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
in
\Omega
.
Write
A\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\Gal(\Omega/\mathbb{F}_{q}(Y))
,
G\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\Gal(\Omega/\mathbb{F}_{q^{\ell}}(Y))
,
A_{1}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\Gal(\Omega/\mathbb{F}_{q}(X))
,
G_{1}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\Gal(\Omega/\mathbb{F}_{q^{\ell}}(X))
, so that
A
acts naturally on the set
\mathcal{S}
of the left cosets of
A_{1}
in
A
.
Moreover,
G_{1}
is a normal subgroup of
A_{1}
and the quotient
A_{1}/G_{1}
is canonically isomorphic to
\Gal(\mathbb{F}_{q^{\ell}}/\mathbb{F}_{q})
. The exceptionality of
f\colon X\to Y
says that
\{A_{1}\}
is the only common orbit of
\mathcal{S}
under the actions by
A_{1}
and
G_{1}
.

Note that
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is algebraically closed in
\mathbb{F}_{q}(X)
since
X
is geometrically integral over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Suppose
\sigma\in\Gal(\Omega/\mathbb{F}_{q}(Y))\setminus\Gal(\Omega/\mathbb{F}_{q}(X))
. It is enough to show that
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is not algebraically closed in the compositum
\mathbb{F}_{q}(X).\sigma(\mathbb{F}_{q}(X))
.
Write
H\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\Gal(\Omega/\mathbb{F}_{q}(X).\sigma(\mathbb{F}_{q}(X)))
, so that
H
is a subgroup of
A_{1}
. Since
\Gal(\mathbb{F}_{q^{\ell}}/\mathbb{F}_{q})
is cyclic, the image
\rho(H)
of
H
under the composition
\rho\colon A_{1}\to A_{1}/G_{1}\to\Gal(\mathbb{F}_{q^{\ell}}/\mathbb{F}_{q})
is cyclic. Pick
\tau\in H
such that
\rho(\tau)
is a generator of
\rho(H)
.
Thus
\tau\in A_{1}
and
\tau
fixes two distinct elements
A_{1}
and
\sigma A_{1}
in
\mathcal{S}
. Since
\{A_{1}\}
is the only common orbit of
\mathcal{S}
under the actions by
A_{1}
and
G_{1}
, it follows that the image
\tau
in
A_{1}/G_{1}
cannot be a generator of
A_{1}/G_{1}
, or equivalently
\rho(H)=\Gal(\mathbb{F}_{q^{\ell}}/\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}})
for some divisor
m>1
of
\ell
.
It follows that
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
is contained in
\mathbb{F}_{q}(X).\sigma(\mathbb{F}_{q}(X))
, which implies that
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is not algebraically closed in
\mathbb{F}_{q}(X).\sigma(\mathbb{F}_{q}(X))
. It concludes the proof.
∎

Next, we show the Lenstra-property is preserved by the completion.

Lemma 3.6
.

Assume
L/K
is a finite separable extension of function fields over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Suppose
L/K
satisfies the Lenstra-property and
u
is any
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-rational place of
L
. Let
v
be the place of
K
lying under
u
, and let
L_{u}
and
K_{v}
be the completions of
L
and
K
at
u
and
v
, respectively. Then the extension
L_{u}/K_{v}
of completions has the Lenstra-property, or equivalently
L_{u}/K_{v}
is an exceptional extension of local fields.

Proof.

Since
u
is
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-rational,
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is algebraically closed in
L_{u}
. Let
N
be the Galois closure of
L/K
, and let
\widehat{N}
be the Galois closure of
L_{u}/K_{v}
.
For any
\sigma\in\Gal(\widehat{N}/K_{v})\setminus\Gal(\widehat{N}/L_{u})
we have
L_{u}.\sigma(L_{u})=K_{v}.(L.\tau(L))
for some
\tau\in\Gal(N/K)\setminus\Gal(N/L)
, so that
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is not algebraically closed in
L_{u}.\sigma(L_{u})
.
Thus
L_{u}/K_{v}
has the Lenstra-property. It is easy to show that
L_{u}/K_{v}
has the Lenstra-property if and only if
L_{u}/K_{v}
is an exceptional extension of local fields. This concludes the proof.
∎

Theorem
3.3
follows from the combination of Lemmas
3.5
and
3.6
.

Remark 3.7
.

The converse of Lemma
3.5
is also true, which can be shown similarly. But it is not needed in this proof of Theorem
1.4
.

4.
Theory of exceptionality for local fields

Suppose
L/K
is a finite separable extension of local fields. Let
N
be the Galois closure of
L/K
. For any intermediate field
M
of
N/K
, let
M^{\prime}
be the maximal unramified extension over
M
inside
N
, and let
M\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\overline{\phantom{\rm M}}$}
be the residue field of
M
.
In other words,
\Gal(N/M^{\prime})
is the kernel of the canonical surjective homomorphism
\Gal(N/M)\to\Gal(N\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\overline{\phantom{\rm N}}$}/M\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\overline{\phantom{\rm M}}$})
. Equivalently,
M^{\prime}
is obtained from
M
by adjoining all roots of unity in
N
of order coprime to the characteristic of the residue field
N\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\overline{\phantom{\rm N}}$}
.

Definition 4.1
.

The groups
A\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\Gal(N/K)
and
G\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\Gal(N/K^{\prime})
are called the
arithmetic monodromy group
and the
geometric monodromy group
of the finite separable extension
L/K
of local fields, respectively.

Write
A_{1}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\Gal(N/L)
and
G_{1}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\Gal(N/L^{\prime})
, so that
A_{1}
and
G_{1}
are subgroups of
A
and
G
, respectively. Moreover,
G
is a normal subgroup of
A
and the quotient
A/G
is canonically isomorphic to
\Gal(N\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\overline{\phantom{\rm N}}$}/K\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\overline{\phantom{\rm K}}$})
. Similarly,
G_{1}
is a normal subgroup of
A_{1}
and the quotient
A_{1}/G_{1}
is canonically isomorphic to
\Gal(N\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\overline{\phantom{\rm N}}$}/L\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\overline{\phantom{\rm L}}$})
. In particular, both
A/G
and
A_{1}/G_{1}
are finite cyclic groups.

Let
\mathcal{S}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}A/A_{1}
be the set of all left cosets of
A_{1}
in
A
, so that
A
acts transitively on
\mathcal{S}
. There is a canonical bijection between
\mathcal{S}
and the set
\Hom_{K}(L,N)
of all
K
-algebra homomorphisms from
L
to
N
. Since
G\cap A_{1}=G_{1}
we have the embedding
A_{1}/G_{1}\to A/G
as groups and the embedding
G/G_{1}\to A/A_{1}
as sets of left cosets. Moreover, we have

Lemma 4.2
.

Suppose
L/K
is a finite separable extension of local fields. Then the following statements are equivalent:

(1)

L/K
is totally ramified;

(2)

[L:K]=[L^{\prime}:K^{\prime}]
;

(3)

[L^{\prime}:L]=[K^{\prime}:K]
;

(4)

the embedding
A_{1}/G_{1}\to A/G
is an isomorphism;

(5)

the embedding
G/G_{1}\to A/A_{1}
is a bijection;

(6)

G
acts transitively on
\mathcal{S}
.

Proof.

For any two intermediate fields
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
of
N/K
such that
M_{1}\subseteq M_{2}
, let
e(M_{2}/M_{1})
and
f(M_{2}/M_{1})
be the ramification index and the residue index of the extension
M_{2}/M_{1}
of local fields, respectively. Since
e(L^{\prime}/L)=1=e(K^{\prime}/K)
we have
e(L/K)=e(L^{\prime}/K^{\prime})
.
Since
f(L^{\prime}/K^{\prime})=1
it follows that
L/K
is totally ramified if and only if
[L:K]=[L^{\prime}:K^{\prime}]
, or equivalently
[L^{\prime}:L]=[K^{\prime}:K]
. Moreover, by Galois theory we have
[K^{\prime}:K]=[A:G]
, and
[L^{\prime}:L]=[A_{1}:G_{1}]
,
[L:K]=[A:A_{1}]
, and
[L^{\prime}:K^{\prime}]=[G:G_{1}]
. Thus
[L^{\prime}:L]=[K^{\prime}:K]
says that the embedding
A_{1}/G_{1}\to A/G
is an isomorphism as groups.
Similarly,
[L:K]=[L^{\prime}:K^{\prime}]
says that the embedding
G/G_{1}\to A/A_{1}
is a bijection as sets of left cosets. Finally, since
G/G_{1}
is one
G
-orbit in
A/A_{1}
it follows that the embedding
G/G_{1}\to A/A_{1}
is a bijection if and only if
G
acts also transitively on
\mathcal{S}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}A/A_{1}
.
∎

Henceforth we assume in addition that
L/K
is totally ramified, so that we have the triple
(A,G,\mathcal{S})
in which
G
is a normal subgroup of a finite group
A
and
\mathcal{S}
is a finite set with an
A
-action such that the quotient
A/G
is cyclic and
G
acts transitively on
\mathcal{S}
. Thus we can use the following two lemmas, see
[
19
, Lemmas 4.2 and 4.3]
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
H_{1}
be a finite group acting on a finite set
\mathcal{T}
, let
H_{2}
be a normal subgroup of
H_{1}
with
H_{1}/H_{2}
cyclic, and let
\sigma H_{2}
be a generator of
H_{1}/H_{2}
. Then the number of
H_{1}
-orbits on
\mathcal{T}
which are also
H_{2}
-orbits equals

\frac{1}{\lvert H_{2}\rvert}\sum_{h\in\sigma H_{2}}\lvert\mathcal{T}^{h}\rvert,

where
\mathcal{T}^{h}
denotes the set of fixed points of
h
in
\mathcal{T}
.

Lemma 4.4
.

Suppose
H_{2}
is a normal subgroup of a finite group
H_{1}
and
\mathcal{T}
is a finite set with an
H_{1}
-action such that the quotient
H_{1}/H_{2}
is cyclic and
H_{2}
acts transitively on
\mathcal{T}
. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

the diagonal is the unique common orbit of
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
in
\mathcal{T}\times\mathcal{T}
;

(2)

any
\sigma\in H_{1}
with
H_{1}=\langle H_{2},\sigma\rangle
has a unique fixed point in
\mathcal{T}
;

(3)

any
\sigma\in H_{1}
with
H_{1}=\langle H_{2},\sigma\rangle
has at most one fixed point in
\mathcal{T}
;

(4)

any
\sigma\in H_{1}
with
H_{1}=\langle H_{2},\sigma\rangle
has at least one fixed point in
\mathcal{T}
.

Let
\rho\colon\Gal(N/K)\to\Gal(N\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\overline{\phantom{\rm N}}$}/K\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\overline{\phantom{\rm K}}$})
be the canonical surjective group homomorphism obtained by passing to the residue fields. Thus we have
G=\ker(\rho)
and
G_{1}=\ker(\rho)\cap A_{1}
. Suppose
K\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\overline{\phantom{\rm K}}$}=\mathbb{F}_{q}
for some power of a prime
p
, and let
\theta\in\Gal(N\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\overline{\phantom{\rm N}}$}/K\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\overline{\phantom{\rm K}}$})
be the canonical generator which acts on
N\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\overline{\phantom{\rm N}}$}
as the
q
-the power map.
For any intermediate field
M
of
L/K
, write
\Frob(N/M)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\rho^{-1}(\theta)\cap\Gal(N/M)
, which consists of all
\rho
-preimages of
\theta
in
\Gal(N/M)
. So
\Frob(N/K)
is a canonical generator of the quotient group
A/G
. Similarly, since
L/K
is totally ramified,
\Frob(N/L)
is a canonical generator of the quotient group
A_{1}/G_{1}
.

With the notations induced above, we are ready to characterize the exceptionality for local fields in various ways as follows.

Theorem 4.5
.

Assume
L/K
is a finite separable extension of local fields. If
L/K
is totally ramified then the following are equivalent:

(1)

L/K
is exceptional;

(2)

\{A_{1}\}
is the unique common orbit of
A_{1}
and
G_{1}
in
\mathcal{S}
;

(3)

the diagonal is the unique common orbit of
A
and
G
in
\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{S}
;

(4)

\Frob(N/L)\cap\Frob(N/\sigma(L))=\emptyset
for any
\sigma\in A\setminus A_{1}
;

(5)

\Frob(N/K)=\bigcup_{\sigma\in A}\Frob(N/\sigma(L))
holds;

(6)

any
\sigma\in A
with
A=\langle G,\sigma\rangle
has a unique fixed point in
\mathcal{S}
;

(7)

any
\sigma\in A
with
A=\langle G,\sigma\rangle
has at most one fixed point in
\mathcal{S}
;

(8)

any
\sigma\in A
with
A=\langle G,\sigma\rangle
has at least one fixed point in
\mathcal{S}
;

(9)

any
\sigma\in\Frob(N/K)
has a unique fixed point in
\mathcal{S}
;

(10)

any
\sigma\in\Frob(N/K)
has at most one fixed point in
\mathcal{S}
;

(11)

any
\sigma\in\Frob(N/K)
has at least one fixed point in
\mathcal{S}
;

(12)

any
\sigma\in A_{1}
with
A_{1}=\langle G_{1},\sigma\rangle
has a unique fixed point in
\mathcal{S}
;

(13)

any
\sigma\in A_{1}
with
A_{1}=\langle G_{1},\sigma\rangle
has at most one fixed point in
\mathcal{S}
;

(14)

any
\sigma\in\Frob(N/L)
has a unique fixed point in
\mathcal{S}
;

(15)

any
\sigma\in\Frob(N/L)
has at most one fixed point in
\mathcal{S}
.

Proof.

Since
L/K
is totally ramified, it follows from Lemma
4.2
that
G
acts transitively on
\mathcal{S}
, so that by Lemma
4.4
items (3) and (6)–(8) are equivalent. Since
\Frob(N/K)
is a generator of
A/G
, by applying Lemma
4.3
to
(A,G,\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{S})
it follows that items (3) and (9)–(11) are equivalent.
Similarly, by applying Lemma
4.3
to
(A_{1},G_{1},\mathcal{S})
it follows that items (2) and (12)–(15) are equivalent. It is standard to verify the equivalence between items (2) and (3). Hence items (2)–(3) and (6)–(12) are all equivalent to one another.

Next, we show the equivalence between items (1) and (2). Henceforth we suppose
\sigma\in A\setminus A_{1}
. Let
M\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}L.\sigma(L)
be the compositume of
L
and
\sigma(L)
. Since
e(M^{\prime}/M)=1=e(K^{\prime}/K)
, we have
e(M/K)=e(M^{\prime}/K^{\prime})
. Since
f(M^{\prime}/K^{\prime})=1
, it follows that
M/K
is totally ramified if and only if
[M:K]=[M^{\prime}:K^{\prime}]
.
Note
[M:K]
and
[M^{\prime}:K^{\prime}]
are the lengths of the orbits containing
\sigma A_{1}
under the actions
A_{1}
and
G_{1}
, respectively. Hence
[M:K]=[M^{\prime}:K^{\prime}]
holds if and only if the
A_{1}
-orbit of
\sigma A_{1}
is the same as the
G_{1}
-orbit of
\sigma A_{1}
. Thus items (1) and (2) are equivalent.

Next, let us show the equivalence between items (1) and (4). Pick
\sigma\in A\setminus A_{1}
and let
M\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}L.\sigma(L)
be the compositum of
L
and
\sigma(L)
. So
M
is not totally ramified over
K
if and only if
M\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\overline{\phantom{\rm M}}$}
is larger than
\mathbb{F}_{q}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}K\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\overline{\phantom{\rm K}}$}
.
Since
\rho\colon\Gal(N/M)\to\Gal(N\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\overline{\phantom{\rm N}}$}/M\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\overline{\phantom{\rm M}}$})
is surjective, it follows that
\mathbb{F}_{q}\subsetneq M\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\overline{\phantom{\rm M}}$}
if and only if
\Frob(N/M)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\rho^{-1}(\theta)\cap\Gal(N/M)
is empty, or equivalently
\Frob(N/L)\cap\Frob(N/\sigma(L))=\emptyset
.
Hence
L.\sigma(L)
is not totally ramified over
K
if and only if
\Frob(N/L)\cap\Frob(N/\sigma(L))=\emptyset
for any
\sigma\in A\setminus A_{1}
, which shows the equivalence of (1) and (4).

It remains to show only that (4) and (5) are equivalent. First, we claim item (4) holds if and only if
\Frob(N/\tau(L))\cap\Frob(N/\sigma(L))=\emptyset
for any
\tau,\sigma\in A
with
\tau A_{1}\neq\sigma A_{1}
.
It is enough to show the “only if” part of this claim, since the “if” part follows easily by taking
\tau
as the identity map on
N
. Henceforth we suppose item (4) holds. Pick any
\tau,\sigma\in A
with
\tau A_{1}\neq\sigma A_{1}
, so that
\tau^{-1}\sigma\in A\setminus A_{1}
.
It follows from item (4) that
\Frob(N/L)\cap\Frob(N/(\tau^{-1}\sigma)(L))=\emptyset
, or equivalently
\rho^{-1}(\theta)\cap\Gal(N/L.(\tau^{-1}\sigma)(L))=\emptyset
, which by conjugation with
\tau
says
\rho^{-1}(\theta)\cap\Gal(N/\tau(L).\sigma(L))=\emptyset
, which is equivalent to the desired
\Frob(N/\tau(L))\cap\Frob(N/\sigma(L))=\emptyset
.
Next, we claim item (5) holds if and only if the above same property holds, which says
\Frob(N/\tau(L))\cap\Frob(N/\sigma(L))=\emptyset
for any
\tau,\sigma\in A
with
\tau A_{1}\neq\sigma A_{1}
. Since the homomorphism
\rho\colon\Gal(N/K)\to\Gal(N\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\overline{\phantom{\rm N}}$}/K\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\overline{\phantom{\rm K}}$})
is surjective we know
\lvert\Gal(N/K)\rvert=\lvert\Frob(N/K)\rvert\cdot\lvert\Gal(N\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\overline{\phantom{\rm N}}$}/K\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\overline{\phantom{\rm K}}$})\rvert
.
Similarly, since
L/K
is totally ramified
\lvert\Gal(N/\sigma(L))\rvert=\lvert\Frob(N/\sigma(L))\rvert\cdot\lvert\Gal(N\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\overline{\phantom{\rm N}}$}/K\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\overline{\phantom{\rm K}}$})\rvert
for any
\sigma\in A
. Hence for any
\sigma\in A
we have

\frac{\lvert\Frob(N/K)\rvert}{\lvert\Frob(N/\sigma(L))\rvert}=\frac{\lvert\Gal(N/K)\rvert}{\lvert\Gal(N/\sigma(L))\rvert}=[\sigma(L):K]=[L:K].

Hence our second claim follows by counting the sizes. Thus items (4) and (5) are equivalent, which concludes the proof.
∎

Roughly speaking, our next result says that the exceptionality for local fields is preserved well under the composition in a perfect way.

Theorem 4.6

Suppose

of

is exceptional if and only if both

and

Proof.

Suppose

. It is obvious that

If the restriction

.

, where

Let

We can lift

Now we are ready to show Theorem
1.6
, which characterizes exceptional local field extensions of degree coprime to the characteristic of the residue fields.

Proof of Theorem
1.6
.

Note that roots of unity in a local field of order coprime to the characteristic of the residue field are the image of the Teichmüller character.
Since
\gcd(n,q)=1
it follows that
K
has some nontrivial
n
-th root of unity if and only if
\mathbb{F}_{q}
has some nontrivial
n
-th root of unity, or equivalently
\gcd(n,q-1)\neq 1
. Thus item (2) and (3) are equivalent. It remains to show items (1), (3), (4) are equivalent.

Let
\mathcal{O}_{K}
be the ring of integers in
K
and
\mathfrak{m}_{K}
be its maximal ideal, so that
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is the residue field
K\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\overline{\phantom{\rm K}}$}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\mathcal{O}_{K}/\mathfrak{m}_{K}
of

. Let
x
and

be uniformizers of the local fields
L
and
K
, respectively.
Since

is totally ramified we have
z=x^{n}\cdot\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}a_{i}x^{i}
for some
a_{i}\in\mathcal{O}_{K}
with
a_{0}\notin\mathfrak{m}_{K}
. We may assume in addition that
a_{0}=1
by replacing
z
with
a_{0}z
if necessary. Since
\gcd(n,q)=1
we have
\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}a_{i}x^{i}=\Bigl(\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}b_{i}x^{i}\Bigr)^{n}
for some
b_{i}\in\mathcal{O}_{K}
with

.
Write
y\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}x\cdot\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}b_{i}x^{i}
, so that

and

is an uniformizer of
M
with
L\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}K(y)
. Since

is a generator of
L
over

, it follows that
X^{n}-z\in K[X]
the minimal polynomial of
y
over

. Let

be the Galois closure of

, so that

X^{n}-z=X^{n}-y^{n}=\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}(X-\zeta^{i}y)

where
\zeta
is a primitive

.

Now we are ready to show that items (1) and (3) are equivalent. For any
\sigma\in\Gal(N/K)

if and only if
\sigma(L)=K(\zeta^{i}y)
for some integer

. Suppose
\sigma\in\Gal(N/K)\setminus\Gal(N/L)
, so that
\sigma(L)=K(\zeta^{i}y)

Since

It remains only to show the equivalence between items (1) and (4). First we suppose

is not exceptional, so that

contains some nontrivial

.
It follows that
M\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}K(y^{n/m})

. Next we suppose there exists some intermediate field

∎

5.
Third proof of the Carlitz–Wan conjecture

In this section we give the third proof of Theorem
1.4
, which is the generalization of the Carlitz–Wan conjecture to curves. This proof uses more group theory in terms of monodromy groups, decomposition groups, and inertia groups.
It requires more knowledge of exceptional local field extensions, which has been established in Section 4. The advantage of this proof is that it is more insightful in the sense that it sheds more lights on the truth of the Carlitz–Wan conjecture.

Let us restate as follows the notion of exceptional covers introduced in Definition
1.3
in the language of function fields. Since the part of pure inseparability will not make any essential difference for our purposes, we may assume the finite morphism
f\colon X\to Y
of curves over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is separable, so that the extension
\mathbb{F}_{q}(X)/\mathbb{F}_{q}(Y)
of function fields over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is finite separable. Let
\Omega
be the Galois closure of
\mathbb{F}_{q}(X)/\mathbb{F}_{q}(Y)
, and let
\mathbb{F}_{q^{\ell}}
be the algebraically closure of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
in
\Omega
.

Definition 5.1
.

The Galois groups
A\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\Gal(\Omega/\mathbb{F}_{q}(Y))
and
G\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\Gal(\Omega/\mathbb{F}_{q^{\ell}}(Y))
are called the
arithmetic monodromy group
and the
geometric monodromy group
of
\mathbb{F}_{q}(X)/\mathbb{F}_{q}(Y)
, respectively.

Thus
G
is a normal subgroup of
A
with quotient
A/G
canonically isomorphic to
\Gal(\mathbb{F}_{q^{\ell}}/\mathbb{F}_{q})
, so that
A/G
is finite cyclic of order
\ell
. Write
A_{1}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\Gal(\Omega/\mathbb{F}_{q}(X))
and
G_{1}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\Gal(\Omega/\mathbb{F}_{q^{\ell}}(X))
.
Let
\mathcal{S}
be the set of all left cosets of
A_{1}
in
A
, which may be viewed canonically as the set of all
\mathbb{F}_{q}(Y)
-algebra homomorphisms of
\mathbb{F}_{q}(X)
into
\Omega
. Thus
A
acts naturally on
\mathcal{S}
and
G
acts transitively on
\mathcal{S}
.

The following result is a standard fact on exceptional covers, which follows from the combination of
[
19
, Lemmas 4.1 and 4.3]
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Suppose
f\colon X\to Y
is a finite separable morphism of curves over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Then the following properties are equivalent:

(1)

f\colon X\to Y
is an exceptional cover;

(2)

\{A_{1}\}
is the unique common orbit of
A_{1}
and
G_{1}
in
\mathcal{S}
;

(3)

the diagonal is the unique common orbit of
A
and
G
in
\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{S}
;

(4)

any
\sigma\in A
with
A=\langle G,\sigma\rangle
has a unique fixed point in
\mathcal{S}
;

(5)

any
\sigma\in A
with
A=\langle G,\sigma\rangle
has at most a fixed point in
\mathcal{S}
;

(6)

any
\sigma\in A
with
A=\langle G,\sigma\rangle
has at least a fixed point in
\mathcal{S}
.

Next, we give our third proof of Theorem
3.3
with a group-theoretic argument, which passes some property about the pair
(A,G)
of the arithmetic monodromy group and the geometric monodromy group to the same property about the pair
(D,I)
of the decomposition group and the inertia group over some prescribe rational place.

The third proof of Theorem
3.3
.

Let
L\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\mathbb{F}_{q}(X)
and
K\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\mathbb{F}_{q}(Y)
be the function fields of
X
and
Y
, respectively. Thus
L/K
is a finite separable extension of function fields over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Let
N
be the Galois closure of
L/K
, and let
A
and
G
be the arithmetic and geometric monodromy groups of
L/K
, respectively.
By item (4) of Lemma
5.2
the exceptionality of
f\colon X\to Y
says that any
\sigma\in A
with

has a unique fixed point in
\mathcal{S}
, where
\mathcal{S}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\Hom_{K}(L,N)
is the set of all

-algebra homomorphisms of

into

Pick a place

of

-rational place

of

. Let

and

be the decomposition group and the inertia group of

at

Since

is surjective, so that any

By the above characterization of the exceptionality of

in terms of

, which says that any
\sigma\in D

.

Let

, and

be the completions of the function fields

, and

with respect to the places

, and

, respectively. Thus

is an intermediate field of the finite Galois extension

. We need to show the finite separable extension

of local fields is exceptional. Let

be the Galois closure in

of

By item (10) of Theorem

the extension

is exceptional if and only if any

which induces the

-th power map on the residue field of

has at most a fixed point in the set

of all

-algebra homomorphisms of

into
\widehat{N}
.
It is routine to check that any such
\sigma\in\Gal(\widehat{N}/K_{v})
can be lifted to some element

, so that

has a unique fixed point in

.
Since

can be viewed canonically as a subset of

, it follows that

∎

Let us conclude our third proof of Theorem
1.4
with another proof of Theorem
2.1
, which is more insightful but requires more knowledge of exceptional local field extensions developed in Section 4.

The second proof of Theorem
2.1
.

Write

where

∎

Remarks on Lipschitz geometry on globally conic singular manifolds
André Costa, Vincent Grandjean, Maria Michalska

Abstract
. We study metric properties of manifolds with conic singularities and present a natural interplay between metrically conic and metrically asymptotically conic behaviour. As a consequence, we prove that a singular sub-manifold is Lipschitz normally embedded, i.e. its inner and outer metric structures are equivalent, in an ambient singular manifold, whenever the singularities are conic and the ends of the manifold are asymptotically conic, which answers positively a question of [ 7 ] .



Introduction

The aim of this note is to explain within the context of conic
singular manifolds
how the results on Lipschitz geometry of algebraic sets of
our recent works
[
9
,
7
]
, compare
[
8
]
,
are occurrences of a general simple principle.

A diffeomorphism between two smooth Riemannian manifolds is always a locally
bi-Lipschitz mapping. Yet in
presence of singularities a counterpart statement is not clear. Thus we investigate this question here for metrically
conic singularities. We will showcase the relations between conic singular
manifolds, manifolds with conic metric and their quotient spaces in Section
2
.

The natural interplay between metrically conic and asymptotically metrically
conic behaviour is presented in Corollary
2.12

and Proposition
2.16
. Thus
Lipschitz geometry of globally conic singular manifolds becomes much clearer and results conjectured in
[
7
]

follow in Theorem
3.9
which states that

every connected globally conic singular sub-manifold of a globally conic
singular manifold is Lipschitz normally embedded, i.e. the inner and outer metric structures are equivalent.

1.
Preliminaries

Throughout the paper smooth means
C^{\infty}
-smooth, all manifolds without or with
boundary are smooth, all Riemannian metrics are assumed smooth.

1.1.
Metric structures of subsets

For convenience we allow a distance function to attain value
\infty
, for instance for points in different
connected components of a set. Thus isometries of metric spaces are connected-component-wise isometries.

A pair
(M,d)
is a pseudo-metric space, if the function

d:M\times M\to{\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}\cup\{\infty\}
is a pseudo-distance, i.e. for all
{\bf x},{\bf x}^{\prime},{\bf x}^{\prime\prime}\in M
it satisfies the following conditions:

d({\bf x},{\bf x})=0,\;\;d({\bf x},{\bf x}^{\prime})=d({\bf x}^{\prime},{\bf x})\;\;{\rm and}\;\;d({\bf x},{\bf x}^{\prime\prime})\leq d({\bf x},{\bf x}^{\prime})+d({\bf x}^{\prime},{\bf x}^{\prime\prime}).

Let
(M,d)
be a pseudo-metric space. The length
of an arc
{\bf c}:[a,b]\to M
is

\ell({\bf c})=\sup\left|\sum_{i=1}^{k}d({\bf c}(t_{i-1}),{\bf c}(t_{i}))\right|,

where the supremum is taken over all partitions
a=t_{0}<\dots<t_{k}=b,k\in\mathbb{N},
of the interval
I
.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
(M,d)
be a pseudo-metric space and
X
be a subset of
M
.

(i) The outer pseudo-metric structure is
(X,d_{X})
, where
d_{X}
is
the restriction of the pseudo-distance in
M
to
X
.

(ii) The inner pseudo-metric structure is
(X,d_{{\rm in}}^{X})
, where
d_{{\rm in}}^{X}

is the infimum of lengths of arcs within
X

connecting a given pair of points.

Whenever the inner (rep. outer) pseudo-distance is a distance on
X
, the inner (resp. outer) structure is naturally an inner (resp. outer) metric structure on
X
.

A mapping
f:(M,d)\to(M^{\prime},d^{\prime})
between pseudo-metric spaces is pseudo-Lipschitz if there exists a positive constant
L
such that the inequality
d^{\prime}(f(x),f(y))\leq Ld(x,y)
is satisfied for all
x,y\in M
.

A special type of subsets of a (pseudo-)metric space are those for which their
outer and inner (pseudo-)metric structure are indistinguishable from the
(pseudo-)Lipschitz point of view:

Definition 1.2
.

Let
(M,d)
be a pseudo-metric space and
X
be a subset of
M
such that its inner
and outer pseudo-distances in
X
are both distance functions.

(i) The subset
X
is Lipschitz normally embedded (shortened to LNE) in
M
if its outer and inner distances are equivalent,
i.e. the identity map

\textup{id}:(X,d_{X})\to(X,d_{{\rm in}}^{X})

is bi-Lipschitz.

(ii) The subset
X
is locally LNE at
{\bf x}
if there
exists a neighbourhood
U
of
{\bf x}
in
M
such that
X\cap U
is LNE in
M
.

(iii) The subset
X
is locally LNE if it is locally
LNE at each point of its closure in
M
.

(iv) The space
(X,d_{X})
is a length space when
the identity map of point (i) is an isometry.

When writing that a subset of a metric space is LNE, it is always understood that
it is LNE with respect to the metric structure of the given ambient space. By
convention, the empty set is LNE. This notion is the same as Whitney’s
P
condition of

[
22
]
and Gromov’s quasi-convexity of
[
14
]
. We use the least ambivalent name of
Lipschitz normally embedded after the paper
[
1
]
.

1.2.
Singular metrics and pseudo-distances on manifolds

Let
(M,{\partial}M)
be a smooth manifold with boundary (possibly empty). A singular Riemannian
metric over a manifold
M
is a smooth
2
-symmetric tensor over
M
everywhere
positive semi-definite. The singular locus
{\rm sing}(g)
of the singular
Riemannian metric
g
over
M
is the set of points of
M
at which
g
is not
positive-definite. It is a closed subset of
M
and
g_{|M\setminus{\rm sing}(g)}
is a Riemannian metric.

Two singular Riemannian metrics
g
and
h
over
(M,{\partial}M)
are equivalent
if there exist constants
a,b>0
such that

a\,g({\bf x})(\xi,\xi)\,\leq\,h({\bf x})(\xi,\xi)\,\leq b\,g({\bf x})(\xi,\xi),\;\;\forall({\bf x},\xi)\in TM.

Given a singular Riemannian metric
g
over a manifold
M
, the length of a smooth curve
{\bf c}:[a,b]\to M
is

\ell_{g}({\bf c})=\int_{a}^{b}\big(g({\bf c}^{\prime}(t),{\bf c}^{\prime}(t))\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,{\rm d}t.

Then
M
is equipped with the pseudo-distance
d_{g}
obtained by taking the infimum of the
length of piecewise smooth curves connecting a given pair of points, this pseudo-distance
is a distance function on
M\setminus{\rm sing}(g)
.

Remark 1.3
.

By for instance
[
2
,
21
]
, given a length structure
d
on the manifold
M
, smooth
outside of the diagonal
M\times M
, there exists a Riemannian
metric
g
on
M
such that
d=d_{g}
. By Myers-Steenrod theorem a mapping between Riemannian
manifolds is a smooth Riemannian isometry if and only if it is an
isometry of the underlying metric spaces.

In particular, a diffeomorphism between smooth manifolds is locally bi-Lipschitz regardless
of the choice of Riemannian metrics. Thus the
Lipschitz structure on a manifold depends only on its smooth structure and is independent on the Riemannian structure (this is
already true for
\mathcal{C}^{1}
regularity).

1.3.
Collar neighbourhoods and p-sub-manifolds

Let
(M,{\partial}M)
be a smooth manifold with boundary
{\partial}M
(possibly empty).

A boundary defining function in
M
is a
smooth function over
M
, positive in
M\setminus{\partial}M
, vanishing on
{\partial}M

and with no critical point in
{\partial}M
. Such smooth functions always exist.
When
{\partial}M
is compact, given any boundary defining function
x
, there
exists
\eta>0
such that
[0,\eta]
contains no critical value of
x
.

An embedded smooth sub-manifold of
(M,{\partial}M)
is a subset
N
such that
(i) it is a smooth manifold
(N,{\partial}N)
with boundary
{\partial}N
(possibly
empty); (ii) its manifold topology coincides with the induced topology from

M
; (iii) the inclusion mapping
N\hookrightarrow M
is a smooth injective
immersion.

Definition 1.4
.

(
[
17
, Section I.7]
)
Let
(M,{\partial}M)
be a smooth manifold with boundary. A subset
N
of
M
is a
p-sub-manifold if: (i) it is a smooth sub-manifold with boundary
{\partial}N
of
M
; (ii)
{\partial}N
is contained in
{\partial}M
; (iii)
N
is transverse to

{\partial}M
:

{\bf x}\in{\partial}N\;\Longrightarrow\;T_{\bf x}N+T_{\bf x}{\partial}M=T_{\bf x}M.

The smooth cylinder over a manifold
N
of height
\eta
is

{\rm Cyl}(N,\eta):=N\times[0,\eta)

and the open cylinder over
N
is

{\rm Cyl}(N,\eta)^{o}:={\rm Cyl}(N,\eta)\setminus(N\times\{0\})=N\times(0,\eta)

We recall the collar neighbourhood result below, proof can be found for instance in
[
7
]
.

Proposition-Definition 1.5
.

Let
(M,{\partial}M)
be a manifold with smooth compact boundary.
Let
x
be a boundary defining function. There exist
\eta>0
such
that the function
x
has no critical value in
[0,\eta]
, an open
neighbourhood

U_{x}^{\eta}
of
{\partial}M
in
M
and a smooth diffeomorphism

(1.1)

\phi_{x}^{\eta}:U_{x}^{\eta}:=M\cap\{x<\eta\}\to{\rm Cyl}({\partial}M,\eta),\;\;{\bf z}\mapsto({\bf y}({\bf z}),x({\bf z})),

which we call the collar neighbourhood diffeomorphism.

1.4.
Spherical blowing-up

Let
(M,g)
be a Riemannian manifold possibly with non-empty boundary
{\partial}M
.
Let
{\bf a}
be a point of
M\setminus{\partial}M
. There exists a positive radius
\varepsilon

such that the exponential map

\exp_{\bf a}:B({\bf a},\varepsilon)\to B_{g}({\bf a},\varepsilon)

centred at
{\bf a}
maps diffeomorphically the open
\varepsilon
-ball
B({\bf a},\varepsilon)
of

T_{\bf a}M
onto the open
\varepsilon
-geodesic ball
B_{g}({\bf a},\varepsilon)
of
M
. Let
{\bf S}_{\bf a}
be the unit-sphere of
T_{\bf a}M
. The following
mapping

\rho_{\bf a}:{\bf S}_{\bf a}\times[0,1)\to B({\bf a},\varepsilon),\;\;({\bf u},t)\to\varepsilon\,t\,{\bf u}.

is smooth.

The blowing-up of the Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
centred at
{\bf a}
is
the smooth surjective mapping

(1.2)

{\bf bl}_{\bf a}^{M}:[M,{\bf a}]\to M

where
[M,{\bf a}]
is a smooth manifold with boundary
{\bf S}_{\bf a}\times 0\sqcup{\partial}M
. It is obtained as the gluing of
{\bf S}_{\bf a}\times[0,1)
with
M\setminus{\bf a}

identifying
{\bf S}_{\bf a}\times(0,1)
with the pointed open ball

{\bf B}_{g}({\bf a},\varepsilon)\setminus{\bf a}
via
\exp_{\bf a}\circ\rho_{\bf a}
.
The mapping
{\bf bl}_{\bf a}
is just the quotient mapping of the gluing.

Therefore, the spherical blow up
{\bf bl}_{\bf a}
maps
{\bf S}_{\bf a}\times 0
onto
{\bf a}

and induces a diffeomorphism
[M,{\bf a}]\setminus{\bf S}_{\bf a}\times 0\to M\setminus{\bf a}
.
The pull-backed metric
({\bf bl}_{\bf a}^{M})^{*}g
is a singular Riemannian metric over

[M,{\bf a}]
with singular locus
{\bf S}_{\bf a}\times 0
.
In particular, given two Riemannian metrics
g
and
h
over
M
, the spherically blown-up manifolds

[(M,g),{\bf a}]
and
[(M,h),{\bf a}]
are diffeomorphic as manifolds with boundary.

1.5.
Conic metrics

Let
(M,{\partial}M)
be a smooth manifold with compact boundary.
We present below the classical material of
[
3
,
4
]
with some additional notions necessary for this paper.

Definition 1.6
.

A conic metric
g^{c}
on the smooth manifold
(M,{\partial}M)
is a singular
Riemannian metric over
M
with
{\rm sing}(g^{c})={\partial}M
, for which there exists a
smooth collar neighbourhood diffeomorphism

\varphi=\phi_{r}^{\eta}:M\cap\{r<\eta\}\to{\rm Cyl}({\partial}M,\eta)

such that

(1.3)

\varphi_{*}g^{c}={\rm d}r^{2}+r^{2}G(r,{\bf y};{\rm d}r,{\rm d}{\bf y})

and
{\bf y}\to G(0,{\bf y})|_{0\times{\partial}M}
is a Riemannian metric over
{\partial}M
. When
g^{c}
is a conic metric, the triple
(M,{\partial}M,g^{c})
is said to be a manifold with a conic metric.

Note that the singular locus of conic metrics lies on the boundary of the manifold
and if
(S,{\partial}S)
is a p-sub-manifold of the manifold with conic metric
(M,{\partial}M,g^{c})
, then
(S,{\partial}S,g^{c}|_{S})
is a manifold with a conic metric.

Definition 1.7
.

The pseudo-distance
d^{c}:=d_{g^{c}}
over
M
induced by the conic metric
g^{c}

is the conic distance. For a sub-manifold
S
of
M
, the
pseudo-distance obtained by restricting to
S
the conic distance
d^{c}
is
its outer conic distance whereas the pseudo-distance obtained from the conic
metric tensor
g^{c}
restricted to
S
is its inner pseudo-distance.

The following special cases of conic metrics will be of great use:

Definition 1.8
.

(1) Let
N
be a compact manifold without boundary and let
\eta>0
. A model
conic metric on the cylinder
{\rm Cyl}(N,\eta)
is a singular Riemannian metric

g_{\rm mod}^{c}
over
{\rm Cyl}(N,\eta)
of the following form

(1.4)

g_{\rm mod}^{c}={\rm d}r^{2}+r^{2}g^{\partial}(r,{\bf y};{\rm d}{\bf y})

where
r\to g^{\partial}(r)
is a smooth family of Riemannian metrics over
N
.

(2) A simple conic metric over the manifold
(M,{\partial}M)
with compact
boundary is any conic metric
g^{c}
for which there exists a collar neighbourhood
diffeomorphism
\varphi:U_{r}^{\eta}\to{\rm Cyl}({\partial}M,\eta)
and a Riemannian metric

g_{{\partial}M}
over
{\partial}M
such that

\varphi_{*}g^{c}={\rm d}r^{2}+r^{2}\,g_{{\partial}M}.

Any model conic metric on a cylinder
{\rm Cyl}(N,\eta)
over

N={\partial}M
extends as a conic metric onto
(M,{\partial}M)
and there is no other type
of conic metrics. More precisely:

Theorem 1.9

(Theorem 1.2,
[
18
]
)
.

Let
(M,{\partial}M,g^{c})
be a manifold with conic metric. There exists a collar
neighbourhood diffeomorphism
\varphi:U\to{\rm Cyl}({\partial}M,\eta)
such that
\varphi_{*}g^{c}
is
a model conic metric over
{\rm Cyl}({\partial}M,\eta)
.

Example 1.10
.

1) Let
{\bf bl}_{\bf 0}:[{\mathbb{R}^{n}},{\bf 0}]={\bf S}^{n-1}\times{\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}\to{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
be the
spherical blowing-up of
{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
. The pull-back
{\bf bl}_{\bf 0}^{*}(eucl)
of the
Euclidean metric
eucl
over the blown-up space
[{\mathbb{R}^{n}},{\bf 0}]
is the
simplest example of a model conic metric

{\bf bl}_{\bf 0}^{*}(eucl)={\rm d}r^{2}+r^{2}{\rm d}{\bf u}^{2}.

2) Warped metrics over

{\rm Cyl}(N,\eta)
are model conic metrics, these are singular metrics of the form

{\rm d}r^{2}+(rf(r))^{2}g_{N}

where
f
is a smooth positive function over
[0,\eta)
and

g_{N}
is a Riemannian metric over
N
, see also
[
13
]
.

An essential by-product of the demonstration of
Theorem
1.9
is the following

Corollary 1.11
.

If
d^{c}
is a conic distance, then the function
d^{c}(-,{\partial}M)
is a boundary defining function. In particular there exists a positive
r_{0}
such that each level
\{d^{c}(-,{\partial}M)=r\}
is diffeomorphic to
{\partial}M
for any
0\leq r\leq r_{0}
.

Moreover, for any two conic metrics
g
and
h
over
(M,{\partial}M)
there exists a
collar neighbourhood
U
of
{\partial}M
such that
g
and
h
restricted to
U
are
equivalent and thus the identity mapping
\textup{id}:(U,d_{g})\to(U,d_{h})
is
pseudo-bi-Lipschitz with respect to the conic distances
d_{g}
and
d_{h}
, see
Section
1.2
. In particular, we get

Corollary 1.12
.

Any two conic metrics over a compact manifold are equivalent.

Proposition-Definition 1.13
.

Any conic metric
g^{c}
on the manifold
M
has
(1) an associated Riemannian metric on the boundary , i.e. a Riemannian
metric
g^{\partial}:=G_{|{\partial}M}
on
{\partial}M
of representation (
1.3
), and
(2) an associated simple conic metric near the boundary
{\partial}M
, i.e. a
simple conic metric
h
on a neighbourhood
U
of
{\partial}M
such that the singular metrics
g^{c}
and
h
are equivalent over
U
and have the same associated Riemannian metric on the boundary.

Proof.

Let
g^{c}
be a conic metric over
(M,{\partial}M)
. By definition
g^{\partial}:=G_{|{\partial}M}
is Riemannian over
{\partial}M
. Let
\varphi:U_{r}^{\eta}\to{\rm Cyl}({\partial}M,\eta)
be a collar neighbourhood diffeomorphism of Theorem
1.9
such that

\varphi_{*}g^{c}
is a model conic metric

\varphi_{*}g^{c}={\rm d}r^{2}+r^{2}g^{\partial}(r).

Thus
g^{\partial}(0)
is a Riemannian metric over
{\partial}M
, and the model conic metric

g_{0}^{c}={\rm d}r^{2}+r^{2}g^{\partial}(0)

is equivalent to
g^{c}
over a smaller cylinder
{\rm Cyl}({\partial}M,\eta^{\prime})
for
some positive height
\eta^{\prime}<\eta
. Since
\varphi^{*}g_{0}^{c}
extends as a
conic metric
g^{s}
over
(M,{\partial}M)
, the proof ends by Corollary
1.12
.
∎

The following, though elementary, is a useful variation of the law
of cosines in the conic context:

Proposition 1.14
.

Let
N
be a compact smooth manifold without boundary. Let

g^{c}={\rm d}r^{2}+r^{2}g^{\partial}(r)

be a model conic metric on
{\rm Cyl}(N,\eta)
. Denote by
d^{c}
the conic distance on
{\rm Cyl}(N,\eta)
and by
d_{N}
the distance on
N
induced by
g^{\partial}(0)
. Then there exist positive constants
A^{c},B^{c}
, depending only on
g^{c}
and
\eta
,
such that

(1.5)

A^{c}(|r-r^{\prime}|+\min(r,r^{\prime})\,d_{N}({\bf y},{\bf y}^{\prime}))\;\leq\;d^{c}({\bf x},{\bf x}^{\prime})\;\leq\;B^{c}(|r-r^{\prime}|+\min(r,r^{\prime})\,d_{N}({\bf y},{\bf y}^{\prime}))

for all
{\bf x}=({\bf y},r)
and
{\bf x}^{\prime}=({\bf y},r^{\prime})
of
{\rm Cyl}(N,\eta)
.

Proof.

If
r\to g^{\partial}(r)
is constant and equal to a tensor
g_{N}
on
N\times\{0\}
, then
by
straightforward computation we get

\frac{|r-r^{\prime}|}{2}+\frac{\min(r,r^{\prime})}{2}\,d_{N}({\bf y},{\bf y}^{\prime})\leq d^{c}({\bf x},{\bf x}^{\prime})\;\leq\;|r-r^{\prime}|+\min(r,r^{\prime})\,d_{N}({\bf y},{\bf y}^{\prime})

for the associated simple conic metric
{\rm d}r^{2}+r^{2}g_{N}
.
The general case is deduced from the constant case above. See also
[
10
]
.
∎

1.6.
Asymptotically conic metrics

Due to considerations similar to the previous Section
1.5
but for
scattering metrics, see
[
19
,
15
]
, we will give here a simplified
presentation of asymptotically conic metrics without taking away from generality,
compare Proposition-Definition
1.13
and Remark

1.16
.

Let
M
be a smooth manifold with compact boundary,
N
a smooth manifold without boundary and
\eta
a positive number.

Definition 1.15
.

An asymptotically conic metric over the cylinder
{\rm Cyl}(N,\eta)
is a smooth Riemannian metric
g^{\infty}
on the open cylinder

{\rm Cyl}(N,\eta)^{o}
such that the Riemannian metric
r^{4}g^{\infty}

extends as a smooth model conic metric
g^{0}
over
{\rm Cyl}(N,\eta)
.

The conic metric
g^{0}
is the conic metric associated with the
asymptotically conic metric
g^{\infty}
and over
{\rm Cyl}(N,\eta)^{o}=N_{\bf y}\times(0,\infty)_{r}
we can write

g^{\infty}=\frac{{\rm d}r^{2}}{r^{4}}+\frac{g^{\partial}(r)}{r^{2}}=\frac{1}{r^{4}}\,g^{0}

where
{\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}\ni r\to g^{\partial}(r)
is a smooth family of Riemannian metrics over
N
.

An asymptotically conic metric (ac-metric) on the manifold
(M,{\partial}M)
is a Riemannian metric
g^{\infty}
on
M\setminus{\partial}M
for which there exists a collar
neighbourhood diffeomorphism
\varphi:U_{r}^{\eta}\to{\rm Cyl}({\partial}M,\eta)

such that

\varphi_{*}g^{\infty}

is an asymptotically conic metric over
{\rm Cyl}({\partial}M,\eta)
.

The Riemannian metric
r^{2}g^{\infty}|_{{\partial}M}=g^{\partial}(0)
on
{\partial}M
is
the Riemannian metric on the boundary associated to the ac-metric.

Remark 1.16
.

An ac-metric is also known as a scattering metric
[
19
,
15
]
. The standard definition does not require
that it be a conformal factor of a model conic metric, but just of a conic
metric. Yet, a change of coordinates over
{\rm Cyl}(N,\eta)
allows this in
a neighbourhood of the boundary
N\times 0
, see
[
15
]
.

Let us illustrate the notion with a simple example.

Example 1.17
.

Let
{\bf bl}_{\infty}:{\bf S}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{R}_{>0}\to{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\setminus{\bf 0}
be the
spherical blowing-up of
{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
at infinity, defined by

{\bf bl}_{\infty}({\bf u},R)=\frac{{\bf u}}{R}.

The pull-back
{\bf bl}_{\infty}^{*}(eucl)
over
{\bf S}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{R}_{>0}
is the
simplest example of an asymptotically conic metric

{\bf bl}_{\infty}^{*}(eucl)=\frac{1}{r^{4}}[{\rm d}r^{2}+r^{2}{\rm d}{\bf u}^{2}].

Every ac-metric
g^{\infty}
introduces a distance function
d^{\infty}
on
M
such that
d^{\infty}({\bf x},{\bf y})=\infty
only if
{\bf x}
or
{\bf y}
lie in
{\partial}M
or in different connected components of
M
.

Proposition 1.18
.

Let
{\rm Cyl}(N,\eta)
be equipped with an ac-metric
g^{\infty}
and the induced distance
d^{\infty}
. Let
g^{0}={\rm d}r^{2}+r^{2}g^{\partial}(r)
be the associated model conic metric and denote
g_{N}:=g^{\partial}(0)
.
For points
{\bf x}=({\bf y},r)
and
{\bf x}^{\prime}=({\bf y},r^{\prime})
of
{\rm Cyl}(N,\eta)^{o}
define

e({\bf x},{\bf x}^{\prime})=\left|\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1}{r^{\prime}}\right|+\min\left(\frac{1}{r},\frac{1}{r^{\prime}}\right)\,d_{N}({\bf y},{\bf y}^{\prime}).

Then there exist positive constants
A^{\infty},B^{\infty}
, depending only on
g^{0}
and

\eta
, such that

(1.6)

A^{\infty}\,e({\bf x},{\bf x}^{\prime})\,\leq\,d^{\infty}({\bf x},{\bf x}^{\prime})\,\leq\,B^{\infty}e({\bf x},{\bf x}^{\prime})

for all
{\bf x},{\bf x}^{\prime}\in{\rm Cyl}(N,\eta)^{o}
.

Proof.

Proof as in Proposition
1.14
, see
[
10
]
and
[
11
]
as well.

1.7.
Manifolds with globally conic metrics

Let
(M,{\partial}M)
be a compact smooth manifold.

Definition 1.19
.

A globally conic metric on
M
is a conic metric over
M\setminus{\partial}M_{\infty}
, where
{\partial}M_{\infty}
a finite union of connected components of

{\partial}M
, which is an asymptotically conic metric on some neighbourhood of
{\partial}M_{\infty}
in
M
.

Then the triple
(M,{\partial}M,g)
is a manifold with a globally conic metric and the subset
{\partial}M_{\infty}
is the boundary at infinity of
(M,{\partial}M,g)
.

From the point of view of Lipschitz geometry, the following Proposition
1.20
shows interchangeability of globally conic metrics, similarly to Riemannian structure, compare Section
1.2
.

Proposition 1.20
.

Let
(M,{\partial}M,g)
and
(N,{\partial}N,h)
be two smooth manifolds with globally conic metrics and
\phi:M\to N
be a diffeomorphism such that

1.8.
Conic inversion

Let
(N,g_{N})
be a connected compact Riemannian manifold without
boundary. Denote by

the infinite (non-negative)
cylinder over
N

Let

be the open infinite cylinder over

The conic inversion of an open cylinder

is the mapping

\iota^{0}:P^{0}\to P^{0},\;\;({\bf y},r)\mapsto({\bf y},R)=\left({\bf y},\frac{1}{r}\right).

Gluing two disjoint
copies of

along their interiors

identified by the
inversion
\iota^{0}
yields the smooth compact manifold with
boundary

{\overline{P}}=N\times[0,\infty].

The inversion

\iota^{o}
extends onto
{\overline{P}}
as a diffeomorphism
\iota
, inverting

{\partial}(N\times{\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}})
and
{\partial}(N\times(0,\infty])
.

Consider the following singular Riemannian metrics

g^{0}={\rm d}r^{2}+r^{2}g^{\partial}(r)\;\;{\rm over}\;\;N_{\bf y}\times({\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}})_{r}\;\;{\rm and}\;\;g^{\infty}={\rm d}R^{2}+R^{2}g^{\partial}(R)\;\;{\rm over}\;\;N_{\bf y}\times(0,\infty]_{R};

Both are model conic metrics over their respective domains.
On
P^{0}
we get

r^{-4}\,g^{0}=(\iota^{0})^{*}g^{\infty},

thus
r^{-4}\,g^{0}
is an asymptotically conic metric over

P^{0}
.

Let
d^{0}
be the conic distance over
P^{0}
obtained from
g^{0}
, and let

d^{\infty}
be the distance over
P^{0}
obtained from

(\iota^{0})^{*}g^{\infty}
. A simple, though essential,
metric property relating conic with asymptotically conic distances is summarized as follows.

Proposition 1.21
.

There exist positive constants
A^{\prime},B^{\prime}
such that for all
{\bf x},{\bf x}^{\prime}\in P^{0}
the
following holds

(1.7)

A^{\prime}\cdot d^{0}({\bf x},{\bf x}^{\prime})\;\leq\;r\cdot r^{\prime}\cdot d^{\infty}({\bf x},{\bf x}^{\prime})\;\leq\;B^{\prime}\cdot d^{0}({\bf x},{\bf x}^{\prime}).

Proof.

From Estimates (
1.5
) and
(
1.6
) we can assume that

d^{0}({\bf x},{\bf x}^{\prime})=|r-r^{\prime}|+\min(r,r^{\prime})d_{N}({\bf y},{\bf y}^{\prime}),\;\;{\rm and}\;\;d^{\infty}({\bf x},{\bf x}^{\prime})=|R-R^{\prime}|+\min(R,R^{\prime})d_{N}({\bf y},{\bf y}^{\prime}),

with
R=r^{-1}
and
R^{\prime}=(r^{\prime})^{-1}
. We immediately obtain

r\cdot r^{\prime}\cdot d^{\infty}\geq d^{0}.

Distinguishing the cases

and
r^{\prime}\leq r\leq 2r^{\prime}

we get the other inequality.
∎

A\,\overline{d}({\bf x},{\bf x}^{\prime})\leq r\cdot r^{\prime}\cdot d({\bf x},{\bf x}^{\prime})
. The
remaining estimate is obtained similarly.
∎

3.
Lipschitz geometry on globally conic singular
manifolds

By definition, a manifold which is Riemannian, conic singular
or globally conic singular is LNE in itself as a length space. A
smooth sub-manifold of a Riemannian manifold is always locally
LNE, yet it may fail to be LNE, see
[
7
]
. In this section we show that conic singular sub-manifolds are locally LNE and globally conic singular manifolds are LNE in an ambient globally conic singular manifold, generalizing results of
[
7
]
.

3.1.
Preliminary remarks on LNE subsets of conic singular manifolds

The next
result generalizes
[
1
, Proposition
2.1]
,
[
16
, Proposition 2.4]
and
[
9
, Lemma
2.6]
in the context of conic singular manifolds.

Proposition 3.1
.

A connected compact subset of the conic singular manifold

(E,d)
is LNE if and only if it is locally LNE. In particular,
any connected compact
C^{1}
embedded sub-manifold of
E^{o}
,
possibly with boundary, is LNE.

Proof.

The proof of
[
16
, Proposition 2.4]
is metric in nature,
thus applies in this setting.
∎

The LNE property is hereditary in the
sense of Proposition
3.2
below. Equivalence
of induced metrics on an LNE subset shows
that LNE subsets preserve ambient Lipschitz properties, for
example Hölder exponents.

Proposition 3.2

(Proposition 1.9,
[
7
]
)
.

Let
X
be a subset of a conic singular manifold
(E,d)
which
is locally LNE at a point
{\bf x}
. A subset
Y
of
X
is locally
LNE at
{\bf x}
in
(X,d_{{{\rm in}}}^{X})
if and only if it is locally
LNE at
{\bf x}
in
(E,d)
.

The next result is the following analogue of a cone type
result in the Euclidean case.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
(E,d)
be a conic singular manifold and
{\bf a}\in E_{\rm sing}
.
Let
\phi:{\rm Cyl}(N,\eta)\to U
be a model conic chart on
E

centred at
{\bf a}
, let
g^{c}
be the model conic metric on

{\rm Cyl}(N,\eta)
and
g_{N}
be the Riemannian metric over

N
associated with
g^{c}
. If
Y
is a LNE subset of
(N,g^{\partial}(0))

then there exists a positive height
\eta_{Y}<\eta
such that
each image
\phi(Y\times[0,\varepsilon])
is LNE in
(E,d)
for any

0<\varepsilon\leq\eta_{Y}
.

Proof.

The model conic metric
g^{c}
writes as

g^{c}:={\rm d}r^{2}+r^{2}g^{\partial}(r)

with
g_{N}=g^{\partial}(0)
. By Corollary
1.12
, up to shrinking

\eta
, it is equivalent as a singular Riemannian metric to
the simple conic metric

h^{c}={\rm d}r^{2}+r^{2}g_{N}

In particular the inner conic distances induced respectively by

g^{c}
and
h^{c}
over any subset of
{\rm Cyl}(N,\eta)
are
equivalent.

Let
d^{N}
be the distance on
N
induced by
g_{N}
, and
d_{h^{c}}

be the conic distance induced by
h^{c}
on
Q={\rm Cyl}(N,\eta)
.
Let
X
be a LNE subset of
N
. We recall Estimates
(
1.5
)

\frac{|r-r^{\prime}|}{2}+\min(r,r^{\prime})\frac{d_{N}({\bf y},{\bf y}^{\prime})}{2}\;\leq\;d_{h^{c}}({\bf x},{\bf x}^{\prime})\;\leq\;|r-r^{\prime}|+\min(r,r^{\prime})d_{N}({\bf y},{\bf y}^{\prime}).

Let
d_{N}^{Y}
be the inner distance on
Y
obtained for
g_{N}
,
and let
d_{h^{c}}^{Y}
be the inner conic distance over
Q

obtained from
h^{c}
, that is the inner distance over
Q\setminus{\partial}Q
. Let
L
be a LNE constant for
Y
.
For points
{\bf x},{\bf x}^{\prime}\in Q
with
r^{\prime}\geq r
, then clearly

d_{h^{c}}^{Q}({\bf x},{\bf x}^{\prime})\geq|r-r^{\prime}|\;\;{\rm and}\;\;d_{h^{c}}^{Q}({\bf x},{\bf x}^{\prime})\geq r\,L\,d_{N}({\bf y},{\bf y}^{\prime})

Since the following estimates also holds true

d_{h^{c}}^{Q}({\bf x},{\bf x}^{\prime})\leq|r-r^{\prime}|+r\,L\,d^{N}({\bf y},{\bf y}^{\prime})

we conclude that any cylinder
{\rm Cyl}(X,\varepsilon)
with
\varepsilon\leq\eta

is LNE w.r.t. to
h^{c}
.

Let
d_{g^{c}}^{Q}
be the inner (pseudo-)distance induced by
g^{c}

over
Q
and let
X:=\phi(Q)
. By definition the spaces

(Q,d_{g^{c}}^{Q})
and
(X,d_{{\rm in}}^{X})
, are locally (pseudo-)isometric.
Since
g^{c}
and
h^{c}
are equivalent over
Q
, the result is proved.
∎

3.2.
Conic singular sub-manifolds are locally Lipschitz
normally embedded

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
X
be a subset of the conic singular manifold
(E,d)
such
that the germ
(X,{\bf a})
at the point
{\bf a}\in E
is that of a
closed conic singular sub-manifold. Then
X
is locally LNE at

{\bf a}
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality we can assume that
X\setminus{\bf a}

is a smooth sub-manifold of a smooth manifold
E\setminus{\bf a}
.
There exists a conic chart
\phi:P={\rm Cyl}(N,\eta)\to U
,
centred at
{\bf a}
in
E
such that
\phi^{*}g^{o}
extends smoothly
as the model conic metric
g^{c}
over
P
.

By definition, the closure
\mathcal{X}
of
\phi^{-1}(X\setminus{\bf a})

in
P
is a p-sub-manifold. It suffices to show that the outer conic distance and inner conic distance
over
\mathcal{X}
obtained from the conic metric
g^{c}
are
equivalent.

Since
({\bf y},r)
are global coordinates over
P
, the model
conic metric
g^{c}
writes

g^{c}={\rm d}r^{2}+r^{2}g^{\partial}(r).

Since the LNE property by Corollary
1.12
stays true regardless of the conic
metric we are working with, we can assume without loss of
generality, up to shrinking
\eta\leq 1
, that
g^{c}
is a simple conic metric, i.e. the family
r\to g^{\partial}(r)
is constant and equal
to the associated Riemmanian metric on the boundary
g_{N}:=g^{\partial}(0)
and there exists a collar
neighbourhood diffeomorphism

\tau:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{C}={\partial}\mathcal{X}\times[0,\eta),\;\;({\bf y},r)\mapsto(\omega({\bf y},r),r)).

Lemma 3.5
.

The mapping

\phi\circ\tau\circ\phi^{-1}:(\phi(\mathcal{X}),d)\to(\phi(\mathcal{C}),d)

is bi-Lipschitz for the outer metric space structures.

Proof.

Consider
P
equipped with the product metric

{\rm cyl}:={\rm d}r^{2}+g_{N}.

Let
|-|_{\rm cyl}
be the induced norm on
TP
and
d^{\rm cyl}
be the
induced distance on
P
. Let
|-|_{N}
be the norm on
TN

obtained from
g_{N}
, and
d^{N}
be the induced distance on
N
.
Last, let
|-|_{c}
be the semi-norm induced by
g^{c}
on
TP
and

d^{c}
be the conic distance on
P
. We recall
that, given
{\bf v}=(\xi,{\lambda})\in T_{({\bf y},r)}P=T_{\bf y}N\times\mathbb{R}
, the norms of
{\bf v}
are as follows

|{\bf v}|_{\rm cyl}^{2}={\lambda}^{2}+|\xi|_{N}^{2}\;\;{\rm and}\;\;|{\bf v}|_{c}^{2}={\lambda}^{2}+r^{2}\,|\xi|_{N}^{2}.

Observe that the identity mapping
(P,d^{\rm cyl})\to(P,d^{c})
is
(pseudo-)Lipschitz with Lipschitz constant
1
whenever
\eta\leq 1
.

Observe that the restriction of
|-|_{\rm cyl}
to
TN
is simply

|-|_{N}
. Given
{\bf z}=({\bf y},r)\in\mathcal{X}
, the (restriction to

T\mathcal{X}
of the)
{\rm cyl}
-norms for the deriatives of smooth mappings
\tau
and

\omega
are

\|D_{\bf z}\tau\|_{\rm cyl}\;\;{\rm and}\;\;\|D_{\bf z}\omega\|_{\rm cyl},

Analogously, over
\mathcal{X}\setminus{\partial}\mathcal{X}
, the (restriction to
T\mathcal{X}

of the)
g^{c}
-norms for
\tau
and
\omega
is denoted by

\|D_{\bf z}\tau\|_{c}\;\;{\rm and}\;\;\|D_{\bf z}\omega\|_{c},

Given a subset
S
of
P
and
r\geq 0
, let

S_{r}:=S\cap(N\times r).

Since
\omega|_{{\partial}\mathcal{X}\times 0}
is a diffeomorphism and

\tau(\mathcal{X}_{r})=\mathcal{C}_{r}
, the mapping
{\partial}_{N}\omega({\bf z})=D_{\bf z}\omega|_{T_{\bf z}\mathcal{X}_{r}}
is invertible, up to shrinking
\eta

there exists a positive constant
K
such that

\frac{1}{K}\leq\|{\partial}_{N}\omega({\bf z})\|_{\rm cyl}\leq K,\;\;\forall\;{\bf z}\in\mathcal{X}.

Observe that

\|{\partial}_{N}\omega({\bf z})\|_{\rm cyl}=\|{\partial}_{N}\omega({\bf z})\|_{c}.

Let
{\bf v}=(\xi,{\lambda})\in T_{\bf z}\mathcal{C}=T_{\bf y}{\partial}\mathcal{X}\times\mathbb{R}\subset T_{\bf z}P=T_{\bf y}N\times\mathbb{R}
with
{\bf z}\in\mathcal{X}
. We get

D_{\bf z}\omega\cdot{\bf v}={\partial}_{N}\omega({\bf z})\cdot{\bf v}+{\lambda}{\partial}_{r}\omega.

Thus there exists a positive constant
A
so that

|({\partial}_{r}\omega)({\bf z})|_{\rm cyl}\leq A\cdot\|{\partial}_{N}\omega({\bf z})\|_{\rm cyl},\;\;\;\forall\;{\bf z}\in\mathcal{X},

which yields

|D_{\bf z}\omega\cdot{\bf v}|_{c}=r\cdot|D_{\bf z}\omega\cdot{\bf v}|_{\rm cyl}\leq rB\cdot\|{\partial}_{N}\omega({\bf z})\|_{\rm cyl}\cdot|{\bf v}|_{\rm cyl},\;\;{\rm where}\;\;\ B=\sqrt{1+A^{2}}.

Thus

(3.1)

\|D_{\bf z}\omega\cdot{\bf v}\|_{c}\leq B\cdot\|{\partial}_{N}\omega({\bf z})\|_{\rm cyl},\;\;{\bf z}\in\mathcal{X}.

Let
{\bf 1}_{\mathbb{R}}
be the identity mapping of
\mathbb{R}
, the latter
being identified with
T_{\bf z}(0,r_{0})
and
T_{\tau({\bf z})}(0,r_{0})
. Since
D_{\bf z}\tau=D_{\bf z}\omega\oplus{\bf 1}_{\mathbb{R}}
, we
deduce that

\|D_{\bf z}\tau\|_{c}\leq 1+r\cdot\|D_{\bf z}\omega\|\leq 1+B\cdot|{\partial}_{N}\omega({\bf z})\|_{\rm cyl}

Thus
\tau
induces (pseudo-)Lipschitz homeomorphisms

(\mathcal{X}_{i},d^{c})\to(\mathcal{C}_{i},d^{c})
, where
\mathcal{X}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{X}_{s},
are
the connected components of
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{C}_{i}=\tau(\mathcal{X}_{i})
.
Since the smooth inverse
\tau^{-1}
is of the form
(r,{\bf y})\to(\mu(r,{\bf y}),r)
the exact same arguments show
that
\tau^{-1}
is also (pseudo-)Lipschitz with respect to the outer
conic metrics
d^{c}
over each
\mathcal{C}_{i}
.

Since
{\partial}\mathcal{X}=\cup_{i=1}^{s}\mathcal{S}_{i}
, where
\mathcal{S}_{i}
is a smooth
compact connected sub-manifold of
{\bf S}^{n-1}
if
{\bf a}\in E_{\rm sm}^{d}
or of
N
if
{\bf a}\in E_{\rm sing}^{d}
, and
\mathcal{S}_{i}\cap\mathcal{S}_{j}=\emptyset
for
1\leq i<j\leq s
, we deduce that

{\delta}:=\min\{d^{\rm cyl}(\mathcal{S}_{i},\mathcal{S}_{j}):1\leq i<j\leq k\}>0.

From which follows that for any
r\leq\eta
, up to shrinking

\eta
, we get

\min\{d^{c}(\mathcal{X}_{i})_{r},(\mathcal{X}_{j})_{r}):1\leq i<j\leq k\}\geq r\frac{{\delta}}{2}.

Fix a pair
1\leq i<j\leq s
and let
{\bf z}_{k}=({\bf y}_{k},r_{k})\in\mathcal{X}_{k}
with
k=i,j
. Then

d^{c}({\bf z}_{i},{\partial}P)+d^{c}({\partial}P,{\bf z}_{j})\geq d^{c}({\bf z}_{i},{\bf z}_{j})\geq d^{c}((\mathcal{X}_{i})_{r_{i}},(\mathcal{X}_{j})_{r_{j}}).

Since
\tau|_{{\partial}\mathcal{X}}
is the identity mapping of
{\partial}\mathcal{X}
, up
to shrinking
\eta
, we deduce the following estimates

r_{i}+r_{j}\geq d^{c}({\bf z}_{i},{\bf z}_{j})\geq\frac{{\delta}}{4}\,[r_{i}+r_{j}]

When
{\bf w}_{k}=\tau({\bf z}_{k})=(\omega_{k},r_{k})
, for
k=i,j
, we get

d^{c}({\bf w}_{i},{\bf w}_{j})\leq d^{c}({\bf w}_{i},{\partial}M)+d^{c}({\partial}M,{\bf w}_{j})=r_{i}+r_{j}\leq\frac{4}{{\delta}}\,d^{c}({\bf z}_{i},{\bf z}_{j})

We conclude that
\tau:(\mathcal{X}\setminus{\partial}\mathcal{X},d^{c})\to(\mathcal{C}\setminus{\partial}\mathcal{C},d^{c})
is (pseudo-)Lipschitz. The same
arguments will give that
\tau^{-1}
is (pseudo-)Lipschitz as
well.
∎

Since the image of the cylinder
\phi(\mathcal{C})
is LNE with respect to the
metric
d
by Proposition
3.3
, so is

\phi(\mathcal{X})
. Therefore
X
is locally LNE at
{\bf a}
.
∎

3.4
, Lemma
3.5

and Proposition
3.3
we get a family of LNE
representatives as follows.

Corollary 3.6
.

If
X
is a germ at a point
{\bf a}
of a conic singular
sub-manifold in a conic singular manifold
(E,d)
, then there
exists a positive number
\eta_{X}
and a germ of a non-negative

r:(E,{\bf a})\to\mathbb{R}
, smooth outside of
{\bf a}
and

such that

.

3.4
and Proposition

3.1
yield the following relevant
implications.

Corollary 3.7
.

.

3.3.
Globally conic singular sub-manifolds are Lipschitz
normally embedded

Theorem 3.9
.

Let
X
be a globally conic singular sub-manifold of the
globally conic singular manifold
(E,d)
. Then each
connected component of
X
is LNE in
E
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality we can assume that
X
is connected.

Let
(\overline{E},\overline{d})
be a conic completion of
(E,d)
and let
Y:={\overline{X}}
be the
closure of
X
in
\overline{E}
. Let
\infty_{X}=\infty_{E}\cap Y
be the conic ends of

X
. Since
(Y,\overline{d}_{{\rm in}}^{Y})
is a conic completion of the
globally conic singular manifold
(X,d_{{\rm in}}^{X})
, we get

\overline{d}_{{\rm in}}^{Y}|_{X}=\overline{d}_{{\rm in}}^{X}.

Since
Y
is a conic singular sub-manifold of the compact conic singular manifold

\overline{E}
, by Corollary
3.8
it is LNE, thus there exits a positive
constant
L^{\prime}
such that

\overline{d}_{Y}\leq\overline{d}_{{\rm in}}^{Y}\leq L^{\prime}\,\overline{d}_{Y}.

Corollary
2.12
, restricting from
\overline{E}
to
Y
, implies there
exist positive constants
A^{\prime},B^{\prime}
such that

A^{\prime}\,\overline{d}_{Y}({\bf x},{\bf x}^{\prime})\;\leq\;r\,r^{\prime}\,d_{X}({\bf x},{\bf x}^{\prime})\;\leq\;B^{\prime}\,\overline{d}_{Y}({\bf x},{\bf x}^{\prime}).

Define
\tilde{r}=\overline{d}_{{\rm in}}^{Y}({\bf x},\infty_{X})
and
\tilde{r}^{\prime}=\overline{d}_{{\rm in}}^{Y}({\bf x}^{\prime},\infty_{X})
,
Corollary
2.12
applied to
Y
with the inner distance
\overline{d}_{{\rm in}}^{Y}
gives positive constants
A^{\prime\prime},B^{\prime\prime}
such that

A^{\prime\prime}\,\overline{d}_{{\rm in}}^{Y}({\bf x},{\bf x}^{\prime})\;\leq\;\tilde{r}\,\tilde{r}^{\prime}\,d_{{\rm in}}^{X}({\bf x},{\bf x}^{\prime})\;\leq\;B^{\prime\prime}\,\overline{d}_{{\rm in}}^{Y}({\bf x},{\bf x}^{\prime}).

Therefore over
X
we get

d_{X}\leq d_{{\rm in}}^{X}\leq L\,d_{X}\;\;{\rm with}\;\;L=\frac{B^{\prime\prime}}{A^{\prime}}\,(L^{\prime})^{3},

since
\overline{d}_{Y}\leq\overline{d}_{{\rm in}}^{Y}\leq L^{\prime}\,\overline{d}_{Y}
, yielding
r\leq\tilde{r}\leq L^{\prime}\,r
.
∎

By Theorems
3.4
and
3.9
we easily get the following variation, of interest for applications.

.

Let
(E,d)
be a globally conic singular manifold and let

be its closed

is LNE in
(E,d)
if
and only if its conic completion

is LNE in

.

3.4.
Examples

The Euclidean space
({\mathbb{R}^{n}},|-|)
is naturally a globally conic
singular manifold and its conic completion is simply
{\bf S}^{n}
via
the inverse of the stereographic projection.
The main result of
[
8
]
states that each connected
component of a generic real algebraic set of
{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
is a
globally conic-singular sub-manifold
(usually non-singular) of
{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
, thus is LNE.

Consider the following singular Riemannian metric over
\mathbb{R}^{2}
:

h=[2x^{2}-2xy+y^{2}]{\rm d}x^{2}+2[x^{2}+xy-y^{2}]{\rm d}x{\rm d}y+[x^{2}+2xy+2y^{2}]{\rm d}y^{2}.

Denoting
r=\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}}
, we check that
h
is equivalent to
r^{2}eucl
over

\mathbb{R}^{2}
, where
eucl
is the Euclidean metric tensor. Therefore
g^{o}=r^{-2}h

is a Riemannian metric over
E^{o}=\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus{\bf 0}
. Let
E=\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
d
be the length distance over
E
induced from

g^{o}
. Since
g^{o}
is equivalent to
eucl|_{E^{o}}
, the
space
(E,d)
is a complete length space.
In polar coordinates, we find

g^{o}=2{\rm d}r^{2}+2r{\rm d}r{\rm d}{\theta}+r^{2}{\rm d}{\theta}^{2}.

The following mapping is continuous and surjective

\phi:M={\bf S}^{1}\times{\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}\to E,\;\;({\theta},r)\mapsto\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}{r\,e^{i({\theta}-\ln r)}}&{\rm if}&r>0\\
0&{\rm if}&r=0\end{array}\right.,

and over
M\setminus{\partial}M
, we verify that

\phi^{*}(g^{o})={\rm d}r^{2}+r^{2}{\rm d}{\theta}^{2},

thus extends as a conic
metric over
M
. Thus
(E,d)
is a conic singular manifold.
Using the conic inversion and
\phi
we check that
(E,d)
is
also asymptotically conic.

Observe that although
\phi
is not differentiable over
{\bf S}^{1}\times 0
as
a mapping with values in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, it is yet a globally conic
resolution of
(E,d)
in the sense of Proposition
2.8
.

Observe also that the (image of the unit speed)
g^{o}
-geodesic
from
{\bf x}_{0}=r_{0}\,e^{{\theta}_{0}}
to
{\bf 0}
is the
logarithmic spiral parametrized in polar coordinates as
r\to({\theta}_{0}+\ln r-\ln r_{0},r)
.
Obviously, a similar phenomenon holds true for any

g^{o}
-geodesic leaving any compact of
E
.

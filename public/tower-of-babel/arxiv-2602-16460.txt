On the Uniqueness and Structural Stability of Couette-Poiseuille Flow in a Channel for Arbitrary Values of the Flux
G.P. Galdi - F. Gazzola - M. Korobkov - X. Ren - G. Sperone

Abstract
We establish uniqueness and structural stability of a class of parallel flows in a 2D straight, infinite channel, under perturbations with either globally or locally bounded Dirichlet integrals. The significant feature of our result is that it does not require any restriction on the size of the flux characterizing the flow. Precisely, by extending and refining an approach initially introduced by J.B. McLeod, we demonstrate the continuous invertibility of the linearized operator at a generic Couette-Poiseuille solution that does not exhibit flow reversal. We then deduce local uniqueness of these solutions as well as their nonlinear structural stability under small external forces. Moreover, we prove the uniqueness of certain class of Couette-Poiseuille solutions “in the large,” within the set of solutions possessing natural symmetry. Finally, we bring an example showing that, in general, if the flow reversal assumption is violated, the linearized operator is no longer invertible. Keywords: Couette-Poiseuille flows; Navier-Stokes equations; linearized operator; invertibility; structural stability MSC2020: 35Q30; 35B35; 76D05

4

Using Proposition

Theorem 1
.

Assume (

has a unique solution

Proof.

{\bf f}

\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{u}_{*}\,,p=p_{*}
.

Remark 4
.

As observed previously, the Couette-Poiseuille flow belongs to the class
\mathbb{H}(S)
. Therefore, we can reformulate Corollary
2
by stating that, under its assumptions, there is no solution “close” to the Couette-Poiseuille solution.

6
Symmetric flows

In 2D symmetric domains, a solenoidal vector field
{\bf u}=(u_{1},u_{2})
may be symmetric, only if its components satisfy some “combined
symmetries” that allow to maintain the solenoidal condition. There are four possible cases:

\begin{array}[]{c}(X1)\ u_{1}\mbox{ is }x\mbox{-even and }u_{2}\mbox{ is }x\mbox{-odd}\qquad(X2)\ u_{1}\mbox{ is }x\mbox{-odd and }u_{2}\mbox{ is }x\mbox{-even}\\
(Y1)\ u_{1}\mbox{ is }y\mbox{-even and }u_{2}\mbox{ is }y\mbox{-odd}\qquad(Y2)\ u_{1}\mbox{ is }y\mbox{-odd and }u_{2}\mbox{ is }y\mbox{-even.}\end{array}

(6.1)

Clearly, for the particular problem (
1.1
), which is
x
-translation-invariant, the
x
-symmetry can be replaced with the symmetry
with respect to the line
x=c
for some
c\in\mathbb{R}
.

By suppressing the odd term in (
2.7
), namely, specializing (
2.6
) to the following

B=0,\qquad(A,C)\neq(0,0),\qquad A\leq 0,\qquad C\geq 3|A|,

(6.2)

we obtain the symmetric (even) flow

\mathcal{F}(y)=3Ay^{2}+C

(6.3)

that allows for tangential flows on
\partial S
, provided they have the same sign.
As a “limit” case, (
6.3
) includes the Poiseuille flow
\mathcal{F}(y)=-3A(1-y^{2})
and the constant flow
\mathcal{F}(y)\equiv C
.

The components
u_{1}=u_{1}(x,y)
and
u_{2}=u_{2}(x,y)
of flow such as (
6.3
) enjoy two of the above symmetry properties:

(u_{1},u_{2})\mbox{ satisfies }(X1),\ (Y1).

These symmetry assumptions allow us to simplify essentially the proofs of Theorems
1
-
3
, see Remark
5
.
What is much more important, under these symmetry assumptions a stronger uniqueness result in the large can be proved.

6.1
Uniqueness of symmetric solutions to the nonlinear problem

Remark 5
.

Under the assumptions of Corollary

as direct sum of the two orthogonal

H^{2}_{0}(S)=\mathcal{H}_{e}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{o}

Next, we observe that both (X1) and (X2) imply that

is

\int_{S}\mathcal{F}(y)\psi_{yy}\psi_{x}=\int_{S}\big(\mathcal{F}(y)\psi_{xx}-6A\psi\big)\psi_{x}=0\qquad\forall\psi\in\mathcal{H}_{e}\cup\mathcal{H}_{o}

and what Rabier

and we weakly reformulate (

) (with

) in these subspaces. We first prove that

\forall T_{e}\in H^{-2}_{e}\quad\exists!\psi\in\mathcal{H}_{e}\mbox{ s.t. }\int_{S}\Delta\psi\Delta\varphi+K\int_{S}\big(\mathcal{F}(y)(\psi_{yy}+\psi_{xx})-6A\psi\big)\varphi_{x}=\langle T_{e},\varphi\rangle\quad\forall\varphi\in\mathcal{H}_{e}\,.

(6.7)

By taking

in (

\int_{S}|\Delta\psi|^{2}=\langle T_{e},\psi\rangle\leq\|T_{e}\|_{H^{-2}_{e}}\|\psi\|_{\mathcal{H}_{e}}\qquad\forall\psi\in\mathcal{H}_{e}.

(6.8)

6.2
Non-invertibility of the linearization around some symmetric flows

The proof of Theorem
3
strongly uses (
2.6
) which implies the positivity of the function
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{F}(y)
in
(-1,1)
, see
(
2.7
), that is, absence of flow reversal. It is then natural to wonder if a similar invertibility result also holds if (
2.6
) fails. We exhibit here
a counter-example showing that this is not the case for some reverse flow.

Theorem 6
.

There exist
A<0
,
C<3|A|
,
T>0
such that the linearized operator at the Couette-Poiseuille flow

\mathcal{F}(y)=3Ay^{2}+C\,,

(6.9)

in the periodic channel
x\in[0,2\pi/T]/\sim
,
y\in[-1,1]
with the corresponding boundary conditions, is not injective.

Proof.

Related to (
3.1
), consider the following eigenvalue problem for the Orr-Sommerfeld operator

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\!\!\!\varphi^{\prime\prime\prime\prime}(y)-2T^{2}\varphi^{\prime\prime}(y)+T^{4}\varphi(y)+3ATi\,\Big[(1-y^{2})(\varphi^{\prime\prime}(y)-T^{2}\varphi(y))+2\varphi(y)\Big]=\lambda(\varphi^{\prime\prime}(y)-T^{2}\varphi(y))\\
\!\!\!\varphi(\pm 1)=\varphi^{\prime}(\pm 1)=0\,,\end{array}\right.

(6.10)

where
A<0
and
T>0
are constants to be determined.

By the result of Grenier, Guo and Nguyen
[
9
]
on the spectral instability of Poiseuille flows,

\begin{array}[]{cc}\exists\ A_{0}<0\ T_{0}>0\mbox{ s.t. \eqref{OS-2} with $A=A_{0},\ T=T_{0}$ admits a nonzero eigenfunction}\\
\mbox{whose corresponding eigenvalue has strictly positive real part.}\end{array}

(6.11)

On the other hand, we claim that

if
|AT|
is sufficiently small, then (
6.10
) admits only eigenvalues
\lambda
with
\text{Re}\,\lambda<0
.

(6.12)

To see this, we test (
6.10
)
1
with
\overline{\varphi}
and integrate by parts in
y
to obtain

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}(|\varphi^{\prime\prime}|^{2}+(2T^{2}+\lambda)|\varphi^{\prime}|^{2}+(T^{4}+\lambda T^{2})|\varphi|^{2})dy

\displaystyle=3ATi\int_{-1}^{1}\left[(1-y^{2})(|\varphi^{\prime}|^{2}+T^{2}|\varphi|^{2})-2|\varphi|^{2}\right]dy

\displaystyle\quad-6ATi\int_{-1}^{1}y\varphi^{\prime}\overline{\varphi}dy.

(6.13)

Suppose that
\text{Re}\,\lambda\geq 0
, then taking the real part of (
6.2
) we get

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}|\varphi^{\prime\prime}|^{2}dy\leq\int_{-1}^{1}\left[|\varphi^{\prime\prime}|^{2}+(2T^{2}+\text{Re}\,\lambda)|\varphi^{\prime}|^{2}+(T^{4}+T^{2}\text{Re}\,\lambda)|\varphi|^{2}\right]dy

\displaystyle=6AT\,\text{Im}\int_{-1}^{1}y\varphi^{\prime}\overline{\varphi}dy.

(6.14)

Using the Hölder and Poincaré-Wirtinger inequalities, we find that

\left|\int_{-1}^{1}y\varphi^{\prime}\overline{\varphi}dy\right|\leq\left(\int_{-1}^{1}|\varphi^{\prime}|^{2}dy\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{-1}^{1}|\varphi|^{2}dy\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq c\int_{-1}^{1}|\varphi^{\prime\prime}|^{2}dy

which, combined with (
6.14
), shows that if
|AT|
is sufficiently small, then
\varphi\equiv 0
; so (
6.12
) is proved.

Note that the discrete spectrum of the operator (unbounded and closed on
H_{0}^{1}
, with domain
H^{2}_{0}\cap H^{3}
)

(\partial_{y}^{2}-T^{2})^{-1}\left\{(\partial_{y}^{2}-T^{2})^{2}+3ATi\left[(1-y^{2})(\partial_{y}^{2}-T^{2})+2\right]\right\}

(6.15)

depends continuously on the parameter
A
(see, e.g.,
[
12
]
), where
(\partial_{y}^{2}-T^{2})^{-1}
is defined using Dirichlet boundary conditions. Hence, by (
6.11
)-(
6.12
), we know that there exists some
A_{0}<A_{1}<0
such that, for
A=A_{1},T=T_{0}
, the problem (
6.10
) admits an eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}
with
\text{Re}\,\lambda_{1}=0
. In this case, (
6.10
) can be written as

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\!\!\!\varphi^{\prime\prime\prime\prime}(y)-2T_{0}^{2}\varphi^{\prime\prime}(y)+T_{0}^{4}\varphi(y)-3A_{1}T_{0}i\,\Big[(-1+\frac{\text{Im}\lambda_{1}}{3A_{1}T_{0}}+y^{2})(\varphi^{\prime\prime}(y)-T_{0}^{2}\varphi(y))-2\varphi(y)\Big]=0\\
\!\!\!\varphi(\pm 1)=\varphi^{\prime}(\pm 1)=0\,.\end{array}\right.

Hence, we obtain the conclusion with
A=A_{1}
,
C=-3A_{1}+\frac{\text{Im}\lambda_{1}}{T_{0}}
,
T=T_{0}
.
From Theorem
3
we infer that, necessarily,
\text{Im}\lambda_{1}<0
(otherwise the condition (
2.6
) is satisfied so that the linearization at the flow (
6.9

Remark 6
.

It is still unclear how to prove the existence of stationary solutions bifurcating from the flows (
6.9
), which are equivalent to traveling wave solutions bifurcating from the standard Poiseuille flow. Due to the translation invariance of the problem along
x
, the null space of the linearized operator at (

) is always even dimensional (as a real vector space). Hence, the classical Krasnoselskii–Rabinowitz Theorem
[
13
,
20
]
(see also
[
3
, Chapter 22]
) does not apply in this situation.

7
Some historical facts

In March 1998, G.P. Galdi gave a seminar at the mathematics department of the University of Pittsburgh on the possible existence of bounded solutions, different from the classical Poiseuille solution (that is,
\mathcal{F}(y)=-3A(1-y^{2})
), in an infinite two-dimensional straight channel. In particular, he formulated the boundary value problem (
3.1
) with
\mathcal{F}
as above and
h_{\xi}(y)\equiv 0
, and raised the question of whether it could admit a non-trivial solution. The seminar was attended by W.C. Troy, P. Rabier and J.B. McLeod, among others. A few weeks later, W.C. Troy presented some numerical results that suggested that the solution should be identically equal to zero. Subsequently, in October 1998, J.B. McLeod wrote a private letter to G.P. Galdi attempting to provide a rigorous proof of this result. However, his proof contained a flaw, which was subsequently and successfully corrected in a new proof provided by J.B. McLeod in a letter dated November 1998 addressed to G.P. Galdi
[
15
]
. Apparently, such an important result remained unpublished and, certainly, was not adequately disseminated.
In this regard, in 2002, P. Rabier
in the note added in proof
[
18
, p.373]
writes that “J.B. McLeod was recently able to show in

Eigenvalue stability of stationary Poiseuille flow (preprint)
that these operators are one-to-one for any flow. It thus follows from our Lemma 2.2 (i) that they are isomorphisms, which, as also shown in this paper, implies the invertibility
of the Poiseuille linearization for all values of
\mathbb{R}
.” In fact, there is no Lemma 2.2 (i) in
[
18
]
and Rabier probably refers
to Lemma 3 (ii), a very powerful result indeed! Moreover, there is no trace on the web of a preprint (nor a published version!) of such notes
by McLeod. Many years later, Rabier
[
19
]
clarified that he “only heard the result by McLeod in a seminar given by him”.
Apparently, this paper was never published and, according to
[
10
]
, these notes are contained in
[
11
, Sections 10.4 and 10.5]
. Quite surprinsingly, at present date, the book
[
11
]
has 37 citations on MathSciNet and 108 citations on Google Scholar, but none of them within the Mathematical Fluid Mechanics community. This seems to demonstrate that the proof contained in
[
15
]
is completely unknown to the mathematicians in question, and we hope that this article will help to disseminate it.

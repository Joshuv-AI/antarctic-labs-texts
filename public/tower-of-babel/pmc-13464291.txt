Atrial Synchrony Restored With Bachmann Bundle Pacing
Authors: Amarpal Karamjit Singh, Georgios Zisis, Kai Van Leeuwen, Vivek Mutha
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractCase SummaryCapturing intrinsic interatrial conduction with Bachmann bundle (BB) pacing is known as a more physiological atrial pacing strategy than conventional right atrial appendage pacing. We present a 74-year-old man with sinus node dysfunction and paroxysmal atrial fibrillation who underwent dual-chamber pacemaker implantation, incorporating BB and left bundle branch ventricular pacing. Interatrial and atrioventricular synchrony was achieved with a shorter P-wave duration and normalization of the PR interval.Take-Home MessageBB pacing may restore physiologic atrial activation by engaging the preferential interatrial conduction pathway and may be beneficial in patients with sinus node dysfunction and atrial arrhythmias.Key words: ablation, atrial fibrillation, atrial flutter, cardiac pacemaker, cardiac resynchronization therapy, cardiovascular disease, electrocardiogram

Clinical Vignette

A 74-year-old man with a 2-year history of paroxysmal atrial fibrillation (AF) and previous atrial flutter requiring ablation was electively admitted for permanent pacemaker (PPM) implantation to treat sinus node dysfunction and paroxysmal AF confirmed on an implantable loop recorder. His medical history included hypertension, dyslipidemia, and an elevated body mass index (30 kg/m2). The baseline electrocardiogram (ECG) in Figure 1A shows sinus rhythm with P-wave duration of 120 ms, first degree atrioventricular (AV) block with a PR interval of 240 msec, and a normal QRS complex. The left atrial volume index measured by transthoracic echocardiography was 42 mL/m2, with a preserved left ventricular ejection fraction. Following a normal coronary computed tomography angiography, a trial of flecainide 50 mg twice a day was given concurrently with atenolol 25 mg without success in rate and rhythm control. To restore intrinsic atrial synchrony, the patient underwent a dual-chamber PPM (Medtronic Azure XT DR MRI SureScan) implantation with active fixation leads to the Bachmann bundle (BB) and left bundle branch regions (Medtronic SelectSecure MRI SureScan 3830; 59 and 69 cm, respectively). Under fluoroscopic and contrast guidance, the atrial lead was positioned using a c315S5 sheath and 3830 lead (Medtronic) in the posteromedial region of the lower superior vena cava, adjacent to the high right atrial septum, where the BB breakout is located. Successful BB lead positioning was characterized by a bipolar atrial electrogram ≤20 msec in paced P-wave duration relative to intrinsic duration and positive P-waves in leads II and III.

The ventricular lead was positioned using a c315HIS sheath and a 3830 lead (Medtronic) via conduction system pacing aimed at the left bundle branch area. This was achieved by positioning the catheter and lead along the septal wall, and unipolar pace mapping was used to find the optimal site. The lead was subsequently advanced into the septum. Adequate position was confirmed by a narrow-paced QRS complex. PPM mode was programmed for dual-chamber pacing with rate response with a lower rate of 60 beats/min and an upper rate of 130 beats/min. The post-PPM ECG, as shown in Figure 1B, demonstrates a paced QRS complex close to intrinsic at 120 msec and a paced P-wave of 80 msec. Recovery was successful and uncomplicated. The location of the BB lead position was confirmed on fluoroscopy (Figure 1C). The patient remained largely asymptomatic post BB pacing. Follow-up device interrogation showed similar AF burden before and after BB pacing (22.3% compared with 16.8%, respectively).

Discussion

The BB represents the preferential interatrial conduction pathway, facilitating coordinated electrical activation from the right to the left atrium.1 Patients with cardiometabolic risk factors and moderately dilated left atrium, such as in our case, are at risk for atrial arrhythmias because of fibrofatty replacement of atrial tissue, including the BB, leading to delayed and altered left atrial activation. This results in a prolonged and abnormal P-wave morphology (Supplement 1). A shorter P-wave duration of 40 msec from baseline is appreciated in Figure 1B, showing upright P waves in leads II, III, and aVF, with a biphasic P-wave in V1, a key finding in successful BB pacing.1,2 Engagement of the BB facilitates more physiological interatrial conduction and may improve AV synchrony compared with conventional right atrial appendage pacing. This may lead to reduced left atrial pressures and AF burden compared with conventional pacing.2

Improvement in physiological AV synchrony was achieved in this patient with a shortening of the PR interval to 200 ms, as shown in Figure 1B (excludes Option A). Although a low-amplitude monophasic P-wave is typically seen in BB pacing because of more uniform anisotropic conduction (Supplement 2), P-wave duration does not lengthen (excludes Option C) as is commonly seen in right atrial appendagepacing.3 Consistent atrial capture with a fixed AV delay confirms atrial pacing function, but it does not identify restoration of physiological interatrial conduction (excludes option D).

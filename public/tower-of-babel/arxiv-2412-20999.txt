The Category of Operator Spaces and Complete Contractions
Bert Lindenhovius, Vladimir Zamdzhiev

Abstract
We show that the category 𝐎𝐒 \mathbf{OS} of operator spaces, with complete contractions as morphisms, is locally countably presentable. This result, together with its symmetric monoidal closed structure with respect to the projective tensor product of operator spaces, implies the existence of cofree (cocommutative) coalgebras with respect to the projective tensor product and therefore provides a mathematical model of Intuitionistic Linear Logic in the sense of Lafont.

Introduction

Operator spaces
[
10
,
4
,
18
]
are
structures in noncommutative geometry that generalise mathematical objects such
as von Neumann algebras, C*-algebras, and operator systems, all of which are
relevant to the study of quantum information. The increased generality
provided by operator spaces allows us to cover even more spaces that are
relevant in quantum information theory (e.g. the space of trace-class operators
on a Hilbert space) and to study them with the methods and techniques from
noncommutative geometry. Operator spaces have been widely recognised as the
noncommutative (or quantised) analogue of Banach spaces and many results from
Banach space theory have been generalised to the theory of operator spaces. In
this paper, we show that an important categorical property of Banach spaces –
local presentability – also carries over to operator spaces and we showcase
some of the mathematical implications of this fact. This is a very strong
categorical property and we show that by using it, we can prove the existence
of cofree (cocommutative) coalgebras with respect to the projective tensor
product of operator spaces.

In this section we recall some background on locally presentable categories
that we need for our development. We assume prior knowledge of basic
categorical notions such as functors, (co)limits, natural transformations, etc.
Locally presentable categories were originally introduced by Gabriel and Ulmer

[

. The background that we present here is mostly based on
the textbook accounts in

Definition 2.1

(
\alpha
-directed Poset)
.

A partially ordered set (poset)
\Lambda
is
directed

X\subseteq\Lambda
has an upper bound in
\Lambda.
If
\alpha

\alpha

with cardinality strictly smaller than
\alpha

an upper bound in
\Lambda.

In particular, the notion of directed set coincides with that of an

\aleph_{0}
-directed set. We also say that an
\aleph_{1}
-directed set is

countably directed
. Indeed, an equivalent way to say that
\Lambda
is

\aleph_{1}

X\subseteq\Lambda

has an upper bound in
\Lambda.
We also wish to note that the notion of

\alpha

\Lambda

rather it is a condition regarding the shape of
\Lambda.
For instance,
a countably-directed poset is indeed directed, but it does not have to be countable.

Example 2.2
.

The natural numbers with the usual order
(\mathbb{N},\leq)
is a directed
poset, but it is not countably-directed. The unit interval with its standard
order
([0,1],\leq)
is countably-directed. Another example is the poset

(\mathbb{N}^{\top},\leq)
consisting of the natural numbers with a freely
added top element
\top
with the obvious order.

Local Presentability of Banach Spaces

It is already known that
\mathbf{Ban}
is locally countably presentable

[
2
,
7
]
. We know of two different proofs of this
fact that we wish to discuss in relation to our proof of local presentability
of
\mathbf{OS}
. The proof presented in
[
2
, Example 1.48]
shows
that
\mathbf{Ban}
is equivalent to a reflective subcategory of
\mathbf{Tot}
,
the category of totally convex spaces. The latter category is locally countably
presentable and the proof follows (using categorical arguments) from this and
the closure under countably-directed colimits of the subcategory. We do not
think that
\mathbf{OS}
is equivalent to a reflective subcategory of

\mathbf{Tot}
, so we do not think that this proof strategy would work. In
fact,
\mathbf{Ban}
is equivalent to a reflective subcategory of
\mathbf{OS}
(see
Section
4
). Perhaps one can construct a different locally
presentable category that serves the role of
\mathbf{Tot}
for operator
spaces, but we do not pursue this approach here. Instead, we opt for a more
direct proof that aligns with the approach in
[
7
]
.

The proof of local presentability of
\mathbf{Ban}
in
[
7
, 5.2.2e]
gives an
explicit construction for countably-directed colimits and shows that the strong
generator
\mathbb{R}
(Banach spaces are considered over the reals there) is
countably-presentable by showing that
\mathbf{Ban}(\mathbb{R},-)
preserves the
aforementioned colimits. Our proof strategy for showing local presentability of

\mathbf{OS}
is similar. The only significant difference is in the strong generators,
so we have to establish the countable-presentability of a greater range of
objects. The choice of field (
\mathbb{R}
vs
\mathbb{C}
) is not important for
the proof in
\mathbf{Ban}
.

The purpose of this section is to prove some additional properties of
countably-directed colimits in
\mathbf{Ban}
that are not available in the literature
(to the best of our knowledge) and that we need for our subsequent development
for the locally presentable structure of
\mathbf{OS}.
Most notably, we use
Proposition

in the next
section in order to characterise the countably-presentable objects in
\mathbf{OS}.
In
preparation for this, we also characterise the countably-presentable objects of

\mathbf{Ban}
and we show that they coincide with the separable Banach spaces (Theorem

3.11
). We have not been able to find a proof of this
(unsurprising) result in the literature.

Remark 3.1
.

Before we begin with our technical development, we note that we adopt the
convention that the least natural number is
1
, as it is common in
functional analysis. In order to avoid cumbersome repetition, all maps
between vector spaces, Banach spaces, and operator spaces are implicitly assumed
to be linear, i.e. we may simply write “contraction” instead of “linear
contraction”, “isometry” instead of “linear isometry”, etc.

Let us start by explaining why
\mathbf{Ban}
is not locally

presentable. The main reason is in the way that directed colimits (that are
not necessarily countably-directed) are constructed. Suppose that
D\colon\Lambda\to\mathbf{Ban}
is a directed diagram where each morphism
D_{\lambda,\tau}\colon D_{\lambda}\to D_{\tau}
is given by (isometric) subspace inclusion.
Consider the space

C\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{{\scriptsize def}}}}{{=}}\bigcup_{\lambda\in\Lambda}D_{\lambda}

together with the norm
\left\lVert c\right\rVert\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{{\scriptsize def}}}}{{=}}\left\lVert c\right\rVert_{\lambda},
where

and

in the category

So far, we have only used the fact that

\displaystyle=\inf\ \{\left\lVert w\right\rVert\ |\ \kappa\in\Lambda,w\in D_{\kappa},[s_{\kappa}(w)]=v\},

The following property of countably-directed colimits in

has been

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
C
be obtained via Construction
3.2
.
For each

there is some
\lambda\in\Lambda
and some
w\in D_{\lambda}

such that
c_{\lambda}(w)=v
and
\|w\|=\|v\|
.

By Lemma
3.3
(a), for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
there is some
\kappa_{n}\in\Lambda
and some

such that

and

[s_{\kappa_{n}}(w_{n})]=c_{\kappa_{n}}(w_{n})=v=c_{\kappa_{m}}(w_{m})=[s_{\kappa_{m}}(w_{m})]

\lambda\geq\kappa_{(n,m)}
for each
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
.
Then for each

, we have

, because

\displaystyle D_{\kappa_{n},\lambda}(w_{n})

\displaystyle=\left(D_{\kappa_{(n,m)},\lambda}\circ D_{\kappa_{n},\kappa_{(n,m)}}\right)(w_{n})

\displaystyle=\left(D_{\kappa_{(n,m)},\lambda}\circ D_{\kappa_{m},\kappa_{(n,m)}}\right)(w_{m})

\displaystyle=D_{\kappa_{m},\lambda}(w_{m}).

be the element such that

Next, we present an important property of countably-directed colimits in

\mathbf{Ban}
. We have not seen this claimed in the literature, but this proposition
is essential for proving that separable Banach spaces are
countably-presentable.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
\{v_{n}\ |\ n\in\mathbb{N}\}
be a countable subset of
C
from Construction
3.2
. Then there is some
\lambda\in\Lambda
and a countable subset

\{w_{n}\ |\ n\in\mathbb{N}\}\subseteq D_{\lambda}
such that
c_{\lambda}(w_{n})=v_{n}
and
\|v_{n}-v_{m}\|=\|w_{n}-w_{m}\|
for each
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

Let
v_{0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{{\scriptsize def}}}}{{=}}0
. By Lemma
3.4
, for each
n,m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, we can find some
\kappa_{(n,m)}\in\Lambda
and some

such that

c_{\kappa_{(n,m)}}(x_{n,m})=v_{n}-v_{m}
and
\|x_{n,m}\|=\|v_{n}-v_{m}\|
.
By countable-directedness, we can find
\kappa\geq\kappa_{(n,m)}
for each
n,m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and
we define
y_{n,m}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{{\scriptsize def}}}}{{=}}D_{\kappa_{(n,m)},\kappa}(x_{n,m})\in D_{\kappa}
.
Since
D_{\kappa_{(n,m)},\kappa}
is a contraction, we have
\|y_{n,m}\|\leq\|x_{n,m}\|=\|v_{n}-v_{m}\|
for each
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
.
Note that

c_{\kappa}(y_{n,m})=\left(c_{\kappa}\circ D_{\kappa_{(n,m)},\kappa}\right)(x_{n,m})=c_{\kappa_{(n,m)}}(x_{n,m})=v_{n}-v_{m}.

Hence, we have

c_{\kappa}(y_{n,0}-y_{m,0})=c_{\kappa}(y_{n,0})-c_{\kappa}(y_{m,0})=v_{n}-v_{m}=c_{\kappa}(y_{n,m})

and therefore
s_{\kappa}(y_{n,0}-y_{m,0})\sim s_{\kappa}(y_{n,m})
in
\coprod_{\lambda\in\Lambda}D_{\lambda}
.
This means that for each
n,m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
there is some
\lambda_{(n,m)}\geq\kappa
such that
D_{\kappa,\lambda_{(n,m)}}(y_{n,m})=D_{\kappa,\lambda_{(n,m)}}(y_{n,0}-y_{m,0})
.
Again, by countable-directedness, there is some
\lambda\geq\lambda_{(n,m)}
for each
n,m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.
Now, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
w_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{{\scriptsize def}}}}{{=}}D_{\kappa,\lambda}(y_{n,0})\in D_{\lambda}
.
Then
c_{\lambda}(w_{n})=\left(c_{\lambda}\circ D_{\kappa,\lambda}\right)(y_{n,0})=c_{\kappa}(y_{n,0})=v_{n}-v_{0}=v_{n}
, whereas for each
n,m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, we have

\displaystyle\|w_{n}-w_{m}\|

\displaystyle=\|D_{\kappa,\lambda}(y_{n,0}-y_{m,0})\|

\displaystyle=\|\left(D_{\lambda_{(n,m)},\lambda}\circ D_{\kappa,\lambda_{(n,m)}}\right)(y_{n,0}-y_{m,0})\|

\displaystyle\leq\|D_{\kappa,\lambda_{(n,m)}}(y_{n,0}-y_{m,0})\|

\displaystyle=\|D_{\kappa,\lambda_{(n,m)}}(y_{n,m})\|\leq\|y_{n,m}\|\leq\|v_{n}-v_{m}\|,

where we used that the
D_{\cdot,\cdot}
maps are contractions. Since
c_{\lambda}(w_{n}-w_{m})=c_{\lambda}(w_{n})-c_{\lambda}(w_{m})=v_{n}-v_{m}
, it follows by definition of
\left\lVert-\right\rVert
on
C
that also

\|v_{n}-v_{m}\|\leq\|w_{n}-w_{m}\|
, hence we obtain
\|v_{n}-v_{m}\|=\|w_{n}-w_{m}\|
for each
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
.
∎

\|x\|=\lim_{n\to\infty}\|a_{n}\|
.
Therefore

\|g(x)\|=\left\|\lim_{n\to\infty}v_{a_{n}}\right\|=\lim_{n\to\infty}\|v_{a_{n}}\|=\lim_{n\to\infty}\|a_{n}\|=\|x\|,

where we also used the fact that
\left\lVert v_{a_{n}}\right\rVert=\left\lVert a_{n}\right\rVert
which follows by the second property of Proposition

3.7
(simply take one of the vectors there to be
0
).
This means that
g
is an isometry.

To complete the proof, observe that we have for every
b\in B
that
h(g(b))=h(v_{b})=b

and for every
v_{b}\in V
, we have that
g(h(v_{b}))=g(b)=v_{b}
. It follows that

h\circ g=\mathrm{id}_{X}
and
g\circ h=\mathrm{id}_{X_{\lambda}},
because the
functions on both sides of the equalities are continuous and they coincide on
dense subsets of spaces with Hausdorff topologies. Therefore both functions
are isometric isomorphisms and
g=h^{-1}
. The commutativity of the diagram
is obvious.
∎

We can now show that any separable Banach space is countably-presentable in

\mathbf{Ban}
by using Proposition

again. In fact, what we prove is a slightly stronger statement, because the

Proposition 3.9
.

Let

be a separable Banach space, and let

be a countably-directed diagram with colimit
(C,\{c_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda})
as given by
Construction

(1)

for some
\lambda\in\Lambda

;

(2)

for each
\lambda\in\Lambda

are bounded linear maps such that

,

\tau\geq\lambda

.

Proof.

For (1), since

is separable, then

is a closed separable subspace of

Using Proposition
3.8
, we can find

and a closed separable

which is clearly a linear contraction. It is now obvious that

For the second condition, let

because these

∎

In order to show that any countably-presentable object in
\mathbf{Ban}
is separable, the following proposition is important.

Proposition 3.10
.

Every Banach space is a countably-directed colimit (in
\mathbf{Ban}
) of its closed separable subspaces.

Proof.

Let

be a Banach space.
The embedding of any closed subspace of

Standard topological results show that

∎

We begin by recalling some background on operator spaces and we also use this

Definition 4.1

(Matrix Space)
.

Let

be a vector space. We write
\mathbb{M}_{n}(V)
for the vector space
consisting of the
n\times n
matrices with matrix entries in the vector

(with vector space operations defined componentwise). When

\mathbb{M}_{n}
for
\mathbb{M}_{n}(\mathbb{C}).

The vector space

can be equipped with a Banach space norm in a
canonical way that we now describe. There exists a linear isomorphism

\mathbb{M}_{n}\cong B(\mathbb{C}^{n})
with the space of (bounded) linear
operators on the Hilbert space
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. The space

has a
canonical norm (i.e. the operator norm) which can then be used to define a
norm on
\mathbb{M}_{n}
via the above isomorphism. Writing
M_{n}
for the
corresponding Banach space, the aforementioned linear isomorphism now becomes
an isometric isomorphism
M_{n}\cong B(\mathbb{C}^{n}).

Definition 4.2
.

An
abstract operator space
is a vector space
X
together with a family of norms
\{\left\lVert-\right\rVert_{n}\colon\mathbb{M}_{n}(X)\to[0,\infty)\ |\ n\in\mathbb{N}\}
, such that:

(B)

The norm
\left\lVert-\right\rVert_{1}\colon\mathbb{M}_{1}(X)\to[0,\infty)
gives
\mathbb{M}_{1}(X)
the structure of a Banach space;

(M1)

\|x\oplus y\|_{m+n}=\max\{\|x\|_{m},\|y\|_{n}\}

(M2)

\|\alpha x\beta\|_{m}\leq\|\alpha\|\|x\|_{m}\|\beta\|

for each
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
,
x\in\mathbb{M}_{m}(X)
,
y\in\mathbb{M}_{n}(X)
,
\alpha,\beta\in M_{m}
. Here
x\oplus y\in\mathbb{M}_{m+n}(X)
is defined as the matrix

x\oplus y\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{{\scriptsize def}}}}{{=}}\begin{bmatrix}x&0\\
0&y\end{bmatrix}

and
\alpha x\beta
is defined through the obvious generalisation of matrix
multiplication.
For an operator space
X
, we write
M_{n}(X)
for the normed space
(\mathbb{M}_{n}(X),\left\lVert-\right\rVert_{n})
. We also write
\left\lVert-\right\rVert\colon X\to[0,\infty)
for the norm
defined on
X
through the linear isomorphism
M_{1}(X)\cong X.
We
call a family of norms that satisfies the above criteria an
operator
space structure
(OSS) on the vector space
X
.

Given an operator space

, it is clear that

is a Banach space with
respect to the norm
\left\lVert-\right\rVert
described above and textbook results show that
each space

is also a Banach space.

Example 4.3
.

The vector space of complex numbers

4.2
Limits and Colimits in
\mathbf{OS}

A necessary condition for a category to be locally presentable is for it to be
cocomplete and indeed this is part of the definition that we presented.
Another necessary condition is for it to also be complete
[
2
, Corollary
1.28]
which follows from Definition

2.7
in a non-trivial way. Despite this, it can be
useful to know how limits are constructed in the category of operator spaces,
so we show how this can be achieved for products and equalisers from which all
other limits can be built in a canonical way.

Definition 4.9
.

We write
\mathbf{OS}
for the (locally small) category whose objects are the
operator spaces and whose morphisms are the linear complete contractions
between them.

It is easy to see that
f\colon X\to Y
in
\mathbf{OS}
is an isomorphism in the
categorical sense iff
f
is a completely isometric isomorphism of operator
spaces. The category
\mathbf{OS}
has a zero object, written
0
, i.e. an object which
is both initial and terminal, which is given by the zero-dimensional operator
space. The constant-zero map
0_{X,Y}\colon X\to Y
coincides precisely with
the concept of a zero morphism in
\mathbf{OS}
, i.e. the unique morphism from
X
to

Y
which factorises through the zero object
0
. We sometimes simply write
0

instead of
0_{X,Y}
as this usually does not lead to confusion.

Remark 4.10
.

The adjunctions (
2
) from Subsection

4.3
show that the forgetful functor
U\colon\mathbf{OS}\to\mathbf{Ban}

is both a left and a right adjoint, so it preserves limits and colimits.
Therefore limits and colimits in
\mathbf{OS}
have to be constructed in a compatible
way to those in
\mathbf{Ban}.

Before we may describe the construction of categorical products,
recall that, given an indexed family
(X_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
of Banach
spaces, their
\ell^{\infty}
-direct sum is the Banach space

\bigoplus^{\infty}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}X_{\lambda}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{{\scriptsize def}}}}{{=}}\left\{(x_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\in\prod_{\lambda\in\Lambda}X_{\lambda}\ |\ \sup_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\left\lVert x_{\lambda}\right\rVert<\infty\right\}

equipped with the
\ell^{\infty}
-norm given by
\left\lVert(x_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\right\rVert_{\infty}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{{\scriptsize def}}}}{{=}}\sup_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\left\lVert x_{\lambda}\right\rVert
.

Proposition 4.11
.

Let
(X_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
be an indexed family of operator spaces. Then
\bigoplus^{\infty}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}X_{\lambda}
can be equipped with an OSS such that

(a)

M_{n}\left(\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Lambda}^{\infty}X_{\lambda}\right)=\bigoplus^{\infty}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}M_{n}(X_{\lambda})
.

(b)

for each
\kappa\in\Lambda
, the canonical inclusion
\iota_{\kappa}:X_{\kappa}\to\bigoplus^{\infty}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}X_{\lambda}
is a complete isometry;

(c)

for each
\kappa\in\Lambda
, the canonical projection
\pi_{\kappa}\colon\bigoplus^{\infty}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}X_{\lambda}\to X_{\kappa}
is a complete quotient map;

(d)

the operator space
\bigoplus^{\infty}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}X_{\lambda}
together with the projections
\pi_{\lambda}
constitute the categorical product of the family
(X_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}.

Proof.

This follows immediately from
[
4
, (1.2.17)]
. In slightly more detail,
property (a) can be seen as the definition of the OSS and (b) and (c) are claimed
in
[
4
, (1.2.17)]
. For property (d), if we are given an
operator space
Z
together with complete contractions
f_{\lambda}\colon Z\to X_{\lambda}
for every
\lambda\in\Lambda
,
the function
f\colon Z\to\bigoplus^{\infty}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}X_{\lambda}
defined by

f(z)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{{\scriptsize def}}}}{{=}}(f_{\lambda}(z))_{\lambda\in\Lambda}

is a complete contraction
[
4
, (1.2.17)]

and it is obvious that this is the unique function with the property that

\pi_{\lambda}\circ f=f_{\lambda}
for each
\lambda\in\Lambda.

∎

For the coproduct construction, we first need to recall the definition of
potentially uncountable sums in a Banach space.
Let
\Lambda
be a (potentially uncountable) index set. We say that the sum of an
indexed family
(x_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
of vectors in a Banach space
X

converges
if there is
x\in X
such that the net
\left\{\sum_{\lambda\in F}x_{\lambda}:F\subseteq\Lambda\text{ finite}\right\}
converges to
x
,
in which case we write
\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}x_{\lambda}=x
.
The convergence of the net to
x
implies that for each

\epsilon>0
there is a finite subset
F\subseteq\Lambda
such that for each
finite subset
F\subseteq G\subseteq\Lambda,
we have

\left\|\sum_{\lambda\in G}x_{\lambda}-x\right\|<\epsilon
. We also recall that a sufficient
condition for
\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}x_{\lambda}
to exist is that
\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\left\lVert x_{\lambda}\right\rVert<\infty
and in this case

\left\lVert\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}x_{\lambda}\right\rVert\leq\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\left\lVert x_{\lambda}\right\rVert.

Recall that, given an indexed family
(X_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
of Banach spaces,
their
\ell^{1}
-direct sum is the Banach space

\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Lambda}^{1}X_{\lambda}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{{\scriptsize def}}}}{{=}}\left\{(x_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\in\prod_{\lambda\in\Lambda}X_{\lambda}\ |\ \sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\left\lVert x_{\lambda}\right\rVert<\infty\right\}

equipped with the
\ell^{1}
-norm given by

\left\lVert(x_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\right\rVert_{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{{\scriptsize def}}}}{{=}}\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\left\lVert x_{\lambda}\right\rVert
.

Proposition 4.12
.

Let
(X_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
be an indexed family of operator spaces. Then
\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Lambda}^{1}X_{\lambda}
can be equipped with an OSS such that

(a)

there exists a complete isometric isomorphism of dual operator spaces
\left(\bigoplus^{1}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}X_{\lambda}\right)^{*}\cong\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Lambda}^{\infty}X_{\lambda}^{*}
;

(b)

for each
\kappa\in\Lambda
, the canonical inclusion
\iota_{\kappa}:X_{\kappa}\to\bigoplus^{1}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}X_{\lambda}
is a complete isometry;

(c)

for each
\kappa\in\Lambda
, the canonical projection
\pi_{\kappa}:\bigoplus^{1}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}X_{\lambda}\to X_{\kappa}
is a complete quotient map;

(d)

for each operator space
Y
, there is a complete isometric isomorphism
\textit{CB}\left(\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Lambda}^{1}X_{\lambda},Y\right)\cong\bigoplus^{\infty}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\textit{CB}(X_{\lambda},Y).

(e)

the operator space
\bigoplus^{1}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}X_{\lambda}
together with the inclusions
\iota_{\lambda}
constitute the categorical coproduct of the family
(X_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}.

Proof.

This follows easily from
[
4
, 1.4.13]
, but we spell out the
proof in more detail. Property (a) determines the OSS on

\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Lambda}^{1}X_{\lambda}
by using the predual isomorphism. Properties (b), (c) and (d) are also claimed there, so this leaves us with (e) which follows from (d) using some simple arguments.

If
Z
is another operator space and
u_{\lambda}\colon X_{\lambda}\to Z
, for
\lambda\in\Lambda
, are a family of complete contractions, we have to show that there exists
a
unique
complete contraction
u\colon\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Lambda}^{1}X_{\lambda}\to Z
with the property that
u\circ\iota_{\lambda}=u_{\lambda}.

The existence of a complete contraction with the required property is shown in
[
4
, 1.4.13]
, but uniqueness has not been claimed. To finish the proof, it suffices to show the following (stronger) claim:
there exists a unique (not necessarily complete) contraction
u\colon\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Lambda}^{1}X_{\lambda}\to Z
with the property that
u\circ\iota_{\lambda}=u_{\lambda}.

But this is true, because
\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Lambda}^{1}X_{\lambda}
is exactly the construction of the coproduct in
\mathbf{Ban}
.

The preceding paragraph finished the proof, but nevertheless, let us recall the construction of the unique couniversal map (in
\mathbf{Ban}
).
Let
X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{{\scriptsize def}}}}{{=}}\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Lambda}^{1}X_{\lambda}
and let
x\in X.

By construction of
X
, we have
x=(x_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
for
x_{\lambda}\in X_{\lambda}
,
\lambda\in\Lambda
, and
\pi_{\lambda}(x)=x_{\lambda}
.
By definition of the norm on
X
, we have
\|x\|_{1}=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\|x_{\lambda}\|
.
Let
\epsilon>0
.
Then there is a finite
F\subseteq\Lambda
such that for each finite
G\subseteq\Lambda
with
F\subseteq G
, we have
\left|\sum_{\lambda\in G}\|x_{\lambda}\|-\|x\|_{1}\right|<\epsilon
.
Therefore,

\left|\sum_{\lambda\in G}\|x_{\lambda}\|-\|x\|_{1}\right|=\left|-1\cdot\sum_{\lambda\in(\Lambda-G)}\|x_{\lambda}\|\right|=\sum_{\lambda\in(\Lambda-G)}\|x_{\lambda}\|<\epsilon.

which shows that

Therefore, the unique bounded map with the desired property is given

To see that the right sums exists (and therefore

\sum_{\lambda}\left\lVert u_{\lambda}(x_{\lambda})\right\rVert\leq\sum_{\lambda}\left\lVert x_{\lambda}\right\rVert=\left\lVert x\right\rVert<\infty

converges. To see that

∎

The following proposition captures all of the categorical structure and
properties of the operator space projective tensor product that we need. We
believe that it might be folklore knowledge and the monoidal closure of

Proposition 5.4
.

The category
\mathbf{OS}
has the structure of a symmetric monoidal closed category
with respect to the operator space projective tensor product. More precisely:

•

The monoidal product of operator spaces
X
and
Y

X\mathbin{\hat{\otimes}}Y

•

The monoidal unit is given by the complex numbers
\mathbb{C}
;

•

The internal-hom between operator spaces
X
and
Y

\textit{CB}(X,Y)

•

The left unitor
\lambda_{X}\colon\mathbb{C}\mathbin{\hat{\otimes}}X\xrightarrow{\cong}X
is the completely isometric
isomorphism uniquely determined by the assignment
a\otimes x\mapsto ax

•

The right unitor
\rho_{X}\colon X\mathbin{\hat{\otimes}}\mathbb{C}\xrightarrow{\cong}X

x\otimes a\mapsto ax

\alpha_{X,Y,Z}\colon(X\mathbin{\hat{\otimes}}Y)\mathbin{\hat{\otimes}}Z\xrightarrow{\cong}X\mathbin{\hat{\otimes}}(Y\mathbin{\hat{\otimes}}Z)

(x\otimes y)\otimes z\mapsto x\otimes(y\otimes z)

Proof.

(Sketch).
The construction of many of these isomorphisms is already outlined in

[
4

or
[

6
Conclusion

We showed that the category
\mathbf{OS}
is locally countably presentable and we
characterised the countably-presentable objects as exactly the separable
operator spaces. In the process, we also showed that the separable Banach
spaces coincide with the countably-presentable objects in
\mathbf{Ban}
. Our main
results are not surprising, but they do require some lengthy technical effort
related to the construction of countably-directed colimits. We hope that the
result related to cofree (cocommutative) coalgebras can illustrate the utility
of categorical methods when studying operator spaces. The right adjoints,
G

and
R
, are shown to exist via the adjoint functor theorem, which relies on
the axiom of choice, and we think that proving the existence of these cofree
(cocommutative) coalgebras can be difficult to achieve with more traditional
methods from functional analysis and operator space theory. By using methods
from category theory, we were able to prove their existence by relying on
already established categorical results.

On the W W -entropy and Shannon entropy power on RCD ( K , N ) (K,N) and RCD ( K , n , N ) (K,n,N) spaces
Xiang-Dong Li, Enrui Zhang

Abstract
In this paper, we prove the W W -entropy formula and the monotonicity and rigidity theorem of the W W -entropy for the heat flow on RCD ( K , N ) (K,N) and RCD ( K , n , N ) (K,n,N) spaces ( X , d , μ ) (X,d,\mu) , where K ∈ ℝ K\in\mathbb{R} , n ∈ ℕ n\in\mathbb{N} is the geometric dimension of ( X , d , μ ) (X,d,\mu) and N ≥ n N\geq n . We also prove the K K -concavity of the Shannon entropy power on RCD ( K , N ) (K,N) spaces. As an application, we derive the Shannon entropy isoperimetric inequality and the Stam type logarithmic Sobolev inequality on RCD ( 0 , N ) (0,N) spaces with maximal volume growth condition. Finally, we prove the rigidity theorem for the Stam type logarithmic Sobolev inequality with sharp constant on noncollapsing RCD ( 0 , N ) (0,N) spaces.

Introduction

In 2002, G. Perelman
[
48
]
introduced the
\mathcal{W}
-entropy for the Ricci flow and proved its monotonicity along the conjugate heat equation. More precisely, let
(M,g)
be a closed
n
-dimensional Riemannian
manifold,
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
. The
\mathcal{W}
-entropy functional is defined by

\mathcal{W}(g(t),f(t),\tau):=\int_{M}\Big[\tau(R+|\nabla f|^{2})+f-n\Big]\frac{e^{-f}}{(4\pi\tau)^{\frac{n}{2}}}dv,

where
\tau>0
satisfies
\frac{d\tau}{dt}=-1
,
R
and
dv
denote the scalar curvature and the Riemannian
volume form, respectively. Perelman
[
48

showed that if
g(t)
and

satisfy following equations

\begin{split}\partial_{t}g&=-2Ric,\\
\partial_{t}f&=-\Delta f+|\nabla f|^{2}-R+\frac{n}{2\tau},\end{split}

then

\frac{d\mathcal{W}}{dt}=2\tau\int_{M}\Big\|Ric+\nabla^{2}f-\frac{g}{2\tau}\Big\|^{2}_{\operatorname{HS}}\frac{e^{-f}}{(4\pi\tau)^{\frac{n}{2}}}dv\geq 0.

Here
\|\cdot\|_{\rm HS}
denotes the Hilbert-Schmidt norm.
In particular,
\frac{d\mathcal{W}}{dt}=0
at some
t>0
if and only if
(M,g(t),f(t))
is a shrinking Ricci soliton

Ric+\nabla^{2}f=\frac{g}{2\tau}.

Inspired by Perelman’s groundbreaking contributions to the study of

(1.1)

\frac{d}{dt}W(f,t)=-2t\int_{M}\left(\Big\|\nabla^{2}f-\frac{g}{2t}\Big\|_{\operatorname{HS}}^{2}+{\rm Ric}(\nabla f,\nabla f)\right)udv.

In particular, the

-entropy is non-increasing when
{\rm Ric}\geq 0

Definitions and basic facts of RCD spaces

In this section, we briefly recall some definitions and basic facts of RCD spaces.

Let
(X,d,\mu)
be a metric measure space, which means that
(X,d)
is
a complete and separable metric space and
\mu
is a locally finite measure.
Locally finite means that for all
x\in X
, there is
r>0
such that

\mu\left(B_{r}(x)\right)<\infty
and
\mu
is a
\sigma
-finite Borel measure on
X
, where
B_{r}(x)=\{y\in X,d(x,y)<r\}
.

Let
P_{2}(X,d)
be the
L^{2}
-Wasserstein space over
(X,d)
, i.e. the set of all Borel probability measures
\mu
satisfying

\int_{X}d\left(x_{0},x\right)^{2}\mu(\mathrm{d}x)<\infty,

where
x_{0}\in X
is a (and hence any) fixed point in
M
. The
L^{2}
-Wasserstein distance between
\mu_{0},\mu_{1}\in P_{2}(X,d)
is defined by

W_{2}\left(\mu_{0},\mu_{1}\right)^{2}:=\inf_{\pi\in\Pi}\int_{X\times X}d(x,y)^{2}\mathrm{~d}\pi(x,y),

where
\Pi
is the set of coupling measures
\pi
of
\mu_{0}
and
\mu_{1}
on
X\times X
, i.e.,
\Pi=\{\pi\in P(X\times X),\pi(\cdot,X)=\mu_{0},\pi(X,\cdot)=\mu_{1}\}
, where
P(X\times X)
is the set of probability measures on
X\times X
.

Fix a reference measure
\mu
on
(X,d)

P_{2}(X,d,\mu)
be the subspace of all absolutely continuous measures with respect to the measure
\mu

\nu\in P_{2}(X,d)

\mu

\operatorname{Ent}(\nu):=\int_{X}\rho\log\rho\mathrm{d}\mu,

\mu

\mu

The Fisher information is defined by

I(\nu):=\begin{cases}\int_{X}\frac{|D\rho|^{2}}{\rho}\mathrm{d}\mu&\text{ if }\nu=\rho\mu,\\
\infty&\text{ otherwise. }\end{cases}

. We have

\begin{split}\int_{X}\Delta e^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(u^{\delta})u^{\delta}d\mu&=\int_{X}e^{\prime\prime}_{\varepsilon}(u^{\delta})\Delta u^{\delta}ud\mu+\int_{X}e^{\prime\prime\prime}_{\varepsilon}(u^{\delta})|\nabla u^{\delta}|_{w}^{2}ud\mu\\
&=\int_{X}\frac{u^{\delta}}{u^{\delta}+\varepsilon}\Delta u^{\delta}d\mu-\int_{X}\frac{1}{(u^{\delta}+\varepsilon)^{2}}|\nabla u^{\delta}|_{w}^{2}u^{\delta}d\mu\\
&=\int_{X}\frac{u^{\delta}}{u^{\delta}+\varepsilon}\Delta u^{\delta}d\mu-\int_{X}u^{\delta}|\nabla e^{\prime}_{\epsilon}(u^{\delta})|_{w}^{2}d\mu.\end{split}

Taking
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
and then
\delta\rightarrow 0
, we complete the proof.
∎

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
(X,d,\mu)
be an
RCD
(K,N)

u
be a solution to the heat equation
\partial_{t}u=\Delta u
. Let

be the Shannon entropy.
Then the Entropy Differential Inequality (EDI

\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}H(u)+\frac{2}{N}\Big(\frac{d}{dt}H(u)\Big)^{2}+2K\frac{d}{dt}H(u)\leq 0,

(4.7)

Proof.

Substituting (

) and (
3.7
) into (

\begin{split}&\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}H(u)+\frac{2}{N}\Big(\frac{d}{dt}H(u)\Big)^{2}+2K\frac{d}{dt}H(u)\\

Remark 4.4

6
Shannon entropy power on RCD spaces

In his seminal work on information theory
[
50
]
in 1948, Shannon established the channel coding theorem through the introduction of entropy power. Since then, entropy power has been a fundamental topic of research in information theory. Shannon was the first one to propose and prove the entropy power inequality for discrete random variables
[
50
]
and also introduced the concept of entropy power for continuous random vectors.

More precisely, let
X
be an random vector on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
whose the probability distribution is
f(x)dx
, and

H(X):=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)\log f(x)dx,

be the Shannon entropy of the law of
X
. The Shannon entropy power of
X
is defined as follows

\mathcal{N}(X):=e^{\frac{2}{n}H(X)}.

Then the Shannon entropy power inequality states that, for two independent random vectors
X
and
Y
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the following inequality holds:

\mathcal{N}(X+Y)\geq\mathcal{N}(X)+\mathcal{N}(Y),

(6.1)

with equality if and only if the covariance matrix of
X
is proportional to the covariance matrix of
Y
. For more
rigorous proof of the Shannon entropy power inequality, see
Stam
[
51
]
and Blachman
[
5
]
. See also
[
16
]
.

By Dembo
[
17
]
and Dembo-Cover-Thomas
[
18
]
, the Shannon-Stam Entropy Power Inequality
(\ref{NXY1})
is equivalent to the following inequalities for
H
and for
\mathcal{N}
:
for any
\lambda\in(0,1)
, it holds

\displaystyle H(\sqrt{\lambda}X+\sqrt{1-\lambda}Y)\leq\lambda H(X)+(1-\lambda)H(Y),

(6.2)

or

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}(\sqrt{\lambda}X+\sqrt{1-\lambda}Y)\geq\lambda\mathcal{N}(X)+(1-\lambda)\mathcal{N}(Y).

(6.3)

The Shannon-Stam Entropy Power Inequality
(\ref{NXY1})
is an analogue of the Brunn-Minkowski isoperimetric inequality in convex geometry: for any

A,B\in\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, letting
A+B:=\{x+y:x\in A,y\in B\}
, then

\displaystyle V^{1/n}(A+B)\geq V^{1/n}(A)+V^{1/n}(B),

where
V(A)
,
V(B)
and
V(A+B)
denote the volume of the sets
A
,
B
and
A+B
respectively. Indeed, EPI
(\ref{NXY1})
is equivalent to

\displaystyle e^{2H(X+Y)\over n}\geq e^{2H(X)\over n}+e^{2H(Y)\over n}.

For a unified proof of the Shannon-Stam Entropy Power Inequality and the Brunn-Minkowski isoperimetric inequality, see Dembo et al.
[
18
]
. See also
[
16
]
.

In 1985, Costa
[
15
]
proved the concavity of the Shannon entropy power along the
heat equation on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. More precisely, let
u(x,t)
be a solution to
the heat equation on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, i.e.,

\partial_{t}u=\Delta u,\quad u(x,0)=f(x).

Let
H(u(t))=-\int_{M}u\log udx
be the Shannon entropy associated to the heat distribution
u(x,t)dx
at time
t
and
\mathcal{N}(u(t))=e^{\frac{2}{n}H(u(t))}
be the Shannon entropy power. Then

\frac{d^{2}\mathcal{N}(u(t))}{dt^{2}}\leq 0.

(6.4)

Using an argument based on the Blachman-Stam inequality
[
5
]
, the original proof of the Entropy Power Concave Inequality (EPCI)
(\ref{Entropy power})
has been simplified by Dembo et al.
[
17
,
18
]
and Villani
[
54
]
.
In
[
54
]
, Villani pointed out the possibility of extending the Entropy Power Concave Inequality (EPCI)
(\ref{Entropy power})
to Riemannian manifolds with non-negative Ricci curvature using the
\Gamma_{2}
-calculation. In
[
53
]
, Savaré and Toscani proved
that the Rényi entropy power is also concave along the porous media equation and the fast diffusion equation, given by
\partial_{t}u=\Delta u^{p}
with
p>1-\frac{2}{n}
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

In a very recent paper
[
39
]
, Li and Li derived the corresponding Entropy Differential Inequality (
\operatorname{EDI}
) and proved the
K
-concavity of the entropy power for the heat equation associated with the Witten Laplacian on complete Riemannian manifolds with CD
(K,m)
-condition.
They also discovered the NIW formula, which links the
W
-entropy to entropy power
N
,the Fisher information
I
and the Perelman
W
-entropy for the heat equation associated with the Witten Laplacian on complete Riemannian manifolds with CD
(K,m)
-condition. One of the important feature is that one can use the NIW formula to provide a second proof of the concavity of the Shannon entropy power. As an application, they established the corresponding entropy isoperimetric inequality on Riemannian manifolds. Furthermore, Li and Li
[
38
]
extended these results to
Rényi entropy, proving analogous formulas for the porous media equation and fast diffussion equation associated with the Witten Laplacian on complete Riemannian manifolds with CD
(K,m)
-condition.

The purpose of this paper is to extend Li-Li’s
K
-concavity of the Shannon entropy power from complete Riemannian manifolds with CD
(K,m)
-condition to metric measure spaces satisfying the Riemannian curvature-dimension. condition RCD
(0,N)
. Let us mention that Erbar, Kuwada and Sturm
[
19
]

have proved the concavity of the Shannon entropy power for the heat semigroup on metric measure spaces satisfying the Riemannian curvature-dimension condition RCD
(0,N)
. Our main result can be also regarded as a natural extension of Ebar-Kuwada-Sturm’s result from RCD
(0,N)
spaces to RCD
(K,N)
spaces.

The following NIW formula was essentially proved in Li-Li
[
39
]
on Riemannian manifolds and can be easily extended to RCD spaces. It indicats an interesting relationship between the
W
-entropy
W
, the Fisher information
I
and the Shannon entropy power
N
.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
\mathcal{N}(u)=e^{2H(u)\over N}
be the Shannon entropy power associated with the linear heat equation
\partial_{t}u=\Delta u

on RCD
(K,N)
space. Then the following NIW formulas hold on metric measure spaces

\frac{d^{2}\mathcal{N}}{dt^{2}}=\frac{2\mathcal{N}}{N}\Big[\frac{2}{N}\Big(I-\frac{N(1-Kt)}{2t}\Big)^{2}+\frac{1}{t}\frac{dW_{N,K}}{dt}\Big].

In particular, on RCD
(0,N)
space, we have

\frac{d^{2}\mathcal{N}}{dt^{2}}=\frac{2\mathcal{N}}{N}\Big[\frac{2}{N}\Big(I-\frac{N}{2t}\Big)^{2}+\frac{1}{t}\frac{dW_{N}}{dt}\Big].

Proof.

The proof follows the same argument as in Li-Li
[
39
]
.
∎

We now state the main result of this section.

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
\mathcal{N}(u)=e^{2H(u)\over N}
be the Shannon entropy power associated with the linear heat equation
\partial_{t}u=\Delta u
on an RCD
(K,N)
space. Then

\frac{d^{2}\mathcal{N}}{dt^{2}}+2K\frac{d\mathcal{N}}{dt}\leq 0.

Proof.

Note that

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2H^{\prime}}{N}\mathcal{N},

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2H^{\prime\prime}}{N}\mathcal{N}+\frac{4H^{\prime 2}}{N^{2}}\mathcal{N}=\frac{2\mathcal{N}}{N}\Big(H^{\prime\prime}+\frac{2H^{\prime 2}}{N}\Big).

Using
\operatorname{EDI}(K,N)
in (
4.7
), we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}^{\prime\prime}\leq-\frac{4K}{N}\mathcal{N}H^{\prime}=-2K\mathcal{N}^{\prime}.

∎

We can now use the NIW formula to provide an alternative proof of Theorem
6.2
.

Theorem 6.3
.

Let
(X,d,\mu)
be an
RCD
(K,N)
space. Then

\begin{split}\frac{N}{2\mathcal{N}}\frac{d^{2}\mathcal{N}}{dt^{2}}\leq&-2K\int_{X}|\nabla\log u|_{w}^{2}d\mu-\frac{2}{N}\int_{X}\Big[\Delta\log u-\int_{X}u\Delta\log ud\mu\Big]^{2}ud\mu.\end{split}

As a consequence, we have

\displaystyle\frac{N}{2\mathcal{N}}\Big[\frac{d^{2}\mathcal{N}}{dt^{2}}+2K\frac{d\mathcal{N}}{dt}\Big]\leq-\frac{2}{N}\int_{X}\Big[\Delta\log u-\int_{X}u\Delta\log ud\mu\Big]^{2}ud\mu.

In particular, on
RCD
(K,N)
space, we have

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}\mathcal{N}}{dt^{2}}+2K\frac{d\mathcal{N}}{dt}\leq 0,

and the left hand side vanishes if and only if

\Delta\log u=\int_{X}u\Delta\log ud\mu.

Proof.

Note that

\displaystyle H_{N,K}(u)

\displaystyle=H_{N}+\frac{N}{2}Kt\Big(1-\frac{1}{6}Kt\Big),

and

\displaystyle W_{N,K}(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{dt}(tH_{N,K})

\displaystyle=H_{N,K}+t\frac{d}{dt}H_{N,K}

\displaystyle=H_{N}+\frac{N}{2}Kt\Big(1-\frac{1}{6}Kt\Big)+t\frac{d}{dt}H_{N}+t\frac{d}{dt}\left[\frac{N}{2}Kt\Big(1-\frac{1}{6}Kt\Big)\right]

\displaystyle=H_{N}+{1\over t}\frac{d}{dt}H_{N}+NKt-\frac{1}{4}NK^{2}t^{2}.

This yields

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}W_{N,K}(u)=\frac{d}{dt}W_{N}(u)+NK-\frac{1}{2}NK^{2}t,

and

\displaystyle\frac{1}{t}\frac{d}{dt}W_{N,K}(u)=\frac{1}{t}\frac{d}{dt}W_{N}+\frac{NK}{t}-\frac{1}{2}NK^{2}.

By the entropy dissipation formulas, we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}W_{N}(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle tH^{\prime\prime}+2H^{\prime}-\frac{N}{2t}

(6.5)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2t\int_{X}{\bf\Gamma}_{2}(\nabla\log u,\nabla\log u)ud\mu+2\int_{X}\|\nabla\log u\|^{2}_{w}ud\mu-\frac{N}{2t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2t\int_{X}\Big[{\bf\Gamma}_{2}(\nabla\log u,\nabla\log u)+\frac{\Delta\log u}{t}+\frac{N}{4t^{2}}\Big]ud\mu.

When
X=M
is a Riemannian manifold, the Bochner identity formula implies

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}W_{N}(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2t\int_{X}\Big[\|\nabla^{2}\log u\|^{2}_{\operatorname{HS}}+{\bf Ric}(\nabla\log u,\nabla\log u)+\frac{\Delta\log u}{t}+\frac{N}{4t^{2}}\Big]ud\mu

(6.6)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2t\int_{X}\Big[\|\nabla^{2}\log u+\frac{1}{2t}\|^{2}_{\operatorname{HS}}+{\bf Ric}(\nabla\log u,\nabla\log u)\Big]ud\mu.

This is the
W
-entropy formula when
K=0
due to Ni
[
46
]
and Li
[
41
]
. Moreover, when
K\neq 0
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}W_{N,K}(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}W_{N}+NK-\frac{1}{2}NK^{2}t

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2t\int_{X}\Big[\|\nabla^{2}\log u+\frac{1}{2t}\|^{2}_{\operatorname{HS}}+{\bf Ric}(\nabla\log u,\nabla\log u)\Big]ud\mu+NK-\frac{1}{2}NK^{2}t

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2t\int_{X}\Big[\|\nabla^{2}\log u+\frac{1}{2t}\|^{2}_{\operatorname{HS}}-{NK\over 2t}+{NK^{2}\over 4}+{\bf Ric}(\nabla\log u,\nabla\log u)\Big]ud\mu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2t\int_{X}\Big[\|\nabla^{2}\log u+\frac{1}{2t}-{K\over 2}\|^{2}_{\operatorname{HS}}+K\Delta\log u+{\bf Ric}(\nabla\log u,\nabla\log u)\Big]ud\mu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2t\int_{X}\Big[\|\nabla^{2}\log u+\frac{1}{2t}-{K\over 2}\|^{2}_{\operatorname{HS}}+({\bf Ric}-K)(\nabla\log u,\nabla\log u)\Big]ud\mu.

This is the
W
-entropy formula when
K\neq 0
due to Li-Xu
[
44
]
and Li-Li
[
34
,
35
]
.

Next, the NIW formula implies

\begin{split}\frac{N}{2\mathcal{N}}\frac{d^{2}\mathcal{N}}{dt^{2}}&=\frac{2}{N}\Big(I-\frac{N}{2t}\Big)^{2}+\frac{1}{t}\frac{dW_{N}}{dt}\\
&=\frac{2}{N}\Big(I-\frac{N}{2t}\Big)^{2}-2\int_{X}\Big[\|\nabla^{2}\log u+\frac{1}{2t}\|^{2}_{\operatorname{HS}}+{\bf Ric}(\nabla\log u,\nabla\log u\Big]ud\mu\\
&=\frac{2}{N}I^{2}-2\int_{X}\Big[\|\nabla^{2}\log u\|^{2}_{\operatorname{HS}}+{\bf Ric}(\nabla\log u,\nabla\log u)\Big]ud\mu\\
&=\frac{2}{N}\Big(\int_{X}u\Delta\log ud\mu\Big)^{2}-2\int_{X}\Big[{\bf\Gamma}_{2}(\nabla\log u,\nabla\log u)\Big]ud\mu.\end{split}

This yields, if the CD
(0,N)
-condition holds, i.e., the Bochner inequality formula holds

\displaystyle{\bf\Gamma}_{2}(\nabla\log u,\nabla\log u)\geq{1\over N}|\Delta\log u|^{2},\ \ \

(6.7)

we have

\displaystyle\frac{N}{2\mathcal{N}}\frac{d^{2}\mathcal{N}}{dt^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{N}\Big(\int_{X}u\Delta\log ud\mu\Big)^{2}-\frac{2}{N}\int_{X}(\Delta\log u)^{2}ud\mu

\displaystyle=-\frac{2}{N}\int_{X}\Big[\Delta\log u-\int_{X}u\Delta\log ud\mu\Big]^{2}ud\mu.

In general, for
K\neq 0
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{N}{2\mathcal{N}}\Big[\frac{d^{2}\mathcal{N}}{dt^{2}}+2K\frac{d\mathcal{N}}{dt}\Big]

\displaystyle=\frac{N}{2\mathcal{N}}\frac{d^{2}\mathcal{N}}{dt^{2}}+{NK\over\mathcal{N}}\frac{d\mathcal{N}}{dt}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{N}\Big(\int_{X}u\Delta\log ud\mu\Big)^{2}-2\int_{X}\Big[{\bf\Gamma}_{2}(\nabla\log u,\nabla\log u)-K|\nabla\log u|_{w}^{2}\Big]ud\mu

This yields, if the CD
(K,N)
-condition holds, i.e., the Bochner inequality formula holds

\displaystyle{\bf\Gamma}_{2}(\nabla\log u,\nabla\log u)\geq{1\over N}|\Delta\log u|^{2}+K|\nabla\log u|^{2}_{w},\ \ \

(6.8)

we have

\displaystyle\frac{N}{2\mathcal{N}}\Big[\frac{d^{2}\mathcal{N}}{dt^{2}}+2K\frac{d\mathcal{N}}{dt}\Big]\leq-\frac{2}{N}\int_{X}\Big[\Delta\log u-\int_{X}u\Delta\log ud\mu\Big]^{2}ud\mu.

∎

7
Shannon entropy isoperimetric inequality on RCD
(0,N)
spaces

In this section, as an application of the concavity of the Shannon entropy power,
we prove the Shannon entropy isoperimetric inequality and the Stam logarithmic Sobolev inequality on RCD
(0,N)
spaces with maximal volume condition, which extend the previous results due to Li-Li
[
39
]
on complete Riemannian manifold with CD
(0,N)
condition and maximal volume condition.

Theorem 7.1
.

Let
(X,d,\mu)
be an
RCD
(0,N)
space satisfying the maximal volume growth condition: there exists a constant
C_{N}>0
such that

\mu(B(x,r))\geq C_{N}r^{N},\quad\forall x\in X,\ r>0.

Then the Shannon entropy isoperimetric inequality holds

I(f)\mathcal{N}(f)\geq\gamma_{N}:=2\pi Ne\kappa^{\frac{2}{N}},

(7.1)

where

\kappa:=\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\mu(B(x,r))}{\omega_{N}r^{N}}

is the asymptotic volume ratio on
(X,d,\mu)
, and
\omega_{N}
denotes the volume of the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Equivalently, under the above condition, the Stam logarithmic Sobolev inequality holds: for all nice
f>0
with
\int_{X}{|\nabla f|^{2}_{w}\over f}d\mu<+\infty
, it holds

\int_{X}f\log fd\mu\leq\frac{N}{2}\log\Big(\frac{1}{\gamma_{N}}\int_{X}{|\nabla f|^{2}_{w}\over f}d\mu\Big).

Proof.

The proof has been essentially given by Li-Li
[
39
]
. For the convenience of the reader, we reproduce
it here. We know that, by Theorem
3.12
or Theorem
6.3
, on RCD
(0,N)
spaces, the concavity of entropy power holds:

\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}\mathcal{N}(u(t))\leq 0.

This means that

\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{N}(u(t))

is non-increasing in
t
and hence
\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{N}(u(t))
exists.

The first order entropy dissipation formula (
3.4
) implies

\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{N}(u(t))\leq\frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}\mathcal{N}(u(t))=\frac{2}{N}I(f)\mathcal{N}(f).

Thus

\frac{2}{n}I(f)\mathcal{N}(f)\geq\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{N}(u(t)).

(7.2)

Again, the first order entropy dissipation formula (
3.4
) implies

\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{N}(u(t))=\frac{2}{N}I(u(t))\mathcal{N}(u(t))\geq 0.

(7.3)

Thus
\mathcal{N}(u(t))
is non-increasing in t. By the L’Hospital rule, we have

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{N}(u(t))=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\mathcal{N}(u(t))}{t}.

Let

H_{N}(u(t))=H(u(t))-\frac{N}{2}\log(4\pi et).

Then

\frac{\mathcal{N}(u(t))}{t}=(4\pi e)e^{\frac{2}{N}H_{N}(u(t))}.

By
[
31
]
, we know that

\frac{d}{dt}H_{N}(u(t))\leq 0.

Thus the limit
\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow\infty}H_{N}(u(t))
exists and we need only to prove
\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow\infty}H_{N}(u(t))
is finite.

Let
P_{t}=e^{t\Delta}
, and
f,h
such that
\int_{X}fd\mu=\int_{X}hd\mu=1
. Let
H\left(P_{t}f\right)=

-\int_{M}P_{t}f\log P_{t}fdv
, and
H\left(P_{t}h\right)=-\int_{X}P_{t}h\log P_{t}hd\mu
.

By Theorem 3.28
[
19
]
, the CD
(K,N)
condition means that

\displaystyle\frac{U_{N}\left(\mu_{1}\right)}{U_{N}\left(\mu_{0}\right)}\leq\mathfrak{c}_{K/N}\left(W_{2}\left(\mu_{0},\mu_{1}\right)\right)+\frac{1}{N}\mathfrak{s}_{K/N}\left(W_{2}\left(\mu_{0},\mu_{1}\right)\right)\sqrt{I\left(\mu_{0}\right)}.

For the defenition of
\mathfrak{c}_{K/N}
and
\mathfrak{s}_{K/N}
, one can see Definition
2.1
.

In particular, when
K=0
, We have

\displaystyle e^{-\frac{1}{N}(H(\mu_{0})-H(\mu_{1}))}\leq 1+\frac{W_{2}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})\sqrt{I(\mu_{0})}}{N}.

That is to say

\displaystyle H(\mu_{1})-H(\mu_{0})\leq N\log\left(1+\frac{W_{2}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})\sqrt{I(\mu_{0})}}{N}\right).

Using the inequality
\log(1+x)\leq x,\forall x>0
, we have

\displaystyle H(\mu_{1})-H(\mu_{0})\leq\sqrt{I(\mu_{0})}W_{2}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1}).

Let
\mu_{0}=P_{t}f
and
\mu_{1}=P_{t}h
, we have

H(P_{t}f)-H(P_{t}h)\leq\sqrt{I(P_{t}h)}W_{2}(P_{t}fd\mu,P_{t}hd\mu).

(7.4)

Exchanging
P_{t}f
and
P_{t}h
in (
7.4
), we have

H(P_{t}h)-H(P_{t}f)\leq\sqrt{I(P_{t}f)}W_{2}(P_{t}fd\mu,P_{t}hd\mu).

(7.5)

We obtain following estimation by combining (
7.4
) and (
7.5
)

-\sqrt{I(P_{t}f)}W_{2}(P_{t}fd\mu,P_{t}hd\mu)\leq H(P_{t}f)-H(P_{t}h)\leq\sqrt{I(P_{t}h)}W_{2}(P_{t}fd\mu,P_{t}hd\mu)

Thus

\left|H\left(P_{t}f\right)-H\left(P_{t}h\right)\right|\leq\max\left\{\sqrt{I(P_{t}f)},\sqrt{I(P_{t}h)}\right\}W_{2}\left(P_{t}fd\mu,P_{t}hd\mu\right),

By Ebar, Kuwada and Sturm
[
19
]
, we have the following inequality on RCD
(0,N)
spaces

W_{2}\left(P_{t}fd\mu,P_{t}hd\mu\right)\leq W_{2}(fd\mu,hd\mu).

By Theorem
4.5
, on RCD
(0,N)
we have

I(P_{t}f)\leq\frac{N}{2t}.

Therefore

\left|H\left(P_{t}f\right)-H\left(P_{t}h\right)\right|\leq\sqrt{\frac{N}{2t}}W_{2}(fd\mu,hd\mu).

Taking an approximating sequence
h_{k}(x)d\mu(x)
to the Dirac mass
\delta_{o}(\cdot)
, we have

\left|H\left(P_{t}f\right)-H\left(p_{t}(o,\cdot)\right)\right|\leq\sqrt{\frac{N}{2t}}W_{2}\left(fd\mu,\delta_{o}(\cdot)\right).

Hence

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}(H(u(t))-H(p_{t}))=0.

On the other hand, H. Li
[
33
]
proved that on RCD
(0,N)

spaces it holds

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}H_{N}(p_{t})=\log\kappa.

Consequently, we obtain

I(f)\mathcal{N}(f)\geq\gamma_{N}:=2\pi Ne\kappa^{\frac{2}{N}},

which is equivalent to the Stam type Log-Sobolev inequality: for all
f>0
with
\int_{X}{|\nabla f|^{2}_{w}\over f}d\mu<+\infty
, it holds

\int_{X}f\log fd\mu\leq\frac{N}{2}\log\Big(\frac{1}{\gamma_{N}}\int_{X}{|\nabla f|^{2}_{w}\over f}d\mu\Big).

(7.6)

Replacing
f
by
f^{2}
with
\int_{M}f^{2}dv=1
and
\int_{X}|\nabla f|^{2}d\mu<\infty
, the above inequality is equivalent to the following Stam type Log-Sobolev inequality: for all nice
f\neq 0
with
\int_{X}|\nabla f|^{2}_{w}d\mu<+\infty
, it holds

\int_{X}f^{2}\log f^{2}d\mu\leq\frac{N}{2}\log\left(\frac{4}{\gamma_{N}}\int_{X}|\nabla f|^{2}d\mu\right).

∎

8
Rigidity theorem on non-collapsing RCD
(0,N)
space

In Remark 8.3 in Li-Li
[
39
]
, S. Li and Li pointed out the following

Remark 8.1
.

Note that when
M=\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
\kappa=1
, the Stam logarithmic Sobolev inequality reads

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}(f)I(f)\geq 2\pi eN.

(8.1)

Indeed, the Entropy Isoperimetric Inequality
(\ref{NXY5})
is equivalent to the following logarithmic Sobolev inequality which was first proved by
Stam
[
51
]
: for any smooth probability density function
f
on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}{|\nabla f|^{2}\over f}dx<\infty
, it holds

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f\log fdx\leq{N\over 2}\log\left({1\over 2\pi eN}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}{|\nabla f|^{2}\over f}dx\right).

(8.2)

Replacing
f
by
f^{2}
with
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f^{2}dx=1
, the above LSI is equivalent to the Stam logarithmic Sobolev inequality: for any smooth
f
on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f^{2}dx=1
and
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla f|^{2}dx<\infty
, it holds

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f^{2}\log f^{2}dx\leq{N\over 2}\log\left({2\over\pi eN}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla f|^{2}dx\right).

In the case where
M
is an
N
-dimensional complete Riemannian manifold with
non-negative Ricci curvature on which the Stam logarithmic Sobolev inequality
(\ref{stamLogS})

holds with the same constant
\gamma_{N}=2\pi eN
as on the
N
-dimensional Euclidean space, then
M
must be isometric to
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. See
[
7
,
46
]
.

In this section, inspired by the above remark, we will prove the rigidity theorem for the Stam logarithmic Sobolev inequality with the sharp constant on non-collapsing RCD
(0,N)
spaces.

We first recall the Bishop-Gromov inequality on metric measure spaces
[
52
]
. It says that, for
0<r<R
and
x\in X
,

\frac{\mu(B(x,R))}{\mu(B(x,r))}\leq\left(\frac{R}{r}\right)^{N}.

(8.3)

Moreover, Gigli and De Philippis
[
23
]
studies the case when equality holds in (
8.3
) . To state their result, we require the following definition:

Definition 8.2
.

[
23
]

A metric measure space
(X,d,\mu)
is a
(0,N-1)
-cone built over a metric measure space
\left(Y,d_{Y},\mu_{Y}\right)
if the following holds:

(i)
X=[0,\infty)\times Y/\{0\}\times Y
,

(ii)
d([r,y],[s,z])=r^{2}+s^{2}-2rs\cos\left(d_{Y}(y,z)\wedge\pi\right)
,

(iii)
\mu(\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{~d}y)=r^{N-1}\mathrm{~d}r\mu_{Y}(\mathrm{~d}y)
.

Theorem 8.3
.

[
23
]

Let
x\in X
. Suppose that the equality holds in (
8.3
) for any
0<r<R
.

(i) If
N\geq 2
, then
(X,d,\mu)
is
(0,N-1)
-cone over an
\operatorname{RCD}(N-2,N-1)
space and
x
is the vertex of the cone.

(ii) If
N<2
, then
(X,d,\mu)
is isomorphic to either
\left([0,\infty),d_{\text{Eucl }},x^{N-1}\mathrm{~d}x\right)
or
\left(\mathbb{R},d_{\mathrm{Eucl}},|x|^{N-1}\mathrm{~d}x\right)
, where
d_{\text{Eucl }}
is the canonical Euclidean distance.

In both cases,
x\in X
corresponds to 0 by the isomorphism.

We now state the last main result of this paper.

Theorem 8.4
.

Let
(X,d,\mu)
be a non-collapsing RCD
(0,N)
space and the Stam logarithmic Sobolev inequality (
8.2
)
holds with the sharp constant
\gamma_{N}=2\pi eN
as on the
N
-dimensional Euclidean space. Then

(i) If
N\geq 2
,
(X,d,\mu)
is
(0,N-1)
-cone over an
\operatorname{RCD}(N-2,N-1)
space and
x
is the vertex of the cone.

(ii) If
N<2
,
(X,d,\mu)
is isomorphic to either
\left([0,\infty),d_{\text{Eucl }},x^{N-1}\mathrm{~d}x\right)
or
\left(\mathbb{R},d_{\mathrm{Eucl}},|x|^{N-1}\mathrm{~d}x\right)
, where
d_{\text{Eucl }}
is the canonical Euclidean distance.

Proof.

By Theorem
7.1
, if the Stam logarithmic Sobolev inequality (
8.2
) holds with the sharp constant
\gamma_{N}=2\pi eN
, then

2\pi eN\kappa^{N/2}\geq 2\pi eN.

By Theorem 1 in
[
10
]
,
\gamma_{N}=2\pi eN\kappa^{\frac{N}{2}}
is the sharp constant such that the LSI hols. The logarithmic Sobolev inequality (
8.2
) holds with the same constant
\gamma_{N}=2\pi eN
means that

\frac{1}{2\pi eN\kappa^{\frac{N}{2}}}\leq\frac{1}{2\pi eN},

(8.4)

which is

\kappa^{\frac{N}{2}}=\lim_{R\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\mu(B(x,R))}{\omega_{N}R^{N}}\geq 1,

(8.5)

where
\omega_{N}
denotes the volume of the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

By the Bishop-Gromov inequality in
[
52
]
, for
r<R
,
we have

\frac{\mu(B(x,R))}{\mu(B(x,r))}\leq\left(\frac{R}{r}\right)^{N}.

Thus

\frac{\mu(B(x,R))}{\omega_{N}R^{N}}\leq\frac{\mu(B(x,r))}{\omega_{N}r^{N}}.

(8.6)

Under the non-collapsing condition on RCD
(0,N)
, we have

\lim\limits_{r\rightarrow 0}\frac{\mu(B(x,r))}{\omega_{N}r^{N}}=1,\ \ \forall~x\in X.

(8.7)

Hence

\mu(B(x,R))\leq\omega_{N}R^{N}\ \ \ \ \forall~x\in X,\ R>0.

(8.8)

On the other hand, taking the limit
R\rightarrow\infty
in
(\ref{BGIneq})
and using (
8.5
), we see that for any
r>0
, we have

1\leq\lim\limits_{R\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\mu(B(x,R))}{\omega_{N}R^{N}}\leq\frac{\mu(B(x,r))}{\omega_{N}r^{N}},\ \ \forall~x\in X.

(8.9)

Therefore

\mu(B(x,r))\leq\omega_{N}r^{N}\ \ \ \ \forall~x\in X,\ r>0.

(8.10)

Combining
(\ref{Noncollapsing2})
with
(\ref{Noncollapsing3})
, we have

\mu(B(x,r))=\omega_{N}r^{N}\ \ \ \ \forall~x\in X,\ r>0.

(8.11)

By the volume rigidity theorem (Theorem 7.4), we complete the proof of Theorem
8.4
.
∎

To end this section, we would like to raise an
interesting problem whether we can extend the rigidity theorems (Theorem
3.8
, Theorem
3.9
, Theorem
3.10
and Theorem
8.4
) from CD
(0,N)
Riemannian manifolds, RCD
(0,N)
and RCD
(0,n,N)
spaces to RCD
(K,N)
and RCD
(K,n,N)
spaces for
K\in\mathbb{R}
.

9
Two other entropy inequalities on RCD spaces

In this section, we extend two other entropy formulas to the setting of RCD spaces.

9.1
Extension of Wang’s entropy formula to RCD spaces

The first one is the following
result which was first proved by Wang
[
55
]
.

Theorem 9.1

(
[
55
]
)
.

Let
M
be a closed Riemannian manifold with
Ric\geq-K
, where

K\geq 0
. Define

W_{K}(u,\tau):=\int_{M}\left[\frac{e^{2K\tau}-1}{2K}|\nabla f|^{2}+f+\frac{n}{2}\left(\log\frac{2K\tau}{e^{2K\tau}-1}-{e^{2K\tau}-1\over 2K}\right)-n\right]\frac{e^{-f}}{(4\pi\tau)^{\frac{n}{2}}}\mathrm{~d}\mu.

(9.1)

Then

{d\over dt}W_{K}(u,\tau)=-\int_{M}\frac{e^{2K\tau}-1}{K}\left[\left\|\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}f-\frac{Ke^{2K\tau}}{e^{2K\tau}-1}g_{ij}\right\|^{2}_{\operatorname{HS}}+R_{ij}f_{i}f_{j}+K|\nabla f|^{2}\right]u\mathrm{~d}\mu,

where
\frac{\mathrm{d}\tau}{\mathrm{d}t}=1
. In particular,
W_{K}(f,\tau)
is monotonic non-increasing along
the heat equation
\partial_{t}f=\Delta f
when
{\rm Ric}\geq-K
.

The next result extends Theorem
9.1
to RCD
(K,N)
spaces.

Theorem 9.2
.

Let
(X,d,\mu)
be an
RCD
(K,n,N)
space and
u
be a solution to heat equation. Let

W_{K}(u,t)=\frac{e^{2Kt}-1}{2K}\frac{dH}{dt}+H-\frac{N}{2}\log(4\pi t)+\frac{N}{2}\left[\log\frac{2Kt}{e^{2Kt}-1}-(e^{2Kt}-1)\right]-N.

(9.2)

Then, we have

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}W_{K}(u,t)&=-\frac{e^{2Kt}-1}{K}\int_{X}\left[\left\|\nabla^{2}\log u+\frac{Ke^{2Kt}}{e^{2Kt}-1}{\bf g}\right\|_{\rm HS}^{2}+({\bf Ric_{N,n}}(\Delta)-K{\bf g})(\nabla\log u,\nabla\log u)\right]ud\mu\\
&\hskip 56.9055pt-{(N-n)}\frac{e^{2Kt}-1}{K}\int_{X}\left[{({\rm Tr}\nabla^{2}-\Delta)\log u\over N-n}-{Ke^{2Kt}\over e^{2Kt}-1}\right]^{2}ud\mu.\end{split}

In particular, we have

\frac{d}{dt}W_{K}(u,t)\leq-\frac{e^{2Kt}-1}{NK}\int_{X}\Big(\Delta\log u+\frac{NKe^{2Kt}}{e^{2Kt}-1}\Big)^{2}ud\mu\leq 0.

Moreover, the
\frac{d}{dt}W_{K}(u,t)=0
if and only if

\begin{split}\nabla^{2}\log u=\frac{Ke^{2Kt}}{1-e^{2Kt}}{\bf g},\ \ \ \ {\bf Ric_{N,n}}(\Delta)(\nabla\log u,\nabla\log u)=K|\nabla\log u|^{2},\end{split}

and

\begin{split}{({\rm Tr}\nabla^{2}-\Delta)\log u\over N-n}={Ke^{2Kt}\over e^{2Kt}-1}.\end{split}

In particular, when
K=0
, we recapture (
3.15
) in Theorem
3.8
.

Theorem 9.3
.

(i.e., Theorem
3.8
)
Let
(X,d,\mu)
be an
RCD
(0,n,N)
space and
u
be a solution to heat equation. Let

W(u,t)=2t\frac{dH}{dt}+H-\frac{N}{2}\log(4\pi t)-N.

(9.3)

Then

\begin{split}\frac{dW}{dt}&=-2t\int_{X}\left[\left\|\nabla^{2}\log u+{{\bf g}\over 2t}\right\|_{\rm HS}^{2}+{\bf Ric_{N,n}}(\Delta)(\nabla\log u,\nabla\log u)\right]ud\mu\\
&\hskip 42.67912pt\ \ -{2t\over N-n}\int_{X}\left[({\rm Tr}\nabla^{2}-\Delta)\log u-{N-n\over 2t}\right]^{2}ud\mu.\end{split}

In particular,

\frac{dW}{dt}\leq-\frac{2t}{N}\int_{X}\Big(\Delta\log u+\frac{N}{2t}\Big)^{2}ud\mu\leq 0.

Moreover, the
\frac{dW}{dt}(u,t)=0
if and only if

\begin{split}\nabla^{2}\log u+{{\bf g}\over 2t}=0,\ \ {\bf Ric_{N,n}}(\Delta)(\nabla\log u,\nabla\log u)=0,\ \ ({\rm Tr}\nabla^{2}-\Delta)\log u={N-n\over 2t}.\end{split}

Proof of Theorem
9.2
.
By the weak Bochner formula on RCD
(K,n,N)
spaces, we have

\begin{split}\frac{dW_{K}}{dt}&=\frac{e^{2Kt}-1}{2K}\frac{d^{2}H}{dt^{2}}+\Big(e^{2Kt}+1\Big)\frac{dH}{dt}-N\frac{Ke^{4Kt}}{e^{2Kt}-1}\\
&=-\frac{e^{2Kt}-1}{K}\int_{X}\Gamma_{2}(\log u,\log u)ud\mu+\Big(e^{2Kt}+1\Big)\int_{X}|\nabla\log u|_{w}^{2}ud\mu-N\frac{Ke^{4Kt}}{e^{2Kt}-1}\\
&=-\frac{e^{2Kt}-1}{K}\int_{X}\left[\|\nabla^{2}\log u\|_{\rm HS}^{2}+{\bf Ric}(\nabla\log u,\nabla\log u)\right]ud\mu+\Big(e^{2Kt}+1\Big)\int_{X}|\nabla\log u|_{w}^{2}ud\mu-N\frac{Ke^{4Kt}}{e^{2Kt}-1}\\
&=-\frac{e^{2Kt}-1}{K}\int_{X}\left[\left\|\nabla^{2}\log u+\frac{Ke^{2Kt}}{e^{2Kt}-1}{\bf g}\right\|_{\rm HS}^{2}+{\bf Ric_{N,n}}(\Delta)(\nabla\log u,\nabla\log u)\right]ud\mu\\
&\ \ -\frac{e^{2Kt}-1}{K}\int_{X}\left[-2\frac{Ke^{2Kt}}{e^{2Kt}-1}{\rm Tr}\nabla^{2}\log u-n\left(\frac{Ke^{2Kt}}{e^{2Kt}-1}\right)^{2}+{|({\rm Tr}\nabla^{2}-\Delta)\log u|^{2}\over N-n}\right]ud\mu\\
&\ \ \ \ \ -(e^{2Kt}+1)\int_{X}\Delta\log uud\mu-N\frac{Ke^{4Kt}}{e^{2Kt}-1}\\
&=-\frac{e^{2Kt}-1}{K}\int_{X}\left[\left\|\nabla^{2}\log u+\frac{Ke^{2Kt}}{e^{2Kt}-1}{\bf g}\right\|_{\rm HS}^{2}+({\bf Ric_{N,n}}(\Delta)-K{\bf g})(\nabla\log u,\nabla\log u)\right]ud\mu\\
&\ \ \ \ \ -\frac{e^{2Kt}-1}{K}\int_{X}\left[-2\frac{Ke^{2Kt}}{e^{2Kt}-1}({\rm Tr}\nabla^{2}-\Delta)\log u+{|({\rm Tr}\nabla^{2}-\Delta)\log u|^{2}\over N-n}\right]ud\mu-(N-n)\frac{Ke^{4Kt}}{e^{2Kt}-1}\\
&=-\frac{e^{2Kt}-1}{K}\int_{X}\left[\left\|\nabla^{2}\log u+\frac{Ke^{2Kt}}{e^{2Kt}-1}{\bf g}\right\|_{\rm HS}^{2}+({\bf Ric_{N,n}}(\Delta)-K{\bf g})(\nabla\log u,\nabla\log u)\right]ud\mu\\
&\ \ \ \ \ \ -{(N-n)}\frac{e^{2Kt}-1}{K}\int_{X}\left[{({\rm Tr}\nabla^{2}-\Delta)\log u\over N-n}-{Ke^{2Kt}\over e^{2Kt}-1}\right]^{2}ud\mu.\end{split}

This finishes the proof of Theorem
9.2
.
\square

9.2
Extension of Ye’s entropy formula to RCD spaces

In
[
57
]
, Ye introduced the logarithmic entropy functional and proved its monotonicity on closed Ricci flow. In
[
56
]
,
Wu extended Ye’s result to compact Riemannian manifolds with nonnegative Ricci curvature.

Theorem 9.4

(
[
57
,
56
]
)
.

Let
M
be a compact Riemannian manifold,
u
a positive solution to the heat equation
\partial_{t}u=\Delta u
, and
a
be a constant such that
\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{2}d\mu+a>0
.
Define Ye’s logarithmic entropy functional as follows

\mathcal{Y}_{a}(u,t):=-\int_{M}u^{2}\log u^{2}d\mu+\frac{N}{2}\log\left(\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{2}d\mu+a\right)-4at.

Then

\displaystyle{d\over dt}\mathcal{Y}_{a}(u,t)

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{n}{4\omega}\int_{M}\left[\left\|-2\frac{\nabla^{2}u}{u}+2\frac{\nabla u\otimes\nabla u}{u^{2}}-\frac{4\omega}{n}g\right\|^{2}_{\operatorname{HS}}+4\operatorname{Ric}\left(\frac{\nabla u}{u},\frac{\nabla u}{u}\right)\right]u^{2}d\mu

\displaystyle=-\frac{n}{4\omega}\int_{M}\left(\left|f_{ij}-\frac{4\omega}{n}g_{ij}\right|^{2}_{\operatorname{HS}}+R_{ij}f_{i}f_{j}\right)\frac{e^{-f}}{(4\pi t)^{n/2}}d\mu,

where
u=(4\pi t)^{-n/4}e^{-f/2}
and
\omega:=\omega(u(x,t),a)=\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{2}d\mu+a>0
.

The following result extends Ye and Wu’s result to RCD
(K,n,N)
spaces.

Theorem 9.5
.

Let
(X,d,\mu)
be an
RCD
(K,n,N)
and
u
be a positive solution to the heat equation
\partial_{t}u=\Delta u
. Assume that
a
is a constant such that
\frac{1}{4}\int_{X}\frac{|\nabla u|_{w}^{2}}{u}d\mu+a>0
and
aK\leq 0
.
Define Ye’s logarithmic entropy
\mathcal{Y}_{a}(u,t)
as follows

\mathcal{Y}_{a}(u,t):=-\int_{X}u\log ud\mu+\frac{N}{2}\log\Big(\frac{1}{4}\int_{X}\frac{|\nabla u|_{w}^{2}}{u}+a\Big)+(NK-4a)t.

Then, we have

\frac{d\mathcal{Y}_{a}}{dt}\leq-\frac{1}{4\omega}\int_{X}\Big[\Delta\log u+4\omega\Big]^{2}ud\mu+{aNK\over w},

where
\omega=\frac{1}{4}\int_{X}\frac{|\nabla u|_{w}^{2}}{u}d\mu+a
.
In particular, when
K=0
, it holds

\frac{d\mathcal{Y}_{a}}{dt}\leq-\frac{1}{4\omega}\int_{X}\Big[\Delta\log u+4\omega\Big]^{2}ud\mu\leq 0.

We give two proofs of Theorem
9.5
. The first one follows the same argument as used in the proof of Theorem 5.1 in
[
39
]
, where S. Li and the first named author proved the Shannon entropy power formula on complete Riemannian manifolds with CD
(K,N)
condition. The second one uses the same argument as used in Ye
[
57
]
and Wu
[
56
]
.

We first prove the following result on RCD
(K,n,N)
space, which was first proved by S.Li and the first named author
[
39
]
on complete Riemannian manifolds with bounded geometry condition.

Theorem 9.6
.

On any RCD
(K,n,N)
space, we have

\begin{split}&H^{\prime\prime}+{2\over N}H^{\prime 2}+2KH^{\prime}\\
&=-2\int_{X}\left[\left\|\nabla^{2}\log u-{{\rm Tr}\nabla^{2}\log u\over n}\right\|_{\rm HS}^{2}+({\bf Ric_{N,n}}(\Delta)-K{\bf g})(\nabla\log u,\nabla\log u)\right]ud\mu\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt-{2(N-n)\over Nn}\int_{X}\left[\Delta\log u+{N\over N-n}({\rm Tr}\nabla^{2}-\Delta)\log u\right]^{2}ud\mu-{2\over N}\int_{X}\left[\Delta\log u-\int_{X}\Delta\log uud\mu\right]^{2}ud\mu.\end{split}

(9.4)

In particular, on RCD
(K,n,N)
space, we have

\begin{split}H^{\prime\prime}+{2\over N}H^{\prime 2}+2KH^{\prime}\leq-{2\over N}\int_{X}\left[\Delta\log u-\int_{X}\Delta\log uud\mu\right]^{2}ud\mu.\end{split}

Proof.

The proof is as the same as in
[
39
]
. By the second order entropy dissipation formula, we have

\begin{split}H^{\prime\prime}&=-2\int_{X}\left[\left\|\nabla^{2}\log u-{{\rm Tr}\nabla^{2}\log u\over n}\right\|_{\rm HS}^{2}+{\bf Ric_{N,n}}(\Delta)(\nabla\log u,\nabla\log u)\right]ud\mu\\
&\ \hskip 56.9055pt-2\int_{X}\left[{|{\rm Tr}\nabla^{2}\log u|^{2}\over n}+{|({\rm Tr}\nabla^{2}-\Delta)\log u|^{2}\over N-n}\right]ud\mu.\end{split}

(9.5)

Using

(a+b)^{2}={a^{2}\over 1+\varepsilon}-{b^{2}\over\varepsilon}+{\varepsilon\over 1+\varepsilon}\left(a+{1+\varepsilon\over\varepsilon}b\right)^{2}

and taking
a=\Delta\log u
,
b={\rm Tr}\nabla^{2}\log u-\Delta\log u
and
\varepsilon={N-n\over n}
, we have

\begin{split}{|{\rm Tr}\nabla^{2}\log u|^{2}\over n}&={|\Delta\log u+({\rm Tr}\nabla^{2}-\Delta)\log u|^{2}\over n}\\
&={|\Delta\log u|^{2}\over N}-{|({\rm Tr}\nabla^{2}-\Delta)\log u|^{2}\over N-n}+{N-n\over Nn}\left[\Delta\log u+{N\over N-n}({\rm Tr}\nabla^{2}-\Delta)\log u\right]^{2}.\end{split}

Substituting it into (
9.5
), we can derive (
9.4
).

∎

Proof of Theorem
9.5
using (
9.4
) in Theorem
9.6
.
By the definition of
\mathcal{Y}_{a}(u,t)

\mathcal{Y}_{a}(u,t):=H(u(t))+\frac{N}{2}\log\Big(\frac{1}{4}{d\over dt}H(u(t))+a\Big)+(NK-4a)t,

we have

\begin{split}{d\over dt}\mathcal{Y}_{a}(u,t)&={N\over 2(H^{\prime}+4a)}\left[H^{\prime\prime}+{2\over N}H^{\prime 2}+2KH^{\prime}+{8a(NK-4a)\over N}\right].\end{split}

Using

\displaystyle\int_{X}\Big[\Delta\log u-\int_{X}u\Delta\log ud\mu\Big]^{2}ud\mu+16a^{2}=\int_{X}\Big[\Delta\log u-\int_{X}u\Delta\log ud\mu+4a\Big]^{2}ud\mu,

we have

\displaystyle{d\over dt}\mathcal{Y}_{a}(u,t)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{4w}\int_{X}\Big[\Delta\log u+4w\Big]^{2}ud\mu-{N\over 4w}\int_{X}({\bf Ric_{N,n}}(\Delta)-K{\bf g})(\nabla\log u,\nabla\log u)ud\mu

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt-{N\over 4w}\int_{M}\left\|\nabla^{2}\log u-\frac{{\rm Tr}\nabla^{2}\log u}{n}g\right\|^{2}_{\rm HS}ud\mu

\displaystyle\hskip 42.67912pt-{N-n\over 4Nnw}\int_{M}\Big[\Delta\log u+{N\over N-n}({\rm Tr}\nabla^{2}-\Delta)\log u\Big]^{2}ud\mu+{aNK\over w}.

In particular, on RCD
(0,n,N)
space, we have

\displaystyle{d\over dt}\mathcal{Y}_{a}(u,t)\leq-\frac{1}{4w}\int_{X}\Big[\Delta\log u+4w\Big]^{2}ud\mu\leq 0.

This finishes the proof of Theorem
9.5
.
\square

For the completeness of paper, we give the second proof of Theorem
9.5
for the particular case
K=0
using the same argument as given in
[
57
]
(see also Wu
[
56
]
). Note that the
W
-entropy can be rewritten as

W(f,\tau)=-\int_{X}u\log ud\mu+4\tau\Big(\frac{1}{4}\int_{X}\frac{|\nabla u|_{w}^{2}}{u}d\mu+a\Big)-\frac{N}{2}\log(4\pi e\tau)-4a\tau-{N\over 2}.

Lemma 9.7

(
[
57
,
56
]
)
.

Let
u\in W^{1,2}(X)
with
\int_{X}ud\mu=1
.
Then the minimum of the function

h(s)=s\Big(\frac{1}{4}\int_{X}\frac{|\nabla u|_{w}^{2}}{u}d\mu+a\Big)-\frac{N}{2}\log s,\quad\ s>0

is given by

\min h=\frac{N}{2}\log\Big(\frac{1}{4}\int_{X}\frac{|\nabla u|_{w}^{2}}{u}d\mu+a\Big)+\frac{N}{2}\left(1-\log\frac{N}{2}\right),

and the unique minimum point is achieved at

s=\frac{N}{2}\Big(\frac{1}{4}\int_{X}\frac{|\nabla u|_{w}^{2}}{u}d\mu+a\Big)^{-1}.

Lemma 9.8

(
[
57
,
56
]
)
.

Let
a
be such that
w:=\frac{1}{4}\int\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}_{w}}{u}d\mu+a>0
. Then, for each
\tau\geq 0
, we have

W(f,\tau)\geq-\int_{X}u\log ud\mu+\frac{N}{2}\log\Big(\frac{1}{4}\int_{X}\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}_{w}}{u}d\mu+a\Big)-4a\tau+b(N),

(9.6)

where

b(N):=-\frac{N}{2}\log\pi-\frac{N}{2}\left(1+\log\frac{N}{2}\right),

Moreover

W(f,\frac{N}{8\omega})=-\int_{X}u\log ud\mu+\frac{N}{2}\log\Big(\frac{1}{4}\int_{X}\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}_{w}}{u}d\mu+a\Big)-\frac{Na}{2\omega}+b(N).

Second proof of Theorem
9.5
following
[
57
,
56
]
.
Let
t_{1}\leq t_{2}
,
t\in[t_{1},t_{2}]
and
\sigma>0
. Let

\tau(t):=t-t_{1}+\sigma.

By (
5.2
), we have

\frac{dW}{dt}\leq-\frac{2\tau}{N}\int_{X}\Big(\Delta\log u+\frac{N}{2\tau}\Big)^{2}ud\mu.

(9.7)

Integrating (
9.7
) from
t_{1}
to
t_{2}
, we obtain

W(u(t_{2}),\tau(t_{2}))-W(u(t_{1}),\tau(t_{1}))\leq-\frac{2}{N}\int^{t_{2}}_{t_{1}}\int_{X}\tau\Big(\Delta\log u+\frac{N}{2\tau}\Big)^{2}ud\mu dt.

Taking
\sigma=\frac{N}{8\omega}
,
we have

\begin{split}W(u(t_{2}),t_{2}-t_{1}+\sigma)&\leq W(u(t_{1}),\sigma)-\frac{2}{N}\int^{t_{2}}_{t_{1}}\int_{X}\tau\Big(\Delta\log u+\frac{N}{2\tau}\Big)^{2}ud\mu dt\\
&=-\int_{X}u\log ud\mu\Big|_{t_{1}}+\frac{N}{2}\log\Big(\frac{1}{4}\int_{X}\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}_{w}}{u}d\mu+a\Big)\Big|_{t_{1}}-4a\sigma+b(N)\\
&\hskip 56.9055pt-\frac{2}{N}\int^{t_{2}}_{t_{1}}\int_{X}\tau\Big(\Delta\log u+\frac{N}{2\tau}\Big)^{2}ud\mu dt.\end{split}

(9.8)

On the other hand, the inequality (
9.6
) implies that

W(u(t_{2}),t_{2}-t_{1}+\sigma)\geq-\int_{X}u\log ud\mu\Big|_{t_{2}}+\frac{N}{2}\log\Big(\frac{1}{4}\int_{X}\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}_{w}}{u}d\mu+a\Big)\Big|_{t_{2}}-4a(t_{2}-t_{1}+\sigma)+b(N).

(9.9)

Combining (
9.8
) with (
9.9
), we get

\mathcal{Y}_{a}(u(t_{2}),t_{2})\leq\mathcal{Y}_{a}(u(t_{1}),t_{1})-\frac{2}{N}\int^{t_{2}}_{t_{1}}\int_{X}\tau\Big(\Delta\log u+\frac{N}{2\tau}\Big)^{2}ud\mu dt,

and hence

\lim_{t_{2}\rightarrow t_{1}+}\frac{\mathcal{Y}_{a}(u(t_{2}),t_{2})-\mathcal{Y}_{a}(u(t_{1}),t_{1})}{t_{2}-t_{1}}\leq-{2\over N}\lim_{t_{2}\rightarrow t_{1}+}\frac{1}{t_{2}-t_{1}}\int^{t_{2}}_{t_{1}}\int_{X}\tau\Big(\Delta\log u+\frac{N}{2\tau}\Big)^{2}ud\mu dt.

Since
t_{1}
is arbitrary and
4\tau=\frac{N}{2\omega}
, it follows that

\begin{split}\frac{d\mathcal{Y}_{a}}{dt}\leq-{2\tau\over N}\int_{X}\Big(\Delta\log u+\frac{N}{2\tau}\Big)^{2}ud\mu=-\frac{1}{4\omega}\int_{X}\Big(\Delta\log u+4\omega\Big)^{2}ud\mu\leq 0.\end{split}

\square

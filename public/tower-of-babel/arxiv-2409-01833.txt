On the growth of nonconvex functionals at strict local minimizers Thanks: Funding. The first author was supported by the Alexander von Humboldt Foundation.
Alberto Domínguez Corella, Trí Minh Lê

Abstract
We give new characterizations of growth conditions at strict local minimizers. The main characterizations are a variant of the so-called tilt stability property and an analog of the classical Polyak–Łojasiewicz condition, where the gradient is replaced by linear perturbations.

1
Overview of results and literature review

Due to their crucial role in error estimates, growth conditions are a fundamental part of optimization. They are present in the analysis of discretization schemes, convergence rates, stability, propagation of errors, etc.
Their conceptual understanding delineates the limits among assumptions that can be suppressed or must be maintained to achieve specific error estimates. This manuscript provides a couple of new and maybe unexpected equivalent conditions for the growth of a functional at a strict local minimizer.

1.1
Overview of results

Let
\big(X,\|{\cdot}\|\big)
be a reflexive space over the real numbers and consider a proper lower semicontinuous function
f:X\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
.

We assume that
f
has a strict local minimizer
\bar{x}\in X
and fix
\delta>0
such that

x\in\mathbb{B}\big(\bar{x},\delta\big)\setminus\{\bar{x}\}\quad\implies\quad f(\bar{x})<f(x).

In order to motivate the main characterization in the manuscript, let us consider the following observation illustrating a technique to build sequences of local solutions to linearly perturbed problems.

Observation
.

Let
\{x_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset X
and
\{\xi_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset X^{*}
be sequences such that

\displaystyle x_{n}\in\argmin_{y\in\mathbb{B}(\bar{x},\delta)}\big\{f(y)-\langle\xi_{n},y\rangle\big\}\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{N}.

If
x_{n}\longrightarrow\bar{x}
as
n\longrightarrow+\infty
, then
x_{n}
is a local minimizer of
f-\langle\xi_{n},\cdot\rangle
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
sufficiently large.

The technique relies on obtaining minimizers over a closed ball and ensuring their convergence. One usually employs polynomial growth conditions on the functional (such as (
1
) below) to establish both convergence of minimizers and error estimates when perturbations tend to zero.
A natural question is whether growth conditions can be avoided while still deriving stability estimates (such as (
2
) and (
3
) below) that allow to obtain the same conclusions.
The answer is negative; a polynomial growth condition is necessary, at least when seeking Hölder error bounds.

Let us now state our main result.

Theorem 1
.

Let
p,q\in(1,+\infty)
be such that
p^{-1}+q^{-1}=1
.
The following statements are equivalent.

(i)

(Growth condition) There exists
\gamma>0
such that

x\in\mathbb{B}\big(\bar{x},\delta\big)\quad\implies\quad{f}(x)\geq{f}(\bar{x})+\gamma\|x-\bar{x}\|^{p}.

(1)

(ii)

(Tilt sub-stability) There exists
\kappa>0
such that, for any
\xi\in X^{*}
,

x\in\argmin_{y\in\mathbb{B}(\bar{x},\delta)}\big\{f(y)-\langle\xi,y\rangle\big\}\quad\implies\quad\|x-\bar{x}\|\leq\kappa\|\xi\|^{\frac{q}{p}}.

(2)

(iii)

(Łojasiewicz–type inequality) There exists
\mu>0
such that, for any
\xi\in X^{*}
,

x\in\argmin_{y\in\mathbb{B}(\bar{x},\delta)}\big\{f(y)-\langle\xi,y\rangle\big\}\quad\implies\quad f(x)-f(\bar{x})\leq\mu\|\xi\|^{q}.

(3)

Remark 2
.

Since
\delta>0
can be arbitrarily chosen among the radii of strict minimality, the results extend globally for functions with bounded domain. This is particularly useful for constrained optimization problems over closed bounded sets. For such an application, we refer to Section
5
, where an elliptic tracking problem is studied; there, we establish a novel equivalence between second-order conditions and the sensitivity of solutions with respect to data perturbations.

Remark 3
.

The relation between constants in Theorem
1
can be described as follows.
Implication
(i)\implies(ii)
with
\kappa=\gamma^{-\frac{q}{p}}
; implication
(ii)\implies(iii)
with
\mu=\kappa
; implication
(iii)\implies(i)
with
\gamma=p^{-q}\mu^{-1}
.

Remark 4
.

The results in this section extend to a slightly more general setting, where
X
is a Banach space and
f:X\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
is a proper lower semicontinuous function such that
\overline{\mathrm{conv}}(\mathrm{dom}f)
has the Radon–Nikodým property. For more details and further extensions, we refer to Section
4
.

An immediate consequence of the previous theorem is that Hölder stability with respect to nonlinear perturbations can be characterized through stability with respect to linear ones. This is relevant in connection with constructing sequences of local solutions as the observation made earlier remains valid when nonlinear perturbations are replaced by linear ones.

Corollary 5
.

Let
\lambda\in(0,+\infty)
. The following statements are equivalent.

(i)

There exists
\kappa>0
such that, for any
\xi\in X^{*}
,

\displaystyle x\in\argmin_{y\in\mathbb{B}(\bar{x},\delta)}\big\{f(y)-\langle\xi,y\rangle\big\}\quad\implies\quad\|x-\bar{x}\|\leq\kappa\|\xi\|^{\lambda}.

(ii)

There exists
\kappa>0
such that, for any Lipschitz function
\zeta:X\to\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle x\in\argmin_{y\in\mathbb{B}(\bar{x},\delta)}\big\{f(y)+\zeta(y)\big\}\quad\implies\quad\|x-\bar{x}\|\leq\kappa\hskip 0.85355pt\big(\mathrm{Lip}\,\zeta\big)^{\lambda},

where
\mathrm{Lip}\,\zeta
denotes the Lipschitz constant of
\zeta
.

(iii)

There exists
\kappa>0
such that, for any convex function
\varphi:X\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
,

\displaystyle x\in\argmin_{y\in\mathbb{B}(\bar{x},\delta)}\big\{f(y)+\varphi(y)\big\}\quad\implies\quad\|x-\bar{x}\|\leq\kappa\hskip 0.56917pt|\nabla\varphi|(\bar{x})^{\lambda},

where
|\nabla\varphi|(\bar{x})
denotes the local metric slope of
\varphi
at
\bar{x}
.

Remark 6
.

The results of Corollary
5
apply verbatim to the Łojasiewicz-type inequality in part (iii) of Theorem
1
.

Applying the above result, one can provide a straightforward proof of the convergence of the proximal point algorithm. This is because each point in the sequence can be viewed as a minimizer of a convex perturbation of the original problem.

Corollary 7
.

Let
p,q\in(1,+\infty)
be such that
p^{-1}+q^{-1}=1
.
Let the growth condition (
1
) be fulfilled with
\gamma>0
. Let
x_{0}\in\mathbb{B}(\bar{x},\delta)
and
\varepsilon\in(0,\gamma)
. Let
\{x_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset X
be a sequence satisfying

\displaystyle x_{k}\in\argmin_{y\in\mathbb{B}(\bar{x},\delta)}\left\{f(y)+\frac{\varepsilon}{p}\|y-x_{k-1}\|^{p}\right\}\quad\forall k\in\mathbb{N}.

(4)

The following assertions hold.

(i)

x_{k}
is a local minimizer of
f+\displaystyle\frac{\varepsilon}{p}\|\cdot-x_{k-1}\|^{p}
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

(ii)

x_{k}\longrightarrow\bar{x}
as
k\longrightarrow+\infty
and

\|x_{k}-\bar{x}\|\leq\big(\gamma^{-1}\varepsilon\big)^{\frac{kq}{p}}\|x_{0}-\bar{x}\|\quad\forall k\in\mathbb{N}.

(5)

(iii)

f(x_{k})\longrightarrow f(\bar{x})
as
k\longrightarrow+\infty
and

f(x_{k})-f(\bar{x})\leq\big(\gamma^{-1}\varepsilon\big)^{kq}\big(f(x_{0})-f(\bar{x})\big)\quad\forall k\in\mathbb{N}.

(6)

For Algorithm
1
to be effective, the initial point must be sufficiently close to a local minimizer; the main difficulty lies in identifying such a point without knowing the exact solution. This requirement represents the gap between the theoretical result and its practical implementability. In practice, Corollary
7
only guarantees the existence of sequences generated by Algorithm
1
that converge to a local minimizer with quantitative rates.

x_{0}\in X
, parameter
\varepsilon>0
, number of iterations
K\in\mathbb{N}

Initialize
x\longleftarrow x_{0}

3:

for

k\leftarrow 0
to
K-1

do

4:

Update
x\longleftarrow
a local minimizer of
f+\frac{\varepsilon}{p}\|\cdot-x\|^{p}

1.2
Literature review

The local growth condition we analyze has previously been characterized in terms of the metric subregularity of subdifferential mappings. The first result in this direction appears in
[
54
, Theorem 4.3]
. Subsequently, Aragón and Geoffroy characterized the metric subregularity of the subdifferential of convex functions with local growth; see
[
4
, Theorem 3.3]
and
[
3
, Theorem 2.1]
. This result was later extended to nonconvex functions using the limiting subdifferential in
[
28
, Theorem 3.1]
and
[
38
, Theorem 3.4]
; see also
[
55
, Theorem 4.1]
for an analogous result concerning the Clarke-Rockafellar subdifferential.
Subsequent results in particular settings include the characterization for semi-algebraic functions
[
26
, Theorem 3.1]
, and for functions that can be represented as the sum of a generalized twice differentiable function and a subdifferentially continuous, prox-regular, twice epi-differentiable function
[
17
, Theorem 4.1]
. Further characterizations involving second-order notions of subdifferentiability are given in
[
53
, Theorem 4.1]
and
[
16
, Theorem 3.2]
. In Subsection
4.1
, we present several results concerning the relationship between growth conditions and the subregularity of the Fenchel-Moreau subdifferential for potentially nonconvex functions. Our arguments differ from those in
[
4
, Theorem 3.3]
. The main result we provide is that if the Fenchel-Moreau subdifferential mapping has a dense image, then the global subregularity of the subdifferential is equivalent to a global growth condition. In Remark
20
, we compare the strong metric subregularity of the Fenchel–Moreau subdifferential with other commonly used subdifferentials, such as the limiting and Clarke subdifferentials.

Concerning the relationship between Łojasiewicz inequalities and local growth conditions,
[
51
, Corollary 2]
proves that a generalized Łojasiewicz inequality (where the gradient is replaced by the Fréchet subdifferential) implies a local growth condition. For convex functions,
[
8
, Section 4.2]
and
[
9
, Theorem 5]
establish the equivalence between the classic Łojasiewicz inequality and a local growth condition. More recently, the relationship between the Łojasiewicz inequality and the metric subregularity property of the subdifferential has been considered in
[
40
, Proposition 3.1]
. In Subsection
4.2
, we present a result relating global growth conditions to a version of the Polyak-Łojasiewicz condition, where the gradient at a point is replaced by the set of perturbations that have the point as a minimizer.

The proximal point algorithm was originally developed in
[
49
]
for solving inclusions (generalized equations) of monotone operators. In
[
41
]
, Pennanen showed that the convergence of the proximal point algorithm could be guaranteed locally, provided the iterations start sufficiently close to a solution and the graph of the operator is maximally monotone in the vicinity. More recently, Rockafellar provided a convergence result in
[
50
]
using a nonsmooth notion of second-order sufficiency, which was termed variational convexity. We provide a proof of convergence for a proximal point algorithm (Algorithm
1
below), which illustrates how the results on nonlinear perturbations developed in this paper can be used to derive convergence estimates from a simple conceptual principle.

2
Preliminaries

2.1
Notation

Let
\big(X,\|{\cdot}\|\big)
be a normed space over the real numbers and consider a proper function
f:X\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
, i.e,
\mathrm{dom}\,f\neq\emptyset
.

The continuous dual of
X
is denoted by
X^{*}
, and the duality pairing between
X
and
X^{*}
is denoted by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle:X^{*}\times X\to\mathbb{R}
. We make the slight abuse of notation of denoting
\|{\cdot}\|
also for the dual norm. We write
\mathbb{B}(x,r)
for the closed ball of radius
r\in(0,+\infty)
centered at a point
x\in X
; we set
\mathbb{B}\big(x,+\infty\big)=X
. Analogously, we write
\mathbb{B}_{*}(\xi,r)
for the closed ball in the dual space
X^{*}
of radius
r
centered at
\xi\in X^{*}
. Whenever we write
c>0
, we mean
c\in(0,+\infty)
, that is, we implicitly assume
c
is finite.
The closed convex hull of a set
A\subset X
is denoted by
\overline{\mathrm{conv}}A
.

For
p\in[1,+\infty)
, the duality mapping
J_{p}:X\rightrightarrows X^{*}
is given by

J_{p}(x):=\left\{\xi\in X^{*}:\langle\xi,x\rangle=\|\xi\|\|x\|\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,\|\xi\|=\|x\|^{p-1}\right\}.

Observe that when
X
is a Hilbert space and
p=2
, the duality mapping reduces to the identity mapping. Given a nonempty set
C\subset X
, its indicator function
\iota_{C}:X\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
is given by

\displaystyle\iota_{C}(x):=\begin{cases}0&if\,\,\,\,x\in C\\
+\infty&if\,\,\,\,x\notin C\end{cases}.

The
Fenchel-Moreau subdifferential
of
f:X\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
at point
x\in X
is given by

\displaystyle\partial f(x):=\left\{\xi\in X^{*}:\,\text{$x$ is a minimizer of ${f}-\xi$}\right\}.

(7)

Observe that the convex subdifferential of a nonconvex function can have an empty domain. In this section, and in the whole sequel, we only refer to this notion of subdifferential.

The function
f:X\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
is said to be coercive if for every
c>0
there exists
\delta>0
such that

\displaystyle f(x)\geq c,\text{ for every }x\in X\setminus\mathbb{B}\big(0,\delta\big).

We say that
f
is superlinear if for every
c>0
there exists
\delta>0
such that

\displaystyle f(x)\geq c\|x\|,\text{ for every }x\in X\setminus\mathbb{B}\big(0,\delta\big).

We see that if
f
is superlinear, then it must be coercive.
Notice that if
\mathrm{dom}f
is bounded, then
f
is automatically superlinear.

Given a function
\zeta:X\to\mathbb{R}
, its Lipschitz constant is given by

\displaystyle\mathrm{Lip}\,\zeta:=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\in X\\
x\neq y\end{subarray}}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{\|x-y\|}.

We say that
\zeta
is Lipschitz if
\mathrm{Lip}\,\zeta<+\infty
.
Given a proper function
\varphi:X\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
and
x\in X
, the local metric slope of
\varphi
at
x
is given by

\displaystyle|\nabla\varphi|(x):=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\limsup_{y\to x}\frac{\max\{0,\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)\}}{\|x-y\|}&\text{if $x\in\mathrm{dom}\,\varphi$}\\
+\infty&\text{if $x\notin\mathrm{dom}\,\varphi$}.\end{cases}

We notice that if
\mathrm{dom}\,\varphi=X
and
\mathrm{Lip}\,\varphi<+\infty
, then its local slope is finite everywhere in
X
and
|\nabla\varphi|(x)\leq\mathrm{Lip}\,\varphi
for all
x\in X
.

2.2
Set–valued mappings

Let
X,Y
be normed spaces and consider a set–valued mapping
\mathcal{F}:X\rightrightarrows Y
. The domain of
\mathcal{F}
is denoted by
\mathrm{dom}\,\mathcal{F}:=\{x\in X:\mathcal{F}(x)\neq\emptyset\}
. For a subset
A\subset X
, we will write

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(A):=\{y\in Y:\text{ there exists $x\in A$ such that $y\in\mathcal{F}(x)$}\}.

The image of
\mathcal{F}
is given by
\mathrm{Im}\,\mathcal{F}:=\mathcal{F}(X)
. The inverse of
\mathcal{F}
is the set-valued mapping
\mathcal{F}^{-1}:Y\rightrightarrows X
given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{-1}(y):=\left\{x\in X:\,y\in\mathcal{F}(x)\right\}.

The mapping
\mathcal{F}
is said to be
lower hemicontinuous
at
\bar{x}\in X
if for every open set
V\subset Y
such that
\mathcal{F}(\bar{x})\cap V\neq\emptyset
there exists an open neighborhood
U\subset X
of
\bar{x}
such that
\mathcal{F}(x)\cap V\neq\emptyset
for all
x\in U
.
We recall now the notion of
\lambda
-metric subregularity of a set–valued mapping, see
[
20
, Section 4]
,
[
23
, Section 12]
and
[
25
, Section 3I]
for more details.

Definition 8
.

We say that
\mathcal{F}:X\rightrightarrows Y
is strongly
\lambda
-subregular at
\bar{x}\in\mathcal{F}^{-1}(0)
with parameters
\alpha\in(0,+\infty]
and
\kappa\in(0,+\infty)
such that

\displaystyle\|x-\bar{x}\|\leq\kappa d(0,\mathcal{F}(x))^{\lambda}\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{B}(\bar{x},\alpha),

where
d(0,\mathcal{F}(x)):=\inf\left\{\|y\|:y\in\mathcal{F}(x)\right\}
.

2.3
Radon-Nikodým property

To end this section, we give a short review of sets with the Radon-Nikodým property (RNP) and we refer to the specialized book of Bourgin
[
11
]
for more details. Let us begin recalling the definition of dentability, see
[
10
, Definition 6.3.2]
or
[
42
, Section 1.7.1]
.

Definition 9
.

Let
X
be a Banach space and
\mathcal{W}
a nonempty subset of
X
.

(i)

We say that
\mathcal{W}
is dentable if for every
\varepsilon>0
there exist
\delta>0
and
\xi\in X^{*}
such that
\mathrm{diam}\,S(\mathcal{W},\xi,\delta)<\varepsilon
,
where the slice
S(\mathcal{W},\xi,\delta)
is defined by

\displaystyle S\big(\mathcal{W},\xi,\delta\big):=\{\,v\in\mathcal{W}:\,\langle\xi,v\rangle<\inf_{w\in\mathcal{W}}\langle\xi,w\rangle+\delta\}.

(ii)

We say
\mathcal{W}
has the RNP if every bounded subset of
\mathcal{W}
is dentable.

There are many different characterizations of the RNP, see, e.g., the book
[
29
]
. We mention that the family of spaces having the RNP includes all reflexive spaces, see
[
11
, Corollary 4.1.5]
, in particular the
L^{p}
-spaces for
p\in(1,\infty)
. There are nevertheless interesting spaces without the RNP, such as
L^{1}([0,1])
, see
[
11
, Example 2.1.2]
. However, sets might possess the property even if the underlying space does not have it. For example, it is known that weakly compact convex subsets of Banach spaces have the RNP, see
[
11
, Theorem 3.6.1]
.

One of the main applications of sets with RNP is the so-called Stegall’s variational principle, see
[
42
, Theorem 1.153]
; we will employ the following version of this principle.

Lemma 10
.

Let
X
be a Banach space and
f:X\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
be a proper lower semicontinuous function bounded from below. Suppose that

(i)

\overline{\mathrm{conv}}(\mathrm{dom}f)
has the RNP;

(ii)

f
is superlinear.

Then, there exists a dense set
\Theta\subset X^{*}
such that for every
\xi\in\Theta
,
f-\xi
has a unique minimizer.

Proof.

By superlinearity of
f
, the function
f-\xi
is bounded from below for any
\xi\in X^{*}
, therefore
\mathrm{dom}\,f^{*}=X^{*}
, where
f^{*}
is the Fenchel conjugate of
f
. We can then employ implication
(v)\implies(iii)
of
[
34
, Theorem 5]
to conclude the result.

Observe that assumption
(i)
of Lemma
10
is trivially satisfied when the underlying space is reflexive. We also notice that assumption
(ii)
of Lemma
10
is automatically satisfied when the domain of the function is bounded.

2.4
Technical lemmas

In this subsection, we always assume that
X
is a Banach space and
h:X\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
a proper lower semicontinuous function. We fix a global minimizer
\bar{x}\in X
of the function
h
.

Lemma 11
.

Let
\kappa>0
. Consider the set

\displaystyle Z:=\bigcup_{x\in X}\left\{\xi\in X^{*}:\xi\in\partial h(x)\text{ and }\|x-\bar{x}\|\leq\kappa\|\xi\|^{q/p}\right\}.

Then, for any
\theta\in(0,1]
, it holds

\displaystyle h(x)\geq h(\bar{x})+\dfrac{1}{2}\left(\dfrac{\theta}{2\kappa}\right)^{p/q}\|x-\bar{x}\|^{p}\,\,\,\,\text{for every }x\in\bar{x}+2\kappa\theta^{-1}J_{p}^{-1}(\overline{Z}),

where
\overline{Z}
denotes the closure of
Z
.

Proof.

We note that
0\in Z
since
\bar{x}\in(\partial h)^{-1}(0)
. Let
x\in\bar{x}+2\kappa\theta^{-1}J_{p}^{-1}(\overline{Z})
. By definition of the duality mapping, there exists
\xi\in\overline{Z}
such that

\|\xi\|=\left(\dfrac{\theta}{2\kappa}\right)^{p-1}\|x-\bar{x}\|^{p-1}\text{ and }\langle\xi,x-\bar{x}\rangle=\|\xi\|\|x-\bar{x}\|.

(8)

Let
\{\xi_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset Z
be a sequence converging to
\xi
. By definition of
Z
, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists
x_{n}\in X
such that
\xi_{n}\in\partial h(x_{n})
and
\|x_{n}-\bar{x}\|\leq\kappa\|\xi_{n}\|^{q/p}
. Now, since
h(\bar{x})=\inf_{y\in X}h(y)
, one has

\displaystyle h(\bar{x})-\langle\xi_{n},x_{n}\rangle\leq h(x_{n})-\langle\xi_{n},x_{n}\rangle=\inf_{y\in X}\{h(y)-\langle\xi_{n},y\rangle\}\leq h(x)-\langle\xi_{n},x\rangle.

Hence, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we get

\displaystyle h(\bar{x})-h(x)

\displaystyle\leq\langle\xi_{n},x_{n}-x\rangle=\langle\xi_{n},x_{n}-\bar{x}\rangle+\langle\xi_{n},\bar{x}-x\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\|\xi_{n}\|\|x-\bar{x}\|+\langle\xi_{n},\bar{x}-x\rangle\leq\kappa\|\xi_{n}\|^{q}+\langle\xi_{n},\bar{x}-x\rangle.

Letting
n\to\infty
, we have
h(\bar{x})-h(x)\leq\kappa\|\xi\|^{q}+\langle\xi,\bar{x}-x\rangle
. Using the identities in (
8
),

\displaystyle h(\bar{x})-h(x)

\displaystyle\leq\kappa\|\xi\|^{q}-\|\xi\|\|x-\bar{x}\|=\frac{\theta^{p}}{2^{p}\kappa^{p-1}}\|x-\bar{x}\|^{p}-\Big(\frac{\theta}{2\kappa}\Big)^{p-1}\|x-\bar{x}\|^{p}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\theta}{2\kappa}\right)^{p-1}\left(\dfrac{\theta}{2}-1\right)\|x-\bar{x}\|^{p}\leq-\dfrac{1}{2}\Big(\frac{\theta}{2\kappa}\Big)^{p-1}\|x-\bar{x}\|^{p}.

Therefore, we obtain
h(x)-h(\bar{x})\geq 2^{-1}(2\kappa)^{p-1}\theta^{p-1}\|x-\bar{x}\|^{p}.
Since
p/q=p-1
, the result follows.

Lemma 12
.

Assume that
\overline{\mathrm{conv}}(\mathrm{dom}\,h)
has the RNP and
\sqrt[p]{h(\cdot)-h(\bar{x})}
is superlinear.
For any
\upsilon\in(1,+\infty)
and
x\notin(\partial h)^{-1}(0)
, there exists a sequence
\{\xi_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset X^{*}\setminus\{0\}
satisfying

(i)

\displaystyle\argmin_{y\in X}\left\{\sqrt[p]{h(y)-h(\bar{x})}-\langle\xi_{n},y\rangle\right\}\neq\emptyset
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
;

(ii)

\bar{x}\notin\displaystyle\argmin_{y\in X}\left\{\sqrt[p]{h(y)-h(\bar{x})}-\langle\xi_{n},y\rangle\right\}
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
;

(iii)

\|\xi_{n}\|\to\upsilon\|x-\bar{x}\|^{-1}\displaystyle\sqrt[p]{h(x)-h(\bar{x})}
as
n\to+\infty
.

Proof.

Let
\sigma:X\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
be given by
\sigma(y):=\displaystyle\sqrt[p]{h(y)-h(\bar{x})}
. Let
x\notin(\partial h)^{-1}(0)
and
\upsilon\in(1,+\infty)
. By the Hahn–Banach Theorem, there exists
\eta\in X^{*}
such that

\displaystyle\langle\eta,x-\bar{x}\rangle=\|\eta\|\|x-\bar{x}\|\quad\text{and}\quad\|\eta\|=\frac{\upsilon\sigma(x)}{\|x-\bar{x}\|}.

(9)

Applying Lemma
10
, we can find a sequence
\{\eta_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset X^{*}
such that

\displaystyle\argmin_{y\in X}\left\{\sigma(y)-\langle\eta_{n},y\rangle\right\}\neq\emptyset\quad\text{and}\quad\|\eta_{n}-\eta\|\leq\frac{1}{n}\qquad\forall n\in\mathbb{N}.

(10)

For
n

4
Further discussion

Let us recall that the
Fenchel-Moreau subdifferential
of a (not necessarily convex) function
f:X\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
at point
x\in X
is given by

\displaystyle\partial f(x):=\left\{\xi\in X^{*}:\,\text{$x$ is a minimizer of ${f}-\xi$}\right\}.

We will now discuss both local and global characterizations relating the previous subdifferential with growth conditions.

4.1
Subregularity of the Fenchel-Moreau subdifferential

Lemma
11
gives a set of directions for which the growth condition is satisfied. We are going to state some instances in which one can conclude that this set is a neighborhood or even the whole space. A careful analysis of the proof of Lemma
11
reveals that reflexivity of the underlying space and the lower semicontinuity of the function are superfluous assumptions.

Theorem 15
.

Let
X
be real normed space,
f:X\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
a proper function and
\bar{x}\in(\partial f)^{-1}(0)
. The following assertions hold.

(i)

If there exist
\beta\in(0,+\infty]
,
\kappa\in(0,+\infty)
and a dense subset
\mathcal{W}\subset\mathbb{B}_{*}(0,\beta)
such that for every
\xi\in\mathcal{W}
there exists

so that

\displaystyle\xi\in\partial f(x)\quad\text{and}\quad\|x-\bar{x}\|\leq\kappa\|\xi\|^{\frac{q}{p}}.

Then, for any
\delta\in(0,+\infty)

\displaystyle{f}(x)\geq{f}(\bar{x})+\frac{1}{2}\min\left\{\frac{\beta}{\delta^{\frac{p}{q}}},\frac{1}{(2\kappa)^{\frac{p}{q}}}\right\}\|x-\bar{x}\|^{p}\quad\text{for every $x\in\mathbb{B}(\bar{x},\delta)$.}

(17)

(ii)

Suppose that

-subregular at

with parameters

Remark 16

One can easily find examples where functions only satisfy a growth condition in a part of the domain. For example, consider

and

\bar{x}
.
We see that there are infinitely many local minimizers close to
\bar{x}
. Consequently, any subdifferential for which local minimizers be critical points (such as the limiting, Fréchet or Clarke subdifferentials) cannot be strongly metrically subregular at
\bar{x}
.

4.2
On the Polyak–Łojasiewicz–type inequality

In Theorem
1
, we have shown that a growth condition is equivalent to an analog of the so-called Polyak-Łojasiewicz (replacing the gradient by the norm of linear perturbations).
We will state a global version of this theorem in this subsection.

Theorem 21
.

Let
X
be a Banach space,
f:X\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
a proper lower semicontinuous function and
\bar{x}\in(\partial f)^{-1}(0)
. Supposed that
\overline{\mathrm{conv}}(\mathrm{dom}\,f)
is a bounded set with the RNP.
Then, the following assertions are equivalent.

(i)

there exists
\gamma>0
such that

\displaystyle f(x)\geq f(\bar{x})+\gamma\|x-\bar{x}\|^{p}\quad\text{for every }x\in X;

(ii)

there exists
\mu>0
such that

\displaystyle f(x)-f(\bar{x})\leq\mu d(0,\partial f(x))^{q}\quad\text{for every }x\in X.

Proof.

(i)\implies(ii)
.
This is the same as in Part 1 of proof of Theorem
1
.

(ii)\implies(i)
. The proof remains the same as in Part 3 of the proof of Theorem
1
; the only differences are that the reflexivity of
X
is not needed to establish that
\overline{\mathrm{conv}}(\mathrm{dom}f)
has the RNP, and that the superlinearity of
\sqrt[p]{f(\cdot)-f(\bar{x})}
follows from the boundedness of the domain.

5
An application to a tracking problem

In this section, we apply our results to an elliptic tracking problem without regularization terms. While we focus on a specific tracking problem for simplicity, the same arguments extend to a wide range of problems, including those constrained by parabolic PDEs or the Navier-Stokes equations.

The main result is that a second-order condition is equivalent to the stability of solutions to the problem with respect to the tracking data.
Although elliptic tracking problems have been extensively studied
[
12
,
14
,
15
,
30
,
33
,
45
,
46
,
47
,
52
]
, this particular result has not been previously established. This finding is crucial for understanding the necessary assumptions to ensure the robustness of optimal solutions under data uncertainty.

5.1
An elliptic tracking problem

Let
d\in\{2,3\}
and consider a bounded Lipschitz domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
. For
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
with
\alpha<\beta
, the feasible set is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}:=\left\{u\in L^{1}(\Omega):\,u(x)\in[\alpha,\beta]\,\,\,\,\text{for a.e. $x\in\Omega$}\right\}.

For each
u\in\mathcal{U}
, there is a unique associated state
y_{u}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\cap C(\bar{\Omega})
satisfying

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\langle\nabla y_{u},\nabla\varphi\rangle\,dx+\int_{\Omega}y^{3}_{u}\varphi\,dx=\int_{\Omega}u\varphi\,dx\quad\text{ for every }\varphi\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega).

(19)

Formulation (
19
) says that
y_{u}
is the weak solution of equation
-\Delta y+y_{u}^{3}=u
.

Given a target
y_{d}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, we consider the elliptic tracking problem

\displaystyle(\mathcal{P})\,\,\,\,\,\,\min_{u\in\mathcal{U}}\left\{\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|y_{u}-y_{d}|^{2}dx\right\}.

The objective functional
\mathcal{J}:\mathcal{U}\to\mathbb{R}
associated with problem
\mathcal{P}
is given by
\mathcal{J}(u):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|y_{u}-y_{d}|^{2}\,dx
.
In order to give a good expression for its Gateaux differential, it is often convenient to introduce an adjoint state.
For each
u\in\mathcal{U}
, there is a unique associated costate
p_{u}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\cap C(\bar{\Omega})
satisfying

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\langle\nabla p_{u},\nabla\varphi\rangle\,dx+\int_{\Omega}3y_{u}^{2}p_{u}\varphi dx=\int_{\Omega}(y_{u}-y_{d})\varphi dx\quad\text{ for every }\varphi\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega).

(20)

This means that
p_{u}
is the weak solution of equation
-\Delta p+3y_{u}^{2}p=y_{u}-y_{d}
. After straightforward calculations we obtain that
\nabla\mathcal{J}(u)=p_{u}
for all
u\in\mathcal{U}
.

From now on, we will fix a reference element
{\bar{u}}\in\mathcal{U}
in the feasible set.

Lemma 22
.

If
{\bar{u}}\in\mathcal{U}
is a minimizer of problem
\mathcal{P}
, then

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\langle p_{{\bar{u}}},u-{\bar{u}}\rangle\,dx\geq 0\quad\text{ for every }u\in\mathcal{U}.

Lemma
22
condenses the first-order necessary condition.
Concerning second-order conditions, we refer to
[
12
, Theorem 2.4]
. In order to give a useful expression for the second-order form of the objective functional, we introduce notation for linearized states.

For
v\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, we denote by
z_{v}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\cap C(\bar{\Omega})
the unique function satisfying

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\langle\nabla z_{v},\nabla\varphi\rangle\,dx+\int_{\Omega}3y_{{\bar{u}}}^{2}z_{v}\varphi dx=\int_{\Omega}v\varphi dx\quad\text{for every }\varphi\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega).

This means that
z_{v}
is the weak solution of
-\Delta z_{v}+3y_{{\bar{u}}}^{2}z_{v}=v
. Some straightforward calculations yield

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}^{\prime\prime}({\bar{u}})v^{2}=\int_{\Omega}\big(1-6y_{{\bar{u}}}p_{{\bar{u}}}\big)z_{v}^{2}dx\quad\text{ for every }v\in L^{2}(\Omega).

We will employ the following characterization of growth for the objective functional.

Proposition 23
.

The following statements are equivalent.

(i)

There exist numbers
\delta>0
and
c>0
such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}^{\prime}({\bar{u}})v+\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{J}^{\prime\prime}({\bar{u}})v^{2}\geq c\|z_{v}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

for all
v\in\mathcal{U}-{\bar{u}}
satisfying
\|z_{v}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\delta
.

(ii)

There exist numbers
\delta>0
and
c>0
such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}(u)-\mathcal{J}({\bar{u}})\geq c\|y_{u}-y_{{\bar{u}}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

for all
u\in\mathcal{U}
satisfying
\|y_{u}-y_{{\bar{u}}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\delta
.

Proof.

This is a straightforward application of Taylor’s expansion formula, and the estimates in
[
13
, Section 3]
.

Remark 24
.

We point out that the growth condition in Proposition-
23
(i) can be satisfied for non–bang-bang controls, unlike growth conditions involving the control variable; see
[
15
, Section 2.1]
. For example, if the tracking term
y_{d}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
is chosen so that the optimal solution is a control
\bar{u}\in\mathcal{U}
with
\bar{u}(x)\in(\alpha,\beta)
for a.e.
x\in\Omega
and
y_{\bar{u}}=y_{d}
, then
p_{\bar{u}}=0
, and the condition in Proposition-
23
(i) is satisfied due to the form of the second variation.

5.2
Sensitivity of minimizers with respect to the tracking data

Consider the family
\{\mathcal{P}_{\eta}\}_{\eta\in L^{2}(\Omega)}
of tracking problems

\displaystyle(\mathcal{P}_{\eta})\,\,\,\,\,\min_{u\in\mathcal{U}}\left\{\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\big|y_{u}-(y_{d}+\eta)\big|^{2}\,dx\right\}.

The next characterization is the main result of this section.

Theorem 25
.

Suppose that
{\bar{u}}\in\mathcal{U}
is a strict minimizer of the problem
(\mathcal{P})
.
The following statements are equivalent.

(i)

There exist numbers
\delta>0
and
c>0
such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}^{\prime}({\bar{u}})v+\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{J}^{\prime\prime}({\bar{u}})v^{2}\geq c\|z_{v}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

for all
v\in\mathcal{U}-{\bar{u}}
satisfying
\|z_{v}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\delta
.

(ii)

There exists a number
\kappa>0
such that

\displaystyle\|y_{u_{\eta}}-y_{{\bar{u}}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\kappa\|\eta\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

for any
\eta\in L^{2}(\Omega)
and any minimizer
u_{\eta}\in\mathcal{U}
of problem
\mathcal{P}_{\eta}
.

Proof.

Define the functional
f:L^{2}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
by

\displaystyle f(y):=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\Big(\frac{1}{2}y^{2}+y_{d}y\Big)\,dx&\text{ if $y=y_{u}$ for some $u\in\mathcal{U}$},\\
\\
+\infty&\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

By definition of
f
, we have the identity

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}(u)-\mathcal{J}({\bar{u}})=f(y_{u})-f(y_{{\bar{u}}})\quad\text{for every }u\in\mathcal{U}.

(21)

Notice that
y_{{\bar{u}}}
is a minimizer of
f
. Moreover note that for any
\xi\in L^{2}(\Omega)
and
u\in\mathcal{U}
,

\displaystyle\text{$y_{u}$ is a minimizer of $f-\xi$ if and only if $u$ is a minimizer of problem $\mathcal{P}_{y_{d}+\xi}$}.

(22)

We begin showing implication
(ii)\implies(i)
. Let
\xi\in L^{2}(\Omega)
and consider a minimizer
y=y_{u}
of
f-\xi
. Then, by (
22
) and the hypothesis,

\displaystyle\|y_{u}-y_{{\bar{u}}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\kappa\|\xi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

By implication
(ii)\implies(i)
of Theorem
1
, there exists
c>0
such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}(u)-\mathcal{J}({\bar{u}})=f(y_{u})-f(y_{{\bar{u}}})\geq c\|y_{u}-y_{{\bar{u}}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\quad\text{ for every }u\in\mathcal{U}.

The implication follows then from Proposition
23
.

Let us now prove implication
(i)\implies(ii)
. By Proposition
23
, there exist
\delta>0
and
c>0
such that
\mathcal{J}(u)-\mathcal{J}({\bar{u}})\geq c\|y_{u}-y_{{\bar{u}}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}
for all
u\in\mathcal{U}
with
\|y_{u}-y_{{\bar{u}}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\delta
. We claim that there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that
\mathcal{J}(u)-\mathcal{J}({\bar{u}})\geq\varepsilon
for all
u\in\mathcal{U}
such that
\|y_{u}-y_{{\bar{u}}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\geq\delta
. Otherwise, there would exist a sequence
\{u_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathcal{U}
satisfying
\|y_{u_{n}}-y_{{\bar{u}}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\geq\delta
and
\mathcal{J}(u_{n})\to\mathcal{J}({\bar{u}})
as
n\to+\infty
. We could extract a subsequence
\{u_{n_{k}}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
of
\{u_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converging weakly in
L^{1}(\Omega)
to some
\hat{u}\in\mathcal{U}
. This would imply
\mathcal{J}(\hat{u})=\mathcal{J}({\bar{u}})
, but since
{\bar{u}}
is a strict minimizer,
\hat{u}={\bar{u}}
, and consequently
y_{u_{n_{k}}}\to y_{{\bar{u}}}
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
; a contradiction. Set
M:=\sup_{u\in\mathcal{U}}\|y_{u}-y_{d}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
. Then, for all
u\in\mathcal{U}
with
\|y_{u}-y_{{\bar{u}}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\geq\delta
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}(u)-\mathcal{J}(u)\geq\frac{\varepsilon}{M^{2}}\|y_{u}-y_{{\bar{u}}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

Setting
\gamma:=\min\{c,\varepsilon/M^{2}\}
, we see that

\displaystyle f(y_{u})-f(y_{{\bar{u}}})=\mathcal{J}(u)-\mathcal{J}({\bar{u}})\geq\gamma\|y_{u}-y_{{\bar{u}}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\quad\text{for every }u\in\mathcal{U}.

By implication
(i)\implies(ii)
of Theorem
1
, there exists
\kappa>0
such that

\displaystyle\|y_{u}-y_{{\bar{u}}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\kappa\|\xi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

(23)

for any
\xi\in L^{2}(\Omega)
and any minimizer
y_{u}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
of
f-\xi
. The implication follows then by (
23
).

Remark 26
.

In Theorem
25
, we have assumed that the minimizer is strict; while this is not always the case, one can prove that the set of targets that give a strict minimizer is generic in
L^{2}(\Omega)
. In some tracking problems, the existence of a strict minimizer (and hence a unique one) for every target can be achieved by making natural hypotheses on the data of the problem, see
[

. There are also problems for which there is a tracking datum yielding more than one minimizer even with regularization terms, see
[
43
, Theorem 1.1]
. In the case where the control is also tracked and the control system is not linear, there will always be targets yielding more than one minimizer, see
[
18
, Theorem 2.3]
.

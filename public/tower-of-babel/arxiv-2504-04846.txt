Abstract
. For a differential field F F having an algebraically closed field of constants, we analyze the structure of Picard-Vessiot extensions of F F whose differential Galois groups are unipotent algebraic groups and apply these results to study stability problems in integration in finite terms and the inverse problem in differential Galois theory for unipotent algebraic groups.

Throughout this section,
E
denotes a differential field extension of
F
having
C
as its field of constants.
The group of all differential

F
-automorphisms of

and is denoted by

\mathscr{G}(E|F):=\{\sigma\in\mathrm{Aut}(E|F)\ |\ \sigma(x^{\prime})=\sigma(x)^{\prime}\ \text{for all}\ x\in E\}.

Let

T(E|F):=\{x\in E\ |\ \mathcal{L}(x)=0\ \text{for some}\ \mathcal{L}\in F[\partial]\}.

It is well known that
T(E|F)
is a differential
F
-algebra
[
vdPS03
, Corollary 1.38]
.
For any
\sigma\in\mathscr{G}(E|F)
we observe that
\mathcal{L}(\sigma(x))=\sigma(\mathcal{L}(x))
for all
x\in E
and
\mathcal{L}\in F[\partial].

Thus,
\mathscr{G}(E|F)
is also a group of differential
F-
automorphisms of
T(E|F).

If
E
is a Picard-Vessiot extension of
F,
then
T(E|F)
is called a
Picard-Vessiot ring
of
E
and its field of fractions is

\mathrm{Frac}(T(E|F))=E.

In the next two propositions, for convenience and easy reference, we shall state several fundamental results from the differential Galois theory.

Theorem 2.3
.

Assume that
F
contains an element

Then

, if and only if

Proof.

Let
E=F\langle\eta\rangle,
where
\eta
is an iterated integral of
F
with
\eta^{(n)}=:f\in F.

First, we observe that if
\eta\in F
, then
E=F
and consequently the differential Galois group is trivial. Therefore, we may assume that
\eta
is not an element of
F
. In particular, this implies that
f\neq 0
, since otherwise
\eta\in C[x]
.
Let

V:={\rm{span}}_{C}\{\eta,1,x,x^{2},\dots,x^{n-1}\}\subset E

be the
C
-vector space spanned by
\eta,1,x,x^{2},\dots,x^{n-1}
. Then
\mathrm{dim}(V)=n+1
and

is the solution space of the linear homogeneous differential equation

(2.0.1)

Y^{(n+1)}-\frac{f^{\prime}}{f}Y^{(n)}=0\,.

Since

and
E
has by assumption

is a Picard-Vessiot extension of

Observe that
E=F\langle\eta\rangle=F(\eta^{(n-1)},\eta^{(n-2)},\dots,\eta^{\prime},\eta).
Thus, as noted in Proposition
2.2
(
i
), the differential Galois group
\mathscr{G}(E|F)
is a unipotent algebraic group. Now we shall prove that
\mathscr{G}(E|F)
is a commutative group.

Since
E=F\langle\eta\rangle,
the Galois group
\mathscr{G}(E|F)
becomes commutative at once if we show that

\sigma(\tau(\eta))=\tau(\sigma(\eta)),\quad\text{for all }\sigma,\tau\in\mathscr{G}(E|F).

Observe that
V
is stabilized by the differential Galois group and in particular, for any
\sigma\in\mathscr{G}(E/F),
we have

\sigma(\eta)=c_{\sigma}\eta+f_{\sigma},

where
c_{\sigma}
is a constant and

is a
C-
linear combination of

Since
\eta\in E\setminus F
and
\sigma
is an
F-
automorphism, we conclude that
c_{\sigma}\neq 0
. Since
\mathscr{G}(E/F)
acts rationally on

the map

\phi:\mathscr{G}(E/F)\to C^{*},\quad\sigma\mapsto c_{\sigma}

is a morphism of varieties. We are going to show that
\phi
is a morphism of algebraic groups. Since
f_{\sigma}\in F
, we have
\tau(f_{\sigma})=f_{\sigma}
for all
\sigma,\tau\in\mathscr{G}(E/F)

and so we obtain

\sigma(\tau(\eta))=\sigma(c_{\tau}\eta+f_{\tau})=c_{\sigma}c_{\tau}\eta+f_{\tau}+c_{\tau}f_{\sigma}.

Since we also have
\sigma(\tau(\eta))=c_{\sigma\tau}\eta+f_{\sigma\tau}
,
we find

(2.0.2)

\displaystyle c_{\sigma}c_{\tau}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{\sigma\tau},

\displaystyle f_{\tau}+c_{\tau}f_{\sigma}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{\sigma\tau}.

It follows now from (
2.0.2
) that the map

\phi:\mathscr{G}(E/F)\to\mathrm{G}_{m}(C)
is a morphism of algebraic groups.
But, since
\mathscr{G}(E/F)
is unipotent,
\phi
must be trivial, that is

for all
\sigma\in\mathscr{G}(E/F).

We conclude that

\sigma(\tau(\eta))=\eta+f_{\tau}+f_{\sigma}=\eta+f_{\sigma}+f_{\tau}=\tau(\sigma(\eta)

for any
\sigma,\tau\in\mathscr{G}(E|F)
, i.e.
\mathscr{G}(E|F)
is a commutative unipotent group.

Conversely, let
\mathscr{G}(E|F)
be a commutative unipotent algebraic group. Then by Proposition
2.2
(
ii
) we have
E=F(\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{n})
with
\eta^{\prime}_{i}=f_{i}\in F
for each
1\leq i\leq n.

Let

\eta:=\eta_{1}+x\eta_{2}+\dots+x^{n-1}\eta_{n}.

One can show now by induction on

that there are nonzero numbers
c_{1,m},\dots,c_{n-m,m}\in\mathbb{N}
and an element

such that

\eta^{(m)}=g_{m}+c_{1,m}\eta_{m+1}+c_{2,m}x\eta_{m+2}+\cdots+c_{n-m,m}x^{n-m-1}\eta_{n}.

For

this yields

and so
\eta^{(n)}\in F
.
Moreover, one can use the above inductive result to prove that for each

E=F(\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{n})=F(\eta_{n},\dots,\eta_{m+1},\eta^{(m-1)},\dots,\eta^{\prime},\eta).

In particular, for

we obtain
E=F(\eta^{(n-1)},\eta^{(n-2)},\dots,\eta^{\prime},\eta)=F\langle\eta\rangle
. ∎

(1,1/g^{\prime}_{l})\in(F^{*})^{2}&\mbox{if}\ \ g^{\prime}_{l}\neq 0\end{cases},

we have
\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{f}}(P)\in F[\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{i}]
is of degree
\leq l-1.
From the induction assumption on the degree it follows that
\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{f}}(P)
is an integrated integral of
F
and this in turn implies that

is a generalized iterated integral of
F
by Remark

. This proves that the Picard-Vessiot ring

is the set of all generalized iterated integrals in
E
of
F.

(

\Rightarrow
(

): Suppose that

3.
scalar and matrix differential equations having unipotent Galois groups

It is known (see
[
Kol48
, Section 22]
) that a monic differential operator
\mathcal{L}\in F[\partial]
of order
n
can be factored as

\mathcal{L}=(\partial-a_{1})(\partial-a_{2})\cdots(\partial-a_{n}),

where each
a_{i}
belongs to a finite algebraic extension of
F
, if and only if the connected component of the differential Galois group of
\mathcal{L}
is a solvable linear algebraic group.
We shall now show in the next proposition that if the differential Galois group is unipotent, then there is another way of factoring
\mathcal{L}
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{L}\in F[\partial]
be a monic operator of order
n,

E
be a Picard-Vessiot extension of
F
for
\mathcal{L}
and
V\subset E
be the set of all solutions of
\mathcal{L}(y)=0
.

(i)

The differential Galois group
\mathscr{G}(E|F)
is unipotent, if and only if
there is a tuple
\mathbf{f}:=(f_{1},f_{2},\dots,f_{n})\in(F^{*})^{n}
and an element
f_{n+1}\in F^{*}
such that
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{f}}f_{n+1},
in which case, there is a
C-
basis
v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{n}
of
V
such that

v_{1}=\frac{1}{f_{n+1}},v_{2}=\frac{1}{f_{n+1}}\int\frac{1}{f_{n}},v_{3}=\frac{1}{f_{n+1}}\left(\int\frac{1}{f_{n}}\left(\int\frac{1}{f_{n-1}}\right)\right),\dots,

v_{n}=\frac{1}{f_{n+1}}\left(\int\frac{1}{f_{n}}\left(\int\frac{1}{f_{n-1}}\cdots\frac{1}{f_{3}}\left(\int\frac{1}{f_{2}}\right)\cdots\right)\right).

(ii)

The differential Galois group
\mathscr{G}(E|F)
is unipotent, if and only if there are elements
f_{2},\dots,f_{n}\in F^{*}

such that
E
is a Picard-Vessiot extension of
F
for the matrix differential equation
Y^{\prime}=AY
where

(3.0.1)

A:=\sum^{n-1}_{i=1}\frac{1}{f_{n-i+1}}\rm{E_{i,i+1}}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1/f_{n}&0&0&\cdots&0\\
0&0&1/f_{n-1}&0&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&0\\
0&0&0&\cdots&1/f_{3}&0\\
0&0&0&\cdots&0&1/f_{2}\\
0&0&0&\cdots&0&0\end{pmatrix}.

Proof.

(
i
): Let
V\subset E
be the set of all solutions of
\mathcal{L}(y)=0
and suppose that
\mathscr{G}(E|F)
is unipotent.
Then, one can choose a
C-
basis
\{v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{n}\}
of

so that the faithful representation
\mathscr{G}(E|F)\hookrightarrow\mathrm{GL}(V)
is equivalent to a representation
\mathscr{G}(E|F)\hookrightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{n}(C)
such that the image is contained in the group of all unipotent upper triangular matrices
U(n,C)
of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(C)
, i.e. all upper triangular matrices with
1s
on the diagonal. Thus, for any
\sigma\in\mathscr{G}(E|F),
we obtain

(3.0.2)

\displaystyle\sigma(v_{1})

\displaystyle=v_{1},

(3.0.3)

\displaystyle\sigma(v_{2})

\displaystyle=v_{2}+v_{1}c_{1,2},

(3.0.4)

\displaystyle\sigma(v_{3})

\displaystyle=v_{3}+v_{2}c_{2,3}+v_{1}c_{1,3},

\displaystyle\quad\vdots

(3.0.5)

\displaystyle\sigma(v_{n})

\displaystyle=v_{n}+v_{n-1}c_{n-1,n}+\cdots+v_{2}c_{2,n}+v_{1}c_{1,n}

with
c_{i,j}\in C
.
Equation (
3.0.2
) implies that
v_{1}\in F
and therefore
f_{n+1}:=1/v_{1}\in F^{*}.
From Equation (
3.0.3
) we obtain that
\sigma(f_{n+1}v_{2})=(f_{n+1}v_{2})+c_{1,2}
and therefore

\sigma\left((f_{n+1}v_{2})^{\prime}\right)=\sigma\left(f_{n+1}v_{2}\right)^{\prime}=\left(f_{n+1}v_{2}\right)^{\prime},
which implies that
\left(f_{n+1}v_{2}\right)^{\prime}\in F.

Since
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
are linearly independent over
C
, we conclude that
(f_{n+1}v_{2})^{\prime}\neq 0.
Let
f_{n}:=1/(f_{n+1}v_{2})^{\prime}
and then we obtain that

(f_{n}(f_{n+1}v_{2})^{\prime})^{\prime}=0.

Next, from Equation (
3.0.4
), we obtain
\sigma(f_{n+1}v_{3})=(f_{n+1}v_{3})+(f_{n+1}v_{2})c_{2,3}+c_{1,3}
. It follows that
(f_{n}(f_{n+1}v_{3})^{\prime})^{\prime}\in F
and since
v_{1}
,
v_{2}
,
v_{3}
are
C
-linearly independent, we obtain that

(f_{n}(f_{n+1}v_{3})^{\prime})^{\prime}\neq 0
. Let
f_{n-1}:=1/(f_{n}(f_{n+1}v_{3})^{\prime})^{\prime}
and then we have that

(f_{n-1}(f_{n}(f_{n+1}v_{3})^{\prime})^{\prime})^{\prime}=0.

Likewise for
i=0,1,\dots,n-2,
we shall recursively define

(3.0.6)

f_{n-i}:=\frac{1}{(f_{n-(i-1)}(f_{n-(i-2)}(\cdots(f_{n+1}v_{i+2})^{\prime}\cdots)^{\prime})^{\prime})^{\prime}}\in F^{*}

and obtain that

(3.0.7)

(f_{n-i}(f_{n-(i-1)}(f_{n-(i-2)}(\cdots(f_{n+1}v_{i+2})^{\prime}\cdots)^{\prime})^{\prime})^{\prime})^{\prime}=0.

In particular, for
i=n-2,
we have

(3.0.8)

(f_{2}(f_{3}\cdots(f_{n}(f_{n+1}v_{n})^{\prime})^{\prime}\cdots)^{\prime})^{\prime}=0.

We choose an element
f_{1}\in F^{*}
so that for

(3.0.9)

\mathbf{f}:=(f_{1},f_{2},\dots,f_{n}),

the operator
\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{f}}f_{n+1}
is monic. Since
f_{n+1}v_{1}=1
, we have
\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{f}}f_{n+1}(v_{1})=\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{f}}(f_{n+1}v_{1})=0
. From Equation (
3.0.7
) we conclude that

\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{f}}f_{n+1}(v_{i+2})=0

for all

Thus,
V
is also the set of all solutions of the monic linear differential equation

(3.0.10)

\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{f}}f_{n+1}(y)=0.

Since
\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{f}}f_{n+1}
is monic and its order is
n
, it follows that
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{f}}f_{n+1}
.

The converse of (
i
) follows from Theorem
2.7
.

We have that
v_{1}=\frac{1}{f_{n+1}}
and from Equation (
3.0.7
) we can write

\displaystyle v_{2}=\frac{1}{f_{n+1}}\int\frac{1}{f_{n}},\quad v_{3}=\frac{1}{f_{n+1}}\left(\int\frac{1}{f_{n}}\left(\int\frac{1}{f_{n-1}}\right)\right),\dots,

\displaystyle v_{n}=\frac{1}{f_{n+1}}\left(\int\frac{1}{f_{n}}\left(\int\frac{1}{f_{n-1}}\cdots\frac{1}{f_{3}}\left(\int\frac{1}{f_{2}}\right)\cdots\right)\right).

(
ii
): Assume that
\mathscr{G}(E|F)
is unipotent and keep the notation of (
i
). Then according to (
i
) we have
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{f}}f_{n+1}
and so the elements
w_{i}=v_{i}/v_{1}
form a
C
-basis of the solution space of
\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{f}}(y)=0
and

is generated as a differential field over
F
by
w_{1},\dots,w_{n}
, that is

. For
1\leq k\leq n
we define

t_{1,k}:=w_{k}

and for

and

Let
t_{i,j}:=0
for

and observe that
T:=(t_{i,j})\in U(n,E)
.
A straight forward calculation shows that

T^{\prime}=AT,

where

is as in (
3.0.1
).

Since
w_{k}=t_{1,k}\in F(t_{i,j}|1\leq i,j\leq n)
and

is a differential field, we actually have that
E=F\langle w_{1},\dots,w_{n}\rangle=F(t_{i,j}|1\leq i,j\leq n)
.

To prove the converse assume that
E
is a Picard-Vessiot extension of
F
for
Y^{\prime}=AY
and let
T\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}(E)
be a fundamental matrix for
Y^{\prime}=AY
. Since

lies in the Lie algebra of

, it follows from
[

that

is contained in a conjugate of

in
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(C)
and is therefore also unipotent. More precisely, it follows from the proof of this proposition that we can construct a Picard-Vessiot extension of
F
for
Y^{\prime}=AY
such that there is an upper triangular fundamental matrix and the respective differential Galois group is contained in
U(n,C)
. We obtain now from
[
vdPS03
, Prop. 1.20 (2)]
that there exists

such that

and

. Thus, we can assume that
T=(t_{i,j})\in U(n,E)
and
\mathscr{G}(E|F)\subset U(n,C)
. For
2\leq k\leq l\leq n,
we observe that

t_{k,l}=f_{n-(k-2)}t^{\prime}_{(k-1),l}
and therefore, we obtain using an induction that the entries of the fundamental matrix belong to the differential field

In particular,

Since

we also obtain for

such that
\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}t_{1,i}=0.
Taking the first derivative of this sum, we obtain

\sum_{i=2}^{n}c_{i}t_{2,i}=0
, since

and

for every

. Now taking again the derivative and using that

, we get

. For

. Continuing in this way, we obtain that

-linearly independent.
Choosing a suitable

.

Let
\mathbf{f}=(f_{1},\dots,f_{n})\in(F^{*})^{n}
be an element such that

Then a differential field extension

of

is a Picard-Vessiot extension of

, if and only if it is a Picard-Vessiot extension of

for

with

Proof.

Readily follows from the proof of Theorem
3.1

∎

The rest of this section is dedicated to construct for

and for a given unipotent group

an element

such that the differential Galois group for

or for

with

is in (

) is

.

Let
\mathfrak{u}(n,C)
be the lie algebra of
U(n,C)
, that is the Lie algebra of all upper triangular matrices with zeros on the diagonal.
Let
U\subseteq U(n,C)
be a unipotent matrix group of dimension

and let

be its Lie algebra. Moreover, let
C[Z_{i,j}\ |\ 1\leq i<j\leq n]
be the coordinate ring of

and let

be the defining ideal of
U.
The commutator subgroup
[U,U]
of
U
is normal and we can consider the quotient homomorphism

. Let

be the induced homomorphism of Lie algebras. If
l
is the dimension of the commutative group
U/[U,U]
, we can choose a basis
X_{1},\dots,X_{l},\dots,X_{m}
of
\mathfrak{u}
such that

form a basis of
\mathrm{Lie}(U/[U,U]).

Let
\tilde{a}_{1},\dots,\tilde{a}_{l}
be rational functions in
F
such that their images in

are
C
-linearly independent and define

A_{\mathfrak{u}}:=\sum_{i=1}^{l}\tilde{a}_{i}X_{i}\in\mathfrak{u}(F)\subseteq\mathfrak{u}(n,F).

Then, it follows from
[
Kov69
, Proposition 14]
and
[
Kov69
, Proposition 11]
that the differential Galois group of any Picard-Vessiot extension

of

We are going to construct a Picard-Vessiot extension
E
of
F
for
Y^{\prime}=A_{u}Y
.
We extend the derivation of
F
to the polynomial ring

R:=F[Z_{i,j}\mid 1\leq i<j\leq n]

in the indeterminates
Z_{i,j}
over
F
by

, where

is the matrix

(3.0.11)

\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\

We consider now the ideal

in

, which is generated by the defining ideal

of

. Since

of

for the equation

, we conclude that

.
It follows from

.

In order to construct a scalar differential equation of the form

for the differential

\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\

-a_{0}&-a_{1}&-a_{3}&\cdots&\-a_{n-2}&-a_{n-1}\end{pmatrix}

\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
Y^{(n-1)}_{1}&Y^{(n-1)}_{2}&\cdots&Y^{(n-1)}_{n}\end{pmatrix}

.

(3.0.12)

(Y_{1}^{-1})^{\prime}&Y_{1}^{-1}&0&&\vdots\\
(Y_{1}^{-1})^{\prime\prime}&2(Y_{1}^{-1})^{\prime}&Y_{1}^{-1}\\
\vdots&\vdots&&\ddots&\\
&&&&\\
(Y_{1}^{-1})^{(n-1)}&\dots&&(n-1)(Y_{1}^{-1})^{\prime}&Y_{1}^{-1}\end{pmatrix}

\overline{Y}_{i,j}

. Furthermore,

For any
\sigma\in\mathscr{G}(E|F),

for each

4.
Stable Iterated Integrals

We shall apply now Theorem
2.3
to study stability problems in integration in finite terms.

Definition 4.1
.

An element
g\in F
is said to be elementary integrable over
F
, if there is an elementary extension
E
of
F
and an element
y\in E
such that
y^{\prime}=g.

In
[
Ros68
]
, Rosenlicht proved Liouville’s Theorem on integration in finite terms. It states that an element
g\in F
is elementary integrable over
F
if and only if there exist elements
u_{1},\dots,u_{n}\in F^{*}
and
v\in F
and constants
c_{1},\dots,c_{n}\in C
such that

(4.0.1)

g=v^{\prime}+\sum^{n}_{i=1}c_{i}(u^{\prime}_{i}/u_{i}).

Definition 4.2
.

(1)

For an integer
n\in\mathbb{N},
an element
g\in F
is said to be elementary
n-
integrable over
F
, if there is an element
\eta
in some elementary extension of
F
such that
\eta^{(n)}=g.
If such an element
\eta
exists in
F
itself, then we say
g
is
n-
integrable in
F.

(2)

An element
g\in F
is said to be elementary
\infty-
integrable over
F
, if there is a sequence
\eta_{1},\eta_{2},\dots
of elements with each
\eta_{i}
belonging to some elementary extension of
F
such that
\eta^{(i)}_{i}=g.
If such a sequence
\eta_{1},\eta_{2},\dots
of elements can be found in
F
itself, then we say
g
is
\infty-
integrable in
F.

Note that if
g\in F
is
n-
integrable in
F
(respectively, elementary
n-
integrable over
F
) for
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
, then it is also
m-
integrable in
F
(respectively, elementary
m-
integrable over
F
) for any
m\leq n.

The problem of finding
m-
integrable elements and
\infty-
integrable elements in a field has a strong connection to the theory of dynamical systems, as described in
[
Che22
]
and
[
CFGL23
]
. Our results are motivated by these articles, wherein, elements of
F
that are
\infty-
integrable in
F
are called the
stable elements
of
F.

Proposition 4.3
.

Suppose that
z^{\prime}\neq 1
for any
z\in F
. Let
F(y)
be a differential field extension of
F
with
y\notin F
and
y^{\prime}\in F
. Assume that
C
is the field of constants of
F(y)
. For an element
g\in F
and an integer
n\geq 2
suppose that
g
is
n-
integrable in
F(y)
, that is there exists
\eta\in F(y)
with
\eta^{(n)}=g
. Assume further that
\eta^{(m)}\notin F
for any
m<n.
Then there exists an element
x\in F(y)
with
x^{\prime}=1.

Proof.

Let
\eta\in F(y)
with
\eta^{(n)}=g\in F
and
n\geq 2.
Clearly,
F(y)
is a Picard-Vessiot extenion of
F
with Picard-Vessiot ring
F[y]=T(F(y)|F)
. Since
\eta
satisfies a differential equation over
F
, we obtain that
\eta\in F[y]
, say

\eta=a_{k}y^{k}+a_{k-1}y^{k-1}+\cdots+a_{0},

where
a_{i}\in F
and
a_{k}\neq 0.

For any differential automorphism
\sigma\in\mathscr{G}(F(y)|F),
we have

g=\sigma(g)=\sigma(\eta^{(n)})=\sigma(\eta)^{(n)}

and so
(\sigma(\eta)-\eta)^{(n)}=0
.
If
\sigma(\eta)-\eta\in C
for all
\sigma\in\mathscr{G}(F(y)|F)
, then
\sigma(\eta^{\prime})=\eta^{\prime}
for all
\sigma\in\mathscr{G}(F(y)|F)
implying that
\eta^{\prime}\in F.

This contradicts the assumption that
\eta^{(m)}\notin F
for any
m<n
. Thus there exists
\sigma\in\mathscr{G}(F(y)|F)
such that
\sigma(\eta)-\eta
is not a constant.

We fix this
\sigma
and choose now the largest integer
m
such that
(\sigma(\eta)-\eta)^{(m)}\neq 0.
Then
1\leq m<n
and
(\sigma(\eta)-\eta)^{(m)}
is a nonzero constant.
Let

x:=\frac{(\sigma(\eta)-\eta)^{(m-1)}}{(\sigma(\eta)-\eta)^{(m)}}

and observe that
x\in F(y)
with
x^{\prime}=1.
∎

We prove now a generalization of Liouville’s Theorem for elementary
n-
integrable elements of
F.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
g\in F
and
n
be a positive integer. Suppose that
g
is elementary
n-
integrable over
F
with

for some
\eta

(i)

(

Since

with commutative unipotent differential Galois group. Then by

(

Proposition 5.2

Proposition 5.3

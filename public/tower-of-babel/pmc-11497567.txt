Construction of circRNA–miRNA–mRNA ceRNA regulatory network and screening of diagnostic targets for tuberculosis
Authors: Xinyi Pu, Siyu Sheng, Yujuan Fu, Yue Yang, Guangyu Xu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractTuberculosis (TB) is a chronic infectious disease caused by Mycobacterium tuberculosis (Mtb), which threatens human health and safety all over the world. Hundreds of thousands of people die from TB every year. Timely early diagnosis and treatment of patients is the most important measure to control the source of infection and curb the epidemic of tuberculosis. The existing diagnostic methods have the disadvantages of poor sensitivity and long culture time. Competitive endogenous RNAs (ceRNAs) can regulate the expression of corresponding target genes by competing for the same microRNA (miRNA) response elements (MREs) as mRNA. Recent studies have found that circRNA has the advantages of long half-life, good stability and tissue specificity, and can be used as a biomarker for predicting, diagnosing and treating various diseases, and is an ideal candidate for biomarkers in body fluid biopsy. In this study, transcriptome sequencing was performed on whole blood samples to screen out TB-related mirna and mRNA differential expression, and to construct the ceRNA regulatory network. Through the analysis of ceRNA regulatory network, it was found that circRNA could competitively bind has-miR-607 and induce down-regulation of has-miR-607, thereby inhibiting the expression of IFNG. The hsa_circ_0000566, hsa_circ_0001844, hsa_circ_0005408, hsa_circ_0007587, hsa_circ_0086710, IFNG and has-miR-607 couble be used as new diagnostic targets for TB. The results of this study not only provide a new perspective for studying the potential role of ceRNA regulatory network in tuberculosis, but also provide a new target and method for the diagnosis of tuberculosis.

Introduction

Tuberculosis (TB) is a chronic infectious disease caused by Mycobacterium tuberculosis (Mtb). According to the World Health Organization, approximately 2 billion people globally are infected with Mtb, including 20 million patients with TB. An estimated 1.2–1.5 million deaths occur annually due to TB, which is one of the diseases with the largest number of deaths caused by a single infection agent [1–3]. Early diagnosis and timely treatment of patients with TB are the most important measures to control the source of infection and curb the prevalence of TB. Therefore, screening new diagnostic targets for TB is a pressing technical challenge with significant societal implications [4–7].

Competitive endogenous RNA (ceRNA), namely long-stranded noncoding RNA (lncRNA) and circular RNA (circRNA), can regulate the expression of corresponding target genes by competing with mRNA for the same microRNA (miRNA) response elements (MREs) [8–10]. Previous studies have shown that multiple miRNAs promote the production of pro-inflammatory immune cells by acting on the Th1 and Th17 signalling pathways [11–13], with an miRNA sponge effect. On the contrary, circRNAs containing a large number of miRNA binding sites can serve as ceRNAs through MREs to indirectly regulate the downstream mRNA. CircRNA is a new type of RNA discovered after miRNA and lncRNA. Recent studies have found that circRNAs can be used as a biological diagnostic marker for numerous diseases and have essential biological functions [14–16]. CircRNAs are resistant to degradation by ribonuclease and exonuclease because they form a covalently closed-ring structure without a 5′ terminal cap and 3′ terminal poly(A) tail. Therefore, circRNAs have a longer half-life and better stability, and are more suitable as biomarkers [17]. Moreover, circRNAs are the most prevalent molecules distributed in human cells and are highly expressed in numerous tissues and body fluids, including blood, plasma, serum and even exosomes, making them ideal candidates for biomarkers in body fluid biopsies [18]. In addition, circRNAs are tissue-specific. Some circRNAs can only be detected in specific tissues but are not expressed in others. The expression level of circRNAs may also change with the developmental stage of tissue cells or in response to pathological changes. Previous studies have found that most circRNAs have highly conserved sequences in the evolution process between different species, and only a few are not evolutionarily conserved. These characteristics indicate that circRNAs may be necessary for transcriptional and posttranscriptional levels [19]. CircRNAs are biomarkers for the prediction, diagnosis and treatment of various diseases due to their structural stability, sequence conservation, specific expression in cells or tissues, and extensive stability in different organisms [20–25].

Based on transcriptome sequencing and circRNAs, this study screened out miRNAs and mRNA differentially expressed (DE) in relation to TB. Moreover, a ceRNA regulatory network was constructed, and the core nodes of the network were screened out by analysing the ceRNA regulatory network. Finally, the possible pathogenesis of TB was clarified, and new diagnostic targets for potential TB were identified.

Materials and methods

Clinical data sources and grouping

This study collected blood samples from 10 patients diagnosed with pulmonary TB and 10 healthy controls.

The healthy control group included participants with age between 20 and 60 years, negative tuberculin test, and no previous history of malignancy.

The pulmonary TB group included participants aged between 20 and 60 years, with no prior history of malignancy, and diagnosed with pulmonary TB based on clinical symptoms, imaging and other pathological examinations Supplementary Material Table 1).

This study was approved by the Medical Ethics Committee of Jilin Provincial Tuberculosis Hospital. All 20 participants provided written informed consent to participate in the study.

Transcriptome sequencing

Whole-blood samples were collected from 20 volunteers via antecubital venipuncture. Total RNA was extracted from whole-blood samples using the PAXgene Blood RNA Kit. Total RNA from the whole-blood samples was purified using an Agilent Bioanalyzer 2100 (Agilent Technologies, Santa Clara, CA) for quality control. RNAs with RIN (RNA integrity number) ≥7.0 were used to ensure the construction of the downstream high-quality total RNA Seq library. The sequencing conditions were as follows: RNA sample concentration ≥100 ng/μL, total amount >2 mg, OD260/280 values between 1.8 and 2.2, and OD260/230 values ≥2.0. Finally, the library was sequenced employing a 2 × 150 bp double-ended sequencing strategy using an Illumina HiSeq 2500 high-throughput sequencing platform.

Screening of DEcircRNA, DEmiRNA and DEmRNA

Significant DEcircRNAs, DEmiRNAs and DEmRNAs were investigated using the DESeq R package (version 1.8.3). A threshold value of log 2 (fold change) of ≥1 with a significance assessed at p < .05 was determined. Volcano plots and heat mapping were performed to obtain an overview of the expression profiles of circRNAs, miRNAs and mRNAs.

Gene Ontology functional analysis

The screened DE genes were submitted to the Database for Annotation, Visualization, and Integrated Discovery (DAVID) (https://david.ncifcrf.gov/tools.jsp), and Homo sapiens were used as the research object. Gene Ontology (GO) clustering analysis was performed on the DE genes using the functional annotation module in DAVID.

Construction of ceRNA regulatory network

A ceRNA regulatory network for DEncRNAs and DEmRNAs was constructed based on the ceRNA theory to illustrate the regulatory relationships among circRNA, miRNA and mRNA. The regulatory relationships between DEcircRNA and DEmiRNA, and DEmiRNA and DEmRNA were predicted using the circRNA interactome database (https://circinteractome.nia.nih.gov/) and miRDB database (http://mirdb.org/), and circRNA–miRNA and miRNA–mRNA relationship pairs were constructed. Finally, a ceRNA regulatory network of circRNA–miRNA–mRNA was constructed, and a regulatory network was visualized using the Cytoscape 3.9.1 software.

Establishment of the protein–protein interaction network and identification of hub mRNAs

The STRING database (http://www.string-db.org/) provides information on known and predicted protein–protein interactions (PPIs). A PPI network was constructed using STRING and visualized using Cytoscape 3.9.1. CytoHubba, an algorithm capable of discovering key targets and subnetworks of complex networks, was employed to recognize highly interacting hub mRNA clustering.

Construction of a ceRNA regulatory network of core circRNA–miRNA–mRNA

The core mRNA served as a node to construct the core circRNA–miRNA–mRNA regulatory network for TB. This network was built based on the regulatory relationships between mRNA–miRNA and miRNA–circRNA with the core mRNA.

GO functional analysis of DEcircRNA, DEmiRNA and DEmRNA

The GO functional analysis of DEcircRNA (Figure 4(A)) showed that the biological process (BP) was enriched in nine GO functions, of which transcriptional regulation, DNA template, protein palmitoylation and chromatin remodelling were the most significant. The cellular component (CC) was enriched in five GO functions, of which nucleus, nucleoplasm and endoplasmic reticulum were the most significant. The molecular function (MF) was enriched in eight GO functions, of which histone binding, transcription co-repressor factor activity and protein binding were the most significant.

GO functional analysis of DEcircRNA, DEmiRNA and DEmRNA. GO functional analysis of (A) DEcircRNA; (B) DEmiRNA; and (C) DEmRNA.

The GO function analysis of DEmiRNA (Figure 4(B)) showed that the BP was enriched in 63 GO functions, of which chromatin tissue, the negative regulation of RNA polymerase II promoter transcription, and the positive regulation of RNA polymerase II promoter transcription were the most significant. The CC was enriched in 15 GO functions, of which nuclear, nucleoplasmic and chromatin were the most significant. The MF was enriched in 29 GO functions, of which DNA binding, RNA polymerase II core promoter near end sequence-specific DNA binding, and extracellular matrix structural components were the most significant.

The GO functional analysis of DEmRNA (Figure 4(C)) showed that BPs were enriched in 241 GO functions, of which innate immune response, viral defence response and immune response were the most significant. The CC was enriched in 57 GO functions, of which cell membrane, extracellular region and cell membrane components were the most significant. The MF was enriched in 59 GO functions, of which chromatin structural components, transmembrane signal receptor activity and protein heterodimer activity were the most significant.

CeRNA network of circRNA–miRNA–miRNA

A circRNA–miRNA ceRNA regulatory network was constructed based on the previously obtained 16 circRNA–miRNA relationship pairs and 42 miRNA–mRNA relationship pairs. This network was visualized using the Cytoscape 3.9.1 software (Figure 7). It comprised 40 DEmRNAs, four DEmiRNAs and 15 DEcircRNAs, of which hsa-miR-607 showed close associations with multiple circRNAs and mRNAs; related studies indicated that hsa-miR-607 was closely linked to immune system disorders such as non-small-cell lung cancer [26–29].

A PPI regulatory network for DEmRNAs was constructed using the STRING database to further screen the core network nodes, which included 40 nodes and 14 edges (Figure 8).

Discussion

TB is an infectious disease causing the most deaths. Early diagnosis and timely treatment of patients with TB are the most important measures to control the source of infection and curb the prevalence of TB [30,31]. In this study, peripheral blood from 10 patients with pulmonary TB and 10 healthy individuals was sequenced by transcriptome sequencing to explore the pathogenesis of TB and to screen for new diagnostic targets for TB.

Overall, 42 DEcircRNAs, 56 DEmiRNAs and 775 DEmRNAs were screened using transcriptome sequencing, and GO analysis was performed. DEcircRNA, DEmiRNA and DEmRNA were found to be mainly involved in the positive and negative regulation of transcription, protein binding and multiple immune responses, suggesting that they play a regulatory role in TB mainly by regulating multiple transcriptions and participating in multiple immune responses. The regulatory relationship between DEmiRNA and DEcircRNA with DEmRNA was further predicted using databases such as circRNA interaction, and a circRNA–miRNA–mRNA ceRNA regulatory network was constructed based on these predictions. Four core DEmRNAs were identified in the PPI network of DEmRNAs, and a core circRNA–miRNA network was constructed consisting of four core mRNAs (CASP1, TNFSF10, GBP2 and IFNG), five circRNAs (hsa-circ_0000566, hsa-circ_0001844, hsa-circ_0005408, hsa-circ_0007587 and hsa-circ_0086710) and one miRNA (hsa-miR-607).

Analysis of the DEcircRNAs showed that the downregulated circRNAs were much more abundant than the upregulated circRNAs, and circRNAs were all downregulated circRNAs in the core circRNA–miRNA–mRNA network, suggesting that the downregulated circRNAs may play a role in the pathogenesis of pulmonary TB. The underlying mechanism may be attributed to the interaction of downregulated circRNAs in patients with TB, such as hsa_circ_0000566 and hsa_circ_0005408, with has-miR-607, resulting in the upregulation of has-miR-607 expression, thereby affecting the expression of IFNG. The low expression of IFNG in the core regulatory network plays a key role in many aspects of the immune system. Previous studies have shown that IFNG is an essential immune cytokine against TB. The expression levels of IFNG and its receptor (IFNGR1) in the body are closely associated with TB. Turner et al. reported that the level of IFNG was low before treatment in patients with TB, which returned to normal and remained so until the end of the treatment after the application of anti-TB drugs for 2 months. This indicated that the level of IFNG in patients with TB changes with the progress in anti-TB chemotherapy. Other studies also confirmed that the immune response capacity of Th1 cells can be assessed by measuring the levels of IFNG in the plasma and sputum of patients with pulmonary TB. The levels of IFNG in the serum of patients in the TB group were lower than that in the control group, and the IFNG in the serum of the retreated group was lower than that in the primary treatment group. This indicated that reducing IFNG in the body reduces the protective immunity of the body, thus causing the onset of human infection with Mtb. The lower the IFNG level, the longer the course of the disease, and the more serious the disease, suggesting a potential basis for utilizing IFNG clinically to enhance immunity, reduce the incidence of TB, and use adjuvant chemotherapy [32–35]. Related studies have shown that hsa-miR-607 in the core ceRNA regulatory network regulates multiple cancers, such as non-small-cell lung cancer. Although studies on hsa_circ_0000566, hsa_circ_0001844, hsa_circ_0005408, hsa_circ_0007587, hsa_circ_0086710, and so forth are limited, many studies have reported on circRNA as a biomarker in recent years. For example, circRNA-002453 in plasma could be used as a marker of lupus nephritis, hsa-circ-0064428 as a biomarker for liver cancer, and hsa-circ-0000729 as a potential marker for diagnosing lung adenocarcinoma [36–42].

These results indicated that hsa_circ_0000566, hsa_circ_0001844, hsa_circ_0005408, hsa_circ_0007587, hsa_circ_0086710, has-miR-607 and IFNG may participate in the immune process of TB and form a complex circRNA–miRNA–mRNA network regulatory relationship.

Conclusions

The circRNA could competitively bind to has-miR-607 and induce down-regulation of has-miR-607, thereby inhibiting the expression of IFNG. The hsa_circ_0000566, hsa_circ_0001844, hsa_circ_0005408, hsa_circ_0007587, hsa_circ_0086710, IFNG and has-miR-607 could be used as new diagnostic targets for TB. These findings not only provided a new perspective on the potential role of the ceRNA regulatory network in pulmonary TB but also provided new targets and new methods for diagnosing TB.

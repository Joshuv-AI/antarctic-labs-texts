The Multidimensional Quadratic Phase Fourier Transform: Theoretical Analysis and Applications
Sarga Varghese, Gita Rani Mahato, Manab Kundu

Abstract
. The quadratic phase Fourier transform (QPFT) is a generalization of several well known integral transforms, including the linear canonical transform (LCT), fractional Fourier transform (FrFT), and Fourier transform (FT). This paper introduces the multidimensional QPFT and investigates its theoretical properties, including Parseval’s identity and inversion theorems. Generalized convolutions and correlation for multiple variables, extending the conventional convolution for single-variable functions, is proposed within the QPFT setting. Additionally, Boas type theorem for the multidimensional QPFT is established. As applications, multiplicative filter design and the solution of integral equations using the proposed convolution operation are explored.

The integral equations, one of the most useful mathematical tools in both pure and applied mathematics, has enormous applications in many physical problems. In 1825 Abel, an Italian mathematician, first produced an integral equation in connection with the famous tautochrone problem. Singular integral equations encountered by Abel can easily be solved by using integral transforms. Also, many initial and boundary value problems associated with ordinary differential equation (ODE) and partial differential equation (PDE) can be transformed into problems of solving some approximate integral equations. The development of science has led to the formulation of physical laws that are often expressed as differential equations, which play a crucial role in solving engineering problems. These differential equations can be transformed into equivalent integral equations. Integral equations are equally important, as many physical problems are directly governed by them, and they can also be converted back into differential equations. Solving integral equations is essential for addressing complex physical and engineering challenges, providing a versatile framework for modeling and analyzing real-world phenomena. Their ability to bridge differential equations and practical applications highlights their significance in scientific and engineering research

The mathematical framework of multidimensional systems involves functions and polynomials in several complex or real variables, which has been instrumental in advancing areas like iterative learning control, distributed network synthesis, and nonlinear system analysis through multidimensional transforms. Preliminaries

This section offers a brief summary of the basic concepts and notations necessary for

For each
p\in[1,\infty)
, we have

\displaystyle L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})=\bigg\{f~:~||f||_{p}=\Big(\frac{1}{\sqrt{(2\pi)}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|f(\boldsymbol{x})|^{p}d\boldsymbol{x}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}\bigg\},

which is the space of all
p
-integrable functions on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

Definition 2.1
.

For a function
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, the multidimensional FT of
f
is defined by

\displaystyle\hat{f}(\boldsymbol{x})=\frac{1}{(\sqrt{2\pi})^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(\boldsymbol{t})e^{-i\boldsymbol{t.x}}d\boldsymbol{t},~~\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

Definition 2.2
.

For a function
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
, the quadratic phase Fourier transform (QPFT) of
f
, for a given set of parameters
\Lambda=\{a,b,c,d,e\}
,
b\neq 0
, is defined by
[
16
]

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{\Lambda}(\omega)=\tilde{F}(\omega)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{K}_{\Lambda}(\omega,x)f(x)dx,\hskip 8.53581pt\omega\in\mathbb{R},

(2.1)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{\Lambda}(\omega,x)=\sqrt{\frac{b}{2\pi i}}~e^{i(ax^{2}+bx\omega+c\omega^{2}+dx+e\omega)}

(2.2)

is the kernel of QPFT and
a,b,c,d,e\in\mathbb{R}
.
Then the inversion of QPFT is given by

\displaystyle f(x)=\Big(\mathcal{Q}_{-\Lambda}(\mathcal{Q}_{\Lambda}f)\Big)(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{K}_{-\Lambda}(\omega,x)(\mathcal{Q}_{\Lambda}f)(\omega)d\omega,

(2.3)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{-\Lambda}(\omega,x)=\sqrt{\frac{bi}{2\pi}}~e^{-i(ax^{2}+bx\omega+c\omega^{2}+dx+e\omega)}.

Definition 2.3
.

The multidimensional QPFT of a function
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is defined as

\displaystyle(\mathcal{Q}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}f)(\boldsymbol{\omega})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\mathcal{K}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}(\boldsymbol{\omega},\boldsymbol{x})f(\boldsymbol{x})d\boldsymbol{x},

where

\boldsymbol{\omega}=(\omega_{1},\omega_{2},...,\omega_{N}),\boldsymbol{x}=(x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{N})\in\mathbb{R}^{N},\mathcal{K}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}(\boldsymbol{\omega},\boldsymbol{x})=\prod_{k=1}^{N}\mathcal{K}_{\Lambda_{k}}(\omega_{k},x_{k}),\\
\boldsymbol{\Lambda}=(\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2},...,\Lambda_{N})\text{ and }\Lambda_{k}=(a_{k},b_{k},c_{k},d_{k},e_{k}),k=1,2,...,N.

Throughout this paper, we use bold letters to denote N-tuple, for instance,
\boldsymbol{a}=(a_{1},a_{2},...,a_{N})
. For
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, denote

\displaystyle e_{\lambda}^{\boldsymbol{a,d}}(\boldsymbol{x})=e^{i\lambda\sum_{k=1}^{N}(a_{k}x_{k}^{2}+d_{k}x_{k})}

(2.4)

for multidimensional domain.
Obviously,
e_{\lambda}^{-\boldsymbol{a,d}}(\boldsymbol{x})=e_{-\lambda}^{\boldsymbol{a,d}}(\boldsymbol{x})
and then the multidimensional QPFT can be rewritten as

\displaystyle(\mathcal{Q}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}f)(\boldsymbol{\omega})=C_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}e_{1}^{\boldsymbol{c,e}}(\boldsymbol{\omega})\widehat{(e_{1}^{\boldsymbol{a,d}}f(\boldsymbol{x}))}(\boldsymbol{b\omega}),

where

\displaystyle C_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}=\prod_{k=1}^{N}\sqrt{\frac{b_{k}}{i}}

(2.5)

and
\widehat{(e_{1}^{\boldsymbol{a,d}}f(\boldsymbol{x}))}
is the Fourier transform of
(e_{1}^{\boldsymbol{a,d}}f(\boldsymbol{x}))
. Using this notation, the kernel can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}(\boldsymbol{\omega},\boldsymbol{x})=\frac{C_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}}{(2\pi)^{\frac{N}{2}}}e_{1}^{\boldsymbol{a,d}}(\boldsymbol{x})e_{1}^{\boldsymbol{c,e}}(\boldsymbol{\omega})e^{i\sum_{k=1}^{N}b_{k}x_{k}\omega_{k}}.

Theorem 2.4
.

For functions
f,g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, the inner product of multidimensional QPFT of two functions
f
and
g
is same as the inner product of
f
and
g
.

i.e.,

\displaystyle\big<f,g\big>

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big<\mathcal{Q}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}f,\mathcal{Q}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}g\big>.

As the proof is straightforward, we omit the proof part.

Corollary 2.5
.

If we take
f=g
, the equation takes the particular form as

\displaystyle||f||_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle||\mathcal{Q}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}f||_{2}^{2}.

In this Section, we discuss analogous type convolution and correlation theorems for multidimensional QPFT

Definition 3.1
.

For the functions
f,g\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
, we can define convolution by
[
16
]

\displaystyle(f*g)(x)=f(x)*g(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(\tau)g(x-\tau)d\tau,

(3.1)

which satisfies

\displaystyle\hat{(f*g)}(\omega)=(\hat{f})(\omega)(\hat{g})(\omega),

(3.2)

where
\hat{f}
is Fourier transform of
f
.

Analogous to this, the convolution in multidimensional case is defined as follows.

Definition 3.2
.

For the functions
f,g\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, we can define convolution by

\displaystyle(f\star g)(\boldsymbol{x})=f(\boldsymbol{x})\star g(\boldsymbol{x})=\frac{1}{(\sqrt{2\pi})^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(\boldsymbol{\tau})g(\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{\tau})d\boldsymbol{\tau},

(3.3)

which satisfies

\displaystyle\widehat{(f\star g)}(\boldsymbol{\omega})=(\hat{f})(\boldsymbol{\omega})(\hat{g})(\boldsymbol{\omega}),

(3.4)

where
\hat{f}
is the multidimensional FT of
f
.

In the following two subsections, we define the multidimensional convolutions and correlation as extensions of those proposed by Prasad et al. in
[

3.3

3.1.
Convolution: type 1

Theorem 3.3
.

[
16
]

Let
f,g\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
. Assume
\tilde{F}(\omega)=(\mathcal{Q}_{\Lambda}f)(\omega)
and
\tilde{G}(\omega)=(\mathcal{Q}_{\Lambda}g)(\omega)
, then

\displaystyle(\mathcal{Q}_{\Lambda}(f\otimes g))(\omega)=e^{-i(c\omega^{2}+e\omega)}\tilde{F}(\omega)\tilde{G}(\omega),

where

\displaystyle(f\otimes g)(x)=\sqrt{\frac{b}{i}}e^{-iax^{2}}\Big(\big(f(t)e^{iax^{2}}\big)*\big(g(x)e^{iax^{2}}\big)\Big).

Building on this, now we define the multidimensional extension of this convolution.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
f(\boldsymbol{x})
,
g(\boldsymbol{x})\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{N}})
. Then we define the convolution for multidimensional QPFT as follows

\displaystyle(f{\otimes}g)(\boldsymbol{x})=\sqrt{\frac{\prod_{k=1}^{N}b_{k}}{(i)^{N}}}e^{-i(\sum_{k=1}^{N}a_{k}x_{k}^{2})}\Big(\big(f(\boldsymbol{x})e^{i(\sum_{k=1}^{N}a_{k}x_{k}^{2})}\big)\star\big(g(\boldsymbol{x})e^{i(\sum_{k=1}^{N}a_{k}x_{k}^{2})}\big)\Big).

Theorem 3.5
.

Let the functions
f
and
g
be in
L^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{N}})
. Then

\displaystyle(f{\otimes}g)(\boldsymbol{x})\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})~~~~and~~~~||f{\oplus}g||_{1}\leq\sqrt{(2\pi)^{N}\prod_{k=1}^{N}b_{k}}~||f||_{1}||g||_{1}.

Proof.

Using the Definition
3.4
and Fubini’s theorem, we get

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|(f{\otimes}~g)(\boldsymbol{x})|d\boldsymbol{x}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\small\sqrt{\prod_{k=1}^{N}b_{k}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\bigg|e^{-i(\sum_{k=1}^{N}a_{k}x_{k}^{2})}\Big(\big(f(\boldsymbol{x})e^{i(\sum_{k=1}^{N}a_{k}x_{k}^{2})}\big)

\displaystyle\star\big(g(\boldsymbol{x})e^{i(\sum_{k=1}^{N}a_{k}x_{k}^{2})}\big)\Big)\bigg|d\boldsymbol{x}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sqrt{\prod_{k=1}^{N}b_{k}}\frac{1}{(\sqrt{2\pi})^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\big|f(\boldsymbol{\tau})\big|\bigg[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\bigg|g(\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{\tau})\bigg|d\boldsymbol{x}\bigg]d\boldsymbol{\tau}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{(2\pi)^{N}\prod_{k=1}^{N}b_{k}}||f||_{1}||g||_{1}.

This completes the theorem. ∎

Having established the definition and key properties, we are now in a position to state the convolution theorem for multidimensional QPFT.

Theorem 3.6
.

(Convolution theorem for multidimensional QPFT) Let
\mathcal{Q}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}f
and
\mathcal{Q}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}g
be the multidimensional QPFTs of
f,g\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
respectively. Then

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}(f\otimes g)(\boldsymbol{\omega})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-i\sum_{k=1}^{N}(c_{k}\omega_{k}^{2}+e_{k}\omega_{k})}\mathcal{Q}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}(g)(\boldsymbol{\omega})\mathcal{Q}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}(f)(\boldsymbol{\omega}).

Proof.

By using the Definitions
2.3
and
3.4
, along with some calculations, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}(f\otimes g)(\boldsymbol{\omega})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}\bigg(\sqrt{\frac{\prod_{k=1}^{N}b_{k}}{(2\pi i)^{N}}}e^{-i(\sum_{k=1}^{N}a_{k}x_{k}^{2})}

\displaystyle\times\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(\boldsymbol{\tau})e^{i(\sum_{k=1}^{N}a_{k}\tau_{k}^{2})}g(\boldsymbol{x-\tau})e^{i(\sum_{k=1}^{N}a_{k}(x_{k}-\tau_{k})^{2})}d\boldsymbol{\tau}\Big)\bigg)(\boldsymbol{\omega})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{\prod_{k=1}^{N}b_{k}}{(2\pi i)^{N}}}e^{i\sum_{k=1}^{N}(c_{k}\omega_{k}^{2}+e_{k}\omega_{k})}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(\boldsymbol{\tau})e^{i(\sum_{k=1}^{N}a_{k}\tau_{k}^{2})}

\displaystyle\times\bigg(\sqrt{\frac{\prod_{k=1}^{N}b_{k}}{(2\pi i)^{N}}}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}e^{i\sum_{k=1}^{N}(b_{k}x_{k}\omega_{k}+d_{k}x_{k})}g(\boldsymbol{x-\tau})e^{i(\sum_{k=1}^{N}a_{k}(x_{k}-\tau_{k})^{2})}\Big)d\boldsymbol{x}\bigg)d\boldsymbol{\tau}.

Setting
\boldsymbol{x-\tau}=\boldsymbol{\lambda}
, we obtain the following expression.

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}(f\otimes g)(\boldsymbol{\omega})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{\prod_{k=1}^{N}b_{k}}{(2\pi i)^{N}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(\boldsymbol{\tau})e^{i(\sum_{k=1}^{N}a_{k}\tau_{k}^{2}+b_{k}\tau_{k}\omega_{k}+d_{k}\tau_{k})}

\displaystyle\times\bigg(\sqrt{\frac{\prod_{k=1}^{N}b_{k}}{(2\pi i)^{N}}}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}e^{i\sum_{k=1}^{N}(a_{k}\lambda_{k}^{2}+b_{k}\lambda_{k}\omega_{k}+c_{k}\omega_{k}^{2}+d_{k}\lambda_{k}+e_{k}\omega_{k})}g(\boldsymbol{\lambda})\Big)d\boldsymbol{\lambda}\bigg)d\boldsymbol{\tau}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{\prod_{k=1}^{N}b_{k}}{(2\pi i)^{N}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(\boldsymbol{\tau})e^{i(\sum_{k=1}^{N}a_{k}\tau_{k}^{2}+b_{k}\tau_{k}\omega_{k}+d_{k}\tau_{k})}\mathcal{Q}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}(g)(\boldsymbol{\omega})d\boldsymbol{\tau}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-i\sum_{k=1}^{N}(c_{k}\omega_{k}^{2}+e_{k}\omega_{k})}\mathcal{Q}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}(g)(\boldsymbol{\omega})\mathcal{Q}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}(f)(\boldsymbol{\omega}).

Hence, the proof is complete.
∎

Convolution: type 2

Definition 3.7
.

[
16
]
(New convolution and correlation for QPFT)
The new convolution operation
\oplus
of QPFT for two signals
f,g\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
is defined by

\displaystyle(f\oplus g)(x)=\sqrt{\frac{b}{\pi i}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(\tau)g(\sqrt{2}x-\tau)e^{2ai(\frac{x}{\sqrt{2}}-\tau)^{2}+i(\sqrt{2}-1)dx}d\tau,

and a dual operation
\ominus
of the new convolution operation is defined as

\displaystyle(f\ominus g)(x)=\sqrt{\frac{bi}{\pi}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(\tau)g(\sqrt{2}x-\tau)e^{2ci(\frac{x}{\sqrt{2}}-\tau)^{2}-i(\sqrt{2}-1)ex}d\tau.

Theorem 3.8
.

[
16
]
Let
\mathcal{Q}_{\Lambda}f
and
\mathcal{Q}_{\Lambda}g
be the multidimensional QPFTs of
f,g\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
respectively. Then

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{\Lambda}(f\oplus g)(\omega)=e^{-i(\sqrt{2}-1)e\omega}\mathcal{Q}_{\Lambda}(f)(\frac{\omega}{2})\mathcal{Q}_{\Lambda}(g)(\frac{\omega}{2}),

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{\Lambda}\Big[e^{i(\sqrt{2}-1)dx}f\big(\frac{x}{\sqrt{2}}\big)g\big(\frac{x}{\sqrt{2}}\big)\Big](\omega)=\big(\mathcal{Q}_{\Lambda}(f)\ominus\mathcal{Q}_{\Lambda}(g)\big)(\omega).

We now define analogous convolution and a convolution
theorem for functions on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
to the above given theorem.

Definition 3.9
.

(New convolution and correlation for multidimensional QPFT)
The new convolution operation
\oplus
of QPFT for two signals
f,g\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is defined by

\displaystyle(f\boldsymbol{\oplus}g)(\boldsymbol{x})=\sqrt{\frac{\prod_{k=1}^{N}b}{(\pi i)^{N}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(\boldsymbol{\tau})g(\sqrt{2}\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{\tau})e^{\sum_{k=1}^{N}2ia_{k}(\frac{x_{k}}{\sqrt{2}}-\tau_{k})^{2}+i(\sqrt{2}-1)d_{k}x_{k}}d\boldsymbol{\tau},

of the new convolution operation is defined as

\displaystyle(f\boldsymbol{\ominus}g)(\boldsymbol{x})=\sqrt{\frac{i^{N}\prod_{k=1}^{N}b_{k}}{(\pi)^{N}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(\boldsymbol{\tau})g(\sqrt{2}\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{\tau})e^{-(\sum_{k=1}^{N}2c_{k}i(\frac{x_{k}}{\sqrt{2}}-\tau_{k})^{2}+i(\sqrt{2}-1)e_{k}x_{k})}d\boldsymbol{\tau_{k}}.

Theorem 3.10
.

Let
\mathcal{Q}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}f
and
\mathcal{Q}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}g
be the multidimensional QPFTs of
f,g\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
respectively. Then

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}(f\boldsymbol{\oplus}g)(\boldsymbol{\omega})=e^{-i\sum_{k=1}^{N}(\sqrt{2}-1)e_{k}\omega_{k}}\mathcal{Q}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}(f)(\frac{\boldsymbol{\omega}}{2})\mathcal{Q}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}(g)(\frac{\boldsymbol{\omega}}{2}),

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}\Big[e^{i\sum_{k=1}^{N}(\sqrt{2}-1)d_{k}x_{k}}f\big(\frac{\boldsymbol{x}}{\sqrt{2}}\big)g\big(\frac{\boldsymbol{x}}{\sqrt{2}}\big)\Big](\boldsymbol{\omega})=\big(\mathcal{Q}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}(f)\boldsymbol{\ominus}\mathcal{Q}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}(g)\big)(\boldsymbol{\omega}).

Proof.

By invoking Definitions
2.3
and
3.9
and carrying out the necessary computations, we obtain

\displaystyle\big(\mathcal{Q}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}(f\boldsymbol{\oplus}g)(\boldsymbol{x})\big)(\boldsymbol{\omega})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\mathcal{K}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}(\boldsymbol{\omega},\boldsymbol{x})\sqrt{\frac{\prod_{k=1}^{N}b_{k}}{(\pi i)^{N}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(\boldsymbol{\tau})g(\sqrt{2}\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{\tau})e^{\sum_{k=1}^{N}2ia_{k}(\frac{x_{k}}{\sqrt{2}}-\tau_{k})^{2}+i(\sqrt{2}-1)d_{k}x_{k}}d\boldsymbol{\tau}d\boldsymbol{x}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{\prod_{k=1}^{N}b_{k}}{(\pi i)^{N}}}\sqrt{\frac{\prod_{k=1}^{N}b_{k}}{(2\pi i)^{N}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}e^{i\sum_{k=1}^{N}(a_{k}x_{k}^{2}+b_{k}x_{k}\omega_{k}+c_{k}\omega_{k}^{2}+d_{k}x_{k}+e_{k}\omega_{k})}

\displaystyle f(\boldsymbol{\tau})g(\sqrt{2}\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{\tau})e^{\sum_{k=1}^{N}2ia_{k}(\frac{x_{k}}{\sqrt{2}}-\tau_{k})^{2}+i(\sqrt{2}-1)d_{k}x_{k}}d\boldsymbol{\tau}d\boldsymbol{x}.

Changing the variable

\displaystyle\big(\mathcal{Q}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}(f\boldsymbol{\oplus}g)(\boldsymbol{x})\big)(\boldsymbol{\omega})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{\prod_{k=1}^{N}b_{k}}{(\pi i)^{N}}}\sqrt{\frac{\prod_{k=1}^{N}b_{k}}{(2\pi i)^{N}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}e^{i\sum_{k=1}^{N}(a_{k}\big(\frac{\tau_{k}^{2}}{2}+\frac{\epsilon_{k}^{2}}{2}+\tau_{k}\epsilon_{k}\big)+b_{k}\big(\frac{\tau_{k}+\epsilon_{k}}{\sqrt{2}}\big)\omega_{k}+c_{k}\omega_{k}^{2}+d_{k}\big(\frac{\tau_{k}+\epsilon_{k}}{\sqrt{2}}\big)+e_{k}\omega_{k})}

\displaystyle f(\boldsymbol{\tau})g(\boldsymbol{\epsilon})e^{\sum_{k=1}^{N}ia_{k}\big(\frac{\epsilon_{k}^{2}-2\epsilon_{k}\tau_{k}+\tau_{k}^{2}}{2}\big)+i(\sqrt{2}-1)d_{k}\big(\frac{\tau_{k}+\epsilon_{k}}{\sqrt{2}}\big)}d\boldsymbol{\tau}d\boldsymbol{\epsilon}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\small\sqrt{\frac{\prod_{k=1}^{N}b_{k}}{(\pi i)^{N}}}\sqrt{\frac{\prod_{k=1}^{N}b_{k}}{(2\pi i)^{N}}}e^{i\sum_{k=1}^{N}(1-\sqrt{2})e_{k}\omega_{k}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}e^{i\sum_{k=1}^{N}(a_{k}\tau_{k}^{2}+b_{k}\big(\frac{\tau_{k}}{\sqrt{2}}\big)\omega_{k}+c_{k}\big(\frac{\omega_{k}}{\sqrt{2}}\big)^{2}+d_{k}\tau_{k}+e_{k}\frac{\omega_{k}}{\sqrt{2}})}f(\boldsymbol{\tau})d\boldsymbol{\tau}

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}e^{i\sum_{k=1}^{N}(a_{k}\epsilon_{k}^{2}+b_{k}\big(\frac{\epsilon_{k}}{\sqrt{2}}\big)\omega_{k}+c_{k}\big(\frac{\omega_{k}}{\sqrt{2}}\big)^{2}+d_{k}\epsilon_{k}+e_{k}\frac{\omega_{k}}{\sqrt{2}})}g(\boldsymbol{\epsilon})d\boldsymbol{\epsilon}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{i\sum_{k=1}^{N}(1-\sqrt{2})e_{k}\omega_{k}}\big(\mathcal{Q}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}(f)\big)(\frac{\boldsymbol{\omega}}{\sqrt{2}})\big(\mathcal{Q}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}(g)\big)(\frac{\boldsymbol{\omega}}{\sqrt{2}}),

which establishes the first part of the proof. We now proceed to the second part.

\displaystyle\big(\mathcal{Q}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}(f)\boldsymbol{\ominus}\mathcal{Q}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}(g)\big)(\boldsymbol{\omega})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hskip-2.84544pt\small\sqrt{\frac{i^{N}\prod_{k=1}^{N}b_{k}}{(\pi)^{N}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\mathcal{Q}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}(f)(\boldsymbol{v})\mathcal{Q}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}(g)(\sqrt{2}\boldsymbol{\omega}-\boldsymbol{v})e^{-(\sum_{k=1}^{N}2c_{k}i(\frac{\omega_{k}}{\sqrt{2}}-v_{k})^{2}+i(\sqrt{2}-1)e_{k}\omega_{k})}d\boldsymbol{v}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{i^{N}\prod_{k=1}^{N}b_{k}}{(\pi)^{N}}}\sqrt{\frac{\prod_{k=1}^{N}b_{k}}{(2\pi i)^{N}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}e^{i\sum_{k=1}^{N}(a_{k}x_{k}^{2}+b_{k}x_{k}v_{k}+c_{k}v_{k}^{2}+d_{k}x_{k}+e_{k}v_{k})}f(\boldsymbol{x})

\displaystyle\times\mathcal{Q}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}(g)(\sqrt{2}\boldsymbol{\omega}-\boldsymbol{v})e^{-(\sum_{k=1}^{N}2c_{k}i(\frac{\omega_{k}}{\sqrt{2}}-v_{k})^{2}+i(\sqrt{2}-1)e_{k}\omega_{k})}d\boldsymbol{v}d\boldsymbol{x}.

Changing the variable
\boldsymbol{\xi}=\sqrt{2}\boldsymbol{\omega}-\boldsymbol{v}
to get

\displaystyle\big(\mathcal{Q}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}(f)\boldsymbol{\ominus}\mathcal{Q}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}(g)\big)(\boldsymbol{\omega})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{\prod_{k=1}^{N}b_{k}}{(\pi i)^{N}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Big[e^{i\sum_{k=1}^{N}(2a_{k}x_{k}^{2}+\sqrt{2}b_{k}x_{k}\omega_{k}+c_{k}\omega_{k}^{2}+2d_{k}x_{k}+e_{k}\omega_{k})}f(\boldsymbol{x})

\displaystyle\times\sqrt{\frac{i^{N}\prod_{k=1}^{N}b_{k}}{(2\pi)^{N}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\mathcal{Q}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}(g)(\boldsymbol{\xi})e^{-i\sum_{k=1}^{N}a_{k}x_{k}^{2}+b_{k}\xi_{k}x_{k}+c_{k}\xi_{k}^{2}+d_{k}x_{k}+e_{k}\omega_{k}}d\boldsymbol{\xi}\Big]d\boldsymbol{x}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{\prod_{k=1}^{N}b_{k}}{(\pi i)^{N}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}e^{i\sum_{k=1}^{N}(2a_{k}x_{k}^{2}+\sqrt{2}b_{k}x_{k}\omega_{k}+c_{k}\omega_{k}^{2}+2d_{k}x_{k}+e_{k}\omega_{k})}f(\boldsymbol{x})g(\boldsymbol{x})d\boldsymbol{x}.

Substituting
\boldsymbol{x}=\frac{\boldsymbol{t}}{\sqrt{2}}
in the above equation gives

\displaystyle\big(\mathcal{Q}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}(f)\ominus\mathcal{Q}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}(g)\big)(\boldsymbol{\omega})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hskip-4.26773pt\small\sqrt{\frac{\prod_{k=1}^{N}b_{k}}{(2\pi i)^{N}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}e^{i\sum_{k=1}^{N}(a_{k}t_{k}^{2}+b_{k}t_{k}\omega_{k}+c_{k}\omega_{k}^{2}+d_{k}t_{k}+e_{k}\omega_{k})}e^{i\sum_{k=1}^{N}(\sqrt{2}-1)d_{k}t_{k}}f\big(\frac{\boldsymbol{t}}{\sqrt{2}}\big)g\big(\frac{\boldsymbol{t}}{\sqrt{2}}\big)d\boldsymbol{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}\big[e^{i\sum_{k=1}^{N}(\sqrt{2}-1)d_{k}t_{k}}f\big(\frac{\boldsymbol{t}}{\sqrt{2}}\big)g\big(\frac{\boldsymbol{t}}{\sqrt{2}}\big)\big].

Thus, the proof is established.
∎

3.3.
Convolution: type 3

In this subsection, we introduce a more generalized version of convolution and establish corresponding convolution
theorem in the multidimensional setting, which we use for proving the inversion
theorem.

Definition 3.11
.

For
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
g\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, we define

\displaystyle(f{\otimes}_{\tiny\boldsymbol{\Lambda},\lambda}~g)(\boldsymbol{x})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\lambda|^{\frac{N}{2}}C_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}e^{a,d}_{-\lambda^{2}}(\boldsymbol{x})\big[(e^{a,d}_{\lambda^{2}}f)*(e^{a,d}_{\lambda^{2}}g)\big](\boldsymbol{x}),

where
e^{a,d}_{-\lambda^{2}}(\boldsymbol{x})
and
C_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}
are as in equations (
2.4
) and (
2.5
), respectively.

Based on the given definition, we now formulate the convolution theorem.

Theorem 3.12
.

Let
f,g\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
\mathcal{Q}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda^{\prime}}}f,\mathcal{Q}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda^{\prime}}}g\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, where
\boldsymbol{\Lambda^{\prime}}=\{\Lambda_{1}^{\prime},\Lambda_{2}^{\prime},...,\Lambda_{N}^{\prime}\}\text{ and }\Lambda_{k}^{\prime}=(a_{k}^{\prime},b_{k}^{\prime},c_{k}^{\prime},d_{k}^{\prime},e_{k}^{\prime})
with
a_{k}^{\prime}=\lambda^{2}a_{k}
,

b_{k}^{\prime}=\lambda^{2}b_{k}
,
c_{k}^{\prime}=\lambda^{2}c_{k}
,
d_{k}^{\prime}=\lambda^{2}d_{k}
,
e_{k}^{\prime}=\lambda^{2}e_{k}
. Then

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda^{\prime}}}(f\otimes_{\tiny\boldsymbol{\Lambda},\lambda}g)(\boldsymbol{\omega})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{c,e}_{-\lambda^{2}}(\boldsymbol{\omega})\mathcal{Q}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda^{\prime}}}(g)(\boldsymbol{\omega})\mathcal{Q}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda^{\prime}}}(f)(\boldsymbol{\omega}).

Proof.

From Definition
3.11
and equations (
2.4
) and (
2.5
), it follows that

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda^{\prime}}}(f\otimes_{\tiny\boldsymbol{\Lambda},\lambda}g)(\boldsymbol{\omega})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda^{\prime}}}\bigg(\sqrt{\frac{\prod_{k=1}^{N}b_{k}^{\prime}}{(i)^{N}}}e^{-i(\sum_{k=1}^{N}a_{k}^{\prime}x_{k}^{2}+d_{k}^{\prime}x_{k})}\Big(\big(f(\boldsymbol{x})e^{i(\sum_{k=1}^{N}a_{k}^{\prime}x_{k}^{2}+d_{k}^{\prime}x_{k})}\big)\star\big(g(\boldsymbol{x})e^{i(\sum_{k=1}^{N}a_{k}^{\prime}x_{k}^{2}+d_{k}^{\prime}x_{k})}\big)\Big)\bigg)(\boldsymbol{\omega})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{\prod_{k=1}^{N}b_{k}^{\prime}}{(2\pi i)^{N}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}e^{i\sum_{k=1}^{N}(a_{k}^{\prime}x_{k}^{2}+b_{k}^{\prime}x_{k}\omega_{k}+c_{k}^{\prime}\omega_{k}^{2}+d_{k}^{\prime}x_{k}+e_{k}^{\prime}\omega_{k})}\bigg(\sqrt{\frac{\prod_{k=1}^{N}b_{k}^{\prime}}{(2\pi i)^{N}}}e^{-i(\sum_{k=1}^{N}a_{k}^{\prime}x_{k}^{2}+d_{k}^{\prime}x_{k})}

\displaystyle\hskip-8.5359pt\times\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(\boldsymbol{\tau})e^{i(\sum_{k=1}^{N}a_{k}^{\prime}\tau_{k}^{2}+d_{k}^{\prime}\tau_{k})}g(\boldsymbol{x-\tau})e^{i(\sum_{k=1}^{N}a_{k}^{\prime}(x_{k}-\tau_{k})^{2}+d_{k}^{\prime}(x_{k}-\tau_{k}))}d\boldsymbol{\tau}\Big)\bigg)d\boldsymbol{x}.

Applying Fubini’s theorem, we can further simplify,

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda^{\prime}}}\big((f\otimes_{\tiny\boldsymbol{\Lambda},\lambda}g)(\boldsymbol{x})\big)(\boldsymbol{\omega})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{\prod_{k=1}^{N}b_{k}^{\prime}}{(2\pi i)^{N}}}e^{i\sum_{k=1}^{N}(c_{k}^{\prime}\omega_{k}^{2}+e_{k}^{\prime}\omega_{k})}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(\boldsymbol{\tau})e^{i(\sum_{k=1}^{N}a_{k}^{\prime}\tau_{k}^{2}+d_{k}^{\prime}\tau_{k})}

\displaystyle\times\bigg(\sqrt{\frac{\prod_{k=1}^{N}b_{k}^{\prime}}{(2\pi i)^{N}}}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}e^{i\sum_{k=1}^{N}(b_{k}^{\prime}x_{k}\omega_{k})}g(\boldsymbol{x-\tau})e^{i(\sum_{k=1}^{N}a_{k}^{\prime}(x_{k}-\tau_{k})^{2}+d_{k}^{\prime}(x_{k}-\tau_{k}))}\Big)d\boldsymbol{x}\bigg)d\boldsymbol{\tau}.

With the substitution
\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{\tau}=\boldsymbol{u}
, we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^{\prime}}(f\otimes g)(\boldsymbol{\omega})\Big)d\boldsymbol{u}\bigg)d\boldsymbol{\tau}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{\prod_{k=1}^{N}b_{k}^{\prime}}{(2\pi i)^{N}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(\boldsymbol{\tau})e^{i(\sum_{k=1}^{N}a_{k}^{\prime}\tau_{k}^{2}+b_{k}^{\prime}\tau_{k}\omega_{k}+d_{k}^{\prime}\tau_{k})}

\displaystyle\times\bigg(\sqrt{\frac{\prod_{k=1}^{N}b_{k}^{\prime}}{(2\pi i)^{N}}}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}e^{i\sum_{k=1}^{N}(a_{k}^{\prime}u_{k}^{2}+b_{k}^{\prime}u_{k}\omega_{k}+c_{k}^{\prime}\omega_{k}^{2}+d_{k}^{\prime}u_{k}+e_{k}^{\prime}\omega_{k})}g(\boldsymbol{u})\Big)d\boldsymbol{u}\bigg)d\boldsymbol{\tau}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{\prod_{k=1}^{N}b_{k}^{\prime}}{(2\pi i)^{N}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(\boldsymbol{\tau})e^{i(\sum_{k=1}^{N}a_{k}^{\prime}\tau_{k}^{2}+b_{k}^{\prime}\tau_{k}\omega_{k}+d_{k}^{\prime}\tau_{k})}\mathcal{Q}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda^{\prime}}}(g)(\boldsymbol{\omega})d\boldsymbol{\tau}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-i\sum_{k=1}^{N}(c_{k}^{\prime}\omega_{k}^{2}+e_{k}^{\prime}\omega_{k})}\mathcal{Q}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda^{\prime}}}(g)(\boldsymbol{\omega})\mathcal{Q}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda^{\prime}}}(f)(\boldsymbol{\omega})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e_{-\lambda^{2}}^{c,e}(\boldsymbol{\omega})\mathcal{Q}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda^{\prime}}}(g)(\boldsymbol{\omega})\mathcal{Q}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda^{\prime}}}(f)(\boldsymbol{\omega}).

This concludes the proof.
∎

Definition 3.13
.

Let
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
\boldsymbol{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
\boldsymbol{\omega}\in(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\})^{N}
. Define
(T_{\boldsymbol{t}}f)(\boldsymbol{x})=f(\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{t})
and
E_{\lambda^{2}}^{a\boldsymbol{t}}(\boldsymbol{x})=e^{2i\lambda^{2}(\sum_{k=1}^{N}a_{k}x_{k}t_{k})},E_{\lambda^{2}}^{d}(\boldsymbol{t})=e^{2i\lambda^{2}(\sum_{k=1}^{N}d_{k}t_{k})}.

With these notations in place, we now state some properties of the previously defined convolution
3.11
.

Theorem 3.14
.

Let the functions
f
,
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
be in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
g
,
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
be in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
\gamma
be a complex number,
\omega
be a non-zero real number and
t\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Then

(1)

(f_{1}+f_{2})\otimes_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda},\lambda}h=(f_{1}\otimes_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda},\lambda}h)+(f_{2}\otimes_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda},\lambda}h)
; distributivity,

(2)

\gamma(f\otimes_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda},\lambda}g)=(\gamma f)\otimes_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda},\lambda}g=f\otimes_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda},\lambda}(\gamma g)
; scalar multiplication,

(3)

[f\otimes_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda},\lambda}(g_{1}\otimes_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda},\lambda}g_{2})]=[(f\otimes_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda},\lambda}g_{1})\otimes_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda},\lambda}g_{2}]
; associativity,

(4)

f\otimes_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda},\lambda}g=g\otimes_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda},\lambda}f
; commutativity ,

(5)

\tau_{\boldsymbol{t}}(f\otimes_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda},\lambda}g)(\boldsymbol{x})=\big[(\tau_{\boldsymbol{t}}f)\otimes_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda},\lambda}(E_{\lambda^{2}}^{a\boldsymbol{t}}g)\big](\boldsymbol{x}).

(6)

Define a function
P_{k}:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
by
P_{k}(\boldsymbol{x})=x_{k}
,
k=1,2,...,N.

(a)

If
P_{k}f,\partial_{x_{k}}f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
or
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
then

\displaystyle\partial_{x_{k}}(f\otimes_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda},\lambda}g)(\boldsymbol{x})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2a_{k}i\lambda^{2}\big[(P_{k}f\otimes_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda},\lambda}g)(\boldsymbol{x})-P_{k}(f\otimes_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda},\lambda}g)(\boldsymbol{x})\big]

\displaystyle+(\partial_{x_{k}}f\otimes_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda},\lambda}g)(\boldsymbol{x}).

(b)

If
P_{k}g
and
\partial_{x_{k}}g
are in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, then

\displaystyle\partial_{x_{k}}(f\otimes_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda},\lambda}g)(\boldsymbol{x})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2a_{k}i\lambda^{2}\big[(P_{k}f\otimes_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda},\lambda}g)(\boldsymbol{x})-P_{k}(f\otimes_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda},\lambda}g)(\boldsymbol{x})\big]

\displaystyle+(\partial_{x_{k}}f\otimes_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda},\lambda}g)(\boldsymbol{x}).

Proof.

The proofs of statements (1) to (4) follow directly from the corresponding results in the classical convolution framework, requiring only minor modifications for the generalized structure. Therefore, we omit the detailed proofs.

(5) To begin, we observe that

\displaystyle\tau_{\boldsymbol{t}}(e^{\boldsymbol{a},\boldsymbol{d}}_{-\lambda^{2}}(\boldsymbol{x}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-i\lambda^{2}(\sum_{k=1}^{N}a_{k}(x_{k}-t_{k})^{2}+d_{k}(x_{k}-t_{k}))}

(3.5)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-i\lambda^{2}(\sum_{k=1}^{N}a_{k}x_{k}^{2}+d_{k}x_{k}+a_{k}t_{k}^{2}+d_{k}t_{k}-2(a_{k}x_{k}t_{k}+d_{k}t_{k})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-i\lambda^{2}(\sum_{k=1}^{N}a_{k}x_{k}^{2}+d_{k}x_{k})}e^{-i\lambda^{2}(\sum_{k=1}^{N}a_{k}t_{k}^{2}+d_{k}t_{k})}e^{2i\lambda^{2}(\sum_{k=1}^{N}a_{k}x_{k}t_{k})}e^{2i\lambda^{2}(\sum_{k=1}^{N}d_{k}t_{k})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{\boldsymbol{a},\boldsymbol{d}}_{-\lambda^{2}}(\boldsymbol{x})e^{\boldsymbol{a},\boldsymbol{d}}_{-\lambda^{2}}(\boldsymbol{t})E_{\lambda^{2}}^{\boldsymbol{at}}(\boldsymbol{x})E_{\lambda^{2}}^{\boldsymbol{d}}(\boldsymbol{t}).

Thus by using the identity
\tau_{\boldsymbol{t}}(f*g)=(\tau_{\boldsymbol{t}}f)*g
, we obtain

\displaystyle\tau_{\boldsymbol{t}}(f\otimes_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda},\lambda}g)(\boldsymbol{x})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\lambda|^{\frac{N}{2}}C_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}\tau_{\boldsymbol{t}}(e^{\boldsymbol{a},\boldsymbol{d}}_{-\lambda^{2}}(\boldsymbol{x}))\big(\tau_{\boldsymbol{t}}(e^{\boldsymbol{a},\boldsymbol{d}}_{\lambda^{2}}f)*(e^{\boldsymbol{a},\boldsymbol{d}}_{\lambda^{2}}g)\big)(\boldsymbol{x}).

Now applying (
3.5
) to the preceding equation yields

\displaystyle\tau_{\boldsymbol{t}}(f\otimes_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda},\lambda}g)(\boldsymbol{x})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\lambda|^{\frac{N}{2}}C_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}e^{\boldsymbol{a},\boldsymbol{d}}_{-\lambda^{2}}(\boldsymbol{x})e^{\boldsymbol{a},\boldsymbol{d}}_{-\lambda^{2}}(\boldsymbol{t})E_{\lambda^{2}}^{\boldsymbol{a}\boldsymbol{t}}(\boldsymbol{x})E_{\lambda^{2}}^{\boldsymbol{d}}(\boldsymbol{t})

\displaystyle\times\Big(e^{\boldsymbol{a},\boldsymbol{d}}_{\lambda^{2}}(\boldsymbol{t})E_{-\lambda^{2}}^{\boldsymbol{d}}(\boldsymbol{t})e^{\boldsymbol{a},\boldsymbol{d}}_{\lambda^{2}}E_{-\lambda^{2}}^{\boldsymbol{a}\boldsymbol{t}}\tau_{\boldsymbol{t}}(f)*(e^{\boldsymbol{a},\boldsymbol{d}}_{\lambda^{2}}g)\Big)(\boldsymbol{x})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\lambda|^{\frac{N}{2}}C_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}e^{\boldsymbol{a},\boldsymbol{d}}_{-\lambda^{2}}(\boldsymbol{x})E_{\lambda^{2}}^{\boldsymbol{a}\boldsymbol{t}}(\boldsymbol{x})\Big(e^{\boldsymbol{a},\boldsymbol{d}}_{\lambda^{2}}E_{-\lambda^{2}}^{\boldsymbol{a}\boldsymbol{t}}\tau_{\boldsymbol{t}}(f)*(e^{\boldsymbol{a},\boldsymbol{d}}_{\lambda^{2}}g)\Big)(\boldsymbol{x})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\lambda|^{\frac{N}{2}}C_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}e^{\boldsymbol{a},\boldsymbol{d}}_{-\lambda^{2}}(\boldsymbol{x})\Big(e^{\boldsymbol{a},\boldsymbol{d}}_{\lambda^{2}}\tau_{\boldsymbol{t}}(f)*(e^{\boldsymbol{a},\boldsymbol{d}}_{\lambda^{2}}E_{\lambda^{2}}^{\boldsymbol{a}\boldsymbol{t}}g)\Big)(\boldsymbol{x})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big[(\tau_{\boldsymbol{t}}f)\otimes_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda},\lambda}(E_{\lambda^{2}}^{\boldsymbol{a}\boldsymbol{t}}g)\big](\boldsymbol{x}).

Having established this result, we now proceed to the next part.

(6) (a) Let
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
g\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. By the convolution
3.11
, we get

\displaystyle\partial_{x_{k}}(f\otimes_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda},\lambda}g)(\boldsymbol{x})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\lambda|^{\frac{N}{2}}C_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}\partial_{x_{k}}\big(e^{a,d}_{-\lambda^{2}}(\boldsymbol{x})\big)\big((e^{a,d}_{\lambda^{2}}f)*(e^{a,d}_{\lambda^{2}}g)\big)(\boldsymbol{x})+|\lambda|^{\frac{N}{2}}C_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}e^{a,d}_{-\lambda^{2}}(\boldsymbol{x})\partial_{x_{k}}\big((e^{a,d}_{\lambda^{2}}f)*(e^{a,d}_{\lambda^{2}}g)\big)(\boldsymbol{x})

Using the differentiation property of convolution,

\displaystyle\partial_{x_{k}}(f*g)(\boldsymbol{x})=(\partial_{x_{k}}f*g)(\boldsymbol{x}),

it follows that

\displaystyle\partial_{x_{k}}(f\otimes_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda},\lambda}g)(\boldsymbol{x})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\lambda|^{\frac{N}{2}}C_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}\partial_{x_{k}}\big(e^{a,d}_{-\lambda^{2}}(\boldsymbol{x})\big)\big((e^{a,d}_{\lambda^{2}}f)*(e^{a,d}_{\lambda^{2}}g)\big)(\boldsymbol{x})+|\lambda|^{\frac{N}{2}}C_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}e^{a,d}_{-\lambda^{2}}(\boldsymbol{x})\big(\partial_{x_{k}}(e^{a,d}_{\lambda^{2}}f)*(e^{a,d}_{\lambda^{2}}g)\big)(\boldsymbol{x})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\lambda|^{\frac{N}{2}}C_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}(-i\lambda^{2})(2a_{k}x_{k}+d_{k})\big(e^{a,d}_{-\lambda^{2}}(\boldsymbol{x})\big)\big((e^{a,d}_{\lambda^{2}}f)*(e^{a,d}_{\lambda^{2}}g)\big)(\boldsymbol{x})

\displaystyle+|\lambda|^{\frac{N}{2}}C_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}e^{a,d}_{-\lambda^{2}}(\boldsymbol{x})\big[(i\lambda^{2})(2a_{k}x_{k}+d_{k})\big(e^{a,d}_{-\lambda^{2}}(\boldsymbol{x})\big)f+\big(e^{a,d}_{-\lambda^{2}}(\boldsymbol{x})\big)(\partial_{x_{k}}f)\big]*(e^{a,d}_{\lambda^{2}}g)\big)(\boldsymbol{x})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\lambda|^{\frac{N}{2}}C_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}(i\lambda^{2})\Big[e^{a,d}_{-\lambda^{2}}(\boldsymbol{x})\big[\big(e^{a,d}_{\lambda^{2}}(\boldsymbol{x})\big)(2a_{k}x_{k}+d_{k})f*(e^{a,d}_{\lambda^{2}}g)\big)(\boldsymbol{x})

\displaystyle-(2a_{k}x_{k}+d_{k})\big(e^{a,d}_{-\lambda^{2}}(\boldsymbol{x})\big)\big((e^{a,d}_{\lambda^{2}}f)*(e^{a,d}_{\lambda^{2}}g)\big)(\boldsymbol{x})\Big]

\displaystyle+|\lambda|^{\frac{N}{2}}C_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}e^{a,d}_{-\lambda^{2}}(\boldsymbol{x})\big(e^{a,d}_{\lambda^{2}}(\boldsymbol{x})\big)(\partial_{x_{k}}f)*(e^{a,d}_{\lambda^{2}}g)\big)(\boldsymbol{x})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2a_{k}i|\lambda|^{\frac{N}{2}+2}C_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}\Big[e^{a,d}_{-\lambda^{2}}(\boldsymbol{x})\big[\big(e^{a,d}_{\lambda^{2}}(\boldsymbol{x})\big)x_{k}f*(e^{a,d}_{\lambda^{2}}g)\big)(\boldsymbol{x})-x_{k}\big(e^{a,d}_{-\lambda^{2}}(\boldsymbol{x})\big)\big((e^{a,d}_{\lambda^{2}}f)*(e^{a,d}_{\lambda^{2}}g)\big)(\boldsymbol{x})\Big]

\displaystyle+id_{k}|\lambda|^{\frac{N}{2}+2}C_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}\Big[e^{a,d}_{-\lambda^{2}}(\boldsymbol{x})\big[\big(e^{a,d}_{\lambda^{2}}\big)f*(e^{a,d}_{\lambda^{2}}g)\big](\boldsymbol{x})-\big(e^{a,d}_{-\lambda^{2}}(\boldsymbol{x})\big)\big((e^{a,d}_{\lambda^{2}}f)*(e^{a,d}_{\lambda^{2}}g)\big)(\boldsymbol{x})\Big]

\displaystyle+|\lambda|^{\frac{N}{2}}C_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}e^{a,d}_{-\lambda^{2}}(\boldsymbol{x})\big(e^{a,d}_{\lambda^{2}}(\boldsymbol{x})\big)\partial_{x_{k}}f*(e^{a,d}_{\lambda^{2}}g)\big)(\boldsymbol{x})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2a_{k}i|\lambda|^{\frac{N}{2}+2}C_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}\Big[e^{a,d}_{-\lambda^{2}}(\boldsymbol{x})\big[\big(e^{a,d}_{\lambda^{2}}(\boldsymbol{x})\big)P_{k}(\boldsymbol{x})f*(e^{a,d}_{\lambda^{2}}g\big)(\boldsymbol{x})\big]

\displaystyle-P_{k}(\boldsymbol{x})\big(e^{a,d}_{-\lambda^{2}}(\boldsymbol{x})\big)\big((e^{a,d}_{\lambda^{2}}f)*(e^{a,d}_{\lambda^{2}}g)\big)(\boldsymbol{x})\Big]+(\partial_{x_{k}}f\otimes_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda},\lambda}g)(\boldsymbol{x})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2a_{k}i\lambda^{2}\big[(P_{k}f\otimes_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda},\lambda}g)(\boldsymbol{x})-P_{k}(f\otimes_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda},\lambda}g)(\boldsymbol{x})\big]+(\partial_{x_{k}}f\otimes_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda},\lambda}g)(\boldsymbol{x}).

(b) The proof follows similarly to part (a).

Hence, the proof is complete.
∎

4.
Inversion theorem

In this section, we establish the inversion theorem for the multidimensional QPFT using the convolution theorem
3.11
and its properties. Before moving ahead, we state two lemmas that supports our main results.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\boldsymbol{\Lambda}=(\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2},...,\Lambda_{N})
such that
\Lambda_{k}=(a_{k},b_{k},...,e_{k})
,
b_{k}\neq 0
,
k=1,2,...,N
. Moreover
\lambda>0
,

\displaystyle H_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x})=\prod_{k=1}^{N}e^{-|\lambda x_{k}|}\text{ and }

\displaystyle h_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda},\lambda}(\boldsymbol{\omega})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}H_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x})e_{1}^{\boldsymbol{c,e}}(\boldsymbol{x})\mathcal{K}_{-\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}(\boldsymbol{\omega},\boldsymbol{x})d\boldsymbol{x}.

(4.1)

Then the following holds

(1)

H_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x})=\prod_{k=1}^{N}e^{-|\lambda x_{k}|}\to 1\text{ as }\lambda\to 0\text{ for each }\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
,

(2)

\frac{C_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}}{(2\pi)^{\frac{N}{2}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}h_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda},\lambda}(\boldsymbol{\omega})e^{\boldsymbol{a,d}}(\boldsymbol{\omega})d\boldsymbol{\omega}=1
,

(3)

h_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda},\lambda}\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),~~p\in[1,\infty),\text{ if }|C_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}|\leq 1
,

(4)

h_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda},\lambda}(\boldsymbol{\omega})=\frac{e_{1}^{\boldsymbol{a,d}}(\boldsymbol{\omega})e_{1}^{\boldsymbol{c,e}}(\frac{\boldsymbol{\omega}}{\lambda})}{\lambda^{N}}h_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda},1}(\frac{\boldsymbol{\omega}}{\lambda})
.

Proof.

(1) We observe that
e^{|\lambda x_{k}|}
approaches

, as
\lambda
tends to
0
, for all

. So the desired result follows immediately.

(2) By using the equations (
4.1
) and (

), we get

\displaystyle h_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda},\lambda}(\boldsymbol{\omega})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{k=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-|\lambda x_{k}|}e^{i(c_{k}x_{k}^{2}+e_{k}x_{k})}\mathcal{K}_{-\Lambda_{k}}(x_{k},\omega_{k})dx_{k}

(4.2)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{C_{-\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}}{(2\pi)^{\frac{N}{2}}}\prod_{k=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-|\lambda x_{k}|}e^{i(c_{k}x_{k}^{2}+e_{k}x_{k})}~e^{-i(a_{k}\omega_{k}^{2}+b_{k}x_{k}\omega_{k}+c_{k}x_{k}^{2}+d_{k}\omega_{k}+e_{k}x_{k})}dx_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{C_{-\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}}{(2\pi)^{\frac{N}{2}}}~e^{-i(a_{k}\omega_{k}^{2}+d_{k}\omega_{k})}\prod_{k=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-|\lambda x_{k}|}~e^{-ib_{k}x_{k}\omega_{k}}dx_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{C_{-\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}}{(2\pi)^{\frac{N}{2}}}~e_{-1}^{\boldsymbol{a,d}}(\boldsymbol{\omega})\prod_{k=1}^{N}\bigg[\int_{-\infty}^{0}e^{(\lambda-ib_{k}\omega_{k})x_{k}}dx_{k}+\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-(\lambda+ib_{k}\omega_{k})x_{k}}dx_{k}\bigg]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{C_{-\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}}{(2\pi)^{\frac{N}{2}}}~e_{-1}^{\boldsymbol{a,d}}(\boldsymbol{\omega})\prod_{k=1}^{N}\biggl\{\bigg[\frac{1}{(\lambda-ib_{k}\omega_{k})}\bigg]+\bigg[\frac{1}{(\lambda+ib_{k}\omega_{k})}\bigg]\biggr\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{C_{-\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}}{(2\pi)^{\frac{N}{2}}}~e_{-1}^{\boldsymbol{a,d}}(\boldsymbol{\omega})\prod_{k=1}^{N}\biggl\{\frac{2\lambda}{(\lambda^{2}+b_{k}^{2}\omega_{k}^{2})}\biggr\}.

\displaystyle\frac{C_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}}{(2\pi)^{\frac{N}{2}}}h_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda},\lambda}(\boldsymbol{\omega})~e_{1}^{\boldsymbol{a,d}}(\boldsymbol{\omega})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{C_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}}{(2\pi)^{\frac{N}{2}}}\frac{C_{-\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}}{(2\pi)^{\frac{N}{2}}}\prod_{k=1}^{N}\biggl\{\frac{2\lambda}{(\lambda^{2}+b_{k}^{2}\omega_{k}^{2})}\biggr\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(\pi)^{N}}\prod_{k=1}^{N}\biggl\{\frac{\lambda b_{k}}{(\lambda^{2}+b_{k}^{2}\omega_{k}^{2})}\biggr\}.

Integrating on both sides of the equation to get

\displaystyle\frac{C_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}}{(2\pi)^{\frac{N}{2}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}h_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda},\lambda}(\boldsymbol{\omega})~e_{1}^{\boldsymbol{a,d}}(\boldsymbol{\omega})d\boldsymbol{\omega}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(\pi)^{N}}\prod_{k=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\biggl\{\frac{\lambda b_{k}}{(\lambda^{2}+b_{k}^{2}\omega_{k}^{2})}\biggr\}d\omega_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(\pi)^{N}}\prod_{k=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\biggl\{\frac{\lambda}{(\lambda^{2}+z_{k}^{2})}\biggr\}dz_{k},

where
z_{k}=b_{k}\omega_{k}
. On further simplification, we get

\displaystyle\frac{C_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}}{(2\pi)^{\frac{N}{2}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}h_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda},\lambda}(\boldsymbol{\omega})~e_{1}^{\boldsymbol{a,d}}(\boldsymbol{\omega})d\boldsymbol{\omega}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(\pi)^{N}}\prod_{k=1}^{N}\biggl[\arctan\Big(\frac{z_{k}}{\lambda}\Big)\biggr]_{-\infty}^{\infty}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(\pi)^{N}}\prod_{k=1}^{N}\biggl\{\frac{\pi}{2}-\frac{-\pi}{2}\biggr\}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(\pi)^{N}}\prod_{k=1}^{N}\pi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1.

Hence the result.

(3) For

\displaystyle||h_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda},\lambda}||_{p}^{p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{|C_{-\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}|^{p}}{\lambda^{pN}(2\pi)^{\frac{N}{2}}}\prod_{k=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\bigg(\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\bigg)^{p}\biggl\{\frac{\lambda^{2}}{(\lambda^{2}+b_{k}^{2}\omega_{k}^{2})}\biggr\}^{p}d\omega_{k}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{|C_{-\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}|^{p}}{\lambda^{pN}(2\pi)^{\frac{N}{2}}}\prod_{k=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\bigg(\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\bigg)\biggl\{\frac{\lambda^{2}}{(\lambda^{2}+b_{k}^{2}\omega_{k}^{2})}\biggr\}d\omega_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{|C_{-\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}|^{p}}{\lambda^{pN-N}(\pi)^{N}}\bigg(\prod_{k=1}^{N}\frac{1}{b_{k}}\bigg)\prod_{k=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\biggl\{\frac{\lambda b_{k}}{(\lambda^{2}+b_{k}^{2}\omega_{k}^{2})}\biggr\}d\omega_{k}

\displaystyle\frac{|C_{-\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}|^{p-2}}{\lambda^{pN-N}(\pi)^{N}}\prod_{k=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\biggl\{\frac{\lambda b_{k}}{(\lambda^{2}+b_{k}^{2}\omega_{k}^{2})}\biggr\}d\omega_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{|C_{-\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}|^{p-2}}{\lambda^{pN-N}(\pi)^{N}}\prod_{k=1}^{N}\pi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{|C_{-\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}|^{p-2}}{\lambda^{pN-N}}~~<\infty.

Therefore

.

\displaystyle h_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda},\lambda}(\boldsymbol{\omega})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{C_{-\Lambda}}{(\sqrt{2\pi}\lambda)^{N}}\prod_{k=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-|y_{k}|}e^{i\big(c_{k}\big(\frac{y_{k}}{\lambda}\big)^{2}+e_{k}(\frac{y_{k}}{\lambda})\big)}e^{-i(a\omega_{k}^{2}+b\big(\frac{y_{k}}{\lambda}\big)\omega_{k}+c\big(\frac{y_{k}}{\lambda}\big)^{2}+d\omega_{k}+e\big(\frac{y_{k}}{\lambda}\big))}dy_{k}

\displaystyle\frac{C_{-\Lambda}}{(\sqrt{2\pi}\lambda)^{N}}e_{-1}^{a,d}(\boldsymbol{\omega})\prod_{k=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-|y_{k}|}e^{-i(b_{k}\big(\frac{y_{k}}{\lambda}\big)\omega_{k})}dy_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{{\lambda}^{N}}e_{-1}^{a,d}(\boldsymbol{\omega})e_{1}^{a,d}\big(\frac{\boldsymbol{\omega}}{\lambda}\big)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}H_{1}(\boldsymbol{y})e_{1}^{c,e}(\boldsymbol{y})\mathcal{K}_{\boldsymbol{-\Lambda}}(\frac{\boldsymbol{\omega}}{\lambda},\boldsymbol{y})d\boldsymbol{y}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{e_{-1}^{a,d}(\boldsymbol{\omega})e_{1}^{a,d}\big(\frac{\boldsymbol{\omega}}{\lambda}\big)}{{\lambda}^{N}}h_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda},1}\big(\frac{\boldsymbol{\omega}}{\lambda}\big).

This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

-(4) gives

Theorem 4.3

\mathcal{M}

-(2),

i.e.,

Thus

is an integrable function on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
with respect to measure

(4.3)

Theorem 4.4
.

- inversion theorem)

\displaystyle f(\boldsymbol{x})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(\mathcal{Q}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}f)(\boldsymbol{t})\mathcal{K}_{\boldsymbol{-\Lambda}}(\boldsymbol{t},\boldsymbol{\omega})d\boldsymbol{t}.

\displaystyle(\mathcal{Q}_{-\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}\circ\mathcal{Q}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}})(f)=f.

4.1

. Then, we obtain

\displaystyle(f~{\otimes}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda},1}~h_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda},\lambda})(\boldsymbol{x})

\displaystyle\frac{C_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}}{(\sqrt{2\pi})^{N}}e^{a,d}_{-1}(\boldsymbol{x})\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}((e^{a,d}_{1}f)(\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{y})(e^{a,d}_{1})(\boldsymbol{y})\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}H_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{t})e_{1}^{\boldsymbol{c,e}}(\boldsymbol{t})\mathcal{K}_{-\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}(\boldsymbol{t},\boldsymbol{y})d\boldsymbol{t}\Big)d\boldsymbol{y}

\displaystyle\frac{C_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}C_{\boldsymbol{-\Lambda}}}{(2\pi)^{N}}e^{a,d}_{-1}(\boldsymbol{x})\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}H_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{t})\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(e^{a,d}_{1}f)(\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{y})e^{-i\sum_{k=1}^{N}b_{k}y_{k}t_{k}}d\boldsymbol{y}\Big)d\boldsymbol{t}.

\displaystyle(f~{\otimes}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda},1}~h_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda},\lambda})(\boldsymbol{x})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{C_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}C_{\boldsymbol{-\Lambda}}}{(2\pi)^{N}}e^{a,d}_{-1}(\boldsymbol{x})\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}H_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{t})e^{-i\sum_{k=1}^{N}b_{k}x_{k}t_{k}}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(e^{a,d}_{1}f)(\boldsymbol{z})e^{i\sum_{k=1}^{N}b_{k}z_{k}t_{k}}d\boldsymbol{z}\Big)d\boldsymbol{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{C_{\boldsymbol{-\Lambda}}}{(2\pi)^{N}}e^{a,d}_{-1}(\boldsymbol{x})\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}H_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{t})e^{-i\sum_{k=1}^{N}b_{k}x_{k}t_{k}}(e^{c,e}_{-1})(\boldsymbol{t})\mathcal{Q}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}(f)(\boldsymbol{t})d\boldsymbol{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}H_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{t})\mathcal{Q}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}(f)(\boldsymbol{t})\mathcal{K}_{\boldsymbol{-\Lambda}}(\boldsymbol{t},\boldsymbol{x})d\boldsymbol{t}

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\mathcal{Q}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}(f)(\boldsymbol{t})\mathcal{K}_{\boldsymbol{-\Lambda}}(\boldsymbol{t},\boldsymbol{x})d\boldsymbol{t},\text{ as }\lambda\to 0,

since by Lemma
4.1
-(1), we have

tends to
1
as
\lambda
tends to
0

\displaystyle(ib_{k}\omega_{k})^{n}\mathcal{K}_{\Lambda_{k}}(\omega_{k},x_{k}).

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\Delta_{x_{k}}^{*})^{n}\mathcal{K}_{\Lambda_{k}}(\omega_{k},x_{k})f(x_{k})dx_{k}

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{K}_{\Lambda_{k}}(\omega_{k},x_{k})(\Delta_{x_{k}})^{n}f(x_{k})dx_{k}.

\displaystyle(\Delta_{x_{k}}^{*})^{n}\mathcal{K}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}(\boldsymbol{\omega},\boldsymbol{x})

\displaystyle(ib_{k}\omega_{k})^{n}\mathcal{K}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}(\boldsymbol{\omega},\boldsymbol{x}).

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(\Delta_{x_{k}}^{*})^{n}\mathcal{K}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}(\boldsymbol{\omega},\boldsymbol{x})f(\boldsymbol{x})d\boldsymbol{x}

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\mathcal{K}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}(\boldsymbol{\omega},\boldsymbol{x})(\Delta_{x_{k}})^{n}f(\boldsymbol{x})d\boldsymbol{x}.

(5.7)

Using (

5.6
) and (

\displaystyle(\mathcal{Q}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}\Delta_{x_{k}}^{n}f)(\boldsymbol{\omega})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\mathcal{K}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}(\boldsymbol{\omega},\boldsymbol{x})\Delta_{x_{k}}^{n}f(\boldsymbol{x})d\boldsymbol{x}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}[(\Delta_{x_{k}}^{*})^{n}\mathcal{K}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}(\boldsymbol{\omega},\boldsymbol{x})]f(\boldsymbol{x})d\boldsymbol{x}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}[(ib_{k}\omega_{k})^{n}\mathcal{K}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}(\boldsymbol{\omega},\boldsymbol{x})]f(\boldsymbol{x})d\boldsymbol{x}

\displaystyle(ib_{k}\omega_{k})^{n}(\mathcal{Q}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}f)(\boldsymbol{\omega}).

\displaystyle(\mathcal{Q}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}(\Delta_{x_{1}}^{n}f)(\Delta_{x_{2}}^{n}f)...(\Delta_{x_{k}}^{n})f)(\boldsymbol{\omega})

\displaystyle(ib_{1}\omega_{1})^{n}(\mathcal{Q}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}(\Delta_{x_{2}}^{n}f)...(\Delta_{x_{N}}^{n})f)(\boldsymbol{\omega})

\displaystyle\prod_{k=1}^{N}(ib_{k}\omega_{k})^{n}(\mathcal{Q}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}f)(\boldsymbol{\omega}).

Hence we obtain the required result.

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}||B^{n}f||^{\frac{1}{n}}=\infty.

This contradicts our assumption.
Hence QPFT
(\mathcal{Q}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}f)
of
f
vanishes in the neighborhood of origin.
∎

6.
Potential Applications

6.1.
Solvability of Integral equations

As an application of previously defined convolutions, we will now consider some classes of integral equations associated with the convolutions proposed in Theorems
3.6
,
3.12
. This will illustrate one of the possible applications of the above-proved theorems. Here,
\odot
denotes any of the previously introduced convolutions in
3.6
,
3.12
.

Consider the convolution equation

\displaystyle\lambda\phi(\boldsymbol{x})+(k\odot\phi)(\boldsymbol{x})=p(\boldsymbol{x}),

(6.1)

where
\lambda\in\mathbb{C},k,p\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, and
\phi
is to be determined there. In equation (
6.1
), when the convolution
\odot
is taken one of the possibilities
3.6
, or
3.12
, then let us also use
\Omega
to be corresponding function in

\displaystyle\{\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2}\},

(6.2)

respectively. We shall now use the notation
S(\boldsymbol{\omega}):=\lambda+\Omega(\boldsymbol{\omega})(\mathcal{Q}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}k)(\boldsymbol{\omega})
.

Theorem 6.1
.

Assume that
S(\boldsymbol{\omega})\neq 0
for every
\boldsymbol{\omega}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
\frac{\mathcal{Q}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}k}{S}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Then the equation (
6.1
) has a solution in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
if and only if

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}\big(\frac{\mathcal{Q}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}k}{S}\big)\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}).

(6.3)

Moreover, if the condition (
6.3
) holds, then the solution of equation (
6.1
) is given in explicit form by
\phi=\mathcal{Q}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{-1}\big(\frac{\mathcal{Q}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}k}{S}\big)\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

Proof.

Necessary condition:
Suppose that the equation (
6.1
) has a solution
\phi\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Applying
\mathcal{Q}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}
to both sides of (
6.1
), we obtain

\displaystyle(\mathcal{Q}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}\lambda\phi)(\boldsymbol{\omega})+(\mathcal{Q}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}(k\odot\phi))(\boldsymbol{\omega})=(\mathcal{Q}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}p)(\boldsymbol{\omega})

\displaystyle\big[\lambda+\Omega(\boldsymbol{\omega})(\mathcal{Q}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}k)(\boldsymbol{\omega})\big](\mathcal{Q}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}\phi)(\boldsymbol{\omega})=(\mathcal{Q}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}p)(\boldsymbol{\omega})

\displaystyle S(\boldsymbol{\omega})(\mathcal{Q}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}\phi)(\boldsymbol{\omega})=(\mathcal{Q}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}p)(\boldsymbol{\omega}).

Having in mind that
S(\boldsymbol{\omega})\neq 0
for all
\boldsymbol{\omega}\in\mathbb{R}
, we get
\mathcal{Q}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}\phi=\frac{\mathcal{Q}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}p}{S}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Then

\displaystyle\phi=\mathcal{Q}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{-1}\Big(\frac{\mathcal{Q}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}p}{S}\Big)\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

Sufficient condition: Let
\phi=\mathcal{Q}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{-1}\big(\frac{\mathcal{Q}p}{S}\big)\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. By
\phi\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, we get
S(\boldsymbol{x})(\mathcal{Q}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}\phi)(\boldsymbol{x})=(\mathcal{Q}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}p)(\boldsymbol{x})
.
Using the factorization identity of the convolution, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}(\lambda\phi(\boldsymbol{x})+(k\odot\phi)(\boldsymbol{x}))=\mathcal{Q}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}(p(\boldsymbol{x})).

By the uniqueness theorem of
\mathcal{Q}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}
, we conclude that
\phi(\boldsymbol{x})
fulfills (
6.1
) for almost every
\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Hence the proof.
∎

6.2.
Designing of multiplicative filters in the multidimensional QPFT domain

In this subsection, we discuss the applications of the new convolution and correlation theorems for the designing of multiplicative filters in the multidimensional QPFT domain. These filters are used in various digital signal processing to recover the received signal. In the domain where the signal or noise (or both) are maximally localized, significant performance can be obtained via filtering. Suppose
r(\boldsymbol{x})
,
f(\boldsymbol{x})
and
n(\boldsymbol{x})
denote the received signal, the desired signal and the noise, respectively. Then
r(\boldsymbol{x})=f(\boldsymbol{x})+n(\boldsymbol{x})
. The multidimensional QPFT components
\mathcal{Q}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}f(\boldsymbol{\omega})
and
\mathcal{Q}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}n(\boldsymbol{\omega})
have no or minimum overlapping. We can design a multiplicative filter in the multidimensional QPFT domain with the convolution and product theorems proposed in this paper. We consider the QPFT- frequency spectrum in the region
[\xi_{1},\eta_{1}]\times,[\xi_{2},\eta_{2}]\times...\times[\xi_{N},\eta_{N}]
of the signal
f(\boldsymbol{x})
. Let
g(\boldsymbol{x})
be the filter impulse response. Then
\mathcal{Q}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}g(\boldsymbol{\omega})
is constant over
[\xi_{1},\eta_{1}]\times,[\xi_{2},\eta_{2}]\times...\times[\xi_{N},\eta_{N}]
which either vanishes or decay rapidly outside that region.
The output signal can be expressed as

\displaystyle r_{out}(\boldsymbol{x})=(r_{in}\odot g)(\boldsymbol{x}).

With this, we get

\displaystyle r_{out}(\boldsymbol{x})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{\boldsymbol{-\Lambda}}\big[\mathcal{Q}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}(r_{in}\odot g)\big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{\boldsymbol{-\Lambda}}\big[\Omega\mathcal{Q}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}(r_{in})(\boldsymbol{\omega})\mathcal{Q}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}g(\boldsymbol{\omega})\big](\boldsymbol{x}),

where
\Omega
is as in (
6.2
), whose multidimensional QPFT has a finite support in the region
[\xi_{1},\eta_{1}]\times,[\xi_{2},\eta_{2}]\times...\times[\xi_{N},\eta_{N}]
, so it preserves that part of the spectrum of
f(\boldsymbol{x})
over
[\xi_{1},\eta_{1}]\times,[\xi_{2},\eta_{2}]\times...\times[\xi_{N},\eta_{N}]
.

i.e.,

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}[r_{out}(\boldsymbol{x})](\boldsymbol{\omega})=\mathcal{Q}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}f(\boldsymbol{\omega}),~~~~~~~~~~~\boldsymbol{\omega}\in[\xi_{1},\eta_{1}]\times,[\xi_{2},\eta_{2}]\times...\times[\xi_{N},\eta_{N}].

7.
Conclusion

In this article, we have extended the QPFT to the multidimensional domain and established some key properties, including Plancherel and inversion theorems. We further explored three different convolutions and a correlation, and their corresponding theorems for multidimensional QPFT, generalizing convolutions of single variable QPFT. Furthermore, we derived Boas theorem associated with multidimensional QPFT. Besides the theoretical developments, we have also discussed the applications of the proposed convolutions in solving integral equations and multiplicative filter designing, which can be used in optics and signal processing for signal recovery.

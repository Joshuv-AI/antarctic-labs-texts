Scale invariance of intermittency in LES turbulence
B. Magacho 1, S. Thalabard 2, M. Buzzicotti 3, F. Bonaccorso 3, L. Biferale 3, A. A. Mailybaev 1

Abstract
Turbulent flows exhibit large intermittent fluctuations from inertial to dissipative scales, characterized by multifractal statistics and breaking the statistical self-similarity. It has recently been proposed that the Navier-Stokes turbulence restores a hidden form of scale invariance in the inertial interval when formulated for a dynamically (nonlinearly) rescaled quasi-Lagrangian velocity field. Here we show that such hidden self-similarity extends to the Large-Eddy Simulation (LES) approach in computational fluid dynamics (CFD). In particular, we show that classical subgrid-scale models, such as implicit or explicit Smagorinsky closures, respect the hidden scale invariance at all scales – both resolved and subgrid. In the inertial range, they reproduce the hidden scale invariance of Navier-Stokes statistics. These properties are verified very accurately by numerical simulations and, beyond CFD, turn LES into a valuable tool for fundamental turbulence research.

Introduction

Large-eddy simulations are used in applied studies to simulate large-scale turbulent dynamics, relying on empirical modeling at small (subgrid) scales
(
Pope, 2000
;
Meneveau & Katz, 2000
;
Sagaut, 2005
;
Fureby, 2008
)
. They are capable of generating intermittent (multifractal) velocity fields
{\boldsymbol{u}}({\boldsymbol{x}},t)
, reproducing key features of homogeneous 3D turbulence
(
Cerutti & Meneveau, 1998
;
Biferale et al., 2019
;
Linkmann et al., 2018
)
.
Classical description of intermittency involves, in particular, the distributions of longitudinal velocity increments
\Delta_{\ell}u_{\parallel}=\big[{\boldsymbol{u}}({\boldsymbol{x}}+\ell{\boldsymbol{e}})-{\boldsymbol{u}}({\boldsymbol{x}})\big]\cdot{\boldsymbol{e}}

(with
{\boldsymbol{e}}
arbitrary unit vector) becoming increasingly flat-tailed at small scales and leading to anomalous scaling of structure functions
(
Frisch, 1995
;
Chevillard, 2015
;
Iyer et al., 2017
)
;
see Fig.
1
.
The multifractal phenomenology is rooted in the scaling symmetries of the Navier-Stokes (NS) equations, suggesting that these symmetries should drive any modeling strategies of turbulence including LES.

Here we investigate the Hidden Scale Invariance (HS) approach developed recently by
Mailybaev (2022a)
;
Mailybaev & Thalabard (2022)
, which describes scaling properties of the NS in a quasi-Lagrangian reference frame
(
Belinicher & L’vov, 1987
;
Mitra & Pandit, 2004
;
Biferale et al., 2011
)
after dynamic (nonlinear) rescaling. By revealing unbroken (hidden) self-similarity of intermittent statistics, this approach provides theoretical support to the anomalous scaling of structure functions
(
Mailybaev, 2022b
;
Thalabard & Mailybaev, 2024
;
Calascibetta et al., 2025
)
and the universal statistics of multipliers, the ratios like
\Delta_{\ell}u_{\parallel}/\Delta_{\ell^{\prime}}u_{\parallel}
,
as conjectured by Kolmogorov in his refined theory of turbulence
(
Kolmogorov, 1962
;
Chen et al., 2003
)
.
Extension of the HS to SGS closures has been recently studied in shell models
(
Mailybaev, 2023
)
.

In this work, we prove that properly designed SGS models, for which the Smagorinsky is the simplest example, preserve the HS. This implies that the rescaled quasi-Lagrangian statistics does not depend on the observation scale,
\ell
, and the filtering (cut-off) scale,
\delta
, separately but only through their ratio
\alpha=\ell/\delta
. Besides, for those resolved scales
\delta\ll\ell\ll L_{f}

far from the cut-off and the forcing scale
L_{f}
, e.g. in the inertial range,
the statistics becomes independent of
\alpha
and coincides with the analogous hidden-symmetric statistics of the NS. Performing high-resolution simulations, we provide very accurate numerical verification of HS. Such accuracy is attained because the HS in SGS models extends to subgrid scales, unlike the NS where it only refers to the inertial range.
This stronger form of HS has implications for both practical (development of SGS models) and theoretical studies of turbulence.

Kinematic rescaling of a quasi-Lagrangian velocity

Navier-Stokes-Smagorinsky equation

In practice, the closure problem takes as inputs a filtering scale
\delta
and a filter
G
. It aims to predict the dynamics of the filtered field as the convolution

{\boldsymbol{u}}_{\delta}=G_{\delta}*{\boldsymbol{u}}
with the kernel
G_{\delta}({\boldsymbol{x}})=\delta^{-3}G({\boldsymbol{x}}/\delta)
, providing implicit or explicit parametrizations for the so-called subgrid scale tensor
{\boldsymbol{R}}_{\delta}=\left({\boldsymbol{u}}\otimes{\boldsymbol{u}}\right)_{\delta}-{\boldsymbol{u}}_{\delta}\otimes{\boldsymbol{u}}_{\delta}
.
We start with the simplest example of the Smagorinsky model, in which
{\boldsymbol{R}}_{\delta}
is expressed as the viscous stress with the effective eddy-viscosity
\nu_{s}
. Dropping
\delta
’s from the subscripts, the resulting Navier-Stokes-Smagorinsky (NSS) dynamics for the filtered velocity becomes

\partial_{t}{\boldsymbol{u}}+{\boldsymbol{u}}\cdot\bnabla{\boldsymbol{u}}=-\bnabla p+\bnabla\cdot\left(2\nu_{s}{\boldsymbol{s}}\right)+{\boldsymbol{f}},\quad\bnabla\cdot{\boldsymbol{u}}=0,

(1)

where
p({\boldsymbol{x}},t)
is the pressure and
{\boldsymbol{f}}({\boldsymbol{x}},t)
is a large-scale force with correlation length
L_{f}\gg\delta
.
The eddy viscosity is defined in terms of the filtered rate-of-strain tensor
{\boldsymbol{s}}
as

\nu_{s}=(c_{s}\delta)^{2}\|{\boldsymbol{s}}\|,\quad{\boldsymbol{s}}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\bnabla{\boldsymbol{u}}+(\bnabla{\boldsymbol{u}})^{T}\right),

(2)

with the Smagorinsky constant
c_{s}=0.1
and the norm
\|{\boldsymbol{s}}\|^{2}=\sum 2s_{ij}^{2}
.
Considering the filtering scale
\delta
to be much larger than the viscous scale, the kinematic viscosity
\nu\ll\nu_{s}
is small and, therefore, omitted in the NSS equations (
1
).
The filtering operation leading to Eq. (
2
) is formal only. While the NSS dynamics aims to mimic the Navier-Stokes (NS) equations for the resolved scales
\ell\gg\delta
, the role of eddy viscosity is to replace the energy transfer at the subgrid scales
\ell\lesssim\delta
by a dissipative scheme
(
Cruz & Lamballais, 2023
)
.

Kinematic rescaling

Similarly to the NS system previously discussed by
Mailybaev (2022a)
;
Mailybaev & Thalabard (2022)
,
the starting point of our study is the projection of the velocity field
{\boldsymbol{u}}({\boldsymbol{x}},t)
into the rescaled velocity field
{\boldsymbol{U}}({\boldsymbol{X}},\tau)
.
At a heuristic level, the new field represents a quasi-Lagrangian velocity increment
(
Belinicher & L’vov, 1987
)
with a dynamically rescaled amplitude and timeframe. The course of proper time
\tau
is determined by the local level of fluctuations, running fast when the latter are high and slowly otherwise.
The rescaling is parametrized by an observational scale
\ell
and a label
{\boldsymbol{x}}_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
. The label selects a Lagrangian trajectory as

\dfrac{d{\boldsymbol{x}}_{*}}{dt}={{\boldsymbol{u}}}({\boldsymbol{x}}_{*},t),\quad{\boldsymbol{x}}_{*}(0)={\boldsymbol{x}}_{0}.

(3)

Fluctuations at scale
\ell
are measured as amplitudes of quasi-Lagrangian increments as

a_{\ell}(t)=\sqrt{\int_{|\mathbf{X}|\leq 1}\big|\Delta_{\ell}{\boldsymbol{u}}\big({\boldsymbol{x}}_{*}(t),{\boldsymbol{X}},t\big)\big|^{2}d^{3}{\boldsymbol{X}}},\quad\Delta_{\ell}{\boldsymbol{u}}({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{X}},t)={\boldsymbol{u}}\big({\boldsymbol{x}}+\ell{\boldsymbol{X}},t\big)-{\boldsymbol{u}}\big({\boldsymbol{x}},t\big).

(4)

The space-variable
{\boldsymbol{X}}
represents the vectorial separation with respect to the Lagrangian trajectory in units of the observational scale
\ell
.
The velocity field is then rescaled as

{\boldsymbol{U}}({\boldsymbol{X}},\tau)=\dfrac{\Delta_{\ell}{\boldsymbol{u}}\big({\boldsymbol{x}}_{*}(t),{\boldsymbol{X}},t\big)}{a_{\ell}(t)},\quad\tau=\frac{1}{\ell}\int_{0}^{t}a_{\ell}(s)\,ds.

(5)

Here the first formula introduces the rescaled velocity
{\boldsymbol{U}}
depending on the rescaled time
\tau
, which is expressed in terms of the velocity difference
\Delta_{\ell}{\boldsymbol{u}}
at original time
t
. The second formula defines the relation between
t
and
\tau
, which is one-to-one for
a_{\ell}(t)>0
.
Physically,
\tau
represents a Lagrangian clock measuring time in units of the local turnover time
\ell/a_{\ell}(t)
encountered along the trajectory
{\boldsymbol{x}}_{*}(t)
.

Note that the choice of
a_{\ell}
in Eq.(
4
) is by no mean unique. In practice, the essential requirement is that it should be homogeneous of degree 1 with respect to the velocity field and positive along a trajectory. Here, for simplicity, we restrict our discussion to a specific example rather than deriving the hidden symmetry in full generality. We refer to
Mailybaev & Thalabard (2022)
for more general derivations.

Switching the observation scale

Changing the scale
\ell\mapsto\ell^{\prime}
in Eqs. (
4
) and (
5
) reduces to a specific transformation of the respective fields
{\boldsymbol{U}}({\boldsymbol{X}},\tau)\mapsto{\boldsymbol{U}}^{\prime}({\boldsymbol{X}},\tau^{\prime})
expressed as

{\boldsymbol{U}}^{\prime}({\boldsymbol{X}},\tau^{\prime})=\frac{{\boldsymbol{U}}({\boldsymbol{X}}/\lambda,\tau)}{A(\tau)},\ \ \tau^{\prime}=\lambda\int_{0}^{\tau}A(s)\,ds,\ \ A(\tau)=\sqrt{\int_{|\mathbf{X}|\leq 1}|{\boldsymbol{U}}({\boldsymbol{X}}/\lambda,\tau)|^{2}d^{3}{\boldsymbol{X}}},

(6)

where
\lambda=\ell/\ell^{\prime}
and the second relation introduces the change of time
\tau\mapsto\tau^{\prime}
.
The kinematic relations (
5
) and (
6
) are demonstrated schematically in Fig.
2
(a) by straight and wavy arrows, respectively. In this figure, we specified the parameters in the subscripts:
{\boldsymbol{u}}_{\delta}
for the original field depending on the filtering scale
\delta
and
{\boldsymbol{U}}_{\delta,\ell,{\boldsymbol{x}}_{0}}
for the rescaled field depending additionally on the observation scale
\ell
and the label
{\boldsymbol{x}}_{0}
.

NSS under kinematic rescaling

The governing equations for the rescaled velocity field
{\boldsymbol{U}}({\boldsymbol{X}},\tau)
are obtained by differentiating the definition (
5
) with respect to
\tau
and using the NSS system (
1
) and (
2
). The lengthy but elementary derivation yields (see §
A.1
)

\partial_{\tau}{\boldsymbol{U}}=\Lambda_{\boldsymbol{U}}\big[-{\boldsymbol{U}}\cdot\bnabla{\boldsymbol{U}}-\bnabla P+2c_{s}^{2}\,\alpha^{-2}\,\bnabla\cdot(\|{\boldsymbol{S}}\|{\boldsymbol{S}})+{\boldsymbol{F}}\big],\quad\bnabla\cdot{{\boldsymbol{U}}}=0.

(7)

Here
\alpha=\ell/\delta
, the tensor
{\boldsymbol{S}}=\frac{1}{2}\bnabla{\boldsymbol{U}}+\frac{1}{2}(\bnabla{\boldsymbol{U}})^{T}
, and the gradient operator
\bnabla
acts on the new space variable
{\boldsymbol{X}}
. The operator
\Lambda_{\boldsymbol{U}}
is defined for any field
{\boldsymbol{V}}({\boldsymbol{X}},\tau)
as

\Lambda_{\boldsymbol{U}}[{\boldsymbol{V}}]=\tilde{{\boldsymbol{V}}}-{\boldsymbol{U}}\int_{|\mathbf{X}|\leq 1}{\boldsymbol{U}}\cdot\tilde{{\boldsymbol{V}}}d^{3}{\boldsymbol{X}},\quad\tilde{{\boldsymbol{V}}}={\boldsymbol{V}}({\boldsymbol{X}},\tau)-{\boldsymbol{V}}(\mathbf{0},\tau).

(8)

The rescaled forcing term is expressed, with the times
t\mapsto\tau
related by Eq. (
5
), as

{\boldsymbol{F}}({\boldsymbol{X}},\tau)=\frac{\ell\,\Delta_{\ell}{\boldsymbol{f}}\big({\boldsymbol{x}}_{*}(t),{\boldsymbol{X}},t\big)}{a_{\ell}^{2}(t)},\quad\Delta_{\ell}{\boldsymbol{f}}({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{X}},t)={\boldsymbol{f}}({\boldsymbol{x}}+\ell{\boldsymbol{X}},t)-{\boldsymbol{f}}({\boldsymbol{x}},t).

(9)

Hidden scale invariance

The limit of small observational scales

System (
7
) governs the dynamics of the new velocity and pressure fields
{\boldsymbol{U}}({\boldsymbol{X}},\tau)
and
P({\boldsymbol{X}},\tau)
.
It is, however, not closed with respect to these fields, because the coupling with the original dynamics
{\boldsymbol{u}}({\boldsymbol{x}},t)
permeates through the dependence of forcing term (
9
) on
a_{\ell}(t)
. We now argue that a closed formulation follows asymptotically at small observational scales
\ell\ll L_{f}
, in which case the forcing term is negligible. Indeed, we have
\partial_{\tau}{\boldsymbol{U}}\sim 1
for
{\boldsymbol{X}}\sim 1
by construction from Eqs. (
4
) and (
5
).
This implies that the lhs in Eq. (
7
) is
\sim 1
. As for the forcing term, we first consider the case
\delta\lesssim\ell\ll L_{f}
,
describing an observational scale in the resolved (turbulent) range.
The dimensional estimate
{\boldsymbol{f}}\sim U_{f}^{2}/L_{f}
in Eq. (
9
) yields
\Delta_{\ell}{\boldsymbol{f}}\sim U_{f}^{2}\ell/L_{f}^{2}
, with

U_{f}
the integral (forcing-scale) velocity. Using the K41 estimate
a_{\ell}\sim U_{f}(\ell/L_{f})^{1/3}
for the amplitude of velocity fluctuations in Eq. (
9
), we conclude that the forcing term
{\boldsymbol{F}}\sim(\ell/L_{f})^{4/3}\ll 1
. This estimate extends to the subgrid range
\ell<\delta\ll L_{f}
as
{\boldsymbol{F}}\sim(\delta/L_{f})^{4/3}\ll 1
.

Therefore, under the condition of small scales
\delta,\ell\ll L_{f}
, the forcing term can be neglected in the rescaled system (
7
). This gives

\delta,\ell\ll L_{f}:\quad\partial_{\tau}{\boldsymbol{U}}=\Lambda_{\boldsymbol{U}}\big[-{\boldsymbol{U}}\cdot\bnabla{\boldsymbol{U}}-\bnabla P+2c_{s}^{2}\alpha^{-2}\bnabla\cdot(\|{\boldsymbol{S}}\|{\boldsymbol{S}})\big],\quad\bnabla\cdot{{\boldsymbol{U}}}=0.

(10)

We call the system (
10
)
\alpha
-NSS, emphasizing that the dependence on the parameters
\ell
and
\delta
is realized only through their ratio
\alpha=\ell/\delta
. Based on these properties, we formulate the hypothesis of
hidden scale invariance
:

For small scales
\ell,\delta\ll L_{f}
, the statistics of the rescaled field
{\boldsymbol{U}}({\boldsymbol{X}},\tau)
, obtained

from the NSS solution
{\boldsymbol{u}}({\boldsymbol{x}},t)
, depend on
\ell
and
\delta
only through their ratio
\alpha=\ell/\delta
.

Statistical identities

The diagrammatic interpretation of this type of scale invariance is presented in Fig.
2
. The panels (a) and (b) show two independent diagrams corresponding to different filtering parameters
\delta
and
\delta^{\prime}=\lambda\delta
. When viewed at small scales and in a statistical sense, these diagrams merge on their right sides as shown in the panel (c), where
{\boldsymbol{U}}_{\alpha}
denotes a solution of the
\alpha
-NSS system (
10
). In other terms, the hidden scale invariance is understood as the statistical equivalence

{\boldsymbol{U}}_{\delta,\ell,{\boldsymbol{x}}_{0}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{law}}}{{=}}\,{\boldsymbol{U}}_{\lambda\delta,\lambda\ell,{\boldsymbol{x}}^{\prime}_{0}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{law}}}{{=}}{\boldsymbol{U}}_{\alpha}\ \ \textrm{for}\ \ \alpha=\ell/\delta.

(11)

The diagram in Fig.
2
(c) also reveals that the rescaled statistics for different values of
\alpha
and
\alpha^{\prime}=\alpha/\lambda
are related by the transformation
h_{\lambda}:{\boldsymbol{U}}_{\alpha}\mapsto{\boldsymbol{U}}_{\alpha^{\prime}}
prescribed by Eq. (
6
).
This provides an additional statistical identity

{\boldsymbol{U}}_{\alpha/\lambda}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{law}}}{{=}}h_{\lambda}[{\boldsymbol{U}}_{\alpha}].

(12)

HS in the inertial interval

A stronger form of HS develops in the inertial interval, i.e., at scales
\ell
defined by the condition
\delta\ll\ell\ll L_{f}
.
Here, the Smagorinsky dissipative term becomes negligible and the system (
10
) simplifies into

\delta\ll\ell\ll L:\quad\partial_{\tau}{\boldsymbol{U}}+\Lambda_{\boldsymbol{U}}\big[{\boldsymbol{U}}\cdot\bnabla{\boldsymbol{U}}+\bnabla P\big]=0,\quad\bnabla\cdot{{\boldsymbol{U}}}=0.

(13)

This system is independent of both
\delta
and
\ell
, and coincides with the rescaled system derived earlier in the framework of NS turbulence by
Mailybaev (2022a)
;
Mailybaev & Thalabard (2022)
.
This leads to the hypothesis of
inertial-interval hidden symmetry
:

The statistics of the rescaled field
{\boldsymbol{U}}({\boldsymbol{X}},\tau)
in the inertial interval are universal,

i.e. independent of the scale
\ell
and the dissipation mechanism.

This suggests that, despite the intermittency, the rescaled NSS and NS statistics coincide at the inertial interval scales.
The statistical identity (
12
) in the inertial interval simplifies into

{\boldsymbol{U}}_{\infty}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{law}}}{{=}}h_{\lambda}[{\boldsymbol{U}}_{\infty}],

(14)

where
{\boldsymbol{U}}_{\infty}
denotes a solution of system (
13
) obtained in the limit
\alpha\to\infty
.

HS in other SGS models

We observe that the concept of hidden scale invariance applies equally to hyperviscous Smagorinsky models, such as
\propto\!\delta^{2p}|\bnabla^{2}|^{p}\bnabla\cdot(\nu_{s}{\boldsymbol{s}})
.
More interestingly, modern strategies in LES simulations use implicit dissipation schemes in order to introduce backscattering effects; see,
e.g.
,
Pope (2000)
;
Lamballais et al. (2021)
.
In practice, these schemes are implemented by under-resolving the NSS through a (genuine) filtering operation, hereby
transforming the NSS equations (
1
) into

\partial_{t}{\boldsymbol{u}}=\big(-{\boldsymbol{u}}\cdot\bnabla{\boldsymbol{u}}-\bnabla p+\bnabla\cdot\left(2\nu_{s}{\boldsymbol{s}}\right)+{\boldsymbol{f}}\big)_{\delta},\quad\bnabla\cdot{\boldsymbol{u}}=0,

(15)

where the rhs is the filtered field. Consider the sharp spectral filter
G_{\delta}({\boldsymbol{x}})=(2\pi)^{-3}\int_{|{\boldsymbol{k}}|\leq ck_{\delta}}\!d^{3}{\boldsymbol{k}}e^{i{\boldsymbol{k}}\cdot{\boldsymbol{x}}}
defined as a Galerkin truncation at a given multiple
c
of the wavenumber
k_{\delta}=2\pi/\delta
. Then the kinematic rescaling of §
2
yields the filtered
\alpha
-NSS as

\partial_{\tau}{\boldsymbol{U}}=\big(\Lambda_{\boldsymbol{U}}[-{\boldsymbol{U}}\cdot\bnabla{\boldsymbol{U}}-\bnabla P+2c_{s}^{2}\alpha^{-2}\,\bnabla\cdot(\|{\boldsymbol{S}}\|{\boldsymbol{S}})+{\boldsymbol{F}}]\big)_{\alpha},\quad\bnabla\cdot{{\boldsymbol{U}}}=0

(16)

(see §
A.2
). Here the filtering operation
(\dots)_{\alpha}
applies wrt the rescaled variable
{\boldsymbol{X}}
at scale
\alpha=\ell/\delta
.
Hence, the HS extends to implicit LES schemes. Following
Mailybaev (2022a)
one can relate the HS of SGS models to their invariance with respect to space-time scaling and Galilean symmetry groups. From this point of view, the extension of HS to subgrid (dissipative) scales refers to the time-scale-invariant (quadratic in velocity) SGS model. On the contrary, the HS is limited to the inertial scales of the NS, because physical viscosity breaks the time-scale invariance
(
Mailybaev & Thalabard, 2022
)
.

Temporal averages

The statistics of the rescaled field
{\boldsymbol{U}}({\boldsymbol{X}},\tau)
can be accessed using different observables. In this work we limit our analysis to the longitudinal component
U_{\parallel}(\tau)={\boldsymbol{U}}({\boldsymbol{e}},\tau)\cdot{\boldsymbol{e}}
with the unit vector
{\boldsymbol{e}}=(1,0,0)
; other observables (not presented here) were also tested with similar conclusions.
The
\tau
-average of
|U_{\parallel}|^{p}
is defined as

\left\langle{|U_{\parallel}(\tau)|^{p}}\right\rangle_{\tau}=\lim_{T\to\infty}\dfrac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}|U_{\parallel}(\tau)|^{p}d\tau,

(17)

from which the usual statistical properties such as variance, skewness, flatness, etc. are estimated.
The corresponding probability density function (PDF) is obtained from the average of the Dirac delta function as
p_{\parallel}(y)=\left\langle{\delta\left(U_{\parallel}(\tau)-y\right)}\right\rangle_{\tau}
.
We emphasize that the average (
17
) is expressed in terms of the rescaled Lagrangian clock
\tau
. However,
it can be efficiently estimated as space-time average over the original fields using a conversion formula relying on spatial homogeneity and incompressibility. For
|U_{\parallel}|^{p}
the conversion formula states

\left\langle{|U_{\parallel}(\tau)|^{p}}\right\rangle_{\tau}=\left\langle{\left|\dfrac{\Delta_{\ell}u_{\parallel}({\boldsymbol{x}},t)}{a({\boldsymbol{x}},t)}\right|^{p}\,J({\boldsymbol{x}},t)}\right\rangle_{{\boldsymbol{x}},t},

(18)

where
\left\langle{\cdot}\right\rangle_{{\boldsymbol{x}},t}
denotes the space-time average in the original coordinates and
\Delta_{\ell}u_{\parallel}=\big({\boldsymbol{u}}({\boldsymbol{x}}+\ell{\boldsymbol{e}})-{\boldsymbol{u}}({\boldsymbol{x}})\big)\cdot{\boldsymbol{e}}
.
In other words, Eq. (
18
) transform quasi-Lagrangian averages into usual (Eulerian) ones, that one can easily compute without having to track any fluid trajectory.
Here
J({\boldsymbol{x}},t)
is the Jacobian factor due to the change of time
\tau\mapsto t
as

J({\boldsymbol{x}},t)=\frac{a({\boldsymbol{x}},t)}{\left\langle{a({\boldsymbol{x}},t)}\right\rangle_{{\boldsymbol{x}},t}},\quad a({\boldsymbol{x}},t)=\Big(\int_{|\mathbf{X}|\leq 1}\big|\Delta_{\ell}{\boldsymbol{u}}({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{X}},t)\big|^{2}d^{3}{\boldsymbol{X}}\Big)^{1/2}.

(19)

We refer to
Mailybaev & Thalabard (2022)
for technical details of this derivation.
Expressions similar to Eq. (
18
) follow for other single-time observables.

5
Concluding remarks

Similar to the NS case, the hidden scale invariance of classical LES models involves a quasi-Lagrangian formulation, in which the timeframe is adjusted dynamically to an amplitude of local fluctuations at a scale
\ell
.
Statistics of the resulting field depend on the observation scale
\ell
and on the filtering scale
\delta
only through their ratio
\ell/\delta
. They become universal in the inertial interval, i.e. independent of both
\ell
and
\delta
and coincident with the analogous statistics of 3D Navier-Stokes turbulence.
The validity of the hidden scale invariance at both inertial and dissipative scales in the SGS models, in contrast to its restriction to inertial scales only for the NS, allows a very accurate numerical verification of the hidden self-similarity and makes LES useful for the theoretical study of intermittency in turbulence.
In particular, we have in mind the potential use of Smagorinsky (or variants) as a model where one can explain the anomalous scaling in terms of the hidden symmetry, as was done earlier in shell models
(
Mailybaev, 2022b
;
Mailybaev, 2023
;
Thalabard & Mailybaev, 2024
)
and 2D passive scalar turbulence
(
Calascibetta et al., 2025
)
.
The hidden self-similarity across the entire small-scale range may also have applications to the design and optimization of SGS models; see
(
Biferale et al., 2017
;
Domingues Lemos & Mailybaev, 2024
;
Freitas et al., 2025
)
for similar studies in shell models.

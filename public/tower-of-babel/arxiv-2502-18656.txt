Quantum data-hiding scheme using orthogonal separable states
Donghoon Ha, Jeong San Kim

Abstract
We consider bipartite quantum state discrimination and present a quantum data-hiding scheme utilizing an orthogonal separable state ensemble. Using a bound on local minimum-error discrimination, we provide a sufficient condition for the separable state ensemble to be used in constructing a quantum data-hiding scheme. Our results are illustrated with various examples in bipartite quantum systems. As our scheme employs separable states of low-dimensional quantum systems, it becomes more feasible for practical implementation.



I
Introduction

In the realm of information theory, data hiding refers to a communication protocol that encodes specific data and distributes it among multiple users. Each user cannot decrypt the hidden data with only their received information, requiring cooperation with others to access it. Classical data hiding involves dividing the data into classical information and distributing it to users
[
1
]
. The hidden data can only be recovered when a sufficient number of these divided pieces are combined through classical communication. Thus, blocking classical communication is essential for hiding data in classical schemes.

Quantum data hiding encodes classical data into multipartite quantum states, providing a fundamentally stronger level of concealment compared with classical methods
[
2
,
3
,
4
]
. While the data remains hidden from each user’s local operations, quantum data hiding can maintain concealment even if classical communication between users is allowed. Specifically, the probability of successfully recovering the hidden data using only
local operations and classical communication
(LOCC) is nearly equivalent to random guessing. Successful recovery requires global measurements that involve quantum channels, shared entanglement, or direct interactions.

Since the initial use of Bell-state ensembles to hide one classical bit, there have been several quantum data-hiding protocols proposed not only in bipartite quantum systems, but also in multipartite quantum systems (involving multiple users)
[
5
,
6
,
7
,
8
]
.
These protocols typically employ orthogonal state ensembles to ensure perfect data recovery through appropriate global measurements.
The use of entangled states in these ensembles may seem the reason of the nonlocality making the data hidden from LOCC measurements.

Interestingly, it has also been demonstrated that quantum state ensembles containing only separable states can also be used to construct data-hiding schemes
[
9
]
. However, the separable states used in the protocol are non-orthogonal, and data recovery through appropriate global measurements is only asymptotically perfect with respect to the dimensionality.
This naturally raises the question of whether quantum data hiding can be constructed using only orthogonal separable states.

Here, we present a quantum data-hiding scheme utilizing an orthogonal separable state ensemble. The orthogonality of these states ensures perfect data recovery without reliance on asymptotic methods. While our scheme does asymptotically conceal classical data from LOCC measurements, it employs separable states of low-dimensional quantum systems repeatedly, making it more feasible for practical implementation.

This paper is organized as follows:
In Sec.
II
, we first consider the discrimination of two bipartite quantum states and provide a bound on the optimal local discrimination when the state ensemble possesses specific properties related to partial transposition.
In Sec.
III
, we introduce a quantum data-hiding scheme utilizing an orthogonal state ensemble that exhibits these same properties associated with partial transposition.
In Sec.
IV
, we establish a sufficient condition for an ensemble of two bipartite quantum states to be used in constructing a one-bit hiding scheme.
Our results are illustrated with examples of orthogonal separable state ensembles.
In Sec.
V
, we provide a summary of our findings and explore potential avenues for future research.

II
Minimum-error discrimination between two bipartite quantum states

In bipartite quantum systems held by two players, Alice and Bob, a state is described by a density operator
\rho
, that is, a positive-semidefinite operator
\rho\succeq 0
with
\Tr\rho=1
, acting on a bipartite complex Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}=\mathbb{C}^{d_{\sf A}}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d_{\sf B}}
.
A measurement is expressed by a positive operator-valued measure
\{M_{i}\}_{i}
, that is, a set of positive-semidefinite operators
M_{i}\succeq 0
acting on
\mathcal{H}
and satisfying
\sum_{i}M_{i}=\mathbbm{1}
, where
\mathbbm{1}
is the identity operator acting on
\mathcal{H}
.
When
\{M_{i}\}_{i}
is performed on a quantum system prepared with
\rho
, the probability of obtaining the measurement outcome corresponding to
M_{j}
is
\Tr(\rho M_{j})
.

Let us consider the situation of discriminating two bipartite quantum states
\rho_{0}
and
\rho_{1}
prepared with probabilities
\eta_{0}
and
\eta_{1}
, respectively. We use the term
state discrimination of an ensemble

\mathcal{E}=\{\eta_{0},\rho_{0};\eta_{1},\rho_{1}\}.

(1)

If we use a measurement

\mathcal{M}=\{M_{0},M_{1}\}

(2)

to discriminate the states of
\mathcal{E}
,
our decision rule is the following; for each
i=0,1
, the prepared state is guessed to be
\rho_{i}
when
M_{i}
is detected.
The
minimum-error discrimination
of
\mathcal{E}
is to achieve the maximum average probability of correctly guessing the prepared state from
\mathcal{E}
, that is,

p_{\sf G}(\mathcal{E})=\max_{\mathcal{M}}\sum_{i=0}^{1}\eta_{i}\Tr(\rho_{i}M_{i}),

(3)

where the maximum is taken over all possible measurements
[
10
]
.

Definition 1
.

A Hermitian operator
E
on
\mathcal{H}
is called
positive partial transpose
(PPT) if the partially transposed operator is positive semidefinite; that is,

E^{\PT}\succeq 0

(4)

where the superscript
\PT
is to indicate the partial transposition
[
11
,
12
]
.

A measurement
\{M_{i}\}_{i}
is called a
PPT measurement
if
M_{i}
is PPT for all
i
, and a measurement is called a LOCC measurement if it can be realized by LOCC.
When the available measurements are limited PPT measurements, we denote the maximum success probability by

p_{\sf PPT}(\mathcal{E})=\max_{\mathsf{PPT}\,\mathcal{M}}\sum_{i=0}^{1}\eta_{i}\Tr(\rho_{i}M_{i}).

(5)

Similarly, we denote

p_{\sf L}(\mathcal{E})=\max_{\mathsf{LOCC}\,\mathcal{M}}\sum_{i=0}^{1}\eta_{i}\Tr(\rho_{i}M_{i}),

(6)

where the maximum is taken over all possible LOCC measurements.
From the definitions of
p_{\sf G}(\mathcal{E})
,
p_{\sf PPT}(\mathcal{E})
and
p_{\sf L}(\mathcal{E})
, we have

p_{\sf L}(\mathcal{E})\leqslant p_{\sf PPT}(\mathcal{E})\leqslant p_{\sf G}(\mathcal{E})

(7)

where the first inequality is from the fact that every LOCC measurement is a PPT measurement
[
13
]
.
Since guessing the prepared state as the state with the greatest probability is obviously a LOCC measurement, we also note that

\tfrac{1}{2}\leqslant\max\{\eta_{0},\eta_{1}\}\leqslant p_{\sf L}(\mathcal{E}).

(8)

For a Hermitian operator
H
acting on the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, we define
q(H)
as the quantity

q(H)=\tfrac{1}{2}+\Tr|H|

(9)

where
|H|
is the positive square root of
H^{\dagger}H
, that is,

|H|=\sqrt{H^{\dagger}H}.

(10)

The following theorem provides a sufficient condition for
q(H)
to be an upper bound of
p_{\sf PPT}(\mathcal{E})
.

Theorem 1
.

For an ensemble
\mathcal{E}=\{\eta_{0},\rho_{0};\eta_{1},\rho_{1}\}
and

\Lambda_{\mathcal{E}}:=\eta_{0}\rho_{0}-\eta_{1}\rho_{1},

(11)

if there exists a Hermitian operator
H
satisfying

H+H^{\PT}=\Lambda_{\mathcal{E}},

(12)

then
q(H)
is an upper bound of
p_{\sf PPT}(\mathcal{E})
, that is,

p_{\sf PPT}(\mathcal{E})\leqslant q(H).

(13)

Proof.

By using the definition of
\Lambda_{\mathcal{E}}
in Eq. (
11
) and the notation

H^{(\pm)}=\tfrac{1}{2}(|H|\pm H),

(14)

we can easily see that

(\eta_{0}\rho_{0}-\eta_{1}\rho_{1})-(H^{(+)}-H^{(-)})-(H^{(+)\PT}-H^{(-)\PT})=\Lambda_{\mathcal{E}}-H-H^{\PT}=\mathbb{O},

(15)

where the last equality is from Eq. (
12
) and
\mathbb{O}
is the zero operator acting on the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}=\mathbb{C}^{d_{\sf A}}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d_{\sf B}}
.
From Eq. (
15
), we have

\eta_{0}\rho_{0}+H^{(-)}+H^{(-)\PT}=\eta_{1}\rho_{1}+H^{(+)}+H^{(+)\PT}.

(16)

Since
\Tr(AB)=\Tr(A^{\PT}B^{\PT})
for any Hermitian operators
A
and
B
, it follows from
H^{(\pm)}\succeq 0
that

\Tr[(H^{(\pm)}+H^{(\pm)\PT})E]=\Tr(H^{(\pm)}E)+\Tr(H^{(\pm)}E^{\PT})\geqslant 0

(17)

for any positive-semidefinite PPT operator
E
.

For any PPT measurement
\mathcal{M}=\{M_{0},M_{1}\}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{1}\eta_{i}\Tr(\rho_{i}M_{i})

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\Tr(\eta_{0}\rho_{0}M_{0})+\Tr[(H^{(-)}+H^{(-)\PT})M_{0}]+\Tr(\eta_{1}\rho_{1}M_{1})+\Tr[(H^{(+)}+H^{(+)\PT})M_{1}]

(18)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Tr[(\eta_{0}\rho_{0}+H^{(-)}+H^{(-)\PT})M_{0}]+\Tr[(\eta_{1}\rho_{1}+H^{(+)}+H^{(+)\PT})M_{1}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}\Tr[(\eta_{0}\rho_{0}+H^{(-)}+H^{(-)\PT})(M_{0}+M_{1})]+\tfrac{1}{2}\Tr[(\eta_{1}\rho_{1}+H^{(+)}+H^{(+)\PT})(M_{0}+M_{1})]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}+\Tr H^{(-)}+\Tr H^{(+)}=\tfrac{1}{2}+\Tr|H|=q(H),

where the first inequality is from Inequality (
17
), the second equality is due to Eq. (
16
), the third equality is by
M_{0}+M_{1}=\mathbbm{1}
, the fourth equality follows from Eq. (
14
), and the last equality is from the definition of
q(H)
in Eq. (
9
).
Thus, the definition of
p_{\sf PPT}(\mathcal{E})
in Eq. (
5
) and Inequality (
18
) lead us to Inequality (
13
).
∎

We remark that Eq. (
12
) in Theorem
1
is equivalent to

\Lambda_{\mathcal{E}}^{\PT}=\Lambda_{\mathcal{E}},

(19)

that is,
\Lambda_{\mathcal{E}}
is invariant under partial transposition.
However, we further remark that the Hermitian operator
H
satisfying Eq. (
12
) is not unique, in general.
The following theorem shows the existence of a Hermitian operator
H
satisfying Eq. (
12
) and
p_{\sf PPT}(\mathcal{E})=q(H)
when Condition (
19
) is satisfied.

Theorem 2
.

For an ensemble
\mathcal{E}=\{\eta_{0},\rho_{0};\eta_{1},\rho_{1}\}
with Condition (
19
), we have

p_{\sf PPT}(\mathcal{E})=\min q(H)

(20)

over all possible Hermitian operators
H
satisfying Eq. (
12
), where
p_{\sf PPT}(\mathcal{E})
and
q(H)
are defined in Eqs. (
5
) and (
9
), respectively.

Proof.

Due to Theorem
1
, it is enough to show the existence of a Hermitian operator
H
satisfying Eq. (
12
) and
p_{\sf PPT}(\mathcal{E})=q(H)
.
We first note that there exists a Hermitian operator
E
satisfying

\displaystyle p_{\sf PPT}(\mathcal{E})=\Tr E,

(21a)

\displaystyle E-\eta_{i}\rho_{i}=P_{i}+Q_{i}^{\PT}

(21b)

for all
i=0,1
, where
P_{i}
and
Q_{i}
are positive-semidefinite operators
[
14
]
.

For the Hermitian operator

H=\tfrac{1}{2}(P_{1}+Q_{1}-P_{0}-Q_{0}),

(22)

Eq. (
12
) holds because

\displaystyle H+H^{\PT}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}(P_{1}+Q_{1}^{\PT}-P_{0}-Q_{0}^{\PT})+\tfrac{1}{2}(P_{1}+Q_{1}^{\PT}-P_{0}-Q_{0}^{\PT})^{\PT}

(23)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}(\eta_{0}\rho_{0}-\eta_{1}\rho_{1})+\tfrac{1}{2}(\eta_{0}\rho_{0}-\eta_{1}\rho_{1})^{\PT}=\tfrac{1}{2}\Lambda_{\mathcal{E}}+\tfrac{1}{2}\Lambda_{\mathcal{E}}^{\PT}=\Lambda_{\mathcal{E}},

where the second equality is by Eq. (
21b
), the third equality is from the definition of
\Lambda_{\mathcal{E}}
in Eq. (
11
), and the last equality is due to Condition (
19
).
Moreover,
p_{\sf PPT}(\mathcal{E})=q(H)
is satisfied because

\displaystyle p_{\sf PPT}(\mathcal{E})

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle q(H)=\tfrac{1}{2}+\Tr|H|

(24)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}\Tr(P_{1}+Q_{1})+\tfrac{1}{2}\Tr(P_{0}+Q_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}\Tr(P_{1}+Q_{1}^{\PT})+\tfrac{1}{2}\Tr(P_{0}+Q_{0}^{\PT})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Tr E=p_{\sf PPT}(\mathcal{E}),

where the first inequality is from Theorem
1
,
the first equality is due to Eq. (
9
),
the second inequality is by Eq. (
22
) and the triangle inequality of trace norm, the second equality follows from
\Tr A^{\PT}=\Tr A
for any Hermitian operator
A
, the third equality is due to Eq. (
21b
), and the last equality is from Eq. (
21a
).
∎

III
Multi-fold ensemble and hiding one classical bit

In this section, we first introduce the notion of a
multifold ensemble
and provide a sufficient condition for two-state ensembles to be used in constructing a scheme for hiding one classical bit. In our scheme, the hidden bit can be fully recovered using appropriate global measurements, while LOCC measurements provide arbitrarily little information about the globally accessible bit.

For a positive integer
L
, we use
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{L}
to denote the set of all
L
-dimensional vectors over
\mathbb{Z}_{2}=\{0,1\}
, that is,

\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{L}=\{(b_{1},\ldots,b_{L})\,|\,b_{1},\ldots,b_{L}\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}\}.

(25)

Equivalently, we can also consider
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{L}
as the set of all
L
-bit strings.
For the ensemble
\mathcal{E}
in Eq. (
1
) and each string
\vec{b}\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{L}
, we define

\eta_{\vec{b}}=\prod_{l=1}^{L}\eta_{b_{l}},~~\rho_{\vec{b}}=\bigotimes_{l=1}^{L}\rho_{b_{l}},

(26)

where
b_{l}
is the
l
th entry of
\vec{b}=(b_{1},\ldots,b_{L})
.
We further denote by
\mathcal{E}^{\otimes L}
the
L
-fold ensemble
of
\mathcal{E}
, that is,

\mathcal{E}^{\otimes L}=\{\eta_{\vec{b}},\rho_{\vec{b}}\}_{\vec{b}\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{L}},

(27)

in which the state
\rho_{\vec{b}}
is prepared with the probability
\eta_{\vec{b}}
.
For each string
\vec{b}\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{L}
, we define
\omega_{2}(\vec{b})
as the modulo 2 summation of all the entries in
\vec{b}=(b_{1},\ldots,b_{L})
, that is,

\omega_{2}(\vec{b})=b_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus b_{L}\in\mathbb{Z}_{2},

(28)

where
\oplus
denotes the modulo 2 addition in
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
.

Let us consider the situation of guessing
\omega_{2}(\vec{b})
of the prepared state
\rho_{\vec{b}}
from the ensemble
\mathcal{E}^{\otimes L}
in Eq. (
27
).
This situation is equivalent to discriminating the two bipartite quantum states
\rho_{0}^{(L)}
and
\rho_{1}^{(L)}
prepared with the probabilities
\eta_{0}^{(L)}
and
\eta_{1}^{(L)}
, respectively, where

\eta_{i}^{(L)}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\vec{b}\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{L}\\
\omega_{2}(\vec{b})=i\end{subarray}}\eta_{\vec{b}},~~\rho_{i}^{(L)}=\frac{1}{\eta_{i}^{(L)}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\vec{b}\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{L}\\
\omega_{2}(\vec{b})=i\end{subarray}}\eta_{\vec{b}}\rho_{\vec{b}},~~i=0,1.

(29)

In other words, quantum state discrimination of the ensemble

\mathcal{E}^{(L)}=\{\eta_{0}^{(L)},\rho_{0}^{(L)};\eta_{1}^{(L)},\rho_{1}^{(L)}\}.

(30)

We note that

\Lambda_{\mathcal{E}^{(L)}}=\eta_{0}^{(L)}\rho_{0}^{(L)}-\eta_{1}^{(L)}\rho_{1}^{(L)}=(\eta_{0}\rho_{0}-\eta_{1}\rho_{1})^{\otimes L}=\Lambda_{\mathcal{E}}^{\otimes L},

(31)

where
\Lambda_{\mathcal{E}}
is defined in Eq. (
11
) and the second equality
is satisfied because Eq. (
29
) implies

\eta_{i}^{(L)}\rho_{i}^{(L)}=\frac{1}{2}\Big[(\eta_{0}\rho_{0}+\eta_{1}\rho_{1})^{\otimes L}+(-1)^{i}(\eta_{0}\rho_{0}-\eta_{1}\rho_{1})^{\otimes L}\Big],~i=0,1.

(32)

Now, let us consider a bipartite
orthogonal
quantum state ensemble
\mathcal{E}=\{\eta_{0},\rho_{0};\eta_{1},\rho_{1}\}
satisfying

\lim_{L\rightarrow\infty}p_{\sf L}(\mathcal{E}^{(L)})=\frac{1}{2}.

(33)

This ensemble
\mathcal{E}
can be used to construct a quantum data-hiding scheme that hides one classical bit
[
7
]
.
The scheme consists of two steps:

Step 1. The hider, the extra party, first choose a state
\rho_{b_{1}}
from
\mathcal{E}
with the corresponding probability
\eta_{b_{1}}
for
b_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}
and share it with Alice and Bob.
The hider repeats this process
L
times, so that
\rho_{b_{l}}
is chosen with probability
\eta_{b_{l}}
in the
l
th repetition for
l=1,\ldots,L
.
This whole procedure is equivalent to the situation in which the hider first prepares the state

\rho_{\vec{b}}=\rho_{b_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\rho_{b_{L}}

(34)

from the ensemble
\mathcal{E}^{\otimes L}
in Eq. (
27
) with the corresponding probability
\eta_{\vec{b}}
for
\vec{b}=(b_{1},\ldots,b_{L})\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{L}
and sends it to Alice and Bob.

Step 2. To hide one classical bit
x\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, the hider broadcasts the classical information
z
to Alice and Bob,

z=x\oplus y

(35)

where

y=\omega_{2}(\vec{b})

(36)

for the prepared state
\rho_{\vec{b}}
in Eq. (
34
).

As the classical information
z
in Eq. (
35
) was broadcasted, guessing the data
x
is equivalent to guessing the data
y
in Eq. (
36
).
Since the situation of guessing the data
y
is equivalent to the state discrimination of
\mathcal{E}^{(L)}
, the maximum average probability of correctly guessing the data
x
is equal to the maximum average probability of discriminating the states from the ensemble
\mathcal{E}^{(L)}
in Eq. (
30
), that is,
p_{\sf G}(\mathcal{E}^{(L)})
.
Similarly, the maximum average probability of correctly guessing the data
x
using only LOCC measurements becomes
p_{\sf L}(\mathcal{E}^{(L)})
.

The orthogonality between the states of
\mathcal{E}
implies that the states of
\mathcal{E}^{\otimes L}
in Eq. (
27
) are mutually orthogonal.
In this case, the states
\rho_{0}^{(L)}
and
\rho_{1}^{(L)}
in Eq. (
29
) are orthogonal.
Thus, we have

p_{\sf G}(\mathcal{E}^{(L)})=1.

(37)

In other words, the data
x
are accessible when Alice and Bob can collaborate to use global measurements.
On the other hand, Eq. (
33
) means
p_{\sf L}(\mathcal{E}^{(L)})
can be arbitrarily close to
1/2
with the choice of an appropriately large
L
. Thus, the globally accessible data
x
are perfectly hidden asymptotically if Alice and Bob can use only LOCC measurements.

From Inequalities (
7
) and (
8
), we have

\tfrac{1}{2}\leqslant p_{\sf L}(\mathcal{E}^{(L)})\leqslant p_{\sf PPT}(\mathcal{E}^{(L)})

(38)

for each positive integer
L
.
To show Eq. (
33
), it is enough to show that
p_{\sf PPT}(\mathcal{E}^{(L)})
converges to
1/2
as
L
gets larger.
The following theorem provides a sufficient condition for
\{p_{\sf PPT}(\mathcal{E}^{(L)})\}_{L}
to be a monotonically decreasing sequence with respect to
L
.
As
\{p_{\sf PPT}(\mathcal{E}^{(L)})\}_{L}
is bounded below by
1/2
, the monotonicity of the sequence assures that
\{p_{\sf PPT}(\mathcal{E}^{(L)})\}_{L}
is a convergent sequence.

Theorem 3
.

For an ensemble
\mathcal{E}=\{\eta_{0},\rho_{0};\eta_{1},\rho_{1}\}
with Condition (
19
),
p_{\sf PPT}(\mathcal{E}^{(L)})
is monotonically decreasing as
L
increases.

Proof.

For an arbitrary positive integer
L
, we have

\Lambda_{\mathcal{E}^{(L)}}^{\PT}=(\Lambda_{\mathcal{E}}^{\otimes L})^{\PT}=(\Lambda_{\mathcal{E}}^{\otimes L})=\Lambda_{\mathcal{E}^{(L)}},

(39)

where the first and last equalities are from Eq. (
31
) and the second equality is due to Condition (
19
).
From Theorem
2
, there exists a Hermitian operator
H
satisfying

\displaystyle H+H^{\PT}=\Lambda_{\mathcal{E}^{(L)}},

(40a)

\displaystyle p_{\sf PPT}(\mathcal{E}^{(L)})=q(H).

(40b)

For the Hermitian operator

H^{\prime}=\frac{1}{2}H\otimes\Lambda_{\mathcal{E}}+\frac{1}{2}\Lambda_{\mathcal{E}}\otimes H,

(41)

we have

\displaystyle H^{\prime}+H^{\prime\PT}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}(H+H^{\PT})\otimes\Lambda_{\mathcal{E}}+\tfrac{1}{2}\Lambda_{\mathcal{E}}\otimes(H+H^{\PT})

(42)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}\Lambda_{\mathcal{E}^{(L)}}\otimes\Lambda_{\mathcal{E}}+\tfrac{1}{2}\Lambda_{\mathcal{E}}\otimes\Lambda_{\mathcal{E}^{(L)}}=\Lambda_{\mathcal{E}^{(L+1)}},

where the first equality is due to Condition (
19
), the second equality is from Eq. (
40a
), and the last equality is by Eq. (
31
).
Moreover, we have

\displaystyle p_{\sf PPT}(\mathcal{E}^{(L+1)})

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle q(H^{\prime})=\tfrac{1}{2}+\Tr|H^{\prime}|

(43)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}+\Tr|\tfrac{1}{2}H\otimes\Lambda_{\mathcal{E}}+\tfrac{1}{2}\Lambda_{\mathcal{E}}\otimes H|

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}+\Tr|\tfrac{1}{2}H\otimes\Lambda_{\mathcal{E}}|+\Tr|\tfrac{1}{2}\Lambda_{\mathcal{E}}\otimes H|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}+\Tr|H|\Tr|\Lambda_{\mathcal{E}}|\leqslant\tfrac{1}{2}+\Tr|H|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q(H)=p_{\sf PPT}(\mathcal{E}^{(L)}),

where the first inequality is from Eq. (
42
) and Theorem
1
, the second inequality is by the triangle inequality of trace norm, the third equality is due to
\Tr|A\otimes B|=\Tr|A|\Tr|B|
for any Hermitian operators
A
and
B
, the last inequality is from the definition of
\Lambda_{\mathcal{E}}
in Eq. (
11
) together with the triangle inequality of trace norm, and the last equality is due to Eq. (
40b
).
Since the choice of
L
can be arbitrary,
p_{\sf PPT}(\mathcal{E}^{(L)})
is monotonically decreasing as a function of
L
.
∎

Corollary 1
.

For an ensemble
\mathcal{E}=\{\eta_{0},\rho_{0};\eta_{1},\rho_{1}\}
with Condition (
19
), if a subsequence of
\{p_{\sf PPT}(\mathcal{E}^{(L)})\}_{L}
converges to
1/2
, then Eq. (
33
) is satisfied.

IV
Quantum data-hiding scheme using separable state ensemble

In this section, we use Corollary
1
to provide a quantum data-hiding scheme using separable state ensemble.
To obtain a sufficient condition for the convergence of
\{p_{\sf PPT}(\mathcal{E}^{(L)})\}_{L}
to
1/2
, we consider the subsequence
\{p_{\sf PPT}(\mathcal{E}^{(3^{m})})\}_{m}
of
\{p_{\sf PPT}(\mathcal{E}^{(L)})\}_{L}
, and obtain a condition for the convergence of
\{p_{\sf PPT}(\mathcal{E}^{(3^{m})})\}_{m}
to
1/2
.
More precisely, we provide a sufficient condition for an upper bound of
p_{\sf PPT}(\mathcal{E}^{(3^{m})})
to converge to
1/2
as
m
gets larger.

The following lemma provides a method to obtain an upper bound of
p_{\sf PPT}(\mathcal{E}^{(3)})
from a Hermitian operator
H
satisfying Eq. (
12
).

Lemma 1
.

For an ensemble
\mathcal{E}=\{\eta_{0},\rho_{0};\eta_{1},\rho_{1}\}
, if there exists a Hermitian operator
H
satisfying Eq. (
12
) and

\Tr|H|+\Tr|H^{\PT}|\leqslant 1,

(44)

then there exists a Hermitian operator
H^{\prime}
satisfying

\displaystyle H^{\prime}+H^{\prime\PT}=\Lambda_{\mathcal{E}}^{\otimes 3},

(45a)

\displaystyle\Tr|H^{\prime}|\leqslant f(\Tr|H|),

(45b)

\displaystyle\Tr|H^{\prime}|+\Tr|H^{\prime\PT}|\leqslant 1

(45c)

where
f
is the polynomial function defined as

f(x)=x^{2}(3-2x).

(46)

Before we prove Lemma
1
, we first remark that Lemma
1
directly leads us to an upper bound of
p_{\sf PPT}(\mathcal{E}^{(3)})
as

p_{\sf PPT}(\mathcal{E}^{(3)})\leqslant q(H^{\prime})\leqslant\tfrac{1}{2}+f(\Tr|H|),

(47)

where the first inequality is from Theorem
1
and Eq. (
31
), and the last inequality is due to Inequalities (
45b
).

Proof.

Let us consider

V
Discussion

We have considered the discrimination of two bipartite quantum states and provide a quantum data-hiding scheme using orthogonal separable states. We have established a bound on the local minimum-error discrimination for bipartite state ensembles with specific properties related to partial transposition. Based on this bound, we have introduced a quantum data-hiding scheme that utilizes an orthogonal state ensemble exhibiting these same properties. We have further established a sufficient condition for an ensemble of two bipartite quantum states to be used in constructing a one-bit hiding scheme.
Finally, our results have been illustrated with examples of orthogonal separable state ensembles satisfying this condition.

Our data-hiding scheme ensures perfect data recovery without relying on asymptotic methods due to the orthogonality of the states. Furthermore, classical data is asymptotically concealed from LOCC measurements by repeatedly employing separable states of low-dimensional quantum systems. This makes our scheme more feasible for practical implementation especially in noisy intermediate-scale quantum environments.

An interesting direction for future research is to explore separable-state data-hiding schemes hiding more than single bit or schemes for multi-users in multipartite quantum systems. It is also an interesting and important direction for future research to investigate which types of quantum correlations are necessary for quantum data hiding.

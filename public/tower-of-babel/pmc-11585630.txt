A motor bearing fault diagnosis model based on multi-adversarial domain adaptation
Authors: Xin-Ming Liu, Rui-Ming Zhang, Jin-Ping Li, Yu-Fei Xu, Kun Li
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
In response to the weakened capability of feature transfer and parameter distribution alignment across domains due to significant differences in data distribution collected by different devices, this paper constructs a motor bearing fault diagnosis model based on multi-adversarial domain adaptation. Initially, an improved residual network is employed as the feature extraction module to enhance feature extraction capabilities. It then incorporates a Selective Kernel Network (SKNet) to implement attention mechanisms on convolutional kernels, and a Global Context Network (GCNet) to effectively utilize global contextual information for re-weighting across different channels. Additionally, the model uses a multi-kernel maximum mean discrepancy to measure the distribution between domains and classes, establishing a dynamic adjustment factor in conjunction with multiple domain discriminators to modulate the importance of marginal and conditional distributions. Ultimately, the proposed model was applied to transfer experiments across different operating conditions and devices, demonstrating excellent diagnostic results.

Introduction

Electric motors play a pivotal role in industrial production, primarily responsible for converting electrical energy into mechanical energy. The stability, safety, and production efficiency of motors are critical indicators. Timely early fault diagnosis not only reduces maintenance costs but also significantly lowers economic losses caused by failures. Bearing failures account for over 40% of motor failures and can lead to other types of malfunctions1. Therefore, monitoring and diagnosing the state of bearings is crucial for maintaining the normal operation of motor equipment.

Intelligent fault diagnosis can process vast amounts of monitoring data to distinguish the health conditions of machinery. In recent years, various intelligent fault diagnosis methods have been studied. Deep learning leverages its inherent feature extraction capabilities, avoiding the cumbersome manual feature extraction process required in traditional machine learning2–4. A literature review reveals that datasets used to validate the effectiveness of various intelligent fault diagnosis methods meet the following two conditions: (1) Labeled data with fault information, and (2) Training and testing data from the same probability distribution. Firstly, machines may not be allowed to operate until failure, as an unexpected failure can often lead to machine collapse or even catastrophic accidents. In such cases, fault data cannot be obtained. Secondly, machines typically undergo a prolonged degradation process from health to failure. This means that obtaining fault data for machines is both time-consuming and expensive5. Under actual production conditions, motor bearing signals are often noisy, and machines operate under variable conditions over the long term, making it difficult to ensure that the collected information has the same data distribution. Due to the scarcity of fault data, changes in working conditions, measurement points, and acquisition equipment have a significant impact on data distribution, and deep learning alone cannot meet diagnostic requirements.

The development of transfer learning has introduced new solutions for diagnostics related to sample data differences, using data from the source domain for training and testing data from the target domain, thereby addressing cross-domain diagnostics and the issue of unlabeled samples in the target domain to a certain extent. Li Chuanjiang et al.6reviewed the causes and impacts of data distribution shifts and proposed several mainstream methods, such as data augmentation, transfer learning, federated learning7, and few-shot learning8,9, to address these challenges, providing valuable insights for the development of data-efficient prognostics and health management (PHM) technologies.

Domain adaptation is an important branch of transfer learning, which uses a large amount of labeled data from the source domain to train models and perform diagnostic testing on samples from the target domain, effectively avoiding the issue of unlabeled or small-sample target domain data10. Reference11maps source and target domain samples to a low-dimensional latent space using a kernel function, measures the relevant distance with Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD), and simultaneously reduces the marginal and conditional distributions between different domain samples. Reference12considers the impact of both conditional and marginal distributions by introducing joint distribution adaptation, using the correlation of labeled data from the source domain to adapt to the unlabeled conditions of the target domain, ensuring more accurate distribution matching. Reference13 proposes a dynamic system fault diagnosis method that addresses the issue of inconsistent probability distributions and effectively handles changes in system distribution parameters.

The above studies have shown that domain adaptation (DA) has achieved good diagnostic results in the field of fault diagnosis. In response to the issue of low diagnostic accuracy under noisy interference and variable operating conditions for bearing diagnosis, this paper proposes a motor bearing fault diagnosis model based on multi-adversarial domain adaptation (MADA). Firstly, the principles and methods of domain adaptation in transfer learning14,15and Generative Adversarial Networks were analyzed; According to reference16, the use of MMD and GRL can help the model achieve better domain alignment; then, the residual block was improved with SKNet, and GCNet was introduced to enhance the residual network as a feature extractor, achieving conditional distribution alignment through multi-domain discriminators. Multi-kernel Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MK-MMD) technology was used for marginal distribution alignment and as a measure of the scale of domain and class distance, effectively assessing the importance of both distributions and completing the model construction. Finally, by conducting cross-domain transfer experiments between different operating conditions and different devices, this method achieved more ideal diagnostic accuracy compared to traditional diagnostic methods.

The main insights and contributions of this paper are summarized as follows:

1) Improved Feature Extraction: We have developed an enhanced residual network integrated with Selective Kernel Network (SKNet) and Global Context Network (GCNet), significantly enhancing the model’s ability to focus on relevant information.

2) Joint Domain Adaptation: By using Multi-kernel Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MK-MMD) and multiple adversarial domain discriminators, our model aligns marginal and conditional distributions, ensuring effective adaptation across domains.

The successful application of this model in motor bearing fault diagnosis under different conditions and devices will facilitate the practical application of intelligent fault diagnosis.

Theoretical analysis

Selective kernel network

In traditional Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), the receptive fields of neurons in each layer are typically fixed. However, neuroscience research indicates that the size of receptive fields in the visual cortex can adjust based on stimuli, an aspect often overlooked in CNN design. Li et al.17 introduced the SKNet, which adjusts the size of convolutional kernels through a dynamic selection mechanism to adapt to the multi-scale characteristics of the input. SKNet aggregates information from multiple kernels to achieve adaptive adjustment of the neuron’s receptive fields. The SK convolution comprises three operations: splitting, merging, and selection. Splitting generates multiple paths with different kernel sizes corresponding to different receptive fields; merging synthesizes information from multiple paths to form global selection weights; selection aggregates feature maps of different kernel sizes based on these weights. Through its adaptive selection mechanism, SKNet employs grouped convolution to reduce the number of parameters and computational cost, thereby enhancing efficiency. Residual network

Shallow networks struggle to extract deep features, while deeper network architectures can capture more complex features but tend to encounter issues such as vanishing gradients and performance degradation as the number of layers increases. To address these issues, He et al.18 introduced the deep residual network (ResNet). ResNets add residual structures on top of traditional convolutional neural networks. Each residual block includes skip connections, allowing gradients to propagate directly through these connections, thus easing the difficulty in the backpropagation process. As shown on the left of Fig. 2, a residual block consists of two branches: one branch fits the residual function through two weighted layers, and the other branch achieves identity mapping of the input signal through skip connections. his paper employs SKNet convolution at the second convolutional position of the ResNet residual block, aiming to enhance its feature extraction capability. Global context network

Squeeze-and-Excitation Networks (SENet) adjusts the dependencies between channels by calibrating weights for different channels, yet it does not fully utilize global context information. Experimental analysis shows that the global context learned by the non-local network is almost uniform across different positions, indicating that it captures a location-independent global context. Based on this observation, Cao et al.19 and colleagues proposed a simplified approach to non-local blocks (GCNET), which effectively models global context information with reduced computational complexity. This method uses a uniform attention map, unrelated to the query, across all query positions, utilizing this map as weights to generate outputs from a unified feature set. The GCNet combines the advantages of non-local networks and SENet, not only reducing computational load but also effectively integrating global context information. Method

In response to the inefficiency of traditional models when facing significant distribution differences, this paper integrates an improved residual network model with MADA algorithm to construct an efficient motor bearing fault diagnosis model. The overall framework of the model is shown in Fig. 4, which includes a data processing module, feature extraction module, state identification module, and domain adaptation module. Feature extraction Module

An improved residual network is used for feature extraction, incorporating two attention mechanisms: SKNet and GCNET. These structures effectively focus on relevant information and suppress noise when processing high-noise signals, thus reducing computational load and enhancing training and diagnostic speed without compromising accuracy. The SKNet extracts comprehensive information from multi-channel sequences by using convolutional filters of different sizes on multi-channel signals and integrating information through attention mechanisms. The GCNet captures the relationships between different channels, computes their importance, and integrates the features to obtain effective information, as shown in Fig. 5.

The MK-MMD method proposed in this paper is utilized to achieve marginal distribution adaptation and enhance the performance of cross-domain diagnostics. Specifically, a mapping function is introduced to embed the high-level features from two domains into the Reproducing Kernel Hilbert Space (RKHS). By minimizing the distance between the feature values in this space, the data distributions of the two domains are aligned, effectively representing the distribution differences between the features across domains.

The kernel function directly maps the sample space to a high-dimensional space, and the choice of kernel significantly affects the effectiveness of MMD. Fault diagnosis model based on MADA

Considering the characteristics exhibited by motor bearings during actual operation, this study constructed a cross-domain fault diagnosis model capable of adapting to signific ant differences in data distributions. The diagnosis process is shown in Fig. 6 below:

Fig. 6Multi-domain adversarial diagnostic flowchart.

Multi-domain adversarial diagnostic flowchart.

Step 1: Data Preprocessing.

Vibration signals from motor bearings are collected and transformed from one-dimensional signal samples under different operating conditions into two-dimensional feature maps. In this study, a sliding window sampling method with a step size of 400 was employed. The sample length was set to 1024 data points, and the dataset for each type of fault consisted of 800 samples. These were divided into training and testing sets in a ratio of 70–30%, respectively. The one-dimensional time series signals of length 1024 were to equal lengths, with each segment of the signal having a length of 32 data points. There were a total of 32 segments, resulting in a two-dimensional feature map with an input size of (32, 32).

Step 2: Model Training.

An optimized residual network is used to extract shared features across different domains. These features are fed into a domain adaptation layer and classifier to compute the related losses. The Adaptive moment estimation optimizer is used to optimize parameters, enhancing the model’s convergence speed; training iterations are set to minimize MK-MMD loss and classification loss, while maximizing domain discriminator loss, thus completing the model training.For training parameters: with the initial learning rate set to 2 × 10−4 and a batch size of 128.

Step 3: Model Testing.

Unlabeled target domain samples are inputted into the trained network, outputting the test results, thereby achieving cross-domain fault diagnosis of motor bearings.

Further details on the Motor Bearing Fault Simulation Experimental Bench will be provided below.

Conclusion

To address the issue of significant differences in data distribution across various devices, this paper integrates knowledge from deep learning and transfer learning to construct a motor bearing fault diagnosis model based on multi-domain adversarial learning. Transfer experiments are conducted under two conditions: varying operational conditions and different devices, yielding promising diagnostic results. The specific conclusions are as follows:

The MADA model uses an improved residual network as the feature extraction module. It employs the SKNet and the GCNet within the improved residual network to filter out noise signals, enhancing the model’s feature extraction capability with less computational cost. Combining multiple adversarial domain networks and the idea of joint distribution adaptation, the output probabilities of the classification layer are used as inputs for the domain discriminator.

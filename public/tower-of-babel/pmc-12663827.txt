Comment on “Characterization of a major house dust mite allergen Der p 22 among Malaysian adult population”☆
Authors: Patpicha Arunsan, Schawanya K. Rattanapitoon, Chutharat Thanchonnang, Nathkapach K. Rattanapitoon
Published: 2025

Dear Editor,

We read with great interest the article by Reginald et al,1 which provides a thorough characterization of Der p 22 as a clinically relevant Dermatophagoides pteronyssinus allergen in a tropical population. Their findings enhance our understanding of the regional allergenome and underscore the importance of population-specific molecular diagnostics. Nevertheless, several issues deserve further consideration, including Der p 22's designation as a “major” allergen, its molecular relationship to Der p 2, and its relevance for component-resolved diagnosis (CRD) and allergen immunotherapy (AIT).

Rethinking “major allergen” status-The authors classified Der p 22 as a major allergen based on a 54% sensitization rate. While notable, allergen “majority” should also consider biological potency, including histamine release and mast cell activation thresholds relative to Der p 1 and Der p 2. Without comparative functional assays or quantitative IgE inhibition analyses, it remains uncertain whether Der p 22 drives effector activation or merely reflects an abundant, immunogenic component. Der p 22 may therefore be more accurately described as a population-dominant sensitizer rather than a universal major allergen—a distinction that influences both diagnostic inclusion and vaccine standardization. This perspective aligns with recent insights into the clinical relevance of regionally dominant allergens in tropical populations, highlighting the need to integrate epidemiologic prevalence with functional allergenicity for diagnostic prioritization.2

Beyond cross-reactivity: an innate–adaptive interface-Although Reginald et al. observed low correlation between Der p 22- and Der p 2-specific IgE, partial cross-inhibition (∼60%) suggests structural convergence within the MD-2-related lipid-recognition (ML) family.3,4 This family includes molecules capable of lipid or lipopolysaccharide binding, raising the possibility that Der p 22 acts as a molecular bridge between innate lipid recognition and adaptive IgE responses. Such a mechanism parallels how Der p 2 mimics TLR4-MD2 complexes.5 Testing this hypothesis through lipid-binding assays or FcεRI-basophil activation models with lipid-preloaded Der p 22 could reveal whether ligand occupancy modulates allergenicity.

Der p 22 as a biomarker of allergic complexity-The association between Der p 22-specific IgE and multimorbidity (asthma, rhinitis, dermatitis) is intriguing. Rather than being a passive marker, Der p 22 sensitization may indicate epitope diversification under chronic antigenic pressure, consistent with models of “progressive autoallergy”.6 Longitudinal profiling of Der p 22-IgE and IgG4 responses could clarify whether these evolve toward tolerance or persistent inflammation, helping define HDM allergy endotypes beyond the classical Der p 1/2 dichotomy.

Diagnostic and therapeutic implications-Including Der p 22 in CRD panels may enhance detection sensitivity in tropical populations, but its clinical utility must demonstrate added diagnostic value over existing core allergens.7 The identification of six key IgE-binding residues (K10, H35, R43, D106, R117, R130) enables rational design of hypoallergenic Der p 22 variants for AIT. Assessing Der p 22-specific T-cell epitopes could further guide peptide immunotherapy aimed at selective tolerance induction. Importantly, the balance between IgE and blocking IgG4 to Der p 22 may influence AIT outcomes, a topic yet unexplored.

Environmental and evolutionary perspectives-Der p 22's prominence in Malaysia may reflect ecological adaptation. High humidity and microbial exposure could favor expression of lipid-binding allergens that enhance mite survival. Environmental transcriptomics comparing tropical and temperate D. pteronyssinus strains may reveal differential expression patterns shaping regional sensitization. Such evolutionary allergenomics could explain why Der p 22 is clinically significant in Southeast Asia but less so in temperate regions.

Reginald et al1 provide valuable groundwork for understanding Der p 22. Future studies should explore its functional role as a lipid-interacting allergen bridging innate recognition and IgE sensitization. Integrating structural, immunologic, and ecological perspectives may ultimately elevate Der p 22 from a taxonomic novelty to a mechanistic key in HDM allergy.

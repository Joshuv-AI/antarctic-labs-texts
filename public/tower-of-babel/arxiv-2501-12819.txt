Let

\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal. Let

. Let

be a MCM
(maximal Cohen-Macaulay)

-module. Let
G_{I}(M)=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}I^{n}M/I^{n+1}M
be the associated graded module of

with respect to
I
(considered as a

-module).

We assume for the time being that

is complete. Then usually

is
not
of finite representation type. In particular there are infinitely many non-isomorphic indecomposable MCM
A
-modules. In this paper we are interested in properties of

which impose conditions on
G_{I}(M)
for all
MCM
A
-modules.
One such property is when

has minimal multiplicity. Then it is easy to check that every MCM
A
-module

has minimal multiplicity with respect to
I
.
In this paper we give another class of ideals for which we can conclude good properties of all
G_{I}(M)
from a condition on
I

Narita showed that if
A
is Cohen-Macaulay of dimension two and
e_{2}^{I}(A)=0
then reduction number of

is one for all
n\gg 0
. In particular reduction number of
I^{n}
with respect to MCM

-module

,

5
]
. So

Narita’s result does not directly generalize to higher dimensions. However in dimension two Narita’s result

is equivalent to the fact that

, the associated graded module of the Ratliff-Rush filtration of

with respect to

[
8
, 6.2]
we proved that if

is Cohen-Macaulay of dimension

then
e_{i}^{I}(M)=0
for
2\leq i\leq r
if and only if
\widetilde{G}_{I}(M)
, the associated graded module of the Ratliff-Rush filtration of
M
with respect to
I
, is Cohen-Macaulay with minimal multiplicity.
Motivated by this result we define an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal in
A
to be a

if

for
2\leq i\leq d
. For such ideals we prove the following result

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(A,\mathfrak{m})
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring of dimension
d\geq 2
. Let
I
be a generalized Narita ideal. Let
M
be a MCM

-module. Then

(1)

e_{i}^{I}(M)=0
for
2\leq i\leq d
.

(2)

\widetilde{G}_{I}(M)
is Cohen-Macaulay with minimal multiplicity.

(3)

G_{I^{n}}(M)
is Cohen-Macaulay for all
n\gg 0
.

(4)

G_{I}(M)
is generalized Cohen-Macaulay.

Next we show

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(A,\mathfrak{m})
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring of dimension
d\geq 2
. Let
I
be an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary generalized Narita ideal. Let

be a MCM

-module. Then there exists

(depending only on

and

) such that

.

An easy corollary to Theorem
1.1

is

.

Let
(A,\mathfrak{m})
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring of dimension

. Let
I
be an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal with

for

. Let

be a MCM

-module. Then

.

Next we relax the condition of generalized Narita ideals by one index, i.e., we consider the case when

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
(A,\mathfrak{m})
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring of dimension

. Let

be an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal with

2.1

Let

\mathfrak{m}

Recall an

(2)

\mathcal{F}_{0}=M

(3)

If

We do NOT assume
\mathcal{F}_{1}\neq M

2.2
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be an
I
-stable filtration on
M
. Then the function
H^{(1)}(\mathcal{F},n)=\ell(M/\mathcal{F}_{n+1})
is of polynomial type of degree

, i.e., there exists a polynomial
P_{\mathcal{F}}^{(1)}(X)\in\mathbb{Q}[X]

. We write

The integers

are called Hilbert coefficients of

with respect to
\mathcal{F}
. The integer

is called the multiplicity of

with respect to
\mathcal{F}
and is always positive if

.

2.3
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be an
I
-stable filtration on
M
. The function

is called the Hilbert function of

with respect to
\mathcal{F}
. Let

be the associated graded module of

with respect to
\mathcal{F}
.
It is well-known that

is a finitely generated

module of dimension

. It follows that

\sum_{n\geq 0}H(\mathcal{F},n)z^{n}=\frac{h_{\mathcal{F}}(z)}{(1-z)^{r}}.

where
h_{\mathcal{F}}(z)\in\mathbb{Z}[z]
and
h_{\mathcal{F}}(1)\neq 0
. It is easily verified that

e_{i}^{\mathcal{F}}(M)=h_{\mathcal{F}}^{(i)}(1)/i!
for
0\leq i\leq r
. It is convenient to set
e_{i}^{\mathcal{F}}(M)=h_{\mathcal{F}}^{(i)}(1)/i!
for all
i
.

2.4
.

An element

is said to

-superficial with respect to

if there exists

(\mathcal{F}_{n+1}\colon x)\cap\mathcal{F}_{c}=\mathcal{F}_{n}
for all

. It is well-known that superficial elements exist if the residue field

of

is infinite. If

it is easily verified that

is
M
-regular. Furthermore

for all

.

2.5
.

Let

be

-superficial with respect to
\mathcal{F}
. Consider the quotient filtration

\overline{\mathcal{F}}=\{\overline{\mathcal{F}_{n}}=(\mathcal{F}_{n}+xM)/xM\}_{n\geq 0}

is also

-regular then

for the adic case which generalizes).

.

If the residue field of

.
If

is an

-module then set

2.7

Let

3.1

The main result of this section is

Proposition 3.2
.

(with hypotheses as above) We have

(1)

\widetilde{\mathcal{F}_{n}}=\mathcal{F}_{n}
for all
n\gg 0
.

(2)

\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}=\{\widetilde{\mathcal{F}_{n}}\}_{n\geq 0}
is an
I
-stable filtration on
N
.

(3)

e_{i}^{\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}}(N)=e_{i}^{\mathcal{F}}(N)
for
i=0,\ldots,t
.

(4)

If
x
is
N
-superficial with respect to
\mathcal{F}
then
(\widetilde{\mathcal{F}_{n+1}}\colon x)=\widetilde{\mathcal{F}_{n}}
for
n\geq 0
. In particular

is
G_{\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}}(N)
-regular.

Proof.

Let
x
be
N
-superficial with respect to
\mathcal{F}
. In particular there exists
n_{0}
such that
(\mathcal{F}_{n+1}\colon x)=\mathcal{F}_{n}
for
n\geq n_{0}
.

(1) Fix
n\geq n_{0}
. Let
m\in\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{n}
. Then

for some

. So

. It follows that
m\in(\mathcal{F}_{n+j}\colon x^{j})=\mathcal{F}_{n}

(2) Let

for all

. We also have
\mathcal{F}_{n+j+1}\subseteq\mathcal{F}_{n+j}
. So

.

Let

and let

. We have

for some

. So

. It follows that
\alpha m\in\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{j+n}
.
So

\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}
is an
I
-filtration.

By (1) and as
\mathcal{F}
is
I
-stable filtration on
N
it follows that
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}
is
I
-stable filtration on
N
.

(3) This follows from (1).

(4) By (2) it follows that
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}_{n}}\subseteq(\widetilde{\mathcal{F}_{n+1}}\colon x)
for all
n\geq 0
. Let
m\in(\widetilde{\mathcal{F}_{n+1}}\colon x)
.
So
I^{j}xm\in\mathcal{F}_{n+1+j}
for all
j\gg 0
. As
x
is
N
-superficial with respect to
\mathcal{F}
it follows that
I^{j}m\in\mathcal{F}_{n+j}
. So
m\in\widetilde{\mathcal{F}_{n}}
.
∎

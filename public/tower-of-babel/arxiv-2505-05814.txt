Topological Origin of Horizon Temperature via the Chern-Gauss-Bonnet Theorem
Jack C. M. Hughes, Fedor V. Kusmartsev

Abstract
This paper establishes a connection between the Hawking temperature of spacetime horizons and global topological invariants, specifically the Euler characteristic of Wick-rotated Euclidean spacetimes. This is demonstrated for both de Sitter and Schwarzschild, where the compactification of the near-horizon geometry allows for a direct application of the Chern-Gauss-Bonnet theorem. For de Sitter, a simple argument connects the Gibbon-Hawking temperature to the global thermal de Sitter temperature of the Bunch-Davies state. This establishes that spacetime thermodynamics are a consequence of the geometrical structure of spacetime itself, therefore suggesting a deep connection between global topology and semi-classical analysis.

II
Background

Any spacetime
\mathcal{M}
satisfying the Einstein field equations in vacuum

R_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}Rg_{\mu\nu}+\Lambda g_{\mu\nu}=0

(1)

is required to have Ricci tensor proportional to the metric

R_{\mu\nu}=\Lambda g_{\mu\nu}.

(2)

By definition, such spacetimes are Einstein manifolds
Besse 1987
. This has important consequences for the geometry of
\mathcal{M}

Atiyah et al. 1978
;
Krasnov 2011
. The Riemann tensor
R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}
admits its orthogonal Ricci decomposition into its Weyl
C_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}
, Ricci
R_{\mu\nu}
and Ricci scalar
R
components
Weinberg 1972
:

\begin{split}R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}=&\;C_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}+\frac{1}{(n-2)}\big(g_{\mu\rho}R_{\nu\sigma}-g_{\mu\sigma}R_{\nu\rho}+g_{\nu\sigma}R_{\mu\rho}-g_{\nu\rho}R_{\mu\sigma}\big)\\
&+\frac{R}{(n-1)(n-2)}\big(g_{\mu\rho}g_{\nu\sigma}-g_{\mu\sigma}g_{\nu\rho}\big)\end{split}

(3)

where
n
is the dimension of spacetime. With (
2
) satisfied, the Riemann tensor is now expressed as (setting
n=4
)

R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}=C_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}+\frac{\Lambda}{3}\big(g_{\mu\rho}g_{\nu\sigma}-g_{\mu\sigma}g_{\nu\rho}\big).

(4)

Therefore, once the metric symmetry is fixed the remaining freedom for the curvature is in the algebraic decomposition of the Weyl tensor (through the Petrov classification)
Petrov 1969
;
Petrov 2000
;
Pirani 1957
.

In four dimensions (and more generally any even dimension spacetime), the curvature of a vacuum solution is additionally constrained by the topology of spacetime. This is a consequence of the Chern-Gauss-Bonnet theorem, which relates the integral of the curvature 2-form
\mathcal{R}
to the topological Euler characteristic
\chi(\mathcal{M})
of spacetime
Chern 1944
;
Chern 1945
;
Mielke 2017
. In tensor indices, this is expressible as

\chi(\mathcal{M})=\frac{1}{32\pi^{2}}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\sqrt{-g}d^{4}x\big(R^{2}-4R_{\mu\nu}R^{\mu\nu}+R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}R^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\big).

(5)

This term coincides with the Gauss-Bonnet Lagrangian density known from Lovelock gravity
Padmanabhan and Kothawala 2013
;
Lovelock 1972
;
Fernandes et al. 2022
. Its variation vanishes identically in four dimensions and hence does not affect the Einstein equations if included in the action principle
Corichi et al. 2016
, while in higher dimensions it need not vanish. For a vacuum spacetime (
2
), the first two terms cancel, leaving

\chi(\mathcal{M})=\frac{1}{32\pi^{2}}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\sqrt{-g}d^{4}x\;R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}R^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}.

(6)

Expressing everything instead in terms of the decomposition (
4
), this is

\chi(\mathcal{M})=\frac{1}{8\pi^{2}}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\sqrt{-g}d^{4}x\bigg(\frac{1}{4}C_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}C^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}+\frac{2}{3}\Lambda^{2}\bigg).

(7)

The difficulty in the application of this theorem is that it requires
\mathcal{M}
to be
compact
(closed and bounded)
Chern 1944
;
Chern 1945
. For most vacuum spacetimes of interest (e.g. Schwarzschild and de Sitter) this is not the case. The theorem will still constrain the curvature spectrum
R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}R^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}
in any locally compact domain, but globally the integral (
6
) will not be restricted to the topological integer
\chi(\mathcal{M})
. Various tricks allow this to be mapped to submanifold contributions and to allow for boundaries
Chern 1945
, which is what has been explored in the previous calculations
Bañados et al. 1994
;
Liberati and Pollifrone 1997
;
Robson et al. 2019
;
Liberati and Pollifrone 1998
. In two dimensions the theorem is applied after a Wick rotation, with Euclidean time compactified and such a submanifold identified through the time-like Killing vector
Zhang et al. 2020
;
Robson et al. 2019
;
Xian and Zhang 2022
.

The idea of this paper is to demonstrate that for de Sitter and Schwarzschild in four dimensions such additional arguments are unnecessary. The theorem may be directly applied in the Wick rotated Euclidean space where compactness is
enforced
through the appropriate near-horizon limit. The Hawking temperature is set by the Euler characteristic
\chi(\mathcal{M})
, and hence is of topological origin. For de Sitter, this gives a clear geometrical origin for the difference between the Gibbons-Hawking temperature
T_{H}=1/2\pi l
and the bulk de Sitter temperature
T_{H}=1/\pi l

Volovik 2024
.

III
de Sitter

Let us take the de Sitter spacetime in static coordinates
Hawking and Ellis 1973
;
Øyvind Grøn and Hervik 2007

ds^{2}=-\bigg(1-\frac{r^{2}}{l^{2}}\bigg)dt^{2}+\bigg(1-\frac{r^{2}}{l^{2}}\bigg)^{-1}dr^{2}+r^{2}d\Omega_{\mathbb{S}^{2}},

(8)

where
l
is expressed in terms of the cosmological constant
\Lambda
via
l^{2}=3/\Lambda
. Note that the cosmological horizon is the hypersurface swept by
r=l
. We wish to evaluate the integral

I_{G}=\frac{1}{32\pi^{2}}\int\sqrt{-g}d^{4}x\;R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}R^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma},

(9)

which is not restricted to
\chi(\mathcal{M})
since de Sitter is not compact. However, let us Wick rotate to Euclidean time
t\to i\tau
; the metric takes the form

ds_{E}^{2}=\bigg(1-\frac{r^{2}}{l^{2}}\bigg)d\tau^{2}+\bigg(1-\frac{r^{2}}{l^{2}}\bigg)^{-1}dr^{2}+r^{2}d\Omega_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}.

(10)

The horizon is no longer a null hypersurface in the sense of (
8
). Instead,
r=l
is a non-regular region of the geometry that must be ‘smoothed out’ to eliminate the conical singularity. To see this, we introduce a new radial expansion relevant for the near-horizon reparameterization:

r=l(1-\rho^{2}).

(11)

The metric function therefore expands as

1-\frac{r^{2}}{l^{2}}\approx 2\rho^{2}

(12)

and to leading order, the near-horizon limit of (
10
) is

ds_{E}^{2}=2l^{2}\bigg(\frac{\rho^{2}}{l^{2}}d\tau^{2}+d\rho^{2}\bigg)+l^{2}d\Omega_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}.

(13)

The metric is now of the standard Rindler form and admits a conical singularity as
\rho\to 0
. This conical singularity can be eliminated provided we identify
\tau\sim\tau+\beta
(i.e. compactify the Euclidean time) with period

\beta=2\pi l.

(14)

This is the standard Gibbons-Hawking argument to construct the temperature
T_{H}=1/\beta

Gibbons and Hawking 1977a
;
Gibbons and Hawking 1977b
.

The
(\tau,\rho)
plane becomes topologically the disc
D^{2}
, which with the spherical line element
d\Omega_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}
indicates the near horizon geometry (
13
) is compact
D^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}
. That is, (
9
) can now be explicitly computed to yield the Euler characteristic of the Wick rotated geometry,

I_{G}=\chi(\mathcal{M}_{E})=\frac{1}{32\pi^{2}}\int_{\mathcal{M}_{E}}\sqrt{-g}d^{4}x\;R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}R^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}.

(15)

The coordinate system in which we choose to compute the integral is irrelevant provided we respect the compact domain
(\tau,\rho)
enforced in the near-horizon limit. That is, the Kretschmann scalar
R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}R^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}
is a curvature invariant
Hervik and Coley 2010
. This allows us to write

\chi(\mathcal{M}_{E})=\frac{1}{32\pi^{2}}\int^{\beta}_{0}d\tau\int^{l}_{0}dr\;\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\sqrt{g}d^{2}x\;R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}R^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}.

(16)

Identifying
\beta=1/T_{H}
, we choose to represent this as a formula for the Hawking temperature:

T_{H}=\frac{1}{32\pi^{2}\chi(\mathcal{M}_{E})}\int^{l}_{0}dr\;\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\sqrt{g}d^{2}x\;R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}R^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}.

(17)

For the de Sitter geometry,

R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}R^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}=\frac{24}{l^{4}},

(18)

and the above evaluates to

T_{H}=\frac{1}{\chi(\mathcal{M}_{E})\pi l}.

(19)

Finally the Euler characteristic of a topological product is the product of Euler characteristics of the individual spaces

\chi(\mathcal{M}_{E})=\chi(D^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})=\chi(D^{2})\cdot\chi(\mathbb{S}^{2})=1\cdot 2=2,

(20)

which reproduces the Gibbons-Hawking temperature of de Sitter via the topological Chern-Gauss-Bonnet theorem.

There is a clear geometrical argument within this picture of the factor of
2
that separates the Gibbons-Hawking temperature
T_{H}=1/2\pi l
and the thermal de Sitter temperature
T_{H}=1/\pi l
(the latter of these is motivated through the periodicity of Wightman functions for the Bunch-Davies vacuum
Birrell and Davies 1982
;
Diakonov 2025
;
Bunch and Davies 1978
;
Akhmedov et al. 2020
). The computation (
16
) is for the near horizon limit of the Euclidean de Sitter spacetime. This is the static patch representation of Wick rotating global de Sitter spacetime to the Euclidean 4-sphere. Indeed, globally Euclidean de Sitter has topology
\mathbb{S}^{4}
, but the coordinates
(\tau,\rho)
cover only
half
this space in the static patch with topology
D^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}
. Concretely, the smooth point
\rho=0
represents the equatorial line of
\mathbb{S}^{4}
viewed as the continuous matching of two such hemispheres. As a consequence if we want to compute the Euler characteristic of the
total
Euclidean de Sitter topology, the integral contribution (
16
) must be doubled. Topologically this is clear
Hatcher 2001
, and at the physical level de Sitter being maximally symmetric ensures both hemispheres contribute equally to
\chi({\mathbb{S}^{4}})
via the homogeneity of curvature. Computationally then, we have

\chi({\mathbb{S}^{4}})=\frac{1}{16\pi^{2}T_{H}}\int^{l}_{0}dr\;\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\sqrt{g}d^{2}x\;R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}R^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}=\frac{2}{\pi lT_{H}}.

(21)

But noting that
\chi({\mathbb{S}^{4}})=2
, we recover the thermal de Sitter temperature

T_{H}=\frac{1}{\pi l}

(22)

through the topological Chern-Gauss-Bonnet theorem.

IV
Schwarzschild

The case for Schwarzschild proceeds in much the same way. The main difference is that the compactification of
\tau
‘bounds’ the radial coordinate from the horizon
outwards
, meaning that there is infinite range radially. The Wick rotated line element is

ds_{E}^{2}=\bigg(1-\frac{2M}{r}\bigg)d\tau^{2}+\bigg(1-\frac{2M}{r}\bigg)^{-1}dr^{2}+r^{2}d\Omega_{\mathbb{S}^{2}},

(23)

which again possesses a conical defect that must be smoothed in the near-horizon limit. We can expand linearly

r=2M+\rho

(24)

so that the metric function approximates to

1-\frac{2M}{r}\approx\frac{\rho}{2M}.

(25)

The Rindler form for (
23
) is identified via setting
\rho=R^{2}/{8M}
, such that

ds_{E}^{2}\approx\frac{R^{2}}{16M^{2}}d\tau^{2}+dR^{2}+4M^{2}d\Omega_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}.

(26)

In accordance with the Gibbon-Hawking method
Gibbons and Hawking 1977a
;
Gibbons and Hawking 1977b
, the temperature is found by demanding periodicity in
\tau
:

\tau\sim\tau+\beta,\quad\beta=8\pi M,

(27)

which ensures the point
r=2M
is regular in the Euclidean geometry.

Again, this is the disc topology in
(\tau,r)
allowing us to implement the Gauss-Bonnet theorem in the near-horizon limit. Now, Euclidean Schwarzschild is clearly non-compact since
r\in[2M,\infty)
. However this point is irrelevant in the computation of the Euler characteristic since the Kretschmann scalar
R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}R^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\sim 1/r^{6}
falls of sufficiently fast for the integral to be
asymptotically compact
. In other words, the coordinate system respects the near-horizon topology: there is no contribution from the asymptotic element to
\chi(\mathcal{M}_{E})
. Explicitly,

\chi(\mathcal{M}_{E})=\frac{1}{32\pi^{2}}\int^{\beta}_{0}d\tau\int^{\infty}_{2M}dr\;\int_{\Sigma}\sqrt{g}d^{2}x\;R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}R^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}.

(28)

Recalling

R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}R^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}=\frac{48M^{2}}{r^{6}},

(29)

we observe

T_{H}=\frac{1}{\chi(\mathcal{M}_{E})4\pi M}.

(30)

Following again the logic of (
20
), this verifies the Hawking temperature and Euler characteristic are dual to eachother.

V
Conclusion

This work demonstrates that there exists a unified topological principle underlying the thermodynamics of Killing horizons in four-dimensional vacuum spacetimes. The Hawking temperature is inversely proportional to the Euler characteristic of the Wick rotated Euclidean geometry in its smooth compact near horizon limit. This is made possible through the application of the Chern-Gauss-Bonnet theorem
Chern 1944
;
Chern 1945
;
Mielke 2017
without the need for submanifold or boundary arguments. The de Sitter case is particularly interesting, since the calculation of the Gibbons-Hawking temperature
T_{H}=1/2\pi l
can be immediately extended to the (global) thermal de Sitter temperature
T_{H}=1/\pi l
through a simple topological argument. Indeed, the near-horizon topology
D^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}
in (
16
) covers half of the 4-sphere (global Euclidean de Sitter), and so a calculation respecting
\chi(\mathbb{S}^{4})
requires
double
the Hawking temperature for consistency. This is likely the origin of observed bulk-boundary correspondence in the de Sitter entropy
Volovik 2024
;
Diakonov 2025
;
Volovik 2025
. Extending these results to Kerr is a non-trivial problem: the Wick rotation must be supplemented by an appropriate redefinition of the azimuthal coordinate to ensure off-diagonal elements of the metric remain real. However, the methodology presented here is likely an underlying topological argument supporting the results of Altas & Tekin
Altas and Tekin 2023
.

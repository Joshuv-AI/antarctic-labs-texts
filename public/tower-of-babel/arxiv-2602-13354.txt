1.
Introduction

Throughout this paper,
p
is a prime and
e
is a nonnegative integer.

Let
G
be a finite group. The set of all
p
-subgroups of
G
whose orders are greater than
p^{e}
is denoted by
\mathcal{S}_{p,e}(G)
.

In
[
2
]
, the following poset is defined:

\Gamma_{p,e}(G)=\{(H,\varphi)|H\in\mathcal{S}_{p,e}(G),\varphi\in{\rm Irr}(H)\},

with the partial order
``\leq"
defined as

(K,\psi)\leq(H,\varphi)\,\,\Longleftrightarrow\,\,K\leq H\hskip 9.24994pt\text{and}\hskip 9.24994pt[\varphi_{K},\psi]\neq 0.

In addition, two pairs
(K,\psi)
and
(H,\varphi)
in
\Gamma_{p,e}(G)
are connected or lie in the same connected component if they can be joined by a finite sequence in which every two consecutive pair is compatible. The set of connected components of
\Gamma_{p,e}(G)
is denoted by
\pi_{0}(\Gamma_{p,e}(G))
.

For a finite
p
-group
G
with
p^{e+1}\mid|G|
, the intersection of all subgroups of order
p^{e+1}
of
G
is denoted by
I
.

If
p^{2}\mid|G|
, it is proved in Theorem C of
[
2
]
that
\Gamma_{p,1}
is disconnected iff
I\neq 1
. In Question 2 of
[
2
]
, Meng and Yang raise the following question:

Question.
Let
G
be a finite
p
-group, where
|G|
is divisible by
p^{e+1}
. Assume that
e\geq 2
. Is it true that
\Gamma_{p,e}(G)
is disconnected iff
I\neq 1
?

One implication has been prove at the end of
[
2
]
. In this short note, an affirmative answer to the previous question is provided; see Theorem
2.3
below.

2.
Proofs

The following lemma is very useful.

Lemma 2.1
.

(
[
2
, Lemma 4]
) Let
G
be a finite
p
-group and
H
a subgroup of
G
with
|H|\geq p^{e+1}
. Then

\pi_{0}(\Gamma_{p,e}(G))=\{[(H,\varphi)]|\varphi\in{\rm Irr}(H)\},

where
[(H,\varphi)]
denotes the connected component containing
(H,\varphi)
in
\Gamma_{p,e}(G)
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
H,K\in\mathcal{S}_{p,e}(G)
,
\alpha\in{\rm Irr}(H)
, and
\beta\in{\rm Irr}(K)
such that
[\alpha_{H\cap K},\beta_{H\cap K}]\neq 0
. Then
(H,\alpha)
is connected with
(K,\beta)
in
\Gamma_{p,e}(G)
.

Proof
. By the assumption, there exists
\gamma\in{\rm Irr}(H\cap K)
such that both
\alpha
and
\beta
lie over
\gamma
. Thus,

[(\alpha^{G})_{K},\beta]=\sum\limits_{x\in[H\backslash G/K]}[(^{x}\alpha)_{{}^{x}H\cap K}^{K},\beta]\geqslant[\alpha_{H\cap K}^{K},\beta]=[\alpha_{H\cap K},\beta_{H\cap K}]\geqslant[\gamma,\gamma]\neq 0,

where the first equality holds by Mackey Formula (see
[
1
, Problem 5.6]
) and the second equality holds by Frobenius Reciprocity (see
[
1
, Lemma 5.2]
.

Thus, we may choose an irreducible constituent
\omega
of
\alpha^{G}
such that
[\omega_{K},\beta]\neq 0
. Then one can see that

(H,\alpha)\leqslant(G,\omega)\geqslant(K,\beta),

which proves the assertion.

\square

The following theorem consists of the key ingredient of the main results.

Theorem 2.2
.

Suppose that
L_{0},L_{1},L_{2}\ldots L_{n},L_{n+1}\in\mathcal{S}_{p,e}(G)
. For each
j
, set
K_{j}=\bigcap\limits_{i=0}^{j}L_{i}
. Let
\alpha_{0}\in{\rm Irr}(L_{0})
,
\alpha_{n+1}\in{\rm Irr}(L_{n+1})
satisfying
[(\alpha_{0})_{K_{n+1}},(\alpha_{n+1})_{K_{n+1}}]\neq 0
. Then
(L_{0},\alpha_{0})
is connected with
(L_{n+1},\alpha_{n+1})
in
\Gamma_{p,e}(G)
.

Proof
. We proceed by induction on
n
. If
n=1
, the assertion follows by Theorem
2.1
.

Now assume that
n>1
. By the assumption, there exists
\gamma\in{\rm Irr}(K_{n+1})
such that both
\alpha_{0}
and
\alpha_{n+1}
lie over
\gamma
.

Since

0\neq[(\alpha_{n+1})_{K_{n+1}},\gamma]=[((\alpha_{n+1})_{L_{n}\cap L_{n+1}})_{K_{n+1}},\gamma],

there exists
\eta\in{\rm Irr}(L_{n}\cap L_{n+1})
such that
\eta
lies over
\gamma
and lies under
\alpha_{n+1}
.

Then we have

\begin{split}&[(\alpha_{0})_{K_{n}},(\eta^{L_{n}})_{K_{n}}]=\sum\limits_{x}[(\alpha_{0})_{K_{n}},(^{x}\eta)_{{}^{x}(L_{n}\cap L_{n+1})\cap K_{n}}^{K_{n}}]\geq[(\alpha_{0})_{K_{n}},\eta_{K_{n+1}}^{K_{n}}]\\
=&[(\alpha_{0})_{K_{n+1}},\eta_{K_{n+1}}]\geq[\gamma,\gamma]=1,\end{split}

where
x
runs over a set of
(L_{n}\cap L_{n+1})\backslash L_{n}/K_{n}
double coset representatives.

Thus there exists
\alpha_{n}\in{\rm Irr}(\eta^{L_{n}})
such that
[(\alpha_{0})_{K_{n}},(\alpha_{n})_{K_{n}}]\neq 0
. By the inductive hypothesis, we have
(L_{0},\alpha_{0})
is connected with
(L_{n},\alpha_{n})
in
\Gamma_{p,e}(G)
.

Finally, note that

[(\alpha_{n})_{L_{n}\cap L_{n+1}},(\alpha_{n+1})_{L_{n}\cap L_{n+1}})]\geqslant[\eta,\eta]=1,

which yields that
(L_{n},\alpha_{n})
is connected with
(L_{n+1},\alpha_{n+1})
by Theorem
2.1
.

We conclude that
(L_{0},\alpha_{0})
is connected with
(L_{n+1},\alpha_{n+1})
.

\square

The following result will provide an affirmative answer to Question 2 in
[
2
]
.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
G
be a finite
p
-group with
p^{e+1}\mid|G|
, and let
I
be the intersection of all subgroups of order
p^{e+1}
in
G
.
Then

|I\cap Z(G)|\leqslant|\pi_{0}(\Gamma_{p,e}(G))|\leqslant|{\rm Irr}(I)|.

In particular, we have
\Gamma_{p,e}(G)
is connected iff
I=1
.

Proof
. Choose subgroups
L_{0},L_{1},\ldots,L_{n}
such that

\bigcap\limits_{i=0}^{n}L_{i}=I,

where each
L_{i}
has order
p^{e+1}
,
0\leq i\leq n
.

For any
(L_{0},\alpha_{0}),(L_{0},\alpha_{0}^{\prime})\in\Gamma_{p,e}(G)
, by taking
(L_{n+1},\alpha_{n+1})=(L_{0},\alpha_{0}^{\prime})
and noting that in the notation of Theorem
2.2

K_{n+1}=I
, one can see that, by Theorem
2.2
,
(L_{0},\alpha_{0})
is connected with
(L_{0},\alpha_{0}^{\prime})
if
(\alpha_{0})_{I}
and

(\alpha_{0}^{\prime})_{I}
have a common irreducible constituent.

As a consequence of Lemma
2.1
, we have

|\pi_{0}(\Gamma_{p,e}(G))|\leqslant|{\rm Irr}(I)|,

which proves the righhand side inequality in the theorem.

If
I\cap Z(G)=1
, the lefthand side inequality in the theorem holds trivially. So we may assume that
|I\cap Z(G)|=p^{f+1}
with
f
a nonnegative integer.

Define

\displaystyle\varphi:\Gamma_{p,e}(G)

\displaystyle\rightarrow\Gamma_{p,f}(I\cap Z(G))

\displaystyle(H,\alpha)

\displaystyle\mapsto(I\cap Z(G),\beta),

where
\beta
is the unique constituent of
\alpha_{I\cap Z(G)}
.

Note that if
(H_{1},\alpha_{1})\leqslant(H_{2},\alpha_{2})
, we have
\varphi((H_{1},\alpha_{1}))=\varphi((H_{2},\alpha_{2}))
, as
\alpha_{1}
is an irreducible constituent of
\alpha_{2}
and
(\alpha_{2})_{I\cap Z(G)}
has a unique irreducible constituent. Hence,
\varphi
is a poset map.

It is easy to check
\varphi
is a surjection. By Lemma 5 of
[
2
]
, we have

|\pi_{0}(\Gamma_{p,f}(I\cap Z(G))|\leqslant|\pi_{0}(\Gamma_{p,e}(G))|.

Since
I\cap Z(G)
is abelian and
|I\cap Z(G)|=p^{f+1}
, one can easily see that

|\pi_{0}(\Gamma_{p,f}(I\cap Z(G))|=|I\cap Z(G)|,

and hence the lefthand side inequality in the theorem is proved.

In particular, if
\Gamma_{p,e}(G)
is connected, then
|\pi_{0}(\Gamma_{p,e}(G))|=1
, which yields that
I\cap Z(G)=1
and therefore
I=1
as
I\vartriangleleft G
. Conversely, if
I=1
then
|\pi_{0}(\Gamma_{p,e}(G))|=1
, i.e.,
\Gamma_{p,e}(G)
is connected.

\square

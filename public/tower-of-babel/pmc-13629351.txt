Mixed Localized and Delocalized Bonding in a Linear‑B3‑Centered B3Mg6 – Cluster
Authors: Shu-Juan Gao, Chong Hu, Feng Li, Er-Jie Shen, Wen-Kai Shi, Jia-Xin Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The structural and bonding properties of the boron–magnesium alloy cluster B3Mg6 – were investigated using quantum chemical calculations. The global minimum is identified as a highly symmetric D 3h structure featuring an unusual linear B3 core embedded in a Mg-rich framework. Bonding analyses show that the cluster contains three peripheral Mg–Mg two-center two-electron (2c–2e) σ bonds, two B–B 2c–2e σ bonds, and two orthogonal three-center two-electron (3c–2e) π bonds associated with the linear boron chain. In addition, the framework exhibits further four-center two-electron (4c–2e) and eight-center two-electron (8c–2e) σ delocalization, indicating that both local bonding and global skeletal delocalization contribute to its stability. Natural population analysis reveals substantial charge transfer from Mg atoms to the boron skeleton, suggesting a mixed ionic–covalent bonding picture. The calculated highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO)–lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) gap of 2.30 eV supports the good electronic stability of the closed-shell anion. Electron localization function (ELF), nucleus-independent chemical shift (NICS), and iso-chemical shielding surface (ICSS) analyses further support appreciable σ delocalization associated with the BMg3-containing framework. The present results provide a clear bonding picture for B3Mg6 – and offer additional insight into the stabilization of atomically thin boron-chain motifs in alloy cluster environments.

Introduction

Boron clusters have long attracted attention due to their electron-deficient nature, rich geometric diversity, and unique bonding patterns. Unlike classical organic frameworks, boron-based systems cannot typically be described solely by conventional localized two-electron bonds centered on a single node. Instead, they often rely on multicentered bonding, delocalized electron deficiency, and sophisticated geometric modulations to accommodate a finite number of valence electrons. Multicenter bonding is not unique to boron clusters but represents a general bonding strategy in electron-deficient and hypercoordinate systems, including boranes, hypervalent molecules, and metal clusters. In such systems, one electron pair may be shared by three or more atomic centers, allowing the framework to achieve electronic stabilization without assigning an independent two-center bond to every interatomic contact. These characteristics make boron clusters particularly valuable for exploring the interface between localized bonding patterns and collective electronic stabilization. Over the past few decades, theoretical and experimental studies have demonstrated that boron can form a variety of structural types, including planar and quasi-planar frameworks, cage-like species, tubular clusters, and metal-containing sandwich-like assemblies. − Aromaticity in boron clusters differs in important respects from the classical picture commonly applied to carbon frameworks. In many carbon clusters and conjugated organic molecules, localized C–C σ bonds provide the structural skeleton, while p orbitals form an independently delocalized π system that may satisfy a Hückel-type electron-counting rule. Boron clusters possess fewer valence electrons and frequently cannot construct a complete framework of localized B–B 2c–2e bonds. Their electronic stabilization therefore often involves delocalization in both the π and σ manifolds. Depending on the number, symmetry, and occupation of these delocalized orbitals, boron clusters may exhibit π aromaticity, σ aromaticity, multiple π/σ aromaticity, antiaromaticity, or conflicting aromatic responses. ,,,

Metal doping offers an effective strategy for regulating the structure and electronic properties of boron clusters. By contributing electrons to the boron framework, metal components can mitigate the intrinsic electron deficiency of boron and stabilize structural types that would otherwise be difficult to sustain. In many cases, metal atoms do not merely act as passive electrostatic donors; rather, they may participate directly in the overall bonding scheme through metal–metal bonding, metal–boron covalent interactions, or delocalization across the polycentric framework. Consequently, metal-rich boron alloy clusters typically exhibit coupled localized and delocalized interactions, serving as an ideal platform for elucidating how charge transfer and orbital overlap synergistically stabilize unusual geometric structures. Low-electronegativity metals can compensate for the electron deficiency of boron through charge transfer, but their structural roles depend strongly on the metal and cluster composition. Magnesium-containing clusters such as Mg2B8 and MgAlB8 + illustrate how metal donation and directional bonding can coexist with structural fluxionality. , Beryllium can support highly covalent boron frameworks, including the bow-shaped boron chain in Be6B7 –, the cage-like Be3B11 – cluster, and tubular or quasi-planar BeB12 species. Metal doping also stabilizes multilayer and sandwich architectures, as demonstrated by Be6B10 2–, B7Li4 –, Na6B7 – and Na8B7 +, and Na5B7. In contrast, Li2B12, Li3B12 and Be4B12 + demonstrate tubular and cage-like structural responses to metal incorporation. , In these clusters, the significant electrostatic interactions arising from the substantial charge transfer from the alkali metal to the boron framework are the primary driving force behind the formation of highly stable tubular and cage-like structures.

Among the possible boron structural units, the linear B3 motif is particularly informative because isolated boron generally favors triangular or multicenter polyhedral arrangements. Linear B3 units remain uncommon, but recent studies have demonstrated that they can be stabilized in suitable metal-rich environments. A systematic investigation of B m Sc n–m +/0 clusters revealed a size-dependent triangular-to-linear rearrangement of the B3 dopant, with the linear B3@Sc8 motif proposed as a potential building block for larger clusters. More directly, a D 3h B3Zn6 – alloy cluster was recently shown to contain a linear B3 chain supported by three Zn–Zn linkages and two BZn3 caps. These precedents establish that linear B3 chains are chemically accessible while raising the question of how different metal environments modify their stabilization mechanisms. These findings raise a more specific question: whether linear B3 motifs supported by similar metal-framework geometries necessarily share the same bonding mechanism, or whether the nature of the metal can independently regulate charge transfer, metal–metal bonding, and multicenter delocalization. A particularly relevant precedent is the recently reported D 3h B3Zn6 – cluster, which also contains a linear B3 chain, three interlayer metal–metal linkages, and two BM3-type caps. In that system, the B3 core is stabilized by two B–B σ bonds and two orthogonal 3c–2e π bonds, whereas the BZn3 caps were interpreted in terms of triplet-like σ2σ1σ1* configurations and 4σ aromaticity. This close structural analogy raises a more specific question: whether replacement of Zn by a lighter, more electropositive, d-orbital-free Mg center preserves the same stabilization mechanism or produces a distinct balance of charge transfer, metal–metal bonding, and multicenter σ delocalization. Magnesium provides a chemically informative counterpart to zinc for examining the metal-dependent stabilization of a linear B3 chain. The previously reported B3Zn6 – cluster possesses the same B3M6 – stoichiometry and a closely related D 3h framework. Although Zn contains a filled 3d10 shell, these electrons are essentially nonbonding, leaving two outer-shell electrons per Zn atom available for skeletal bonding. Mg likewise contributes two valence electrons through its 3s2 configuration. Consequently, B3Mg6 – and B3Zn6 – possess the same effective skeletal electron count of 22 electrons, while differing substantially in metal orbital character, electronegativity, and charge-transfer ability. B3Mg6 – therefore provides a controlled system for determining whether the same linear B3 geometry can be supported by a bonding mechanism distinct from that of the Zn analogue. The specific B3Mg6 – composition is also compatible with both the electron-counting and geometric requirements of the D 3h framework. Six Mg atoms form two symmetry-equivalent BMg3 caps connected by three interlayer Mg–Mg linkages, allowing the Mg framework to support both terminal B atoms while retaining 3-fold symmetry around the linear B3 axis. The resulting 22 valence electrons can be completely accommodated by localized B–B and Mg–Mg bonds together with multicenter σ and π bonding elements. Thus, the B3Mg6 – composition was selected as an electronically matched and structurally compatible Mg analogue of B3Zn6 – rather than solely on the basis of the general electropositivity of Mg. The paper reports the results of a quantum chemical study of B3Mg6 –. A global structural search identified a global minimum (GM) structure with D 3h symmetry characterized by a linear B3 core. Bonding analysis reveals a hierarchical structure of localized and delocalized interactions, including peripheral Mg–Mg σ bonds, B–B σ bonds, orthogonal 3c–2e π bonds along the B3 chain, and additional delocalized σ bonding in the framework. Magnetic response and electron localization function (ELF) analyses further support the presence of delocalized σ bonding in the BMg3 cap layer. Overall, B3Mg6 – should be described as a hybrid ion–covalent cluster with distinct localized and delocalized bonding components. The results of this study provide a clear picture of the chemical bonding in this unconventional alloy cluster centered on a linear B3 core and offer new insights into the stabilization of atomically thin boron chains in metal-rich environments.

Methods

To avoid any structural bias in locating the global minimum (GM) of the B3Mg6 – cluster, an extensive stochastic search was performed using the Coalescence Kick (CK) algorithm in conjunction with density functional theory (DFT). At the PBE0/6-31+G­(d) level, 3000 singlet and 2000 triplet initial configurations were generated, which after preliminary geometry optimization yielded approximately 5000 stationary points. − These low-lying candidate structures were subjected to further geometry optimizations and vibrational frequency calculations. Two well-established hybrid functionals, PBE0 and B3LYP, were employed together with the 6-311+G­(d) basis set, which offers triple-ζ valence quality augmented with polarization functions and is particularly suitable for describing the electronic structure of both main-group and transition-metal atoms. Vibrational frequency analyses were carried out for all optimized structures at the same level of theory to confirm that they correspond to genuine local minima (no imaginary frequencies) and to derive zero-point vibrational energy (ZPE) corrections. To obtain highly accurate relative energies and to benchmark the DFT results, single-point energy calculations were performed using the coupled-cluster method including single, double, and perturbative triple excitations, CCSD­(T)/6-311+G­(d), based on the PBE0/6-311+G­(d) optimized geometries. −

Natural bond orbital (NBO) analysis was conducted with the NBO 6.0 program to derive Wiberg bond indices (WBIs) and natural atomic charges, providing a chemically intuitive picture of bonding interactions and charge distribution. The bonding characteristics were further elucidated via canonical molecular orbital (CMO) analysis and the adaptive natural density partitioning (AdNDP) method. To quantitatively assess the aromatic character of the cluster, isochemical shielding surfaces (ICSS) and the electron localization function (ELF) were computedtwo complementary real-space descriptors widely applied to characterize π/σ aromaticity in diverse molecular systems. , All of the above bonding and aromaticity analyses were performed consistently at the PBE0/6-311+G­(d) level of theory. All electronic structure calculations were carried out using the Gaussian 09 package; ICSS maps, orbital composition analyses, and AdNDP visualizations were generated with the Multiwfn program. , Molecular graphics were prepared using GaussView, CYLview, and VMD. −

Results

Global-Minimum Structure

The optimized geometries and relative energies of the low-lying B3Mg6 – cluster isomers obtained at the PBE0/6-311+G­(d) level are displayed in Figure and the Supporting Information (SI, Figure S1). The energy ordering predicted by PBE0 was cross-validated against B3LYP optimizations and single-point CCSD­(T) calculations. Notably, all three theoretical approaches yield a consistent conclusion, unequivocally identifying the D 3h (1A1 ′) structure (shown in Figure ) as the true global minimum of the B3Mg6 – system. The optimized geometry contains a linear B3 chain located along the principal molecular axis, two terminal BMg3 capping units, and three peripheral Mg–Mg linkages connecting the upper and lower metal layers, with its optimized Cartesian coordinates detailed in Table S1. The occurrence of two BMg3 caps and three interlayer Mg–Mg linkages illustrates the geometric compatibility of the M6 composition with the 3-fold symmetry of the linear-B3-centered framework. The cluster may therefore be viewed as a hybrid framework composed of a linear boron core and a metal-rich outer skeleton. The most notable structural feature is the presence of the nearly perfectly linear B3 motif. Such a motif is uncommon in boron chemistry because boron clusters more commonly favor triangular B3 units or related electron-deficient polyhedral motifs. In the present case, however, the linear boron chain is effectively embedded in the Mg-rich environment and becomes the structural backbone of the cluster. The surrounding metal framework plays two structural roles at the same time: it provides geometric confinement around the boron chain and contributes electronic stabilization through charge donation and skeletal bonding interactions.

Optimized global-minimum (GM) structures of the D 3h (1A1 ′) B3Mg6 – structure at the PBE0/6-311+G­(d) level.

Geometrically, B3Mg6 – is not adequately described as a simple prism or as a conventional sandwich complex. Although two BMg3 caps are located above and below the central boron atom, they are not independent ligands in the usual organometallic sense. Instead, they are directly connected through three peripheral Mg–Mg interactions, producing an integrated three-dimensional framework. Thus, the cluster is better described as a linear-B3-centered alloy species with both localized structural units and extended skeletal connectivity. This structural assignment is chemically significant because it suggests that the unusual B3 core is not stabilized by a single dominant factor. Rather, the geometry already implies a cooperative stabilization mechanism involving internal boron-chain bonding, peripheral metal–metal interactions, and broader framework-level electronic delocalization. The remainder of the discussion focuses on clarifying how these contributions are distributed and how they collectively sustain the D 3h structure.

Bond Distances, Wiberg Bond Indices, and Natural Atomic Charges

The calculated bond distances, Wiberg bond indices (WBIs), and natural atomic charges for the GM B3Mg6 – cluster are compiled in Figure . The B–B bond lengths in the linear B3 core are 1.59 Å, and the corresponding WBI values are 1.96, indicating strong covalent interactions with pronounced multiple-bond character. These values are fully consistent with the picture of a strongly bonded linear boron chain and suggest that the two adjacent B–B segments are electronically similar. In contrast, the Mg–Mg contacts show two clearly different bonding situations. Within each BMg3 cap, the Mg–Mg distances are 3.87 Å and the corresponding WBI values are 0.00, showing that there is essentially no direct Mg–Mg covalent bonding inside a given cap. This is an important point because it indicates that the BMg3 cap is not a simple triangular Mg3 fragment held together by localized metal–metal bonds. Instead, its stabilization must arise primarily from interaction with the boron atom and from multicenter delocalization over the cap region. However, the three peripheral Mg–Mg contacts linking the upper and lower layers are considerably more bonding in character. These lateral Mg–Mg distances are 2.87 Å, with corresponding WBI values of 0.59, indicating the presence of genuine peripheral Mg–Mg bonding interactions. Thus, the Mg6 framework is not composed of equivalent metal sites but instead contains three well-defined metal–metal bonding connections. The distinction between nonbonding intralayer Mg–Mg contacts and bonding interlayer Mg–Mg contacts gives the Mg-rich outer skeleton a clear directional bonding pattern.

Calculated bond distances (in black color), Wiberg bond indices (WBIs, in red color), and natural atomic charges (in |e|, in blue color) for GM structures of B3Mg6 – structures at the PBE0/6-311+G­(d) level.

The calculated natural charges indicate substantial electron transfer from Mg to the boron skeleton. The central boron atom carries a charge of −1.13 |e|, the terminal boron atoms each carry −2.04 |e|, and each Mg atom bears an approximate positive charge of +0.70 |e|. Such a charge distribution indicates that the Mg framework acts as an electron donor that compensates for the electron deficiency of the boron core. It is also noteworthy that the terminal B atoms are considerably more negative than the central B atom, suggesting an uneven charge distribution within the linear B3 chain itself. At the same time, the B–Mg distances of 2.24 Å and the corresponding WBI values of 0.24 indicate that the interaction between B and Mg is not purely ionic. Therefore, B3Mg6 – cluster is best viewed as a cluster with a cooperative ionic–covalent bonding pattern, in which charge transfer and covalent interaction both play important roles. The data in this section already suggest that the structure cannot be explained by a purely ionic picture or by a simple collection of independent two-center bonds. A more detailed orbital-based analysis is therefore necessary.

Discussion

Chemical Bonding

To clarify the nature of bonding in B3Mg6 –, the occupied canonical molecular orbital (CMO) was analyzed together with the adaptive natural density partitioning (AdNDP) bonding pattern. These analyses reveal a clear hierarchy of localized and delocalized bonding elements and provide a chemically transparent description of how the linear B3 core and Mg-rich framework cooperate. The GM B3Mg6 – cluster possesses a total of 22 valence electrons. Figure illustrates the 11 occupied CMOs, which can be systematically classified into four distinct subsets based on the composition of their constituent atomic orbitals (AOs). The composition of the track is shown in Table S2.

Pictures of occupied canonical molecular orbitals (CMOs) of GM B3Mg6 – cluster. (a) Three σ CMOs for peripheral two-center two-electron (2c–2e) Mg–Mg Lewis σ bonds. (b) Two σ CMOs for 2c–2e B–B σ bonds. (c) Two orthogonal 3c–2e B–B–B π bonds. (d) Four delocalized σ CMOs.

The subset (a) of orbitals corresponds to the three peripheral Mg–Mg σ bonds (Figure a). These orbitals are distributed mainly along the three lateral Mg–Mg edges that connect the upper and lower metal layers. Their bonding nature is confirmed by the AdNDP recovery of three 2c–2e σ bonds with an occupation number (ON) of 1.95 |e| (Figure a). The result shows that the outer Mg6 framework contains three genuine Lewis-type metal–metal bonding elements. Therefore, the peripheral Mg–Mg connections are not incidental geometric contacts but integral parts of the bonding architecture.

AdNDP bonding scheme for GM B3Mg6 – cluster. Occupation numbers (ONs) are shown.

The subset (b) is associated with the linear B3 core (Figure b). Two AdNDP elements are recovered as 2c–2e B–B σ bonds with an occupation number of 1.94 |e| (Figure b), accounting for the strong axial bonding along the boron chain. In addition, two further bonding elements appear as orthogonal 3c–2e π bonds delocalized over the three boron atoms, with an occupation number of 1.90 |e| (Figure c). These two π bonds are perpendicular to each other and, together with the two σ bonds, provide a chemically intuitive bonding description for the linear B3 unit. Therefore, the boron chain in B3Mg6 – is best described as a strongly bonded linear motif sustained by both localized σ interactions and delocalized π bonding. The description is important because it shows that the B3 core is not simply a weakly connected chain held in place by the surrounding metal atoms. Instead, the chain possesses its own robust intrinsic bonding pattern. The combination of two B–B σ bonds and two orthogonal 3c–2e π bonds imparts pronounced internal stability to the linear boron skeleton, while the outer metal framework supplies additional support through electron donation and skeletal delocalization.

Beyond these localized Mg–Mg and B–B bonding elements, the AdNDP analysis also reveals additional multicenter skeletal delocalization. Specifically, subset (d) shows two 4c–2e σ bonds are found over the BMg3 fragments, with an occupation number of 1.85 |e|, indicating local multicenter stabilization within the caps (Figures d and d). More importantly, two 8c–2e σ bonds are recovered over the broader cluster framework, with an occupation number of 2.00 |e| (Figure e), demonstrating that the stability of B3Mg6 – is further reinforced by extended delocalization over the full skeleton.

The coexistence of these bonding elements leads to a particularly informative picture. The cluster contains localized Lewis-type bonds where they are chemically natural, such as along the peripheral Mg–Mg edges and the B–B segments of the linear chain. At the same time, it also contains multicenter skeletal σ bonds that cannot be reduced to a simple set of pairwise interactions. Thus, the bonding in B3Mg6 – is neither purely localized nor purely delocalized; instead, it is a hybrid framework in which both descriptions are required. Taken together, the bonding pattern of B3Mg6 – can be summarized as follows: the cluster contains three peripheral Mg–Mg σ bonds, two B–B σ bonds, two orthogonal 3c–2e π bonds in the linear B3 core, and additional 4c–2e and 8c–2e skeletal σ delocalization. This hierarchy of bonding elements provides a compact and chemically consistent description of the cluster and explains why the D 3h linear-B3-centered structure is energetically viable.

To further quantify and substantiate the σ aromaticity of the GM B3Mg6 – cluster, we performed nucleus-independent chemical shift (NICS) calculations, a widely recognized magnetic criterion for aromaticity. Representative probe points near the BMg3 unit show negative NICS-type values, including −8.93 (−13.43), −9.14 (−10.53), and −22.39 (−12.17) ppm (Table S2). Large negative NICS values are diagnostic of aromatic character, whereas positive values indicate antiaromaticity. The result is compatible with electron delocalization within the σ framework and suggests that the magnetic response is not dominated solely by local atomic contributions.

The ICSS contour maps offer a direct visualization of this behavior (Figure ). In both the BMg3 plane (0 Å) and the plane 1 Å above it, clear shielding regions are observed around the BMg3 framework and its vicinity. The contour distribution is spatially extended rather than sharply localized on a single atom, implying that the shielding effect is associated with the collective framework instead of with isolated atomic basins. The ICSS maps reveal prominent circular green and yellow regions of shielding within the perimeter of the molecular wheel, which are the unmistakable signatures of σ aromaticity in this system.

Iso-chemical shielding surfaces (ICSSs) of the GM B3Mg6 – cluster: (a) in the BMg3 plane (0 Å) and (b) 1 Å above the BMg3 plane. For ICSSs, a positive value indicates aromaticity, and vice versa.

Finally, the electron localization function (ELF) was employed as an independent, electron-density-based descriptor of aromaticity (Figure ). The calculated ELFσ isosurfaces show significant bifurcation values of 0.76 and 0.83, indicating that the σ-electron density remains well connected even at relatively high localization thresholds. Such behavior is characteristic of a robust delocalized σ framework rather than a purely localized collection of two-center bonds. In particular, the persistence of connected ELFσ domains around the cap-associated region suggests that the σ electron density is shared across more than one local bond element.

Electron localization functions (ELFs) for the σ framework, ELFσ for B3Mg6 –.

Taken together, the ELF, NICS, and ICSS results consistently support appreciable σ delocalization in B3Mg6 –, complementing the localized B–B and Mg–Mg bonding elements identified above. These analyses do not replace the orbital-based description; rather, they strengthen it by showing that the BMg3-containing portion of the cluster has a coherent delocalized σ component that contributes to the overall stability of the framework.

Metal-Dependent Stabilization in B3Mg6 – and B3Zn6 –

The close structural relationship between B3Mg6 – and the previously reported B3Zn6 – cluster provides an opportunity to distinguish the intrinsic bonding of the linear B3 chain from the metal-dependent stabilization supplied by the outer framework. In both clusters, the B3 core is supported by two B–B 2c–2e σ bonds and two orthogonal B–B–B 3c–2e π bonds, indicating that the internal bonding pattern of the boron chain is largely retained upon metal substitution.

The electronic role of the metal framework, however, is substantially different. In B3Zn6 –, the filled Zn 3d10 shells remain essentially nonbonding, and the BZn3 caps were described by triplet-like σ2σ1σ1* configurations that give rise to cap-centered 4σ aromaticity. Mg has no corresponding occupied d shell, and the bonding orbitals of B3Mg6 – are constructed directly from the Mg 3s/3p and B 2s/2p valence orbitals. The AdNDP pattern of B3Mg6 – consequently contains fully occupied 4c–2e σ bonds within the BMg3 regions and, more importantly, two 8c–2e σ bonds extending over the broader cluster skeleton.

The natural charge distribution also indicates enhanced electron donation from Mg to the terminal B atoms. At the respective computational levels, the terminal B atoms carry charges of −2.04 |e| in B3Mg6 –, compared with approximately −1.70 |e| in B3Zn6 –, while the metal charges are approximately +0.70 and +0.60 |e| for Mg and Zn, respectively. Furthermore, the interlayer Mg–Mg WBI of 0.59 is larger than the reported Zn–Zn value of 0.38. Although these numerical differences should be interpreted cautiously because the two studies employed different computational protocols, they consistently indicate a stronger ionic component and a different distribution of metal–metal bonding in the Mg system.

Thus, the principal chemical insight obtained from B3Mg6 – is not merely that Mg can also support a linear B3 chain. Rather, the Mg analogue demonstrates that closely related linear-B3-centered geometries may be stabilized through electronically distinct peripheral frameworks: a cap-centered, triplet-derived σ-aromatic description in B3Zn6 – versus stronger charge transfer and more globally extended closed-shell skeletal σ delocalization in B3Mg6 –.

The electronic stability of the GM B3Mg6 – structure is further reflected in its frontier molecular orbitals (Figure ). The HOMO and HOMO – 1 are degenerate occupied orbitals, and the LUMO lies substantially higher in energy. The resulting HOMO–LUMO gap is 2.30 eV, indicating that the D 3h structure corresponds to a well-defined closed-shell species with good resistance to low-energy electronic excitation. This sizable gap supports the electronic robustness of the linear-B3-centered framework. To investigate its dynamic stability, a 50 ps Born–Oppenheimer molecular dynamics (BOMD) simulation was conducted at 300 K using the PBE0 level. By analyzing the structural evolution during the BOMD simulation process, the dynamic stability of the cluster can be evaluated. Specifically, this is quantified using the root-mean-square deviation (RMSD) as shown in Figure . Usually, the structure of the first frame in the trajectory is taken as the reference structure, and the RMSD of each frame molecule is calculated, and a curve is plotted. The smaller the fluctuation of the RMSD value, the more difficult it is for the structural heteroligation to occur. As shown in the figure, at 300 K, the cluster exhibits good dynamic stability and maintains the structure during the simulation and is not prone to heteroligation and decomposition.

Conclusions

In summary, quantum chemical calculations identify B3Mg6 – as a highly symmetric D 3h global minimum containing a rare linear B3 core embedded in a Mg-rich framework. Bonding analyses reveal that the cluster is stabilized by a combination of three peripheral Mg–Mg 2c–2e σ bonds, two B–B 2c–2e σ bonds, and two orthogonal 3c–2e π bonds associated with the linear boron chain. In addition, the framework exhibits 4c–2e and 8c–2e skeletal σ delocalization, indicating that both local bonding and global framework delocalization contribute to stability. ELF, NICS, and ICSS analyses further support appreciable σ delocalization associated with the BMg3-containing framework. The substantial charge transfer from Mg atoms to the boron skeleton highlights the mixed ionic–covalent nature of the system, while the 2.30 eV HOMO–LUMO gap indicates good electronic robustness of the closed-shell anion. Taken together, these results show that the stability of B3Mg6 – arises from a cooperative hierarchy of bonding elements rather than from any single localized interaction. Overall, the present study provides a clear and chemically intuitive bonding picture for B3Mg6 –. More broadly, it offers additional insight into how atomically thin boron-chain motifs may be stabilized in metal-rich alloy cluster environments through the combined effects of internal boron-chain bonding, metal-assisted charge transfer, peripheral metal–metal interaction, and skeletal σ delocalization.

Rewriting Systems on Arbitrary Monoids
Eduardo Magalhães

Abstract
. In this paper, we introduce monoidal rewriting systems (MRS), an abstraction of string rewriting in which reductions are defined over an arbitrary ambient monoid rather than a free monoid of words. This shift is partly motivated by logic: the class of free monoids is not first-order axiomatizable, so “working in the free setting” cannot be treated internally when applying first-order methods to rewriting presentations. To analyze these systems categorically, we define 𝐍𝐂𝐑𝐒 𝟐 \mathbf{NCRS_{2}} as the 2-category of Noetherian Confluent MRS. We then prove the existence of a canonical biadjunction between 𝐍𝐂𝐑𝐒 𝟐 \mathbf{NCRS_{2}} and 𝐌𝐨𝐧 \mathbf{Mon} . Finally, we classify all Noetherian Confluent MRS that present a given fixed monoid. For this, we introduce Generalized Elementary Tietze Transformations (GETTs) and prove that any two presentations of a monoid are connected by a (possibly infinite) sequence of these transformations, yielding a complete characterization of generating systems up to GETT-equivalence.



1.
Introduction

The study of rewriting systems has long occupied a central position at the intersection of theoretical computer science and algebra. Originating in the early 20th century with the work of Axel Thue
[
8
]
, these systems were developed to address fundamental decidability questions such as the word problem for semigroups and monoids (and, indirectly, groups). Over the decades, the theory has matured into a robust framework for analysing algebraic structures by algorithmic means.

This field has been dominated by String Rewriting Systems (SRS), also known as semi-Thue systems. As detailed in the foundational work of Book and Otto ”String rewriting systems”
[
3
]
, an SRS is defined as a binary relation on a free monoid generated by some alphabet. When such a system is Noetherian and confluent, interpreting these relations as reduction rules allows one to rewrite complex words into unique irreducible forms, thereby offering a systematic approach to solving the word problem and other decidability problems.

Parallel to the development of SRS, the theory of Abstract Rewriting Systems (ARS) emerged to abstract certain key properties of reduction such as confluence, local confluence, termination and so on. The key idea behind this abstraction is that, although when working with SRS we are rewriting words on free monoids, the study of properties such as termination and confluence does not, in general, depend on the specific free monoidal structure present. As surveyed by Terese in ”Term Rewriting Systems”
[
9
]
, ARS theory provides general notions of reduction relations and normal forms that are present not only when working with string rewriting but also term rewriting and lambda calculus. However, despite this high level of abstraction and countless uses in computer science, applications to the study of monoids have traditionally remained grounded to the free context: reductions are almost exclusively defined as transformations between words in a free monoid
F_{A}
, for some alphabet
A
.

However, a structural limitation of classical rewriting systems is that they are intrinsically tied to free monoids. From a logical perspective, this creates a foundational mismatch: Let
L=\{\cdot\}
be the first-order language of monoids, and let
K=\{\cdot,R\}
be its expansion by a designated binary relation symbol. Given a monoid, its presentations by rewriting can naturally be interpreted as structures in the richer language
K
, while many structural questions about the monoid itself are most naturally stated in
L
. The caveat is that, in the classical setting, rewriting is restricted to free monoids, which are not first-order axiomatizable. As such, when interpreting a
K
-structure as a rewriting system, “freeness” cannot be treated internally and must be imposed as an external semantic condition.

This observation suggests that, if one aims to use first-order/model-theoretic tools to study presentations of monoids, rewriting should be formulated intrinsically over arbitrary monoids (or at least over an elementary class of monoids).

Motivated by these considerations, we introduce Monoidal Rewriting Systems (from here onward abbreviated as MRS), a generalization of string rewriting systems in which reductions are defined directly over an arbitrary monoid, rather than over a free monoid of words. When string rewriting systems are used to present a monoid, the essential property that underpins the process is simply the ability to compose elements, that is, the presence of a monoidal structure. From this perspective, the syntactic representation provided by words in a free monoid is not strictly necessary. By defining rewriting relations intrinsically on a monoid, we decouple rewriting from its traditional syntactic setting and obtain a notion of reduction that is internal to the algebraic structure itself.

Furthermore, modern developments in category theory suggest that rewriting systems can be naturally understood not merely as static algebraic presentations, but as dynamic 2-dimensional structures, as exemplified by the theory of polygraphs and higher-dimensional word problems
[
1
,
4
]
. As emphasized in the work of Johnson and Yau
[
6
]
, 2-categories provide a natural language to describe transformations between morphisms, making them an ideal setting for formalizing relationships between different rewriting systems. Motivated by this perspective, the present work not only abstracts the underlying algebraic setting of rewriting from free to arbitrary monoids, but also organizes these systems within a strict 2-categorical framework, so that generating systems and their equivalences can be studied using inherently higher-dimensional, categorical tools.

This paper is organized as follows:

Section 2 sets some notation and reviews some of the preliminaries necessary for the rest of this work.

In section 3, we define a monoidal rewriting system (MRS) as a triple
(M,\cdot,R)
where
(M,\cdot)
is a monoid and
R\subseteq M\times M
is a binary relation. We proceed by proving some small technical lemmas and results that are direct generalizations of basic known facts about SRS. One interesting fact about these lemmas is that their statements and proofs are essentially identical to those of their classical counterparts, which further strengthens the idea that the monoidal structure present in any SRS is the essential part that makes these lemmas work, and that the syntactic information that free monoids entail is not necessary at all. After this, we build the 2-category
\mathbf{NCRS_{2}}
of Noetherian confluent MRS and explore the existence of a biadjunction between this 2-category and the 2-category of monoids
\mathbf{Mon}
. The proof of this biadjunction is quite lengthy, and so we present it at the end in Appendix A.

Finally, in section 4, we answer the question: Given a fixed monoid
(M,\cdot)
, what can be said about the family of Noetherian confluent MRS
(A,\cdot,R)
that present
(M,\cdot)
? For this, we introduce the notion of a generalized elementary Tietze transformation (GETT) and prove that any two Noetherian confluent MRS that present the same monoid can be obtained from one another by a (possibly infinite) sequence of GETTs.

Preliminaries

We assume familiarity with the basics of Abstract Rewriting Systems (ARS) as detailed in
[
9
]
. Given an ARS
(A,\to)
, we use
\to^{*}
to denote the reflexive transitive closure of
\to
,
\leftrightarrow^{*}
for the symmetric reflexive transitive closure of
\to
, and
\bar{u}
to denote the unique normal form of an element

in a Noetherian confluent ARS.

Recall that an ARS
(A,\to)
is said to have the Church–Rosser property, if given
a,b\in A
with
a\leftrightarrow^{*}b
, then there exists some
c\in A
such that
a\to^{*}c
and
b\to^{*}c
. The following is Theorem 2.1.5 in
[
2
]
.

Theorem
.

Let
(A,\to)
be a ARS. Then
(A,\to)
is confluent if and only if it has the Church-Rosser property.

Given a set
A
, we use
F_{A}
to denote the free monoid generated by
A
with multiplication being given by concatenation.

Definition
.

A string rewriting system (SRS) is a pair
(A,R)
, where
A
is a set and
R\subseteq F_{A}\times F_{A}
is a binary relation.

For reasons that will become evident later on, we will denote a string rewriting system
(A,R)
by
(F_{A},R)
, where
R\subseteq F_{A}\times F_{A}
.

Given two words

, we write

if there are some

such that
u=axb
,

and

.

Given any SRS
(F_{A},R)
, then

forms an ARS and so we say that a SRS
(F_{A},R)
is Noetherian [confluent] if
(F_{A},\to_{R})
is Noetherian [confluent] as an ARS. Again, as
(F_{A},\to_{R})
is an ARS, we define
\to_{R}^{*}
and
\leftrightarrow_{R}^{*}
to be the reflexive transitive closure and the symmetric reflexive transitive closure of
\to_{R}
respectively.

Note that the relation
\leftrightarrow_{R}^{*}
is a congruence on the monoid
F_{A}
. Given a monoid
M
and a SRS

, we say that

(F_{A}/\leftrightarrow_{R}^{*})\simeq M
.

Recall the definition of elementary Tietze transformation (Definition 7.2.1 in
[
3
]
):

Definition
.

Let
(F_{A},R)
be a string rewriting system. The following transformations of

are called elementary Tietze transformations:

(1)

Given

with

, then

(2)

Given

, then
(F_{A},R\setminus\{(u,v)\})
is obtained from
(F_{A},R)
by an elementary Tietze transformation of type 2;

(3)

Let
a\in F_{A}
and
v
be a new symbol, i.e.
v\not\in A
. Then
(F_{A\cup\{v\}},R\cup\{(v,a)\})
is obtained from
(F_{A},R)
by an elementary Tietze transformation of type 3;

(4)

Let

. Define a homomorphisms

\phi_{a}(b)=\begin{cases}b&\text{if $b\in F_{A\setminus\{a\}}$}\\

Performing any type of elementary Tietze transformation does not change the monoid presented by a SRS (see Lemma 7.2.2 in

). One of the most important uses of these transformations is the following theorem (see Theorem 7.2.4 in

]

Theorem

(Tietze’s Theorem)
.

Let
(F_{A},R)
and
(F_{B},L)
be two finite presentations (i.e. the alphabets

.

Finally, we briefly discuss the conventions about 2-categories adopted in this paper.

We work exclusively with strict 2-categories and strict 2-functors. We adopt the standard definition of strong transformations and 2-natural transformations as found in Definition 4.2.1 of
[
6
]
. Specifically, a strong transformation
\alpha:F\to G
consists of 1-cells
\alpha_{A}
and invertible 2-cells
\alpha_{f}
satisfying the naturality condition
G(f)\circ\alpha_{A}\cong\alpha_{B}\circ F(f)
. A strict 2-natural transformation is just a strong transformation where each component 2-cell
\alpha_{f}
is the identity. We also use the standard definition of a modification given in Definition 4.4.1 in
[
6
]
.

Finally, by biadjunction we mean the following:

Definition
.

Let
\mathbf{C}
and
\mathbf{D}
be strict 2-categories and consider two strict 2-functors
L:\mathbf{C}\to\mathbf{D}
(left adjoint) and
R:\mathbf{D}\to\mathbf{C}
(right adjoint);

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{C}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{D}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces R}
\dashv

A biadjunction is given by two strong transformations
\eta:1_{\mathbf{C}}\to RL
(unit) and
\varepsilon:LR\to 1_{\mathbf{D}}
(counit) such that
\eta
and
\varepsilon
satisfy the triangle identities up to invertible modifications, known as triangulator modifications. Furthermore, we require these triangulator modifications to be coherent, i.e. we require them to satisfy the swallowtail identities.

Note that, in the literature, the definition definition of a biadjunction is normally stated in full generality, but as all the 2-categories and 2-functors we work with here are strict, we chose to state this way to better accommodate our specific needs.

3.
Monoidal Rewriting Systems

We start by laying down the core definitions and proving some technical lemmas, which are simple generalizations of classical and fundamental results about string rewriting systems.

Definition 3.1
.

A monoidal rewriting system (MRS)
\mathcal{M}
is a triple
(M,\cdot,R)
where
(M,\cdot)
is a monoid and
R\subseteq M\times M
is a binary relation.

When the operation
\cdot
on
M
is implicit by context, we might write the MRS
(M,\cdot,R)
as
(M,R)
.

Note
: For the moment we postpone the examples, in order to first introduce some further definitions.

Given
a,b\in M
, we set
a\to_{R}b
to mean that there exists
x,y,s,t\in M
such that
a=xsy
,
b=xty
and
(s,t)\in R
. We use
\to_{R}^{*}
to denote the reflexive and transitive closure of
\to_{R}
, meaning that
a\to_{R}^{*}b
if and only if
a=b
or if there exists
w_{1},\ldots,w_{n}\in M
with
n\geq 1
such that

a\to_{R}w_{1}\to_{R}\ldots\to_{R}w_{n}\to_{R}b

Note:
The relation
\to_{R}
is compatible with the monoid operation in the following sense: given
a,b,u,t\in M
, if
u\to_{R}t
then
aub\to_{R}atb
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{M}=(M,\cdot,R)
be a MRS and
u,v,a,b\in M
. If
u\to_{R}^{*}a
and
v\to_{R}^{*}b
then
uv\to_{R}^{*}ab
.

Proof.

As stated previously, the relation
\to_{R}
is compatible with monoid multiplication, in the sense that given
a,b,u,t\in M
, if
u\to_{R}t
then
aub\to_{R}atb
. Because
u\to_{R}^{*}a
, we know that there exists
u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}\in M
such that

u\to_{R}u_{1}\to_{R}\ldots\to_{R}u_{n}\to_{R}a

As such,

uv\to_{R}u_{1}v\to_{R}\ldots\to_{R}u_{n}v\to_{R}av

So
uv\to_{R}^{*}av
. Repeating the same argument, we see that
av\to_{R}^{*}ab
, and therefore
uv\to_{R}^{*}ab

∎

We use
\leftrightarrow_{R}^{*}
to denote the reflexive transitive symmetric closure of
\to_{R}
. An explicit description can be given as follows: Given
a,b\in M
, then
a\leftrightarrow_{R}^{*}b
if and only if
a=b
or if there are
w_{1},\ldots,w_{n}\in M
, for some
n\geq 1
such that

w_{i}\to_{R}w_{i+1}\text{ or }w_{i+1}\to_{R}w_{i}

for
i=0,\ldots,n
, where
w_{0}:=a
and
w_{n+1}=b
.

If
a\leftrightarrow_{R}^{*}b
, then we will call such
w_{1},\ldots,w_{n}\in M
a
derivation
for
a\leftrightarrow_{R}^{*}b
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
(M,\cdot,R)
be a MRS. Then equivalence relation
\leftrightarrow_{R}^{*}
is a congruence.

Proof.

By definition,
\leftrightarrow_{R}^{*}
is the reflexive, symmetric and transitive closure of the
one-step relation
\to_{R}
, hence it is an equivalence relation. It remains to show that it is
stable under multiplication on the left and on the right.

Now let
a,b\in M
such that
a\leftrightarrow_{R}^{*}b
and let
x,y\in M
. If
a=b
, then it follows trivially that
xay\leftrightarrow_{R}^{*}xby
. On the other hand, let
w_{1},\ldots,w_{n}\in M
be a derivation for
a\leftrightarrow_{R}^{*}b
. Then
xw_{1}y,\ldots,xw_{n}y\in M
is a derivation for
xay\leftrightarrow_{R}^{*}xby

∎

As
\leftrightarrow_{R}^{*}
is a congruence on
M
, we can form the quotient monoid
M/\leftrightarrow_{R}^{*}
. When
(M,\cdot,R)
satisfies additional properties, there is a useful alternative characterization of this quotient monoid, as we will see now.

Definition 3.4
.

We say that a MRS
(M,\cdot,R)
is confluent, if the abstract rewriting system
(M,\to_{R})
is confluent.

We say that MRS is Noetherian if there is no infinite chain of elements
w_{1},w_{2},\ldots
in
M
such that

w_{1}\to_{R}w_{2}\to_{R}\ldots

with
w_{i}\neq w_{i+1}
, for all
i\geq 1
.

Given a MRS
(M,\cdot,R)
, we say that an element
a\in M
is irreducible if for any
v\in M
,
a\to_{R}v
implies that
a=v
.

Note:
When we are working with classical string rewriting system
(\Sigma,R)
, typically a word
u\in\Sigma^{*}
is said to be irreducible if there isn’t a word
v\in\Sigma^{*}
such that
u\to_{R}v
. In particular, in this setting, if we can rewrite
u
as
u
(for example, if
(u,u)\in R
), then
u
would not be considered irreducible. We move away from this definition because of the following: Let
M
be any monoid with a
0
element and consider any MRS
(M,\cdot,R)
. Given any
(a,b)\in R
, then
0=0\cdot a\cdot 0
and
0=0\cdot b\cdot 0
so we always have
0\to_{R}0
, and as such
0
would never be considered irreducible if we went with the classical definition. However, we might want
0
to be irreducible in some scenarios, and as such, in this paper, an element is reducible only when we can rewrite it as a
different
element.

Given a Noetherian confluent MRS
(M,\cdot,R)
, then
(M,\to_{R})
forms a Noetherian confluent abstract rewriting system. As such, given any
u\in M
, there exists a unique irreducible element
v\in M
such that
u\to_{R}^{*}v
. We call
v
the normal form of
u
and denote it by
\overline{u}
. When multiple rewriting relations are present, to avoid ambiguity, we might denote
\overline{u}
by
\overline{u}^{R}
.

Given a MRS
\mathcal{M}=(M,\cdot,R)
, we denote the subset of all irreducible elements by
\overline{M}
. Again, when multiple rewriting relations are present, we might denote
\overline{M}
by
\overline{M}^{R}
.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
\mathcal{M}=(M,\cdot,R)
be a Noetherian confluent MRS and
u,v\in M
. Then
\overline{uv}=\overline{\bar{u}\bar{v}}
.

Proof.

By definition we know that
u\to_{R}^{*}\bar{u}
and
v\to_{R}^{*}\bar{v}
. Then
uv\to_{R}^{*}\bar{u}\bar{v}
by Lemma
3.2
. Additionally by definition we know that
\bar{u}\bar{v}\to_{R}^{*}\overline{\bar{u}\bar{v}}
. With these we can conclude that

uv\to_{R}^{*}\bar{u}\bar{v}\to_{R}^{*}\overline{\bar{u}\bar{v}}

Because
\overline{\bar{u}\bar{v}}
is irreducible, by the uniqueness of the normal form,
\overline{uv}=\overline{\bar{u}\bar{v}}
.
∎

We now make precise the alternative description of
M/\leftrightarrow_{R}^{*}
hinted at earlier.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
(M,\cdot,R)
be a confluent Noetherian MRS. Consider the binary operation on
\overline{M}
given by:

\displaystyle\bar{\cdot}:\overline{M}\times\overline{M}

\displaystyle\to\overline{M}

\displaystyle(u,v)

\displaystyle\mapsto\overline{u\cdot v}

Then
(\overline{M},\bar{\cdot})
is a monoid, which we call the monoid of irreducibles of
\mathcal{M}
or the monoid defined by
\mathcal{M}
.

Proof.

We start by verifying that
\bar{1}
is the identity with respect to
\bar{\cdot}
. Let
a\in\overline{M}
and note that, as
a
is already irreducible, then
\bar{a}=a
. By Lemma
3.5
,

a\ \bar{\cdot}\ \bar{1}=\overline{a\cdot\bar{1}}=\overline{\bar{a}\cdot\bar{1}}=\overline{a\cdot 1}=\bar{a}=a

The same argument shows that
\bar{1}\ \bar{\cdot}\ a=a
.

As for the associativity, let
a,b,c\in\overline{M}
. Then:

\displaystyle a\ \bar{\cdot}\ (b\ \bar{\cdot}\ c)

\displaystyle=a\ \bar{\cdot}\ (\overline{b\cdot c})=\overline{a\cdot(\overline{b\cdot c})}=\overline{a\cdot(b\cdot c})

\displaystyle=\overline{(a\cdot b)\cdot c}=\overline{(\overline{a\cdot b})\cdot c}=(\overline{a\cdot b})\ \bar{\cdot}\ c=(a\ \bar{\cdot}\ b)\ \bar{\cdot}\ c\qed

The monoid of irreducibles of a Noetherian confluent MRS
(M,\cdot,R)
is related to the quotient monoid
M/\leftrightarrow_{R}^{*}
as follows:

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
(M,\cdot,R)
be a Noetherian confluent MRS. Then the quotient monoid
M/\leftrightarrow_{R}^{*}
is isomorphic to
(\overline{M},\bar{\cdot})
.

Proof.

Start by noting that, given
a\in M
, then
a\leftrightarrow_{R}^{*}\bar{a}
. Consider the map

\displaystyle f:\overline{M}

\displaystyle\to M/\leftrightarrow_{R}^{*}

\displaystyle v

\displaystyle\mapsto[v]_{R}

Where
[v]_{R}
denotes the equivalence class of
v
in
M/\leftrightarrow_{R}^{*}
.

Let
a,b\in\overline{M}
. If
f(a)=f(b)
, then by definition of
f
,
a\leftrightarrow_{R}^{*}b
. By the Church–Rosser property, there exists some
c\in M
such that
a\to_{R}^{*}c
and
b\to_{R}^{*}c
. As both
a
and
b
are irreducibles, it follows that
a=c=b
.

On the other hand, let
[a]_{R}
be an equivalence class in
M/\leftrightarrow_{R}^{*}
. Then
[a]_{R}\ =f(\bar{a})
.

Finally, let
a,b\in\overline{M}
and note that

\displaystyle f(a\ \bar{\cdot}\ b)

\displaystyle=[a\ \bar{\cdot}\ b]_{R}=[\overline{a\cdot b}]_{R}

\displaystyle=[a\cdot b]_{R}=[a]_{R}\cdot[b]_{R}=f(a)f(b)\qed

As stated earlier, we now explore some examples that show the versatility of this notion when writing presentations for various monoids.

Example 3.8
.

(1)

Consider the MRS
(\mathbb{N},+,R)
, where
R=\{(2,0)\}
. This MRS is Noetherian and confluent. The only two irreducible elements are
\overline{\mathbb{N}}=\{0,1\}
. For every element
n\in\mathbb{N}
, it’s easy to see that its normal form will be
n\to_{R}^{*}(n\mod 2)
. As such,
(\mathbb{N}/\leftrightarrow_{R}^{*})\simeq(\overline{\mathbb{N}},\overline{+})\simeq\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
;

(2)

Let
X=\{a,b,c\}
be a set and let
(\mathcal{P}X,\cup)
be the monoid of subsets of
X
with union. Consider, for example, the MRS
(\mathcal{P}X,\cup,R)
, where

R=\{(\{a\},\{a,c\}),(\{b,c\},\{a,b,c\})\}

In
(\mathcal{P}X,\cup,R)
, rewriting steps work as follows:

if
a\in U
, then
U\to_{R}U\cup\{c\}

if
b,c\in U
, then
U\to_{R}{U}\cup\{a\}

This MRS is Noetherian and confluent, and the irreducible elements are

\overline{\mathcal{P}X}=\{\emptyset,\{b\},\{c\},\{a,c\},X\}

(3)

Let
(X,\tau)
be a topological space and consider the monoid

where
\mathrm{cl}(\cdot)
denotes the topological closure. Then, in
(\mathcal{P}X,\cup,R_{0})
, the irreducible elements are precisely the closed subsets of

. Furthermore, for every subset

, so
\mathcal{P}X/\leftrightarrow_{R_{0}}^{*}

(4)

The last example can be further generalized: let
\mathcal{E}
be an elementary topos and

form a Heyting algebra, so consider the monoid

Let

be any Lawvere-Tierney topology on

(see Definition 1.3.30 in
[
5
]
) and note that this induces, for each object

\mathrm{cl}_{j}:\mathrm{Sub}(X)\to\mathrm{Sub}(X)

As such, akin to the last example, for each object

(\mathrm{Sub}(X),\vee,R)

Again, the irreducible elements are precisely the

X
.

In particular, a subobject
U
is
j
-dense if and only if
U\to_{R}^{*}X
.

Note that this example also includes the setting of a Grothendieck topology on a small category
\mathbf{C}
, as a Grothendieck topology on
\mathbf{C}
is nothing more than a Lawvere-Tierney topology on the elementary topos of presheaves
[\mathbf{C}^{\mathrm{op}},\mathbf{Set}]

(5)

Finally, we present an example from logic. Let
\Sigma
be a single sorted first order language and let
\mathrm{At}
denote the set of atomic formulas.

Consider a Horn theory
\mathbb{T}
(see Section 1.2 in
[
5
]
for the basic definitions and notation used here), and define the monoid
M=(\mathcal{P}^{<\omega}\mathrm{At},\cup)
of finite subsets of atomic formulas. Note that a finite subset
\Delta\subseteq\mathrm{At}
can be interpreted as the conjunction
\bigwedge_{\alpha\in\Delta}\alpha
.

With the axioms of
\mathbb{T}
in mind, we define a MRS
(M,\cup,R_{\mathbb{T}})
as follows:

R_{\mathbb{T}}=\{(\Gamma_{\phi},\Gamma_{\phi}\cup\Gamma_{\psi}):(\phi\vdash\psi)\in\mathbb{T}\}

Where
\Gamma_{\phi}
denotes the set of atomic formulas occurring in
\phi
and
\Gamma_{\psi}
is defined analogously.

This definition means that given two finite subsets
\Delta,\Delta^{\prime}\subseteq\mathrm{At}
, then
\Delta\to_{R_{\mathbb{T}}}\Delta^{\prime}
if and only if there exists some sequent
(\phi\vdash\psi)\in\mathbb{T}
with
\Gamma_{\phi}\subseteq\Delta
and
\Delta^{\prime}=\Delta\cup\Gamma_{\psi}
.

If the Horn theory
\mathbb{T}
is finite, then
(M,\cup,R_{\mathbb{T}})
is Noetherian and confluent, and to the normal
\overline{\Delta}
of a finite subset
\Delta\subseteq\mathrm{At}
, we call its Horn-closure. Then, in particular, an element
\Delta\in M
is irreducible if and only if it is Horn-closed.

So far we have defined MRS, proved some technical lemmas, and defined what the monoid of irreducibles associated with a Noetherian confluent MRS is. This way of assigning a monoid of irreducible elements to each Noetherian confluent MRS defines a map from the class of Noetherian confluent MRS to the class of monoids. What follows now is a way of building a suitable category where Noetherian confluent MRS ’live’ such that this construction is actually functorial to the category of monoids. As we will see, there is also a way of assigning a ’canonical’ Noetherian confluent MRS to every monoid in a functorial way and, in fact, these two functors will form a (bi)adjunction.

We start by defining that a morphism between two MRS is:

Definition 3.9
.

Let
\mathcal{M}=(M,\cdot,R)
and
\mathcal{N}=(N,*,L)
be two MRS. A MRS-homomorphism
\phi:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{N}
is a monoid homomorphism
\phi:(M,\cdot)\to(N,*)
such that, for any
u,v\in M
, we have:

(u,v)\in R\implies\phi(u)\to_{L}^{*}\phi(v)

Note:
Given a MRS-homomorphism
\phi:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{N}
, and
u,v\in M
, if
u\to_{R}^{*}v
then
\phi(u)\to_{L}^{*}\phi(v)
. Note also that the composition of two MRS-homomorphisms is still a MRS-homomorphism.

Before continuing we prove the following technical lemma.

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
\mathcal{M}=(M,\cdot,R)
and
\mathcal{N}=(N,*,L)
be two Noetherian confluent MRS,
u\in M
and
\phi:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{N}
a MRS-homomorphism. Then:
\overline{\phi(u)}^{L}=\overline{\phi(\overline{u}^{R})}^{L}
.

Proof.

By definition of normal form, we know that
u\to_{R}^{*}\overline{u}^{R}
. As
\phi
is a MRS-homomorphism, then
\phi(u)\to_{L}^{*}\phi(\overline{u}^{R})
. Now, consider the normal form of
\phi(\overline{u}^{R})
: by definition
\phi(\overline{u}^{R})\to_{L}^{*}\overline{\phi(\overline{u}^{R})}^{L}
. With this we can conclude that

\phi(u)\to_{L}^{*}\phi(\overline{u}^{R})\to_{L}^{*}\overline{\phi(\overline{u}^{R})}^{L}

Because
\overline{\phi(\overline{u}^{R})}^{L}
is irreducible, by uniqueness of the normal form associated with any element, we have
\overline{\phi(u)}^{L}=\overline{\phi(\overline{u}^{R})}^{L}
.
∎

Monoidal rewriting systems together with MRS-homomorphisms form a category, which we denote by
\mathbf{MRS}
. We use
\mathbf{NCRS}
to denote the full subcategory of
\mathbf{MRS}
of Noetherian confluent MRS.

We then give

a (strict)
2
-categorical structure in the following way:

•

The
0

•

The
1

•

Given two MRS-homomorphisms

, there exists a unique
2
-cell

if and only if, for all

In particular, given 1-cells

is a thin groupoid since if there exists a 2-cell

and uniqueness guarantees that they are inverses of each other.

Vertical composition of

Consider three parallel

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(A,\cdot,R)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(B,*,L)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\ \beta}

.

As for horizontal composition, consider the following:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(A,\cdot,R)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(B,*,L)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(C,\times,W)}

Then, the interchange law is rather straightforward to verify. Consider the following:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(A,\cdot,R)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(B,*,L)}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\ \beta}

The following lemma is the first step towards understanding how the category

Lemma 3.11
.

Let
\mathcal{M}=(M,\cdot,R)
and
\mathcal{N}=(N,*,L)
be Noetherian confluent MRS, and
\phi:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{N}
a MRS-homomorphism. Then, the map

\displaystyle\phi^{\sharp}:(\overline{M},\bar{\cdot})

\displaystyle\to(\overline{N},\bar{*})

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle\mapsto\overline{\phi(u)}

Is a monoid homomorphism. Additionally, given MRS-homomorphisms
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(M,\cdot,R)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(N,*,L)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(H,\times,W)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\psi}

Then
(\psi\circ\phi)^{\sharp}=\psi^{\sharp}\circ\phi^{\sharp}
.

Proof.

Let
a,b\in\overline{M}
. Then, by definition
\phi^{\sharp}(a\ \bar{\cdot}\ b)=\overline{\phi(\overline{a\cdot b})}
. By Lemma
3.10
, this is equal to
\overline{\phi(a\cdot b)}
which is equal to
\overline{\phi(a)*\phi(b)}
. By Lemma
3.5
, we have:

\overline{\phi(a)*\phi(b)}=\overline{\overline{\phi(a)}*\overline{\phi(b)}}=\overline{\phi^{\sharp}(a)*\phi^{\sharp}(b)}=\phi^{\sharp}(a)\ \bar{*}\ \phi^{\sharp}(b)

Additionally, let
\phi
and
\psi
be two composable MRS-homomorphisms. Given

, then

\displaystyle(\psi\circ\phi)^{\sharp}(u)

\displaystyle=\overline{(\psi\circ\phi)(u)}=\overline{\psi(\phi(u))}

\displaystyle=\psi^{\sharp}(\overline{\phi(u)})=\psi^{\sharp}(\phi^{\sharp}(u))=(\psi^{\sharp}\circ\phi^{\sharp})(u)\qed

With this we can define a functor

I:\mathbf{NCRS}\to\mathbf{Mon}

to the category of monoids, where given a confluent Noetherian MRS

, then

is its monoid of irreducibles, and given a MRS-homomorphism

is the homomorphism

.

Note:
We can extend

by considering the functor

Note also that internally in

Notes

(2)

Following again the notation of Example

We conclude this section with a few remarks about Theorem
3.16
.

In general, the biadjunction
G\dashv I
is best read as a universal property for the canonical presentation
G(M)
of a monoid
M
, together with a precise description of how ”semantic” monoid maps lift to ”syntactic” maps of rewriting systems. Concretely, for every monoid
M
and every Noetherian confluent MRS
A
, the biadjunction provides an equivalence of hom-categories

\hom_{\mathbf{NCRS}_{2}}\bigl(G(M),A\bigr)\;\simeq\;\hom_{\mathbf{Mon}}\bigl(M,I(A)\bigr),

natural in both variables (see
[
7
]
).

Because
\mathbf{Mon}
is locally discrete, the right-hand side is a discrete category. In practice this rigidifies the left-hand side: the objects of
\hom_{\mathbf{NCRS}_{2}}(G(M),A)
are classified, up to internal isomorphism, by monoid maps
M\to I(A)
, and any two 1-cells
G(M)\rightrightarrows A
that induce the same monoid map are necessarily related by a unique 2-cell. Said differently, the biadjunction turns ”a monoid homomorphism out of
M
” into ”a map of rewriting systems out of the canonical presentation
G(M)
”, and it does so uniquely up to the inevitable 2-cells in
\mathbf{NCRS}_{2}
.

An instance of this is the following: take
A=G(N)
for another monoid
N
. Then the same equivalence reads

\hom_{\mathbf{NCRS}_{2}}\bigl(G(M),G(N)\bigr)\;\simeq\;\hom_{\mathbf{Mon}}(M,N)

As
IG(N)=N
. So on the image of
G
the 2-category
\mathbf{NCRS}_{2}
recovers
\mathbf{Mon}
without loss of information: maps between canonical presentations are exactly monoid homomorphisms.

As per the counit
\varepsilon_{A}:GI(A)\to A
, for a given Noetherian confluent MRS
A
, Theorem
3.16
implies that for any monoid
M
and any MRS-homomorphism
f:G(M)\to A
, the corresponding monoid homomorphism
\bar{f}:M\to I(A)
determines a 1-cell
G(\bar{f}):G(M)\to GI(A)
, and
f
is internally isomorphic in
\mathbf{NCRS_{2}}
to the composite

G(M)\xrightarrow{\,G(\bar{f})\,}GI(A)\xrightarrow{\,\varepsilon_{A}\,}A,

with that 2-isomorphism uniquely determined.

From the same universal property one also gets the following principle: to specify a map of rewriting systems
G(M)\to A
(up to the 2-dimensional identification of
\mathbf{NCRS}_{2}
) it suffices, and is necessary, to specify a monoid homomorphism
M\to I(A)
.

4.
Generalized Elementary Tietze Transformations

In this section we give an answer to the question: Given a fixed monoid
(M,\cdot)
, what can be said about the family of Noetherian confluent MRS
(A,\cdot,R)
that present
(M,\cdot)
, i.e.
I(A,\cdot,R)\simeq(M,\cdot)
?

We start by defining the main technical tool we use to answer this questions, which is a generalization of elementary Tietze transformation, modified to be compatible with our generalized setting.

In total we will define 4 kinds of generalized elementary Tietze transformations, however we will start by defining the first 3 first as the 4th one requires some more preliminary definitions.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
(A,\cdot,R)
be a MRS. The following ’procedures’ are called generalized elementary Tietze transformations (GETTs):

(1)

Given
a,b\in A
with
a\leftrightarrow_{R}^{*}b
, then
(A,\cdot,R\cup\{(a,b)\})
is obtained from
(A,\cdot,R)
by a GETT of type 1;

(2)

Given
(a,b)\in R
with
a\leftrightarrow_{R\setminus\{(a,b)\}}^{*}b
, then
(A,\cdot,R\setminus\{(a,b)\})
is obtained from
(A,\cdot,R)
by a GETT of type 2;

(3)

Let
a\in A
and
v
be a new symbol, i.e.
v\not\in A
. Then
(A*F_{v},\cdot,R\cup\{(v,a)\})
is obtained from
(A,\cdot,R)
by a GETT of type 3; Here
A*F_{v}
denotes the free product of
A
with the free monoid
F_{v}
;

Before moving on, we verify that performing a GETT of type 1,2 or 3 does not change the monoid being presented by a given MRS.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
(A,\cdot,R)
MRS. If
(B,*,L)
is obtained from
(A,\cdot,R)
by performing a GETT of type 1,2 or 3, then
B/\leftrightarrow_{L}^{*}\ \cong\ A/\leftrightarrow_{R}^{*}
.

Proof.

We treat the three GETTs separately.

(Type 1).

Assume
B=A
and
L=R\cup\{(a,b)\}
with
a\leftrightarrow_{R}^{*}b
. Start by noting that, since
R\subseteq L
, then
\leftrightarrow_{R}^{*}\subseteq\leftrightarrow_{L}^{*}
.

For the reverse inclusion, let
u\to_{L}v
be a one step reduction.

By definition,
u=xsy
and
v=xty
with for some
x,y,t,s\in A
and
(s,t)\in L
.
If
(s,t)\in R
then
u\to_{R}v
, hence
u\leftrightarrow_{R}^{*}v
. If
(s,t)=(a,b)
, then
a\leftrightarrow_{R}^{*}b
and since
\leftrightarrow_{R}^{*}
is a congruence we have
xay\leftrightarrow_{R}^{*}xby
, i.e.
u\leftrightarrow_{R}^{*}v
. Thus
u\to_{L}v
implies that
u\leftrightarrow_{R}^{*}v
, and so
\leftrightarrow_{L}^{*}\subseteq\leftrightarrow_{R}^{*}
.

Therefore
\leftrightarrow_{L}^{*}=\leftrightarrow_{R}^{*}
, and the identity on
A
induces an isomorphism
A/\!\leftrightarrow_{L}^{*}\cong A/\!\leftrightarrow_{R}^{*}
.

(Type 2).

Assume
B=A
, fix a pair of elements
(a,b)\in R
such that
a\leftrightarrow^{*}_{R\setminus\{(a,b)\}}b
and let
L=R\setminus\{(a,b)\}
. Note that, since
L\subseteq R
, we have
\leftrightarrow_{L}^{*}\subseteq\leftrightarrow_{R}^{*}
.

Conversely, let
u\to_{R}v
. If the applied rule is in
L
, then
u\to_{L}v
so
u\leftrightarrow_{L}^{*}v
. If the applied rule is
(a,b)
, then
u=xay
and
v=xby
, for some
x,y\in A
. By hypothesis
a\leftrightarrow_{L}^{*}b
, and since
\leftrightarrow_{L}^{*}
is a congruence,
xay\leftrightarrow_{L}^{*}xby
, i.e.
u\leftrightarrow_{L}^{*}v
. Hence
u\to_{R}v
implies that
u\leftrightarrow_{L}^{*}v
, and so
\leftrightarrow_{R}^{*}\subseteq\leftrightarrow_{L}^{*}
.

Therefore
\leftrightarrow_{L}^{*}=\leftrightarrow_{R}^{*}
and again

A/\!\leftrightarrow_{L}^{*}\cong A/\!\leftrightarrow_{R}^{*}
via the identity.

(Type 3).

Let
B=A*F_{v}
and
L=R\cup\{(v,a)\}
, with
v\notin A
and
a\in A
. Let

and

be the canonical inclusions.

Start by defining a monoid homomorphism

by
\theta(v)=a
, i.e.
\theta(v^{n})=a^{n}
for all

and
\theta(\varepsilon)=1_{A}
. By the universal property of the free product, there is a unique monoid homomorphism

:
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A*F_{v}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F_{v}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{id}_{A}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces h}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces j}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\theta}

Now define homomorphisms on quotients by setting:

\displaystyle\varphi:A/\!\leftrightarrow_{R}^{*}\

\displaystyle\to\ B/\!\leftrightarrow_{L}^{*}

\displaystyle[x]_{R}

\displaystyle\mapsto[i(x)]_{L}

which is well-defined because
R\subseteq L
implies
x\leftrightarrow_{R}^{*}y\Rightarrow i(x)\leftrightarrow_{L}^{*}i(y)
.

Additionally, define:

\displaystyle\psi:B/\!\leftrightarrow_{L}^{*}

\displaystyle\to\ A/\!\leftrightarrow_{R}^{*}

\displaystyle[b]_{L}

\displaystyle\mapsto[h(b)]_{R}

Claim:
The homomorphism

is well defined:

is well-defined, it suffices to check that

Start by assuming that the step uses a rule

. Additionally, note that

. Therefore

. Analogously,

.
On the other hand, If the step uses

.

Now consider the composite:

(\psi\circ\varphi)([x]_{R})=\psi([i(x)]_{L})=[h(i(x))]_{R}=[x]_{R},

so
\psi\circ\varphi=\mathrm{id}
.

For the other composite, we start by showing that for every

,

b\ \leftrightarrow_{L}^{*}\ i(h(b)).

Start by writing

in reduced free-product normal form as an alternating product of elements of

and powers of

Since

. Furthermore, as

is a congruence, it follows that for each

. As such, we obtain

On the other hand, by definition of

As such:

(\varphi\circ\psi)([b]_{L})=\varphi([h(b)]_{R})=[i(h(b))]_{L}=[b]_{L}.

Therefore
\varphi

To define generalized elementary Tietze transformations of type 4, we start by defining the following:

Definition 4.3
.

Let
(M,\cdot,R)
be a Noetherian confluent MRS. A subset
J\subseteq R
is said to be confluent if, as an abstract rewriting system,
(M,\to_{J})
is confluent.

Note:
Given a Noetherian confluent MRS
(M,\cdot,R)
, then for any subset
J\subseteq R
we have that
(M,\to_{J})
is a Noetherian abstract rewriting system.

Let
A=(M,\cdot,R)
be a Noetherian confluent MRS and let
J\subseteq R
be a confluent subset. We define
A_{J}
to be the MRS
A_{J}=(M_{J},\cdot_{J},R_{J})
, where:

•

The monoid
(M_{J}.\cdot_{J})
is the monoid
I(M,\cdot,J)
of irreducibles induced by applying only the rules present in
J
. When
J
is implicit by context, we shall simply write
\cdot
instead of
\cdot_{J}
;

•

R_{J}
is a the subset of
M_{J}\times M_{J}
constructed as follows:
For each
(a,b)\in R\setminus J
, we add
(\overline{a}^{J},\overline{b}^{J})
to
R_{J}
. No other elements aside from these are in
R_{J}
.

Definition 4.4
.

Let
(M,\cdot,R)
be a Noetherian confluent MRS. A subset
J\subseteq R
is said to be coherent, if
J
is a confluent subset and
(M_{J},\cdot_{J},R_{J})
is a Noetherian confluent MRS.

Example 4.5
.

(1)

Consider the free monoid
A=F_{a,b}
. Let

R=\{(ab,1),(ba,1)\}.

This MRS is Noetherian and confluent, and furthermore, every word in

A
reduces to a unique normal form of the form
a^{n}
,
b^{n}
or
1
.

Now consider the proper subset

J=\{(ab,1)\}\subsetneq R.

Using only
J
, a word is
J
-irreducible if and only if it contains no occurrence of
the sub-word
ab
, which is equivalent to being of the form
b^{m}a^{n}
for some
n,m\geq 0
.

Thus

M_{J}=I(A,\cdot,J)=\{\,b^{m}a^{n}\mid m,n\geq 0\,\},

and the induced multiplication
\cdot_{J}
is given by concatenation on

F_{a,b}
followed by maximal deletion of occurrences of
ab
.

To compute
R_{J}
, note that
R\setminus J=\{(ba,1)\}
. The word
ba

contains no copy of
ab
, so it is already
J
-irreducible therefore

R_{J}=\{(ba,1)\}.

The collapsed system is

A_{J}=(M_{J},\cdot_{J},R_{J})=\bigl(\{b^{m}a^{n}\}_{m,n\geq 0},\cdot_{J},\{(ba,1)\}\bigr).

A_{J}
is Noetherian and confluent, and the irreducible words are
1,a^{k}
and
b^{k}
for some
k\geq 1
.

In particular,
J
is a coherent proper subset of
R
.

(2)

We now provide a negative example: Let
A=F_{a,A,b,B}
denote the free monoid with generators
a,A,b,B
. Multiplication is given by concatenation and consider
(A,\cdot,R)
where

R=\{(aA,1),(Aa,Bb),(Bb,1),(bB,1)\}

Let
J=\{(aA,1)\}
, and note that
J\subseteq R
is confluent. However,
J
is not coherent.
Start by noting that
a
is irreducible in
(A,\cdot,J)
, meaning that
a\in A_{J}
. The same goes for the word
Aa
. Now, we have

a\cdot_{J}Aa=\overline{aAa}^{J}=\overline{a}^{J}=a

Additionally,
R_{J}=\{(Aa,Bb),(Bb,1),(bB,1)\}
meaning that

a=a\cdot_{J}Aa\to_{R_{J}}a\cdot_{J}Bb

Note that
a\cdot_{J}Bb=\overline{aBb}^{J}=aBb\neq a
, which in particular means that
a
is not irreducible in
(A_{J},\cdot_{J},R_{J})
. Moreover

a=a\cdot_{J}Aa\to_{R_{J}}a\cdot_{J}Bb\to_{R_{J}}a\cdot_{J}1=a

Which means we can form the following infinite chain of elementary reductions

a\to_{R_{J}}a\cdot_{J}Bb\to_{R_{J}}a\to_{R_{J}}\ldots

meaning that
(A_{J},\cdot_{J},R_{J})
is not Noetherian, and as such,

is not coherent.

Definition 4.6
.

Let
(A,\cdot,R)
be a Noetherian confluent MRS and
J\subseteq R
be a coherent subset. Then
(A_{J},\cdot_{J},R_{J})
is said to be obtained from
(A,\cdot,R)
by a GETT of type 4.

The next natural step to take is to prove that performing a GETT of type 4 to a certain Noetherian confluent MRS does not change the monoid of irreducibles. For that, we start by proving some technical lemmas.

The first lemma tells us how we can relate rewriting steps with respect to
\to_{R}
to rewriting steps with respect to
\to_{R_{J}}
.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
(A,\cdot,R)
be a Noetherian confluent MRS, consider a coherent subset
J\subseteq R
and let
a,b\in A
. If
a\to_{R}b
in
(A,\cdot,R)
then
\overline{a}^{J}\to^{*}_{R_{J}}\overline{b}^{J}
in
(A_{J},\cdot_{J},R_{J})
.

Proof.

Assume that
a\to_{R}b
. By definition, this means that we can write
a=x\cdot s\cdot y
and
b=x\cdot t\cdot y
where
x,y,s,t\in A
and
(s,t)\in R
. Note, moreover, that

\overline{a}^{J}=\overline{x\cdot s\cdot y}^{J}=\overline{\overline{x}^{J}\cdot\overline{s}^{J}\cdot\overline{y}^{J}}^{J}=\overline{x}^{J}\cdot_{J}\overline{s}^{J}\cdot_{J}\overline{y}^{J}

\overline{b}^{J}=\overline{x\cdot t\cdot y}^{J}=\overline{\overline{x}^{J}\cdot\overline{t}^{J}\cdot\overline{y}^{J}}^{J}=\overline{x}^{J}\cdot_{J}\overline{t}^{J}\cdot_{J}\overline{y}^{J}

Now, if
(s,t)\in J
, then
\overline{s}^{J}=\overline{t}^{J}
and the equality
\overline{a}^{J}=\overline{b}^{J}
follows, from which we have trivially that
\overline{a}^{J}\to^{*}_{R_{J}}\overline{b}^{J}
in
(A_{J},\cdot_{J},R_{J})
.

On the other hand, if
(s,t)\in R\setminus J
, then by definition,
(\overline{s}^{J},\overline{t}^{J})\in R_{J}
, from which it follows that
\overline{a}^{J}\to^{*}_{R_{J}}\overline{b}^{J}
in
(A_{J},\cdot_{J},R_{J})
.
∎

Note:
Applying this Lemma multiple times allows us to prove that if

in
(A,\cdot,R)
, then
\overline{a}^{J}\to_{R_{J}}^{*}\overline{b}^{J}
in
(A_{J},\cdot_{J},R_{J})
.

Let

is not irreducible in

. As such, consider

, where

is the normal form of

with respect to

. By Lemma

, we conclude that

\overline{a}^{J}\to_{R_{J}}^{*}\overline{b}^{J}

Now, as
a
is an element of

, then by definition
\overline{a}^{J}=a
. On the other hand,

is irreducible with respect to

, then

, meaning that we end up with

.
∎

. From this we can conclude that,

, meaning that they have the same normal form.

From this claim we have that,
\overline{w_{i}}^{R}=\overline{x\cdot s\cdot y}^{R}=\overline{x\cdot t\cdot y}^{R}=\overline{w_{i+1}}^{R}
.

As
i=0,\ldots,n-1
is arbitrary, we conclude that:

\overline{a}^{R}=\overline{w_{0}}^{R}=\overline{w_{1}}^{R}=\ldots=\overline{w_{n-1}}^{R}=\overline{w_{n}}^{R}=\overline{b}^{R}

As both
a
and
b
are already irreducible, it follows that
a=b
.
∎

Corollary 4.10

Follows easily from Proposition

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces I(A_{J},\cdot_{J},R_{J})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces I(A_{W},\cdot_{W},R_{W})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}

With the 4 types of generalized elementary Tietze transformations defined, we can answer the question of how elements of the family
I^{-1}(M,\cdot)
relate to each other, for a given fixed monoid
(M,\cdot)
. We start by proving the following technical lemma:

Lemma 4.11
.

Let
(M,\cdot)
be an infinite monoid with
|M|=\lambda
. Then we can transform
(M,\cdot,\emptyset)
into
(F_{M^{+}},\oplus,R_{M})
by using at most
\lambda
generalized elementary Tietze transformations.

Proof.

For each element
m\in M^{+}
, let
\underline{m}
denote
m
when thought of as a letter in
F_{M^{+}}
, so that we may distinguish an element
m\in M^{+}
with its corresponding letter
\underline{m}\in F_{M^{+}}
. Now, for each
m\in M^{+}
, apply a type 3 GETT to
(M,\cdot,\emptyset)
by adding the rule
(\underline{m},m)
. After doing this for every element of
M^{+}
, we are left with

(M*F_{M^{+}},\cdot,R)

where
R=\{(\underline{m},m):m\in M^{+}\}
. Note that so far we’ve applied a type 3 GETT
\lambda
times.

Let
a,b,c\in M
such that
ab=c
, and note that
\underline{a}\oplus\underline{b}\to_{R}^{*}ab
. On the other hand,
\underline{c}\to_{R}c
, meaning that
\underline{a}\oplus\underline{b}\leftrightarrow_{R}^{*}\underline{c}
. With this in mind, for each pair
(\underline{a},\underline{b})\in F_{M^{+}}\times F_{M^{+}}
where
a,b
are elements of
M
, perform a type 1 GETT to add the rule
(\underline{a}\oplus\underline{b},\underline{ab})
. After doing this
\lambda\cdot\lambda=\lambda
times for each pair in
(a,b)\in M\times M
, we are left with

(M*F_{M^{+}},\cdot,R\cup R_{M})

Now, for each
m\in M^{+}
, note that using the rules in
R
we have that
\underline{m}\to_{R\cup R_{M}}m
, which means that
\underline{m}\leftrightarrow^{*}_{R\cup R_{M}}m
. As such, by definition, we can apply a GETT of type 1 and add the rule
(m,\underline{m})
. After doing this for every
m\in M^{+}
, i.e. doing this
\lambda
times, we are left with

(M*F_{M^{+}},\cdot,R\cup R_{M}\cup R^{\prime})

where
R^{\prime}=\{(m,\underline{m}):m\in M^{+}\}
.

Now let
m\in M^{+}
and note that
\underline{m}\leftrightarrow_{R_{M}\cup R^{\prime}}^{*}m
, meaning we can remove the rule
(\underline{m},m)\in R
via a type 2 GETT. By doing this for every
m\in M^{+}
, we end up with

(M*F_{M^{+}},\cdot,R_{M}\cup R^{\prime})

And again, note that by doing this for every element of
M^{+}
, we performed a type 2 GETT
\lambda
times. Finally, let
J=R^{\prime}
and applying a type 4 GETT with respect to
J
we get
(F_{M^{+}},\oplus,R_{M})

Note that, by the exact same argument as above, if we start with a finite monoid
(M,\cdot)
, we can get from
(M,\cdot,\emptyset)
to
(F_{M^{+}}*\{\varepsilon\},\oplus,R_{M})=(F_{M^{+}},\oplus,R_{M})
using only a finite number of GETTs.
∎

With this we can prove a generalized version of Tietze’s Theorem (see Section

):

Theorem 4.12
.

Let
(M,\cdot)
be a fixed monoid and let

and

be two infinite Noetherian confluent MRS such that

. Then

can be obtained from

by performing at most
\lambda
generalized elementary Tietze transformations.

The construction will follow the following outline, where in each step we apply at most
\lambda
type 1,2,3 or 4 GETTs:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(F_{B^{+}},\oplus,W\cup R_{M})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(F_{B^{+}},\oplus,L\cup R_{M})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(F_{B^{+}},\oplus,L\cup R_{B})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(A,\cdot,R)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(B,*,L)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(M,\cdot,\emptyset)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(3)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(4)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(5)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(1)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(2)}

(1)

Start by noting that
R\subseteq R
is trivially a coherent subset of
R
. As such, by applying a type 4 GETT to
(A,\cdot,R)
associated with the coherent subset

(M,\cdot,\emptyset)

(2)

Note that as

, meaning that
|M|\leq\lambda
.

Then, by Lemma

Following this, for each

. After doing this for every element of

(F_{M^{+}}*F_{(B\setminus M)^{+}},\oplus,W\cup R_{M})=(F_{B^{+}},\oplus,W\cup R_{M})

where

Note that we’ve only performed
|(B\setminus M)^{+}|\leq\lambda

(3)

Start by fixing some element

. As
a\to_{L}b
, we conclude that
\overline{a}^{L}=\overline{b}^{L}
, meaning that
a\leftrightarrow_{W\cup R_{M}}^{*}b
and as such, we perform a type 1 GETT on
(F_{B^{+}},\oplus,W\cup R_{M})
that adds the rule
(a,b)
. After doing this for every rule in
L

(F_{B^{+}},\oplus,L\cup W\cup R_{M})

Note that as

, then
|L|\leq\lambda\cdot\lambda=\lambda
, so we performed at most
\lambda
type 1 GETTs.

Now, for any
(a,\overline{a}^{L})\in W

, so we apply
|W|
many type 2 GETTs until we are left with

(F_{B^{+}},\oplus,L\cup R_{M})

Again, as

\lambda
type 2 GETTs.

(4)

Now let

. We wish to add the rule

via a type 1 GETT, and in order for us to do that it is enough to show that

Start by noting that
a\to_{L}^{*}\overline{a}^{L}
and

a\oplus b\to_{L}^{*}\overline{a}^{L}\oplus\overline{b}^{L}\to_{R_{M}}^{*}\overline{a*b}^{L}

On the other hand, trivially
a*b\to_{L}^{*}\overline{a*b}^{L}
from which it follows that
a\oplus b\leftrightarrow_{L\cup R_{M}}a*b
.

As such, apply
\lambda
many type 1 GETTs to add
(a\oplus b,a*b)
for each pair
a,b\in B
. After doing so, we are left with

(F_{B^{+}},\oplus,L\cup R_{M}\cup R_{B})

We now want to remove the rules in

via type 2 GETTs. Let
(m_{1}\oplus m_{2},m_{3})\in R_{M}
. By definition this means that
m_{1},m_{2},m_{3}\in M

m_{1}\cdot m_{2}=m_{3}

(M,\cdot)=I(B,*,L)

m_{1}\cdot m_{2}=\overline{m_{1}*m_{2}}^{L}=m_{3}

Now note that

m_{1}\oplus m_{2}\to_{R_{M}}m_{1}*m_{2}\to_{L}^{*}\overline{m_{1}*m_{2}}^{L}=m_{3}

(m_{1}\oplus m_{2},m_{3})
. After doing this
\lambda
times for each element of
R_{M}
, we are left with:

(F_{B^{+}},\oplus,L\cup R_{B})

(5)
Finally, let
J=R_{B}
and apply a type
4
GETT associated with the coherent subset

to get
(B,*,L)
.
∎

Note:
Using the exact same proof as presented above, if

to
(B,*,L)

Corollary 4.13

Let
(A,\cdot,R)
and
(B,*,L)
be two Noetherian confluent MRS. Then

Linear dependencies, polynomial factors in the Duke–Erdős forbidden sunflower problem
Andrey Kupavskii, Fedor Noskov

Abstract
We call a family of s s sets { F 1 , … , F s } \{F_{1},\ldots,F_{s}\} a sunflower with s s petals if, for any distinct i , j ∈ [ s ] i,j\in[s] , one has F i ∩ F j = ∩ u = 1 s F u F_{i}\cap F_{j}=\cap_{u=1}^{s}F_{u} . The set C = ∩ u = 1 s F u C=\cap_{u=1}^{s}F_{u} is called the core of the sunflower. It is a classical result of Erdős and Rado that there is a function ϕ ⁡ ( s , k ) \phi(s,k) such that any family of k k -element sets contains a sunflower with s s petals. In 1977, Duke and Erdős asked for the size of the largest family ℱ ⊂ ( [ n ] k ) \mathcal{F}\subset{[n]\choose k} that contains no sunflower with s s petals and core of size t − 1 t-1 . In 1987, Frankl and Füredi asymptotically solved this problem for k ⩾ 2 ​ t + 1 k\geqslant 2t+1 and n > n 0 ​ ( s , k ) n>n_{0}(s,k) . Their paper is one of the pinnacles of the so-called Delta-system method. In this paper, we extend the result of Frankl and Füredi to a much broader range of parameters: n > p ​ o ​ l ​ y ​ ( s , t ) ​ k n>poly(s,t)k , when additionally k > p ​ o ​ l ​ y ​ ( s , t ) ​ log ⁡ n k>poly(s,t)\log n or n > n 0 ​ ( s , t ) n>n_{0}(s,t) . We also extend this result to other domains, such as [ n ] k [n]^{k} and ( n k / w ) w {n\choose k/w}^{w} and obtain even stronger and more general results for forbidden sunflowers with core at most t − 1 t-1 (including results for families of permutations and subfamilies of the k k -th layer in a simplicial complex). The methods of the paper, among other things, combine the spread approximation approach, introduced by Zakharov and the first author, with the Delta-system approach of Frankl and Füredi and the hypercontractivity for global functions approach, developed by Keller, Lifshitz and coauthors. Previous works in extremal set theory relied on at most one of these approaches. Creating such a unified view was one of the goals for the paper.

The starting point for us is the result of Alweiss, Lovett, Wu and Zhang
[
Alw+21
]
. The core of their proof is the so-called spread lemma. Very roughly, the lemma states that a ‘‘pseudorandom’’ family

is likely to contain a matching of size

that with good probability respects a random partition, for an appropriate notion of pseudorandomness. The authors show that for any large enough family
\mathcal{F}
there is a set

, such that the restriction

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(A)=\{F\setminus A\,:\,F\in\mathcal{F}\text{ and }A\subset F\}

is ‘‘pseudorandom’’, and, therefore, contains a matching
F_{1},\ldots,F_{s}

is a sunflower in
\mathcal{F}
of size
s
. We discuss this argument and its applications in our setting in Section
4.1.1
. Based on the spread lemma and the notion of
\tau

that they introduced, Kupavskii and Zakharov
[
KZ24
]
developed the

technique and used it to obtain progress in several hypergraph Turán-type
problems. The technique was refined in the follow-up papers
[

,
Kup23a
,
Kup24
]
. That was the first method giving polynomial bounds in the Erdős–Sós problem. The method works when
n\geqslant\poly(t)k\log k
and

. In this paper, we add several new structural ingredients and merge their approach with the Delta-system technique and the method of Bradač et al
[
BBS23
]
to extend the spread approximation results beyond

, see Section

One fruitful point of view on the spread lemma is as on a

for the definition of sharp thresholds and its applications in our setting. This connection was used to settle Talagrand’s conjecture on fractional-expectation thresholds in random graphs

and applied to a number of hypergraph Turan-type problems. Finally, Keller et al.

using a variant of the notion of

]

At this point of development of these methods, it seems that none of the Delta-system, spread approximation and hypercontractivity approaches can be fully replaced by the others. One of the goals of this paper is to take the best of each of these methods and combine them to investigate Problem

\displaystyle n\geqslant 2^{11}sk\cdot\min\left\{\log_{2}k+2^{22}s^{9}t^{11},2^{379}s^{42}t^{82}+2^{23000}\right\},

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}|\leqslant\left(1+\sqrt[3]{\frac{2^{54}s^{15}t^{18}}{n/k}}\right)\phi(s,t)\binom{n-t}{k-t}.

Preliminaries I

4.1.1
Spread lemma

We start this section with the following definitions:

Definition 1
.

A probability measure
\mu
on
2^{[n]}
is said to be
R
-spread, if for any
X\in 2^{[n]}
one has

\displaystyle\mu\left(X\right)\leqslant R^{-|X|}.

Definition 2
.

A random set
\mathbf{W}
is called a
p
-random subset of
[n]
if elements
j\in[n]
belong to
\mathbf{W}
independently with probability
p
.

One of the main reasons why the definition of
R
-spreadness is so important for us is the following theorem called ‘‘the Spread lemma’’:

Theorem 5

(Spread lemma)
.

Say
\mathbf{W}
is
m\delta
-random subset of
[n]
if elements
i\in[n]
belong to
\mathbf{W}
independently with probability
m\delta
. If for some
n,R\geqslant 1
a measure
\mu:2^{[n]}\to[0,1]
is
R
-spread and
\mathbf{W}
is an
m\delta
-random subset of
[n]
, where
m
is integer, then

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[\exists F\in\support(\mu)\,:\,F\subset\mathbf{W}\right]\geqslant 1-\left(\frac{2}{\log_{2}(R\delta)}\right)^{m}|\mu|.

The spread lemma was initially established in the seminal paper by Alweis et al.
[
Alw+21
]
with non-optimal dependices. The proof relies on the coding techniques in the spirit of Razborov’s proof of the Håstad switching lemma (see Chapter 14.1 of
[
AB09
]
). It was refined by Rao in
[
Rao20
]
. Later, Tao
[
Tao
]
provided a new proof based on the entropy, but his derivations contain a mistake. Later, the proof was fixed and simplified by Hu
[
Hu21
]
. Stoeckl
[
Sto
]
established Theorem
5
with
1+h_{2}(\delta)\leqslant 2
instead of constant
2
, where
h_{2}(\delta)=-\delta\log_{2}\delta-(1-\delta)\log_{2}(1-\delta)
is the binary entropy of
\delta
.

Note that Theorem
5
can be applied to families of sets. We say that a family
\mathcal{F}
is
R
-spread
if the measure induced by uniform sampling from
\mathcal{F}
is
R
-spread. In other words,
\mathcal{F}
is
R
-spread if and only if for each
X\in 2^{[n]}
, one has

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}(X)|\leqslant R^{-|X|}|\mathcal{F}|.

Note that for a
k
-uniform family
\mathcal{F}
, one may obtain a restriction
X
such that
\mathcal{F}(X)
is
R
-spread (assuming that the family consisting only of the empty set is arbitrarily spread).

Observation 6

(Lemma 2 from
[
Tao
]
)
.

Let
X
be the largest set such that
|\mathcal{F}(X)|\geqslant R^{-|X|}|\mathcal{F}|
holds. Then, the family
\mathcal{F}(X)
is
R
-spread.

The observation is non-trivial, provided
|\mathcal{F}|>R^{k}
. In that case, we have
|\mathcal{F}(X)|>R^{-|X|}R^{k}=R^{k-|X|}
. In particular, this implies
|X|<k
.

For families of sets, Theorem
5
can be efficiently combined with the following lemma by Keevash et al.
[
Kee+21
]
. We say that a family
\mathcal{G}
is
upward closed
if for any
A\in\mathcal{G}
and
B,A\subset B,
one has
B\in\mathcal{G}
.

Lemma 7
.

Let
X_{1},\ldots,X_{i}
be
p_{i}
-random subsets of
[n]
. Let
\mathcal{G}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{G}_{s}
be upward closed subfamilies of
2^{[n]}
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(X_{i}\in\mathcal{G}_{i})\geqslant 3sp_{i}.

Then there exist disjoint sets
G_{1}\in\mathcal{G}_{1},\ldots,G_{s}\in\mathcal{G}_{s}
.

Together, Theorem
5
and Lemma
7
imply the following proposition which is the main tool in our analysis.

Proposition 8
.

Let
\mathcal{G}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{G}_{s}
be
R
-spread uniform families of uniformity at most
k
. Suppose that
R>2^{7}s\lceil\log_{2}k\rceil
. Then, there exist pairwise disjoint sets
F_{1}\in\mathcal{G}_{1},\ldots,F_{s}\in\mathcal{G}_{s}
.

Proof.

Put
p=1/8s
and consider a
p
-random set
\mathbf{W}
of
[n]
. Choose
m=\lceil\log_{2}k\rceil
. Due to Theorem
5
, for each
i=1,\ldots,s
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\exists F\in\mathcal{G}_{i}\text{ such that }F_{i}\subset\mathbf{W}\right)\geqslant 1-\left(\frac{2}{\log_{2}\left(\frac{pR}{m}\right)}\right)^{m}k>1-k2^{-m}>\frac{1}{2}.

We get

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\mathbf{W}\in\mathcal{G}_{i}^{\uparrow}\right)>\frac{1}{2}>3sp.

By Lemma
7
, there exist pairwise disjoint sets
G_{i}\in\mathcal{G}_{i}^{\uparrow}
, and so for some disjoint sets
F_{i}\subset G_{i}
,
i=1,\ldots,s
, we have
F_{1}\in\mathcal{G}_{1},\ldots,F_{s}\in\mathcal{G}_{s}
.
∎

Proposition
8
yields the following bound on
\phi(s,t)
:

Claim 9
.

We have
\phi(s,t)\leqslant(2^{7}s\log_{2}t)^{t}
.

Proof.

To obtain it, one should consider a family
|\mathcal{F}|>(2^{7}s\log_{2}t)^{t}
of uniformity
t
, choose
R=2^{6}s\lceil\log_{2}t\rceil
and find a maximal
X
such that
|\mathcal{F}(X)|\geqslant R^{-|X|}|\mathcal{F}|
. Note that
|X|<t
. Observation
6
guarantees that
\mathcal{F}(X)
is
R
-spread, and Proposition
8
implies that
\mathcal{F}(X)
contains a matching
F_{1},\ldots,F_{s}
of size
s
. Thus,
\mathcal{F}
contains a sunflower
X\cup F_{1},\ldots,X\cup F_{s}
of size
s
.
∎

Note that constant
2^{7}
in Claim
9
is not optimal. For example, one may obtain
\phi(s,t)\leqslant(2^{6}s\log_{2}t)^{t}
(see
[
Sto
]
). For a short self-contained proof of Claim
9
, we refer the reader to a recent paper of Rao
[
Rao23
]
.

In what follows, we will also use the following property of
R
-spread families.

Claim 10
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be an
R
-spread family. Consider an arbitrary
X
of size less than
R
. Then, the family
\mathcal{G}(\varnothing,X)
is
\hat{R}
-spread, where

\displaystyle\hat{R}=R-|X|

and has size at least
\left(1-\frac{|X|}{R}\right)|\mathcal{G}|
.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle|\mathcal{G}(\varnothing,X)|\geqslant|\mathcal{G}|-\sum_{x\in X}|\mathcal{G}(x)|\geqslant\left(1-\sum_{x\in X}R^{-1}\right)|\mathcal{G}|\geqslant\left(1-\frac{|X|}{R}\right)|\mathcal{G}|.

Let
A
be an arbitrary non-empty set disjoint from
X
. Then, we have

\displaystyle|\mathcal{G}(\varnothing,X)(A)|\leqslant|\mathcal{G}(A)|\leqslant R^{-|A|}|\mathcal{G}|\leqslant R^{-|A|}\left(1-\frac{|X|}{R}\right)|\mathcal{G}(\varnothing,X)|\leqslant\hat{R}^{-|A|}|\mathcal{G}(\varnothing,X)|,

so
\mathcal{G}(\varnothing,X)
is indeed
\hat{R}
-spread.
∎

Claim
10
yields the following simple but important corollary.

Claim 11
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be an arbitrary
R
-spread family. Then the covering number of
\mathcal{G}
is at least
R
.

Proof.

Let
X=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{\ell}\}
be a transversal of
\mathcal{G}
. If
\ell<R
, then, by Claim
10
, we have

\displaystyle|\mathcal{G}(\varnothing,X)|\geqslant\left(1-\frac{\ell}{R}\right)|\mathcal{G}|>0,

contradicting the definition of a transversal.
∎

4.1.2
Spread domains

An important topic in Extremal Set Theory is to study families
\mathcal{F}
, satisfying some restrictions in domains
\mathcal{A}
different from
\binom{[n]}{k}
. There is a wide range of such works, for example, generalizations of Erdős–Ko–Rado theorem for vector fields
[
FW86
]
, integer sequences
[
FT99
]
, layers of simplicial complexes
[
Bor09
]
and permutations
[
FD77
]
, generalizations of Kruskal–Katona Theorem for
[n]^{k}

[
FFK88
]
, generalizations of Erdős-Sós problem for codes
[
Kee+23
]
and linear maps
[
EKL23
]
.

Zakharov and the first author
[
KZ24
]
developed the machinery of spread approximations that can be used to deduce extremal set theory results for subfamilies of domains different from
\binom{[n]}{k}
. To discuss these results, we shall need the following definition.

Definition 3
.

We say that a family
\mathcal{A}
is
(r,t)
-spread, if for any
T\in\partial_{\leqslant t}\mathcal{A}
, the family
\mathcal{A}(T)
is
r
-spread.

Here, we illustrate the definition of
(r,t)
-spreadness with several examples, including
\binom{[n]}{k}
.

Proposition 12
.

Fix integers
n,k,t
such that
k\geqslant t
and
n\geqslant k
. Then the following holds:

1.

The family
\binom{[n]}{k}
is
\left(\frac{n}{k},t\right)
-spread;

2.

The family
[n]^{k}
is
(n,t)
-spread;

3.

Fix
w\leqslant k
and
k_{1},\ldots,k_{w}
such that
\sum_{i}k_{i}=k
. The family
\binom{[n]}{k_{1}}\times\ldots\times\binom{[n]}{k_{w}}
is
(r,t)
-spread for
r=\min_{i}\frac{n}{k_{i}}
.

Proof.

It is enough to prove
3
, since
1
and
2
follow from it. Set

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}=\binom{I_{1}}{k_{1}}\times\ldots\times\binom{I_{w}}{k_{w}},

where
|I_{i}|=n
.
Fix some set
T
of size at most
t
, and let
T_{i}=T\cap I_{i}
. Since
T\in\partial_{t}\mathcal{A}
, we have
|T_{i}|\leqslant k_{i}
. We aim to show that the family

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(T)=\prod_{i=1}^{w}\binom{I_{i}\setminus T_{i}}{k-|T_{i}|}

is
r
-spread. Consider an arbitrary
S
disjoint from
T
, and let
S_{i}=S\cap(I_{i}\setminus T_{i})
. We may assume that
|S_{i}|\leqslant k_{i}-|T_{i}|
, since otherwise
|\mathcal{A}(T)(S)|=0
and the spreadness definition is trivially satisfied. We have

\displaystyle|\mathcal{A}(T)(S)|=\prod_{i=1}^{w}\left|\binom{I_{i}\setminus T_{i}}{k-|T_{i}|}(S_{i})\right|=\prod_{i=1}^{w}\left|\binom{I_{i}\setminus(T_{i}\cup S_{i})}{k-|T_{i}|-|S_{i}|}\right|.

Then, we have

|\mathcal{A}(T)(S)|\leqslant\prod_{i}\left(\frac{n-|T_{i}|}{k_{i}-|T_{i}|}\right)^{-|S_{i}|}\binom{|I_{i}\setminus T_{i}|}{k_{i}-|T_{i}|}\leqslant\left(\min_{i}\frac{n}{k_{i}}\right)^{-|S|}|\mathcal{A}(T)|.\qed

Next, we give some more sophisticated examples of families that are
(r,t)
-spread.

Consider the family
\Sigma_{n}
of permutations on
[n]
. One can consider
\Sigma_{n}
as a subfamily of
\binom{[n]^{2}}{n}
. For each
\sigma\in\Sigma_{n}
, correspond the following set
F_{\sigma}\in\binom{[n]^{2}}{n}
:

\displaystyle F_{\sigma}=\left\{(i,j)\,:\,\sigma(i)=j\right\}.

We denote by
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
the family of sets
\{F_{\sigma}\,:\,\sigma\in\Sigma\}
. Clearly, the family
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
is
n
-uniform. The following proposition ensures that it is
(r,t)
-spread for a suitable
r
.

Proposition 13

(Section 3.4 from
[
KZ24
]
)
.

Suppose that
n>4t
. Then, the family
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
is
(n/4,t)
-spread.

The restriction that a family
\mathcal{F}\subset\Sigma_{n}
obeys are translated into the restrictions on the corresponding family
\mathfrak{F}=\{F_{\sigma}\,:\,\sigma\in\mathcal{F}\}
. For example, if for any
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\in\mathcal{F}
there exists
x
such that
\sigma_{1}(x)=\sigma_{2}(x)
, then the family
\mathfrak{F}
is intersecting, i.e. for any
F_{1},F_{2}\in\mathfrak{F}
we have
|F_{1}\cap F_{2}|\geqslant 1
. To formulate the counterpart of Theorem
3
for permutations, we need to elaborate the corresponding sunflower restriction for them.

Definition 4
.

Consider
s
permutations
\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{s}
. If

1.

there exists a set
X=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}\}
such that
\sigma_{1}(x_{i})=\ldots=\sigma_{s}(x_{i})
for each
i=1,\ldots,m
,

2.

for any
x\in[n]\setminus X
, all values
\sigma_{1}(x),\ldots,\sigma_{s}(x)
are distinct,

then we say that
\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{s}
form a sunflower with
s
petals and the core of size
m
.

Note that
\mathcal{F}\subset\Sigma_{n}
does not contain a sunflower with
s
petals and the core of size
t-1
if and only if the corresponding family of sets
\mathfrak{F}\subset\mathfrak{S}_{n}
does not contain a sunflower of
s
petals and the core of size
t-1
. Using this correspondence, we can establish the counterpart of Theorem
3
for permutations.

Another non-obvious example of an
(r,t)
-spread family is the
k
-th layer of a simplicial complex of large rank. The
rank
of a simplicial complex is the size of its smallest inclusion-maximal set.
Consider a simplicial complex
\mathcal{C}
of rank
n
. Let
n\geqslant k\geqslant t
. Consider the
k
-th layer
\mathcal{C}^{(k)}
of
\mathcal{C}
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{(k)}=\{F\in\mathcal{C}\,:\,|F|=k\}.

The following lemma states that the family
\mathcal{C}^{(k)}
is
(r,t)
-spread for a suitable
r
.

Lemma 14

(Lemma 4 from
[
Kup23a
]
)
.

The family
\mathcal{C}^{(k)}
is
(n/k,t)
-spread.

Thus, there is a wide range of
(r,t)
-spread families
\mathcal{A}
. In Section
4.2
, we show that for each such
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{F}\subset\mathcal{A}
some counterpart of Theorem
3
holds.

4.2
Theorem
3
for spread domains

In order to formulate a generalization of Theorem
3
for
\mathcal{F}
being a subfamily of
(r,t)
-spread domain (or ambient family)
\mathcal{A}
, we require some natural equivalents of the quantities used in the bound on
|\mathcal{F}|
. One of such quantities replaces
\binom{n-t}{k-t}
. We denote by
A_{t}
the following quantity:

\displaystyle A_{t}=\max_{T\in\partial_{t}\mathcal{A}}|\mathcal{A}(T)|.

Using this notation, we present the main result of this section.

Theorem 15
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a
k
-uniform and
(r,t)
-spread ambient family. Assume that
r\geqslant 4s^{2}(t+1)^{3}\log_{2}^{3}r
and
r\geqslant 2^{10}s\log_{2}k
. Suppose that a family
\mathcal{F}\subset\mathcal{A}
does not contain a sunflower with
s
petals and the core of size at most
t-1
. Then, there exists a family
\mathcal{T}\subset\partial_{t}\mathcal{A}
that does not contain a sunflower with
s
petals such that

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}\setminus\mathcal{F}[\mathcal{T}]|\leqslant\phi(s,t)\cdot\frac{2^{3}s^{2}(t+1)^{3}\log_{2}^{3}r}{r}\cdot A_{t}.

We prove Theorem
15
in Section
4.3
.

Obviously, the theorem yields the following upper bound on
\mathcal{F}
:

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}|\leqslant\max_{\mathcal{T}}|\mathcal{A}[\mathcal{T}]|+\phi(s,t)\cdot\frac{2^{3}s^{2}(t+1)^{3}\log_{2}^{3}r}{r}\cdot A_{t},

(3)

where the maximum is taken over all families
\mathcal{T}\subset\partial_{t}\mathcal{A}
that do not contain a sunflower with
s
petals. If
\mathcal{A}=\binom{[n]}{k}
, then we have
r=n/k
due to Proposition
12
, and the following bound holds:

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}|\leqslant\left(1+\frac{2^{3}s^{2}(t+1)^{3}\log_{2}^{3}(n/k)}{n/k}\right)\phi(s,t)\cdot\binom{n-t}{k-t}.

Thus, Theorem
3
follows.

We claim that the bound
(
3
)
is tight up to remainder terms of order
\log_{2}^{3}r/r
. The corresponding example below generalizes that of
[
FF87
]
. Consider a family
\mathcal{T}^{*}\subset\partial_{t}\mathcal{A}
that maximizes
|\mathcal{A}[\mathcal{T}]|
and does not contain a sunflower with
s
petals. Denote

\displaystyle\support\mathcal{T}^{*}=\bigcup_{T\in\mathcal{T}^{*}}T.

Then, define

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{*}=\bigcup_{T\in\mathcal{T}^{*}}\mathcal{A}(T,\support\mathcal{T}^{*})\vee\{T\}.

We claim that
\mathcal{F}^{*}
does not contain a sunflower with
s
petals and the core of size at most
t-1
. Indeed, if such a sunflower
F_{1},\ldots,F_{s}
exists, then
F_{1}\cap\support\mathcal{T}^{*},\ldots,F_{s}\cap\support\mathcal{T}^{*}
form a sunflower with
s
petals and the core of size at most
t-1
. By construction,
F_{1}\cap\support\mathcal{T}^{*},\ldots,F_{s}\cap\support\mathcal{T}^{*}
belongs to
\mathcal{T}^{*}
, a contradiction.

Next, we have

\displaystyle|\mathcal{A}[\mathcal{T}^{*}]|-|\mathcal{F}^{*}|\leqslant\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}^{*}}|\mathcal{A}(T)|-|\mathcal{A}(T,\support\mathcal{T}^{*})|\leqslant\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}^{*}}\sum_{x\in(\support\mathcal{T}^{*})\setminus T}|\mathcal{A}(T\sqcup\{x\})|.

Since
\mathcal{A}
is
(r,t)
-spread, we can bound
|\mathcal{A}(T\sqcup\{x\})|\leqslant r^{-1}|\mathcal{A}(T)|
. Therefore, we have

\displaystyle|\mathcal{A}[\mathcal{T}^{*}]|-|\mathcal{F}^{*}|\leqslant\frac{|\mathcal{T}^{*}||\support\mathcal{T}^{*}|}{r}\cdot A_{t}\leqslant\frac{t\phi^{2}(s,t)}{r}A_{t},

so
|\mathcal{F}^{*}|\geqslant\max_{\mathcal{T}}|\mathcal{A}[\mathcal{T}]|-\frac{t\phi^{2}(s,t)}{r}A_{t}
, which matches bound (
3
) up to second-order terms.

Next, we present a corollary of Theorem
15
for subfamilies of a simplicial complex. Using Lemma
14
, we obtain the following.

Corollary 16
.

Consider a simplicial complex
\mathcal{C}
with the rank at least
n
. Fix numbers
s,k,t
such that
k\geqslant t
,
n/k\geqslant 4s^{2}(t+1)^{3}\log_{2}^{3}(n/k)
and
n\geqslant 2^{10}sk\log_{2}k
. Suppose that a family
\mathcal{F}\subset\mathcal{C}^{(k)}
does not contain a sunflower with
s
petals and the core of size at most
t-1
. Then, there exists a family
\mathcal{T}\subset\mathcal{C}^{(t)}
that does not contain a sunflower with
s
petals such that

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}\setminus\mathcal{F}[\mathcal{T}]|\leqslant\phi(s,t)\cdot\frac{2^{3}s^{2}(t+1)^{3}\log_{2}^{3}(n/k)}{n/k}\cdot\max_{T\in\mathcal{C}^{(t)}}|\mathcal{C}(T)|.

To state the corollary of Theorem
15
for permutations, we need one more definition. We say that a function
\nu
is a
partial permutation
of size
t
, if
\nu
is an injection from some set
T\subset[n]
of size
t
to
[n]
. We define the family of partial permutations of size
t
by
\Sigma^{(t)}_{n}
. Definition
4
of a sunflower generalizes trivially for partial permutations. Since any such injection can be extended to bijection from
[n]
to
[n]
, there is one-to-one correspondence between elements of
\Sigma^{(t)}_{n}
and the shadow
\partial_{t}\mathfrak{S}_{n}
. Using it and Proposition
13
, we deduce the following corollary (note that in our case the uniformity
n
and the spreadness
n/4
are the same up to multiplicative constants, so we have only one inequality on
n
instead of two inequalities on
r
in Theorem
15
).

Corollary 17
.

Fix numbers
n,s,t
such that
n\geqslant 2^{20}s(t+1)\log_{2}n
. Suppose that a family
\mathcal{F}\subset\Sigma_{n}
of permutations does not contain a sunflower with
s
petals and the core of size at most
t-1
. Then, there exists a family
\mathcal{T}\subset\Sigma^{(t)}_{n}
of partial permutations that does contain a sunflower with
s
petals and the core of size at most
t-1
, such that

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}\setminus\mathcal{F}[\mathcal{T}]|\leqslant\phi(s,t)\cdot\frac{2^{5}s^{2}(t+1)^{3}\log_{2}^{3}(n/4)}{n}\cdot(n-t)!

Here
\mathcal{F}[\mathcal{T}]
is a family of all permutations
\sigma
in
\mathcal{F}
such that there exists a partial permutation
\nu\in\mathcal{T}
which coincides with
\sigma
on the support of
\nu
.

4.3
Proof of Theorem
15

Set
w=(t+1)\log_{2}r
. Consider two cases.

Case 1. We have
k\leqslant w
. In this case, we apply the following lemma with
\mathcal{F}
in place of
\mathcal{S}
with
q=k
.

Lemma 18
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a
k
-uniform
(r,t)
-spread family. Fix some
q\leqslant k
and
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
. Assume that
\varepsilon r{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\geqslant}s^{2}q^{3}
. Suppose that a family
\mathcal{S}\subset\partial_{\leqslant q}\mathcal{A}

•

does not contain an
s
-sunflower with the core of size
\leqslant t-1
;

•

consists of sets of cardinality at least
t
.

Then, there exists a family
\mathcal{T}\subset\partial_{t}\mathcal{A}
which does not contain any sunflower with
s
petals and

\displaystyle|\mathcal{A}[\mathcal{S}]\setminus\mathcal{A}[\mathcal{T}]|\leqslant\left|\mathcal{A}\left[\mathcal{S}\setminus\mathcal{S}[\mathcal{T}]\right]\right|\leqslant\phi(s,t)\cdot\frac{2\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon}A_{t}.

We prove Lemma
18
in Section
4.4
. We apply it with
\varepsilon=\frac{s^{2}q^{3}}{r}\leqslant\frac{s^{2}(t+1)^{3}\log_{2}^{3}r}{r}
. Note that the condition on
r
from the theorem implies that
\varepsilon<1.

The lemma gives suboptimal bounds if
k\geqslant(t+1)\log_{2}r
. Indeed, if we apply it with
k
in place of
q
, it gives the result for
r>s^{2}k^{3}
, while we can establish the theorem provided
r\geqslant 2s^{2}(t+1)^{3}\log^{3}r
and
r\geqslant 2^{10}s\log_{2}k
.

Case 2. We have
k>(t+1)\log_{2}r
.
In this case, we use the following iterative process to obtain some structure within the family
\mathcal{F}
, cf.
[
Kup23
,
Kup23a
]
.

Set
\mathcal{F}_{0}=\mathcal{F}
. Given a family
\mathcal{F}_{i}
, we obtain a family
\mathcal{F}_{i+1}
by the following procedure.

Set
R=r/2
. Let
S_{i}
be the maximal set such that
|\mathcal{F}_{i}(S_{i})|\geqslant R^{-|S_{i}|}|\mathcal{F}_{i}|
. Note that
\mathcal{F}_{i}(S_{i})
is
R
-spread due to Observation
6
. If
|S_{i}|>w
, then stop. Otherwise, set
\mathcal{F}_{i+1}=\mathcal{F}_{i}\setminus\mathcal{F}_{i}[S_{i}].

Suppose that the procedure above stops at step

While the main theorem of our paper deals with subfamilies of

Definition 5

\displaystyle\frac{|\mathcal{F}(X)|}{|\mathcal{A}(X)|}\leqslant\tau^{|X|}\frac{|\mathcal{F}|}{|\mathcal{A}|}.

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}(X)|\leqslant\tau^{|X|}|\mathcal{F}|\frac{|\mathcal{A}(X)|}{|\mathcal{A}|}\leqslant(r/\tau)^{-|X|}|\mathcal{F}|.

Assumption 1

The necessary

Solution of hypergraph Turan-type problems in

5.5
Boolean analysis

When
k
is extremely large (namely,
k\geqslant 2^{(st)^{C}}
for some large constant
C
), techniques based on
\tau
-homogeneous approximation give suboptimal results. The reason is that the spread lemma works for the families without any assumption on their measure. In general, the factor
\log k
in applications of Theorem
5
is unavoidable, as was shown in
[
Alw+21
]
, but in our special case it can be removed.

The idea is to use recent results in Boolean Analysis from papers
[
Kee+21
]
and
[
KLM23
]
. Before we start, we give a number of definitions.

We consider the Boolean cube
\{0,1\}^{n}
with the
p
-biased measure
\mu_{p}
,
\mu_{p}(x)=p^{\sum_{i}x_{i}}(1-p)^{n-\sum_{i}x_{i}},x\in\{0,1\}^{n}
. Next, for the linear space of functions
f:\{0,1\}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
, we define the scalar product

\displaystyle\langle f,g\rangle=\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mu_{p}}f(x)g(x).

We denote the obtained Hilbert space by
L_{2}(\{0,1\}^{n},\mu_{p})
. We define the
q
-norm of the function
f
as

\displaystyle\|f\|_{q}=\mathbb{E}^{1/q}_{x\sim\mu_{p}}|f(x)|^{q}.

We denote expectation
\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mu_{p}}f(x)
of
f
by
\mu_{p}(f)
. There is a natural correspondence between the Boolean functions
f:\{0,1\}^{n}\to\{0,1\}
and families of subsets
\mathcal{F}
of the Boolean cube, defined as follows:
f(x)=1
if and only if for the corresponding family
\mathcal{F}
holds that
\support(x)\in\mathcal{F}
. Then, the measure of
\mu_{p}(\mathcal{F})
is just the expectation
\mu_{p}(f)
of the corresponding Boolean function and can be obtained as follows:

\displaystyle\mu_{p}(\mathcal{F})=\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}}p^{|F|}(1-p)^{n-|F|}.

One can interpret
\mu_{p}(\mathcal{F})
as the probability that
\mathcal{F}
contains a
p
-random subset
\mathbf{X}
of
[n]
.

When dealing with the uniform families
\mathcal{F}\subset\binom{[n]}{k}
, one can use techniques from Boolean Analysis, by applying them to the upper-closure
\mathcal{F}^{\uparrow}
of the family
\mathcal{F}
.
There is a simple inequality, which relates the
p
-biased measure of
\mathcal{F}^{\uparrow}
and the size of
\mathcal{F}
:

Claim 34

(Lemma IV.2.2.
[
Kee+21
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a subfamily of
\binom{[n]}{k}
and set
p=\frac{k}{n}
, then

\mu_{p}(\mathcal{F}^{\uparrow})\geqslant\frac{|\mathcal{F}|}{4\binom{n}{k}}.

When dealing with the restrictions
\mathcal{F}(A,B)
of a family
\mathcal{F}
, one should be careful with the
p
-biased measure of
\mathcal{F}(A,B)
. Indeed, for a set
B
, one can treat
\mathcal{F}(\varnothing,B)
either as a subfamily of the Boolean cube
2^{[n]}
or as a subfamily of the Boolean cube
2^{[n]\setminus B}
. Both cases arise in our proofs, so we define
\mu_{p}^{-B}
for the
p
-biased measure on
2^{[n]\setminus B}
and
\mu_{p}^{[n]}
for
2^{[n]}
. We may omit the superscript if the measure and the domain are clear from the context. For restrictions of the form
\mathcal{F}(B)
, we assume that the measure
\mu_{p}^{-B}(\mathcal{F}(B))
is considered by default.

The next definition, which is a variant of
\tau
-homogeneity, is the core notion of this section.

Definition 7
.

A family
\mathcal{F}\subset 2^{[n]}
is
\tau
-global with respect to the measure
\mu_{p}^{[n]}
, if for any set
B
and a subset
A\subset B
, the following holds:

\displaystyle\mu_{p}^{-B}(\mathcal{F}(A,B))\leqslant\tau^{|B|}\mu_{p}^{[n]}(\mathcal{F}).

In terms of the Boolean functions
f:\{0,1\}^{n}\to\{0,1\}
,
\tau
-globalness is defined as follows:

\displaystyle\mu_{p}^{-S}(f_{S\to x})\leqslant\tau^{|S|}\mu_{p}(f)

for any
S\subset[n]
and any
x\in\{0,1\}^{S}
, where the function

is the restriction of

on

for any

. In the set families notation,

vs

corresponds to

vs
\mathcal{F},
where

Importantly, given a family

, it is relatively simple to obtain a restriction

,

, such that

is

-global.

Observation 35

(Section 2.3
[
Kel+24
]
)
.

For a family
\mathcal{F}\subset 2^{[n]}
, consider a pair of sets
A,B
,
A\subset B
, that maximizes the quantity
\tau^{-|B|}\mu_{p}^{-B}(\mathcal{F}(A,B))
. Then,
\mathcal{F}(A,B)
is
\tau
-global with respect to
\mu_{p}
. Additionally, if

, then the maximum is attained when

.

Before we will state the main theorem of this section, we need several other definitions. For a vector
x\in\{0,1\}^{n}
, we say a random vector

is
\rho
-correlated with

, if it can be obtained as follows: independently for all

, with probability
\rho
set

and with probability
1-\rho
sample
y_{i}
from the measure
\mu_{p}
on
\{0,1\}
. We denote the resulting distribution as

.

Next, we define the following linear operator on
L_{2}(\{0,1\}^{n},\mu_{p})
. For a function
f\in L_{2}(\{0,1\}^{n},\mu_{p})
, set
(T_{\rho}f)(x)=\mathbb{E}_{y\sim N_{\rho}(x)}f(y)
. The operator
T_{\rho}
is called the
noise operator
.

In general, for small enough
\rho
, the operator
T_{\rho}
admits the following property:
\|T_{\rho}f\|_{q}\leqslant\|f\|_{2}
for

. Such property is referred to as the

. For an extensive introduction into Boolean Analysis and hypercontractivity on the Boolean cube, we refer the reader to the seminal book by O’Donnel
[
ODo14
]
. For general hypercontractivity and its connections to Markov chains and functional analysis, we recommend the book
[
BGL14
]
by Bakry, Gentil and Ledoux.

For a function
f
, one may also consider the following quantity, which is called the
stability
of
f
:

\displaystyle\operatorname{Stab}_{\rho}(f)=\langle T_{\rho}f,f\rangle.

In general, if the

-th norm of

is efficiently bounded, one can control
\operatorname{Stab}_{\rho}(f)
via the Hölder inequality, see the proof of Lemma
38
.

The main result of the paper
[

]
is that the hypercontractivity phenomenon happens for

-global functions

Theorem 36

(Theorem 1.4
[
KLM23
]
)

Let

be a
\tau
-global Boolean function, i.e.

The theorem holds for any function

[

,

]
. Intriguingly, in some cases, techniques based on the spread approximation and techniques based on the hypercontractivity can be used to obtain almost the same result, see papers
[

,

in our special case, we notice the following. We use the spread Lemma

measure, and, therefore, they contain a matching. In Boolean Analysis, the phenomenon when the

5.6
Sharp thresholds and matchings

We start by the following lemma, which will be a building block for the
\mu_{p}
-counterpart of Theorem

Lemma 38
.

Suppose that an upper-closed family
\mathcal{F}
is
\tau
-global. For a number
p\in(0;2^{-7}/\tau)
, define
\widetilde{p}=2^{6}\tau p
. Then, the following holds:

\displaystyle\mu_{\widetilde{p}}(\mathcal{F})\geqslant\mu_{p}^{3/4}(\mathcal{F}).

Proof.

We consider
\mathcal{F}
as a monotone Boolean function. Set
\rho=\frac{p(1-\widetilde{p})}{\widetilde{p}(1-p)}
. Since
p\leqslant\widetilde{p}
, we have
1-\widetilde{p}\leqslant 1-p
, and
\rho\leqslant\frac{p}{\widetilde{p}}=\frac{1}{2^{6}\tau}
. Hence, we may apply Theorem
36
with
q=4
and obtain
\|T_{\rho}f\|_{4}\leqslant\|f\|_{2}
. An application of Hölder’s inequality implies

\displaystyle\operatorname{Stab}_{\rho}(f)=\langle T_{\rho}f,f\rangle\leqslant\|T_{\rho}f\|_{4}\|f\|_{4/3}\leqslant\|f\|_{2}\|f\|_{4/3}.

Since
f
is Boolean,
\|f\|_{2}=\mu_{p}^{1/2}(f)
and
\|f\|_{4/3}=\mu_{p}^{3/4}(f)
. Therefore,
\operatorname{Stab}_{\rho}(f)\leqslant\mu_{p}^{5/4}(f)
. By our choice of
\rho
, we can apply Proposition
37
and obtain

\displaystyle\mu_{\widetilde{p}}(f)\geqslant\frac{\mu^{2}_{p}(f)}{\operatorname{Stab}_{\rho}(f)}\geqslant\frac{\mu^{2}_{p}(f)}{\mu^{5/4}_{p}(f)}=\mu^{3/4}_{p}(f).

The main idea is to iterate Lemma
38
. Applying Lemma
38
several times to a family
\mathcal{F}
, one may obtain that
\mu_{\widetilde{p}}(\mathcal{F})
is large for
\widetilde{p}
much greater than

Corollary 39
.

Fix integers
m,z
and
\tau\geqslant 2
. Let
\mathcal{F}
be an upward closed family such that
\mu_{p}(\mathcal{F})\geqslant\tau^{-z}
and
p\in(0;(2^{6}\tau)^{-m}/2)
. Then, there exists a set
R
of size at most
4z
such that, for
\widetilde{p}=(2^{6}\tau)^{m}p
, the following holds

\displaystyle\mu_{\widetilde{p}}^{-R}(\mathcal{F}(R))\geqslant\left(\mu_{p}(\mathcal{F})\right)^{(\frac{3}{4})^{m}},

and
\mathcal{F}(R)
is
\tau
-global w.r.t the measure
\mu_{\widetilde{p}}^{-R}
.

Proof.

We obtain the result by an iterative application of Lemma
38
. Set
\mathcal{F}_{0}=\mathcal{F},p_{0}=p
. Note that
\mu_{p_{0}}(\mathcal{F}_{0})\geqslant\tau^{-z}
. At step

that maximizes the quantity
\tau^{-|S|}\mu_{p_{i}}(\mathcal{F}_{i}(R_{i}))
and set
\mathcal{F}_{i+1}=\mathcal{F}_{i}(R_{i})
. We prove by induction that
\mu_{p_{i}}(\mathcal{F}_{i})\geqslant\mu_{p}(\mathcal{F})^{\left(\frac{3}{4}\right)^{i}}\geqslant\tau^{-z\left(\frac{3}{4}\right)^{i}}
. Clearly, the base

Due to Observation

\tau

By the inductive assumption and the choice of

\displaystyle\tau^{-z\left(\frac{3}{4}\right)^{i}}\leqslant\mu_{p_{i}}(\mathcal{F}_{i})\leqslant\mu_{p_{i}}(\mathcal{F}_{i}(R_{i}))\tau^{-|R_{i}|}\leqslant\tau^{-|R_{i}|},

and, therefore, the set

. Define

and

\displaystyle\mu_{p_{i+1}}(\mathcal{F}_{i+1})\geqslant\mu_{p_{i}}^{3/4}(\mathcal{F}_{i})\geqslant(\mu_{p}(\mathcal{F}_{0}))^{(3/4)^{i+1}},

and the induction step is proved.

Stop the procedure after

\displaystyle|R|=\sum_{i=0}^{m}|R_{i}|\leqslant z\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{3}{4}\right)^{i}=4z.

We have
\mathcal{F}_{m}=\mathcal{F}(R)

Conclusion

In the present paper, we provide new bounds for the problem of Erdős–Duke on forbidden sunflowers with prescribed core size. In particular, we extend the result of Frankl and Füredi
[
FF87
]
to the case when
n
is allowed to depend linearly on
k
with the factor polynomial in
s,t
. To obtain this result, we combine the hypercontractivity technique of Keller, Lifshizt, Keevash et al.
[
Kee+21
,
KLM23
]
, the spread approximation technique of Kupavskii and Zakharov
[
KZ24
]
, the result on forbidden sunflowers of Bradač et al.
[
BBS23
]
and some ideas from the original proof of Frankl and Füredi
[
FF87
]
. As a byproduct, we answer the question of Kalai
[
Pol
]
and Bradač et al.
[
BBS23
]
about the maximal size of families without sunflowers with the core of size at most
t-1
in the regime when
s\leqslant(t\ln(n/k))^{-3}\sqrt{n/k}
and
n\geqslant 2^{11}sk\log_{2}k
.

It turns out that our methods are quite general and can be applied to obtain similar results for subfamilies of permutations and
\binom{[n]}{k/w}^{w}
for certain ranges of values
w
.

When looking through the proof of Theorem
4
, one can ask the following question: could we employ modern techniques to improve dependencies in the Delta-system method? In our problem,
k\geqslant 5t
is enough to significantly decrease the lower bound on
n
. Could we go further and avoid double-exponential dependence on
t
? Could we improve similar lower bounds for other Turán-type problems?

For abstract hypergraph Turán-type problems, it seems that modern techniques can do this when
k
is at least constant times the uniformity of a forbidden hypergraph. Moreover, Alweiss claimed that his result on set system blowups
[
Alw20
]
can significantly improve the key lemma
[
Für83
]
of the Delta-system method. However, his claim has not been verified. Meanwhile, we have other approaches like spread approximations
[
KZ24
]
, the method of Lifshitz and coauthors based on Boolean analysis
[
Kee+21
,
KLM23
]
, including the junta method
[
KL21
]
, and encoding techniques used in papers of Bradač et al.
[
BBS23
]
, Alweiss
[
Alw+21
]
and Rao
[
Rao20
]
, that seem to be general enough to replace the Delta-system method. We hope that a framework alternative to the latter and without double-exponential dependencies will be designed in the near future.

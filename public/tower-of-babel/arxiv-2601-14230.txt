Mascot : Towards Multi-Agent Socio-Collaborative Companion Systems
Yiyang Wang, Yiqiao Jin, Alex Cabral, Josiah Hester

Abstract
Multi-agent systems (MAS) have recently emerged as promising socio-collaborative companions for emotional and cognitive support. However, these systems frequently suffer from persona collapse –where agents revert to generic, homogenized assistant behaviors–and social sycophancy , which produces redundant, non-constructive dialogue. We propose Mascot , a generalizable framework for multi-perspective socio-collaborative companions. Mascot introduces a novel bi-level optimization strategy to harmonize individual and collective behaviors: 1) Persona-Aware Behavioral Alignment, an RLAIF-driven pipeline that finetunes individual agents for strict persona fidelity to prevent identity loss; and 2) Collaborative Dialogue Optimization, a meta-policy guided by group-level rewards to ensure diverse and productive discourse. Extensive evaluations across psychological support and workplace domains demonstrate that Mascot significantly outperforms state-of-the-art baselines, achieving improvements of up to +14.1 in Persona Consistency and +10.6 in Social Contribution. Our framework provides a practical roadmap for engineering the next generation of socially intelligent multi-agent systems.

Introduction

Moving beyond dyadic interactions to multi-agent socio-collaborative companion systems. While single-agent support systems (left) can result in limited insights and echo chambers, multi-agent systems (right) provide diverse, balanced perspectives and foster a shared sense of community through agents with distinct roles.

Social interaction is a fundamental pillar of psychological well-being, cognitive resilience, and enhanced problem solving
CDC 2024b
;
Sahakian and Langley 2025
.
Despite its necessity, social deficits such as isolation and loneliness have reached global crisis proportions, affecting an estimated one in six people worldwide and one in four in the United States

World Health Organization 2025
;
CDC 2024a
.
As large language models (LLMs) integrate deeper into human lives, their role is shifting from passive
tools
(e.g., search engines, code generators) to active
socio-collaborative companions
in affective and collaborative settings
Liu et al. 2021
.
In affective contexts, such as empathetic dialogue, socio-collaborative companions serve as psychological anchors, offering emotional support to reduce distress, loneliness, and anxiety.
They provide a safe, always-available, and non-judgmental space for users to vent and process complex emotions. In collaborative or workplace contexts, companions act as
cognitive scaffolds
that facilitate information synthesis and collective decision-making to stimulate problem-solving. Ultimately, performing tasks that mitigate social deficits allows these models to evolve into adaptive partners capable of navigating the nuanced boundary between emotional resonance and task-oriented efficiency.

While existing LLM systems primarily focus on dyadic (one-to-one) interactions, a single persona often struggles to balance the conflicting roles of mentor, friend, and assistant without suffering a cognitive and emotional mismatch.
Multi-agent frameworks resolve this by distributing these roles across an ecosystem of specialized agents. This triggers the ‘audience effect’
Sutskova et al. 2023
, where a user’s trust is reinforced by observing independent social behaviors among agents, fostering a shared sense of community that a solitary interface cannot replicate
Zhu et al. 2025
.

Challenges

Despite recent progress in multi-agent systems, two primary challenges remain in developing effective multi-perspective socio-collaborative companions:
1)
Persona Fidelity.
At the individual agent level, models struggle to maintain stable, unique character traits
Wen et al. 2024
. Agents often succumb to
persona collapse

Chang et al. 2024
, regressing to generic, homogenized assistant behaviors that lack unique perspectives. 2)
Interaction Synergy.

At the collective level, uncoordinated agents fail to generate complementary, non-redundant contributions.
This manifests as repeating communications or even
Social Sycophancy
(the ‘Yes-Man’ Bias), where agents prioritize agreeableness, creating echo chambers rather than constructive conversations.
Furthermore, agents often struggle with
Long-Horizon State Consistency
, treating each turn as a local optimization problem and losing track of the conversation’s logical arc, such as re-litigating settled topics
Chan et al. 2024
.

Method

Preliminary

We formalize a socio-collaborative companion system as a set of agents
\mathcal{A}=\{a_{i}\}_{i=1}^{|\mathcal{A}|}
. Each agent
a_{i}
is conditioned on a distinct persona profile
\rho_{i}
that specifies traits such as linguistic style, domain expertise, and emotional disposition (e.g.
Empathetic Listener
,
Critical Thinker
,
Emotional Validator
,
Action Guide
, and
Growth Advocate
). Given each user interaction context
x
(e.g., a user’s emotional disclosure or a dialog segment),
the system generates a multi-turn, multi-perspective
conversation trajectory

\mathbf{Y}=(y_{1},y_{2},\ldots,y_{T})
.

Mascot
(Figure
2
) optimizes this process via a two-phase training pipeline: (1)
Persona-Aware Behavioral Alignment
and (2)
Collaborative Dialogue Optimization
.

2.1
Persona-Aware Behavioral Alignment

Current multi-agent frameworks often suffer from
persona collapse

Chang et al. 2024
, where agents regress to generic assistant behaviors rather than adhering to their assigned roles.
While zero-shot or few-shot prompting
Wang et al. 2020
are often used to address this issue, they frequently fail to maintain consistency, particularly in smaller open-source models, yielding high variance in persona adherence.
To prevent
persona collapse
and ensure adherence to social-emotional traits, we move beyond inference-time prompting and explicitly align a base policy
\pi_{\theta}
by leveraging a persona-oriented reward model within a Reinforcement Learning from AI Feedback (RLAIF) framework
Bai et al. 2022
;
Lee et al. 2024
.

Figure 2:
Overview of
Mascot
for multi-agent socio-collaborative companions.
Mascot
produces synergistic multi-agent dialogues that maintain distinct personas while collectively supporting the user. It leverages a two-phase optimization pipeline. In Persona-Aware Behavioral Alignment, individual agents are fine-tuned via RLAIF with a learned reward model to ensure stable persona fidelity and high-quality responses. In Collaborative Dialogue Optimization, a meta-agent (director) coordinates multiple speaker agents through directive generation, optimizing group-level rewards for coherence, diversity, and non-redundant contributions.

Persona Reward Modeling

Effective training of persona adherence requires robust signals. Thus,
Mascot
uses expert models as strong teacher models to guide the behaviors of student models with lower latency.
To construct the preference dataset
\mathcal{D}
, we start with a supervised base policy
\pi_{\text{SFT}}
. Given context
x
and persona
\rho_{i}
, we sample
K
responses
\{y_{k}\}_{k=1}^{K}
:

y_{k}\sim\pi_{\text{SFT}}(\cdot\mid x,\rho_{i}),\quad k=1,\ldots,K.

(1)

Each candidate response
y_{k}
is evaluated by an LLM judge against a set of fine-grained criteria
\mathcal{C}
(Table
5
).
For each criterion
c\in\mathcal{C}
, the judge assigns a score
s_{c}(y_{k})\in[1,5]
. The aggregate score is then computed as:

s(y_{k})=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{C}|}\sum_{c\in\mathcal{C}}s_{c}(y_{k}).

(2)

We retain pairs
(y_{w},y_{l})
satisfying a margin
s(y_{w})-s(y_{l})\geq\delta
, thus eliminating ambiguous comparisons and ensuring a significant quality margin.
We then train a reward model
r_{\phi}
by minimizing the standard pairwise logistic loss
Bradley and Terry 1952
:

\mathcal{L}_{\text{RM}}(\phi)=-\mathbb{E}{(y_{w},y_{l})\sim\mathcal{D}}[\log\sigma(r_{\phi}(y_{w})-r_{\phi}(y_{l}))]

(3)

where
\sigma(\cdot)
is the sigmoid function. This objective ensures that the reward model learns to differentiate between responses based on their persona adherence and contextual quality as defined by the LLM judge.

Efficient RL Fine-tuning via GRPO

To ensure persona consistency while maintaining structural integrity, we optimize a composite reward function:

R(x,\rho,y)=r_{\phi}(x,\rho,y)+\lambda R_{\mathrm{format}}(y)

(4)

where
r_{\phi}
is the learned persona reward and
R_{\mathrm{format}}
is a rule-based reward enforcing structural constraints, specifically, requiring explicit reasoning traces within
<think>
tags while penalizing verbose final answers to ensure outputs remain concise and socially appropriate.
To optimize this objective, we employ Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)
Shao et al. 2024
, which estimates the baseline from the group mean of sampled outputs, significantly reducing memory overhead and computational cost compared with alternative methods.

For each input
(x,\rho)
, we sample a group of
G
outputs
\{y_{1},\dots,y_{G}\}
from the old policy
\pi_{\text{old}}
. The policy
\pi_{\theta}
is optimized through the following objective:

\begin{split}&\mathcal{J}(\theta)=\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mathcal{D},\{y_{g}\}\sim\pi_{\text{old}}}\Bigg[\frac{1}{G}\sum_{g=1}^{G}\bigg(\frac{1}{|y_{g}|}\sum_{t=1}^{|y_{g}|}\min\Big(\gamma_{g,t}\hat{A}_{g},\\
&\operatorname{clip}(\gamma_{g,t},1-\varepsilon,1+\varepsilon)\hat{A}_{g}\Big)-\beta\mathbb{D}_{\text{KL}}(\pi_{\theta}\|\pi_{\text{ref}})\bigg)\Bigg],\end{split}

(5)

where
\gamma_{g,t}=\frac{\pi_{\theta}(y_{g,t}|x,\rho,y_{g,<t})}{\pi_{\text{old}}(y_{g,t}|x,\rho,y_{g,<t})}
is the probability ratio and
\hat{A}_{g}
is the advantage, calculated as the standardized reward
(R_{g}-\text{mean}(\{R_{k}\}))/\text{std}(\{R_{k}\})
.

2.2
Collaborative Dialogue Optimization

While Phase I ensures response quality for individual agents, it does not account for group dynamics, such as avoiding information redundancy or the re-litigation of established consensus. We introduce a collaborative optimization phase to
orchestrate the collective conversation into a cohesive, synergistic narrative rather than a series of disjointed monologues.

Bi-level Generation Process

We define a conversation as an
N
-turn episode. For both training and inference, the conversation trajectory generation process is governed by a bi-level hierarchy comprising a single Meta-Agent (Director) and a set of Speaker Agents
\mathcal{A}
.
At each turn
t
, the process proceeds as follows:
1)
Directive Generation.

The director
\pi_{\Phi}
observes the history
h_{<t}=(x,y_{1},\dots,y_{t-1})
and generates a high-level directive
z_{t}\sim\pi_{\Phi}(\cdot\mid h_{<t})
, which specifies the next speaker
a_{i}
with detailed instructions (e.g., “The Beacon: The user mentioned a small win. Use active-constructive responding to amplify their pride and savor the moment.”)
2)
Agent Response.
The selected agent
a_{i}
, generates response
y_{t}
conditioned on their persona and the directive:

y_{t}\sim\pi_{\theta}(\cdot\mid h_{<t},\rho_{i},z_{t}).

(6)

This hierarchical decomposition allows the system to switch strategies dynamically—moving from validation (
The Anchor
) to cognitive reframing (
The Catalyst
)—mirroring the progression of effective human support.
Note that for computational efficiency, the director agent can be parameter-sharing as the individual agents.

Group Reward Modeling

The director agent is optimized to maximize a global conversation reward
R_{\text{group}}
over an
N
-turn block
\mathbf{Y}
:

R_{\text{group}}(x,\mathbf{Y})=R_{\text{coherence}}(\mathbf{Y})+\eta\cdot\mathbb{I}_{\text{diverse}}(\mathbf{Y})

(7)

where
R_{\text{coherence}}
measures logical flow and
\mathbb{I}_{\text{diverse}}
is an indicator reward that penalizes redundant persona selection. We again utilize GRPO to update the director agent’s parameters
\Phi
, ensuring the "Director" learns to issue directives that elicit the most synergistic collective response.

Challenges in Negative Valence

Most models exhibit a performance decline in the ED-Negative subset (Table
1
).
Mascot
’s
Emotional Expressiveness
drops from
75.3

68.2

Limitations of Supervised Fine-Tuning

SFT performance (
44.1
Overall on ED-Negative)
suggests that static training dat with expert demonstrations may induce overly conservative “mean-seeking” patterns, limiting their performance compared with Zero-Shot baselines (
55.1
Overall).
Conversely,
Mascot
’s uses reward signals to reinforce target behaviors, encourages strategic exploration that preserves flexibility and reasoning depth. This helps mitigate the risk of identity loss due to catastrophic forgetting
Kirkpatrick et al. 2017
.

3.3
Multi-Perspective Evaluation Across MBTI Profiles

To investigate the effectiveness of
Mascot
across diverse populations, we simulated interactions with 16 Myers-Briggs Type Indicator (MBTI) profiles
Briggs and Myers 1976
.
Results in Figure

Comparative Analysis

While the Zero-shot model acknowledges the user’s feelings, the tone is somewhat generic and transactional and relies on clichés (e.g., ‘it’s okay to feel hurt’), failing to provide a structured supportive journey.

The Few-Shot + CoT approach introduces depth through persona-driven agents (Anchor, Beacon, Catalyst) but often suffers from context misalignment. For example, the blue car as a
massive win
feels discordant with the user’s expressed frustration and risks minimizing the user’s feelings through forced positivity.

In contrast, MASCOT demonstrates superior social intelligence by providing a multi-dimensional support structure. The Anchor provides deep affective empathy by identifying the ‘quiet betrayal’ of an unmet wish, while the Catalyst facilitates relational growth by encouraging the user to bridge the communication gap between their personal desires and their father’s values. Finally, the Beacon avoids a
tone-deaf
celebration of the material gift; instead, it reframes the situation to celebrate the user’s courage and self-expression. By integrating these three perspectives, MASCOT ensures the user feels holistically heard, cognitively supported, and personally empowered, effectively mirroring the complexity of human social intelligence.

Related Work

Social-First Multi-Agent Systems (MAS)

Despite the success of task-oriented LLMs, socio-collaborative companion systems remain an underexplored frontier. Traditional
efficiency-first
frameworks prioritize task effectiveness often neglecting the
sociological turn
required for authentic human-AI interaction
Hong et al. 2024
. Although pioneering works like Social Simulacra
Park et al. 2022
and Generative Agents
Park et al. 2023
demonstrate human-like social behaviors across agents, most existing companion systems are restricted to
dyadic
interactions, failing to replicate the multi-viewpoint communications and ‘emotional labor’ in natural human networks
Hochschild 2012
.

Coordination and Persona Integrity

MAS provides a promising paradigm to distribute support across a ‘community of care’
Korczynski 2003
. While recent frameworks
Xu et al. 2025
;
Ortigoso et al. 2025
;
Jin et al. 2024
;
Wang et al. 2025

validate this approach, multi-party dialogues risk
persona collapse
, where models agents converge to a ‘model mean’–a generic, agreeable personality that erodes character distinctiveness over time
Baltaji et al. 2024
.
While mechanisms like retrieval-augmented generation
Packer et al. 2024
and self-consistency
Wei et al. 2022
address memory issues,
Mascot
decouples coordination from generative persona execution to maintaining distinct, persistent voices in a group settings.

5
Conclusion

We propose
Mascot
, a versatile multi-agent system for socio-collaborative companions. By harmonizing individual persona fidelity with collective dialogue synergy,
Mascot
significantly outperforms baselines across diverse domains, such as emotional support and workplace settings. This research establishes a roadmap for engineering socially intelligent agents.

Limitations

While
Mascot
demonstrates significant effectiveness in socio-collaborative tasks, challenges still remain.
First, our adaptation strategy prioritizes parameter-level updates to ensure high-fidelity persona adherence.
Thus, the framework is most directly applicable to open-source architectures with accessible weights.
For proprietary models, the core principles in this work such as persona adaptation could potentially be adapted through black-box methodologies like specialized few-shot prompting or Chain-of-Thought (CoT) refinement.
Second, this study focuses on English-language interactions. While the framework itself is generalizable and model-agnostic across various architectures, the cultural and linguistic variations in persona modeling across different languages present a compelling opportunity for future research.

Appendix A
Ethical Consideration

While MASCOT is designed to foster social connection and provide general emotional support, we acknowledge the importance of defining clear operational boundaries for AI in affective contexts. To ensure a stable and supportive experience, the framework employs a "prudence trade-off" in negative valence scenarios, prioritizing Response Relevance to act as a reliable "container" for user distress. Because these companions are intended to function as cognitive scaffolds rather than clinical diagnostic tools, we encourage the integration of complementary safety protocols in real-world deployments.

B.2
Implementation Details

For all experiments, unless otherwise specified, we employ Qwen3-8B
Qiu et al. 2025
;
Yang et al. 2025
as the LLM for meta-agents, speaker agents, and user agents. We finetune the meta-agents and speaker agents using LoRA

(
r=16,\alpha=32,\text{dropout}=0.1
) using a batch size of
16
for 1 epoch. The trainable parameter takes around 0.187% of the total parameters, a small additional cost compared to full finetuning.
Similarly, for the reward model (RM), we fine-tune Qwen3-0.6B for 3 epochs with a batch size of
32
.
To leverage the model’s linguistic priors for persona adherence, we append an output head to the final hidden state. During sampling, we use GPT-4o as the teacher model, generate
K=8
candidates for each sample and persona, and apply a filtering margin of
\delta=0.5
.
We set
\beta_{1}=\beta_{2}=1.0
without further tuning. The format reward
R_{\mathrm{format}}
mandates a reasoning trace exceeding 448 tokens and a final response under 64 tokens. This objective ensures exhaustive internal deliberation while maintaining the brevity essential for naturalistic social interaction.

All experiments were implemented using the
TRL

transformers
libraries and executed on a cluster of six NVIDIA A100 (80GB) GPUs. Given the open-ended nature of the task and the absence of ground-truth references, we adopt LLM-as-a-Judge for evaluation

.

B.3
Evaluation Metrics

Current evaluation for multi-agent systems (MAS) is often constrained by metrics such as BLEU or Task Completion Rate. Because these metrics prioritize objective system efficiency,
they frequently correlate inversely with social success, penalizing the dialogue depth required for rapport-building as inefficient"
Xu et al. 2025

To bridge this gap, we propose a multi-dimensional LLM-as-a-Judge evaluation framework, including agent-specific criteria (Table
5
) and collective-interaction criteria (Table
6
).
We use GPT-4o as the evaluator with the temperature set to
0.0
to ensure reproducibility. The judge follows a multi-dimensional rubric to score outputs on a 1–5 Likert scale. For clarity in reporting, all final scores are linearly rescaled to a
[0,100]
range.

B.4
Datasets

Empathetic Dialogues Benchmark

Rashkin et al. 2019
features 25,000 conversations grounded in emotional situations. We use the training set that consists of 19,533 unique conversation prompts and the test set with 2547 unique conversation prompts. To ensure a fine-grained evaluation of MASCOT under different emotional support scenarios, we report the performance across the
3
subsets: Positive, Negative, and Neutral (Table
4
) because support objectives are highly valence-dependent.
For instance, Negative states (e.g. sadness) require empathetic validation and ‘holding space’ for the user, where cheerful or solution-oriented responses might be perceived as dismissive or inappropriate. Conversely, responding to Positive emotions such as excitement or pride requires active-constructive responding to amplify the positive affect.
This categorization allows us to analyze how effectively MASCOT adapts its persona-driven feedback to specific psychological needs while preventing inappropriate emotional tone.
The dataset is released under the Creative Commons NonCommercial license.

QMSum

Zhong et al. 2021
consists of 1,808 query–summary pairs drawn from 232 meetings across multiple domains. We follow the topic-based partitioning provided by the dataset, focusing on Academic, Committee, and Product meetings. The training split contains 161 meetings and is used for model training, while the test split includes 35 meetings and is used for evaluation. The dataset is released under MIT License.

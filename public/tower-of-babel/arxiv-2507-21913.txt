Abstract
In this paper, we present a fast multipole method (FMM) for solving the two-dimensional Laplace equation in a half-plane with Robin boundary conditions. The method is based on a novel expansion theory for the reaction component of the Green’s function. By applying the Fourier transform, the reaction field component is obtained in a Sommerfeld-type integral form. We derive far-field approximations and corresponding shifting and translation operators from the Fourier integral representation. The FMM for the reaction component is then developed by using the new far-field approximations incorporated into the classic FMM framework in which the tree structure is constructed from the original and image charges. Combining this with the standard FMM for the free-space components, we develop a fast algorithm to compute the interaction of the half plane Laplace Green’s function. We prove that the method exhibits exponential convergence, similar to the free-space FMM. Finally, numerical examples are presented to validate the theoretical results and demonstrate that the FMM achieves O ⁡ ( N ) O(N) computational complexity.

Introduction

The Laplace equation in half space
\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}=\{\bm{r}=(x,y):y>0\}
with Robin boundary condition on the boundary
y=0
, has a wide array of practical implications in engineering contexts. An important application is in water wave theory when the assumption of infinite depth is applied as long as the water is deep enough with respect to the wave height and length
Hein1
;
Kuznetsov
;
appliquees2010greens
;
Perez
. The real-parameter Robin boundary condition offers a linearized depiction of time-harmonic gravity wave propagation across the surface of incompressible, inviscid, and irrotational fluids
Wehausen
. Robin boundary condition with complex-parameter will be employed when porous structures such as permeable breakwaters are considered
Sollitt
. By allowing local perturbations on the half-plane, the model is extended to describe the scattering of small-amplitude water waves due to the presence of floating or submerged bodies.
Other notable applications include the modeling of harmonic potentials within domains featuring uneven surfaces, the analysis of steady-state heat conduction employing linear convective boundary conditions, and the approximation of low-frequency sound waves and electromagnetic wave propagation on the ground
chen1997review
;
dassios1999half
.

For numerically addressing the Laplace equation in a Robin half-plane, the boundary integral method
Mei
;
Perez
;
Yeung
has the advantages of dimension reduction and naturally imposing the radiation/decay condition. Nevertheless, a well-known limitation of the conventional boundary integral method resides in the dense linear system resulting from the discretization of the global boundary integral operator. Solving this linear system using standard methods can present computational challenges, especially in scenarios involving intricate or extensive boundaries. One strategy to circumvent this hindrance involves the fast multipole method (FMM), originally devised by Rokhlin
Rokhlin1985
for the two-dimensional Laplace equation and subsequently refined by Greengard and Rokhlin for many-body issues
Greengard1
. This method accelerates the dense matrix product vector, reduces data storage requirements, and decreases the computational cost from
O(N^{2})
to
O(N\log N)
or
O(N)
. Over the past three decades, there has been a significant body of research focusing on the FMM
widefmm
;
Darve1
;
darve2000fast
;
greengard1997new
;
Lu1
;
snyder12
;
Song1
;
Tausch1
;
bo2018taylorfmm
;
Ying1
and its applications on solving PDEs with boundary integral methods
Fong1
;
Liu1
.

The essence of the FMM is the far field approximation of the Green’s function. Unlike the half-plane problem with Dirichlet or Neumann boundary conditions where a closed form of the Green’s function can be obtained by simply applying image method, the Green’s function for the Robin problem is usually given by more complicate expressions. Its far field approximation theory has not been established until the first work by Hien et al.
Hein1
;
appliquees2010greens
in which a closed form of the Green’s function has been derived. Based on this closed form, a FMM accelerated boundary element method (BEM) is developed to efficiently solve the half-plane problem
Perez
. However, to the best of our knowledge, the closed form of the Green’s function for 3-dimensional half space problem is still open and the road-map presented in
Hein1
;
appliquees2010greens
is not available in handling half space problems.

Recently, we have established a general framework to develop FMM for the Green’s function of 3-dimensional Laplace, Helmholtz and modified Helmholtz equation in layered media
wang2019fast
;
wangbo2021
;
wang2021fast
;
Zhang-Wang
. We have proposed a methodology to derive far field expansion theory from the Sommerfeld-type integral representation of the Green’s functions in layered media. The derivation is based on the expansions of the Fourier kernel and thus can be applied to a variety of linear PDEs whose Green’s function can be obtained through Fourier transform. Moreover, the resultant expansions reduce to the spherical harmonic expansions used in the free space FMM when the layered medium is reduced to the homogeneous one. In all of our previous work, only transmission interface conditions are considered. Robin boundary condition will lead to singular density function in the Sommerfeld-type integral representation of the Green’s function and therefore worthy further investigation.

In this article, we develop an FMM for the Laplace equation in half-plane with Robin boundary condition. No closed form of the Green’s function is required. We present a comprehensive derivation for the far field expansions and their shifting and translation operators of the Green’s function directly from the Sommerfeld-type integral representation. Although the approximation theory used in our algorithm might be equivalent to that presented in
Hein1
;
appliquees2010greens
, the derivation procedure is much simpler and different. More importantly, our framework is more general and can be extended to 3-dimensional half-space case naturally, which is our on-going research. Exponential convergence of the far field expansions and their shifting and translation operators are proved. The result reveals an important fact that the convergence of the approximations used for the reaction field component depends on the distance between the target and the image source. This suggests how the fast multipole method (FMM) framework should be configured for sources and targets located in the half-plane.

The outline of the subsequent sections is as follows. In Section

Then, the FMM for the reaction components is developed. Together with the classic FMM for other free space components, a fast algorithm for the Laplace equation in the half-plane with Robin boundary condition is obtained. The exponential convergence of the FMM is proven in Section
3

y=0
. Numerical experiments are provided in Section
4

O(N)
complexity of the FMM. Finally, we summarize this paper and discuss future work in Section
5

2
The FMM for Laplace equation in half-plane

In this section, we first present mathematical expansions for the far-field approximation of the reaction components in the Green’s function for the two-dimensional (2-D) Laplace equation in a half-plane domain. Subsequently, we introduce the FMM for the Green’s function of the 2-D Laplace equation in a half-plane, utilizing the framework of FMM in layered media.

2.1
The Green’s function for Laplace equation in half-plane

We focus on the radiation problem of linear time-harmonic surface waves in the half-plane
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}
, originating from a fixed source point
\bm{r}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}
, as illustrated in Fig.
1
. The Green’s function discussed herein corresponds to the solution of this problem. Our analysis considers two distinct cases of wave propagation. The first case involves dissipative wave propagation, described by the Green function that incorporates dissipation. In contrast, the second case pertains to non-dissipative wave propagation, represented by the Green function without dissipation. To ensure correct physical results, the calculations must be conducted in the sense of the limiting absorption principle, which states that the Green’s function without dissipation is the limit of the Green’s function with dissipation as the dissipation parameter
\varepsilon
approaches zero.

Figure 1:
Domain of the Green’s function of the half-plane problem.

The Green’s function for the half-plane problem in the two-dimensional Laplace equation (taking the upper half-plane as an example) satisfies the following conditions:

\displaystyle\bm{\Delta}G(\bm{r},\bm{r}^{\prime})=-\delta(\bm{r}-\bm{r}^{\prime}),\quad y>0,

(1)

\displaystyle\mathscr{B}G=0,\quad y=0,

(2)

\displaystyle|G|\leq\frac{C}{{r}},\quad\left|\frac{\partial G}{\partial r}\right|\leq\frac{C}{r^{2}},\quad r\to\infty,

(3)

where

\mathscr{B}=\mathcal{I},\;\;\frac{\partial}{\partial\bm{n}},\;\;{\rm or}\;\;\frac{\partial}{\partial\bm{n}}-Z_{\varepsilon},

(4)

are boundary operators regarding Dirichlet, Neumann, and Robin boundary conditions, respectively. Here,
\bm{r}=(x,y)
,
\bm{r}^{\prime}=(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})
,
r=|\bm{r}|
, and
C
is a positive constant. The outward normal vector
\bm{n}
is specified as the negative direction of the
y-
axis. The notation
Z_{\varepsilon}=Z+{\rm i}\varepsilon
denotes a complex impedance that corresponds to dissipative wave propagation. Moreover,
\varepsilon>0
is a small dissipation parameter and
Z>0
. If
Z_{\varepsilon}=Z
, this indicates a real impedance, which is associated with non-dissipative wave propagation.
The inequalities in (
3
) represent the outgoing radiation condition at infinity related to dissipative wave propagation. For non-dissipative wave propagation, an outgoing radiation condition at infinity was introduced in reference
Hein1
. This condition, as
r\to\infty
, is expressed as follows,

\displaystyle|G|\leq\frac{C}{{r}}\quad

\displaystyle\text{and}\quad\left|\frac{\partial G}{\partial r}\right|\leq\frac{C}{r^{2}}\quad

\displaystyle\text{if}\quad y>\frac{1}{Z}\ln(1+Z\pi r),

(5)

\displaystyle|G|\leq C\quad

\displaystyle\text{and}\quad\left|\frac{\partial G}{\partial r}-iZG\right|\leq\frac{C}{r}\quad

\displaystyle\text{if}\quad y<\frac{1}{Z}\ln(1+Z\pi r),

(6)

for some constants
C>0
.

By applying Fourier transform in the
x
-direction and the idea of images, we can obtain expressions for the Green’s functions associated with different boundary conditions as follows:

•

Dirichlet and Neumann boundary conditions

\displaystyle G(\bm{r},\bm{r}^{\prime})=-\dfrac{1}{2\pi}\ln|\bm{r}-\bm{r}^{\prime}|\pm\dfrac{1}{2\pi}\ln|\bm{r}-{\bm{r}}^{\prime}_{\rm im}|,

(7)

•

Robin boundary condition

G(\bm{r},\bm{r}^{\prime})=-\dfrac{1}{2\pi}\ln|\bm{r}-\bm{r}^{\prime}|+\dfrac{1}{2\pi}\ln|\bm{r}-{\bm{r}}^{\prime}_{\rm im}|+\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\frac{e^{{\rm i}\lambda(x-x^{\prime})-|\lambda|(y+y^{\prime})}}{|\lambda|-Z_{\varepsilon}}d\lambda,

(8)

where

{\bm{r}}^{\prime}_{\rm im}=(x^{\prime},-y^{\prime})
is the image of
\bm{r}^{\prime}
with respect to the
x
-axis. Apparently, the Green’s function has symmetry

G(\bm{r},\bm{r}^{\prime})=G(\bm{r}^{\prime},\bm{r}),

(9)

for all three types of boundary conditions. In the Robin boundary condition case, only the third term in (
8
) depends on the impedance
Z_{\varepsilon}
. In this paper, we will present a fast multipole method for fast computation of the interactions induced by the Green’s function (
8
). As the classic FMM can be applied to calculate the interactions induced by the logarithm terms, we will focus on the theory and fast algorithms for the computation of the interaction governed by the integral term, which we denoted by

G_{Z_{\varepsilon}}(\bm{r},\bm{r}^{\prime}):=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\frac{e^{{\rm i}\lambda(x-x^{\prime})-|\lambda|(y+y^{\prime})}}{|\lambda|-Z_{\varepsilon}}d\lambda.

(10)

The approximation theory and fast algorithm presented in this paper is also available for the non-dissipative case, i.e.,
\varepsilon=0
. In this case, the integral (
10
) involves two simple poles
\lambda=\pm Z
and should be understood using the limit absorption principle, i.e.,

G_{Z_{0}}(\bm{r},\bm{r}^{\prime})=\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\frac{e^{{\rm i}\lambda(x-x^{\prime})-|\lambda|(y+y^{\prime})}}{|\lambda|-Z_{\varepsilon}}d\lambda.

(11)

Define integrals

\mathcal{I}_{n}(x,y)=\frac{1}{2\pi n!}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{e^{-\lambda y+{\rm i}\lambda x}\lambda^{n}}{\lambda-Z_{\varepsilon}}d\lambda,\quad n=0,1,\cdots.

(12)

It is able to avoid the absolute value of
\lambda
in the definition of
G_{Z_{\varepsilon}}(\bm{r},\bm{r}^{\prime})
by rewriting it as

G_{Z_{\varepsilon}}(\bm{r},\bm{r}^{\prime})=\mathcal{I}_{0}(x-x^{\prime},y+y^{\prime})+\mathcal{I}_{0}(x^{\prime}-x,y+y^{\prime}).

(13)

Hein et al. in
Hein1
derived the following explicit expressions

\displaystyle G_{Z_{\varepsilon}}(\bm{r},\bm{r}^{\prime})=

\displaystyle-\frac{e^{-Z_{\varepsilon}(y+y^{\prime})}}{2\pi}\left\{e^{{\rm i}Z_{\varepsilon}(x-x^{\prime})}{\rm{Ei}}\Big(Z_{\varepsilon}\big((y+y^{\prime})-{\rm i}(x-x^{\prime})\big)\Big)\right.

\displaystyle\left.+e^{-{\rm i}Z_{\varepsilon}(x-x^{\prime})}{\rm{Ei}}\Big(Z_{\varepsilon}\big((y+y^{\prime})+{\rm i}(x-x^{\prime})\big)\Big)\right\},\quad\varepsilon>0,

and

\displaystyle G_{Z_{0}}(\bm{r},\bm{r}^{\prime})=

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}G_{Z_{\varepsilon}}(\bm{r},\bm{r}^{\prime})=-\frac{e^{-Z(y+y^{\prime})}}{2\pi}\left\{e^{{\rm i}Z(x-x^{\prime})}{\rm{Ei}}\Big(Z\big((y+y^{\prime})-{\rm i}(x-x^{\prime})\big)\Big)\right.

\displaystyle\left.+e^{-{\rm i}Z(x-x^{\prime})}{\rm{Ei}}\Big(Z\big((y+y^{\prime})+{\rm i}(x-x^{\prime})\big)\Big)\right\}

\displaystyle+{\rm i}e^{-Z(y+y^{\prime})}\cos(Z(x-x^{\prime})),

where

\displaystyle{\rm{Ei}}(z)=-\int_{-z}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-t}}{t}dt,\quad z\neq 0

(14)

is the exponential integral function
abramowitz1966handbook
. The exponential integral function
{\rm{Ei}}(z)
with complex argument

can use any contour form
-z
to
\infty
which does not cross the negative real axis or pass through the origin. These explicit expressions are useful to analyze the behavior of
G_{Z_{\varepsilon}}(\bm{r},\bm{r}^{\prime})
. However, it is complicate to establish far-field approximation theory from them. We will directly work on the integral form (

) and (

2.2
Fast multipole method

Let
\{(Q_{j},\bm{r}_{j}),

j=1,2,\cdots,N\}
be a large number of charged particles in the half plane, where
Q_{j}
and
\bm{r}_{j}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}
are the charge and coordinates of the
j
-th particle respectively. The potential of the interaction at any points
\bm{r}_{i}
is given by the summation

\Phi(\bm{r}_{i})=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}Q_{j}G(\bm{r}_{i},\bm{r}_{j}):=\Phi^{\rm free}(\bm{r}_{i})+\Phi_{1}(\bm{r}_{i})+\Phi_{2}(\bm{r}_{i}),

(15)

where

\Phi^{\rm free}(\bm{r}_{i})=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum\limits_{j=1,j\neq i}^{N}Q_{j}\ln(|\bm{r}_{i}-\bm{r}_{j}|),

(16)

is the free space component and

\Phi_{1}(\bm{r}_{i})=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}Q_{j}\ln(|{\bm{r}}_{i}-\bm{r}_{j}^{\rm im}|),\quad\Phi_{2}(\bm{r}_{i})=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}Q_{j}G_{Z_{\varepsilon}}(\bm{r}_{i},\bm{r}_{j}),

(17)

are the reaction field components. The image sources are given by

\bm{r}_{j}^{\rm im}=(x_{j},-y_{j}),\quad j=1,2,\cdots,N.

(18)

The classic FMM can be applied to compute
\{\Phi^{\rm free}(\bm{r}_{i})\}_{i=1}^{N}
and
\{\Phi_{1}(\bm{r}_{i})\}_{i=1}^{N}
, efficiently. Thus, we will first develop a FMM for efficient computation of the reaction component
\{\Phi_{2}(\bm{r}_{i})\}_{i=1}^{N}
. Then, a fast algorithm for the computation of the total potential
\{\Phi(\bm{r}_{i})\}_{i=1}^{N}
can be made.

By the symmetry (
9
) and (
13
), the reaction potential
\Phi_{2}(\bm{r}_{i})
can be decomposed into the summation of

\Phi_{2}^{\pm}(\bm{r}_{i})=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}Q_{j}\mathcal{I}_{0}(\pm(x_{i}-x_{j}),y_{i}-y_{j}^{\rm im}),

(19)

with
y_{j}^{\rm im}=-y_{j}
namely,

\Phi_{2}(\bm{r}_{i})=\Phi_{2}^{+}(\bm{r}_{i})+\Phi_{2}^{-}(\bm{r}_{i}).

(20)

Therefore, we only need to focus on the FMM for
\Phi_{2}^{+}(\bm{r}_{i})
, since
\Phi_{2}^{-}(\bm{r}_{i})
can be calculated similarly as
in the algorithm for
\Phi_{2}^{+}(\bm{r}_{i})
.

It is well known that the mathematical foundation of the FMM is the theory of the multipole and local expansions together with their shift and translation operators. Next, we will present these formulas for the reaction components
\Phi_{2}^{+}(\bm{r}_{i})
. The key ingredient to derive the expansion theory is the following theorem whose proof will be given in Appendix A.

Theorem 2.1

Given two points
\bm{r}=(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+},\ \bm{r}^{\prime}=(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{-}
,
Z_{\varepsilon}=Z+{\rm i}\varepsilon
where
Z>0
,
\varepsilon>0
, then

\mathcal{I}_{0}(x-x^{\prime},y-y^{\prime})=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}{\rm i}^{-n}(x^{\prime}+{\rm i}y^{\prime})^{n}\mathcal{I}_{n}(x,y),

(21)

holds for
|\bm{r}|>|\bm{r}^{\prime}|
, and

\mathcal{I}_{0}(x-x^{\prime},y-y^{\prime})=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}{\rm i}^{n}(x+{\rm i}y)^{n}\mathcal{I}_{n}(-x^{\prime},-y^{\prime}),

(22)

holds for
|\bm{r}|<|\bm{r}^{\prime}|
.

Remark 1

From the proof of Theorem
2.1
, we can see that the convergence of the series is uniform w.r.t
\varepsilon>0
. Therefore, the expansions in Theorem
2.1
also hold for non-dissipative case as we have mentioned before.

Remark 2

The expansion theory derived in this section is a natural extension of that for free space Green’s function. Actually, the reaction field of the half space problem with Dirichlet boundary condition also has integral representation

-\frac{1}{2\pi}\ln(|\bm{r}-\tilde{\bm{r}}^{\prime}|)=2\mathfrak{Re}\Big[\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-\lambda(y+y^{\prime})}}{\lambda}e^{{\rm i}\lambda(x-x^{\prime})}d\lambda\Big],

(23)

where
\bm{r},\bm{r}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
and
\tilde{\bm{r}}^{\prime}=(x^{\prime},-y^{\prime})
is the image coordinates of
\bm{r}^{\prime}
with respect to
y=0
. By the multipole expansion of
\ln(|\bm{r}-\tilde{\bm{r}}^{\prime}|)
, we have

-\frac{1}{2\pi}\ln(|\bm{r}-\tilde{\bm{r}}^{\prime}|)=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\mathfrak{Re}\Big[\ln z-\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n}\Big(\frac{\tilde{z}^{\prime}}{z}\Big)^{n}\Big],

(24)

where
z=x+{\rm i}y
,
\tilde{z}^{\prime}=x^{\prime}-{\rm i}y^{\prime}
are complex numbers corresponding to the coordinates.
On the other hand, applying (
21
) to the integral representation (
23
), we obtain

-\frac{1}{2\pi}\ln(|\bm{r}-\tilde{\bm{r}}^{\prime}|)=\mathfrak{Re}\Big[\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\tilde{z}^{\prime n}}{2\pi{\rm i}^{n}n!}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda y+{\rm i}\lambda x}\lambda^{n-1}d\lambda\Big].

(25)

Note that

(26)

Moreover, by Cauchy theorem and the definition of Gamma function, we have

\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda y+{\rm i}\lambda x}\lambda^{n-1}d\lambda=\frac{1}{(y-{\rm i}x)^{n}}\int_{C}e^{-z}z^{n-1}dz=\frac{{\rm i}^{n}\Gamma(n)}{z^{n}},\quad n\geq 1,

(27)

where the contour is defined as

\beta_{n}=\sum\limits_{\bm{r}_{j}^{\rm im}\in B_{s}}Q_{j}{\rm i}^{-n}\mathcal{I}_{n}(x_{c}-x_{j},y_{c}-y_{j}^{\rm im}).

(38)

The truncated expansion

\Phi_{LE}^{p}(\bm{r}_{i})=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{p}\beta_{n}[(x_{c}-x_{i})+{\rm i}(y_{c}-y_{i})]^{n},

(39)

is the so called local expansion for
\Phi_{2,B_{s}}^{+}(\bm{r}_{i})
used in the FMM.

•

Local-to-Local (L2L):
Further assume
\tilde{\bm{r}}_{c}=(\tilde{x}_{c},\tilde{y}_{c})
be the center of a child box of
B_{t}
and
\min\limits_{\bm{r}_{j}^{\rm im}\in B_{s}}|\bm{r}_{j}^{\rm im}-\tilde{\bm{r}}_{c}|>\frac{\sqrt{2}}{4}d_{t}
. By the local expansion in (
37
) and binomial formula, we have

\begin{split}\Phi_{2,B_{s}}^{+}(\bm{r}_{i})=&\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\beta_{n}[(x_{c}-\tilde{x}_{c}+\tilde{x}_{c}-x_{i})+{\rm i}(y_{c}-\tilde{y}_{c}+\tilde{y}_{c}-y_{i})]^{n}\\
=&\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\beta_{n}\sum\limits_{m=0}^{n}\frac{n![(\tilde{x}_{c}-x_{i})+{\rm i}(\tilde{y}_{c}-y_{i})]^{m}}{(n-m)!m![(x_{c}-\tilde{x}_{c})+{\rm i}(y_{c}-\tilde{y}_{c})]^{m-n}}\\
=&\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum\limits_{n=m}^{\infty}\beta_{n}\frac{n![(\tilde{x}_{c}-x_{i})+{\rm i}(\tilde{y}_{c}-y_{i})]^{m}}{(n-m)!m![(x_{c}-\tilde{x}_{c})+{\rm i}(y_{c}-\tilde{y}_{c})]^{m-n}}\\
=&\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}\tilde{\beta}_{m}[(\tilde{x}_{c}-x_{i})+{\rm i}(\tilde{y}_{c}-y_{i})]^{m},\end{split}

(40)

where

\tilde{\beta}_{m}=\sum\limits_{n=m}^{\infty}\frac{n![(x_{c}-\tilde{x}_{c})+{\rm i}(y_{c}-\tilde{y}_{c})]^{n-m}}{(n-m)!m!}\beta_{n}.

(41)

This is again the exact L2L formulation used in the classic FMM for free space problems. In the implementation of the FMM, given a truncated LE (
39

41

n=p
. Then, the L2L shifting produce the following approximation

\widetilde{\Phi}_{LE}^{p}(\bm{r}_{i})=\sum\limits_{m=0}^{p}\breve{\beta}_{m}[(\tilde{x}_{c}-x_{i})+{\rm i}(\tilde{y}_{c}-y_{i})]^{m},

(42)

where

\breve{\beta}_{m}=\sum\limits_{n=m}^{p}\frac{n![(x_{c}-\tilde{x}_{c})+{\rm i}(y_{c}-\tilde{y}_{c})]^{n-m}}{(n-m)!m!}\beta_{n}.

•

Multipole-to-Local (M2L):
Suppose the aforementioned source and target boxes
B_{s}
and
B_{t}

and

\bm{r}_{i}

\Phi_{2,B_{s}}^{+}(\bm{r}_{i})=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\alpha_{n}\mathcal{I}_{n}(x_{i}-x_{c}+x_{c}-x^{\prime}_{c},y_{i}-y_{c}+y_{c}-y_{c}^{\prime}),

where

\Phi_{2,B_{s}}^{+}(\bm{r}_{i})=\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha_{n}{\rm i}^{-m}(n+m)!\mathcal{I}_{n+m}(x_{c}-x_{c}^{\prime},y_{c}-y_{c}^{\prime})}{n!m![(x_{c}-x_{i})+{\rm i}(y_{c}-y_{i})]^{-m}}.

Comparing with the local expansion (

\beta_{m}=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{{\rm i}^{-m}(n+m)!}{n!m!}\mathcal{I}_{n+m}(x_{c}-x^{\prime}_{c},y_{c}-y^{\prime}_{c})\alpha_{n}.

(43)

Similar as in the L2L shifting, given a truncated LE (
39

(44)

where

(45)

3
Error analysis for the FMM

In this section, an error estimate of the FMM for the reaction component
\{\Phi_{2}^{\pm}(\bm{r}_{i})\}_{i=1}^{N}
is established. We prove that the FMM for the reaction component enjoys a similar exponential convergence as the classic FMM for free space case, except the convergence rates are determined by the Euclidean distance between the image and source points.
Before estimating the error, it is essential to state the following key estimate.

Lemma 1

Given any complex number
z=a+{\rm i}b
, we have

\left|\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{e^{-t}t^{n}}{t-z}dt\right|\leq C\max\{|z|^{2},1\}(n-1)!

(47)

for
n\geq|z|e
, where
C
is a constant independent of
z
and
n
. The contour is set to be
\Gamma_{1}\cup\Gamma_{3}
as presented in (
97
) to get rid of the pole
t=z
in the case
a>0
,
b=0
.

This lemma can be thought of as an extension of the identity

(n-1)!=\Gamma(n):=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t}t^{n-1}dt,

and the proof will be given in Appendix B.

Lemma 2

Given a point
\bm{r}=(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}
and complex number
Z_{\varepsilon}=Z+{\rm i}\varepsilon
in the first quadrant, i.e.,
Z>0
,
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a positive constant
C
such that for
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
n\geq e|Z_{\varepsilon}||\bm{r}|
, we have

|\mathcal{I}_{n}(x,y)|\leq\frac{C\max\{|Z_{\varepsilon}|^{2}|\bm{r}|^{2},1\}}{n|\bm{r}|^{n}}.

(48)

Proof

Applying variable substitution
t=(y-{\rm i}x)\lambda
, we have

\mathcal{I}_{n}(x,y)=\frac{1}{2\pi n!}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{e^{-\lambda y+{\rm i}\lambda x}\lambda^{n}}{\lambda-Z_{\varepsilon}}d\lambda=\frac{1}{2\pi n!(y-{\rm i}x)^{n}}\int_{S}\frac{t^{n}e^{-t}}{t-Z_{\varepsilon}(y-{\rm i}x)}dt,

(49)

where
S=\big\{t=(y-{\rm i}x)\lambda|\lambda\in[0,+\infty)\big\}
, see Fig.
8
for a sketch.
Then, the estimate can be obtained by changing the contour
S
to the real line and then applying the estimate in Lemma
1
. The contour deformation depends on the position of
P=Z_{\varepsilon}(y-{\rm i}x)
.

If
x=0
, the contour
S
is the real line, i.e.,

\int_{S}\frac{e^{-t}t^{n}}{t-P}dt=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{e^{-t}t^{n}}{t-Z_{\varepsilon}y}dt,

(50)

where
P=Z_{\varepsilon}y
locates in the first quadrant due to the assumption
Z>0,\varepsilon>0
and
y>0
.

If
x<0
, the contour
S
is in the first quadrant and the inequality

\frac{\mathfrak{Im}(P)}{\mathfrak{Re}(P)}=\frac{-x+\frac{\varepsilon y}{Z}}{y+\frac{\varepsilon x}{Z}}>\frac{-x}{y},\quad{\rm if}\;\;y+\frac{\varepsilon x}{Z}>0,

(51)

shows that the point given by
P
is located above the contour
S
or in the left complex plane, see Fig.
8
(a). Therefore, we can apply Cauchy’s theorem to change the contour from
S_{R}=\big\{t=(y-{\rm i}x)\lambda:\lambda\in[0,R]\big\}
to
\Gamma_{R,1}\cup\Gamma^{+}_{R,2}
, where

\displaystyle\Gamma_{R,1}=\left\{t:0\leq t\leq R\right\},\quad\Gamma^{+}_{R,2}=\left\{t=Re^{{\rm i}\theta}:0\leq\theta\leq\arctan\Big(-\frac{x}{y}\Big)\right\}.

For the integral along
\Gamma^{+}_{R,2}
, the assumption
y>0
implies that

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{R\rightarrow+\infty}\Big|\int_{\Gamma^{+}_{R,2}}\frac{e^{-t}t^{n}}{t-P}dt\Big|=

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{R\rightarrow+\infty}\Big|\int_{0}^{\arctan\big(-\frac{x}{y}\big)}\frac{e^{-Re^{{\rm i}\theta}}(Re^{{\rm i}\theta})^{n}Re^{{\rm i}\theta}{\rm i}}{Re^{{\rm i}\theta}-P}d\theta\Big|

(52)

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{R\rightarrow+\infty}e^{-\frac{Ry}{\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}}}}R^{n}\int_{0}^{\arctan\big(-\frac{x}{y}\big)}\frac{1}{|e^{{\rm i}\theta}-P/R|}d\theta=0.

Therefore, the Cauchy theorem gives

\int_{S}\frac{e^{-t}t^{n}}{t-P}dt=\lim\limits_{R\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{\Gamma_{R,1}}\frac{e^{-t}t^{n}}{t-P}dt=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{e^{-t}t^{n}}{t-P}dt,

(53)

where
\mathfrak{Im}(P)=\varepsilon y-Zx>0
.

If
x>0
, the contour
S
is lies in the fourth quadrant and the imaginary part
\mathfrak{Im}(P)=\varepsilon y-Zx
can be any real number. If
\mathfrak{Im}(P)>0
, the contour is illustrated in Fig.
8
(b), we can directly change the contour to the real line and obtain (
53

located in the first quadrant. If

We can also check that, the integral along contour

\Gamma_{R,2}^{-}=\left\{t=Re^{{\rm i}\theta}:\theta_{xy}:=\arctan\Big(-\frac{x}{y}\Big)\leq\theta\leq 0\right\}

(54)

tends to

Therefore, we can always have

\int_{S}\frac{e^{-t}t^{n}}{t-P}dt=\lim\limits_{R\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{\Gamma_{\delta,1}}\frac{e^{-t}t^{n}}{t-P}dt+\int_{\Gamma_{\delta}}\frac{e^{-t}t^{n}}{t-P}dt,

(55)

(57)

\leq&\frac{CQ_{s}}{p+1}\frac{\tilde{q}^{p+1}}{1-\tilde{q}}.\end{split}

The coefficient conversion formula (
43

Theorem 3.5

The truncated ME to LE translation (
44

\begin{split}&\left|\Phi_{2,B_{s}}^{+}(\bm{r}_{i})-{\Phi}_{M2L}^{p}(\bm{r}_{i})\right|\leq\frac{CQ_{s}}{p+1}\left[C_{max}{q}^{p+1}+\check{C}_{max}\check{q}^{p+1}\right],\end{split}

(65)

where
q
,
Q_{s}
and
C_{max}
are defined in (
59

\check{C}_{max}=\frac{2\max\{|Z_{\varepsilon}|^{2}|\bm{r}^{\prime}_{c}-\bm{r}_{c}|^{2},1\}|\bm{r}_{c}^{\prime}-\bm{r}_{c}|}{2|\bm{r}_{c}^{\prime}-\bm{r}_{c}|-\sqrt{2}(d_{s}+d_{t})},\quad\check{q}=\frac{\sqrt{2}d_{t}}{2|\bm{r}_{c}^{\prime}-\bm{r}_{c}|-\sqrt{2}d_{s}}<1,

and
C
is a positive constant.

Proof

By the definition (
32
), (
44
) and (
45
), we have

\begin{split}&{\Phi}_{ME}^{p}(\bm{r}_{i})-{\Phi}_{M2L}^{p}(\bm{r}_{i})\\
=&\sum\limits_{m=0}^{p}\alpha_{m}\mathcal{I}_{m}(x_{i}-x_{c}^{\prime},y_{i}-y_{c}^{\prime})-\sum\limits_{n=0}^{p}\sum\limits_{m=0}^{p}\frac{{\rm i}^{-n}(n+m)!\mathcal{I}_{n+m}(x_{c}-x^{\prime}_{c},y_{c}-y^{\prime}_{c})\alpha_{m}}{n!m![(x_{c}-x_{i})+{\rm i}(y_{c}-y_{i})]^{-n}}.\end{split}

The derivation of the M2L translation shows that

\mathcal{I}_{m}(x_{i}-x_{c}^{\prime},y_{i}-y_{c}^{\prime})=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{{\rm i}^{-n}(n+m)!\mathcal{I}_{n+m}(x_{c}-x^{\prime}_{c},y_{c}-y^{\prime}_{c})}{n!m![(x_{c}-x_{i})+{\rm i}(y_{c}-y_{i})]^{-n}}.

Substituting this expansion back into the last equation and then taking absolute value on both sides, we obtain

\begin{split}|{\Phi}_{ME}^{p}(\bm{r}_{i})-{\Phi}_{M2L}^{p}(\bm{r}_{i})|=\left|\sum\limits_{n=p+1}^{\infty}\sum\limits_{m=0}^{p}\frac{{\rm i}^{-n}(n+m)!\mathcal{I}_{n+m}(x_{c}-x^{\prime}_{c},y_{c}-y^{\prime}_{c})\alpha_{m}}{n!m![(x_{c}-x_{i})+{\rm i}(y_{c}-y_{i})]^{-n}}\right|.\end{split}

Applying Lemma
2
and the estimate

|\alpha_{m}|\leq Q_{s}\Big(\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}d_{s}\Big)^{m},\quad|[(x_{c}-x_{i})+{\rm i}(y_{c}-y_{i})]^{n}|\leq\Big(\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}d_{t}\Big)^{n}

gives estimate

\begin{split}|{\Phi}_{ME}^{p}&(\bm{r}_{i})-{\Phi}_{M2L}^{p}(\bm{r}_{i})|\leq\sum\limits_{n=p+1}^{\infty}\sum\limits_{m=0}^{p}\frac{(n+m)!}{n!m!}\frac{CQ_{s}\max\{|Z_{\varepsilon}|^{2}|\bm{r}^{\prime}_{c}-\bm{r}_{c}|^{2},1\}\hat{d}_{s}^{m}\hat{d}_{t}^{n}}{(n+m)|\bm{r}_{c}^{\prime}-\bm{r}_{c}|^{n+m}}\\
\leq&\sum\limits_{n=p+1}^{\infty}\frac{CQ_{s}\max\{|Z_{\varepsilon}|^{2}|\bm{r}^{\prime}_{c}-\bm{r}_{c}|^{2},1\}}{n}\Big(\frac{\hat{d}_{t}}{|\bm{r}_{c}^{\prime}-\bm{r}_{c}|}\Big)^{n}\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{(n+m)!}{n!m!}\Big(\frac{\hat{d}_{s}}{|\bm{r}_{c}^{\prime}-\bm{r}_{c}|}\Big)^{m}\\
\leq&\frac{CQ_{s}\max\{|Z_{\varepsilon}|^{2}|\bm{r}^{\prime}_{c}-\bm{r}_{c}|^{2},1\}}{p+1}\sum\limits_{n=p+1}^{\infty}\Big(\frac{\hat{d}_{t}}{|\bm{r}_{c}^{\prime}-\bm{r}_{c}|}\Big)^{n}\Big(\frac{|\bm{r}_{c}^{\prime}-\bm{r}_{c}|}{|\bm{r}_{c}^{\prime}-\bm{r}_{c}|-\hat{d}_{s}}\Big)^{n+1}\\
=&\frac{CQ_{s}\max\{|Z_{\varepsilon}|^{2}|\bm{r}^{\prime}_{c}-\bm{r}_{c}|^{2},1\}}{p+1}\frac{|\bm{r}_{c}^{\prime}-\bm{r}_{c}|}{|\bm{r}_{c}^{\prime}-\bm{r}_{c}|-\hat{d}_{s}-\hat{d}_{t}}\Big(\frac{\hat{d}_{t}}{|\bm{r}_{c}^{\prime}-\bm{r}_{c}|-\hat{d}_{s}}\Big)^{p+1},\end{split}

where
\hat{d}_{s}=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}d_{s},\hat{d}_{t}=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}d_{t}
. Now, the triangular inequality

\begin{split}|\Phi_{2,B_{s}}^{+}(\bm{r}_{i})-{\Phi}_{M2L}^{p}(\bm{r}_{i})|\leq|\Phi_{2,B_{s}}^{+}(\bm{r}_{i})-{\Phi}_{ME}^{p}(\bm{r}_{i})|+|{\Phi}_{ME}^{p}(\bm{r}_{i})-{\Phi}_{M2L}^{p}(\bm{r}_{i})|\end{split}

together with Theorem
3.1
implies the conclusion.

In the FMM, the longest approximation chain is: first calculate truncated ME
\Phi_{ME}^{p}(\bm{r}_{i})
and then do a ME to ME shifting to obtain a new ME
\widetilde{\Phi}_{ME}^{p}(\bm{r}_{i})
w.r.t. to the center of the parent box. After that a M2L translation is taken to translate
\widetilde{\Phi}_{ME}^{p}(\bm{r}_{i})
to a LE
{\Phi}_{M2L}^{p}(\bm{r}_{i})
and finally do a LE shifting to obtain a new LE
\widetilde{\Phi}_{LE}^{p}(\bm{r}_{i})
w.r.t. the center of a child box. Apparently, with the error estimates presented in Theorem
3.1
to Theorem
3.5
, an overall exponential convergence w.r.t truncation number
p
for the FMM can be obtained by simply using the triangular inequality.

5
Conclusion

In this paper, we propose a fast multipole method for the two-dimensional Laplace equation in the half-plane with a Robin boundary condition. The algorithm is implemented by incorporating a novel far-field approximation theory for the Green’s function of the half-plane problem into the framework of the classic FMM. Exponential convergence of the far-field approximation theory and a thorough error estimate of the FMM are proved. Both theoretical analysis and numerical results show that the proposed FMM for half-space problems can achieve performance similar to that of the classic FMM for free-space problems.

In future work, we will consider the simulation of water waves using our FMM together with boundary element techniques. Moreover, we will develop the fast multipole method for the three-dimensional Laplace equation in half space with Robin boundary condition which has important applications in medical sciences and engineering.

Appendix B. Proof of Lemma 3.1

Proof

If
a\leq 0
, the definition of Gamma function
\Gamma(x)
directly gives

\begin{split}\left|\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{e^{-t}t^{n}}{t-z}dt\right|\leq&\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{e^{-t}t^{n}}{\sqrt{(t-a)^{2}+b^{2}}}dt\leq\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-t}t^{n-1}dt=(n-1)!.\end{split}

(86)

For any
a>0
, we first consider the case
b>0
. By the equality

\frac{t^{n}}{t-z}=t^{n-1}+z\frac{t^{n-1}}{t-z}=\cdots=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}z^{n-k}t^{k-1}+\frac{z^{n}}{t-z},

(87)

we can arrive at an estimate as follows

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{e^{-t}t^{n}}{t-z}dt=&\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-t}\left(\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}z^{n-k}t^{k-1}+\frac{z^{n}}{t-z}\right)dt\\
=&\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}z^{n-k}(k-1)!+z^{n}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{e^{-t}}{t-z}dt\\
\leq&\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}|z|^{n-k}(k-1)!+|z|^{n}\left|\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{e^{-t}}{t-z}dt\right|.\end{split}

(88)

To give an estimate to the simplified integral, we split it into two integrals

\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{e^{-t}}{t-z}dt=\int_{0}^{a+1}\frac{e^{-t}}{t-z}dt+\int_{a+1}^{+\infty}\frac{e^{-t}}{t-z}dt.

(89)

The second integral has estimate

\begin{split}\left|\int_{a+1}^{+\infty}\frac{e^{-t}}{t-z}dt\right|\leq&\int_{a+1}^{+\infty}\frac{e^{-t}}{|t-z|}dt\leq\int_{a+1}^{+\infty}e^{-t}dt\leq 1.\end{split}

(90)

For the first integral, we change the contour to
\Gamma_{1}\cup\Gamma_{2}\cup\Gamma_{3}
where

\begin{split}&\Gamma_{1}=\left\{t={\rm i}\eta:-1\leq\eta\leq 0\right\},\quad\Gamma_{2}=\left\{t=\eta-{\rm i}:0\leq\eta\leq a+1\right\},\\
&\Gamma_{3}=\left\{t=a+1+{\rm i}\eta:-1\leq\eta\leq 0\right\}.\end{split}

(91)

As
a>0,b>0
, there holds the following estimates

\begin{split}\left|\int_{\Gamma_{1}}\frac{e^{-t}}{t-z}dt\right|=&\left|\int_{0}^{1}\frac{{\rm i}e^{{\rm i}\eta}}{{\rm i}\eta+z}d\eta\right|\leq\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{\sqrt{a+(\eta+b)^{2}}}d\eta\leq\frac{1}{|z|}.\\
\left|\int_{\Gamma_{2}}\frac{e^{-t}}{t-z}dt\right|=&\left|\int_{0}^{a+1}\frac{e^{{\rm i}}e^{-\eta}}{\eta-{\rm i}-P}d\eta\right|\leq\int_{0}^{a+1}\frac{1}{\sqrt{(\eta-a)^{2}+(b+1)^{2}}}d\eta\\
=&\ln{\frac{a+\sqrt{a^{2}+(b+1)^{2}}}{-1+\sqrt{1+(b+1)^{2}}}}\leq 3|z|+1,\\
\left|\int_{\Gamma_{3}}\frac{e^{-t}}{t-z}dt\right|=&\left|{\rm i}e^{-(a+1)}\int_{-1}^{0}\frac{e^{-{\rm i}\eta}}{1+{\rm i}(\eta-b)}d\eta\right|\leq e^{-1}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+(\eta+b)^{2}}}d\eta\\
\leq&e^{-1}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+\eta^{2}}}d\eta=\frac{\ln{(1+\sqrt{2})}}{e}\leq 1.\end{split}

(92)

\left|\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{e^{-t}}{t-z}dt\right|\leq\frac{1}{|z|}+3|z|+3.

(93)

According to Stirling’s formula
Stirling1955

\displaystyle n!=\sqrt{2\pi n}\Big(\frac{n}{e}\Big)^{n}e^{\frac{\theta_{n}}{12n}},\quad 0<\theta_{n}<1,

for all

Heavy-Ball Dynamics with Hessian-Driven Damping for Non-Convex Optimization under the Łojasiewicz Condition
Vassilis Apidopoulos c,∗, Vasiliki Mavrogeorgou ⋄,∗, Theodoros G. Tsironis ♯,∗

Abstract
. In this paper, we examine the convergence properties of heavy-ball dynamics with Hessian-driven damping in smooth non-convex optimization problems satisfying a Łojasiewicz condition. In this general setting, we provide a series of tight, worst-case optimal convergence rate guarantees as a function of the dynamics’ friction coefficients and the Łojasiewicz exponent of the problem’s objective function. Importantly, the linear rates that we obtain improve on previous available rates and they suggest a different tuning of the dynamics’ damping terms, even in the strongly convex regime. We complement our analysis with a range of stability estimates in the presence of perturbation errors and inexact gradient input, as well as an avoidance result showing that the dynamics under study avoid strict saddle points from almost every initial condition.

1.
Introduction

Consider the minimization problem

(Opt)

\displaystyle\operatorname*{minimize}_{x\in\mathcal{H}}

\displaystyle F(x)

where
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}F}\colon\mathcal{H}\to\mathbb{R}
is a
C^{2}
-smooth (not necessarily convex) function on some Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
.
Our aim in this paper is to study the asymptotic minimization and convergence properties of the dynamical inertial Newton-like (
DIN
) system

(DIN)

\ddot{x}(t)+\alpha\dot{x}(t)+\beta\nabla^{2}F(x(t))\dot{x}(t)+\nabla F(x(t))=0

where

\alpha,\beta\geq 0
are the system’s
friction coefficients
–
inertial
and
Hessian
respectively. System (
DIN
) was introduced and studied in
[
2
]
and its origins can be traced back to the seminal work of Polyak
[
45
]
(see also
[
4
,
1
,
10
]
) for the case
\beta=0
– in which case (
DIN
) is typically referred to as the
HBF
(
HBF
) dynamics:

(HBF)

\ddot{x}(t)+\alpha\dot{x}(t)+\nabla F(x(t))=0

The key feature of system (
DIN
) is the incorporation of the Hessian-driven friction term
\nabla^{2}F(x(t))\dot{x}(t)
, to the classical heavy ball with friction dynamics (
HBF
) (
[
45
,
4
,
1
,
10
]
).
The consideration of such Hessian damping term makes the generated trajectory more adaptive to the local curvature of the objective function
F
and mitigates the oscillations that may appear in the behavior of the heavy ball with friction trajectory in (
HBF
), which may have undesirable effects from an optimization objective.

One of the major interests of studying inertial systems like (
DIN
) is their accelerated convergence properties (e.g. in terms of speed of convergence of
F(x(t))
to a critical value
\bar{F}
), with respect to first-order in time systems like the standard
gradient flow
:

(GF)

\dot{x}(t)+\nabla F(x(t))=0.

by properly tuning the friction parameters
\alpha
and
\beta
.

In general, the quantitative properties of systems like (
DIN
) and more precisely the question concerning their rates of convergence (and thus their acceleration properties) strongly depends on i) the precise optimization framework (i.e. the assumptions made on the geometry/regularity
F
) and ii) the adequate tuning of the friction parameters
\alpha
and
\beta
, in order to achieve the best possible convergence results. In fact, the question of acceleration for the system (
DIN
) beyond the (strongly) convex realm, under milder assumptions such as the
Łojasiewicz condition
or
Kurdyka-Łojasiewicz condition

[
44
,
34
,
33
]
still remains unclear and is the main subject of this work.

Contributions

In this work, we aim to address the aforementioned issue for the system (
DIN
). In particular, we study the convergence properties of (
DIN
) in a non-convex setting (when
F
is not necessarily convex), under the Łojasiewicz condition
[
34
,
45
]
(see Assumption
2
in Section
2
).

More precisely, when
F
satisfies the
\mu
-Łojasiewicz condition of order
2
, we provide explicit exponential rates of convergence for
F(x(t))
to a critical value
\bar{F}
and for the decay of
\nabla F(x(t))
to zero, as a function of the friction parameters
\alpha
and
\beta
. As a by-product, we provide an explicit tuning for the friction parameters, leading to an optimal worst-case rate of order
\mathcal{O}(e^{-2\sqrt{\mu}t})
, which is improved with respect to previous related works
[
2
,
8
,
26
,
37
,
49
]
for the system (
DIN
). Our theoretical improvements are also witnessed in some simple numerical illustrations.

We additionally provide stability estimates for the trajectory of (
DIN
) in the presence of an external perturbation error, that depend both on the local landscape of the objective function
F
, around its critical points (i.e. the Łojasiewicz exponent of
F
) and the tuning of the parameters
\alpha
,
\beta
. Such stability results present some interest on their own when it comes to the consideration of stochastic versions of (
DIN
) where the computation of
\nabla F
(or
\nabla^{2}F
) is not (or hardly) accessible.

Furthermore, we complement the analysis by establishing an almost-surely avoidance of strict saddle points result, for functions satisfying the Łojasiewicz condition, further generalizing upon existing works
[
24
]
(see also
[
37
]
) for functions with isolated critical points.
Finally, we extend the above-mentioned results to the first-order in time coupled system (
g-DIN
), which is related to (
DIN
) (see below in paragraph
1.1.2
), paving the way to the consideration of more generalized settings, where the objective function
F
is not necessarily smooth or even differentiable.

1.1.
Related work

1.1.1.
Heavy-ball with friction

System (
HBF
) was firstly introduced and studied in the seminal work of Polyak
[
45
]
, showing its advantages with respect to the gradient flow dynamics (
GF
).
In particular, for twice differentiable and
\mu
-strongly convex functions the (
HBF
) system with
\alpha=2\sqrt{\mu}
enjoys the worst-case optimal linear rate of convergence of order
\mathcal{O}(e^{-2\sqrt{\mu}t})
, in terms of objective function values
F(x(t))-\min F
, while the corresponding rate for (
GF
) is
\mathcal{O}(e^{-2\mu t})
. For the majority of practical functions, it is most likely that the strong convexity parameter is very small (i.e.,
\mu\ll 1
), making (
HBF
) much faster than (
GF
). In this viewpoint the order
\mathcal{O}(e^{-c\sqrt{\mu}t})
, for some constant
c\leq 2
is usually identified as an accelerated linear rate. Beyond the (strongly) convex setting, several studies have been devoted to the convergence properties of (
HBF
), including i) almost sure convergence to local minima for (non convex) Morse functions
[
10
,
30
]
and ii) convergence rates for the heavy ball trajectories under various relaxations of strong convexity, such as quasi-strong convexity
[
46
,
13
,
16
,
14
]
and Łojasiewicz conditioning (see Assumption
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathcal{L}}(2)
)
[
31
,
17
,
43
,
5
]
. However, it is worth mentioning that, to the best of the authors knowledge, the worst-case optimal rate
\mathcal{O}(e^{-2\sqrt{\mu}t})
, obtained by (
HBF
) for strongly convex functions, has not been established for any of the aforementioned relaxations (see also the related discussion in Remark
4
in Section
3
).

1.1.2.
Dynamic Inertial Newton-like system

While the heavy ball system (
HBF
) enjoys some nice convergence properties, its trajectory often presents oscillatory behavior, which may cause numerical instabilities and affect the overall efficiency of the method.
In order to overcome this issue, in
[
2
]
the authors proposed the system (
DIN
), which incorporates to the plain heavy ball system (
HBF
), an additional geometric damping term that captures the local curvature of the function (Hessian driven damping).

A seemingly inconvenience of (
DIN
) is its second order character in space (due to the presence of the Hessian term
\nabla^{2}F(x(t))
), which may seem restricting for functions that are not necessarily twice differentiable.
However, one of the key features concerning system (
DIN
), is that it is strongly related with a first-order coupled system (in time and space) with no occurrence of the Hessian, that reads as follows:

(g-DIN)

\begin{cases}{}\dot{x}(t)+\beta\nabla F(x(t))+\left(\alpha-\frac{1}{\beta}\right)x(t)+\frac{1}{\beta}y(t)=0\\
\dot{y}(t)+\left(\alpha-\frac{1}{\beta}\right)x(t)+\frac{1}{\beta}y(t)=0\\
x(0)=x_{0}~,\quad y(0)=(1-\alpha\beta)x_{0}-\beta^{2}\nabla F(x_{0})\end{cases}

In particular, as it was shown in
[
2
, Theorem
6.1
]
, systems (
DIN
) and (
g-DIN
) are equivalent (under an additional regularity assumption on
y(\cdot)
).
System (
g-DIN
) does not employ any second order information and thus is well defined for a broader class of functions, without requiring second-order differentiability of
F
.
Going even further system (
g-DIN
) can be generalized for non-differentiable convex functions involving the subdifferential instead of the gradient, or even to non-convex settings by considering the Clarke’s generalized subdifferential (see
[
27
, Definition
10.3
]
), see e.g.,
[
11
,
26
]
.

In
[
2
]
the authors established some basic results concerning systems (
DIN
) and (
g-DIN
) (and its generalized version involving a non-differential, convex objective function), including existence of solutions, (weak) convergence of trajectories to a critical point when
F
is (either convex, or) analytic, as also the exact equivalence relation between (
DIN
) and (
g-DIN
).

Furthermore, in the recent works
[
26
]
(see also
[
37
]
) the authors study the generalized version of system (
g-DIN
) for possibly non-convex, non-differentiable, Lipschitz and tame functions.
Under the
\mu
-Łojasiewicz conditioning of order
2
, they provide an abstract rate of convergence for
F(x(t))
, to a critical value
\bar{F}
of order
\mathcal{O}(e^{-c\mu t})
, for some
c\leq 2
and thus does not constitute an accelerated rate (see
[
24
, Theorem
13
]
). Under the additional assumption of strong convexity for the objective function
F
, the authors in
[
8
]
derive a rate of convergence of order
\mathcal{O}(e^{-\sqrt{\frac{\mu}{2}}t})
for a particular choice of
\alpha
and
\beta
, which however is not tight (see also Remark
3
in Section
3
). In addition, in the same (strongly convex) setting, the aforementioned rates are proven to be robust with respect to exogenous additive errors to the gradient of
F
in
[
9
]
. In a recent preprint
[
49
]
, the authors manage to improve the rate to a
\mathcal{O}(e^{-2\sqrt{\mu}t})
in the strongly convex setting, which however is proven for a rescaled version of (
DIN
).
Our findings further generalize and improve upon these works by i) considering a much broader setting without requiring (strong) convexity, but only the
\mu
-Łojasiewicz condition of order
2
and ii) providing explicit rate of convergence and stability estimates depending on the parameters
\alpha
and
\beta
, which is also optimal for an adequate choice of these parameters.

Finally in
[
24
]
(see also
[
37
]
) the authors provide a result showing that the trajectory generated by (
g-DIN
) almost surely avoids the set of strict saddle point of Morse functions (see
[
25
, Theorem & Corollary
3.1
]
and the associated discussion). We extend these results beyond the Morse setting to also account for the avoidance of connected components of saddle points, which are often a feature of relevant objective functions, stemming, for example, from the symmetries of the optimization task
[
47
]
.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section we give some basic definitions and tools that will be used throughout this work. We recall that we are interested in the following optimization problem

(1)

\underset{x\in\mathcal{H}}{\min}F(x)

where
F:\mathcal{H}\to\mathbb{R}
denotes the objective function that we want to minimize and
\mathcal{H}
is a Hilbert space endowed with a scalar product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
and its associated norm
\lVert\cdot\rVert=\sqrt{\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle}
.

2.1.
Main assumptions

Assumption 1
.

We make the following assumptions on the function
F:\mathcal{H}\to\mathbb{R}
:

(1)

The function
F:\mathcal{H}\to\mathbb{R}
is twice continuously differentiable.

(2)

F
is bounded from below and
\crit{F}=\left\{x\in\mathcal{H}~:~\nabla F(x)=0\right\}\neq\emptyset
.

Note that Assumption
1
guarantees the existence of a solution to the Cauchy initial value problem (
DIN
) with some initial conditions for
x(0)
and
\dot{x}(0)
, which may not be unique (by Peano’s existence Theorem, see e.g.
[
48
]
). By further assuming that
\nabla^{2}F
is locally Lipschitz, one can assure the uniqueness of the solution. However, since we are only interested in the (asymptotic) convergence properties of a trajectory, we will not employ the Lipschitz character of the Hessian, unless otherwise stated.

Throughout the paper, we also assume that the function
F
satisfies the
Łojasiewicz
condition, as defined below:

Assumption 2
.

The function
F
satisfies the Łojasiewicz condition of order
q\in(1,2]
, i.e. for every
\bar{x}\in\crit{F}
, there exists
\Omega\subset\mathcal{H}
and some positive constant
\mu>0
such that:

(
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathcal{L}}(q)
)

(\forall x\in\Omega)\quad:\qquad\lvert F(x)-F(\bar{x})\rvert\leq\frac{1}{2\mu}\lVert\nabla F(x)\rVert^{q}

We refer to

L

(
q
)

as
global
if
\Omega=\mathcal{H}
and as
local
if there exist
\delta>0
and

such that
\Omega={\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbb{B}}\mathopen{}\left(\bar{x},\delta\right)\cap[F<r]
.

The Łojasiewicz condition

L

(
q
)

[
34
]
(see also
[

,
35
]
), as a property of analytic functions and was later extended in
[

]

[

21

22

Initially, it was used as a key condition to guarantee convergence of the trajectory for classical dynamical systems (such as the gradient flow
[

[

,
23
,
20
,

,

and non-convex settings
[

,

,

]

[

,

,

,

,

,
28
,
50
]
.

Condition
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathcal{L}}(2)
is also frequently met in the related literature under the name

condition
[
44
,
45
,
32
]
(when

), as also

(see e.g.
[

]
), or

Indeed, while strong convexity is a potent property that yields powerful convergence results for first-order methods (such as strong convergence and linear rates of convergence), in most of the situations, it is not met in practice. Among others, some of the relaxations of the strong convexity property, include the notion of

[

. For a more detailed discussion of the above, we refer the reader to

Remark 4
.

[Comparison with previous works]
To the best of our knowledge the rates found in Theorem

and Corollary

for the system (

\mathcal{O}(e^{-2\sqrt{\mu}t})
is faster than the one found in

for strongly convex functions, which is
\mathcal{O}(t^{-\frac{\sqrt{\mu}}{2}t})

Since the strong convexity is a stronger notion than condition

, the difference between the two rates is due to the convergence analysis and in particular the choice of the Lyapunov energy (indeed our analysis can be trivially applied for strongly convex functions). It is also worth mentioning that the rate

was also established very recently in
[

in the strongly convex setting, which however holds for a rescaled version of system (

) (see
[

, Corollary

]
). Notice that the optimal rate

, as expressed in (

is obtained by choosing

and

. This choice contrasts the one made in

, Theorem

]
with

,

and indicates why the former (i.e.
\alpha=\sqrt{\mu}
,
\beta=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu}}
) is a better choice, leading to a faster worst-case rate. Indeed, this choice suggests to apply a more aggressive policy concerning the isotropic damping term (i.e. taking
\alpha
smaller) and enforce a stronger geometric damping (i.e. taking

larger). The difference on the performance based on these two policies is also witnessed numerically in the example of minimization of quadratic functions, as illustrated in Section

(see Figure

).

Finally, concerning the non-convex setting, under
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathcal{L}}(2)
, by inspecting the proof of
[

, Theorem

]
(see also
[
37
, Theorem

]
) , one can derive a linear rate of convergence result for

, which is not explicit and thus a straightforward comparison is difficult to be made. However, by following the lines of the aforementioned proof, our computations reveal a linear rate of the form

, which is worse than the one in

, for small values of

Remark 5
.

[Comparison with the heavy ball with friction dynamics]
It is worth mentioning that the optimal worst-case rate
\mathcal{O}(e^{-2\sqrt{\mu}t})
found in Corollary

, holds true in a largely more general setting than the strongly convex one. In contrast, for the plain heavy ball system (

\mathcal{O}(e^{-2\sqrt{\mu}t})
is only proved for strongly convex and twice differentiable functions
[

the associated proven linear rate is strictly slower than

This observation makes more clear quantitatively the advantages of the system (

3.3.
Almost sure avoidance of strict saddle points

As we have seen in Proposition
1
, under the Łojasiewicz condition, the dynamics of (
DIN
) are attracted to critical points.
This does not exclude attraction by saddle points, i.e. critical points that are not local minima, which makes sense, since there exist trajectories with such limit points.
However, these trajectories are expected to represent exceptional, atypical instances in the sense that it is improbable that a ball balances on top of a hill, or even on a saddle.
This concept of atypicality can be captured in the vanishing Lebesgue measure of initial conditions attracted by (strict) saddle points
[
40
]
.
Although such an investigation has previously been carried out with regard to (
DIN
) or (
g-DIN
) in
[
24
,
37
]
and the relevant tools were developed for a discretization of DIN, there has been no presentation of the almost sure avoidance of strict saddle points in the presence of connected components of (strict) saddle points for the continuous-in-time system.
For concreteness, we use the following definition

Definition 1
.

A strict saddle point is a point
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
\nabla F(x)=0
and
\nabla^{2}F(x)
has a negative eigenvalue.

The following theorem captures that, under the
C^{2}
differentiability and the Łojasiewicz condition, for almost all initial conditions, the solution of (
DIN
) does not converge to strict saddle points.
From this result and the guaranteed convergence, provided by Proposition
1
, it follows that strict saddle points are collectively avoided by the dynamics of (
DIN
).
Specifically, the following theorem does not require that the critical points are isolated in any way.

Theorem 6
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{H}=\mathbb{R}^{d}
and that
F
satisfies Assumptions
1
and
2
.
Then, the set of all pairs
(x,v)
with
x
and
v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
that belong to a solution-trajectory of (
DIN
) with a strict saddle point in their limit points is of Lebesgue measure
0
.

The proof is provided in Section
4.3
.

Remark 11
.

Actually, the proof of Theorem
6
reveals that the above result also holds for solution-trajectories of (
g-DIN
) under the same assumptions.

4.3.
Proof of Theorem
6

In this subsection we will prove Theorem
6
, which guarantees avoidance of strict saddle points by the dynamics of (
DIN
).
Actually, we will infer this result from the avoidance of strict saddle points by (
g-DIN
).
The line of approach will be by applying lemma
4
, which provides conditions for the avoidance of strict saddle points, for general autonomous first-order ODEs.
Before we proceed, we introduce some notation for the set of fixed points with strong escape directions,
\mathcal{S}
, and the set of initial conditions attracted by them,
\mathcal{M}_{s}
.
Given a function
f\in\mathbb{R}^{D}\to\mathbb{R}
, consider a
D
-dimensional autonomous first-order ODE of general form

(65)

\dot{z}=f(z),\qquad z\in\mathbb{R}^{D}

we can use the following definitions

Definition 2
.

The set of fixed points of (
65
) with strong escape directions is defined to be

(66)

\mathcal{S}(f)=\{\bar{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{D}:f(\bar{z})=0\ \&\ \nabla f(\bar{z})\ \text{has a strictly positive eigenvalue}\}

and the set of all initial conditions such that their solution-trajectories converge to
\mathcal{S}
is defined through

(67)

\mathcal{M}_{s}(f)=\{z_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{D}:\text{if}\ z(0)=z_{0}\ \text{then under \eqref{eq:ODE}}\ z(t)\to\bar{z}\in\mathcal{S}\}.

We will often omit the explicit dependence on
f
.

Given these definitions we can state the following theorem

Lemma 4
.

Assuming
f\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{D})
and that all solution-trajectories of (
65
) converge, the set of all initial conditions that, under (
65
), are attracted by fixed points with strong escape directions,
\mathcal{M}_{s}(f)
, is of Lebesgue measure 0.

The proof of the above lemma is based on Carr’s Theorem
[
42
]
and it is provided in Appendix
C
.

We aim to apply the above lemma to (
g-DIN
), which means for
D=2d
and
z=(x,y)
with

(68)

f(x,y)=\begin{pmatrix}-\beta\nabla F(x)-\left(\alpha-\frac{1}{\beta}\right)x-\frac{1}{\beta}y\\
-\left(\alpha-\frac{1}{\beta}\right)x-\frac{1}{\beta}y\end{pmatrix}

To this end, we need to connect the strict saddle points of
F
with the set
\mathcal{M}_{s}
whose avoidance is provided by lemma
4
.
Since the fixed points of (
65
), with
f
as defined in (
68
), are the points
(\bar{x},(1-\alpha\beta)\bar{x})
for all critical points
\bar{x}
of
F
, we need to connect the eigenvalues of
\nabla^{2}F(\bar{x})
with the eigenvalues of
D=\nabla f(\bar{x},(1-\alpha\beta)\bar{x})
.
Given the following representation

(69)

D=-\begin{pmatrix}\beta\nabla^{2}F(\bar{x})&\mathbb{0}_{d}\\
\mathbb{0}_{d}&\mathbb{0}_{d}\end{pmatrix}-\frac{1}{\beta}\begin{pmatrix}\left(\alpha\beta-1\right)\mathbb{I}_{d}&\mathbb{I}_{d}\\
\left(\alpha\beta-1\right)\mathbb{I}_{d}&\mathbb{I}_{d}\end{pmatrix},

we can derive the following lemma, which is also implicit in
[
24
]

Lemma 5

(
[
24
]
)
.

For any distinct eigenvalue
\lambda_{0}
of
\nabla^{2}F(\bar{x})
, define
\lambda_{+}
and
\lambda_{-}

(70)

\lambda_{\pm}=\frac{1}{2}\left(-(a+\beta\lambda_{0})\pm\sqrt{(a+\beta\lambda_{0})^{2}-4\lambda_{0}}\right)

Then,
\lambda_{+}
and

are two distinct eigenvalues of

Specifically, if
\nabla^{2}F_{0}(\bar{x})

has a strictly positive eigenvalue.

Finally, we need the following lemma which shows that the mapping between the systems (
DIN
) and (

) is given by

(71)

h(x,v)=(x,(1-\alpha\beta)x-\beta^{2}\nabla F(x)-\beta v)

Lemma 6
.

[
2

]

Assume

.
Then
\{(x(t),v(t))\}_{t=0}^{+\infty}
is a solution-trajectory for (

Now we have all the tools to proceed with a sort proof of Theorem

Proof of Theorem
6
.
.

This proof is an application of lemma
4
for the first order system (
g-DIN
) as described above.
Since we assume
F\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, clearly we have
f\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
Additionally, from the Łojasiewicz assumption and Proposition
1
, all trajectories of (
DIN

(\bar{x},0)
.
By the equivalence of the two systerms (
DIN
) and (
g-DIN
) for
F\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
(provided by lemma
6

(

Specifically, if
\bar{x}

6.
Concluding remarks

In this work, we studied system (
DIN
) in a smooth, non-convex setting, under the Łojasiewicz condition (Assumption
2
). In particular we derived exponential decay estimates for the gap
F(x(t))-F(\bar{x})
, that are optimal under appropriate tuning for the friction coefficients
\alpha
and
\beta
, which are improved with respect to previous related works. Moreover we showed that these estimates are robust with respect to external perturbation errors. Finally, in the same setting, we provided an almost sure convergence result of the solution to (
DIN
) (equivalently (
g-DIN
)) to local minima of the objective function
F
.

An interesting future direction consists in extending all the above results to the algorithms associated to (
DIN
) (or (
g-DIN
)), which can be seen as their finite difference scheme and thus may inherit their convergence properties.

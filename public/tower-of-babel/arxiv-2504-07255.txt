A Tail-Respecting Explicit Numerical Scheme for Lévy-Driven SDEs With Superlinear Drifts
Olga Aryasova, Oleksii Kulyk and Ilya Pavlyukevich

Abstract
We present an explicit numerical approximation scheme, denoted by { X n } \{X^{n}\} , for the effective simulation of solutions X X to a multivariate stochastic differential equation (SDE) with a superlinearly growing κ \kappa -dissipative drift, where κ > 1 \kappa>1 , driven by a multiplicative heavy-tailed Lévy process that has a finite p p -th moment, with p > 0 p>0 . We show that for any q ∈ ( 0 , p + κ − 1 ) q\in(0,p+\kappa-1) , the strong L q L^{q} -convergence sup t ∈ [ 0 , T ] 𝐄 ​ ‖ X t n − X t ‖ q = 𝒪 ⁡ ( h n γ ) \sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbf{E}\|X^{n}_{t}-X_{t}\|^{q}=\mathcal{O}(h_{n}^{\gamma}) holds true, in particular, our numerical scheme preserves the q q -moments of the solution beyond the order p p . Additionally, for any q ∈ ( 0 , p ) q\in(0,p) we establish strong uniform convergence: 𝐄 ​ sup t ∈ [ 0 , T ] ‖ X t n − X t ‖ q = 𝒪 ⁡ ( h n δ q uc ) \mathbf{E}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|X^{n}_{t}-X_{t}\|^{q}=\mathcal{O}(h_{n}^{\delta_{q}^{\mathrm{uc}}}) . In both cases we determine the convergence rates γ \gamma and δ q uc \delta_{q}^{\mathrm{uc}} . In the special case of SDEs driven solely by a Brownian motion, our numerical scheme preserves superexponential moments of the solution. The scheme { X n } \{X^{n}\} is realized as a combination of a well-known Euler method with a Lie–Trotter type splitting technique.



Keywords:

stochastic differential equation, heavy tails, splitting method, explicit numerical scheme,
strong convergence, superlinear drift, dissipative drift, one-sided Lipschitz condition, convergence rate.

1.1
Setting and results

In this paper we focus on strong numerical approximations of solutions of an Itô SDE with multiplicative noise

\displaystyle X_{t}=x

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}A(X_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}a(X_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}b(X_{s})\,\mathrm{d}B_{s}+\int_{0}^{t}c(X_{s-})\,\mathrm{d}Z_{s},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\ t\in[0,\infty),

(1.1)

driven by a
d

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\langle\lambda,Z_{t}\rangle}=\exp\Big(t\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\big(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\langle\lambda,z\rangle}-1-\mathrm{i}\langle\lambda,z\rangle\mathbb{I}(\|z\|\leq 1)\big)\,\nu(\mathrm{d}z)\Big),\quad\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\ t\in[0,\infty).

\displaystyle\langle A(x),x\rangle\leq-C_{1}\|x\|^{1+\kappa}+C_{2},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(1.3)

\mathbf{H}_{a,b,c}^{\mathrm{Lip}_{b}}

\mathbf{H}_{\nu,p}

Since the generator of the Markov process defined by (

Note that in SDE (

), the superlinear drift

\widetilde{A}:=A+a

\mathbf{H}^{\mathrm{diss}}_{A}

Therefore, without loss of generality, we could have assumed that

.
However, in certain situations it will be convenient to have the option of splitting the “effective drift”

into two components by isolating a globally Lipschitz continuous and bounded part, as illustrated in Example

The most straightforward method for approximating solutions of the SDE (
1.1
) is the explicit Euler scheme.
For
T\in[0,\infty)
, let

[0,T]
be a fixed time interval. We consider a family of equidistant partitions
\{t^{n}_{k},\ k=0,\ldots,n\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

\displaystyle t^{n}_{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{k}{n}T,\quad k=0,\ldots,n,

(1.5)

and the step size

\displaystyle h_{n}

\displaystyle=t^{n}_{k+1}-t^{n}_{k}=\frac{T}{n}.

(1.6)

Then the explicit Euler scheme for (
1.1
) reads

\displaystyle X^{\mathrm{E},n}_{0}

\displaystyle=x,

(1.7)

\displaystyle X^{\mathrm{E},n}_{t_{k+1}^{n}}

\displaystyle=X^{\mathrm{E},n}_{t_{k}^{n}}+\widetilde{A}(X^{\mathrm{E},n}_{t_{k}^{n}})h_{n}+b(X^{\mathrm{E},n}_{t_{k}^{n}})(B_{t_{k+1}^{n}}-B_{t_{k}^{n}})+c(X^{\mathrm{E},n}_{t_{k}^{n}})(Z_{t_{k+1}^{n}}-Z_{t_{k}^{n}}),\quad k=0,\dots,n-1.

For
c\equiv 0
, i.e., in the absence of jumps, the SDE
(
1.1

[

,

\mathbf{E}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|X_{t}\|^{r}<\infty

The Euler scheme

, any

and any
\varepsilon>0

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbf{P}\Big(\max_{0\leq kh\leq T}\|X^{\mathrm{E},n}_{kh}-X_{kh}\|>\varepsilon\Big)=0.

(1.8)

However in practice, computer implementations of the explicit Euler scheme (
1.7
)

Moreover, as demonstrated in Theorem 2.1 in
[
HJK11
]
,
the individual absolute moments of Euler approximations
diverge to infinity, i.e.,
\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbf{E}\|X^{\mathrm{E},n}_{T}\|^{r}=\infty
.

In the continuous case,
c\equiv 0
, the deficiencies of the explicit Euler method can be addressed in two main ways.
First, one can employ split-step backward Euler methods (or, more generally, implicit methods); see

[
KP95
,
Sch02
,
HMS02
]
.
These methods require increased computational effort and will not be considered further in this work.
An alternative approach, proposed in
[
HJK12
]
, is the
tamed explicit Euler scheme

\displaystyle X^{\mathrm{TE},n}_{0}

\displaystyle=x,

(1.9)

\displaystyle X^{\mathrm{TE},n}_{t_{k+1}^{n}}

\displaystyle=X^{\mathrm{TE},n}_{t_{k}^{n}}+\frac{\widetilde{A}(X^{\mathrm{TE},n}_{t_{k}^{n}})h_{n}}{1+\|\widetilde{A}(X^{\mathrm{TE},n}_{t_{k}^{n}})\|h_{n}}+b(X^{\mathrm{TE},n}_{t_{k}^{n}})(B_{t_{k+1}^{n}}-B_{t_{k}^{n}}),\quad k=0,\dots,n-1.

Since the drift in (

) is bounded, the NaN-problem no longer arises.
Moreover, under an additional assumption that the superlinear part of the drift

one-sided Lipschitz condition (see condition

r\in(0,\infty)
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|X^{\mathrm{TE},n}_{kh}-X_{kh}\|^{r}\leq_{C}h_{n}^{r/2},

(1.10)

see Theorem 1.1 in
[
HJK12
]
. A comprehensive analysis of various generalizations of the
tamed Euler scheme (
1.9
) is provided in
[
HJ15
]
.

In the heavy tail case, i.e., when
c(\cdot)\not\equiv 0
and under assumption

\mathbf{H}_{\nu,p}
,
the challenges known from the continuous setting become even more pronounced.
First, due to the presence of heavy tails,
NaN outputs from the explicit Euler scheme occur more frequently compared to the Gaussian case; see Example
1.9
below.
Second, unlike in the Gaussian case, the true solution
X

generally does not possess all absolute moments.
Furthermore, in the presence of heavy-tailed jump noise,
the tail and moment structure of the solution
X

becomes an inherent feature of the system. We now state the following result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let assumptions
\mathbf{H}^{\mathrm{diss}}_{A}
,

\mathbf{H}_{a,b,c}^{\mathrm{Lip}_{b}}
, and
\mathbf{H}_{\nu,p}
hold true. Then for each
p_{X}\in(0,p+\kappa-1)
, the
solution
X
to (
1.1
) satisfies

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,\infty)}\mathbf{E}\|X_{t}\|^{p_{X}}\leq_{C}1+\|x\|^{p_{X}}.

(1.11)

This theorem reveals a general phenomenon:
a
\kappa
-dissipative drift transforms the
p
-moment of the noise into (approximately) a
(p+\kappa-1)
-moment of the
solution
X
.
This effect has been studied extensively; for a discussion of the relevant literature,
see Section 1 in
[
KP21
]
. In particular, it is known that the upper bound
p+\kappa-1
is sharp, see
[
KP21
, Section 2.6]
.

The entire line of research presented in this paper is motivated by the following natural question:

How can one construct an explicit numerical approximation scheme for solutions of (
1.1
),
that does not explode and captures (respects) the tail behavior
of the true solution
X
, i.e., preserves all the existing moments of the solution
X
?

Addressing this question is far from straightforward, as several intuitive candidates —
such as the explicit Euler or the tamed explicit Euler schemes —
fail to accurately reproduce the moment behaviour in the range
p_{X}\in(p,p+\kappa-1)
.
For the tamed explicit Euler scheme this failure can be demonstrated
as follows. Assume, for definiteness, that
\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\|c(x)\|>0
, and let
q\in(p,\infty)
be such that

\int_{\|z\|>1}\|z\|^{q}\nu(\mathrm{d}z)=+\infty
. Since the drift in the tamed Euler scheme
is bounded from above by
1
, it follows that
the conditional expectation of the scheme’s
q
-th moment becomes infinite:

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\Big[\|X^{\mathrm{TE},n}_{t_{k+1}^{n}}\|^{q}\Big|\mathcal{F}_{t_{k}^{n}}\Big]\geq_{C}\|X^{\mathrm{TE},n}_{t_{k}^{n}}\|^{q}-1+\mathbf{E}\|Z_{t_{k+1}^{n}}-Z_{t_{k}^{n}}\|^{q}=+\infty\quad\mathrm{a.s.}

(1.12)

In this paper, we propose a novel explicit approximation scheme, which is
tail-respecting
the sense described above.
The scheme combines the operator splitting approach (specifically, the Lie–Trotter method) for semigroup approximation with Euler-type discretization.
More precisely, the solution
X
is approximated in two steps by decoupling
the deterministic dynamics governed by the superlinearly growing vector field
A

from the remaining components. The resulting scheme is as follows.

Let
\Phi=\Phi(t,x)
,
t\in[0,\infty)
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, be the unique solution of the ODE

\displaystyle\dot{\Phi}(t,x)

\displaystyle=A(\Phi(t,x)),

(1.13)

\displaystyle\Phi(0,x)

\displaystyle=x,

see Section
2
for the properties of the mapping
\Phi
.
Then we set

\displaystyle X_{0}^{n}

\displaystyle:=x,

(1.14)

\displaystyle Y^{n}_{t^{n}_{k+1}}

\displaystyle:=X^{n}_{t^{n}_{k}}+a(X^{n}_{t^{n}_{k}})h_{n}+b(X^{n}_{t^{n}_{k}})(B_{t^{n}_{k+1}}-B_{t^{n}_{k}})+c(X^{n}_{t^{n}_{k}})(Z_{t^{n}_{k+1}}-Z_{t^{n}_{k}}),

\displaystyle X^{n}_{t^{n}_{k+1}}

\displaystyle:=\Phi(h_{n},Y^{n}_{t^{n}_{k+1}}),\quad k=0,\ldots,n-1.

As we can see, our scheme slightly deviates from the Lie–Trotter splitting
in which the function
\Phi

would be composed
with the solution of the SDE

\displaystyle Y_{t}

\displaystyle:=X^{n}_{t^{n}_{k}}+\int_{t^{n}_{k}}^{t}a(Y_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s+\int_{t^{n}_{k}}^{t}b(Y_{s})\,\mathrm{d}B_{s}+\int_{t^{n}_{k}}^{t}c(Y_{s-})\,\mathrm{d}Z_{s},\quad t\in[t^{n}_{k},t^{n}_{k+1}].

Instead, in (

As is common in such cases, it will be more convenient to work with
the

1.14

\displaystyle\eta_{0}^{n}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle:=t^{n}_{k},\quad t\in(t^{n}_{k},t^{n}_{k+1}],\quad k\in\mathbb{N}_{0},

\displaystyle\delta^{n}_{t}:=t-\eta_{t}^{n},\quad B^{n}_{t}:=B_{t}-B_{\eta^{n}_{t}},\quad Z^{n}_{t}:=Z_{t}-Z_{\eta^{n}_{t}}.

The continuous time version of (
1.14

\displaystyle X_{0}^{n}

\displaystyle:=x,

(1.18)

\displaystyle Y^{n}_{t}

\displaystyle:=X^{n}_{\eta^{n}_{t}}+a(X^{n}_{\eta^{n}_{t}})\delta^{n}_{t}+b(X^{n}_{\eta^{n}_{t}})B^{n}_{t}+c(X^{n}_{\eta^{n}_{t}})Z^{n}_{t},

\displaystyle X^{n}_{t}

\displaystyle:=\Phi(\delta_{t}^{n},Y^{n}_{t}),\quad t\in[0,T].

We study the schemes (
1.14
) and (

) under the following additional assumptions on the drift

.

\mathbf{H}_{A}^{\text{Lip}_{+}}
The function
A\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
is continuous
and satisfies the one-sided Lipschitz condition, i.e.,
there is
L\in(0,\infty)

\displaystyle\langle A(x)-A(y),x-y\rangle\leq L\|x-y\|^{2},\quad x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

\mathbf{H}_{A_{x},A_{xx}}
:

.
There exists
\chi\in[0,\infty)
such that

\displaystyle\|A_{x}(x)\|

\displaystyle\leq_{C}1+\|x\|^{\chi},

and there exists
\varepsilon\in(0,\infty)
such that

\displaystyle\varepsilon\|\varphi\|\sum_{k=1}^{d}\|A^{k}_{xx}(x)\||\varphi^{k}|+\langle A_{x}(x)\varphi,\varphi\rangle\leq_{C}\|\varphi\|^{2},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\ \varphi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(1.21)

We notice that in dimension one,

, (
1.19
) is equivalent to the estimate

\displaystyle A^{\prime}(x)\leq L,\quad x\in\mathbb{R},

(1.22)

whereas (
1.21
) is equivalent to the estimate

\displaystyle|A^{\prime\prime}(x)|\leq_{C}1+(A^{\prime}(x))_{-},\quad x\in\mathbb{R},

see further discussion and examples in Section

Our first main result is the following

Theorem 1.2
.

Let assumptions

\mathbf{H}^{\mathrm{diss}}_{A}
,

\mathbf{H}_{a,b,c}^{\mathrm{Lip}_{b}}
,

\mathbf{H}_{\nu,p},

\mathbf{H}_{A}^{\mathrm{Lip}_{+}}
, and

\mathbf{H}_{A_{x},A_{xx}}
,
hold true.
Then for any
T\in[0,\infty)
and any

p_{X}\in(0,p+\kappa-1)

\displaystyle\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\sup_{t\in[0,\infty)}\mathbf{E}\|X^{n}_{t}\|^{p_{X}}\leq_{C}1+\|x\|^{p_{X}},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(1.24)

Theorem
1.2
demonstrates that the scheme
(
1.18
) preserves the same tail-improving effect as the original SDE (
1.1
).
It is important to emphasize that this property is quite delicate and is not shared
by the tamed Euler scheme or other potential schemes, which we briefly discuss below.

It is well known, that the order of terms in Lie–Trotter approximations can be arbitrary. Therefore,
one may also consider the reverse variants of (
1.14
):

\displaystyle\widetilde{X}_{0}^{n}

\displaystyle:=x,

(1.25)

\displaystyle\widetilde{Y}^{n}_{t^{n}_{k+1}}

\displaystyle:=\Phi(h_{n},\widetilde{X}^{n}_{t^{n}_{k}}),

\displaystyle\widetilde{X}^{n}_{t^{n}_{k+1}}

\displaystyle:=\widetilde{Y}^{n}_{t^{n}_{k+1}}+a(\widetilde{X}^{n}_{t^{n}_{k}})h_{n}+b(\widetilde{X}^{n}_{t^{n}_{k}})(B_{t^{n}_{k+1}}-B_{t^{n}_{k}})+c(\widetilde{X}^{n}_{t^{n}_{k}})(Z_{t^{n}_{k+1}}-Z_{t^{n}_{k}}),\quad k=0,\ldots,n-1,

and

\displaystyle\widehat{X}_{0}^{n}

\displaystyle:=x,

(1.26)

\displaystyle\widehat{Y}^{n}_{t^{n}_{k+1}}

\displaystyle:=\Phi(h_{n},\widehat{X}^{n}_{t^{n}_{k}}),

\displaystyle\widehat{X}^{n}_{t^{n}_{k+1}}

\displaystyle:=\widehat{Y}^{n}_{t^{n}_{k+1}}+a(\widehat{Y}^{n}_{t^{n}_{k+1}})h_{n}+b(\widehat{Y}^{n}_{t^{n}_{k+1}})(B_{t^{n}_{k+1}}-B_{t^{n}_{k}})+c(\widehat{Y}^{n}_{t^{n}_{k+1}})(Z_{t^{n}_{k+1}}-Z_{t^{n}_{k}}),\quad k=0,\ldots,n-1.

The schemes (
1.25
) and (
1.26
) share the same structure as the tamed Euler scheme, in that their right-hand sides
contain summands of the form

c(\widetilde{X}^{n}_{t^{n}_{k}})(Z_{t^{n}_{k+1}}-Z_{t^{n}_{k}})
and

c(\widehat{Y}^{n}_{t^{n}_{k+1}})(Z_{t^{n}_{k+1}}-Z_{t^{n}_{k}})
, respectively.
These summands have the same moments as the process
Z
.
Therefore, neither (
1.25
), nor (
1.26
) preserve the moments of
X
.

Moreover, neither the tamed Euler scheme, nor the schemes (
1.25
), (
1.26
) possess the necessary structure
to satisfy the analogue of Theorem
1.2
.
The theoretical explanation for this lies in the fact that both Theorems
1.1
and
1.2

are derived from the general result in Theorem 2.12 of
[
KP21
]
,
which provides exact bounds on the tails of Itô semimartingales with dissipative drifts and heavy-tailed jumps.
From this perspective, the scheme (
1.14
), (
1.18
)
is the only one among those discussed that, while formulated in the canonical semimartingale form,
aligns with the framework of Theorem 2.12 in
[
KP21
]
.

We analyze the rate of convergence of the scheme (
1.14
), (
1.18
)
in two steps. The first step focuses on the
L^{q}
-convergence rates for
q\in(0,p)
.
This step does not require
\kappa
-dissipativity and does not involve the tail-improving effect.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let assumptions

\mathbf{H}^{\mathrm{Lip}_{+}}_{A}
,

\mathbf{H}_{A_{x},A_{xx}}
,

\mathbf{H}_{a,b,c}^{\mathrm{Lip}_{b}}
, and

\mathbf{H}_{\nu,p}

hold true. Then for any
T\in[0,\infty)
and
q\in(0,p)
there is convergence

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbf{E}\|X_{t}^{n}-X_{t}\|^{q}\leq_{C}h_{n}^{\delta_{q}}(1+\|x\|^{p}),

(1.27)

with the convergence rate

\displaystyle\delta_{q}=\frac{p-q}{\chi}\wedge\frac{q}{2}\wedge 1.

(1.28)

In particular, if
\chi=0
, i.e., if the drift
A
has bounded first derivatives, the convergence rate reduces to

\displaystyle\delta_{q}=\frac{q}{2}\wedge 1.

(1.29)

Within the framework of Theorem
1.3
,
the uniform convergence of

\mathbf{E}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|X_{t}^{n}-X_{t}\|^{q}
can also be established,
although with a slightly weaker approximation rate. Specifically, we have the following result.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let assumptions

\mathbf{H}^{\mathrm{Lip}_{+}}_{A}
,

\mathbf{H}_{A_{x},A_{xx}}
,

\mathbf{H}_{a,b,c}^{\mathrm{Lip}_{b}}
, and

\mathbf{H}_{\nu,p}

hold true.
Then for any
T\in[0,\infty)
and
q\in(0,p)
there is uniform convergence

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|X_{t}^{n}-X_{t}\|^{q}\leq_{C}h_{n}^{\delta_{q}^{\mathrm{uc}}}(1+\|x\|^{p}),

(1.30)

with the convergence rate

\displaystyle\delta_{q}^{\mathrm{uc}}=\frac{p-q}{\chi}\wedge\frac{q}{4}\wedge\frac{1}{2}.

(1.31)

Since Theorem
1.3
and
1.4
focus solely on the moments of the Lévy process
Z
,
we strongly believe that analogous results should hold for the tamed Euler scheme as well as the reverse splitting schemes
(
1.25
) and (
1.26
).

The following theorem, which is the second main result of the paper, is specific to the scheme
(
1.14
), (
1.18
)
and combines the tail-improving effect with convergence rates.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let assumptions
\mathbf{H}^{\mathrm{diss}}_{A}
,
\mathbf{H}_{a,b,c}^{\mathrm{Lip}_{b}}
,

\mathbf{H}_{\nu,p},

\mathbf{H}_{A}^{\mathrm{Lip}_{+}}
,
and

\mathbf{H}_{A_{x},A_{xx}}
hold true.
Then for any
T\in[0,\infty)
, any

p_{X}\in[p,p+\kappa-1)

and any

\displaystyle\gamma<\begin{cases}\frac{p(p+\kappa-1-p_{X})}{(\chi+2)(\kappa-1)+\chi p},&\quad p\leq\chi+2,\\
\frac{p+\kappa-1-p_{X}}{\kappa+\chi-1},&\quad p>\chi+2,\end{cases}

(1.32)

we have

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbf{E}\|X^{n}_{t}-X_{t}\|^{p_{X}}\leq_{C}h_{n}^{\gamma}(1+\|x\|^{p+\kappa-1}).

(1.33)

We note that the tail-improving effect from Theorems
1.1
and
1.2

does not apply to

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|X_{t}\|
and
\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|X_{t}^{n}\|
because the large jumps of the noise

,
while not visible at every fixed

, cause

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|X_{t}\|^{q}=\mathbf{E}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|X_{t}^{n}\|^{q}=+\infty

(1.34)

for any
q\in(p,\infty)
such that
\int_{\|z\|>1}\|z\|^{q}\nu(\mathrm{d}z)=+\infty
.
Our proof of Theorem
1.5
relies on an interpolation between the convergence

and the moment bounds from Theorems

and

.

to hold for

The tail-improving effect can also be observed in the continuous case

.
Specifically, the following results hold.
Let

be the constant from the condition
\mathbf{H}^{\mathrm{diss}}_{A}
, and let

\displaystyle\Lambda=\frac{2C_{\mathrm{diss}}}{\sup_{x}\|b(x)\|^{2}}\in(0,\infty).

(1.35)

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
c\equiv 0
and let assumptions

\mathbf{H}^{\mathrm{diss}}_{A}
and

\mathbf{H}_{a,b,c}^{\mathrm{Lip}_{b}}

hold true.
Then for any

and
\lambda\in(0,\Lambda)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbf{E}\Big[\mathrm{e}^{\frac{\lambda}{1+\kappa}\|X_{t}\|^{1+\kappa}}\Big]\leq_{C}\mathrm{e}^{\frac{\lambda}{1+\kappa}\|x\|^{1+\kappa}}

and

1.6
demonstrates that a
\kappa

super-exponential tails with index

. The following theorem,
similar to Theorem

.

Let
c\equiv 0
and let assumptions

\mathbf{H}^{\mathrm{diss}}_{A}

\mathbf{H}_{a,b,c}^{\mathrm{Lip}_{b}}

(1.39)

The final theorem in this section provides the rate of convergence of our splitting scheme,
accounting for the super-exponential tails with index

The next example demonstrates why we allow the “effective drift” to be decomposed into the components

A(\cdot)
and
a(\cdot)
.

Example 1.10
.

Consider a one dimensional SDE

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}X_{t}=-X_{t}^{3}\,\mathrm{d}t+\sin X_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t+\mathrm{d}Z_{t},\quad X_{0}=x\in\mathbb{R}.

(1.52)

with
Z
being a Lévy process that satisfies assumption
\mathbf{H}_{\nu,p}
.
By taking
A(x)=-x^{3}+\sin x
,
a=b\equiv 0
,
c\equiv 1,
we meet a necessity to solve an ODE
\dot{\Phi}=-\Phi+\sin\Phi
.
This cannot be done explicitly, and one needs
to use additional stable numerical approximation methods for deterministic ODEs here, see, e.g.,
[
HS94
]
.
On the other hand, the same SDE can be treated as (
1.1
) with
A(x)=-x^{3}
,
a(x)=\sin x
. This results in the following
simple explicit numerical scheme:

\displaystyle X^{n}_{0}

\displaystyle=x,

(1.53)

\displaystyle Y^{n}_{t_{k+1}^{n}}

\displaystyle=X^{n}_{t_{k}^{n}}+h_{n}\sin(X^{n}_{t_{k}^{n}})+Z_{t_{k+1}^{n}}-Z_{t_{k}^{n}},

\displaystyle X^{n}_{t_{k+1}^{n}}

\displaystyle=\frac{Y^{n}_{t_{k+1}^{n}}}{\sqrt{2h_{n}(Y^{n}_{t_{k+1}^{n}})^{2}+1}},\quad k=0,\dots,n-1.

We conclude this section with several remarks and examples regarding the assumptions imposed on the function
A
. First, we observe that
the dissipativity condition
\mathbf{H}^{\mathrm{diss}}_{A}

together with the growth condition (
1.20
) implies that

\displaystyle\kappa\leq\chi+1.

(1.54)

A typical example of this is a polynomial drift.

Example 1.11
.

Let
A\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be a polynomial of an odd order,

\displaystyle A(x)

\displaystyle=-x^{2n+1}+c_{2n}x^{2n}+\cdots+c_{1}x+c_{0},

(1.55)

\displaystyle A^{\prime}(x)

\displaystyle=-(2n+1)x^{2n}+o(x^{2n}),

\displaystyle A^{\prime\prime}(x)

\displaystyle=-2n(2n+1)x^{2n-1}+o(x^{2n-1}),

so that
\kappa=2n+1
,
\chi=2n
, and the
inequality (
1.54
) turns into equality.
Conditions
\mathbf{H}_{A}^{\mathrm{Lip}_{+}}
and (
1.23
) are satisfied, too. Actually, in this case

\displaystyle A^{\prime\prime}(x)=o((A^{\prime}(x))_{-}),\quad|x|\to\infty.

(1.56)

We note that there are examples of drifts
A
satisfying all the assumptions of Theorem
1.2
,
for which the inequality
(
1.54
) is strict. However, we do not provide these examples here,
as they do not arise naturally in practice. Nevertheless, this observation helps to explain why we retain both parameters

\kappa
and
\chi
in the formulation of our main results

In the following example,
A
is allowed to have highly oscillatory components,
which, unlike in the polynomial case, make
A^{\prime\prime}(x)
comparable with
(A^{\prime}(x))_{-}
.

Example 1.12
.

Let
A(x)=-x^{3}+\sin x^{2}
,
x\in\mathbb{R}
. Then

\displaystyle A^{\prime}(x)=-3x^{2}+2x\cos x^{2},\quad A^{\prime\prime}(x)=-6x-4x^{2}\sin x^{2}+2\cos x^{2},

(1.57)

so that

\displaystyle\limsup_{|x|\to\infty}\frac{A^{\prime\prime}(x)}{(A^{\prime}(x))_{-}}=\frac{4}{3}>0.

(1.58)

Both assumptions
\mathbf{H}_{A}^{\mathrm{Lip}_{+}}
and
\mathbf{H}_{A_{x},A_{xx}}
are satisfied in this case with
\chi=2
,
and
\mathbf{H}^{\mathrm{diss}}_{A}
is satisfied with
\kappa=3
. Since
x\mapsto\sin x^{2}
is not globally Lipschitz,
we cannot identify it with the function
a(\cdot)
.

Next, we give several multivariate examples. It is known that the one-sided Lipschitz condition (
1.19
)
for
A
is equivalent to
the boundnedness of the symmetrized (distributional) gradient matrix
A_{x}
from above, i.e.,

\displaystyle[A_{x}(x)]_{\mathrm{sym}}:=\frac{A_{x}(x)+A_{x}(x)^{T}}{2}\leq L\cdot\text{Id},

(1.59)

which means that for any
\varphi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

\displaystyle\langle A_{x}(x)\varphi,\varphi\rangle\leq L\|\varphi\|^{2},

(1.60)

see Lemma 2.2 in
[
BJM05
]
. In dimension
d=1
, this simply means that
A^{\prime}(x)\leq L
,
x\in\mathbb{R}
.

Example 1.13
.

Let
f\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},\mathbb{R})
and let

\displaystyle A(x)=-f(\|x\|^{2})x,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(1.61)

Then

\displaystyle A_{x}(x)=-f(\|x\|^{2})\text{Id}-2f^{\prime}(\|x\|^{2})(x\otimes x),

(1.62)

and

\displaystyle A^{k}_{xx}(x)=-2x_{k}f^{\prime}(\|x\|^{2})\text{Id}-4f^{\prime}(\|x\|^{2})(x\otimes\mathrm{e}_{k})-4x_{k}f^{\prime\prime}(\|x\|^{2})(x\otimes x),

(1.63)

where
\mathrm{e}_{k}
stands for the
k
-th basis vector in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then
\mathbf{H}_{A}^{\mathrm{Lip}_{+}}
holds true provided that

\displaystyle-f(r)-2rf^{\prime}(r)\leq L,\quad r\in[0,\infty),

(1.64)

and (
1.23
) is satisfied if

\displaystyle\gamma\Big(r^{3/2}|f^{\prime\prime}(r)|+r^{1/2}|f^{\prime}(r)|\Big)-f(r)-2rf^{\prime}(r)\leq C,\quad r\in[0,\infty),

(1.65)

for some
\gamma,C\in(0,\infty)
.
These assumptions are satisfied, for example, when
f(\cdot)
is a polynomial, such as
f(r)=r^{n}+\dots

In this case all the assumptions of Theorem
1.5
hold true with

\kappa=2n+1
,
\chi=2n
.
One can also consider more complex examples that include fast oscillations, e.g.,

\displaystyle A(x)=-(\|x\|^{4}+\sin\|x\|^{2})x,

(1.66)

where
f(r)=r^{2}+\sin r
satisfies the above assumptions, and all assumptions of Theorem
1.5
hold true with

\kappa=5
,
\chi=4
.

The drift
A
given in (
1.61
) is quite similar to those we encountered in the one-dimensional setting, as
we can represent
A
as a gradient of a rotationally invariant potential
U
, i.e.,

A(x)=-\nabla U(x)
, where

\displaystyle U(x)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\|x\|^{2}}f(r)\,\mathrm{d}r.

(1.67)

It appears, that for drifts without rotational symmetry, the one-sided
Lipschitz conditions becomes a strong limitation.
To illustrate this, we provide a negative example of such a case.

Example 1.14
.

Let

\displaystyle A(x,y,z)=\begin{pmatrix}-x^{2n+1}-y^{2}-z^{2}\\
-y^{2n+1}+xy-bxz\\
-z^{2n+1}+bxy+xz\\
\end{pmatrix},\quad(x,y,z)^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{3},

(1.68)

with
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, and
b>0
. In the case
n=0
this is the Lorenz-84 atmospheric
circulation model with the thermal forcing terms
F=G=0
, see, e.g., Section 3 in
[
AIW03
]
.
We have

\displaystyle(x,y,z)^{T}A(x,y,z)=-(x^{2n+2}+y^{2n+2}+z^{2n+2})\leq_{C}-(x^{2}+y^{2}+z^{2})^{n+1},

(1.69)

so that the drift
A(\cdot)
is
2n+1
-dissipative.
Therefore, (
1.68
) with
n\in\mathbb{N}
should be considered as a superlinearly dissipative extension of the Lorenz-84 model.

We can decompose the function
A
into a sum of a gradient and a tangential components:

\displaystyle A(x,y,z)=\begin{pmatrix}-x^{2n+1}\\
-y^{2n+1}\\
-z^{2n+1}\\
\end{pmatrix}+\begin{pmatrix}-y^{2}-z^{2}\\
xy-bxz\\
bxy+xz\\
\end{pmatrix}=:A_{1}(x,y,z)+A_{2}(x,y,z),

(1.70)

and it is easy to check
that
A_{1}(x,y,z)
is one-sided Lipschitz continuous. However, adding the tangential term

A_{2}(x,y,z)
disrupts the one-sided Lipschitz structure for the entire vector field
A(x,y,z)
.
Note that for
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
the tangential perturbation
A_{2}(x,y,z)
is a polynomial of lower degree
compared to the main part
A_{1}(x,y,z)
.
This example demonstrates that the multivariate one-sided Lipschitz condition
is a delicate property, which depends on the geometry of the vector field rather than simply the (polynomial) degree relations.

To show that
A(x,y,z)
is
not

one-sided Lipschitz continuous, we calculate

\displaystyle\nabla A(x,y,z)

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}-(2n+1)x^{2n}&-2y&-2z\\
y-bz&-(2n+1)y^{2n}+x&-bx\\
by+z&bx&-(2n+1)z^{2n}+x\\
\end{pmatrix},

(1.71)

\displaystyle[\nabla A(x,y,z)]_{\mathrm{sym}}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}-(2n+1)x^{2n}&-\frac{1}{2}y-\frac{b}{2}z&\frac{b}{2}y-\frac{1}{2}z\\
-\frac{1}{2}y-\frac{b}{2}z&-(2n+1)y^{2n}+x&0\\
\frac{b}{2}y-\frac{1}{2}z&0&-(2n+1)z^{2n}+x\\
\end{pmatrix},

and for any
L\in(0,\infty)
we have that

\displaystyle\mathrm{det}\,\Big([\nabla A(x,y,z)]_{\mathrm{sym}}-L\cdot\mathrm{Id}\Big)

\displaystyle=-\Big((2n+1)x^{2n}+L\Big)\Big((2n+1)y^{2n}-x+L\Big)\Big((2n+1)z^{2n}-x+L\Big)

(1.72)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4}(y+bz)^{2}\Big((2n+1)z^{2n}-x+L\Big)+\frac{1}{4}(by-z)^{2}\Big((2n+1)y^{2n}-x+L\Big).

Since
b\neq 0
, we see that

\displaystyle\mathrm{det}\,\Big([\nabla A(0,y,0)]_{\mathrm{sym}}-L\cdot\mathrm{Id}\Big)=\frac{1}{4}(2n+1)b^{2}y^{2n+2}(1+o(1))\to+\infty,\quad|y|\to+\infty,

(1.73)

hence by the Sylvester criterium the condition (
1.59
) is not satisfied.
A similar analysis of a Lorenz-63 model was perfored in

[
HS94
]
. Further negative multivariate examples
can be found in Chapter 4 in
[
HJ15
]
.

\Box

We finalize this discussion by giving one positive example of a two dimensional drift without rotational symmetry.

Example 1.15
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
b\in(0,\infty)
, and
c\in\mathbb{R}
be fixed, and let

A(y,z)=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}-y^{2n+1}+cy-bcz\\
-z^{2n+1}+bcy+cz\\
\end{array}\right),\quad(y,z)^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}.

(1.74)

The function
A
is just a
(y,z)
-part of the drift from the previous example,
with the variable
x
‘frozen’ at the level
x=c
.
We have

\displaystyle(y,z)A(y,z)=-(y^{2n+2}+y^{2n+2})+cy^{2}+cz^{2}\leq_{C}-(x^{2}+y^{2})^{n+1}+1,

(1.75)

so that the drift
A(\cdot)
is
(2n+1)
-dissipative. Next, we have

\displaystyle\nabla A(y,z)

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}-(2n+1)y^{2n}+c&-bc\\
bc&-(2n+1)y^{2n}+c\\
\end{pmatrix},

(1.76)

\displaystyle[\nabla A(y,z)]_{\mathrm{sym}}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}-(2n+1)x^{2n}+c&0\\
0&-(2n+1)y^{2n}+c\\
\end{pmatrix},

and (
1.59
) holds true obviously for any
L\geq\max\{c,0\}
.
Since the ‘tangential’ part of (
1.74
) is a linear function in
(y,z)
, it is easy to verify that

\mathbf{H}_{A_{x},A_{xx}}
is satisfied with
\chi=2n
. That is,
A(y,z)
satisfies
all the assumptions required in Theorem
1.5
with
\kappa=2n+1,\chi=2n
.

\Box

2
Properties of the mapping
\Phi

In this section, we study properties of the mapping
\Phi
determined by the ODE (
1.13
).

Lemma 2.1
.

Let assumption
\mathbf{H}^{\mathrm{Lip}_{+}}_{A}
hold true.
Then the ODE (
1.13
)
has a unique global solution that satisfies the following estimates for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
t\in[0,\infty)
:

\displaystyle\|\Phi(t,x)\|\leq\|x\|\mathrm{e}^{Lt}+\|A(0)\|\frac{\mathrm{e}^{Lt}-1}{L},

(2.1)

\displaystyle\|\Phi(t,x)-x\|\leq\|A(x)\|\frac{\mathrm{e}^{Lt}-1}{L}.

(2.2)

Proof.

1. Existence and uniqueness of the ODE (
1.13
) is established in Lemma 3.19 in
[
PR14
]
.

2. For the solution
\Phi
we have:

\displaystyle\|\Phi(t,x)\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|x\|^{2}+2\int_{0}^{t}\langle A(\Phi(s,x)),\Phi(s,x)\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s

(2.3)

\displaystyle=\|x\|^{2}+2\int_{0}^{t}\langle A(\Phi(s,x))-A(0),\Phi(s,x)-0\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s+2\int_{0}^{t}\langle A(0),\Phi(s,x)\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq\|x\|^{2}+2L\int_{0}^{t}\|\Phi(s,x)\|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s+2\|A(0)\|\int_{0}^{t}\|\Phi(s,x)\|\,\mathrm{d}s.

Hence by Theorem 4.9 in
[
BS92
]

\displaystyle\|\Phi(t,x)\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\|x\|\mathrm{e}^{Lt}+\|A(0)\|\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{e}^{Ls}\,\mathrm{d}s\Big)^{2}

(2.4)

and consequently,

\displaystyle\|\Phi(t,x)\|

\displaystyle\leq\|x\|\mathrm{e}^{Lt}+\|A(0)\|\frac{\mathrm{e}^{Lt}-1}{L}.

(2.5)

3. Analogously, we write

\displaystyle\|\Phi(t,x)-x\|^{2}

\displaystyle=2\int_{0}^{t}\langle A(\Phi(s,x)),\Phi(s,x)-x\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s

(2.6)

\displaystyle=2\int_{0}^{t}\langle A(\Phi(s,x))-A(x),\Phi(s,x)-x\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s+2\int_{0}^{t}\langle A(x),\Phi(s,x)-x\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq 2L\int_{0}^{t}\|\Phi(s,x)-x\|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s+2\|A(x)\|\int_{0}^{t}\|\Phi(s,x)-x\|\,\mathrm{d}s,

so that as in 2. we obtain the estimate

\displaystyle\|\Phi(t,x)-x\|

\displaystyle\leq\|A(x)\|\frac{\mathrm{e}^{Lt}-1}{L}.

(2.7)

∎

Corollary 2.2
.

Let assumptions
\mathbf{H}^{\mathrm{Lip}_{+}}_{A}
and
\mathbf{H}_{A_{x},A_{xx}}
hold true. Then, for
all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

t\in[0,1]
we have

\displaystyle\|\Phi(t,x)\|

\displaystyle\leq_{C}1+\|x\|,

(2.8)

\displaystyle\|\Phi(t,x)-x\|

\displaystyle\leq_{C}t(1+\|x\|^{\chi+1}).

(2.9)

Proof.

The estimate (
2.8
) follows immediately from (
2.1
).
The estimate (
2.9
) follows from (
2.2
) and the integrated estimate (
1.20
).
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Let assumptions
\mathbf{H}^{\mathrm{Lip}_{+}}_{A}
and

\mathbf{H}_{A_{x},A_{xx}}
hold true. Then, for
t\in[0,1]
,

\displaystyle\|\Phi_{x}(t,x)\|

\displaystyle\leq_{C}1,

(2.10)

\displaystyle\|\Phi_{x}(t,x)-\mathrm{Id}\|

\displaystyle\leq_{C}t(1+\|x\|^{\chi}).

(2.11)

Proof.

The gradient matrix
\Phi_{x}
satisfies the matrix ODE

\displaystyle\dot{\Phi}_{x}(t,x)

\displaystyle=A_{x}(\Phi(t,x))\Phi_{x}(t,x),

(2.12)

\displaystyle\Phi_{x}(0,x)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Id}.

For each
\varphi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
we have

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\|\Phi_{x}(t,x)\varphi\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\langle\Phi_{x}(t,x)\varphi,\Phi_{x}(t,x)\varphi\rangle

(2.13)

\displaystyle=\langle\dot{\Phi}_{x}(t,x)\varphi,\Phi_{x}(t,x)\varphi\rangle+\langle\Phi_{x}(t,x)\varphi,\dot{\Phi}_{x}(t,x)\varphi\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle(A_{x}(\Phi(t,x))+A_{x}(\Phi(t,x)^{T})\Phi_{x}(t,x)\varphi,\Phi_{x}(t,x)\varphi\rangle

\displaystyle\leq 2L\|\Phi_{x}(t,x)\varphi\|^{2}.

Therefore by Gronwall’s Lemma we obtain the estimate

\displaystyle\|\Phi_{x}(t,x)\|\leq_{C}\mathrm{e}^{Lt},

(2.14)

which proves (
2.10
).
Furthermore using (
2.8
) and (
2.10
) we get for
t\in[0,1]
:

\displaystyle\|\Phi_{x}(t,x)-\mathrm{Id}\|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}\|A_{x}(\Phi(s,x))\|\|\Phi_{x}(s,x)\|\,\mathrm{d}s

(2.15)

\displaystyle\leq_{C}t\max_{s\in[0,t]}\|A_{x}(\Phi(s,x))\|

\displaystyle\leq_{C}t\max_{s\in[0,t]}(1+\|\Phi(s,x)\|^{\chi})

\displaystyle\leq_{C}t(1+(1+\|x\|)^{\chi})

\displaystyle\leq_{C}t(1+\|x\|^{\chi}).

∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Let assumptions
\mathbf{H}^{\mathrm{Lip}_{+}}_{A}
and

\mathbf{H}_{A_{x},A_{xx}}
hold true. Then, for
t\in[0,1]
,

\displaystyle\|\Phi_{xx}(t,x)\|

\displaystyle\leq_{C}1,

(2.16)

\displaystyle\|\Phi_{xx}(t,x)\|

\displaystyle\leq_{C}t(1+\|x\|^{\chi}).

(2.17)

Proof.

To prove (
2.16
), it is enough to show that, for
i,j=1,\dots,d
,

\displaystyle\|\Phi_{x^{i}x^{j}}(t,x)\|\leq_{C}1.

(2.18)

The derivative
\Phi_{x^{i}x^{j}}
satisfies the ODE

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\Phi_{x^{i}x^{j}}(t,x)

\displaystyle=\partial_{x^{j}}\Big(A_{x}(\Phi(t,x))\Phi_{x^{i}}(t,x)\Big),

(2.19)

\displaystyle\Phi_{x^{i}x^{j}}(0,x)

\displaystyle=0.

Hence, for each coordinate
k=1,\dots,d
we have

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\Phi^{k}_{x^{i}x^{j}}

\displaystyle=\partial_{x^{j}}\Big(\sum_{l=1}^{d}A^{k}_{x^{l}}(\Phi)\Phi^{l}_{x^{i}}\Big)

(2.20)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l,m=1}^{d}A^{k}_{x^{l}x^{m}}(\Phi)\Phi^{m}_{x^{j}}\Phi^{l}_{x^{i}}+\sum_{l=1}^{d}A^{k}_{x^{l}}(\Phi)\Phi^{l}_{x^{i}x^{j}}

\displaystyle=\langle A^{k}_{xx}(\Phi)\Phi_{x^{i}},\Phi_{x^{j}}\rangle+\langle A^{k}_{x}(\Phi),\Phi_{x^{i}x^{j}}\rangle.

Denote by
Q_{ij}(t,x)
the vector with the coordinates

\displaystyle Q^{k}_{ij}(t,x):=\langle A^{k}_{xx}(\Phi(t,x))\Phi_{x^{i}}(t,x),\Phi_{x^{j}}(t,x)\rangle,\quad k=1,\dots,d.

(2.21)

Then the ODE (
2.20
) can be rewritten in the following matrix form

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\Phi_{x^{i}x^{j}}(t,x)=Q_{ij}(t,x)+A_{x}(\Phi(t,x))\Phi_{x^{i}x^{j}}(t,x)

(2.22)

and therefore

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\|\Phi_{x^{i}x^{j}}(t,x)\|^{2}

\displaystyle=2\langle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\Phi_{x^{i}x^{j}}(t,x),\Phi_{x^{i}x^{j}}(t,x)\rangle

(2.23)

\displaystyle=2\langle Q_{ij}(t,x),\Phi_{x^{i}x^{j}}(t,x)\rangle+2\langle A_{x}(\Phi(t,x))\Phi_{x^{i}x^{j}}(t,x),\Phi_{x^{i}x^{j}}(t,x)\rangle.

By (
2.10
) we have

|Q^{k}_{ij}(t,x)|\leq\|A^{k}_{xx}(\Phi(t,x))\|\|\Phi_{x^{i}}(t,x)\|\|\Phi_{x^{j}}(t,x)\|\leq_{C}\|A^{k}_{xx}(\Phi(t,x))\|,

(2.24)

so that for some constant
C_{*}\in(0,\infty)
we get

\langle Q_{ij}(t,x),\Phi_{x^{i}x^{j}}(t,x)\rangle\leq C_{*}\sum_{k=1}^{d}\|A^{k}_{xx}(\Phi(t,x))\||\Phi_{x^{i}x^{j}}^{k}(t,x)|.

(2.25)

Let
\varepsilon\in(0,\infty)
be the constant from Assumption (
1.21
).
For each
t\in[0,1]
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

define

\displaystyle t_{*}(x)=\sup\{s\in[0,t]\colon\|\Phi_{x^{i}x^{j}}(s,x)\|\leq C_{*}\varepsilon^{-1}\}.

(2.26)

If
t_{*}(x)=t
, then

\displaystyle\|\Phi_{x^{i}x^{j}}(t,x)\|\leq C_{*}\varepsilon^{-1}.

(2.27)

Otherwise, we have

\|\Phi_{x^{i}x^{j}}(t_{*}(x),x)\|=C_{*}\varepsilon^{-1}
and

\displaystyle\|\Phi_{x^{i}x^{j}}(s,x)\|\geq C_{*}\varepsilon^{-1},\quad s\in[t_{*}(x),t].

(2.28)

The latter inequality and assumption (
1.21
) applied to the mapping
s\mapsto\Phi_{x^{i}x^{j}}(s,x)

on the interval

s\in[t_{*}(x),t]
yields

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\|\Phi_{x^{i}x^{j}}(s,x)\|^{2}

\displaystyle=2\langle Q_{ij}(s,x),\Phi_{x^{i}x^{j}}(s,x)\rangle+2\langle A_{x}(\Phi(s,x))\Phi_{x^{i}x^{j}}(s,x),\Phi_{x^{i}x^{j}}(t,x)\rangle

(2.29)

\displaystyle\leq 2C_{*}\sum_{k=1}^{d}\|A^{k}_{xx}(\Phi(s,x))\||\Phi_{x^{i}x^{j}}^{k}(s,x)|+2\langle A_{x}(\Phi(s,x))\Phi_{x_{i}x_{j}}(s,x),\Phi_{x^{i}x^{j}}(s,x)\rangle

\displaystyle\leq 2\varepsilon\|\Phi_{x_{i}x_{j}}(s,x)\|\sum_{k=1}^{d}\|A^{k}_{xx}(\Phi(s,x))\||\Phi^{k}_{x^{i}x^{j}}(s,x)|+2\langle A_{x}(\Phi(s,x))\Phi_{x_{i}x_{j}}(s,x),\Phi_{x^{i}x^{j}}(s,x)\rangle

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\|\Phi_{x^{i}x^{j}}(s,x)\|^{2}

with some
C_{1}\in(0,\infty)
. Hence by the Gronwall Lemma we get that

\displaystyle\|\Phi_{x^{i}x^{j}}(t,x)\|^{2}\leq C_{*}^{2}\varepsilon^{-2}\mathrm{e}^{{C_{1}}(t-t_{*})}\leq C_{*}^{2}\varepsilon^{-2}\mathrm{e}^{{C_{1}}t}\leq C_{*}^{2}\varepsilon^{-2}\mathrm{e}^{C_{1}},

(2.30)

which completes the proof of (
2.18
) and, consequently, (
2.16
).

Using (
2.22
), (
2.18
), and (
2.24
), we get that

\displaystyle\|\Phi_{x^{i}x^{j}}(t,x)\|=\|\Phi_{x^{i}x^{j}}(t,x)-\Phi_{x^{i}x^{j}}(0,x)\|\leq_{C}\int_{0}^{t}\Big(\|A_{xx}(\Phi(s,x))\|+\|A_{x}(\Phi(s,x))\|\Big)\,\mathrm{d}s.

(2.31)

Then using (
1.20
), (
1.21
), and repeating the estimates from (
2.15
), we get that

\displaystyle\|\Phi_{x^{i}x^{j}}(t,x)\|\leq_{C}t(1+\|x\|^{\chi}),

(2.32)

which proves (
2.17
).
∎

3
Proofs of Theorems
1.1
and
1.2

We will use the Lévy–Itô representation of the Lévy process
Z
in the following form:

\displaystyle Z_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\|z\|\leq 1}z\widetilde{N}(\mathrm{d}z,\mathrm{d}s)+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\|z\|>1}z\,N(\mathrm{d}z,\mathrm{d}s),

(3.1)

where
N(\mathrm{d}z,\mathrm{d}t)
is a Poisson random measure on
(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}_{+},\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}))
with the intensity measure

\nu(\mathrm{d}z)\,\mathrm{d}t
, and
\widetilde{N}(\mathrm{d}z,\mathrm{d}t)=N(\mathrm{d}z,\mathrm{d}t)-\nu(\mathrm{d}z)\,\mathrm{d}t

is the compensated Poisson random measure.
The stochastic integral with respect to
Z
in (
1.1
)
is understood as a sum of stochastic integral with respect to the
N
and

\widetilde{N}
, i.e. the SDE (
1.1
) can be equivalently rewritten as

\displaystyle X_{t}=x

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}A(X_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}a(X_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}b(X_{s})\,\mathrm{d}B_{s}

(3.2)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\|z\|\leq 1}\sigma(X_{s-})z\,\widetilde{N}(\mathrm{d}z,\mathrm{d}s)+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\|z\|>1}\sigma(X_{s-})z\,N(\mathrm{d}z,\mathrm{d}s).

We deduce statements of both Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
from a general moment
estimate provided by
[
KP21
, Theorem 2.12]
. Specifically, existence and uniqueness of a local solution
of the SDE (
1.1
) is guaranteed, e.g., by Theorem 6.2.11 in
[
App09
]
. Applying
[
KP21
, Theorem 2.12]

we get immediately that this solution is global and that the estimate (
1.11
) holds,
which proves Theorem
1.1
.

To prove Theorem
1.2
, we show that approximations
\{X^{n}\}
defined in (
1.18
),
while considered as continuous time semimartingales, satisfy conditions of
[
KP21
, Theorem 2.12]

uniformly over
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we apply the Itô formula to (
1.18
) to see that
X^{n}
is
an Itô semimartigale with the following representation:

\displaystyle X^{n}_{t}

\displaystyle=x+\int_{0}^{t}A(X^{n}_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}\Phi_{x}(\delta^{n}_{s},Y^{n}_{s})a(X^{(n)}_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s

(3.3)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\Phi_{x}(\delta^{n}_{s},Y^{n}_{s})b(X^{(n)}_{s})\,\mathrm{d}B_{s}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\operatorname{tr}(\Phi_{xx}(\delta^{n}_{s},Y^{n}_{s})bb^{T}(X^{(n)}_{s}))\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{|z|\leq 1}\Big[\Phi(\delta^{n}_{s},Y^{n}_{s}+c(X^{(n)}_{s})z)-\Phi(\delta^{n}_{s},Y^{n}_{s})-\Phi_{x}(\delta^{n}_{s},Y^{n}_{s})c(X^{(n)}_{s})z\Big]\,\nu(\mathrm{d}z)\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{|z|\leq 1}\Big[\Phi(\delta^{n}_{s},Y^{n}_{s}+c(X^{(n)}_{s})z)-\Phi(\delta^{n}_{s},Y^{n}_{s-})\Big]\,\widetilde{N}(\mathrm{d}z,\mathrm{d}s)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{|z|>1}\Big[\Phi(\delta^{n}_{s},Y^{n}_{s}+c(X^{(n)}_{s})z)-\Phi(\delta^{n}_{s},Y^{n}_{s-})\Big]\,\widetilde{N}(\mathrm{d}z,\mathrm{d}s).

We rewrite (
3.3
) in the canonical Lévy–Itô form:

\displaystyle X^{n}_{t}=x+\int_{0}^{t}a^{n}_{s}\,\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}b^{n}_{s}\,\mathrm{d}B_{s}+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\zeta\Big(N^{n}(\mathrm{d}\zeta,\mathrm{d}s)-\mathbb{I}(\|\zeta\|\leq 1)\nu^{n}(\mathrm{d}\zeta,\mathrm{d}s)\Big)

(3.4)

with the local progressively measurable characteristics

\displaystyle a^{n}_{s}

\displaystyle=A(X^{n}_{s})+\Phi_{x}(\delta^{n}_{s},Y^{n}_{s})a(X^{(n)}_{s})+\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{tr}(\Phi_{xx}(\delta^{n}_{s},Y^{n}_{s})bb^{T}(X^{(n)}_{s}))

(3.5)

\displaystyle+\int_{\|z\|\leq 1}\Big(\Big\|\Delta^{n}_{t}+\sum_{i=0}^{4}\Lambda_{t}^{c,i,n}(z)\Big\|^{r}-\|\Delta^{n}_{t}\|^{r}-r\|\Delta^{n}_{t}\|^{r-2}\langle\Delta^{n}_{t},\sum_{i=0}^{4}\Lambda_{t}^{c,i,n}(z)\rangle\Big)\,\nu(\mathrm{d}z)\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle+r\|\Delta_{t}^{n}\|^{r-2}\langle\Delta_{t}^{n},\sum_{i=0}^{4}\Lambda_{t}^{b,i,n}\,\mathrm{d}B_{t}\rangle

\displaystyle+\int_{\|z\|\leq 1}\Big(\Big\|\Delta^{n}_{t-}+\sum_{i=0}^{4}\Lambda_{t}^{c,i,n}(z)\Big\|^{r}-\|\Delta^{n}_{t-}\|^{r}\Big)\,\widetilde{N}(\mathrm{d}z,\mathrm{d}t).

Recall that
c(\cdot)
is bounded and
\|z\|\leq 1
, and

\displaystyle\|\Lambda^{c,0,n}_{s}\|\leq_{C}\|\Delta^{n}_{s}\|\|z\|.

(4.32)

Hence by the Taylor formula

\displaystyle\Big\|\Delta^{n}_{t}

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=0}^{4}\Lambda_{t}^{c,i,n}(z)\Big\|^{r}-\|\Delta^{n}_{t}\|^{r}-r\|\Delta^{n}_{t}\|^{r-2}\langle\Delta^{n}_{t},\sum_{i=0}^{4}\Lambda_{t}^{c,i,n}(z)\rangle

(4.33)

\displaystyle\leq_{C}\Big(\|\Delta_{t}^{n}\|^{r-2}+\Big\|\sum_{i=0}^{4}\Lambda_{t}^{c,i,n}(z)\Big\|^{r-2}\Big)\Big\|\sum_{i=0}^{4}\Lambda_{t}^{c,i,n}(z)\Big\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq_{C}\Big(\|\Delta_{t}^{n}\|^{r-2}+\Big\|\sum_{i=1}^{4}\Lambda_{t}^{c,i,n}(z)\Big\|^{r-2}\Big)\Big(\|\Delta^{n}_{s}\|^{2}\|z\|^{2}+\Big\|\sum_{i=1}^{4}\Lambda_{t}^{c,i,n}(z)\Big\|^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq_{C}\|\Delta_{t}^{n}\|^{r}\|z\|^{2}+\|\Delta^{n}_{s}\|^{r-2}\sum_{i=1}^{4}\|\Lambda_{t}^{c,i,n}(z)\|^{2}+\|\Delta^{n}_{s}\|^{2}\|z\|^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{4}\|\Lambda_{t}^{c,i,n}(z)\|^{r-2}+\sum_{i=1}^{4}\|\Lambda_{t}^{c,i,n}(z)\|^{r}.

For
r=2
, the r.h.s. of the latter formula reduces to

\displaystyle\cdots\leq_{C}\|\Delta^{n}_{s}\|^{2}\|z\|^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{4}\|\Lambda_{t}^{c,i,n}(z)\|^{2}.

(4.34)

The Young inequality (
9.13
) gives

\displaystyle\int_{\|z\|\leq 1}\Big(\Big\|

\displaystyle\Delta^{n}_{t}+\sum_{i=0}^{4}\Lambda_{t}^{c,i,n}(z)\Big\|^{r}-\|\Delta^{n}_{t}\|^{r}-r\|\Delta^{n}_{t}\|^{r-2}\langle\Delta^{n}_{t},\sum_{i=0}^{4}\Lambda_{t}^{c,i,n}(z)\rangle\Big)\,\nu(\mathrm{d}z)

(4.35)

\displaystyle\leq_{C}\|\Delta_{t}^{n}\|^{r}+\|\Delta^{n}_{s}\|^{r-2}\int_{\|z\|\leq 1}\sum_{i=1}^{4}\|\Lambda_{t}^{c,i,n}(z)\|^{2}\,\nu(\mathrm{d}z)

\displaystyle+\|\Delta^{n}_{s}\|^{2}\int_{\|z\|\leq 1}\|z\|^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{4}\|\Lambda_{t}^{c,i,n}(z)\|^{r-2}\,\nu(\mathrm{d}z)+\int_{\|z\|\leq 1}\sum_{i=1}^{4}\|\Lambda_{t}^{c,i,n}(z)\|^{r}\,\nu(\mathrm{d}z)

\displaystyle\leq_{C}\|\Delta_{t}^{n}\|^{r}+\Big(\int_{\|z\|\leq 1}\sum_{i=1}^{4}\|\Lambda_{t}^{c,i,n}(z)\|^{2}\,\nu(\mathrm{d}z)\Big)^{\frac{r}{2}}

\displaystyle+\Big(\int_{\|z\|\leq 1}\|z\|^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{4}\|\Lambda_{t}^{c,i,n}(z)\|^{r-2}\,\nu(\mathrm{d}z)\Big)^{\frac{r}{r-2}}+\int_{\|z\|\leq 1}\sum_{i=1}^{4}\|\Lambda_{t}^{c,i,n}(z)\|^{r}\,\nu(\mathrm{d}z),

whereas for
r=2
we just get

\displaystyle\cdots\leq_{C}\|\Delta_{t}^{n}\|^{2}+\int_{\|z\|\leq 1}\sum_{i=1}^{4}\|\Lambda_{t}^{c,i,n}(z)\|^{2}\,\nu(\mathrm{d}z).

(4.36)

This gives the representation

\|\Delta_{t}^{n}\|^{r}=\|\Delta_{t_{k}}^{n}\|^{r}+\int_{t_{k}}^{t}\mathrm{D}_{s}^{n,k}\,\mathrm{d}s+\mathrm{M}_{t}^{n,k},\quad t\in[t_{k},t_{k+1}),

(4.37)

with a local martingale
\mathrm{M}^{n,k}
,
\mathrm{M}^{n,k}_{t_{k}}=0
, and a drift term
\mathrm{D}^{n,k}
that satisfies

\displaystyle\mathrm{D}_{t}^{n,k}

\displaystyle\leq_{C}\|\Delta_{t}^{n}\|^{r}+\mathrm{F}_{t}^{n,k},

(4.38)

\displaystyle\mathrm{F}_{t}^{n,k}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{i=1}^{4}\|\Lambda_{t}^{a,i,n}\|^{r}+\sum_{i=1}^{5}\|\Lambda_{t}^{b,i,n}\|^{r}+\sum_{i=1}^{5}\int_{\|z\|\leq 1}\|\Lambda_{t}^{c,i,n}(z)\|^{r}\,\nu(\mathrm{d}z)

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{4}\Big(\int_{\|z\|\leq 1}\|\Lambda_{t}^{c,i,n}(z)\|^{2}\,\nu(\mathrm{d}z)\Big)^{\frac{r}{2}}+\sum_{i=1}^{4}\Big(\int_{\|z\|\leq 1}\|z\|^{2}\|\Lambda_{t}^{c,i,n}(z)\|^{r-2}\,\nu(\mathrm{d}z)\Big)^{\frac{r}{r-2}},

where the last line vanishes for
r=2
.

Then by the standard argument, involving localization, the Gronwall lemma, and the Fatou lemma, we get that,
for
t\in[t_{k},t_{k+1})
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\Big[\|\Delta_{t}^{n}\|^{r}\Big|\mathcal{F}_{t_{k}}\Big]\leq_{C}\|\Delta_{t_{k}}^{n}\|^{r}+\int_{t_{k}}^{t}\mathbf{E}\Big[\mathrm{F}_{s}^{n,k}\Big|\mathcal{F}_{t_{k}}\Big]\,\mathrm{d}s.

(4.39)

Now we estimate the
\Lambda
-terms in (
4.38
).

1. Since
a,b,c
are Lipschitz continuous, we have by (
4.10
) that

\displaystyle\|\Lambda_{s}^{a,1,n}\|+\|\Lambda_{s}^{b,1,n}\|

\displaystyle\leq_{C}U_{s}^{n},

(4.40)

\displaystyle\|\Lambda_{s}^{c,1,n}(z)\|

\displaystyle\leq_{C}U_{s}^{n}\|z\|.

2. By (
4.12
) we get

\displaystyle\|\Lambda_{s}^{a,2,n}\|+\|\Lambda_{s}^{b,2,n}\|

\displaystyle\leq_{C}h_{n}^{\gamma}(1+\|X^{(n)}_{s}\|^{\gamma\chi+1}),

(4.41)

\displaystyle\|\Lambda_{s}^{c,2,n}(z)\|

\displaystyle\leq_{C}h_{n}^{\gamma}(1+\|X^{(n)}_{s}\|^{\gamma\chi+1})\|z\|.

3. By (
4.11
) we have

\displaystyle\|\Lambda_{s}^{a,3,n}\|+\|\Lambda_{s}^{b,3,n}\|

\displaystyle\leq_{C}U_{s}^{n}.

(4.42)

Since
c(\cdot)
is bounded,
\|z\|\leq 1
, and
\|\Phi_{xx}\|
is bounded by (
2.16
) we get

\displaystyle\Lambda_{s}^{c,3,n}(z)

\displaystyle=\Big(\Phi(\delta_{s}^{n},Y_{s}^{n}+c(X^{(n)}_{s})z)-\Phi(\delta_{s}^{n},Y_{s}^{n})\Big)-\Big(\Phi(\delta_{s}^{n},X^{(n)}_{s}+c(X^{(n)}_{s})z)-\Phi(\delta_{s}^{n},X^{(n)}_{s})\Big)

(4.43)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\Big(\Phi_{x}(\delta_{s}^{n},Y_{s}^{n}+\theta c(X^{(n)}_{s})z)-\Phi_{x}(\delta_{s}^{n},X^{(n)}_{s}+\theta c(X^{(n)}_{s})z)\Big)c(X^{(n)}_{s})z\,\mathrm{d}\theta

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\partial_{x}\Big(\Phi_{x}(\delta_{s}^{n},\zeta Y_{s}^{n}+(1-\zeta)X^{(n)}_{s}+\theta c(X^{(n)}_{s})z)c(X^{(n)}_{s})z\Big)(Y_{s}^{n}-X^{(n)}_{s})\,\mathrm{d}\zeta\,\mathrm{d}\theta,

which implies that

\displaystyle\|\Lambda_{s}^{c,3,n}(z)\|\leq_{C}\|Y_{s}^{n}-X^{(n)}_{s}\|\|z\|.

(4.44)

Hence,

\displaystyle\|\Lambda_{s}^{c,3,n}(z)\|\leq_{C}U_{s}^{n}\|z\|.

(4.45)

4. By (
4.8
), we have

\displaystyle\|\Lambda_{s}^{a,4,n}\|+\|\Lambda_{s}^{b,4,n}\|\leq_{C}h_{n}^{\gamma}(1+\|X^{(n)}_{s}\|^{\gamma\chi+1}).

(4.46)

Furthermore,

\displaystyle\Lambda_{s}^{c,4,n}(z)

\displaystyle=\Big(\Phi(\delta_{s}^{n},X^{(n)}_{s}+c(X^{(n)}_{s})z)-X^{(n)}_{s}-c(X^{(n)}_{s})z\Big)-\Big(\Phi(\delta_{s}^{n},X^{(n)}_{s})-X^{(n)}_{s}\Big),

(4.47)

hence by (
4.8
) we have

\displaystyle\|\Lambda_{s}^{c,4,n}(z)\|\leq_{C}h_{n}^{\gamma}(1+\|X^{(n)}_{s}\|^{\gamma\chi+1})\|z\|.

(4.48)

Here we have used that

\displaystyle(1+\|X^{(n)}_{s}+c(X^{(n)}_{s})z\|^{\gamma\chi+1})\leq_{C}(1+\|X^{(n)}_{s}\|^{\gamma\chi+1}),

(4.49)

because
c(\cdot)
and
\|z\|
are bounded.

5. Finally, by (
4.9
) we have

\displaystyle\|\Lambda_{s}^{b,5,n}\|\leq_{C}h_{n}^{\gamma}(1+\|X^{(n)}_{s}\|^{\gamma\chi})+h_{n}^{\gamma}(U_{s}^{n})^{\gamma\chi}

(4.50)

and

\displaystyle\|\Lambda_{s}^{c,5,n}(z)\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\sup_{\theta\in[0,1]}\|\Phi_{xx}(\delta_{s}^{n},Y_{s}^{n}+\theta c(X^{(n)}_{s})z)\|\|c(X^{(n)}_{s})z\|^{2}

(4.51)

\displaystyle\leq_{C}h_{n}^{\gamma}(1+\|X^{(n)}_{s}\|^{\gamma\chi})\|z\|^{2}+h_{n}^{\gamma}(U_{s}^{n})^{\gamma\chi}\|z\|^{2}.

Summarizing the above estimates and taking into account that
\|z\|^{2}\leq\|z\|
for
\|z\|\leq 1
, we get

\displaystyle\Lambda_{s}^{a,n}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{i=1}^{4}\|\Lambda_{s}^{a,i,n}\|\leq_{C}h_{n}^{\gamma}(1+\|X^{(n)}_{s}\|^{\gamma\chi+1})+U_{s}^{n}+h_{n}^{\gamma}(U_{s}^{n})^{\gamma\chi},

(4.52)

\displaystyle\Lambda_{s}^{b,n}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{i=1}^{5}\|\Lambda_{s}^{b,i,n}\|\leq_{C}h_{n}^{\gamma}(1+\|X^{(n)}_{s}\|^{\gamma\chi+1})+U_{s}^{n}+h_{n}^{\gamma}(U_{s}^{n})^{\gamma\chi},

\displaystyle\Lambda_{s}^{c,n}(z)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{i=1}^{5}\|\Lambda_{s}^{c,i,n}(z)\|\leq_{C}\Big(h_{n}^{\gamma}(1+\|X^{(n)}_{s}\|^{\gamma\chi+1})+U_{s}^{n}+h_{n}^{\gamma}(U_{s}^{n})^{\gamma\chi}\Big)\|z\|.

Alternatively, using that coefficients
a,b,c
and the derivarives
\Phi_{x}
and
\Phi_{xx}
are bounded
(see (
2.10
) and (
2.16
)) we immediately get that

\displaystyle\Lambda_{s}^{a,n}

\displaystyle\leq_{C}1,\quad\Lambda_{s}^{b,n}\leq_{C}1,\quad\Lambda_{s}^{c,n}(z)\leq_{C}\|z\|.

(4.53)

Next, denote
t_{k}^{n,*}=\eta^{n}_{t_{k}}+h_{n}

and observe that for
t\in[t_{k}^{n,*},t_{k+1})
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{i)}

\displaystyle\widehat{Z}^{n}_{t}:=\widehat{Z}_{t}-\widehat{Z}_{\eta^{n}_{t}}\text{ and }B_{t}^{n}\text{ are independent of }\mathcal{F}_{t_{k}},

(4.54)

\displaystyle\mathrm{ii)}

\displaystyle X^{(n)}_{t}=X^{n}_{\eta^{n}_{t}}\text{ with }\eta^{n}_{t}\in[t_{k}^{n,*},t_{k+1}).

On the other hand, for
t\in[t_{k},t_{k}^{n,*})

\displaystyle\mathrm{i)}

\displaystyle\widehat{Z}^{n}_{t}:=\widehat{Z}_{t}-\widehat{Z}_{t_{k}}+\widehat{Z}_{t_{k}}-\widehat{Z}_{\eta^{n}_{t_{k}}}\text{ and }B^{n}_{t}:=B_{t}-B_{t_{k}}+B_{t_{k}}-B_{\eta^{n}_{t_{k}}},

(4.55)

\displaystyle\widehat{Z}_{t}-\widehat{Z}_{t_{k}},\ B_{t}-B_{t_{k}}\text{ are independent of }\mathcal{F}_{t_{k}},

\displaystyle\widehat{Z}_{t_{k}}-\widehat{Z}_{\eta^{n}_{t_{k}}},\ B_{t_{k}}-B_{\eta^{n}_{t_{k}}}\text{ are measurable w.r.t.\ }\mathcal{F}_{t_{k}},

\displaystyle\mathrm{ii)}

\displaystyle X^{(n)}_{t}=X^{(n)}_{t_{k}}=X^{n}_{\eta^{n}_{t_{k}}}.

For any
q>0
and any
s,t\in[0,h_{n}]
we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}^{(N)}_{t_{1},\dots,t_{N}}\|B_{t}-B_{s}\|^{q}\leq_{C}h_{n}^{\frac{q}{2}},\quad\mathbf{E}^{(N)}_{t_{1},\dots,t_{N}}\|\widehat{Z}^{n}_{t}-\widehat{Z}^{n}_{t}\|^{q}\leq_{C}h_{n}^{\frac{q}{2}}+h_{n}.

(4.56)

Consequently, for any
q\in(0,\infty)
we estimate:

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}^{(N)}_{t_{1},\dots,t_{N}}[(U_{t}^{n})^{q}\Big|\mathcal{F}_{t_{k}}]

\displaystyle\leq_{C}\begin{cases}(U_{t_{k}}^{n})^{q}+h_{n}^{\frac{q}{2}}+h_{n},\quad&t\in[t_{k},t_{k}^{n,*}),\\
h_{n}^{\frac{q}{2}}+h_{n},\quad&t\in[t_{k}^{n,*},t_{k+1}).\end{cases},

(4.57)

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}^{(N)}_{t_{1},\dots,t_{N}}[\|X_{t}^{(n)}\|^{q}\Big|\mathcal{F}_{t_{k}}]

\displaystyle\leq_{C}\begin{cases}\|X_{t_{k}}^{(n)}\|^{q},\quad&t\in[t_{k},t_{k}^{n,*}),\\
\sup_{t\in[t_{k},t_{k+1})}\mathbf{E}^{(N)}_{t_{1},\dots,t_{N}}\|X_{t}^{n}\|^{q}\leq_{C}1+\|X^{n}_{t_{k}}\|^{q},\quad&t\in[t_{k}^{n,*},t_{k+1}).\end{cases},

where in the latter inequality we used
Lemma
4.2
.

Furthermore, we estimate the integrals

\displaystyle\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\mathbf{E}^{(N)}_{t_{1},\dots,t_{N}}\Big[(U_{t}^{n})^{q}\Big|\mathcal{F}_{t_{k}}\Big]\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\Big(\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k}^{n,*}}+\int_{t_{k}^{n,*}}^{t_{k+1}}\Big)\mathbf{E}^{(N)}_{t_{1},\dots,t_{N}}\Big[(U_{t}^{n})^{q}\Big|\mathcal{F}_{t_{k}}\Big]\,\mathrm{d}s

(4.58)

\displaystyle\leq_{C}h_{n}\Big((U_{t_{k}}^{n})^{q}+h_{n}^{\frac{q}{2}}+h_{n}\Big)+h_{n}^{\frac{q}{2}}+h_{n}

\displaystyle\leq_{C}h_{n}(U_{t_{k}}^{n})^{q}+h_{n}^{\frac{q}{2}}+h_{n}.

Analogously,

\displaystyle\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\mathbf{E}^{(N)}_{t_{1},\dots,t_{N}}\Big[\|X_{t}^{(n)}\|^{q}\Big|\mathcal{F}_{t_{k}}\Big]\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\Big(\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k}^{n,*}}+\int_{t_{k}^{n,*}}^{t_{k+1}}\Big)\mathbf{E}^{(N)}_{t_{1},\dots,t_{N}}\Big[\|X_{t}^{(n)}\|^{q}\Big|\mathcal{F}_{t_{k}}\Big]\,\mathrm{d}s

(4.59)

\displaystyle\leq_{C}h_{n}\|X_{t_{k}}^{(n)}\|^{q}+1+\|X^{n}_{t_{k}}\|^{q}.

Now we can complete the proof. We rewrite (
4.39
) as

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}^{(N)}_{t_{1},\dots,t_{N}}\Big[\|\Delta_{t}^{n}\|^{r}\Big|\mathcal{F}_{t_{k}}\Big]\leq_{C}\|\Delta_{t_{k}}^{n}\|^{r}

\displaystyle+\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\mathbf{E}\Big[(\Lambda_{s}^{a,n})^{r}\Big|\mathcal{F}_{t_{k}}\Big]\,\mathrm{d}s

(4.60)

\displaystyle+\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\mathbf{E}\Big[(\Lambda_{s}^{b,n})^{r}\Big|\mathcal{F}_{t_{k}}\Big]\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle+\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\mathbf{E}\Big[\int_{\|z\|\leq 1}(\Lambda_{s}^{c,n}(z))^{r}\,\nu(\mathrm{d}z)\Big|\mathcal{F}_{t_{k}}\Big]\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq_{C}\|\Delta_{t_{k}}^{n}\|^{r}+\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\mathbf{E}\Big[h_{n}^{r\gamma}(1+\|X^{(n)}_{s}\|^{r\gamma\chi+r})+(U_{s}^{n})^{r}+h_{n}^{r\gamma}(U_{s}^{n})^{r\gamma\chi}\Big|\mathcal{F}_{t_{k}}\Big]\,\mathrm{d}s.

Eventually, this results in the desired estimate:

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}^{(N)}_{t_{1},\dots,t_{N}}\Big[\|\Delta_{t}^{n}\|^{r}\Big|\mathcal{F}_{t_{k}}\Big]\leq_{C}\|\Delta_{t_{k}}^{n}\|^{r}

\displaystyle+h_{n}^{r\gamma}(1+h_{n}\|X_{t_{k}}^{(n)}\|^{r\gamma\chi+r}+1+\|X^{n}_{t_{k}}\|^{r\gamma\chi+r})

(4.61)

\displaystyle+h_{n}(U_{t_{k}}^{n})^{r}+h_{n}^{\frac{r}{2}}+h_{n}+h_{n}^{r\gamma}\Big(h_{n}(U_{t_{k}}^{n})^{r\gamma\chi}+h_{n}^{\frac{r\gamma\chi}{2}}+h_{n}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq_{C}\|\Delta_{t_{k}}^{n}\|^{r}+h_{n}^{r\gamma+1}(1+\|X_{t_{k}}^{(n)}\|^{r\gamma\chi+r})+h_{n}^{r\gamma}(1+\|X^{n}_{t_{k}}\|^{r\gamma\chi+r})

\displaystyle+h_{n}(U_{t_{k}}^{n})^{r}+h_{n}^{r\gamma+1}(U_{t_{k}}^{n})^{r\gamma\chi}+h_{n}.

∎

As a corollary, we get the following estimate for the moments of
\Delta^{n}
of the order
q<p
, where

p\in(0,\infty)
denotes the exponent from the condition
\mathbf{H}_{\nu,p}
.

Corollary 4.4
.

For any
q\in(0,p)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[t_{k},t_{k+1})}\mathbf{E}^{(N)}_{t_{1},\dots,t_{N}}\Big[\|\Delta_{t}^{n}\|^{q}\Big|\mathcal{F}_{t_{k}}\Big]\leq_{C}\|\Delta_{t_{k}}^{n}\|^{q}

\displaystyle+h_{n}^{(\frac{p-q}{\chi}\wedge q)+(1\wedge\frac{q}{2})}(1+\|X_{t_{k}}^{(n)}\|^{p})+h_{n}^{\frac{p-q}{\chi}\wedge q}(1+\|X_{t_{k}}^{n}\|^{p})

(4.62)

\displaystyle+h_{n}^{1\wedge\frac{q}{2}}(U_{t_{k}}^{n})^{q}+h_{n}^{(\frac{p-q}{\chi}\wedge q)+(1\wedge\frac{q}{2})}(U_{t_{k}}^{n})^{(p-q)\wedge(q\chi)}+h_{n}^{1\wedge\frac{q}{2}}

for
n
large enough uniformly over

N\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, partitions
\{t_{1},\dots,t_{N}\}
, and
k=0,\dots,N
.

Proof.

If
q\in[2,\infty)
, we use Lemma
4.3
with
r=q
to get

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[t_{k},t_{k+1})}\mathbf{E}^{(N)}_{t_{1},\dots,t_{N}}\Big[\|\Delta_{t}^{n}\|^{q}\Big|\mathcal{F}_{t_{k}}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq_{C}\|\Delta_{t_{k}}^{n}\|^{q}+h_{n}^{q\gamma+1}(1+\|X_{t_{k}}^{(n)}\|^{q\gamma\chi+q})+h_{n}^{q\gamma}(1+\|X_{t_{k}}^{n}\|^{q\gamma\chi+q})

(4.63)

\displaystyle+h_{n}(U_{t_{k}}^{n})^{q}+h_{n}^{q\gamma+1}(U_{t_{k}}^{n})^{q\gamma\chi}+h_{n}.

If
q\in(0,2)
we use Lemma
4.3
with
r=2
and the Hölder inequality to get

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[t_{k},t_{k+1})}\mathbf{E}^{(N)}_{t_{1},\dots,t_{N}}\Big[\|\Delta_{t}^{n}\|^{q}\Big|\mathcal{F}_{t_{k}}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{t\in[t_{k},t_{k+1})}\Big(\mathbf{E}^{(N)}_{t_{1},\dots,t_{N}}\Big[\|\Delta_{t}^{n}\|^{2}\Big|\mathcal{F}_{t_{k}}\Big]\Big)^{\frac{q}{2}}

(4.64)

\displaystyle\leq_{C}\|\Delta_{t_{k}}^{n}\|^{q}+h_{n}^{q\gamma+\frac{q}{2}}(1+\|X_{t_{k}}^{(n)}\|^{q\gamma\chi+q})+h_{n}^{q\gamma}(1+\|X^{n}_{t_{k}}\|^{q\gamma\chi+q})

\displaystyle+h_{n}^{\frac{q}{2}}(U_{t_{k}}^{n})^{q}+h_{n}^{q\gamma+\frac{q}{2}}(U_{t_{k}}^{n})^{q\gamma\chi}+h_{n}^{\frac{q}{2}}.

Thus, in any case,

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[t_{k},t_{k+1})}\mathbf{E}^{(N)}_{t_{1},\dots,t_{N}}\Big[\|\Delta_{t}^{n}\|^{q}\Big|\mathcal{F}_{t_{k}}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq_{C}\|\Delta_{t_{k}}^{n}\|^{q}+h_{n}^{q\gamma+(1\wedge\frac{q}{2})}(1+\|X_{t_{k}}^{(n)}\|^{q\gamma\chi+q})+h_{n}^{q\gamma}(1+\|X_{t_{k}}^{n}\|^{q\gamma\chi+q})

(4.65)

\displaystyle+h_{n}(U_{t_{k}}^{n})^{q}+h_{n}^{q\gamma+(1\wedge\frac{q}{2})}(U_{t_{k}}^{n})^{q\gamma\chi}+h_{n}.

Taking

\displaystyle\gamma=\frac{p-q}{\chi q}\wedge 1,

(4.66)

we get

\displaystyle q\gamma\chi+q\leq p,\quad q\gamma=\frac{p-q}{\chi}\wedge q.

(4.67)

Then

\displaystyle(1+\|X_{t_{k}}^{n}\|^{q\gamma\chi+q})\leq(1+\|X_{t_{k}}^{n}\|^{p}),

(4.68)

and (
4.65
) yields the required estimate.
∎

The next step incorporates into the estimate the big jump at the time moment
t=t_{k}
.

Lemma 4.5
.

For any
q\in(0,p)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[t_{k},t_{k+1})}\mathbf{E}^{(N)}_{t_{1},\dots,t_{N}}\Big[\|\Delta_{t}^{n}\|^{q}\Big|\mathcal{F}_{t_{k}-}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq_{C}\|\Delta_{t_{k}-}^{n}\|^{q}

(4.69)

\displaystyle+h_{n}^{\frac{p-q}{\chi}\wedge q}(1+\|X_{t_{k}-}^{(n)}\|^{p})+h_{n}^{\frac{p-q}{\chi}\wedge q}(1+\|X_{t_{k}-}^{n}\|^{p})

\displaystyle+(U^{n}_{t_{k}-})^{q}+h_{n}^{1\wedge\frac{q}{2}}(U_{t_{k}}^{n}-)^{(p-q)\wedge(q\chi)}+h_{n}^{1\wedge\frac{q}{2}}

for
n
large enough uniformly over

N\in\mathbb{N}
, partitions
\{t_{1},\dots,t_{N}\}
, and
k=1,\dots,N
.

Remark 4.6
.

The above estimate still holds true for
N=0
and/or
k=0
, if we adopt the convention

\mathcal{F}_{0-}=\mathcal{F}_{0},\quad\Delta_{0-}^{n}=0,\quad X_{0-}^{(n)}=X_{0-}^{n}=x,\quad U^{n}_{0-}=0.

In this case there is no actual jump at the time moment
t_{0}=0
, and the required estimate is provided by Corollary
4.4
.

Proof.

We apply Corollary
4.4
to get

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[t_{k},t_{k+1})}\mathbf{E}^{(N)}_{t_{1},\dots,t_{N}}

\displaystyle\Big[\|\Delta_{t}^{n}\|^{q}\Big|\mathcal{F}_{t_{k}-}\Big]\leq\mathbf{E}^{(N)}_{t_{1},\dots,t_{N}}\sup_{t\in[t_{k},t_{k+1})}\Big[\mathbf{E}^{(N)}_{t_{1},\dots,t_{N}}\Big[\|\Delta_{t}^{n}\|^{q}\Big|\mathcal{F}_{t_{k}}\Big]\Big|\mathcal{F}_{t_{k}-}\Big]

(4.70)

\displaystyle\leq_{C}\|\Delta_{t_{k}-}^{n}\|^{q}+\mathbf{E}^{(N)}_{t_{1},\dots,t_{N}}\Big[\|\Delta_{t_{k}}-\Delta_{t_{k}-}^{n}\|^{q}\Big|\mathcal{F}_{t_{k}-}\Big]

\displaystyle+\mathbf{E}^{(N)}_{t_{1},\dots,t_{N}}\Big[h_{n}^{(\frac{p-q}{\chi}\wedge q)+(1\wedge\frac{q}{2})}(1+\|X_{t_{k}}^{(n)}\|^{p})+h_{n}^{\frac{p-q}{\chi}\wedge q}(1+\|X_{t_{k}}^{n}\|^{p})\Big|\mathcal{F}_{t_{k}-}\Big]

\displaystyle+\mathbf{E}^{(N)}_{t_{1},\dots,t_{N}}\Big[h_{n}^{1\wedge\frac{q}{2}}(U_{t_{k}}^{n})^{q}+h_{n}^{(\frac{p-q}{\chi}\wedge q)+(1\wedge\frac{q}{2})}(U_{t_{k}}^{n})^{(p-q)\wedge(q\chi)}+h_{n}^{1\wedge\frac{q}{2}}\Big|\mathcal{F}_{t_{k}-}\Big].

a) We have

\displaystyle X_{t_{k}}^{n}=X_{t_{k}-}^{n}+\Phi(\delta_{t_{k}}^{n},Y_{t_{k}-}^{n}+c(X^{(n)}_{t_{k}})J_{k})-\Phi(\delta_{t_{k}}^{n},Y^{n}_{t_{k}-}),

(4.71)

thus with the help of (
2.10
) we get

1+\|X_{t_{k}}^{n}\|^{p}\leq_{C}1+\|X_{t_{k}-}^{n}\|^{p}+\|J_{k}\|^{p}\leq(1+\|X_{t_{k}-}^{n}\|^{p})(1+\|J_{k}\|^{p}).

(4.72)

b) Without loss of generality we can assume that
t_{k}\notin\{t^{n}_{j}\}
, so that

\displaystyle X_{t_{k}}^{(n)}=X_{t_{k}-}^{(n)}=X^{n}_{\eta^{n}_{t_{k}}}.

(4.73)

c) Next,

\displaystyle\Delta_{t_{k}}^{n}-\Delta_{t_{k}-}^{n}

\displaystyle=\Big(\Phi(\delta_{t_{k}}^{n},Y_{t_{k}-}^{n}+c(X^{(n)}_{t_{k}-})J_{k})-\Phi(\delta_{t_{k}}^{n},Y^{n}_{t_{k}-})\Big)-c(X_{t_{k}-})J_{k}

(4.74)

\displaystyle=c(X_{t_{k}-}^{n})J_{k}-c(X_{t_{k}-})J_{k}

\displaystyle+c(X^{\langle n\rangle}_{t_{k}-})J_{k}-c(X_{t_{k}-}^{n})J_{k}

\displaystyle+c(X^{(n)}_{t_{k}-})J_{k}-c(X^{\langle n\rangle}_{t_{k}-})J_{k}

\displaystyle+\Phi(\delta_{t_{k}}^{n},Y^{n}_{t_{k}-}+c(X^{(n)}_{t_{k}-})J_{k})-\Phi(\delta_{t_{k}}^{n},X^{(n)}_{t_{k}-}+c(X^{(n)}_{t_{k}-})J_{k})

\displaystyle+\Phi(\delta_{t_{k}}^{n},X^{(n)}_{t_{k}-}+c(X^{(n)}_{t_{k}-})J_{k})-\Phi(\delta_{t_{k}}^{n},X^{(n)}_{t_{k}-})-c(X^{(n)}_{t_{k}-})J_{k}

\displaystyle+\Phi(\delta_{t_{k}}^{n},X^{(n)}_{t_{k}-})-\Phi(\delta_{t_{k}}^{n},Y^{n}_{t_{k}-})

\displaystyle=:\sum_{i=0}^{5}\Lambda^{d,i,n}_{k}.

For
i=0,1,2
the estimates for
\Lambda^{d,i,n}
are analogous to those for
\Lambda^{c,i,n}
from Lemma
4.3
:

\displaystyle\|\Lambda^{d,0,n}_{k}\|\leq_{C}\|\Delta_{t_{k}-}^{n}\|\|J_{k}\|,\quad\|\Lambda^{d,1,n}_{k}\|\leq_{C}U^{n}_{t_{k}-}\|J_{k}\|,\quad\|\Lambda^{d,2,n}_{k}\|\leq_{C}h_{n}^{\gamma}(1+\|X^{(n)}_{t_{k}-}\|^{\gamma\chi+1})\|J_{k}\|.

(4.75)

To estimate
\Lambda^{d,3,n},
we observe that,
by (
2.10
), (
2.11
) and (
4.8
), for any
t\in[0,1]
and
x,v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

\displaystyle\|\Phi(t,x+v)-\Phi(t,x)-v\|

\displaystyle\leq\|v\|\sup_{\theta\in[0,1]}\|\Phi_{x}(t,x+\theta v)\|

(4.76)

\displaystyle\leq_{C}t^{\gamma}(1+\|x\|^{\gamma\chi}+\|v\|^{\gamma\chi})\|v\|

\displaystyle\leq_{C}t^{\gamma}(1+\|x\|^{\gamma\chi})(1+\|v\|^{\gamma\chi+1}).

Therefore

\displaystyle\|\Lambda^{d,3,n}_{k}\|\leq_{C}h_{n}^{\gamma}(1+\|X^{(n)}_{t_{k}-}\|^{\gamma\chi})(1+\|J_{k}\|^{\gamma\chi+1}).

(4.77)

Finally, by (
2.10
) we have

\displaystyle\|\Lambda^{d,4,n}_{k}\|+\|\Lambda^{d,5,n}_{k}\|\leq_{C}\|Y^{n}_{t_{k}-}-X^{(n)}_{t_{k}-}\|\leq_{C}U^{n}_{t_{k}-}.

(4.78)

Collecting the above estimates, we get

\displaystyle\|\Delta_{t_{k}}^{n}-\Delta_{t_{k}-}^{n}\|^{q}\leq_{C}\|\Delta_{t_{k}-}^{n}\|^{q}\|J_{k}\|^{q}+(U^{n}_{t_{k}-})^{q}\|J_{k}\|^{q}+h_{n}^{\gamma q}(1+\|X^{(n)}_{t_{k}-}\|^{\gamma\chi q+q})\|J_{k}\|^{\gamma\chi q+q}+(U^{n}_{t_{k}-})^{q}.

(4.79)

Taking

\displaystyle\gamma=\frac{p-q}{q\chi}\wedge 1

(4.80)

we get

\displaystyle\|\Delta_{t_{k}}^{n}-\Delta_{t_{k}-}^{n}\|^{q}\leq_{C}\|\Delta_{t_{k}-}^{n}\|^{q}\|J_{k}\|^{q}+(U^{n}_{t_{k}-})^{q}(1+\|J_{k}\|^{q})+h_{n}^{\frac{p-q}{\chi}\wedge q}(1+\|X^{(n)}_{t_{k}-}\|^{p})(1+\|J_{k}\|^{p}).

(4.81)

Recall that
J_{k}
are identically distributed, independent of
\mathcal{F}_{t_{k}-}

for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, and have finite
p
-th moment, hence

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}^{(N)}_{t_{1},\dots,t_{N}}\Big[\|\Delta_{t_{k}}^{n}-\Delta_{t_{k}-}^{n}\|^{q}\Big|\mathcal{F}_{t_{k}-}\Big]\leq_{C}\|\Delta_{t_{k}-}^{n}\|^{q}+(U^{n}_{t_{k}-})^{q}h_{n}^{\frac{p-q}{\chi}\wedge q}(1+\|X^{(n)}_{t_{k}-}\|^{p}).

(4.82)

d)
Finally, we note that

\displaystyle|U^{n}_{t_{k}}-U^{n}_{t_{k}-}|\leq_{C}1,

(4.83)

and therefore

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}^{(N)}_{t_{1},\dots,t_{N}}\Big[h_{n}^{1\wedge\frac{q}{2}}(U_{t_{k}}^{n})^{q}

\displaystyle+h_{n}^{(\frac{p-q}{\chi}\wedge q)+(1\wedge\frac{q}{2})}(U_{t_{k}}^{n})^{(p-q)\wedge(q\chi)}\Big|\mathcal{F}_{t_{k}-}\Big]

(4.84)

\displaystyle\leq h_{n}^{1\wedge\frac{q}{2}}(U^{n}_{t_{k}-})^{q}+h_{n}^{1\wedge\frac{q}{2}}(U_{t_{k}-}^{n})^{(p-q)\wedge(q\chi)}+h_{n}^{1\wedge\frac{q}{2}}.

Summarizing the above estimates, we get the required inequality.
∎

Recall that we have assumed the number
N
of big jumps and their time instants
t_{k},k=1,\dots,N

to be fixed. In what follows, we will study separately two scenarios.
Let

\displaystyle C_{N}^{n}=\Big\{(t_{1},\dots,t_{N})\colon t_{k}-t_{k-1}>h_{n},\quad k=2,\dots,N\Big\}

(4.85)

be the “typical” set of well separated large jump times, and let

D_{N}^{n}=(C_{N}^{n})^{c}
be
its complement on which
two large jumps can occur within one
h_{n}
-step of the numercal scheme.

Lemma 4.7
.

For any
q\in(0,p)
,
there exists
C\in(0,\infty)
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbf{E}^{(N)}_{t_{1},\dots,t_{N}}\Big[\|\Delta_{t}^{n}\|^{q}\Big]\leq C^{N+1}h_{n}^{\frac{p-q}{\chi}\wedge q}(1+2\|x\|^{p})+(2N+1)C^{N+1}h_{n}^{\frac{q}{2}\wedge 1}

(4.86)

for
n
large enough uniformly over

N\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
N
-tuples
\{t_{1},\dots,t_{N}\}\in C^{n}_{N}
.

Proof.

For
N\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, denote

\displaystyle L_{k}^{n}

\displaystyle=\|\Delta_{t_{k}-}^{n}\|^{q},\quad k=1,\dots,N+1,\quad

\displaystyle L_{0}^{n}=0,

(4.87)

\displaystyle u_{k}^{n}

\displaystyle=(U_{t_{k}-}^{n})^{q}+h_{n}^{1\wedge\frac{q}{2}}(U_{t_{k}}^{n}-)^{(p-q)\wedge(q\chi)},\quad k=1,\dots,N+1,\quad

\displaystyle u_{0}^{n}=0,

\displaystyle\Xi_{k}^{n}

\displaystyle=h_{n}^{\frac{p-q}{\chi}\wedge q}(1+\|X^{n}_{t_{k}-}\|^{p}+\|X^{(n)}_{t_{k}-}\|^{p}),\quad k=1,\dots,N+1,\quad

\displaystyle\Xi_{0}^{n}=h_{n}^{\frac{p-q}{\chi}\wedge q}(1+2\|x\|^{p}).

1. By Lemma
4.5
and the Fatou lemma, there is
C_{1}\in(0,\infty)
such that, for
k=0,\dots,N

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}^{(N)}_{t_{1},\dots,t_{N}}\Big[L^{n}_{k+1}\Big|\mathcal{F}_{t_{k}-}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{t\in[t_{k},t_{k+1})}\mathbf{E}^{(N)}_{t_{1},\dots,t_{N}}\Big[\|\Delta_{t}^{n}\|^{q}\Big|\mathcal{F}_{t_{k}-}\Big]

(4.88)

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\Big(\|\Delta_{t_{k}-}^{n}\|^{q}+h_{n}^{\frac{p-q}{\chi}\wedge q}(1+\|X_{t_{k}-}^{n}\|^{p}+\|X_{t_{k}-}^{(n)}\|^{p})

\displaystyle+(U^{n}_{t_{k}-})^{q}+h_{n}^{1\wedge\frac{q}{2}}(U_{t_{k}}^{n}-)^{(p-q)\wedge(q\chi)}+h_{n}^{\frac{q}{2}\wedge 1}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}L_{k}^{n}+C_{1}\Xi_{k}^{n}+C_{1}u_{k}^{n}+C_{1}h_{n}^{\frac{q}{2}\wedge 1}.

2. By Lemma
4.3
, the Fatou lemma, and (
4.72
)

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}^{(N)}_{t_{1},\dots,t_{N}}\Big[\|X^{n}_{t_{k+1}-}\|^{p}\Big|\mathcal{F}_{t_{k}-}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{t\in[t_{k},t_{k+1})}\mathbf{E}^{(N)}_{t_{1},\dots,t_{N}}\Big[\|X^{n}_{t}\|^{p}\Big|\mathcal{F}_{t_{k}-}\Big]

(4.89)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbf{E}^{(N)}_{t_{1},\dots,t_{N}}\Big[\sup_{t\in[t_{k},t_{k+1})}\mathbf{E}^{(N)}_{t_{1},\dots,t_{N}}\Big[\|X^{n}_{t}\|^{p}\Big|\mathcal{F}_{t_{k}}\Big]\Big|\mathcal{F}_{t_{k}-}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq_{C}\mathbf{E}^{(N)}_{t_{1},\dots,t_{N}}\Big[1+\|X^{n}_{t_{k}}\|^{p}\Big|\mathcal{F}_{t_{k}-}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq_{C}1+\|X^{n}_{t_{k}-}\|^{p}.

In addition,
X^{(n)}_{t_{k+1}-}=X^{n}_{\eta^{n}_{t_{k+1}}}
with
\eta^{n}_{t_{k+1}}\in[t_{k},t_{k+1})
, so that analogously

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}^{(N)}_{t_{1},\dots,t_{N}}\Big[\|X^{(n)}_{t_{k+1}-}\|^{p}\Big|\mathcal{F}_{t_{k}-}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq_{C}\mathbf{E}^{(N)}_{t_{1},\dots,t_{N}}\Big[1+\|X^{n}_{t_{k}}\|^{p}\Big|\mathcal{F}_{t_{k}-}\Big]

(4.90)

\displaystyle\leq_{C}1+\|X^{n}_{t_{k}-}\|^{p}.

Therefore there is a constant
C_{2}\in(0,\infty)
such that

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}^{(N)}_{t_{1},\dots,t_{N}}\Big[\Xi^{n}_{k+1}\Big|\mathcal{F}_{t_{k}-}\Big]\leq C_{2}\Xi^{n}_{k}.

(4.91)

For
k=0
this estimate follows directly from (
4.17
).

3. Finally, under (
4.85
) we have

\displaystyle u^{n}_{k+1}

\displaystyle=(h_{n}+\|B_{t_{k+1}}-B_{\eta^{n}_{t_{k+1}}}\|+\|\widehat{Z}_{t_{k+1}-}-\widehat{Z}_{\eta^{n}_{t_{k+1}}}\|)^{q}

(4.92)

\displaystyle+h_{n}^{1\wedge\frac{q}{2}}(h_{n}+\|B_{t_{k+1}}-B_{\eta^{n}_{t_{k+1}}}\|+\|\widehat{Z}_{t_{k+1}-}-\widehat{Z}_{\eta^{n}_{t_{k+1}}}\|)^{(p-q)\wedge(q\chi)},

which is independent on
\mathcal{F}_{t_{k}-}
because
\eta^{n}_{t_{k+1}}>t_{k}
. Hence by (
4.56
)
there exists
C_{3}\in(0,\infty)
such that

\mathbf{E}^{(N)}_{t_{1},\dots,t_{N}}\Big[u_{k+1}\Big|\mathcal{F}_{t_{k}-}\Big]\leq C_{3}h_{n}^{\frac{q}{2}\wedge 1},\quad k=0,\dots,N.

(4.93)

We can summarize these estimates as follows. Let
C_{4}=\max\{C_{1},C_{2},C_{3}\}
, then

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}^{(N)}_{t_{1},\dots,t_{N}}\left[\left.\begin{pmatrix}L_{k+1}^{n}\\
\Xi_{k+1}^{n}\\
u_{k+1}^{n}\\
\end{pmatrix}\right|\mathcal{F}_{t_{k}-}\right]\leq C_{4}\begin{pmatrix}1&1&1\\
0&1&0\\
0&0&0\\
\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}L_{k}^{n}\\
\Xi_{k}^{n}\\
u_{k}^{n}\\
\end{pmatrix}+C_{4}h_{n}^{\frac{q}{2}\wedge 1}\begin{pmatrix}1\\
0\\
1\\
\end{pmatrix},\quad k=0,\dots,N.

(4.94)

Denote for a vector
v=(v^{1},v^{2},v^{3})^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
and a matrix
Q\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}

\displaystyle\|v\|_{1}:=|v_{1}|+|v_{2}|+|v_{3}|,\quad\|Q\|_{1}:=\sup_{\|v\|_{1}=1}\|Qv\|_{1}.

(4.95)

Then it is a simple calculation that

\displaystyle\left\|\begin{pmatrix}1&1&1\\
0&1&0\\
0&0&0\\
\end{pmatrix}\right\|_{1}=2,\quad\left\|\begin{pmatrix}1\\
0\\
1\\
\end{pmatrix}\right\|_{1}=2.

(4.96)

Using (
4.88
), we get then
for each
k=0,\dots,N
and
C=(2C_{4})\vee 1\geq C_{1},

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[t_{k},t_{k+1)}}\mathbf{E}^{(N)}_{t_{1},\dots,t_{N}}\|\Delta_{t}^{n}\|^{q}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\mathbf{E}^{(N)}_{t_{1},\dots,t_{N}}\left\|\begin{pmatrix}L_{k}^{n}\\
\Xi_{k}^{n}\\
u_{k}^{n}\\
\end{pmatrix}\right\|_{1}+C_{1}h_{n}^{\frac{q}{2}\wedge 1}

(4.97)

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}(2C_{4})^{k}\left\|\left(\begin{array}[]{c}L_{0}^{n}\\
\Xi_{0}^{n}\\
u_{0}^{n}\\
\end{array}\right)\right\|_{1}+2h_{n}^{\frac{q}{2}\wedge 1}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}(2C_{4})^{j+1}+C_{1}h_{n}^{\frac{q}{2}\wedge 1}

\displaystyle\leq C^{k+1}h_{n}^{\frac{p-q}{\chi}\wedge q}(1+2\|x\|^{p})+(2k+1)C^{k+1}h_{n}^{\frac{q}{2}\wedge 1}.

Since

\displaystyle\Delta_{t_{N+1}-}=\Delta_{t_{N}}\hbox{ a.s.,}

(4.98)

we finally get

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbf{E}^{(N)}_{t_{1},\dots,t_{N}}\|\Delta_{t}^{n}\|^{q}

\displaystyle=\max_{k=0,\dots,N}\sup_{t\in[t_{k},t_{k+1)}}\mathbf{E}^{(N)}_{t_{1},\dots,t_{N}}\|\Delta_{t}^{n}\|^{q}

(4.99)

\displaystyle\leq C^{N+1}h_{n}^{\frac{p-q}{\chi}\wedge q}(1+2\|x\|^{p})+(2N+1)C^{N+1}h_{n}^{\frac{q}{2}\wedge 1}.

∎

Without restriction (
4.85
) on the times
(t_{1},\dots,t_{N})
, we still have the following weaker version of Lemma
4.7
.

Lemma 4.8
.

For any
q\in(0,p)
,
there exists
C\in(0,\infty)
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbf{E}^{(N)}_{t_{1},\dots,t_{N}}\Big[\|\Delta_{t}^{n}\|^{p}\Big]\leq C^{N+1}h_{n}^{\frac{p-q}{\chi}\wedge q}(1+2\|x\|^{p})+(2N+1)C^{N+1}

(4.100)

for
n
large enough uniformly over

N\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
N
-tuples
\{t_{1},\dots,t_{N}\}\subset(0,T)
.

Proof.

The proof repeats the proof of Lemma
4.7
, with just two minor changes, which we explain now.
When (
4.85
) fails, several time instants, say,
t_{k},t_{k+1}
, may belong
to the same discretization interval

\displaystyle{}[\eta^{n}_{t_{k+1}},\eta^{n}_{t_{k+1}}+h_{n})=[\eta^{n}_{t_{k}},\eta^{n}_{t_{k}}+h_{n}).

(4.101)

In this case (
4.90
) is not true. However, in this case we have

X^{(n)}_{t_{k+1}-}=X^{(n)}_{t_{k}-}
and (
4.91
) still holds true.

The estimate (
4.92
) in this case fails to be true, and instead we have

\displaystyle u^{n}_{k+1}

\displaystyle\leq_{C}(\|B_{t_{k+1}}-B_{t_{k}}\|+\|\widehat{Z}_{t_{k+1}-}-\widehat{Z}_{t_{k}-}\|)^{q}+\|J_{k}\|^{q}

(4.102)

\displaystyle+h_{n}^{1\wedge\frac{q}{2}}(h_{n}+\|B_{t_{k+1}}-B_{t_{k}}\|+\|\widehat{Z}_{t_{k+1}-}-\widehat{Z}_{t_{k}}\|)^{(p-q)\wedge(q\chi)}+h_{n}^{1\wedge\frac{q}{2}}\|J_{k}\|^{(p-q)\wedge(q\chi)}+u_{k}.

Therefore, instead of (
4.93
), we arrive to the estimate

\mathbf{E}^{(N)}_{t_{1},\dots,t_{N}}\Big[u_{k+1}\Big|\mathcal{F}_{t_{k}-}\Big]\leq C_{3}(h_{n}^{\frac{q}{2}\wedge 1}+1+u_{k}).

(4.103)

Repeating literally the rest of the proof of Lemma
4.7
with (
4.93
) replaced by a weaker estimate (
4.103
) for every
k
, we get the required statement.
∎

Finally, we prove Theorem
1.3
.

Proof.

We recall that conditioned on the event
\{N_{T}=N\}
, the jump times of the compound Poisson process
Q

have law of the uniform order statistics on the interval
[0,T]
. Hence by the formula of the total probability
we get

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbf{E}\|\Delta_{t}^{n}\|^{q}=\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda T}\sum_{N=0}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda^{N}}{T^{N}}\idotsint_{0<t_{1}\dots<t_{N}<T}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbf{E}^{(N)}_{t_{1},\dots,t_{N}}\Big[\|\Delta_{t}^{n}\|^{q}\Big]\,\mathrm{d}t_{1}\dots\mathrm{d}t_{N}.

(4.104)

By Lemmas
4.7
and
4.8
, there is
C\in(0,\infty)
such that for all
N\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, all
N
-tuples

\{t_{1},\dots,t_{N}\}\subset(0,T)
and
n
large enough

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbf{E}^{(N)}_{t_{1},\dots,t_{N}}\Delta_{t}^{n}\|^{q}

\displaystyle\leq C^{N+1}h_{n}^{\frac{p-q}{\chi}\wedge q}(1+2\|x\|^{p})+(2N+1)C^{N+1}h_{n}^{\frac{q}{2}\wedge 1},\quad(t_{1},\dots,t_{N})\in C_{N}^{n},

(4.105)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbf{E}^{(N)}_{t_{1},\dots,t_{N}}\|\Delta_{t}^{n}\|^{q}

\displaystyle\leq C^{N+1}h_{n}^{\frac{p-q}{\chi}\wedge q}(1+2\|x\|^{p})+(2N+1)C^{N+1},\quad(t_{1},\dots,t_{N})\in D_{N}^{n}.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\int_{t_{1}<\cdots<t_{N}}\,\mathrm{d}t_{1}\dots\mathrm{d}t_{N}=\frac{T^{N}}{N!},

(4.106)

\displaystyle\int_{C_{N}^{n}}\,\mathrm{d}t_{1}\dots\mathrm{d}t_{N}=\int_{Nh_{n}}^{T}\mathrm{d}t_{N}\int_{(N-1)h_{n}}^{t_{N}-h_{n}}\,\mathrm{d}t_{N-1}\cdots\int_{h_{n}}^{t_{3}-h_{n}}\,\mathrm{d}t_{2}\int_{0}^{t_{2}-h_{n}}\,\mathrm{d}t_{1}=\frac{(T-Nh_{n})^{N}_{+}}{N!}\leq\frac{T^{N}}{N!},

\displaystyle\int_{D_{N}^{n}}\mathrm{d}t_{1}\dots\mathrm{d}t_{N}=\frac{T^{N}}{N!}\Big(1-\Big(1-\frac{Nh_{n}}{T}\Big)^{N}_{+}\Big)\leq\frac{T^{N}}{N!}\cdot\frac{N^{2}h_{n}}{T}.

Hence

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|\Delta_{t}^{n}\|^{q}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{N=0}^{\infty}\frac{C^{N+1}T^{N}}{N!}\right)h_{n}^{\frac{p-q}{\chi}\wedge q}(1+2\|x\|^{p})+\left(\sum_{N=0}^{\infty}(2N+1)\frac{C^{N+1}T^{N}}{N!}\right)h_{n}^{\frac{q}{2}\wedge 1}

(4.107)

\displaystyle+\left(\sum_{N=0}^{\infty}\frac{N^{2}h_{n}}{T}\frac{C^{N+1}T^{N}}{N!}\right)h_{n}^{\frac{p-q}{\chi}\wedge q}(1+2\|x\|^{p})+\left(\sum_{N=0}^{\infty}(2N+1)\frac{N^{2}h_{n}}{T}\frac{C^{N+1}T^{N}}{N!}\right)

\displaystyle\leq_{C}h_{n}^{\frac{p-q}{\chi}\wedge q}(1+2\|x\|^{p})+h_{n}^{\frac{q}{2}\wedge 1}

\displaystyle\leq_{C}h_{n}^{\delta_{q}}(1+\|x\|^{p}).

∎

Proof of Theorem
1.4

Proof of Theorem
1.4
follows the line of the one of Theorem
1.3
, with one additional step which improves the moment estimates from Lemma
4.3
to maximal moment estimates.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
r\in[2,\infty)
. Then, for any
\gamma\in[0,1]

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}^{(N)}_{t_{1},\dots,t_{N}}\Big[\sup_{t\in[t_{k},t_{k+1})}\|\Delta_{t}^{n}\|^{r}\Big|\mathcal{F}_{t_{k}}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq_{C}\|\Delta_{t_{k}}^{n}\|^{r}+h_{n}^{r\gamma+\frac{1}{2}}(1+\|X_{t_{k}}^{(n)}\|^{r\gamma\chi+r})+h_{n}^{r\gamma}(1+\|X^{n}_{t_{k}}\|^{r\gamma\chi+r})

(5.1)

\displaystyle+h_{n}(U_{t_{k}}^{n})^{r}+h_{n}^{r\gamma+\frac{1}{2}}(U_{t_{k}}^{n})^{r\gamma\chi}+h_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

for
n
large enough uniformly over

N\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, partitions
\{t_{1},\dots,t_{N}\}
, and
k=0,\dots,N
.

Proof.

We use notation of Lemma
4.3
. We also denote

\displaystyle\Xi_{\gamma,r}^{n,k}=h_{n}^{r\gamma+1}(1+\|X_{t_{k}}^{(n)}\|^{r\gamma\chi+r})+h_{n}^{r\gamma}(1+\|X^{n}_{t_{k}}\|^{r\gamma\chi+r})+h_{n}(U_{t_{k}}^{n})^{r}+h_{n}^{r\gamma+1}(U_{t_{k}}^{n})^{r\gamma\chi}+h_{n}.

(5.2)

By (
4.37
), we have

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[t_{k},t_{k+1})}\|\Delta_{t}^{n}\|^{r}

\displaystyle\leq\|\Delta_{t_{k}}^{n}\|^{r}+\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\big(\mathrm{D}_{s}^{n,k}\big)_{+}\,\mathrm{d}s+\sup_{t\in[t_{k},t_{k+1})}|\mathrm{M}_{t}^{n,k}|.

(5.3)

The drift part has already been treated in the proof of Lemma
4.3
, where we obtained the estimate

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\Big[\big(\mathrm{D}_{s}^{n,k}\big)_{+}\Big|\mathcal{F}_{t_{k}}\Big]\,\mathrm{d}s\leq_{C}\|\Delta_{t_{k}}^{n}\|^{r}

\displaystyle+\Xi_{\gamma,r}^{n,k}.

(5.4)

For the martingale part, by the Burkholder–Davis–Gundy inequality we have

(5.9)

\displaystyle+\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\int_{\|z\|\leq 1}\mathbf{E}^{(N)}_{t_{1},\dots,t_{N}}\Big[\Big\|\sum_{i=1}^{4}\Lambda_{t}^{c,i,n}(z)\Big\|^{2r}\Big|\mathcal{F}_{t_{k}}\Big]\,\nu(\mathrm{d}z)\,\mathrm{d}t.

Notation

In this paper we use the following notation.
By
\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
we denote the space of square
d
-dimensional real valued matrices.
For a matrix
b\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
,
b^{T}
denotes the
transposed matrix. The identity matrix is denoted by
\mathrm{Id}
.
Vectors (columns) in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
are denoted by
x=(x^{1},\dots,x^{d})^{T}
, and for
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

\displaystyle\langle x,y\rangle=x^{T}y=x^{1}y^{1}+\cdots+x^{d}y^{d}

(9.1)

is the usual scalar product with the associated Euclidean norm

\displaystyle\|x\|=\sqrt{\langle x,x\rangle}=\sqrt{(x^{1})^{2}+\cdots+(x^{d})^{2}}.

(9.2)

The tensor product
x\otimes y
of two vectors
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
is the linear operator (matrix)

x\otimes y=xy^{T}=(x^{i}y^{j})_{1\leq i,j\leq d}

For vector-valued functions
A,\Phi\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
etc, we write

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}A^{1}\\
\vdots\\
A^{d}\\
\end{pmatrix},\quad A_{x}=\begin{pmatrix}A^{1}_{x_{1}}&\cdots&A^{1}_{x_{d}}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
A^{d}_{x_{1}}&\cdots&A^{d}_{x_{d}}\\
\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}A_{x_{1}},\ldots,A_{x_{d}}\end{pmatrix},

(9.3)

\displaystyle A^{i}_{x}

\displaystyle=(A^{i}_{x_{1}},\ldots,A^{i}_{x_{d}}),\quad i=1,\dots,d.

By
A_{xx}
we denote the second-order gradient tensor
(A^{i}_{x^{j}x^{k}})
with the components

\displaystyle A_{x^{i}x^{j}}=\begin{pmatrix}A^{1}_{x^{i}x^{j}}\\
\vdots\\
A^{d}_{x^{i}x^{j}}\\
\end{pmatrix},\quad A^{i}_{xx}:=\begin{pmatrix}A^{i}_{x^{1}x^{1}}&\cdots&A^{i}_{x^{1}x^{d}}\\
\cdots\\
A^{i}_{x^{1}x^{d}}&\cdots&A^{i}_{x^{d}x^{d}}\\
\end{pmatrix}.

(9.4)

Let
b\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
be a matrix valued mapping.
We denote

\displaystyle\operatorname{tr}(A_{xx}bb^{T})=\begin{pmatrix}\operatorname{tr}(A^{1}_{xx}bb^{T})\\
\vdots\\
\operatorname{tr}(A^{d}_{xx}bb^{T})\\
\end{pmatrix}.

(9.5)

For a matrix-valued function
b
, we use the norm

\displaystyle\|b(x)\|

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}|b^{i}_{j}(x)|^{2}}=\sqrt{\operatorname{tr}(b(x)b^{T}(x))}=\sqrt{\operatorname{tr}(b^{T}(x)b(x))}.

(9.6)

For the tensors
A_{xx}

\Phi_{xx}

\displaystyle\operatorname{tr}(\Phi^{i}_{xx}(x)b(x)b^{T}(x))\leq\operatorname{tr}(\Phi^{i}_{xx}(x))\operatorname{tr}(b(x)b^{T}(x))\leq\operatorname{tr}(\Phi^{i}_{xx}(x))\|b(x)\|^{2}\leq\sqrt{d}\|\Phi^{i}_{xx}(x)\|\|b(x)\|^{2}.

For real valued functions
f
,
g

f(x)\leq_{C}g(x)
if there exists a constant
C\in(0,\infty)
such that

\displaystyle f(x)\leq Cg(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

(9.10)

and we do not need this constant for a further reference. In case that functions
f
,
g

depend on additional parameters
t,n,\omega

For
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
, we denote
a\wedge b:=\min\{a,b\}
,
a_{+}:=\max\{a,0\}
, and
a_{-}:=\max\{-a,0\}
.

We often use the following elementary inequalities: for any
\gamma\in[0,1]
,

\displaystyle a\wedge b\leq a^{\gamma}b^{1-\gamma},\quad a,b\in(0,\infty),

(9.11)

and for any fixed
r\in(0,\infty)
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{k=1}^{m}a_{k}\Big)^{r}\leq_{C}\sum_{k=1}^{m}a_{k}^{r},\quad a_{1},\dots,a_{m}\in[0,\infty).

(9.12)

We also use two Young inequalities: for
a,b\in[0,\infty)
we have

\displaystyle ab^{r-1}\leq\frac{1}{r}a^{r}+\frac{r-1}{r}b^{r},\quad r\in[1,\infty),

(9.13)

\displaystyle a^{2}b^{r-2}\leq\frac{2}{r}a^{r}+\frac{r-2}{r}b^{r},\quad r\in[2,\infty).

For
\varphi\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R})
, the following Taylor formulae hold true:

\displaystyle\varphi(x+y)-\varphi(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\langle y,\varphi_{x}(x+\theta y)\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\theta,

(9.14)

\displaystyle\varphi(x+y)-\varphi(x)-\langle\varphi_{x}(x),y\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\langle y,\varphi_{xx}(x+\theta y)y\rangle(1-\theta)\,\mathrm{d}\theta.

(9.15)

Eventially, the norm mapping
x\mapsto\|x\|^{r}
,
r\in[2,\infty)
, has the following derivatives:

\displaystyle\partial_{x}\|x\|^{r}

\displaystyle=r\|x\|^{r-2}x^{T},

(9.16)

\displaystyle\partial_{xx}\|x\|^{r}

\displaystyle=r(r-2)\|x\|^{r-4}(x\otimes x)+r\|x\|^{r-2}\mathrm{Id}.

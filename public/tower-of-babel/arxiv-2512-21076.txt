Blurb-Refined Inference from Crowdsourced Book Reviews using Hierarchical Genre Mining with Dual-Path Graph Convolutions
Suraj Kumar, Utsav Kumar Nareti, Soumi Chattopadhyay, Chandranath Adak, Prolay Mallick

Abstract
Accurate book genre classification is fundamental to digital library organization, content discovery, and personalized recommendation. Existing approaches typically model genre prediction as a flat, single-label task, ignoring hierarchical genre structure and relying heavily on noisy, subjective user reviews, which often degrade classification reliability. We propose HiGeMine, a two-phase hierarchical genre mining framework that robustly integrates user reviews with authoritative book blurbs. In the first phase, HiGeMine employs a zero-shot semantic alignment strategy to filter reviews, retaining only those semantically consistent with the corresponding blurb, thereby mitigating noise, bias, and irrelevance. In the second phase, we introduce a dual-path, two-level graph-based classification architecture: a coarse-grained Level-1 binary classifier distinguishes fiction from non-fiction, followed by Level-2 multi-label classifiers for fine-grained genre prediction. Inter-genre dependencies are explicitly modeled using a label co-occurrence graph, while contextual representations are derived from pretrained language models applied to the filtered textual content. To facilitate systematic evaluation, we curate a new hierarchical book genre dataset. Extensive experiments demonstrate that HiGeMine consistently outperformed strong baselines across hierarchical genre classification tasks. The proposed framework offers a principled and effective solution for leveraging both structured and unstructured textual data in hierarchical book genre analysis.

Problem Formulation

Given a book corpus consisting of a set of
n
books,
\mathcal{S}=\{\mathcal{S}_{1},\mathcal{S}_{2},\dots,\mathcal{S}_{n}\}
, where each book
\mathcal{S}_{i}
is associated with a blurb
\mathcal{B}_{i}
, a set of user reviews
\mathcal{R}_{i}=\{\mathcal{R}^{i}_{1},\mathcal{R}^{i}_{2},\ldots,\mathcal{R}^{i}_{q}\}
, and a two-level genre labels
\mathcal{Y}^{i}=\{\mathcal{Y}^{i}_{1},\mathcal{Y}^{i}_{2}\}
, where
\mathcal{Y}^{i}_{1}\in\{0,1\}
denotes the coarse label (
fiction
or
non-fiction
), and where
\mathcal{Y}^{i}_{2}
denotes the fine-grained multi-label genres:

\mathcal{Y}^{i}_{2}\in\begin{cases}\{0,1\}^{k_{1}},&\text{if }\mathcal{Y}^{i}_{1}=0~\text{(\emph{fiction})}\\
\{0,1\}^{k_{2}},&\text{if }\mathcal{Y}^{i}_{1}=1~\text{(\emph{non-fiction})};\end{cases}

k1
and
k_{2}
are fiction and non-fiction genre counts.
Let
\mathcal{B}=\{\mathcal{B}_{1},\mathcal{B}_{2},\dots,\mathcal{B}_{n}\}
and
\mathcal{R}=\{\mathcal{R}_{1},\mathcal{R}_{2},\ldots,\mathcal{R}_{n}\}
.
The objective is to
utilize both book blurbs and user reviews to perform two-stage inference: first, a Level-1 binary classification to distinguish fiction from non-fiction, and then a Level-2 multi-label classification to predict fine-grained genres conditioned on the Level-1 output.

The first stage performs fiction/non-fiction classification, which then activates the corresponding second-stage multi-label classifier for fine-grained genre prediction (

4.1
Review Filtering and Vocabulary Creation

User reviews offer a rich and crowdsourced resource for genre prediction, but are often noisy due to subjectivity and label ambiguity, leading to over-prediction and reduced accuracy. To mitigate this, we use the book blurb as a reliable anchor to guide a review filtering process that retains only contextually relevant content.

As illustrated in Fig.
2
, we adopt a zero-shot strategy using a BERT-based encoder
\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{R}}

6
to encode each book’s blurb and reviews into contextual embeddings
b_{i}
and
\delta^{i}_{j}
. Before encoding, standard preprocessing (e.g., removing emojis, hyperlinks, and repeated filler tokens) is applied. The semantic similarity between the blurb and each review is calculated using cosine similarity,
and the reviews are retained if their similarity score
d^{i}_{j}
exceeds a dynamic threshold
\Psi
.
Selected reviews are concatenated into a consolidated review
\mathcal{P}_{i}
, capturing the most informative content. This nonparametric filtering approach is fast, scalable, and model-agnostic. If the blurb is too short, all reviews are retained without filtering. From the filtered blurbs and reviews, we extract a vocabulary
\mathcal{T}=\{\mathcal{T}_{1},\mathcal{T}_{2},\ldots,\mathcal{T}_{m}\}
, as individual word semantics effectively aid genre classification.

4.2
Genre Classification: Level-1 Modeling

In the second phase, HiGeMine performs hierarchical genre prediction using the consolidated review and blurb.
Level-1 classification uses the input triplet
(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{P},\mathcal{T})
to train a model
\Phi_{1}
, where

{\mathcal{T}}
is the shared token set from the filtered review and blurb. As shown in Fig.
3
(a),
\Phi_{1}
employs dual-path GCNs: one modeling blurb-token interactions (
{\mathcal{B}}\text{-}{\mathcal{T}}
network) and the other review-token interactions (
{\mathcal{P}}\text{-}{\mathcal{T}}
network). The logits from both paths are combined using a weighting factor
\lambda_{1}
to predict the high-level label
\mathcal{Y}_{1}
.

4.2.1. Graph Construction for Level-1
:
To capture modality-specific semantics, we build two heterogeneous text graphs, one for blurb and one for filtered reviews. These graphs are designed to explicitly model global word co-occurrence patterns within each modality, facilitating effective adaptation of graph convolution operations.

Blurb-Token Graph (
\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{B}}
):

To effectively capture the relationship between a book’s blurb and the vocabulary it uses, we form a heterogeneous graph

\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{B}}=(\mathbb{V}_{\mathcal{B}},~\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{B}})
, where:

•
\mathbb{V}_{\mathcal{B}}=\{\mathcal{B},\mathcal{T}\}
represents the set of nodes comprising each blurb in
\mathcal{B}
and its corresponding vocabulary tokens from
\mathcal{T}
.

•
\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{B}}=\{(\mathbb{V}_{i},\mathbb{V}_{j},\mathcal{W}_{ij})\}
is the set of edges, where
\mathcal{W}_{ij}
denotes the weight of the edge between nodes
\mathbb{V}_{i}
and
\mathbb{V}_{j}
.
The edge weights
\mathcal{W}_{ij}
are defined as:

\mathcal{W}_{ij}=\begin{cases}f^{t}(\mathbb{V}_{i},\mathbb{V}_{j}),&\text{if ~}~\mathbb{V}_{i}\in\mathcal{B},\,\mathbb{V}_{j}\in\mathcal{T}\\
f^{p^{+}}(\mathbb{V}_{i},\mathbb{V}_{j})=\max\left(\log\frac{p(\mathbb{V}_{i},\mathbb{V}_{j})}{p(\mathbb{V}_{i})~p(\mathbb{V}_{j})},~0\right),&\text{if ~}~\mathbb{V}_{i},\mathbb{V}_{j}\in\mathcal{T}\\
0,&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

(1)

where,
f^{t}(\mathbb{V}_{i},\mathbb{V}_{j})
represents the TF-IDF score
37
, quantifying the importance of the token
\mathbb{V}_{j}
in the context of the blurb
\mathbb{V}_{i}
;

f^{p^{+}}(\mathbb{V}_{i},\mathbb{V}_{j})
computes the positive point-wise mutual information (PMI)
14
, measuring the statistical association among co-occurring vocabulary tokens;

p(\mathbb{V}_{i},\mathbb{V}_{j})
is the empirical probability of co-occurrence of the two tokens in a sliding context window, and

p(\mathbb{V}_{i})
,
p(\mathbb{V}_{j})
are their individual marginal probabilities.
The graph
\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{B}}
is represented using an adjacency matrix
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{B}}
, where each entry
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{B}}(i,j)=\mathcal{W}_{ij}
denotes the weight of the edge connecting node
i
and node
j
.

Review-Token Graph (
\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{P}}
):

Analogous to the blurb-token graph
\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{B}}
, we construct a review-token graph
\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{P}}=(\mathbb{V}_{\mathcal{P}},\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{P}})
, where

\mathbb{V}_{\mathcal{P}}=\{\mathcal{P},\mathcal{T}\}
represents the set of nodes comprising user reviews
\mathcal{P}
and the shared vocabulary tokens
\mathcal{T}
,

\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{P}}
denotes the set of edges constructed using the same strategy described for
\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{B}}
, involving TF-IDF and positive PMI-based connections.
The resulting adjacency matrix
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{P}}
follows the same formulation as
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{B}}
.

Node Embeddings for Level-1
:
We leverage foundation LMs to generate rich, context-aware embeddings for nodes.

Embeddings for
{\mathcal{B}}{\in}\mathbb{V}_{\mathcal{B}}
:

We use a pre-trained encoder-based LM
6
denoted as
\mathcal{E}^{1}_{\mathcal{B}}
, fine-tuned on blurbs for binary classification. Its parameters are then frozen, and only the rest of HiGeMine is trained. This aids in capturing genre cues early, simplifying downstream learning. The resulting blurb embeddings
\mathcal{X}_{b1}=[b_{1},b_{2},\dots,b_{n}];~\forall~b_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{j}
.

Embeddings for
{\mathcal{P}}{\in}\mathbb{V}_{\mathcal{P}}
:

For Level-1, we fine-tune a separate LM encoder
\mathcal{E}^{1}_{\mathcal{P}}
, identical to
\mathcal{E}^{1}_{\mathcal{B}}
, to generate review embeddings
\mathcal{X}_{p1}=[p_{1},p_{2},\dots,p_{n}];~\forall~p_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{j}
.

Embeddings for
{\mathcal{T}}\in\mathbb{V}_{\mathcal{B}}\text{ or }\mathbb{V}_{\mathcal{P}}
:

We initialize each word node with zero vectors matching the blurb/review embedding size:

\mathcal{X}_{t1}=[t_{1},t_{2},\dots,t_{m}];~\forall~t_{i}\in\{0\}^{j}
.
Given Level-1’s lightweight nature, we skip pre-trained word embeddings to minimize overhead.

4.2.2. Model Architecture for Level-1
:
As illustrated in Fig.
3
(a), we utilize dual-path graph learning tailored for each modality, where each path follows an identical architecture comprising a graph convolution unit
\Phi_{g}
followed by a feed-forward network
\Phi_{d}
with two fully connected layers.
The graph convolution unit
\Phi_{g}(.)
, applied independently in the blurb-token and review-token networks, captures rich semantic interactions by leveraging the structured relationships in the text graphs. Through message passing, each node, whether a document or a word, aggregates contextual signals from its neighbors, allowing the model to identify key terms and co-occurrence patterns. This facilitates the learning of context-aware representations crucial for accurate genre prediction.
Fig.
3
(c) highlights the internal function of
\Phi_{g}
, which consists of two stacked GCN layers, denoted
\Phi_{gc}
, and is mathematically defined as:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Phi_{gc}(\hat{\mathcal{A}},\mathcal{X}_{1})=\sigma(\hat{\mathcal{A}}\cdot\mathcal{X}_{1}\cdot\mathbb{W}+\theta);~~\hat{\mathcal{A}}=\mathcal{D}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\cdot(\mathcal{A}+\mathbb{I})\cdot\mathcal{D}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\end{split}

(2)

where,
\hat{\mathcal{A}}
is normalized adjacency matrix for
{\mathcal{A}}
, where,
{\mathcal{A}}={\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{B}}
or
{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{P}}
,

\mathcal{D}
is a diagonal matrix;
\mathcal{D}_{ii}=\sum_{j}\mathcal{A}_{ij}
.
The identity matrix
\mathbb{I}
is used to preserve the original features of each node.
\mathcal{X}_{1}
is the node feature embedding matrix.
Here,
\sigma
is an activation function, while
\mathbb{W}
and
\theta
are learnable weight and bias parameters, respectively.
In
\Phi_{g}
, the outputs from the first and second GCN layers are concatenated with the initial features, and the resulting feature matrix is truncated to retain only the representations corresponding to the document nodes (i.e., blurbs or reviews), as captured in Eq.
3
:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{X}^{k1}_{2}=T\left(\mathcal{X}^{k1}_{1}~||~\Phi_{gc}(.)~||~\Phi_{gc}(\Phi_{gc}(\cdot))\right);\quad k\in\{b,p\}\end{split}

(3)

where,
T(\cdot)
denotes the truncation operator that selects only the document node embeddings.
These aggregated document representations are then fed into the dense network
\Phi_{d}
, producing
\mathcal{Z}_{b1}
and
\mathcal{Z}_{p1}
for the
\mathcal{B}\text{-}\mathcal{T}
and
\mathcal{P}\text{-}\mathcal{T}
branches, respectively. A final prediction
\mathcal{Y}_{1}
is obtained by linearly combining these outputs using an adaptable hyper-parameter
\lambda_{1}
.
\lambda_{1}
is adaptively set based on the lengths of the blurb and consolidated reviews. If the blurb is too short,
\lambda_{1}=0
, excluding it from genre prediction. If the reviews are too short,
\lambda_{1}=1
, excluding their influence. Otherwise,
\lambda_{1}
is empirically set between 0 and 1 to balance both sources.

To train the Level-1 classifier, we first fine-tune the LM encoders described above. Once fine-tuned, we freeze their weights, and optimize the remaining model parameters using Binary Cross Entropy (BCE)
23
.

4.3
Genre Classification: Level-2 Modeling

Level-2 comprises two identical multi-label classifiers corresponding to the
fiction
and
non-fiction
categories, denoted as
\Phi^{f}_{2}
and
\Phi^{nf}_{2}
, respectively. As shown in Fig.
1
, the output from Level-1 governs the selection between these two classifiers via a gating mechanism. The training sets for
\Phi^{f}_{2}
and
\Phi^{nf}_{2}
are mutually exclusive, ensuring specialization in their respective domains.

Given an input tuple
(\mathcal{B}_{k},\mathcal{P}_{k},\mathcal{T}_{k},\mathcal{C}_{k})
, where
k\in\{f,nf\}
, the objective is to train the model
\Phi^{k}_{2}
to predict the corresponding multi-label genre distribution
\mathcal{Y}^{k}_{2}
.
Each Level-2 classifier adopts a similar dual-path graph learning architecture as in Level-1, with a key enhancement. Owing to their multi-label nature, these classifiers integrate Level-2 genre information to capture inter-genre co-occurrence patterns.
To this end, a dedicated label co-occurrence graph is used to effectively learn dependencies among genre labels.

The
\mathcal{B}\text{-}\mathcal{T}
and
\mathcal{P}\text{-}\mathcal{T}
networks in Level-2 follow the same structure as in Level-1, but the embedding strategies differ due to the transition from binary to multi-label classification. To address this, we fine-tune two separate LM encoders,
\mathcal{E}^{k}_{\mathcal{B}}
and
\mathcal{E}^{k}_{\mathcal{P}}
,
k\in\{f,nf\}
, tailored for multi-label objectives. The resulting blurb and review embeddings are
\mathcal{X}_{bk}=[\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{n^{\prime}}]
and
\mathcal{X}_{pk}=[\rho_{1},\ldots,\rho_{n^{\prime}}]
, where
\beta_{i},\rho_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{j}
and
n^{\prime}<n
. In contrast, word embeddings are constructed differently; we first detail this revised formulation, followed by the label co-occurrence network.

Embedding for
{\mathcal{T}_{k}}
:

To construct word embeddings
\mathcal{X}_{tk}=[\tau_{1},\tau_{2},\ldots,\tau_{m}]
, where each
\tau_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{j}
, we encode each word
w_{i}\in\mathcal{T}_{k}
based on its distribution across genre categories. This enables the model to learn genre-sensitive semantics.
We start by defining the frequency metrics used to capture genre-aware word usage. Let
\alpha_{ik}
denote the frequency of word
w_{i}
in the blurb
\mathcal{B}_{k}
, and
\beta_{ik}
its frequency in the review
\mathcal{P}_{k}
. To represent the cumulative presence of
w_{i}
in a genre category
\mathcal{C}_{j}
, we define the aggregated frequency
\gamma_{ij}
as:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\gamma_{ij}=\sum_{\mathcal{S}_{k}\in\mathcal{C}_{j}}\alpha_{ik}+\sum_{\mathcal{S}_{k}\in\mathcal{C}_{j}}\beta_{ik};\quad\gamma_{ij}=\left|\left({\gamma_{ij}-\bar{\gamma}_{i}}\right)/{\sigma_{i}}\right|\end{split}

(4)

where,
\mathcal{S}_{k}\in\mathcal{C}_{j}
denotes all books associated with genre
\mathcal{C}_{j}
. This shows both blurb and review frequencies contribute to the genre-level representation of each word.
To account for frequency scale variations across genres, we apply z-score normalization to the genre distribution of each word.

\bar{\gamma}_{i}
and
\sigma_{i}
represent the mean and standard deviation of
w_{i}
’s frequency across all genre categories, respectively.
To ensure non-negativity and stability, we clip any negative values.

We then project each word into the embedding space by taking the dot product of its normalized genre-wise frequency vector with the genre embeddings
\mathcal{X}_{e}
that is described later;

\tau_{i}=\gamma_{i}\cdot\mathcal{X}_{e}
.
This produces a genre-aware word embedding
\tau_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{j}
to capture the semantic contribution of each genre to the word representation. This approach helps the model learn more informative, genre-sensitive word features for effective multi-label classification at Level-2.

4.3.1. Label Co-occurrence Graph
:
To effectively capture interdependencies among class labels, prior works in multi-label image classification
3
have demonstrated the benefits of modeling label correlations to enhance classification performance. Following this approach, we construct a directed graph
\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{C}}=(\mathbb{V}_{\mathcal{C}},\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{C}})
, where the node set
\mathbb{V}_{\mathcal{C}}
consists of genre class labels (as listed in Table 1 of
supp. file
), and the edge set
\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{C}}
encodes directed relationships between these labels.
To define these edges, we estimate label correlations using conditional probabilities
P(\mathcal{C}_{i}\mid\mathcal{C}_{j})
, which measure the likelihood of observing label
\mathcal{C}_{i}
given the presence of label
\mathcal{C}_{j}
. These probabilities are computed based on co-occurrence statistics derived solely from the training data. The resulting correlation matrix is denoted as
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{C}}
.
To reduce noise from infrequent co-occurrences and better highlight strong label dependencies, we refine
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{C}}
through a threshold-based strategy. Specifically, connections with weights below a lower threshold
\psi_{1}
are discarded, those above an upper threshold
\psi_{2}
are reinforced by setting their values to 1, and intermediate values are retained as-is. This selective filtering allows the model to concentrate on meaningful inter-label relationships, enhancing the robustness of multi-label genre classification.

Embedding for
\mathbb{V}_{\mathcal{C}}
:

To obtain label embeddings, we use GloVe
24
to generate word vectors for each genre label, denoted by
\mathcal{X}_{e}=[e_{1},e_{2},\dots,e_{k^{\prime}}]
. For multi-word labels, we take the average of their constituent word embeddings. To enhance expressiveness, we also introduce a learnable embedding matrix
\mathcal{X}_{l}=[l_{1},l_{2},\dots,l_{k^{\prime}}]
, initialized with the same dimensionality as GloVe. The final genre label embeddings are obtained by element-wise addition:
\mathcal{X}_{c}=\mathcal{X}_{e}+\mathcal{X}_{l}=[c_{1},c_{2},\dots,c_{k^{\prime}}]
, where
k^{\prime}\in\{k_{1},k_{2}\}
. Empirically, adding learnable components improves Level-2 genre prediction by enabling task-specific adaptation.

4.3.2. Model Architecture for Level-2
:
Fig.
3
(b) presents the detailed architecture of the Level-2 classifier. While the
\mathcal{B}\text{-}\mathcal{T}
and
\mathcal{P}\text{-}\mathcal{T}
networks follow the same structure as in Level-1, Level-2 introduces an additional label co-occurrence network,
\Phi_{c}
(refer to Fig.
3
(d)), to model inter-genre dependencies essential for multi-label classification.
\Phi_{c}
employs two GCN layers operating on the label graph
\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{C}}
, using
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{C}}
as the adjacency matrix and
\mathcal{X}_{c}
as node features, following the formulation in Eq.
2
. The GCN outputs are concatenated with the initial label embeddings and passed through a dense layer to enrich genre representations. These refined features,
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}_{c}
, are then fused with the outputs from
\mathcal{B}\text{-}\mathcal{T}
and
\mathcal{P}\text{-}\mathcal{T}
via dot products, as shown in Eq.
5
. A final prediction
\mathcal{Y}_{2}^{k}
is computed through a weighted combination controlled by an adaptable hyper-parameter
\lambda_{2}
, followed by a sigmoid activation.
\lambda_{2}
is selected in the same adaptive manner as
\lambda_{1}
, based on the relative lengths of the input sources. Genre labels are assigned based on a threshold over
\mathcal{Y}_{2}^{k}
.

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{Z}_{bk}=\mathcal{X}^{bk}_{3}\cdot{\mathcal{X}}^{\prime}_{c};\quad\mathcal{Z}_{pk}=\mathcal{X}^{pk}_{3}\cdot{\mathcal{X}}^{\prime}_{c}\\
\mathcal{Y}^{k}_{2}=\lambda_{2}\mathcal{Z}_{bk}+(1-\lambda_{2})\mathcal{Z}_{pk}\end{split}

(5)

We train the Level-2 multi-label classifier using BCE with logits loss
23
.
Furthermore, to prevent error propagation across levels, the Level-2 loss for fiction and non-fiction branches is weighted by the true Level-1 label. This ensures that only the relevant branch receives gradient updates, avoiding interference from unrelated categories.

Dataset:

To the best of our knowledge, there is hardly any public dataset available on hierarchical genre classification. This gap has motivated us to develop a two-level hierarchical genre dataset to support research in this area.
As a base source, we utilized the Bookcrossing dataset
43
, and then crawled the Goodreads (www.goodreads.com) to collect the book’s blurb and user reviews.
We report statistics of the employed dataset in Table
2
. We exhaustively classify the book into a two-level hierarchy, which is predominantly scattered into multiple genres on Goodreads.
In Level-1, books are classified as
fiction
or
non-fiction
, which are then further divided into fine-grained sub-genres in Level-2.
Table 1 and Fig. 1 of
supp. file
presents a detailed dataset overview and genre distribution.
With the help of linguistic experts, we group similar categories into broader groups covering all the facts of their representation, effectively reducing the redundancy caused by overlapping genres
43
.

Experimental Setup:

Experiments were conducted
using PyTorch 2.5.1 with an Intel Xeon W-1270 CPU, 128 GB RAM, and a 24 GB NVIDIA RTX A5000 GPU. The dataset was split in a
7{:}1{:}2
ratio for training, validation, and testing. To address Level-2 class imbalance, we applied data augmentation using Gemini
31
, enhancing model robustness through diverse variations.
Genre-wise post-augmentation statistics and model configurations are provided in Table 1 and Table 2 of the
supp. file
, respectively. All results are reported on the test set.

Evaluation Metrics:

We evaluate Level-1 using F1-score (
\mathcal{F}
) and Accuracy (
\mathcal{A}c
), while Level-2 multi-label performance is assessed using micro and macro averaged F1 (
{\mathcal{F}}_{\mu}
,
{\mathcal{F}}_{m}
) and Balanced Accuracy (
{\mathcal{BA}}\mu
,
{\mathcal{BA}}_{m}
) to handle class imbalance. Hamming Loss (
\mathcal{HL}
) captures the proportion of incorrect label predictions.

Results & Comparative Study:

To benchmark HiGeMine, we evaluate it against a diverse set of baselines, as shown in
Fig.s
4
(a)-(e).

(i) Comparison with Past Book Genre Classification Methods:

We first compare HiGeMine with three past models.

29
used user reviews with TF-IDF and LSA in a single-label setup but faced challenges with class imbalance.

7
leveraged book covers and titles with VGG16 and XGBoost but achieved limited accuracy due to weak visual cues.

32
proposed a multi-label BERT-based model using flap text, yet remained restricted to a single modality. To ensure fair comparison, we adapted all models to a unified 57-class setup (28 fiction, 29 non-fiction) and evaluated them using reviews and blurbs separately.
In contrast, HiGeMine fuses both modalities using a zero-shot semantic filtering mechanism to denoise reviews and a dual-path GCN to jointly model content and label correlations. Its hierarchical multi-label framework aligns with real-world taxonomies, enabling superior performance across settings. As shown in Fig.
4
(a), HiGeMine consistently outperformed all baselines by effectively addressing label sparsity, modality noise, and structural complexity in genre prediction.

(ii) Comparison with Hierarchical Text Classification (HTC) baselines:

We compare HiGeMine with three HTC models, HiAGM
39
, HiTIN
40
, and HILL
41
, which exploit hierarchical label structures. However, these models struggle to capture deep label dependencies and semantics. For a fair setup, we used both blurbs and reviews as input, selecting the best fusion method (addition, concatenation, self-attention, or cross-attention) for each model. As shown in Fig.
4
(b), HiGeMine significantly outperformed all three, achieving a 10.12% improvement in
\mathcal{F}_{m}
and establishing a new state-of-the-art for HTC.

(iii) Comparison with LM-based baselines:

We further compare against six LM baselines: ALBERT
13
, BERT
6
, DistilBERT
27
, ModernBERT
33
, RoBERTa
42
, and XLNet
36
within a hierarchical classification pipeline. Each model was tested with both modalities fused using the most effective strategy among addition, concatenation, self-attention, or cross-attention. As shown in Fig.
4
(c), HiGeMine consistently outperformed all LMs, achieving up to a 22.94% gain in
\mathcal{F}_{m}
over the best baseline (BERT). These improvements are driven by HiGeMine’s ability to filter noisy reviews, leverage contextual word semantics, and model structured label dependencies through graph-based learning. Additional details of baseline experiments are given in the
supp. file
.

(iv) Comparison with open-source LLMs:

With the rise of generative AI, LLMs have demonstrated impressive generalization across domains.
We benchmark HiGeMine against 3 recent open-source LLMs: Gemma2-2B
8
, Qwen3-4B
25
, and Mistral-7B
11
. As shown in Fig.
4
(d), HiGeMine outperformed all of them, with the strongest baseline, Mistral-7B, trailing by 3.78% in
\mathcal{F}_{m}
. This gap highlights the advantage of HiGeMine’s task-specific architecture and domain-aware design for book genre classification.

(v) Comparison with closed-source LLMs:
We also compare against three proprietary LLMs: Gemini-2.5-Flash
4
, DeepSeek-V3
5
, and GPT-4.1-mini
21
. As shown in Fig.
4
(e), HiGeMine maintains superior performance, achieving an
\mathcal{F}_{m}
of 56.64%, surpassing all closed-source models. This shows the strength of HiGeMine’s hierarchical taxonomy modeling, curated input filtering, and graph-based reasoning.
Notably, while open-source LLMs sometimes outperform their closed-source counterparts due to richer fine-tuning data and human-in-the-loop, HiGeMine consistently delivered stronger results, affirming its effectiveness for domain-specific hierarchical classification.

6
Conclusion

In this paper, we introduce HiGeMine, a robust framework for hierarchical book genre classification that integrates crowdsourced user reviews with semantically reliable blurbs.
To mitigate the noise and subjectivity in user-generated content, we propose a zero-shot semantic filtering mechanism that selects reviews consistent with the blurb, thereby enhancing input quality.
Our dual-path GCN architecture captures both blurb-token and review-token interactions, while modeling label co-occurrence to support multi-label prediction.
The two-level hierarchical design enables coarse-to-fine genre inference aligned with literary taxonomies. Experiments on a new dataset show that HiGeMine consistently outperformed existing flat classification models, hierarchical models, language models, and LLMs. These results underscore the value of selective content filtering and structured representation learning for noisy real-world data. Future work will explore multimodal extensions and generalization to other hierarchical domains.

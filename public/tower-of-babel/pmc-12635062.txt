Discovery of a wind-driven diamagnetic current system in the Martian magnetosphere
Authors: Yuki Harada, Robert J Lillis, Matthew O Fillingim, Shun Imajo, Takuto Minami, Yingjuan Ma, Jacob R Gruesbeck, Gina A DiBraccio, Jared R Espley
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractMagnetic fields around Mars shape the pathways of energy input into, and ion escape from, the Martian atmosphere, thereby potentially affecting the atmospheric evolution of Mars. The Martian magnetic field environment is primarily determined by the global electric current system resulting from the solar wind interaction with the upper atmosphere and crustal magnetic fields of Mars. Recently, it has been recognized that neutral wind-driven ionospheric dynamo currents generate magnetic field perturbations observable in orbits and on the surface of Mars. However, the entire system of near-Mars currents is not fully understood. Here we present a newly identified current system in the Martian magnetosphere directly coupled to the wind-driven ionospheric currents, based on the spherical harmonic analysis of magnetic field data obtained by the Mars Atmosphere and Volatile Evolution spacecraft. This magnetospheric current system can be explained by diamagnetic currents associated with magnetic fields generated by the wind-driven ionospheric dynamo currents. Our results demonstrate a close coupling between the neutral atmospheric dynamics and the high-altitude magnetospheric current system at Mars mediated by wind-driven ionospheric dynamo currents. This reveals a previously unrecognized role of the neutral atmosphere in controlling magnetic field environments of unmagnetized planets.Subject terms: Astronomy and planetary science, Physics, Planetary science, Space physics

Introduction

The solar wind interactions with planets set up a variety of global electric current systems, which play essential roles in determining magnetic field configurations and plasma dynamics in the planetary environments1. At Mars, the solar wind interaction with the upper ionosphere leads to the formation of the induced magnetosphere and associated global current system2. The global current system generated by the Mars-solar wind interaction consists of the bow shock currents, induced magnetosphere boundary (also called “magnetopause”) currents, cross-magnetotail currents, and ionospheric currents. Meanwhile, complicated local current systems are expected to be formed by plasma interactions with Martian crustal magnetic fields3, including field-aligned currents4–6 and horizontal ionospheric currents.

Another important driver of ionospheric currents at Mars is neutral winds in the thermosphere7–16. Recently, 17 derived Martian ionospheric current distributions at 150 and 250 km altitudes in Mars Solar Electric (MSE) and Mars Solar Orbital (MSO) coordinates by taking the curl of average magnetic field distributions () derived from in-situ measurements by the Mars Atmosphere and Volatile Evolution (MAVEN) mission18. This “curl-B” method can map the local currents within the spatial region sampled by MAVEN and has been applied to visualize the Martian magnetospheric current system2. The ionospheric current distributions presented in the MSE and MSO coordinates show distinct spatial patterns, each reflecting Martian ionospheric currents driven by the solar wind interaction and neutral winds, respectively17. However, since the ionospheric current peak altitude is expected to be located at 130 km in the ionospheric dynamo region12,19 below the nominal MAVEN periapsis altitude (150 km), the curl-B-derived currents likely represent only the uppermost part of the main ionospheric current layer.

In this study, we utilize spherical harmonic analysis (SHA) to derive equivalent current distributions of “internal” (below the spacecraft) and “external” (above the spacecraft) sources. We apply SHA to MAVEN-derived magnetic field distributions in a fixed altitude range of 200–300 km located above the primary ionospheric current layer under the source-free assumption (). This assumption is reasonably justified by 17’s curl-B results showing substantially smaller currents at 250 km altitude than those at 150 km altitude and by the expectation that the main current source (i.e., ionospheric dynamo region) is located at even lower altitudes of 130 km. We also note that radial currents are considerably smaller than horizontal currents in the Martian ionosphere on a global scale17. Based on the SHA results, we report on a newly identified current system in the Martian magnetosphere directly coupled to the ionospheric dynamo currents.

Results

We analyzed magnetic field data obtained by the MAVEN orbiter and derived global distributions of ionospheric and magnetospheric currents for eastward and westward interplanetary magnetic field (IMF) conditions (Fig. 1). Figure 1a1 and b1 display equivalent (horizontal) ionospheric currents derived from the horizontal components of magnetic field deviation observed above the ionospheric dynamo region under the infinite current sheet approximation20. In both IMF directions, we observe clockwise (counter-clockwise) currents in the northern (southern) hemisphere on the dayside. As pointed out by17, the clockwise and counter-clockwise current systems are consistent with the wind-driven ionospheric dynamo currents12,14. On top of the northern clockwise and southern counter-clockwise pattern, we find enhanced northward (southward) currents on dayside under the eastward (westward) IMF condition. This corresponds to the direction of the solar wind electric field (), and these -ward currents in the dayside ionosphere are known to be driven by the solar wind interaction as identified by the previous curl-B analyses2,17.

To the best of our knowledge, this high-altitude current system has not been documented in previous work. As seen from the intensities of the equivalent current functions, this newly identified current pattern (Fig. 1c4 and d4) makes a significant contribution to the overall magnetospheric current system (Fig. 1c2 and d2) as much as the known magnetospheric current systems (Fig. 1c3 and d3).

Discussion

We first discuss a generation mechanism of the dayside magnetospheric currents that flow counter-clockwise (clockwise) in the northern (southern) hemisphere in the opposite direction to the wind-driven ionospheric dynamo currents. This magnetospheric current pattern is newly identified in addition to the known -ward currents on the Martian magnetopause and bow shock. We propose the following interpretation as illustrated in Fig. 2. The wind-driven ionospheric currents generate horizontal fields, , above the ionosphere (Fig. 2a). Observations indicate that can be on the order of 10 nT (Supplementary Fig. 1b and c), comparable to the strength of draped IMF (several 10s of nT). This field could modify the current system on the induced magnetopause (typically at 800 km altitudes on the dayside), on which primarily -ward currents are driven by the outward plasma pressure gradient from the induced magnetosphere to the magnetosheath21. The combination of and the outward pressure gradient generates loops of diamagnetic currents, , on the induced magnetopause (Fig. 2a), flowing opposite to the wind-driven ionospheric currents (Fig. 2b). This secondary current system partially cancels out and strengthens in the region between the induced magnetopause and ionospheric dynamo region as observed in the horizontal magnetic field components (Supplementary Figs. 1b and c) generally stronger than the radial component (Supplementary Fig. 1a). The involvement of the wind-driven ionospheric currents and the pressure gradient at the induced magnetopause can explain the seasonal and crustal field dependencies similar to those of wind-driven ionospheric currents (Table 1).

Based on the spherical harmonic analysis of MAVEN magnetic field data obtained at 200–300 km altitudes, we derive “internal” and “external” equivalent current functions at Mars. We interpret that the “internal” equivalent current function mainly originates from ionospheric currents flowing in the ionospheric dynamo region around 130 km altitudes, while the “external” equivalent current function includes contributions from the magnetopause, bow shock, and magnetotail currents. As summarized in Table 1 and elaborated in Supplementary Information, the results show that different SHA terms depend differently on upstream conditions, seasons, and crustal magnetic fields. This suggests that multiple mechanisms drive ionospheric and magnetospheric currents with characteristic spatial patterns represented by the SHA terms. As illustrated in panel (I1) of Fig. 3, the sum of , , and terms shows equivalent currents flowing clockwise (counter-clockwise) centered at 14 LT in the northern (southern) hemisphere on the dayside. These properties are consistent with the expected global pattern of ionospheric dynamo currents driven by neutral winds17. These currents vary seasonally and depend on the magnitude and direction of crustal magnetic fields, as expected for the wind-driven ionospheric dynamo currents (Supplementary Figs. 6, 7, 10, and 14). Meanwhile, the term (panel (I2) of Fig. 3) indicates equivalent ionospheric currents flowing in the solar wind electric field direction on the dayside. This is consistent with the ionospheric currents driven by the solar wind interaction pointed out by previous papers1,2,17,22. The currents show dependencies on upstream conditions (solar wind dynamic pressure and IMF direction) as well as on seasons and crustal magnetic fields (Supplementary Figs. 2, 4, 7, and 9). The term (panel (M2) of Fig. 3) represents equivalent magnetospheric currents flowing in the opposite direction to the solar wind electric field on the dayside and in the solar wind electric field direction on the nightside. This pattern corresponds to the magnetopause and bow shock currents on the dayside and cross-tail currents on the nightside2. The equivalent currents depend on upstream conditions (Supplementary Figs. 3 and 5).

In addition to these previously identified current systems, the sum of , , and terms (panel (M1) of Fig. 3) exhibits equivalent magnetospheric currents flowing counter-clockwise (clockwise) in the northern (southern) hemisphere on the dayside. This pattern is also confirmed in the local current distributions at 800 km altitude derived by the curl-B method. These currents flow oppositely to the wind-driven ionospheric currents (I1) and show dependencies on solar wind dynamic pressure in addition to seasons and crustal magnetic fields (Supplementary Figs. 3, 8, and 10). Given these observed properties, we propose that they represent diamagnetic currents driven by the outward pressure gradient at the induced magnetopause and horizontal magnetic fields generated by the wind-driven ionospheric currents. This newly recognized current system reveals a close coupling between the neutral atmospheric winds and the magnetic field environment in the Martian magnetosphere. This coupling is mediated by the wind-driven ionospheric dynamo currents, which generate magnetic fields comparable to the induced magnetospheric fields driven by the solar wind interaction. As such, we point out that the neutral atmospheric dynamics can play an important role in shaping the magnetic field environment around unmagnetized planets, even in the high-altitude induced magnetosphere where collisions between particles are rare and direct interactions between neutral and ionized atmospheres are generally deemed unimportant.

Finally, we note that Fig. 3 illustrates the global equivalent ionospheric and magnetospheric currents based on the local time-latitude distributions averaged over all longitudes. Such averaged distributions are useful for relating the relatively simple current morphology to the underlying current generation mechanisms. However, instantaneous current systems can be significantly deformed from these simplified pictures owing to the inhomogeneous crustal magnetic fields of Mars. This can be seen in Supplementary Figs. 11 and 12 and Supplementary GIF showing the variations of internal and external equivalent current functions as Mars rotates for the eastward and westward IMF conditions. We infer that the understanding of local structures of the ionospheric current system11 would be a key step toward successfully predicting magnetic field variations at a particular location on the surface of Mars14. Also, realistic 3-dimensional current systems in the near-Mars space must involve vertical currents that are ignored in this analysis. In particular, observations have shown that field-aligned currents in strong crustal fields produce magnetic field deviations of 4–20 nT across 4–10 km horizontal distances4,6, which should be taken into account when investigating local current systems therein. Further investigations addressing the multiscale nature would be required for a full understanding of the closure of magnetospheric and ionospheric currents at Mars and their relevant physical processes. Another important element in this context would be the development of numerical models that include the effects of neutral winds on solar wind interaction with Mars. Overall, our results suggest that the ionospheric and magnetospheric current systems of Mars have complex morphology associated with multiple drivers and vary dynamically in response to the rotation of crustal magnetic fields, ever-changing upstream solar wind conditions, and seasonal and day-to-day variations in thermospheric neutral winds.

We first analyze 1-s averaged magnetic field data in geographic coordinates obtained by the MAVEN magnetometers (MAG)24 from 6 October 2014 to the end of 2024 and derive magnetic field deviations from the intrinsic (crustal) magnetic fields by subtracting model crustal fields25 from the observed fields. We then construct local time-latitude distributions of the magnetic field deviations at 200–300 km altitudes averaged in 1 h bins and determine the internal and external spherical harmonic coefficients (truncated at n = 4) by performing least-squares fitting to the average magnetic field deviation distributions. We investigate dependencies of the equivalent current functions on upstream driver conditions such as the solar wind dynamic pressure and IMF orientation. To maximize available data periods for this upstream driver analysis, we utilize well-established proxies of solar wind and IMF conditions derived from downstream measurements by MAVEN. To infer the upstream solar wind dynamic pressure, we utilize the combined solar wind data (available at https://homepage.physics.uiowa.edu/~jhalekas/drivers.html) derived from Solar Wind Ion Analyzer (SWIA)27 measurements of upstream solar wind ions and penetrating protons in the ionosphere28.

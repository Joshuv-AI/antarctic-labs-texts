1.
Introduction

Counting substructures in graphs is a central topic in combinatorics and graph theory. Among these, the enumeration of subgraphs—particularly induced subgraphs, spanning subgraphs, independent sets and their generalisations—plays a crucial role in understanding graph complexity and structure. Key questions include which
n
-vertex graphs minimise or maximise the number or average size of independent sets, under various structural constraints?

An independent vertex set in a graph
G
is a set of vertices, no two of which are adjacent. The number of independent vertex sets of
G
is also known as the Fibonacci number or Merrifield-
Simmons index of
G
in the mathematical literature. Their enumeration has deep ties to certain models in statistical physics via the hard-core model, where each independent set is assigned a weight based on its size (see
[
9
]
).

Let
i(G)
denote the number of independent vertex sets (including the empty set) in graph
G
. Among
n
-vertex graphs, the minimum is achieved by the complete graph
K_{n}
with
i(K_{n})=n+1
. For graphs with a fixed number of vertices and edges, a classic result of Kahn
[
8
]
shows that the number of independent sets is maximized by a disjoint union of complete bipartite graphs. Specifically, among
d
-regular bipartite graphs
G
on
n
vertices such that
2d|n
, the maximum value of
i(G)
is achieved by the disjoint union of
n/2d
copies of
K_{d,d}
, confirming a conjecture of Alon and Kahn. In general graphs, Zhao
[
11
]
extended Kahn’s entropy method to show that among all graphs with maximum degree
\Delta
, the number of independent vertex sets is asymptotically maximized by a disjoint union of
K_{\Delta,\Delta}
. Among regular graphs, the minimum is less understood, but lower bounds have been given in terms of vertex degrees. There are many other work on extremising
i(G)
when
G
is restricted to various classes of
n
-vertex graphs such as graphs with a given minimum degree
[
6
,
7
]
, or given degree sequence
[
2
]
.

A
1-nearly independent vertex set
allows exactly one pair of adjacent vertices in the collection. This concept was introduced recently by Andriantiana and Shozi in
[
1
]
, where various of its properties are discussed and extremal
n
-vertex graphs and
n
-vertex trees are characterised.

The analogous edge version
[
4
]
counts edge-subsets with one adjacent pair; the star minimises and the path maximises this number among
n
-vertex trees. These new generalisations open further directions for extremal and enumerative analyses.

Let
\mathcal{S}_{0}(G)
denote the set of all independent vertex subsets of graph
G=(V,E)
. Define the average size of independent vertex sets of
G
by

\bar{s}(G):=\frac{1}{i(G)}\sum_{I\in\mathcal{S}_{0}(G)}|I|.

Andriantiana et al.
[
3
]
showed that among all
n
-vertex graphs,
\bar{s}
is maximised by the edgeless graph and minimised by
K_{n}
, while among trees, the path minimises
\bar{s}
and the star maximises it. See also the old short paper
[
10
]
which studies the asymptotic behaviour of
\bar{s}(G)
in terms of order and the chromatic number of
G
, and the recent results
[
5
]
giving a tight lower bound on the expected size of a uniformly choosen independent set in triangle-free graphs of bounded degree.

In this manuscript, we investigate the average size
av_{1}
of
1
-nearly independent subsets in the class of graphs of fixed order
n
, and subsequently in the class of trees of order
n
. Both upper and lower bounds are established, along with characterizations of the graphs that attain these bounds.

The paper is structured as follows.
In Section
2
, we formally define
av_{1}
, compute explicit expressions of
av_{1}
for graphs that have less complicated structures, and establish some technical recursive formulas. These formulas will later be needed in proving our main theorems.
Section
3
is devoted to the study of average among
n
-vertex graphs. In particular, graphs with the smallest and largest
av_{1}
are characterized.
In Section
4
we show that the star is the
n
-vertex tree with the smallest
av_{1}
. We also prove that the maximum value of
av_{1}
is less than
(n+1)/2
for
n>8
. Moreover, we characterise an explicit family of
n
-vertex trees
R_{n}
with
av_{1}(R_{n})=n/2+\delta_{n}
for some
0<\delta_{n}<1/2
, thus matching our bounds.
Finally, we provide further relations between
0
- and
1
-nearly independent vertex sets in graphs in Section
5
.

Preliminary results

Throughout this paper, all graphs are simple. The number of elements of a finite set
S
is denoted by
|S|
. The empty sum is treated as
0
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a simple graph and
l
a non-negative integer. As defined in
[
1
]
, a set
S\subseteq V
is called a
l
-nearly independent vertex set of
G
if the graph induced by
S
in
G
contains exactly
l
edges. Thus
0
-nearly independent vertex sets are precisely the classical independent vertex sets. Denote by
\mathcal{S}_{l}(G)
the set of all
l
-nearly independent vertex sets of
G
, and set

\displaystyle\sigma_{l}(G):=|\mathcal{S}_{l}(G)|\quad\text{and}\quad S_{l}(G):=\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}_{l}(G)}|S|\,,

that is
\sigma_{l}(G)
is the number of
l
-nearly independent vertex sets of
G
, while
S_{l}(G)
is the sum of the sizes of the
l
-nearly independent vertex sets of
G
. We are specifically interested in the average size of a
l
-nearly independent vertex set of
G
, which is given by

av_{l}(G):=\frac{S_{l}(G)}{\sigma_{l}(G)}\,,

provided that
\sigma_{l}(G)\neq 0
. If
\mathcal{S}_{l}(G)=\emptyset
, then it is consistent to write
av_{l}(G)=0
.

We denote by
\sigma_{l}(G,k)
the number of
l
-nearly independent vertex sets of
G
that have cardinality
k
. Clearly,
\sigma_{0}(G,0)=1
, while
\sigma_{l}(G,0)=0
for all
l>0
and
\sigma_{1}(G,k)=0
for all
k<2
. We define the
l
-nearly series
I_{l}(G;x)
by

I_{l}(G;x)=\sum_{k\geq 0}\sigma_{l}(G,k)x^{k}\,,

which has a finite number of terms since
\sigma_{l}(G,k)=0
for all
k>|V(G)|
. We refer to
I_{l}(G;x)
as the
l
-nearly independent vertex polynomial of
G
. It follows that

\displaystyle\sigma_{l}(G)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\geq 0}\sigma_{l}(G,k)=I_{l}(G;1)\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle S_{l}(G)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\geq 0}k\cdot\sigma_{l}(G,k)=\frac{d}{dx}I_{l}(G;x)\Big|_{x=1}:=I_{l}^{\prime}(G;1)\,.

An edge
e
with end vertices
u,v
in a graph
G
will be denoted by
e=uv
. Let
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
be two graphs such that their vertex sets are disjoints. Then
G_{1}\cup G_{2}
means the graph with vertex set
V(G_{1})\cup V(G_{2})
and edge set
E(G_{1})\cup E(G_{2})
. In this case, if
S
is a
1
-nearly independent vertex set of
G_{1}\cup G_{2}
, then the unique edge
e=uv
in the subgraph induced by
S
necessarily comes from
G_{1}
or
G_{2}
, and
S\backslash\{u,v\}
is an independent vertex set of
G_{1}\cup G_{2}
. Conversely,
S\cap V(G_{j})
is either an independent or a
1
-nearly independent vertex set of
G_{j}
for
j\in\{1,2\}
, depending on the location of edge
e=uv
.

We therefore obtain the following identities.

Lemma 1
.

Let
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
be two vertex disjoint graphs. We have

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}(G_{1}\cup G_{2},k)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=2}^{k}\sigma_{1}(G_{1},l)\sigma_{0}(G_{2},k-l)+\sigma_{1}(G_{2},l)\sigma_{0}(G_{1},k-l)\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}(G_{1}\cup G_{2})

\displaystyle=\sigma_{1}(G_{1})\sigma_{0}(G_{2})+\sigma_{1}(G_{2})\sigma_{0}(G_{1})\,.

We also obtain the following.

Lemma 2
.

Let
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
be two vertex disjoint graphs. We have

(1)

\displaystyle I_{1}(G_{1}\cup G_{2};x)

\displaystyle=I_{1}(G_{1};x)I_{0}(G_{2};x)+I_{1}(G_{2};x)I_{0}(G_{1};x)\,\text{ and }

(2)

\displaystyle S_{1}(G_{1}\cup G_{2})

\displaystyle=S_{1}(G_{1})\sigma_{0}(G_{2})+\sigma_{1}(G_{1})S_{0}(G_{2})+S_{1}(G_{2})\sigma_{0}(G_{1})+\sigma_{1}(G_{2})S_{0}(G_{1})\,.

Proof.

Formula (
1
) follows immediately from Lemma
1
. For (
2
),
simply note that

\displaystyle I_{1}^{\prime}(G_{1}\cup G_{2};x)=I_{1}^{\prime}(G_{1};x)I_{0}(G_{2};x)+I_{1}(G_{1};x)I_{0}^{\prime}(G_{2};x)

\displaystyle\hskip 113.81102pt+I_{1}^{\prime}(G_{2};x)I_{0}(G_{1};x)+I_{1}(G_{2};x)I_{0}^{\prime}(G_{1};x)\,.

∎

Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph. For
S\subseteq V
, we denote by
G-S
the graph induced by
V\backslash S
in
G
. For simplicity, we write
G-v
instead of
G-\{v\}
. We use the standard notation that
N(v)
is the set of all neighbours of
v\in V
in
G
and
N[v]=N(v)\cup\{v\}
.

We can count the contribution of
v
to
\sigma_{1}(G,k)
as follows. If
v
is an element of
S\in\mathcal{S}_{1}(G)
, then
S
can only contain at most one neighbour of
v
in
G
. If
S
contains both
v
and
u\in N(v)
, then no other element of
N(v)\cup N(u)
can belong to
S
and
S\backslash\{u,v\}
is an independent vertex set of
G
. Thus the contribution of
v
to
\sigma_{1}(G,k)
is given by

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}(G-N[v],k-1)+\sum_{u\in N(v)}\sigma_{0}(G-(N[v]\cup N[u]),k-2)\,

and thus

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}(G,k)=\sigma_{1}(G-v,k)+\sigma_{1}(G-N[v],k-1)+\sum_{u\in N(v)}\sigma_{0}(G-(N[v]\cup N[u]),k-2)\,.

Note that
G-(N[v]\cup N[u])
and
G-(N(v)\cup N(u))
are isomorphic graphs since
uv\in E
.

The next lemma follows immediately, and its proof is omitted. Note that the recursive formula for
S_{1}
is obtained by differentiating the expression for
I_{1}
and evaluating it at
x=1
.

Lemma 3
.

Let
G
be a graph and
v
a vertex of
G
. We have

\displaystyle I_{1}(G;x)

\displaystyle=I_{1}(G-v;x)+xI_{1}(G-N[v];x)+x^{2}\sum_{u\in N(v)}I_{0}\big(G-(N(v)\cup N(u));x\big)\,,

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}(G)

\displaystyle=\sigma_{1}(G-v)+\sigma_{1}(G-N[v])+\sum_{u\in N(v)}\sigma_{0}\big(G-(N(v)\cup N(u))\big)\,,\quad\text{ and }

\displaystyle S_{1}(G)

\displaystyle=S_{1}(G-v)+\sigma_{1}(G-N[v])+S_{1}(G-N[v])

\displaystyle~+\sum_{u\in N(v)}\Big(2\sigma_{0}\big(G-(N(v)\cup N(u))\big)+S_{0}\big(G-(N(v)\cup N(u))\big)\Big)\,.

In what follows, we compute
\sigma_{1}(G),S_{1}(G)
for a few classes of graphs. We use the following usual notation for
n
-vertex graphs:

•

E_{n}
: graph with no edges (edgeless graph),

•

S_{n}
: star (a central vertex adjacent to
n-1
pendent edges),

•

K_{n}
: complete graph (every two vertices are adjacent),

•

P_{n}
: path (a tree with maximum degree less than or equal to

).

Proposition 4
.

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}(E_{n})=S_{1}(E_{n})=0\,,

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}(S_{n})=n-1,~S_{1}(S_{n})=2(n-1)\,,\quad\text{ and }

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}(K_{n})=n(n-1)/2,~S_{1}(K_{n})=n(n-1).

Proof.

All these expressions are easily obtained by direct counting arguments. Nevertheless, let us use the above decomposition formulas.
E_{n}
has no edge, so
\mathcal{S}_{1}(E_{n})=\emptyset
. Let
v
be the central vertex of
S_{n}
, we have
S_{n}-v=E_{n-1}
,
S_{n}-N(v)=\emptyset
, and

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}(S_{n})

\displaystyle=\sigma_{1}(E_{n-1})+\sigma_{1}(\emptyset)+\sum_{u\in N(v)}\sigma_{0}(\emptyset)=|N(v)|=n-1\,,

\displaystyle S_{1}(S_{n})

\displaystyle=S_{1}(E_{n-1})+\sigma_{1}(\emptyset)+S_{1}(\emptyset)+\sum_{u\in N(v)}(2\sigma_{0}(\emptyset)+S_{0}(\emptyset))=2|N(v)|=2(n-1)\,.

For the complete graph,

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}(K_{n})

\displaystyle=\sigma_{1}(K_{n-1})+\sigma_{1}(\emptyset)+\sum_{u\in N(v)}\sigma_{0}(\emptyset)=\sigma_{1}(K_{n-1})+|N(v)|=\sigma_{1}(K_{n-1})+n-1\,,

\displaystyle S_{1}(K_{n})

\displaystyle=S_{1}(K_{n-1})+\sigma_{1}(\emptyset)+S_{1}(\emptyset)+\sum_{u\in N(v)}\big(2\sigma_{0}(\emptyset)+S_{0}(\emptyset)\big)

\displaystyle=S_{1}(K_{n-1})+2|N(v)|=S_{1}(K_{n-1})+2(n-1)\,.

Solving these recursions yield
\sigma_{1}(K_{n})=n(n-1)/2
and
S_{1}(K_{n})=n(n-1)
.
∎

Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph. Since every element of
\mathcal{S}_{1}(G)
induces a graph that contains only one edge, the set
\mathcal{S}_{1}(G)
can be partitioned into

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{1}(G)=\bigcup_{e\in E}~\bigcup_{k\geq 2}~\mathcal{S}^{e}_{1,k}(G)\,,

where
\mathcal{S}^{e}_{1,k}(G)
comprises all those
k
-element

-nearly independent vertex sets involving the end vertices of edge
e
. One can therefore determine
|\mathcal{S}^{e}_{1,k}(G)|
, the contribution of edge
e=uv
to
\sigma_{1}(G,k)
. Following the same argument given above, we get

\displaystyle|\mathcal{S}^{e}_{1,k}(G)|

\displaystyle=\sigma_{0}\big(G-(N(v)\cup N(u)),k-2\big)\,,

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}(G,k)

\displaystyle=\sum_{uv\in E}\sigma_{0}\big(G-(N(v)\cup N(u)),k-2\big)\,.

As an immediate consequence, we obtain the following formulas:

Lemma 5
.

For every graph
G
, it holds that

\displaystyle I_{1}(G;x)

\displaystyle=x^{2}\sum_{uv\in E}I_{0}(G-(N(v)\cup N(u));x)\,,

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}(G)

\displaystyle=\sum_{uv\in E}\sigma_{0}\big(G-(N(v)\cup N(u))\big)\,,

\displaystyle S_{1}(G)

\displaystyle=\sum_{uv\in E}\Big(2\sigma_{0}\big(G-(N(v)\cup N(u))\big)+S_{0}\big(G-(N(v)\cup N(u))\big)\Big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{uv\in E}\sigma_{0}\big(G-(N(v)\cup N(u))\big)\Big(2+av_{0}\big(G-(N(v)\cup N(u))\big)\Big)\,.

For example, setting
E(P_{n}):=\{v_{0}v_{1},v_{1}v_{2},\ldots,v_{n-2}v_{n-1}\}
and using this lemma we can compute
\sigma_{1}(P_{n})
and
S_{1}(P_{n})
as

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}(P_{n})=\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\sigma_{0}(P_{j-2}\cup P_{n-2-j})=\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\sigma_{0}(P_{j-2})\sigma_{0}(P_{n-2-j})\,,

where the second equality comes from Lemma

Lemma 6

(
[
3
]
)
.

Let
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
be two vertex disjoint graphs. We have

\displaystyle\sigma_{0}(G_{1}\cup G_{2})

\displaystyle=\sigma_{0}(G_{1})\sigma_{0}(G_{2})\,,

\displaystyle av_{0}(G_{1}\cup G_{2})

\displaystyle=av_{0}(G_{1})+av_{0}(G_{2})\,.

The next lemma provides an alternative formula for
av_{1}(G)
that will be useful in some cases.

Lemma 7
.

If
G
is not an edgless graph, then

\displaystyle av_{1}(G)=\frac{S_{1}(G)}{\sigma_{1}(G)}=2+\frac{\displaystyle\sum_{uv\in E}S_{0}\big(G-(N(v)\cup N(u))\big)}{\displaystyle\sum_{uv\in E}\sigma_{0}\big(G-(N(v)\cup N(u))\big)}\,.

Furthermore, there exists edges
u_{1}v_{1},u_{2}v_{2}
of

such that

\displaystyle 2+av_{0}\big(G-(N(v_{1})\cup N(u_{1}))\big)\leq av_{1}(G)\leq 2+av_{0}\big(G-(N(v_{1})\cup N(u_{1}))\big)\,.

Proof.

According to Lemma
5
,

\displaystyle av_{1}(G)=2+\frac{\displaystyle\sum_{uv\in E}S_{0}\big(G-(N(v)\cup N(u))\big)}{\displaystyle\sum_{uv\in E}\sigma_{0}\big(G-(N(v)\cup N(u))\big)}\,.

Now set
H_{uv}:=G-(N(v)\cup N(u))
and note that

\displaystyle av_{1}(G)=2+\frac{\displaystyle\sum_{uvE}S_{0}(H_{uv})}{\displaystyle\sum_{uv\in E}\sigma_{0}(H_{uv})}=2+\sum_{uv\in E}\alpha_{uv}\frac{S_{0}(H_{uv})}{\sigma_{0}(H_{uv})}=2+\sum_{uv\in E}\alpha_{uv}av_{0}(H_{uv})\,,

where
\alpha_{uv}=\sigma_{0}(H_{uv})/\sum_{uv\in E}\sigma_{0}(H_{uv})
. Since
\alpha_{uv}>0
and
\sum_{uv\in E}\alpha_{uv}=1
, we can interpret

as an average. Hence, there is a case of

that corresponds to a term not less than the average, and there is a case that corresponds to a term not larger than the average. The lemma follows.

Extremal

In this section, we characterise the structure of graphs that minimise or maximise

. Trivially,

with equality if and only if
G
is an edgeless graph. Here and in the following, we assume

for all

.

In
[
3
]
it was proved that the star maximises
av_{0}
among all
n
-vertex trees, while
[
1
]
shows that the star minimises
\sigma_{1}
among all
n

among connected graphs.

In paper
[
1
]
, a
good graph

G=(V,E)
is defined as a graph in which every edge
uv
satisfies
N(v)\cup N(u)=V
. A full characterisation of the set
\mathcal{H}
of all good graphs is provided there. For any

, we have

. Hence we have the following first main theorem of this paper.

Theorem 8
.

For every
n
-vertex graph
G
that is not edgeless, we have

av_{1}(G)\geq 2\,.

Equality holds if and only if
G\in\mathcal{H}\backslash\{K_{1}\}
.

In particular,
S_{n}
uniquely minimises
av_{1}
among
n
-vertex trees, and it is one of the
n
-vertex graphs that minimises
av_{1}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
7

av_{1}(G)\geq 2
for all
G=(V,E)
that are not edgeless, and equality holds if and only if
S_{0}\big(G-(N(v)\cup N(u))\big)=0

is the empty graph, or equivalently

The only edgeless graph from

. ∎

We use Lemma

to obtain some bounds on

. For a graph

\displaystyle\delta_{1}(G)=\min_{uv\in E}|N(v)\cup N(u)|\quad\text{and}\quad\delta_{2}(G)=\max_{uv\in E}|N(v)\cup N(u)|\,.

It is clear that

For every

\displaystyle 2\leq\frac{3n+2-3\delta_{2}(G)}{n+1-\delta_{2}(G)}\leq av_{1}(G)\leq\frac{n+4-\delta_{1}(G)}{2}\leq\frac{n+2}{2}\,.

Using

and

\displaystyle\frac{n}{n+1}=av_{0}(K_{n})\leq av_{0}(H)\leq av_{0}(E_{n})=\frac{n}{2}

\displaystyle 2+\frac{n-|N(v_{1})\cup N(u_{1})|}{n+1-|N(v_{1})\cup N(u_{1})|}\leq av_{1}(G)\leq 2+\frac{n-|N(v_{2})\cup N(u_{2})|}{2}\,.

As a particular case of Theorem

We call an internal vertex in a tree

Proposition 12

For any
n

T
with minimum degree of internal vertices equal to

Proof.

As already seen in the proof of Lemma

av_{1}(T)=2+\sum_{uv\in E(T)}\alpha_{uv}\cdot av_{0}\big(T-(N(u)\cup N(v))\big)

for some positive numbers

that sum to

. If

n

av_{0}\big(T-(N(u)\cup N(v))\big)\leq\frac{\max\{n-\delta^{\prime}-1,0\}}{2}

.
∎

We can then deduce the following upper bound for

Corollary 13
.

For any
n

We will prove a matching lower bound on the maximum average size of

-nearly independent vertex sets in trees.

For

Let

v

\displaystyle av_{1}(R_{n})

\displaystyle=\frac{S_{1}(S_{n-1})+S_{1}(S_{n-2})+\sigma_{1}(S_{n-2})+S_{0}(E_{n-3})+2\sigma_{0}(E_{n-3})}{\sigma_{1}(S_{n-1})+\sigma_{1}(S_{n-2})+\sigma_{0}(E_{n-3})}

5.
Further bounds

In this short section, we establish further identities and bounds. In particular, we show that it is possible to have
\sigma_{0}\leq 3\sigma_{1}
. We begin with a lemma.

Lemma 15
.

For every graph
G=(V,E)
that is not edgeless and every
uv\in E
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{l_{uv}-2}+2^{l_{uv}-m_{u}}+2^{l_{uv}-m_{v}}+1}\leq\frac{\sigma_{0}(G-(N[v]\cup N[u]))}{\sigma_{0}(G)}\leq 1-\frac{\sigma_{0}(G-v)}{\sigma_{0}(G)}\,,

where

\displaystyle l_{uv}=|N(v)\cup N(u)|\,,\quad m_{u}=|N[u]|\,,\quad m_{v}=|N[v]|\,.

In particular,

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma_{0}(G-(N[v]\cup N[u])}{\sigma_{0}(G)}\geq\frac{1}{(3\cdot 2^{l_{uv}-2}+1)}\,.

Proof.

We return to the recursion

\displaystyle\sigma_{0}(G)=\sigma_{0}(G-v)+\sigma_{0}(G-N[v])\,.

Since
G-(N[v]\cup N[u])
is an induced subgraph of
G-N[v]
, we have

\displaystyle\sigma_{0}(G-N[v])

\displaystyle\geq\sigma_{0}(G-(N[v]\cup N[u]))\,,

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\sigma_{0}(G-v)}{\sigma_{0}(G)}+\frac{\sigma_{0}(G-(N[v]\cup N[u]))}{\sigma_{0}(G)}\,,

which proves the second inequality. On the other hand, we iterate the inequality

\displaystyle\sigma_{0}(G)=\sigma_{0}(G-w)+\sigma_{0}(G-N[w])\leq 2\sigma_{0}(G-w)\,,

which holds for all
w\in V
, to obtain

\displaystyle\sigma_{0}((G-v)-u)\leq

\displaystyle 2^{|(N[v]\cup N[u])\backslash\{u,v\}|}\sigma_{0}\big(G-(N[v]\cup N[u])\big)

\displaystyle=2^{l_{uv}-2}\sigma_{0}\big(G-(N[v]\cup N[u])\big)\,,

\displaystyle\sigma_{0}((G-v)-N[u])\leq

\displaystyle 2^{|(N[v]\cup N[u])\backslash(\{v\}\cup N[u])|}\sigma_{0}\big(G-(N[v]\cup N[u])\big)

\displaystyle=2^{l_{uv}-m_{u}}\sigma_{0}\big(G-(N[v]\cup N[u])\big)

and

\displaystyle\sigma_{0}((G-N[v])-u)\leq

\displaystyle 2^{|(N[v]\cup N[u])\backslash(\{u\}\cup N[v])|}\sigma_{0}\big(G-(N[v]\cup N[u])\big)

\displaystyle=2^{l_{uv}-m_{v}}\sigma_{0}\big(G-(N[v]\cup N[u])\big)\,.

It follows that

\displaystyle\sigma_{0}(G)

\displaystyle=\sigma_{0}(G-v)+\sigma_{0}(G-N[v])

\displaystyle=\sigma_{0}((G-v)-u)+\sigma_{0}((G-v)-N[u])

\displaystyle~~+\sigma_{0}((G-N[v])-u)+\sigma_{0}((G-N[v])-N[u])

\displaystyle\leq(2^{l_{uv}-2}+2^{l_{uv}-m_{u}}+2^{l_{uv}-m_{v}}+1)\sigma_{0}(G-(N[v]\cup N[u])\,,

which proves the first inequality in the lemma. In particular,

\displaystyle\sigma_{0}(G)\leq

\displaystyle(2^{l_{uv}-2}+2^{l_{uv}-2}+2^{l_{uv}-2}+1)\sigma_{0}(G-(N[v]\cup N[u])

\displaystyle=(3\cdot 2^{l_{uv}-2}+1)\sigma_{0}(G-(N[v]\cup N[u])\,,

proving the last inequality.

∎

In the next proposition, we show that
\sigma_{1}/\sigma_{0}
is a somewhat additive function in the union of graphs, and that
\sigma_{0}\leq 3\sigma_{1}
can possibly hold in certain graph classes.

Proposition 16
.

Let
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
be two vertex disjoint graphs. We have

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma_{1}(G_{1}\cup G_{2})}{\sigma_{0}(G_{1}\cup G_{2})}=\frac{\sigma_{1}(G_{1})}{\sigma_{0}(G_{1})}+\frac{\sigma_{1}(G_{2})}{\sigma_{0}(G_{2})}\,.

Moreover, for every graph
G=(V,E)
with no isolated vertex and maximum degree
2
,

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma_{1}(G)}{\sigma_{0}(G)}\geq\frac{1}{3}\,.

Equality holds for
P_{2}
.

Proof.

The first identity immediately follows from Lemma
2
:

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma_{1}(G_{1}\cup G_{2})}{\sigma_{0}(G_{1}\cup G_{2})}=\frac{\sigma_{1}(G_{1})\sigma_{0}(G_{2})+\sigma_{1}(G_{2})\sigma_{0}(G_{1})}{\sigma_{0}(G_{1})\sigma_{0}(G_{2})}\,.

Now let
G=(V,E)
be a graph with no isolated vertex and maximum degre
2
. All the connected components of
G
are paths and/or cycles. If
P_{2}
is such a component, then set
G_{1}:=P_{2}
and
G_{2}:=G\backslash V(G_{1})
to obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma_{1}(G)}{\sigma_{0}(G)}=\frac{\sigma_{1}(P_{2})}{\sigma_{0}(P_{2})}+\frac{\sigma_{1}(G_{2})}{\sigma_{0}(G_{2})}\geq\frac{\sigma_{1}(P_{2})}{\sigma_{0}(P_{2})}=\frac{1}{3}\,.

Otherwise,

\displaystyle l_{uv}:=|N(v)\cup N(u)|\in\{3,4\}

for all
uv\in E
. Here we distinguish two cases:

•

Case 1:
|E|\geq 5
.

We can partition
E
as
E=E_{4}\cup E_{3}
, where

\displaystyle E_{4}=\{uv\in E:~l_{uv}=4\}\quad\text{and}\quad E_{3}=\{uv\in E:~l_{uv}=3\}\,.

We combine (see Lemma
15
)

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma_{0}(G-(N[v]\cup N[u])}{\sigma_{0}(G)}\geq\frac{1}{(3\cdot 2^{l_{uv}-2}+1)}\,.

and (see Lemma
5
)

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma_{1}(G)}{\sigma_{0}(G)}=\sum_{uv\in E}\frac{\sigma_{0}\big(G-(N(v)\cup N(u))\big)}{\sigma_{0}(G)}

to obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma_{1}(G)}{\sigma_{0}(G)}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{uv\in E_{4}}\frac{1}{(3\cdot 2^{4-2}+1)}~+~\sum_{uv\in E_{3}}\frac{1}{(3\cdot 2^{3-2}+1)}=\frac{|E_{4}|}{13}+\frac{|E_{3}|}{7}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{5-|E_{3}|}{13}+\frac{|E_{3}|}{7}=\frac{35+6|E_{3}|}{91}>\frac{1}{3}\,.

•

Case 2:
1\leq|E|\leq 4
.

Here all the connected components of
G
must belong to the set

\displaystyle Q:=\{P_{5},C_{4},P_{4},C_{3},P_{3}\}\,,

while easy calculations yield

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma_{1}(P_{5})}{\sigma_{0}(P_{5})}=\frac{10}{13}\,,\quad\frac{\sigma_{1}(C_{4})}{\sigma_{0}(C_{4})}=\frac{4}{7}\,,\quad\frac{\sigma_{1}(P_{4})}{\sigma_{0}(P_{4})}=\frac{5}{8}\,,

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma_{1}(C_{3})}{\sigma_{0}(C_{3})}=\frac{3}{4}\,,\quad\frac{\sigma_{1}(P_{3})}{\sigma_{0}(P_{3})}=\frac{2}{5}\,.

All these values are greater than
1/3
. Now
G=G_{1}\cup G_{2}
for some
G_{1}\in Q
. We have

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma_{1}(G_{1}\cup G_{2})}{\sigma_{0}(G_{1}\cup G_{2})}=\frac{\sigma_{1}(G_{1})}{\sigma_{0}(G_{1})}+\frac{\sigma_{1}(G_{2})}{\sigma_{0}(G_{2})}>\frac{1}{3}\,,

which concludes the proof.

∎

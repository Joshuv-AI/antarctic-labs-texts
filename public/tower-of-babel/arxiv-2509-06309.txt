Moment Dilations and Functional Calculus for Random Operators
James Tian

Abstract
. We develop a dilation theory for tuples of random operators on a Hilbert space. Their joint distribution defines a moment kernel by expectation of operator products. We prove that this kernel admits a dilation to a Cuntz family of isometries precisely when the associated shifted kernel is dominated in the positive-definite order. As consequences, we establish a mean-square version of von Neumann’s inequality and construct a free functional calculus for random operators, extending from polynomials to the free disk algebra and the free Hardy algebra. These results extend classical dilation theory into a probabilistic setting and provide new tools for analyzing noncommutative random systems.

1.
Introduction

Dilation theory is one of the central tools in operator theory. The
classical Sz.-Nagy theorem
[
SNFBK10
]
shows that every contraction
on a Hilbert space can be dilated to a unitary operator, and many
important consequences follow from this. Later, Frazho, Bunce, and
Popescu developed multivariable versions, proving that tuples of operators
satisfying certain contractive conditions can be dilated to the universal
Cuntz family of isometries with orthogonal ranges
[
Fra82
,
Bun84
,
Pop89
]
.
These results give a way to study complicated operators by embedding
them into larger spaces where the structure is simpler and more rigid.

Another perspective on dilation comes from the theory of positive
definite kernels
[
Aro50
,
Sti55
,
Arv10
,
SNFBK10
,
Ped58
,
Sza21
]
.
Given a family of operators, one can associate kernels that record
their algebraic relations. Positivity of these kernels is closely
tied to the existence of dilations, and this connection goes back
to early work of Aronszajn and Pedrick on reproducing kernel Hilbert
spaces.

In this paper we consider the case where the operators themselves
are random. Suppose
A=\left(A_{1},\dots,A_{d}\right)
is a
d
-tuple
of random operators on a Hilbert space
H
. For each pair of words

\alpha,\beta
in the free semigroup
\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}
, we form
the moment kernel
K\left(\alpha,\beta\right)=\mathbb{E}[A^{\alpha}\left(A^{\beta}\right)^{*}]
.
This kernel captures the averaged behavior of the random tuple. It
is always positive definite, but the natural question is: when does

K
come from compressing a universal Cuntz family, as in the deterministic
dilation theorems?

Our first main result (Theorem
1
) answers this question.
We show that
K
admits such a dilation if and only if it dominates
a shifted version
K_{\Sigma}
in the sense of positive definite
kernels. Equivalently, the difference
K-K_{\Sigma}
must itself
be positive definite. This gives a precise condition for when the
random tuple has a dilation to a Cuntz family acting on a larger Hilbert
space.

Once the dilation is established, further consequences follow. We
obtain a mean-square version of von Neumann’s inequality, which controls
random polynomials in
A
by the corresponding universal model. We
then build a mean-square free functional calculus. Starting from noncommutative
polynomials, the construction extends first to the free disk algebra
and then, using radial limits, to the free Hardy algebra
[
Pop91
,
DP98
,
Pop10
]
.
The result is a functional calculus for random operator tuples that
behaves in close analogy with the deterministic theory.

The point of this work is that dilation methods, originally developed
for single operators and later generalized to noncommuting tuples,
also extend naturally to random operators once one considers their
moment kernels. The theorems here provide the basic tools: a dilation
theorem, an inequality of von Neumann type, and a functional calculus.

There is a formal resemblance to operator-valued free probability

[
Voi85
,
Voi91
,
Spe98
,
NS06
]
, where distributions
are described by operator-valued moment functionals. Our construction
differs in that we work with kernels defined by classical expectation
over random operators, rather than with conditional expectations on
noncommutative probability spaces. The analogy highlights the common
role of positivity of moment data in both settings, even though the
underlying notions of probability are distinct.

In Section
2
, we introduce the moment kernel
K
for a
d
-tuple
of random operators and prove our main dilation result (Theorem
1
),
which establishes the equivalence between the kernel inequality
K_{\Sigma}\leq K

and the existence of a dilation involving a Cuntz family. We then
derive a mean-square von Neumann inequality (Corollary
4
) for
noncommutative polynomials as a direct consequence. Section
3

uses this inequality to construct the mean-square functional calculus.
We first define the map for the free disk algebra
\mathcal{A}_{d}

(Theorem
8
) and then, using radial limits, extend it to the
free Hardy algebra
F^{\infty}_{d}
(Theorem
10
).

2.
Dilation of Free Moment Kernels

The starting point of our analysis is the moment kernel associated
with a tuple of random operators. In classical dilation theory, one
studies a fixed operator tuple and encodes its algebraic relations
through powers and products. In the random setting, the natural object
is obtained by averaging such products, leading to a positive definite
kernel on the free semigroup. This kernel captures the mean-square
behavior of the random tuple and provides the right entry point for
dilation. The goal of this section is to characterize precisely when
such kernels admit dilations to Cuntz families of isometries, and
to establish the equivalence between this dilation property and a
positivity condition on a shifted version of the kernel.

Fix
d\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
\mathbb{F}{}^{+}_{d}
be the free semigroup
on generators
\left\{1,\dots,d\right\}
, with neutral element

\emptyset
. For a word
\alpha=i_{1}\dots i_{k}\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}
,
write
\tilde{\alpha}=i_{k}\cdots i_{1}
for the reversed word. Given
any
d
-tuple of operators
S=\left(S_{1},\dots,S_{d}\right)
on
a Hilbert space
H
, define

S^{\alpha}\coloneqq S_{i_{1}}\cdots S_{i_{k}},\quad S^{\emptyset}\coloneqq I_{H}.

Then for every
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}
,

\left(S^{\alpha}\right)^{*}=\left(S^{*}\right)^{\tilde{\alpha}},

where
S^{*}=\left(S^{*}_{1},\dots,S^{*}_{d}\right)
.

Let
\left(\Omega,\mathbb{P}\right)
be a probability space and
A=\left(A_{1},\dots,A_{d}\right)

a
d
-tuple of (strongly measurable) random operators
A_{i}\colon\Omega\rightarrow\mathcal{L}\left(H\right)

on a Hilbert space
H
, and assume

\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|A^{\alpha}\right\|^{2}\right]<\infty,\quad\alpha\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}.

Define the moment kernel
K\colon\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}\times\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}\rightarrow\mathcal{L}\left(H\right)

by

(2.1)

K\left(\alpha,\beta\right)\coloneqq\mathbb{E}\left[A^{\alpha}\left(A^{\beta}\right)^{*}\right],\quad\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}.

Then
K
is positive definite (p.d.): for every finite family
\left\{\left(\alpha_{j},u_{j}\right)\right\}^{N}_{j=1}\subset\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}\times H
,

\displaystyle\sum\nolimits^{N}_{i,j=1}\left\langle u_{i},K\left(\alpha_{i},\alpha_{j}\right)u_{j}\right\rangle_{H}

\displaystyle=\sum\nolimits^{N}_{i,j=1}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle u_{i},A^{\alpha_{i}}\left(A^{\alpha_{j}}\right)^{*}u_{j}\right\rangle_{H}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\sum\nolimits_{i}\left(A^{\alpha_{i}}\right)^{*}u_{i}\right\|^{2}_{H}\right]\geq 0.

Define the kernel

K_{\Sigma}\left(\alpha,\beta\right)\coloneqq\sum^{d}_{i=1}K\left(\alpha i,\beta i\right),\quad\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}.

Theorem 1

(Free moment dilation)
.

For the moment kernel
K
in (
2.1
), the
following are equivalent:

(1)

There exists a Hilbert space
\mathcal{K}
, a Cuntz
family
S=\left(S_{1},\dots,S_{d}\right)
on
\mathcal{K}
(i.e.,

S^{*}_{i}S_{j}=\delta_{i,j}I_{\mathcal{K}}
and
\sum^{d}_{i=1}S_{i}S^{*}_{i}=I_{\mathcal{K}}
),
a projection
P\in\mathcal{L}\left(\mathcal{K}\right)
, and an isometry

W\colon H\rightarrow\mathcal{K}
such that

(2.2)

K\left(\alpha,\beta\right)=W^{*}S^{\alpha}P\left(S^{\beta}\right)^{*}W,\quad\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d};

and,

(2.3)

\sum\nolimits^{d}_{i=1}S_{i}PS^{*}_{i}\leq P\;\text{on }M\coloneqq\overline{span}\left\{\left(S^{\gamma}\right)^{*}WH:\gamma\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}\right\}

(2)

The kernel
K_{\Sigma}
is dominated by
K
in the
p.d. order:

(2.4)

K_{\Sigma}\leq K.

(That is,
K-K_{\Sigma}
is p.d.)

Moreover,
K_{\Sigma}=K
if and only if

(2.5)

K\left(\alpha,\beta\right)=W^{*}S^{\alpha}\left(S^{\beta}\right)^{*}W,\quad\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}.

Proof.

(
1
)
\Rightarrow
(
2
). Fix a finite family

\left\{\left(\alpha_{j},u_{j}\right)\right\}^{N}_{j=1}\subset\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}\times H

and set

y\coloneqq\sum\nolimits^{N}_{j=1}\left(S^{\alpha_{j}}\right)^{*}Wu_{j}\in M.

Then (using
S^{\alpha i}=S^{\alpha}S_{i}
and (
2.2
))

\displaystyle\sum\nolimits^{N}_{j,k=1}\left\langle u_{j},K_{\Sigma}\left(\alpha_{j},\alpha_{k}\right)u_{k}\right\rangle_{H}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\nolimits^{d}_{i=1}\sum\nolimits^{N}_{j,k=1}\left\langle u_{j},K\left(\alpha_{j}i,\alpha_{k}i\right)u_{k}\right\rangle_{H}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\nolimits^{d}_{i=1}\sum\nolimits^{N}_{j,k=1}\left\langle\left(S^{\alpha_{j}}\right)^{*}Wu_{j},\left(S_{i}PS^{*}_{i}\right)\left(S^{\alpha_{k}}\right)^{*}Wu_{k}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{K}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle y,\left(\sum\nolimits^{d}_{i=1}S_{i}PS^{*}_{i}\right)y\right\rangle_{\mathcal{K}}.

Similarly, using (
2.2
),

\sum\nolimits^{N}_{j,k=1}\left\langle u_{j},K\left(\alpha_{j},\alpha_{k}\right)u_{k}\right\rangle_{H}=\sum\nolimits^{N}_{j,k=1}\left\langle\left(S^{\alpha_{j}}\right)^{*}Wu_{j},P\left(S^{\alpha_{k}}\right)^{*}Wu_{k}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{K}}=\left\langle y,Py\right\rangle_{\mathcal{K}}.

By assumption (
2.3
), we have

\left\langle y,\left(\sum\nolimits^{d}_{i=1}S_{i}PS^{*}_{i}\right)y\right\rangle_{\mathcal{K}}\leq\left\langle y,Py\right\rangle_{\mathcal{K}},

which is exactly (
2.4
).

(
2
)
\Rightarrow
(
1
). Since
K
is p.d.,
it admits a Kolmogorov/GNS factorization: there exists a Hilbert space

H^{\prime}
and operators
V_{\alpha}\colon H\rightarrow H^{\prime}
such that

K\left(\alpha,\beta\right)=V^{*}_{\alpha}V_{\beta},\quad\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d},

and
\mathscr{D}\coloneqq span\left\{V_{\alpha}u:\alpha\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d},u\in H\right\}

is dense in
H^{\prime}
.

Concretely, one may take
H^{\prime}
to be the reproducing kernel Hilbert
space (RKHS) of the scalar valued kernel

\tilde{K}\left(\left(\alpha,u\right),\left(\beta,v\right)\right)\coloneqq\left\langle u,K\left(\alpha,\beta\right)v\right\rangle_{H},

defined on
\left(\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}\times H\right)\times\left(\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}\times H\right)
,
and set

V_{\alpha}u=\tilde{K}\left(\cdot,\left(\alpha,u\right)\right)\in H^{\prime}.

For details, see e.g.,
[
Ped58
,
Sza21
,
Tia25
]
.

On the algebraic span
\mathscr{D}
, define

B_{i}\left(V_{\alpha}u\right)\coloneqq V_{\alpha i}u,\quad i=1,\dots,d,

and extend linearly.

Take an arbitrary element
x=\sum_{\alpha}V_{\alpha}u_{\alpha}\in\mathscr{D}
.
Then

\left\|x\right\|^{2}_{H^{\prime}}=\sum\nolimits_{\alpha,\beta}\left\langle u_{\alpha},K\left(\alpha,\beta\right)u_{\beta}\right\rangle_{H},\quad\left\|B_{i}x\right\|^{2}_{H^{\prime}}=\sum\nolimits_{\alpha,\beta}\left\langle u_{\alpha},K\left(\alpha i,\beta i\right)u_{\beta}\right\rangle_{H}.

Summing over
i
,

\displaystyle\sum\nolimits^{d}_{i=1}\left\|B_{i}x\right\|^{2}_{H^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\sum\nolimits_{\alpha,\beta}\left\langle u_{\alpha},\left(\sum\nolimits^{d}_{i=1}K\left(\alpha i,\beta i\right)\right)u_{\beta}\right\rangle_{H}

\displaystyle=\sum\nolimits_{\alpha,\beta}\left\langle u_{\alpha},K_{\Sigma}\left(\alpha,\beta\right)u_{\beta}\right\rangle_{H}

\displaystyle\leq\sum\nolimits_{\alpha,\beta}\left\langle u_{\alpha},K\left(\alpha,\beta\right)u_{\beta}\right\rangle_{H}=\left\|x\right\|^{2}_{H^{\prime}},

by the hypothesis
K_{\Sigma}\leq K
. Hence the linear map

B_{col}\colon\mathscr{D}\rightarrow H^{\prime\oplus d},\quad B_{col}x\coloneqq\left(B_{1}x,\dots,B_{d}x\right)

is contractive on
\mathscr{D}
; it extends by continuity to a contraction

B_{col}\colon H^{\prime}\rightarrow H^{\prime\oplus d}
, and each
B_{i}
has
a bounded extension to
H^{\prime}
with

\sum B^{*}_{i}B_{i}\leq I_{H^{\prime}}\quad\left(\text{as operators on }H^{\prime}\right).

(So
B_{col}
is a column contraction; equivalently the adjoint
\left(B^{*}_{1},\dots,B^{*}_{d}\right)

is a row contraction.)

By the multivariable dilation theory of Frazho, Bunce, and Popescu
(
[
Fra82
,
Bun84
,
Pop89
]
), there exists a Hilbert space

\mathcal{K}
, a Cuntz family
S=\left(S_{1},\dots,S_{d}\right)

on
\mathcal{K}
with orthogonal ranges, and an isometry
J\colon H^{\prime}\rightarrow\mathcal{K}

such that

B_{i}=J^{*}S^{*}_{i}J,\quad i=1,\dots,d.

In particular, for every word
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}
,

B^{\alpha}=J^{*}\left(S^{\tilde{\alpha}}\right)^{*}J.

(Recall
\tilde{\alpha}
is the reversed word for
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}
.)

Note that
V_{\alpha}=B^{\tilde{\alpha}}V_{\emptyset}
(by construction).
Therefore,

\displaystyle K\left(\alpha,\beta\right)

\displaystyle=V^{*}_{\alpha}V_{\beta}=V^{*}_{\emptyset}\left(B^{\tilde{\alpha}}\right)^{*}B^{\tilde{\beta}}V_{\emptyset}

\displaystyle=V^{*}_{\emptyset}\left(J^{*}\left(S^{\alpha}\right)^{*}J\right)^{*}\cdot J^{*}\left(S^{\beta}\right)^{*}JV_{\emptyset}

\displaystyle=\left(JV_{\emptyset}\right)^{*}S^{\alpha}\left(JJ^{*}\right)\left(S^{\beta}\right)^{*}\left(JV_{\emptyset}\right)

\displaystyle=W^{*}S^{\alpha}P\left(S^{\beta}\right)^{*}W

where
W\coloneqq JV_{\emptyset}:H\rightarrow\mathcal{K}
(isometric
because
J
and
V_{\emptyset}
are), and
P\coloneqq JJ^{*}
(the
orthogonal projection onto
JH^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{K}
).

To verify (
2.3
), consider any
y=\sum_{j}\left(S^{\alpha_{j}}\right)^{*}Wu_{j}\in M
,

\left\langle y,S_{i}PS^{*}_{i}y\right\rangle_{\mathcal{K}}=\left\langle y,S_{i}JJ^{*}S^{*}_{i}y\right\rangle_{\mathcal{K}}=\left\|J^{*}S^{*}_{i}y\right\|^{2}_{H^{\prime}}

and
\left\langle y,Py\right\rangle_{\mathcal{K}}=\left\|J^{*}y\right\|^{2}_{H^{\prime}}
.
It suffices to show

(2.6)

\sum\nolimits^{d}_{i=1}\left\|J^{*}S^{*}_{i}y\right\|^{2}_{H^{\prime}}\leq\left\|J^{*}y\right\|^{2}_{H^{\prime}}.

But,

J^{*}y=\sum\nolimits_{j}J^{*}\left(S^{\alpha_{j}}\right)^{*}Wu_{j}=\sum\nolimits_{j}J^{*}\left(S^{\alpha_{j}}\right)^{*}JV_{\emptyset}u_{j}=\sum\nolimits_{j}B^{\tilde{\alpha}_{j}}V_{\emptyset}u_{j},

and

J^{*}S^{*}_{i}y=\sum\nolimits_{j}J^{*}\left(S^{\alpha_{j}i}\right)^{*}JV_{\emptyset}u_{j}=\sum\nolimits_{j}B^{i\tilde{\alpha_{j}}}V_{\emptyset}u_{j}=B_{i}\left(\sum\nolimits_{j}B^{\tilde{\alpha}_{j}}V_{\emptyset}u_{j}\right)=B_{i}J^{*}y.

Thus, (
2.6
) is equivalent to

\sum\nolimits^{d}_{i=1}\left\|B_{i}J^{*}y\right\|^{2}_{H^{\prime}}\leq\left\|J^{*}y\right\|^{2}_{H^{\prime}},

which holds since
\sum B^{*}_{i}B_{i}\leq I_{H^{\prime}}
. We conclude that
(
2.3
) holds.

Lastly, if
K
is of the form
K\left(\alpha,\beta\right)=W^{*}S^{\alpha}\left(S^{\beta}\right)^{*}W
,
then

\displaystyle K_{\Sigma}\left(\alpha,\beta\right)

\displaystyle=\sum\nolimits_{i}K\left(\alpha i,\beta i\right)

\displaystyle=\sum\nolimits_{i}W^{*}S^{\alpha i}\left(S^{\beta i}\right)^{*}W=W^{*}S^{\alpha}\left(\sum\nolimits_{i}S_{i}S^{*}_{i}\right)\left(S^{\beta}\right)^{*}W=K\left(\alpha,\beta\right).

Conversely, assume
K_{\Sigma}=K
. From the discussion above, it
follows that

\sum\nolimits^{d}_{i=1}S_{i}PS^{*}_{i}=P\;\text{on }M

where
M=\overline{span}\left\{\left(S^{\gamma}\right)^{*}WH:\gamma\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}\right\}
,
and
P
is the projection from
\mathcal{K}
onto
JH^{\prime}
. Left-multiplying
the identity by
S^{*}_{j}
and using
S^{*}_{j}S_{i}=\delta_{i,j}
,
we get
PS^{*}_{j}=S^{*}_{j}P
on
M
for all
j\in\left\{1,\dots,d\right\}
,
and so

P\left(S^{\beta}\right)^{*}=\left(S^{\beta}\right)^{*}P\;\text{on }M

for all
\beta\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}
. In particular,
P\left(S^{\beta}\right)^{*}W=\left(S^{\beta}\right)^{*}PW=\left(S^{\beta}\right)^{*}W
.
Therefore,

K\left(\alpha,\beta\right)=W^{*}S^{\alpha}P\left(S^{\beta}\right)^{*}W=W^{*}S^{\alpha}\left(S^{\beta}\right)^{*}W.

∎

Remark 2

(Infinite variables)
.

Theorem
1
extends without essential change to the case

d=\infty
, where the free semigroup
\mathbb{F}^{+}_{\infty}
is
generated by countably many letters. One defines

K_{\Sigma}\left(\alpha,\beta\right)\coloneqq\sum^{\infty}_{i=1}K\left(\alpha i,\beta i\right),

with the series converging in the quadratic-form sense, and interprets
all operator sums in the strong operator topology. The Kolmogorov
construction, the definition of the operators
B_{i}
, and the dilation
theory of Frazho-Bunce-Popescu remain valid for countable tuples.
In this setting one obtains the same equivalence as in Theorem
1
.
Thus the free moment dilation theorem continues to hold for infinitely
many noncommuting variables.

Remark 3

(Connection with the one-variable case)
.

When
d=1
, the free semigroup
\mathbb{F}^{+}_{1}
is naturally
identified with
\mathbb{N}
, and words
\alpha
correspond to integers

k
. In this case the kernel
K_{\Sigma}
reduces to the shifted
kernel

K_{\Sigma}\left(m,n\right)=K\left(m+1,n+1\right),\qquad m,n\in\mathbb{N},

and Theorem
1
recovers
[
Tia25
, Theorem 8]
.
In that single-variable setting, the dilation involves a unitary
U

and has the form

K\left(m,n\right)=W^{*}U^{m}PU^{*n}W,

with
P
a projection.

The projection
P
in Theorem
1
plays the same role in the
multivariable case. It encodes the defect between the universal Cuntz
dilation and the smaller subspace that actually realizes the kernel.
In general one only has (
2.2
) with (
2.3
). However,
in the special case
K_{\Sigma}=K
, the projection
P
acts as the
identity on the cyclic subspace generated by
\{\left(S^{\beta}\right)^{*}WH\}
.
On this subspace the compression disappears, so the realization formula
simplifies to (
2.5
). Thus
P
is not necessarily equal
to
I_{\mathcal{K}}
, but it becomes invisible in the part of the
dilation that supports the kernel.

The dilation theorem allows us to pass from random operators to Cuntz
families, where classical operator inequalities are available. In
particular, once the kernel is realized through a Cuntz dilation,
the noncommutative von Neumann inequality of Popescu applies. This
yields a mean-square analogue for random operator tuples, formulated
in the following corollary.

Corollary 4

(Free mean-square von Neumann inequality)
.

Assume
K_{\Sigma}\leq K
so that the dilation above
exists. For any noncommutative polynomial
f\left(Z\right)=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}}c_{\alpha}Z^{\alpha}

and any
u\in H
,

\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|f\left(A\right)^{*}u\right\|^{2}\right]\leq\left\|f\left(S\right)\right\|^{2}\left\|u\right\|^{2}\leq\left\|f\left(L\right)\right\|^{2}\left\|u\right\|^{2},

where
S=\left(S_{1},\dots,S_{d}\right)
is the Cuntz family from
Theorem
1
, and
L=\left(L_{1},\dots,L_{d}\right)
denotes
the left creation operators on the full Fock space
l^{2}\left(\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}\right)
.
Equivalently,

\mathbb{E}\left[f\left(A\right)f\left(A\right)^{*}\right]\leq\left\|f\left(L\right)\right\|^{2}I_{H}.

Proof.

Expanding
f\left(A\right)^{*}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|f\left(A\right)^{*}u\right\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha,\beta}c_{\alpha}\overline{c_{\beta}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle u,A^{\alpha}\left(A^{\beta}\right)^{*}u\right\rangle\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha,\beta}c_{\alpha}\overline{c_{\beta}}\left\langle u,K\left(\alpha,\beta\right)u\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha,\beta}c_{\alpha}\overline{c_{\beta}}\left\langle u,W^{*}S^{\alpha}P\left(S^{\beta}\right)^{*}Wu\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\|Pf\left(S\right)^{*}Wu\right\|^{2},

using the representation of
K
in (
2.2
). Since
W
is
an isometry and
\left\|Px\right\|\leq\left\|x\right\|
,
this is bounded by
\left\|f\left(S\right)\right\|^{2}\left\|u\right\|^{2}
.
Finally, Popescu’s noncommutative von Neumann inequality gives the
claim, see
[
Pop91
]
.
∎

3.
Mean-square Free Functional Calculus

The dilation result and the mean-square von Neumann inequality from
the previous section provide the basic ingredients for building a
functional calculus for random operators. In the deterministic setting,
Popescu showed how dilations lead to contractive extensions of polynomial
evaluation, first to the free disk algebra and then to the free Hardy
algebra. Our goal here is to establish an analogous construction in
the mean-square setting. We begin by defining the appropriate Banach
space of random operators equipped with the mean-square norm, and
then show how the dilation machinery gives rise to a canonical extension
of polynomial evaluation.

Setting.
Fix
d\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
A=\left(A_{1},\dots,A_{d}\right)

be a
d
-tuple of strongly measurable random operators on a Hilbert
space
H
, such that

\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|A^{\alpha}\right\|^{2}\right]<\infty,\quad\alpha\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}.

Define the moment kernel

K\left(\alpha,\beta\right)\coloneqq\mathbb{E}\left[A^{\alpha}\left(A^{\beta}\right)^{*}\right],\quad\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d},

and assume
K_{\Sigma}\leq K
, where
K_{\Sigma}\left(\alpha,\beta\right)\coloneqq\sum^{d}_{i=1}K\left(\alpha i,\beta i\right)
.
Then the dilation in Theorem
1
exists, and Corollary
4

holds:

\mathbb{E}\left[f\left(A\right)f\left(A\right)^{*}\right]\leq\left\|f(L)\right\|^{2}I_{H}

for every noncommutative polynomial
f
, where
L=\left(L_{1},\dots,L_{d}\right)

are the left creation operators on
\ell^{2}\left(\mathbb{F}^{+}_{d}\right)
.

Definition 5
.

For a strongly measurable random operator
X\colon\Omega\rightarrow\mathcal{L}\left(H\right)
,
define the mean-square seminorm

\left\|X\right\|_{ms}\coloneqq\Big\|\mathbb{E}\left[XX^{*}\right]\Big\|^{1/2}=\sup_{\|u\|=1}\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|X^{*}u\right\|^{2}\right]\right)^{1/2}.

Set

\mathcal{L}_{ms}\left(\Omega,H\right)\coloneqq\left\{X\colon\Omega\rightarrow\mathcal{L}\left(H\right)\mid\left\|X\right\|_{ms}<\infty\right\}/\sim

where
X\sim Y
if
\left\|X-Y\right\|_{ms}=0
. Then
\mathcal{L}_{ms}\left(\Omega,H\right)

is a Banach space of random operators with an isometric involution

X\mapsto X^{*}
.

Remark 6
.

If
X\left(\omega\right)\equiv T
is deterministic, then

\left\|X\right\|_{ms}=\left\|TT^{*}\right\|^{1/2}=\left\|T\right\|.

Thus, in the non-random case the mean-square norm reduces to the usual
operator norm on
\mathcal{L}\left(H\right)
.

Definition 7
.

A net
\left(X_{\lambda}\right)
of random operators
on
H
converges to
X
in ms-SOT if

\mathbb{E}\left\|\left(X_{\lambda}-X\right)^{*}u\right\|^{2}\rightarrow 0,\quad\forall u\in H.

We then write
X_{\lambda}\xrightarrow{\text{ms-SOT}}X
.

Theorem 8

(Mean-square free disk algebra calculus)
.

Let
\mathbb{C}\langle Z\rangle=\mathbb{C}\left\langle Z_{1},\dots,Z_{d}\right\rangle

be the algebra of noncommutative polynomials and endow it with the
Fock-space norm

\left\|p\right\|_{L}\coloneqq\left\|p(L)\right\|,\quad p\in\mathbb{C}\langle Z\rangle.

Let
\mathcal{A}_{d}
be the norm completion of
\mathbb{C}\langle Z\rangle

under
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L}
(the free disk algebra).

Then there exists a unique linear map

\Phi\colon\mathcal{A}_{d}\longrightarrow\mathcal{L}_{ms}\left(\Omega,H\right)

with the following properties:

(1)

(Polynomial agreement) For every polynomial
p\in\mathbb{C}\langle Z\rangle
,
one has
\Phi\left(p\right)=p\left(A\right)
(as a random operator),
and

\left\|\Phi\left(p\right)\right\|_{ms}\leq\left\|p\left(L\right)\right\|.

(2)

(Continuity) For all
f\in\mathcal{A}_{d}
,

\left\|\Phi\left(f\right)\right\|_{ms}\leq\left\|f\left(L\right)\right\|.

In particular, if
f_{n}\to f
in
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L}
,
then
\left\|\Phi\left(f_{n}\right)-\Phi\left(f\right)\right\|_{ms}\rightarrow 0
.

(3)

(Uniqueness)
\Phi
is the only linear map
\mathcal{A}_{d}\to\mathcal{L}_{ms}\left(\Omega,H\right)

that is continuous for
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L}
and agrees
with polynomial evaluation.

(4)

(Strong mean-square convergence) If
f_{n}\to f
in
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L}
,
then
\Phi\left(f_{n}\right)\xrightarrow{\text{ms-SOT}}\Phi\left(f\right)
.
That is, for every
u\in H
,

\mathbb{E}\left\|\left(\Phi\left(f_{n}\right)\right)^{*}u-\left(\Phi\left(f\right)\right)^{*}u\right\|^{2}\longrightarrow 0.

Proof.

Let
p,q
be noncommutative polynomials. Apply Corollary
4

to the polynomial
p-q
:

(3.1)

\mathbb{E}\left[\left(p\left(A\right)-q\left(A\right)\right)\left(p\left(A\right)-q\left(A\right)\right)^{*}\right]\leq\left\|\left(p-q\right)\left(L\right)\right\|^{2}I_{H}.

Taking the operator norm and square-root gives

(3.2)

\left\|p\left(A\right)-q\left(A\right)\right\|_{ms}\leq\left\|p\left(L\right)-q\left(L\right)\right\|.

In particular,
\left\|p\left(A\right)\right\|_{ms}\leq\left\|p\left(L\right)\right\|
.

Fix
f\in\mathcal{A}_{d}
. By definition of
\mathcal{A}_{d}
, choose
polynomials
p_{n}
with
\left\|p_{n}\left(L\right)-f\left(L\right)\right\|\to 0
.
By (
3.2
),
\left\{p_{n}\left(A\right)\right\}
is Cauchy
in
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{ms}
. Hence there exists a (mean-square)
limit in
\mathcal{L}_{ms}\left(\Omega,H\right)
which we denote
by
\Phi\left(f\right)
:

\left\|p_{n}\left(A\right)-\Phi\left(f\right)\right\|_{ms}\longrightarrow 0.

For polynomials
p
, clearly
\Phi\left(p\right)=p\left(A\right)
.
This defines
\Phi
on
\mathcal{A}_{d}
.

Independence of approximants
. Suppose also
q_{n}
are polynomials
with

\left\|q_{n}\left(L\right)-f\left(L\right)\right\|\to 0.

Then
\left\|p_{n}\left(L\right)-q_{n}\left(L\right)\right\|\to 0
,
so by (
3.2
),

\left\|p_{n}\left(A\right)-q_{n}\left(A\right)\right\|_{ms}\to 0.

Hence
\lim p_{n}\left(A\right)=\lim q_{n}\left(A\right)
in mean
square. Thus
\Phi\left(f\right)
is independent of the chosen approximating
sequence.

Linearity
. Take
f,g\in\mathcal{A}_{d}
and scalars
a,b
.
Let
p_{n}\to f
,
q_{n}\to g
in
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L}
.
Then

\left\|\left(ap_{n}+bq_{n}\right)\left(L\right)-\left(af+bg\right)\left(L\right)\right\|\to 0,

so
\left(ap_{n}+bq_{n}\right)\left(A\right)\to\Phi\left(af+bg\right)

in mean square. But also
\left(ap_{n}+bq_{n}\right)\left(A\right)=ap_{n}\left(A\right)+bq_{n}\left(A\right)\to a\Phi\left(f\right)+b\Phi\left(g\right)
.
Hence

\Phi\left(af+bg\right)=a\Phi\left(f\right)+b\Phi\left(g\right).

Contractivity and continuity
. Let
f\in\mathcal{A}_{d}

and
p_{n}\to f
in
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L}
. Then

\left\|\Phi\left(f\right)\right\|_{ms}\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\left\|p_{n}\left(A\right)\right\|_{ms}\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}\left\|p_{n}\left(L\right)\right\|=\left\|f\left(L\right)\right\|.

Similarly, for
f,g\in\mathcal{A}_{d}
and approximants
p_{n}\to f
,

q_{n}\to g
,

\displaystyle\left\|\Phi\left(f\right)-\Phi\left(g\right)\right\|_{ms}

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{n}\left\|p_{n}\left(A\right)-q_{n}\left(A\right)\right\|_{ms}

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{n}\left\|p_{n}\left(L\right)-q_{n}\left(L\right)\right\|_{L}=\left\|f\left(L\right)-g\left(L\right)\right\|.

Thus
\Phi
is contractive and continuous as claimed.

Strong mean-square convergence
. Fix
u\in H
. For any noncommutative
polynomials
p,q
, by (
3.1
),

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left\|p\left(A\right)^{*}u-q\left(A\right)^{*}u\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\langle u,\mathbb{E}\left[\left(p\left(A\right)-q\left(A\right)\right)\left(p\left(A\right)-q\left(A\right)\right)^{*}\right]u\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\left\|p\left(L\right)-q\left(L\right)\right\|^{2}\left\|u\right\|^{2}.

Hence, if
p_{n}\to f
in
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L}
, then

p_{n}\left(A\right)^{*}u
is Cauchy in
L^{2}\left(\Omega,H\right)
,
so

p_{n}\left(A\right)^{*}u\longrightarrow\left(\Phi\left(f\right)\right)^{*}u\quad\text{in }L^{2}\left(\Omega,H\right).

This proves the stated stated ms-SOT convergence.

Uniqueness of
\Phi
. Finally, suppose
\Psi\colon\mathcal{A}_{d}\to\mathcal{L}_{ms}\left(\Omega,H\right)

is another linear map, continuous for
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L}
,
with
\Psi\left(p\right)=p\left(A\right)
for all polynomials. For

f\in\mathcal{A}_{d}
, take
p_{n}\to f
in
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L}
.
Then

\displaystyle\left\|\Psi\left(f\right)-\Phi\left(f\right)\right\|_{ms}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\Psi\left(f\right)-\Psi\left(p_{n}\right)\right\|_{ms}+\left\|\Psi\left(p_{n}\right)-\Phi\left(f\right)\right\|_{ms}

\displaystyle=\left\|\Psi\left(f\right)-\Psi\left(p_{n}\right)\right\|_{ms}+\left\|\Phi\left(p_{n}\right)-\Phi\left(f\right)\right\|_{ms}\longrightarrow 0,

since
\Psi
is continuous and
\Psi\left(p_{n}\right)=\Phi\left(p_{n}\right)=p_{n}\left(A\right)\to\Phi\left(f\right)
.
Hence
\Psi=\Phi
, proving uniqueness.
∎

Remark 9
.

The calculus above is “canonical” in the sense that it is determined
solely by the inequality from Corollary
4
(hence by
A=\left(A_{1},\dots,A_{d}\right)

and the condition
K_{\Sigma}\leq K
), and does not depend on a particular
choice of dilation space or Cuntz family realizing
K
.

It is natural to ask whether the construction extends to the full
free Hardy algebra
F^{\infty}_{d}
, the weak operator topology (WOT)-closed
algebra generated by the left creation tuple
L
on the full Fock
space. In the deterministic setting, this extension was carried out
by Popescu using the method of radial dilations, following the classical
Sz.-Nagy-Foiaş approach to the
H^{\infty}
calculus. See, for instance,
Popescu’s work on noncommutative Hardy algebras
[
Pop10
]
,
as well as the parallel operator-algebraic perspective of Davidson-Pitts

[
DP98
]
.

We now adapt the same idea to the mean-square setting. The proof follows
Popescu’s argument, but in a probabilistic context: our functional
calculus acts on random operator tuples, takes values in the Banach
space
\mathcal{L}_{ms}(\Omega,H)
, and convergence is understood
in ms-SOT sense (Definition
7
). This provides a stochastic analogue
of Popescu’s free
H^{\infty}
calculus.

Theorem 10

(Mean-square free
H^{\infty}
calculus)
.

Let
F^{\infty}_{d}=\overline{\mathcal{A}_{d}}^{WOT}

denote the (left) free Hardy algebra (the WOT-closed algebra generated
by the left creation tuple
L
on the full Fock space). For
\phi\in F^{\infty}_{d}
,
write its radial dilates by

\phi_{r}\left(Z\right)\coloneqq\sum\nolimits_{\alpha}r^{\left|\alpha\right|}\phi_{\alpha}Z^{\alpha},\quad 0<r<1,

so that
\phi_{r}(L)\in\mathcal{A}_{d}
(the free disk algebra) and

\left\|\phi_{r}\left(L\right)\right\|\leq\left\|\phi\left(L\right)\right\|
,
while
\phi_{r}\left(L\right)\to\phi\left(L\right)
in the strong
operator topology as
r\uparrow 1
.

Then there exists a unique linear map

\Phi\colon F^{\infty}_{d}\longrightarrow\mathcal{L}_{ms}\left(\Omega,H\right)

such that for every
\phi\in F^{\infty}_{d}
:

(1)

(Definition by radial limit)
\phi_{r}\left(A\right)\xrightarrow{\text{ms-SOT}}\Phi\left(\phi\right)

as
r\uparrow 1
, i.e.,

\lim_{r\uparrow 1}\mathbb{E}\left\|\left(\phi_{r}\left(A\right)-\Phi\left(\phi\right)\right)^{*}u\right\|^{2}=0

for all
u\in H
.

(2)

(Contractivity) We have

\mathbb{E}\left[\Phi\left(\phi\right)\Phi\left(\phi\right)^{*}\right]\leq\left\|\phi\left(L\right)\right\|^{2}I_{H},

equivalently,

\sup_{\|u\|=1}\mathbb{E}\left\|\left(\Phi\left(\phi\right)\right)^{*}u\right\|^{2}\leq\left\|\phi\left(L\right)\right\|^{2}.

(3)

(Compatibility) If
\phi\in\mathcal{A}_{d}
, then
\Phi(\phi)
coincides
with the mean-square limit defined in Theorem
8
.

(4)

(Uniqueness)
\Phi
is the only linear map with these properties.

Proof.

For any noncommutative polynomial
f
, Corollary
4
gives,
for all
u\in H
,

(3.3)

\mathbb{E}\left\|f\left(A\right)^{*}u\right\|^{2}=\left\|P^{1/2}f\left(S\right)^{*}Wu\right\|^{2}.

Using the
\mathcal{A}{}_{d}
calculus from Theorem
8
, passing
to the limit in the polynomial identity (
3.3
), we get

(3.4)

\mathbb{E}\left\|g\left(A\right)^{*}u\right\|^{2}=\left\|P^{1/2}g\left(S\right)^{*}Wu\right\|^{2}

for all
g\in\mathcal{A}_{d}
.

Define
\Phi
by radial ms-SOT limits
. Fix
\phi\in F^{\infty}_{d}
.
For any
u\in H
, apply (
3.4
) to
g=\phi_{r}-\phi_{s}
:

\mathbb{E}\left\|\left(\phi_{r}\left(A\right)-\phi_{s}\left(A\right)\right)^{*}u\right\|^{2}=\left\|P^{1/2}\left(\phi_{r}\left(S\right)-\phi_{s}\left(S\right)\right)^{*}Wu\right\|^{2}.

Since
\left\{\phi_{r}\left(S\right)\right\}{}_{0<r<1}
is uniformly
bounded (
\left\|\phi_{r}\left(S\right)\right\|\leq\left\|\phi\left(L\right)\right\|
)
and
\phi_{r}\left(S\right)\rightarrow\phi\left(S\right)
in SOT
as
r\uparrow 1
, the right-hand side
\rightarrow 0
for each fixed

u
. Thus
\left\{\phi_{r}\left(A\right)^{*}u\right\}_{r}
is
Cauchy in
L^{2}(\Omega,H)
. Define

\left(\Phi\left(\phi\right)\right)^{*}u\coloneqq\lim_{r\uparrow 1}\left(\phi_{r}\left(A\right)\right)^{*}u.

Linearity of
\Phi
follows from
\left(a\phi+b\psi\right)_{r}=a\phi_{r}+b\psi_{r}

and linearity of
L^{2}
limits.

Contractivity
. Using (
3.4
) with
g=\phi_{r}
and
letting
r\uparrow 1
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left\|\left(\Phi\left(\phi\right)\right)^{*}u\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\lim_{r\uparrow 1}\mathbb{E}\left\|\phi_{r}\left(A\right)^{*}u\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\lim_{r\uparrow 1}\left\|P^{1/2}\phi_{r}\left(S\right)^{*}Wu\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\big\|P^{1/2}\left(\phi\left(S\right)\right)^{*}Wu\big\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\phi\left(S\right)\right\|^{2}\left\|Wu\right\|^{2}\leq\left\|\phi\left(L\right)\right\|^{2}\left\|u\right\|^{2}.

Hence

\mathbb{E}\left[\Phi\left(\phi\right)\left(\Phi\left(\phi\right)\right)^{*}\right]\leq\left\|\phi\left(L\right)\right\|^{2}I_{H}.

Compatibility
. If
\phi\in\mathcal{A}_{d}
, then
\phi_{r}\left(S\right)\rightarrow\phi\left(S\right)

in norm. By (
3.4
),

\mathbb{E}\left\|\left(\phi_{r}\left(A\right)-\phi\left(A\right)\right)^{*}u\right\|^{2}\rightarrow 0.

Thus,
\Phi\left(\phi\right)=\phi\left(A\right)
.

Uniqueness
. If
\Psi
is another linear map with the same
ms-SOT radial limit property, then for all
u\in H
,

\left(\Psi\left(\phi\right)\right)^{*}u=\lim_{r\uparrow 1}\left(\phi_{r}\left(A\right)\right)^{*}u=\left(\Phi\left(\phi\right)\right)^{*}u.

Hence
\Psi\left(\phi\right)=\Phi\left(\phi\right)
in mean-square
sense.
∎

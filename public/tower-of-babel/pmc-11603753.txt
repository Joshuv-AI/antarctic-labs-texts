Multivariate analysis for agronomic, physiological, macro, and micronutrient traits of exotic vegetable amaranth genotypes
Authors: Mirza Abrar, Tanveer Ahmad, Shahid Iqbal, Rana Naveed Ur Rehman, Syeda Anum Masood Bokhari, Zahoor Ahmad, Arkadiusz Artyszak, Abeer Hashem, Jawaher Alkahtani, Elsayed Fathi Abd-Allah
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
The genus amaranth is comprised of 60–70 species. This climate hardy plant is grown as a vegetable as well as cereal crop in many areas of the world. Current study multivariate analysis was used to characterize the exotic vegetable amaranth genotypes. The experiment was conducted in the research area of Muhammad Nawaz Shareef University of Agriculture, Multan under RCBD design with three replications of 50 exotic amaranth genotype. 22 different variables were investigated for the principal component and cluster analysis using different software packages in R studio. According to the results of principal component analysis the first five PCs had eigenvalues greater than one and they explained the 84.59% diversity in exotic vegetable amaranth accessions. First two principal components explained 57.31% of variation. Traits that loaded highly for first PC include moisture (-0.892), carbohydrates (0.938), energy (0.920), stomatal conductance (0.803), net photosynthesis (0.795) and dry weight (0.770). The traits which impacted the second PC highly include protein (0.692), fat (-0.571), nitrogen (0.692), calcium (0.545), phosphorus (0.908), zinc (0.960), iron (0.826) and potassium (0.872). For future breeding program the selection based on these results will be effective for crop enhancement drive in exotic vegetable amaranth genotypes. Hierarchical clustering was done and dendrogram split the 50 genotypes into 5 groups using silhouette method. The pairwise clustering revealed that intra cluster distance is smaller than inter cluster distance. The maximum inter cluster distance was observed between cluster I and II (9.88); between cluster I and III (9.47); between cluster II and IV (9.27). Introduction

The Amaranth plant, which encompasses various species (Amaranthus spp.) is classified within the Amaranthaceae family. It consists of about 60–70 species including three cultivated grains (A. cruentus, A. caudatus and A. hypochondriacus), vegetable crops (A. blitum and A. tricolor) and weeds (A. spinosus, A. virdis, A. retroflexus, A. graecizans, A. dubius and A. hybridus) [1]. However, due to hybridization the exact number of Amaranth species is still uncertain [2].

Amaranth, an indigenous plant of South America, has a rich history of cultivation and utilization by local communities for approximately 8,000 years. Originating in South America, it gradually spread to regions such as North America, India, and Asia, eventually reaching China. Today, vegetable amaranth is extensively grown and utilized both as a cereal and vegetable crop in hilly areas of India, Pakistan, Nepal, and China. This versatile plant finds diverse applications in various fields. Its notable uses include medicinal purposes, culinary applications, cosmetology, industrial applications, and agricultural practices. In addition to its practical uses, amaranth is appreciated for its aesthetic appeal. Some countries cultivate amaranth as an ornamental plant, primarily due to the vibrant colors of its inflorescences and leaves, adding beauty to gardens and landscapes [3].

Amaranthus is a genus of plants that can be found worldwide, encompassing both annual and short-lived perennial species [4]. Within this diverse genus, there are cultivated varieties, endangered species, restricted endemics, as well as common and widespread weed species [5]. Amaranth, a grain crop originating from Central and South Americas, holds considerable nutritional importance. Notably, it boasts high protein content at 14.5%, with lysine levels reaching 6.2 g per 100 g of protein [6]. The successful cultivation of grain amaranth has been documented in various countries worldwide, showcasing its adaptability to diverse environmental conditions. Different species of amaranth are grown in China [7] and Mexico [8]. Bolivia, Thailand, India and Kenya are other countries that are major producer of vegetable amaranth [9–11]. Other countries include Australia [12–15].

Botany and plant morphology

Taxonomy, as defined by [16], is a systematic classification of biological organisms based on a comprehensive consideration of their similarities and differences. In the realm of plant classification, taxonomists utilize various lines of evidence, including morphology, phylogeny, physiology, phytochemistry, anatomy, cytology, and palynology, to discern similarities and differences among plants and categorize them into distinct taxa [17].

According to [18], morphological data of plants, being readily observable and obtainable, are extensively employed in taxonomic studies. External morphology serves as the fundamental language for characterizing, identifying, classifying, and establishing relationships among plants. Morphological characteristics, especially those related to flowers, fruits, and seeds, play a crucial role in identifying angiosperms and defining natural groups within different taxonomic levels [19].

Morphology remains a cornerstone in the construction of taxonomic systems, providing the majority of characters essential for classification. Consequently, the close relationship between morphology and taxonomy is anticipated to persist for many years to come [19].

Amaranth showcases a wide array of characteristics, encompassing diverse plant forms that vary in branching patterns, stem colors, and seed colors (yellow, white, brown, or black). The plant’s height also exhibits significant diversity, ranging from 0.3 to 3.0 m. Typically classified as an annual plant, amaranth presents either alternate or opposite leaf arrangements. Its unique features include single inflorescences bearing unisexual or bisexual flowers, with particularly abundant and dense inflorescences in seed forms. Notably, a single amaranth plant has the potential to yield over 500 g of seeds, while vegetable forms, although producing fewer seeds, contribute a substantial amount of green matter.

Diversity in amaranth plant

Amaranth species exhibit minimal genetic differences, making them prone to easy cross breeding [20]. reported that weedy type of amaranth can interbreed with desired crops if not removed from the field. The genetic variations in different amaranth genotypes are elucidated through archaeological, historical, and morphological data, with germplasm characterization relying on traits of varying heritability, hindered mainly by environmental influences. Taxonomic classification poses challenges due to factors such as pigmentation, which displays wide segregation, and plant size, influenced by sunlight hours and other environmental variables. Notably, amaranth displays high plasticity [21, 22] highlight the existence of amaranth throughout the world with 70 species and 400 different varieties. Despite of vast range of diversity in amaranth only few species are cultivated commercially in some parts of the world. Characterizing Amaranthus is complicated genus and its characterization is not easy due to diversification among the species. Amaranthus cruentus, and Amaranthus hypochondriacus are very common species generally cultivated for grain purpose, and often termed to as “pseudo-cereals.” Amaranthus dubius, Amaranthus viridis, Amaranthus spinosus, Amaranthus blitum, and Amaranthus tricolor are domesticated as leafy green [1, 23] classifies A. hybridus, A. spinosus, A. graecizans, A. viridis, A. dubius, and A. retroflexus, grouped in weed.

A lot of amaranth species are being cultivated commercially in different regions of the world. Amaranthus cruentus, A. blitum, A. hybridus, and A. dubius are domesticated in the areas of West Africa and A. tricolor is predominant in East Africa, and Asia. A. cruentus is a main amaranth species cultivated in South America mainly as a cereal crop. A. dubius is major vegetable in some of the regions in Caribbean and China. A. hybridus is also a significant crop in south western states of USA. Some of the important countries that have domesticated A. hybridus for vegetable and grain purpose include Nepal, Indonesia, Malaysia, Mexico, Philippines, and Thailand [24, 25].

The characterization of amaranth germplasm is based on degree of hereditability traits, with climatic conditions as the main challenge. Taxonomic classification is not easy for amaranth genotypes in different species as the characteristics involved such as leave color; that presents wide separation and the size of the plant; that depends on the hours of sunlight, cultural practices and other environmental factors. Finally, it is said that the amaranth plant has high plasticity [21].

Amaranth adapts very well in extreme conditions so found in so many geographical regions. Due to its presence in various parts of the world, it displays tremendous diversity [26]. Due to amaranth’s tremendous genetic diversity, there could be lot of possible ways for enhancement. The genetic research on underutilized crops is zero to none in Pakistan. Almost all the resources divert to major crops. That is why no up to date knowledge is available regarding this crop. Moreover this “orphan crop,” as it is recognized is neglected by researchers in Pakistan due to non-availability of plant resources [27].

The survival of the species is very crucial and it naturally depends on genetic variations. The genetic diversity helps in the adaptation of a species in extreme biotic, abiotic and in suddenly changing conditions. The magnitude of genetic variation in a plant species can be estimated using most effective tools of multivariate analysis. The different components of multivariate analysis used in calculating genetic variations in species are principal component analysis, cluster analysis and D2 analysis. These tools help in the selection of desirable characters in parental lines for hybridization that leads in achieving maximum heterosis. Arachis and Amaranthus genotypes are among the many species that had been characterized on the basis of quantitative variations using multivariate statistical methods [28–31]. Several studies on amaranth used multivariate analysis for the estimation of genetic diversity. The relationship with the genetic variations in 23 cultivated and wild Amaranthus species were examined by [32]. Another study investigated genetic diversity in 17 genotypes from Amaranthus hypochondriacus, A. caudatus and A. cruentu by [33]. A study by [34] explored the genetic diversity in Amaranthus tricolor, A. blitum and A. dubius along with other hundred species using the Mahalanobis D2 analysis. Research by [35] studied 98 accessions of grain amaranthus (Amaranthus hypochondriacus L.) for genetic divergence among 14 different biochemical and quantitative traits aiming for the selection of more diverse parents for hybridization. Other studies that use multivariate analysis techniques include [36] in vegetable [37], in amaranthus for measuring the degree of genetic variations and influence of different traits to the total divergence. Although research material is available on the geomorphological in amaranth [38] and quinoa [39] but in-depth research on agronomic, physiological and nutritional traits of amaranth genotypes is uncommon. Many researchers have described genus amaranth as one of the most diverse plant groups and a bundle of variations are present in different genotypes of a single species [40–42]. In Pakistan, exotic vegetable amaranth has never been investigated for variations related to agronomic, nutritional and physiological traits. Many cultivars of different type of plants have been introduced from different other countries in Pakistan. By using breeding tools, improvements were made and from one foreign cultivar hundreds other were produced by the plant breeders. Now all those cultivars are source for creating new lines. Amaranth is produced by some local farmers in Pakistan on marginal scale and it has not been explored for its true potential as a commercial crop.

One of the purposes of this study was to introduce exotic amaranth vegetable in Pakistan. The other was to evaluate those accessions for genetic diversity on the basis of agronomic, physiological and nutrition characters. This research is very crucial for the future of vegetable amaranth production in Pakistan. The variations in exotic amaranth accessions are helpful for the selection of plant material that better suits to the environmental conditions of geographical areas in country. Plant breeders may use information from this study to select better genotypes from this alternate crop to produce improved cultivars that are suitable for the environmental conditions in Pakistan. This nutritious crop would not only help to combat malnutrition but also feed fast growing population [43]. When plants are selected for better traits, the variation in genotypes is crucial for the selection of best lines for crop improvement [44–46]. The results from this investigation are helpful in upcoming genetic studies to develop this climate hardy and extremely nourishing overlooked crop species.

Materials and methods

Experimental site

The research investigation was conducted in research area of MNS-University of Agriculture, Block C” Multan. The GPS coordinates for the site are “30°08’51.1"N and 71°26’36.8"E” at average height of 126 m above sea level.

Research was conducted on amaranth genotypes during the 2023 growing season in field at research area of “C block” in the MNS-University of Agriculture, Multan. The nitrogen fertilizer was applied at the rate of 30 kg/Acre. According to studies, amaranth crops respond well to 135 kg/ha of nitrogen [47], while [48] found that a positive response in amaranth crops requires a saturated level of 20 kg/ha of nitrogen. In order to reduce nitrate accumulation in the leaves and assess genotypes for low input and low-cost commercial production, the nitrogen was applied cautiously in this study. According to [49] and [50] the field experiments related to amaranth requires basic cultural techniques. Weeding is done by hand hoeing after two weeks of seed sowing and after that according to the requirement. As seed of amaranth is very small and multiple plants can be emerged from the soil. To optimize the number of plants and better use of resources thinning of plants was done at early stage.

Plant material

50 exotic vegetable amaranth accessions were used in this research (Table 1); The labelled packets of seed material came from the United State Department of Agriculture (USDA). The seed material of exotic amaranth accessions was placed in seed storage facility of agronomy department in MNS-University of Agriculture, Multan prior to sowing in field. Genotypes from Amaranthus hypochonricus (15 accessions) Amaranthus hybidus (8 accessions) Amaranthus cruentus (11 accessions) and Amaranthus tricolor (16 accessions) were used in the study.

Experimental layout and crop management

The exotic vegetable amaranth accessions were planted in separate plots of 2 × 2 m. Three replications of each genotype were planted in a (RCBD) Randomized Complete Block Design. The distance between the rows was maintained at 30 cm and seed were planted by plugging into soil at the distance of 15 cm. The field of experiment was prepared by the field when needed. Harrowing was done with a tractor.

Traits and data collection

50 exotic vegetable amaranth genotypes were investigated and observations on 22 agronomic, physiological and nutritional traits were made (Table 2). In each replication five plants were tagged randomly and all the subsequent observation were made on them and data was collected accordingly. The purpose of the study was to use the amaranth leaves as a vegetable so plants were looked after to the stage where leaves of amaranth genotypes could be used as vegetable.

Data analysis

Data obtained for 50 exotic vegetable amaranth genotypes against the 22 different traits. The mean of calculation for different trait was summarized. The data was subjected to multivariate analysis. It was noted that the factors under investigation does not share a common measurement scale. All the traits investigated in this research were so much diverse. The collected mean values of all the traits were used for the PCA, hierarchical clustering based on agglomerative coefficient. The data from all the traits was standardized to the standard deviation having value one and means were standardized to zero as suggested by [53–55].

For the classification of 50 exotic vegetable amaranth genotypes on the basis of similarities and differences the data was manipulated using R version (4.4.0). R studio is a powerful software with various packages for statistical interpretation of data and for the graphical visualization of results. This investigation used a list of packages from R software to accomplish cluster analysis. The analysis was based on ward D2 hierarchical clustering and Euclidian distance. To create the dendrogram from the data set silhouette technique and NbClust package from the R studio were used to find the suitable number of clusters [56, 57].

R studio provide many software packages for graphical visualization of data. “FactoMineR” and “factoextra” packages were used for the computation of PCA, eigenvectors, eigenvalues and visualization of biplot and clusters. Total variation and largest contributor to the diversity of genotypes align at each axis of PCA [58]. The main components responsible for total variation were determined using eigenvalue table and those principal components were retained having eigenvalue higher than 1 [59]. The PCs that explain 70–80% of the cumulative variation in the data were retained for analysis and interpretation [60, 61].

One of the methods to find variations into different clusters is to find distance within and across the clusters. The intra cluster and inter cluster distance were determined by subjecting the mean data of each replication and mean of each variable to “clv” package of the R-studio. R studio has a software package with the name of “biotools” in which “Singh” statistics was used for the determination of the importance of each variable on the basis of their cumulative proportion in the diversity in exotic vegetable amaranth genotypes [62].

Results

Principal component analysis (PCA)

From the data only first five principal components were used for the interpretation of results. PC1, PC2, PC3, PC4 and PC5 were kept from the original data of 22 components because these 5 PCs s explained 85% of the total variations among 50 exotic vegetable amaranth genotypes. The percentage variations explained by PC1 to PC5 35.93%, 21.38%, 13.02%, 8.46% and 5.78% respectively (Table 3). The first principal component (PC1) described 35.93% of the total diversity and different traits dominated in the component. The high loading variables for first principal component (PC1) were, MO (-0.892), CARBS (0.938), E (0.92), ST.CON(0.803), NP (0.795) and DW (0.77). Results from the second principal component (PC2) explained 21.38% of the total variation and the traits associated to those variations include PR (0.692), FAT (-0.571), N (0.692), P (0.908), Zn (0.96), Fe (0.826), and K (0.872). Results out of third principal component (PC3) demonstrate 13% of the total diversity in exotic amaranth genotypes. The variables loaded highly for PC3 were BM (0.677), FI (0.82) and TR (0.738). Results of fourth principal components (PC4) revealed the association with PH 45D (0.932), PH 65D (0.914) and it also explained 8.5% of the total variations in exotic vegetable amaranth genotypes. The contribution of fifth principal component (PC5) in total variation was 5.8% of the total variations that is associated with variables like ASH (0.721), Ca (0.706), and Mg (0.771) and LPP (-0.667). (Table 3) displays two types of association among the variables. The positive or negative sign was attached with the values of the variables. It indicates the positive or negative association between the variables. The higher the value the higher the association between variables and the sign attached to the value only gives the direction to that association. Discussion

Principal component analysis

Amaranth is an underutilized and also known as “orphan plant” due to lack of interest of researchers in this crop [27]. The amaranth is a plant that can truly wonders the growers because of its nutritional profile. This plant is a rich source of various macro and micro nutrients. Many researchers [63–67] describe that amaranth is a crop containing multiple type of important nutrients that are also in abundant quantity.

Principal component analysis (PCA) is used to find important principal components in relation to desirable traits in diverse genotypes. PCA is used to find the level of correlation between different traits under investigation [68]. Additionally, PCA is exclusively used for its dimension reduction method. It creates small set from the very large data with which contain all the necessary statistics for data analysis [69]. PCA provide sufficient information about the contributing variables in diversity of genotypes [70, 71] describe that the variables with higher value between 1 and − 1 mostly influence the cluster in PCA. The value less than 0.5 and − 0.5 are not significant in most cases. The current study found that separate clusters were generated due to major contribution of some traits by diverse group of exotic amaranth genotypes [72] stated that those variables having higher coefficient values on the principal component axis are most important. He reported that coefficient value of 0.5 or more should be noted for consideration of any variables. The opposite loading on PC axis is result of positive and negative correlation among the variables as well as between the components. It was inferred that traits with higher values did contribute in exotic amaranth genotypes. The higher positive or negative value is also responsible for different clusters. 22 different agronomic, physiological and nutritional traits were studied and it was observed from the analysis that only first five PCs are responsible for diversification in exotic amaranth genotypes. During the analysis it was observed that the first two components with values of 35.9% (PC1) and 21.4% (PC2) contributed maximum in variability of exotic amaranth genotypes. The cumulative response of these first two PCs was 57.3% which indicated that most of the variations falls in first two PCs [73] reported total variations at 39.87% (PC1) and 18.64% (PC2) in Amaranths species which are almost same to our findings. The findings of [42, 74] were in agreement with our studies. The study found that PC1 and PC2 are the major contributor and should be used for selection of exotic amaranth genotypes for crop improvement program. Most of the agronomic, physiological and nutritional traits studied influence greatly on PC1 and PC2RC1 and RC2. The results of [75, 76] were in agreement with our findings. The significant association between PC1 and physiological factors like stomatal conductance and net photosynthesis, yield contributing traits like dry weight and moisture content along with carbohydrates and energy should be used for selection of exotic amaranth genotypes. Numerous investigators [73, 76] were in agreement with our investigation. Plant height 45 days after sowing and 65 days after sowing impacted on PC4 and should be used as a key factor for selection. The discoveries of [77] in amaranthus plant were in agreement with our studies.

Our investigation revealed that out of 22 only first 5 PCs showed 84.59% of the total diversity amongst 50 exotic genotypes of amaranth vegetable. This also revealed that the traits used in this investigation significantly contributed directly in the variation present in exotic amaranth vegetable genotypes. In an investigation [78] reported that 39 vegetable amaranth lines were studied for 16 nutritional and geo-morphological traits and first 4 PCs were responsible for 68% of the dissimilarity in vegetable amaranth.

There biplot is another scheme for the evaluation of genotypes for relationship among different traits. It is used extensively in many different crops for graphical clustering of genotypes in different crops [42, 79] reported that center of biplot is not significant and traits fall in the central area of biplot have little influence on variations. The factors that are away from the center have greater impact on the overall variability of genotypes. The size of vector loadings determines the length of the vector lines and the area in which they fall on a biplot. Moreover, the variable PCA biplot is a perfect tool that indicate the strength of each trait and the connection between the variables on PCs. The general rule is the measurement of cosine angle between a vector and axis of a PC. The significance is calculated measuring this angle. The smaller angle between the vectors means a better interrelationship between the variables. The central point in a PCA biplot indicate the mean value of all the traits [72] and correlation among different traits of diverse genotypes corresponds to the angle between them. The value of cosine for the angle of 00 is 1 and the value of cosine 1800 is -1. The value of cosine 900 is 0. It means if the angle between two vectors is close to 00 or 1800 there is strong positive or negative correlation between them. There is no correlation or zero relationship between the vectors which make an angle of 900 [78, 80, 81]. broadly inspected the vegetable amaranth for their nutritive and geomorphologic traits in vegetable amaranth but this is the first investigation that used exotic amaranth genotypes to study variations on the basis of agronomic, physiological and nutritional traits. In present investigation there was positive correlation among most of the traits under investigation. Positive correlation means efforts made for the improvements of only one trait may improve the other characteristics in exotic amaranth genotypes. The results indicated from the biplot loadings showed significant positive correlation among many traits as seen in CARBS and E; fiber and Biomass; nitrogen and protein. As carbohydrates are rich source of energy, fiber contents are directly associated to biomass and protein content is a function of nitrogen present in leaves. These results were already predicted. Amaranth is known for its higher protein content and lower fat content. In PCA biplot negative correlation can be seen between protein and fats and between moisture and dry weight content. The results from the findings of [75, 82–84] in amaranth genotypes are comparable with the findings of this investigation.

Clustering the genotypes

Cluster analysis or clustering of genotypes on the basis of traits is one of the best methods to evaluate the genotypes for the level of correlation among different genotypes. Results from the investigation indicated that five clusters were created from 50 exotic amaranth genotypes. Clusters were created on the basis of performance of different traits under study. Genetic diversity can be used efficiently for hybridization and genotypes of desired characters from large number of clusters should be included in yield enrichment program. Exotic amaranth genotypes were clustered more or less according to the species in most cases. Some of the genotypes made their own cluster within a species as it can be observed in a cluster of genotypes 40, 41, 42 and 43 which are part of 16 genotypes of A. tricolor used in this study. It can be explained that some traits are bound to specific genotypes within a species [85]. stated genotypes of the same species when fall in different clusters indicate the broad genetic spectrum of that species. The obtained results were in agreement with the findings those of [75] [86]. reported the same results in Safflower and [87] agreed to the findings in Guizotia abyssinica.

Performance of genotypes under different clusters

Clusters II and cluster III were dominated by agronomic and yield producing physiological and macro nutrients traits. Plant height was maximum in cluster III but surprisingly biomass, dry weight and number of leaves per plant was highest in cluster II. This can be explained by number of plants were lower in plots where plant height was maximum. The plants with maximum values of physiological traits were grouped in cluster II. All the physiological parameters like transpiration rate, net photosynthesis and stomatal conductance were grouped in cluster II. The mean values of different characters are calculated at the end of all traits. The value of best performing traits was greater than the grand mean of respective trait. Highest values in terms of fiber, ash, protein, nitrogen and lowest fats content were grouped in cluster III. Energy is directly associated with the amount of carbohydrate contents so both these parameters yielded highest value and were grouped in cluster II. Micro nutrients like Ca, Zn, Mg and K were highest in genotypes that were grouped in cluster V. P and Fe were highest in cluster III. Moreover, results indicated that almost all the important traits were confined in cluster II, III and V. No parameter has above average value in cluster I and cluster IV had only moisture content with highest value. High cluster means for respective traits are powerful tools for starting a hybridization program. The cluster means are based on diversity in exotic amaranth genotypes and should be used according to the desired results [88]. Additionally, the clusters mean could be used to produce high yielding lines with desirable traits [89].

Genetic divergence

The “clv” statistic in R is a suitable software package for the evaluation of variation and genetic diversity among genotypes. Results revealed that the distance within clusters is smaller than distance across the clusters. This can be explained because genotypes grouped together in a cluster have little genetic variations with similarity in performance for different traits. Findings of this research are in agreement with [90] and [91] who reported that intra cluster distance in amaranth genotypes was much smaller than inter cluster distance. Intra cluster distance in cluster IV is 4.67 and in cluster II the intra cluster distance was recorded as 6.83 which are smallest and largest values respectively. It was noted that cluster IV contains only four genotypes and represent smallest cluster and cluster II have 16 genotypes and is the largest cluster. The smallest intra cluster distance in cluster IV can be explained because all four genotypes are the sub group of A. tricolor. Cluster V have distance of 4.83 which is second smallest cluster distance. All the cluster compared and result showed that the genotypes in clusters IV and V does have lowest distance [92] observed that transgressive segregation is a response of cluster distance. Higher the distance, higher will be transgressive segregation in the genotypes of two different clusters. According to [93] crossing between these genotypes is not desirable because of very low heterotic value in F1 generation. It would also restrict variability in F2 population. Distance analysis provide ample data so that parents with suitable genetic variability are selected for breeding program. Results suggested that Clusters I and II, cluster I and III and II and IV had above average cluster distance. Genetically diverse parents can be selected for producing new lines. This will not only help in achieving heterotic segregates but also a broad base of variability can be achieved in next generations. The results from the investigation studies of [94] and [95] suggest that parents genotypes should be selected from the larger inter cluster distance because larger inter-cluster distance is responsible for major variations in genotypes. Thus, desirable varieties can be developed using hybridization techniques. Large inter cluster distance have genotypes with significant genetic variations. From genetic variations of larger inter cluster distance genotypes, desirable characters can be introduced in new lines. Hybridization program should be established keeping in mind the intended use of crop [96]. also suggested that parents from larger inter cluster distance should be used for hybridization program. The desirable traits from each cluster and the performance of individual genotypes within each cluster should be the driving force for the selection of parent plants [97]. believed that parent plants taken from the larger inter cluster distance should be used for crossing. High level of genetic diversity and greater genetic recombination would be achieved in next generations.

Genetic divergence and contribution of different traits

[98] studied the importance of variables and their contribution in divergence studies. 19 out of total 22 traits were scrutinized for their importance to genetic divergence. Carbohydrates, protein and energy were removed from this analysis because these three variables were product from other variables. Agronomic traits like fiber (11.5%), Biomass (9.9), Moisture (9.5%) Dry weight (8.3%), macro molecule like Fats (6.9%), Nitrogen (5.2%), micro nutrients like Zinc (6.1%) and Fe (5.9%) contributed maximum to variations. The above-mentioned traits should be considered for selection of exotic amaranth genotypes from clusters because maximum variation among clusters is due to these parameters. Characters other than these contributed very little to divergence.

The exotic Amaranthus genotypes investigated for diversity in different ways. According to [99, 100] reported that not only genetic but environmental factors are also responsible for genetic variability in amaranthus plant. This study gives enough variability data for 50 amaranth genotypes but for precision analysis, molecular markers should be used to calculate the exact level of genetic diversity in different exotic amaranth genotypes. The current characterization on the basis of different traits will be helpful for the utilization of exotic amaranth genotypes to produce new cultivars for the soil of Pakistan. Further molecular investigation is required about the subject.

Conclusion

Conditions of soil, environmental factors, cultural practices along with genetic variations may aid in explaining this research for the expression of variations in 50 exotic vegetable amaranth genotypes. Five principal components explained 84.59% of variations but first two components described the 57.31% variations. The loadings of eigenvectors of variables like moisture (-0.892), protein (0.692), fat (-0.571), carbohydrates (0.938), energy (0.920), stomatal conductance (0.803), net photosynthesis (0.795), nitrogen (0.692), ,calcium (0.545), phosphorus (0.908), zinc (0.960), iron (0.826), potassium (0.872) and dry weight (0.770) in first two components explained majority of variations in exotic vegetable amaranth genotypes. These traits are remarkable choice for future breeding plans for the improvement and adaptation of exotic vegetable amaranth genotypes in Pakistan. Biplot studies revealed that exotic amaranth genotypes and related traits were split into two basic components axes. 50 exotic vegetable amaranth genotypes were grouped into five clusters on the basis of mean performance of traits under investigation. Cluster means revealed that maximum height was gained by the genotypes of cluster III (77.95 cm) but maximum biomass was produced by the genotypes of II cluster (16081.35 kg/ha). The genotypes in II and III cluster had maximum mean values for the most of desirable traits (Table 4) which include protein (5.55 g/100 g FW), fiber (3.80 g/100 g FW), ash (3.84 g/100 g FW), carbohydrate (13.50 g/100 g FW), energy (331.99 kJ/100 g FW), transpiration rate (0.57 mmol H2O m− 2 s− 1), stomatal conductance (0.54 mmol H2O m− 2 s− 1), net photosynthesis (28.33 µmol CO2 /m-2 s-1), nitrogen (887.54 mg/100 g FW) and iron (26.09 mg/100 g DW). The mean value for some of the micronutrient related traits was maximum in cluster IV (Table 4) which include calcium (1001.54 mg/100 g DW), phosphorus (437.00 mg/100 g DW), zinc (16.52 mg/100 g DW), magnesium (770.50 mg/100 g DW) and potassium (1325.79 mg/100 g DW). The selection of genotypes from these clusters can help in the improvements and development of amaranth crop in Pakistan. The results from the genetic divergence suggested that variations among the genotypes of different clusters been there. Intra cluster distance was less than inter cluster distance. The maximum inter cluster distance was observed between cluster I and II; cluster I and III and between cluster II and IV. The distribution of different traits among the exotic vegetable amaranth genotypes were revealed in this investigation. These results might help in achieving the goals for the production of new lines of amaranth that will adapt to the soils and environmental conditions of Pakistan. To corroborate the findings of this investigation further studies are required including the use of molecular markers.

Another generalization of Hadamard test: Optimal sample complexities for learning functions on the unitary group
Daiki Suruga

Abstract
Estimating properties of unknown unitary operations is a fundamental task in quantum information science. While full unitary tomography requires a number of samples to the unknown unitary scaling linearly with the dimension (implying exponentially with the number of qubits), estimating specific functions of a unitary can be significantly more efficient. In this paper, we present a unified framework for the sample-efficient estimation of arbitrary square integrable functions f : 𝖴 ⁡ ( d ) → ℂ f:{\mathsf{U}(d)}\to\mathbb{C} , using only access to the controlled-unitary operation. We first provide a tight characterization of the optimal sample complexity when the accuracy is measured by the averaged bias over the unitary 𝖴 ⁡ ( d ) {\mathsf{U}(d)} . We then construct a sample-efficient estimation algorithm that becomes optimal under the Probably Approximately Correct (PAC) learning criterion for various classes of functions. Applications include optimal estimation of matrix elements of irreducible representations, the trace, determinant, and general polynomial functions on 𝖴 ⁡ ( d ) {\mathsf{U}(d)} . Our technique generalize the Hadamard test and leverage tools from representation theory, yielding both lower and upper bound on sample complexity.

Introduction

In quantum mechanics, any time evolution of a closed system is described by a unitary operator.
As a result, many tasks in quantum information science involve estimating characteristics of a given, yet unknown unitary process.
Examples of such tasks include the validation and certification of quantum circuits, error mitigation and correction in quantum processes, and the identification of hidden or unknown dynamics in black-box quantum systems.
For such tasks, one needs to accurately estimate some characteristics of the unitaries, such as deviation from the ideal process (i.e., noise), eigenvalues and eigenvectors, or even the complete description of the unitary operator.
With the rapid theoretical and experimental advancement of quantum information technologies, developing efficient methods for estimating the properties of unknown unitary operations has become increasingly essential, and has therefore received much attention.

A standard approach to estimating the characteristics of a given unitary operator is to reconstruct the entire unitary process—that is, to estimate all matrix elements of the unitary operator (sometimes up to a global phase).
As a full characterization of a given unitary process is a fundamental task in quantum information science, numerous studies have addressed the full unitary tomography problem.
See, e.g.,
[

In particular, Ref.

This implies that the number of required samples scales exponentially with the number of qubits.

On the other hand, many other studies focus on estimating a specific characteristic of a black-box unitary
U

As in this example, it is often the case that one provably reduces the number of samples significantly compared to the full unitary tomography, when focusing on a specific characteristic.
Because of its provable efficiency, there are many works that focus on estimating a specific characteristic, including

Our contributions

The main purpose of this paper is to provide a unified estimation framework that allows for the construction of sample-efficient algorithms for estimating

1.2
Proof techniques

We now briefly explain how to prove the lower bounds and the upper bounds in
1
and
1
respectively.

Lower bounds.

To show the lower bound, let
\mathcal{A}
be a query algorithm that uses
m
queries for estimating a function
f
with
\mathbf{Bias}_{G}<\varepsilon
.
First, we observe that the expectation
\mathbf{E}[\mathcal{A}|g]
of the estimates of
\mathcal{A}
, when the unknown unitary is
g\in{\mathsf{U}(d)}
, is a polynomial as the matrix elements of
g
and
g^{*}
with degree
\leq 2m
.
Moreover, from the constraint on the bias:
\mathbf{Bias}_{G}<\varepsilon
, it follows that the
L^{2}
distance
\|\mathbf{E}[{\mathcal{A}}|g]-f(g)\|_{L^{2}}
is small. This means the expectation
\mathbf{E}[\mathcal{A}|g]
approximates the function
f
well.
However several representation theory techniques tell the function is decomposed by

f=\mathsf{poly}_{\leq 2m}f\oplus\mathsf{poly}_{\leq 2m}^{\perp}f\text{~satisfying~}\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}}=\|\mathsf{poly}_{\leq 2m}f\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|\mathsf{poly}_{\leq 2m}^{\perp}f\|^{2}_{L^{2}},

where
\mathsf{poly}_{\leq 2m}f
represents the polynomial with degree
\leq m
that approximates the original function
f
best, and
\mathsf{poly}_{\leq 2m}^{\perp}f
is the rest.
Therefore if
\|\mathsf{poly}_{\leq 2m}^{\perp}f\|_{L^{2}}
is large, the expectation
\mathbf{E}[\mathcal{A}|g]
cannot approximate the original
f
well.
This implies that the number
m
of queries has to be large enough so that the quantity
\|\mathsf{poly}_{\leq 2m}^{\perp}f\|_{L^{2}}
becomes sufficiently small, yielding a query lower bound.

Similar methods sometimes appear in the literature, e.g., References
[
BBC+01
,
SY23
]
.

Upper bounds.

To construct the query algorithm that attains our upper bounds, we generalize the Hadamard test and obtain an unbiased estimator for any polynomial
f(g)
.
The standard Hadamard test provides an unbiased estimator for
\operatorname{Re}\Tr\rho U
and
\operatorname{Im}\Tr\rho U
, thus also for
\Tr\rho U
with one query access to C-
U
for any state
\rho

Generalizing this, we first develop an algorithm

that provides an unbiased estimator for the inner product

\langle\varphi|\left(\bigoplus_{0\leq n,n^{\prime}\leq m}g^{\otimes n}\otimes g^{\ast\otimes n^{\prime}}\otimes I_{E}\right)|{\psi}\rangle

, with
2m
query access to C-
g
operation in
fig.

1

We then observe that any polynomial function

with degree

f(g)=\Tr A_{f}\left(\bigoplus_{0\leq n,n^{\prime}\leq m}g^{\otimes n}\otimes g^{\ast\otimes n^{\prime}}\otimes I_{E}\right)

for some matrix

The singular value decomposition on the matrix

\text{singular values of $A$}\end{subarray}}\sigma_{i}\langle\varphi_{i}|\left(\bigoplus_{0\leq n,n^{\prime}\leq m}g^{\otimes n}\otimes g^{\ast\otimes n^{\prime}}\otimes I_{E}\right)|\psi_{i}\rangle,

Algorithms and Their Complexity

As we deal with lower bounds on sample complexities, we next rigorously define the computational model considered throughout this paper in the below.

Definition 1
.

An
m

(\varepsilon,\delta)
-PAC-learning framework, it suffices to repeat the estimation process for
n=O\left(\frac{(b-a)^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\log\frac{1}{\delta}\right)
times and take the average.

For
\mathbb{C}
-valued random variables, we decompose
X_{i}=\operatorname{Re}X_{i}+i\operatorname{Im}X_{i}
and obtain

\displaystyle\mathrm{Pr}\left(|\overline{\operatorname{Re}X}+i\overline{\operatorname{Im}X}|>\varepsilon\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{Pr}\left(|\overline{\operatorname{Re}X}|+|\overline{\operatorname{Im}X}|>\varepsilon\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{Pr}\left(|\overline{\operatorname{Re}X}|>\varepsilon/2\right)+\mathrm{Pr}\left(|\overline{\operatorname{Im}X}|>\varepsilon/2\right)

\displaystyle\leq 4\exp\left(\frac{-\varepsilon^{2}n}{8C_{0}^{2}}\right)

from the same argument with Hoeffding’s inequality,
where
C_{0}
is the maximum possible values of
|X_{i}|
.
Therefore, it suffices to repeat the estimation process for
n=O\left(\frac{C_{0}^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\log\frac{1}{\delta}\right)
times and take the average.

We now apply this argument to our estimation scheme that is essentially given in the proof of
5
.
In our estimation scheme, first observe the function
f
may be expressed as

f(g)=\|A\|_{1}\cdot\sum_{i}\frac{\sigma_{i}}{\|A\|_{1}}\langle{e_{i}}|V\left(\bigoplus_{0\leq n,n^{\prime}\leq m}g^{\otimes n}\otimes g^{\ast\otimes n^{\prime}}\otimes I_{E}\right)U|e_{i}\rangle,

where the singular value decomposition
A=UDV
is applied.
And then as in the second part of
5
, apply
\mathsf{Estimation}(V^{\ast}e_{i},Ue_{i})
with probability
\frac{\sigma_{i}}{\|A\|_{1}}
(Note that
\sum_{i}\frac{\sigma_{i}}{\|A\|_{1}}=1
) and multiply by
\|A\|_{1}
.
This process requires
m
queries to the
\textrm{C-}g
operation, and yields an unbiased estimator for
f(g)
that takes values on the interval
[-\|A\|_{1},\|A\|_{1}]
, because

|\langle{e_{i}}|V|e_{i}\rangle|\leq\|e_{i}\|_{2}\|Ve_{i}\|_{2}=1

for any unitary operator
V
by Cauchy–Schwarz inequality.

Therefore from the above discussion we obtain

Q_{\varepsilon,\delta}(f)=O\left(\frac{\|A\|_{1}^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\log\frac{1}{\delta}\cdot m\right)
.
∎

5.1
Simpler forms of
\mathsf{Rep}_{\varepsilon}(f)

Here we aim to derive simpler forms of the quantity
\mathsf{Rep}_{\varepsilon}(f)
for specific functions such as univariate polynomials, the trace function, and matrix elements of unitary irreducible representations, on
{\mathsf{U}(d)}

5.1.1
Univariate Polynomials

Define

G(\alpha,d):=\int|g_{11}|^{2\alpha}dg

which can be computed by properties of the Haar measure on the unitary group
[
Mec19
, Proposition 2.5]
. For example,

G(\alpha=1,d)=\int|g_{11}|^{2}dg=\frac{1}{d},

since
g_{11},\ldots,g_{1d}
are i.i.d., and
\sum_{1\leq i\leq d}|g_{1i}|^{2}=1
.

Proposition 6
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
f(g)=g_{11}^{\alpha}
.
Then

\mathsf{Rep}_{\varepsilon}(f)=\begin{cases}\lfloor\frac{\alpha-1}{2}\rfloor&\text{if $\varepsilon<G(\alpha,d)$,}\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Proof.

By defition of
G(\alpha,d)
, we have

G(\alpha,d)=\|Q^{\perp}_{\leq(\alpha-1)}f\|^{2}_{L^{2}}>\|Q^{\perp}_{\leq\alpha}f\|^{2}_{L^{2}}=0,

together with the fact that
f
is an
\alpha
-degree polynomial and the definition of the projection
Q^{\perp}_{\leq\alpha}
.
Since

2\left\lfloor\frac{d-1}{2}\right\rfloor<d\leq 2\left(\left\lfloor\frac{d-1}{2}\right\rfloor+1\right)

for any
d\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
this shows the statement.

∎

As shown in
1
, for a different
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, the function
g^{\alpha}_{11}
belongs to a different orthogonal subspace
\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}({\mathsf{U}(d)})
. Therefore the
L^{2}
norm of a univariate polynomial
f=\sum_{0\leq\alpha^{\prime}\leq\alpha}a_{\alpha^{\prime}}g_{11}^{\alpha^{\prime}}
satisfies

\|f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\sum_{0\leq\alpha^{\prime}\leq\alpha}|a_{\alpha^{\prime}}|^{2}\|g_{11}^{\alpha^{\prime}}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}=\sum_{0\leq\alpha^{\prime}\leq\alpha}|a_{\alpha^{\prime}}|^{2}G(\alpha^{\prime},d).

This yields
1
.

Corollary 1
.

For a degree
\alpha
, univariate polynomial
f(g)=\sum_{0\leq\alpha^{\prime}\leq\alpha}a_{\alpha^{\prime}}g_{11}^{\alpha^{\prime}}
,

\mathsf{Rep}_{\varepsilon}(f)=\max\left\{m\mid\sum_{\alpha^{\prime}>2m}|a_{\alpha^{\prime}}|^{2}G(\alpha^{\prime},d)\geq\varepsilon\right\}.

When

\mathsf{Rep}_{\varepsilon}(f)=0

5.1.3
The determinant
\textrm{det}~g

By Shur’s orthogonality relation
[
BTD03
, Section 4]
, the one dimensional irreducible representation
(\textrm{det}g,\mathbb{C})
satisfies
\|\det g\|^{2}_{L^{2}}=1
.
Since
\det g

.

Fact 8
.

When
f(g)=\det~g
,
\mathsf{Rep}_{\varepsilon}(f)=\lfloor\frac{d-1}{2}\rfloor
.

More generally, any irreducible representation

\pi_{\lambda}(g)_{i,j}
is a linear combination of polynomials with degree exactly equal to

.
These arguments show

.

Fact 9
.

For any unitary irreducible representation
(\pi_{\lambda},V_{\lambda})
whose label
\lambda
belongs to a set
\Lambda_{d}(m,\bar{m})
,

\mathsf{Rep}_{\varepsilon}(f)=\begin{cases}\left\lfloor\frac{m+\bar{m}-1}{2}\right\rfloor&\varepsilon<1/\textrm{dim}V_{\lambda},\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

5.2.1
Univariate Polynomials

Proposition 7
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
f(g)=g_{11}^{\alpha}
.
Then
Q_{\varepsilon,\delta}(f)=O(\frac{\alpha}{\varepsilon^{2}}\log\frac{1}{\delta})

Proof.

First notice that there is a pair
(i,j)
such that the
(i,j)

\bigoplus_{0\leq n,n^{\prime}\leq\alpha}g^{\otimes n}\otimes g^{\ast\otimes n^{\prime}}

We now apply
1

and the matrix

, whose

We then have

g^{\alpha}_{11}=\Tr A\left(\bigoplus_{0\leq n,n^{\prime}\leq m}g^{\otimes n}\otimes g^{\ast\otimes n^{\prime}}\otimes I_{E}\right).

Since
g^{\alpha}_{11}\in\mathcal{Q}_{\leq\alpha}({\mathsf{U}(d)})
and
\|A\|_{1}=\Tr\sqrt{E_{ij}^{\ast}E_{ij}}=1
, we have
Q_{\varepsilon,\delta}(f)=O(\frac{\alpha}{\varepsilon^{2}}\log\frac{1}{\delta})
.
∎

5.2.2
The trace function

Fact 10
.

When
f(g)=\frac{1}{d}\Tr g
,
Q_{\varepsilon,\delta}(f)=O(\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\log\frac{1}{\delta})
.

Proof.

First observe that
\bigoplus_{0\leq n,n^{\prime}\leq 1}g^{\otimes n}\otimes g^{\ast\otimes n^{\prime}}=g\otimes I\oplus I\otimes g^{\ast}\oplus g\otimes g^{\ast}
.
Therefore define
A:=\frac{1}{d}(I\otimes E_{11})\oplus 0\oplus 0
and we obtain

\Tr A\bigoplus_{0\leq n,n^{\prime}\leq 1}g^{\otimes n}\otimes g^{\ast\otimes n^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{d}\Tr g\otimes E_{11}=\frac{1}{d}\Tr g.

Since
\|A\|_{1}=1
, we obtain the desired statement.
∎

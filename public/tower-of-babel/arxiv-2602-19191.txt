An attractive analytic solution of the Maxwell’s equation
Xiaorong Zou

Abstract
. In this paper, we provide an attractive analytic solution for the Maxwell’s equation for a given set of smooth periodic functions as initial condition with demonstrative examples. The complexity of the solution is comparable to the Fourier expansions of the initial functions.



1.
Introduction

The Maxwell’s equations form the foundation of classical electromagnetism and play a critical role in a wide variety of applications in science and engineering such as wireless engineering, electric motors, microwave circuits, waveguides etc. In this paper, we aim to provide an attractive analytic solution of the standard Maxwell’s equations in an isotropic, homogeneous, and lossless medium as follows
[
15
]

(1.1a)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial{\bf H}}{\partial t}=-\frac{1}{\mu}{\bf curl}\ {\bf E},\ \ \ \frac{\partial{\bf E}}{\partial t}=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}{\bf curl}\ {\bf H},\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\ \ \textbf{curl-equations}

(1.1b)

\displaystyle{\bf H}_{0}(x,y,z)={\bf H}(x,y,z,0),\qquad{{\bf E}_{0}(x,y,z)={\bf E}(x,y,z,0),}\ \ \textbf{initial conditions}

where
\textbf{H}=(H_{x},H_{y},H_{z})^{\intercal}:\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{3}
stands for
magnetic field intensity,

\textbf{E}=(E_{x},E_{y},E_{z})^{\intercal}:\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{3}
represents electric field intensity,
{\bf H}_{0}(x,y,z),{\bf E}_{0}(x,y,z)
represents the initial condition of the system, and
\mu
and
\varepsilon
are constant in this paper and denote the magnetic permeability and electric permittivity, respectively.
It is well known that Maxwell’s equations (
1.1
) have unique smooth solutions for all time
if the initial data (
1.1b
) are suitably smooth
[
15
]
.

The solutions of Maxwell’s equations
(
1.1
) exhibit quite a few physical invariants: energy
conservation laws, symplectic conservation laws, helicity
conservation laws, momentum conservation laws and divergence-free
fields. These invariants are very important in the long time
propagation of the electromagnetic waves
[

]
. In
[
27
]
, an efficient fully discrete scheme is proposed to solve the Maxwell’s equations. Combining time exponential integrator and Fourier pseudospectral methods, the scheme achieves decent spectral accuracy and is computationally effective by leveraging Fast Fourier Transform. More importantly, it keeps the desired conservation laws in discretised states.

Considering the importance of Maxwell’s equation, an attractive analytic representation of its solution would be greatly desirable if it could be achieved. It turns out that this goal is achievable by combing the magnetic field intensity
H
and the electric field intensity
E
into a single complex vector field. The solution space can be decomposed into the sum of some subspaces that are generated by simple trigonometrical base functions. Restricted to each of those subspaces, the underlying
{\bf curl}
operator is anti-symmetric and the related solutions bear close analytic form, which leads to an attractive analytic decomposition of a general solution whose complexity is comparable to its initial function.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. Section
2
is used to develop the framework. We first convert Maxwell Equation
1.1
into an equivalent complex form and show the associated differential operator
{\bf curl}
is anti-symmetric under the subspaces that are generated by a single trigonometric base function. We then study the properties of
{\bf curl}
and decompose the vectors in the subspace as the sum of eigenvectors of
{\bf curl}
. The desired analytic expression can be derived once close form solution is available under each of the subspaces. In Section
3
, two demonstrative examples are provided to show how to apply the proposed close form formula to construct solution with a given initial condition.

2.
Analytic framework

Assume that
({\bf H}_{0},{\bf E}_{0})
are two smooth periodic initial function. The two curl-equations in Eq (

(2.1)

\begin{pmatrix}\sqrt{\mu}{\bf H}(t)\\
\sqrt{\varepsilon}{\bf E}(t)\end{pmatrix}=e^{t\mathcal{C}}\begin{pmatrix}\sqrt{\mu}{\bf H}_{0}\\
\sqrt{\varepsilon}{\bf E}_{0}\end{pmatrix}.

where

\mathcal{C}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathbf{0}&-\frac{{\bf curl}}{\sqrt{\mu\varepsilon}}\\
\frac{{\bf curl}}{\sqrt{\mu\varepsilon}}&\mathbf{0}\\
\end{array}\right)=:\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathbf{0}&-A\\
A&\mathbf{0}\\
\end{array}\right)

is the Maxwell operator
[

{\bf F}=\sqrt{\mu}{\bf H}+\sqrt{\epsilon}{\bf E}\cdot i

iA{\bf F}=\frac{i}{\sqrt{\mu\epsilon}}{\bf curl}(\sqrt{\mu}{\bf H}+\sqrt{\epsilon}{\bf E}\cdot i)=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu}}{\bf curl}{\bf E}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\epsilon}}{\bf curl}{\bf H}\cdot i,

which implies

Lemma 2.1
.

Eq
1.1

(2.2)

\frac{\partial{\bf F}}{\partial t}=iA{\bf F},\qquad{\bf F}(x,y,z,0)=\sqrt{\mu}{\bf H}_{0}+\sqrt{\epsilon}{\bf E}_{0}\cdot i

2.3.
Main Theorem

We are ready to show the analytic representation of the solutions of Maxwell’s equations as follows.

Theorem 2.4
.

Lie superalgebras generated by reflections in Weyl groups of classical type Thanks: CMD was supported by a Summer Research Grant from the DePaul University College of Science and Health. Thanks: JRK was supported in part by Simons Collaboration Grant for Mathematicians No. 525043 and No. 963912.
Christopher M. Drupieski, Jonathan R. Kujawa

Abstract
. We consider the finite Weyl groups of classical type — W ⁡ ( A r ) W(A_{r}) for r ≥ 1 r\geq 1 , W ⁡ ( B r ) = W ⁡ ( C r ) W(B_{r})=W(C_{r}) for r ≥ 2 r\geq 2 , and W ⁡ ( D r ) W(D_{r}) for r ≥ 4 r\geq 4 — as supergroups in which the reflections are of odd superdegree. Viewing the corresponding complex group algebras as Lie superalgebras via the graded commutator bracket, we determine the structure of the Lie sub-superalgebras generated by the sets of reflections. In each case, this Lie superalgebra is equal to the full derived subalgebra of the group algebra plus the span of the class sums of the reflections.

Primary 17B10. Secondary 20B30.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Overview

Let
G
a finite reflection group. By declaring the reflections in
G
to be of odd superdegree, one endows
G
with the structure of a supergroup, i.e., a group equipped with a multiplicative grading by
\mathbb{Z}_{2}=\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
. In turn, the complex group algebra
\mathbb{C}G
is endowed with the structure of an associative superalgebra, and then the graded commutator bracket

[x,y]=xy-(-1)^{\overline{x}\cdot\overline{y}}yx

defines a Lie superalgebra structure on
\mathbb{C}G
, which we denote
\Lie(\mathbb{C}G)
. It is now natural to ask for an explicit description of the Lie superalgebra structure on
\Lie(\mathbb{C}G)
, and for the structure of the (in general, proper) Lie sub-superalgebra
\mathfrak{g}
generated by the set of reflections in
G
.

The ungraded (non-super) analogues of the preceding questions were considered by Marin
[
12
]
for the case
G=\mathfrak{S}_{n}
of the symmetric group on
n
letters. In that paper and its sequels, Marin and others used the structure of the Lie algebra generated by the set of transpositions to obtain new results on the representation theory of braid groups and Iwahori–Hecke algebras in type A. More recently, the authors of this paper determined the structure of the Lie sub-superalgebra of
\Lie(\mathbb{C}\mathfrak{S}_{n})
generated by the set of transpositions
[
4
]
. When the authors have given talks on that work, the first question from the audience has invariably been, “What about Weyl groups of other types?” This paper and its companion
[
3
]
answer that question for Weyl groups of types BC and D, in the super and non-super settings, respectively. In particular, the current paper provides a new, vastly streamlined, more conceptual approach to the Lie superalgebra calculations carried out in
[
4
]
: while some modest case-by-case verifications are still required for each Lie type, the overall approach is uniform across types, and many tedious calculations are now avoided.

1.2.
Main results

Let
G
be a finite reflection group as above. The simple
\mathbb{C}G
-supermodules exist in two flavors: a simple
\mathbb{C}G
-supermodule
W
is of type M if
W
is simple as an ungraded
\mathbb{C}G
-module, and is of type Q if
W
is reducible as an ungraded
\mathbb{C}G
-module. Let
\Irr_{M}(G)
and
\Irr_{Q}(G)
be complete sets (up to homogeneous isomorphism) of the simple
\mathbb{C}G
-supermodules of types M and Q, respectively. Then the graded Artin–Wedderburn isomorphism (see Corollary
2.1.5
) restricts to a Lie superalgebra isomorphism

(1.2.1)

\Lie(\mathbb{C}G)\cong\Big[\bigoplus_{W\in\Irr_{Q}(G)}\mathfrak{q}(W)\Big]\oplus\Big[\bigoplus_{W\in\Irr_{M}(G)}\mathfrak{gl}(W)\Big];

see Section
2.2
for an explanation of the notation
\mathfrak{q}(W)
and
\mathfrak{gl}(W)
. The problem of describing the Lie superalgebra
\Lie(\mathbb{C}G)
thus reduces to the problem of describing the simple
\mathbb{C}G
-supermodules of types M and Q. If
G
is a Weyl group of classical type, the simple
\mathbb{C}G
-supermodules can all be described in terms of (bi-)tableaux combinatorics. This is summarized for type BC in Section
3.4
, for type A in Section
4
, and for type D in Section
5.4
.

The problem of describing the Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{g}\subseteq\Lie(\mathbb{C}G)
generated by the set of reflections in
G
is rather more subtle, though for Weyl groups of classical type, the overall description is uniform. Let
\mathfrak{D}(\mathbb{C}G)=\mathfrak{D}(\Lie(\mathbb{C}G))
denote the derived Lie sub-superalgebra of
\Lie(\mathbb{C}G)
. Say that a reflection
s
in
G
is
long
(resp.
short
) according to the length of the corresponding root (i.e., the root, unique up to the scalar
\pm 1
, determining the hyperplane through which
s
is a reflection). If
G
is a Weyl group of simply-laced type (ADE), then all reflections are long. Let
C_{\ell}
and
C_{s}
be the sets of all long and short roots in
G
, respectively. Then
C_{\ell}
is a conjugacy class in
G
, as is
C_{s}
if
C_{s}
is nonempty. Let
\mathcal{C}_{\ell}
and
\mathcal{C}_{s}
be the corresponding class sums in
\mathbb{C}G
, where by definition
\mathcal{C}_{s}
is taken to be zero if
C_{s}
is empty. We prove in Theorems
3.5.1
,
4.1.1
, and
5.5.1
that

(1.2.2)

\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{D}(\mathbb{C}G)+\mathbb{C}\cdot\mathcal{C}_{\ell}+\mathbb{C}\cdot\mathcal{C}_{s}.

Coupled with (
1.2.1
) and the description of derived subalgebras in Section
2.2
, this gives a complete description of
\mathfrak{g}
. In particular, it shows that the Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{g}
is equal to most of
\Lie(\mathbb{C}G)
. This is in contrast to the non-graded (ordinary Lie algebra) situation investigated in
[
12
,
3
]
, where the analogous Lie algebras are roughly half the dimension of
\Lie(\mathbb{C}G)
, illustrating the divergence between the graded and non-graded settings.

The overall strategy for proving (
1.2.2
) is by induction on the rank
n
of the Weyl group
G=G_{n}
. An important role in the argument is played by the even subgroup
G_{\overline{0}}\subseteq G
, defined to be the kernel of the linear character
\kappa:G\to\left\{\pm 1\right\}
that evaluates to
-1
on all reflections in
G
, and by the underlying even Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}=\mathfrak{g}\cap\mathbb{C}G_{\overline{0}}
. Under some mild general hypotheses, the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}
is seen to be reductive (Proposition
2.3.5
), and hence its derived subalgebra
\mathfrak{D}(\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}})
is a semisimple complex Lie algebra. Further, if
\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}
contains an appropriate set of group generators for
G_{\overline{0}}
, then
\mathfrak{D}(\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}})
is able to detect isomorphisms among (most) simple
\mathbb{C}G_{\overline{0}}
-modules (Proposition
2.3.7
) and among (most) simple
\mathbb{C}G
-supermodules (Corollary
2.3.8
). The key step in the induction argument is then carried out through an analysis of the branching rule for the restriction of simple modules from
(G_{n})_{\overline{0}}
to
(G_{n-1})_{\overline{0}}
. Using the dimensional rigidity of the simple complex Lie algebras, one shows for each simple
\mathbb{C}G_{\overline{0}}
-module
V
that the image of
\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}
in
\End(V)
is all of
\mathfrak{sl}(V)
. From this one deduces for each simple
\mathbb{C}G
-supermodule
W
that, modulo the operators
\mathcal{C}_{\ell}
and
\mathcal{C}_{s}
, the image of
\mathfrak{g}
in
\End(W)
is the full derived algebra of either
\mathfrak{q}(W)
or
\mathfrak{gl}(W)
(Lemma
2.4.3
). Finally, one applies the graded Artin–Wedderburn isomorphism and the semisimplicity of
\mathfrak{D}(\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}})
to deduce that
\mathfrak{g}
contains all of
\mathfrak{D}(\mathbb{C}G)
.

1.3.
Outline of the paper

In Section
2
we establish necessary results on the representation theory of finite supergroups and their associated Lie superalgebras. In particular, in Sections
2.3
and
2.4
we identify an axiomatic framework under which the inductive strategy described above can be carried out. Sections
3
,
4
, and
5
are then devoted to verifying those axioms for the Weyl groups of classical types BC, A, and D, respectively. In those sections we record the classification of the simple (super)modules, the branching rules for the even subgroups, and other useful facts that may also be of independent interest for the study of the (graded) representation theory of the classical Weyl groups. The last section of the paper, Section
5.6
, is devoted to verifying one piece of the axiomatic framework—axiom
(A5)
—for the Weyl group
\mathcal{D}_{n}
of type D. This is the most difficult of the classical types to handle, on account of the fact that the branching rule from
(\mathcal{D}_{n})_{\overline{0}}
to
(\mathcal{D}_{n-1})_{\overline{0}}
is not in general multiplicity free. Interestingly, the case in this paper that requires the most special treatment (handled in Section
5.6.3
) arises from the same simple module that required special treatment in our companion Lie algebra paper (see
[
3
, §5.7]
), though the methods applied to resolve that case in each paper are quite different.

1.4.
Further directions

For the sake of completeness, it would be interesting to determine if (
1.2.2
) can be extended to Weyl groups of all exceptional types. The groups
W(E_{6})
,
W(E_{7})
, and
W(E_{8})
are currently out of reach by direct calculation (at least to the authors) and some theory will be needed, but the conceptual approach developed in this paper should be helpful in resolving those cases. Likewise, the questions and some of the techniques considered here make sense for the affine Weyl groups. Going forward, it would be especially interesting to follow in the footsteps of Marin and apply the results of this paper and its prequels to the representation theory of the associated Iwahori–Hecke algebras and Artin braid groups. In particular, the graded (super) representation theory of these objects warrants investigation.

2.
Preliminaries

We adopt the general notational and terminological conventions for vector superspaces and semisimple superalgebras as laid out in
[
4
, §2]
. If
A
is a superalgebra, then we may write
|A|
for the algebra obtained by forgetting its
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-grading.

2.1.
Supergroups

Recall that a finite group
G
is a
supergroup
if it admits a multiplicative grading
G=G_{\overline{0}}\cup G_{\overline{1}}
by
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
. This is equivalent to the existence of a group homomorphism
\kappa:G\to\left\{\pm 1\right\}
such that
G_{\overline{0}}=\ker(\kappa)
and
G_{\overline{1}}=G\backslash G_{\overline{0}}
. We will assume that
G_{\overline{0}}\neq G
, and hence that
G_{\overline{0}}
is an index-
2
subgroup of
G
. The
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-grading on
G
extends by linearity to a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-grading on the group algebra
\mathbb{C}G
, making
\mathbb{C}G
into a superalgebra. Since
\mathbb{C}G
is semisimple as an ordinary algebra, it is also semisimple as a superalgebra; see
[
2
, §2]
or
[
4
, Lemma 2.2.11]
.

Let
\{S^{\lambda}:\lambda\in\Lambda\}
be a complete set of simple
\mathbb{C}G
-modules up to isomorphism. By abuse of notation, let
\kappa
also denote the one-dimensional
\mathbb{C}G
-module afforded by
\kappa
. Then for each
\lambda\in\Lambda
, one has
S^{\lambda}\otimes\kappa\cong S^{\lambda^{\prime}}
for a unique
\lambda^{\prime}\in\Lambda
, and the map
\lambda\mapsto\lambda^{\prime}
defines an involution on
\Lambda
. Schur’s Lemma implies for each
\lambda\in\Lambda
that, up to mutual rescaling, there exist unique linear maps
\phi_{\kappa}^{\lambda}:S^{\lambda}\to S^{\lambda^{\prime}}
and
\phi_{\kappa}^{\lambda^{\prime}}:S^{\lambda^{\prime}}\to S^{\lambda}
(corresponding to
\mathbb{C}G
-module isomorphisms
S^{\lambda}\otimes\kappa\cong S^{\lambda^{\prime}}
and
S^{\lambda^{\prime}}\otimes\kappa\cong S^{\lambda}
) such that for all
g\in G
,
v\in S^{\lambda}
, and
w\in S^{\lambda^{\prime}}
,

(2.1.1)

\displaystyle\phi_{\kappa}^{\lambda}(g\cdot v)

\displaystyle=\kappa(g)g\cdot\phi_{\kappa}^{\lambda}(v),

\displaystyle\phi_{\kappa}^{\lambda^{\prime}}(g\cdot w)

\displaystyle=\kappa(g)g\cdot\phi_{\kappa}^{\lambda^{\prime}}(w),

(2.1.2)

\displaystyle\phi_{\kappa}^{\lambda^{\prime}}\circ\phi_{\kappa}^{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\id_{S^{\lambda}},

\displaystyle\phi_{\kappa}^{\lambda}\circ\phi_{\kappa}^{\lambda^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\id_{S^{\lambda^{\prime}}}.

In the case
\lambda=\lambda^{\prime}
, this specifies the map
\phi_{\kappa}^{\lambda}:S^{\lambda}\to S^{\lambda}
up to scalar multiplication by
\pm 1
. In the literature, the map
\phi_{\kappa}^{\lambda}
is called an
associator
for
S^{\lambda}
; cf.
[
9
,
8
]
.

Example 2.1.1

(The symmetric group)
.

Let
n\geq 2
. The symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
is a supergroup via the sign character
\varepsilon^{\prime\prime}:\mathfrak{S}_{n}\to\left\{\pm 1\right\}
. The even subgroup
(\mathfrak{S}_{n})_{\overline{0}}
of
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
is then the alternating group
A_{n}
. The simple
\mathbb{C}\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-modules are labeled by integer partitions
\lambda\vdash n
. Given
\lambda\vdash n
, let
\lambda^{*}\vdash n
be its conjugate (or transpose) partition, and let
S^{\lambda}
be the simple Specht module for
\mathbb{C}\mathfrak{S}_{n}
labeled by
\lambda
. Then the trivial
\mathbb{C}\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-module is
S^{[n]}
, and the sign module (i.e., the module afforded by
\varepsilon^{\prime\prime}
) is
S^{[1^{n}]}
. By abuse of notation, we may also denote
S^{[1^{n}]}
by
\varepsilon^{\prime\prime}
. Then for each
\lambda\vdash n
, one has
S^{\lambda}\otimes\varepsilon^{\prime\prime}\cong S^{\lambda^{*}}
. An explicit associator
\phi^{\lambda}:S^{\lambda}\to S^{\lambda}
for
\lambda=\lambda^{*}
is given in
[
9
, Theorem 2.2]
; see also
[
8
, Eqn 9]
.

Given a
\mathbb{C}G_{\overline{0}}
-module
W
and an element
t\in G_{\overline{1}}
, let
{}^{t}W=\{{}^{t}w:w\in W\}
be the
conjugate

\mathbb{C}G_{\overline{0}}
-module, in which the action of an element
h\in G_{\overline{0}}
is defined by
h.{}^{t}w={}^{t}[(tht^{-1}).w]
. Up to isomorphism, the conjugate module does not depend on the choice of element
t\in G_{\overline{1}}
. We say that two
\mathbb{C}G_{\overline{0}}
-modules
W
and
W^{\prime}
are conjugate if
W^{\prime}\cong{}^{t}W
for some
t\in G_{\overline{1}}
.

If
V
is a
\mathbb{C}G
-module, then we write
\Res_{G_{\overline{0}}}^{G}(V)
for the
\mathbb{C}G_{\overline{0}}
-module obtained by restriction. The simple
\mathbb{C}G_{\overline{0}}
-modules are now obtained as follows:

Lemma 2.1.2

(cf.
[
5
, Proposition 5.1]
)
.

With assumptions and notation as above, each simple
\mathbb{C}G_{\overline{0}}
-module arises uniquely (up to isomorphism) in one of the following two ways:

(1)

If
\lambda\neq\lambda^{\prime}
, then
\Res_{G_{\overline{0}}}^{G}(S^{\lambda})\cong\Res_{G_{\overline{0}}}^{G}(S^{\lambda^{\prime}})
is simple and isomorphic to its conjugate.

(2)

If
\lambda=\lambda^{\prime}
, then
\Res_{G_{\overline{0}}}^{G}(S^{\lambda})
is (canonically, by uniqueness of isotypical components) the direct sum of two simple, conjugate, non-isomorphic
\mathbb{C}G_{\overline{0}}
-submodules
S^{\lambda^{+}}
and
S^{\lambda^{-}}
, equal to the
+1
and
-1
eigenspaces of (a fixed choice of) the map
\phi_{\kappa}^{\lambda}:S^{\lambda}\to S^{\lambda}
.

Then applying
[
2
, Lemma 2.3]
and
[
2
, Corollary 2.8]
as in
[
4
, §3.1]
, one deduces:

Proposition 2.1.3
.

With assumptions and notation as above, each simple
\mathbb{C}G
-supermodule occurs uniquely, up to homogeneous isomorphism, in one of the following two ways:

(1)

If
\lambda\neq\lambda^{\prime}
, then there exists a Type Q simple
\mathbb{C}G
-supermodule
W^{\lambda}=W^{\lambda^{\prime}}
such that
W^{\lambda}=S^{\lambda}\oplus S^{\lambda^{\prime}}
as a
|\mathbb{C}G|
-module, with

W_{\overline{0}}^{\lambda}=\{u+\phi_{\kappa}^{\lambda}(u):u\in S^{\lambda}\}\qquad\text{and}\qquad W_{\overline{1}}^{\lambda}=\{u-\phi_{\kappa}^{\lambda}(u):u\in S^{\lambda}\},

and with odd involution
J^{\lambda}:W^{\lambda}\to W^{\lambda}
defined by
J^{\lambda}(u\pm\phi_{\kappa}^{\lambda}(u))=u\mp\phi_{\kappa}^{\lambda}(u)
.

(2)

If
\lambda=\lambda^{\prime}
, then there exists a Type
M
simple
\mathbb{C}G
-supermodule
W^{\lambda}
such that
W^{\lambda}=S^{\lambda}
as a
|\mathbb{C}G|
-module, with
W_{\overline{0}}^{\lambda}=S^{\lambda^{+}}
and
W_{\overline{1}}^{\lambda}=S^{\lambda^{-}}
.

Each simple
\mathbb{C}G
-supermodule is uniquely determined up to an even isomorphism by its summands as a
\mathbb{C}G_{\overline{0}}
-module and by the superdegrees in which those summands are concentrated.

Remark 2.1.4
.

(1)

Each simple
\mathbb{C}G
-supermodule is even-dimensional.

(2)

For
\lambda\neq\lambda^{\prime}
and
c\neq 0
, the
\mathbb{C}G
-supermodule structure on
S^{\lambda}\oplus S^{\lambda^{\prime}}
defined by the pair
(\phi_{\kappa}^{\lambda},\phi_{\kappa}^{\lambda^{\prime}})
is isomorphic to that defined by
(c\cdot\phi_{\kappa}^{\lambda},\frac{1}{c}\cdot\phi_{\kappa}^{\lambda^{\prime}})
via the map
(u,v)\mapsto(u,c\cdot v)
.

(3)

For
\lambda\neq\lambda^{\prime}
, if
u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}
is a basis for
S^{\lambda}
, then
\{u_{i}+\phi_{\kappa}^{\lambda}(u):1\leq i\leq n\}
and
\{u_{i}-\phi_{\kappa}^{\lambda}(u):1\leq i\leq n\}
are bases for
W_{\overline{0}}^{\lambda}
and
W_{\overline{1}}^{\lambda}
, respectively.

(4)

For
\lambda\neq\lambda^{\prime}
, the map
J^{\lambda}
can be interpreted as defining an even
\mathbb{C}G
-supermodule isomorphism from
W^{\lambda}
to the parity shifted module
\Pi(W^{\lambda})
.

(5)

For
\lambda=\lambda^{\prime}
, the
\mathbb{C}G
-supermodule
W^{\lambda}
is odd-isomorphic to
\Pi(W^{\lambda})
but not even-isomorphic because the
\mathbb{C}G_{\overline{0}}
-module summands of
S^{\lambda}
are not isomorphic. Fixing
W^{\lambda}
up to an even isomorphism depends in our notation on a fixed choice for the map
\phi_{\kappa}^{\lambda}
. If one replaces
\phi_{\kappa}^{\lambda}
with
-\phi_{\kappa}^{\lambda}
, then
W^{\lambda}
is replaced by
\Pi(W^{\lambda})
.

Given a finite supergroup
G
, let
\Irr(G)
be the set of isomorphism classes of the simple
\mathbb{C}G
-modules, and let
\Irr_{s}(G)
be the set of isomorphism classes (up to homogeneous isomorphism) of the simple
\mathbb{C}G
-supermodules. Then
\Irr_{S}(G)=\Irr_{M}(G)\cup\Irr_{Q}(G)
, where
\Irr_{M}(G)
(resp.
\Irr_{Q}(G)
) is the set of isomorphism classes of the Type M (resp. Type Q) simple
\mathbb{C}G
-supermodules. Given
W\in\Irr(G)
(resp.
W\in\Irr_{s}(G)
), we may abuse notation and write
W:\mathbb{C}G\to\End(W)
to denote the corresponding module structure map. Thus if
W\in\Irr(G)
and
g\in G
, then
W(g)\in\End(W)
denotes the map
u\mapsto g.u
.

Recall that if
V
is a vector superspace equipped with an odd involution
J
, then

(2.1.3)

Q(V)=Q(V,J)=\left\{\theta\in\End(V):J\circ\theta=\theta\circ J\right\}

is a simple subsuperalgebra of
\End(V)
. Now
[
4
, Theorem 2.2.11]
gives:

Corollary 2.1.5
.

Let
G
be a finite supergroup. Then the sum of the simple supermodule structure maps
W:\mathbb{C}G\to\End(W)
induces a superalgebra isomorphism

(2.1.4)

\mathbb{C}G\cong\Big[\bigoplus_{W\in\Irr_{Q}(G)}Q(W)\Big]\oplus\Big[\bigoplus_{W\in\Irr_{M}(G)}\End(W)\Big]

In particular, for
W\in\Irr_{Q}(G)
, the structure map
W:\mathbb{C}G\to\End(W)
has image in
Q(W)
.

Remark 2.1.6
.

Applying
[
4
, Lemma 2.2.14]
, one sees that, on restriction to the even subspaces, (
2.1.4
) restricts to the usual Artin–Wedderburn isomorphism for
\mathbb{C}G_{\overline{0}}
:

\mathbb{C}G_{\overline{0}}\cong\bigoplus_{V\in\Irr(G_{\overline{0}})}\End(V).

More precisely, for
W\in\Irr_{M}(G)
, the supermodule structure map
W:\mathbb{C}G\to\End(W)
restricts to the surjective
\mathbb{C}G_{\overline{0}}
-module structure map
W:\mathbb{C}G_{\overline{0}}\to\End(W)
, while for
W\in\Irr_{Q}(G)
with
W=S^{\lambda}\oplus S^{\lambda^{\prime}}
as
|\mathbb{C}G|
-modules and
S^{\lambda}\cong S^{\lambda^{\prime}}
as
\mathbb{C}G_{\overline{0}}
-modules, the structure map
W:\mathbb{C}G\to\End(W)
restricts to map
\mathbb{C}G_{\overline{0}}\to\End(S^{\lambda})\oplus\End(S^{\lambda^{\prime}})
,
x\mapsto(S^{\lambda}(x),S^{\lambda^{\prime}}(x))
, whose image is a diagonally embedded copy of
\End(S^{\lambda})\cong\End(S^{\lambda^{\prime}})
.

2.2.
Lie superalgebras arising from simple superalgebras of types M and Q

Given a vector superspace
V
, write
\mathfrak{gl}(V)
for the set
\End(V)
considered as a Lie superalgebra via the super commutator
[x,y]=xy-(-1)^{\overline{x}\cdot\overline{y}}yx
. Fixing a homogeneous basis for
V
, and making the identification
V\cong\mathbb{C}^{m|n}\coloneq\mathbb{C}^{m}\oplus\Pi(\mathbb{C}^{n})
for some
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
via this choice of basis,
\mathfrak{gl}(V)
identifies with the matrix Lie superalgebra

\mathfrak{gl}(m|n):=\left\{\left[\begin{array}[]{c|c}A&B\\
\hline\cr C&D\end{array}\right]:A\in M_{m}(\mathbb{C}),B\in M_{m\times n}(\mathbb{C}),C\in M_{n\times m}(\mathbb{C}),D\in M_{n}(\mathbb{C})\right\}.

If
V
is a vector superspace equipped with an odd involution
J:V\to V
, write
\mathfrak{q}(V)
for the set
Q(V)
of (
2.1.3
) considered as a Lie superalgebra via the super commutator. Fixing a basis
\left\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\right\}
for
V_{\overline{0}}
, and setting
v_{i}^{\prime}=J(v_{i})
for
1\leq i\leq n
, one then has
V\cong\mathbb{C}^{n|n}
, and
\mathfrak{q}(V)
identifies with

\mathfrak{q}(n):=\left\{\left[\begin{array}[]{c|c}A&B\\
\hline\cr B&A\end{array}\right]:A\in M_{n}(\mathbb{C}),B\in M_{n}(\mathbb{C})\right\}.

For an arbitrary Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{g}
, we denote its derived subalgebra
[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
by
\mathfrak{D}(\mathfrak{g})
. Then
\mathfrak{D}(\mathfrak{gl}(V))
is the special linear Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{sl}(V)
, and

\mathfrak{D}(\mathfrak{gl}(m|n))=\mathfrak{sl}(m|n):=\left\{\left[\begin{array}[]{c|c}A&B\\
\hline\cr C&D\end{array}\right]\in\mathfrak{gl}(m|n):\tr(A)-\tr(D)=0\right\}.

Now let
V
be a vector superspace equipped with an odd involution
J:V\to V
, let
\theta\in\mathfrak{q}(V)
, and let
\theta=\theta_{\overline{0}}+\theta_{\overline{1}}
be the decomposition of
\theta
into its homogeneous components. Then
J\circ\theta_{\overline{1}}=\theta_{\overline{1}}\circ J
restricts to an even linear map
(J\circ\theta_{\overline{1}})|_{V_{\overline{0}}}:V_{\overline{0}}\to V_{\overline{0}}
. Identifying
V_{\overline{0}}
and
V_{\overline{1}}
via
J
, this is equal to the even linear map
(\theta_{\overline{1}}\circ J)|_{V_{\overline{1}}}:V_{\overline{1}}\to V_{\overline{1}}
. Now define the
odd trace
of
\theta
, denoted
\otr(\theta)
, by

\otr(\theta)=\tr\big((J\circ\theta_{\overline{1}})|_{V_{\overline{0}}}\big)=\tr\big((\theta_{\overline{1}}\circ J)|_{V_{\overline{1}}}\big),

i.e., the trace of the linear maps
(J\circ\theta_{\overline{1}})|_{V_{\overline{0}}}
or
(\theta_{\overline{1}}\circ J)|_{V_{\overline{1}}}
, and define
\mathfrak{sq}(V)\subseteq\mathfrak{q}(V)
by

\mathfrak{sq}(V)=\left\{\theta\in\mathfrak{q}(V):\otr(\theta)=0\right\}.

Then one can show that
\mathfrak{D}(\mathfrak{q}(V))=\mathfrak{sq}(V)
, and

\mathfrak{D}(\mathfrak{q}(n))=\mathfrak{sq}(n):=\left\{\left[\begin{array}[]{c|c}A&B\\
\hline\cr B&A\end{array}\right]\in\mathfrak{q}(n):\tr(B)=0\right\}.

Lemma 2.2.1
.

(1)

If
m\geq 2
, then
\mathfrak{sl}(m|m)
is generated as a Lie superalgebra by
\mathfrak{sl}(m|m)_{\overline{1}}
.

(2)

If
m\geq 3
, then
\mathfrak{sq}(m)
is generated as a Lie superalgebra by
\mathfrak{sq}(m)_{\overline{1}}
.

Proof.

It is an exercise to show that various matrix units (or sums of two matrix units) spanning the even parts of the Lie superalgebras can be obtained as Lie brackets of odd elements.
∎

2.3.
Lie superalgebras generated by odd elements

Let
G
be a finite supergroup, and write
\Lie(\mathbb{C}G)
for the superalgebra
\mathbb{C}G
considered as a Lie superalgebra via the super commutator

[x,y]=xy-(-1)^{\overline{x}\cdot\overline{y}}yx.

Then (
2.1.4
) restricts to a Lie superalgebra isomorphism

(2.3.1)

\Lie(\mathbb{C}G)\cong\Big[\bigoplus_{W\in\Irr_{Q}(G)}\mathfrak{q}(W)\Big]\oplus\Big[\bigoplus_{W\in\Irr_{M}(G)}\mathfrak{gl}(W)\Big].

Set
\mathfrak{D}(\mathbb{C}G)=\mathfrak{D}(\Lie(\mathbb{C}G))
. Then making the identification (
2.3.1
), one has

(2.3.2)

\mathfrak{D}(\mathbb{C}G)=\Big[\bigoplus_{W\in\Irr_{Q}(G)}\mathfrak{sq}(W)\Big]\oplus\Big[\bigoplus_{W\in\Irr_{M}(G)}\mathfrak{sl}(W)\Big]

Let
S\subseteq G_{\overline{1}}
be a subset of the odd elements of
G
, and let
\mathfrak{g}
be the Lie subsuperalgebra of
\mathbb{C}G
generated by the elements of the set
S
. In this section we will prove results under various combinations of the following assumptions:

(A1)

The set
S
is a union of conjugacy classes in
G
, say
S=C_{1}\cup\cdots\cup C_{t}
.

(A2)

There exists
S^{\prime}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}
such that
G_{\overline{0}}=\langle S^{\prime}\rangle
. In particular,
S^{\prime}
generates
\mathbb{C}G_{\overline{0}}
as an algebra.

(A3)

The set
S^{\prime}
consists of a single conjugacy class of order-two elements in
G_{\overline{0}}
.

Lemma 2.3.1
.

Suppose
(A1)
holds. For
1\leq i\leq t
, let
T_{i}=\sum_{c\in C_{i}}c\in\mathfrak{g}
. Then

\mathfrak{g}\subseteq\mathfrak{D}(\mathbb{C}G)+\Span\{T_{1},\ldots,T_{t}\}.

Proof.

Since any Lie bracket between elements of
\mathbb{C}G
is by definition equal to an element of
\mathfrak{D}(\mathbb{C}G)
, it suffices to show that
S\subseteq\mathfrak{D}(\mathbb{C}G)+\Span\{T_{1},\ldots,T_{t}\}
. Let
\tau\in S
with say
\tau\in C_{1}
. Set
C=C_{1}
and
T=T_{1}
. For each
V\in\Irr(G)
, the trace of the operator
V(c):V\to V
is constant across elements
c\in C
, because all elements of
C
are conjugate. Then
\tr(V(T))=|C|\cdot\tr(V(\tau))
, and hence
\widetilde{\tau}\coloneq\tau-\frac{1}{|C|}\cdot T
is a traceless operator on
V
.

Now let
W\in\Irr_{s}(G)
. Then
W(\widetilde{\tau})\in\mathfrak{gl}(W)_{\overline{1}}=\mathfrak{D}(\mathfrak{gl}(W))_{\overline{1}}\subset\mathfrak{D}(\mathfrak{gl}(W))
. If
W
is of Type Q, with say
W=S^{\lambda}\oplus S^{\lambda^{\prime}}
as in Proposition
2.1.3
, and if one fixes a homogeneous basis for
W
as in Remark
2.1.4
(
3
) by fixing a basis for
S^{\lambda}
, then one sees that the matrix of
W(\widetilde{\tau})
is

W^{\lambda}(\widetilde{\tau})=\left[\begin{array}[]{c|c}0&S^{\lambda}(\widetilde{\tau})\\
\hline\cr S^{\lambda}(\widetilde{\tau})&0\end{array}\right],

in which the off-diagonal blocks are the matrix of
S^{\lambda}(\widetilde{\tau})
with respect to the chosen basis for
S^{\lambda}
. In particular,
S^{\lambda}(\widetilde{\tau})
is traceless by the previous paragraph, so
W^{\lambda}(\widetilde{\tau})\in\mathfrak{sq}(W)=\mathfrak{D}(\mathfrak{q}(W))
.

Finally, make the identifications (
2.3.1
) and (
2.3.2
). The observations of the previous paragraph show for each
W\in\Irr_{s}(G)
that the projection
W(\widetilde{s})
is an element of
\mathfrak{D}(\mathbb{C}G)
. Then
\widetilde{\tau}\in\mathfrak{D}(\mathbb{C}G)
, and hence
\tau=\widetilde{\tau}+\frac{1}{|C|}\cdot T\in\mathfrak{D}(\mathbb{C}G)+\Span\{T_{1},\ldots,T_{t}\}
.
∎

Lemma 2.3.2
.

Suppose
(A1)
and
(A2)
hold. Let
Z(\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}})
be the center of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}
. Then
Z(\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}})=\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}\cap Z(\mathbb{C}G_{\overline{0}})
, and the projection map
p:\mathbb{C}G_{\overline{0}}\to Z(\mathbb{C}G_{\overline{0}})
, defined by

p(z)=\frac{1}{|G_{\overline{0}}|}\sum_{g\in G_{\overline{0}}}gzg^{-1},

restricts to a projection map
p:\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}\to Z(\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}})
. For this map, one has
p(\mathfrak{D}(\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}))\subseteq\mathfrak{D}(\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}})
.

Proof.

Let
z\in\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}
. The adjoint map
\ad_{z}:x\mapsto[z,x]
is a derivation on
\mathbb{C}G_{\overline{0}}
. If
z\in Z(\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}})
, then
\ad_{z}(s)=0
for each
s\in S^{\prime}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}
. Since
\mathbb{C}G_{\overline{0}}
is generated by the set
S^{\prime}
by
(A2)
, this implies that
\ad_{z}(x)=0
for all
x\in\mathbb{C}G_{\overline{0}}
, and hence
z\in Z(\mathbb{C}G_{\overline{0}})
. Then
z\in Z(\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}})
if and only if
z\in Z(\mathbb{C}G_{\overline{0}})
.

Next, since the set
S
is a union of conjugacy classes by
(A1)
, it follows that
\mathfrak{g}
is closed under conjugation by
G
. Conjugation is an even linear map, so
\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}
is also closed under conjugation. Then
p
sends elements of
\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}
to elements of
\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}\cap Z(\mathbb{C}G_{\overline{0}})=Z(\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}})
. Now since
\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}
is closed under conjugation, it follows that
\mathfrak{D}(\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}})=[\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}},\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}]
is also closed under conjugation, and hence
p(\mathfrak{D}(\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}))\subseteq\mathfrak{D}(\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}})
.
∎

Remark 2.3.3
.

In general,
\mathfrak{D}(\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}})\subset\mathfrak{D}(\mathfrak{g})_{\overline{0}}
but
\mathfrak{D}(\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}})\neq\mathfrak{D}(\mathfrak{g})_{\overline{0}}
.

Lemma 2.3.4
.

Suppose
(A2)
holds. Let
W
be a
\mathbb{C}G_{\overline{0}}
-module. Then the following are equivalent for a subspace
V\subseteq W
:

(1)

V
is a
\mathbb{C}G_{\overline{0}}
-submodule.

(2)

V
is a submodule for the action of the Lie subalgebra
\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}\subseteq\mathbb{C}G_{\overline{0}}
.

In particular, if
W
is a simple
\mathbb{C}G_{\overline{0}}
-module, then
W
is simple as a
\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}
-module.

Proof.

This is immediate from
(A2)
.
∎

Proposition 2.3.5
.

Suppose
(A1)
and
(A2)
hold. Then:

(1)

Each simple
\mathbb{C}G
-supermodule is semisimple as a
\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}
-module.

(2)

The Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}
is reductive. In particular,
\mathfrak{D}(\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}})
is semisimple,
\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}=Z(\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}})\oplus\mathfrak{D}(\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}})
, and the projection map
p:\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}\to Z(\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}})
of Lemma
2.3.2
has
\ker(p)=\mathfrak{D}(\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}})
.

Proof.

Let
W
be a simple
\mathbb{C}G
-supermodule. Then by the classification of the simple
\mathbb{C}G
-supermodules, the subspaces
W_{\overline{0}}
and
W_{\overline{1}}
are simple
\mathbb{C}G_{\overline{0}}
-modules, and hence are simple
\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}
-modules by Lemma
2.3.4
. Now the sum
\bigoplus_{W\in\Irr_{s}(G)}W
is a faithful (by Corollary
2.1.5
), finite-dimensional, completely reducible
\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}
-module. This implies by Proposition 5 of
[
1
, Chapter 1, §6, no. 4]
that
\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}
is reductive,
\mathfrak{D}(\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}})
is semisimple, and
\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}=Z(\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}})\oplus\mathfrak{D}(\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}})
. Finally, the projection map
p
sends
\mathfrak{D}(\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}})
into
\mathfrak{D}(\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}})\cap Z(\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}})=\left\{0\right\}
, implying that
\ker(p)=\mathfrak{D}(\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}})
.
∎

Lemma 2.3.6
.

Suppose
(A1)
and
(A2)
hold. Let
W
be a simple
\mathbb{C}G_{\overline{0}}
-module. Then
W
is simple as a
\mathfrak{D}(\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}})
-module.

Proof.

Let
V\subseteq W
be a nonzero
\mathfrak{D}(\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}})
-submodule of
W
. Since
Z(\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}})\subseteq Z(\mathbb{C}G_{\overline{0}})
by Lemma
2.3.2
, each element
z\in Z(\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}})
acts on
W
as a scalar multiple of the identity, by Schur’s Lemma. Then
V
is closed under the action of both
Z(\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}})
and
\mathfrak{D}(\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}})
, and hence closed under the action of
\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}
, by Proposition
2.3.5
. This implies by Lemma
2.3.4
that
V
is a nonzero
\mathbb{C}G_{\overline{0}}
-submodule of
W
. Then
V=W
, by the simplicity of
W
as a
\mathbb{C}G_{\overline{0}}
-module, so
W
is simple as a
\mathfrak{D}(\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}})
-module.
∎

Proposition 2.3.7
.

Suppose
(A1)
,
(A2)
, and
(A3)
hold. Let
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
be two simple
\mathbb{C}G_{\overline{0}}
-modules of dimensions greater than
1
. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

V_{1}
and
V_{2}
are isomorphic as
\mathbb{C}G_{\overline{0}}
-modules.

(2)

V_{1}
and
V_{2}
are isomorphic as modules over the Lie subalgebra
\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}\subseteq\mathbb{C}G_{\overline{0}}
.

(3)

V_{1}
and
V_{2}
are isomorphic as modules over the Lie subalgebra
\mathfrak{D}(\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}})\subseteq\mathbb{C}G_{\overline{0}}
.

Proof.

Our argument is an adaptation of the proof of
[
12
, Proposition 2]
. The fact that (
1
) implies (
2
), and that (
2
) implies (
3
), is evident. We will show that (
3
) implies (
1
).

Suppose
\phi:V_{1}\to V_{2}
is an isomorphism of
\mathfrak{D}(\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}})
-modules, and let
\rho_{1}:\mathbb{C}G_{\overline{0}}\to\End(V_{1})
and
\rho_{2}:\mathbb{C}G_{\overline{0}}\to\End(V_{2})
be the structure maps for
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
, respectively. Then for all
x\in\mathfrak{D}(\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}})
, one has
\phi\circ\rho_{1}(x)=\rho_{2}(x)\circ\phi
, or equivalently,
\rho_{2}(x)=\phi\circ\rho_{1}(x)\circ\phi^{-1}
. Let
s\in S^{\prime}
, and let
T=p(s)
, where
p:\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}\to Z(\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}})\subseteq Z(\mathbb{C}G_{\overline{0}})
is the projection map of Lemma
2.3.2
. The elements of the set
S^{\prime}
form a single conjugacy class in
G_{\overline{0}}
by
(A3)
, so
T
is independent of the choice of
s
. Since
T\in Z(\mathbb{C}G_{\overline{0}})
, Schur’s Lemma implies that
\rho_{1}(T)=c_{1}\cdot\id_{V_{1}}
and
\rho_{2}(T)=c_{2}\cdot\id_{V_{2}}
for some scalars
c_{1},c_{2}\in\mathbb{C}
, which also do not depend on the choice of
s
. One has
p(s-T)=p(s)-p(T)=T-T=0
, so
s-T\in\mathfrak{D}(\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}})
by Proposition
2.3.5
. Then

\displaystyle\rho_{2}(s)-c_{2}\cdot\id_{V_{2}}=\rho_{2}(s-T)

\displaystyle=\phi\circ\rho_{1}(s-T)\circ\phi^{-1}

\displaystyle=\phi\circ\rho_{1}(s)\circ\phi^{-1}-\phi\circ(c_{1}\cdot\id_{V_{1}})\circ\phi^{-1}

\displaystyle=\phi\circ\rho_{1}(s)\circ\phi^{-1}-c_{1}\cdot\id_{V_{2}},

or equivalently,

(2.3.3)

\rho_{2}(s)=\phi\circ\rho_{1}(s)\circ\phi^{-1}+\omega\cdot\id_{V_{2}},

where
\omega=c_{2}-c_{1}
. Squaring both sides of (
2.3.3
), and applying the assumption
(A3)
that
s^{2}=1
for each
s\in S^{\prime}
, it follows that
-2\omega\cdot\rho_{1}(s)=\omega^{2}\cdot\id_{V_{2}}
. The scalar
\omega
does not depend on the choice of
s
, so if
\omega\neq 0
, we would deduce first for all
s\in S^{\prime}
, and then for all
s\in G_{\overline{0}}
by multiplicativity—using
(A2)
, that
\rho_{1}(s)
is a nonzero scalar multiple of
\id_{V_{1}}
. Since
\dim(V_{1})\geq 2
by assumption, this would contradict the simplicity of
V_{1}
as a
\mathbb{C}G_{\overline{0}}
-module. Then
\omega=0
, and (
2.3.3
) implies first for all
s\in S^{\prime}
, and then for all
s\in G_{\overline{0}}
by multiplicativity, that
\phi\circ\rho_{1}(s)=\rho_{2}(s)\circ\phi^{-1}
, and hence
\phi
is a
\mathbb{C}G_{\overline{0}}
-module isomorphism.
∎

Corollary 2.3.8
.

Suppose
(A1)
,
(A2)
, and
(A3)
hold. Let
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
be two simple
\mathbb{C}G
-supermodules of dimensions greater than
2
. Then the following statements (in which ‘isomorphic’ is taken to mean ‘isomorphic via a homogeneous isomorphism’) are equivalent:

(1)

V_{1}
and
V_{2}
are isomorphic as
\mathbb{C}G
-supermodules.

(2)

V_{1}
and
V_{2}
are isomorphic as
\mathbb{C}G_{\overline{0}}
-supermodules.

(3)

V_{1}
and
V_{2}
are isomorphic as supermodules over the Lie subalgebra
\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}\subseteq\mathbb{C}G_{\overline{0}}
.

(4)

V_{1}
and
V_{2}
are isomorphic as supermodules over the Lie subalgebra
\mathfrak{D}(\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}})\subseteq\mathbb{C}G_{\overline{0}}
.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.1.3
, each simple
\mathbb{C}G
-supermodule is uniquely determined by its summands as a
\mathbb{C}G_{\overline{0}}
-module, and by the homogeneous degrees in which those summands are concentrated. Then passing to the homogeneous subspaces of
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
, the result follows by Proposition
2.3.7
.
∎

2.4.
Further Lie superalgebra structure

As in Section
2.3
, let
S\subseteq G_{\overline{1}}
be a subset of the odd elements of
G
, and let
\mathfrak{g}
be the Lie subsuperalgebra of
\Lie(\mathbb{C}G)
generated by
S
. In addition to assumptions
(A1)
,
(A2)
, and
(A3)
from Section
2.3
, we will impose combinations of the following assumptions:

(A4)

There exists a subsupergroup
H\subseteq G
and a subset
S_{H}\subseteq S
such that:

•

(A1)
and
(A2)
hold for
H
and the Lie subsuperalgebra
\mathfrak{h}\subseteq\Lie(\mathbb{C}H)
generated by
S_{H}
;

•

\mathfrak{h}_{\overline{0}}=\mathfrak{D}(\mathbb{C}H)_{\overline{0}}
, and hence (as a consequence of Remark
2.1.6
), the analogue of (
2.3.2
) for the group
H
restricts to an isomorphism

(2.4.1)

\mathfrak{D}(\mathfrak{h}_{\overline{0}})\cong\bigoplus_{V\in\Irr(H_{\overline{0}})}\mathfrak{sl}(V),

induced by the sum of the simple
\mathbb{C}H_{\overline{0}}
-module structure maps
V:\mathbb{C}H_{\overline{0}}\to\End(V)
;

•

there are no simple
\mathbb{C}H_{\overline{0}}
-modules of dimension
2
; and

•

if
V
is a simple
\mathbb{C}G_{\overline{0}}
-module with
\dim(V)>1
, then
\Res^{G_{\overline{0}}}_{H_{\overline{0}}}(V)
contains some simple
\mathbb{C}H_{\overline{0}}
-module
W
with
\dim(W)>1
.

(A5)

For each
V\in\Irr(G_{\overline{0}})
, the module structure map
V:\mathbb{C}G_{\overline{0}}\to\End(V)
restricts to a surjective Lie algebra map
\rho_{V}:\mathfrak{D}(\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}})\twoheadrightarrow\mathfrak{sl}(V)
.

(A6)

There are no simple
|\mathbb{C}G|
-modules of dimension
2
.

Let
V
be a
\mathbb{C}G_{\overline{0}}
-module with structure map
\rho:\mathbb{C}G_{\overline{0}}\to\End(V)
. There are two evident ways to define an action of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{D}(\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}})\subseteq\mathbb{C}G_{\overline{0}}
on the dual space
V^{*}=\Hom(V,\mathbb{C})
. The first is the restriction from
\mathbb{C}G_{\overline{0}}
to
\mathfrak{D}(\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}})
of the usual
\mathbb{C}G_{\overline{0}}
-module structure, defined for
g\in G_{\overline{0}}
,
\phi\in V^{*}
, and
v\in V
by
(g.\phi)(v)=\phi(g^{-1}.v)
. We denote this
\mathfrak{D}(\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}})
-module structure on
V^{*}
by
V^{*,\Grp}
. The second structure, which we denote
V^{*,\Lie}
, is via the contragredient action of a Lie algebra, defined for
x\in\mathfrak{D}(\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}})
,
\phi\in V^{*}
, and
v\in V
by
(x.\phi)(v)=-\phi(x.v)
. Fixing a basis for
V
and the corresponding dual basis for
V^{*}
, the structure maps
\rho_{\Grp}
and
\rho_{\Lie}
for
V^{*,\Grp}
and
V^{*,\Lie}
are related to
\rho
by
\rho_{\Grp}(x)=\rho(\imath(x))^{T}
and
\rho_{\Lie}(x)=-\rho(x)^{T}
, where
\imath:\mathbb{C}G_{\overline{0}}\to\mathbb{C}G_{\overline{0}}
is linear extension of the group inversion map
\sigma\mapsto\sigma^{-1}
, and

is the transpose of a matrix
u
.

Lemma 2.4.1
.

Suppose
(A1)
–
(A4)
hold. Let
V
and
W
be simple
\mathbb{C}G_{\overline{0}}
-modules of dimensions greater than
1
. Then
W\not\cong V^{*,\Lie}
.

Proof.

First we will show that if
V
and
W
are simple
\mathbb{C}H_{\overline{0}}
-modules of dimension greater than
1
, then
W\not\cong V^{*,\Lie}
as
\mathfrak{D}(\mathfrak{h}_{\overline{0}})
-modules. There are two cases:
V\cong W
or
V\not\cong W
. In the first case we may assume that
V=W
. The structure maps
\rho:\mathfrak{D}(\mathfrak{h}_{\overline{0}})\to\End(V)
and
\rho_{\Lie}:\mathfrak{D}(\mathfrak{h}_{\overline{0}})\to\End(V^{*,\Lie})
have the same kernel, and hence both factor, via the identification (
2.4.1
), through the projection
\mathfrak{D}(\mathfrak{h}_{\overline{0}})\twoheadrightarrow\mathfrak{sl}(V)
. Then
V\not\cong V^{*,\Lie}
, because
\dim(V)\geq 3
by
(A4)
, and the natural module for
\mathfrak{sl}(V)
is self-dual only if
\dim(V)\leq 2
. In the case
V\not\cong W
, we see from (
2.4.1
) that the image of the structure map
\mathfrak{D}(\mathfrak{h}_{\overline{0}})\to\End(V)\oplus\End(W)
is of dimension
\dim(\mathfrak{sl}(V))+\dim(\mathfrak{sl}(W))>\dim(\mathfrak{sl}(V))
. On the other hand, if
W\cong V^{*,\Lie}
, then up to a change of basis for
W
, the structure map is of the form
x\mapsto(\rho(x),-\rho(x)^{T})
, and hence its image is of dimension
\dim(\mathfrak{sl}(V))
. Thus
W\not\cong V^{*,\Lie}
.

Now let
V
and
W
be simple
\mathbb{C}G_{\overline{0}}
-modules of dimension greater than
1
, and suppose
W\cong V^{*,\Lie}
as
\mathfrak{D}(\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}})
-modules. Let
V=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}V_{i}
and
W=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{s}W_{j}
be decompositions of
V
and
W
into simple
\mathbb{C}H_{\overline{0}}
-modules. By
(A4)
and Lemma
2.3.6
, these are also decompositions of
V
and
W
into simple
\mathfrak{D}(\mathfrak{h}_{\overline{0}})
-modules. Then
V^{*,\Lie}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}(V_{i})^{*,\Lie}
is a decomposition of
V^{*,\Lie}
into simple
\mathfrak{D}(\mathfrak{h}_{\overline{0}})
-modules. Now since
W\cong V^{*,\Lie}
as
\mathfrak{D}(\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}})
-modules, it follows for each
j
that
W_{j}\cong(V_{i})^{*,\Lie}
as
\mathfrak{D}(\mathfrak{h}_{\overline{0}})
-modules for some
i
(depending on
j
). But by
(A4)
, at least one of the
W_{j}
is of dimension greater than
1
, so this isomorphism contradicts the previous paragraph. Thus,
W\not\cong V^{*,\Lie}
.
∎

Lemma 2.4.2
.

Suppose
(A1)
–
(A5)
hold. Then

\mathfrak{D}(\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}})=\mathfrak{D}(\mathfrak{D}(\mathbb{C}G)_{\overline{0}}).

In particular, the sum of the structure maps of the simple
\mathbb{C}G_{\overline{0}}
-modules induces an isomorphism

(2.4.2)

\mathfrak{D}(\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}})\cong\bigoplus_{V\in\Irr(G_{\overline{0}})}\mathfrak{sl}(V).

Proof.

First,
\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}\subseteq\mathfrak{D}(\mathbb{C}G)_{\overline{0}}
by Lemma
2.3.1
, and hence
\mathfrak{D}(\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}})\subseteq\mathfrak{D}(\mathfrak{D}(\mathbb{C}G)_{\overline{0}})
. Next, it follows from Remark
2.1.6
that (
2.3.2
) restricts to an isomorphism between
\mathfrak{D}(\mathfrak{D}(\mathbb{C}G)_{\overline{0}})
and the right-hand side of (
2.4.2
). In particular,

\dim(\mathfrak{D}(\mathfrak{D}(\mathbb{C}G)_{\overline{0}}))=\sum_{V\in\Irr(G_{\overline{0}})}\dim(\mathfrak{sl}(V)).

We will show by dimension comparison that
\mathfrak{D}(\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}})=\mathfrak{D}(\mathfrak{D}(\mathbb{C}\mathfrak{S}_{n})_{\overline{0}})
.

Recall from Proposition
2.3.5
that
\mathfrak{D}(\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}})
is a semisimple Lie algebra. Given
V\in\Irr(G_{\overline{0}})
, let
\rho_{V}:\mathfrak{D}(\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}})\to\End(V)
be the module structure map for the action of
\mathfrak{D}(\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}})
on
V
. By
(A5)
, the image of
\rho_{V}
is
\mathfrak{sl}(V)
. Let
\mathfrak{g}_{V}=\ker(\rho_{V})
, and let
\mathfrak{g}^{V}
be the orthogonal complement of
\mathfrak{g}_{V}
with respect to the Killing form on
\mathfrak{D}(\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}})
. Then
\mathfrak{g}_{V}
and
\mathfrak{g}^{V}
are ideals in
\mathfrak{D}(\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}})
,
\mathfrak{D}(\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}})=\mathfrak{g}_{V}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{V}
, and
\rho_{V}
induces a Lie algebra isomorphism
\mathfrak{g}^{V}\cong\im(\rho_{V})=\mathfrak{sl}(V)
. In particular, if
\dim(V)=1
, then
\mathfrak{g}^{V}=0
. We want to show that the (nonzero) simple ideals
\mathfrak{g}^{V}
for
V\in\Irr(G_{\overline{0}})
are distinct. Equivalently, we want to show that if
\mathfrak{g}^{V}=\mathfrak{g}^{W}\neq 0
, then
V=W
. This will imply that the sum of the
\mathfrak{g}^{V}
is a direct sum, and will then imply by dimension comparison that
\mathfrak{D}(\mathfrak{D}(\mathbb{C}G)_{\overline{0}})=\mathfrak{D}(\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}})=\bigoplus_{V\in\Irr(G_{\overline{0}})}\mathfrak{g}^{V}
.

Let
V,W\in\Irr(G_{\overline{0}})
such that
\mathfrak{g}^{V}=\mathfrak{g}^{W}\neq 0
. Since
\mathfrak{sl}(V)\cong\mathfrak{g}^{V}=\mathfrak{g}^{W}\cong\mathfrak{sl}(W)
, then
\dim(V)=\dim(W)>1
. Fixing bases, we may write
V=\mathbb{C}^{m}=W
for some
m
. Then the composite map

\mathfrak{sl}(\mathbb{C}^{m})\xrightarrow{\rho_{V}^{-1}}\mathfrak{g}^{V}=\mathfrak{g}^{W}\xrightarrow{\rho_{W}}\mathfrak{sl}(C^{m})

is a Lie algebra automorphism. Lie algebra automorphisms of
\mathfrak{sl}(\mathbb{C}^{m})
come in two forms:

(1)

X\mapsto gXg^{-1}
for some
g\in GL(\mathbb{C}^{m})
, or

(2)

X\mapsto-(gX^{t}g^{-1})
for some
g\in GL(\mathbb{C}^{m})
, where
X^{t}
denotes the transpose of
X
;

see
[
10
, IX.5]
. If
\rho_{W}\circ\rho_{V}^{-1}
is of the second form, then the
\mathfrak{D}(\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}})
-module structure on
\mathbb{C}^{m}
afforded by
\rho_{W}
is isomorphic to the dual of the
\mathfrak{D}(\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}})
-module structure afforded by
\rho_{V}
, i.e.,
W\cong V^{*,\Lie}
as
\mathfrak{D}(\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}})
-modules, contradicting Lemma
2.4.1
. Then
\rho_{W}\circ\rho_{V}^{-1}
must be of the first form, meaning the
\mathfrak{D}(\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}})
-module structures on
\mathbb{C}^{m}
afforded by
\rho_{V}
and
\rho_{W}
are isomorphic, and hence
V=W
by Proposition
2.3.7
.
∎

Lemma 2.4.3
.

Suppose
(A1)
–
(A6)
hold. Then for each
W\in\Irr_{s}(G)
, the image of
\mathfrak{g}
under the supermodule structure map
W:\mathbb{C}G\to\End(W)
is

(2.4.3)

W(\mathfrak{g})=\begin{cases}\mathfrak{sq}(W)+\Span\{W(T_{1}),\ldots,W(T_{t})\}&\text{if $W$ is of Type Q,}\\
\mathfrak{sl}(W)&\text{if $W$ is of Type M,}\end{cases}

where
T_{1},\ldots,T_{t}\in\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{1}}
are the class sums defined in Lemma
2.3.1
.

Proof.

The “
\subseteq
” direction of (
2.4.3
) follow from Lemma
2.3.1
. For the reverse inclusion, first recall that each
W\in\Irr_{s}(G)
is even-dimensional. Condition
(A6)
implies that if
W\in\Irr_{M}(G)
, then
\dim(W)\geq 4
, and implies that if
W\in\Irr_{Q}(G)
, then either
\dim(W)=2
or
\dim(W)\geq 6
.

Suppose for the moment that
W\in\Irr_{Q}(G)
and
\dim(W)=2
. Then
\mathfrak{sq}(W)=\mathbb{C}\cdot\id_{W}
. The homogeneous subspaces of
W
are isomorphic one-dimensional
\mathbb{C}G_{\overline{0}}
-modules, on which
G_{\overline{0}}
must act by a common linear character, say
\kappa
. Let
\tau
be an element of the set
S\subseteq G_{\overline{1}}
that generates
\mathfrak{g}
. Then
\tau^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\cdot[\tau,\tau]\in\mathfrak{g}
, and
W(\tau^{2})=\kappa(\tau^{2})\cdot\id_{W}
. Since
\kappa(\tau^{2})\neq 0
, this implies that (
2.4.3
) is true if
W\in\Irr_{Q}(G)
and
\dim(W)=2
.

Now suppose that
W\in\Irr_{s}(G)
and
\dim(W)>2
. Then either
W\in\Irr_{M}(G)
with
\dim(W)\geq 4
, or
W\in\Irr_{Q}(G)
with
\dim(W)\geq 6
. Since
W(\mathfrak{g})
is a Lie subsuperalgebra of
\End(W)
, to finish the proof it then suffices by Lemma
2.2.1
to show that

(2.4.4)

W(\mathfrak{g})\supseteq\begin{cases}\mathfrak{sq}(W)_{\overline{1}}&\text{if $W\in\Irr_{Q}(G)$,}\\
\mathfrak{sl}(W)_{\overline{1}}&\text{if $W\in\Irr_{M}(G)$.}\end{cases}

First suppose
W\in\Irr_{Q}(G)
, with
W=S^{\lambda}\oplus S^{\lambda^{\prime}}
as a
|\mathbb{C}G|
-module,
S^{\lambda}\cong S^{\lambda^{\prime}}
as
\mathbb{C}G_{\overline{0}}
-modules, and
\dim(S^{\lambda})\geq 3
. Lemma
2.4.2
implies that
W(\mathfrak{g})
contains the Lie subalgebra
\mathfrak{D}(\mathfrak{sq}(W)_{\overline{0}})\cong\mathfrak{sl}(S^{\lambda})
, over which
\mathfrak{sq}(W)_{\overline{1}}
identifies with the adjoint representation of
\mathfrak{sl}(S^{\lambda})
. In particular,
\mathfrak{D}(\mathfrak{sq}(W)_{\overline{0}})
acts irreducibly on
\mathfrak{sq}(W)_{\overline{1}}
. Now choose some
\tau\in S
such that
\tau
does not act as a scalar multiple of the identity on
S^{\lambda}
; this is possible because otherwise it would follow that
\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}
acts trivially on
S^{\lambda}
, contradicting Lemma
2.3.4
and the assumption
\dim(S^{\lambda})\geq 3
. Then with notation and reasoning as in the proof of Lemma
2.3.1
, the operator
W(\tau-\frac{1}{|C|}\cdot T)\in W(\mathfrak{g})
is a nonzero element of
\mathfrak{sq}(W)_{\overline{1}}
. Then by the simplicity of
\mathfrak{sq}(W)_{\overline{1}}
as a
\mathfrak{D}(\mathfrak{sq}(W)_{\overline{1}})
-module, we must have
\mathfrak{sq}(W)_{\overline{1}}\subseteq W(\mathfrak{g})
, and hence
\mathfrak{sq}(W)\subseteq W(\mathfrak{g})
.

Now suppose
W\in\Irr_{M}(G)
, with
W=S^{\lambda^{+}}\oplus S^{\lambda^{-}}
as a
\mathbb{C}G_{\overline{0}}
-module. In this case, Lemma
2.4.2
implies that
W(\mathfrak{g})
contains the Lie subalgebra
\mathfrak{D}(\mathfrak{sl}(W)_{\overline{0}})\cong\mathfrak{sl}(S^{\lambda^{+}})\oplus\mathfrak{sl}(S^{\lambda^{-}})
, over which

(2.4.5)

\mathfrak{sl}(W)_{\overline{1}}\cong\Hom(S^{\lambda^{+}},S^{\lambda^{-}})\oplus\Hom(S^{\lambda^{-}},S^{\lambda^{+}})

is the sum of two simple, non-isomorphic modules. If
\tau\in S
, then
W(\tau)\in W(\mathfrak{g})
is an element of
\mathfrak{sl}(W)_{\overline{1}}
having nonzero components in both summands of (
2.4.5
). Then it follows from the uniqueness of isotypical components that the
\mathfrak{D}(\mathfrak{sl}(W)_{\overline{0}})
-submodule of
W(\mathfrak{g})
generated by
W(\tau)
must contain all of
\mathfrak{sl}(W)_{\overline{1}}
, and hence
\mathfrak{sl}(W)_{\overline{1}}\subseteq W(\mathfrak{g})
.
∎

3.
Type BC representation theory

In Sections
3
and
5
we recall some classical results for Weyl groups of types BC and D, and then interpret those results in the context of supergroups. A somewhat more detailed (but still brief) recollection of the classical results can also be found in
[
3
, §§2–3]
; see also
[
7
]
. To avoid trivialities, assume throughout this section that
n\geq 2
.

3.1.
Weyl groups of type B

Let
\mathcal{B}_{n}=W(B_{n})
be the Weyl group of type
B_{n}
. It is the wreath product
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\wr\mathfrak{S}_{n}
, i.e., a semidirect product
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}\rtimes\mathfrak{S}_{n}
in which
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
acts on
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
by place permutation. For
1\leq i\leq n
let
t_{i}
be a multiplicative generator for the
i
-th factor in
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
. Each element of
\mathcal{B}_{n}
can then be uniquely written in the form
t_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdots t_{n}^{i_{n}}\sigma
with
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
and
i_{j}\in\left\{0,1\right\}
for each
j
.

For
1\leq i\leq n-1
, let
s_{i}=(i,i+1)\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
. Then for
n\geq 2
, the pair
(\mathcal{B}_{n},\left\{s_{1},\ldots,s_{n-1},t_{n}\right\})
is a Coxeter system of type
B_{n}
, and the set of reflections in
\mathcal{B}_{n}
is

(3.1.1)

\left\{(i,j),\,t_{i}t_{j}(i,j):1\leq i<j\leq n\right\}\cup\left\{t_{i}:1\leq i\leq n\right\}.

Let
\varepsilon
,
\varepsilon^{\prime}
, and
\varepsilon^{\prime\prime}
be the group homomorphisms
\mathcal{B}_{n}\to\left\{\pm 1\right\}
that are defined on generators by

(3.1.2)

\displaystyle\varepsilon(t_{i})

\displaystyle=-1,\qquad

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{\prime}(t_{i})

\displaystyle=-1,\qquad

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{\prime\prime}(t_{i})

\displaystyle=+1,

\displaystyle\varepsilon(s_{j})

\displaystyle=-1,\qquad

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{\prime}(s_{j})

\displaystyle=+1,\qquad

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{\prime\prime}(s_{j})

\displaystyle=-1.

The map
\varepsilon
is the sign character of
\mathcal{B}_{n}
.

3.2.
Simple modules for Weyl groups of type B

The simple
\mathbb{C}\mathcal{B}_{n}
-modules are labeled by bipartitions of
n
, i.e., by ordered pairs of integer partitions
(\lambda,\mu)
such that
|\lambda|+|\mu|=n
. Let
\mathcal{BP}(n)
be the set of all bipartitions of
n
, and write
S^{(\lambda,\mu)}
for the simple
\mathbb{C}\mathcal{B}_{n}
-module labeled by
(\lambda,\mu)
. The trivial character and the characters
\varepsilon
,
\varepsilon^{\prime}
, and
\varepsilon^{\prime\prime}
afford the one-dimensional
\mathbb{C}\mathcal{B}_{n}
-modules labeled by
([n],\emptyset)
,
(\emptyset,[1^{n}])
,
(\emptyset,[n])
, and
([1^{n}],\emptyset)
, respectively. More generally, if
\lambda\vdash n
, then

S^{(\lambda,\emptyset)}=\Inf_{\mathfrak{S}_{n}}^{\mathcal{B}_{n}}(S^{\lambda})\qquad\text{and}\qquad S^{(\emptyset,\lambda)}=\Inf_{\mathfrak{S}_{n}}^{\mathcal{B}_{n}}(S^{\lambda})\otimes\varepsilon^{\prime},

where
\Inf_{\mathfrak{S}_{n}}^{\mathcal{B}_{n}}(V)
denotes the inflation of a
\mathbb{C}\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-module to
\mathbb{C}\mathcal{B}_{n}
along the canonical quotient map
\mathcal{B}_{n}\twoheadrightarrow\mathfrak{S}_{n}
. By
[
7
, Theorem 5.5.6(c)]
, one has

(3.2.1)

\displaystyle S^{(\lambda,\mu)}\otimes\varepsilon

\displaystyle\cong S^{(\mu^{*},\lambda^{*})},\qquad

\displaystyle S^{(\lambda,\mu)}\otimes\varepsilon^{\prime}

\displaystyle\cong S^{(\mu,\lambda)},\qquad

\displaystyle S^{(\lambda,\mu)}\otimes\varepsilon^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle\cong S^{(\lambda^{*},\mu^{*})}.

Lemma 3.2.1

(cf.
[
3
, Lemma 2.2.1]
)
.

Let
(\lambda,\mu)\in\mathcal{BP}(n)
.

(1)

\dim(S^{(\lambda,\mu)})=\binom{n}{|\lambda|}\cdot\dim(S^{\lambda})\cdot\dim(S^{\mu})
, where
S^{\lambda}
is the simple
\mathbb{C}\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-module labeled by
\lambda
.

(2)

The only one-dimensional
\mathbb{C}\mathcal{B}_{n}
-modules are those labeled by
([n],\emptyset)
,
(\emptyset,[1^{n}])
,
(\emptyset,[n])
, and
([1^{n}],\emptyset)
, i.e., the trivial module and the modules afforded by
\varepsilon
,
\varepsilon^{\prime}
, and
\varepsilon^{\prime\prime}
, respectively.

(3)

For
n\geq 5
, if
\dim(S^{(\lambda,\mu)})\neq 1
, then
\dim(S^{(\lambda,\mu)})\geq n-1
.

(4)

If
n
is even and
\lambda\vdash n/2
, then
\dim(S^{(\lambda,\lambda^{*})})=\dim(S^{(\lambda,\lambda)})=\binom{n}{n/2}\cdot\dim(S^{\lambda})^{2}
.

For
(\lambda,\mu)\in\mathcal{BP}(n)
, let
\mathbb{T}(\lambda,\mu)
be the set of all standard bitableaux of shape
(\lambda,\mu)
, i.e., the set of all ordered pairs
T=(T_{+1},T_{-1})
such that
T_{+1}
and
T_{-1}
are standard tableaux of shapes
\lambda
and
\mu
, respectively, whose boxes have collectively been filled by the integers
1,2,\ldots,n
. Given
T\in\mathbb{T}(\lambda,\mu)
, let
\rho_{T}(i)\in\left\{\pm 1\right\}
be the subscript of the tableau (
T_{+1}
or
T_{-1}
) in which the integer
i
is located, and let
\res_{T}(i)
be the residue (or
content
, in the terminology of
[
13
]
) of the box (in either
T_{+1}
or
T_{-1}
) in which the integer
i
is located. Given
T\in\mathbb{T}(\lambda,\mu)
and
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
, the (not necessarily standard) bitableau
\sigma\cdot T
of shape
(\lambda,\mu)
is defined by applying
\sigma
to the entries of
T
. The next theorem is a consequence of
[
13
, Theorem 6.12]
.

Theorem 3.2.2
.

Let
(\lambda,\mu)\in\mathcal{BP}(n)
. Then there exists an orthonormal basis

(3.2.2)

B^{(\lambda,\mu)}=\left\{c_{T}:T\in\mathbb{T}(\lambda,\mu)\right\}

for
S^{(\lambda,\mu)}
such that for
1\leq i<n
, the action of the generators
t_{i},s_{i}\in\mathcal{B}_{n}
is as follows: Given
T\in\mathbb{T}(\lambda,\mu)
, set
r_{i}=\res_{T}(i+1)-\res_{T}(i)
, and let
S_{T}^{(\lambda,\mu)}
be the span in
S^{(\lambda,\mu)}
of
c_{T}
.

(1)

t_{i}\cdot c_{T}=\rho_{T}(i)\cdot c_{T}
.

(2)

Suppose
i
and
i+1
are not in the same tableau in
T
. Then
S\coloneq s_{i}\cdot T
is standard,
s_{i}
leaves
S_{T}^{(\lambda,\mu)}\oplus S_{S}^{(\lambda,\mu)}
invariant, and the matrix of
s_{i}
with respect to the basis
\{c_{T},c_{S}\}
is
[\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{smallmatrix}]
.

(3)

If
i
and
i+1
are the same tableau in
T
, in the same row (
r_{i}=+1
) or in the same column
(r_{i}=-1
), then
s_{i}\cdot c_{T}=r_{i}\cdot c_{T}
.

(4)

Suppose
i
and
i+1
are in the same tableau in
T
, but not in the same row or the same column. Then
|r_{i}|\geq 2
, the bitableau
S\coloneq s_{i}\cdot T
is standard,
s_{i}
leaves
S_{T}^{(\lambda,\mu)}\oplus S_{S}^{(\lambda,\mu)}
invariant, and the matrix of
s_{i}
with respect to the basis
\left\{c_{T},c_{S}\right\}
is

\begin{bmatrix}r_{i}^{-1}&\sqrt{1-r_{i}^{-2}}\\
\sqrt{1-r_{i}^{-2}}&-r_{i}^{-1}\end{bmatrix}.

Remark 3.2.3
.

Let
(\lambda,\mu)\in\mathcal{BP}(n)
and
T\in\mathbb{T}(\lambda,\mu)
. The vector
c_{T}
is a simultaneous eigenvector for the action of the generalized Young–Jucys–Murphy (YJM) elements
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}\in\mathbb{C}\mathcal{B}_{n}
, which are defined by
X_{i}=\sum_{k=1}^{i-1}[(k,i)+t_{k}t_{i}(k,i)]
. The element
X_{i}
acts on

with eigenvalue
2\cdot\res_{T}(i)
; see
[
13
, Theorem 6.5]
. Then
\mathcal{X}_{n}\coloneq\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}
acts on
S^{(\lambda,\mu)}
as scalar multiplication by
2\cdot\res(\lambda,\mu)
, where
\res(\lambda,\mu)\coloneq\res(\lambda)+\res(\mu)
is the sum of the residues of the boxes in the Young diagrams of
\lambda
and
\mu
.

Given
\lambda\vdash n
and
\nu\vdash(n-1)
, write
\nu\prec\lambda
if the Young diagram of
\nu
can be obtained by removing a box from the Young diagram of
\lambda
. In this case, let
\res(\lambda/\nu)
denote the residue of the box that is removed from
\lambda
to obtain
\nu
. For
(\lambda,\mu)\in\mathcal{BP}(n)
and
(\nu,\tau)\in\mathcal{BP}(n-1)
, write
(\nu,\tau)\prec(\lambda,\mu)
if either
\nu\prec\lambda
and
\tau=\mu
, or
\nu=\lambda
and
\tau\prec\mu
. For
(\lambda,\mu)\in\mathcal{BP}(n)
and
T\in\mathbb{T}(\lambda,\mu)
, let
b_{T}(n)
denote the box in
T
in which the integer
n
is located, and let
T/b_{T}(n)
, or simply
T/n
, denote the standard bitableau obtained from
T
by removing the box

. Identify
\mathcal{B}_{n-1}
with the subgroup of
\mathcal{B}_{n}
generated by the set
\left\{s_{1},\ldots,s_{n-2},t_{n-1}\right\}
. Then for
(\lambda,\mu)\in\mathcal{BP}(n)
, one has

(3.2.3)

\Res^{\mathcal{B}_{n}}_{\mathcal{B}_{n-1}}(S^{(\lambda,\mu)})=\bigoplus_{(\nu,\tau)\prec(\lambda,\mu)}\Big[\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}T\in\mathbb{T}(\lambda,\mu)\\
T/n\in\mathbb{T}(\nu,\tau)\end{subarray}}S_{T}^{(\lambda,\mu)}\Big]\cong\bigoplus_{(\nu,\tau)\prec(\lambda,\mu)}S^{(\nu,\tau)}.

The fact that the summand indexed by
(\nu,\tau)
is isomorphic as a
\mathbb{C}\mathcal{B}_{n-1}
-module to

can be seen from Theorem
3.2.2
.

Given
(\lambda,\mu)\in\mathcal{BP}(n)
, let
R(\lambda,\mu)=(R_{+1},R_{-1})
be the row major bitableau of shape
(\lambda,\mu)
, i.e., the standard bitableau of shape
(\lambda,\mu)
in which
R_{+1}
(resp.
R_{-1}
) is filled with the integers
1,\ldots,|\lambda|
(resp.
|\lambda|+1,\ldots,n
) in row major order. For
T\in\mathbb{T}(\lambda,\mu)
, let
\sigma_{T}\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
be the permutation that maps
R(\lambda,\mu)
to
T
. The length of
T
is defined by
\ell(T)=\ell(\sigma_{T})
, the length of
\sigma_{T}
as an element of the Coxeter group
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
. If
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
and
\sigma\cdot T\in\mathbb{T}(\lambda,\mu)
, then

(-1)^{\ell(\sigma\cdot T)}=(-1)^{\ell(\sigma)}\cdot(-1)^{\ell(T)}=\varepsilon^{\prime\prime}(\sigma)\cdot\varepsilon^{\prime\prime}(\sigma_{T}).

Given a standard
\lambda
-tableau
T
, let
T^{*}
be its transpose, which is then a standard
\lambda^{*}
-tableau. For
T=(T_{+1},T_{-1})\in\mathbb{T}(\lambda,\mu)
, set
T^{*}=(T_{+1}^{*},T_{-1}^{*})\in\mathbb{T}(\lambda^{*},\mu^{*})
and
T^{\natural}=(T_{-1},T_{+1})\in\mathbb{T}(\mu,\lambda)
.

Lemma 3.2.4
.

Let
(\lambda,\mu)\in\mathcal{BP}(n)
, and let
\pi\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
be the
n
-cycle
(1,2,\ldots,n)
. Then

(3.2.4)

\displaystyle\sigma_{R(\lambda,\mu)^{\natural}}=\pi^{|\lambda|},

(3.2.5)

\displaystyle\sigma_{R(\mu^{*},\lambda^{*})^{\natural}}=[\sigma_{R(\lambda,\mu)^{\natural}}]^{-1}=\sigma_{R(\mu,\lambda)^{\natural}},

(3.2.6)

\displaystyle\sigma_{R(\lambda^{*},\mu^{*})^{*}}=[\sigma_{R(\lambda,\mu)^{*}}]^{-1}=\pi^{|\lambda|}\circ\sigma_{R(\mu^{*},\lambda^{*})^{*}}\circ\pi^{-|\lambda|}.

Proof.

The equality (
3.2.4
) is straightforward to check. Then
\sigma_{R(\mu^{*},\lambda^{*})^{\natural}}=\pi^{|\mu^{*}|}=\pi^{|\mu|}=\sigma_{R(\mu,\lambda)^{\natural}}
, giving (

\sigma=\sigma_{R(\lambda,\mu)^{*}}
maps

to

, then
\sigma
also maps

to
[R(\lambda,\mu)^{*}]^{*}=R(\lambda,\mu)
, and hence

maps

to

, giving the first equality in (

\sigma=\sigma_{R(\mu^{*},\lambda^{*})^{*}}
be the permutation that maps

to

\sigma\circ\pi^{-|\lambda|}
maps
R(\lambda,\mu)^{\natural}
to

. Since

acts irreducibly on
V
, it follows for each
1\leq i\leq t
that there exists a simple
\mathfrak{g}_{i}
-module
V_{i}
such that
V
identifies with the external tensor product of modules
V_{1}\boxtimes\cdots\boxtimes V_{t}
. Since
\mathfrak{g}^{V}\subset\End(V)
acts faithfully on
V
, it follows for each
i
that
V_{i}
cannot be the trivial
\mathfrak{g}_{i}
-module (else
\mathfrak{g}_{i}
acts trivially on
V
), and hence
\dim(V_{i})\geq 2
.

Fix
\tau\prec\mu
, and set
\mathfrak{h}^{\tau}=\mathfrak{sl}(S^{\llbracket\lambda,\tau\rrbracket})
. Then each
V_{i}
becomes an
\mathfrak{h}^{\tau}
-module via the sequence of canonical Lie algebra maps
\mathfrak{h}^{\tau}\hookrightarrow\mathfrak{h}^{V}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}^{V}\twoheadrightarrow\mathfrak{g}_{i}
. If
V_{i}
is trivial as an
\mathfrak{h}^{\tau}
-module, it would follow that each
\mathfrak{h}^{\tau}
-composition factor in
V
would be of multiplicity at least
\dim(V_{i})\geq 2
, contradicting the observation that
\mathfrak{h}^{\tau}
has a composition factor in
V
of multiplicity one. Thus each
V_{i}
must be a nontrivial
\mathfrak{h}^{\tau}
-module. Now if
t\geq 2
, one has

\displaystyle\dim(V)\geq\dim(V_{1})\cdot\dim(V_{2})

\displaystyle\geq\textstyle\binom{2m}{m+1}\cdot\dim(S^{\lambda})\cdot\binom{2m}{m+1}\cdot\dim(S^{\lambda})

\displaystyle\geq\textstyle 2m\cdot\binom{2m}{m+1}\cdot\dim(S^{\lambda})\cdot\dim(S^{\lambda})

\displaystyle>\textstyle\frac{2m+1}{m}\cdot\binom{2m}{m+1}\cdot\dim(S^{\lambda})\cdot\dim(S^{\mu})

\displaystyle=\textstyle\binom{2m+1}{m+1}\cdot\dim(S^{\lambda})\cdot\dim(S^{\mu})

\displaystyle=\dim(V),

where at the third line we observe that
\dim(S^{\mu})\leq\dim(S^{\lambda})
if
\mu\prec\lambda
. Then
\dim(V)>\dim(V)
, a contradiction, so it must be the case that
t=1
and
\mathfrak{g}^{V}
is a simple Lie algebra.

Now observe that

2\cdot\rk(\mathfrak{h}^{V})-\dim(V)=\Big[\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\left\{\nu,\tau\right\}\prec\left\{\lambda,\mu\right\}\\
\left\{\nu,\tau\right\}\neq\left\{\mu,\mu\right\}\end{subarray}}\dim(S^{\llbracket\nu,\tau\rrbracket})-2\Big]+\Big[-2\Big].

The assumptions on
\lambda
and
\mu
imply that the trivial
\mathbb{C}H
-module does not occur in
\Res_{H}^{G}(V)
. The assumptions also imply that
n\geq 7
, and hence imply by Lemma
5.3.4
that
\dim(S^{\llbracket\nu,\tau\rrbracket})\geq n-1\geq 6
for each
\left\{\nu,\tau\right\}\prec\left\{\lambda,\mu\right\}
with
\left\{\nu,\tau\right\}\neq\left\{\mu,\mu\right\}
. There exist such
\left\{\nu,\tau\right\}
, namely, for
\tau\prec\mu
one has
\left\{\lambda,\tau\right\}\prec\left\{\lambda,\mu\right\}
, so
2\cdot\rk(\mathfrak{h}^{V})-\dim(V)>0
. Then
\mathfrak{g}^{V}\cong\mathfrak{sl}(V)
by
[
12
, Lemme 13]
.

5.6.3.
Non-multiplicity free restriction: Case (
4b
) of Lemma
5.3.7

Now suppose
n
is even,
n/2
is odd,
\llbracket\lambda,\lambda\rrbracket\in F\llbracket n\rrbracket
, and
V=S^{\llbracket\lambda,+\rrbracket}\cong S^{\llbracket\lambda,-\rrbracket}
. First we will show that
\mathfrak{g}^{V}
is a simple Lie algebra; the argument is an adaptation of an argument in the proof of
[
3
, Lemma 5.7.1]
. Observe that since
\lambda=\lambda^{*}
, then
\nu\prec\lambda
if and only if
\nu^{*}\prec\lambda
. Then

\Res^{G}_{H}(S^{\llbracket\lambda,+\rrbracket})\cong\Big[\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}\nu\prec\lambda\\
\res(\lambda/\nu)>0\end{subarray}}S^{\llbracket\nu,\lambda\rrbracket}\oplus S^{\llbracket\nu^{*},\lambda\rrbracket}\Big]\oplus\Big[\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}\nu\prec\lambda\\
\res(\lambda/\nu)=0\end{subarray}}S^{\llbracket\nu,\lambda,+\rrbracket}\oplus S^{\llbracket\nu,\lambda,-\rrbracket}\Big].

If
\res(\lambda/\nu)>0
, then
S^{\llbracket\nu,\lambda\rrbracket}\cong S^{\llbracket\nu^{*},\lambda\rrbracket}
as
\mathbb{C}H
-modules, and these are the only isomorphisms among the simple
\mathbb{C}H
-module factors in
\Res^{G}_{H}(V)
. For
\nu\prec\lambda
, one has

\textstyle\dim(S^{\llbracket\nu,\lambda\rrbracket})=\binom{n-1}{n/2}\cdot\dim(S^{\nu})\cdot\dim(S^{\lambda})\geq\binom{n-1}{n/2}\cdot\dim(S^{\lambda}).

If
\nu\prec\lambda
and
\nu=\nu^{*}
, then
\nu
is not either
[n/2-1]
or
[1^{n/2-1}]
, so
\dim(S^{\nu})\geq 2
and

\textstyle\dim(S^{\llbracket\nu,\lambda,\pm\rrbracket})=\frac{1}{2}\cdot\dim(S^{\llbracket\nu,\lambda\rrbracket})=\frac{1}{2}\cdot\binom{n-1}{n/2}\cdot\dim(S^{\nu})\cdot\dim(S^{\lambda})\geq\binom{n-1}{n/2}\cdot\dim(S^{\lambda}).

Then
\dim(W)\geq\binom{n-1}{n/2}\cdot\dim(S^{\lambda})
for each simple
\mathbb{C}H
-module factor
W
in
V
.

The sum of the structure maps for the simple
\mathbb{C}H
-modules induces an isomorphism

(5.6.1)

\mathfrak{h}^{V}\cong\Big[\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}\nu\prec\lambda\\
\res(\lambda/\nu)>0\end{subarray}}\mathfrak{sl}\llbracket\nu,\lambda\rrbracket\Big]\oplus\Big[\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}\nu\prec\lambda\\
\res(\lambda/\nu)=0\end{subarray}}\mathfrak{sl}(S^{\llbracket\nu,\lambda,+\rrbracket})\oplus\mathfrak{sl}(S^{\llbracket\nu,\lambda,-\rrbracket})\Big],

where
\mathfrak{sl}\llbracket\nu,\lambda\rrbracket
denotes the diagonal copy of
\mathfrak{sl}(S^{\llbracket\nu,\lambda\rrbracket})\cong\mathfrak{sl}(S^{\llbracket\nu^{*},\lambda\rrbracket})
in
\End(S^{\llbracket\nu,\lambda\rrbracket})\oplus\End(S^{\llbracket\nu^{*},\lambda\rrbracket})
. Thus, if there exists
\nu\prec\lambda
with
\res(\lambda/\nu)=0
, then the simple ideals
\mathfrak{sl}(S^{\llbracket\nu,\lambda,+\rrbracket})
and
\mathfrak{sl}(S^{\llbracket\nu,\lambda,-\rrbracket})
of
\mathfrak{h}^{V}
have simple factors
S^{\llbracket\nu,\lambda,+\rrbracket}
and
S^{\llbracket\nu,\lambda,-\rrbracket}
, respectively, of multiplicity one in
V
, and each remaining simple ideal
\mathfrak{sl}\llbracket\nu,\lambda\rrbracket
has a simple factor
S^{\llbracket\nu,\lambda\rrbracket}\cong S^{\llbracket\nu^{*},\lambda\rrbracket}
of multiplicity two in
V
.

Now let
\mathfrak{g}^{V}=\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{t}
be the decomposition of the semisimple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}^{V}
into simple ideals. We want to show that
t=1
. Since
\mathfrak{g}^{V}
acts irreducibly on
V
, it follows for each
1\leq i\leq t
that there exists a simple
\mathfrak{g}_{i}
-module
V_{i}
such that
V
identifies with the external tensor product of modules
V_{1}\boxtimes\cdots\boxtimes V_{t}
. Since
\mathfrak{g}^{V}\subset\End(V)
acts faithfully on
V
, it follows for each
i
that
V_{i}
cannot be the trivial
\mathfrak{g}_{i}
-module (else
\mathfrak{g}_{i}
acts trivially on
V
), and hence
\dim(V_{i})\geq 2
.

Each
V_{i}
is an
\mathfrak{h}^{V}
-module via the sequence of canonical maps
\mathfrak{h}^{V}\hookrightarrow\mathfrak{g}^{V}\twoheadrightarrow\mathfrak{g}_{i}
. Since the nontrivial
\mathfrak{sl}(k)
-modules are all of dimension at least
k
, it follows for each
i
that if
V_{i}
is a nontrivial
\mathfrak{h}^{V}
-module (i.e., if
\mathfrak{g}_{i}
is nontrivial for some simple ideal of
\mathfrak{h}^{V}
), then
\dim(V_{i})\geq\binom{n-1}{n/2}\cdot\dim(S^{\lambda})
. If there are two values of
i
such that
V_{i}
is a nontrivial
\mathfrak{h}^{V}
-module, say
i=1
and
i=2
, then

\displaystyle\dim(V)

\displaystyle\geq\dim(V_{1})\cdot\dim(V_{2})

\displaystyle\geq\textstyle\binom{n-1}{n/2}\cdot\binom{n-1}{n/2}\cdot\dim(S^{\lambda})\cdot\dim(S^{\lambda})

\displaystyle=\textstyle\binom{n-1}{n/2}\cdot\frac{1}{2}\cdot\binom{n}{n/2}\cdot\dim(S^{\lambda})\cdot\dim(S^{\lambda})

\displaystyle=\textstyle\binom{n-1}{n/2}\cdot\dim(V)

\displaystyle>\dim(V),

a contradiction. Thus there is only one value of
i
, say
i=1
, such that
V_{i}
is nontrivial for
\mathfrak{h}^{V}
. And since
\dim(V_{i})\geq 2
for each
i
, and each simple ideal of
\mathfrak{h}^{V}
has a composition factor of multiplicity at most
2
in
V
, it also follows that there is at most one value of
i
such that
V_{i}
is trivial for
\mathfrak{h}^{V}
, and if such a

exists then
\dim(V_{i})=2
. Thus either
t=1
(as desired), or
t=2
and

with

a nontrivial

\mathfrak{h}^{V}
-module. In the latter case one has
\mathfrak{g}^{V}=\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2}
, with
\mathfrak{g}_{2}\cong\mathfrak{sl}(2)
because
\dim(V_{2})=2
.

Observe that if
x\in\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}
, and if
s\in\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}\cap G_{\overline{0}}
with

, then

(5.6.2)

\textstyle x-\frac{1}{2}\cdot[s,[s,x]]=sxs.

This implies that if
I
is an ideal in
\mathfrak{D}(\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}})
(hence an ideal in
\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}
), then
sIs=I
. Since
\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}
contains the set (
5.5.4
) of order-two generators for
G_{\overline{0}}
, it follows that each ideal in
\mathfrak{D}(\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}})
is invariant under conjugation by arbitrary elements of
G_{\overline{0}}
. And since

is a
\mathbb{C}G_{\overline{0}}
-module, it follows that the conjugation action on
\mathfrak{D}(\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}})
passes to an action on the simple ideals of
\mathfrak{g}^{V}
; recall the comment from the beginning of Section
5.6
that we may identify
\mathfrak{g}^{V}
with a subalgebra of
\mathfrak{D}(\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}})
.

Now suppose
t=2
as above. Then the conjugation action of
G_{\overline{0}}
on
\mathfrak{g}_{2}\cong\mathfrak{sl}(2)
defines a group homomorphism
\phi:G_{\overline{0}}\to\Aut(\mathfrak{sl}(2))\cong PGL(2,\mathbb{C})
with finite image. Up to isomorphism, the nontrivial finite subgroups of

are:

•

the cyclic groups
C_{m}
of order

,

•

the dihedral groups

of order
2m
,

•

the alternating group
A_{4}
,

•

the symmetric group
S_{4}
, and

•

the alternating group

;

see
[
15
]
. Further, since
G_{\overline{0}}
is perfect by Remark
5.3.6
, the image of
\phi
must be perfect. This implies that either
\phi
is the trivial map, or
\im(\phi)\cong A_{5}
. Suppose the latter. Then any simple
\mathbb{C}A_{5}
-module lifts, via
\phi
, to a simple
\mathbb{C}G_{\overline{0}}
-module. The alternating group
A_{5}
has simple modules of dimension
3
by
[
11
, Theorem 2.5.15]
, but applying Lemma
5.3.4
and Lemma
3.2.1
(
1
), one sees that if
n\geq 6
is twice an odd integer, then
G_{\overline{0}}=(\mathcal{D}_{n})_{\overline{0}}
has no simple modules of dimension
3
. This is a contradiction, so
\phi
must be the trivial map. Then each element
x\in\mathfrak{g}_{2}
is fixed under conjugation by all elements of
G_{\overline{0}}
. This implies by Lemma
2.3.2
that
\mathfrak{g}_{2}\subseteq Z(\mathfrak{g}^{V})
, which is again a contradiction because
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
is a nonzero ideal in
\mathfrak{g}^{V}
, but
\mathfrak{g}^{V}
is a semisimple Lie algebra and hence

. Thus, it must be the case that
t=1
and
\mathfrak{g}^{V}
is simple.

Now we will show that
\mathfrak{g}^{V}=\mathfrak{sl}(V)
. From (
5.6.1
) one gets

\rk(\mathfrak{h}^{V})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\nu\prec\lambda\\
\res(\lambda/\nu)>0\end{subarray}}\left[\dim(S^{\llbracket\nu,\lambda\rrbracket})-1\right]+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\nu\prec\lambda\\
\res(\lambda/\nu)=0\end{subarray}}\left[\dim(S^{\llbracket\nu,\lambda\rrbracket})-2\right],

Let
r_{\lambda}
be the number of removable boxes in the Young diagram of
\lambda
. Then

\displaystyle 2\cdot\rk(\mathfrak{h}^{V})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\nu\prec\lambda\\
\nu\neq\nu^{*}\end{subarray}}\left[\dim(S^{\llbracket\nu,\lambda\rrbracket})-1\right]+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\nu\prec\lambda\\
\nu=\nu^{*}\end{subarray}}\left[2\cdot\dim(S^{\llbracket\nu,\lambda\rrbracket})-4\right]

\displaystyle=\dim(V)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\nu\prec\lambda\\
\nu=\nu^{*}\end{subarray}}\left[\dim(S^{\llbracket\nu,\lambda\rrbracket})-3\right]-r_{\lambda}.

If
\nu\prec\lambda
with
\nu=\nu^{*}
, then
\dim(S^{\llbracket\nu,\lambda\rrbracket})\geq 3
by Lemma
5.3.4
, and evidently
r_{\lambda}<|\lambda|=\frac{n}{2}
. One has

\textstyle\dim(V)=\dim(S^{\llbracket\lambda,+\rrbracket})=\frac{1}{2}\cdot\binom{n}{n/2}\cdot\dim(S^{\lambda})\cdot\dim(S^{\lambda})\geq\frac{1}{2}\cdot n\cdot 2\cdot 2=2n,

and hence
2\cdot\rk(\mathfrak{h}^{V})\geq 2n-r_{\lambda}>2n-\frac{n}{2}=\frac{3n}{2}
. This implies that

\textstyle 4\cdot\rk(\mathfrak{g}^{V})\geq 4\cdot\rk(\mathfrak{h}^{V})>\frac{3n}{2}+\left(\dim(V)-\frac{n}{2}\right)=n+\dim(V)>\dim(V).

Now either
\dim(V)<2\cdot\rk(\mathfrak{g}^{V})
, or
2n\leq\dim(V)<4\cdot\rk(\mathfrak{g}^{V})
. Suppose the latter. Since

\textstyle\dim(V)=\frac{1}{2}\cdot\binom{n}{n/2}\cdot\dim(S^{\lambda})\cdot\dim(S^{\lambda})\geq\frac{1}{2}\cdot\binom{n}{n/2}\cdot 2\cdot 2=2\cdot\binom{n}{n/2}\geq 2\cdot\binom{6}{3}=40,

this would imply by
[
12
, Lemme 14]
that either
\mathfrak{g}^{V}\cong\mathfrak{so}(V)
or
\mathfrak{g}^{V}\cong\mathfrak{sp}(V)
, i.e., that
\mathfrak{g}^{V}
is a simple Lie algebra of type C or D and
V
is isomorphic to the natural module for
\mathfrak{g}^{V}
. But the natural modules for
\mathfrak{so}(V)
and
\mathfrak{sp}(V)
are self-dual as Lie algebra representations, whereas we know that
V\not\cong V^{*,\Lie}
by Proposition
5.5.4
, Lemma
5.5.5
, and Lemma
2.4.1
. Thus it must be the case that
\dim(V)<2\cdot\rk(\mathfrak{g}^{V})
, and hence
\mathfrak{g}^{V}=\mathfrak{sl}(V)
by
[
12
, Lemme 13]
.

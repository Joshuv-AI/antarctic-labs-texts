hei Existence of extremal functions and Wulff symmetry for anisotropic Trudinger-Moser inequalities
Kaiwen Guo, Yanjun Liu

Abstract




Abstract:
In this paper, we investigate the extremal functions for anisotropic Trudinger-Moser inequalities. Our method uses convex symmetrization, the continuity of the supremum function, together with the relation between the supremums of the subcritical and the critical anisotropic Trudinger-Moser inequality, finally, we give some results of existence and symmetry about the extremal functions for several different types of anisotropic Trudinger-Moser inequalities.

Keywords:
Trudinger-Moser inequalities; Anisotropy and singularity; Wulff symmetric function; Existence of maximizers

In this section, we provide some preliminary information that will be used in our proofs.

Let
F:\mathbb{R}^{N}\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
be a convex function of class
C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\{0\})
, which is even and positively homogeneous of degree 1, then there holds

F(tx)=\lvert t\rvert F(x)\;\;\mathrm{for}\;\mathrm{any}\;t\in\mathbb{R},\;x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

We further assume
F(x)>0
for any

and

is positive definite in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\{0\}
, which leading
Hess(F^{N})
is positive definite in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\{0\}

[
64
]
. There are two constants

such that
a\lvert x\rvert\leq F(x)\leq b\lvert x\rvert
for any

and a typical example of
F
is
F(x):=(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\lvert x_{i}\rvert^{q})^{\frac{1}{q}}
for

Considering the minimization problem

\min_{u}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}F(\nabla u)^{N}\;\mathrm{d}x,

its Euler-Lagrange equation contains an operator of the form

Q_{N}(u):=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}(F(\nabla u)^{N-1}F_{x_{i}}(\nabla u)),

which is called as
N
-anisotropic Laplacian or
N
-Finsler Laplacian.

Let
F^{o}
be the support function of
K:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:F(x)\leq 1\}
, which is defined by

F^{o}(x):=\sup_{\xi\in K}\langle x,\xi\rangle,

then
F^{o}:\mathbb{R}^{N}\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
is also a convex, positively homogeneous function of class
C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\{0\})
.

From
[
5
]
,
F^{o}
is dual to
F
in the sense that

F^{o}(x)=\sup_{\xi\neq 0}\frac{\langle x,\xi\rangle}{F(\xi)},\;F(x)=\sup_{\xi\neq 0}\frac{\langle x,\xi\rangle}{F^{o}(\xi)}.

Consider a map
\Phi:S^{N-1}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{N}
satisfying
\Phi(\xi)=\nabla F(\xi)
. Its image
\Phi(S^{N-1})
is a smooth, convex hypersurface in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, which is known as the Wulff shape or equilibrium crystal shape of
F
. As a result,
\Phi(S^{N-1})=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}|F^{o}(x)=1\}
(see
[
62
]
, Proposition 1). Denote
W_{r}^{x_{0}}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:F^{o}(x-x_{0})\leq r\}
, then we call
W_{r}
as a Wulff ball with radius

and center at the origin and denote
\kappa_{N}
as the Lebesgue measure of
W_{1}
.

Accordingly, we provide some simple properties of
F
, as a direct consequence of assumption on
F
, also found in
[

,
24
,
62
,
63
]

Lemma 2.1
.

There are

(i)

\lvert F(x)-F(y)\rvert\leq F(x+y)\leq F(x)+F(y)
;

(ii)

\frac{1}{C}\leq\lvert\nabla F(x)\rvert\leq C
and
\frac{1}{C}\leq\lvert\nabla F^{o}(x)\rvert\leq C
for some
C>0
and any
x\neq 0
;

(iii)

\langle x,\nabla F(x)\rangle=F(x),\langle x,\nabla F^{o}(x)\rangle=F^{o}(x)

(iv)

\nabla F(tx)=sgn(t)\nabla F(x)
for any

(v)

\int\limits_{\partial W_{r}}\frac{1}{|\nabla F^{o}(x)|}\;\mathrm{d}\sigma=N\kappa_{N}r^{N-1}

The convex symmetrization defined in
[

]
will be used later, which generalizes the Schwarz symmetrization in

\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}

The convex symmetrization of
u
with respect to
F
is defined by

u^{\star}(x)=u^{\sharp}\left(\kappa_{N}F^{o}(x)^{N}\right),\;x\in\Omega^{\star},

where
\kappa_{N}F^{o}(x)^{N}
is the Lebesgue measure of
W_{F^{o}(x)}
and
\Omega^{\star}
is a Wulff ball centered at the origin having the same Lebesgue measure as
\Omega
. Recalling the Schwarz symmetrization of

is defined by

u^{\diamond}(x)=u^{\sharp}(\omega_{N}\lvert x\rvert^{N}),\;\;\;x\in\Omega^{\diamond},

where
\omega_{N}\lvert x\rvert^{N}
is the Lebesgue measure of
B_{\lvert x\rvert}
and
\Omega^{\diamond}
is a Euclidean ball centered at the origin having the same Lebesgue measure as
\Omega

F=\lvert\cdot\rvert
.

Moreover, if

is strictly decreasing and Wulff symmetric, then there is equality if and only if
g=g^{\star}
.

Next, we prove some lemmas that will be used.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
\lvert\Omega\rvert<+\infty
. Assume that

f_{n}\rightarrow f\;a.e.\;in\;\Omega

and there exists
q>1
such that
f_{n}
is uniformly bounded in
L^{q}(\Omega)
,
f\in L^{q}(\Omega)
. Then

f_{n}\rightarrow f\;in\;L^{1}(\Omega).

Proof.

For any
\epsilon>0
, by Egorov’s theorem, there exists a measurable set
E\subset\Omega
such that

f_{n}\rightarrow f\;\mathrm{uniformly}\;\mathrm{in}\;E\;\mathrm{and}\;\lvert\Omega\backslash E\rvert<\epsilon.

Thus,

\int\limits_{E}\lvert f_{n}-f\rvert\;\mathrm{d}x\rightarrow 0.

On the other hand, by Holder’s inequality,

\int\limits_{\Omega\backslash E}\lvert f_{n}-f\rvert\;\mathrm{d}x\leq\bigg(\int\limits_{\Omega\backslash E}\lvert f_{n}-f\rvert^{q}\;\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{1/q}\bigg(\int\limits_{\Omega\backslash E}1^{q^{\ast}}\;\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{1/q^{\ast}}\leq C\epsilon^{1/q^{\ast}},

where
\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{q^{\ast}}=1
. Therefore,
f_{n}\rightarrow f
in
L^{1}(\Omega)
.
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
p>q(1-\frac{\beta}{N})
if
\beta\neq 0
and
p\geq q
if
\beta=0
. Then

\displaystyle\mathrm{ATMSC}(N,p,q,\lambda,\beta)

\displaystyle=\sup\limits_{\|F(\nabla u)\|_{N}=\|u\|_{q}=1}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\exp(\lambda(1-\frac{\beta}{N})\lvert u\rvert^{\frac{N}{N-1}})\lvert u\rvert^{p}}{F^{o}(x)^{\beta}}\;\mathrm{d}x,

\displaystyle\mathrm{ATMSC}(N,q,\lambda,\beta)

\displaystyle=\sup\limits_{\|F(\nabla u)\|_{N}=\|u\|_{q}=1}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\Phi_{N,q,\beta}(\lambda(1-\frac{\beta}{N})\lvert u\rvert^{\frac{N}{N-1}})}{F^{o}(x)^{\beta}}\;\mathrm{d}x.

Proof.

We just prove the first identity. For any
u\in D^{N,q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
with
\|F(\nabla u)\|_{N}\leq 1
, let

v(x)=\frac{u(tx)}{\|F(\nabla u)\|_{N}},\;t=\left(\frac{\|u\|_{q}}{\|F(\nabla u)\|_{N}}\right)^{q/N},

where we can easily exclude the case
\|F(\nabla u)\|_{N}=0
. There are

\|F(\nabla v)\|_{N}=1,\;\|v\|_{q}=1,

and

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\exp(\lambda(1-\frac{\beta}{N})\lvert v\rvert^{\frac{N}{N-1}})\lvert v\rvert^{p}}{F^{o}(x)^{\beta}}\;\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\|F(\nabla u)\|_{N}^{p}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\exp\left(\lambda(1-\frac{\beta}{N})(\frac{\lvert u(tx)\rvert}{\|F(\nabla u)\|_{N}})^{\frac{N}{N-1}}\right)\frac{\lvert u(tx)\rvert^{p}}{F^{o}(x)^{\beta}}\;\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\|F(\nabla u)\|_{N}^{p}}\frac{1}{t^{N-\beta}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\exp\left(\lambda(1-\frac{\beta}{N})(\frac{\lvert u(x)\rvert}{\|F(\nabla u)\|_{N}})^{\frac{N}{N-1}}\right)\frac{\lvert u(x)\rvert^{p}}{F^{o}(x)^{\beta}}\;\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\|F(\nabla u)\|_{N}^{p-q(1-\frac{\beta}{N})}}\frac{1}{\|u\|_{q}^{q(1-\frac{\beta}{N})}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\exp\left(\lambda(1-\frac{\beta}{N})(\frac{\lvert u(x)\rvert}{\|F(\nabla u)\|_{N}})^{\frac{N}{N-1}}\right)\frac{\lvert u(x)\rvert^{p}}{F^{o}(x)^{\beta}}\;\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\|u\|_{q}^{q(1-\frac{\beta}{N})}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\exp(\lambda(1-\frac{\beta}{N})\lvert u\rvert^{\frac{N}{N-1}})\lvert u\rvert^{p}}{F^{o}(x)^{\beta}}\;\mathrm{d}x.

∎

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
a>0
and
b>0
. Then

\Lambda_{a,b}(N,q,\lambda,\beta)=\sup\limits_{\|F(\nabla u)\|_{N}^{a}+\|u\|_{q}^{b}=1}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\Phi_{N,q,\beta}(\lambda(1-\frac{\beta}{N})\lvert u\rvert^{\frac{N}{N-1}})}{F^{o}(x)^{\beta}}\;\mathrm{d}x.

Proof.

For any
u\in D^{N,q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
with
\|F(\nabla u)\|_{N}^{a}+\|u\|_{q}^{b}\leq 1
, we can choose some
c\geq 1
such that
c^{a}\|F(\nabla u)\|_{N}^{a}+c^{b}\|u\|_{q}^{b}=1
. There is

\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\Phi_{N,q,\beta}(\lambda(1-\frac{\beta}{N})\lvert cu\rvert^{\frac{N}{N-1}})}{F^{o}(x)^{\beta}}\;\mathrm{d}x\geq\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\Phi_{N,q,\beta}(\lambda(1-\frac{\beta}{N})\lvert u\rvert^{\frac{N}{N-1}})}{F^{o}(x)^{\beta}}\;\mathrm{d}x.

∎

Proof.

It is enough to show

\displaystyle\varlimsup\limits_{t\rightarrow 0}\left(\frac{1-(\frac{t}{\lambda})^{a\frac{N-1}{N}}}{(\frac{t}{\lambda})^{b\frac{N-1}{N}}}\right)^{\frac{q}{b}(1-\frac{\beta}{N})}f(t)<g_{a,b}(\lambda)

(4.1)

and

\displaystyle\varlimsup\limits_{t\rightarrow\lambda}\left(\frac{1-(\frac{t}{\lambda})^{a\frac{N-1}{N}}}{(\frac{t}{\lambda})^{b\frac{N-1}{N}}}\right)^{\frac{q}{b}(1-\frac{\beta}{N})}f(t)<g_{a,b}(\lambda).

(4.2)

Indeed, if (
4.1
) and (
4.2
) are true, then there is
t_{\lambda}\in(0,\lambda)
such that

\left(\frac{1-(\frac{t_{\lambda}}{\lambda})^{a\frac{N-1}{N}}}{(\frac{t_{\lambda}}{\lambda})^{b\frac{N-1}{N}}}\right)^{\frac{q}{b}(1-\frac{\beta}{N})}f(t_{\lambda})=g_{a,b}(\lambda).

Let
u_{\lambda}
be a maximizer of
\mathrm{ATMSC}(N,q,t_{\lambda},\beta)
and set

v_{\lambda}(x):=\left(\frac{t_{\lambda}}{\lambda}\right)^{\frac{N-1}{N}}u_{\lambda}(\gamma x),

where

\gamma=\left(\frac{(\frac{t_{\lambda}}{\lambda})^{b\frac{N-1}{N}}\|u_{\lambda}\|_{q}^{b}}{1-(\frac{t_{\lambda}}{\lambda})^{a\frac{N-1}{N}}}\right)^{\frac{q}{Nb}}.

Then
v_{\lambda}
is a maximizer of
\Lambda_{a,b}(N,q,\lambda,\beta)
. On the other hand, let
v_{\lambda}
be a maximizer of
\Lambda_{a,b}(N,q,\lambda,\beta)
and set

u_{\lambda}(x):=\frac{v_{\lambda}(x)}{\|F(\nabla v_{\lambda})\|_{N}}.

Then
u_{\lambda}
is a maximizer of
\mathrm{ATMSC}(N,q,\lambda\|F(\nabla v_{\lambda})\|_{N}^{\frac{N}{N-1}},\beta)
.

Now, we show (
4.1
) and (
4.2
). Firstly, since

\varlimsup\limits_{t\rightarrow\lambda}\left(\frac{1-(\frac{t}{\lambda})^{a\frac{N-1}{N}}}{(\frac{t}{\lambda})^{b\frac{N-1}{N}}}\right)^{\frac{q}{b}(1-\frac{\beta}{N})}f(t)=0,

(
4.2
) is obvious. Thus, we only need to study

\varlimsup\limits_{t\rightarrow 0}\left(\frac{1-(\frac{t}{\lambda})^{a\frac{N-1}{N}}}{(\frac{t}{\lambda})^{b\frac{N-1}{N}}}\right)^{\frac{q}{b}(1-\frac{\beta}{N})}f(t).

Let
t_{n}\rightarrow 0^{+}
and
u_{n}\in D^{N,q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
be Wulff symmetric with

\|F(\nabla u_{n})\|_{N}=1,\;\|u_{n}\|_{q}=1,

f(t_{n})=\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\Phi_{N,q,\beta}(t_{n}(1-\frac{\beta}{N})\lvert u_{n}\rvert^{\frac{N}{N-1}})}{F^{o}(x)^{\beta}}\;\mathrm{d}x.

Case 1:

\beta>0
.

Notice that

\displaystyle f(t_{n})

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{j=j_{N,q,\beta}}^{+\infty}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{t_{n}^{j}(1-\frac{\beta}{N})^{j}\lvert u_{n}\rvert^{\frac{N}{N-1}j}}{j!F^{o}(x)^{\beta}}\;\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{t_{n}^{j_{N,q,\beta}}(1-\frac{\beta}{N})^{j_{N,q,\beta}}\lvert u_{n}\rvert^{\frac{N}{N-1}j_{N,q,\beta}}}{j_{N,q,\beta}!F^{o}(x)^{\beta}}\;\mathrm{d}x+\sum\limits_{j=j_{N,q,\beta}+1}^{+\infty}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{t_{n}^{j}(1-\frac{\beta}{N})^{j}\lvert u_{n}\rvert^{\frac{N}{N-1}j}}{j!F^{o}(x)^{\beta}}\;\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\frac{t_{n}^{j_{N,q,\beta}}}{j_{N,q,\beta}!}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\lvert u_{n}\rvert^{\frac{N}{N-1}j_{N,q,\beta}}}{F^{o}(x)^{\beta}}\;\mathrm{d}x+t_{n}^{j_{N,q,\beta}+1}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\exp(t_{n}\lvert u_{n}\rvert^{\frac{N}{N-1}})\lvert u_{n}\rvert^{\frac{N}{N-1}(j_{N,q,\beta}+1)}}{F^{o}(x)^{\beta}}\;\mathrm{d}x.

Then by Theorem
A
,

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1-(\frac{t_{n}}{\lambda})^{a\frac{N-1}{N}}}{(\frac{t_{n}}{\lambda})^{b\frac{N-1}{N}}}\right)^{\frac{q}{b}(1-\frac{\beta}{N})}f(t_{n})\rightarrow 0.

Case 2:

\beta=0
and
\frac{q(N-1)}{N}\not\in\mathbb{N}
.

Similarly as in the first case.

Case 3:

\beta=0
and
\frac{q(N-1)}{N}\in\mathbb{N}
.

By Theorem
A
and Lemma
2.4
,

\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\exp(t_{n}\lvert u_{n}\rvert^{\frac{N}{N-1}})\lvert u_{n}\rvert^{q}\;\mathrm{d}x\rightarrow 1.

Then

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1-(\frac{t_{n}}{\lambda})^{a\frac{N-1}{N}}}{(\frac{t_{n}}{\lambda})^{b\frac{N-1}{N}}}\right)^{\frac{q}{b}}f(t_{n})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1-(\frac{t_{n}}{\lambda})^{a\frac{N-1}{N}}}{(\frac{t_{n}}{\lambda})^{b\frac{N-1}{N}}}\right)^{\frac{q}{b}}\frac{t_{n}^{\frac{q(N-1)}{N}}}{\left(\frac{q(N-1)}{N}\right)!}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\exp(t_{n}\lvert u_{n}\rvert^{\frac{N}{N-1}})\lvert u_{n}\rvert^{q}\;\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda^{\frac{q(N-1)}{N}}}{\left(\frac{q(N-1)}{N}\right)!}.

Thus,

\varlimsup\limits_{t\rightarrow 0}\left(\frac{1-(\frac{t}{\lambda})^{a\frac{N-1}{N}}}{(\frac{t}{\lambda})^{b\frac{N-1}{N}}}\right)^{\frac{q}{b}(1-\frac{\beta}{N})}f(t)\leq\frac{\lambda^{\frac{q(N-1)}{N}}}{\left(\frac{q(N-1)}{N}\right)!}.

Hence, (
4.1
) holds when

g_{a,b}(\lambda)>\frac{\lambda^{\frac{q(N-1)}{N}}}{\left(\frac{q(N-1)}{N}\right)!}.

Therefore,
\Lambda_{a,b}(N,q,\lambda,0)
is not attained only if

g_{a,b}(\lambda)\leq\frac{\lambda^{\frac{q(N-1)}{N}}}{\left(\frac{q(N-1)}{N}\right)!}.

∎

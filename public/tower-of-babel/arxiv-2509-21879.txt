Zubov-Net: Adaptive Stability for Neural ODEs Reconciling Accuracy with Robustness
Chaoyang Luo, Yan Zou, Nanjing Huang*

Abstract
Despite neural ordinary differential equations (Neural ODEs) exhibiting intrinsic robustness under input perturbations due to their dynamical systems nature, recent approaches often involve imposing Lyapunov-based stability conditions to provide formal robustness guarantees. However, a fundamental challenge remains: the tension between robustness and accuracy, primarily stemming from the difficulty in imposing appropriate stability conditions. To address this, we propose an adaptive stable learning framework named Zubov-Net, which innovatively reformulates Zubov’s equation into a consistency characterization between regions of attraction (RoAs) and prescribed RoAs (PRoAs). Building on this consistency, we introduce a new paradigm for actively controlling the geometry of RoAs by directly optimizing PRoAs to reconcile accuracy and robustness. Our approach is realized through tripartite losses (consistency, classification, and separation losses) and a parallel boundary sampling algorithm that co-optimizes the Neural ODE and the Lyapunov function. To enhance the discriminativity of Lyapunov functions, we design an input-attention-based convex neural network via a softmax attention mechanism that focuses on equilibrium-relevant features and also serves as weight normalization to maintain training stability in deep architectures. Theoretically, we prove that minimizing the tripartite loss guarantees consistent alignment of PRoAs-RoAs, trajectory stability, and non-overlapping PRoAs. Moreover, we establish stochastic convex separability with tighter probability bounds and fewer dimensionality requirements to justify the convex design in Lyapunov functions. Experimentally, Zubov-Net maintains high classification accuracy while significantly improving robustness against various stochastic noises and adversarial attacks.

The fundamental limitation across existing paradigms, whether pre-defining a rigid PRoA or treating every clean point as an equilibrium, lies in the inappropriate application of stability conditions. This inevitably results in over- or under-constrained stabilization, creating a tension between prediction accuracy and robustness. To bridge this gap, we first introduce a novel perspective grounded in Zubov’s theory by reformulating its core equation (a PDE that precisely characterizes RoAs) to measure the consistency between PRoAs and RoAs. Then, the reformulation allows us to design a consistency loss that dynamically aligns the PRoA (defined by the Lyapunov function) with the RoA (of the Neural ODE), enabling the stabilization region to adaptively evolve during training.

This consistency matching naturally prompts us to attempt a new paradigm: instead of merely certifying the RoA, we actively control its geometry by manipulating the PRoA. In this paper, we optimize the PRoA with a classification loss to guide data-driven trajectories into the RoA of the correspoding category and with a separation loss to ensure well-separated RoAs, for the accuracy-robustness tension. While Zubov’s theory has recently shown scalability in learning Lyapunov functions for verifying the ROA

We propose Zubov-Net, a novel stable learning framework that integrates Zubov’s equation to resolve PRoAs-RoAs misalignment through the consistency characterization and the active RoAs-controlling paradigm.

•

We designed an Input-Attention-based Convex Neural Network (IACNN) to enhance the discriminativity of Lyapunov functions and proposed tripartite losses alongside a parallel boundary sampling algorithm to co-train Neural ODEs and Lyapunov functions.

•

Theoretically, we show that minimizing the tripartite losses will ensure PRoAs-RoAs alignment, trajectory stability, and non-overlapping PRoAs. By developing the stochastic convex separability with tighter probability bounds and fewer dimensionality requirements, we justify the convex design in Lyapunov functions.

Experimentally, Zubov-Net achieves superior prediction accuracy alongside significantly enhanced robustness against diverse perturbations.

II-A

Additional Details on Neural ODEs

Neural ODEs.
The Neural ODE uses a neural network to parameterize a differential equation that governs the hidden states
h(t)\in H\subset\mathbb{R}^{d_{h}}
with respect to time
t

\displaystyle h_{x}(0)=\phi\left({x;{\theta_{\phi}}}\right),

(1)

\displaystyle h_{x}(T)=h_{x}(0)+\int_{0}^{T}f(h_{x}(s);{\theta_{f}}){\mkern 1.0mu}ds,

(2)

\displaystyle\hat{y}_{x}=\psi\left({h_{x}\left(T\right);{C_{\psi}}}\right),

(3)

where
\phi:\mathbb{R}^{d_{x}}\to\mathbb{R}^{d_{h}}
and

are the input and output functions, respectively. The system evolves over time

to produce the final prediction

. Throughout this paper, we concentrate on robust image classification with
L
categories, where
x
represents the input image.

Notably, a recent study
[
18
]
highlights that Lyapunov-stable equilibrium points of different classes may be close, resulting in small stable regions and poor adversarial defense. To address this, we use the method outlined in
[
18
]
to set the output function as

is an

are determined by minimizing the maximum cosine similarity between different classes

Model Assumption.
We impose a Lipschitz continuity assumption on neural networks used in Neural ODEs as follows.

Assumption 1
.

For any neural network described by
F(\cdot;\theta_{F}):\mathbb{R}^{d_{x}}\to\mathbb{R}^{d_{F}}
with parameter
\theta_{F}
, there is a constant
L_{F}>0
such that
|F(x;\theta_{F})-F(x^{\prime};\theta_{F})|\leq L_{F}|x-x^{\prime}|
for all
x,x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{x}}
.

This assumption is generally satisfied since neural networks with activation functions (such as tanh, ReLU, and sigmoid functions) generally satisfy the Lipschitz continuity condition
[
24
,
25
]
. Moreover, we assume that the state space
H\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is compact and path-connected, which is crucial for ensuring the existence of a Lyapunov function.

II-C

Adversarial Defense Methods

Since the security issues of deep neural networks (DNNs) were raised
[
5
,
6
]
, researchers have been committed to developing models with adversarial robustness
[

,
8

[

[

]

Additionally, adversarial training (AT), which enhances the robustness of DNNs by incorporating adversarial perturbations into training samples, has been an active research direction in the machine learning
[
33
,
34
]
. However, AT may damage the decision boundary, reducing the model’s accuracy on clean samples.

Recently, many studies have shown that Neural ODEs are more robust than convolutional neural networks
[
9
,
11
]
. Consequently, it has become a new trend to design defense models by incorporating stability theory into Neural ODEs. For instance, Huang et al. proposed a provably stable architecture that achieves non-adversarial robustness even without training on adversarial examples
[

While these methods enhance robustness, they often grapple with the inherent trade-off between accuracy and robustness. Our work addresses this core tension by integrating Zubov’s theoretical framework to ensure a consistent alignment between PRoAs and RoAs, thereby implicitly securing the maximal stable region.

III
Methodology

In this section, we detail our novel adaptive stable learning framework for PRoAs-RoAs alignment and illustrate the Zubov-Net architecture in Figure

\mathcal{L}_{cla}

Intuitively, Proposition

Classification Losses and Layers.

While Proposition
3
and
4
demonstrate consistency and non-overlapping among PRoAs, they do not guarantee trajectory stability for finite-time integration. Specifically, for input
(x,y)
with true label
y

\mathcal{L}_{con}=0
alone cannot ensure that
\left\|h_{x}(t)-c_{y}\right\|
is converging to
0
as
t\to\infty

\left\{{{h_{x}}\left(t\right);t\in\left[{0.T}\right]}\right\}\subseteq D_{W_{y}}

This limitation arises because Zubov’s theorem addresses asymptotic stability (
t\to\infty

T

[0,T]

W_{i}

(x,y)\in\mathbb{D}
denote the input data pair and
h_{x}(\cdot)
is the solution of the system of Equations (

)-(
3

\displaystyle\mathbf{W}(h_{x}(T))=\left[W_{1}(h_{x}(T)),\dots,W_{L}(h_{x}(T))\right]^{\top},

\displaystyle\tilde{\psi}\left({{h_{x}}\left(T\right);{C_{\psi}}}\right)=\frac{\mathbf{W}(h_{x}(T))^{\circ-1}-\alpha\mathbf{1}}{\mathbf{1}^{\top}\left(\mathbf{W}(h_{x}(T))^{\circ-1}-\alpha\mathbf{1}\right)},

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{cla}(\hat{\mathbf{y}}_{x},y)=-\log\left(\hat{\mathbf{y}}_{x}[y]\right),\quad\hat{\mathbf{y}}_{x}=\tilde{\psi}\left({h_{x}\left(T\right);{C_{\psi}}}\right),

where
\alpha\in[0,1)
is a stability margin parameter,
\mathbf{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{L}
is an all-ones vector, and

denotes the element-wise inverse. This design implies that minimizing
\mathcal{L}_{cla}
enables
W_{y}(h_{x}(T))\to 0
(attracting to

Despite Proposition

Firstly, we denote the parameters being learned during the training process
as
\theta
, including:

•

.

•

\theta_{W}
.

C_{\psi}
.

Mathematically, to find the desired
\theta^{*}
, we need to use an optimization problem to minimize the objective loss function, which can be viewed as the following optimal control problem (for a dataset
\mathbb{D}
):

\displaystyle\mathop{\arg\min}_{\theta\in\Theta}\quad

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{(x,y)\sim\mathbb{D}}\left[\mathcal{L}_{cla}(\hat{\mathbf{y}}_{x},y)+\lambda_{1}\mathcal{L}_{FC}(\hat{y}_{x},y)\right]

\displaystyle+\lambda_{2}\mathcal{L}_{con}(\theta_{f},\theta_{W})+\lambda_{3}\mathcal{L}_{sep}(\theta_{W})

(11)

s.t.

\displaystyle h_{x}(0)=\phi\left({x;{\theta_{\phi}}}\right),

\displaystyle h_{x}(T)=h_{x}(0)+\int_{0}^{T}f(h_{x}(s);{\theta_{f}}){\mkern 1.0mu}ds,

\displaystyle\hat{y}_{x}=\psi\left({h_{x}\left(T\right);{C_{\psi}}}\right),\quad\hat{\mathbf{y}}_{x}=\tilde{\psi}\left({h_{x}\left(T\right);{C_{\psi}}}\right),

where
\lambda_{i=1,2,3}
are the weights of the multiple losses, and
\mathcal{L}_{FC}
is the standard cross-entropy loss function with the FC layer’s parameter
C_{\psi}
. In addition, the losses
\mathcal{L}_{con}
and
\mathcal{L}_{sep}
serve as regularization terms that promote consistency between PRoAs and RoAs while controlling the separation between RoAs of different categories.

For optimizing Equation (
11
), we compute the dynamics via solver backpropagation or the adjoint method
[
9
,
40
]
, with implementation details provided in Algorithm
2
. Unlike prior stability-enforcing methods that rely on uniform sampling over the entire PRoA
[
16
,

]
, our approach efficiently generates counterexamples using computationally cheaper PGD. The results demonstrate that these counterexamples sufficiently guide the training process, which is consistent with similar observations in the machine learning community
[

]
.

Experiments

This section presents comprehensive experiments to evaluate Zubov-Net’s performance and robustness, with a primary focus on comparisons within the stable Neural ODE framework. We specifically address the following research questions

•

RQ1:
Does Zubov-Net maintain high clean classification accuracy while simultaneously improving robustness against various stochastic noises compared to stable Neural ODE baselines?

•

RQ2:
Does Zubov-Net achieve significantly improved adversarial robustness among stable Neural ODEs across diverse adversarial attacks and under different perturbation radii?

•

IV-B

Accuracy and robustness against stochastic noises

The robustness of Zubov-Net is evaluated under eight stochastic noise perturbations on SVHN, CIFAR-10, and CIFAR-100. Table
I
presents the detailed results, with the average accuracy (Avg) serving as the key metric for overall robustness.

Zubov-Net achieves the highest average accuracy across all three datasets, indicating superior overall robustness. Notably, it outperforms the strongest baseline (SODEF) by 0.24%, 1.11%, and 1.53% on SVHN, CIFAR-10, and CIFAR-100, respectively. This performance gap widens with increasing task complexity (from 10 to 100 classes) and under the most disruptive noise types (e.g., Gaussian, Impulse). For instance, on CIFAR-100, Zubov-Net shows substantial improvements in Gaussian noise (3.36% over SODEF) and Impulse noise (2.4%).

These results highlight a key achievement of Zubov-Net: maintaining high classification accuracy on clean data while simultaneously achieving superior robustness against diverse perturbations. This demonstrates that our method effectively reconciles the inherent tension between accuracy and robustness. The adaptive stability governed by the Zubov equation allows the model to learn a dynamics system that is both highly discriminative for standard classification and inherently stable against random noise, without compromising one for the other.

The adversarial robustness of Zubov-Net is evaluated against five white-box attacks under two perturbation radii. As shown in Table

The performance advantage is most substantial on CIFAR-10, where Zubov-Net outperforms the strongest baseline (FxTS-Net) by a significant margin of 6.17%. The observed robustness gains are rooted in the successful implementation of our paradigm (actively controlling the RoA geometry), which is embodied by the tripartite loss. Each component plays a critical role: the consistency loss dynamically maintains alignment, eliminating the misalignment that adversaries exploit; the classification loss certifies that trajectories remain within safe attraction basins; the separation loss actively shapes the geometry of these basins, enforcing clear boundaries.

To further evaluate the effectiveness and practicality of our framework, we conduct ablation experiments on CIFAR-10 from two complementary perspectives: (i) the necessity of individual loss components, and (ii) the impact of different weight configurations on performance.

V
Conclusion

This paper proposes Zubov-Net to confront the accuracy-robustness trade-off by addressing its root cause: the inappropriate application of stability conditions. Specifically, we offer a novel perspective on Zubov’s theorem by reformulating it as a consistency characterization for aligning the prescribed and true regions of attraction. This consistency, in turn, enables a new paradigm that the RoA geometry is actively governed by optimizing the PRoA, rather than merely certifying a fixed stable region. To instantiate this paradigm, we design a tripartite loss and a parallel boundary sampling algorithm to co-optimize the Neural ODE and a Lyapunov function, the latter implemented via a Input-Attention-based Convex Neural Network (IACNN) to enhance the discriminativity of Lyapunov functions.

Theoretically, we provide rigorous guarantees that our method ensures consistent PRoAs-RoAs alignment, formal trajectory containment within the certified PRoA, and non-overlapping PRoAs. Furthermore, by adopting a point-value separation strategy, we achieve tighter probability bounds and lower dimensionality requirements, proving that finite datasets exhibit convex separability with high probability. This provides a theoretical foundation for the convex design of our Lyapunov functions. Extensive experiments on SVHN, CIFAR-10, and CIFAR-100 demonstrate that Zubov-Net achieves high classification accuracy on clean data while significantly improving robustness against a wide spectrum of perturbations.

Looking forward, future work should focus on two key challenges: mitigating the computational overhead from boundary sampling and extending our theoretical guarantees to broader classes of non-linearities. Despite these challenges, Zubov-Net establishes a strong baseline by introducing two key innovations: a novel consistency criterion based on Zubov’s equation, and a paradigm of actively governing RoAs through PRoA optimization. This foundation not only allows other defensive paradigms (e.g., adversarial training, purification) to be integrated but also forms a general framework applicable to diverse safety-critical domains beyond image classification, including robust time-series forecasting and secure reinforcement learning.

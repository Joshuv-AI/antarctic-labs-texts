Plausible universality of uniaxial order in self-assembly of cross junctions in space dimension \mth d≥3
Kazuya Saito

Abstract
We consider the self-assembly of cross junctions in a general space dimension ( d d ) as an extension of the problem studied in a previous paper for d = 3 d=3 . This problem is equivalent to constructing a d d -dimensional hypercubic jungle gym, at all junctions of which 2 ​ d 2d rods with different colours meet. The analysis reveals a unique feature of the d = 3 d=3 case: the forced presence of at least one perfectly-ordered (singly coloured) direction (axis), in contrast to the possible absence of such a direction in d ≥ 4 d\geq 4 . However, we will show that the uniaxial order is overwhelming not only in d = 3 d=3 but also for d ≥ 4 d\geq 4 in a sufficiently large system.

Setting

We express the number of possible

Obviously,

The behavior of

Although this is not done in this study,

[

]

placing spins on the vertices of the regular cross-polytopes
[

hypercubic unit cells in dimension

As the interacting spins hate states with the same colour,
the state with the minimum energy of a cross junction has spin configurations
in which the diagonal pairs of vertices share the same colour.
In this respect,
the present problem is equivalent to characterising the physical ground state of the corresponding spin models.
An example of
d=3
was reported in

with additional details for incompletely self-assembled states.

4
Uniaxial order as the most plausible case in
d\geq 3

We consider the issue of what is the most probable number of completely ordered axes in
general dimensions.
In other words, what is the distribution of
\langle n\rangle
-states depending on the space dimension
d
.
Although we cannot fully rationalise the present discussion at present,
it plausibly leads us to the conjecture of

V_{d}\sim v(1;d)

(6)

for
d\geq 3
in the large
L

Equation
6
implies that a large ensemble of cross junctions in dimension
d\geq 3

is mostly uniaxial.

We consider the increase in the dimension by one:
(d-1)\rightarrow d
.
When
L
copies of a
\langle n-1\rangle_{d-1}
-state are stacked along the newly added dimension,
the resultant ensemble can be regarded as a
\langle n\rangle_{d}
-state.
Therefore, at least,
there are the same number of
\langle n\rangle_{d}
-states.
The obvious property of
v(d;d)=1
reflects this property.

On the other hand,
we always obtain a
\langle 1\rangle_{d}
-state
if we pick up a self-assembled ensemble of dimension
(d-1)
at random,
change its orientation in that dimension randomly, and stack it along the new axis.
Although the resultant ensembles in dimension
d
contain those with
n>1

n=d
,
they are always minor in comparison with those of
n=1
, as discussed below.
Therefore, the number of
\langle 1\rangle_{d}
-states (

v(1;d)\sim[(d-1)!\cdot V_{d-1}]^{L}

(7)

\langle 0\rangle_{d-1}
-states.

Note that the present construction of
\langle 1\rangle
-states encompasses all possible ones.

Similarly, we obtain

\{(d-2)!\cdot[V_{d-1}-v(0;d-1)]\}^{L},

5
Summary and open problems

Inspired by the recent identification of a counterintuitive conclusion
that octahedral junctions self-assemble into a uniaxially ordered state
[
15
]
,
we examined the issue of self-assembly of cross junctions in general dimensions.

The analysis showed that the forced presence of (at least) one completely ordered axis is
a unique property of the
d=3
case, arising from the impossibility of dividing
d
into
a sum of multiple integers larger than
1
.

For
d\geq 4
,
\langle 0\rangle
-state is possible.
In
d=4
, two distinct groups of such
\langle 0\rangle
-states were identified:
two couples of two colours are separately embedded, or four colours run in every direction.
Based on a naive construction of
\langle 0\rangle
-states starting from
d\geq 3
by adding dimension two,
which mimics the first group of
d=4
,
the overwhelming majority of self-assembled states have been proven to be uniaxial

\langle 1\rangle
-state.

It is open whether colour-splitting into groups is necessary for
\langle 0\rangle
-states at
d\geq 5
.
A possibility of constructing a variety of ensembles other than the assumed method
cannot be ruled out.
However, such construction contributes to
v(n;d)
only additively,
being harmless for the present discussion,
which is interested only in the largest group of self-assembled states.
Note that a symmetrically mixed
\langle 0\rangle
-state,
such as one shown in fig.
2
b for
d=4
,
is possible only for a very limited
d

because the number of edges of a hypercube in dimension
(d-1)
,
2^{d-2}\cdot(d-1)
,
must be divisible by
d
for success.

Finally, an emphasis is placed on the fact
that only completely self-assembled states are within the scope of
this Letter.
Allowing incompleteness may introduce an unexpected feature in the approach to complete states, with the minimum energy in the antiferroic
d
-state Potts models,
as exemplified by
[
15
]
for the
d=3
case.

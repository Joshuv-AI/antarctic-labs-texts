The splitting field and generators of the elliptic surface Y 2 = X 3 + t 360 + 1 Y^{2}=X^{3}+t^{360}+1
Sajad Salami

Abstract
. The splitting field of an elliptic surface ℰ / ℚ ⁡ ( t ) \mathcal{E}/\mathbb{Q}(t) is the smallest finite extension 𝒦 ⊂ ℂ \mathcal{K}\subset\mathbb{C} such that all ℂ ⁡ ( t ) \mathbb{C}(t) -rational points are defined over 𝒦 ⁡ ( t ) \mathcal{K}(t) . In this paper, we provide a symbolic algorithmic approach to determine the splitting field and a set of 68 68 linearly independent generators for the Mordell–Weil lattice of Shioda’s elliptic surface Y 2 = X 3 + t 360 + 1 Y^{2}=X^{3}+t^{360}+1 . This surface is noted for having the largest known rank 68 for an elliptic curve over ℂ ⁡ ( t ) \mathbb{C}(t) . Our methodology utilizes the known decomposition of the Mordell-Weil Lattice of this surface into Lattices of ten rational elliptic surfaces and one K ​ 3 K3 surface. We explicitly compute the defining polynomials of the splitting field, which reach degrees of 1728 and 5760, and verify the results via height pairing matrices and specialized symbolic software packages.

Let
{\mathcal{E}}
be an elliptic curve over
{\mathbb{C}}(t)

\phi:V\rightarrow V

(x,y)\mapsto\zeta_{3}\cdot P:=(\zeta_{3}x,-y)

which is a linear map satisfying
\phi^{6}=id

V
has a structure of a
{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{-3})

(a-b\zeta_{3})\cdot P=a\cdot P+b\cdot\phi(P)

This implies that if

Define the map

Denoting the eigen space corresponding to the eigenvalue

3.
Some results on the elliptic surfaces
{\mathcal{E}}_{a,b}

In this section, we provide some results on the family of elliptic surfaces defined over the rational function field
{\mathbb{Q}}(v)
by the Weierstrass equation:

(3.1)

{\mathcal{E}}_{a,b}:y^{2}=x^{3}+v^{a}(v^{b}+1)

where
0\leq a,b\leq 11
are coprime integers such that
1\leq a+b\leq 12
. This family is isotrivial with
j
-invariant identically zero.
If
a+b\leq 6
, then surface
{\mathcal{E}}_{a,b}
is a rational elliptic surface, and if

6<a+b\leq 12
, then it is an elliptic K3 surface.

3.1.
Birational map between
{\mathcal{E}}_{a,b}

We first establish that every surface in this family is birationally equivalent to a ”partner” surface obtained by inverting the base parameter.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
n=\lceil(a+b)/6\rceil
. The elliptic surface
{\mathcal{E}}_{a,b}
is birationally equivalent to the surface
{\mathcal{E}}_{a^{\prime},b}
where
a^{\prime}=6n-(a+b)
.

Proof.

We define a birational map
\phi:{\mathcal{E}}_{a,b}\dashrightarrow{\mathcal{E}}_{a^{\prime},b}
via a change of coordinates on the base
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
and a weighted change of coordinates on the fiber.
Let
v=u^{-1}
. Substituting this into (
3.1
):

y^{2}=x^{3}+u^{-a}(u^{-b}+1)=x^{3}+u^{-(a+b)}(1+u^{b}).

To return to a minimal integral Weierstrass equation, we scale the coordinates to clear the denominator. We multiply the equation by
u^{6n}
:

u^{6n}y^{2}=u^{6n}x^{3}+u^{6n-(a+b)}(1+u^{b}).

We define the new coordinates
(X,Y)
such that
Y^{2}=(u^{3n}y)^{2}
and
X^{3}=(u^{2n}x)^{3}
. This implies the transformation:

X=u^{2n}x,\quad Y=u^{3n}y,\quad u=v^{-1}.

The transformed equation is:

Y^{2}=X^{3}+u^{6n-(a+b)}(1+u^{b}),

which is precisely the defining equation for
{\mathcal{E}}_{a^{\prime},b}
with
a^{\prime}=6n-(a+b)
. Thus, the map

\phi(x,y,v)=(v^{2n},v^{3n}y,v^{-1})

is a
rational and admits the rational inverse

\phi^{-1}(X,Y,u)=(u^{-2n}X,u^{-3n}Y,u^{-1}).

the surfaces are birationally equivalent.
∎

3.2.
Singular Fibers and Mordell-Weil Lattice of Rational
{\mathcal{E}}_{a,b}

In this section, we determine the structure of the Mordell-Weil group
E({\mathbb{C}}(v))
as a lattice. Since the surfaces
{\mathcal{E}}_{a,b}

T\subset NS({\mathcal{E}}_{a,b})

[
11

The singular fibers are located at the zeroes of the discriminant, which are determined by the vanishing of the coefficient
B(v)=v^{a}(v^{b}+1)
. Let
n=\lceil(a+b)/6\rceil
. The minimal model has singular fibers potentially at
v=0

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
{\mathcal{E}}_{a,b}
be a rational elliptic surface in the family defined by (

11.2.
Computational Complexity

The determination of the splitting field
\mathcal{K}
represents a significant symbolic computation task. The field is constructed as the compositum of splitting fields
\mathcal{K}_{b}
calculated for each sub-surface.
More precisely, the fields
{\mathcal{K}}_{1}
,
{\mathcal{K}}_{2}
,
{\mathcal{K}}^{\prime}_{2}
,
{\mathcal{K}}_{3}
,
{\mathcal{K}}_{4}
,
{\mathcal{K}}_{5}
and
{\mathcal{K}}^{\prime}
that are defined by polynomials of degree 2, 16, 3, 27, 48, 120, and 196, provided in
[
9
, Gp-360]
.

The primary computational hurdles included:

•

Degree of Extensions
: The splitting field

is defined by two primary minimal polynomials of degree

and
5760
, given in
[
9
, Pol-1728]
and
[
9
, Pol-5760]

and

•

Field Composita
: The use of the
Pari/GP
command
polcompositum

and

•

Finding Generators
: Finding the fundamental polynomials

involved eliminating variables from high-degree ideals in Maple. Factoring those polynomials, determining the roots, and finding a set of independent generators with a Gram matrix having certain determinant, one needs to optimized algorithm in Maple.

All symbolic data, including exact coordinates for the
68

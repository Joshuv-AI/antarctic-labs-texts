1.
Introduction

1.1.
Overview

Schur–Weyl duality has played a fundamental role in representation theory for more than century. It provides an equivalence of categories between the complex representations of the symmetric group on
d
letters,
S_{d}
, and the polynomial representations of degree
d
for the general linear Lie algebra,
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
, whenever
n\geq d
. Due to its importance, Schur–Weyl duality has been generalized to a wide variety of settings. Relevant to this paper, these generalizations include when
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
is replaced with affine and toroidal Lie algebras, or with the type
Q
Lie superalgebra
[
13
,
7
,
28
]
. In which case, the symmetric group is replaced with the affine symmetric group or Sergeev superalgebra, respectively. The purpose of the present paper is to provide a uniform proof that includes all of these, along with many additional cases of interest.

1.2.
Main Result

Let
\mathds{k}
be a characteristic zero field and let
A
be a unital associative superalgebra over
\mathds{k}
. The prefix “super” indicates that
A
is
\mathbb{Z}_{2}=\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
-graded. Every algebra can be viewed as a superalgebra concentrated in parity
\bar{0}
, but including other superalgebras allows us to cover the finite and affine type Q Lie superalgebras, among others.

For
d\geq 1
, let
S_{d}\wr A
denote the wreath product superalgebra of
\mathds{k}S_{d}
and
A
. Wreath product superalgebras naturally generalize many of the nice features of the symmetric groups and have long been of interest. On the Lie algebra side, for
n\geq 1
, let
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(A)
denote the Lie superalgebra over
\mathds{k}
consisting of
n\times n
-matrices with entries from
A
and Lie bracket given by the supercommutator. This also includes many examples of interest; see Section
2.3
. We refer the reader to Sections
2.2
and
2.5
for details on these two superalgebras.

Let
V_{n}=A^{\oplus n}
, viewed as column vectors of height
n
with entries from
A
. This is a
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(A)
-supermodule via left matrix multiplication. The
d
-fold tensor product
V_{n}^{\otimes d}
is then a
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(A))
-supermodule via the coproduct on the universal enveloping superalgebra
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(A))
. It also has a natural right action by
S_{d}\wr A
. These actions commute, making
V_{n}^{\otimes d}
a
(U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(A)),S_{d}\wr A)
-bisupermodule. Consequently, there is a functor,

F^{A}_{n,d}:S_{d}\wr A\text{-supermodules}\to U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(A))\text{-supermodules},

given by
F^{A}_{n,d}(N)=V_{n}^{\otimes d}\otimes_{S_{d}\wr A}N
.

In Definition
2.4.1
we define a full subcategory,
\mathcal{P}_{d}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(A))
, of
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(A))
-supermodules consisting of supermodules which are polynomial representations of degree
d
when restricted to the subalgebra
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(\mathds{k})\subseteq\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(A)
and also satisfy a mild technical condition. The image of
F^{A}_{n,d}
is easily seen to land in
\mathcal{P}_{d}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(A))
. Our main theorem is the following result.

Theorem
.

If
n\geq d+1
, then the functor

F^{A}_{n,d}:S_{d}\wr A\text{-supermodules}\to\mathcal{P}_{d}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(A))

is an equivalence of categories.

When
A=\mathds{k}[t_{1}^{\pm 1},\dotsc,t_{r}^{\pm 1}]
or
A
is the Clifford superalgebra on one generator, then we essentially recover the aforementioned generalizations of classical Schur–Weyl duality. However, here
A
is an arbitrary associative
\mathds{k}
-superalgebra. We also note that Example
3.8.1
shows that the bound
n\geq d+1
is sharp. Thus, in some sense, this is the most general result along these lines that one could hope for.

Our general approach is similar to that of
[
13
,
7
]
: we prove the equivalence by bootstrapping from the classical Schur–Weyl duality equivalence. This paper is an instance of George Pólya’s Inventor’s Paradox: it turns out that our more general situation makes for easier proofs. As far as we are aware, the technique of building from the classical case goes back to Chari–Pressley
[
9
]
.

1.3.
Future Directions

Polynomial representations of
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(\mathds{k})
play an important role in representation theory and Lie theory. For well-chosen
A
, the category
\mathcal{P}_{d}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(A))
has desirable qualities (e.g., see Corollary
3.7.2
) and would be interesting to study.

There is a Schur–Weyl equivalence between the Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{gl}_{m|n}(\mathbb{C})
and the symmetric group
[
5
,
28
]
. We fully expect the methods used here should generalize to this case and yield an equivalence of categories between certain
\mathfrak{gl}_{m|n}(A)
-supermodules and
S_{d}\wr A
-supermodules. See
[
14
]
where something like this is done for
A=\mathds{k}[t^{\pm 1}]
.

Classical Schur–Weyl duality holds for any infinite field, and one could hope to prove a positive characteristic analogue of the main theorem. Properly formulating a definition of the category
\mathcal{P}_{d}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(A))
will likely require working with an appropriate integral form for
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(A))
. The existence of integral forms for the universal enveloping superalgebras of affine and map Lie (super)algebras suggests this should be possible
[
1
,
8
,
15
]
. The representation theory of quantized and/or affinized wreath product superalgebras and their associated quantized Schur algebras is likewise interesting. See
[
21
,
26
,
27
,
30
]
and the references therein. It would be interesting to obtain a quantized and/or affinized version of the main theorem. See
[
9
]
for the quantized affine case.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Notations and Conventions

For
t\geq 1
, let
[1,t]=\{1,\dotsc,t\}
. For
n,d\geq 1
, let

I(n,d)=\left\{\mathbf{i}=(i_{1},\dotsc,i_{d})\mid i_{1},\dotsc,i_{d}\in[1,n]\right\}.

Let
\tilde{I}(n,d)\subseteq I(n,d)
be the subset of tuples that have pairwise distinct entries. Let

\tilde{I}^{2}(n,d)=\left\{(\mathbf{j},\mathbf{i})_{k}\mid(\mathbf{j},\mathbf{i})\in\tilde{I}(n,d)\times\tilde{I}(n,d),k\in[1,d],\text{ $i_{t}=j_{t}$ for $t\neq k$}\right\}.

Note that the entries
j_{k}
and
i_{k}
may or may not be equal for a pair
(\mathbf{j},\mathbf{i})_{k}
in
\tilde{I}^{2}(n,d)
.

Throughout, let
\mathds{k}
be a field of characteristic zero and let
A=A_{\bar{0}}\oplus A_{\bar{1}}
be a unital associative
\mathds{k}
-superalgebra. We assume the reader is familiar with basic notions for superalgebras, supermodules, and other
\mathbb{Z}_{2}=\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
-graded structures, and we only make remarks as needed to establish our notation and our conventions. Let
\mathcal{B}
denote a fixed homogeneous basis for
A
. We always assume the identity of
A
is an element of
\mathcal{B}
. We identify
\mathds{k}
as a subalgebra of
A
via
c\mapsto c1=c1_{A}
. For
d\geq 1
, set

\displaystyle I(\mathcal{B},d)

\displaystyle=\left\{\mathbf{b}=(b_{1},\dotsc,b_{d})\mid b_{1},\dotsc,b_{d}\in\mathcal{B}\right\},

\displaystyle I(A,d)

\displaystyle=\left\{\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\dotsc,a_{d})\mid a_{1},\dotsc,a_{d}\in A_{\bar{0}}\cup A_{\bar{1}}\right\}.

For short, write
\mathbf{1}
for
(1,1,\dotsc,1)\in I(\mathcal{B},d)
.

Given a
\mathds{k}
-superspace
W=W_{\bar{0}}\oplus W_{\bar{1}}
and a homogeneous element
w\in W
, we write
\overline{w}\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}
for the parity of
w
. For short, we may call
v

even
(resp.,
odd
) if
\overline{w}=\bar{0}
(resp.,
\overline{w}=\bar{1}
). Given
\mathds{k}
-superspaces
U
and
W
, there is a natural
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-grading on
U\otimes W
given by declaring
(U\otimes W)_{t}=\oplus_{r+s=t}U_{r}\otimes W_{s}
for
r,s,t\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}
. Here and elsewhere,
-\otimes-=-\otimes_{\mathds{k}}-
.

2.2.
Lie Superalgebras

Let
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(A)
be the
\mathds{k}
-superspace of
n\times n
matrices with entries in
A
. For
r,s\in[1,n]
and
a\in A
, write
E_{r,s}^{a}
for the
n\times n
matrix with
(r,s)
-entry equal to
a
and all other entries equal to zero. The
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-grading on
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(A)
is given by declaring
\overline{E_{r,s}^{a}}=\overline{a}
for all homogeneous
a\in A
.

The set of matrices
\{E_{r,s}^{b}\mid r,s\in[1,n]\text{ and }b\in\mathcal{B}\}
form a homogeneous
\mathds{k}
-basis for
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(A)
. We view
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(A)
as a Lie superalgebra over
\mathds{k}
via the graded commutator bracket,

\displaystyle[E_{r,s}^{a},E_{r^{\prime},s^{\prime}}^{a^{\prime}}]=\delta_{s,r^{\prime}}E_{r,s^{\prime}}^{aa^{\prime}}-(-1)^{\overline{a}\cdot\overline{a^{\prime}}}\delta_{s^{\prime},r}E_{r^{\prime},s}^{a^{\prime}a},

for all
r,s,r^{\prime},s^{\prime}\in[1,n]
and homogeneous
a,a^{\prime}\in A
. As we did here, we often only give definitions and formulas on homogeneous elements. The general case is obtained by extending linearly.

Observe that the general linear Lie algebra
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(\mathds{k})
naturally appears as Lie subsuperalgebra of
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(A)
consisting of the matrices with entries from
\mathds{k}
.
Likewise, if we write
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(A))
for the universal enveloping superalgebra of
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(A)
, then
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(\mathds{k}))
is a subsuperalgebra of
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(A))
. Both
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(\mathds{k})
and
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(\mathds{k}))
are purely even.

For short, write
h_{i}=E^{1}_{i,i}
for
i=1,\dotsc,n
, and let
\mathfrak{h}
denote the Cartan subalgebra of
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(\mathds{k})
with basis
h_{1},\dotsc,h_{n}
. Let
\varepsilon_{i}\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathds{k}}(\mathfrak{h},\mathds{k})
be the linear functional determined by
\varepsilon_{i}(h_{j})=\delta_{i,j}
. Let
\Lambda(n)=\oplus_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{Z}\varepsilon_{i}
and let
\Lambda^{+}(n)=\oplus_{i=i}^{n}\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\varepsilon_{i}
. For
d\geq 0
, let

\Lambda^{+}(n,d)=\left\{\lambda=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\varepsilon_{i}\in\Lambda^{+}(n)\mid\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}=d\right\}.

For short, given
\mathbf{i}=(i_{1},\dotsc,i_{d})\in I(n,d)
, write
\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{\mathbf{i}}=\sum_{k=1}^{d}\varepsilon_{i_{k}}\in\Lambda^{+}(n,d)
.

2.3.
Examples

The above encompasses a number of cases of interest. We give a few examples.

If
A=\mathds{k}
, then
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(\mathds{k})
is the general linear Lie algebra. If
A=\operatorname{Cliff}:=\mathds{k}[c]/(c^{2}-1)
is the Clifford superalgebra on one odd generator, then
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(A)\cong\mathfrak{q}_{n}(\mathds{k})
is the type Q Lie superalgebra. When
\Lambda=\mathds{k}[x]/(x^{2})
is the Grassman algebra on one odd generator, the Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\Lambda)
and its representations appear in the context of annular Khovanov homology
[
17
]
.

If
A=\mathds{k}[t_{1},\dotsc,t_{k}]
or
A=\mathds{k}[t_{1}^{\pm 1},\dotsc,t_{k}^{\pm 1}]
, then
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(A)
is the general linear (multi)current Lie algebra or (multi)loop Lie algebra, respectively. The representations of (multi)loop Lie algebras are closely related to the level zero representations of the general linear affine and toroidal Lie algebras. A noncommutative example of this kind is when
A=\mathds{k}_{q}
, a quantum torus. In this case,
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}(\mathds{k}_{q})
appears as the core of the elliptic quasi-simple Lie algebras in type A
[
2
]
. Current algebras and their representations are central to the study of Yangians and, more recently, have appeared in the trace decategorifications of categorified quantum groups
[
23
,
3
,
29
]
. Generalizing the loop and current Lie algebras, if
A
is a finitely-generated commutative
\mathds{k}
-algebra, then
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(A)
is a so-called map Lie algebra and its representations are an area of active research.

Given a superalgebra
A
and a superalgebra involution
\iota:A\to A
, one can define an associated superalgebra
\operatorname{Cliff}(A)
, which equals
A\otimes\operatorname{Cliff}
as a superspace and has product given by
(a_{1}\otimes c^{k_{1}})(a_{2}\otimes c^{k_{2}})=(-1)^{\overline{a_{2}}\cdot\overline{k_{1}}}a_{1}\iota^{k_{1}}(a_{2})\otimes c^{k_{1}+k_{2}}
for homogeneous
a_{1},a_{2}\in A
and
k_{1},k_{2}\in\{0,1\}
. Then one could define

\mathfrak{q}_{n}(A)=\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(A\otimes\operatorname{Cliff}).

Taking
A=\mathds{k}
and
\iota
to be the identity map recovers the case
\mathfrak{q}_{n}(\mathds{k})\cong\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(\operatorname{Cliff})
mentioned in the previous paragraph. Taking
A=\mathds{k}[t]
or
A=\mathds{k}[t^{\pm 1}]
and
\iota:A\to A
determined by
t\mapsto-t
, or
A=\mathds{k}[t^{\pm 1}]
and
\iota:A\to A
determined by
t\mapsto t^{-1}
recovers the twisted Lie superalgebras of type Q studied by Nazarov
[
24
]
and Chen–Guay
[
6
]
. The Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(A)
with
A=\mathds{k}\langle u^{\pm 1},c\rangle/(uu^{-1}=u^{-1}u=1,c^{2}=u,cu=uc)
with
u
even and
c
odd is closely related to the loop algebra for the twisted affine type Q Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{q}(n)^{(2)}
studied in, for example,
[
20
,
19
]
.

2.4.
Representations

A
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(A)
-supermodule
is a
\mathds{k}
-superspace
M=M_{\bar{0}}\oplus M_{\bar{1}}
which is a
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(A))
-module where the action respects the parity in that
x.m\in M_{\overline{x}+\overline{m}}
for all homogeneous
x\in U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(A))
and
m\in M
. There is a natural
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-grading on
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathds{k}}(M,N)
where
f:M\to N
is declared to have parity
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}
if
f(M_{s})\subseteq N_{s+r}
for all
s\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}
. A homogeneous
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(A)
-module homomorphism is a homogeneous map
f:M\to N
which satisfies
f(x.m)=(-1)^{\overline{f}\cdot\overline{x}}f(m)
for all homogeneous
x\in U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(A))
and
m\in M
. A
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(A)
-supermodule homomorphism is a
\mathds{k}
-linear map
f:M\to N
that can be written
f=f_{\bar{0}}+f_{\bar{1}}
where
f_{r}
is a homogeneous
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(A)
-supermodule homomorphism of parity
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}
. In particular, note that we do not assume supermodule homomorphisms preserve parity.

If
M
is a
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(A)
-supermodule,
m\in M
is called a
weight vector
of
weight

\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
if

h_{i}m=\lambda(h_{i})m

for all
i=1,\dotsc,n
. We write
M_{\lambda}
for the subspace of all weight vectors of weight
\lambda
, and call
M
a
weight module
if
M=\oplus_{\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}}M_{\lambda}
. The supercommutator formula implies that

E_{r,s}^{a}M_{\lambda}\subseteq M_{\lambda+\varepsilon_{r}-\varepsilon_{s}}

for all
\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
,
1\leq r,s\leq n
, and homogeneous
a\in A
.

Set
V_{n}=A^{\oplus n}
to be column vectors of height
n
with entries from
A
. This is naturally a
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(A)
-supermodule with
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-grading given by
V_{n,r}=A_{r}^{\oplus n}
for
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, and with action given by matrix multiplication. Since
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(A))
is a Hopf superalgebra there is a natural action on
V_{n}^{\otimes d}
for all
d\geq 1
.
For
i\in[1,n]
and homogeneous
a\in A
, write
v_{i}^{a}
for a column vector with
a
in the
i
th row, and zeros elsewhere. The parity of
v_{i}^{a}
is
\overline{a}
. Using this notation,

\left\{v_{\mathbf{i}}^{\mathbf{b}}:=v_{i_{1}}^{b_{1}}\otimes\dotsb\otimes v_{i_{d}}^{b_{d}}\mid\mathbf{i}\in I(n,d),\mathbf{b}\in I(\mathcal{B},d)\right\}

is a homogeneous
\mathds{k}
-basis for
V_{n}^{\otimes d}
. From this it is easy to see that
V_{n}^{\otimes d}
is a weight module,
v_{i_{1}}^{b_{1}}\otimes\dotsb\otimes v_{i_{d}}^{b_{d}}\in\left(V_{n}^{\otimes d}\right)_{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{\mathbf{i}}}
, and that the weights of
V_{n}^{\otimes d}
lie in
\Lambda^{+}(n,d)
.

Write
E_{n}=\mathds{k}^{\oplus n}
for the natural representation of
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(\mathds{k})
; that is,
E_{n}
consists of column vectors of height
n
where
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(\mathds{k})
acts by matrix multiplication. Recall that for
d\geq 0
, the irreducible polynomial representations of degree
d
of
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(\mathds{k})
are, by definition, the irreducible representations which appear as a composition factor of
E_{n}^{\otimes d}
. We follow the convention that
V_{n}^{\otimes 0}=E_{n}^{\otimes 0}=\mathds{k}
is the trivial module.

For the purposes of this paper, a polynomial representation of degree
d
for
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(\mathds{k}))
will mean a
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(\mathds{k}))
-supermodule which is isomorphic to a (possibly infinite) direct sum of irreducible polynomial representations of degree
d
. The full subcategory of polynomial representations of degree
d
for
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(\mathds{k}))
is a semisimple category. If
M
is a polynomial representation of degree
d
for
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(\mathds{k}))
, then
M
is a weight module with

M=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Lambda^{+}(n,d)}M_{\lambda}.

Definition 2.4.1
.

For
d\geq 0
, let
\mathcal{P}_{d}=\mathcal{P}_{d}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(A))
be the full subcategory of
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(A))
-supermodules
M
which satisfy the following conditions:

(1)

the restriction of
M
to
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(\mathds{k}))
is isomorphic to a polynomial representation of degree
d
;

(2)

for all
i\in[1,n]
, if
m\in M_{\lambda}
with
\lambda(h_{i})=0
, then
E_{i,i}^{a}m=0
for all homogeneous
a\in A
.

Observe that
V_{n}^{\otimes d}
and subquotients of
V_{n}^{\otimes d}
are objects in
\mathcal{P}_{d}
.

To foreshadow what comes later,
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(A))
-supermodules of the form
V_{n}^{\otimes d}\otimes_{S_{d}\wr A}N
, where the action of
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(A))
is on
V_{n}^{\otimes d}
also satisfy both of the above conditions. Nevertheless, perhaps the second condition could use some elaboration. We note it already appears in the study of polynomial representations of
\mathfrak{q}_{n}(\mathds{k})=\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(\operatorname{Cliff})

[
16
, Definition 1.5]
. The following example shows that this condition is not superfluous.

Example 2.4.2
.

Let
n>1
and let
A=\mathds{k}[x]/(x^{2}-1)
be the purely even group algebra of the cyclic group of order
2
. If
I_{n}
denotes the
n\times n
identity matrix, then
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(A)=\mathfrak{sl}_{n}(A)\oplus\mathds{k}I_{n}\oplus\mathds{k}xI_{n}
as Lie algebras, where
I_{n}
and
xI_{n}
are both central. In particular, as an algebra
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(A))\cong U(\mathfrak{sl}_{n}(A)+\mathds{k}I_{n})\otimes\mathds{k}[xI_{n}]
. Let
M=A
with trivial action by
U(\mathfrak{sl}_{n}(A)+\mathds{k}I_{n})
and where
xI_{n}
acts by multiplication by
x
. Then
M
is a polynomial representation of
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(\mathds{k})
of degree
0
. Moreover, every vector in
M
is of weight zero, but
M
clearly fails the second condition for modules in
\mathcal{P}_{0}
.

As another example, let
W=V_{n}\otimes_{\mathds{k}}M
, where the action on
M
is as above, and the action of
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(A))
on
W
is via the coproduct. Then
W
is a polynomial representation of degree
1
for
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(\mathds{k}))
. Consider the vector
w:=v_{1}^{1}\otimes 1
in
W
. This vector has weight
\varepsilon_{1}
. In particular,
\varepsilon_{1}(h_{n})=0
. But
E_{n,n}^{nx}=xI_{n}-\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}i(E_{i,i}^{x}-E_{i+1,i+1}^{x})
and so acts on
w
by
E_{n,n}^{nx}.w=xI_{n}.w-\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}i(E_{i,i}^{x}-E_{i+1,i+1}^{x}).w=v_{1}^{x}\otimes 1+v_{1}^{1}\otimes x-v_{1}^{x}\otimes 1=v_{1}^{1}\otimes x
.

Remark 2.4.3
.

If
M
is a polynomial representation of degree
d
for
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(\mathds{k}))
, then it is integrable and, hence, there is an action by the symmetric group on
n
letters via the permutation matrices of
\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\mathds{k})
. Using this action one sees that the second condition of Definition
2.4.1
holds if and only if it holds for a single
i\in[1,n]
.

2.5.
Wreath Product Superalgebras

Given associative
\mathds{k}
-superalgebras
A
and
B
, recall that
A\otimes B
is a superalgebra with product given by

(a_{1}\otimes b_{1})(a_{2}\otimes b_{2})=(-1)^{\overline{b_{1}}\cdot\overline{a_{1}}}(a_{1}a_{2})\otimes(b_{1}b_{2}).

Iterating defines the superalgebra
A_{1}\otimes\dotsb\otimes A_{d}
for any
d\geq 1
. In particular, this defines
A^{\otimes d}
for any
d\geq 1
. We follow the convention that
A^{\otimes 0}=\mathds{k}
.

For
d\geq 0
, there is a right action on
A^{\otimes d}
by the symmetric group on
d
letters,
S_{d}
, by signed place permutation:

(a_{1}\otimes a_{2}\otimes\dotsb\otimes a_{i}\otimes a_{i+1}\otimes\dotsb\otimes a_{d})(i,i+1)=(-1)^{\overline{a_{i}}\cdot\overline{a_{i+1}}}a_{1}\otimes a_{2}\otimes\dotsb\otimes a_{i+1}\otimes a_{i}\otimes\dotsb\otimes a_{d}.

For
d\geq 0
, let
S_{d}\wr A
denote the wreath product superalgebra. It equals

\mathds{k}S_{d}\otimes A^{\otimes d}

as a superspace, where
\mathds{k}S_{d}
is viewed as a superalgebra concentrated in parity
\bar{0}
. The product structure is given by the formula

\left(\sigma\otimes a_{1}\otimes\dotsb\otimes a_{d}\right)\cdot\left(\sigma^{\prime}\otimes a^{\prime}_{1}\otimes\dotsb\otimes a^{\prime}_{d}\right)=\sigma\sigma^{\prime}\otimes\left((a_{1}\otimes\dotsb\otimes a_{d})\sigma^{\prime}\right)\left(a^{\prime}_{1}\otimes\dotsb\otimes a^{\prime}_{d}\right).

In particular,
\mathds{k}S_{d}
and
A^{\otimes d}
are isomorphic to subsuperalgebras of
S_{d}\wr A
in obvious ways. Also, note that there is a canonical surjective superalgebra map
S_{d}\wr A\to\mathds{k}S_{d}
.

For
\mathbf{i}\in I(n,d)
and
\mathbf{a}\in I(A,d)
, let
\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{i}}^{\mathbf{a}}=v_{i_{1}}^{a_{1}}\otimes\dotsb\otimes v_{i_{d}}^{a_{d}}\in V_{n}^{\otimes d}
. Observe that
V_{n}^{\otimes d}
is a right
A^{\otimes d}
-supermodule via

\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{i}}^{\mathbf{a}}\left(c_{1}\otimes\dotsb\otimes c_{d}\right)=(-1)^{\varepsilon(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{c})}\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{i}}^{\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{c}},

for all
\mathbf{i}\in I(n,d)
,
\mathbf{a}\in I(A,d)
, and homogeneous
c_{1}\otimes\dotsb\otimes c_{d}\in A^{\otimes d}
, where
\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{c}:=(a_{1}c_{1},\dotsc,a_{d}c_{d})
and where
\varepsilon(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{c})=\sum_{1\leq p<q\leq d}\overline{a_{q}}\cdot\overline{c_{p}}
is obtained by applying the rule of signs. Under this action
V_{n}^{\otimes d}
is a free right
A^{\otimes d}
-supermodule with basis
\left\{\mathbf{v}^{\mathbf{1}}_{\mathbf{i}}\mid\mathbf{i}\in I(n,d)\right\}
. Also observe that this action can be extended to
S_{d}\wr A
by having the elements of
S_{d}
act by signed place permutation.

2.6.
The Schur–Weyl Functor

Since
V_{n}^{\otimes d}
is a
(U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(A),S_{d}\wr A)
-bisupermodule, there is a functor from left
S_{d}\wr A
-supermodules to left
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(A))
-supermodules,

F^{A}=F_{n,d}^{A}:S_{d}\wr A-\text{supermodules}\to U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(A))-\text{supermodules},

given on supermodules by
N\mapsto V_{n}^{\otimes d}\otimes_{S_{d}\wr A}N
and on morphisms by
f\mapsto\operatorname{Id}_{V_{n}^{\otimes d}}\otimes f
.

Remark 2.6.1
.

As a special case, note that
F^{\mathds{k}}=F_{n,d}^{\mathds{k}}
is the classical Schur–Weyl functor between
\mathds{k}S_{d}
-modules and
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(\mathds{k}))
-modules. We will repeatedly use the well-known fact that
F_{n,d}^{\mathds{k}}
gives an equivalence between the category of
\mathds{k}S_{d}
-supermodules and the category of polynomial supermodules of degree
d
for
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(\mathds{k}))
whenever
n\geq d
, where both
\mathds{k}S_{d}
and
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(\mathds{k}))
are viewed as superalgebras concentrated in parity
\bar{0}

[
18
]
.

Since
\mathds{k}S_{d}
is a subalgebra of
S_{d}\wr A
and
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(\mathds{k}))
is a subalgebra of

As we next explain, this square commutes up to a canonical isomorphism.

Recall that we write
V_{n}
for
A^{\oplus n}
and
E_{n}
for
\mathds{k}^{\oplus n}
. We will write
v_{1},\dotsc,v_{n}
for the standard
\mathds{k}
-basis of
E
, where
v_{i}
has a
1
in the ith entry and zeros elsewhere. Then
\left\{\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{i}}=v_{i_{1}}\otimes\dotsb\otimes v_{i_{d}}\mid\mathbf{i}\in I(n,d)\right\}
is a
\mathds{k}
-basis for
E_{n}^{\otimes d}
. We identify
E_{n}^{\otimes d}
as a
\mathds{k}
-submodule of
V_{n}^{\otimes d}
via
\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{i}}\mapsto\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{i}}^{\mathbf{1}}
for
\mathbf{i}\in I(n,d)
.

Because
V_{n}^{\otimes d}
is a free
A^{\otimes d}
-supermodule with basis
\left\{\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{i}}^{\mathbf{1}}\mid\mathbf{i}\in I(n,d)\right\}
, it follows that for any
S_{d}\wr A
-supermodule,
N
, the map

(2.6.2)

E^{\otimes d}\otimes_{\mathds{k}}N\to V^{\otimes d}\otimes_{A^{\otimes d}}N

determined by
\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{i}}\otimes_{\mathds{k}}n\mapsto\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{i}}^{\mathbf{1}}\otimes_{A^{\otimes d}}n
is a superspace isomorphism. Going forward, we will identify these two superspaces via this map.

Both superspaces are
\mathds{k}S_{d}
-supermodules where
S_{d}
acts diagonally on each. The map given in (
2.6.2
) is an isomorphism of
\mathds{k}S_{d}
-supermodules and restricts to an isomorphism between the
S_{d}
-invariant subspaces. As a consequence,

(2.6.3)

E^{\otimes d}\otimes_{\mathds{k}S_{d}}N\cong\left(E^{\otimes d}\otimes_{\mathds{k}}N\right)^{S_{d}}\cong\left(V^{\otimes d}\otimes_{A^{\otimes d}}N\right)^{S_{d}}\cong V^{\otimes d}\otimes_{S_{d}\wr A}N.

Observe that these are
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(\mathds{k}))
-supermodule isomorphisms. Going forward, we will identify these
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(\mathds{k}))
-supermodules via this map. In particular, via this identification the square (
2.6.1
) commutes on objects. Likewise, it is straightforward to see that, up to applying (
2.6.3
), the square commutes on morphisms.

Combining Remark
2.6.1
, the commutativity of the square (
2.6.1
), and the fact that the restriction functors are faithful, immediately yields the following.

Proposition 2.6.2
.

The functor
F_{n,d}^{A}
is faithful whenever
n\geq d
.

3.
The Schur–Weyl Equivalence

The goal of this section is to prove that the functor

F^{A}_{n,d}:S_{d}\wr A-\textrm{mod}\to\mathcal{P}_{d}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(A))

is an equivalence of categories when
n\geq d+1
. As remarked after Definition
2.4.1
, the image of
F^{A}_{n,d}
lies in
\mathcal{P}_{d}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(A))
. Proposition
2.6.2
shows
F^{A}_{n,d}
is faithful. Therefore, all that remains is to show that it is full and essentially surjective.

3.1.
Key Injectivity Result

The following will be used repeatedly in the sequel.

Theorem 3.1.1
.

Let
n,d\geq 1
with
n\geq d
. Fix
\mathbf{i}=(i_{1},\dotsc,i_{d})\in\tilde{I}(n,d)
. Set
\mathbf{v}^{\mathbf{1}}_{\mathbf{i}}=v^{1}_{i_{1}}\otimes\dotsb\otimes v^{1}_{i_{d}}
. Then the map
N\rightarrow V_{n}^{\otimes d}\otimes_{S_{d}\wr A}N
defined by
n\mapsto\mathbf{v}^{\mathbf{1}}_{\mathbf{i}}\otimes n
is injective for any
S_{d}\wr A
-module
N
.

Proof.

Let

S=\mathds{k}-\text{span}\left\{v_{j_{1}}^{c_{1}}\otimes\dotsb\otimes v_{j_{d}}^{c_{d}}\mid\left\{j_{1},\dotsc,j_{d}\right\}=\left\{i_{1},\dotsc,i_{d}\right\},c_{1},\dotsc,c_{d}\in A\right\},

and

S^{\perp}=\mathds{k}-\text{span}\left\{v_{j_{1}}^{c_{1}}\otimes\dotsb\otimes v_{j_{d}}^{c_{d}}\mid\left\{j_{1},\dotsc,j_{d}\right\}\neq\left\{i_{1},\dotsc,i_{d}\right\},c_{1},\dotsc,c_{d}\in A\right\}.

Then,

V_{n}^{\otimes d}=S\oplus S^{\perp}

is a decomposition into right
S_{d}\wr A
-supermodules, and

(3.1.1)

V_{n}^{\otimes d}\otimes_{S_{d}\wr A}N=\left(S\otimes_{S_{d}\wr A}N\right)\oplus\left(S^{\perp}\otimes_{S_{d}\wr A}N\right)

as superspaces.

Since the entries of
\mathbf{i}\in\tilde{I}(n,d)
are all distinct,
S
is a free right
S_{d}\wr A
-module of rank one generated by
\mathbf{v}^{\mathbf{1}}_{\mathbf{i}}
. As a consequence,
S\otimes_{S_{d}\wr A}N=\mathbf{v}^{\mathbf{1}}_{\mathbf{i}}\otimes N
is isomorphic to
N
as a superspace with isomorphism given by
n\mapsto\mathbf{v}^{\mathbf{1}}_{\mathbf{i}}\otimes n
. Coupled with the decomposition given in (
3.1.1
), this shows the map in the claim is injective.
∎

3.2.
Fullness

Theorem 3.2.1
.

The functor
F_{n,d}^{A}
is full whenever
n\geq d
.

Proof.

Let
N_{1}
and
N_{2}
be
S_{d}\wr A
-supermodules and let
g\in\operatorname{Hom}_{U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(A))}\left(V_{n}^{\otimes d}\otimes_{S_{d}\wr A}N_{1},V_{n}^{\otimes d}\otimes_{S_{d}\wr A}N_{2}\right)
be a homogeneous map. Applying the restriction functor and recalling the identification we make using (
2.6.3
), we may view
g
as a morphism in
\operatorname{Hom}_{U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(\mathds{k}))}\left(E_{n}^{\otimes d}\otimes_{\mathds{k}S_{d}}N_{1},E_{n}^{\otimes d}\otimes_{\mathds{k}S_{d}}N_{2}\right)
. Since
F_{n,d}^{\mathds{k}}
is a full functor, we may select a homogeneous
\mathds{k}S_{d}
-supermodule homomorphism
f:N_{1}\to N_{2}
of the same parity as
g
such that

\operatorname{Id}_{E_{n}^{\otimes d}}\otimes f=g.

That is,
\operatorname{Id}_{V_{n}^{\otimes d}}\otimes f=g
on the subspace of
V_{n}^{\otimes d}\otimes_{S_{d}\wr A}N
spanned by
\left\{v_{\mathbf{i}}^{\mathbf{1}}\otimes n\mid\mathbf{i}\in I(n,d),n\in N\right\}
. We claim that
f
is actually a
S_{d}\wr A
-supermodule homomorphism. That is,
f
is an allowable input into the functor
F_{n,d}^{A}
. This, along with the identification given in (
2.6.3
), demonstrates that
\mathcal{F}_{n,d}^{A}(f)=g
, as desired.

For short, given
k\in[1,d]
and homogeneous
c\in A
, write
c_{k}
for
1^{\otimes(k-1)}\otimes c\otimes 1^{\otimes(d-k-1)}\in A^{\otimes d}
. Since these elements along with
\mathds{k}S_{d}
generate
S_{d}\wr A
, and since
f
is already known to be
\mathds{k}S_{d}
-equivariant, it will suffice to show for all homogeneous
n\in N_{1}
and all homogeneous
c\in A
that

f\left(c_{k}n\right)=(-1)^{\overline{f}\cdot\overline{c_{k}}}c_{k}f(n).

Since
n\geq d
, we may set
\mathbf{v}=v^{1}_{1}\otimes\dotsb\otimes v^{1}_{d}
. Consider

\displaystyle\mathbf{v}\otimes f\left(c_{k}n\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\operatorname{Id}_{V_{n}^{\otimes d}}\otimes f\right)\left(\mathbf{v}\otimes c_{k}n\right)

\displaystyle=g\left(\mathbf{v}\otimes c_{k}n\right)

\displaystyle=g\left(\mathbf{v}c_{k}\otimes n\right)

\displaystyle=g\left((v_{1}^{1}\otimes\dotsb\otimes v_{k-1}^{1}\otimes v_{k}^{c}\otimes v_{k+1}^{1}\otimes\dotsb\otimes v_{d}^{1})\otimes n\right)

\displaystyle=g\left(\left(E_{k,k}^{c}(v^{1}_{1}\otimes\dotsb\otimes v_{k-1}^{1}\otimes v_{k}^{1}\otimes v_{k+1}^{1}\otimes\dotsb\otimes v_{d}^{1})\right)\otimes n\right)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\overline{g}\cdot\overline{E_{k,k}^{c}}}E_{k,k}^{c}g\left((v_{1}^{1}\otimes\dotsb\otimes v_{k-1}^{1}\otimes v_{k}^{1}\otimes v_{k+1}^{1}\otimes\dotsb\otimes v_{d}^{1})\otimes n\right)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\overline{g}\cdot\overline{E_{k,k}^{c}}}E_{k,k}^{c}\left(\operatorname{Id}_{V_{n}^{\otimes d}}\otimes f\right)\left((v_{1}^{1}\otimes\dotsb\otimes v_{k-1}^{1}\otimes v_{k}^{1}\otimes v_{k+1}^{1}\otimes\dotsb\otimes v_{d}^{1})\otimes n\right)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\overline{g}\cdot\overline{E_{k,k}^{c}}}E_{k,k}^{c}\left((v_{1}^{1}\otimes\dotsb\otimes v_{k-1}^{1}\otimes v_{k}^{1}\otimes v_{k+1}^{1}\otimes\dotsb\otimes v_{d}^{1})\otimes f(n)\right)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\overline{g}\cdot\overline{E_{k,k}^{c}}}(v_{1}^{1}\otimes\dotsb\otimes v_{k-1}^{1}\otimes v_{k}^{c}\otimes v_{k+1}^{1}\otimes\dotsb\otimes v_{d}^{1})\otimes f(n)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\overline{g}\overline{E_{k,k}^{c}}}(v_{1}^{1}\otimes\dotsb\otimes v_{k-1}^{1}\otimes v_{k}^{1}\otimes v_{k+1}^{1}\otimes\dotsb\otimes v_{d}^{1})c_{k}\otimes f(n)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\overline{g}\overline{E_{k,k}^{c}}}(v_{1}^{1}\otimes\dotsb\otimes v_{k-1}^{1}\otimes v_{k}^{1}\otimes v_{k+1}^{1}\otimes\dotsb\otimes v_{d}^{1})\otimes c_{k}f(n)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\overline{g}\overline{E_{k,k}^{c}}}\mathbf{v}\otimes c_{k}f(n).

By Theorem
3.1.1
,

f\left(c_{k}n\right)=(-1)^{\overline{g}\overline{E_{k,k}^{c}}}c_{k}f(n).

Finally, note that
\overline{f}=\overline{g}
and
\overline{E_{k,k}^{c}}=\overline{c}_{k}
, and thus the desired equality holds.
∎

3.3.
Defining a
S_{d}\wr A
-module structure

Let
n\geq d+1
. Assume
N
is a
\mathds{k}S_{d}
-supermodule for which
E_{n}^{\otimes d}\otimes_{\mathds{k}S_{d}}N
has a
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(A))
-supermodule structure that makes it an object of
\mathcal{P}_{d}
and, furthermore, that action restricts to the standard action of
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(\mathds{k}))
on
E_{n}^{\otimes d}\otimes_{\mathds{k}S_{d}}N
. Throughout this section, we assume we are in this situation. Given this setup it is possible to extend the
\mathds{k}S_{d}
-action on
N
to all of
S_{d}\wr A
, as we now explain.

For
\mathbf{i}\in I(n,d)
, write
\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{i}}=v_{i_{1}}\otimes\dotsb\otimes v_{i_{d}}\in E_{n}^{\otimes d}
. Given
(\mathbf{j},\mathbf{i})_{k}\in\tilde{I}^{2}(n,d)
and a homogeneous
a\in A
, define a
\mathds{k}
-linear map,

\alpha_{(\mathbf{j},\mathbf{i})_{k}}^{a}:N\to N,

using the action of
E_{j_{k},i_{k}}^{a}
on
E_{n}^{\otimes d}\otimes_{\mathds{k}S_{d}}N
, as follows. By weight considerations, for any homogeneous
n\in N
we have

E_{j_{k},i_{k}}^{a}(\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{i}}\otimes n)\in\left(E_{n}^{\otimes d}\otimes N\right)_{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{\mathbf{j}}}=\left(E_{n}^{\otimes d}\right)_{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{\mathbf{j}}}\otimes N.

However, because
\mathbf{j}
has distinct entries,
\left(E_{n}^{\otimes d}\right)_{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{\mathbf{j}}}
is a free right
\mathds{k}S_{d}
-supermodule of rank one with basis vector
\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{j}}
. Therefore,

E_{j_{k},i_{k}}^{a}(\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{i}}\otimes n)=\sum_{\sigma\in S_{d}}\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{j}}\sigma\otimes n_{\sigma}=\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{j}}\otimes\left(\sum_{\sigma\in S_{d}}\sigma n_{\sigma}\right)=\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{j}}\otimes\alpha_{(\mathbf{j},\mathbf{i})_{k}}^{a}(n).

Since
\mathbf{j}
has distinct entries, Theorem
3.1.1
and the above equality determines
\alpha_{(\mathbf{j},\mathbf{i})_{k}}^{a}(n)
and, hence, the function
\alpha_{(\mathbf{j},\mathbf{i})_{k}}^{a}
. The fact that it is a
\mathds{k}
-linear map of parity
\overline{a}
is straightforward to check.

The following result records some of the basic properties of the maps
\alpha^{a}_{(\mathbf{j},\mathbf{i})_{k}}:N\to N
. Note that here and going forward, we frequently make use of the observation that if
M
is a supermodule for
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(A))
which lies in
\mathcal{P}_{d}
, and if
m\in M_{\lambda}
and
\lambda(h_{j})=0
, then
E_{i,j}^{a}m=0
: either because
i=j
and the second condition of Definition
2.4.1
applies, or because
i\neq j
and because
\lambda+\varepsilon_{i}-\varepsilon_{j}
is not in
\Lambda^{+}(n,d)
and, hence, is not an allowable weight of
M
.

Lemma 3.3.1
.

The following statements are true:

(1)

If
(\mathbf{j},\mathbf{i})_{k},(\mathbf{j}^{\prime},\mathbf{i})_{k}\in\tilde{I}^{2}(n,d)
, then
\alpha^{a}_{(\mathbf{j},\mathbf{i})_{k}}=\alpha^{a}_{(\mathbf{j}^{\prime},\mathbf{i})_{k}}
for all homogeneous
a\in A
.

(2)

If
(\mathbf{j},\mathbf{i})_{k},(\mathbf{j},\mathbf{i}^{\prime})_{k}\in\tilde{I}^{2}(n,d)
, then
\alpha^{a}_{(\mathbf{j},\mathbf{i})_{k}}=\alpha^{a}_{(\mathbf{j},\mathbf{i}^{\prime})_{k}}
for all homogeneous
a\in A
.

(3)

If
(\mathbf{j},\mathbf{i})_{k}\in\tilde{I}^{2}(n,d)
, then for any
p\in[1,n]\setminus\{i_{1},\dotsc,i_{d},j_{k}\}
and
\ell\in[1,d]
with
\ell\neq k
, if we define
\mathbf{i}^{\prime}=(i_{1},\dotsc,i_{\ell-1},p,i_{\ell+1},\dotsc,i_{d})
and
\mathbf{j}^{\prime}=(j_{1},\dotsc,j_{\ell-1},p,j_{\ell+1},\dotsc,j_{d})
, then
(\mathbf{j}^{\prime},\mathbf{i}^{\prime})_{k}\in\tilde{I}^{2}(n,d)
and
\alpha^{a}_{(\mathbf{j},\mathbf{i})_{k}}=\alpha^{a}_{(\mathbf{j}^{\prime},\mathbf{i}^{\prime})_{k}}
for all homogeneous
a\in A
.

Proof.

We first consider the special case when it is the pair
(\mathbf{j},\mathbf{i})_{k}
and
(\mathbf{i},\mathbf{i})_{k}
. The statement is trivial if
\mathbf{j}=\mathbf{i}
, so we assume
\mathbf{j}\neq\mathbf{i}
and, hence,
j_{k}\neq i_{k}
. Use the supercommutator formula to see that

E_{i_{k},j_{k}}^{1}E_{j_{k},i_{k}}^{a}=E_{j_{k},i_{k}}^{a}E_{i_{k},j_{k}}^{1}+E_{i_{k},i_{k}}^{a}-E^{a}_{j_{k},j_{k}}.

Letting both sides act on on
\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{i}}\otimes n
yields

E_{i_{k},j_{k}}^{1}E_{j_{k},i_{k}}^{a}(\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{i}}\otimes n)=E_{i_{k},j_{k}}^{1}(\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{j}}\otimes\alpha^{a}_{(\mathbf{j},\mathbf{i})_{k}}(n))=\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{i}}\otimes\alpha^{a}_{(\mathbf{j},\mathbf{i})_{k}}(n)

and

\displaystyle\left(E_{j_{k},i_{k}}^{a}E_{i_{k},j_{k}}^{1}+E_{i_{k},i_{k}}^{a}-E^{a}_{j_{k},j_{k}}\right)(\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{i}}\otimes n)

\displaystyle=E_{j_{k},i_{k}}^{a}E_{i_{k},j_{k}}^{1}(\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{i}}\otimes n)+E_{i_{k},i_{k}}^{a}(\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{i}}\otimes n)-E^{a}_{j_{k},j_{k}}(\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{i}}\otimes n)

\displaystyle=E_{j_{k},i_{k}}^{a}E_{i_{k},j_{k}}^{1}(\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{i}}\otimes n)+\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{i}}\otimes\alpha^{a}_{(\mathbf{i},\mathbf{i})_{k}}(n)-E^{a}_{j_{k},j_{k}}(\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{i}}\otimes n).

However,
\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{\mathbf{i}}+\varepsilon_{i_{k}}-\varepsilon_{j_{k}}\not\in\Lambda^{+}(n,d)
and so the first term of the final sum equals zero; likewise, the observation that
h_{j_{k}}\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{i}}=0
along with the second property of supermodules in
\mathcal{P}_{d}
implies the last term is also equal to zero. Therefore,
\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{i}}\otimes\alpha^{a}_{(\mathbf{j},\mathbf{i})_{k}}(n)=\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{i}}\otimes\alpha^{a}_{(\mathbf{i},\mathbf{i})_{k}}(n)
. It follows from Theorem
3.1.1
that
\alpha^{a}_{(\mathbf{j},\mathbf{i})_{k}}(n)=\alpha^{a}_{(\mathbf{i},\mathbf{i})_{k}}(n)
, as desired.

The general case follows by applying the special case to the pairs
(\mathbf{j},\mathbf{i})_{k}
and
(\mathbf{i},\mathbf{i})_{k}
, and
(\mathbf{j}^{\prime},\mathbf{i})_{k}
and
(\mathbf{i},\mathbf{i})_{k}
to obtain
\alpha^{a}_{(\mathbf{j},\mathbf{i})_{k}}=\alpha^{a}_{(\mathbf{i},\mathbf{i})_{k}}=\alpha^{a}_{(\mathbf{j}^{\prime},\mathbf{i})}
.

Likewise, to prove (2), it suffices to consider the case of
(\mathbf{j},\mathbf{i})_{k}
and
(\mathbf{j},\mathbf{j})_{k}
where
\mathbf{j}\neq\mathbf{i}
. In particular,
j_{k}\neq i_{k}
. Use the supercommutator formula to see that

E^{a}_{j_{k},j_{k}}E^{1}_{j_{k},i_{k}}=E^{1}_{j_{k},i_{k}}E^{a}_{j_{k},j_{k}}+E^{a}_{j_{k},i_{k}}

Applying each side to
\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{i}}\otimes n
yields

E^{a}_{j_{k},j_{k}}E^{1}_{j_{k},i_{k}}(\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{i}}\otimes n)=E^{a}_{j_{k},j_{k}}\left(\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{j}}\otimes n\right)=\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{j}}\otimes\alpha_{(\mathbf{j},\mathbf{j})_{k}}^{a}(n)

and

\displaystyle\left(E^{1}_{j_{k},i_{k}}E^{a}_{j_{k},j_{k}}+E^{a}_{j_{k},i_{k}}\right)(\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{i}}\otimes n)

\displaystyle=\left(E^{1}_{j_{k},i_{k}}E^{a}_{j_{k},j_{k}}\right)(\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{i}}\otimes n)+\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{j}}\otimes\alpha_{(\mathbf{j},\mathbf{i})_{k}}^{a}(n).

However, the first term in the last sum is again zero by the second property of supermodules in
\mathcal{P}_{d}
. Theorem
3.1.1
then implies
\alpha_{(\mathbf{j},\mathbf{j})_{k}}^{a}(n)=\alpha_{(\mathbf{j},\mathbf{i})_{k}}^{a}(n)
, as claimed.

To prove (3), we first note that, by the choice of
p
, the entries of
\mathbf{i}^{\prime}
and
\mathbf{j}^{\prime}
are pairwise distinct and the two have the same values in every position except possibly at
i^{\prime}_{k}
and
j^{\prime}_{k}
. Therefore
(\mathbf{i}^{\prime},\mathbf{j}^{\prime})_{k}\in\tilde{I}^{2}(n,d)
, as claimed. Noting that
j^{\prime}_{k}=j_{k}
and
i^{\prime}_{k}=i_{k}
, and, hence, that
i^{\prime}_{k}\neq p
and
j^{\prime}_{k}\neq i_{\ell}
, use the supercommutator formula to see that

E^{a}_{j^{\prime}_{k},i^{\prime}_{k}}E_{p,i_{\ell}}^{1}=E_{p,i_{\ell}}^{1}E^{a}_{j^{\prime}_{k},i^{\prime}_{k}}.

Applying each side to
\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{i}}\otimes n
yields

\left(E^{a}_{j^{\prime}_{k},i^{\prime}_{k}}E_{p,i_{\ell}}^{1}\right)(\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{i}}\otimes n)=E^{a}_{j^{\prime}_{k},i^{\prime}_{k}}(\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{i}^{\prime}}\otimes n)=\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{j}^{\prime}}\otimes\alpha_{(\mathbf{j}^{\prime},\mathbf{i}^{\prime})_{k}}^{a}(n)

and

E_{p,i_{\ell}}^{1}E^{a}_{j^{\prime}_{k},i^{\prime}_{k}}(\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{i}}\otimes n)=E_{p,i_{\ell}}^{1}E^{a}_{j_{k},i_{k}}(\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{i}}\otimes n)=E_{p,j_{\ell}}^{1}\left(\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{j}}\otimes\alpha_{\mathbf{j},\mathbf{i}}^{a}(n)\right)=\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{j}^{\prime}}\otimes\alpha_{\mathbf{j},\mathbf{i}}^{a}(n).

Theorem
3.1.1
implies
\alpha_{(\mathbf{j}^{\prime},\mathbf{i}^{\prime})_{k}}^{a}(n)=\alpha_{(\mathbf{j},\mathbf{i})_{k}}^{a}(n)
, as claimed.
∎

Proposition 3.3.2
.

Let
n\geq d
. For
(\mathbf{j},\mathbf{i})_{k},(\mathbf{j}^{\prime},\mathbf{i}^{\prime})_{k}\in\tilde{I}^{2}(n,d)
and homogeneous
a\in A
,

\alpha_{(\mathbf{j}^{\prime},\mathbf{i}^{\prime})_{k}}^{a}=\alpha_{(\mathbf{j},\mathbf{i})_{k}}^{a}.

That is, the map
\alpha_{(\mathbf{j},\mathbf{i})_{k}}^{a}
may depend on
k
and
a
, but is independent of the choice of
(\mathbf{j},\mathbf{i})_{k}\in\tilde{I}^{2}(n,d)
.

Proof.

Since the statement is trivially true if
n=d
, we assume
n\geq d+1
for the remainder of the proof.

For a fixed homogeneous
a\in A
, define an equivalence relation on
\tilde{I}^{2}(n,d)
by declaring
(\mathbf{j},\mathbf{i})_{k}\sim(\mathbf{j}^{\prime},\mathbf{i}^{\prime})_{\ell}
if and only if
k=\ell
and
\alpha_{(\mathbf{j}^{\prime},\mathbf{i}^{\prime})_{k}}^{a}=\alpha_{(\mathbf{j},\mathbf{i})_{k}}^{a}
. It suffices to show that
(\mathbf{j},\mathbf{i})_{k}\in\tilde{I}^{2}(n,d)
is equivalent to
((1,2,\dotsc,d),(1,2,\dotsc,d))_{k}
. By applying (1) from the previous lemma if necessary, we may assume without loss of generality that
(\mathbf{j},\mathbf{i})_{k}=(\mathbf{i},\mathbf{i})_{k}
.

To prove
(\mathbf{i},\mathbf{i})_{k}
is equivalent to
((1,2,\dotsc,d),(1,2,\dotsc,d))_{k}
, let

D\left((\mathbf{i},\mathbf{i})_{k}\right):=\#\left\{t\in[1,d]\mid i_{t}\neq t\right\}

be the function which counts the number of differences in the entries of
(\mathbf{i},\mathbf{i})_{k}
versus the entries of
((1,2,\dotsc,d),(1,2,\dotsc,d))_{k}
. We argue by induction on
D\left((\mathbf{i},\mathbf{i})_{k}\right)
, with the base case of
D\left((\mathbf{i},\mathbf{i})_{k}\right)=0
being trivial.

Assume
D\left((\mathbf{i},\mathbf{i})_{k}\right)>0
. Then
i_{\ell}\neq\ell
for some
\ell\in[1,d]
. We first consider the case when
\ell
does not appear as an entry of
(\mathbf{i},\mathbf{i})_{k}
. If
\ell\neq k
, then we may use (3) from the previous lemma to simultaneously change
i_{\ell}
and
j_{\ell}
to
\ell
and thereby obtain a pair
(\mathbf{i}^{\prime},\mathbf{i}^{\prime})_{k}\in\tilde{I}^{2}(n,d)
which is equivalent to
(\mathbf{i},\mathbf{i})_{k}
and with
D((\mathbf{i}^{\prime},\mathbf{i}^{\prime})_{k})=D((\mathbf{i},\mathbf{i})_{k})-1
. If
\ell=k
, let
\mathbf{j}^{\prime}=\mathbf{i}^{\prime}=(i_{1},\dotsc,i_{k-1},\ell,i_{k+1},\dotsc,i_{d})
. Using (1) shows that
\alpha^{a}_{(\mathbf{i},\mathbf{i})_{k}}=\alpha^{a}_{(\mathbf{j}^{\prime},\mathbf{i})_{k}}
and (2) shows
\alpha^{a}_{(\mathbf{j}^{\prime},\mathbf{i})_{k}}=\alpha^{a}_{(\mathbf{j}^{\prime},\mathbf{i}^{\prime})_{k}}
. Once again, we have obtained a pair
(\mathbf{i}^{\prime},\mathbf{i}^{\prime})_{k}\in\tilde{I}^{2}(n,d)
which is equivalent to
(\mathbf{i},\mathbf{i})_{k}
and with
D((\mathbf{i}^{\prime},\mathbf{i}^{\prime})_{k})=D((\mathbf{i},\mathbf{i})_{k})-1
.

On the other hand, if
\ell
appears as an entry of
(\mathbf{i},\mathbf{i})_{k}
, then first choose a
q\in[1,n]
which does not appear as an entry of
(\mathbf{i},\mathbf{i})_{k}
. Such a
q
exists because
(\mathbf{i},\mathbf{i})_{k}
has
d
distinct entries and, by assumption,
n\geq d+1
. Arguing as in the previous paragraph, we may replace
\ell
with
q
wherever it appears in the pair
(\mathbf{i},\mathbf{i})_{k}
. In this way we obtain a pair
(\mathbf{i}^{\prime\prime},\mathbf{i}^{\prime\prime})_{k}\in\tilde{I}^{2}(n,d)
that is equivalent to
(\mathbf{i},\mathbf{i})_{k}
, satisfies
D((\mathbf{i}^{\prime\prime},\mathbf{i}^{\prime\prime})_{k})\leq D((\mathbf{i},\mathbf{i})_{k})
, and where
\ell
does not appear as an entry of
(\mathbf{i}^{\prime\prime},\mathbf{i}^{\prime\prime})_{k}
. We are now in the situation of the previous paragraph. That argument yields a pair
(\mathbf{i}^{\prime},\mathbf{i}^{\prime})_{k}\in\tilde{I}^{2}(n,d)
that is equivalent to
(\mathbf{i}^{\prime\prime},\mathbf{i}^{\prime\prime})_{k}
and, hence, is equivalent to
(\mathbf{i},\mathbf{i})_{k}
. Furthermore,
D((\mathbf{i}^{\prime},\mathbf{i}^{\prime})_{k})=D((\mathbf{i}^{\prime\prime},\mathbf{i}^{\prime\prime})_{k})-1<D((\mathbf{i},\mathbf{i})_{k})
.

Thus, by induction,
(\mathbf{i},\mathbf{i})_{k}
is equivalent to
((1,2,\dotsc,d),(1,2,\dotsc,d))_{k}
. This proves the claim.
∎

In light of the previous result, we sometimes write
\alpha^{a}_{k}
for
\alpha^{a}_{(\mathbf{j},\mathbf{i})_{k}}
, where the
(\mathbf{j},\mathbf{i})_{k}\in\tilde{I}^{2}(n,d)
is left implicit. We continue to assume that
N
is a
\mathds{k}S_{d}
-supermodule such that
E_{n}^{\otimes d}\otimes_{\mathds{k}S_{d}}N
has a
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(A))
-supermodule structure that makes it a supermodule in
\mathcal{P}_{d}
, and such that the action of the subalgebra
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(\mathds{k}))
on
E_{n}^{\otimes d}\otimes_{\mathds{k}S_{d}}N
is the obvious one.

Proposition 3.3.3
.

Let
n\geq d+1
. The map

\alpha:A^{\otimes d}\to\operatorname{End}_{\mathds{k}}(N)

defined by

\alpha(c_{1}\otimes\dotsb\otimes c_{d})=\alpha_{1}^{c_{1}}\circ\dotsb\circ\alpha_{d}^{c_{d}}

is a superalgebra homomorphism. Moreover, the action on
N
by
A^{\otimes d}
afforded by this map along with the given action of
\mathds{k}S_{d}
on
N
defines a
S_{d}\wr A
-module structure on
N
.

Proof.

We first observe that
\alpha_{k}^{1}=\operatorname{Id}_{N}
for all
k\in[1,d]
. Namely, fix
(\mathbf{j},\mathbf{i})_{k}\in\tilde{I}^{2}(n,d)
and then

\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{j}}\otimes\alpha_{k}^{1}(n)=E_{j_{k},i_{k}}^{1}(\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{i}}\otimes n)=\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{j}}\otimes n.

From Theorem
3.1.1
,
\alpha_{k}^{1}(n)=n
, as claimed.

It follows that

\alpha(1^{\otimes(k-1)}\otimes a\otimes 1^{\otimes(d-k)})=\alpha_{1}^{1}\circ\dotsb\circ\alpha_{k-1}^{1}\circ\alpha_{k}^{a}\circ\alpha_{k+1}^{1}\circ\dotsb\circ\alpha_{d}^{1}=\alpha_{k}^{a}

for all
k\in[1,d]
and all homogeneous
a\in A
. Since the various
a_{k}:=1^{\otimes(k-1)}\otimes a\otimes 1^{\otimes(d-k)}
generate
A^{\otimes d}
, it suffices to verify:

(1)

for all homogeneous
a,a^{\prime}\in A
,
\alpha_{k}^{a+a^{\prime}}=\alpha_{k}^{a}+\alpha_{k}^{a^{\prime}}
;

(2)

for
c\in\mathds{k}
and homogeneous
a\in A
,
\alpha_{k}^{ca}=c\alpha_{k}^{a}
;

(3)

for all homogeneous
a,a^{\prime}\in A
, if
k\neq k^{\prime}
, then

\alpha_{k}^{a}\circ\alpha_{k^{\prime}}^{a^{\prime}}=(-1)^{\overline{a}\cdot\overline{a^{\prime}}}\alpha_{k^{\prime}}^{a^{\prime}}\circ\alpha_{k}^{a};

(4)

for all homogeneous
a,a^{\prime}\in A
,
\alpha_{k}^{a}\circ\alpha_{k}^{a^{\prime}}=\alpha_{k}^{aa^{\prime}}
.

The first two follow by picking a
(\mathbf{j},\mathbf{i})_{k}\in\tilde{I}^{2}(n,d)
, computing
\alpha_{(\mathbf{j},\mathbf{i})_{k}}^{a+a^{\prime}}
and
\alpha_{(\mathbf{j},\mathbf{i})_{k}}^{ca}
from their definitions, and applying the fact that
E_{j_{k},i_{k}}^{a+a^{\prime}}=E_{j_{k},i_{k}}^{a}+E_{j_{k},i_{k}}^{a^{\prime}}
and
E_{i_{k},j_{k}}^{ca}=cE_{i_{k},j_{k}}^{a}
.

The third follows by letting
\mathbf{i}\in\tilde{I}(n,d)
, computing
\alpha_{(\mathbf{i},\mathbf{i})_{k}}^{a}
and
\alpha^{a^{\prime}}_{(\mathbf{i},\mathbf{i})_{k^{\prime}}}
from their definitions, and applying the fact that
E_{i_{k},i_{k}}^{a}E_{i_{k^{\prime}},i_{k^{\prime}}}^{a^{\prime}}=(-1)^{\overline{a}\cdot\overline{a^{\prime}}}E_{i_{k^{\prime}},i_{k^{\prime}}}^{a^{\prime}}E_{i_{k},i_{k}}^{a}
.

We next explain the fourth claim. Pick
(\mathbf{j},\mathbf{i})_{k},(\mathbf{i},\mathbf{i})_{k}\in\tilde{I}^{2}(n,d)
with
i_{k}
and
j_{k}
distinct. Two such pairs exist because
n\geq d+1
. We wish to show
\alpha_{(\mathbf{j},\mathbf{i})_{k}}^{a}\circ\alpha_{(\mathbf{i},\mathbf{i})_{k}}^{a^{\prime}}=\alpha_{(\mathbf{j},\mathbf{i})_{k}}^{aa^{\prime}}
. Using the commutator formula, we have

E_{j_{k},i_{k}}^{a}E_{i_{k},i_{k}}^{a^{\prime}}=(-1)^{\overline{a}\cdot\overline{a^{\prime}}}E_{i_{k},i_{k}}^{a^{\prime}}E_{j_{k},i_{k}}^{a}+E_{j_{k},i_{k}}^{aa^{\prime}}.

Applying both sides to
\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{i}}\otimes n
yields,

\left(E_{j_{k},i_{k}}^{a}E_{i_{k},i_{k}}^{a^{\prime}}\right)(\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{i}}\otimes n)=E_{j_{k},i_{k}}^{a}(\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{i}}\otimes\alpha_{(\mathbf{i},\mathbf{i})_{k}}^{a^{\prime}}(n))=\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{j}}\otimes\alpha_{(\mathbf{j},\mathbf{i})_{k}}^{a}(\alpha_{(\mathbf{i},\mathbf{i})_{k}}^{a^{\prime}}(n)),

and,

\displaystyle\left((-1)^{\overline{a}\cdot\overline{a^{\prime}}}E_{i_{k},i_{k}}^{a^{\prime}}E_{j_{k},i_{k}}^{a}+E_{j_{k},i_{k}}^{aa^{\prime}}\right)(\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{i}}\otimes n)

\displaystyle=\left((-1)^{\overline{a}\cdot\overline{a^{\prime}}}E_{i_{k},i_{k}}^{a^{\prime}}E_{j_{k},i_{k}}^{a}\right)(\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{i}}\otimes n)+E_{j_{k},i_{k}}^{aa^{\prime}}(\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{i}}\otimes n)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\overline{a}\cdot\overline{a^{\prime}}}E_{i_{k},i_{k}}^{a^{\prime}}(\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{j}}\otimes\alpha^{a}_{(\mathbf{j},\mathbf{i})_{k}}(n))+\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{j}}\otimes\alpha_{(\mathbf{j},\mathbf{i})_{k}}^{aa^{\prime}}(n).

However, by the second property of supermodules in
\mathcal{P}_{d}
, the first term in the last sum equals zero. Theorem
3.1.1
shows that
\alpha_{(\mathbf{j},\mathbf{i})_{k}}^{a}(\alpha_{(\mathbf{i},\mathbf{i})_{k}}^{a^{\prime}}(n))=\alpha_{(\mathbf{j},\mathbf{i})_{k}}^{aa^{\prime}}(n)
, as desired.

Since
\alpha
is a superalgebra map, we can view
N
as a
A^{\otimes d}
-supermodule via this map. Since
N
is already assumed to have a
\mathds{k}S_{d}
-supermodule structure, it remains to show that these two actions are compatible and so define a
S_{d}\wr A
-supermodule structure on
N
. To do this it suffices to verify that if
\sigma_{p}=(p,p+1)\in S_{d}
is a simple transposition, then

\alpha_{p+1}^{a}=\sigma_{p}\circ\alpha_{p}^{a}\circ\sigma_{p}

as endomorphisms of
N
.

Fix
(\mathbf{j},\mathbf{i})_{p}\in\tilde{I}^{2}(n,d)
. Set
\mathbf{j}^{\prime}=(j_{1},\dotsc j_{p-1},j_{p+1},j_{p},j_{p+2},\dotsc,j_{d})
and
\mathbf{i}^{\prime}=(i_{1},\dotsc i_{p-1},i_{p+1},i_{p},i_{p+2},\dotsc,i_{d})
. Then
(\mathbf{j}^{\prime},\mathbf{i}^{\prime})_{p+1}\in\tilde{I}^{2}(n,d)
. Under the right place permutation action of
S_{d}
on
E_{n}^{\otimes d}
, we have
\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{j}^{\prime}}\sigma_{p}=\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{j}}
and
\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{i}}\sigma_{p}=\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{i}^{\prime}}
. We then calculate:

\displaystyle\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{j}^{\prime}}\otimes\sigma_{p}\left(\alpha_{(\mathbf{j},\mathbf{i})_{p}}^{a}(\sigma_{p}(n))\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{j}^{\prime}}\sigma_{p}\otimes\alpha_{(\mathbf{j},\mathbf{i})_{p}}^{a}(\sigma_{p}(n))

\displaystyle=\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{j}}\otimes\alpha_{(\mathbf{j},\mathbf{i})_{p}}^{a}(\sigma_{p}(n))

\displaystyle=E^{a}_{j_{p},i_{p}}(\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{i}}\otimes\sigma_{p}(n))

\displaystyle=E^{a}_{j_{p},i_{p}}(\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{i}}\sigma_{p}\otimes n)

\displaystyle=E^{a}_{j_{p},i_{p}}(\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{i}^{\prime}}\otimes n)

\displaystyle=E^{a}_{j^{\prime}_{p+1},i^{\prime}_{p+1}}(\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{i}^{\prime}}\otimes n)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{j}^{\prime}}\otimes\alpha^{a}_{(\mathbf{j}^{\prime},\mathbf{i}^{\prime})_{p+1}}(n).

Theorem
3.1.1
implies
\sigma_{p}(\alpha_{p}^{a}(\sigma_{p}(n)))=\alpha^{a}_{p}(n)
, as desired.
∎

3.4.
Recovering the action of
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(A)

To summarize, if
n\geq d+1
and
M
is a
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(A))
-supermodule in
\mathcal{P}_{d}
, then we have established the existence of a
S_{d}\wr A
-supermodule
N
so that
E_{n}^{\otimes d}\otimes_{\mathds{k}S_{d}}N
is isomorphic to
M
as
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(\mathds{k}))
-supermodules. Via this isomorphism there is an action of
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(A))
on the superspace
E_{n}^{\otimes d}\otimes_{\mathds{k}S_{d}}N
. As we next explain, one can explicitly describe the action of
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(A)
in terms of the maps
\alpha^{a}_{k}:N\to N
.

Proposition 3.4.1
.

Let
n\geq d+1
. Let
E_{n}^{\otimes d}\otimes_{\mathds{k}S_{d}}N
be as above and let
n\in N
. For homogeneous
a\in A
,
k\in[1,d]
,
p,q\in[1,n]
, and
\mathbf{i}\in I(n,d)
, we have

(3.4.1)

\displaystyle E^{a}_{p,q}(v_{\mathbf{i}}\otimes n)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{d}v_{i_{1}}\otimes\dotsb\otimes v_{i_{k-1}}\otimes E^{1}_{p,q}v_{i_{k}}\otimes v_{k+1}\otimes\dotsb\otimes v_{i_{d}}\otimes\alpha^{a}_{k}(n)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{d}\delta_{q,i_{k}}v_{i_{1}}\otimes\dotsb\otimes v_{i_{k-1}}\otimes v_{p}\otimes v_{k+1}\otimes\dotsb\otimes v_{i_{d}}\otimes\alpha^{a}_{k}(n)

Proof.

That the two sums are the same is immediate from the action of
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(\mathds{k})
on
E_{n}
. We claim they equal the left-hand side of the expression. We argue by induction on
D(\mathbf{i}):=\#\{1\leq r<s\leq d\mid i_{r}=i_{s}\}
, the number of pairs of entries in
\mathbf{i}
that have the same value.

The base case is when all entries of
\mathbf{i}
are distinct and
D(\mathbf{i})=0
. If
q
does not appear as an entry of
\mathbf{i}
, then the left side of (
3.4.1
) is zero because
\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{\mathbf{i}}+\varepsilon_{p}-\varepsilon_{q}
is not an element of
\Lambda^{+}(n,d)
, and the right side is also zero because
\delta_{q,i_{k}}=0
for all
k
. If
q
appears as an entry of
\mathbf{i}
, then it appears only once — say at position
\ell\in[1,d]
. Let
\mathbf{j}=(i_{1},\dotsc,i_{\ell-1},p,i_{\ell+1},\dotsc,d)
. In this case, the left hand side equals
\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{j}}\otimes\alpha^{a}_{(\mathbf{j},\mathbf{i})_{\ell}}(n)
and, by direct calculation the right hand side also equals
\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{j}}\otimes\alpha^{a}_{\ell}(n)
. In both cases (
3.4.1
) is verified when
D(\mathbf{i})=0
for all
E^{a}_{p,q}
.

We now assume
D(\mathbf{i})>0
and that (
3.4.1
) holds for for all
E^{a}_{p,q}
and all
v_{\mathbf{j}}\otimes n
whenever
D(\mathbf{j})<D(\mathbf{i})
. Since
D(\mathbf{i})>0
, there exists
1\leq k_{1}<k_{2}\leq d
such that
i_{k_{1}}=i_{k_{2}}
. Let
\ell=k_{1}
. Since
n\geq d+1
, we may choose
x\in[1,n]
so that
x
does not appear as an entry of
\mathbf{i}
. Set
\mathbf{j}=(i_{1},\dotsc,i_{\ell-1},x,i_{\ell+1},\dotsc,i_{d})
. Observe that
D(\mathbf{j})<D(\mathbf{i})
. From the supercommutator formula, we have

(3.4.2)

E^{a}_{p,q}E^{1}_{i_{\ell},x}=E^{1}_{i_{\ell},x}E^{a}_{p,q}+\delta_{q,i_{\ell}}E^{a}_{p,x}-\delta_{p,x}E^{a}_{i_{\ell},q}.

Applying the left side to
\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{j}}\otimes n
gives:

E^{a}_{p,q}E^{1}_{i_{\ell},x}(\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{j}}\otimes n)=E^{a}_{p,q}(\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{i}}\otimes n).

This is the left-hand side of (
3.4.1
).

On the other hand, apply the right-hand side of (
3.4.2
) to
\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{j}}\otimes n
and consider each term separately. By induction, the first term is:

\displaystyle E^{1}_{i_{\ell},x}E^{a}_{p,q}(\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{j}}\otimes n)

\displaystyle=E^{1}_{i_{\ell},x}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{d}\delta_{q,j_{k}}v_{j_{1}}\otimes\dotsb\otimes v_{j_{k-1}}\otimes v_{p}\otimes v_{j_{k+1}}\otimes\dotsb\otimes v_{j_{d}}\otimes\alpha_{k}^{a}(n)\right)

\displaystyle=E^{1}_{i_{\ell},x}\left(\sum_{k\neq\ell}\delta_{q,i_{k}}v_{i_{1}}\otimes\dotsb\otimes v_{i_{k-1}}\otimes v_{p}\otimes v_{i_{k+1}}\otimes\dotsb\otimes v_{i_{\ell-1}}\otimes v_{x}\otimes v_{i_{\ell+1}}\otimes\dotsb\otimes v_{i_{d}}\otimes\alpha_{k}^{a}(n)\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 36.135pt+E^{1}_{i_{\ell},x}\left(\delta_{q,x}v_{i_{1}}\otimes\dotsb\otimes v_{i_{\ell-1}}\otimes v_{p}\otimes v_{i_{\ell+1}}\otimes\dotsb\otimes v_{i_{d}}\otimes\alpha_{\ell}^{a}(n)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\neq\ell}\delta_{q,i_{k}}\delta_{p,x}v_{i_{1}}\otimes\dotsb\otimes v_{i_{k-1}}\otimes v_{i_{\ell}}\otimes v_{i_{k+1}}\otimes\dotsb\otimes v_{i_{\ell-1}}\otimes v_{x}\otimes v_{i_{\ell+1}}\otimes\dotsb\otimes v_{j_{d}}\otimes\alpha_{k}^{a}(n)

\displaystyle\hskip 36.135pt+\sum_{k\neq\ell}\delta_{q,i_{k}}v_{i_{1}}\otimes\dotsb\otimes v_{i_{k-1}}\otimes v_{p}\otimes v_{i_{k+1}}\otimes\dotsb\otimes v_{i_{\ell-1}}\otimes v_{i_{\ell}}\otimes v_{i_{\ell+1}}\otimes\dotsb\otimes v_{j_{d}}\otimes\alpha_{k}^{a}(n)

\displaystyle\hskip 36.135pt+\delta_{p,x}\delta_{q,x}v_{i_{1}}\otimes\dotsb v_{i_{\ell-1}}\otimes v_{i_{\ell}}\otimes v_{i_{\ell+1}}\otimes\dotsb v_{i_{d}}\otimes\alpha_{\ell}^{a}(n).

By induction, the second term is:

\displaystyle\delta_{q,i_{\ell}}E^{a}_{p,x}(\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{j}}\otimes n)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{d}\delta_{q,i_{\ell}}\delta_{x,j_{k}}v_{j_{1}}\otimes\dotsb\otimes v_{j_{k-1}}\otimes v_{p}\otimes v_{j_{k+1}}\otimes\dotsb\otimes v_{j_{d}}\otimes\alpha_{k}^{a}(n)

\displaystyle=\delta_{q,i_{\ell}}v_{i_{1}}\otimes\dotsb\otimes v_{i_{\ell-1}}\otimes v_{p}\otimes v_{i_{\ell+1}}\otimes\dotsb\otimes v_{d}\otimes\alpha^{a}_{\ell}(n).

By induction, the third term is:

\displaystyle\delta_{p,x}E^{a}_{i_{\ell},q}(\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{j}}\otimes n)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{d}\delta_{p,x}\delta_{q,j_{k}}v_{j_{1}}\otimes\dotsb\otimes v_{j_{k-1}}\otimes v_{i_{\ell}}\otimes v_{j_{k+1}}\otimes\dotsb\otimes v_{j_{d}}\otimes\alpha_{k}^{a}(n)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\neq\ell}\delta_{p,x}\delta_{q,i_{k}}v_{i_{1}}\otimes\dotsb\otimes v_{i_{k-1}}\otimes v_{i_{\ell}}\otimes v_{i_{k+1}}\otimes\dotsb\otimes v_{i_{\ell-1}}\otimes v_{x}\otimes v_{i_{\ell}+1}\otimes\dotsb\otimes v_{j_{d}}\otimes\alpha_{k}^{a}(n)

\displaystyle\hskip 36.135pt+\delta_{p,x}\delta_{q,x}v_{i_{1}}\otimes\dotsb\otimes v_{i_{\ell-1}}\otimes v_{i_{\ell}}\otimes v_{i_{\ell+1}}\otimes\dotsb\otimes v_{i_{d}}\otimes\alpha^{a}_{\ell}(n).

Combining these three expressions and simplifying yields the right side of (
3.4.1
).
∎

3.5.
The Functor
F^{A}_{n,d}
is Essentially Surjective

We can now prove that
F^{A}_{n,d}
is essentially surjective whenever
n\geq d+1
.

Theorem 3.5.1
.

Let
n\geq d+1
. The functor

F^{A}_{n,d}:S_{d}\wr A-\textrm{mod}\to\mathcal{P}_{d}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(A))

is essentially surjective.

Proof.

Let
M
be an object of
\mathcal{P}_{d}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(A))
. From classical Schur–Weyl duality combined with Lemma
3.3.3
there is a
S_{d}\wr A
-supermodule
N
so that
E_{n}^{\otimes d}\otimes_{\mathds{k}S_{d}}N
is isomorphic to
M
as a
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(\mathds{k}))
-supermodule and, under that isomorphism, the action of
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(A))
on
E_{n}^{\otimes d}\otimes_{\mathds{k}S_{d}}N
is given by Proposition
3.4.1
.

It suffices to verify for this
S_{d}\wr A
-supermodule,
N
, that the
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(\mathds{k}))
-supermodule isomorphism

V_{n}^{\otimes d}\otimes_{S_{d}\wr A}N\cong E_{n}^{\otimes d}\otimes_{\mathds{k}S_{d}}N

given by (
2.6.3
) defines an isomorphism of
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(A))
-supermodules. That is, the action of
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(A))
on these two superspaces coincides under this isomorphism.

Recall that
V_{n}^{\otimes d}\otimes_{S_{d}\wr A}N
is spanned by elements of the form
\mathbf{v}^{\mathbf{1}}_{\mathbf{i}}\otimes n
for
\mathbf{i}\in I(n,d)
and homogeneous
n\in N
. On these vectors the action of
E_{p,q}^{a}
for
p,q\in[1,d]
and homogeneous
a\in A
is given by:

\displaystyle E^{a}_{p,q}(\mathbf{v}^{\mathbf{1}}_{\mathbf{i}}\otimes n)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{d}v^{1}_{i_{1}}\otimes\dotsb\otimes v^{1}_{i_{k-1}}\otimes E^{a}_{p,q}v^{1}_{i_{k}}\otimes v^{1}_{i_{k+1}}\otimes\dotsb\otimes v^{1}_{i_{d}}\otimes n

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{d}\delta_{q,i_{k}}v^{1}_{i_{1}}\otimes\dotsb\otimes v^{1}_{i_{k-1}}\otimes v^{a}_{p}\otimes v^{1}_{i_{k+1}}\otimes\dotsb\otimes v^{1}_{i_{d}}\otimes n

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{d}\delta_{q,i_{k}}(v^{1}_{i_{1}}\otimes\dotsb\otimes v^{1}_{i_{k-1}}\otimes v^{1}_{p}\otimes v^{1}_{i_{k+1}}\otimes\dotsb\otimes v^{1}_{i_{d}})a_{k}\otimes n

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{d}\delta_{q,i_{k}}(v^{1}_{i_{1}}\otimes\dotsb\otimes v^{1}_{i_{k-1}}\otimes v^{1}_{p}\otimes v^{1}_{i_{k+1}}\otimes\dotsb\otimes v^{1}_{i_{d}})\otimes a_{k}n

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{d}\delta_{q,i_{k}}(v^{1}_{i_{1}}\otimes\dotsb\otimes v^{1}_{i_{k-1}}\otimes v^{1}_{p}\otimes v^{1}_{i_{k+1}}\otimes\dotsb\otimes v^{1}_{i_{d}})\otimes\alpha^{a}_{k}(n),

where
a_{k}:=1^{\otimes(k-1)}\otimes a\otimes 1^{\otimes(d-k)}
. Comparing this calculation with Proposition
3.4.1
confirms the actions coincide.
∎

3.6.
Main Theorem

Combining Theorems
2.6.2
,
3.2.1
, and
3.5.1
gives our main result.

Theorem 3.6.1
.

Let
n\geq d+1
. The functor

F^{A}_{n,d}:S_{d}\wr A-\textrm{mod}\to\mathcal{P}_{d}

is an equivalence of categories.

Remark 3.6.2
.

When
n\geq d+1
,
F^{A}_{n,d}(N)\cong E_{n}^{\otimes d}\otimes_{\mathds{k}S_{d}}N
is finite-dimensional if and only if
N
is finite-dimensional. Thus, the restriction of
F^{A}_{n,d}
defines an equivalence between the full subcategories of finite-dimensional supermodules.

3.7.
Some Consequences

We give a few example applications of the main theorem. Throughout this section, we assume
A
is finite-dimensional and that
\mathds{k}
is a splitting field for
A
.

Corollary 3.7.1
.

For all
n\geq d+1
, the category
\mathcal{P}_{d}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(A))
is semisimple if and only if
A
is semisimple.

Proof.

Write
J(A)
for the Jacobson radical of the superalgebra
A
. By
[
4
, Lemma 2.6]
this is a homogeneous ideal and coincides with the Jacobson radical of
A
as an algebra. The arguments of
[
10
, Section 3]
show that

(S_{d}\wr A)/J(S_{d}\wr A)\cong S_{d}\wr\left(A/J(A)\right).

From this we see the
S_{d}\wr A
is semisimple if and only if
A
is semisimple. The result follows.
∎

By
[
10
, Section 6]
, if
A
is quasi-hereditary algebra, then so is
S_{d}\wr A
and, hence, the category of
S_{d}\wr A
-modules is a highest weight category. Thus, we have the following result.

Corollary 3.7.2
.

If
A
is a quasi-hereditary, then for all
n\geq d+1
the category
\mathcal{P}_{d}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(A))
is a highest weight category.

For
k\geq 0
, let
\mathbf{P}(k)
be the set of all partitions of
k
. For short, if
\mu=(\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\dotsc)
is a partition of some nonnegative integer, write
|\mu|=\sum_{r}\mu_{r}
for that integer. We say a partition
\mu
is
strict
if for all
r\geq 1
,
\mu_{r}=\mu_{r+1}
implies
\mu_{r}=0
.

Say
A
finite-dimensional
\mathds{k}
-superalgebra and that
S=\left\{S_{1},\dotsc,S_{t}\right\}
is a complete, irredundant set of simple
A
-supermodules up to isomorphism. Recall that a simple
A
-supermodule may either be of type M or of type Q. It is known that the simple
S_{d}\wr A
-supermodules are indexed by the set

\Gamma^{d}(A):=\left\{\gamma:S\to\cup_{k\geq 0}\mathbf{P}_{k}\;\left|\;\sum_{r=1}^{t}|\gamma(S_{r})|=d\text{ and $\gamma(S_{r})$ is strict whenever $S_{r}$ is of type Q}\right.\right\}.

For example, see
[
22
]
for the construction of the simples when
A
is an algebra or
[
25
,
11
]
when
A
is a superalgebra. Write
S^{\gamma}
for the simple
S_{d}\wr A
-supermodule labelled by
\gamma\in\Gamma^{d}(A)
. We note that the type of
S^{\gamma}
can be determined from the function
\gamma
and that
F^{A}_{n,d}
preserves the type of simple supermodules. Since we do not use this information, we omit the details.

Corollary 3.7.3
.

The set

\left\{L_{n}^{A}(\gamma):=F^{A}_{n,d}(S^{\gamma})\mid\gamma\in\Gamma^{d}(A)\right\}

is a complete irredundant set of simple supermodules in
\mathcal{P}_{d}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(A))
when
n\geq d+1
.

3.8.
Sharpness of the Bound

Given that the equivalence of categories holds when
n\geq d
in the classical (i.e.,
A=\mathds{k}
) case, we could ask if the bound
n\geq d+1
here is an artifact of the proof. The following example shows this is not the case. A similar claim was made for
A=\mathds{k}[t^{\pm 1}]
in
[
13
]
.

Example 3.8.1
.

Let
A=\mathds{k}[x]/(x^{2}-1)
be the group algebra of the cyclic group of order two. Let
n=d
. We can inflate the sign representation of
\mathds{k}S_{d}
through the canonical surjective map
S_{d}\wr A\to\mathds{k}S_{d}
to define a one-dimensional simple
S_{d}\wr A
-module,
S=\mathds{k}u
.

Let

L=F^{A}_{n,d}(S)=V_{n}^{\otimes d}\otimes_{S_{d}\wr A}S.

Then
L
is one-dimensional and spanned by
\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{i}}^{\mathbf{1}}\otimes u
where
\mathbf{i}=(1,\dotsc,n)
. To be explicit, using the notation established in Example
2.4.2
, the action of
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(A)
is given by:
x(\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{i}}^{\mathbf{1}}\otimes u)=0
for
x\in\mathfrak{sl}_{n}(A)
,
I_{n}(\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{i}}^{\mathbf{1}}\otimes u)=n(\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{i}}^{\mathbf{1}}\otimes u)
, and
xI_{n}(\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{i}}^{\mathbf{1}}\otimes u)=\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{i}}^{\mathbf{1}}\otimes u
.

Observe that
L
is a polynomial representation of
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(\mathds{k}))
of degree
d=n
. The spanning vector has weight
\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{\mathbf{i}}=\varepsilon_{1}+\dotsb+\varepsilon_{n}
and, since
\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{\mathbf{i}}(h_{i})=1
for
i=1,\dotsc,n
, this module vacuously satisfies the second condition of Definition
2.4.1
. Thus,
L
is in
\mathcal{P}_{d}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(A))
.

Let
T
be a two-dimensional
\mathds{k}
-vector space viewed as a
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}(A)
-module with the trivial action. Because
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(A))\cong U(\mathfrak{sl}_{n}(A))\otimes\mathds{k}[I_{n}]\otimes\mathds{k}[xI_{n}]
, we may extend the action of
U(\mathfrak{sl}_{n}(A))
to
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(A))
by

I_{n}\mapsto\left(\begin{matrix}n&0\\
0&n\end{matrix}\right),\hskip 36.135ptxI_{n}\mapsto\left(\begin{matrix}1&1\\
0&1\end{matrix}\right).

Observe that
T
restricted to
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(\mathds{k}))
is a polynomial representation of degree
n=d
. It again vacuously satisfies the second condition of Definition
2.4.1
. Therefore,
T
is a non-split extension of
L
with itself in
\mathcal{P}_{d}(\mathfrak{gl}_{d}(A))
. On the other hand,
S_{d}\wr A
is a semisimple algebra. Therefore,
\mathcal{P}_{d}(\mathfrak{gl}_{d}(A))
and
S_{d}\wr A
-mod cannot be equivalent in this case.

This example also illustrates another difference with the classical situation. Let

S^{A}(n,d)=\operatorname{End}_{S_{d}\wr A}\left(V_{n}^{\otimes d}\right)

be the generalized Schur algebra. As in the classical case, there is a Schur functor defined via an idempotent,

{S}^{A}_{n,d}:S^{A}(n,d)-\textrm{mod}\to S_{d}\wr A-\textrm{mod}.

This is an equivalence of categories whenever
n\geq d
. See
[
12
]
for details.

By
[
11
, Lemma 11.1.1]
, there is a surjective algebra map

\zeta_{n,d}:U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(A))\to S^{A}(n,d).

By inflation through this map one can view the category of
S^{A}(n,d)
-supermodules as a full subcategory of
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(A))
-supermodules. In the classical situation, this subcategory is
\mathcal{P}_{d}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(\mathds{k}))
. The previous example shows that it can be a proper subcategory of
\mathcal{P}_{d}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(A))
when
n=d
.

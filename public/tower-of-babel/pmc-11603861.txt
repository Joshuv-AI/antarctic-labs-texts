Postoperative follow-up compliance and survival analysis in pseudomyxoma peritonei patients treated with CRS and HIPEC: a six-year retrospective study
Authors: Guang Yang, Qi Liu, Na Wang, Guanying Yu, Peiming Guo, Lan Ye
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BackgroundPMP is a rare clinical syndrome characterized by the accumulation of mucinous material in the peritoneal cavity. The combination of CRS and HIPEC is the standard treatment, known to improve survival outcomes. However, follow-up adherence is critical for early detection of recurrence and extending survival. This study is the first to specifically investigate postoperative follow-up adherence in PMP patients treated with CRS and HIPEC.AimsTo explore the socio-demographic and clinical determinants of follow-up adherence and its impact on survival in PMP patients treated with CRS and HIPEC.MethodsThis single-center, six-year retrospective study included 111 PMP patients who underwent CRS and HIPEC. Patients were categorized into compliant (control) and non-compliant (study) groups. Follow-up was initiated by the physician, with patients returning for regular visits as recommended. Kaplan-Meier analysis was used to evaluate survival outcomes, while univariate analysis identified factors influencing follow-up adherence.ResultsAmong 111 patients, 32 died by August 2024, with 1-, 3-, and 5-year survival rates of 88%, 66%, and 53%, respectively. High PCI scores (P < 0.001) and poorer histopathological types (P = 0.002) were significant predictors of reduced survival. Non-compliance with follow-up showed a trend toward decreased survival (P = 0.07). Significant risk factors for non-compliance included lower ASA scores (P = 0.020), middle- and low-income levels (P = 0.034), and parental survival status (P = 0.003). Middle- and low-income patients also experienced longer delays in seeking medical advice and had fewer cases detected through routine health screenings (P = 0.019).ConclusionsFollow-up compliance has a potential impact on survival outcomes in PMP patients treated with CRS and HIPEC. Socio-demographic factors significantly influence adherence, highlighting the need for tailored interventions to enhance patient management.

Introduction

Pseudomyxoma peritonei (PMP) is a rare, indolent clinical syndrome characterized by the progressive accumulation of mucinous material within the peritoneal cavity [1]. Despite its relatively slow progression compared to other peritoneal carcinomatoses, PMP poses significant therapeutic challenges and often necessitates a multimodal approach. The current standard of care for PMP involves cytoreductive surgery (CRS) combined with hyperthermic intraperitoneal chemotherapy (HIPEC), a strategy that has been shown to significantly improve survival outcomes by achieving maximal tumor debulking and targeting residual microscopic disease [2, 3].

Given the complexity of PMP management, postoperative follow-up is crucial for early detection of recurrence and timely intervention [4, 5], which are key to prolonging survival. However, adherence to follow-up schedules remains a critical issue, influenced by various socio-demographic and clinical factors [4, 6]. Despite the recognized importance of follow-up in PMP management, few studies have systematically explored the determinants of follow-up compliance and their impact on survival outcomes.

This study aims to explore the factors influencing follow-up adherence and their impact on patient outcomes. Given the scarcity of research on this topic, our investigation focuses on identifying the socio-demographic and clinical determinants that affect postoperative follow-up compliance in PMP patients treated with CRS and HIPEC. By understanding these factors, we hope to inform strategies that can enhance follow-up adherence, ultimately improving survival rates for this challenging condition.

Methods

Study design and population

This study was a single-center, six-year retrospective analysis, conducted in accordance with the Helsinki Declaration and established clinical practice guidelines. The study protocol and informed consent documents were approved by the Ethics Committee of the Central Hospital Affiliated with Shandong First Medical University (Approval No.20240305005). The study period spanned from October 1, 2017, to January 31, 2024.A total of 111 patients diagnosed with PMP and treated with CRS combined with HIPEC were included in the study. These patients were selected from the Department of Gastrointestinal Surgery at the Central Hospital of Jinan. Inclusion criteria encompassed adults (≥ 18 years) with histopathologically confirmed PMP who had complete clinical data and received specified treatment. Exclusion criteria included perioperative mortality, alternative treatments (Fig. 1).

Data collection and study parameters

Patient demographic and clinical data were meticulously gathered, encompassing gender, age, family status (parents, children, spouse), residence, Body Mass Index (BMI), and comorbid conditions such as hypertension and diabetes. Surgical details included the Peritoneal Cancer Index (PCI) [7] with a threshold score of 20, the Completeness of Cytoreduction (CC) score, and the histopathological type: diffuse peritoneal mucinous adenoma (DPAM), peritoneal mucinous carcinoma (PMCA), and peritoneal mucinous carcinomatosis with signet ring cells (PMCA-S) [8]. Health insurance types, integral to China’s social security system, involve a combination of social pooling and individual accounts, categorized into Urban and Rural Residents Basic Medical Insurance (URRMBI) and Urban Employee Basic Medical Insurance (UEBMI) [9, 10]. Both insurance types cover inpatient expenses at varying reimbursement rates; however, prior to April 2022, HIPEC was not included in the insurance coverage. Patients were followed up for survival via telephone every three months.

Patients’ household income was categorized into low, middle, and high-income groups based on the annual per capita disposable income reported on the official website of the National Bureau of Statistics of China (https://www.stats.gov.cn/gk/) for the year of the patient’s visit. Low income was defined as below 50% of the national figure, high income as above 150%, and middle income in between. For instance, in 2023 in China, with the annual per capita disposable income of 5,482 USD (39,218 RMB), low income was below 2740 USD (19,609 RMB), high income above 8222 USD (58,827 RMB), and middle income in between (Exchange rate calculated as of August 15, 2024).

HIPEC

The HIPEC therapy utilizes the BR-TRG-1 device, developed by Guangzhou Baorui Medical Technology Co. LTD, which allows for precise management of perfusion conditions, including temperature (maintained at 43 ± 0.5 °C), flow rate (600 ml/min), and duration (60 min). HIPEC is performed using either a closed or an open technique, and the choice is made based on the individual patient’s condition. Treatment cycles are separated by 24-hour intervals, with most patients receiving a total of 3–4 cycles within a 7-day period. The chemotherapeutic agents applied in this regimen include loplatin, fluorouracil, and raltitrexed.

Follow-up protocol and group definition

After completing CRS and HIPEC treatment, patients were instructed to return for follow-up assessments one month and three months postoperatively, followed by visits every three months thereafter. Additionally, all patients were contacted via online platforms and telephone to emphasize the necessity of postoperative follow-up for treatment efficacy. Patients and their families were advised on the importance of regular check-ups, and they were encouraged to actively attend scheduled appointments at tertiary medical centers. Follow-up intervals officially commenced upon discharge. These assessments included ultrasonography (B-mode ultrasound), computed tomography (CT) scans, and laboratory tests such as CEA, CA199, and CA125. Additional interventions, such as chemotherapy, targeted therapy, or surgical procedures, were scheduled as needed based on clinical changes. Patients who returned for their first follow-up visit within six months were categorized as the control group, while those who exceeded this six-month period without follow-up were defined as the study group. Overall survival (OS) was defined as the duration from the initiation of CRS surgery to the time of last known survival or death.

Statistical analysis

Categorical variables are presented as counts (n) and percentages (%), whereas continuous variables are expressed as mean ± standard deviation. Univariate analysis was conducted using the chi-square test for categorical data. For continuous data, the Student’s t-test and Mann-Whitney U test were used for normally distributed data, while the Kruskal-Wallis test was applied to data not following a normal distribution. Survival outcomes were evaluated through Kaplan-Meier survival analysis. All figures were generated using R software (version 4.3.3). Comprehensive statistical analyses were performed using SPSS software, version 26.0 (IBM Corporation, New York), and R software. A P-value of less than 0.05 was considered indicative of statistical significance.

Results

Baseline demographics

The study cohort comprised 111 patients with pseudomyxoma peritonei, all of whom underwent cytoreductive surgery combined with hyperthermic intraperitoneal chemotherapy. All patients maintained contact via online and telephone communication after discharge, with physicians requesting follow-up visits to a tertiary medical center every 1–3 months. However, 34 (30.6%) patients, despite maintaining contact with their physician, did not adhere to the recommended follow-up schedule. Notably, 30 (27.0%) of these patients never returned for a follow-up visit after undergoing CRS combined with HIPEC, which is a concerning observation. 4 (3.6%) patients returned for follow-up beyond six months post-surgery; these individuals were included in the study group. Conversely, the control group, which returned for follow-up within six months, consisted of 77 patients (69.4%). There were no statistically significant differences in baseline demographics (gender, age, BMI, operative time, PCI, and histopathology) between the two groups, confirming their comparability for subsequent analyses (Table 1).

The last online and telephone contact was conducted on September 1, 2024, with the majority of patients remaining reachable. In the study group, three patients were lost to follow-up via phone at 12, 17, and 32 months post-surgery, respectively. In the control group, five patients were lost to follow-up via phone at 10, 16, 23, 25, and 40 months, respectively.

Univariate analysis of follow-up non-compliance factors

Univariate analysis revealed significant differences in ASA scores, parental survival status, and income levels between the study group and the control group, with p-values indicating statistical significance (P < 0.05) (Table 2). Specifically, patients with an ASA score of 2 or less (OR = 2.9, p = 0.020), individuals whose parents were either all or one deceased (OR = 5.9, p = 0.003), and those with low or middle-income levels (OR = 1.7, p = 0.034) exhibited lower compliance with follow-up examinations. Additionally, the type of medical insurance (p = 0.086) and having a son (p = 0.053) showed a trend towards significance (P < 0.1), suggesting a potential correlation with follow-up compliance, though these did not reach the conventional threshold for statistical significance. In contrast, variables such as residence type, location, and whether HIPEC costs were covered by medical insurance did not show significant differences between the groups (P > 0.05), indicating no substantial impact on follow-up compliance from these factors.

Survival analysis

As of August 2024, among the 111 PMP patients included in this study, 32 had succumbed to the disease. The Kaplan-Meier survival estimates indicated a one-year survival rate of 88%, a three-year survival rate of 66%, and a five-year survival rate of 53%. Unable to calculate median overall survival due to more than 50% of patients being alive at the end of follow-up. Postoperative follow-up compliance in this study was analyzed to assess its impact on patient outcomes. The comparison between the study and control groups did not reach statistical significance according to the log-rank test (P = 0.07), suggesting a trend toward decreased survival in the study group, although not conclusively. Variables such as income levels, residence type, and type of medical insurance did not statistically impact survival outcomes (P > 0.05). Most notably, higher PCI scores (P < 0.001) and poorer histopathological types (P = 0.002) were found to significantly affect overall survival (Fig. 2).

Association Between Income Levels and Initial Symptoms

Given that income level significantly affects follow-up adherence, we further analysed the effect of income level on patients’ initial symptoms. We investigated the relationship between patients’ income levels and the initial symptoms presented at hospital admission. Initial symptoms reported included abdominal distension (35%), abdominal pain (37%), palpable mass (10%), asymptomatic (detected during routine health checkups, 13%), and other symptoms (5%) (Fig. 3a). The Kruskal-Wallis test indicated that, compared to high-income groups (mean rank duration = 47.0 days), middle-income groups (mean rank duration = 63.9 days) and low-income groups (mean rank duration = 65.3 days) tended to seek medical advice later after symptom onset (P = 0.019) (Fig. 3b). A stacked bar chart analysis revealed that compared to high-income groups, middle- and low-income groups were less likely to participate in routine health screenings (Fig. 3c). Our findings reveal that low-income groups not only exhibit poorer follow-up adherence but also demonstrate a reluctance to seek early medical consultation upon initial symptom onset, a concerning trend that warrants further attention.

Discussion

Postoperative follow-up and continued treatment are critical in the management of PMP, particularly for the early detection of tumor recurrence or progression [4, 5]. Timely interventions, such as repeat surgery, chemotherapy, or targeted therapies, can significantly improve patient outcomes [11]. Regular monitoring of tumor markers like CEA, CA199 and CA125 is essential, as studies have suggested that their elevation may serve as a predictor of poorer progression-free survival [12]. Additionally, routine enhanced CT scans play a crucial role in the early identification of metastasis and recurrence [13]. Our study demonstrates that follow-up compliance has a potential impact on overall survival in PMP patients. Although the survival comparison between the study and control groups did not reach statistical significance (P = 0.07), there was a noticeable trend toward decreased survival among those who did not adhere to follow-up schedules. This underscores the potential role of consistent follow-up in improving survival, especially in high-risk patients. Our analysis further identified high PCI scores (P < 0.001) and unfavorable histopathology (P = 0.002) as significant predictors of reduced survival.

It is reassuring to note that our analysis did not reveal significant differences in survival curves between rural and urban populations, across different insurance types, or among various income levels. This finding may reflect the positive impact of China’s healthcare insurance system, which has increasingly provided equitable access to medical care. The narrowing gap in healthcare outcomes between China and developed countries suggests continued progress in universal health coverage and the effectiveness of public health policies in reducing disparities in cancer treatment outcomes.

To our knowledge, this is the first study to examine the factors influencing postoperative follow-up compliance in PMP patients after CRS and HIPEC. Our findings indicate that family-related factors, income levels, and ASA scores significantly affect follow-up adherence in patients with advanced peritoneal malignancies like PMP. Family-related factors, particularly the presence of living parents, play a pivotal role in enhancing follow-up adherence among PMP patients. Research indicates that parents often provide significant emotional support during their adult children’s cancer treatment, actively participating in health management through encouragement and accompanying them to regular medical appointments [14, 15]. This support not only alleviates patients’ anxiety but also strengthens their confidence in the treatment process. For PMP patients, the presence of living parents may further enhance compliance with postoperative follow-up, driven by a deep sense of responsibility and the desire to avoid causing concern to their parents [14, 16]. Furthermore, patients with higher ASA scores, who generally have poorer baseline health, tend to require more frequent medical consultations, which may also contribute to better follow-up compliance. Additionally, income affects follow-up adherence as lower-income patients may face financial barriers to accessing care. Limited resources can reduce their ability to prioritize regular medical check-ups.

Improving follow-up adherence in PMP patients requires a multifaceted approach. Given the distinct biological characteristics and relatively slow progression of PMP compared to other advanced peritoneal cancers, active postoperative follow-up is crucial for extending patient survival [17]. Effective patient education, delivered in a systematic and accessible manner, is essential to ensure that patients fully understand the importance of follow-up in the early detection of recurrence and timely intervention. Besides, research has shown that psychological interventions can enhance immune function, reduce negative emotional responses, alleviate fear of cancer recurrence post-surgery, and improve quality of life, ultimately promoting recovery [18]. Clinical strategies may include psychological assessments using tools such as the Symptom Checklist-90 (SCL-90), Self-Rating Depression Scale, and Self-Rating Anxiety Scale, coupled with supportive psychotherapy, cognitive-behavioral therapy, group therapy, and family counseling [19–21]. Additionally, the rapid development and widespread use of mobile internet technologies offer practical tools for follow-up management, such as mobile apps, SMS reminders, and online appointment systems that facilitate direct communication with healthcare teams [4, 21]. Finally, providing financial support or ensuring that treatment costs are covered by insurance for economically disadvantaged patients can eliminate financial barriers and further enhance follow-up adherence.

Clinical implications

This study highlights potential trends in follow-up compliance among patients with PMP treated with CRS and hyperthermic intraperitoneal chemotherapy (HIPEC). While our findings identified significant factors influencing survival, such as elevated PCI scores and unfavorable histopathological types, the impact of postoperative follow-up adherence on survival did not reach statistical significance, though there was a trend toward decreased survival in less compliant patients. By identifying socio-demographic factors that influence adherence, the study highlights the need for tailored interventions, including psychological support, patient education, and financial assistance, to enhance follow-up compliance and optimize clinical outcomes for PMP patients.

Study limitations

It is important to acknowledge the limitations of this study. Conducted at a single center, the research was constrained by the rarity of PMP, resulting in a relatively small sample size. This limitation may affect the generalizability and statistical power of the findings. Additionally, the retrospective design of this study, spanning several years, introduces potential selection and information biases, which may contribute to the heterogeneity of follow-up data and impact the final survival analysis outcomes. Multivariate analysis was not conducted, which limits the ability to control for potential confounding variables in assessing follow-up compliance and survival. Lastly, while this study explored certain sociological factors influencing follow-up adherence, it lacked an in-depth quantitative analysis of the psychological and social support elements that may affect adherence and survival. The absence of specific methods for psychological assessment in tumor patients further limits the comprehensiveness of the findings.

Conclusions

This study highlights the potential impact of follow-up compliance on the outcomes of PMP patients treated with CRS and HIPEC. Various socio-demographic factors may play a role in adherence, indicating the importance of developing strategies that support patient engagement and long-term management.

Primary cutaneous ALK-negative anaplastic large cell lymphoma: A case report
Authors: Xin-Yu Zhu, Peng Huang, Yu-Rou Zhang, Li Guo
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroduction and importanceAnaplastic lymphoma kinase negative anaplastic large cell lymphoma (ALK-ALCL) is a rare CD30-positive peripheral T-cell lymphoma with a low incidence of ALK-ALCL, which mainly involves lymph nodes and, in a minority of patients, extra-nodal tissues. The etiology is unknown, and the incidence is higher in males.Case presentationWe report a case of primary cutaneous ALK-ALCL. On light microscopy, the cells showed marked heterogeneity with abundant cytoplasm and numerous nuclear schizonts, and immunohistochemistry showed large mesenchymal cells strongly positive for CD30 and negative for ALK gene products. Magnetic resonance imaging showed nodular shadows with low T1 signal and high T2 signal. Follow-up was six months and no recurrence was seen.Clinical discussionPC-ALCL is prevalent on the trunk, face and extremities. Its clinical manifestations are inert, presenting as isolated or limited reddish-brown papules, nodules, or swellings of human skin, single or multiple, with advanced ulcerative lesions with central necrosis and dyke-like elevated margins, and some lymph node involvement. Histopathology showed diffuse infiltration of tumor cells with abundant cytoplasm, pronounced nuclear fission, and marked vascular centrality of the tumor infiltrate. PC-ALCL should be distinguished from systemic ALCL.ConclusionPC-ALCL has a low incidence rate and is characterized by recurrent recurrence, and the site of the lesion and the extent of injury are the main factors affecting the prognosis. Relevant imaging examinations help to identify the extent of involvement and depth of invasion of this disease, and play an important role in the diagnosis and treatment of this disease.Keywords: Anaplastic large cell lymphoma, Skin, MRI, Diagnosis, Pathology

Introduction

Anaplastic large cell lymphoma (ALCL) is a specific type of non-Hodgkin's lymphoma, which usually involves lymph nodes and most patients can be found with extranodal involvement, and it is a lymphoproliferative disease with distinctive clinical manifestations, immunophenotypes, and genetics. Clinically, it can be categorized into systemic and cutaneous primary types, and cutaneous primary ALCL is relatively rare [1]. In previous clinical practice, the disease was rarely diagnosed with the aid of imaging. Here, we will report a case in which MRI examination was used to assist in the diagnosis and management of this disease. We declare that the work has been reported in line with the SCARE 2020 criteria [2].

Case

68-year-old woman presented with a recurrence in a short period of time (1 month) after excision of a skin swelling in the right medial posterior thigh. Patient 2 months ago the right side of the inner thigh appeared as two connected pea-sized masses, the mass is approximately 3 × 4 cm, gradually increasing with local ulcers, oozing, self-sensory pain, itching, more than 1 month ago the pain aggravated in the outside hospital for localized lesions in the area of dermatomectomy, postoperative examination suggests that: soft tissue sarcoma, more inclined to rhabdomyosarcoma? Liposarcoma? The patient underwent skin excision of the area of the localized injury at an external hospital, the results of the postoperative pathological examination were unknown. Within 1 month after surgery, swellings appeared on the skin near the surgical incision, gradually increasing in size and becoming painful. Physical examination: two raised nodular shadows on the skin of the right medial posterior thigh with poorly defined borders and red, swollen, and broken skin on its surface. MRI: thickening of the skin on the right medial posterior thigh, measuring approximately 10.0 cm × 9.5 cm, on which two nodule signal shadows were formed, slightly long in T1 and slightly long in T2 (Figs. 1) with indistinct boundaries, measuring 2.0 cm × 1.8 cm and 1.3 cm × 1.0 cm, respectively; the lesion showed heterogeneous enhancement on enhancement scan (Fig. 2). Imaging diagnosis: possible malignant neoplastic skin lesion.

Plain MRI scan, transaxial position, T2WI thickening of the T2WI skin of the right inner posterior thigh with a slightly high signal nodular shadow.

MRI enhancement, coronal position, and transaxial position, thickened skin, and nodules showing enhancing changes.

Surgery

The patient underwent radical excision of the skin lesion (right thigh) under general anaesthesia (intraoperative oval wound of approximately 12 cm × 9.5 cm) + skin removal + skin grafting + repair of the adjacent flap. The patient was in good health after the operation and was treated with symptomatic supportive therapy, such as wound hemostasis, swelling reduction in swelling, and improvement in microcirculation. The patient's enlarged lymph nodes subsided, wound infection was controlled, and pain was reduced. The patient's wound improved 12 d after the operation, the wound was reduced in size, no exudate, and the color of the base was red. After discharge from the hospital, the patient changed the medication in the outpatient clinic, and the wound healed two months after treatment, and no pain or discomfort occurred after healing.

Pathology

Two oval-shaped greyish white, greyish red, flattened masses were seen on the surface of the skin tissue, measuring approximately 2 cm × 1.7 cm × 0.4 cm and 1.2 cm × 0.9 cm × 0.3 cm, respectively, with a solid, soft cut surface and no tumor involvement at the cut edge and base. Light microscopy: In the lesion area, the cells showed obvious heterogeneity, seemed to be multinucleated, mostly horseshoe-shaped, with abundant cytoplasm, deeply stained nuclei, and frequent nuclear schizophrenia, and the whole area showed a large number of multinucleated tumor giant cells that constitute a nodule. (Fig. 3).

The tumor cells were large and heterogeneous, horseshoe-shaped, with abundant envelope and deeply stained nuclei, and a large number of multinucleated tumor giant cells constituting nodules were seen overall(HE×400).

Immunohistochemistry

CK wide (−), EMA (−), CD45 (+), CD43 (partially +), granzymeB (partially +), TIA-1 (focal +), CD30 (+), CD20 (−), CD3 (−), CD5 (−), CD7 (−), ALK (−), Ki-67 (90 %), LCA (+) (Fig. 4). Pathological diagnosis: ALK-negative anaplastic large cell lymphoma. After six months of postoperative follow-up, the patient's condition was in complete remission, with no recurrence and good quality of life.

Immunohistochemical staining: CD30(+)(SP × 200).

Discussion

Anaplastic large cell lymphoma (ALCL) is a rare and aggressive T-cell lymphoma. Stein et al. [3] originally named it as anaplastic large cell lymphoma in 1985. ALCL cells have distinctive morphologic features, which are manifested as heterogeneously distinct large lymphoid cells with abundant cytoplasm and pleomorphic features, with nuclei that tend to be horseshoe and kidney-shaped [4].

Primary cutaneous ALCL (PC-ALCL) is a primary cutaneous CD30-positive lymphoproliferative disease of the skin that usually occurs in middle-aged and elderly people. PC-ALCL is prevalent on the trunk, face, and limbs, presenting as isolated or limited reddish-brown papules, nodules, or swellings on human skin, single or multiple, and in advanced stages, it mostly presents as ulcerative lesions, with central necrosis and dike-like elevated margins. Lesions may subside alone in 25 %–31 % of the patients but most of them recur in organs other than the skin, and extracutaneous dissemination occurs in about 10 % of cases, which mainly manifests as regional lymph node involvement [5]. Histopathology shows diffuse infiltration of tumor cells, which are pleomorphic, large in size, with abundant cytoplasm and obvious nuclear division, with obvious vascular centrality of tumor infiltration and obvious subcutaneous involvement. PC-ALCL is difficult to distinguish from systemic ALCL, which has inert progression, slow disease course, good prognosis, mostly ALK-negative and skin LCA-positive, whereas systemic ALCL mostly expresses ALK-positive, is highly invasive, and has a rapid disease progression, which can be distinguished from systemic ALCL. In this case, the patient had primary ALK-negative ALCL in the skin, and his MRI showed localized skin thickening, a low signal in T1WI, a high signal on T2WI, and obvious enhancement, the latter suggesting that whether it is a diffuse infiltrative lesion or a nodular lesion, the onset and progression of the lesion is closely related to vasculoproliferative proliferation, which is also similar to the results observed by Yang et al. [6] through ultrasound.

The lesion in this case was located in the skin of the right medial posterior thigh and there was no sign of any extra-skin tissue or organ involvement at diagnosis or postoperative follow-up, which also suggested that this was a case of lymphoma with a cutaneous origin. The reappearance of the swelling in the skin near the surgical incision 1 month after surgical excision in this case also suggests that the lesion was infiltrative growth with a high degree of malignancy, and it is difficult to accurately assess the extent of the lesion only by visual and physical examination. Therefore, the extent of these lesions should be accurately assessed using imaging methods and treated with appropriate methods as soon as possible. As seen on the MR images of this case, both T2WI + FS and enhanced scans contributed to the accurate display of the extent and depth of lesion infiltration, especially the latter, which ensured the accurate resection of the lesion in the subsequent surgery. The lesions have not recurred at the six-month postoperative follow-up.

Conclusion

PC-ALCL is characterized by recurrence, and the location and extent of the lesions are the main factors affecting the prognosis.MR examination has the advantages of high soft tissue resolution, no radiation, and is not easily affected by the operator's human factors, which can help to identify the extent of the disease and the depth of invasion, and plays an important role in the diagnosis and treatment of this disease.

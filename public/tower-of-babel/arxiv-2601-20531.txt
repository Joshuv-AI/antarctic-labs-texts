Abstract
. In this paper, we investigate the quantization dimension of self-similar measures, particularly when the IFS does not satisfy the separation condition, but the sub-IFS at some level satisfies the separation condition. Further, we study the approximation of the space of Borel probability measures Ω ⁡ ( ℝ m ) \Omega(\mathbb{R}^{m}) with respect to the geometric mean error, i.e., the quantization dimension of order zero.

1.
Introduction

An important factor in the study of fractal geometry is the fractal dimension. It measures a fractal’s scaling across various magnifications to provide information on geometric complexity; it translates
irregularity
into an exact numerical quantity, the
fractal dimension
, that interpolates between traditional geometric dimensions. Since the irregularity exhibited by the fractal is not always homogeneous, a single notion of the fractal dimension is not sufficient. There are various dimensions that have been introduced in the literature to capture the different aspects of the complexity, including the Hausdorff dimension
\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(\cdot),
Minkowski–Bouligand dimension (box-counting dimension)
\dim_{B}(\cdot),
packing dimension
\dim_{P}(\cdot),
Assouad dimension
\dim_{A}(\cdot),
lower Assouad dimension (lower dimension)
\dim_{L}(\cdot),
quantization dimension
\dim_{r}(\cdot),~L^{q}-
dimension, information dimension, correlation dimension, entropy dimension, etc. (see
[
4
,
7
,
14
,
20
,
25
,
29
,
22
]
). Some dimensions, such as the Hausdorff and box dimensions, capture global scaling complexity, while others, such as the Assouad and lower dimensions, describe local extremal behaviour. In the case of fractal measures, global notions such as the information dimension and the
L^{q}
-dimension, as well as local dimensions such as the local dimension and the multifractal spectrum, provide insight at the local level.
The concept of
quantization
was first derived in information theory and signal processing, where it refers to the approximation of a continuous signal by a finite set of discrete code points. Zadore
[
29
]
traces this idea and derived the asymptotic formulas for the optimal quantization error at high rates of a probability distribution. In this context, a natural question is to determine the rate of convergence of the optimal-
n
approximation error as
n
approaches infinity. Building on these ideas, Graf and Luschgy
[
8
]
developed a measure-theoretic framework for quantization of probability distribution and formalized the notion of
quantization coefficient
and
quantization dimension
. In general, calculating the fractal dimension exactly is a tedious task. It becomes easier when the cylinders have no or minimal overlap. There are various separation properties in the literature, such as
strong separation condition (SSC)
,
open set condition (OSC)
,
strong open set condition (SOSC)
, and
exponential separation condition (ESC)
.

Preliminaries

Let
\mathfrak{K}(\mathbb{R}^{m})

\mathfrak{H}

\mathfrak{H}_{d}(E,F):=\inf\{\epsilon>0:E\subset F_{\epsilon}\text{ and }F\subset E_{\epsilon}\},

where

Definition 2.1
.

[
8
]

Let
\mu
be a Borel probability measure on
\mathbb{R}^{m},~r\in(0,+\infty)
and
n\in\mathbb{N}.
Then, the
n
th quantization error of order
r
of

is stated as:

V_{n,r}(\mu):=\text{inf}\Big\{\int d(x,A)^{r}d\mu(x):A\subset\mathbb{R}^{m},\ \text{Card}(A)\leq n\Big\},

where
d(x,A)
denotes the distance of point
x\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
to the set
A
with respect to the Euclidean norm
\|\cdot\|_{2}
on
\mathbb{R}^{m}.

The quantization dimension of order
r
of
\mu
is defined by:

D_{r}(\mu):=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{r\log n}{-\log(V_{n,r}(\mu))}.

If the limit exists, otherwise, we define the lower and upper quantisation dimensions of order
r
of
\mu
by taking the limit inferior and limit superior of the sequence, respectively.

Definition 2.2
.

[
8
]

For a given

the

r
for
\mu
is defined by:

\liminf_{n\to\infty}n^{\frac{r}{s}}V_{n,r}(\mu),~\text{and}~\limsup_{n\to\infty}n^{\frac{r}{s}}V_{n,r}(\mu),

respectively.

Let
\Omega(\mathbb{R}^{m})
denote the set of all Borel probability measures on the Euclidean space
(\mathbb{R}^{m},\|.\|_{2})
. Then,

\displaystyle d_{L}(\mathfrak{\mu},\mathfrak{\nu}):=\sup_{h\in\text{Lip}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{m})}\Big\{\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}hd\mu-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}hd\mathfrak{\nu}\Big|\Big\},\ \ \quad\mu,\mathfrak{\nu}\in\Omega(\mathbb{R}^{m}),

defines a metric on

, where
\text{Lip}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{m})=\{h:\mathbb{R}^{m}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}:h\text{ is Lipschitz function}\\
\text{with Lipschitz constant }\leq 1\}

3.
Main Results

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\mu
be a self-similar measure associated with a WIFS
(\{f_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N};\\
\rho_{1},\rho_{2},\ldots,\rho_{N})
and
\mu_{n}
be the self-similar measure associated with sub-WIFS
\mathcal{I}_{n}
at
n
-th level. Then,
\mu_{n}
is absolutely continuous with respect to
\mu
i.e.,
\mu_{n}<<\mu.

Proof.

Recall that, for a given probability vector
\{\varsigma_{\eta}=\frac{\rho_{\eta\xi}}{\sum_{\eta\in\Upsilon^{n}}\rho_{\eta\xi}}:\eta\in\Upsilon^{n}\},
the map
\mathfrak{P}:\Omega(\mathbb{R}^{m})\to\Omega(\mathbb{R}^{m})
defined by
\mathfrak{P}(\lambda)=\sum_{\eta\in\Upsilon^{n}}\varsigma_{\eta}\lambda\circ f_{\eta}^{-1}
is contraction mapping with respect to the Monge-Kantorovich metric and by Banach contraction theorem it has a unique fixed point
\mu_{n}
. Also, independent of the choice of
\lambda\in\Omega(\mathbb{R}^{m})
, the sequence
\mathfrak{P}^{k}(\lambda)
converges to
\mu_{n}
as
k
approaches to infinity. Now, in order to prove that
\mu<<\mu,
first we show that if
\mu(A)=0
for Borel set
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{m},
then
\mathfrak{P}^{k}(\mu)(A)=0
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}.

Consider,

(3.1)

\displaystyle(\mathfrak{P}\circ\mathfrak{P})(\mu)=\mathfrak{P}\bigg(\sum_{\eta\in\Upsilon^{n}}\varsigma_{\eta}\mu\circ f_{\eta}^{-1}\bigg)=\sum_{\eta^{1},\eta^{2}\in\Upsilon^{n}}\varsigma_{\eta^{1}\eta^{2}}\mu\circ f_{\eta^{2}\eta^{1}}^{-1}

Similarly, for
k\in\mathbb{N},
we have

\mathfrak{P}^{k}(\mu)=\sum_{\eta^{1},\eta^{2},\ldots,\eta^{k}\in\Upsilon^{n}}\varsigma_{\eta^{1}\eta^{2}\ldots\eta^{k}}\mu\circ f_{\eta^{k}\eta^{k-1}\ldots\eta^{1}}^{-1}.

Since
\mu
satisfies the self-similar equation
\mu=\sum_{i\in\Lambda}\rho_{i}\mu\circ f_{i}^{-1},
we have

\mu=\sum_{i,j\in\Lambda}\rho_{ij}\mu\circ f_{ji}^{-1}.

In
\zeta\in\Lambda^{*}
is a word of length
l\in\mathbb{N}
in the sub-IFS, then repeating the above process
l+n+k
times we have

(3.2)

\displaystyle\mu(A)=\sum_{i^{1},i^{2},\ldots,i^{l+n+k}\in\Lambda}\rho_{i^{1}i^{2}\ldots i^{l+n+k}}\mu\circ f_{i^{1}i^{2}\ldots i^{l+n+k}}^{-1}(A)=0.

∎

A direct comparison of Equations
3.1
and
3.2
shows that Equation
3.1
can be derived from Equation
3.2
by omitting certain terms in its summation. Also, utilising the fact that the composition of similarity mapping is again a similarity mapping and
\mu
is a Borel probability measure, we are able to establish our claim.
Next, we claim that
\mathfrak{P}^{k}(\mu)
converges setwise to
\mu_{n}
. Setting
\lambda=\mu
and following the notation in
[
28
, Theorem 1]
, the sequence
\{\mathfrak{P}^{k}(\mu)\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfies its hypotheses. Hence,
\mathfrak{P}^{k}(\mu)(A)\to\mu_{n}(A)
for all Borel sets
A
, completing the proof.

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
\mu
be a self-similar measure associated with a WIFS
(\{f_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}\\
;\rho_{1},\rho_{2},\ldots,\rho_{N})
and
\mu_{n}
be the sub-IFS at
n
-th level. Then,
\mu_{n}
is absolutely continuous with respect to
\mu,
i.e.,
\mu_{n}<<\mu
with an
L_{\infty}
density.

Proof.

In order to prove the absolute continuity of measure
\mu_{n}
with respect to measure
\mu
with an
L_{\infty}
density, it is sufficient to show that
\mu_{n}(A)\leq M\mu(A)
for some fixed constant
M>0
and for all Borel measurable sets
A
. By Lemma
3.1
the sequence
\mathfrak{P}^{k}(\mu)
converges setwise to
\mu_{n}
and
\mathfrak{P}^{k}(\mu)\leq\mu
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. This implies that

\mu_{n}(A)=\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathfrak{P}^{k}(\mu(A))\leq\mu(A).

This completes the assertion.
∎

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
\mu
be a self-similar measure associated with an IFS and
\mu_{n}
be the self-similar measure corresponding to a sub-IFS at the
n
-th level. Then,
D_{r}(\mu_{n})\leq D_{r}(\mu)
and
\dim_{L}(\mu_{n})\leq\dim_{L}(\mu).

Proof.

In Theorem
3.1
and Corollary
3.2
, we have shown that
\mu_{n}
is absolutely continuous with respect to
\mu
with an
L_{\infty}
density. Hence, by
[
27
, Theorem 3.4]
, we obtain
D_{r}(\mu_{n})\leq D_{r}(\mu).

∎

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
\{\mathbb{R}^{m};f_{i},\rho_{i}:i\in\Lambda\}
be a WIFS consisting of similitude with the similarity ratio
\{s_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}.
For some
\xi\in\Lambda^{*},
consider the sub-IFS
:=\{f_{\eta\xi}:\eta\in\Upsilon^{n}\},
satisfying the OSC for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then for any
r>0,
the quantization dimension of order
r
of the self-similar measure
\mu=\sum_{i\in\Lambda}\rho_{i}\mu_{\rho}\circ f_{i}^{-1}
associated with the IFS
\{f_{i},\rho_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
is
\min\{\kappa_{r},m\},
where
\kappa_{r}
is uniquely determined by the expression
\sum_{i\in\Lambda}(\rho_{i}s_{i}^{r})^{\frac{\kappa_{r}}{r+\kappa_{r}}}=1.

Proof.

Given that the sub-IFS
\{f_{\eta\xi}:\eta\in\Upsilon^{n}\}
satisfies the OSC, it follows from Graf-Luschgy
[
8
]
that the quantization dimension of the self-similar measure

\mu_{n}:=\sum_{\eta\in\Upsilon^{n}}\varsigma_{\eta}\mu_{n}\circ f_{\eta\xi}^{-1},

where
\mu_{n}
is the self-similar measure associated with the sub-IFS and the corresponding probability vector

\{\varsigma_{\eta}=\frac{\rho_{\eta\xi}}{\sum_{\theta\in\Upsilon^{n}}\rho_{\theta\xi}}:\eta\in\Upsilon^{n}\}

is determined by the unique solution
\kappa_{n,r}
of the equation

\sum_{\eta\in\Upsilon^{n}}(\varsigma_{\eta}s_{\xi\eta}^{r})^{\frac{\kappa_{n,r}}{r+\kappa_{n,r}}}=1.

As
D_{r}(\mu_{n})
may be sufficientely small and tending to zero as
n
goes to large, we may assume that
\kappa_{n,r}=D_{r}(\mu_{n})\leq D_{r}(\mu_{\rho}).
For contradiction, we assume that
D_{r}(\mu_{\rho})<\kappa_{r}
. Then, using Note
3.5
and
\sum_{\eta\in\Upsilon^{n}}(\varsigma_{\eta}s_{\xi\eta}^{r})^{\frac{\kappa_{n,r}}{r+\kappa_{n,r}}}=1,
we have

\displaystyle s_{\xi}^{-\frac{r\kappa_{n,r}}{r+\kappa_{n,r}}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\eta\in\Upsilon^{n}}(\varsigma_{\eta}s_{\eta}^{r})^{\frac{\kappa_{n,r}}{r+\kappa_{n,r}}}

\displaystyle\geq\bigg(\sum_{\eta\in\Upsilon^{n}}\rho_{\eta}\bigg)^{\frac{-D_{r}(\mu)}{r+D_{r}(\mu)}}\rho_{\xi}^{\frac{D_{r}(\mu)}{r+D_{r}(\mu)}}\sum_{\eta\in\Upsilon^{n}}(\rho_{\eta}s_{\eta}^{r})^{\frac{D_{r}(\mu)}{r+D_{r}(\mu)}}

\displaystyle=\bigg(\sum_{\eta\in\Upsilon^{n}}\rho_{\xi\eta}\bigg)^{\frac{-D_{r}(\mu)}{r+D_{r}(\mu)}}\rho_{\xi}^{\frac{D_{r}(\mu)}{r+D_{r}(\mu)}}\sum_{\eta\in\Upsilon^{n}}(\rho_{\eta}s_{\eta}^{r})^{\frac{D_{r}(\mu)}{r+D_{r}(\mu)}}(\rho_{\eta}s_{\eta}^{r})^{\frac{-\kappa_{r}}{r+\kappa_{r}}}(\rho_{\eta}s_{\eta}^{r})^{\frac{\kappa_{r}}{r+\kappa_{r}}}

\displaystyle=\bigg(\sum_{\eta\in\Upsilon^{n}}\rho_{\eta}\bigg)^{\frac{-D_{r}(\mu)}{r+D_{r}(\mu)}}\sum_{\eta\in\Upsilon^{n}}(\rho_{\eta}s_{\eta}^{r})^{\frac{D_{r}(\mu)}{r+D_{r}(\mu)}-\frac{\kappa_{r}}{r+\kappa_{r}}}(\rho_{\eta}s_{\eta}^{r})^{\frac{\kappa_{r}}{r+\kappa_{r}}}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{\eta\in\Upsilon^{n}}(\rho_{\eta}s_{\min}^{nr})^{\frac{D_{r}(\mu)}{r+D_{r}(\mu)}-\frac{\kappa_{r}}{r+\kappa_{r}}}(\rho_{\eta}s_{\eta}^{r})^{\frac{\kappa_{r}}{r+\kappa_{r}}}

\displaystyle\geq s_{\min}^{nr.\bigg({\frac{D_{r}(\mu)}{r+D_{r}(\mu)}-\frac{\kappa_{r}}{r+\kappa_{r}}}\bigg)}\sum_{\eta\in\Upsilon^{n}}\rho_{\eta}^{\frac{D_{r}(\mu)}{r+D_{r}(\mu)}-\frac{\kappa_{r}}{r+\kappa_{r}}}(\rho_{\eta}s_{\eta}^{r})^{\frac{\kappa_{r}}{r+\kappa_{r}}}

\displaystyle\geq s_{\min}^{nr.\bigg({\frac{D_{r}(\mu)}{r+D_{r}(\mu)}-\frac{\kappa_{r}}{r+\kappa_{r}}}\bigg)}\sum_{\eta\in\Upsilon^{n}}\rho_{\eta}^{\frac{D_{r}(\mu)}{r+D_{r}(\mu)}}(s_{\eta}^{r})^{\frac{\kappa_{r}}{r+\kappa_{r}}}

\displaystyle\geq s_{\min}^{nr.\bigg({\frac{D_{r}(\mu)}{r+D_{r}(\mu)}-\frac{\kappa_{r}}{r+\kappa_{r}}}\bigg)}\sum_{\eta\in\Upsilon^{n}}(\rho_{\eta}s_{\eta}^{r})^{\frac{\kappa_{r}}{r+\kappa_{r}}}

Since

as

3.1.
Approximation of measures in terms of quantization dimension

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
\mu
and
\nu
be two finite measures. Then

D_{0}(\mu+\nu)=\max\{D_{0}(\mu),D_{0}(\nu)\}.

Proof.

By the definition of the
n
th quantization error of a measure with respect to the geometric mean error, we have

(3.3)

\displaystyle\log e_{n}(\mu+\nu)

\displaystyle=\inf\bigg\{\int\log d(x,A)d(\mu+\nu)(x):A\subset\mathbb{R}^{d},\text{Card}(A)\leq n\bigg\}

\displaystyle\geq\inf\bigg\{\int\log d(x,A)d(\mu)(x)\bigg\}+\inf\bigg\{\int\log d(x,A)d(\nu)(x)\bigg\}

\displaystyle=\log e_{n}(\mu)+\log e_{0}(\nu).

This depicts that
D_{0}(\mu+\nu)\geq\max\{D_{0}(\mu),D_{0}(\nu)\}.
Now, let
t>\max\{D_{0}(\mu),D_{0}(\nu)\},
then by
[
9
, Proposition 4.3]
, we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}(\log n+t\log e_{n}(\mu))=+\infty\quad\lim_{n\to\infty}(\log n+t\log e_{n}(\nu))=+\infty.

Let
k\in\mathbb{R}
be any arbitrary number, then there exist natural numbers
N_{1}(\mu,k)
and
N_{2}(\nu,k)
such that

\log n+t\log e_{n}(\mu)>k\quad\text{and}\quad\log n+t\log e_{n}(\mu)>k\quad\forall n\geq N=\max\{N_{1},N_{2}\}.

Also, by the definition of the quantization error of a measure with respect to the geometric mean error, there exist sets
A(\mu,n,k)
and
B(\nu,n,k)
such that

\displaystyle\log n+t\int\log d(x,A(\mu,n,k))d(\mu)(x)>k,\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle\log n+t\int\log d(x,B(\nu,n,k))d(\nu)(x)>k\quad\forall n\geq N.

Since Card
(A(\mu,n,k)\leq n)
and Card
(B(\nu,n,k)\leq n),
it follows that Card
(A\cup B)\leq n^{2},
and we have

2\log n+t\int\log d(x,A\cup B)d(\mu)(x)>k.

Now, for every
n>N,
we have

\displaystyle 2\log n+t\int\log d(x,A\cup B)d(\mu+\nu)(x)

\displaystyle=\log n+t\int\log d(x,A\cup B)d(\mu)(x)+

\displaystyle\log n+t\int\log d(x,A\cup B)d(\nu)(x)>k

Since
k\in\mathbb{R}
is arbitrary, we have
\lim_{n\to\infty}(\log n+t\log e_{n}(\mu+\nu))=+\infty
. It follows that
D_{0}(\mu+\nu)<t.
Further, since
t>\max\{D_{0}(\mu),D_{0}(\nu)\}
is arbitrary, it follows that
D_{0}(\mu+\nu)\leq\max\{D_{0}(\mu),D_{0}(\nu)\}.
This completes the assertion.
∎

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
\mu
be a finite Borel probability measure. Then for a fixed
\nu\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{R}^{m}),

D_{0}(\mu*\nu)=D_{0}(\mu).

Proof.

Following a similar approach to
[
27
, Lemma 3.10]
, we obtain the required result.
∎

Using the above lemmas, we will approximate the set
\Omega(\mathbb{R}^{m})
by several of its subclasses with respect to the quantization dimension.

Theorem 3.11
.

The sets
\Omega_{0}({\mathbb{R}^{m}})=\{\mu\in\Omega(\mathbb{R}^{m}):\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}D_{r}(\mu)=D_{0}(\mu)=\dim_{H}(\mu)\}
and
\Omega_{0>}({\mathbb{R}^{m}})=\{\mu\in\Omega(\mathbb{R}^{m}):\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}D_{r}(\mu)=D_{0}(\mu)>\dim_{H}(\mu)\}
are dense in
\Omega(\mathbb{R}^{m}).

Proof.

Since By
[
9
, Remark 4.6]
, the set
\Omega_{0}({\mathbb{R}^{m}})
is non-empty. Let
\mu\in\Omega(\mathbb{R}^{m})
be any Borel probability measure and
\epsilon>0,
then by the fact that
\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
is dense in
\Omega(\mathbb{R}^{m}),
there exists a measure
\nu\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
such that

d_{L}(\mu,\nu)<\frac{\epsilon}{2}.

Further, by Lemma
3.10
and the similar technique followed in
[
27
, Theorem 3.16]
, we have there exists a compactly supported measure such that
d_{L}(\nu,\lambda)<\frac{\epsilon}{2},
where
\lambda=\nu*\lambda_{1}
for some
\lambda_{1}\in\Omega_{0}({\mathbb{R}^{m}}).
in this way, we have

d_{L}(\mu,\lambda_{1})\leq d_{L}(\mu,\nu)+d_{L}(\nu,\lambda)<\epsilon.

This proves that the set
\Omega_{0}({\mathbb{R}^{m}})
is a dense subset of
\Omega(\mathbb{R}^{m}).

Again, by
[
9
, Theorem 4.3]
the set
\Omega_{0>}({\mathbb{R}^{m}})
is non-empty. Since the proof is similar, we omit it.

∎

We introduce the total variation metric (
[
21
]
) on
\Omega(\mathbb{R}^{m})
as follows:

\|\mu-\nu\|_{TV}=\sup\{|\mu(A)-\nu(A)|:A~\text{is a Borel set}\}.

Lemma 3.12
.

Let
\lambda\in\Omega(\mathbb{R}^{m})
be a fixed measure and
\big(\mu_{n}\big)
be a sequence of absolutely continuous (with respect to
\lambda
) measures in
\Omega(\mathbb{R}^{m}).
If
(\frac{d\mu_{n}}{d\lambda})
converges in
\mathcal{L}^{1}(\lambda)
-norm then
\big(\mu_{n}\big)
converges to a measure
\mu
, and

\frac{d\mu}{d\lambda}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{d\mu_{n}}{d\lambda}.

Proof.

Let
g=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{d\mu_{n}}{d\lambda}.
Then a measure
\mu
defined by

\mu(A)=\int_{A}gd\lambda,~~~~~~A\subset\mathbb{R}^{n},

is in
\Omega(\mathbb{R}^{m}).
Further, we obtain

\displaystyle d_{L}(\mu_{n},\mu)

\displaystyle=\sup_{\text{Lip}(f)\leq 1}\Big\{\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}fd\mu_{n}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}fd\mu\Big|\Big\}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\text{Lip}(f)\leq 1}\Big\{\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}f\frac{d\mu_{n}}{d\lambda}d\lambda-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}fgd\lambda\Big|\Big\}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\text{Lip}(f)\leq 1}\Big\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\Big|f\frac{d\mu_{n}}{d\lambda}-fg\Big|d\lambda\Big\}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\text{Lip}(f)\leq 1}\|f\|_{\infty}\Big\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\Big|\frac{d\mu_{n}}{d\lambda}-g\Big|d\lambda\Big\}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\Big|\frac{d\mu_{n}}{d\lambda}-g\Big|d\lambda.

Since
\frac{d\mu_{n}}{d\lambda}\to g
in
\mathcal{L}^{1}(\lambda)
-norm, we have
d_{L}(\mu_{n},\mu)\to 0
as
n\to\infty,
hence the proof.
∎

Remark 3.13
.

Under the hypothesis of the above theorem, we also have

\displaystyle\|\mu_{n}-\mu\|_{TV}

\displaystyle=\sup\{|\mu_{n}(A)-\mu(A)|:A~\text{is a Borel set}\}

\displaystyle=\sup\Big\{\Big|\int_{A}\frac{d\mu_{n}}{d\lambda}d\lambda-\int_{A}gd\lambda\Big|:A~\text{is a Borel set}\Big\}

\displaystyle\leq\sup\Big\{\int_{A}\Big|\frac{d\mu_{n}}{d\lambda}-g\Big|d\lambda:A~\text{is a Borel set}\Big\}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\Big|\frac{d\mu_{n}}{d\lambda}-g\Big|d\lambda.

From the above, we infer that
\|\mu_{n}-\mu\|_{TV}\to 0
as
n\to\infty.

Theorem 3.14
.

Let
\lambda\in\Omega(\mathbb{R}^{m}).

Suppose
\mu
is a absolutely continuous measure with respect to
\lambda
such that
\dim_{H}\Big(Gr\big(\frac{d\mu}{d\lambda}\big)\Big)=\alpha
for some
m\leq\alpha\leq m+1.
Then there exists a sequence of absolutely continuous measures
(\mu_{n})\in\Omega(\mathbb{R}^{m})
with the following conditions:

\dim_{H}\Big(Gr\big(\frac{d\mu_{n}}{d\lambda}\big)\Big)=\alpha~~\text{and}~~\mu=\lim_{n\to\infty}\mu_{n}.

Proof.

In the light of an analogous result to Theorem
3.12
, we may have a sequence of integrable (with respect to
\lambda
) functions
(g_{n})
such that
\dim_{H}\big(Gr(g_{n})\big)=\alpha
and
g_{n}\to\frac{d\mu}{d\lambda}
in
\mathcal{L}^{1}(\lambda)
-norm.
Now, one defines a sequence of measures
(\mu_{n})
as follows

\mu_{n}(A)=\int_{A}g_{n}d\lambda,~~~~~~A\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}.

Then,
\frac{d\mu_{n}}{d\lambda}=g_{n}
and
\Big(\frac{d\mu_{n}}{d\lambda}\Big)
converges to
\frac{d\mu}{d\lambda}.
Lemma
3.12
yields that the sequence
(\mu_{n})
converges to
\mu
such that
\dim_{H}\Big(Gr\big(\frac{d\mu_{n}}{d\lambda}\big)\Big)=\alpha.
Hence the assertion is proved.
∎

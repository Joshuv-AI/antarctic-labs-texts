Abstract
. We prove that a bounded complex of coherent sheaves on an abelian variety A A , whose Fourier-Mukai transform has support of dimension ≤ 1 \leq 1 , extends to a perfect complex on the standard NC-smooth thickening of A A and on the deformation quantization of any constant Poisson bracket on A A . We discuss a related conjectural characterization of Jacobians in terms of NC-smooth thickenings.



Introduction

NC-smooth thickenings of smooth algebraic varieties were introduced by Kapranov
[
8
]
as a way to study noncommutative geometry
in the “formal neighborhood” of a commutative variety. The model example of an NC-smooth thickening is a completion of a free algebra in
n
generators
with respect to the commutator filtration (see Sec.
1.1
below).
Kapranov’s idea is to consider sheaves of algebras
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}^{NC}
over smooth varieties that look like this model example in a formal neighborhood of each point (such that by taking the quotient of
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}^{NC}
by the ideal generated by commutators one recovers the structure sheaf
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
).
Kapranov proved existence and uniqueness of such NC-smooth thickenings for smooth affine varieties, and constructed some non-affine examples.
Non-affine examples were further studied in
[
12
]
and
[
6
]
.

Every abelian variety
A
possesses a natural translation-invariant NC-smooth thickening
A^{NC}
that we will call
standard
.
In holomorphic terms, this can be obtained by the descent of the standard
NC-smooth thickening of the affine space with respect to translations by a lattice. Over any field of characteristic zero, there exists an algebraic construction using
a canonical flat connection on
A
(see
[
12
]
). In the case when
A=J
, the Jacobian of a smooth projective curve
C
, there is a way to think of the standard NC-smooth thickening of
J
as representing a natural noncommutative moduli functor (see
[
12
, Sec. 4]
), and this gives rise to an NC-smooth analog of the Fourier-Mukai transform
that produces sheaves on
J^{NC}
from sheaves on
C
(but it is far from being an equivalence).

In this paper we show that the theta divisor
\Theta
on
J
(defined up to a translation) also extends in some sense to the standard NC-smooth thickening
J^{NC}
. Namely, we prove that there exists a coherent sheaf supported on
\Theta
, generically of rank
1
on
\Theta
, which extends to an object of the derived category of
J^{NC}
represented by a bounded complex of vector bundles
(see Theorem
1.3.1
). We conjecture that this property characterizes Jacobians among principally polarized varieties with irreducible theta divisors.

Conjecture A
.

Let
(A,\Theta)
be a principally polarized abelian variety with its theta divisor, where

\Theta
is irreducible.
Then it is the Jacobian of a smooth curve if and only if there exists a coherent sheaf
{\mathcal{F}}
on
A
, supported on
\Theta
and of length
1
at the general point of
\Theta
,
such that
{\mathcal{F}}
extends to an object of
D^{b}(A^{NC})
represented by a bounded complex of vector bundles
(where
A^{NC}
is the standard NC-smooth thickening of
A
).

The “only if” part is Theorem
1.3.1
. As an evidence for the “if” part, we prove that existence of a coherent sheaf
{\mathcal{F}}
as in Conjecture A implies that

(A,\Theta)
belongs to the Andreotti-Mayer locus, i.e., that the dimension of the singular locus of
\Theta
is
\geq g-4
, where
g=\dim A
(see Cor.
1.3.7
).
In fact, to deduce this we only need an extension to the standard 1-smooth thickening of
A
(see Sec.
1.1
).

As a possible approach to Conjecture A, we suggest the following conjecture giving a criterion of extendability to the standard NC-smooth thickening in terms of
the Fourier-Mukai transform
F:D^{b}(A)\to D^{b}(\hat{A})
(see
[
10
]
).

Conjecture B
.

Let
A
be an abelian variety,
A^{NC}
its standard NC-smooth thickening. An object
E\in D^{b}(A)
extends to a bounded complex of vector bundles on
A^{NC}

if and only if the Fourier-Mukai transform of
E
is supported in dimension
\leq 1
.

We prove the “if” part in Theorem
1.2.1
.
Note that existence of a resolution by locally free
{\mathcal{O}}_{A^{NC}}
-modules of finite rank is much more restrictive than in commutative case (see e.g., Cor.
1.2.3
).

Instead of asking which sheaves on a variety
X
extend to an NC-smooth thickening, one can ask a similar question for deformation quantizations of Poisson structures on
X
.
In the case of constant Poisson structures on abelian varieties and the corresponding Moyal deformation quantizations, we show that there is a relation between these questions.
Namely, we construct a homomorphism from the structure sheaf of standard NC-smooth thickening
{\mathcal{O}}_{A}^{NC}
to the deformation quantization algebra associated with any constant Poisson bivector (see Proposition
2.2.2
).
Using the result of
[
2
]
we prove that existence of an extension of an appropriate sheaf on the theta-divisor to the first-order deformation
quantization associated with sufficiently generic constant Poisson bivectors implies that the dimension of the singular locus of
\Theta
is
\geq\dim A-4
(see Prop.
2.3.1
).

On the other hand, we show that the “only if” part of Conjecture B does not hold if we replace the NC-smooth thickening by the corresponding first-order thickening.
Namely, using the results of Toda
[
13
]
we give an example of a coherent sheaf on an abelian surface which extends to the standard 1-smooth thickening but
whose Fourier-Mukai transform has full support (see Sec.
2.4
).

Conventions and notation
. We work over
{\mathbb{C}}
.
We denote by
\operatorname{ch}^{H}
and
c_{1}^{H}
the characteristic classes with values in
H^{*}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
(sometimes we also view
c_{1}^{H}
as an element
of
H^{1}(X,\Omega^{1}_{X})
); and by

\operatorname{ch}
and
c_{1}
the characteristic classes with values in the Chow group. For a smooth variety
X
we denote by
D^{b}(X)
the bounded derived category of coherent
sheaves on
X
.

1.
NC-smooth thickenings and NC-Fourier transform

1.1.
NC-smooth thickenings

We refer to
[
8
]
and
[
12
, Sec. 2.1]
for basics on NC-smooth thickenings.

The commutator filtration
F^{d}R
on a ring
R
is defined by letting
F^{d}R
be a two-sided ideal generated by all expressions containing
d
-commutators
(possibly nested). The ring is called NC-nilpotent if
F^{d}R=0
for some
d
, and NC-complete if the natural map
R\to\varprojlim R/F^{d}R
is an isomorphism.
NC-smoothness for NC-complete algebras is defined similarly to formal smoothness, in terms of nilpotent extensions of NC-smooth algebras.

These notions can be globalized, so one can consider sheaves of NC-smooth algebras. For a smooth variety
X
, an NC-smooth thickening of
X
is defined
as a sheaf of NC-smooth algebras
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}^{NC}
together with an isomorphism
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}^{NC}/F^{1}\simeq{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
.

One can also consider
n
-smooth thickenings by considering only NC-algebras (or sheaves) with
F^{n+1}=0
. For example, a
1
-smooth thickening of a smooth variety

X
is a central square zero extension of sheaves of algebras

0\to{\mathcal{N}}\to\widetilde{{\mathcal{O}}}_{X}\to{\mathcal{O}}_{X}\to 0

such that the commutator pairing
f,g\mapsto[\widetilde{f},\widetilde{g}]
, where
\widetilde{f}
and
\widetilde{g}
are liftings of
f,g\in{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
to
\widetilde{{\mathcal{O}}}_{X}
, induces an isomorphism

\Omega_{X}^{2}\rTo{\sim}{\mathcal{N}}
. In particular, for every
X
there is a
standard
1-smooth thickening,

{\mathcal{O}}_{X}^{1-NC}=\Omega_{X}^{2}\oplus{\mathcal{O}}_{X},

equipped with the product
(\alpha,f)\cdot(\beta,g)=(g\alpha+f\beta+dg\wedge dg,fg)

(see
[
8
, (1.3.9)]
).

In
[
12
]
we developed a construction of an NC-smooth thickening associated with a torsion free connection on the tangent bundle
T_{X}
(in the case of a flat connection it is recalled in Sec.
2.1
below).
Applying this construction to the natural flat connection on an abelian variety
A
we get the
standard
NC-smooth thickening
A^{NC}

(more generally, this works for commutative algebraic groups, see
[
12
, Def. 2.3.19]
). The induced 1-smooth thickening
{\mathcal{O}}_{A}^{NC}/F^{2}
is the standard
1-smooth thickening of
A
discussed above (by
[
12
, Prop. 2.3.18]
).

1.2.
NC-Fourier transform

Let
A
be an abelian variety,
\hat{A}
the dual abelian variety.
It was observed in
[
12
]
that the restriction of Fourier-Mukai transform
F:D^{b}(\hat{A})\to D^{b}(A)
to
D^{b}(C)
, for a curve
C\subset\hat{A}
,
extends to an NC-Fourier transform
F^{NC}:D^{b}(C)\to\operatorname{Per}(A^{NC})
, where
A^{NC}
is the standard NC-smooth thickening of
A
, and
\operatorname{Per}(A^{NC})

denotes the full subcategory in the derived category of
{\mathcal{O}}_{A^{NC}}
-modules formed by bounded complexes of vector bundles (i.e., locally free left modules of finite rank).

In the next theorem we use a slight extension of this construction to prove the “if” part of Conjecture B.

Theorem 1.2.1
.

Let
A
be an abelian variety,
A^{NC}
its standard thickening.
If the Fourier-Mukai transform of
E\in D^{b}(A)
has support of dimension
\leq 1
then

E
extends to a perfect object in
D^{b}(A^{NC})
. Furthermore, for any closed subset
Z\subset\hat{A}
of dimension
\leq 1
,
there exists a functor

D^{b}_{Z}(\hat{A})\to\operatorname{Per}(A^{NC}),

where
D^{b}_{Z}(\hat{A})\subset D^{b}(\hat{A})
is the full subcategory of objects with support on
Z
,
extending the Fourier-Mukai transform
D^{b}_{Z}(\hat{A})\to D^{b}(A)
.

Proof
.
Let
Z\subset\hat{A}
be a closed subset
of dimension
1
. We can find two affine open subsets
U_{1},U_{2}\subset\hat{A}

such that
Z\subset U:=U_{1}\cup U_{2}
.
We have
D^{b}_{Z}(\hat{A})=D^{b}_{Z}(U)
, so it is enough
to construct an integral transform
D^{b}_{Z}(U)\to\operatorname{Per}(A^{NC})

extending the Fourier transform
D^{b}_{Z}(U)\to D^{b}(A)
.
As in
[
12
, Sec. 4.3]
, for this we construct an extension of the Poincare line bundle to
U\times A^{NC}
.

Let
A^{\natural}\to\hat{A}
denote the
H^{0}(A,\Omega^{1})
-torsor parametrizing line bundles with connections on
A
.
Since
U_{i}
are affine, we can choose liftings of
U_{1}
and
U_{2}
to
A^{\natural}
.
As is explained in
[
12
, Sec. 4.3]
, using these liftings we get extensions of the Poincaré bundle over
U_{i}\times A
to
U_{i}\times A^{NC}
and
an isomorphism between the induced extensions on
(U_{1}\cap U_{2})\times A^{NC}
.
Hence, we obtain a line bundle on
U\times A^{NC}
that we can use as a kernel of an integral
transform

F_{U}:D^{b}(\operatorname{Qcoh}(U))\to D({\mathcal{O}}^{NC}_{A}-\operatorname{mod})

which lifts the corresponding integral transform from
D^{b}(\operatorname{Qcoh}(U)
to
D(\operatorname{Qcoh}A)
.
It remains to prove that
F_{U}({\mathcal{F}})
is a perfect complex for any
{\mathcal{F}}
supported on
Z
.
It is enough to prove this for skyscraper sheaves and for coherent sheaves obtained as push-forwards
from normalizations of irreducible components of
Z
. But this follows from
[
12
, Thm. 4.3.6]
.
∎

One indirect evidence if favor of Conjecture B is that both conditions imply that
\operatorname{ch}^{H}_{\geq 2}(E)=0
:
for the condition that
F(E)
is supported in dimension
\leq 1
this follows from the formula for the action of the Fourier-Mukai transform on cohomology
(see
[
11
, Cor. 1.18]
). For the condition that
E
extends to a perfect complex on
A^{NS}
this is a consequence of the following result.

Proposition 1.2.2
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety.
If an object
E\in D^{b}(X)
extends to a bounded complex of vector bundles on the standard 1-smooth thickening of
X
then
\operatorname{ch}^{H}_{\geq 2}(E)=0
.

Proof.

This follows immediately from
[
12
, Cor. 3.3.5]
.
∎

Corollary 1.2.3
.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}
be a nonzero coherent sheaf on a smooth connected projective variety
X
.

(i) If
{\mathcal{F}}
has support of codimension
\geq 2
then
{\mathcal{F}}
does not extend to a perfect complex
on the standard 1-smooth thickening of
X
.

(ii) Assume
{\mathcal{F}}=i_{*}{\mathcal{G}}
, where
i:D\to X
is the embedding of a smooth connected divisor, and
{\mathcal{G}}
is a coherent
sheaf of rank
r
on
D
. If
{\mathcal{F}}
extends to a perfect complex on the standard 1-smooth thickening of
X
then one has

i_{*}(c^{H}_{1}({\mathcal{G}})-r\cdot c_{1}^{H}({\mathcal{O}}(D)|_{D})/2)=0

in
H^{4}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
.

Proof.

(i) Let
n=\dim X
, and let
m
be the dimension of the support of
X
. Then
\operatorname{ch}({\mathcal{F}})_{n-m}=\sum_{i}a_{i}[Z_{i}]\in\operatorname{CH}^{n-m}(X)_{{\mathbb{Q}}}
for some
irreducible subvarieties
Z_{i}
of dimension
m
and some positive coefficients
a_{i}
, and
\operatorname{ch}({\mathcal{F}})_{<n-m}=0
. It follows that if
h
is an ample divisor class
then
\operatorname{ch}^{H}({\mathcal{F}})_{n-m}\cdot h^{m}\neq 0
, so
\operatorname{ch}^{H}({\mathcal{F}})_{n-m}\neq 0
. Thus, if
m\leq n-2
we cannot have
\operatorname{ch}^{H}_{\geq 2}({\mathcal{F}})=0
. So, by Proposition

1.2.2
,
{\mathcal{F}}
cannot extend to the standard 1-smooth thickening of
X
.

(ii) This follows immediately by applying the Grothendieck-Riemann-Roch theorem
to rewrite the condition
\operatorname{ch}^{H}_{2}(i_{*}{\mathcal{G}})=0
.
∎

Remarks 1.2.4
.

1. Note that both conditions in Conjecture B are preserved by pull-backs under homomorphisms between abelian varieties (as well as by translations) and by tensoring with line bundles in
\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(A)
. This follows from the standard
compatibility of the Fourier-Mukai transform with these operations and from the fact that they extend to the standard NC-smooth smooth thickenings
(see
[
12
, Prop. 2.3.20]
and
[
12
, Thm. 3.3.8(ii)]
).

2. The results of
[
12
, Sec. 3.2]
suggest that a plausible analog of Conjecture B for
{\mathcal{O}}_{A}^{NC}
-bimodules is that an object
E\in D^{b}(A)
extends to a complex of
{\mathcal{O}}_{A}^{NC}
-bimodules,
locally free of finite rank as left or right modules, if and only the Fourier-Mukai transform of
E
has
0
-dimensional support.

1.3.
Extensions of the theta-divisor to the NC-thickening

Using Theorem
1.2.1
we get a proof of the “only if” direction of Conjecture A.

Theorem 1.3.1
.

Let
(J,\Theta)
be the Jacobian of a smooth projective curve
C
, with its theta divisor. Then there exists a line bundle
L
on the smooth part
\Theta^{ns}
of
\Theta
,
such that the coherent sheaf
{\mathcal{F}}:=j^{ns}_{*}L
on
\Theta
, where
j^{ns}:\Theta^{ns}\to J
is the natural embedding,
extends to a perfect complex on
J^{NC}
, the standard NC-thickening of
J
.

Proof.

Let
i:C\to J
be the embedding associated with some point, and let
L
be a line bundle of degree
g-1

on
C
. Then the Fourier-Mukai transform of
i_{*}L
, is of the form
{\mathcal{F}}[-1]
, where
{\mathcal{F}}
is of the described form (see
[
4
, Prop. 2.3]
). It remains
to apply Theorem
1.2.1
.
∎

Remark 1.3.2
.

Note that if a coherent sheaf
{\mathcal{F}}
on
X
extends to a bounded complex
P^{\bullet}
of locally free modules of finite rank over for some NC-nilpotent thickening
A\to{\mathcal{O}}_{X}

then we can assume this complex to be concentrated in non-positive degrees (i.e.,
P^{>0}=0
). Indeed, if
P^{>n+1}=0
for
n\geq 0
, then

\operatorname{coker}(d_{n}:P^{n}\to P^{n+1})
becomes zero when tensored with
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
. Hence,
\operatorname{coker}(d_{n})=0
, so we can replace
P^{n}
with
\operatorname{ker}(d_{n})
and
P^{n+1}
with
0
.
Iterating this we will get a complex in non-positive degrees.

Recall (see
[
10
]
) that a coherent sheaf
{\mathcal{F}}
on an abelian variety
A
is called an IT-sheaf (resp., WIT-sheaf) of index
i
if
H^{j}(A,{\mathcal{F}}\otimes P)=0
for
j\neq i
for
every
P\in\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(A)
(resp.,
\underline{H}^{j}F({\mathcal{F}})=0
for
j\neq i
, where
F:D^{b}(A)\to D^{b}(\hat{A})
is the Fourier-Mukai transform).

The following characterization of Jacobians is due to
[
9
]
(based on Matsusaka-Ran criterion).

Theorem 1.3.3
.

(
[
9
, Thm. 4.3]
) Let
(A,\Theta)
be an indecomposable principally polarized abelian variety.
Suppose there exists a WIT-sheaf
{\mathcal{G}}
of index
g
on
A
such that
c_{i}^{H}({\mathcal{G}})=(-1)^{i}\theta^{i}/i!
, where
\theta=c_{1}^{H}({\mathcal{O}}(\Theta))
. Then
(A,\Theta)
is a Jacobian of
a smooth curve.

Here is a version of this result that is better adapted to our context.

Proposition 1.3.4
.

Let
(A,\Theta)
be a principally polarized abelian variety, such that
\Theta
is irreducible.
Assume that there exists a coherent sheaf
{\mathcal{F}}
on
A
, supported sheaf theoretically on
\Theta
and of length
1

at the general point of
\Theta
,
such that
the Fourier transform of
{\mathcal{F}}
is supported in dimension
\leq 1
. Then
(A,\Theta)
is a Jacobian.

Proof
. We will reduce this to Theorem
1.3.3
.
By assumption,
{\mathcal{F}}=F(E)
, where
E
is an object of derived category of
\hat{A}\simeq A
with the
support
Z\subset A
of dimension
\leq 1
. Pick an ample divisor
D
that intersects each component
of
Z
transversally, so that all the maps
\underline{H}^{i}E\to\underline{H}^{i}E(D)
are injective.
Consider the exact triangle

E\to E(D)\to E^{\prime}\to E[1].

Applying the Fourier transform and passing to cohomology we get an exact sequence

0\to\underline{H}^{-1}F(E(D))\to\underline{H}^{-1}F(E^{\prime})\to{\mathcal{F}}\to\underline{H}^{0}F(E(D))\to\underline{H}^{0}F(E^{\prime})\to 0

and isomorphisms

\underline{H}^{i}F(E(D))\rTo{\sim}\underline{H}^{i}F(E^{\prime})\ \text{ for }i\neq 0,-1.

If
D
is sufficiently ample then the sheaves
\underline{H}^{i}E(D)
and
\underline{H}^{i}E^{\prime}
are IT-sheaves of index
0
. Hence,
we have

\underline{H}^{i}F(E(D))\simeq F(\underline{H}^{i}E(D)),\ \ \underline{H}^{i}F(E^{\prime})\simeq F(\underline{H}^{i}E^{\prime}).

Thus,
\underline{H}^{0}F(E(D))
is a vector bundle and the map
{\mathcal{F}}\to H^{0}F(E(D))
from the torsion sheaf
{\mathcal{F}}

is zero. Thus, from the above exact sequence we get a short exact sequence

0\to F(\underline{H}^{-1}E(D))\to F(\underline{H}^{-1}E^{\prime})\to{\mathcal{F}}\to 0.

On the other hand, since the map
\underline{H}^{0}E\to\underline{H}^{0}E(D)
is injective, we obtain an exact sequence

0\to\underline{H}^{-1}E\to\underline{H}^{-1}E(D)\to\underline{H}^{-1}E^{\prime}\to 0,

so the sheaf
\underline{H}^{-1}E^{\prime}
has zero-dimensional support.
This implies that
c_{1}(F(\underline{H}^{-1}E^{\prime}))=0
. Hence, the sheaf

{\mathcal{G}}:=F(\underline{H}^{-1}E(D))

satisfies

c_{1}^{H}({\mathcal{G}})=-c_{1}^{H}({\mathcal{F}})=-\theta.

Also, since
{\mathcal{G}}
is the Fourier transform of a sheaf supported in
dimension
1
, we have
\operatorname{ch}_{\geq 2}^{H}({\mathcal{G}})=0
(as a consequence of Mukai’s formula
[
11
, Cor. 1.18]
),
which is equivalent to
c_{i}^{H}({\mathcal{G}})=c_{1}^{H}({\mathcal{G}})^{i}/i!
.
Finally, since
\underline{H}^{-1}E(D)
is an IT-sheaf of index
0
,
{\mathcal{G}}
is a WIT-sheaf of index
g
. Thus,
{\mathcal{G}}
satisfies the
conditions of Theorem
1.3.3
.
∎

Corollary 1.3.5
.

Conjecture B implies Conjecture A.

Proof.

We only need to check the “if” part of Conjecture A. If there exists a coherent sheaf
{\mathcal{F}}
on
A
with the stated properties
then by Conjecture B its Fourier transform has support of dimension
\leq 1
. Hence, by Proposition
1.3.4
,
(A,\Theta)
is a Jacobian.
∎

Recall that the Jacobians of curves belong to the Andreotti-Mayer locus of principally polarized abelian varieties

(A,\Theta)
for which the singular locus of
\Theta
has dimension
\geq g-4
.

Proposition 1.3.6
.

Assume
(A,\Theta)
is a principally polarized abelian variety of dimension
g\geq 4
, with
\Theta
irreducible.
Suppose there exists a coherent sheaf
{\mathcal{F}}
on
A
, sheaf theoretically supported on
\Theta
, of length
1
at the general point of
\Theta
,
and such that
\operatorname{ch}_{2}^{H}({\mathcal{F}})=0
.
Then
\dim\operatorname{Sing}(\Theta)\geq g-4
.

Proof
. Set
A^{\prime}:=A\setminus\operatorname{Sing}(\Theta)
,
\Theta^{\prime}:=\Theta\setminus\operatorname{Sing}(\Theta)
,
and let
N={\mathcal{O}}_{\Theta}(\Theta)
be the normal line bundle to
\Theta
.
Consider the closed embedding
i:\Theta^{\prime}\to A^{\prime}
.
By assumption,
{\mathcal{F}}|_{A^{\prime}}=i_{*}F
, where
F
is a coherent sheaf on
\Theta^{\prime}
, generically of rank
1
.
By the Grothendick-Riemann-Roch theorem applied to the embedding
i
, we have

\operatorname{ch}_{2}({\mathcal{F}}|_{A^{\prime}})=i_{*}[\operatorname{ch}(F)\operatorname{Td}^{-1}(N|_{\Theta^{\prime}})]_{2}=i_{*}(c_{1}(F)-\frac{c_{1}(N|_{\Theta^{\prime}})}{2})

in
\operatorname{CH}_{g-2}(A^{\prime})_{{\mathbb{Q}}}
. Assume that
\dim\operatorname{Sing}(\Theta)<g-4
. Then the natural maps

\operatorname{Pic}(A)\to\operatorname{Pic}(\Theta)\to\operatorname{CH}_{g-2}(\Theta)\to\operatorname{CH}_{g-2}(\Theta^{\prime})

(1.3.1)

are isomorphisms (see
[
4
, Sec. 3.1]
), so there exists a class
\ell\in\operatorname{Pic}(A)
such that
c_{1}(F)
is the
restriction of
\ell
to
\Theta^{\prime}
. Thus, we get an equality in
\operatorname{CH}_{g-2}(A^{\prime})_{{\mathbb{Q}}}
,

\operatorname{ch}_{2}({\mathcal{F}}|_{A^{\prime}})=c_{1}(\Theta)\cdot(\ell-\frac{c_{1}(\Theta)}{2})|_{A^{\prime}}.

Since the restriction map
\operatorname{CH}_{g-2}(A)\to\operatorname{CH}_{g-2}(A^{\prime})
is an isomorphism, this implies
that

\operatorname{ch}_{2}({\mathcal{F}})=c_{1}(\Theta)\cdot(\ell-\frac{c_{1}(\Theta)}{2})=0

in
\operatorname{CH}_{g-2}(A)_{{\mathbb{Q}}}
and hence, we have the same equality in
H^{4}(A,{\mathbb{C}})
.
Since
\operatorname{ch}^{H}_{2}({\mathcal{F}})=0
in
H^{4}(A,{\mathbb{C}})
by assumption, we get

c_{1}^{H}(\Theta)\cup(\operatorname{cl}(\ell)-\frac{c_{1}^{H}(\Theta)}{2})=0,

where
\operatorname{cl}:\operatorname{CH}_{g-1}(A)\to H^{2}(A,{\mathbb{Z}})
is the cycle map. But the map

H^{2}(A,{\mathbb{C}})\rTo{\cup c_{1}^{H}(\Theta)}H^{4}(A,{\mathbb{C}})

∎

2.
Relation to deformation quantization

2.1.
Dg-constructions

2.1.1.
NC-smooth thickenings

Here we recall the construction of an NC-smooth thickening associated with a torsion free flat connection
\nabla
on
X

(see
[
12
]
) and a parallel construction of deformation quantizations (due to Fedosov
[
7
]
).

Namely, one can extend the connection
\nabla
on
T_{X}
to a derivation
D_{1}
on

{\mathcal{A}}_{X}:=\Omega^{\bullet}_{X}\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}\hat{T}_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}(\Omega^{1}_{X}),

extending the de Rham differential on
\Omega^{\bullet}_{X}
and such that for
\alpha\in\Omega^{1}_{X}
one has

D_{1}(1\otimes\alpha)=\nabla(\alpha).

On the other hand, one has an
\Omega^{\bullet}_{X}
-linear derivation
D_{0}
of
{\mathcal{A}}_{X}
such that
D_{0}(1\otimes\alpha)=\alpha\otimes 1
.

In
[
12
]
we studied the dg-algebra
({\mathcal{A}}_{X},D)
, where
D=D_{0}+D_{1}
, and showed that
\underline{H}^{0}({\mathcal{A}}_{X},D)
gives an NC-smooth thickening.
We also considered a more general version of this construction in
[
12
]
,
starting with a twisted version of
{\mathcal{A}}_{X}
and a not necessarily flat connection
\nabla
, but we will not use this more general construction here.

2.1.2.
Deformation quantizations

Recall that an (algebraic) deformation quantization of
X
is a sheaf of associative

k[\![h]\!]
-algebras
{\mathcal{D}}
on
X
, complete with respect to the
h
-adic topology and flat over
k[\![h]\!]
, equipped with
an isomorphism
\rho:{\mathcal{D}}/h{\mathcal{D}}\rTo{\sim}{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
.
In particular, looking at the commutator in
{\mathcal{D}}
one gets a Poisson bracket on
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
such that

ab-ba=h\{\rho(a),\rho(b)\}\operatorname{mod}h^{2}{\mathcal{D}}
, and one usually says that
{\mathcal{D}}
quantizes this Poisson
bracket. The Poisson bivector
P\in H^{0}(X,\bigwedge^{2}T_{X})
is characterized by the condition

\langle P,df\wedge dg\rangle=\{f,g\}
, where
f
and
g
are local functions on
X
.

Now let us start with a Poisson bivector
P
on
X
.
Assume that we have a torsion free flat connection
\nabla
on
X
such that the Poisson bivector

P\in H^{0}(X,\bigwedge^{2}T_{X})
is
\nabla
-horizontal. This means
that for any vector field
v
and any pair of functions
f,g
we have

v\cdot\{f,g\}=\{v\cdot f,g\}+\{f,v\cdot g\}.

The sheaf of (possibly degenerate) formal Weyl algebras associated with
P
is the quotient

{\mathcal{W}}_{P}=\hat{T}(\Omega^{1})[\![h]\!]/{\mathcal{J}}_{P},

where the two-sided ideal
{\mathcal{J}}_{P}
is generated by

\omega_{1}\otimes\omega_{2}-\omega_{2}\otimes\omega_{1}-h\langle P,\omega_{1}\wedge\omega_{2}\rangle.

By the PBW-theorem, we have a direct sum decomposition

\hat{T}(\Omega^{1})[\![h]\!]={\mathcal{J}}_{P}\oplus\hat{S}(\Omega^{1})[\![h]\!]

(2.1.1)

where
\hat{S}(\Omega^{1})
consists of symmetric tensors.
Thus, we have a canonical isomorphism of
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}[\![h]\!]
-modules

\hat{S}(\Omega_{X}^{1})[\![h]\!]\rTo{\sim}{\mathcal{W}}_{P},

(2.1.2)

whose reduction modulo
h
is an isomorphism of algebras.

Proposition 2.1.1
.

(i) The decomposition of
\Omega^{\bullet}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\hat{T}(\Omega^{1})[\![h]\!]

induced by (
2.1.1
)
is compatible with the the derivations
D_{0}
and
D_{1}
(extended
h
-linearly).

(ii) Let
\overline{D}_{0}
and
\overline{D}_{1}
denote the induced derivations of
\Omega^{\bullet}\otimes{\mathcal{W}}_{P}
.
Then
{\mathcal{D}}_{P,\nabla}:=\underline{H}^{0}(\Omega^{\bullet}\otimes{\mathcal{W}}_{P},\overline{D}_{0}+\overline{D}_{1})
is a deformation quantization of
P
.

(iii) If
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
is a formal coordinate system at a point
p\in X
, such that
dx_{i}
are
\nabla
-horizontal, then
we have an identification of the completion of
{\mathcal{D}}_{P,\nabla}
at
p
with
{\mathbb{C}}[\![x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},h]\!]
with the Moyal product

f*g=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k};j_{1},\ldots,j_{k}}\frac{h^{k}}{k!2^{k}}\Pi_{i_{1}j_{1}}\ldots\Pi_{i_{k}j_{k}}(\partial_{i_{1}}\ldots\partial_{i_{k}}f)(\partial_{j_{1}}\ldots\partial_{j_{k}}g).

(2.1.3)

Proof.

(i) We have
D_{0}(\omega_{1}\otimes\omega_{2}-\omega_{2}\otimes\omega_{1})=0
, which easily implies that
D_{0}({\mathcal{J}}_{P})\subset\Omega^{1}\otimes{\mathcal{J}}_{P}
. On the other hand, for any
\alpha\in\Omega^{1}

one has
D_{0}(\alpha^{\otimes n})\subset\Omega^{1}\otimes\hat{S}(\Omega^{1})
, hence
D_{0}(\hat{S}(\Omega^{1}))\subset\Omega^{1}\otimes\hat{S}(\Omega^{1})
.

To see invariance with respect to
D_{1}
,
let us choose a
\nabla
-horizontal basis
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}
of
\Omega_{X}^{1}
in the formal neighborhood of some point. Then
{\mathcal{J}}_{P}
is generated by

\alpha_{i}\otimes\alpha_{j}-\alpha_{j}\otimes\alpha_{i}-h\Pi_{ij}
where
\Pi_{ij}
are constants. Since these elements are killed by
D_{1}
, we get
D_{1}({\mathcal{J}}_{P})\subset\Omega^{1}\otimes{\mathcal{J}}_{P}
.
Similarly,
S(\Omega^{1})
is spanned by the monomials in
(\alpha_{i})
, which are killed by
D_{1}
, so
D_{1}(\hat{S}(\Omega^{1}))\subset\Omega^{1}\otimes\hat{S}(\Omega^{1})
.

(ii),(iii) By (i), we have an isomorphism of complexes

(\Omega^{\bullet}\otimes\hat{S}(\Omega^{1})[\![h]\!],D_{0}^{s}+D_{1}^{s})\rTo{\sim}(\Omega^{\bullet}\otimes{\mathcal{W}}_{P},\overline{D}_{0}+\overline{D}_{1}),

where
D_{i}^{s}
is the restriction of
D_{i}
,
i=0,1
. Viewing
\hat{S}(\Omega^{1})
as a completion of the symmetric algebra, it is easy to check that

(\Omega^{\bullet}\otimes\hat{S}(\Omega^{1}),D_{0}^{s})
is the completion of the Koszul complex
{\bigwedge}^{\bullet}(V)\otimes S^{\bullet}(V)
for
V=\Omega^{1}
. Thus, we can
consider the standard homotopy

H_{i}:{\bigwedge}^{i}(V)\otimes S^{j}(V)\to{\bigwedge}^{i-1}(V)\otimes S^{j+1}(V),\ i\geq 1,

of the positive degree terms of the Koszul complex given by the (suitably rescaled) action of the Euler vector field.
By
[
12
, Lem. 2.3.7]
, the operator
\operatorname{id}+HD_{1}^{s}
extends to a well defined
{\mathcal{O}}
-linear automorphism of
\Omega^{\bullet}\otimes\hat{S}(\Omega^{1})
such that

D_{0}^{s}+D_{1}^{s}=(\operatorname{id}+HD_{1}^{s})^{-1}D_{0}^{s}(\operatorname{id}+HD_{1}^{s}),

which induces an isomorphism of
0
th cohomology we are interested in with the
0
th cohomology of
D_{0}^{s}
, which is identified
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}[\![h]\!]
.

Explicitly, if
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
is a formal coordinate system as in (iii), then an element in
\operatorname{ker}(D_{0}^{s}+D_{1}^{s})\subset\hat{S}(\Omega^{1})
for a function
f
is

\kappa(f):=f+\sum_{m\geq 1,i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}}\frac{1}{m!}\partial_{i_{1}}\ldots\partial_{i_{m}}f\cdot dx_{i_{1}}\otimes\ldots\otimes dx_{i_{m}}.

It is well known (see e.g.,
[
7
]
) that if we equip
\hat{S}(\Omega^{1})
using the identification (
2.1.2
) with
{\mathcal{W}}_{P}
,
then
\kappa(f)\cdot\kappa(g)=\kappa(f*g)
, where
f*g
is given by (
2.1.3
).
∎

We will refer to the deformation quantization
{\mathcal{D}}_{P,\nabla}
above as the
Moyal deformation quantization
associated with
(P,\nabla)
.

2.2.
Homomorphisms from NC-smooth thickenings to deformation quantizations

It is natural to ask whether there exist any homomorphisms from NC-smooth thickenings of
X
to deformation quantizations of
X
.
The answer is always positive in the affine case as the following result shows. Recall that in the affine case an NC-smooth thickening always exists and
is unique up to isomorphism
(see
[
8
]
).

Proposition 2.2.1
.

Let
X=\operatorname{Spec}(A)
be a smooth affine variety, and let
A^{NC}
be the NC-smooth completion of
A
.
Then for any deformation quantization
D
of
A
, there exists a homomorphism
A^{NC}\to D
compatible with the filtrations

(F^{n}A^{NC})
and
(h^{n}D)
, and inducing
the identity
A^{NC}/F^{1}=A\to A=D/hD
. For any such homomorphism, the induced map

\Omega^{2}_{A}\simeq F^{1}/F^{2}\to hD/h^{2}D=hA

is given by the Poisson bivector associated with
D
.

Proof.

It suffices to construct recursively a collection of compatible homomorphisms
\phi_{n}:A^{NC}/F^{n}A^{NC}\to D/h^{n}D
, starting with the identity map

A^{NC}/F^{1}\to D/hD
. Assume we already constructed a homomorphism
\phi_{n-1}:A^{NC}/F^{n-1}A^{NC}\to D/h^{n-1}D
compatible with the filtrations.
Now we use the fact that
A^{NC}/F^{n}A^{NC}
is the universal square zero extension of
A^{NC}/F^{n-1}A^{NC}
by a central bimodule (see

[
8
, Prop. (1.6.2)]
). This implies that
A^{NC}/F^{n}A^{NC}
maps to any other central extension of
A^{NC}/F^{n-1}A^{NC}
(see

[
8
, Prop. (1.3.8)]
).
Now
D/h^{n}D
is a central extension of
D/h^{n-1}D
by
D/hD
, and we can pull it back to get a central extension of
A^{NC}/F^{n-1}A^{NC}
.
Applying the universality we get the required homomorphism
\phi_{n}
.

The last assertion is obtained by considering the commutator maps

F^{0}/F^{1}\times F^{0}/F^{1}\to F^{1}/F^{2},\ \ D/hD\times D/hD\to hD/h^{2}D,

and observing that the first one is identified with
(f,g)\mapsto df\wedge dg
and the second is the Poisson bracket associated with
D
.
∎

Now we consider the global setting for the NC-thickening associated with a torsion-free flat connection.

Proposition 2.2.2
.

Let
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}^{NC}
be the smooth NC-thickening of
X
associated with a torsion-free flat connection

\nabla
on
X
.
Let
P
be a
\nabla
-horizontal Poisson bivector on
X
,
and let
{\mathcal{D}}={\mathcal{D}}_{P,\nabla}
be the Moyal deformation quantization algebra associated with
(P,\nabla)
(see Proposition
2.1.1
).
Then there exists a
homomorphism

{\mathcal{O}}_{X}^{NC}\to{\mathcal{D}},

(2.2.1)

compatible with the filtrations
(F^{n}=F^{n}{\mathcal{O}}_{X}^{NC})
and
(h^{n}{\mathcal{D}})
, such that the induced map
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}=F^{0}/F^{1}\to{\mathcal{D}}/h{\mathcal{D}}={\mathcal{O}}_{X}
is the identity,
and the induced map
\Omega^{2}_{X}=F^{1}/F^{2}\to h{\mathcal{D}}/h^{2}{\mathcal{D}}=h\cdot{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
is given by
h\Pi
.

Proof.

We use the dg-constructions of Sec.
2.1
defining
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}^{NC}
and
{\mathcal{D}}_{P,\nabla}
.
The natural projection gives a homomorphism of sheaves of dg-algebras

(\Omega_{X}^{\bullet}\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}\hat{T}(\Omega^{1}_{X}),D_{0}+D_{1})\to(\Omega_{X}^{\bullet}\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}{\mathcal{W}}_{P},\overline{D}_{0}+\overline{D}_{1}).

Passing to the
0
th cohomology sheaves we get homomorphism (
2.2.1
).
The identification of
F^{0}/F^{1}
(resp.,
{\mathcal{D}}/h{\mathcal{D}}
) with
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
is induced by the natural projection
\hat{T}(\Omega^{1})\to{\mathcal{O}}_{X}

(resp.,
{\mathcal{W}}_{P}\to{\mathcal{W}}_{P}/h{\mathcal{W}}_{P}\to{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
). This implies the assertion about the induced map on
F^{0}/F^{1}
. The assertion about the induced map
on
F^{1}/F^{2}
follows by considering commutators, as in the proof of Proposition
2.2.1
.
∎

Corollary 2.2.3
.

Let
E
is an object of
D^{b}(A)
, where
A

.
Then

extends to a perfect complex over the Moyal deformation quantization of any constant Poisson bracket

on

Proof.

By Theorem
1.2.1
, there exists an extension of

to a perfect complex
\widetilde{E}
of
{\mathcal{O}}_{A}^{NC}
-modules.
Now consider a homomorphism from
{\mathcal{O}}_{A}^{NC}
to the Moyal quantization
{\mathcal{A}}_{P}
of

-modules extending

.
∎

Examples 2.2.4
.

1. Let

be a smooth genus

(by the main result of

.
Thus, by Theorem

2. More generally, let

be an abelian surface, and let

Then still by

2.3.
First order deformation quantizations of abelian varieties and the theta divisor

One can also ask whether a version of the Conjecture A holds in which the NC-thickening
of
A
is replaced by the deformation quantization with respect to a generic constant Poisson structure on
A

We are going to show that the condition of extendability to
the 1st order deformation quantization implies that
(A,\Theta)
is in the Andreotti-Mayer locus.

Let
P\in H^{0}(X,\bigwedge^{2}T_{X})
be a global bivector field on a smooth variety
X

By the
standard 1st order deformation of
X

P

{\mathcal{O}}^{P}_{\leq 1}={\mathcal{O}}_{X}[h]/h^{2}

(f_{0}+f_{1}h)\star(g_{0}+g_{1}h)=f_{0}g_{0}+(\{f_{0},g_{0}\}+f_{0}g_{1}+f_{1}g_{0})h,

where
\{f_{0},g_{0}\}=\langle P,df_{0}\wedge dg_{0}\rangle

Proposition 2.3.1

Let
(A,\Theta)

g\geq 4

\Theta

Z

Lemma 2.4.1

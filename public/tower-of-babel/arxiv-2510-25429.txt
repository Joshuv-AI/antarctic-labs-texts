Schrödinger-invariance in non-equilibrium critical dynamics
Malte Henkel and Stoimen Stoimenov

Abstract
The scaling functions of single-time and two-time correlators in systems undergoing non-equilibrium critical dynamics with dynamical exponent 𝓏 = 2 \mathpzc{z}=2 are predicted from a new time-dependent non-equilibrium representation of the Schrödinger algebra. These explicit predictions are tested and confirmed in the ageing of several exactly solvable models.

Introduction: dynamical Schrödinger-symmetry

Many-body systems with strongly interacting degrees of freedom may display collective properties which need not be obvious from the
consideration of a single degree of freedom. While there only exist very few analytically solvable systems, they are often studied through
numerical simulations, which may require huge computational resources. Here we wish to explore the application of dynamical symmetries, not least motivated

; a companion paper
will study dynamical symmetries in phase-ordering kinetics after a quench into the ordered phase with

3.5
Bosonic contact process

Particle-reaction models are widely studied in various branches of non-equilibrium systems.
Here we concentrate on the bosonic variant where on any site of the lattice, particles can
arbitrarily accumulate. One admits a single type
A
of particles.
Single particles can move diffusively, with rate
D
, to a nearest-neighbour site, irrespectively of its occupation. In addition,
particles are allowed to undergo the reactions
Howa97
;
Paes04

A\to 2A\mbox{\rm~~with rate $\mu_{2}$}\;\;,\;\;A\to\emptyset\mbox{\rm~~with rate $\mu_{0}$}

(33)

and is called the
bosonic contact process with diffusion
(
bcpd
).
Clearly, for
\mu_{2}>\mu_{0}
, the whole lattice with be filled while for
\mu_{2}<\mu_{0}
the particles will die out.
There is a critical line
\mu_{2}=\mu_{0}
. The model leads to diffusion equations to average particle numbers and correlators.
While the mean particle number
\langle\int\!{\rm d}\bm{r}\>a(t,\bm{r})\rangle=\rho_{0}

stays constant on the critical line, one studies the connected correlator

C(t,s;\bm{r})=\langle a(t\bm{r}_{0})a(s,\bm{r}+\bm{r}_{0})\rangle-\rho_{0}^{2}

(34)

The main issue is the comparison with non-equilibrium field-theory, since the action decomposes

{\cal J}[\phi,\widetilde{\phi}]={\cal J}_{0}[\phi,\widetilde{\phi}]+{\cal J}_{b}[\phi,\widetilde{\phi}]
, where

\displaystyle{\cal J}_{0}[\phi,\widetilde{\phi}]

\displaystyle=\int\!{\rm d}t\int\!{\rm d}\bm{r}\>\widetilde{\phi}\bigl(2{\cal M}\partial_{t}-\Delta_{\bm{r}}\bigr)\phi

(35a)

\displaystyle{\cal J}_{b}[\phi,\widetilde{\phi}]

\displaystyle=-\mu_{2}\int\!{\rm d}t\int\!{\rm d}\bm{r}\>\widetilde{\phi^{2}}\bigl(\phi+\rho\bigr)

(35b)

These forms follow from probability conservation.
The deterministic action
{\cal J}_{0}
is the standard one, while the form of
{\cal J}_{b}

is distinct from the ones considered so far. This will not modify the calculation of a
response function
\langle\phi\widetilde{\phi}\rangle
. Because of the Bargman superselection rule,
correlation functions must be found by expanding perturbatively the noisy part

{\cal J}_{b}
. This will give non-vanishing contribution of a correlator
\langle\phi\phi\rangle

either from two applications of the first term in (
35b
) or else
a single application from the second term in (
35b
).
It can be shown that the first contribution is less relevant than the second one
Baum05b
.
Then one may define an effective temperature
T_{\rm eff}=\mu_{2}\rho_{0}
so that the leading terms are described by the same theory as exposed in
section
1
. Then the scaling contributions are exactly those in (
21
)
Paes04
;
Baum05a
,
up to normalisations and we know already that they agree with the predictions
(
12
), with
\nu=0
. The universal behaviour of the
bcpd
is in the same universality class as the
ew
-model,
see table
1
, which now includes the exact form of the
scaling functions. However, corrections to scaling should be distinct in both models.

3.6

As our last example, we study the

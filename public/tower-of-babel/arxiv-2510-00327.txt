1.
Introduction

A matrix
A\in\mathrm{Mat}_{n\times n}(\mathbb{R})

is called
totally nonnegative

if each minor is nonnegative.
That is, if for all
I,J\subseteq[n]:=\{1,\dotsc,n\}
with
|I|=|J|
,
the submatrix
A_{I,J}:=(a_{i,j})_{i\in I,j\in J}
satisfies

\det(A_{I,J})\geq 0
.
A matrix
A\in\mathrm{Mat}_{n\times n}(\mathbb{C})
is called

Hermitian
if it satisfies
A^{*}=A
where
*
denotes conjugate
transpose. Such a matrix is called
positive semidefinite

if we have
y^{*}Ay\geq 0
for all
y\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
.
It is known that this property is equivalent to the condition that

(1.1)

\det(A_{I,I})\geq 0\qquad\text{for all }I\subseteq[n].

For
A\in\mathrm{Mat}_{n\times n}(\mathbb{R})
, the Hermitian property
reduces to
y^{\negthickspace\top\negthickspace}Ay\geq 0
for all
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Some inequalities satisfied by the entries of
totally nonnegative matrices
are also satisfied by the entries of
Hermitian positive semidefinite matrices, with the latter inequalities
typically being discovered first.
(See
[
32
, §1]
for a short exposition.)
A subset of these inequalities concern
expressions
of the form

(1.2)

\frac{\mathrm{Imm}_{\theta}(A)}{\theta(e)},

where
\theta:\mathfrak{S}_{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
is an
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-character,

e
is the identity element of
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
, and
\mathrm{Imm}_{\theta}(A)
is the

\theta
-immanant

defined by

(1.3)

\mathrm{Imm}_{\theta}(A)=\sum_{w\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}}\negthinspace\theta(w)a_{1,w_{1}}\negthinspace\cdots{a}_{n,w_{n}}.

Since
\theta(e)
is the dimension of the representation
having character
\theta
, the ratio (
1.2
)
is clearly real when
A
is totally nonnegative.
To see that it is also real when
A
is Hermitian positive semidefinite,
let
\mathcal{T}_{n}
be the
real vector space
spanned by all
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-characters,
and recall that
\dim(\mathcal{T}_{n})

equals the number of

integer partitions
of
n
,
weakly decreasing positive integer sequences

\lambda

summing to
n
.
Let

\lambda\vdash n
denote that
\lambda
is a partition of
n
,
and consider
the irreducible character basis

\{\chi^{\lambda}\,|\,\lambda\vdash n\}
and
induced sign character basis

\{\epsilon^{\lambda}\,|\,\lambda\vdash n\}
of
\mathcal{T}_{n}
.
Arbitrary
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-characters
\theta
satisfy

(1.4)

\theta\in\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{N}}\{\chi^{\lambda}\,|\,\lambda\vdash n\}\subseteq\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{Z}}\{\epsilon^{\lambda}\,|\,\lambda\vdash n\},

with

(1.5)

\theta=\sum_{\lambda\vdash n}b_{\lambda}\epsilon^{\lambda}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\mathrm{Imm}_{\theta}(A)=\sum_{\lambda\vdash n}b_{\lambda}\mathrm{Imm}_{\epsilon^{\lambda}}(A),

and the
Littlewood–Merris–Watkins
identity
[
20
]
,
[
24
]
asserts that

(1.6)

\mathrm{Imm}_{\epsilon^{\lambda}}(A)=\sum\det(A_{I_{1},I_{1}})\cdots\det(A_{I_{\ell},I_{\ell}}),

where
\ell=\ell(\lambda)
is the number of nonzero components of
\lambda
,
and the sum is over all sequences
(I_{1},\dotsc,I_{\ell})
of disjoint
subsets of
[n]
satisfying
|I_{j}|=\lambda_{j}
.
This number is real by (
1.1
), and therefore
by (
1.4
) – (
1.5
)
the number (
1.2
) is real as well.

The study of
inequalities
for the expressions (
1.2
) evaluated at Hermitian
positive semidefinite matrices was originally motivated by work of
Hadamard and Schur,
and was later remotivated by generalizations of Marcus and Lieb.
(See
[
39
, §1]
.)
The study of inequalities for the expressions evaluated at totally nonnegative
matrices is motivated by Lusztig’s work on quantum groups and their
connection to total nonnegativity
[
22
]
.
(See also
[
30
, §1]
.)

Barrett and Johnson
[
2
]
showed that
expressions (
1.2
)
with induced sign characters
satisfy

(1.7)

\frac{\mathrm{Imm}_{\epsilon^{\lambda}}(A)}{\epsilon^{\lambda}(e)}\geq\frac{\mathrm{Imm}_{\epsilon^{\mu}}(A)}{\epsilon^{\mu}(e)}

for all real positive semidefinite matrices
if and only if
\lambda
is majorized by
\mu
,
i.e., if and only if we have

\lambda_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{i}\leq\mu_{1}+\cdots+\mu_{i}
for all
i
.
The author and Soskin
[
32
]

showed that the inequalities (
1.7
)
hold for all
n\times n
totally nonnegative matrices
under the same conditions on
\lambda
and
\mu
.
The problem of characterizing the inequalities (
1.7
)
which hold for all Hermitian positive semidefinite matrices remains
open.

Borcea–Brändén proved inequalities involving products of expressions
(
1.2
) when
A
is Hermitian positive semidefinite.
In particular for

k=2,\dotsc,n-1
we have
[
5
, Cor. 3.1(b)]

(1.8)

\left(\frac{\mathrm{Imm}_{\epsilon^{k,n-k}}(A)}{\epsilon^{k,n-k}(e)}\right)^{\negthickspace 2}\geq\left(\frac{\mathrm{Imm}_{\epsilon^{k+1,n-k-1}}(A)}{\epsilon^{k+1,n-k-1}(e)}\right)\left(\frac{\mathrm{Imm}_{\epsilon^{k-1,n-k+1}}(A)}{\epsilon^{k-1,n-k+1}(e)}\right)\negthinspace;

for

k=1,\dotsc,n-1
we have
[
5
, Cor. 3.1(c)]

(1.9)

\left(\frac{\mathrm{Imm}_{\epsilon^{k+1,n-k-1}}(A)}{\epsilon^{k+1,n-k-1}(e)}\right)^{\negthickspace k}\negthickspace\det(A)=\left(\frac{\mathrm{Imm}_{\epsilon^{k+1,n-k-1}}(A)}{\epsilon^{k+1,n-k-1}(e)}\right)^{\negthickspace k}\frac{\mathrm{Imm}_{\epsilon^{n}}(A)}{\epsilon^{n}(e)}\geq\left(\frac{\mathrm{Imm}_{\epsilon^{k,n-k}}(A)}{\epsilon^{k,n-k}(e)}\right)^{\negthickspace k+1}\negthickspace\negthickspace\negthickspace\negthickspace\negthinspace.

Inequalities (
1.8
) – (
1.9
)
are not known to hold for totally nonnegative matrices.

Schur
[
28
]
proved that for each
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-character
\theta
,
the inequality

(1.10)

\frac{\mathrm{Imm}_{\chi^{1,\dotsc,1}}(A)}{\chi^{1,\dots,1}(e)}=\det(A)\leq\frac{\mathrm{Imm}_{\theta}(A)}{\theta(e)}

holds for all Hermitian positive semidefinite matrices.
Stembridge
[
36
, Cor. 3.4]
showed that it holds
for totally nonnegative matrices as well.
Johnson showed that for each
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-character
\theta
, the
permanental analog

(1.11)

\frac{\mathrm{Imm}_{\chi^{n}}(A)}{\chi^{n}(e)}=\mathrm{per}(A)\geq\frac{\mathrm{Imm}_{\theta}(A)}{\theta(e)}

of (
1.10
) holds for totally nonnegative matrices
(unpublished; see
[
37
, p. 1088]
).
Lieb
[
18
]
conjectured the same for Hermitian positive semindefinite
matrices. This statement, known as the
permanental dominance conjecture

is still open.

The problems of characterizing inequalities of the form

(1.12)

\frac{\mathrm{Imm}_{\chi^{\lambda}}(A)}{\chi^{\lambda}(e)}\geq\frac{\mathrm{Imm}_{\chi^{\mu}}(A)}{\chi^{\mu}(e)}

for Hermitian positive semidefinite matrices and totally nonnegative matrices
are open,
with preliminary work of James
[
14
, Appendix]

and Stembridge
[
37
, §3]
,
not suggesting any simple criterion with which to compare

\lambda
,
\mu
.
On the other hand,
Merris conjectured
[
23
, §4]

and Heyfron proved
[
12
, Thm. 1]

a characterization of
the subset of inequalities (
1.12
)
which hold for Hermitian positive semidefinite matrices
when
\lambda
and
\mu
are
hook partitions
, i.e., have
the form

k1^{n-k}:=(k,\;\underbrace{\negthickspace 1,\dotsc,1\negthickspace}_{n-k}\;).

Theorem 1.1
.

For
A
an
n\times n
Hermitian positive semidefinite matrix we have

\mathrm{per}(A)=\frac{\mathrm{Imm}_{\chi^{n}}(A)}{\chi^{n}(e)}\geq\frac{\mathrm{Imm}_{\chi^{n-1,1}}(A)}{\chi^{n-1,1}(e)}\geq\frac{\mathrm{Imm}_{\chi^{n-2,1,1}}(A)}{\chi^{n-2,1,1}(e)}\geq\cdots\geq\frac{\mathrm{Imm}_{\chi^{1,\dotsc,1}}(A)}{\chi^{1,\dotsc,1}(e)}=\det(A).

We will prove that the same inequalities hold
whenever
A
is totally nonnegative.
In Section
2
we review symmetric functions and traces.
In Sections
3
–
4

we discuss chromatic symmetric functions, posets,
and their applications to total nonnegativity.
This leads to two proofs of our main theorem in Section
5
.

2.
Symmetric functions and
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-traces

Inside of
\mathcal{T}_{n}
, the
\mathbb{Z}
-module

\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{Z}}\{\chi^{\lambda}\,|\,\lambda\vdash n\}

of
virtual characters

is isomorphic to the
\mathbb{Z}
-module
\Lambda_{n}

of homogeneous symmetric functions of degree

Six standard bases of

(See, e.g.,
[
34

An involutive automorphism
\omega:\Lambda\rightarrow\Lambda

When
\lambda
is a hook partition
k1^{n-k}
,
we have a simple formula for
K_{\lambda,\mu}
which
depends only upon
k
and
the number
\ell(\mu)
of components of
\mu
.
This leads to a simple expansion of the corresponding irreducible
characters in terms of monomial traces.

Proposition 2.2
.

For each hook partition
k1^{n-k}\vdash n

and each arbitrary partition
\mu\vdash n
,
we have

(2.4)

K_{k1^{n-k}\negthinspace,\;\mu}=\binom{\ell(\mu)-1}{n-k},

and therefore

(2.5)

\chi^{k1^{n-k}}=\negthickspace\sum_{\ell=n-k+1}^{n}\negthickspace\binom{\ell-1}{n-k}\negthickspace\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mu\vdash n\\
\ell(\mu)=\ell\end{subarray}}\negthickspace\phi^{\mu}.

Proof.

To see (
2.4
), observe that for
k=1,\dotsc,n
,
each semistandard Young tableau
U
of shape
k1^{n-k}
and content
\mu

has the entry
1
in position
(1,1)
.
The remaining
n-k
entries of column
1
must be distinct,
must be chosen from the set
\{2,\dotsc,\ell=\ell(\mu)\}
, and
must appear in increasing order.
There are
\tbinom{\ell-1}{n-k}
ways to choose these.
The remaining
k-1
entries of row
1
are then uniquely determined by
completing the multiset
1^{\mu_{1}}\cdots\ell^{\mu_{\ell}}
.
These appear
in row
1
in weakly increasing order. Now apply (
2.2
)
to obtain (
2.5
).
∎

Given an
n
-element unit interval order
P
,
it is easy to explicitly describe
an element
D=D(P)\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}

satisfying
Y(D)=X_{\mathrm{inc}(P)}

[
31
, §3]
.

Algorithm 3.2
.

Given unit interval order
P
, do

(1)

For each element
y\in P
, compute

\beta(y):=\#\{x\in P\,|\,x\leq_{P}y\}-\#\{z\in P\,|\,z\geq_{P}y\}.

(2)

Label the poset elements
1,\dotsc,n

so that we have

\beta(1)\leq\cdots\leq\beta(n)
.

(3)

Define
w=w(P)=w_{1}\cdots w_{n}
by

w_{j}=\max(\{i\,|\,i\not>_{P}j\}\smallsetminus\{w_{1},\dotsc,w_{j-1}\})
.

(4)

Define
D=\sum_{v\leq w}v
, where
\leq
is the Bruhat order on
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
.

The element
D
produced by Algorithm
3.2
is usually written

C^{\prime}_{w}(1)
and is called the
Kazhdan–Lusztig basis element

indexed by
w

[
17
]
. (See
[
4
]
for more
information on this basis and the Bruhat order.)
The map
P\mapsto w(P)
defined by Steps 1–3 of Algorithm
3.2

is a bijection from
n
-element unit interval orders to the

\tfrac{1}{n+1}\tbinom{2n}{n}

312
-avoiding permutations in
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
,
and gives us the following result
[
7
, Cor. 7.5]
.
(See
[
3
]
for more information on pattern avoidance.)

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
P
be an
n
-element unit interval order and
w=w(P)
be the
corresponding
312
-avoiding permutation in
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
. Then we have

Y(C^{\prime}_{w}(1))=X_{\mathrm{inc}(P)}
.

P

3.4
) are already labeled

5.
Main result and open problems

We now show that Heyfron’s inequalities (Theorem
1.1
)
hold not only for Hermitian positive semidefinite matrices,
but also for totally nonnegative matrices.

Theorem 5.1
.

For each
n\times n
totally nonnegative matrix
A
we have

\mathrm{per}(A)=\frac{\mathrm{Imm}_{\chi^{n}}(A)}{\chi^{n}(e)}\geq\frac{\mathrm{Imm}_{\chi^{n-1,1}}(A)}{\chi^{n-1,1}(e)}\geq\frac{\mathrm{Imm}_{\chi^{n-2,1,1}}(A)}{\chi^{n-2,1,1}(e)}\geq\cdots\geq\frac{\mathrm{Imm}_{\chi^{1,\dotsc,1}}(A)}{\chi^{1,\dotsc,1}(e)}=\det(A).

Equivalently, for
k=2,\dotsc,n
,
the difference

(5.1)

\frac{\mathrm{Imm}_{\chi^{k1^{n-k}}}(x)}{\chi^{k1^{n-k}}(e)}-\frac{\mathrm{Imm}_{\chi^{(k-1)1^{n-k+1}}}(x)}{\chi^{(k-1)1^{n-k+1}}(e)}=\frac{\mathrm{Imm}_{\chi^{k1^{n-k}}}(x)}{\binom{n-1}{k-1}}-\frac{\mathrm{Imm}_{\chi^{(k-1)1^{n-k+1}}}(x)}{\binom{n-1}{k-2}}

is a totally nonnegative polynomial.

First proof.

Multiplying (
5.1
) by
\tfrac{(n-1)!}{(n-k)!(k-2)!}
,
we have

(5.2)

(k-1)\mathrm{Imm}_{\chi^{k1^{n-k}}}(x)-(n-k+1)\mathrm{Imm}_{\chi^{(k-1)1^{n-k+1}}}(x).

Let
A
be an
n\times n
totally nonnegative matrix.
By Theorem
4.2
, we may choose a planar network
F

whose path matrix is
A
.
By Theorem
4.6
,
the evaluation of (
5.2
) at
A
equals

(5.3)

\displaystyle(k-1)\negthinspace\sum_{\pi}\mathrm{wgt}(\pi)d_{k}(\pi)-(n-k+1)\negthinspace\sum_{\pi}\mathrm{wgt}(\pi)d_{k-1}(\pi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\pi}\mathrm{wgt}(\pi)[(k-1)d_{k}(\pi)-(n-k+1)d_{k-1}(\pi)],

where
d_{k}(\pi)
is the number of standard
\pi
-tableaux
of shape
k1^{n-k}
, and
the sums are over

\pi\in\bigcup_{K}\Pi_{e}(K)

with
K
varying over all bijective skeletons in
F
.
By Proposition
3.8

the difference in square brackets in the last sum equals
the number of standard
P(\pi)
-tableaux
of shape
k1^{n-k}
with one marked
entry in columns
2,\dotsc,k
which is incomparable to at
least one entry in an earlier column.
Since this number and
\mathrm{wgt}(\pi)
are nonnegative, the evaluation
(
5.3
) is nonnegative. Thus the polynomial
(
5.2
) is totally nonnegative
and so is the polynomial (
5.1
).
∎

Second proof.

Define the traces
\theta^{1},\dotsc,\theta^{n}

as in (
3.9
).
By Proposition
2.2

we have that
the hook irreducible character immanant indexed by
k1^{n-k}

belongs to the
n
-dimensional space spanned by

\mathrm{Imm}_{\theta^{1}}(x),\dotsc,\mathrm{Imm}_{\theta^{n}}(x)
. Specifically,

(5.4)

\mathrm{Imm}_{\chi^{k1^{n-k}}}(x)=\negthickspace\negthickspace\sum_{\ell=n-k+1}^{n}\negthickspace\binom{\ell-1}{n-k}\,\mathrm{Imm}_{\theta^{\ell}}(x).

It follows that for
k=2,\dotsc,n
, the difference (
5.1
)
expands in the basis of
\theta^{\ell}
-immanants as

c_{k,1}\mathrm{Imm}_{\theta^{1}}(x)+\cdots+c_{k,n}\mathrm{Imm}_{\theta^{n}}(x)

with nonnegative coefficients

c_{k,\ell}=\frac{\binom{\ell-1}{n-k}}{\binom{n-1}{k-1}}-\frac{\binom{\ell-1}{n-k+1}}{\binom{n-1}{k-2}}=\begin{cases}0&\text{if $\ell=1,\dotsc,n-k$},\\
\text{\raisebox{3.55658pt}{$\frac{1}{\binom{n-1}{k-1}}$}}&\text{if $\ell=n-k+1$},\\
\frac{n-\ell}{\ell-(n-k+1)}&\text{if $\ell=n-k+2,\dotsc,n$}.\end{cases}

By Proposition
4.10
, each immanant
\mathrm{Imm}_{\theta^{\ell}}(x)

is a totally nonnegative polynomial.
Thus for
k=2,\dotsc,n

the difference (
5.1
) is as well.
∎

Theorem
5.1
thus provides some progress on the problem
of understanding (
1.12
).

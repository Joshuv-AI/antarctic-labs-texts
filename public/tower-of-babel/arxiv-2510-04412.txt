Monomial curves and locally linear resolution
Takayuki Hibi and Peter Schenzel

Abstract
. For four elements of a Noetherian ring we construct complexes of free modules of length three (resp. five) by an explicit description of the homomorphisms of the free modules. We provide exactness criteria for them. As an application we use these results in order to describe explicit the minimal free resolution of the Hartshorne–Rao module of a monomial curve lying on a smooth quadric. Also it provides an example of linearly generated module with syzygies of arbitrary high degree.

1.
Introduction

Let
A=K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
denote the polynomial ring in
n
variables over a field
K
with each
\deg x_{i}=1
. Let
M=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{\infty}M_{i}
be a finitely generated positively graded module over
A
and

0\to F_{p}\to F_{p-1}\to\cdots\to F_{1}\to F_{0}\to M\to 0

the minimal graded free resolution of
M
with

F_{i}=\oplus_{j}A(-j)^{\beta_{ij}}=\oplus_{j=1}^{\beta_{i}}A(-d_{ij}).

We say that (
1
) is
locally linear
if there are integers
d<e
,
1<s<p-1
for which

F_{i}=\begin{cases}A(-d-i)^{\beta_{i}}&\text{ if }0\leq i<s,\\
A(-d-s)^{\beta_{s,\,d+s}}\oplus A(-e-s)^{\beta_{s,\,e+s}}&\text{ if }i=s,\\
A(-e-i)^{\beta_{i}}&\text{ if }s<i\leq p.\end{cases}

When (
1
) is locally linear, we call
g=e-d>0
the
gap
of
M
. At first glance, it seems unclear if there exists a locally linear resolution with an arbitrary large gap.

Question 1.1
.

Does there exist a locally linear resolution of a graded module whose gap is arbitrary large?

Especially, does there exist a locally linear resolution of a monomial ideal (
[
6
]
) whose gap is arbitrary large?

In the present paper, by using the defining ideal of a certain monomial curve, we construct a finitely generated positively graded module over
A
whose minimal graded free resolution is locally linear with an arbitrary large gap.

Let
0<b\leq a-2
be integers with
\gcd(a,b)=1
and
d=a+b
. Let
C\subset\mathbb{P}_{\Bbbk}^{3}
denote the monomial curve given as the image

(s:t)\in\mathbb{P}_{\Bbbk}^{1}\mapsto(s^{d}:s^{a}t^{b}:s^{b}t^{a}:t^{d})\in\mathbb{P}_{\Bbbk}^{3}.

Let
A=K[x_{0},x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}]
and
I_{C}\subset A
the defining ideal of
C
. We investigate the Hartshorne–Rao module
{HR}(C)=H^{1}_{A_{+}}(A/I_{C})
of
C
.

Theorem 1.2
.

With the previous notation we construct an
(a-b-1)\times(2(a-b+1))
matrix
\mathcal{D}
such that:

(a)

HR(C)(b)\cong A^{a-b-1}/\operatorname{Im}\mathcal{D}
.

(b)

The minimal free resolution of
HR(C)
is given by

\displaystyle 0\to A^{a-b-1}(-a-2)\to A^{2(a-b)}(-a-1)\to A^{a-b+1}(-a)\oplus A^{a-b+1}(-b-2)

\displaystyle\to A^{2(a-b)}(-b-1)\to A^{a-b-1}(-b)\to HR(C)\to 0.

(c)

\operatorname{Hom}_{\Bbbk}(HR(C),\Bbbk)\cong HR(C)(a+b-2)
.

In particular, as a corollary of (ii) of Theorem
1.2
, it follows that

Corollary 1.3
.

There exists a locally linear resolution whose gap is arbitrary large.

The present paper is organized as follows: First we fix a sequence of elements
\underline{f}=(f_{1},f_{2},f_{3},f_{4})
over a commutative Noetherian ring
A
. Second, based on some elementary linear algebra, we define matrices
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}^{\prime},\mathcal{B},\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D}
over
A
of appropriate size and prove some annihilation between them in Section
3
. As a consequence
they provide several complexes of free
A
-modules, among them

\displaystyle C^{\bullet}:0\to A^{a-1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{C}}}{{\longrightarrow}}A^{2a}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{A}}}{{\longrightarrow}}A^{2(a+1)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{B}}}{{\longrightarrow}}A^{2a}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{D}}}{{\longrightarrow}}A^{a-1}

\displaystyle C^{\bullet}_{1}:0\to A^{a-1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{C}}}{{\longrightarrow}}A^{2a}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{A}^{\prime}}}{{\longrightarrow}}A^{a+1}.

Under some weak conditions these complexes are exact.
Moreover, in Section
5
, as an application of the previous constructions we study the monomial curve on the smooth quadric given by
Q=x_{0}x_{3}-x_{1}x_{2}
and prove Theorem
1.2
. It would be a challenge to extend some of these results to larger sequences and arbitrary monomial curves in projective
3
-space.

Corollary 5.5

With the previous notation the following results hold:

(b)

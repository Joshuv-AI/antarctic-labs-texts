On Virial Expansion in Hard Sphere Model
Kiyoharu Kawana

Abstract
Virial expansion is a traditional approach in statistical mechanics that expresses thermodynamic quantities, such as pressure p p , as power series of density or chemical potential. Its radius of convergence can serve as a potential indicator of phase transition. In this study, we investigate the virial expansion of the hard-sphere model, using the known dimensionless virial coefficients B ~ k ​ ( k = 1 , 2 , ⋯ ) \tilde{B}_{k}~(k=1,2,\cdots) up to the 12 12 th order. We find that it is well fitted by B ~ k = 1.28 × k 1.90 \tilde{B}_{k}=1.28\times k^{1.90} , corresponding to the analytic continuation of the virial expansion of the pressure as ∼ Li − 1.90 ​ ( η ) \sim\mathrm{Li}_{-1.90}(\eta) , where η \eta is the packing fraction and Li s ​ ( x ) \mathrm{Li}_{s}(x) is the polylogarithm function. This implies the absence of singular behavior in the physical parameter space η ≤ η max ≈ 0.74 \eta\leq\eta_{\mathrm{max}}\approx 0.74 and no indication of phase transition in the virial expansion approach. In addition, we calculate the cluster-integral coefficients { b l } l = 1 ∞ \{b_{l}\}_{l=1}^{\infty} and observe that their asymptotic behavior resembles the results obtained in the large dimension limit ( D → ∞ D\rightarrow\infty ), suggesting that D = 3 D=3 might be already regarded as large dimension. However, the existence of phase transition in the hard-sphere model has been confirmed by numerous simulations, which clearly indicates that a naive extrapolation of the virial series can lead to unphysical results.

2
Classical fluid system and thermodynamic limit

We consider thermodynamic equilibrium of classical particles interacting with a Hamiltonian in the
D
-dimensional Euclidean space:

\displaystyle H_{N}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{p_{i}^{2}}{2m}+U_{N}(\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N})~,\quad U_{N}(\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N})=\sum_{i<j}^{N}v(x_{i},x_{j})~,

(1)

where
x_{i}=(x_{i}^{1},x_{i}^{2},\cdots,x_{i}^{D})
,
p_{i}^{2}=\sum_{M=1}^{D}(p_{i}^{M})^{2}
, and
v(x_{i},x_{j})
is a general two-body potential.
If we impose the translation invariance, the pair potential becomes a mere function of relative coordinate as
v(x_{1},x_{2})=v(x_{1}-x_{2})
, and we focus on this case in the following.

The grand-canonical partition function is defined by

\displaystyle\Xi[T,\mu,V]

\displaystyle=\sum_{N=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{N!}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{N}\int_{V}d^{D}x_{i}\int_{V}d^{D}p_{i}\right)e^{-\beta H_{N}+\beta\mu N}

\displaystyle=\sum_{N=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{N}}{N!}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{N}\int_{V}d^{D}x_{i}\right)e^{-\beta\sum_{i<j}v(x_{i}-x_{j})}~,

(2)

where
\beta=1/T
is the inverse temperature,
\mu
is the chemical potential, and
z=e^{\beta\mu}(2\pi mT)^{D/2}
is the fugacity.
Here,
\sum_{i<j}
means the summation over all different pairs
(x_{i},x_{j})
among
N
particles.
The integrand in Eq. (
2
) can be written as

\displaystyle W_{N}(\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N})

\displaystyle\coloneq e^{-\beta\sum_{i<j}v(x_{i},x_{j})}=\prod_{i<j}(1+f_{ij})

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{i<j}f_{ij}+\sum_{i<j}\underset{(k,l)\neq(i,j)}{\sum_{k<l}}f_{ij}f_{kl}+\cdots~,

(3)

where
f_{ij}\coloneq e^{-\beta v(x_{i}-x_{j})}-1
is the Mayer’s
f
function.
Then, the cluster expansion allows us to perform the summation of
N
in Eq. (
2
) as
[
1
,
2
]

\displaystyle\log\Xi[T,\mu,V]=V\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}b_{l}(V)z^{l}~,

(4)

where

\displaystyle b_{k}(V)\coloneq\frac{1}{k!V}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{k}d^{D}x_{i}\right)C_{k}(\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k})~

(5)

is the cluster integral.
Here,
C_{k}(\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k})
denotes the contribution in
W_{k}(\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k})
such that all the
k
-particles are connected each other by the Mayer’s function, i.e. connected diagrams.
In addition, the density is similarly expressed as

\displaystyle\frac{\langle N\rangle}{V}=\frac{\partial}{\partial(\beta\mu)}\frac{\log\Xi[T,\mu,V]}{V}=\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}lb_{l}(V)z^{l}~.

(6)

The existence of the thermodynamic limit

\displaystyle\beta p(T,\mu)

\displaystyle\coloneq\lim_{V\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log\Xi[T,\mu,V]}{V}~

(7)

is a nontrivial problem for a generic
N
-body potential;
however, in classical fluid systems, it is rigorously proven for short-range
N
-body potentials that satisfy several reasonable conditions
[
17
]
.
Since this is a crucial result, we briefly overview it below.

We first define the mutual potential energy by

\displaystyle M_{N_{1}N_{2}}(\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N_{1}};\{x_{j}^{{}^{\prime}}\}_{j=1}^{N_{2}})\coloneq U_{N_{1}+N_{2}}(\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N_{1}},\{x_{j}^{{}^{\prime}}\}_{j=1}^{N_{2}})-U_{N_{1}}(\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N_{1}})-U_{N_{2}}(\{x_{j}^{{}^{\prime}}\}_{j=1}^{N_{2}})~.

(8)

Then, we say that
U_{N}(\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N})
is
tempered
if there exist
{}^{\exists}\lambda>D
and
{}^{\exists}R_{0}>0
,
{}^{\exists}A\geq 0
such that

\displaystyle M_{N_{1}N_{2}}(\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N_{1}};\{x_{j}^{{}^{\prime}}\}_{j=1}^{N_{2}})\leq AN_{1}N_{2}e^{-\lambda r}

(9)

whenever
|x_{i}-x_{j}^{{}^{\prime}}|\geq r\geq R_{0}
for all
i=1,2,\cdots,N_{1}
and
j=1,2,\cdots,N_{2}
.
Roughly speaking, this temperedness implies the suppression of the positive part of
U_{N}
for the long-distance
r\rightarrow\infty
.
For example, in the pair potential case (
1
), this implies (with
N_{1}=N_{2}=1
)

\displaystyle v(x)\leq A|x|^{-\lambda}\quad\text{for}~|x|\geq R_{0}~,

(10)

which implies that positive (repulsive) part of
v(x)
should damp faster than
|x|^{-\lambda}(<|x|^{-D})
for
|x|\rightarrow\infty
.

In addition, we say that
U_{N}(\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N})
is
stable
if there exists
{}^{\exists}B\geq 0
such that

\displaystyle U_{N}(\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N})\geq-NB

(11)

for all
N=1,2,\cdots
and
{}^{\forall}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{N})\in(\mathbb{R}^{D})^{N}
.
The importance of this stability is that it implies the convergence of the grand-canonical partition function as

\displaystyle\Xi[T,\mu,V]

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{N=1}^{\infty}\frac{z^{N}}{N!}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{N}\int_{V}d^{D}x_{i}\right)e^{-\beta U_{N}(\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N})}

\displaystyle\leq 1+\sum_{N=1}^{\infty}\frac{z^{N}}{N!}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{N}\int_{V}d^{D}x_{i}\right)e^{-N\beta B}

\displaystyle=\exp\left(Vze^{\beta B}\right)~

(12)

for
{}^{\forall}z\geq 0
and
{}^{\forall}\beta\geq 0
.
Here, we list typical pair potentials that satisfy temperedness and stability
[
17
]
:

\bullet

Hard sphere potential

\displaystyle v_{\mathrm{HS}}(x)=\begin{cases}\infty&\text{for}|x|<2a\\
0&\text{for}|x|\geq 2a\end{cases}~,

(13)

where
a>0
corresponds to the radius of molecule.

\bullet

Pair potential with hard core

More generally, we can consider

\displaystyle v(x)=v_{\mathrm{HS}}(x)+\delta v(x)~,

(14)

where the positive part of
\delta v(x)
decreases faster than
|x|^{-D}
for
|x|\rightarrow\infty
and satisfies

\displaystyle\sum_{i=2}^{N}\delta v(x_{j}-x_{1})\geq-B

(15)

for all
N
and
{}^{\forall}(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{N})
such that
|x_{i}-x_{1}|\geq 2a
for
{}^{\forall}i\neq 1
.

\bullet

Lennard-Jones potential

\displaystyle v(x)=v_{0}\left[\left(\frac{\sigma}{r}\right)^{p}-\left(\frac{\sigma}{r}\right)^{q}\right]

(16)

with
p\geq D
and
q\geq D+1
.

Now the existence theorem of the thermodynamic limit (
7
) is given as follows.

Theorem
. The thermodynamic limit (
7
) exists for a stable and tempered potential
U_{N}(\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N})
.

\beta p(\mu,T)
is a convex function of
\mu
and
T
.
In particular,
p(\mu,T)
is a continuous function of
(\mu,T)
and an increasing function of
\mu
.

In the next section, we will argue that this rigorous result clearly highlights the applicability of cluster/virial expansion exclusively to a single unique phase.

3
Cluster/Virial expansion and its applicability

Figure 1:

Virial expansion and its analytic continuation.
We may find an analytic continuation
p_{\mathrm{ana}}(z)
of virial series, but it cannot represent the true pressure
p(z)
beyond
z=z_{c}
when a system exhibits a phase transition at this point.

We have seen that the grand-canonical partition function is given by Eq. (
4
) based on cluster expansion.
The existence of thermodynamic limit implies that the pressure and density can be written as

\displaystyle\beta p(T,\mu)=\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}b_{l}(\infty)z^{l}~,\quad n(T,\mu)=\frac{\partial(\beta p)}{\partial\log z}=\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}lb_{l}(\infty)z^{l}~.

(17)

However, the convergence of these series is not necessarily guaranteed for
{}^{\forall}z\geq 0
.
If the radius of convergence is finite and given by
|z|=z_{c}<\infty
, it may be possible to construct an analytic continuation
p_{\mathrm{ana}}(z)
of the virial series, whose domain contains some interval of the positive real axis
z\geq z_{c}
, as illustrated in Fig.
1
.
Then, there are typically three possibilities:

(a)

z=z_{c}
is a pole or essential singularity of
p_{\mathrm{ana}}(z)
.

(b)

z=z_{c}
is a regular point of
p_{\mathrm{ana}}(z)
.

(c)

z=z_{c}
is a branch point of
p_{\mathrm{ana}}(z)
.

In case (a),
p_{\mathrm{ana}}(z)
cannot represent the true pressure for
z\geq z_{c}
, as the singular behavior at
z=z_{c}
is inconsistent with the theorem.
Thus, it can only describe the equilibrium of classical fluid for
z<z_{c}
, i.e. gas phase.
On the other hand, the case (b) can be consistent with the theorem, and
p_{\mathrm{ana}}(z)
can represent the true pressure even for
z\geq z_{c}
.
In this case, there is no singularity at
z=z_{c}
and the system stays in an unique phase throughout the domain of
p_{\mathrm{ana}}(z)
.
The case (c) is subtle and may also be consistent with the theorem if
\lim_{z\rightarrow z_{c}}p_{\mathrm{ana}}(z)<\infty
and
p_{\mathrm{ana}}(z)
is real and positive for
z\geq z_{c}
in a certain branch.

In any cases, the cluster/virial expansion (
17
) can only describe a single phase and cannot account for phase transition
[
18
,
19
]
.

Nevertheless, the estimation of radius of convergence offers a potential indication of phase transition or critical point.
One qualitative way to estimate the convergence radius is the steepest decent method in the
l\rightarrow\infty
limit, based on the irreducible cluster integrals.
In general,
b_{k}(V)
can be expressed as

\displaystyle l^{2}b_{l}(V)=\sum_{\{n_{k}\}}\prod_{k=1}^{l-1}\frac{(l\beta_{k}(V))^{n_{k}}}{n_{k}!}~,

(18)

where
\sum_{\{n_{k}\}}
is the summation over all combinations of
(l-1)
positive integers
\{n_{k}\}_{i=1}^{l-1}
satisfying
\sum_{k=1}^{l-1}kn_{k}=l-1
, and
\beta_{k}(V)
denotes the irreducible contribution in
b_{k}(V)
.
By the residue theorem, Eq. (
18
) is also expressed as

\displaystyle l^{2}b_{l}(V)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{C}d\xi\frac{e^{l\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\beta_{k}(V)\xi^{k}}}{\xi^{l}}~,

(19)

where
C
is an anticlockwise contour around
\xi=0
in the complex
\xi
plane.
We denote the integrand as

\displaystyle e^{l\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\beta_{k}(V)\xi^{k}}/\xi^{l}=e^{lF(\xi)}~,

(20)

where

\displaystyle F(\xi)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\beta_{k}(V)\xi^{k}-\log\xi~.

(21)

One can check that a saddle point exists at the point
\xi_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
satisfying
1
1

1

This statement is always true when
\beta_{k}(V)\geq 0
for
{}^{\forall}k
while it is not when
\beta_{k}(V)
can take negative values.

\displaystyle\frac{dF(\xi)}{d\xi}\bigg|_{\xi=\xi_{0}}=0\quad\leftrightarrow\quad\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}k\beta_{k}(V)\xi_{0}^{k}=1~,

(22)

and we can modify the contour
C
so that it passes this saddle point.
Then,
F(\xi)
is expanded around the saddle point as

\displaystyle F(\xi)=F(\xi_{0})+\frac{F^{\prime\prime}(\xi_{0})}{2}(\xi-\xi_{0})^{2}+{\cal O}((\xi-\xi_{0})^{3})~,

(23)

where

\displaystyle F^{\prime\prime}(\xi_{0})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\beta_{k}(V)k(k-1)\xi_{0}^{k-2}+\frac{1}{\xi_{0}^{2}}=\frac{1}{\xi_{0}^{2}}\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}k^{2}\beta_{k}(V)\xi_{0}^{k}~.

(24)

which can be positive or negative depending on
\beta_{k}(V)
.
When it is negative, this already indicates the instability of system at the density
n=\xi_{0}
because the series
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}k^{2}\beta_{k}(V)n^{k}
corresponds to the second-oder derivative of the grand-canonical potential with respect to the density
n
. (See Eq. (
29
) below.)
Thus, it suffices to consider the positive case, and the direction of steepest decent is given by

\displaystyle\xi-\xi_{0}=ix~,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}~,

(25)

and the saddle point approximation of Eq. (
19
) is

\displaystyle l^{2}b_{l}(V)\simeq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi lF^{\prime\prime}(\xi_{0})}}e^{lF(\xi_{0})}\quad\therefore~b_{l}(V)\simeq\frac{c}{l^{5/2}}b_{0}^{l}~,

(26)

where

\displaystyle b_{0}=e^{F(\xi_{0})}=\frac{1}{\xi_{0}}e^{\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\beta_{k}(V)\xi_{0}^{k}}~,\quad c=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi F^{\prime\prime}(\xi_{0})}}~,

(27)

and
\xi_{0}
is determined by Eq. (
22
).
Now, we can estimate the radius of convergence
z_{c}
of the series (
4
) as

\displaystyle z_{c}=\left(\lim_{l\rightarrow\infty}b_{l}(V)^{1/l}\right)^{-1}=b_{0}^{-1}=\xi_{0}e^{-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\beta_{k}(V)\xi_{0}^{k}}~.

(28)

Namely, the radius of convergence is determined by the irreducible cluster integrals
\{\beta_{k}(V)\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
in the large
l
limit.
Note that these irreducible cluster integrals also allow us to express the pressure as a power series of density as
[
1
,
18
]

\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}b_{l}(V)z^{l}=n\left(1-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{k}{k+1}\beta_{k}(V)n^{k}\right)~,\quad n=ze^{-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\beta_{k}(V)z^{k}}~,

(29)

which is the popular form of the Virial expansion.

4
Hard Sphere Model

We study the asymptotic behavior of the virial expansion of the hard-sphere model (
13
).
Note that, the cluster/virial coefficients do not depend on temperature
T
because the Mayer’s
f
function behaves as

\displaystyle f(x)=e^{-\beta v(x)}-1=\begin{cases}-1&|x|<2a\\
0&|x|>2a\end{cases}

(30)

in the hard-sphere model.

4.1
D=1
and
\infty

The hard-sphere model is exactly solvable for
D=1
and
\infty
.

D=1

When
D=1
, the canonical partition function can be evaluated as (with
x_{N+1}=L
)

\displaystyle\frac{Z_{N}(L)}{N!}

\displaystyle=\left(\prod_{i=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{x_{i+1}}dx_{i}\right)\prod_{i=1}^{N-1}e^{-\beta\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}v(x_{i+1}-x_{i})}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N!}\left(\int_{0}^{L-2Na}dxe^{-\beta v(x)}\right)^{N}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N!}(L-2Na-2a)^{N}~.

(31)

By using the Stirling’s formula
N!\sim\sqrt{2\pi N}(N/e)^{N}
, we obtain

\displaystyle Z_{N}(L)\sim\sqrt{2\pi(N-1)}\left(\frac{L}{N}-2a\right)^{N}~.

(32)

Then, the pressure is given by

\displaystyle\beta pa=\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}a\frac{\partial}{\partial L}\log Z_{N}(L)=\frac{\eta}{1-\eta}~,\quad\eta=\frac{2Na}{L}~.

(33)

In this case, the pressure diverges at
\eta=\eta_{\mathrm{max}}=1
, and there is no phase transition in the physical density region
\eta<1
.

D=\infty

For
D\rightarrow\infty
, the evaluation of the cluster integral
b_{l}(V)
is extremely simplified because only the connected tree-diagrams survive
[
20
]
.
Namely, it is

\displaystyle\lim_{D\rightarrow\infty}b_{l}(V)=\frac{l^{l-2}}{l!}(-v)^{l-1}~,

(34)

where
l^{l-2}
is the number of connected tree diagrams with
l
distinguished particles (Cayler’s theorem), and

\displaystyle-v=\lim_{D\rightarrow\infty}\int d^{D}xf(x)=\lim_{D\rightarrow\infty}V_{D}(a)~,\quad V_{D}(a)=\frac{\pi^{D/2}a^{D}}{\Gamma(\frac{D}{2}+1)}~.

(35)

Here,
V_{D}(a)
is the volume of
D
-dimensional ball.
We assume that this limit is finite.

Now, the pressure and density are given in cluster expansion as

\displaystyle\beta pv

\displaystyle=v\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}b_{l}(V)z^{l}=\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{l+1}\frac{l^{l-2}}{l!}(vz)^{l}~,

(36)

\displaystyle nv

\displaystyle=v\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}lb_{l}(V)z^{l}=\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{l+1}\frac{l^{l-1}}{l!}(vz)^{l}~,

(37)

and both series have the same radius of convergence

\displaystyle vz_{c}=\left(\lim_{l\rightarrow\infty}\left(\frac{l^{l}}{l!}\right)^{\frac{1}{l}}\right)^{-1}=e^{-1}~.

(38)

In this case, we can find approximate analytic continuations by using the Stirling’s formula as

\displaystyle\beta pv\approx-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{l^{\frac{5}{2}}}(-ezv)^{l}=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\mathrm{Li}_{\frac{5}{2}}(-evz)~,

(39)

\displaystyle nv\approx-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{l^{\frac{3}{2}}}(-ezv)^{l}=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\mathrm{Li}_{\frac{3}{2}}(-evz)~,

(40)

where
\mathrm{Li}_{s}(x)
is the polylogarithm function defined by analytic continuation of

\displaystyle\mathrm{Li}_{s}(x)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{x^{n}}{k^{s}}~,

(41)

which is holomorphic except for a branch cut
x\geq 1
in the real axis.
This implies that Eqs. (
39
)(
40
) provide approximate pressure and density of the system in the entire physical parameter space
z\geq 0
in the large
D
limit.
Apparently, there is no phase transition.

The equation of state is much simpler compared to the above expansions in terms of
z
.
In the large
D
limit, all the irreducible cluster integrals
\beta_{k}(V)
for
k\geq 2
are negligible because they contain loops.
Thus, only
\beta_{1}(V)
survives, and we have

\displaystyle\beta pv=\eta+\frac{1}{2}\eta^{2}~,\quad\eta=vn~,

(42)

which is nothing but the mean-field result.

4.2
D=3

Now let us consider
D=3

In this case, only the first few virial coefficients are known.
The virial expansion in the right-hand side in Eq. (

\displaystyle\beta p=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}B_{k}n^{k}~,\quad B_{k}=-\frac{k-1}{k}\beta_{k-1}~,\quad B_{1}=1~.

In the hard-sphere model, it is conventional to express this expansion in terms of dimensionless density as

\displaystyle\beta pV_{3}(a)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\tilde{B}_{k}(T)\eta^{k}~,

(44)

\displaystyle\tilde{B}_{k}(T)\coloneq\frac{B_{k}(T)}{V_{3}(a)^{k-1}}~,\quad\eta\coloneq V_{3}(a)n~,

Here,
\eta

Before concluding, an additional comment is necessary.
In the above analysis, it was simply assumed that the fitted virial coefficients (
48
) are all positive for
k\geq 13
.
However, as pointed out in the literatures
[
12

24
]
, we cannot rule out a possibility that these coefficients may become negative.
At present, a slight discrepancy between the known
\tilde{B}_{12}
and Eq. (
48

For illustration, we plot the ratio
\tilde{B}_{k}/\tilde{B}_{k-1}
in the lower-right panel in Fig.
2
.
More information on the higher-order virial coefficients is essential to improve the analysis.

5
Conclusion

We have studied the asymptotic behavior of the virial series of the hard-sphere model.
We have found that the known virial coefficients are well fitted by Eq. (
48
), leading to the approximate analytic continuation (
49
).
These results imply that there is no indications of phase transition all the way up to the close-packing density
\eta_{\mathrm{max}}\approx 0.74
in the virial expansion approach.
However, this result apparently contradicts the well established fact of the Alder phase transition.
Additional information on the higher-order virial coefficients is necessary to improve the analysis.

2.
Functional framework for d-hypercubic Bravais lattices

Several properties of the discrete fractional Laplacian
\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\vec{\mathbf{r}}}
such as its diagonalization via Fourier transform, its representation as a convolution operator, and its binomial series expansion are known in the literature in various forms, particularly in the context of translation-invariant or convolution operators on abelian groups. However, these results are often stated in abstract harmonic analysis language, without explicit formulas adapted to the fractional setting or without a unified treatment of both positive and negative powers
r\in\mathbb{R}
.

In this section, we provide a self-contained and detailed derivation of these results in the discrete one-dimensional setting. Our aim is twofold. First, we adapt the classical theory to a framework that is directly usable for spectral and commutator estimates in later sections (e.g., Mourre theory, limiting absorption principle, and propagation estimates). Second, we present fully explicit expressions-such as the series expansion involving shift operators
U
,
U^{*}
and binomial coefficients-that are rarely written out in the literature but play a crucial role in our analysis.

These structural properties will be essential for establishing regularity with respect to conjugate operators, studying spectral stability, and analyzing dynamics of nonlocal evolution equations on
\mathbb{Z}
and related domains.

2.1.
Fractional powers of discrete Laplacians in the unidimensional setting

The (forward) shift operator
U
on
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})
is defined by

(Uf)(n):=f(n-1),\qquad(U^{*}f)(n):=f(n+1).

Then
U
is unitary and
UU^{*}=U^{*}U=\mathrm{id}_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})}
.

We also denote by
Q
the position operator on
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})
defined by
(Qf)(n)=nf(n)
.
For any
s\in\mathbb{R}
, we define the weighted space
\ell^{2}_{s}(\mathbb{Z}):=\{f\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})\mid\|\langle Q\rangle^{s}f\|<\infty\}
, where
\langle Q\rangle:=(1+Q^{2})^{1/2}
.
Let
\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}}:=2\mathrm{id}-U-U^{*}
denote the standard discrete Laplacian on
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})
.
This operator is self-adjoint and nonnegative on
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})
.
To extend the notion of fractional powers
\Delta^{r}_{\mathbb{Z}}
for any real
r
, we use the discrete Fourier transform framework and the Borel functional calculus; this defines
\Delta^{r}_{\mathbb{Z}}
also for
r<0
(as an unbounded operator with appropriate domain).

Proposition 2.1
.

For
r\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
u\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}({\mathbb{Z}})
,

\widehat{\Delta_{{\mathbb{Z}}}^{\,r}u}(\theta)=\bigl(2(1-\cos\theta)\bigr)^{r}\,\widehat{u}(\theta),\qquad(\Delta_{{\mathbb{Z}}}^{\,r}u)(n)=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}a_{r}(k)\,u(n-k),

where

a_{r}(k)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\bigl(2(1-\cos\theta)\bigr)^{r}e^{\mathrm{i}k\theta}\,d\theta.

The kernel
a_{r}
is real-valued and even:
a_{r}(k)=a_{r}(-k)
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{F}:\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}})\to L^{2}([-\pi,\pi])
be the unitary discrete Fourier transform

(\mathcal{F}u)(\theta)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}u(n)e^{-\mathrm{i}n\theta}.

A direct computation gives

(\mathcal{F}U\mathcal{F}^{*}f)(\theta)=e^{-\mathrm{i}\theta}f(\theta),\qquad(\mathcal{F}U^{*}\mathcal{F}^{*}f)(\theta)=e^{\mathrm{i}\theta}f(\theta),

hence

(2.1)

\mathcal{F}\,\Delta_{{\mathbb{Z}}}\,\mathcal{F}^{*}=\mathcal{F}\,(2I-(U+U^{*}))\,\mathcal{F}^{*}=M_{\,2-e^{\mathrm{i}\theta}-e^{-\mathrm{i}\theta}}=M_{\vartheta},\quad\vartheta(\theta):=2(1-\cos\theta).

By the Borel functional calculus,

\mathcal{F}\,\Delta_{{\mathbb{Z}}}^{\,r}\,\mathcal{F}^{*}=M_{\vartheta^{r}}
,
whence the multiplier identity on
\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}})
; the extension to
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}({\mathbb{Z}})
follows by density/duality.
Define
a_{r}
by
\widehat{a_{r}}=\vartheta^{r}
(as a tempered distribution when needed). Then

\widehat{a_{r}*u}=\widehat{a_{r}}\,\widehat{u}=\vartheta^{r}\widehat{u}=\widehat{\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\,r}u}
,
and injectivity of
\mathcal{F}
on
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}({\mathbb{Z}})
yields
\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\,r}u=a_{r}*u
.
Evenness and reality are clear from the integrand.
∎

Regularity and summability of the kernel.

•

Integer
r\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
:

a_{r}
has finite support (local stencil).

•

Positive
r>0
(non-integer allowed):

a_{r}\in\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z})
and, as

a_{r}(k)=c_{r}\,|k|^{-1-2r}+\mathcal{O}\bigl(|k|^{-3-2r}\bigr),\qquad c_{r}\neq 0.

•

r=0
:

a_{0}=\delta_{0}
.

•

Negative

.
For

(i.e.

For

(

),

is only a tempered distribution. In all cases

While the diagonalization principle for

is diagonalized by the Fourier transform with symbol

is particularly useful for stability analysis, the Limiting Absorption Principle, and propagation estimates.

Proposition 2.2
.

For

\sigma(\Delta_{{\mathbb{Z}}}^{\,r})=[0,\,4^{\,r}],

Since

f\in\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}})\hbox{ such that }f\neq 0
. We have

\langle Uf,Uf\rangle+\langle U^{*}f,U^{*}f\rangle+\langle U^{*}f,Uf\rangle+\langle Uf,U^{*}f\rangle=4\|f\|^{2}.

Since
U
is unitary, then

\langle Uf,Uf\rangle+\langle U^{*}f,U^{*}f\rangle-\langle U^{*}f,Uf\rangle-\langle Uf,U^{*}f\rangle=0.

Hence,
\langle(U-U^{*})f,(U-U^{*})f\rangle=0
. Then
(U-U^{*})f=0
for all
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}

f(n+1)-f(n-1)=0
.
Therefore,
f\!\restriction_{\overline{q}}
is constant for all
\overline{q}\in{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}
.
thus,
\|f\|^{2}_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}})}=\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}};\overline{n}=1}|c_{1}|^{2}+\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}};\overline{n}=0}|c_{2}|^{2}=+\infty
.
∎

The fractional discrete Laplacian
\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}}^{r}
can be expressed as a power series in the symmetric shift operator
\tfrac{1}{2}(U+U^{*})
using the generalized binomial expansion. The resulting expression is a combination of powers of the shift operators
U
and
U^{*}
, weighted by combinatorial coefficients. While this structure is implicit in many works on fractional difference operators (see, e.g., Ortigueira
[
Or
]
), the fully explicit form in terms of double binomial sums is rarely written out and is detailed here for completeness.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
r\in\mathbb{R}
. Then for every
f\in\mathcal{C}_{c}(\mathbb{Z})
, we have:

(2.2)

\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}}^{r}f(n)=\sum_{h=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{h}\,2^{r-h}\binom{r}{h}\sum_{k=0}^{h}\binom{h}{k}U^{h-2k}f(n),

where the generalized binomial coefficients
\binom{r}{h}
are defined using the Gamma function:

\binom{r}{h}:=\frac{\Gamma(r+1)}{\Gamma(h+1)\Gamma(r-h+1)}.

Proof.

We start from the functional definition of the fractional Laplacian on
\mathbb{Z}
:

\Delta^{r}_{\mathbb{Z}}=2^{r}\left(\mathrm{id}_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}})}-\tfrac{1}{2}(U+U^{*})\right)^{r}.

We now expand the operator power
(I-T)^{r}
using the generalized binomial theorem and Lemma
2.3
, with
T:=\tfrac{1}{2}(U+U^{*})
. For any
r\in\mathbb{R}
, the expansion holds as a strongly convergent series on the dense subspace
\mathcal{C}_{c}(\mathbb{Z})
:

(\mathrm{id}_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}})}-T)^{r}=\sum_{h=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{h}\binom{r}{h}T^{h}.

Therefore, applying this expansion to
f\in\mathcal{C}_{c}(\mathbb{Z})
, we obtain:

\Delta^{r}_{\mathbb{Z}}f=2^{r}\sum_{h=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{h}\binom{r}{h}\left(\tfrac{1}{2}(U+U^{*})\right)^{h}f=\sum_{h=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{h}2^{r-h}\binom{r}{h}(U+U^{*})^{h}f.

Next, we compute the
h
-th power of the symmetric shift operator:

(U+U^{*})^{h}=\sum_{k=0}^{h}\binom{h}{k}U^{h-2k},

where we used the binomial identity for commuting inverse operators
U,U^{*}
.

Combining the two expansions gives:

\Delta^{r}_{\mathbb{Z}}f(n)=\sum_{h=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{h}2^{r-h}\binom{r}{h}\sum_{k=0}^{h}\binom{h}{k}U^{h-2k}f(n).

Since
f\in\mathcal{C}_{c}(\mathbb{Z})
, only finitely many terms in
U^{h-2k}f(n)
are nonzero for fixed
n
, so the double series converges absolutely for each
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. This completes the proof.
∎

Now, we deal with the question of the commutativity of
\Delta_{{\mathbb{Z}}}^{r}
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
r,s\in\mathbb{R}
and define

\mathcal{H}^{s\to r}:=\{f\in\mathcal{D}(\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}}^{s})\;:\;\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}}^{s}f\in\mathcal{D}(\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}}^{r})\}.

Then:

(1)

If
r,s\geq 0
, one has

\mathcal{H}^{s\to r}\cap\mathcal{H}^{r\to s}=\mathcal{D}(\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}}^{r+s})=\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}).

(2)

If
r,s<0
, one has

\mathcal{H}^{s\to r}\cap\mathcal{H}^{r\to s}=\mathcal{D}(\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}}^{r+s}).

(3)

If
s\geq 0
and
r\leq 0
, then

\mathcal{H}^{s\to r}\cap\mathcal{H}^{r\to s}=\mathcal{D}(\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}}^{r})\subsetneq\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}),

and in particular:

•

if
r=0
, equality holds with
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})
;

•

if
r+s<0
, then
\mathcal{H}^{s\to r}\cap\mathcal{H}^{r\to s}\subsetneq\mathcal{D}(\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}}^{r+s})
.

Proof.

Applying (
2.1
), the operator
\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}}^{t}
becomes multiplication by

\vartheta(\theta):=\left(2(1-\cos\theta)\right)^{t},\quad\theta\in[-\pi,\pi].

The domain
\mathcal{D}(\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}}^{t})
corresponds in Fourier space to the weighted space

\{g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}):(1-\cos\theta)^{t}g(\theta)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T})\}.

(1) If
r,s\geq 0
, the Fourier multiplier
(1-\cos\theta)^{t}
is bounded for
t\geq 0
, hence
\mathcal{D}(\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}}^{t})=\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})
for such
t
. Therefore the intersection domain is the whole space.

(2) If
r,s<0
, the weights
(1-\cos\theta)^{r}
and
(1-\cos\theta)^{s}
blow up near
\theta=0
, and integrability determines the domain. The product structure implies

\mathcal{D}(\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}}^{s})\cap\{\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}}^{s}f\in\mathcal{D}(\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}}^{r})\}=\mathcal{D}(\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}}^{r+s}),

and symmetry in
r,s
yields the claim.

(3) If
s\geq 0
and
r\leq 0
, boundedness of
(1-\cos\theta)^{s}
implies
\mathcal{D}(\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}}^{s})=\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})
, but
\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}}^{s}f
must belong to
\mathcal{D}(\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}}^{r})
, which forces
f\in\mathcal{D}(\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}}^{r})
. The strictness of the inclusion for
r<0
and
r+s<0
follows from explicit counterexamples, e.g.
f(\theta)=(1-\cos\theta)^{-1/2}
when
(r,s)=(-1,-1/2)
.
∎

Remark 2.6
.

On
\mathcal{H}^{s\to r}\cap\mathcal{H}^{r\to s}
, the fractional powers commute:

\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}}^{r}\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}}^{s}f=\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}}^{s}\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}}^{r}f=\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}}^{r+s}f.

For
r>0
, the operator
\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}}^{r}
is a positive, boundedly invertible map from
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})
onto
\mathcal{D}(\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}}^{-r})
, with inverse
\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}}^{-r}
.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
r,s\in\mathbb{R}
and
f\in\mathcal{H}^{s\to r}\cap\mathcal{H}^{r\to s}
.
Assuming the binomial-series representation (
2.2
) holds (in the strong sense) on
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})
,
one has on this intersection domain

\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\,s}\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\,r}f=\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\,r+s}f=\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\,r}\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\,s}f.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.4
with
t=r
and strong convergence, we may apply
\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\,s}
termwise:

\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\,s}\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\,r}f=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}\,2^{\,r-k}\binom{r}{k}\,\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\,s}\!\big(U^{k}f\big).

Applying (
2.2
) with
t=s
to each
U^{k}f
and using
U^{h}U^{k}=U^{h+k}
gives

\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\,s}\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\,r}f=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{h=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{h+k}\,2^{\,r-k}\,2^{\,s-h}\binom{r}{k}\binom{s}{h}\,U^{h+k}f.

The scalar coefficients form an absolutely summable family (hence Fubini-Tonelli applies), so regrouping
by
m=h+k
yields

\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\,s}\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\,r}f=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{m}\,2^{\,r+s-m}\Bigg(\sum_{k=0}^{m}\binom{r}{k}\binom{s}{m-k}\Bigg)U^{m}f.

By Vandermonde’s identity
\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{m}\binom{r}{k}\binom{s}{m-k}=\binom{r+s}{m}
,

\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\,s}\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\,r}f=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{m}\,2^{\,r+s-m}\binom{r+s}{m}\,U^{m}f=\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\,r+s}f,

where the last equality is (
2.2
) with
t=r+s
. Interchanging
r
and
s
gives the
same conclusion, hence
\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\,r}
and
\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\,s}
commute on

\mathcal{H}^{s\to r}\cap\mathcal{H}^{r\to s}
.
∎

2.2.
Anisotropic Fractional Powers of Translation-Invariant Laplacians on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}

The previous one-dimensional analysis naturally extends to the multidimensional setting via a tensorized construction. In particular, both the domain
\mathcal{D}(\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}^{\vec{\mathbf{r}}})
and the associated quadratic form inherit an anisotropic structure, dictated by the one-dimensional components in each coordinate direction.

The results presented in this section adapt classical constructions from the spectral theory of self-adjoint operators on tensor product Hilbert spaces. Using the spectral theorem, we define anisotropic fractional powers of discrete Laplacians on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
by treating each direction independently.

While the functional calculus for commuting self-adjoint operators, the additive behavior of spectra, and the structure of tensorial domains are well-established (see, e.g.,
[
ReSi
,
Da
,
Ic
]
), we tailor these tools to the discrete setting with nonlocal and anisotropic features.

Our goal is to provide an explicit description of the domain, spectral structure, and Sobolev-type regularity associated with the anisotropic operator

(2.3)

\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}^{\vec{\mathbf{r}}}:=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\Delta_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{d},j}^{r_{j}},

where
\Delta_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{d},j}^{r_{j}}:=\mathrm{id}^{\otimes(j-1)}\otimes\Delta^{r_{j}}\otimes\mathrm{id}^{\otimes(d-j)}
and each
\Delta_{{\mathbb{Z}}}^{r_{j}}
denotes the one-dimensional fractional Laplacian of order
r_{j}\in\mathbb{R}
acting on
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})
.
Under the Fourier transform
\mathcal{F}:\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{d})\to L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})
,
H_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}^{\vec{\mathrm{r}}}
is the multiplication operator by

\vartheta_{\vec{\mathrm{r}}}(\theta)\ :=\ \sum_{j=1}^{d}\bigl(2-2\cos\theta_{j}\bigr)^{r_{j}},\qquad\theta=(\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{d})\in{\mathbb{T}}^{d}=[-\pi,\pi]^{d}.

We emphasize the distinction between the cases
r_{j}>0
and
r_{j}<0
, which leads to significantly different domain properties and low-frequency behavior. These distinctions are critical for the Mourre estimate and the limiting absorption principle studied later in the paper.

Fractional discrete Laplacians.
Given
\vec{\mathbf{r}}=(r_{1},\dots,r_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we define the anisotropic fractional Laplacian as in (
2.3
). If
r_{j}=r
for all
j
, we denote the operator simply as
\Delta^{r}_{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
. See
[
ReSi
, Section VIII.10]
for background on tensor products of essential self-adjoint operators.

Domain Characterization and Discussion.

Let us discuss the domain of the operator
\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}^{\vec{\mathbf{r}}}
when the fractional orders
\vec{\mathbf{r}}=(r_{1},\dots,r_{d})
are not all positive.

For all
r_{j}>0
, this yields a convolution-type operator of order
2r_{j}
with fast off-diagonal decay, and its domain is
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})
.

However, when
r_{j}<0
, the operator
\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}}^{r_{j}}
becomes a nonlocal pseudo-inverse of the Laplacian, and its domain is a strictly smaller subspace of
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})
, typically a weighted Sobolev-type space. In particular, for
r_{j}<0
, we have:

\mathcal{D}(\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}}^{r_{j}})\subsetneq\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}).

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
\vec{\mathbf{r}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then the domain of
\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}^{\vec{\mathbf{r}}}
is:

\mathcal{D}\left(\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}^{\vec{\mathbf{r}}}\right)=\bigcap_{j=1}^{d}\left(\mathrm{id}^{\otimes(j-1)}\otimes\mathcal{D}\left(\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}}^{r_{j}}\right)\otimes\mathrm{id}^{\otimes(d-j)}\right).

Moreover:

•

If all
r_{j}\geq 0
, then
\mathcal{D}(\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}^{\vec{\mathbf{r}}})=\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
.

•

If at least one
r_{j}<0
, then
\mathcal{D}(\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}^{\vec{\mathbf{r}}})\subsetneq\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
and is characterized by directional regularity constraints.

Proof.

By (
2.3
) and the summands act on different coordinates, hence commute strongly.
It follows from the spectral theorem for strongly commuting self-adjoint operators
(see e.g.
[
Sch
, Thm. 5.29]
)
that

\mathcal{D}\!\left(\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}^{\vec{\mathrm{r}}}\right)=\bigcap_{j=1}^{d}\Bigl(\mathrm{id}^{\otimes(j-1)}\otimes\mathcal{D}(\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}}^{r_{j}})\otimes\mathrm{id}^{\otimes(d-j)}\Bigr).

The description of each factor
\mathcal{D}(\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}}^{r_{j}})

is given by Lemma
2.5
.
If all
r_{j}\geq 0
, then
\mathcal{D}(\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}}^{r_{j}})=\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})
for each
j
,
hence the full domain is
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
.
If at least one
r_{j}<0
, then the corresponding one-dimensional domain is strictly contained in
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})
,
which yields the strict inclusion in the multidimensional case.
∎

Proposition 2.9
.

Let
\vec{\mathbf{r}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then the operator
\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}^{\vec{\mathbf{r}}}
is essentially self-adjoint on
\ell^{2}_{c}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
.

Proof.

Each component
\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}}^{r_{j}}
is essential self-adjoint as shown in the one-dimensional case. The sum of commuting self-adjoint operators on independent tensor components is again self-adjoint,
[
Da
]
.
∎

Proposition 2.10
.

The spectrum of
\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}^{\vec{\mathbf{r}}}
is given by:

\sigma\left(\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}^{\vec{\mathbf{r}}}\right)=\left\{\sum_{j=1}^{d}\lambda_{j}\,\middle|\,\lambda_{j}\in\sigma\left(\Delta_{\mathbb{Z},}^{r_{j}}\right)\right\}.

In particular, if
r_{j}>0
for all
j
, then:

\sigma\left(\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}^{\vec{\mathbf{r}}}\right)=\left[0,\sum_{j=1}^{d}2^{2r_{j}}\right].

Proof.

Each
\Delta^{r_{j}}_{\mathbb{Z}}
acts only on the
j
-th coordinate and is self-adjoint. The operators
(\Delta^{r_{j}}_{\mathbb{Z}})_{j=1}^{d}
commute and act on tensor components. The spectral theorem for commuting self-adjoint operators gives the result (see
[
Da
,
DaMaTi
]
).
∎

Definition 2.11
.

The (finite) threshold set of

is

\operatorname{Thr}_{\mathrm{fin}}\!\big(H_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}^{\vec{\mathrm{r}}}\big):=\bigl\{\,\vartheta_{\vec{\mathrm{r}}}(\theta):\ \theta\in{\mathbb{T}}^{d},\ \nabla\vartheta_{\vec{\mathrm{r}}}(\theta)=0,\ \vartheta_{\vec{\mathrm{r}}}(\theta)<\infty\,\bigr\}.

Proposition 2.12
.

With the notation of Definition
2.11
,

\operatorname{Thr}_{\mathrm{fin}}\!\big(H_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}^{\vec{\mathrm{r}}}\big)=\Bigl\{\,\sum_{j\in N}4^{\,r_{j}}\;+\;\sum_{j\in P}\epsilon_{j}\,4^{\,r_{j}}\ :\ \epsilon_{j}\in\{0,1\}\ \Bigr\},

where
P:=\{j:\ r_{j}>0\}
and

.

Proof.

Compute the gradient:

\partial_{\theta_{j}}\vartheta_{\vec{\mathrm{r}}}(\theta)=2\,r_{j}\,\sin\theta_{j}\,\bigl(2-2\cos\theta_{j}\bigr)^{r_{j}-1}.

Thus
\nabla\vartheta_{\vec{\mathrm{r}}}(\theta)=0
if and only if each
\theta_{j}\in\{0,\pi\}
. Evaluating
\vartheta_{\vec{\mathrm{r}}}
at such points gives:
if
j\in P

\theta_{j}=0
contributes

and
\theta_{j}=\pi
contributes

; if

(since

∎

Remark 2.13
.

For

Poles of the symbol on

.
We define the

\Sigma:=\bigcup_{j\in N}\{\theta\in\mathbb{T}^{d}:\ \theta_{j}=0\}.

We recall:

Proposition 2.14
.

The symbol
\vartheta_{\vec{\mathbf{r}}}
is real-valued and

on

.
Moreover, for each

such that, uniformly in

\vartheta_{\vec{\mathbf{r}}}(\theta)=\ |\theta_{j}|^{2r_{j}}\,\bigl(1+\mathcal{O}(\theta_{j}^{2})\bigr)\ +\ C(\theta_{\perp}),\quad C(\theta_{\perp}):=\sum_{i\neq j}\bigl(2-2\cos\theta_{i}\bigr)^{r_{i}}.

In particular, along the

-th coordinate the symbol has an algebraic pole of order

\alpha_{j}\;=\;-2\,r_{j}\;>\;0,

i.e.
\vartheta_{\vec{\mathbf{r}}}(\theta)\sim c_{j}\,|\theta_{j}|^{-\alpha_{j}}

(with

.
If several indices in

\sum_{j\in N}c_{j}\,|\theta_{j}|^{-2|r_{j}|}

.

Smoothness on

Remark 2.16

For the isotropic model

Quadratic form associated to the fractional Laplacian.
Let
\vec{r}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. The fractional Laplacian
\Delta^{\vec{r}}_{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
is diagonalized by the discrete Fourier transform

\hat{\psi}(\theta):=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\psi(n)\,e^{-in\cdot\theta},\quad\theta\in[-\pi,\pi]^{d}.

Under this transform,

\widehat{\big(\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}^{\vec{\mathbf{r}}}\psi\big)}(\theta)=\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{d}\lambda_{j}(\theta)^{r_{j}}\Big)\,\hat{\psi}(\theta),\quad\lambda_{j}(\theta):=2-2\cos\theta_{j}.

The associated closed quadratic form is

q_{\vec{r}}[\psi]=\langle\Delta^{\vec{\mathbf{r}}}\psi,\psi\rangle=\int_{[-\pi,\pi]^{d}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{d}\lambda_{j}(\theta)^{r_{j}}\right)|\hat{\psi}(\theta)|^{2}\,\frac{d\theta}{(2\pi)^{d}}.

Near

implies that
\lambda_{j}(\theta)^{r_{j}}\sim|\theta_{j}|^{2r_{j}}
, which constrains the decay of
\hat{\psi}(\theta)
when some

Anisotropic Sobolev spaces.
Define

H^{\vec{\mathbf{r}}}(\mathbb{T}^{d}):=\left\{f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})\;\middle|\;\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left(1+\sum_{j=1}^{d}|\theta_{j}|^{2r_{j}}\right)|\hat{f}(\theta)|^{2}\,d\theta<\infty\right\}.

Then the form domain of
\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}^{\vec{\mathbf{r}}}
satisfies

\mathcal{Q}(q_{\vec{\mathbf{r}}})\simeq\mathrm{H}^{\vec{r}}(\mathbb{T}^{d}),

up to identification via the discrete Fourier transform.

Anisotropic Sobolev scale (case

).

H^{\vec{\mathrm{r}}}({\mathbb{T}}^{d}):=\Big\{f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{d}):\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{d}}\Big(1+\sum_{j=1}^{d}\lambda_{j}(\theta)^{r_{j}}\Big)|\hat{f}(\theta)|^{2}\,d\theta<\infty\Big\}.

For directions with

we keep the domain description through

and the intersection rule.

2.3.
Conjugate operator and commutator estimates

In this section, we establish a Mourre estimate for the operator
\Delta_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}^{\vec{\mathbf{r}}}
.
Since the fractional powers
\Delta^{r_{j}}_{\mathbb{Z}}
may be unbounded.
(precisely, when some
r_{j}<0
), the commutator
[\Delta_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}^{\vec{\mathbf{r}}},\mathrm{i}A_{\mathbb{Z}^{d},\vec{\mathbf{r}}}]
, where
A_{\mathbb{Z}^{d},\vec{\mathbf{r}}}
is a conjugate operator given in Eq. (
2.4
),
may not define a bounded operator. Therefore, the correct framework is to interpret the commutator
in the sense of quadratic forms. That is, for any
f\in\mathcal{C}_{c}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
, we define

Definition 2.17
.

Let
H
and
A
be self-adjoint on
\mathcal{H}
. The
form commutator
of
H
with
A
is the sesquilinear form

\mathfrak{q}_{A}^{H}(f,g)\;:=\;\langle Hf,\,\mathrm{i}Ag\rangle-\langle\mathrm{i}Af,\,Hg\rangle,\qquad f,g\in\mathcal{D}(H)\cap\mathcal{D}(A).

We say that
[H,\mathrm{i}A]_{\circ}
exists (on a reducing subspace
Mc\subset\mathcal{H}
) if
\mathfrak{q}_{A}^{H}
extends by continuity
to a bounded form on
Mc\times Mc
. In that case there is a unique bounded operator
B\in\mathcal{B}(Mc)
such that

\langle f,\,Bg\rangle=\mathfrak{q}_{A}^{H}(f,g)\quad\text{for all }f,g\in\mathcal{D}(H)\cap\mathcal{D}(A)\cap Mc,

and we
define

B:=[H,\mathrm{i}A]_{\circ}
on
Mc
.
In applications below we take
Mc=E_{\mathcal{I}}(H)\mathcal{H}
for compact interior windows
\mathcal{I}\Subset\sigma(H)^{\circ}
and write

E_{\mathcal{I}}(H)\,[H,\mathrm{i}A]_{\circ}\,E_{\mathcal{I}}(H)

for the corresponding bounded operator on
E_{\mathcal{I}}(H)\mathcal{H}
.

2.3.1.
Localized regularity and Mourre estimate

In what follows we work exclusively on compact interior windows

\mathcal{I}\Subset\sigma(H)^{\circ}
and use only the
localized
classes

\mathcal{C}^{k}_{loc}(A)
and
\mathcal{C}^{1,1}_{loc}(A)
.

Definition 2.18
.

Let
H
be self-adjoint and
\mathcal{I}\Subset\sigma(H)^{\circ}
.
We say
H\in\mathcal{C}^{k}_{loc}(A)
on
\mathcal{I}
if, for every
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathcal{I})
,
the bounded operator
\varphi(H)
belongs to
\mathcal{C}^{k}(A)
.
Similarly,
H\in\mathcal{C}^{1,1}_{loc}(A)
on
\mathcal{I}
if
\varphi(H)\in\mathcal{C}^{1,1}(A)
for all

\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathcal{I})
.
Equivalently,
H\in\mathcal{C}^{k}_{loc}(A)
on
\mathcal{I}
iff
E_{\mathcal{I}}(H)\,(H-\mathrm{i})^{-1}\in\mathcal{C}^{k}(A)

(and likewise for
\mathcal{C}^{1,1}
).

We construct a conjugate operator
A_{\mathbb{Z}^{d},\vec{\mathbf{r}}}
on
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
by summing the one-dimensional generators:

(2.4)

A_{\mathbb{Z}^{d},\vec{\mathbf{r}}}:=\sum_{j=1}^{d}A_{\mathbb{Z},r_{j}},\quad\text{with}\quad A_{\mathbb{Z},r_{j}}:=-\frac{\mathrm{i}\cdot\mathrm{sign}(r_{j})}{2}\left(U_{j}(Q_{j}+\tfrac{1}{2})-(Q_{j}+\tfrac{1}{2})U_{j}^{*}\right),

where
\mathrm{sign}(r_{j})=1
if
r_{j}\geq 0,\ -1
if
r_{j}<0
. Each
A_{\mathbb{Z},r_{j}}
is essentially self-adjoint on
\mathcal{C}_{c}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
. We refer to
[
GeGo
]
and
[
Mic
, Lemma 5.7]
for the essential self-adjointness and
[
GeGo
, Lemma 3.1]
for the domain.

Remark 2.19
.

For
H=\Delta_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}^{\vec{\mathrm{r}}}
and the conjugate operator
A=A_{\mathbb{Z}^{d},\vec{\mathrm{r}}}
constructed above,
the form
\mathfrak{q}_{A}^{H}
is well-defined on the core
\mathcal{C}_{c}({\mathbb{Z}}^{d})
even when some
r_{j}<0
(unbounded from below).
By the localized
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-regularity established earlier and the interior Mourre framework,

\mathfrak{q}_{A}^{H}
extends to a bounded form on
E_{\mathcal{I}}(H)\mathcal{H}
; hence
E_{\mathcal{I}}(H)[H,\mathrm{i}A]_{\circ}E_{\mathcal{I}}(H)\in\mathcal{B}(E_{\mathcal{I}}(H)\mathcal{H})
.
This is the object that appears in the Mourre estimate on
\mathcal{I}
.

This ensures symmetry and the correct interpretation on a dense domain.
The commutator estimate that follows is then valid in this sense of forms on the dense core
\mathcal{C}_{c}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
, and no compactness remainder is needed. This extends classical Mourre theory to anisotropic fractional powers of discrete Laplacians, even when the operators involved are unbounded from below. We make a preliminary work for the construction of a conjugate operator for
\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\vec{\mathbf{r}}}
. This is a known result, e.g.,
[
AlFr
]
, see also
[
GeGo
,
Mic
]
.

We fix an anisotropic fractional Laplacian
\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}^{\vec{\mathbf{r}}}
on
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
, with
\vec{\mathbf{r}}=(r_{1},\dots,r_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
. The operator is self-adjoint and non-local. It may be unbounded or non-positive when some
r_{j}<0
, and its spectral properties can vary drastically depending on the sign and size of each exponent. We denote by

\lambda_{\vec{\mathbf{r}}}:=\sup\sigma(\Delta^{\vec{\mathbf{r}}})

the top of its spectrum, and we work under the assumption that the essential spectrum is purely absolutely continuous on the open interval
(0,\lambda_{\vec{\mathbf{r}}})
.

2.3.2.
Commutator structure.

Theorem 2.20
.

Let
\vec{\mathbf{r}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
.
Then for any compact interval
\mathcal{I}\subset\sigma(\Delta_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}^{\vec{\mathbf{r}}})^{\circ}
,
there exists
c_{\mathcal{I}}>0
such that

E_{\mathcal{I}}(\Delta_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}^{\vec{\mathbf{r}}})\,[\Delta_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}^{\vec{\mathbf{r}}},\mathrm{i}A_{\mathbb{Z}^{d},\vec{\mathbf{r}}}]_{\circ}\,E_{\mathcal{I}}(\Delta_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}^{\vec{\mathbf{r}}})\geq c_{\mathcal{I}}\,E_{\mathcal{I}}(\Delta_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}^{\vec{\mathbf{r}}}),

in the sense of quadratic forms on
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
.

Proof.

Since
\Delta_{{\mathbb{Z}},j}
is bounded self–adjoint with
\sigma(\Delta_{{\mathbb{Z}},j})=[0,4]
,
the fractional power
\Delta_{{\mathbb{Z}},j}^{r_{j}}
is defined by the functional calculus.
For the function
f(\lambda)=\lambda^{r_{j}}
one has
f^{\prime}(\lambda)=r_{j}\,\lambda^{r_{j}-1}
,
which is bounded on every compact subinterval of
(0,4)
.
Therefore, for any spectral localization
E_{\mathcal{I}}(\Delta_{{\mathbb{Z}},j})
with

\mathcal{I}\Subset(0,4)
, the chain rule for commutators
(see
[
AmBoGe
, Prop. 5.1.5]
) applies and yields

[\Delta_{{\mathbb{Z}},j}^{r_{j}},\mathrm{i}A_{\mathbb{Z},r_{j}}]=r_{j}\,\Delta_{{\mathbb{Z}},j}^{\,r_{j}-1}\,[\Delta_{{\mathbb{Z}},j},\mathrm{i}A_{\mathbb{Z},r_{j}}]\quad\text{on }\mathrm{Ran}E_{\mathcal{I}}(\Delta_{{\mathbb{Z}},j}).

Since
[\Delta_{{\mathbb{Z}},j},\mathrm{i}A_{\mathbb{Z},r_{j}}]=\mathrm{sign}(r_{j})(4-\Delta_{{\mathbb{Z}},j})
,
it follows that

[\Delta_{{\mathbb{Z}},j}^{r_{j}},\mathrm{i}A_{\mathbb{Z},r_{j}}]=|r_{j}|\,(4-\Delta_{{\mathbb{Z}},j})\,\Delta_{{\mathbb{Z}},j}^{\,r_{j}-1}\quad\text{on }\mathrm{Ran}E_{\mathcal{I}}(\Delta_{{\mathbb{Z}},j}).

∎

Lemma 2.21
.

Let
r_{j}\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
.
Then, in the sense of quadratic forms on
\mathcal{C}_{c}({\mathbb{Z}}^{d})
, one has

\big[\,[\Delta_{{\mathbb{Z}},j}^{\,r_{j}},\mathrm{i}A_{{\mathbb{Z}},r_{j}}],\,\mathrm{i}A_{{\mathbb{Z}},r_{j}}\big]=r_{j}(r_{j}-1)\,\Delta_{{\mathbb{Z}},j}^{\,r_{j}-2}\,(4-\Delta_{{\mathbb{Z}},j})^{2}\;-\;r_{j}\,\Delta_{{\mathbb{Z}},j}^{\,r_{j}-1}\,(4-\Delta_{{\mathbb{Z}},j}).

If
r_{j}>0

(
bounded case
)
, the identity holds in operator norm; if
r_{j}<0

(
unbounded case
)
, it holds as a form identity on
\mathcal{C}_{c}({\mathbb{Z}}^{d})
.

Proof.

Set
B_{j}:=\Delta_{{\mathbb{Z}},j}
and
C_{j}:=\mathrm{i}A_{{\mathbb{Z}},r_{j}}
.
Since
[B_{j},C_{j}]=\mathrm{sign}(r_{j})\,(4-B_{j})
is a polynomial in
B_{j}
, it commutes with
B_{j}
and with all
\psi^{(k)}(B_{j})
.
Let
\psi(\lambda)=\lambda^{r_{j}}
so that
\Delta_{{\mathbb{Z}},j}^{r_{j}}=\psi(B_{j})
.
By the standard rules for
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-functional calculus (see
[
AmBoGe
, Prop. 5.1.5 & Thm. 7.2.9]
),

[\psi(B_{j}),C_{j}]=\psi^{\prime}(B_{j})\,[B_{j},C_{j}],\qquad\big[\,[\psi(B_{j}),C_{j}],\,C_{j}\big]=[\psi^{\prime}(B_{j}),C_{j}]\,[B_{j},C_{j}]+\psi^{\prime}(B_{j})\,[[B_{j},C_{j}],C_{j}].

Using
[
AmBoGe
, Theorem]
again
[\psi^{\prime}(B_{j}),C_{j}]=\psi^{\prime\prime}(B_{j})\,[B_{j},C_{j}]
, and the facts

[B_{j},C_{j}]=\mathrm{sign}(r_{j})\,(4-B_{j}),\quad[B_{j},C_{j}]^{2}=(4-B_{j})^{2},\quad[[B_{j},C_{j}],C_{j}]=-(4-B_{j}),

together with
\psi^{\prime}(\lambda)=r_{j}\lambda^{r_{j}-1}
and
\psi^{\prime\prime}(\lambda)=r_{j}(r_{j}-1)\lambda^{r_{j}-2}
, we obtain

\big[\,[\Delta_{{\mathbb{Z}},j}^{\,r_{j}},\mathrm{i}A_{{\mathbb{Z}},j}],\,\mathrm{i}A_{{\mathbb{Z}},j}\big]=r_{j}(r_{j}-1)\,\Delta_{{\mathbb{Z}},j}^{\,r_{j}-2}(4-\Delta_{j})^{2}-r_{j}\,\Delta_{{\mathbb{Z}},j}^{\,r_{j}-1}(4-\Delta_{{\mathbb{Z}},j}),

as claimed.
∎

This ensures that
\Delta_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{d},\vec{\mathbf{r}}}^{\vec{\mathbf{r}}}\in\mathcal{C}_{loc}^{2}(A_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{d},\vec{\mathbf{r}}})
, and justifies the application of Mourre theory in both bounded and unbounded cases.

Proposition 2.22
.

For
j\in\{1,\dots,d\}
and
r_{j}\in{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\{0\}
, we define

g_{r_{j}}(\lambda):=r_{j}(r_{j}-1)\,\lambda^{r_{j}-2}(4-\lambda)^{2}\;-\;r_{j}\,\lambda^{r_{j}-1}(4-\lambda),\qquad\lambda\in[0,4].

Then, in the sense of quadratic forms on
\mathcal{C}_{c}({\mathbb{Z}})
,

\big[\,[\Delta_{{\mathbb{Z}},j}^{\,r_{j}},\mathrm{i}A_{{\mathbb{Z}},r_{j}}],\,\mathrm{i}A_{{\mathbb{Z}},r_{j}}\big]\;=\;g_{r_{j}}(\Delta_{{\mathbb{Z}},j}).

Moreover, for every compact
\mathcal{J}\subset\sigma(\Delta_{{\mathbb{Z}},j}^{\,r_{j}})^{\circ}
,

E_{\mathcal{J}}(\Delta_{{\mathbb{Z}},j}^{\,r_{j}})\,\big[\,[\Delta_{{\mathbb{Z}},j}^{\,r_{j}},\mathrm{i}A_{{\mathbb{Z}},r_{j}}],\,\mathrm{i}A_{{\mathbb{Z}},r_{j}}\big]_{\circ}\,E_{\mathcal{J}}(\Delta_{{\mathbb{Z}},j}^{\,r_{j}})\in\mathcal{B}(\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}})).

In particular:

•

if
r_{j}>0
, then
\sigma(\Delta_{{\mathbb{Z}},j}^{\,r_{j}})=[0,4^{r_{j}}]
and one may take
\mathcal{J}\Subset(0,4^{r_{j}})
;

•

if
r_{j}<0
, then
\sigma(\Delta_{{\mathbb{Z}},j}^{\,r_{j}})=[4^{r_{j}},\infty)
and one may take
\mathcal{J}\Subset(4^{r_{j}},\infty)
.

By a finite sum over
j
, it follows that

\Delta_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}^{\vec{\mathbf{r}}}\in\mathcal{C}^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(A_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{d},\vec{\mathbf{r}}}).

Proof.

Set
\varphi(\lambda)=\lambda^{r_{j}}
on
[0,4]
. By Lemma
2.21
(proved for the one-dimensional
component along direction
j
) we know, as quadratic forms on
\mathcal{C}_{c}({\mathbb{Z}})
,

[\Delta_{{\mathbb{Z}},j},\mathrm{i}A_{{\mathbb{Z}},r_{j}}]=4-\Delta_{{\mathbb{Z}},j},\qquad[[\Delta_{{\mathbb{Z}},j},\mathrm{i}A_{{\mathbb{Z}},r_{j}}],\mathrm{i}A_{{\mathbb{Z}},r_{j}}]=-(4-\Delta_{{\mathbb{Z}},j}).

Using the standard
C^{2}
-chain rule for form-commutators (e.g.
[
AmBoGe
, Prop. 6.2.10]
),
for
\varphi(\lambda)=\lambda^{r_{j}}
one gets, still as forms on
\mathcal{C}_{c}({\mathbb{Z}})
,

\big[\,[\varphi(\Delta_{{\mathbb{Z}},j}),\mathrm{i}A_{{\mathbb{Z}},r_{j}}],\,\mathrm{i}A_{{\mathbb{Z}},r_{j}}\big]=\varphi^{\prime\prime}(\Delta_{{\mathbb{Z}},j})\,[\Delta_{{\mathbb{Z}},j},\mathrm{i}A_{{\mathbb{Z}},r_{j}}]^{2}\;+\;\varphi^{\prime}(\Delta_{{\mathbb{Z}},j})\,[[\Delta_{{\mathbb{Z}},j},\mathrm{i}A_{{\mathbb{Z}},r_{j}}],\mathrm{i}A_{{\mathbb{Z}},r_{j}}].

Plugging the identities from above and
\varphi^{\prime}(\lambda)=r_{j}\lambda^{r_{j}-1}
,

\varphi^{\prime\prime}(\lambda)=r_{j}(r_{j}-1)\lambda^{r_{j}-2}
yields

\big[\,[\Delta_{{\mathbb{Z}},j}^{\,r_{j}},\mathrm{i}A_{{\mathbb{Z}},r_{j}}],\,\mathrm{i}A_{{\mathbb{Z}},r_{j}}\big]=r_{j}(r_{j}-1)\,\Delta_{{\mathbb{Z}},j}^{\,r_{j}-2}(4-\Delta_{{\mathbb{Z}},j})^{2}\;-\;r_{j}\,\Delta_{{\mathbb{Z}},j}^{\,r_{j}-1}(4-\Delta_{{\mathbb{Z}},j})=g_{r_{j}}(\Delta_{{\mathbb{Z}},j}),

which proves the first claim as a form identity.

Let
\mathcal{J}\subset\sigma(\Delta_{{\mathbb{Z}},j}^{\,r_{j}})^{\circ}
be compact and set

\mathcal{I}:=\varphi^{-1}(\mathcal{J})\subset(0,4)
. By the Borel functional calculus,

E_{\mathcal{J}}(\Delta_{{\mathbb{Z}},j}^{\,r_{j}})=E_{\mathcal{I}}(\Delta_{{\mathbb{Z}},j})
.
Hence

\displaystyle E_{\mathcal{J}}(\Delta_{{\mathbb{Z}},j}^{\,r_{j}})\big[\,[\Delta_{{\mathbb{Z}},j}^{\,r_{j}},\mathrm{i}A_{{\mathbb{Z}},r_{j}}],\,\mathrm{i}A_{{\mathbb{Z}},r_{j}}\big]E_{\mathcal{J}}(\Delta_{{\mathbb{Z}},j}^{\,r_{j}})

\displaystyle=E_{\mathcal{I}}(\Delta_{{\mathbb{Z}},j})\;\big\{\,r_{j}(r_{j}-1)\,\Delta_{{\mathbb{Z}},j}^{\,r_{j}-2}(4-\Delta_{{\mathbb{Z}},j})^{2}\;-\;r_{j}\,\Delta_{{\mathbb{Z}},j}^{\,r_{j}-1}(4-\Delta_{{\mathbb{Z}},j})\,\big\}\;E_{\mathcal{I}}(\Delta_{{\mathbb{Z}},j}).

The right-hand side is
E_{\mathcal{I}}(\Delta_{{\mathbb{Z}},j})\,\psi(\Delta_{{\mathbb{Z}},j})\,E_{\mathcal{I}}(\Delta_{{\mathbb{Z}},j})
with

\psi(\lambda)=g_{r_{j}}(\lambda)
, a continuous function on the compact set
\mathcal{I}\subset(0,4)
.
By the spectral theorem this localized operator is bounded, with

\|E_{\mathcal{I}}\psi(\Delta_{{\mathbb{Z}},j})E_{\mathcal{I}}\|\leq\sup_{\lambda\in\mathcal{I}}|g_{r_{j}}(\lambda)|<\infty
.
We thus obtain the bounded extension, denoted by the subscript
\circ
:

E_{\mathcal{J}}(\Delta_{{\mathbb{Z}},j}^{\,r_{j}})\,\big[\,[\Delta_{{\mathbb{Z}},j}^{\,r_{j}},\mathrm{i}A_{{\mathbb{Z}},r_{j}}],\,\mathrm{i}A_{{\mathbb{Z}},r_{j}}\big]_{\circ}\,E_{\mathcal{J}}(\Delta_{{\mathbb{Z}},j}^{\,r_{j}})\in\mathcal{B}(\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}})).

Now, if
r_{j}>0
, then
\sigma(\Delta_{{\mathbb{Z}},j}^{\,r_{j}})=[0,4^{r_{j}}]
and we may choose

\mathcal{J}\Subset(0,4^{r_{j}})
. If
r_{j}<0
, then
\sigma(\Delta_{{\mathbb{Z}},j}^{\,r_{j}})=[4^{r_{j}},\infty)
and we
may choose
\mathcal{J}\Subset(4^{r_{j}},\infty)
.

Finally, since
\Delta_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}^{\vec{\mathbf{r}}}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\mathrm{id}^{\otimes(j-1)}\otimes\Delta_{{\mathbb{Z}},j}^{\,r_{j}}\otimes\mathrm{id}^{\otimes(d-j)}
is a finite sum of components that lie in
\mathcal{C}^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(A_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{d},\vec{\mathbf{r}}})
,
we conclude that

\Delta_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}^{\vec{\mathbf{r}}}\in\mathcal{C}^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(A_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{d},\vec{\mathbf{r}}})
.
∎

Convention.

We use
[T,\mathrm{i}A]
for the
form
commutator on a common core (e.g.
\mathcal{C}_{c}({\mathbb{Z}})
).
When a localization renders the operator bounded, its
bounded extension
is denoted by

[T,\mathrm{i}A]_{\circ}
; similarly for nested commutators.

Remark 2.23
.

We do not use global statements. For orientation only: a
global
Mourre estimate (without spectral localization)
would require that the derivative of
h_{\vec{r}}
along the
A
-flow does not vanish on the region considered,
which typically fails at thresholds (critical/von Hove energies) for periodic discrete Laplacians.
This is why we remain
localized
on
\mathcal{I}\Subset\sigma(H)^{\circ}\setminus\operatorname{Thr}(H)
.

3.
Proof of main result

To prove our main result, we first establish and recall the following intermediate and technical statements.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
A_{\mathbb{Z}^{d},\vec{\mathbf{r}}}
be the standard conjugate operator on
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
, as given in (
2.4
) and

\Lambda(Q):=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\langle Q_{j}\rangle
.
Assume
T\in\mathcal{B}(\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d}))
is symmetric and

\int_{1}^{\infty}\Bigl\|\,\xi\!\Bigl(\tfrac{\Lambda(Q)}{t}\Bigr)\,T\,\Bigr\|\,\frac{dt}{t}<\infty\quad\text{for some }\ \xi\in C_{c}^{\infty}((0,\infty)).

Then
T\in\mathcal{C}^{0,1}(A_{\mathbb{Z}^{d},\vec{\mathbf{r}}})
.

Proof.

Fix a dyadic partition
\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\rho_{k}(\Lambda)=\mathop{\rm id}\nolimits
with
\rho_{k}(\lambda)=\rho(2^{-k}\lambda)
,
\rho\in C_{c}^{\infty}((1/2,2))
, and set
T_{k}:=\rho_{k}(\Lambda)\,T\,\rho_{k}(\Lambda)
. The assumption implies
\sum_{k}\|T_{k}\|\lesssim\int_{1}^{\infty}\|\xi(\Lambda/t)T\|\,dt/t<\infty
. Since
A_{\mathbb{Z}^{d},\vec{\mathbf{r}}}
is first order,
\sup_{k}\|[A_{\mathbb{Z}^{d},\vec{\mathbf{r}}},\rho_{k}(\Lambda)]\|<\infty
. Hence

[A_{\mathbb{Z}^{d},\vec{\mathbf{r}}},T_{k}]=[A_{\mathbb{Z}^{d},\vec{\mathbf{r}}},\rho_{k}(\Lambda)]\,T\,\rho_{k}(\Lambda)+\rho_{k}(\Lambda)\,T\,[A_{\mathbb{Z}^{d},\vec{\mathbf{r}}},\rho_{k}(\Lambda)],

so
\|[A_{\mathbb{Z}^{d},\vec{\mathbf{r}}},T_{k}]\|\lesssim\|T_{k}\|
. By Duhamel,

\|e^{\mathrm{i}sA_{\mathbb{Z}^{d},\vec{\mathbf{r}}}}T_{k}e^{-\mathrm{i}sA_{\mathbb{Z}^{d},\vec{\mathbf{r}}}}-T_{k}\|\leq|s|\int_{0}^{1}\|[A_{\mathbb{Z}^{d},\vec{\mathbf{r}}},T_{k}]\|\,d\tau\lesssim|s|\,\|T_{k}\|.

Summing over
k
gives
\sup_{0<|s|\leq 1}\|e^{\mathrm{i}sA}Te^{-\mathrm{i}sA}-T\|/|s|<\infty
.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

This is the standard Lipschitz regularity criterion in Mourre theory (see
[
AmBoGe
]
). The proof is adapted to the discrete setting and avoids assuming
[A_{\mathbb{Z}^{d},\vec{\mathbf{r}}},T]
exists a priori by localizing in position.

Remark 3.3
.

If
T=T^{*}
and
\xi_{k}(\Lambda)=\xi_{k}(\Lambda)^{*}
, then
\|T\,\xi_{k}(\Lambda)\|=\|\xi_{k}(\Lambda)\,T\|
. Choosing
\rho\leq C\,\xi
(functional calculus) yields

\sum_{k}\|\rho_{k}(\Lambda)T\rho_{k}(\Lambda)\|\ \leq\ C^{2}\sum_{k}\|\xi_{k}(\Lambda)T\|<\infty,

which is the estimate used in the proof.

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
and
T\in\mathcal{B}(\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d}))
be symmetric.
If
\langle\Lambda(Q)\rangle^{\varepsilon}T\in\mathcal{B}(\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d}))
, then
T\in\mathcal{C}^{0,1}(A_{\mathbb{Z}^{d},\vec{\mathrm{r}}})
.

Proof.

Pick
\xi\in C_{c}^{\infty}((0,\infty))
with
\xi\equiv 1
on
[1,2]
and
\mathrm{supp}\,\xi\subset[\tfrac{1}{2},4]
.
Then
\|\xi(\Lambda(Q)/t)T\|\lesssim t^{-\varepsilon}\|\langle\Lambda(Q)\rangle^{\varepsilon}T\|
for
t\geq 1
, so Proposition
3.1
applies.
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

For every
s\in[0,1]
there exist constants
C_{s}>0
and
c_{s}>0
such that for all
f\in\mathcal{C}_{c}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
,

(3.1)

\|\langle A_{\mathbb{Z}^{d},\vec{\mathrm{r}}}\rangle^{s}f\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}\leq C_{s}\|\Lambda^{s}f\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})},\qquad\|\Lambda^{s}f\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}\leq c_{s}\|\langle A_{\mathbb{Z}^{d},\vec{\mathrm{r}}}\rangle^{s}f\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}.

Proof.

A_{\mathbb{Z}^{d},\vec{\mathrm{r}}}
is first order with coefficients linear in
Q
, so

\|A_{\mathbb{Z}^{d},\vec{\mathrm{r}}}f\|\lesssim\|\Lambda f\|+\|f\|
on
\mathcal{C}_{c}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
. By Heinz-Kato and interpolation between
s=0,1
, the first inequality follows. The second comes from the ellipticity of
A_{\mathbb{Z}^{d},\vec{\mathrm{r}}}
in configuration space: for
|n|
large,
c\,\Lambda(n)\leq\langle A_{\mathbb{Z}^{d},\vec{\mathrm{r}}}\rangle(n)\leq C\,\Lambda(n)
; a partition of unity and interpolation finish the proof.
∎

Remark 3.6
.

Proposition
3.1
is purely spatial (it only uses
\Lambda(Q)
and that
A_{\mathbb{Z}^{d},\vec{\mathrm{r}}}
is first order), hence it does not depend on the exponents
r_{j}
.
By contrast, the analysis of commutators for
\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}^{\vec{\mathrm{r}}}
does depend on
\vec{\mathrm{r}}
, especially near thresholds, where the symbol-level factors (e.g.
\lambda^{\,r_{j}-1}
) may degenerate. This motivates the localization in energy used below.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
W=W(Q)
be a bounded real multiplication on
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
.
Assume that
(H0)
and
(H1)
hold. Then
W\in\mathcal{C}^{1}(A_{\mathbb{Z}^{d},\vec{\mathrm{r}}})
and

[W(Q),{\rm i}A_{\mathbb{Z}^{d},\vec{\mathrm{r}}}]_{\circ}\in\mathcal{C}^{0,1}(A_{\mathbb{Z}^{d},\vec{\mathrm{r}}})
.
In particular,
W\in\mathcal{C}^{1,1}(A_{\mathbb{Z}^{d},\vec{\mathrm{r}}})
.

Proof.

Recalling (
2.4
). We show the lemma in two steps.

Step 1:
W(Q)\in\mathcal{C}^{1}(A_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{d},\vec{\mathrm{r}}})
.

It suffices to prove that there exists
c>0
such that

\big\|\,[W(Q),{\rm i}A_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{d},\vec{\mathrm{r}}}]f\,\big\|^{2}\ \leq\ c\,\|f\|^{2},\qquad\forall f\in\mathcal{S}.

Write
A_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{d},\vec{\mathrm{r}}}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\mathrm{sign}(r_{j})\,A_{{\mathbb{Z}},r_{j}}^{(j)}
with
A_{{\mathbb{Z}},r_{j}}^{(j)}

the one-dimensional conjugate operator acting along
e_{j}
(see (
2.4
)).
A direct computation on
\mathcal{S}({\mathbb{Z}}^{d})
gives, for each
j
,

\big([W,{\rm i}A_{{\mathbb{Z}},r_{j}}^{(j)}]f\big)(n)=\frac{\mathrm{sign}(r_{j})}{2}\Big[(n_{j}+\tfrac{1}{2})\big(W(n+e_{j})-W(n)\big)f(n+e_{j})+(n_{j}-\tfrac{1}{2})\big(W(n-e_{j})-W(n)\big)f(n-e_{j})\Big].

By
(H1)
,

\big|(n_{j}\pm\tfrac{1}{2})\big(W(n\pm e_{j})-W(n)\big)\big|\lesssim\Lambda(n)^{-\delta}.

Hence each summand is a shift composed with a bounded multiplication by a coefficient

c_{j,\pm}(n)
satisfying
\sup_{n}|c_{j,\pm}(n)|<\infty
, so

\|[W,{\rm i}A_{{\mathbb{Z}},r_{j}}^{(j)}]f\|\ \leq\ C\,\|f\|

for all
f\in\mathcal{S}
. Summing over
j
yields

\|[W,{\rm i}A_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{d},\vec{\mathrm{r}}}]f\|\leq C\|f\|
,
and by density and
[
AmBoGe
, Lemma 6.2.9]
we obtain
W\in\mathcal{C}^{1}(A_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{d},\vec{\mathrm{r}}})
.

Step 2:

4.1.
Stationary representation of the scattering matrix on interior windows

In this section we exploit the localized Mourre framework proved above on the full lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.
Assume
(H0)
-
(H1)
, set

H_{0}:=\Delta^{\vec{\mathbf{r}}}_{\mathbb{Z}^{d}},\qquad H:=H_{0}+W(Q),

and fix a compact window

\mathcal{I}\Subset\sigma(H_{0})^{\circ}\setminus\operatorname{Thr}\!\big(\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}^{\vec{\mathbf{r}}}\big)

on which
H_{0},H\in\mathcal{C}^{1,1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(A_{\mathbb{Z}^{d},\vec{\mathrm{r}}})
and a strict localized Mourre estimate holds.
Consequently, the localized LAP for
H_{0}
and
H
on
\mathcal{I}
follows from
[
AmBoGe
]
.
We gather several corollaries valid on every compact window

\mathcal{I}\Subset\sigma(H_{0})^{\circ}\setminus\operatorname{Thr}(\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}^{\vec{\mathbf{r}}}).

Throughout, write

\mathcal{H}_{\pm s}:=\langle A_{\mathbb{Z}^{d},\vec{\mathrm{r}}}\rangle^{\mp s}\,\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})\qquad(s>1/2).

Spectral representation and boundary trace.

There exists a measurable Hilbert field
\{\mathscr{H}_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\mathcal{I}}

and a unitary

\mathcal{U}_{0}:\ E_{\mathcal{I}}(H_{0})\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})\longrightarrow\int_{\mathcal{I}}^{\oplus}\mathscr{H}_{\lambda}\,d\lambda,\qquad(\mathcal{U}_{0}H_{0}f)(\lambda)=\lambda\,(\mathcal{U}_{0}f)(\lambda).

Define
\Gamma_{0}(\lambda):E_{\mathcal{I}}(H_{0})\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})\to\mathscr{H}_{\lambda}
by

\Gamma_{0}(\lambda)f:=(\mathcal{U}_{0}f)(\lambda)
. By Stone’s formula and the LAP, for
\chi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathcal{I})
and
s>\frac{1}{2}
,

(4.1)

\chi(H_{0})=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\mathcal{I}}\chi(\lambda)\,\Im R_{0}(\lambda+\mathrm{i}0)\,d\lambda,\qquad\Gamma_{0}(\lambda)^{*}\Gamma_{0}(\lambda)=\frac{1}{\pi}\,\Im R_{0}(\lambda+\mathrm{i}0)

as bounded maps
\mathcal{H}_{s}\to\mathcal{H}_{-s}
; here
R_{0}(z)=(H_{0}-z)^{-1}
.

The
T
-operator.

Let
R(z)=(H-z)^{-1}
. Define

(4.2)

T(z):=W\,(\mathrm{id}-R_{0}(z)W)^{-1}=W-WR(z)W,

as bounded maps
\mathcal{H}_{s}\to\mathcal{H}_{-s}
for
\Im z\neq 0
, with a.e. limits
T(\lambda\pm\mathrm{i}0)
on
\mathcal{I}
by the localized LAP.

Wave matrices and the on-shell scattering matrix.

Let
\chi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathcal{I})
equal
1
on
\mathcal{I}_{0}\Subset\mathcal{I}
. The local wave operators

\mathcal{W}_{\pm}(H,H_{0};\mathcal{I})=\mathrm{s}\!-\!\lim_{t\to\pm\infty}e^{-\mathrm{i}tH}\,\chi(H_{0})\,e^{\mathrm{i}tH_{0}}

exist and are complete on
\mathcal{I}
(
[
AmBoGe
, Thm. 7.7.1]
). In the representation of
H_{0}
,

\mathcal{U}_{0}\mathcal{W}_{\pm}\mathcal{U}_{0}^{*}
acts as multiplication by

(4.3)

W_{\pm}(\lambda)=\mathrm{id}_{\mathscr{H}_{\lambda}}-2\pi\mathrm{i}\,\Gamma_{0}(\lambda)\,T(\lambda\pm\mathrm{i}0)\,\Gamma_{0}(\lambda)^{*},\qquad\text{a.e. }\lambda\in\mathcal{I},

and the on-shell scattering matrix
S(\lambda):=W_{+}(\lambda)^{*}W_{-}(\lambda)
satisfies

(4.4)

S(\lambda)=\mathrm{id}_{\mathscr{H}_{\lambda}}-2\pi\mathrm{i}\,\Gamma_{0}(\lambda)\,T(\lambda+\mathrm{i}0)\,\Gamma_{0}(\lambda)^{*},\qquad\text{a.e. }\lambda\in\mathcal{I}.

Optical theorem, unitarity, and continuity.

Assume the localized LAP holds on
\mathcal{I}
, i.e.

\sup_{\lambda\in\mathcal{I}}\,\big\|\langle A\rangle^{-s}R_{0}(\lambda\pm\mathrm{i}0)\langle A\rangle^{-s}\big\|+\sup_{\lambda\in\mathcal{I}}\,\big\|\langle A\rangle^{-s}R(\lambda\pm\mathrm{i}0)\langle A\rangle^{-s}\big\|<\infty,\quad s>\tfrac{1}{2},

and that
T(z)
is defined by (
4.2
) and admits boundary values

T(\lambda\pm\mathrm{i}0)\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{s},\mathcal{H}_{-s})
for a.e.
\lambda\in\mathcal{I}
.
Let
\Gamma_{0}(\lambda):\mathcal{H}_{s}\to\mathscr{H}_{\lambda}
be the boundary trace operator (the
“free Fourier transform on the energy shell”) satisfying Stone’s formula

(4.5)

\frac{1}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\big(R_{0}(\lambda+\mathrm{i}0)-R_{0}(\lambda-\mathrm{i}0)\big)=\Gamma_{0}(\lambda)^{*}\Gamma_{0}(\lambda)\quad\text{in }\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{s},\mathcal{H}_{-s}),\quad s>\tfrac{1}{2}.

Using the resolvent identity

R_{0}(z)-R_{0}(\bar{z})=(z-\bar{z})\,R_{0}(\bar{z})R_{0}(z),

and (
4.2
), one obtains for a.e.
\lambda\in\mathcal{I}
the
optical theorem

(4.6)

T(\lambda-\mathrm{i}0)-T(\lambda+\mathrm{i}0)=2\pi\mathrm{i}\,T(\lambda-\mathrm{i}0)\,\Gamma_{0}(\lambda)^{*}\Gamma_{0}(\lambda)\,T(\lambda+\mathrm{i}0),

as a form identity on
\mathcal{H}_{-s}
(
s>\tfrac{1}{2}
).

Define the stationary wave operators on the shell by

W_{\pm}(\lambda):=\mathrm{id}_{\mathscr{H}_{\lambda}}-2\pi\mathrm{i}\,\Gamma_{0}(\lambda)\,T(\lambda\pm\mathrm{i}0)\,\Gamma_{0}(\lambda)^{*}\quad\in\mathcal{B}\big(\mathscr{H}_{\lambda}\big),

and the scattering matrix
S(\lambda):=W_{+}(\lambda)^{*}W_{-}(\lambda)
.
Combining (
4.5
)–(
4.6
) yields for a.e.
\lambda\in\mathcal{I}

W_{\pm}(\lambda)^{*}W_{\pm}(\lambda)=\mathrm{id}_{\mathscr{H}_{\lambda}},\qquad S(\lambda)^{*}S(\lambda)=S(\lambda)S(\lambda)^{*}=\mathrm{id}_{\mathscr{H}_{\lambda}},

so
S(\lambda)
is unitary almost everywhere on
\mathcal{I}
.

Finally, by the localized LAP and the continuity of
\Gamma_{0}(\lambda)
in
\lambda
as a map

\mathcal{H}_{s}\to\mathscr{H}_{\lambda}
, the boundary values
T(\lambda\pm\mathrm{i}0)
depend
continuously on
\lambda
in
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{s},\mathcal{H}_{-s})
. Hence
\lambda\mapsto S(\lambda)
is

strongly continuous
on
\mathcal{I}
.

Remark 4.1
.

It is convenient to factor
T(z)
as

T(z)=W\,\Gamma(z),\qquad\Gamma(z):=(\mathrm{id}-R_{0}(z)W)^{-1}.

In particular,

S(\lambda)=\mathrm{id}-2\pi\mathrm{i}\,\Gamma_{0}(\lambda)\,W\,\Gamma(\lambda+\mathrm{i}0)\,\Gamma_{0}(\lambda)^{*}.

We stress that

is
not
the full interacting resolvent

4.2.
Consequences on interior energies

Based on the localized Mourre estimate and the LAP on
\mathcal{I}
,
we derive a time-averaged escape bound for the conjugate observable
A_{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
,
quantifying ballistic propagation for states with energies in
\mathcal{I}
.
We also fix the weighted space

\ell^{1}\!\big(\langle n\rangle^{1+\epsilon}\big):=\Big\{\,f:\mathbb{Z}^{d}\to\mathbb{C}\ :\ \|f\|_{\ell^{1}(\langle n\rangle^{1+\epsilon})}:=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\langle n\rangle^{1+\epsilon}\,|f(n)|<\infty\ \Big\}.

4.2.1.
Birman-Kreĭn identity

In the sequel
\mathfrak{S}_{1}
denotes the trace class on the underlying Hilbert space:

T\in\mathfrak{S}_{1}
iff
\sum_{n\geq 1}s_{n}(T)<\infty
, in which case
\mathrm{Tr}\,T

is well-defined and
\|T\|_{\mathfrak{S}_{1}}=\mathrm{Tr}\,|T|
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Assume either
W
has finite support, or
W\in\ell^{1}(\langle n\rangle^{1+\epsilon})
for some
\epsilon>0
.
Then for every
\chi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathcal{I})
,
\chi(H)-\chi(H_{0})\in\mathfrak{S}_{1}
, and there exists a spectral shift
function
\xi(\lambda)
on
\mathcal{I}
such that

\det S(\lambda)=\exp\!\bigl(-2\pi\mathrm{i}\,\xi(\lambda)\bigr)\quad\text{for a.e. }\lambda\in\mathcal{I}.

Proof.

(a) Trace-class input).

If
W
has finite support, then
W(Q)
is finite rank. If
W\in\ell^{1}(\langle n\rangle^{1+\epsilon})
,
then
\sum_{n}|W(n)|<\infty
, hence
W(Q)\in\mathfrak{S}_{1}
with

\|W(Q)\|_{\mathfrak{S}_{1}}=\sum_{n}|W(n)|
.

(b) Functional calculus difference is trace class).

For
\chi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathcal{I})
, the Helffer-Sjöstrand formula and the resolvent identity give

\chi(H)-\chi(H_{0})=\frac{1}{\pi}\!\int_{{\mathbb{C}}}\!\partial_{\bar{z}}\widetilde{\chi}(z)\,(H-z)^{-1}W(Q)(H_{0}-z)^{-1}\,dx\,dy.

Since
W(Q)\in\mathfrak{S}_{1}
and
\|(H-z)^{-1}\|,\|(H_{0}-z)^{-1}\|\leq|\Im z|^{-1}
,
the integrand is
\mathfrak{S}_{1}
-valued with norm
\leq C|\Im z|^{-2}\|W(Q)\|_{\mathfrak{S}_{1}}
;
choosing the almost-analytic extension with
|\partial_{\bar{z}}\widetilde{\chi}(z)|\lesssim|\Im z|^{N}
(
N\geq 3
)
makes the integral convergent in
\mathfrak{S}_{1}
. Hence
\chi(H)-\chi(H_{0})\in\mathfrak{S}_{1}

(see e.g.
[
AmBoGe
, App. A & Ch. 7]
).

(c) Spectral shift & Birman-Krein).

By the Lifshits-Krein theory for trace-class perturbations, there exists a spectral shift
function
\xi\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathcal{I})
such that

\mathrm{Tr}\!\left(\varphi(H)-\varphi(H_{0})\right)=\int_{\mathcal{I}}\varphi^{\prime}(\lambda)\,\xi(\lambda)\,d\lambda

for all
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathcal{I})
(see
[
AmBoGe
, Ch. 8]
).
On
\mathcal{I}
the localized Mourre theory yields the LAP and the stationary scattering matrix
S(\lambda)
.
Therefore the Birman-Krein identity applies,

\det S(\lambda)=\exp\!\bigl(-2\pi i\,\xi(\lambda)\bigr)\quad\text{for a.e. }\lambda\in\mathcal{I},

which proves the theorem.
∎

4.2.2.
Ballistic transport in time average

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
\chi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathcal{I})
. There exist
v_{\mathcal{I}}>0
and
C_{\mathcal{I}}<\infty
such that, for all
\mathcal{T}\geq 1
and
f\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
,

\frac{1}{\mathcal{T}}\int_{0}^{\mathcal{T}}\big\|\mathbf{1}_{\{|A_{\mathbb{Z}^{d},\vec{\mathrm{r}}}|\leq v_{\mathcal{I}}t\}}\,e^{-\mathrm{i}tH}\chi(H)\,f\big\|^{2}\,dt\ \leq\ \frac{C_{\mathcal{I}}}{\log(1+\mathcal{T})}\,\|f\|^{2}.

Proof.

Set
u:=\chi(H)f
and
f(t):=e^{-\mathrm{i}tH}u
.
Pick
\Phi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
, bounded and constant at both ends, such that

\Phi^{\prime}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
,
\Phi^{\prime}\geq 0
, and
\Phi^{\prime}\not\equiv 0
.
For
v>0
(to be chosen later) and
t\geq 1
, define the time–dependent observable

F_{t}:=\Phi\!\big(A_{\mathbb{Z}^{d},\vec{\mathrm{r}}}/(vt)\big).

Then, for
g(t):=\langle f(t),F_{t}f(t)\rangle
, one has

\frac{d}{dt}g(t)=\big\langle f(t),\partial_{t}F_{t}f(t)\big\rangle\ +\ \mathrm{i}\,\big\langle f(t),[H,F_{t}]f(t)\big\rangle.

The explicit time derivative reads

\partial_{t}F_{t}\ =\ -\frac{1}{t}\,\frac{A_{\mathbb{Z}^{d},\vec{\mathrm{r}}}}{vt}\,\Phi^{\prime}\!\big(A_{\mathbb{Z}^{d},\vec{\mathrm{r}}}/(vt)\big),

and, since
\Phi^{\prime}\geq 0
and
\Phi
is nondecreasing, one has

\langle f(t),\partial_{t}F_{t}f(t)\rangle\leq 0
.
Thus, this term can be dropped in an upper bound.

By the commutator expansion (see
[
AmBoGe
, Prop. 6.2.10]
) and the assumption

H\in\mathcal{C}^{1,1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(A_{\mathbb{Z}^{d},\vec{\mathrm{r}}})
on
\mathcal{I}
, one has on
\mathrm{Ran}\,\chi(H)
:

\mathrm{i}[H,F_{t}]\ =\ \frac{1}{vt}\,\Phi^{\prime}\!\big(A_{\mathbb{Z}^{d},\vec{\mathrm{r}}}/(vt)\big)\,\mathrm{i}[H,A_{\mathbb{Z}^{d},\vec{\mathrm{r}}}]\ +\ \mathcal{O}(t^{-2})\quad\text{in }\mathcal{B}(\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})).

Inserting
\chi(H)
around
\mathrm{i}[H,A_{\mathbb{Z}^{d},\vec{\mathrm{r}}}]
and applying the localized Mourre estimate with compact remainder (
[
AmBoGe
, Thm. 7.2.9]
), there exist
c_{\mathcal{I}}>0
and a compact operator
K_{\mathcal{I}}
such that

\chi(H)\,\mathrm{i}[H,A_{\mathbb{Z}^{d},\vec{\mathrm{r}}}]\,\chi(H)\ \geq\ c_{\mathcal{I}}\,\chi(H)^{2}\ -\ \chi(H)K_{\mathcal{I}}\chi(H).

Thus, for
t\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}g(t)

\displaystyle\ \geq\ \frac{c_{\mathcal{I}}}{vt}\,\big\langle f(t),\Phi^{\prime}\!\big(A_{\mathbb{Z}^{d},\vec{\mathrm{r}}}/(vt)\big)f(t)\big\rangle

\displaystyle\quad-\ \frac{1}{vt}\,\big|\big\langle f(t),\Phi^{\prime}\!\big(A_{\mathbb{Z}^{d},\vec{\mathrm{r}}}/(vt)\big)K_{\mathcal{I}}f(t)\big\rangle\big|\ -\ C\,t^{-2}\,\|u\|^{2},

with
C<\infty
independent of
t
.

Integrating from
1
to
\mathcal{T}
, the left–hand side telescopes and is bounded by

|g(\mathcal{T})-g(1)|\leq 2\|\Phi\|_{\infty}\|u\|^{2}
. Hence

(4.7)

\frac{c_{\mathcal{I}}}{v}\int_{1}^{\mathcal{T}}\frac{1}{t}\,\big\langle f(t),\Phi^{\prime}\!\big(A_{\mathbb{Z}^{d},\vec{\mathrm{r}}}/(vt)\big)f(t)\big\rangle\,dt\ \leq\ C_{1}\|u\|^{2}\ +\ \frac{1}{v}\int_{1}^{\mathcal{T}}\frac{1}{t}\,R(t)\,dt,

with

R(t):=\big|\langle f(t),\Phi^{\prime}(A_{\mathbb{Z}^{d},\vec{\mathrm{r}}}/(vt))K_{\mathcal{I}}f(t)\rangle\big|+C^{\prime}t^{-1}\|u\|^{2}
.

The compact term is handled via Kato smoothness stemming from the LAP (
[
AmBoGe
, Thm. 7.5.1]
).
Decompose
K_{\mathcal{I}}=K_{\mathrm{fr}}+K_{\mathrm{sm}}
(finite rank + small norm).
By Cauchy–Schwarz and
H
–smoothness on
\mathrm{Ran}\chi(H)
, one obtains

\int_{1}^{\mathcal{T}}\frac{1}{t}\,\big|\langle f(t),\Phi^{\prime}(A_{\mathbb{Z}^{d},\vec{\mathrm{r}}}/(vt))K_{\mathcal{I}}f(t)\rangle\big|\,dt\ \leq\ C_{2}\,\log(1+\mathcal{T})\,\|u\|^{2},

uniformly in
v>0
; also
\int_{1}^{\mathcal{T}}t^{-2}\,dt\leq 1
.
Inserted into (
4.7
), this yields

\int_{1}^{\mathcal{T}}\frac{1}{t}\,\big\langle f(t),\Phi^{\prime}\!\big(A_{\mathbb{Z}^{d},\vec{\mathrm{r}}}/(vt)\big)f(t)\big\rangle\,dt\ \leq\ C_{3}\,\log(1+\mathcal{T})\,\|u\|^{2}.

Finally, choose
\Phi
such that
\Phi^{\prime}\geq c\,\mathbf{1}_{\{|x|\leq 1\}}
for some
c>0
. Then

\big\langle f(t),\Phi^{\prime}\!\big(A_{\mathbb{Z}^{d},\vec{\mathrm{r}}}/(vt)\big)f(t)\big\rangle\ \geq\ c\,\big\|\mathbf{1}_{\{|A_{\mathbb{Z}^{d},\vec{\mathrm{r}}}|\leq vt\}}\,f(t)\big\|^{2}.

Therefore,

\int_{1}^{\mathcal{T}}\frac{1}{t}\,\big\|\mathbf{1}_{\{|A_{\mathbb{Z}^{d},\vec{\mathrm{r}}}|\leq vt\}}\,e^{-\mathrm{i}tH}\chi(H)f\big\|^{2}\,dt\ \leq\ C_{4}\,\log(1+\mathcal{T})\,\|f\|^{2}.

Since
\int_{1}^{\mathcal{T}}t^{-1}\,dt=\log\mathcal{T}
, the standard logarithmic optimization argument (
[
AmBoGe
, §7.4]
) gives, after division by
\mathcal{T}
and with
v=v_{\mathcal{I}}
chosen small enough, the desired inequality on
[0,\mathcal{T}]
(the interval
[0,1]
being controlled trivially by
C\,\mathcal{T}^{-1}\|f\|^{2}
). This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 4.4

(On the choice of
\Phi
)
.

There is no nontrivial
\Phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}
that is monotone.
Instead, one chooses
\Phi\in C^{\infty}
bounded, constant at both ends, with

\Phi^{\prime}\in C_{c}^{\infty}
,
\Phi^{\prime}\geq 0
.
For instance, take
\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}
,
\psi\geq 0
, and set

\Phi(x):=\int_{-\infty}^{x}\psi(s)\,ds
.
After normalization, this satisfies
\Phi^{\prime}\geq c\,\mathbf{1}_{[-1,1]}
and is perfectly suited for the argument.

4.2.3.
Continuity in the fractional exponents

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
\mathcal{R}\subset\{\vec{\mathbf{r}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\ \min_{j}r_{j}>r_{*}>-1\}
be compact.
Then for any
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{R}
,
\vec{\mathbf{r}}\mapsto(H_{0}(\vec{\mathbf{r}})-z)^{-1}
is norm-continuous.
If, moreover,

\mathcal{I}\Subset\bigcap_{\vec{\mathbf{r}}\in\mathcal{R}}\big(\sigma(H_{0}(\vec{\mathbf{r}}))^{\circ}\setminus\operatorname{Thr}\big),

the LAP constants can be chosen uniformly in
\vec{\mathbf{r}}\in\mathcal{R}
, and

\vec{\mathbf{r}}\mapsto S_{\vec{\mathbf{r}}}(\lambda)
is strongly continuous on
\mathcal{I}
.

Proof.

In Fourier variables,

\mathcal{F}H_{0}(\vec{\mathbf{r}})\mathcal{F}^{-1}

is multiplication by

\sigma_{\vec{\mathbf{r}}}(k)=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\varphi_{r_{j}}(k_{j}),

where
(r,k)\mapsto\varphi_{r}(k)
is continuous on
[r_{*},R]\times\mathbb{T}
and bounded uniformly
(for any fixed compact
[r_{*},R]
). Hence
\vec{\mathbf{r}}\mapsto H_{0}(\vec{\mathbf{r}})
is norm-continuous,
and by the resolvent identity (
[
Kat80
, Thm. IV.1.16]
) so is

\vec{\mathbf{r}}\mapsto(H_{0}(\vec{\mathbf{r}})-z)^{-1}.

If
\mathcal{I}
avoids thresholds uniformly in
\vec{\mathbf{r}}\in\mathcal{R}
, the Mourre constants and
LAP bounds on
\mathcal{I}
can be chosen uniformly in
\vec{\mathbf{r}}
. Using (
4.4
)
and dominated convergence for
T(\lambda+\mathrm{i}0)
in
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{s},\mathcal{H}_{-s})

(again uniform LAP), we obtain strong continuity of
\vec{\mathbf{r}}\mapsto S_{\vec{\mathbf{r}}}(\lambda)
on
\mathcal{I}
.
∎

Remark 4.6
.

The norm-resolvent continuity in
\vec{\mathrm{r}}
implies inner–outer continuity of the spectra.
This is in the spirit of results on magnetic families on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}

[
PR16
]

and of results for magnetic pseudodifferential families in the continuum
[
AMP10
]
.
In particular, a Mourre estimate uniform on
\mathcal{I}
yields a uniform LAP and the strong continuity of
the scattering matrix
\lambda\mapsto S_{\vec{\mathrm{r}}}(\lambda)
on
\mathcal{I}
.

Proposition 4.7
.

If
W\in\ell^{1}(\langle n\rangle^{1+\epsilon})
for some
\epsilon>0
, then

\sigma_{\mathrm{pp}}(H)\cap\mathcal{I}
is finite, counting multiplicities.

Proof.

Let
\chi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathcal{I})
. As in the proof of Theorem
4.2
, one has

\chi(H)-\chi(H_{0})\in\mathfrak{S}_{1},

where
\mathfrak{S}_{1}
denotes the trace class.
By the Helffer–Sjöstrand formula and stability of the functional calculus under trace class perturbations, it follows that

E_{\mathcal{I}}(H)-E_{\mathcal{I}}(H_{0})\in\mathfrak{S}_{1}.

Since
H_{0}
is purely absolutely continuous on
\mathcal{I}
(being inside the interior of the spectrum), the spectral projection
E_{\mathcal{I}}(H_{0})
has no pure point component. Consequently, the compact perturbation above implies that
E_{\mathcal{I}}(H)
differs from a projection of infinite rank by a trace class operator, hence
E_{\mathcal{I}}(H)
has at most finite rank.

Now recall that the rank of
E_{\mathcal{I}}(H)
is exactly the sum of the multiplicities of eigenvalues of
H
in
\mathcal{I}
, i.e.

\mathrm{rank}\,E_{\mathcal{I}}(H)\ =\ \sum_{\lambda\in\sigma_{\mathrm{pp}}(H)\cap\mathcal{I}}m(\lambda),

where
m(\lambda)
is the algebraic multiplicity of
\lambda
.
Since
E_{\mathcal{I}}(H)
has finite rank, this sum is finite, which proves that

\#\big(\sigma_{\mathrm{pp}}(H)\cap\mathcal{I}\big)<\infty,

counting multiplicities.
∎

Remark 4.8
.

The conclusion that
\#\bigl(\sigma_{\mathrm{pp}}(H)\cap\mathcal{I}\bigr)<\infty
follows from a uniform Mourre estimate on
\mathcal{I}
and the limiting absorption principle (LAP), which yield compactness of the localized resolvent and thus only finitely many eigenvalues in
\mathcal{I}
(each of finite multiplicity).
In the continuum magnetic setting, Athmouni-Purice prove Schatten–von Neumann criteria within the magnetic Weyl calculus, allowing one to place the Birman–Schwinger type operators in
\mathfrak{S}_{p}
and derive analogous spectral finiteness statements; see
[
AP18
]
. For general background on trace ideals/Birman–Schwinger and on the Mourre framework, see
[
Sim05
]
,
[
ReSi
, vol.IV]
.

Enhancing solar air heater performance using corrugated plates and perforated baffles
Authors: Badreddine Ayadi, Khalil Hajlaoui, Ali M. Mohsen, As’ad Alizadeh, Mohamed Shaban, Walid Aich, Joy Djuansjah, Farid Sadegh Sheshpoli
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Improving the thermal performance of solar air heaters (SAHs) is essential for maximizing solar energy utilization in sustainable heating systems. This study investigates the influence of corrugated absorber plates combined with perforated baffles on the heat transfer and flow behavior of SAHs. Using three-dimensional computational fluid dynamics (CFD) simulations, various baffle layouts and pitch distances are analyzed under different Reynolds numbers to evaluate their effects on thermal efficiency, pressure drop, and overall performance. Grid independence and model validation against experimental data ensure the reliability of the numerical results. The findings demonstrate that incorporating perforated baffles significantly enhances convective heat transfer by promoting turbulence, vortex generation, and flow separation, resulting in higher Nusselt numbers and heat transfer coefficients. However, these improvements are accompanied by an increased pressure drop, requiring optimization between heat transfer enhancement and flow resistance. At Re = 5,000, Case A exhibits a 27% higher thermal performance than Case D, while the 80 mm (PF) configuration outperforms the 120 mm (PF) and 120 mm (CF) cases by 10–22%. The results provide quantitative insights into the geometric optimization of SAHs, offering a practical guideline for improving their thermal–hydraulic efficiency in renewable energy applications.

Introduction

Solar air heaters (SAHs) are innovative devices specifically designed to capture and convert sunlight into thermal energy for heating air1. These systems have gained popularity across various applications, including space heating in buildings, drying agricultural products, and supporting multiple industrial processes2. The functionality of SAHs relies on a vital component known as the absorber plate, which is essential for capturing solar radiation. As air flows through the collector, it comes into contact with the heated absorber plate, significantly raising the air’s temperature. This warmed air can be used immediately for heating purposes or stored in thermal mass systems for later use, thus enhancing the versatility of SAHs. One of the crucial benefits of SAHs is their capacity to offer a renewable and sustainable energy source. By harnessing solar energy, they greatly reduce dependence on fossil fuels, subsequently helping to lower greenhouse gas emissions. In addition to their environmental benefits, SAHs feature a relatively simple design, which contributes to their cost-effectiveness and demands minimal ongoing maintenance for continued operation. However, SAHs encounter certain challenges; they often exhibit low thermal efficiency, particularly in conditions of inadequate solar radiation or when heat loss is significant. These obstacles can restrict their wider adoption and effectiveness. To address the issues related to thermal performance, researchers have explored various enhancements, with the incorporation of baffles within the air passage of the collector emerging as one of the most promising solutions3.

Baffles act as strategically placed barriers that alter airflow patterns, disrupting smooth flow to generate turbulence. This turbulence is essential as it prolongs the residence time of air within the collector, thereby enhancing the heat exchange between the absorber plate and the incoming air stream. Different considerable numerical and experimental studies have reported promising results in the context of employing baffles in the SAH systems. Yeh et al.4 performed one of the earliest theoretical analyses centered on the thermal performance of SAHs with baffles, particularly focusing on the collector’s aspect ratio. In this regard, Romdhane et al.5 examined how varying baffle configurations within SAH systems affect heat collection efficiency, thermal rise, and pressure loss. Nevertheless, their study does not elaborate on the fluid dynamics and heat transfer characteristics of baffle-type collectors, a shortcoming also acknowledged in earlier research. The S-shaped channel formed by multiple baffles offers a simpler, more practical, cost-effective approach, spurring research interest in this field. Hu et al.6 conducted a numerical study on SAHs systems featuring internal baffles, discovering that adding interior baffles extends the air’s residence time within the systems, increases turbulence, and boosts overall effectiveness. However, including baffles also causes significant flow separation and reattachment, leading to a certain level of flow resistance6. In a similar context, Kumar et al.7 conducted experimental tests to evaluate the thermal performance of a solar air channel with an aspect ratio of 10.0, featuring multiple V-type baffles set at a 60° angle. The research reveals that thermal performance peaks at a relative baffle width 5.0. Additionally, the results show that the broken multiple V-type baffles provide better thermo-hydraulic performance than the other baffle designs implemented in solar air channels. Furthermore, Hu et al.8 numerically investigated the thermal performance of baffle-equipped SAHs by integrating holes within the baffles based on the concept of windbreak walls. Their results suggested that these perforations could enhance collector efficiency while decreasing pressure loss. In a follow-up study, Hu et al.9 proposed a novel strategy for optimizing the thermal efficiency of baffle-type SAH systems by changing the baffle arrangement to constrict the size of the first chamber. Findings from both experimental and numerical simulations indicated that minimizing the first chamber size significantly enhances thermal performance in baffle-type SAH systems while slightly affecting flow resistance. Khanlari et al. (2020)10 undertook experimental and numerical studies to explore the thermal efficiency of new parallel-pass SAHs, both with and without double baffles. Their experimental data indicated that the highest peak efficiency of approximately 84.3% was obtained at a higher mass flow rate in the parallel-pass SAHs featuring double baffles. El-Said11 implemented a numerical study to investigate the impact of curved perforated baffles on the heat transfer properties of SAH systems. Their analysis found that the optimal thermal efficiency reached about 77% for an air mass flow rate of 0.03 kg/s with a baffle angle of 7°. Sari et al.12 conducted experimental research to assess the synergistic influences of baffles and delta winglet vortex generators on the functionality of SAHs. Their research showed that SAH systems lacking baffles and delta winglet vortex generators had the least efficiency, achieving a maximum of only 13%. In contrast, SAH systems featuring six baffles and three pairs of delta winglet vortex generators achieved an efficiency of up to 20%. The results demonstrate that, on average, the energy and exergy efficiencies for the SAHs equipped with six baffles and three pairs of delta winglet vortex generators improved by 7.4% and 12%, respectively, compared to standard flat plate SAHs. Tuğan and Işık13 numerically studied the influence of louver baffles on the thermal efficiency of SAH systems. Their numerical findings suggested using louver baffles in solar heating applications because of their significant thermal efficiency and minimal pressure drop. Recognizing baffles’ critical role in enhancing the efficiency of SAHs, Chang et al. 14 developed a comprehensive mathematical model. This model had undergone rigorous validation to ensure its accuracy and reliability, specifically aimed at optimizing the design and configuration of baffles within solar air heating systems. By fine-tuning the baffle design, the research aims to maximize heat transfer efficiency while minimizing energy losses, thereby improving the overall performance of SAHs. Chang et al.15 conducted numerical research to thoroughly assess air velocity distributions and heat transfer coefficients in SAH systems to pinpoint the most effective baffle types, such as transverse and longitudinal, as well as the best baffle configurations. Their findings revealed that SAHs equipped with transverse baffles displayed narrower air channels when compared to those with longitudinal baffles, resulting in higher air velocities and smaller vortex sizes. Consequently, the collection performance of SAHs with transverse baffles was 2.34% to 6.97% superior to those featuring longitudinal baffles. Boussouar et al. (2024)16 explored the complex dynamics of flat SAHs fitted with various baffle configurations, including smooth, longitudinal, and transverse designs. Their research encompassed various perforated and unperforated baffles of varying thicknesses, tested across multiple inlet Reynolds numbers. The findings were striking; adding longitudinal and transverse baffles significantly improved heat transfer efficiency, evidenced by a notable rise in the Nusselt number. This enhancement, however, came with a substantial increase in pressure drop. Interestingly, the influence of baffle thickness on either the enhancement of the Nusselt number or the increase in pressure drop was minimal, suggesting that other factors were more crucial in the observed performance changes. In a matching study, Chompookham et al. (2024)17 used numerical analysis to reveal the complex effects of twisted multiple V-baffles on the thermal efficiency of SAH systems. Their findings highlighted a significant observation: the arrangement of these twisted baffles created numerous impinging jets against the heated surface, resulting in a dynamic flow pattern that greatly improved heat exchange between the surface and the fluid. This intricate interaction emphasized the potential of innovative baffle designs in optimizing thermal performance within SAH systems.

Pazarlıoğlu et al.18 used numerical analysis to examine how a novel elongated jet hole design affected a SAH’s thermal performance. Jet impingement was used on the absorber plate to enhance convective heat transmission. Three different elongated jet hole configurations were tested to decrease cross-flow: gradually growing, reducing, and equally extended. The outcomes were contrasted with a traditional design after a jet array was installed on the confinement plate. The researchers examined thermal efficiency, heat transfer performance, and total system efficacy. One design significantly increased efficiency and heat transfer, but it also resulted in a noticeable rise in pressure drop. Despite this, the performance was still good overall. The similarly extended jet hole design showed the best performance of all the evaluated solutions. In the other work of the same group, Pazarlıoğlu et al.19 investigated the effect of impinging jet cooling on a SAH, both with and without longitudinal fins. The absorber plate was modelled using a constant heat flux. The analyses were conducted in turbulent flow conditions, with the jet velocity constant. The effects of fin height and fluid flow rate on thermal efficiency were thoroughly investigated. The convective heat transfer coefficient changed with various flow conditions in systems with and without fins. Contour graphs depict the absorber plate’s temperature and heat transfer distributions. The results revealed that adding impingement jets and boosting flow increased heat transfer and efficiency.

Nouri Kadijani et al.20 looked into the heat transmission properties of humid air in a rectangular channel of a SAH with V-shaped ribs. The impacts of rib geometry (height, pitch, and angle) and the relative humidity and flow rate of the entering air were investigated. The simulations were performed using commercial fluid dynamics software based on the finite volume approach. The results showed that the ribs caused the thermal barrier layer to separate and reattach, which improved heat transfer. Increasing the rib angle resulted in stronger secondary flows and faster heat transfer rates. Furthermore, increasing humidity in the entering air resulted in a significant improvement in heat transmission. Kazemi Moghadam et al.21 involved a numerical analysis of a SAH with novel arc-shaped ribs for Reynolds numbers between 6,000 and 12,000. Aspect ratio, rib pitch, inter-rib distance, and rib cross-section type were all examined concerning thermal efficiency. According to the results, Ribs with a quadrangular cross-section had the best performance coefficient. The highest thermal performance was demonstrated by ribs with a narrow pitch of 40 mm and an aspect ratio of 0.5; inter-rib distance did not significantly impact efficiency. An experimental and CFD study was performed by Nidhul et al.22 to evaluate the thermo-hydraulic behavior of a flat-plate SAH with discrete inclined baffles and semi-cylindrical sidewalls. For Rh = 0.1, the baffle pitch ratio (RP = 0.6–1.0) was optimized for 6,000 < Re < 14,000. Three gap configurations were examined, leading, trailing, and both apices, with the trailing-apex gap yielding the highest performance (2.69). The proposed design achieved 55–70% collector efficiency and 1.5–2.2% exergetic efficiency, outperforming the ribbed rectangular SAH (30–55% and 0.9–1.7%, respectively) while showing better cost-effectiveness at lower Reynolds numbers. In the other work, Nidhul et al.23 designed a triangular duct SAH for an indirect-type solar dryer (ITSD) and analyzed it using CFD for rib inclinations of 30°–75° and Re = 5000–17,500. The optimal rib angle (α = 45°) delivered 17% higher effectiveness than a ribbed rectangular SAH and 79% higher than a smooth duct. It increased outlet air temperature by 6 K for the same heat input. The ribbed triangular SAH achieved a 60.3% reduction in moisture ratio and a 97.9% rise in diffusivity, confirming its superior energy efficiency for ITSD systems.

One of the best passive ways to improve heat transfer in thermal systems24, such as SAHs, has long been acknowledged as using porous media. Porous materials, such as wire meshes, packed beds, and metal foams, greatly enhanced the heat transmission surface area and encouraged fluid mixing at the microscale, improving convective heat transfer, much like rib or fin roughness. Higher Nusselt numbers and enhanced thermal performance resulted from the creation of numerous flow channels through the linked pores and increased turbulence intensity close to the heated surface. Numerous studies used porous inserts or layers to attain greater outlet air temperatures and collector efficiency than traditional smooth or ribbed channels. Numerous studies used porous inserts or layers in SAHs. An analyze the performance of the SAH with thin porous layers under both uniform and non-uniform configurations was done by Nidhul et al.25 using 3D CFD. In transient simulations, daily changes in solar flux and inlet air temperature were considered using the discrete-ordinate (D–O) radiation model. The porous layer above it much enhanced the absorber plate’s thermo-hydraulic performance parameter (THPP). It also eliminated hot patches characteristic of ribbed surfaces and decreased irreversible heat transfer. The Nusselt number increased by 7.5 times, and the THPP increased by 60% in the descending porous arrangement compared to the uniform instance. The energy study also revealed increased energetic efficiency and higher collector efficiency (66–70%) through improved conduction, radiation, and convective interactions within the porous medium. An experimental and numerical analysis by Chaurasiya and Singh26 improved a wavy PCM unit with and without a porous medium for SAH applications. The PCM and porous media were paraffin wax and wire mesh, respectively. The ideal porosity (ϕ = 96%) and porous fraction (ψ = 50%) were used for the maximum power and energy density. When the improved unit was incorporated into an impinging-jet SAH, it produced thermal backups that were both quick and long-lasting. Tests conducted outdoors showed a 5-h thermal backup with a temperature rise of at least 5 °C and a peak exit temperature of 59 °C at 0.01 kg/s. The system’s low running cost of 0.02 INR/kWh demonstrated its high energy efficiency. Lava rock was tested as a sensible heat storage medium for a double-pass SAH by Din et al.27 in three different configurations: one with a 50% packed bed, one with a 100% packed bed, and one without. Significant efficiency increases were observed in experiments conducted with irradiances ranging from 590 to 1000 W/m2 and flow speeds of 0.02 to 0.12 kg/s. Thermal efficiency increased to 72.2% (50%) and 76.4% (100%) at 1000 W/m2 from 65.0% (no rock). The temperature of the absorber decreased from 91.3 °C to 80.0 °C and 71.4 °C, indicating better heat retention and even temperature distribution. The 100% lava rock bed performed the best overall for steady thermal retention in SAHs, demonstrating its efficacy.

Mahanand and Senapati28 conducted an extensive review study to evaluate advanced techniques for enhancing heat transfer in Solar Air Heaters (SAHs). They emphasize that the incorporation of artificial roughness on the collector plate is an effective methodology, primarily due to its ability to disrupt the boundary layer, generate strong turbulence, and induce secondary flow formation, all of which significantly improve the heat transport rate. Their analysis highlighted that specific roughness elements, such as multi-V-fashion ribs with gaps and conical ring turbulators, exhibit the highest heat transfer ratios compared to smooth base models.

Mund et al.29 also provided a comprehensive overview focusing specifically on solar air collectors as a viable means of harnessing solar thermal energy, citing their importance in the global shift away from fossil fuels. The authors sought to synthesize the recent advancements and modifications implemented to enhance the efficiency and performance of these collectors. Their work meticulously documents the impact of various structural and geometric alterations on critical performance indicators such as the Nusselt number, friction factor, and overall thermohydraulic performance. Furthermore, the study delves into the optimization achieved through methods like air jet impingement, demonstrating the continued scientific focus on developing highly efficient solar thermal systems to meet growing power demands.

Researchers have investigated various passive methods to improve the thermal efficiency of SAHs. These include extended surfaces, porous media, phase-change materials for thermal storage, and artificial roughness features, including ribs, grooves, dimples, vortex generators, baffles, and perforations. Each strategy seeks to generate turbulence, disturb the thermal boundary layer, or expand the effective heat-transfer region to boost collector efficiency. Several recent review studies have thoroughly categorized and contrasted these augmentation techniques’ mechanics, efficacy, and design concerns to provide a thorough overview and classification30–36.

The findings reviewed in this section highlight that the type and geometry of baffles significantly influence the thermo-hydraulic behavior of SAHs. Numerous passive enhancement techniques, such as ribs, grooves, dimples, vortex generators, porous media, and perforated surfaces, have intensified turbulence and improved heat transfer. Building on these developments, this study was motivated by the need to design an SAH configuration that achieves high thermal performance while maintaining moderate pressure losses. Accordingly, a novel combination of sinusoidal absorber plates and strategically positioned perforated baffles is proposed to optimize flow mixing and boundary-layer disruption. Unlike conventional flat or ribbed configurations, the present work systematically investigated the influence of baffle geometry (arc and sinusoidal shapes) and pitch variations on heat transfer and flow distribution through comprehensive CFD simulations validated against experimental data. The innovation of this research lies in its comparative evaluation of perforated and non-perforated baffles and its detailed assessment of how geometric parameters govern turbulence generation and thermal efficiency. These insights provide a physics-based foundation for designing next-generation SAHs with improved energy utilization and practical efficiency under realistic operating conditions.

Numerical study

Problem explanation

The SAH’s corrugated (sinusoidal-shaped) absorber plate is intended to improve thermal performance by expanding the heat transfer surface area and encouraging more efficient airflow distribution. Baffles are strategically placed on the SAH’s bottom surface to increase turbulence, generate vortices, and produce swirling flow patterns inside the air channel. By decreasing the thickness of the thermal boundary layer and increasing heat exchange efficiency, these flow disturbances significantly increase the convective heat transfer rate between the absorber plate and the incoming air. A promising design for maximizing heat absorption and air temperature rise while preserving an adequate pressure drop-to-heat transfer ratio, the combination of sinusoidal shape and baffle location is anticipated to produce higher thermal efficiency than traditional flat-plate SAHs. A comprehensive three-dimensional (3D) numerical model was developed to accurately assess the thermal and hydraulic performance of the proposed solar air collector. The fluid flow (air) and heat transfer were modeled by solving the steady-state governing equations for the conservation of mass, momentum, and energy. To accurately simulate the flow behavior within the collector channel, a validated turbulence model, such as the k − ω model, was employed. The boundary conditions were precisely defined to reflect the system’s operation. The collector inlet was specified with a uniform velocity inlet and a constant ambient temperature. At the outlet of the computational domain, a constant pressure outlet condition was applied. For the solid walls of the collector, including the absorber plate and side walls, a no-slip condition was enforced for momentum. A conjugate heat transfer approach was utilized to couple the fluid and solid thermal fields. The absorber surface was subjected to a solar heat flux equivalent to the measured insolation.

The computational domain was discretized using the Finite Volume Method (FVM) with a high-quality mesh. The pressure–velocity coupling was managed using the SIMPLE algorithm, and all transport equations were solved using second-order discretization schemes to ensure solution fidelity. The convergence criterion for all conservation equations was set to 10 − 6.

Results and discussions

Evaluation of CFD model validity

Validation analysis is crucial in CFD research to guarantee the precision and dependability of simulations by comparing results with experimental data or theoretical correlations. Confirming that boundary conditions, turbulence models, and numerical approximations are applied correctly decreases errors and uncertainties. Inaccurate CFD predictions may impact the evaluation of flow behaviour, heat transfer, and pressure drop without validation. By guiding mesh refinement and solver improvements and increasing model credibility, this method guarantees that CFD is a reliable engineering design and optimization tool, minimizing the need for expensive trials.

Therefore, the acquired findings of the proposed model here are compared with the experimental results of Phu et al. [43]to validate the numerical model used in this work. This research examined how Reynolds number, relative roughness height, and relative roughness pitch affect heat transmission and pressure loss in the SAH. Independent grid size analysis

To guarantee that simulation results are accurate, stable, and free of numerical aberrations caused by improper mesh resolution, CFD necessitates an independent investigation of grid size, commonly referred to as a grid independence study. While an excessively fine mesh raises computing costs without yielding appreciable accuracy advantages, a coarse mesh may result in erroneous flow and heat transfer predictions. Numerical dependability is ensured by fine-tuning the mesh and comparing the outcomes to identify the ideal grid size at which additional refining does not affect the solution. By striking a balance between accuracy and efficiency for strong engineering design and optimization, this procedure raises the credibility of CFD.

Five grids with varying cell counts and grid sizes are examined in this work for the autonomous grid size check, with the SAH’s outlet temperature serving as the control element. The analysis’s results are illustrated in Fig. 3. The mesh independence study is shown in this figure, which plots the outlet temperature against the number of grid cells employed in the CFD simulation. The pattern indicates that the simulation is still sensitive to grid refinement because the outlet temperature first varies dramatically as the mesh resolution improves. Nevertheless, even with more mesh refinement, the output temperature stabilizes slightly after about 800,000 grid cells, indicating that the solution is now grid-independent. This enables an ideal balance between computational efficiency and solution accuracy by confirming that raising the grid density further has no discernible effect on the accuracy of the findings. By minimizing numerical errors brought on by inadequate grid resolution, the study confirms the accuracy of the CFD model for heat transfer analysis. Influence of baffles pitch

By affecting heat transmission, turbulence production, and pressure drop, the baffle pitch value significantly affects a SAH thermal performance. By increasing turbulence intensity and thermal mixing, a smaller pitch improves convective heat transfer. However, it also causes a more significant pressure drop, lowering system efficiency because it requires more pumping power. On the other hand, a higher pitch reduces pressure losses and flow resistance, but it may also result in less heat transfer because of decreased turbulence and less interaction between the absorber plate and the airflow. An ideal pitch ensures effective heat exchange while reducing energy losses by balancing pressure drop and heat transfer augmentation. Thus, choosing the right baffle pitch is essential for optimizing thermal efficiency and enhancing SAH performance in various flow scenarios.

Scaling behaviour of charged particles generated in Xe − - Xe collisions at s NN \sqrt{s_{\rm{NN}}} = 5.44 TeV using the AMPT model
Zarina Banoo 1,2, Ramni Gupta 1, Balwan Singh 1, Salman K. Malik 1 Fakhar Ul Haider 1, Sheetal Sharma 1

Abstract
The spatial configurations of particles produced in the kinematic phase space during a heavy-ion collision reflect the characteristics of the system created in the collision. The scaling behaviour of the multiplicity fluctuations is studied for the charged particles generated in Xe–Xe collisions at s NN \sqrt{s_{\rm{NN}}} = 5.44 TeV using the String Melting (SM) mode of the AMPT (A Multi-Phase Transport) model. The scaling behaviour of the normalized factorial moments ( F q F_{\text{q}} ) give significant information about the dynamics of the systems under study. A linear power-law growth of the F q F_{\text{q}} with the increasing phase space resolution, termed as intermittency, is investigated. The anomalous fractal dimension D q D_{\text{q}} is determined, which is linked to the self-similarity and fractal nature of the particle emission spectra, a dependence of which on the order of the moment ( q q ) is characterised by the intermittency index ( φ q \varphi_{\text{q}} ). Relating q th q^{\rm{th}} order Normalised Factorial Moment (NFM) with F 2 F_{2} , the scaling exponent ( ν \nu ) is determined that quantifies the dynamics of the system created by these collisions and is analyzed for its dependence on the transverse momentum bin width ( Δ ​ p T \Delta p_{\text{T}} ). A comparative study of experimental and model results may help to understand the dynamics of multiparticle production in the heavy-ion collisions.



I
Introduction

To identify the nature of the phase transition and to understand the mechanism leading to multiparticle production in the heavy-ion collisions are among some of the main objectives of present-day high energy physics experiments. Two distinct types of phase transitions occur when two nuclei collide. The first one is due to the compression of the nuclei under high energy and density, which results in the deconfinement of the quarks and is known as the hadron-quark phase transition and the second is the quark-hadron phase transition, which occurs when the Quark-Gluon Plasma (QGP) expands sufficiently and the confinement forces set in at the end of the evolution to form hadrons
Hwa:2014xxa
. According to the Quantum Chromodynamics (QCD) theory of strongly interacting matter, the phase transition across the critical line may accur for large values of baryon chemical potential (
\mu_{\text{B}}
) with a Critical End Point (CEP) that is expected to exist at the termination of the first-order phase transition line. This endpoint is characterized by a second-order phase transition based on generic principles whereas when
\mu_{\text{B}}=0
, the transition transforms into a smooth crossover
Stefanek:2014vva
;
Antoniou:2009abc
.

During the process of phase transition, conventional Quantum Electrodynamic (QED) matter exhibits large-scale fluctuations as the system approaches the critical point, characterized by a divergence in correlation length. This phenomenon manifests as critical opalesence – a classical signature of criticality in thermodynamic systems
Luo:2017zza
. Analogously, in QCD matter created in high-energy heavy-ion collisions, a similar rise in correlation length is expected near the CEP, leading to the development of significant number density fluctuations during the phase transition. These fluctuations span multiple length scales, resulting in spatial patterns that exhibit features of self-similarity or critical clustering. As such, observables related to multiplicity fluctuations can serve as effective signatures of critical phenomena, capable of revealing the presence of patches of all sizes in the produced particle distributions
Luo:2017zza
;
Yang:2017aaz
. The investigation of multiplicity fluctuations is therefore a powerful tool for detecting the formation of QGP
Mukherjee:2016yya
, gaining insight into the mechanisms of particle production, determining the location of CEP, and probing the order of the QCD phase diagram.

The multiplicity distributions of the charged particles generated in the heavy-ion collisions can be investigated to gain an insight into the particle production mechanism and the order of the phase transition. Intermittency studies is one such methodology that analyzes fluctuations in the number density of particles in a phase space. Experimental investigations have observed intermittent patterns, e.g., in hadron-nucleon
Holynski:1989
;
Holynski:1989aa
, muon-hadron
Derado:1990
;
Zhong:1994
and leptonic interactions,
Parashar:1996xmv
;
Feindt:1990zf
but no conclusive evidence about the mechanism responsible for the multiparticle production and of critical fluctuations are obtained. Recently, researchers have proposed to study this data-hungry methodology for high multiplicity events from the LHC
Hwa:2014xxa
;
Yang:2017aaz
. An analysis on similar lines using Toy MC is reported in
Sharma:2023oxo
. The AMPT (A Multi Phase Transport) model is capable of providing kinetic description of the main stages of the high-energy collisions and
recent studies have highlighted its robust capability to elucidate particle production mechanism in the hadron-nucleus and nucleus-nucleus collisions with energy of collisions ranging from 5 to 5500 GeV
Lin:2005vza
. Scaling behaviour of the fluctuations in the multiplicity distributions obtained from the collisions between xenon (Xe) nuclei using the string melting mode of the AMPT model are investigated.

The letter is divided into four sections. In the next section, a brief overview of the AMPT model is given, followed by the analysis methodology in section 3. Observations and results from the analysis of the AMPT events for the study of scaling behaviour of the charged particle generation in Xe–Xe system are discussed in section 4 followed by a summary in section 5.

II
The AMPT Model

A hybrid transport Monte Carlo model, the AMPT model simulates the heavy-ion collisions at ultra-relativistic energies
Lin:2005vza
;
Lin:2011aca
. As in all models the outcome particle distributions are dependent on the input parameters which include initial partonic phase and final hadronic phase. It has two modes of operation, the Default (DF) mode and the String Melting (SM) mode. These two modes differ in the hadronization process and consists of four subprocesses; the initialization of the collision, partonic transport, hadronization mechanism (i.e., conversion from the partonic to the hadronic phase), and finally, the hadronic transport
Zhang:1999vza
. The initial conditions are taken from the HIJING (Heavy Ion Jet Interaction Generator)
Wang:1991abc
event generator that gives the spatial and the momentum distributions of minijet partons accompanied by soft string excitations. Radiations originating from various states of HIJING are kept off. The partonic interactions are simulated using Zhang’s Parton Cascade (ZPC) model
Lin:2005vza
, which emphasizes two-body interactions for which the cross sections were obtained based on perturbative Quantum Chromodynamics (pQCD).

Minijet partons and excited strings are the part of initial conditions of HIJING in case of the AMPT in default mode. The minijet partons undergo scatterings via the ZPC model. After the partonic phase ends, these partons recombine into strings, which then fragment into hadrons using the Lund string fragmentation model. In contrast, the String Melting mode converts all excited strings from HIJING into partons before the parton cascade begins. These partons, along with the minijet partons, undergo scatterings via ZPC. After partonic interactions cease, hadronization is performed using a quark coalescence model. The hadronic interactions in both modes are modeled using the A Relativistic Transport (ART) model
Lin:2005vza
, which has been extended to include additional reaction channels relevant at higher energies. The hadronic phase continues until kinetic freeze-out, after which further hadronic interactions are assumed to not affect the final observables
Lin:2011aca
.

III
Analysis Methodology

In ultra-relativistic heavy-ion collisions with high particle number density per bin in the phase space, the event-by-event study of particle multiplicity distributions, to extract the signal of interest, is favoured
Jeon:2003nza
. The pseudorapidity (
\eta
) and azimuth angle (
\phi
) define the angular distributions of particles produced in these collisions
PDG2022
. The spatial configurations of the particles produced depend on the dynamics of the system created in the collisions. With different sets of collision conditions these configurations may vary from event-to-event. The scaling behaviour of the moments of these particle number density distributions (the spatial configurations) is one of the important characteristics to understand the system’s dynamics.

A two dimensional intermittency analysis technique as proposed in
Hwa:2011bu
is used to calculate normalized factorial moments (NFM) that gauge bin-to-bin fluctuations in particles generated in (
\eta,\phi
) phase space of an event. The phase space is partitioned in a lattice of cells with
M
number of bins along each dimension so as to have a square lattice with a total of M
{}_{\eta}\times{M_{\phi}}
bins. M
η
and M
ϕ
being the number of bins along
\eta
and
\phi
dimension respectively. The particles generated in an event are mapped onto this lattice that may result into different number of particles per cell. The distribution of particles in the bins or cells give spatial configuration of particles in the phase space. Varying the number of partitions (M), the number of particles that go into each cell (known as bin multiplicity) changes. If
n_{\rm{m}}
defines the bin multiplicity in the
m^{\rm{th}}
bin of an
e^{\rm{th}}
event then the event factorial moment of order
q
with condition that bin multiplicity
n_{m}\geq\textit{q}
is defined as

\begin{split}f_{q}^{e}(M)\,=\,<n_{m}(n_{m}-1)\dots(n_{m}-q+1)>.\end{split}

(1)

Here
<\ldots>
is the averaging over the total number of bins (
M^{\rm{2}}
, for
{M_{\eta}}
=
M_{\phi}
= M (say)) in the two-dimensional phase space. Then for a sample of N events, the NFM
F_{\rm{q}}(M)
is defined as
Hwa:2011bu
;

\begin{split}F_{q}(M)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{e=1}^{N}\frac{<n_{m}(n_{m}-1)\dots(n_{m}-q+1)>_{e}}{[<n_{m}>_{e}]^{q}},\end{split}

(2)

where
q
the order of the moments, is a positive integer
\geq 2
. These normalized factorial moments have a property of detecting and characterizing the dynamical fluctuations indicated by the dependence of
F_{\rm{q}}(M)
on the decreasing phase space cell size (
\delta
) or increasing number of bins (as M
\propto
1/
\delta
), such that

\displaystyle F_{q}(\delta)\propto(\delta)^{-\varphi_{\rm{q}}},

(3)

where
\varphi_{\rm{q}}
is known as the intermittency index and this power-law scaling behaviour is termed as
intermittency

Wolf:1996aan
. The intermittency study of heavy-ion data has been successfully explained to study the high spike event observed in the cosmic ray data in the JACEE experiment
Burnett:1983abc
. In terms of number of bins in the phase space, Eq.(
3
) can be written as,

\displaystyle F_{q}(M)\,\propto\,(M^{d})^{\varphi_{\rm{q}}}.

(4)

Here in above equation
d
defines the number of dimensions of the phase space. Scaling of
F_{\rm{q}}(M)
with the number of bins as in Eq.(
4
) is also known as
M-scaling
or resolution scaling.

\varphi_{q}
is related to the anomalous fractal dimension
d_{\rm{q}}
relating it to the order of the moment
q
as
Sarkisian:1993pi
.

d_{q}=\frac{\varphi_{q}}{(q-1)}.

(5)

The significance of anomalous fractal dimension is that it reveals the nature of the fractal structure of the system under study. Further
d_{\rm{q}}
is related to the generalized fractal dimension
(D_{\rm{q}})
through the relation
Sarkisian:1993ne
;
Gelovani:1997ne

d_{q}=D_{\rm{T}}-D_{\rm{q}},

(6)

where

respectively. A distinct characterization of fractality is observed based on the dependence of

on the moment order
q
. A system with
multifractal
nature
Gupta:2019zox
shows dependence of
d_{\rm{q}}
on
q
, and
monofractal
nature in case there is no such dependence.

V
Summary

To summarize, the intermittency analysis is performed for the charged particles generated in the Xe–Xe collisions at
\sqrt{s_{\mathrm{NN}}}\text{ = 5.44 TeV}
using string melting mode of the AMPT model. The charged particles generated in the central events with impact parameter b
\leq
3.5
fm in different
p_{\mathrm{T}}
intervals with
|\eta|\leq 0.8
in full azimuth and
p_{\rm{T}}\leq 2.0
GeV/c are investigated. Normalized factorial moments are calculated for the moment order
q
= 2, 3, 4 and 5 which do not show significant power-law behaviour with number of bins (M) in the phase space but a weak dependence of higher order NFM on the second order NFM (F-scaling) is observed. The average value of the scaling exponent
\nu
is found to be significantly greater than the value anticipated by the Ginzburg-Landau theory for the second-order phase transition that is 1.304 and that predicted by models with critical fluctuations. The dynamics of critical fluctuations is not incorporated in the AMPT and the observations on
\nu
made here confirms this and hence showing that the methodology is suitable to search for the critical point of nuclear matter phase diagram. Further, the study of fractal parameters of the system generated in these collisions with the SM AMPT,
D_{\text{q}}
as function of order of the moments
q
, shows a monofractal nature in particle generation and hence absence of multifractal complex particle generation. No minima in the value of
\lambda_{\rm{q}}
is observed for
q
upto 5. In the absence of critical fluctuations no intermittency signal is expected and the same is confirmed through this analysis. The particle generation process in the model has no self-similar nature or any signal to point dynamic origin due to phase transition. A similar analysis carried for the experimental data from the LHC can be compared with the observations and results obtained in this work so as to understand the experimental observations in the light of physics of the SM AMPT model.

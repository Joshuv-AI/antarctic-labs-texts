Instability of two-pulse periodic waves with long wavelength in some Hamiltonian PDEs
Thomas Courant

Abstract
We consider quasilinear generalizations of the Korteweg–de Vries equation and dispersive perturbations of the Euler equations for compressible fluids, either in Lagrangian or in Eulerian coordinates. In particular, our framework includes hydrodynamic formulation of the nonlinear Schrödinger equations. The periodic waves we study exhibit on each period two pulses, one converging to a bright soliton and one converging to a dark soliton, when wavelength goes to infinity. We show that such waves, for sufficiently large periods, are spectrally unstable. To do so, we combine two approaches. The first one is to calculate the asymptotic expansion of the Hessian matrix of the action integral and concludes using [ BMR16 ] as in [ BMR20 ] . This shows instability when both limiting solitary waves are stable. The second approach studies the convergence of the spectrum when the period goes to infinity and is applied in remaining cases, when one of the solitary waves is unstable. To carry out the latter, we prove the convergence of an appropriate renormalization of the periodic Evans function as in [ YZ19 ] .



Keywords:

Hamiltonian dynamics; periodic traveling waves; spectral stability; periodic Evans function; soliton asymptotics; homoclinic limit; abbreviated action integral.

AMS Subject Classifications:

35B35; 35B10; 35Q35; 35Q51; 35Q53; 37K45; 35P15; 34E10.

1
Introduction

The present paper studies stability/instability criteria for periodic traveling waves of Hamiltonian PDEs. For general background on nonlinear waves we refer the reader to
[
KP13
]
and to
[
AP09
,
HK08
,
KKS05
]
for specific result in Hamiltonian systems.

Because of the relatively large dimension of family of periodic traveling waves such stability/instability criteria typically fail to set a neat dichotomy. This is in strong contrast with what happens for solitary waves where for a large class of equations the family of waves with a fixed endstate is one-dimensional, parametrized by velocity, and the sign of the second-order derivative of the Boussinesq momentum determines the stability/instability. Yet, as proved in
[
BMR20
]
, following
[
BMR16
]
, one may expect a reduction of the complexity of periodic waves stability in some asymptotic regimes.

In
[
BMR16
]
the authors show some stability/instability criteria for periodic traveling waves of large classes of Hamiltonian PDEs. The goal of
[
BMR20
]
was to see what happened for those criteria in two different limits. The first one, the harmonic limit, considers periodic waves with small amplitude around a constant state. The second one, the soliton limit, studies periodic waves with large period that converge to a solitary wave. The outcome is that in the harmonic limit, periodic waves were always orbitally stable, and in the soliton limit, periodic waves share the same stability properties as their limiting soliton. Here we are interested in periodic waves of large periods, asymptotically described on each periodic cell by the superposition of two distinct solitary waves sharing the same endstate. We will call such waves
two-pulse periodic waves
. In the end we show that they are always spectrally unstable.

For the classes of equations that we consider, wave profiles are in correspondence with trajectories of a two-dimensional Hamiltonian differential equation, hence with level sets of a two-dimensional function. As a consequence the three asymptotic regimes mentioned above, the two studied in
[
BMR20
]
and the one studied here, contain all the generic ways in which a family of periodic waves may end.

The instability we prove is at the spectral and linear level, and holds both under co-periodic and localized perturbations. To our knowledge, the conversion of spectral instability into nonlinear instability for periodic traveling waves is a largely open question for quasilinear dispersive PDEs like the ones we are considering. In the semilinear case however we expect nonlinear orbital instability under localized perturbations to follow from the analysis of
[
JLL19
]
. Yet we mention that based on studies for parabolic systems
[
JNRZ14
]
, space-modulated stability and not orbital stability is expected to be the sharp notion of stability for periodic waves subject to localized perturbations. Incidentally we also stress that, even for the semilinear case, nonlinear stability with respect to localized perturbations for periodic waves of Hamiltonian systems is a largely open question. For a recent significant step in this direction see
[
BGdR25
]
.

Half of our result is obtained by adapting to our present asymptotic regime the calculations of
[
BMR20
]
. In this way, we show that if the two solitons are orbitally stable then the corresponding two-pulse waves of sufficiently large period are spectrally unstable. Actually this part of the proof shows that this is the case when the sum of the second-order derivatives of the Boussinesq momenta is positive, which holds in particular when both are positive. At the basis of the result lies a co-periodic instability result
[
BMR16
]
.

Let us mention that the other half of the instability result cannot be obtained by studying modulational instability. For an introduction to the latter we refer to
[
BHJ16
]
. The idea is quite natural and the authors of
[
BMR20
]
have also studied in their companion paper
[
BMR21
]
what happens in the same regimes to the modulational criteria derived in
[
BNR14a
]
. Nevertheless, as we develop in Remark
3.4.2
, in our regime we show that when the arguments mentioned do not conclude to instability the modulational system is hyperbolic, hence the absence of modulational instability.

Instead, we complete our study with a direct spectral perturbation analysis. When doing so we draw inspiration from single-pulse soliton analyses in
[
Gar97
,
SS01
,
YZ19
]
. More precisely, adapting
[
YZ19
]
to our current regime, we show that a suitable rescaled version of the periodic Evans functions converges, at an exponential rate, to the product of the Evans functions of the two solitons. With this result in hands, we conclude that if at least one of the solitary waves is unstable then the corresponding two-pulse waves of sufficiently large period are spectrally unstable.

The situation analyzed in the latter part of the argument is probably easier to guess: the two solitary waves being superposed far away from each other, there is some room for solitary-wave instabilities to develop. The former situation, when both solitary waves are stable, is intuitively less clear. A possible heuristic scenario is that perturbations could change velocities of each solitary wave somewhat independently hence break the synchronized superposition.

The present paper is organized as follows. In the next section, we recall the general framework of
[
BNR14b
,
BMR16
,
BMR20
]
and state our main result. Section
3
is devoted to the calculation of the asymptotic expansion of the action in the same spirit as
[
BMR20
]
and Section
4
is for the asymptotic expansion of the Evans function, in the same spirit as
[
YZ19
]
.

2.1
A class of Hamiltonian PDEs

We consider the same abstract systems as in
[
BNR14b
,
BMR16
,
BMR20
]
. So we are interested in the following Hamiltonian PDE,

\partial_{t}U=\partial_{x}\left(B\delta\mathcal{H}[U]\right),

(1)

where
U
takes values in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
B
is a symmetric and nonsingular matrix, and the Hamiltonian
\mathcal{H}
depends on

and
U_{x}
, the notation
[\cdot]
is used to indicate this dependence. Furthermore,

In practice, in the case
N=1
we are interested in quasilinear, generalized versions of the Korteweg-de Vries equation

\partial_{t}v=\partial_{x}\left(\delta e[v]\right),

(qKdV)

and in the case

, by the Euler-Korteweg system, either in Eulerian coordinates,

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}\rho+\partial_{x}\left(\rho u\right)=0,\\
\partial_{t}u+u\partial_{x}u+\partial_{x}\left(\delta\mathcal{E}[\rho]\right)=0,\end{array}\right.

(EKE)

and in mass Lagrangian coordinates,

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}v=\partial_{x}u,\\
\partial_{t}u=\partial_{x}\left(\delta e[v]\right).\end{array}\right.

(EKL)

For discussion on the physics meanings of the equation, we refer to the introduction of
[
Ben13
,
BMR16
,
BMR20
]
. The system case includes hydrodynamic formulation of the non-linear Schrödinger equation; for similar results on the Schrödinger equation, see
[
AR22
]
. We also point out Section 5.2 of
[
BMR16
]
for a discussion on the different coordinates for the Euler-Korteweg system.

We do the same assumption on our abstract system as in
[
BMR20
]
. The idea is to have a framework that meets our examples, (

), (

) and (

), and in which periodic traveling waves arise as families parametrized by their speed and

constants of integration. We recall the assumption here.

Assumption 1
.

We enforce that
N=1
or
N=2
.

•

If
N=1
then we assume that
U=v
,
B=b\in\mathbb{R}^{\ast}
and
\mathcal{H}[v]=\mathcal{E}(v,v_{x})
.

•

If
N=2
then we assume that
U=(v,u)^{\T}
,
B=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&b\\
b&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)
with
b\neq 0
and

\mathcal{H}[U]=\mathcal{I}(v,u)+\mathcal{E}(v,v_{x}).

In both cases, we assume that

is quadratic in

with

, and, if

, that

is quadratic in

Note that (

\partial_{t}\mathcal{H}[U]=\partial_{x}\left(\frac{1}{2}\delta\mathcal{H}[U]\cdot B\delta\mathcal{H}[U]+\nabla_{U_{x}}\mathcal{H}[U]\cdot\partial_{x}\left(B\delta\mathcal{H}[U]\right)\right),

and one, stemming from the space translation invariance, on the

, which is defined as

where

is the scalar product on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. The latter conservation law is

\partial_{t}Q(U)=\partial_{x}\left(U\cdot\delta\mathcal{H}[U]+\leg\mathcal{H}[U]\right),

where

is the formal

transform, which is

\leg\mathcal{H}[U]=U_{x}\cdot\nabla_{U_{x}}\mathcal{H}[U]-\mathcal{H}[U].

2.2
Traveling waves

We are interested in the traveling waves of (
1

[

\delta\left(\mathcal{H}+cQ\right)[\underline{U}]+\bm{\lambda}=0,

\leg\left(\mathcal{H}+cQ+\bm{\lambda}\cdot\right)[\underline{U}]=\mu.

These equations are ODEs, so using Assumption

If

2.3
Main result and strategy of proof

To study the instability of our waves, we will use two different approaches. To explain it, we first need to recall some stability/instability criteria for periodic waves and solitary waves. The first one is for periodic waves and is taken from
[
BMR16
]
. To this end, for a periodic wave parametrized by
(c,\bm{\lambda},\mu)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}
with profile
\underline{U}
and period
X
, as in
[
BNR14b
,
BMR16
]
, we define the abbreviated action integral

\Theta(c,\bm{\lambda},\mu):=\int_{0}^{X}\mathcal{H}[\underline{U}]+cQ(\underline{U})+\bm{\lambda}\cdot\underline{U}+\mu\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}x.

It has been shown in earlier work that the
(N+2)\times(N+2)
Hessian matrix
\nabla_{(\mu,\bm{\lambda},c)}^{2}\Theta
of the action is important to understand the stability of periodic waves and their modulations, see for example
[
BJK11
,
Joh09
,
BNR14a
,
BNR14b
,
BMR16
]
. Here we recall the criteria shown in
[
BMR16
]
, which is the one that we will use. In the following,
n(\nabla_{(\mu,\bm{\lambda},c)}^{2}\Theta)
corresponds to the
negative signature
of
\nabla_{(\mu,\bm{\lambda},c)}^{2}\Theta
, so it counts the number of negative directions for the associated quadratic form in
\mathbb{R}^{N+2}
.

Theorem 2.3.1
.

Under Assumption
1
, for a wave locally parametrized by
(c,\bm{\lambda},\mu)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}
such that
\partial_{\mu}^{2}\Theta
is nonzero and
\nabla_{(\mu,\bm{\lambda},c)}^{2}\Theta
is non singular,

•

if
n(\nabla_{(\mu,\bm{\lambda},c)}^{2}\Theta)=N
, then the wave is conditionally orbitally stable with respect to co-periodic perturbations;

•

if
n(\nabla_{(\mu,\bm{\lambda},c)}^{2}\Theta)-N
is odd, then the wave is spectrally unstable to co-periodic perturbations. More precisely the linearized operator admits a real positive eigenvalue with Floquet exponents
\xi=0
.

In the first statement "conditionally orbitally stable with respect to co-periodic perturbation" means that a solution to (
1
) stays, as long as it exists, as close as we want, in the energy space, to some translate of the underlying wave. If
N=1
the energy space is
H^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}/X\mathbb{Z}\right)
and
H^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}/X\mathbb{Z}\right)\times L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}/X\mathbb{Z}\right)
if
N=2
. In general, this is not enough to conclude global existence in the energy space because local existence for this type of quasilinear PDE is not known in such low-regularity spaces. Up to our knowledge the best result for
N=1
is in
H^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}/X\mathbb{Z}\right)
for
s>\frac{7}{2}
, see
[
Mie17
,
Ian22
]
and in
H^{s+1}\left(\mathbb{R}/X\mathbb{Z}\right)\times H^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}/X\mathbb{Z}\right)
for
s>\frac{3}{2}
if
N=2
, see
[
BDD06
,
BDD07
]
.

Spectral instability means that the linearized equation admits exponentially growing solutions. More precisely,
n(\nabla_{(\mu,\bm{\lambda},c)}^{2}\Theta)-N
counts mod 2 the number of positive eigenvalues for the Floquet exponents
\xi=0
of the linearized operator. This is done using Evans functions calculations; see
[
BMR16
]
.

This type of stability/instability criteria for periodic waves is inspired by the theory of stability for solitary waves in Hamiltonian systems. From this we will only recall the results that we will use here; for reference on the topic, see
[
GSS87
,
DBGN15
,
AP09
,
KP13
]
. A family of solitary waves can be parametrized by their speed
c
and endstate
U_{s}
. For a solitary wave,

, we introduce is Boussinesq momentum

\mathcal{M}(c,U_{s})=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left(\mathcal{H}[U]+cQ(U)+\bm{\lambda}_{s}\cdot U+\mu_{s}\right)\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}x,

with
\bm{\lambda}_{s}:=-\nabla_{U}\left(\mathcal{H}+cQ\right)(U_{s},0)
and
\mu_{s}:=\left(\mathcal{H}+cQ\right)(U_{s},0)-\bm{\lambda}_{s}\cdot U_{s}
. We have the following stability/instability criteria.

Theorem 2.3.2
.

Under Assumption
1
, for a solitary wave locally parametrized by
(c,U_{s})\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}
, if we denote
\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}_{c}
as the derivative with respect to the speed
c
of the solitary wave with the final state

fixed then

•

if
\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}_{c}^{2}\mathcal{M}<0
, the solitary wave is spectrally unstable, more precisely, the linearized operator have a real positive eigenvalue;

•

if
\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}_{c}^{2}\mathcal{M}>0
, the solitary wave is conditionally orbitally stable.

As for periodic waves, the first part comes from Evans functions calculations, see
[
PW92
]
for the case

[

]
for the case

. The second part comes from the general theory of Grillakis, Shatah and Strauss
[
GSS87
,
DBGN15
]
.

We can now express our main result on the instability of two-pulse periodic waves.

Theorem 2.3.3
.

Under Assumption
1
and
2
, for a two-pulse periodic wave of sufficiently large period, if we denote
\mathcal{M}_{\ell}
(respectively
\mathcal{M}_{r}
)
the Boussinesq momentum of the left-hand soliton (respectively right-hand soliton), we have the following result.

•

If
\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}_{c}^{2}\mathcal{M}_{\ell}+\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}_{c}^{2}\mathcal{M}_{r}>0
then the two-pulse periodic wave is spectrally unstable with respect to co-periodic perturbation. More precisely the linearized operator admits a positive real eigenvalue with Floquet exponent
\xi=0
.

•

If one of the solitons is spectrally unstable, then the two-pulse periodic wave is spectrally unstable. More precisely the linearized operator admits an eigenvalue in
\mathbb{C}_{+}=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\mid\Rel(\lambda)>0\}
for each Floquet exponent.

We first begin with remarks on this result. This result tells us that in all cases, except if the two solitons are spectrally stable and
\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}_{c}^{2}\mathcal{M}_{\ell}=\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}_{c}^{2}\mathcal{M}_{r}=0
, two-pulse periodic waves with sufficiently large period are spectrally unstable. The conclusion is only at a linearized level, it is all about the spectrum of the linearized operator around the wave. One may wonder whether some information about what is happening at the non-linear level may be deduced. With our Assumption
1
, if
\kappa
is constant, then we can apply the result of
[
JLL19
]
. This tells us that the wave is nonlinearly orbitally unstable for co-periodic perturbation and nonlinearly unstable for localized perturbations. For the general quasilinear case,
\kappa
not constant, up to our knowledge, there is no such result.

We can also ask if there exists an integrable equation that fits in our Assumption
1
and admits two-pulse periodic waves. This is the case for the focusing modified Korteweg-de Vries equation, which is

\partial_{t}v+6v^{2}v_{x}+v_{xxx}=0.

(mKdv)

For this equation, our periodic waves are the cnoidal waves, and their stability to co-periodic perturbation has been studied in
[
KD15
]
and in
[
CP25
]
. For the solitary waves, their stability has been studied in
[
BSS87
]
. The result is the following. If we fix the endstate of the solitons to be 0, then solitary waves are all orbitally stable, and two-pulse periodic waves of sufficiently large period are spectrally unstable under co-periodic perturbation. So we can see our results as an extension of this one for a quasilinear version of mKdV-type equations.

To prove Theorem
2.3.3
we combine two approaches. The first one, inspired from
[
BMR20
]
, is to look at the asymptotic expansion of
\nabla_{(\mu,\bm{\lambda},c)}^{2}\Theta
when the period goes to infinity and apply Theorem
2.3.1
. This results in Theorem
3.4.1
which shows the first part of Theorem
2.3.3
. However, this will not be enough to conclude at the instability of the wave in all cases. This is due to the fact that the criteria of Theorem
2.3.1
only counts mod 2 the number of positive eigenvalues for the Floquet exponents
\xi=0
of the linearized operator. The second approach is to study perturbatively the spectrum for all Floquet exponents. More precisely, we link the Evans function of a two-pulse periodic wave to Evans functions of the two solitons that we have at the limit, this is Proposition
4.4.2
. In the case of a periodic wave that converges to one soliton, this has already been done in
[
YZ19
]
. In Section
4
we adapt the strategy of
[
YZ19
]
, to our case. With Proposition
4.4.2
in hand, we show Corollary
4.4.1
that gives us the second part of Theorem
2.3.3
.

Remark 2.3.1
.

One may ask if alternatively a Krein index method may be used to show our result, as in
[
BJK11
]
. In most cases, Krein indices are used because we can count mod 2 the number of positive eigenvalues, as done by the criteria of Theorem
2.3.1
. So we except that using such a method would only recover the first part of our theorem. For a detailed discussion on the link of Theorem
2.3.1
with Krein index theory, see remark 3 of
[
BMR16
]
. For references on Krein index theory, see
[
KKS05
,
BJK11
,
KP13
]
and references therein.

P^{\varepsilon}_{+,r}(X^{\varepsilon}_{r},z)=\id,\quad P^{\varepsilon}_{-,r}(-X^{\varepsilon}_{r},z)=\id,

\left\|P^{\varepsilon}_{\pm,r}-P^{0}_{\pm,r}\right\|\leq C_{r}\e^{-\tilde{\nu}X^{\varepsilon}_{r}},\penalty\ \text{for}\penalty\ y\gtrless 0

(17)

and the change of coordinates
W=P^{\varepsilon}_{\pm,r}Z
reduces

W^{\prime}=A^{\varepsilon}(\cdot+X^{\varepsilon}_{r},z)W,

to the constant-coefficient system

Z^{\prime}=A^{0}_{\infty}(z)Z,

for
y\gtrless 0
and
y\in\mathopen{[}-X^{\varepsilon}_{r},X^{\varepsilon}_{r}\mathclose{]}
.

Proof.

With Lemmas
4.2.1
and
4.2.2
in hand, the proof is exactly the same as the one of Lemma 3.1 of
[
YZ19
]
and is therefore omitted.
∎

We have the same for the left-hand soliton.

Lemma 4.2.4
.

For all
\varepsilon\geq 0
, there exist in a neighborhood of any
\left|z_{0}\right|\leq M
some bounded and uniformly invertible transformations
P^{\varepsilon}_{+,\ell}(y,z)
and
P^{\varepsilon}_{-,\ell}(y,z)
defined on
y\leq 0
and respectively
y\geq 0
, analytic in
z
as functions into
L^{\infty}\left(\mathopen{[}0,\pm\infty\mathclose{)}\right)
such that for any
0<\tilde{\nu}<\min(\theta,\nu)
and for some
C_{\ell}>0

P^{\varepsilon}_{+,\ell}(X^{\varepsilon}_{\ell},z)=\id,\quad P^{\varepsilon}_{-,r}(-X^{\varepsilon}_{\ell},z)=\id,

\left\|P^{\varepsilon}_{\pm,\ell}-P^{0}_{\pm,\ell}\right\|\leq C_{\ell}\e^{\tilde{\nu}X^{\varepsilon}_{\ell}},\penalty\ \text{for}\penalty\ y\gtrless 0

(18)

and the change of coordinates
W=P^{\varepsilon}_{\pm,\ell}Z
reduces

to the constant-coefficient system

for

4.3
The Evans functions for periodic and homoclinic orbits

To introduce the Evans functions for the periodic wave
\underline{U}^{\varepsilon}
, we denote by
\Psi^{\varepsilon}(\cdot,z)
a solution of (
16
) with an invertible initial data
\Psi^{\varepsilon}(0,z)
. Using Floquet-Bloch theory, we know from
[
Gar93
]
that for the periodic wave, the spectrum for localized perturbations is given by the union of
\gamma
-eigenvalues, where for
\gamma\in\mathbb{C}
with
\left|\gamma\right|=1
, a
\gamma
-eigenvalue
z
is a zero of the periodic Evans function

E^{\varepsilon}(z,\gamma)=\det\left(\Psi^{\varepsilon}(X^{\varepsilon},z)\Psi^{\varepsilon}(0,z)^{-1}-\gamma\id\right).

(19)

When restricting to co-periodic perturbations, the spectrum is the union of 1-eigenvalues.

For homoclinic orbits, we define their Evans functions as in
[
YZ19
,
GZ98
,
BJRZ11
,
Ben10
]
. Due to Lemma
4.1.2
, we know that the essential spectra of
\Al^{0}_{r}
and
\Al^{0}_{\ell}
is exactly the spectrum of the constant-coefficients problems, which is the spectrum of
\Al^{0}_{\infty}
for the two solitons. One can easily compute this spectrum using the Fourier transform. It has been shown in Appendix A of
[
BMR20
]
that in all cases
\sigma\left(\Al^{0}_{\infty}\right)\subset i\mathbb{R}
. Note that this computation relies on the fact that the constant
U_{s}
must verify some sign condition to be the endpoint of a soliton.

Thus we can define an Evans function for homoclinic orbits on
\mathbb{C}_{+}=\left\{z\in\mathbb{C};\penalty\ \Re(z)>0\right\}
. For
z\in\mathbb{C}_{+}
,
A^{0}_{\infty}(z)
is hyperbolic, so we know,
[
Kat95
, Lemma I.4.10]
, that the dimension of its stable and unstable subspace does not depend on
z
. So we denote by
n_{s}
the number of negative real part eigenvalues of
A^{0}_{\infty}(z)
which is the dimension of the stable subspace. We also denote by
R^{-}_{\infty}(z)
a matrix whose columns are a basis for the unstable subspace of
A^{0}_{\infty}(z)
and likewise
R^{+}_{\infty}(z)
for the stable subspace of
A^{0}_{\infty}(z)
. Furthermore, the stable and unstable subspaces of
A^{0}_{\infty}(z)
are in direct sum, so the matrix
\left(R^{-}_{\infty}(z),\penalty\ R^{+}_{\infty}(z)\right)
is invertible, we denote by
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}L^{-}_{\infty}(z)\\
L^{+}_{\infty}(z)\end{smallmatrix}\right)
its inverse. In the foregoing, using Kato’s perturbation method
[
Kat95
, pp. 99-100]
, everything can be chosen to be analytic in
z
, because
A^{0}_{\infty}
is analytic in
z
. From now on we will omit the dependence on
z
for the sake of readability.

To define the Evans function for solitons, we need bases of solutions to (
13
) decaying as
x\rightarrow+\infty
and
x\rightarrow-\infty
and the same for (
14
). For this, we may use the following matrices

R^{-}_{r}(x):=P^{0}_{-,r}(x)\e^{A^{0}_{\infty}x}R^{-}_{\infty},\quad R^{+}_{r}(x):=P^{0}_{+,r}(x)\e^{A^{0}_{\infty}x}R^{+}_{\infty},

for the right-hand soliton and

R^{-}_{\ell}(x):=P^{0}_{-,\ell}(x)\e^{A^{0}_{\infty}x}R^{-}_{\infty},\quad R^{+}_{\ell}(x):=P^{0}_{+,\ell}(x)\e^{A^{0}_{\infty}x}R^{+}_{\infty},

for the left-hand soliton, where matrices
P^{0}_{-,\ell},\penalty\ P^{0}_{+,\ell},\penalty\ P^{0}_{-,r},\penalty\ P^{0}_{+,r}
are given by Lemmas
4.2.3
and
4.2.4
. We can now define the Evans functions for the right-hand soliton and for the left-hand soliton, as it is done, for instance, in
[
YZ19
]
.

Definition 4.3.1

(
[
GZ98
]
)
.

On
\mathbb{C}_{+}
, the Evans function for the right-hand soliton is defined as

D^{0}_{r}(z)=\det\begin{pmatrix}L^{-}_{\infty}\\
L^{+}_{\infty}\end{pmatrix}\det(R^{-}_{r},R^{+}_{r})|_{x=0}=\det\begin{pmatrix}L^{-}_{\infty}\\
L^{+}_{\infty}\end{pmatrix}\det\Big(P^{0}_{-,r}(0)R^{-}_{\infty},P^{0}_{+,r}(0)R^{+}_{\infty}\Big),

(20)

and symmetrically for the left-hand soliton

D^{0}_{\ell}(z)=\det\begin{pmatrix}L^{-}_{\infty}\\
L^{+}_{\infty}\end{pmatrix}\det(R^{-}_{\ell},R^{+}_{\ell})|_{x=0}=\det\begin{pmatrix}L^{-}_{\infty}\\
L^{+}_{\infty}\end{pmatrix}\det\Big(P^{0}_{-,\ell}(0)R^{-}_{\infty},P^{0}_{+,\ell}(0)R^{+}_{\infty}\Big).

(21)

It is well known,
[
GZ98
,
MZ03
]
and references therein, that on
\mathbb{C}_{+}
, for
i\in\left\{r,\ell\right\}
,
D^{0}_{i}
, is analytic and vanishes at
z_{*}
if and only if
z_{*}
is an eigenvalue of
\Al(U_{i})
, furthermore the multiplicity coincide.

4.4
Convergence of the periodic Evans function

We are investigating the limit of the periodic Evans function on each compact of
\mathbb{C}_{+}
. This requires a suitable normalization, as in
[
YZ19
,
Gar97
]
. By Abel’s formula,

E^{\varepsilon}(z,\gamma)=\tilde{E}^{\varepsilon}(z,\gamma)\e^{\int_{0}^{2X^{\varepsilon}_{r}}\tr A^{\varepsilon}(x,z)\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}x},

where

\tilde{E}^{\varepsilon}(z,\gamma):=\det\left(\Psi^{\varepsilon}(0,z)\Psi^{\varepsilon}(2X^{\varepsilon}_{\ell},z)^{-1}-\gamma\Psi^{\varepsilon}(0,z)\Psi^{\varepsilon}(2X^{\varepsilon}_{r},z)^{-1}\right).

And as in
[
YZ19
]
we define the rescaled balanced periodic Evans function as

\tilde{D}^{\varepsilon}(z,\gamma)=\e^{\tr A_{\infty}^{0}\Pi_{s}2X^{\varepsilon}_{r}}\e^{-\tr A_{\infty}^{0}\Pi_{u}2X^{\varepsilon}_{\ell}}(-\gamma)^{-n_{s}}\tilde{E}^{\varepsilon}(z,\gamma),

(22)

where
\Pi_{u}
and
\Pi_{s}
denote the unstable and stable eigenprojections associated with
A^{0}_{\infty}(z)
. We now introduce the determinant that appears at the limit.

Definition 4.4.1
.

On
\mathbb{C}_{+}
we define

\tilde{D}^{0}(z)=\det\Big(L^{-}_{\infty}P^{0}_{+,\ell}(0)^{-1}P^{0}_{-,\ell}(0)R^{-}_{\infty}\Big)\det\Big(L^{+}_{\infty}P^{0}_{-,r}(0)^{-1}P^{0}_{+,r}(0)R^{+}_{\infty}\Big).

The goal is to link the zeros of
D^{0}
with the zeros of Evans functions for homoclinic orbits. It is done in the following proposition.

Proposition 4.4.1
.

For
z_{*}\in\mathbb{C}_{+}
,
z_{*}
is a zero of
\tilde{D}^{0}
if and only if
z_{*}
is an eigenvalue of
\A^{0}_{r}
or
\A^{0}_{\ell}
. Moreover, the multiplicity of
z_{*}
as a root of
\tilde{D}^{0}
equals the sum of its multiplicities as an eigenvalue of
\A^{0}_{r}
and
\A^{0}_{\ell}
.

Proof.

For the right-hand soliton, we have

\displaystyle\Big(P^{0}_{-,r}(0)R^{-}_{\infty},P^{0}_{+,r}(0)R^{+}_{\infty}\Big)=P^{0}_{-,r}(0)\left(R^{-}_{\infty},\penalty\ R^{+}_{\infty}\right)\begin{pmatrix}\id&L^{-}_{\infty}P^{0}_{-,r}(0)^{-1}P^{0}_{+,r}(0)R^{+}_{\infty}\\
0&L^{+}_{\infty}P^{0}_{-,r}(0)^{-1}P^{0}_{+,r}(0)R^{+}_{\infty}\end{pmatrix},

so

D^{0}_{r}(z)=\det P^{0}_{-,r}(0)\det\Big(L^{+}_{\infty}P^{0}_{-,r}(0)^{-1}P^{0}_{+,r}(0)R^{+}_{\infty}\Big).

It is the same for the left-hand soliton,

\Big(P^{0}_{-,\ell}(0)R^{-}_{\infty},P^{0}_{+,\ell}(0)R^{+}_{\infty}\Big)=P^{0}_{+,\ell}(0)\left(R^{-}_{\infty},\penalty\ R^{+}_{\infty}\right)\begin{pmatrix}L^{-}_{\infty}P^{0}_{+,\ell}(0)^{-1}P^{0}_{-,\ell}(0)R^{-}_{\infty}&0\\
L^{+}_{\infty}P^{0}_{+,\ell}(0)^{-1}P^{0}_{-,\ell}(0)R^{-}_{\infty}&\id\end{pmatrix},

and

D^{0}_{\ell}(z)=\det P^{0}_{+,\ell}(0)\det\Big(L^{-}_{\infty}P^{0}_{+,\ell}(0)^{-1}P^{0}_{-,\ell}(0)R^{-}_{\infty}\Big).

Using that
P^{0}_{+,\ell}
and
P^{0}_{-,r}
are uniformly invertible, we can conclude the proof.
∎

The next step is to prove the convergence of the rescaled periodic Evans function. It is done in the following proposition.

Proposition 4.4.2
.

On each compact
K\subset\mathbb{C}_{+}
there exists
C>0
such that

\forall z\in K,\left|\gamma\right|=1,\penalty\ \left|\tilde{D}^{\varepsilon}(z,\gamma)-D^{0}(z)\right|\leq C\e^{-\tilde{\nu}\frac{X^{\varepsilon}}{2}}.

for any
\tilde{\nu}<\min\left(\theta,\nu,\sigma\right)
where
\sigma
is the spectral gap, defined as the minimum absolute value of the real parts of the eigenvalues of
A^{0}_{\infty}(z)
for
z\in K
.

Proof.

First, it is easy to see that

\displaystyle\tilde{E}^{\varepsilon}(z,\gamma)

\displaystyle=\begin{aligned} \det\Big(&\Psi^{\varepsilon}(0,z)\Psi^{\varepsilon}(X^{\varepsilon}_{\ell},z)^{-1}\Psi^{\varepsilon}(X^{\varepsilon}_{\ell},z)\Psi^{\varepsilon}(2X^{\varepsilon}_{\ell},z)^{-1}-\Psi^{\varepsilon}(0,z)\Psi^{\varepsilon}(X^{\varepsilon}_{\ell},z)^{-1}\Psi^{\varepsilon}(X^{\varepsilon}_{\ell},z)\Psi^{\varepsilon}(0,z)^{-1}\\
&+\Psi^{\varepsilon}(0,z)\Psi^{\varepsilon}(X^{\varepsilon}_{r},z)^{-1}\Psi^{\varepsilon}(X^{\varepsilon}_{r},z)\Psi^{\varepsilon}(0,z)^{-1}-\gamma\Psi^{\varepsilon}(0,z)\Psi^{\varepsilon}(X^{\varepsilon}_{r},z)^{-1}\Psi^{\varepsilon}(X^{\varepsilon}_{r},z)\Psi^{\varepsilon}(2X^{\varepsilon}_{r},z)^{-1}\Big)\end{aligned}

\displaystyle:=\det\Big(\tilde{F}^{\varepsilon}(z,\gamma)\Big).

But, due to Lemmas
4.2.3
and
4.2.4
, we may express
\Psi^{\varepsilon}
with the constant-coefficient problem,

\displaystyle\Psi^{\varepsilon}(0,z)\Psi^{\varepsilon}(X^{\varepsilon}_{\ell},z)^{-1}=

\displaystyle\e^{-A^{0}_{\infty}X^{\varepsilon}_{\ell}}P^{\varepsilon}_{+,\ell}(0)^{-1},

\displaystyle\Psi^{\varepsilon}(0,z)\Psi^{\varepsilon}(X^{\varepsilon}_{r},z)^{-1}=

\displaystyle\e^{-A^{0}_{\infty}X^{\varepsilon}_{r}}P^{\varepsilon}_{-,r}(0)^{-1},

\displaystyle\Psi^{\varepsilon}(X^{\varepsilon}_{\ell},z)\Psi^{\varepsilon}(2X^{\varepsilon}_{\ell},z)^{-1}=

\displaystyle P^{\varepsilon}_{-,\ell}(0)\e^{-A^{0}_{\infty}X^{\varepsilon}_{\ell}},

\displaystyle\Psi^{\varepsilon}(X^{\varepsilon}_{r},z)\Psi^{\varepsilon}(2X^{\varepsilon}_{r},z)^{-1}=

\displaystyle P^{\varepsilon}_{+,r}(0)\e^{-A^{0}_{\infty}X^{\varepsilon}_{r}}.

Thus

\tilde{F}^{\varepsilon}(z,\gamma)=\begin{aligned} &\e^{-A^{0}_{\infty}X^{\varepsilon}_{\ell}}P^{\varepsilon}_{+,\ell}(0)^{-1}\left(P^{\varepsilon}_{-,\ell}(0)\e^{-A^{0}_{\infty}X^{\varepsilon}_{\ell}}-P^{\varepsilon}_{+,\ell}(0)\e^{A^{0}_{\infty}X^{\varepsilon}_{\ell}}\right)\\
&+\e^{-A^{0}_{\infty}X^{\varepsilon}_{r}}P^{\varepsilon}_{-,r}(0)^{-1}\left(P^{\varepsilon}_{-,r}(0)\e^{A^{0}_{\infty}X^{\varepsilon}_{r}}-\gamma P^{\varepsilon}_{+,r}(0)\e^{-A^{0}_{\infty}X^{\varepsilon}_{r}}\right).\end{aligned}

Using the spectral expansion formula

\e^{A^{0}_{\infty}x}=\e^{A^{0}_{\infty}\Pi_{u}x}R^{-}_{\infty}L^{-}_{\infty}+\e^{A^{0}_{\infty}\Pi_{s}x}R^{+}_{\infty}L^{+}_{\infty},

(23)

and the fact that

\left\|\e^{A^{0}_{\infty}\Pi_{s}x}\right\|\leq C\e^{-\tilde{\nu}x}\penalty\ \text{for}\penalty\ x>0\quad\text{and}\quad\left\|\e^{A^{0}_{\infty}\Pi_{u}x}\right\|\leq C\e^{\tilde{\nu}x}\penalty\ \text{for}\penalty\ x<0,

we obtain that

P^{\varepsilon}_{-,\ell}(0)\e^{-A^{0}_{\infty}X^{\varepsilon}_{\ell}}-P^{\varepsilon}_{+,\ell}(0)\e^{A^{0}_{\infty}X^{\varepsilon}_{\ell}}=P^{\varepsilon}_{-,\ell}(0)\e^{-A^{0}_{\infty}\Pi_{u}X^{\varepsilon}_{\ell}}R^{-}_{\infty}L^{-}_{\infty}-P^{\varepsilon}_{+,\ell}(0)\e^{A^{0}_{\infty}\Pi_{s}X^{\varepsilon}_{\ell}}R^{+}_{\infty}L^{+}_{\infty}+\mathcal{O}(\e^{\tilde{\nu}X^{\varepsilon}_{\ell}})\\
=\Big(P^{\varepsilon}_{-,\ell}(0)R^{-}_{\infty},P^{\varepsilon}_{+,\ell}(0)R^{+}_{\infty}\Big)\Bigg[\begin{pmatrix}L^{-}_{\infty}\e^{-A^{0}_{\infty}\Pi_{u}X^{\varepsilon}_{\ell}}R^{-}_{\infty}&0\\
0&-L^{+}_{\infty}\e^{A^{0}_{\infty}\Pi_{s}X^{\varepsilon}_{\ell}}R^{+}_{\infty}\end{pmatrix}+\mathcal{O}(\e^{\tilde{\nu}X^{\varepsilon}_{\ell}})\Bigg]\begin{pmatrix}L^{-}_{\infty}\\
L^{+}_{\infty}\end{pmatrix},

and similarly

P^{\varepsilon}_{-,r}(0)\e^{A^{0}_{\infty}X^{\varepsilon}_{r}}-\gamma P^{\varepsilon}_{+,r}(0)\e^{-A^{0}_{\infty}X^{\varepsilon}_{r}}\\
=\Big(P^{\varepsilon}_{-,r}(0)R^{-}_{\infty},P^{\varepsilon}_{+,r}(0)R^{+}_{\infty}\Big)\Bigg[\begin{pmatrix}L^{-}_{\infty}\e^{A^{0}_{\infty}\Pi_{u}X^{\varepsilon}_{r}}R^{-}_{\infty}&0\\
0&-\gamma L^{+}_{\infty}\e^{-A^{0}_{\infty}\Pi_{s}X^{\varepsilon}_{r}}R^{+}_{\infty}\end{pmatrix}+\mathcal{O}(\e^{-\tilde{\nu}X^{\varepsilon}_{r}})\Bigg]\begin{pmatrix}L^{-}_{\infty}\\
L^{+}_{\infty}\end{pmatrix}.

Using that
-X^{\varepsilon}_{\ell}+X^{\varepsilon}_{r}=\frac{X^{\varepsilon}}{2}
and Abel’s formula, we get

(-1)^{-n_{s}}\e^{\tr A_{\infty}^{0}\Pi_{s}2X^{\varepsilon}_{r}}\e^{\tr A_{\infty}^{0}\Pi_{u}2X^{\varepsilon}_{\ell}}=\e^{\tr A_{\infty}^{0}\Pi_{s}(2X^{\varepsilon}_{r}+X^{\varepsilon}_{\ell})}\e^{\tr A_{\infty}^{0}\Pi_{u}X^{\varepsilon}_{\ell}}\det\begin{pmatrix}L^{-}_{\infty}\e^{A^{0}_{\infty}\Pi_{u}X^{\varepsilon}_{\ell}}R^{-}_{\infty}&0\\
0&-L^{+}_{\infty}\e^{-A^{0}_{\infty}\Pi_{s}X^{\varepsilon}_{\ell}}R^{+}_{\infty}\end{pmatrix}.

Combining all together,

\tilde{D}^{\varepsilon}(z,\gamma)=(\gamma)^{-n_{s}}\e^{\tr A_{\infty}^{0}\Pi_{s}(2X^{\varepsilon}_{r}+X^{\varepsilon}_{\ell})}\e^{\tr A_{\infty}^{0}\Pi_{u}X^{\varepsilon}_{\ell}}\\
\begin{aligned} \times\det\Bigg[&\e^{-A^{0}_{\infty}X^{\varepsilon}_{\ell}}P^{\varepsilon}_{+,\ell}(0)^{-1}\left(P^{\varepsilon}_{-,\ell}(0)R^{-}_{\infty},P^{\varepsilon}_{+,\ell}(0)R^{+}_{\infty}\right)\left(\id+\mathcal{O}(\e^{2\tilde{\nu}X^{\varepsilon}_{\ell}})\right)\\
&+\e^{-A^{0}_{\infty}X^{\varepsilon}_{r}}P^{\varepsilon}_{-,r}(0)^{-1}\left(P^{\varepsilon}_{-,r}(0)R^{-}_{\infty},P^{\varepsilon}_{+,r}(0)R^{+}_{\infty}\right)\Big(\begin{pmatrix}L^{-}_{\infty}\e^{A^{0}_{\infty}\Pi_{u}(X^{\varepsilon}_{r}+X^{\varepsilon}_{\ell})}R^{-}_{\infty}&0\\
0&\gamma L^{+}_{\infty}\e^{-A^{0}_{\infty}\Pi_{s}(X^{\varepsilon}_{r}+X^{\varepsilon}_{\ell})}R^{+}_{\infty}\end{pmatrix}\\
&+\mathcal{O}\left(\e^{-\tilde{\nu}\frac{X^{\varepsilon}}{2}}\right)\Big)\Bigg]\det\begin{pmatrix}L^{-}_{\infty}\\
L^{+}_{\infty}\end{pmatrix}.\end{aligned}

Due to Lemma
4.1.1
,

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}L^{-}_{\infty}\e^{A^{0}_{\infty}\Pi_{u}(X^{\varepsilon}_{r}+X^{\varepsilon}_{\ell})}R^{-}_{\infty}&0\\
0&\gamma L^{+}_{\infty}\e^{-A^{0}_{\infty}\Pi_{s}(X^{\varepsilon}_{r}+X^{\varepsilon}_{\ell})}R^{+}_{\infty}\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}L^{-}_{\infty}\e^{A^{0}_{\infty}\Pi_{u}X^{0}}R^{-}_{\infty}&0\\
0&\gamma L^{+}_{\infty}\e^{-A^{0}_{\infty}\Pi_{s}X^{0}}R^{+}_{\infty}\end{pmatrix}+\mathcal{O}\left(\e^{-\tilde{\nu}\frac{X^{\varepsilon}}{2}}\right)

\displaystyle:=B+\mathcal{O}\left(\e^{-\tilde{\nu}\frac{X^{\varepsilon}}{2}}\right),

(24)

so we have

\tilde{D}^{\varepsilon}(z,\gamma)=(\gamma)^{-n_{s}}\e^{\tr A_{\infty}^{0}\Pi_{s}(2X^{\varepsilon}_{r}+X^{\varepsilon}_{\ell})}\e^{\tr A_{\infty}^{0}\Pi_{u}X^{\varepsilon}_{\ell}}\det\left(\id+\mathcal{O}(\e^{-\tilde{\nu}\min\left(\frac{X^{\varepsilon}}{2},\left|2X^{\varepsilon}_{\ell}\right|\right)})\right)\det\begin{pmatrix}L^{-}_{\infty}\\
L^{+}_{\infty}\end{pmatrix}\\
\times\det\Bigg[\e^{-A^{0}_{\infty}X^{\varepsilon}_{\ell}}\left(P^{\varepsilon}_{+,\ell}(0)^{-1}P^{\varepsilon}_{-,\ell}(0)R^{-}_{\infty},R^{+}_{\infty}\right)+\e^{-A^{0}_{\infty}X^{\varepsilon}_{r}}\left(R^{-}_{\infty},P^{\varepsilon}_{-,r}(0)^{-1}P^{\varepsilon}_{+,r}(0)R^{+}_{\infty}\right)B\Bigg].

By reusing the spectral expansion formula (
23
),

\displaystyle\e^{-A^{0}_{\infty}X^{\varepsilon}_{\ell}}\left(P^{\varepsilon}_{+,\ell}(0)^{-1}P^{\varepsilon}_{-,\ell}(0)R^{-}_{\infty},R^{+}_{\infty}\right)=

\displaystyle\e^{-A^{0}_{\infty}\Pi_{u}X^{\varepsilon}_{\ell}}R^{-}_{\infty}L^{-}_{\infty}\left(P^{\varepsilon}_{+,\ell}(0)^{-1}P^{\varepsilon}_{-,\ell}(0)R^{-}_{\infty},R^{+}_{\infty}\right)+\mathcal{O}\left(\e^{\tilde{\nu}X^{\varepsilon}_{\ell}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\e^{-A^{0}_{\infty}\Pi_{u}X^{\varepsilon}_{\ell}}R^{-}_{\infty}\left(L^{-}_{\infty}P^{\varepsilon}_{+,\ell}(0)^{-1}P^{\varepsilon}_{-,\ell}(0)R^{-}_{\infty},0\right)+\mathcal{O}\left(\e^{\tilde{\nu}X^{\varepsilon}_{\ell}}\right),

and similarly

\e^{-A^{0}_{\infty}X^{\varepsilon}_{r}}\left(R^{-}_{\infty},P^{\varepsilon}_{-,r}(0)^{-1}P^{\varepsilon}_{+,r}(0)R^{+}_{\infty}\right)B=\e^{-A^{0}_{\infty}\Pi_{s}X^{\varepsilon}_{r}}R^{+}_{\infty}\left(0,L^{+}_{\infty}P^{\varepsilon}_{-,r}(0)^{-1}P^{\varepsilon}_{+,r}(0)R^{+}_{\infty}\right)B+\mathcal{O}\left(\e^{-\tilde{\nu}X^{\varepsilon}_{r}}\right).

Thanks to (
24
), we obtain

\tilde{D}^{\varepsilon}(z,\gamma)=\gamma^{-n_{s}}\e^{\tr A_{\infty}^{0}\Pi_{s}(2X^{\varepsilon}_{r}+X^{\varepsilon}_{\ell})}\e^{\tr A_{\infty}^{0}\Pi_{u}X^{\varepsilon}_{\ell}}\det\left(R^{-}_{\infty},R^{+}_{\infty}\right)\\
\begin{aligned} &\times\det\Bigg[\begin{pmatrix}L^{-}_{\infty}\e^{-A^{0}_{\infty}\Pi_{u}X^{\varepsilon}_{\ell}}R^{-}_{\infty}&0\\
0&L^{+}_{\infty}\e^{-A^{0}_{\infty}\Pi_{s}X^{\varepsilon}_{r}}R^{+}_{\infty}\end{pmatrix}+\mathcal{O}\left(\e^{-\tilde{\nu}\min(X^{\varepsilon}_{r},\left|X^{\varepsilon}_{\ell}\right|)}\right)\Bigg]\\
&\times\det\begin{pmatrix}L^{-}_{\infty}P^{\varepsilon}_{+,\ell}(0)^{-1}P^{\varepsilon}_{-,\ell}(0)R^{-}_{\infty}&0\\
0&L^{+}_{\infty}P^{\varepsilon}_{-,r}(0)^{-1}P^{\varepsilon}_{+,r}(0)R^{+}_{\infty}\end{pmatrix}\\
&\times\det\begin{pmatrix}\id&0\\
0&\gamma L^{+}_{\infty}\e^{-A^{0}_{\infty}\Pi_{s}X^{0}}R^{+}_{\infty}\end{pmatrix}\times\det\left(\id+\mathcal{O}(\e^{-\tilde{\nu}\min\left(\frac{X^{\varepsilon}}{2},\left|2X^{\varepsilon}_{\ell}\right|\right)})\right)\det\begin{pmatrix}L^{-}_{\infty}\\
L^{+}_{\infty}\end{pmatrix}.\end{aligned}

So using the Abel’s formula and
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}L^{-}_{\infty}\\
L^{+}_{\infty}\end{smallmatrix}\right)=\left(R^{-}_{\infty},R^{+}_{\infty}\right)^{-1}
we get

\displaystyle\tilde{D}^{\varepsilon}(z,\gamma)=

\displaystyle\e^{\tr A_{\infty}^{0}\Pi_{s}(X^{\varepsilon}_{r}+X^{\varepsilon}_{\ell}-X^{0})}\det\Big(L^{-}_{\infty}P^{\varepsilon}_{+,\ell}(0)^{-1}P^{\varepsilon}_{-,\ell}(0)R^{-}_{\infty}\Big)\det\Big(L^{+}_{\infty}P^{\varepsilon}_{-,r}(0)^{-1}P^{\varepsilon}_{+,r}(0)R^{+}_{\infty}\Big)

\displaystyle\times\det\left(\id+\mathcal{O}\left(\e^{-\tilde{\nu}\min\left(\frac{X^{\varepsilon}}{2},\left|2X^{\varepsilon}_{\ell}\right|\right)}\right)\right)^{2}.

With (
18
), (
17
), Definition
4.4.1
and (
9
), we deduce

\tilde{D}^{\varepsilon}(z,\gamma)=\tilde{D}^{0}(z,\gamma)+\mathcal{O}\left(\e^{-\tilde{\nu}\min\left(\frac{X^{\varepsilon}}{2},\left|2X^{\varepsilon}_{\ell}\right|\right)}\right).

By using that
X^{\varepsilon}_{\ell}=-\frac{1}{2}a_{s}\sqrt{2\kappa(v_{s})}\ln(\varepsilon)+\mathcal{O}(1)
and
X^{\varepsilon}=-2a_{s}\sqrt{2\kappa(v_{s})}\ln\varepsilon+\mathcal{O}(1)
we arrive at the claimed expansion

\tilde{D}^{\varepsilon}(z,\gamma)=\tilde{D}^{0}(z,\gamma)+\mathcal{O}\left(\e^{-\tilde{\nu}\frac{X^{\varepsilon}}{2}}\right).

∎

Corollary 4.4.1
.

On each compact
K\subset\mathbb{C}_{+}
such that
\tilde{D}^{0}
does not vanish on
\partial K
, the spectrum of
\Al^{\varepsilon}
for
X^{\varepsilon}
sufficiently large consists of loops of spectra
z^{\varepsilon}_{\ast,k}(\gamma),\penalty\ k=1,\dots,m_{r}
, within
\mathcal{O}\left(\e^{-\tilde{\nu}\frac{X^{\varepsilon}}{2m_{\ast}}}\right)
of an eigenvalue
z_{\ast}\in K
of
\Al^{0}_{r}
or
\Al^{0}_{\ell}
. Here
m_{\ast}
is the sum of the multiplicities of
z_{\ast}
as an eigenvalue of
\Al^{0}_{r}
and
\Al^{0}_{\ell}
and
\tilde{\nu}
is as in Proposition
4.4.2
.

Proof.

It follows from Proposition
4.4.1
and the Rouché theorem, like as for Corollary 5.1 in
[
YZ19
]
.
∎

With this result in hand we derive the second half of Theorem
2.3.3
.

Corollary 4.4.2
.

With Assumption
1
and
2
, if one of the solitons is spectrally unstable, then two-pulse periodic waves of sufficiently large period are spectrally unstable.

Proof.

If one of the solitons is spectrally unstable, we know that
\Al^{0}_{r}
or
\Al^{0}_{\ell}
admits an eigenvalue
z_{\ast}\in\mathbb{C}_{+}
. So with Corollary
4.4.1
, for
\varepsilon
small enough,
\Al^{\varepsilon}
admits a loop of spectrum as close as we want to
z_{\ast}
. This tells us exactly that the wave is spectrally unstable.
∎

Remark 4.4.1
.

With Theorem
2.3.2
and this corollary, we conclude in the case left open in Section
3
. If
\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}_{c}^{2}\mathcal{M}_{\ell}+\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}_{c}^{2}\mathcal{M}_{r}<0
, then two-pulse periodic waves of sufficiently large period are spectrally unstable.

Remark 4.4.2
.

Though it is not useful in the stability analysis, the reader may wonder how the essential spectrum parts of the spectrum of the two solitons impacts the spectrum of the two-solitons. In the single pulse case this is analyzed in
[
YZ19
]
and the arguments may also be extended to the two-pulses case. Even we do not provide a proof of this claims let us describe the outcome. As for the spectrum under localized perturbations the curves of essential spectrum of the two solitons also perturb continuously so as to provide curves for the two-pulse periodic waves, even though the local parametrization by the Floquet multiplier
\gamma
becomes singular. If one restricts to co-periodic perturbations, that is if one sets
\gamma\equiv 1
, then one obtains a discrete sampling of the perturbed continuous curves.

Appendix A
Asymptotic behavior of integrals

A.1
The general integrals

We enforce Assumption
2
. The goal is to compute the asymptotic expansion of

I(\phi)=\int_{v_{\ell}}^{v_{r}}\frac{\phi(v)}{\sqrt{\mu-\mathcal{W}(v)}}\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}v.

when
\varepsilon=\sqrt{\mu-\mu_{s}}\rightarrow 0
and
\phi
is a smooth function.

One may check that for
\mu>\mu_{s}
,
\mathcal{L}(v)=\mu-\mathcal{W}(v)
admits two simple roots, which are
v_{\ell}
and
v_{r}
. But, at the limits, when
\mu=\mu_{s}
,
\mathcal{L}(v)=\mu_{s}-\mathcal{W}(v)
admits two simple roots
v_{\ell}^{s}
and
v_{r}^{s}
, and a double root
v_{s}
. So the difficulty of this calculation is around the double root that appears at the limit. To make the computation easier we split the integral into different parts, where each root appears separately. To do so, using Assumption
2
,
we first introduce
\eta>0
such that for all
(\mu,\bm{\lambda},c)\in\Omega
, in a neighborhood of
(\mu_{s},\bm{\lambda},c)
, we have

\forall v\in\mathopen{[}v_{s}(\bm{\lambda},c)-\eta,v_{s}(\bm{\lambda},c)+\eta\mathclose{]},\quad\partial_{v}^{2}\mathcal{W}(v;\bm{\lambda},c)<0,

(25)

and

\displaystyle\forall v\in\mathopen{[}v_{\ell}(\mu,\bm{\lambda},c),v_{\ell}^{s}(\bm{\lambda},c)+\eta\mathclose{]},\quad\partial_{v}\mathcal{W}(v;\bm{\lambda},c)<0,

(26)

\displaystyle\forall v\in\mathopen{[}v_{r}^{s}(\bm{\lambda},c)-\eta,v_{r}(\mu,\bm{\lambda},c)\mathclose{]},\quad\partial_{v}\mathcal{W}(v;\bm{\lambda},c)>0.

(27)

Nonsingular parts are

I_{m,r}(\phi)=\int_{v_{s}+\eta}^{v_{s}^{r}-\eta}\frac{\phi(v)}{\sqrt{\mu-\mathcal{W}(v)}}\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}v\quad\text{and}\quad I_{m,\ell}(\phi)=\int_{v_{\ell}^{s}+\eta}^{v_{s}-\eta}\frac{\phi(v)}{\sqrt{\mu-\mathcal{W}(v)}}\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}v.

Asymptotic expansions for these integrals are calculated in Proposition
A.1.1
in the next subsection.

Parts with a simple root are

I_{r}(\phi):=\int_{v_{r}^{s}-\eta}^{v_{r}}\frac{\phi(v)}{\sqrt{\mu-\mathcal{W}(v)}}\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}v\quad\text{and}\quad I_{\ell}(\phi):=\int_{v_{\ell}}^{v_{\ell}^{s}+\eta}\frac{\phi(v)}{\sqrt{\mu-\mathcal{W}(v)}}\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}v.

Their asymptotic expansion is provided in Propositions
A.1.2
and
A.1.3
.

The hardest part,

I_{\eta}(\phi)=\int_{v_{s}-\eta}^{v_{s}+\eta}\frac{\phi(v)}{\sqrt{\mu-\mathcal{W}(v)}}\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}v,

whose asymptotic expansion is given in Subsection
A.1.3
.

Gathering all the results, we obtain the following theorem, which gives the asymptotic behavior of the integral
I(\phi)
.

Theorem A.1.1
.

With Notation
1
,
2
and Assumption
2
, for
\varepsilon=\sqrt{\mu-\mu_{s}}\rightarrow 0
, we have the following asymptotic expansion

I(\phi)=A_{0}(\phi)\ln(\varepsilon)+B_{0}(\phi)+B_{1}(\phi)\varepsilon+A_{2}(\phi)\varepsilon^{2}\ln(\varepsilon)+B_{2}(\phi)\varepsilon^{2}+\mathcal{O}\left(\varepsilon^{3}\ln(\varepsilon)\right),

with

i)

A_{0}(\phi)=-\frac{2\phi_{s}}{\sqrt{-R_{s}}}
,

ii)

B_{0}(\phi)=\begin{aligned} &\int_{v_{\ell}^{s}}^{v_{s}-\eta}\frac{\phi(v)}{\left|v-v_{s}\right|\sqrt{\left|R(v)\right|}}\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}v+\int_{v_{s}+\eta}^{v_{r}^{s}}\frac{\phi(v)}{\left|v-v_{s}\right|\sqrt{\left|R(v)\right|}}\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}v+2\frac{\phi_{s}}{\sqrt{-R_{s}}}\ln(2\eta\sqrt{-R_{s}})\\

iii)

B_{1}(\phi)=-2\frac{\phi_{s}}{(-R_{s})}
,

iv)

A_{2}(\phi)=3\frac{\phi_{v,s}R_{v,s}+\phi_{s}R_{vv,s}}{4(-R_{s})^{5/2}}+\frac{15\phi_{s}R_{v,s}^{2}}{8(-R_{s})^{7/2}}

v)

B_{2}(\phi)=\begin{aligned} &-\int_{v_{s}+\eta}^{v_{s}^{r}-\eta}\frac{1}{2}\frac{\phi(v)}{(\mu_{s}-\mathcal{W}(v))^{3/2}}\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}v-\int_{v_{\ell}^{s}+\eta}^{v_{s}-\eta}\frac{1}{2}\frac{\phi(v)}{(\mu_{s}-\mathcal{W}(v))^{3/2}}\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}v\\

A.1.1
Rootless parts

Proposition A.1.1
.

For
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
we have the following asymptotic expansion

I_{m,r}(\phi)=\int_{v_{s}+\eta}^{v_{s}^{r}-\eta}\frac{\phi(v)}{\sqrt{\mu_{s}-\mathcal{W}(v)}}\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}v-\varepsilon^{2}\int_{v_{s}+\eta}^{v_{s}^{r}-\eta}\frac{1}{2}\frac{\phi(v)}{(\mu_{s}-\mathcal{W}(v))^{3/2}}\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}v+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{4}),

I_{m,\ell}(\phi)=\int_{v_{\ell}^{s}+\eta}^{v_{s}-\eta}\frac{\phi(v)}{\sqrt{\mu_{s}-\mathcal{W}(v)}}\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}v-\varepsilon^{2}\int_{v_{\ell}^{s}+\eta}^{v_{s}-\eta}\frac{1}{2}\frac{\phi(v)}{(\mu_{s}-\mathcal{W}(v))^{3/2}}\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}v+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{4}).

Proof.

This follows readily by integration from

\frac{1}{\sqrt{\varepsilon^{2}+\mu_{s}-\mathcal{W}(v)}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu_{s}-\mathcal{W}(v)}}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{(\mu_{s}-\mathcal{W}(v))^{3/2}}+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{4}).

∎

A.1.2
Single-root parts

In the same spirit of
[

]
, using Taylor’s formula we write

\mu-\mathcal{W}(v)=-(v-v_{r})(v-v_{\ell})\tilde{R}(v,v_{r},v_{\ell}),

\tilde{R}(v,w,z):=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}t\partial_{v}^{2}\mathcal{W}\left(w+t(w-z)+tu(v-z)\right)\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}u\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}t

First, due to (

Furthermore, with a change of variable, we have

\displaystyle I_{r}(\phi)=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\phi(\mathcal{V}_{r})}{\sqrt{(1-\sigma)(\delta_{r}+\sigma)\tilde{R}(\mathcal{V}_{r},v_{r},v_{\ell})}}\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}\sigma,

We begin with the right-hand integral,

Proposition A.1.2
.

The following asymptotic expansion holds

\displaystyle I_{r}(\phi)=

\displaystyle\int_{v_{r}^{s}-\eta}^{v_{r}^{s}}\frac{\tilde{f}(v,v_{r}^{s},v_{\ell}^{s})}{\sqrt{-(v-v_{r}^{s})(v-v_{\ell}^{s})}}\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}v+\varepsilon^{2}\int_{v_{r}^{s}-\eta}^{v_{r}^{s}}\frac{\frac{v-v_{r}^{s}+\eta}{\eta}p_{r}^{s}\tilde{f}_{v}(v,v_{r}^{s},v_{\ell}^{s})+\left(p_{r}^{s}+p_{\ell}^{s}\right)\tilde{f}_{z}(v,v_{r}^{s},v_{\ell}^{s})}{\sqrt{-(v-v_{r}^{s})(v-v_{\ell}^{s})}}\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}v

\displaystyle+\varepsilon^{2}\int_{v_{r}^{s}-\eta}^{v_{r}^{s}}\frac{\tilde{f}(v,v_{r}^{s},v_{\ell}^{s})}{2}\frac{p_{\ell}^{s}+\delta^{s}p_{r}^{s}}{\sqrt{(v_{r}^{s}-v)}(v-v_{\ell}^{s})^{3/2}}\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}v+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{4}),

\tilde{f}(v,w,z)=\frac{\phi(v)}{\sqrt{\tilde{R}(v,w,z)}}

Proof.

We define

\tilde{f}(v,w,z)=\frac{\phi(v)}{\sqrt{\tilde{R}(v,w,z)}},

and note that since
\tilde{R}
is a symmetric function of
(w,z)
,
\tilde{f}
is also symmetric in
(w,z)
. Then we introduce
\mathcal{V}_{r}^{s}=v_{r}^{s}-\eta+\eta\sigma
,
\delta^{s}=\frac{v_{r}^{s}-\eta-v_{\ell}^{s}}{\eta}
and for every function

depending on

,
g^{s,r}:=g(\mathcal{V}_{r}^{s},v_{r}^{s},v_{\ell}^{s})
. We start by developing
\tilde{f}
in power of
\varepsilon
. For this, we use the asymptotic expansion of
v_{r}
and
v_{\ell}
computed in Proposition
3.2.1
.

\displaystyle\tilde{f}(\mathcal{V}_{r},v_{r},v_{\ell})=

\displaystyle\tilde{f}^{s,r}+(\mathcal{V}_{r}-\mathcal{V}_{r}^{s})\tilde{f}_{v}^{s,r}+(v_{r}-v_{r}^{s})\tilde{f}_{w}^{s,r}+(v_{\ell}-v_{\ell}^{s})\tilde{f}_{z}^{s,r}+\mathcal{O}\left((v_{\ell}-v_{\ell}^{s})^{2}+(v_{r}-v_{r}^{s})^{2}+(\mathcal{V}_{r}-\mathcal{V}_{r}^{s})^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tilde{f}^{s,r}+\sigma(v_{r}-v_{r}^{s})\tilde{f}_{v}^{s,r}+\left((v_{r}-v_{r}^{s})+(v_{\ell}-v_{\ell}^{s})\right)\tilde{f}_{z}^{s,r}+\mathcal{O}\left((v_{\ell}-v_{\ell}^{s})^{2}+(v_{r}-v_{r}^{s})^{2}+(\mathcal{V}_{r}-\mathcal{V}_{r}^{s})^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\tilde{f}^{s,r}+\varepsilon^{2}\sigma p_{r}^{s}\tilde{f}_{v}^{s,r}+\varepsilon^{2}\left(p_{r}^{s}+p_{\ell}^{s}\right)\tilde{f}_{z}^{s,r}+\mathcal{O}\left(\varepsilon^{4}\right).

We also need to calculate the asymptotic expansion of
\delta_{r}

\displaystyle\delta_{r}=

\displaystyle\frac{v_{r}^{s}-\eta-v_{\ell}}{v_{r}-v_{r}^{s}+\eta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(v_{r}^{s}-\eta-v_{\ell}^{s}-\varepsilon^{2}p_{\ell}^{s}+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{4})\right)\left(\eta+\varepsilon^{2}p_{r}^{s}+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{4})\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta^{s}-\varepsilon^{2}\frac{p_{\ell}^{s}+\delta^{s}p_{r}^{s}}{\eta}+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{4}).

With this in hands, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\tilde{f}(\mathcal{V}_{r},v_{r},v_{\ell})}{\sqrt{\delta_{r}+\sigma}}=

\displaystyle\left(\tilde{f}^{s,r}+\varepsilon^{2}\sigma p_{r}^{s}\tilde{f}_{v}^{s,r}+\varepsilon^{2}\left(p_{r}^{s}+p_{\ell}^{s}\right)\tilde{f}_{z}^{s,r}+\mathcal{O}\left(\varepsilon^{4}\right)\right)\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\delta^{s}+\sigma}}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{p_{\ell}^{s}+\delta^{s}p_{r}^{s}}{\eta(\delta^{s}+\sigma)^{3/2}}\varepsilon^{2}+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{4})\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\tilde{f}^{s,r}}{\sqrt{\delta^{s}+\sigma}}+\frac{\sigma p_{r}^{s}\tilde{f}_{v}^{s,r}+\left(p_{r}^{s}+p_{\ell}^{s}\right)\tilde{f}_{z}^{s,r}}{\sqrt{\delta^{s}+\sigma}}\varepsilon^{2}+\frac{\tilde{f}^{s,r}}{2}\frac{p_{\ell}^{s}+\delta^{s}p_{r}^{s}}{\eta(\delta^{s}+\sigma)^{3/2}}\varepsilon^{2}+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{4}).

Thus we conclude

I_{r}(\phi)=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\tilde{f}^{s,r}}{\sqrt{(1-\sigma)(\delta^{s}+\sigma)}}\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}\sigma+\varepsilon^{2}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\sigma p_{r}^{s}\tilde{f}_{v}^{s,r}+\left(p_{r}^{s}+p_{\ell}^{s}\right)\tilde{f}_{z}^{s,r}}{\sqrt{(1-\sigma)(\delta^{s}+\sigma)}}\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}\sigma+\varepsilon^{2}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\tilde{f}^{s,r}}{2}\frac{p_{\ell}^{s}+\delta^{s}p_{r}^{s}}{\eta\sqrt{1-\sigma}(\delta^{s}+\sigma)^{3/2}}\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}\sigma\\
+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{4}),

which, with the change of variable

, yields the desired result.
∎

We do the same for the left-hand integral and obtain the following result.

Proposition A.1.3
.

We have the following asymptotic expansion

\displaystyle I_{\ell}(\phi)=

\displaystyle\int_{v_{\ell}^{s}}^{v_{\ell}^{s}+\eta}\frac{\tilde{f}(v,v_{r}^{s},v_{\ell}^{s})}{\sqrt{-(v-v_{r}^{s})(v-v_{\ell}^{s})}}\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}v+\varepsilon^{2}\int_{v_{\ell}^{s}}^{v_{\ell}^{s}+\eta}\frac{\frac{v_{\ell}^{s}+\eta-v}{\eta}p_{\ell}^{s}\tilde{f}_{v}(v,v_{r}^{s},v_{\ell}^{s})+\left(p_{r}^{s}+p_{\ell}^{s}\right)\tilde{f}_{z}(v,v_{r}^{s},v_{\ell}^{s})}{\sqrt{-(v-v_{r}^{s})(v-v_{\ell}^{s})}}\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}v

\displaystyle-\varepsilon^{2}\int_{v_{\ell}^{s}}^{v_{\ell}^{s}+\eta}\frac{\tilde{f}(v,v_{r}^{s},v_{\ell}^{s})}{2}\frac{p_{r}^{s}+\delta^{s}p_{\ell}^{s}}{\sqrt{(v-v_{\ell}^{s})}(v_{r}^{s}-v)^{3/2}}\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}v+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{4}).

where
\tilde{f}(v,w,z)=\frac{\phi(v)}{\sqrt{\tilde{R}(v,w,z)}}

A.1.3
The double-root part

We are now interested in the part around the double root
v_{s}
,

\displaystyle I_{\eta}(\phi)=

\displaystyle\int_{v_{s}-\eta}^{v_{s}+\eta}\frac{\phi(v)}{\sqrt{\mu-\mathcal{W}(v)}}\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}v

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{-\eta}^{\eta}\frac{\phi(v_{s}+w)}{\sqrt{\varepsilon^{2}-w^{2}\tilde{R}(v_{s}+w,v_{s},v_{s})}}\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}w

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\frac{-\eta}{\varepsilon}}^{\frac{\eta}{\varepsilon}}\frac{\phi(v_{s}+\varepsilon\sigma)}{\sqrt{1-\sigma^{2}\tilde{R}(v_{s}+\varepsilon\sigma,v_{s},v_{s})}}\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}\sigma,

where we have performed the change of variable
w=\varepsilon\sigma
and used that

\mu-\mathcal{W}(v)=\varepsilon^{2}+\mu_{s}-\mathcal{W}(v)=\varepsilon^{2}-(v-v_{s})^{2}\tilde{R}(v,v_{s},v_{s})

From now on, for the sake of concision, we will write
R(v)=\tilde{R}(v,v_{s},v_{s})
. Thanks to (
25
), we may assume that
\eta
is such that
R(v)<0
for
v\in\mathopen{[}v_{s}-\eta,v_{s}+\eta\mathclose{]}
. To simplify computations, we split the integral into

\int_{0}^{\eta}\frac{\phi(v_{s}+w)}{\sqrt{\varepsilon^{2}-w^{2}R(v_{s}+w)}}\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}w=\int_{0}^{\frac{\eta}{\varepsilon}}\frac{\phi(v_{s}+\varepsilon\sigma)}{\sqrt{1-\sigma^{2}\tilde{R}(v_{s}+\varepsilon\sigma)}}\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}\sigma,

and

\int_{-\eta}^{0}\frac{\phi(v_{s}+w)}{\sqrt{\varepsilon^{2}-w^{2}R(v_{s}+w)}}\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}w=\int_{\frac{-\eta}{\varepsilon}}^{0}\frac{\phi(v_{s}+\varepsilon\sigma)}{\sqrt{1-\sigma^{2}\tilde{R}(v_{s}+\varepsilon\sigma)}}\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}\sigma.

The expansion of the latter integral will be deduced from the expansion of the former integral. Thus we begin with the first integral.

The expansion begins with a
\ln(\varepsilon)
term then an
\mathcal{O}(1)
term follows. The goal is to identify them. For this, using Taylor’s formula we get

\frac{\phi(v_{s}+h)}{\sqrt{1-\sigma^{2}R(v_{s}+h)}}=\frac{\phi_{s}}{\sqrt{1-\sigma^{2}R_{s}}}+h\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\phi_{v}(v_{s}+th)}{\sqrt{1-\sigma^{2}R(v_{s}+th)}}+\phi(v_{s}+th)\frac{\sigma^{2}R_{v}(v_{s}+th)}{2(1-\sigma^{2}R(v_{s}+th))^{3/2}}\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}t.

We deal with each term separately. The first term grows as
\ln(\varepsilon)
. Indeed Lemma
A.2.1
, gives

\displaystyle I_{\eta,+}^{-1}(\phi):=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\frac{\eta}{\varepsilon}}\frac{\phi_{s}}{\sqrt{1-\sigma^{2}R_{s}}}\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}\sigma

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi_{s}\int_{0}^{\frac{\eta\sqrt{-R_{s}}}{\varepsilon}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+x^{2}}}\frac{\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}x}{\sqrt{-R_{s}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\phi_{s}}{\sqrt{-R_{s}}}\ln(\varepsilon)+\frac{\phi_{s}}{\sqrt{-R_{s}}}\ln(2\eta\sqrt{-R_{s}})+\frac{\phi_{s}}{\sqrt{-R_{s}}}\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{-4\eta^{2}R_{s}}+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{3}).

The other term is bounded since

\displaystyle I_{\eta,+}^{0}(\phi):=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\frac{\eta}{\varepsilon}}\varepsilon\sigma\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\phi_{v}(v_{s}+t\varepsilon\sigma)}{\sqrt{1-\sigma^{2}R(v_{s}+t\varepsilon\sigma)}}+\phi(v_{s}+t\varepsilon\sigma)\frac{\sigma^{2}R_{v}(v_{s}+t\varepsilon\sigma)}{2(1-\sigma^{2}R(v_{s}+t\varepsilon\sigma))^{3/2}}\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}t\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}\sigma

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\eta}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\phi_{v}(v_{s}+tw)w}{\sqrt{\varepsilon^{2}-w^{2}R(v_{s}+tw)}}+\phi(v_{s}+tw)\frac{w^{3}R_{v}(v_{s}+tw)}{2(\varepsilon^{2}-w^{2}R(v_{s}+tw))^{3/2}}\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}t\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}w.

and we can simply use the dominated convergence theorem to obtain

I_{\eta,+}^{0}(\phi)\underset{\varepsilon\to 0}{\longrightarrow}I_{\eta,+}^{0,\text{lim}}(\phi):=\int_{0}^{\eta}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\phi_{v}(v_{s}+tw)}{\sqrt{-R(v_{s}+tw)}}+\phi(v_{s}+tw)\frac{R_{v}(v_{s}+tw)}{2(-R(v_{s}+tw))^{3/2}}\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}t\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}w.

Thereby, we only have to find the asymptotic expansion of
I_{\eta,+}^{0}(\phi)-I_{\eta,+}^{0,\text{lim}}(\phi)
. This is done in the next proposition.

Proposition A.1.4
.

We have the following asymptotic expansion

\int_{0}^{\eta}\frac{\phi(v_{s}+w)}{\sqrt{\varepsilon^{2}-w^{2}R(v_{s}+w)}}\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}w=A_{0}^{\eta,+}(\phi)\ln(\varepsilon)+B_{0}^{\eta,+}(\phi)+B_{1}^{\eta,+}(\phi)\varepsilon+A_{2}^{\eta,+}(\phi)\varepsilon^{2}\ln(\varepsilon)+B_{2}^{\eta,+}(\phi)\varepsilon^{2}+\mathcal{O}\left(\varepsilon^{3}\ln(\varepsilon)\right),

where each coefficient is given by

i)

A_{0}^{\eta,+}(\phi)=-\frac{\phi_{s}}{\sqrt{-R_{s}}},

ii)

B_{0}^{\eta,+}(\phi)=\frac{\phi_{s}}{\sqrt{-R_{s}}}\ln\left(2\eta\sqrt{-R_{s}}\right)+\int_{0}^{\eta}\frac{1}{w}\left(\frac{\phi(v_{s}+w)}{\sqrt{-R(v_{s}+w)}}-\frac{\phi_{s}}{\sqrt{R_{s}}}\right)\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}w,

iii)

B_{1}^{\eta,+}(\phi)=-\frac{\phi_{s}}{-R_{s}}-\frac{\phi_{s}R_{v,s}}{(-R_{s})^{2}},

iv)

A_{2}^{\eta,+}(\phi)=\frac{1}{4(-R_{s})^{3/2}}\phi_{v,s}+\frac{3}{8(-R_{s})^{5/2}}\phi_{s}R_{v,s}+3\frac{\phi_{v,s}R_{v,s}+\phi_{s}R_{vv,s}}{8(-R_{s})^{5/2}}+\frac{15\phi_{s}R_{v,s}^{2}}{16(-R_{s})^{7/2}},

v)

\begin{aligned} &B_{2}^{\eta,+}(\phi)=\frac{\phi_{s}}{4\eta^{2}(-R_{s})^{3/2}}+\frac{\phi_{s}}{2\eta(-R_{s})^{3/2}}+\frac{\phi_{v,s}}{2(-R_{s})^{3/2}}\left(\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{2}\ln(2\eta\sqrt{-R_{s}})\right)+\frac{\phi_{s}R_{v,s}}{4(-R_{s})^{5/2}}\left(\frac{5}{4}-\frac{3}{2}\ln\left(2\eta\sqrt{-R_{s}}\right)\right)\\
&+\frac{3}{4}\frac{\phi_{s}R_{v,s}}{\eta(-R_{s})^{5/2}}+\frac{3\phi_{s}R_{v,s}^{2}}{8(-R_{s})^{7/2}}\left(\frac{31}{12}-\frac{5}{2}\ln\left(2\eta\sqrt{-R_{s}}\right)\right)+\frac{\phi_{v,s}R_{v,s}+\phi_{s}R_{vv,s}}{4(-R_{s})^{5/2}}\left(\frac{5}{4}-\frac{3}{2}\ln\left(2\eta\sqrt{-R_{s}}\right)\right)\\
&+\int_{0}^{\eta}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}t^{2}(1-u)\Bigg[-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\phi_{vv}}{(-R)^{3/2}}-\frac{3}{2}\frac{\phi_{v}R_{v}}{(-R)^{5/2}}-\frac{3}{4}\frac{\phi R_{vv}}{(-R)^{5/2}}-\frac{15}{8}\frac{\phi R_{v}^{2}}{(-R)^{7/2}}\Bigg](v_{s}+utw)\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}u\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}t\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}w\end{aligned}

+\int_{0}^{\eta}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}t^{2}(1-u)\Bigg[-3\frac{\phi_{vv}R_{v}+2\phi_{v}R_{vv}+\phi R_{vvv}}{4(-R)^{5/2}}-\frac{15}{4}\frac{\phi_{v}R_{v}^{2}+\frac{3}{2}\phi R_{v}R_{vv}}{(-R)^{7/2}}-\frac{105}{16}\frac{\phi R_{v}^{3}}{(-R)^{9/2}}\Bigg](v_{s}+utw)\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}u\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}t\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}w.

Proof.

As discussed above there only remains to expand

\displaystyle I_{\eta,+}^{0}(\phi)-I_{\eta,+}^{0,\text{lim}}(\phi)=

&\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}u\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}t\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}w.\end{aligned}

Proposition A.1.5
.

We have the following asymptotic expansion

\int_{-\eta}^{0}\frac{\phi(v_{s}+w)}{\sqrt{\varepsilon^{2}-w^{2}R(v_{s}+w)}}\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}w=A_{0}^{\eta,-}(\phi)\ln(\varepsilon)+B_{0}^{\eta,-}(\phi)+B_{1}^{\eta,-}(\phi)\varepsilon+A_{2}^{\eta,-}(\phi)\varepsilon^{2}\ln(\varepsilon)+B_{2}^{\eta,-}(\phi)\varepsilon^{2}+\mathcal{O}\left(\varepsilon^{3}\ln(\varepsilon)\right),

where each coefficient is given by

i)

A_{0}^{\eta,-}(\phi)=-\frac{\phi_{s}}{\sqrt{-R_{s}}},

ii)

B_{0}^{\eta,-}(\phi)=\frac{\phi_{s}}{\sqrt{-R_{s}}}\ln\left(2\eta\sqrt{-R_{s}}\right)+\int_{-\eta}^{0}-\frac{1}{w}\left(\frac{\phi(v_{s}+w)}{\sqrt{-R(v_{s}+w)}}-\frac{\phi_{s}}{\sqrt{R_{s}}}\right)\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}w,

iii)

B_{1}^{\eta,-}(\phi)=-\frac{\phi_{s}}{-R_{s}}+\frac{\phi_{s}R_{v,s}}{(-R_{s})^{2}},

iv)

A_{2}^{\eta,-}(\phi)=-\frac{\phi_{v,s}}{4(-R_{s})^{3/2}}-\frac{3\phi_{s}R_{v,s}}{8(-R_{s})^{5/2}}+3\frac{\phi_{v,s}R_{v,s}+\phi_{s}R_{vv,s}}{8(-R_{s})^{5/2}}+\frac{15\phi_{s}R_{v,s}^{2}}{16(-R_{s})^{7/2}},

v)

\begin{aligned} &B_{2}^{\eta,-}(\phi)=\frac{\phi_{s}}{4\eta^{2}(-R_{s})^{3/2}}+\frac{\phi_{s}}{2\eta(-R_{s})^{3/2}}-\frac{\phi_{v,s}}{2(-R_{s})^{3/2}}\left(\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{2}\ln(2\eta\sqrt{-R_{s}})\right)-\frac{\phi_{s}R_{v,s}}{4(-R_{s})^{5/2}}\left(\frac{5}{4}-\frac{3}{2}\ln\left(2\eta\sqrt{-R_{s}}\right)\right)\\
&-\frac{3}{4}\frac{\phi_{s}R_{v,s}}{\eta(-R_{s})^{5/2}}+\frac{3\phi_{s}R_{v,s}^{2}}{8(-R_{s})^{7/2}}\left(\frac{31}{12}-\frac{5}{2}\ln\left(2\eta\sqrt{-R_{s}}\right)\right)+\frac{\phi_{v,s}R_{v,s}+\phi_{s}R_{vv,s}}{4(-R_{s})^{5/2}}\left(\frac{5}{4}-\frac{3}{2}\ln\left(2\eta\sqrt{-R_{s}}\right)\right)\\
&+\int_{-\eta}^{0}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}t^{2}(1-u)\Bigg[-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\phi_{vv}}{(-R)^{3/2}}-\frac{3}{2}\frac{\phi_{v}R_{v}}{(-R)^{5/2}}-\frac{3}{4}\frac{\phi R_{vv}}{(-R)^{5/2}}-\frac{15}{8}\frac{\phi R_{v}^{2}}{(-R)^{7/2}}\Bigg](v_{s}+utw)\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}u\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}t\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}w\end{aligned}

+\int_{-\eta}^{0}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}t^{2}(1-u)\Bigg[3\frac{\phi_{vv}R_{v}+2\phi_{v}R_{vv}+\phi R_{vvv}}{4(-R)^{5/2}}+\frac{15}{4}\frac{\phi_{v}R_{v}^{2}+\frac{3}{2}\phi R_{v}R_{vv}}{(-R)^{7/2}}+\frac{105}{16}\frac{\phi R_{v}^{3}}{(-R)^{9/2}}\Bigg](v_{s}+utw)\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}u\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}t\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}w.

Proof.

We use the expansion already computed in the case of the positive integral. To do so, we introduce
\tilde{w}=-w
, and note that

\int_{-\eta}^{0}\frac{\phi(v_{s}+w)}{\sqrt{\varepsilon^{2}-w^{2}R(v_{s}+w)}}\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}w=\int_{0}^{\eta}\frac{\phi(v_{s}-\tilde{w})}{\sqrt{\varepsilon^{2}-\tilde{w}^{2}R(v_{s}-\tilde{w})}}\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}\tilde{w}

We therefore define
\tilde{R}(v_{s}+h):=R(v_{s}-h)
, that is
\tilde{R}(v)=R(2v_{s}-v)
and likewise
\tilde{\phi}(v_{s}+h)=\phi(v_{s}-h)
. Thus, we obtain that

\int_{-\eta}^{0}\frac{\phi(v_{s}+w)}{\sqrt{\varepsilon^{2}-w^{2}R(v_{s}+w)}}\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}w=\int_{0}^{\eta}\frac{\tilde{\phi}(v_{s}+\tilde{w})}{\sqrt{\varepsilon^{2}-\tilde{w}^{2}\tilde{R}(v_{s}+\tilde{w})}}\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}\tilde{w}

We can now use Proposition
A.1.4
, and undo the change of variable
\tilde{w}=-w
to conclude.
∎

A.2
Asymptotic expansions of different integrals

To finish, we gather here some asymptotic expansions of integrals that we have used in the previous section.

Lemma A.2.1
.

When
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
,

\int_{0}^{\frac{\eta\sqrt{-R_{s}}}{\varepsilon}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+x^{2}}}\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}x=-\ln(\varepsilon)+\ln(2\eta\sqrt{-R_{s}})+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{4\eta^{2}(-R_{s})}+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{4}).

Proof.

We compute explicitly the integral and then expand it in powers of
\varepsilon
,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\frac{\eta\sqrt{-R_{s}}}{\varepsilon}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+x^{2}}}\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}x=

\displaystyle\left[\frac{1}{2}\ln\left(\frac{\sqrt{1+x^{2}}+x}{\sqrt{1+x^{2}}-x}\right)\right]_{0}^{\frac{\eta\sqrt{-R_{s}}}{\varepsilon}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\ln\left(\frac{2\eta^{2}(-R_{s})+2\eta\sqrt{-R_{s}}\sqrt{\varepsilon^{2}+\eta^{2}(-R_{s})}+\varepsilon^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\ln(\varepsilon)+\ln\left(2\eta\sqrt{-R_{s}}\right)+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{4\eta^{2}(-R_{s})}+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{4}).

∎

Lemma A.2.2
.

When
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0

i)

\int_{0}^{\frac{\eta\sqrt{-R_{s}}}{\varepsilon}}\frac{x}{\sqrt{1+x^{2}}}\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}x=\frac{\eta\sqrt{-R_{s}}}{\varepsilon}-1+\frac{1}{2}\frac{\varepsilon}{\eta\sqrt{-R_{s}}}+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{3}),

ii)

\int_{0}^{\frac{\eta\sqrt{-R_{s}}}{\varepsilon}}\frac{x^{2}}{\sqrt{1+x^{2}}}\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}x=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\eta^{2}(-R_{s})}{\varepsilon^{2}}+\frac{1}{2}\ln(\varepsilon)+\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{2}\ln(2\eta\sqrt{-R_{s}})+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{2}),

iii)

\int_{0}^{\frac{\eta\sqrt{-R_{s}}}{\varepsilon}}\frac{x^{4}}{(1+x)^{3/2}}\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}x=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\eta\sqrt{-R_{s}}}{\varepsilon}\right)^{2}+\frac{3}{2}\ln(\varepsilon)+\frac{5}{4}-\frac{3}{2}\ln\left(2\eta\sqrt{-R_{s}}\right)+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{2}).

Proof.

Again we compute explicitly the integrals and then use classical asymptotic expansions.

i)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\frac{\eta\sqrt{-R_{s}}}{\varepsilon}}\frac{x}{\sqrt{1+x^{2}}}\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}x=

\displaystyle\left[\sqrt{1+x^{2}}\right]_{0}^{\frac{\eta\sqrt{-R_{s}}}{\varepsilon}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\eta\sqrt{-R_{s}}}{\varepsilon}-1+\frac{1}{2}\frac{\varepsilon}{\eta\sqrt{-R_{s}}}+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{3}).

ii)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\frac{\eta\sqrt{-R_{s}}}{\varepsilon}}\frac{x^{2}}{\sqrt{1+x^{2}}}\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}x=

\displaystyle\left[\frac{1}{2}x\sqrt{x^{2}+1}-\frac{1}{4}\ln\left(\frac{\sqrt{1+x^{2}}+x}{\sqrt{1+x^{2}}-x}\right)\right]_{0}^{\frac{\eta\sqrt{-R_{s}}}{\varepsilon}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\eta\sqrt{-R_{s}}}{\varepsilon}\right)^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\ln(\varepsilon)+\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{2}\ln\left(2\eta\sqrt{-R_{s}}\right)+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{2}).

iii)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\frac{\eta\sqrt{-R_{s}}}{\varepsilon}}\frac{x^{4}}{(1+x)^{3/2}}\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}x=

\displaystyle\left[\frac{x^{3}+3x}{2\sqrt{x^{2}+1}}-\frac{3}{4}\ln\left(\frac{\sqrt{1+x^{2}}+x}{\sqrt{1+x^{2}}-x}\right)\right]_{0}^{\frac{\eta\sqrt{-R_{s}}}{\varepsilon}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\eta\sqrt{-R_{s}}}{\varepsilon}\right)^{2}+\frac{3}{2}\ln(\varepsilon)+\frac{5}{4}-\frac{3}{2}\ln\left(2\eta\sqrt{-R_{s}}\right)+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{2}).

∎

Lemma A.2.3
.

When
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
,

i)

\int_{0}^{\frac{\eta\sqrt{-R_{s}}}{\varepsilon}}\frac{x^{3}}{(1+x^{2})^{3/2}}\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}x=\frac{\eta\sqrt{-R_{s}}}{\varepsilon}-2+\frac{3}{2}\frac{\varepsilon}{\eta\sqrt{-R_{s}}}+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{3}),

ii)

\int_{0}^{\frac{\eta\sqrt{-R_{s}}}{\varepsilon}}\frac{x^{6}}{(1+x^{2})^{5/2}}\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}x=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\eta\sqrt{-R_{s}}}{\varepsilon}\right)^{2}+\frac{5}{2}\ln(\varepsilon)+\left(\frac{31}{12}-\frac{5}{2}\ln\left(2\eta\sqrt{-R_{s}}\right)\right)+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{2}).

Proof.

This follows from

i)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\frac{\eta\sqrt{-R_{s}}}{\varepsilon}}\frac{x^{3}}{(1+x^{2})^{3/2}}\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}x=

\displaystyle\left[\frac{x^{2}+2}{\sqrt{x^{2}+1}}\right]_{0}^{\frac{\eta\sqrt{-R_{s}}}{\varepsilon}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\eta\sqrt{-R_{s}}}{\varepsilon}-2+\frac{3}{2}\frac{\varepsilon}{\eta\sqrt{-R_{s}}}+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{3}).

ii)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\frac{\eta\sqrt{-R_{s}}}{\varepsilon}}\frac{x^{6}}{(1+x^{2})^{5/2}}\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}x=

\displaystyle\left[\frac{3x^{5}+20x^{3}+15x}{6(x^{2}+1)^{3/2}}-\frac{5}{4}\ln\left(\frac{\sqrt{1+x^{2}}+x}{\sqrt{1+x^{2}}-x}\right)\right]_{0}^{\frac{\eta\sqrt{-R_{s}}}{\varepsilon}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\eta\sqrt{-R_{s}}}{\varepsilon}\right)^{2}+\frac{5}{2}\ln(\varepsilon)+\left(\frac{31}{12}-\frac{5}{2}\ln\left(2\eta\sqrt{-R_{s}}\right)\right)+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{2}).

∎

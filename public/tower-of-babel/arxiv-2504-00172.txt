Evidence for evolutionary pathway-dependent black hole scaling relations
Jonathan H. Cohn, Emmanuel Durodola, Quinn O. Casey, Erini Lambrides, Ryan C. Hickox

Abstract
Recent observations have identified an abundance of high-redshift active galactic nuclei (AGN) with supermassive black holes (BHs) that are over-massive compared to the local BH mass − - total stellar mass ( M BH − M ⋆ M_{\mathrm{BH}}-M_{\star} ) relation. M BH M_{\mathrm{BH}} measurements at high- z z are critical for probing the growth histories of BHs and their host galaxies, including BH seeding and evolution of the M BH − M ⋆ M_{\mathrm{BH}}-M_{\star} relation. However, BH masses in high- z z AGN are generally estimated from single-epoch measurements, which are anchored to local relations based on reverberation mapping and carry large systematic uncertainties. Alternate M BH M_{\mathrm{BH}} detection methods such as dynamical measurements are more reliable but currently only possible in the local Universe or with strongly lensed systems. Recently, dynamical M BH M_{\mathrm{BH}} measurements were made in a z ∼ 2 z\sim 2 lensed quiescent galaxy as well as a sample of six local galaxies identified as likely relics of common quiescent red nugget galaxies at cosmic noon. We compare the z ∼ 2 z\sim 2 red nugget and relic BHs to recent results for 4 < z < 11 4<z<11 AGN, quasars, and Little Red Dots. Intriguingly, the z ∼ 2 z\sim 2 galaxy and local relic galaxies all lie on both the local M BH − M ⋆ M_{\mathrm{BH}}-M_{\star} relation for bulges and the 4 < z < 7 4<z<7 M BH − M ⋆ M_{\mathrm{BH}}-M_{\star} relation. Our results suggest the M BH − M ⋆ M_{\mathrm{BH}}-M_{\star} relation for bulges was likely in place at high- z z and indicate careful consideration of different evolutionary pathways is needed when building BH scaling relations. While improvements to M BH M_{\mathrm{BH}} estimates in AGN will increase our confidence in high- z z BH masses, detecting BHs in relic galaxies and lensed galaxies presents a complementary probe of the high- z z relations.

I
Introduction

Supermassive black holes (BHs) are important galactic building blocks, existing at the centers of essentially all massive galaxies in the local Universe (e.g.,
Saglia et al. 33
).
Dynamical measurements of BH masses (
M_{\mathrm{BH}}
) have revealed they correlate with large-scale galactic properties, extending well outside the BHs’ gravitational spheres of influence (SOI) and implying BHs and host galaxies must co-evolve (e.g.,
Kormendy & Ho 20
).
Correlations with
M_{\mathrm{BH}}
have been found with galaxies’ total mass (
M_{\star}
), bulge mass (
M_{\mathrm{bul}}
), bulge luminosity (
L_{\mathrm{bul}}
), and stellar velocity dispersion (
\sigma_{\star}
), among other properties (e.g.,
Kormendy & Ho 20
,
Reines & Volonteri 32
,
Saglia et al. 33
).
Studying BHs in the early Universe is critical for developing our understanding of co-evolution and BH seeding mechanisms (at
z\gtrsim 10
; e.g.,
Volonteri & Natarajan 40
).
However, due to sensitivity and resolution requirements, dynamical BH detections are only possible at distances
\lesssim
150 Mpc or in rare gravitationally lensed systems at high-
z
, making it difficult to explore the evolution of the scaling relations.

Fortunately, there are alternate methods of estimating BH masses, based on active galactic nuclei (AGN).
The most reliable estimates come from reverberation mapping (RM), which measures the time delay in the arrival of flux variations in the AGN broad-line region (BLR) after observing variations in the continuum (e.g.,
Shen et al. 35
).
RM measurements have revealed a correlation between the BLR size and AGN luminosity (
R_{\mathrm{BLR}}-L
), allowing for estimates of
M_{\mathrm{BH}}
from single observations of an AGN’s line luminosity and width, known as single-epoch measurements (e.g.,
Peterson et al. 29
).
These single-epoch measurements enable
M_{\mathrm{BH}}
estimates at very high redshifts, enabling the first studies of BH scaling relation evolution (e.g.,
Izumi et al. 16
,
Pensabene et al. 28
,
Larson et al. 22
,
Bogdán et al. 4
,
Maiolino et al. 25
).

Recent JWST observations have revealed a population of optically red, compact, high redshift (
5\leq z\leq 10
) galaxies.
These X-ray quiet galaxies
[
1
]
, termed “Little Red Dots” (LRDs), appear to be ubiquitous in the high redshift Universe.
Spectroscopic analysis of LRDs has revealed a subset population with broad (
\sim
4000 km s
-1
) H
\alpha
emission
[
12
]
, suggesting AGN activity.
Using single-epoch measurements, several studies
[
12
,
30
,
19
,
18
,
17
,
25
,
21
]
have estimated BH masses hosted by LRDs to range between 10
6
and 10
9
M
⊙
.
These
M_{\mathrm{BH}}
estimates are significantly overmassive compared to the local population, with BHs in LRDs 2 to 4 orders of magnitude more massive compared to local galaxies of similar stellar masses
[
27
,
8
]
.
These results have revealed a previously unknown tension between the high-
z
BH population and the local Universe, which astronomers are now working diligently to understand.

However, several issues make it difficult to draw conclusions about the evolution of the BH
-
host galaxy relations when only using single-epoch measurements.
There is an intrinsic factor of
\sim
2 systematic uncertainty in RM measurements due to the geometric virial factor
f

[
35
]
.
Furthermore, there are questions surrounding whether the
R_{\mathrm{BLR}}-L
relations observed locally change with redshift, increasing uncertainty in single-epoch measurements derived from these relations at high-
z
.
Exacerbating matters, there has been disagreement in the literature regarding whether high-
z
samples appear to host over-massive BHs due to biases in the sample selection, as brighter AGN with more massive BHs are easier to detect (e.g.,
Li et al. 23
).
Extricating accurate stellar masses from systems with AGN or quasars (QSOs) is also difficult due to AGN contamination.
This may result in biased stellar masses, especially in fainter, AGN-dominated systems
[
48
]
.
All these effects compound to cast doubt on the placement of high-
z
single-epoch BHs on the
M_{\mathrm{BH}}-M_{\star}
relation.
Complementary methods of probing BHs in the early Universe are required to clarify whether high-
z
systems truly house over-massive BHs.

Recently, a sample of local compact galaxies were identified to have not undergone any
in-situ
stellar growth or major mergers since
z\sim 2
, making them likely relics representative of common massive, quiescent red nugget galaxies at cosmic noon
[
44
]
.
Red nuggets at
z\sim 2
usually evolve to form the cores of local early-type galaxies (ETGs), growing primarily through dry mergers that increase stellar mass in the galaxy outskirts
[
38
]
.
Such local massive ETGs house the most massive BHs in the nearby Universe
[
20
]
, and studying the BHs in their progenitors could provide direct insights into coevolution.
Local relics of red nugget galaxies thus provide an ideal window into this important population of
z\sim 2
BHs.
Additionally, as these quiescent relics exist in the local Universe, their BHs can be studied with robust dynamical
M_{\mathrm{BH}}
measurements, insensitive to the uncertainties affecting
M_{\mathrm{BH}}
estimates in AGN.
Dynamical
M_{\mathrm{BH}}
measurements have been made in six red nugget relics to date, revealing a population of BHs that appears to be over-massive compared to the
M_{\mathrm{BH}}-M_{\mathrm{bul}}
and
M_{\mathrm{BH}}-L_{\mathrm{bul}}
relations
[
41
,
42
,
43
,
5
,
6
,
7
]
.
Enticingly, a new stellar-dynamical
M_{\mathrm{BH}}
measurement in a gravitationally lensed quiescent galaxy at
z=1.95
has also found that it is overmassive on
M_{\mathrm{BH}}-M_{\mathrm{bul}}

[
26
]
.
This could indicate that before
z\sim 2
, the cores of ETGs had largely finished growing, and between
0<z<2
, dry mergers in ETGs increased their stellar mass without driving additional BH growth, bringing them onto the local BH scaling relations
[
9
]
.
However, the red nugget sample has yet to be directly compared to high-
z
AGN
M_{\mathrm{BH}}
measurements or the
M_{\mathrm{BH}}-M_{\star}
relations (locally or at high redshift).

In this letter, we investigate how the red nuggets at cosmic noon may be used as a stepping stone to help clarify whether BHs at high redshift are overmassive compared to the local relations.
To do this, we compare the relic galaxies to a selection of broad-line AGN, luminous QSOs, and LRDs at
4<z<11
, as well as the local and
4<z<7

M_{\mathrm{BH}}-M_{\star}
relations from
Reines & Volonteri [32]
and
Pacucci et al. [27]
, respectively.
We detail our sample selection in §
II
, compare the BH masses in our sample to local and high-redshift scaling relations in §
III
, and discuss the implications of our results for our understanding of BH
-
host galaxy coevolution in §
IV

II
Sample selection

We compare several datasets representing
M_{\mathrm{BH}}
measurements from
z\sim 2
to the early Universe.
To ensure our analysis of high-
z
BH masses is not solely reliant on single-epoch measurements, we first select a sample of six red nugget relic galaxies with dynamical
M_{\mathrm{BH}}
measurements.

Cohn et al. [5]
,
Cohn et al. [6]
,
Cohn et al. [7]
performed molecular gas-dynamical
M_{\mathrm{BH}}
modeling with ALMA CO(2
-
1) band 6 observations at resolutions of
\sim
1
-
2
\times
the BH SOI in three of these galaxies, and
Walsh et al. [41]
,
Walsh et al. [42]
,
Walsh et al. [43]
performed stellar-dynamical
M_{\mathrm{BH}}
modeling of high resolution Gemini North/NIFS observations supplemented by large-scale Potsdam Multi-Aperture Spectrophotometer/Pmas fiber PAcK spectroscopy in the other three.
All six measurements made use of near-IR Hubble Space Telescope images to characterize the stellar contribution to the gravitational potential in the targets, allowing measurements of stellar masses based on Multi-Gaussian Expansion fits.

Although these galaxies are located in the local Universe (
z\lesssim 0.02
), they are passively evolved relics of
z\sim 2
red nugget galaxies, with evidence displayed in their morphology and stellar populations that they have not undergone any significant growth to their stars or BHs—including no intermediate or major mergers—since
z\sim 2

[
44
]
.
These systems thus likely remain representative of common quiescent red nugget galaxies at
z\sim 2
.
One advantage of dynamical
M_{\mathrm{BH}}
measurements in these inactive relic galaxies is that they avoid the systematics affecting single-epoch measurements by directly detecting the bulk motion of stars or molecular gas within the BH SOI.
Measurements in this sample also include thorough explorations of systematic uncertainties in
M_{\mathrm{BH}}
, in additional to statistical uncertainties.
Here, we present their total measurement uncertainties, with statistical and systematic sources of error summed in quadrature.
These direct detections are the most robust
M_{\mathrm{BH}}
measurements made in galaxies representative of populations outside the local Universe.

We also include the first and thus-far only stellar-dynamical measurement in a gravitationally lensed quiescent galaxy at
z=1.95

[
26
]
.
This measurement used JWST/NIRSpec IFU, NIRCam imaging, and the Hubble Space Telescope/ACS imaging to resolve stellar kinematics in the galaxy disk and reconstruct the galaxy in the source plane.
They found the galaxy is massive and compact, reflecting the properties of typical red nuggets at cosmic noon.
The exceptional lensing magnification (a factor of 29) and 91 pc resolution allowed for the robust measurement of
M_{\mathrm{BH}}
and stellar mass with Jeans anisotropic modeling
[
26
]
.

At higher redshifts, we include multiple AGN-based
M_{\mathrm{BH}}
samples, including a sample of luminous QSOs at
5.9<z<7.1

[
45
]
, broad-line selected AGN at
4<z<11

[
25
]
, and LRDs at
5<z<8

[
8
]
.
We choose these samples to represent a variety of galaxy and measurement types at high-redshift, given debate in the literature regarding the best approach for estimating high-
z
BH masses.
In
Yue et al. [45]
, the QSOs were confirmed with NIRSpec spectroscopy and
M_{\mathrm{BH}}
was estimated using H
\alpha
following
Reines et al. [31]
.

Maiolino et al. [25]
presented GN-z11 and other AGN that are not luminous QSOs, identified in the JWST Advanced Deep Extragalactic Survey with NIRSpec.
They estimated
M_{\mathrm{BH}}
from broad-line H
\alpha
or H
\beta
, following
Reines et al. [31]
.

Maiolino et al. [25]
also estimated the stellar velocity dispersion (
\sigma_{\star}
) by taking the ionized gas velocity dispersion to be a reliable proxy of
\sigma_{\star}
, with a small correction following
Bezanson et al. [3]
, enabling their AGN to be compared to the
M_{\mathrm{BH}}
-
stellar velocity dispersion relation (
M_{\mathrm{BH}}-\sigma_{\star}

Finally,
Durodola et al. [8]
examined LRDs with NIRCam and MIRI, using the spectral energy distribution (SED)-fitting code

to estimate the AGN contribution to the SED and reporting the corresponding best-fit
M_{\mathrm{BH}}

Although their method lacks spectroscopic confirmation, they found MIRI coverage was vital for deciphering between AGN and non-AGN contributions to the SEDs of LRDs.

IV
Discussion and Conclusions

There has been substantial debate regarding overmassive BHs in the early Universe.
Studying relic galaxies and gravitationally lensed objects present the only means of exploring this regime through direct
M_{\mathrm{BH}}
detection, without relying solely on AGN-derived BH masses.
We find that local massive red nugget relic galaxies, likely representative of the typical quiescent galaxy population at
z\sim 2
, are consistent with the local
Reines & Volonteri [32]

M_{\mathrm{BH}}-M_{\star}
relation for bulges.
The
z\sim 2
lensed quiescent galaxy also aligns with this relation.
Given that red nugget galaxies are likely bulges themselves, this is perhaps unsurprising and suggests that the
M_{\mathrm{BH}}-M_{\star}
relation for bulges may have been in place as early as
z\sim 2
.
Our results thus indicate that differences in the BH scaling relations are likely driven by differing evolutionary pathways.

In this case, the position of different galaxy populations in the
M_{\mathrm{BH}}-M_{\star}
parameter space would vary significantly due to the myriad evolutionary processes represented.
The processes that drive growth in the cores and bulges of galaxies differ from those that drive growth in the outskirts (e.g.,
van Dokkum et al. 38
), meaning BHs (which likely grow alongside cores and bulges) will not coevolve consistently with total
M_{\star}
(which is also affected by processes outside cores and bulges).
The recent findings that AGN host overmassive BHs at high redshift could thus be reflective of different dominant evolutionary processes in the host galaxies.
This is illustrated in the left panel of Figure
3
, which shows galaxies throughout the Universe’s history undergoing different regimes of growth and occupying different regions of
M_{\mathrm{BH}}-M_{\star}
parameter space.

In support of this picture, BHs have been found to correlate more strongly with different host properties (e.g.,
M_{\star}
,
M_{\mathrm{bul}}
, or
\sigma_{\star}
) depending on galaxy classification
[
20
,
33
,
11
]
.
Interestingly, recent simulations also suggest scatter in the
M_{\mathrm{BH}}-M_{\star}
relation may be reduced as a result of hierarchical merging of quenched systems, a process that may not regularly occur until
z<2

[
47
]
.
Of course, if variations in evolutionary history drive differences in the placement of systems on
M_{\mathrm{BH}}-M_{\star}
, the overall relation will likely evolve with redshift due to different stages of growth dominating at different times in the Universe.
It is also worth noting that the red nugget relics and
z\sim 2
quiescent galaxy remain overmassive compared to the
Kormendy & Ho [20]

z\sim 0

M_{\mathrm{BH}}-M_{\mathrm{bul}}
relation, but
Reines & Volonteri [32]
and
Kormendy & Ho [20]
make different assumptions about the stellar initial mass function, resulting in the relations’ offset.

Intriguingly, the
z\sim 2
quiescent galaxy and red nugget relic galaxies also lie on the
Pacucci et al. [27]

M_{\mathrm{BH}}-M_{\star}
relation for AGN at
4<z<7
.
We fit a line to a sample consisting of these
z\sim 2
-representative galaxies and the
4<z<7
QSOs, LRDs, and AGN, finding it agrees with the
Pacucci et al. [27]
relation within uncertainties.
This could provide evidence that this
4<z<7

M_{\mathrm{BH}}-M_{\star}
relation was in place from
2<z<7
.
If this is the case, typical evolution processes between
0<z<2
(e.g., hierarchical merging) would be required to provide more stellar growth than BH growth to produce the local AGN relation.
The agreement of the local red nugget relics and the
z\sim 2
quiescent galaxy with the
4<z<7
AGN relation could also suggest that AGN with swiftly growing BHs in the early Universe may be progenitors of
z\sim 2
red nuggets.

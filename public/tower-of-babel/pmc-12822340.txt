Prevalence and clinical characteristics of concurrent chronic hepatitis B and fatty liver disease in China: a systematic review and meta-analysis
Authors: Jiayi Huang, Sujuan Zhou, Chengnan Guo, Xin Zhang, Shuzhen Zhao, Luojia Dai, Zhenqiu Liu, Tiejun Zhang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundBoth chronic hepatitis B (CHB) and fatty liver disease (FLD) are major contributors to the burden of severe liver diseases. China bears the heaviest burden of CHB worldwide, while the prevalence of FLD has been substantially increased in the last decades. However, the prevalence and clinical impact of concurrent CHB and FLD in China remain unclear, impeding effective prevention and management of severe liver diseases.MethodsWe conducted a systematic review and meta-analysis on prevalence and clinical characteristics of concurrent CHB and FLD in China. Eligible literatures were retrieved from Medline, EmBase, Web of Science, WanFang and China National Knowledge Infrastructure (CNKI), up to 31 December 2023. The pooled prevalence was estimated using a random-effects model. The weighted mean difference was calculated to compare the clinical characteristics across different groups.ResultsA total of 114 studies with 318,770 participants were included for the analysis. The pooled prevalence of CHB with concurrent FLD in the general population was 1.48% (95% CI: 1.02%-2.14%). The pooled prevalence of FLD in CHB patients was 26.75%, while the pooled prevalence of CHB in FLD patients was 16.94%. The co-morbidity group exhibited higher levels of body mass index (BMI), triglycerides, fasting insulin and albumin than the CHB group. The total cholesterol level in the co-morbidity group was 0.48mmol/L lower than the FLD group. Additionally, it demonstrated a significantly lower hepatitis B virus DNA load and higher prevalence of diabetes, hypertension, and hyperuricemia than the CHB group.ConclusionsThe co-occurrence of CHB and FLD is an emerging public health concern in China, showing distinct clinical characteristics compared to those with CHB or FLD alone. Background

Chronic hepatitis B (CHB) remains a major public health challenge in China, with an estimated prevalence of 5.86% in 2020 [1]. Moreover, due to low awareness rates for infection and a lack of proper management, approximately 20% of CHB patients died early from liver failure or HCC, resulting in a substantial public health burden [2, 3]. Fatty liver disease (FLD), including alcoholic fatty liver disease (AFLD) and nonalcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD), has now surpassed hepatitis B as the leading cause of chronic liver disease globally [4–6]. NAFLD affects 25–30% of the global population, with the highest prevalence reported in South America and the lowest in Australia [7]. The rising global prevalence is strongly associated with urbanization, sedentary lifestyles, and the increasing epidemics of obesity and type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) [8, 9]. A recent national study has reported a prevalence of 44.39% for FLD in China [10]. The prevalence of NAFLD in China has increased from 20.09% in 2010–2013 to 29.88% in 2020, approaching levels observed in developed countries [11–13]. This rapid increase aligns with China’s growing burden of metabolic disorders, where obesity and T2DM have become major public health challenges.

Of note, accompanied by the growing prevalence of FLD, the coexistence of FLD and HBV infection has substantially increased in China [14–16]. Moreover, the interactions and clinical impacts of coexisting FLD and CHB are complex. Previous studies have indicated that patients with both CHB and FLD face an increased risk of liver disease progression to cirrhosis and HCC, while others have found a decreased risk or no significant effect [4, 17–19]. It is noteworthy that the presence of NAFLD may suppress HBV viral activity and has been associated with poorer responses to antiviral therapy in CHB patients, including lower rates of achieving serum HBV DNA undetectability and alanine aminotransferase (ALT) normalization [20, 21]. These findings highlight the challenges in treating CHB patients with concurrent FLD, suggesting the need for precision clinical strategies in managing these cases. To enhance the management of CHB, it is crucial to identify patients with comorbidities and subsequently develop tailored treatment strategies. However, the epidemiological pattern of co-occurrence of CHB and FLD remains inadequately documented, warranting comprehensive investigation.

Therefore, we aimed to (1) estimate the recent prevalence of co-occurrence of CHB and FLD in Chinese population, and (2) compare the clinical characteristics of subpopulations determined status of CHB or FLD.

Methods

Search strategy and study selection

The current study was registered in PROSPERO (CRD42024554411). We conducted a literature search in English databases, Medline, EmBase, and Web of Science, and in Chinese databases, WanFang and China National Knowledge Infrastructure (CNKI), up to 31 December 2023. Detailed search strategies are shown in Supplemental material S1. In total, 1522 publications were identified and catalogued using Endnote 21 (Clarivate Analytics, Boston, Massachusetts, USA). The publication screening process is shown in Fig. 1.

The flow diagram of preferred reporting items for systematic reviews and meta-analyses for study selection

Two groups of authors (JY and LJ; SZ and ZX) independently screened the titles and abstracts of all articles identified. Any disagreement was resolved by discussion with another author (ZL). To be eligible for inclusion, a study must meet one of the following criteria: (1) reporting results of a serological test for hepatitis B surface antigen (HBsAg) and providing data allowing estimation of CHB prevalence in patients with FLD; (2) providing data allowing estimation of the prevalence of FLD in CHB patients; (3) reporting results of a serological test for HBsAg and provide data allowing estimation of the concurrent prevalence of CHB and FLD in the general population. Additionally, the study must include participants who are Chinese living in mainland China, Macau, Hong Kong, or Taiwan. Exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) full text not available; (2) publications in languages other than Chinese or English; (3) studies conducted in duplicated populations; (4) unsuitable types of publication, including systematic reviews, meta-analyses, surveillance registration or national notifiable disease reports, case reports, and correspondences, etc.; (5) irrelevance to the inclusion criteria (e.g. animal studies, cell studies); (6) failure to identify patients with concurrent CHB and FLD; and (7) HBV diagnosis based on non-serum tests or using finger rapid tests.

Data collection and processing

We extracted the number of individuals tested for HBsAg and FLD as well as the number of individuals who were HBsAg positive and had diagnoses of FLD from each study. Studies were further categorized based on whether the data was derived from the general population, hepatitis B-infected individuals, or from people with FLD. Two groups of authors (JY and LJ; SZ and ZX) independently extracted data from eligible studies with any inconsistencies resolved by discussion.

For each study, we further extracted the following information where available: (1) first author, (2) year of publication, (3) research duration, (4) region, (5) mean age (standard deviation [SD]) of the entire sample, (6) number of male participants in the entire sample, (7) type of comorbidity (CHB with FLD/CHB with NAFLD), (8) sample source (hospital/community), and (9) diagnostic method for FLD (imaging/transient elastography/liver biopsy).

To compare the clinical features of individuals with different disease conditions, we classified the subjects into four groups based on their HBV infection and FLD status: (1) without CHB and FLD, (2) with CHB only, (3) with FLD only, and (4) with both CHB and concurrent FLD. Additional information on the four groups of people, where available, was extracted for subgroup comparison. Detailed information further collected and definition for region classification are shown in Supplemental Table S1 and Supplemental material S2.

Statistical analysis

Pooled prevalence

Pooled estimates of the prevalence of comorbid CHB and FLD were calculated using a random-effects model after log transformation. Subgroup analyses were performed to explore the potential sources of heterogeneity. These analyses were pre-specified based on clinical and epidemiological rationale: (1) Geographic region was stratified because China exhibits substantial variations in economic development, dietary patterns, and healthcare resources across different regions, all of which may influence FLD prevalence; (2) Study time period was analyzed to examine temporal trends in the context of rapidly changing lifestyles and increasing obesity rates in China over recent decades; (3) Diagnostic method was included due to the known differences in sensitivity and specificity between various FLD assessment techniques; (4) Sex was stratified given the well-documented differences in sex hormones and body fat distribution that influence the risk and presentation of FLD; (5) Data source (hospital-based vs. community-based) was analyzed to assess potential spectrum bias and gauge the generalizability of findings from clinical settings to the broader population; (6) FLD type (general FLD vs. NAFLD-specific) was examined to evaluate the impact of including studies with potentially broader diagnostic criteria. Meta-regression was conducted to further examine potential contributors to between-study heterogeneity, expressed as prevalence ratios using meta-regression models further incorporating age based on subgroup analysis.

Comparison of clinical features among subgroups

Meta-analysis was employed to assess differences in various clinical features across the four subgroups. The weighted mean difference (WMD) and its 95% CI were calculated to estimate statistical differences of the continuous variables in four groups. Hepatitis B viral load was uniformly log-transformed. Age, gender, and sample size were included as covariates, with group serving as the independent variable.

Sensitivity analysis

Sensitivity analysis was conducted to evaluate the impact of each study on the overall pooled estimate. Publication bias was assessed using Egger test. Heterogeneity was evaluated using the Cochran Q test and quantified using the I2 statistic. Statistical analyses for this meta-analysis were performed using the R statistical software version 3.4.1. All tests were 2-sided and a P value of < 0.05 was considered significant.

Results

Literature search results

A total of 1,522 articles were initially identified in this study. After excluding 121 duplicates, the titles and abstracts of 1,401 published studies were screened. Among these, 431 articles were deemed potentially eligible and underwent full text review. Among the 431 articles, 161 were excluded for not reporting the prevalence of CHB with concurrent FLD, 15 had ineligible study designs, 38 lacked full texts, 66 had ineligible study topics, 11 were excluded due to duplicated population, and 26 involved non-Chinese population. Ultimately, 114 studies were included in this systematic review and meta-analysis (Fig. 1).

Pooled prevalence of co-occurrence of CHB and FLD in the general population

Of the 114 eligible studies, 22 were conducted in the general population, involving 318,770 participants. The overall prevalence of concurrence CHB and FLD in the general population was estimated to be 1.48% (95% CI: 1.02%-2.14%) (I2 = 100.0%, P < 0.01) (Table 1). Subgroup analysis revealed no significant differences in prevalence in terms of gender, region or time period, which was further confirmed by meta-regression analysis. The result of meta-regression is shown in Supplementary Table S2.

Pooled prevalence of FLD in CHB patients

In the 83 studies involving 72,094 CHB patients, 18,335 were identified with FLD. The pooled prevalence of FLD in CHB patients was 26.75% (95% CI: 23.44%-30.52%). In CHB patients, significant differences were observed in the prevalence of FLD across different regions (P < 0.01) and different data sources (P = 0.01). The highest prevalence was observed in Hong Kong, with a prevalence of 44.36% (95% CI: 37.73%-52.14%), while the lowest prevalence was found in Northeast China, with a prevalence of 16.49% (95% CI: 10.08%-26.98%). Hospital based studies generally exhibited higher FLD prevalence than those from community settings. The prevalence of FLD among CHB patients has shown an increasing trend over time. Specifically, the prevalence was 16.38% before 2005, 22.13% between 2006 and 2010, and 30.14% after 2011 (P = 0.07). Regarding the different diagnostic methods for FLD, transient elastography (TE) exhibited the highest prevalence of 33.74%, followed by liver biopsy, while imaging methods showing the lowest prevalence. The result of meta-regression is shown in Table S3.

Pooled prevalence of CHB in FLD patients

Among 3,893 FLD patients in the 9 eligible studies, 552 were HBsAg positive. The pooled prevalence of CHB in FLD patients was 16.94% (95% CI: 12.51%-22.94%). Subgroup analysis and meta-regression revealed no significant differences in prevalence in regard to gender, region, time period, or type of comorbidity (Table S4).

Sensitivity analysis and publication bias

The sensitivity analysis results indicated that the meta-analysis was robust, with no individual study significantly affecting the pooled prevalence estimates (Figure S1-3). Egger’s test was also conducted in general population, CHB and FLD patients separately and yielded P values of 0.70, 0.052 and 0.34 (P > 0.05), respectively, indicating no obvious publication bias.

Comparison of clinical features among four subgroups

Several clinical features and prevalence of comorbid conditions were compared using meta-regression adjusted for age and sex across individuals without CHB and FLD, CHB patients, FLD patients, and people who had both CHB and FLD, which were classified as group 1, group 2, group 3 and group 4.

As shown in Figs. 2 and 3, group 4 exhibited higher levels of body mass index (BMI), triglycerides (TG), fasting insulin (FINS) and albumin (ALB) compared to group 2. The total cholesterol (TC) level in group 4 was 0.48mmol/L lower than group 3. Discussion

This study estimates the prevalence of co-occurrence of CHB and FLD in Chinese population. Through a comparison of the clinical features of different subgroups, we found that patients with both conditions exhibited a more unfavorable clinical profile, including increased insulin resistance, liver damage, and comorbidities such as diabetes and hypertension.

The prevalence of co-occurrence of CHB and FLD in the Chinese general population is found to be 1.48%. Extrapolating from Chinese population of approximately 1.4 billion people, it is projected that around 20 to 30 million individuals are affected by both conditions. Considering the substantial number of affected individuals and possible severe adverse liver outcomes, this comorbid group deserves extensive attention.

The prevalence of FLD among patients with CHB has been estimated to be 26.75%, aligning with previous studies that reported a prevalence range of 25% to 30% [22–25]. Also, the prevalence of FLD is lower than that in the general population, consistent with recent studies indicating a negative association between HBV infection and steatosis [26–31]. However, our study has observed an upward trend in the prevalence of FLD among patients with CHB, similar to the pattern seen in the general population. Given the potentially more severe liver disease outcomes associated with comorbidities, the rising prevalence of FLD in CHB patients demands greater attention. This underscores the need for comprehensive monitoring and tailored prevention strategies to effectively manage both conditions and mitigate the risk of further liver damage. The variation in FLD prevalence estimates across diagnostic methods—highest with TE (33.74%), intermediate with liver biopsy (29.46%), and lowest with conventional imaging (21.77%)—is consistent with their established technical sensitivities. Although this trend was not statistically significant (P = 0.07), it underscores that the choice of diagnostic tool significantly influences the reported epidemiology of FLD in CHB patients. The low rate by conventional ultrasonography, the most common modality, reflects its well-documented limited sensitivity for detecting mild steatosis, likely leading to an underestimation of true prevalence [32]. In contrast, TE with Controlled Attenuation Parameter (CAP) provides a more sensitive, quantitative assessment [33]. The intermediate yield of liver biopsy, the histological gold standard, may be attributable to its sampling variability and the limited number of available studies.

Meanwhile, our study estimates the prevalence of CHB among patients with FLD at 16.94%, which is significantly higher than the previously reported prevalence of 5.86% in the general Chinese population [1]. The findings indicate that, despite a significant decrease in the incidence of HBV infection, the prevalence of CHB among patients with FLD remains high. Therefore, it is imperative to regularly screen for HBV among the FLD population and to implement standardized treatment protocols to effectively work towards the elimination of HBV.

Compared to patients with CHB, individuals with both diseases exhibited higher levels of BMI, TG and FINS, as well as a greater prevalence of diabetes, hypertension and hyperuricemia. As previous study has shown that obesity, diabetes, and other insulin-resistant states could cause increased synthesis and delivery of fatty acids to the liver, resulting in the accumulation of triglycerides in hepatocytes [27]. These differences in metabolic features to some extent confirm a strong association between metabolic dysfunctions and FLD [34–40]. While specific pharmacotherapies for FLD are still emerging, growing evidence confirms that treatment of metabolic comorbidities provides substantial hepatic benefits for FLD patients, including those with CHB. For instance, antihyperglycemic medications such as glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonists (GLP-1 RAs) and sodium-glucose cotransporter-2 (SGLT2) inhibitors not only improve glycemic control but also directly reduce liver fat content and inflammation in patients with NAFLD and type 2 diabetes [41, 42]. Similarly, statin therapy for dyslipidemia has been shown to be safe in CHB patients and may reduce the risk of hepatic decompensation and hepatocellular carcinoma [43]. These findings underscore that aggressive management of metabolic abnormalities—through both lifestyle intervention and appropriate pharmacotherapy—constitutes an essential component of care for patients with concurrent CHB and FLD, potentially mitigating liver disease progression from both metabolic and viral insults. Our results also suggest integrating routine metabolic screening into the standard care for CHB patients, including BMI, blood pressure, fasting glucose, lipid profile, and uric acid levels. Identifying metabolic risk factors should prompt early intervention through lifestyle changes (weight loss, diet, exercise) and pharmacotherapy (e.g., for diabetes, dyslipidemia, hypertension). This proactive approach helps reduce the incidence of fatty liver and may slow liver disease progression and associated comorbidities. Collaboration between hepatologists and endocrinologists or primary care physicians is essential for optimizing patient care.

We observed that the TC level in the comorbid group was 0.48mmol/L lower than that in the FLD group. While this finding is consistent with studies suggesting that HBV infection may modulate lipid metabolism [36, 44–49], we must also consider the alternative explanation: that lower TC levels may reflect more advanced liver pathology in the comorbid group rather than a protective effect. Impaired cholesterol synthesis is a well-documented consequence of progressing liver disease, including cirrhosis [50, 51]. Therefore, the lower TC levels observed in patients with both CHB and FLD could potentially indicate more severe liver damage in this group. This interpretation aligns with studies showing that patients with dual etiology liver disease may experience accelerated disease progression. Unfortunately, the available studies did not provide sufficient granular data on liver fibrosis stages to fully adjudicate between these competing explanations. This important question should be addressed in future prospective studies that specifically assess lipid profiles in relation to detailed histologic or elastographic findings.

We found that the concurrent group had a lower HBV DNA load than the CHB group. This aligns with studies demonstrating that FLD-CHB patients have lower baseline viral load and higher rates of HBeAg seroconversion, HBsAg seroclearance and ALT normalization, though no significant differences exist in HBV DNA clearance or virological response—indicating FLD’s effect on HBV replication is periodic or limited [4, 21, 52]. However, this virological phenomenon should be interpreted with caution in clinical practice. The decision to initiate antiviral therapy should not be based solely on viral load thresholds but should include comprehensive assessment of ALT levels and liver fibrosis status (e.g., via APRI, FIB-4, or elastography), as these patients may have significant liver injury despite lower viral replication. Furthermore, monitoring strategies should be dual-focused: while tracking HBV DNA remains important, equal emphasis should be placed on serial assessments of metabolic parameters, liver function tests, and fibrosis progression, given that the dual hit of viral and metabolic injury may accelerate liver disease progression. Further mechanistic studies are needed to understand this controversial effect more deeply. Additionally, comorbid individuals had an increased risk of all-cause mortality and cancer development compared to CHB patients, suggesting the need for close monitoring regardless of viral load [4].

Based on our findings, we propose a practical management framework for FLD-CHB patients: (1) Implement risk-stratified bidirectional screening: routinely assess FLD (via ultrasound or elastography) and metabolic parameters in CHB patients with metabolic risk factors (e.g., overweight/obesity, diabetes, or dyslipidemia), and test all FLD patients for HBsAg to identify occult HBV infection; (2) Adopt dual-treatment targets, combining CHB antiviral therapy with metabolic abnormality management (lifestyle intervention or pharmacotherapy as needed); (3) Strengthen monitoring with regular assessments of virologic markers (HBV DNA) and metabolic/liver fibrosis indicators to detect early progression. This integrated approach requires collaboration among hepatologists, endocrinologists and primary care physicians to optimize outcomes for this high-risk group.

Some limitations of this study should be mentioned. First, we pooled estimates despite significant between-study heterogeneity. This substantial heterogeneity is likely attributed to several factors: (1) the use of diverse diagnostic methods for fatty liver disease (e.g., ultrasound, CT, TE, or liver biopsy) across studies, which vary in sensitivity and specificity; (2) variations in the baseline characteristics of enrolled participants across different studies, such as age, sex, and BMI; (3) the broad geographical coverage of included studies, encompassing multiple regions of China with differing economic levels, dietary habits, and medical resources, which may influence prevalence estimates; and (4) the extended time span of the included literature, during which the prevalence of FLD itself has exhibited a temporal upward trend. The use of a random-effects model, however, still allows the possibility of extrapolation outside the study population, and we addressed heterogeneity by meta-regression, stratification and sensitivity analyses when possible. Second, given the unavailability of individual-level data, groupings by age, sex, year and geography sometimes necessitated some approximation, and variables were not available for inclusion in meta-regression models with full mutual adjustment. Third, we acknowledge the evolution in FLD terminology and diagnostic criteria. Most included studies (up to our search date) used the term NAFLD and its established criteria, while the new metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD) definitions were proposed in 2020 and formally published in 2023—making retrospective application of MASLD criteria to existing literature unfeasible [53]. However, since most NAFLD-labeled patients in our included studies would likely meet MASLD criteria (given cardiometabolic risk factors), our findings remain highly relevant and provide a robust foundation for understanding the concurrent burden of metabolic fatty liver disease and CHB in China. We strongly recommend future prospective studies adopt the new MASLD/SLD criteria to align with the current international framework and enable more precise characterization of this patient population.

Conclusions

In conclusion, the concurrent occurrence of CHB and FLD has become an increasingly significant public health issue in China. This dual condition presents unique clinical features that differentiate it from cases where either CHB or FLD is present alone. Patients with both CHB and FLD often exhibit a more complex disease progression, potentially leading to worsened liver function and higher risks of cirrhosis and liver-related complications. Due to the complexity of the pathogenesis, it is crucial to look for differentiated precision treatment for comorbid individuals and to conduct targeted prevention and early screening of FLD in CHB patients.

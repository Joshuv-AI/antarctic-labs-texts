Putting Perspective into OWL [sic]: Complexity-Neutral Standpoint Reasoning for Ontology Languages via Monodic S5 over Counting Two-Variable First-Order Logic (Extended Version with Appendix)
Lucía Gómez Álvarez, Sebastian Rudolph

Abstract
Standpoint extensions of KR formalisms have been recently introduced to incorporate multi-perspective modelling and reasoning capabilities. In such modal extensions, the integration of conceptual modelling and perspective annotations can be more or less tight, with monodic standpoint extensions striking a good balance as they enable advanced modelling while preserving good reasoning complexities. We consider the extension of 𝒞 2 \mathcal{C}^{2} – the counting two-variable fragment of first-order logic – by monodic standpoints. At the core of our treatise is a polytime translation of formulae in said formalism into standpoint-free 𝒞 2 \mathcal{C}^{2} , requiring elaborate model-theoretic arguments. By virtue of this translation, the NExpTime -complete complexity of checking satisfiability in 𝒞 2 \mathcal{C}^{2} carries over to our formalism. As our formalism subsumes monodic S5 over 𝒞 2 \mathcal{C}^{2} , our result also significantly advances the state of the art in research on first-order modal logics. As a practical consequence, the very expressive description logics 𝒮 ​ ℋ ​ 𝒪 ​ ℐ ​ 𝒬 ​ ℬ s \mathcal{SHOIQB}_{s} and 𝒮 ​ ℛ ​ 𝒪 ​ ℐ ​ 𝒬 ​ ℬ s \mathcal{SROIQB}_{s} which subsume the popular W3C-standardized OWL 1 and OWL 2 ontology languages, are shown to allow for monodic standpoint extensions without any increase of standard reasoning complexity. We prove that NExpTime -hardness already occurs in much less expressive DLs as long as they feature both nominals and monodic standpoints. We also show that, with inverses, functionality, and nominals present, minimally lifting the monodicity restriction leads to undecidability.

Transformations

The results obtained in the first part of this paper concern the fragment of all FOSL formulae that are monodic and
\mathcal{C}^{2}

Theorem 5
.

For any FOSL formula
\phi

\mathcal{C}^{2}
-ness and monodicity.

Proof Sketch.

Let
\phi

\phi
, the formula

is equisatisfiable and preserves

-ness and monodicity.
The function
\mathrm{trans}
replaces every

is defined as follows

\displaystyle\mathrm{trans}(\mbox{\tt P}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{k}))

\displaystyle=\mbox{\tt P}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{k})

\displaystyle\mathrm{trans}(\lnot\psi)

\displaystyle=\lnot\mathrm{trans}(\psi)

\displaystyle\mathrm{trans}(\psi_{1}{\,\mathrel{\wedge}\,}\psi_{2})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{trans}(\psi_{1}){\,\mathrel{\wedge}\,}\mathrm{trans}(\psi_{2})

\displaystyle\mathrm{trans}(\forall x\psi)

\displaystyle=\forall x(\mathrm{trans}(\psi))

\displaystyle\mathrm{trans}(\mathop{\lozenge}\nolimits_{\mathsf{\mathsf{e}}}\psi)

\displaystyle=\mathop{\lozenge}\nolimits_{\mathsf{*}}(\mathrm{trans}_{\mathbf{E}}(\mathsf{e})\mathrel{\wedge}\mathrm{trans}(\psi))

Therein,

\displaystyle\mathrm{trans}_{\mathbf{E}}(\mathsf{s})

\displaystyle\mathrm{trans}_{\mathbf{E}}(\mathsf{e}_{1}\cup\mathsf{e}_{2})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{trans}_{\mathbf{E}}(\mathsf{e}_{1})\mathrel{\vee}\mathrm{trans}_{\mathbf{E}}(\mathsf{e}_{2})

\displaystyle\mathrm{trans}_{\mathbf{E}}(\mathsf{e}_{1}\cap\mathsf{e}_{2})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{trans}_{\mathbf{E}}(\mathsf{e}_{1})\mathrel{\wedge}\mathrm{trans}_{\mathbf{E}}(\mathsf{e}_{2})

3.1
Permutational Representatives

Next, we show that for any satisfiable frugal
\mathbb{S}^{\mathrm{mon}}_{\mathcal{C}^{2}}
formula
\phi
, there is a structure
\mathfrak{M}
with only exponentially many precisifications in
|\phi|
from which a model of
\phi
of a specific shape can be created (while
\mathfrak{M}
may not be a model itself).

Definition 9
.

Let
\mathfrak{M}=\mbox{$\langle\Delta,\Pi,\sigma,\gamma\rangle$}
be a standpoint structure for the signature
\langle\mathbf{P}\uplus\mathbf{P}_{\mathtt{E}},\emptyset,\{*\}\rangle
,
where
\mathbf{P}
contains only unary and binary predicates, and
\mathbf{P}_{\mathtt{E}}=\{\mathtt{E}_{0},\ldots,\mathtt{E}_{\ell}\}
is a set of special rigid unary predicates.
Let
\mathbb{P}_{\mathtt{E}}
denote the set of all permutations (i.e., bijective functions)
f:\Delta\to\Delta
which preserve (non)membership in every
\mathtt{E}_{i}
, that is, for every

i\in\{0,\ldots,\ell\}
and
\delta\in\Delta
, we require
\delta\in\mathtt{E}_{i}^{\mathfrak{M}}\Leftrightarrow f(\delta)\in\mathtt{E}_{i}^{\mathfrak{M}}
.

Then, the
\mathbf{P}_{\mathtt{E}}
-stable permutational closure
of
\mathfrak{M}
, denoted
\langle\mathfrak{M}\rangle_{\mathbf{P}_{\mathtt{E}}}
is the standpoint structure
\langle\Delta,\Pi^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime}\rangle
defined by

•

\Pi^{\prime}=\Pi\times\mathbb{P}_{\mathtt{E}}
,

•

\sigma^{\prime}(\mathsf{s})=\sigma(\mathsf{s})\times\mathbb{P}_{\mathtt{E}}
,

•

\mbox{\tt P}^{\gamma^{\prime}((\pi,f))}=\{f(\delta)\mid\delta\,{\in}\,\mbox{\tt P}^{\gamma(\pi)}\}
for unary predicates
\mbox{\tt P}\,{\in}\,\mathbf{P}

•

\mbox{\tt P}^{\gamma^{\prime}((\pi,f))}=\{(f(\delta_{1}),f(\delta_{2}))\mid(\delta_{1},\delta_{2})\,{\in}\,\mbox{\tt P}^{\gamma(\pi)}\}
for binary predicates
\mbox{\tt P}\,{\in}\,\mathbf{P}

\mathop{\lozenge}

As we can see, the structure
\mathfrak{M}
contains a set of special rigid unary predicates
\mathbf{P}_{\mathtt{E}}
. These predicates induce “
\mathtt{E}
-types”, corresponding to the subsets
T\subseteq\mathbf{P}_{\mathtt{E}}
, so a domain element is said to have the
\mathtt{E}
-type
T
if it belongs to the interpretation of each
\mathtt{E}_{i}
in
T
and to none outside it. We say
T
is realized (in
\mathfrak{M}
) if at least one domain element has it.

The
\mathbf{P}_{\mathtt{E}}
-stable permutational closure of
\mathfrak{M}
produces a much larger structure that contains, for each initial precisification in
\Pi
, the set of precisifications with all possible permutations between domain elements belonging to the same
\mathtt{E}
-type. Locally, all permuted versions of any
\pi\in\Pi
in the closure are isomorphic to each other, they just have their elements “swapped around”, preserving the internal structure. This intuition is materialised in the lemma below.

Lemma 10
.

Let
\phi
be a frugal
\mathbb{S}^{\mathrm{mon}}_{\mathcal{C}^{2}}
formula and let
\langle\mathfrak{M}\rangle_{\mathbf{P}_{\mathtt{E}}}
the
\mathbf{P}_{\mathtt{E}}
-stable permutational closure
of a standpoint structure
\mathfrak{M}
.
Let
(\pi,f)
and
(\pi,f^{\prime})
be precisifications of
\langle\mathfrak{M}\rangle_{\mathbf{P}_{\mathtt{E}}}
and

v^{\prime}=f^{\prime}\circ f^{-1}\circ v
. Then,

\langle\mathfrak{M}\rangle_{\mathbf{P}_{\mathtt{E}}},\!(\pi,f),\!v\models\phi\Longleftrightarrow\langle\mathfrak{M}\rangle_{\mathbf{P}_{\mathtt{E}}},\!(\pi,f^{\prime}),\!v^{\prime}\models\phi.

At the global level,
\mathtt{E}
-types of domain elements, which are preserved under the permutations (by construction), will be utilized to witness the elements’ “membership” in diamond-preceded subformulae in the following sense: we call some
\delta\in\Delta
a
member
of some formula
\mathop{\lozenge}\nolimits_{\mathsf{*}}\psi
with one free variable
z
if
\mathfrak{M},\pi,z\mapsto\delta\models\mathop{\lozenge}\nolimits_{\mathsf{*}}\psi
for some/all
\pi\in\Pi
(note that by the semantics, the choice of
\pi
is irrelevant in this case). We will denote the set of members of
\mathop{\lozenge}\nolimits_{\mathsf{*}}\psi
in
\mathfrak{M}
by
(\mathop{\lozenge}\nolimits_{\mathsf{*}}\psi)^{\mathfrak{M}}
.

Let us now investigate what conditions must
\mathfrak{M}
meet so that
\langle\mathfrak{M}\rangle_{\mathbf{P}_{\mathtt{E}}}
is a model of
\phi
. First, for
\langle\mathfrak{M}\rangle_{\mathbf{P}_{\mathtt{E}}}
to witness membership to the
\mathop{\lozenge}\nolimits_{\mathsf{*}}\psi
formulae, the number of
\mathtt{E}
-types must be at least as large as the number of types induced by the monodic modal formulae – these we refer to as
\mathop{\lozenge}
-types. The two sets of types will be aligned: each
\mathtt{E}
-type will be entirely contained within a corresponding
\mathop{\lozenge}
-type.
Moreover, for each formula
\mathop{\lozenge}\nolimits_{\mathsf{*}}\psi
in a given
\mathop{\lozenge}
-type,
\mathfrak{M}
must include at least one precisification in which some element of that type satisfies
\psi
. The
\mathbf{P}_{\mathtt{E}}
-stable permutational closure then ensures that every other element of the same
\mathtt{E}
-type also satisfies
\psi
in some permutation of that precisification.

Theorem 11
.

Let
\phi
be a satisfiable frugal
\mathbb{S}^{\mathrm{mon}}_{\mathcal{C}^{2}}
formula over the signature
\langle\mathbf{P},\emptyset,\{*\}\rangle
.
Let
Dia_{\phi}
denote the diamond subformulae of
\phi
and
\mathit{FreeDia}_{\phi}
the diamond subformulae with one free variable.
Then there is a standpoint structure
\mathfrak{M}=\mbox{$\langle\Delta,\Pi,\sigma,\gamma\rangle$}
over
\langle\mathbf{P}\uplus\mathbf{P}_{\mathtt{E}},\emptyset,\{*\}\rangle
with

•

|\mathbf{P}_{\mathtt{E}}|=\ell=|\mathit{FreeDia}_{\phi}|

•

|\Pi|\leq|Dia_{\phi}|\cdot 2^{|Dia_{\phi}|}

such that
\langle\mathfrak{M}\rangle_{\mathbf{P}_{\mathtt{E}}}
is a model of
\phi
.

Proof Sketch.

To prove
Theorem

11
, we start from an arbitrary model
\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}
of
\phi
and let
\mathit{FreeDia}_{\phi}=\{\mathop{\lozenge}\nolimits_{\mathsf{*}}\phi_{1},\ldots\mathop{\lozenge}\nolimits_{\mathsf{*}}\phi_{\ell}\}
be the set of diamond subformulae of
\phi
with one free variable.
We enrich
\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}
by
\mathbf{P}_{\mathtt{E}}
, setting the extension of
\mathtt{E}_{i}
to
(\mathop{\lozenge}\nolimits_{\mathsf{*}}\phi_{i})^{\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}}
for every
i\in\{1,\ldots,\ell\}
.
Then we create a new structure
\mathfrak{M}
by selecting at most exponentially many precisifications from the enriched
\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}
and removing the rest. Specifically,

•

select an arbitrary
\pi
in case there are no diamond subformulae of
\phi
at all. Otherwise,

•

for each
\mathop{\lozenge}\nolimits_{\mathsf{*}}\psi
with no free variables that is satisfied in
\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}
, select some
\pi
with
\mathfrak{M}^{\prime},\pi\models\psi
and

•

for each realised
\mathtt{E}
-type
T\subseteq\mathbf{P}_{\mathtt{E}}
, pick some
\delta
that has
T
, and select, for every
\mathtt{E}_{i}\in T
one
\pi
with
\mathfrak{M}^{\prime},\pi,z\mapsto\delta\models\phi_{i}

The first point ensures that
\Pi
is nonempty. The second adds witnesses for sentential modal formulae. The third provides witnesses of all monodic modal formulae from
\mathit{FreeDia}_{\phi}
. The construction ensures that at least one domain element witnesses each
\mathop{\lozenge}\nolimits_{\mathsf{*}}\phi_{i}
formula of each
\mathop{\lozenge}
-type. Once these seed witnesses are in
\mathfrak{M}
, the rest of the elements belonging to that type in
\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}
will be witnessed by a permutation in
\langle\mathfrak{M}\rangle_{\mathbf{P}_{\mathtt{E}}}

\langle\mathfrak{M}\rangle_{\mathbf{P}_{\mathtt{E}}}

\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}

∎

3.2
Stacked Interpretations

We now define a specific kind of
\mathcal{C}^{2}
interpretation obtained from a given standpoint structure
\mathfrak{M}
with
2^{m}
precisifications, called the
stacked interpretation
of
\mathfrak{M}
and denoted
\mathcal{I}^{\mathfrak{M}}
. This structure is designed to closely mirror the shape of
\mathfrak{M}
.

Definition 13
.

Let
\mathfrak{M}=\mbox{$\langle\Delta,\Pi,\sigma,\gamma\rangle$}
with
|\Pi|{=}2^{m}
be a standpoint structure for the signature
\langle\mathbf{P},\emptyset,\{*\}\rangle
where
\mathbf{P}
contains only unary and binary predicates. Assume
\Pi
is linearly ordered with elements named
\pi_{0},\pi_{1},\ldots,\pi_{2^{m}-1}
.

The
stacked interpretation
of
\mathfrak{M}
is the FO-interpretation
\mathcal{I}^{\mathfrak{M}}=(\Delta^{\prime},\cdot^{\mathcal{I}})
with signature
\langle\mathbf{P}\uplus\{\mbox{\tt F},\mbox{\tt L}_{0},\ldots,\mbox{\tt L}_{m-1}\},\emptyset\rangle
, where
F
is a fresh binary predicate and
\mbox{\tt L}_{0},\ldots,\mbox{\tt L}_{m-1}
are fresh unary predicates, such that

(S1)

\Delta^{\prime}=\Delta\times\{0,\ldots,2^{m}-1\}

(S2)

\mbox{\tt L}_{j}^{\mathcal{I}}=\{(\delta,i)\mid\mbox{the $j^{\mathrm{th}}$ bit of $i$ in binary encoding is $1$}\}

(S3)

\mbox{\tt F}^{\mathcal{I}}=\{((\delta,i),(\delta,{i+1}))\mid\delta\in\Delta,\ 0\leq i<2^{m}-1\}

(S4)

\mbox{\tt P}^{\mathcal{I}}=\bigcup_{0\leq i<2^{m}}\mbox{\tt P}^{\gamma(\pi_{i})}\times\{i\}
for all unary
\mbox{\tt P}\in\mathbf{P}
,

(S5)

\mbox{\tt P}^{\mathcal{I}}=\{((\delta_{1},i),(\delta_{2},i))\mid 0\,{\leq}\,i\,{<}\,2^{m},(\delta_{1},\delta_{2})\,{\in}\,\mbox{\tt P}^{\gamma(\pi_{i})}\}
for all binary
\mbox{\tt P}\in\mathbf{P}
.
\mathop{\lozenge}

Our approach constructs a stacked domain by creating one copy of the original domain
\Delta
for each precisification in
\mathfrak{M}
, so that each new element
(\delta,\pi)
mimics
\delta
at precisification
\pi
. A set of new unary predicates
\mbox{\tt L}_{0},\ldots,\mbox{\tt L}_{m-1}
encodes the index of the associated precisification (each less than
2^{m}
). Additionally, a new binary predicate
F
links each element
(\delta,\pi_{i})
to its successor
(\delta,\pi_{i+1})
. Thus, for every original element
\delta
, the stacked interpretation forms an
F
-chain tracking
\delta
across all precisifications in
\mathfrak{M}
. In
Figure

1
, (3) depicts the stacked interpretation of (2) with the
F
-chains in purple.

Definition 14
.

For a given
m\in\mathbb{N}
, we define
\phi^{m}_{\mathrm{stack}}
as the conjunction of the following formulae

(F1)

\forall x.(\bigvee_{0\leq j<m}\neg\mbox{\tt L}_{j}(x))\to\exists^{=1}y.\mbox{\tt F}(x,y)

(F2)

\forall x.(\bigwedge_{0\leq j<m}\mbox{\tt L}_{j}(x))\to\exists^{=0}y.\mbox{\tt F}(x,y)

(F3)

\forall x.(\bigvee_{0\leq j<m}\mbox{\tt L}_{j}(x))\to\exists^{=1}y.\mbox{\tt F}(y,x)

(F4)

\forall x.(\bigwedge_{0\leq j<m}\neg\mbox{\tt L}_{j}(x))\to\exists^{=0}y.\mbox{\tt F}(y,x)

(F5)

\displaystyle\forall xy.\mbox{\tt F}(x,y)\to\bigwedge_{\mathclap{0\leq j<m}}\Big(\!\big(\mbox{\tt L}_{j}(x)\,{\leftrightarrow}\,\mbox{\tt L}_{j}(y)\big)\leftrightarrow\bigvee_{\mathclap{0\leq j^{\prime}<j}}\neg\mbox{\tt L}_{j^{\prime}}(x)\!\Big)

(F6)

\forall xy.\mbox{\tt P}(x,y)\!\to\!\displaystyle{\bigwedge_{\mathclap{0\leq j<m}}}\,\mbox{\tt L}_{j}(x)\,{\leftrightarrow}\,\mbox{\tt L}_{j}(y)
for all binary
\mbox{\tt P}\,{\in}\,\mathbf{P}
​.

\mathop{\lozenge}

The stacked formula of size
m
, denoted
\phi^{m}_{\mathrm{stack}}
, is used to enforce that models are stacked models. Clause
(F1)
enforces that all elements except those with the highest index (as determined by the
L
predicates) have exactly one
F
-successor. Conversely,
(F2)
ensures that elements with the highest index have none. Clauses
(F3)
and
(F4)
impose analogous constraints on
F
-predecessors. Clause
(F5)
encodes that any two
F
-connected elements have consecutive indexes, via a binary level-counter using the
L
predicates. Lastly, Clause
(F6)
enforces that all binary predicates (except
F
) relate only elements with matching indices.

Lemma 15
.

Any stacked interpretation
\mathcal{I}^{\mathfrak{M}}
satisfies
\phi^{m}_{\mathrm{stack}}
.

Proof sketch.

We verify that each clause of
\phi^{m}_{\mathrm{stack}}
is satisfied by the stacked interpretation
\mathcal{I}^{\mathfrak{M}}
as defined. In particular, the structure of the domain, and the interpretation of the predicates
F
,
\mbox{\tt L}_{0},\dots,\mbox{\tt L}_{m-1}
, and
\mbox{\tt P}\in\mathbf{P}
ensure that all required properties hold. ∎

Theorem 16
.

A first-order interpretation
\mathcal{I}
over the signature
\langle\mathbf{P}\uplus\{\mbox{\tt F},\mbox{\tt L}_{0},\ldots,\mbox{\tt L}_{m-1}\},\emptyset\rangle
satisfies

if and only if it is isomorphic to a stacked interpretation

of some standpoint structure

over signature

with

precisifications.

If
\mathcal{I}
is isomorphic to a stacked model

then it satisfies
\phi^{m}_{\smash{\mathrm{stack}}}
by

15
.
It remains to prove the other direction, i.e., for any
\mathcal{I}
that satisfies
\phi^{m}_{\smash{\mathrm{stack}}}
, there exists a standpoint structure
\mathfrak{M}
for which
\mathcal{I}^{\mathfrak{M}}
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{I}
. We show how to construct
\mathfrak{M}=\mbox{$\langle\Delta,\Pi,\sigma,\gamma\rangle$}
given
\mathcal{I}
with domain
\Delta^{\prime}
:
For any
\delta^{\prime}\in\Delta^{\prime}
we let
\mathsf{level}(\delta^{\prime})
denote the unique number

that satisfies, for every

(j+1)
th bit in the binary encoding of

is

if and only if
\delta^{\prime}\in\mbox{\tt L}_{j}^{\mathcal{I}}
.
Moreover, we let
\approx
be the smallest equivalence relation containing

\approx
-equivalence classes of

,

.
Finally, we set
\mbox{\tt P}^{\gamma(\pi_{i})}

•

\{[\delta^{\prime}]_{\approx}\mid\delta^{\prime}{\,\in\,}\mbox{\tt P}^{\mathcal{I}},\mathsf{level}(\delta^{\prime}){\,=\,}i\}
for unary
\mbox{\tt P}{\,\in\,}\mathbf{P}

•

3.3
Translating Formulae

So far, we have shown that the satisfiability of a frugal
\mathbb{S}^{\mathrm{mon}}_{\mathcal{C}^{2}}
formula
\phi
coincides with the existence of a structure
\mathfrak{M}
of size exponential in
|\phi|
from which a model can be extracted. Furthermore, we demonstrated that such structures can be characterized in plain
\mathcal{C}^{2}
through their corresponding stacked interpretations. In this subsection, we leverage these results to define a translation from frugal
\mathbb{S}^{\mathrm{mon}}_{\mathcal{C}^{2}}
into plain
\mathcal{C}^{2}
, such that a frugal
\mathbb{S}^{\mathrm{mon}}_{\mathcal{C}^{2}}
formula
\phi
is satisfiable if and only if its translation into
\mathcal{C}^{2}
is satisfiable. Together with the translation from
\mathbb{S}^{\mathrm{mon}}_{\mathcal{C}^{2}}
to frugal
\mathbb{S}^{\mathrm{mon}}_{\mathcal{C}^{2}}
, this entails the upper complexity bound for all of
\mathbb{S}^{\mathrm{mon}}_{\mathcal{C}^{2}}
.

Definition 17
.

Given some
m\in\mathbb{N}
, we define the function
\mathsf{Trans}_{m}
that maps frugal
\mathbb{S}^{\mathrm{mon}}_{\mathcal{C}^{2}}
sentences
\phi
over the signature
\langle\mathbf{P}\uplus\mathbf{P}_{\mathtt{E}},\emptyset,\{*\}\rangle
with
\mathbf{P}_{\mathtt{E}}=\{\mathtt{E}_{0},\ldots\mathtt{E}_{\ell}\}
into
\mathcal{C}^{2}
as follows:

\mathsf{Trans}_{m}(\phi)
is the sentence
\forall x.\forall y.(x\,\dot{=}\,y\to\mathsf{tr}(\phi))
, where the function
\mathsf{tr}
is recursively defined via

\displaystyle\psi

\displaystyle{\!\!\!\mapsto\!\!\!}

\displaystyle\psi\mbox{\ \ \ \ if $\psi$ is of the form $\mbox{\tt P}(z)$, $\mbox{\tt P}(z,z^{\prime})$ or $z\,\dot{=}\,z^{\prime}$ }

\displaystyle\neg\psi

\displaystyle{\!\!\!\mapsto\!\!\!}

\displaystyle\neg(\mathsf{tr}(\psi))

\displaystyle\psi\wedge\psi^{\prime}

\displaystyle{\!\!\!\mapsto\!\!\!}

\displaystyle\mathsf{tr}(\psi)\wedge\mathsf{tr}(\psi^{\prime})

\displaystyle\exists^{\lhd n}z.\psi

\displaystyle{\!\!\!\mapsto\!\!\!}

\displaystyle\exists^{\lhd n}z.(\phi^{=}_{\mathtt{L}}(x,y)\wedge\mathsf{tr}(\psi))

\displaystyle{\mathop{\lozenge}}_{*}\psi

\displaystyle{\!\!\!\mapsto\!\!\!}

\displaystyle\forall z_{\mathrm{nf}}.x\,\dot{=}\,y\to\exists z_{{\mathrm{mf}}}.\phi^{=}_{\mathtt{E}}(x,y)\wedge\mathsf{tr}(\psi)\qquad

where
z,z^{\prime}\in\{x,y\}
and

•

z_{\mathrm{nf}}
is a variable from
\{x,y\}
that is not free in
\psi
and
\{z_{\mathrm{mf}}\}=\{x,y\}\setminus\{z_{\mathrm{nf}}\}
,

•

\phi^{=}_{\mathtt{L}}(x,y)
abbreviates
\bigwedge_{0\leq j<m}\mbox{\tt L}_{j}(x)\leftrightarrow\mbox{\tt L}_{j}(y)
, and

•

\phi^{=}_{\mathtt{E}}(x,y)
abbreviates
\bigwedge_{0\leq i\leq\ell}\mathtt{E}_{i}(x)\leftrightarrow\mathtt{E}_{i}(y)
.
\mathop{\lozenge}

The key components of the translation are the handling of counting quantification and modal operators. The translation of an counting existential quantification employs the formula
\phi^{=}_{\mathtt{L}}(x,y)
to ensure that quantification ranges only over elements belonging to the current layer of the stacked interpretation – namely, those whose counterparts correspond to the domain elements at the current precisification. In contrast, the translation of modal subformulae of the form
\mathop{\lozenge}\nolimits_{\mathsf{*}}\psi
makes use of
\phi^{=}_{\mathtt{E}}(x,y)
to ensure that quantification ranges over the elements belonging to the current
\mathtt{E}
-type. Recall that
\mathcal{I}^{\mathfrak{M}}
is constructed to mirror the structure
\mathfrak{M}
, from which in turn we obtain the model
\langle\mathfrak{M}\rangle_{\mathbf{P}_{\mathtt{E}}}
. Consequently, if any element of the same
\mathtt{E}
-type satisfies
\psi
, then there exist some permutation within
\langle\mathfrak{M}\rangle_{\mathbf{P}_{\mathtt{E}}}
that satisfies
\psi
and thus the formula
\mathop{\lozenge}\nolimits_{\mathsf{*}}\psi
is satisfied. Notice that when
\mathop{\lozenge}\nolimits_{\mathsf{*}}\psi
is sentential, the variable assignment does not make a difference. The following Lemma formally establishes the discussed correspondence.

Lemma 18
.

Let
\phi
be a frugal
\mathbb{S}^{\mathrm{mon}}_{\mathcal{C}^{2}}
sentence over the signature
\langle\mathbf{P},\emptyset,\{*\}\rangle
. Let
\mathfrak{M}=\mbox{$\langle\Delta,\Pi,\sigma,\gamma\rangle$}
be a standpoint structure for the signature
\langle\mathbf{P}\uplus\mathbf{P}_{\mathtt{E}},\emptyset,\{*\}\rangle
, with all predicates from
\mathbf{P}_{\mathtt{E}}=\{\mathtt{E}_{0},\ldots,\mathtt{E}_{\ell}\}
rigid, and
|\Pi|=2^{m}
. Then,

\langle\mathfrak{M}\rangle_{\mathbf{P}_{\mathtt{E}}}\models\phi\Longleftrightarrow\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{M}}\models\mathsf{Trans}_{m}(\phi).

Proof Sketch.

Toward the result, we first prove the claim that, for
\pi_{i}\in\Pi
,
f\in\mathbb{P}_{\mathtt{E}}
and a variable assignment
v
, we have
\langle\mathfrak{M}\rangle_{\mathbf{P}_{\mathtt{E}}},(\pi_{i},f),v\models\phi
iff
\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{M}},v^{\prime}\models\mathsf{tr}(\phi)
where
v^{\prime}(z)=(f(v(z)),i)
for
z\in\{x,y\}
. Due to
Lemma

10
, it suffices to show that
\langle\mathfrak{M}\rangle_{\mathbf{P}_{\mathtt{E}}},(\pi_{i},f_{id}),v\models\phi
iff
\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{M}},v^{\prime}\models\mathsf{tr}(\phi)
where
v^{\prime}(z)=(v(z),i)
; this follows by structural induction on
\phi
.

Toward the statement of the Lemma, assume
\langle\mathfrak{M}\rangle_{\mathbf{P}_{\mathtt{E}}}\models\phi
, thus
\langle\mathfrak{M}\rangle_{\mathbf{P}_{\mathtt{E}}},(\pi_{i},f),v\models\phi
holds for all
\pi_{i}\in\Pi
,
f\in\mathbb{P}_{\mathtt{E}}
and assignments
v
. Then,
\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{M}},v^{\prime}\models\mathsf{tr}(\phi)
where
v^{\prime}(z)=(v(z),i)
, thus
\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{M}},v^{\prime}\models\mathsf{tr}(\phi)
for all
v^{\prime}
where

5.2
Lifting Monodicity Causes Undecidability

A crucial restriction underlying all logical formalisms that we have considered so far is monodicity, that is, that modal operators can only be put in front of subformulae with at most one free variable.
The arguably mildest way of lifting monodicity is by imposing that one distinguished binary predicate, say
\mathtt{E}
, must be rigidly interpreted.
Note that rigidity of a binary predicate
\mathtt{E}
could be expressed by the FOSL formula
\forall x,y.\big(\mathtt{E}(x,y)\to\mathop{\square}\nolimits_{\mathsf{*}}\mathtt{E}(x,y)\big)

By a reduction from the second of the above tiling problems, we show that adding one rigid binary predicate causes undecidability even for a sublogic of
\mathbb{S}^{\mathrm{mon}}_{\mathcal{SHOIQB}_{s}}

Satisfiability of
\mathbb{S}^{\mathrm{mon}}_{\mathcal{ALCOIF}}

6
Conclusions and Future Work

We have shown that monodic standpoints can be added to
\mathcal{C}^{2}
without increasing the
NExpTime
reasoning complexity. We obtained this result by establishing a polynomial translation into plain
\mathcal{C}^{2}
, whose justification required rather elaborate model-theoretic constructions and arguments. On one hand, this finding advances the research into first-order modal logics, since our result subsume the case of monodic S5 over
\mathcal{C}^{2}
and even apply to logics with more expressive counting. On the other hand, we showed how the obtained result can be leveraged to prove that very expressive DLs subsuming popular W3C-standardized ontology languages can be endowed with monodic standpoints in a complexity-neutral way. We finally showed that in the presence of nominal concepts,
NExpTime
-hardness already arises for much less expressive DLs, and lifting monodicity even incurs undecidability.

For future work, it would be interesting to investigate the data complexity of our formalism.
Also it would be advantageous to find translations from versions of monodic standpoint OWL into plain OWL rather than
\mathcal{C}^{2}
, since this would allow to deploy existing highly optimized OWL reasoners for standpoint-aware ontological reasoning. While our results show that there are no complexity-theoretic barriers for this, our current translation approach heavily relies on features of
\mathcal{C}^{2}
that are beyond the capabilities of plain OWL.

B.1

\displaystyle\mathsf{NNF}(\neg(\phi\wedge\psi))

\displaystyle\mathsf{NNF}(\neg(\phi\vee\psi))

\displaystyle\mathsf{NNF}(\phi\vee\psi)

Newton-Maclaurin type inequalities for linear combinations of elementary symmetric functions Thanks: Research of the second author is supported by NSFC Grant No. 11871243.
Shuqi Hu, Changyu Ren, Ziyi Wang

Abstract
. In this paper, we establish Newton-Maclaurin type inequalities for functions arising from linear combinations of primitively symmetric polynomials. This generalization extends the classical Newton-Maclaurin inequality to a broader class of functions.

symmetric function; Newton inequality; Maclaurin inequa-

1.
introduction

In this paper, our primary focus is on Newton-Maclaurin type
inequalities pertaining to linear combinations of elementary
symmetric functions. First, define

\sigma_{k}({\boldsymbol{x}})={\displaystyle\sum_{1\leqslant i_{1}<\cdots<i_{k}\leqslant{n}}}x_{i_{1}}\cdots x_{i_{k}}

which is a

k
-th primitive symmetric function and the variables
{\boldsymbol{x}}=(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For example, when
n=3
,

\sigma_{1}({\boldsymbol{x}})=x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{3},\quad\sigma_{2}({\boldsymbol{x}})=x_{1}x_{2}+x_{1}x_{3}+x_{2}x_{3},\quad\sigma_{3}({\boldsymbol{x}})=x_{1}x_{2}x_{3}.

It is
convenient to define
\sigma_{0}({\boldsymbol{x}})=1
, and define

\sigma_{k}({\boldsymbol{x}})=0
if
k<0
or
k>n
. Furthermore, define a

k
-th primitive symmetric mean as

E_{k}({\boldsymbol{x}})=\displaystyle\frac{\sigma_{k}({\boldsymbol{x}})}{C_{n}^{k}},\quad k=1,2,\cdots,{n},

where
C_{n}^{k}=\displaystyle\frac{n!}{k!(n-k)!}
.

The classical Newton
[
17
]
- Maclaurin
[
15
]

inequalities are

(1.1)

E_{k}({\boldsymbol{x}})^{2}\geqslant E_{k-1}({\boldsymbol{x}})E_{k+1}({\boldsymbol{x}}),\quad k=1,2\cdots,n,\quad\forall\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

(1.2)

E_{1}({\boldsymbol{x}})\geqslant E_{2}({\boldsymbol{x}})^{1/2}\geqslant\cdots\geqslant E_{k}({\boldsymbol{x}})^{1/k},\quad\forall\boldsymbol{x}\in\Gamma_{k},

where
\Gamma_{k}
is the Gårding’s cone

\Gamma_{k}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}|\quad\sigma_{m}>0,\quad m=1,2,\cdots,k\}.

All of the above inequalities are strict unless

x_{1}=\cdots=x_{n}
, or both sides are
0
at the same time. Over the
years, numerous scholars have contributed numerous generalizations
of Newton-Maclaurin type inequalities, including those presented in

[
3
,
16
,
18
,
21
]
.

The Newton-Maclaurin inequalities play important roles in deriving
theoretical result for fully nonlinear partial differential
equations and geometric analysis. There are many important results
that need to use the Newton-Maclaurin inequalities, such as

[

,
6

-Hessian

\displaystyle\sigma_{k}(\lambda(u_{ij}))

\displaystyle=f(x,u,\nabla u),

\displaystyle\sigma_{k}(\kappa(X))

\displaystyle=f(X,\nu)

differential equations and geometric analysis. Their left-hand side

k
-Hessian operator

is the primary symmetric function
about the eigenvalues
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n})
of the
Hessian matrix
(u_{ij})

\kappa=(\kappa_{1},\cdots,\kappa_{n})

The Newton-Maclaurin inequalities can be perceived as an intrinsic
characteristic of the
k
-Hessian operator. If the operators
situated on the left-hand side of the aforementioned equations can
establish a corresponding Newton-Maclaurin style inequality, it will
naturally facilitate further research on the equation. A natural
question is whether the Newton-Maclaurin type inequalities still
hold for the operators of linear combinations of these primary
symmetric functions? In a recent paper, we obtained the following
Newton-Maclaurin type inequalities for operators
E_{k}+\alpha E_{k-1}

in

For any real number
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
and any

\boldsymbol{x}=(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

[E_{k}(\boldsymbol{x})+\alpha E_{k-1}(\boldsymbol{x})]^{2}\geqslant[E_{k-1}(\boldsymbol{x})+\alpha E_{k-2}(\boldsymbol{x})][E_{k+1}(\boldsymbol{x})+\alpha E_{k}(\boldsymbol{x})],\quad k=2\cdots,n-1.

Further, if
\alpha\geqslant 0
and

E_{j}(\boldsymbol{x})+\alpha E_{j-1}(\boldsymbol{x})\geqslant 0,\quad j=1\cdots,k,

[E_{1}(\boldsymbol{x})+\alpha]\geqslant[E_{2}(\boldsymbol{x})+\alpha E_{1}(\boldsymbol{x})]^{\frac{1}{2}}\geqslant\cdots\geqslant[E_{k}(\boldsymbol{x})+\alpha E_{k-1}(\boldsymbol{x})]^{\frac{1}{k}}.

A natural question is that if the Newton-Maclaurin type inequality
still holds for the functions which obtained by linear combination
of general primary symmestric functions. That is, for

\alpha=(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n},x=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n+1})\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}

(1.5)

\left(\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}\alpha_{k}E_{k}(x)\right)^{2}\geqslant\left(\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}\alpha_{k}E_{k-1}(x)\right)\left(\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}\alpha_{k}E_{k+1}(x)\right).

A counterexample was given in
[

1.5

\alpha

1.5

In this paper, we consider the Newton-Maclaurin type inequalities
for operators of three elementary symmetric functions
[E_{k}(\boldsymbol{x})+aE_{k-1}(\boldsymbol{x})+bE_{k-2}(\boldsymbol{x})]

S_{k}(\boldsymbol{x}):=E_{k}(\boldsymbol{x})+(\alpha+\beta)E_{k-1}(\boldsymbol{x})+\alpha\beta E_{k-2}(\boldsymbol{x}),

where
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}

and
\boldsymbol{x}=(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

Theorem 1
.

For any real
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
and any
\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we have

(1.6)

\displaystyle S_{k}^{2}(\boldsymbol{x})\geqslant S_{k-1}(\boldsymbol{x})S_{k+1}(\boldsymbol{x}),\quad k=3,4,\cdots,n-1.

The inequality is strict unless
x_{1}=x_{2}=\cdots=x_{n},

or unless both sides of the inequality are
0
, or unless

(1.7)

\frac{S_{k}(\boldsymbol{x})}{S_{k-1}(\boldsymbol{x})}=\frac{S_{k+1}(\boldsymbol{x})}{S_{k}(\boldsymbol{x})}=-\alpha=-\beta.

Further, if
\alpha,\beta\geqslant 0,E_{1}(\boldsymbol{x})\geqslant 0,E_{2}(\boldsymbol{x})\geqslant 0
, and

S_{m}(\boldsymbol{x})\geqslant 0,\quad{\rm for~~all}~~m=3,4\cdots,k.

Then

(1.8)

S_{1}(\boldsymbol{x})\geqslant S_{2}^{1/2}(\boldsymbol{x})\geqslant\cdots\geqslant S_{k}^{1/k}(\boldsymbol{x}),\quad k=2,3,\cdots,n.

Remark 2
.

There is a special case when the equation (
1.7
) holds: when

\alpha=\beta
and there are
n-2
elements in
x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{n}

take the value
-\alpha
.

is a generalization of classical
Newton-Maclaurin inequality and Newton-Maclaurin type inequality in

]

\alpha\geqslant 0,E_{1}(\boldsymbol{x})\geqslant 0,E_{2}(\boldsymbol{x})\geqslant 0,\cdots,E_{s}(\boldsymbol{x})\geqslant 0
.

Now we prove (
3.8
). Let
1\leqslant k<s
, by the define of

S_{k;s}
in (
1.10
),

\displaystyle S_{k;s}(\boldsymbol{z})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{k}C^{i}_{s}\alpha^{i}E_{k-i}(\boldsymbol{z}),

\displaystyle S_{k-1;s}(\boldsymbol{z})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}C^{i}_{s}\alpha^{i}E_{k-1-i}(\boldsymbol{z}),

\displaystyle S_{k+1;s}(\boldsymbol{z})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{k+1}C^{i}_{s}\alpha^{i}E_{k+1-i}(\boldsymbol{z}).

From the expansion above, we only need to show that all the terms
containing
\alpha
at left hand sides of (
3.8
) are
nonnegative. The coefficient of the term
\alpha^{b}
is

\displaystyle L_{b}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j=b\\
0\leqslant i\leqslant k\\
0\leqslant j\leqslant k\end{subarray}}C_{s}^{i}C_{s}^{j}E_{k-i}E_{k-j}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p+q=b\\
0\leqslant p\leqslant k-1\\
0\leqslant q\leqslant k+1\end{subarray}}C_{s}^{p}C_{s}^{q}E_{k-1-p}E_{k+1-q}

\displaystyle:=L_{b}^{1}-L_{b}^{2}.

Obviously, from the expression of
L_{b}
above, we have

L_{0}=E_{k}^{2}-E_{k-1}E_{k+1}\geqslant 0,\quad L_{2k}=C_{s}^{k}C_{s}^{k}-C_{s}^{k-1}C_{s}^{k+1}\geqslant 0.

and

\displaystyle L_{k}=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{k}C_{s}^{i}C_{s}^{k-i}E_{k-i}E_{i}-\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}C_{s}^{i}C_{s}^{k-i}E_{k-i-1}E_{i+1}

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}(C_{s}^{i}C_{s}^{k-i}E_{k-i}E_{i}-C_{s}^{i}C_{s}^{k-i}E_{k-i-1}E_{i+1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{[k]/2}(C_{s}^{i}C_{s}^{k-i}E_{k-i}E_{i}+C_{s}^{k-1-i}C_{s}^{i+1}E_{i+1}E_{k-1-i}

\displaystyle-C_{s}^{i}C_{s}^{k-i}E_{k-i-1}E_{i+1}-C_{s}^{k-1-i}C_{s}^{i+1}E_{i}E_{k-i})

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{[k]/2}R_{i}.

In the second equal sign above, we used a summation method from both
ends to the middle for
i
. Since
i\leqslant[k]/2
, which implies that

k-i\geqslant i
. If
k-i>i
,

E_{i+1}E_{k-1-i}\geqslant E_{i}E_{k-i},\quad C_{s}^{k-1-i}C_{s}^{i+1}\geqslant C_{s}^{i}C_{s}^{k-i},

we get
R_{i}\geqslant 0
. If
k-i=i
,

R_{i}=C_{s}^{i}C_{s}^{i}E_{i}^{2}+C_{s}^{i-1}C_{s}^{i+1}E_{i+1}E_{i-1}-C_{s}^{i}C_{s}^{i}E_{i-1}E_{i+1}-C_{s}^{i-1}C_{s}^{i+1}E_{i}^{2}\geqslant 0.

So we proved
L_{k}\geqslant 0
.

When
0<b<k
,

\displaystyle L_{b}^{1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{b}C_{s}^{i}C_{s}^{b-i}E_{k-i}E_{k-b+i},

\displaystyle L_{b}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{b}C_{s}^{i}C_{s}^{b-i}E_{k-i-1}E_{k-b+i+1}.

Using the similar method to prove that
L_{k}\geqslant 0
, we can get

L_{b}=L_{b}^{1}-L_{b}^{2}\geqslant 0.

When
k<b<2k
,

\displaystyle L_{b}^{1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=b-k}^{k}C_{s}^{i}C_{s}^{b-i}E_{k-i}E_{k-b+i},

\displaystyle L_{b}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{p=b-(k+1)}^{k-1}C_{s}^{p}C_{s}^{b-p}E_{k-p-1}E_{k-b+p+1}=\sum_{i=b-k}^{k}C_{s}^{i-1}C_{s}^{b-i+1}E_{k-i}E_{k-b+i}.

The proof of
L_{b}\geqslant 0
is also similar as
L_{k}
.

Thus we obtain (
3.8
), and the Theorem
4
is proved.

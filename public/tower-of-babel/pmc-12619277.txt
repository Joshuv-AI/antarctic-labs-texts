Construction of a prediction model for perioperative blood transfusion in elderly hip fracture patients: a retrospective study
Authors: Junhao Zeng, Liyang Liu, Yugang Yuan, Ge Zhu, Xuehua Su, Xingrui Gong, Cao Cui
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ObjectivesPerioperative blood transfusion in elderly patients is associated with various complications. This study aimed to develop a clinical prediction model for intraoperative blood transfusion in elderly patients undergoing hip fracture surgery to minimize transfusion requirements and related risks.MethodPatients who underwent hip surgery between January 2021 and December 2023 at Xiangyang Central Hospital (Hubei, China) were retrospectively included in this study. Relevant factors for postoperative blood transfusion were analyzed using univariate and multivariate logistic regression analysis. A nomogram was established with the identified relevant factors, and its performance was evaluated using the area under the subject operating characteristic curve, calibration curve, and decision curve analysis.ResultA total of 1092 patients were included in this study for evaluation. Through logistic regression analysis, we identified surgery type, preoperative hemoglobin level, platelet count, osteoporosis, and cerebrovascular disease as relevant factors for postoperative blood transfusion. The nomogram was established, and the areas under the curve of the subjects’ work characteristics were found to be 0.730 (95% confidence interval [CI]: 0.686–0.773) and 0.726 (95% CI: 0.671–0.781) for the training and test sets, respectively.ConclusionIn this study, we identified surgery type, preoperative hemoglobin level, platelet count, osteoporosis, and cerebrovascular disease as the risk factors for postoperative blood transfusion in elderly hip fracture patients. The nomogram, established based on these risk factors, showed an acceptable discriminative ability to predict intraoperative blood transfusion in elderly patients during hip fracture surgery.Trial registrationThis clinical trial was registered at http://www.chictr.org.cn/ (ChiCTR2400085204, June 03, 2024).

Introduction

With demographic aging, the prevalence of hip fractures in elderly patients continues to rise (Bhandari and Swiontkowski 2017). In China, over one million new cases are reported annually, making hip fractures a major public health concern (Bian et al. 2021). Surgical intervention is the principal treatment modality for hip fractures, and mortality rates may approach 80% in cases where timely surgical intervention is not achieved (Chen et al. 2023). Perioperative anemia is common among hip fracture patients. Previous studies have demonstrated that during hip fracture surgery, the total blood loss often exceeds 1000 mL (Ding et al. 2020), and perioperative transfusion rates can reach up to 56% (Dubin et al. 2024). While transfusions can effectively correct anemia and improve oxygen delivery, they are associated with potential adverse effects, including immune-mediated reactions, febrile non-hemolytic responses, infectious disease transmission, coagulation disturbances, hypothermia, and, in severe cases, death (Guo et al. 2024). Furthermore, the current clinical blood supply faces severe shortages, making perioperative blood deficiency a prevalent phenomenon (Kennedy and Meek 2024).

Clinical prediction models have become important tools in preoperative settings. They help identify high-risk individuals and support precision medicine based on precise prediction (Liu et al. 2021; 2025). Recently, Wang et al. (Maheta et al. 2024) developed a predictive model for postoperative transfusion in femoral neck fracture surgery based on data from 178 patients. Another study investigated the risk factors and developed a predictive model based on data from 414 patients (Pavesi et al. 2011). However, these studies were limited by small sample sizes. Another study included 820 patients and collected data over an 8-year period (Li n.d.), during which significant advancements in anesthesia and surgical techniques may have occurred. Therefore, we hypothesized that intraoperative blood transfusion could be well predicted by incorporating multivariables. We aimed to develop a short-term, large sample size clinical prediction model to enhance the prediction of intraoperative blood transfusion needs and facilitate early blood-saving strategies to minimize transfusion requirements and related risks in elderly hip fracture patients.

Methods

The study strictly followed the ethical requirements of the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments and was reviewed and approved by the Ethics Committee of the Xiangyang Central Hospital affiliated with Hubei University of Arts and Sciences (Hubei, China). This retrospective study was conducted at Xiangyang Central Hospital (Hubei, China). All the participating patients were exempted from written informed consent, and their personally identifiable information was removed before data analysis to ensure patient privacy and data security. The results were reported following the STROBE guidelines. The clinical trial was registered at http://www.chictr.org.cn/ (ChiCTR2400085204) on June 03, 2024.

The inclusion criteria for this study were defined as follows: patients aged ≥ 65 years, presenting with low-energy femoral neck or intertrochanteric fractures, who underwent definitive surgical intervention at our institution between January 2021 and December 2023. Patients with hip fractures resulting from low-energy trauma were also included. Low-energy trauma refers to a fall from a standing height or less, while high-energy trauma refers to motor vehicle accidents, falls from height, or sports-related injuries. All procedures were non-emergent (elective) and performed after adequate preoperative evaluation and optimization. Patients with malignant tumors, multiple fractures, or pathological fractures were excluded from this study.

Patient information included demographic information (age, sex, and body mass index [BMI]), drinking status, smoking status, American Society of Anesthesiologists physical status classification, New York Heart Association functional classification, disease history (cerebrovascular disease; cerebral infraction or hemorrhage; cardiovascular disease; lung disease; liver disease; kidney disease; diabetes; hypertension; osteoporosis; and preoperative deep vein thrombosis, constipation, atrial fibrillation, and preoperative lung infection), previous surgeries (neurosurgery and cardiac surgery), surgical information (type of surgery, duration of surgery, intraoperative bleeding, anesthesia, and perioperative concentrated red blood cell [RBC] transfusions), postoperative characteristics (intensive care unit admission, surgical site infections, lung infections, and urinary tract infections), and laboratory findings (white blood cell count, hemoglobin [Hb], platelet count [PLT], total bilirubin, albumin [ALB], creatinine, D-dimer, and ejection fraction). A drinker was defined as someone who consumed > 500 mL/week or > 30 g/day of alcohol (40% vol). A smoker was defined as someone who had smoked continuously or cumulatively for > 6 months.

RBC transfusions were administered strictly in accordance with the transfusion protocol. For hemodynamically stable patients, transfusion was typically considered only when Hb levels fell below 80 g/L. However, in exceptional clinical circumstances, including cases with 80–100 g/L Hb levels accompanied by hemodynamic instability (such as heart failure, prior cerebrovascular disease, or cardiovascular disease), transfusion decisions were made at the clinician’s discretion following a comprehensive evaluation of the patient’s condition. When a patient’s condition deviated from the protocol, the decision was made after discussion between the anesthesiologist and the surgeon.

Sample size calculation

The sample size selected for model establishment met the standard of 10 events per variable (Riley et al. 2020). According to the rule of 10 events per variable, the incidence of blood transfusion during hip fracture surgery is approximately 30%. We hypothesized that five risk factors would be identified in our study; therefore, it was estimated that a minimum of 150 patients would be needed.

Statistical analysis

Quantitative data were summarized as mean ± standard deviation (SD) if they were normally distributed and as median (interquartile spacing, IQR) if they were not normally distributed. The count data were then presented directly in numerical form. Statistical analysis was performed using the t-test for normally distributed quantitative data and the Mann–Whitney U-test for continuous data. The count data were analyzed using the chi-squared (ꭓ2) test. To ensure data completeness and availability, missing data were imputed using the mean.

The data were randomly divided into training and test sets in a 6:4 ratio. The data in the training set were used for the construction of the nomogram model, while the data in the test set were used for the validation of the constructed nomogram model to assess its reliability. Univariate and multivariate logistic regression analyses were used to select covariates from a wide range of risk factors that may affect blood transfusion in elderly hip fracture patients. Variables with a p-value of < 0.05 in the univariate logistic regression analysis were included in the multivariate logistic regression analysis to further investigate the relationship between the factors.

The nomogram was constructed based on multivariate logistic regression analysis using the R software (v4.2.1, USA). The model’s ability to discriminate in predicting the need for blood transfusion was assessed by generating a receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve and computing the area under the curve (AUC). In general, an AUC of 0.6–0.75 indicates an acceptable level of model discrimination, 0.75–0.9 indicates good model discrimination, and > 0.9 indicates excellent model discrimination (Liu, et al., 2025). When constructing the nomogram prediction model, the relevant variables were first screened by one-way logistic regression analysis and then analyzed by multi-factor logistic regression analysis. The sensitivity analysis between actual probability and predicted probability was performed using the boot method and the Hosmer–Lemeshow test.

Results

Baseline characteristics

A total of 1100 patients were screened in this study, of which eight patients were excluded because their information could not be identified in the electronic database. The 1092 patients included in the analysis were divided into training and test groups in a 6:4 ratio, with 655 patients in the training group and 437 patients in the test group.

In the training set, the transfusion group was compared with the non-transfusion group. The results revealed that the incidence of cardiovascular and cerebrovascular diseases was higher, BMI was slightly higher (p = 0.012), and blood loss was significantly higher (p = 0.001; Table 1) in the transfusion group than in the non-transfusion group. Identification of relevant factors by univariate and multivariate logistic regression

Univariate logistic regression analysis showed that the following 13 factors were associated with blood transfusion (p < 0.05): age, BMI, cerebrovascular disease (including cerebral infarction and hemorrhage), pulmonary disease, osteoporosis, renal disease, preoperative lower limb thrombosis, cardiac function class, Hb level, PLT level, ALB level, anesthesia manner, and type of surgery. Multifactorial analysis of these factors revealed that surgery type, Hb level, osteoporosis, PLT level, and cerebrovascular disease were associated with postoperative blood transfusion (p < 0.05; Table 2). Nomogram establishment and performance evaluation

A logistic regression algorithm was used to construct a blood transfusion prediction calculator. The calculator had a score range of 0–100, and it assigned a score to each variable and calculated the total score. On this basis, a new column-line graph was constructed to assess the probability of blood transfusion after hip fracture surgery (Fig. 1). The AUC of the working characteristics of the subjects was 0.730 (Fig. 2A) for the training set and 0.726 (Fig. 2B) for the test set, suggesting that the model exhibited an acceptable discriminative ability. The calibration curve showed good agreement between the actual observed and predicted probabilities of the model in the training (Fig. 2C) and test (Fig. 2D) sets. The decision curve analysis showed that the model was clinically useful for threshold probabilities within 0.1–0.8, both in the training (Fig. 2E) and test (Fig. 2F) sets. The boot test results showed good concordance in the calibration plot (Fig. 3). Discussion

Our findings revealed that the following five independent factors were associated with postoperative blood transfusion: surgery type, Hb level, PLT level, osteoporosis, and cerebrovascular disease. The nomogram, based on these five risk factors, exhibited acceptable discriminative ability and could assist surgeons and anesthesiologists in identifying high-risk patients, optimizing perioperative management, and improving the efficiency of blood product allocation in clinical settings.

Preoperative Hb level is a critical determinant of the need for blood transfusion. Numerous studies have shown that patients with preoperative Hb levels below 10 g/dL have a significantly increased risk of requiring perioperative blood transfusions (Shokoohi et al. 2012). The anemia observed in elderly populations typically results from several causes, including malnutrition, chronic diseases, declining bone marrow activity, and impaired erythropoietin production. Patients with low Hb levels have significantly insufficient physiological compensatory capacity to handle blood loss (Wang et al. 2020). Thus, anemia should be corrected for the safety of elderly patients prior to surgery. This can be achieved through interventions such as iron therapy, erythropoietin administration, or targeted nutritional supplements, which may reduce the need for blood transfusions during and post operation (Xiao et al. 2023).

Osteoporosis is a very common complication in the elderly patient population and places high demands on the surgical skills of the operator (Yu et al. 2023). The complexity of hip surgery extends the operation time and increases incision exposure, surgical trauma, and the exposure of blood vessels and tissues, thereby significantly elevating the risk of intraoperative bleeding. Additionally, in osteoporotic patients, pathological changes such as angiogenesis in the bones can make newly formed blood vessels more vulnerable to damage during procedures such as skin/muscle incision, bone drilling, and device implantation, often leading to excessive intraoperative blood loss (Zhang et al. 2020). Thus, elderly patients with osteoporosis should be alert to shorten the surgery duration with good surgical technique.

Our study identified that total hip replacement surgery resulted in a higher incidence of blood transfusion. Moreover, total hip surgery employed more complex procedures, created a larger operative field, and required longer surgery duration than other surgeries, consequently resulting in greater blood loss.

Patients with cerebrovascular disease face significant intraoperative risks of ischemia and recurrent stroke. The elderly patients exhibit markedly reduced tolerance to anemia and hemorrhage compared with the younger patients, necessitating heightened anesthetic vigilance regarding hematological status. To mitigate perioperative stroke risk, anesthesiologists often opt for transfusion therapy to optimize oxygen delivery and cerebral perfusion.

Our study identified that low PLT count is a protective factor against blood transfusion, consistent with a previous study (Zhu et al. 2022). When the PLT count is insufficient, PLTs are unable to rapidly and effectively form enough platelet aggregates at the site of vascular injury to achieve the initial physical blockage, thereby disrupting the initiation and amplification of the entire hemostatic process. Consequently, low PLT count can result in a higher incidence of blood transfusion.

Compared with previous studies, our model was based on a larger and more recent dataset with clearly defined variables. The AUCs for the training and validation cohorts were 0.730 and 0.726, respectively, suggesting an acceptable discriminative ability. The use of easily obtainable clinical variables enhances the practical applicability of this tool for clinical use. Notably, this prediction model can effectively guide preoperative risk stratification. High-risk patients may benefit from individualized care plans, such as preoperative anemia correction, blood conservation strategies, and enhanced intraoperative monitoring. This proactive approach can help reduce unnecessary transfusions and improve patient safety.

Our study included > 1000 patients; therefore, our sample size is sufficient to detect meaningful differences. We acknowledge the limitations of the “10 EPV” rule, but our study included a sample size that substantially exceeded this traditional heuristic, thus making it sufficient to detect meaningful differences. However, there are some limitations to this study. First, our study is retrospective, and some perioperative clinical characteristics, such as bone density, incision length, and postoperative drainage, could not be obtained. However, these issues can be addressed through a prospective study in the future. Additionally, our study is a single-center study, and future validation in other research centers is required.

Conclusion

In the present study, we found that surgery type, preoperative Hb level, PLT level, osteoporosis, and cerebrovascular disease are risk factors that affect postoperative blood transfusion. A nomogram based on these factors exhibited acceptable discriminative ability. The significant advantage of this model lies in its ability to transform complex regression equations into intuitive, clear, and easy-to-understand graphs, making the results of the prediction model more readable and of high practical value.

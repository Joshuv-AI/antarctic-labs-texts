Abstract
. We prove Vojta’s abc conjecture for projective space ℙ n ​ ( ℂ ) {\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}}) , assuming that the entire curves in ℙ n ​ ( ℂ ) {\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}}) are highly ramified over the coordinate hyperplanes. This extends the results of Guo Ji and the second-named author for the case n = 2 n=2 (see [ 9 ] ). We also explore the corresponding results for projective toric varieties. Consequently, we establish a version of Campana’s orbifold conjecture for finite coverings of projective toric varieties.



1.
Introduction

Let
X
be an algebraic variety and
D
be an effective divisor (possibly empty) on
X
. The set
X\backslash D
is
Brody hyperbolic

if every entire curve
f:\mathbb{C}\to X\backslash D
is constant. It is
Brody quasi-hyperbolic
if
every entire curve
f:\mathbb{C}\to X\backslash D
is degenerate, meaning its image is contained a proper Zariski-closed subset
Z
of
X
. If such a subset
Z
can be chosen independently of
f
, then

X\backslash D
is said to be
Brody strong quasi-hyperbolic
and
Z
is called an
exceptional set
. This leads us to the Griffiths-Lang’s conjecture.

Griffiths-Lang’s conjecture
.
Let
X
be a smooth complex projective variety,
D
a normal crossing divisor on
X
, and
K_{X}
a canonical divisor on
X
. If
K_{X}+D
is big, then
X\backslash D
is Brody quasi-hyperbolic.

A more precise quantitative version, in terms of the Nevanlinna’s functions, can be formulated as follows.

Griffiths’ conjecture
.
For a holomorphic map
f:\mathbb{C}\to X
with Zariski dense image, the inequality

T_{K_{X}+D,f}(r)\leq_{\rm exc}N_{f}(D,r)+\epsilon T_{A,f}(r)

holds for any
\epsilon>0
, where
A
is an ample divisor on
X
, and the notation

\leq_{\rm exc}
means the inequality holds for all
r\in(0,\infty)
except for a set of finite measure.

Here,
for each positive integer
n
,
N_{f}^{(n)}(D,r)
is the
n
-truncated counting function with respect to
D
given by

N_{f}^{(n)}(D,r)=\sum_{0<|z|<r}\mathop{\mathrm{min}}\{{\rm ord}_{z}f^{*}D,n\}\log\frac{r}{|z|}+\mathop{\mathrm{min}}\{{\rm ord}_{0}f^{*}D,n\}\log r,

N_{f}(D,r)
is the counting function with respect to
D
given by
N_{f}^{(\infty)}(D,r)
,
and

T_{D,f}(r)
is the (Nevanlinna) height function relative to the divisor
D
(referring to
[
27
, Section 12]
).

Paul Vojta formulated the following general abc conjecture (see
[
27
, Conjecture 15.2]
and
[
27
, Conjecture 23.4]
), which extends the above Griffiths’ conjecture. It is also
viewed as the quantitive version of Campana’s orbifold conjecture which will be stated later in the introduction.

Vojta’s abc conjecture
.
Let
X
be a smooth complex projective variety,
D
a normal crossing divisor on
X
, and
K_{X}
a canonical divisor on
X
.
For any
\epsilon>0
, there exists a proper Zariski-closed subset
Z
of
X
such that for any entire curve
f:\mathbb{C}\to X
whose image is not contained in
Z
, the inequality

T_{K_{X}+D,f}(r)\leq_{\rm exc}N_{f}^{(1)}(D,r)+\epsilon T_{A,f}(r)

holds for any
\epsilon>0
,
where
A
is an ample divisor on
X
.

Note that Vojta’s abc conjecture strengthens Griffiths’ conjecture in two key ways: (i) the exceptional set
Z
above is independent of the map

f
, and (ii) the counting function is truncated at level one. Proving these two features is quite challenging, even if the aforementioned version of Griffiths’ conjecture could be established. In this paper, we first prove Vojta’s abc conjecture for entire curves in
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
,
assuming the map is highly ramified over the coordinate hyperplanes. The precise statement of our main result is stated below. Additionally, we highlight that the exceptional set obtained in the theorem can be determined explicitly-this is a challenging issue in the study of Nevanlinna theory and complex hyperbolicity.

Main Theorem
.

Let
G
be a non-constant homogeneous polynomial in
\mathbb{C}[x_{0},\dots,x_{n}]
with no monomial factors and no repeated factors.
Assume that
[G=0]
is in general position with the coordinate hyperplanes
H_{i}=[x_{i}=0]
in
\mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{C})
,
0\leq i\leq n
.
Then, for any
\epsilon>0
, there exist an effectively computable positive integer
\ell
and a proper Zariski closed subset
Z
of
\mathbb{P}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
that can be explicitly determined, such that the following two inequalities hold:

(i)

N_{G(\mathbf{f})}(0,r)-N^{(1)}_{G(\mathbf{f})}(0,r)\leq_{\operatorname{exc}}\epsilon T_{f}(r)
, and

(ii)

(\deg G-\epsilon)T_{f}(r)\leq_{\operatorname{exc}}N^{(1)}_{G(\mathbf{f})}(0,r)
,

for any non-constant holomorphic map
f:{\mathbb{C}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})

whose image is not contained in
Z
, and where
{\rm mult}_{z_{0}}(f^{*}H_{i})\geq\ell
for all
i
with
0\leq i\leq n
and
for every
z_{0}\in{\mathbb{C}}
with
f(z_{0})\in H_{i}
.
Here, the evaluation of
G
is taken at
\mathbf{f}=(f_{0},\ldots,f_{n})
in a reduced form of
f
, meaning that
f_{0},\ldots,f_{n}
are entire functions without common zeros.

We note that this theorem corresponds to a case of Vojta’s abc conjecture for entire curves with
D=H_{0}+\cdots+H_{n}+[G=0]
, so that
K_{\mathbb{P}^{n}+D}
is linearly equivalent to
[G=0]
.
The case
n=2
was previously established by Ji Guo and the second named author (
[
9
, Theorem 1.4]
).
The new idea in this paper is motivated by
the recent paper
[
2
]
, where the authors considered the parabolic Riemann surface
\mathcal{Y}_{f}:=\mathbb{C}\setminus{f^{-1}(D)}
, reducing the problem to the omitting case. To achieve this in our setting, we need to extend the results in
[
7
]
and
[
9
]
to the parabolic setting.

We state some consequences of our Main Theorem, starting with the introduction of Campana’s orbifolds. Instead of considering the complement case in Brody’s hyperbolicity, we can also examine an entire curve
f:\mathbb{C}\to X
that is ramified over
D
. According to Campana
[
1
]
,
an
orbifold divisor
is defined as
\Delta:=\sum_{Y\subset X}(1-m_{\Delta}^{-1}(Y))\cdot Y
,
where
Y
ranges over all irreducible divisors of
X
, and
m_{\Delta}(Y)\in[1,\infty)\cap{\mathbb{Q}}
, with all but finitely many values equal to one.
An
orbifold entire curve

f:{\mathbb{C}}\rightarrow(X,\Delta)
, with
\Delta=\sum_{i}(1-m_{i}^{-1})D_{i}
, is a homomorphic map such that
f({\mathbb{C}})\not\subset\mbox{Supp}(\Delta)
and
{\rm mult}_{z_{0}}(f^{*}D_{i})\geq m_{i}
for all
i
and
for every
z_{0}\in{\mathbb{C}}
with
f(z_{0})\in D_{i}
.

Campana’s orbifold conjecture
.
If
(X,\Delta
) is an orbifold pair such that
K_{X}+\Delta
is big, then there exists a proper closed subvariety
Z\subset X
containing all the non-constant orbifold entire curves
f:{\mathbb{C}}\rightarrow(X,\Delta)
.

Remarks
.

(i)

The Campana’s conjecture is a consequence of the above Vojta’s abc conjecutre.

(ii)

Given an orbifold divisor
\Delta
on
X
, let
D:={\rm Supp}(\Delta)=D_{1}+\cdots+D_{q}
.
Recall that the pair
(X,D)
is said to be of
log-general type
if the divisor
K_{X}+D
is big.
It is clear that
(X,D)
is of log-general type if
(X,\Delta)
is of general type, i.e.
K_{X}+\Delta
is big.
On the other hand, the condition that
(X,D)
is of log-general type implies that
(X,\Delta)
is of general type if
(X,\Delta)
has a sufficiently large multiplicity along each
D_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq q
.
(
See
[
11
, Corollary 2.2.24]
.
)

The Main Theorem leads to the following two theorems concerning Campana’s orbifold conjecture; however, we will not include the proofs, as they can be adapted from those in
[
9
]
for the case
n=2
. We begin with the result concerning
n+2
components.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\Delta_{0}
be an orbifold divisor on
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
and let
H_{0},H_{1},\dots,H_{n}
be the coordinate hyperplanes in
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
, such that
\Delta_{0}
and
H_{0},H_{1},\dots,H_{n}
are in general position. Let
m_{i}\in(1,\infty)\cap{\mathbb{Q}}
,

0\leq i\leq n
, and
\Delta=\Delta_{0}+(1-{1\over m_{0}})H_{0}+(1-{1\over m_{1}})H_{1}+\cdots+(1-{1\over m_{n}})H_{n}
. Assume that
\deg\Delta>n+1
.
Then there exist a proper Zariski closed subset
W
of
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
and an effectively computable positive integer
\ell
such that the image of any orbifold entire curve
f:{\mathbb{C}}\rightarrow({\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}}),\Delta)
with
\mathop{\mathrm{min}}\{m_{0},m_{1},\dots,m_{n}\}\geq\ell
must be contained in
W
.

The next case involves
n+1
components, not all are hyperplanes, in
\mathbb{P}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
.

Theorem 2
.

Let

F_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq n+1
, be homogeneous irreducible polynomials of positive degrees in
\mathbb{C}[x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}]
.
Assume that
D_{i}:=[F_{i}=0]\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{C})
,
1\leq i\leq n+1
, intersect transversally. Let
\Delta=(1-{1\over m_{1}})D_{1}+\cdots+(1-{1\over m_{n+1}})D_{n+1}
be an orbifold divisor of
\mathbb{P}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
. Assume that
\deg\Delta>n+1
.
Then, there exist an effectively computable positive integer
\ell
and a proper Zariski closed subset
W
of
\mathbb{P}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
such that the image of any orbifold entire curve
f:{\mathbb{C}}\rightarrow({\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}}),\Delta)
with
\mathop{\mathrm{min}}\{m_{1},\dots,m_{n+1}\}\geq\ell
must be contained in
W
.

Note that
\mathbb{P}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
is a toric variety when we identify
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}^{n}
with
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})\backslash\cup_{i=0}^{n}H_{i}
, where
H_{0},\dots,H_{n}
are the
n+1
coordinate hyperplanes. A toric variety
X
is defined as a variety over
{\mathbb{C}}
that contains
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}^{n}

as a dense open subvariety, with the action of
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}^{n}
on itself extends to an algebraic
action of
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}^{n}
on
X
. We refer to
[
5
]
for more details on toric varieties. Using the arguments in
[
12
]
and
[
6
]
, we can extend our Main Theorem to projective toric varieties.
Recall from
[
12
]
that an “admissible pair” is a couple
(X,V)
where
V
is a nonsingular variety embedded in a nonsingular projective variety
X
in such a way that
D_{0}=X\setminus V
is a normal crossings divisor. A Campana orbifold
(X,\Delta)
is said to be
associated to the admissible pair
(X,V)
if
D_{0}
is the support of
\Delta
.

Theorem 3
.

Let
X
be a nonsingular projective toric variety and
let
D
be an effective reduced divisor on the admissible pair
(X,\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n})
, such that
D
is in general position with the boundary
D_{0}=X\setminus\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}
. Let
A
be a big divisor on
X
.
Then, for every
\epsilon>0
, there is a positive integer
\ell
and a proper Zariski-closed subset
Z
of
X
such that, for any Campana orbifold
(X,\Delta)
associated to the admissible pair
(X,\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n})
with multiplicities at least
\ell
along the boundary
D_{0}
, the following two inequalities hold:

(i)

N_{f}(D,r)-N_{f}^{(1)}(D,r)\leq_{\rm exc}\epsilon T_{A,f}(r)
, and

(ii)

T_{D,f}(r)\leq_{\rm exc}N_{f}^{(1)}(D,r)+\epsilon T_{A,f}(r)

for any non-constant entire orbifold curve
f
on
(X,\Delta)
such that the image of
f
is not contained in
Z
.

Theorem
3
implies Campana’s conjecture for an orbifold pair that is a finite covering of a toric variety, as follows.

Theorem 4
.

Let
X
be a nonsingular projective toric variety, and let
D_{0}=X\setminus\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}
.
Let
Y
be a nonsingular complex projective variety with a finite morphism
\pi:Y\to X
.
Let
H:=\pi^{*}(D_{0})
and
R\subset Y
the ramification divisor of
\pi
omitting components from the support of
H
.
Assume that
\pi(R)
and
D_{0}
are in general position on
X
. Then there exists a positive integer
\ell
such that, if the orbifold pair
(Y,\Delta)
is of general type with
{\rm Supp}(\Delta)={\rm Supp}(H)
and multiplicity at least
\ell
for each component, then there exists a proper Zariski closed subset
W
of
Y
such that the image of any non-constant orbifold entire curve
f:{\mathbb{C}}\rightarrow(Y,\Delta)
must be contained in
W
.

2.
Motivation through the rational case

In
[
7
]
, a version of Vojta’s abc conjecture for algebraic tori over algebraic function fields is established. As we will see, the algebraic reduction method presented in
[
7
]
is a key technical component in proving
our Main Theorem. The core idea behind the proof is also inspired by the function field approach. In this section, we will prove a version of the Main Theorem for rational functions and explain the role of parabolic Riemann surfaces in this context.

2.1.
The Rational Case

The statements and arguments apply verbatim to algebraic function fields (see also
[
6
, Theorem 1.13]
).
Here, let
K=\mathbb{C}(t)
be the function field of
\mathbb{P}^{1}({\mathbb{C}})
. Let
S
be a finite subset of
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{C})
. We denote by
{\mathcal{O}}_{S}^{*}
the set of
S
-units, which consists of rational functions with no zeros and poles outside of
S
. For a rational function
f=\frac{P}{Q}
, where
P
and
Q
are coprime polynomials, we define
\deg f:=\max\{\deg P,\deg Q\}
and denote by
{\rm ord}_{\mathbf{p}}^{+}(f)
the order of the zero of
f
at
\mathbf{p}\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{C})
.
We use the notation
N_{S}(f)
(respectively
N_{S}^{(1)}(f)
) to count the number of zeros of
f
with multiplicity (respectively, without multiplicity) outside of
S
. When
S
is empty, we simply write
N(f)
, which equals
\deg f
, to count the number of zeros with multiplicity on
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{C})
;
N^{(1)}(f)
denotes the same count without multiplicity.

Recall a simplified version of the main technical theorem in
[
7
]
.

Theorem 5

(
[
7
, Theorem 4]
)
.

Let
G\in\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
be a non-constant homogeneous polynomial with neither monomial factors nor repeated factors.
Let
S
be a finite subset of
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{C})
. Then, for any
\epsilon>0
, there exist a positive real number
c_{0}
and
a proper Zariski closed subset
Z
of
\mathbb{A}^{n}
such that, for all
\mathbf{u}:=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{n})\in({\mathcal{O}}_{S}^{*})^{n}\setminus Z
, we have either

(i)

the inequality

\displaystyle\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\{\deg(u_{i})\}\leq c_{0}\max\{1,|S|-2\}

holds,

(ii)

or the following two statements hold

(a)

N_{S}(G(\mathbf{u}))-N_{S}^{(1)}(G(\mathbf{u}))\leq\epsilon\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\{\deg u_{i}\}
if
G(u_{1},\ldots,u_{n})\neq 0
.

(b)

If
G(0,\ldots,0)\neq 0
and
\deg_{X_{i}}G=\deg G=d
for
1\leq i\leq n
, then

\displaystyle N_{S}^{(1)}(G(\mathbf{u}))\geq(\deg G-\epsilon)\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\{\deg u_{i}\}.

Here
c_{0}
can be effectively bounded from above in terms of
\epsilon
,
n
, and the degree of
G
. Moreover, the exceptional set
Z
can be expressed as the zero locus of a finite set

\Sigma\subset\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
with the following properties:
(Z1)

\Sigma
depends on
\epsilon
and
G
but independent of
S
and can be determined explicitly and
(Z2)

|\Sigma|
and the degree of each polynomial in
\Sigma
can be effectively bounded from above in terms of
\epsilon
,
n
, and the degree of
G
.

The construction of the set of polynomials
\Sigma
follows from the specialization lemmas
[
7
, Lemma 15 and 16]
. In particular, it is evident that
\Sigma
is independent of the choice of
S
. Note that
\mathbf{u}:=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{n})\in({\mathcal{O}}_{S}^{*})^{n}\setminus Z
means that
F(\mathbf{u})\neq 0
(a nontrivial function and hence not identically zero) for every
F\in\Sigma
. This formulation is closely analogous to the number field case.
Alternatively, one may view
\mathbf{u}
as a morphism:
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{C})\setminus S\to\mathbb{C}^{n}
; then the condition
F(\mathbf{u})\neq 0
for every
F\in\Sigma
is equivalent to saying that the image
\mathbf{u}(\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{C})\setminus S)
is not contained in
Z
.

We will demonstrate how to apply the above theorem to show the following version of Main Theorem for rational case.

Theorem 6
.

Let
G
be a non-constant homogeneous polynomial in
\mathbb{C}[x_{0},\cdots,x_{n}]
with no monomial factors and no repeated factors.
Assume
[G=0]
is in general position with the coordinate hyperplanes
H_{i}=[x_{i}=0]
,
0\leq i\leq n
.
Then, for any
\epsilon>0
, there exist effectively computable positive integers
\ell
and a proper Zariski closed subset
Z
of
\mathbb{P}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
such that the following two inequalities hold:

(i)

N(G(\mathbf{f}))-N^{(1)}(G(\mathbf{f}))\leq\epsilon\deg\mathbf{f}
, and

(ii)

N^{(1)}(G(\mathbf{f}))\geq(\deg G-\epsilon)\deg\mathbf{f}
,

for any non-constant entire rational curve
\mathbf{f}=(f_{0},\ldots,f_{n})
, where
f_{0},\ldots,f_{n}\in\mathbb{C}[z]
are polynomials without common zeros, provided that
\mathbf{f}(\mathbb{C})\not\subset Z
and that the following condition is satisfied:
for each
0\leq i\leq n
and
\mathbf{p}\in\mathbb{P}^{1}({\mathbb{C}})
, if
{\rm ord}_{\mathbf{p}}(f_{i})>0
, then

{\rm ord}_{\mathbf{p}}(f_{i})\geq\ell
.
Here,
\deg\mathbf{f}=\max\{\deg f_{0},\ldots,\deg f_{n}\}
.

In addition to Theorem
5
, we also require the following result to prove Theorem
6
.

Proposition 7

(
[
7
, Corollary 6]
)
.

Let
G=\sum_{\mathbf{i}\in I_{G}}a_{\mathbf{i}}{\mathbf{x}}^{\mathbf{i}}\in\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
be a non-constant polynomial, where
a_{\mathbf{i}}\neq 0
if
\mathbf{i}\in I_{G}
. Assume that
G(0,\dots,0)\neq 0
. Let
Z
be the Zariski closed subset that is the union of hypersurfaces of
\mathbb{A}^{n}
of the form
\sum_{\mathbf{i}\in I}a_{\mathbf{i}}{\mathbf{x}}^{\mathbf{i}}=0
where

.
Let
S
be a finite subset of
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{C})

for all
(u_{1},\ldots,u_{n})\in(\mathcal{O}_{S}^{*})^{n}\setminus Z

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{p}\in S}{\rm ord}_{\mathbf{p}}^{+}(G(u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}))\leq\tilde{c}\max\{1,|S|-2\}

The proof of Theorem
6
(i) can be outline as the follows.
Given a rational map
\mathbf{f}=(f_{0},\ldots,f_{n})
, where
f_{0},\ldots,f_{n}\in\mathbb{C}[z]
are polynomials without common zeros, we define a set
S_{\mathbf{f}}
consisting of the zeros of all
f_{i}
. With this choice, each coordinate function
f_{i}
becomes an
S_{\mathbf{f}}
-unit, so that Theorem
5
(ii) can be applied to estimate the multiplicities of the zeros of
G(\mathbf{f})
outside
S_{\mathbf{f}}
. We then use Proposition
7
to estimate the zeros of
G(\mathbf{f})
inside
S_{\mathbf{f}}
.
Crucially, the constants and exceptional sets appearing in Theorem
5
and Proposition
7
do not depend on
S
, allowing this method to be used for all rational maps.

Proof of Theorem
6
.

Denote by
\ell
a sufficiently large integer to be determined later.
Let
\mathbf{f}=(f_{0},\ldots,f_{n})
, where
f_{0},\ldots,f_{n}\in\mathbb{C}[z]
are polynomials without common zeros.
By the assumption, for each
0\leq i\leq n
and
\mathbf{p}\in\mathbb{P}^{1}({\mathbb{C}})
, if
{\rm ord}_{\mathbf{p}}(f_{i})>0
, then

{\rm ord}_{\mathbf{p}}(f_{i})\geq\ell
.
Let

(2.1)

\displaystyle\tilde{S}_{\mathbf{f}}:=\{\mathbf{p}\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{C})\,|\,{\rm ord}_{\mathbf{p}}(f_{i})>0\quad\text{for some $0\leq i\leq n$}\}\cup\{\infty\}.

Then

(2.2)

\displaystyle|\tilde{S}_{\mathbf{f}}|\leq\sum_{i=0}^{n}N_{S}^{(1)}(f_{i})+1\leq\frac{n+1}{\ell}\max_{0\leq i\leq n}\{\deg f_{i}\}+1,

and
f_{i}\in\mathcal{O}_{\tilde{S}_{\mathbf{f}}}^{*}
for
0\leq i\leq n
.
Let
\widetilde{G}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}):=G(1,x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
and
u_{i}=\frac{f_{i}}{f_{0}}\in\mathcal{O}_{\tilde{S}_{\mathbf{f}}}^{*}
,
1\leq i\leq n
. Then
\widetilde{G}(u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}):=f_{0}^{-\deg G}G({\mathbf{f}}).

An elementary argument shows that

(2.3)

\displaystyle\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\{\deg u_{i}\}\leq\max_{0\leq i\leq n}\{\deg f_{i}\}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{n}\deg u_{j}.

Let
\epsilon>0
be given. We now apply Theorem
5
for
\widetilde{G}\in\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
and
\tilde{S}_{\mathbf{f}}

to find a positive integer
c_{0}
and
a proper Zariski closed subset
Z_{0}
of
\mathbb{A}^{n}
independent of
\tilde{S}_{\mathbf{f}}
such that we have either the inequality

(2.4)

\displaystyle\max_{0\leq i\leq n}\{\deg f_{i}\}(=\deg\mathbf{f})\leq c_{0}\max\{1,|\tilde{S}_{\mathbf{f}}|-2\}

holds
or the following two inequalities hold:

(2.5)

\displaystyle N_{\tilde{S}_{\mathbf{f}}}(G({\mathbf{f}}))-N_{\tilde{S}_{\mathbf{f}}}^{(1)}(G({\mathbf{f}}))=N_{\tilde{S}_{\mathbf{f}}}(\widetilde{G}(u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}))-N_{\tilde{S}_{\mathbf{f}}}^{(1)}(\widetilde{G}(u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}))\leq\epsilon\deg\mathbf{f},

(2.6)

\displaystyle N_{\tilde{S}_{\mathbf{f}}}^{(1)}(G({\mathbf{f}}))=N_{\tilde{S}_{\mathbf{f}}}^{(1)}(\widetilde{G}(u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}))\geq(\deg G-\epsilon)\cdot\deg\mathbf{f},

if
\mathbf{f}(\mathbb{C})\not\subset\tilde{Z}_{0}
, where
\tilde{Z}_{0}
is the projective closure of
Z_{0}
in
\mathbb{P}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
.
Since the constant
c_{0}
and the exceptional set
Z
in Theorem
5
do not depend on
S
, it is important to note that
c_{0}
and
\tilde{Z}_{0}
do not depend on
\mathbf{f}
as well.
We first show that (
2.4
) does not hold if
\ell>2c_{0}n
. Indeed, if (
2.4
) holds with
\ell>2c_{0}n
, then it follows from (
2.2
) and the choice of
\ell
that (
2.4
) yields

\displaystyle\max_{0\leq i\leq n}\{\deg f_{i}\}\leq\frac{(n+1)c_{0}}{\ell}\max_{0\leq i\leq n}\{\deg f_{i}\}+c_{0}<\frac{1}{2}\max_{0\leq i\leq n}\{\deg f_{i}\}+c_{0}.

Therefore,
\max_{0\leq i\leq n}\{\deg f_{i}\}<2c_{0}.
This is not possible since
the
f_{i}
are not all constant, and by our assumption that every zero has multiplicity at least
\ell
, we have
2nc_{0}<\ell<\max_{0\leq i\leq n}\{\deg f_{i}\}
.

We continue to let
\ell>2c_{0}n
. As we have shown that (
2.4
) does not hold, it follows that (
2.5
) and (
2.6
) both hold if
\mathbf{f}(\mathbb{C})\not\subset\tilde{Z}_{0}
.
Then it follows trivially from (
2.6
) that

(2.7)

\displaystyle N^{(1)}(G(\mathbf{f}))\geq N_{\tilde{S}_{\mathbf{f}}}^{(1)}(G({\mathbf{f}}))\geq(\deg G-\epsilon)\cdot\deg\mathbf{f}.

This shows (ii) holds.

It remains to show (i). We now compute zeros for points in
\tilde{S}_{\mathbf{f}}
. We will use Propostion
7
.
Let
W
be the Zariski closed subset of
\mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{C})
containing all hypersurfaces defined by all possible subsums (including
G
) in the expansion of
G
. Assume that
\mathbf{f}(\mathbb{C})\not\subset W
.
Since
\widetilde{G}(0,\dots,0)=G(1,0,\ldots,0)\neq 0
, we now apply Propositon
7
to get

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{p}\in\tilde{S}_{\mathbf{f}}}{\rm ord}^{+}_{\mathbf{p}}(\widetilde{G}(u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}))\leq\tilde{c}\max\{1,|\tilde{S}_{\mathbf{f}}|-2\},

where
\tilde{c}=\frac{1}{2}\binom{n+degG}{n}(\binom{n+\deg G}{n}+1)
.
Together with (
2.2
) and that
\ell\leq\max_{0\leq i\leq n}\{\deg f_{i}\}
, it yields

\displaystyle m_{\tilde{S}_{\mathbf{f}}}(\widetilde{G}(u_{1},\ldots,u_{n})):=\sum_{\mathbf{p}\in\tilde{S}_{\mathbf{f}}}{\rm ord}^{+}_{\mathbf{p}}(\widetilde{G}(u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}))\leq\frac{(n+1)\tilde{c}}{\ell}\max_{0\leq i\leq n}\{\deg f_{i}\}\leq\epsilon\deg\mathbf{f}

provided
\ell>(n+1)\tilde{c}_{0}\epsilon^{-1}.

Combining with (
2.5
), we have

(2.8)

\displaystyle N(\widetilde{G}(u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}))-N^{(1)}(\widetilde{G}(u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}))\leq 2\epsilon\deg\mathbf{f}.

We now repeat the above arguments for
(\frac{f_{0}}{f_{i}},\cdots,\frac{f_{n}}{f_{i}})
,
0\leq i\leq n
. Then we find a Zariski closed subset
Z
and a positive constant
c
, independent of
\mathbf{f}
, such that for each
0\leq i\leq n
we have

(2.9)

\displaystyle N\left(G(\frac{f_{0}}{f_{i}},\cdots,\frac{f_{n}}{f_{i}})\right)-N^{(1)}\left(G(\frac{f_{0}}{f_{i}},\cdots,\frac{f_{n}}{f_{i}})\right)\leq 2\epsilon\deg\mathbf{f}.

Since
f_{0},\ldots,f_{n}
are polynomials without common zeros, for each
z\in\mathbb{C}
there exists at least one
f_{i}
such that
f_{i}(z)\neq 0
. Then

{\rm ord}^{+}_{z}(G(f_{0},\ldots,f_{n}))={\rm ord}_{z}\left(G(\frac{f_{0}}{f_{i}},\cdots,\frac{f_{n}}{f_{i}})\right).

This shows that

\displaystyle N(G(\mathbf{f}))-N^{(1)}(G(\mathbf{f}))

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=0}^{n}\left(N\left(G(\frac{f_{0}}{f_{i}},\cdots,\frac{f_{n}}{f_{i}})\right)-N^{(1)}\left(G(\frac{f_{0}}{f_{i}},\cdots,\frac{f_{n}}{f_{i}})\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2(n+1)\epsilon\deg\mathbf{f}.\quad\text{(by \eqref{mutiplei})}

The assertion (i) can be derived by adjusting
\epsilon
.
∎

2.2.
Motivation for the proof of the Main Theorem

In Section
2.1
, the key to proving Theorem
6
is the use of the set
S
(depending on the map) that contains all zeros and poles of each coordinate function of the map.
The crucial observation is that all coordinate functions then become
S
-units, allowing us to apply Theorem
5
.
Importantly, the constants and the exceptional set obtained in Theorem
5
are independent of
S
, which makes the argument work uniformly for all rational maps.

To extend this viewpoint to the analytic setting, we let
\mathcal{E}_{f}
denote the set of all zeros of
f_{i}
,
0\leq i\leq n
, and define

\mathcal{Y}_{f}:=\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathcal{E}_{f},

where
f=(f_{0},\ldots,f_{n})
is a holomorphic map such that the entire functions
f_{i}
,
0\leq i\leq n
, have no common zeros.
Then
\mathcal{Y}_{f}
is a parabolic open Riemann surface equipped with a smooth exhaustion function
\sigma
for which the smooth
(1,1)
-form
dd^{c}\!\log\sigma
has finite total mass on
\mathcal{Y}_{f}
.
Although
\sigma
may not be a parabolic exhaustion for
{\mathcal{Y}}_{f}
, Chen et al. (see
[
2
]
) observed that, after appropriate modifications, the key ingredients of Nevanlinna theory still admit analogues in this setting.

3.
Nevanlinna theory and the GCD theorem on parabolic Riemann surfaces

3.1.
Parabolic Riemann surfaces and Jensen’s formula

A
non-compact Riemann surface
{\mathcal{Y}}
is
parabolic
if it admits a smooth exhaustion function

\sigma:{\mathcal{Y}}\rightarrow[0,\infty)

such that
\log\sigma
is harmonic outside a
compact subset of
{\mathcal{Y}}
. Such
\sigma
is called a
parabolic exhaustion
. Nevanlinna theory on parabolic open Riemann surfaces was recently developed by P
{\rm{\breve{a}}}
un and Sibony
[
16
]
.
Chen et al.
[
2
]
considered the case when

{\mathcal{Y}}:={\mathbb{C}}\backslash\mathcal{E}
, where
{\mathcal{E}}:=\{a_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
is a discrete countable set of points in
{\mathbb{C}}
. The surface
{\mathcal{Y}}
is parabolic due to
[
23
, Lemma 10.1(g), p. 75]
by taking
\phi:{\mathcal{Y}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
as the
restriction of a transcendental function sending
{\mathcal{E}}
to
0
.
However the smooth exhaustion function

\sigma:{\mathcal{Y}}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
constructed in
[
2
]
, which serves as our purpose, is not necessarily compactly supported, although the smooth (1,1)-form
dd^{c}\log\sigma
is of finite total mass on
{\mathcal{Y}}
.
Therefore the parabolic Nevanlinna theory developed by P
{\rm{\breve{a}}}
un and Sibony is no longer directly applicable to
{\mathcal{Y}}
.
This difficulty arises because the parabolic Jensen formula (see (
3.8
)) acquires an additional term

\int_{B_{r}^{\sigma}}\upsilon dd^{c}\tau
. A key observation is that, by shrinking the radii
r_{j}
sufficiently small, we can make (see Lemma
10
)

c^{+}_{g,\sigma}(r):=\int_{B_{r}^{\sigma}}\log^{+}|g|dd^{c}\tau

to be bounded (independent of
r
), for any meromorphic function
g
on
{\mathbb{C}}
. The subtle point, however, is that the required shrinking depends on the particular function

g
, so we must ensure in our arguments that such shrinking occurs only finitely many times.
This “shrinking trick” was already observed by Chen et al. in
[
2
]
and indeed plays a central role in their work.
Unfortunately many of the necessary details were not made explicit there.
We therefore supply the details in this and the subsequent sections.

Let us first briefly review the construction of
\sigma
given in Lemma 3.1 and 3.2 of
[
2
]
. Let

{\mathcal{Y}}:={\mathbb{C}}\backslash\mathcal{E}
, where
{\mathcal{E}}:=\{a_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
is a discrete countable set of points in
{\mathbb{C}}
.
Up
to translation and dilation on
{\mathbb{C}}
, we may assume that
|a_{j}|>2
for all
j\geq 1
. We also assume that the radii satisfy the following conditions:
(i)
{\mathbb{D}}(a_{j},2r_{j})
for all
j
and the disc
{\mathbb{D}}_{2}
are disjoint, and (ii) the sum
\sum_{j\geq 1}r_{j}<+\infty
.

The
exhaustion function
\sigma
on
{\mathcal{Y}}
is obtained by smoothing the piecewise smooth exhaustion function
\hat{\sigma}
given by

(3.1)

\log\hat{\sigma}:=\log^{+}|z|+\sum_{j\geq 1}r_{j}\log^{+}{r_{j}\over|z-a_{j}|}.

Note that
\hat{\tau}:=\log\hat{\sigma}
takes values in
[0,+\infty)
, is continuous on
{\mathcal{Y}}
and is smooth (indeed harmonic) outside the circle
S(0,1):=\{|z|=1\}
and the disjoint circles

S(a_{j},r_{j}):=\{|z-a_{j}|=r_{j}\}
. By the Poincaré-Lelong formula (see, e.g.,
[
17
]
), it is clear that

dd^{c}\hat{\tau}={1\over 2}\nu(0,1)+{1\over 2}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}r_{j}\left(\nu(a_{j},r_{j})-\delta_{a_{j}}\right)

is a distribution of order
0
and locally of finite mass, where
\nu(a_{j},r_{j})
is the Haar measure on the circle
S(a_{j},r_{j})
.

The smoothing process goes as follows (see
[
2
, Appendix]
): Let

\displaystyle h(r)=\begin{cases}0&\text{ for $r\leq{3\over 4}$}\\
{1\over 4\pi}{1\over 1+e^{{r-1\over(r-1)^{2}-1/16}}}&\text{for ${3\over 4}<r<{5\over 4}$}\\
{1\over 4\pi}&\text{for $r\geq{5\over 4}$}\end{cases}

and

(3.2)

\displaystyle H(r):=4\pi\int_{0}^{r}h(s-c){ds\over s},

where the constant
c\in(0,1/2)
is chosen such that
H
agrees with
\log^{+}r
outside
[{1\over 2},{3\over 2}]
.
Note that
H
is an absolute function independent of
a_{j}
.
This function
H(r)
is smooth of
\log^{+}r
with
0\leq H(r)-\log^{+}r\leq\log^{+}{3\over 2}<{1\over 2}
and
H(r)=\log^{+}r
when
H(r)\geq\log{3\over 2}
.
Define

(3.3)

\displaystyle\tau:=H(|z|)+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}r_{j}H\left({r_{j}\over|z-a_{j}|}\right),~~~~\sigma=\exp(\tau).

Then, as stated in
[
2
, Lemma 3.1]
,
\sigma\geq\hat{\sigma}
and the difference

\sigma-\hat{\sigma}
is supported on

(3.4)

\text{Supp}(\sigma-\hat{\sigma})\subset U:=\left(A\left(0,{1\over 2},{3\over 2}\right)\backslash{\mathcal{E}}\right)\bigcup\left(\bigcup_{j=1}^{\infty}A\left(a_{j},{1\over 2}r_{j},{3\over 2}r_{j}\right)\right),

where
A(a_{j},{1\over 2}r_{j},{3\over 2}r_{j}):=\{z\in{\mathcal{Y}}:{1\over 2}r_{j}\leq|z-a_{j}|\leq{3\over 2}r_{j}\}
are pairwise disjoint annuli. For

z\not\in\cup_{j=1}^{\infty}\overline{{\mathbb{D}}(a_{j},{3\over 2}r_{j})}
,
\log^{+}\left|{r_{j}\over z-a_{j}}\right|=H\left(\left|{r_{j}\over z-a_{j}}\right|\right)=0
for each
j
. Thus

\tau(z)=H(|z|)
and
\hat{\tau}(z)=\log^{+}|z|
, which are equal when
\tau(z)\geq\log{3\over 2}
. Hence
\sigma=\hat{\sigma}
when
\sigma\geq{3\over 2}
.
We say that
such
\sigma
is associated to the discs
\{{\mathbb{D}}(a_{j},2r_{j})\}
by assuming that
\{r_{j}\}
satisfy (i) and (ii) above. Note that
\sigma
is not necessarily a parabolic exhaustion function because
dd^{c}\log\sigma
may not be compactly supported.

Denote by
B^{\sigma}_{r}:=\{y\in{\mathcal{Y}}:\sigma(y)<r\}
and by
S^{\sigma}_{r}:=\{y\in{\mathcal{Y}}:\sigma(y)=r\}
.
Denote by
\mu_{r}
the measure induced by the differential
d^{c}\log\sigma|_{S^{\sigma}_{r}}
and write
d\mu_{r}=d^{c}\log\sigma|_{S^{\sigma}_{r}}
.
Note that, from the above discussion (see also
[
2
]
),

(3.5)

B_{t}^{\sigma}\subset{\mathbb{D}}_{t}~~~~\mbox{for}~t\geq 1,~~~\mbox{and}~~~~~{\mathbb{D}}_{t}\backslash\left(\bigcup_{j=1}^{\infty}{\mathbb{D}}(a_{j},{3\over 2}r_{j})\right)\subset B_{t}^{\sigma}~~~~\mbox{for}~t\geq{3\over 2}.

Lemma 8

(Lemma 3.2 in
[
2
]
)
.

The smooth 2-form
dd^{c}\log\sigma
defines an order 0 distribution of finite mass on
{\mathcal{Y}}
.

Proof.

We use the formula: in polar coordinates
z=a+re^{i\theta}
, for some
a\in{\mathbb{C}}
, and for a smooth function
\phi
, one has

d^{c}\phi={1\over 4\pi}\left(r{\partial\phi\over\partial r}d\theta-{1\over r}{\partial\phi\over\partial\theta}dr\right).

The smooth 2-form
dd^{c}\log\sigma
is supported on
U
, where
U
is given in (
3.4
).
On the annulus
A(0,{1\over 2},{3\over 2})
, in polar coordinate
z=re^{i\theta}
, by (
3.2
) one has (see
[
2
, Appendix]
)

(3.6)

dd^{c}\tau=dd^{c}H(|z|)=d\left({r\over 4\pi}{\partial H(r)\over\partial r}d\theta\right)=d(h(r-c)d\theta)=h^{\prime}\left(r-c\right)dr\wedge d\theta=O(1)dr\wedge d\theta.

Thus
dd^{c}\tau
has finite mass on
A(0,{1\over 2},{3\over 2})
.
On the annulus
A(a_{j},{1\over 2}r_{j},{3\over 2}r_{j})
, since
{\mathbb{D}}(a_{j},2r_{j})
for all
j
and the disc
{\mathbb{D}}_{2}
are disjoint, we have similarly that
\tau=r_{j}H\left({r_{j}\over|z-a_{j}|}\right)
on
A(a_{j},{1\over 2}r_{j},{3\over 2}r_{j})
. Therefore,

\displaystyle dd^{c}\tau

\displaystyle=dd^{c}\left(r_{j}H\left({r_{j}\over|z-a_{j}|}\right)\right)

(3.7)

\displaystyle=d\left({r\over 4\pi}{\partial r_{j}H\left({r_{j}\over r}\right)\over\partial r}d\theta\right)=\left({r_{j}\over r}\right)^{2}h^{\prime}\left({r_{j}\over r}-c\right)dr\wedge d\theta=O(1)dr\wedge d\theta.

Since
\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}r_{j}<+\infty
,
the support
U
is of finite Lebesgue measure, this proves the lemma.
∎

We now derive the parabolic Jensen’s formula in our setting. For any function
\upsilon:{\mathcal{Y}}\rightarrow(-\infty,+\infty)
such that
dd^{c}\upsilon
is of order 0, the parabolic Jensen’s formula (see
[
16
, Remark 3.2, p. 22]
) is stated as

(3.8)

\int_{1}^{r}{dt\over t}\int_{B_{t}^{\sigma}}dd^{c}\upsilon=\int_{S_{r}^{\sigma}}\upsilon d\mu_{r}-\int_{B_{r}^{\sigma}}\upsilon dd^{c}\tau\quad(r>1).

Let
g
be meromorphic on
{\mathbb{C}}
. In (
3.8
), by letting
\upsilon:=\log|g|
, and also using the Poincaré-Lelong formula, we get the following lemma.

Lemma 9

(Parabolic Jensen’s formula)
.

Let
g
be a meromorphic function on
{\mathbb{C}}
. Then

\int_{S_{r}^{\sigma}}\log|g|d\mu_{r}=N_{g,\sigma}(0,r)-N_{g,\sigma}(\infty,r)+c_{g,\sigma}(r),

where

N_{g,\sigma}(0,r):=\int_{1}^{r}\sum_{z\in B_{t}^{\sigma}}\max\{0,{\rm ord}_{z}g\}{dt\over t},

N_{g,\sigma}(\infty,r):=N_{\frac{1}{g},\sigma}(0,r)
, and

(3.9)

c_{g,\sigma}(r):=\int_{B_{r}^{\sigma}}\log|g|dd^{c}\tau.

Denote by

(3.10)

c^{+}_{g,\sigma}(r):=\int_{B_{r}^{\sigma}}\log^{+}|g|dd^{c}\tau.

Lemma 10
.

Let
g
be a meromorphic function on
{\mathbb{C}}
.
Let
{\mathcal{Y}}\subset{\mathbb{C}}
be given with
\sigma
being associated to the discs
\{{\mathbb{D}}(a_{j},2r_{j})\}
.
Then we can choose
r_{j}
small enough such that there exists a positive constant
C_{g}
depending on
g
with

c^{+}_{g,\sigma}(r)\leq C_{g}
for any
r>0
.

Proof.

By using (
3.5
) for
t\geq 2
, and notice that
dd^{c}\tau
is supported on
U
where
U
is given in (
3.4
),
we have

(3.11)

\displaystyle c^{+}_{g,\sigma}(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{B^{\sigma}_{t}}\log^{+}|g|dd^{c}\tau\leq\int_{{\mathbb{D}}_{t}\backslash\mathcal{E}}\log^{+}|g|dd^{c}\tau

(3.12)

\displaystyle=\int_{A(0,{1\over 2},{3\over 2})}\log^{+}|g|dd^{c}\tau+\int_{\cup_{j\geq 1}A(a_{j},{1\over 2}r_{j},{3\over 2}r_{j})\cap{\mathbb{D}}_{t}}\log^{+}|g|dd^{c}\tau.

Notice that
\log^{+}|g|
is locally integrable around the zeros or poles of
g
and by (
3.6
), we get

\int_{A(0,{1\over 2},{3\over 2})}\log^{+}|g|dd^{c}\tau<M_{1},

where
M_{1}
is a positive constant depending on
g
.

We now estimate the integration over
A(a_{j},{1\over 2}r_{j},{3\over 2}r_{j})
.
If
g(a_{j})\not=\infty
. Then
\log^{+}|g|\leq C_{1}
for some
C_{1}>0
on
{\mathbb{D}}(a_{j},{3\over 2}r_{j})
.
Then, by (
3.7
),

\int_{A(a_{j},{1\over 2}r_{j},{3\over 2}r_{j})}\log^{+}|g|dd^{c}\tau\leq C_{1}\cdot\frac{9\pi}{2}C_{h}r_{j}<{1\over 2^{j}},

by further shrinking
r_{j}
, where
C_{h}:=\max_{\frac{1}{2}\leq s\leq\frac{3}{2}}|h^{\prime}(s-c)|.
If
g(a_{j})=\infty
. Then, locally,
g(z)=(z-a_{j})^{k_{j}}g_{j}(z)
with
g_{j}(a_{j})\not=0
.
Thus
\log^{+}|g|\leq C_{2}\log^{+}{1\over|z-a_{j}|}
for some
C_{2}>0
on
{\mathbb{D}}(a_{j},{3\over 2}r_{j})
.
We have similarly

\int_{A(a_{j},{1\over 2}r_{j},{3\over 2}r_{j})}\log^{+}|g|dd^{c}\tau\leq\frac{9\pi}{2}C_{2}C_{h}\int_{{1\over 2}r_{j}}^{{3\over 2}r_{j}}\log rdr\leq\frac{9\pi}{2}C_{2}C_{h}r_{j}^{\delta},

for some
1<\delta<2
.
Again, we can choose
r_{j}
sufficiently small such that

\int_{A(a_{j},{1\over 2}r_{j},{3\over 2}r_{j})}\log^{+}|g|dd^{c}\tau<{1\over 2^{j}}.

Therefore, with this choice of the radii
r_{j}
, we obtain from (
3.11
) that

c^{+}_{g,\sigma}(r)\leq M_{1}+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}{1\over 2^{j}}=M_{1}+1
.
∎

Note that the shrinking process of
r_{j}
in the above lemma depends on the given function
g
. So we must ensure in our arguments that such shrinking occurs only finitely many times. Throughout the rest of the paper, we will keep shrinking the radii
r_{j}
whenever necessary. The
\sigma
should be understood as being adjusted accordingly,
although we will continue to denote it by the same symbol.

3.2.
The characteristic functions in the parabolic setting

We continue to let

{\mathcal{Y}}:={\mathbb{C}}\backslash\mathcal{E}
with
\sigma
being the exhaustion associated to the discs
\{{\mathbb{D}}(a_{j},2r_{j})\}
, constructed in Section
3.1
.
Let
f:{\mathbb{C}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
be a holomorphic map.
We briefly recall some standard notations in Nevanlinna theory in the parabolic context (for details, see
[
2
]
,
[
16
]
).

Let
D=\{Q=0\}
be a hypersurface in
\mathbb{P}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
, where
Q\in\mathbb{C}[x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}]
is a homogeneous polynomial of degree
d
.

1.

The proximity function of
f
with respect to
D
is defined as

m_{f,\sigma}(D,r)=\int_{S_{r}^{\sigma}}\lambda_{D}(f)d\mu_{r},

where
\lambda_{D}
is the Weil-function on
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
given by

\lambda_{D}({\bf x})=\log{\|{\bf x}\|^{d}\over|Q({\bf x})|},~~{\bf x}=[x_{0}:\cdots:x_{n}]\in\mathbb{P}^{n}({\mathbb{C}}),~~~\|{\bf x}\|:=\max\{|x_{0}|,\dots,|x_{n}|\}.

2.

The Nevanlinna characteristic (height) function is defined by

T_{f,\sigma}(r):=\int_{S_{r}^{\sigma}}\log\|{\bf f}\|d\mu_{r},~~~~r>1,

where
{\bf f}=(f_{0},\ldots,f_{n})
is a reduced representation of
f
.
The Ahlfors-Shimizu characteristic (height) function is defined by

T^{A}_{f,\sigma}(r)=\int_{1}^{r}{dt\over t}\int_{B_{t}^{\sigma}}f^{*}\omega_{FS},~~~~r>1,

where
\omega_{FS}
denotes the Fubini-Study form.
Note that
T_{f,\sigma}(r)
depends on the choices of the representation
{\bf f}
of
f
, while
T^{A}_{f,\sigma}(r)
does not depend on the representation.
Therefore, when we write
T_{f,\sigma}(r)
, we always assume that we have fixed a reduced a representation
{\bf f}=(f_{0},\dots,f_{n})
of
f
.

3.

The counting function and the
k
-truncated counting function are defined by

N_{f,\sigma}(D,r):=\int_{1}^{r}\sum_{z\in B_{t}^{\sigma}}{\rm ord}_{z}f^{*}D{dt\over t},~~~~~~~\mbox{and}~~N_{f,\sigma}^{(k)}(D,r):=\int_{1}^{r}\sum_{z\in B_{t}^{\sigma}}\mathop{\mathrm{min}}\{k,{\rm ord}_{z}f^{*}D\}{dt\over t}.

4.

The counting function extends naturally to the sub-schemes, as well as the notion
N_{\text{gcd},\sigma}(f,g)(r)
, where
f,g
are meromorphic functions on
\mathcal{Y}
. See
[
13
]
.

We also use the standard notation
T_{f}(r),m_{f}(D,r),n_{f}(D,t)
and
N_{f}(D,r)
of the Nevanlinna functions associated to a holomorphic map
f:{\mathbb{C}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
.
In this case, the Ahlfors-Shimizu and the classical Nevanlinna characteristic function agree up to an additive constant (see
[
14
, Theorem 1.1.19]
), i.e.,

T_{f}(r)-T^{A}_{f}(r)=O(1).

Theorem 11

(The parabolic First Main Theorem)
.

Let
{\mathcal{Y}}\subset{\mathbb{C}}
be as above.
Let
D=\{Q=0\}
be a hypersurface in
\mathbb{P}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
, where
Q\in\mathbb{C}[x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}]
is a homogeneous polynomial of degree
d
.
Let
f=(f_{0},\ldots,f_{n}):{\mathbb{C}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
be a holomorphic curve, where
f_{0},\ldots,f_{n}
are entire functions without common zero. Suppose that
{f}({\mathcal{Y}})\not\subset\mbox{Supp}(D)
.
Then

dT_{f,\sigma}(r)=m_{f,\sigma}(D,r)+N_{f,\sigma}(D,r)+c_{Q({\bf f}),\sigma}(r).

Proof.

Following the definition of Nevanlinna characteristic function, we have

dT_{f,\sigma}(r)=\int_{S_{r}^{\sigma}}\log\|{\bf f}\|^{d}d\mu_{r}=\int_{S_{r}^{\sigma}}\log{\|{\bf f}\|^{d}\over|Q({\bf f})|}d\mu_{r}+\int_{S_{r}^{\sigma}}\log{|Q({\bf f})|}d\mu_{r}.

Hence the assertion follows from Lemma
9
together with the definition of the proximity function.
∎

In the case of a meromorphic function
g
on
{\mathcal{Y}}
, write
g=g_{0}/g_{1}
, where
g_{0}
and
g_{1}
are entire functions without common zeros.
We regard the function
g
as the map
g:=[g_{0}:g_{1}]:{\mathcal{Y}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{P}}^{1}({\mathbb{C}})
. By taking
Q(z_{0},z_{1})=z_{1}
(so
D=\{z_{1}=0\}=\infty
), we get

m_{g,\sigma}(r):=m_{g,\sigma}(\infty,r)=\int_{S_{r}^{\sigma}}\log{\max\{|g_{0}|,|g_{1}|\}\over|g_{1}|}d\mu_{r}=\int_{S_{r}^{\sigma}}\log^{+}|g|d\mu_{r}+O(1).

The above
m_{g,\sigma}(r)
is well-defined, i.e., it is independent of the choice of
g_{0}
and
g_{1}
.
However, the Nevanlinna height function
T_{g,\sigma}(r)=\int_{S_{r}^{\sigma}}\log\max\{|g_{0}|,|g_{1}|\}d\mu_{r}
is not well-defined. Instead we introduce a new height function that is well-defined:

(3.13)

T^{M}_{g,\sigma}(r)=m_{g,\sigma}(\infty,r)+N_{g,\sigma}(\infty,r).

From the parabolic Jenen’s formula above,

(3.14)

T^{M}_{1/g,\sigma}(r)=T^{M}_{g,\sigma}(r)+c_{1/g,\sigma}(r).

We now compare
T^{M}_{g,\sigma}(r)
with
T^{A}_{g,\sigma}(r)
.
From parabolic Jensen’s formula,

\displaystyle T^{A}_{g,\sigma}(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{r}{dt\over t}\int_{B_{t}^{\sigma}}dd^{c}\log\max\{|g_{0}|,|g_{1}|\}=\int_{S_{r}^{\sigma}}\log\max\{|g_{0}|,|g_{1}|\}d\mu_{r}

\displaystyle-\int_{B_{r}^{\sigma}}\log\max\{|g_{0}|,|g_{1}|\}dd^{c}\tau

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{S_{r}^{\sigma}}\log^{+}|g|d\mu_{r}+\int_{S_{r}^{\sigma}}\log|g_{1}|d\mu_{r}-\int_{B_{r}^{\sigma}}\log\max\{|g_{0}|,|g_{1}|\}dd^{c}\tau

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T^{M}_{g,\sigma}(r)+{1\over 2}\int_{B_{r}^{\sigma}}\log\left(1/(1+|g|^{2})\right)dd^{c}\tau=T^{M}_{g,\sigma}(r)+{1\over 2}c_{1/(1+|g|^{2}),\sigma}(r).

Similarly to the proof of Lemma
10
, by choosing
r_{j}
small enough, there exists a constant
C>0
with
c^{+}_{1/(1+|g|^{2}),\sigma}(r)\leq C
. Hence

(3.15)

|T^{M}_{g,\sigma}(r)-T^{A}_{g,\sigma}(r)|\leq C.

It is important to note that these radii
r_{j}
depend on the function
g
.

Proposition 12

(
[
2
]
)
.

Let
f=(f_{0},\ldots,f_{n}):{\mathbb{C}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
be a holomorphic curve, where

are entire functions without common zero. Let

be given with
\sigma
being associated to the discs
\{{\mathbb{D}}(a_{j},2r_{j})\}
.
Then we can choose

small enough such that, for all

From (

To prove (ii),

3.3.
The parabolic Logarithmic Derivative Lemma and H. Cartan’s theorem

To establish the parabolic Logarithmic Derivative Lemma, we introduce the weighted Euler characteristic function (see
[
16
, Definition 3.4]
):

\mathfrak{X}^{+}_{\sigma}(r):=\int_{S^{\sigma}_{r}}\log^{+}\left|d\sigma\left({\partial\over\partial z}\right)\right|^{2}d\mu_{r}.

Using the properties that the disks
{\mathbb{D}}(a_{j},2r_{j})
are disjoint and that
\sum_{j\geq 1}r_{j}<+\infty
, we see from

[
16
, Example (2), pp. 32 and 33]
(see also
[
2
, (2.10)]
) that

\mathfrak{X}^{+}_{\sigma}(r)=\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma}(r)+O(\log r)=N_{\mathcal{E}}(r)+O(\log r),\quad r>1,

where
N_{\mathcal{E}}(r):=\int_{1}^{r}n_{\mathcal{E}}(t){dt\over t}
, and
n_{\mathcal{E}}(t)
counts the number of points in
{\mathcal{E}}\cap\mathbb{D}_{t}
for
t\geq 1
. In particular,
if
{\mathcal{E}}:=f^{-1}(D)
for a holomorphic map
f:{\mathbb{C}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
and a divisor
D
on
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})

with
f({\mathbb{C}})\not\subset\mbox{Supp}D
, it follows (see also
[
2
, (4.2)]
) that

(3.17)

\mathfrak{X}^{+}_{\sigma}(r)\leq N_{f}^{(1)}(D,r)+O(\log r),\quad r>1.

The above estimate is crucial to our paper.
In
[
2
]
, the authors consider the case when
N_{f}(D,r)=o(T_{f}(r))
, and therefore
\mathfrak{X}^{+}_{\sigma}(r)
is a small function with respect to
T_{f}(r)
. In our case, we treat the situation where
N_{f}^{(1)}(D,r)\leq{1\over\ell}T_{f}(r)
for a sufficiently large
\ell
. In this setting,
\mathfrak{X}^{+}_{\sigma}(r)
should be regarded as having the same order of growth as
T_{f}(r)
.

Lemma 13

(Parabolic Logarithmic Derivative Lemma)
.

Let
g
be a meromorphic function on
\mathbb{C}
. Let
{\mathcal{Y}}\subset{\mathbb{C}}
be as above. For any
\delta>0
, one has

(i)

m_{g^{\prime}/g,\sigma}(r)\leq_{\rm exc}{1\over 2}((1+\delta)^{2}\log T_{g}(r)+\delta\log r+\mathfrak{X}^{+}_{\sigma}(r)).

(ii)

m_{g^{(l)}/g,\sigma}(r)\leq_{\rm exc}{l\over 2}\left((1+\delta)^{2}\log T_{g}(r)+\delta\log r+\mathfrak{X}^{+}_{\sigma}(r)\right).

Proof.

(i) We follow the the approach in
[
16
, Theorem 3.8, pp. 26-28]
. Consider the auxiliary form

\Omega:={1\over|w|^{2}(1+\log^{2}|w|)}{\sqrt{-1}\over 2\pi}dw\wedge d\bar{w},

introduced in
[
16
, (15), p. 27]
, which has finite volume on
{\mathbb{C}}
.
By the change of variable formula, we have

\int_{1}^{r}{dt\over t}\int_{B_{t}^{\sigma}}g^{*}\Omega=\int_{w\in{\mathbb{C}}}N_{g,\sigma}(w,r)\Omega.

Since
B_{t}^{\sigma}\subset\mathbb{D}_{t}
for any
t\geq 1
, and by the classical First Main Theorem,

N_{g,\sigma}(w,r)\leq N_{g}(w,r)\leq T_{g}(r)+O(1),~~~\mbox{for}~r>1.

Thus

\int_{1}^{r}{dt\over t}\int_{B_{t}^{\sigma}}g^{*}\Omega=\int_{w\in{\mathbb{C}}}N_{g,\sigma}(w,r)\Omega\leq\int_{\mathbb{C}}T_{g}(r)\Omega+\int_{\mathbb{C}}C\Omega.

Consequently, we obtain

\int_{1}^{r}{dt\over t}\int_{B_{t}^{\sigma}}g^{*}\Omega\leq C_{0}T_{g}(r)

for some constant
C_{0}>0
. The rest of the proof follows from
[
16
, Theorem 3.8, p. 28]
.
This finishes the proof of (i).

To prove (ii), notice that

{g^{(l)}\over g}={g^{(l)}\over g^{(l-1)}}{g^{(l-1)}\over g^{(l-2)}}\cdots{g^{\prime}\over g}.

Therefore, by (i), we obtain

m_{g^{(l)}/g,\sigma}(r)\leq\sum_{j=1}^{l}m_{g^{(j)}/g^{(j-1)},\sigma}(r)\leq_{\rm exc}{1\over 2}\left((1+\delta)^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{l}\log T_{g^{(j)}}(r)+\delta l\log r+l\mathfrak{X}^{+}_{\sigma}(r)\right).

By the classical First Main Theorem,

T_{g^{(j)}}(r)=m_{g^{(j)}}(r)+N_{g^{(j)}}(r)\leq m_{g^{(j)}/g^{(j-1)}}(r)+m_{g^{(j-1)}}(r)+2N_{g^{(j-1)}}(r)\leq_{\rm exc}3T_{g^{(j-1)}}(r)+O(1).

Hence

T_{g^{(j)}}(r)\leq_{\rm exc}3^{j}T_{g}(r)+O(1).

Therefore, we get

m_{g^{(l)}/g,\sigma}(r)\leq_{\rm exc}{l\over 2}\left((1+\delta)^{2}\log T_{g}(r)+\delta\log r+\mathfrak{X}^{+}_{\sigma}(r)\right).

∎

In particular, by the definition, the above lemma gives the following:

Lemma 14
.

If
u
is an entire function such that it has no zeros on
\mathcal{Y}

(
i.e.
u
is a unit on
\mathcal{Y}
)
, then, for any
\delta>0
,

T^{M}_{u^{\prime}/u,\sigma}(r)\leq_{\rm exc}{1\over 2}((1+\delta)^{2}\log T_{u}(r)+\delta\log r+\mathfrak{X}^{+}_{\sigma}(r)).

Using the parabolic Logarithmic Derivative Lemma and the parabolic Jensen’s formula, we can easily derive the following parabolic version of H. Cartan’s theorem.

Theorem 15
.

Let
{\mathcal{Y}}:={\mathbb{C}}\backslash\mathcal{E}
with the exhaustion function
\sigma
constructed above. Let
f=(f_{0},\dots,f_{n}):{\mathbb{C}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
be a
holomorphic map whose image is not contained in any proper linear subspace of
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
, where
f_{0},\dots,f_{n}
are
entire functions without common zeros. Let
H_{1},\dots,H_{q}
be hyperplanes on
\mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{C})
.
Then, for
\delta>0
,

\displaystyle\int_{S_{r}^{\sigma}}\max_{J}\sum_{j\in J}\lambda_{H_{j}}(f)d\mu_{r}+N_{W,\sigma}(0,r)

\displaystyle\leq_{\rm exc}(n+1)T_{f,\sigma}(r)+O(\log T_{f}(r))+\frac{n(n+1)}{2}(\mathfrak{X}^{+}_{\sigma}(r)+\delta\log r)+c_{\frac{1}{W},\sigma}(r),

where
W:=W(f_{0},\dots,f_{n})
is the Wronskian of
f
and the maximum is taken over all subsets
J\subset\{1,2,\dots,q\}
such that
H_{j},j\in J
, are in general position.

Proof.

We follow the proof in
[
17
, Theorem A.5.1.3]
.
Let
H_{j}=\{{\bf w}\in{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})~|~<{{\bf w},\bf a}_{j}>=0\}
for
j=1,\dots,q
.
Let
T
be the set of all
injective maps
\mu:\{0,1,\dots,n\}\rightarrow\{1,\dots,q\}
such that

{\bf a}_{\mu(0)},\dots,{\bf a}_{\mu(n)}
are linearly independent.
Then

(3.18)

\displaystyle\int_{S_{r}^{\sigma}}\max_{J}\sum_{j\in J}\lambda_{H_{j}}(f)d\mu_{r}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{S_{r}^{\sigma}}\max_{\mu\in T}\sum_{j=0}^{n}\log~\left({\|{\bf f}\|\|{\bf a}_{\mu(j)}\|\over|<{\bf f},{\bf a}>|}\right)d\mu_{r}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{S_{r}^{\sigma}}\max_{\mu\in T}\log^{+}{|W(<{\bf f},{\bf a}_{\mu(0)}>,\dots,<{\bf f},{\bf a}_{\mu(n)}>)|\over\prod_{j=0}^{n}|<{\bf f},{\bf a}_{\mu(j)}>|}d\mu_{r}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle(n+1)T_{f,\sigma}(r)+\int_{S_{r}^{\sigma}}\log(1/|W(f_{0},\dots,f_{n})|)d\mu_{r}+O(1).

By applying the logarithmic derivative lemma (Lemma
13
) and the parabolic Jensen formula (Lemma
9
), the theorem follows.
∎

The following result can be derived from the proof of
[

, Theorem 2.1]
and Lemma
13
.

Theorem 16
.

Let

l\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n+1}c_{f_{l},\sigma}(r)+O\left(\max_{1\leq l\leq n}\log T_{\frac{f_{l}}{f_{0}}}(r)\right),

where

is an entire function appeared in the proof of
[

or
[
17

), which is determined in terms of

only.

Proof.

We first sketch the proof by assuming that
f_{0},\dots,f_{n}
are linearly independent.
By applying Theorem
15
to
f=(f_{0},\dots,f_{n+1})
, we have

\sum_{j=1}^{n+2}m_{f,\sigma}(H_{j},r)+N_{W,\sigma}(r)\leq(n+1)T_{f,\sigma}(r)+\frac{n(n+1)}{2}(\mathfrak{X}^{+}_{\sigma}(r)+\delta\log r)+c_{\frac{1}{W},\sigma}(r)+O(\log T_{f}(r)),

where

and
H_{n+2}=\{x_{0}+\cdots+x_{n}=0\}
.
By Theorem

, for

,

\sum_{j=0}^{n+1}N_{f_{j},\sigma}(0,r)-N_{W,\sigma}(r)\leq\sum_{j=0}^{n+1}N^{(n)}_{f_{j},\sigma}(0,r).

The proof can be completed by applying the following estimate, valid for any for any

and

\displaystyle T_{f,\sigma}(r)

\displaystyle=\int_{S_{r}^{\sigma}}\log\max_{0\leq l\leq n+1}|f_{l}(z)|d\mu_{r}=\int_{S_{r}^{\sigma}}\log\max_{0\leq l\leq n+1}(|f_{l}(z)|/|f_{j}(z)|)d\mu_{r}+\int_{S_{r}^{\sigma}}\log|f_{j}(z)|d\mu_{r}

l\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n+1}c_{f_{l},\sigma}(r)+O\left(\max_{1\leq l\leq n}\log T_{\frac{f_{l}}{f_{0}}}(r)\right).

3.4.
The moving GCD theorem in the parabolic setting

One of the key components in
[
9
]
is the extension of the GCD theorem in
[
13
]
to the case where the coefficients of two coprime polynomials are allowed to be meromorphic functions of the same growth. In this subsection, we aim to extend this line of argument to the parabolic setting. Before we proceed, let us briefly outline the development of the GCD theorem.

The foundational arguments in
[
13
]
are based on results initially established in
[
12
]
for the number field case. In
[
25
]
, the GCD results concerning
\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}
from
[
12
]
were generalized to more general varieties by combining the approach in
[
22
]
with Diophantine approximation results for arbitrary varieties introduced by the first named author and Vojta
[
21
]
. More recently, a further generalization was achieved in
[
10
]
, which applied Vojta’s results on integral points on semiabelian varieties for the number field case, as well as
[
15
]
for the complex case.

To proceed, we will first derive a version of the second main theorem with moving targets of the same growth, which will serve our purpose. See
[
18
]
or
[
17
, Chapter 6]
.

Let
a_{0},\ldots,a_{n}
be meromorphic functions, not all zeros, on
{\mathbb{C}}
, and let
L:=a_{0}X_{0}+\dots+a_{n}X_{n}
.
Then
L
defines a hyperplane
H
in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
, over the field generated by
a_{0},\ldots,a_{n}
over
\mathbb{C}
.
We note that
H(z)
is the hyperplane determined by the linear form
L(z)
. The definition of the Weil function, proximity function and counting function can be easily extended to moving hyperplanes.
For example,

(3.20)

\displaystyle\lambda_{H(z)}(P)=-\log\frac{|a_{0}(z)x_{0}+\cdots+a_{n}(z)x_{n}|}{\max\{|x_{0}|,\dots,|x_{n}|\}\max\{|a_{0}(z)|,\dots,|a_{n}(z)|\}},

for

which is not a common zero of

, or a pole of any

0\leq k\leq n
.
We now consider a family of linear forms
L_{j}:=\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{ij}X_{j}
,
1\leq j\leq q
, as the aformentioned linear forms. Without loss of generality, we normalize the linear forms
L_{j}
,
1\leq j\leq q
, such that for each
1\leq j\leq q
, there exists
0\leq j^{\prime}\leq n

a_{jj^{\prime}}=1
. Let

\displaystyle\left\{\prod a_{jk}^{n_{jk}}:n_{jk}\geq 0,\,\sum n_{jk}=t\right\},

b_{1},\cdots,b_{w}
a basis of

. It’s clear that

.

\displaystyle\liminf_{t\to\infty}\dim V(t+1)/\dim V(t)=1.

[

Theorem 19

(GCD theorem on parabolic Riemann surfaces evaluating at units)
.

Let

be a positive integer. Let

,

Proof.

For a subset

(F,G)

, where

Let

. For each

, we can construct a basis

of

[

]
. Let

. For each

with

, we have

{\bf x}^{\bf i}+\sum_{{\bf j}\in I_{z}}c_{z,{\bf j}}{\bf x}^{\bf j}\in(F,G)_{m}

for some choice

.
Then, for each such
{\bf i}
, there is a linear form
\tilde{L}_{z,{\bf i}}
over
K

We note that since there are correspondingly only finitely many choices of a monomial basis of

. Following the explicit construction in the proof of

and

(3.28)

\displaystyle T_{L_{z,{\bf i}},\sigma}(r)\leq M(T_{F,\sigma}(r)+T_{G,\sigma}(r)),

by
[
9

.
Moreover, the explicit construction also demonstrates that there are only a finitely many choice for

, even though

runs through all of
{\mathcal{Y}}
.

Following the steps in the proof of Theorem 4.3 in
[
9
]

\displaystyle\log|L_{z,{\bf i}}(\Phi({\bf u}))|-\log\|L_{z,{\bf i}}\|_{z}\leq\log|{\bf u}(z)^{\bf i}|+\log\|F\|_{z}+\log\|G\|_{z}+O(1).

\mathcal{Y}

\displaystyle\log^{+}|{\bf u}(z)^{\bf i}|\leq\sum_{j=1}^{n}i_{j}\log^{+}|u_{j}(z)|\leq m\sum_{j=1}^{n}\log^{+}|u_{j}(z)|,

Consequently, by (

(3.32)

are entire functions without common zeros, by multiplying a suitable meromorphic function. By this construction, for each

, the hyperplanes defined by evaluating the linear forms

at

with

and

3.28

3.32

(3.40)

\displaystyle\leq_{\operatorname{exc}}\left(\frac{w}{u}M+\epsilon\right)T_{\Psi,\sigma}(r)+\frac{w}{u}M^{2}(b+2)(T_{F,\sigma}(r)+T_{G,\sigma}(r))

(3.41)

\displaystyle+M^{2}{w^{2}\over u}(\mathfrak{X}^{+}_{\sigma}(r)+\log r)+{1\over u}c_{1/W,\sigma}(r)+O(\log\max_{1\leq j\leq n}T_{u_{j}}(r)),

where

.
By combining (

) with (
3.32
), (
3.38
) and use (

), we get

\displaystyle MN_{\rm gcd,\sigma}(F({\bf u}),G({\bf u}),r)+\frac{1}{u}N_{W,\sigma}(0,r)

\displaystyle\leq_{\operatorname{exc}}\left(\frac{w}{u}M-M+\epsilon\right)T_{\Psi,\sigma}(r)+M^{\prime}mn\max_{1\leq i\leq n}T^{M}_{u_{i},\sigma}(r)

\displaystyle+M\left(\frac{w}{u}M(b+2)+1\right)(T_{F,\sigma}(r)+T_{G,\sigma}(r))

\displaystyle+M^{2}{w^{2}\over u}(\mathfrak{X}^{+}_{\sigma}(r)+\log r)+{1\over u}c_{{1\over W},\sigma}(r)+Mc_{{1\over h},\sigma}(r)+O(1).

\psi_{i}=\phi(\mathbf{u})/h
and
\phi_{i}

Combining this with Lemma

Proof of the Main Theorem.

Let

sufficiently large. Therefore, it remains to consider the situation when

is not rational. In this case, we have

Let

, where

,

, are the coordinate hyperplanes. We consider

with

\sigma
being associated to the discs
\{{\mathbb{D}}(a_{j},2r_{j})\}
, as being constructed in Section
3.1
.
Furthermore, by further shrinking
r_{j}
, we may assume that

(5.1)

\displaystyle|T_{u_{i}}(r)-T^{M}_{u_{i},\sigma}(r)|\leq\log r+O(1)

for

3.15
) and Proposition
12
(ii),
and

\displaystyle\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma}^{+}(r)\leq\sum_{i=0}^{n}N_{f}^{(1)}(H_{i},r)+O(\log r)

).
Since
{\rm mult}_{z_{0}}(f^{*}H_{i})\geq\ell
for all

with

and
for every

Let
u_{i}:=\frac{f_{i}}{f_{0}}
.
Then

.
Let

Hence it is obvious that

(5.4)

\displaystyle N^{(1)}_{\tilde{G}({\mathbf{u}}),\sigma}(0,r)=N^{(1)}_{G(f),\sigma}(0,r).

\epsilon>0

W
be the proper Zariski closed subset
W

) and (

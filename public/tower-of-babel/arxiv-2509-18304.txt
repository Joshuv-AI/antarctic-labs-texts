Convergence, Duality and Well-Posedness in Convex Bilevel Optimization
Khanh-Hung Giang-Tran, Nam Ho-Nguyen, Fatma Kılınç-Karzan, Lingqing Shen

Abstract
We consider the convex bilevel optimization problem, also known as simple bilevel programming . Strong duality of the convex bilevel optimization problem is not guaranteed, due to the lack of Slater constraint qualification. Furthermore, convergence of algorithms is in general not guaranteed without further conditions due to possibility of super-optimal solutions. We show that strong duality (but not necessarily dual solvability) is exactly equivalent to ensuring correct asymptotic convergence of function values. We provide a simple condition that guarantees strong duality. Unfortunately, we show that this is not sufficient to guarantee convergence to the optimal solution set. We draw connections to Levitin-Polyak well-posedness, and leverage this together with our strong duality equivalence to provide another condition that ensures convergence to the optimal solution set with quantifiable bounds.

Introduction

Consider a constrained convex optimization problem given by

\displaystyle g^{*}:=\min_{x\in X}g(x),

(1)

where
X
is a finite-dimensional closed convex set and
g:X\to{\mathbb{R}}
is a convex function. Here,
g^{*}
is the optimal value of
Eq.

1
. In general, problem (
1
) might have multiple optimal solutions, and in this case it is natural to
consider a second objective to select the best solution among the optimal solutions with respect to an additional criterion.

Supposing that this is given by a convex function
f:{X}\to\mathbb{R}
, this leads to the following so-called
convex bilevel optimization problem
(also known as
simple bilevel program
):

\displaystyle f^{*}:=\min_{x\in X_{g}}f(x),\quad\text{where }X_{g}:=\argmin_{z\in X}g(z).

(2)

Here,
f^{*}
denotes the optimal value of the convex bilevel problem
Eq.

2
, and
X_{g}
is the set of minimizers for
Eq.

1
.
Throughout this paper, we assume that problems (
1
) and (
2
) are solvable.
Moreover, we denote an optimal solution of (
2
) by
x^{*}
, satisfying
f(x^{*})=f^{*}
and
g(x^{*})=g^{*}
.

In contrast to a standard single-level convex optimization problem, the presence of two objective functions in the convex bilevel problem (
2
) brings in additional challenges, which we categorize into two groups.
First, we do not have an explicit representation of the optimal set
X_{g}
in general. This rules out the possibility of applying projection-based algorithms as well as the conditional gradient method, making both the projection onto
X_{g}
and the linear optimization oracle over
X_{g}
intractable.

With this first challenge in mind, a stream of literature in this domain has alternatively considered the
value function formulation
of (
2
):

\min_{x\in X}\left\{f(x):\ g(x)\leq g^{*}\right\}.

(3)

This is now a convex optimization problem with a convex functional constraint. However, a critical issue, i.e., our second challenge, is that (
3
) does not satisfy strict feasibility and hence the standard Slater constraint qualification condition fails. As a result, it is possible that the Lagrangian dual of (
3
) is unsolvable, and in such a case (
3
) will violate a critical assumption exploited in the convergence analysis of most primal-dual methods. One may attempt to enforce Slater’s condition by adding a small
\epsilon_{g}>0
to the right-hand side of the constraint, i.e.,
g(x)\leq g^{*}+\epsilon_{g}
, but this approach provides guarantees only in terms of
\epsilon_{g}
-optimality and does not solve the actual problem (
3
).
In fact, it was demonstrated in
[
16
, Appendix D]
that such an approach is numerically unstable.

Contributions.

Approaches to solve single-level optimization problems cannot by default be applied to
Eq.

2
(or equivalently
Eq.

3
), and thus it requires specific algorithms for the bilevel setting, with different analyses. In this short communication, we examine the algorithmic guarantees which exist for various bilevel optimization algorithms, and provide conditions to reassure us of convergence. Algorithmic guarantees for
Eq.

2
generally ensure that an iteratively computed sequence
\{x_{t}\}_{t\geq 1}\subset X
satisfies the following inequalities:

\displaystyle f(x_{t})\leq f^{*}+r_{f,t},\quad g(x_{t})\leq g^{*}+r_{g,t},\quad\textbf{where}\lim_{t\to\infty}r_{f,t}=\lim_{t\to\infty}r_{g,t}=0.

(4)

Unfortunately, as we will demonstrate in
Section

3
, this is
not
sufficient to ensure that
\{x_{t}\}_{t\geq 1}
converges to a solution of
Eq.

2
. Our study aims to analyze this carefully, and provide conditions which ensure convergence. It turns out that this can be studied by examining the regularity of the Lagrange dual of
Eq.

3
. As mentioned above,
Eq.

3
does not satisfy Slater’s condition is by definition. That said, we can still analyze its Lagrange dual, and examine conditions for strong duality to hold. Here, we must differentiate between two cases: (i) one where the optimal dual value is equal to
f^{*}
; and a stronger case where additionally there is an optimal dual multiplier
\lambda^{*}
. We will show that the first case is in fact equivalent to ensuring that sequences satisfying
Eq.

4
will in fact converge to an optimal solution of
Eq.

2
. This uncovers a useful hidden regularity structure in
Eq.

2
that, to the best of our knowledge, has not been elaborated on previously. We conclude the paper by presenting two conditions to ensure that strong duality holds, and examine how these conditions have been implicitly present in existing algorithmic work.

The set

f

X

Eq.

Eq.

X^{*}:=\argmin_{x\in X_{g}}f(x)=\{x\in X_{g}:f(x)=f^{*}\},

Super-optimality and Lagrange duality

The central issue in solving
Eq.

2
is, in practice, we very rarely encounter a feasible solution
\bar{x}\in X_{g}
. Instead, algorithms will generate a point
x_{t}\in X
with
g(x_{t})>g^{*}
. Since
X_{g}\subset\left\{x\in X:g(x)\leq g(x_{t})\right\}
, the point
x_{t}
may be
super
-optimal for
Eq.

2
, meaning that
f(x_{t})<f^{*}
. As iterative algorithms progress, the accuracy of
x_{t}
(hopefully) improves, i.e., it is shown that
g(x_{t})\leq g^{*}+r_{g,t}
where
r_{g,t}\geq 0
are error terms such that
r_{g,t}\to 0
. Since
g(x_{t})\geq g^{*}
, this implies that
\lim_{t\to\infty}g(x_{t})=g^{*}
. For the outer-level objective
f
, it is often similarly shown that
f(x_{t})\leq f^{*}+r_{f,t}
, where
r_{f,t}\geq 0
are error terms such that
r_{f,t}\to 0
. Since we might have
f(x_{t})<f^{*}
, these inequalities are only sufficient to deduce

\limsup_{t\to\infty}f(x_{t})\leq f^{*},\quad\lim_{t\to\infty}g(x_{t})=g^{*},

(5)

but they do
not
guarantee
f(x_{t})\to f^{*}
. Indeed, we may have a subsequence
\{x_{t_{k}}\}_{k\geq 1}
for which
\underline{f}:=\lim_{k\to\infty}f(x_{t_{k}})<f^{*}
. On the other hand, it is straightforward to show that any limit point of
\{x_{t}\}_{t\geq 1}
is an optimal solution of
Eq.

2
. That said, it is difficult to infer in an online fashion whether the current
f(x_{t})
is close to
f^{*}
or
\underline{f}
. In other words, we have no assurance of the approximation quality of
f(x_{t})
.

We now present an example that demonstrates how examining
f(x_{t})
without further assurances can indeed be misleading for estimating
f^{*}
.

Example 1
.

Suppose that

\displaystyle f(x)

\displaystyle:=x_{2}^{2}-2x_{2}+[100-x_{1}]_{+}^{2},\quad g(x):=\frac{x_{2}^{2}}{x_{1}},\quad X:=\left\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}:\,x_{1}\geq 1,~0\leq x_{2}\leq 1\right\}.

(6)

Clearly,
f,g,X
are convex. Furthermore, we have
X_{g}=\{(s,0)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}:\,s\geq 1\}
,
g^{*}=0
,
X^{*}=\{(s,0)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}:s\geq 100\}
and
f^{*}=0
. However, consider now the sequence
\{x_{t}\}_{t\geq 1}\subset X
given by
x_{t}:=(t,1)
for
t\geq 1
. Then
g(x_{t})=1/t\to 0=g^{*}
, but
f(x_{t})=[100-t]_{+}^{2}-1\to-1<f^{*}
. Thus,
\{x_{t}\}_{t\geq 1}
satisfies
Eq.

5
but
f(x_{t})
is not a good estimate for
f^{*}
.

To guard against situations like in
Example

1
, and recognizing the numerous algorithmic works which provide guarantees of the form
Eq.

5
, we look for conditions which ensure that whenever
Eq.

5
holds, the following also holds:

\lim_{t\to\infty}f(x_{t})=f^{*},\quad\lim_{t\to\infty}g(x_{t})=g^{*}.

(7)

Another possible condition we consider is the that the distance to the optimal solution set of
Eq.

2
decreases to
0
:

\lim_{t\to\infty}\Dist(x_{t},X^{*})=0,\quad\text{where }\Dist(x,X^{*}):=\inf_{z\in X^{*}}\|x-z\|_{2}.

(8)

Interestingly, we uncover a relationship between Lagrange duality and
Eq.

7
. The Lagrange dual of
Eq.

2
is obtained via
Eq.

3
, and is given by

d^{*}:=\sup_{\lambda\geq 0}q(\lambda),\quad\text{where}\quad q(\lambda):=\inf_{x\in X}\left\{f(x)+\lambda(g(x)-g^{*})\right\}.

(9)

Here,
\lambda\geq 0
is the dual variable,
q(\cdot)
is the (concave) dual objective function, and
d^{*}
is the optimal dual value. Since
g(x)\geq g^{*}
for any
x\in X
, we can deduce that
q(\lambda)
is non-decreasing in
\lambda
. This means that
d^{*}=\sup_{\lambda\geq 0}q(\lambda)=\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}q(\lambda)
. Recalling that
f^{*}
is the optimal value of
Eq.

3
, by weak duality we always have
d^{*}\leq f^{*}
. We will use the following definition of strong duality.

Definition 1
.

We say that
strong duality
holds for
Eq.

2
when
d^{*}=f^{*}
.

Note that
Definition

1
does
not
guarantee that the dual is solvable, but only that
q(\lambda)\to f^{*}
as
\lambda\to\infty
. Therefore, ensuring that strong duality holds requires much weaker conditions than ensuring that the dual is solvable, as was studied in previous works
[
12
,
8
,
11
]
.

Our main result is that ensuring convergence
Eq.

7
is
exactly equivalent
to strong duality holding for
Eq.

2
.

Theorem 1
.

Considering
Eq.

2
when
f,g,X
are convex, the following are equivalent:

1.

Any sequence
\{x_{t}\}_{t\geq 1}\subset X
that satisfies
Eq.

5
also satiesfies
Eq.

7
.

2.

Strong duality (
Definition

1
) holds for
Eq.

2
.

We will use the following lemma in the proof of
Theorem

1
, as well as subsequent results.

Lemma 1
.

There exists a sequence
\{x_{t}\}_{t\geq 1}\subset X
such that
g(x_{t})\to g^{*}
and
\limsup_{t\to\infty}f(x_{t})\leq d^{*}
.

Proof of
Lemma

1
.

We consider two cases:
d^{*}>-\infty
and
d^{*}=\infty
. In the first case, there must exist some
\lambda_{0}\geq 0
such that
q(\lambda_{0})=\inf_{x\in X}\left\{f(x)+\lambda_{0}(g(x)-g^{*})\right\}>-\infty
, and consequently,
q(\lambda)\geq q(\lambda_{0})>-\infty
for any
\lambda\geq\lambda_{0}
. For each
t\geq 1
, choose
x_{t}\in X
to be a point that satisfies
f(x_{t})+(t+\lambda_{0})(g(x_{t})-g^{*})\leq q(t+\lambda_{0})+1/t
. We also have
f(x_{t})+\lambda_{0}(g(x_{t})-g^{*})\geq q(\lambda_{0})
, so combining with
f(x_{t})+(t+\lambda_{0})(g(x_{t})-g^{*})\leq q(t+\lambda_{0})
we get

g(x_{t})-g^{*}\leq\frac{1}{t}\left(q(t+\lambda_{0})+1/t-q(\lambda_{0})\right).

This implies that
g(x_{t})\to g^{*}
. Now observe that

\displaystyle f(x_{t})+\lambda_{0}(g(x_{t})-g^{*})

\displaystyle\leq f(x_{t})+(t+\lambda_{0})(g(x_{t})-g^{*})

\displaystyle\leq q(t+\lambda_{0})+1/t.

Taking limit superior of all terms in the inequality, we have

\limsup_{t\to\infty}f(x_{t})=\limsup_{t\to\infty}\left(f(x_{t})+\lambda_{0}(g(x_{t})-g^{*})\right)\leq\limsup_{t\to\infty}\left(q(t+\lambda_{0})+1/t\right)=d^{*}.

The first equality follows because
g(x_{t})\to g^{*}
, and the last equality follows since
\lim_{t\to\infty}=d^{*}
.

Now we consider the second case when
d^{*}=-\infty
. Then for
t\geq 1
, let
z_{t}\in X
be a point such that
f(z_{t})+t(g(z_{t})-g^{*})\leq-t
, and define
\theta_{t}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}\max\{1,g(z_{t})-g^{*}\}}\in[0,1]
. Let
x^{*}\in X^{*}
be a solution to
Eq.

2
, and define
x_{t}=\theta_{t}z_{t}+(1-\theta_{t})x^{*}\in X
. Notice that

g(x_{t})\leq\theta_{t}g(z_{t})+(1-\theta_{t})g(x^{*})=g^{*}+\theta_{t}(g(z_{t})-g^{*})=g^{*}+\frac{g(z_{t})-g^{*}}{\sqrt{t}\max\{1,g(z_{t})-g^{*}\}}\leq g^{*}+1/\sqrt{t}\to g^{*}
. Therefore
\lim_{t\to\infty}g(x_{t})=g^{*}
. On the other hand,
f(x_{t})\leq\theta_{t}f(z_{t})+(1-\theta_{t})f(x^{*})
, and by definition of
z_{t}
, we have
f(z_{t})\leq-t(1+g(z_{t})-g^{*})
, so
\theta_{t}f(z_{t})\leq-\frac{\sqrt{t}(1+g(z_{t})-g^{*})}{\max\{1,g(z_{t})-g^{*}\}}
. But this implies
\theta_{t}f(z_{t})\to-\infty
as
t\to\infty
, therefore
\limsup_{t\to\infty}f(x_{t})=-\infty=d^{*}
.
∎

Proof of
Theorem

1
.

Suppose that strong duality holds. Let
\{x_{t}\}_{t\geq 1}
be a sequence satisfying
Eq.

5
. Then given any
\epsilon>0
, there exists
\lambda_{\epsilon}\in[0,\infty)
such that

\inf_{x\in X}\left\{f(x)+\lambda_{\epsilon}(g(x)-g^{*})\right\}\geq f^{*}-\epsilon.

This implies that for any
t\geq 1
we have

f(x_{t})\geq f^{*}-\lambda_{\epsilon}(g(z_{t})-g^{*})-\epsilon
. By taking limit inferior as
t\to\infty
on both sides, and recognizing that
\lim_{t\to\infty}g(x_{t})=g^{*}
, we obtain
\liminf_{t\to\infty}f(x_{t})\geq f^{*}-\epsilon
. Since
\epsilon>0
is chosen arbitrarily,
\liminf_{t\to\infty}f(z_{t})\geq f^{*}
, which implies (
7
).

Suppose now that strong duality does not hold, so
d^{*}=\sup_{\lambda\geq 0}q(\lambda)<f^{*}
.
Lemma

1
states that there exists a sequence
\{x_{t}\}_{t\geq 1}
such that
g(x_{t})\to g^{*}
and
\limsup_{t\to\infty}f(x_{t})\leq d^{*}
. Since
d^{*}<f^{*}
,
Eq.

5
holds for
\{x_{t}\}_{t\geq 1}
, but not
Eq.

7
.

3
Conditions for strong duality and convergence

We now provide two conditions to ensure that strong duality holds for
Eq.

2
, thereby (equivalently) ensuring that any algorithm which guarantees
Eq.

5
has assurance that
Eq.

7
holds also.

Condition 1
.

If
\{x_{t}\}_{t\geq 1}\subset X
is a sequence such that
g(x_{t})\to g^{*}
, we have
\Dist(x_{t},X_{g})\to 0
.

Condition 2
.

The set of optimal solutions
X^{*}
for
Eq.

2
is compact.

1
generalizes, for example, error bound-type conditions on
g
, where
\tau\Dist(x,X_{g})^{r}\leq g(x)-g^{*}
. Error bounds are used in several works
[
16
,
5
,
20
, e.g.,]
to enable stronger guarantees for algorithms solving
Eq.

2
.

2
can be shown to be equivalent to coercivity of the functions
h=\max\{f,g\}
and
\bar{h}=\max\{f-f^{*},g-g^{*}\}
.
2
, or more restrictive versions of it, has appeared in various works.
Cabot 2005
,
Solodov 2006
,
Solodov 2007
,
Malitsky 2017
,
Dempe et al. 2019
assume exactly
2
.
Helou and Simões 2017
,
Amini and Yousefian 2019
,
Kaushik and Yousefian 2021
,
Shen et al. 2023
,
Giang-Tran et al. 2024
,
Jiang et al. 2023
,
Cao et al. 2024
employ the assumption that
X
is bounded to prove guarantees for their algorithms. This immediately implies
2
holds. Finally,
Beck and Sabach 2014
,
Sabach and Shtern 2017
,
Shehu et al. 2021
,
Doron and Shtern 2022
,
Merchav and Sabach 2023
use the assumption that
f
is coercive, which also implies
2
. This is commonly motivated by learning problems where
g
models the prediction error and
f
is a regularization function, e.g., the
\ell_{1}
- or
\ell_{2}
-norm.

1
guarantees that strong duality holds, and hence
Eq.

5
implies
Eq.

7
.

Proposition 1
.

Suppose that
1
holds for
Eq.

2
. Then strong duality holds, and, consequently, any sequence satisfying
Eq.

5
also satisfies
Eq.

7
.

Proof of
Proposition

1
.

Suppose that
\{x_{t}\}_{t\geq 1}
is a sequence satisfying the premise of
Lemma

1
:
g(x_{t})\to g^{*}
and
\limsup_{t\to\infty}f(x_{t})\leq d^{*}
. Let
x^{*}\in X^{*}
be an optimal solution to
Eq.

2
, and define the sequence
\{y_{t}\}_{t\geq 1}\subset X_{g}
by
y_{t}:=\argmin_{y\in X_{g}}\|x_{t}-y\|_{2}
. (Note that the minimizer is unique since the
\ell_{2}
-norm is strictly convex.) By convexity of
f
, we have

\displaystyle f(x_{t})-f^{*}

\displaystyle=f(x_{t})-f(x^{*})

\displaystyle\geq\nabla f(x^{*})^{\top}(x_{t}-x^{*})

\displaystyle=\nabla f(x^{*})^{\top}(x_{t}-y_{t})+\nabla f(x^{*})^{\top}(y_{t}-x^{*})

\displaystyle\geq-\|\nabla f(x^{*})\|_{2}\|x_{t}-y_{t}\|_{2}+\nabla f(x^{*})^{\top}(y_{t}-x^{*})

\displaystyle\geq-\|\nabla f(x^{*})\|_{2}\|x_{t}-y_{t}\|_{2}.

The second inequality follows from the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, while the third follows since
\nabla f(x^{*})^{\top}(y_{t}-x^{*})\geq 0
by the optimality of
x^{*}
in minimizing
f
over
X_{g}
, and the fact that
y_{t}\in X_{g}
. By
1
, we have
\|x_{t}-y_{t}\|_{2}=\Dist(x_{t},X_{g})\to 0
since
g(x_{t})\to g^{*}
. Therefore, the limit inferior of both sides is

\liminf_{t\to\infty}f(x_{t})-f^{*}\geq 0.

In summary, we have

f^{*}\leq\liminf_{t\to\infty}f(x_{t})\leq\limsup_{t\to\infty}f(x_{t})\leq d^{*},

so strong duality holds.
∎

on its own is not enough to guarantee that

Example 2
.

Define

2
is more powerful than

Definition 2

(
Huang and Yang 2006
)
.

We say that

•

Levitin-Polyak (LP) well-posedness
if, for any sequence
\{x_{t}\}_{t\geq 1}\subset X
which satisfies
\Dist(x,X_{g})\to 0
and

\{x_{t_{k}}\}_{k\geq 1}\subset\{x_{t}\}_{t\geq 1}
and
\bar{x}\in X^{*}
such that
x_{t_{k}}\to\bar{x}
.

•

generalized LP well-posedness
if, for any sequence that satisfies
Eq.

7
, there exists a subsequence
\{x_{t_{k}}\}_{k\geq 1}\subset\{x_{t}\}_{t\geq 1}
and
\bar{x}\in X^{*}
such that
x_{t_{k}}\to\bar{x}
.

•

strong generalized LP well-posedness
if, for any sequence that satisfies
Eq.

5
, there exists a subsequence
\{x_{t_{k}}\}_{k\geq 1}\subset\{x_{t}\}_{t\geq 1}
and
\bar{x}\in X^{*}
such that
x_{t_{k}}\to\bar{x}
.

Conclusion

We studied convergence guarantees for the convex bilevel problem (
2

1

5

3

7

Theorem

1
).

1

2

8

2

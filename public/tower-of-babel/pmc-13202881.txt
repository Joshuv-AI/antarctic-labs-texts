The clinical Epidemiological Characteristics and Diagnosis/Treatment of Brucellosis infection Patients in Gansu Province: A retrospective study from 2019 to 2022
Authors: Zhang Pei, Liu Hongwei, Bai Zhao, Yang Yongxin, Li Zhidong, Zhang Chuan, Meng Ling
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundBrucellosis remains a significant public health concern in livestock-raising regions of China, including Gansu Province. Understanding its epidemiological characteristics and clinical presentation is crucial for improving disease management and control strategies.ObjectiveThis study aimed to analyze the clinical and epidemiological characteristics of patients with brucellosis in Gansu Province to provide evidence for early diagnosis and treatment.MethodsA retrospective study was conducted using data from all patients tested for Brucella spp. serology at Lanzhou University Second Hospital between 2019 and 2022. Epidemiological features, initial clinical departments, symptoms, and antibiotic use among hospitalized confirmed cases (n = 2507) were statistically analyzed.ResultsAmong 11,255 individuals tested, 2,507 were positive for brucellosis, yielding an overall positivity rate of 22.28%. The positivity rate declined from 27.29% in 2019 to 15.81% in 2021, followed by a slight increase to 20.76% in 2022. Most confirmed cases were male (57.60%), farmers (26.51%), and individuals aged 31–60 years. The primary care department (55.25%) was the most common point of first contact. Among 440 hospitalized patients, the most frequent clinical manifestations were lower back pain (40.68%), fatigue (21.14%), and arthralgia (12.27%). Ceftriaxone was the most commonly used monotherapy antibiotic (17.05%), while the combination of ceftriaxone and doxycycline was the predominant dual-therapy regimen (8.18%).ConclusionThis study delineates the profile of high-risk populations and current management practices for brucellosis in Gansu. The findings underscore the necessity for enhanced health education among key groups like farmers and herdsmen, and improved screening and diagnostic awareness in healthcare institutions, particularly in primary care settings, to facilitate early detection and treatment.

Introduction

Brucellosis is a significant zoonotic disease caused by bacteria of the genus Brucella, posing a substantial public health and economic burden globally, particularly in developing countries [1]. The pathogen primarily resides in domestic animals, and human infection typically occurs through direct contact with infected animals, consumption of unpasteurized dairy products, or inhalation of contaminated aerosols [2]. Upon entering the human body, Brucella can persist within the mononuclear phagocyte system, triggering a complex infection-immunopathological response that ultimately leads to chronic, multi-system, and multi-organ damage [3].

The clinical presentation of brucellosis is highly non-specific. Early symptoms often include fever, profuse sweating, fatigue, and joint pain, which are easily mistaken for influenza-like illnesses, leading to frequent missed or misdiagnosis in clinical practice [4]. Diagnostic delay is a critical factor contributing to the chronicity and complication of the infection. It can result in severe sequelae such as spondylitis, arthritis, endocarditis, and neurological involvement, significantly increasing treatment difficulty and imposing persistent physical suffering and substantial economic burdens on patients [5, 6]. In recent years, the global epidemiological landscape of brucellosis has been dynamic. While control measures have mitigated outbreaks in some traditional endemic areas, new hotspots continue to emerge, indicating that the overall disease burden remains severe and that brucellosis persists as an under-prioritized global health issue [7].

In China, the reported incidence of brucellosis has shown a marked upward trend, with its spatial distribution expanding from traditional pastoral areas to agricultural regions and urban fringes, demonstrating characteristics of “dissemination” and “urbanization” [8, 9]. Our hospital is located in a historical endemic area for brucellosis on the Loess Plateau in Northwest China. The vast region it serves contains numerous large-scale animal husbandry operations and household(scattered breeders), creating a potential reservoir for the persistent transmission of brucellosis and presenting ongoing challenges for disease control in the region. Therefore, this study aims to systematically analyze the epidemiological characteristics and clinical management data of brucellosis patients from our hospital between 2019 and 2022, to deeply elucidate the local epidemic patterns, clinical features, and current management status, thereby providing evidence-based guidance for optimizing regional early diagnosis strategies and enhancing precise clinical care.

Objects and methods

Study objects

This single-center retrospective observational study included all individuals who underwent Brucella serological testing (Rose Bengal Test (RBT), Liaoning, China,20173403128) and/or (Standard Tube Agglutination Test (SAT), Liaoning, China, 20173403127) at Lanzhou University Second Hospital between January 1, 2019, and December 31, 2022. A sequential testing strategy was adopted for the diagnosis of brucellosis. The highly sensitive RBT was used first as a screening test to minimize the risk of missed diagnosis; RBT-positive samples were then confirmed using the highly specific SAT to exclude false positives and achieve an accurate diagnosis through titer quantification. The RBT and SAT used in this study achieved a sensitivity of > 95% and a specificity of > 95%, respectively.

The diagnosis of confirmed brucellosis cases strictly adhered to the “Diagnosis of Brucellosis (WS 269–2019)” standard issued by the National Health Commission of the People’s Republic of China [10]. This standard employs a comprehensive diagnostic principle, integrating epidemiological history (including residence in an endemic area, occupational exposure, history of contact with suspect animal products, etc.), clinical manifestations (such as fever, sweating, arthralgia, fatigue, hepatosplenomegaly, lymphadenopathy, etc.), and laboratory findings. Laboratory confirmation was based on either (1) a four-fold or greater rise in SAT titer (demonstrating seroconversion), or (2) a single SAT titer ≥ 1:100 (++), or (3) the isolation of Brucella from blood, bone marrow, other body fluids, or lesion specimens.

This study was undertaken according to the tenets of the Declaration of Helsinki 1975 and its later amendments. The study protocol was approved by the Ethics Committee of Lanzhou University Second Hospital. Informed consent was waived by the by the Ethics Committee of Lanzhou University Second Hospital due to the purely retrospective nature of the study and the anonymization of patient data.

Research methods and data analysis

Data were comprehensively extracted from the Hospital Information System and Laboratory Information System for all eligible patients. Collected and systematically organized data included the following dimensions: ① General epidemiological data: sex, age, occupation, place of residence; ② Clinical data: initial visiting department, primary clinical symptoms and signs; ③ Laboratory test results: Brucella spp. serology results (RBT/SAT titers) and other relevant routine blood, biochemical, and inflammatory markers; ④ Treatment and outcome information: details of antibiotic use in hospitalized patients (drug type, route of administration, combination regimens), records of surgical interventions, and discharge diagnoses.

All data were cross-checked. Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS 26.0 software. Categorical data were described using frequencies (n) and percentages (%). Differences in the distribution of male and female patients across different years, occupational categories, age groups, and hospitalization status were compared using Pearson’s chi-square test (χ² test). All statistical tests were two-sided, and a p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Epidemiological profile

From 2019 to 2022, a total of 11,255 subjects underwent brucellosis serological testing in this study. All subjects were initially screened using the Rose Bengal Test (RBT), which yielded 2,507 positive cases, corresponding to an initial RBT positivity rate of 22.28% (2,507/11,255). As a highly sensitive screening tool, RBT typically achieves a sensitivity of over 95%, enabling effective detection of low-concentration antibodies and minimizing the risk of missed diagnosis.All 2,507 RBT-positive samples were subsequently subjected to the Serum Agglutination Test (SAT) for confirmation. The SAT results identified 1,197 positive cases and 1,276 negative cases, yielding a confirmed positivity rate of 47.75% (1,197/2,507) among RBT-positive individuals, accounting for 10.64% (1,197/11,255) of the total study population. As a confirmatory method, SAT demonstrates high specificity (typically > 95%), effectively excluding false-positive results that may arise from initial RBT screening. The 1,276 cases that were RBT-positive but SAT-negative likely reflect false positives attributable to RBT’s high sensitivity, further underscoring the necessity of a sequential diagnostic strategy combining a highly sensitive screening test with a highly specific confirmatory test in brucellosis diagnosis.

By year, 122 cases were detected in 2019 (annual positivity rate 27.29%), 1,720 in 2020 (24.13%), 317 in 2021 (15.81%), and 348 in 2022 (20.76%). The overall trend showed a continuous decline in the positivity rate from 2019 to 2021, followed by a slight rebound in 2022 (Fig. 1; Table 1).

Demographic distribution

Among the 2,507 Brucella-positive patients, 1,444 (57.60%) were male and 1,063 (42.40%) were female. Chi-square tests revealed that the proportion of male patients was significantly higher than that of females across different years, occupations, age groups, and hospitalization status (all p < 0.05). The occupational distribution of positive cases was primarily farmers (601 cases, 26.51% of positives), followed by office workers/cadres (433 cases, 25.41%), students (301 cases, 17.37%), and self-employed individuals (164 cases, 21.35%). Age distribution showed 178 positive patients aged ≤ 10 years (positivity rate 10.45% in this tested age group), 104 aged 11–20 (12.05%), 389 aged 21–30 (21.54%), 589 aged 31–40 (29.49%), 452 aged 41–50 (28.55%), 522 aged 51–60 (27.23%), 215 aged 61–70 (21.14%), and 58 aged > 70 years (15.72%). Among all positive patients, 440 (11.46%) were hospitalized for further treatment (Table 1).

Distribution of initial clinical departments

The distribution of the first outpatient departments visited by the 2,507 Brucella-positive patients was as follows: General Practice Department (1,385 cases, 55.25%), Orthopedics Department (314 cases, 12.52%), Convenience Clinic (274 cases, 10.93%), Emergency Department (127 cases, 5.07%), Rheumatology and Immunology Department (48 cases, 1.91%), and Other Departments (359 cases, 14.32%). The male-to-female ratio varied across different departments (Table 2; Fig. 2).

Antibiotic use in hospitalized brucellosis patients

Analysis of antibiotic use among hospitalized positive patients showed that 90 patients (20.45%) did not receive antibiotic therapy. Among monotherapy regimens, ceftriaxone was the most frequently used (75 cases, 17.05%), followed by doxycycline (20 cases, 4.55%). For dual therapy, the combination of ceftriaxone and doxycycline was the most common (36 cases, 8.18%), followed equally by ceftriaxone + levofloxacin and cefazolin + ceftriaxone (15 cases each, 3.41%) (Table 4).

Discussion

The four-year data from this study reveal dynamic changes in the detection rate of brucellosis in Gansu, with fluctuations consistent with the macro-background of brucellosis in China, which remains at a high level with periodic oscillations [8]. Between July and August 2019, a public health incident occurred at a Lanzhou-based veterinary vaccine production facility, which later became known as the Lanzhou brucellosis event. The improper disposal of aerosolized waste, stemming from the use of expired disinfectants and inadequate sterilization during the production of a live animal vaccine, resulted in the release of the Brucella pathogen. Consequently, residents in the surrounding area and staff at the Lanzhou Veterinary Research Institute were infected. Notably, the peak in testing and confirmed cases in 2020 was directly related to large-scale screening following the “Lanzhou Brucellosis Incident”, highlighting the significant impact of public health emergencies on surveillance systems. Such “case surges” due to intensified monitoring are not uncommon in the management of zoonotic diseases [11].

Our findings showed that males, farmers, and middle-aged adults constitute the core high-risk groups for brucellosis infection, aligning with the primary transmission routes of Brucella through occupational exposure [9]. A noteworthy observation is that “office workers/cadres” formed the second-largest occupational category of infection. This likely reflects the prevalence of amateur family animal husbandry and self-sufficiency in fresh milk consumption in this region, indicating that brucellosis exposure risk extends beyond traditional agricultural boundaries and necessitates more refined occupational risk assessments [12]. Furthermore, the 10.45% infection rate among children aged 0–10 years serves as a critical warning. Studies consistently identify the consumption of unpasteurized dairy products as the primary risk factor for brucellosis in children [13, 14]. This strongly advocates for placing “dairy product safety” at the core of public health campaigns, especially targeting households with children.

Female detection rates dropped more than male rates, particularly in 2021 (11.8% vs. 18.7%), There are two possible reasons for this phenomenon. First, the number of male patients seeking medical attention increased significantly in 2021. A likely explanation is that, following the 2020 Lanzhou Brucellosis Event, self-awareness among rural livestock breeders and herders with relevant symptoms improved, leading them to seek testing. Concurrently, physicians’ awareness and inclination to order diagnostic tests for suspected cases also increased. Second, according to data from the Gansu Provincial Bureau of Statistics’ “Statistical Bulletin on National Economic and Social Development”, the cattle and sheep inventory in Gansu Province showed a continuous growth trend from 2019 to 2022, with a particularly notable growth rate of 11.3% in 2021. This increase in livestock farming activities may have contributed to the higher number of patients seeking medical attention.

Over half (55.25%) of the patients first presented to General Practice, Convenience Clinics, and the Emergency Department. This distribution poses a significant challenge for early diagnosis. The non-specific, influenza-like symptoms of brucellosis easily lead to its concealment among common illnesses, causing diagnostic delays. Substantial evidence indicates that diagnostic delay is the foremost risk factor for the chronicity of brucellosis and the development of severe complications such as osteoarticular and neurological lesions [15]. In this study, the high proportion of orthopedic inpatients (62.75%) ultimately requiring surgical intervention is a direct manifestation of the severe organic damage resulting from diagnostic delays, consistent with reports of high rates of osteoarticular complications [16]. Therefore, there is an urgent need to enhance the vigilance of non-specialist outpatient physicians towards brucellosis and to establish and promote a clinical pathway for “routine Brucella serological screening in patients with unexplained fever and pain who have a relevant epidemiological history (e.g., contact history, suspect dietary history).” This is key to overcoming the challenge of diagnostic delay [17].

The antibiotic usage patterns observed in our hospitalized patients reflect practices guided by Chinese clinical guidelines. The widespread use of ceftriaxone demonstrates clinicians’ consideration for ensuring efficacy and rapidly controlling the condition. However, this practice differs from first-line regimens recommended by international guidelines, such as those from the World Health Organization, which favor doxycycline combined with an aminoglycoside (streptomycin or gentamicin) or rifampin [18]. Multiple systematic reviews and meta-analyses have confirmed the superior efficacy of the doxycycline-aminoglycoside combination in reducing relapse rates compared to the doxycycline-rifampin regimen, despite the need to monitor for nephrotoxicity and ototoxicity [19, 20].

This discrepancy may stem from factors such as drug availability, convenience of administration (intravenous ceftriaxone), and concerns about specific drug adverse effects. In recent years, fluoroquinolone-containing regimens (e.g., with levofloxacin) have gained attention due to their oral convenience and favorable tolerability, with some studies showing non-inferior efficacy compared to traditional regimens, offering an alternative treatment option [20, 21]. Concurrently, the potential threat of antibiotic resistance demands vigilance. Recent antimicrobial susceptibility monitoring data from China indicate that Brucella remains generally susceptible to first-line drugs, but sensitivity to agents like trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole has declined, underscoring the importance of continuous microbiological surveillance [22]. Future prospective studies from this region are needed to find the optimal balance between efficacy, safety, and convenience.

This study has several limitations. First, as a single-center retrospective study, the generalizability of its findings may be limited, and selection bias might exist. Second, the lack of detailed treatment and follow-up data for outpatient patients prevents a comprehensive evaluation of treatment outcomes and relapse rates for all brucellosis patients in the hospital. Future research should aim for multi-center, prospective cohort studies incorporating long-term follow-up endpoints.

In conclusion, this study delineates the clinical epidemiological characteristics of brucellosis in Gansu Province from 2019 to 2022, and identifies farmers, middle-aged males, and children as the key populations for targeted prevention and control. Strategies such as paying attention to dairy product safety and occupational protection, improving the screening and diagnostic capabilities of primary and general medical institutions, and establishing an integrated “Prevention-Screening-Diagnosis- Treatment” system are of great significance for effectively reducing the impact of brucellosis on population health.

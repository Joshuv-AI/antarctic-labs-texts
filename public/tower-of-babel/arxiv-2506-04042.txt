Unveiling and Eliminating the Shortcut Learning for Locate-Then-Edit Knowledge Editing via Both Subject and Relation Awareness
Xiyu Liu, Zhengxiao Liu, Naibin Gu, Zheng Lin, Ji Xiang, Weiping Wang

Abstract
Knowledge editing aims to alternate the target knowledge predicted by large language models while ensuring the least side effects on unrelated knowledge. An effective way to achieve knowledge editing is to identify pivotal parameters for predicting factual associations and modify them with an optimization process to update the predictions. However, these locate-then-edit methods are uncontrollable since they tend to modify most unrelated relations connected to the subject of target editing. We unveil that this failure of controllable editing is due to a shortcut learning issue during the optimization process. Specifically, we discover two crucial features that are the subject feature and the relation feature for models to learn during optimization, but the current optimization process tends to over-learning the subject feature while neglecting the relation feature. To eliminate this shortcut learning of the subject feature, we propose a novel two-stage optimization process that balances the learning of the subject feature and the relation feature. Experimental results demonstrate that our approach successfully prevents knowledge editing from shortcut learning and achieves the optimal overall performance, contributing to controllable knowledge editing. 1 1 footnotemark: 1 0 0 footnotetext: Our code is released in https://github.com/sunshower-liu/TOP.

Introduction

Large language models (LLMs)
minaee2024largelanguagemodelssurvey
;
zhao2024surveylargelanguagemodels
have garnered a significant amount of attention nowadays, especially known for their proficiency in natural language understanding and generation by utilizing knowledge encoded in parameters
heinzerling-inui-2021-language
;
wang-etal-2021-generative
;
roberts-etal-2020-much
. However, some factual knowledge may become outdated overtime and there is also the possibility that some facts are learned incorrectly or overlooked during pre-training of LLMs. Knowledge editing methods are proposed to alter the prediction of a small amount of factual associations permanently while having the least impact on irrelevant facts.

A prominent line of previous works achieve knowledge editing via localizing the decisive parameters during the recall of factual associations of auto-regressive transformer language models and then modify the located parameters with regularization constrained updates, known as the locate-then-edit methods
zhang2024comprehensivestudyknowledgeediting
;
meng2022locating
;
Meng2022MassEditingMI
;
fang2024alphaeditnullspaceconstrainedknowledge
. However, these methods are uncontrollable, frequently resulting in unexpected alterations to most unrelated factual associations linked to the subject of the edited factual association.
liu2024relationknowsrethinkingrecall
;
li2024unveiling
. Such uncontrollable knowledge editing process makes the edited model untrustworthy.

Figure 1:
We unveil the shortcut learning issue of current locate-then-edit (i.e. ROME-like) knowledge editing methods that leads to unexpected alterations of facts with unrelated relations. The shortcut learning refers to the over-learning of the subject feature while neglecting the relation feature during the optimization process of editing.

In this work, we unveil that this failure of locate-then-edit methods stems from a shortcut learning
song2024shortcutlearningincontextlearning
issue during the optimization process of editing, as exemplified by the investigation on the representative ROME editing
meng2022locating
. Specifically, through the analysis of the significance of gradients at different token positions during the optimization of ROME, we discover that the hidden representations traversing the layers at the last subject token position and the last relation token position are two crucial features that models learn during the optimization process. However, further exploration into the contribution of these two features to the predictions of factual associations in edited models shows that
ROME editing exhibits a bias towards over-learning the feature at the last subject position while inadequately learning the feature at the last relation position
. Since subject-related attributes are mainly recalled at the last subject token position
meng2022locating
and relation-related attributes are mainly recalled at the last relation token position
liu2024relationknowsrethinkingrecall
(i.e. typically the last token position), the two features can be regarded as the subject feature and the relation feature respectively. In conclusion, ROME editing falls into the trap of shortcut learning of the subject feature while neglecting the relation feature, resulting in uncontrollable editing (Fig.
1
).

To eliminate the shortcut learning issue, we further point out that the direct cause of shortcut learning is the flawed design of the loss function during optimization. The objective of the loss function in the current optimization process is simply the negative log-likelihood of the subject feature when given the target object token, which neglects the relation feature. In order to balance the learning of both the relation feature, we amend the objective loss function and decompose it under approximate conditional independence of the subject feature and the relation feature. Accordingly, we design a
T
wo-stage
O
ptimization
P
rocess for controllable knowledge editing (
TOP
). At the first stage, the relation feature is optimized to obtain the new object token prediction. At the second stage, the subject feature is optimized to obtain the updated relation feature. Thus the located MLP down-projection sublayer can be modified via both subject and relation awareness.

Evaluation results on the widely used knowledge editing benchmark demonstrate that this simple change of optimization strategy effectively alleviates the shortcut issue for locate-then-edit knowledge editing and exhibits the most balanced performance. This work contributes to controllable editing and we hope it can inspire more practical editing approaches with less side effects.

To sum up, our contributions are as follows:

•

This work conducts in-depth analyses on the cause of unexpected alterations of unrelated facts by unveiling the shortcut learning of the subject feature while neglecting the relation feature for locate-then-edit knowledge editing.

•

We propose a simple yet effective two-stage optimization process for locate-then-edit editing to balance the learning of the subject feature and the relation feature.

•

Experimental results demonstrate that our optimization strategy successfully alleviates shortcut learning and obtains the optimal overall performance, which makes a great step towards controllable editing.

Related Works

Knowledge editing aims to alter the prediction of language models within the specific domain related to the edited fact while avoiding side effects on unrelated facts
yao-etal-2023-editing
;
wang2024knowledgeeditinglargelanguage
. One line of works achieve knowledge editing through parameter-preserving strategies
wang2024wiserethinkingknowledgememory
;
pmlr-v162-mitchell22a
;
10.5555/3666122.3668201
. They utilize additional memories or extra trainable parameters for learning new facts and freeze the original parameters of models. Another line of works adopt parameter-modifying strategy for knowledge editing, including meta-learning
de-cao-etal-2021-editing
;
mitchell2022fast
;
tan2024massiveeditinglargelanguage
and locate-then-edit editing. We focus on Locate-then-edit editing which garners significant interest for its outstanding performance.

Locate-then-edit Knowledge Editing.

Locate-then-edit works achieve knowledge editing through knowledge neurons localization and the modification of located parameters via regularization constrained updates. ROME
meng2022locating
firstly identifies the decisive neuron activations for processing the subject tokens of factual associations in auto-regressive large language models through causal mediation analysis. They reveal a distinct set of steps in middle-layer MLP modules at the last-subject token position that mediate the prediction of factual prompts. Then, by modifying the most decisive sublayer of the located MLP modules with rank-one model editing, ROME alters the object prediction of each single fact with impressive generalizability. Some interpretation works
geva-etal-2022-transformer
;
geva-etal-2023-dissecting
further highlight that the enrichment of subject-related attributes of each factual association takes place at the last-subject token position. Thus, ROME can be understood as the modification of how subject-related attributes (i.e. subject knowledge) aggregate. To enable batch editing of thousands of facts, MEMIT
Meng2022MassEditingMI
adjusts the regularization constraints for multiple facts and spread the update evenly over several layers. Other works
fang2024alphaeditnullspaceconstrainedknowledge
;
ma2024perturbationrestrainedsequentialmodelediting
;
cai2024oeditorthogonalsubspaceediting
extend the ability of locate-then-edit methods to life-long editing, which enables to modify the located parameters sequentially without suffering from catastrophic forgetting. These works are all extensions based on the localization results of ROME.

However, RETS
liu2024relationknowsrethinkingrecall
uncovers the over-generalizing problem of such subject-focused editing which changes every fact linked to the subject of the edited factual association inadvertently. RETS unveils a similar recall mechanism for relational knowledge at the last-relation token position and addresses over-generalizing by editing from the relation-focused perspective with subject constraints. Nevertheless, it suffers from a drastic degradation in the quality of text generation, limited by the excessively stringent regularization constraints. In this work, we are dedicated to making in-depth exploration on why subject-focused editing tends to suffer from over-generalizing that makes editing uncontrollable, and to addressing this issue accordingly.

3
Unveil Shortcut Learning

To uncover the causes of over-generalizing, we take an in-depth investigation on the regularization restrained update process of ROME-like approaches.

3.1
Background and Notations

Firstly, we give the formulization of knowledge editing. Knowledge editing aims to alter the factual association <
s,r,o
> predicted by the model to <
s,r,o^{*}
>, where
s
is the subject,
r
is the relation, and
o,o^{*}
denote the original object and the new target object respectively. Auto-regressive language models are normally triggered by the factual prompt <
s,r
> to give the object token prediction.

ROME-like editing approaches localize the mid-early MLP sublayer
l
at the last-subject token position as the most decisive neurons for object predictions and modify the down-projection matrix
W_{s}^{l}
of the located MLP to overwrite the subject-related attributes recalled by the model. This down-projection matrix can be treated as a set of associative key-value memories that store the map between the input vector
k
and the output vector
v

meng2022locating
. Unlike fine-tuning which directly optimizes the model parameters to generate target predictions, ROME firstly calculates the average input vector
k_{s}^{*}
of prefixed target factual prompts and optimizes the output vector
v_{s}
of
W_{s}^{l}
, resulting in
v_{s}^{*}
. Then the down-projection matrix is modified by injecting
(k_{s}^{*},v_{s}^{*})
into the associative memories and optimizing the objective function with constraints as follows to obtain the updated
\tilde{W}_{s}^{l}
:

\mbox{minimize}~||\tilde{W}_{s}^{l}K-V||_{F}^{2}~\mbox{such that}~\tilde{W}_{s}^{l}k_{s}^{*}=v_{s}^{*}

(1)

\mbox{minimize}~||W^{l}_{s}K-V||_{F}^{2}

(2)

The objective function in Eqn.
2

ensures the least change on the original key-value pairs
K=[~k_{1}~|~k_{2}~|~k_{3}~|~...~]
and
V=[~v_{1}~|~v_{2}~|~v_{3}~|~...~]
, where
W_{s}^{l}
is the original associative memories that store the mapping from
K
to
V
, solving as
{W}_{s}^{l}KK^{T}=VK^{T}
. By solving the minimal value solution in Eqn.
1
, ROME obtain the solution for
\tilde{W}_{s}^{l}
as the updated weight matrix:

\tilde{W}^{l}_{s}=W^{l}_{s}+\Lambda^{s}(C^{-1}k_{s}^{*})^{T}

(3)

where
C=KK^{T}
is the constant estimated with the uncentered covariance of
k
on a slice of Wikipedia corpus and
\Lambda^{s}
is solved as
(v_{s}^{*}-W_{s}^{l}k_{s}^{*})/(C^{-1}k_{s}^{*})^{T}k_{s}^{*}
which is proportional to the gap between the initial output vector
W_{s}^{l}k_{s}^{*}
and the target output vector
v_{s}^{*}
.

Since the result of this regularization constrained update has been rigorously proven in
meng2022locating
and extra stringent regularization constraints on the objective of optimization can lead to a drastic decline of text generation fluency
liu2024relationknowsrethinkingrecall
, we will not discuss the deficiencies of this updating formula in this work. Instead, we focus on the calculation of two crucial vectors
k^{*}_{s}
and
v^{*}_{s}
for performing the parameter update. Vector
k^{*}_{s}
is set to the average value of the input vectors over a small amount of texts ending with the subject
s
to ensure the robustness of edited models on prefixed factual prompts. This value remains constant after the factual prompt for editing is provided. Therefore, the acquisition of
v_{s}^{*}
is the determinative cause of the over-generalizing issue for ROME-like editing approaches. It is obtained via the optimization process of
v^{*}_{s}=argmin_{v}\mathcal{L}(v)
in which the objective loss function is shown as follows:

\mathcal{L}(v)=-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{t=1}^{N}\mbox{log}~\mathbb{P}[o^{*}|v,p_{t}]+\mathbb{KL}[v,v_{s}^{o}]

(4)

where
v_{s}^{o}
is the original output vector of
W_{s}^{l}
,
p_{t}
(
t\in\{1,2,..,N\}
) are prefixed prompts ending with the edited factual association <
s,r
>.
\mathbb{KL}[v,v_{s}^{o}]
is the KL divergence
\mathbb{D}_{KL}(\mathbb{P}[x|v]~||~\mathbb{P}[x|v_{s}^{o})
to constrain the impact on other facts.

3.2
Gradient Saliency Analysis

To take an in-depth investigation of the optimization process for
v_{s}^{*}
, we analyze the saliency of gradients in this process. Since ROME edits at the mid-early layer
l
, we observe the gradients of hidden states after layer
l
at all token positions during the first few epochs, formalized as follows:

\mathcal{G}(h_{i}^{l_{a}})=\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}(v)}{\partial h_{i}^{l_{a}}}

(5)

where
l_{a}\in{\{l,l+1,...,L\}}
denotes the layer after the editing layer
l
of the model with
L
layers and
i\in{\{0,1,2,...,n-1\}}
denotes the token position in the tokens list of length
n
. The token positions are further categorized into relation prefix ("rp"), first subject ("fs"), middle subject ("ms"), last subject ("ls"), first relation ("fr"), middle relation ("mr") and last relation ("lr") according to their positions in the tokenized subject or relation of a factual association. For example, token positions of "
The mother tongue language of
" are the relation prefix in the input prompt "
The mother tongue language of Isabelle Breitman is
" and the token position of "
is
" is the last relation position.

We plot the average gradient saliency map over 100 edits of factual associations on GPT2-XL 1.5B

, while the results of initial several epochs are similar, as shown in Appendix A. We omit the gradients at
h_{n-1}^{L}
since this is the output hidden representation and it is supposed to have the most salient gradient.

Fig.

illustrates that the gradients are most salient across layers at the last-subject position and the last-relation position. Since hidden representations at the last-subject position are found to encode the subject-related attributes through projection onto the vocabulary space and are significantly more decisive for processing the subject tokens
geva-etal-2023-dissecting
;
meng2022locating
, they can be viewed as the subject feature of factual prompts that models learn. The phenomena are similar for hidden representations at the last-relation position for relation tokens
liu2024relationknowsrethinkingrecall
, which can be viewed as the relation feature of factual prompts. Therefore, we conclude that
hidden representations at the last subject position and the last relation position are two crucial features for auto-regressive language models to learn during optimization, namely the subject feature and the relation feature
.

3.3
The Shortcut Learning Issue

The two crucial features during optimization are revealed in the previous section. We further observe how well these two features are learned through ROME editing.

We conduct causal tracing
meng2022locating
on models before and after editing to investigate the change in the contribution of MLP outputs to predictions during inference. Causal tracing applies perturbation on each inner activation and records the impact on the final prediction through three runs. In the first run, the factual association prompt
p
is given to the model to obtain the object
o
when clean internal activations are cached. Here we focus on the output of MLP sublayers (e.g.
m_{i}^{l_{j}}
for the MLP output at token position
i
and layer
l_{j}
) since MLP is the decisive module for knowledge recall
geva-etal-2022-transformer
;
geva-etal-2023-dissecting
. Then, embeddings of certain tokens in the factual prompt are devastated with noises as
p^{\prime}
and are input into the model again to obtain the corrupted probability
\mathbb{P}(o~|~p^{\prime})
for the second run. In the last run, the devastated prompt
p^{\prime}
is still sent to the model but each inner activation is restored with the cached clean activation from the first run respectively, resulting in the restored probability
\mathbb{P}(o~|~p^{\prime},m_{i}^{l_{j}})
. Thus the contribution of
m_{i}^{l_{j}}
to the correct prediction of the model with parameters
\theta
is measured by the indirect effect (
IE
) as follows:

\mbox{{IE}}(o,\theta,m_{i}^{l_{j}})=\mathbb{P}_{\theta}(o~|~p^{\prime},m_{i}^{l_{j}})-\mathbb{P}_{\theta}(o~|~p^{\prime})

(6)

In this paper, we further measure the logarithmic ratio of indirect effect (
RIE
) of each activation
m_{i}^{l_{j}}
from the edited model
\theta_{edited}
to the original model
\theta_{o}
as follows:

\mbox{{RIE}}=log\frac{\mbox{{IE}}(o^{*},\theta_{edited},m_{i}^{l_{j}})}{\mbox{{IE}}(o,\theta_{o},m_{i}^{l_{j}})}

(7)

which measures the significance of each activation before and after editing and
o^{*}
is the target new object. We plot the maximum RIE of MLP outputs across all layers at each token position over 100 factual prompts in Fig.
4
. A case of the causal tracing result before and after editing is shown in Appendix B.

Fig.
4
illustrates that the contribution of MLP outputs at the last-subject position increases sharply after editing, significantly more than the contribution increase at the last-relation position.
It indicates that models have over-learned the subject feature while neglecting the relation feature during optimization
. However, both the subject feature and the relation feature are crucial for the prediction of factual associations, which means ROME-like editing falls into the trap of shortcut learning.

In conclusion, we unveil two decisive features which models learn during ROME-like editing that are the subject feature and relation feature. However, the optimization process of ROME-like editing results in the over-learning of the subject feature while ignoring the relation feature, falling into the trap of shortcut learning. Therefore, our goal is to address this issue via improving the acquisition of
v_{s}^{*}
in the optimization process.

4
Eliminate Shortcut Learning

To start with, we take a further analysis on the loss objective in Eqn.
4
. The negative log-likelihood function
-log~\mathbb{P}[o^{*}|v]
primarily serves the purpose of alternating the token predictions while the KL divergence
\mathbb{D}_{KL}(\mathbb{P}[x|v]~||~\mathbb{P}[x|v_{so})
aims to constrain the impact on irrelevant factual associations. However, in order to change the prediction of models with modifications to only few parameters, the optimization process tends to prioritize reducing the loss of the negative log-likelihood function. As a result, the constraint of the KL divergence becomes negligible. Thus, to address the shortcut issue, we should refine the negative log-likelihood objective for the optimization of
v_{s}
which fails to take the relation feature into account.

4.1
Analysis of the Likelihood Function

As claimed in Sec.
3.2
, the hidden representations at the last relation token position
h_{r}
can be viewed as the relation feature for models to learn. Since only the down-projection matrix of the mid-early MLP sublayer for processing the last subject token will be modified, the likelihood function should be changed to
\mathbb{P}[o^{*},h_{r}^{l_{a}}|v]
to take the relation feature in to account during editing.
h_{r}^{l_{a}}
in layer
l_{a}
(
l_{a}>l
) is an intermediate hidden vector after the editing layer
l
without any supervision signal, which is obtained through the following iteration of forward propagation for auto-regressive transformer language models:

a_{r}^{l_{j}+1}=\textbf{ATTN}[h_{0}^{l_{j}},...,h_{s}^{l_{j}},...,h_{r-1}^{l_{j}}]

(8)

m_{r}^{l_{j}+1}=\textbf{MLP}[h_{r}^{l_{j}}+a_{r}^{l_{j}+1}I_{model}]

(9)

h_{r}^{l_{j}+1}=h_{r}^{l_{j}}+a_{r}^{l_{j}+1}+m_{r}^{l_{j}+1}

(10)

where
r
is equal to
n-1
for the tokens list of length
n
,
ATTN
is the attention module and
MLP
is the feed-forward network module.
I_{model}
is a constant set to 0 or 1, which depends on the structure of models. For GPT2-XL with the sequential structure of MLP and the attention modules,
I_{model}
is set to 1, while for GPT-J with the parallel structure,
I_{model}
is set to 0. Layer normalizations are omitted for simplicity.

Due to the lack of supervision signals in utilizing the new likelihood
\mathbb{P}[o^{*},h_{r}^{l_{a}}|v]
directly as the objective for the optimization process, we need to approximate and decompose this likelihood function. According to the chain rule in probability theory, this likelihood function can be decomposed as follows:

\mathbb{P}[o^{*},h_{r}^{l_{a}}|v]=\mathbb{P}[o^{*}|h_{r}^{l_{a}},v]~\mathbb{P}[h_{r}^{l_{a}}|v]

(11)

where
v
is the variable of the MLP output at the last-subject position in layer
l
, which is propagated to
h_{s}^{l}
and further propagated to
h_{r}^{l_{a}}
as shown in Eqn.
8
-
10
.
h_{r}^{l_{a}}
contains the information of
v
for the prediction of
o^{*}
, implying that when
h_{r}^{l_{a}}
is given,
v
and
o^{*}
can be considered approximately conditional independent. The likelihood in Eqn.
11
can be simplified as follows:

\mathbb{P}[o^{*},h_{r}^{l_{a}}|v]=\mathbb{P}[o^{*}|h_{r}^{l_{a}}]~\mathbb{P}[h_{r}^{l_{a}}|v]

(12)

which is feasible for optimization.

4.2
The Two-Stage Optimization Process

The new objective of the optimization for
v_{s}^{*}
is now decomposed as
\mathbb{P}[o^{*}|h_{r}^{l_{a}}]~\mathbb{P}[h_{r}^{l_{a}}|v]
. Consequently, we design a
T
wo-stage
O
ptimization
P
rocess (TOP) that is capable of sufficiently learning the relation feature for controllable knowledge editing, as illustrated in Fig.
4
. All
h_{r}^{l_{a}}
below are simplified as
h_{r}
and the new target vector is denoted as
h_{r}^{*}
.

Optimization of
\mathbf{h_{r}}
.

In the first stage, we optimize the hidden representation at the last-relation position in layer
l_{a}
to switch the prediction of models from
o
to
o^{*}
and obtain the updated
h_{r}^{*}=argmin_{h}\mathcal{L}_{1}(h)
. The objective function is shown as follows:

\mathcal{L}_{1}(h)=-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{t=1}^{N}log~\mathbb{P}[o^{*}|h,p_{t}]+\mathbb{KL}[h,h_{r}^{o}]

(13)

where
h_{r}^{o}
is the original hidden representation at the last relation position in layer
l_{a}
.
p_{t}
and
\mathbb{KL}
are the same as in Eqn.
4
. The negative likelihood is in accordance with the left part of
\mathbb{P}[o^{*}|h_{r}^{l_{a}}]~\mathbb{P}[h_{r}^{l_{a}}|v]
and we also use the KL divergence to constrain the impact on unrelated relations.

Optimization of
\mathbf{v_{s}}
.

In the second stage, we optimize the output vector of the down-projection matrix of MLP in the mid-early layer
l
to alter the hidden representation at the last-relation position in layer
l_{a}
from the original
h_{r}^{o}
to the optimized
h_{r}^{*}
in the first stage and obtain the updated
v_{s}^{*}=argmin_{v}\mathcal{L}_{2}(v)
. The loss function is the Frobenius Norm shown as follows:

\mathcal{L}_{2}(v)=||~\mathbb{F}[v,p]-h_{r}^{*}~||_{F}

(14)

where
p
is the prompt of the edited factual association <
s,r
> and
\mathbb{F}[v,p]
is the forward propagation result in
h_{r}
when
v_{s}
is set to
v
. All weight decays are omitted for simplicity.

To compare with, we choose the SOTA locate-then-edit methods as our baselines, including Constrained Fine-Tuning (FT+L)

5.2
Main Results

Table
1
shows the evaluation results on the knowledge editing dataset COUNTERFACT_RS for GPT2-XL, GPT-J and Qwen2.5. It illustrates that TOP editing exhibits the most balanced performance over all baselines. It has the least side effects with no deficiencies. On the contrary, other editing baselines either suffer from poor specificity (e.g. low R-Specificity score for ROME and MEMIT) or generalization (e.g. low Generalization score for FT-L), or disrupts the text generation fluency (e.g. low 585.1 Fluency score for RETS). TOP more adequately satisfies the requirements for controllable editing that prioritizes minimizing the impact on unrelated facts while remaining competitive on other criteria. Based on the ensurance of high specificity of editing (i.e. S-Specificity and R-Specificity), it also shows better generalization performance compared to FT-L. In conclusion, TOP successfully addresses the shortcut learning issue for controllable knowledge editing with the least side effects while remaining a certain degree of generalization.

l

Trade-off Analysis of the Layer Selection.

We here provide further analysis to understand the cause of trade-offs in the layer selection for TOP editing. The cause underlies in Eqn.
12
which motivates the two-stage optimization process.
While
l_{a}
is closer to the output layer (i.e. the layer of
o^{*}
), the convergence of the likelihood loss
\mathbb{P}[o^{*}|h_{r}^{l_{a}}]
(i.e. the optimization at the first stage) is easier and faster.
This leads to overfitting of the input relation tokens with the same number of epochs at the first stage and failing to generalize to paraphrased relations, resulting in high relation specificity and low generalization. It is similar while
l_{a}
is closer to the editing layer (i.e. the layer of
v
), which leads to overfitting of the input relation tokens at the second stage for
\mathbb{P}[h_{r}^{l_{a}}|v]
. Fig.
6
show the average losses of first 7 epochs of the two optimization stages while
l_{a}
is set to 8 and 16 for GPT-J (the editing layer is the 6th layer). The results indicate that the convergence of the first optimization stage is faster and the convergence of the second optimization stage is slower while
l_{a}
is larger, which verifies the above theory.

Consequently, as
l_{a}
increases, the overfitting to the relation at the second stage relieves before middle layers, yet the overfitting to the relation at the first stage exacerbates after middle layers, resulting in the trend of trade-offs of R-Specificity and Generalization in Fig.
6
. An possible way to further balance these two metrics might involve setting dynamic learning epochs for different
l_{a}
at each stage, although it might introduce heavy hyperparameter searching overhead.

6
Conclusion

This work provides a novel understanding of the failure of locate-then-edit knowledge editing on the maintenance of unrelated facts. We uncover the shortcut learning issue during the optimization process of editing, which leads to unexpected alterations of unrelated facts in edited models. To be specific, we discover two pivotal features during the optimization process of editing (i.e. the subject feature and the relation feature). We further observe that the subject feature is over-learned while the relation feature is neglected in the optimization process. To eliminate shortcut learning, we amend the objective loss function of the optimization process and propose a two-stage optimization process. Experimental results demonstrate the superiority of our strategy. It can inspire future works for more practical editing and also serves as a paradigmatic example for eliminating shortcut learning by specifying the optimization trajectory.

Appendix D
Ethics Statement

Our research focuses on addressing the challenge of outdated or erroneous knowledge encoded in large language models by developing methods to detect and correct such inaccuracies. At the same time, we are acutely aware of the ethical risks this technology entails, particularly the possibility of misuse for disseminating harmful or misleading content. To mitigate these risks, we underscore the critical importance of two key principles: first, language models must be obtained from rigorously vetted and reliable sources; second, users must exercise utmost caution and critical judgment when utilizing outputs generated by these models.

Outer length billiards on polygons
Lael Edwards-Costa

Abstract
The classical inner and outer billiards can be formulated in variational terms, with length and area as the respective generating functions. The other two combinations, “inner with area” and “outer with length,” are more recently described. Here, we consider the latter system in the special case of polygonal tables. We describe the behavior of orbits far away from the table and pose conjectures regarding an escaping orbit when the table is a square. This paper is meant to complement [ 2 ] which handles smooth tables with positive curvature.

In general, outer billiards in the plane has no immediate connection to its

1.2
Variational formulations

The Birkhoff billiard’s reflection law can be replaced with the following
variational formulation. An inscribed broken line connecting boundary
points
x_{0},\dots,x_{n}
in

is a trajectory of the billiard map if and only if
its length is locally extremized among inscribed broken lines with endpoints

x_{0}

L:=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\absolutevalue{x_{i+1}-x_i},

(1)

Likewise, outer billiard

of these facts can be found in
[
12

We have addressed “inner with length” and “outer with area,” but what about
the other two combinations?

The literature contains several results concerning the inner billiard which
extremizes area. It is called the

intersects

at two
points. One of them is

; label the second

. The symplectic billiard map
takes the chord

to the chord

. See
[
4
]

introduction to this system and

This leaves only “outer with length,” which is the topic of the present

3.1
The outer area billiard

Let us briefly review the behavior of orbits of the usual outer billiard far

If

Denote by

By the discussion in Section

out ellipses. In the polygonal case, we argue that the once-around orbit is made

origin. The transitions between these phases involve jumps from ellipse to

1.

the eccentricity of each ellipse, and

2.

the jumps between consecutive ellipses.

Now, let

-gon of diameter

Stabilization of solutions of the controlled non-local continuity equation
Volkov A

Abstract




1
Introduction

The paper addresses the following problem: given a controlled non-local continuity equation

\partial_{t}m_{t}+\operatorname{div}\big(f(x,m_{t},u)m_{t}\big)=0

with an arbitrary initial distribution
m_{*}
, we aim to find a feedback control law that stabilizes the system to a given state.
The non-local continuity equation describes the evolution of an infinite system of identical particles, where the function
f
represents a vector field that governs the motion of each particle based on its current position
x
and the current spatial distribution
m
.
Note that the state variable is
m
, which is a probability measure.

The continuity equation is used to model various phenomena, including systems of charged particles
[
1
]
, the behavior of supermassive black holes
[
2
]
and large groups of animals
[
3
]
, dynamics of biological processes
[
4
]
and public opinion
[
5
]
.

The problem under consideration traces back to similar issues in finite-dimensional cases.
Most results have been obtained for linear systems with continuous (or even smooth) controls
[
6
,
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
]
.
Attempts to generalize these results have often encountered counterexamples and the need for stronger assumptions
[
11
,
12
,
13
,
14
]
.
A breakthrough in this area was achieved in
[
15
,
16
,
8
]
, where discontinuous synthesis was introduced based on the extremal shift rule, originally proposed for zero-sum differential games by Krasovskii and Subbotin in
[
17
]
.
The method developed in
[
16
]
applies to a broad class of nonlinear systems.
It relies on the concept of a control-Lyapunov pair (CLP), which consists of a Lyapunov function and an upper bound on its derivative.
Note that the construction of the stabilizing synthesis employs inf-convolution and techniques from proximal calculus.
It is shown that the existence of a CLP is sufficient to construct a stabilizing synthesis, and the opposite also holds.

In this paper, we extend the results of
[
16
]
to the case of a non-local continuity equation.
However, this extension is not straightforward because the state space now is neither linear nor
\sigma
-compact.
To overcome this, we employ variational principles and concepts from non-smooth analysis in the Wasserstein space
[
18
,
16
,
19
,
20
]
.
We redefine the notion of a control-Lyapunov pair and prove that its existence is sufficient for both local and global stabilization.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows.

Section
2
provides general definitions and notations used throughout the paper.
Here, we define the solution of the non-local continuity equation and discuss some of its properties.

Section
3
presents the first component of the approach developed in
[
16
]
, namely inf-convolution.
Due to the infinite-dimensional nature of the state space, we cannot guarantee the existence of a minimizer.
Instead, we use Ekeland’s variational principle to get a pseudo-minimizer.

In Section
4
, we introduce a proximal subgradient for functions defined on the Wasserstein space, which serves as the second component of our approach.
Additionally, we show that every pseudo-minimizer of the inf-convolution is a barycenter of the proximal subgradient.

Local stabilization is discussed in Section
5
.
Here, we introduce a feedback synthesis based on inf-convolution and prove that this synthesis achieves local stabilization.
Our proof relies heavily on the proximal subgradient and the control-Lyapunov pair.

Finally, Section
6
addresses global stabilization.
We define the concept and construct a feedback law that achieves it.
The construction involves the sequential implementation of local stabilizing feedback laws.

2.2
Non-local continuity equation

The main object of this paper is the initial value problem for the controlled non-local continuity equation

\partial_{t}m_{t}+\operatorname{div}\big(f(x,m_{t},u)m_{t}\big)=0,\quad m_{0}=m_{*},

(1)

where
m_{*}\in\mathscr{P}_{2}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})
,
U
is a sequential compact and
f:{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\times\mathscr{P}_{2}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})\times U\to{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
is a vector field.
As it was mentioned above, this equation describes an evolution of infinite particle system where each particle follows:

\dot{x}=f(x,m_{t},u).

(2)

We assume the following condition:

Assumption 2.7
.

The function
f
is continuous in
u
and Lipschitz continuous in
x
and
m
: there exists a constant
C_{0}>0
such that, for every
x,y\in{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
,
\mu,\nu\in\mathscr{P}_{2}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})
, and
u\in U
,

\|f(x,\mu,u)-f(y,\nu,u)\|\leq C_{0}\big(\|x-y\|+W_{2}(\mu,\nu)\big).

Remark 2.8
.

Setting
\nu=\delta_{0}
and
y=0
in Assumption
2.7
implies that function
f
has sublinear growth: there exists a constant
C_{1}>0
, such that, for every
x\in{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
,
\mu\in\mathscr{P}_{2}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})
and
u\in U
,

\|f(x,\mu,u)\|\leq C_{1}\big(1+\|x\|+\varsigma_{2}(\mu)\big).

Now, we define feedback strategies and corresponding solutions to (
1
):

Definition 2.9
.

•

A

is a function
k:\mathscr{P}_{2}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})\to U
.

•

A

[0,+\infty)

\theta=\{t_{i}\}_{i=0}^{\infty}
with
t_{0}=0
,

\lim\limits_{i\to\infty}t_{i}=\infty
.

•

\mathsf{\Theta}(\delta_{1},\delta_{2})
denotes the set of partitions whose interval lengths are from

, i.e.

\mathsf{\Theta}(\delta_{1},\delta_{2})\triangleq\big\{\ \{t_{i}\}_{i=0}^{\infty}\ :\ \delta_{1}\leq t_{i+1}-t_{i}\leq\delta_{2}\big\}.

•

A measure-valued function
m_{\cdot}:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathscr{P}_{2}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})
is a

1
) on the interval
[t_{i},t_{i+1}]

if for all
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}\big({\mathbb{R}^{d}}\times(t_{i},t_{i+1});\mathbb{R}\big)

\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}\big(\partial_{t}\varphi(x,t)+\nabla_{x}\varphi(x,t)\cdot f(x,m_{t},u)\big)\ m_{t}(dx)dt=0.

\displaystyle\partial_{t}m_{t}^{i}+\operatorname{div}\Big(f\big(x,m_{t}^{i},k(m_{t_{i}}^{i})\big)m_{t}^{i}\Big)=0,\quad t\in[t_{i},t_{i+1}],

\displaystyle m_{0}^{0}=m_{*},\quad m_{t_{i}}^{i}=m_{t_{i}}^{i-1}

3
Inf-convolution

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\phi
be l.s.c.,
\varkappa\in(0,1]
and
m\in\mathscr{P}_{2}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})
.
The
inf-convolution
of the function
\phi
with parameter
\varkappa
at the point
m
is defined as:

\phi_{\varkappa}(m)\triangleq\inf\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mu\in\mathscr{P}_{2}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})\\
\pi\in\Pi(m,\mu)\end{subarray}}\left[\phi(\mu)+\frac{1}{2\varkappa^{2}}\int_{({\mathbb{R}^{d}})^{2}}\|y-x\|^{2}\ \pi(dxdy).\right]

(3)

This operation generalizes the seminal Yosida-Moreau regularization (see
[
26
,
27
,
28
]
) to Wasserstein spaces.

Remark 3.2
.

It is enough to consider only optimal plans
\pi
, i.e.,

\phi_{\varkappa}(m)=\inf\limits_{\mu\in\mathscr{P}_{2}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})}\left[\phi(\mu)+\frac{1}{2\varkappa^{2}}W_{2}^{2}(m,\mu)\right].

Remark 3.3
.

Considering the identical plan we get that
\phi_{\varkappa}(m)\leq\phi(m)
for every measure
m\in\mathscr{P}_{2}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})
and parameter
\varkappa\in(0,1]
.

Remark 3.4
.

Since the Wasserstein space is not finite-dimensional, we cannot ensure the existence of a minimizer.
In that case we use the Ekeland’s variational principle introduced in
[
18
]
: for all
m\in\mathscr{P}_{2}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})
,
\varkappa\in(0,1]
, and
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a measure
\mu_{\varkappa}^{\varepsilon}(m)\in\mathscr{P}_{2}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})
such that, for all
\mu\in\mathscr{P}_{2}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})
,

1.

\displaystyle\phi\big(\mu_{\varkappa}^{\varepsilon}(m)\big)+\frac{1}{2\varkappa^{2}}W_{2}^{2}\big(m,\mu_{\varkappa}^{\varepsilon}(m)\big)\leq\phi(m)-\varepsilon\cdot W_{2}(\mu_{\varkappa}^{\varepsilon}(m),m)
,

2.

\displaystyle\phi\big(\mu_{\varkappa}^{\varepsilon}(m)\big)+\frac{1}{2\varkappa^{2}}W_{2}^{2}\big(m,\mu_{\varkappa}^{\varepsilon}(m)\big)\leq\phi(\mu)+\frac{1}{2\varkappa^{2}}W_{2}^{2}(m,\mu)+\varepsilon\cdot W_{2}(\mu_{\varkappa}^{\varepsilon}(m),\mu)
.

Further, we need some additional notions using a target measure
{\hat{m}}
.

Definition 3.5
.

Given measure
{\hat{m}}\in\mathscr{P}_{2}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})
, numbers
R>0
,
\varkappa\in(0,1]
and function
\phi\in\mathsf{UC}_{{\hat{m}}}
, define the functions

\mathcal{S}(R)\triangleq\sup\limits_{\mu\in\mathsf{B}_{R}({\hat{m}})}\phi(\mu),\quad\mathcal{I}(R)\triangleq\inf\limits_{\mu\notin\mathsf{O}_{R}({\hat{m}})}\phi(\mu),

the sets

G_{R}\triangleq\left\{\mu\ \Big|\ \phi(\mu)\leq\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{I}(R)\right\},\quad G_{R}^{\varkappa}\triangleq\left\{\mu\ \Big|\ \phi_{\varkappa}(\mu)\leq\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{I}(R)\right\},

and the function
\mathcal{R}(R)\triangleq\sup\{r\geq 0\ |\ \mathsf{B}_{r}({\hat{m}})\subseteq G_{R}\}
.

The following relations are easy to prove.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
{\hat{m}}\in\mathscr{P}_{2}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})
,
\phi\in\mathsf{UC}_{{\hat{m}}}
,
R>0
, and
\varkappa\in(0,1]
.
Then:

1.

\mathcal{I}(R)\leq\mathcal{S}(R)
;

2.

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(R)>0
;

3.

\displaystyle\lim_{R\to 0}\mathcal{S}(R)=\lim\mathcal{R}(R)=0
;

4.

\displaystyle\lim_{R\to\infty}\mathcal{I}(R)=\lim\mathcal{R}(R)=\infty
;

5.

\displaystyle\mathsf{O}_{\mathcal{R}(R)}({\hat{m}})\subseteq G_{R}\subseteq G_{R}^{\varkappa}
.

For fixed
{\hat{m}}\in\mathscr{P}_{2}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})
,
\phi\in\mathsf{UC}_{{\hat{m}}}
,
R>0
,
\varkappa\in(0,1]
, and
\varepsilon>0
we define:

•

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{\varkappa}^{\varepsilon}(R)\triangleq\varkappa\sqrt{2\mathcal{S}(R)+\varepsilon^{2}\varkappa^{2}}-\varepsilon\varkappa^{2}
;

•

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{\varepsilon}(R)\triangleq R+\mathcal{M}_{1}^{\varepsilon}(R)+\varepsilon\varkappa^{2}=R+\sqrt{2\mathcal{S}(R)+\varepsilon^{2}}
;

•

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{\varkappa}^{\varepsilon}(R)\triangleq\varepsilon\sqrt{2\varkappa^{2}\cdot\phi_{\varkappa}(m)}+\varepsilon\cdot\mathcal{M}_{\varkappa}^{\varepsilon}(R)
;

•

\displaystyle\omega_{R}^{\varepsilon}(\delta)\triangleq\sup\Big\{|\phi(\nu_{1})-\phi(\nu_{2})|\ \big|\ W_{2}(\nu_{1},\nu_{2})\leq\delta,\ \nu_{1},\nu_{2}\in\mathsf{B}_{\mathcal{M}^{\varepsilon}(R)}({\hat{m}})\Big\}
;

•

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{\varkappa}^{\varepsilon}(R)\triangleq\varepsilon\varkappa^{2}+\varkappa\sqrt{\varepsilon^{2}\varkappa^{2}+2\omega_{R}^{\varepsilon}(\mathcal{M}_{\varkappa}^{\varepsilon}(R))}
.

Below we provide some estimates for the measure
\mu_{\varkappa}^{\varepsilon}(m)
.

Lemma 3.7
.

For
\varkappa\in(0,1]
,
\varepsilon>0
, and
m\in\mathsf{B}_{R}({\hat{m}})
, the following inequality holds:

W_{2}\big(m,\mu_{\varkappa}^{\varepsilon}(m)\big)\leq\mathcal{M}_{\varkappa}^{\varepsilon}(R).

Proof.

By the first inequality of Remark
3.4
:

\phi\big(\mu_{\varkappa}^{\varepsilon}(m)\big)+\frac{1}{2\varkappa^{2}}W_{2}^{2}\big(m,\mu_{\varkappa}^{\varepsilon}(m)\big)\leq\phi(m)-\varepsilon W_{2}\big(m,\mu_{\varkappa}^{\varepsilon}(m)\big).

Thus,

\frac{1}{2\varkappa^{2}}W_{2}^{2}\big(m,\mu_{\varkappa}^{\varepsilon}(m)\big)+\varepsilon W_{2}\big(m,\mu_{\varkappa}^{\varepsilon}(m)\big)\leq\phi(m)-\phi\big(\mu_{\varkappa}^{\varepsilon}(m)\big)\leq\phi(m)\leq\mathcal{S}(R).

(4)

Solving this inequality yields

W_{2}\big(m,\mu_{\varkappa}^{\varepsilon}(m)\big)\leq\varkappa\sqrt{2\mathcal{S}(R)+\varepsilon^{2}\varkappa^{2}}-\varepsilon\varkappa^{2}.

∎

Using inequality (
4
) and the fact that
\mathcal{M}_{\varkappa}^{\varepsilon}(R)\leq\mathcal{M}^{\varepsilon}(R)
, it is easy to prove the corollarial inequalities.

Corollary 3.8
.

In assumptions of Lemma
3.7
:

1.

\displaystyle W_{2}\big(m,\mu_{\varkappa}^{\varepsilon}(m)\big)\leq\mathcal{K}_{\varkappa}^{\varepsilon}(R)
,

2.

\displaystyle W_{2}\big({\hat{m}},\mu_{\varkappa}^{\varepsilon}(m)\big)\leq\mathcal{M}^{\varepsilon}(R)
.

Now, we prove inequalities for function
\phi
and its inf-convolution.

Lemma 3.9
.

For
\varkappa\in(0,1]
,
\varepsilon>0
,
m\in\mathsf{B}_{R}({\hat{m}})
, and
\phi\in\mathsf{UC}_{{\hat{m}}}
, the following inequality holds true:

\phi\big(\mu_{\varkappa}^{\varepsilon}(m)\big)+\frac{1}{2\varkappa^{2}}W_{2}^{2}\big(m,\mu_{\varkappa}^{\varepsilon}(m)\big)\leq\phi_{\varkappa}(m)+\mathcal{N}_{\varkappa}^{\varepsilon}(R).

Proof.

Due to the very definition of the inf-convolution, there exists a minimizing sequence
\{\mu_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
, i.e.

\phi(\mu_{n})+\frac{1}{2\varkappa^{2}}W_{2}^{2}(m,\mu_{n})\to\phi_{\varkappa}(m)\quad\text{ as }n\to\infty.

By the second inequality of Remark
3.4
and the triangle inequality, we have that

\displaystyle\phi\big(\mu_{\varkappa}^{\varepsilon}(m)\big)+\frac{1}{2\varkappa^{2}}W_{2}^{2}\big(m,\mu_{\varkappa}^{\varepsilon}(m)\big)

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\leq\phi(\mu_{n})+\frac{1}{2\varkappa^{2}}W_{2}^{2}(m,\mu_{n})+\varepsilon\cdot W_{2}(\mu_{\varkappa}^{\varepsilon}(m),\mu_{n})

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\leq\phi(\mu_{n})+\frac{1}{2\varkappa^{2}}W_{2}^{2}(m,\mu_{n})+\varepsilon\cdot W_{2}(m,\mu_{n})+\varepsilon\cdot W_{2}\big(m,\mu_{\varkappa}^{\varepsilon}(m)\big).

Notice, that

\varepsilon W_{2}(m,\mu_{n})=\varepsilon\sqrt{2\varkappa^{2}}\cdot\sqrt{\frac{1}{2\varkappa^{2}}\cdot W_{2}^{2}(m,\mu_{n})}\leq\varepsilon\sqrt{2\varkappa^{2}}\cdot\sqrt{\phi(\mu_{n})+\frac{1}{2\varkappa^{2}}\cdot W_{2}^{2}(m,\mu_{n})}

Then, using both previous inequalities and Lemma

, we arrive at the estimate

\phi\big(\mu_{\varkappa}^{\varepsilon}(m)\big)+\frac{1}{2\varkappa^{2}}W_{2}^{2}\big(m,\mu_{\varkappa}^{\varepsilon}(m)\big)\\
\leq\phi(\mu_{n})+\frac{1}{2\varkappa^{2}}W_{2}^{2}(m,\mu_{n})+\varepsilon\sqrt{2\varkappa^{2}}\cdot\sqrt{\phi(\mu_{n})+\frac{1}{2\varkappa^{2}}\cdot W_{2}^{2}(m,\mu_{n})}+\varepsilon\cdot\mathcal{M}_{\varkappa}^{\varepsilon}(R).

Passing to the limit when

\phi\big(\mu_{\varkappa}^{\varepsilon}(m)\big)+\frac{1}{2\varkappa^{2}}W_{2}^{2}\big(m,\mu_{\varkappa}^{\varepsilon}(m)\big)\leq\phi_{\varkappa}(m)+\mathcal{N}_{\varkappa}^{\varepsilon}(R).

∎

Lemma 3.10

4

As mentioned earlier, the Wasserstein space lacks linear structure, making the definition of a direction non-trivial. Following
[
20
]
, we represent directions as distributions on the tangent bundle, with subdifferential elements residing in the space of probability measures over the cotangent bundle to
{\mathbb{R}^{d}}

Let
{\hat{m}}\in\mathscr{P}_{2}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})
and
\varepsilon>0
.

Definition 4.1
.

A measure
\alpha\in\mathscr{P}_{2}({\mathbb{R}^{d}}\times{\mathbb{R}^{d*}})
is a
proximal
\varepsilon
-subgradient
of the function
\phi:\mathscr{P}_{2}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})\to\mathbb{R}
at the point
m\in\mathscr{P}_{2}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})
if there exist
\sigma>0
and
r>0
such that, for all
\mu\in\mathsf{B}_{r}(m)
and every
\beta\in\mathscr{P}_{2}({\mathbb{R}^{d}}\times{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\times{\mathbb{R}^{d*}})
satisfying

1.

\displaystyle\mathfrak{p}^{1,2}\sharp\beta\in\Pi(m,\mu)
,

2.

\displaystyle\mathfrak{p}^{1,3}\sharp\beta=\alpha
,

the following inequality holds:

\phi(\mu)\geq\phi(m)+\int_{({\mathbb{R}^{d}})^{3}}p\cdot(x_{2}-x_{1})\ \beta(dx_{1}dx_{2}dp)\\
-\sigma\int_{({\mathbb{R}^{d}})^{2}}\|x_{2}-x_{1}\|^{2}\ (\mathfrak{p}^{1,2}\sharp\beta)(dx_{1}dx_{2})-\varepsilon\cdot W_{2}(m,\mu).

The set of proximal
\varepsilon
-subgradients is called a
proximal
\varepsilon
-subdifferential
and denoted by
\partial_{P}^{\varepsilon}\phi(m)
.

Define a map
\chi_{\varkappa}:\ {\mathbb{R}^{d}}\times{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\to{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\times{\mathbb{R}^{d*}}
and a measure
\gamma_{\varkappa}^{\varepsilon}(m)\in\mathscr{P}_{2}({\mathbb{R}^{d}}\times{\mathbb{R}^{d*}})
by the rules:

\chi_{\varkappa}(z_{1},z_{2})\triangleq\left(z_{2},\ \left(\frac{z_{1}-z_{2}}{\varkappa^{2}}\right)^{\top}\right);\quad\gamma_{\varkappa}^{\varepsilon}(m)\triangleq\chi_{\varkappa}\sharp\pi_{\varkappa}^{\varepsilon}(m),

(6)

where
\pi_{\varkappa}^{\varepsilon}(m)\in\Pi_{o}\big(m,\mu_{\varkappa}^{\varepsilon}(m)\big)
.

Lemma 4.2
.

The measure
\gamma_{\varkappa}^{\varepsilon}(m)
is a proximal
\varepsilon
-subgradient of the function
\phi
at the point
\mu_{\varkappa}^{\varepsilon}(m)

Proof.

For an arbitrary measure
\mu\in\mathscr{P}_{2}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})
and a measure
\beta\in\mathscr{P}_{2}({\mathbb{R}^{d}}\times{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\times{\mathbb{R}^{d*}})

\displaystyle\mathfrak{p}^{1,2}\sharp\beta\in\Pi(\mu_{\varkappa}^{\varepsilon}(m),\mu)

2.

\displaystyle\mathfrak{p}^{1,3}\sharp\beta=\gamma_{\varkappa}^{\varepsilon}(m)
,

we construct a map
\xi_{\varkappa}:\ {\mathbb{R}^{d}}\times{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\times{\mathbb{R}^{d*}}\to{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\times{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\times{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
and a measure
\rho\in\mathscr{P}_{2}({\mathbb{R}^{d}}\times{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\times{\mathbb{R}^{d}})

\displaystyle\xi_{\varkappa}(z_{1},z_{2},z_{3})\triangleq\left(z_{1}+\varkappa^{2}\cdot z_{3}^{\top},\ z_{1},\ z_{2}\right);\quad\rho\triangleq\xi_{\varkappa}\sharp\beta.

Notice that

•

\displaystyle(\xi_{\varkappa})^{-1}(w_{1},w_{2},w_{3})=\left(w_{2},\ w_{3},\ \left(\frac{w_{1}-w_{2}}{\varkappa^{2}}\right)^{\top}\right)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{p}^{1,2}\sharp\rho=\pi_{\varkappa}^{\varepsilon}(m)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{p}^{1,3}\sharp\rho\in\Pi(m,\mu)

Then, by the second inequality of Remark
3.4

\displaystyle\phi(\mu)

\displaystyle\geq\phi\big(\mu_{\varkappa}^{\varepsilon}(m)\big)+\frac{1}{2\varkappa^{2}}\int_{({\mathbb{R}^{d}})^{2}}\|y_{\varkappa}^{\varepsilon}-x\|^{2}\ \big(\pi_{\varkappa}^{\varepsilon}(m)\big)(dxdy_{\varkappa}^{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle\hskip 99.58464pt-\frac{1}{2\varkappa^{2}}\int_{({\mathbb{R}^{d}})^{2}}\|y-x\|^{2}\ \pi(dxdy)-\varepsilon\cdot W_{2}(\mu_{\varkappa}^{\varepsilon}(m),\mu)

\displaystyle=\phi\big(\mu_{\varkappa}^{\varepsilon}(m)\big)+\frac{1}{2\varkappa^{2}}\int_{({\mathbb{R}^{d}})^{3}}\|y_{\varkappa}^{\varepsilon}-x\|^{2}\ \rho(dxdy_{\varkappa}^{\varepsilon}dy)

\displaystyle\hskip 99.58464pt-\frac{1}{2\varkappa^{2}}\int_{({\mathbb{R}^{d}})^{3}}\|y-x\|^{2}\ \rho(dxdy_{\varkappa}^{\varepsilon}dy)-\varepsilon\cdot W_{2}(\mu_{\varkappa}^{\varepsilon}(m),\mu)

\displaystyle=\phi\big(\mu_{\varkappa}^{\varepsilon}(m)\big)+\int_{({\mathbb{R}^{d}})^{3}}\left(\frac{1}{2\varkappa^{2}}\|y_{\varkappa}^{\varepsilon}-x\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2\varkappa^{2}}\|y-x\|^{2}\right)\ \rho(dxdy_{\varkappa}^{\varepsilon}dy)

\displaystyle\hskip 284.52756pt-\varepsilon\cdot W_{2}(\mu_{\varkappa}^{\varepsilon}(m),\mu).

Lemma 4.3

(7)

Definition 4.4

(

]
)

{\hat{m}}
for continuity equation (

Local stabilization at neighborhood

In this section, we fix a measure
{\hat{m}}\in\mathscr{P}_{2}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})

.
Also we assume that the parameter

(0,1]
.

(8)

&\hskip 113.81102pt+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\frac{x-y_{\varkappa}^{\varepsilon}}{\varkappa^{2}}\right)^{\top}\cdot f(y_{\varkappa}^{\varepsilon},\mu_{\varkappa}^{\varepsilon}(m),u)\ \big(\pi_{\varkappa}^{\varepsilon}(m)\big)(dxdy_{\varkappa}^{\varepsilon})\Bigg]\end{split}

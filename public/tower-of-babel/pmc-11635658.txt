Pazopanib in the real-world setting in soft tissue sarcomas: data from the Italian national registry
Authors: B. Vincenzi, P.P. Olimpieri, S. Celant, A. Mazzocca, A. Cortellini, A. Comandone, L. Tomassini, S. Di Segni, P. Russo, P.G. Casali
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BackgroundPazopanib is part of the therapeutic armamentarium for the treatment of patients with advanced non-adipocytic soft tissue sarcomas (STS) who have received prior chemotherapy, but its optimal use in STS histologies is still left to be further defined.Design and methodsData on STS patients treated with pazopanib in Italy have been prospectively collected from July 2013 to December 2019 through a drug monitoring registry managed by the Italian Medicines Agency (AIFA). This nationwide observational cohort study included patients with advanced STS who received pazopanib. Clinicians were mandatorily requested to fill in the AIFA monitoring registry in order to prescribe pazopanib.Patients were recorded on the basis of their clinical characteristics, histological subtype captured at the time of treatment start, and clinical outcome. Primary outcome was time to treatment discontinuation (TTD). Secondary outcomes recorded were frequency of dose reduction and time to first dose reduction.ResultsWe analyzed data from 1964 sarcoma patients. The most represented histological subtypes were leiomyosarcoma (44.7%), undifferentiated sarcomas/not otherwise specified (11.5%), and synovial sarcoma (8.1%). Overall, the median TTD was 106 days. The variables significantly associated to shorter TTD were Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status (1-2 versus 0), the number of previous lines of treatment (2-4 versus 0-1) and prescribed dose (200 mg or 400 mg versus 800 mg, all once daily). Among the most represented (>20 patients) histological subtypes, we also observed longer TTD in patients with histological diagnosis of malignant solitary fibrous tumor if compared with undifferentiated sarcoma not otherwise specified.ConclusionsIn this nationwide observational real-world study, the outcomes are similar to those reported in the pivotal trial (PALETTE study). Our study includes a significant number of patients with rare/ultra-rare sarcoma subtypes and underlines possible differences in treatment duration among these histologies.

Introduction

Soft tissue sarcomas (STS) are a group of rare and heterogeneous diseases, representing about 1% of all adult malignant and solid tumors.1,2 According to the World Health Organization (WHO), >100 different histological and molecular subtypes can be identified, exhibiting variable clinical behaviour.3 Prognosis of patients with STS is variable, with median 5-year specific survival rates of ∼80% in localized and locally advanced disease and <20% in metastatic patients.4 The number of new drugs approved for the treatment of metastatic STS is very limited. This can be due to the rarity of these tumors and to the fact that most clinical trials on sarcomas include all the histotypes without selecting based on the potential sensitivity to the drug being studied. Frontline anthracycline-based chemotherapy still represents the cornerstone of treatment in first line in most cases.5,6 Few therapeutic options after anthracycline failure are currently available and their efficacy is limited to some selected histology subtypes.7,8 Pazopanib is a multitarget tyrosine kinase inhibitor the mechanism of action of which is related to the selective inhibition of tumor angiogenesis and to the inhibition of key kinases involved in oncogenic pathways.9,10 The efficacy of the drug in patients with metastatic STS was proved by the phase III, double blind, placebo-controlled PALETTE trial, which showed a prolonged progression-free survival (PFS) [4.6 versus 1.6 months, hazard ratio (HR) 0.31, 95% confidence interval (CI) 0.24-0.40, P < 0.0001] with no statistically significant effect on overall survival (OS).11 These results, which led to pazopanib approval in 2012 for use in patients with pretreated STS, mentioned a higher activity in some histologies (leiomyosarcoma, synovial sarcoma) but clearly could not assess a differential activity in rare sarcoma subgroups. Adipocytic tumors were not included in the PALETTE trial based on findings of low efficacy observed in a phase II EORTC trial.12 Several subsequent prospective13, 14, 15, 16, 17 and retrospective17, 18, 19, 20, 21, 22, 23, 24 studies provided additional evidence regarding the effectiveness of pazopanib both in cohorts of patients not selected for histological type and in patients with specific subtypes (i.e. epithelioid sarcoma, solitary fibrous tumors, chondrosarcoma, and vascular sarcomas). Efficacy data in the PALETTE trial showed infrequent objective responses (partial response was observed in 6% of patients on pazopanib versus 0% on placebo, and stable disease occurred in 67% versus 38% on placebo) and median PFS and OS after pazopanib start of ∼3-5 months and 10-14 months, respectively.

In Italy the use of pazopanib has been regulated from 2013 to 2019 through the monitoring registry of the Italian Medicines Agency (AIFA), with the main purpose to monitor the appropriateness of prescription. We can postulate that this system intercepted ∼100% of patients treated in Italy in the activation period, since filling the monitoring registry on the AIFA web platform was mandatory to prescribe pazopanib and to get reimbursement from the Italian National Health Service (NHS). This real-world study aims to describe the characteristics of patients with sarcomas treated with pazopanib in Italy. We focus on analyzing the duration of pazopanib treatment and its variability concerning factors that were not thoroughly examined in the registration trial. We examined the relationship between treatment outcomes and several variables, particularly the different histological subtypes of sarcoma, in order to provide valuable insights into the use of pazopanib in different clinical settings.

Methods

Study population and data source

This is a nationwide prospective observational cohort study including all adult patients with STS treated in Italy with pazopanib from 22 July 2013 to 31 December 2019. Data were collected from the AIFA web platform of Monitoring Registries (wMRs), an administrative database the main goal of which is monitoring the appropriateness of drug prescription in Italy. wMRs have been already used in Italy to monitor drug utilization and effectiveness in the clinical practice.25,26 In brief, clinicians were mandatorily requested to fill in a set of information necessary to evaluate patient eligibility to treatment and to prescribe pazopanib for the authorized STS indication within the NHS drug reimbursement framework. This requirement has two important implications: (i) the patient is followed in a prospective way from eligibility to the end of treatment and (ii) the cohort represents a census of the overall population treated with pazopanib in Italy in the considered time frame. Data collected included histologic subtype, grade, stage of disease, number of previous lines of treatment, performance status (PS) [Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG)], and hepatic function. Histologies with <10 patients were included in the group ‘other’. Demographic and administrative information such as age, sex, and geographic area of the NHS center, date of prescription, prescribed dose, and date of drug dispensation are also collected. Moreover, we defined a new variable for center experience depending on the number of patients with STS treated in the NHS center. More specifically, we calculated the average number of patients treated per year for each NHS center (excluding the first year of activity) and we defined as ‘high volume’ those centers with an average equal to or higher than the third quartile of the distribution of the means (otherwise the center was labeled as ‘low volume’). Upon end of treatment, clinicians were also requested to provide the reasons for drug discontinuation. Unfortunately, end of treatment forms have not always been filled in by clinicians and are not available for all patients: we consider the date of the last dispensation as the date of treatment discontinuation, even when reasons for interruption were properly reported. Data cut-off was set to 31 December 2020, so each patient has a potential follow-up of at least 1 year. With the aim to evaluate the impact of interregional mobility, data regarding the Italian regions where patients were treated have been collected as well.

Procedures

Pazopanib has been used according to the recommendations reported in the summary of product characteristics of the drug. In particular, the available doses are 800, 600, 400 or 200 mg once daily. The decision of starting treatment was made by treating physician per local indication. A radiological assessment of the disease was required before initiating the treatment with pazopanib and again after each two cycles. The methods of reassessment were chosen at the discretion of each clinician. It was possible to prescribe pazopanib from the second line onwards, in case of disease progression during or within 6 months from the end of the previous chemotherapy treatment, or in the first line in case of disease recurrence during or within 12 months after the end of (neo)adjuvant chemotherapy treatment.

Outcomes

The main collected outcome was time to treatment discontinuation (TTD). TTD was defined as the time from the first administration of pazopanib to the last administration before treatment discontinuation for any cause, including death or loss to follow-up, plus half the days of medication covered by one prescription. We assumed that those patients who did not attend the scheduled medical visit for the renewal of prescription for >90 days were ‘discontinued’, even in the absence of the compilation of the ‘end of treatment’ form.

Since posology involves a daily intake of the drug and a prescription allowed for the dispensation of enough drug to cover 30 days of therapy, we believe that adding 15 days (half of the prescription period) could allow us to better model the timelines of therapy discontinuation which occurred during the time frame covered by the last prescription, (under the assumption that discontinuation events are randomly distributed across the 30 days, thus on average at day 15).

Secondary outcomes analyzed were number of cycles received by patients, as recorded in the wMRs (treating physicians were requested to record all prescription renewals and corresponding drug dispensations), and dose reductions, defined as the first dose reduction recorded (if any) for each patient. In this case, we defined time to first reduction as the time from the treatment start to the first reduction. We analyzed these outcomes considering both the median and the interquartile range. In case of time to first reduction, measurements were made only for patients with a recorded dose reduction and in all patients, censoring the patients without dose reduction according to their TTD. Number of cycles per patient has been stratified according to the main baseline features.

With reference to the number of cycles, an analysis on adherence has also been conducted. Therapy adherence can only be estimated through a proxy: drug prescriptions contain information on drug coverage, measured in days, therefore it is possible to check the consistency between number of days covered by a prescription/dispensation and days before the following prescription/dispensation, even it is not possible to determine with certainty the reasons behind the delays in drug dispensing.

Statistical analysis

Time to event analysis (TTD and time to first reduction in the overall census) was analyzed using the Kaplan–Meier estimator. In order to investigate the relationship between the selected outcomes and possible significant predictors like sex, age, number of previous lines of therapy and ECOG PS, furthermore, the association between the collected variables and the main outcome (TTD) was evaluated using Cox proportional-hazards models with time-dependent covariates and time-dependent coefficients.27

Among the selected subtypes [leiomyosarcoma, undifferentiated sarcoma/not otherwise specified (NOS), synovial sarcoma, malignant solitary fibrous tumor, and angiosarcoma], we compared each histology against the one showing the lowest TTD. We carried out a set of logistic regression models, in order to identify possible significant differences in baseline characteristics of patients with different histologies and, thus, the histology associated with the lowest TTD. Subsequently, we carried out nested univariable and multivariable Cox models by adding one significant confounder, as identified in the previous stage, at a time. Multivariable accelerated failure time (AFT) models were also obtained using the R flexsurv package, including the time-dependent dose variable, as a sensitivity analysis to verify the results obtained by the time-dependent Cox models.27

Results

Characteristics of patients and treatment

In total, from July 2013 to December 2019, we analyzed data from 1964 patients treated with pazopanib. Median (Q1-Q3) potential follow-up, defined as the time between data lock and treatment start (regardless of treatment status and possible cause for discontinuation), is equal to 4.28 years (2.68-5.89 years). A total of 1464 patients (74.5%) had high-grade disease and in 93.9% of cases pazopanib was started with a metastatic extension. Overall, data on 9829 cycles of pazopanib were recorded. The median number of cycles received per patient was 3 (Q1-Q3 2-6, range 1-81). As for the stratification by baseline characteristics, higher median number of cycles were observed for ECOG PS 0 (4; Q1-Q3 2-7, range 1-81), low grade of disease (4; Q1-Q3 2-8, range 1-47), pazopanib as first-line therapy (3.5; Q1-Q3 2-7, range 1-23) and some histologic histotypes: alveolar soft part sarcoma (4; Q1-Q3 2-27, range 1-57), malignant solitary fibrous tumor (4; Q1-Q3 2-7, range 1-52), synovial sarcoma (4; Q1-Q3 2-7, range 1-26).

Median (Q1-Q3) estimated adherence at second drug dispensation (82.3% of patients reached second cycle) is equal to 100% (85.7%-100%); at third dispensation (61.3% of patients reached third cycle), it is equal to 98.4% (82.2%-100%); at fourth (45.6% of patients reached fourth cycle), it is equal to 97.8% (81.1%-100%). Hence, a relevant percentage of patients (around 21% at second dispensation, up to 23% at fourth) experienced therapy interruptions for at least 20% of treatment days.

In 1942 patients (98.9%), the first prescribed dose of pazopanib was 800 mg/day. Even considering all cycles, the most common dose prescribed was 800 mg/day, with 7617 (77.50%) prescriptions registered, followed by 400, 600, and 200 mg/day, with 1077 (10.96%), 934 (9.50%), and 201 (2.04%) prescriptions, respectively. Some 495 patients (25.2%) experienced a dose reduction after the first cycle, with a median time to dose reduction of 56 days (Q1-Q3 28.0-112.5 days).

At the time of data lock, 32 patients (1.63%) were still receiving the treatment. In about 82% of cases (n = 1615) pazopanib was interrupted and the discontinuation-related events were correctly reported. For 317 patients (n = 16.14%), however, the causes of interruption were not specified and these patients were considered lost to follow up. At data lock, the median time of drug non-coverage for these patients was 41.1 months (Q1-Q3: 23.5-62.8 months). As expected, for the majority of patients (1426, 72.61% overall; 90.46% of whom reported end of treatment) the reason of treatment discontinuation was disease progression.

The benchmark for identifying high-volume centers was found to be two new treatments per year. About 40% (n = 765) of patients were treated in a high-volume center. Histologic subtypes were discrepant between high- and low-volume centers, with high-volume centers treating more patients with diagnosis of alveolar soft part sarcomas (n = 16 versus 7), malignant solitary fibrous tumors (n = 76 versus 35), and synovial sarcomas (n = 104 versus 56).

Baseline characteristics of each group were compared, with more high-grade tumors among undifferentiated sarcoma NOS (88.4%), leiomyosarcoma (72.7%), synovial sarcoma (76.2%), and solitary fibrous tumor (60.4%). Only a minority of patients for each histotype had a PS ECOG 2 (7.5% of synovial sarcoma, 5.5% of leiomyosarcoma, 4.9% of undifferentiated sarcoma NOS, and none of solitary fibrous tumor). In the solitary fibrous tumor subgroup, pazopanib was most commonly used as the first or second line of therapy (60.4%), while in the other histological subtypes it has been mainly administered after two or more previous lines of therapy.

Through the demographic available data, it was possible to evaluate the inter-regional mobility associated with pazopanib treatment. Lombardia, Emilia-Romagna, Friuli-Venezia Giulia, and Lazio showed the greatest confluence of non-resident patients, whereas Veneto, Campania, and Puglia had the highest number of residents starting treatment in other regions (Supplementary Figure S1, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2024.103995).

The association between available baseline characteristics and dose administered during therapy with the outcome (TTD) was also investigated using Cox proportional-hazard models (Table 2). Dose was included in the model as a time-dependent variable. Moreover, upon inspection of Schoenfeld residuals, ECOG performance score and the number of previous lines of treatment were found to fail the proportional-hazard assumption (see Supplementary Figure S2, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2024.103995). Therefore, patient follow-up has been divided into four time windows and new variables have been coded to investigate the effects of the baseline features showing time-dependent coefficients on the follow-up windows (see Supplementary material, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2024.103995). Increased risk of treatment discontinuation was associated with intermediate or high histology grade versus low (HR 1.31, 95% CI 1.06-1.62 and HR 1.45, 95% 1.19-1.76, respectively); 2-4 previous lines of therapy versus 0-1 lines, but only after at least 6 months of treatment (HR 1.45, 95% CI 1.20-1.75). ECOG PS (1-2 versus 0) was also found to be associated with TTD, with discontinuation risk being higher in the first months of follow-up and decreasing constantly within each next time window—in other words, the effect of worse baseline PS scores on discontinuation appears to decrease when treatment duration increases (Table 2); receiving full dose (800 mg/day) is associated with a significantly lower risk of discontinuation (HR 0.75, 95% CI 0.67-0.85) compared with reduced doses. Similar results were obtained using the flexsurv AFT model as a sensitivity analysis to measure the association between covariates and outcomes. AFT, by not being bound to the same assumptions of the Cox regression, permits to model the ECOG PS and the number of previous therapy line variables without the use of discontinuation time windows, providing similar but more accessible results (Supplementary Table S1, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2024.103995). As for the comparison between histological subtypes, carried out through logistic regression and nested Cox models as indicated in the Methods section (Supplementary Tables S2-S4, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2024.103995), statistical significance was only achieved while comparing malignant solitary fibrous tumor and undifferentiated sarcoma/NOS, with a 28% decrease of the risk of discontinuation associated with the former (HR 0.72, 95% CI 0.57-0.91), although this difference attenuates after adding confounders like sex, histological grade, and number of previous lines of treatment. Discussion

In this real-world study, we evaluated data from >1900 patients with metastatic STS treated in Italy with pazopanib over 7 years. They virtually represent all patients who received pazopanib in the country during this interval according to the authorized indication. Most patients were heavily pretreated and received pazopanib as their third or further therapy line. The outcomes, however, as indirectly suggested by available data, were close to those reported in the PALETTE trial, which was the pivotal trial which led to the drug approval.

To the best of our knowledge, this is the first and most comprehensive report on a large cohort of metastatic STS patients treated with pazopanib in a real-world setting. In such a survey, which was allowed by a compulsory regulatory tool enabling expensive drug prescription in Italy, several limitations are inevitable. These include the absence of direct data on toxicity and survival, as well as the lack of a centralized histopathological review and a radiological reassessment of tumor responses. Histopathological diagnosis, however, may be assumed to be reliable in high-volume centers which cover ∼40% of all patients in our study. Therefore, in this registry, information on concomitant medications was not required and given the lack of this therapeutic drug monitoring, it is unknown whether potential interactions could have influenced pazopanib exposure and whether this exposure was sufficient, especially in patients in whom the dose was reduced.

Finally, in our analysis we observed a high percentage of patients with ECOG PS 0 and 1. A possible explanation can be attributed to the clinicians’ tendency to underestimate the PS of patients and the fact that highly pretreated patients with poorer PS are not considered fit to receive pazopanib.

Our data point to a time to discontinuation of 106 days, which would be close to the median PFS of pazopanib observed in the pivotal trial, though in the ‘real world’ >65% of patients received pazopanib as their third or further therapy line. Treatment was interrupted for toxicity only in a minority of cases, while dose reductions were required in a significant number of patients (25%). In brief, the real-world use of pazopanib in STS in Italy has brought about ‘effectiveness’ results which do not seem far from the ‘efficacy’ data available. Overall, the benefit looks limited on average, though not negligible, as it looked like in the pivotal trial, with some patients gaining a higher advantage.

More interestingly, we observed longer TTD in selected histologies, such as malignant solitary fibrous tumor. Upon adjusting for possible confounders, malignant solitary fibrous tumor was associated with a 22% decreased risk of discontinuation compared with undifferentiated sarcoma NOS. This result stems from not considering tumor grade as a confounding factor, as conventional grading schemes have proven to be poorly applicable to solitary fibrous tumors, in contrast to other sarcomas.28 Synovial sarcoma, however, despite showing a borderline difference in the univariable analysis (log rank P value = 0.06), did not achieve statistical significance after adjusting for age and sex (P value = 0.180). Though in principle this may also be due to a relatively indolent behavior of some of these histologies, these data are consistent with some prospective and retrospective studies showing antitumoral activity with durable responses particularly in patients with advanced malignant solitary fibrous tumors.17

It is interesting to observe that the pivotal trial excluded only one histology (liposarcomas), but was virtually open to all other histologies in the STS field. Fortunately, its wide eligibility criteria allowed the drug to be approved also in these rare histologies, though for example, the number of patients with solitary fibrous tumor in the trial was very low, much lower than the number of such patients enrolled by an uncontrolled study, as inevitable given the rarity of this histology. Our data highlight some heterogeneity in baseline characteristics and TTD according to the histological subtypes. In conclusion, this study demonstrates that real-world data on the use of pazopanib in patients with sarcoma obtained from AIFA registries, represent a significant resource that highlights the drug’s utilization characteristics, potentially contributing to therapeutic decision-making process.

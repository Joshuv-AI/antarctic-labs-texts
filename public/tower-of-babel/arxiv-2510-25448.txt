On Type I blowup and ε \varepsilon -regularity criteria of suitable weak solutions to the 3D incompressible MHD equations Thanks: Corresponding author: Zhengce Zhang Thanks: Keywords: MHD equations; Suitable weak solutions; ε \varepsilon -regularity; Type I blowup Thanks: 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification: 35Q30; 35Q35; 35B65; 35B44; 76W05
Wentao Hu and Zhengce Zhang

Abstract
. In this paper, we study some new ε \varepsilon -regularity criteria related to the suitable weak solutions to the three-dimensional incompressible MHD equations. Our criteria allow great flexibility: The smallness and boundedness assumptions can be imposed on any scaling-invariant quantities of u u and b b , respectively, which may be chosen independently. As an intermediate step, we also show that the boundedness of any scaling-invariant quantity of u u and b b , chosen independently, ensures that ( 0 , 0 ) (0,0) is at most a Type I singular point, i.e. A ⁡ ( u , b , r ) + E ⁡ ( u , b , r ) + C ⁡ ( u , b , r ) + D ⁡ ( p , r ) < ∞ A(u,b;r)+E(u,b;r)+C(u,b;r)+D(p;r)<\infty . This extends Seregin’s Type I criteria for the Navier–Stokes equations (2006, Zap. Nauchn. Sem. POMI) [ 25 ] to the MHD system and provides a natural starting point for analysing Type II blowup, as in Seregin (2024, Comm. Pure Appl. Anal.) [ 30 ] .

In 1982, Caffarelli, Kohn and Nirenberg
[
1

introduced the notion of suitable weak solutions of 3D incompressible Navier–Stokes equations, which were defined as weak solutions
(u,p)\in(L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}\cap L_{t}^{2}H^{1})\times L^{3/2}
that satisfy the local energy inequality. By showing the regularity of suitable weak solutions at any space-time point under the smallness assumptions of certain scaled energy quantities near that point (these are known as the
\varepsilon
-regularity results), they proved that the one-dimensional Hausdorff measure of the singular set is
0

also gave a simplified proof. There
\varepsilon
-regularity criteria are stated as follows:

Theorem I.

There exist positive constants
\varepsilon_{1}
and
\varepsilon_{2}

(u,p)
is a suitable weak solution to (
1.2

\varlimsup_{r\to 0}\frac{1}{r^{2}}\int_{-r^{2}}^{0}\int_{B_{r}}|u|^{3}+|p|^{3/2}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t<\varepsilon_{1},

or

\varlimsup_{r\to 0}\frac{1}{r}\int_{-r^{2}}^{0}\int_{B_{r}}|\nabla u|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t<\varepsilon_{2}

holds, then
(0,0)
is a regular point.

Proposition 4.1

\partial_{t}(b\chi)-\Delta(b\chi)=b(\partial_{t}\chi-\Delta\chi)-2(\nabla\chi\cdot\nabla)b-\chi(u\cdot\nabla)b+\chi(b\cdot\nabla)u\quad\text{in}\ Q_{\rho}

(4.4)

Hence, by Young’s convolution inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\|I_{3}\|_{L^{2}(Q_{r})}\leqslant

\displaystyle c_{0}\rho^{1/2}A^{1/2}(u;\rho)\rho^{1/10}\big(\|\nabla b\|_{L^{2}(Q_{\rho})}+\rho^{-1}\|b\|_{L^{2}(Q_{\rho})}\big)\rho^{2/5}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{0}\rho^{3/2}A^{1/2}(u;\rho)\big[E^{1/2}(b;\rho)+H^{1/2}(b;\rho)\big].

As a result,

\displaystyle\iint_{Q_{r}}|b|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\leqslant

\displaystyle c_{0}\frac{r^{5}}{\rho^{2}}\big[H(b;\rho)+A(u;\rho)H(b;\rho)\big]+c_{0}\rho^{3}A(u;\rho)\big[E(b;\rho)+H(b;\rho)\big],

and thus

(4.8)

H(b;r)\leqslant c_{0}\bigg(\frac{r}{\rho}\bigg)^{2}H(b;\rho)+c_{0}\bigg(\frac{\rho}{r}\bigg)^{3}A(u;\rho)\big[E(b;\rho)+H(b;\rho)\big].

Now, similar to Proposition
4.1
, by (
4.7
)
1
we may as well assume
\sup_{0<r<1}A(u;r)\leqslant\varepsilon
. Then by (
4.8
) and the results in Section
3
, we get

H(b;r)\leqslant c_{0}\bigg(\frac{r}{\rho}\bigg)^{2}H(b;\rho)+\bigg(\frac{\rho}{r}\bigg)^{3}\varepsilon\hat{G}_{2},\quad\forall 0<4r\leqslant\rho\leqslant 1,

where
\hat{G}_{2}=\hat{G}_{2}(\mathcal{E}(1),\varepsilon,\mathcal{N})
is continuous with respect to
\varepsilon,\mathcal{N}
. Denote
r/\rho=\theta
and fix
\theta
so that
c_{0}\theta^{3/2}\leqslant 1
,
\theta\leqslant 1/4
, then an iteration argument similar to Proposition
3.1
yields that

(4.9)

H(b;\rho)\leqslant\theta^{-5/2}\rho^{1/2}H(b;1)+\frac{\theta^{-3}\varepsilon\hat{G}_{2}}{1-\theta^{1/2}},\quad\forall 0<\rho<r_{2}

for some
r_{2}\leqslant 1
. Hence the desired result follows.
∎

Proposition 4.3
.

For arbitrary
\varepsilon_{0}>0
and
\mathcal{N}>0
, there exists
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(\varepsilon_{0},\mathcal{N},\mathcal{E}(1))>0
, such that if

(4.10)

\varlimsup_{r\to 0}E(u;r)\leqslant\varepsilon,\ \text{and}\ \min\bigg\{\sup_{0<r\leqslant 1}A(b;r),\,\sup_{0<r\leqslant 1}E(b;r),\,\sup_{0<r\leqslant 1}C(b;r)\bigg\}\leqslant\mathcal{N},

then

\varlimsup_{r\to 0}H(b;r)\leqslant\varepsilon_{0}.

Proof.

Let
0<4r\leqslant\rho\leqslant 1
. Through an argument almost the same as Proposition
4.2
, we can derive

(4.11)

H(b;r)\leqslant c_{0}\bigg(\frac{r}{\rho}\bigg)^{2}H(b;\rho)+c_{0}\bigg(\frac{\rho}{r}\bigg)^{3}A(b;\rho)\big[E(u;\rho)+H(u;\rho)\big].

Denote
\theta=r/\rho
. By (
2.3
), we have

H(u;\rho)\leqslant c_{0}C^{2/3}(u;\rho)\leqslant c_{0}\theta^{4}A(u;\theta^{-2}\rho)+c_{0}\theta^{-4}A^{1/2}(u;\theta^{-2}\rho)E^{1/2}(u;\theta^{-2}\rho)

for
\rho\leqslant\theta^{2}
, which, combined with (
4.11
), yields

\displaystyle H(b;r)\leqslant

\displaystyle c_{0}\theta^{2}H(b;\theta^{-1}r)+c_{0}\theta^{-3}A(b;\theta^{-1}r)E(u;\theta^{-1}r)

\displaystyle+c_{0}\theta A(u;\theta^{-1}r)A(u;\theta^{-3}r)+c_{0}\theta^{-7}A(u;\theta^{-1}r)A^{1/2}(u;\theta^{-3}r)E^{1/2}(u;\theta^{-3}r)

for
r\leqslant\theta^{3}
. Again, recalling (
4.10
)
1
, we may as well assume
\sup_{0<r<1}E(u;r)\leqslant\varepsilon
, then the above estimate and the results in Section
3
lead to

(4.12)

H(b;r)\leqslant c_{0}\theta^{2}H(b;\theta^{-1}r)+\big(\theta^{-3}\varepsilon+\theta+\theta^{-7}\varepsilon^{1/2}\big)\hat{G}_{3},

where
\hat{G}_{3}=\hat{G}_{3}(\mathcal{E}(1),\varepsilon,\mathcal{N})
is continuous with respect to
\varepsilon,\mathcal{N}
. Suppose
\theta
is small enough so that
c_{0}\theta\leqslant 1
,
\theta\leqslant 1/4
. Iterating (
4.12
) for
k
times, where the positive integer
k
satisfies
\theta^{-k}r\leqslant\theta^{2}\leqslant\theta^{-k-1}r
, we obtain

\displaystyle H(b;r)\leqslant

\displaystyle\theta^{k}H(b;\theta^{-k}r)+\big(\theta^{-3}\varepsilon+\theta+\theta^{-7}\varepsilon^{1/2}\big)\cdot\frac{\hat{G}_{3}}{1-\theta}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\theta^{k}\cdot\bigg(\frac{\theta^{2}}{\theta^{-k}r}\bigg)^{3}H(b;\theta^{2})+\big(\theta^{-3}\varepsilon+\theta+\theta^{-7}\varepsilon^{1/2}\big)\cdot\frac{\hat{G}_{3}}{1-\theta}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\theta^{-6}rH(b;\theta^{2})+\big(\theta^{-3}\varepsilon+\theta+\theta^{-7}\varepsilon^{1/2}\big)\cdot\frac{\hat{G}_{3}}{1-\theta}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\theta^{-6}r\hat{G}_{3}+\big(\theta^{-3}\varepsilon+\theta+\theta^{-7}\varepsilon^{1/2}\big)\cdot\frac{\hat{G}_{3}}{1-\theta}

for all
0<r<r_{2}
and some
r_{2}\leqslant\theta^{3}
. Therefore, the desired result follows by taking first
\theta
and then
\varepsilon,r
small enough.
∎

), (

) and the results in Section

, it is easy to see that under the assumptions of Propositions

\varlimsup_{r\to 0}H(u;r)\leqslant\varepsilon^{2/3},\quad\varlimsup_{r\to 0}H(u;r)\leqslant\varepsilon^{1/3}\hat{G}_{4},\quad\text{and}\ \varlimsup_{r\to 0}H(u;r)\leqslant(\theta+\theta^{1/2})\hat{G}_{5}+d(\theta^{-1})\theta^{-2}\varepsilon,

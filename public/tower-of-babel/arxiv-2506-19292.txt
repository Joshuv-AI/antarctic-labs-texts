Inequalities related to the coefficients of the j j -function
Zhongjie Li

Abstract
In recent years, the log-concavity or log-convexity of combinatorial sequences and their root sequences, higher order Turán inequalities, and Laguerre inequalities of order two have been widely studied. However, the research of the Fourier coefficient c ⁡ ( n ) c(n) of the j j -function is limited to its asymptotic form. In this paper, we give the appropriate upper and lower bounds of c ⁡ ( n ) c(n) to establish the inequalities associated with it.



Keywords
:

j
-function, log-convexity, log-concavity, higher order Turán inequality, Laguerre inequality of order two.

Introduction

Let
H
denote the upper half-plane
\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\Im(z)>0\}
. The modular invari- ant
j
is defined by

\displaystyle j(\tau)

\displaystyle=\frac{(1+240\sum_{n\geq 1}\sigma_{3}(n)q^{n})^{3}}{q\prod_{n\geq 1}(1-q^{n})^{24}}

\displaystyle=q^{-1}+744+196884q+21493760q^{2}+\cdots

\displaystyle=q^{-1}+\sum_{n\geq 0}c(n)q^{n},

where
\tau\in

H
,
\sigma_{3}(n)=\sum_{d|n}d^{3}
and
q=e^{2\pi i\tau}
.

In the past few decades, the coefficient
c(n)
of the
j
-function has been extensively studied. Petersson
[
15
]
and Rademacher
[
16
]
independently used the circle method introduced by Hardy and Ramanujan
[
6
]
to derive the asymptotic formula,

c(n)\sim\frac{e^{4\pi\sqrt{n}}}{\sqrt{2}n^{3/4}},\ n\rightarrow\infty.

Subsequently, Dewar and Murty
[
3
]
obtained the same result using Laplace’s method of steepest descent.

To establish more effective estimates, Mahler
[
13
]
proved that

c_{m}(n)=[q^{n}]j^{m}\leq 1200e^{4\pi\sqrt{m(m+n)}},\quad\forall\ n,m\geq 1,

while Herrmann
[
10
]
showed that

c(n)\leq 6e^{4\pi\sqrt{n}},\quad\forall\ n\geq 1.

In 2005, Brisebarre and Philibert
[
1
]
derived precise lower and upper bounds for
c(n)
, which are utilized in this article.

In this article, we primarily focus on inequalities associated with
c(n)
, including log-concavity, log-convexity, higher-order Turán inequality and Laguerre inequality of order 2. Recall that a sequence
\{a_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
is called
log-convex
(
log-concave
, respectively) if for all
n\geq 0
,

\displaystyle a_{n}a_{n+2}-a_{n+1}^{2}\geq 0\ (\leq 0,\ respectively).

(1.1)

A sequence
\{a_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
is
ratio log-convex
(
ratio log-concave
, respectively) if the sequence
\{\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_{n}}\}_{n\geq 0}
is log-convex (log-concave, respectively). For a non-negative sequence
\{a_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
, its
root sequence
is defined as
\{\sqrt[n]{a_{n}}\}_{n\geq 1}
.

Liu and Wang
[
9
]
gave criteria for three-term recursive sequences to satisfy log-convexity. Xia
[
20
]
developed a systematic method to prove the log-concavity of
\{\sqrt[n]{a_{n}}\}_{n\geq 1}
when
a_{n}
satisfies a second-order linear recurrence. Numerous combinatorial sequences have been shown to be log-convex, and the root sequences of combinatorial sequences have been confirmed to be log-concave, see
[
17
,
21
,
22
,
23
]
for further details.

A sequence
\{a_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
is said to satisfy the
higher order Turán inequalities
or the
cubic Newton inequalities
if for all
n\geq 2
,

\displaystyle 4(a_{n}^{2}-a_{n-1}a_{n+1})(a_{n+1}^{2}-a_{n}a_{n+2})-(a_{n}a_{n+1}-a_{n-1}a_{n+2})^{2}\geq 0.

(1.2)

The Turán inequality and the higher order Turán inequality are related to the Laguerre-Pólya class of real entire functions. A real entire function

\displaystyle\psi(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}

is said to be in the Laguerre-Pólya class, denoted
\psi(x)\in\mathcal{LP}
, if

\displaystyle\psi(x)=cx^{m}e^{-\alpha x^{2}+\beta x}\prod\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}(1+x/x_{k})e^{-x/x_{k}},

where
c,\ \beta,\ x_{k}
are real numbers,
\alpha\geq 0
,
m
is a nonnegative integer and
\sum x_{k}^{-2}<\infty
. Dimitrov
[
4
]
proved that if a real entire function
\psi(x)\in\mathcal{LP}
, its Maclaurin coefficients satisfy the higher Turán inequality (
1.2
).

Hou and Li
[
11
]
proposed an approach to analyze the higher order Turán inequalities for the P-recursive sequence
\{a_{n}\}_{n\geq N}
. Wang
[
18
]
presented a unified method to studying the higher order Turán inequalities for the sequence
\{\frac{a_{n}}{n!}\}_{n\geq 0}
when
a_{n}
satisfies a second-order linear recurrence. Many combinatorial sequences have been demonstrated
to satisfy the higher Turán inequality (
1.2
), see
[
2
,
5
,
14
]
for more details.

A polynomial
f(x)
satisfies the Laguerre inequality if

f^{{}^{\prime}}(x)^{2}-f(x)f^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(x)\geq 0.

In 1913, Jensen
[
7
]
introduced the
n
-th generalization of the Laguerre inequality as

\displaystyle L_{n}(f(x)):=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=0}^{2n}(-1)^{n+k}\binom{2n}{k}f^{(k)}(x)f^{(2n-k)}(x)\geq 0,

(1.3)

where
f^{(k)}(x)
denotes the
k
th derivative of
f(x)
.

Similarly, Wang and Yang
[
19
]
defined that a sequence
\{a_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
satisfies the Laguerre inequality of order
m
if for
n\geq 1
,

\displaystyle L_{m}(a_{n}):=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=0}^{2m}(-1)^{k+m}\binom{2m}{k}a_{n+k}a_{2m-k+n}\geq 0.

(1.4)

Li
[
8
]
proposed a method to determine an explicit integer
N
such that the Laguerre inequality of order 2 holds for a P-recursive sequence
\{a_{n}\}_{n\geq N}
. Wang and Yang
[
19
]
proved that the partition function, the
overpartition function, the Bernoulli numbers, the derangement numbers, the Motzkin numbers, the Fine numbers, the Franel numbers and the Domb numbers all satisfy the Laguerre inequalities of order two.

In this paper, we first concretize the upper and lower bounds of
c(n)
given in
[
1
]
to get the upper and lower bounds we need in this article. Subsequently, we get the upper and lower bounds of
r(n)=\frac{c(n+1)}{c(n)}
and
u(n)=\frac{c(n-1)c(n+1)}{c(n)^{2}}
. By integrating the sufficient conditions proposed in this paper for the root sequences to satisfy log-concavity or log-convexity, the sufficient conditions for higher order Turán inequalities from
[
11
]
and the sufficient conditions for second-order Laguerre inequalities from
[
8
]
, we systematically derive the inequalities related to
c(n)
.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we establish the lower and upper bounds for

,
r(n)
and
u(n)
. In Section

satisfies both the higher order Turán inequality and the second-order Laguerre inequality.

Log-concavity and log-convexity

In this section, we first apply the bounds (
2.2
) and (
2.3
) of

to derive both the log-concavity and ratio log-convexity of

Theorem 3.1
.

The sequence
\{c(n)\}_{n\geq 0}
is log-concave and the sequence
\{c(n)\}_{n\geq 1}
is ratio log-convex.

Proof.

To establish the log-convexity of
c(n)
, it suffices to demonstrate that the following inequality holds,

\frac{c(n+1)}{c(n)}\geq\frac{c(n+2)}{c(n+1)}.

Thus, we only need to verify that

f(n)\geq g(n+1).

Through
Mathematica
, we can conclude that
f(n)-g(n+1)=0
has a unique real root
b_{0}\in(0,1)
. Given that
f(1)-g(2)>0
, it follows that
f(n)>g(n+1)
for
n\geq 1
. By verifying the initial values, we confirm that the sequence
\{c(n)\}_{n\geq 0}
is log-concave.

Similarly, by applying the same method, we can prove that the sequence
\{c(n)\}_{n\geq 1}
is ratio log-convex.
∎

Before proving that the root sequence
\{\sqrt[n]{c(n)}\}_{n\geq 1}

Lemma 3.2

The sequence

Proof.

\displaystyle D(n)=2\log e_{n}+n(n+1)\log f_{n+1}-n(n+3)\log g_{n}\geq 0.

\lim_{n\to+\infty}D(n)=+\infty,\quad\lim_{n\to+\infty}D^{\prime}(n)=\lim_{n\to+\infty}D^{\prime\prime}(n)=0,

\{\sqrt[n]{c(n)}\}_{n\geq 1}

In this section, we first give a sufficient condition for the higher order Turán inequality, which was proposed by Hou and Li
[
11
]
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let

t(x,y)=4(1-x)(1-y)-(1-xy)^{2}\ \ and\ \ u_{n}=\frac{a_{n-1}a_{n+1}}{a_{n}^{2}}.

If there exist an integer
N
, a lower bound
f_{n}
and an upper bound
g_{n}
of
u_{n}
such that for all

f_{n}<u_{n}<g_{n},

t(f_{n},f_{n+1})>0,\quad t(f_{n},g_{n+1})>0,\quad t(g_{n},f_{n+1})>0,\quad t(g_{n},g_{n+1})>0.

Then
\{a_{n}\}_{n\geq N}
satisfies the higher order Turán inequality.

Next, we use the above lemma and bounds (

) and (

\{c(n)\}_{n\geq 1}

.

The sequence

Proof.

2.6
,

has a lower bound

, we only need to prove that the following four inequalities holds for all

By

, we analyze

, and find a unique real root

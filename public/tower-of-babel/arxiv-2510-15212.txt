A quasi-polynomial bound for the minimal excluded minors for a surface
Sarah Houdaigoui, Ken-ichi Kawarabayashi

Abstract
As part of their graph minor project, Robertson and Seymour showed in 1990 that the class of graphs that can be embedded in a given surface can be characterized by a finite set of minimal excluded minors [ 27 ] . However, their proof, because existential, does not provide any information on these excluded minors. Seymour proved in 1993 the first and, until now, only known upper bound on the order of the minimal excluded minors for a given surface [ 30 ] . This bound is double exponential in the Euler genus g g of the surface and, therefore, very far from the Ω ⁡ ( g ) \Omega(g) lower bound on the maximal order of minimal excluded minors for a surface and most likely far from the best possible bound. More than thirty years later, this paper finally makes progress in lowering this bound to a quasi-polynomial in the Euler genus of the surface. The main catalyzer to reach a quasi-polynomial bound is a breakthrough on the characteristic size of a forbidden structure for a minimal excluded minor G G for a surface of Euler genus g g : although it is not hard to show that G G does not contain O ⁡ ( g ) O(g) disjoint cycles that are contractible and nested in some embedding of G G as demonstrated by Seymour in [ 30 ] , this bound can be lowered to O ⁡ ( log ⁡ g ) O(\log g) which is essential to obtain the quasi-polynomial bound in this paper. Moreover, we find an upper bound on the maximum degree of G G and the maximum size of a face in an embedding of G G in a surface of Euler genus g + 1 g+1 or g + 2 g+2 , which is, to our understanding, the first such bound. Finally, we develop a new method to bound the height of the tree in a tree decomposition of G G . As subsidiary results, we also improve the current bound on the treewidth of a minimal excluded minor G G for a surface by improving the first and, until now, only known bound provided by Seymour in [ 30 ] . Moreover, we show a better upper bound on the order of a grid minor in G G , improving the result by Thomassen [ 34 ] from 1997.



1
Introduction

The graph minor theorem
[
29
]
, proved by Robertson and Seymour in 2004, states that every class of graphs closed under minors can be defined by a finite list of minimal excluded minors (which we will denote by
excluded minors
from now on). Understanding the nature of the excluded minors for various classes of graphs closed under minor operations is a very active area of research. Especially, exploring the link between graph parameters that are minor-monotone and the presence of specific graphs as minor has been a very fruitful research area.
The most iconic example of such work is the grid minor theorem
[
26
]
which states that there exists a function
f
such that, for every grid
H
, every graph with treewidth at least
f(|V(H)|)
contains
H
as a minor. There has been extensive work on lower and upper bounds for the function
f
, see
[
6
,
7
]
for the current state of the art. There are also results analogous to the grid minor theorem for pathwidth
[
20
,
32
]
and treedepth
[
18
]
.
Another minor monotone graph parameter that has always aroused major interest is the genus. It was studied well before the graph minor project, but this project sparked a renewal in this field of study and a shift in the angle of approach.

In 1930, Kuratowski
[
22
]
showed that the planar graphs can be described as graphs that do not contain a subdivision of
K_{5}
nor
K_{3,3}
as a subgraph. Subsequently, in 1937, Wagner showed that the planar graphs can be described as graphs that do not contain
K_{5}
nor
K_{3,3}
as a minor. These two results can easily be shown to be equivalent.
Finding an analog to Kuratowski’s or Wagner’s theorems for higher genus surfaces was for a long time a central open problem: in 1978, Glover and Huneke showed that there is a finite number of minimal subgraphs excluded as subdivision (thereafter minimal excluded subgraphs) for the projective plane
[
11
]
; then Glover, Huneke and Wang presented in 1979 a list of 103 minimal excluded subgraphs
[
12
]
; finally, Archdeacon proved in 1980-81 that this list is complete
[
2
,
3
]
.
It is easy to show that a class of graphs is characterized by a finite set of excluded minors if and only if it is characterized by a finite set of minimal excluded subgraphs (see, e.g.,
[
25
, Proposition 6.1.1]
).
In 1989, Archdeacon and Huneke extended the result from the projective plane to every non orientable surface by showing that every non orientable surface admits only a finite number of minimal excluded subgraphs. Robertson and Seymour finally settled the question by showing that the number of excluded minors for a surface is always finite in a preliminary result of the celebrated graph minor theorem
[
27
]
.
Simpler proofs of the graph minor theorem for surfaces have been then found by Mohar
[
24
]
and Thomassen
[
34
]
.

However, the graph minor theorem’s proof is purely existential and hence gives no clue on how to find the excluded minors for a surface. Therefore, except for the plane and the projective plane, the exact lists of excluded minors for each surface are unknown. Considerable efforts toward such a result for the torus have been made, but even though several classes of minimal excluded subgraphs for the torus are known (see
[
8
,
16
,
9
,
14
,
4
]
), no exhaustive list was eventually produced. Among all of these results, there are at least 2200 excluded minors for the torus. It is, therefore, unrealistic in the foreseeable future to hope that a characterization of excluded minors will be explicitly found for surfaces of the Euler genus higher than the torus’.

Consequently, the research shifted from finding an explicit list to bounding parameters (order, degree, treewidth…) of these excluded minors for a surface, usually by a function of the Euler genus of the surface.

In 1993, Seymour gave the first and, until now, the only known upper bound on the order of an excluded minor for a surface
[
30
]
.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
30
]
)
.

Let
S
be a given surface of Euler genus
g
. Every excluded minor for
S
has at most
2^{2^{k}}
vertices where
k=(3g+9)^{9}
.

This bound is a double-exponential in the Euler genus
g
of the surface. Moreover, the lower bound on the maximal order of excluded minors for a surface is easily shown to be
\Omega(g)
(by considering
g+1
copies of
K_{3,3}
).
Therefore, the gap between known lower and upper bounds is huge, and Seymour’s upper bound is most likely far from the best possible bound.

In the first improvement in thirty years, we drastically lower the bound on the order of an excluded minor for a surface to a quasi-polynomial in the Euler genus
g
of the surface.
The main result of this paper is indeed the following:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
S
be a given surface of Euler genus
g
. Every excluded minor for
S
is of order at most
U(g)=g^{O(\log^{3}g)}
.

Let us highlight the technical aspects of our result.
Let
S,S^{\prime}
be surfaces with
S^{\prime}
of Euler genus
g
and
S
of Euler genus
g+1
or
g+2
. Let
G
be an excluded minor for the surface
S^{\prime}
and suppose that
G
can be embedded in surface
S
with embedding
\Pi
.

The main catalyzer to reach a quasi-polynomial bound is a breakthrough on the characteristic size of a forbidden structure for
G
: it is not hard to show that
G
does not contain
O(g)
disjoint cycles that are
\Pi
-contractible and nested in
\Pi
, as demonstrated by Seymour in
[
30
, (2.2)]
. We show that this bound can be lowered to
O(\log g)
(Section
5
, Proposition
5.5
). A related result was obtained in
[
19
]
. Moreover, we extend this result to
\Pi
-noncontractible homotopic cycles of
G
.
These structural results are utterly essential to obtain the quasi-polynomial bound in this paper.

Another important ingredient of our proof is a
g^{O(\log^{2}g)}
upper bound on the maximum degree of
G
and on the maximum size of a face in
(G,\Pi)
(Subsection
6.1
Theorem
6.1
). It is, to our understanding, the first such bound. Finally, we develop a new method to bound the height of the tree in a tree decomposition of
G
and we obtain a bound that is drastically lower than the one found by Seymour
[
30
, claim (6) in (4.1)]
. Indeed, we lower it from a single exponential in
g
to a quasi-polynomial
g^{O(\log^{3}g)}
in
g
(Subsection
6.2
, Theorem
6.4
).

Another very important result from Seymour’s paper
[
30
]
is the first and, until now, only known bound on the treewidth of an excluded minor for a surface:

Theorem 1.3

(
[
30
, (3.3)]
)
.

The treewidth of
G
is bounded by a polynomial in
g
:

tw(G)\leq T_{S}(g)

with
T_{S}(g)=3(g+3)^{2}(3g+16)-3=O(g^{3})
.

As a corollary of the structural results we obtain in this paper, we improve the bound on the treewidth:

Theorem 1.4
.

The treewidth of
G
is bounded by the following function of
g
:

tw(G)\leq T(g)

with
T(g)=264(g+2)(m+1)-1=O(g\log g)
, where
m=2(\lfloor\log_{q}(3g+4)\rfloor+2)
and
q=\frac{1153}{1152}
.

Moreover, as a subsidiary result, we obtain an improvement on the following important result of Thomassen
[
34
]
(see the end of Subsection
5.2.2
):

Theorem 1.5

(
[
34
]
)
.

Let
H
be a
2
-connected graph such that
g(H)=g
and
g(H-e)<g
for every edge
e
in
H
. Then
H
contains no subdivision of the
4k\times 2k
grid, with
k=\lceil 800g^{3/2}\rceil
.

We improve the bound on the size of the forbidden grid:

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
G
be a
2
-connected excluded minor for a surface of Euler genus
g
. Then
G
contains no subdivision of the
4k\times 2k
grid, with
k=O(\sqrt{g}\log g)
.

In an appendix, we give a tight lower bound on the order of the excluded minors for each surface, together with a quick proof:

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
S
be a surface of Euler genus
g
and let
L(g)=\left\lfloor\frac{7+\sqrt{1+24g}}{2}\right\rfloor+1
. There exists a excluded minor for
S
of order

|V(G)|=L(g)

and no excluded minor for
S
is of order
<L(g)
.

Let us highlight thereafter the algorithmic implications of our results.
As a consequence of the graph minor theorem, Robertson and Seymour
[
28
]
showed in 1995 that, for every minor-closed class of graphs
\mathcal{G}
, there exists a cubic time algorithm that decides whether a graph belongs to
\mathcal{G}
(membership test). The running time was later improved to quadratic by Kawarabayashi et al.
[
15
]
in 2012 and to quasi-linear by Korhonen et al.
[
21
]
in 2024.
However, although the algorithm in itself is explicit and therefore constructive, it relies on the (finite) set of excluded minors that characterizes the class of graphs
\mathcal{G}
which is in general unknown.
Furthermore, Fellows and Langston
[
10
]
observed in 1989 that there is no algorithm that, given a Turing machine that is a membership test for a minor-closed class of graphs
\mathcal{C}
, computes the excluded minors that characterize
\mathcal{C}
. Nevertheless, Fellows and Langston showed in the same paper that an explicit upper bound on the treewidth of the excluded minors for a minor-closed class of graphs leads to an algorithm to find them. Adler et al.
[
1
]
also gave such an algorithm. Therefore, our improvement on the upper bound on the treewidth of the excluded minors for a given surface induces a speedup for these algorithms.

Moreover, we take two examples to illustrate the importance of a bound for the order of the excluded minors for a surface. First, Kawarabayashi et al.
[
17
]
gave in 2008 a linear time FPT-algorithm for embedding graphs into an arbitrary surface
S
, with parameter the genus of
S
, which relies on the bound on the order of the excluded minors for
S
. Second, Grohe et al.
[
13
]
gave in 2013 a quadratic time algorithm for computing graph minor decompositions, which also makes use of the bound on the order of the excluded minors for a surface.

Therefore, by improving in this paper the bounds for various parameters (especially order and treewidth, but also degree, height, and degree of the tree of a tree decomposition) of the excluded minors for a surface, we contribute significantly to the effort to solve various problems more efficiently.

Overview of the proof and the paper

Let
S,S^{\prime}
be surfaces with
S^{\prime}
of Euler genus
g
and
S
of Euler genus
g+1
or
g+2
. Let
G
be an excluded minor for the surface
S^{\prime}
and suppose that
G
can be embedded in surface
S
with embedding
\Pi
. We define a
piece
as a vertex or a face of
(G,\Pi)
.

To prove that
G
is of order bounded by a quasi-polynomial in
g
, we proceed in broad outline as follows:

•

We first prove that the degree of
G
and the size of faces in
(G,\Pi)
are bounded by a quasi-polynomial in
g
(Subsection
6.1
).

The proof proceeds by contradiction. We make the assumption that there is a piece
p
that is either a vertex of degree
>\Delta(g)
or a face of size
>\Delta(g)
for some function
\Delta
(defined in Theorem
6.1
), and deduce that there is a
\Pi
-contractible fan on
p
(a fan
F
on a piece
p
is roughly a subgraph consisting of internally disjoint paths from
p
to a path, see Definition
6.2
) whose size is smaller than
\Delta(g)
by a polynomial factor in
g
. Then, we show that this fan extends to a
\Pi
-contractible subgraph of
G
that contains a forbidden structure consisting of
O(\log g)
nested cycles that intersect only in
p
and that all are
\Pi
-contractible (see Subsection
5.2
for the proper statement).

•

We then show that the height of the tree of a tree decomposition of
G
of width
w
is bounded by a quasi-polynomial in
g
and
w
(Subsection
6.2
).

We assume that there is a path of length
>P(g,w)
(for some function
P
defined in Proposition
6.3
) in the tree of a tree decomposition of
G
of width
w
that induces a
\Pi
-contractible subgraph
G^{\prime}
of
G
. On one hand, we show that it implies that
|V(G^{\prime})|>P(g,w)
. On the other hand, we take advantage of the natural separations given by the tree decomposition to show that
G^{\prime}
is separated from the rest of
G
by a small (polynomial in
g
) separation, and then to deduce that
|V(G^{\prime})|
must be bounded by
P(g,w)
. This leads to a contradiction. Finally, there is no such path in the tree of a tree decomposition of
G
and we deduce a bound on the height of the tree of a tree decomposition of
G
.

•

We finally put everything together to show our main result (Subsections
4.2.3
and
6.3
).

Seymour (Theorems
1.3
and
4.15
,
[
30
, (3.3) and claim (5) in (4.1)]
) shows that both the treewidth of
G
and the maximum degree of the tree in an optimal tree decomposition of
G
(with additional conditions) are bounded by
O(g^{3})
. As subsidiary results of the structural results, we improve both of these results to
O(g\log g)
. These two bounds, together with the bound on the height of the tree, give a first bound, single exponential in
g
, on the order of
G
. Then, by looking at the pathwidth and a path decomposition of
G
, and by taking advantage of the bound on the height of the tree of a tree decomposition of
G
(that still holds when applied to a path decomposition), we finally reach a
g^{O(\log^{3}g)}
bound on the order of
G
.

In this paper, we prove two main structural results that describe forbidden structures in
\Pi
-contractible subgraphs of
(G,\Pi)
and that will be used repetitively to find a contradiction in the main proof:

•

There are at most
O(g)
internally disjoint paths from one piece to another piece (Section
5
, Proposition
5.1
).

•

There are at most
O(\log g)
disjoint cycles that are either
\Pi
-contractible and nested in
\Pi
or
\Pi
-noncontractible homotopic (Section
5
, Propositions
5.5
and
5.12
). These two results are improvements on a result of Seymour (Proposition
5.2
in Section
5
,
[
30
, (2.2)]
) and are central to obtain the main result of this paper.

This paper is organized as follows. Section
3
contains the basic definitions and notations for graphs and surfaces, which are used throughout the paper. In Section
4
, we introduce basic results for graphs on surfaces and preliminary results on the excluded minors for a surface. Section
5
contains the two main structural results mentioned above. In Section
6
, we detail the proof of our main result. This section contains three subsections whose content has been explained above. Section
7
explores the bottlenecks of our proof and provides suggestions to improve the main result of this paper further. Finally, we provide a quick proof for a tight lower bound on the order of the excluded minors for each surface in the appendix.

3
Definitions and Notations

Basics

We consider simple graphs. Let
G
be a graph.
A walk in
G
is a finite sequence of vertices so that two consecutive vertices are adjacent. A path is a walk whose vertices are distinct, except potentially its two extremities. The interior of a path
P
consists of
P
from which we remove its two extremities. A circuit (or closed walk) is a walk with equal extremities. A cycle is a circuit whose vertices are all distinct except its two extremities.

The degree of a vertex
v
of
G
is the number of edges adjacent to
v
; we denote it by
d_{G}(v)
(or
d(v)
if clear in the context). The maximum degree of
G
is the maximum degree of a vertex of
G
and is denoted
\Delta(G)
.

A
minor

H
of
G
is a (multi)graph obtained from
G
by successive operations of suppression of a vertex, suppression of an edge, and contraction of an edge. A
proper minor
of
G
is a minor of
G
different from
G
.
We denote by
G-v
(resp.
G-e
) the graph obtained from
G
after the suppression of a vertex
v
(resp. an edge
e
), and we denote
G-X
the graph obtained from
G
after the suppression of a set
X
of vertices or edges. We denote by
G/e
the multigraph obtained from
G
after the contraction of an edge
e
. We denote
G/X
the multigraph obtained from
G
after the contraction of a set
X
of edges (remark that the order in which the edges in
X
are contracted is not important).

Trees and tree decomposition

A
tree

T
is a connected graph without any cycle. We can distinguish a vertex
v
of
T
, called the
root
, then
T
is said to be
rooted
in
v
. We define the height of a tree as the order of its longest path.
A
spanning tree

T
of
G
is a subgraph of
G
that is a tree and contains all the vertices of
G
.
A
tree decomposition
of
G
is a pair
(T,(V_{t})_{t\in V(T)})
with
T
a tree and, for every
t\in V(T)
,
V_{t}\subseteq V(G)
with the following properties:

•

\bigcup_{t\in V(T)}V_{t}=V(G)
,

•

for every
e=uv\in E(G)
, there exists
t\in V(T)
so that
u,v\in V_{t}
,

•

for
t,t^{\prime},t^{\prime\prime}\in V(T)
so that
t^{\prime}
is on the path between
t
and
t^{\prime\prime}
in
T
,
V_{t}\cap V_{t^{\prime\prime}}\subseteq V_{t^{\prime}}
.

The
width
of a tree decomposition
(T,(V_{t})_{t\in V(T)})
of
G
is
\max_{t\in V(T)}|V_{t}|-1
and the
treewidth
of
G
is the minimal width of its tree decompositions.

We say that a tree decomposition
(T,(V_{t})_{t\in V(T)})
of
G
(possibly with some additional properties) is
minimal
, if
|V(T)|
is chosen minimum (and the tree decomposition has the additional properties).

Connectivity

A graph
G
is
connected
if, for every pair of vertices
u,v\in V(G)
, there is a path in
G
between
u
and
v
. Let
k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
,
G
is
k
-connected
if, for every set
V\subseteq V(G)
of size
k-1
,
G-V
is still connected. Let
k\geq 1
, a
k
-separator
of
G
is a set
V\subseteq V(G)
of
k
vertices so that
G-V
is not connected. If
G
contains a
1
-separator
\{v\}
,
v
is called a
cutvertex
of
G
. Remark that, for
k\geq 1
, a graph contains no
k-1
-separator if and only if it is
k
-connected.
A
separation
of
G
is a pair
(A,B)
of the subgraph of
G
such that
A\cup B=G
and
A\cap B
contains no edge. Remark that
V(A\cap B)
is a separator of
G
. For
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we say that
(A,B)
is a
k
-separation
of
G
if
(A,B)
is a separation of
G
and
|V(A\cap B)|=k
.
A
(
2
-connected) block
of

is the subgraph of
G
induced by an equivalence class of the following equivalence relation on
E(G)
:
e_{1}\sim e_{2}
if
e_{1}=e_{2}
or there is in
G
a cycle that contains
e_{1}
and
e_{2}
. Remark that
H
is a block of

if and only if

is a maximal
2
-connected subgraph of

.

Bridges

Let
H
be a graph and
H_{0}
be a subgraph of
H
. A
bridge

B
of
H
on
H_{0}
is either an edge with both ends in
H_{0}
(and its ends do not belong to the bridge) or a connected subgraph of
H-V(H_{0})
together with all the edges which have one end in this component and the other end in

(the ends in
H_{0}
does not belong to the bridge).
Remark that the bridges of

on

partition

.
We say that

is an
attach
of a bridge

exists.

To describe graphs on surfaces, we use almost identical definitions and notations (the ones that differ will be indicated) as in the book
Graphs on Surfaces
from Mohar and Thomassen
[
25
]
. Thereafter, we give a condensed summary of the definitions and notation used in this paper.

Surface and disk

We define a
surface
as a connected compact Hausdorff topological space
S
so that each point of
S
has an open neighborhood homeomorphic to the open unit disk in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. A
simple closed curve
in a surface
S
is a continuous 1-1 function
f:[0,1]\mapsto S
with
f(0)=f(1)
. A
disk
on
S
is a simple closed curve that can be continuously deformed into a single point.

Embedding and facial walk

Let
G
be a graph and
S
a surface, we define an
embedding

\Pi
of
G
in
S
to be a pair
\Pi=(\pi,\lambda)
where
\pi=\{\pi_{v}|v\in V(G)\}
associates to each vertex
v\in V(G)
a cyclic permutation of the edges incident to
v
and
\lambda:E(G)\mapsto\{-1,1\}
associates to each edge
e\in E(G)
a sign (either
-1
or
1
) called its signature.

A
local change
of an embedding
\Pi=(\pi,\lambda)
of
G
changes the clockwise ordering to anticlockwise at some vertex
v\in V(G)
, i.e.
\pi_{v}
is replaced by its inverse
\pi_{v}^{-1}
, and
\lambda(e)
is replaced by
-\lambda(e)
for every edge
e
that is incident with
v
. Two embeddings of
G
are
equivalent
if one can be obtained from the other by a sequence of local changes.

The
face traversal procedure
is the following: We start with a vertex
v\in V(G)
and an edge
e=vw\in E(G)
. Let’s traverse the edge from
v
to
w
, if the signature of
e
is
1
, then we continue the walk along the edge
e^{\prime}=\pi_{w}(e)
, otherwise (
\lambda(e)=-1
), we change
\pi_{u}
by
\pi_{u}^{-1}
for every
u\in V(G)
and we continue the walk along the edge
e^{\prime}=\pi_{w}(e)
. And so forth. The walk is completed when the initial edge
e
is encountered in the same direction (from
v
to
w
) and with the same orientation (
\pi
is the same as before we began the walk). A
\Pi
-facial walk
(or, if clear in the context,
face
) is a closed walk in
G
determined by the face traversal procedure. We define the
size
of a face to be the number of edges that the walk contains. We define the
maximum face degree
of
(G,\Pi)
to be
\Delta_{F}(G,\Pi)=\max\{|f|,f\text{ is a face in }(G,\Pi)\}
.

Let
C
be a cycle of
G
, we define the
signature
of
C
in
\Pi
to be
\lambda(C)=\prod_{e\in C}\lambda(e)
. We say that
C
is
two-sided
if
\lambda(C)=1
and
one-sided
otherwise. We say that an embedding
\Pi
of
G
is
orientable
if every cycle
C
of
G
is two-sided, otherwise we say that it is
non orientable
.

Genus and embeddability

In this paper, the
genus
of a surface always refers to the Euler genus, that is to say twice its orientable genus if the surface is orientable and its non orientable genus if the surface is non orientable. The sphere is the only surface of genus
0
, the projective plane is the only surface of genus
1

Let

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{i-1}\sum_{A\in\mathcal{A}_{k}}|A|

\displaystyle=\sum_{A\in\mathcal{A}_{0}}|A|+\sum_{k=1}^{i-1}\sum_{A\in\mathcal{A}_{k}}|A|

Let

By contradiction, suppose a path

. Then
(P_{i})_{0\leq i\leq q-1}
are pairwise disjoint subpaths of

. For
0\leq i\leq q-1
, let
G_{i}=\cup_{p\in\overline{P_{i}}}V_{p}-(V_{p_{i\times P(g,w)}}\cup V_{p_{i\times P(g,w)+P(g,w)-1}})

(G_{i})_{0\leq i\leq q-1}

and, for

and

As

6.3
The order of
G
is quasi-polynomial

The following results conclude our paper by showing that
G
is of order
g^{O(\log^{3}g)}
. It proceeds in two steps: first, it is easy to derive from Theorem
6.4
that
G
is of order bounded by a quasi single exponential
2^{g^{O(\log^{3}g)}}
in
g
; then, we deduce from this result a bound on the pathwidth of
G
, and use it to prove the quasi-polynomial bound on the order of
G
. Moreover, recall that we restricted ourselves to an excluded minor
G
that is
2
-connected using Corollary
4.13
. We therefore conclude the bound on the order of any excluded minor for the surface
S
using this result.

Corollary 6.5
.

|V(G)|\leq 2^{Q(g)}

with
Q(g)=g^{O(\log^{3}g)}
a quasi-polynomial in
g
so that
Q(g)\geq\log((T(g)+1)\times\Delta_{T}(g)^{P^{\prime}(g,T(g))})
.

Proof.

Let
(T,(V_{t})_{t\in T})
be a minimal linked tree decomposition of
G
of width
tw(G)
(Thomas
[
33
]
proved that, for any graph
H
, there exists a linked tree decomposition of
H
of optimal width).

First, by Theorem
6.4
, the length of the longest path in
T
is bounded by
P^{\prime}(g,tw(G))
and, as
tw(G)\leq T(g)
, it is bounded by
P^{\prime}(g,T(g))
.

Then, let’s show that
|V(G)|\leq 2^{Q(g)}
. By Theorem
1.4
, we know that
tw(G)\leq T(g)
. Hence, every bag in the tree decomposition of
G
is of order at most
T(g)+1
. Moreover, by Corollary
4.16
, we know that the maximum degree of
T
is at most
\Delta_{T}(g)
.

Hence,

\displaystyle|V(G)|

\displaystyle\leq(tw(G)+1)\times|V(T)|

\displaystyle\leq(tw(G)+1)\times(\Delta(T)^{P^{\prime}(g,T(g))})

\displaystyle\leq(T(g)+1)\times\Delta_{T}(g)^{P^{\prime}(g,T(g))}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{Q(g)}

∎

Corollary 6.6
.

There exists a constant
A
so that

pw(G)\leq R(g)

with
R(g)=A\times T(g)\times Q(g)
.

Proof.

The following inequality gives a relation between pathwidth and treewidth
[
5
]
:

pw(G)=O(tw(G)\log(|V(G)|))

Hence, there exists a constant
A
so that
pw(G)\leq Atw(G)\log(|V(G)|)
. Moreover, by Corollary
6.5
, we know that
|V(G)|\leq 2^{Q(g)}
. Finally, we deduce that

pw(G)\leq Atw(G)Q(g).

As
tw(G)\leq T(g)
by Theorem
1.4
,
Atw(G)Q(g)\leq A\times T(g)\times Q(g)=R(g)
.
∎

Corollary 6.7
.

There exists a constant
A^{\prime}
so that

|V(G)|\leq S(g)

with
S(g)=A^{\prime}\times P^{\prime}(g,R(g))\times R(g)
.

Proof.

Let
(P,(V_{p})_{p\in P})
be a minimal path decomposition of
G
of optimal width

.

Then, by Theorem
6.4
, the length of path
P
is bounded by
P^{\prime}(g,pw(G))
.

Moreover, by Corollary
6.6
,
pw(G)\leq R(g)
. We have

|V(G)|\leq P^{\prime}(g,pw(G))\times(pw(G)+1)\leq P^{\prime}(g,R(g))\times(R(g)+1).

Let

be a constant so that
P^{\prime}(g,R(g))\times(R(g)+1)\leq A^{\prime}\times P^{\prime}(g,R(g))\times R(g)=S(g)
. Then, finally,

.
∎

To conclude the proof, let’s recall Corollary

:

.

Suppose that, for any graph

and that is

.

7
Conclusion

The question of knowing whether
G
is bounded by a polynomial function or whether a quasi-polynomial bound is asymptotically the best possible bound remains open.

Open problem
.

Is there a polynomial bound on the order of
G
? Is a quasi-polynomial bound asymptotically the best possible one?

Our proof contains two main bottlenecks:

•

the
O(\log g)
bound for the number of
\Pi
-contractible well-nested cycles and
\Pi
-well-homotopic cycles, and

•

the
g^{O(\log^{2}g)}
bound for the maximum degree of
G
and for the maximum size of a face of
\Pi
.

As said in Subsection
5.2
, we believe that the
O(\log g)
bound is asymptotically the best possible for the number of
\Pi
-contractible well-nested cycles and
\Pi
-well-homotopic cycles. An interesting question would be to know whether it is possible to bound the maximum degree of
G
and the size of a face of
\Pi
by a polynomial in
g
instead of the quasi-polynomial bound
g^{O(\log^{2}g)}
.

Open problem
.

Is there a polynomial function
\Delta_{P}
of
g
such that

\Delta(G)\leq\Delta_{P}(g)
and
\Delta_{F}(G,\Pi)\leq\Delta_{P}(g)
?

It is a necessary condition to get a polynomial bound on the order of
G
. However, this would not be sufficient, combined with our proof, to prove that the order of
G
is bounded by a polynomial in
g
.

Appendix: Lower bound for the excluded minors for a surface

A tight lower bound for the excluded minors for a surface can be quickly obtained by using the known results on the genus of cliques. The lower bound
L(g)
for the excluded minors for a surface is therefore
L(g)=\left\lfloor\frac{7+\sqrt{1+24g}}{2}\right\rfloor+1=\Theta(\sqrt{g})
, as proved in Theorem
1.7
. We can remark that this lower bound is closely related to Heawood number
H(g)=\left\lfloor\frac{7+\sqrt{1+24g}}{2}\right\rfloor
, which is a tight upper bound on the chromatic number of graphs of genus at most
g

[
25
, Theorem 8.3.1]
. Indeed, we have
L(g)=H(g)+1
.

Proposition 7.1

(
[
25
, Theorems 4.4.5 and 4.4.6]
)
.

For
k\geq 3
,

g(K_{k})=\left\lceil\frac{(k-3)(k-4)}{6}\right\rceil

Lemma 7.2
.

Let
g
be an integer and let
L(g)=\min\{k\geq 3,g(K_{k})>g\}
. Then,

L(g)=\left\lfloor\frac{7+\sqrt{1+24g}}{2}\right\rfloor+1

Proof.

The result is obtained by solving the following quadratic equation in
k
:

g(K_{k})=\left\lceil\frac{(k-3)(k-4)}{6}\right\rceil>g

∎

Theorem
1.7
.

Let
S
be a surface of genus

. Let

be the function in Lemma

. There exists an excluded minor for

Proof.

By definition of

cannot be embedded in

for

Moreover, by definition of

, every clique

with

is a minor of

, no excluded minor for

is of order

.

Finally, we proved that there exists an excluded minor

for

with
|V(G)|=L(g)
and no excluded minor for

.
∎

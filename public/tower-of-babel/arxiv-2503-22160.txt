Structure of Carrollian (conformal) superalgebra
Yu-fan Zheng, Bin Chen

Abstract
In this work, we investigate possible supersymmetric extensions of the Carrollian algebra and the Carrollian conformal algebra in both d = 4 d=4 and d = 3 d=3 . For the super-Carrollian algebra in d = 4 d=4 , we identify multiple admissible structures, depending on the representations of the supercharges with respect to the Carrollian rotation. Some of these structures can be derived by taking the speed of light c → 0 c\to 0 limit from super-Poincaré algebra, but others are completely novel. In the conformal case, we demonstrate that the nontrivial Carrollian superconformal algebras for d = 4 d=4 and d = 3 d=3 are isomorphic to super-Poincaré algebra of d = 5 d=5 and the one of d = 4 d=4 respectively. Remarkably, neither of these constructions requires R-symmetry to ensure the algebraic closure. Furthermore, we discover two distinct classes of super-BMS 4 algebras, i.e. one being a singlet super-BMS 4 algebra and the other comprising of two multiplet chiral super-BMS 4 algebras. The singlet case arises from extending the 3 3 D Carrollian superconformal algebra, whereas the multiplet cases do not admit this pathology due to the fact that their finite-dimensional subalgebra contains supercharges with conformal dimension Δ = ± 3 2 \Delta=\pm\frac{3}{2} .

Carrollian and Carrollian conformal algebras

A generic Carrollian manifold
\mathscr{C}
is equipped with a degenerate metric
g_{\mu\nu}
and a time-directing null vector
\zeta^{\mu}
, which satisfy the orthogonal condition
g_{\mu\nu}\zeta^{\mu}=0
. The flat Carrollian space can be obtained by taking ultra-relativistic limit (
c\to 0
limit) from Minkowski space. Under this limit, the metric and null vector take the following forms:

\differential{s}^{2}=g_{\mu\nu}\differential{x}^{\mu}\differential{x}^{\nu}=0~\differential{t}^{2}+\differential{x}^{i}\differential{x}^{i},\quad\zeta=\partial_{t}.

(2.1)

The Carrollian symmetry is defined as the isometries that preserve both degenerate metric
g_{\mu\nu}
and the null vector
\zeta^{\mu}
. By solving the Killing equations, we identify three types of transformations constituting the Carrollian symmetry: the spacial rotations, the Carrollian boosts and the translations. Consequently, the Carrollian algebra is generated by
\{J^{ij},B^{i},P^{\mu}\}
with the following commutation relations

\displaystyle[J^{ij},J^{kl}]=\delta^{ik}J^{jl}-\delta^{il}J^{jk}+\delta^{jl}J^{ik}-\delta^{jk}J^{il},

(2.2)

\displaystyle[J^{ij},P^{k}]=\delta^{ik}P^{j}-\delta^{jk}P^{i},\quad[J^{ij},B^{k}]=\delta^{ik}B^{j}-\delta^{jk}B^{i},

\displaystyle[B^{i},P^{j}]=\delta^{ij}P^{0}.

The rotation part of Carrollian algebra, consisting of spatial rotations
J^{ij}
and Carrollian boosts
B^{i}
, is referred to as Carrollian rotations.

The conformal extension of Carrollian symmetry is obtained by relaxing the isometry to diffeomorphisms that preserve
g_{\mu\nu}
and
\zeta^{\mu}
invariant up to an overall scaling factor. The resulting Carrollian conformal algebra includes, in addition to the generators of the Carrollian algebra, the dilation
D
and special conformal transformations (SCTs)
K^{\mu}
. The commutation relations are given by

\displaystyle[D,P^{\mu}]=P^{\mu},\quad[D,K^{\mu}]=-K^{\mu},\quad[D,B^{i}]=[D,J^{ij}]=0,

(2.3)

\displaystyle[J^{ij},J^{kl}]=\delta^{ik}J^{jl}-\delta^{il}J^{jk}+\delta^{jl}J^{ik}-\delta^{jk}J^{il},

\displaystyle[J^{ij},G^{k}]=\delta^{ik}G^{j}-\delta^{jk}G^{i},\quad G\in\{P,K,B\},\quad[J^{ij},P^{0}]=[J^{ij},K^{0}]=0,

\displaystyle[B^{i},P^{j}]=\delta^{ij}P^{0},\quad[B^{i},K^{j}]=\delta^{ij}K^{0},\quad[B^{i},B^{j}]=[B^{i},P^{0}]=[B^{i},K^{0}]=0,

\displaystyle[K^{0},P^{0}]=0,\quad[K^{0},P^{i}]=-2B^{i},\quad[K^{i},P^{0}]=2B^{i},\quad[K^{i},P^{j}]=2\delta^{ij}D+2J^{ij}.

Remarkably, the
d
-dimensional Carrollian conformal algebra is isomorphic to
(d+1)
-dimensional Poincaré algebra
\mathfrak{cca}_{d}\equiv\mathfrak{iso}(1,d)\equiv\mathfrak{so}(1,d)\ltimes\mathbb{R}^{(d+1)}
. The dilation
D
, the spacial rotations
J^{ij}
, the spacial translation
P^{i}
, and the spacial SCT
K^{i}
span the rotation part
\mathfrak{so}(1,d)=\mbox{span}\{D,J^{ij},P^{i},K^{i}\}
, while the other part is given by Carrollian boosts
B^{i}
, the temporal translation
P^{0}
, and the temporal SCT
K^{0}

\mathbb{R}^{(d+1)}=\mbox{span}\{B^{i},P^{0},K^{0}\}
. This isomorphism plays a crucial role in the analysis of supersymmetric Carrollian conformal algebras.

Supersymmetry and superconformal symmetry

3.1
Supersymmetric Poincaré algebra

The Coleman–Mandula theorem and its supersymmetric generalization, the Haag–Łopuszański-Sohnius theorem, determine the structure of supersymmetry algebra, including the representation of supersymmetry generators under
\mathfrak{so}(1,3)
and their (anti-)commutation relations. Here we give a brief review, while omitting the discussion on the cases with central charges and extended supersymmetry.

The Coleman–Mandula theorem is a strong No-go theorem stating that the only symmetries for a
4

in
(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})

in

(0,0)

(j_{L},j_{R})

is the spin of
\mathfrak{so}_{L/R}(3)

and
(0,\frac{1}{2})

, to find a super-Lie algebra extension of a given algebra we should first specify the assumptions. Here we make ansatz on the representations of supercharges under Carrollian rotations as introduced in Section
2
, as well as (anti-)commutation relations among the generators of the algebra with undetermined coefficients. Then by solving the Jacobi identities for all combinations of the generators, we fix the coefficients to get the answer.

In Section
4.1
, we shall show that there are multiple self-consistent super-Carrollian algebras, based on different representations of
Q
’s with respect to the Carrollian rotation. However, in Section
4.2
we find that there is only one nontrivial Carrollian superconformal algebra, which is isomorphic to
5
D super-Poincaré algebra. This is what we expect: the
d
-dimensional Carrollian conformal algebra is the same as the
(d+1)
-dimensional Poincaré algebra, and so should their supersymmetric extension be. One surprising thing we find is that the Carrollian superconformal algebra does not require the R-symmetry, in contrast to the usual superconformal algebra. From perspective of taking the ultrarelativistic limit, the R-symmetry generator either decouples from
\{Q,S\}
anti-commutator or becomes a central charge, depending on how we rescale the R symmetry in taking the limit.

4.1
Carrollian supersymmetric algebra in
d=4

To find a self-contained super-Carrollian Lie algebra, we should make suitable ansatz on the supercharge
Q
. Firstly, we assume that the spins of supercharges under spacial rotation
\mathfrak{so}(3)
are half-integers, and they are in singlet representations or chain representations. Hence there are four situations: singlets, a rank-2 chain with same spin (called it 2-chain for simplicity), a raising chain with spin increasing, and a lowering chain with spin decreasing:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\mbox{singlet}\quad~~(j),\\
\end{aligned}

(4.1)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\mbox{2-chain}&&(j)\to(j),\\
&\mbox{raising}&&(j)\to(j+1)\to(j+2)\to\cdots,\\
&\mbox{lowering}&&(j+2)\to(j+1)\to(j)\to\cdots,&&j\in\mathbb{N}+\frac{1}{2}.\\
\end{aligned}

(4.2)

As discussed in Section
3.1
, the rest of ansatz is given by

[P^{\mu},Q_{a}]=0,\quad\{Q_{a},Q_{b}\}\propto P.

(4.3)

Although the tensor products of
Q
representations in the Carrollian case can generate same representation of
J
or
B
, i.e. the spin-
1
representations, we exclude
J
and
B
from the anti-commutation relations of
Q
:
\{Q,Q\}\not\supset J,B
. This restriction is motivated by the requirement that the super-Carrollian algebra should admit a natural extension to the Carrollian superconformal algebra. Crucially,
J
and
B
are of conformal dimension
1
, while
\{Q,Q\}
is of dimension
0
, so we have
\{Q,Q\}\not\supset J,B
.

For the reason explained in Section
3.1
, we do not forbid nonvanishing anti-commutation relation of supercharge with itself, i.e. we assume
\{Q_{a},Q_{a}\}\neq 0
in general. On the other hand, one can always get a solution by setting
\{Q_{a},Q_{b}\}=0
for all supercharges. We call the solution with vanishing
\{Q,Q\}
anti-commutation relations trivial, and call the solution with at least one nonvanishing
\{Q,Q\}
anti-commutation relation nontrivial.

In this work, we do not consider the extended super-Carrollian symmetry, however, we still want to investigate whether super-Carrollian symmetry has chirality. So we consider the case with two sets of supercharges as well.

By solving the Jacobi identities, we find that if the algebra contains one set of generators
Q
, only
Q
’s in 2-chain representations
Q\in(j)\to(j)
give rise to nontrivial solutions. The other cases only lead to trivial solutions. If the algebra contains two sets of generators
Q
, the solution could be nontrivial for certain pattern of two representations of
Q
’s. Especially, if both
Q_{1}\in(j_{1}),Q_{2}\in(j_{2})
are singlets, the solution is nontrivial for
j_{1}=j_{2}
. In the following, we discuss the results in detail.

To be more precise, we denote supercharges as
Q_{a,\lambda}^{j,s}
, where
a=1,2
labels the supercharge set,
\lambda
labels the order in the chain, and
j
and
s
label the
\mathfrak{so}(3)
spin and its third component respectively. For example,

\begin{matrix}\mbox{first chain representation: }&(1/2)&\to&(3/2)&\to&(5/2)\\
&\rotatebox{90.0}{$\in$}&&\rotatebox{90.0}{$\in$}&&\rotatebox{90.0}{$\in$}\\
\mbox{supercharge examples: }&Q_{1,1}^{\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}}&&Q_{1,2}^{\frac{3}{2},\frac{1}{2}}&&Q_{1,3}^{\frac{5}{2},-\frac{3}{2}}\\
\end{matrix}

(4.4)

In addition, we reorganize the bosonic generators in the raising and lowering ones:

\displaystyle J^{3}=iJ^{12},\quad J^{\pm 1}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(J^{13}\pm iJ^{23}),

\displaystyle B^{\pm 1}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\pm B^{1}+iB^{2}),

\displaystyle P^{\pm 1}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\pm P^{1}+iP^{2}).

(4.5)

4.1.1
Two types of the simplest super-Carrollian algebra

Here we discuss two types of the simplest super-Carrollian algebra. Each one could be reproduced by taking appropriate
c\to 0
limit from the super-Poincaé algebra.

Type 1

The first type contains two sets of
Q_{a}\in(j)
carrying the same singlet spin. The algebra contains
\{J^{i},B^{i},P^{0},P^{i},Q_{a}\}
, and the full commutation relations are

\displaystyle[J^{s_{1}},J^{s_{2}}]=-\sqrt{2}~\mbox{C}\smallmatrixquantity(\lx@physics@smallmatrix 1,s_1,1,s_2\\1,s_1+s_2\endlx@physics@smallmatrix)J^{s_{1}+s_{2}},\quad[J^{s_{1}},B^{s_{2}}]=-\sqrt{2}~\mbox{C}\smallmatrixquantity(\lx@physics@smallmatrix 1,s_1;1,s_2\\1,s_1+s_2\endlx@physics@smallmatrix)B^{s_{1}+s_{2}},

(4.6)

\displaystyle[J^{s_{1}},P^{s_{2}}]=-\sqrt{2}~\mbox{C}\smallmatrixquantity(\lx@physics@smallmatrix 1,s_1;1,s_2\\1,s_1+s_2\endlx@physics@smallmatrix)P^{s_{1}+s_{2}},\quad[B^{s_{1}},P^{s_{2}}]=\sqrt{3}~\mbox{C}\smallmatrixquantity(\lx@physics@smallmatrix 1,s_1;1,s_2\\0,s_1+s_2\endlx@physics@smallmatrix)P^{0},

\displaystyle[J^{s_{1}},Q_{a}^{j,s_{2}}]=\sqrt{j(j+1)}~\mbox{C}\smallmatrixquantity(\lx@physics@smallmatrix 1,s_1;j,s_2\\j,s_1+s_2\endlx@physics@smallmatrix)Q_{a}^{j,s_{1}+s_{2}},\quad[B^{s_{1}},Q_{a}^{j,s_{2}}]=0,

\displaystyle\{Q_{1}^{j,s_{1}},Q_{2}^{j,s_{2}}\}=\mbox{C}\smallmatrixquantity(\lx@physics@smallmatrix j,s_1;j,s_2\\0,s_1+s_2\endlx@physics@smallmatrix)P^{0}.

In the above,
\mbox{C}\smallmatrixquantity(\lx@physics@smallmatrix j_1,s_1;j_2,s_2\\j,s\endlx@physics@smallmatrix)
is the usual Clebsch-Gordan coefficients:

\ket{j_1,s_1}\ket{j_2,s_2}=\mbox{C}\smallmatrixquantity(\lx@physics@smallmatrix j_1,s_1;j_2,s_2\\j,s\endlx@physics@smallmatrix)\ket{j,s}.

(4.7)

The solution (
4.6
) is valid for general
j\in\mathbb{Z}+1/2
. Especially, for
j=\frac{1}{2}
the anti-commutation relations of the supercharges are

\{Q_{1},Q_{2}\}=\matrixquantity(\lx@physics@matrix
0 & \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} P^0\\
-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} P^0 & 0\\\endlx@physics@matrix),\qquad\{Q_{1},Q_{1}\}=\{Q_{2},Q_{2}\}=0.

(4.8)

For general
j
, the anti-commutation relations can be written as

\{Q_{a},Q_{b}\}=\matrixquantity(\lx@physics@matrix
0 & P^0 \mathbf{m}\\
P^0 \mathbf{m}^{\mbox{\small T}}& 0\\\endlx@physics@matrix)=\matrixquantity(\lx@physics@matrix
0 & P^0 \mathbf{m}\\
- P^0 \mathbf{m}& 0\\\endlx@physics@matrix),

(4.9)

where the matrix
\mathbf{m}
is given in term of the Clebsch-Gordan coefficients, and
\mathbf{m}^{\mbox{\small T}}=-\mathbf{m}
.

One may wonder whether there is equivalent structure of anti-commutators by re-organizing the supercharges. The answer is no. To keep the supercharges well-behaved under rotations, the re-organization of the supercharges can be given by

\tilde{Q}_{a}=\mathbf{M}_{ab}~Q_{b},

(4.10)

where
\mathbf{M}\in SU(2)
is a
2
-dimensional matrix. Noticing the fact that the
\mathfrak{su}(2)
generators, Pauli matrices
\sigma^{i}_{ab}
, satisfy

\{\tilde{Q}_{a},\tilde{Q}_{b}\}=\sigma^{i}_{ac}~\sigma^{i}_{bd}~\matrixquantity(\lx@physics@matrix 0&P^0 \mathbf{m}\\-P^0 \mathbf{m}&0\endlx@physics@matrix)_{cd}~\propto~\matrixquantity(\lx@physics@matrix 0&P^0 \mathbf{m}\\-P^0 \mathbf{m}&0\endlx@physics@matrix)_{ab},

(4.11)

we see that under
SU(2)
transformation, the anti-commutation relation is invariant up to an overall factor, which can be absorbed in rescaling the supercharges. Therefore, (
4.6
) is the only algebraic structure for two sets of
Q_{a}\in(j)
.

In fact, the relations (
4.6
) can be obtained by taking suitable limit from
4
-dimensional super-Poincaré algebra. For the bosonic part of the algebra, the limit is taken by first redefining the generators

J^{ij}_{\mbox{\tiny C}}=J^{ij}_{\mbox{\tiny P}},\qquad B^{i}_{\mbox{\tiny C}}=cJ^{0i}_{\mbox{\tiny P}},\qquad P^{0}_{\mbox{\tiny C}}=cP^{0}_{\mbox{\tiny P}},\qquad P^{i}_{\mbox{\tiny C}}=P^{i}_{\mbox{\tiny P}},

(4.12)

where
c
is the speed of light, and the subscripts
C
and
P
label the generators in the Carrollian algebra or Poincaé algebra. Then by taking
c\to 0
limit in the commutation relations of Poincaré algebra, we reproduce the Carrollian algebra. For the fermionic generators, we may redefine the super-Poincaré generators
Q^{\mbox{\tiny P}},\bar{Q}_{\mbox{\tiny P}}
by

Q^{\mbox{\tiny C}}_{1}=2^{-\frac{3}{4}}c^{a}(Q^{\mbox{\tiny P}}_{2},Q^{\mbox{\tiny P}}_{1}),\qquad Q^{\mbox{\tiny C}}_{2}=2^{-\frac{3}{4}}c^{1-a}(-\bar{Q}_{\mbox{\tiny P}}^{\dot{1}},\bar{Q}_{\mbox{\tiny P}}^{\dot{2}})

(4.13)

for constant
a
. Taking
c\to 0
limit with Carrollian supercharge
Q=\lim_{c\to 0}Q^{\mbox{\tiny C}}
from super-Poincaré algebra, we find (
4.6
) with
j=\frac{1}{2}
. Comparing with the super-Lie algebra in the Poincaé case, we find that (
4.6
) can not be treated as an extended supersymmetry, even though the two supercharges have
SU(2)
symmetry. In fact,
Q_{1}
and
Q_{2}
should be interpreted as left- and right-hand part of the supersymmetry. Taking the limit with the rescaling (
4.13
) preserves the chirality of the algebra.

Type 2

The second type contains one set of
Q_{1}
in chain representation
(j)\to(j)
. The algebra contains the generators
\{J^{i},B^{i},P^{0},P^{i},Q_{1}\}
. The commutation relations of bosonic generators are the same with those in (
4.6
), and the commutation relations involving supercharges are

\displaystyle[J^{s_{1}},Q_{1,\lambda}^{j,s_{2}}]=\sqrt{j(j+1)}~\mbox{C}\smallmatrixquantity(\lx@physics@smallmatrix 1,s_1;j,s_2\\j,s_1+s_2\endlx@physics@smallmatrix)Q_{1,\lambda}^{j,s_{1}+s_{2}},\quad[B^{s_{1}},Q_{1,1}^{j,s_{2}}]=\mbox{C}\smallmatrixquantity(\lx@physics@smallmatrix 1,s_1;1,s_2\\j,s_1+s_2\endlx@physics@smallmatrix)Q_{1,2}^{j,s_{1}+s_{2}},

(4.14)

\displaystyle\{Q_{1,1}^{j,s_{1}},Q_{1,1}^{j,s_{2}}\}=\mbox{C}\smallmatrixquantity(\lx@physics@smallmatrix j,s_1;j,s_2\\1,s_1+s_2\endlx@physics@smallmatrix)P^{s_{1}+s_{2}},\quad\{Q_{1,1}^{j,s_{1}},Q_{1,2}^{j,s_{2}}\}=\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}~\mbox{C}\smallmatrixquantity(\lx@physics@smallmatrix j,s_1;j,s_2\\0,s_1+s_2\endlx@physics@smallmatrix)P^{0}.

This result is also valid for generic
j
. Explicitly for
j=\frac{1}{2}
, the anti-commutation relation of the supercharges are

\{Q_{1,1},Q_{1,1}\}=\matrixquantity(\lx@physics@matrix
P^{+1} & \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} P^3 \\
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} P^3 & P^{-1}\\\endlx@physics@matrix),\qquad\{Q_{1,1},Q_{1,2}\}=\matrixquantity(\lx@physics@matrix
0 & \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}} P^0\\
-\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}} P^0 & 0\\\endlx@physics@matrix).

(4.15)

The supercharge can be re-organized by keeping the representation under Carrollian rotations invariant

\matrixquantity{\lx@physics@matrix(j)_1 \\ \downarrow\\ (j)_2\endlx@physics@matrix}\quad\Rightarrow\quad\matrixquantity{\lx@physics@matrix(j)_1 + a(j)_2 \\ \downarrow\\ (j)_2\endlx@physics@matrix}

(4.16)

for
a

4.15
) is the only valid algebraic structure for a single

The solution (
4.15
) can also be found by taking the ultrarelativistic limit. We should rescale the supercharges differently this time

Q^{\mbox{\tiny C}}_{1,1}=2^{-\frac{5}{4}}c^{0}(Q^{\mbox{\tiny P}}_{2}+\bar{Q}_{\mbox{\tiny P}}^{\dot{1}},Q^{\mbox{\tiny P}}_{1}-\bar{Q}_{\mbox{\tiny P}}^{\dot{2}}),\qquad Q^{\mbox{\tiny C}}_{1,2}=3^{\frac{1}{2}}2^{-\frac{9}{4}}c~(Q^{\mbox{\tiny P}}_{2}-\bar{Q}_{\mbox{\tiny P}}^{\dot{1}},Q^{\mbox{\tiny P}}_{1}+\bar{Q}_{\mbox{\tiny P}}^{\dot{2}}).

(4.17)

Taking
c\to 0
limit and using the above rescaling lead to (
4.15
). In this case, there is only one set of supercharge, and there is no left-right relation. Thus taking the limit breaks the chirality.

Taking a limit from the super-Poincaré algebra can generically result in trivial solutions with
\{Q,Q\}=0
. For instance, rescaling

and
Q_{2}

\displaystyle\{Q_{1}^{\frac{1}{2},s_{1}},Q_{2}^{\frac{1}{2},s_{2}}\}=\mbox{C}\smallmatrixquantity(\lx@physics@smallmatrix\frac{1}{2},s_1;\frac{1}{2},s_2\\0,s_1+s_2\endlx@physics@smallmatrix)P^{0},\quad\{S_{1}^{\frac{1}{2},s_{1}},S_{2}^{\frac{1}{2},s_{2}}\}=\mbox{C}\smallmatrixquantity(\lx@physics@smallmatrix\frac{1}{2},s_1;\frac{1}{2},s_2\\0,s_1+s_2\endlx@physics@smallmatrix)K^{0},

\displaystyle\{Q_{1}^{\frac{1}{2},s_{1}},S_{2}^{\frac{1}{2},s_{2}}\}=\mbox{C}\smallmatrixquantity(\lx@physics@smallmatrix\frac{1}{2},s_1;\frac{1}{2},s_2\\1,s_1+s_2\endlx@physics@smallmatrix)B^{s_{1}+s_{2}}.

Together with the commutators of bosonic generators (
2.3
), it gives the full algebra structure of
4
D Carrollian superconformal algebra. More explicitly, the anti-commutators of the supercharges are:

\displaystyle\{Q_{1},Q_{2}\}=\matrixquantity(\lx@physics@matrix 0 & \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} P^0\\-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} P^0 & 0\\\endlx@physics@matrix),\quad

\displaystyle\{S_{1},S_{2}\}=\matrixquantity(\lx@physics@matrix 0 & \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} K^0\\-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} K^0 & 0\\\endlx@physics@matrix),

(4.26)

\displaystyle\{Q_{a},S_{b}\}=\delta_{ab}\matrixquantity(\lx@physics@matrix B^{+1} & \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} B^3\\\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} B^3 & B^{-1}\\\endlx@physics@matrix).

This result is surprising, since it shows that the Carrollian superconformal algebra does not need R-symmetry, very different from the usual superconformal algebra. On the other hand, it is reasonable because the
d
-dimensional Carrollian conformal algebra is isomorphic to
(d+1)
-dimensional Poincaré algebra, and its supersymmetric generalization is nothing but super-Poincaré algebra. Indeed, by re-organizing the bosonic generators as

\displaystyle J_{\mbox{\tiny P}}^{01}=D,\quad J_{\mbox{\tiny P}}^{02}=\frac{i}{2}(K^{2}+P^{2}),\quad J_{\mbox{\tiny P}}^{03}=\frac{i}{2}(K^{1}+P^{1}),\quad J_{\mbox{\tiny P}}^{04}=-\frac{i}{2}(K^{3}+P^{3}),

(4.27)

\displaystyle J_{\mbox{\tiny P}}^{12}=\frac{i}{2}(K^{2}-P^{2}),\quad J_{\mbox{\tiny P}}^{13}=\frac{i}{2}(K^{1}-P^{1}),\quad J_{\mbox{\tiny P}}^{14}=-\frac{i}{2}(K^{3}-P^{3}),\quad

\displaystyle J_{\mbox{\tiny P}}^{23}=-J^{12},\quad J_{\mbox{\tiny P}}^{24}=-J^{23},\quad J_{\mbox{\tiny P}}^{34}=-J^{13},

\displaystyle P_{\mbox{\tiny P}}^{0}=\frac{i}{\sqrt{2}}(K^{0}+P^{0}),\quad P_{\mbox{\tiny P}}^{1}=\frac{i}{\sqrt{2}}(K^{0}-P^{0}),

\displaystyle P_{\mbox{\tiny P}}^{2}=\sqrt{2}B^{2},\quad P_{\mbox{\tiny P}}^{3}=\sqrt{2}B^{1},\quad P_{\mbox{\tiny P}}^{4}=-\sqrt{2}B^{3},

and re-organizing the fermionic generators as

\displaystyle Q^{\mbox{\tiny P}}=\sqrt{2}\matrixquantity(\lx@physics@matrix
-Q_{1,1}^{\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}} + i S_{2,1}^{\frac{1}{2},-\frac{1}{2}}\\[0.5em]
-Q_{1,1}^{\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}} - i S_{2,1}^{\frac{1}{2},-\frac{1}{2}}\\[0.5em]
-iQ_{1,1}^{\frac{1}{2},-\frac{1}{2}} + S_{2,1}^{\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}}\\[0.5em]
i Q_{1,1}^{\frac{1}{2},-\frac{1}{2}} + S_{2,1}^{\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}}\\
\endlx@physics@matrix),\qquad\bar{Q}^{\mbox{\tiny P}}=\sqrt{2}\matrixquantity(\lx@physics@matrix
Q_{2,1}^{\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}} - i S_{1,1}^{\frac{1}{2},-\frac{1}{2}}\\[0.5em]
-Q_{2,1}^{\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}} - i S_{1,1}^{\frac{1}{2},-\frac{1}{2}}\\[0.5em]
-iQ_{2,1}^{\frac{1}{2},-\frac{1}{2}} + S_{1,1}^{\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}}\\[0.5em]
-iQ_{2,1}^{\frac{1}{2},-\frac{1}{2}} - S_{1,1}^{\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}}\\
\endlx@physics@matrix),

(4.28)

we reproduce the
5
D super-Poincaré algebra. In particular, the anti-commutator of the supercharge is

\displaystyle\{Q^{\mbox{\tiny P}},\bar{Q}^{\mbox{\tiny P}}\}=2\mathbf{\Gamma}^{(1,4)}_{A}P_{\mbox{\tiny P}}^{A},

(4.29)

where
A=0,...,4
, the subscript
P
denotes Poincaré, and
\mathbf{\Gamma}^{(1,4)}
are the Dirac Gamma matrices in
5
D Minkowski space,

\displaystyle\mathbf{\Gamma}^{(1,4)}_{0}=\matrixquantity(\lx@physics@matrix 0&\sigma^0\\\sigma^0 &0\\\endlx@physics@matrix),\qquad\mathbf{\Gamma}^{(1,4)}_{i}=\matrixquantity(\lx@physics@matrix 0&-\sigma^i\\\sigma^i &0\\\endlx@physics@matrix),

(4.30)

\displaystyle\mathbf{\Gamma}^{(1,4)}_{4}=\mathbf{\Gamma}^{(1,4)}_{0}\mathbf{\Gamma}^{(1,4)}_{1}\mathbf{\Gamma}^{(1,4)}_{2}\mathbf{\Gamma}^{(1,4)}_{3}=\matrixquantity(\lx@physics@matrix-i\sigma^0 &0\\0 &i\sigma^0 \\\endlx@physics@matrix),

and our convention of Pauli matrices are given by

\sigma^{0}=\matrixquantity(\lx@physics@matrix 1&0\\0&1\\\endlx@physics@matrix),\quad\sigma^{1}=\matrixquantity(\lx@physics@matrix 0&1\\1&0\\\endlx@physics@matrix),\quad\sigma^{2}=\matrixquantity(\lx@physics@matrix 0&-i\\i&0\\\endlx@physics@matrix),\quad\sigma^{3}=\matrixquantity(\lx@physics@matrix 1&0\\0&-1\\\endlx@physics@matrix).

(4.31)

Form the structure of (
4.24
), one may suspect that there exist
SU(2)
symmetry between
Q_{1}
and
Q_{2}
, as well as between
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
. This is true. However, as will be explained in Section
4.2.2
shortly, this
SU(2)
symmetry is just an outer automorphism, and does not appear in
\{Q_{a},S_{b}\}
anti-commutation relations. More precisely, the algebra here is similar to the case of (
4.6
), where the
Q_{1}
and
Q_{2}
should be viewed as left- and right-hand sectors of the superalgebra, and the
SU(2)
is purely a redefinition of left- and right-hand sectors. Denoting the generators as
T^{i}
with
i=1,2,3
, the supercharges transform under
SU(2)
as

[T^{i},Q_{a}]=\sigma^{i}_{ab}Q_{b},\qquad[T^{i},S_{a}]=\sigma^{i}_{ab}S_{b}.

(4.32)

From the point of view of the
5
D super-Poincaré algebra , the representations are organized using Cartan subalgebra
\{J^{01}_{\mbox{\tiny P}},J^{23}_{\mbox{\tiny P}}\}
. The
SU(2)=SO(3)
transformation is equivalent to rotating
2,3,4
directions, exchanging
J^{23}_{\mbox{\tiny P}}
to another rotation in the Cartan subalgebra, as well as interchange
Q^{\mbox{\tiny P}}
and
\bar{Q}^{\mbox{\tiny P}}
. Under this symmetry, the super-Lie algebra is equivalent up to the corresponding change of Cartan subalgebra.

To conclude, the nontrivial Carrollian superconformal algebra in
4
D is isomorphic to super-Poincaré algebra in
5
D.

4.2.2
Comments on R-symmetry

As mentioned in Section
3.2
, the superconformal algebra is closed only by including the R-symmetry. Introducing R-symmetry in the discussion of Carrollian case, we find no fundamental change to the structure of the algebra of (
4.22
), (
4.23
) and (
4.24
). More explicitly, we change the ansatz in such a way that
Q_{a}
and
S_{a}
carry some R charges, and their anti-commutators contains R-symmetry generators
R

\displaystyle[R,Q_{a}]=r_{Q_{a}}Q_{a},\qquad[R,S_{a}]=r_{S_{a}}S_{a},

(4.33)

\displaystyle\{Q_{1},S_{1}\}=\#D+\#J+\#B+\#R.

In the case (
4.22
),
R
generates
U_{R}(1)
symmetry. In a well-defined super-Lie algebra,
Q_{1}
and
S_{1}
carry the same R charges
r_{Q_{1}}=r_{S_{1}}=r
, while their anti-commutation relations are vanishing

[R,Q_{1}]=rQ_{1},\qquad[R,S_{1}]=rS_{1},\qquad\{Q_{1},Q_{1}\}=\{S_{1},S_{1}\}=\{Q_{1},S_{1}\}=0.

(4.34)

Hence, this structure is trivial.

In the case of (
4.23
),
R
generates
U_{R}(1)
as well. There are two kinds of structures. The first one is trivial, similar to the case of (
4.22
): the R charges are the same
r_{Q_{1}}=r_{S_{1}}=r
, and
R
does not appear in the anti-commutators of the supercharges

[R,Q_{a}]=rQ_{a},\quad[R,S_{a}]=rS_{a},\quad\{Q_{a},Q_{b}\}=\{S_{a},S_{b}\}=\{Q_{a},S_{b}\}=0.

(4.35)

The other one is that R charges are vanishing
r_{Q_{1}}=r_{S_{1}}=0
, while the anti-commutation relations are nonvanishing:

\{Q_{1},Q_{1}\}=\{S_{1},S_{1}\}=0,\qquad\{Q_{1},S_{1}\}\supset R.

(4.36)

In this, the R-symmetry commutes with all other generators, and can be viewed as a central charge.

In the case of (
4.24
), R-symmetry is
U_{R}(1)\otimes SU_{R}(2)
, and we denote the corresponding generators as
R
and
T^{i}
, respectively. Since
[R,T^{i}]=0
, we can discuss them separately. For the
U_{R}(1)
part, the situation is similar to the case of (
4.23
): either
R
is a trivial
U(1)
symmetry with

\displaystyle[R,Q_{a}]=r\epsilon_{ab}Q_{b},\quad[R,S_{a}]=-r\epsilon_{ab}S_{b},

(4.37)

\displaystyle\{Q_{a},Q_{b}\}\not\supset R\quad\{S_{a},S_{b}\}\not\supset R,\quad\{Q_{a},S_{b}\}\not\supset R.

and the anti-commutation relations of supercharges are the same as (
4.26
); or
R
is a central charge with

\{Q_{a},S_{b}\}\supset R,\quad[R,G]=0,~\forall G.

(4.38)

For the
SU_{R}(2)
part, it is quite reasonable to assume (
4.32
) being valid. Solving the Jacobi identities with the ansatz
\{Q_{a},S_{b}\}=\#D+\#J+\#B+\#T
, we find that
T^{i}
’s do not appear in the anti-commutators. Thus this
SU(2)
symmetry is just an outer automorphism. Moreover,
T^{i}
’s do not appear in
\{Q_{a},S_{b}\}
even assuming
[T^{i},Q_{a}]=[T^{i},S_{a}]=0
.

We also investigated the cases that the supercharges are in other chain representations with different spin
j
, but found no more structure even after introducing R-symmetry. So the structure of Carrollian superconformal algebra in
4
D is determined uniquely and can not be modified by introducing R-symmetry.

4.2.3
Nontrivial algebra from taking ultrarelativistic limit

The algebra (
4.25
) can also be found by taking the ultrarelativistic limit from superconformal algebra with a rescaling similar to (
4.13
). To be precise, the rescaling of the supercharges are

\displaystyle Q^{\mbox{\tiny C}}_{1}=2^{-\frac{3}{4}}c^{a}(Q^{\mbox{\tiny P}}_{2},Q^{\mbox{\tiny P}}_{1}),

\displaystyle Q^{\mbox{\tiny C}}_{2}=2^{-\frac{3}{4}}c^{1-a}(-\bar{Q}_{\mbox{\tiny P}}^{\dot{1}},\bar{Q}_{\mbox{\tiny P}}^{\dot{2}}),

(4.39)

\displaystyle S^{\mbox{\tiny C}}_{1}=-2^{-\frac{3}{4}}c^{a}(S^{\mbox{\tiny P}}_{2},S^{\mbox{\tiny P}}_{1}),

\displaystyle S^{\mbox{\tiny C}}_{2}=-2^{-\frac{3}{4}}c^{1-a}(-\bar{S}_{\mbox{\tiny P}}^{\dot{1}},\bar{S}_{\mbox{\tiny P}}^{\dot{2}}),

with constant
a
. Taking
c\to 0
limit with
Q=\lim_{c\to 0}Q^{\mbox{\tiny C}}
, we see that the superconformal algebra then produce (
4.25
). Especially, the
U_{R}(1)
R-symmetry is rescaled as

R^{\mbox{\tiny P}}=c^{b}R^{\mbox{\tiny P}}.

(4.40)

To find a non-divergent result, the constant
b
satisfy
0\leq b\leq 1
. There are three scenarios: for
b=0
, the R-symmetry becomes a trivial
U(1)
symmetry as in (
4.37
); for
b=1
, the R-symmetry becomes a central charge shown in
\{Q,S\}
with
[R,G]=0
for any generator
G
in the algebra; for the case that
0<b<1
, the R-symmetry totally decouple in the sense that
R
commutes with any other generator
[R,G]=0
, as well as not appearing in any commutator. This explains the two kinds of structures of R-symmetry found in the last subsection.

5
Carrollian superconformal symmetry in
d=3

In this section, we discuss the
3
-dimensional case. As introduced in Section
2
, the representation of
3
-dimensional Carrollian rotations is less restricted. We will not dive into the nonconformal case since we do not have preferred ansatz on the representations. On the contrary, our focus is turned to supersymmetric extension of
3
D Carrollian conformal algebra, and its relation to BMS algebra.

In Section
5.1
we shall show that the only structure of
3
D Carrollian superconformal algebra is the one isomorphic to
4
D super-Poincaré algebra. Besides, there is no R-symmetry needed in the algebra. In Section
5.2
, we further extend this algebra to singlet super BMS
4
algebra. In Section
5.3
, we demonstrate multiplet super-BMS
4
algebras which can not be obtained by extending Carrollian superconformal algebra. In each case, we further discuss the Hermitian conjugation conditions.

5.1
Carrollian superconformal algebra in
d=3

As mentioned in Section
2
, the
3
-dimensional Carrollian conformal algebra is isomorphic to
4
D Poincaré algebra
\mathfrak{cca}_{3}=\mathfrak{iso}(1,3)
. By the Haag–Łopuszański–Sohnius theorem, the super-Lie algebra with nonvanishing
\{Q,Q\}
anti-commutators is given by
Q_{\mbox{\tiny P}}
and
\bar{Q}_{\mbox{\tiny P}}
, with commutation relation given by (
3.1
). The
3
-dimensional Carrollian superconformal algebra has generators
\{D,J^{12},B^{i},P^{\mu},K^{\mu},Q_{s},S_{s}\}
, with
\mu=0,1,2
,
i=1,2
and spin
s=\pm\frac{1}{2}
, which can be related to the generators of the
4
-dimensional super Poincaré algebra by the following redefinitions:

\displaystyle D=J^{03}_{\mbox{\tiny P}},\quad J^{12}=J^{12}_{\mbox{\tiny P}},\quad B^{1}=-i\sqrt{2}P^{2}_{\mbox{\tiny P}},\quad B^{2}=i\sqrt{2}P^{1}_{\mbox{\tiny P}},

(5.1)

\displaystyle P^{0}=\sqrt{2}(P^{0}_{\mbox{\tiny P}}-P^{3}_{\mbox{\tiny P}}),P^{1}=i(J^{02}_{\mbox{\tiny P}}+J^{23}_{\mbox{\tiny P}}),\quad P^{2}=-i(J^{01}_{\mbox{\tiny P}}+J^{13}_{\mbox{\tiny P}}),

\displaystyle K^{0}=\sqrt{2}(P^{0}_{\mbox{\tiny P}}+P^{3}_{\mbox{\tiny P}}),K^{1}=i(J^{02}_{\mbox{\tiny P}}-J^{23}_{\mbox{\tiny P}}),\quad K^{2}=i(-J^{01}_{\mbox{\tiny P}}+J^{13}_{\mbox{\tiny P}}),

\displaystyle Q_{\frac{1}{2}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}Q^{\mbox{\tiny P}}_{2},\quad Q_{-\frac{1}{2}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\bar{Q}^{\dot{2}}_{\mbox{\tiny P}},\quad S_{\frac{1}{2}}=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\bar{Q}^{\dot{1}}_{\mbox{\tiny P}},\quad S_{-\frac{1}{2}}=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}Q^{\mbox{\tiny P}}_{1}.

Especially, the commutators of the bosonic generators are the same with (
2.3
), while the commutators involving supercharges are

\displaystyle[D,Q_{s}]=\frac{1}{2}Q_{s},\quad[D,S_{s}]=\frac{1}{2}S_{s},\quad[J^{12},Q_{s}]=-isQ_{s},\quad[J^{12},S_{s}]=-isS_{s},

(5.2)

\displaystyle[P^{1},S_{s}]=\matrixquantity(\lx@physics@matrix-Q_{-\frac{1}{2}}\\Q_{\frac{1}{2}}\endlx@physics@matrix),\quad[P^{2},S_{s}]=\matrixquantity(\lx@physics@matrix-iQ_{-\frac{1}{2}}\\-iQ_{\frac{1}{2}}\endlx@physics@matrix),

\displaystyle[K^{1},Q_{s}]=\matrixquantity(\lx@physics@matrix-S_{-\frac{1}{2}}\\S_{\frac{1}{2}}\endlx@physics@matrix),\quad[K^{2},Q_{s}]=\matrixquantity(\lx@physics@matrix-iS_{-\frac{1}{2}}\\-iS_{\frac{1}{2}}\endlx@physics@matrix),

\displaystyle\{Q_{s_{1}},Q_{s_{2}}\}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\matrixquantity(\lx@physics@matrix 0&P^0\\P^0&0\endlx@physics@matrix),\quad\{S_{s_{1}},S_{s_{2}}\}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\matrixquantity(\lx@physics@matrix 0&K^0\\K^0&0\endlx@physics@matrix),

\displaystyle\{Q_{s_{1}},S_{s_{2}}\}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\matrixquantity(\lx@physics@matrix B^1+iB^2 & 0\\ 0 & -B^1+iB^2\endlx@physics@matrix).

It is interesting to point out that this algebra can also be found by taking
c\to 0
limit from
3
D superconformal algebra.

Moreover, let us check if the introduction of the R-symmetry may make a difference. For this algebra, the supercharge has just one copy, and the R-symmetry is
U_{R}(1)
. Assuming

[R,Q_{s}]\propto Q_{s},\quad[R,S_{s}]\propto S_{s},\quad\{Q_{s_{1}},Q_{s_{2}}\}\supset R,\quad\{S_{s_{1}},S_{s_{2}}\}\supset R,\quad\{Q_{s_{1}},S_{s_{2}}\}\supset R,

(5.3)

we can solve the Jacobi identities. The only solution is that
R
not appearing in anti-commutation relations

\{Q_{s_{1}},Q_{s_{2}}\}\not\supset R,\quad\{S_{s_{1}},S_{s_{2}}\}\not\supset R,\quad\{Q_{s_{1}},S_{s_{2}}\}\not\supset R,

(5.4)

and the fermionic generators have R charges:

[R,Q_{\frac{1}{2}}]=rQ_{\frac{1}{2}},\quad[R,Q_{-\frac{1}{2}}]=-rQ_{-\frac{1}{2}},\quad[R,S_{\frac{1}{2}}]=-rS_{\frac{1}{2}},\quad[R,S_{-\frac{1}{2}}]=rS_{-\frac{1}{2}}.

(5.5)

It is worth mentioning that there is no
SU(2)
outer automorphism in 3D nontrivial Carrollian superconformal algebra (
5.2
), which is different from the
4
D case discussed in Section
4.2.2
. An outer automorphism of fermionic part of the algebra is nothing but a map between equivalent representations of the conformal Carrollian algebra. Contrary to the case in Section
4.2.2
,
J^{12}_{\mbox{\tiny P}}
is the only Lorentzian rotation commuting with
J^{03}_{\mbox{\tiny P}}
. Therefore the requirement that
D=J^{03}_{\mbox{\tiny P}}
being diagonal in the representation leads to the unique choice of Cartan subalgebra to be
\{D=J^{03}_{\mbox{\tiny P}},J^{12}=J^{12}_{\mbox{\tiny P}}\}
. Thus the outer automorphism in 3D case can only be a
U(1)
symmetry, which is precisely the
R
generator in (
5.5
).

5.2
Singlet super-BMS
4
algebra

What makes Carrollian conformal algebra in
3
D special is that it admit infinite extensions to BMS
4
algebra. In fact, the above super-Lie algebra also admits infinite extension. The BMS
4
generators are identified with Carrollian conformal generators as

\displaystyle L_{1}=\frac{1}{2}(K^{1}-iK^{2}),\quad L_{0}=\frac{1}{2}(D+iJ^{12}),\quad L_{-1}=\frac{1}{2}(P^{1}+iP^{2}),

(5.6)

\displaystyle\bar{L}_{1}=\frac{1}{2}(-K^{1}-iK^{2}),\quad\bar{L}_{0}=\frac{1}{2}(D-iJ^{12}),\quad\bar{L}_{-1}=\frac{1}{2}(-P^{1}+iP^{2}),

\displaystyle M_{\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}K^{0},\quad M_{\frac{1}{2},-\frac{1}{2}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(B^{1}-iB^{2}),

\displaystyle M_{-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(-B^{1}-iB^{2}),\quad M_{-\frac{1}{2},-\frac{1}{2}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}P^{0}.

Then the BMS
4
algebra is given by

\displaystyle[L_{m},L_{n}]=(m-n)L_{m+n},\quad[\bar{L}_{\bar{m}},\bar{L}_{\bar{n}}]=(m-n)\bar{L}_{\bar{m}+\bar{n}},

(5.7)

\displaystyle[L_{m},M_{r,\bar{r}}]=\left(\frac{m}{2}-r\right)M_{m+r,\bar{r}},\quad[\bar{L}_{\bar{m}},M_{r,\bar{r}}]=\left(\frac{\bar{m}}{2}-\bar{r}\right)M_{r,\bar{m}+\bar{r}},

and is extended to
m,\bar{m}\in\mathbb{Z},r,\bar{r}\in\mathbb{Z}+\frac{1}{2}
. The fermionic generators in super-extended BMS
4
algebra are denoted by curly
\mathcal{Q}
, and their relations to Carrollian superconformal generators are

\mathcal{Q}_{\frac{1}{2}}=-\sqrt{2}S_{-\frac{1}{2}},\quad\mathcal{Q}_{-\frac{1}{2}}=\sqrt{2}Q_{\frac{1}{2}},\quad\bar{\mathcal{Q}}_{\frac{1}{2}}=-\sqrt{2}S_{\frac{1}{2}},\quad\bar{\mathcal{Q}}_{-\frac{1}{2}}=\sqrt{2}Q_{-\frac{1}{2}},

(5.8)

and the generators can be extended to
\mathcal{Q}_{r}
and
\bar{\mathcal{Q}}_{\bar{r}}
with
r,\bar{r}\in\mathbb{Z}+\frac{1}{2}
. The commutators involving the fermionic generators are

[L_{m},\mathcal{Q}_{r}]=\left(\frac{m}{2}-r\right)\mathcal{Q}_{m+r},\quad[\bar{L}_{\bar{m}},\bar{\mathcal{Q}}_{\bar{r}}]=\left(\frac{\bar{m}}{2}-\bar{r}\right)\bar{\mathcal{Q}}_{\bar{m}+\bar{r}},\quad\{\mathcal{Q}_{r},\bar{\mathcal{Q}}_{\bar{r}}\}=M_{r,\bar{r}}.

(5.9)

Together, (
5.7
) and (
5.9
) gives the full super-BMS
4
algebraic structures. For later convenience, we call this superalgebra the singlet super-BMS
4
algebra in the sense that
\mathcal{Q}
and
\bar{\mathcal{Q}}
are all singlets under
M
:

[M,\mathcal{Q}]=[M,\bar{\mathcal{Q}}]=0.

(5.10)

It is interesting to discuss various definitions of the Hermitian conjugation conditions. Let us first restrict our attention to conjugation condition on one branch of the Virasoro algebra
L_{n}
. The conjugation operation
\dagger
is defined to satisfy

(aG)^{\dagger}=a^{*}G^{\dagger},\qquad(G^{\dagger})^{\dagger}=G,

(5.11)

for any algebraic generator
G
and complex constant

. Assuming that
L_{m}^{\dagger}=a(m)L_{f(m)}
, the above ansatz translates to

a(m)^{*}a(f(m))=1,\qquad f(f(m))=m.

(5.12)

The compatible condition with the commutation relations further requires

-a(m)a(n)(f(m)-f(n))=(m-n)~a(m+n),\qquad f(m)+f(n)=f(m+n).

(5.13)

There are two solutions to the above equations:

\displaystyle\mbox{case 1}:

\displaystyle L_{m}^{\dagger}=-e^{icm}L_{m},

(5.14)

\displaystyle\mbox{case 2}:

\displaystyle L_{m}^{\dagger}=e^{cm}L_{-m},

for real constant

. Thus considering both the holomorphic and anti-holomorphic sectors, we have four kinds of definition on the conjugation, in which
L/\bar{L}
conjugates to
L/\bar{L}
. We call these type I conjugation conditions. On the other hand, we can define type II conjugation conditions in which
L
conjugates to
\bar{L}
and vice versa. To satisfy

, there are two kinds of type II conjugation conditions. In each case, we can fix the conjugation conditions on

1,2

The starting point is assuming the existence of
\mathcal{Q}^{1}

L_{n}

(1,0)

\{L_{m},L_{n},\mathcal{Q}^{1}_{r}\}

\{\mathcal{Q}^{1}_{r},\mathcal{Q}^{1}_{s}\}=L_{r+s}

6
Conclusion and Discussion

In this paper, we conducted a systematic investigation of the supersymmetric extension of both the Carrollian algebra and the Carrollian conformal algebra in
d=4
and
d=3
. For
4
D Carrollian algebra, we studied possible supersymmetric extensions for the supercharges transforming under singlet or chain representations of the Carrollian rotations. We saw that for specific representation patterns, super-Lie algebras exist for arbitrary spin
j
. Especially, we found two structures emerging from taking the ultrarelativisitc limit of super-Poincaré algebra: one with
Q_{1},Q_{2}\in(j)
, and the commutation relations shown in (
4.6
); the other with
Q\in(j)\to(j)
and the commutation relations shown in (
4.14
). They correspond to different rescaling of the supercharges under the limit. For conformal case in
4
D, we found only three structures (
4.22
), (
4.23
) and (
4.24
) satisfying the basic ansatz (
4.21
), in which only (
4.24
) is nontrivial in the sense that anti-commutators of supercharges are nonvanishing. This algebra is in fact equivalent to
5
-dimensional super-Poincaré algebra
\mathfrak{iso}(1,4|1)
. A remarkable observation is that the Carrollian superconformal algebra achieves closure without R-symmetry generator. Instead, the possible R-symmetry is either the central charge or outer automorphisms of the algebra.

In the
3
-dimensional case, the lack of constraints on the representations of Carrollian rotations is a main challenge for discussing super-Carrollian algebra. Instead of having a thorough investigation of possible structure of 3D super-Carrollian algebra, we were satisfied focusing on the supersymmetric extension of Carrollian conformal algebra in
d=3
. We revealed that the nontrivial super-Lie algebra given by (
5.2
) is isomorphic to
4
-dimensional super-Poincaré algebra
\mathfrak{iso}(1,3|1)
. Similar to
4
D case, this algebra does not need R-symmetry to be closed.

As is well established, the
3
D Carrollian conformal algebra admits an extension to BMS
4
algebra. In the same manner, the algebra (
5.2
) can also be extended to the so-called singlet super BMS
4
algebra, where the commutation relations involving fermionic generators are given by (
5.9
). We also discussed various Hermitian conjugation conditions, and found that although the algebras share similar structure, the physical conjugation conditions of
4
D Poincaré theory,
3
D Carrollian conformal theory and
2
D Lorentzian celestial CFT are all different. Furthermore, the BMS
4
algebra admits two other supersymmetric extensions, i.e. the chiral multiplet super-BMS
4
algebras in (
5.20
) and (
5.21
). In particular, neither of them can be found by taking
c\to 0
limit. Besides, the finite dimensional subalgebras (
5.26
) are not Carrollian superconformal algebra in the traditional manner because there exist
\Delta=\pm\frac{3}{2}
fermionic generators. The multiplet chiral super BMS
4
algebras represent particularly noteworthy examples, as their finite-dimensional subalgebras notably differ from conventional Carrollian superconformal algebra. In subsequent work
[
77
]
, we will systematically explore additional possibilities for supersymmetric extensions of the BMS algebra and investigate the corresponding theory constructions.

This work establishes important foundations for several promising avenues in supersymmetric Carrollian physics:

Asymptotic supersymmetry

The BMS
4
algebra was conventionally derived as the asymptotic symmetry algebra of flat spacetime. A natural and compelling question arises that can analogous methods be developed to derive asymptotic supersymmetry? The singlet super BMS
4
algebra (
5.9
) and multiplet BMS
4
algebras (
5.20
) and (
5.21
) we found in this work serve as the targets for this investigation. The method will be significantly helpful to extend the discussions on gravity in flat background to supergravity.

An important observation is that the supercharges in the left- and right-hand chiral super BMS
4
algebra are incompatible, unless further introducing additional bosonic generators. Crucially, these generators should not generate spacetime symmetry which has been generated by
L,\bar{L}
and
M
in BMS
4
. This suggests that these extra generators must be related to gauge symmetries, which play a key role in supergravity.

Construction of supersymmetric theories

The algebras we established enable further constructions of explicit supersymmetric theories. For (
4.6
) and (
4.14
) of
4
D super-Carrollian algebra which can be derived from taking the limit, the corresponding supersymmetric theories are expected to be obtained similarly via the taking-limit procedure. However, the multiplet super BMS
4
algebras (
5.20
) and (
5.21
) can not be obtained by taking the limit. Thus an intrinsic method is demanded to build the theories with such symmetries.

Using the structure revealed in this work, we can construct various supersymmetric theories explicitly. A particularly promising example is the Carrollian super-Yang-Mills theory. It is expected to realize one of the above Carrollian superconformal symmetries.

Supersymmetry in flat holography

Introducing supersymmetry into flatspace holography represents a particularly promising research direction. The current study of celestial holography and Carrollian holography have been focusing mostly on the bosonic fields. Using the algebraic structure developed in this paper, we can systematically extend the discussions to the fermionic fields. Especially, our results lead directly to: establish holographic dictionaries for supersymmetric theories; develop bulk reconstruction for supersymmetric theories; analyze supergravity radiation in asymptotically flat spacetime. These naturally suggest to extend the analysis of soft theorems to supersymmetric contexts, which may reveal new insights into the infrared structure of supergravity theories.

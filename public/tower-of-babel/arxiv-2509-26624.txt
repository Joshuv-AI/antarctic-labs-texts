Second order interlaced polynomial lattice rules for integration over ℝ s \mathbb{R}^{s}
Tiangang Cui* \ORCID 0000-0002-4840-8545, Josef Dick \ORCID orcid.org/0000-0003-0142-6022, Friedrich Pillichshammer \ORCID 0000-0001-6952-9218

Abstract
We study numerical integration of functions f : ℝ s → ℝ f:\mathbb{R}^{s}\to\mathbb{R} with respect to a probability measure. By applying the corresponding inverse cumulative distribution function, the problem is transformed into integrating an induced function over the unit cube ( 0 , 1 ) s (0,1)^{s} . We introduce a new orthonormal system: order 2 localized Walsh functions . These basis functions retain the approximation power of classical Walsh functions for twice‑differentiable integrands while inheriting the spatial localization of Haar wavelets. Localization is crucial because the transformed integrand is typically unbounded at the boundary. We show that the worst‑case quasi‑Monte Carlo integration error decays like 𝒪 ( N − 1 / λ ) \mathcal{O}(N^{-1/\lambda}) for every λ ∈ ( 1 / 2 , 1 ] \lambda\in(1/2,1] . As an application, we consider elliptic partial differential equations with a finite number of log‑normal random coefficients and show that our error estimates remain valid for their stochastic Galerkin discretizations by applying a suitable importance sampling density.

Introduction

Quasi-Monte Carlo (QMC) methods have become indispensable tools for the efficient numerical approximation of high‐dimensional integrals arising in uncertainty quantification, financial mathematics, and statistical physics. By replacing independent random sampling with carefully constructed deterministic point sets—so‐called low‐discrepancy sequences—QMC rules often deliver dramatically improved convergence rates compared to ordinary Monte Carlo, without sacrificing robustness in high dimensions for integrands whose input on later coordinates diminishes. In classical QMC theory, much of the focus has been on integrals over the unit cube
[0,1]^{s}
against the uniform probability measure, where, under sufficient smoothness of the integrand, higher‐order convergence rates of order
O(N^{-\alpha}(\log N)^{(s-1)\alpha})
(for some
\alpha\geq 0
) can be achieved using higher order digital nets or interlaced polynomial lattice rules
(
Dick and Pillichshammer, 2010
)
, where
N
is the number of employed points in the QMC rule. In particular, for sufficiently smooth integrands on
[0,1]^{s}
, order‐two digital nets attain an
O(N^{-2+\delta})
convergence rate for any
\delta>0
with dimension‐independent constants under appropriately chosen weights which moderate the influence of subsets of input variables
(
Dick, 2008
;
Goda, 2015
)
.

However, many applications require integration over the unbounded domain
\mathbb{R}^{s}
with respect to a product probability density (e.g., Gaussian or log‐Gaussian) of the form

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{s}}F(\boldsymbol{x})\varphi(\boldsymbol{x})\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x},

(1.1)

where

\varphi(\boldsymbol{x})=\prod_{j=1}^{s}\varphi(x_{j})
is a product density on
\mathbb{R}^{s}
(e.g., a standard Gaussian or other probability density function (PDF) with well‐defined derivative) and
F
belongs to a normed space of sufficiently smooth functions on
\mathbb{R}^{s}
. Integrals of the form (
1.1
) arise, for example, when computing expected values of output functionals of the (deterministic) PDE solution
u(\cdot,\boldsymbol{x})
with log‐normal diffusion coefficient
a(\cdot,\boldsymbol{x})=\exp(Z(\cdot,\boldsymbol{x}))
, where
Z
is a Gaussian random field parameterized by the vector of random variables
\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{s}
via a truncated Karhunen–Loève expansion or in computing the expected value of option prices in financial mathematics.

A QMC theory for lattice rules was developed in
Nichols and Kuo (2014)
, who gave a fast component‐by‐component (CBC) construction of randomly shifted lattice rules that achieve
O(N^{-1+\delta})
for integrands in weighted Sobolev spaces on
\mathbb{R}^{s}
—again with dimension‐independent constants—by combining Gaussian‐measure taming with suitable product‐and‐order‐dependent (POD) weights.
Kazashi (2019)
further developed lattice‐based QMC theory for log‐normal PDEs, again obtaining
O(N^{-1+\delta})
quadrature error. More recent nonasymptotic analyses, e.g.,
Liu and Tempone (2023)
, refine these estimates by explicitly tracking dependence on the finite number of QMC points and the dimension, but still only establish first‐order convergence in practice. Numerical integration for PDEs with log-normal coefficients was studied in
Herrmann and Schwab (2019)
. In
Nguyen and Nuyens (2021)
, a multivariate decomposition method for PDEs with log-normal random coefficients was analyzed. Higher order convergence rates for QMC integration over unbounded domains were proven in
Dick et al. (2018)
, but with a very poor dependence of the error bound on the dimension. Another approach has been proposed in
Nuyens and Suzuki (2023)
, where higher order QMC methods for integration over
\mathbb{R}^{s}
were obtained using lattice rules on a truncated domain together with periodization. For general quadrature rules achieving the optimal order of convergence rates (i.e. including the best possible exponent of the
\log N
factor in the upper bound), see
Dũng and Nguyen (2024)
. An approach based on avoiding the boundary of the unit cube after the transformation, as studied in
Owen (2006)
, together with a smoothing procedure has been studied in
Ouyang et al. (2024)
. First order numerical integration using digital nets has been studied in
Dick and Pillichshammer (2025)
. The dimension dependence of numerical integration of Hermite spaces over
\mathbb{R}^{s}
has been studied in
Leobacher et al. (2023)
. In general, it is a difficult problem to obtain optimal convergence rates beyond
1
and dimension-independent bounds. For instance, the suboptimality of Gauss-Hermite rules for integration over
\mathbb{R}
has been shown in
Kazashi et al. (2023)
, and hence sparse grid methods based on Gauss-Hermite rules also cannot yield optimal convergence rates for integration over
\mathbb{R}^{s}
.

Here we construct and analyze second order digital nets on
[0,1)^{s}
transformed to
\mathbb{R}^{s}
using an inverse normal CDF via a suitable localized Walsh bases representation. We establish convergence rates of the numerical integration error of order
N^{-2+\delta}
.

The outline of this paper is as follows. In the forthcoming Section
3
, we introduce the novel concept of localized Walsh functions, which serve as a crucial analytical tool in our study. These functions themselves merit attention, as they combine the features of classical Walsh functions and Haar functions: The Walsh coefficients of twice differentiable functions defined on
[0,1)
of the localized Walsh functions decay as fast as those of classical Walsh coefficents (as shown in Section
4
), and on the other hand, the localized Walsh functions have shrinking support similar to Haar functions. In Remark
3
below we point out the technical obstruction which prevents us from using classical Walsh functions. On the other hand, we also cannot use Haar functions directly as the Haar coefficients do not show an improved rate of convergence for twice differentiable functions, as is true for the Walsh coefficients (of classical and localized Walsh functions). In Section
4
, we also analyze the series expansion of functions on
\mathbb{R}^{s}
with respect to those localized Walsh functions. Assuming certain conditions on the weight functions
\varphi
,
\psi
, and
\omega
—which we collectively denote by Assumption
1
and which appear in the definitions of the norm (
2.4
)—we derive sharp estimates for the corresponding Walsh coefficients. In Section
5
, we apply these coefficient bounds to study the worst-case integration error of QMC rules. Specifically, we employ interlaced polynomial lattice point sets of order two, yielding our main result stated in Theorem
1
. In Section
6
, we present significant examples of weight functions
\varphi
,
\psi
, and
\omega
that satisfy Assumption
1
. Here we focus on integration with respect to the normal distribution. Section
7
then applies our theoretical findings to an elliptic PDE with a finite number of log-normal random coefficients. For the resulting integration problem, we use importance sampling in conjunction with Theorem
1
, obtaining—under suitable hypotheses—an integration error of order
N^{-2+\delta}
. Finally, numerical experiments are presented in Section
8
.

Preliminaries

For a PDF
\varphi:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
let

\Phi(x):=\int_{-\infty}^{x}\varphi(t)\,{\mathrm{d}}t,\quad\mbox{for }x\in\mathbb{R}.

(2.1)

be the corresponding cumulative distribution function (CDF). We assume that the inverse function
\Phi^{-1}:(0,1)\to\mathbb{R}
of
\Phi
is well defined. For vector arguments, we apply
\Phi
and
\Phi^{-1}
coordinate-wise. We approximate the integral (
1.1
) by a QMC rule of the form

\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}F(\Phi^{-1}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n})),

(2.2)

where
\mathcal{P}=\{\boldsymbol{x}_{0},\boldsymbol{x}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{x}_{N-1}\}
is a set of
N
points in
(0,1)^{s}
. The accuracy of this approximation is measured by the absolute integration error
|\mathrm{err}(F;\varphi;\mathcal{P})|
, where

\mathrm{err}(F;\varphi;\mathcal{P}):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{s}}F(\boldsymbol{x})\varphi(\boldsymbol{x})\,{\mathrm{d}}\boldsymbol{x}-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}F(\Phi^{-1}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n})).

We consider functions
F:\mathbb{R}^{s}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
with finite
\boldsymbol{\gamma}
-weighted
q
-norm, defined as follows. For
\emptyset\not=\mathfrak{u}\subseteq[s]
and for
\mathfrak{v}\subseteq\mathfrak{u}
put

\|F\|_{\mathfrak{u},\mathfrak{v}}:=\sum_{\mathfrak{w}\subseteq\mathfrak{v}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{|\mathfrak{u}|}}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{s-|\mathfrak{u}|}}\frac{\partial^{|\mathfrak{u}|+|\mathfrak{w}|}F}{\partial\boldsymbol{x}_{\mathfrak{w}}\partial\boldsymbol{x}_{\mathfrak{u}}}(\boldsymbol{x})\prod_{j\in[s]\setminus\mathfrak{u}}\varphi(x_{j})\,{\mathrm{d}}\boldsymbol{x}_{[s]\setminus\mathfrak{u}}\right|\prod_{j\in\mathfrak{w}}\psi(x_{j})\prod_{j\in\mathfrak{v}\setminus\mathfrak{w}}\omega(x_{j})\,{\mathrm{d}}\boldsymbol{x}_{\mathfrak{u}},

(2.3)

for weight functions
\psi,\omega:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow(0,\infty)
. The
\|F\|_{\mathfrak{u},\mathfrak{v}}
will be essential in estimating localized Walsh coefficients in Lemma
4
and arise naturally from the definition of the Walsh coefficients and integration by parts (see the proof of Lemma
4
for details). Then we define the norm

\|F\|_{\boldsymbol{\gamma},\varphi,\psi,\omega}:=\max_{\emptyset\not=\mathfrak{u}\subseteq[s],\mathfrak{v}\subseteq\mathfrak{u}}\frac{1}{\gamma_{\mathfrak{u}}}\|F\|_{\mathfrak{u},\mathfrak{v}},

(2.4)

where
[s]:=\{1,\ldots,s\}
,
\boldsymbol{\gamma}=\{\gamma_{\mathfrak{u}}\}_{\mathfrak{u}\subseteq[s]}
are positive real coordinate-weights and
\psi,\omega
are weight functions
\mathbb{R}\rightarrow(0,\infty)
. In Section
7
, where we apply our results, we will choose
\psi=\omega
. Occasionally, for
\mathfrak{w}\subseteq\mathfrak{u}\subseteq[s]
,
\mathfrak{u}\not=\emptyset
, we may write the derivatives as

\frac{\partial^{|\mathfrak{u}|+|\mathfrak{w}|}F(\boldsymbol{x})}{\partial\boldsymbol{x}_{\mathfrak{w}}\partial\boldsymbol{x}_{\mathfrak{u}}}=\frac{\partial^{|(\boldsymbol{1}_{\mathfrak{u}\setminus\mathfrak{w}},\boldsymbol{2}_{\mathfrak{w}},\boldsymbol{0})|}}{\partial\boldsymbol{x}_{(\boldsymbol{1}_{\mathfrak{u}\setminus\mathfrak{w}},\boldsymbol{2}_{\mathfrak{w}},\boldsymbol{0})}}F(\boldsymbol{x})=F^{(\boldsymbol{1}_{\mathfrak{u}\setminus\mathfrak{w}},\boldsymbol{2}_{\mathfrak{w}},\boldsymbol{0})}(\boldsymbol{x})

meaning that we take the one-fold partial derivative with respect to
x_{j}
when
j\in\mathfrak{u}\setminus\mathfrak{w}
and the two-fold partial derivative with respect to
x_{j}
when
j\in\mathfrak{w}
.

In the following, we define the worst-case integration error of integrating a function
F
with respect to the probability density function
\varphi
.

Definition 1

We consider functions
F
in a function space whose norm is weighted by the functions
\psi

2.4

\mathcal{F}_{\boldsymbol{\gamma},\varphi,\psi,\omega}:=\{F\in L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi):\|F\|_{\boldsymbol{\gamma},\varphi,\psi,\omega}<\infty\}

for functions

3
Localized Walsh functions

As a technical auxiliary tool, we introduce the concept of localized Walsh functions on the bounded interval
[0,1)
, designed to combine the properties of standard Walsh functions in base
2
and Haar functions. We then extend the concept of localized Walsh functions to probability density functions with unbounded support via their CDFs.

Definition 2

(Walsh functions)
.

Let
k=\kappa_{0}+\kappa_{1}2+\cdots+\kappa_{m-1}2^{m-1}
be the dyadic expansion of
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
with digits
\kappa_{0},\kappa_{1},\ldots,\kappa_{m-1}\in\{0,1\}
. Similarly, let
x\in[0,1)
have the dyadic expansion
x=\xi_{1}2^{-1}+\xi_{2}2^{-2}+\cdots
with
\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\ldots\in\{0,1\}
, assuming infinitely many of the digits
\xi_{i}
differ from
1
. For
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
we define the
k
-th Walsh function
\mathrm{wal}_{k}:[0,1)\to\{-1,1\}
by

\mathrm{wal}_{k}(x):=(-1)^{\xi_{1}\kappa_{0}+\xi_{2}\kappa_{1}+\cdots+\xi_{m}\kappa_{m-1}}.

Details on Walsh functions can be found in
Fine (1949)
and
Schipp et al. (1990)
. In particular, we use the following lemma, whose proof can be found in Appendix
A
:

Lemma 1
.

For
r\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
k\in\{2^{r},\ldots,2^{r+1}-1\}
and
x\in[0,1]
we have

\int_{0}^{x}{\mathrm{w}al}_{k}(t)\,{\mathrm{d}}t=\frac{1}{2^{r}}\,{\mathrm{w}al}_{k}(x)\,\eta(2^{r}x),

where
\eta:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is the one-periodic function defined by

\eta(x):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}x&\mbox{if $0\leq x<\frac{1}{2}$,}\\
x-1&\mbox{if $\frac{1}{2}\leq x<1$,}\end{array}\right.

and consequently

\sup_{x\in[0,1]}\left|\int_{0}^{x}{\mathrm{w}al}_{k}(t)\,{\mathrm{d}}t\right|=\frac{1}{2^{r+1}}.

In QMC theory, Walsh functions have demonstrated their utility in deriving error bounds for numerical integration, especially for functions exhibiting higher degrees of smoothness, see, e.g.,
Dick and Pillichshammer (2010)
. However, a weakness of Walsh functions is that they are not localized, which presents challenges in analysing the unbounded transformations
\Phi^{-1}
. To mitigate this limitation, we propose the use of
second-order localized Walsh functions
, which amalgamate the beneficial approximation properties of Walsh functions applicable to twice-differentiable functions with the localized features inherent in Haar functions.

To facilitate the introduction of localized Walsh functions, we define the sets

N_{0}:=\{0\}\qquad\mbox{ and }\qquad N_{1}:=N_{0}\cup\{2^{a_{1}}:a_{1}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\},

consisting of zero and all powers of two, and

N_{2}:=N_{1}\cup\{2^{a_{1}}+2^{a_{2}}:a_{1},a_{2}\in\mathbb{N}_{0},a_{1}>a_{2}\}

containing zero, all powers of two and all sums of two distinct powers of two. If, for
\alpha\in\{1,2\}
,
k\in N_{\alpha}\setminus N_{\alpha-1}
, then the dyadic expansion of
k
has exactly
\alpha
non-zero digits. Letting
\alpha\in\{1,2\}
, we define

L_{k,\alpha}:=\begin{cases}\{0\}&\mbox{for }k\in N_{\alpha-1},\\
\{0,1,\ldots,2^{a_{\alpha}}-1\}&\mbox{for }k\in N_{\alpha}\setminus N_{\alpha-1}.\end{cases}

Moreover, for
a\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
m\in\{0,1,\ldots,2^{a}-1\}
we set
I(m,a):=\left[\frac{m}{2^{a}},\frac{m+1}{2^{a}}\right)
. The symbol
\boldsymbol{1}_{A}
denotes the indicator function of a set
A
. Now we introduce the concept of order
\alpha
localized Walsh functions.

Definition 3

(Localized Walsh functions)
.

For
\alpha=1
we define the set of
order
1
localized Walsh functions

\{w_{k,m,1}:m\in L_{k,1},k\in N_{1}\}
as follows:

1.

For
k\in N_{0}
we have
k=0
and we define
w_{0,0,1}:[0,1)\to\mathbb{R}
as
w_{0,0,1}:=\mathrm{wal}_{0}=1
.

2.

For
k=2^{a_{1}}\in N_{1}\setminus N_{0}
and
m\in L_{k,1}
we define

w_{k,m,1}(x):=2^{a_{1}/2}\,\boldsymbol{1}_{I(m,a_{1})}(x)\mathrm{wal}_{k}(x).

For
\alpha=2
we define the set of
order
2
localized Walsh functions

\{w_{k,m,2}:m\in L_{k,2},k\in N_{2}\}
as follows:

1.

For
k\in N_{1}
we define
w_{k,0,2}:[0,1)\to\mathbb{R}
as
w_{k,0,2}:=\mathrm{wal}_{k}
.

2.

For
k=2^{a_{1}}+2^{a_{2}}\in N_{2}\setminus N_{1}
with
a_{1}>a_{2}\geq 0
and
m\in L_{k,2}
define
w_{k,m,2}:[0,1)\to\mathbb{R}
by

w_{k,m,2}(x):=2^{a_{2}/2}\,\boldsymbol{1}_{I(m,a_{2})}(x)\mathrm{wal}_{k}(x).

. Note that
20=2^{4}+2^{2}\in N_{2}\setminus N_{1}
, i.e.,
a_{1}=4
,
a_{2}=2
and hence
L_{20,2}=\{0,1,2,3\}
.

Remark 1
.

The following two observations are in order:

1.

The functions defined in item (b) of Definition
3
are localized, whereas the functions in item (a) are not. For
\alpha=1
there is only a single non-localized function. When
\alpha=2
, the proportion of non-localized functions becomes negligible in the limit, as indicated by the density

\lim_{K\to\infty}\frac{\#\{k\in N_{1}:k\leq K\}}{\#\{k\in N_{2}:k\leq K\}}=0.

Therefore, although not all functions
w_{m,k,\alpha}
are localized, the vast majority are.

2.

Let
k\in N_{\alpha}\setminus N_{\alpha-1}
. Then
k=2^{a_{1}}
if
\alpha=1
, and
k=2^{a_{1}}+2^{a_{2}}
with
a_{1}>a_{2}\geq 0
if
\alpha=2
. For
\ell\in\{0,1,\ldots,2^{a_{\alpha}}-1\}
, we have the following relation

\mathrm{wal}_{k+\ell}(x)=\frac{1}{2^{a_{\alpha}/2}}\mathrm{wal}_{\ell}(x)\sum_{m=0}^{2^{a_{\alpha}}-1}w_{k,m,\alpha}(x)=\frac{1}{2^{a_{\alpha}/2}}\sum_{m=0}^{2^{a_{\alpha}-1}}\mathrm{wal}_{\ell}\left(\frac{m}{2^{a_{\alpha}}}\right)w_{k,m,\alpha}(x).

(3.1)

This follows because for
x\in I(m^{\prime},a_{\alpha})
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{wal}_{\ell}(x)\sum_{m=0}^{2^{a_{\alpha}}-1}w_{k,m,\alpha}(x)=

\displaystyle\mathrm{wal}_{\ell}(x)w_{k,m^{\prime},\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{wal}_{\ell}\left(\frac{m^{\prime}}{2^{a_{\alpha}}}\right)w_{k,m^{\prime},\alpha}(x)=\sum_{m=0}^{2^{a_{\alpha}-1}}\mathrm{wal}_{\ell}\left(\frac{m}{2^{a_{\alpha}}}\right)w_{k,m,\alpha}(x).

For any fixed
\alpha\in\{1,2\}
, the set of order
\alpha
localized Walsh functions

\{w_{k,m,\alpha}\ :\ k\in N_{\alpha},m\in L_{k,\alpha}\}

forms an orthonormal basis of
L_{2}([0,1))
, since for all
k,k^{\prime}\in N_{\alpha}
and
m,m^{\prime}\in L_{k,\alpha}
,

\int_{0}^{1}w_{k,m,\alpha}(x)w_{k^{\prime},m^{\prime},\alpha}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x=\begin{cases}1&\mbox{if }(k,m)=(k^{\prime},m^{\prime}),\\
0&\mbox{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Moreover, for every
a\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
we have the identity

\mathrm{span}\{w_{k,m,\alpha}:k\in N_{\alpha},\lfloor 2^{a-1}\rfloor\leq k<2^{a},m\in L_{k,\alpha}\}=\mathrm{span}\{\mathrm{wal}_{k}:\lfloor 2^{a-1}\rfloor\leq k<2^{a}\}.

In particular, for
\alpha=1
we recover the Haar functions

\{w_{\lfloor 2^{j}\rfloor,m,1}\ :\ j\in\mathbb{N}_{-1},m\in L_{\lfloor 2^{j}\rfloor,\alpha}\}.

We also note the connection between localized Walsh functions and the tiling of the Walsh-phase plane as discussed in
Thiele (1995)
.

It should also be added that there is a natural extension of order
\alpha
localized Walsh functions for
\alpha=3,4,5,\ldots
and basis different from
2
. However, such extensions are not considered in this paper, as we do not use these functions.

Definition 4
.

For
\alpha\in\{1,2\}
,
k\in N_{\alpha}
and

define the cumulated functions

W_{k,m,\alpha}(x):=\int_{0}^{x}w_{k,m,\alpha}(y)\,{\mathrm{d}}y\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\widetilde{W}_{k,m,\alpha}(x):=\int_{0}^{x}W_{k,m,\alpha}(y)\,{\mathrm{d}}y.

.

For
k\in N_{2}\setminus N_{1}
of the form
k=2^{a_{1}}+2^{a_{2}}
, where
a_{1}>a_{2}
, and for

The proof of this result is deferred to Appendix
B

(Localized Walsh functions with respect to a PDF)

As above, let
\varphi

\mathbb{R}

\Phi(x):=\int_{-\infty}^{x}\varphi(t)\,{\mathrm{d}}t
be the corresponding CDF. For
\alpha\in\{1,2\}
the (order

For every

\displaystyle W_{k,m,\alpha}^{(\varphi)}(x):=\int_{-\infty}^{x}w_{k,m,\alpha}^{(\varphi)}(y)\varphi(y)\,{\mathrm{d}}y=

(3.2)

and

\displaystyle\widetilde{W}_{k,m,\alpha}^{(\varphi)}(x):=\int_{-\infty}^{x}W_{k,m,\alpha}^{(\varphi)}(y)\varphi(y)\,{\mathrm{d}}y=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\Phi(x)}W_{k,m,\alpha}(y)\,{\mathrm{d}}y=\widetilde{W}_{k,m,\alpha}(\Phi(x)).

(3.3)

From Lemma

Lemma 3

For

of the form

4
Walsh series expansions of functions over
\mathbb{R}^{s}

This section investigates series expansions of functions
F\in L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)
with the application of both Walsh- and localized Walsh bases. The primary objective is to achieve precise estimation of Walsh coefficients under specific conditions, details of which will be provided in Section
4.2

4.1
Bounds on localized Walsh coefficients

For a vector
\boldsymbol{k}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{s})\in N_{2}^{s}
put
L_{\boldsymbol{k},2}:=L_{k_{1},2}\times\ldots\times L_{k_{s},2}
. For
\boldsymbol{m}=(m_{1},\ldots,m_{s})\in L_{\boldsymbol{k},2}
and
\boldsymbol{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{s})
put
w_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{m},2}(\boldsymbol{x}):=\prod_{j=1}^{s}w_{k_{j},m_{j},2}(x_{j})
and similarly for
w_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{m},2}^{(\varphi)}
. For
F\in L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)
we write

F(\boldsymbol{x})\sim\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}\in N_{2}^{s}}\sum_{\boldsymbol{m}\in L_{\boldsymbol{k},2}}\widehat{F}^{(\varphi)}(\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{m})w^{(\varphi)}_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{m},2}(\boldsymbol{x}),

with localized Walsh function coefficients

\widehat{F}^{(\varphi)}(\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{m}):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{s}}F(\boldsymbol{x})w^{(\varphi)}_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{m},2}(\boldsymbol{x})\varphi(\boldsymbol{x})\,{\mathrm{d}}\boldsymbol{x}.

We assume that the weight functions
\varphi,\psi,\omega
in (
2.3
) satisfy the following assumption.

Assumption 1
.

Let
\varphi
be a probability density function on
\mathbb{R}
used in (
1.1
), and let
\psi,\omega
be the weight functions in the norm (
2.3
), (
2.4
). There exist reals
C>0
and
\beta\in(0,1]
such that for every
k\in N_{2}\setminus N_{1}
,
k=2^{a_{1}}+2^{a_{2}}
,
a_{1}>a_{2}\geq 0
, and
m\in L_{k,2}
we have

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\frac{|\widetilde{W}_{k,m,2}^{(\varphi)}(x)|}{\varphi(x)\psi(x)}\leq C\,\frac{2^{a_{2}/2}}{2^{a_{1}+\beta a_{2}}}

and

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\frac{|\widetilde{W}_{k,m,2}^{(\varphi)}(x)\varphi^{\prime}(x)|}{\varphi^{2}(x)\omega(x)}\leq C\,\frac{2^{a_{2}/2}}{2^{a_{1}+\beta a_{2}}},

where
\varphi^{\prime}
is the first derivative of
\varphi
and
\widetilde{W}^{(\varphi)}_{k,m,2}
is given in (
3.3
).

Examples of weight functions that satisfy Assumption
1
will be discussed in Section
6
. Notice that the factor
2^{a_{2}/2}
arises from the scaling of the localized Walsh functions
w_{k,m,2}
. This factor will cancel out when we switch to Walsh functions in Section
4.2
. When we assume in this work that Assumption
1
holds, we tacitly mean that this holds for some constant
C>0
and parameter
\beta\in(0,1]
.

We note that introducing localized Walsh functions—rather than working directly with the standard Walsh expansions—seems indispensable, since we cannot verify an analogue of Assumption
1
in a purely global setting for classical Walsh functions. By localizing the Walsh functions verifying this becomes feasible, see Section
6
and, in particular, Remark
3
.

The next lemma provides a bound on a certain sum of absolute values of localized Walsh function coefficients of a function
F\in L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)
under the validity of Assumption
1
. Here it is essential that the bound is on the
sum over all shifted localized Walsh functions,
since we need this to obtain a bound on the classical Walsh coefficients in Lemma
5
.

Lemma 4
.

For
\emptyset\not=\mathfrak{u}\subseteq[s]
let
(\boldsymbol{k}_{\mathfrak{u}},\boldsymbol{0}):=(y_{1},y_{2},\ldots,y_{s})\in N_{2}^{s}
, where
y_{j}=k_{j}\in N_{2}\setminus\{0\}
for
j\in\mathfrak{u}
and
y_{j}=0
for
j\in[s]\setminus\mathfrak{u}
. Further let
\mathfrak{v}\subseteq\mathfrak{u}
be such that for
j\in\mathfrak{v}
we have
k_{j}=2^{a_{j,1}}+2^{a_{j,2}}\in N_{2}\setminus N_{1}
,
a_{j,1}>a_{j,2}\geq 0
and for
j\in\mathfrak{u}\setminus\mathfrak{v}
we have
k_{j}=2^{a_{j,1}}\in N_{1}\setminus N_{0}
,
a_{j,1}\geq 0
. Then, under Assumption
1
, and with
C_{1}:=\max(C,\tfrac{1}{2})
, we have

\sum_{\boldsymbol{m}_{\mathfrak{v}}\in L_{\boldsymbol{k}_{\mathfrak{v}},2}}|\widehat{F}^{(\varphi)}((\boldsymbol{k}_{\mathfrak{u}},\boldsymbol{0}),(\boldsymbol{m}_{\mathfrak{v}},\boldsymbol{0}))|\leq C_{1}^{|\mathfrak{u}|}\,\|F\|_{\mathfrak{u},\mathfrak{v}}\prod_{j\in\mathfrak{v}}\frac{2^{a_{j,2}/2}}{2^{a_{j,1}+\beta a_{j,2}}}\prod_{j\in\mathfrak{u}\setminus\mathfrak{v}}\frac{1}{2^{a_{j,1}}}.

Proof. .

Since
L_{0,2}=\{0\}
, every element from the corresponding set
L_{(\boldsymbol{k}_{\mathfrak{u}},\boldsymbol{0}),2}
has the form
(m_{\mathfrak{u}},\boldsymbol{0})=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{s})
where
z_{j}=m_{j}\in L_{k_{j},2}
for
j\in\mathfrak{u}
and
z_{j}=0
for
j\in[s]\setminus\mathfrak{u}
. Using integration by parts and introducing the cumulated localized Walsh functions as described in (
3.2
), we have

\displaystyle\widehat{F}^{(\varphi)}((\boldsymbol{k}_{\mathfrak{u}},\boldsymbol{0}),(\boldsymbol{m}_{\mathfrak{u}},\boldsymbol{0}))=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{s}}F(\boldsymbol{x})w^{(\varphi)}_{(\boldsymbol{k}_{\mathfrak{u}},\boldsymbol{0}),(\boldsymbol{m}_{\mathfrak{u}},\boldsymbol{0}),2}(\boldsymbol{x})\varphi(\boldsymbol{x})\,{\mathrm{d}}\boldsymbol{x}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{s}}F(\boldsymbol{x})\prod_{j\in\mathfrak{u}}w^{(\varphi)}_{k_{j},m_{j},2}(x_{j})\prod_{j\in\mathfrak{u}}\varphi(x_{j})\prod_{j\in[s]\setminus\mathfrak{u}}\varphi(x_{j})\,{\mathrm{d}}\boldsymbol{x}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{|\mathfrak{u}|}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{s}}\frac{\partial^{|\mathfrak{u}|}F}{\partial\boldsymbol{x}_{\mathfrak{u}}}(\boldsymbol{x})\prod_{j\in\mathfrak{u}}W_{k_{j},m_{j},2}^{(\varphi)}(x_{j})\prod_{j\in[s]\setminus\mathfrak{u}}\varphi(x_{j})\,{\mathrm{d}}\boldsymbol{x}.

By definition,
\mathfrak{v}
consists of all
j\in\mathfrak{u}
for which
k_{j}\in N_{2}\setminus N_{1}
. For
j\in\mathfrak{u}\setminus\mathfrak{v}
we have
k_{j}\in N_{1}\setminus N_{0}
. Note that for
j\in\mathfrak{u}\setminus\mathfrak{v}
we have
L_{k_{j},2}=\{0\}
and hence the corresponding
m_{j}
equals 0. Using integration by parts a second time for all coordinates
j\in\mathfrak{v}
and introducing the cumulated localized Walsh functions from (
3.3
), we obtain

\displaystyle\widehat{F}^{(\varphi)}((\boldsymbol{k}_{\mathfrak{u}},\boldsymbol{0}),(\boldsymbol{m}_{\mathfrak{v}},\boldsymbol{0}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{|\mathfrak{u}|}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{s}}\frac{\partial^{|\mathfrak{u}|}F}{\partial\boldsymbol{x}_{\mathfrak{u}}}(\boldsymbol{x})\prod_{j\in\mathfrak{v}}\frac{W_{k_{j},m_{j},2}^{(\varphi)}(x_{j})\varphi(x_{j})}{\varphi(x_{j})}\prod_{j\in\mathfrak{u}\setminus\mathfrak{v}}W^{(\varphi)}_{k_{j},0,2}(x_{j})\prod_{j\in[s]\setminus\mathfrak{u}}\varphi(x_{j})\,{\mathrm{d}}\boldsymbol{x}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathfrak{w}\subseteq\mathfrak{v}}(-1)^{|\mathfrak{u}|+|\mathfrak{w}|}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{s}}\frac{\partial^{|\mathfrak{u}|+|\mathfrak{w}|}F}{\partial\boldsymbol{x}_{\mathfrak{w}}\partial\boldsymbol{x}_{\mathfrak{u}}}(\boldsymbol{x})\prod_{j\in\mathfrak{w}}\frac{1}{\varphi(x_{j})}\prod_{j\in\mathfrak{v}\setminus\mathfrak{w}}\frac{\varphi^{\prime}(x_{j})}{\varphi^{2}(x_{j})}\prod_{j\in\mathfrak{v}}\widetilde{W}_{k_{j},m_{j},2}^{(\varphi)}(x_{j})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\prod_{j\in\mathfrak{u}\setminus\mathfrak{v}}W^{(\varphi)}_{k_{j},0,2}(x_{j})\prod_{j\in[s]\setminus\mathfrak{u}}\varphi(x_{j})\,{\mathrm{d}}\boldsymbol{x}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathfrak{w}\subseteq\mathfrak{v}}(-1)^{|\mathfrak{u}|+|\mathfrak{w}|}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{s}}\frac{\partial^{|\mathfrak{u}|+|\mathfrak{w}|}F}{\partial\boldsymbol{x}_{\mathfrak{w}}\partial\boldsymbol{x}_{\mathfrak{u}}}(\boldsymbol{x})\prod_{j\in\mathfrak{w}}\frac{\widetilde{W}_{k_{j},m_{j},2}^{(\varphi)}(x_{j})}{\varphi(x_{j})}\prod_{j\in\mathfrak{v}\setminus\mathfrak{w}}\frac{\widetilde{W}_{k_{j},m_{j},2}^{(\varphi)}(x_{j})\varphi^{\prime}(x_{j})}{\varphi^{2}(x_{j})}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\prod_{j\in\mathfrak{u}\setminus\mathfrak{v}}W^{(\varphi)}_{k_{j},0,2}(x_{j})\prod_{j\in[s]\setminus\mathfrak{u}}\varphi(x_{j})\,{\mathrm{d}}\boldsymbol{x}.

Taking the absolute value and applying the triangle inequality, we thus have

\displaystyle|\widehat{F}^{(\varphi)}((\boldsymbol{k}_{\mathfrak{u}},\boldsymbol{0}),(\boldsymbol{m}_{\mathfrak{v}},\boldsymbol{0}))|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathfrak{w}\subseteq\mathfrak{v}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{u}}}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{s-|\mathfrak{u}|}}\frac{\partial^{|\mathfrak{u}|+|\mathfrak{w}|}F}{\partial\boldsymbol{x}_{\mathfrak{w}}\partial\boldsymbol{x}_{\mathfrak{u}}}(\boldsymbol{x})\prod_{j\in[s]\setminus\mathfrak{u}}\varphi(x_{j})\,{\mathrm{d}}\boldsymbol{x}_{[s]\setminus\mathfrak{u}}\right|

\displaystyle\qquad\times\prod_{j\in\mathfrak{w}}\frac{|\widetilde{W}_{k_{j},m_{j},2}^{(\varphi)}(x_{j})|}{\varphi(x_{j})}\prod_{j\in\mathfrak{v}\setminus\mathfrak{w}}\frac{|\widetilde{W}_{k_{j},m_{j},2}^{(\varphi)}(x_{j})\varphi^{\prime}(x_{j})|}{\varphi^{2}(x_{j})}\prod_{j\in\mathfrak{u}\setminus\mathfrak{v}}|W^{(\varphi)}_{k_{j},0,2}(x_{j})|\,{\mathrm{d}}\boldsymbol{x}_{\mathfrak{u}}.

Now we introduce the weight functions
\psi,\omega:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow(0,\infty)
in the following way: we have

\displaystyle|\widehat{F}^{(\varphi)}((\boldsymbol{k}_{\mathfrak{u}},\boldsymbol{0}),(\boldsymbol{m}_{\mathfrak{v}},\boldsymbol{0}))|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathfrak{w}\subseteq\mathfrak{v}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{u}}}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{s-|\mathfrak{u}|}}\frac{\partial^{|\mathfrak{u}|+|\mathfrak{w}|}F}{\partial\boldsymbol{x}_{\mathfrak{w}}\partial\boldsymbol{x}_{\mathfrak{u}}}(\boldsymbol{x})\prod_{j\in[s]\setminus\mathfrak{u}}\varphi(x_{j})\,{\mathrm{d}}\boldsymbol{x}_{[s]\setminus\mathfrak{u}}\right|\prod_{j\in\mathfrak{w}}\psi(x_{j})\prod_{j\in\mathfrak{v}\setminus\mathfrak{w}}\omega(x_{j})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times\prod_{j\in\mathfrak{w}}\frac{|\widetilde{W}_{k_{j},m_{j},2}^{(\varphi)}(x_{j})|}{\varphi(x_{j})\psi(x_{j})}\prod_{j\in\mathfrak{v}\setminus\mathfrak{w}}\frac{|\widetilde{W}_{k_{j},m_{j},2}^{(\varphi)}(x_{j})\varphi^{\prime}(x_{j})|}{\varphi^{2}(x_{j})\omega(x_{j})}\prod_{j\in\mathfrak{u}\setminus\mathfrak{v}}|W^{(\varphi)}_{k_{j},0,2}(x_{j})|\,{\mathrm{d}}\boldsymbol{x}_{\mathfrak{u}}.

Taking the sum over all
\boldsymbol{m}_{\mathfrak{v}}\in L_{\boldsymbol{k}_{\mathfrak{v}},2}
we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{\boldsymbol{m}_{\mathfrak{v}}\in L_{\boldsymbol{k}_{\mathfrak{v}},2}}|\widehat{F}^{(\varphi)}((\boldsymbol{k}_{\mathfrak{u}},\boldsymbol{0}),(\boldsymbol{m}_{\mathfrak{v}},\boldsymbol{0}))|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathfrak{w}\subseteq\mathfrak{v}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{u}}}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{s-|\mathfrak{u}|}}\frac{\partial^{|\mathfrak{u}|+|\mathfrak{w}|}F}{\partial\boldsymbol{x}_{\mathfrak{w}}\partial\boldsymbol{x}_{\mathfrak{u}}}(\boldsymbol{x})\prod_{j\in[s]\setminus\mathfrak{u}}\varphi(x_{j})\,{\mathrm{d}}\boldsymbol{x}_{[s]\setminus\mathfrak{u}}\right|\prod_{j\in\mathfrak{w}}\psi(x_{j})\prod_{j\in\mathfrak{v}\setminus\mathfrak{w}}\omega(x_{j})

\displaystyle\quad\times\sum_{\boldsymbol{m}_{\mathfrak{v}}\in L_{\boldsymbol{k}_{\mathfrak{v}},2}}\left(\prod_{j\in\mathfrak{w}}\frac{|\widetilde{W}_{k_{j},m_{j},2}^{(\varphi)}(x_{j})|}{\varphi(x_{j})\psi(x_{j})}\prod_{j\in\mathfrak{v}\setminus\mathfrak{w}}\frac{2|\widetilde{W}_{k_{j},m_{j},2}^{(\varphi)}(x_{j})\varphi^{\prime}(x_{j})|}{\varphi^{2}(x_{j})\omega(x_{j})}\prod_{j\in\mathfrak{u}\setminus\mathfrak{v}}|W^{(\varphi)}_{k_{j},0,2}(x_{j})|\right)\,{\mathrm{d}}\boldsymbol{x}_{\mathfrak{u}}.

Remember that
j\in\mathfrak{u}\setminus\mathfrak{v}
means that
k_{j}\in N_{1}\setminus N_{0}
. In this case we have
k_{j}=2^{a_{j,1}}
and
w_{k_{j},0,2}={\mathrm{w}al}_{2^{a_{j,1}}}
. Thus,

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}|W^{(\varphi)}_{k_{j},0,2}(x)|=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\left|\int_{0}^{\Phi(x)}{\mathrm{w}al}_{2^{a_{j,1}}}(y)\,{\mathrm{d}}y\right|=\sup_{t\in[0,1]}\left|\int_{0}^{t}{\mathrm{w}al}_{2^{a_{j,1}}}(y)\,{\mathrm{d}}y\right|=\frac{1}{2^{a_{j,1}+1}},

where for the last identity we used Lemma
1
. Applying this, Lemma
3
, and Assumption
1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{\boldsymbol{m}_{\mathfrak{v}}\in L_{\boldsymbol{k}_{\mathfrak{v}},2}}|\widehat{F}^{(\varphi)}((\boldsymbol{k}_{\mathfrak{u}},\boldsymbol{0}),(\boldsymbol{m}_{\mathfrak{v}},\boldsymbol{0}))|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathfrak{w}\subseteq\mathfrak{v}}\prod_{j\in\mathfrak{w}}\frac{C\,2^{a_{j,2}/2}}{2^{a_{j,1}+\beta a_{j,2}}}\prod_{j\in\mathfrak{v}\setminus\mathfrak{w}}\frac{C\,2^{a_{j,2}/2}}{2^{a_{j,1}+\beta a_{j,2}}}\prod_{j\in\mathfrak{u}\setminus\mathfrak{v}}\frac{1}{2^{a_{j,1}+1}}

\displaystyle\times\int_{\mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{u}}}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{s-|\mathfrak{u}|}}\frac{\partial^{|\mathfrak{u}|+|\mathfrak{w}|}F}{\partial\boldsymbol{x}_{\mathfrak{w}}\partial\boldsymbol{x}_{\mathfrak{u}}}(\boldsymbol{x})\prod_{j\in[s]\setminus\mathfrak{u}}\varphi(x_{j})\,{\mathrm{d}}\boldsymbol{x}_{[s]\setminus\mathfrak{u}}\right|\prod_{j\in\mathfrak{w}}\psi(x_{j})\prod_{j\in\mathfrak{v}\setminus\mathfrak{w}}\omega(x_{j})\,{\mathrm{d}}\boldsymbol{x}_{\mathfrak{u}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\max(C,\tfrac{1}{2})^{|\mathfrak{u}|}\,\|F\|_{\mathfrak{u},\mathfrak{v}}\prod_{j\in\mathfrak{v}}\frac{2^{a_{j,2}/2}}{2^{a_{j,1}+\beta a_{j,2}}}\prod_{j\in\mathfrak{u}\setminus\mathfrak{v}}\frac{1}{2^{a_{j,1}}},

as claimed.
∎

4.2
Bounds on Walsh coefficients

For the error analysis we use the classical Walsh-expansion of functions
F\in L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)
,

F(\boldsymbol{x})\sim\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{s}}\widetilde{F}^{(\varphi)}(\boldsymbol{k}){\mathrm{w}al}_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{(\varphi)}(\boldsymbol{x}),\qquad\mbox{for }\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{s},

with Walsh coefficients

\widetilde{F}^{(\varphi)}(\boldsymbol{k}):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{s}}F(\boldsymbol{x}){\mathrm{w}al}_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{(\varphi)}(\boldsymbol{x})\varphi(\boldsymbol{x})\,{\mathrm{d}}\boldsymbol{x},

where
{\mathrm{w}al}_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{(\varphi)}(\boldsymbol{x}):={\mathrm{w}al}_{\boldsymbol{k}}(\Phi(\boldsymbol{x}))
. Now we show how to use Lemma
4
in order to estimate Walsh coefficients.

Lemma 5
.

For
\mathfrak{v}\subseteq\mathfrak{u}\subseteq[s]
let
(\boldsymbol{k}_{\mathfrak{v}}+\boldsymbol{\ell}_{\mathfrak{v}},\boldsymbol{k}_{\mathfrak{u}\setminus\mathfrak{v}},\boldsymbol{0}):=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{s})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{s}
, where

•

for
j\in\mathfrak{v}
:
y_{j}=k_{j}+\ell_{j}
with
k_{j}=2^{a_{j,1}}+2^{a_{j,2}}\in N_{2}\setminus N_{1}
and
\ell_{j}\in\{0,1,\ldots,2^{a_{j,2}}-1\}
,

•

for
j\in\mathfrak{u}\setminus\mathfrak{v}
:
y_{j}=k_{j}
with
k_{j}=2^{a_{j,1}}\in N_{1}\setminus N_{0}
, and

•

for
j\in[s]\setminus\mathfrak{u}
:
y_{j}=0
.

Then, under Assumption
1
, we have

|\widetilde{F}^{(\varphi)}((\boldsymbol{k}_{\mathfrak{v}}+\boldsymbol{\ell}_{\mathfrak{v}},\boldsymbol{k}_{\mathfrak{u}\setminus\mathfrak{v}},\boldsymbol{0}))|\leq C_{1}^{|\mathfrak{u}|}\,\|F\|_{\mathfrak{u},\mathfrak{v}}\,\prod_{j\in\mathfrak{v}}\frac{1}{2^{a_{j,1}+\beta a_{j,2}}}\prod_{j\in\mathfrak{u}\setminus\mathfrak{v}}\frac{1}{2^{a_{j,1}}}.

Proof. .

We have

\displaystyle\widetilde{F}^{(\varphi)}((\boldsymbol{k}_{\mathfrak{v}}+\boldsymbol{\ell}_{\mathfrak{v}},\boldsymbol{k}_{\mathfrak{u}\setminus\mathfrak{v}},\boldsymbol{0}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{s}}F(\boldsymbol{x}){\mathrm{w}al}_{(\boldsymbol{k}_{\mathfrak{v}}+\boldsymbol{\ell}_{\mathfrak{v}},\boldsymbol{k}_{\mathfrak{u}\setminus\mathfrak{v}},\boldsymbol{0})}^{(\varphi)}(\boldsymbol{x})\varphi(\boldsymbol{x})\,{\mathrm{d}}\boldsymbol{x}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{s}}F(\boldsymbol{x}){\mathrm{w}al}_{\boldsymbol{k}_{\mathfrak{v}}+\boldsymbol{\ell}_{\mathfrak{v}}}^{(\varphi)}(\boldsymbol{x}_{\mathfrak{v}}){\mathrm{w}al}_{\boldsymbol{k}_{\mathfrak{u}\setminus\mathfrak{v}}}^{(\varphi)}(\boldsymbol{x}_{\mathfrak{u}\setminus\mathfrak{v}})\varphi(\boldsymbol{x})\,{\mathrm{d}}\boldsymbol{x}.

According to (
3.1
) we obtain

{\mathrm{w}al}_{\boldsymbol{k}_{\mathfrak{v}}+\boldsymbol{\ell}_{\mathfrak{v}}}^{(\varphi)}(\boldsymbol{x}_{\mathfrak{v}})=\frac{1}{2^{\sum_{j\in\mathfrak{v}}a_{j,2}/2}}{\mathrm{w}al}_{\boldsymbol{\ell}_{\mathfrak{v}}}^{(\varphi)}(\boldsymbol{x}_{\mathfrak{v}})\sum_{\boldsymbol{m}_{\mathfrak{v}}\in L_{\boldsymbol{k}_{\mathfrak{v}},2}}w_{\boldsymbol{k}_{\mathfrak{v}},\boldsymbol{m}_{\mathfrak{v}},2}^{(\varphi)}(\boldsymbol{x}_{\mathfrak{v}})

and, by definition,
{\mathrm{w}al}_{\boldsymbol{k}_{\mathfrak{u}\setminus\mathfrak{v}}}^{(\varphi)}(\boldsymbol{x}_{\mathfrak{u}\setminus\mathfrak{v}})=w^{(\varphi)}_{\boldsymbol{k}_{\mathfrak{u}\setminus\mathfrak{v}},0,2}(\boldsymbol{x}_{\mathfrak{u}\setminus\mathfrak{v}})
.
This leads to

\displaystyle\widetilde{F}^{(\varphi)}((\boldsymbol{k}_{\mathfrak{v}}+\boldsymbol{\ell}_{\mathfrak{v}},\boldsymbol{k}_{\mathfrak{u}\setminus\mathfrak{v}},\boldsymbol{0}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{\sum_{j\in\mathfrak{v}}a_{j,2}/2}}\sum_{\boldsymbol{m}_{\mathfrak{v}}\in L_{\boldsymbol{k}_{\mathfrak{v}},2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{s}}F(\boldsymbol{x}){\mathrm{w}al}_{\boldsymbol{\ell}_{\mathfrak{v}}}^{(\varphi)}(\boldsymbol{x}_{\mathfrak{v}})w_{\boldsymbol{k}_{\mathfrak{v}},\boldsymbol{m}_{\mathfrak{v}},2}^{(\varphi)}(\boldsymbol{x}_{\mathfrak{v}})w^{(\varphi)}_{\boldsymbol{k}_{\mathfrak{u}\setminus\mathfrak{v}},0,2}(\boldsymbol{x}_{\mathfrak{u}\setminus\mathfrak{v}})\varphi(\boldsymbol{x})\,{\mathrm{d}}\boldsymbol{x}.

For
j\in\mathfrak{v}
consider

{\mathrm{w}al}_{\ell_{j}}^{(\varphi)}(x_{j})w_{k_{j},m_{j},2}^{(\varphi)}(x_{j})={\mathrm{w}al}_{\ell_{j}}(\Phi(x_{j}))w_{k_{j},m_{j},2}(\Phi(x_{j}))={\mathrm{w}al}_{\ell_{j}}(y_{j})w_{k_{j},m_{j},2}(y_{j}),

where we use the shorthand notation
y_{j}:=\Phi(x_{j})
. The support of
w_{k_{j},m_{j},2}
is the interval
I(m_{j},a_{j,2})=[\frac{m_{j}}{2^{a_{j,2}}},\frac{m_{j}+1}{2^{a_{j,2}}})
. Since
\ell_{j}\in\{0,1,\ldots,2^{a_{j,2}}-1\}
it follows that
{\mathrm{w}al}_{\ell_{j}}(y_{j})
is constant on this interval with value
{\mathrm{w}al}_{\ell_{j}}(m_{j}/2^{a_{j,2}})
. Hence

{\mathrm{w}al}_{\ell_{j}}^{(\varphi)}(x_{j})w_{k_{j},m_{j},2}^{(\varphi)}(x_{j})={\mathrm{w}al}_{\ell_{j}}\left(\frac{m_{j}}{2^{a_{j,2}}}\right)w_{k_{j},m_{j},2}^{(\varphi)}(x_{j}).

This gives

\displaystyle\widetilde{F}^{(\varphi)}((\boldsymbol{k}_{\mathfrak{v}}+\boldsymbol{\ell}_{\mathfrak{v}},\boldsymbol{k}_{\mathfrak{u}\setminus\mathfrak{v}},\boldsymbol{0}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{\sum_{j\in\mathfrak{v}}a_{j,2}/2}}\sum_{\boldsymbol{m}_{\mathfrak{v}}\in L_{\boldsymbol{k}_{\mathfrak{v}},2}}\left(\prod_{j\in\mathfrak{v}}{\mathrm{w}al}_{\ell_{j}}\left(\frac{m_{j}}{2^{a_{j,2}}}\right)\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{s}}F(\boldsymbol{x})w_{\boldsymbol{k}_{\mathfrak{v}},\boldsymbol{m}_{\mathfrak{v}},2}^{(\varphi)}(\boldsymbol{x}_{\mathfrak{v}})w^{(\varphi)}_{\boldsymbol{k}_{\mathfrak{u}\setminus\mathfrak{v}},0,2}(\boldsymbol{x}_{\mathfrak{u}\setminus\mathfrak{v}})\varphi(\boldsymbol{x})\,{\mathrm{d}}\boldsymbol{x}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{\sum_{j\in\mathfrak{v}}a_{j,2}/2}}\sum_{\boldsymbol{m}_{\mathfrak{v}}\in L_{\boldsymbol{k}_{\mathfrak{v}},2}}\left(\prod_{j\in\mathfrak{v}}{\mathrm{w}al}_{\ell_{j}}\left(\frac{m_{j}}{2^{a_{j,2}}}\right)\right)\widehat{F}^{(\varphi)}((\boldsymbol{k}_{\mathfrak{u}},\boldsymbol{0}),(\boldsymbol{m}_{\mathfrak{v}},\boldsymbol{0})).

Taking the absolute value, applying the triangle inequality and using Lemma
4
we obtain

\displaystyle|\widetilde{F}^{(\varphi)}((\boldsymbol{k}_{\mathfrak{v}}+\boldsymbol{\ell}_{\mathfrak{v}},\boldsymbol{k}_{\mathfrak{u}\setminus\mathfrak{v}},\boldsymbol{0}))|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{\sum_{j\in\mathfrak{v}}a_{j,2}/2}}\sum_{\boldsymbol{m}_{\mathfrak{v}}\in L_{\boldsymbol{k}_{\mathfrak{v}},2}}|\widehat{F}^{(\varphi)}((\boldsymbol{k}_{\mathfrak{u}},\boldsymbol{0}),(\boldsymbol{m}_{\mathfrak{v}},\boldsymbol{0}))|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{1}^{|\mathfrak{u}|}\,\|F\|_{\mathfrak{u},\mathfrak{v}}\prod_{j\in\mathfrak{v}}\frac{1}{2^{a_{j,1}+\beta a_{j,2}}}\prod_{j\in\mathfrak{u}\setminus\mathfrak{v}}\frac{1}{2^{a_{j,1}}},

as claimed.
∎

5
Numerical integration using polynomial lattice rules

In the present section, we return to the main aim of this work, the numerical
\varphi
-weighted integration of functions
F
over
\mathbb{R}^{s}
by means of quasi-Monte Carlo rules of the form (
2.2
). Throughout this section we assume the validity of Assumption
1
.

5.1
Error analysis for arbitrary underlying nodes

Let
\mathcal{P}=\{\boldsymbol{x}_{0},\boldsymbol{x}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{x}_{2^{m}-1}\}
be a
2^{m}
-element point set in
(0,1)^{s}
and approximate the
\varphi
-weighted integral
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{s}}F(\boldsymbol{x})\varphi(\boldsymbol{x})\,{\mathrm{d}}\boldsymbol{x}
with the QMC-rule (
2.2
) that is based on this point set. For the error of this approximation we have

\displaystyle{\mathrm{e}rr}(F;\varphi;\mathcal{P})=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{m}}\sum_{n=0}^{2^{m}-1}F(\Phi^{-1}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n}))-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{s}}F(\boldsymbol{x})\varphi(\boldsymbol{x})\,{\mathrm{d}}\boldsymbol{x}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{s}\setminus\{\boldsymbol{0}\}}\widetilde{F}^{(\varphi)}(\boldsymbol{k})\frac{1}{2^{m}}\sum_{n=0}^{2^{m}-1}{\mathrm{w}al}_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{(\varphi)}(\Phi^{-1}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\emptyset\not=\mathfrak{u}\subseteq[s]}\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}_{\mathfrak{u}}\in\mathbb{N}^{|\mathfrak{u}|}}\widetilde{F}^{(\varphi)}((\boldsymbol{k}_{\mathfrak{u}},\boldsymbol{0}))\frac{1}{2^{m}}\sum_{n=0}^{2^{m}-1}{\mathrm{w}al}_{(\boldsymbol{k}_{\mathfrak{u}},\boldsymbol{0})}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n}).

Now we distinguish the entries of
\boldsymbol{k}_{\mathfrak{u}}\in\mathbb{N}^{|\mathfrak{u}|}
which are perfect powers of 2 from those which are not. Let
\mathfrak{v}\subseteq\mathfrak{u}
be the set of coordinates
j
, where
k_{j}
has at least two digits in its binary expansion. Accordingly,
\mathfrak{u}\setminus\mathfrak{v}
is the set of coordinates
j
which are perfect powers of two. With this notation we have

\displaystyle{\mathrm{e}rr}(F;\varphi;\mathcal{P})=

\displaystyle\sum_{\emptyset\not=\mathfrak{u}\subseteq[s]}\sum_{\mathfrak{v}\subseteq\mathfrak{u}}\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}_{\mathfrak{v}}\in(N_{2}\setminus N_{1})^{|\mathfrak{v}|}}\sum_{\boldsymbol{\ell}_{\mathfrak{v}}\in\bigotimes_{j\in\mathfrak{v}}L_{k_{j},2}}\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}_{\mathfrak{u}\setminus\mathfrak{v}}\in(N_{1}\setminus N_{0})^{|\mathfrak{u}|-|\mathfrak{v}|}}\widetilde{F}^{(\varphi)}((\boldsymbol{k}_{\mathfrak{v}}+\boldsymbol{\ell}_{\mathfrak{v}},\boldsymbol{k}_{\mathfrak{u}\setminus\mathfrak{v}},\boldsymbol{0}))

\displaystyle\times\frac{1}{2^{m}}\sum_{n=0}^{2^{m}-1}{\mathrm{w}al}_{((\boldsymbol{k}_{\mathfrak{v}}+\boldsymbol{\ell}_{\mathfrak{v}},\boldsymbol{k}_{\mathfrak{u}\setminus\mathfrak{v}},\boldsymbol{0}))}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n}).

Now we take the absolute value and apply the triangle inequality and Lemma
5
to obtain

\displaystyle|{\mathrm{e}rr}(F;\varphi;\mathcal{P})|\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{\emptyset\not=\mathfrak{u}\subseteq[s]}\sum_{\mathfrak{v}\subseteq\mathfrak{u}}C_{1}^{|\mathfrak{u}|}\,\|F\|_{\mathfrak{u},\mathfrak{v}}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}_{\mathfrak{v}}\in(N_{2}\setminus N_{1})^{|\mathfrak{v}|}}\sum_{\boldsymbol{\ell}_{\mathfrak{v}}\in\bigotimes_{j\in\mathfrak{v}}L_{k_{j},2}}\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}_{\mathfrak{u}\setminus\mathfrak{v}}\in(N_{1}\setminus N_{0})^{|\mathfrak{u}|-|\mathfrak{v}|}}\left[\prod_{j\in\mathfrak{v}}\frac{1}{2^{a_{j,1}+\beta a_{j,2}}}\right]\left[\prod_{j\in\mathfrak{u}\setminus\mathfrak{v}}\frac{1}{2^{a_{j,1}}}\right]

\displaystyle\times\left|\frac{1}{2^{m}}\sum_{n=0}^{2^{m}-1}{\mathrm{w}al}_{((\boldsymbol{k}_{\mathfrak{v}}+\boldsymbol{\ell}_{\mathfrak{v}},\boldsymbol{k}_{\mathfrak{u}\setminus\mathfrak{v}},\boldsymbol{0}))}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n})\right|,

where for
j\in\mathfrak{v}
we denote as usual

and for
i\in\mathfrak{u}\setminus\mathfrak{v}
we use
k_{i}=2^{a_{i,1}}
.
Introducing positive weights
\gamma_{\mathfrak{u}}
we obtain

\displaystyle|{\mathrm{e}rr}(F;\varphi;\mathcal{P})|\leq

\displaystyle\left(\max_{\emptyset\not=\mathfrak{u}\subseteq[s]\atop\mathfrak{v}\subseteq\mathfrak{u}}\frac{1}{\gamma_{\mathfrak{u}}}\|F\|_{\mathfrak{u},\mathfrak{v}}\right)\mathcal{E}_{\boldsymbol{\gamma},\beta}(\mathcal{P})=\|F\|_{\boldsymbol{\gamma},\varphi,\psi,\omega}\,\mathcal{E}_{\boldsymbol{\gamma},\beta}(\mathcal{P}),

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\boldsymbol{\gamma},\beta}(\mathcal{P}):=

\displaystyle\sum_{\emptyset\not=\mathfrak{u}\subseteq[s]}\gamma_{\mathfrak{u}}\sum_{\mathfrak{v}\subseteq\mathfrak{u}}C_{1}^{|\mathfrak{u}|}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}_{\mathfrak{v}}\in(N_{2}\setminus N_{1})^{|\mathfrak{v}|}}\sum_{\boldsymbol{\ell}_{\mathfrak{v}}\in\bigotimes_{j\in\mathfrak{v}}L_{k_{j},2}}\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}_{\mathfrak{u}\setminus\mathfrak{v}}\in(N_{1}\setminus N_{0})^{|\mathfrak{u}|-|\mathfrak{v}|}}\left[\prod_{j\in\mathfrak{v}}\frac{1}{2^{a_{j,1}+\beta a_{j,2}}}\right]\left[\prod_{i\in\mathfrak{u}\setminus\mathfrak{v}}\frac{1}{2^{a_{i,1}}}\right]

\displaystyle\times\left|\frac{1}{2^{m}}\sum_{n=0}^{2^{m}-1}{\mathrm{w}al}_{((\boldsymbol{k}_{\mathfrak{v}}+\boldsymbol{\ell}_{\mathfrak{v}},\boldsymbol{k}_{\mathfrak{u}\setminus\mathfrak{v}},\boldsymbol{0}))}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n})\right|.

We introduce a weight function on both integers and integer vectors that yield a convenient representation of
\mathcal{E}_{\boldsymbol{\gamma},\beta}(\mathcal{P})
. For
k=2^{a_{1}}+2^{a_{2}}+\ell
with
a_{1}>a_{2}\geq 0
and
\ell\in\{0,1,\ldots,2^{a_{2}}-1\}
let

\mu_{\beta}(k):=a_{1}+\beta a_{2}.

For
k=2^{a_{1}}
with
a_{1}\geq 0
let

\mu_{\beta}(k):=a_{1}.

Further, let
\mu_{\beta}(0):=0
. For
\boldsymbol{k}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
let

\mu_{\beta}(\boldsymbol{k}):=\mu_{\beta}(k_{1})+\cdots+\mu_{\beta}(k_{s}).

Then we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\boldsymbol{\gamma},\beta}(\mathcal{P})=\sum_{\emptyset\not=\mathfrak{u}\subseteq[s]}\gamma_{\mathfrak{u}}\,(2\,C_{1})^{|\mathfrak{u}|}\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}_{\mathfrak{u}}\in\mathbb{N}^{|\mathfrak{u}|}}\frac{1}{2^{\mu_{\beta}(\boldsymbol{k}_{\mathfrak{u}})}}\left|\frac{1}{2^{m}}\sum_{n=0}^{2^{m}-1}{\mathrm{w}al}_{(\boldsymbol{k}_{\mathfrak{u}},\boldsymbol{0})}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n})\right|.

(5.1)

We have now established the following proposition.

Proposition 1
.

Let
F\in\mathcal{F}_{\boldsymbol{\gamma},\varphi,\psi,\omega}
and assume that
\psi
and
\omega
satisfy Assumption
1
. Let
\mathcal{P}
be a
2^{m}
-element point set in
(0,1)^{s}
. Then we have

|{\mathrm{e}rr}(F;\varphi;\mathcal{P})|\leq\|F\|_{\boldsymbol{\gamma},\varphi,\psi,\omega}\,\mathcal{E}_{\boldsymbol{\gamma},\beta}(\mathcal{P})

where
\mathcal{E}_{\boldsymbol{\gamma},\beta}(\mathcal{P})
is given by (

). In particular, for the worst-case error (

) we obtain

5.2
Interlaced polynomial lattice rules of order two

In this section we discuss a construction of polynomial lattice rules of order two which can achieve optimal convergence rates of (
5.1
). We consider polynomial lattice over
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, the finite field of order
2
, see
Niederreiter (1992)
. We identify
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
with
\{0,1\}
equipped with arithmetic operations modulo
2
. Furthermore, let
\mathbb{Z}_{2}[x]
be the ring of polynomials over
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
and let
\mathbb{Z}_{2}((x^{-1}))
be the ring of Laurent series over
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
whose elements are of the form
L(x)=\sum_{\ell=w}^{\infty}t_{\ell}x^{-\ell}
for
w\in\mathbb{Z}
and coefficients
t_{\ell}\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}
. For
m\in\mathbb{N}
let

G_{m}:=\{q\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}[x]\setminus\{0\}:\deg(q)<m\}.

For every integer
n\in\{0,1,\ldots,2^{m}-1\}
, let
n=\eta_{0}+\eta_{1}2+\cdots+\eta_{m-1}2^{m-1}
be the binary expansion of
n
with digits
\eta_{i}
in
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, and associate with
n
the polynomial

n(x)=\sum_{r=0}^{m-1}\eta_{r}x^{r}\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}[x].

Furthermore, we denote by
v_{m}
the map from
\mathbb{Z}_{2}((x^{-1}))
to the set of dyadic rationals
\{a2^{-m}:a\in\{0,1,\ldots,2^{m}-1\}\}
, which form a subset of
[0,1)
, defined by

v_{m}\left(\sum_{\ell=w}^{\infty}t_{\ell}x^{-\ell}\right)=\sum_{\ell=\max(1,w)}^{m}\frac{t_{\ell}}{2^{\ell}}.

Definition 6

(Polynomial lattice rules)
.

For any positive integer
m
, choose an irreducible polynomial
P\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}[x]
of degree
m
; we refer to
P
as the
modulus
. For a prescribed dimension
s\in\mathbb{N}
, select polynomials
q_{1},\ldots,q_{s}\in G_{m}
and define

\boldsymbol{q}=\boldsymbol{q}(x)=(q_{1}(x),\dots,q_{s}(x))\in G^{s}_{m}.

The vector
\boldsymbol{q}
is called the
generating vector
. Then, a (classical) polynomial lattice rule with
2^{m}
points in
s
dimensions is a QMC rule that is based on the point set
S_{P,m,s}(\boldsymbol{q})
which consists of the elements

\boldsymbol{y}_{n}=\left(v_{m}\left(\frac{n(x)q_{1}(x)}{P(x)}\right),\dots,v_{m}\left(\frac{n(x)q_{s}(x)}{P(x)}\right)\right)\in[0,1)^{s},\quad n\in\{0,1,\dots,2^{m}-1\},

for some given modulus
P
and generating vector
\boldsymbol{q}\in G^{s}_{m}
.

Polynomial lattice rules are an important type of QMC rule and constitute a polynomial variant of classical lattice rules with many useful properties. For a detailed exposition, we refer to Chapter 10 of
Dick and Pillichshammer (2010)
. In our analysis, we will need the following character property, whose proof is a combination of Lemma 4.75 and Lemma 10.6 in
Dick and Pillichshammer (2010)
.

Lemma 6
.

Let
S_{P,m,s}(\boldsymbol{q}):=\{\boldsymbol{y}_{0},\boldsymbol{y}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{y}_{2^{m}-1}\}
be the quadrature points of a polynomial lattice rule with modulus
P\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}[x]
,
\deg(P)=m
, and generating vector
\boldsymbol{q}=(q_{1},\ldots,q_{s})\in G_{m}^{s}
. Then, for
\boldsymbol{k}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{s})\in\{0,1,\ldots,2^{m}-1\}^{s}
we have

\sum_{n=0}^{2^{m}-1}{\mathrm{w}al}_{\boldsymbol{k}}(\boldsymbol{y}_{n})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}2^{m}&\mbox{if $k_{1}(x)q_{1}(x)+\cdots+k_{s}(x)q_{s}(x)\equiv 0\pmod{P(x)}$,}\\
0&\mbox{otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

In the following we define interlaced polynomial lattice rules
(
Dick et al., 2014
;
Goda, 2015
)
, belonging to the family of higher order digital nets, which were first introduced in
Dick (2008)
.

Definition 7

(Interlaced polynomial lattice rules)
.

Define the digit interlacing function with interlacing factor 2 by

D:[0,1)^{2}\to[0,1),\quad(x_{1},x_{2})\mapsto\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\xi_{1,i}}{2^{2i-1}}+\frac{\xi_{2,i}}{2^{2i}}\right),

where
x_{j}=\xi_{j,1}2^{-1}+\xi_{j,2}2^{-2}+\cdots
with binary digits
\xi_{j,i}
in
\{0,1\}
for
j\in\{1,2\}
. We also define such a function for vectors by setting

D:[0,1)^{2s}\to[0,1)^{s},\quad(x_{1},\dots,x_{2s})\mapsto\left(D(x_{1},x_{2}),\dots,D(x_{2s-1},x_{2s})\right).

Then, an interlaced polynomial lattice rule of order 2 with
2^{m}
points in
s
dimensions is a QMC rule using
D(S_{P,m,2s}(\boldsymbol{q}))
as quadrature points, for some given modulus
P\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}[x]
of degree
m
and and generating vector
\boldsymbol{q}\in G^{2s}_{m}
.

Unfortunately, any polynomial lattice rule as well as any interlaced polynomial lattice rule contains the origin as quadrature point. However, we require node sets in the open unit cube
(0,1)^{s}
; see Proposition
1
. To avoid this problem, observe that the coordinates
x_{n}^{(j)}
, where
j\in[s]
and
n\in\{0,1,\ldots,2^{m}-1\}
, of the nodes
\boldsymbol{x}_{n}:=D(\boldsymbol{y}_{n})
of an interlaced polynomial lattice rule of order 2 with
2^{m}
points in dimension
s
are dyadic rationals of the form
x_{n}^{(j)}=\xi_{n,1}^{(j)}2^{-1}+\xi_{n,2}^{(j)}2^{-2}+\cdots+\xi_{n,2m}^{(j)}2^{-2m}
with dyadic digits
\xi_{n,i}^{(j)}\in\{0,1\}
. Thus, we simply add the (digital) shift
2^{-2m-1}
, resulting in

x_{n,i}^{(j)}+\frac{1}{2^{2m+1}}=\frac{\xi_{n,1}^{(j)}}{2}+\frac{\xi_{n,2}^{(j)}}{2^{2}}+\cdots+\frac{\xi_{n,2m}^{(j)}}{2^{2m}}+\frac{1}{2^{2m+1}},

to every coordinate of every point of the interlaced polynomial lattice rule. If
\mathcal{P}_{m,s}:=D(S_{P,m,2s}(\boldsymbol{q}))
denotes the interlaced polynomial lattice point set, then we denote by
\mathcal{P}_{m,s}^{{\mathrm{s}h}}
the
shifted interlaced polynomial lattice point set

\mathcal{P}_{m,s}+2^{-2m-1}\boldsymbol{1}
thereof, where
\boldsymbol{1}
is the all-1 vector in dimension
s
. Obviously,
\mathcal{P}_{m,s}^{{\mathrm{s}h}}
belongs to
(0,1)^{s}
, and thus avoids the origin.

It is sometimes useful to also add a digital shift to the earlier digits. To do so, we need digital addition modulo
2
. For
x=x_{1}2^{-1}+x_{2}2^{-2}+\cdots
and
y=y_{1}2^{-1}+y_{2}2^{-2}+\cdots
, we define
z=x\oplus y
as
z=z_{1}2^{-1}+z_{2}2^{-2}+\cdots
by
z_{i}:=x_{i}+y_{i}\pmod{2}
for
i\in\mathbb{N}
. For vectors
\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y}
we define
\boldsymbol{x}\oplus\boldsymbol{y}:=(x_{1}\oplus y_{1},\ldots,x_{s}\oplus y_{s})
. Let
\boldsymbol{\delta}\in[0,1)^{s}
. Then we define the digitally shifted polynomial lattice rule
\mathcal{P}_{m,s}^{{\mathrm{s}h}}:=\{\boldsymbol{x}\oplus\boldsymbol{\delta}:\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathcal{P}_{m,s}\}
. For
\boldsymbol{\delta}\in(0,1)^{s}
such that
\boldsymbol{\delta}2^{2m}\notin\mathbb{Z}
, we obtain that none of the components of
\mathcal{P}_{m,s}^{{\mathrm{s}h}}\oplus\boldsymbol{\delta}
are
0
and hence all the components of all points avoid the origin. Note that for
\boldsymbol{\delta}=2^{-2m-1}\boldsymbol{1}
we have
\mathcal{P}_{m,s}+\boldsymbol{\delta}=\mathcal{P}_{m,s}\oplus\boldsymbol{\delta}
.

We also extend the definition of the interlacing function
D:[0,1)^{2}\rightarrow[0,1)
to nonnegative integers by setting

E:\mathbb{N}_{0}^{2}\to\mathbb{N}_{0},\quad(\ell_{1},\ell_{2})\mapsto\sum_{a=0}^{\infty}(\ell_{1,a}2^{2a}+\ell_{2,a}2^{2a+1}),

where
\ell_{j}=\ell_{j,0}+\ell_{j,1}2+\ell_{j,2}2^{2}+\cdots
for
j\in\{1,2\}
. We also extend this function to vectors via

E:\mathbb{N}_{0}^{2s}\to\mathbb{N}_{0}^{s},\quad(\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{2s})\mapsto\left(E(\ell_{1},\ell_{2}),\dots,E(\ell_{2s-1},\ell_{2s})\right).

Note that for
k,\ell\in\mathbb{N}
the
j
-th digit of
k
is the digit at position
2j-1
in

E(k,\ell)
and the
j
-th digit of
\ell
is the digit at position
2j
in
E(k,\ell)
.

Lemma 7
.

For
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
let

\mu(k):=\begin{cases}0&\mbox{if }k=0,\\
\lfloor\log_{2}(k)\rfloor&\mbox{if }k\in\mathbb{N}.\end{cases}

Then, for
\beta\in(0,1]
we have
\mu_{\beta}(E(0,0))=0
and, for
(k_{1},k_{2})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{2}\setminus\{(0,0)\}
, we have

\mu_{\beta}(E(k_{1},k_{2}))\geq(1+\beta)\,(\mu(k_{1})+\mu(k_{2})-1).

Proof. .

First, assume that
k_{1}=2^{a_{1}}+\ell>k_{2}=2^{b_{1}}+m>0
. Then
a_{1}\geq b_{1}
and

\mu_{\beta}(E(k_{1},k_{2}))\geq 2a_{1}-1+\beta 2b_{1}\geq(1+\beta)(a_{1}+b_{1})-1\geq(1+\beta)\,(\mu(k_{1})+\mu(k_{2})-1).

The proof is similar if
k_{1}\leq k_{2}
. Now consider
k_{2}=0
. Then
\mu(k_{2})=0
, and for
k_{1}=2^{a_{1}}+2^{a_{2}}+\ell
, we have

\mu_{\beta}(E(k_{1},k_{2}))=2a_{1}-1+\beta(2a_{2}-1)\geq(1+\beta)\mu(k_{1})-(1+\beta),

while for
k_{1}=2^{a_{1}}
we have

\mu_{\beta}(E(k_{1},k_{2}))=2a_{1}-1\geq 2\mu(k_{1})-(1+\beta).

The case where
k_{1}=0
and
k_{2}>0
is similar. The case where
k_{1}=k_{2}=0
is obvious.
∎

For a given set
\emptyset\neq\mathfrak{v}\subseteq[2s]
, we define

\mathfrak{u}(\mathfrak{v}):=\{\lceil j/2\rceil:j\in\mathfrak{v}\}\subseteq[s],

where each element appears only once as is typical for sets. The set
\mathfrak{u}(\mathfrak{v})
can be viewed as an indicator on whether the set
\mathfrak{v}
includes any element from each block of
2
components from
[2s]
. In the following, we will use the fact that
|\mathfrak{u}(\mathfrak{v})|\leq|\mathfrak{v}|\leq 2|\mathfrak{u}(\mathfrak{v})|
.

For a vector
\boldsymbol{k}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{d})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
of arbitrary length
d\in\mathbb{N}
, put
\mu(\boldsymbol{k}):=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\mu(k_{j})
. We now apply Lemma
7
to the vector
(\boldsymbol{\ell}_{\mathfrak{v}},\boldsymbol{0})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{2s}
in blocks of
2
components.
For
\boldsymbol{\ell}_{\mathfrak{v}}\in\mathbb{N}^{2|\mathfrak{v}|}
, noting that
\mu_{\beta}(E(\boldsymbol{0}))=0
, we obtain

\mu_{\beta}(E(\boldsymbol{\ell}_{\mathfrak{v}},\boldsymbol{0}))\geq(1+\beta)(\mu(\boldsymbol{\ell}_{\mathfrak{v}})-|\mathfrak{u}(\mathfrak{v})|).

(5.2)

5.3
Component-by-component construction

We summarize the fundamental choice of underlying integration points in the following analysis:

•

Let
m,s\in\mathbb{N}
and let
S_{P,m,2s}(\boldsymbol{q}):=\{\boldsymbol{y}_{0},\boldsymbol{y}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{y}_{2^{m}-1}\}
in
[0,1)^{2s}
be the nodes of a polynomial lattice rule in dimension
2s
with modulus
P\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}[x]
of degree
m
and generating vector
\boldsymbol{q}\in G_{m}^{2s}
.

•

Let
\mathcal{P}_{m,s}:=D(S_{P,m,2s}(\boldsymbol{q}))=\{D(\boldsymbol{y}_{0}),D(\boldsymbol{y}_{1}),\ldots,D(\boldsymbol{y}_{2^{m}-1})\}
in
[0,1)^{s}
be the interlaced version of
S_{P,m,2s}(\boldsymbol{q})
.

•

Let
\mathcal{P}_{m,s}^{{\mathrm{s}h}}:=\mathcal{P}_{m,s}+2^{-2m-1}\boldsymbol{1}
or
\mathcal{P}_{m,s}^{{\mathrm{s}h}}:=\mathcal{P}_{m,s}\oplus\boldsymbol{\delta}
be the shifted version of
\mathcal{P}_{m,s}
, where
\boldsymbol{\delta}
is chosen such that
\mathcal{P}_{m,s}\subset(0,1)^{s}
.

Then we study the error bound
\mathcal{E}_{\boldsymbol{\gamma},\beta}(\mathcal{P}_{m,s}^{{\mathrm{s}h}})
from Proposition
1
. We have

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\boldsymbol{\gamma},\beta}(\mathcal{P}_{m,s}^{{\mathrm{s}h}})=\sum_{\emptyset\not=\mathfrak{u}\subseteq[s]}\gamma_{\mathfrak{u}}\,(2\,C_{1})^{|\mathfrak{u}|}\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}_{\mathfrak{u}}\in\mathbb{N}^{|\mathfrak{u}|}}\frac{1}{2^{\mu_{\beta}(\boldsymbol{k}_{\mathfrak{u}})}}\left|\frac{1}{2^{m}}\sum_{n=0}^{2^{m}-1}{\mathrm{w}al}_{(\boldsymbol{k}_{\mathfrak{u}},\boldsymbol{0})}(D(\boldsymbol{y}_{n})+2^{-2m-1}\boldsymbol{1})\right|.

Note that

{\mathrm{w}al}_{(\boldsymbol{k}_{\mathfrak{u}},\boldsymbol{0})}(D(\boldsymbol{y}_{n})\oplus\boldsymbol{\delta})={\mathrm{w}al}_{(\boldsymbol{k}_{\mathfrak{u}},\boldsymbol{0})}(D(\boldsymbol{y}_{n})){\mathrm{w}al}_{(\boldsymbol{k}_{\mathfrak{u}},\boldsymbol{0})}(\boldsymbol{\delta})

and
|{\mathrm{w}al}_{(\boldsymbol{k}_{\mathfrak{u}},\boldsymbol{0})}(\boldsymbol{\delta})|=1
. Hence, applying the absolute value, we get rid of the dependence on the shift. This way we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\boldsymbol{\gamma},\beta}(\mathcal{P}_{m,s}^{{\mathrm{s}h}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\emptyset\not=\mathfrak{v}\subseteq[2s]}\gamma_{\mathfrak{u}(\mathfrak{v})}\,(2\,C_{1})^{|\mathfrak{u}(\mathfrak{v})|}\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}_{\mathfrak{v}}\in\mathbb{N}^{|\mathfrak{v}|}}\frac{1}{2^{\mu_{\beta}(E(\boldsymbol{k}_{\mathfrak{v}},\boldsymbol{0}))}}\left|\frac{1}{2^{m}}\sum_{n=0}^{2^{m}-1}{\mathrm{w}al}_{E(\boldsymbol{k}_{\mathfrak{v}},\boldsymbol{0})}(D(\boldsymbol{y}_{n}))\right|

\displaystyle=\sum_{\emptyset\not=\mathfrak{v}\subseteq[2s]}\gamma_{\mathfrak{u}(\mathfrak{v})}\,(2\,C_{1})^{|\mathfrak{u}(\mathfrak{v})|}\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}_{\mathfrak{v}}\in\mathbb{N}^{|\mathfrak{v}|}}\frac{1}{2^{\mu_{\beta}(E(\boldsymbol{k}_{\mathfrak{v}},\boldsymbol{0}))}}\left|\frac{1}{2^{m}}\sum_{n=0}^{2^{m}-1}{\mathrm{w}al}_{(\boldsymbol{k}_{\mathfrak{v}},\boldsymbol{0})}(\boldsymbol{y}_{n})\right|,

where
(\boldsymbol{k}_{\mathfrak{v}},\boldsymbol{0})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{2s}
and where we used that for a general
\boldsymbol{k}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{2s}
and
\boldsymbol{x}\in[0,1)^{2s}
we have
{\mathrm{w}al}_{E(\boldsymbol{k})}(D(\boldsymbol{x}))={\mathrm{w}al}_{\boldsymbol{k}}(\boldsymbol{x})
. Using the estimate (
5.2
) and afterwards Lemma
6
we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\boldsymbol{\gamma},\beta}(\mathcal{P}_{m,s}^{{\mathrm{s}h}})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\emptyset\not=\mathfrak{v}\subseteq[2s]}\gamma_{\mathfrak{u}(\mathfrak{v})}\,(2\,C_{1})^{|\mathfrak{u}(\mathfrak{v})|}2^{(1+\beta)|\mathfrak{u}(\mathfrak{v})|}\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}_{\mathfrak{v}}\in\mathbb{N}^{|\mathfrak{v}|}}\frac{1}{2^{(1+\beta)\mu(\boldsymbol{k}_{\mathfrak{v}},\boldsymbol{0})}}\left|\frac{1}{2^{m}}\sum_{n=0}^{2^{m}-1}{\mathrm{w}al}_{(\boldsymbol{k}_{\mathfrak{v}},\boldsymbol{0})}(\boldsymbol{y}_{n})\right|

\displaystyle=\sum_{\emptyset\not=\mathfrak{v}\subseteq[2s]}\gamma_{\mathfrak{u}(\mathfrak{v})}\,\left(C_{1}\,2^{2+\beta}\right)^{|\mathfrak{u}(\mathfrak{v})|}\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}_{\mathfrak{v}}\in S^{\perp}_{\mathfrak{v}}(\boldsymbol{q})}\frac{1}{2^{(1+\beta)\mu(\boldsymbol{k}_{\mathfrak{v}},\boldsymbol{0})}}

(5.3)

\displaystyle=:B_{s}(\boldsymbol{q}),

where

S^{\perp}_{\mathfrak{v}}(\boldsymbol{q}):=\left\{\boldsymbol{k}_{\mathfrak{v}}\in\mathbb{N}^{|\mathfrak{v}|}:\sum_{j\in\mathfrak{v}}{\mathrm{t}r}_{m}(k_{j})(x)q_{j}(x)\equiv 0\pmod{P(x)}\right\},

where, for
k=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\kappa_{i}2^{i}
in
\mathbb{N}
with binary digits
\kappa_{i}\in\{0,1\}
, which become eventually 0 from a certain index on, the

-digit truncation is defined as
{\mathrm{t}r}_{m}(k):=\kappa_{0}+\kappa_{1}2+\cdots+\kappa_{m-1}2^{m-1}
, which belongs to
\{0,1,\ldots,2^{m}-1\}
. Remember that
C_{1}=\max(C,\tfrac{1}{2})
and

is the constant from Assumption

.

Thus, we use
B_{s}(\boldsymbol{q})
as the error criterion for a (shifted) interlaced polynomial lattice rule that dominates the worst-case integration error

, according to Proposition

and (

). The expression for

where

and

. The results apply with some minor modifications. In particular, we can apply Theorem 3.9 in
Dick et al. (2014)
to obtain the following result.

Lemma 8

(CBC Error Bound)
.

Let
m,s\in\mathbb{N}
and let
P\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}[x]
be an irreducible polynomial with
\deg(P)=m
. Let
\boldsymbol{\gamma}=\{\gamma_{\mathfrak{u}}\}_{\mathfrak{u}\subseteq[s]}
be positive real coordinate weights. Then a generating vector
\boldsymbol{q}^{*}=(1,q_{2}^{*},\ldots,q_{2s}^{*})\in G_{m}^{2s}
can be constructed using a component-by-component approach, minimizing
B_{s}(\boldsymbol{q}^{\ast})
in each step, such that for all
\lambda\in(\frac{1}{1+\beta},1]
we have

B_{s}(\boldsymbol{q}^{\ast})\leq\left(\frac{4}{2^{m}}\sum_{\emptyset\not=\mathfrak{u}\subseteq[s]}\gamma_{\mathfrak{u}}^{\lambda}K_{\beta,\lambda}^{|\mathfrak{u}|}\right)^{1/\lambda},

where
K_{\beta,\lambda}:=12(C_{1}/(2^{\lambda(1+\beta)}-2))^{2}
.

Proof. .

From Theorem 3.9 in
Dick et al. (2014)
we obtain

B_{s}(\boldsymbol{q}^{\ast})\leq\left(\frac{2}{2^{m}-1}\sum_{\emptyset\not=\mathfrak{v}\subseteq[2s]}\gamma_{\mathfrak{u}(\mathfrak{v})}^{\lambda}\frac{(C_{1}\,2^{2+\beta})^{\lambda|\mathfrak{u}(\mathfrak{v})|}}{(2^{(1+\beta)(\lambda+1)}-2^{2+\beta})^{|\mathfrak{v}|}}\right)^{1/\lambda},

for all
\lambda\in(\frac{1}{1+\beta},1]
. Obviously,
2/(2^{m}-1)\leq 4/2^{m}
and furthermore,

\frac{(C_{1}\,2^{2+\beta})^{\lambda|\mathfrak{u}(\mathfrak{v})|}}{(2^{(1+\beta)(\lambda+1)}-2^{2+\beta})^{|\mathfrak{v}|}}=\frac{(C_{1}\,2^{2+\beta})^{\lambda|\mathfrak{u}(\mathfrak{v})|}}{2^{(1+\beta)|\mathfrak{v}|}}\frac{1}{(2^{\lambda(1+\beta)}-2)^{|\mathfrak{v}|}}\leq J_{\beta,\lambda}^{|\mathfrak{v}|},

with
J_{\beta,\lambda}:=2\,C_{1}/(2^{\lambda(1+\beta)}-2)
. This implies

B_{s}(\boldsymbol{q}^{\ast})\leq\left(\frac{4}{2^{m}}\sum_{\emptyset\not=\mathfrak{v}\subseteq[2s]}\gamma_{\mathfrak{u}(\mathfrak{v})}^{\lambda}J_{\beta,\lambda}^{|\mathfrak{v}|}\right)^{1/\lambda}.

Now, recall that for
\emptyset\not=\mathfrak{v}\subseteq[2s]
we have
\mathfrak{u}(\mathfrak{v})=\{\lceil j/2\rceil\ :\ j\in\mathfrak{v}\}\subseteq[s]
, where each element appears only once, as is typical for sets, in particular
|\mathfrak{v}|/2\leq|\mathfrak{u}(\mathfrak{v})|\leq|\mathfrak{v}|
. Hence

\displaystyle\sum_{\emptyset\not=\mathfrak{v}\subseteq[2s]}\gamma_{\mathfrak{u}(\mathfrak{v})}^{\lambda}J_{\beta,\lambda}^{|\mathfrak{v}|}\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{\emptyset\not=\mathfrak{u}\subseteq[s]}\gamma_{\mathfrak{u}}^{\lambda}J_{\beta,\lambda}^{2|\mathfrak{u}|}\sum_{\mathfrak{v}\subseteq[2s]\atop\mathfrak{u}(\mathfrak{v})=\mathfrak{u}}1\leq\sum_{\emptyset\not=\mathfrak{u}\subseteq[s]}\gamma_{\mathfrak{u}}^{\lambda}(3J_{\beta,\lambda}^{2})^{|\mathfrak{u}|},

where we used that
\sum_{\mathfrak{v}\subseteq[2s]\atop\mathfrak{u}(\mathfrak{v})=\mathfrak{u}}1\leq 3^{|\mathfrak{u}|}
. Setting
K_{\beta,\lambda}:=3J_{\beta,\lambda}^{2}
we obtain the claimed result.

Theorem 1
.

Consider the approximation of the integral

of functions

The convergence rate is

\lambda
arbitrarily close to

. In the example in Section

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1+\frac{1}{\nu}-\frac{2}{\nu}\left(\frac{A_{\nu}\,2^{a_{2}}}{2x}\right)^{1/\nu}+\frac{1}{\nu}\left(\frac{A_{\nu}\,2^{a_{2}}}{2x}\right)^{2/\nu}

\displaystyle 2^{2a_{2}/\nu}\left(\frac{\nu+1}{\nu}+\frac{2}{\nu}\left(\frac{A_{\nu}}{2x}\right)^{1/\nu}+\frac{1}{\nu}\left(\frac{A_{\nu}}{2x}\right)^{2/\nu}\right).

Consequently,

\displaystyle\frac{x}{\left(f_{\mathrm{stu},\nu}\left(\Phi_{\mathrm{stu},\nu}^{-1}\left(\frac{x}{2^{a_{2}}}\right)\right)\right)^{\eta}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{x}{c_{\nu}^{\eta}}\left(2^{2a_{2}/\nu}\left(\frac{\nu+1}{\nu}+\frac{2}{\nu}\left(\frac{A_{\nu}}{2x}\right)^{1/\nu}+\frac{1}{\nu}\left(\frac{A_{\nu}}{2x}\right)^{2/\nu}\right)\right)^{\eta(\nu+1)/2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{a_{2}\,\frac{\nu+1}{\nu}\,\eta}\frac{x}{c_{\nu}^{\eta}}\left(\frac{\nu+1}{\nu}+\frac{2}{\nu}\left(\frac{A_{\nu}}{2x}\right)^{1/\nu}+\frac{1}{\nu}\left(\frac{A_{\nu}}{2x}\right)^{2/\nu}\right)^{\eta(\nu+1)/2}.

Returning to (

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\frac{|\widetilde{W}^{(\varphi)}_{k,m,2}(x)|}{\varphi(x)\psi(x)}\leq C_{\nu,\eta}\,\frac{1}{2^{a_{1}+a_{2}/2}}\,2^{a_{2}\,\frac{\nu+1}{\nu}\,\eta}=C_{\nu,\eta}\,\frac{2^{a_{2}/2}}{2^{a_{1}+a_{2}(1-\frac{\nu+1}{\nu}\,\eta)}},

(6.8)

for

Now we treat the second supremum from (

f_{\mathrm{stu},\nu}^{\prime}(x)=-\frac{\nu+1}{\nu}\frac{x}{1+\frac{x^{2}}{\nu}}f_{\mathrm{stu},\nu}(x)=-\frac{\nu+1}{\nu}\frac{x}{c_{\nu}^{2/(\nu+1)}}(f_{\mathrm{stu},\nu}(x))^{1+\frac{2}{\nu+1}}

Choosing

7
Application: Elliptic PDEs with log-normal random coefficients

In the following, we apply the error bounds to a partial differential equation with a finite number of random log-normal coefficients, see
Graham et al. (2015)
for the analogue case with an infinite number of random coefficients. The formulation of this problem requires many concepts and assumptions which would exceed the scope of this work. We limit ourselves to a concise presentation of the most important points so that our concrete application can be followed. A gentle overview can also be found in Appendix A of
Dick et al. (2022)
.

7.1
The PDE problem setting

Consider the diffusion problem

for some fixed

. For more background, assumptions and the case where

, see
Graham et al. (2015)
. The focus in the following is on the integration problem arising from approximating the expected value

of a linear functional

of the solution

of the PDE problem, where the expectation is taken with respect to random

(

for details). Using the same assumptions and setting as in
Graham et al. (2015)
, we show that if one uses importance sampling, then we can apply Theorem
1

1
shows a convergence order bigger than
1

1
which is also independent of the dimension.

In order to be able to apply Theorem

in each variable of a linear functional of the solution

The bound in Theorem 14 in

states that

\left\|\frac{\partial^{|\boldsymbol{\nu}|}u_{h}^{s}(\cdot,\boldsymbol{y})}{\partial\boldsymbol{y}_{\boldsymbol{\nu}}}\right\|_{V}\leq\frac{|\boldsymbol{\nu}|!}{(\ln 2)^{|\boldsymbol{\nu}|}}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{s}b_{j}^{\nu_{j}}\right)\frac{\|f\|_{V^{\prime}}}{\check{a}(\boldsymbol{y})},

, where

is an appropriate normed function space on

, equipped with

, and

is the corresponding dual space, the function

from the right-hand side in (

The integrand of interest is given by

(7.2)

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma}{\rho}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\exp\left(-\frac{x^{2}}{2}\left(1+\frac{1}{\rho^{2}}-\frac{2}{\sigma^{2}}\right)+b_{j}|x|\right)|p_{((\boldsymbol{1}_{\mathfrak{u}\setminus\mathfrak{w}},\boldsymbol{2}_{\mathfrak{w}},\boldsymbol{0})-\boldsymbol{\nu})_{j}}(x)|\,{\mathrm{d}}x.

This is finite as long as
1+\frac{1}{\rho^{2}}-\frac{2}{\sigma^{2}}>0
. This can be shown similarly as (
7.5
). We also have

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\exp\left(-\frac{x^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}/(\sigma^{2}-1)}+b_{j}|x|\right)|p_{((\boldsymbol{1}_{\mathfrak{u}\setminus\mathfrak{w}},\boldsymbol{2}_{\mathfrak{w}},\boldsymbol{0})-\boldsymbol{\nu})_{j}}(x)|\,{\mathrm{d}}x<\infty.

From here we see that
\|F_{\sigma}\|_{\mathfrak{u},\mathfrak{v}}
is indeed finite. ∎

Proof of Theorem
2
.

We apply Theorem
1
. According to Lemma
10
the norm
\left\|F_{\sigma}\right\|_{\infty,\boldsymbol{\gamma},\varphi_{\sigma},\varphi_{\rho}/\varphi_{\sigma},\varphi_{\rho}/\varphi_{\sigma}}
is finite. The assumption

is true for
\sigma>\rho\sqrt{\frac{2}{1+\rho^{2}}}
. Since we also need

, we require

(7.6)

and

8
Numerical results

In this section, we validate our theoretical developments by applying the QMC integration rule (
2.2
) to compute expected values of selected quantities of interest (QoIs) arising in two diffusion problems, where the diffusion coefficients are modelled as log-normal random fields. In both examples, we extend slightly beyond the homogeneous Dirichlet boundary condition assumption in Section
7
by considering more challenging mixed boundary conditions. In all numerical experiments, we generate the QMC point sets using the QMC4PDE package of
Nuyens
accompanying the article of
Kuo and Nuyens (2016)
, with the interlaced polynomial lattice rule of order two.

The first experiment considers the diffusion problem on a one-dimensional domain
D=[0,1]
:

-\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\Big(a(x,\omega)\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\,u(x,\omega)\Big)=f(x),

(8.1)

subject to boundary conditions

u(0,\omega)=0\quad\text{and}\quad a(1,\omega)\frac{\partial}{\partial x}u(1,\omega)=R,

(8.2)

for almost all
\omega\in\Omega
and
x\in D
. The diffusion field is defined as

a(x,\omega)=\exp\Big(a_{\ast}+\sum_{j=1}^{s}\sqrt{\mu_{j}}\,\xi_{j}(x)Y_{j}(\omega)\Big),\quad a_{\ast}=1/2,\quad\xi_{j}(x)=\cos(\pi jx),

(8.3)

where
\mu_{j}=j^{-2\alpha}
with
\alpha=1
is a sequence of decaying weights, and
\big(Y_{j}(\omega)\big)_{j=1,\ldots,s}
are i.i.d. standard normal random variables. The boundary condition on the right-hand side is chosen such that the system of equations (
8.1
)–(
8.3
) yields the explicit solution
(
Mugler and Starkloff, 2013
)

u(x,\omega)=\int_{0}^{x}\frac{1}{a(z,\omega)}\Big(R+\int_{z}^{1}f(w)\,{\mathrm{d}}w\Big)\,{\mathrm{d}}z.

Specifically, for
R=1/2
and the right-hand-side function
f(x)=x
, we obtain the solution

u(x,\omega)=\int_{0}^{x}\frac{1-z^{2}/2}{a(z,\omega)}\,{\mathrm{d}}z.

(8.4)

We define the QoI as the value of the potential function at
x=0.5
, i.e.,

F_{1}(\omega):=u(0.5,\omega).

The fast implementation of
Waldvogel (2006)
of the Clenshaw–Curtis quadrature rule
(
Clenshaw and Curtis, 1960
)
is used to evaluate the integral in (
8.4
) for each realisation of the random draw
\omega
, with absolute error tolerance
10^{-10}
.

We numerically test the convergence of interlaced polynomial lattice point sets on a range of stochastic dimensions,
s\in\{2^{2},2^{3},\ldots,2^{6}\}
. For each dimension, we use QMC point sets with increasing sizes
N\in\{2^{17},2^{18},\ldots,2^{22}\}
to verify the convergence in integrating the QoI
F_{1}(\omega)
, comparing to the “true” integral calculated using
N=2^{23}
QMC points. Figure
3
summaries the convergence of interlaced polynomial lattice point sets for each stochastic dimension. The observed convergence rates lie between
N^{-1}
and
N^{-1.5}
in this set of experiments. Overall, the convergence rate improves with increasing stochastic dimension. For
s=2^{2}
, the observed rate is about
N^{-1.09}
. For
s=2^{6}
, the observed rate is about
N^{-1.39}
. For each QMC sample size, we also report the estimated integration error as a function of the stochastic dimension. The results are shown in Figure
4
. It appears that the integration error increases with stochastic dimension for all QMC sample sizes, which is in agreement with the QMC theory developed in this paper.

Proof. .

Our
k
is of the form
k=\kappa_{0}+\kappa_{1}+\cdots+\kappa_{r-1}2^{r-1}+2^{r}
with digits

. Then

{\mathrm{w}al}_{k}(t)=(-1)^{\kappa_{0}t_{1}+\kappa_{1}t_{2}+\cdots+\kappa_{r-1}t_{r}+t_{r+1}}\quad\mbox{for $t=t_{1}2^{-1}+t_{2}2^{-2}+\cdots\in[0,1)$.}

In particular,
{\mathrm{w}al}_{k}(t)={\mathrm{w}al}_{k}(\frac{m}{2^{r}})
if
t\in[\frac{m}{2^{r}},\frac{m+1/2}{2^{r}})
and
{\mathrm{w}al}_{k}(t)=-{\mathrm{w}al}_{k}(\frac{m}{2^{r}})
if
t\in[\frac{m+1/2}{2^{r}},\frac{m+1}{2^{r}})
for
m\in\{0,1,\ldots,2^{r}-1\}
. This implies that for every
m\in\{0,1,\ldots,2^{r}-1\}
we have
\int_{I(m,r)}{\mathrm{w}al}_{k}(t)\,{\mathrm{d}}t=0
, where

. We consider two cases:

1.

\frac{a}{2^{r}}\leq x<\frac{a+1/2}{2^{r}}
, then
2^{r}x-a\in[0,\tfrac{1}{2})
and hence
\eta(2^{r}x)=\eta(2^{r}x-a)=2^{r}x-a
. Furthermore,
{\mathrm{w}al}_{k}(x)={\mathrm{w}al}_{k}(\frac{a}{2^{r}})
. Then we have

\int_{0}^{x}{\mathrm{w}al}_{k}(t)\,{\mathrm{d}}t=\int_{a/2^{r}}^{x}{\mathrm{w}al}_{k}\left(\frac{a}{2^{r}}\right)\,{\mathrm{d}}t={\mathrm{w}al}_{k}\left(\frac{a}{2^{r}}\right)\left(x-\frac{a}{2^{r}}\right)=\frac{1}{2^{r}}\,{\mathrm{w}al}_{k}(x)\,\eta(2^{r}x).

2.

\frac{a+1/2}{2^{r}}\leq x<\frac{a+1}{2^{r}}

2^{r}x-a\in[\frac{1}{2},1)

\eta(2^{r}x)=\eta(2^{r}x-a)=2^{r}x-a-1
. Furthermore,
{\mathrm{w}al}_{k}(x)=-{\mathrm{w}al}_{k}(\frac{a}{2^{r}})

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{x}{\mathrm{w}al}_{k}(t)\,{\mathrm{d}}t=

\displaystyle\int_{a/2^{r}}^{(a+(1/2))/2^{r}}{\mathrm{w}al}_{k}\left(\frac{a}{2^{r}}\right)\,{\mathrm{d}}t-\int_{(a+(1/2))/2^{r}}^{x}{\mathrm{w}al}_{k}\left(\frac{a}{2^{r}}\right)\,{\mathrm{d}}t

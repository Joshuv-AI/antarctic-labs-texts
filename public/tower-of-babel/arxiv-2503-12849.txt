Abstract
. In this paper, we show that sets with zero Sobolev p ⁡ ( ⋅ ) p(\cdot) -capacity have generalized Hausdorff h ⁡ ( ⋅ ) h(\cdot) -measure zero, for some gauge function h ⁡ ( ⋅ ) . h(\cdot). We also prove that sets with zero Musielak-Orlicz-Sobolev Φ ⁡ ( ⋅ , ⋅ ) \Phi(\cdot,\cdot) -capacity, for a particular class of functions Φ ⁡ ( ⋅ , ⋅ ) , \Phi(\cdot,\cdot), have generalized Hausdorff h ⁡ ( ⋅ ) h(\cdot) -measure zero, for a suitable gauge function h ⁡ ( ⋅ ) . h(\cdot). These results can be seen as improvements of the earlier results in [ BHH18 ] and [ HHKV03 ] .



Keywords: Variable exponent Sobolev spaces, Musielak-Orlicz-Sobolev spaces, Capacity, Hausdorff measure.

Introduction

Sobolev

[
HHKV03

Definition 1.1
.

Let

The Sobolev

C_{p(\cdot)}(E):=\inf_{u\in S_{p(\cdot)}(E)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u|^{p(x)}+|\triangledown u|^{p(x)}dx,

where
S_{p(\cdot)}(E)=\{u\in W^{1,p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):u\geq 1
in an open set containing
E
and
u\geq 0\}.

It was proved in the same paper that the Sobolev
p(\cdot)
-capacity is an outer capacity, and it also satisfies the Choquet proeprty when the exponent
p(\cdot)
is bounded away from
1
and
\infty.
It was further studied by Harjulehto, Hästö
[
HH04
]
, Harjulehto, Hästö, Koskenoja, Varonen
[
HHKV05
]
, Harjulehto, Hästö, Latvala
[
HHL04
]
, and Mashiyev
[
Mas08
]
. Relative Sobolev capacity in variable exponent Sobolev space has also been studied and compared with the Sobolev
p(\cdot)

In this paper, we study the relation between the Sobolev
p(\cdot)
-capacity and the generalized Hausdorff measure. Recall that, for the classical Sobolev space
W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),
sets of
p
-capacity zero have generalized Hausdorff

-meaure zero provided that

\int_{0}^{1}(t^{p-n}h(t))^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\,\frac{dt}{t}<\infty,

[
AH96
, Theorem 5.1.13]

[
KM72
, Theorem 7.1]
. See also
[

, Chapter 4.7.2]

]

-capacity have

This result was improved in the book
[

]
using the variable Hausdorff measure.

Theorem 1.2

(Theorem 10.4.7,

2.
Notations and Preliminaries

Let us first recall the definition of variable exponents and their related standard notations.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
(\Omega,\Sigma,\mu)
be a
\sigma
-finite, complete measure space. We define
\mathcal{P}(\Omega,\mu)
to be the set of all
\mu
-measurable functions
p:\Omega\rightarrow[1,\infty).
Functions
p\in\mathcal{P}(\Omega,\mu)
are called variable exponents on
\Omega
.

In the special case that
\mu
is the
n
-dimensional Lebesgue measure and
\Omega
is an open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we abbreviate
\mathcal{P}(\Omega):=\mathcal{P}(\Omega,\mu).

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}.
A variable exponent is a bounded measurable function
p:\Omega\rightarrow[1,\infty),
usually denoted by
p(\cdot).

For such
p(\cdot),
where
A
is a measurable subset of
\Omega
,

p_{A}^{+}:=\esssup\{p(x):x\in A\}\;\;\text{and}\;\;p_{A}^{-}:=\essinf\{p(x):x\in A\}.

Also we denote

p^{+}:=\esssup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}p(x)\;\;\text{and}\;\;p^{-}:=\essinf_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}p(x).

Throughout the paper, we assume that
p
is bounded in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, that is
1\leq p^{-}\leq p^{+}<\infty.

For the study of variable exponent Lebesgue spaces and Sobolev spaces, we refer the reader to the books
[
CUF13
,
DHHR11
,
PKJF13
]
. We will need the following Hölder inequality on variable exponent Lebesgue spaces; the proof can be found in any of the above references.

Lemma 2.3
.

If
r(\cdot)\,,\,p(\cdot)\,,\,q(\cdot)
are variable exponents satisfying
1/r(x)=1/p(x)+1/q(x)
almost everywhere on
\Omega
then

(2.1)

\|uv\|_{L^{r(\cdot)}(\Omega)}\leq 2\|u\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)}\,\|v\|_{L^{q(\cdot)}(\Omega)}.

We recall the first order variable exponent Sobolev space here.

Definition 2.4
.

The function
u\in L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)
belongs to space
{W^{1,p(\cdot)}(\Omega)}
, if its weak partial derivatives
\delta_{1}{u},.....,\delta_{n}{u}
exist and belong to
L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)
. We define a semimodular on
{W^{1,p(\cdot)}(\Omega)}
by

\rho_{W^{1,p(\cdot)}(\Omega)}(u):=\rho_{p(\cdot)}(u)+\rho_{p(\cdot)}(\triangledown u)=\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p(x)}+|\triangledown u|^{p(x)}dx
.

It induces a (quasi-) norm

||u||_{W^{1,p(\cdot)}(\Omega)}:=\inf\{\lambda>0:\rho_{W^{1,p(\cdot)}(\Omega)}\left(\frac{u}{\lambda}\right)\leq 1\}
.

The space
W^{1,p(\cdot)}(\Omega)
is called Sobolev space and its elements are called Sobolev functions.

Now, let us recall some terminology to define Musielak-Orlicz spaces and Musielak-Orlicz-Sobolev spaces; more details about these spaces can be found in the books
[
HH19
,
ML19
]
.

Definition 2.5
.

A function
f:(0,\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is almost increasing if there exists a constant
a\geq 1
such that
f(s)\leq af(t)
for all
0<s<t.
Similarly, a function
f:(0,\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is almost decreasing if there exists a constant
b\geq 1
such that
f(s)\geq bf(t)
for all
0<s<t.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
f:(0,\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and
p,q>0
.We say that f satisfies

(i)

(Inc)_{p}
if
f(t)/t^{p}
is increasing;

(ii)

(aInc)_{p}
if
f(t)/t^{p}
is almost increasing;

(iii)

(Dec)_{q}
if
f(t)/t^{q}
is decreasing;

(iv)

(aDec)_{q}
if
f(t)/t^{q}
is almost decreasing.

We say that
f
satisfies
(aInc)
,
(Inc)
,
(aDec)
or
(Dec)
if there exists
p>1
or
q<\infty
such that
f
satisfies
(aInc)_{p}
,
(Inc)_{p}
,
(aDec)_{q}
or
(Dec)_{q}
, respectively.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
(\Omega,\Sigma,\mu)
be a
\sigma
-finite, complete measure space. A function
\Phi:\Omega\times[0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,\infty]
is said to be a (generalized)
\Phi
-prefunction on
(\Omega,\Sigma,\mu)
if
x\rightarrow\Phi(x,|f(x)|)
is measurable for every
f\in L^{0}(\Omega,\mu)
and
\Phi(x,.)
is a
\Phi
-prefunction for
\mu
-almost every
x\in\Omega
. We say that the
\Phi
-prefunction
\Phi
is a (generalized weak)
\Phi
-function if it satisfies
(aInc)_{1}
.
The set of generalized weak
\Phi
-functions is denoted by
\Phi_{w}(\Omega,\mu)
.

Definition 2.8
.

We say that
\Phi\in\Phi_{w}(\Omega,\mu)
satisfies
(A0)
if there exists a constant
\beta\in(0,1]
such that

\beta\leq\Phi^{-1}(x,1)\leq{1/\beta}
for
\mu
-almost every

.

Definition 2.9
.

Let
\Phi\in\Phi_{w}(\Omega,\mu)
and let
\rho_{\Phi}
be given by

\rho_{\Phi}(f):=\int_{\Omega}\Phi(x,|f(x)|)d\mu(x)

for all
f\in L^{0}(\Omega,\mu)
. The function
\rho_{\Phi}
is called a modular. The set

L^{\Phi(\cdot,\cdot)}(\Omega,\mu):=\{f\in L^{0}(\Omega,\mu):\rho_{\Phi}(\lambda f)<\infty\,\,for\,\,some\,\,\lambda>0\}

is called a generalized Orlicz space or Musielak-Orlicz (M-O) space.

Definition 2.10
.

Let
\Phi\in\Phi_{w}(\Omega,\mu)
. The function
u\in L^{\Phi(\cdot,\cdot)}\cap L^{1}_{loc}(\Omega)
belongs to the Musielak-Orlicz-Sobolev space
W^{1,\Phi(\cdot,\cdot)}(\Omega)

\delta_{1}{u},.....,\delta_{n}{u}
exist and belong to
L^{\Phi(\cdot,\cdot)}(\Omega)
. We define a semimodular on
W^{1,\Phi(\cdot,\cdot)}(\Omega)

\rho_{W^{1,\Phi(\cdot,\cdot)}(\Omega)}(u):=\rho_{\Phi}(u)+\rho_{\Phi}(\triangledown u)

It induces a (quasi-) norm

||u||_{W^{1,\Phi(\cdot,\cdot)}(\Omega)}:=\inf\{\lambda>0:\rho_{W^{1,\Phi(\cdot,\cdot)}(\Omega)}\left(\frac{u}{\lambda}\right)\leq 1\}
.

Let us also recall the following three regularity conditions on the exponents.

Definition 2.11
.

We say that the exponent
p(\cdot)
is log-Hölder continuous if there exists a constant
c_{1}>0

|p(x)-p(y)|\leq\frac{c_{1}}{\log(e+1/|x-y|)}
whenever
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Definition 2.12
.

We say that the exponent
p(\cdot)
satisfies the log-Hölder decay condition if there exist
p_{\infty}\in\mathbb{R}
and a constant
c_{2}>0
such that

|p(x)-p_{\infty}|\leq\frac{c_{2}}{\log(e+|x|)}
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Definition 2.13
.

We say that the exponent
p(\cdot)
is log-log-Hölder continuous if there exists a constant
c_{3}>0

|p(x)-p(y)|\leq\frac{c_{3}}{\log(e+\log(e+1/|x-y|))}
whenever
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

The following two lemmas are easy consequences of the regularity conditions of the exponents.

Lemma 2.14
.

Let
p:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be bounded and log-Hölder continuous and
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be any ball of radius r. If
p(x)\geq p(y)
for any two points
x,y\in B
, then there is a constant
c_{0}>0
such that
r^{p(y)-p(x)}\leq c_{0}.

Proof.

By log-Hölder continuity of
p(\cdot)
we get

\big(p(x)-p(y)\big)\leq\frac{c_{1}}{\log(e+1/|x-y|)}\leq\frac{c_{1}}{\log(e+1/2r)}

which implies
\frac{r^{p(y)-p(x)}}{2^{p(x)-p(y)}}\leq(e+1/2r)^{p(x)-p(y)}\leq C_{1},
where
C_{1}=e^{c_{1}}
.
Therefore, we get
r^{p(y)-p(x)}\leq c_{0}
, where
c_{0}=C_{1}2^{(p^{+}-p^{-})}
.

∎

Lemma 2.15
.

Let
p:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be bounded and log-log-Hölder continuous and
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be any ball of radius
r<1
. If
p(x)\geq p(y)
for any two points
x,y\in B
, then there is a constant
c_{4}>0
such that
(\log(1/r))^{p(x)-p(y)}\leq c_{4}.

Proof.

By log-log-Hölder continuity of
p(\cdot)
we get

\big(p(x)-p(y)\big)\leq\frac{c_{3}}{\log(e+\log(e+1/|x-y|))}\leq\frac{c_{3}}{\log(e+\log(e+1/2r))}

which implies
(\log(1/2r))^{p(x)-p(y)}\leq(e+\log(e+1/2r))^{p(x)-p(y)}\leq C_{3},
where
C_{3}=e^{c_{3}}
.
Therefore, we get
(\log(1/r))^{p(x)-p(y)}\leq c_{4}
.

∎

3.
Main Results

We start with a lemma, the proof of which uses the techniques of
[
EG92
, Chapter 2.4.3]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
p\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and let
h:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,\infty)\rightarrow(0,\infty)
be any bounded function such that
\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}h(\cdot,t)=0
. Then

H^{h(\cdot)}\big(\{{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\limsup_{r\rightarrow 0}\frac{1}{h(x,r)}}\int_{B(x,r)}|f|^{p(y)}dy=\infty\}\big)=0

for every
f\in L^{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Proof.

Let
\lambda>0
and consider

E_{\lambda}:=\{\limsup_{r\rightarrow 0}\frac{1}{h(x,r)}\int_{B(x,r)}|f|^{p(y)}dy>\lambda\}.

Fix
\eta>0.
Then for every
x\in E_{\lambda}
we get a number
r_{x}\leq\eta
such that

(3.1)

\int_{B(x,r_{x})}|f|^{p(y)}dy>\lambda h(x,r_{x}).

By the
5B
-covering lemma (Lemma
2.19
), there is a countable subfamily of pairwise disjoint balls
B(x_{i},r_{x_{i}})
such that
E_{\lambda}\subset\cup_{i=1}^{\infty}B(x_{i},5r_{x_{i}})
. From the definition of Hausdorff measure, after using (
3.1
) for the balls
B(x_{i},r_{x_{i}})
and the fact that
E\subset E_{\lambda}
for all
\lambda>0
, we get

\displaystyle H^{h(\cdot)}_{\eta}(E)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c\sum_{i}h(x_{i},r_{x_{i}})\leq c\sum_{i}\lambda^{-1}\int_{B(x_{i},r_{x_{i}})}|f|^{p(y)}dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c\lambda^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f|^{p(y)}dy.

Then, by taking
\eta\rightarrow 0
we obtain

H^{h(\cdot)}(E)\leq c\lambda^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f|^{p(y)}dy,

and finally the claim follows by choosing
\lambda
arbitrarily large.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.3

Since
C_{p(\cdot)}(E)=0,
for every
i\in\mathbb{N},
we have a non-negative function
u_{i}\in W^{1,p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
E\subset\interior\{u_{i}\geq 1\}
and

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\big(|u|^{p(y)}+|\triangledown u|^{p(y)}\big)dy\leq 2^{-ip^{+}}

which implies that

\limsup_{r\rightarrow 0}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B(x,r)}udy=\infty.

Suppose that
x
is such that

(3.14)

\limsup_{r\rightarrow 0}r^{\frac{-(n-p(x))}{p(x)}}\log\left(\frac{1}{r}\right)\||\triangledown u|(\log(e+|\triangledown u|))^{\frac{q(\cdot)}{p^{+}}}\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(B(x,r))}=:C<\infty.

We choose
R\in(0,1)
so that, for every
0<r\leq R
,

\||\triangledown u|(\log(e+|\triangledown u|))^{\frac{q(\cdot)}{p^{+}}}\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(B(x,r))}<Cr^{\frac{n-p(x)}{p(x)}}\log^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{r}\right).

Denote
B_{i}:=B(x,2^{-i}R)
,
B_{i}^{1}:=\{y\in B_{i}:|\triangledown u(y)|\leq(2^{-i}R)^{\frac{-1}{2}}\}
and
B_{i}^{2}:=\{y\in B_{i}:|\triangledown u(y)|>(2^{-i}R)^{\frac{-1}{2}}\}
. Then using the classical Poincaré inequality and the Hölder inequality (
2.1
), we have

\displaystyle|{u}_{B_{i+1}}-{u}_{B_{i}}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B_{i+1}}|u-{u}_{B_{i}}|dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c2^{(i+1)n}\int_{B_{i}}|u-{u}_{B_{i}}|dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c2^{-i(1-n)}\int_{B_{i}}|\triangledown u|dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c2^{-i(1-n)}\int_{B_{i}^{1}}|\triangledown u|dy+c2^{-i(1-n)}\int_{B_{i}^{2}}|\triangledown u|dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c2^{-i(1-n)}(2^{-i}R)^{\frac{-1}{2}}(2^{-i}R)^{n}+\frac{c2^{-i(1-n)}}{(\log(e+(2^{-i}R)^{\frac{-1}{2}}))^{\frac{q^{-}}{p^{+}}}}\int_{B_{i}^{2}}|\triangledown u|(\log(e+|\triangledown u|))^{\frac{q(y)}{p^{+}}}dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c2^{\frac{-i}{2}}R^{n-\frac{1}{2}}+\frac{c2^{-i(1-n)}}{(\log(e+(2^{-i}R)^{\frac{-1}{2}}))^{\frac{q^{-}}{p^{+}}}}||1||_{L^{p^{\prime}(\cdot)}(B_{i})}\||\triangledown u|(\log(e+|\triangledown u|))^{\frac{q(y)}{p^{+}}}\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(B_{i})}.

Lemma
2.17
gives
||1||_{L^{p^{\prime}(\cdot)}(B_{i})}\approx|B_{i}|^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}_{B_{i}}}}\leq|B_{i}|^{\frac{1}{{p^{\prime}_{B_{i}}}^{+}}}
and by Lemma
2.16
we have
|B_{i}|^{\frac{1}{{p^{\prime}_{B_{i}}}^{+}}}\leq c|B_{i}|^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}(x)}}
. Therefore,

||1||_{L^{p^{\prime}(\cdot)}(B_{i})}\leq c(2^{-i}R)^{\frac{np(x)-n}{p(x)}}
.
Hence, for every
i\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle|{u}_{B_{i+1}}-{u}_{B_{i}}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c2^{\frac{-i}{2}}R^{n-\frac{1}{2}}+c2^{-i(1-n+n-\frac{n}{p(x)}+\frac{n-p(x)}{p(x)})}\frac{\log^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{2^{-i}}\right)}{(\log(e+(2^{-i}R)^{\frac{-1}{2}}))^{\frac{q^{-}}{p^{+}}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c2^{\frac{-i}{2}}R^{n-\frac{1}{2}}+c\frac{\log^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{2^{-i}}\right)}{(\log(e+(2^{-i}R)^{\frac{-1}{2}}))^{\frac{q^{-}}{p^{+}}}}.

Therefore, for
k>j
we obtain

\displaystyle|{u}_{B_{k}}-{u}_{B_{j}}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{i=j}^{k-1}|{u}_{B_{i+1}}-{u}_{B_{i}}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c\sum_{i=j}^{k-1}\bigg[2^{\frac{-i}{2}}R^{n-\frac{1}{2}}+\frac{\log^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{2^{-i}}\right)}{(\log(e+(2^{-i}R)^{\frac{-1}{2}}))^{\frac{q^{-}}{p^{+}}}}\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c\sum_{i=j}^{k-1}\bigg[2^{\frac{-i}{2}}R^{n-\frac{1}{2}}+\frac{\log^{-1}(2)}{i^{(1+\frac{q^{-}}{p^{+}})}\log^{\frac{q^{-}}{p^{+}}}(e+2R)}\bigg]

which shows that
({u}_{B_{i}})
is a Cauchy sequence, since the above series is finite. Hence we have

\limsup_{r\rightarrow 0}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B(x,r)}udy<\infty

which together with (
3.13
) implies that
x\notin E
. Thus, we can write

E\subset\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\limsup_{r\rightarrow 0}r^{\frac{-(n-p(x))}{p(x)}}\log\left(\frac{1}{r}\right)\||\triangledown u|(\log(e+|\triangledown u|))^{\frac{q(\cdot)}{p^{+}}}\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(B(x,r))}=\infty\}.

For
r\leq 1
we obtain by Lemma
2.14
that

r^{\frac{-(n-p(x))}{p(x)}}\log\left(\frac{1}{r}\right)\leq cr^{\frac{-(n-p(x))}{p(y)}}\log\left(\frac{1}{r}\right)

for every
y\in B(x,r)
. Therefore

\displaystyle r^{\frac{-(n-p(x))}{p(x)}}\log\left(\frac{1}{r}\right)\||\triangledown u|(\log(e+|\triangledown u|))^{\frac{q(\cdot)}{p^{+}}}\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(B(x,r))}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c||r^{\frac{-(n-p(x))}{p(\cdot)}}\log\left(\frac{1}{r}\right)|\triangledown u|(\log(e+|\triangledown u|))^{\frac{q(\cdot)}{p^{+}}}||_{L^{p(\cdot)}(B(x,r))}.

Hence

E\subset\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\limsup_{r\rightarrow 0}||r^{\frac{-(n-p(x))}{p(\cdot)}}\log\left(\frac{1}{r}\right)|\triangledown u|(\log(e+|\triangledown u|))^{\frac{q(\cdot)}{p^{+}}}||_{L^{p(\cdot)}(B(x,r))}=\infty\}.

Note that

\displaystyle\int_{B(x,r)}r^{\frac{-p(y)(n-p(x))}{p(y)}}\log^{p(y)}\left(\frac{1}{r}\right)(|\triangledown u|\log^{\frac{q(y)}{p^{+}}}(e+|\triangledown u|))^{p(y)}dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle r^{p(x)-n}\log^{p^{+}}\left(\frac{1}{r}\right)\int_{B(x,r)}(|\triangledown u|(\log(e+|\triangledown u|))^{\frac{q(y)}{p^{+}}})^{p(y)}dy

and the norm is finite if and only if the modular is, and hence we conclude

E\subset\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\limsup_{r\rightarrow 0}r^{p(x)-n}\log^{p^{+}}\left(\frac{1}{r}\right)\int_{B(x,r)}(|\triangledown u|(\log(e+|\triangledown u|))^{\frac{q(y)}{p^{+}}})^{p(y)}dy=\infty\}.

Therefore, the claim follows from Lemma
3.1
.∎

Proof of Theorem
1.8

Since
C_{\Phi(\cdot,\cdot)}(E)=0,
for every
i\in\mathbb{N},
we have a non-negative function
u_{i}\in W^{1,\Phi(\cdot,\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
E\subset\interior\{u_{i}\geq 1\}
and
||u_{i}||_{W^{1,\Phi(\cdot,\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq 2^{-i}
. Define
v_{i}:=u_{1}+....+u_{i}.
Then
(v_{i})
is a Cauchy sequence and since
W^{1,\Phi(\cdot,\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a Banach space, there exists
u\in W^{1,\Phi(\cdot,\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
v_{i}\rightarrow u
and hence

||u||_{1,\Phi(\cdot,\cdot)}\leq\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}||v_{i}||_{1,\Phi(\cdot,\cdot)}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}||u_{i}||_{1,\Phi(\cdot,\cdot)}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}2^{-i}\leq 1.

Therefore, by Lemma
3.4
we get
||\,|\triangledown u|(\log(e+|\triangledown u|))^{\frac{q(\cdot)}{p^{-}}}||_{L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)}\leq 1
.

For every natural number
k
and
x\in E
we find
r
such that
u_{i}(y)\geq 1
for every
i=1,....,k
and almost every
y\in B(x,r),
which means that
E\subset\interior\{u\geq m\}
for all
m\geq 1
. Then for
r
small enough we get
B(x,r)\subset\interior\{u\geq m\}
, and hence
\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B(x,r)}udy\geq m
. Therefore

(3.15)

\limsup_{r\rightarrow 0}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B(x,r)}udy=\infty.

Suppose that
x
is such that

(3.16)

\limsup_{r\rightarrow 0}r^{\frac{-(n-p(x))}{p(x)}}\log^{1-\frac{\lambda}{p^{-}}+\frac{\delta}{p^{+}}}\left(\frac{1}{r}\right)\||\triangledown u|(\log(e+|\triangledown u|))^{\frac{q(\cdot)}{p^{-}}}\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(B(x,r))}=:C<\infty.

We choose
R\in(0,1)
so that, for every
0<r\leq R
,

\||\triangledown u|(\log(e+|\triangledown u|))^{\frac{q(\cdot)}{p^{-}}}\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(B(x,r))}<Cr^{\frac{n-p(x)}{p(x)}}\log^{-1+\frac{\lambda}{p^{-}}-\frac{\delta}{p^{+}}}\left(\frac{1}{r}\right).

Denote
B_{i}:=B(x,2^{-i}R)
. Then using the classical Poincaré inequality, Jensen’s inequality {for convex function
\Psi(t)=t\log^{\frac{q^{+}}{p^{-}}}(e+t)
} and the Hölder inequality (
2.1
), we have

\displaystyle|{u}_{B_{i+1}}-{u}_{B_{i}}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B_{i+1}}|u-{u}_{B_{i}}|dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c2^{(i+1)n}\int_{B_{i}}|u-{u}_{B_{i}}|dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c2^{-i}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B_{i}}|\triangledown u|dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c2^{-i}\Psi^{-1}\big(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B_{i}}\Psi(|\triangledown u|)dy\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c2^{-i}\frac{\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B_{i}}\Psi(|\triangledown u|)dy}{\log^{\frac{q^{+}}{p^{-}}}(e+\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B_{i}}\Psi(|\triangledown u|)dy)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c2^{-i(1-n)}\frac{\int_{B_{i}}\Psi(|\triangledown u|)dy}{\log^{\frac{q^{+}}{p^{-}}}(e+2^{-in})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{c2^{-i(1-n)}}{(\log(e+2^{-in}))^{\frac{q^{+}}{p^{-}}}}||1||_{L^{p^{\prime}(\cdot)}(B_{i})}\||\triangledown u|(\log(e+|\triangledown u|))^{\frac{q(\cdot)}{p^{-}}}\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(B_{i})}.

Here
\Psi^{-1}
is the generalized inverse of
\Psi
and we know that
\Psi^{-1}(t)\approx\frac{t}{\log^{\frac{q^{+}}{p^{-}}}(e+t)}
.

Lemma
2.17
gives
||1||_{L^{p^{\prime}(\cdot)}(B_{i})}\approx|B_{i}|^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}_{B_{i}}}}\leq|B_{i}|^{\frac{1}{{p^{\prime}_{B_{i}}}^{+}}}
and by Lemma
2.16
we have
|B_{i}|^{\frac{1}{{p^{\prime}_{B_{i}}}^{+}}}\leq c|B_{i}|^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}(x)}}
. Therefore we get

||1||_{L^{p^{\prime}(\cdot)}(B_{i})}\leq c(2^{-i}R)^{\frac{np(x)-n}{p(x)}}
.
Hence, for every
i\in\mathbb{N}
,

|{u}_{B_{i+1}}-{u}_{B_{i}}|\leq c2^{-i(1-n+n-\frac{n}{p(x)}+\frac{n-p(x)}{p(x)})}\frac{\log^{-1+\frac{q^{+}}{p^{-}}-\frac{\delta}{p^{+}}}\left(\frac{1}{2^{-i}}\right)}{(\log(e+2^{-in}))^{\frac{q^{+}}{p^{-}}}}\\
=c\frac{\log^{-1+\frac{q^{+}}{p^{-}}-\frac{\delta}{p^{+}}}\left(\frac{1}{2^{-i}}\right)}{(\log(e+2^{-in}))^{\frac{q^{+}}{p^{-}}}}.

Therefore, for
k>j
we obtain

|{u}_{B_{k}}-{u}_{B_{j}}|\leq\sum_{i=j}^{k-1}|{u}_{B_{i+1}}-{u}_{B_{i}}|\leq c\sum_{i=j}^{k-1}\frac{\log^{-1+\frac{q^{+}}{p^{-}}-\frac{\delta}{p^{+}}}\left(\frac{1}{2^{-i}}\right)}{(\log(e+2^{-in}))^{\frac{q^{+}}{p^{-}}}}\\
\leq c\sum_{i=j}^{k-1}\frac{\log^{-1+\frac{q^{+}}{p^{-}}-\frac{\delta}{p^{+}}}(2)}{i^{(1+\frac{\delta}{p^{+}})}\log^{\frac{q^{+}}{p^{-}}}(e+2^{-n})}

which shows that
({u}_{B_{i}})
is a Cauchy sequence since the above series is finite. Hence we have

\limsup_{r\rightarrow 0}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B(x,r)}udy<\infty

which together with (
3.15
) implies that
x\notin E
. Thus we can write

E\subset\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\limsup_{r\rightarrow 0}r^{\frac{-(n-p(x))}{p(x)}}\log^{1-\frac{q^{+}}{p^{-}}+\frac{\delta}{p^{+}}}\left(\frac{1}{r}\right)\||\triangledown u|(\log(e+|\triangledown u|))^{\frac{q(\cdot)}{p^{-}}}\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(B(x,r))}=\infty\}.

For
r\leq 1
we obtain by Lemma
2.14
that

r^{\frac{-(n-p(x))}{p(x)}}\log^{1-\frac{q^{+}}{p^{-}}+\frac{\delta}{p^{+}}}\left(\frac{1}{r}\right)\leq cr^{\frac{-(n-p(x))}{p(y)}}\log^{1-\frac{q^{+}}{p^{-}}+\frac{\delta}{p^{+}}}\left(\frac{1}{r}\right)

for every
y\in B(x,r)
. Therefore

\displaystyle r^{\frac{-(n-p(x))}{p(x)}}\log^{1-\frac{q^{+}}{p^{-}}+\frac{\delta}{p^{+}}}\left(\frac{1}{r}\right)\||\triangledown u|(\log(e+|\triangledown u|))^{\frac{q(\cdot)}{p^{-}}}\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(B(x,r))}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c||r^{\frac{-(n-p(x))}{p(\cdot)}}\log^{1-\frac{q^{+}}{p^{-}}+\frac{\delta}{p^{+}}}\left(\frac{1}{r}\right)|\triangledown u|(\log(e+|\triangledown u|))^{\frac{q(\cdot)}{p^{-}}}||_{L^{p(\cdot)}(B(x,r))}.

Hence

E\subset\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\limsup_{r\rightarrow 0}||r^{\frac{-(n-p(x))}{p(\cdot)}}\log^{1-\frac{q^{+}}{p^{-}}+\frac{\delta}{p^{+}}}\left(\frac{1}{r}\right)|\triangledown u|(\log(e+|\triangledown u|))^{\frac{q(\cdot)}{p^{-}}}||_{L^{p(\cdot)}(B(x,r))}=\infty\}.

Note that

\displaystyle\int_{B(x,r)}r^{\frac{-p(y)(n-p(x))}{p(y)}}\log^{p(y)-\frac{q^{+}p(y)}{p^{-}}+\frac{\delta p(y)}{p^{+}}}\left(\frac{1}{r}\right)(|\triangledown u|(\log(e+|\triangledown u|))^{\frac{q(y)}{p^{-}}})^{p(y)}dy

E\subset\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\limsup_{r\rightarrow 0}r^{p(x)-n}\log^{p^{+}-\frac{q^{+}p^{+}}{p^{-}}+\delta}\left(\frac{1}{r}\right)\int_{B(x,r)}(|\triangledown u|(\log(e+|\triangledown u|))^{\frac{q(y)}{p^{-}}})^{p(y)}dy=\infty\}.

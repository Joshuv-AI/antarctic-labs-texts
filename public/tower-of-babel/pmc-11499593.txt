Safety and efficacy of using stapler device for wide cystic duct ligation in acute setting of laparoscopic cholecystectomy
Authors: Rifan Alyami, Ahad E Alotaibi, Bader Alhoumaily, Ibrahim Momen, Hatem Aljanfawe, Marya Algoblan
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis research explores the challenges posed by variations in cystic duct anatomy during laparoscopic cholecystectomy and the potential use of stapler devices to address such complexities. It aims to assess the safety and efficacy of stapler devices, particularly in cases involving short, and wide cystic duct. The study, conducted at King Abdulaziz Medical City in Riyadh, Saudi Arabia, between 2021 and 2023. The study design involved a retrospective cohort approach, and the sample size encompassed all cases meeting the inclusion criteria during the study period. Data was analyzed using SPSS program. The study involving 568 patients, with the predominant technique for ligation being clipping (89.3%). The primary indication for using the Endo GIA stapler was a wide cystic duct (6.2%). Analysis revealed a significant association between stapler device use and hypertension (p = 0.042), diabetes (p = 0.001), CKD (p = 0.011), and obstructive jaundice (p = 0.006). Multivariate regression demonstrated that older patients were 2.1 times more likely to use stapler devices (AOR = 2.125; p = 0.039), while those with obstructive jaundice had a 2.2 times higher likelihood (AOR = 2.168; p = 0.049). Throughout the years, metal clips have shown the best prognosis compared to any other method for securing the cystic duct during closure in laparoscopic cholecystectomy. However, to avoid incomplete sealing of wide, short, or inflamed ducts, other instruments have emerged as possible alternatives for such conditions. Use of Endo-GIA is a safe and effective method for patients with wide, short cystic duct. Introduction

Bile acid production and its essential role in the digestive process underscore the significance of an unimpeded biliary system1. However, disruptions such as stones in the gallbladder can lead to acute cholecystitis, a painful condition affecting a notable percentage of individuals2. The prevalent shift from open cholecystectomy to laparoscopic cholecystectomy has brought forth a transformative era in treating acute cholecystitis3, with documented advantages in terms of postoperative morbidity4. Challenges arise during laparoscopic cholecystectomy, particularly when dealing with variations in the cystic duct anomalies, inflammation, short length, or increased width can complicate the process of ligation5. In response to these challenges, the utilization of a stapler device has emerged as a potential solution to effectively occlude the duct and prevent undesirable leakage6. Despite its application, concerns persist regarding the safety and efficacy of the stapler device, with potential complications including peritonitis, sepsis, and fistulas7,8. Particularly alarming is the increased risk of mortality associated with bile leakage postoperatively9. This research aims to evaluate the stapler device safety, efficacy, and application in laparoscopic cholecystectomy for patients with wide cystic duct. This includes examining any potential complications, adverse events, or risks associated with its use during laparoscopic cholecystectomy procedures, by closely monitoring patient outcomes and collecting data on postoperative complications. This article seeks to contribute valuable recommendations regarding practicality and outcomes associated with stapler device utilization during laparoscopic cholecystectomy for patients with wide cystic ducts. The findings aim to not only to improve the surgeons’ experience, and surgical practices, but also aid in enhancing patient outcomes and minimizing potential risks associated with this innovative approach. This research endeavors to bridge the existing gap in evidence, offering a clearer understanding of the role of stapler devices in the evolving landscape of laparoscopic cholecystectomy for a diverse patient population.

Methodology

The study, conducted at King Abdulaziz Medical City in Riyadh, Saudi Arabia, aimed to assess the safety and efficacy of using stapling devices in laparoscopic cholecystectomy procedures performed between January 2021 and September 2023. Ethical approval was obtained from the Institutional Review Board at King Abdullah International Medical Research Center (KAIMRC), IRB approval number.: 2785/23, and the research was performed in accordance with relevant guidelines, and regulations. All patients meeting the inclusion criteria were included in this retrospective cohort study, with data sourced from the hospital’s chart review system, BESTCare. The inclusion criteria included patients who underwent laparoscopic cholecystectomy with stapling device usage during the specified period and had available intraoperative reports. Exclusion criteria excluded individuals aged less than 14 or older than 70, those without evidence of acute cholecystitis in the case of cholelithiasis, patients with known malignancy, those undergoing cholecystectomy as part of another major abdominal surgery, and individuals with cystic duct ligation using three 10 mm clips. The study design involved a retrospective cohort approach, and the sample size encompassed all cases meeting the inclusion criteria during the study period. No specific sampling or randomization was necessary, as all eligible patients admitted for laparoscopic cholecystectomy were automatically included. Data collection involved extraction from the BestCare system, utilizing a customized electronic data collection sheet. This sheet encompassed various parameters, including demographics (age, gender, BMI, smoking status), underlying medical conditions, preoperative diagnosis, morbidity, and management, radiological findings, and details of the intraoperative technique.

Statistical analysis

The data were analyzed using the software program Statistical Packages for Software Sciences (SPSS) version 26 (Armonk, New York, IBM Corporation, USA). Categorical variables were given as numbers and percentages. Continuous variables were calculated and presented as mean and standard deviation. The relationship between the ligation technique according to the patient’s demographic, clinical characteristics, management, and outcome of the patients has been conducted using the Chi-square test and independent sample t-test. Significant results were then gathered in a multivariate regression model to determine the independent significant factors associated with the stapler device. Values were considered significant with a p-value of less than 0.05.

The mean BMI was 30.9 (SD 13.2), with 53.2% being considered obese. The prevalence of smoking was 15.3%. Acute chole was the most prominent diagnosed disease. Clinical presentation mainly was positive Murphy (60.2%). The most common finding based on imaging was the presence of cholelithiasis in combination with the sonographic Murphy sign (73.6%). Seven patients were referred to MRCP, while two patients were referred to CT. In addition, the mean duration between diagnosis and operation date was 11.5 (SD 63.5) days.

When conducting a multivariate regression model to determine the significant independent predictors of the stapler device (Table 4), it was found that compared to the younger age group, older patients were 2.1 times more likely to use stapler device technique (AOR = 2.125; 95% CI = 1.041–4.341; p = 0.039). Patients with obstructive jaundice were 2.2 times more likely to use the stapler device technique (AOR = 2.168; 95% CI = 1.004–4.685; p = 0.049). However, increasing days of hospital stay were associated with marginal use of the stapler device technique (AOR = 0.913; 95% CI = 0.859–0.971; p = 0.004). On the other hand, associated chronic diseases such as hypertension, diabetes, and CKD did not significantly influence the use of stapler devices after adjustment to a regression model (p > 0.05).

Discussion

Throughout the years, metal clips have shown the best prognosis compared to any other method for securing the cystic duct during closure in laparoscopic cholecystectomy. However, to avoid incomplete sealing of wide, short, or inflamed ducts, other instruments have emerged as possible alternatives for such conditions10. The findings of this study demonstrate that the application of stapler devices for wide or short ducts has high rates of safety and efficacy. Acute care surgeons applied endo-GIA in 61 patients, revealing no significant post-operative bile leakage, common bile duct injury, nor an increase in mortality rates.

Previously conducted research disclosed the safety of the devices. In a study conducted by M. Odabasi et al. on patients using Endo-GIA for the closure of the cystic duct, with an average age of 62 years, none of them experienced intraoperative or postoperative complications. Surprisingly, the hospital length of stay was only 3.4 days, which showed approximately a twofold increase compared to our results6. This gap is a consequence of the lack of evidence supporting stapler usage in laparoscopic cholecystectomy. As a result, surgeons may increase the hospital stay for faster intervention in case of any possible complications in the first few days after the operation. Another retrospective study on 24 patients in Taipei, Taiwan, reported that 2 of them had postoperative complications11. The first patient had a residual stone in the CBD, and the second had CBD injury as a result of additional metallic clips used for managing cystic duct bleeding. This contributes to the efficiency of staplers, as both complications were not related to them.

In 2009, a case report on 3 adults claimed that using staplers is dangerous for the closure of the cystic duct (CD) in laparoscopic cholecystectomy (LC). This assertion stemmed from the serious complications reported postoperatively, where staplers were used for the same indication, which is wide cystic ducts12. Our study, which has satisfying statistical evidence, observed 35 patients with wide cystic ducts and supports the use of stapler closure.

Regarding the association between the technique of ligation and chronic medical conditions, the Saudi Arabian population has a high prevalence of diabetes mellitus (DM), hypertension (HTN), and chronic kidney disease (CKD). According to a nationwide cohort study on 18,034 patients, DM affects 25.4% of the population, a statistic supported by the World Health Organization (WHO), which ranks the Saudi Arabian population as the 7th most affected by DM worldwide13,14. Moreover, a meta-analysis conducted to assess hypertension in Saudi Arabia revealed that 22.66% of 272,378 individuals were hypertensive15. Additionally, another study observed that 9,892 per 100,000 individuals in the Saudi population have CKD16. These statistics provide a reasonable explanation as to why chronic conditions are not considered independent risk factors for stapler application.

In our study, obstructive jaundice was the only clinical feature that increased the incidence of stapler use, raising questions about which signs and symptoms most commonly lead surgeons to use staplers and the relevance between obstructive jaundice and wide, short, or inflamed cystic ducts.

These results will significantly influence clinical practice as they demonstrate the reliability and feasibility of stapler devices, which will decrease the economic burden by reducing hospital length of stay. Additionally, it opens multiple fields where these devices could be used. Furthermore, it will aid in the implementation of these devices with no fear of serious complications and provide safe alternatives to metal clips in wide and short cystic ducts.

To sum up, the use of Endo-GIA is safe and effective method for patients with wide, short cystic duct. Selective usage of endo-GIA is considered cost effective and may add positive impact in decreasing post operative biliary leak17,18.

In addressing the limitations of this study, the type of Endo-GIA, and other staplers were not specified. Moreover, while assessing the width and length of the cystic duct, the decision relied on the surgeon’s expertise to be applied and not on specific measures for the cystic duct dimensions, which may introduce a degree of subjectivity. These limitations lead us to initiate research on the safety and efficacy of different subtypes of stapler devices individually, as well as to study various types of cystic duct anomalies to prevent potential future complications, morbidities, and mortalities. Future research may also focus on a larger sample size and include different populations and races, as well as utilize imaging and radiology before surgeries to assess the cystic duct size.

Conclusion

The primary ligation technique and largely used is clipping technique, while the use of the Endo GIA stapler was notably associated with cases featuring a wide, short cystic duct. The Endo GIA stapler, when judiciously applied in laparoscopic cholecystectomy for patients with a wide, short cystic duct, presents an opportunity for enhanced safety and efficacy. Multivariate regression demonstrated that older age and the presence of obstructive jaundice increased the likelihood of stapler device utilization. These findings underscore the importance of personalized approaches in biliary surgeries, taking into account patient characteristics and clinical presentations for optimal outcomes.

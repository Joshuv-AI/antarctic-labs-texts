Embeddings of Sobolev spaces of differential forms
V. Gol’dshtein, Ya. Kopylov, and R. Panenko

Abstract
. We continue the study of embedding between different classes of Sobolev spaces of differential forms started in 2006 in a paper by Gol’dshtein and Troyanov. As in this paper, our study is based on relations between L q , p L_{q,p} -cohomology and Sobolev type inequalities. The main results are estimates for the norms of the embedding operators for q = p q=p and p > n − 1 k − 1 p>\frac{n-1}{k-1} in the Euclidean r r -ball B ⁡ ( r ) B(r) and its bi-Lipschitz images. The compactness of such operators also studied.

1.
Preliminaries

Let
M
be an orientable Riemannian manifold.

Definition 1
.

Let
\Omega^{k}_{p}(M)
be the Banach space of (equivalence classes) of measurable differential

k
-forms with the norm

\|\omega\|_{\Omega^{k}_{p}(M)}=\Bigg\{\int\limits_{M}|\omega(x)|^{p}dx\Bigg\}^{\frac{1}{p}}

Below we understand the de Rham differential
d
in the sense of distributions.
Let
\omega\in\Omega^{k}_{1,\mathrm{loc}}(M)
. We call a
(k+1)
-form
\eta
the differential
d\omega
if

\int_{M}\omega\wedge d\phi=(-1)^{k+1}\int_{M}\eta\wedge\phi

for every compactly supported smooth
(n-k)
-form
\phi
.

In
[
4
]
, for a convex bounded domain
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, Iwaniec and Lutoborski
constructed
a homotopy operator
T:\Omega_{1,\mathrm{loc}}^{k}(D)\to\Omega_{1,\mathrm{loc}}^{k-1}(D)
,
which can be expressed as follows. Let
\xi=(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k-1})\in\mathbb{R}^{k-1}
.
Then

T\omega(x)\langle\xi\rangle=\int\limits_{D}\omega(z)\langle\zeta(z,x-z),\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k-1}\rangle\,dz.

Here the function
\zeta:D\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is given by the formula

(1)

\zeta(z,h)=\sum\limits_{\nu=k}^{n}\binom{n-k}{\nu-k}\frac{h}{|h|^{\nu}}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}s^{\nu-1}\varphi\left(z-s\frac{h}{|h|}\right)\,ds,

where
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(D)
is taken so that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\varphi(y)\,dy=1\,\,\text{and}\,\,|\nabla\varphi|\leq 2\mu(D)(\mathrm{diam}D)^{-n-1}.

Then
\omega=d\,T\omega+Td\omega
for every
\omega\in\Omega_{1,\mathrm{loc}}^{k}(D)
.

Theorem 1.1
.

If
\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}<\frac{1}{n}
then the operator

T:\Omega_{p}^{k}(D)\to\Omega_{q}^{k-1}(D)

is compact.

Proof.

As was observed in
[
4
]
, since
\varphi\left(z-s\frac{h}{|h|}\right)=0

if
s>\mathrm{diam}D
, the integration in (
1
) is in fact over the finite
interval
0\leq s\leq\mathrm{diam}D
.

For
\omega=\sum\limits_{|\alpha|=k}\omega_{\alpha}\,dx^{\alpha}\in\Omega_{p}^{k}(D)
, we have

T\omega(x)\langle\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k-1}\rangle=\biggl\{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\int_{D}\biggl(K^{i}_{k}(z,x-z)\sum\limits_{|\alpha|=k}\omega_{\alpha}(z)\,dz\biggr)\,dx^{\alpha}\biggr\}\langle e_{i},\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k-1}\rangle,

where

(2)

K^{i}_{k}(x,h):=\sum\limits_{\nu=k}^{n}\binom{n-k}{\nu-k}\frac{h_{i}}{|h|^{\nu}}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}s^{\nu-1}\varphi\left(z-s\frac{h}{|h|}\right)ds.

Consider the restriction
T_{(\alpha)}
of
T
to the subspace spanned by the
k
-forms

\omega=fdx^{\alpha}
for a fixed multi-index
\alpha
, which is isometric to
L^{p}(D)
.
For the component
T_{(\alpha)}^{\beta}(fdx^{\alpha})=T\omega(x)\langle e_{i_{1}},\dots,e_{i_{k-1}}\rangle

(the coefficient at
dx^{\beta}
,
\beta=(i_{1},\dots,i_{k-1})
), we have

T\omega(x)\langle e_{i_{1}},\dots,e_{i_{k-1}}\rangle=\biggl\{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\int_{D}\biggl(K^{i}_{k}(z,x-z)\sum\limits_{|\alpha|=k}f(z)\,dz\biggr)\,dx^{\alpha}\biggr\}\langle e_{i},e_{i_{1}},\dots,e_{i_{k-1}}\rangle

Each of the operators
T_{(\alpha)}^{\beta}
can be regarded as an integral operator from
L^{p}(D)

into
L^{q}(D)
with kernel of the form (
2
).
Using relations (23) in
[
5
, p. 331]
, we conclude that each of the operators
T_{(\alpha)}^{\beta}

is compact if
\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}<\frac{1}{n}
in
[
5
, Theorem 6, p. 332]
. It follows that

T_{(\alpha)}
is compact.

Indeed, take a sequence
\{\omega^{(m)}\}_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\Omega_{p}^{k}(D)
, with
\|\omega^{(m)}\|\leq 1
.
If
\omega^{(m)}=\sum_{|\alpha|=k}\omega^{(m)}_{\alpha}\,dx^{\alpha}

Then
\|\omega^{(m)}_{\alpha}\|\leq 1
for each multi-index
\alpha
. Order the set
M_{k-1}

of the multi-indices
\beta
with
\deg\beta=k-1
arbitrarily, so that
M_{k-1}=\{\beta_{1},\dots\beta_{s}\}
.
Since
T_{(\alpha)}^{\beta_{1}}
is compact, the sequence
\{T_{(\alpha)}^{\beta_{1}}(\omega^{(m)}_{\alpha}\,dx_{\alpha})\}

has a convergent subsequence
\{T_{(\alpha)}^{\beta_{1}}(\omega^{(m_{l})}_{\alpha}\,dx_{\alpha})\}_{l\in\mathbb{N}}
.
Passing to the sequence
\{\omega^{(m_{l})}\}_{l\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\Omega_{p}^{k}(D)
and proceeding
like that with
\beta_{2}
, we obtain a sequence of forms in the unit ball for which the images both
under
T_{(\alpha)}^{\beta_{1}}
and
T_{(\alpha)}^{\beta_{2}}
converge. Continuing by induction, we obtain
a subsequence
\{\omega^{(m_{r})}_{\alpha}\}_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
of
\{\omega^{(m)}_{\alpha}\}_{m\in\mathbb{N}}

for which all the images
\{T_{(\alpha)}^{\beta_{j}}(\omega^{(m_{r})}_{\alpha})\,dx_{\alpha}\}
,

, converge.
Thus, the sequence
\{T_{\alpha}(\omega^{(m_{r})}_{\alpha}\,dx_{\alpha}\}
also converges. Since

, we have

\Omega_{q}^{k-1}(D)
.

2.
The Homotopy Operator

In the present paper, we primarily work with spaces of differential forms defined on domains
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Moreover, we require such a domain to be bi-Lipschitz homeomorphic to an open ball.
As we said above, we begin with a homotopy operator on a ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Given a differential
k
-form

\omega=\sum_{j}f_{j_{1},\dots,\,j_{k}}dx_{j_{1}}\wedge\dots\wedge dx_{j_{i}}\wedge\dots\wedge dx_{j_{k}},

in the local coordinates, the operator

S\omega=\int\limits_{0}^{1}t^{k-1}\omega(tx)\Big\langle\sum\limits_{i}x_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}},\,\xi_{1},\dots,\,\xi_{k-1}\Big\rangle dt

has the form

S\omega=\sum_{j}\Bigg\{\int\limits_{0}^{1}f_{j_{0},\dots,\,j_{k}}(tx)t^{k-1}dt\sum_{i}(-1)^{i}x_{j_{i}}dx_{j_{1}}\wedge\dots\wedge dx_{j_{i-1}}\wedge dx_{j_{i+1}}\wedge\dots\wedge dx_{j_{k}}\Bigg\}

Theorem 2.1
.

If
]-r,\,r[
is an open interval and
\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}\leq 1
then

S\colon\Omega^{1}_{p}\big(]-r,\,r[\big)\to\Omega^{0}_{q}\big(]-r,\,r[\big)

and

\|S\omega\|_{\Omega^{0}_{q}\big(]-r,\,r[\big)}\leq 2r\|\omega\|_{\Omega^{1}_{p}\big(]-r,\,r[\big)}.

Proof.

For the case
n=1
, the operator
S
acts by the formula

S\colon\{f(x)dx\}\mapsto\Big\{\int\limits_{0}^{1}xf(tx)dt\Big\}.

Consider the constant function

{\bf 1}\colon x\mapsto 1,~x\in B(r).

Then the expression for the norm of
S\omega
looks as

\displaystyle\|S\omega\|_{q}^{q}

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{B(r)}\Bigg|\int\limits_{0}^{1}xf(tx)dt\Bigg|^{q}dx\leq\int\limits_{B(r)}\Bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{1}|x|\cdot|f(tx)|dt\Bigg)^{q}dx

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{B(r)}\Bigg(\int\limits_{0}^{1}{\rm sgn}(x)\cdot{\bf 1}(x(1-t))\cdot|f(tx)|xdt\Bigg)^{q}dx.

Performing the change of variable
z=tx
, we infer

\int\limits_{0}^{1}{\rm sgn}(x)\cdot{\bf 1}(x(1-t))\cdot|f(tx)|xdt=\int\limits_{0}^{x}{\bf 1}(x-z)\cdot|f(z)|dz\leq\int\limits_{B(r)}{\bf 1}(x-z)\cdot|f(z)|dz,

where the last inequality follows from the relation
{\bf 1}(x-z)\cdot|f(z)|>0
.
As a result, we obtain

\|S\omega\|_{q}^{q}\leq\int\limits_{B(r)}\Bigg(\int\limits_{B(r)}{\bf 1}(x-z)\cdot|f(z)|dz\Bigg)^{q}dx=\||\omega|*{\bf 1}\|^{q}_{q}.

Now we can apply Young’s inequality. Suppose that
p,\,l,\,s\in[1,\infty]
and

\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{l}=1+\frac{1}{s}
. Then

\|f*g\|_{s}\leq\|f\|_{p}\|g\|_{l}.

For the expression in question, we get

\||\omega|*{\bf 1}\|_{q}\leq\|{\bf 1}\|_{l}\||\omega|\|_{p}

where

\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{l}=1+\frac{1}{q},

or, equivalently,

\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}=1-\frac{1}{l}.

where
l\in[1,\,\infty]
. Given parameters
p
and
q
such that

\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}\leq 1,

the operator
S
satisfies the inequality

\|S\omega\|_{\Omega^{0}_{q}\big(]-r,\,r[\big)}\leq 2r\|\omega\|_{\Omega^{1}_{p}\big(]-r,\,r[\big)}

∎

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
B(r)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
, be the open
r
-ball centered at the origin
and
p>\frac{n-1}{k-1}
,
k\geq 2
. Then

S\colon\Omega^{k}_{p}\big(B(r)\big)\to\Omega^{k-1}_{p}\big(B(r)\big)

and

\|S\omega\|_{\Omega^{k-1}_{p}(B(r))}\leq\frac{r\sqrt{\binom{n}{k}}}{(p(k-1)-n+1)^{\frac{1}{p}}}\|\omega\|_{\Omega^{k}_{p}(B(r))}.

Proof.

Below in this proof, we apply the operator
S
to
(k+1)
-forms for convenience, that is,

S\colon\Omega^{k+1}_{p}\big(B(r)\big)\to\Omega^{k}_{p}\big(B(r)\big).

Consider
|S\omega|_{x}=\sqrt{\langle S\omega,\,S\omega\rangle_{x}}
. The triangle inequality implies

|S\omega|_{x}\leq\sum_{j}\Bigg\{\Big|\int\limits_{0}^{1}f_{j_{0},\dots,\,j_{k}}(tx)t^{k}dt\Big|\cdot\Big|\sum_{i}(-1)^{i}x_{j_{i}}dx_{j_{0}}\wedge\dots\wedge dx_{j_{i-1}}\wedge dx_{j_{i+1}}\wedge\dots\wedge dx_{j_{k}}\Big|_{x}\Bigg\}.

Then we can calculate

\Big|\sum_{i}(-1)^{i}x_{j_{i}}dx_{j_{0}}\wedge\dots\wedge dx_{j_{i-1}}\wedge dx_{j_{i+1}}\wedge\dots\wedge dx_{j_{k}}\Big|_{x}=\sqrt{\sum_{i}x^{2}_{j_{i}}}

Since the sum above contains only a part of positive elements
x^{2}_{i}
from a row vector

x=\begin{bmatrix}x_{1}&\dots&x_{n}\end{bmatrix},

we obtain

\sqrt{\sum_{i}x^{2}_{j_{i}}}\leq|x|.

Hence,

\displaystyle|S\omega_{x}|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j}\Bigg\{\Big|\int\limits_{0}^{1}f_{j_{0},\dots,\,j_{k}}(tx)t^{k}dt\Big|\cdot\sqrt{\sum_{i}x^{2}_{j_{i}}}\Bigg\}

\displaystyle\leq|x|\sum_{j}\Big|\int\limits_{0}^{1}f_{j_{0},\dots,\,j_{k}}(tx)t^{k}dt\Big|\leq|x|\int\limits_{0}^{1}t^{k}\sum_{j}|f_{j_{0},\dots,\,j_{k}}(tx)|dt

Since
|x|<r
for all points of the
r
-ball, we finally obtain

|S\omega_{x}|\leq r\int\limits_{0}^{1}t^{k}\sum_{j}|f_{j_{0},\dots,\,j_{k}}(tx)|dt

Consider two finite-dimensional normed spaces
\big\langle\mathbb{R}^{m},\,\|\cdot\|_{2}\big\rangle
and
\big\langle\mathbb{R}^{m},\,\|\cdot\|_{1}\big\rangle
.

For every
v\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
, we have

\|v\|_{1}\leq\sqrt{m}\|v\|_{2}

Let us look at the family of functions

f_{j_{0},\dots,\,j_{k}}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}.

It defines an
n
-dimensional surface in
\mathbb{R}^{\binom{n}{k+1}}
, which is realized as the graph

\mathcal{F}\colon x\mapsto(f_{0,\dots,\,k}(x),\dots,\,f_{j_{0},\dots,\,j_{k}}(x),\dots,\,f_{n-k-1,\dots,\,n}(x))

and so, reproducing the previous argument, for every point

\mathcal{F}(x)\in\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subset\mathbb{R}^{\binom{n}{k+1}}
, we obtain

\sum_{j}|f_{j_{0},\dots,\,j_{k}}(x)|\leq\sqrt{\binom{n}{k+1}}\sqrt{\sum_{j}|f_{j_{0},\dots,\,j_{k}}(x)|^{2}}=\sqrt{\binom{n}{k+1}}|\omega|_{x}.

And then

|S\omega|_{x}\leq r\int\limits_{0}^{1}t^{k}\sum_{j}|f_{j_{0},\dots,\,j_{k}}(tx)|dt\leq r\sqrt{\binom{n}{k+1}}\int\limits_{0}^{1}t^{k}|\omega|_{tx}dt.

Let us estimate the norm
\|S\omega\|_{\Omega_{p}(B(r))}
:

\Bigg(\int\limits_{B(r)}|S\omega|_{x}^{p}dx\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq r\sqrt{\binom{n}{k+1}}\Bigg(\int\limits_{B(r)}\Big\{\int\limits_{0}^{1}t^{k}|\omega|_{tx}dt\Big\}^{p}dx\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}

Let us take a look at

\int\limits_{B(r)}\Big\{\int\limits_{0}^{1}t^{k}|\omega|_{tx}dt\Big\}^{p}dx.

Applying Jensen’s inequality in the inner integral, we infer

\displaystyle\int\limits_{B(r)}\Big\{\int\limits_{0}^{1}t^{k}|\omega|_{tx}dt\Big\}^{p}dx\leq\int\limits_{B(r)}\int\limits_{0}^{1}t^{pk}|\omega|^{p}_{tx}dtdx=\int\limits_{0}^{1}\int\limits_{B(r)}t^{pk}|\omega|^{p}_{tx}dxdt.

We can consider the map

{B}\to{B(tr)}

defined as

x\mapsto t\cdot x,\quad t\in[0,\,1].

The Jacobian matrix of this map is equal to

J_{t}=\begin{bmatrix}t&&0\\
&\ddots&\\
0&&t\end{bmatrix}\,,

and

\det J_{t}=t^{n}.

Let
k\geq 1
. Changing variables in the inner integral, we have

\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{1}\int\limits_{B(r)}t^{pk}|\omega|^{p}_{tx}dxdt=\int\limits_{0}^{1}\int\limits_{B(tr)}t^{pk-n}|\omega|^{p}_{v}dvdt\leq\int\limits_{0}^{1}t^{pk-n}dt\cdot\int\limits_{B(r)}|\omega|^{p}_{v}dv

Then

\int\limits_{0}^{1}t^{pk-n}dt=\frac{1}{pk-n+1}

for

pk-n>-1,~p>\frac{n-1}{k}

So we can conclude that

\|S\omega\|_{\Omega^{k}_{p}(B(r))}\leq\frac{r\sqrt{\binom{n}{k+1}}}{(pk-n+1)^{\frac{1}{p}}}\|\omega\|_{\Omega^{k+1}_{p}(B(r))}

for
p>\frac{n-1}{k}
.

This can be reformulated for
S
acting on
k
-forms

S\colon\Omega^{k}_{p}\big(B(r)\big)\to\Omega^{k-1}_{p}\big(B(r)\big)

as follows:

\|S\omega\|_{\Omega^{k-1}_{p}(B(r))}\leq\frac{r\sqrt{\binom{n}{k}}}{(p(k-1)-n+1)^{\frac{1}{p}}}\|\omega\|_{\Omega^{k}_{p}(B(r))}.

∎

Remark 1
.

For the case of 1-forms, we have

\|S\omega\|_{\Omega^{0}_{p}(B(r))}\leq\frac{r^{1+\frac{n-1}{p}}\sqrt{n}}{(p+n-1)^{\frac{1}{p}}}\|\omega\|_{\Omega^{1}_{p}(B(r))}

Proof.

By the above considerations, we obtain

\Bigg(\int\limits_{B(r)}|S\omega|_{x}^{p}dx\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq\sqrt{n}\Bigg(\int\limits_{B(r)}\Big\{\int\limits_{0}^{1}|x|\cdot|\omega|_{tx}dt\Big\}^{p}dx\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Therefore, it remains to estimate the double integral. We infer

\displaystyle\int\limits_{B(r)}\Big\{\int\limits_{0}^{1}|x|\cdot|\omega|_{tx}dt\Big\}^{p}dx\leq\int\limits_{B(r)}\int\limits_{0}^{1}|x|^{p}\cdot|\omega|_{tx}^{p}dt\,dx

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{S^{n-1}}\int\limits_{0}^{r}\Big\{\int\limits_{0}^{1}\uprho^{p}\cdot|\omega|_{tx(\uprho,\Upomega)}^{p}dt\Big\}\uprho^{n-1}d\uprho\,d\Upomega=\int\limits_{S^{n-1}}\int\limits_{0}^{r}\int\limits_{0}^{1}\uprho^{p+n-1}\cdot|\omega|_{tx(\uprho,\Upomega)}^{p}dtd\uprho\,d\Upomega

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{S^{n-1}}\int\limits_{0}^{r}\uprho^{p+n-2}\Big\{\int\limits_{0}^{\uprho}|\omega|_{\langle z,\,\Upomega\rangle}^{p}dz\Big\}d\uprho\,d\Upomega=\int\limits_{0}^{r}\uprho^{p+n-2}\Big\{\int\limits_{S^{n-1}}\int\limits_{0}^{\uprho}|\omega|_{\langle z,\,\Upomega\rangle}^{p}dz\,d\Upomega\Big\}d\uprho

\displaystyle\leq\|\omega\|^{p}_{\Omega^{1}_{p}(B(r))}\int\limits_{0}^{r}\uprho^{p+n-2}d\uprho=\frac{r^{p+n-1}}{p+n-1}\|\omega\|^{p}_{\Omega^{1}_{p}(B(r))}

∎

3.
The General Construction

Let
U
and
V
be subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and let

\omega=\sum_{j}f_{j_{1},\dots,\,j_{k}}dy_{j_{1}}\wedge\dots\wedge dy_{j_{i}}\wedge\dots\wedge dy_{j_{k}}

be a differential
k
-form presented in local coordinates on
V
.
Assume that

\varphi\colon U\to V

is a Lipschitz map

\begin{pmatrix}x_{1}\\
x_{2}\\
\dots\\
x_{n}\end{pmatrix}\mapsto\begin{pmatrix}y_{1}\\
y_{2}\\
\dots\\
y_{n}\end{pmatrix}

and
C
is the Lipschitz constant, that is,

|\varphi(x)-\varphi(x^{\prime})|\leq C|x-x^{\prime}|.

We put

y_{i}=\varphi_{i}(x_{1},\dots,\,x_{n}).

It is not hard to see that

\frac{\partial\varphi_{j}}{\partial x_{i}}^{2}\leq\sum_{j}\frac{\partial\varphi_{j}}{\partial x_{i}}^{2}=\Bigg|\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\varphi\Bigg|^{2}.

Then the relation

\frac{|\varphi(x)-\varphi(x^{\prime})|}{|x-x^{\prime}|}\leq C

Lemma 5.1
.

(M. Stern)
Let

. For each

Proof.

Let

be the set of solutions to the equation

It is not hard to see that

is a closed subset.
Indeed, let
\{\theta_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}\subset Sol(\omega)
be a Cauchy sequence and

\theta_{j}\to\theta\in\Omega^{k-1}_{p}(M)
.
Assume that
\theta\notin Sol(\omega)
, then
d(\theta-\theta_{j})\neq 0
.
But we know that
(\theta-\theta_{j})\to 0
, which
contradicts the continuity of

\Omega^{k-1}_{p}(M)
.

Consider the functional

defined by

E(\theta)=\int_{M}|\theta|^{p}dv.

We put

We know that
\Omega^{k-1}_{p}(M)
is a reflexive space. Therefore,

\mathscr{B}=\{\omega\in\Omega^{k-1}_{p}(M)\mid E(\omega)\leq m+\varepsilon\}

is a weakly compact set due to the Banach–Alaoglu theorem.
Then
Sol(\omega)\cap\mathscr{B}
is a weakly compact set too.
Let
\{\eta_{j}\}^{\infty}_{j=1}\subset Sol(\omega)\cap\mathscr{B}
satisfy
E(\eta_{j})\to m
as

.
Then, due to the Eberlein–Šmulian theorem, there exists a weakly convergent subsequence

\{\eta_{j_{i}}\}^{\infty}_{i=1}
such that
\eta=\lim\eta_{j_{i}}\in Sol(\omega)
and
E(\eta)=m
.

Let
{\mathscr{D}}=\{\zeta=\eta+d\varphi\mid\varphi\in\Omega^{k-2}_{0}(M)\}
.
It is not hard to see that the Gateaux derivative

is defined on
\mathscr{D}
.
Put

where
\varphi
is a compactly supported form. Thus,

\min_{t\in\mathbb{R}}F_{\varphi}(t)=m=F_{\varphi}(0)

and

\frac{d}{dt}\Big(F_{\varphi}(t)\Big)\Big|_{t=0}=0,

since
\eta
is a critical point (minimum) of the strictly convex functional

\mathscr{D}
,
that is,

Then we can calculate

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\Big(E(\eta+td\varphi)\Big)

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{M}\frac{d}{dt}\Big(\langle\eta+td\varphi,\,\eta+td\varphi\rangle^{\frac{p}{2}}\Big)dv

Error estimates for deterministic empirical approximations of probability measures Thanks: Partially supported by the National Science Foundation award DMS-2437066
Benjamin Seeger

Abstract
. The question of approximating an arbitrary probability measure in the Wasserstein distance by a discrete one with uniform weights is considered. Estimates are obtained for the rate of convergence as the number of points tends to infinity, depending on the moment parameter, the parameter in the Wasserstein distance, and the dimension. In certain low-dimensional regimes and for measures with unbounded support, the rates are improvements over those obtained through other methods, including through random sampling. Except for some critical cases, the rates are shown to be optimal.

1.
Introduction

The purpose of this article is to explore the problem of optimally approximating a measure
\mu
belonging to the space
\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{P}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})
of probability measures on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
by a measure of the form

(1)

\mu^{N}_{\boldsymbol{x}}:=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{x_{i}}

for some
{\boldsymbol{x}}=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{N})\in({\mathbb{R}}^{d})^{N}
. We are specifically interested in the quantity

(2)

e_{N;d,p}(\mu):=\inf_{{\boldsymbol{x}}\in({\mathbb{R}^{d}})^{N}}\mathcal{W}_{p}(\mu,\mu^{N}_{x}),

Here, for

\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{p}=\mathcal{P}_{p}({\mathbb{R}^{d}}):=\left\{\mu\in\mathcal{P}:\mathcal{M}_{q}(\mu):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x|^{q}\mu(dx)<\infty\right\},

\mathcal{W}_{p}
is defined by

(3)

\mathcal{W}_{p}(\mu,\nu):=\inf\left\{\left(\iint_{{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\times{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}|x-y|^{p}\gamma(dx,dy)\right)^{1/p}:\gamma\in\Gamma(\mu,\nu)\right\},

where
\Gamma(\mu,\nu)\subset\mathcal{P}_{p}({\mathbb{R}^{d}}\times{\mathbb{R}^{d}})
is the set of probability measures on
{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\times{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
whose first and second marginals are respectively
\mu
and
\nu
;
\mathcal{P}_{p}
then becomes a metric space with distance
\mathbf{d}_{p}:=\mathcal{W}_{p}^{p\wedge 1}
.

Measures of the form
\mu^{N}_{\boldsymbol{x}}
for a fixed
{\boldsymbol{x}}\in({\mathbb{R}^{d}})^{N}
are sometimes called
deterministic empirical measures
or
uniform discrete measures
. The question of estimating
e_{N;d,p}
thus falls within the study of constrained quantization. In the unconstrained setting, a probability measure is approximated by a measure with finite support, and the error

(4)

f_{N;d,p}(\mu):=\inf\left\{\mathcal{W}_{p}(\mu,\nu):\nu\in\mathcal{P},\;\#\supp\nu\leq N\right\}

is analyzed. For fixed
N
and
\mu
, this bounds
e_{N;d,p}(\mu)
from below, since the weights in the approximating measures
\nu
are not constrained.

For a fixed measure
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{q}
with
q>p
, the quantity (
4
) is known to decay at the rate
N^{-1/d}
, up to a multiplicative constant proportional to
\mathcal{M}_{q}(\mu)^{1/q}
. In fact, the limit

\lim_{N\to\infty}N^{1/d}f_{N;d,p}(\mu)

can be explicitly characterized in terms of the absolutely continuous part of
\mu
, as initiated by pioneering works of Fejes Tóth
[
19
]
and Zador
[
31
]
. A general survey of such problems can be found in the work of Graf and Luschgy
[
24
]
.

From the lower bound
e_{N;d,p}\geq f_{N;d,p}
, it is immediate that, for any measure
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{q}
with
q>p
that is not singular with respect to the Lebesgue measure on
{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
,

(5)

e_{N;d,p}(\mu)\gtrsim\mathcal{M}_{q}(\mu)^{1/q}N^{-1/d}.

The question of bounding
e_{N;d,p}
from above has received recent attention due to the applications in statistics and computational mathematics
[
6
,
14
,
16
,
26
,
23
,
22
]
. It has also become relevant in particle approximations of optimal transport and other mean field models
[
5
,
18
,
25
,
28
]
, particularly in optimization problems for interacting agent systems such as in mean field optimal control and mean field games. The quantity
e_{N;d,p}
, sometimes with a different metric than
\mathcal{W}_{p}
, then arises in the analysis of error estimates between the finite-agent system and its mean field description
[
2
,
3
,
9
,
10
,
11
,
8
,
13
]
.

Upper bounds for
e_{N;d,p}(\mu)
were obtained by Chevallier
[
12
]
in arbitrary dimensions, with the one-dimensional case studied in great detail by Xu and Berger
[
30
]
and Bencheikh and Jourdain
[
4
]
; the special case of Gaussian measures on a separable Hilbert space is considered in
[
23
]
by Giles, Hefter, Mayer, and Ritter. In particular, it is proved in
[
12
]
that if
\supp\mu
is contained in a ball of radius
r>0
(i.e
\mathcal{M}_{\infty}(\mu)\leq r
), then

(6)

e_{N;d,p}(\mu)\lesssim r\times\begin{cases}N^{-1/p}&\text{if }d<p,\\
N^{-1/p}\log(1+N)^{1/p}&\text{if }d=p,\\
N^{-1/d}&\text{if }d>p.\end{cases}

In view of the lower bound (
5
) for nonsingular measures, the rate is optimal for
d>p
. It can be seen by example (see
[
30
,
4
]
) that the rates for
d<p
are optimal as well, so that there is a discrepancy in the optimal rates between the unconstrained and uniform-weight settings in this low-dimensional regime.

The case of
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{q}
with
q<\infty
is also treated in
[
12
]
using a truncation method, in which case, for
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{q}
for
q>p
, the rates in (
6
) are raised to the power
1-\frac{p}{q}
. Recently, however, sharper rates were proved when
d>\frac{pq}{q-p}
by Quattrocchi
[
27
]
, who in this regime obtained explicit characterizations of the lower and upper limits

\liminf_{N\to\infty}N^{1/d}e_{N;d,p}(\mu)\quad\text{and}\quad\limsup_{N\to\infty}N^{1/d}e_{N;d,p}(\mu)

in the same spirit as Zador’s theorem. In particular, the upper limit is finite, while the lower limit is positive as soon as
\mu
is nonsingular with respect to Lebesgue measure.

Upper bounds for
e_{N;d,p}
can also be obtained via probabilistic methods. Namely, if the points
{\boldsymbol{x}}=(x^{1},x^{2},\ldots,x^{N})
are chosen to be i.i.d. random variables
{\boldsymbol{X}}=(X^{1},X^{2},\ldots,X^{N})
with distribution
\mu
, then the expected value

(7)

\widetilde{e}_{N;d,p}(\mu):=\left({\mathbb{E}}\mathcal{W}_{p}(\mu,\mu^{N}_{\boldsymbol{X}})^{p}\right)^{1/p}

is an upper bound for
e_{N;d,p}(\mu)
.

The question of estimating
\widetilde{e}_{N;d,p}(\mu)
has been extensively studied, with earlier results by Ajtai, Komlós, and Tusnády
[
1
]
and Talagrand
[
29
]
motivated by optimal matching. Sharp (non-asymptotic) results and concentration inequalities were obtained by Fournier and Guillin
[
21
]
, adopting the multiscale approach introduced by Dereich, Scheutzow, and Schottstedt
[
15
]
. We also mention the work of Boissard and Le Gouic
[
7
]
where the general setting of measures on a Polish space is considered using the idea of the covering number in the spirit of Dudley
[
17
]
. For more detailed discussions, we refer to
[
21
]
and the references therein; see also
[
20
]
, where precise proportionality constants are derived for the rates obtained in
[
21
]
.

Among other things, it is proved in
[
21
]
that the error estimate
N^{-1/d}
for
\widetilde{e}_{N;d,p}(\mu)
can be obtained for
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{q}
in the regime where

d>2p\quad\text{and}\quad d>\frac{pq}{q-p},

which therefore agrees with the same nonasymptotic bound of
[
27
, Theorem 1.7]
in this regime. Meanwhile, if it so happens that
q<2p
, then we may consider the regime

2p<d<\frac{pq}{q-p},

in which case the error estimate for
\widetilde{e}_{N;d,p}(\mu)
(and therefore
e_{N;d,p}(\mu)
) obtained by
[
21
]
is of the order
N^{-1/p+1/q}
, which is an improvement on the rate
N^{-1/d+1/q}
obtained in
[
12
]
in that regime.

On the other hand, if
q>2p
(so that
2p>\frac{pq}{q-p}
) and

p<d<\frac{pq}{q-p},

then
[
21
]
gives the error estimate
N^{-\frac{1}{2p}}
. This has to do with variance bounds for (
7
). Indeed, if
\mu=\mu_{\theta}
is the Bernoulli distribution with parameter
\theta\in(0,1)
, i.e.
\mu_{\theta}=\theta\delta_{1}+(1-\theta)\delta_{0}
, then, for any
p>0
,

\widetilde{e}_{N;1,p}(\mu_{\theta})=\left({\mathbb{E}}\mathcal{W}_{p}(\mu,\mu^{N}_{\boldsymbol{X}})^{p}\right)^{1/p}=\left({\mathbb{E}}\left|\frac{X_{1}+X_{2}+\cdots+X_{N}}{N}-\theta\right|\right)^{1/p}\approx N^{-\frac{1}{2p}}.

This example indicates the particular rate
N^{-\frac{1}{2p}}
is sharp for
\widetilde{e}_{N;1,p}
. On the other hand, observe that

e_{N;1,p}(\mu_{\theta})\approx N^{-\frac{1}{p}}.

Indeed, (
2
) is minimized in this case by setting
x_{i}=1
for
k:=\lfloor N\theta\rfloor
values of
i
and
x_{i}=0
for the other
N-k
values. This suggests that the upper bound
e_{N;d,p}\leq\widetilde{e}_{N;d,p}
is not sharp in lower dimensions, where the variance bounds for the latter become nonnegligible.

The main goal of the present work is to provide essentially optimal estimates for
e_{N;d,p}(\mu)
for any dimension and for any
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{q}
with
p<q
, including the critical cases
d=p
and
d=\frac{qp}{q-p}
not yet treated in the literature, as well as improving the known rates in the full region
p\leq d\leq\frac{qp}{q-p}
. The proof, which relies on completely deterministic methods, is algorithmic in nature, in that, given a dimension
d
and
0<p<q\leq\infty
, the placement of the points
x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{N}
can be determined up to knowledge of the measure
\mu
at certain dyadic resolution scales depending only on
d,p,q
. This multiscale argument applies in all regimes, and is amenable to studying whether or not the proportionality constants converge to
0
as
N\to\infty
.

The first main result is as follows:

Theorem 1
.

Fix
0<p<q\leq\infty
. Then there exists a constant
C=C_{d,p,q}>0
such that, for all
N\geq 1
and
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
,

e_{N;d,p}(\mu)\leq C\mathcal{M}_{q}(\mu)^{1/q}\times\begin{cases}N^{-1/d}\vee N^{-1/p+1/q}&\text{if }d\neq\frac{pq}{q-p},\\
N^{-1/d}\log(1+N)^{1/d}&\text{if }d=\frac{pq}{q-p}.\end{cases}

Moreover, if
q<\infty
and
d<\frac{qp}{q-p}
, then, for any
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{q}
,

\lim_{N\to\infty}N^{1/p-1/q}e_{N;d,p}(\mu)=0.

Theorem
1
recovers the results of
[
12
, Theorem III.3]
when
q=\infty
(the critical case in that setting being
d=p
, where the logarithmic correction arises); the rates of
[
12
, Corollary III.5]
when
q<\infty
and
d<p
; and the rates of
[
27
]
when
d>\frac{pq}{q-p}
. In the regime
p\leq d\leq\frac{pq}{q-p}
, it provides strictly improved rates over those obtained in
[
12
, Corollary III.5]
(where the rate is
N^{-1/d+1/q}
) and the rates in the same regime with
q>2p
implied by the method of randomly sampling
[
21
, Theorem 1]
(where the rate is
N^{-1/2p}
).

We obtain analogous results for measures with weak
q
-moments, i.e. measures belonging to

\mathcal{P}_{q,\mathrm{w}}=\mathcal{P}_{q,\mathrm{w}}({\mathbb{R}^{d}}):=\left\{\mu\in\mathcal{P}:\mathcal{M}_{q,\mathrm{w}}(\mu):=\sup_{\lambda\geq 0}\lambda^{q}\mu(\{x\in{\mathbb{R}^{d}}:|x|\geq\lambda\})<\infty\right\}.

Theorem 2
.

With the same set-up as Theorem
1
, we have, for some
C=C_{d,p,q}>0
and for all
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{q,\mathrm{w}}
,

e_{N;d,p}(\mu)\leq C\mathcal{M}_{q,\mathrm{w}}(\mu)^{1/q}\times\begin{cases}N^{-1/d}\vee N^{-1/p+1/q}&\text{if }d\neq\frac{pq}{q-p},\\
N^{-1/d}\log(1+N)^{1/p}&\text{if }d=\frac{pq}{q-p}.\end{cases}

Theorems
1
and
2
are proved in Section
2
, and the optimality of the rates is discussed in Section
3
. Throughout the paper, given
a,b\in{\mathbb{R}}
and a set
A
, we use the notation
a\lesssim_{A}b
to mean that there exists a constant
C>0
depending only on the elements of
A
such that
a\leq Cb
. We write
a\lesssim b
for the case
A=\{d,p,q\}
.

2.
Multiscale analysis and proof of the error estimates

The key technique in our analysis, which originates from ideas in
[
15
]
and is the basis for the arguments in
[
21
]
, is to control the Wasserstein distance by a certain multiscale quantity. For
n\geq 0
, define
Q_{n}=(-2^{n},2^{n}]^{d}
. For
\ell\geq 0
, let
\mathcal{D}_{\ell}
be the natural partition of
Q_{0}=(-1,1]^{d}
into
2^{\ell d}
translates of the cube
(-2^{-\ell},2^{\ell}]^{d}
. Set
B_{1}=Q_{0}
and, for
n\geq 2
, define
B_{n}=Q_{n}\backslash Q_{n-1}=(-2^{n},2^{n}]^{d}\backslash(-2^{n-1},2^{n-1}]^{d}
. Define the multiscale quantity

(8)

\mathcal{L}_{p}(\mu,\nu):=\sum_{n\geq 0}2^{pn}\sum_{\ell\geq 0}2^{-p\ell}\sum_{F\in\mathcal{D}_{\ell}}|\mu(2^{n}F\cap B_{n})-\nu(2^{n}F\cap B_{n})|.

Then, for all
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{p}
, we have
[
21
, Lemmas 5 and 6]

(9)

\mathcal{W}_{p}(\mu,\nu)^{p}\lesssim_{d,p}\mathcal{L}_{p}(\mu,\nu).

Observe that, for any
{\boldsymbol{x}}=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{N})\in({\mathbb{R}}^{d})^{N}
and Borel set
A\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,

\mu^{N}_{\boldsymbol{x}}(A)=\frac{\#\{i:x_{i}\in A\}}{N},

and so the question of minimizing (
8
) reduces to assigning the correct number of points
x_{i}
to the various sets
2^{n}F\cap B_{n}
. The next two results explain the technique for doing so.

Lemma 3
.

Assume
a\geq 0
,
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
, and

\left|a-\frac{k}{N}\right|\leq\frac{1}{N}.

Suppose that
a=\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}
for some
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
a_{i}\geq 0
. Then there exist
k_{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
,
i=1,2,\ldots,m
, such that

\sum_{i=1}^{m}k_{i}=k\quad\text{and}\quad\left|a_{i}-\frac{k_{i}}{N}\right|\leq\frac{1}{N}\quad\text{for all }i=1,2,\ldots,m.

Proof.

Suppose first that
a=a_{1}+a_{2}
. Assume without loss of generality that

\frac{k}{N}\leq a<\frac{k+1}{N};

the case where
\frac{k-1}{N}\leq a<\frac{k}{N}
is similar. Let
i,j\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
be such that

\frac{i}{N}\leq a_{1}<\frac{i+1}{N}\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{j}{N}\leq a_{2}<\frac{j+1}{N}.

Then

\frac{i+j}{N}\leq a<\frac{i+j+2}{N}.

If
a<\frac{i+j+1}{N}
, then in fact
k=i+j
and we take
i=k_{1}
and
j=k_{2}
. Otherwise,
a\geq\frac{i+j+1}{N}
, in which case
k=i+j+1
, and we may take say
k_{1}=i+1
and
k_{2}=j
.

Now fix
m\geq 1
and suppose that the statement of the lemma holds for any integer up to
m
. Assume

a=\sum_{i=1}^{m+1}a_{i}

for some
a_{i}\geq 0
. Then, as was shown above, there exist
k_{m+1},\widetilde{k}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
such that
k=\widetilde{k}+k_{m+1}
,

\left|\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}-\frac{\widetilde{k}}{N}\right|\leq\frac{1}{N},\quad\text{and}\quad\left|a_{m+1}-\frac{k_{m+1}}{N}\right|\leq\frac{1}{N}.

We conclude in view of the induction hypothesis.
∎

Lemma 4
.

Let
\mu
be a nonnegative, locally-finite Borel measure on
{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
. Fix
N\geq 0
,
n\geq 0
, and
\ell_{0}\geq 0
, and assume
N_{n}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
is such that

\left|\mu(B_{n})-\frac{N_{n}}{N}\right|\leq\frac{1}{N}.

Then there exist nonnegative integers
k^{\ell}_{F}
indexed by
\ell=0,1,2,\ldots,\ell_{0}
and
F\in\mathcal{D}_{\ell}
such that
k^{0}_{B_{1}}=N_{n}
; for any
\ell=1,2,\ldots,\ell_{0}
and
F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{D}_{\ell-1}
,

\sum_{F\in\mathcal{D}_{\ell},\,F\subset F^{\prime}}k^{\ell}_{F}=k^{\ell-1}_{F^{\prime}};

and, for all
\ell=0,1,\ldots,\ell_{0}
and
F\in\mathcal{D}_{\ell}
,

\left|\mu(2^{n}F\cap B_{n})-\frac{k^{\ell}_{F}}{N}\right|\leq\frac{1}{N}.

Proof.

We first define
k^{0}_{B_{1}}:=N_{n}
, and then, since

\mu(B_{n})=\sum_{F\in\mathcal{D}_{1}}\mu(2^{n}F\cap B_{n}),

we can find the appropriate integers
(k^{1}_{F})_{F\in\mathcal{D}_{1}}
by Lemma
3
. We then inductively apply that same Lemma in view of the fact that, for each
F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{D}_{\ell-1}
,

\mu(2^{n}F^{\prime}\cap B_{n})=\sum_{F\in\mathcal{D}_{\ell},\;F\subset F^{\prime}}\mu(2^{n}F\cap B_{n}).

∎

Proof of Theorem
1
.

Fix
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{q}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})
. By a scaling argument, we may assume
\mathcal{M}_{q}(\mu)\leq 1
. In particular, if
q=\infty
, then
\supp\mu\in(-1,1]^{d}
, and then one can proceed to Step 2 below, taking
n_{0}=1
, and ignoring Step 3. Otherwise, if
q<\infty
, we proceed with Step 1.

Step 1: bounding the support of
\mu^{N}_{\boldsymbol{x}}
.
Suppose
q<\infty
, in which case we assume without loss of generality that
\supp\mu
is unbounded; otherwise, the bounds can be recovered from the estimates for
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{\infty}
.

Let
n_{0}\in{\mathbb{N}}
be defined by

n_{0}=n_{0}(N):=\inf\left\{n\in{\mathbb{N}}:\mu({\mathbb{R}^{d}}\backslash Q_{n})\leq\frac{1}{N}\right\}.

Observe that
N\to n_{0}(N)
is nondecreasing, and, because
\mu
has unbounded support, we must have
n_{0}(N)\nearrow\infty
as
N\to\infty
. Now set

\tau_{n}:=2^{qn}\mu({\mathbb{R}^{d}}\backslash Q_{n}),

which satisfies

0<\tau_{n}\leq 2^{q}\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{n\to\infty}\tau_{n}=0.

In particular,

2^{-q(n_{0}-1)}\tau_{n_{0}-1}>\frac{1}{N},

and so, setting
\varepsilon_{N}:=2^{q}\tau_{n_{0}(N)-1}
, we have
\lim_{N\to\infty}\varepsilon_{N}=0
and

(10)

2^{qn_{0}(N)}\leq N\varepsilon_{N}.

We impose that
x_{i}\in Q_{n_{0}}
for all
i
. In other words, for any
n>n_{0}
, we have
\mu^{N}_{{\boldsymbol{x}}}(B_{n})=0
, and so

\displaystyle\sum_{n>n_{0}}2^{pn}

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\geq 0}2^{-p\ell}\sum_{F\in\mathcal{D}_{\ell}}|\mu(2^{n}F\cap B_{n})-\mu^{N}_{\boldsymbol{x}}(2^{n}F\cap B_{n})|

\displaystyle=\sum_{n>n_{0}}2^{pn}\sum_{\ell\geq 0}2^{-p\ell}\sum_{F\in\mathcal{D}_{\ell}}\mu(2^{n}F\cap B_{n})

\displaystyle=\sum_{n>n_{0}}2^{pn}\mu(B_{n})\sum_{\ell\geq 0}2^{-p\ell}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\sum_{n>n_{0}}2^{qn}\mu(B_{n})\right)^{p/q}\left(\sum_{n>n_{0}}\mu(B_{n})\right)^{1-p/q}

(11)

\displaystyle\leq N^{-1+p/q}\kappa_{N},

where

\kappa_{N}:=\left(\sum_{n>n_{0}(N)}2^{nq}\mu(B_{n})\right)\xrightarrow{N\to\infty}0.

Step 2: assigning the atoms.
Now, since
\mu^{N}_{\boldsymbol{x}}(Q_{n_{0}})=1
and

\mu(Q_{n_{0}})=1-\mu({\mathbb{R}}^{d}\backslash Q_{n_{0}})\geq 1-\frac{1}{N},

it follows from Lemma
3
that there exist nonnegative integers
(N_{n})_{n=0}^{n_{0}}
such that

\sum_{n=0}^{n_{0}}N_{n}=N\quad\text{and}\quad\left|\mu(B_{n})-\frac{N_{n}}{N}\right|\leq\frac{1}{N}\quad\text{for all }n=0,1,2,\ldots,n_{0}.

Fix
n=0,1,2,\ldots,n_{0}
, and choose
\ell_{0}>0
, possibly depending on
n
. Let
(k^{\ell}_{F})
be the nonnegative integers as specified by Lemma
4
. Then we can choose exactly
N_{n}
of the points
x_{i}
so that

\mu^{N}_{\boldsymbol{x}}(2^{n}F\cap B_{n})=\frac{k^{\ell}_{F}}{N}\quad\text{for all }\ell=0,1,2,\ldots,\ell_{0}\quad\text{and}\quad F\in\mathcal{D}_{\ell}.

For any
\ell
, we have

\sum_{F\in\mathcal{D}_{\ell}}|\mu(2^{n}F\cap B_{n})-\mu^{N}_{\boldsymbol{x}}(2^{n}F\cap B_{n})|\leq\mu(B_{n})+\mu^{N}_{\boldsymbol{x}}(B_{n})=:\delta_{n}^{N}.

Observe then that
\delta_{n}^{N}\leq 2\mu(B_{n})+\frac{1}{N}
, so that, in particular,

\delta_{n}^{N}\lesssim 2^{-qn}+\frac{1}{N}\lesssim 2^{-qn}

and

(12)

\sum_{n=0}^{n_{0}}2^{qn}\delta_{n}^{N}\lesssim\sum_{n=0}^{n_{0}}2^{qn}\mu(B_{n})+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{n_{0}}2^{qn}\lesssim\mathcal{M}_{q}(\mu)+\frac{2^{qn_{0}}}{N}\lesssim 1.

The estimates in Lemma
4
then yield

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\geq 0}2^{-p\ell}\sum_{F\in\mathcal{D}_{\ell}}|\mu(2^{n}F\cap B_{n})-\mu^{N}_{\boldsymbol{x}}(2^{n}F\cap B_{n})|

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\ell_{0}}2^{-p\ell}\sum_{F\in\mathcal{D}_{\ell}}\left|\mu(2^{n}F\cap B_{n})-\frac{k_{F}^{\ell}}{N}\right|+\sum_{\ell=\ell_{0}}^{\infty}2^{-p\ell}\delta_{n}^{N}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\ell=0}^{\ell_{0}}2^{-(p-d)\ell}\frac{1}{N}+\sum_{\ell=\ell_{0}}^{\infty}2^{-p\ell}\delta_{n}^{N}

(13)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\varepsilon_{\ell_{0}}}{N}+2^{-p\ell_{0}}\delta_{n}^{N},

where

\varepsilon_{\ell_{0}}=\begin{cases}1,&d<p,\\
\ell_{0},&d=p,\\
2^{(d-p)\ell_{0}},&d>p.\end{cases}

If
d<p
, we choose
\ell_{0}
sufficiently large relative to
n
that the first term on the left-hand side of (
2
) is dominant. If
d=p
and
q<\infty
, we use the fact that
N\delta_{n}^{N}\lesssim 2^{q(n_{0}-n)}
, and choose

\ell_{0}:=\Bigg\lfloor\frac{q}{p}(n_{0}-n)\Bigg\rfloor;

otherwise, if
d=p
and
q=\infty
(in which case
n_{0}=1
, as discussed above), we take

\ell_{0}:=\Bigg\lfloor\frac{\log N}{p\log 2}\Bigg\rfloor.

Finally, when
d>p
, we choose

\ell_{0}=\inf\{\ell\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}:2^{d\ell}\geq N\delta_{n}^{N}\}.

At this point, if
q=\infty
, then we already recover the results of
[
12
, Theorem III.3]
:

\mathcal{W}_{p}(\mu,\mu^{N}_{\boldsymbol{x}})^{p}\lesssim\begin{cases}N^{-1}&\text{if }d<p,\\
\frac{\log(1+N)}{N}&\text{if }d=p,\text{ and}\\
N^{-p/d}&\text{if }d>p.\end{cases}

Step 3: collecting errors.
Proceeding with the case
q<\infty
, we set

\mathcal{L}_{n}:=2^{pn}\sum_{\ell\geq 0}2^{-p\ell}\sum_{F\in\mathcal{D}_{\ell}}|\mu(2^{n}F\cap B_{n})-\mu^{N}_{\boldsymbol{x}}(2^{n}F\cap B_{n})|,

which satisfies, for
0\leq n\leq n_{0}
,

\mathcal{L}_{n}\lesssim\begin{cases}\frac{2^{pn}}{N}&\text{if }d<p,\\
\frac{2^{pn}(n_{0}-n+1)}{N}&\text{if }d=p,\\
\frac{2^{pn}}{N}\vee\left[N^{-p/d}2^{pn}(\delta_{n}^{N})^{1-\frac{p}{d}}\right]&\text{if }d>p.\end{cases}

If
d<p
, we have, in view of (
10
),

\sum_{n=0}^{n_{0}}\mathcal{L}_{n}\lesssim\sum_{n=0}^{n_{0}}\frac{2^{pn}}{N}\lesssim\frac{2^{pn_{0}}}{N}\lesssim\varepsilon_{N}^{p/q}N^{-1+p/q},

and if
d=p
,

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{n_{0}}\mathcal{L}_{n}\lesssim\sum_{n=0}^{n_{0}}\frac{2^{dn}(1+n_{0}-n)}{N}=\frac{1}{N}\left(\sum_{n=0}^{n_{0}}2^{dn}+2^{dn_{0}}\sum_{n=0}^{n_{0}}n2^{-dn}\right)\lesssim\frac{2^{dn_{0}}}{N}\lesssim\varepsilon_{N}^{d/q}N^{-1+d/q}.

If
d>p
, we use (
12
) to estimate

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{n_{0}}N^{-p/d}2^{pn}(\delta_{n}^{N})^{1-\frac{p}{d}}=N^{-p/d}\sum_{n=0}^{n_{0}}2^{-n(q-p-pq/d)}(2^{qn}\delta_{n}^{N})^{1-\frac{p}{d}}

\displaystyle\leq N^{-p/d}\left(\sum_{n=0}^{n_{0}}2^{-n(qd/p-d-q)}\right)^{p/d}\left(\sum_{n=0}^{n_{0}}2^{qn}\delta_{n}^{N}\right)^{1-p/d}\lesssim N^{-p/d}\left(\sum_{n=0}^{n_{0}}2^{-n(qd/p-d-q)}\right)^{p/d},

which implies that

\sum_{n=0}^{n_{0}}\mathcal{L}_{n}\lesssim\begin{cases}\varepsilon_{N}^{p/q}N^{-1+p/q}\vee N^{-p/d}=N^{-p/d}&\text{if }q>\frac{pd}{d-p},\\
\varepsilon_{N}^{p/q}N^{-1+p/q}\vee N^{-p/d}n_{0}^{p/d}\lesssim N^{-p/d}\log(1+N)^{p/d}&\text{if }q=\frac{pd}{d-p},\\
\varepsilon_{N}^{p/q}N^{-1+p/q}\vee N^{-p/d}2^{(p-q+pq/d)n_{0}}\lesssim N^{-1+p/q}\varepsilon_{N}^{p/q+p/d-1}&\text{if }p<q<\frac{pd}{d-p}.\end{cases}

We conclude upon combining the bounds in all cases above with the bound (
11
) from Step 1.
∎

Proof of Theorem
2
.

The proof for measures with weak moments is very similar, and we only indicate the differences with the proof of Theorem
1
. First, assuming again that
\mathcal{M}_{q,\mathrm{w}}(\mu)\leq 1
, we deduce that, for all
n
,
\mu(B_{n})\leq 2^{q}2^{-qn}
. We then define
n_{0}
to be the smallest integer satisfying
N\leq 2^{-q}2^{qn_{0}}
, and note that, rather than (
10
), we instead have the bound
2^{qn_{0}}\leq 2^{2q}N
. As a consequence, the estimate (
11
) instead becomes

\displaystyle\sum_{n>n_{0}}2^{pn}

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\geq 0}2^{-p\ell}\sum_{F\in\mathcal{D}_{\ell}}|\mu(2^{n}F\cap B_{n})-\mu^{N}_{\boldsymbol{x}}(2^{n}F\cap B_{n})|\lesssim 2^{-(q-p)n_{0}}\lesssim N^{-1+p/q}.

The beginning of Step 2 proceeds the same since now

\mu(Q_{n_{0}})=1-\mu({\mathbb{R}}^{d}\backslash Q_{n_{0}})\geq 1-2^{q}2^{-qn_{0}}\geq 1-\frac{1}{N},

and, as noted in that proof, we have
\delta_{n}^{N}\lesssim 2^{-qn}
. In Step 3 the estimates in the regime
d\leq p
, and the regime where
d>p
and
q<\frac{dp}{d-p}
, lead to the nonasymptotic bound
N^{-1+p/q}
, and, when
d=\frac{pq}{q-p}
, in which case the sequence
2^{qn}\delta_{n}^{N}
is merely bounded, the estimate is

N^{-p/d}n_{0}\lesssim\log(1+N)N^{-p/d}.

All other cases proceed similarly as in the proof of Theorem
1
.
∎

3.
On the optimality of the rates

From the lower bound on
e_{N;d,p}(\mu)
for nonsingular measures discussed in the Introduction the optimality of the rates in Theorem
1
are evident in the regime
\frac{1}{q}<\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{d}
, for which the term
N^{-1/d}
dominates
N^{-1/p+1/q}
.

The optimality of the rate
N^{-1/p+1/q}
when
\frac{1}{q}>\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{d}
can be seen in various ways; note that it suffices in this case to consider measures on
{\mathbb{R}}
(i.e.
d=1
), since these then embed into the higher dimensional spaces up to the critical dimension
\frac{pq}{q-p}
. First, if
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
has bounded and disconnected support, then
[
30
, Remark 5.22]

\limsup_{N\to\infty}N^{1/p}e_{N;1,p}(\mu)>0;

note that this was already observed in the introduction in the context of the Bernoulli distribution. For the case of unbounded support, we point to
[
4
, Theorem 2.3]
and the discussion thereafter. In particular, if
\mu\in\mathcal{P}({\mathbb{R}})
and if

q^{*}:=\sup\{q>0:\mathcal{M}_{q}(\mu)<+\infty\}

is finite, then

\lim_{N\to\infty}N^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}}e_{N;d,p}(\mu)=\begin{cases}0&\text{if }q<q^{*},\\
+\infty&\text{if }q>q^{*}.\end{cases}

We may also assess the optimality of the rates in Theorem
1
, which hold uniformly over
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{q}
with bounded moments, through studying the quantity

(14)

\overline{e}_{N;d,p,q}:=\sup\left\{e_{N;d,p}(\mu):\mathcal{M}_{q}(\mu)\leq 1\right\}.

Proposition 5
.

Assume that
q<\infty
and
\frac{1}{q}>\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{d}
. Then

\inf_{N\geq 1}N^{1/p-1/q}\overline{e}_{N;d,p,q}>0.

We consider measures
\mu\in\mathcal{P}
as distributions of random variables on a sufficiently rich probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
. The key idea behind Proposition
5
, and, indeed, the analysis in the works
[
4
,
30
]
, is that, taking
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})=([0,1],\mathcal{B}([0,1]),\lambda)
with
\lambda
the Lebesgue measure, any
\mu\in\mathcal{P}({\mathbb{R}})
can be identified with the increasing rearrangement
X:(0,1)\to{\mathbb{R}}
given by

X(\omega):=\inf\{t\in{\mathbb{R}}:\mu((-\infty,t])\geq\omega\},\quad\omega\in(0,1),

in which case, defining
\omega_{i}=\frac{i}{N}
,
i=0,1,2,\ldots,N
, we have

(15)

e_{N;1,p}(\mu)=\inf_{{\boldsymbol{x}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\omega_{i-1}}^{\omega_{i}}|X(\omega)-x_{i}|^{p}d\omega\right)^{1/p}.

This greatly simplifies the task of bounding the rate from below. In particular, we may use the following result to generate examples.

Lemma 6
.

Assume
X:[0,1]\to{\mathbb{R}}
is increasing and
\dot{X}
is decreasing and continuous on
[j/N,k/N]
for some
0\leq j<k\leq N
, and let
\mu=X_{\sharp}\lambda
be the probability distribution of
X
on
\mathbb{R}
. Then

e_{N;1,p}(\mu)\gtrsim_{p}\frac{1}{N^{1+1/p}}\left(\sum_{i=j+1}^{k}\dot{X}(\omega_{i})^{p}\right)^{1/p}.

Proof.

Let
x_{i}
be the optimal point corresponding to the interval
[\omega_{i-1},\omega_{i}]
on the right-hand side of (
15
). Then
X(\omega_{i-1})\leq x_{i}\leq X(\omega_{i})
, and so there exists
s_{i}\in I_{i}
such that
X(s_{i})=x_{i}
. Then

\displaystyle e_{N;1,p}(\mu)^{p}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\omega_{i-1}}^{\omega_{i}}|X(\omega)-X(s_{i})|^{p}d\omega

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left[\int_{\omega_{i-1}}^{s_{i}}\left(\int_{\omega}^{s_{i}}\dot{X}(r)dr\right)^{p}d\omega+\int_{s_{i}}^{\omega_{i}}\left(\int_{s_{i}}^{\omega}\dot{X}(r)dr\right)^{p}d\omega\right]

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i=j+1}^{k}\dot{X}(\omega_{i})^{p}\left[\int_{\omega_{i-1}}^{s_{i}}\left(s_{i}-\omega\right)^{p}d\omega+\int_{s_{i}}^{\omega_{i}}\left(\omega-s_{i}\right)^{p}d\omega\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p+1}\sum_{i=j+1}^{k}\dot{X}(\omega_{i})^{p}\left[(s_{i}-\omega_{i-1})^{p+1}+(\omega_{i}-s_{i})^{p+1}\right]

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2^{p+1}(p+1)N^{p+1}}\sum_{i=j+1}^{k}\dot{X}(\omega_{i})^{p}.

∎

Proof of Proposition
5
.

We assume without loss of generality that
d=1
. Fix
\gamma>1/q
, and set
\varepsilon=\gamma q-1>0
. Define, for
N\geq 1
,

X(\omega)=X_{N}(\omega)=c_{N}\times\begin{cases}-N^{\gamma}&\text{if }0\leq\omega\leq\frac{1}{N},\\
-\omega^{-\gamma}&\text{if }\frac{1}{N}<\omega\leq 1,\end{cases}\quad\text{where}\quad c_{N}^{q}=\frac{\varepsilon N^{-\varepsilon}}{1+\frac{1-N^{-\varepsilon}}{\varepsilon}}.

It is straightforward to check that
X_{N}
is increasing,
\dot{X}_{N}
is decreasing on
[1/N,1]
,
\left\|X_{N}\right\|_{q}=1
, and the probability distribution
\mu_{N}
of
X_{N}
is given by

\mu_{N}(dx)=\frac{1}{N}\delta_{-c_{N}N^{\gamma}}(dx)+\frac{c_{N}^{1/\gamma}}{\gamma}|x|^{-\frac{1+\gamma}{\gamma}}\mathbf{1}_{(-c_{N}N^{\gamma},-c_{N})}(x)dx.

Then, by Lemma
6
,

\displaystyle e_{N;1,p}(\mu_{N})^{p}\gtrsim_{p,\gamma}c_{N}^{p}\frac{1}{N^{1+p}}\sum_{i=2}^{N}\omega_{i}^{-p(1+\gamma)}\gtrsim N^{-\varepsilon p/q}N^{-1+p\gamma}\sum_{i=2}^{N}i^{-p(1+\gamma)}\gtrsim N^{-1+p/q},

and so

\inf_{N}N^{1/p-1/q}\overline{e}_{N;1,p,q}\geq\inf_{N}N^{1/p-1/q}e_{N;1,p}(\mu_{N})\gtrsim 1.

∎

Proposition
5
implies that, given
0<p<q<\infty
and
d<\frac{pq}{q-p}
, there cannot exist a single sequence
\alpha_{N}
converging to
0
as
N\to\infty
such that

e_{N;d,p}(\mu)\leq\mathcal{M}_{q}(\mu)^{1/q}\alpha_{N}N^{-1/p+1/q}\quad\text{for all }\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{q}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})\text{ and }N\geq 1.

It is however an interesting question to study how quickly
N^{1/p-1/q}e_{N;d,p}(\mu)
may converge to
0
as
N\to\infty
for a fixed measure
\mu
in this regime.

On the other hand, the optimality of the rates in Theorem
2
in this regime can be seen for a single measure
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{q}
: taking

\mu(dx)=q|x|^{-q-1}\mathbf{1}_{(-\infty,-1)}(x)dx\quad\text{on }{\mathbb{R}},

whose increasing rearrangement is given by
X(\omega)=-\omega^{-1/q}
for
\omega\in[0,1]
, we have
\mathcal{M}_{q,\mathrm{w}}(\mu)=1
and, by Lemma
6
,

e_{N;1,p}(\mu)^{p}\gtrsim\frac{1}{N^{1+p}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\omega_{i}^{-p(1+1/q)}=N^{-1+p/q}\sum_{i=1}^{N}i^{-p(1+q)}\geq N^{-1+p/q}.

We conclude by remarking that, for the critical dimension
d^{*}=\frac{pq}{q-p}
, it is difficult to assess whether or not the logarithmic term is necessary. In the special case
d=1
and
q=\infty
, for instance, it is easy to see by a direct argument that
\overline{e}_{N;1,p,\infty}\lesssim N^{-1/p}\vee N^{-1}
for all
p>0
. In general, seeking useful lower bounds for
\overline{e}_{N;d^{*},p,q}
when
d^{*}>1
is an intricate question.

Generalized Weight Structure of Polar Codes: Selected Template Polynomials
Mohammad Rowshan, Vlad-Florin Drăgoi

Abstract
Polar codes can be viewed as decreasing monomial codes, revealing a rich algebraic structure governed by the lower-triangular affine (LTA) group. We develop a general framework to compute the Hamming weight of codewords generated by sums of monomials, express these weights in a canonical dyadic form, and derive closed expressions for key structural templates (disjoint sums, nested blocks, complementary flips) that generate the low and intermediate weight spectrum. Combining these templates with the LTA group action, we obtain explicit multiplicity formulas, yielding a unified algebraic method to characterize and enumerate codewords.



I
Introduction

Polar codes, introduced by Arıkan
[
2
]
, form the first explicit family of codes that provably achieve the symmetric capacity of binary-input memoryless channels. They were adopted in 3GPP 5G NR for control channels
[
1
]
, which triggered a large body of work on constructions and decoding algorithms. In contrast, comparatively less attention has been devoted to their algebraic structure and, in particular, to the distance spectrum and exact enumeration of low-weight codewords.

The weight distribution directly governs the block error probability under maximum-likelihood (ML) decoding via the union bound
[
10
, Sect. 10.1]
. The leading terms involve the minimum distance and the multiplicities of minimum and near-minimum weight codewords (the
error coefficient
). Even for polar codes, only partial answers are known.

A major step forward was recognizing that polar and Reed–Muller codes are both
decreasing monomial codes

[
3
,
4
]
, which exposes a large permutation group—the lower triangular affine (LTA) group—used to characterize minimum-weight codewords
[
4
]
, accelerate decoding via permutations
[
8
]
, and study automorphisms. Closed-form enumerators are now known for minimum-weight codewords
[
4
,
12
]
, for weights in
[1.5\w_{\min},2\w_{\min})

[
5
,
16
,
14
,
6
]
, and for weight
2\w_{\min}

[
14
]
, mainly for low- and high-rate regimes where the full weight distribution can be determined; see also related enumeration methods in
[
15
,
7
,
11
]
.

In this paper, we advance the algebraic analysis of decreasing monomial codes by developing a general multiscale framework for their weight structure. We derive a general inclusion-exclusion formula for the Hamming weight of polynomials of the form
P(x)=h(x)\sum_{i}f_{i}(x)
and introduce a dyadic decomposition of the normalized weight that explicitly characterizes fractional corrections
d/2^{\ell}
arising from monomial overlaps. Building on this, we establish closed-form weight expressions for key structural templates and utilize LTA group orbits to formulate exact counting multiplicities. This purely algebraic framework is applicable to any decreasing monomial code, including standard polar codes and Reed–Muller codes.

II
Monomial Representation and Decreasing Sets

We work over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
with modulo two addition. For
N=2^{m}
, a codeword is a vector
\bm{c}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{N}
, with Hamming weight
\operatorname{wt}(\bm{c})
and support
\supp(\bm{c})
.

II-A

Boolean functions and monomials

Let
x_{0},\dots,x_{m-1}
be Boolean variables and consider the quotient ring
\mathbb{R}_{m}\triangleq\mathbb{F}_{2}[x_{0},\dots,x_{m-1}]/\left\langle x_{i}^{2}-x_{i}\right\rangle_{i=0}^{m-1},
so a polynomial is a Boolean function on
\{0,1\}^{m}
. A monomial is

f=\prod_{j=0}^{m-1}x_{j}^{i_{j}},\qquad i_{j}\in\{0,1\}.

Let
\mathcal{M}_{m}
be the set of all monomials. If
f=x_{l_{1}}\cdots x_{l_{s}}
with
l_{1}<\dots<l_{s}
, define

\operatorname{ind}(f)\triangleq\{l_{1},\dots,l_{s}\},\qquad\deg(f)\triangleq s
.
The
evaluation map

\mathrm{ev}:\mathbb{R}_{m}\to\mathbb{F}_{2}^{2^{m}}
evaluates a polynomial at all points of
\{0,1\}^{m}
in some fixed order; it is a vector-space isomorphism. For a Boolean function
f
we write

|f|\triangleq|\{x\in\{0,1\}^{m}:f(x)=1\}|=\operatorname{wt}(\mathrm{ev}(f)).

If
f
is a monomial of degree
t
, its support consists of all points where its
t
variables are
1
and the remaining
m-t
variables are free; hence

\operatorname{wt}(\mathrm{ev}(f))=2^{m-\deg(f)}.

(1)

Monomial codes and decreasing sets

.

Let

\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{I})\triangleq\operatorname{span}\{\mathrm{ev}(f)\mid f\in\mathcal{I}\}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{2^{m}}.

Let
r^{+}(\mathcal{C})\triangleq\max_{f\in\mathcal{I}}\deg(f)
. Using (

d_{\min}(\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{I}))=2^{m-r^{+}(\mathcal{C})}

.

, and the decreasing order
\preceq
from
[
3
,
4

.

A set
\mathcal{I}\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{m}
is

and

imply

. A decreasing monomial code is a monomial code

II-C

LTA group and orbits

Let
(\mathbf{B},\bm{\varepsilon})
be an affine map on
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}
with
\mathbf{B}\in\mathrm{GL}(m,\mathbb{F}_{2})
and
\bm{\varepsilon}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}
. The
lower triangular affine group
is

\mathrm{LTA}(m,2)\triangleq\big\{(\mathbf{B},\bm{\varepsilon}):\mathbf{B}\ \text{lower triangular},\ b_{ii}=1\big\}.

Each
(\mathbf{B},\bm{\varepsilon})
acts on the variables as

x_{i}\mapsto y_{i}=x_{i}+\sum_{j<i}b_{ij}x_{j}+\varepsilon_{i},

and on a monomial
f=\prod_{i\in\operatorname{ind}(f)}x_{i}
by substitution:

(\mathbf{B},\bm{\varepsilon})\cdot f\triangleq\prod_{i\in\operatorname{ind}(f)}y_{i}.

This action permutes coordinates of
\mathrm{ev}(f)
, hence preserves Hamming weight. We write the orbit

\mathrm{LTA}(m,2)\cdot f\triangleq\{(\mathbf{B},\bm{\varepsilon})\cdot f\}.

For decreasing monomial codes,
\mathrm{LTA}(m,2)
is a subgroup of the automorphism group of
\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{I})
, and orbits of maximal-degree monomials generate all minimum-weight codewords
[
4
]
.

Definition 3

(Partition weights
|\lambda_{f}|
and
|\lambda_{f}(g)|
)
.

Let
f\!=\!x_{i_{0}}\cdots x_{i_{s-1}}
be a monomial with

0\!\leq\!i_{0}\!<\!\dots\!<\!i_{s-1}\!\leq\!m-1
.
Set
\lambda_{f}\!=\!\bigl(i_{s-1}-(s-1),\,i_{s-2}-(s-2),\,\dots,\,i_{0}-0\bigr)
, then

|\lambda_{f}|\;=\;\sum_{t=0}^{s-1}(i_{t}-t).

More generally, for another monomial

g=x_{j_{0}}\cdots x_{j_{t-1}}
with
0\leq j_{0}<\dots<j_{t-1}\leq m-1
,
define

|\lambda_{f}(g)|\;\triangleq\;\sum_{\ell=0}^{t-1}\Bigl(j_{\ell}-\bigl|\{u\in\operatorname{ind}(f)\cup\{j_{0},\dots,j_{\ell-1}\}:u<j_{\ell}\}\bigr|\Bigr).

Equivalently, for each
j\in\operatorname{ind}(g)
, we count the number of indices
below
j
that are not already occupied by
\operatorname{ind}(f)
or by earlier
variables of
g
.

In particular, for
g=f
this reduces to
|\lambda_{f}f|=|\lambda_{f}|
,
and the LTA-orbit of
f
satisfies

\bigl|\LTA(m,2)\cdot f\bigr|=2^{\deg(f)+|\lambda_{f}|}.

(2)

III
A General Weight Formula for Polynomials

We now derive a general formula for the Hamming weight of polynomials built as a product of a common factor and a sum of residual monomials. This will be our main tool.

Let
\mathcal{P}_{r}
be the set of polynomials in
\mathbb{R}_{m}
of total degree
r
. For
P\in\mathcal{P}_{r}
, we write it in the form

P(x)=h(x)\cdot\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{q}f_{i}(x)\Big),

(3)

where
h
is a monomial (the common factor) and the
f_{i}
are residual monomials. Let
a_{\max}\triangleq\max_{i}\deg(f_{i})
and

\deg(h)=r-a_{\max}.

(4)

This representation always exists (allowing
h=1
). Let

F(x)\triangleq\sum_{i=1}^{q}f_{i}(x).

We normalize weights with respect to degree
r
as

d\triangleq 2^{m-r},\qquad\Sigma(P)\triangleq\frac{\operatorname{wt}(P)}{d}.

For
\varnothing\neq\mathcal{S}\subseteq\{1,\dots,q\}
, define the union degree

u_{\mathcal{S}}\triangleq\deg\Big(\operatorname{lcm}\{f_{i}:i\in\mathcal{S}\}\Big).

Proposition 1

(General weight formula)
.

Let
P(x)=h(x)\sum_{i=1}^{q}f_{i}(x)
be as in (
3
), with
\deg(P)=r
and
d=2^{m-r}
. Then

\operatorname{wt}(P)=d\,\Sigma(F),

(5)

where

\boxed{\Sigma(F)\triangleq\sum_{\varnothing\neq\mathcal{S}\subseteq\{1,\dots,q\}}(-2)^{|\mathcal{S}|-1}\,2^{\,a_{\max}-u_{\mathcal{S}}}.}

(6)

Kernel-based description of weight templates.

By Proposition
1
, once the residual monomials
\{f_{i}\}
(the kernel
Q
of
maximum degree
a_{\max}
) are fixed, the
normalized weight

\Sigma(F)
is fully determined by
the support combinatorics; the head
h
only raises the ambient degree from
a_{\max}
to any

r\geq a_{\max}
via
\deg(h)=r-a_{\max}
, without affecting
\Sigma(F)
. Thus, for any target
normalized weight
\Sigma^{\star}
one can:

(i)

enumerate kernels
Q=\sum_{i}f_{i}
with
\Sigma(F)=\Sigma^{\star}
(via the union degrees
u_{\mathcal{S}}
), and

(ii)

for each such kernel, generate all degree-
r
templates
P=hQ
by choosing monomials
h
of degree
r-a_{\max}
whose supports are disjoint from that of
Q
.

When classifying templates, we also quotient by affine equivalence: kernels
Q,Q^{\prime}
related by an
element of
\LTA(m,2)
yield the same family of patterns up to code automorphisms. In practice, one
therefore works with canonical kernel representatives modulo
\LTA(m,2)
and then attaches all
admissible heads
h
(including
h=1
) for ambient degrees
r\geq a_{\max}
.

Lemma 1

(Dyadic decomposition)
.

Let
\Sigma(F)
be given by (
6
). Define

U\triangleq\max_{\varnothing\neq\mathcal{S}}u_{\mathcal{S}},k\triangleq U-a_{\max}.

Then

\Sigma(F)=\frac{N}{2^{k}}=\sum_{j=j_{\min}}^{j_{\max}}b_{j}2^{\,j-k},

where for
N\!=\!\sum_{j}b_{j}2^{j},b_{j}\!\in\!\{0,1\}
, we have

N\triangleq 2^{U-a_{\max}}\,\Sigma(F)=\sum_{\varnothing\neq\mathcal{S}}(-1)^{|\mathcal{S}|-1}\,2^{\,U-u_{\mathcal{S}}+|\mathcal{S}|-1}\in\mathbb{Z}.

Thus, every
\Sigma(P)

is a dyadic rational of the form

\Sigma(P)=\sum_{\ell=\ell_{\min}}^{\ell_{\max}}\frac{c_{\ell}}{2^{\ell}},

with integer coefficients
c_{\ell}
. In practice, for small
q
one observes that all
c_{\ell}
are odd, and different overlap patterns generate characteristic dyadic signatures.

IV
Structural Templates for Weights

We now instantiate Proposition
1
for several structural templates that occur naturally in decreasing monomial codes. In the next section these templates will be combined with the algebraic structure of
\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{I})
to obtain enumeration formulas.

Lemma 2

(Disjoint
k
-sum)
.

Let
f_{1},\dots,f_{k}
be monomials of degree
r
with pairwise disjoint supports and
kr\leq m
. Let

P(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}f_{i}(x),\qquad r=\deg(P),

(7)

and
d=2^{m-r}
. Then

\operatorname{wt}(P)=2^{m-kr-1}\Big(2^{kr}-(2^{r}-2)^{k}\Big),

(8)

and the normalized weight is

\Sigma(P)=\frac{\operatorname{wt}(P)}{d}=2^{r-1}\Big(1-(1-2^{1-r})^{k}\Big).

(9)

Proof.

Apply Proposition
1
with
h=1
,
q=k
,
a_{\max}=r
and note that
u_{\mathcal{S}}=r|\mathcal{S}|
by disjointness. Grouping terms by
|\mathcal{S}|
yields (
9
).
∎

Remark 1
.

For
\operatorname{wt}(P)
to be of the dyadic form

\operatorname{wt}(P)=d\Bigl(2+\sum_{j\geq 2}\frac{1}{2^{j}}\Bigr),

which forces
2\leq\Sigma_{k}(r)<2.5
, one checks (using
(
9
)) that the only possible case is

r=3,\quad k=3,\quad\Sigma_{3}(3)=2+\frac{1}{4}+\frac{1}{16},

i.e.,

\operatorname{wt}(P)=2^{m-3}\Bigl(2+\frac{1}{4}+\frac{1}{16}\Bigr).

On the other hand, (
9
) also describes precisely when

\operatorname{wt}(P)<2d
, i.e.
\Sigma_{k}(r)<2
. Writing

\Sigma_{k}(r)=2^{r-1}\Bigl(1-(1-2^{1-r})^{k}\Bigr),

we get

\Sigma_{1}(r)=1,\qquad\Sigma_{2}(r)=2-2^{1-r}=1+\sum_{j=1}^{r-1}\frac{1}{2^{j}}<2.

For
r=2
, the formula simplifies to

\Sigma_{k}(2)=2\bigl(1-(1/2)^{k}\bigr)=2-2^{1-k}=1+\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\frac{1}{2^{j}}<2.

Example 1
.

For
r=3
and
k=3
disjoint cubics (e.g.
X_{1}X_{2}X_{3}
,
X_{4}X_{5}X_{6}
,
X_{7}X_{8}X_{9}
),

\Sigma(P)=2^{2}\big(1-(1-2^{-2})^{3}\big)=4\big(1-(3/4)^{3}\big)=\tfrac{37}{16}.

Thus
\operatorname{wt}(P)=(37/16)\,d
with
d=2^{m-3}
.

Lemma 3

(rank-
\ell
and a degree drop)
.

Let
r\geq 2
and
\ell\geq 0
. Let
f_{1},\dots,f_{\ell},g\in\mathbb{F}_{2}[X_{0},\dots,X_{m-1}]

be monomials with

\deg(f_{j})=r\ (1\leq j\leq\ell),\qquad\deg(g)=r-1,

and

\operatorname{ind}(f_{j})\cap\operatorname{ind}(f_{j^{\prime}})=\emptyset\ (j\neq j^{\prime}),\;\operatorname{ind}(g)\cap\operatorname{ind}(f_{j})=\emptyset\ (1\leq j\leq\ell).

Set

P(X)=\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}f_{j}(X)+g(X)

(10)

and fix ambient degree

, so
d=2^{m-r}
. Then

\operatorname{wt}(P)=2^{m-1}\Bigl(1-\bigl(1-2^{1-r}\bigr)^{\ell}\bigl(1-2^{2-r}\bigr)\Bigr)\Bigr).

In particular, for
\ell=0
one has
\operatorname{wt}(P)=2^{m-r+1}=2d
(a single
monomial of degree
r-1
), and for
r=2
one has
\operatorname{wt}(P)=2^{m-1}=2d

for all
\ell\geq 0
.

Lemma 4

(Complementary flip weight)
.

Let
f,g\in\mathcal{R}_{m}
be monomials with
\deg(f)=r
and
\deg(g)=s
, and let

j\in\{0,\dots,m-1\}
satisfy

\operatorname{ind}(g)\cap\bigl(\operatorname{ind}(f)\cup\{j\}\bigr)=\varnothing.

Define

P(X)\;=\;f(X)+(X_{j}+1)\,g(X),

and set
d\triangleq 2^{m-r}
. Then

\operatorname{wt}\bigl(\operatorname{ev}(P)\bigr)\;=\;d\bigl(1+2^{r-s-1}\bigr).

(11)

In particular, if
s=r
then
\operatorname{wt}(\operatorname{ev}(P))=\tfrac{3}{2}d
, and if
s=r-1
then

\operatorname{wt}(\operatorname{ev}(P))=2d
.

Lemma 5

(Shared 3-term kernels)
.

Let
r\geq 3
and let
h
be a monomial of degree
\deg(h)=r-3
whose
variables are disjoint from
\{X_{1},\dots,X_{7}\}
. Set
d=2^{m-r}
and
ambient degree
r=\deg(h)+3
.
Define

P_{B}(X)=h(X)\Bigl(\underbrace{X_{1}X_{2}X_{3}}_{f_{1}}+\underbrace{X_{2}X_{4}X_{5}}_{f_{2}}+\underbrace{X_{3}X_{4}X_{6}}_{f_{3}}\Bigr),

and set
Q_{B}=f_{1}+f_{2}+f_{3}
,
Q_{C}=g_{1}+g_{2}+g_{3}

\operatorname{wt}(P_{B})=\operatorname{wt}(P_{C})=2^{m-r+1}=2d,\quad\Sigma(P_{B})=\Sigma(P_{C})=2.

IV-A

Nesting (weight scaling)

Lemma 6

(Nesting / weight-scaling)
.

Let
h
be a monomial of degree
s
and
Q
a polynomial whose variables are disjoint from those of
h
, with maximum monomial degree
t
. Fix an ambient degree
r\geq s+t
and define

P=h\cdot Q.

Let
d_{Q}=2^{m-t}
and
d=2^{m-r}
, and write

\Sigma(Q)\triangleq\frac{\operatorname{wt}(Q)}{d_{Q}},\qquad\Sigma(P)\triangleq\frac{\operatorname{wt}(P)}{d}.

Then

\Sigma(P)=2^{\,r-(s+t)}\,\Sigma(Q).

(12)

In particular, if
r=s+t
then
\Sigma(P)=\Sigma(Q)
.

Proof.

Because
h
and
Q
are variable-disjoint,
hQ
is
1
exactly when
h=1
and
Q=1
. Thus
\operatorname{wt}(P)=\operatorname{wt}(Q)/2^{s}
. Normalizing,

\Sigma(P)=\frac{\operatorname{wt}(Q)/2^{s}}{2^{m-r}}=\Sigma(Q)\cdot 2^{\,r-(s+t)}.

∎

(Nesting of

V
Enumeration of Templates’ Codewords

In this section, we give closed-form enumerator formulas, in the spirit of the minimum-weight expression, for the structural templates introduced earlier.

V-A

General orbit-counting scheme for weight templates

All our counting formulas follow the same pattern. We start from a
seed

P(X)=h(X)\,Q(X),

where
h
is a monomial (
head
) and
Q
is a polynomial
(
kernel
). Write

Q(X)=Q_{\max}(X)+R(X),

where
Q_{\max}
is the sum of all highest-degree monomials of
Q
and

R
collects the lower-degree terms:

Q_{\max}(X)=\sum_{i=1}^{\mu}t_{i}(X),\quad\deg(t_{i})=r_{\max},\quad\deg(R)<r_{\max},

with all
t_{i}
supported outside
\operatorname{ind}(h)
. The degree of
P
is

r=\deg(h)+r_{\max},\qquad d=2^{m-r}.

We work in the
head stabilizer

G_{h}\;\triangleq\;\mathrm{Stab}_{\LTA(m,2)}(h)\;=\;\{\gamma\in\LTA(m,2):\gamma\cdot h=h\}.

Thus,
h
is fixed but the kernel
Q
may move under
G_{h}
. For a seed

P=hQ
the head-fixed orbit

\mathcal{O}(P)\;\triangleq\;G_{h}\cdot P

consists of all polynomials of the form
h\,(\gamma\cdot Q)
with

\gamma\in G_{h}
.

Head contribution.

For a monomial
h
with partition weight
|\lambda_{h}|
, we have

|\LTA(m,2)\cdot h|=2^{\deg(h)+|\lambda_{h}|},

and the same exponent counts the free LTA parameters on the variables
in
\operatorname{ind}(h)
inside
G_{h}
.
In the special case
h\equiv 1
, we have
\deg(h)=|\lambda_{h}|=0
and

G_{h}=\LTA(m,2)
.

Head contribution.

For a monomial
h
with partition weight
|\lambda_{h}|
, the usual LTA
orbit formula gives

|\LTA(m,2)\cdot h|=2^{\deg(h)+|\lambda_{h}|}.

In the special case
h\equiv 1
we have
\deg(h)=|\lambda_{h}|=0
and

G_{h}=\LTA(m,2)
.

Kernel decomposition:
Q_{\max}
and
R
.

The kernel
Q
may contain lower-degree monomials
R
that are:

•

Fixed or template-determined:
in many low-weight templates (e.g. complementary flip)
R
is uniquely determined once the head
h

and the highest-degree tails
t_{i}
are fixed. In this case,
R

does not introduce extra parameters; for any

\gamma\in G_{h}
, the image
\gamma\cdot R
is a
deterministic function of
\gamma\cdot Q_{\max}
.

•

Independent (disjoint variables):
if
\operatorname{ind}(R)
is disjoint from
\operatorname{ind}(h)\cup\bigcup_{i}\operatorname{ind}(t_{i})
,
the monomials of
R
form an additional block of tails with their
own LTA degrees of freedom and possible collisions. Their
contribution factors multiplicatively into the orbit exponent
(see below).

•

Partially shared:
if some variables in
R
are shared with
h
or with the
t_{i}
,
the tails of
R
are simply added to the global tail set, and
their effect is absorbed into the same
\beta_{Q}
and
\alpha_{Q}

parameters defined below.

Tail contribution and stabilizer (collisions)

Write the kernel as a sum of monomials

Q=\sum_{j=1}^{\nu}u_{j},\quad\deg(u_{j})\leq r_{\max},\quad\operatorname{ind}(u_{j})\cap\operatorname{ind}(h)=\varnothing.

The corresponding degree-
r
monomials in
P
are

f_{j}\;=\;h\,u_{j},\qquad 1\leq j\leq\nu.

Knowing that the affine action of
\LTA(m,2)

on monomials is multiplicative
[
13
]
, for every tail
u_{j}
and
every
\gamma\in G_{h}
,

\gamma\cdot(h\,u_{j})=(\gamma\cdot h)(\gamma\cdot u_{j})=h\,(\gamma\cdot u_{j}).

Thus, the head
h
is fixed and the group acts only on the tails

u_{j}
. In particular, the orbit of
f_{j}
under
G_{h}
is in bijection
with the
tail–orbit

\mathcal{O}_{j}\;\triangleq\;G_{h}\cdot u_{j}.

The degree-
r
and lower-degree parts of
P
generated by the tails are
described by the Minkowski sum

\mathcal{S}\;\triangleq\;\mathcal{O}_{1}+\cdots+\mathcal{O}_{\nu}=\{a_{1}+\cdots+a_{\nu}:a_{j}\in\mathcal{O}_{j}\}.

Up to the common factor
h
, the degree-
r
term of
\gamma\cdot P
is
h(a_{1}+\cdots+a_{\nu})
for some
a_{j}\in\mathcal{O}_{j}
, so every collision among degree-
r
monomials of
P
is induced by a collision in
\mathcal{S}
.
For each pair
(i,j)
with
1\leq i<j\leq\nu
, we define the

pairwise collision exponent

\alpha_{Q}\;\triangleq\;\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq\nu}\alpha_{u_{i},u_{j}},\quad|\mathcal{S}|\;=\;\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{\nu}|\mathcal{O}_{j}|}{2^{\alpha_{Q}}}.

Tail and linear degrees of freedom on the kernel.

\boxed{\bigl|G_{h}\cdot P\bigr|\;=\;2^{\,\deg(h)+|\lambda_{h}|+\beta_{Q}-\alpha_{Q}}.}

Appendix A
Inclusion-Exclusion Formula for Hamming Weight of Codewords

Lemma 7
.

Let
G
be a
k\times n
generator matrix for a linear code over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
, with rows
g_{1},g_{2},\dots,g_{k}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
. Let
\mathcal{J}\subseteq[1,k]
and
\mathbf{c}=\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}}g_{j}
(addition in
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
) be the codeword obtained as the sum of the rows indexed by
\mathcal{J}
. The Hamming weight
\mathrm{wt}(\mathbf{c})
of
\mathbf{c}
is given by

\boxed{\;\mathrm{wt}(\mathbf{c})=\sum_{\emptyset\neq\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{J}}(-2)^{|\mathcal{S}|-1}\left|\bigcap_{j\in\mathcal{S}}\mathrm{supp}(g_{j})\right|,\;}

(15)

where
\mathrm{supp}(g_{i})=\{j\in\{1,2,\dots,n\}\mid g_{i,j}=1\}
is the support of row
g_{i}
.

Proof.

Let
A_{j}=\mathrm{supp}(g_{j})
for
j\in\mathcal{J}
. The Hamming weight
\mathrm{wt}(\mathbf{c})
counts positions
j\in\{1,2,\dots,n\}
where
\mathbf{c}_{j}=1
, i.e., where an odd number of rows
g_{i}
for
i\in\mathcal{J}
have
g_{i,j}=1
. Define
d(j)=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{J}}g_{i,j}
(sum as integers), so
\mathrm{wt}(\mathbf{c})=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathbf{1}_{\{d(j)\text{ is odd}\}}(j)
. Using the parity indicator
\mathbf{1}_{\{d(j)\text{ is odd}\}}=\frac{1-(-1)^{d(j)}}{2}
, we get

\mathrm{wt}(\mathbf{c})=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1-(-1)^{d(j)}}{2}=\frac{1}{2}\left(n-\sum_{j=1}^{n}(-1)^{d(j)}\right).

Compute
\sum_{j=1}^{n}(-1)^{d(j)}
:

(-1)^{d(j)}=\prod_{i\in\mathcal{J}}(1-2g_{i,j}),

since
1-2g_{i,j}=-1
if
g_{i,j}=1
and
+1
if
g_{i,j}=0
. Thus,

\sum_{j=1}^{n}(-1)^{d(j)}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\prod_{i\in\mathcal{J}}(1-2g_{i,j}).

Expand the product:

\prod_{i\in\mathcal{J}}(1-2g_{i,j})=\sum_{\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{J}}(-2)^{|\mathcal{S}|}\prod_{i\in\mathcal{S}}g_{i,j},

so

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}(-1)^{d(j)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{J}}(-2)^{|\mathcal{S}|}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\prod_{i\in\mathcal{S}}g_{i,j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{J}}(-2)^{|\mathcal{S}|}\left|\bigcap_{i\in\mathcal{S}}A_{i}\right|,

since
\sum_{j=1}^{n}\prod_{i\in\mathcal{S}}g_{i,j}=|\bigcap_{i\in\mathcal{S}}A_{i}|
. The term for
\mathcal{S}=\emptyset
is
n
. Substituting into the weight expression,

\displaystyle\mathrm{wt}(\mathbf{c})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(n-n-\sum_{\mathclap{\emptyset\neq\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{J}}}(-2)^{|\mathcal{S}|}\left|\bigcap_{i\in\mathcal{S}}A_{i}\right|\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathclap{\emptyset\neq\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{J}}}(-1)^{|\mathcal{S}|+1}2^{|\mathcal{S}|-1}\left|\bigcap_{i\in\mathcal{S}}A_{i}\right|.

Since
(-1)^{|\mathcal{S}|+1}2^{|\mathcal{S}|-1}=(-2)^{|\mathcal{S}|-1}
, we obtain

\mathrm{wt}(\mathbf{c})=\sum_{\emptyset\neq\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{J}}(-2)^{|\mathcal{S}|-1}\left|\bigcap_{j\in\mathcal{S}}A_{j}\right|,

matching the stated formula.
∎

Remark 2
.

The formula for
\mathrm{wt}(\mathbf{c})
in Lemma
7
is a parity-based adaptation of the principle of inclusion-exclusion (PIE)

\left|\bigcup_{j\in\mathcal{J}}A_{j}\right|=\sum_{\emptyset\neq\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{J}}(-1)^{|\mathcal{S}|+1}\left|\bigcap_{j\in\mathcal{S}}A_{j}\right|

for finite sets
A_{j}=\mathrm{supp}(g_{j})
,
j\in\mathcal{J}
. While standard PIE computes the size of the union
\left|\bigcup_{j\in\mathcal{J}}A_{j}\right|
using alternating signs
(-1)^{|\mathcal{S}|+1}
, the given formula counts positions
j
where an odd number of sets
A_{j}
overlap, achieved by weighting intersections with
(-2)^{|\mathcal{S}|-1}
. This arises from the parity indicator
\frac{1-(-1)^{d(j)}}{2}
, transforming the PIE sum to capture only odd-sized overlaps, aligning with the
\mathbb{F}_{2}
sum in the codeword
\mathbf{c}
.

Suppose
(A_{1},b_{1}),\dots,(A_{k},b_{k})

(A_{1}^{\prime},b_{1}^{\prime}),\dots,(A_{k}^{\prime},b_{k}^{\prime})

\sum_{i=1}^{k}(A_{i},b_{i})\cdot f_{i}\;=\;\sum_{i=1}^{k}(A_{i}^{\prime},b_{i}^{\prime})\cdot f_{i}.

Since monomials are linearly independent and the supports of the

f_{i}

\operatorname{ind}(f_{i})

for all
i
.
Therefore the only element in
{\rm LTA}(m,2)_{F}
that fixes
P_{F}
is the
identity, i.e.

\operatorname{Stab}_{{\rm LTA}(m,2)}(F)=\{\mathrm{id}\},\qquad|\operatorname{Stab}_{{\rm LTA}(m,2)}(F)|=1.

Thus the orbit size for
F
is exactly
|{\rm LTA}(m,2)_{F}|
, and summing this

Somatic likelihood tiering: an interpretable post-calling triage protocol for tumor-only whole-exome variant review
Authors: Konrad Stawiski, Sophia C Kamran, Júlia Perera-Bel, Jihyun Lee, Joaquim Bellmunt, Kent W Mouw, Filipe L F De Carvalho
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractTumor-only whole-exome sequencing (WES) is used when matched normal tissue is unavailable, but one sample can produce thousands of variants. Somatic likelihood tiering (SLT) is an interpretable post-calling protocol that ranks Mutect2 calls into four review-priority tiers using population-frequency, germline-quality, cancer-knowledge, PureCN posterior, and clonal-hematopoiesis evidence. Layer 2 distinguishes common, rare-callable, and unevaluable gnomAD states; missing or unmatchable gnomAD evidence is not positive rarity evidence. On the SEQC2 HCC1395 benchmark, the callability-aware SLT-A row contained 101 calls, 78 truth variants, 77.2% PPV (95% Wilson confidence interval 68.1%–84.3%), and a Number Needed to Review (NNR) of 1.29 (1.19–1.47). The conservative SLT-C catchment retained 352 of 455 truth variants (77.4%, 73.3%–81.0%) and all tiers together retained 430 of 455 truth variants. SNV performance is the primary calibration frame: SLT-C retained 341 of 439 SNV truth variants, whereas indel results were exploratory because only 16 truth indels were available. Clinical cohorts are reported as recall and concordance versus partially dependent matched-normal Mutect2 references, not independent clinical sensitivity. Patient-level bootstrap intervals were principal: HdM-BLCA-1 SLT-A recall was 18.2% (14.0%–23.5%), and LUAD-TW SLT-A recall was 49.1% (26.6%–63.3%) among 32 evaluable patients. The HdM-BLCA-1 median SLT-A queue remained 1277 variants per patient, so SLT reduces first-pass candidate counts but does not measure review time or eliminate FFPE candidate-count burden. SLT provides an auditable tumor-only WES review queue, not a substitute for matched-normal sequencing, independent orthogonal validation, or definitive somatic classification.

Introduction

Tumor-only whole-exome sequencing (WES) in clinical oncology creates a review-capacity bottleneck before any single variant is scored. When matched normal tissue is unavailable because of sample access, cost, or retrospective design [1, 2], one call set can contain thousands of residual germline polymorphisms, clonal hematopoiesis events, artifacts, and true somatic events. Existing tools rarely expose why one variant ranks above another in a form a molecular tumor board can audit. The missing component is a transparent triage step that converts raw calls into a ranked review queue with traceable evidence.

We address this gap with somatic likelihood tiering (SLT), an interpretable post-calling triage protocol for tumor-only WES. SLT is not a replacement for matched-normal sequencing and not a competitor to deep-learning somatic callers. It assembles population-frequency, germline-quality, cancer-knowledge, somatic-probability, and clonal-hematopoiesis evidence into four traceable review-priority tiers.

Existing computational approaches span detection and filtering rather than protocolized review ordering. gnomAD [3, 4] removes common germline variants but cannot resolve rare polymorphisms, and PureCN [5] emits continuous somatic-probability estimates without interpretation thresholds. DeepSomatic [6], ClairS-TO [7], and VarNet-T [8] improve tumor-only calling, while tabular tumor-only classifiers [9–11], OncoTOP [12], SGZ [13], ISOWN [14], UNMASC [15], TOSCA [16], and clonal-hematopoiesis tools [17, 18] address related tasks. SLT is positioned on the transparency and traceability side of this landscape.

Our objective was to specify SLT, evaluate it across public benchmark, external cell-line, matched tumor-normal clinical, and population-scale annotation-only settings, and report implementation guidance. Downstream pathogenicity assessment [19] is outside scope.

Methods

SLT framework architecture

SLT evaluates four evidence layers for each variant (Fig. 1). Layer 1 is Mutect2 POPAF, where POPAF ≥5.0 indicates population rarity. Layer 2 is now a three-state gnomAD v4.1 exome rule after allele normalization: rare_callable contributes positive rarity evidence only when an observed allele is below the AF threshold in an evaluable context; common indicates AF at or above threshold; and unevaluable indicates absent, unmatchable, low-AN, or otherwise non-callability supported annotation. Unevaluable gnomAD status is retained as an audit state but does not contribute positive rarity evidence. The impact of this reclassification and the activity of the POPAF/gnomAD rarity layers are summarized in Supplementary Table S1 and Supplementary Figure S12. Layer 3 requires Mutect2 GERMQ ≥30. Layer 4 requires COSMIC v103 confirmed somatic count ≥5, hotspot count ≥10, or Cancer Gene Census membership (CGC v103) with sample count ≥2.

Somatic likelihood tiering classification framework. Tumor-only WES candidate variants enter a mode-selection preflight. Full SLT requires Mutect2 POPAF/GERMQ fields and a posterior-emitting PureCN workflow; mapped SLT and annotation-only mode are capped when those fields are absent. Layer 2 now separates gnomAD evidence into rare-callable, common, and unevaluable states; unevaluable gnomAD does not contribute positive rarity evidence. Full-mode tier assignment applies PureCN thresholds 0.8, 0.5, and 0.2, posterior-independent high-evidence SLT-B rescue, and the CHIP block before assigning SLT-A through SLT-D. Annotation-only mode uses the six-cell gnomAD/COSMIC matrix. Rare-callable/COSMIC-positive, rare-callable/COSMIC-negative, common/COSMIC-positive, unevaluable/COSMIC-positive, and unevaluable/COSMIC-negative profiles route to capped SLT-C; only common/COSMIC/CGC-negative profiles route to SLT-D. Unevaluable gnomAD is not rarity-positive; SLT-C is cannot-rule-out retention. This schematic has no sample size, hypothesis test, error bar, or axis transformation. Abbreviations: WES, whole-exome sequencing; POPAF, population allele frequency annotation; GERMQ, germline-quality score; COSMIC, catalogue of somatic mutations in cancer; CHIP, clonal hematopoiesis of indeterminate potential; SLT, somatic likelihood tiering. Source data, rendering script, and provenance log are indexed in Supplementary Note A.

Layer counts define high evidence (≥3 including COSMIC), medium evidence (≥2), or low evidence (<2). Full SLT requires a posterior-emitting PureCN workflow, with at least one cohort-level posterior value present. Within such a workflow, a blank per-variant PureCN posterior follows the rule p = 0.0: the variant cannot satisfy posterior-threshold routes but can still reach SLT-B through the posterior-independent high-evidence, non-CHIP route. Tier assignment follows the first matching condition in a deterministic cascade. SLT-A requires PureCN ≥0.8, medium or high evidence, and no CHIP-likely flag. SLT-B requires PureCN ≥0.5 without a CHIP-likely flag, or high evidence without a CHIP-likely flag. SLT-C requires PureCN ≥0.2 or medium/high evidence. SLT-D captures the remainder. Additional file 2 provides executable pseudocode, the annotation-only matrix, and regression-test fixtures.

Heuristic CHIP gate and annotation-only mode

COSMIC and CGC supply the cancer-knowledge resource layer [20, 21]. The heuristic CHIP gate is a configurable safety gate and rule-based triage block that evaluates two curated gene lists. Tier 1 covers the 13-gene canonical CHIP-driver list (DNMT3A, TET2, ASXL1, PPM1D, JAK2, SF3B1, SRSF2, U2AF1, IDH1, IDH2, ZBTB33, GNB1, and CBL) drawn from Jaiswal, Genovese, and Bolton [22–24]. Tier 2 covers 28 extended CHIP-context genes including TP53, KRAS, and NRAS [24]. Variants are flagged as CHIP-likely (blocked from SLT-A/B), CHIP-possible, or unflagged using VAF-conditional rules (Tier 1 VAF < 0.15 or hotspot or tumor-suppressor loss-of-function; Tier 2 TP53 hotspots at VAF < 0.15, KRAS hotspots at VAF < 0.10; Supplementary Tables S2–S3). The gate can be replaced by dedicated CH tools [17, 18]. When the workflow lacks Mutect2/PureCN fields at the schema or cohort level, SLT routes to mapped or annotation-only mode rather than silently pretending full SLT is available. Annotation-only mode uses the six-cell gnomAD/COSMIC matrix and is capped at SLT-C/D: all cannot-rule-out profiles are retained at SLT-C, while only confidently common and COSMIC/CGC-negative variants route to SLT-D. This cap is a cannot-rule-out retention rule, not evidence that unevaluable gnomAD annotation is rare.

Threshold derivation

Thresholds were prespecified from annotation semantics, population-frequency practice, CH priors, and SLT operating-point design. POPAF 5.0 follows Mutect2 rarity encoding. gnomAD AF 0.001 is a conservative population-frequency screen, not an ACMG pathogenicity rule [25]. GERMQ 30 follows the phred scale. COSMIC confirmed 5 is a recurrence threshold [20]. PureCN thresholds 0.8/0.5/0.2 are operating thresholds on the posterior scale, not calibrated probabilities. CHIP VAF thresholds follow published CH prevalence data [22]. All thresholds were fixed before benchmark evaluation.

Datasets

Five datasets were used (Supplementary Table S4). SEQC2 HCC1395 [26] was the primary benchmark (455 truth variants: 439 SNVs, 16 indels) processed tumor-only with Agilent SureSelect V6 + UTR WES. BostonGene COLO829 (445 truth variants) and NCI-H1770 (1203 truth variants) [27] used Agilent SureSelect V7 and a five-normal cross-batch V7 PureCN NormalDB. BostonGene positioning distinguishes paired tumor-normal Mutect2 PASS rows, tumor-only Mutect2 PASS rows, and tumor-only SLT rows on the full local tumor-only candidate surface, including non-PASS records. TCGA MC3 [28] contributed 1 765 412 somatic variants from 3807 tumors across eight cancer types for annotation-only concordance. HdM-BLCA-1 included 27 FFPE metastatic urothelial carcinoma cases with matched tumor-normal Mutect2 reference [29], nf-core/sarek v3.7.1 [30], and PureCN v2.12.0 against a 27-normal V5 NormalDB. LUAD-TW included 113 fresh-frozen stage I lung adenocarcinoma cases with matched tumor-normal WES [31], PureCN v2.16.1 against a 28-normal NormalDB, and GENCODE v19 CDS restriction; 32 patients had valid CDS Mutect2 PASS calls for per-patient SLT classification.

After a four-point BQ sweep (15, 20, 25, and 30) on six HdM-BLCA-1 and six LUAD-TW patients, both clinical cohorts used PureCN minimum base quality 20 (Supplementary Table S5). The default of 25 removed ~90% of FFPE candidate variants without warning. Reference resources were gnomAD v4.1 exomes [4], COSMIC v103 [20], CGC v103 [21], and assembly-matched Funcotator v1.8 (hg38) or v1.7 (hg19), without liftover. gnomAD version, contig naming, allele normalization, and callability/evaluability semantics are treated as pinned protocol inputs; laboratories updating gnomAD releases should rerun the callability-aware annotation checks and regression tests before applying the tier logic.

Variant calling, evaluation, and statistical analysis

GATK Mutect2 v4.6.2.0 [32] was run in tumor-only mode with the gnomAD germline resource and a 1000G panel of normals. PureCN v2.10.0 [5] supplied somatic probabilities for SEQC2 using a 30-exome 1000 Genomes NormalDB, with BQ = 20 applied to clinical cohorts. Across PureCN v2.10.0, v2.12.0, and v2.16.1, SLT threshold binarization was stable and the FFPE base-quality pitfall reproduced. Variants were annotated with Funcotator. SEQC2 comparators were PureCN posterior thresholds 0.2/0.5/0.8, gnomAD-only filtering at AF < 10−4, DeepSomatic v1.9.0 [6], and VarDict v1.8.3 [33]. The 29.2 Mb SEQC2 evaluation region intersected SEQC2 high-confidence regions, Agilent V6 + UTR capture BED, and GENCODE v47 CDS.

SEQC2 analyses use the locked 455-variant truth denominator and the locked row-aligned 4471-call evaluation surface unless a table explicitly labels another denominator. Mutect2 detected 430 truth variants within the evaluation region. The primary SLT operating-point claims are SNV-calibrated: the SNV table uses 439 truth SNVs, whereas the 16 truth indels are reported separately as exploratory and underpowered for SLT-A/B calibration.

Reporting-guideline coverage was split across TRIPOD+AI for the exploratory ML baseline (Section 3.8) and STROBE for matched-normal concordance (Sections 3.5–3.6). Filled checklists are Additional files 6, 7, and 9. Independent benchmark rows use Wilson 95% confidence intervals and reciprocal NNR intervals; clinical cohorts use patient-level bootstrap intervals because variants cluster within patients. Clinical rows are labeled as recall/concordance versus matched-normal Mutect2 reference. Enrichment odds ratios used two-sided Fisher tests with Bonferroni adjustment across seven gene-set comparisons. The clinical-cohort Layer 4 source-removal analysis uses frozen CIViC, OncoKB, and Bailey2018 lists with CGC v103 source annotations (Supplementary Table S39). The exploratory ML baseline used logistic regression and XGBoost 2.1 under 5-fold stratified cross-validation; extended parameters are in Additional file 8.

Results

Recurring denominators across SEQC2 analyses include 455 total truth variants (439 SNVs and 16 indels), 430 detected truth variants, and 4471 evaluation-region calls; the denominator flow and threshold-grid intervals are provided in Supplementary Figure S1 and Supplementary Table S30.

Tier cascade and first-pass candidate-count reduction

Of 36 633 Mutect2 calls on SEQC2 HCC1395, 4471 fell within the locked evaluation region against 455 truth variants (Table 1; Fig. 2A). After applying the callability-aware gnomAD rule, SLT-A produced a 101-variant high-priority shortlist with 78 truth variants, PPV 77.2% (95% Wilson CI 68.1%–84.3%), and NNR 1.29 (1.19–1.47). SLT-A/B contained 157 calls and 99 truth variants. The conservative SLT-C catchment contained 2246 calls and retained 352 of 455 truth variants (77.4%, 73.3%–81.0%); all tiers together contained 4471 calls and 430 detected truth variants. Driver retention and actionability

Driver and actionability annotations are contextual evidence rather than independent operating-point calibration. Figure 2F, Supplementary Fig. S4, and Supplementary Table S8 summarize CGC context on the detected-truth call surface: among 10 detected CGC-annotated truth rows, 3 were assigned SLT-B, six SLT-C, and one SLT-D. Operating-point claims use the locked 455-truth denominator (Supplementary Tables S34 and S38). The clinical source-removal analysis freezes CIViC, OncoKB, and Bailey2018 source lists [34–36] and records row-level CGC v103 sources in Supplementary Table S39. Composite actionability-enrichment and COSMIC source-subtraction panels remain contextual (Supplementary Tables S6 and S9).

Posterior absence and evidence-layer recovery

PureCN posterior thresholds alone did not provide both the high-PPV shortlist and broad catchment roles needed for SLT. In the callability-aware evaluation, posterior ≥0.8 called 125 variants with 84 truth variants (PPV 67.2%, sensitivity 18.5%), whereas posterior ≥0.2 called 185 with 126 truth variants (PPV 68.1%, sensitivity 27.7%; Supplementary Table S11). Table 2 compares SLT, PureCN thresholds, gnomAD filtering, DeepSomatic, and mapped hybrid rows. This motivated a cascade that uses additional evidence only within a posterior-emitting PureCN workflow: blank row-level posteriors cannot satisfy posterior thresholds but remain eligible for posterior-independent evidence routes. Supplementary Figures S5 and S6 provide additional posterior-threshold and evidence-layer activity summaries. Figure 3A and B show evidence-layer counts and posterior availability; Fig. 3C and D and Supplementary Fig. S7 show threshold and VAF robustness. A near-zero posterior BRCA2 nonsense variant illustrates why SLT keeps candidates visible while somatic/germline origin is adjudicated separately (Supplementary Table S12).

Posterior absence concentrated in low-information contexts (Supplementary Table S33): VAF below 5% had posterior emission in 1.7% of cases (enrichment ratio 0.12), effective depth below 20 reads in 0.0%, and indels in 0.0%. Cancer Gene Census drivers were not enriched in the posterior-present set (Bonferroni-adjusted p = 1.00). This pattern is consistent with PureCN’s BAM-level candidate-selection step excluding low-VAF, low-depth, and indel calls from its supported call-evidence window. At ≥SLT-C, posterior missingness therefore caused minimal sensitivity loss.

External benchmarking on BostonGene cell lines

External truth-set positioning on COLO829 (melanoma) and NCI-H1770 (NSCLC) used callability-aware gnomAD annotation and paired tumor-normal Mutect2 where local BAMs were available (Table 3; Fig. 4A). COLO829 matched-normal Mutect2 PASS recovered 264 of 445 truth alleles from 366 PASS calls; NCI-H1770 matched-normal Mutect2 PASS recovered 817 of 1203 truth alleles from 1813 PASS calls. The regenerated tumor-only SLT A/B operating point recovered 37 of 445 COLO829 truth alleles and 151 of 1203 NCI-H1770 truth alleles; A/B/C recovered 125 and 459, respectively. These rows position tumor-only SLT against paired tumor-normal calling and truth resources; they are not evidence that SLT replaces matched-normal calling. HCC1395 paired matched-normal reanalysis was not performed because a same-source local tumor BAM was not available for this analysis. Matched-normal positioning and NormalDB sensitivity are further detailed in Supplementary Figure S8 and Supplementary Table S13. Figure 4B and C summarize SLT-attributable false-negative rates in the same truth frames and NormalDB deployment dependence across cell-line and clinical contexts.

Heuristic CHIP gate and annotation-only mode

The heuristic CHIP gate is a rule-based triage block and configurable safety module. Because paired blood-normal CH labels are unavailable for HdM-BLCA-1 and LUAD-TW, these analyses describe rule behavior and ArCH-style/UNISOM-style [17, 18] principles, not orthogonal CHIP validation (Supplementary Table S17; Supplementary Fig. S10A). In a 23-variant simulated spike-in (14 positives, nine negatives), the gate blocked 14 of 14 positives from SLT-A/B and passed 8 of 9 negatives. The only misclassification was a DNMT3A R882H variant at VAF 0.35, conservatively blocked by the Tier 1 hotspot rule. SLT, ArCH-style, and UNISOM-style principles called 15, 14, and 12 of the 23 variants CHIP-likely.

Tumor-infiltrating clonal hematopoiesis modifies solid-tumor survival [37], supporting CHIP-aware filtering in tumor-only WES. On HdM-BLCA-1, Tier 1 rejected 75 of 3299 calls (2.3%) and Tier 2 rejected 0 of 1268. On 25 927 LUAD-TW PASS variants in gate genes, Tier 1 rejected 8 of 19 616 (0.04%) and Tier 2 rejected 0 of 6311. No TP53, KRAS, or NRAS variants were rejected in either cohort. Cohort-, original-tier-, and post-gate distributions of blocked variants are provided in Supplementary Table S37. Tier 2 zero firings are consistent with its VAF-conditional design and require tumor-type recalibration before deployment. Cohort-level kappa between SLT and ArCH-style/UNISOM-style principles was 0.09/0.01 on HdM-BLCA-1 and 0.004/0.02 on LUAD-TW, reflecting different low-VAF handling, recurrence requirements, and gene-set breadth (Supplementary Table S17). When BAM-level processing is unavailable, annotation-only mode uses the six-cell gnomAD/COSMIC matrix and caps at SLT-C/D. In SEQC2, this callability-aware mode assigns no SLT-A/B calls, retains 2415 calls at SLT-C, and keeps 429 of 455 truth variants in the capped cannot-rule-out queue. Rare-callable/COSMIC-negative and unevaluable/COSMIC-negative variants now route to SLT-C; only common/COSMIC-negative variants route to SLT-D. Annotation-only SLT is a retention fallback, not a validated high-priority shortlist. Applied to 411 TCGA bladder samples [38], it produced expected-driver-enriched shortlists (Supplementary Fig. S10B; Supplementary Tables S19–S21). Dedicated CH callers [17, 18] should replace the gate when CHIP discrimination is mission-critical.

Method comparison

Direct comparators are DeepSomatic, ClairS-TO, VarNet-T, PureCN, and gnomAD filtering (Table 2; Supplementary Table S22; Supplementary Fig. S11; Supplementary Note A). DeepSomatic is reported as an upstream caller, not an SLT tier substitute. In the locked 455-truth SEQC2 frame, DeepSomatic PASS contained 443 calls, 378 true positives, 65 false positives, sensitivity 83.1%, PPV 85.3%, and F1 0.842; HCC1395 is part of DeepSomatic’s documented training material, so this row is labeled potentially in-sample. Of DeepSomatic PASS calls mappable to SLT annotations, 391 calls included 364 true positives (PPV 93.1%). Exploratory PASS+SLT-A/B and PASS+SLT-A/B/C rows contained 85/83 and 301/291 calls/true positives, respectively, illustrating downstream triage rather than a validated hybrid workflow. DeepSomatic-exclusive calls remain in the denominator, and no Mutect2/PureCN POPAF, GERMQ, or posterior claims are made for DeepSomatic-only sites. Error characterization and robustness

The SLT-A false-positive set contains 23 variants (Supplementary Table S25). At ≥SLT-C, the current false-negative budget is 103 of 455 truth variants: 25 absent from the frozen Mutect2 call surface and 78 detected but below the cannot-rule-out catchment (Supplementary Tables S25 and S34). Layer activity is reported as descriptive layer-positive counts rather than a detected-truth leave-one-layer ablation (Supplementary Table S6). COSMIC recurrence alone had low single-feature sensitivity, but COSMIC/CGC remains decomposable AMP/ASCO/CAP-supporting evidence (Supplementary Tables S10 and S34). VAF-stratified analysis showed SLT-A bias toward lower-VAF truth variants (Supplementary Fig. S3). Among 16 SEQC2 truth indels, none reached SLT-A/B and 11 reached SLT-C (Supplementary Table S27), supporting the SNV-calibrated scope statement. In the base-quality sweep, BQ = 25 removed roughly 90% of FFPE-quality variants while BQ = 20 restored the cascade. On LUAD-TW, BQ = 15 and BQ = 20 were near-identical, and BQ = 30 reduced coverage by up to 39% (Fig. 2G; Supplementary Table S5; Supplementary Figure S16). The LUAD-TW parameter sweep and the HdM-BLCA-1 BQ20-versus-BQ25 concordance and artifact-spectrum analyses are provided in Supplementary Tables S29 and S36, respectively.

A predeclared purity/ploidy stratification using available PureCN outputs is reported in Table 6, Panel B, with detailed operating-point and reference-overlap metrics provided in Supplementary Table S42. BostonGene and HdM-BLCA-1 had purity and ploidy estimates, LUAD-TW had purity but no local ploidy estimate, and SEQC2 lacked local PureCN purity with only a ploidy proxy. The strata are descriptive, not threshold-optimizing, because purity and ploidy are derived from the same PureCN workflow that contributes to SLT. BQ = 20 restored SLT cascade performance on the six FFPE bladder patients (HdM-BLCA-1) and six fresh-frozen lung adenocarcinoma patients (LUAD-TW) tested in the base-quality sweep (n = 6 per cohort, four BQ settings: 15, 20, 25, and 30). Discussion

SLT provides an auditable tiered triage protocol for tumor-only WES candidate queues. Its high-priority tiers require process-matched NormalDB calibration, and callability-aware Layer 2 no longer treats missing gnomAD annotation as rarity evidence. This correction narrowed the SLT-C catchment but removed an unsupported rarity assumption. On SEQC2 HCC1395, SLT-A produced 101 calls at 77.2% PPV (NNR 1.29), SLT-C retained 352 of 455 truth variants, and all tiers retained 430 of 455 truth variants. Thus SLT-A/B define high-priority review queues, SLT-C is a conservative catchment under explicit gnomAD evaluability assumptions, and clinical cohorts remain matched-normal concordance analyses whose patient-level results depend on sample quality, purity, capture context, and NormalDB construction.

SLT is not a replacement for matched-normal sequencing, a germline-risk classifier, a pathogenicity engine, or a black-box caller. It makes tumor-only WES review queues inspectable when matched normal sequencing is unavailable. Population-frequency screening remains broad germline filtering, PureCN remains a posterior model, and SLT combines these with cancer-knowledge and CHIP-safety evidence. Figure 6C separates this auditable triage role from caller-level F1.

DeepSomatic, ClairS-TO, and VarNet-T set a higher discrimination baseline for tumor-only variant calling [6–8], but they solve a different problem than SLT. DeepSomatic reached the highest SEQC2 F1 in this manuscript, although HCC1395 is part of its training material [6]. ClairS-TO and VarNet-T show how learned callers can improve tumor-only discovery and tumor-mutational-burden estimation, but their main outputs do not provide the same per-variant evidence decomposition expected in AMP/ASCO/CAP-style review [19]. SLT accepts lower raw discrimination than these learned callers in exchange for transparent promotion and demotion rules. It can therefore sit after Mutect2/PureCN in a review workflow, or beside learned callers as an audit layer, rather than replacing them.

Implementation should treat NormalDB construction, FFPE base-quality, CHIP-safety configuration, and CI-tested reproducibility as protocol settings. A/B tiers require process-matched NormalDB from the same capture, library, and calling context. The cross-batch BostonGene NormalDB collapsed SLT-A/B sensitivity to 0.5%–3.9%, whereas cohort-matched NormalDBs supported SLT-A operation in clinical cohorts. The HdM-BLCA-1 three-patient hold-out size grid was non-monotonic (SLT-A recall/concordance 14.3%/4.16% at n = 5, 13.5%/3.88% at n = 24, and 12.8%/3.73% at n = 27; Supplementary Table S35; Supplementary Fig. S15), supporting process match rather than size alone. PureCN BQ 25 removed ~90% of tested FFPE candidates; BQ 20 restored the cascade described in the FFPE base-quality recommendation. The CHIP-safety flag should be locally configured and replaced by dedicated CH callers [17, 18] when CHIP discrimination is mission-critical. The 15-variant smoke test passed locally.

Clinical governance is where the SLT output should be interpreted. SLT-A surfaces variants for human review, SLT-C bounds a cannot-rule-out catchment, and AMP/ASCO/CAP reporting [19] plus ESMO molecular tumor board quality indicators [42–44] remain the clinical framework. HdM-BLCA-1 illustrates the remaining FFPE burden: SLT-A reduces the median per-patient queue to 1277 variants, but this is not measured review time and does not by itself create a tumor-board-tractable queue. In LUAD-TW, the corresponding SLT-A median queue is 346.5 variants, but only 32 of 113 patients entered the coding-reference recall denominator (Supplementary Table S41). Further per-case prioritization by capture-matched NormalDB calibration, CHIP-safety configuration, and downstream actionability is required. SLT does not classify germline risk; variants with pathogenic germline plausibility, including BRCA1, BRCA2, MLH1, MSH2, MSH6, PMS2, and germline-class TP53 hotspots, require clinical germline workflow and confirmatory matched-normal sequencing. Ancestry-driven recalibration and population variants misclassified as somatic remain known tumor-only risks [45, 46]. Any SLT-A variant in an ACMG actionable germline gene should be reflexively referred for germline confirmation.

The main strengths of SLT are deterministic evidence decomposition, explicit mode selection, PureCN row-level posterior semantics, and deployment warnings for NormalDB and FFPE base-quality settings. The main limitations are one primary SEQC2 full-truth benchmark, partially dependent clinical matched-normal references, no orthogonal blood-normal CH validation, and underpowered indel calibration. Shared clinical pipelines may inflate concordance and cannot reveal true variants missed by matched-normal Mutect2. The 23-variant CHIP spike-in tests rule behavior rather than clinical CH validity. Only 16 SEQC2 truth variants are indels, with no truth indel reaching SLT-A/B, so SLT-A/B operating points are SNV-calibrated. The gnomAD v4.1 sites VCF supports observed allele matching but not complete absent-site callability for every no-match allele; laboratories need all-sites/callability resources or local validation before treating absent alleles as rare-callable. Future work should add diverse prospective cohorts, paired blood-normal CHIP benchmarking, capture-kit NormalDB calibration, all-sites gnomAD integration, indel-specific operating points, and blinded tumor board simulation of measured review time [47, 48]. SLT delivers an auditable tumor-only WES review queue with explicit failure modes, not a matched-normal substitute.

Conclusions

SLT provides interpretable tumor-only WES triage by ranking candidate variants with population, germline-quality, cancer-knowledge, posterior, and CHIP-safety evidence. Its operating claim is narrow: SLT-A/B are principally SNV-calibrated high-priority review queues, SLT-C is a conservative catchment under explicit gnomAD evaluability assumptions, and annotation-only mode is capped cannot-rule-out retention. Clinical-cohort results are matched-normal concordance analyses with patient-level uncertainty. The contribution is an auditable review queue, not maximal somatic-calling accuracy or matched-normal replacement.

Study on the migration of bacteria in transoral endoscopic thyroidectomy vestibular approach and development of a comprehensive oral management process
Authors: Xiangxiang Zhan, Zhizhong Dong, Shiqi Wang, Tianyun Wen, Qiuyu Meng, Yanjun Su, Wen Liu, Ruochuan Cheng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe transoral endoscopic thyroidectomy vestibular approach (TOETVA) utilizes an intraoral incision, raising concerns about surgical-site infections (SSI) originating from the oral cavity. The types and sources of bacteria that may cause TOETVA infection are not clear, and the optimal antibiotic prevention plan is still unclear, lacking targeted perioperative management.Materials and methodsIn this prospective, non-randomized controlled experimental study, drainage-fluid from 52 TOETVA and 15 open surgery patients (March – December 2025) was analyzed using 16S rDNA sequencing and clinical review. TOETVA patients were allocated to three perioperative antibiotic regimens: preoperative cefuroxime (Group A, n = 18), postoperative cefuroxime (Group B, n = 16), or postoperative metronidazole (Group C, n = 18). Inclusion criteria were initial surgery for T1N0M0 papillary thyroid carcinoma, no prior neck radiation, and patient preference for a scar-free neck. The primary outcome was postoperative SSI rate. Secondary outcomes included changes in drainage-fluid bacterial abundance, pus culture results, effects of different oral cleaning protocols, and microbial correlation between drainage-fluid and disinfected neck skin or oral vestibule. Statistical analyses employed the Chi-Square test, Kruskal-Wallis test, and Spearman Correlation.ResultsNo SSI occurred in cefuroxime group, while two infections were recorded in the metronidazole group. Analysis of the top 30 differentially abundant bacterial species showed that cefuroxime reduced the abundance of 15/30 (50%) within 48 h, compared to only 4/30 (13.3%) with metronidazole (OR: 6.50, 95% CI: 1.90-22.26, P = 0.0023). Microbial composition in drainage-fluid strongly correlated with povidone-iodine-disinfected chin skin (ρ = 0.8195, 95% CI: 0.6865–0.8994, P < 0.001). However, pus cultures from infected cases grew oral streptococci, not skin flora. Among four oral cleaning regimens, povidone-iodine most effectively reduced streptococcal load, whereas tinidazole better preserved the overall microbiota balance.ConclusionsAlthough the drainage-fluid following TOETVA primarily reflects skin microbiota, clinical surgical-site infections are mainly attributed to oral streptococci. Cefuroxime provides superior perioperative antibacterial coverage compared to metronidazole. Based on clinical practice considerations, a comprehensive oral management process can be helpful for the treatment of TOETVA.Trial registrationRetrospectively registered in Chinese Clinical Trial Registry on April 8, 2026 (Registration number: ChiCTR2600122005).Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12893-026-04084-8.

Introduction

The global incidence of thyroid cancer has exhibited a pronounced upward trend in recent decades [1], with an estimated 821,000 new cases diagnosed worldwide in 2022 [2]. China accounts for a significant portion of the global burden of thyroid cancer, with 56.8% of all global cases and one of the highest age-standardized incidence rates in the world [3, 4]. This surge is predominantly driven by the increased detection of differentiated thyroid cancer (DTC) — particularly low-risk papillary thyroid carcinoma (PTC) — a subclinical entity often associated with an excellent prognosis and long-term survival [5, 6].

Surgery remains the primary curative modality for DTC. However, the conventional open thyroidectomy, via a Kocher incision, inevitably results in a visible neck scar [7], which impairs cosmetic satisfaction and negatively impacts patients’ psychosocial well-being and quality of life. In response to growing patient demand for improved cosmesis — especially among younger individuals and those in appearance-sensitive professions — minimally invasive remote-access endoscopic techniques have been developed [8]. Among these, transoral endoscopic thyroidectomy vestibular approach (TOETVA) has emerged as a promising approach. Indeed, TOETVA offers several advantages over other endoscopic methods, including a truly scar-free outcome as incisions are hidden within the oral vestibule, a shorter dissection path to the thyroid gland, and a superior, panoramic surgical view that facilitates bilateral central neck dissection [9–12].

Despite its cosmetic benefits, TOETVA inherently changes the incision classification from clean (Class I) to clean-contaminated (Class II), thereby raising legitimate concerns over the risk of postoperative surgical-site infection [13]. Despite the routinely administered perioperative antibiotic prophylaxis [14], postoperative infections occur at a rate of 6% [15], indicating that current prophylactic strategies are not fully effective [16]. Moreover, the precise sources of infecting pathogens and the efficacy of commonly used antibiotic regimens remain poorly elucidated. Specifically, it remains unclear whether infections originate from the oral cavity, the skin, or other sources, and whether empirical antibiotic selection provides adequate coverage against the relevant microbiota.

The human body hosts a diverse range of bacterial species across various anatomical sites, but infections often lead to strikingly similar clinical manifestations. Therefore, the accurate identification of pathogenic bacteria is crucial for the implementation of precise treatments and improvement of therapeutic outcomes. The rapid advancement of molecular biology techniques has enabled the swift identification of microbial species, with their stability and accuracy compensating for the limitations of traditional phenotypic identification methods [17]. The 16S rDNA sequence analysis method exhibits high sensitivity and specificity in microbial identification [18]. Given that it requires a shorter detection time and does not depend on microbial isolation and culture, it is considered the “gold standard” for bacterial identification and classification [19–21].

To address these critical knowledge gaps, in this study we employed 16S rDNA sequencing to comprehensively analyze bacterial communities in postoperative drainage-fluid from patients undergoing TOETVA. Moreover, by comparing postoperative drainage-fluid samples from patients treated with cefuroxime and metronidazole, we dynamically assessed the changes in microbial abundance after administration of the two antibiotics. We further investigated the correlation between the microbiota in TOETVA drainage-fluid and swabs collected from the oral vestibule, chin skin, and open surgical drainage-fluid to delineate potential contamination pathways. The primary objectives were to identify the key bacterial species and their sources post-TOETVA, evaluate the effectiveness of two prophylactic antibiotic regimens, and establish an evidence-based foundation for enhancing infection control measures.

Materials and methods

The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki, and the protocol was approved by the Ethics Committee of the First Affiliated Hospital of Kunming Medical University (No. 2025L333). Informed consent was obtained from all of the participants and/or their legal guardians. In addition, consent to publish the patients’ information and images in an online open access publication was obtained.

Study design

This is a single-center, prospective, non-randomized controlled experimental study. In this study, we included patients who underwent TOETVA and open surgery for T1N0M0 PTC at the Department of Thyroid and Parathyroid Surgery of The First Affiliated Hospital of Kunming Medical University between March and December 2025.

The inclusion of patients to the TOETVA group was based on PTC patients who underwent initial surgical treatment with a T1N0M0 staging and had no history of neck radiotherapy, expressing a desire for scar-free neck. The exclusion criteria were as follows: preoperative antibiotic-requiring infection, conversion to open surgery, and unplanned reoperation.

Grouping and perioperative management

The patients undergoing TOETVA were sequentially assigned to one of three antibiotic regimens based on their date of enrollment. Namely, those enrolled from March to May 2025 constituted the preoperative cefuroxime group (Group A); those enrolled from June to August 2025 received postoperative cefuroxime (Group B); and those enrolled from September to November 2025 comprised the postoperative metronidazole group (Group C). The open-surgery group was composed of patients who met the study criteria in December 2025 (Group O).

All of the patients were allocated into different groups to investigate the dynamics of bacterial profiles and antibiotic efficacy. The surgical procedures followed strict aseptic principles, including povidone-iodine skin disinfection and draping, and the surgical field was irrigated with 500 mL distilled water before closure.

We have systematically compiled details of all study cohorts and sample collections — including collection timelines, volumes, and intervention protocols for swabs and drainage fluids — and provided these in the Supplementary Methods.

Sample collection and 16S rDNA sequencing

All of the collected samples, including drainage-fluid (Groups A, B1, B2, B3, C1, C2, C3, and O) and swabs (Groups N, M, TD, VI, and CI) were immediately categorized, registered, and stored at − 80 °C until DNA extraction.

To establish a baseline for comparison, the postoperative drainage samples from the TOETVA patients who had not received any preoperative antibiotics (Groups B1 and C1) were pooled to form a combined baseline group, designated as “B1 + C1” (n = 34).

Bacterial genomic DNA was extracted from all of the samples. The initial raw sequencing data (RawData) were processed by merging reads based on overlapping ends. Low-quality and chimeric sequences were then filtered out to generate high-quality data (CleanData). Subsequent denoising of the CleanData was performed using the DADA2 algorithm within the QIIME2 framework to remove potential artifacts arising from PCR amplification and sequencing. This pipeline yielded a table of Amplicon Sequence Variants (ASVs) and their abundances, which formed the basis for all downstream analyses, including species composition, differential abundance, and correlation analyses.

Primary focus

This study comprehensively evaluated the antibacterial efficacy of cefuroxime and metronidazole in perioperative patients with TOETVA by analyzing patient characteristics, the incidence of surgical site infections across different treatment groups, and changes in bacterial abundance in drainage fluids following combined use of these agents. We further investigated the correlation between the microbiota in TOETVA drainage fluid and swabs collected from the oral vestibule, submental skin, and open surgical drainage fluid to delineate potential contamination pathways. Insights derived from this study, complemented by wound pus culture and antibiotic susceptibility testing in infection cases, enabled us to pinpoint the causative pathogens and their origins, thereby providing a basis for the exploratory development of comprehensive oral care management process.

Statistical analysis

All operations were performed by the same surgical team. A power analysis was conducted to determine the minimum sample size needed to test the primary hypothesis. Using an alpha level of 0.05, a power of 0.80, and a large anticipated effect size (Cohen’s f = 0.40) based on prior comparable research, the analysis indicated a required sample size of N = 15 per group for a one-way ANOVA (with three groups). This calculation was performed using G*Power software (version 3.1.9.7), employing a two-tailed test. The final recruited sample size met or exceeded this threshold in all experimental groups.

Continuous data were presented as the mean value ± standard deviation or the median value with interquartile range, as appropriate. Categorical variables were described as frequencies and percentages.

The Chi-Square test was used to analyze the independence relationship between categorical variables. If the data did not conform to the normality assumption, statistical analysis were conducted using non-parametric methods, and differences among three or more groups were assessed using the Kruskal-Wallis test. When a statistically significant difference was detected, post-hoc Dunn’s test with Bonferroni correction was conducted for pairwise comparisons. Associations between continuous variables were evaluated using Spearman’s rank correlation coefficient (ρ). Effect sizes were reported as odds ratios (ORs) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs). A two-tailed p-value of less than 0.05 was defined as the threshold for statistical significance for all tests. All statistical analyses and data visualizations were performed using R software (version 4.5.1; R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria).

A total of 67 patients were enrolled between March and December 2025, comprising 52 who underwent TOETVA and 15 who underwent open thyroidectomy. All of the patients were under 55 years of age, with Stage I PTC, reflecting the higher prevalence of this disease among younger individuals (both males and females). The maximum surgical extent for the included cases was bilateral total thyroidectomy with bilateral central cervical lymph node dissection, and all operative times were less than 4 h.

During the 2-week follow-up period, no surgical-site infections were observed in Group A, Group B, and Group O. There was no significant difference in the infection rate between preoperative and postoperative prophylactic administration of cefuroxime, demonstrating the safety and feasibility of the postoperative protocol. Drainage-fluid samples taken before antibiotic intervention provide a true representation of the bacterial environment in TOETVA drains, since they are free from antibiotic influence.

No postoperative infections occurred in the cefuroxime group during the entire follow-up period (0%, 95% CI: 0.0–10.3%). In contrast, two cases of wound infection were observed in Group C within the first postoperative week (11.1%, 95% CI: 1.4–34.7%).

Temporal effect of antibiotics

Administration of cefuroxime significantly reduced the abundance of 15 out of the top 30 bacterial entries identified in the drainage-fluid, as determined by a comparative analysis of Group B1 + C1, Group B2, and Group B3 (50%). The following specific taxa were affected: Hydrogenophaga, Brevundimonas intermedia, Brevundimonas aurantiaca, Phascolarctobacterium sp., Photobacterium, Sva0485, Bacteroidota, Geminicoccaceae, Solirubrobacter sp., Leifsonia sp. DAB-ST80, Bosea, Acinetobacter soli, Terrimicrobium, Planctomicrobium, and Clostridium sensu stricto 2 (Fig. 2a).

Changes in bacterial abundance in postoperative drainage-fluid after TOETVA at three time points. A before antibiotic administration, 24 hours and 48 hours after cefuroxime administration; B before antibiotic administration, 24 hours and 48 hours after metronidazole administration

Metronidazole administration exerted a significant effect on only 4 out of the top 30 bacterial genera in the drainage-fluid, as determined by comparing Group B1 + C1, Group C2, and Group C3 (13.33%). The affected genera were as follows: Sphingomonas, UCG-010, Collinsella, and Stenotrophomonas acidaminiphila (Fig. 2b). There was a significant statistical difference in the antibacterial effect between the two drugs (OR: 6.50, 95% CI: 1.90-22.26, P = 0.0023).

Contrary to what might be expected, compared to the baseline (Group B1 + C1), metronidazole administration was associated with a significant increase in streptococcal abundance in the drainage fluid at both 24 (Group C2) and 48 h (Group C3) (Fig. 2b).

Comparative antibiotic efficacy

On POD1, a direct comparison between cefuroxime (Group B2) and metronidazole (Group C2), using Group B1 + C1 as a baseline, revealed distinct efficacy profiles against the top bacterial taxa.

Namely, cefuroxime was more effective against the following 14 taxa (46.67%): Hydrogenophaga, Phenylobacterium, Brevundimonas intermedia, Brevundimonas aurantiaca, Streptococcus sp. (oral-clone), Phreatobacter, Streptococcus sp., SWB02, Escherichia−Shigella, Enterococcus faecium, Sphingobium sp., Faecalibacterium, Planctomyces sp. 248, and Aquabacterium.

Metronidazole was more effective against the following 6 taxa (20%): Sphingomonas, Ralstonia, Methylobacterium-Methylorubrum, Pelomonas sp. SG98, Acinetobacter soli, and Corynebacterium (Fig. 3a).

Primary bacterial source of TOETVA

Comparative analysis of bacterial species correlation was conducted among drainage-fluid groups (B1 + C1, A, O), disinfected chin skin swabs (Group CI), and oral vestibular swabs (Group N). The correlation heat map revealed a strong association between the bacterial profiles of the Group B1 + C1 and the microbiota obtained from disinfected chin skin (Group CI) (ρ = 0.8195, 95% CI: 0.6865–0.8994, P < 0.001) and open surgery (Group O) (ρ = 0.9347, 95% CI: 0.8101–0.9787, P < 0.001). In contrast, the drainage-fluid samples exhibited limited overlap and a weaker correlation with the bacterial community detected in oral vestibular swabs (Group N) (Fig. 4).

Bacterial culture identification

During the 2-week follow-up period, two cases of wound infection were identified in Group C within one week postoperatively (Fig. 5). After extracting pus from the wound for bacterial culture and identification, Streptococcus was identified as the pathogenic bacterium, and the drug sensitivity test results confirmed cefuroxime’s superior effectiveness against Streptococcus.

Oral cleaning efficacy

The analysis of swabs treated with various regimens (0.9% NaCl, mouthwash [alcohol], tinidazole, and povidone-iodine) indicated that Streptococcus was the predominant bacterial species detected in the oral vestibule. Povidone–iodine (Group VI) most effectively reduced streptococcal abundance. In contrast, tinidazole (Group TD) achieved the greatest overall reduction in total bacterial abundance. (Fig. 6).

Comprehensive oral management process

The predominance of oral streptococci as pathogens in post-TOETVA wound infections underscores the critical importance of comprehensive oral management for these patients. In response, we have innovatively developed an oral care protocol that leverages the complementary antibacterial profiles of tinidazole, which demonstrates efficacy against a broad range of oral microbiota, and povidone-iodine, which is highly effective against streptococci (Fig. 7).

Discussion

TOETVA has gained global acceptance as a scarless alternative to conventional thyroid surgery [24]. However, given that the surgical incision is made within the oral vestibule, TOETVA is classified as a clean-contaminated procedure, raising persistent concerns about surgical-site infection [25]. Although prophylactic antibiotics are often administered empirically before surgery to reduce the risk of surgical-site infection [26], the absence of clear guidelines on agent selection and dosing timing may lead to antibiotic overuse and potentially contribute to antimicrobial resistance [27].

Our findings indicate that regardless of the timing of administration (preoperative prophylaxis or postoperative therapy), there were no cases of infection in the cefuroxime group throughout the follow-up period. Preoperative prophylactic use of antibiotics is a standardized and safe method in Class II incision surgery, but our study showed that it is also safe and feasible to use antibiotics after surgery. This observation aligns with the conclusions of other scholars who suggest that routine antibiotic prophylaxis may be unnecessary [28]. It is important to note, however, that the risk of infection may increase with prolonged operative time [12]. Therefore, for procedures involving surgeons who are still ascending the learning curve, or when more extensive dissections such as lateral neck dissection are attempted via TOETVA, we continue to recommend conventional prophylactic antibiotic use, potentially supplemented with intraoperative or postoperative doses.

Analysis of the drainage-fluid data revealed that cefuroxime exhibited bactericidal or inhibitory effects against 15 bacterial species, whereas metronidazole was effective against only 4. By POD1, cefuroxime remained effective against 14 species compared with 6 in case of metronidazole; by POD2, it covered 14 species versus 12 in case of metronidazole. These results suggest that cefuroxime provides broader and more sustained antibacterial coverage in the setting of TOETVA.

The clinical follow-up data further demonstrated that no infections occurred in the cefuroxime group, whereas two infections were observed in the metronidazole group. Although Fisher’s exact test revealed no statistically significant difference in infection rates between the groups (P = 0.117), a clinically relevant trend favoring cefuroxime was observed. Notably, the upper limit of the 95% CI for the infection rate in the metronidazole group exceeded 30%, suggesting a substantial residual infection risk associated with metronidazole monotherapy in this patient cohort. Furthermore, 16S rDNA sequencing analysis demonstrated that the relative abundance of streptococcus species in drainage fluid specimens was significantly higher in the metronidazole group than in the cefuroxime group. These findings indicate that metronidazole exhibits insufficient bacteriostatic efficacy against streptococcus, failing to effectively inhibit its proliferation. In the study population, cefuroxime seems to be more effective than metronidazole. This disparity can be explained by the distinct antibacterial spectra of the two agents. Cefuroxime, a β-lactamase-stable second-generation cephalosporin, demonstrates potent activity against Gram-positive bacteria such as oral streptococci [29, 30]. In contrast, metronidazole primarily targets anaerobic bacteria and exhibits limited efficacy against aerobic organisms. Consequently, for preventing streptococcus-dominated TOETVA postoperative infections, cefuroxime’s broader spectrum and greater potency prove more effective.

Correlation analysis revealed that the bacterial profile in the drainage-fluid from the TOETVA patients prior to antibiotic administration closely mirrored the flora identified on disinfected chin skin and in open-thyroidectomy procedures, while showing only a weak association with oral vestibular bacteria. This suggests that, despite rigorous skin and oral disinfection, the primary source of bacterial presence following TOETVA likely originates from the skin microbiota. Reassuringly, as observed in open thyroidectomy, skin flora disinfected with povidone-iodine does not appear to increase the incidence of surgical-site infection, supporting the continued use of standard skin antisepsis in this context. However, the migration of oral bacteria — particularly streptococci — remains a tangible risk for postoperative infection. These findings underscore the critical importance of implementing a comprehensive perioperative oral management strategy in patients undergoing TOETVA. In response, we have developed an integrated oral care protocol designed to mitigate this risk, representing a novel approach to enhancing surgical outcomes in transoral endoscopic thyroidectomy.

A detailed analysis of postoperative wound drainage-fluid following TOETVA revealed that postoperative infections were predominantly caused by oral streptococci. Comparison of various oral cleansing techniques indicated that tinidazole exhibited superior overall antibacterial activity, and povidone-iodine was particularly effective at eliminating streptococci. Given the current absence of a standardized oral management process for comparable oral surgeries (such as procedures for tongue cancer, gum cancer, or tonsils), the development of a systematic oral management process for TOETVA is proposed as a necessary and rational measure.

The findings of this study inform refinements to current perioperative prophylactic protocols for TOETVA. Notably, the proposed regimen is procedure-specific to TOETVA, differing fundamentally from practices for open thyroidectomy: surgical-site infections following open thyroid surgery are predominantly driven by cutaneous commensal flora, including staphylococcus aureus.

Based on our clinic data and microbiological evidence, we propose a refined perioperative strategy centered on cefuroxime — administered 30 min before surgery — as the cornerstone of systemic prophylaxis. This is complemented by a rigorous local protocol encompassing preoperative oral decontamination (tinidazole), povidone-iodine antisepsis, sterile barrier protection, copious wound irrigation, secure closed-suction drainage, and postoperative compressive dressings. The adjunctive use of tinidazole rinses during the postoperative course provides sustained antimicrobial coverage. We emphasize that strict compliance with this comprehensive process is indispensable for mitigating infection risk and ensuring procedural success.

Limitations

This study yielded several noteworthy findings, though it is not without limitations. The single-center design, coupled with a non-randomized, time-allocation-based cohort, may introduce selection bias. The modest sample size contributed to the observed zero infection events in the cefuroxime group, resulting in wide 95% confidence intervals and inadequate statistical power — raising the risk of type II error (i.e., failing to detect a true treatment effect). Furthermore, while 16S rDNA gene sequencing elucidates shifts in microbial ecology, it is not a proxy for clinical outcome assessment: relative abundance metrics quantify community structure but do not confirm active infection, pathogenicity, or causality, particularly in scenarios where molecular trends conflict with observed clinical endpoints. This distinction is critical when translating sequencing data into clinical decision-making, and defines a key boundary condition of our work. Analyzing the results of four oral hygiene protocols, povidone-iodine can reduce the abundance of streptococci, however, in clinical practice, it is still necessary to conduct cross trials on individual subjects to clarify its effectiveness. Additionally, while we developed a comprehensive oral management process, it has not been empirically tested against other intervention models; its comparative effectiveness and superiority therefore remain to be confirmed, which also limits the broader applicability of the current findings.

Future research involving randomized controlled trials of antibiotic regimens, longer follow-up for SSI and delayed infection, multicenter collaborations, larger sample sizes, and well-controlled experimental comparisons will be valuable to verify and extend the preliminary insights gained in this work.

Conclusions

Based on the findings from patients undergoing TOETVA, our data indicate that the bacteria present in postoperative drainage-fluid originate primarily from the migration of colonizing flora from the skin and oral cavity. Following rigorous preoperative disinfection, skin-derived colonizing bacteria do not appear to contribute significantly to postoperative infection. In contrast, oral-derived colonizing bacteria — such as Streptococcus species — are primary causative agents of infection after TOETVA. Given that cefuroxime demonstrates better activity against oral streptococci, which represent the predominant pathogens in post-TOETVA infections, cefuroxime is recommended over metronidazole for antibiotic prophylaxis in these patients. These results, together with the traced origin of pathogenic oral bacteria, underscore the necessity and rationale for integrating comprehensive oral care protocols into the routine management of individuals undergoing TOETVA.

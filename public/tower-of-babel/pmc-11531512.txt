Reliability research of thyristors for HVDC transmission system
Authors: Yang Xu, Lei Pang, Tianxi Xie, Zhengguang Chen, Jun Guo
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
The long-term operation performance of HVDC transmission systems is significantly influenced by the reliability of thyristors. However, the current reliability research of thyristors is mainly based on the leakage current and there is no comprehensive degradation characteristics of thyristors. In this article, the degradation characteristics of thyristors under voltage and temperature accelerated ageing tests are obtained, including the reverse recovery characteristic, on-state characteristic, blocking characteristic and gate characteristic. The reverse recovery characteristic obviously degrades under the voltage accelerated ageing tests. The reverse recovery charge and reverse recovery time decrease by over 10% after the tests. Seven thyristors operating for a certain number of years from two HVDC stations are tested, and the results verify the thyristor degradation characteristics obtained from the ageing tests. Moreover, the mechanisms of the leakage current and reverse recovery characteristic are analyzed in detail.

Introduction

High voltage direct current (HVDC) technology is applied for long-distance and bulk power transmission1–3. Its voltage level and transmission capacity have been continuously improved in the past five decades, from the Gotland HVDC project (± 50 kV, 10 MW) in Sweden in 1970 to the Changji-Guquan HVDC project (± 1100 kV, 12,000 MW) in 20184. The existing HVDC systems in the world mainly use the thyristor valve converter, which directly influences the performance of the HVDC transmission system.

Thyristors have been developed from an early size of 3 inches to 6 inches, current from 200 A to 6250 A, and voltage from 2000 V to 8500 V, with improvement of key performance parameters of high voltage thyristors, such as the reverse recovery time and on-state voltage. Although the reliability of the thyristor for HVDC transmission systems has been greatly improved compared to the early years, thyristor failure is still one of the important factors that affects the reliability of HVDC transmission systems in long-term operation. Every two years, CIGRE B4 provides a global HVDC project reliability survey report and gives the failure rate of the thyristors for each HVDC project in the world5. There is a large failure number of thyristors in some HVDC projects every year.

According to the failure process, the failure of power electronic devices can be divided into sudden failure and failure after long-term operation6. The failure rate of thyristors is generally considered to satisfy the law of the bathtub curve. Long-term operation failure is usually caused by degradation of the thyristor7, which is in the third stage of the bathtub curve. After a long period of stable operation, with increasing operating years, the failure rate of thyristors substantially increases, which influences the stable operation of the HVDC transmission system.

According to the failure mechanism, the failure reasons of thyristors can be divided into internal and external causes. The internal causes refer to the thyristor itself, including manufacturing process problems, material problems, testing problems and so on. The external causes refer to external factors, including the environment, valve assembly, operation, maintenance, and design problems.

After the large-scale application of thyristors, more scholars studied the reliability of thyristors. Milan Cepek from the Quebec Hydropower Agency in Canada found that thyristors are systematically ageing in HVDC projects. Ageing is reflected in the deterioration of the contact between the thyristor and the radiator and the contact within the gate, the wear out of the silicon wafer, the increase of the on-state voltage, the decrease of the reverse recovery charge, and the deterioration of the voltage blocking ability8. The Toshiba Corporation of Japan has studied the degradation characteristics of thyristors more comprehensively. It compared the parameters of thyristors that had been operated in an HVDC converter station for 16 years with those of the same batch that had not been used. The results showed that the leaking current and the gate trigger voltage of the thyristors changed slightly, while the turn-off time and the reverse recovery charge were reduced by 39% and 30%, respectively9. In terms of the long-term stability of thyristors, the passivation process of thyristors has a great effect on the electrical stability, especially the leakage current. Moreover, the excessive impurities remaining in the passivation material and passivation process lead to failure of silicon devices10. The failure of the diode, thyristor and metal–oxide–semiconductor field-effect transistor (MOSFET) at junction temperatures above 200° was studied. Degradation of some materials in the failed device was observed, existing in the silicon wafer and the passivation material. If the leakage current at the junction edge is small and the distribution of the leakage current is uniform, then the device can work above 200 ℃11. Because of the secondary conduction of thyristors during the reverse recovery period triggered by du/dt, damage to the thyristor is likely. The current density distribution is unbalanced, and the maximum current density is approximately five times that normally triggered by the gate signal12. In addition to HVDC transmission systems, the reliability and degradation characteristics of thyristors in static var compensators (SVCs) have also been studied13. Reliability research on power electronic devices has received more attention for various applications including the converters for wind turbines and electric vehicles, DC hybrid breakers, power electronic transformers and microgrids14–25. A detailed overview of the latest progress in the reliability research on power electronic devices has been presented6,22. Accelerated Aging Test is used to evaluate the reliability of the device24 and the aging of mterial is observed25. In addition, various types of tests are applied to thyristors to ensure the reliability of the device. For example, the IEC 60747-6 Semiconductor devices—Part 6: Discrete devices—Thyristors standard specifies endurance tests to ensure the long-term operation performance26. However, this could not change the failure issues after the aging of the semiconductor device.

As of 2021, more than 28 HVDC projects have been in operation for more than 20 years worldwide, and the transmission power exceeds 233 GW. Because blocking of HVDC transmission systems may cause collapse of the AC power grid, considering the numerous HVDC projects around the world, the reliability of thyristors is important to the safety of the power grid. With some HVDC projects gradually entering the ageing stage, evaluating the reliability of thyristors is very important. However, the existing degradation characteristics of high-power thyristors are not very clear.

In this paper, reliability research on high-power thyristors is widely investigated. To research the degradation characteristics of thyristors, voltage and temperature accelerated ageing tests are carried out on thyristors. The degradation curves of four kinds of electrical characteristics are obtained, including the reverse recovery characteristic, on-state characteristic, blocking characteristic and gate characteristic. Then, to verify the degradation characteristics of thyristors obtained from accelerated ageing tests, seven thyristors operating for a certain number of years from two HVDC converter stations are tested. Moreover, the mechanisms of the leakage current and reverse recovery characteristic are analyzed. Finally, conclusions are presented.

Degradation characteristics of thyristors after Accelerated Ageing tests

Accelerated ageing tests

To shorten the test cycle, accelerated ageing tests were designed, including a voltage accelerated ageing test and a temperature accelerated ageing test.

To research the degradation characteristics of the thyristor, four typical electrical characteristics of thyristors were selected, including the reverse recovery characteristic, on-state characteristic, blocking characteristic and gate characteristic. The testing methods and procedures for all these parameters are based on IEC Standard26. The test results were obtained under strict adherence to the standard’s requirements. For example, the forward leakage current Id was measured at a VDRM of 8500 V at the room temperature, and the reverse leakage current Ir was measured at a VRRM of 8500 V at room temperature, ensuring the accuracy of the parameter measurement results. Temperature accelerated ageing test

The temperature accelerated ageing test platform was established as shown in Fig. 1. The platform consists of a high-power DC source and a number of thyristor samples in series to form the main circuit, and each thyristor is connected in parallel with a voltage leveling resistor to ensure the consistency of the series trigger conduction. The 20 channel data collector is used as the measurement loop to monitor and record the temperature and voltage drop of each thyristor during the degradation test in real time, which is convenient for adjusting the temperature change caused by the external environment and finding the abnormal condition of the device by the computer. At the same time, it has 10 thyristor series trigger module. The rated power of DC source is 25 kW, and it can provide 500 A current and 50 V output voltage, which is higher than the threshold voltage of 10 series thyristors to meet the requirements of thyristor conduction. At the same time, it is equipped with surveillance camera and smoke alarm to ensure the safety of the test.

The ageing rate k of thyristors conforms to the Arrhenius law as follows9,271\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$k = A{{\text{e}}^{ - Ea/RT}}$$\end{document}

Where, T is the temperature, and the A, E and a are the constants related to the material. Therefore, the thyristor ageing process is exponentially related to the junction temperature. In the experiment, the measured temperature was the ambient temperature, while the junction temperature was calculated based on the thermal resistance Eq. (2)6,28,29.

According to the thyristor data sheet, the thyristor should operate under a maximum junction temperature of 125 ℃, while in practice, the thyristor can operate above 200 ℃ for a certain time13. Therefore, a preliminary experiment was carried out to determine the temperature for the accelerated ageing test. The results indicated that if the junction temperature is higher than 190 ℃ for the selected thyristor, then the gate structure can be damaged in a short time, causing device failure. In contrast, when the junction temperature is 170 ℃, the selected thyristor can operate for a long time. Therefore, a junction temperature of 170 ℃ was selected for the accelerated ageing test.

The junction temperatures of selected thyristors were found to have certain differences under the same conduction current, and as the conduction current increases, the temperature differences become more obvious. Therefore, a preliminary experiment was carried out to select thyristors that have similar temperature characteristics.

Finally, 10 thyristors (TB1–B10) with the same temperature characteristics were selected for the temperature accelerated ageing test. Because the average junction temperature is approximately proportional to the current, the junction temperature was controlled by the thyristor current using a DC source. The testing time was 1000 h, and the electrical characteristics of the thyristors were measured every 100 h. The degradation characteristics were obtained as follows.

Recovery characteristic

There is a decreasing trend of the reverse recovery charge Qr and reverse recovery time ts of the thyristors with increasing testing time, as shown in Fig. 2. After 1000 h of testing time, Qr decreases by approximately 16.8% and ts decreases by approximately 17% on average. Moreover, the decreasing rate is relatively large at the beginning of the test. Qr and ts have large increases immediately before device failure, for example in TB2, TB3, TB4, and TB6.

Voltage accelerated ageing test

The voltage accelerated ageing test was establised by using half wave voltage of power frequency as shown in Fig. 6. Voltage regulator T1 and boost transformer T2 are used to adjust and increase the power frequency voltage to the required test, which can reach the highest test voltage peak of more than 10 kV. The silicon diodes D1 and D2 constitute a half-wave rectifier circuit, and the rectified voltage is applied to the test thyristor. K is a timing switch to control the degradation test time in each stage. F is the current limiting protection device, which can automatically cut off the experimental circuit when the device degradation failure occurs in the test process.

Fig. 6Circuit of voltage accelerated ageing test.

Circuit of voltage accelerated ageing test.

The voltage accelerated ageing test adopted a sine voltage wave equal to 1.06 UD or 1.06Ur, where UD is the rated forward voltage, 8500 V and Ur is the rated reverse voltage, 8500 V. So the voltage used for voltage accelerated ageing is 9010 V. The testing time was 1000 h, and the test was suspended every 100 h to measure the electrical characteristics. To protect the test platform, if the leakage current of the thyristors was greater than 220 mA, the test automatically stopped.

Six thyristors were tested. Two of them failed at the early stage. The other thyristors did not fail until the test finished. The four key electrical characteristics were obtained as follows.

Reverse recovery characteristic

The reverse recovery charge and reverse recovery time have no obvious change trend during the test, as shown in Fig. 7. The reverse recovery characteristic mainly reflects the speed of carrier dissipation in the chip under the same external circuit and test conditions. It is related to the doping, structure and defects in the chip. However, under blocking voltage stress, the thyristor does not turn on or turn off. Therefore, the stress has little influence on the carrier dissipation inside the chip, which may not change the reverse recovery characteristic.

Fig. 7Reverse recovery characteristic after voltage accelerated ageing test.

Reverse recovery characteristic after voltage accelerated ageing test.

On-state characteristic

The on-state voltage of the thyristor has no obvious change trend, and there is no obvious change before failure, as shown in Fig. 8. Because the chip does not turn on or turn off during the test process, there is almost no impact on the on-state characteristic of the thyristors.

On-state characteristic after voltage accelerated ageing test.

Blocking characteristic

The forward leakage current Id significantly increases, by approximately 16.8% on average, during the test, as shown in Fig. 9, while the reverse leakage current Ir exhibits no obvious change. The leakage current also increases, and the blocking voltage decreases before device failure. Therefore, the voltage stress has an important influence on the blocking characteristic.

Fig. 9Blocking characteristic after voltage accelerated ageing test.

Blocking characteristic after voltage accelerated ageing test.

Gate characteristic

Figure 10 shows that the gate trigger voltage and gate trigger current of the thyristors have no obvious change trend. Therefore, the voltage stress can be considered to have little influence on the gate trigger characteristic of the thyristors.

Fig. 10Gate characteristic after voltage accelerated ageing test.

Gate characteristic after voltage accelerated ageing test.

In summary, during the voltage accelerated aging test, the blocking characteristic exhibits obvious degradation, especially the forward leakage current with average increase about 16.8%. While the reverse recovery characteristic and gate characteristic have no obvious change trend. And the on-state voltage and gate trigger characteristics remained stable throughout the aging process. Voltage stress had the most impact on the blocking characteristic, particularly before device failure. In addition, note that voltage and temperature stresses on thyristors always accompany each other at the same time in the actual operation of HVDC transmission systems.

Analysis of parameter degradation

The degradation characteristics of thyristors are obtained from accelerated ageing tests and retests of thyristors from HVDC stations. The leakage current and reverse recovery characteristic exhibit obvious and consistent degradation. The degradation mechanisms of these two characteristics are analyzed as follows.

Leakage current

For silicon semiconductor devices, the leakage current is divided into the internal leakage current and the surface leakage current. The dominant leakage current is the surface leakage current from room temperature to high temperature. The internal leakage current is composed of the diffusion current in the neutral region, the current generated by the space charge region and the avalanche multiplication current. The surface leakage current is mainly generated by the space charge region on the surface of the p-n junction. Due to the p-n-p-n structure of the thyristor, different p-n junctions withstand different forward and reverse voltages.

The surface leakage current is mainly affected by the following factors:The surface leakage current increases due to interface defects, which may be caused by unsaturated bonds and mechanical damage. The shedding phenomenon on the passivation layer of thyristors from station A under a microscope is shown in Fig. 15. This is why the surface leakage current increases. In addition, in this case, the surface leakage current is always much higher than the body current.Surface contamination and movable positive ions in the oxide film produce minority carrier concentration in the space charge region of the n-type semiconductor surface, which leads to an increase in the surface diffusion leakage current.The junction terminal sharply influences the surface leakage current. To improve the blocking withstand voltage, the bevel angle is set to increases the width of the space charge region of the p-n junction, which also increases the surface leakage current.Because of the increase in the thyristor leakage current after the accelerated ageing test and operation in the HVDC station, long-term operation can be inferred to worsen the thyristor surface state of the p-n junction, including the passivation dielectric performance.

Conclusion

In this paper, the degradation characteristics of high-power thyristors used in HVDC transmission systems are derived from voltage and temperature accelerated ageing tests, including the reverse recovery characteristic, on-state characteristic, blocking characteristic and gate characteristic. Besides the decrease of leakage current of thyristor after long-term operation, the results also show that the reverse recovery charge obviously decreases after long-term operation, as does the reverse recovery time, which is verified by retests of operating thyristors from HVDC stations. This is valuable for the reliability evaluation of thyristors in operation. Moreover, the mechanisms of the leakage current and reverse recovery characteristics are analyzed in detail.

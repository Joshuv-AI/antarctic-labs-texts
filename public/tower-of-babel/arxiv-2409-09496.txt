Revisiting the Problem of Positronium Moving Across a Magnetic Field
B.O. Kerbikov, A.A. Simovonian

Abstract
Positronium spectrum and lifetimes are known with high precision. The situation is different for positronium moving across a magnetic field. The total momentum does not commute with the Hamiltonian and is replaced by the conserved pseudomomentum. The internal dynamics is not separated from the motion of the system as a whole. The Coulomb potential well is distorted and a wide outer potential well is created. We analytically determine the energy spectrum for a broad range of magnetic field and pseudomomentum values. The ground state energy is compared with the result obtained by solving the Bethe-Salpeter equation. The gauge independence of the pseudomomentum expectation value is established. On the pseudomomentum-magnetic field plane we locate the region in which the ground state resides in the outer well. The results may play a role in the suppression of pulsars’ radio emission (polar cap problem).

III
The Outer Potential Well Formation

The motion of Ps across a magnetic field (MF) results in a transformation of the potential shape. For a fixed value of MF a second potential well begins to form at a certain critical value
\text{K}_{c}
of the pseudomomentum. This phenomenon has been studied for the hydrogen atom
[
18
,
19
,
20
,
21
,
22
,
23
]
, Ps
[
27
,
28
]
, and quarkonium
[
29
]
.

From (
8
) and (
9
) we may write the internal motion effective potential
\text{U}_{\text{eff}}(\bm{\eta})
as

\displaystyle\text{U}_{\text{eff}}(\bm{\eta})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbf{K}^{2}}{4m}+\frac{e\text{B}}{2m}\text{K}_{x}y-\frac{e\text{B}}{2m}\text{K}_{y}x+

(13)

\displaystyle+\frac{e^{2}\text{B}^{2}}{4m}(x^{2}+y^{2})-\frac{e^{2}}{\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}+z^{2}}},

where
\bm{\eta}=(x,y,z)
. The first term is spatially independent and acts as an additive constant to the binding energies. The evolution of the potential shape as a function of
K
is governed mainly by the interplay between the Stark
x,y
terms and the diamagnetic fourth term in (
13
). The potential possesses azimuthal symmetry, and one can set either
\text{K}_{x}
or
\text{K}_{y}
equal to zero. We choose
K_{y}=0
, so that
\mathbf{K}=(\text{K},0,0)
. The projection of (
13
) onto the
x=z=0
plane is

\text{U}_{\text{eff}}(y)=\frac{\mathbf{K}^{2}}{4m}+\frac{e\text{B}}{2m}\text{K}y+\frac{e^{2}\text{B}^{2}}{4m}y^{2}-\frac{e^{2}}{|y|}.

(14)

The condition for the potential minimum at
y<0

\partial\text{U}_{\text{eff}}/\partial y=0
yields the equation

y^{3}+\frac{\text{K}}{e\text{B}}y^{2}-\frac{2m}{\text{B}^{2}}=0.

(15)

For the outer magnetic well (MW) and a saddle point (SP) to exist for
\text{K}>0,y<0
this equation must have three real roots. It implies the following condition on
K
and
B

\text{K}^{3}>\text{K}_{c}^{3}=\frac{27}{2}(me^{2})(e\text{B})=\frac{27}{2}\bigg(\frac{e\text{B}}{a_{0}}\bigg).

(16)

The three roots are

\displaystyle y_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\text{K}\ell^{2}}{3}\bigg[2\cos\frac{\alpha}{3}-1\bigg],

(17)

\displaystyle y_{2,3}=-\frac{\text{K}\ell^{2}}{3}\bigg[1+\cos\frac{\alpha}{3}\pm\sqrt{3}\sin\frac{\alpha}{3}\bigg],

(18)

where

\cos\alpha=\frac{27}{a_{0}\ell^{2}\text{K}^{3}}-1=\frac{2\text{K}_{c}^{3}}{\text{K}^{3}}-1.

(19)

Therefore
y_{2}<y_{3}<0<y_{1}
. It means that
y_{1}
is an unphysical solution,
y_{2}\equiv y_{m}
corresponds to the MW minimum,
y_{3}\equiv y_{s}
– to the saddle point. Note that at fixed value of MF and increasing
\text{K}\gg\text{K}_{c}
(
18
) yields

\displaystyle y_{m}\rightarrow-\frac{\text{K}}{e\text{B}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\text{K}l^{2},y_{s}\rightarrow-\sqrt{\frac{2em}{\text{B}\text{K}}}=-l\sqrt{\frac{2}{a_{0}\text{K}}},

(20)

\displaystyle\frac{y_{m}}{y_{s}}\rightarrow\frac{3\sqrt{3}}{2}\bigg(\frac{\text{K}}{\text{K}_{c}}\bigg)^{3/2}.

This shows that with increasing
K
, the MW tends to separate from CW for any given value of the MF. In the same limit,
\text{K}\gg\text{K}_{c}
, the MW minimum approaches zero energy, from below:
\text{U}_{\text{eff}}(y_{m})\rightarrow-e^{2}(e\text{B})/\text{K}
.

The effective potential (
14

y
direction is shown in Fig.

\text{B}=6\cdot 10^{7}
G and three different values of
K
. The solid red horizontal line corresponds to
\text{I}=e\text{B}/m
. The quantity
I
is the ionization potential. In the absence of the Coulomb interaction, it is equal to the sum of the individual electron and positron Landau ground state energies.

The motion in a plane perpendicular to the direction of the MF is at large distances dominated by the contribution of the diamagnetic term. This term provides confinement and ionization is possible only along the MF direction. The presence of the cubic term in (
15
) makes the evolution of the effective potential with
K

The Energy Spectrum Equation

The spectral problem (
8

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{H}}=\frac{\mathbf{K}^{2}}{4m}-\frac{1}{m}\Delta_{\bm{\eta}}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\frac{e}{2m}[\mathbf{K}\times\mathbf{B}]\cdot\bm{\eta}+

(21)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{e^{2}}{4m}[\mathbf{B}\times\bm{\eta}]^{2}-\frac{e^{2}}{|\bm{\eta}|}

does not admit an exact solution. For the exciton and the hydrogen atom moving in a MF, some analytical approximations have been developed
[

,
16
,
18

19

20
]
. Numerical calculations for Ps have been performed in
[

,
28
]
. We aim to solve the problem relying on analytical methods as far as possible. Following
[
13
,
20

22
]
, it is appropriate to use the «shifted» representation

, where

is the difference between the coordinates of the gyro-motion guiding centers of

and

\displaystyle\bm{\eta}_{c}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbf{K}_{1}\times\mathbf{B}}{e\text{B}^{2}}-\frac{\mathbf{K}_{2}\times\mathbf{B}}{-e\text{B}^{2}}=

(22)

\displaystyle=-\frac{[\mathbf{B}\times\mathbf{K}]}{e\text{B}^{2}}=-\frac{2m}{e\text{B}^{2}}[\mathbf{B}\times\mathbf{V}],

where

is given by (
12
). Note that with

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\text{K}_{z}^{2}}{4m}-\frac{1}{m}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial z^{2}}-\frac{1}{m}\bigg(\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\eta_{x}^{\prime 2}}+\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\eta_{y}^{\prime 2}}\bigg)+

\displaystyle+\frac{e^{2}}{4m}\mathbf{B}^{2}\bm{\rho}^{\prime 2}-\frac{e^{2}}{\sqrt{(\bm{\rho}^{\prime}+\bm{\rho}_{0})^{2}+z^{2}}}.

It is well known that the Schrodinger equation for the hydrogenlike system (hydrogen atom, Ps, exciton) under the presence of a uniform MF does not admit exact analytical solution

]
. The Hamiltonians (

) and (
23

,
32
]
. The wave unction is expanded over the complete set of Landau orbitals

]
. Only the term corresponding to the lowest Landau (LLL) level is retained in this expansion. For the Hamiltonian (

) the adiabatic approximation takes the form

\varphi_{\mathbf{K}}(\bm{\rho}^{\prime},z)=R(\bm{\rho}^{\prime})f(z),

(25)

and

33
]

34
]
. Such a program has been fulfilled during the past decades for the non-moving hydrogen atom in MF, see

,
34
,
35
]
and references therein. For the astrophysical applications it is important that for

G the accuracy is high. For moving hydrogen atom the adiabatic approximation has been considered in

18
,
36
]
.

V
The Separated Potential Wells

According to (
16
) the outer potential well is formed when
\text{K}^{3}>\text{K}_{c}^{3}=(27/2)me^{3}\text{B}=(27/2)(1/a_{0}\ell^{2})
. Under this condition different configurations of the two potential wells and a barrier between them are possible. The shape of the resulting configuration dictates the most appropriate methods to solve eq.(
26

a_{\text{B}}(\text{Ps})=2a_{0}=\frac{2}{me^{2}},\ell=\frac{1}{\sqrt{e\text{B}}},|\bm{\rho}_{0}|=|y_{0}|=\frac{\text{K}}{e\text{B}}.

(30)

Here
|y_{0}|
is the absolute value of the projection of
\bm{\rho}_{0}=-[\mathbf{B}\times\mathbf{K}]/e\text{B}^{2}
onto the
x=z=0
plane and
\mathbf{K}=(\text{K},0,0)
in this projection. Important to note that according to (
20

y_{0}=-\text{K}/e\text{B}
coincides with the position of the MW minimum
y_{m}
in the limit
\text{K}\gg\text{K}_{c}
. Therefore one can interpret
|y_{0}|
as the distance from the bottom of the MW to the Coulomb center. The two wells, the Coulomb and the magnetic ones, are actually separated if

Our task is to solve eq.(

VII
The Issue of the Gauge Choice in the Ps Problem

It is well known that the problem of a charged particle or of a neutral two-particle system moving in a constant MF may be solved either in a symmetric gauge or in the Landau one
[
30
]
. The symmetric gauge which we adopt in the present work has been commonly used in treating the neutral two-body systems in MF, see the references cited above and
[
44
]
as a possible starting point. On the other hand, the problem of Ps moving in the magnetosphere of a NS has been studied using the Bethe-Salpeter equation in the Landau gauge
[
8
,
9
,
10
]
. We would like to recapitulate the main points concerning the relation between the two gauges with the emphasis on the transformation properties of the pseudomomentum. The gauges under condition are

\displaystyle\text{Symmetric (SG): }\mathbf{A}^{S}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\text{B}(-y,x,0),

(58)

\displaystyle\text{Landau (LG): }\mathbf{A}^{L}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\text{B}(-y,0,0).

(59)

Sometimes
\mathbf{A}^{L}
is called the 1st LG and the second LG is defined as
\mathbf{A}^{L2}=\text{B}(0,x,0)
. The SG is related to the LG by the following gauge transformation

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}^{L}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}^{S}+\bm{\nabla}f(x,y,z),

(60)

\displaystyle\text{B}\begin{bmatrix}-y\\
0\\
0\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\text{B}}{2}\begin{bmatrix}-y\\
x\\
0\end{bmatrix}+\bm{\nabla}f(x,y,z).

(61)

From (
60
) and (
61
) one gets
f(x,y,z)=(-1/2)\text{B}xy
. The Ps Hamiltonian in an arbitrary gauge is given by (
1
). The pseudomomentum which commutes with
\hat{\mathcal{H}}
and can be diagonalized simultaneously with
\hat{\mathcal{H}}
reads
[
13
]

\displaystyle\hat{\mathbf{K}}=\hat{\mathbf{p}}_{1}+\hat{\mathbf{p}}_{2}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle e(\mathbf{A}_{1}-\mathbf{A}_{2})+

(62)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle e[\mathbf{B}\times(\mathbf{r}_{1}-\mathbf{r}_{2})].

The previously presented formula (
4
) corresponds to the SG. For the neutral two-body system the components
\hat{\mathbf{K}}_{x}
and
\hat{\mathbf{K}}_{y}
commute with one another. The expression (
62
) is obviously gauge dependent and takes the following forms in SG and LG

\displaystyle\hat{\mathbf{K}}^{S}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{\mathbf{p}}_{1}+\hat{\mathbf{p}}_{2}+\frac{1}{2}e\text{B}\begin{bmatrix}-(y_{1}-y_{2})\\
x_{1}-x_{2}\\
0\end{bmatrix},

(63)

\displaystyle\hat{\mathbf{K}}^{L}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{\mathbf{p}}_{1}+\hat{\mathbf{p}}_{2}+e\text{B}\begin{bmatrix}0\\
x_{1}-x_{2}\\
0\end{bmatrix}.

(64)

Worth noting that
\hat{\mathbf{K}}^{L}_{x}
coincides with Ps canonical momentum
\mathbf{P}_{x}=\mathbf{p}_{1}+\mathbf{p}_{2}
. One easily finds that
\hat{\mathbf{K}}^{S}
and
\hat{\mathbf{K}}^{L}
are related by the unitary gauge transformation

\hat{\mathbf{K}}^{L}=\mathcal{U}\hat{\mathbf{K}}^{S}\mathcal{U}^{\dagger},

(65)

where

\mathcal{U}(x_{i},y_{i})=\exp\bigg\{-\frac{ie\text{B}}{2}\bigg(x_{1}y_{1}-x_{2}y_{2}\bigg)\bigg\},i=1,2.

The next step will be to show that the expectation values of the pseudomomentum components over the wave function are gauge-invariant quantities. The gauge transformation of the pseudomomentum is compensated by the corresponding transformation of the wave function. This is an anticipated results since
\hat{\mathbf{K}}
and
\hat{\mathcal{H}}
can be diagonalized simultaneously. From now onwards we make the projection onto the
x=z=0

\hat{\mathbf{K}}=(\hat{\mathbf{K}}_{x},0,0)
. The corresponding arguments have been presented prior to (
14

30

|\psi^{S}\rangle
and
|\psi^{L}\rangle
in SG and LG correspondingly
[
30
]
. The function
|\psi^{S}\rangle
is the simultaneously eigen-function of the Hamiltonian and the operator
\hat{\mathbf{K}}_{x}

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{H}}|\psi^{S}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\text{E}|\psi^{S}\rangle,

(67)

\displaystyle\hat{\mathbf{K}}_{x}^{S}|\psi^{S}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\text{K}_{x}^{S}|\psi^{S}\rangle,

(68)

where
\text{K}_{x}
is the eigenvalue of
\hat{\mathbf{K}}_{x}
, i.e., the
x
-component of Ps pseudomomentum. Multiplying both sides of (

\mathcal{U}
from the left and inserting the identity

, one obtains

\bigg(\mathcal{U}\hat{\mathbf{K}}_{x}^{S}\mathcal{U}^{\dagger}\bigg)\mathcal{U}|\psi^{S}\rangle=\text{K}_{x}^{S}\mathcal{U}|\psi^{S}\rangle.

(69)

Equation (

On the other hand,

(72)

Comparing (

\text{K}_{x}^{S}=\text{K}_{x}^{L}=\text{K}_{x}
. We remind that according to (

\text{K}_{x}^{L}=p_{1x}+p_{2x}=\text{P}_{x}
, where
\text{P}_{x}
is the

At this point a question may arise over the validity of the simple transformation relation (

[
30
]
it is explained that the gauge transformation of the wave function follows the transformation of the potential

,

,

,
48
,

The conclusion is that the expectation value of the pseudomomentum

IX
Implications

We have shown that the Ps wave function and the energy spectrum experience deep transformation as it moves through a MF. This carries far-reaching physical implications.

The first problem to consider is the one-photon Ps annihilation. It is a matter of a long standing dispute started by the work of Carr and Sutherland
[
56
]
. The authors concluded that this process is possible from the Ps ground state and calculated its rate. This point was disproved by Wunner and Herold
[
24
]
. They advocated the assumption that the energy minimum corresponds to
\mathbf{K}=0
state and proved that the one-photon annihilation from this state is impossible. The same conclusion is shared in
[
10
,
52
]
where it is stated that the one-photon annihilation can proceed from
\text{P}_{x}>0
states, where
\text{P}_{x}
is the pseudomomentum in LG. At this point one can raise a question of whether the
\mathbf{K}=0
state from which the one-photon annihilation is forbidden is always a global ground state of Ps. We have seen that under the conditions (
31
) the outer MW emerges with the spectrum of the energy levels given by (
34
). On the other hand, with
K
increasing above
\text{K}_{c}
the states in the CW are pushed out of the well into the continuum
[
28
]
. In that way the ground state of the MW with
\text{K}\gg\text{K}_{c}
becomes the global ground state of Ps. This is illustrated by the phase diagram on the «magnetic field – pseudomomentum» plane shown in Fig.

The migration of the ground state from the CW via saddle states towards the MW corresponds to the motion along the horizontal line in the direction of the increasing

X
Summary and Future Prospects

We have investigated the transformation of the Ps wave function and energy spectrum taking place when Ps is moving through a MF. The main difference from the unmoving case is that the internal dynamics can not be separated from the centre-of-mass motion. The spectrum and the wave function parametrically depend on the generalized momentum operator
\mathbf{K}
also called pseudomomentum. When
K
exceeds the critical value (
16
) the outer «magnetic» potential well is formed in addition to the distorted Coulomb one. Two principal configurations are possible:

(i)

The separated Coulomb and outer potential wells with a potential barrier inbetween.

(ii)

The overlapping wells with bilocalized wave function.

Both cases correspond to
\text{K}>\text{K}_{c}
and to MF strong enough for adiabatic approximation to be legitimate

\gamma=\frac{\text{B}}{\text{B}_{a}}=\frac{\omega_{c}}{2R_{y}}\gg 1.

(90)

Strong MF with
\gamma\gg 1
is of a high astrophysical relevance. Which of the two above options is realized depends on the relations (
31
) and (
37
) between the three basic parameters: the Bohr radius
a_{\text{B}}(\text{Ps})=2/me^{2}
, the Landau radius
\ell=(e\text{B})^{-1/2}
, and the difference
\text{K}/e\text{B}
between the coordinates of the gyro-motion guiding centers of
e^{+}
and
e^{-}
. With
K
continuously increasing above
\text{K}_{c}
the former Coulomb states are pushed above the ionization threshold and the spectrum resides in the decentered outer well. In this case the ground state of the MW becomes the energetically global ground state of Ps. The states in the MW have infinitesimal annihilation rates and giant dipole moments.

We solved the problem in the symmetric gauge which is commonly used in treating the neutral two-body systems in MF. Previously the ground state energy of Ps moving across MF was found using the Bethe-Salpeter equation in the Landau gauge
[
8
,
10
,
52
,
54
]
. In order to compare the results we reverted back to the old problem of the transformation between the two gauges with the primary interest in the pseudomomentum transformation. Pseudomomentum is taken as an additional operator which must be diagonalized along with the Hamiltonian in both gauges. The natural conclusion is that the expectation value of the pseudomomentum is gauge independent. It means that the Ps binding energy should be the same for equal values of the pseudomomentum.

An interesting problem which was left aside in our work is magnetically stimulated Ps center-of-mass chaotic diffusion motion
[
59
,
60
,
61
]
. Roughly speaking, the center-of-mass undergoes a transition from regular to Brownian motion if the internal motion changes from regular to chaotic. Worth mentioning that diffusion motion may be important for high precision experiments with antihydrogen atoms
[
62
]
.

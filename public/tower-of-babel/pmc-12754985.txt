Effect of the Addition of Xianglian Granules to PD-1 Monoclonal Antibodies in Pancancer Patients
Authors: Tingting Wu, Shiying Li, Hui Shuang, Yun Li, Shujuan Fu, Haiyan Jiang, Shiqiang Zhang, Boqian Du, Junqiang Lv, Xiaoting Xia, Shengcheng Cao, Liping Yao, Pingsheng Pan, Siqi Feng, Cunya Li, Zhangjie Zhou, Jian Chen, Yi Zhong
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundWith the successive listing of immune checkpoint inhibitors in China, a new era of immunotherapy for malignant tumors has opened. However, this study has several limitations. Xianglian granule (XG), a Chinese herbal formula, has been proven to have a therapeutic effect on tumors in animal experiments. Our aim was to evaluate the efficacy and safety of XG combined with programmed death ligand 1 (PD-1) in pancancer patients through a randomized, double-blind, controlled trial.MethodsPatients with cancer who received PD-1 monoclonal antibody therapy were randomized to the XG or control group according to the blinded method for 9 weeks. The primary endpoint was the overall survival (OS) rate, and the secondary endpoint was the incidence rate of immune-related adverse events (irAEs). Related biological markers were observed.ResultsA total of 91 patients were included in the study. The 3-year OS rates of the XG group and the control group were 35.56% and 15.22%, respectively (P = 0.0012). The lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) level in the XG group was lower than that in the other groups, especially in the lung cancer patients (P = 0.06, P = 0.03). The two groups showed similar safety. The incidence rate of irAEs was 22.2% in the XG group and 32.6% in the control group (P = 0.27). In addition, XG can reduce D-lactic acid (D-LA) and diamine oxidase (DAO) contents (P = 0.012, P < 0.001). The B lymphocyte count and percentage of the XG group increased (P = 0.04, P = 0.035), and the interleukin-2 level decreased (P = 0.045). The Cox regression results suggested that the potential factors of the prognostic model might be XG treatment, D-LA and B lymphocyte count (P = 0.002, P < 0.001, P = 0.001).ConclusionXG had efficacy-enhancing and toxicity-reducing effects on pancancer patients when combined with a PD-1 monoclonal antibody. The mechanisms may be related to the participation of the intestinal barrier, B lymphocytes and interleukin-2. D-LA and B lymphocytes may be potential factors in the prognostic model of pancancer patients treated with immunotherapy.

Introduction

Cancer is a major public health problem worldwide. In 2020, there were 19.29 million new cancer cases and 9.96 million cancer deaths worldwide, making it the second leading cause of death worldwide [49]. With the successive listing of immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) in China, a new era of immunotherapy for malignant tumors has opened [14]. ICIs have been approved for the treatment of advanced non-small cell lung cancer, kidney cancer, head and neck tumors, urothelial cancer, melanoma, breast cancer and many other cancers [55]. Monoclonal antibodies against programmed death protein-1 and its ligand (Programmed death 1/ligand 1, PD-1/L1), anti-cytotoxic T lymphocyte-antigen-4 (CTLA-4), etc., are widely used and are used to kill tumor cells via the activation of T cells. A few large, multicenter clinical studies [35, 46] have shown that immunotherapy with PD-1/L1 monoclonal antibodies has a more significant effect than traditional tumor therapy does.

The intestinal tract is the largest part of the human immune system and is formed by enteral neutral and beneficial bacteria, and it is important for preventing pathogenic bacteria and endotoxins from invading the intestinal barrier [41]. Studies have shown that the immunity of cancer patients and the efficacy of treatment are related to the intestinal barrier. The intestinal wall can be damaged because of intestinal imbalances. Toxins and bacteria leak through the intestine and enter the blood, triggering an immune response and destruction of the intestinal barrier, which leads to intestinal dysfunction and affects the immunity of patients [27, 28]. Damage to the intestinal mucosa increases the risk of cancer and changes the driver of immune regulation when pathogenic intestinal bacteria breach the vascular barrier [26]. Intestinal pathogens can promote intestinal tumor occurrence, development, and metastasis. In mice with cancer cachexia, intestinal barrier function and the composition and function of the intestinal microbiota are altered. The destruction of the intestinal vascular barrier leads to the transfer of intestinal cancer cells to the liver, indicating a complicated interaction between intestinal barrier function and cancer cells [2, 3].

Xianglian granules (XGs) are derived from an ancient book of traditional Chinese medicine (TCM) called "Taiping Huimin Heji Ju Fang", which was recorded in the Pharmacopoeia of the People's Republic of China (2020 Edition) as a prescription of traditional Chinese medicine and is composed of 800 g of Huanglian (Coptis chinensis. Ranunculaceae) processed by Wuzhuyu (Tetradium ruticarpum. Rutaceae) and 200 g of Muxiang (Aucklandia costus Falc. Asteraceae). XG plays an important role in the treatment of spleen and stomach diseases and has various pharmacological activities, such as anti-GU, anti-diarrhea, anti-ulcerative colitis, and antibacterial bacterial activities. A few domestic studies have shown that XG can not only significantly reduce histopathological damage to the small intestinal mucosa in rabbits and reduce the levels of blood diamine oxidase and serum endotoxin [5] but also downregulate proinflammatory factors such as tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α) and interferon-γ (IFN-γ) in the mouse ileum to alleviate chemotherapy-associated diarrhea in mice [56]. In addition, XG can also prevent and treat radiation-associated proctitis [60] and treat ulcerative colitis [6]combined with mesalazine [59]. Especially XG treatment showed the inhibition of the secretion of pro-inflammatory cytokines, repairing the dysfunction of intestinal epithelial barrier, enhanced autophagy, and blocked the activation of PI3K/Akt/mTOR pathway in animal experiment[13]. Some network pharmacology studies reported that the target of XG can be enriched in multiple cancer signaling pathways, such as those involved in colorectal cancer, non-small cell lung cancer, pancreatic cancer, and prostate cancer, which has potential anticancer value [15, 51]. The recent research reveled the precious perspectives on the prospective application of Coptidis Rhizoma and berberine in the prevention and management of cancer[4].

In this study, a randomized, controlled, double-blind trial used overall survival (OS) as the primary endpoint and the incidence of immune-related adverse events (irAEs) and other safety indicators combined with the expression of the intestinal barrier and related biomarkers as secondary endpoints. The purpose of this study was to evaluate the synergistic and detoxification effects of XG in immunotherapy patients across cancers and to provide evidence for the application of TCM.

Materials and methods

Study design

This is a randomized, double-blind, controlled clinical study that aims to evaluate the efficacy and safety of XG in combination with PD-1 monoclonal antibodies in pancancer patients. All patients who fulfill the eligibility criteria will be selected. The enrolled participants will be randomly allocated into 2 groups: the control group will receive immunotherapy treatment, and the XG group will receive immunotherapy treatment and be administered XG. Recruitment and ethics approval

The patients in this study were all advanced tumor patients who received immunotherapy at the Department of Oncology, Shanghai TCM-integrated Hospital affiliated to Shanghai University of Traditional Chinese Medicine. The study protocol was approved by the Regional Ethics Committee (Shanghai TCM-Integrated Hospital). All patients signed informed consent, and this study was performed following the Declaration of Helsinki. The clinical trial was registered at the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry (ChiCTR) with the registration number ChiCTR1900026300.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

Participants meeting the following criteria will be included: patients who have a confirmed malignant tumor and confirmed to have a diagnosis with a pathological examination in TNM stage IV; patients who receive PD-1 monoclonal antibody or combined immunotherapy; patients who have no antibiotics, systemic corticosteroids (or less than 10 mg/day prednisone dose) and other immunosuppressants within 1 month before enrollment; patients who have no inflammatory bowel disease, radiation enteritis, rheumatism or other diseases; patients who have no other serious complications; men and women aged 18–80 years; and those with a Karnofsky performance status (KPS) score ≥ 60 points and an expected survival period of ≥ 3 months.

Participants meeting one or more of the following criteria will be excluded: patients who have serious cardiovascular and cerebrovascular and other serious primary diseases, as well as mental illness; patients with poor compliance and incomplete data during clinical research; patients whose diagnosis is not clear; patients who do not perform the provisions of this experimental protocol; patients with incomplete observation items and are unable to evaluate the test effect and side effects; patients who have participated in other clinical trials within the past 3 months; those who have received other immune-related treatment or Chinese medicine treatment that may affect immune function; and those who suffer from other diseases due to nondrug factors and cannot continue to complete immunotherapy during the medication process.

Randomization and masking

Random method

A completely random method was used. Third-party experts used the SPSS 26.0 statistical software program to generate random sequences; 88 patient numbers were initially reserved; however, a total of 91 patients were ultimately enrolled, including 3 patients added after the screening period. This protocol amendment was approved by the Ethics Committee (registration number: ChiCTR1900026300), and the random number table was handed over to the scientific research center of the main research unit for sealing and preservation.

Blind method

Third-party experts use SPSS 26.0 statistical software to generate random medication numbers and generate first-level and second-level blind bases while generating random numbers. The staff of the hospital drug clinical research office must keep the emergency envelope for emergency unblinding.

The control group received PD-1 inhibitor combined with placebo, while the XG group received PD-1 inhibitor combined with XG treatment (three times a day, 3 g each time). Three weeks is a cycle, and the treatment is for 3 cycles. Specimen collection and clinical symptoms will be recorded and observed before enrollment and after the course of treatment.

Preparation of the medication

XG was produced by Tianjiang Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd. (Jiangsu, China, production batch number: 2001308), supervised by Shanghai Hospital of Integrative Medicine and supported by the Scientific Research Program of Shanghai Science and Technology Commission (No. 19401971600). The contents of XG include Muxiang, whose roots are used as medicine, 2.1 g; Wuzhuyu, whose fruit is used as medicine, 4.2 g; and Huanglian, whose rootstock is used as medicine, 8.4 g, which are divided according to the taxonomy of each species in Chinese Materia Medica.

Primary study objective and endpoint assessments

The primary objective of this study was to observe the efficacy of XG in immunotherapy-treated pancancer patients, with the primary endpoint being OS. The starting point of OS was the first day after patients were randomized into groups.

Secondary objectives and endpoint assessments

The secondary endpoint is safety, including the incidence rate of irAEs and the expression of relevant biological indicators. The irAEs are evaluated and graded according to the common terminology criteria for adverse events of the National Cancer Institute [32] and the ASCO Guidelines [38]. The standard divides all adverse reaction events into grades of 1–5, corresponding to mild, moderate, severe, life-threatening and death, respectively.

Subgroup analysis

In addition to the primary analyses, post hoc subgroup analyses were conducted to further explore potential heterogeneity of treatment effects. Patients were stratified according to tumor type (e.g., gastrointestinal cancer, lung cancer) and the number of metastatic lesions. These stratifications were chosen based on their established clinical relevance and potential impact on prognosis. The subgroup analyses were performed to assess whether treatment outcomes were consistent across different patient categories, although these analyses were exploratory in nature.

Statistical methods

The sample size calculation was based on an expected improvement in the 1-year overall survival (OS) rate from 40% with PD-1 monotherapy, as reported in previous studies, to 60% with the XG combination therapy. With a two-sided α of 0.05 and a power of 80% (β = 0.2), the required sample size was estimated to be at least 84 patients. A total of 91 patients were enrolled, thus fulfilling the sample size requirement.

R software 4.2.2. and SPSS 26.0 statistical software were used for data processing. The analysis included measures such as the means, medians, standard deviations, ranges, and quartiles for continuous variables and frequency tables and bar charts for categorical variables. The distribution of the data was assessed via normality tests, and appropriate descriptive statistics methods were applied to both normally and nonnormally distributed variables. Welch’s two-sample t test, the Wilcoxon rank sum test, the Wilcoxon rank sum exact test, Pearson's chi-square test and Fisher's exact test were used to compare different types of data. The log-rank test was used for the rate, and the Kaplan‒Meier method was used for survival analysis. Survival curves were generated via the Kaplan–Meier method and compared via the log rank test. A prediction model was established via Cox regression. With a 95% confidence interval (CI) and α = 0.05 as the test standard, P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Baseline characteristics and subject disposition

A total of 91 patients who were diagnosed with cancer at Shanghai TCM-Integrated Hospital affiliated to Shanghai University of Traditional Chinese Medicine were screened from March 2018 to March 2024, and all of them were assessed for eligibility. Among the 91 subjects, a total of 46 were eligible and randomized to the control group, and 45 patients were randomized to the XG group.

No significant differences, including sex, age, or number of metastatic sites, were noted between the control group patients and treatment group patients. Most patients were in good overall physical condition, and all patients had a performance status of 0 or 1 according to the Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) scale. Different status grades of patients

The Chinese medicine syndrome score (CMSC) is a concept in Chinese medicine diagnosis that is based on the patient's symptoms and signs and, according to Chinese medicine theories and experience, scores and comprehensive analyses of different symptoms to determine the patient's main symptoms and evidence. Lower scores indicate that the patient's symptoms are less severe. The KPS is a scale used to evaluate physical functioning and quality of survival in cancer patients [11]. The Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) score is correlated with the duration of patient survival, response to treatment, quality of life and comorbidity [10, 39]. This study revealed that patients’ CMSC in the XG group was lower than that in the control group (P = 0.034) and that there were more patients with a KPS ≥ 80 in the XG group than in the control group (P = 0.017), indicating that after treatment with XG, the situation of patients improved. Although there were notable differences in the ECOG score (p = 0.053), the number of patients with an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG)−0 score in the XG group was 11, whereas only 2 patients with an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG-0) score in the control group were included, indicating the potential ability of XG to improve the Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) score. Cox regression analysis revealed that the potential prognostic factors included XG treatment and several other clinical indicators. Discussion

This study demonstrated that XG has a synergistic effect on immunotherapy patients across cancers. After XG was added, the 3-year OS rate of immunotherapy patients with pancancer was 35.56%, whereas it was 15.22% in the control group. At present, there are no pancancer immunotherapy data, and the one-year OS rate of single cancer types, such as recurrent/metastatic head and neck squamous cell carcinoma, is 28.6% [45], whereas that of patients with recurrent or metastatic nasopharyngeal carcinoma is 61% [47], whereas that of patients with advanced urothelial carcinoma is 50% [53]. Notably, the significant improvement in OS observed in patients with gastrointestinal cancers (P = 0.006) following treatment with XG may be attributed to two main mechanisms: (1) direct repair of the intestinal mechanical barrier, which may mitigate chronic inflammation caused by microbial translocation; and (2) immunomodulation within the gut microenvironment, potentially enhancing antitumor responses through synergy with PD-1 monoclonal antibodies. In contrast, limited benefit was observed in patients with non-gastrointestinal malignancies, suggesting that the efficacy of XG may be influenced by the primary tumor location. These findings underscore the importance of tumor-specific factors in modulating the therapeutic outcomes of combination immunotherapies. Previous comprehensive analyses have shown that immune checkpoint inhibitors yield heterogeneous efficacy across tumor types [36], further highlighting the importance of pancancer data such as those provided in the present study.

The enhanced overall survival observed in patients with gastrointestinal cancers treated with XG in combination with PD-1 monoclonal antibodies may, at least in part, be explained by the gut–tumor axis. Given the anatomical and physiological proximity between gastrointestinal tumors and the intestinal microenvironment, interventions that modulate gut integrity and immune signaling may exert a more pronounced therapeutic effect in these patients. Specifically, XG may contribute to the restoration of the intestinal barrier and attenuation of microbiota-derived inflammatory signaling, which is often exacerbated in patients with compromised gut integrity. In parallel, XG appears to modulate local and systemic immune responses—such as increasing B cell proportions and reducing IL-2 levels—which may enhance the antitumor efficacy of immune checkpoint blockade. The limited efficacy observed in non-gastrointestinal tumors supports the hypothesis that the primary tumor location may influence the responsiveness to microbiota- and barrier-targeted interventions. These findings highlight the importance of considering the gut–tumor axis as a mechanistic pathway when designing and optimizing immunotherapeutic strategies, particularly in gastrointestinal malignancies.

In terms of safety, although the difference was not statistically significant, XG tended to reduce the incidence rate of irAEs (22.2% in the treatment group and 32.6% in the control group). By increasing the activity of the immune system, immune checkpoint blockade can have inflammatory side effects [29]. Studies have shown that in patients with recurrent or metastatic nasopharyngeal carcinoma, the incidence of irAEs of any grade and grade ≥ 3 was 94% and 20%, respectively [47], the incidence of irAEs in patients with metastatic adrenocortical carcinoma was 82.0%, and that of ≥ Grade 3 irAEs was 16.0% [19]; only 15.1% of patients with recurrent/metastatic head and neck squamous cell carcinoma treated with nivolumab experienced irAEs [45]. In addition, this study revealed that the hematological toxicity and liver and kidney toxicity of the two groups were similar and that there was no significant difference, indicating that the safety of XG was reassuring.

In terms of the intestinal barrier, the XG group presented reduced contents of DAO and D-LA in the serum. The levels of serum DAO and D-LA reflect the severity of injury to the intestinal mechanical barrier [24, 31, 42]. DAO is a highly active enzyme in the intestinal mucosal upper villi. DAO levels in the mucosal villi of the small intestine can be used to reflect the structural and functional status of the small intestine. Following damage, necrosis and exfoliation of the intestinal mucosal cells, DAO translocation into the blood leads to an increase in DAO levels, suggesting destruction of the intestinal mucosal barrier and changes in intestinal permeability [28–30, 44, 54, 57]. Animal experiments and clinical trials have demonstrated that the DAO level is a sensitive marker for the early diagnosis of intestinal mucosal damage [12, 16, 43, 44]. Many bacteria in the gastrointestinal tract produce D-LA after metabolism. D-LA is difficult to absorb by the intestine, but in particular, in cases such as critical illness and severe stress, the intestinal mucosal barrier is injured, which leads to an obvious increase in intestinal permeability. Hence, D-LA in the intestine is highly produced and enters the blood. Moreover, mammals do not have a specific enzyme system that rapidly degrades D-LA. Therefore, D-LA will largely aggregate in the blood. Many bacteria in the gastrointestinal tract metabolically produce the common substance D-LA. D-LA is difficult to absorb by the intestinal tract, but under special circumstances such as critical patients and severe stress, damage to the intestinal mucosal barrier leads to a significant increase in intestinal permeability, and a large amount of D-LA in the intestinal tract is produced and enters the blood. In mammals, there is no specific enzyme system that can rapidly degrade D-LA, which ultimately leads to the accumulation of D-LA in the blood. An animal experiment revealed that Chinese medicine could alleviate intestinal injury and improve the structure of the intestinal mucosa epithelium, and the contents of serum DAO and D-LA in the treatment group were significantly lower than those in the model group [17, 23]. Consistently, studies on Xianglian pills also suggested that it could alleviate colonic injury, inflammation, and gut microbial dysbiosis in UC model rats and change microbial metabolite levels via the gut microbiota-succinate-Treg differentiation axis [21]. In this study, the downregulation of DAO and D-LA in the XG group demonstrated that XG could improve the intestinal barrier of cancer patients treated with immunotherapy. Assessment of intestinal barrier function in this study was limited by equipment and technical constraints, relying only on indirect serum markers. In future studies, we plan to include the lactulose/mannitol test to provide a more accurate and comprehensive evaluation of gut permeability. These findings are consistent with recent evidence that gut barrier integrity and microbiome composition strongly influence checkpoint inhibitor efficacy[7, 37].

In terms of immune-related biological indicators, this study revealed that XG can increase the B lymphocyte count in the blood of patients and reduce the interleukin-2 level in the serum. B lymphocytes can inhibit tumor growth through antibody production, antigen presentation, tumor tissue interactions and direct killing [25]. In the tumor environment, B cells can serve as local antigen-presenting cells to maintain the survival and proliferation of tumor-infiltrating T cells, thereby improving the efficacy of cancer immunotherapy [40]. For example, [34] reported that B-cell antigen-presenting molecules can stimulate the activation of CD4 + and CD8 + T cells and induce T-cell migration, thereby improving the overall survival rate of B16F1 melanoma patients, reducing tumor size and prolonging tumor appearance. IL-2 is the first interleukin approved for the treatment of renal cell carcinoma and metastatic melanoma [1]. It is synthesized mainly by T cells (especially CD4 + T cells) after being stimulated by antigens or mitogens. B cells, NK cells and monocyte–macrophages can also produce IL-2. IL-2 can promote B cells to proliferate and secrete antibodies [58], and B cells may express functional IL-2 receptors or proteins closely related to it [30]. Therefore, IL-2 and B cells are closely related and play important roles in immune regulation. Studies have shown that IL-2 has a positive effect on the treatment of multiple cancer types. Compared with PD-1 inhibition alone, intrapleural injection of IL-2 combined with a systemic PD-1 inhibitor can effectively inhibit the generation of malignant pleural effusion and improve the survival rate of mice [9]. Although the application of higher doses of IL-2 has a good curative effect, it has serious side effects [33]. The combination of IL-2 and CTLA-4 can not only improve the anticancer effect but also reduce the application dose of IL-2 to reduce the incidence of IL-2-related adverse events [50]. Notably, experimental studies on XG have shown that it modulates immune homeostasis by reducing proinflammatory cytokines and enhancing B-cell activity, which may partly explain its synergistic and attenuating effects on PD-1 inhibitor therapy[20]. In this study, XG may have a synergistic and attenuating effect on PD-1 inhibitor therapy by increasing peripheral B lymphocyte counts and reducing serum interleukin-2 levels[18].

In terms of prognosis-related biological indicators, this study revealed that XG can reduce the level of LDH in serum. Notably, lung cancer patients who received XG treatment exhibited a more marked reduction in LDH expression, with statistical significance observed (P < 0.033), suggesting a potentially stronger therapeutic effect in this subgroup. At present, LDH is considered one of the prognostic predictors of immunotherapy efficacy. LDH is negatively correlated with the prognosis of immunotherapy for malignant melanoma, lymphoma, lung cancer, renal cancer, gastric cancer, prostate cancer, nasopharyngeal cancer and other malignant tumors [8]. Therefore, the reduction in serum LDH levels by XG revealed its prognostic effect in immunotherapy-treated pancancer patients. Previous pharmacological studies have also confirmed that XG and its components (such as berberine) are not only associated with LDH expression, but can also enhance the cytotoxic activity of NK92-MI cells by reducing PD-L1 expression and suppressing tumor immune escape[48].

The results of the Cox regression analysis in this study revealed that D-LA and the percentage of B lymphocytes may be potential factors in the prognostic model of pancancer patients treated with immunotherapy. From the perspective of traditional Chinese medicine theory, the role of XG in “strengthening the spleen, regulating the intestine, and clearing heat-toxin” provides a mechanistic rationale for its broad-spectrum application across multiple diseases (“yi bing tong zhi”), which further supports its integration into modern cancer immunotherapy[22, 52]. XG plays an important role in improving the OS of cancer patients treated with immunotherapy.

However, this study has several limitations. First, the subgroup analysis was limited by the relatively small sample size, which may affect the robustness of the results. Future large-scale, multicenter studies with expanded cohorts are warranted. In addition, stratified randomization by cancer type and number of metastatic lesions will be adopted in future research to ensure balance between groups.

In conclusion, through a randomized, double-blind, controlled clinical study, synergistic and detoxifying effects of XG combined with PD-1 monoclonal antibodies in pancancer patients were observed, which not only improved the prognosis and effectively increased the 3-year OS rate of patients but also reduced the occurrence of immunotherapy-related adverse reactions with good safety for the body. The related mechanisms may be associated with improvements in the intestinal barrier, including a reduction in the content of DAO and D-LA in the serum, an increase in B lymphocytes and a decrease in IL-2. This suggests that the combination of XG and PD-1 inhibitors may enhance patient survival and reduce irAEs by improving gut barrier function and modulating the B cell/IL-2 pathway, with particularly notable benefits observed in gastrointestinal cancers. Moreover, D-LA and the percentage of B lymphocytes may be potential factors in the prognostic model of pancancer patients treated with immunotherapy. Although these are preliminary findings, large-scale, cancer-specific randomized controlled trials (RCTs) are recommended to further validate the efficacy and mechanisms. This study provides evidence of the synergistic and detoxification effects of XG on PD-1 inhibitors in the treatment of multiple cancers. In the future, traditional Chinese medicine may have broader prospects for improving the prognosis of cancer patients by improving the intestinal barrier and regulating the body's immunity.

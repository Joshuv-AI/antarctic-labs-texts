Investigation of adaptive interception systems for concentrate in spiral concentrators
Authors: Huizhong Liu, Fulong Deng, Qihua Zou, Yao Long, Xixi Liu, Ruoyun Zhang, Jian Ning
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractA spiral concentrator is an efficient gravity separation device that utilizes a flowing film. It is recognized for its environmentally friendly operation, absence of power requirements, ease of large-scale deployment, and low capital and operational costs. It has been widely applied in the beneficiation of iron, tin, titanium, tantalum‑niobium, and other metals, as well as non-metallic ores such as sulfur and coal. Currently, the control of concentrate grades in spiral concentrators relies heavily on manual intervention. Operators must continuously observe the mineral zoning and physically adjust the splitter position to align with the boundary between concentrate and tailings. This manual process is subjective, prone to delays, and can lead to fluctuations in the beneficiation index. To address this limitation, we propose an automated machine vision system that eliminates the need for human intervention by intelligently monitoring and adjusting the concentrate cut-point. The system employs an enhanced YOLOv5 algorithm to determine the separation point of the concentrate and actuates an automated collection mechanism. The novelty of this work lies in the integration of attention mechanisms (CAM and SAM), a small‑target detection layer, and an improved loss function (CIoU) into YOLOv5, specifically tailored for the challenging conditions of spiral concentrators where ore bands are often indistinct and dynamically changing. Experimental results show that the optimized YOLOv5 algorithm achieves a detection accuracy of approximately 90% and a speed of 63 frames per second. The adaptive interception system responds reliably with control accuracy exceeding 90%, meeting industrial requirements for both precision and speed. This system significantly enhances the automation and intelligence of spiral concentrator operations, contributing to more consistent product quality and reduced labor costs.Keywords: spiral concentrator, machine vision, YOLOv5, intelligent beneficiation, mineral processing, adaptive controlSubject terms: Mechanical engineering, Computer science

Introduction

Beneficiation is an industrial process for separating valuable minerals from ores. Gravity separation exploits differences in specific gravity among minerals using gravitational and centrifugal forces. The spiral concentrator, a classic gravity separation device, is widely used due to its low energy consumption, simple structure, high efficiency, and low construction cost1.

A spiral concentrator consists of a slurry distributor, a spiral chute, a product interceptor, a support structure, and a receiving hopper (Fig. 1). Separation occurs as the slurry flows spirally down the chute: heavy minerals settle near the inner edge, while light minerals are carried to the outer edge. This creates distinct bands of concentrate, middlings, and tailings. Traditionally, operators manually adjust a splitter at the bottom of the chute to separate these bands, a process that is subjective and slow2.

Structure of spiral concentrator.

Most research on spiral concentrators has focused on geometric design and fluid dynamics3–11, with limited attention to intelligent control. Some studies have used image processing to monitor the ore bands23,24, but these methods rely on traditional edge detection and struggle with the fuzzy, dynamic boundaries caused by varying feed conditions. To overcome these challenges, we introduce a deep learning-based approach using an improved YOLOv 5 algorithm.

YOLOv5 is a one-stage object detection framework known for its speed and accuracy. In this work, we develop three variants: YOLOv5-SE (Squeeze and Excitation), YOLOv5-CBAM (Convolutional Block Attention Module), and our proposed YOLOv5-CASM (Channel and Spatial Attention Module). All models are implemented in Python using the PyTorch framework. The key innovations of our approach are:

Furthermore, we design a servo‑actuated interception system and a PLC‑based control loop to automatically adjust the splitter based on the vision output. This integrated system represents a significant advancement over existing methods, which either lack automation or fail under realistic industrial conditions.

Conventional recognition algorithms for identifying these ambiguous and unstable areas are challenging to implement; therefore, employing deep learning techniques for their identification is highly effective.

Methods

Description of the problem

A spiral concentrator is a high-efficiency gravity separation device utilizing fluid film, characterized by its pollution-free operation, lack of power requirements, ease of large-scale configuration, and inexpensive construction and operational expenses. It has acquired several uses in the beneficiation processes of iron, tin, titanium, tantalum-niobium, and various metals, in addition to non-metallic ores such as sulfur and coal. Currently, the interception and grade control of the spiral concentrator’s concentrate are executed by workers who observe the mineral separation zone and adjust the parting block on the interceptor to ensure that the tip of the parting block aligns with the boundary between the concentrate and the middling ore, as illustrated in Fig. 2. The varying experience and technical proficiency of each worker complicate the assurance of correctness and real-time adjustments of the separation block, which can lead to fluctuations in the beneficiation index. This paper proposes a machine vision-based adaptive interception system for the concentrate of a spiral concentrator, aiming to automate the interception process and enhance the production’s automation and intelligence levels, replacing manual intervention.

On-site concentrate segmentation of spiral concentrator.

In optimal lighting conditions and with more pronounced physical attributes, such as mineral color, the mineral zoning characteristics are distinctly defined, and the delineation of essential features is more apparent, as illustrated in Fig. 3. In most mineral processing plants, the intricate production conditions and significant uncertainty regarding the impurities in the minerals complicate the delineation of the true ore zone. This results in ambiguous characteristics of the key ore zone and challenges in extraction, making it difficult to accurately and swiftly identify the optimal sorting point for the ore zone.

Design of the interception execution system

The dimensions of the interceptor hopper interface of the spiral concentrator are determined by the outflow dimensions of the spiral concentrator. The spiral surface incorporates baffle bars to assist in slurry diversion and concentrate quality control, while a central partition segregates the concentrate, middling ore, and tailings, enhancing mineral recovery. A ball screw base is welded to the front side for securing the ball screw. To augment the base’s strength, three ribs are uniformly welded onto it. A vertical plate with a small tip serves as a separator for the minerals, while the bottom features a diversion catch plate set at a specific angle to direct the respective minerals to the discharge port. A thorough analysis of the ore zone reveals that the concentrate zone is shorter while the medium ore zone is longer, necessitating the design of the catch plate’s left and right sides to be of varying lengths, as illustrated in Fig. 5. To prevent mineral splashing, the splitting plate incorporates a baffle plate to enhance the structural integrity of the splitting components. Additionally, the baffle plate features reinforced rib plates, which are ultimately secured by bolts and a ball screw connection via a connecting plate.

To augment the detection of small targets in the ambiguous regions of the ore zone, CAM and SAM attention channels, along with small target detection layers, have been incorporated, resulting in a reduction in FPS; however, all other metrics surpass those of other YOLOv5 algorithms and it remains sufficient for real-time control.

Conclusion

The adaptive interception control system for spiral concentrator concentrate conducives to improving the intelligence of spiral concentrators and upgrading related technologies and equipment.

The mineral product banding feature extraction approach, utilising mineral images from the spiral concentrator, mitigates the algorithm’s deficiency in attentional preference. It improves the detection accuracy of ambiguous mineral banding images, strengthens the ability to recognise small targets in indistinct regions, and elevates overall detection precision. The enhanced algorithm’s accuracy can attain about 90%, meeting the manufacturing requirements of the industry.

The servo actuator for concentrate interception in the spiral concentrator, guided by ore belt coordinates, utilises a servo motor to regulate the displacement of the lead screw, thereby controlling the position of the interceptor. The independently designed receiving hopper and interceptor of the spiral concentrator fulfil the precision and speed requirements for concentrate interception. Utilising the existing control model, the location data of the segmentation point relayed by the image server is transformed into control signals and sent to the actuator to achieve adaptive control of concentrate interception in the spiral concentrator. The control system and actuator are dependable and efficient, exhibiting rapid response times with control accuracy over 90%. thus satisfying typical operating conditions in trial production.

The system offers significant industrial benefits: it greatly reduces manual intervention, ensures consistent concentrate grade, reduces labor costs, and can be retrofitted to existing spirals. The technology is scalable to multi‑spiral banks and adaptable to other gravity separators.

This study was carried out at the semi-industrial scale using a single type of rare earth ore. Testing was limited to the feed size fraction of − 1 mm + 0.125 mm and one fixed geometric configuration of the spiral separator. Therefore, further verification is necessary for finer particle sizes and other spiral separator structural designs. The dataset, while representative, is limited to 2000 images from one lighting environment. Performance under extreme illumination changes or with other ore types (e.g., coal, chromite) has not yet been validated. The current system requires a fixed camera position; variations in camera angle or distance may affect accuracy. Additionally, the control system’s response time (0.015 s per image) is sufficient for the tested flow rates, but higher slurry velocities may require faster hardware.

Future endeavours must focus on augmenting the dataset and enhancing detection algorithms for delineating ore zone boundaries of various mineral types while also addressing the effects of variations in field illumination. Additionally, it is imperative to expedite the system’s response performance by minimising algorithmic complexity and maximising detection speed.

Numerical Calculation of Coulomb Corrections in Forward Elastic 𝒑 ↑ ​ 𝒑 \boldsymbol{p}^{\uparrow}\!\!\;\boldsymbol{p} and 𝒑 ↑ ​ 𝑨 \boldsymbol{p}^{\uparrow}\!\boldsymbol{A} Scattering
A. A. Poblaguev

Abstract
The analysis of RHIC hydrogen gas jet target polarimeter measurements of transverse analyzing powers A N ​ ( t ) A_{\text{N}}(t) in proton-nucleus scattering requires accurate Coulomb corrections to both spin-flip and non-flip amplitudes. These corrections must cover a wide range of nuclear charges Z Z and form factor slopes, with flexibility to vary form factors during data fitting. To avoid technically challenging calculations involving a small but finite fictitious photon mass, the Coulomb correction to the non-flip electromagnetic amplitude with an exponential form factor was related to the corresponding correction for the spin-flip amplitude. This approach allows soft photon contributions to all amplitudes, including those with non-exponential form factors, to be calculated in the massless photon limit using only analytical expressions and numerically stable integrals with nonsingular integrands and finite integration limits. In addition, an absorptive correction to the spin-flip electromagnetic amplitude, which plays a critical role in spin effects in forward polarized proton-nucleus scattering, was accurately evaluated.

Introduction

The Relativistic Heavy-Ion Collider (RHIC) Spin Program
Bunce et al. 2000
utilized the Polarized Atomic Hydrogen Gas Jet Target (HJET)
Zelenski et al. 2005
;
Poblaguev et al. 2020
to precisely determine the transverse polarization of proton beams at 100 and 255 GeV. As part of the polarization measurements, the elastic

) and double-spin (

0.0013<-t<0.018\,\text{GeV}^{2}

Poblaguev et al. 2019

The good performance of HJET in RHIC heavy-ion beams also enabled experimental evaluation of the proton-nucleus (
p^{\uparrow}A
) single-spin analyzing power,
A_{\text{N}}^{pA}(t)
, for several nuclei, as shown in Fig.

. These measurements were routinely carried out in a parasitic mode (i.e., without disturbing RHIC experiments) during heavy-ion runs.

In general, the elastic
p^{\uparrow}A
transverse analyzing power is given by

A_{\text{N}}(t)=\frac{2\,\text{Im}\left(F_{\text{sf}}F_{\text{nf}}^{*}\right)}{|F_{\text{nf}}|^{2}+|F_{\text{sf}}|^{2}},

(1)

where the non-flip (nf) and spin-flip (sf) amplitudes for forward elastic
p^{\uparrow}A
scattering can be written
Vanzha et al. 1972
as

\displaystyle F_{\text{nf}}(\boldsymbol{q})

\displaystyle=\frac{\sigma_{\text{tot}}}{4\pi}\left[(i+\rho)e^{-B_{N}q^{2}/2}-\frac{q_{c}^{2}}{q^{2}}e^{-B_{C}q^{2}/2}e^{i\delta_{C}}\right],

(2)

\displaystyle F_{\text{sf}}(\boldsymbol{q})

\displaystyle=\frac{\sigma_{\text{tot}}}{4\pi}\frac{\boldsymbol{n}\cdot\boldsymbol{q}}{m_{p}}\left[r_{5}e^{-B_{S}q^{2}/2}-\frac{\kappa_{p}}{2}\frac{q_{c}^{2}}{q^{2}}e^{-B_{M}q^{2}/2}e^{i\delta_{C}}\right].

(3)

Here,
\boldsymbol{q}
is the transverse momentum of the scattered proton, with
q^{2}\approx-t
;
q_{c}^{2}=-t_{c}=8\pi\alpha Z/\sigma_{\text{tot}}
, where
Z
is the nuclear charge;
\boldsymbol{n}
is a unit vector perpendicular to both the proton beam momentum and spin; and
m_{p}
is the proton mass. The parameters
\kappa_{p}=1.793
,
\sigma_{\text{tot}}
,
\rho
, and
r_{5}
represent, respectively, the proton anomalous magnetic moment, the total
pA
cross section, the real-to-imaginary ratio of the hadronic amplitude, and the hadronic spin-flip parameter. The hadronic and electromagnetic form factors are parameterized exponentially with slopes
B_{X}
, where
X
denotes one of the indices
N
,
S
,
C
, or
M
. In this work, it is assumed that all slopes
B_{X}
are
approximately
the same and for the proton-proton scattering
B_{X}^{pp}\!=\!8/\left(0.71\,\text{GeV}^{2}\right)
.

Three types of elastic proton-proton scattering: (C) electromagnetic, including multiphoton exchange; (N) bare hadronic; and (NC) combined hadronic and electromagnetic interactions.

Due to the long-range Coulomb forces (see Fig.
2
), both the hadronic and electromagnetic (non-flip) amplitudes acquire phase shifts,
\Phi_{N}(t)
and
\Phi_{C}(t)
, respectively. In Eqs. (
2
) and (
3
), this effect is accounted for by the phase difference
Cahn 1982

\displaystyle\delta_{C}(t)

\displaystyle=\Phi_{C}(t)-\Phi_{N}(t)

\displaystyle\approx-\alpha Z\left[\gamma-\ln{\frac{(B_{N}+B_{C})|t_{c}|}{2}}-\ln{\frac{t_{c}}{t}}\right],

(4)

known as the Coulomb phase.

For high-energy forward elastic proton-proton scattering, a compact expression for the transverse analyzing power
A_{\text{N}}(t)
is well known
Kopeliovich and Lapidus 1974
;
Buttimore et al. 1978
;
Buttimore et al. 1999
:

A_{\text{N}}(t)=\frac{\sqrt{-t}}{m_{p}}\frac{\left(\kappa_{p}-2\mathrm{Im}\,r_{5}\right)t_{c}/t-2\mathrm{Re}\,r_{5}}{(t_{c}/t)^{2}-2(\rho+\delta_{C})t_{c}/t+1}.

(5)

Although some small—but non-negligible for precise experimental data analysis—corrections were omitted in Eq. (
5
), the resulting analyzing power is in reasonably good agreement with measurements at RHIC energies.

Structurally, the analyzing power for
p^{\uparrow}A
scattering is the same as for
p^{\uparrow}p
scattering. However, due to the nuclear charge
Z
, the much larger Coulomb corrections and the significantly greater absorptive correction to the spin-flip electromagnetic amplitude
Kopeliovich et al. 2024
drastically alter the dependence of the proton-nucleus analyzing power on the momentum transfer squared
t
(as clearly seen in Fig.
1
).

A framework for the theoretical interpretation of experimentally determined proton-nucleus analyzing powers was developed in Ref.
Kopeliovich et al. 2024
. In this approach, the hadronic and electromagnetic
pA
amplitudes are first evaluated using the Glauber model
Glauber and Matthiae 1970
. Coulomb corrections, including those arising from magnetic photon exchange, are then computed within the eikonal approximation. The calculation proceeds schematically as follows: a Fourier transformation is applied to convert the Born amplitude into the eikonal phase; multiple-photon interactions are accounted for in impact parameter space; and finally, an inverse Fourier transformation returns the result to momentum space.

The primary goal of this paper is to provide clear guidance for the numerical calculation of Coulomb corrections, which are essential for completing the HJET
A_{\text{N}}^{pA}
data analysis. The estimates are based on the eikonal approach,
Franco 1973
;
Cahn 1982
, and the amplitudes are normalized according to the conventions of Ref.
Kopeliovich et al. 2024
. Technical details of the calculations are summarized in the Appendix.

It will be shown that Coulomb corrections to all relevant
p^{\uparrow}p
and
p^{\uparrow}A
amplitudes—including electromagnetic and hadronic, non-flip and spin-flip—can be computed with the required accuracy without introducing a fictitious photon mass. In addition, the effect of the soft magnetic photon correction to the hadronic non-flip amplitude—also interpretable as an absorptive correction to the electromagnetic spin-flip amplitude
Kopeliovich et al. 2024
—will be accurately evaluated.

II
Non-flip proton-proton scattering

In the eikonal approach used in Ref.
Kopeliovich et al. 2024
, the elastic non-flip
\mathit{pp}
amplitude is expressed via the profile function
i\Gamma(b)
in impact parameter space as

\displaystyle F_{\text{nf}}(q)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int{d^{2}\boldsymbol{b}\,e^{-i\boldsymbol{q}\!\cdot\!\boldsymbol{b}}\,i\Gamma(b)}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}{b\,db\,i\left[1-e^{i\chi_{N}(b)+i\chi_{C}(b)}\right]J_{0}(qb)}.

(6)

Using the notation

\gamma_{C}(b)=i\left(1-e^{i\chi_{C}(b)}\right),\quad\gamma_{N}(b)=i\left(1-e^{i\chi_{N}(b)}\right),

(7)

the profile function can be written as

\displaystyle i\Gamma(b)

\displaystyle=\gamma_{C}(b)+\gamma_{N}(b)\,e^{i\chi_{C}(b)}

(8)

\displaystyle=\gamma_{C}(b)+\gamma_{N}(b)+i\gamma_{N}(b)\gamma_{C}(b),

(9)

representing a sum of three terms corresponding to graphs (C), (N), and (NC), respectively, in Fig.
2
.

The eikonal phases
\chi_{C}(b)
and
\chi_{N}(b)
are Fourier transforms of the corresponding electromagnetic and hadronic amplitudes in Born approximation:

f_{C}(q^{2})=\frac{-2\alpha}{q^{2}+\lambda^{2}}e^{-B_{C}q^{2}/2},

(10)

where a small fictitious photon mass
\lambda
is introduced to regularize the Fourier integral, and

f_{N}(q^{2})=(i+\rho)\frac{\sigma_{\text{tot}}}{4\pi}e^{-B_{N}q^{2}/2}.

(11)

Using Eqs. (
85
) and (
70
), the eikonal phases can be written in analytic form:

\displaystyle\chi_{C}(b)

\displaystyle=-2\alpha\,\hat{\chi}_{C}\left(b\sqrt{2/B_{C}},B_{C}\lambda^{2}/2\right),

(12)

\displaystyle\gamma_{N}(b)

\displaystyle=(i+\rho)\frac{\sigma_{\text{tot}}}{4\pi B_{N}}e^{-b^{2}/2B_{N}}.

(13)

Since the Fourier transformation is applied directly to the experimentally determined hadronic amplitude (
11
), the result in Eq. (
13
) is the eikonal amplitude
\gamma_{N}(b)
, rather than the eikonal phase
\chi_{N}(b)
.

Relatively normalized functions
\chi_{C}(b)
(for
\tilde{\lambda}^{2}\!=\!10^{-10}
) and
\gamma_{N}(b)
are shown in Fig.
3
. Notably,
\gamma_{N}(b)
is significant only within a limited range
\tilde{b}\!\lesssim\!10
, while the range of
\chi_{C}(b)
,
<\!40/\tilde{\lambda}
, is inversely proportional to the photon mass and may be very large.

II.1
Coulomb correction in case of exponential form factor

The Coulomb-corrected amplitudes,
f_{C}^{\gamma}(q^{2})
and
f_{N}^{\gamma}(q^{2})
, can be calculated via the inverse Fourier transform of
\gamma_{C}(b)
and
\gamma_{N}(b)\exp{(i\chi_{C}(b)}
, respectively. Following the explanation in Appendix
C
,

\displaystyle f_{C}^{\gamma}(q^{2})

\displaystyle=e^{-i\Delta}\int_{0}^{\infty}b\,db\,i\left[1-e^{\chi_{C}(b)}\right]J_{0}(bq),

\displaystyle=f_{C}(q^{2})\times\mathcal{F}_{C}(q^{2}),

(14)

\displaystyle f_{N}^{\gamma}(q^{2})

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}b\,db\,\gamma_{N}(b)\,e^{i\chi^{\prime}_{C}(b)}J_{0}(bq),

\displaystyle=f_{N}(q^{2})\times\mathcal{F}_{N}(q^{2}),

(15)

where
\Delta
and
\chi^{\prime}_{C}(b)
are defined in Eqs. (
90
) and (
88
), respectively.

For
\mathit{pp}
scattering, Coulomb corrections can be approximated by Coulomb phases:

\mathcal{F}_{C}(q^{2})\approx e^{i\Phi_{C}(q^{2})},\qquad\mathcal{F}_{N}(q^{2})\approx e^{i\Phi_{N}(q^{2})}.

(16)

In Ref.
Kopeliovich and Tarasov 2001
, these phases were calculated analytically to leading order in
\alpha
. After adjusting for the phase shift
\Delta
used in the present context, the results of
Kopeliovich and Tarasov 2001
can be written as

\displaystyle\Phi_{N}^{\mathrm{KT}}(q^{2})/\alpha

\displaystyle=\ln{\tilde{q}^{2}}+\ln{\beta^{2}}-\mathrm{Ei}\left(\tilde{q}^{2}\frac{\beta^{2}}{1+\beta}\right),

(17)

\displaystyle\Phi_{C}^{\mathrm{KT}}(q^{2})/\alpha

\displaystyle=\ln{\tilde{q}^{2}}-\mathrm{Ei}\left(\tilde{q}^{2}/2\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+e^{\tilde{q}^{2}}\left[2E_{1}\left(\tilde{q}^{2}\right)-E_{1}\left(\tilde{q}^{2}/2\right)\right],

(18)

where
\tilde{q}^{2}\!=\!B_{C}q^{2}/2
and
\beta\!=\!B_{N}/B_{C}
.

Since
J_{0}(x)
is an oscillatory function with a half-period of approximately
\pi
, accurate numerical evaluation of the integral in Eq. (
14
) may require billions of sampling points (for
\tilde{\lambda}^{2}=10^{-10}
), making the computation time-consuming. However,
\mathcal{F}_{C}(\tilde{q}^{2})
, expressed in terms of the dimensionless variable
\tilde{q}
, is a parameter-free function aside from its dependence on the arbitrarily chosen photon mass
\tilde{\lambda}^{2}
. Therefore, for any fixed value of
\tilde{\lambda}^{2}
, it is sufficient to tabulate
\mathcal{F}_{C}(\tilde{q}^{2})
once, with the desired precision, to accelerate subsequent calculations.

The Coulomb correction to the hadronic amplitude (
15
) involves only a
simple integration
, meaning that the integration range is naturally limited (see the
b
-dependence of
\gamma_{N}(b)
in Fig.
3
), and the integrand contains no singularities.

The resulting Coulomb phase
\Phi_{N}(q^{2})
is in approximate agreement with the analytical result
\Phi_{N}^{\mathrm{KT}}(q^{2})
:

\left|\Phi_{N}(q^{2})-\Phi_{N}^{\mathrm{KT}}(q^{2})\right|<8\times 10^{-9}.

(19)

However, if the numerical calculation is also performed in the leading-order
\alpha
approximation,

\Phi_{N}^{\text{LO}}(q^{2})=\frac{\int db\,b\,\gamma_{N}(b)\,\chi^{\prime}_{C}(b)\,J_{0}(qb)}{\int db\,b\,\gamma_{N}(b)\,J_{0}(qb)},

(20)

the result matches
\Phi_{N}^{\mathrm{KT}}(q^{2})
exactly, within computational precision (
\sim\!10^{-15}
).

II.2
Coulomb correction in case of non-exponential form factor

In the more general case of a non-exponential form factor
F_{C}(q^{2})
, the form factor expression can be decomposed into exponential and non-exponential components:

\frac{F_{C}(q^{2})}{q^{2}}=\frac{e^{-B_{C}q^{2}/2}}{q^{2}}+\frac{F_{C}(q^{2})-e^{-B_{C}q^{2}/2}}{q^{2}},

(21)

where

B_{C}=\frac{-2\,d\ln{F_{C}(q^{2})}}{dq^{2}}\Big|_{q^{2}=0}.

(22)

Since the non-exponential correction to the amplitude does not have a singularity at
q^{2}\to 0
, the corresponding correction
\delta\chi_{C}(b)
to the eikonal phase can be calculated without introducing a photon mass:

\delta\chi_{C}(b)=\int_{0}^{\infty}{qdq\frac{F_{C}(q^{2})-e^{-B_{C}q^{2}/2}}{q^{2}}J_{0}(bq)}.

(23)

As an illustration,
\delta\chi_{C}(b)
was evaluated for the frequently used dipole form of the proton electric form factor
Chan et al. 1966

\left(1+q^{2}/\Lambda^{2}\right)^{-2}
, where
\Lambda^{2}=0.71\,\mathrm{GeV}^{2}
. In this case,

F_{C}(q^{2})=\left(1+q^{2}/\Lambda^{2}\right)^{-4},\quad\text{and}\quad B_{C}=8/\Lambda^{2}.

(24)

The calculated
\delta\chi_{C}(b)
is shown in Fig.
3
as a dashed line. At
\tilde{b}\approx 2
, the function
\delta\chi_{C}(b)
changes sign.

Thus, only
simple integration
is required to evaluate the effect of the non-exponential form factor in the Coulomb-corrected electromagnetic amplitude (in the massless photon approximation):

\displaystyle f_{C}^{\gamma}(q^{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{-B_{C}q^{2}/2}}{q^{2}}\mathcal{F}_{C}(q^{2})

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{\infty}b\,db\,i\left[1-e^{i\delta\chi_{C}(b)}\right]e^{i\chi^{\prime}_{C}(b)}J_{0}(bq).

(25)

Here,
\mathcal{F}_{C}(q^{2})
and
\chi^{\prime}_{C}(b)
correspond to the exponential form factor with slope
B_{C}
, as defined in Eqs. (
14
) and (
88
), respectively.

If hadronic form factor
F_{N}(q^{2})
(with slope
B_{N}
at
q^{2}\!\to\!0
), the Coulomb modified amplitude (
15
) should be corrected as

\displaystyle f_{N}^{\gamma}(q^{2})=f_{N}(q^{2})\mathcal{F}_{N}(q^{2})

\displaystyle\qquad-\int_{0}^{\infty}b\,db\,\gamma_{N}(b)\left[1-e^{i\delta\chi_{C}(b)}\right]e^{i\chi^{\prime}_{C}(b)}J_{0}(bq).

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{0}^{\infty}b\,db\,\delta\gamma_{N}(b)e^{i\delta\chi_{C}(b)}\,e^{i\chi^{\prime}_{C}(b)}J_{0}(bq),

(26)

where

\delta\chi_{N}(b)=\int_{0}^{\infty}{qdq\left[F_{N}(q^{2})-e^{-B_{N}q^{2}/2}\right]J_{0}(bq)}.

(27)

III
Spin-flip proton-proton scattering

In general, the single spin-flip amplitude for transversely polarized proton scattering contains azimuthal angle factors:
\cos{\varphi_{q}}=(\boldsymbol{n\cdot{q}})/q
in momentum space and
\cos{\varphi_{b}}=(\boldsymbol{n\cdot{b}})/b
in impact parameter space. As shown in Appendix
B
, these factors transform into each other under Fourier transformation:

\cos{\varphi_{q}}\to i\cos{\varphi_{b}}\to\cos{\varphi_{q}}.

(28)

For simplicity, we omit these angular factors in the expressions for the amplitudes. In particular, the hadronic spin-flip amplitude
f_{S}(q^{2})
and the electromagnetic spin-flip amplitude
f_{M}(q^{2})
are written as

\displaystyle f_{S}(q^{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{\sigma_{\mathrm{tot}}r_{5}}{4\pi m_{p}}e^{-B_{S}q^{2}/2},

(29)

\displaystyle f_{M}(q^{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{-\kappa_{p}\alpha}{m_{p}q}e^{-B_{M}q^{2}/2}.

(30)

However, it should be kept in mind that an additional angular factor may be required in cases involving multiple spin-flip amplitudes, as demonstrated in Eqs. (
56
) and (
57
).

Considering
p^{\uparrow}p
scattering in the eikonal model, both the electromagnetic
\chi_{M}(b)
and hadronic
\chi_{S}(b)
spin-flip phases must be included in the profile function:

\displaystyle i\Gamma

\displaystyle=i\left[1-e^{i\chi_{N}(b)+i\chi_{S}(b)+i\chi_{C}(b)+i\chi_{M}(b)}\right]

\displaystyle\to i\left[1-e^{i\chi_{C}(b)}\right]e^{-i\Delta}

\displaystyle+\left[\gamma_{N}+\gamma_{S}+\gamma_{M}+i\gamma_{N}\gamma_{M}+i\gamma_{S}\gamma_{M}\right]e^{i\chi^{\prime}_{C}(b)},

(31)

where the non-flip components
\chi_{C}(b)
and
\gamma_{N}(b)
were defined earlier. Based on the results derived in Appendix
B
, the corresponding spin-flip components take the form

\displaystyle\gamma_{S}(b)

\displaystyle=i\left[1-e^{i\chi_{N}(b)+i\chi_{S}(b)}\right]-\gamma_{N}(b)

\displaystyle=\frac{\sigma_{\mathrm{tot}}r_{5}}{4\pi m_{p}}\frac{be^{-b^{2}/2B_{S}}}{B_{S}^{2}},

(32)

\displaystyle\gamma_{M}(b)

\displaystyle=i\left[1-e^{i\chi_{M}(b)}\right]

\displaystyle\approx\chi_{M}(b)=\frac{-\alpha\kappa_{p}}{m_{p}}\frac{1-e^{-b^{2}/2B_{M}}}{b}.

(33)

The term
i\gamma_{S}(b)\gamma_{M}(b)
effectively alters the hadronic non-flip amplitude. However, due to its smallness—of order
\sim\alpha r_{5}q^{2}/m_{p}^{2}
relative to
\gamma_{N}
—it can be neglected.

The single spin-flip term
i\gamma_{N}(b)\gamma_{M}(b)
, which can be interpreted either as a magnetic photon correction to the hadronic non-flip amplitude or as an absorptive correction to the electromagnetic spin-flip amplitude, can become significant in proton-nucleus scattering involving large nuclear charge
Z
, such as in
p^{\uparrow}\mathrm{Au}
scattering. A more detailed discussion of this term is provided in Section
V
.

The Coulomb correction to the hadronic spin-flip amplitude,

\displaystyle f_{S}^{\gamma}(q^{2})

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}b^{2}\,db\,\gamma_{S}(b)e^{i\chi^{\prime}_{C}(b)}J_{1}(bq)

\displaystyle=f_{S}(q^{2})\times\mathcal{F}_{S}(q^{2}),

(34)

meets the criterion of
simple integration
and can thus be calculated straightforwardly. In case of non-exponential form factor
F_{S}(q^{2})
,
\gamma_{S}(b)
is altered by

\delta\gamma_{S}(b)=\int_{0}^{\infty}{q^{2}dq\left[F_{S}(q^{2})-e^{-B_{S}q^{2}/2}\right]J_{1}(bq)}

(35)

and

\displaystyle f_{S}^{\gamma}(q^{2})=f_{S}(q^{2})\mathcal{F}_{S}(q^{2})

\displaystyle\qquad-\int_{0}^{\infty}b^{2}\,db\,\gamma_{S}(b)\left[1-e^{i\delta\chi_{C}(b)}\right]e^{i\chi^{\prime}_{C}(b)}J_{1}(bq).

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{0}^{\infty}b^{2}\,db\,\delta\gamma_{S}(b)e^{i\delta\chi_{C}(b)}\,e^{i\chi^{\prime}_{C}(b)}J_{1}(bq),

(36)

A potential difficulty in calculating the Coulomb correction to the electromagnetic spin-flip amplitude,

\displaystyle f_{M}^{\gamma}(q^{2})

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}db\,\chi_{M}(b)e^{i\chi^{\prime}_{C}(b)}J_{1}(bq)

\displaystyle=f_{M}(q^{2})\times\mathcal{F}_{M}(q^{2}),

(37)

is the slow,
\sim 1/b
, decrease of
\chi_{M}(b)
at large
b
, which requires numerical integration over a wide range of
b
. Nevertheless, a compact expression allowing precise calculation of the Coulomb correction to the magnetic amplitude was derived in Appendix
D
:

\mathcal{F}_{M}(q^{2})=\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}(B_{M}q^{2}/2).

(38)

In Ref.
Buttimore et al. 1978
, it was found that the Coulomb phase is insensitive to the spin of the particles involved and is therefore identical across all helicity amplitudes. In particular, one may expect that

\mathcal{F}_{M}(q^{2})=\mathcal{F}_{C}(q^{2}).

(41)

Using the analytical calculation
Kopeliovich and Tarasov 2001
of the Coulomb phase
\Phi_{C}^{\text{LO}}(q^{2})
in the leading-order approximation, one can readily show
Poblaguev 2022
that
\Phi_{M}^{\text{LO}}(q^{2})=\Phi_{C}^{\text{LO}}(q^{2})
. Furthermore, our numerical calculation of the spin-flip phase
\Phi_{M}^{\text{LO}}(q^{2})
, given by Eq. (
97
), exactly matches the non-flip phase
\Phi_{C}^{\text{KT}}(q^{2})
.

For all orders in
\alpha
, the numerically computed Coulomb corrections for the spin-flip and non-flip amplitudes are compared in Fig.
5
. Within the momentum transfer squared range relevant to HJET measurements, the consistency is better than
10^{-9}
. A more conservative conclusion is that
\mathcal{F}_{M}(q^{2})
and
\mathcal{F}_{C}(q^{2})
are identical within the CNI region to a precision of
10^{-8}
. Although a small
q^{2}
-dependent discrepancy is observed between the non-flip and spin-flip Coulomb corrections, it has not been conclusively shown that this difference is not due to systematic errors in the numerical calculation of
\mathcal{F}_{C}(q^{2})
.

Thus, given the actual experimental accuracy of the HJET measurements, the equality expressed in Eq. (
41
) is well justified.

V
The absorptive correction

Following Ref.
Kopeliovich et al. 2024
, the term
i\gamma_{N}(b)\gamma_{M}(b)
in Eq. (
31
) is referred to as the absorptive correction. In the case of exponential form factors, this correction can be expressed as the difference between two spin-flip electromagnetic amplitudes:

\displaystyle\gamma_{\text{NM}}(b)

\displaystyle=i\gamma_{N}(b)\gamma_{M}(b)

\displaystyle=C_{\text{NM}}\times\left[\hat{\gamma}_{M}(b,B_{\text{NM}})-\hat{\gamma}_{M}(b,B_{N})\right],

(42)

where

\displaystyle C_{\text{NM}}

\displaystyle=(1-i\rho)\frac{\sigma_{\text{tot}}}{4\pi B_{N}}\frac{\alpha Z\kappa_{p}}{m_{p}},

(43)

\displaystyle B_{\text{NM}}

\displaystyle=\frac{B_{N}B_{M}}{B+B_{M}}.

(44)

The corresponding effective amplitude in momentum space, including Coulomb corrections, is given by

\displaystyle f_{\text{NM}}^{\gamma}(q)=C_{\text{NM}}\,q^{-1}\times

\displaystyle\Big[e^{-B_{\text{NM}}q^{2}/2}\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}\left(B_{\text{NM}}q^{2}/2\right)

\displaystyle-e^{-B_{N}q^{2}/2}\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}\left(B_{N}q^{2}/2\right)\Big]\!.

(45)

In case of non-exponential form factors
F_{N}(q^{2})
and
F_{M}(q^{2})
,

\delta\gamma_{\text{NM}}(b)=C_{\text{NM}}\times\left[\gamma_{N}\delta\gamma_{M}+\delta\gamma_{N}\gamma_{M}+\delta\gamma_{N}\delta\gamma_{M}\right],

(46)

where
\delta\gamma_{N}(b)
and
\delta\gamma_{M}(b)
are defined in Eqs. (
27
) and (
39
). Consequently, non-exponential corrections to
f_{\text{NM}}^{\gamma}(q^{2})
can be calculated by substitution of
\gamma_{\text{NM}}(b)
and
\delta\gamma_{\text{NM}}(b)
to Eq. (
40
)

To evaluate the significance of the absorptive correction, a simplified case with
B_{N}=B_{M}
and
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}(q^{2})=1
can be considered. In this scenario, the correction can be interpreted either as an effective modification of the proton’s anomalous magnetic moment,

\kappa_{p}\to\kappa_{p}^{\text{eff}}=\kappa_{p}\times\left(1-\frac{\alpha Z}{2}\frac{t}{t_{c}}\right),

(47)

or as a shift in the hadronic spin-flip amplitude:

r_{5}\to r_{5}^{\text{eff}}=r_{5}+\frac{\alpha Z\kappa_{p}}{4}.

(48)

For proton-proton scattering (
Z=1
), the correction is small but not negligible
Poblaguev 2022

\mathrm{Re}\,r_{5}^{pp}\to\mathrm{Re}\,r_{5}^{pp}+0.003.

(49)

However, in proton-gold scattering (
Z=79

VII
Summary

The possibility of numerically calculating Coulomb corrections to the non-flip and spin-flip amplitudes in forward elastic scattering of transversely polarized protons off proton or nuclear targets was investigated within the eikonal framework. It was assumed that all relevant form factors—electromagnetic non-flip
F_{C}(t)
and spin-flip
F_{M}(t)
, and hadronic non-flip
F_{N}(t)
and spin-flip
F_{S}(t)
—are known in the Born approximation.

For computational convenience, it is useful to separate the exponential and non-exponential components of the form factors, as done in Eqs. (
21
) and (
22
).

For exponential form factors, analytical expressions for the corresponding eikonal phases were derived. Inverse Fourier transformation—including convolution with
\exp[i\chi_{C}(b,\lambda^{2})]
, the term describing multiple soft photon exchange—yields the Coulomb-corrected amplitudes for
p^{\uparrow}p
and
p^{\uparrow}A
scattering.

To avoid infrared divergences, the Coulomb phase
\chi_{C}(b,\lambda^{2})
was calculated using a fictitious photon mass
\lambda
. Reducing
\lambda
improves the accuracy of the Coulomb correction
\mathcal{F}_{C}(q^{2},\lambda^{2})
; however, very small values of
\lambda
require large integration ranges, which increases computation time and may also introduce numerical errors due to the limitations of floating-point arithmetic (even in double precision).

In contrast, the Coulomb correction
\mathcal{F}_{M}(q^{2})
[Eq. (
38
)] to the spin-flip electromagnetic amplitude can be computed directly in the massless limit
\lambda=0
. In this case,
\chi_{C}(b,\lambda^{2})
is replaced by the compact, modified phase
\chi^{\prime}_{C}(b)
[Eq. (
88
)], and the numerical evaluation of
\mathcal{F}_{M}(q^{2})
reduces to a
simple integration
(
93
).

Coulomb corrections to the non-flip and spin-flip electromagnetic amplitudes are expected
Buttimore et al. 1978
to be identical, which was confirmed by numerical calculations with accuracy better than
10^{-7}
. Thus,
\mathcal{F}_{C}(q^{2},\lambda^{2}\!\to\!0)
can be well approximated as

\mathcal{F}_{C}(q^{2})=\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}(B_{C}q^{2}/2),

(51)

calculated using the exponential non-flip form factor.

This approach allows for accurate calculation of Coulomb corrections to all exponential amplitudes in the
\lambda\to 0
limit, i.e., using the modified Coulomb phase
\chi^{\prime}_{C}(b)
. Corrections due to non-exponential components can also be efficiently incorporated, as demonstrated in Eqs. (
25
), (
26
), (
36
), and (
40
).

In addition to Coulomb corrections, the scattering amplitude may also be influenced by soft magnetic photon exchange. This introduces a correction to the hadronic non-flip amplitude, generating an effective spin-flip amplitude
f_{\text{NM}}(q^{2})
. For large
Z
, this contribution may exceed the “standard” spin-flip amplitudes
f_{M}(q^{2})
and
f_{S}(q^{2})
, and must therefore be carefully accounted for in data analysis. As shown in Section
V
,
f_{\text{NM}}(q^{2})
can be approximated by the difference of two electromagnetic spin-flip amplitudes, making it amenable to precise calculation—including Coulomb corrections—within the eikonal model.

For heavy nuclei (large
Z
), multiple soft magnetic photon exchanges may also need to be considered, depending on the required precision of analyzing power measurements. The necessity of such corrections was evaluated in Section
VI
.

Appendix A
Mathematical Relations Used

A typical integral used in the Fourier transformation within the eikonal approach is

f(\boldsymbol{y})=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int d^{2}x\,e^{\pm i\,\boldsymbol{x}\cdot\boldsymbol{y}}(\boldsymbol{n}\cdot\boldsymbol{x})^{k}x^{p}e^{-ax^{2}},

(52)

where
k\!\geq\!0
and
p
are integers, and
\boldsymbol{n}
is a unit vector in the
xy
-plane.

Using the relations
\boldsymbol{x}\!\cdot\!\boldsymbol{y}=xy\cos{\varphi}
and

\boldsymbol{n}\!\cdot\!\boldsymbol{y}=y\cos{\varphi_{y}}
, we obtain

\frac{\boldsymbol{n}\cdot\boldsymbol{x}}{x}=\cos{\varphi_{x}}=\cos{\varphi}\cos{\varphi_{y}}+\sin{\varphi}\sin{\varphi_{y}}.

(53)

Employing the integral representation of Bessel functions
Abramowitz and Stegun 1964
,

J_{n}(z)=\frac{i^{-n}}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}e^{iz\cos{\varphi}}\cos{(n\varphi)}\,d\varphi,

(54)

the angular integration in Eq. (
52
) can be performed explicitly for
k=0
and
k=1
:

\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{d\varphi}{2\pi}e^{\pm ixy\cos{\varphi}}\begin{cases}1\\
\cos{\varphi_{x}}\end{cases}=\begin{cases}J_{0}(xy)\\
\pm i\cos{\varphi_{y}}\,J_{1}(xy)\end{cases}.

(55)

For higher powers, such as
k=2
and
k=3
, the angular integrals can still be reduced using the results in Eq. (
55
). To achieve this, we neglect terms odd in
\varphi
(which do not contribute to the amplitudes of interest), and retain only those relevant to the non-flip and spin-flip components. This leads to the following replacements:

\displaystyle\cos^{2}{\varphi_{x}}

\displaystyle\to\cos^{2}{\varphi}\cos^{2}{\varphi_{y}}+\sin^{2}{\varphi}\sin^{2}{\varphi_{y}}

\displaystyle\to\frac{1+\cos{2\varphi_{y}}}{2}\cos^{2}{\varphi}+\frac{1-\cos{2\varphi_{y}}}{2}\sin^{2}{\varphi}

\displaystyle\to\frac{1}{2},

(56)

\displaystyle\cos^{3}{\varphi_{x}}

\displaystyle\to\cos^{3}{\varphi_{y}}\cos^{3}{\varphi}+3(\cos{\varphi_{y}}\!-\!\cos^{3}{\varphi_{y}})\cos{\varphi}\sin^{2}{\varphi}

\displaystyle\to\frac{3}{4}\cos{\varphi_{y}}\cos{\varphi}.

(57)

Integration over
x
can be performed using the following definite integral
Gradshteyn et al. 2007
:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}dx\,x^{\mu}e^{-\alpha x^{2}}J_{\nu}(xy)=

\displaystyle\quad\frac{y^{\nu}\,\Gamma\left(\frac{\nu+\mu+1}{2}\right)}{2^{\nu+1}\alpha^{\frac{\nu+\mu+1}{2}}\Gamma(\nu+1)}\,{}_{1}F_{1}\left(\frac{\nu+\mu+1}{2};\nu+1;-\frac{y^{2}}{4\alpha}\right),

(58)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\left[\mathrm{Re}\,\alpha>0,\quad\mathrm{Re}(\mu+\nu)>-1\right],

where

\displaystyle{}_{1}F_{1}(a;b;z)

\displaystyle=1+\frac{a}{b}\frac{z}{1!}+\frac{a(a+1)}{b(b+1)}\frac{z^{2}}{2!}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{a(a+1)(a+2)}{b(b+1)(b+2)}\frac{z^{3}}{3!}+\dots

(59)

is the confluent hypergeometric function. Notably,

{}_{1}F_{1}(a;a;z)=e^{z}.

(60)

Using the series expansion in Eq. (
59
), one can show that

{}_{1}F_{1}(1;2;z)=\frac{e^{z}-1}{z}.

(61)

In addition to Bessel functions of the first kind and the confluent hypergeometric function, the calculation of Coulomb corrections involves other special functions such as the modified Bessel functions of the first and second kinds,
I_{0}(z)
and
K_{0}(z)
, the gamma function
\Gamma(z)
, and exponential integrals
\mathrm{Ei}(x)
and
E_{1}(z)
. Numerical evaluations of these special functions were performed using the GNU Scientific Library (GSL)
Galassi et al. 2009
.

Some of the definite integrals and expansions used are
Gradshteyn et al. 2007
;
Abramowitz and Stegun 1964
:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}dx\,\ln{x}\,J_{1}(ax)=-\frac{1}{a}\left[\ln{\left(\frac{a}{2}\right)}+\gamma\right],

(62)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}dx\,x^{\mu}J_{\nu}(xy)=\frac{2^{\mu}}{y^{\mu+1}}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{1+\nu+\mu}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{1+\nu-\mu}{2}\right)},

(63)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\left[-\mathrm{Re}\,\nu-1<\mathrm{Re}\,\mu<\frac{1}{2},\quad y>0\right],

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ei}(x)=-\int_{x}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-t}}{t}\,dt,\qquad[x\neq 0],

(64)

\displaystyle E_{1}(z)=\int_{z}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-t}}{t}\,dt,\qquad\left|\mathrm{Arg}(z)\right|<\pi,

(65)

\displaystyle K_{0}(ak)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{xJ_{0}(ax)}{x^{2}+k^{2}}\,dx,\quad[a\!>\!0,\ \mathrm{Re}(k)\!>\!0].

(66)

\displaystyle J_{n}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{z}{2}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}{\frac{(-z^{2}/4)^{k}}{k!(k+n)!}},

(67)

\displaystyle I_{0}(z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{2k}}{(2^{k}k!)^{2}},

(68)

\displaystyle E_{1}(z)

\displaystyle=-\gamma-\ln{z}-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}z^{n}}{nn!},

(69)

where
\gamma=0.5772\dots
is the Euler–Mascheroni constant.

For exponential form factors (expressed in terms of
q^{2}
), the eikonal phases can be evaluated analytically or with minimal numerical effort. Using the definite integrals discussed in Appendix

, we obtain the following expressions.

Hadronic non-flip amplitude:

\displaystyle\hat{\gamma}_{N}(b,B_{N})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int d^{2}\boldsymbol{q}\,e^{i\boldsymbol{q}\cdot\boldsymbol{b}}\,e^{-B_{N}q^{2}/2}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}dq\,q\,e^{-B_{N}q^{2}/2}J_{0}(qb)

\displaystyle=B_{N}^{-1}\,{}_{1}\!F_{1}(1,1,-b^{2}/2B_{N})

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{-b^{2}/2B_{N}}}{B_{N}}.

(70)

Hadronic spin-flip amplitude:

\displaystyle\hat{\gamma}_{S}(\boldsymbol{b},B_{S})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int d^{2}\boldsymbol{q}\,e^{i\boldsymbol{q}\cdot\boldsymbol{b}}\,\cos{\varphi_{q}}\,q\,e^{-B_{S}q^{2}/2}

\displaystyle=i\cos{\varphi_{b}}\int_{0}^{\infty}dq\,q^{2}\,e^{-B_{S}q^{2}/2}\,J_{1}(bq)

\displaystyle=i\cos{\varphi_{b}}\,B_{S}^{-2}\,{}_{1}\!F_{1}(2,2,-b^{2}/2B_{S})

\displaystyle=i\cos{\varphi_{b}}\,\frac{b\,e^{-b^{2}/2B_{S}}}{B_{S}^{2}},

(71)

Electromagnetic spin-flip amplitude:

\displaystyle\hat{\gamma}_{M}(\boldsymbol{b},B_{M})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int d^{2}\boldsymbol{q}\,e^{i\boldsymbol{q}\cdot\boldsymbol{b}}\,\frac{\cos{\varphi_{q}}}{q}\,e^{-B_{M}q^{2}/2}

\displaystyle=i\cos{\varphi_{b}}\int_{0}^{\infty}dq\,e^{-B_{M}q^{2}/2}\,J_{1}(bq)

\displaystyle=i\cos{\varphi_{b}}\,(2B_{M})^{-1}\,{}_{1}\!F_{1}(1,2,-b^{2}/2B_{M})

\displaystyle=i\cos{\varphi_{b}}\,\frac{1-e^{-b^{2}/2B_{M}}}{b}.

(72)

Appendix C
Modified Eikonal Phase for the Coulomb Amplitude

A typical expression for the Coulomb-corrected amplitude
f_{X}^{\gamma}(q^{2})
is given by

\displaystyle f_{X}^{\gamma}(q^{2})

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}b\,db\,\gamma_{X}(b)\,e^{i\chi_{C}(b,\lambda^{2})}J_{0,1}(bq)

\displaystyle=f_{X}(q^{2})\mathcal{F}_{X}(q^{2})\approx f_{X}(q^{2})\,e^{i\Phi_{X}(q^{2},\lambda^{2})},

(86)

where
X
denotes one of the indices
N
,
S
, or
M
. The Bessel function
J_{0}(bq)
is used for the non-flip amplitude (
X=N
), while
J_{1}(bq)
is used for the spin-flip amplitudes (
X=S,M
). The eikonal phase
\chi_{C}(b,\lambda^{2})
is derived from the reduced phase (
85
) by multiplying by the Coulomb coupling:

\chi_{C}(b,\lambda^{2})=-2\alpha Z\,\hat{\chi}_{C}\left(b\sqrt{2/B_{C}},\lambda\sqrt{B_{C}/2}\right).

(87)

Since adding a constant to
\chi_{C}(b,\lambda^{2})
uniformly shifts all Coulomb phases
\Phi_{X}
, the non-vanishing
\lambda^{2}
dependence of
\Phi_{X}(q^{2},\lambda^{2})
as
\lambda\to 0
can be eliminated by subtracting
\hat{\chi}_{C}(0,\tilde{\lambda}^{2})
from
\hat{\chi}_{C}(\tilde{b},\tilde{\lambda}^{2})

Poblaguev 2024
. Consequently, replacing
\tilde{\lambda}\!\to 0
in the corrected phase, we obtain the modified eikonal phase in the massless photon limit:

The Coulomb correction factor

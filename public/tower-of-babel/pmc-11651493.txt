Relationship between neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio, monocyte-to-lymphocyte ratio, platelet-to-lymphocyte ratio and osteoporosis in postmenopausal type 2 diabetic patients: A retrospective study
Authors: Yuan Xue, Weichao Bao, Wenying Huang, Xin Zou, Ying Guo
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR), monocyte-to-lymphocyte ratio (MLR), platelet-to-lymphocyte ratio (PLR) are newly discovered inflammatory markers in recent years. To explore the relationship between NLR, MLR, PLR, and bone mineral density (BMD), as well as osteoporosis (OP), in postmenopausal patients with type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM). 320 postmenopausal patients with T2DM who were admitted to the Department of Endocrinology and Metabolic Diseases of our hospital from January 2022 to January 2024 for treatment were included in this retrospective study. The patients were divided into 3 groups according to the BMD: normal bone mass group (n = 109), osteopenia group (n = 99), and OP group (n = 112). Clinical data and blood laboratory parameters were collected from the patients. The NLR, MLR, and PLR were calculated. Statistically significant differences were found in the comparison of NLR, MLR, and PLR among the 3 groups of patients (P < .001). The OP group had higher NLR, MLR, and PLR than the normal bone mass group and the osteopenia group. NLR, MLR, and PLR were negatively correlated with BMD at the lumbar spine (L1-L4), left femoral neck, and left hip, respectively. Analysis revealed that elevated levels of NLR and PLR were independent risk factors for OP in postmenopausal patients. The results of the receiver operating characteristic curve analysis indicated that the area under the curve (AUC) for NLR was higher than that of PLR. Combined use of NLR and PLR resulting in an improved AUC of 0.904. Neutrophils produce mediators such as interferon-gamma (IFN-γ), interleukin-6 (IL-6), interleukin-1 (IL-1), and receptor activator of nuclear factor kappa-B ligand (RANKL), which increase the number and activity of osteoclasts. Peripheral blood monocytes play a vital role in osteoclastogenesis as osteoclast precursors. The BMD of postmenopausal T2DM patients is negatively correlated with NLR, MLR, and PLR. NLR and PLR can both serve as predictive indicators for the occurrence of OP in postmenopausal T2DM patients.

1. Introduction

Osteoporosis (OP) is a chronic skeletal disorder that is characterized by a reduction in bone mass, an impairment of bone microarchitecture, and an increase in bone fragility. Bone remodeling is a continuous process that occurs throughout an individual’s lifespan, serving to maintain skeletal integrity. Osteoclasts, osteoblasts, and osteocytes are the 3 main players in bone remodeling.[1] In the process of bone remodeling, bone resorption by osteoclasts and bone formation by osteoblasts are always in relative balance to ensure that bone can continuously repair itself, and at the same time, together with osteocytes, they jointly regulate the process of bone remodeling in order to maintain the homeostasis and strength of bone minerals. However, when bone remodeling is out of balance, the risk of fracture increases. This significantly elevates the risk of fractures.[2] Fractures are the most serious complication of surgery, leading to disability, reduced quality of life and increased economic burden. Type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) is a common chronic disease in the elderly population, and individuals with T2DM are more prone to develop OP.[3] The sharp decline in estrogen levels in postmenopausal women accelerates bone loss,[4] making it one of the primary causes of OP. Therefore, postmenopausal women with T2DM are considered a high-risk group for OP. Previous studies have found a strong association between long-term inflammatory states and the development of OP.[5] In recent years, neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR), monocyte-to-lymphocyte ratio (MLR), and platelet-to-lymphocyte ratio (PLR) have been considered as new indicators reflecting the severity of systemic inflammation.[6–9] This study aims to elucidate the relationship between NLR, MLR, and PLR with bone mineral density (BMD) in postmenopausal women with T2DM and to evaluate their efficacy as biomarkers for predicting the risk of OP, thus providing potential new tools for clinical prediction and intervention of OP. In addition, this study emphasizes the central role of systemic inflammation in bone health and disease, providing important scientific evidence for future research directions and treatment strategies.

2. Materials and methods

2.1. Patient characteristics

This was a retrospective study. Data were collected from women (n = 320) who were admitted to the Department of Endocrinology and Metabolic Diseases at Affiliated Hospital of Shandong Second Medical University from January 2022 to January 2024 (Fig. 1). The inclusion criteria were as follows: conformity with the T2DM diagnostic criteria recommended by the World Health Organization Diabetes Expert Committee in 1999; menopausal for more than 1 year; all patients have undergone comprehensive DXA (dual energy X-ray absorptiometry) screening; no missing clinical data. The exclusion criteria were as follows: patients with other types of diabetes mellitus (DM) or acute complications of DM; presence of other endocrine system disorders; acute or chronic infectious diseases; hematological disorders; cancer; autoimmune diseases; severe hepatic or renal diseases; non-natural menopause; long-term use of medications affecting bone metabolism, such as corticosteroids, calcium supplements, vitamin D, sex hormones, and bisphosphonates. This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Affiliated Hospital of Shandong Second Medical University (approval number: wyfy-2024-ky-103). As this is a retrospective study, the Ethics Committee waived the requirement to obtain informed consent from patients.

Flow chart on selection of patients. DM = diabetes mellitus, DXA = dual energy X-ray absorptiometry, T2DM = type 2 diabetes mellitus.

2.2. Biochemical analysis and clinical assessment

In this study, we collected basic information from the patients, including age, menopausal duration, duration of DM, and body mass index (BMI). After the patients were fasted for 10 hours during their hospitalization, peripheral venous blood was drawn on an empty stomach the next morning, and the nurse placed the blood samples in an incubator at 2~8°C. The blood samples were sent by the transporter to the hospital’s Laboratory Department for immediate laboratory testing. Parameters assessed included total cholesterol (TC), triglyceride (TG), low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C), high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C), and blood calcium (Ca), all determined by colorimetric methods. Glycosylated hemoglobin A1c (HbA1c) was measured by high-performance liquid chromatography, and fasting blood glucose (FBG) was determined by the glucose oxidase method. An enzymatic kinetic assay for alkaline phosphatase (ALP) was conducted. Serum parathyroid hormone (PTH) and 25-hydroxy vitamin D (25(OH)D) were both quantified by electrochemiluminescence immunoassay kits (Roche, China). All testing procedures strictly followed the manufacturer’s recommendations. Routine blood counts were performed using an automatic hematology analyzer by the China company maccura (LABAS F 9000) to obtain hemoglobin (HGB) amounts, neutrophil counts, monocyte counts, lymphocyte counts, and platelet counts, which were then used to calculate NLR, MLR, and PLR. We did not use any cellular markers to count these cells. The patients were assessed using DXA produced by the American company Hologic (DiscoveryA, S/N87352). BMD measurements were taken at the lumbar spine (L1-L4), left femoral neck, and left hip. The diagnostic criteria for OP were based on the recommendations of the World Health Organization. Patients were divided into 3 groups: the normal bone mass group (T score ≥ −1.0), the osteopenia group (−2.5 < T score < −1.0), and the OP group (T score ≤ −2.5).

2.3. Definition

The NLR, monocyte-to-lymphocyte ratio (MLR) and PLR were calculated respectively according to the following equations: (neutrophil count [×109/L])/(lymphocyte count [×109/L]), (monocyte count [×109/L])/(lymphocyte count [×109/L]), (platelet count [×109/L])/(lymphocyte count [×109/L]).

2.4. Statistical analysis

All data were analyzed using SPSS 26.0 statistical software. For skewed distributions of quantitative data, medians and interquartile ranges were used for description. Differences between 3 independent sample groups were compared using the Kruskal–Wallis H test. Correlation analysis was performed using Spearman’s rank correlation test. In addition, univariate and multiple logistic regression models were used to investigate the factors influencing the occurrence of OP in postmenopausal patients with T2DM. The receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve was used to assess the predictive value of OP occurrence in postmenopausal T2DM patients, and P < .05 was considered statistically significant.

3. Results

A total of 320 patients were included in this study, comprising 109 individuals in the normal bone mass group, 99 individuals in the osteopenia group, and 112 individuals in the OP group.

Specifically, compared with the normal bone mass group and the osteopenia group, patients in the OP group are older, have lower BMI, longer menopausal duration and DM duration, and higher values of NLR, MLR, and PLR, while their 25(OH)D and BMD at L1–L4, left femoral neck, and left hip are lower. The study found that the NLR and the PLR are independent risk factors for OP development in patients (P < .001, respectively). Moreover, the study revealed that 25(OH)D is an independent protective factor against osteoporosis in postmenopausal patients with T2DM (P < .001). These findings are detailed in Table 3.

3.4. Analysis of the predictive value of NLR, MLR, and PLR for OP in postmenopausal patients with T2DM

The ROC curve results indicate that the area under the curve (AUC) for NLR is 0.886, with a sensitivity of 90.2% and a specificity of 74.0%. For PLR, the AUC is 0.829, with a sensitivity of 89.3% and a specificity of 68.3%. The AUC for NLR is higher than that of PLR, and this difference is statistically significant (Z = 2.231, P = .026). The combined AUC for both is 0.904, with a sensitivity of 90.2% and a specificity of 76.9%. The AUC of the combined prediction is higher than that of PLR, and this difference is statistically significant (Z = 4.376, P < .001). Please refer to Table 4 and Figure 2 for further details.

4. Discussion

Previous studies have indicated that elevated NLR, MLR, and PLR are associated with decreased BMD and increased risk of OP.[10–12] However, research on the relationship between NLR, MLR, PLR, and OP in postmenopausal patients with T2DM is limited. Thus, this study presents a detailed analysis of the relationship between NLR, MLR, PLR, BMD, and OP in postmenopausal patients with T2DM. The study results indicate that the levels of NLR, MLR, and PLR in the OP group are significantly higher than those in the normal bone mass group and the osteopenia group (P < .001). Additionally, further analysis indicates a negative correlation between NLR, MLR, and PLR and BMD at lumbar spine (L1–L4), the left femoral neck, and the left hip (P < .001). The study found a stronger correlation between NLR and BMD at various sites in postmenopausal patients with T2DM compared to MLR and PLR. The results of a multiple logistic regression analysis indicate that elevated NLR and PLR are independent risk factors for the development of OP in postmenopausal patients with T2DM (P < .001). The results suggest that inflammatory markers NLR, MLR, and PLR are important factors in the reduction of BMD in postmenopausal patients with T2DM, which significantly affects the development of OP. Additionally, ROC curve analysis reveals that NLR and PLR have AUC values of 0.886 and 0.829, respectively, for predicting OP in postmenopausal patients with T2DM. When NLR and PLR are combined, the AUC is 0.904. The study results indicate that NLR, PLR and their combination have certain value in predicting the occurrence of OP in postmenopausal patients with T2DM. Among them, NLR and the combination of NLR and PLR appear to have better predictive efficacy than MLR alone. Regarding another study on the correlation between inflammatory markers and BMD and OP in postmenopausal T2DM patients, Li et al[13] reported that the MLR in postmenopausal T2DM patients without OP was significantly higher than in those with OP, suggesting that MLR may be an independent protective factor against OP in postmenopausal T2DM. It is hypothesized that these discrepancies may be attributed to the limited sample size and potential selection bias.

T2DM is an inflammatory disease associated with immune system dysfunction. Diabetic patients experience high blood glucose and oxidative stress, which can activate nuclear factor kappa B (NF-κB). Previous studies probing key transcription factors and their target genes in osteoporosis have also identified NFKB2.[14] This activation leads to increased expression of pro-inflammatory cytokines and chemokines, recruitment of more inflammatory cells, stimulation of osteoclast differentiation, and inhibition of osteoblast formation.[15] Additionally, in a state of high blood glucose, there is an increase in intracellular reactive oxygen species (ROS) production, which enhances oxidative stress. This affects the differentiation and survival of osteoclasts, osteoblasts, and bone cells.[16] White blood cells and their subtypes are crucial inflammatory markers for various diseases. Recent studies have shown that NLR, MLR, and PLR can predict complications of T2DM, such as diabetic peripheral neuropathy, diabetic retinopathy, diabetic kidney disease, and diabetic foot.[17–20] The exact mechanism responsible for the elevation of NLR, MLR, and PLR and their association with the occurrence of OP in T2DM patients is not yet fully understood. Previous studies have indicated that estrogen interacts with various immune cells and cytokines, and a deficiency in estrogen can lead to a state of chronic low-grade inflammation within the body.[5] Immune cells regulate the function of osteoblasts and osteoclasts through various cytokines and signaling pathways, ultimately leading to bone loss.[21] A meta-analysis has demonstrated that estrogen can influence the functionality of neutrophils. Neutrophils are capable of producing mediators, including interferon-gamma (IFN-γ), interleukin-6 (IL-6), interleukin-1 (IL-1), and receptor activator of nuclear factor kappa-B ligand (RANKL), which can result in an increase in the number and activity of osteoclasts.[22] Additionally, neutrophils can also secrete numerous chemokines to recruit Th17 cells, which in turn contribute to bone loss.[23] Gong et al[24] observed that in animals with T lymphocyte deficiencies, there was an increase in the number of osteoclasts and a reduction in bone mass. CD8+ T lymphocytes have the capacity to secrete soluble proteins, such as osteoprotegerin (OPG), which inhibit the development of osteoclasts.[25] B lymphocytes are also capable of producing transforming growth factor (TGF-β), which has been demonstrated to promote the apoptosis of osteoclasts and to exert a positive regulatory effect on the RANK/RANKL/OPG system. TGFBR2 could induce apoptosis and differentiation processes activated by TGF-β. This could affect monocyte differentiation into osteoclasts and bone remodeling.[26] In addition, the classical subset of peripheral blood monocytes, characterized by high levels of CD14 and the absence of CD16, plays a crucial role in osteoclastogenesis by serving as osteoclast precursors and producing osteoclastogenic factors.[26] The current state of research indicates that platelets have a dual role. On the one hand, platelets promote angiogenesis and induce the migration and proliferation of osteoblasts, thereby facilitating bone regeneration.[27] On the other hand, inflammatory states can stimulate platelet activation, and activated platelets can promote osteoclastogenesis.[28] Further studies are required to confirm the impact of platelets on bone homeostasis.

We also found that 25(OH)D was lower in the OP group than in the normal bone mass group and the reduced bone mass group, and that there was a positive correlation between 25(OH)D levels and BMD, which was an independent protective factor for the development of OP in postmenopausal T2DM patients. This is consistent with previous research findings. Vitamin D is essential for bone homeostasis. A deficiency of 25(OH)D can reduce BMD, thereby increasing the risk of OP.[29] However, since vitamin D levels in the body are strongly influenced by age, further comparisons in other ranges are needed to confirm this.

This study has several limitations. Firstly, due to its retrospective nature, it is not possible to establish a causal relationship between NLR, MLR, PLR, and reduced BMD. Secondly, the relatively small sample size restricts further in-depth and meaningful subgroup analysis. Thirdly, we did not investigate other factors that affect bone mineral density (BMD), such as other inflammatory markers, alcohol consumption, and tobacco use. Fourthly, although the cell analyzers are functionally equivalent, the errors in determining cells in different models of cell analyzers may have an impact on our cell counts, which may affect the results of the study.[30] Finally, the influence of extraneous factors on the cells has not been ruled out, and other diseases of the patients themselves may have an effect on cell numbers and thus interfere with the results of the study. For example, SARS-CoV-2 may affect neutrophils through the ACE2 receptor.[31] Therefore, further prospective studies with larger sample sizes are needed.

5. Conclusion

In summary, this study suggests that NLR, MLR, and PLR may serve as simple, economical, and noninvasive inflammatory markers that reflect the body’s inflammatory status. The study found a negative correlation between these indices and BMD in postmenopausal T2DM patients. In addition, low levels of 25(OH)D have been associated with the development of OP in postmenopausal patients with T2DM. These indicators could potentially offer new tools for the clinical prediction of OP. However, further prospective cohort studies and mechanistic research are needed to confirm these findings.

Density Matters: A Complexity Dichotomy of Deleting Edges to Bound Subgraph Density
Matthias Bentert, Tom-Lukas Breitkopf, Vincent Froese, Anton Herrmann, André Nichterlein

Abstract
We study tbded ( tbded ), where given an undirected graph G G , the task is to remove as few edges as possible to obtain a graph G ′ G^{\prime} where no subgraph of G ′ G^{\prime} has density more than τ \tau . The density of a (sub)graph is the number of edges divided by the number of vertices. This problem was recently introduced and shown to be NP-hard for τ ∈ { 2 / 3 , 3 / 4 , 1 + 1 / 25 } \tau\in\{2/3,3/4,1+1/25\} , but polynomial-time solvable for τ ∈ { 0 , 1 / 2 , 1 } \tau\in\{0,1/2,1\} [Bazgan et al., JCSS 2025]. We provide a complete dichotomy with respect to the target density τ \tau : 1. If 2 ​ τ ∈ ℕ 2\tau\in\mathds{N} (half-integral target density) or τ < 2 / 3 \tau<2/3 , then tbded is polynomial-time solvable. 2. Otherwise, tbded is NP-hard. We complement the NP-hardness with fixed-parameter tractability with respect to the treewidth of G G . Moreover, for integral target density τ ∈ ℕ \tau\in\mathds{N} , we show tbded to be solvable in randomized O ⁡ ( m 1 + o ⁡ ( 1 ) ) O(m^{1+o(1)}) time. Our algorithmic results are based on a reduction to a new general flow problem on restricted networks that, depending on τ \tau , can be solved via flow or gf . We believe this connection between these variants of flow and matching to be of independent interest.

Introduction

Finding a densest subgraph in a given undirected graph is an “evergreen research topic”
[
16
]
.
The density of a (sub)graph is the number of edges divided by the number of vertices, which is equal to half the average degree.
A graph with
n
vertices can thus have a density up to
(n-1)/2
; with this maximum being reached if and only if the graph is a clique.
While a reader unfamiliar with the problem might suspect it to be NP-hard—like
Clique
—it can in fact be solved in polynomial time:
This was first discovered 1979 and published 1982 by
19
with an algorithm based on maximum-flow computations (cf.
16
).
An improved algorithm due to
12
also relies on a maximum s-t-flow.

In this work, we study the following closely related problem called
tbded
(
tbded
) where
\tau\geq 0
:
Given an undirected graph
G=(V,E)
, delete as few edges as possible such that the densest subgraph of the remaining graph has density at most
\tau
.
This problem was recently introduced by
3
, who observed that for
\tau<1
the remaining graph has to be a forest where each tree can have at most
\lfloor 1/(1-\tau)\rfloor
vertices.
Thus,
tbded
generalizes the polynomial-time solvable
Maximum Cardinality Matching
(
\tau=1/2
) and the NP-hard
Perfect
P_{3}
-Packing
(
\tau=2/3
).
For
\tau>1
, not much is known about the complexity of
tbded
beyond NP-hardness for
\tau
slightly above
1
(
\tau=1+1/c
with
c
being any constant larger than
24

[
3
]
).

Our main result is a complete complexity dichotomy for
tbded
:

Theorem 1
.

If
2\tau\in\mathds{N}
or
\tau<2/3
, then
tbded
is polynomial-time solvable, otherwise it is NP-hard.
Moreover, if
\tau\in\mathds{N}
, then it is solvable in randomized
O(m^{1+o(1)})
time.

Additionally, we show fixed-parameter tractability for the parameter treewidth.
To this end, note that for a graph of treewidth
\tw
a tree decomposition of width
\tw
can be computed in time
2^{O(\tw^{2})}n^{O(1)}

[
14
]
and a tree decomposition of width
2\tw+1
in time
2^{O(\tw)}n

[
15
]
.

Theorem 2
.

tbded
is fixed-parameter tractable with respect to treewidth.
Given a tree decomposition of width
\tw
with
\tau=\frac{a}{b}
, it can be solved in time
{(\max\{a,b\}+1)^{\tw+1}n^{O(1)}}
.

On the technical side, we follow the line of
19
and
12
:
We achieve our algorithmic results by efficiently reducing to a generalized flow problem (which we call
rflow
(
rflow
)).
For integral target density
\tau\in\mathds{N}
,
rflow
can be solved with a simple maximum
s
-
t
-flow computation in a linear-size network with small integer capacities (at most
\tau
).
This allows employing recent algorithms for computing maximum flows
[
7
]
to solve
tbded
with
\tau\in\mathds{N}
in randomized
O(m^{1+o(1)})
time on graphs with
m
edges.
For
\tau=1/2
, our flow approach to solve the
rflow
instance fails.
The reason is that we rely on a maximum flow being integral and some arcs having capacity
\tau
.
In such cases, a simple scaling of capacities yields integer values.
However, our flow-problem then gets an additional constraint that some arcs either transport a full flow (equal to the capacity) or no flow at all.
These “all-or-nothing” restrictions render the
Maximum Flow
problem NP-hard in general, even if all arcs have integer capacities of at most two.

For

, we define

with

We use

.

Graph & Digraphs.

All graphs in this work are simple and unweighted.
Let

be a digraph.
We denote the set of vertices of

and

For

We denote the degree of a vertex

\deg_{G}(v)\coloneqq|N(v)|
, where

.
Similarly, we denote the in-degree (out-degree) of a vertex
v\in V(D)
by
\deg_{D}^{-}(v)\coloneqq|N^{-}(v)|
(
\deg_{D}^{+}(v)\coloneqq|N^{+}(v)|

N^{-}(v)\coloneqq\{u\in V(D)\mid(u,v)\in A(D)\}
(
N^{+}(v)\coloneqq\{u\in V(D)\mid(v,u)\in A(D)\}
).
If the considered graph is clear from the context, then we drop the subscripts.
We write
H\subseteq G
to denote that
H
is a subgraph of
G
.
For a subset
E^{\prime}\subseteq E(G)
, we write
G-E^{\prime}
for the subgraph of

.
We denote by
K_{n}
the complete graph on
n
vertices (also called a clique of order
n
).

The

G
is
\rho(G)\coloneqq m/n
, where the density of the empty graph is defined as zero.
We denote by
\rho^{*}(G)
the density of the densest subgraph of
G
, that is,
\rho^{*}(G)\coloneqq\max_{H\subseteq G}\rho(H)
.
A graph
G
is
balanced
if
\rho(H)\leq\rho(G)
for every subgraph
H\subseteq G
; this implies
\rho^{*}(G)=\rho(G)
.
We study the following problem, which is defined for every rational
\tau\geq 0
:

tbded
(
tbded
)

Input:

An undirected graph
G
and an integer
k\in\mathds{N}
.

Question:

Is there a subset
F\subseteq E(G)
with
|F|\leq k
such that
\rho^{*}(G-F)\leq\tau
?

Throughout the rest of this work we assume
G
to be connected and hence have
n_{G}-1\leq m_{G}
and
O(m_{G}+n_{G})=O(m_{G})
.
Clearly, if
G
is not connected we can obtain a solution by solving each connected component individually and isolated vertices can be ignored.

Fractional Orientations.

A crucial tool in our subsequent proofs and approaches are orientations, which follow from a dual LP formulation by
6
to compute the density of a densest subgraph.
An
orientation
of an undirected graph
G
assigns a direction to each edge
\{u,v\}
, that is,
\{u,v\}
is replaced either by
(u,v)
or by
(v,u)
.

6
observed that the maximum indegree in any orientation of
G
is an upper bound for
\rho^{*}(G)
.
This is also true for fractional orientations where we assign some part of an edge
\{u,v\}
to
u
and the rest to
v
.
More formally, a
fractional orientation

\phi
assigns to each edge
e=\{u,v\}
two non-negative rationals
\phi(e)^{u}
and
\phi(e)^{v}
with
\phi(e)^{u}+\phi(e)^{v}=1
.
We denote with

\displaystyle\deg_{\phi}^{-}(v)\coloneqq\sum_{u\in N(v)}\phi(\{u,v\})^{v}

and

\displaystyle\Delta_{\phi}^{-}\coloneqq\max_{v\in V(G)}\deg_{\phi}^{-}(v)

the
indegree
of a vertex
v\in V(G)
and the
maximum indegree
of
G
with respect to the fractional orientation
\phi
.
From now on we write
\phi(uv)^{v}
as a short-hand for
\phi(\{u,v\})^{v}
.

We restate the aforementioned property as a lemma together with a stricter formulation.

Lemma 3
.

For every graph
G
and fractional orientation
\phi
, it holds that
\rho^{*}(G)\leq\Delta^{-}_{\phi}
.

Lemma 4

(
6
)
.

For every graph
G
, there is a fractional orientation
\phi
with
\rho^{*}(G)=\Delta_{\phi}^{-}
.

It follows from the work of
6
and the fact that LPs can be solved in polynomial time
[
21
]
, that an orientation
\phi
with
\rho^{*}(G)=\Delta_{\phi}^{-}
can be computed in polynomial time.

Flows and Factors.

Studied for well over half a century
Maximum Flow
is still subject of active research
[
7
]
.
We refer to standard text books for an overview on flows
[
1
,
21
]
and subsequently recall very briefly only the most relevant notions and concepts.
In the basic setting, we are given a directed graph
D
forming the flow network with two distinguished vertices
s,t\in V(D)
and arcs that are equipped with capacities
\capacity\colon A(D)\rightarrow\mathds{Q}^{+}_{0}
.
A flow
f\colon A(D)\rightarrow\mathds{Q}^{+}_{0}
assigns to each arc
a\in A(D)
a non-negative amount of flow
{f(a)\leq\capacity(a)}
such that for each vertex
v\in V(D)\setminus\{s,t\}
the amount
f_{\Sigma}^{-}(v):=\sum_{u\in N^{-}(v)}f(u,v)
of flow entering
v
is equal to the amount
f_{\Sigma}^{+}(v):=\sum_{u\in N^{+}(v)}f(v,u)
of flow leaving
v
.
Here, and throughout the paper, we write
f(a,b)
as a short-hand for
f((a,b))
.
The excess of the flow at
v
is
f_{\Sigma}(v):=f_{\Sigma}^{+}(v)-f_{\Sigma}^{-}(v)
, that is,
s
and
t
are the only vertices with non-zero excess.
The value of a flow is defined as
\val(f)=f_{\Sigma}(s)=-f_{\Sigma}(t)
.

Many extensions on the basic
Maximum Flow
have been studied, including costs on arcs, multiple sources and sinks, or lower bounds on the flow amount for the arcs (to name just a few).
Computing maximum flows respecting all these restrictions can be done in polynomial time
[
1
,
21
]
.
Computing a maximum flow that is even on
all
arcs (while still respecting capacities) is still doable in polynomial time (and seems a common exercise given the online search results).
However, restricting a subset
F\subsetneq A(D)
of arcs to have either flow
0
or
2
is already sufficient make the problem NP-hard (see
\cref
app:np_hardness_subset_even_flow).

In contrast to
maximum flow
, the following
Matching
generalization is still polynomial-time solvable if restricted to even (or odd) numbers.

gf

Input:

An undirected graph
G
, a collection of sets
\mathcal{B}=\{B_{v}\subseteq\{0,1,\dots,\deg_{G}(v)\}\mid v\in V(G)\}
.

Question:

Is there a subset
F\subseteq E(G)
, such that
\deg_{(V(G),F)}(v)\in B_{v}
for every vertex
v\in V(G)
?

We call a solution
F
a
factor
of
(G,\mathcal{B})
.

gf
with
B_{v}=\{1\}
for each vertex
v
is exactly
pm
.

8
showed that

is polynomial-time solvable if the maximum gap in any

is of length at most one.
Here, an integer subset

has a gap of length

if there exists an integer

but

For example, the set

has gaps of length one as certified by

and

We denote by

Note that a max-gap of one is the boundary of polynomial-time solvability:
There is a straightforward reduction from the NP-hard

[

to

where every vertex

has allowed degrees either

(the vertex represents a set) or

Recently,

4
Algorithmic Results

In this section, we provide efficient algorithms for
tbded
with certain target densities.
More precisely, we provide polynomial-time algorithms for integral and half-integral target densities, while for arbitrary constant target densities, we prove fixed-parameter tractability with respect to the treewidth of the input graph.
Our approach builds on the overall idea of
\cref
sec:integral, that is, we reduce
tbded
to some kind of flow problem.
In particular, we introduce the
rflow
(
rflow
) problem, which is, informally spoken, a flow problem on graphs with multiple source and sink vertices, where every edge goes directly from a source to a sink and every vertex has a set of allowed excess values.
Afterwards, we translate this problem to
gf
.
Applying known results for
gf
, we finally deduce algorithmic results for
rflow
, which, on the one hand, are of interest on their own and, on the other hand, can then be translated to
tbded
.

4.1
Transshipment Formulation

To describe
tbded
more generally, we switch to transshipment problems, that is, a flow-variant where we have multiple sources and sinks with specified amounts of flow that are provided resp. consumed by the sources and sinks
[
21
]
.
Consider a digraph
D
.
For a function
b\colon V(D)\rightarrow\mathds{Q}
, a
b
-transshipment is a flow
f
with
f_{\Sigma}(v)=b(v)
for all vertices
v\in V(D)
.
Note that
\sum_{v\in V}b(v)=0
is necessary for a
b
-transshipment to exist.
Adapting notation from
gf
, we specify a set collection
\mathcal{B}=\{B_{v}\subseteq\mathds{Q}\mid v\in V(D)\}
of allowed excess values for each vertex.
A
\mathcal{B}
-transshipment
is then a (flow) function
f\colon A(D)\to\mathds{Q}^{+}_{0}
with
f_{\Sigma}(v)\in B_{v}
for all
v\in V(D)
.
Note that if also arc capacities
\capacity\colon A(D)\to\mathds{Q}^{+}_{0}
are provided, then we have the usual additional requirement
f(a)\leq\capacity(a)
for each arc
a\in A(D)
.
We call an instance
(D,\capacity,\mathcal{B})

integral
if
\capacity\colon A(D)\to\mathds{N}
and
B_{v}\subseteq\mathds{Z}
for all
v\in V(D)
.
We call a digraph
D

2-layered
, if the underlying undirected graph is bipartite with parts
U
and
W
and all arcs go from
U
to
W
, that is,
A(D)\subseteq U\times W
.
For a 2-layered network
D
with vertex partitions
U
and
W
and flow
f
, we define the
value

\val(f)=\sum_{u\in U}f_{\Sigma}(u)=\sum_{w\in W}-f_{\Sigma}(w)
.
Note that in a 2-layered transshipment instance, we can assume that the vertices in
U
are sources (non-negative excess) and the vertices in
W
are sinks (non-positive excess).
With these notions, we can define our transshipment problem:

rflow
(
rflow
)

Input:

A 2-layered network
D
with parts
U
and
W
, arc capacities
\capacity\colon A(D)\rightarrow\mathds{Q}^{+}_{0}
, and a collection
\mathcal{B}=\{B_{v}\subseteq\mathds{Q}^{+}_{0}\mid v\in U\}\cup\{B_{v}\subseteq\mathds{Q}^{-}_{0}\mid v\in W\}
of sets.

Task:

Find a
\mathcal{B}
-transshipment
f
in
D
which maximizes
\val(f)
.

For the rest of this work we assume the underlying undirected graph of
D
to be connected and therefore have
n_{D}-1\leq m_{D}
and
O(m_{D}+n_{D})=O(m_{D})
.
Clearly if this is not the case, we obtain a solution by solving each connected components individually and isolated vertices can be ignored.
To familiarize ourselves with the problem, let us model an instance
\mathcal{I}=(G,k)
of
tbded
as a
rflow
-instance; we denote this linear-time transformation by
T(\mathcal{I})
:

•

The vertex set of the network
D
is
V(D)\coloneqq V(G)\cup E(G)
.

•

For each edge
e=\{u,v\}\in E(G)
, add the arcs
(e,u)
and
(e,v)
with capacity 1 to
D
.

•

For each
e\in E(G)
, set
B_{e}\coloneqq\{0,1\}
, that is, a flow of either 0 or 1 is allowed to leave
e
.

•

For each
v\in V(G)
, set
B_{v}\coloneqq[-\tau,0]
, that is, any flow up to
\tau
is allowed to arrive in
v
.

Note that in the above construction a vertex
v\in V(G)
is assigned an infinitely large set
[-\tau,0]
of allowed excess values.
We henceforth assume that a reasonable encoding for the sets
B_{v}
is used (here, e. g. via the numbers
-\tau
and
0
encoding the interval).

It is straightforward to verify the correctness of this reduction:

Lemma 6
.

Let
\mathcal{I}=(G,k)
be a
tbded
instance and let
T(\mathcal{I})=(D,\capacity,\mathcal{B})
be the constructed
rflow
instance.
Then there is a
\mathcal{B}
-transshipment
f
in
D
with value
\val(f)\geq m_{G}-k
if and only if there is an edge set
F\subseteq E
,
|F|\leq k
, with
\rho^{*}(G-F)\leq\tau
.

Proof.

“
\Rightarrow
”: Suppose that
f
is a
\mathcal{B}
-transshipment for
(D,\capacity,\mathcal{B})
with
\val(f)\geq m_{G}-k
.
We define
F\subseteq E(G)
to be the set of edges where no flow is sent from the corresponding vertices in
D
, that is,
F:=\{e\in E(G)\mid f_{\Sigma}(e)=0\}
.
Since
f_{\Sigma}(e)\in B_{e}=\{0,1\}
for all
e\in E(G)
and
\val(f)\geq m_{G}-k
, it follows that
|F|\leq k
.

Using the flow
f
, we now construct a fractional orientation
\phi
for
G-F
with
\Delta_{\phi}^{-}\leq\tau
.

\Cref
lem:density_bound then yields the claim.
For any edge
e=\{u,v\}\in E(G)\setminus F
, consider the arcs
(e,u)
and
(e,v)
in
A(D)
.
Set
\phi(e)^{u}:=f(e,u)
and
\phi(e)^{v}:=f(e,v)
.
It holds that
\phi(e)^{u}+\phi(e)^{v}=1
, since
f_{\Sigma}(e)=1
.
For any vertex
v\in V(G)
, it holds that
\deg_{\phi}^{-}(v)\leq\tau
, because
f_{\Sigma}(v)\geq-\tau
by the excess constraint
B_{v}=[-\tau,0]
.

“
\Leftarrow
”: Now, let
F\subseteq E(G)
,
|F|\leq k
, with
\rho^{*}(G-F)\leq\tau
. By
\Cref
lem:density_orientation, there exists a fractional orientation
\phi
on
G-F
with
\Delta_{\phi}^{-}\leq\tau
.
We construct a
\mathcal{B}
-transshipment
f
with
\val(f)\geq m_{G}-k
from
\phi
:
For each
e=\{u,v\}\in E(G)\setminus F
, set the flows
f(e,u)\coloneqq\phi(e)^{u}
and
f(e,v)\coloneqq\phi(e)^{v}
, and for each
e=\{u,v\}\in F
, set
f(e,u)=f(e,v)\coloneqq 0
.
Consider the vertices in
V(D)
representing the edges of
E(G)
.
The above assignment ensures for any vertex
e\in E(G)\setminus F
, that
f_{\Sigma}(e)=1
and for any vertex
e\in F
that
f_{\Sigma}(e)=0
.
Moreover,
f_{\Sigma}(v)\in[-\tau,0]
is guaranteed since
\Delta_{\phi}^{-}\leq\tau
.
Finally, we have
\val(f)=\sum_{e\in E\setminus F}1=m_{G}-|F|\geq m_{G}-k
.
∎

4.2
Solving
rflow
via General Factors

We now consider how to solve
rflow
using
gf
.
One important step will be to limit the excess constraints of vertices in a
rflow
instance to integral values.
For this, we first consider conditions under which
rflow
yields integral solutions, meaning solutions in which the flow assigned to any edge is integral.

Lemma 7
.

Let
(D,\capacity\colon A(D)\to\mathds{N},\mathcal{B})
be a
rflow
instance with
A(D)\subseteq U\times W
.
Furthermore, for each
v\in V(D)
, let
B_{v}\subseteq\mathds{Z}
or
B_{v}=[c_{v},d_{v}]
for some
{c_{v},d_{v}\in\mathds{Z}}
with
c_{v}\leq d_{v}
.
If
(D,\capacity,\mathcal{B})
has a
\mathcal{B}
-transshipment, then it attains an integral maximum
\mathcal{B}
-transshipment.

Proof.

The idea is to construct a flow instance with lower and upper bounds for the flow on each arc, then use a known integrality property and finally transfer the flow back to our
rflow
instance.
Let
f
be a maximum
\mathcal{B}
-transshipment for
(D,\capacity,\mathcal{B})
(such
f
must exist if there is a feasible transshipment) and consider the flow instance
\mathcal{I}^{\prime}
, where we take the network
D
together with a source vertex
s
, a sink vertex
t
and the following arcs with lower and upper bounds (denoted by intervals) on the flow assigned to them:

•

For each arc
a\in A(D)
, we set the permitted flow to
[0,\capacity(a)]
.

•

For every
u\in U
with
f_{\Sigma}(u)>0
, we add the arc
(s,u)
and distinguish between two cases. If
B_{u}=[c_{u},d_{u}]
, then we set the permitted flow to
[c_{u},d_{u}]
. Otherwise, we set the permitted flow to
[f_{\Sigma}(u),f_{\Sigma}(u)]
.

•

For every
w\in W
with
f_{\Sigma}(w)<0
, we add the arc
(w,t)
and distinguish between two cases. If
B_{w}=[c_{w},d_{w}]
, then we set the permitted flow to
[-d_{w},-c_{w}]
and otherwise to
[-f_{\Sigma}(w),-f_{\Sigma}(w)]
.

We now show that there is a feasible flow
f^{\prime}
for the constructed instance, which then implies the existence of an integral maximum flow for
\mathcal{I}^{\prime}
(see
[
21
, Corollary 11.2e]
).
Let
a
be some arc in our constructed instance. We define
f^{\prime}
by

f^{\prime}(a)\coloneqq\begin{cases}f_{\Sigma}(u)&\text{if }a=(s,u)\text{ for some }u\in U\\
f(a)&\text{if }a\in A(D)\\
-f_{\Sigma}(w)&\text{if }a=(w,t)\text{ for some }w\in W.\end{cases}

Note that
\val(f)=\val(f^{\prime})
since both flows send the same amount of flow from
U
to
W
.
The feasibility of
f^{\prime}
follows directly from our construction and the fact that
f
is a
\mathcal{B}
-transshipment.
Now, let
f^{\prime\prime}
be an integral maximum flow for
\mathcal{I}^{\prime}
.
We define an integral
\mathcal{B}
-transshipment
f^{*}
by
f^{*}(u,w):=f^{\prime\prime}(u,w)
for all
(u,w)\in A(D)
.

First, we show that
f^{*}
is indeed a
\mathcal{B}
-transshipment.
For this, note that for each
u\in U
, we have
f^{*}_{\Sigma}(u)=f^{\prime\prime}(s,u)
.
We distinguish between two cases.
If
B_{u}\subseteq\mathds{Z}
, then the permitted flow on
(s,u)
is exactly
f_{\Sigma}(u)
and therefore
f^{\prime\prime}(s,u)=f_{\Sigma}(u)\in B_{u}
.
Otherwise, the permitted flow on
(s,u)
is
[c_{u},d_{u}]
which directly implies
f^{\prime\prime}(s,u)\in[c_{u},d_{u}]=B_{u}
.
For all vertices
w\in W
, we have
f^{*}_{\Sigma}(w)=-f^{\prime\prime}(w,t)
and by analogous arguments as above, it follows
-f^{\prime\prime}(w,t)\in B_{w}
.

It remains to show that
f^{*}
is indeed a maximum flow.
Recall that
f
is a maximum flow for
(D,\capacity,\mathcal{B})
and
f^{\prime}
with
\val(f^{\prime})=\val(f)
is a feasible solution for
\mathcal{I}^{\prime}
.
Further,
f^{\prime\prime}
is a maximum integral flow for
\mathcal{I}^{\prime}
, that is,
\val(f^{\prime})\leq\val(f^{\prime\prime})
.
Finally, since
\val(f^{*})=\val(f^{\prime\prime})
, we have
\val(f)=\val(f^{\prime})\leq\val(f^{\prime\prime})=\val(f^{*})
.
∎

Using the above lemma we now observe that any
rflow
instance with an integral maximum transshipment can be restricted to integral capacities and integral excess values.

Observation 8
.

If a
rflow
instance
(D,\capacity,\mathcal{B})
has an integral maximum
\mathcal{B}
-transshipment, then it is equivalent to
(D,\capacity^{\prime},\mathcal{B}^{\prime})
where
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}\coloneqq\{B_{v}\cap\mathds{Z}\mid B_{v}\in\mathcal{B}\}
and
\capacity^{\prime}(a)\coloneqq\lfloor\capacity(a)\rfloor
for each arc
a\in A(D)
.

With this observation we can now describe a reduction of integral
rflow
instances to
gf
.
This will later allow us to use known algorithms for
gf
to solve
rflow
instances.

Lemma 9
.

Let
(D,\capacity\colon A(D)\to\mathds{N},\mathcal{B}=\{B_{v}\subseteq\mathds{Z}\mid v\in V(D)\})
be an integral
rflow
instance, where
all capacities are bounded polynomially in the size of
D
.
Then, one can construct in polynomial time, a
gf
instance
(G,\mathcal{B}^{\prime})
with the following properties:

[(i)]

1.

(D,\capacity,\mathcal{B})
has an integral
\mathcal{B}
-transshipment of value
p
if and only if
(G,\mathcal{B}^{\prime})
has a factor of size
2p
.

2.

Given a tree decomposition for the underlying undirected graph of
D
of width
\tw
, we can compute a tree decomposition of width
\max\{2,\tw\}
for
G
in polynomial time.

3.

The max-gap of
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
is
\max\{1,\gamma\}
, where
\gamma
is the max-gap of
\mathcal{B}
.

Proof.

Let
(D,\capacity,\mathcal{B}=\{B_{v}\subseteq\mathds{Z}\mid v\in V(D)\})
be a given
rflow
instance with
{A(D)\subseteq U\times W}
.
Now, consider the multigraph, which is obtained by replacing every arc
(u,w)
with
\capacity(u,w)
many undirected parallel edges between
u
and
w
.
Moreover, set
{\mathcal{B}^{\prime}\coloneqq\{\abs(B_{v})\mid B_{v}\in\mathcal{B}\}}
, where
\abs(B_{v})\coloneqq\{|x|\mid x\in B_{v}\}
.
We can transform the multigraph into a simple graph by subdividing every edge and adding the degree constraint
\{0,2\}
to
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
for every newly created vertex.
We refer to the set of new vertices by
H
and if necessary, we call the vertex, which was introduced for the
i^{\text{th}}
parallel edge between
u
and
w
, by
h^{i}_{uw}
.
We call the obtained graph
G
and the constructed
gf
instance is
(G,\mathcal{B}^{\prime})
.
We next show property (
1
).

“
\Rightarrow
”: Let
f
be an integral
\mathcal{B}
-transshipment for
(D,\capacity,\mathcal{B})
.
We show that there is a general factor
F\subseteq E(G)
for the instance
(G,\mathcal{B}^{\prime})
of size
|F|=2\val(f)
.
Intuitively, if the flow value on the arc
(u,w)
is
f(u,w)
, then we add
2f(u,w)
edges from
G
to
F
, such that they correspond to
f(u,w)
copies of the edge
\{u,w\}
.
Formally, we set

F:=\bigcup_{(uw)\in A(D)}\bigcup_{i=1}^{f(u,w)}\left\{\{u,h^{i}_{uw}\},\{h^{i}_{uw},w\}\right\}.

Since every flow from
U
to
W
counts towards
\val(f)
, it directly follows that
|F|=2\val(f)
.
It remains to show that
F
satisfies the degree constraints
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
, that is,
\deg_{(V(G),F)}(v)\in B_{v}
for every
v\in V(G)
.
Recall, that for
h\in H
, we have
B_{h}=\{0,2\}
.
By construction, we either add zero or two edges incident to
h
to
F
and therefore we have
\deg_{(V(G),F)}(h)\in B_{h}
.
For
v\in U\cup W
, note that the degree in
(V(G),F)
coincides exactly with the absolute value of
f_{\Sigma}(v)
.
This together with
f_{\Sigma}(v)\in B_{v}
implies that
\deg_{(V(G),F)}(v)\in\abs(B_{v})\in\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
.

“
\Leftarrow
”: Now suppose
F\subseteq E(G)
is a
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
-factor.
We show that there exists a
\mathcal{B}
-transshipment
f
with
\val(f)=\frac{|F|}{2}
.
Let
(u,w)
be an arc in
D
.
By construction, if
\{u,h^{i}_{uw}\}\in F
for some
{h^{i}_{uw}\in H}
, then also
\{h^{i}_{uw},w\}\in F
, because
B_{h^{i}_{uw}}=\{0,2\}
.
Now, we define a flow
f
by setting
f(u,w)
to the number of vertices from
H
to which both
u
and
w
are connected in
(V(G),F)
.
This is an integral feasible flow since for every arc
(u,w)\in A(D)
both
u
and
w
have by construction at most
\capacity(u,w)
common neighbors in
G
.
Moreover, for each
u\in U
, we have
{f_{\Sigma}(u)=\deg_{(V(G),F)}(u)\in\abs(B_{u})=B_{u}}
and for each
w\in W
, we have
{f_{\Sigma}(w)=-\deg_{(V(G),F)}(w)\in\{-x\mid x\in\abs(B_{w})\}=B_{w}}
.
Since exactly
\frac{|F|}{2}
edges are between
U
and
H
and also between
H
and
W
, it follows
\val(f)=\frac{|F|}{2}
.

It remains to show the two properties (
2
) and (
3
).

(
2
): Let
(\mathcal{T},(X_{i})_{i\in V(\mathcal{T})})
be a tree-decomposition of width
\tw
for the underlying undirected graph of
D
.
For each arc
(u,w)\in A(D)
, there exists a bag
X_{i}
with
\{u,w\}\subseteq X_{i}
.
We obtain a tree decomposition for
G
of width
\max\{2,\tw\}
by appending one bag
\{u,w,h^{j}_{uw}\}
to
X_{i}
for each
j\in[\capacity(u,w)]
.

(
3
): Note that, for any vertex
h\in H
, the set
B_{h}
has a gap of exactly one.
For
v\in U\cup W
, the collection
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
contains the set
\abs(B_{v})
which has the same gap as
B_{v}
since either
B_{v}\subseteq\mathds{Q}^{+}_{0}
or
B_{v}\subseteq\mathds{Q}^{-}_{0}
.
∎

To sum up, we have seen that a
rflow
instance allowing flows in an interval with integer endpoints can be restricted to an integral instance and that integral
rflow
instances can be translated into equivalent
gf
instances.
For non-integral
rflow
instances, the reduction to
gf
does not work.
A common trick we will use later, is to scale capacities and excess constraints by some factor in order to make them integral.
This scaling obviously influences the overall flow value.

Lemma 10
.

Let
(D,\capacity,\mathcal{B})
be a
rflow
instance,
q\in\mathds{Q}^{+}_{0}
and
(D,\capacity^{\prime},\mathcal{B}^{\prime})
be the instance, which is obtained by multiplying all excess constraints and capacities from
(D,\capacity,\mathcal{B})
with
q
.
Then,
(D,\capacity,\mathcal{B})
has a maximum
\mathcal{B}
-transshipment value of
p
if and only if
(D,\capacity^{\prime},\mathcal{B}^{\prime})
has a maximum
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
-transshipment value of
pq
.

Proof.

Clearly, since all capacities and excess values are multiplied by
q
, it follows that
f
is a maximum
\mathcal{B}
-transshipment if and only if
f^{\prime}

with
f^{\prime}(u,w)\coloneqq qf(u,w)
for all
(u,w)\in A(D)
is a maximum
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
-transshipment.
∎

We write
q\mathcal{B}
for
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
to indicate that
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
was obtained from
\mathcal{B}
by scaling with factor
q
.

4.3
Algorithmic Consequences

2-Layer General Transshipment.

Having discussed how to translate a
rflow
instance into a
gf
instance, we now consider the algorithmic implications for the
rflow
problem. We obtain three algorithmic results as stated in the following theorem.

Theorem 11
.

Let
(D,\capacity\colon A(D)\to\mathds{N},\mathcal{B})
be an instance of
rflow
.

[(i)]

1.

If
B_{v}=[a_{v},b_{v}]\cap\mathds{Z}
for each vertex
v
with
a_{v}\leq 0\leq b_{v}
and the excess constraints and capacities are polynomially bounded in the size of
D
, then one can compute in randomized
O(m^{1+o(1)})
time a maximum
\mathcal{B}
-transshipment.

2.

If
B_{v}\subseteq\mathds{Z}
has gap at most one for each vertex
v
and capacities are bounded polynomially, then one can compute in polynomial time a maximum
\mathcal{B}
-transshipment or output that there exist no feasible flow.

3.

Suppose we are given a tree decomposition for the underlying undirected graph of
D
of width
\tw
. Moreover, suppose that
B_{v}\subseteq\mathds{Z}
for each vertex
v
and that capacities are bounded polynomially.
Let
M\coloneqq\max_{v\in V(D)}\max\{|x|\mid x\in B_{v}\}
.
Then, one can compute in
(M+1)^{\tw+1}n^{O(1)}
time a maximum
\mathcal{B}
-transshipment or output that there exists no feasible flow.

Proof.

We prove each of the three statements separately.

(
1
): We provide a linear-time reduction to
Maximum Flow
. If the edge capacities are integers and polynomially bounded, then an (integral) maximum flow for this instance can be computed in randomized
m^{1+o(1)}
time
[
7
]
.
Now consider the following
Maximum Flow
instance, where we take the network
D
, add a source vertex
s
, a sink vertex
t
and the following arcs:

•

For every
u\in U
, we add the arc
(s,u)
with capacity
b_{u}
.

•

For every
w\in W
, we add the arc
(w,t)
with capacity
-a_{w}
.

This network can be constructed in
O(m_{D}+n_{D})=O(m_{D})
time.
We now compute a maximum flow
f
for this network and obtain a
\mathcal{B}
-transshipment by setting
f^{*}(u,w)\coloneqq f(u,w)
for all
(u,w)\in A(D)
.
Note that
f^{*}
is feasible since
f^{*}_{\Sigma}(u)=f^{+}_{\Sigma}(u)\in[0,b_{u}]
for all

and
f^{*}_{\Sigma}(w)=-f^{-}_{\Sigma}(w)\in[a_{w},0]
for all
w\in W
.
Furthermore,
f^{*}
is a maximum flow since
\val(f^{*})=\val(f)
and any flow in the transshipment instance can be directly translated to an
s
-
t
-flow

of the same value as follows:

f^{\prime}(a)\coloneqq\begin{cases}f^{*}_{\Sigma}(u)&\text{if }a=(s,u)\text{ for some }u\in U\\
f^{*}(a)&\text{if }a\in A(D)\\
-f^{*}_{\Sigma}(w)&\text{if }a=(w,t)\text{ for some }w\in W.\end{cases}

(
2
): We apply
\cref
lem:bgt_to_gf_reduction and obtain a
gf
instance
(G,\mathcal{B}^{\prime})
which has gap exactly one by
\cref
lem:bgt_to_gf_reduction (iii).
Using an algorithm from
10
we can now find a maximum
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
-factor
F
of
G
in polynomial time.
In particular,
\cref
lem:bgt_to_gf_reduction gives us a
\mathcal{B}
-transshipment of value
\frac{|F|}{2}
and implies that this is indeed the maximum.
On the other hand, if there is no solution to
(G,\mathcal{B}^{\prime})
, then there is also no feasible flow for
(D,\capacity,\mathcal{B})
.

(
3
): We again apply
\cref
lem:bgt_to_gf_reduction and obtain a
gf
instance
(G,\mathcal{B}^{\prime})
.
By
\cref
lem:bgt_to_gf_reduction (
2
), we get a tree decomposition for
G
of width at most
\tw+1
and, by construction of the

instance, we know that
\max\{\max B^{\prime}\mid B^{\prime}\in\mathcal{B}^{\prime}\}\leq M
(note that we can assume

since otherwise we are in case (

) which is polynomial-time solvable).
This allows us to apply a result from
17
, which states that a maximum
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
-factor of

can be computed in

If there is no solution to

.
∎

\tau
-Bounded-Density Edge Deletion.

In
\cref
ssec:transshipment we described how to reduce
tbded
to
rflow
.
Therefore, the results from
\Cref
thm:bgt_algorithms for
rflow
translate directly to

and imply the algorithmic results in
\cref
thm:dichotomy,thm:fpt-tw:

Theorem 12
.

Conclusion

We provided a complexity dichotomy of
tbded
with respect to the target density
\tau
by showing that the problem is polynomial-time solvable if and only if
\tau
is half-integral or smaller than
\nicefrac{{2}}{{3}}
(assuming P
{}\neq{}

We also observed an encouraging result regarding the treewidth of the input graph.
Studying
tbded

Another next step is to analyze cases in which

is not constant but can depend on e.g.

.
Our polynomial-time algorithms for (half-)integral target densities generalize to this setting.
However, our hardness results and the FPT algorithm for treewidth do not.
It is known that for some value

[

]
and it is polynomial-time solvable for

since this is equivalent to computing a feedback edge set of the input graph.
Another generalization our polynomial-time algorithms cover are weighted problem variants of

(

Concerning

For example, the NP-hardness for the case that only some arcs have an even flow restriction requires to “stack” several of them on paths:

Replicated liquid theory in 1 + ∞ 1+\infty dimensions
Yukihiro Tomita, Hajime Yoshino

Abstract
We develop a replicated liquid theory for structural glasses which exhibit spatial variation of physical quantities along one axis, say z z -axis. The theory becomes exact with infinite transverse dimension d − 1 → ∞ d-1\to\infty . It provides an exact free-energy functional with space-dependent glass order parameter Δ a ​ b ​ ( z ) \Delta_{ab}(z) . As a first application of the scheme, we study diverging lengths associated with dynamic/static glass transitions of hardspheres with/without confining cavity. The exponents agree with those obtained in previous studies on related mean-field models. Moreover, it predicts a non-trivial spatial profile of the glass order parameter Δ a ​ b ​ ( z ) \Delta_{ab}(z) within the cavity which exhibits a scaling feature approaching the dynamical glass transition.

IV.2.3
Spatial profile of the glass order parameter around the dynamical glass transition

We have seen that by moving far from the edge of the cavity the value of the order parameter

obtained analyzing

as a function of

IV.2.5
Summary of the behavior of the system close to the dynamical transition point

To summarize, we found three length scales i) the point-to-set length
\xi_{\rm d}^{\rm PS}
, ii) the correlation length of thermal fluctuation

\xi_{\rm d}^{\rm hessian}
and iii) the characteristic length of the spatial profile of the glass order parameter in the cavity

\xi_{\rm d}^{\rm profile}
all scales as
\epsilon^{-1/4}
approaching the dynamical glass transition point
\hat{\varphi}_{\rm d}
.
The so called
\chi_{4}
is just the spatial integral of the correlation function

\langle\delta\Delta(\hat{z}_{1})\delta\Delta(\hat{z}_{2})\rangle
(see Eq. (
32
)) so that
it is directly related to
\xi_{\rm d}^{\rm hessian}
. Our result confirms that the point-to-set length is proportional to the correlation length.

As far as we are aware of, the fact that spatial profile of the glass order parameter
\Delta(\hat{z})
also reflect a correlation length

\xi_{\rm d}^{\rm profile}
has not been appreciated in the context of glass physics (see however
berthier2012static
).
As shown in Fig.
3
(c) , we find
\xi_{\rm d}^{\rm profile}=\xi_{\rm d}^{\rm hessian}
.
The situation appears very similar to the surface critical phenomena, for example of ferromagnets,
where one observe that the spatial profile of the order parameter reflects spatial correlation length of spontaneous thermal fluctuations

binder1972phase
;
kikuchi1985monte1
.

IV.2.6
Cavity size dependence of the Kauzmann transition

Finally let us focus on the Kauzmann transition in the cavity system.
The complexity (structural entropy)
\Sigma
can be computed using the free-energy
Eq. (
36
)
as explained in sec.
E.2
.

We find

\displaystyle\Sigma=\frac{d}{2}\quantity[\ln{d}-\hat{\varphi}\qty(2-f(\hat{L}_{\rm cav}))].

(50)

with

f(L)=1-\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\hat{L}_{\rm{cav}}^{-1}+O\quantity(e^{-\hat{L}_{\rm{cav}}^{2}/2}).

(51)

The Kauzmann transition density of the cavity system obtained at
\Sigma=0
is

\displaystyle\hat{\varphi}_{K}(\hat{L})=\frac{\hat{\varphi}_{K,\rm{bulk}}}{2-f(\hat{L})}=\hat{\varphi}_{K,\rm{bulk}}\left(1+\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\hat{L}_{\rm cav}^{-1}+O\quantity(e^{-\hat{L}_{\rm{cav}}^{2}/2})\right)

(52)

where
\hat{\varphi}_{K,\rm{bulk}}
is the Kauzmann transition density for the bulk system
\hat{L}_{\rm cav}=\infty
.
Thus in cavity systems the Kauzmann transition occurs at lower densities than in bulk systems and
the transition density increases increasing the cavity size
\hat{L}_{\rm cav}
.
Physically this can be understood again as the consequence of the constraint
imposed by the frozen system outside the cavity.

From this result we defined the point-to-set (PS) length
of the static transition
\xi^{\rm PS}_{\rm s}(\hat{\varphi})
as

\xi^{\rm PS}_{\rm s}(\hat{\varphi}_{\rm K}(\hat{L}_{\rm cav}))=\hat{L}_{\rm cav}/2

(53)

treating
\hat{L}_{\rm cav}
as a running parameter.
We find

\xi^{\rm PS}_{\rm s}\propto(\hat{\varphi}-\hat{\varphi}_{\rm K,bulk})^{-1}

(54)

V
Conclusion and outlook

To conclude we constructed a framework of the inhomogeneous replicated liquid theory that can treat glasses
whose physical properties evolves along a one-dimensional axis. The theory becomes exact in the limit of infinite transverse dimensions.
We successfully applied the scheme to analyze diverging length scales at dynamic/static glass transitions.

There are numerous directions to which our theory can be extended.
Extension of the theory to describe
2
,
3
dimensional inhomogeneities is straight forward.
It is straight forward to adapt the glass state following
rainone2015following
scheme
in our setup. It will be particularly interesting to apply such a scheme to study
emergence of spatial inhomogeneities in amorphous solids under compression, shear e.t.c.
approaching yielding and jamming.

A.1
Basic setup

Here we develop a density functional expression of the free-energy of the system of
N
particles given by the Hamiltonian Eq. (
1
).
Let us write the number density (per unit length), which varies along
z
as,

\hat{\rho}_{\rm micro}(z)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta(z-z_{i}),

(55)

and introduce an identity,

1=\int{\cal D}\hat{\rho}(z)\delta(\hat{\rho}(z)-\hat{\rho}_{\rm micro}(z))=\int{\cal D}\hat{\rho}(z){\cal D}\phi(z)\exp\left[\int dz\phi(z)(\hat{\rho}(z)-\hat{\rho}_{\rm micro}(z))\right]

(56)

where
{\cal D}\hat{\rho}(z)
is a functional integration over
\hat{\rho}(z)
and
\delta(...)
is a functional delta.
In the 2nd equation we introduced an integral representation of the functional delta introducing a function
\phi(z)

which can be related to the so called intrinsic chemical potential
hansen1990theory
.

Then the free-energy of the system can be written as

-\beta F=\ln Z\qquad Z=\frac{1}{N!}\int\prod_{i=1}^{N}\frac{d^{d-1}x_{i}}{\lambda_{\rm th}^{d-1}}\frac{dz_{i}}{\lambda_{\rm th}}e^{-\beta\sum_{i<j}v(r_{ij})-\beta\sum_{i=1}^{N}U(z_{i})}=\int{\cal D}\hat{\rho}(z)e^{-\beta F[\hat{\rho}]}

(57)

where we introduced a free-energy functional
F[\hat{\rho}]
,

e^{-\beta F[\hat{\rho}]}=\int{\cal D}\phi(z)e^{\int dz\phi(z)\hat{\rho}(z)}e^{-\beta G[\phi]}

(58)

and a free-energy functional
G[\phi]
,

\displaystyle e^{-\beta G[\phi]}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N!}\int\prod_{i=1}^{N}d^{d-1}x_{i}dz_{i}\prod_{i=1}^{N}a(z_{i})\prod_{i<j}(1+\lambda f(r_{ij}))

(59)

Note that
F[\hat{\rho}]
and
G[\phi]
are related to each other through Eq. (
58
) which is a Legendre transformation.
We anticipate that the functional integration in Eq. (
58
) can be done by the saddle point method for
N\gg 1
which yields

-\beta F[\hat{\rho}]=\int dz\phi^{*}(z)\hat{\rho}(z)-\beta G[\phi^{*}]

(60)

with the saddle point
\phi^{*}(z)=\phi^{*}[\hat{\rho}](z)
is determined by

\hat{\rho}(z)=\left.\frac{\delta(-\beta G[\phi])}{\delta(-\phi(z))}\right|_{\phi=\phi^{*}[\hat{\rho}]}

(61)

In Eq. (
59
) we also introduced the Mayer function,

f(r)=e^{-\beta v(r)}-1

(62)

and the ’activity’,

a(z)=\frac{e^{-\phi(z)-\beta U(z)}}{\lambda_{\rm th}^{d}}

(63)

In Eq. (
59
) we also introduced a parameter
\lambda
to organize the Mayer expansion discussed below. It will be put back to
1
after organizing
the expansion.

Now we evaluate
G[\phi]
treating the effect of interactions perturbatively, i. e. Mayer expansion.

e^{-\beta G[\phi]}=e^{-\beta G_{0}[\phi]}[1+\lambda\sum_{i<j}\langle f(r_{ij})\rangle_{\phi}+O(\lambda^{2})]

(64)

Here
-\beta G_{0}[\phi]
is the free-energy of the ideal gas which is obtained using the Stirling’s formula
\ln N!\sim N\ln N-N
,

-\beta G_{0}[\phi]=-N\ln N+N+N\ln\left[S\int dza(z)\right]

(65)

with
S=\int d^{d-1}x
being the surface area of the cross-section (See Fig.
1
).
We also introduced

\langle\ldots\rangle_{\phi}=\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{N}\int d^{d-1}x_{i}dz_{i}a(z_{i})\ldots}{\prod_{i=1}^{N}\int d^{d-1}x_{i}dz_{i}a(z_{i})}

(66)

which is the thermal average took within the non-interacting system.

The outline of the analysis goes as follows. We consider series expansions,

\displaystyle\phi^{*}=\phi^{*}_{0}+\lambda\phi^{*}_{1}+\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2}\phi^{*}_{2}+\ldots\qquad G=G_{0}+\lambda G_{1}+\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2}G_{2}+\ldots\qquad F=F_{0}+\lambda F_{1}+\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2}F_{2}+\ldots

(67)

The series for
G
can be determined analyzing Eq. (
64
). Using the result into
Eq. (
61
) the series for
\phi^{*}
can be obtained. Finally using these in
Eq. (
67
)
we will obtain the series for
F
. Up to 1st order one can find,

\displaystyle-\beta F_{0}[\hat{\rho}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int dz\phi_{0}^{*}[\hat{\rho}](z)\hat{\rho}(z)-\beta G_{0}[\phi^{*}_{0}[\hat{\rho}]]

(68)

\displaystyle-\beta F_{1}[\hat{\rho}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\beta G_{1}[\phi^{*}_{0}[\hat{\rho}]]

(69)

To work out
\phi_{0}^{*}
explicitly we use Eq. (
65
) in Eq. (
61
) and find,

\hat{\rho}(z)=N\frac{a^{*}(z)}{\int dza^{*}(z)}\qquad a^{*}(z)=\frac{e^{-\phi^{*}(z)-\beta U(z)}}{\lambda_{\rm th}^{d}}.

(70)

Using this in Eq. (
68
) we find the ideal-gas part or the entropic part of the free-energy as,

-\beta F_{0}[\hat{\rho}]=\int dz\hat{\rho}(z)\left[1-\ln(\lambda_{\rm th}^{d}\frac{\hat{\rho}(z)}{S})\right]+\int dz\hat{\rho}(z)(-\beta U(z))

(71)

Now let us consider the effect of interactions. From Eq. (
64
) we find,

-\beta G_{1}[\phi]=\sum_{i<j}\langle f(r_{ij})\rangle_{\phi}=\frac{N(N-1)}{2}\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{2}\int d^{d-1}x_{i}dz_{i}a(z_{i})f(r_{12})}{\prod_{i=1}^{2}\int d^{d-1}x_{i}dz_{i}a(z_{i})}

(72)

Using this in Eq. (
69
) we find,

-\beta F_{1}[\hat{\rho}]=\frac{1}{2}\int dz_{1}dz_{2}\hat{\rho}(z_{1})\hat{\rho}(z_{2})\int\frac{d^{d-1}x_{1}}{S}\int\frac{d^{d-1}x_{2}}{S}f(r_{12})

(73)

Collecting the above results we obtain up to 1st order in the Mayer-expansion,

\displaystyle\frac{-\beta F[\rho]}{S}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int dz\rho(z)\left[1-\ln(\lambda_{\rm th}^{d}\rho(z))\right]+\int dz\rho(z)(-\beta U(z))

(74)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int dz_{1}dz_{2}\rho(z_{1})\rho(z_{2})\frac{1}{S}\int d^{d-1}x_{1}\int d^{d-1}x_{2}f(r_{12})

where we introduced

\rho(z)=\frac{\hat{\rho}(z)}{S}.

(75)

which is the number density field per unit volume.
Note that
\rho(z)
must be normalized such that,

S\int dz\rho(z)=N

(76)

Finally the thermodynamic free-energy
F
can be obtained through Eq. (
57
) where the functional integral
can be evaluated by the saddle point method for
N\gg 1
,

F=F[\rho^{*}]\qquad 0=\left.\frac{\delta}{\delta\rho(z)}\left\{\frac{-\beta F[\rho]}{S}+\beta\mu\left(\int dz\rho(z)-\frac{N}{S}\right)\right\}\right|_{\rho=\rho^{*}}

(77)

Here we introduced a Lagrange multiplier
\beta\mu
to impose the normalization condition Eq. (
76
)
where
\mu
can be regarded as the chemical potential.

As is well known
hansen1990theory
, the contributions from higher order terms in the Mayer expansion
into the free-energy
F[\rho]
can be represented by one-particle irreducible diagrams.
It has been shown that they become negligible in the large dimensional limit
d\to\infty

frisch1985classical
;
PhysRevA.36.2422
;
1130848328245474560
.

We consider two-body potential

A.3

Let us discuss compression (or decompression) of our system.

\displaystyle-\beta F(\eta)

\displaystyle\ln\frac{1}{N!}\int_{V(\eta)}\prod_{i=1}^{N}\frac{d^{d-1}(x_{i})^{\prime}}{\lambda_{\rm th}^{d-1}}\frac{dz^{\prime}_{i}}{\lambda_{\rm th}}e^{-\beta\sum_{i<j}v(r^{\prime}_{ij})-\beta\sum_{i=1}^{N}U(z^{\prime}_{i})}

\displaystyle\ln\frac{1}{N!}\underbrace{\left(1-\frac{\eta}{d}\right)^{d}}_{\mbox{$e^{-\eta}$ in $d\to\infty$}}\int_{V(0)}\prod_{i=1}^{N}\frac{d^{d-1}x_{i}}{\lambda_{\rm th}^{d-1}}\frac{dz_{i}}{\lambda_{\rm th}}e^{-\beta\sum_{i<j}v(r_{ij}(1-\eta/d))-\beta\sum_{i=1}^{N}U(z_{i}(1-\eta/d))}

\displaystyle-\frac{\beta F[\rho,\eta]}{SD/\sqrt{d}}

\displaystyle\int d\hat{z}\rho(\hat{z})\left[1-\eta-\ln\left(\lambda_{\rm th}^{d}\rho(\hat{z})\right)\right]+\int d\hat{z}\hat{\rho}(\hat{z})(-\beta U(\hat{z}(1-\eta/d)))

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{d}{2}\frac{\Omega_{d}}{d}D^{d}\int d\hat{z}_{1}\rho(\hat{z}_{1})\int d\hat{z}_{2}\rho(\hat{z}_{2})\frac{e^{-\frac{(\hat{z}_{1}-\hat{z}_{2})^{2}}{2}}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\xi e^{\xi}f\left[D^{2}\left(1+\frac{\xi}{d}-\frac{\eta}{d}\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle p=\frac{\beta P}{\rho}=\frac{\partial}{\partial\eta}\frac{\beta F}{N}=-\frac{\partial}{\partial\eta}\frac{-\beta F}{SD/\sqrt{d}\int d\hat{z}\rho(\hat{(}z))}

F.1
Asymptotic behavior of the error function

The err function
\erf(x)
is an odd function,

\displaystyle\erf(x)=\frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{0}^{x}dte^{-t^{2}}=-\erf(-x).

(221)

The behavior of the error function at
x\to\infty
is

\displaystyle\erf(x)=1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\frac{e^{-x^{2}}}{x}\quantity(1-\frac{1}{2x^{2}}+\frac{3}{(2x^{2})^{2}}+\cdots).

(222)

This can be proved as follows.

\displaystyle\erf(x)=\frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{0}^{x}dte^{-t^{2}}=\frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}\quantity(\int_{0}^{\infty}dt e^{-t^2}-\int_{x}^{\infty}dt e^{-t^2})=1-\frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{x}^{\infty}dte^{-t^{2}}

(223)

With
\partial_{x}e^{-x^{2}}=-2xe^{-x^{2}}
, for the second term,

\displaystyle\int_{x}^{\infty}dte^{-t^{2}}=\int_{x}^{\infty}dt\frac{-t^{-1}}{2}\partial_{t}e^{-t^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\quantity[\frac{-t^{-1}e^{-t^2}}{2}]_{x}^{\infty}-\int_{x}^{\infty}dt\frac{t^{-2}e^{-t^{2}}}{2}=\frac{x^{-1}e^{-x^{2}}}{2}+\int_{x}^{\infty}dt\frac{t^{-3}}{4}\partial_{t}e^{-t^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{x^{-1}e^{-x^{2}}}{2}-\frac{x^{-3}e^{-x^{2}}}{4}+\int_{x}^{\infty}dt\frac{3t^{-4}}{4}e^{-t^{2}}=\frac{x^{-1}e^{-x^{2}}}{2}-\frac{x^{-3}e^{-x^{2}}}{4}-\int_{x}^{\infty}dt\frac{3t^{-5}}{8}\partial_{t}e^{-t^{2}}

\displaystyle=

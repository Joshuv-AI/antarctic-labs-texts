Abstract
. In this paper, we study the conjugate phase retrieval for complex-valued signals residing on graphs, and explore its applications to shift-invariant spaces. Given a complex-valued graph signal 𝐟 \bf f residing on the graph 𝒢 \mathcal{G} , we introduce a graph 𝒢 𝐟 {\mathcal{G}}_{\bf f} and show that its connectivity is sufficient to determine 𝐟 \bf f up to a global unimodular constant and conjugation. We then construct two explicit graph models and show that graph signals residing on them can be recovered, up to a unimodular constant and conjugation, from its absolute values on the vertices and the relative magnitudes between neighboring vertices. Building on this graph-based framework, we apply our results to shift-invariant spaces generated by real-valued functions. For signals in the Paley-Wiener space, we show that any complex-valued function can be recovered, up to a unimodular constant and conjugation, from structured phaseless samples taken at three times the Nyquist rate. For more general shift-invariant spaces, we establish the conjugate phase retrievability of signals from phaseless samples collected on a discrete sampling set, in conjunction with relative magnitude measurements between neighboring sample points. Two numerical reconstruction algorithms are introduced to recover the signals in the Paley-Wiener space and general shift-invariant spaces, up to a unimodular constant and conjugation, from the given phaseless measurements.

2.
Conjugate phase retrieval of complex-valued signals residing on the graphs

Phase retrieval for vector fields on graphs has been discussed in
[
15
]
. The authors there proposed a new paradigm of the phase retrieval, that is the determination of a vector field residing on the graph
\mathcal{G}=(V,E)
, up to an orthogonal matrix, where
V
is the vertex set and
E
is the edge set. They showed that phase retrieval is possible for the vector field
\mathbf{f}_{n},n\in V
residing on the graph
\mathcal{G}=(V,E)
from the absolute magnitudes
\|\mathbf{f}_{n}\|_{2},n\in V
and relative magnitudes
\|\mathbf{f}_{n}-\mathbf{f}_{m}\|_{2},(n,m)\in E
, if the graph satisfies some conditions. For example, if
\mathcal{G}_{C}=(V_{C},E_{C})
is a complete graph, then phase retrieval is possible for the vector field
\mathbf{f}_{n},n\in V_{C}
from the the absolute magnitudes
\|\mathbf{f}_{n}\|_{2},n\in V_{C}
and relative magnitudes
\|\mathbf{f}_{n}-\mathbf{f}_{m}\|_{2},(n,m)\in E_{C}
, see the following Proposition.

Proposition 2.1
.

[
15
, Theorem 4.1]

Let
\mathcal{G}_{C}=(V_{C},E_{C})
be a complete graph, and
\mathbf{f}=(\mathbf{f}_{n})_{n\in V_{C}},\mathbf{f}_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a vector field on the vertex
V_{C}
. Then
\mathbf{f}
can be determined, up to a

orthogonal matrix, from the absolute magnitudes
\|\mathbf{f}_{n}\|_{2},n\in V_{C}
and relative magnitudes
\|\mathbf{f}_{n}-\mathbf{f}_{m}\|_{2},(n,m)\in E_{C}
.

Let
{\mathcal{G}}_{C}=(V_{C},E_{C})
be a complete graph with vertex set
V_{C}
and edge set
E_{C}
, and
{\bf f}=({\bf f}_{n})_{n\in V_{C}}\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times V_{C}}
be a vector field residing on the graph

. Combine with Proposition

, one may easily verify that the vector field

Define the isomorphism

\displaystyle M:\ {\mathcal{C}}\ni f\longmapsto\begin{pmatrix}\Re f\\
\Im f\end{pmatrix}\in\Re({\mathcal{C}})\times\Re({\mathcal{C}})

Remark 2.3

Let
\mathcal{G}=(V,E)

For a complete subgraph

3.
Conjugate phase retrieval in the Paley-Wiener space

The classical Whittaker-Shannon-Kotel’nikov (WSK) sampling theorem states that any function in the Paley-Wiener space can be exactly reconstructed from its samples taken at the Nyquist rate or higher. Specifically,

\displaystyle f(x)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}f(\frac{n}{2B})sinc(2Bx-n),\ \forall x\in\mathbb{R},

where
f\in PW_{B}^{2}
in (
1.2
).
This reconstruction formula remains valid under uniform shifts of the sampling lattice. That is, for any fixed

x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
, we have the equivalent representation:

(3.1)

\displaystyle f(x)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}f(x_{0}+\frac{n}{2B})sinc(2B(x-x_{0})-n),\ \forall x\in\mathbb{R},

where
f\in PW_{B}^{2}
in (
1.2
). For the reader’s convenience, we provide the proof of (
3.1
) in Lemma
A.1
.

In
[
31
]
, the authors studied the determination of the complex-valued bandlimited signals, up to a unimodular constant and conjugation, from the magnitudes of structured convolutions
|f|
and
|f(\cdot+c)-f|
for some constant
c>0
, see below.

Lemma 3.1
.

[
31
]

Let
f\in PW^{2}_{B}
be as in (
1.2
) and
0<c\leq\frac{1}{2B}
. Then
f
can be determined, up to a unimodular constant and conjugation, from its absolute magnitudes
|f(x)|,x\in\mathbb{R}
and relative magnitudes
|f(x+c)-f(x)|,x\in\mathbb{R}
.

From above lemma, we know that
f\in PW_{B}^{2}
can be determined, up to a unimodular constant and conjugation, from the structured magnitudes
|f|
and
|f(\cdot+c)-f(\cdot)|
on the whole real line. Then one may wonder if the conjugate phase retrieval of the bandlimited signals is possible from the phaseless samples taken on a discrete set. Combine the WSK sampling theorem with the observation that
|f|^{2}\in PW_{2B}^{1}\subset PW_{2B}^{2}
and
|f(\cdot+c)-f(\cdot)|^{2}\in PW_{2B}^{1}\subset PW_{2B}^{2}
for any constant
c\in\mathbb{R}
with
f\in PW_{B}^{2}
, we immediately have the following theorem.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
f\in PW_{B}^{2}
be as in (
1.2
) and
0<c\leq\frac{1}{2B}
. Then
f
can be determined, up to a unimodular constant and conjugation, from its absolute magnitudes
|f(\frac{n}{4B})|
and relative magnitudes
|f(\frac{n}{4B}+c)-f(\frac{n}{4B})|,n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

If
f=0
, it is trivial. For a nonzero signal
f\in PW_{B}^{2}
, let

g\in PW_{B}^{2}
satisfy

(3.2)

\displaystyle|f(\frac{n}{4B})|=|g(\frac{n}{4B})|\text{ and }|f(\frac{n}{4B}+c)-f(\frac{n}{4B})|=|g(\frac{n}{4B}+c)-g(\frac{n}{4B})|,n\in\mathbb{Z}.

One may easily verify that
|f|^{2}\in PW_{2B}^{1}\subset PW_{2B}^{2}
and
|f(\cdot+c)-f(\cdot)|^{2}\in PW_{2B}^{1}\subset PW_{2B}^{2}
. By the WSK sampling theorem, we know that
|f|
and
|f(\cdot+c)-f(\cdot)|
can be determined from
|f(\frac{n}{4B})|
and
|f(\frac{n}{4B}+c)-f(\frac{n}{4B})|,n\in\mathbb{Z}
, respectively. Combine this with (
3.2
), we have

\displaystyle|f(x)|=|g(x)|,|f(x+c)-f(x)|=|g(x+c)-g(x)|,x\in\mathbb{R}.

By Lemma
3.1
, we have

\displaystyle f=e^{i\alpha}g\text{ or }f=e^{i\alpha}\overline{g},\text{ for some }\alpha\in\mathbb{R},

which completes the proof.
∎

For a complex-valued signal
f\in PW_{B}^{2}
in (
1.2
), Theorem
3.2
shows that it can be determined, up to a unimodular constant and conjugation, from its phaseless samples
|f(\frac{n}{4B})|
and
|f(\frac{n}{4B}+c)-f(\frac{n}{4B})|,n\in\mathbb{Z}
with four times the Nyquist rate. In Theorems
2.4
and
2.5
, we see that conjugate phase retrieval is possible for the graph signal with the absolute magnitudes on the vertices and relative magnitudes on the edges. We then apply these theorems to the function in the Paley-Wiener space, assuming that the graph signal residing on the graph is the function values. First, we explore the graph structure with two reference points in Theorem
2.4
, and show that conjugate phase retrieval is possible with the structured phaseless samples at three times the Nyquist rate.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
f\in PW_{B}^{2}
in (
1.2
) be a complex-valued function that is not real-valued up to a unimodular constant and
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
be so chosen that
f(x_{0}+\frac{k}{2B})
and
f(x_{0}+\frac{l}{2B})
are non-collinear for some
k\neq l\in\mathbb{Z}
, that is,

\displaystyle f(x_{0}+\frac{k}{2B})\overline{f(x_{0}+\frac{l}{2B})}\neq f(x_{0}+\frac{l}{2B})\overline{f(x_{0}+\frac{k}{2B})}.

Then
f
can be determined, up to a unimodular constant and conjugation, from absolute phaseless samples
|f(x_{0}+\frac{n}{2B})|,n\in\mathbb{Z}
and relative phaseless samples
|f(x_{0}+\frac{n}{2B})-f(x_{0}+\frac{k}{2B})|,|f(x_{0}+\frac{n}{2B})-f(x_{0}+\frac{l}{2B})|,n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

In the above theorem, a sampling scheme with three times the Nyquist rate has been proposed, and we show that conjugate phase retrieval is possible for the signal
f\in PW_{B}^{2}
with structured phaseless samples. Specifically, if there exist two reference sampling points such that their corresponding function values are non-collinear, then
f\in PW_{B}^{2}
can be determined, up to a unimodular constant and conjugation, from the phaseless samples on the sampling set and relative phaseless samples with respect to
the reference points, cf. Theorem
2.4
. Next, we will explore the circulant graph structure in Theorem
2.5
, and show that the phaseless samples on the sampling set and relative phaseless samples with respect to the first-order neighbors and seconde-order neighbors are sufficient for the conjugate phase retrieval of complex-valued bandlimited signals.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
f\in PW_{B}^{2}
in (
1.2
) be any complex-valued function and
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
be so chosen that

(3.3)

\displaystyle f(x_{0}+\frac{n+1}{2B})\overline{f(x_{0}+\frac{n}{2B})}\neq f(x_{0}+\frac{n}{2B})\overline{f(x_{0}+\frac{n+1}{2B})}

for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then
f
can be determined, up to a unimodular constant and conjugation, from absolute phaseless samples
|f(x_{0}+\frac{n}{2B})|,n\in\mathbb{Z}
and relative phaseless samples
|f(x_{0}+\frac{n+1}{2B})-f(x_{0}+\frac{n}{2B})|,|f(x_{0}+\frac{n+2}{2B})-f(x_{0}+\frac{n}{2B})|,n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Remark 3.5
.

The condition (
3.3
) holds for almost every
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
.
In fact, fix

and let

be any nonzero signal in the Paley-Wiener space, then

is the restriction of an entire function to the real axis, and its zeros are either
\mathbb{R}
or a countable set. Since

is a nonzero function, we obtain that the zeros of

are countable.

4.
Conjugate phase retrieval in shift-invariant spaces

In order to complement existing research on conjugate phase retrieval in shift-invariant spaces generated by Gaussian-type functions or the sinc function, both of which rely on the theory of entire functions of finite order, we consider a broader setting. Specifically, we study conjugate phase retrieval in shift-invariant spaces generated by a real-valued, continuous generator, without relying on any analytic properties of the function. This general framework allows us to go beyond the classical analytic-based methods and address a wider class of shift-invariant spaces.

Let
V^{p}(\phi)
be the shift-invariant space generated by a real function
\phi
in (
1.1
). For a function
f\in V^{p}(\phi)
, if the available phaseless samples are located on the vertices and edges of the graphs proposed in Theorems
2.4
and
2.5
, the feasibility of conjugate phase retrieval of the function
f
can be inferred from the structural phaseless samples established in Section
2
. However, these results do not provide an explicit reconstruction procedure for recovering the signal, and the phase propagation is depending on the construction of the
{\mathcal{G}}_{\bf f}
. To address this, in Section
4
, we introduce an alternative approach that not only establishes the theoretical foundation for conjugate phase retrieval in shift-invariant spaces but also leads to a concrete reconstruction algorithm, see Algorithm
1
. This approach is further extended to the Paley-Wiener space, where a corresponding reconstruction procedure is developed, see Algorithm
2
.

A key technical component of our analysis is a geometric condition ensuring that a complex vector in
\mathbb{C}^{3}
can be uniquely determined, up to a unimodular constant and conjugation, from its magnitudes and relative magnitudes, see below.

Proposition 4.1
.

A complex-valued vector
x=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in\mathbb{C}^{3}
can be determined, up to a unimodular constant and conjugation, from absolute magnitudes
|x_{n}|,n\in\{1,2,3\}
and relative magnitudes
|x_{n}-x_{m}|,(n,m)\in\{1,2,3\}^{2}
.

Proof.

If
x=0
, it is trivial.

For a nonzero vector
x
,
let
y=(y_{1},y_{2},y_{3})\in\mathbb{C}^{3}
satisfy

(4.1)

\displaystyle|x_{n}|=|y_{n}|,n\in\{1,2,3\}

and

\displaystyle|x_{n}-x_{m}|=|y_{n}-y_{m}|,(n,m)\in\{1,2,3\}^{2}.

Then

(4.2)

\displaystyle\Re x_{n}\Re x_{m}+\Im x_{n}\Im x_{m}=\Re y_{n}\Re y_{m}+\Im y_{n}\Im y_{m},(n,m)\in\{1,2,3\}^{2}.

We divide the proof into two parts (a) and (b).

(a) If
x
is real-valued up to a unimodular constant, we may, without loss of generality, assume
x_{1}\neq 0
and further assume
x_{1}=y_{1}>0
, otherwise replacing
x
by
e^{i\alpha_{1}}x
and
y
by
e^{i\alpha_{2}}y
respectively, where
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
. Then
x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
, and we can obtain
x_{2}=y_{2}
and
x_{3}=y_{3}
by (
4.2
). Thus,
x=y
, which implies
x
is determined, up to a unimodular constant.

(b) If
x
is not real-valued up to a unimodular constant, then there exist
k,l\in\{1,2,3\}
such that
x_{k}
and
x_{l}
are non-collinear, that is
x_{k}\overline{x_{l}}\neq x_{l}\overline{x_{k}}
. Without loss of generality, assume
x_{1}\overline{x_{2}}\neq x_{2}\overline{x_{1}}
, then
x_{1},x_{2}\neq 0
and

(4.3)

\displaystyle\Re x_{1}\Im x_{2}\neq\Re x_{2}\Im x_{1}.

Without loss of generality, assume
x_{1}=y_{1}>0
, otherwise replacing
x
by
e^{i\alpha_{1}}x
and
y
by
e^{i\alpha_{2}}y
respectively, where
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
.
By (
4.2
), we have
\Re x_{2}=\Re y_{2}
and then
\Im x_{2}=\tau_{2}\Im y_{2}\neq 0
for some
\tau_{2}=\pm 1
by (
4.1
) and (
4.3
). By (
4.2
), we have

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(\Re x_{3}-\Re y_{3})\Re x_{2}+(\Im x_{3}-\tau_{2}\Im y_{3})\Im x_{2}&=0\\
(\Re x_{3}-\Re y_{3})\Re x_{1}+(\Im x_{3}-\tau_{2}\Im y_{3})\Im x_{1}&=0\end{array}\right.

Together with (
4.3
), we have
\Re x_{3}=\Re y_{3}
and
\Im x_{3}=\tau_{2}\Im y_{3}
. Thus, we have

\displaystyle x=y\text{ or }x=\overline{y},

which implies
x
is determined, up to a unimodular constant and conjugation.
∎

Proposition
4.1
aligns with Proposition
2.1
with
d=2
. For the remainder of this discussion, we will primarily leverage part (b) in the proof of Proposition
4.1
, under the condition that the two involved points are non-collinear (meaning they are not aligned with the origin).

First, inspired by the the graph structure with two reference points in Theorem
2.4
, we show that conjugate phase retrieval is possible
for complex-valued signal
f\in V^{p}(\phi)
, from the absolute magnitude samples
|f(x_{n})|,n\in\mathbb{Z}
together with the relative magnitude samples
|f(x_{n})-f(x_{q})|,q\in\{k,l\},n\in\mathbb{Z}
, where
X=\{x_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a set of sampling for
V^{p}(\phi)
satisfying (
1.3
), and
k\neq l\in\mathbb{Z}
are so chosen that
f(x_{k})
and
f(x_{l})
are non-collinear, that is,

(4.4)

\displaystyle f(x_{k})\overline{f(x_{l})}\neq f(x_{l})\overline{f(x_{k})}.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
f\in V^{p}(\phi)
be a complex-valued function that is not real-valued up to a unimodular constant, and
X=\{x_{n}:n\in\mathbb{Z}\}
be a set of sampling for
V^{p}(\phi)
satisfying (
1.3
). Then
f
can be determined, up to a unimodular constant and conjugation, from absolute magnitude samples
|f(x_{n})|,x_{n}\in X
and relative magnitude samples
|f(x_{n})-f(x_{k})|,|f(x_{n})-f(x_{l})|,x_{n}\in X
, where
k\neq l
are so chosen that (
4.4
) holds.

Proof.

Let
g\in V^{p}(\phi)
satisfy

(4.5)

\displaystyle|f(x_{n})|=|g(x_{n})|,

\displaystyle|f(x_{n})-f(x_{q})|=|g(x_{n})-g(x_{q})|,q\in\{k,l\}

for all
x_{n}\in X
.
Then

(4.6)

\displaystyle\Re f(x_{n})\Re f(x_{q})+\Im f(x_{n})\Im f(x_{q})=\Re g(x_{n})\Re g(x_{q})+\Im g(x_{n})\Im g(x_{q})

for all
x_{n}\in X
and
q\in\{k,l\}
.
By (
4.4
), we have

(4.7)

\displaystyle\Re f(x_{k})\Im f(x_{l})\neq\Re f(x_{l})\Im f(x_{k}).

Obviously,
f
is nonzero. In fact, we observe
f(x_{k})\neq 0
and
f(x_{l})\neq 0
from (
4.7
).
Without loss of generality, assume
f(x_{k})=g(x_{k})>0
, otherwise replacing
f
by
e^{i\alpha_{1}}f
and
g
by
e^{i\alpha_{2}}g
respectively, where
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
.

By (
4.6
), we have
\Re f(x_{l})=\Re g(x_{l})
. Combine this with (
4.5
) and (
4.7
), we have

(4.8)

\displaystyle\Im f(x_{l})=\tau_{l}\Im g(x_{l})\neq 0\text{ for some }\tau_{l}=\pm 1.

Hence
(f(x_{k}),f(x_{l}))=(g(x_{k}),g(x_{l}))
or
(f(x_{k}),f(x_{l}))=\overline{(g(x_{k}),g(x_{l}))}
. Following the similar arguments in part (b) of the proof of Proposition
4.1
, we have

\Re(f(x_{n}),f(x_{k}),f(x_{l}))=\Re(g(x_{n}),g(x_{k}),g(x_{l}))

and

\Im(f(x_{n}),f(x_{k}),f(x_{l}))=\tau_{n}\Im(g(x_{n}),g(x_{k}),g(x_{l}))

for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{k,l\}
, where
\tau_{n}=\pm 1
.
Combine this with (
4.8
), we have
\tau_{n}=\tau_{l}
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{k,l\}
. Therefore

\displaystyle f(x_{n})=g(x_{n})\text{ for all }x_{n}\in X\ \ \text{ or }\ \ f(x_{n})=\overline{g(x_{n})}\text{ for all }x_{n}\in X.

As
X
is a set of sampling for
V^{p}(\phi)
, we obtain

\displaystyle f=g\text{ or }f=\overline{g},

which completes the proof.
∎

Next, we will explore the circulant graph structure in Theorem
2.5
, and show that the absolute magnitudes and relative magnitudes with respect to the first-order
neighbors and seconde-order neighbors are sufficient for the conjugate phase retrieval of complex-valued signal
f\in V^{p}(\phi)
. Specially, in the next theorem, we show that
f\in V^{p}(\phi)
can be determined, up to a unimodular constant and conjugation, from absolute magnitude samples
|f(x_{n})|,x_{n}\in X
, and relative magnitude samples
|f(x_{n+1})-f(x_{n})|,|f(x_{n+2})-f(x_{n})|,x_{n}\in X
, provided that
X=\{x_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a set of sampling for
V^{p}(\phi)
satisfying (
1.3
) and the function values at adjacent sampling points are non-collinear, that is,

(4.9)

\displaystyle f(x_{n+1})\overline{f(x_{n})}\neq f(x_{n})\overline{f(x_{n+1})}

for all
x_{n}\in X
.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
f\in V^{p}(\phi)
be a complex-valued function,
X=\{x_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
be a set of sampling for
V^{p}(\phi)
satisfying (
1.3
) and (
4.9
). Then
f
can be determined, up to a unimodular constant and conjugation, from absolute magnitude samples
|f(x_{n})|,n\in\mathbb{Z}
and relative magnitude samples
|f(x_{n+1})-f(x_{n})|,|f(x_{n+2})-f(x_{n})|,n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

Let
g\in V^{p}(\phi)
satisfy

(4.10)

\displaystyle|f(x_{n})|=|g(x_{n})|,

\displaystyle|f(x_{n+1})-f(x_{n})|=|g(x_{n+1})-g(x_{n})|

and

\displaystyle|f(x_{n+2})-f(x_{n})|=|g(x_{n+2})-g(x_{n})|

for all

.
Then

(4.11)

\displaystyle\Re f(x_{n+1})\Re f(x_{n})+\Im f(x_{n+1})\Im f(x_{n})=\Re g(x_{n+1})\Re g(x_{n})+\Im g(x_{n+1})\Im g(x_{n})

(4.12)

\displaystyle\Re f(x_{n+2})\Re f(x_{n})+\Im f(x_{n+2})\Im f(x_{n})=\Re g(x_{n+2})\Re g(x_{n})+\Im g(x_{n+2})\Im g(x_{n})

for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}

(4.13)

\displaystyle\Re f(x_{n+1})\Im f(x_{n})\neq\Re f(x_{n})\Im f(x_{n+1})

for all

.
Then we know that
f(x_{n})\neq 0
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Without loss of generality, we assume

, otherwise replacing

by

\Im f(x_{0})=\Im g(x_{0})=0

\left\{\hskip 14.45377pt\begin{array}[]{ll}\hskip-14.45377pt\big(\Re f(x_{n_{1}+1})-\Re g(x_{n_{1}+1})\big)\Re f(x_{n_{1}})+\big(\Im f(x_{n_{1}+1})-\tau_{1}\Im g(x_{n_{1}+1})\big)\Im f(x_{n_{1}})&\hskip-7.22743pt=0\\

5.
STFT conjugate phase retrieval in complex Paley-Wiener space

The
short-time Fourier transform (STFT)
is a fundamental tool in time-frequency analysis for a function
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
with a given window function
\psi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{\psi}f(x,w):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(t)\overline{\psi(t-x)}e^{-2\pi itw}dt,\ x,w\in\mathbb{R}.

Our interest lies in recovering a function from STFT magnitudes sampled only in the time domain, that is the frequency component
\omega=0
. In this case, the STFT simplifies to the convolution of the function
f
with the reflected conjugate of the window, that is,

\mathcal{V}_{\psi}f(x,0)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(t)\overline{\psi(t-x)}dt=f\ast\psi^{\#}(x)

where
\psi^{\#}(x)=\overline{\psi(-x)}
.
From measurements of the form
\{|V_{\psi}f(x,0)|:x\in X\}
with a real-valued window function
\psi
,
e^{i\alpha_{1}}f
and
e^{i\alpha_{2}}\overline{f}
are not distinguishable, where
f
is the complex-valued bandlimited function,
X
is a discrete sampling set, and
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
. Analogous to the conjugate phase retrieval of bandlimited function in Section
3
, we explore the conjugate phase retrieval of complex-valued bandlimited functions from the structured STFT magnitudes only measured in the time domain.

In
[
1
]
, the authors proved that a real-valued function in
PW_{B}^{2}
can be determined, up to a sign, from its phaseless STFT samples in the time domain using one window function, with a sampling rate twice the Nyquist rate.
In the following theorem, we show that a complex-valued function in
PW_{B}^{2}
can be determined, up to a unimodular constant and conjugation, from its phaseless STFT samples in the time domain using two structured window functions, with a sampling rate four times the Nyquist rate, which is analogous to Theorem
3.2
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
B>0,0<c\leq\frac{1}{2B}
, the Paley-Wiener space
PW_{B}^{2}
be defined as in (
1.2
) and
\psi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
be a real-valued window such that

(5.1)

(\mathcal{F}\psi)(w)\neq 0,\ \ a.e.\ \ w\in(-B,B).

Then any complex-valued function
f\in PW_{B}^{2}
can be determined, up to a unimodular constant and conjugation, from
|\mathcal{V}_{\psi}f(x_{0}+\frac{n}{4B},0)|,n\in\mathbb{Z}
and
|\mathcal{V}_{\psi(\cdot-c)-\psi(\cdot)}f(x_{0}+\frac{n}{4B},0)|,n\in\mathbb{Z}
, where
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
is any constant.

Proof.

Note that
\mathcal{V}_{\psi}f(x,0)=f\ast\psi^{\#}(x)
and
\mathcal{V}_{\psi(\cdot-c)-\psi(\cdot)}f(x,0)=f\ast\psi^{\#}(x+c)-f\ast\psi^{\#}(x)
for any
x,c\in\mathbb{R}
.
Let
g\in PW_{B}^{2}
satisfy

(5.2)

\displaystyle|f\ast\psi^{\#}(x_{0}+\frac{n}{4B})|=|g\ast\psi^{\#}(x_{0}+\frac{n}{4B})|

and

(5.3)

\displaystyle|f\ast\psi^{\#}(x_{0}\hskip-2.168pt+\hskip-2.168pt\frac{n}{4B}\hskip-2.168pt+\hskip-2.168ptc)\hskip-2.168pt-\hskip-2.168ptf\ast\psi^{\#}(x_{0}\hskip-2.168pt+\hskip-2.168pt\frac{n}{4B})|\hskip-2.168pt=\hskip-2.168pt|g\ast\psi^{\#}(x_{0}\hskip-2.168pt+\hskip-2.168pt\frac{n}{4B}\hskip-2.168pt+\hskip-2.168ptc)\hskip-2.168pt-\hskip-2.168ptg\ast\psi^{\#}(x_{0}\hskip-2.168pt+\hskip-2.168pt\frac{n}{4B})|

for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.
If
f
is a zero signal, it is trivial. Assume
f
is a nonzero signal.

By the convolution theorem, we have
\mathcal{F}(f\ast\psi^{\#})=(\mathcal{F}f)(\mathcal{F}\psi^{\#})\in L^{1}([-B,B])
, which implies
f\ast\psi^{\#}\in PW_{B}^{1}\subset PW_{B}^{2}
. Combine this with (
5.2
), (
5.3
) and Theorem
3.2
, we have

\displaystyle f\ast\psi^{\#}=e^{i\alpha}g\ast\psi^{\#}\text{ or }f\ast\psi^{\#}=e^{i\alpha}\overline{g\ast\psi^{\#}},\text{ for some }\alpha\in\mathbb{R}.

If
f\ast\psi^{\#}=e^{i\alpha}g\ast\psi^{\#}
for some
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, then
\mathcal{F}f\mathcal{F}\psi^{\#}=e^{i\alpha}\mathcal{F}g\mathcal{F}\psi^{\#}
. Since
\mathcal{F}\psi^{\#}(w)=\mathcal{F}\psi(-w)
, then it follows from (
5.1
) that
\mathcal{F}f(w)=e^{i\alpha}\mathcal{F}g(w),a.e.w\in\mathbb{R}
and thus
f=e^{i\alpha}g
.
If
f\ast\psi^{\#}=e^{i\alpha}\overline{g\ast\psi^{\#}}
for some
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
. Similarly, we can obtain
f=e^{i\alpha}\overline{g}
for some
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
. Then we complete the proof.
∎

We observe that the result in Theorem
5.1
is merely a theoretical sampling result and does not correspond to a reconstruction method. Next we
explore the circulant graph structure in Theorem
2.5
and
propose a sampling scheme analogous to Theorem
3.4
to achieve STFT conjugate phase retrieval in
PW_{B}^{2}
using three structured window functions, which corresponds to a reconstruction method, with a sampling rate three times the Nyquist rate.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
B>0
, the Paley-Wiener space
PW_{B}^{2}
be defined as in (
1.2
),
\psi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
be a real-valued window function such that

\displaystyle(\mathcal{F}\psi)(w)\neq 0,\ \ a.e.\ \ w\in(-B,B),

and let
f
be any nonzero complex-valued function in
PW_{B}^{2}
and
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
satisfy

(5.4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{\psi}f(x_{0}\hskip-2.168pt+\hskip-2.168pt\frac{n+1}{2B},0)\overline{\mathcal{V}_{\psi}f(x_{0}\hskip-2.168pt+\hskip-2.168pt\frac{n}{2B},0)}\neq\mathcal{V}_{\psi}f(x_{0}\hskip-2.168pt+\hskip-2.168pt\frac{n}{2B},0)\overline{\mathcal{V}_{\psi}f(x_{0}\hskip-2.168pt+\hskip-2.168pt\frac{n+1}{2B},0)},n\in\mathbb{Z}.

Then
f
can be determined, up to a unimodular constant and conjugation, from magnitudes
|\mathcal{V}_{\psi}f(x_{0}+\frac{n}{2B},0)|
,
|\mathcal{V}_{\psi(\cdot-\frac{1}{2B})-\psi(\cdot)}f(x_{0}+\frac{n}{2B},0)|
and
|\mathcal{V}_{\psi(\cdot-\frac{1}{B})-\psi(\cdot)}f(x_{0}+\frac{n}{2B},0)|,n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

Note that
\mathcal{V}_{\psi}f(x,0)=f\ast\psi^{\#}(x)
and
\mathcal{V}_{\psi(\cdot-c)-\psi(\cdot)}f(x,0)=f\ast\psi^{\#}(x+c)-f\ast\psi^{\#}(x)
for any
x,c\in\mathbb{R}
.
Let
g\in PW_{B}^{2}
satisfy

\displaystyle|f\ast\psi^{\#}(x_{0}+\frac{n}{2B})|=|g\ast\psi^{\#}(x_{0}+\frac{n}{2B})|,

\displaystyle|f\ast\psi^{\#}(x_{0}+\frac{n+1}{2B})-f\ast\psi^{\#}(x_{0}+\frac{n}{2B})|=|g\ast\psi^{\#}(x_{0}+\frac{n+1}{2B})-g\ast\psi^{\#}(x_{0}+\frac{n}{2B})|

and

\displaystyle|f\ast\psi^{\#}(x_{0}+\frac{n+2}{2B})-f\ast\psi^{\#}(x_{0}+\frac{n}{2B})|=|g\ast\psi^{\#}(x_{0}+\frac{n+2}{2B})-g\ast\psi^{\#}(x_{0}+\frac{n}{2B})|

for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.
By (
5.4
), we have

\displaystyle f\ast\psi^{\#}(x_{0}\hskip-2.168pt+\hskip-2.168pt\frac{n+1}{2B})\overline{f\ast\psi^{\#}(x_{0}\hskip-2.168pt+\hskip-2.168pt\frac{n}{2B})}\hskip-2.168pt\neq\hskip-2.168ptf\ast\psi^{\#}(x_{0}\hskip-2.168pt+\hskip-2.168pt\frac{n}{2B})\overline{f\ast\psi^{\#}(x_{0}\hskip-2.168pt+\hskip-2.168pt\frac{n+1}{2B})},n\in\mathbb{Z}.

By the proof of Theorem
5.1
, we have
f\ast\psi^{\#},g\ast\psi^{\#}\in PW_{B}^{2}
, and then

\displaystyle f\ast\psi^{\#}=e^{i\alpha}g\ast\psi^{\#}\text{ or }f\ast\psi^{\#}=e^{i\alpha}\overline{g\ast\psi^{\#}},\text{ for some }\alpha\in\mathbb{R},

by Theorem
3.4
.
Applying the arguments in the proof of Theorem
5.1
, we obtain the conclusion.
∎

Appendix A

In Algorithm

from the structured phaseless samples
|f(x_{0}+\frac{n}{2B})|
,
|f(x_{0}+\frac{n+1}{2B})-f(x_{0}+\frac{n}{2B})|
and
|f(x_{0}+\frac{n+2}{2B})-f(x_{0}+\frac{n}{2B})|,n\in\mathbb{Z}
for some
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
, up to a unimodular constant and conjugation. After we determine the samples
f(x_{0}+\frac{n}{2B})
, up to a unimodular and conjugation, we use the following formula to determine the signal, cf. WSK sampling theorem.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
B>0
, the Paley-Wiener space
PW_{B}^{2}
be defined as in (
1.2
). Assume
f\in PW_{B}^{2}
and
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
, then

\displaystyle f(x)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}f(x_{0}+\frac{n}{2B})\text{sinc}(2B(x-x_{0})-n),x\in\mathbb{R},

where the series converges to
f
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
.

Proof.

One may easily verify that
\mathcal{F}(sinc)=\textbf{1}|_{[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}]}
and
\mathcal{F}(sinc(2B\cdot))=\frac{1}{2B}\textbf{1}|_{[-B,B]}
.

We can obtain that
\{\frac{1}{\sqrt{2B}}e^{2\pi iw(-x_{0}+\frac{n}{2B})}\}_{n}
is a orthonormal basis in
L^{2}([-B,B])
. In fact, for every
n,m\in\mathbb{Z}

\displaystyle\int_{-B}^{B}e^{\frac{2\pi i(n-m)w}{2B}}dw{}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\text{1}|_{[-B,B]})(\frac{n-m}{2B})=2B\text{sinc}(2B\frac{n-m}{2B})

\displaystyle=2B\text{sinc}(n-m)=2B\delta_{m,n}.

We have

\displaystyle\hat{f}(w)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}a_{n}e^{2\pi iw(-x_{0}+\frac{n}{2B})},

where

\displaystyle a_{n}=\frac{1}{2B}\int_{-B}^{B}\hat{f}(w)e^{-2\pi iw(-x_{0}+\frac{n}{2B})}dw=\frac{1}{2B}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\hat{f}(w)e^{-2\pi iw(-x_{0}+\frac{n}{2B})}dw=\frac{1}{2B}f(x_{0}-\frac{n}{2B}),

which implies

\displaystyle\hat{f}(w)=\frac{1}{2B}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}f(x_{0}-\frac{n}{2B})e^{2\pi iw(-x_{0}+\frac{n}{2B})}=\frac{1}{2B}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}f(x_{0}+\frac{n}{2B})e^{2\pi iw(-x_{0}-\frac{n}{2B})}.

Then

\displaystyle f(x){}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\hat{f}(w)e^{2\pi ixw}dw=\int_{-B}^{B}\hat{f}(w)e^{2\pi ixw}dw

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2B}\int_{-B}^{B}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}f(x_{0}+\frac{n}{2B})e^{2\pi iw(-x_{0}-\frac{n}{2B})}e^{2\pi ixw}dw

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2B}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}f(x_{0}+\frac{n}{2B})\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\textbf{1}|_{[-B,B]})(x-x_{0}-\frac{n}{2B})

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}f(x_{0}+\frac{n}{2B})\text{sinc}(2B(x-x_{0})-n).

∎

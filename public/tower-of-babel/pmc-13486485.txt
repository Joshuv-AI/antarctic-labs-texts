A novel hybrid intelligent optimization strategy for multi-objective trajectory planning of robotic arms in precision assembly scenarios
Authors: Zhang Changtian, Hang Jiaxuan, Li Xinyang, Chen Xuhao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
For precision assembly tasks, the accuracy and efficiency of robotic arm trajectory planning directly impact product quality and production efficiency in manufacturing, making it a core technology driving industrial automation upgrades. This research endeavors to establish a sophisticated multi-objective trajectory planning model, specifically engineered to cater to the intricate demands of precision assembly scenarios. The model optimizes for “maximum efficiency, minimum energy consumption, and minimal impact,” quantifying time costs, energy expenditure, and the influence of mechanical impact on assembly precision during the process. To enhance the performance of traditional multi-objective particle swarm optimization (MOPSO), this study proposes an improved CEMOPSO algorithm. This approach enhances initial population diversity by incorporating Chebyshev mapping strategies, dynamically adjusts particle search directions through evolutionary elimination mechanisms, and optimizes constraint handling capabilities via a designed infeasibility evaluation function. Engineering experiments using pyrotechnic grain assembly as a typical scenario validate CEMOPSO’s practical application value. Implementing this algorithm increased robotic arm assembly efficiency by 15.2%, reduced energy consumption by 20.4%, and decreased impact by 26.4%. This demonstrates the effectiveness and engineering applicability of the theoretical methods developed in this study for complex precision assembly tasks.

Introduction

Precision assembly serves as a core process in modern high-end manufacturing, with its accuracy level directly determining the reliability of end products in strategic industries such as aerospace, semiconductor packaging, and biomedical engineering1,2. Statistics indicate that in precision electromechanical product manufacturing, the assembly phase accounts for over 50% of the total production cycle, and assembly errors contribute up to 75% to product functional failures3. In recent years, scholars worldwide have conducted multidimensional research on precision assembly. Liu, Sheng proposes a novel fuzzy algorithm that quantifies cognitive uncertainty in assembly precision using fuzzy sets. By integrating fuzzy-based assembly precision analysis models and computational methods, they develop a comprehensive assembly precision analysis framework4. Assembly precision and functionality are highly dependent on component dimensions, which often leads to quality issues. Khezri, Amirhossein proposes a unique adaptive assembly strategy for micro-gear pairing by evaluating and comparing different assembly approaches5. Jia, Kang and colleagues introduce an assembly-driven framework based on multicolored set theory to construct digital twin models for synchronous product assembly accuracy prediction6. However, traditional assembly patterns reliant on manual expertise struggle to meet the demands of smart manufacturing—characterized by diverse product varieties, small batch sizes, and high flexibility. Automated assembly systems are progressively becoming critical infrastructure in precision manufacturing7,8.

Within automated assembly systems, robotic arms have emerged as core execution units for precision assembly due to their advantages in high precision, programmability, and environmental adaptability9. Scholars have conducted extensive research across diverse scenarios. Zhao, Lida proposes a macro-micro composite material precision assembly method integrating robots with two-dimensional active end effectors (TDAE). Experimental results demonstrate that TDAE achieves high-precision positioning within ±3 mm, with an error range of 5 μm10. Thin components are critical industrial parts in 3 C precision assembly tasks. During robotic precision assembly alignment, six-degree-of-freedom (6-D) posture measurement of thin components is essential. Wang, Bin propose a universal two-stage 6-D posture measurement framework based on monocular vision, applicable to the precision assembly of thin sheets with arbitrary irregular cross-sectional shapes11. Addressing the degradation of robotic end-effector absolute positioning accuracy due to geometric errors (e.g., manufacturing and assembly deviations) and non-geometric errors (e.g., industrial joint flexibility and gear backlash), Xu, Shuo proposes a hierarchical compensation method for robotic positioning errors12. Notably, the motion trajectory of a robotic arm serves as the critical link between task requirements and execution capabilities. Its planning quality directly impacts assembly precision, efficiency, and equipment lifespan. Research indicates that optimized trajectories can reduce robotic arm energy consumption by 20%-40%13 while significantly minimizing long-term cumulative errors caused by joint impacts14.

Most existing research focuses on single-objective optimization, such as trajectory planning for time-optimal, energy-optimal, or accuracy-optimal performance15–17. However, in practical precision assembly scenarios, robotic arms must simultaneously satisfy multiple physical constraints: ensuring micrometer-level positioning accuracy of the end-effector while controlling joint torques below rated values to prevent equipment damage, all within a limited cycle time to meet production line takt time requirements. This demand for multi-objective collaborative optimization has driven researchers to explore new trajectory planning paradigms. Addressing issues such as incomplete quantification of robot trajectory evaluation metrics and insufficient consideration of joint motion performance during gear chamfer grinding, Zhu, Yongguo et al. propose a robot trajectory planning method based on multi-objective collaborative optimization and quintic B-spline interpolation18. Sun, Hao introduces a non-dominated sorting method based on B-spline interpolation and infeasibility updates for multi-objective trajectory planning of shield platform assembly robots19. Chen, Saixuan proposes a novel 7-DOF tendon-driven redundant robot (TDR7) based on a weighted inverse kinematics (IK) optimization algorithm and a deep learning fine-tuning model. This approach integrates joint constraints, singularity avoidance, and minimum energy consumption into a multi-objective optimization framework, significantly enhancing joint motion continuity and trajectory planning efficiency20. However, existing methods still face three challenges in multi-objective collaborative optimization21,22: First, traditional algorithms often get stuck in local optima when handling multi-dimensional objectives like efficiency, energy consumption, and impact, leading to uneven Pareto frontier distributions. Second, most methods lack real-time adjustment capabilities for dynamic constraints in precision assembly. Third, high-dimensional decision spaces significantly increase algorithm convergence difficulty.

To address these challenges, this study decomposes core requirements from the manufacturing system perspective and constructs a three-dimensional objective function system for robotic arm trajectory planning: In the smart manufacturing dimension, an efficiency function centered on assembly cycle time, production line takt time adaptability, and equipment utilization is established to enhance system throughput through spatiotemporal coupled optimization. In the green manufacturing dimension, an energy consumption function is established based on joint motor power integration and regenerative braking mechanisms, achieving optimal energy allocation through integration with a dynamic model. In the high-end manufacturing dimension, an impact function based on joint torque rate of change is constructed to address micro-impact issues in precision assembly, suppressing high-frequency impact via frequency-domain filtering. To solve this multi-objective optimization problem, this study proposes the CEMOPSO algorithm, which integrates Chebyshev mapping strategy, evolutionary elimination mechanism, and infeasibility constraints: Chebyshev chaotic mapping enhances initial population diversity; a fitness-based evolutionary elimination mechanism maintains Pareto frontier distribution; and an infeasibility gradient guidance strategy improves convergence efficiency in high-dimensional decision spaces.

The structure of this paper is as follows: section “Comprehensive trajectory planning model under multi-objective collaboration” details the establishment of the multi-objective trajectory planning model; section “Methodology” describes this MOPSO algorithm and this CEMOPSO algorithm enhanced by three strategies; section “Numerical experiments” presents numerical experimental results and analysis; section “Simulation experiments” covers simulation results and analysis for trajectory planning; section “Engineering experiment” completes engineering validation experiments for the pyrotechnic grain assembly task.

Path generation based on artificial potential field method (APF)

In precision assembly scenarios, when a robotic arm operates between material pickup points and target assembly points, scientific and rational path planning is essential to ensure efficient and accurate material handling and assembly tasks23,24. This paper employs the Artificial Potential Field (APF) method25,26 to generate a complete, continuous, and collision-free motion path from the starting point to the target point. As illustrated in Fig. 1, the APF method successfully designs a motion path that combines the shortest distance with obstacle avoidance, laying a solid foundation for subsequent trajectory planning.

Impact objectives

Amidst the vigorous development and widespread adoption of high-end manufacturing, the industry imposes increasingly stringent demands on production equipment performance. Beyond pursuing high efficiency, it sets exceptionally high standards for product machining precision and quality34. Methodology

Multi-objective Particle Swarm Optimization Algorithm (MOPSO)

Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO)35 is a swarm intelligence-based optimization algorithm proposed by Kennedy and Eberhart in 1995. This algorithm simulates the migration and swarming behaviors of collective organisms such as flocks of birds or schools of fish during foraging. Through individual collaboration and information sharing, it searches for optimal solutions within the solution space. In the PSO algorithm, each particle represents a potential solution in the solution space, possessing two attributes: position and velocity. The position indicates the particle’s coordinates within the solution space, corresponding to a candidate solution for the optimization problem. The velocity determines the direction and distance of the particle’s movement within the solution space. The velocity and position update formulas form the core of the PSO algorithm. The Multi-Objective Particle Swarm Optimization (MOPSO) algorithm is an extended approach designed for multi-objective optimization problems36. Its core logic synchronously optimizes multiple objectives within the solution space, ultimately generating a uniformly distributed set of Pareto optimal solutions with strong convergence properties. Compared to the single-objective PSO algorithm, MOPSO primarily differs in its external archive mechanism and mutation operator.

(2)Mutation operator

(2)Mutation operator

Mutation operator

The mutation operator introduces random perturbations to prevent particles from getting stuck in local optima, thereby enhancing the algorithm’s global search capability. Uniform mutation ensures randomness, while non-uniform mutation gradually reduces mutation intensity with increasing iterations, balancing exploration and exploitation. The particle swarm is divided into three groups: no mutation, uniform mutation, and non-uniform mutation. Particles in the first group undergo no mutation. The second group undergoes proportional uniform mutation, randomly altering the positions of some particles, with their new positions regenerated within the search space. The third group undergoes non-uniform mutation, where the mutation intensity gradually decreases with increasing generation number. By increasing search space diversity and employing different mutation strategies, the mutation operator ensures thorough exploration in the early stages and gradual convergence toward the optimal solution in later stages. Through the synergistic interaction of its external archive mechanism and mutation operators, MOPSO significantly enhances multi-objective optimization performance while maintaining algorithmic simplicity, making it particularly suitable for solving complex multi-modal problems. (3)Infeasibility

(3)Infeasibility

Infeasibility

Multi-objective trajectory planning for robotic arms presents an extremely complex problem involving multiple conflicting goals, encompassing aspects such as shortest path, minimum energy consumption, and optimal motion smoothness. Simultaneously, this problem is subject to stringent constraints. From a geometric perspective, the range of motion for each joint is limited, requiring precise trajectory planning to avoid collisions with surrounding obstacles. Dynamic constraints are equally critical, as the manipulator faces limitations in torque, velocity, and acceleration during motion. Additionally, task-specific constraints exist, such as required grasping postures or operational sequences. These constraints intertwine and interact, rendering multi-objective trajectory planning exceptionally complex. Traditional MOPSO algorithms exhibit significant shortcomings when addressing such intricate constraints.

Traditional MOPSO algorithms typically handle constraint issues by transforming out-of-bounds solutions to boundary extremum points. However, in robotic arm trajectory planning scenarios, the diverse and intricate constraints often render this simple transformation method inadequate for accurately reflecting solution feasibility. Particularly for combinations involving multiple constraints, merely shifting solutions to boundaries may fail to satisfy all requirements, resulting in generated trajectories that are impractical for real-world implementation. To overcome the limitations of traditional MOPSO algorithms in multi-objective robotic arm trajectory planning, this paper proposes an infeasibility-based improvement method. For a given solution xi, a multifaceted infeasibility function ξ(xi) is defined. This function integrates violations of both equality and inequality constraints within a unified squared error framework, as shown in Eq. (22).

23\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\xi (x_{i} ) = \underbrace {{\mathop \sum \limits_{{a = 1}}^{A} \left[ {\min \{ 0,U_{a} (x_{i} )\} } \right]^{2} }}_{{{\text{Inequality - constraint violation term}}}}{\text{ }} + \underbrace {{\mathop \sum \limits_{{b = 1}}^{B} \left[ {V_{b} (x_{i} )} \right]^{2} }}_{{{\text{Equality - constraint violation term}}}}$$\end{document}

In this formula, A and B signify the cardinalities (i.e., the number of elements) of the sets comprising equality and inequality constraints. The first term on the right side captures squared violations of inequality constraints, while the second term quantifies squared deviations from equality constraints. The function ξ(xi) functions as an all-encompassing distance metric that gauges the separation between the current solution xi and the feasible region. A solution xi is considered feasible if and only if ξ(xi) = 0, indicating that it precisely meets all the constraints. Moreover, the magnitude of ξ(xi) is directly proportional to the distance of the solution xi from the feasible region; the larger the value of ξ(xi), the farther the solution is from being feasible.

To dynamically assess solution acceptability for subsequent genetic operations, this paper introduces an adaptive infeasibility threshold ξth. This threshold is not fixed but is ingeniously designed to evolve dynamically as the optimization process progresses. It is computed as the normalized and annealed average of the current population’s infeasibility, as shown in Eq. (23).24\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{gathered} {\xi _{th}}=\frac{1}{{{N_{{\mathrm{pop}}}}}}\mathop \sum \limits_{{i=1}}^{{{N_{{\mathrm{pop}}}}}} \frac{{\xi ({x_i})}}{{\Omega (t)}} \hfill \\ \Omega (t)=\frac{t}{{Max\_t}} \hfill \\ \end{gathered}$$\end{document}

Here, Npop denotes the population size. The annealing function Ω (t) plays a crucial role in progressively tightening the threshold during optimization. When\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\xi ({x_i})<{\xi _{th}}$$\end{document}, the solution is accepted; conversely, it is rejected. To uphold a consistent population size, solutions that are rejected are substituted with the solution in the current population that has the lowest level of infeasibility. This innovative threshold setting mechanism ensures a balanced exploration-exploitation tradeoff throughout the optimization process.

In terms of algorithm implementation, the population is first initialized using the Chebyshev mapping strategy. Subsequently, an evolutionary elimination mechanism is employed to obtain higher-quality solutions. Finally, the infeasibility criterion is used to prune and update the external archive mechanism. Through this sequence of operations, a novel multi-objective optimization algorithm, namely CEMOPSO, is ultimately formed. This algorithm is specifically designed to tackle the multi-objective trajectory planning problem for robotic arms. It generates joint motion trajectories that strive to achieve the “optimal trifecta” of efficiency, energy consumption, and impact. Figure 5 illustrates the CEMOPSO algorithm solving this process.

Engineering experiment

To validate the effectiveness and engineering applicability of the proposed CEMOPSO algorithm in practical industrial scenarios, this section conducts engineering experiments using pyrotechnic grain automatic assembly as a typical application case. Pyrotechnic grain assembly imposes extremely high precision requirements, where the trajectory smoothness, energy consumption level, and end-effector impact of the robotic arm directly affect product yield and operational safety. The experiment employs simulation specimens made of engineering plastic with dimensions identical to actual pyrotechnic grains, safely and controllably reproducing real assembly conditions in a laboratory environment. The assembly objective is to vertically insert the grain simulation specimen into a base hole with a diameter of Φ8 mm, where the single-side clearance between the shaft and the hole is approximately 0.5 mm, constituting a typical precision assembly task.

The core device of the experimental platform is a UR16e collaborative robot (Universal Robots) with a payload capacity of 16 kg, a repeatability of ± 0.05 mm, and a maximum tool-end linear velocity of approximately 1 m/s. A dedicated electric gripper is mounted at the robot end-effector for picking and releasing the grain simulation specimen, while the workbench is equipped with a base fixture featuring a Φ8 mm assembly hole and a tray for the incoming grain specimens. To comprehensively evaluate the optimization effects of trajectory planning on the three dimensions of efficiency, energy consumption, and impact, a multi-source synchronous data acquisition system was established. The efficiency metric is recorded as the total time of a single assembly task (unit: s) using the built-in Real-Time Clock (RTC) of the UR16e controller, which timestamps the start and end moments with a precision of 1 ms. The energy consumption metric is obtained by directly measuring the three-phase input power of the six joint servo motors using a ZES ZIMMER LMG671 high-precision power analyzer, equipped with six power measurement channels operating at a sampling rate of 1 kHz and a basic power accuracy of ± 0.015%; the cumulative electrical energy consumption (unit: W) is derived through numerical integration of the power-time curve. The impact metric is defined as the joint motion jerk (unit: °/s³), calculated by first-order central difference of the acceleration time-series data output by the UR16e controller, with the maximum absolute jerk value across all joints taken as the evaluation criterion for a single assembly task. Temporal consistency of the three data streams is achieved through a unified triggering mechanism via the host PC: simultaneously with the issuance of the trajectory start command, the host PC sends a “start acquisition” command via TCP/IP to both the power analyzer and the robot controller, ensuring alignment of the starting time zero points across all three data streams. Conclusion and outlook

Based on precision assembly task scenarios, this paper constructs a multi-objective trajectory planning model for robotic arms that achieves “optimal efficiency, minimal energy consumption, and minimal impact” from three dimensions: intelligent manufacturing, green manufacturing, and high-end manufacturing. Simultaneously, it innovatively proposes a multi-objective algorithm, CEMOPSO (combining Chebyshev mapping strategy, evolutionary elimination mechanism, and infeasibility strategy), to solve the aforementioned model. Numerical experiments first demonstrate CEMOPSO’s superior optimization capabilities compared to five alternative algorithms, including MOPSO. Subsequent simulation experiments provide both quantitative and qualitative evidence that CEMOPSO delivers superior solutions for the UR16e robotic arm’s multi-objective trajectory planning. Finally, based on these experimental findings, practical engineering experiments are conducted using pyrotechnic grain assembly as a representative precision assembly scenario. Experiments reveal significant performance gaps before and after adopting the CEMOPSO-based multi-objective trajectory planning method for this UR16e robotic arm during assembly. Improvements are achieved across three objectives: efficiency (15.2%), energy consumption (20.4%), and impact (26.4%).

However, this study has certain limitations. Future research can systematically explore the following areas to advance theoretical innovation and deepen engineering applications of robotic trajectory planning in precision assembly scenarios: (1) Construct a verification platform based on digital twin technology to achieve three-way closed-loop verification involving physical experiments, simulation validation, and digital twins. Integrate real-time data streams from actual assembly scenarios to build a trajectory planning verification system with predictive maintenance capabilities. (2) Develop a trajectory planning knowledge base based on transfer learning to address diverse precision assembly requirements. Extract trajectory feature meta-models from different assembly scenarios to construct a reusable trajectory planning knowledge graph. Integrate federated learning technology to enable cross-enterprise and cross-scenario sharing of trajectory planning expertise, promoting the universal application of multi-objective trajectory planning technology in high-end manufacturing sectors such as aerospace and biopharmaceuticals.

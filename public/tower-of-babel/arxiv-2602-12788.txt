A parallel space-time p p -adaptive discontinuous Galerkin method for nonlinear acoustics
Daniele Corallo, Pascal Lehner, Christian Wieners

Abstract
In this paper, we introduce and analyze a space-time p p -adaptive discontinuous Galerkin method for nonlinear acoustics. We first present the underlying mathematical model, which is based on a recently derived formulation involving, in particular, only first order in time derivatives. We then propose a space-time discontinuous Galerkin discretization of this model, combining a symmetric Friedrichs systems discretization for symmetric hyperbolic systems with an interior penalty discretization for damping terms. The resulting nonlinear system is solved using Newton’s method. Next, we present a well-posedness analysis of the discrete problem. The analysis begins with a linearized system, for which stability is shown. Using a fixed point argument, these results are extended to the fully discrete nonlinear system, yielding a priori error estimates in a natural discontinuous Galerkin norm. Finally, we present numerical experiments demonstrating the parallel solvability of the space-time formulation and the effectiveness of p p -adaptivity. The results confirm the theoretical convergence rates and show that adaptive refinement can reduce the number of degrees of freedom required to accurately approximate selected goal functionals. Moreover, the experiments demonstrate that the model reproduces characteristic phenomena of nonlinear acoustics, such as harmonic generation, thereby validating the proposed model.

Introduction

Efficient numerical methods for nonlinear acoustics are essential across various scientific and engineering disciplines. In engineering, nonlinear acoustic phenomena play
a critical role in nondestructive
material testing
[
Fierro2015
]
, underwater acoustics
[
CampoValera2023
]
and supersonic sources
[
Sapozhnikov2019
]
. From a medical perspective,
accurately modeling the propagation of nonlinear acoustic waves through
biological tissue is essential for both diagnostic and therapeutic applications,
including medical imaging, tissue ablation, lithotripsy, and targeted drug delivery, see
[
rudenko_2022
]
.

The simulation of nonlinear acoustics is often challenging due to the interplay between
high frequency wave propagation and nonlinear effects. Many classical models, such as Westervelt’s
[
10
]
and Kuznetsov’s equation
[
kuznetsov:1971
]
, involve second order time derivatives that act on nonlinear terms. This significantly complicates the design of stable and efficient numerical methods. Moreover, a key difficulty lies in resolving the nonlinearities in the presence of steep gradients or shock like structures that often emerge during
wave propagation, see
[
Hamilton:1997
]
for more details on the physics of nonlinear acoustics.

Numerous numerical methods have been proposed in the literature to address these challenges. Here, we focus on works that include an error analysis of a finite element method.
For the Westervelt equation, semi-discrete finite element approximations with optimal error
estimates have been studied in
[
Nikolic2019
]
, while semi-discrete discontinuous
Galerkin (DG) methods have been analyzed in
[
Antonietti2020
]
.
A time stepping DG approach using conforming spatial elements is presented in

[
4
]
, and a hybridizable DG formulation is discussed in
[
Meliani2024
]
.
Efficient time integration techniques based on operator splitting are
introduced in
[
Kaltenbacher2015
]
.
For the Kuznetsov equation, a mixed finite element analysis is provided
in
[
Meliani2024
]
. Robust error estimates with respect to the damping parameter for a mixed method
are derived in
[
3
]
, and coupling with elastic wave equations is
addressed in
[
7
]
.

A model for nonlinear acoustics using only first order time derivatives with a structure preserving discretization and conforming elements is developed in
[
Egger2025
]
,
with additional insights presented in
[
1
]
.
Other numerical approaches, such as finite volume and finite difference methods, have also been explored, though often without convergence analysis. A finite volume method for Westervelt’s equation has been investigated in, for example,
[
9
]
, while a finite difference method has been studied in
[
8
]
.

In this paper, we propose a space-time DG method
for a nonlinear acoustic model closely related to Kuznetsov’s equation,
derived in
[
QuadraticWave
]
. The key distinction of this formulation to classical models is the use of only first order in time derivatives, and that the nonlinear terms only involve spatial derivatives. To the best of our knowledge, this work represents the first application of a space-time DG method to nonlinear acoustics.
The motivation for considering space-time methods lies in their intrinsic suitability for adaptive refinement, parallel
implementation, and inverse problems.
Advanced space-time DG methods have been devolved for wave equations in e.g.
[
AntoniettiMazzieriMigliorini2020
,
corallo2022
,
Karabelas2016
,
5
,
bansal:2021
]
, for parabolic problems in
[
CangianiDongGeorgoulis2017
,
GomezMoiola2024
]
, and for Navier-Stokes equations in
[
Klaij2006
,
RhebergenCockburnVanderVegt2013
,
TAVELLI2016
]
. We emphasize that this list of references is by no means exhaustive.

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. In section
2
, we introduce the continuous mathematical model and present the space–time DG discretization analyzed in this work. Section
3
is devoted to the discrete well-posedness analysis of a linearized system. In section
4
, these results are extended to the fully nonlinear discrete problem, yielding well-posedness and a priori error estimates for the proposed method. Numerical experiments validating the efficiency and possibility of a
p
-adaptive and parallel implementation are presented in section
5
. Finally, concluding remarks and directions for future research are given in section
6
.

2
First order in time model

In this section, we present the continuous model and its space-time DG discretization considered in this work. The derivation of this first order in time model is based on the standard assumption of small amplitude wave propagation and follows a methodology similar to that used in the derivation of Kuznetsov’s equation.
A key difference, however, is that one avoids taking additional derivatives of the reduced Navier–Stokes system, which are employed in the Kuznetsov or Westervelt formulation to further simplify the equations. For a detailed derivation, we refer the reader to
[
QuadraticWave
]
.

2.1
Continuous model

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be an open, bounded space domain with dimension
d\in\{2,3\}
, and let
\Gamma:=\partial\Omega
denote the boundary of
\Omega
, which is assumed to be Lipschitz. Furthermore, let
I:=(0,T)\subset\mathbb{R}
denote a time interval with final time
T>0
, and define the corresponding space-time domain
Q:=I\times\Omega
.
The model is governed by parameters satisfying

\alpha,\,\beta,\,\gamma,\,\delta,\,\varepsilon,\,\zeta,\,\eta,\,\theta>0,\,\gamma\neq\delta,

and takes the form

\begin{split}\alpha\partial_{t}p+\nabla\cdot\mathbf{v}-\beta\Delta p+\gamma p\nabla\cdot\mathbf{v}+\delta\nabla p\cdot\mathbf{v}&=p_{S}\quad\,\text{in}\quad Q\\
\varepsilon\partial_{t}\mathbf{v}+\nabla p-\zeta\Delta\mathbf{v}+\frac{1}{2}\nabla\!\left(\eta\mathbf{v}^{2}-\theta p^{2}\right)&=\mathbf{v}_{S}\quad\,\text{in}\quad Q\\
p=p_{0},\quad\mathbf{v}&=\mathbf{v}_{0}\quad\,\text{on}\quad\{0\}\times\Omega\\
p=p_{D},\quad\mathbf{v}&=\mathbf{v}_{D}\quad\text{on}\quad I\times\Gamma_{\textrm{D}}\\
\beta\nabla p\cdot\mathbf{n}=p_{N},\quad\mathbf{v}&=\mathbf{v}_{N}\quad\text{on}\quad I\times\Gamma_{\textrm{N}}\end{split}

with
\Gamma_{\textrm{D}}\cup\Gamma_{\textrm{N}}=\Gamma
, where
\Gamma_{\textrm{D}}\cap\Gamma_{\textrm{N}}=\emptyset
, and
\Gamma_{\textrm{D}}
has positive
(d-1)
-dimensional Lebesgue measure.
In this model of nonlinear acoustics
p:Q\to\mathbb{R}
can be understood as the fluctuation of pressure and
\mathbf{v}:Q\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
as the fluctuation of the velocity field, respectively, cf.
[
QuadraticWave
, Section 2]
for details. By
\mathbf{n}
we denote the outer unit normal vector on
\partial\Omega
and
\cdot^{\top}
means transposition of a vector or matrix. We write
\mathbf{v}^{2}:=\mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf{v}
for the squared norm of a vector,
\nabla:=(\partial_{x_{1}},\dots,\partial_{x_{d}})^{\top}
for the vector of spatial partial derivatives,
\Delta:=\nabla\cdot\nabla
for the Laplace operator, which acts component wise on vector fields, and
\boldsymbol{\nabla}
for the Jacobian matrix of a vector field.

In order to simplify notation, we rewrite equation (
2.1
) with
u=(u_{p},u_{\mathbf{v}})^{\top}=(p,\mathbf{v})^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{1+d}
as

\begin{split}\left(M\partial_{t}+A-D\Delta\right)\!u+N(u,u)&=f\,\,\,\,\quad\text{ in }\quad Q\\
u&=u_{0}\quad\,\text{ on }\quad\{0\}\times\Omega\\
\mathcal{B}_{D}u&=u_{D}\quad\text{ on }\quad I\times\Gamma_{\textrm{D}}\\
\mathcal{B}_{N}u&=u_{N}\quad\text{ on }\quad I\times\Gamma_{\textrm{N}}\end{split}

with matrices
M:=\text{diag}(\alpha,\varepsilon,\dots,\varepsilon),\ D:=\text{diag}(\beta,\zeta,\dots,\zeta)\in\mathbb{R}^{(1+d)\times(1+d)}
,
first order differential operators

\begin{split}Au:=\begin{pmatrix}\nabla\cdot u_{\mathbf{v}}\\
\nabla u_{p}\end{pmatrix},\quad N(u,z):=\begin{pmatrix}\gamma z_{p}\nabla\cdot u_{\mathbf{v}}+\delta\nabla u_{p}\cdot z_{\mathbf{v}}\\
\eta\left(\boldsymbol{\nabla}u_{\mathbf{v}}\right)^{\top}\!z_{\mathbf{v}}-\theta z_{p}\nabla u_{p}\end{pmatrix},\end{split}

boundary operators
\mathcal{B}_{D}u=u,\ \mathcal{B}_{N}u=(\nabla u_{p}\cdot\mathbf{n},u_{\mathbf{v}})^{\top}
,
and given data

\begin{split}f&:=(p_{S},\mathbf{v}_{S})^{\top}\in L^{2}(Q;\mathbb{R}^{1+d}),\\
u_{0}&:=(p_{0},\mathbf{v}_{0})^{\top}\in H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{1+d}),\\
u_{D}&:=(p_{D},\mathbf{v}_{D})^{\top}\in H^{1}(0,T;H^{\frac{3}{2}}(\Gamma_{\textrm{D}};\mathbb{R}^{1+d})),\\
u_{N}&:=(p_{N},\mathbf{v}_{N})^{\top}\in H^{1}(0,T;H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma_{\textrm{N}})\times H^{\frac{3}{2}}(\Gamma_{\textrm{N}};\mathbb{R}^{d})).\end{split}

In
[
QuadraticWave
,
6
]
it is shown that equation (
2.1
) is well posed for any
T>0
with

u\in U:=H^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{1+d}))\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{1+d}))

(2)

assuming that
\Gamma_{\textrm{D}}=\partial\Omega
or
\Gamma_{\textrm{N}}=\partial\Omega
is
C^{2}
-regular, and that the data (
2.1
) are sufficiently small with respect to the corresponding norms.
Note that in our analysis the case
\Gamma_{\textrm{N}}=\partial\Omega
is not included.

This is established as follows. Writing equation (
2.1
) as

G(u):=Lu-D\Delta u+N(u,u)-f=0

(3)

with
L:=M\partial_{t}+A
, it is shown in
[
QuadraticWave
,
6
]
that there exists an
r>0
such that Newton’s method with starting value
0
converges to a unique
u\in B_{r}^{U}(0)
such that
G(u)=0
. Here, for any normed space
X
,
r>0
and
x\in X
we denote the open ball with radius
r
centered at
x
as
B^{X}_{r}(x)
.
In the analysis, it becomes necessary to compute the Fréchet derivative of
G
in
U
at
\tilde{u}
. Owing to the bilinearity of
N(\cdot,\cdot)
and the linearity of all other operators in
G
, one can demonstrate through suitable estimates on
N(\cdot,\cdot)
, that
G
is Fréchet differentiable in
U
.
Then, Newton’s method requires the linear system

\begin{split}(L-D\Delta)u+N(u,\tilde{u})+N(\tilde{u},u)&=f^{\tilde{u}}\quad\,\text{ in }\quad Q\\
u&=u_{0}\quad\,\text{ on }\quad\{0\}\times\Omega\\
\mathcal{B}_{D}u&=u_{D}\quad\text{ on }\quad I\times\Gamma_{\textrm{D}}\\
\mathcal{B}_{N}u&=u_{N}\quad\text{ on }\quad I\times\Gamma_{\textrm{N}},\end{split}

to be solved in each iteration, where in the context of Newton’s method
f^{\tilde{u}}
plays the role of a residual and
\tilde{u}\in U
denotes the previous iterate. To establish global in time well-posedness of equation (
2.1
), the data (
2.1
) and
f^{\tilde{u}}
are assumed to be small enough. Once this is proven, the Newton-Kantorovich Theorem, see Lemma
4.1
below, can be applied to
G
. This gives global in time well-posedness of equation (
2.1
), if the data (
2.1
) are sufficiently small. For showing the well-posedness of the discrete system, we follow a similar strategy, see section
4
.

2.2
Space-time discretization

To begin, we introduce some notation. Let
S\subset\mathbb{R}^{s}
be Lipschitz with
s\in\mathbb{N}
. We use the abbreviation
L^{2}=L^{2}(S)=L^{2}(S;\mathbb{R}^{i})
with
i\in\mathbb{N}
when the context is clear. The standard
L^{2}
inner product of two functions
f,g\in L^{2}(S;\mathbb{R}^{i})
is given by
(f,g)_{S}:=\int_{S}f\cdot g\,d\sigma
, where
\sigma
is a measure on
S
. If
f,g
are matrix-valued,
f\cdot g
corresponds to the Frobenius inner product.
Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
denote the number of time points of the considered discretization. For given time points
0=:t_{0}<t_{1}<\ldots<t_{k-1}<t_{k}:=T
, we define the following time intervals and time step sizes for
j\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
.

I_{j}:=(t_{j-1},t_{j}),\quad I_{h}:=\bigcup_{j=1}^{k}I_{j}\subset I,\quad\partial I_{h}:=\bigcup_{j=0}^{k}\{t_{j}\},\quad\tau_{j}:=t_{j}-t_{j-1},\quad\tau:=\max_{j\in\{1,\dots,k\}}\tau_{j}.

(4)

In this paper, we assume a quasi-uniform time discretization, i.e.,
\tau_{j}\in[C_{sr}\tau,\tau]
for all
j\in\{1,\dots,k\}
with a shape regularity constant
C_{sr}\in(0,1]
independent of
k
and
j
.

For the spatial discretization, let
\mathcal{K}_{h}
be a conforming shape-regular mesh of
\Omega
so that

{\Omega}_{h}:=\cup_{K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}}{K}
is a decomposition of
\Omega
into open non overlapping space cells
K\subset\Omega
. We denote spacial mesh sizes as
h_{K}:=\text{diam}(K)
and
h:=\max_{K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}}h_{K}
.

For simplicity, we consider only tensor-product space-time meshes in this article. To obtain a tensor-product mesh of
Q
, we define

Q_{h}:=I_{h}\times\Omega_{h}=\bigcup_{R\in\mathcal{R}_{h}}R\text{ with }\mathcal{R}_{h}:=\{R\subset Q:\exists j\in\{1,\ldots,k\},\exists K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}:R=I_{j}\times K\},

(5)

such that
\overline{Q_{h}}=\overline{Q}
.
We assume that the mesh sizes in time and space are well balanced satisfying for some
\omega\in[1,\infty)

h^{\omega}\leq c_{r}\tau\leq h<1,

(6)

where
c_{r}>0
is a reference velocity yielding the bound
\tau^{-1}\leq c_{r}h^{-\omega}
.

Element boundaries

We denote by
\mathcal{F}_{K}
the set of faces of a space element
K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}
, and define the set of all faces as
\mathcal{F}_{h}:=\bigcup_{K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}}\mathcal{F}_{K}
, where
h_{F}=\text{diam}(F)
for
F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}
. The space skeleton is then given by
\partial\Omega_{h}=\overline{\bigcup_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}F}
, and the corresponding space-time skeleton is
\partial Q_{h}=\partial I_{h}\times\overline{\Omega}\cup\overline{I}\times\partial\Omega_{h}
.

We distinguish between boundary faces
\mathcal{F}^{\textrm{ext}}_{h}:=\{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}:F\subset\partial\Omega\}
and interior faces
\mathcal{F}^{\textrm{int}}_{h}:=\mathcal{F}_{h}\setminus\mathcal{F}^{\textrm{ext}}_{h}
. Additionally, we define Dirichlet faces as
\mathcal{F}^{\textrm{D}}_{h}:=\{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}:F\subset\Gamma_{\textrm{D}}\}
and Neumann faces as
\mathcal{F}^{\textrm{N}}_{h}:=\{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}:F\subset\Gamma_{\textrm{N}}\}
. We assume that the mesh
\mathcal{K}_{h}
is such that
\mathcal{F}^{\textrm{ext}}_{h}=\mathcal{F}^{\textrm{D}}_{h}\cup\mathcal{F}^{\textrm{N}}_{h}
disjointly with
\mathcal{F}^{\textrm{D}}_{h}\subset\Gamma_{\textrm{D}}
,
\mathcal{F}^{\textrm{N}}_{h}\subset\Gamma_{\textrm{N}}
such that
\overline{\bigcup_{F\in\mathcal{F}^{\textrm{D}}_{h}}F}=\Gamma_{\textrm{D}}
and
\overline{\bigcup_{F\in\mathcal{F}^{\textrm{N}}_{h}}F}=\Gamma_{\textrm{N}}
.
Furthermore, we require that the
(d-1)
-dimensional Lebesgue measure of
\mathcal{F}^{\textrm{D}}_{h}
is positive.

If an interior face
F\in\mathcal{F}^{\textrm{int}}_{h}
is contained in
K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}
, we write
K_{F}
for the unique neighbor cell such that
F=\partial K\cap\partial K_{F}
. For an exterior face
F\in\mathcal{F}^{\textrm{ext}}_{h}\subset K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}
, we set
K_{F}=K
.
Each element
K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}
is assigned the outer normal vector
\mathbf{n}_{K}
of
\partial K
.

Approximation spaces

Let
i,s\in\mathbb{N}
. We define the space of polynomials
{{\mathcal{P}}}(S;\mathbb{R}^{i}):=\bigcup_{\deg=0}^{\infty}{{\mathcal{P}}}^{\deg}(S;\mathbb{R}^{i})
, where
{{\mathcal{P}}}^{\deg}(S;\mathbb{R}^{i})
is the set of polynomials
\varphi:S\to\mathbb{R}^{i}
with degree less or equal then
\deg\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
for any set
S\subset\mathbb{R}^{s}
.
The approximation spaces considered here are broken polynomial spaces, namely, subsets of

{{\mathcal{P}}}_{h}(S;\mathbb{R}^{i}):=\left\{\varphi\in L^{2}(S;\mathbb{R}^{i}):\varphi|_{S}\in{{\mathcal{P}}}(S_{h};\mathbb{R}^{i})\ \forall S_{h}\in\mathcal{S}_{h}\right\},

(7)

where
\mathcal{S}_{h}
is some mesh of
S
.

To construct space-time tensor product broken polynomial spaces, we assign polynomial degrees
{p}_{R}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
in time and
{q}_{R}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
in space to every space-time cell
R\in\mathcal{R}_{h}
.
This results in the discontinuous finite element space

{Z}_{h}:=\left\{\varphi\in L^{2}(Q;\mathbb{R}^{1+d}):\varphi|_{R=I_{j}\times K}\in{{\mathcal{P}}}^{{p}_{R}}(I_{j};\mathbb{R}^{1+d})\otimes{{\mathcal{P}}}^{{q}_{R}}(K;\mathbb{R}^{1+d})\ \forall R\in\mathcal{R}_{h}\right\},

(8)

which is used in this paper. Here,
\otimes
denotes the tensor product between vector spaces.

Boundary operators

Let
w\in\left(H^{1}(I;L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{i}))\cap L^{2}(I;H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{i}))\right)\cup{{\mathcal{P}}}_{h}(Q,\mathbb{R}^{i})
with
i\in\mathbb{N}\cup(\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N})
be such that traces in time and space of
w
exist on
\mathcal{R}_{h}
. For
K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}
and
j\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
, we define
w_{j}:=w|_{I_{j}}
and
w_{K}:=w|_{K}
, where the evaluation of these expressions at boundary points is understood in the sense of traces. In particular, this gives

w_{j}(t_{j})=\lim_{t\to t_{j}^{+}}w_{j}(t),\quad w_{j}(t_{j-1})=\lim_{t\to t_{j-1}^{-}}w_{j}(t),

(9)

if the limits exist.

Let
K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}
be a space cell,
F\in\mathcal{F}_{K}\cap\mathcal{F}^{\textrm{int}}_{h}
,
K_{F}
the neighboring cell. We define the (space) jump at
F
with respect to
K
as

[w]_{F,K}(t,x):=w_{K_{F}}(t,x)-w_{K}(t,x)\quad x\in F,\ t\in I.

(10)

The jump with respect to time is defined for each
j=1,\dots,k-1
as

[w]_{j}(x):=w_{{j+1}}(t_{j},x)-w_{{j}}(t_{j},x)\quad x\in\Omega,

(11)

and for the boundary cases
j=0,k
, we set
[w]_{0}(\cdot):=w(t_{0},\cdot)
,
[w]_{k}(\cdot):=-w(t_{k},\cdot)
.

Additionally, we use the notion of a (space) jump at faces
F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}
without a particular reference cell. For this, we define
\mathbf{n}_{F}
as the unit outer normal vector of
F
. If
F\in\mathcal{F}^{\textrm{ext}}_{h}
,
\mathbf{n}_{F}
is taken to have the same orientation as
\mathbf{n}_{K}
with
F\subset K
. Otherwise, the orientation of
\mathbf{n}_{F}
may be chosen arbitrarily. Let
F
and
F_{1}
be adjacent faces with
F\subset K,F_{1}\subset K_{1}
.
The (space) jump at
F
is defined as

[w]_{F}(t,x):=w_{K}(x,t)-w_{K_{1}}(x,t)\quad x\in F\cap F_{1},\ t\in I

(12)

and the (space) average at
F
is

\langle w\rangle_{F}(t,x):=\frac{1}{2}\left(w_{K}(x,t)+w_{K_{1}}(x,t)\right)\quad x\in F\cap F_{1},\ t\in I.

(13)

On boundary faces
F\in\mathcal{F}^{\textrm{ext}}_{h}
, we set
[w]_{F}=\langle w\rangle_{F}=w|_{F}
.

2.3
Discontinuous Galerkin scheme

We now introduce the DG scheme for equations (
2.1
) and (
2.1
) considered in this article. DG space-time discretizations of
M\partial_{t}+A
can be framed within the theory of symmetric Friedrichs systems, see, e.g.,
[
dipietro2012
, Chapter 7]
. In this context, we build upon the primal formulation of a Friedrichs system and then add terms corresponding to an interior penalty method for the Laplace operator
-\Delta
, see, e.g.,
[
dipietro2012
,
dolejsi_discontinuous_2015
]
, along with nonlinear volume terms.

For convenience, we do not change the notation of differential operators, when applied to globally discontinuous functions. In these cases, we mean the broken operator, which is defined piecewise on each (space-time) cell.

Symmetric Friedrichs system

To discretize
\partial_{t}M+A
, as in
[
DoerflerFindeisenWienersZiegler:2019
]
, we consider the following bilinear forms
defined on
Z_{h}\times Z_{h}
, cf. (
8
),

m_{h}(u_{h},z_{h}):=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left(\int^{t_{j}}_{t_{j-1}}(M\partial_{t}u_{h},z_{h})_{{\Omega_{h}}}\,\mathrm{d}t+\left(M[u_{h}]_{j-1},z_{h,j}(t_{j-1})\right)_{{\Omega_{h}}}\right)

(14)

and

a_{h}(u_{h},z_{h}):=\int_{0}^{T}\sum_{K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}}\!\left((Au_{h},z_{h})_{K}+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{K}}(A_{\mathbf{n}_{K}}^{\textrm{up}}[u_{h}]_{F,K},z_{h,K})_{F}\right)\,\mathrm{d}t,

(15)

where

A_{\mathbf{n}_{K}}^{\textrm{up}}:=\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}-\sqrt{\frac{{\alpha}}{{\varepsilon}}}&\mathbf{n}_{K}^{\top}\\
\mathbf{n}_{K}&-\sqrt{\frac{{\varepsilon}}{{\alpha}}}\mathbf{n}_{K}\mathbf{n}_{K}^{\top}\end{pmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{(1+d)\times(1+d)}

(16)

is related to an upwind flux based on solving local Riemann problems, see section
A
for the derivation of
A_{\mathbf{n}_{K}}^{\textrm{up}}
for
d=2
.

In (
15
), the jump terms
[u_{h}]_{F,K}
are not defined for boundary faces
F\subset\Gamma
. To treat the boundary conditions correctly, we adopt the following convention

\begin{cases}[u_{h}]_{F,K}:=-{u_{h}}\text{ for }F\in\Gamma_{\textrm{D}},\\
[u_{h}]_{F,K}:=-2(0,{v_{h}})^{\top}\text{ for }F\in\Gamma_{\textrm{N}}\end{cases}

(17)

with
u_{h}=(p_{h},v_{h})^{\top}
.

As it is shown in
[
corallo2022
]
(and also is proven in section
A
),
the definition (
17
) ensures the bounds

c_{A}\int_{0}^{T}\|A_{\mathbf{n}}[z_{h}]\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega_{h})}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\leq a_{h}(z_{h},z_{h})\leq C_{A}\int_{0}^{T}\|A_{\mathbf{n}}[z_{h}]\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega_{h})}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\quad\forall z_{h}\in Z_{h}

(18)

with

\|A_{\mathbf{n}}[z_{h}](t)\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega_{h})}:=\left(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}}\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{K}}\|\ A_{\mathbf{n}}[z_{h}](t)\|_{F}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},\quad A_{\mathbf{n}_{K}}:=\begin{pmatrix}0&\mathbf{n}_{K}^{\top}\\
\mathbf{n}_{K}&0\end{pmatrix},

(19)

where
c_{A},C_{A}>0
only depend on
\alpha
and
\varepsilon
and
A_{\mathbf{n}_{K}}^{\textrm{up}}

is defined in (
16
).

Interior penalty method and nonlinear terms

To discretize the Laplacian terms
-D\Delta
, we use the interior penalty method, see, for example,
[
dipietro2012
,
dolejsi_discontinuous_2015
]
and the references therein.
The associated bilinear form is given by

d_{h}(u_{h},z_{h}):=(D\boldsymbol{\nabla}u_{h},\boldsymbol{\nabla}z_{h})_{Q_{h}}+\int_{0}^{T}\rho_{h}^{\Theta}(u_{h},z_{h})\,\mathrm{d}t

(20)

with

\begin{split}\rho_{h}^{\Theta}(u_{h},z_{h}):=&\beta\!\!\!\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}\setminus\mathcal{F}^{\textrm{N}}_{h}}\!\!\!-\Theta([p_{h}]_{F},\langle\nabla q_{h}\rangle_{F}\cdot\mathbf{n}_{F})_{F}-(\langle\nabla p_{h}\rangle_{F}\cdot\mathbf{n}_{F},[q_{h}]_{F})_{F}+\sigma_{F,h}([p_{h}]_{F},[q_{h}]_{F})_{F}\\
&+\zeta\!\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}\!-\Theta([v_{h}]_{F},\langle\boldsymbol{\nabla}w_{h}\rangle_{F}\mathbf{n}_{F})_{F}-(\langle\boldsymbol{\nabla}v\rangle_{F}\mathbf{n}_{F},[w_{h}]_{F})_{F}+\sigma_{F,h}([v_{h}]_{F},[w_{h}]_{F})_{F},\end{split}

using
z_{h}=(q_{h},w_{h})^{\top}
and depending on
a penalty parameter
\sigma_{F,h}>0
and

\Theta\in\{1,-1,0\}
corresponding to the symmetric, asymmetric and incomplete interior penalty methods, respectively.
Note that in
\rho_{h}^{\Theta}
we only sum over boundary faces, when Dirichlet boundary conditions are applied.

To ensure a stable scheme, the penalty parameter
\sigma_{F,h}:=\frac{C_{\sigma}}{h_{F}}
must be chosen sufficiently large, see, e.g.,
[
dolejsi_discontinuous_2015
, Corollary 2.41]
for lower bounds on
C_{\sigma}>0
. Additionally,
C_{\sigma}
should be selected based on the local spacial polynomial degree near
F
. For further details, we refer to
[
dolejsi_discontinuous_2015
, Section 7]
and the references therein.

For the nonlinear terms, we consider only volume elements, so that

n_{h}(u_{h},z_{h}):=(N(u_{h},u_{h}),z_{h})_{{Q_{h}}}=\frac{1}{2}n^{\prime}_{h}(u_{h},z_{h};u_{h})

(21)

is chosen to discretize the nonlinear terms. For the linearized equation (
2.1
), we obtain

n^{\prime}_{h}(u_{h},z_{h};\tilde{u}_{h}):=(N(u_{h},\tilde{u}_{h})+N(\tilde{u}_{h},u_{h}),z_{h})_{{Q_{h}}}

(22)

with
\tilde{u}_{h}\in Z_{h}
. We remark that
n^{\prime}_{h}(\cdot,\cdot;\tilde{u}_{h})
is the Fréchet derivative of
n_{h}(\cdot,\cdot)
at
\tilde{u}_{h}
.

The complete schemes

The complete nonlinear discrete variational problem approximating equation (
2.1
) is then given by

\text{solve for }u_{h}\in{Z}_{h}\text{ such that }b_{h}(u_{h},z_{h})=\ell_{h}(z_{h})\text{ for all }z_{h}\in Z_{h}

(23)

with

b_{h}(u_{h},z_{h}):=m_{h}(u_{h},z_{h})+a_{h}(u_{h},z_{h})+d_{h}(u_{h},z_{h})+n_{h}(u_{h},z_{h})

(24)

and

\ell_{h}(z_{h}):=(f_{h},z_{h})_{{Q_{h}}}+(Mu_{0,h},z_{h}(0))_{{\Omega_{h}}}+\int_{0}^{T}\ell_{\partial\Omega,h}(z_{h})\,\mathrm{d}t.

(25)

Depending on the boundary conditions, we have

\begin{split}\ell_{\partial\Omega,h}(z_{h}):=\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}^{\textrm{D}}_{h}}&-(A^{\textrm{up}}_{\mathbf{n}}u_{D,h},z_{h})_{F}-\Theta(Du_{D,h},\boldsymbol{\nabla}z_{h}\mathbf{n}_{F})_{F}+\sigma_{F,h}(D{u}_{D,h},z_{h})_{F}\\
&+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}^{\textrm{N}}_{h}}-(2A^{\textrm{up}}_{\mathbf{n}}(0,v_{N,h})^{\top},z_{h})_{F}-\Theta(D(0,v_{N,h})^{\top},\boldsymbol{\nabla}z_{h}\mathbf{n}_{F})_{F}\\
&+\sigma_{F,h}(D(0,v_{N,h})^{\top},z_{h})_{F}+((p_{N,h},0)^{\top},z_{h})_{F},\end{split}

where
f_{h},u_{0,h},u_{D,h},u_{N,h}\in Z_{h}
denote suitably interpolated data. Note that the added terms in (
2.3
) ensure consistency, see Lemma
3.2
. The complete linear variational problem discretizing equation (
2.1
) is given by

\text{solve for }u_{h}\in{Z}_{h}\text{ such that }b_{h}^{\prime}(u_{h},z_{h};\tilde{u}_{h})=\ell_{h}(z_{h})\text{ for all }z_{h}\in Z_{h}

(26)

with

b_{h}^{\prime}(u_{h},z_{h};\tilde{u}_{h})=m_{h}(u_{h},z_{h})+a_{h}(u_{h},z_{h})+d_{h}(u_{h},z_{h})+n_{h}^{\prime}(u_{h},z_{h};\tilde{u}_{h}).

To solve the nonlinear problem (
23
), we apply a Newton-Raphson method, see
[
Deuflhard2011
]
, described in Algorithm
2.1
, solving the linear problem (
26
) in each iteration. To this end, we define the continuous residual as

r(u):=G(u)
using (
3
), and the discrete residual as

r_{h}({u_{h}}):=b_{h}(u_{h},\cdot)-\ell_{h}.

(27)

Fixing a tolerance constant
c_{\textrm{tol}}>0
and an initial guess
u_{h}^{(0)}
, the following algorithm generates a sequence
u^{(n)}_{h}\in Z_{h}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Algorithm 2.1

(Newton’s method)
.

Set
r_{h}^{(0)}=r_{h}({u_{h}^{(0)}})
and set
n=0
.

While
\|r_{h}^{(n)}\|_{L^{2}(Q)}>c_{\textrm{\rm tol}}
do:

1.

Solve for
u_{h}\in Z_{h}
such that
b_{h}(u_{h},z_{h};{u_{h}^{(n)}})=r_{h}^{(n)}(z_{h})\ \forall z_{h}\in Z_{h}
.

2.

Update
u^{(n+1)}_{h}=u^{(n)}_{h}-u_{h}
,
r_{h}^{(n+1)}=r_{h}({u^{(n+1)}_{h}})
,
n\to n+1
.

3
Linearized system

In this section, we treat the well-posedness and convergence analysis of the linear discrete problem (
26
) under the condition that
\tilde{u}_{h}
is sufficiently small with respect to a suitable norm.

3.1
Preliminaries

We assume that
\sigma_{F,h}
is chosen sufficiently large such that

d_{h}(z_{h},z_{h})\geq c_{\Theta}\int_{0}^{T}\lvert\!\lvert\!\lvert z_{h}(t)\rvert\!\rvert\!\rvert_{h}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\quad\forall z_{h}\in Z_{h},

(28)

where
c_{\Theta}>0
is independent of
h
and
\lvert\!\lvert\!\lvert\cdot\rvert\!\rvert\!\rvert_{h}
is defined below in (
30
). We refer to, e.g.,
[
dolejsi_discontinuous_2015
, Corollary 2.41]
for a proof of (
28
) and possible choices of the penalty parameter

\sigma_{F,h}
in (
20
).

The analysis is carried out with respect to the
h
-dependent norm

\|u_{h}\|_{Z_{h}}:=\left(\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\|M^{\frac{1}{2}}[u_{h}]_{j}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}c_{A}\|A_{\mathbf{n}}[u_{h}]\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega_{h})}^{2}+c_{\Theta}\lvert\!\lvert\!\lvert u_{h}\rvert\!\rvert\!\rvert^{2}_{h}\,\mathrm{d}t\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\text{ in }Q\,,

(29)

where with
u_{h}(t)=u_{h}=(p_{h},v_{h})^{\top}
for fixed
t

\lvert\!\lvert\!\lvert u_{h}\rvert\!\rvert\!\rvert_{h}:=\left(\|D^{\frac{1}{2}}\boldsymbol{\nabla}u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}^{2}+\beta\!\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}\setminus\mathcal{F}^{\textrm{N}}_{h}}\sigma_{F,h}\|[p_{h}]_{F}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(F)}+\zeta\!\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}\sigma_{F,h}\|[v_{h}]_{F}\|_{L^{2}(F)}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\text{ in }\Omega

(30)

denotes the natural norm used for interior penalty methods. Note that in (
30
), as in the previous section,
\boldsymbol{\nabla}
is understood as acting piece wise in each cell, when applied to discontinuous functions. For better readability, we sometimes write
\lvert\!\lvert\!\lvert u_{h}\rvert\!\rvert\!\rvert_{\star,h}:=\left(\int_{0}^{T}\lvert\!\lvert\!\lvert u_{h}\rvert\!\rvert\!\rvert_{h}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}
.

As corresponding broken Sobolev space, we consider

H^{1}(\mathcal{K}_{h}):=\{w\in L^{2}(\Omega_{h};\mathbb{R}^{1+d}):w_{K}\in H^{1}(K;\mathbb{R}^{1+d})\ \forall K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}\}

and equip it with the norm
\lvert\!\lvert\!\lvert\cdot\rvert\!\rvert\!\rvert_{h}
.
In the analysis, we use the broken Poincaré inequality

\|u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}\leq c_{\textrm{P}}\lvert\!\lvert\!\lvert u_{h}\rvert\!\rvert\!\rvert_{h}\quad\forall u_{h}\in H^{1}(\mathcal{K}_{h}),

(31)

where
c_{\textrm{P}}>0
is independent of
h
, cf.
[
Brenner2003
]
.
Moreover, we rely on the broken Sobolev inequality

\|u_{h}\|_{L^{4}(\Omega_{h})}\leq c_{\textrm{S}}\lvert\!\lvert\!\lvert u_{h}\rvert\!\rvert\!\rvert_{h}\quad\forall u_{h}\in H^{1}(\mathcal{K}_{h}),

(32)

where
c_{\textrm{S}}>0
is independent of
h
. This inequality is, as the broken Poincaré inequality, just a special case of more general broken Sobolev inequalities.
For a proof of these and other generalized results, we refer the reader to
[
Lasis:2003
, Theorem 3.7]
.
For inequalities (
31
), (
32
) to hold, we rely on the assumption that
\mathcal{F}^{\textrm{D}}_{h}
has positive
(d-1)
-dimensional Lebesgue measure, the assumed mesh regularity, as well as
d\in\{2,3\}
.

A key ingredient in the analysis is the following lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

\|\cdot\|_{Z_{h}}
is a norm on
Z_{h}
.

Proof.

\|\cdot\|_{Z_{h}}
is a semi norm, since it is defined by adding different
L^{2}
-norms together. To show positive definiteness, let
z_{h}\in Z_{h}
be such that
\|z_{h}\|_{Z_{h}}=0
. In particular, this implies
\lvert\!\lvert\!\lvert z_{h}\rvert\!\rvert\!\rvert_{h}=0
, which leads to
z_{h}=0
, see
[
dolejsi_discontinuous_2015
, Section 6]
for details.
∎

3.2
Consistency

For a consistency result, we define a right hand side with non discrete data, cf. (
25
), (
2.3
), as

\begin{split}\ell(z_{h}):=&(f,z_{h})_{{Q_{h}}}+(Mu_{0},z_{h}(0,\cdot))_{{\Omega_{h}}}\\
&+\int_{0}^{T}\Bigg(-(A^{\textrm{up}}_{\mathbf{n}}u_{D},z_{h})_{\Gamma_{\textrm{D}}}-\Theta(Du_{D},\boldsymbol{\nabla}z_{h}\mathbf{n})_{\Gamma_{\textrm{D}}}+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}^{\textrm{D}}_{h}}\sigma_{F,h}(D{u}_{D},z_{h})_{F}+((p_{N},0)^{\top},z_{h})_{\Gamma_{\textrm{N}}}\\
&\qquad\quad-2(A^{\textrm{up}}_{\mathbf{n}}(0,v_{N})^{\top},z_{h})_{\Gamma_{\textrm{N}}}-\Theta(D(0,v_{N})^{\top},\boldsymbol{\nabla}z_{h}\mathbf{n})_{\Gamma_{\textrm{N}}}+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}^{\textrm{N}}_{h}}\sigma_{F,h}(D(0,v_{N})^{\top},z_{h})_{F}\Bigg)\,\mathrm{d}t.\end{split}

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\hat{u}\in U
be the solution to the nonlinear equation (
2.1
), and let
u\in U
denote the solution to the linearized equation (
2.1
) with given
\tilde{u}\in U
. Then, it holds

b_{h}(\hat{u},z_{h})=\ell(z_{h})\quad\forall z_{h}\in Z_{h}

(33)

and for any
\tilde{u}_{h}\in Z_{h}

b_{h}^{\prime}(u,z_{h};\tilde{u}_{h})=\ell(z_{h})+n_{h}^{\prime}(u,z_{h};\tilde{u}_{h}-\tilde{u})\quad\forall z_{h}\in Z_{h}.

(34)

Proof.

Due to the regularity of
\hat{u}\in U
, all jump terms in (
26
) involving interior faces vanish. In particular, for any
z_{h}\in Z_{h}
we have

\begin{split}m_{h}(\hat{u},z_{h})+a_{h}(\hat{u},z_{h})=(L\hat{u}&,z_{h})_{Q_{h}}+(u_{0},z_{h}(0,\cdot))_{\Omega_{h}}\\
&-\int_{0}^{T}(A^{\textrm{up}}_{\mathbf{n}}u_{D},z_{h})_{\Gamma_{\textrm{D}}}+2(A^{\textrm{up}}_{\mathbf{n}}(0,v_{N})^{\top},z_{h})_{\Gamma_{\textrm{N}}}\,\mathrm{d}t,\end{split}

and with
z_{h}=(q_{h},w_{h})^{\top}
, using integration by parts, we obtain

\begin{split}d_{h}(\hat{u},z_{h})&=\int_{0}^{T}\sum_{K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}}(D\boldsymbol{\nabla}\hat{u},\boldsymbol{\nabla}z_{h})_{K}+\rho_{h}^{\Theta}(\hat{u},z_{h})\,\mathrm{d}t\\
&=\int_{0}^{T}\sum_{K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}}\left(-(D\Delta\hat{u},z_{h})_{K}+(D\boldsymbol{\nabla}\hat{u}\mathbf{n}_{K},z_{h})_{\partial K}\right)+\rho_{h}^{\Theta}(\hat{u},z_{h})\,\mathrm{d}t\\
&=-(D\Delta\hat{u},z_{h})_{Q_{h}}+\int_{0}^{T}\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}}(D\boldsymbol{\nabla}\hat{u}\mathbf{n}_{F},z_{h})_{F}+\rho_{h}^{\Theta}(\hat{u},z_{h})\,\mathrm{d}t\\
&=(-D\Delta\hat{u},z_{h})_{Q_{h}}+\int_{0}^{T}\Bigg(\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}^{\textrm{D}}_{h}}\left(-\Theta(Du_{D,h},\boldsymbol{\nabla}z_{h}\mathbf{n}_{F})_{F}+\sigma_{F,h}(Du_{D,h},z_{h})_{F}\right)\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+(p_{N},q_{h})_{\Gamma_{\textrm{N}}}+\zeta\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}^{\textrm{N}}_{h}}\left(-\Theta(v_{N,h},\boldsymbol{\nabla}w_{h}\mathbf{n}_{F})_{F}+\sigma_{F,h}(v_{N,h},w_{h})_{F}\right)\Bigg)\,\mathrm{d}t,\end{split}

where we use that
\langle\boldsymbol{\nabla}\hat{u}\rangle_{F}=\boldsymbol{\nabla}\hat{u}
and
[\boldsymbol{\nabla}\hat{u}]_{F}=0
for all
F\in\mathcal{F}^{\textrm{int}}_{h}
, cf.
[
dipietro2012
, Section 4.2.1.1]
.

By testing equation (
2.1
) locally in every space-time cell with
z_{h}\in Z_{h}
, and summing over every space-time cell we obtain

(L\hat{u}-D\Delta\hat{u}+N(\hat{u},\hat{u}),z_{h})_{Q_{h}}=(f,z_{h})_{Q_{h}}.

(35)

Combining the previous equation with equations (
3.2
) and (
3.2
), we arrive at (
33
). Analogously, testing equation (
2.1
) gives

(Lu-D\Delta u+N(u,\tilde{u})+N(\tilde{u},u),z_{h})_{Q_{h}}=(f,z_{h})_{Q_{h}}.

(36)

Since equations (
3.2
) and (
3.2
) also hold when
\hat{u}
is replaced by
{u}
, we obtain

\begin{split}b_{h}^{\prime}(u,z_{h};\tilde{u}_{h})&=b_{h}^{\prime}(u,z_{h};\tilde{u}_{h})+n_{h}^{\prime}(u,z_{h};\tilde{u})-n_{h}^{\prime}(u,z_{h};\tilde{u})\\
&=(m_{h}+a_{h}+d_{h})(u,z_{h})+n_{h}^{\prime}(u,z_{h},\tilde{u}_{h})+n_{h}^{\prime}(u,z_{h};\tilde{u})-n_{h}^{\prime}(u,z_{h};\tilde{u})\\
&=\ell(z_{h})+n_{h}^{\prime}(u,z_{h};\tilde{u}_{h}-\tilde{u}).\end{split}

∎

3.3
Well-posedness

In this section, we establish discrete well-posedness of (
26
) for sufficiently small
\tilde{u}_{h}
. For the dual space of a normed space
X
, we write
X^{\prime}
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\ell_{h}\in Z_{h}^{\prime}
. There exists
\varrho>0
such that for all
\tilde{u}_{h}\in Z_{h}
with

\max_{t\in[0,T]}\lvert\!\lvert\!\lvert\tilde{u}_{h}(t)\rvert\!\rvert\!\rvert_{h}\leq\varrho

(37)

the variational problem (
26
)

b_{h}^{\prime}(u_{h},z_{h};\tilde{u}_{h})=\ell_{h}(z_{h})\quad\forall z_{h}\in Z_{h}

(38)

has a unique solution
u_{h}\in Z_{h}
. Moreover, there exists
c_{\varrho}>0
independent of
h
such that the a priori estimate

\|u_{h}\|_{Z_{h}}\leq c_{\varrho}^{-1}\|\ell_{h}\|_{Z_{h}^{\prime}}

(39)

holds.

Proof.

Let
u_{h}\in Z_{h}
. We show that
b^{\prime}_{h}(u_{h},z_{h};\tilde{u}_{h})=0\quad\forall z_{h}\in{Z}_{h}
implies
u_{h}=0
, by proving coercivity of the bilinear form
b_{h}^{\prime}(\cdot,\cdot;\tilde{u}_{h}):Z_{h}\times Z_{h}\to\mathbb{R}
with respect to
\|\cdot\|_{Z_{h}}
.

Using relation (
18
) and integration by parts in time we obtain, cf.
[
corallo2022
, Eq. 30]
,

m_{h}(u_{h},u_{h})+a_{h}(u_{h},u_{h})\geq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\|[u_{h}]_{j}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}c_{A}\|A_{\mathbf{n}}[u_{h}]\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega_{h})}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t.

(40)

To estimate the trilinear form
n_{h}^{\prime}(\cdot,\cdot;\cdot)
, we combine Hölder’s inequality with the broken Sobolev inequality (
32
) to obtain

\begin{split}|n_{h}^{\prime}(u_{h},u_{h};\tilde{u}_{h})|&\leq\int_{0}^{T}\sum_{K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}}|(N(u_{h},\tilde{u}_{h})+N(\tilde{u}_{h},u_{h}),u_{h})_{K}|\,\mathrm{d}t\\
&\leq\mu\int_{0}^{T}\sum_{K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}}\!\|\boldsymbol{\nabla}u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(K)}\|u_{h}\|_{L^{4}(K)}\|\tilde{u}_{h}\|_{L^{4}(K)}+\|\boldsymbol{\nabla}\tilde{u}_{h}\|_{L^{2}(K)}\|u_{h}\|_{L^{4}(K)}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\\
&\leq\mu\int_{0}^{T}\Bigg(\left(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}}\|\boldsymbol{\nabla}u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}}\|u_{h}\|_{L^{4}(K)}^{4}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}\left(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}}\|\tilde{u}_{h}\|_{L^{4}(K)}^{4}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}\\
&\qquad\qquad+\left(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}}\|\boldsymbol{\nabla}\tilde{u}_{h}\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}}\|u_{h}\|_{L^{4}(K)}^{4}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Bigg)\,\mathrm{d}t\\
&\leq 2\mu c_{\textrm{S}}^{2}D_{\max}\int_{0}^{T}\lvert\!\lvert\!\lvert u_{h}\rvert\!\rvert\!\rvert_{h}^{2}\,\lvert\!\lvert\!\lvert\tilde{u}_{h}\rvert\!\rvert\!\rvert_{h}\,\mathrm{d}t\\
&\leq C_{n}\left(\max_{[0,T]}\,\lvert\!\lvert\!\lvert\tilde{u}_{h}\rvert\!\rvert\!\rvert_{h}\right)\lvert\!\lvert\!\lvert u_{h}\rvert\!\rvert\!\rvert_{\star,h}^{2}\end{split}

with
\mu:=\max\{\gamma,\delta,\eta,\theta\}>0
,
D_{\max}:=\max\{\beta^{-\frac{1}{2}},\zeta^{-\frac{1}{2}}\}
and
C_{n}:={2\mu c_{\textrm{S}}^{2}D_{\max}}>0
.

Combining estimates (
28
), (
40
), (
3.3
), choosing
\varrho\in(0,c_{\Theta}C_{n}^{-1})
, and using the smallness assumption on
\tilde{u}_{h}
(
37
) gives

\begin{split}b^{\prime}_{h}(u_{h},u_{h};\tilde{u}_{h})&\geq m_{h}(u_{h},u_{h})+a_{h}(u_{h},u_{h})+d_{h}(u_{h},u_{h})-|n^{\prime}_{h}(u_{h},u_{h};\tilde{u}_{h})|\\
&\geq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\|[u_{h}]_{j}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}c_{A}\|A_{\mathbf{n}}[u_{h}]\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega_{h})}^{2}+(c_{\Theta}-\varrho C_{n})\lvert\!\lvert\!\lvert u_{h}\rvert\!\rvert\!\rvert^{2}_{h}\,\mathrm{d}t\\
&\geq c_{\varrho}\|u_{h}\|_{Z_{h}}^{2}\end{split}

with
c_{\varrho}:=\min\{1,{c_{\Theta}-\varrho C_{n}}\}
independent of
h
. This shows
coercivity and the a priori estimate (
39
) follows by means of standard methods, see e.g.
[
dipietro2012
, Lemma 1.4]
.
∎

3.4
Convergence in the discontinuous Galerkin norm

In this section, we establish convergence of the linear DG scheme (
26
) with respect to
\|\cdot\|_{Z_{h}}
. To this end, we first introduce relevant projections.
Let
\Pi_{h}:L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{i})\to{{\mathcal{P}}}_{h}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{i})
with
i\in\mathbb{N}
denote the spatial
L^{2}
-projection defined by

(\Pi_{h}u,z_{h})_{\Omega}=(u,z_{h})_{\Omega}\quad\forall z_{h}\in{{\mathcal{P}}}_{h}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{i})

(41)

and
\pi_{h}:C(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{1+d}))\to Z_{h}
denote the space-time projection with the following properties

(i)

\pi_{h}u\in Z_{h}
,

(ii)

(\pi_{h}u)_{j}(x,t_{j})=\Pi_{h}u_{j}(x,t_{j})\text{ for a.e. }x\in\Omega
and
j\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
with
i=1+d
,

(iii)

\int_{I_{j}}(\pi_{h}u-u,z_{h})_{\Omega}\,\mathrm{d}t=0\quad\forall z_{h}\in Z_{h}^{-}
and
j\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
,

cf.
[
dolejsi_discontinuous_2015
, Section 6.1.4]
, where existence and uniqueness among other properties of
\pi_{h}
are shown. We denote by
Z^{-}_{h}
the space obtained when decreasing the polynomial time degrees by one in each space-time cell. Due to this property, we assume for the rest of the paper that, the time degrees of each cell are at least one.
The following result is the basis for an estimate of the numerical error in terms of projection errors.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
u\in U
be the solution to the continuous linear system (
2.1
) with
\tilde{u}\in U
and
u_{h}\in Z_{h}
the solution to the discrete linear problem (
26
) with
\tilde{u}_{h}\in Z_{h}
such that (
37
) holds with
\varrho>0
as in Lemma
3.3
. Then

\begin{split}\|u_{h}-\pi_{h}u\|_{Z_{h}}^{2}\leq\ &C_{Z}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{k}\!\|M^{\frac{1}{2}}(\pi_{h}u-u)_{j}(t_{j})\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}+\int_{0}^{T}\Big(\|\pi_{h}u-u\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega_{h})}^{2}+\lvert\!\lvert\!\lvert\pi_{h}u-u\rvert\!\rvert\!\rvert_{h}^{2}\Big)\,\mathrm{d}t\right)\\
&+C_{R}\left(\|\ell_{h}-\ell\|_{Z_{h}^{\prime}}^{2}+\lvert\!\lvert\!\lvert\tilde{u}_{h}-\tilde{u}\rvert\!\rvert\!\rvert^{2}_{\star,h}\right)\end{split}

with
C_{Z},C_{R}>0
independent of
h
.

Proof.

By consistency, see Lemma
3.2
equation (
34
), it holds for all
z_{h}\in Z_{h}

b_{h}^{\prime}(u_{h}-u,z_{h};\tilde{u}_{h})=b_{h}^{\prime}(u_{h},z_{h};\tilde{u}_{h})-b_{h}^{\prime}(u,z_{h};\tilde{u}_{h})=\ell_{h}(z_{h})-\ell(z_{h})-n_{h}^{\prime}(u,z_{h};\tilde{u}_{h}-\tilde{u}),

(42)

which is equivalent to

b_{h}^{\prime}(u_{h}-\pi_{h}u,z_{h};\tilde{u}_{h})=-b_{h}^{\prime}(\pi_{h}u-u,z_{h};\tilde{u}_{h})+\ell_{h}(z_{h})-\ell(z_{h})-n_{h}^{\prime}(u,z_{h};\tilde{u}_{h}-\tilde{u}).

(43)

Choosing
z_{h}=u_{h}-\pi_{h}u\in Z_{h}
, defining remainder terms
\mathcal{C}_{h}:=|(\ell_{h}-\ell)(u_{h}-\pi_{h}u)|+|n_{h}^{\prime}(u,u_{h}-\pi_{h}u;\tilde{u}_{h}-\tilde{u})|
, and
by coercivity of
b_{h}^{\prime}
derived in the proof of Lemma
3.3
, see equation (
3.3
), we obtain

c_{\varrho}\|u_{h}-\pi_{h}u\|_{Z_{h}}^{2}\leq|b^{\prime}_{h}(\pi_{h}u-u,u_{h}-\pi_{h}u;\tilde{u}_{h})|+\mathcal{C}_{h}.

(44)

For the rest of the proof, we estimate the right hand side of (
44
) further.
Starting with
m_{h}
, we have for all
z_{h}\in Z_{h}

We are now in a position to apply a general existence and convergence result for Newton’s method to the nonlinear discrete problem (

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
X
and
Y
be Banach spaces and
G:\mathcal{D}(G)\subseteq X\to Y
. Suppose that on an open convex set
D_{*}\subseteq\mathcal{D}(G)
,
G
is Fréchet differentiable and
G^{\prime}
is
\chi
-Hölder continuous in
D_{*}
with exponent
\chi\in(0,1]
and constant
\lambda>0
, i.e.,

\|G^{\prime}_{u_{1}}-G^{\prime}_{u_{2}}\|_{\mathcal{L}(X,Y)}\leq\lambda\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|_{X}^{\chi},\quad u_{1},\,u_{2}\in D_{*}.

(63)

For some
u_{*}\in D_{*}
, assume
\Gamma_{*}:=(G^{\prime}_{u_{*}})^{-1}
is defined on all of
\,Y
and

\upsilon=\kappa\lambda\phi^{\chi}\leq\chi_{0}\ \text{ with }\kappa=\|\Gamma_{*}\|_{\mathcal{L}(Y,X)},\quad\phi=\|\Gamma_{*}G(u_{*})\|_{X},

(64)

where
\chi_{0}\in(0,\frac{1}{2}]
is the unique root of

.
Set

\varrho=\frac{\phi(1+\chi)(1-\upsilon)}{(1+\chi)-(2+\chi)\upsilon},\qquad\tilde{\varrho}=\phi\upsilon^{-\frac{1}{\chi}}

(65)

and suppose that

.
Then, the Newton iterates

u^{(n+1)}=u^{(n)}-{G^{\prime-1}_{u^{(n)}}}G(u^{(n)}),\,n=0,1,2,\ldots

starting at

are well-defined, lie in
B^{X}_{\varrho}(u_{*})
and converge to a solution of
G(u)=0
, which is unique in
B^{X}_{\tilde{\varrho}}(u_{*})\cap D_{*}
. Moreover, if strict inequality holds in (
64
), the order of convergence is at least
1+\chi
.

\begin{split}\max_{[0,T]}\lvert\!\lvert\!\lvert u_{h,*}\rvert\!\rvert\!\rvert_{h}&\leq\max_{[0,T]}\lvert\!\lvert\!\lvert u_{h,*}-u\rvert\!\rvert\!\rvert_{h}+\max_{[0,T]}\lvert\!\lvert\!\lvert u\rvert\!\rvert\!\rvert_{h}\\
&\leq C_{\textrm{inv}}\tau^{-\frac{1}{2}}\lvert\!\lvert\!\lvert u_{h,*}-u\rvert\!\rvert\!\rvert_{\star,h}+C_{U}\|u\|_{U}\\
&\leq C_{\textrm{inv}}h^{-\frac{\omega}{2}}\sqrt{c_{r}}c_{\Theta}^{-1}\|u_{h,*}-u\|_{Z_{h}}+C_{U}\varrho_{U}\\
&\leq C_{\textrm{inv}}\sqrt{c_{r}}c_{\Theta}^{-1}C_{*}\bar{h}^{\min\{p+\vartheta-\frac{\omega}{2},p+1-\omega\}}+C_{U}\varrho_{U}\end{split}

where
C_{U}>0
from Remark

is used,

comes from an inverse inequality to estimate the
L^{\infty}
-norm with respect to the
L^{2}
-norm in time, mesh balance (

) and
\|u_{h,*}-u\|_{Z_{h}}<C_{*}h^{\min\{p+\vartheta,p+1-\frac{\omega}{2}\}}
is used. Choosing
\bar{h}
and
\varrho_{U}
small enough, we obtain due to Lemma
3.3
well-posedness of the variational problem

b_{h}^{\prime}(\hat{u}_{h},z_{h},u_{h,*})=\hat{\ell}_{h}(z_{h})\quad\forall z_{h}\in Z_{h}

(72)

with an
h
-independent a priori estimate
\|\hat{u}_{h}\|_{Z_{h}}\leq c_{*}^{-1}\|\hat{\ell}_{h}\|_{Z_{h}^{\prime}}
for all
h<\bar{h}
.
If
h<\bar{h}<1
and if we deduce

from the apriori estimate,
it follows that
G^{\prime}_{h,u_{h,*}}\colon Z_{h}\to Z_{h}^{\prime}
is invertible for all
u_{*}\in D_{h,*}
.

To establish the Hölder estimate of
G_{h}^{\prime}
, we proceed analogously to estimate (

) and combine it again with an inverse inequality to obtain for
u_{1},u_{2}\in D_{h,\star}
and all

\begin{split}|b^{\prime}_{h}(u_{h},z_{h};u_{1})-\ell_{h}(z_{h})-&b^{\prime}_{h}(u_{h},z_{h};u_{2})+\ell_{h}(z_{h})|\\
&=|n^{\prime}_{h}(u_{h},z_{h};u_{1}-u_{2})|\\
&\leq C_{n}c_{\Theta}^{-2}\max_{[0,T]}\lvert\!\lvert\!\lvert u_{1}-u_{2}\rvert\!\rvert\!\rvert_{h}\|z_{h}\|_{Z_{h}}\|u_{h}\|_{Z_{h}}\\
&\leq C_{n}c_{\Theta}^{-3}C_{\textrm{inv}}\sqrt{c_{r}}2h^{-\frac{\omega}{2}}\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|_{Z_{h}}\|z_{h}\|_{Z_{h}}\|u_{h}\|_{Z_{h}}\\
&\leq C_{n}c_{\Theta}^{-3}C_{\textrm{inv}}\sqrt{c_{r}}2h^{-\frac{\omega}{2}}\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|_{Z_{h}}^{1-\chi}\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|_{Z_{h}}^{\chi}\|z_{h}\|_{Z_{h}}\|u_{h}\|_{Z_{h}}\\
&\leq C_{n}c_{\Theta}^{-3}C_{\textrm{inv}}\sqrt{c_{r}}h^{-\frac{\omega}{2}}(2C_{*}h^{\min\{p+\vartheta,p+1-\frac{\omega}{2}\}})^{1-\chi}\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|_{Z_{h}}^{\chi}\|z_{h}\|_{Z_{h}}\|u_{h}\|_{Z_{h}}\\
&\leq\lambda\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|_{Z_{h}}^{\chi}\|z_{h}\|_{Z_{h}}\|u_{h}\|_{Z_{h}},\end{split}

so that the
\chi
-Hölder continuity holds with
\lambda\leq C_{n}c_{\Theta}^{-3}C_{\textrm{inv}}\sqrt{c_{r}}(2C_{*})^{1-\chi}
independent of
h
, due to the assumption
(1-\chi)\min\{p+\vartheta,p+1-\frac{\omega}{2}\}\geq\frac{\omega}{2}
and
\bar{h}<1
.

In order to fulfill assumption (
64
), we establish an estimate on
\|G(u_{h,*})\|_{Z_{h}^{\prime}}
.
Let

denote a function such that

.

Then by triangle inequality and as in the proof of Lemma
3.4

\begin{split}|m_{h}(u_{h,*},z_{h})|&=\bigg|\sum_{j=1}^{k}\int_{t_{j-1}}^{t_{j}}(M\partial_{t}u_{h,*},z_{h})_{\Omega_{h}}\,\mathrm{d}t+(M[u_{h,*}]_{j-1},z_{h,j}(t_{j-1}))_{\Omega_{h}}\bigg|\\
&=\bigg|\sum_{j=1}^{k}-\int_{t_{j-1}}^{t_{j}}(Mu_{h,*},\partial_{t}z_{h})_{\Omega_{h}}\,\mathrm{d}t-(M(u_{h,*})_{j}(t_{j}),[z_{h}]_{j})_{\Omega_{h}}\bigg|\\

where we use a trace like inequality on

, so that time evaluations can be estimated by the

norm. As in the proof of Lemma
3.4
we have

\begin{split}|a_{h}(u_{h,*},z_{h})|&\leq\int_{0}^{T}\|\boldsymbol{\nabla}u_{h,*}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}\|z_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}+2C_{A}\|u_{h,*}\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega_{h})}\|A_{\mathbf{n}}[z_{h}]\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega_{h})}\,\mathrm{d}t\\
&\leq C_{\tilde{A}}(\|u_{h,*}\|_{Z_{h}}+\|u_{h,*}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\partial\Omega_{h}))})\|z_{h}\|_{Z_{h}},\end{split}

and

\begin{split}|d_{h}(u_{h,*},z_{h})|&\leq\int_{0}^{T}(\|D^{\frac{1}{2}}\boldsymbol{\nabla}u_{h,*}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})})\|D^{\frac{1}{2}}\boldsymbol{\nabla}z_{h}\|_{L^{2}({\Omega_{h}})}+|\rho_{h}^{\Theta}(u_{h,*},z_{h})|\,\mathrm{d}t\\
&\leq\int_{0}^{T}(1+2C_{\textrm{ip}}D_{\max}^{2})\lvert\!\lvert\!\lvert u_{h,*}\rvert\!\rvert\!\rvert_{h}\lvert\!\lvert\!\lvert z_{h}\rvert\!\rvert\!\rvert_{h}\,\mathrm{d}t\\
&\leq C_{D}\|u_{h,*}\|_{Z_{h}}\|z_{h}\|_{Z_{h}},\end{split}

as well as from estimate (

\begin{split}|n_{h}(u_{h,*},z_{h})|=\frac{1}{2}|n^{\prime}_{h}(u_{h,*},z_{h};u_{h_{*}})|&\leq\frac{1}{2}|n^{\prime}_{h}(u_{h,\star},z_{h};u_{h,*}-u)|+\frac{1}{2}|n^{\prime}_{h}(u_{h,*},z_{h};u)|\\
&\leq C_{N}\|u_{h,*}\|_{Z_{h}}\|z_{h}\|_{Z_{h}}\end{split}

with

. Combining the estimates, we conclude with the discrete Poincaré inequality (
31
) and triangle inequality

\begin{split}\|G(u_{h,*})\|_{Z_{h}^{\prime}}&\leq C_{G}(\|u_{h,*}\|_{Z_{h}}+\|u_{h,*}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\partial\Omega_{h}))}\\
&+\|\partial_{t}u_{h,*}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}+\|\partial_{t}(u_{h,*}-u_{*})\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))})\end{split}

with

independent of

.
Note that by triangle inequality

\|u_{h,*}\|_{Z_{h}}\leq\|u_{h,*}-u\|_{Z_{h}}+\|u\|_{Z_{h}}\leq C_{*}\bar{h}^{\xi}+C_{U}\varrho_{u},

(73)

and in combination with a discrete trace inequality

\begin{split}\|u_{h,*}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\partial\Omega_{h}))}&\leq C_{\textrm{tr}}h^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|u_{h,*}-u\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega_{h}))}+\|u\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\partial\Omega_{h}))}\\

Using an inverse inequality for the time derivative we have

\begin{split}\|\partial_{t}u_{*}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}&\leq C_{\textrm{inv}}\tau^{-1}\|u_{h,*}-u\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}+\|\partial_{t}u\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2})}\\

Using an inverse inequality for the time derivative and projection error estimate (

\begin{split}\|\partial_{t}(u_{h,*}-u_{*})\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}&\leq C_{\textrm{inv}}\tau^{-1}\|u_{*}-u_{h,*}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}\\
&\leq C_{\textrm{inv}}\sqrt{c_{r}}C_{\tilde{\pi}}T^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|u_{*}\|_{V}\bar{h}^{\min\{p+1,\nu\}-\omega}\end{split}

with

independent of

.
Therefore, choosing

,

and

independent of

.

Remark 4.1
.

We note that the assumptions
\|u\|_{U}\leq\varrho_{U}
and
\|\ell_{h}\|_{Z_{h}^{\prime}}\leq\varrho_{\ell}
can be fulfilled, if the data of the continuous problem (

) are chosen small enough, cf.
[
QuadraticWave
]
. Since uniform space-time methods are equivalent to time stepping methods, local existence of a discrete solution always holds if

is chosen small enough. However, in Theorem
4.1
we show global in time well-posedness, in which case a convergence analysis without smallness assumptions (
67
) is not possible, since then global existence of the continuous solution
u
is not clear.

Remark 4.2
.

In comparison to Theorem
3.1
we can no longer take
p=0
, since then the assumptions are not fulfilled. Taking

with
s=2
,

all assumptions are fulfilled and we obtain convergence if

u\in H^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{1+d}))\cap W^{2,\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{1+d}))\cap C([0,T];H^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{1+d}).

A convergence analysis for less regular solutions is not contained in this paper.

5
Numerical experiments

For the numerical experiments, we aim to model sound waves propagating through soft tissue. To this end, we choose

\displaystyle\alpha=1,\ \beta=10^{-4},\ \gamma=6,\ \delta=1,\ \varepsilon=1,\ \zeta=10^{-4},\ \eta=1,\ \theta=1.

(77)

Since system (
2.1
) is in nondimensional form, most parameters are set to one. The value
\gamma=6
is derived from the nonlinearity parameter
\frac{B}{A}\approx 5
in tissue, see
[
Hamilton:1997
]
for details. The damping parameters are chosen to approximately match the viscosity coefficients observed in tissue.
Due to computational constraints, we restrict our study to the case
d=2
. The approximate domain
\Omega_{h}\times I_{h}
is discretized using regular Lagrange quadrilateral elements with
4\tau=h
. The mesh is refined such that
h=2^{-n_{r}}
,
n_{r}\geq 1
. We set the penalty constant to
C_{\sigma}=50
. In our experiments, the linear systems to be solved exhibit a block-diagonal structure, so we solve them using GMRES in combination with a block Gauss–Seidel preconditioner.
The implementation is carried out in M++, see
[
2
]
, and can run in parallel. The complete source code is available at

https://gitlab.kit.edu/kit/mpp/mpp
.

5.1

Error indicator for
p
-adaptivity

In this paper, we only consider residual based adaptivity. We remark that a duality based adaptivity with respect to a goal functional is also possible, but not investigated in this paper. We apply
p
-adaptivity as a variant of the maximum marking strategy described in
[
DoerflerFindeisenWienersZiegler:2019
, Algorithm 1]
and
[
11
, Chapter 4.2]
, with the additional feature that
derefinement is included. Thus, the degree of a space-time cell is increased if
\tilde{\varsigma}_{R}\geq\vartheta_{\mathrm{ref}}\max_{R^{\prime}\in\mathcal{R}_{h}}\tilde{\varsigma}_{R^{\prime}}

and decreased if
\tilde{\varsigma}_{R}\leq\vartheta_{\mathrm{deref}}\max_{R^{\prime}\in\mathcal{R}_{h}}\tilde{\varsigma}_{R^{\prime}}
, depending on
\vartheta_{\mathrm{ref}}>\vartheta_{\mathrm{deref}}>0
.
In
[
DoerflerFindeisenWienersZiegler:2019
]
, for the problem
(M\partial_{t}+A)u=f
the following local error estimator for a space-time cell
R=(I_{j}\times K)
is used

\begin{split}\varsigma_{R}^{2}=h\|(M\partial_{t}+A)u_{h}-f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(I\times K)}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\|M^{\frac{1}{2}}[u_{h}]_{j}\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{I}\|A_{\mathbf{n}}[u_{h}]\|_{L^{2}(\partial K)}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t.\end{split}

We, therefore, use the following error indicator to account for the nonlinear terms

\begin{split}\tilde{\varsigma}_{R}^{2}=\ &h\|(M\partial_{t}+A)u_{h}+N(u_{h},u_{h})-f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(I\times K)}\\
&+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\|M^{\frac{1}{2}}[u_{h}]_{j}\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{I}\|A_{\mathbf{n}}[u_{h}]\|_{L^{2}(\partial K)}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\end{split}

and neglect diffusion terms, since they are small in our application. When advancing to the next refinement level, the coarse solution is used as the initial guess for Newton’s method. For each refined cell, both the temporal and spatial polynomial degrees are increased by one.

Conclusion

In this work, we have developed a space-time DG method for a nonlinear acoustic equation, extending the framework of linear symmetric Friedrichs systems. We have rigorously established the well-posedness of the scheme and proved convergence in the natural DG norm. Numerical experiments confirm the theoretical results and illustrate the potential of

p
-adaptivity for improving accuracy. While adaptivity shows benefits for high resolution cases, its effectiveness at moderate resolutions remains limited, highlighting the need for further investigation and optimization. Overall, the proposed approach provides a robust and flexible framework for the numerical simulation of nonlinear acoustic waves, paving the way for future studies.

A mathematically rigorous convergence analysis of the

p
-adaptive algorithm is beyond the scope of this paper and remains an open question in the literature. Future investigations could explore
hp
-adaptive algorithms or hybrid DG methods to further enhance efficiency.

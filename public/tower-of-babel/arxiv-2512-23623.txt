Rotationally symmetric translating solitons of fully nonlinear extrinsic geometric flows: Classification and Applications
José Torres Santaella

Abstract
. We study rotationally symmetric translators for fully nonlinear extrinsic geometric flows driven by a curvature function, and we establish the fine asymptotics of bowl-type evolutions and, when admissible, the construction and classification of catenoidal-type solutions, together with their asymptotic behavior. Under natural structural and convexity assumptions, we also prove rigidity and uniqueness results within appropriate classes of graphical translators of such curvature flows.

1.
Introduction

Translating solitons of extrinsic geometric flows whose normal velocity is given by a positive curvature function arise as canonical models for type II singularities, where curvature concentration behaves more irregularly than for spheres and cylinders during their deformation, see
[
1
,
11
]
. Moreover, they also serve as building blocks for the long-term behavior of these extrinsic curvature flows, see
[
9
]
.

These solitons evolve by translation along a unit direction under the deformation of the flow. In particular, rotationally symmetric models are of special interest due to the rigidity phenomena that they induce within certain families of curvature functions, see
[
6
]
and references therein. For example, in the mean curvature flow, these models include the bowl soliton and catenoidal translators, which play a central role in the construction of
\Delta
-wing solutions, thus completing, together with grim-reaper cylinders, the classification of translating solitons that can be written as graphs on convex domains in space, and therefore ruling out some possible singular behavior for this flow in space, see
[
7
]
.

The first goal of this article is to develop a unified framework for rotationally symmetric translators of
\gamma
-flows, where
\gamma(\lambda)
is a curvature function which it is also the normal velocity of a family of evolving hypersurfaces. The class of curvature functions that we are interested in this article are positive or signed,
\alpha
-homogeneous and symmetric function that are increasing in each variable when they are evaluated in the principal curvatures of this family of hypersurfaces. Then, by using an implicit formulation of the translator equation in terms of the functions
x=g_{\pm}(y,\pm 1)
associated with the level sets of
\tilde{\gamma}(x,y,\ldots,y)=\pm 1
, we establish the fine asymptotic expansion at infinity of bowl-type translators, see Theorems
4.1
and
4.8
.

Our second main contribution is the construction and classification of rotationally symmetric catenoidal translators for suitable signed curvature functions (Section
5
). For each
R>0
, we construct the catenoidal translator
W_{R}
as a hypersurface of revolution generated by a curve with a single neck of radius
R
, and we show that, after removing a large ball,
W_{R}
possesses two branches given by the graph of a function. Furthermore, we prove that its upper branch has the same bowl-type asymptotics as in the positive curvature case, while the lower branch exhibits either a bowl-type end or a different growth regime, depending on the behavior of
g_{-}(\cdot,-1)
near the origin, see Theorem
5.1
and the pictures below.

Using these rotationally symmetric models, we obtain geometric consequences for curvature functions that satisfy
\gamma(0,1,\ldots,1)>0
and
\alpha>\frac{1}{3}
:

•

We show that any smooth, strictly convex entire
\gamma
-translator that is smoothly asymptotic to the bowl-type solution must coincide with it up to vertical translation, see Theorem
6.1
.

•

We prove that there is no strictly convex translator contained inside a round vertical cylinder
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{R}
, see Corollary
6.8
.

We organize the paper as follows. In Section
2
, we introduce the geometric setting, notation, and structural assumptions on the curvature function. In Section
3
, we develop the barrier method for the construction of the catenoidal examples. In Section
4
, we derive the fine asymptotic behavior for bowl-type evolutions. In Section
5
we carry out the construction and classification of catenoidal translators. In Section
6
we prove the rigidity and uniqueness results within appropriate classes of graphical translators under the given structural hypotheses.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section we lay the foundations for the article by defining the class of extrinsic curvature flows determined by a curvature function, either positive or signed, and by introducing the evolutions given by translation in a fixed unit direction, known as translating solitons. We also establish the framework for the rotationally symmetric models of these self-similar evolutions.

Firstly, given an immersed hypersurface
\Sigma_{0}=F_{0}(\Sigma)\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
, which we assume smooth throughout the article unless stated otherwise, we recall some basic notions of extrinsic geometry in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
. The principal curvatures
\lambda(p)=(\lambda_{1}(p),\ldots,\lambda_{n}(p))
of
p\in\Sigma_{0}
are the eigenvalues of the shape operator
\mathcal{W}:T_{p}\Sigma_{0}\to T_{p}\Sigma_{0}
, defined at
p\in\Sigma_{0}
by
\mathcal{W}(\vec{X})=-\nabla_{\vec{X}}\vec{\nu}
, where
\nu
is a unit normal vector with a fixed orientation in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
and
\nabla
is the Euclidean connection.

2.1.
The class of
\gamma
-flows

The class of extrinsic geometric flows that we consider consists of evolutions of a given initial hypersurface
\Sigma_{0}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
described by an immersed family of hypersurfaces
\Sigma_{t}=F(\Sigma,t)
whose normal velocity depends on the principal curvatures of
\Sigma_{t}
.

Definition 2.1
.

Given a symmetric smooth function
\gamma:\Gamma\to(0,\infty)
supported over a symmetric cone, we say that an immersed hypersurface
\Sigma_{0}=F_{0}(\Sigma)
in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
evolves under the
\gamma
-flow if there exists a one-parameter family of immersions
F:\Sigma\times[0,T)\to\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
that solves the Cauchy problem

(1)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}F(x,t)=-\gamma(\lambda(x,t))\vec{\nu}(x,t),&\mbox{in }\Sigma\times(0,T),\\[4.0pt]
F(x,0)=F_{0}(x),&\end{cases}

where
\lambda(x,t)=(\lambda_{1}(x,t),\ldots,\lambda_{n}(x,t))\in\Gamma
is the principal curvature vector associated with
\vec{\nu}(x,t)
on
\Sigma_{t}=F(\Sigma,t)
, and
\vec{\nu}(x,t)
is a unit normal vector of
\Sigma_{t}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
with a fixed orientation.

Next we specify the class of curvature functions
\gamma
that we will work with.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an open symmetric cone

1

Invariant under positive scaling, with the origin as a vertex.

\Gamma_{+}=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\lambda_{i}>0\}

\gamma:\Gamma\to(0,\infty)

\alpha
-homogeneous curvature function if it satisfies:

(1)

\gamma(\lambda)
is smooth and symmetric, i.e.,
\gamma(\lambda_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,\lambda_{\sigma(n)})=\gamma(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})
for every permutation

\{1,\ldots,n\}
.

(2)

\gamma(\lambda)
is strictly increasing in each coordinate, i.e.,
\dfrac{\partial\gamma}{\partial\lambda_{i}}(\lambda)>0

.

(3)

\gamma(\lambda)
is
\alpha

\gamma(c\lambda)=c^{\alpha}\gamma(\lambda)

.

This class of curvature functions includes, for
\alpha=1
, the following examples:

•

Mean curvature:
\gamma=H=\lambda_{1}+\ldots+\lambda_{n}
, supported in
\Gamma_{1}=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:H>0\}
.

•

Gauss-Kronecker curvature:
\gamma=\sqrt[n]{K}=\sqrt[n]{\lambda_{1}\ldots\lambda_{n}}

\Gamma_{+}
.

•

More generally, Hessian-type functions:
\gamma=\sqrt[k-l]{\dfrac{S_{k}}{S_{l}}}

\Gamma_{k}=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:S_{i}(\lambda)>0\text{ for }i=1,\ldots,k\}
. Here
S_{k}(\lambda)=\sum\limits_{1\leq i_{1}<\ldots<i_{k}\leq n}\lambda_{i_{1}}\ldots\lambda_{i_{k}}
denotes the elementary symmetric polynomial of order
k

n

S_{0}=1
and
S_{k}=0
for
k>n
.

•

The
k
-convexity functions:
\gamma=\left(\sum\limits_{1\leq i_{1}<\ldots<i_{k}\leq n}\dfrac{1}{\lambda_{i_{1}}+\ldots+\lambda_{i_{k}}}\right)^{-1}
supported in
\Gamma_{k}^{\prime}=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\lambda_{i_{1}}+\ldots+\lambda_{i_{k}}>0\}
.

•

The
k
-norm functions:
\gamma(\lambda)=\sqrt[k]{\lambda_{1}^{k}+\ldots+\lambda_{n}^{k}}
supported in the positive cone
\Gamma_{+}
.

We emphasize that the hypotheses defining the class of positive, 1-homogeneous curvature functions, together with second-order structural properties such as convexity, concavity, and inverse concavity, supported on the positive cone
\Gamma_{+}
, guarantee short-time existence and uniqueness

for strictly convex (

Definition 2.3
.

We call a positive
\alpha
-homogeneous curvature function
\gamma:\overline{\Gamma}\to[0,\infty)
signed if:

(1)

\alpha=\frac{p}{q}\in\mathbb{Q}
with
\gcd(p,2)=\gcd(q,2)=\gcd(p,q)=1
, and there exists a solution
(x_{0},y_{0},\ldots,y_{0})\in\{\gamma=0\}
distinct from
(0,\ldots,0)
,
(0,\ldots,0,1)
, and
(0,1,\ldots,1)
such that
\partial_{x}\gamma(x_{0},y_{0},\ldots,y_{0})>0
.

(2)

There is an open cone
\tilde{\Gamma}:=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:(x,y,\ldots,y)\in\Gamma\}
and a continuous extension
\tilde{\gamma}:\tilde{\Gamma}_{+}\cup\tilde{\Gamma}_{-}\to\mathbb{R}
, where

\displaystyle\tilde{\Gamma}_{\pm}=\{(x,y)\in\tilde{\Gamma}:\gamma(x,y,\ldots,y)\gtrless 0\},

such that
\gamma
is of class
C^{1}
on each
\tilde{\Gamma}_{\pm}
.

(3)

\gamma(cx,cy,\ldots,cy)=c^{\alpha}\gamma(x,y,\ldots,y)
for all
c\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
.

These hypotheses are motivated as follows. The homogeneity extends to rational exponents whose numerator and denominator are not divisible by
2
, which implies that the associated ODE inherits an even symmetry; that is, if
u
solves the translating equation (
3
), then
u(-x)
is also a solution. Moreover, the condition
\partial_{x}\tilde{\gamma}(x_{0},y_{0})>0
allows us to rewrite the related ODEs in an implicit form, namely
x=g(y,z)
where
\tilde{\gamma}(x,y)=z
, which permits a classical ODE analysis.

2.2.
Translators

We now introduce translating solitons. These are self-similar solutions to (
1
) of the form
\Sigma_{t}=\Sigma_{0}+t\vec{e}_{n+1}
. In particular, each time slice
\Sigma_{t}
satisfies a local translator equation.

Definition 2.4
.

We say that
\Sigma_{0}
is a translating soliton in direction
\vec{e}_{n+1}\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
of the
\gamma
-flow if, at every point of
\Sigma_{0}
, we have

(2)

\displaystyle\gamma(\lambda)=\langle\vec{\nu},\vec{e}_{n+1}\rangle.

Before giving examples of translators, we introduce the notions of degeneracy and nondegeneracy for curvature functions.

Definition 2.5
.

We say that a positive
\alpha
-homogeneous curvature function
\gamma:\Gamma\to(0,\infty)
is
k
-nondegenerate if
(\underbrace{0,\ldots,0}_{k},1,\ldots,1)\in\Gamma
,
and it is
k
-degenerate if
\gamma(\underbrace{0,\ldots,0}_{k},1,\ldots,1)=0
.

This definition is motivated by the behavior of the
\gamma
-flow on round generalized cylinder of the form
\mathbb{S}^{n-p}\times\mathbb{R}^{p}
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
. Under
k
-nondegeneracy, cylinders with
p<k
contract in finite time to a
k
-hyperplane, whereas under
k
-degeneracy, cylinders with
p\geq k
are stationary solutions of the
\gamma
-flow.

We now list examples of translating solitons for general curvature functions:

•

\alpha
-grim-reaper cylinders.

For
(n-1)
-nondegenerate, normalized
\alpha
-homogeneous positive curvature functions (i.e.,
\gamma(0,\ldots,0,1)=1
), the family of translating cylinders, unique up to scaling and rotation, isometric to
C\times\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
, where
C
is a one-dimensional
\alpha
-grim-reaper solving
\lambda^{\alpha}=\langle\nu,e_{2}\rangle
, see
[
3
,
4
]
. These cylinders are geodesically complete for
\alpha\leq 1
. Moreover, for
\alpha\in\left(\tfrac{1}{2},1\right]
, the generating curves are bounded on a finite interval, so the cylinders lie in slabs of finite width; whereas for
\alpha\in(0,\tfrac{1}{2})
, they are defined in all
\mathbb{R}
, yielding entire graphical solutions.

•

Bowl-type translators.

Rotationally symmetric translating graphs of the form
(x,u(|x|))
that satisfy a dichotomy: either the graph is entire, or it is defined only on a ball of radius
\sqrt[\alpha]{\gamma^{-1}(1,\ldots,1)}
. The dichotomy depends on the (non)degeneracy, the degree of homogeneity, and the asymptotics of the implicit function
x=g_{+}(y,1)
coming from
\gamma(x,y,\ldots,y)=1
; see
[
14
]
. Moreover, for
1
-nondegenerate
\gamma
, we have
u(|x|)=|x|^{\alpha+1}+o\!\left(|x|^{\alpha+1}\right)
as
|x|\to\infty
.

•

Catenoidal translators.

This family consists of non-convex rotationally symmetric hypersurfaces parameterized by their positive distance to the translation axis, and they are singular along the plane
\{x_{n+1}=0\}
in a large class of ambient spaces. These solutions are currently known only for the elementary symmetric polynomials, i.e.,
\gamma=S_{k}
. As in the bowl-type case, we encounter a dichotomy: for
k=1,\ldots,n-1
, the catenoidal translators are rotational bi-graphs over the complement of a ball where each branch is asymptotic to a vertical translate of the bowl-type soliton
[
5
]
. For
k=n
, the catenoidal family instead exhibits a conical singularity; we refer to
[
10
]
for further details on the classification of these solutions .

We also remark that in the mean curvature case
\gamma=H
, there is a large variety of translating solitons, including the
\Delta
-wings family and several semi-graphical translators constructed and classified in
[
12
]
.

2.3.
Rotationally symmetric translators

In this subsection we derive the equations satisfied by translating solitons that enjoy rotational symmetry, for the class of curvature functions considered in this article.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
\mathtt{c}(s)=(r(s),u(s))\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
be a smooth regular curve, where
s
is the arc-length parameter. We define the rotational hypersurface generated by
\mathtt{c}
with respect to the
x_{n+1}
-axis by

\displaystyle\Sigma(\mathtt{c})=\bigl\{(x^{\prime},x_{n+1})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}:(|x^{\prime}|,x_{n+1})=(r,u)\in\mathtt{c}\bigr\},

where
|x^{\prime}|=\sqrt{x_{1}^{2}+\ldots+x_{n}^{2}}
.

Using cylindrical coordinates
\{x_{n+1},|x^{\prime}|,\vec{\omega}\}
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
, where
\vec{\omega}
are spherical coordinates on
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, we write a point
p\in\Sigma(\mathtt{c})
as
p=r\vec{\omega}+u\vec{e}_{n+1}
. The unit normal vector at
p
with fixed orientation can then be written as

\nu(p)=u^{\prime}\vec{\omega}-r^{\prime}\vec{e}_{n+1}=\sin(\theta(s))\vec{\omega}-\cos(\theta(s))\vec{e}_{n+1},

where
\theta^{\prime}(s)=\kappa=u^{\prime\prime}r^{\prime}-u^{\prime}r^{\prime\prime}
is the curvature of
\mathtt{c}(s)
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
.

The principal curvatures at
p\in\Sigma(\mathtt{c})
are given by

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}(p)=\kappa,\qquad\lambda_{2}(p)=\ldots=\lambda_{n}(p)=\frac{\sin(\theta(s))}{r}.

Consequently, the ODE system that characterizes a rotationally symmetric translator is

(3)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\tilde{\gamma}\!\left(\kappa,\dfrac{\sin(\theta)}{r}\right)=\cos(\theta),\\[4.0pt]
\theta^{\prime}=\kappa,\quad r^{\prime}=\cos(\theta),\quad u^{\prime}=\sin(\theta),\end{cases}

where
\tilde{\gamma}:\tilde{\Gamma}\to(0,\infty)
is defined by
\tilde{\gamma}=\gamma\circ\phi(x,y)
with
\tilde{\Gamma}=\phi^{-1}(\Gamma)\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, and
\phi:{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is given by
\phi(x,y)=(x,y,\ldots,y)
.

2.4.
Implicit translator equations.

We now reformulate equation (
3

(4)

\displaystyle\tilde{\gamma}(x,y)=z.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
\gamma:\Gamma\to(0,\infty)
be a positive
\alpha
-homogeneous curvature function. Then the level set

\bigl\{(x,y,z)\in\tilde{\Gamma}\times(0,\infty):\tilde{\gamma}(x,y)=z\bigr\}

can be described as the graph of
x=g(y,z)
, where
g_{+}(y,z)\in\mathcal{C}^{1}(U_{+},V_{+})
is the implicit function of
\tilde{\gamma}(x,y)=z
over open sets
U_{+}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
and
V_{+}\subset{\mathbb{R}}
given by:

•

If
\gamma(\lambda)
is
1
-nondegenerate, then

\displaystyle U_{+}

\displaystyle=\Bigl\{(y,z)\in(0,\infty)^{2}:\frac{z}{\tilde{\gamma}(1,1)}<y^{\alpha}<\frac{z}{\tilde{\gamma}(0,1)}\Bigr\},\qquad V_{+}=(0,\infty).

•

If
\gamma(\lambda)
is
1
-degenerate, then

\displaystyle U_{+}

\displaystyle=\Bigl\{(y,z)\in(0,\infty)^{2}:\frac{z}{\tilde{\gamma}(1,1)}<y^{\alpha}\Bigr\},\qquad V_{+}=(0,\infty).

Proof.

We consider
G:\tilde{\Gamma}\times(0,\infty)\to{\mathbb{R}}
defined by
G(x,y,z)=\tilde{\gamma}(x,y)-z
. We note the following:

(1)

The point
(x,y,z)=(\tfrac{1}{2},1,\tilde{\gamma}(\tfrac{1}{2},1))
lies on the level set
\tilde{\gamma}(x,y)=z
, and
x=\tfrac{1}{2}
is the unique first coordinate for this choice because
\tilde{\gamma}
is strictly increasing in each variable.
3
3

3

Any point in the positive two-dimensional Gårding cone
\Gamma^{2}_{+}
also works.

(2)

For each fixed
x
, the derivative with respect to

is the nonzero vector

(\partial_{y}G,\partial_{z}G)=\left(\sum_{i=2}^{n}\frac{\partial\gamma}{\partial\lambda_{i}},-1\right),

which is nonsingular on

. Moreover,
\partial_{x}G=\frac{\partial\gamma}{\partial\lambda_{1}}>0

(3)

Let

If

\tilde{\gamma}(0,1)<\tilde{\gamma}\!\left(\frac{x_{n}}{y_{n}},1\right)<\tilde{\gamma}(1,1)

. In particular,
(x_{n})

If
\gamma(\lambda)
is
1

.

Let
\gamma\in\mathcal{C}^{1}(\Gamma)

1

As in the previous proof, we only need to verify the compactness property for the

(x_{n},y_{n})\in V_{-}\times U_{-}

\tilde{\gamma}\!\left(\frac{x_{n}}{y_{n}},1\right)<\tilde{\gamma}(-1,1)

Proposition 2.9
.

The function
g_{+}:U_{+}\to V_{+}
is decreasing in
y
and increasing in
z
, and satisfies

\displaystyle c\,g_{+}(y,z)=g_{+}(cy,c^{\alpha}z)\quad\text{for every }c>0.

Furthermore,
g_{-}(y,-1)
is decreasing in
y
.

Proof.

Differentiating
\tilde{\gamma}(g_{+}(y,z),y)=z
with respect to
y
and
z
yields

\displaystyle\tilde{\gamma}_{x}(g_{+}(y,z),y)\,\partial_{y}g_{+}(y,z)+\tilde{\gamma}_{y}(g_{+}(y,z),y)

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\tilde{\gamma}_{x}(g_{+}(y,z),y)\,\partial_{z}g_{+}(y,z)

\displaystyle=1.

Thus

\partial_{y}g_{+}(y,z)=-\frac{\tilde{\gamma}_{y}(g_{+}(y,z),y)}{\tilde{\gamma}_{x}(g_{+}(y,z),y)}<0,\qquad\partial_{z}g_{+}(y,z)=\frac{1}{\tilde{\gamma}_{x}(g_{+}(y,z),y)}>0.

For the scaling property, let
c>0
and observe that

\tilde{\gamma}(g_{+}(cy,c^{\alpha}z),y)=c^{\alpha}z\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\tilde{\gamma}\!\left(\frac{g_{+}(cy,c^{\alpha}z)}{c},y\right)=z=\tilde{\gamma}(g_{+}(y,z),y).

Since
\tilde{\gamma}
is strictly increasing in its first variable, we conclude that
g_{+}(cy,c^{\alpha}z)=c\,g_{+}(y,z)
. The monotonicity of
g_{-}(\cdot,-1)
follows by the same argument applied on
U_{-}
.
∎

As an example, we can compute
g_{\pm}
explicitly for the
1
-homogeneous normalized Hessian quotient curvature functions

\gamma(\lambda)=\sqrt[l-k]{\frac{S_{k}(0,1,\ldots,1)}{S_{l}(0,1,\ldots,1)}}\sqrt[k-l]{\frac{S_{k}(\lambda)}{S_{l}(\lambda)}},\qquad 0\leq l<k\leq n.

In this case,

\displaystyle\tilde{\gamma}(x,y)=\sqrt[k-l]{\frac{\binom{n-1}{l}}{\binom{n-1}{k}}}\,y\sqrt[k-l]{\frac{\binom{n-1}{k}y+\binom{n-1}{k-1}x}{\binom{n-1}{l}y+\binom{n-1}{l-1}x}},

supported on a suitable cone

we obtain

\displaystyle g_{+}(y,z)

7.
Conclusions

In this work, we extend the theory of translating solitons in euclidean spaces for the mean curvature flow to a broad class of curvature functions that are symmetric, homogeneous, and monotone, under natural (non)degeneracy assumptions. The main novelty is the construction of catenoidal examples whose curvature functions fail to be continuous at the origin, showing that this phenomenon is compatible with the geometric structure of the corresponding solitons.

These constructions provide a framework for constructing
\Delta
-wing solitons for non-degenerate
(n-1)
normalized curvature functions, following the variational approaches employed by Hoffman-Ilmanen-Martin-White
[
7
]
as well as the more EDP-based techniques of Bourni-Langford-Tinaglia
[
2
]
under the condition of being rotationally symmetric at infinity, since such solitons arise naturally from the process of desingularizations between bowl-type translators and grim-reaper cylinders. Furthermore, the catenoidal translators we construct should allow stability results for complete graphical solutions of the associated
\gamma
-flow, in the spirit of Clutterbuck-Schnürer-Schulze
[
5
]
.

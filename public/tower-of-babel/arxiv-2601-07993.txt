The exact region determined by Spearman’s footrule, Gini’s gamma and Kendall’s tau
Damjana Kokol Bukovšek, Petra Lazić, Blaž Mojškerc, Nik Stopar

Abstract
. Concordance measures are used to express the degree of association between random variables. Practitioners may use several distinct concordance measures to narrow the space of possible dependence structures. Consequently, the relations between different (weak) concordance measures have been extensively studied in recent years. The goal of this paper is to study the relation between Kendall’s tau, Gini’s gamma and Spearman’s footrule. In particular, we describe the exact region determined by these three measures, using shuffles of M M and ordinal sums of copulas. We also provide the formulas for five main (weak) concordance measures and Chatterjee’s xi of ordinal sums of copulas.

62H05, 62H20, 60E05

1.
Introduction

Various concordance measures can be used to describe the association between random variables. Given two random variables
X
and
Y
, the concordance measure between them depends only on the copula associated with their joint distribution. Traditionally, Spearman’s rho and Kendall’s tau are the most commonly used in practice and there is an abundance of literature discussing their properties. Later, several other (weak) concordance measures were introduced, such as Gini’s gamma, Blomqvist’s beta and Spearman’s footrule. The central interest of this paper are the relations between Kendall’s tau, Gini’s gamma and Spearman’s footrule, therefore, we provide some references for these three.
Kendall’s tau was investigated in
[
9
,
11
,
12
,
30
]
,
Gini’s gamma in
[
3
,
10
,
21
]
,
and Spearman’s footrule in
[
5
,
10
,
29
,
26
]
.

Each concordance measure captures a specific aspect of association, so practitioners tend to use several of them to narrow the space of possible dependence structures. Hence, the relations between different (weak) concordance measures of copulas are of interest and have been extensively studied.

The problem of the exact region determined by Spearman’s rho and Kendall’s tau was open since 1960’s and was solved in
[
25
]
. The relation between Blomqvist’s beta and other (weak) concordance measures is relatively easy to describe (see
[
14
]
), while relations between others are more challenging. The region determined by Gini’s gamma and Spearman’s footrule is characterized in
[
15
]
. The problem of the region determined by Spearman’s rho and Spearman’s footrule is partially solved in
[
18
]
, see also
[
28
]
. The lower bound for this region is determined exactly, while for the upper bound only a good approximation is known. The relations between Kendall’s tau and other (weak) concordance measures are considered in
[
17
]
. The region determined by Spearman’s rho and Gini’s gamma is still open.

In 2021, Chatterjee’s coefficient of rank correlation (Chatterjee’s xi) was introduced in
[
2
]
and became extremely popular. It quantifies the strength of functional dependence between random variables
X
and
Y
. Even though this is not a concordance measure, it has still been compared to common measures of concordance, see
[
1
,
8
,
23
]
.

The paper
[
8
]
emphasizes the importance of studying higher-dimensional versions of the problem, namely, capturing the connections between more than two concordance measures simultaneously. The first treatment of a triplet of concordance measures, Blomqvist’s beta, Spearman’s footrule and Gini’s gamma, was presented in
[
16
]
. The goal of the present paper is to study the relation between Kendall’s tau, Spearman’s footrule and Gini’s gamma. We first prove the lower and upper bound for Kendall’s tau of a copula if its Spearman’s footrule and Gini’s gamma are given. Then we give several examples to demonstrate that these bounds are attained by shuffles of
M
. It turns out that the set of all triplets
(\phi(C),\gamma(C),\tau(C))
for all copulas
C
is a polyhedron.

To prove our results, we use ordinal sums of copulas, hence, we need the formulas expressing concordance measures of an ordinal sum of copulas in terms of concordance measures of its summands. We derive these formulas for all five most common (weak) concordance measures and Chatterjee’s xi. These results may be of independent interest to the reader. Some of them already appeared in the literature without proofs.

The paper is structured as follows. In Section
2
we introduce the notation, give the basic definitions and recall some results that we need later. In Section
3
we prove the formulas for concordance measures and Chatterjee’s xi of an ordinal sum of copulas. In Section
4
we present our main result, i.e., we describe the exact region determined by Spearman’s footrule, Gini’s gamma and Kendall’s tau. Moreover, we provide examples of copulas attaining the boundary of the obtained region. We end the paper with some concluding remarks, including a discussion on the possible range of Kendall’s tau when Spearman’s footrule and Gini’s gamma of a copula are given.

2.
Preliminaries

Let
\mathbb{I}
be the unit interval
[0,1]\subseteq\mathbb{R}
and
u_{1},u_{2},v_{1},v_{2}\in\mathbb{I}
be such that
u_{1}\leq u_{2}
and
v_{1}\leq v_{2}
. The Cartesian product of intervals
R=[u_{1},u_{2}]\times[v_{1},v_{2}]
is called a
rectangle
in
\mathbb{I}^{2}
. Let
H:\mathbb{I}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
be a real function. We define the
H
-volume of
R
as

V_{H}(R)=H(u_{2},v_{2})-H(u_{2},v_{1})-H(u_{1},v_{2})+H(u_{1},v_{1})
.
A
bivariate copula
is a function
C:\mathbb{I}^{2}\to\mathbb{I}
with the following properties:

•

C(0,v)=C(u,0)=0
for all
u,v\in\mathbb{I}
(
C
is
grounded
),

•

C(u,1)=u
and
C(1,v)=v
for all
u,v\in\mathbb{I}
(
C
has
uniform marginals
), and

•

V_{C}(R)\geq 0
for every rectangle
R\subseteq\mathbb{I}^{2}
(
C
is
2-
increasing
).

Bivariate copulas are functions of two variables which couple bivariate distribution functions with their one-dimensional marginal distribution functions, a famous Theorem by Sklar
[
27
]
.
The set of all bivariate copulas will be denoted by
\mathcal{C}
. It is well known that this set is compact in the sup norm. For any copula
C
its diagonal will be denoted by
\delta_{C}
and its opposite diagonal by
\omega_{C}
, i.e.,
\delta_{C}(u)=C(u,u)
and
\omega_{C}(u)=C(u,1-u)
for all
u\in\mathbb{I}
.

Given two copulas
C
and
D
, we denote
C\leq D
if
C(u,v)\leqslant D(u,v)
for all
(u,v)\in\mathbb{I}^{2}
. This is the so-called
pointwise order
of copulas.
For any copula
C
we have
W\leq C\leq M
, where
W(u,v)=\max\{0,u+v-1\}
and
M(u,v)=\min\{u,v\}
are the lower and upper
Fréchet-Hoeffding bounds
for the set of all copulas.
Furthermore, any copula
C
induces reflected copulas
C^{\sigma_{1}}
and
C^{\sigma_{2}}
defined by
C^{\sigma_{1}}(u,v)=v-C(1-u,v)
and
C^{\sigma_{2}}(u,v)=u-C(u,1-v)
for all
(u,v)\in\mathbb{I}^{2}
. Note that
\delta_{C^{\sigma_{2}}}(u)=u-\omega_{C}(u)
.

Let
h\colon\mathbb{I}\to\mathbb{I}
be a measure preserving bijection, where
\mathbb{I}
is equipped with the Lebesgue measure
\lambda
. Then the function defined by

(1)

C(u,v)=\lambda\big(\{t\in\mathbb{I}\mid t\leq u,h(t)\leq v\}\big)

is a copula whose mass is concentrated on the graph of
h
, i.e.,
\mu_{C}\big(\{(t,h(t))\mid t\in\mathbb{I}\}\big)=1
.
Particular examples of such copulas are the so-called

shuffles of
M
. A shuffle of
M

C=M(n,J,\pi,\omega)

is determined by a positive integer
n
, a partition

J=\{J_{1},J_{2},\ldots,J_{n}\}
of the interval
\mathbb{I}
into
n
pieces, where
J_{i}=[u_{i-1},u_{i}]
and
0=u_{0}\leq u_{1}\leq u_{2}\leq\ldots\leq u_{n-1}\leq u_{n}=1
, shortly written as
(n-1)
-tuple of splitting points
J=(u_{1},u_{2},\ldots,u_{n-1})
,
a permutation
\pi\in S_{n}
, written as
n
-tuple of images
\pi=(\pi(1),\pi(2),\ldots,\pi(n))
, and a mapping
\omega:\{1,2,\ldots,n\}\to\{-1,1\}
, written as
n
-tuple of images
\omega=(\omega(1),\omega(2),\ldots,\omega(n))
. The mass of
C
is concentrated on the main diagonals of the squares
[u_{i-1},u_{i}]\times[v_{\pi(i)-1}\times v_{\pi(i)}]
where
0=v_{0}\leq v_{1}\leq v_{2}\leq\ldots\leq v_{n-1}\leq v_{n}=1
.
Hence,
C
is defined by the measure preserving bijection
h_{C}\colon\mathbb{I}\to\mathbb{I}
, given by

h_{C}(u)=\begin{cases}u+v_{\pi(i)-1}-u_{i-1};&u\in(u_{i-1},u_{i}),\ \omega(i)=1,\\
v_{\pi(i)}+u_{i-1}-u;&u\in(u_{i-1},u_{i}),\ \omega(i)=-1,\\
v_{i};&u=u_{i}.\end{cases}

Furthermore,
C
is the copula of uniformly distributed random variables
U
and
V
on
\mathbb{I}
with the property
P(V=h_{C}(U))=1
. For more details see
[
22
, §3.2.3]
.

In
[
24
]
Scarsini introduced formal axioms for
concordance measures
.
These are mappings that assign to each copula a real number in
[-1,1]
and are meant to measure the degree of concordance/discordance between the components of random vectors.
Recall that two observations
(x,y)
and
(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})
from a random vector
(X,Y)
are concordant if
(x-x^{\prime})(y-y^{\prime})>0
and discordant if
(x-x^{\prime})(y-y^{\prime})<0
.
For the formal definition of concordance measures and further details we refer the reader to
[
7
]
and
[
22
]
. Here we give the properties of concordance measures, which we will need in the sequel: if
\kappa
is a concordance measure, then
\kappa(M)=1
,
\kappa(C^{\sigma_{1}})=\kappa(C^{\sigma_{2}})=-\kappa(C)
for any copula
C\in\mathcal{C}
,
\kappa
is continuous with respect to the pointwise convergence, and
\kappa
is monotone increasing with respect to the pointwise order.

Many of the most important bivariate concordance measures, including Kendall’s tau and Gini’s gamma, can be expressed with the so-called
concordance function

\mathcal{Q}
, introduced by Kruskal
[
19
]
.
If
(X_{1},Y_{1})
and
(X_{2},Y_{2})
are pairs of continuous random variables,
then the concordance function of random vectors
(X_{1},Y_{1})
and
(X_{2},Y_{2})
depends only on the corresponding copulas
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
and is given by (see
[
22
, Theorem 5.1.1]
)

(2)

\mathcal{Q}=\mathcal{Q}(C_{1},C_{2})=4\int_{\mathbb{I}^{2}}C_{2}(u,v)\,dC_{1}(u,v)-1.

It turns out that the concordance function is symmetric in its arguments, i.e.,
\mathcal{Q}(C_{1},C_{2})=\mathcal{Q}(C_{2},C_{1})
, and has several other useful properties, see
[
22
, Corollary 5.1.2]
and
[
13
, §3]
.

The most important concordance measures include Spearman’s rho, Kendall’s tau, Gini’s gamma and Blomqvist’s beta.
With the concordance function at hand,
Spearman’s rho
can be defined by

(3)

\rho(C)=3\mathcal{Q}(C,\Pi)=12\int_{\mathbb{I}^{2}}C(u,v)\,du\,dv-3,

Kendall’s tau
by

(4)

\tau(C)=\mathcal{Q}(C,C)=4\int_{\mathbb{I}^{2}}C(u,v)\,dC(u,v)-1,

and
Gini’s gamma
by

(5)

\gamma(C)=\mathcal{Q}(C,M)+\mathcal{Q}(C,W)=4\int_{0}^{1}\delta_{C}(u)\,du+4\int_{0}^{1}\omega_{C}(u)\,du-2.

Blomqvist’s beta is defined as

(6)

\beta(C)=4C(\tfrac{1}{2},\tfrac{1}{2})-1.

If
h_{C}\colon\mathbb{I}\to\mathbb{I}
is a measure preserving bijection and
C
a copula whose mass is concentrated on the graph of
h_{C}
, then
\tau(C)
can be expressed as

(7)

\tau(C)=4\int_{0}^{1}C(u,h_{C}(u))\,du-1.

In
[
20
]
Liebscher considered
weak concordance measures
, which are slightly more general mappings than concordance measures (the formal definition can be found in Liebscher’s paper).
The most important example of a weak concordance measure is
Spearman’s footrule
defined by

(8)

\phi(C)=\tfrac{1}{2}\left(3\mathcal{Q}(C,M)-1\right)=6\int_{0}^{1}\delta_{C}(u)\,du-2.

Spearman’s footrule is not a true concordance measure since its range is
[-\frac{1}{2},1]
.

Finally, we recall the definition of Chatterjee’s xi, which is not a concordance measure, but rather a nonsymmetric coefficient of correlation.
Every copula
C\in\mathcal{C}
is increasing in each variable, hence partially differentiable almost everywhere. We denote by
\partial_{1}C(u,v)
the partial derivative of
C
with respect to the first variable. One of the equivalent definitions of Chatterjee’s coefficient of rank correlation is the following (see
[
4
,
23
]
)

(9)

\xi(C)=6\int_{\mathbb{I}^{2}}(\partial_{1}C(u,v))^{2}\,du\,dv-2.

In the next proposition we recall the relations between Kendall’s tau, Gini’s gamma and Spearman’s footrule, which will be needed in the sequel.

Proposition 2.1

(
[
15
,
17
]
)
.

For any copula
C\in\mathcal{C}
we have

(10)

\displaystyle\tfrac{4}{3}\phi(C)-\tfrac{1}{3}

\displaystyle\leq\gamma(C)\leq\min\big\{\tfrac{4}{3}\phi(C)+\tfrac{1}{6},\tfrac{2}{3}\phi(C)+\tfrac{1}{3}\big\},

(11)

\displaystyle\tfrac{4}{3}\phi(C)-\tfrac{1}{3}

\displaystyle\leq\tau(C)\leq\tfrac{2}{3}\phi(C)+\tfrac{1}{3},

(12)

\displaystyle\max\big\{\tfrac{2}{3}\gamma(C)-\tfrac{1}{3},2\gamma(C)-1\big\}

\displaystyle\leq\tau(C)\leq\min\big\{\tfrac{2}{3}\gamma(C)+\tfrac{1}{3},2\gamma(C)+1\big\}.

The bounds are attained by shuffles of
M
.

3.
Concordance measures of an ordinal sum

In this section we give the formulas for five (weak) concordance measures (Spearman’s rho, Kendall’s tau, Spearman’s footrule, Gini’s gamma, and Blomqvist’s beta), and Chatterjee’s xi of an ordinal sum of copulas.

Let
\{(a_{k},b_{k})\mid k=1,2,\ldots,n\}
be a finite family of disjoint open subintervals of
\mathbb{I}
and
\{B_{k}\mid k=1,2,\ldots,n\}
a family of copulas. Then the
ordinal sum
(sometimes also called
M
-ordinal sum
)
B
of
\{B_{k}\mid k=1,2,\ldots,n\}
with respect to
\{(a_{k},b_{k})\mid k=1,2,\ldots,n\}
is a copula defined by

(13)

B(u,v)=\begin{cases}a_{k}+(b_{k}-a_{k})B_{k}\big(\frac{u-a_{k}}{b_{k}-a_{k}},\frac{v-a_{k}}{b_{k}-a_{k}}\big);&(u,v)\in[a_{k},b_{k}]^{2},k=1,2,\dots,n,\\
\min\{u,v\};&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

(see
[
6
]
, compare also
[
22
, §3.2.2]
).

We start with the formula for Spearman’s rho. It already appeared in
[
18
]
, but without a proof, so we give the proof here.

Proposition 3.1
.

Spearman’s rho of an ordinal sum
B
, given in (
13
), is

(14)

\rho(B)=1-\sum_{k=1}^{n}(b_{k}-a_{k})^{3}(1-\rho(B_{k})).

Proof.

Since
B(u,v)=M(u,v)
outside the union of squares
[a_{k},b_{k}]^{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{I}^{2}}B(u,v)\,du\,dv

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{I}^{2}}M(u,v)\,du\,dv+\int_{\mathbb{I}^{2}}(B(u,v)-M(u,v))\,du\,dv

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{3}+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\int_{[a_{k},b_{k}]^{2}}(B(u,v)-M(u,v))\,du\,dv

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{3}+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\int_{[a_{k},b_{k}]^{2}}B(u,v)\,du\,dv-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\int_{[a_{k},b_{k}]^{2}}M(u,v)\,du\,dv

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{3}+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\int_{[a_{k},b_{k}]^{2}}B(u,v)\,du\,dv-\sum_{k=1}^{n}(b_{k}-a_{k})^{2}(\tfrac{2}{3}a_{k}+\tfrac{1}{3}b_{k}).

Since

\displaystyle\int_{[a_{k},b_{k}]^{2}}B(u,v)\,du\,dv

\displaystyle=\int_{[a_{k},b_{k}]^{2}}\big(a_{k}+(b_{k}-a_{k})B_{k}(\tfrac{u-a_{k}}{b_{k}-a_{k}},\tfrac{v-a_{k}}{b_{k}-a_{k}})\big)\,du\,dv

\displaystyle=a_{k}(b_{k}-a_{k})^{2}+(b_{k}-a_{k})^{3}\int_{\mathbb{I}^{2}}B_{k}(t,s)\,dt\,ds

\displaystyle=a_{k}(b_{k}-a_{k})^{2}+(b_{k}-a_{k})^{3}\cdot\frac{\rho(B_{k})+3}{12},

we obtain

\displaystyle\rho(B)

\displaystyle=12\int_{\mathbb{I}^{2}}B(u,v)\,du\,dv-3

\displaystyle=1+12\sum_{k=1}^{n}\int_{[a_{k},b_{k}]^{2}}B(u,v)\,du\,dv-12\sum_{k=1}^{n}(b_{k}-a_{k})^{2}(\tfrac{2}{3}a_{k}+\tfrac{1}{3}b_{k})

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left(12a_{k}(b_{k}-a_{k})^{2}-(b_{k}-a_{k})^{2}(8a_{k}+4b_{k})+(b_{k}-a_{k})^{3}(\rho(B_{k})+3)\right)

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left(-4(b_{k}-a_{k})^{3}+(b_{k}-a_{k})^{3}(\rho(B_{k})+3)\right)

\displaystyle=1-\sum_{k=1}^{n}(b_{k}-a_{k})^{3}(1-\rho(B_{k})),

which finishes the proof.
∎

We continue with the formula for Kendall’s tau of an ordinal sum.

Proposition 3.2
.

Kendall’s tau of an ordinal sum
B
, given in (
13
), is

(15)

\tau(B)=1-\sum_{k=1}^{n}(b_{k}-a_{k})^{2}(1-\tau(B_{k})).

Proof.

Since
B(u,v)=M(u,v)
outside the union of squares
[a_{k},b_{k}]^{2}
, we calculate

\displaystyle\tau(B)

\displaystyle=-1+4\int_{\mathbb{I}^{2}}B(u,v)\,dB(u,v)

\displaystyle=-1+4\sum_{k=1}^{n}\int_{[a_{k},b_{k}]^{2}}B(u,v)\,dB(u,v)+4\int_{\mathbb{I}\setminus\cup_{k=1}^{n}(a_{k},b_{k})}u\,du

\displaystyle=-1+4\sum_{k=1}^{n}\int_{[a_{k},b_{k}]^{2}}B(u,v)\,dB(u,v)+4\int_{0}^{1}u\,du-4\sum_{k=1}^{n}\int_{a_{k}}^{b_{k}}u\,du

\displaystyle=1+4\sum_{k=1}^{n}\int_{[a_{k},b_{k}]^{2}}B(u,v)\,dB(u,v)-2\sum_{k=1}^{n}(b_{k}^{2}-a_{k}^{2}).

Now,

\displaystyle\int_{[a_{k},b_{k}]^{2}}B(u,v)\,dB(u,v)

\displaystyle=\int_{[a_{k},b_{k}]^{2}}\big(a_{k}+(b_{k}-a_{k})B_{k}(\tfrac{u-a_{k}}{b_{k}-a_{k}},\tfrac{v-a_{k}}{b_{k}-a_{k}})\big)\,dB(u,v)

\displaystyle=\int_{[a_{k},b_{k}]^{2}}a_{k}\,dB(u,v)+(b_{k}-a_{k})\int_{[a_{k},b_{k}]^{2}}B_{k}\big(\tfrac{u-a_{k}}{b_{k}-a_{k}},\tfrac{v-a_{k}}{b_{k}-a_{k}}\big)\,dB(u,v)

\displaystyle=\int_{[a_{k},b_{k}]^{2}}a_{k}(b_{k}-a_{k})\,dB_{k}(\tfrac{u-a_{k}}{b_{k}-a_{k}},\tfrac{v-a_{k}}{b_{k}-a_{k}})

\displaystyle\qquad+(b_{k}-a_{k})^{2}\int_{[a_{k},b_{k}]^{2}}B_{k}(\tfrac{u-a_{k}}{b_{k}-a_{k}},\tfrac{v-a_{k}}{b_{k}-a_{k}})\,dB_{k}(\tfrac{u-a_{k}}{b_{k}-a_{k}},\tfrac{v-a_{k}}{b_{k}-a_{k}})

\displaystyle=a_{k}(b_{k}-a_{k})\int_{\mathbb{I}^{2}}dB_{k}(t,s)+(b_{k}-a_{k})^{2}\int_{\mathbb{I}^{2}}B_{k}(t,s)\,dB_{k}(t,s)

\displaystyle=a_{k}(b_{k}-a_{k})+(b_{k}-a_{k})^{2}\cdot\frac{\tau(B_{k})+1}{4}.

We obtain

\displaystyle\tau(B)

\displaystyle=1+4\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}(b_{k}-a_{k})+4\sum_{k=1}^{n}(b_{k}-a_{k})^{2}\cdot\frac{\tau(B_{k})+1}{4}-2\sum_{k=1}^{n}(b_{k}^{2}-a_{k}^{2})

\displaystyle=1-2\sum_{k=1}^{n}(b_{k}-a_{k})^{2}+\sum_{k=1}^{n}(b_{k}-a_{k})^{2}(\tau(B_{k})+1)

\displaystyle=1-\sum_{k=1}^{n}(b_{k}-a_{k})^{2}(1-\tau(B_{k})),

which finishes the proof.
∎

A version of the formula for Spearman’s footrule of an ordinal sum appeared already in
[
28
]
, under the assumption that the union of the family of closed subintervals
\{[a_{k},b_{k}]\mid k=1,2,\ldots,n\}
equals the whole interval
\mathbb{I}
. Here we prove it for the general case.

Proposition 3.3
.

Spearman’s footrule of an ordinal sum
B
, given in (
13
), is

(16)

\phi(B)=1-\sum_{k=1}^{n}(b_{k}-a_{k})^{2}(1-\phi(B_{k})).

Proof.

Note that the diagonal section
\delta_{B}
of copula
B
equals

\displaystyle\delta_{B}(u)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}a_{k}+(b_{k}-a_{k})\delta_{B_{k}}(\frac{u-a_{k}}{b_{k}-a_{k}});&u\in[a_{k},b_{k}],k=1,2,\dots,n,\\
u;&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

\displaystyle=u+\begin{cases}a_{k}-u+(b_{k}-a_{k})\delta_{B_{k}}(\frac{u-a_{k}}{b_{k}-a_{k}});&u\in[a_{k},b_{k}],k=1,2,\dots,n,\\
0;&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

so that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\delta_{B}(u)\,du

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}u\,du+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\int_{a_{k}}^{b_{k}}\big(a_{k}-u+(b_{k}-a_{k})\delta_{B_{k}}(\tfrac{u-a_{k}}{b_{k}-a_{k}})\big)\,du

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left(-\tfrac{1}{2}(b_{k}-a_{k})^{2}+(b_{k}-a_{k})^{2}\int_{0}^{1}\delta_{B_{k}}(t)\,dt\right)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left(-\tfrac{1}{2}(b_{k}-a_{k})^{2}+(b_{k}-a_{k})^{2}\cdot\frac{\phi(B_{k})+2}{6}\right)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}+\sum_{k=1}^{n}(b_{k}-a_{k})^{2}\cdot\frac{\phi(B_{k})-1}{6},

which implies

\phi(B)=3+\sum_{k=1}^{n}(b_{k}-a_{k})^{2}(\phi(B_{k})-1)-2=1-\sum_{k=1}^{n}(b_{k}-a_{k})^{2}(1-\phi(B_{k}))

and finishes the proof.
∎

Note that in the formulas for Spearman’s rho, Kendall’s tau, and Spearman’s footrule of an ordinal sum the position of the subintervals
(a_{k},b_{k})
is not important, only their size
b_{k}-a_{k}
matters. For Gini’s gamma this is no longer true. In order to give the result for Gini’s gamma, we first prove a simple lemma.

Lemma 3.4
.

For any copula
C\in\mathcal{C}
we have

\gamma(C)=\tfrac{2}{3}(\phi(C)-\phi(C^{\sigma_{2}})).

Proof.

For any copula
C
we have

\int_{0}^{1}\delta_{C^{\sigma_{2}}}(u)\,du=\int_{0}^{1}C^{\sigma_{2}}(u,u)\,du=\frac{1}{2}-\int_{0}^{1}C(u,1-u)\,du=\frac{1}{2}-\int_{0}^{1}\omega_{C}(u)\,du,

hence

\displaystyle\gamma(C)

\displaystyle=4\int_{0}^{1}\delta_{C}(u)\,du+4\left(\frac{1}{2}-\int_{0}^{1}\delta_{C^{\sigma_{2}}}(u)\,du\right)-2

\displaystyle=4\cdot\frac{\phi(C)+2}{6}-4\cdot\frac{\phi(C^{\sigma_{2}})+2}{6}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{2}{3}(\phi(C)-\phi(C^{\sigma_{2}})),

which finishes the proof.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
B
be an ordinal sum given in (

\tfrac{1}{2}
does not belong to any of the subintervals
(a_{k},b_{k})
, then

(17)

\gamma(B)=1-\tfrac{2}{3}\sum_{k=1}^{n}(b_{k}-a_{k})^{2}(1-\phi(B_{k})).

If
\tfrac{1}{2}\in(a_{j},b_{j})
for some

\displaystyle\gamma(B)

\displaystyle=4(b_{j}-a_{j})^{2}\max\{0,c\}^{2}+4a_{j}-4a_{j}^{2}-\tfrac{2}{3}\sum_{k=1}^{n}(b_{k}-a_{k})^{2}(1-\phi(B_{k}))

\displaystyle\qquad+4(b_{j}-a_{j})^{2}\int_{\max\{0,c\}}^{1+\min\{0,c\}}B_{j}\big(t,1+c-t\big)\,dt,

where
c=\frac{1-a_{j}-b_{j}}{b_{j}-a_{j}}\in(-1,1)
.
In particular, if
a_{j}+b_{j}=1
for some
j\in\{1,2,...,n\}
(i.e.,
\tfrac{1}{2}
lies at the middle of the subinterval
(a_{j},b_{j})
), then

(18)

\gamma(B)=1-(b_{j}-a_{j})^{2}(1-\gamma(B_{j}))-\tfrac{2}{3}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=1\\
k\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n}(b_{k}-a_{k})^{2}(1-\phi(B_{k})).

Proof.

If
\tfrac{1}{2}\notin\cup_{k=1}^{n}(a_{k},b_{k})
then
B(\tfrac{1}{2},\tfrac{1}{2})=\tfrac{1}{2}
and

\omega_{B}=\omega_{M}
. It follows that
\delta_{B^{\sigma_{2}}}=\delta_{M^{\sigma_{2}}}=\delta_{W}

\phi(B^{\sigma_{2}})=\phi(W)=-\tfrac{1}{2}

By Lemma
3.4
and Proposition
3.3
we have

\displaystyle\gamma(B)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{2}{3}\phi(B)-\tfrac{2}{3}\phi(B^{\sigma_{2}})=\tfrac{2}{3}-\tfrac{2}{3}\sum_{k=1}^{n}(b_{k}-a_{k})^{2}(1-\phi(B_{k}))+\tfrac{1}{3}

\displaystyle=1-\tfrac{2}{3}\sum_{k=1}^{n}(b_{k}-a_{k})^{2}(1-\phi(B_{k})).

Suppose now that
\tfrac{1}{2}\in(a_{j},b_{j})

a_{j}\geq 1-b_{j}

Then

\omega_{B}(u)=\begin{cases}u;&0\leq u\leq a_{j},\\

\displaystyle=6\int_{0}^{1}\delta_{B^{\sigma_{2}}}(u)\,du-2

\displaystyle=-2+6\int_{a_{j}}^{1-a_{j}}\big(u-a_{j}-(b_{j}-a_{j})B_{j}\big(\tfrac{u-a_{j}}{b_{j}-a_{j}},\tfrac{1-u-a_{j}}{b_{j}-a_{j}}\big)\big)\,du+6\int_{1-a_{j}}^{1}(2u-1)\,du

\displaystyle=6a_{j}^{2}-6a_{j}+1-6(b_{j}-a_{j})\int_{a_{j}}^{1-a_{j}}B_{j}\big(\tfrac{u-a_{j}}{b_{j}-a_{j}},\tfrac{1-u-a_{j}}{b_{j}-a_{j}}\big)\,du

\displaystyle\phi(B^{\sigma_{2}})

\displaystyle=12b_{j}-6b_{j}^{2}-12a_{j}b_{j}+6a_{j}-5

\displaystyle\qquad-6(b_{j}-a_{j})^{2}\int_{\frac{1-a_{j}-b_{j}}{b_{j}-a_{j}}}^{1}B_{j}\big(t,\tfrac{1-2a_{j}}{b_{j}-a_{j}}-t\big)\,dt

\displaystyle=6a_{j}^{2}-6a_{j}+1-6(1-a_{j}-b_{j})^{2}-6(b_{j}-a_{j})^{2}\int_{\frac{1-a_{j}-b_{j}}{b_{j}-a_{j}}}^{1}B_{j}\big(t,\tfrac{1-2a_{j}}{b_{j}-a_{j}}-t\big)\,dt.

\displaystyle\phi(B^{\sigma_{2}})

\displaystyle=6a_{j}^{2}-6a_{j}+1-6\max\{0,1-a_{j}-b_{j}\}^{2}-6(b_{j}-a_{j})^{2}\int_{\max\big\{0,\frac{1-a_{j}-b_{j}}{b_{j}-a_{j}}\big\}}^{\min\big\{1,\frac{1-2a_{j}}{b_{j}-a_{j}}\big\}}B_{j}\big(t,\tfrac{1-2a_{j}}{b_{j}-a_{j}}-t\big)\,dt

\displaystyle=6a_{j}^{2}-6a_{j}+1-6(b_{j}-a_{j})^{2}\max\{0,c\}^{2}-6(b_{j}-a_{j})^{2}\int_{\max\{0,c\}}^{1+\min\{0,c\}}B_{j}\big(t,1+c-t\big)\,dt,

where

\displaystyle\gamma(B)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{2}{3}\phi(B)-\tfrac{2}{3}\phi(B^{\sigma_{2}})

\displaystyle\qquad+4(b_{j}-a_{j})^{2}\int_{\max\{0,c\}}^{1+\min\{0,c\}}B_{j}\big(t,1+c-t\big)\,dt

\displaystyle=4(b_{j}-a_{j})^{2}\max\{0,c\}^{2}+4a_{j}-4a_{j}^{2}-\tfrac{2}{3}\sum_{k=1}^{n}(b_{k}-a_{k})^{2}(1-\phi(B_{k}))

5.
Concluding remarks

In this paper, we described the exact region
\Omega_{\phi,\gamma,\tau}
determined by Spearman’s footrule, Gini’s gamma and Kendall’s tau. It is a polyhedron
\Omega
depicted in Figure
1
, with
6
vertices,
11
edges and
7
faces. We proved that every point on the boundary of
\Omega
can be realized using a shuffle of
M
.

The polyhedron
\Omega_{\phi,\gamma,\tau}
has volume
\mathrm{vol}({\Omega_{\phi,\gamma,\tau}})=\frac{3}{16}=0.1875
, which represents
\frac{1}{32}=0.03125
of the volume of the
(\phi,\gamma,\tau)
-box
[-\tfrac{1}{2},1]\times[-1,1]\times[-1,1]
.
In
[
16
]
, the authors computed the volume of
\Omega_{\beta,\phi,\gamma}
, i.e.,
\text{vol}(\Omega_{\beta,\phi,\gamma})=\frac{19}{40}=0.475
, which represents
\frac{19}{240}\approx 0.07916
of the volume of the
(\beta,\phi,\gamma)
-box
[-1,1]\times[-\tfrac{1}{2},1]\times[-1,1]
.

Proposition
2.1
shows that for any copula
C
with given value
\phi(C)
, the spread of
\tau(C)
, i.e., the difference between the maximal and the minimal possible value of
\tau(C)
, is at most
1
. On average, the spread of
\tau(C)
given
\phi(C)
is

\frac{\text{area}(\Omega_{\phi,\tau})}{\text{length}([-\frac{1}{2},1])}=\tfrac{1}{2},

since
\text{area}(\Omega_{\phi,\tau})=\frac{3}{4}
, see
[
17
]
.
Similarly, given the value of
\gamma(C)
, the spread of
\tau(C)
is at most
\frac{2}{3}
. On average, the spread of
\tau(C)
given
\gamma(C)
is again

\frac{\text{area}(\Omega_{\gamma,\tau})}{\text{length}([-1,1])}=\tfrac{1}{2},

since
\text{area}(\Omega_{\gamma,\tau})=1
, see
[
17
]
.
If both
\phi(C)
and
\gamma(C)
are given, the maximal spread of
\tau(C)
is still
\frac{2}{3}
. Indeed, if
\phi(C)=\gamma(C)=0
, then
\tau(C)\in[-\frac{1}{3},\frac{1}{3}]
by Lemma
4.1
. The average spread of
\tau(C)
given
\phi(C)
and
\gamma(C)
is

\frac{\mathrm{vol}({\Omega_{\phi,\gamma,\tau}})}{\text{area}(\Omega_{\phi,\gamma})}=\tfrac{1}{3},

since
\text{area}(\Omega_{\phi,\gamma})=\frac{9}{16}
, see
[
15
]
.
Using results from
[
16
]
, the average spread of
\beta(C)
given
\phi(C)
and
\gamma(C)
is

\frac{\mathrm{vol}({\Omega_{\beta,\phi,\gamma}})}{\text{area}(\Omega_{\phi,\gamma})}=\tfrac{38}{45}\approx 0.8444.

We conclude that the value of
\tau(C)
is more closely related to the values of
\phi(C)
and
\gamma(C)
than the value of
\beta(C)
is.

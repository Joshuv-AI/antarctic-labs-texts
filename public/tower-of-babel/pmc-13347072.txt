A memory-driven pneumonia dynamics model validated against Ethiopian mortality data: a fractional-order differential equation framework
Authors: Mideksa Tola Jiru, Sathish Kumar Kumaravel
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Every year, pneumonia kills millions across the globe, disproportionately claiming the lives of children under five and immunocompromised adults in low-resource settings, yet the mathematical tools deployed to understand and contain it remain fundamentally inadequate. Classical integer-order epidemic models assume that disease dynamics depend solely on the present state of a system, a biologically untenable assumption for pneumonia, where cumulative immune history, pathogen exposure memory, and treatment delays collectively govern transmission trajectories. This study deviates from that tradition by introducing a well-stablished Caputo fractional-order SEIHR model including hereditary effects and epidemiological memory as part of the mathematical framework governing the disease progression. Biological validity is guaranteed through comprehensive well-posedness analysis, with solution positivity and boundedness established via fractional comparison theorems and Mittag -Leffler function theory within a positively invariant feasible region. In this work, the basic reproduction number \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{R}_{0}$$\end{document} is calculated by the next-generation matrix method and, by employing the fractional linearization theory and Lyapunov functional construction, both the local and global asymptotic stability of the disease-free equilibrium and the endemic equilibrium are formally proved, which are the first complete stability proofs ever obtained for a fractional-order pneumonia mode. A landmark theoretical finding reveals that the fractional-order parameter α operates as a precise memory-strength regulator, whereby lower values generate slower convergence trajectories and prolonged infectious periods that far more faithfully capture the biological reality of pneumonia than any integer-order model achieves. Sensitivity analysis demonstrates that parameters β and Λ should be targeted for intervention first since they are most crucial. On the other hand, by carrying out bifurcation analysis, we could observe that there is a threshold that divides the system between elimination and endemicity, namely \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{R}_{0}$$\end{document}=1. The proposed model yields a good fitting to the WHO pneumonia mortality in Ethiopia for 2018–2020, with an \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{R}^{2}$$\end{document} of 0.995 and maximum relative error below 0.55%. The conclusions drawn are obvious and decisive: memory effect is neither an artistic detail, nor a mathematically luxury, but an indispensable element of epidemic modeling.

Introduction

Pneumonia continues to pose a major source of morbidity and mortality related to infectious diseases, despite the tremendous strides made in vaccine development, antibiotic treatment, and healthcare infrastructures1. The epidemiology of pneumonia comprises multiple interrelated factors that determine the risk, severity, and outcome of the disease. Such factors include variability in individuals’ immune systems, differences in environmental geography and seasonality, pathogen attributes, delays in diagnosis, and delays from diagnosis to treatment initiation2–5. The complex interactions among these factors result in high unpredictability in disease dynamics, necessitating the development of predictive quantitative frameworks. In mathematical terms, models have been indispensable tools for studying infectious diseases, allowing researchers to examine the dynamics of disease transmission and assess the effects of interventions to control epidemics6–8.

Classical disease models generally utilize ordinary differential equations of integer order, where the rate of change in each compartment is determined solely by the current state of the system6. While such classical models have proven effective across a spectrum of infectious diseases, they fundamentally lack the capacity to incorporate temporal memory and the influence of historical states on current dynamics. Real-world epidemiological models of pneumonia transmission exhibit path dependence due to pathogen exposure history, the dynamics of the immune response to pathogens, and differences in health care accessibility, among other factors. Memory effects appear as non-exponential recovery, altered transmission rates following infection or vaccination, and delayed immune response, all of which cannot be modelled using ordinary integer-order Eqs2,9.

Fractional calculus offers a principled extension of classical epidemic models by using fractional-order derivatives, which naturally encode memory and hereditary effects into the mathematical structure10,11. Among available formulations of fractional derivatives, the Caputo derivative has proven particularly suitable for epidemiological applications because it accommodates standard initial conditions, typically measured as population counts at a specific time point, and permits a direct physical interpretation as the accumulated influence of past system states12,13. In the context of disease transmission, the order of the fractional derivative functions as a quantifiable parameter measuring the strength of memory coupling; lower fractional orders strengthen memory dependence, while integer-order limiting behavior diminishes historical influence. This parametric flexibility enables more accurate representation of observed disease behavior, including slow relaxation toward equilibrium, sustained endemic persistence, and temporal lag phenomena commonly encountered in pneumonia epidemiology.

Accumulating empirical evidence demonstrates the utility of fractional-order models in studying complex biological systems. Recent investigations have developed fractional epidemic models that incorporate analytical stability assessment, sensitivity analysis, and bifurcation analysis to clarify disease dynamics under memory-dependent conditions14–17. Additional research has implemented optimal control frameworks, incorporated heterogeneous treatment responses, and validated models against observational data within fractional formulations, demonstrating practical applicability18–22. Contemporary work combining fractional approaches with machine learning methods has further enhanced predictive accuracy in epidemiological forecasting23,24. In parallel, advances in nonlinear dynamical systems theory, particularly bifurcation and chaos analysis, have enriched the understanding of complex behaviors relevant to biological processes25–28.

Despite these methodological developments, fractional-order modeling remains underutilized in the specific context of pneumonia epidemiology. Current pneumonia models predominantly employ classical integer-order formulations and tend to emphasize optimization methods or purely numerical investigations, rather than rigorous mathematical analysis29–34. Though these studies generate useful insights into disease behavior, they generally omit critical mathematical properties, particularly positivity of solutions (the biological requirement that populations cannot be negative), boundedness (restricting populations to feasible levels), and global stability within a fractional-order setting. Furthermore, only limited research has examined how behavioral or awareness-related factors might be incorporated into pneumonia models35, and the explicit mechanistic role of fractional memory in shaping pneumonia transmission has not been systematically characterized.

Contemporary literature across adjacent biological and epidemiological systems consistently emphasizes the importance of integrating realistic mechanistic components, vaccination dynamics, stratified treatment protocols, and environmental cofactors into mathematical formulations19,20,22,36–38. However, a notable gap persists in synthesizing these modeling perspectives with fractional-order frameworks specifically for pneumonia, particularly regarding rigorous theoretical analysis and interpretation of results in terms of epidemiological mechanisms.

Addressing these identified limitations, this investigation proposes a fractional-order SEIHR (Susceptible–Exposed–Infectious–Hospitalized–Recovered) model for pneumonia transmission using the Caputo derivative formulation. The incorporation of a distinct hospitalized compartment reflects the critical role of hospital-based treatment in pneumonia management and represents a substantive distinction from existing formulations. The model employs a bilinear incidence function, a standard assumption in epidemiological modeling selected deliberately to ensure analytical tractability while isolating the effects of fractional memory without introducing additional nonlinear complications.

The principal contribution of this work is to provide a comprehensive fractional-order analysis of pneumonia transmission dynamics. Specifically, the investigation pursues four objectives: (i) to establish the model’s mathematical well-posedness by proving solutions remain non-negative and bounded, (ii) to derive the basic reproduction number using established next-generation matrix methods applied consistently within the fractional framework, (iii) to conduct thorough local and global stability analysis of disease-free and endemic equilibrium points using appropriate mathematical techniques, and (iv) to systematically investigate how variation in the fractional-order parameter influences disease dynamics through comparison with classical integer-order predictions.

Unlike existing pneumonia models29–35, the present work offers three novel contributions. First, prior fractional pneumonia studies29–34 lack a distinct hospitalized (H) compartment, whereas our SEIHR formulation explicitly includes H to capture treatment delays and hospital capacity effects. Second, references29–34 provide no rigorous well-posedness analysis (positivity, boundedness, invariant region) within a fractional framework; we prove these properties via fractional comparison theorems and Mittag–Leffler functions. Third, global asymptotic stability of the endemic equilibrium using Lyapunov methods has never been established for any fractional-order pneumonia model our Theorem 5 provides the first such proof. These innovations, (i) a hospitalized compartment, (ii) complete well-posedness analysis, and (iii) novel global stability proofs constitute the original contribution of this work relative to29–35.

Mathematical model formulation

A fractional-order mathematical model for analyzing the dynamics of pneumonia in a nonlinear deterministic setting has been constructed. The formulation incorporates epidemiological compartments, structure and fractional calculus to model instant interactions and history-dependent mechanisms. An analytical method to study the qualitative characteristics of the system and a numerical simulation to demonstrate the analytical findings are given in this paper. All simulations were performed in Python, and the parameters were chosen from the literature within dimensionally consistent, biologically plausible ranges.

Pneumonia is an infectious and complicated disease which is characterized by a set of environmental factors, immunity and the impact of medical assistance programs. An integer-order epidemiological model considers disease dynamics based solely on the current state of the system; this hypothesis is not correct for pneumonia, as it depends on past interactions, immune responses, and treatment delays. So a fractional-order mathematical framework is used for modeling pneumonia that accounts for memory and heredity.

The memory and heredity are modeled using Caputo fractional derivative, which is preferred for modeling biological systems with standard initial conditions. The bilinear incidence function is common in compartmental modeling and is a mathematically simple expression for the incidence that is logically implied by the assumption of homogenous mixing in the population, that is to say, that each person is as likely to interact with any other person as with themselves. Although there are several options for nonlinear incidence rates, their usage complicates the mathematical modeling with only marginal improvement in biological realism. Therefore, the bilinear incidence rate equation is used, as this model is the simplest. The model is based on the SEIHR compartmental model, which means the patient goes through all stages in the order S-E-I-H-R, representing pneumonia transmission dynamics and its outcomes.

Condition of existence of disease

The EE is unique when reproduction number is greater than one. At the endemic equilibrium, pneumonia exists at positive values of infected exposed, infectious, hospital, and recovered classes. This equilibrium state exists when the transmission rates between compartments (transmission to infected, transition to hospitalized, recovery from hospital, death and from recovered class) are in equilibrium. This equilibrium state is attained when the pneumonia cannot be eradicated by control measures.

Stability analyses

Stability analysis is one of the key tools for revealing the long-term behavior of epidemiological models, i.e., whether an infectious disease will be extinct. Stability analysis for fractional order systems differs considerably from classical integer-order systems because of memory and non-locality properties of fractional derivatives. The conventional stability criterion of stability analysis through eigenvalues must therefore be modified to fractional-order systems. Stability analysis of the proposed pneumonia system in terms of both local and global equilibrium points will be discussed in this chapter. This chapter presents stability analysis of the disease-free and endemic equilibrium points by means of fractional linearization technique and Lyapunov approach.

Preliminaries

In this part, we provide the required theoretical basis and the mathematical background needed to analyze the stability of the suggested fractional order pneumonia model. One unique feature that separates the fractional order dynamical systems from their counterparts, the classical integer order systems, is the hereditary effect in which the status of the system depends not only on its present status but also on its full past experience11,13.

The stability criterion of fractional order systems is not the same as the stability criterion of conventional integer order systems. For a classical system, a local asymptotically stable equilibrium point requires all eigenvalues of the Jacobian matrix to have negative real parts. For a fractional order system, a local asymptotically stable equilibrium point requires eigenvalue arguments in the complex plane. It is stated that in the theory of fractional calculus, an equilibrium point is locally asymptotically stable if all eigenvalues \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{\lambda\:}_{i}\:$$\end{document}of the Jacobian matrix satisfy.11\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\mid\:arg\left({\lambda\:}_{i}\right)\mid\:>\frac{\alpha\:\pi\:}{2},i=\mathrm{1,2},\dots\:,n.$$\end{document}

This is a known result in the literature10,11,13, stating that a system with fractional orders can still be stable even if some eigenvalues have a positive real part, provided their arguments satisfy the relation stated above. This property is due to the memory property of the fractional derivatives, which tends to make the system more stable.

Furthermore, solutions of fractional order systems usually take the form of the Mittag-Leffler function, which is the generalization of the exponential function; the properties of this function are important for studying the asymptotic behavior of the solutions10.

From this result, we will study the stability, in the local and global senses, of the free disease equilibrium points and the endemic equilibrium points of the proposed model.

The WHO epidemiological surveillance system and the UNICEF estimates of child mortality provide the source of data for this study. Ethiopia is a major pneumonia endemic region in sub-Saharan Africa, with approximately 49,000 LRI deaths estimated in 2018, decreasing steadily to 44,000 deaths in 2020.

Validation conclusions for the caputo fractional-order SEIHR model: a statistical assessment of ethiopian pneumonia mortality, 2018–2020

Model fit: The Caputo fractional-order SEIHR model with α = 0.925 explains 99.5% of observed variance in WHO Ethiopia LRI mortality data (2018–2020), with a maximum relative error of less than 0.55% across both training and validation periods.

Memory effects: The calibrated fractional order α = 0.925 significantly less than unity confirms that memory effects are a statistically identifiable feature of Ethiopian pneumonia mortality dynamics. This finding is consistent with the theoretical result established in Sect. 4 that fractional memory prolongs transient dynamics and alters convergence rates without changing equilibrium values.

Integer-model performance: The integer-order model (α = 1.00) produces comparable point estimates but lacks the mechanistic representation of historical dependence that characterizes realistic pneumonia progression. The fractional model’s superior validation MAR (116.4 vs. 124.65 deaths) provides empirical support for the memory-based formulation.

Epidemiological context: While the number of annual deaths from LRI in Ethiopia has fallen from around 49,000 in 2018 to 44,000 in 2020, a decline of 10.2%, pneumonia is one of the biggest causes of mortality among Ethiopian children; the death rate from under-five LRI infections fell from 22,000 to 18,000 over that time period (see Table 3 below).

Overall verdict: Compared to its integer-order analogue, the SEIHR model based on fractional-order derivatives exhibits superior performance in terms of validation metrics. Moreover, using fractional-order models to account for memory dependence is a theoretically sound method of modeling dynamic processes.

Conclusions and future work

This study presents a rigorous mathematical framework for analyzing pneumonia transmission dynamics through a fractional-order SEIHR compartmental model, incorporating memory-dependent effects via Caputo fractional derivatives to authentically capture the historical complexity inherent in real disease progression. The well-posedness of the model was established by proving the positivity and boundedness of all solutions within a biologically feasible invariant region, utilizing fractional comparison theorems and Mittag–Leffler function analysis to guarantee biological validity across all time scales. The basic reproduction number \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{R}_{0}$$\end{document}was derived systematically using the next-generation matrix method, adapted to fractional systems, followed by rigorous proofs of both local and global asymptotic stability at the disease-free and endemic equilibrium points through fractional linearization theory and Lyapunov functional approaches.

A central scientific contribution of this work lies in demonstrating that the fractional-order parameter α fundamentally reshapes system dynamics in ways that are categorically absent from classical integer-order models. Lower values of α induce slower convergence trajectories and prolonged infectious periods that are essential for realistically capturing pneumonia epidemiology. Parametric sensitivity analysis identified the transmission rate β and population recruitment rate Λ as the dominant drivers of disease spread, while bifurcation analysis revealed a transcritical phase transition at \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{R}_{0}$$\end{document} = 1, confirming that small increases in transmission shift the system from elimination to endemic persistence. The negative sensitivity indices of hospitalization rate η and disease-induced mortality δ underscore the critical importance of healthcare capacity and treatment efficacy as intervention targets.

Through empirical validation using World Health Organization (WHO) surveillance data on pneumonia deaths from Ethiopia between 2018 and 2020, it was found that the fractional model with α = 0.925 accounts for ~ 99.5% of the observed variance, and its performance in the validation is better than its integer-order counterpart, supporting the statistical identification of memory effects in pneumonia mortality dynamics. However, with these gains, the current model does not consider a vaccination component or include assumptions of homogeneous population mixing and constant transmission rates. These limitations will be addressed in future extensions by incorporating contact heterogeneity and time-varying transmission, optimal vaccination and control strategies, age-structured and spatially explicit representation, stochastic fractional models for probabilistic risk assessment, and machine learning frameworks and real-time surveillance data.

Ultimately, this work redefines how pneumonia dynamics are mathematically understood by embedding memory and hereditary effects at the core of the model, it bridges the gap between abstract fractional theory and real-world epidemiological practice, delivering a scientifically rigorous, empirically proven framework that empowers public health decision-makers to craft smarter, faster, and more effective strategies in the global fight against pneumonia.

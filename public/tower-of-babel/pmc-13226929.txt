Dietary dimethylglycine alleviates cyclic heat stress-induced jejunal injury in yellow-feathered broilers by inhibiting the TLR4/NF-κB signaling pathway and restoring MUC2 expression
Authors: Dan Shao, Longqing Hu, Wei Guo, Jinglong Chen, Xuming Guo, Hong Yao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study aimed to investigate the protective effect and mechanism of dietary dimethylglycine (DMG) on jejunal injury in yellow-feathered broilers subjected to cyclic heat stress (HS). A total of 180 35-day-old male yellow-feathered broilers were randomly allocated into 3 groups: the thermoneutral condition with basal diet group (CON), the cycle heat stress with basal diet group (HS, 32-35°C), and the cycle heat stress with 0.1% DMG in basal diet group (HS_DMG, 32-35°C). The experiment included a 7-day heating period followed by a 14-day cyclic heat stress period. Growth performance, rectal temperature, jejunal morphology and ultrastructure, inflammatory cytokine levels, and protein expression related to the TLR4/NF-κB pathway and MUC2 were evaluated. Additionally, primary chicken jejunal epithelial cells were isolated and exposed to heat stress with or without DMG pretreatment to validate in vivo findings. Results showed that heat stress significantly increased rectal temperature (P < 0.05), had a tendency to reduce body weight (BW, P = 0.058) and average daily gain (ADG, P = 0.050), and increase the feed-to-gain ratio (F/G, P = 0.053). Histologically, HS caused epithelial shedding, reduced villus height and villus height/crypt depth, and induced microvilli damage and mitochondrial swelling, all of which were attenuated by DMG supplementation. Heat stress also elevated jejunal mucosa levels of IL-6, IL-8, IL-1β, and TNF-α (P < 0.05), while DMG reduced IL-6, IL-8, and TNF-α (P < 0.05). Immunohistochemistry and western blotting revealed that HS decreased MUC2 expression and increased TLR4 and p-NF-κB-p65 protein levels (P < 0.05), whereas DMG reversed these changes (P < 0.05). In vitro, DMG pretreatment alleviated HS-induced reduction in inflammatory cytokine release, and alterations in TLR4, NF-κB, and MUC2 protein expression. In conclusion, dietary DMG supplementation alleviates cyclic heat stress-induced jejunal injury in yellow-feathered broilers by suppressing the TLR4/NF-κB signaling pathway and restoring MUC2 expression.Keywords: Cyclic heat stress, Dimethylglycine, Jejunum, TLR4/NF-κB, MUC2

Introduction

For recent years, extreme heat events occur constantly accompanied by the global warming (Ma et al., 2022). And the elevated environment temperatures have the feasibility to adversely affect livestock industry, with results to reduce livestock welfare and productivity (Johnson, 2018). Compared to the other livestock, poultry is extreme susceptible to heat tress due to their limited ability to regulate heat loss resulting from covering feathering and lacking sweat glands, as well as the genetic selection on increasing growth rates and improving feed efficiency of poultry (Wasti et al., 2020). Especially, modern broilers, who provide one of the most efficient protein sources for human (Jing et al., 2023), are characterized of more metabolic activities resulting in increased body heat production (Al-Zghoul et al., 2019). Therefore, heat stress can cause poor growth performance and health status of broilers, which is mainly accompanied with metabolic disorders, tissue damage and organ dysfunction (Andretta et al., 2021). The intestine could be likely considered the most assailable tissue in heat-stressed broiler (Quinteiro-Filho et al., 2010). When broilers are exposed to high temperature, the heat dissipation mechanism is activated, and blood tends to flow from internal organ to other organs with heat dissipation function. Thus, less blood flow toward digestive organs such as intestine, which may cause intestinal ischemia and hypoxia, villi shortening, mucosal barrier damage, and nutrient absorption obstruction (Sun et al., 2015). Particularly, as the central section of the small intestine, the jejunum was identified as the most segment in response to heat stress (Hu et al., 2024; Rajput et al., 2017). Hence, it is essential to alleviate the injury to the jejunum of broilers exposed to heat stress.

Under heat stress condition, inflammatory damage is inevitable in the intestine by exhibiting increased release of pro-inflammatory cytokines such as IL-1β, TNF-α in the jejunum of broiler (Wu et al., 2018; Lan et al., 2020). Toll-like receptor 4 (TLR4) plays a pivotal role in stress-induced inflammatory priming (Tang et al., 2021), who can activate intracellular inflammatory response signaling pathways, including the transcription nuclear factor-kappa B (NF-κB) pathway to regulate the production of inflammatory cytokines (Byun et al., 2015). A previous study confirmed that heat-stressed with 41°C aggravated the inflammatory state of the chicken’ intestine by activating the TLR4/NF-κB pathway to upregulate the expression of IL-1β and TNF-α (Tang et al., 2021). Similarly, after 3 weeks of high-temperature heat stress at 35°C in broilers, the renal TLR4/NF-κB pathway was activated, and the content of inflammatory factors significantly increased, thereby causing inflammatory enteritis (Dong et al., 2024). Mucin 2 (MUC2), a glycoprotein secreted by goblet cells of the intestine forms the first line of innate host to defense against pathogenic bacteria and viruses (Cornick et al., 2015), and its expression would be inhibited by the intestinal inflammatory response (Lee et al., 2018). Besides, a decreased expression of MUC2 was found in the jejunum of broiler under heat stress treatment, and its expression was negatively correlated with NF-κB (Song et al., 2019), who was proved to be directly involved in MUC2 regulation (Lee et al., 2002). Thus, it can be inferred the TLR4/NF-κB pathway may affect the intestinal inflammation by regulating the expression of MUC2, and it is imperative to reduce the inflammatory status by restraining the activation of TLR4-mediated NF-κB pathway under heat stress condition.

Nowadays, dimethylglycine (DMG) is diffusely used as a dietary supplement for health improvement (Wolfsegger et al., 2021), as it is similar to betaine that can gain body’s immune status (Friesen et al., 2007; Cools et al., 2010). On account of its functional capabilities, DMG have been applied to animal nutrition effectively. Diet administration of DMG was found relieving intestinal structural damage in intrauterine growth retardation (IUGR) weaned piglets (Bai et al., 2021), and inhibiting jejunal inflammatory reaction and immune disorder in IUGR newborns via TLR4/NF-κB pathway (Bai et al., 2022). As regards broilers, positive effects on body weight, feed conversion ratio were discovered with DMG supplementation (Kalmar et al., 2014; Chalvatzi et al., 2020). Furthermore, the protective effects of DMG against intestinal inflammatory were observed in broilers under heat stress (Wang et al., 2022). Thus, supplying DMG is a valuable candidate nutritional strategy for protecting broilers from heat stress. However, the role of DMG in improving intestinal inflammatory of broilers and whether TLR4/NF-κB pathway and MUC2 play a role remains deficiently explored.

Based on those perceptions, we carried out the present study to investigate the reply of broilers under heat stress to dietary DMG supplementation by evaluating growth performance, Jejunal histological morphology, expression level of TLR4/NF-κB pathway and MUC2 in jejunal mucosa of broilers and Jejunal epithelial cells.

Materials and methods

Animal ethics statement

The Animal Care and Use Committee of Jiangsu Institute of Poultry Science approved these animal experiments with an approval number of NO.2025-0020 (Yangzhou, China).

Animals and experimental design

The yellow-feather broilers used in this study were obtained from Yangzhou Lihua Poultry and Livestock Co., Ltd. (Jiangsu, China). A total of 180 35-day-old male yellow feather broilers with similar initial body weight (1173.26 ± 13.61 g) were randomly divided into 3 groups: the thermoneutral condition with basal diet group (CON), the cycle heat stress with basal diet group (HS), and the cycle heat stress with 0.1% DMG in basal diet (≥99% purity, Aladdin Chemical reagent Co., Ltd., China.) group (HS_DMG). For each group, 6 replicates and 10 birds per replicate were used. During the experiment, after a 7-day heating period, a 14-day cycle heat stress period was taken (Fig. 1A). The 7-day heating procedure in HS and HS_DMG groups was set as shown in Table 1. The 14-day cycle heat stress procedure was as follows: 00:00-06:00, 32 °C; 06:00-10:00, 34 °C; 10:00-16:00, 35 °C; 16:00-20:00, 34 °C; 20:00-00:00, 32 °C. While the CON group was set at 26 ± 1 °C. The experiment was conducted in the environmental control cabin (Beijing Kulan Technology Co., LTD) of Yizheng Base of Jiangsu Institute of Poultry Science, with a stable humidity (60 ± 5%). All the birds were raised in the 3-layer stacked cages (100 cm×80 cm×45 cm), and provided ad libitum water and feed throughout the experiment. The ingredient formulation and nutritional concentrations of the basal diet were in line with the occupation standard named Nutritional Requirements for Yellow-Feathered Broilers (NY/T 3645-2020) and shown in Table 2.

Sample collection

During cyclic heat stress, rectal temperature was measured every 3 days (on days 1, 4, 7, 10, and 14, excluding a 7-day adaptation period) using a food-grade digital thermometer (−50 ∼ 300 °C, 8897, Deli Group Co., Ltd., China). On day 14 of the experiment, body weight (BW) was recorded on a replicate basis following a 12-hour fasting period to compute the average daily gain (ADG), and based on feed consumption, average daily feed intake (ADFI) and the fee to gain ratio (F/G) were also determined. Subsequently, 1 bird from each replicate was randomly chosen for sample collection. The birds were anesthetized with intravenous injection of 50 mg/mL ketamine hydrochloride (50 mg/kg body weight), then euthanized by cervical dislocation after unconsciousness was achieved, and an approximately 1.5 cm segment from the mid-jejunum was collected and fixed with 10% paraformaldehyde solution for subsequent hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining and immunohistochemistry analysis. The mid-jejunum was then opened longitudinally rinsed with distilled water to remove residual digesta, and a small piece of fragment (approximately 0.5 cm×0.5 cm) was excised and fixed in 2.5% glutaraldehyde and store at 4 °C in the dark for ultrastructural examination. Finally, jejunal mucosa was scraped from the remaining mid-jejunum with a glass slide, immediately frozen in liquid nitrogen, and then stored at −80°C for subsequent analysis of inflammatory factor content and protein expression.

Jejunal histological morphology measurements

Formalin-fixed jejunum samples were routinely washed, dehydrated, embedded in paraffin, and sectioned into 5-micron-thick slices. Then the slices were stained with hematoxylin and eosin for histomorphology evaluation. Villus height and crypt depth were measured under a light microscope (CX23, Olympus, Japan) using Image J 1.53 software (National Institutes of Health, Bethesda, USA), following the method described by Zhong et al. (2020). Glutaraldehyde-fixed jejunum samples were post-fixed in 1% osmium tetroxide, dehydrated, permeated and then embedded in resin. The embedded jejunum samples were cut into 100 nm-sections and stained with uranyl acetate and lead citrate, finally examined under a transmission electron microscope (TEM, H-7650, Hitachi, Japan) for changes in jejunal morphology.

Jejunal immunohistochemistry

Beside for hematoxylin and eosin staining, the left embedded tissue sections were performed for immunohistochemical (IHC) staining. Following deparaffinization and rehydration, these sections were treated with 3% hydrogen peroxide solution to block endogenous peroxidase activity. After rising with phosphate buffer saline (PBS), these sections were blocked with goat serum for 20 min at room temperature and next incubated overnight at 4 °C with a MUC2 antibody (1:500, A14659, ABclonal Biotechnology Co., Ltd., Wuhan, China). The sections were subsequently incubated with a horseradish peroxidase (HRP)-conjugated secondary antibody for 20 min at room temperature. Immunoreactivity was visualized using 3,3′-diaminobenzidine (DAB), and the sections were counterstained with hematoxylin. Images were captured with a light microscope (CX23, Olympus, Japan).

Jejunal epithelial cells isolation and culture

19-day-old chicken embryos were obtained from Yangzhou Xianglong Poultry Industry Development Co., Ltd. (Jiangsu, China). Primary jejunal epithelial cells were isolated mainly following the methods described by Ghiselli et al. (2021). In simple terms, jejunum was isolated and placed in the ice-cold d-hank’s Balanced Salt Solution (D-HBSS, H1045, Beijing solarbio science&technology co., ltd., Beijing, China), and cleaned from mesentery. Next, Jejunum was cut it into 1 mm small pieces and washed once with ice-cold d-HBSS, digested with 10% Collagenase Ⅰ (C8140, Beijing solarbio science&technology co., ltd., Beijing, China) at 37°C for 40 min and centrifuged at 154.95 × g for 5 min. After that, the obtained pellet was resuspended with DMEM/F12 (C11330500BT, Gibco, Carlsbad, CA) and filtered through a 100 μm, 40 μm cell-strainer (352350, Corning Incorporated, Corning, NY, USA) respectively, to recover the > 40 μm fraction. The recovered aggregates were again centrifuged at 154.95 × g for 5 min, and resuspended in complete culture medium consisting of DMEM/F12 medium (C11330500BT, Gibco, Carlsbad, CA) supplemented with 2% fetal bovine serum (10099141, Gibco, Carlsbad, CA) and 1% penicillin-ptreptomycin (P1400, Beijing solarbio science&technology co., ltd., Beijing, China). The obtained aggregates were subsequently seeded into 96-well and 6-well culture plates coated with basement membrane matrix (Matrigel®, 354230, Corning Incorporated, Corning, NY, USA) and cells were cultured at 37°C in a humidified incubator with 5% CO2.

After being cultured in 96-well culture plates for 24 h when cells reached nearly 80∼90% confluence, they were subjected to DMG treatment and heat stress exposure. In brief, DMG were added to cells at final concentrations of 0, 100 μM, 250 μM, 500 μM, 750 μM, 1 mM and incubated for 6 h under standard culture conditions (37°C, 5% CO₂). Subsequently, cells were exposed to heat stress by transferring the culture plates to a 45°C incubator for duration of 4 h, 8 h, 12 h and 24 h, while control cells remained at 37°C. After heat stress exposure, these cells were used to assess cell viability, and cell culture supernatants were collected, centrifuged at 154.95 × g for 5 min to remove debris, and stored at −80°C for subsequent inflammatory cytokine analysis. Based on the optimal DMG concentration and heat stress duration determined from the viability and cytokine assays, subsequent experiments were performed in 6-well plates under the selected conditions. Cells were harvested for western blot analysis.

Cell viability assay

For cell viability, cells were incubated with Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8) solution (CA1210, Beijing Solarbio Science & Technology Co., Ltd., Beijing, China) for an appropriate duration according to the manufacturer’s protocol. Absorbance was measured at 450 nm with a microplate reader (Infinite M200 Pro, Tecan, Switzerland). Cell viability was expressed as a percentage relative to the untreated control group.

Inflammatory cytokine contents analysis of jejunal mucosa and cell supernatants

Jejunal mucosa samples were homogenized in ice-cold PBS and centrifuged at 1,288 × g for 10 min at 4°C. The supernatants were collected together with cell supernatants for cytokine quantification. The levels of interleukin-1β (IL-1β), interleukin-6 (IL-6), interleukin-8 (IL-8), and tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α) in both jejunal mucosa and cell supernatants were measured using commercial enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) kits (Shanghai Enzyme-linked Biotechnology Co., Ltd., Shanghai, China) according to the manufacturer’s protocols. Absorbance was read at 450 nm using a microplate reader (Infinite 200 Pro, TECAN, Austria). Total protein concentration in each sample was determined using a bicinchoninic acid (BCA) protein assay kit (Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute, Nanjing, China) for data normalization.

Western blot analysis of jejunal mucosa and cells

Total protein was extracted from jejunal mucosa together with cells using a whole protein extraction kit (KGP250, KeyGEN BioTECH, China) supplemented with protease and phosphatase inhibitors. Protein concentrations were determined using a BCA assay kit (KGA902, KeyGEN BioTECH, China). Equal amounts of protein were separated by SDS-PAGE and transferred onto polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) membranes. After blocking with skim milk in TBST for 2 h at room temperature, the membranes were incubated overnight at 4°C with primary antibodies against TLR4 (1:1000, 19811-1-AP, Proteintech Group, Inc., Wuhan, China), NF-κB-p65 (1:1000, 10745-1-AP, Proteintech Group, Inc., Wuhan, China), Phospho-NF-Kb-p65 (1:2000, AP0124, ABclonal Biotechnology Co., Ltd., Wuhan, China), MUC2 (1:1000, A14659, ABclonal Biotechnology Co., Ltd., Wuhan, China), and β-actin (1:10000, AC026, ABclonal Biotechnology Co., Ltd., Wuhan, China). Following washes, the membranes were incubated with horseradish peroxidase (HRP)-conjugated anti-rabbit IgG secondary antibody (KGC6202-0.1, KeyGEN BioTECH, China) for 2 h at room temperature. Immunoreactive bands were visualized using an enhanced chemiluminescence (ECL) kit (KGP1201, KeyGEN BioTECH, China) and imaged with the ChemiDoc MP Imaging System (Bio-Rad, USA). Densitometric analysis was performed using Gel-Pro32 software, and target protein expression levels were normalized to β-actin.

Statistical analysis

The statistical analyses were performed with one-way ANOVA followed by Tukey’s honestly significant difference (HSD) post-hoc test for multiple comparisons in SPSS 20.0 software (SPSS Inc., Chicago, USA). All data were presented as mean ± standard error (SE). Statistical significance was set at P < 0.05, with trends were considered at 0.05 < P < 0.1. Graphs were generated using GraphPad Prism 9.0.0 software (GraphPad Prism Inc., CA, USA).

Results

Rectal temperature and growth performance

As shown in Fig. 1B, heat stress led to increased rectal temperature compare to CON group, but there was no difference between HS group and HS_DMG group except for rectal temperature on 14-day with cycle heat stress. It was also observed dietary DMG supplement alleviated adverse effects of heat stress on growth performance of broilers (Fig. 1C-F), including increased BW (P = 0.058), ADG (P = 0.050) and decreased F/G (P = 0.053).

Jejunal histological morphology

Histological examination showed heat stress induced jejunum damage characterized with shedding of the epithelium, exposed lamina propria, and significant decreased villus height, villus height/crypt depth (P < 0.05), and DMG treatment availably moderated this decrease (Fig. 2A, 2C-E). Based on TEM observation, aligned microvilli and intact mitochondria were observed in CON group. On the contrary, heat stress treatment caused decreased microvilli, mitochondria swelling and ridge disappearance, which were attenuated by DMG (Fig. 2B).

Inflammation factors content in jejunal mucosa

The changes of inflammation factors contents were shown in Fig. 3A-D. Compared to the CON group, heat stress significantly increased the IL-6, IL-8, IL-1β, and TNF-α level in jejunal mucosa (P < 0.05), and DMG supplement significantly reduced the amount of IL-6, IL-8 (P < 0.05), and had a tendency to decrease IL-1β and TNF-α (P = 0.085, P = 0.068, respectively).

Protein expression of MUC2 and TLR4/NF-kB signaling pathway in jejunal mucosa

Compared to the control treatment, the immunohistochemical results indicated that heat stress resulted in a lower ratio of the positive region of MUC2 protein in the jejunum tissue (Fig. 3E). Consistently, heat stress depressed level of MUC2, but enhanced the protein level of TLR4 and p-NF-κB-p65 (Fig. 3F-J, P < 0.05). Pretreatment with DMG effectively increased level of MUC2, and decreased protein level of TLR4 and p-NF-κB-p65 (P < 0.05).

Inflammation factors content, protein expression of MUC2 and TLR4/NF-kB signaling pathway in jejunal epithelial cells

Under heat stress conditions, the viability of jejunal epithelial cells was significantly reduced, with a viability of approximately 60% after 12 h of exposure. However, pretreatment with 500 μM DMG markedly enhanced cell viability (Fig. 4B-E). Based on these findings, jejunal epithelial cells pretreated with 500 μM DMG followed by 12 h of heat stress were used for subsequent experiments. Morphological examination revealed that heat stress decreased cell density, increased intercellular spaces, and induced cell detachment and apoptosis, whereas DMG supplementation alleviated these effects (Fig. 4A).

Consistent with the in vitro observations, heat stress significantly elevated the levels of IL-6, IL-8, IL-1β, and TNF-α in the supernatant of jejunal epithelial cells. Pretreatment with 500 μM DMG significantly reduced IL-1β and TNF-α levels (Fig. 5A-D, P < 0.05) and tended to decrease IL-6 and IL-8 secretion (P = 0.073, P = 0.053, respectively). Furthermore, heat stress increased the protein expression of TLR4 and p-NF-κB-p65 while decreasing MUC2 expression. Notably, DMG treatment effectively reversed these changes in protein expression (Fig. 5E-I, P < 0.05).

Discussion

Environment pressures like heat stress have been considered an environmental stressor affect poultry production that can disrupt homeothermy and impair production performance (McEwen, 2000; Quinteiro-Filho et al., 2010). In present research, we observed reduced BW, ADFI, ADG and increased rectal temperature, F/G in broilers under cyclic heat stress. From this, impaired growth performance under heat stress was an undisputed phenomenon. While after DMG supplement, BW, ADG and F/G was improved to a certain extent, though ADFI and rectal temperature were not promoted, which was in accordance with a previous work by Wang et al. (2022), suggesting a potential improved digestion and absorption of nutrients by effects of emulsifying and improving intestinal structure of DMG (Prola et al., 2013; Hariganesh and Prathiba, 2000). Thus, these results indicated DMG might alleviate impaired intestinal morphology induced by heat stress.

As the most sensitive organ to heat stress, the normal morphology of intestine directly affects the metabolism and immune function of poultry (Santos et al., 2019). Earlier studies have revealed heat stress could disrupt morphology of intestine, especially jejunum, the major site for digesting and absorbing, resulting in variation of classical morphology indexes, such as reduced in villus height, villus height/crypt depth, thus damaging the integrity of the gut epithelial tissue (Wu et al., 2018; Wang et al., 2022). Analogously, histological examination and TEM observation in this study revealed that cyclic heat stress induced jejunal damage, characterized by epithelial shedding, exposed lamina propria, reduced villus height, villus height/crypt depth, and decreased microvilli, mitochondria swelling and ridge disappearance. With DMG supplementation relieved those heat stress induced jejunal damage, indicating DMG might strengthen the integrity of intestinal battier (Bai et al., 2022). Serving as a temperature stress protective agent (Bashir et al., 2014), DMG was supposed to regulate the mucosal layer (Chalvatzi et al., 2020). Previous studies found heat stress stimulated mucosal production of inflammatory cytokines such as IL-1β, THF-α (Lan et al., 2020), which was consistent with the results in this study that heat stress increased inflammatory cytokines (IL‑6, IL‑8, IL‑1β, and TNF‑α) secretion. In parallel with morphological protection, DMG positively reduced the heat‑induced elevation of inflammatory cytokines in the jejunal mucosa as well as in jejunal cell supernatants. When it comes to intestinal inflammation, it is inevitable to speak of MUC2, the primary component of the mucus barrier (Qiao et al., 2025). At the present study, heat stress decreased the content of MUC2. Notably, MUC2 has been demonstrated could actively promote an anti-inflammatory phenotype, and MUC2 deficiency Spontaneously resulted in enhancive contents of inflammatory Cytokines (Van der Sluis et al., 2006). The administration of DMG was verified to improve the concentration of MUC2 (Bai et al., 2022), which was also be validated in our result, thus it was supposed DMG might increase the numbers of goblet cells to accelerate MUC2 secretion (Wang et al., 2022, 2025). As a result, on account of its anti-inflammatory activity, our results showed DMG could moderate the integrity of jejunum by regulating the release of inflammatory factors and MUC2.

A pivotal finding of this study was that heat stress significantly upregulated the protein expression of TLR4 and p-NF-κB-p65 in both jejunal mucosa and primary jejunal epithelial cells, and DMG had a positive regulatory effect. Indeed, TLR4/NF-κB signaling pathway was confirmed participating in inflammatory response to heat stress (Yang et al., 2021). As a characterized TLR member, TLR4 can activates the transcriptional factor NF-κB, in turn to regulate the release of pro-inflammatory cytokines (Meng et al., 2023). In agreement with our results, some studies also demonstrated that heat stress enhanced of IL-6, TNF-α with gained protein expression of TLR4 and p-NF-κB-p65 (Liu et al., 2022), and DMG could weaken the activity of TLR4/NF-κB pathway, accelerate the content of cytoplasmic NF-κB, thus to alleviate intestinal inflammatory state (Bai et al., 2022). When it comes to TLR4/NF-κB signaling pathway activation closely related to inflammatory response of the intestine, MUC2 is without exception affected. The notion was supported by the study showed overactivation of TLR4/NF-κB suppressed MUC2 expression and exacerbated intestinal inflammation, whereas inhibition of this pathway restored MUC2 levels and reduced inflammatory cytokine secretion (Tang et al., 2024). In addition, probiotic intervention simultaneously downregulated TLR2/4/NF-κB pathway-related genes and upregulated MUC2 expression, thereby improving intestinal barrier function (Barroso et al., 2022). Our results are highly consistent with this mechanistic framework. Based on these findings, we inferred that DMG alleviated heat stress-induced jejunal inflammation by inhibiting the overactivation of the TLR4/NF-κB pathway, thereby promoting the MUC2 protein expression, repairing the intestinal mucus barrier, and ultimately reducing the production and release of pro-inflammatory cytokines. While, the assured regulatory network by DMG mediated to give play to ant-inflammation effect under heat stress needs further investigation.

Conclusion

In conclusion, heat stress induced a significant increase in rectal temperature and impaired growth performance in broilers, characterized by decreased BW and ADG and increased F/G. These physiological changes were associated with jejunal damage, including epithelial shedding, villus atrophy, and ultrastructural disruption of microvilli and mitochondria. Mechanistically, heat stress activated the TLR4/NF-κB signaling pathway, leading to elevated pro-inflammatory cytokines and reduced MUC2 expression in both jejunal mucosa and primary jejunal epithelial cells. Dietary supplementation with DMG effectively attenuated these adverse effects, as evidenced by restored jejunal morphology, reduced TLR4/NF-κB activation and inflammatory responses, and improved MUC2 expression, ultimately contributing to better growth performance under heat stress conditions. These findings suggest that DMG exerted protective effects against heat stress-induced intestinal injury, potentially through suppression of the TLR4/NF-κB inflammatory pathway and preservation of intestinal barrier function.

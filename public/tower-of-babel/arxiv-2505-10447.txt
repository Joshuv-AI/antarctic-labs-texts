Abstract
. We extend the previously established zesting techniques from fusion categories to general tensor categories. In particular we consider the category of comodules over a Hopf algebra, providing a detailed translation of the categorical zesting construction into explicit Hopf algebraic terms: we show that the associative zesting of the category of comodules yields a coquasi-Hopf algebra whose comodule category is precisely the zested category. We explicitly write the modified multiplication and the associator, as well as the structures involved in the braided case. For pointed Hopf algebras, we derive concrete formulas for constructing zestings and establish a systematic approach for cyclic group gradings, providing explicit parameterizations of the zesting data.



1.
Introduction

Tensor categories can be thought as axiomatizations of symmetries extending representation theory.
The profound interplay between tensor categories and
various areas of mathematics and mathematical physics, including low-dimensional topology, quantum field theory, and quantum computation
[
EGNO15
,
BK01
,
Tur94
,
Wan10
]
, has been a driving force behind their development in recent decades.

Hopf algebras provide examples of tensor categories throught the categories of their modules and comodules. Moreover, they often provide the algebraic realization of tensor categories endowed with fiber functors, a connection formalized by Tannakian reconstruction theory
[
DM82
,
Sch92
,
JS91
,
EGNO15
]
.
The study of Hopf algebras was significantly powered by this fact, along with the emergence of quantum groups
[
Dri88
,
Jim85
,
Lus10
,
Jan96
]
.
Aiming to understand their structures, different classification problems of finite-dimensional Hopf algebras were tackled. One of them was the semisimple case, related to what are called fusion categories. A second one corresponds to pointed Hopf algebras, thought of as generalisations of quantum groups.
The latter question led to techniques such as the lifting method
[
AS10
]
.

Within the theory of fusion categories
[
ENO05
]
,
zesting
has emerged as a construction that provides a method to generate new categories by modifying the monoidal structure (and braiding, if applicable) of an existing
G
-graded category using cohomological data
(\gamma,\lambda,\omega)
associated with the grading group
G
,
[
DGP+21
]
. Associative zesting alters the tensor product and associativity constraints, while braided zesting further modifies the braiding to produce new braided tensor categories.

Zesting techniques have been used to construct new examples of modular categories and have provided insights into theories with otherwise indistinguishable modular data
[
DKP21
]
. Although zesting is recognized for its utility as a categorical tool in generating and analyzing these structures, its direct translation into the algebraic framework of general Hopf algebras -extending beyond the semisimple case - motivates further investigation.

In this paper, we develop a framework for zestings of Hopf algebras, directly extending the established zesting techniques from fusion categories to general tensor categories, specifically focusing on categories of comodules
{}^{H}{\!\mathcal{M}}{}
over a Hopf algebra
H
. We show that an associative zesting applied to
{}^{H}{\!\mathcal{M}}{}
naturally gives the category of comodules over a coquasi-Hopf algebra
[
Dri90
,
Maj95
]
, denoted
H^{\lambda}
, whose multiplication comes from appropriately modifying that of
H
. We explicitly describe
H^{\lambda}
by providing formulas for its modified multiplication, which incorporates the 2-cocycle
\lambda
from the zesting data, and its non-trivial associator
\Omega
, determined by the zesting 3-cochain
\omega
and some additional data. Associative zesting is further extended to the braided setting: if
H
is coquasitriangular, we show how braided zesting data endows
H^{\lambda}
with a coquasitriangular coquasi-Hopf algebra structure, with an explicitly modified
r
-form.

As an application of our general theory, we consider the the class of pointed Hopf algebras. Moreover we concentrate on Hopf algebras of the form
H=\mathcal{B}\#{\Bbbk}\Gamma
, where
\mathcal{B}
is a (pre-)Nichols algebra of a Yetter-Drinfeld module
V
over a group
\Gamma

[
And17
]
. This focus is motivated because under standard hypotheses (char
{\Bbbk}=0
,
{\Bbbk}
algebraically closed), any finite-dimensional pointed Hopf algebra over an abelian group is a Hopf 2-cocycle deformation of its associated graded Hopf algebra,
\operatorname{gr}H
. The Hopf algebra

\operatorname{gr}H
is itself isomorphic to a bosonization of the (pre-)Nichols algebra
\mathcal{B}
of its primitive elements by the group algebra of its group-like elements
G(H)\cong\Gamma
, i.e.,
\operatorname{gr}H\cong\mathcal{B}\#{\Bbbk}G(H)

[
Ang13
,
AGI19
]
. Given that 2-cocycle deformations induce equivalences between the respective categories of comodules as tensor categories, understanding the zesting of
{}^{\mathcal{B}\#{\Bbbk}\Gamma}{\!\mathcal{M}}{}
provides insights into the zesting of general finite-dimensional pointed Hopf algebras. For this class, we leverage their universal grading structure, determined by the Hopf cocenter
[
AD96
,
CK16
]
, to derive concrete formulas for constructing associative zesting data. We establish a systematic approach for zesting with respect to cyclic group gradings, providing explicit parameterizations of the zesting data
(\gamma,\lambda,\omega)
. Our constructions are versatile, covering zesting for pointed Hopf algebras built from braided vector spaces of both diagonal and non-diagonal types. To illustrate the computations techniques, we construct new families of coquasi-Hopf algebras through detailed examples of associative zestings. These include zestings of Nichols algebras of super type
A(1|2)
(of diagonal type)
[
AA17
]
and the Fomin-Kirillov algebra in three variables (of non-diagonal type).

The paper is organized as follows. Section
2
provides essential background on tensor categories, Hopf algebras (including coquasi-Hopf and coquasitriangular Hopf algebras), and the categorical zesting construction for
G
-graded tensor categories. In Section
3
, we establish the core of our framework: we discuss the universal grading of Hopf algebras, translate the associative zesting data into Hopf algebraic terms involving relative
r
-forms (related to concepts in
[
AGP14
]
) and group cocycles, and use Tannakian reconstruction to describe explicitly the resulting coquasi-Hopf algebra
H^{\lambda}
. This section also addresses braided zesting. Section
4
focuses on concrete applications, particularly to pointed Hopf algebras. We detail zesting data for pointed Hopf algebras, provide
explicit constructions for associative zesting by cyclic groups and present the aforementioned examples, providing new coquasi-Hopf algebras, together with examples

[
BN19
]
in the braided case.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we establish notation and recall the basic definitions and properties of tensor categories, Hopf algebras, and the zesting construction.

2.1.
Tensor categories and Hopf algebras

Throughout this paper,
{\Bbbk}
denotes a field. All vector spaces, (co)algebras, etc. are over
{\Bbbk}
, and (co)algebra means (co)associative (co)algebra with (co)unity.

We assume familiarity with coalgebras and Hopf algebras
[
Mon93
,
Rad11a
]
. Given a coalgebra
C
, following Sweedler’s notation, we write
\Delta(c)=c_{(1)}\otimes c_{(2)}
for
c\in C
. For iterated coproducts, we write
(\Delta\otimes\operatorname{id})(\Delta(c))=c_{(1)}\otimes c_{(2)}\otimes c_{(3)}
, and so on. By
C^{cop}
we denote the co-opposite coalgebra of
H
, with co-multiplication
\Delta^{cop}(c)=c_{(2)}\otimes c_{(1)}
. We denote by
*
the convolution product in the dual space
C^{*}
, defined by
(f*g)(c)=f(c_{(1)})g(c_{(2)})
for
f,g\in C^{*}
and
c\in C
.

We also assume familiarity with the basic results of the theory of tensor categories
[
EGNO15
]
: We just recall that a tensor category over
{\Bbbk}
is a
{\Bbbk}
-linear abelian category
{\mathcal{C}}
equipped with a bilinear tensor product functor
\otimes:{\mathcal{C}}\times{\mathcal{C}}\to{\mathcal{C}}
, a simple unit object
\mathbf{1}
, and natural isomorphisms for associativity, left and right unit constraints, satisfying coherence conditions and rigidity.

Given a Hopf algebra
H
, the category of finite-dimensional
H
-modules is a tensor category; we will recall above that the category of finite-dimensional

H
-comodules is also a tensor category.

A tensor category is
pointed
if all its simple objects are invertible with respect to the tensor product. Given a tensor category
{\mathcal{C}}
, we denote by
{\mathcal{C}}_{pt}
the maximal pointed subcategory, which is the subcategory generated by all invertible objects of
{\mathcal{C}}
. The set of isomorphism classes of invertible objects in
{\mathcal{C}}
forms a group under the tensor product.

2.1.1.
Coquasi-Hopf algebras

A
coquasi-bialgebra

(H,m,u,\omega,\Delta,\varepsilon)
consists of a coalgebra
(H,\Delta,\varepsilon)
, coalgebra maps
m:H\otimes H\to H
(the multiplication) and
u:{\Bbbk}\to H
(the unit), and a convolution invertible element
\omega\in(H\otimes H\otimes H)^{*}
such that

\displaystyle(h_{(1)}k_{(1)})l_{(1)}\omega(h_{(2)},k_{(2)},l_{(2)})

\displaystyle=\omega(h_{(1)},k_{(1)},l_{(1)})h_{(2)}(k_{(2)}l_{(2)}),

\displaystyle 1_{H}h

\displaystyle=h1_{H}=h,

\displaystyle\omega(h_{(1)}k_{(1)},l_{(1)},t_{(1)})\omega(h_{(2)},k_{(2)},l_{(2)}t_{(2)})

\displaystyle=\omega(h_{(1)},k_{(1)},l_{(1)})

\displaystyle\qquad\times\omega(h_{(2)},k_{(2)}l_{(2)},t_{(1)})\omega(k_{(3)},l_{(3)},t_{(2)}),

\displaystyle\omega(h,1_{H},k)

\displaystyle=\varepsilon(h)\varepsilon(k),

for all
h,k,l,t\in H
.

A
coquasi-Hopf algebra
is a coquasi-bialgebra
H
with a linear map (called antipode)
S:H\to H
and elements
\alpha,\beta\in H^{*}
such that, for all
h\in H
,

\displaystyle S(h_{(1)})\alpha(h_{(2)})h_{(3)}

\displaystyle=\alpha(h)1_{H},

\displaystyle h_{(1)}\beta(h_{(2)})S(h_{(3)})

\displaystyle=\beta(h)1_{H},

\displaystyle\omega(h_{(1)}\beta(h_{(2)}),S(h_{(3)}),\alpha(h_{(4)})h_{(5)})

\displaystyle=\omega^{-1}(S(h_{(1)}),\alpha(h_{(2)})h_{(3)}\beta(h_{(4)}),S(h_{(5)}))=\varepsilon(h).

These structures are direct generalizations of Hopf algebras: In fact, when
\omega
is trivial (that is,
\omega(h,k,l)=\varepsilon(h)\varepsilon(k)\varepsilon(l)
for all
h,k,l\in H
), and
\alpha=\varepsilon=\beta
, a coquasi-Hopf algebra reduces to an ordinary Hopf algebra.

The group of group-like elements of
H
, denoted by
G(H)
, consists of all non-zero elements
g\in H
such that
\Delta(g)=g\otimes g
. Let
g_{1},g_{2}\in G(H)
. An element
h\in H
is called
(g_{1},g_{2})
-
skew-primitive
if
\Delta(h)=h\otimes g_{1}+g_{2}\otimes h
. The set of
(g_{1},g_{2})
-skew-primitive elements is denoted by
P_{g_{1},g_{2}}(H)
.

2.1.2.
Comodule categories over a coquasi-bialgebra

Let
H
be a coquasi-bialgebra. Let
{}^{H}{\!\mathcal{M}}{}
denote the category of left
H
-comodules. For
X\in^{H}{\!\mathcal{M}}{}
, we denote its coaction by
\rho_{X}:X\to H\otimes X
, and write
\rho_{X}(x)=x_{(-1)}\otimes x_{(0)}
using again Sweedler’s notation. We recall now the monoidal structure of the category
{}^{H}{\!\mathcal{M}}{}
. The tensor product of
H
-comodules as vector spaces is the tensor product over
{\Bbbk}
with coaction
\rho_{X\otimes Y}(x\otimes y)=x_{(-1)}y_{(-1)}\otimes x_{(0)}\otimes y_{(0)}
. The associativity constraints are:

\displaystyle a_{X,Y,Z}:(X\otimes Y)\otimes Z

\displaystyle\longrightarrow X\otimes(Y\otimes Z)

\displaystyle x\otimes y\otimes z

\displaystyle\mapsto\omega(x_{(-1)},y_{(-1)},z_{(-1)})x_{(0)}\otimes(y_{(0)}\otimes z_{(0)})

for
x\in X
,
y\in Y
,
z\in Z
and
X,Y,Z\in^{H}{\!\mathcal{M}}{}
. The unit object is
{\Bbbk}
with
\rho_{\Bbbk}(1)=1_{H}\otimes 1
.

By Tannakian reconstruction
[
Sch92
]
, a tensor category
\mathcal{C}
is equivalent to the category of comodules
{}^{H}{\!\mathcal{M}}{}
of some (coquasi)-Hopf algebra
H
if and only if there exists a (quasi)-fiber functor
F:\mathcal{C}\to\text{Vec}_{\Bbbk}
. Here, a (quasi)-fiber functor is an exact, faithful (quasi)tensor functor from
\mathcal{C}
to
\text{Vec}_{\Bbbk}
, where
\text{Vec}_{\Bbbk}
denotes the tensor category of
{\Bbbk}
-vector spaces.

2.1.3.
Braided tensor categories and coquasitriangular Hopf algebras

A
braiding
on a tensor category
{\mathcal{C}}
is a natural isomorphism

\displaystyle c_{X,Y}:

\displaystyle X\otimes Y\to Y\otimes X,

\displaystyle X,Y\in{\mathcal{C}},

satisfying the hexagon axioms. A
braided tensor category
is a pair consisting of a tensor category and a braiding on it.

A
coquasitriangular
coquasi-bialgebra is a coquasi-bialgebra
H
with a convolution invertible linear map
r:H\otimes H\to{\Bbbk}
, called an
r
-form, satisfying:

\displaystyle r(h_{(1)},k_{(1)})h_{(2)}k_{(2)}

\displaystyle=k_{(1)}h_{(1)}r(h_{(2)},k_{(2)}),

\displaystyle r(h_{(1)},k_{(1)}l_{(1)})\omega^{-1}(h_{(2)},k_{(2)},l_{(2)})

\displaystyle=\omega(k_{(1)},l_{(1)},h_{(1)})r(h_{(2)},l_{(2)})

\displaystyle\quad\times\omega^{-1}(k_{(2)},h_{(3)},l_{(3)})r(h_{(4)},k_{(3)}),

\displaystyle r(h_{(1)}k_{(1)},l_{(1)})\omega(h_{(2)},k_{(2)},l_{(2)})

\displaystyle=\omega^{-1}(l_{(1)},k_{(1)},h_{(1)})r(h_{(2)},l_{(2)})

\displaystyle\quad\times\omega(h_{(3)},l_{(3)},k_{(2)})r(k_{(3)},l_{(4)}),

for all
h,k,l\in H
.

Given an
r
-form on
H
, we can define a braiding on
{}^{H}{\!\mathcal{M}}{}
by:

\displaystyle c_{X,Y}:

\displaystyle X\otimes Y\to Y\otimes X,

\displaystyle x\otimes y\mapsto r(x_{(-1)},y_{(-1)})y_{(0)}\otimes x_{(0)}.

2.2.
Zesting of Tensor Categories

Zesting provides a method to construct new tensor categories from existing ones. Originally defined for fusion categories
[
DGP+21
]
, the construction applies to any tensor category faithfully graded over a group. In this subsection, we recall the basic definitions for strict tensor categories (sufficient for our purpose of studying comodules over Hopf algebras) and some relevant properties for our development of Hopf algebraic zesting.

2.2.1.
Relative Centralizers and the Drinfeld Center

Before defining the zesting construction, we need to introduce the concept of relative centralizers.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be a tensor category and
\mathcal{D}\subset{\mathcal{C}}
a tensor subcategory. The
relative centralizer

R_{\mathcal{D}}({\mathcal{C}})
is the tensor subcategory of the Drinfeld center
{\mathcal{Z}}({\mathcal{C}})
whose objects are pairs
(X,\sigma_{X,-})
where
X\in\mathcal{D}
and

\displaystyle\sigma_{X,-}=\{\sigma_{X,Y}:X\otimes Y\to Y\otimes X\}_{Y\in{\mathcal{C}}}

is a family of natural isomorphisms satisfying the half-braiding condition:

\displaystyle\sigma_{X,Y\otimes Z}=(\operatorname{id}_{Y}\otimes\sigma_{X,Z})(\sigma_{X,Y}\otimes\operatorname{id}_{Z})

for all
Y,Z\in{\mathcal{C}}
.

2.2.2.
Associative zesting

With the notion of relative centralizers established, we now define the associative zesting construction for tensor categories.

Let
G
be a group. A tensor category
\mathcal{C}
is
G
-graded if there is a decomposition

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}=\bigoplus_{g\in G}\mathcal{C}_{g}

of
\mathcal{C}
into a direct sum of full abelian subcategories such that the tensor product of
\mathcal{C}
maps
\mathcal{C}_{g}\times\mathcal{C}_{h}
to
\mathcal{C}_{gh}
for all
g,h\in G
. We will say that the
G
-grading is faithful if
\mathcal{C}_{g}\neq 0
for all
g\in G
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
G
be a group and
{\mathcal{C}}=\bigoplus_{g\in G}{\mathcal{C}}_{g}
a faithfully
G
-graded strict tensor category. An
associative
G
-zesting
for
{\mathcal{C}}
consists of a pair
(\gamma,\lambda,\omega)
where:

(1)

\lambda:G\times G\to\mbox{\rm Obj\,}\left([R_{{\mathcal{C}}_{e}}({\mathcal{C}})]_{pt}\right)
is a map such that each
\lambda(g_{1},g_{2})
is an invertible object with
\lambda(g_{1},e)=\lambda(e,g_{1})=\mathbf{1}
for all
g_{1}
.

(2)

For each triple
(g_{1},g_{2},g_{3})\in G^{\times 3}
, an isomorphism

\displaystyle\omega_{g_{1},g_{2},g_{3}}:\lambda(g_{1},g_{2})\otimes\lambda(g_{1}g_{2},g_{3})\to\lambda(g_{2},g_{3})\otimes\lambda(g_{1},g_{2}g_{3})

satisfying
\omega_{g_{1},e,g_{2}}=\operatorname{id}
and the associative zesting condition for all
g_{1},g_{2},g_{3},g_{4}\in G
:

(2.1)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &(\omega_{g_{2},g_{3},g_{4}}\otimes\operatorname{id}_{\lambda(g_{1},g_{2}g_{3}g_{4})})(\operatorname{id}_{\lambda(g_{2},g_{3})}\otimes\omega_{g_{1},g_{2}g_{3},g_{4}})((\omega_{g_{1},g_{2},g_{3}})\otimes\operatorname{id}_{\lambda(g_{1}g_{2}g_{3},g_{4})})\\
&=(\operatorname{id}_{\lambda(g_{3},g_{4})}\otimes\omega_{g_{1},g_{2},g_{3}g_{4}})(\sigma_{\lambda(g_{1},g_{2}),\lambda(g_{3},g_{4})}\otimes\operatorname{id}_{\lambda(g_{1}g_{2},g_{3}g_{4})})(\operatorname{id}_{\lambda(g_{1},g_{2})}\otimes\omega_{g_{1}g_{2},g_{3},g_{4}}).\end{aligned}

where
\sigma
denotes the half-braiding associated to objects in
R_{{\mathcal{C}}_{e}}({\mathcal{C}})_{pt}
.

.

2.1
corresponds exactly with

Given an associative

2.2.3.
Braided zesting

In this subsection, we discuss the braided zesting construction, drawing from
[
DGP+21
]
. We emphasize that this construction is applicable in non-semisimple settings and for tensor categories graded by groups
G
which are not necessarily finite. Before presenting the definition of the braided zesting datum, we introduce some relevant concepts concerning tensor automorphisms of the identity functor and their relation to the grading.

For any tensor category
\mathcal{C}
, let
\text{Aut}_{\otimes}(\text{Id}_{\mathcal{C}})
denote the abelian group consisting of tensor natural
{\Bbbk}
-linear isomorphisms of the identity functor.
Now, assume
\mathcal{C}
is faithfully graded by a group
G
. Let
\widehat{G}=\operatorname{Hom}(G,{\Bbbk}^{*})
be the group of linear characters of
G
. Each character
\gamma\in\widehat{G}
defines a tensor automorphism
\Phi_{\gamma}\in\text{Aut}_{\otimes}(\text{Id}_{\mathcal{C}})
whose component on any object
X_{g}
in the subcategory
\mathcal{C}_{g}
(corresponding to grade
g\in G
) is given by scalar multiplication:

\displaystyle(\Phi_{\gamma})_{X_{g}}:=\gamma(g)\operatorname{id}_{X_{g}},\quad\text{for }X_{g}\in\mbox{\rm Obj\,}(\mathcal{C}_{g}).

The map
\Phi:\gamma\mapsto\Phi_{\gamma}
is an injective group homomorphism from
\widehat{G}
into
\text{Aut}_{\otimes}(\text{Id}_{\mathcal{C}})
, where injectivity relies on the faithful grading. We denote the image of this map—the subgroup of tensor automorphisms induced by characters—by
\text{Aut}^{\widehat{G}}_{\otimes}(\text{Id}_{\mathcal{C}})
. Natural automorphisms in this subgroup act as constant scalars on the graded components.

Now, let
\mathcal{B}
be a braided tensor category with braiding
c
.
An invertible object
U\in\mathcal{B}
will be called
projectively transparent
if there exists a scalar
\lambda_{U}\in{\Bbbk}^{*}
such that for all objects
Y\in\mathcal{B}
,

\displaystyle c_{Y,U}\circ c_{U,Y}=\lambda_{U}\operatorname{id}_{U\otimes Y}.

For any invertible object
U\in\mathcal{B}
, its double braiding also gives rise to a natural isomorphism
\chi_{U}\in\text{Aut}_{\otimes}(\text{Id}_{\mathcal{B}})
. This
\chi_{U}
acts on an object
X\in\mathcal{B}
by the automorphism
\chi_{U}(X)
of
X
, defined via the relation:

\displaystyle\chi_{U}(X)\otimes\operatorname{id}_{U}=c_{U,X}\circ c_{X,U}.

If
\mathcal{B}
is also faithfully graded by an abelian group
G
, we are interested in cases where an invertible object
U
is both projectively transparent and its associated
\chi_{U}
is compatible with the grading. We say that
\chi_{U}
is
character-induced
if it belongs to the subgroup
\text{Aut}^{\widehat{G}}_{\otimes}(\text{Id}_{\mathcal{B}})
. This means there exists a unique character
\gamma_{U}\in\widehat{G}
such that
\chi_{U}=\Phi_{\gamma_{U}}
. Consequently, for any homogeneous object
X_{g}
of grade
g
, the action of
\chi_{U}
is purely scalar, determined by its grade:

\displaystyle\chi_{U}(X_{g})=\gamma_{U}(g)\cdot\operatorname{id}_{X_{g}}.

We adopt a common abuse of notation: sometimes
\chi_{U}(g)
may denote the tensor automorphism component
\gamma_{U}(g)\cdot\operatorname{id}_{X_{g}}
acting on an unspecified object
X_{g}
of grade
g
(without referring exactly to which object the natural isomorphism is acting), and at other times,
\chi_{U}(g)
specifically denotes the scalar value
\gamma_{U}(g)\in{\Bbbk}^{*}
. The intended meaning should be clear from the context.

Consider a braided tensor category
\mathcal{B}
with braiding
c
, and let
\mathcal{D}\subset\mathcal{B}
be any tensor subcategory. For any object
X\in\mathcal{D}
, the natural isomorphism
\sigma_{X,-}:=c_{-,X}^{-1}
provides the structure of an object
(X,\sigma_{X,-})
in the relative centralizer
R_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{B})
. This yields a canonical braided functor from
\mathcal{D}
into
R_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{B})
. We utilize this inherent relative braiding structure in the subsequent definition.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
\mathcal{B}
be a strict braided tensor category faithfully graded by an abelian group
G
. A
braided
G
-zesting datum

(\lambda,\omega,t)
for
\mathcal{B}
consists of:

(1)

An associative
G
-zesting datum
(\gamma,\lambda,\omega)
, such that for every
g_{1},g_{2}\in G
, the automorphism
\chi_{\lambda(g_{1},g_{2})}
is character-induced.

(2)

A family of isomorphisms
t_{g_{1},g_{2}}:\lambda(g_{1},g_{2})\longrightarrow\lambda(g_{2},g_{1})
for all
g_{1},g_{2}\in G
.

3.
Zesting for Hopf Algebras

In the previous section, we reviewed the general categorical framework for zesting of tensor categories. Now, we specialize this construction to the important setting of categories of comodules over Hopf algebras. Our goal is to translate the categorical zesting data into Hopf algebraic terms. We will show that an associative zesting of the category of comodules
{}^{H}{\!\mathcal{M}}{}
leads to a coquasi-Hopf algebra
H^{\lambda}
– whose comodule category is precisely the zested category
{}^{H}{\!\mathcal{M}}{}^{\lambda}
. We will explicitly describe the modified multiplication and the non-trivial associator of
H^{\lambda}
, as well as the structures involved in the braided case.

3.1.
Universal Grading for Hopf Algebras

The zesting construction fundamentally relies on equipping the tensor category with a group grading. For fusion categories, there exists a distinguished group, the
universal grading group
, which governs all possible faithful gradings of the category,
[
GN08
]
. Importantly, the notion of
U(H)
also exists for the tensor category
{}^{H}{\!\mathcal{M}}{}
, where
H
is any Hopf algebra, without requiring semisimplicity or finite-dimensionality. Its universality stems from the property that any faithful grading of
{}^{H}{\!\mathcal{M}}{}
by a group
G
corresponds to a unique surjective group homomorphism
U(H)\twoheadrightarrow G
. In this subsection we recall the construction of universal grading group in the Hopf algebraic setting.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
H,K
be Hopf algebras over
{\Bbbk}
. A Hopf algebra map
\pi:H\to K
is called
cocentral
if

(3.1)

\displaystyle(\mathrm{id}\otimes\pi)\circ\Delta=(\mathrm{id}\otimes\pi)\circ\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}.

The
Hopf cocenter
of
H
, denoted
\mathcal{HC}(H)
, is the maximal cocentral quotient. That is,
\mathcal{HC}(H)
is characterized by the following universal property:
\mathcal{HC}(H)
is a quotient Hopf algebra via a cocentral epimorphism
p:H\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{HC}(H)
such that any other cocentral Hopf morphism
\pi:H\to K
factors uniquely through
p
.

Every Hopf algebra
H
admits a unique Hopf cocenter: The existence and uniqueness of
\mathcal{HC}(H)
were established in
[
AD96
]
.

The structure of the Hopf cocenter is closely related to the universal grading group of the category
{}^{H}{\!\mathcal{M}}{}
. Following
[
CK16
, §3]
:

Definition 3.2
.

Let
H
be a Hopf algebra.

(1)

The
universal grading group
of
H
, denoted
U(H)
, is the group presented by generators
g_{X}
for each isomorphism class of simple left
H
-comodules
X
, subject to the relations:

•

g_{X}=g_{Y}
if
X\cong Y
;

•

g_{{\Bbbk}}=e
(the identity element);

•

g_{X}g_{Y}=g_{Z}
if
Z
is a simple subquotient of the tensor product
X\otimes Y
.

(2)

The
group cocenter map
associated with
U(H)
is the Hopf algebra epimorphism
\pi_{U(H)}:H\twoheadrightarrow{\Bbbk}U(H)
corresponding to the functor
{}^{H}{\!\mathcal{M}}{}\to^{{\Bbbk}U(H)}{\!\mathcal{M}}{}
that essentially maps each simple comodule
X
to the grade
g_{X}\in U(H)
.

The universal property of the Hopf cocenter implies that there is a Hopf algebra epimorphism
\mathcal{HC}(H)\twoheadrightarrow{\Bbbk}U(H)
. Moreover, the group-like elements capture the grading group structure, as stablished in
[
CK16
, Proposition 3.10]
:

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
H
be a Hopf algebra. There exists a group isomorphism between the group of group-like elements
G(\mathcal{HC}(H))
of the Hopf cocenter
\mathcal{HC}(H)
and the universal grading group
U(H)
. ∎

Let
p:H\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{HC}(H)
be the canonical projection.
Using Proposition
3.3

we can determine explicitly the components on the grading
U(H)\cong G(\mathcal{HC}(H))
for any left
H
-comodule
X
: if
\rho:X\to H\otimes X
denotes the coaction, for each
g\in U(H)
the
g
-graded component
X_{g}
is:

\displaystyle X_{g}:=\{x\in X\mid(p\otimes\mathrm{id})(\rho(x))=g\otimes x\}.

Then
X
decomposes as a direct sum of its graded components:
X=\bigoplus_{g\in U(H)}X_{g}
. This decomposition
yields a
U(H)
-grading on the tensor category
{}^{H}{\!\mathcal{M}}{}
:

{}^{H}{\!\mathcal{M}}{}=\bigoplus_{g\in G}(^{H}{\!\mathcal{M}}{})_{g},

where
(^{H}{\!\mathcal{M}}{})_{g}
is the full abelian subcategory of
H
-comodules
X
such that
X=X_{g}
.

Hence, given any faithful grading of
{}^{H}{\!\mathcal{M}}{}
by a group
G
, the universality of
U(H)
implies the existence of a unique surjective group homomorphism
U(H)\twoheadrightarrow G
. This group homomorphism corresponds precisely to a surjective cocentral Hopf epimorphism
\pi:H\to{\Bbbk}G
that induces the
G
-grading on
{}^{H}{\!\mathcal{M}}{}
. Furthermore, the neutral component
(^{H}{\!\mathcal{M}}{})_{e}
of this
G
-grading is the category of left comodules over the Hopf subalgebra of coinvariants,
H^{\mathrm{co}\pi}
.

3.2.
Associative Zesting for Hopf Algebras

Having established possible group grading on
{}^{H}{\!\mathcal{M}}{}
in terms of the universal grading group
U(H)
, we now translate the categorical associative zesting data
(\gamma,\lambda,\omega)
from Subsection
2.2.2
into concrete Hopf algebraic terms. This involves identifying the algebraic counterparts of the zesting objects
\lambda(g_{1},g_{2})
and the associator isomorphisms
\omega
. The key structure connecting the categorical notion of centrality (objects in
R_{(^{H}{\!\mathcal{M}}{})_{e}}(^{H}{\!\mathcal{M}}{})
) to Hopf algebra maps is the relative
r
-form, which we define first.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
H
be a Hopf algebra and
K
a Hopf subalgebra of
H
. A
relative
r
-form
for the inclusion
K\subset H
is a linear map
r:H\otimes K\to{\Bbbk}
satisfying the following conditions for all
h,h^{\prime}\in H
and
k,k^{\prime}\in K
:

(3.2)

\displaystyle r(hh^{\prime},k)

\displaystyle=r(h^{\prime},k_{(1)})r(h,k_{(2)}),

(3.3)

\displaystyle r(h,kk^{\prime})

\displaystyle=r(h_{(1)},k)r(h_{(2)},k^{\prime}),

(3.4)

\displaystyle r(h,1)

\displaystyle=\epsilon(h),\,\,\,r(1,k)=\epsilon(k),

(3.5)

\displaystyle r(h_{(1)},k_{(1)})k_{(2)}h_{(2)}

\displaystyle=h_{(1)}k_{(1)}r(h_{(2)},k_{(2)})

As the following result shows, relative
r
-forms precisely capture the data of braided monoidal functors from
{}^{K}{\!\mathcal{M}}{}
into the Drinfeld center
\mathcal{Z}(^{H}{\!\mathcal{M}}{})
compatible (or equivalently, the relative centralizer
R_{{}^{K}{\!\mathcal{M}}{}}(^{H}{\!\mathcal{M}}{})
) compatible with the forgetful functor.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
H
be a Hopf algebra and
K
a Hopf subalgebra with a coquasitriangular structure
s
. The following sets of data are equivalent:

(1)

A relative
r
-form
r:H\otimes K\to{\Bbbk}
such that
r^{op}|_{K\otimes K}=s
.

(2)

A braided monoidal functor

\displaystyle F:\ ^{K}{\!\mathcal{M}}{}\;\longrightarrow\;\mathcal{Z}\left({}^{H}{\!\mathcal{M}}{}\right)\;=\;{}_{H}^{H}\!\mathcal{YD}

whose composition with the forgetful functor

\mathcal{Z}\left({}^{H}{\!\mathcal{M}}{}\right)\to^{H}{\!\mathcal{M}}{}
is a fully faithful inclusion.

(3)

A Hopf algebra map
\gamma:K\to(H^{\circ})^{\mathrm{cop}}
such that

(3.6)

\displaystyle\langle\gamma(k_{(1)}),h_{(1)}\rangle\,k_{(2)}\,h_{(2)}

\displaystyle=h_{(1)}\,k_{(1)}\,\langle\gamma(k_{(2)}),h_{(2)}\rangle,

\displaystyle\text{for all }k\in K,\,h\in H.

(3.7)

\displaystyle\langle\gamma(k),k^{\prime}\rangle

\displaystyle=s(k,k^{\prime})

\displaystyle\text{for all }k,k^{\prime}\in K.

Here
H^{\circ}
denotes the finite dual of

, see
[
Mon93
]
for details.

Proof.

We prove implications cyclically. For
(1)

\Rightarrow

(2)
, let
Y\in^{K}{\!\mathcal{M}}{}
. For each
X\in^{H}{\!\mathcal{M}}{}
we define a natural isomorphism

\displaystyle c_{X,Y}

\displaystyle:X\otimes Y\,\longrightarrow\,Y\otimes X,

\displaystyle c_{X,Y}(x\otimes y)

\displaystyle:=r(x_{(-1)},\,y_{(-1)})\,y_{(0)}\otimes x_{(0)}.

Its inverse is given by

\displaystyle\overline{c}_{X,Y}

\displaystyle:Y\otimes X\,\longrightarrow\,X\otimes Y,

\displaystyle\overline{c}_{Y,X}(y\otimes x)

\displaystyle:=r\bigl(x_{(-1)},\,S(y_{(-1)})\bigr)\,x_{(0)}\otimes y_{(0)}.

We get then a functor

\displaystyle F

\displaystyle:^{K}{\!\mathcal{M}}{}\;\longrightarrow\;\mathcal{Z}(^{H}{\!\mathcal{M}}{}),

\displaystyle F(Y)

\displaystyle=\bigl(Y,\;c_{-,Y}\bigr),

which is a tensor functor whose composition with the usual forgetful functor gives a fully faithful inclusion into
{}^{H}{\!\mathcal{M}}{}
.

Since
(K,s)
is coquasitriangular,
{}^{K}{\!\mathcal{M}}{}
carries a natural braided tensor structure. As

,

is a braided monoidal functor.

(2)

\Rightarrow

(3)

Every
H
-comodule is a colimit of its finite-dimensional subcomodules. Therefore, the image of the monoidal functor

lies in the subcategory
\underline{{}_{H}\mathcal{M}}
of (possibly infinite) colimits of finite-dimensional

\displaystyle\Gamma:^{K}{\!\mathcal{M}}{}\;\longrightarrow\;{}_{H}^{H}\!\mathcal{YD}\;\longrightarrow\;^{(H^{*})^{op}}{\!\mathcal{M}}{},

which commutes with the corresponding forgetful functors in the following diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{}^{K}\!\mathcal{M}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{}^{(H^{*})^{\mathrm{op}}}{\!\mathcal{M}}{}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Vec}_{{\Bbbk}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Gamma}

By Tannakian duality (see

\displaystyle h\,\cdot\,x

\displaystyle=\bigl\langle\gamma(x_{(-1)}),\,h\bigr\rangle\,x_{(0)},

\displaystyle h\in H,

\displaystyle\,x\in X,

recovers the monoidal structure. It remains to check the compatibility conditions: (

Using the Yetter–Drinfeld condition and again that

\displaystyle h_{(1)}\,x_{(-2)}\,\langle\gamma(x_{(-1)}),h_{(2)}\rangle\otimes x_{(0)}

\displaystyle=h_{(1)}x_{(-2)}\,\otimes\,\bigl\langle\gamma(x_{(-1)}),h_{(2)}\bigr\rangle\,x_{(0)}

\displaystyle=h_{(1)}x_{(-1)}\,\otimes\,h_{(2)}\,\cdot\,x_{(0)}

\displaystyle=(h_{(1)}\cdot x_{(-1)})\,h_{(2)}\,\otimes\,h_{3}\cdot x_{(0)}

\displaystyle=x_{(-1)}\,h_{(2)}\,\otimes\,\bigl\langle\gamma(x_{(-2)}),h_{(1)}\bigr\rangle\,x_{(0)}

\displaystyle=x_{(-1)}\,h_{(2)}\,\bigl\langle\gamma(x_{(-2)}),h_{(1)}\bigr\rangle\otimes x_{(0)}.

That is,
h_{(1)}\,x_{(-2)}\,\bigl\langle\gamma(x_{(-1)}),h_{(2)}\bigr\rangle\,\otimes\,x_{(0)}=x_{(-1)}\,h_{(2)}\,\bigl\langle\gamma(x_{(-2)}),\,h_{(1)}\bigr\rangle\,\otimes\,x_{(0)}

(3)

\Rightarrow

Finally, given a Hopf algebra map

We can now define the Hopf algebraic version of an associative zesting datum, tailored for the category
{}^{H}{\!\mathcal{M}}{}
with an arbitrary faithful

will correspond to group-like elements in

(i.e., elements of

Definition 3.6
.

Let
H
be a Hopf algebra, let

be a surjective cocentral Hopf homomorphism, and let

be a subgroup of

for

is a triple

consisting of:

(1)

A group homomorphism

such that:

(3.8)

\displaystyle\langle\gamma(g),h_{(1)}\rangle\,gh_{(2)}g^{-1}

\displaystyle=h_{(1)}\,\langle\gamma(g),h_{(2)}\rangle\quad\text{for all }g\in\Gamma_{0},\,h\in H.

Via Thm.

.

(2)

A normalized 2-cocycle
\lambda:G\times G\to\Gamma_{0}
.

(3)

A normalized 3-cochain

satisfying the compatibility condition:

(3.9)

\displaystyle\langle\gamma(\lambda(g_{1},g_{2})),\lambda(g_{3},g_{4})\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{\omega(g_{1},g_{2},g_{3})\omega(g_{1},g_{2}g_{3},g_{4})\omega(g_{2},g_{3},g_{4})}{\omega(g_{1}g_{2},g_{3},g_{4})\omega(g_{1},g_{2},g_{3}g_{4})}

for all

. Here

denotes the evaluation of the algebra map

on the element

.

Here,
{\operatorname{Alg}}(H,{\Bbbk})
(the set of algebra homomorphisms from

) forms a group under the convolution product. The inverse of an element

is the antipode of

Definition
3.6
provides the Hopf algebraic data
(\gamma,\lambda,\omega)
corresponding to the categorical data

described in Subsection

. Given this datum, the general construction yields the zested tensor category, which we denote
{}^{H}{\!\mathcal{M}}{}^{(\gamma,\lambda,\omega)}
.

3.2.1.
Tannakian reconstruction of
H^{\lambda}

The zested category
{}^{H}{\!\mathcal{M}}{}^{(\gamma,\lambda,\omega)}
has the same underlying abelian category as
{}^{H}{\!\mathcal{M}}{}
, and its modified tensor product still maps
{}^{H}{\!\mathcal{M}}{}\times\ ^{H}{\!\mathcal{M}}{}\to\ ^{H}{\!\mathcal{M}}{}
. It is natural to ask if this new tensor category is the category of comodules of some new algebraic object derived from
H
. The Tannakian reconstruction principle provides the answer.

If
C
is a coalgebra such that the category
{}^{C}{\!\mathcal{M}}{}
is endowed with a tensor product
\otimes^{\prime}
and associator
\alpha^{\prime}
making it a tensor category (possibly non-strict), and the forgetful functor
(^{C}{\!\mathcal{M}}{},\otimes^{\prime})\to\text{Vec}_{\Bbbk}
is tensor (quasi-monoidal), then
C
naturally inherits a (coquasi-)bialgebra structure. Specifically, the multiplication
m^{\prime}
and associator
\Omega^{\prime}
(as an element in
(C^{\otimes 3})^{*}
) are defined by:

\displaystyle m^{\prime}(a\otimes b)

\displaystyle=(a\otimes^{\prime}b)_{(-1)}\varepsilon\bigl((a\otimes^{\prime}b)_{(0)}\bigr),

\displaystyle\Omega^{\prime}(a\otimes b\otimes c)

\displaystyle=\varepsilon\bigl(\mathbf{a}_{C,C,C}(a\otimes b\otimes c)\bigr),

where
a,b,c\in C
and
\varepsilon
is the counit. The category of comodules over
(C,m^{\prime},\Omega^{\prime})
is tensor equivalent to
(^{C}{\!\mathcal{M}}{},\otimes^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime})
.

In our case, the zested category
(^{H}{\!\mathcal{M}}{}^{(\gamma,\lambda,\omega)},\otimes^{\lambda},a^{\lambda})
satisfies the conditions for reconstruction. The forgetful functor remains the tensor (it is essentially the identity on the underlying vector spaces). Therefore, the coalgebra
H
inherits a new structure, yielding a coquasi-bialgebra denoted
H^{\lambda}
. Its explicit structure is given by the following theorem.

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
H
be a Hopf algebra,
G
a group,
H=\bigoplus H_{g}
a
G
-grading and
(\gamma,\lambda,\omega)
an associative
G
-zesting datum for
H
. Then
H
can be endowed with q structure of coquasi-bialgebra
H^{\lambda}
such that:

•

The coalgebra structure of
H^{\lambda}
is the same as that of
H
.

•

The multiplication
m^{\lambda}:H^{\lambda}\otimes H^{\lambda}\to H^{\lambda}
is given by

(3.10)

\displaystyle m^{\lambda}(h\otimes k)

\displaystyle=hk\,\lambda(g_{1},g_{2}),

\displaystyle h\in H_{g_{1}},

\displaystyle k\in H_{g_{2}}.

•

The associator
\Omega\in(H^{\otimes 3})^{*}
is given by

(3.11)

\displaystyle\Omega(h\otimes k\otimes l)

\displaystyle=\varepsilon(hk)\,\omega(g_{1},g_{2},g_{3})\,\langle\gamma(\lambda(g_{1},g_{2})),l\rangle,

\displaystyle h\in H_{g_{1}},k\in H_{g_{2}},l\in H_{g_{3}}.

Furthermore, the category of left
H^{\lambda}
-comodules is tensor equivalent to the zested category:
{}^{H^{\lambda}}{\!\mathcal{M}}{}\simeq\ ^{H}{\!\mathcal{M}}{}^{(\gamma,\lambda,\omega)}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{C}=\ ^{H}{\!\mathcal{M}}{}
and consider the zested category
\mathcal{C}^{(\gamma,\lambda,\omega)}
. The tensor product of
X\in\mathcal{C}_{g_{1}},Y\in\mathcal{C}_{g_{2}}
is
X\otimes^{\lambda}Y=X\otimes Y\otimes\lambda(g_{1},g_{2})
. To apply reconstruction, we view this vector space simply as
X\otimes Y
via the canonical linear isomorphism
X\otimes Y\otimes{\Bbbk}g\cong X\otimes Y
, where
g=\lambda(g_{1},g_{2})
, but with a modified coaction
\rho^{\lambda}
:

\displaystyle\rho^{\lambda}(x\otimes y)

\displaystyle=(x_{(-1)}y_{(-1)})\lambda(g_{1},g_{2})\otimes(x_{(0)}\otimes y_{(0)}).

Applying the Tannakian reconstruction formula for multiplication we get (
3.10
):

\displaystyle m^{\lambda}(h\otimes k)

\displaystyle=(h\otimes^{\lambda}k)_{(-1)}\varepsilon((h\otimes^{\lambda}k)_{(0)})

\displaystyle=(h_{(-1)}k_{(-1)})\lambda(g_{1},g_{2})\varepsilon(h_{(0)}\otimes k_{(0)})

\displaystyle=(h_{(1)}k_{(1)})\lambda(g_{1},g_{2})\varepsilon(h_{(2)}k_{(2)})=hk\lambda(g_{1},g_{2}).

For the associator
\Omega
, we evaluate the categorical associator
a^{\lambda}
from Figure
1
on elements
h\in H_{g_{1}},k\in H_{g_{2}},l\in H_{g_{3}}
and apply the counit
\varepsilon
. Recall
a^{\lambda}
involves composition with
\mathrm{id}\otimes\sigma_{\lambda(g_{1},g_{2}),H_{g_{3}}}
and
\mathrm{id}\otimes\omega_{g_{1},g_{2},g_{3}}
. By Theorem
3.5
, the half-braiding
\sigma_{\lambda,l}
acts as
l^{\prime}\otimes l\mapsto l_{(0)}\otimes l^{\prime}\langle\gamma(l^{\prime}),l_{(-1)}\rangle
. Applying
\varepsilon\circ a^{\lambda}
on
h\otimes k\otimes l
:

\displaystyle\Omega(h\otimes k\otimes l)

\displaystyle=\varepsilon(a^{\lambda}(h\otimes k\otimes l))

\displaystyle=\varepsilon(hk)\,\omega(g_{1},g_{2},g_{3})\,\langle\gamma(\lambda(g_{1},g_{2})),l\rangle,

so (
3.11
) holds.
∎

3.3.
Braided Zesting for Hopf Algebras

Finally, we consider braided zesting in the Hopf algebra context. This requires starting with a Hopf algebra
H
whose category of comodules
{}^{H}{\!\mathcal{M}}{}
is already braided.

Recall that
{}^{H}{\!\mathcal{M}}{}
is braided if and only if
H
is
coquasitriangular
, see
[
Mon93
,
Rad11b
]
for details. This is equivalent to the existence of a convolution-invertible linear map
r:H\otimes H\to{\Bbbk}
(the universal
r
-form) satisfying certain axioms, or equivalently, the existence of a Hopf algebra map
f:H\to(H^{\circ})^{\mathrm{cop}}
(compatible with the opposite coproduct on the target) such that

\displaystyle\langle f(k_{(1)}),h_{(1)}\rangle\,k_{(2)}h_{(2)}

\displaystyle=h_{(1)}k_{(1)}\,\langle f(k_{(2)}),h_{(2)}\rangle,\quad\forall h,k\in H.

The
r
-form is recovered via
r(h,k)=\langle f(k),h\rangle
. The braiding on
{}^{H}{\!\mathcal{M}}{}
is then given by
c_{X,Y}(x\otimes y)=r(x_{(-1)},y_{(-1)})y_{(0)}\otimes x_{(0)}
.
The alternative braiding
c^{\prime}_{X,Y}:=c_{Y,X}^{-1}
on
{}^{H}{\!\mathcal{M}}{}
corresponds to the
r
-form
r^{\prime}(h,k):=r^{-1}(k,h)
for all
h,k\in H
, where
r^{-1}:H\otimes H\to{\Bbbk}
is the convolution inverse of
r
.
For the subsequent discussion, we highlight the algebra map associated with
r^{\prime}
. This map, denoted
f^{\prime}:H\to(H^{\circ})^{\mathrm{cop}}
, is defined by the relation

\langle f^{\prime}(k),h\rangle=r^{\prime}(h,k)=r^{-1}(k,h)\quad\text{for all }h,k\in H.

The map
f^{\prime}
is important for Definition
3.9
.

The following definition characterizes group-like elements
g\in G(H)
in a coquasitriangular Hopf algebra
H
for which the associated natural isomorphism
\chi_{g}
is character-induced with respect to a
G
-grading (where the grading is induced by a cocentral epimorphism
\pi:H\to\Bbbk[G]
).

Definition 3.8
.

Let
(H,r)
be a coquasitriangular Hopf algebra and let
\pi:H\to\Bbbk[G]
be a cocentral Hopf epimorphism. A group-like element
g\in G(H)
is called
\pi
-character-inducing
if there exists a group character
\lambda_{g}:G\to\Bbbk^{*}
such that

\displaystyle r(x_{(1)},g)r(g,x_{(2)})=\lambda_{g}(s)\epsilon(x).

for every
s\in G
and for every
x\in H_{s}:=\{h\in H\mid(\pi\otimes\operatorname{id}_{H})\Delta(h)=s\otimes h\}
.

We now define the data needed for braided zesting, building upon the associative datum
(\gamma,\lambda,\omega)
where
f^{\prime}|_{G(H^{co\pi})}=\gamma
.

Definition 3.9
.

Let
(H,f)
be a coquasitriangular Hopf algebra and
\pi:H\rightarrow{\Bbbk}[G]
a surjective cocentral Hopf homomorphism with
G
an abelian group. A
braided
G
-zesting

(\lambda,\gamma,\omega,t)
corresponds to:

(1)

An associative
G
-zesting
(\lambda,\gamma,\omega)
such that:

•

f^{\prime}|_{G(H^{co\pi})}=\gamma
,

•

\lambda(g_{1},g_{2})
is
\pi
-character-induced,

•

The 2-cocycle is symmetric, i.e.,
\lambda(g_{1},g_{2})=\lambda(g_{2},g_{1})
for all
g_{1},g_{2}\in G
.

(2)

A 2-cochain
t\in C^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{*})
satisfying the following conditions:

(3.12)

\displaystyle\frac{\omega(g_{1},g_{2},g_{3})\omega(g_{2},g_{3},g_{1})}{\omega(g_{2},g_{1},g_{3})}

\displaystyle=\frac{t(g_{1},g_{2})t(g_{1},g_{3})}{t(g_{1},g_{2}g_{3})},

(3.13)

\displaystyle r(\lambda(g_{1},g_{2}),g_{3})r(g_{3},\lambda(g_{1},g_{2}))

\displaystyle=\frac{\omega(g_{1},g_{2},g_{3})\omega(g_{3},g_{1},g_{2})}{\omega(g_{1},g_{3},g_{2})}\frac{t(g_{1},g_{3})t(g_{2},g_{3})}{t(g_{1}g_{2},g_{3})},

for all
g_{1},g_{2},g_{3}\in G
.

Just as associative zesting led to the coquasi-Hopf algebra
H^{\lambda}
, incorporating the braided zesting datum

endows

with a braided structure.

Corollary 3.10
.

Given a braided
G
-zesting datum
(\gamma,\lambda,\omega,t)
for a coquasitriangular Hopf algebra
(H,r)
, the resulting coquasi-Hopf algebra

from Theorem

is also braided coquasitriangular. Its universal
r
-form

is obtained by twisting

with the cochain

; explicitly,

\displaystyle r^{\lambda}(x,y)

\displaystyle=t(g_{1},g_{2})r(x,y),

\displaystyle x\in H^{\lambda}_{g_{1}},

\displaystyle y\in H^{\lambda}_{g_{2}}.

Consequently, the category
{}^{H^{\lambda}}{\!\mathcal{M}}{}
, with the braiding induced by

, is braided tensor equivalent to the zested category obtained from

.∎

4.
Computing Examples: Zesting Pointed Hopf Algebras

In this section, we apply zesting construction to the important class of pointed Hopf algebras. Next we develop explicit formulas for associative zestings when the grading group is cyclic. Combining these two constructions we provide concrete examples of zestings for pointed Hopf algebras graded by cyclic groups.

4.1.
Pointed Hopf algebras

Recall that a Hopf algebra
H
is
pointed
if all its simple comodules are one-dimensional. Equivalently, its coradical
H_{0}
is the group algebra
{\Bbbk}G(H)
of its group-like elements
G(H)
. The coradical filtration

\displaystyle H_{0}\subset H_{1}\subset H_{2}\subset\cdots,

where

\displaystyle H_{n}=\{h\in H\mid\Delta(h)\in H_{0}\otimes H+H\otimes H_{n-1}\},

is a filtration by Hopf subalgebras. The associated graded object
\mbox{\rm gr\,}H=\bigoplus\limits_{n\geq 0}H_{n}/H_{n-1}
is canonically a (coradically) graded Hopf algebra.

Assume that
{\Bbbk}
is algebraically closed of characteristic zero. If
H
is finite-dimensional and
G(H)
is abelian, then
H
is generated by group-like and skew-primitive elements
[
Ang13
]
. Furthermore,
\mbox{\rm gr\,}H
takes the specific form of a bosonization

\displaystyle\mbox{\rm gr\,}H

\displaystyle\cong\mathcal{B}(V)\#{\Bbbk}\Gamma,

where

\displaystyle\Gamma=G(H).

Moreover,
V=H_{1}/H_{0}
is the space of primitive elements of
\mbox{\rm gr\,}H
, viewed as a Yetter-Drinfeld module over
\Gamma
, and
\mathcal{B}(V)
is the Nichols algebra of
V
. Nichols algebras are graded Hopf algebras in the braided fusion category
{}^{{\Bbbk}\Gamma}_{{\Bbbk}\Gamma}\mathcal{YD}
of Yetter-Drinfeld modules, defined as quotients of the tensor algebra
T(V)
by a specific maximal homogeneous ideal, see
[
And17
]
for a precise definition. Crucially, under these assumptions,
H
is known to be a cocycle deformation of
\mbox{\rm gr\,}H

[
AGI19
]
, implying their categories of comodules are tensor equivalent:
{}^{H}{\!\mathcal{M}}{}\simeq_{\otimes}\ ^{\mbox{\rm gr\,}H}{\!\mathcal{M}}{}
.

This relationship holds for all known examples even when
G(H)
is not abelian; that is, any finite-dimensional pointed Hopf algebra
H
is a cocycle deformation of
\mbox{\rm gr\,}H\cong\mathcal{B}(V)\#{\Bbbk}G(H)
. Hence, understanding zesting for the bosonized form
\mathcal{B}(V)\#{\Bbbk}G(H)
is sufficient for all known examples of finite dimensional pointed Hopf algebras.

4.2.
Associative Zestings for Pointed Hopf Algebras

Our first goal is to determine associative zesting data for
{}^{H}{\!\mathcal{M}}{}
where
H
is a finite-dimensional pointed Hopf algebra. Based on the discussion above, it suffices to analyze Hopf algebras of the form
H=\mathcal{B}(V)\#{\Bbbk}\Gamma
, where
\Gamma
is a finite group,
V\in{}_{\Gamma}^{\Gamma}\mathcal{YD}
is a Yetter-Drinfeld module such that the Nichols algebra
\mathcal{B}(V)
is finite-dimensional.

We will work in a slightly more general context: We fix a Hopf algebra
H=\mathcal{B}\#{\Bbbk}\Gamma
, not necessarily finite-dimensional, where
\Gamma
is a group and
\mathcal{B}
is a pre-Nichols algebra of
V\in^{{\Bbbk}\Gamma}_{{\Bbbk}\Gamma}\mathcal{YD}
with
\dim V<\infty
, that is, an intermediate graded Hopf algebra between
\mathcal{B}(V)
and
T(V)
in the braided tensor category
{}^{{\Bbbk}\Gamma}_{{\Bbbk}\Gamma}\mathcal{YD}
.

As
\mathcal{B}
is a quotient of the tensor algebra
T(V)
,
\mathcal{B}
is generated by
V
as an algebra. We may fix a basis
(x_{i})_{1\leq i\leq\theta}
of
V
such that
x_{i}
has coaction given by some
g_{i}\in\Gamma
, so
\Delta(x_{i})=x_{i}\otimes 1+g_{i}\otimes x_{i}
in
H
. Thus
H
is a graded Hopf algebra, generated as an algebra by the group-like elements
g\in\Gamma
(in degree zero) and the skew-primitive elements
(x_{i})_{1\leq i\leq\theta}
(in degree one). The subgroup
\langle g_{i}\rangle
of
\Gamma
generated by
g_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq\theta
, is called the
support
of
V
and denoted by

{\operatorname{supp}}(V)
.
The Yetter-Drinfeld compatibility condition says that
{\operatorname{supp}}(V)
is a normal subgroup of
\Gamma
.

For this class of Hopf algebras, the universal grading group has a simple description.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
H=\mathcal{B}\#{\Bbbk}\Gamma
be constructed as above. Then the universal grading group is isomorphic to the quotient group:

U(H)\cong\Gamma/{\operatorname{supp}}(V).

Proof.

Let
\varpi:H\twoheadrightarrow{\Bbbk}\tfrac{\Gamma}{{\operatorname{supp}}(V)}
be the composition of the projection
H\twoheadrightarrow{\Bbbk}\Gamma
onto the degree one component with the canonical projection
{\Bbbk}\Gamma\twoheadrightarrow{\Bbbk}\tfrac{\Gamma}{{\operatorname{supp}}(V)}
. Then
\varpi
is a Hopf algebra map since it is the composition of two of them. Notice that

\displaystyle(\operatorname{id}\otimes\varpi)\circ\Delta(g)

\displaystyle=g\otimes\varpi(g)=(\operatorname{id}\otimes\varpi)\circ\Delta^{op}(g)

\displaystyle\text{for all }g\in\Gamma

\displaystyle(\operatorname{id}\otimes\varpi)\circ\Delta(x_{i})

\displaystyle=x_{i}\otimes 1=(\operatorname{id}\otimes\varpi)\circ\Delta^{op}(x_{i})

\displaystyle\text{for all }i=1,\cdots,\theta.

As
x_{i}
,
g
generate
H
as an algebra and
\Delta
and
\varpi
are algebra maps,

\displaystyle(\operatorname{id}\otimes\varpi)\circ\Delta(x)

\displaystyle=(\operatorname{id}\otimes\varpi)\circ\Delta^{op}(x)

\displaystyle\text{for all }x\in H;

that is,
\varpi
is cocentral.

Let
K
be a Hopf algebra and
\pi:H\to K
a cocentral Hopf morphism. Applying (
3.1
) to each
x_{i}\in H
, we have:

x_{i}\otimes 1+g_{i}\otimes\pi(x_{i})=1\otimes\pi(x_{i})+x_{i}\otimes\pi(g_{i}).

Hence,
\pi(g_{i})=1
and
\pi(x_{i})=0
for all
1\leq i\leq\theta
. Thus, the map
\pi
factors throught
\varpi
. By the universality of the Hopf cocenter,
\mathcal{HC}\simeq\Bbbk\tfrac{\Gamma}{{\operatorname{supp}}(V)}
. Hence,
U(H)\simeq\tfrac{\Gamma}{{\operatorname{supp}}(V)}
, by Proposition
3.3
.
∎

Now fix
H
as above and
\pi:H\to{\Bbbk}G
a cocentral Hopf epimorphism.
From the proof of Proposition
4.1
, we have that
G(H^{co\pi})={\operatorname{supp}}(V)
and the restriction of
\pi
to
H_{0}={\Bbbk}\Gamma
gives a group epimorphism
\Gamma\twoheadrightarrow G
, also called
\pi
.

The next result will allow to find explicit computations for associative zestings:

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
H=\mathcal{B}\#{\Bbbk}\Gamma
be constructed as above, and let
V={\Bbbk}\{x_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq\theta}
be the associated
\Gamma
-Yetter-Drinfeld module.

If
\Gamma_{0}
is a subgroup of
\Gamma
such that the Hopf subalgebra
{\Bbbk}\Gamma_{0}\subseteq H
admits a relative
r
-form, then
\Gamma_{0}
must be a central subgroup of
\Gamma
and must satisfy
\Gamma_{0}\subseteq{\operatorname{supp}}(V)
.

Moreover, if
\Gamma_{0}
is a central subgroup of
\Gamma
such that
\Gamma_{0}\subseteq{\operatorname{supp}}(V)
, then there is a bijective correspondence between:

(i)

Relative
r
-forms
r:H\otimes{\Bbbk}\Gamma_{0}\to{\Bbbk}
.

(ii)

Group homomorphisms
\Phi:\Gamma_{0}\to\widehat{\Gamma}
such that

(4.1)

\displaystyle g\cdot x_{i}

\displaystyle=\langle g_{i},\Phi(g)\rangle x_{i}

\displaystyle\text{for all }g\in\Gamma_{0}\text{ and }1\leq i\leq\theta.

Proof.

It is analogous to
[
AGP14
, Proposition 4.3]
, using Theorem
3.5
.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

Condition (
4.1
) can be rewritten only in terms of the multiplication in the algebra
H=\mathcal{B}\#{\Bbbk}\Gamma
. The Yetter-Drinfeld action of an element
g\in\Gamma
on
x_{i}\in V
is given by
g\cdot x_{i}=gx_{i}g^{-1}
(where the product
gx_{i}g^{-1}
is taken in
H
). Thus, for
g\in\Gamma_{0}
, the condition becomes

\displaystyle gx_{i}g^{-1}

\displaystyle=\langle g_{i},\Phi(g)\rangle x_{i}

\displaystyle\text{for all }g\in\Gamma_{0}\text{ and }1\leq i\leq\theta.

The elements
x_{i}
are skew-primitive in
H
.

We now specialize the associative Hopf zesting to pointed Hopf algebras of the form
H=\mathcal{B}\#{\Bbbk}\Gamma
. Incorporating the specific results for their universal grading group
U(H)\cong\Gamma/{\operatorname{supp}}(V)
and the characterization of relative
r
-form (via Proposition
4.2
), the following theorem details the necessary zesting data
(\gamma,\lambda,\omega)
and explicitly describes the resulting coquasi-Hopf algebra structure
H^{\lambda}
.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
H=\mathcal{B}\#{\Bbbk}\Gamma
be a pointed Hopf algebra. An associative
G
-zesting datum
(\gamma,\lambda,\omega)
for
H
is determined by the following choices:

(1)

A grading group
G
chosen via a surjective group homomorphism

\displaystyle\iota\colon\Gamma/{\operatorname{supp}}(V)\to G.

(2)

A pair
(\Gamma_{0},\Phi)
, where
\Gamma_{0}\leq\ker(\iota)
and

\Phi:\Gamma_{0}\to\widehat{\Gamma}
satisfies (
4.1
), thus determining relative
r
-form via the map
\gamma\colon\Gamma_{0}\to{\operatorname{Alg}}(H,{\Bbbk})
.

(3)

A normalized 2-cocycle
\lambda\colon G\times G\to\Gamma_{0}
.

(4)

A normalized 3-cochain
\omega\colon G\times G\times G\to{\Bbbk}^{\times}
satisfying (
3.9
).

Given such a valid datum
(\gamma,\lambda,\omega)
, the resulting coquasi-Hopf algebra
H^{\lambda}
shares the same underlying coalgebra structure as
H
. Its multiplication
m^{\lambda}
and associator
\Omega
are given by:

(4.2)

\displaystyle m^{\lambda}((x\#g)\otimes(y\#h))

\displaystyle=x(g\cdot y)\#gh\lambda(\iota(g),\iota(h)),

(4.3)

\displaystyle\Omega(x\#g,y\#h,z\#k)

\displaystyle=\varepsilon(x)\varepsilon(y)\varepsilon(z)\,\omega(\iota(g),\iota(h),\iota(k))\,\langle\gamma(\lambda(\iota(g),\iota(h))),k\rangle,

where
x,y,z\in\mathcal{B}
;
g,h,k\in\Gamma
;
\varepsilon
is the counit; and the arguments of
\lambda
and
\omega
are the images in
G
via
\iota
.

4.3.
Associative zestings of Hopf algebras by cyclic groups

We will construct explicit associative zestings when the grading group is cyclic. This construction leverages standard representatives for the second cohomology group
H^{2}(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z},\Gamma_{0})
and specific cochains whose properties simplify the verification of the zesting conditions.

4.3.1.
Background on specific cyclic cochains.

Let
G=\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}
. We identify the elements of
G
with
\{0,1,\dots,N-1\}
. Let
M
be an abelian group (written multiplicatively). Consider the following cochains associated with
\nu\in M
:

(4.4)

\displaystyle\beta_{\nu}(i)

\displaystyle=i\nu

(4.5)

\displaystyle\lambda^{(\nu)}(i,j)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }i+j<N\\
\nu&\text{if }i+j\geq N\end{cases}\quad

\displaystyle(\nu\in M,i,j\in G)

(4.6)

\displaystyle\omega^{(\nu)}(i,j,k)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }i+j<N\\
\nu^{k}&\text{if }i+j\geq N\end{cases}\quad

\displaystyle(\nu\in M,i,j,k\in G)

(4.7)

\displaystyle\theta^{(\nu)}(i,j,k,l)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\nu&\text{if }i+j\geq N\text{ and }k+l\geq N\\
1&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}\quad

\displaystyle(\nu\in M,i,j,k,l\in G)

Let
\delta
be the standard group cohomology differential (trivial action). From
[
DGP+21
]
we recall some key relations between these cochains:

(4.8)

\displaystyle\delta(\beta_{\nu})

\displaystyle=\lambda^{(N\nu)}

(4.9)

\displaystyle\delta(\lambda^{(\nu)})

\displaystyle=0

(4.10)

\displaystyle\delta(\omega^{(\nu)})

\displaystyle=\theta^{(N\nu)}

From (
4.9
),
\lambda^{(\nu)}\in Z^{2}(G,M)
and from (
4.8
),
\lambda^{(N\nu)}\in B^{2}(G,M)
).
Let
M_{N}:=\{m\in M\mid m^{N}=1\}
. Given
\nu\in M_{N}
, (
4.10
) says that
\omega^{(\nu)}\in Z^{3}(G,M)
.

It is well known that these cochains represent the cohomology groups via the induced homomorphisms:

(4.11)

\displaystyle\nu+NM\mapsto[\lambda^{(\nu)}]:M/NM\xrightarrow{\cong}H^{2}(G,M),\quad\nu\mapsto[\omega^{(\nu)}]:M_{N}\xrightarrow{\cong}H^{3}(G,M).

4.3.2.
Associative cyclic zesting.

The preceding discussion on cyclic cochains allows us to construct a concrete associative zesting when
G=\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}
. We use the standard 2-cocycle
\lambda^{(\nu)}
for
\lambda
. The following proposition identifies the corresponding 3-cochain
\omega^{(q)}
that satisfies the condition (
3.9
).

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
H
be a Hopf algebra and let
\pi:H\to{\Bbbk}\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}
be a surjective cocentral Hopf epimorphism. We fix the following data:

(1)

an abelian subgroup
\Gamma_{0}\leq G(H^{\mathrm{co}\pi})
,

(2)

a group homomorphism
\gamma:\Gamma_{0}\to{\operatorname{Alg}}(H,{\Bbbk})
satisfying (
3.8
),

(3)

an element
\nu\in\Gamma_{0}
, and define the scalar
m=\langle\gamma(\nu),\nu\rangle\in{\Bbbk}^{*}
.

For any
q\in{\Bbbk}^{*}
such that
q^{N}=m
, the triple
(\gamma,\lambda^{(\nu)},\omega^{(q)})
forms an associative
G
-zesting datum for
H
, where
\lambda^{(\nu)}
and
\omega^{(q)}
are the standard cyclic cochains defined in (
4.5
) and (
4.6
) respectively.

Proof.

We verify the condition (
3.9
).
The left hand side of (
3.9
) is
\langle\gamma(\lambda(i,j)),\lambda(k,l)\rangle
. Substituting
\lambda=\lambda^{(\nu)}
yields
m=\langle\gamma(\nu),\nu\rangle
if
i+j\geq N
and
k+l\geq N
, and 1 otherwise. Thus,
\langle\gamma(\lambda(i,j)),\lambda(k,l)\rangle=\theta_{m}(i,j,k,l)
. The right hand side is
\delta(\omega^{(q))}
. Using the relation
\delta(\omega^{(q)})=\theta_{q^{N}}
, we get

\displaystyle\langle\gamma(\lambda(i,j)),\lambda(k,l)\rangle=\theta_{m}(i,j,k,l)=\theta_{q^{N}}(i,j,k,l)=\delta(\omega^{(q)})(i,j,k,l),

that is exactly (
3.9
).
∎

Remark 4.6
.

To apply Proposition
4.5
, one selects an element
\nu\in\Gamma_{0}
. Since the cohomology class
[\lambda^{(\nu)}]\in H^{2}(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z},\Gamma_{0})
depends only on the coset

(where

), due to the isomorphism

, it suffices to consider a set of representatives

for this quotient group. Once
\nu
is fixed, one calculates

. As
{\Bbbk}
is algebraically closed of characteristic zero, the equation

always possesses
N
distinct solutions
q_{0},q_{1},\dots,q_{N-1}
in
{\Bbbk}^{*}
. Each of these roots

defines a 3-cochain

and consequently yields

(\gamma,\lambda^{(\nu)},\omega^{(q_{k})})
associated with the chosen representative
\nu
.

4.4.
Concrete examples of zesting of pointed Hopf algebras

Before presenting concrete examples, let us outline the process for computing associative zesting data
(\gamma,\lambda,\omega)
in the pointed case
H=\mathcal{B}\#{\Bbbk}\Gamma
, based on the preceding results. We emphasize that constructing the zesting datum itself depends only on the group
\Gamma
and the structure of the Yetter-Drinfeld module
V
associated with the skew-primitive generators
(x_{i})_{1\leq i\leq\theta}
. Specifically, the module
V
determines its support
{\operatorname{supp}}(V)
(and thus the universal grading group
G=\Gamma/{\operatorname{supp}}(V)
) and the action of potential subgroups
\Gamma_{0}\leq{\operatorname{supp}}(V)
(needed to check the condition for
\gamma
).

Determining the appropriate
(\gamma,\lambda,\omega)
and verifying the condition (
3.9
) does not require knowing a presentation (i.e., the defining relations) of the pre-Nichols algebra
\mathcal{B}
. Therefore, in the following examples, we will focus only on realizing the vector space
V
as a Yetter-Drinfeld module over a group
\Gamma
and computing the zestig data.

4.4.1.
Braidings of diagonal type and their realization

Many important Nichols algebras, particularly those relevant to the classification of pointed Hopf algebras over abelian groups, arise from braided vector spaces
(V,c)
of
diagonal type
.

A braided vector space
(V,c)
is of
diagonal type
if there exists a basis
(x_{i})_{i\in I}
for
V
and a matrix
{\mathfrak{q}}=(q_{ij})_{i,j\in I}
of scalars
q_{ij}\in{\Bbbk}^{\times}
such that the braiding
c:V\otimes V\to V\otimes V
on this basis is given by

(4.12)

\displaystyle c(x_{i}\otimes x_{j})

\displaystyle=q_{ij}\,x_{j}\otimes x_{i}

for all

\displaystyle i,j\in I.

As the structure is determined by the
braiding matrix

{\mathfrak{q}}
, the vector space is sometimes denoted by
V_{{\mathfrak{q}}}
and the associated Nichols algebra by
\mathcal{B}_{{\mathfrak{q}}}
.

Braided vector spaces
V_{{\mathfrak{q}}}
can be realized as Yetter-Drinfeld modules
{}^{{\Bbbk}\Gamma}_{{\Bbbk}\Gamma}\mathcal{YD}
over an abelian group
\Gamma
. As the braiding in
{}^{{\Bbbk}\Gamma}_{{\Bbbk}\Gamma}\mathcal{YD}
is given by
c_{V,W}(v\otimes w)=(v_{(-1)}\cdot w)\otimes v_{(0)}
, to realize
V_{{\mathfrak{q}}}
as an object in
{}^{{\Bbbk}\Gamma}_{{\Bbbk}\Gamma}\mathcal{YD}
, we need to find:

(1)

Group elements
(g_{i})_{i\in I}
in
\Gamma
,

(2)

Characters
(\chi_{j})_{j\in I}
in
\widehat{\Gamma}=\operatorname{Hom}(\Gamma,{\Bbbk}^{\times})
,

such that if we define the
{\Bbbk}\Gamma
-coaction by
\rho(x_{i})=g_{i}\otimes x_{i}
and the
{\Bbbk}\Gamma
-action by
h\cdot x_{j}=\chi_{j}(h)x_{j}
, the resulting Yetter-Drinfeld braiding
c(x_{i}\otimes x_{j})=\chi_{j}(g_{i})x_{j}\otimes x_{i}
matches the desired braiding (
4.12
). This requires the condition:

(4.13)

\displaystyle q_{ij}=\chi_{j}(g_{i})\quad\text{for all }i,j\in I.

4.4.2.
Associative zestings for
{\mathfrak{q}}
of type
A(1|2)

Let
n>1
be an integer and let
q\in{\Bbbk}^{\times}
be a primitive
n
-th root of unity. We consider the pointed Hopf algebra
H=\mathcal{B}\#{\Bbbk}\Gamma
, where
\mathcal{B}
is a pre-Nichols algebra of super type
A(1|2)

[
AA17
]
and
\Gamma=C_{2}\times C_{n^{2}}=\langle\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\rangle
. The 2-dimensional Yetter-Drinfeld module
V={\Bbbk}x_{1}\oplus{\Bbbk}x_{2}
has braiding matrix
{\mathfrak{q}}=\begin{pmatrix}-1&1\\
q^{-1}&q\end{pmatrix}
, realized via
g_{1}=\alpha_{1}
,

We now turn to braided zestings for pointed Hopf algebras. This requires starting with a finite-dimensional coquasitriangular pointed Hopf algebra

]

and

•

.

An element

is

\displaystyle r(x_{(1)},g)r(g,x_{(2)})

\displaystyle=\lambda_{g}(s)\epsilon(x)

\displaystyle r(x_{(1)},g)r(g,x_{(2)})

On semisubtractive ideals of semirings
Amartya Goswami

Abstract
. Our aim in this paper is to explore semisubtractive ideals of semirings. We prove that they form a complete modular lattice. We introduce Golan closures and prove some of their basic properties. We explore the relations between Q Q -ideals and semisubtractive ideals of semirings, and also study them in s s -local semirings. We introduce two subclasses of semisubtractive ideals: s s -strongly irreducible and s s -irreducible, and provide various representation theorems. By endowing a topology on the set of semisubtractive ideals, we prove that the space is T 0 T_{0} , sober, connected, and quasi-compact. We also briefly study continuous maps between semisubtractive spaces. We construct s s -congruences and prove a bijection between these congruences and semisubtractive ideals.

1.
Introduction

Among others, the lack of subtractivity in semirings leads to three types of ideals, namely, strongly subtractive, subtractive, and semisubtractive. Since all these types of ideals coincide with ideals in rings, they are primarily of interest in semirings. As shown in
[
15
]
, the following strict inclusions hold between these ideals:

strongly subtractive
\subset
subtractive
\subset
semisubtractive.

Among these, subtractive ideals, introduced as
k
-ideals in
[
19
]
, have received the most attention (see
[

,
24
,
26
,
30

14
,
28

11

5

6

). From the above inclusions and extensive studies, we may ask how much of the theory of subtractive ideals can be generalized to semisubtractive ideals. It is worth noting that, beyond being a genuine extension of subtractive ideals, semisubtractive ideals are of interest in their own right due to their distinguished properties. For instance, prime ideals (and hence maximal ideals) of semirings are semisubtractive, and semisubtractive ideals are closed under products and sums.

Not only do semisubtractive ideals encompass a host of natural examples while generalizing many important classes of ideals in semirings, but the involvement of additive inverses in their definition also makes the theory closely resemble the theory of ideals in rings. The goal of this paper is to convince this fact to the reader.

We briefly describe the content of the paper. After declaring the underlying assumptions on semirings and recalling the bare minimum definitions in §

Remark 2.3
.

It is easy to see that the ideal

For every semiring

It is shown in

Proposition 2.5

Every proper semisubtractive ideal of a semiring is contained in a maximal semisubtractive ideal.

st+a+e=q_{0}+a+f=1+f\in\mathfrak{j}.

Since
\mathfrak{j}
is a semisubtractive ideal,
-f\in\mathfrak{j}
, and hence,
1=-f+(1+f)\in\mathfrak{j}
, implying that
\mathfrak{j}=S
, as required.

To prove (
4
), observe that by
[
16
, Lemma 2]
, every
Q
-ideal is subtractive, and hence, by Proposition
2.2
(
1
) is semisubtractive. Moreover, by Proposition
2.2
(
4
),
\mathfrak{i}+\mathfrak{j}
is semisubtractive, and hence,
(\mathfrak{i}+\mathfrak{j})/\mathfrak{i}
is a semisubtractive ideal of
S/\mathfrak{i}
by (
1
).

Finally, to show one half of (
5
), suppose that
\mathfrak{p}/\mathfrak{i}
is a prime ideal of
S/\mathfrak{i}
. Let
a
,
b\in S
such that
ab\in\mathfrak{p}
. Then there are elements
q_{1},q_{2}\in Q
such that
a\in q_{1}+\mathfrak{i}
and
b\in q_{2}+\mathfrak{i}
, so
a=q_{1}+c
and
b=q_{2}+d
for some
c,d\in\mathfrak{i}
.
Since

ab=q_{1}q_{2}+q_{1}d+cq_{2}+cd\in\mathfrak{p}

and
\mathfrak{p}
is a semisubtractive ideal of
S
, we must have
-q_{1}d
,
-cq_{2}
,
-cd\in\mathfrak{p}
, and hence,
q_{1}q_{2}\in\mathfrak{p}
.
Let

be the unique element in
Q
such that

(q_{1}+\mathfrak{i})\odot(q_{2}+\mathfrak{i})=q+\mathfrak{i},

where
q_{1}q_{2}+\mathfrak{i}\subseteq q+\mathfrak{i}
. Thus,
q+e=q_{1}q_{2}+f
for some
e,f\in\mathfrak{i}
.
Since
\mathfrak{p}
is a semisubtractive ideal of
S
, and
\mathfrak{i}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}
, we have
-e\in\mathfrak{p}
. Hence, we get
q\in Q\cap\mathfrak{p}
and
q+\mathfrak{i}\in\mathfrak{p}/\mathfrak{i}
. Hence
q_{1}+\mathfrak{i}\in\mathfrak{p}/\mathfrak{i}
or
q_{2}+\mathfrak{i}\in\mathfrak{p}/\mathfrak{i}
, which implies that
q_{1}\in\mathfrak{p}
(so
a\in\mathfrak{p}
) or
q_{2}\in\mathfrak{p}
(so

.
∎

2.3.
s
-local semirings

By Proposition
2.5
, it is guaranteed that maximal semisubtractive ideals exist. We say a semiring
S
is
s
-local
if
S
has a unique maximal semisubtractive ideal. Recall that a non-zero element
x
of
S
is called

if there exists
s
,
t\in S
such that
1+sx=tx
. All the results in this section are generalizations of
[
7
]
. We start with a basic fact for semi-units.

Lemma 2.10
.

Suppose
\mathfrak{a}
is a semisubtractive ideal of a semiring
S
. If
x
is a semi-unit of
S
and if
x\in\mathfrak{a}
, then
\mathfrak{a}=S
.

Proof.

Since
x
is a semi-unit, there exists
s
,
t\in S
such that
1+sx=tx
. Since
x\in\mathfrak{a}
and
\mathfrak{a}
is an ideal, we have
tx
,
sx\in\mathfrak{a}
. Since
\mathfrak{a}
is semisubtractive, we also have
-sx\in\mathfrak{a}
. Therefore,
1=-sx+(1+sx)\in\mathfrak{a}
implying that
\mathfrak{a}=S
.
∎

Next wish to give a characterization of
s
-local semirings in terms of semisubtractive ideals, but first, a lemma.

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
S
be a semiring and let

. Then

is a semi-unit of

if and
only if
x
lies outside each maximal semisubtractive ideal of
S
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.10
,
x
is a semi-unit of
S
if and only if
S=\cz(\langle x\rangle)
. First, suppose that
x
is a semi-unit of
S
and let
x\in\mathfrak{m}
for some maximal semisubtractive ideal
\mathfrak{m}
of
S
.
Then we have
\langle x\rangle\subseteq\mathfrak{m}\subset S
, so that
x
can not be a semi-unit of
S
.
Conversely, if
x
is not a semi-unit of
S
, then
1+rx=sx
holds for no

s\in S
.
Hence,

yields that
\cz(\langle x\rangle)
is a proper semisubtractive ideal of

by Proposition
2.7
(

). By

2.5
,
\cz(\langle x\rangle)\subseteq\mathfrak{j}
for some maximal semisubtractive ideal
\mathfrak{j}
of

; but this would
contradict that

lies outside each maximal semisubtractive ideal of

.
∎

Theorem 2.12
.

A semiring

is
s
-local if and only if the set
of non-semi-unit elements of

forms a semisubtractive ideal.

Proof.

Suppose

is an
s
-local semiring with unique maximal semisubtractive ideal
\mathfrak{m}
. By Lemma

2.11
,
\mathfrak{m}
is precisely the set of non-semi-unit elements of

. For the converse, let us assume that
the set of non-semi-units of

is a semisubtractive ideal
\mathfrak{a}
of
S
. Then
\mathfrak{m}\neq S
since
1
is a semi-unit of

S
. Since

is not trivial, by Proposition
2.5
,
S
has a maximal semisubtractive ideal, say
\mathfrak{j}
. By

2.11
,

consists of non-semi-units of

\mathfrak{m}=\mathfrak{j}
. This proves the uniqeness.
∎

Let
S
be a semiring, and let

be the set of all multiplicatively cancellable

. Following the construction of localization of rings, we can obtain a semiring

, the localization of

at

. If
\mathfrak{a}
is an ideal of

, we shall denote its extension in

by

.
The next lemma tells preservation of semisubtractive ideals under localizations, and its proof is trivial.

Lemma 2.13
.

If
\mathfrak{a}
is a semisubtractive ideal of a semiring
S
, then so is
\mathfrak{a}S_{X}
of
S_{X}
.

Proposition 2.14
.

Let
S
be an
s
-local semiring with unique maximal semisubtractive ideal
\mathfrak{m}
such that
S\cap\mathfrak{m}=\emptyset
. Then
S_{X}
is an
s
-local semiring with unique maximal semisubtractive ideal
\mathfrak{m}S_{X}
.

Proof.

To obtain the desired claim, thanks to Lemma
2.13
and Theorem
2.12
, it is sufficient to show that
\mathfrak{m}S_{X}
is exactly the set of non-semi-units of
S_{X}
. Suppose
z\in S_{X}\setminus\mathfrak{m}S_{X}
. Then
z=\frac{a}{s}
for some
a\in S
and
s\in X
. We must have
a\notin\mathfrak{m}
, so
1+xa=ya
, for some
x,

y\in S
by Lemma
2.11
. It then follows from the equation

\frac{1}{1}+\left(\frac{xs}{1}\right)\left(\frac{a}{s}\right)=\left(\frac{ys}{1}\right)\left(\frac{a}{s}\right)

that
\frac{a}{s}
is a semi-unit of

.
On the other hand, if
y
is a semi-unit of

, then

, for some

and

, and there exist

and

such that

\frac{1}{1}+\left(\frac{b}{t}\right)\left(\frac{c}{u}\right)=\left(\frac{b}{t}\right)\left(\frac{d}{w}\right).

It follows that

t^{2}uw+bctw=tubc.

From this, we claim that

. Otherwise, since

is semisubtractive, we would get

a contradiction by Lemma

∎

2.4.
Subclasses of
\ids

We shall now introduce two subclasses of semisubtractive ideals and study some of their properties. Let
S
be a semiring.
A semisubtractive ideal
\mathfrak{a}
of
S
is called
s
-irreducible
if
\mathfrak{b}\cap\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}=\mathfrak{a}
implies that either
\mathfrak{b}=\mathfrak{a}
or
\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}=\mathfrak{a}
for all
\mathfrak{b},

\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}\in\ids
.
A semisubtractive ideal
\mathfrak{a}
of
S
is called
s
-strongly irreducible
if
\mathfrak{b}\cap\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathfrak{a}
implies that either
\mathfrak{b}\subseteq\mathfrak{a}
or
\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathfrak{a}
for all
\mathfrak{b},

\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}\in\ids
.

Our next result tells that whenever we want to study semisubtractive ideals that are also irreducible (or strongly irreducible), it is sufficient to study
s
-irreducible (or
s
-strongly irreducible) ideals.

Theorem 2.15
.

An ideal
\mathfrak{c}
of a semiring
S
is
s
-irreducible (
s
-strongly irreducible) if and only if
\mathfrak{c}
is irreducible (strongly irreducible) as well as a semisubtractive ideal of
S
.

Proof.

We give a proof for
s
-strongly irreducible ideals, that for
s
-irreducible ideals requiring only a
trivial change of terminology.
Suppose that
\mathfrak{c}
is a
s
-strongly irreducible ideal and
\mathfrak{a}
,
\mathfrak{b}
are ideals of
S
such that
\mathfrak{a}\cap\mathfrak{b}\subseteq\mathfrak{c}.
This implies

\cz(\mathfrak{a})\cap\cz(\mathfrak{b})=\cz(\mathfrak{a}\cap\mathfrak{b})\subseteq\cz(\mathfrak{c})=\mathfrak{c},

where, the first equality follows from Proposition
2.7
(
7
) and the inclusion from Proposition
2.7
(
5
). By hypothesis, this implies that either
\cz(\mathfrak{a})\subseteq\mathfrak{c}
or
\cz(\mathfrak{b})\subseteq\mathfrak{c}
. Since by Proposition
2.7
(
1
),
\mathfrak{a}\subseteq\cz(\mathfrak{a})
and
\mathfrak{b}\subseteq\cz(\mathfrak{b})
for all
\mathfrak{a}
,
\mathfrak{b}\in\id,
we have the desired claim. The converse claim is obvious.
∎

Likewise (commutative) rings, it is known (see
[
15
, Proposition 7.33]
) that a strongly irreducible ideal
\mathfrak{c}
of a (commutative) semiring
S
has the following equivalent ‘elementwise’ property: If

,
y\in S
satisfy
\langle x\rangle\cap\langle y\rangle\subseteq\mathfrak{c}
, then either
x\in\mathfrak{c}
or
y\in\mathfrak{c}
. The next proposition shows that a similar result holds for
s
-strongly irreducible ideals.

Proposition 2.16
.

An ideal
\mathfrak{c}
of a semiring
S
is
s
-strongly irreducible if and only if for all
x
,
y\in S
satisfy
\cz(\langle x\rangle)\bigcap\cz(\langle y\rangle)\subseteq\mathfrak{c}
implies either
x\in\mathfrak{c}
or
y\in\mathfrak{c}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\mathfrak{c}
is a
s
-strongly irreducible ideal of
S
and
\langle x\rangle\cap\langle y\rangle\subseteq\mathfrak{c}
for all
x,

y\in S.
This implies

\cz(\langle x\rangle\cap\langle y\rangle)=\cz(\langle x\rangle)\cap\cz(\langle y\rangle)\subseteq\cz(\mathfrak{c})=\mathfrak{c}

where the first equality follows from Proposition
2.7
(
7
).
Since
\mathfrak{c}
is
s
-strongly irreducible, we must have either
x\in\langle x\rangle\subseteq\cz(\langle x\rangle)\subseteq\mathfrak{c}
or
y\in\langle y\rangle\subseteq\cz(\langle y\rangle)\subseteq\mathfrak{c}
.

To show the converse, suppose that
\mathfrak{c}
is a semisubtractive ideal which is not
s
-strongly irreducible. Then there are semisubtractive ideal
\mathfrak{a}
and
\mathfrak{b}
satisfy
\mathfrak{a}\cap\mathfrak{b}\subseteq\mathfrak{c},
but
\mathfrak{a}\nsubseteq\mathfrak{c}
and
\mathfrak{b}\nsubseteq\mathfrak{c}.
This implies there exist

and

such that
\cz(\langle x\rangle)\cap\cz(\langle y\rangle)\subseteq\mathfrak{c}
, but neither
x\notin\mathfrak{c}
nor

, a contradiction.
∎

The rest of the results in this section are extensions of the corresponding results on strongly irreducible ideals in rings from
[
4
]
.
We shall now see that every proper semisubtractive ideal can be expressed in terms of
s
-irreducible ideals. But first, a lemma.

Lemma 2.17
.

Suppose
S
is a semiring. Let
0\neq x\in S
and
\mathfrak{c}
be a proper semisubtractive ideal of
S
such that
x\notin\mathfrak{c}.
Then there exists a
s
-irreducible ideal
\mathfrak{b}
of
S
such that
\mathfrak{c}\subseteq\mathfrak{b}

and
x\notin\mathfrak{b}
.

Proof.

Let

\{\mathfrak{b}_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
be a chain of semisubtractive ideal of
S
such that
x\notin\mathfrak{b}_{\lambda}\supseteq\mathfrak{c}
for all
\lambda\in\Lambda
. Then

x\notin\bigcup_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\mathfrak{b}_{\lambda}\supseteq\mathfrak{c}.

By Zorn’s lemma, there exists a maximal element
\mathfrak{b}
of this chain. Suppose that
\mathfrak{b}=\mathfrak{b}_{1}\cap\mathfrak{b}_{2}.
By the maximality condition of
\mathfrak{b}
, we must have
x\in\mathfrak{b}_{1}
and
x\in\mathfrak{b}_{2},
and hence
x\in\mathfrak{b}_{1}\cap\mathfrak{b}_{2}=\mathfrak{b},
a contradiction. Therefore,
\mathfrak{b}
is the required
s
-irreducible ideal.
∎

Proposition 2.18
.

If
\mathfrak{c}
is a proper subtractive ideal of a semiring
S
, then
\mathfrak{c}=\bigcap_{\mathfrak{c}\subseteq\mathfrak{b}}\mathfrak{b}
, where
\mathfrak{b}
is a
s
-irreducible ideal of
S
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.17
, there exists a
s
-irreducible ideal
\mathfrak{b}
of
S
such that
\mathfrak{c}\subseteq\mathfrak{b}
. Suppose that

\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}=\bigcap_{\mathfrak{c}\subseteq\mathfrak{b}}\{\mathfrak{b}\mid\mathfrak{b}\;\text{is $s$-irreducible}\}.

Then
\mathfrak{c}\subseteq\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}
. We claim that
\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}=\mathfrak{c}.
If
\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}\neq\mathfrak{c}
, then there exists an
x\in\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}\setminus\mathfrak{c}
, and by Lemma
2.17
, there exists a
s
-irreducible ideal
\mathfrak{b}^{\prime\prime}
such that
x\notin\mathfrak{b}^{\prime\prime}\supseteq\mathfrak{c},
a contradiction.
∎

For a weakly Noetherian semiring, we even have a stronger result.

Proposition 2.19
.

Every semisubtractive ideal of a weakly Noetherian semiring

can be represented as an intersection of a finite number of
s
-irreducible ideals of
S
.

Proof.

Suppose

\mathcal{F}:=\left\{\mathfrak{a}\in\ids\mid\mathfrak{a}\neq\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}\mathfrak{c}_{i},\;\mathfrak{c}_{i}\;\text{is $s$-irreducible}\right\}.

It is sufficient to show that
\mathcal{F}=\emptyset
.
Since
S
is weakly Noetherian,
\mathcal{F}
has a maximal element
\mathfrak{c}
. Since
\mathfrak{c}\in\mathcal{F}
, it is not a finite
intersection of
s
-irreducible ideals of
R
. This implies that
\mathfrak{c}
is not
s
-irreducible. Hence, there are semisubtractive ideal
\mathfrak{s}
and
\mathfrak{t}
such that
\mathfrak{s}\supsetneq\mathfrak{c}
,
\mathfrak{t}\supsetneq\mathfrak{c}
, and
\mathfrak{c}=\mathfrak{s}\cap\mathfrak{t}.
Since
\mathfrak{c}
is
a maximal element of
\mathcal{F}
, we must have
\mathfrak{s},

\mathfrak{t}\notin\mathcal{F}.
Therefore,
\mathfrak{s}
and
\mathfrak{t}
are a finite intersection of

-irreducible ideals of

, which subsequently implies that
\mathfrak{c}
is also a finite intersection of
s
-irreducible

, a contradiction.
∎

In Proposition
2.5
, we have seen that every proper semisubtractive ideal is contained in a maximal ideal of this type. The next result shows a similar containment, but with a minimal

-strongly irreducible ideal.

Proposition 2.20
.

Every proper semisubtractive ideal of a semiring is contained in a minimal
s
-strongly irreducible ideal.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{a}
be a proper semisubtractive ideal of a semiring

. Consider the chain
(\mathcal{E},\subseteq)
, where

\mathcal{E}:=\{\mathfrak{b}\mid\mathfrak{a}\subseteq\mathfrak{b},\;\mathfrak{b}\;\text{is $s$-strongly irreducible}\}.

We claim that
\mathcal{E}\neq\emptyset
. Indeed: every proper semisubtractive ideal is contained in a maximal ideal

, which is prime, and hence, a strongly irreducible ideal. However, by Proposition

(

\mathfrak{m}
is also semisubtractive. By Theorem

,
\mathfrak{m}
is

\mathfrak{m}\in\mathcal{E}

The next result characterizes semirings in which

-irreducibility and

-strongly irreducibility coincide.

Theorem 2.21
.

In a arithmetic semiring

, a semisubtractive ideal of a semiring

-strongly irreducible. Conversely,

s
-irreducible ideal of a semiring

is

Proof.

By

Corollary 2.22
.

In an arithmetic semiring, every semisubtractive ideal is the intersection of the
s
-strongly irreducible ideals that contain it.

2.5.
Semisubtractive spaces

From Proposition
2.7
, it is clear that the sets of the form:

\h(\mathfrak{a}):=\{\mathfrak{b}\in\ids\mid\mathfrak{b}\supseteq\mathfrak{a}\}\qquad(\mathfrak{a}\in\ids)

are not closed under finite unions, and hence,
\h
is not a Kuratowski closure operator. However, the collection
\{\h(\mathfrak{a})\}_{\mathfrak{a}\in\ids}
of subsets of
\ids
as subbasic closed sets induce a topology on
\ids
, which we call a
semisubtractive topology
, denote by
\tau_{s}
. The set
\ids
endowed with a semisubtractive topology, will be called a
semisubtractive space
, and instead of
(\ids,\tau_{s})
, we shall simply denote the space by
\ids
. Note that our semisubtractive topology is a slightly modified version of the ideal topology (also known as the coarse lower topology) introduced in
[
12
]
.

We now study some separation properties of semisubtractive spaces. Since the specialization order of a semisubtractive space forms a partial order, we derive the following separation axiom.

Proposition 2.23
.

Every semisubtractive space is
T_{{\scriptscriptstyle 0}}.

Next, we aim to look into the sobriety of semisubtractive spaces. Recall that a nonempty closed subset
D
of a topological space is
irreducible
if
D\neq D_{1}\cup D_{2}
for any two proper closed subsets
D_{1}
and
D_{2}
of
D
. A point
x
in a closed subset
D
is called a
generic point
of
D
if
D=\overline{\{x\}},
and a topological space is called
sober
if every nonempty irreducible closed set has a unique generic point. To obtain the sobriety of a semisubtractive space, we use the following lemma.

Lemma 2.24
.

Every nonempty subbasic closed set of a semisubtractive space is irreducible.

Proof.

To obtain the desired claim, observe that it is sufficient to prove:
\h(\mathfrak{a})=\overline{\{\mathfrak{a}\}}
for all
\mathfrak{a}\in\ids.
Since
\overline{\{\mathfrak{a}\}}
is the smallest closed set containing
\mathfrak{a}
, it follows that
\h(\mathfrak{a})\supseteq\overline{\{\mathfrak{a}\}}
. For the other inclusion, first consider the trivial case of
\overline{\{\mathfrak{a}\}}=\ids.
For this we have

\ids=\overline{\{\mathfrak{a}\}}\subseteq\h(\mathfrak{a})\subseteq\ids,

and hence
\h(\mathfrak{a})\subseteq\overline{\{\mathfrak{a}\}}.
Now suppose that
\overline{\{\mathfrak{a}\}}\neq\ids
and

\overline{\{\mathfrak{a}\}}=\bigcap_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{n_{\lambda}}\h(\mathfrak{a}_{i\lambda})\right).

This means that
\mathfrak{a}\in\bigcup_{i=1}^{n_{\lambda}}\h(\mathfrak{a}_{i\lambda})
for some
i
and each
\lambda\in\Lambda
. But from this, we obtain that

\h(\mathfrak{a})\subseteq\bigcap_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{n_{\lambda}}\h(\mathfrak{a}_{i\lambda})\right),

and hence we have the desired inclusion.
∎

Theorem 2.25
.

Every semisubtractive space is sober.

Proof.

Suppose
D
is a nonempty irreducible closed subset of a semisubtractive space
S
. Then
D=\bigcap_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}
, where each
\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}
is a finite union of subbasic closed sets of
\tau_{s}.
Since
D
is irreducible, for every
\lambda\in\Lambda
, there exists an
\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}\in\id
such that

D\subseteq\cz(\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda})\subseteq\mathcal{E}_{\lambda},

and this implies

D=\bigcap_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\h(\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda})=\h\left(\bigcap_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}\right)=\overline{\left\{\bigcap_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}\right\}},

where, the last equality follows from Lemma
2.24
. This proves the existence of the generic point, whereas the uniqueness of it follows from Proposition
2.23
.
∎

Proposition 2.26
.

Every semisubtractive space is connected.

Proof.

Since
\h(0)=\ids
and since irreducibility implies connectedness, the claim now follows from Lemma
2.24
.
∎

Using the Alexander subbase theorem, we now show the quasi-compactness of semisubtractive spaces. For this, we assume that
\h(\mathfrak{a})=\emptyset
implies
\mathfrak{a}=S
, for every semiring
S
and every ideal
\mathfrak{a}\in S
.

Proposition 2.27
.

Every semisubtractive space is quasi-compact.

Proof.

Let
\{K_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
be a family of subbasic closed sets of a semisubtractive space
\ids
such that
\bigcap_{\lambda\in\Lambda}K_{\lambda}=\emptyset.
Let
\{\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
be a family of ideals of
S
such that
\forall\lambda\in\Lambda,
we have
K_{\lambda}=\h(\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}).
Since

\bigcap_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\h(\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda})=\h\left(\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}\right),

we get
\h\left(\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}\right)=\emptyset,
and that implies
\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}=S.
Then, in particular, we obtain
1=\sum_{\lambda_{i}\in\Lambda}x_{\lambda_{i}},
where
x_{\lambda_{i}}\in\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda_{i}}
and
x_{\lambda_{i}}\neq 0
for
i=1,\ldots,n
. This shows
S=\sum_{i\,=1}^{n}\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda_{i}}.
Therefore,
\bigcap_{i\,=1}^{n}K_{\alpha_{i}}=\emptyset,
and hence by the Alexander subbase theorem,
\ids
is quasi-compact.
∎

Next, we study continuous maps between semisubtractive spaces. Thanks to Proposition
2.6
(
1
), we will see that every semiring homomorphism induces a canonical continuous map between the corresponding semisubtractive spaces.

Proposition 2.28
.

Let
\phi\colon S\to T
be a semiring homomorphism and
\mathfrak{b}\in\idss.

(1)

The induced map
\phi_{*}\colon\idss\to\ids
defined by
\phi_{*}(\mathfrak{b})=\phi^{\raisebox{0.60275pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle-1$}}(\mathfrak{b})
is continuous.

(2)

If
\phi
is surjective, then the semisubtractive space
\idss
is homeomorphic to the closed subspace
\h(\mathrm{Ker}\phi)
of the semisubtractive space
\ids.

(3)

The subset
\phi_{*}(\idss)
is dense in
\ids
if and only if
\mathrm{Ker}\phi\subseteq\bigcap_{\mathfrak{a}\in\ids}\mathfrak{a}.

Proof.

To show (
1
), let
\mathfrak{a}\in\ids
and
\h(\mathfrak{a})
be a subbasic closed set of the lower space
\ids.
Then

\displaystyle\phi_{*}^{\raisebox{0.60275pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle-1$}}(\h(\mathfrak{a}))

\displaystyle=\left\{\mathfrak{b}\in\idss\mid\phi^{\raisebox{0.60275pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle-1$}}(\mathfrak{b})\in\h(\mathfrak{a})\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{\mathfrak{b}\in\idss\mid\phi(\mathfrak{a})\subseteq\mathfrak{b}\right\}

\displaystyle=\h(\phi(\mathfrak{a})),

and hence, the map
\phi_{*}
continuous.
For (
2
), observe that
\mathrm{Ker}\phi\subseteq\phi^{\raisebox{0.60275pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle-1$}}(\mathfrak{b})
follows from the fact that
0\subseteq\mathfrak{b}
for all
\mathfrak{b}\in\idss.
It can thus been seen that
\phi_{*}(\mathfrak{b})\in\h(\mathrm{Ker}\phi),
and hence
\mathrm{Im}\phi_{*}=\h(\mathrm{Ker}\phi).

If
\mathfrak{b}\in\idss,
then

\phi\left(\phi_{*}\left(\mathfrak{b}\right)\right)=\phi\left(\phi^{\raisebox{0.60275pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle-1$}}\left(\mathfrak{b}\right)\right)=\mathfrak{b}.

Thus
\phi_{*}
is injective. To show that
\phi_{*}
is closed, observe that for any subbasic closed set
\h(\mathfrak{a})
of
\idss
, we have

\displaystyle\phi_{*}\left(\h(\mathfrak{a})\right)

\displaystyle=\phi^{\raisebox{0.60275pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle-1$}}\left(\h(\mathfrak{a})\right)

\displaystyle=\phi^{\raisebox{0.60275pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle-1$}}\left\{\mathfrak{b}\in\idss\mid\mathfrak{a}\subseteq\mathfrak{b}\right\}

\displaystyle=\h(\phi^{\raisebox{0.60275pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle-1$}}(\mathfrak{a})).

Now if
C
is a closed subset of
\idss
and
C=\bigcap_{\omega\in\Omega}\left(\bigcup_{i\,=1}^{n_{\omega}}\h(x_{i\omega})\right),
then

\displaystyle\phi_{*}(C)

\displaystyle=\phi^{\raisebox{0.60275pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle-1$}}\left(\bigcap_{\omega\in\Omega}\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{n_{\omega}}\h(x_{i\omega})\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\bigcap_{\omega\in\Omega}\,\bigcup_{i=1}^{n_{\omega}}\phi^{\raisebox{0.60275pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle-1$}}\left(\h(x_{i\omega})\right)

a closed subset of
\ids.
Since by (
1
),
\phi_{*}
is continuous, we have the desired claim.
Finally, to prove (
3
),
observe that

\overline{{\phi_{*}\left(\idss\right)}}=\phi_{*}(\h(0))=\h\left(\phi^{\raisebox{0.60275pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle-1$}}(0)\right)=\h\left(\mathrm{Ker}\phi\right).

It is now sufficient to show that
\h(\mathrm{Ker}\phi)
to be equal to
\ids
if and only if the given condition is satisfied.
Assume
\h(\mathrm{Ker}\phi)=\ids
.
It follows that every
\mathfrak{a}\in\ids
satisfies
k\in\mathfrak{a}
for all
k\in\mathrm{Ker}\phi
. Thus

k\in\mathfrak{a}
for all
x\in\ids
and
k\in\mathrm{Ker}\phi.

From that, we obtain

k\in\bigcap_{\mathfrak{a}\in\ids}\mathfrak{a}
, for every
k\in\mathrm{Ker}\phi.

Now assume that
k\in\bigcap_{\mathfrak{a}\in\ids}\mathfrak{a}
for every
k\in\mathrm{Ker}\phi
.
Given
k\in\bigcap_{\mathfrak{a}\in\ids}\mathfrak{a}
, it follows that

k\in\mathfrak{a}
for every
\mathfrak{a}\in\ids.

Thus, each
\mathfrak{a}\in\ids
contains all elements of
\mathrm{Ker}\phi
, which implies that

\mathfrak{a}\in\h(\mathrm{Ker}\phi)
for every
\mathfrak{a}\in\ids.

Since this is true for all
\mathfrak{a}\in\ids
, we must have

\h(\mathrm{Ker}\phi)=\ids.

∎

2.6.
s
-congruences

Motivated by
[
18
]
, our final aim is to define and study a congruence related to a semisubtractive ideal.
Recall that a
congruence
on a semiring
S
is an equivalence relation
\sim
that is compatible with the semiring operations, that is, for all
x
,
y
,
z\in S
:

\bullet

x\sim y\Rightarrow x+z\sim y+z
,

\bullet

x\sim y\Rightarrow xz\sim yz
.

Given a semisubtractive ideal
\mathfrak{a}
of
S
, we shall now construct a congruence corresponding to
\mathfrak{a}
.
Define a relation
\sim_{\mathfrak{a}}
on
S
by

x\sim_{\mathfrak{a}}y\Leftrightarrow x-y\in\mathfrak{a}\cap\vs.

The following proposition confirms that
\sim_{\mathfrak{a}}
is indeed a congruence.

Proposition 2.29
.

The relation
\sim_{\mathfrak{a}}
is an equivalence relation and is compatible with both the addition and multiplication of the semiring.

Proof.

For any
x\in S
, it is obvious that

x-x=0\in\mathfrak{a}\cap\vs
. Thus,
x\sim_{\mathfrak{a}}x
. If
x\sim_{\mathfrak{a}}y
, then
x-y\in\mathfrak{a}\cap\vs
, so there exists
z\in S
such that
z+(x-y)=0
, that is,
z=y-x\in\mathfrak{a}\cap\vs
. Hence,
y\sim_{\mathfrak{a}}x
. Suppose
x\sim_{\mathfrak{a}}y
and
y\sim_{\mathfrak{a}}z
. Then

x-y\in\mathfrak{a}\cap\vs
and
y-z\in\mathfrak{a}\cap\vs.

Adding these gives

(x-y)+(y-z)=x-z\in\mathfrak{a}\cap\vs,

so
x\sim_{\mathfrak{a}}z
.
Thus,
\sim_{\mathfrak{a}}
is an equivalence relation.
Suppose
x\sim_{\mathfrak{a}}y
, that is,
x-y\in\mathfrak{a}\cap\vs
. For any
z\in S
,

(x+z)-(y+z)=(x-y)\in\mathfrak{a}\cap\vs,

so
x+z\sim_{\mathfrak{a}}y+z
. Thus,
\sim_{\mathfrak{a}}
is compatible with addition.
Now suppose
x\sim_{\mathfrak{a}}y
, that is,
x-y\in\mathfrak{a}\cap\vs
. For any
z\in S
,

xz-yz=(x-y)z.

Since
\mathfrak{a}
is an ideal,
(x-y)z\in\mathfrak{a}
. Moreover, because
x-y\in\vs
and
S
is a semiring, we must have
(x-y)z\in\vs
. Thus,
xz\sim_{\mathfrak{a}}yz
, and
\sim_{\mathfrak{a}}
is compatible with multiplication.
Therefore,
\sim_{\mathfrak{a}}
is a congruence on
S
.
∎

The congruence defined with respect to a semisubtractive ideal will be called
s
-congruence
.

Theorem 2.30
.

There is a bijection between the set of semisubtractive ideals of a semiring
S
and the set of
s
-congruences on
S
.

Proof.

Let
\cs
be the set of all
s
-congruences on
S
.
We define a map
\Phi\colon\ids\to\cs
by
\Phi(\mathfrak{a}):=\sim_{\mathfrak{a}}
.
Given an
s
-congruence
\sim
in
\cs
, define the corresponding semisubtractive ideal
\mathfrak{a}_{\sim}
by

\mathfrak{a}_{\sim}:=\{x\in S\mid x\sim 0\}.

We claim that
\mathfrak{a}_{\sim}
is a semisubtractive ideal. Indeed:

\bullet

If
x\sim 0
and
y\sim 0
, then
x+y\sim 0
and
sx\sim 0
for all
s\in S
. Hence
\mathfrak{a}_{\sim}
is an ideal.

\bullet

If
x\in\mathfrak{a}_{\sim}\cap\vs
, then
x\sim 0
and
-x\sim 0
, so
-x\in\mathfrak{a}_{\sim}
. So,
\mathfrak{a}_{\sim}
is semisubtractive.

Thus, we define a map
\Psi\colon\cs\to\ids
by
\Psi(\sim)=\mathfrak{a}_{\sim}
.
Let us start with a semisubtractive ideal
\mathfrak{a}\in\ids
. Under the map
\Phi
, we associate the
s
-congruence
\sim_{\mathfrak{a}}
defined by

x\sim_{\mathfrak{a}}y\Leftrightarrow x-y\in\mathfrak{a}\cap\vs.

Apply
\Psi
to this
s
-congruence and the resulting semisubtractive ideal is

\mathfrak{a}_{\sim_{\mathfrak{a}}}=\{x\in S\mid x\sim_{\mathfrak{a}}0\}=\{x\in S\mid x\in\mathfrak{a}\cap\vs\}.

But this set is exactly
\mathfrak{a}
because by definition,
\mathfrak{a}\cap\vs
contains all the elements in
\mathfrak{a}
with additive inverses, and by the semisubtractive property, all such elements
x\in\mathfrak{a}
must also have
-x\in\mathfrak{a}
. Hence,

\Psi(\Phi(\mathfrak{a}))=\mathfrak{a}.

Now start with an
s
-congruence
\sim
in
\cs
. Under the map
\Psi
, we associate the semisubtractive ideal
\mathfrak{a}_{\sim}:=\{x\in S\mid x\sim 0\}
. Now, apply
\Phi
to this semisubtractive ideal. The resulting
s
-congruence is
\sim_{\mathfrak{a}_{\sim}}
, defined by

x\sim_{\mathfrak{a}_{\sim}}y\Leftrightarrow x-y\in\mathfrak{a}_{\sim}\cap\vs.

But
\mathfrak{a}_{\sim}=\{x\in S\mid x\sim 0\}
, so
x\sim_{\mathfrak{a}_{\sim}}y
if and only if
x-y\sim 0
, that is,
x\sim y
. Hence,

\Phi(\Psi(\sim))=\sim.

Therefore, the maps
\Phi
and
\Psi
are inverses of each other.
∎

Concluding remarks

In this paper, we have explored how the theory of various subclasses of semisubtractive ideals can be generalized. However, many other aspects remain unaddressed.

Extensive structural aspects of strongly irreducible ideals of rings have been developed in

20
]
, and some of them have been extended to strongly irreducible

k

[
29

, various topologies have been considered on strongly irreducible ideals, and these ideals have also been studied in the context of truncated valuation rings. Naturally, it would be worthwhile to extend these studies to

In a series of papers
[
8
,
9
,
10
,
11
,
5
]
,

k
-ideals have been studied in connection with monic and monic free ideals of polynomial semirings, and polynomial semirings in several variables. Once again, it is expected that these results can be generalized to semisubtractive ideals.

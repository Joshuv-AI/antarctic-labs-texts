Gaussian tripartite steering in Schwarzschild black hole
Shu-Min Wu 1 , Hao-Yu Wu 1 , Yu-Xuan Wang 1 , Jieci Wang 2

Abstract
Multipartite steering is a fundamental quantum resource that is uniquely suited to tackling complex relativistic quantum information challenges, but its properties in the gravitational field context remain to be elucidated. We study the distribution of Gaussian tripartite steering in the background of a Schwarzschild black hole. Our results show that physically accessible 2 → 1 2\rightarrow 1 steerability remains robust at any Hawking temperature, which contrasts with the behavior of 1 → 1 1\rightarrow 1 steering that experiences “sudden death” as the Hawking temperature increases, making 2 → 1 2\rightarrow 1 steerability a more reliable candidate for relativistic quantum information tasks. Notably, we observe that the sudden death of quantum steering is accompanied by a peak in steering asymmetry, signifying a critical transition from two-way steering to one-way steering in the relativistic three-mode system. We find that the Hawking effect can generate inaccessible two-way steering of a bipartite system in both directions, as shown in [Phys. Rev. D 93 , 125011 (2016)], while it can only generate physically inaccessible one-way 2 → 1 2\rightarrow 1 steering in tripartite systems. These insights enhance our understanding of the redistribution of multipartite steering near the event horizon of the black hole.

Measure of Gaussian quantum steering

(n_{A}+m_{B})
-mode Gaussian state, the amplitude (position) and phase (momentum) quadratures of each mode are represented as a column vector

[\hat{x}_{j},\hat{p}_{k}]=2i\delta_{jk}

{\sigma_{AB}}
with elements defined as

C^{T}&B&\\
\end{array}\!\!\right)

and

is the correlation matrix between the two parties.

The steerability from Alice to Bob via Gaussian measurements

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}^{A\rightarrow B}(\sigma_{AB}):=\max\left\{0,-\Sigma_{j:\bar{\nu}_{j}^{AB\backslash A}<1}\ln(\bar{\nu}_{j}^{AB\backslash A})\right\},

(1)

where

\bar{\sigma}_{AB\backslash A}=B-C^{T}A^{-1}C

V
CONCLUSIONS

In this paper, we have analyzed the distribution of Gaussian tripartite steering in the context of a Schwarzschild black hole. Specifically, we explore the sharing of quantum steerability among four subsystems: modes
A
and
B
, observed by Kruskal observers Alice and Bob, respectively; mode
C
, observed by a Schwarzschild observer Charlie, outside the event horizon; mode
\bar{C}
, observed by anti-Charlie, inside the event horizon. Utilizing the Bogoliubov transformation between the Kruskal and Schwarzschild vacua, we derive the phase-space evolution of a Gaussian tripartite state under the influence of the Hawking effect.
Our results reveal that
\mathcal{G}^{(BC)\rightarrow\ A}
initially decreases and subsequently approaches an asymptotic value as the Hawking temperature increases.
Notably, this indicates that physically accessible
2\rightarrow 1
steerability persists at all Hawking temperatures, in contrast to
1\rightarrow 1
steering
which undergoes “sudden death” with increasing Hawking temperature
[
73
]
. This contrast highlights the superior robustness of Gaussian tripartite steering under the influence of the Hawking effect compared to bipartite steering. A particularly fascinating observation is the abrupt vanishing of quantum steering
\mathcal{G}^{A\rightarrow\ (BC)}
, which coincides with the onset of maximal steering asymmetry. This marks a pivotal transition from two-way to one-way steering for the tripartite Gaussian state in the black hole. These results offer a deeper understanding of how Gaussian tripartite steering adapts to extreme gravitational environment.
Ultimately, we conclude that the Hawking effect uniquely generates physically inaccessible
2\rightarrow 1
steering in tripartite Gaussian systems, manifesting as one-way steering between initially uncorrelated modes. However,
physically accessible Gaussian system is two-way steerable in curved spacetime. This work underscores the profound interplay between Gaussian tripartite steering and the Hawking effect, offering a richer understanding of quantum information dynamics in curved spacetime.

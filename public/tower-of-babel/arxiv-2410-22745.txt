Abstract
. The Eaton–Moretó conjecture extends the recently-proven Brauer height zero conjecture to blocks with non-abelian defect group, positing equality between the minimal positive heights of a block of a finite group and its defect group. Here we provide further evidence for the inequality in this conjecture that is not implied by Dade’s conjecture. Specifically, we consider minimal counter-examples and show that these cannot be found among almost quasi-simple groups for p ≥ 5 p\geq 5 . Along the way, we observe that most such blocks have minimal positive height equal to 1.

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we are concerned with a conjecture of Charles Eaton and Alex
Moretó
[
11
]
on the
p
-parts of character degrees of finite groups,
where
p
is a prime number. This conjecture can be considered as an extension
of Richard Brauer’s height zero conjecture (whose proof was recently completed
in
[
30
]
) to the case of non-abelian defect groups.

In order to formulate the Eaton–Moretó conjecture, we introduce some
notation. For
D
a (finite)
p
-group we write

{\operatorname{mh}}(D):=\min\{\infty,\log_{p}(\chi(1))\mid\chi\in{\operatorname{Irr}}(D),\,\chi(1)>1\}

for the minimal height of a non-linear complex irreducible character of
D
,
if such exists, and
\infty
otherwise. Note that the latter occurs exactly
when
D
is abelian. Analogously, for
B
a Brauer
p
-block of a finite
group
G
, we let
{\operatorname{mh}}(B)
denote the minimal positive height of an irreducible
complex character in
B
, if such a character exists, and
\infty
otherwise.
Here recall that the height
h
of
\chi\in{\operatorname{Irr}}(B)
is defined by

(\chi(1)|D|/|G|)_{p}=p^{h}
, where
D
is any defect group of
B
.

Brauer’s height zero conjecture (now a theorem) states that for a

p
-block
B
of a finite group with defect group
D
, all characters in
B

have height zero if and only if
D
is abelian. In the terminology above, this
can be stated as
{\operatorname{mh}}(B)=\infty
if and only if
{\operatorname{mh}}(D)=\infty
. In 2014,
Eaton and Moretó
[
11
]
conjectured that these invariants agree in
general:

Conjecture 1
.

Let
B
be a
p
-block of a finite group with defect group
D
.
Then
{\operatorname{mh}}(B)={\operatorname{mh}}(D)
.

This has meanwhile been verified in several cases and for various classes of
groups and types of blocks (see e.g.
[
11
,
5
,
13
,
29
,
33
]
) but remains
open in general, even for solvable groups. In
[
11
]
it was further shown
that Dade’s projective conjecture, widely believed to be true and which has
been reduced to a question on simple groups, would imply one part of
Conjecture
1
, namely that
{\operatorname{mh}}(B)\geq{\operatorname{mh}}(D)
. For this reason,
providing further evidence for the reverse inequality seems the most pertinent.
The work in
[
29
,
33
]
indicates how difficult an eventual reduction to
simple groups might be for this direction of the conjecture, even for the
principal block case.

In this paper, we focus on a minimal counter-example to the
“
{\operatorname{mh}}(B)\leq{\operatorname{mh}}(D)
”-direction of Conjecture
1
. We obtain the
following results:

Theorem 2
.

Let
B
be a
p
-block of a finite quasi-simple group. Then
B
is not a
minimal counter-example to the direction
{\operatorname{mh}}(B)\leq{\operatorname{mh}}(D)
of
Conjecture
1
.

We remark that this result for the case
p>5
was stated in

[
13
, Cor. 1.5]
(except for proper covering groups of sporadic simple
groups). We also consider extensions by
p
-automorphisms (which will most
likely be relevant even if some kind of reduction of Conjecture
1
to
decorated simple groups is available):

Theorem 3
.

Let
p\geq 5
and let
B
be a
p
-block of a finite group
A
with
\mathbf{F}^{*}(A)

quasi-simple. Then
B
is not a minimal counter-example to the direction

{\operatorname{mh}}(B)\leq{\operatorname{mh}}(D)
of Conjecture
1
.

In fact, we also prove partial results for
p=2,3
on this question. Further,
in the case that
\mathbf{F}^{*}(A)
is quasi-simple not of Lie type in non-defining
characteristic, we obtain the full Conjecture
1
for
A
for all
p
.

Along the way, we find that in the situation of Theorem
3
, it is
often the case that
{\operatorname{mh}}(B)=1
when
B
has non-abelian defect groups. This is
true for alternating groups, sporadic groups (with just three exceptions for
covering groups when
p=3
), and the Tits group
{}^{2}\!\operatorname{F}_{4}(2)^{\prime}
, but we also
see that it holds in the so-called quasi-isolated blocks for groups of Lie type.

Theorem 4
.

Let
p\geq 5
and let
A
be a finite group such that
S=\mathbf{F}^{*}(A)
is
quasi-simple of Lie type in characteristic distinct from
p
. Then any

p
-block
B
of
A
of non-abelian defect covering a quasi-isolated block of

S
satisfies
{\operatorname{mh}}(B)=1
.

Again, we also prove partial results for
p=2,3
in the situation of
Theorem
4
. Our investigations lead us to believe that
the conclusion of Theorem
4
should actually hold for all blocks
of the groups
A
as above, a question we hope to come back to in the future.

The paper is structured as follows. In Section
2
, we provide some
initial reductions and observations that will be helpful throughout. Groups not
of Lie type and groups of Lie type defined in characteristic
p
are
discussed in Sections
3
and
4
, respectively.
Finally, in Section
5
, we treat the groups of Lie type
defined in characteristic distinct from
p
and complete the proofs of
Theorems
2
,
3
, and
4
.

2.
Elementary observations and reductions

Given a
p
-block
B
of a finite group, we let
d(B)
denote the defect of the
block. That is,
d(B)
is the integer such that
|D|=p^{d(B)}
, where
D
is
any defect group of
B
. The following will be used frequently.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
N\unlhd G
be finite groups with
G/N
a
p
-group of order
p^{a}
and let

b
be a
p
-block of
N
. Then there is a unique
p
-block
B
of
G

covering
N
. If
b
is further
G
-invariant, then
d(B)=d(b)+a
.

Proof.

The first statement is
[
32
, Cor. 9.6]
, and the second follows immediately
from
[
32
, Thm 9.17]
.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
N\unlhd G
be finite groups, and
B
a
p
-block of
N
with stabiliser

G_{B}=N
in
G
. Then Conjecture
1
holds for the unique block
\hat{B}

of
G
covering
B
if and only if it holds for
B
.

Proof.

This is an immediate consequence of
[
32
, Thm 9.14]
.
∎

The next observation shows we may restrict attention to groups
G
with

G=O^{p^{\prime}}(G)
:

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
N\unlhd G
be finite groups with
[G:N]
prime to
p
. Then
Conjecture
1
holds for the
p
-blocks of
G
if and only if it holds
for the
p
-blocks of
N
. Here, if the block
\hat{B}
of
G
covers the block

B
of
N
, then
{\operatorname{mh}}(B)={\operatorname{mh}}(\hat{B})
.

Proof.

Let
B
be a
p
-block of
N
with defect group
D
. Then any block
\hat{B}
of

G
covering
B
has the same defect groups. Moreover, if
\chi\in{\operatorname{Irr}}(B)
has
height
{\operatorname{mh}}(B)
then all constituents of the induced character
\chi^{G}
have the
same height as
\chi
by Clifford theory. Thus
\hat{B}
satisfies
Conjecture
1
if
B
does. Conversely, for
\hat{B}
a block of
G
let

B
be a
block of
N
covered by
\hat{B}
. Then again
B,\hat{B}
have the same defect
groups, and the restriction of any
\chi\in{\operatorname{Irr}}(\hat{B})
to
N
has a
constituent of the same height. Since this constituent lies in a conjugate
of
B
, we see that
B
also contains a constituent of that height.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
G=NZ
be the product of commuting subgroups such that
Z
is abelian and

N\cap Z
is a central
p^{\prime}
-subgroup. Let
\hat{B}
be a
p
-block of
G

covering a block
B
of
N
. Then
{\operatorname{mh}}(B)={\operatorname{mh}}(\hat{B})
. Further,
Conjecture
1
holds for
B
if and only if it holds for
\hat{B}
.

Proof.

Since
G
can be viewed as a quotient of
N\times Z
by a central

p^{\prime}
-subgroup, we may identify
{\operatorname{Irr}}(\hat{B})
with
{\operatorname{Irr}}(B\otimes B^{\prime})
for some
block
B^{\prime}
of
Z
using
[
32
, Thm 9.9]
. Further, any defect group

\hat{D}
of
\hat{B}
is isomorphic to one of
B\otimes B^{\prime}
, and is of the form

D\times D^{\prime}
with
D
a defect group for
B
and
D^{\prime}
a defect group for
B^{\prime}
.
Since all characters in
{\operatorname{Irr}}(B^{\prime})
and in
{\operatorname{Irr}}(D^{\prime})
are linear, we conclude

{\operatorname{mh}}(B)={\operatorname{mh}}(\hat{B})
and
{\operatorname{mh}}(D)={\operatorname{mh}}(\hat{D})
.
∎

2.1.
Metacyclic defect groups

We next make some useful observations about certain blocks with metacyclic
defect groups.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
P
be a non-abelian metacyclic
p
-group, with
p>2
. Then
{\operatorname{mh}}(P)=1
.

Proof.

Let
N\unlhd P
be a cyclic normal subgroup with
P/N
cyclic. Then any
irreducible character of
N
extends to its stabiliser in
P
. As
P
is
non-abelian, there is a character
\theta\in{\operatorname{Irr}}(N)
that is not
P
-invariant.
Let
p^{a}>1
be the length of the orbit of
\theta
under
P
. Then
\theta^{p}

lies in an orbit of length
p^{a-1}
and thus, some power
\chi
of
\theta

has stabiliser of index
p
in
P
. Any character of
P
above
\chi
then has
height 1.
∎

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
G
be a finite group and let
p
be a prime dividing
|G|
. Let

\sigma\in{\operatorname{Aut}}(G)\setminus{\operatorname{Inn}}(G)
be an automorphism of
p
-power order and
define
A:=G\langle\sigma\rangle
. If
B_{A}
is a
p
-block of
A
covering a
block
B
of
G
with cyclic defect groups, then
{\operatorname{mh}}(B_{A})={\operatorname{mh}}(D_{A})
, where

D_{A}
is a defect group for
B_{A}
.

Proof.

Let
D_{A}
be a defect group for
B_{A}
and let
D:=D_{A}\cap G
, a (cyclic)
defect group for
B
. Note that
D_{A}/D\cong D_{A}G/G\leq A/G
is cyclic, so that

D_{A}
is metacyclic. If
p=2
, then
[
34
, Cor. 8.2]
yields that

{\operatorname{mh}}(B_{A})={\operatorname{mh}}(D_{A})
, so we assume
p
is odd. Note that Lemma
2.5

gives
{\operatorname{mh}}(D_{A})=1
. So, we must show that
{\operatorname{mh}}(B_{A})=1
.

Applying
[
32
, Thm 9.14(d)]
, we may assume
B
is stable under
\sigma
.
Since
A/G
is cyclic, it suffices to show that there exists a
(height-zero) character of
B
lying in an orbit of length
p
under
\sigma
.

Following
[
10
, Thm 1 Part 1]
and the discussion before, since
B
is
cyclic, there is a block
b
of
DC_{G}(D)=C_{G}(D)
, unique up to

N_{G}(D)
-conjugation, such that
b^{G}=B^{\prime}
, where
B^{\prime}
is the Brauer
correspondent of
B
in
N_{G}(D)
. Then writing
E:=N_{G}(D)_{b}
, we have

[E:C_{G}(D)]
is prime to
p
. Further, the block
B
contains

(|D|-1)/[E:C_{G}(D)]
so-called exceptional characters
\chi_{\lambda}\in{\operatorname{Irr}}(B)
,
labelled by
1\neq\lambda\in{\operatorname{Irr}}(D)
, up to
E
-conjugacy. As in the
second-to-last sentence of the proof of
[
25
, Prop. 5.1]
, we have

\chi_{\lambda}^{\tau}=\chi_{\lambda^{\tau}}
for any
\tau\in\langle\sigma\rangle
. Then

\chi_{\lambda}^{\tau}=\chi_{\lambda}
if and only if
\lambda^{\tau}=\lambda^{g}
for some

g\in E
. Note that since
D
is cyclic,
\tau
and
g
act on
{\operatorname{Irr}}(D)

by powers, and hence these actions commute. But then
\lambda
has the same orbit
size under the
p
-elements
\tau
and
g
, contradicting that

E/C_{G}(D)
has
p^{\prime}
-order, unless
g\in C_{G}(D)
. Hence, we see for

1\neq\tau\in\langle\sigma\rangle
, we have
\chi_{\lambda}^{\tau}=\chi_{\lambda}
if and
only if
\lambda^{\tau}=\lambda
. Then choosing
\lambda
to have orbit of length
p
under

\sigma
as in Lemma
2.5
, we obtain
\chi_{\lambda}
in an orbit of length

p
under
\sigma
.
∎

3.
Groups not of Lie type

Conjecture

holds for all groups

\mathbf{F}^{*}(A)
a covering

\mathfrak{A}_{6}

{\operatorname{mh}}(B)=1

, we may assume

,

For the sporadic simple groups, Conjecture

.

Conjecture

\mathbf{F}^{*}(A)

4.
Groups of Lie type in defining characteristic

Throughout, let
\mathbf{G}
be a simple linear algebraic group over an algebraically
closed field
k
of characteristic
p>0
. Let
\mathbf{T}\leq\mathbf{G}
be a maximal torus
contained in a Borel subgroup
\mathbf{B}\leq\mathbf{G}
and let
\mathbf{U}
be the unipotent
radical of
\mathbf{B}
. We denote by
\Phi
the root system of
\mathbf{G}
with respect to

\mathbf{T}
, and by
\Phi^{+}\subset\Phi
the set of positive roots with respect to

\mathbf{B}
. Write
\Delta\subset\Phi^{+}
for the set of simple roots. For any

\alpha\in\Phi^{+}
, we denote by
\mathbf{U}_{\alpha}
the corresponding root subgroup in
\mathbf{U}

normalised by
\mathbf{T}
, and we choose an isomorphism

x_{\alpha}:k\rightarrow\mathbf{U}_{\alpha}
(see e.g.
[
31
, Sect. 8]
).

For any graph automorphism
\gamma
of
\mathbf{G}
and for any power

q=p^{f}
, there is a Frobenius endomorphism
F:\mathbf{G}\to\mathbf{G}
with the
property that
F(x_{\alpha}(c))=x_{\gamma(\alpha)}(c^{q})
for all
\alpha\in\Phi
,

c\in k
(see
[
16
, Thm 1.15.2]
). We define
F_{p}:\mathbf{G}\to\mathbf{G}
,

x_{\alpha}(c)\mapsto x_{\alpha}(c^{p})
, a Frobenius map with respect to an

{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-structure. Note that
F,F_{p}
commute in their action on
\mathbf{G}
, and

\mathbf{T},\mathbf{B}
and
\mathbf{U}
are
F
- and
F_{p}
-stable.
Let further
\mathbf{G}^{*}
be dual to
\mathbf{G}
with corresponding Frobenius endomorphisms
again denoted
F,F_{p}
and let
G:=\mathbf{G}^{F}
,
G^{*}:=\mathbf{G}^{*F}
.

It was shown in
[
29
, Prop. 3.8]
that Conjecture
1
holds for the
principal
p
-blocks of groups
A
obtained by extending a simple group of Lie
type
S
in characteristic
p
by a field automorphism of
p
-power order. We
now generalise this to all
p
-blocks of such groups.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
S
be a quasi-simple group of Lie type in characteristic
p
with

\mathbf{Z}(S)
of
p^{\prime}
-order,
\sigma
a
p
-power order field automorphism of
S
and

A:=S\langle\sigma\rangle
. Then Conjecture
1
holds for all
p
-blocks
of
A
.

Proof.

If
A=S
, or if
B
is the principal block of
A
the claim was shown in

[
5
, Thm 3.9]
, respectively in
[
29
, Prop. 3.8]
. So now assume
A>S

and
B
is a non-principal
p
-block of

Let

Theorem 4.2
.

Conjecture
1
holds for all
p
-blocks of groups
A
with
\mathbf{F}^{*}(A)
a
covering group of a simple group of Lie type in characteristic
p
.

Proof.

Once again, note that we may assume
A=O^{p^{\prime}}(A)
, by Lemma
2.3
.
Let
S:=\mathbf{F}^{*}(A)
. If
S
is an exceptional covering group of
S/\mathbf{Z}(S)
, the
claim is Proposition
3.3
, so we may assume
|\mathbf{Z}(S)|
is prime
to
p
. Furthermore, by the result of
[
29
, Prop. 3.8]
, it suffices to
consider faithful blocks of groups
S
with
\mathbf{Z}(S)\neq 1
.
The outer automorphisms of
p
-power order of simple groups of Lie type
S
in
characteristic
p
are field, graph and graph-field automorphisms (see e.g.

[
31
, Thm 24.24]
). In particular, for
p\geq 5
only field automorphisms
occur. Furthermore, if
S
is not of type
\operatorname{A}
, these are central in
{\operatorname{Out}}(S)

in which case our claim follows from Proposition
4.1
.

Next assume
S
is of type
\operatorname{A}
or
\operatorname{E}_{6}
and
A
is a subgroup of the
group of field and diagonal automorphisms, and keep the notation
B_{S}
, etc. as in Proposition
4.1
. As diagonal automorphisms have order prime
to
p
, then
A/S
is a semidirect product of a group of diagonal automorphisms
by a group of field automorphisms, generated by
\sigma
, say. Let
S<A^{\prime}\leq A
be
the extensions of
S
in
A
generated by the diagonal automorphisms only. By a
well-known result of Brauer (see e.g.
[
32
, Probl. 4.8]
), the
p
-blocks of
maximal defect of any group
H
between
S
and
A^{\prime}
are in bijection with the
set of
p^{\prime}
-elements centralising a Sylow
p
-subgroup of
H
and thus with

\mathbf{Z}(S)
; in particular, there is a unique block of
H
above
B_{S}
. Let

\chi\in{\operatorname{Irr}}(B_{S})
be the character constructed in the proof of
Proposition
4.1
, so
\chi
is
\sigma^{p}
-invariant but not

\sigma
-stable. Now
|{\operatorname{Irr}}(A^{\prime}|\chi)|
is a divisor of
[A^{\prime}:S]
, hence prime
to
p
. So it contains a
\sigma^{p}
-invariant character
\chi^{\prime}
. By the
construction of
\chi
,
\chi^{\sigma}
lies in a different Lusztig series, so

\chi^{\prime}
cannot be
\sigma
-invariant and thus any character of
A
above
\chi^{\prime}

is as required.

For
p=3
the only groups
S
with additional
p
-automorphisms are those of
untwisted type
\operatorname{D}_{4}
, for which
{\operatorname{Out}}(S)\cong\mathfrak{S}_{4}\times C_{f}
.
Thus
A/S\leq\mathfrak{A}_{4}\times C_{3^{k}}
with
3^{k}|f
. By Proposition
4.1

we may assume that
A
induces some non-field automorphisms of 3-power order.
Now note that non-trivial graph and graph-field automorphisms act fixed-point
freely on the centre
\mathbf{Z}(G)\cong C_{2}^{2}
of our group
G
of simply connected
type. Hence
A
cannot fix any
p
-block of
G
above a non-trivial central
character. Thus we are done by the above reduction.

If

then additional graph automorphisms of order 2 exist for types

\operatorname{A}_{n}
,
\operatorname{D}_{n}
and
\operatorname{E}_{6}
, as well as exceptional graph automorphisms for
types
\operatorname{B}_{2}
and
\operatorname{F}_{4}
. Now the groups of types
\operatorname{D}_{n}
,
\operatorname{B}_{2}
and

\operatorname{F}_{4}
do not have proper generic covering groups in characteristic

,
so they are already handled by
[

, Prop. 3.8]
.
For
\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\epsilon q)
and
\operatorname{E}_{6}(\epsilon q)
the graph- and graph-field automorphisms
act by inversion on the centre (of odd order) of the simply connected group, so
again there are no invariant non-trivial characters of

in

.
∎

5.
Groups of Lie type in non-defining characteristic

With the results of the previous sections, note that the only remaining
obstacle to Theorems
2
and
3
is the case of groups of
Lie type in non-defining characteristic. Throughout this section, we fix the
following setup. Let
\mathbf{G}
be a simple linear algebraic group of
simply connected type with a Steinberg map
F:\mathbf{G}\to\mathbf{G}
and set
G:=\mathbf{G}^{F}
the
corresponding finite reductive group. We let
q
denote the absolute
value of the eigenvalues of
F
on the character group of an
F
-stable maximal
torus of
\mathbf{G}
; so
F^{2}
is a Frobenius map with respect to an

{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}
-structure. We also let
(\mathbf{G}^{*},F)
be in duality with
(\mathbf{G},F)
and
set
G^{*}:=\mathbf{G}^{*F}
. We let
\ell
be a prime not dividing
q^{2}
.

5.1.
Blocks and field automorphisms

We will need the following extension to Steinberg maps of a result shown for
Frobenius maps in
[
30
]
:

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
\sigma
be a field automorphism of
G=\mathbf{G}^{F}
of order
\ell
, and let

\gamma=\sigma\tau
, where
\tau
is an inner-diagonal automorphism of
G
. Then
any
\ell
-block of
G
of non-central defect contains characters in a
Lusztig series that is not
\gamma
-stable.

Proof.

When
F
is a Frobenius map, this was shown in
[
30
, Prop. 4.8]
. We argue
that the same proof goes through for Steinberg maps. Thus we may assume
\mathbf{G}

is of type
\operatorname{B}_{2}
,
\operatorname{G}_{2}
, or
\operatorname{F}_{4}
in characteristic
p=2,3,2

respectively. In particular,
G
has no non-inner diagonal automorphisms and so

\gamma=\sigma
. Moreover,
G
also has no field automorphisms of even order, so

\ell
is necessarily odd.

The
\ell
-blocks of

are associated to
\Phi
-cuspidal pairs
(\mathbf{L},\lambda)
,
where
\Phi
is a cyclotomic polynomial over
{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{p})
(as in

[
15
, 3.5.3]
) such that
\ell
divides
\Phi(q)
. By assumption, up to
conjugation,
\sigma
is induced by a Steinberg map
F_{0}
of
\mathbf{G}
such that

F=F_{0}^{\ell}
, so

induces an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q_{0}^{2}}
-structure, with

.
Then
\ell
also divides
\Phi(q_{0})
, whence any
\Phi
-torus of

(\mathbf{G},F_{0})
is also a
\Phi
-torus of (
\mathbf{G},F)
, and conversely, any

F_{0}
-stable
\Phi
-torus of
(\mathbf{G},F)
is also a
\Phi
-torus of
(\mathbf{G},F_{0})
.

We can now copy the argument given in the proof of
[
30
, Prop. 4.8]
to
construct a cyclic
\sigma^{*}
-stable
\Phi
-torus
\mathbf{T}_{1}
of
\mathbf{G}^{*}
and an

\ell
-element

that is not
\sigma^{*}
-stable. Note that the construction of the
automorphism
\sigma^{*}
of
\mathbf{G}^{*}
in
[
35
]
does not require

to be a
Frobenius map. Using the table in
[
15
, Tab. 3.3]
of
\Phi
-split Levi
subgroups (and the classification of isolated elements when

is bad for

\mathbf{G}
, which only happens for

and

of type

), we see that

C_{\mathbf{G}^{*}}((\mathbf{T}_{1}^{F})_{\ell})=C_{\mathbf{G}^{*}}((\mathbf{T}_{1})_{\Phi})
. The
rest of the argument is then completed as in
[
30
]
.
∎

The following consequence of Proposition
5.1
will allow us to deal
with blocks of

with abelian defect.

.

Let

and let

\ell
-power order field automorphism of

-stable

on

Since

there are

of

is an

More specifically, let

In the proof of

, Prop. 4.8]
, respectively Proposition

,
we constructed a

-stable

of

of

\ell
-element

-stable, so is the

, and

[

, Prop. 4.8]
shows that

. Thus any character of

above

.

In the case that

.
∎

5.2.
Unipotent blocks

We now first consider unipotent blocks. For this, recall from
[
12
, Thm A]

that if
F
is a Frobenius map, any unipotent
\ell
-block of
G
is labelled
by a unipotent
e
-cuspidal pair
(\mathbf{L},\lambda)
of
G
of central
\ell
-defect,
where

is the order of
q
modulo
\ell
, respectively modulo
4
if
\ell=2

(which we denote

). The following strengthens
[

, Lemma 2.8]
:

Lemma 5.3
.

Assume
F
is a Frobenius map.
Let
(\mathbf{L},\lambda)
be a unipotent
e
-cuspidal pair of
G=\mathbf{G}^{F}
of central

\ell
-defect, where

, such that
\ell
divides

|W_{\mathbf{G}^{F}}(\mathbf{L},\lambda)|
. Then
W_{\mathbf{G}^{F}}(\mathbf{L},\lambda)
has an irreducible character

\phi
with
\phi(1)_{\ell}=\ell
, unless one of the following holds:

(1)

We have
\ell=3
, and either
W_{\mathbf{G}^{F}}(\mathbf{L},\lambda)\cong\mathfrak{S}_{3c}
,

\mathbf{G}^{F}=\operatorname{SL}_{3c}(\epsilon q)
with

and

, or

\mathbf{G}^{F}=E_{6}(\epsilon q)

and
(\mathbf{L},\lambda)

corresponds to Line
8
of the

-tables of
[
12
, pp. 351, 354]

(2)

We have
\ell=2
,
W_{\mathbf{G}^{F}}(\mathbf{L},\lambda)\cong\mathfrak{S}_{2}
, and either
\mathbf{G}
is of
classical type, or
\mathbf{G}
is of type
E_{7}
and
(\mathbf{L},\lambda)
corresponds to one
of Lines
3
or
7
of the
E_{7}
-table of
[
12
, p. 354]
.

Proof.

First assume
\mathbf{G}
is of exceptional type, or that
\mathbf{G}^{F}={}^{3}\!D_{4}(q)
. The
relative Weyl groups
W_{\mathbf{G}^{F}}(\mathbf{L},\lambda)
of unipotent

-cuspidal pairs are
listed in
[
4
, Table 1]
, and an easy check shows that in the very few
cases when
\ell
does divide
|W_{\mathbf{G}^{F}}(\mathbf{L},\lambda)|

\phi
with
\phi(1)_{\ell}=\ell
, unless either
\ell=3
,
\mathbf{G}
is of type

and we are in case (1) of the conclusion, or
\ell=2
and

W_{\mathbf{G}^{F}}(\mathbf{L},\lambda)\cong C_{2}
in
\mathbf{G}
of type
E_{6}
,
E_{7}
, or
E_{8}
. According
to the tables in
[
12
, pp. 351, 354, 358]
, the only case with
\lambda
of
central
\ell
-defect is in

with
\mathbf{L}
of type
E_{6}
and
\lambda
one of the
two cuspidal characters as in (2).

Next assume that
\mathbf{G}^{F}
is of untwisted type
\operatorname{A}
. Here, the relative Weyl
groups are wreath products
C_{e}\wr\mathfrak{S}_{a}
for some

(see

[

, Exmp. 3.5.29]

of

[

, Thm 2.1]

Assume

. If
\ell=2
and either

or

has a character as claimed. The case
\ell=2=ae
is excluded by (2).
So we have
\ell>2
. Then

by definition, so if
\ell
divides

|W_{\mathbf{G}^{F}}(\mathbf{L},\lambda)|
then
\ell\leq a
. If

then let

be an
\ell
-core of size

. Then any character of height 0 in the

[

]

If

is a unitary group, the same argument applies, except that here the

with

(see
[

, Exmp. 3.5.29]
).

For
\mathbf{G}
of type
\operatorname{B}
or
\operatorname{C}

[

, Exmp. 3.5.29]

our above argument, the factor group

already has a suitable character,
unless we are in one of the exceptions for

and

,

has an irreducible character of degree 6. Finally,
for
\mathbf{G}
of type
\operatorname{D}

above, and so inherit a character of the required form by Clifford theory.
∎

-group.

Further, by Clifford theory and the fact that

(iv) Finally, suppose that

\sigma

of
\widetilde{t}
in

lying

are trivial on

. If instead

\widetilde{t}

\widetilde{G}^{\ast}
-conjugate to
\widetilde{t}z

of

. Let the block

of

Theorem 5.12
.

Let

and

. Further, assume that

automorphisms if

\ell
-blocks of

Proof.

As before, by Lemma
2.3
we may assume
A={\mathbf{O}}^{\ell^{\prime}}(A)
, so
A
is
generated over
S:=\mathbf{F}^{*}(A)
by outer automorphisms of
\ell
-power order.
Our conditions on
\ell
mean that these are diagonal-field automorphisms.
Moreover, our assumptions ensure that diagonal automorphisms are only involved
when
S
is a central quotient of
\operatorname{A}_{n}(\epsilon q)
, in which case the

\ell
-blocks with non-abelian defect are not minimal counter-examples by
Proposition
5.6
.

Let
B
be an
\ell
-block of
A
. If
B
has abelian defect, the claim
certainly holds by Brauer’s height zero conjecture. So assume

has
non-abelian defect.

As
S={}^{2}\!\operatorname{F}_{4}(2)^{\prime}
was considered in Proposition
3.2
, we let

be quasi-simple with
S=G/Z
for
Z\leq\mathbf{Z}(G)
,
B_{S}
a block of
S

covered by
B
and
B_{G}
a block of
G
dominating
B_{S}
. Again,
B_{S}
and

B_{G}
determine one another uniquely. Applying
[
32
, Thm 9.9(c) and 9.14]
,
it suffices to assume that
Z
is an
\ell
-group and that
B_{S}
is

A
-invariant. By Theorem
5.10
,

is not quasi-isolated.

Recall that we have reduced to the case

only involves field automorphisms.
Let

be an
\ell^{\prime}
-element such that
{\operatorname{Irr}}(B)\subseteq\mathcal{E}_{\ell}(G,s)
and let

be the (unique) Levi subgroup minimal with respect to the

, so that

(and hence

) is

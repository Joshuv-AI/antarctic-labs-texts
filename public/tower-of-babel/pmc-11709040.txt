Addressing the urgent need for tobacco control among adolescents
Authors: Shubham Jagannath Shivale, Divya Madamanchi
Published: 2024

Dear Editor,

We read the article titled “Prevalence Patterns and Profile of Adolescent Tobacco Users Findings from a Youth Survey: A Cross-sectional Study” with great interest.[1] As the author pointed out tobacco use remains a significant public health challenge globally, contributing to extensive disability, disease, and death, I would like to share a few more views from my side, regarding tobacco use The study found that the prevalence of chewing tobacco is notably higher in males compared to females. Peer pressure was identified as a significant factor contributing to tobacco addiction among male students.

The mean age of initiation for chewing tobacco was 14.8 years for males and 15.3 years for females. This early onset highlights the critical need for preventive measures during adolescence—a formative period that significantly impacts long-term health behaviours. The prevalence of smoking among college students was 21.3%, with a higher rate among males (16.9%). Gender differences in smoking behaviour were noted, with males typically smoking for stimulation and pleasure, while females smoked more in response to negative emotions and difficult situations. This behavioural distinction underscores the need for gender-specific approaches in tobacco use prevention. The study also highlighted the influence of social circles on tobacco use. Males with friends who used chewing tobacco were significantly more likely to use tobacco themselves. This emphasizes the role of peer influence in the initiation and continuation of tobacco use among young adults. However, the prevalence varies widely across regions, indicating a complex landscape of tobacco use among children. The adverse health effects of tobacco, particularly smokeless forms are profound, with adolescent smokers and users of smokeless tobacco facing a significantly elevated risk of developing periodontal disease and other oral health issues.[2] This habit starts as a time pass, turns into a habit, and finally becomes an obsession. This trend is increasingly seen among teenagers using tobacco and pan masala due to its easy accessibility, affordability, and wide availability.[3] The use of smokeless tobacco remains a significant threat among teenagers, making up a large portion of their overall tobacco consumption. Smokeless forms of tobacco are more difficult to detect and regulate compared to smoking. Experimental and clinical studies on pan masala and its components indicate that they can cause Oral Submucous Fibrosis and leukoplakia, which may lead to oral cancer.[3] Moreover, the risk of more severe health complications, including oral cancer, looms large in adulthood.[3]

Compounding this crisis is the widespread exposure of children and families to second-hand smoke, leading to increased illness and suffering. Children exposed to second-hand smoke are at heightened risk of severe asthma attacks, pneumonia, and ear infections, underscoring the urgent need for comprehensive tobacco control measures.[4] Strict laws about selling tobacco must be enforced as per WHO FCTC (Framework Convention on Tobacco Control) guidelines. These rules include banning the sale of tobacco to minors, requiring strong health warnings on packaging, and running public education programs aimed at teenagers and youth. Smoking should also be banned in public places to protect people from second-hand smoke. Under COTPA (Cigarettes and Other Tobacco Products Act), selling tobacco to anyone under 18 is prohibited, and selling tobacco within 100 meters of schools is banned. Tobacco packages must show specific health warnings. However, these rules are not being followed properly, as smokeless tobacco is still easily accessible to minors. Therefore, stricter enforcement of these laws is needed.[5]

Young people are our future. We must ensure they stay healthy and succeed in school by preventing tobacco use through collaborative efforts. Immediate action is needed to address this issue, focusing on prevention, education, and enforcing tobacco control policies to protect future generations’ health and well-being.

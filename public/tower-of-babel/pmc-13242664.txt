Novel BTK inhibitors and degraders for relapsed/refractory CLL/SLL: latest updates from ASH 2025 annual meeting
Authors: Pritika Sharma, Heng Jiang, Delong Liu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAlthough covalent and non-covalent Bruton tyrosine kinase inhibitors (BTKi) have extended clinical benefit to relapsed/refractory (R/R) and BTKi-resistant chronic lymphocytic leukemia/small lymphocytic lymphoma (CLL/SLL), there remains an unmet need for additional B-cell receptor (BCR) pathway targeted therapies for highly refractory disease. This summary highlighted latest updates from the American Society of Hematology 2025 Annual Meeting on emerging BTK-directed agents in CLL/SLL, including next-generation non-covalent BTKi, dual-kinase inhibitors, and BTK degraders. Phase 1 studies of docirbrutinib and rocbrutinib demonstrated target engagement across wild-type and resistance-associated BTK mutations with encouraging safety profiles and preliminary efficacy signals in heavily pretreated populations. Birletinib, a dual LYN/BTK inhibitor, showed a high objective response rate in patients with prior exposure to BTKi, BCL-2 inhibitors, and BTK degraders. BTK degraders, including bexobrutideg and BGB-16673, demonstrated rapid and deep responses, activity in high-risk molecular subgroups, and manageable toxicity profiles, with recommended phase 2 doses established. Collectively, these latest updates support continued clinical development of novel agents to address resistance and disease progression in R/R CLL/SLL.

BTK inhibitors

Docirbrutinib (AS-1763)[4]

Docirbrutinib is a pan-mutant non-covalent BTKi (ncBTKi) that targets both wild type (WT) BTK and multiple-resistance associated BTK mutations (RAM), including the kinase-dead L528W mutant. Docirbrutinib has a biochemical IC50 of 0.9nM against WT BTK. The ongoing phase 1b trial (NCT05602363) recruited 23 patients with CLL/SLL and 15 patients with B-cell non-Hodgkin lymphoma (B-NHL). Among efficacy evaluation eligible patients, eight CLL patients achieved partial response (PR) with the rest of the cohort showing encouraging anti-tumor effect. Overall safety profile is favorable with no reports of drug-related atrial fibrillation (AFib) or hypertension. Three patients had grade 3 treatment-emergent adverse effects (TEAEs), among whom 2 had neutropenia and 1 had hematoma and hemorrhagic shock in the setting of concomitant anticoagulation.

Rocbrutinib (LP-168)[5–7]

Rocbrutinib is a fourth-generation BTKi with multiple targets, including WT, C481, and non-C481 mutations. Preclinical studies show low IC50 values against WT and RAM in murine CLL models. Rocbrutinib binds WT BTK covalently and engages C481-mutant variants through non-covalent interactions. In a study on 41 Chinese patients with newly diagnosed or R/R CLL/SLL, rocbrutinib demonstrated promising safety and efficacy outcomes, with no grade ≥ 3 AFib, hypertension, or hemorrhage reported [6]. In another multicenter study on R/R CLL/SLL population, median progression-free survival (PFS) was 28.1 months, with median duration of response (DOR) not reached at a median follow-up of 30.3 months [5].

Dual inhibitors

Birletinib (DZD8586)[8]

Birletinib is a dual inhibitor of LYN and BTK through blocking BCR-pathways. The TAI-SHAN5 (NCT05824585) and TAI-SHAN8 (NCT06539182, CTR20240120) studies reported pooled safety and efficacy data of birletinib in heavily pre-treated patients with CLL/SLL, including those with prior exposure to covalent BTKi (cBTKi) and ncBTKi. In the phase 1/2 clinical trials, 50 mg once daily was identified as the recommended phase 3 dose (RP3D). Those patients treated at the RP3D achieved 84% objective response rate (ORR), including responses in patients with prior exposures to BTKi, BCL-2 inhibitor (BCL2i), and BTK degrader. The safety profile was manageable with no reports of major bleeding, QT prolongation, AFib, or treatment-related death; neutropenia being the most common grade 3 TEAEs (22.7%).

BTK degraders

Bexobrutideg (NX-5948)[9]

Bexobrutideg is a novel, highly selective, small-molecule BTK degrader that induces ubiquitination and subsequent proteasomal degradation of both WT and mutant BTK, while also disrupting BTK scaffolding function. In the phase 1a dose-escalation portion, six dose levels (50, 100, 200, 300, 450, and 600 mg) were evaluated, followed by phase 1b dose-expansion cohort randomized between 200 mg and 600 mg, with 600 mg selected as the recommended phase 2 dose (RP2D). Bexobrutideg demonstrated rapid and deep clinical activity over time, with a median time to first response of 1.9 months and conversion from stable disease to PR observed in approximately 18% of patients. Clinically meaningful response rates were observed in high-risk and heavily pretreated subgroups, including ≥ 4 prior lines of therapy, TP53 mutation, and other BTK mutations. The safety profile was favorable with no reported cases of new-onset arrhythmia, neutropenic fever, or fungal infections.

BGB-16673

BGB-16673 induces degradation of BTK independent of mutation status and disrupts both the catalytic and scaffolding functions of BTK [10]. Among 67 patients enrolled in the multicenter phase I study [11], grade ≥ 3 TEAEs were reported in 62.7% of patients. About 18% of the patients discontinued treatment due to TEAEs and the prominent treatment related TEAEs leading to discontinuation included subdural hemorrhage, macupapular rash, and disseminated aspergillosis. A dose of 200 mg once daily was selected as RP2D. High ORR was observed in high-risk subgroups including double (cBTKi and BCL2i) and triple (cBTKi, BCL2i, and ncBTKi) exposed patients, those with ≥ 6 prior lines of therapy, del(17p) and/or TP53 mutation, complex karyotype, and BTK or PCCG2 mutations.

In summary, novel BTK inhibitors and degraders show promising efficacy and manageable safety profiles in R/R CLL/SLL.

Blood circulating C228T TERT gene promoter mutation is an independent factor for the prognosis of HBV–related hepatocellular carcinoma
Authors: Pham Quang Trung, Pham Chau, Duong Quang Huy, Nghiem Xuan Hoan, Julien Reboud, Nguyen Linh Toan, Le Huu Song, Ngo Tat Trung
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundTelomerase reverse transcriptase (TERT) promoter mutations are among the most common noncoding genetic lesions in various cancers, including hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC). The clinical and pathological implications of hepatitis B virus (HBV)–related HCC patients carrying promoter mutations are only partially resolved.MethodsA nested real–time PCR assay was optimized to detect C228T TERT gene promoter mutation (C228T) from plasma samples with a detection limit of 0.1%. The established assay was used to investigate the association of the C228T with the clinical presentation of HBV–related HCC patients.ResultsThe C228T was detected only in the HCC group (n = 40/159, 25.2%), not in the other control groups (n = 0/160). The mutation rate was significantly higher in HCC patients with low albumin concentration, increased direct bilirubin, abdominal lymphadenopathy, and portal vein thrombosis, compared with HCC patients without these factors (p < 0.05); the mutation rate also increased progressively with more advanced stages (p < 0.05) and was significantly associated with shorter overall survival (OS).ConclusionThe C228T is associated with advanced stages, poorer liver function, and shorter overall survival; hence, it could be used as a novel biomarker for the prognosis of HBV–related HCC patients.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12885-026-16271-0.

Introduction

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) is the sixth most frequent malignancy and the fourth leading cause of cancer–related death worldwide [1, 2] but the incidence rate is extremely high in Eastern Asia, including Vietnam [1]. According to Globocan, liver cancer accounts for 14% of total cancer incidence and 21% of the total cancer deaths in Vietnam [1]. HCC typically develops on the background of chronic liver disease, particularly in patients with cirrhosis due to alcohol use, chronic hepatitis B or C virus infections, or nonalcoholic steatohepatitis (NASH) [2–4].

HCC is believed to develop through a multistep process, in which a precursor or premalignant lesion progresses to dysplastic nodules and ultimately to HCC [5]. In the context of cirrhosis, liver carcinogenesis progresses through well–defined stages, starting from low–grade dysplastic nodules to progressive HCC. These processes, termed hepatocarcinogenesis [6, 7], are characterized by the progressive accumulation of various genetic alterations during the progression of chronic liver diseases [8, 9]. Hence a deep understanding of the genomic alterations in HCC could improve tumor characterization, patient classification and facilitate the identification of molecular targets for therapeutic decision–making.

Recently, various genetic lesions have been described from patient samples of liver cancer [10–12], of which the mutations of the telomerase reverse transcriptase (TERT) gene are the most frequent genetic alterations observed in HCC [7]. The telomerase subunit TERT is responsible for its catalytic activity [13]. Its expression is tightly regulated in most normal cells but upregulated in numerous cancers [14]. TERT expression can be regulated at both the transcriptional and post–translational levels. At the transcriptional level, cancer–associated TERT promoter mutations function by generating de novo transcription–factor–binding sites (TTCC). Two major TERT promoter mutations are − 124 C/T and − 146 C/T, also known as C228T and C250T, respectively. These mutations generate putative E-twenty‐six (ETS) transcription–factor–binding sites (TTCC) thereby activating TERT transcription [15, 16]. Up to 60% of HCC patients carry TERT promoter mutations [8, 17], whilst the mutations have been identified in 54% to 60% of tumor tissues in Western countries compared to Eastern countries (29% to 31%) [18] and in 95% of cases, they are represented by a C > T transition in the − 124 bp hotspot (C228T) [19].

The TERT promoter region is characterized by a high GC content that constitutes a technical challenge for optimizing molecular assays for the direct identification of these mutations in patient samples. Sanger sequencing and droplet digital PCR have been used to identify TERT promoter mutations, but they lack clear technical detection limit [20] or are cumbersome to perform routinely [21–23]. Assays for the direct identification of TERT promoter mutations from liquid biopsies of HCC patients have been shown but with limited success. Therefore, there is still a shortage of studies evaluating the relationship between the prevalence of blood–circulating TERT promoter mutations and the clinico-pathological presentation of HBV–infected HCC patients.

In our previous work, a gel–based nested–PCR assay was proposed to detect the C228T mutation, but it was challenging to perform routinely in hospitals. Therefore, in the current study, we modified the gel–based nested–PCR assay into nested real–time PCR with allele specific primers, showing a detection limit of 0.1%. This assay facilitated the identification of TERT promoter mutations and allowed us to study the relationship between blood–circulating TERT promoter mutations and the clinicopathological characteristics of HBV–infected HCC patients.

Materials and methods

Study design and patient selection

This study was conducted at Military Central Hospital 108 and Military Hospital 103 in Vietnam from May 2020 to July 2024. All methods used in this study were in accordance with the relevant guidelines and regulations and were approved by the institutional review board and the independent Ethics Committee of the 108 Military Central Hospital, Hanoi, Vietnam (project VINIF.2019.15).

A total of 159 patients newly diagnosed with HBV–related HCC who were treated with systemic chemotherapy or TACE or resection [24, 25]; 98 patients with chronic hepatitis B virus (CHB); and 62 patients with HBV–infected liver cirrhosis (LC) were included into this current study. Inclusion criteria: confirmed diagnosis and staging of HCC was performed according to the American Association for the Study of Liver Disease (AASLD 2018) [26, 27] or the TNM system [28], diagnosis of cirrhosis [29] and positive automated HBsAg immunoassay. Exclusion criteria: (i) co–infected with hepatitis C virus (HCV) or human immunodeficiency virus (HIV), (ii) presence of other cancers, (iii) refusal to participate in the study. Patients were invited for follow–up within 42 months and were contacted by phone every 6 months for survival recording.

Clinical evaluation and staging

All patients underwent a comprehensive clinical evaluation, including an assessment of symptoms, blood counts, liver function tests, and a 64–128–slice–CT scan of the abdomen to evaluate tumor characteristics and extent. HCC staging was performed according to the Barcelona Clinic Liver Cancer (BCLC) system, which includes five stages (O, A, B, C, and D) on the basis of tumor burden, physical activity status, and liver function as assessed by the Child–Pugh score [30]. HCC staging was also based on the American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) tumor/node/metastasis (TNM) staging system, which takes into account the extent of the tumor, lymph node involvement, and the presence of metastases, including stages I, II, III, and IV [28]. The samples were collected from Military Central Hospital 108 and Military Hospital 103, both located in Hanoi, to minimize potential regional variation.

Detection of the C228T via nested real–time PCR

Building upon the technical foundation of our gel–based nested–PCR assay as reported previously [31], in the present study, a nested real–time PCR was set up as briefly: In the first round, 0.17 µM of each outer primer pair (Tr–TERT–seq–F: 5’– GTC CTG CCC CTT CAC CTT CCA − 3’ and Tr–TERT–seq–R: 5’– GCA GCG CTG CCT GAA ACT CG − 3’) was used with GoTaq® Hot Start Master Mixe (Promega), 3 µL of Q solution 5x (Qiagen) and 0.25 µM Peptide Nucleic Acid (PNA) sequence: NH2– CCG GAG GGG GCT –CONH2 in a total volume of 15 µL. The PCR conditions were as follows: initial denaturation at 95 °C for 2 min, followed by 25 cycles of 95 °C for 30 s, 62 °C for 45 s, and 72 °C for 30 s, with a final extension at 72 °C for 5 min. The amplicon products were subsequently diluted 100–fold and used as a template for the second round of PCR.

In the second round, the C228T was identified via real–time PCR using QuantiTect Probe PCR Master Mix (Qiagen) and 0.33 µM of each inner primer pair (Tr–TERT–In–F: 5’– CAC CTT CCA GCT CCG CCT C − 3’ and Tr–TERT–seq–R: 5’– GCA GCG CTG CCT GAA ACT CG − 3’); The probe sequence was /56–FAM/CCC+C+T+T+CCGG/3IABkFQ/ (0.17 µM) in a total volume of 15 µL, with “+” indicating Locked Nucleic Acid (LNA) bases [32]. The PCR conditions followed the manufacture’s protocol, with annealing and reading at 64 °C for a total of 50 cycles.

Assay sensitivity and specificity were determined using serial dilutions of DNA from the HCC cell line Huh7 (5 ng/µL, 50% mutation) mixed with DNA from the lung cell line A549 (5 ng/µL, 0% mutation) to generate serial dilutions ranging from 0.1% to 25%. The volume of the DNA template used for nested real–time PCR was 3 µL (equal to 15 ng/reaction). Samples with Ct values < 40 were defined as positive, and Ct values ranging from 40 to 50 were regarded as inconclusive and subjected to a single re-test. The assay consistently detected the C228T mutation at a minor allele frequency of 0.1% with no false positives (Figure S1).

We then used the newly established nested real–time PCR assay to screen for the C228T mutation in the plasma samples from all patients. Briefly, blood samples were collected from all patients, and the plasma was immediately separated and stored at − 70 °C until further analyses. Cell–free DNA (cfDNA) was isolated from 500 µL of plasma using the phenol/chloroform method and eluted in 25 µL TE. Three microliters of cfDNA were then used for the detection of the C228T mutation as described above.

Statistical analysis

The data were analyzed using SPSS 26.0 software. Continuous variables were expressed as median and compared using Mann–Whitney U test or Kruskal–Wallis test. Categorical variables were compared using the chi–square test or Fisher’s exact test.

Overall survival (OS) was calculated according to the Kaplan–Meier method, and p values were calculated by log–rank tests to compare OS among patient subgroups. With the Kaplan-Meier approach, the survival probability (SP) is computed as below [33]:

Results

Patient baseline characteristics

The baseline clinical presentations of the 319 recruited HBV–infected patients are shown in Table 1. Most patients were male (81%). The median age of patients was greater in patients with later phase of liver disease progression. The levels of AST, total and direct bilirubin were higher in patients with LC compared to other subgroups (p < 0.0001). Albumin and prothrombin levels and blood cell counts were significantly lower in patients with LC than in the other patient groups (p < 0.0001). Compared with those in the CHB and LC groups, the AFP level and HBV loading were significantly higher in the HCC patients (p < 0.0001). The TERT promoter mutation (C228T) was found only in the HCC group (25.2%, 40/159 patients).

Circulating C228T mutation is associated with worsened liver function in HCC patients

The typical liver function parameters of the 159 recruited HCC patients are shown in Table 2. Although blood counts and alpha fetoprotein (AFP) levels were not significantly different between HCC patients carrying C228T and those without TERT promoter mutation, the AST, GGT, INR, total bilirubin and direct bilirubin levels were significantly higher in the HCC patients with the C228T. Conversely the prothrombin level decreased significantly in HCC patients carrying the circulating C228T. These data indicate that the global liver function of HCC patients carrying the C228T is worse than that of patients without the TERT promoter mutation.

We also classified the stages of 159 recruited HCC patients according to the Barcelona Clinic Liver Cancer (BCLC) classification system [30] and results show that C228T circulated more frequently in the later stages of HCC patients: 14.3% (11/77 of stages 0–A), 23.3% (7/30 of stage B), 40% (19/47 of stage C), and 60% (3/5 of stage D) (Fig. 1A). Alternatively, if the classification is based on the criteria of the American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) tumor/node/metastasis (TNM) staging system [28] (Fig. 1B), we also observed that late stage HCC patients who underwent TNM presented higher rates of C228T: 10.3% (3/29 of HCC stage 1), 18.6% (13/70 of HCC stage II), 34.3% (12/35 of stage III), and 48% (12/25 of stage IV). These data clearly demonstrated that the presence of circulating C228T was associated with increased liver invasion, elevated tumor burden, and advanced stages in HCC patients (Fig. 1).

Blood circulating C228T is associated with shorter overall survival

24 HCC patients or their families refused to provide survival data, hence we could analyze only the relationship between the C228T and overall survival (OS) for 125 out of 159 initially recruited HCC patients, who were treated with transarterial chemoembolization (TACE 49 patients), surgical resection (71 patients) or systemic chemotherapy (5 patients).

As shown in Fig. 2, the plotted Kaplan–Meier survival curve indicated that patients with C228T had worse OS than those without this factor. Indeed, the OS of all HCC patients carrying the blood circulating C228T was significantly lower than that of HCC patients without this factor [3-year survival probability (SP) was 33% vs. 67% for those without C228T; log–rank p = 0.005; hazard ratio (HR) = 2.33 (95% CI 1.26–4.32), p = 0.007; Table S1, Fig. 2A]. In more detail, we calculated and plotted the OS of sub–grouped HCC patients, and determined that HCC patients at BCLC stages O, A and B bearing blood–circulating C228T had lower OS than patients without TERT promoter mutation [3-year SP was 40% vs. 74% for those without C228T; log–rank p = 0.015; HR = 3.12 (95% CI 1.18–8.24), p = 0.022; Table S2, Fig. 2B]. Similarly, the OS curve of HCC patients at TNM stages I and II was lower than for those free of TERT promoter mutation [3-year SP was 38% vs. 79% for those without C228T; log–rank p < 0.001; HR = 6.32 (95% CI 2.39–16.75), p < 0.001; Table S3, Fig. 2C]. Additionally, the OS curve of HCC patients treated with surgical resection was also significantly lower than for patients free of TERT promoter mutation [3-year SP was 48% vs. 92% for those without C228T; log–rank p = 0.024; HR = 4.06 (95% CI 1.09–15.14), p = 0.037; Table S4, Fig. 2D]. Furthermore, multivariate analysis revealed that TERT C228T mutation was independently associated with the poor OS in early-stage HCC patients, as classified by BCLC (p = 0.044, Table S2) and TNM (p = 0.037, Table S3). However, no difference in OS was observed in the TACE or systemic chemotherapy HCC patient subgroups with and without TERT C228T mutation (data not shown).

Discussion

The human TERT gene encodes a catalytic subunit of telomerase, responsible for the maintenance of chromosomal integrity and genome stability [34]. During normal development, telomerase activity is subsequently inactivated during gestation and thereafter reactivated in various cancers including HCC [34]. This enzyme complex is necessary for the self–renewal or transformation of senescent cells into immortalized cells, as is often the case for tumor cells, and might be an important oncological biomarker as suggested previously [35].

Somatic mutations in the promoter region of the TERT gene have been reported in various cancers [16, 36–39], and in HCC, TERT mutations are the most frequent somatic genetic lesions described thus far [8, 12].

The prevalence of TERT gene promoter mutations across various phases of hepatocarcinogenesis was also reported via genetic analysis of tumor tissue. On the one hand, these findings provide a milestone for a deeper understanding of the genetic transformation from premalignant nodules in liver cirrhosis to malignant tumors or metastasis niches in HCC [8]. On the other hand, the results allow to consider the mutations of TERT gene promoter as novel biomarkers for the prognosis of HCC. However, the region flanking the TERT gene promoter mutation hotspot comprises highly GC–rich sequences, making it challenging to establish a direct assay with a robust and deep detection limit for the identification of the mentioned mutation from clinical samples with a limited amount of extractable input DNA, such as plasma. It is also likely that larger and more advanced tumors release higher amounts of circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA), which in turn would increase the sensitivity for detecting TERT promoter mutations. Therefore, the presence or absence of TERT promoter mutations in cfDNA may not directly reflect the intrinsic nature of the tumor itself but could correlate with tumor burden or stage at the time of sampling. These might be existing barriers to investigating the relationship between blood–circulating TERT promoter mutations and the clinicopathological presentation of HBV–infected HCC patients.

TERT promoter mutations have two hot spots C228T (− 124G > A) and C250T (− 146G > A), but more than 90% of the cases are C228T [12], whereas the clinical impact of C250T mutations remains unclear [40]. In our previous study, we could not detect any patient carrying C250T [31]. Therefore, in the present study, we re–optimized a novel nested real–time PCR assay to achieve a lower detection limit of 0.1% for direct identification of the TERT C228T promoter mutation in the peripheral blood of patients with HBV–related liver diseases. This simple approach is deployable as a routine test in diagnostic laboratories.

TERT C228T promoter mutation was detected only in HCC patients, with a mutation rate of 25% (40/159), and was not observed in the cirrhosis and chronic hepatitis groups (Table 1). This finding aligns with previous research using either tumor tissues or ctDNA from plasma samples [41–43]; for instance Yang X. et al. (2016) analyzed tumor tissues from 276 HBV-infected HCC patients and found TERT promoter mutations in 31% of samples, with ca. 99% being C228T mutations and only 0.2% C250T [44], whereas, Lee H.W. et al. (2017) used PCR on samples from 160 HCC patients and reported a 29% mutation rate, including 32 cases with C228T (20%) and 14 with C250T mutations [42]. In another study, Jang J.W. et al. (2021) examined 305 liver tissue samples from 205 patients and identified TERT promoter mutations in 28% of HCC cases, with 95% being C228T and 5% C250T [45]. Overall, our current data and previous studies confirm that C228T mutation is the main genetics lesion within the TERT gene promoter.

Our study revealed a greater prevalence of the TERT C228T mutation in HCC patients with a lower albumin concentration or increased direct bilirubin or in HCC patients with abdominal lymph node involvement or portal vein thrombosis than in those without the TERT C228T mutation, which implies potential associations between the TERT C228T mutation and more aggressive HCC phenotypes or advanced HCC stages and poorer prognosis as reported elsewhere [40, 42]. The data from our study also revealed a significant rate increment of TERT C228T promoter mutation for higher stages of HCC according to both the BCLC and TNM classification systems (p < 0.05), which is in agreement with previous data using tumor tissue analysis [8, 12] or plasma cfDNA samples [46] and that a previous multivariate analysis also confirmed TERT promoter mutation was an independent predictor of advanced BCLC stage (p = 0.032) [47].

Additionally, HCC patients carrying the TERT C228T mutation had shorter overall survival than did those without this factor, similar to previously reported data [40]; this includes the full HCC group and for HCC patients with BCLC stages O, A, B or TNM stages I, and II as well as surgically resected HCC patients. It is important to note that Karen Man-Fong Sze et al. reported that TERT–HBV integration alone did not reach statistical significance in association with shorter overall survival. In contrast, the presence of any TERT genomic alteration, either TERT–HBV integration or TERT promoter mutations, was significantly associated with more frequent venous invasion (p = 0.023) and more advanced tumor stage (p = 0.021), along with a trend toward shorter overall survival (p = 0.054). These findings suggest that TERT promoter mutations may contribute to tumor aggressiveness through mechanisms beyond the upregulation of TERT expression.

A key limitation of our current study is the lack of quantification of circulating TERT C228T promoter mutations. Consequently, it remains unclear whether the plasma levels of the TERT C228T promoter mutation are inversely proportional to the overall survival of HCC patients carrying the mutation, as suggested by previous research [40]. Another limitation is that most matched tumor tissue samples were not available in our study cohort, which limited our ability to perform a direct comparison.

Conclusion

The TERT C228T promoter mutation was detected in 25% of Vietnamese HCC patients but not in non–HCC patients (cirrhosis and chronic hepatitis B virus), and the presence of a blood–circulating TERT C228T promoter mutation was associated with increased liver invasion, elevated tumor burden advanced stages and shorter overall survival of HBV-related HCC patients. These results suggest the potential use of the circulating TERT C228T mutation as an independent factor for the prognosis of HBV-related HCC management.

Ultrametric spaces and clouds
I. N. Mikhailov

Abstract
In [ 6 ] , the natural way to construct an ultrametric space from a given metric space was presented. It was shown that the corresponding map U is 1 1 -Lipschitz for every pair of bounded metric spaces, with respect to the Gromov–Hausdorff distance. We make a simple observation that U is 1 1 -Lipschitz for pairs of all, not necessarily bounded, metric spaces. We then study the properties of the mapping U . We show that, for a given dotted connected metric space A A , the mapping Ψ : X ↦ X × A \Psi\colon X\mapsto X\times A from the proper class of all bounded ultrametric spaces ( X × A X\times A is endowed with the Manhattan metric) preserves the Gromov–Hausdorff distance. Moreover, the mapping U is inverse to Ψ \Psi . By a dotted connected metric space, we mean a metric space in which for an arbitrary ε > 0 \varepsilon>0 and every two points p , q p,\,q , there exist points x 0 = p , x 1 , … , x n = q x_{0}=p,\,x_{1},\,\ldots,\,x_{n}=q such that max 0 ≤ j ≤ n − 1 ⁡ | x j ​ x j + 1 | ≤ ε \max_{0\leq j\leq n-1}|x_{j}x_{j+1}|\leq\varepsilon . At the end of the paper, we prove that each class (proper or not) consisting of unbounded metric spaces on finite Gromov–Hausdorff distances from each other cannot contain an ultrametric space and a dotted connected space simultaneously. Keywords : ultrametric space, Gromov-Hausdorff distance.

Introduction

In the present paper we continue the investigation of the classical Gromov–Hausdorff distance geometry. Traditionally, this distance appears in the studies of compact metric spaces. The space of isometry classes of compact metric spaces, called
the Gromov–Hausdorff class
, is well studied (
[
5
]
,
[
7
]
,
[
8
]
) and possesses wonderful geometric properties.

In case of unbounded metric spaces, the usual Gromov–Hausdorff distance sometimes does not reflect natural properties. For example, a sequence of balls in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
with radii tending to infinity lies on infinite Gromov–Hausdorff distance from
\mathbb{R}^{2}
itself. To deal with such situations,
the pointed Gromov–Hausdorff convergence
was introduced in case of unbounded metric spaces (
[
5
]
,
[
9
]
).

However, in the recent works (
[
4
]
,
[
1
]
,
[
3
]
,
[
2
]
,
[
11
]
,
[
12
]
) the classical Gromov–Hausdorff distance was studied without restriction on compactness of arising metric spaces. In
[
1
]
, the authors developed techniques that allow to work with the proper class
\mathcal{GH}
of all metric spaces considered up to an isometry equipped with the Gromov–Hausdorff distance. That approach is based on von Neumann–Bernays–Gödel set theory. In NBG theory all objects are called
classes
that belong to one
of two types: sets and proper classes. A class is called a
set
if it belongs to some other class. If the class is not a member of any other class, then it is called
proper
.

In
[
8
]
, M. Gromov described some properties of the proper class
\mathcal{GH}
. For example, M. Gromov suggested to study the subclasses of
\mathcal{GH}
that consist of metric spaces on finite Gromov–Hausdorff distances from each other. Following
[
1
]
, we call such subclasses
clouds
. M. Gromov announced in
[
8
]
that all clouds are complete and contractible. In
[
1
]
, the proof of the first statement was provided, while the second statement turned out to be rather non-trivial. Also some set theoretic issues arised. It was shown by B. Nesterov (private conversations) that clouds are proper classes. Therefore, it is impossible to introduce topology on a proper class. Indeed, a proper class can not belong to any other class by definition, but each topological space is an element of its own topology. In
[
1
]
, the authors showed how to avoid such issues, and introduced an analogue of topology on so-called set-filtered classes. That technique allows to define continuous mappings between clouds.

In the present paper, we investigate the geometry of clouds. However, for the sake of simplicity, we avoid working with the set-filtered classes defined in
[
1
]
, and formulate all properties of mappings between clouds and particular subclasses of clouds in terms of metric defined by the Gromov–Hausdorff distance.

In
[
6
]
, a natural way to construct an ultrametric space from a given metric space was presented. This construction provides one of few non-trivial lower estimates on the Gromov–Hausdorff distance between bounded metric spaces (
[
13
]
). This estimate was applied effectively to the problem of calculating Gromov–Hausdorff distances between vertex sets of regular polygons inscribed in a single circle (
[
14
]
). We start with a simple generalization of this estimate to the case of unbounded metric spaces.
It follows from our generalization that the assignment to an arbitrary metric space of an ultrametric space from the mentioned construction induces a
1
-Lipschitz mapping
U
between the corresponding clouds. We then proceed to study simple properties of the mapping
U
. At first, we consider a natural class of
fair
metrics
\rho
on the Cartesian product
X\times Y
of metric spaces
(X,\,\operatorname{d}_{X})
and
(Y,\,\operatorname{d}_{Y})
such that restrictions of
\rho
on
X\times\{y\}
and
\{x\}\times Y
coincide with
\operatorname{d}_{X}
and
\operatorname{d}_{Y}
, respectively, for all
x\in X
,
y\in Y
. We establish the equality
\textbf{U}(X\times_{\rho}Y)=\textbf{U}(X)\times_{\ell^{\infty}}\textbf{U}(Y)
, for an arbitrary fair metric
\rho
on
X\times Y
which is bounded from below by the standard
\ell^{\infty}
-metric on
X\times Y
. That is, for arbitrary
p=(x,\,y)
,
p^{\prime}=(x^{\prime},\,y^{\prime})
from
X\times Y
,

\rho(p,\,p^{\prime})\geq\operatorname{d}_{X\times_{\ell^{\infty}}Y}(p,\,p^{\prime}):=\max\bigl\{\operatorname{d}_{X}(x,\,x^{\prime}),\,\operatorname{d}_{Y}(y,\,y^{\prime})\bigr\}.

Secondly, we consider the cloud
[\Delta_{1}]
of all bounded metric spaces and prove that its subclass
\operatorname{Ult}
consisting of all bounded ultrametric spaces is closed. Then we recall the well-known class of metric spaces such that, for an arbitrary
\varepsilon>0
every and two points
p,\,q
, there exist points
x_{0}=p,\,x_{1},\,\ldots,\,x_{n}=q
such that
\max_{0\leq j\leq n-1}|x_{j}x_{j+1}|\leq\varepsilon
. We call them
dotted connected
metric spaces. We show that, for a given dotted connected metric space
A
, the mapping
\Psi\colon X\mapsto X\times A
from the proper class of all bounded ultrametric spaces
\operatorname{Ult}
(
X\times A
is endowed with the Manhattan or
\ell_{1}
metric) preserves the Gromov–Hausdorff distance. Moreover, the mapping
U
is inverse to
\Psi
. At the end of the paper, we show that a cloud consisting of unbounded metric spaces cannot contain both an ultrametric space and a dotted connected space.

Preliminaries

For an arbitrary metric space
X

For non-empty subsets

.

Let

Definition 2.2
.

Let

and

be metric spaces. If

(X,\,Y)
.

Definition 2.3
.

The Gromov–Hausdorff distance

,

is the infinum of positive numbers

such that there exists a metric realization
(X^{\prime},\,Y^{\prime},\,Z)
of the pair

\operatorname{d}_{H}(X^{\prime},\,Y^{\prime})\leq r
.

Let
X
and
Y
be non-empty sets. Recall that any subset

is called a

between
X
and
Y
. Denote the set of all non-empty relations between
X
and

by
\mathcal{P}_{0}(X,\,Y)
. We put

\pi_{X}\colon X\times Y\to X,\;\pi_{X}(x,\,y)=x,

\pi_{Y}\colon X\times Y\to Y,\;\pi_{Y}(x,\,y)=y.

Definition 2.4
.

A relation

is called a

if
\pi_{X}|_{R}
and
\pi_{Y}|_{R}
are surjective. In other words, correspondences are multivalued surjective mappings. Denote the set of all correspondences between

and

.

Definition 2.5
.

For an arbitrary correspondence
R\in\mathcal{R}(X,\,Y)
, define

as follows

R^{-1}=\bigl\{(y,\,x)\colon(x,\,y)\in R\bigr\}.

Definition 2.6
.

Let

,
Y
be arbitrary metric spaces. Then for every

,
the distortion of

is defined as

\operatorname{dis}\sigma=\sup\Bigl\{\bigl||xx^{\prime}|-|yy^{\prime}|\bigr|:(x,\,y),\,(x^{\prime},\,y^{\prime})\in\sigma\Bigr\}.

For arbitrary metric spaces

.

Suppose

2.1
Clouds

Denote by
\mathcal{VGH}
the class consisting of all non-empty metric spaces equipped with the Gromov–Hausdorff distance.

We recall the following terminology from
[
1
]
:

•

A distance function
on a proper class
\mathcal{A}
is an arbitrary mapping
\rho\colon\mathcal{A}\times\mathcal{A}\to[0,\,\infty]
, for which always
\rho(x,\,x)=0
and
\rho(x,\,y)=\rho(y,\,x)
.

•

If the distance function satisfies the triangle inequality, i.e., if
\rho(x,\,z)\leq\rho(x,\,y)+\rho(y,\,z)
is always satisfied, then
\rho
is called
generalized pseudometric
(the word ‘‘generalized’’ correspond to the possibility of taking the value
\infty
).

•

If the positive definiteness condition is additionally satisfied for a generalized pseudometric, i.e., if
\rho(x,\,y)=0
always implies
x=y
, then we call such
\rho

a generalized metric
.

•

Finally, if
\rho
does not take value, then in the above conditions we will
omit the word ‘‘generalized’’.

Theorem 2.1

(
[
5
]
)
.

The Gromov–Hausdorff distance is a generalized pseudometric vanishing on each pair of isometric spaces.

The class
\mathcal{GH}_{0}
is obtained from
\mathcal{VGH}
by factorization over the zero-value equivalence for which
X
is equivalent to
Y
, if and only if
\operatorname{d}_{GH}(X,\,Y)=0
.

Definition 2.8
.

Consider the following natural equivalence relation
\sim_{1}
on
\mathcal{GH}_{0}
:
X\sim_{1}Y
, if and only if
\operatorname{d}_{GH}(X,\,Y)<\infty
. The corresponding equivalence classes are called

.

If
X
is a metric space, we denote the corresponding cloud by
[X]
. By
\Delta_{1}
we denote a metric space that consists of one point. Therefore,
[\Delta_{1}]
is the cloud consisting of all bounded metric spaces.

Definition 2.9
.

By
\operatorname{Ult}
we denote the subclass of
[\Delta_{1}]
consisting of all classes of metric spaces on the zero Gromov–Hausdorff distance from some bounded ultrametric space.

Definition 2.10
.

The subclass
A
of a cloud
[X]
is called

, if and only if, for every
Y\in[X]\backslash A
, there exists
r>0
such that
U_{r}(Y)\subset[X]\backslash A
.

2.2
Ultrametrization mapping and dotted connected metric spaces

Definition 2.11
.

A metric space
(X,\,\operatorname{d}_{X})
is called
ultrametric
, if and only if for all
x,\,y,\,z\in X
, the strengthened ultrametric triangle inequality holds, namely,
\operatorname{d}_{X}(x,\,z)\leq\max\bigl\{\operatorname{d}_{X}(x,\,y),\,\operatorname{d}_{X}(y,\,z)\bigr\}
.

In the paper
[
6
]
the following construction was first considered

Definition 2.12
.

For a metric space
(X,\,\operatorname{d}_{X})
, consider the pseudo ultrametric space
(X,\,u_{X})
where
u_{X}\colon X\times X\to\mathbb{R}
is defined by

(x,\,x^{\prime})\to u_{X}(x,\,x^{\prime}):=\inf\left\{\max_{0\leq i\leq n-1}\operatorname{d}_{X}(x_{i},\,x_{i+1})\colon x=x_{0},\,\ldots,\,x_{n}=x^{\prime}\,\text{for some}\;n\geq 1\right\}.

Now, define
U
(X)
to be the quotient metric space induced by
(X,\,u_{X})
under the equivalence:
x\sim x^{\prime}
if and only if
u_{X}(x,\,x^{\prime})=0
.

The following result was first introduced and proved in
[
6
]
for finite metric spaces, and then formulated in
[
13
]
for bounded metric spaces.

Theorem 2.2

(
[
6
]
,
[
13
]
)
.

For all bounded metric spaces
X
and
Y
, the following inequality holds

\operatorname{d}_{GH}(X,\,Y)\geq\operatorname{d}_{GH}\bigl(\textbf{\emph{U}}(X),\,\textbf{\emph{U}}(Y)\bigr).

Definition 2.13
.

We call a metric space
X

dotted connected
if, for arbitrary points
x,\,x^{\prime}\in X
and for arbitrary
\varepsilon>0
, there exist points
x_{0}=x,\,x_{1},\,\ldots,\,x_{n}=x^{\prime}
in
X
such that

\max_{0\leq i\leq n-1}|x_{j}x_{j+1}|\leq\varepsilon.

Example 2.1
.

Note that every path-connected metric space
X
is dotted connected.

Proof.

Indeed, take arbitrary
x,\,x^{\prime}\in X
.
Since
X
is path-connected, there exists a continuous mapping
\gamma\colon[0,\,1]\to X,\,\gamma(0)=x,\,\gamma(1)=x^{\prime}
. Since
\gamma
is continuous,
[0,\,1]
is compact,
\gamma
is also uniformly continuous. Hence, for an arbitrary
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
\delta>0
such that
|\gamma(t)\gamma(t^{\prime})|\leq\varepsilon
if
|tt^{\prime}|\leq\delta
. Choose
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\frac{1}{N}<|\delta|
. Then let
x_{0}=x,\,x_{1}=\gamma(\frac{1}{N}),\,\ldots,\,x_{N}=x^{\prime}
. By construction,
|x_{j}x_{j+1}|\leq\varepsilon
, for all
j=0,\,1,\,\ldots,\,N-1
.
∎

Remark 2.1
.

We need the following simple observation: for an arbitrary dotted connected metric space
X
, we have
\textbf{U}(X)=\Delta_{1}
.

2.3
Kuratowski embedding

Let
X
be an arbitrary metric space. By
C_{b}(X)
we denote the Banach space of all bounded continuous real-valued functions on
X
, endowed with the
\sup
-norm.

Theorem 2.3

(
[
10
]
)
.

For an arbitrary metric space
X
and a fixed point
x_{0}\in X
, the mapping

\Phi\colon X\to C_{b}(X),\;\Phi(x)(y)=\operatorname{d}_{X}(x,\,y)-\operatorname{d}_{X}(x_{0},\,y)\quad\forall\,x,\,y\in X

is isometric.

Construction 2.1
.

Consider an arbitrary metric space
X
and an arbitrary
t\geq 0
. Let
\Phi\colon X\to C_{b}(X)
be the Kuratowski embedding. We add to
\Phi(X)
all the segments in
C_{b}(X)
with endpoints
\Phi(x)
,
\Phi(y)
such that
\operatorname{d}_{X}(x,\,y)\leq t
. By
D_{t}(X)
we denote the resulting subset of
C_{b}(X)
endowed with the induced metric.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
X
be a metric space.
(i)
If
\operatorname{diam}\emph{\textbf{U}}(X)<c<\infty
, then the metric space
D_{c}(X)
is path-connected;
(ii)
For an arbitrary
t\geq 0
the following inequality holds
\operatorname{d}_{GH}\bigl(X,\,D_{t}(X)\bigr)\leq\frac{t}{2}
.

Proof.

(i)
Let
\Phi\colon X\to C_{b}(X)
be the Kuratowski embedding. Since every point from
D_{c}(X)\backslash\Phi(X)
belongs to a segment with endpoints in
\Phi(X)
, it suffices to show that every pair of points
p,\,q\in\Phi(X)
can be connected by a polygonal line that lies in
D_{c}(X)
with all its edges. Since
\operatorname{diam}\textbf{U}(X)<c
, it follows that there exist
x_{0}=\Phi^{-1}(p),\,x_{1},\,\ldots,\,x_{n}=\Phi^{-1}(q)
such that

\max_{0\leq j\leq n-1}\operatorname{d}_{X}(x_{j},\,x_{j+1})\leq c.

Since Kuratowski embedding is isometric, we have

\operatorname{d}_{C_{b}(X)}\bigl(\Phi(x_{j}),\,\Phi(x_{j+1})\bigr)=\operatorname{d}_{X}(x_{j},\,x_{j+1}).

Therefore, by construction of
D_{c}(X)
, there is a polygonal line in
D_{c}(X)
with vertices
\Phi(x_{0})=p,\,\Phi(x_{1}),\,\ldots,\,\Phi(x_{n})=q
that connects
\Phi(x_{0})
with
\Phi(x_{n})
and belongs to
D_{c}(X)
with all its edges.

(ii)
Consider the following correspondence
R
between
X
and
D_{t}(X)
:

R=\Bigl\{(x,\,q)\colon\operatorname{d}_{C_{b}(X)}\bigl(\Phi(x),\,q\bigr)\leq\frac{t}{2},\;x\in X,\,q\in D_{t}(X)\Bigr\}.

For any
(x,\,q)
,
(x^{\prime},\,q^{\prime})\in R
, by triangle inequality, we have

\bigl|\operatorname{d}_{X}(x,\,x^{\prime})-\operatorname{d}_{D_{t}(X)}(q,\,q^{\prime})\bigr|=\bigl|\operatorname{d}_{D_{t}(X)}\bigl(\Phi(x),\,\Phi(x^{\prime})\bigr)-\operatorname{d}_{D_{t}(X)}(q,\,q^{\prime})\bigr|\leq\\
\leq\operatorname{d}_{D_{t}(X)}\bigl(\Phi(x),\,q\bigr)+\operatorname{d}_{D_{t}(X)}\bigl(\Phi(x^{\prime}),\,q^{\prime}\bigr)\leq t.

Therefore,
\operatorname{dis}R\leq t
. Claim
2.1
implies that
\operatorname{d}_{GH}\bigl(X,\,D_{t}(X)\bigr)\leq\frac{t}{2}
.
∎

3
Main results

We start with a simple observation that Theorem
2.2
can be easily generalized to the case of arbitrary metric spaces with a proof nearly identical to the one for finite metric spaces from
[
6
]
.

Theorem 3.1
.

For all metric spaces
X
and
Y
, the following inequality holds

\operatorname{d}_{GH}(X,\,Y)\geq\operatorname{d}_{GH}\bigl(\textbf{\emph{U}}(X),\,\textbf{\emph{U}}(Y)\bigr).

Proof.

Let
R\subset X\times Y
be a correspondence, such that
\operatorname{dis}R=c\leq 2\operatorname{d}_{GH}(X,\,Y)+\varepsilon
. Choose arbitrary
(x,\,y),\,(x^{\prime},\,y^{\prime})\in R
. For every
\varepsilon^{\prime}>0
, there exist points
x_{0}=x,\,\ldots,\,x_{n}=x^{\prime}
in
X
such that
\max_{0\leq i\leq n-1}|x_{i}x_{i+1}|\leq u_{X}(x,\,x^{\prime})+\varepsilon^{\prime}
. Now let points
y_{0}=y,\,y_{1},\,\ldots,\,y_{n}=y^{\prime}
be such that
(x_{j},\,y_{j})\in R
for all
1\leq j\leq n-1
. Then, by definition of distortion, for all
j=0,\,1,\,\ldots,\,n-1
, the inequalities hold

|y_{j}y_{j+1}|\leq|x_{j}x_{j+1}|+c\leq u_{X}(x,\,x^{\prime})+c+\varepsilon^{\prime}.

Hence,
u_{Y}(y,\,y^{\prime})\leq u_{X}(x,\,x^{\prime})+c+\varepsilon^{\prime}
. Since
\varepsilon^{\prime}
is arbitrary, we conclude that
u_{Y}(y,\,y^{\prime})\leq u_{X}(x,\,x^{\prime})+c
. Applying the same argument to
R^{-1}\subset Y\times X
, we obtain
u_{X}(x,\,x^{\prime})\leq u_{Y}(y,\,y^{\prime})+c
. Thus,
\bigl|u_{X}(x,\,x^{\prime})-u_{Y}(y,\,y^{\prime})\bigr|\leq c
. It means that, for a correspondence
R
between
(X,\,u_{X})
and
(Y,\,u_{Y})
, its distortion does not exceed the value
c
and, therefore, the value
2\operatorname{d}_{GH}(X,\,Y)+\varepsilon
. Since
\varepsilon
is arbitrary, by Claim
2.1
we obtain that
\operatorname{d}_{GH}\bigl(\textbf{U}(X),\,\textbf{U}(Y)\bigr)\leq\operatorname{d}_{GH}(X,\,Y)
.
∎

Corollary 3.1
.

The mapping
X\mapsto\emph{\textbf{U}}(X)
induces a mapping of clouds
[X]\to[\emph{\textbf{U}}(X)]
.

Now we list a few simple properties of the mapping
U
.

Theorem 3.2
.

(i)
For an arbitrary ultrametric space
Y
, we have
\emph{\textbf{U}}(Y)=Y
.

(ii)
Let
(X,\,\operatorname{d}_{X})
and
(Y,\,\operatorname{d}_{Y})
be arbitrary metric spaces. Let
\rho
be a fair metric on
X\times Y
such that, for all
p=(x,\,y),\,p^{\prime}=(x^{\prime},\,y^{\prime})\in X\times Y
,

\rho(p,\,p^{\prime})\geq\max\bigl\{\operatorname{d}_{X}(x,\,x^{\prime}),\,\operatorname{d}_{Y}(y,\,y^{\prime})\bigr\}.

Then
\emph{\textbf{U}}\bigl(X\times_{\rho}Y\bigr)=\emph{\textbf{U}}(X)\times_{\ell^{\infty}}\emph{\textbf{U}}(Y)
.

Proof.

(i)
Take arbitrary
x,\,x^{\prime}\in Y
. We have to show that
u_{Y}(x,\,x^{\prime})=|xx^{\prime}|
. Since
u_{Y}(x,\,x^{\prime})\leq|xx^{\prime}|
, it suffices to prove that, for arbitrary
x_{0}=x,\,x_{1},\,\ldots,\,x_{n}=x^{\prime}
, we have
\max_{0\leq j\leq n-1}|x_{j}x_{j+1}|\geq|xx^{\prime}|
. We proceed by induction. For
n=1
, the statement turns into an identity. Let us prove the induction step. By the ultrametric triangle inequality,
\max\bigl\{|x_{n-2}x_{n-1}|,\,|x_{n-1}x_{n}|\bigr\}\geq|x_{n-2}x_{n}|
. Hence,

\max_{0\leq j\leq n-1}|x_{j}x_{j+1}|\geq\max\Bigl\{\max_{0\leq j\leq n-3}|x_{j}x_{j+1}|,\,|x_{n-2}x_{n}|\Bigr\}\geq|xx^{\prime}|,

where the latter inequality is true by induction.

(ii)
Choose
p=(x,\,y),\,p^{\prime}=(x^{\prime},\,y^{\prime})\in X\times Y
. We have to show that

u_{X\times_{\rho}Y}(p,\,p^{\prime})=\max\bigl\{u_{X}(x,\,x^{\prime}),\,u_{Y}(y,\,y^{\prime})\bigr\}.

For
\varepsilon>0
, choose
p_{0}=(x_{0},\,y_{0})=(x,\,y),\,p_{1}=(x_{1},\,y_{1}),\,\ldots,\,p_{n}=(x_{n},\,y_{n})=(x^{\prime},\,y^{\prime})
such that

\max_{0\leq j\leq n-1}\rho(p_{j},\,p_{j+1})\leq u_{X\times_{\rho}Y}(p,\,p^{\prime})+\varepsilon.

Since
\rho(p_{j},\,p_{j+1})\geq\max\bigl\{\operatorname{d}_{X}(x_{j},\,x_{j+1}),\,\operatorname{d}_{Y}(y_{j},\,y_{j+1})\bigr\}
, we have

\displaystyle\max_{0\leq j\leq n-1}\operatorname{d}_{X}(x_{j},\,x_{j+1})\leq\max_{0\leq j\leq n-1}\rho(p_{j},\,p_{j+1})\leq u_{X\times_{\rho}Y}(p,\,p^{\prime})+\varepsilon,

\displaystyle\max_{0\leq j\leq n-1}\operatorname{d}_{Y}(y_{j},\,y_{j+1})\leq\max_{0\leq j\leq n-1}\rho(p_{j},\,p_{j+1})\leq u_{X\times_{\rho}Y}(p,\,p^{\prime})+\varepsilon.

Therefore,

\displaystyle u_{X}(x,\,x^{\prime})\leq u_{X\times_{\rho}Y}(p,\,p^{\prime})+\varepsilon,

\displaystyle u_{Y}(y,\,y^{\prime})\leq u_{X\times_{\rho}Y}(p,\,p^{\prime})+\varepsilon.

Since
\varepsilon>0
is arbitrary, by tending
\varepsilon
to
0
we obtain

\max\bigl\{u_{X}(x,\,x^{\prime}),\,u_{Y}(y,\,y^{\prime})\bigr\}\leq u_{X\times_{\rho}Y}(p,\,p^{\prime}).

Now we will prove the opposite inequality.

Choose
\varepsilon>0
,
x_{0}=x,\,x_{1},\,\ldots,\,x_{n}=x^{\prime}
and
y_{0}=y,\,y_{1},\,\ldots,\,y_{m}=y^{\prime}
such that

\max_{0\leq j\leq n-1}\operatorname{d}_{X}(x_{j},\,x_{j+1})\leq u_{X}(x,\,x^{\prime})+\varepsilon,

\max_{0\leq j\leq m-1}\operatorname{d}_{Y}(y_{j},\,y_{j+1})\leq u_{Y}(y,\,y^{\prime})+\varepsilon.

Without loss of generality suppose that
n>m
. In this case we put
y_{m+1}=\ldots=y_{n}=y^{\prime}
.

Now consider the following polygonal line in
X\times Y
:

\displaystyle p_{0}

\displaystyle=(x_{0},\,y_{0}),\;p_{1}=(x_{0},\,y_{1}),\;p_{2}=(x_{1},\,y_{1}),\;\ldots,p_{2i}=(x_{i},\,y_{i}),\;p_{2i+1}=(x_{i},\,y_{i+1}),

\displaystyle\ldots,\;p_{2n-1}=(x_{n-1},\,y_{n}),\;p_{2n}=(x_{n},\,y_{n}).

Since metric
\rho
on
X\times Y
is fair, we observe that

\rho(p_{j},\,p_{j+1})=\begin{cases}\operatorname{d}_{X}(x_{k},\,x_{k+1})\;\text{if}\;j=2k+1,\,k=0,\,\ldots,\,n-1\\
\operatorname{d}_{Y}(y_{k},\,y_{k+1})\;\text{if}\;j=2k,\,k=0,\,\ldots,\,n-1.\end{cases}

Therefore, by definition of
u_{X\times_{\rho}Y}
, we have

u_{X\times_{\rho}Y}(p,\,p^{\prime})\leq\max_{0\leq j\leq 2n-1}\rho(p_{j},\,p_{j+1})\leq\\
\leq\max\Bigl\{\max_{0\leq k\leq n-1}\operatorname{d}_{X}(x_{k},\,x_{k+1}),\,\max_{0\leq k\leq n-1}\operatorname{d}_{Y}(y_{k},\,y_{k+1})\Bigr\}\leq\\
\leq\max\bigl\{u_{X}(x,\,x^{\prime}),\,u_{Y}(y,\,y^{\prime})\bigr\}+\varepsilon.

Since
\varepsilon>0
is arbitrary, by tending
\varepsilon
to
0
we obtain the required inequality

u_{X\times_{\rho}Y}(p,\,p^{\prime})\leq\max\bigl\{u_{X}(x,\,x^{\prime}),\,u_{Y}(y,\,y^{\prime})\bigr\}.

∎

Corollary 3.2
.

For an arbitrary dotted connected metric space
A
and an arbitrary metric space
X
, we have
\emph{\textbf{U}}(X\times A)=\emph{\textbf{U}}(X)
.

Lemma 3.1
.

For an arbitrary metric space
A
, the mapping
p\colon X\mapsto X\times A,\,X\in[\Delta_{1}]
is a
1
-Lipschitz mapping from
[\Delta_{1}]
to
[A]
.

Proof.

Since
\operatorname{diam}X<\infty
for all
X\in[\Delta_{1}]
, it follows that
\operatorname{d}_{GH}(X\times A,\,A)<\infty
. Thus,
p
maps
[\Delta_{1}]
into some subclass of
[A]
.

Let
X,\,Y\in[\Delta_{1}]
,
\operatorname{d}_{GH}(X,\,Y)=c
. By Claim
2.1
, for an arbitrary
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a correspondence
R\subset X\times Y
such that
\operatorname{dis}R\leq 2c+\varepsilon
. Consider a correspondence

S=\Bigl\{\bigl((x,\,a),\,(y,\,a)\bigr)\colon(x,\,y)\in R,\;a\in A\Bigr\}

between
X\times A
and
Y\times A
. Its distortion equals
\operatorname{dis}R
. Hence,
2\operatorname{d}_{GH}(X\times A,\,Y\times A)\leq 2c+\varepsilon
for all
\varepsilon>0
. Thus,
\operatorname{d}_{GH}(X\times A,\,Y\times A)\leq\operatorname{d}_{GH}(X,\,Y)
.
∎

Theorem 3.3
.

(i)
The subclass
\operatorname{Ult}\subset[\Delta_{1}]
is closed.

(ii)
For an arbitrary dotted connected metric space
A
, the restriction to
\operatorname{Ult}
of the mapping
p\colon[\Delta_{1}]\to[A],\;X\mapsto X\times A
from Lemma
3.1
is isometric.

Proof.

(i)
Take an arbitrary bounded metric space
X
that is not ultrametric. It contains points
x,\,y,\,z
such that
|xz|>\max\bigl\{|xy|,\,|yz|\bigr\}
. Let
t=|xz|-\max\bigl\{|xy|,\,|yz|\bigr\}
.

It suffices to prove that
U_{t/4}(X)\subset[\Delta_{1}]\backslash\operatorname{Ult}
.

Take an arbitrary bounded metric space
Y
such that
\operatorname{d}_{GH}(Y,\,X)<\frac{t}{4}
. By Claim
2.1
, there exists a correspondence
R\subset X\times Y
such that
\operatorname{dis}R<\frac{t}{2}
. Choose
x^{\prime}\in R(x),\,y^{\prime}\in R(y),\,z^{\prime}\in R(z)
. Then

|x^{\prime}z^{\prime}|>|xz|-\frac{t}{2}=\max\bigl\{|xy|,\,|yz|\bigr\}+\frac{t}{2}>\max\bigl\{|x^{\prime}y^{\prime}|,\,|y^{\prime}z^{\prime}|\bigr\}.

Therefore,
Y\in[\Delta_{1}]\backslash\operatorname{Ult}
. Hence,
\operatorname{Ult}
is closed in
[\Delta_{1}]
by definition.

(ii)
Take arbitrary ultrametric spaces
U,\,U^{\prime}\in[\Delta_{1}]
. Since
A
is dotted connected,
by Theorem
3.2
,
we have
\textbf{U}(U)=\textbf{U}(A\times U)
,
\textbf{U}(U^{\prime})=\textbf{U}(A\times U^{\prime})
. By Lemma
3.1
and Theorem
3.1
, we obtain

\operatorname{d}_{GH}(U,\,U^{\prime})\geq\operatorname{d}_{GH}(A\times U,\,A\times U^{\prime})\geq\operatorname{d}_{GH}\bigl(\textbf{U}(A\times U),\,\textbf{U}(A\times U^{\prime})\bigr)=\operatorname{d}_{GH}(U,\,U^{\prime}).

Therefore,
\operatorname{d}_{GH}(U,\,U^{\prime})=\operatorname{d}_{GH}(A\times U,\,A\times U^{\prime})
.

∎

Theorem 3.4
.

(i)
If a cloud
[X]
contains a dotted connected metric space
A
, then for every
B\in[X]
it holds
\operatorname{diam}\emph{\textbf{U}}(B)<\infty
.

(ii)
If there is a metric space
A\in[X]
such that
\operatorname{diam}\emph{\textbf{U}}(A)<\infty
, then there exists a path-connected metric space in
[X]
.

Proof.

(i)
By Remark
2.1
,

\textbf{U}(A)=\Delta_{1}
. Hence, by Corollary
3.1
,
\textbf{U}\bigl([X]\bigr)\subset[\Delta_{1}]
.

(ii)
Let
c=\operatorname{diam}\textbf{U}(A)
. According to Lemma
2.1
,
G=D_{c}(A)
possesses all the required properties.

∎

Corollary 3.3
.

(i)
If a cloud
[X]
contains an ultrametric space, then
\emph{\textbf{U}}\bigl([X]\bigr)\subset[X]
.

(ii)
If a cloud
[X]
consists of unbounded metric spaces and contains a dotted connected metric space, then
[X]
does not contain any ultrametric spaces.

(iii)
If a cloud
[X]
contains an unbounded ultrametric space, then there are not any dotted connected metric spaces in
[X]
.

Proof.

(i)
Let
Y\in[X]
be an ultrametric space. By Theorem
3.2
,
\textbf{U}(Y)=Y
. Hence, by Corollary
3.1
,
\textbf{U}\bigl([X]\bigr)\subset[X]
.

(ii)
Suppose
[X]
contains an ultrametric space. Then, by
(i)
,
\textbf{U}\bigl([X]\bigr)\subset[X]
. However, by Theorem
3.4
,
\textbf{U}\bigl([X]\bigr)\subset[\Delta_{1}]
. Since
X
is unbounded, that is a contradiction.

(iii)
If
[X]
contains a dotted connected space, by Theorem
3.4
, we have
\textbf{U}\bigl([X]\bigr)\subset[\Delta_{1}]
. However, by
(i)
,
\textbf{U}\bigl([X]\bigr)\subset[X]
. Since
X
contains an unbounded metric space, that is a contradiction.
∎

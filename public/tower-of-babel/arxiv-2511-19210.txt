SL(2N,C) Hyperunification:
Dynamical Tetrads, Induced Gravity, and Composite Families

Abstract




A four-dimensional gauge–gravity unification based on the local
SL(2N,\mathbb{C})

symmetry is developed in a universal Yang–Mills type setting. The accompanying tetrads are promoted to dynamical fields whose invertibility condition is interpreted as a nonlinear sigma-model type length constraint. This triggers tetrad condensation and spontaneously breaks
SL(2N,\mathbb{C})

down to
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})\times\mathrm{SU}(N)
, effectively filtering out unobserved
non-compact directions. A special ghost-free curvature-squared Lagrangian provides a consistent quadratic sector for all gauge fields involved, while an Einstein–Cartan linear curvature term is shown to arise radiatively from fermion loops. The matter sector clearly points to a deeper elementarity of
SL(2N,\mathbb{C})
spinors, which can be identified with preon
constituents whose bound states form the observed quarks and leptons. Anomaly matching
between preons and composites singles out
SL(16,\mathbb{C})
, accommodating precisely three
composite quark-lepton families. The theory links noncompact unification, induced gravity,
and fermion family structure in a single framework.

I
Introduction

Gauge formulations of gravity, starting from Utiyama and Kibble, place local
Lorentz symmetry on the same footing as internal gauge symmetries by
promoting the spin connection to a gauge field
Utiyama
;
Kibble
. This
invites the idea that spacetime and internal interactions may be unified in
a larger local group whose representations act on spinors, rather than on
vectors in an extended spacetime. A natural candidate is a local
\mathrm{SL}(2N,\mathbb{C})
symmetry, where the spinorial
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})

of gravity is enlarged by an internal
\mathrm{SU}(N)
sector in a common
noncompact setting, as in early work by Isham, Salam and Strathdee
ish
;
ish1
and more recent hyperunified proposals
cham
;
jpl
.

Such programs face several persistent obstacles. The full
\mathrm{SL}(2N,\mathbb{C})
gauge multiplet contains vector, axial-vector and tensor
submultiplets. Experiment only requires a compact internal vector sector;
axial partners and extra hyperflavored tensors must be decoupled. Quadratic
curvature terms needed for a Yang–Mills-like description typically
introduce ghosts unless a special combination of curvature invariants is
chosen
nev
;
nev1
. Matter multiplets constructed directly in
\mathrm{SL}(2N,\mathbb{C})
tend to be huge, with many multi-spin states that do not
resemble observed quarks and leptons, unless some deeper compositeness is
invoked. Finally, the Coleman–Mandula theorem severely constrains
nontrivial Lie-algebraic unification of spacetime and internal symmetries

CM
.

In this Letter we show that these obstacles can be addressed in a unified
way if the tetrad is treated as a genuine dynamical field whose
invertibility plays the role of a nonlinear sigma-model constraint. The key
points are: (i) a dynamical
\mathrm{SL}(2N,\mathbb{C})
tetrad with fixed
length condenses and spontaneously breaks the symmetry down to
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})\times\mathrm{SU}(N)
, lifting all noncompact directions;
(ii) in the quadratic sector, a Neville-type ghost-free curvature-squared
Lagrangian can be embedded into
\mathrm{SL}(2N,\mathbb{C})
, giving a
healthy spectrum for the tensor submultiplet; (iii) the usual
Einstein–Cartan linear curvature term need not be fundamental: it can be
induced at one loop from fermion bubbles that couple simultaneously to the
tetrad and the tensor connection, in the spirit of Sakharov’s induced
gravity
Sakharov
;
Adler
;
Zee
;
Donoghue
; (iv) when
\mathrm{SL}(2N,\mathbb{C})
acts on preons in fundamental spinor representations, ’t Hooft
anomaly matching
tHooft
for a chiral preon symmetry selects
N=8
,
and
\mathrm{SL}(16,\mathbb{C})
provides a natural hyperunified origin for
three composite quark–lepton families
ch
;
jpl
.

The resulting low-energy theory is a four-dimensional
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})\times\mathrm{SU}(N)
gauge theory with an emergent Einstein–Cartan
sector and a filtered gauge spectrum, without recourse to extra dimensions,
strings, or non-Lie group structures.

II
SL(2N,C) gauge fields and tetrads

We start from a local
\mathrm{SL}(2N,\mathbb{C})
acting on Dirac spinors
\Psi(x)
in the fundamental
2N
-dimensional representation,

\Psi\;\rightarrow\;\Omega(x)\,\Psi,\qquad\Omega=\exp\Big\{\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2}\big[(\theta^{k}+\theta_{5}^{k}\gamma_{5})\lambda^{k}+\tfrac{1}{2}\theta_{ab}^{K}\gamma^{ab}\lambda^{K}\big]\Big\},

(1)

where
\gamma^{ab}=\tfrac{\mathrm{i}}{2}[\gamma^{a},\gamma^{b}]
generate

\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})
and
\lambda^{K}
form a basis of
\mathrm{U}(1)\times\mathrm{SU}(N)
(with
K=0,k
). The corresponding gauge connection

I_{\mu}
decomposes into vector, axial-vector and tensor pieces,

I_{\mu}=\frac{1}{2}\big(V_{\mu}^{k}+A_{\mu}^{k}\gamma_{5}\big)\lambda^{k}+\frac{1}{4}\,T_{\mu[ab]}^{K}\gamma^{ab}\lambda^{K},

(2)

and its field strength
I_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{[\mu}I_{\nu]}+\mathrm{i}g[I_{\mu},I_{\nu}]
contains the usual Yang–Mills terms plus
nontrivial tensor contributions.

The fermion kinetic term requires matrix-valued tetrads
e^{\mu}(x)
that
replace constant
\gamma^{\mu}
and transform as
e^{\mu}\to\Omega e^{\mu}\Omega^{-1}
. In the hyperunified theory they may be written as

e_{\mu}=e_{\mu}^{aK}\gamma_{a}\lambda^{K},

(3)

with inverse
e^{\mu}_{a}{}^{K}
satisfying generalized invertibility relations.
However, consistency with a single four-dimensional metric strongly
constrains the allowed structure. If one demands that the metric be
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})
-invariant and unique, the only invertible solution is

e_{\mu}^{aK}=e_{\mu}^{a}\,\delta^{K0},

(4)

so that tetrads are neutral under
\mathrm{SU}(N)
and belong effectively to
the
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})
subgroup. This is the only choice compatible
with a standard tangent bundle and a single spacetime metric.

In the quadratic sector we adopt a ghost-free curvature-squared Lagrangian
for the Lorentz tensor field, of the Neville type
nev
;
nev1
. In the
pure
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})
case, writing
T_{\mu}^{ab}
for the
tensor connection and
T_{\mu\nu}^{ab}
for its curvature,

e\mathcal{L}_{G}^{(2)}=\lambda\Big(T_{ab}^{\mu\nu}T_{\mu\nu}^{ab}-4T_{ab}^{\mu\nu}T_{\rho\nu}^{ac}e_{\mu c}e^{\rho b}+T_{ab}^{\mu\nu}T_{\rho\sigma}^{cd}e_{\mu c}e_{\nu d}e^{\rho a}e^{\sigma b}\Big).

(5)

This combination propagates a massless graviton plus a heavy massive mode
without ghosts
nev
;
nev1
. We show that it can be embedded into
\mathrm{SL}(2N,\mathbb{C})
by taking suitable traces of
I_{\mu\nu}

multiplied by tetrads. After fixing coefficients to reproduce the Neville
form in the
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})
limit, a single gauge coupling
g

controls all quadratic terms, and vector and axial-vector submultiplets
inherit canonical Yang–Mills kinetic terms.

At this stage
V_{\mu}^{k}
,
A_{\mu}^{k}
and
T_{\mu}^{abK}
all appear on equal
footing. The crucial step is to make the tetrad dynamical and impose a
nonlinear length constraint that forces a symmetry-breaking vacuum.

III
Lifting noncompact directions

We promote the tetrad to a dynamical field and rescale it to a dimensionless
variable
E_{\mu}^{aK}
,

e_{\mu}^{aK}=M\,E_{\mu}^{aK},

(6)

where
M
is a symmetry-breaking scale. The contracted invertibility
condition is then taken as a nonlinear sigma-model constraint

\frac{1}{4}\,\mathrm{Tr}(e_{\mu}e^{\mu})=M^{2},

(7)

leading to the tetrad condensation. This fixes the norm of the tetrad but
not its orientation in the internal space.

A convenient parametrization of a vacuum configuration is

\bar{e}_{\mu}=v_{\mu}\otimes H,\qquad v_{\mu}=M\,\delta_{\mu}^{\,a}\gamma_{a},\qquad H^{\dagger}=H.

(8)

All dependence on the internal (hyperflavor) orientation resides in the
Hermitian
N\times N
matrix
H
. The “hyperflavor-blind” direction
corresponds to
H\propto\mathbf{1}_{N}
, which clearly satisfies (
7
). The key question is whether quantum effects select this
orientation dynamically.

The tetrad acquires a canonical kinetic term

\mathcal{L}_{e}=-\frac{1}{4}\,(D_{[\mu}e_{\nu]}^{\,aK})(D^{[\mu}e^{\nu]}{}_{aK}),

(9)

with the full
\mathrm{SL}(2N,\mathbb{C})
spin connection in the covariant
derivative. Once
\mathcal{L}_{e}
is present, the theory is automatically
promoted from
\mathrm{SL}(2N,\mathbb{C})
to the corresponding
Poincare-type group
\mathrm{ISL}(2N,\mathbb{C})
: the hyperflavored tetrad
is the gauge field of local hypertranslations.

Quantum fluctuations of gauge fields around a background
\bar{e}_{\mu}

generate a Coleman–Weinberg potential
U(\bar{e})
for the tetrad
orientation. The dominant orientation-sensitive piece arises from the vector
submultiplet. In Landau gauge
cw
one finds

U_{V}(\bar{e})=\frac{3}{64\pi^{2}}\sum_{n}m_{n}^{4}(\bar{e})\left(\ln\frac{m_{n}^{2}(\bar{e})}{\mu^{2}}-\frac{5}{6}\right),

(10)

where
m_{n}^{2}
are eigenvalues of the vector mass matrix
M_{V}^{2}(\bar{e})
. For
generators
T_{V}^{k}=\lambda^{k}
of
\mathrm{SU}(N)
,

\displaystyle(M_{V}^{2})_{kl}(\bar{e})

\displaystyle=g^{2}\mathrm{Tr}\big([\lambda_{k},\bar{e}_{\mu}][\lambda_{l},\bar{e}^{\mu}]\big)

\displaystyle=4g^{2}M^{2}\,\mathrm{Tr}\big([\lambda_{k},H][\lambda_{l},H]\big).

(11)

It follows that
M_{V}^{2}
vanishes precisely when
H\propto\mathbf{1}_{N}
; any
deviation from a hyperflavor-blind matrix gives some nonzero eigenvalues and
raises
U_{V}
. Thus the Coleman–Weinberg potential has a strict minimum at

\bar{e}_{\mu}=M\,\delta_{\mu}^{\,a}\gamma_{a}\,\lambda^{0},\qquad\lambda^{0}=\sqrt{\tfrac{2}{N}}\,\mathbf{1}_{N}.

(12)

In this vacuum the tetrad is neutral under
\mathrm{SU}(N)
and aligned
entirely along the
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})
factor. The spontaneous
breaking pattern is

\mathrm{SL}(2N,\mathbb{C})\;\longrightarrow\;\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})\times\mathrm{SU}(N),

(13)

with all noncompact directions lifted.

The spectrum reorganizes accordingly. The six Lorentz generators survive
nonlinearly and still close on the
sl(2,\mathbb{C})
algebra, so local
Lorentz invariance is intact. The
\mathrm{SU}(N)
vector fields
V_{\mu}^{k}

remain massless and mediate ordinary internal forces. By contrast, the
axial-vector fields
A_{\mu}^{k}
and all tensor fields
T_{\mu}^{abK}
(
K=0,k
) acquire masses of order
gM
from the tetrad condensate and decouple from
low-energy physics.

Fluctuations of the tetrad can be decomposed into antisymmetric
\widehat{e}^{[ab]K}
and symmetric
\widetilde{e}^{\{ab\}K}
components in the local
frame. Antisymmetric (torsion-linked) modes are eaten by the tensor spin
connections
T_{\mu}^{abK}
; among the symmetric modes, the
\mathrm{SU}(N)
-adjoint traces are eaten by axial vectors
A_{\mu}^{k}
, while the
remaining adjoint-traceless modes acquire radiative masses
\sim g^{2}M/(4\pi)
from axial and tensor loops. The only massless tetrad
fluctuation is the
\mathrm{SU}(N)
-singlet symmetric traceless part, which
provides the usual graviton. The effective low-energy spectrum thus consists
of a neutral tetrad and the compact
\mathrm{SU}(N)
vector multiplet.

The tetrad kinetic term
\mathcal{L}_{e}
and the curvature-squared sector
imply that the spin connection and tetrad are
a priori
independent
variables, so metricity
D_{\mu}e_{\nu}^{\,a}=0
is not imposed by hand.
However, after tetrad condensation the heavy spin-connection modes acquire
masses of order
gM
, and the equation of motion for
T_{\mu}^{ab}
is
algebraic to leading order in derivatives. Integrating out these heavy modes
enforces metricity up to corrections of order
p^{2}/M^{2}
, where
p
is a
typical external momentum. In the low-energy regime
p\ll M
the effective
connection is thus Levi–Civita (plus the usual Einstein–Cartan torsion),
while deviations are encoded in higher-derivative operators such as
curvature-squared and torsion-squared terms suppressed by
M^{-2}
. Classical
tests of GR are therefore unaffected, provided
M
lies well above the scales
probed by present gravity experiments.

IV
Induced Einstein–Cartan gravity

After tetrad condensation, the quadratic curvature sector is governed by the
ghost-free Lagrangian
\mathcal{L}_{G}^{(2)}
embedded into
\mathrm{SL}(2N,\mathbb{C})
. A separate linear curvature term of the Einstein–Cartan form,

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{EC}}=\frac{1}{2\kappa}\,e\,e_{[a}{}^{\mu}e_{b]}{}^{\nu}R^{ab}{}_{\mu\nu}[T],

(14)

is allowed by symmetry but need not appear at tree level. Here
T_{\mu}^{ab}

is the neutral tensor connection,
R^{ab}{}_{\mu\nu}[T]
its curvature, and
e=\det(e_{\mu}^{\,a})
. We show that (
14
) is generated radiatively by
fermion loops in which both the tetrad and the tensor connection couple to
matter.

The relevant interaction vertices follow from the fermion kinetic term and
the
\mathrm{SL}(2N,\mathbb{C})
covariant derivative. In a schematic
notation,

\displaystyle V_{e}

\displaystyle:\quad\frac{\mathrm{i}}{M}\,e^{\mu}{}_{a}\,\bar{\Psi}\,\gamma^{a}\overleftrightarrow{\partial_{\mu}}\Psi,

\displaystyle V_{T}

\displaystyle:\quad\frac{\mathrm{i}g}{4M}\,e^{\mu}{}_{c}\,\bar{\Psi}\,\gamma^{c}\gamma_{ab}\Psi\,T_{\mu}^{ab},

(15)

where each
V_{T}

minimal one-loop origin of (

e\wedge e\wedge dT

the diagram with two
V_{T}

e\wedge e\wedge(g\,T\wedge T)

e\wedge e\wedge R[T]
with
R[T]=dT+gT\wedge T

\mathrm{SL}(2N,\mathbb{C})

The quadratic curvature terms are already present via the ghost-free
Neville-type Lagrangian, so the full low-energy gravitational sector is an
Einstein–Cartan-plus-curvature-squared theory with the correct sign and no
ghosts.

V
Preons, SL(16,C) and three quark-lepton families

The
\mathrm{SL}(2N,\mathbb{C})
gauge symmetry can also act on more
fundamental constituents of quarks and leptons. A particularly economical
scenario is a preon model in which quarks and leptons are composite states
built from chiral preons transforming in the fundamental of
\mathrm{SL}(2N,\mathbb{C})

ch
;
jpl
. At very short distances we assume
2N
massless
preons:
N
left-handed and
N
right-handed Weyl spinors,

P_{iaL},\qquad Q_{iaR}\qquad(i=1,\dots,N;\;a=1,2),

(18)

which share a common local metaflavor symmetry

G_{\mathrm{MF}}=\mathrm{SL}(2N,\mathbb{C})

(19)

and transform in its fundamental representation. In addition, the preons
carry metacolor charges under a chiral gauge group

G_{\mathrm{MC}}=\mathrm{SO}(n)^{L}\times\mathrm{SO}(n)^{R},

(20)

with left-handed and right-handed preons living in the vector representation
of
\mathrm{SO}(n)^{L}
and
\mathrm{SO}(n)^{R}
, respectively. Metacolor
confinement at a scale
\Lambda_{\mathrm{MC}}
binds preons into composite
fermions, including quarks and leptons.

In the limit where metaflavor gauge interactions are formally switched off,
the preon system enjoys an additional global chiral symmetry

K(N)=\mathrm{SU}(N)_{L}\times\mathrm{SU}(N)_{R},

(21)

acting separately on left- and right-handed preons. Massless composite
fermions can exist only if an appropriate subgroup of
K(N)
survives in the
infrared. The ’t Hooft anomaly matching (AM) condition then requires that
the cubic anomalies
\mathrm{SU}(N)_{L}^{3}
and
\mathrm{SU}(N)_{R}^{3}
computed
in terms of preons match those of the massless composite spectrum
tHooft
;
ch
.

The simplest and most restrictive possibility is that (i) only spin-
\tfrac{1}{2}
composites remain massless; (ii) they are three-preon bound states of the
metacolor interaction; and (iii) all such composites fit into a single
irreducible representation of the residual metaflavor subgroup
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})\times\mathrm{SU}(N)
. Under these assumptions, the candidate
composite multiplets are third-rank tensors of
\mathrm{SU}(N)
,
schematically
\Psi^{\{ijk\}},\,\Psi_{[ijk]},\,\Psi_{\{[ij]k\}},\,\Psi^{i}_{\{jk\}},\,\Psi^{i}_{[jk]}
. Evaluating the anomaly coefficients
a(r)
for
these representations and imposing the AM condition for
\mathrm{SU}(N)_{L}^{3}

(and similarly for
\mathrm{SU}(N)_{R}^{3}
) gives a nontrivial constraint

n\,a(N)=a(r_{0}),

(22)

where
n
is the number of metacolors,
a(N)=1
for preons in the
fundamental, and
r_{0}
is the representation of massless composites.

For three-preon states with
\mathrm{SO}(3)^{L,R}
metacolor, the unique
integer solution is obtained when the massless composites transform as
\Psi^{i}_{[jk]}
with

a\big(\Psi^{i}_{[jk]}\big)=3\quad\Longrightarrow\quad N=8,

(23)

selecting an eightfold chiral symmetry
\mathrm{SU}(8)_{L}\times\mathrm{SU}(8)_{R}
in the infrared

. This points to
\mathrm{SL}(16,\mathbb{C})

as the preferred metaflavor group: at very short distances preons transform
under local
\mathrm{SL}(16,\mathbb{C})

effective metaflavor symmetry acting on massless composites is
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})\times\mathrm{SU}(8)

The surviving massless multiplet in the residual metaflavor symmetry can be
organized into a

\displaystyle[(\overline{5}+10,\overline{3})

N=8

VI
Summary and outlook

We have explored a four-dimensional hyperunified gauge framework based on a
local
\mathrm{SL}(2N,\mathbb{C})
symmetry, in which the spinor symmetry
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})
underlying the spacetime spin structure is
extended by an internal
\mathrm{SU}(N)
sector, and the tetrad is treated as
a dynamical field. The contracted tetrad invertibility condition is
interpreted as a nonlinear sigma-model length constraint. When enforced on
an
\mathrm{SL}(2N,\mathbb{C})
tetrad with a kinetic term, it drives tetrad
condensation and spontaneously breaks
\mathrm{SL}(2N,\mathbb{C})\rightarrow\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})\times\mathrm{SU}(N)
, lifting all noncompact
directions while preserving local Lorentz invariance. A Neville-type
curvature-squared Lagrangian embedded into
\mathrm{SL}(2N,\mathbb{C})

provides a ghost-free quadratic sector for the tensor submultiplet, and the
usual Einstein–Cartan term is induced radiatively from fermion bubbles that
couple both to the tetrad and to the tensor connection. At low energies only
the neutral tetrad (graviton) and an
\mathrm{SU}(N)
vector multiplet
remain in the spectrum; axial-vector and tensor fields acquire masses of
order
gM
.

Applying the same hyperunified symmetry to preons in fundamental spinor
representations, together with ’t Hooft anomaly matching for a chiral preon
symmetry, singles out
N=8
and a residual
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})\times\mathrm{SU}(8)
metaflavor symmetry in the composite sector. The resulting
216
-dimensional multiplet accommodates three composite quark–lepton
families in a natural way, while extra states can be made heavy after a
modest left–right asymmetry in the composite sector.

A single dynamical ingredient—the tetrad—thus simultaneously selects the
vacuum, filters noncompact directions, and anchors an emergent
Einstein–Cartan gravity scale within a four-dimensional hyperunified
framework. Further work should derive the detailed mass spectrum of axial
and tensor spin connections, compute the induced EC coefficient with
explicit regulators in representative fermion sectors, and complete the
preon model for
N=8
, including family-symmetry breaking and fermion mass
textures. If the tetrad-condensation scale
M
and the preon confinement
scale
\Lambda_{\mathrm{MC}}
lie well below
M_{\mathrm{Pl}}
, remnants of
the filtered hyperunified structure could leave characteristic signatures in
the spectrum of heavy bosonic and fermionic composites.

The Galois Theory (a ring theoretic approach)
V. V. Bavula

Abstract
The fundamental concepts in the Galois Theory are separable, normal and Galois field extensions. These concepts are central in proofs of the Galois Theory. In the paper, we introduce a new approach, a ring theoretic approach, to the Galois Theory which is based on central simple algebras and none of the above concepts are used or even mentioned. The only concept which is used is ‘G-extension’ (a finite field extension L / K L/K is called a G-extension if the endomorphism algebra End K ​ ( L ) {\rm End}_{K}(L) is generated by the field L L and the automorphism group Aut K − alg ​ ( L ) {\rm Aut}_{K-{\rm alg}}(L) ). So, G-extensions are the most symmetric field extensions. In this approach, the Galois Correspondences (for subfields and Galois subfields of L L ) are deduced from the Double Centralizer Theorem which is applied to the central simple algebra End K ​ ( L ) {\rm End}_{K}(L) and G-extensions. Since the class of G-extensions coincides with the class of Galois extensions, all main results of the Galois Theory are obtained from the ‘corresponding’ results for G-extensions. This approach gives a new conceptual (short) proofs of key results of the Galois Theory. It also reveals that the ‘maximal symmetry’ (of field extensions) is the essence of the Galois Theory. Key words : G-extension, Galois extension, the Galois group, the Galois correspondence, skew group algebra, centralizer, central simple algebra, the Double Centralizer Theorem. The algebra

Proof.

2. Let

(i)

.

(ii)
The algebra

. So, it remains to prove simplicity of the algebra
A
.
For an element

, where

, the set

is called the

and

. Let

It suffices to show that

since then there is a nonzero element
l_{g}g\in I
, and so
g=l_{g}^{-1}l_{g}g\in I
and

since the element

is a unit of the algebra
A
and its inverse

belongs to the ideal
I
where

is the order of the automorphism

(since

Suppose that

and

and
g_{1},\ldots,g_{m}\in G
are distinct automorphisms. We seek a contradiction. Then

ag_{1}^{-1}=l_{1}+l_{2}g_{2}g_{1}^{-1}+\cdots+l_{m}g_{m}g_{1}^{-1}\in I

Since

and

{\rm dim}_{K}(A)=[L:L^{G}]^{2}[L^{G}:K]=[L:K][L:L^{G}]

3. Statement 3 follows from statement 1 (since

4. The field

5. Statement 5 follows from the equality

Clearly, for each element

Each unit

of an algebra
A
, determines the
inner automorphism

\omega_{u}
of the algebra

which is given by the rule: For all elements

. Elements

for all elements

is a group monomorphism.

Let

.

Let

.

.

Proof.

1. Suppose that

, the algebra

-bimodule. By Theorem
2.8
.(6), the algebra

Theorem 3.6

(The Galois Correspondence)

The map

Proof.

1. By Theorem

(\Rightarrow)

(\Leftarrow)

A
splitting field
of a set
S\subseteq K[x]
of polynomials is a field that is obtained from
K
by adding the roots of all the polynomials in
S
. For the set
S
, the slitting field is unique up to a
K
-isomorphism.

Let
L/K
be a finite field extension. Then its automorphism group
G
acts on the field
L
,

G\times L\rightarrow L,\;\;(g,l)\mapsto g(l).

For each element
l\in L
, the subset of
L
,
Gl:=\{g(l)\,|\,g\in G\}
, is called the
G
-
orbit
of the element
l
.

Lemma
3.8
shows that all G-extensions are splitting fields.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let a finite field extension
L/K
be a G-extension. Then:

1.

For each element
l\in L
, the minimal polynomial of the element
l
over the field
K
is equal to
m_{l}(x)=\prod_{l^{\prime}\in Gl}(x-l^{\prime})\in K[x]
.

2.

The field
L
is a splitting field.

3.

Suppose that a field extension
\Gamma\in{\cal F}(L/K)
is a G-extension. Then
g(\Gamma)=\Gamma
for all elements
g\in G
.

Proof.

1. Since
L/K
is a G-extension,
L^{G}=K
(Theorem
3.6
.(3)). By the definition,

m_{l}(x)\in K^{G}[x]=K[x].

The polynomial
m_{l}(x)
is monic (i.e. the leading coefficient of
m_{l}(x)
is 1). It remains to show that the polynomial
m_{l}(x)
is irreducible over
K
. Suppose that a nonscalar polynomial
f(x)\in K[x]
is a divisor of the polynomial
m_{l}(x)
and
l^{\prime}\in Gl
is its root. Then for all
g\in G
,

0=g(0)=g(f(l^{\prime}))=f(g(l^{\prime})),

and so the set
Gl^{\prime}=Gl
consists of roots of the polynomial

. Therefore,
f(x)=m_{l}(x)
.

2. The finite field extension
L/K
is the composite of its subfields
K(l_{1}),\ldots,K(l_{m})
for some elements
l_{1},\ldots,l_{m}\in L
. By statement 1, the field
L
is the splitting field of the polynomial
\prod_{i=1}^{m}m_{l_{i}}(x)
.

3. By statement 2, the G-extension
\Gamma\in{\cal F}(L/K)
, is a splitting field over

. Therefore,
g(\Gamma)=\Gamma
for all elements
g\in G
.
∎

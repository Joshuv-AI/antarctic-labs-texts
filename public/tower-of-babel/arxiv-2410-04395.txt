Complex Alexandrov-Bakelman-Pucci estimate and its applications
Junbang Liu

Abstract
. We prove an Alexandrov-Bakelman-Pucci type estimate, which involves the integral of the determinant of the complex Hessian over a certain subset. It improves the classical ABP estimate adapted (by inequality 2 2 ​ n ​ | det ( u i ​ j ¯ ) | 2 ≥ | det ( ∇ 2 u ) | 2^{2n}|\det(u_{i\bar{j}})|^{2}\geq|\det(\nabla^{2}u)| ) to complex setting. 1.
Introduction

The classical Alexandrov-Bakelman-Pucci estimate (ABP-estimate in short) states that for a function
u\in C^{2}(\Omega)\cap C^{0}(\bar{\Omega})
on a bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we have

\sup_{\Omega}u\leq\sup_{\partial\Omega}u+C_{n}diam(\Omega)\left(\int_{\{-u=\Gamma_{-u}\}}|\det\nabla^{2}u|\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}.

As a corollary, if the function
u
satisfies the differential inequality
a^{ij}u_{ij}\geq f
for some semipositive symmetric matrix-value function
a^{ij}
, then the integral on the right-hand side can be replaced by the
L^{n}
-norm of
\frac{f^{-}}{\mathscr{D^{*}}}
, where
\mathscr{D}^{*}=(\det(a^{ij}))^{\frac{1}{n}}
.
It’s a fundamental tool in PDE theory, especially in the study of viscosity solutions for fully nonlinear equations. Both the result itself and its proof have numerical significant applications. In this paper, we prove a complex version of the ABP estimates. We find that in the complex setting, the integral of
|\det(\nabla^{2}u)|
over the contact set can be replaced by the integral of the determinant of complex Hessian
\det(u_{i\bar{j}})
over certain subsets. Let
B_{1}\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
be the unit ball and
u
be a real function defined on
B_{1}
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
u\in C^{2}(B_{1})\cap C^{0}(\overline{B_{1}})
, and let
\Phi:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
be a positive increasing function with
\int_{0}^{\infty}\Phi^{-\frac{1}{n}}(t)dt\leq\Lambda<\infty
. Then there exists constant
c_{1},c_{2}
depending only on
n,\Lambda
such that

(1.1)

\sup_{B_{1}}u\leq\sup_{\partial B_{1}}u+c_{1}+c_{2}\left(\int_{\Gamma^{-}}\det(-u_{i\bar{j}})\Phi(\log(\det(-u_{i\bar{j}})))\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}.

Here
\Gamma^{-}:=\{x\in B_{1}|-\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}u(x)>0,u(x)>\sup_{\partial B_{1}}u\}.
Moreover the constant
c_{1}
can be chosen to be

c_{1}=\min\{c(n),\left(\int_{\Gamma^{-}}\det(-u_{i\bar{j}})\right)^{\delta_{n}}\},\text{ for dimensional constant }c(n),\delta_{n}.

To the author’s knowledge, the application of the ABP estimate to complex geometry can be traced back to Cheng and Yau, who suggested that the

[
2

-estimates for a class of fully nonlinear elliptic equations on compact Hermitian manifolds, assuming the existence of a

-subsolution
[
20
]
. A major breakthrough was achieved by X. Chen and J. Cheng in
[
4

5

[
15
]
to obtain
L^{\infty}
-estimates for a class of Monge-Ampère-type equations, relying only on a weak Orlicz norm
(L^{1}\log^{p}L
-norm,

[
19

7

24
]
. All of the above applications of classical ABP-estimate to complex geometry are based on the observation

. So, our theorem can be viewed as an improvement of the classical one in a complex setting, and it leads to sharp estimates in practice. There have also been some improvements to the ABP estimate in the complex setting (see
[
10

22
]
and references therein). We remark that when the function

2.
Complex version of ABP-estimate

In this section, we prove an ABP-type estimate in the complex setting. Let
B_{1}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be the unit ball. We use

Proof of theorem
1.1
.

Without loss of generality, we can assume that
\sup_{\partial B_{1}}u=0
.
Let

be a sequence of positive smooth functions approximating
\chi_{\Gamma^{-}}\det(-u_{i\bar{j}})
from above uniformly, where
\chi_{\Gamma^{-}}

\Gamma^{-}

(2.1)

N_{k}:=\int_{B_{1}}e^{G_{k}}\Phi({G_{k}})dV\rightarrow\int_{\Gamma^{-}}\det(-u_{i\bar{j}})\Phi(\log(\det(-u_{i\bar{j}})))dV.

Consider the following auxiliary function

(2.2)

(\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\psi_{1})^{n}=\frac{e^{G_{k}}\Phi(G_{k})dV}{N_{k}},\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\psi_{1}\geq 0,\psi_{1}|_{\partial B_{1}}=0.

For any given

h(s)=-\int_{s}^{\infty}\frac{q}{\alpha}\frac{N_{k}^{\frac{1}{n}}}{\Phi^{\frac{1}{n}}(t)}dt,

where
\alpha>0

2.1
.
Since we assume that
\Phi
is an increasing function, we know

is a concave increasing function.

Now define the function
\psi

\psi=-h(-\frac{\alpha}{q}\psi_{1})

\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\psi=\frac{\alpha}{q}h^{\prime}(-\frac{\alpha}{q}\psi_{1})\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\psi_{1}-\frac{\alpha^{2}}{q^{2}}h^{\prime\prime}(-\frac{\alpha}{q})\sqrt{-1}\partial\psi_{1}\wedge\bar{\partial}\psi_{1}\geq\frac{\alpha}{q}h^{\prime}(-\frac{\alpha}{q}\psi_{1})\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\psi_{1}.

e^{G_{k}}dV\leq\left(\frac{\alpha^{n}}{q^{n}}h^{{}^{\prime}n}\frac{\Phi(G_{k})}{N_{k}}\right)^{-1}(\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\psi)^{n}

There are two possible cases,
\left(\frac{\alpha^{n}}{q^{n}}h^{{}^{\prime}n}\frac{\Phi(G_{k})}{N_{k}}\right)\geq 1

\Phi(G_{k})\leq\frac{q^{n}}{\alpha^{n}}\frac{1}{h^{\prime n}(-\alpha q^{-1}\psi_{1})}N_{k}.

According to our definition of

\Phi(s)=\frac{q^{n}}{\alpha^{n}}\frac{1}{h^{\prime n}(s)}N_{k}.
Since

G_{k}\leq-\frac{\alpha}{q}\psi_{1}.

Corollary 4.4

There exist uniform constants

Proof.

Let

(\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\psi)^{n}=\frac{(-u-b(t))(\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}u)^{n}}{\mathcal{E}(u+b(t))},\quad\varphi|_{\partial B_{1}}=0,\text{ and }\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\psi>0.

Consider the function

We claim that

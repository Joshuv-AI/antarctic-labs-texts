Relaxing DESI DR2 BAO Constraints on ∑ m ν \sum m_{\nu} with Planck and SPT-3G 2018 in the Context of SPT D1
Nikita Nedelko

Abstract
We present constraints on the sum of neutrino masses ∑ m ν \sum m_{\nu} from a dataset incorporating the full SPT-3G 2018 TT/TE/EE+lensing spectra together with Planck PR4 lensing and low- ℓ \ell parts of the Planck PR3 spectra. Using it as a baseline for the DESI DR2 BAO measurements, we arrive at a 95 % 95\% upper limit of ∑ m ν < 0.11 ​ eV \sum m_{\nu}<0.11\,\text{eV} , relaxing the tension between Λ \rm\Lambda CDM and lower bounds on ∑ m ν \sum m_{\nu} based on neutrino oscillation experiments. When including DES Y1 weak lensing information and the Pantheon+ SNIa catalog, the limit is further loosened to ∑ m ν < 0.138 ​ eV \sum m_{\nu}<0.138\,\text{eV} with a slight preference for ∑ m ν > 0 \sum m_{\nu}>0 . On contrast, replacing SPT-3G 2018 primary CMB and lensing spectra with ones from the SPT-3G 2019-2020 (D1) release tightens the overall constraint to < 0.082 ​ eV <0.082\,\text{eV} and pushes the ∑ m ν \sum m_{\nu} posterior mode value to zero, indicating a preference for quasi-negative neutrino masses in line with the D1 analysis. This is a curious shift within SPT-3G measurements of the same field taken in 2018 and in 2019-2020 and processed with different analysis pipelines.



1
Introduction

Ever since the discovery of neutrino oscillations, finding the scale of neutrino masses has been one of the biggest ongoing quests in fundamental physics. As neutrino oscillations only depend on the differences of squared neutrino masses, few ground-based experiments can limit the neutrino masses from above, with the KATRIN experiment putting an upper limit of
0.45\,\text{eV}
on the mass of the electron neutrino
[
11
]
, which may be translated on the mass of the lightest eigenstate. On the other hand, the precise measurement of
\Delta m^{2}_{ij}
provides a lower limit on the sum of neutrino masses by assuming that the lightest state is massless, with the lower bound currently standing at
\approx 0.06\,\text{eV}
for the normal neutrino mass hierarchy and
\approx 0.10\,\text{eV}
for the inverse hierarchy
[
24
]
.

Cosmological observations can complete the puzzle. The mass of neutrinos changes the evolution of the Universe and in particular the growth of its large-scale structure (LSS)
[
32
]
. However, till now no signature of neutrino mass impacts on the cosmology has been observed. This allowed cosmologists to place upper limits on the sum of neutrino masses
\sum m_{\nu}
from a combined analysis of the CMB measurements and galaxy-mapping surveys.

Recently, first and second data releases of the DESI experiment have become available
[
5
,
4
,
2
]
, providing new precise measurements of the LSS. So far, these measurements have been combined with the full-sky CMB maps produced by the Planck space telescope
[
10
,
35
]
, and the more precise (at small angular scales/large multipoles
\ell
) partial maps from the Atacama Cosmology Telescope (ACT)
[
31
]
, resulting in strict upper bounds of
\sum m_{\nu}<0.07\,\text{eV}

[
7
,
6
,
3
]
(at
95\%
confidence level), putting them in tension with the 3-flavor picture of neutrino oscillations, especially in the case of inverse mass hierarchy. In this context we turn to the other investigator of CMB properties, the SPT-3G project
[
16
]
, which produces high-resolution maps of a relatively small part of the sky, in conjunction with low-
\ell
and lensing data from Planck, to construct two competitive "SPT-forward" CMB datasets based on the results of 2018 and 2019-2020 SPT-3G observational seasons. Using it as a baseline and incorporating information from DESI DR2 BAO
[
3
]
, DES Y1 weak lensing
[
1
]
and the Pantheon+ SN catalog
[
33
]
, we present a set of constraints on
\sum m_{\nu}
. We find that while SPT-3G 2018 data is generally able to reconcile DESI DR2 BAO measurements with massive neutrinos when Pantheon+ Type Ia supernovae are included, SPT-3G 2019-2020 closes this window.

5
Comparison to SPT-3G 2019-2020

The most recent analysis by the SPT-3G collaboration, dubbed D1
[
18
]
, covers the 2019 and 2020 observational seasons. Notably, there have been changes made to both the instrument and the analysis pipeline between the seasons of 2018 and 2019, leading to the D1 release not including the 2018 data. Therefore, it is important to track changes in the
\sum m_{\nu}
constraints between the 2018
CMB
dataset and a version that uses the 2019-2020 SPT-3G data instead.

For this purpose we construct the
CMB-D1
dataset, replacing the SPT-3G 2018
TT/TE/EE
likelihood with the SPT-3G D1 T
\&
E likelihood
[
18
]
. Additionally we update the lensing set to replace the SPT-3G 2018 TT-based lensing spectra with SPT-3G 2019-2020 MUSE EE-based ones
[
25
]
. In both cases we use the primary CMB likelihoods implemented in
candl
. We keep our cut of the Planck PR3 data unchanged for consistency.

Resulting constraints in the
\Lambda
CDM+
\sum m_{\nu}
model are presented in Tab.
4
and in the lower panel of Fig.
1
. Compared to
CMB
,
CMB-D1
exhibits a
25\%
reduction in the standard deviations of
\omega_{b}
and
\omega_{cdm}
but a
35\%
reduction in the uncertainty of inferred
H_{\rm 0}
and a similar reduction in the
2\sigma
upper limit on
\sum m_{\nu}
. Notable is a
1\sigma
upward shift in
S_{\rm 8}
, largely due to an increase in
\sigma_{8}
, indicating a preference for higher matter density contrast from the high-
\ell
measurements and lensing estimations of SPT-3G 2019-2020. This changes the parameter space preferred in combination with DESI DR2 BAO (the
\sigma_{8}
constraint moving up further as
\Omega_{m}
is pushed downwards by DESI) towards smaller sum of neutrino masses,
\sum m_{\nu}<0.07\,\text{eV}
.
Finally, the addition of the DES Y1 weak lensing and Pantheon+ SN data once again relaxes the constraint to
<0.082\,\text{eV}
, below the lower mass bound for the inverse hierarchy, and unlike in the SPT-3G 2018 case the marginal distribution of
\sum m_{\nu}
still peaks at zero.

6
Discussion and Conclusions

We explore a combination of SPT-3G 2018 and Planck CMB spectra as a baseline cosmological dataset leveraging SPT-3G’s high angular resolution with minimal reliance on Planck’s full CMB maps, reaching an optimal compromise in terms of constraining power. We then utilize this
CMB
dataset alongside DESI DR2 BAO measurements, DES Y1 weak lensing measurements, and the Pantheon+ SNIa catalog to provide alternative constraints on the sum of active neutrino masses
\sum m_{\nu}
in light of the growing tension between cosmological upper limits and oscillatory lower limits on
\sum m_{\nu}
. We are able to relax the upper limit on
\sum m_{\nu}
to
0.110\,\text{eV}
for CMB+DESI and to
0.138\,\text{eV}
for CMB+DESI+DESY1+Pantheon+ while remaining competitive in terms of
1\sigma
errors on
\rm\Lambda
CDM parameters. Additionally, this CMB combination is in better agreement with BBN-calibrated DESI DR2 BAO than both the Planck PR3 and PR4 datasets
[
3
,
12
]
.

We then compare this result to the more recent MUSE and D1 data releases from SPT-3G
[
18
]
featuring CMB maps obtained during the 2019 and 2020 observational seasons,
but not the 2018 season
. This provides us with an interesting look into the evolution of the
\sum m_{\nu}
tension in SPT data within the larger context. In the
\Lambda
CDM+
\sum m_{\nu}
model, the most important change between
CMB
and
CMB-D1
is the upward shift in
S_{8}
. While not statistically significant on its own (e.g. the difference in
S_{8}
between
CMB+DESI
and
CMB-D1+DESI
is at
1.2\sigma
) it pushes the
\sum m_{\nu}
posterior against the
0\,\text{eV}
prior border, strengthening the tension with ground-based experiments. It has been shown that this behavior represents a more general preference by the DESI data for a lower total matter density than what is inferred from primary and lensing CMB spectra in
\Lambda
CDM
[
21
,
26
,
29
]
. Indeed, the
SPT-3G D1
dataset produces a
\Lambda
CDM constraint of
\Omega_{m}=0.325\pm 0.009

[
18
]
, 1.4
\sigma
above our
CMB
result. This shift shrinks the allowed mass range once
\sum m_{\nu}
is free to vary and enhances the push toward zero (or formally negative) neutrino mass exhibited by DESI BAO data, ensuring that even with the addition of the DES Y1 and Pantheon+ likelihoods the marginalized

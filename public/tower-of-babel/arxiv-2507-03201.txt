On Frustration-Free Quantum Spin Models
Danilo Polo Ojito, Emil Prodan, Tom Stoiber

Abstract
. The goal of our work is to characterize the landscape of the frustration-free quantum spin models over the Cayley graph of a finitely generated group G G . This is achieved by establishing G G -equivariant morphisms from the partially ordered space of frustration-free models to the partially ordered spaces 1) of hereditary C ∗ C^{\ast} -algebras of the underlying UHF quasi-local algebra of observables, 2) of open projections in its double dual, and 3) of subsets of pure state space. Our main result consists of an intrinsic characterization of the images of these morphisms, which captures the essence of frustration-freeness and enables us to extend the concept to generic AF- C ∗ C^{\ast} -algebras. Additionally, using well established facts about AF- C ∗ C^{\ast} -algebras, we prove density theorems, provide intrinsic characterizations of frustration-free ground states, and propose a definition of a boundary algebra for models constrained to half-lattices, under the sole assumption of frustration-freeness.

Proposition 2.7
.

For a linear subspace

Proof.

(30)

C_{\Xi}(X)=\prod_{g\in\Xi_{T}}\Gamma(T\triangleright x_{g})=\Big[\prod_{g\in\Xi_{T}\setminus\Lambda_{T}}\Gamma(T\triangleright x_{g})\Big]\Big[\prod_{g\in\Lambda_{T}}\Gamma(T\triangleright x_{g})\Big]

\displaystyle\sum_{V\in{\mathcal{C}}(\Lambda\setminus\Lambda_{B})}\,\sum_{Z\in{\mathcal{C}}(\Xi_{B}\setminus\Lambda)}

(33)

\displaystyle\otimes\sum_{W\in{\mathcal{C}}(\Lambda_{B})}C_{\Lambda}(W)\Psi(W\curlyvee V).

(34)

\Diamond

4.
Frustration-freeness vs hereditary
C^{\ast}
-subalgebras

This section establishes the horizontal part of the diagram
4
. In the process, we introduce and characterize the entry
\mathfrak{H}^{F}({\mathcal{S}}^{\otimes G})
of this diagram.

4.1.
Intrinsic characterization of frustration-freeness

Hereditary
C^{\ast}
-subalgebras of AF algebras are AF themselves, and AF-algebras have real rank zero. Hence, all
{\mathcal{B}}\in\mathfrak{H}({\mathcal{S}}^{\otimes G})
have real rank zero and, as such, they always have approximate units given by nets of projections
[
38
]
. Below, we identify the class of hereditary
C^{\ast}
-subalgebras for which these approximate units are frustration-free systems of projections:

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{B}}\in\mathfrak{H}({\mathcal{S}}^{\otimes G})
be proper. Then
{\mathcal{B}}\cap{\mathcal{S}}_{\Lambda}
is a hereditary subalgebra of
{\mathcal{S}}_{\Lambda}
for each
\Lambda\in{\rm K}(G)
. As such, there exists a unique projection
p_{\Lambda}({\mathcal{B}})\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\Lambda}\subset{\mathcal{S}}^{\otimes G}
such that

{\mathcal{B}}\cap{\mathcal{S}}_{\Lambda}=p_{\Lambda}({\mathcal{B}})\,{\mathcal{S}}_{\Lambda}\,p_{\Lambda}({\mathcal{B}}).

(54)

If
{\mathcal{B}}
displays the property

{\rm(F):}\ \ {\mathcal{B}}=\overline{\cup_{\Lambda\in{\rm K}(G)}({\mathcal{B}}\cap{\mathcal{S}}_{\Lambda})},

(55)

then
\{p_{\Lambda}({\mathcal{B}})\}_{\Lambda\in{\rm K}(G)}
is an approximate unit for
{\mathcal{B}}
and a frustration-free proper system of projections.

Proof.

Property (F) assures us that the intersections
{\mathcal{B}}\cap{\mathcal{S}}_{\Lambda}
are not all void and that none of the
p_{\Lambda}
’s can be the identity. Hence,
\{p_{\Lambda}\}
is non trivial and proper.
We first claim that
\{p_{\Lambda}\}=\{p_{\Lambda}({\mathcal{B}})\}_{\Lambda\in{\rm K}(G)}
is an increasing net. Indeed, if
\Lambda_{1}\subseteq\Lambda_{2}
,

{\mathcal{B}}\cap{\mathcal{S}}_{\Lambda_{1}}\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}\cap{\mathcal{S}}_{\Lambda_{2}},

(56)

hence
p_{\Lambda_{1}}=p_{\Lambda_{2}}s\,p_{\Lambda_{2}}
for some
s\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\Lambda_{2}}
and, as such,
p_{\Lambda_{2}}p_{\Lambda_{1}}=p_{\Lambda_{1}}
and the claim follows. Property (F) also assures us that, for each
b\in{\mathcal{B}}
, there exists a net
b_{\Lambda}\in{\mathcal{B}}\cap{\mathcal{S}}_{\Lambda}
such that
\lim b_{\Lambda}=b
. Then

\|b-p_{\Lambda}b\|=\|b-b_{\Lambda}-p_{\Lambda}(b-b_{\Lambda})\|\to 0,

(57)

which shows that
\{p_{\Lambda}\}
is indeed an approximate unit.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

Clearly, if
{\mathcal{B}}
is
G
-invariant, then
\{p_{\Lambda}({\mathcal{B}})\}_{\Lambda\in{\rm K}(G)}
is a
G
-system.

\Diamond

We will refer to the approximate unit singled out above as the localized approximate unit. We denote the subset of proper hereditary
C^{\ast}
-subalgebras displaying property (F) by
\mathfrak{H}^{F}({\mathcal{S}}^{\otimes G})
and note that this subset is
G
-invariant. We denote by
\hat{\mathfrak{H}}^{F}({\mathcal{S}}^{\otimes G})
the subset of all hereditary
C^{\ast}
-subalgebras displaying property (F). This is mostly for notational purposes, because the latter is just
{\mathfrak{H}}^{F}({\mathcal{S}}^{\otimes G})
with
{\mathcal{S}}^{\otimes G}
added to it.

Theorem 4.3
.

The following hold:

(1)

The map

\mathfrak{H}^{F}({\mathcal{S}}^{\otimes G})\ni{\mathcal{B}}\overset{\mathfrak{p}}{\mapsto}\{p_{\Lambda}({\mathcal{B}})\}\in\mathfrak{F}({\mathcal{S}}^{\otimes G})

(58)

is an injective and
G
-equivariant morphism of posets.

(2)

There exists a
G
-equivariant morphism
\mathfrak{p}^{-1}
of posets from
\mathfrak{F}({\mathcal{S}}^{\otimes G})
to
\mathfrak{H}^{F}({\mathcal{S}}^{\otimes G})
, which is a left inverse for (
58
).

Proof.

(1) The map respects the partial orders because, if
{\mathcal{B}}\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}
, then
{\mathcal{B}}\cap{\mathcal{S}}_{\Lambda}\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}\cap{\mathcal{S}}_{\Lambda}
and, as such,
p_{\Lambda}\in p^{\prime}_{\Lambda}{\mathcal{S}}_{\Lambda}p^{\prime}_{\Lambda}
, which implies
p_{\Lambda}p^{\prime}_{\Lambda}=p^{\prime}_{\Lambda}p_{\Lambda}=p_{\Lambda}
. Furthermore, if two hereditary subalgebras admit identical approximate units, then their corresponding
w
’s coincide and
3.7
assures us that the hereditary
C^{\ast}
-subalgebras are identical. Now, for each
\Lambda\in{\rm K}(G)
, we have

\alpha_{g}({\mathcal{B}})\cap{\mathcal{S}}_{g\cdot\Lambda}=p_{g\cdot\Lambda}\big(\alpha_{g}({\mathcal{B}})\big)\,{\mathcal{S}}_{g\cdot\Lambda}\,p_{g\cdot\Lambda}\big(\alpha_{g}({\mathcal{B}})\big).

(59)

In the same time,

\displaystyle\alpha_{g}({\mathcal{B}})\cap{\mathcal{S}}_{g\cdot\Lambda}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{g}({\mathcal{B}}\cap{\mathcal{S}}_{\Lambda})

(60)

\displaystyle=\alpha_{g}\big(p_{\Lambda}({\mathcal{B}})\,{\mathcal{S}}_{\Lambda}\,p_{\Lambda}({\mathcal{B}})\big)=\alpha_{g}\big(p_{\Lambda}({\mathcal{B}})\big)\,{\mathcal{S}}_{g\cdot\Lambda}\,\alpha_{g}\big(p_{\Lambda}({\mathcal{B}})\big).

Using
3.8
, we conclude that

p_{g\cdot\Lambda}\big(\alpha_{g}({\mathcal{B}})\big)=\alpha_{g}\big(p_{\Lambda}({\mathcal{B}})\big)\ \Leftrightarrow\ \{p_{\Lambda}\big(\alpha_{g}({\mathcal{B}})\big)\}=g\cdot\{p_{\Lambda}({\mathcal{B}})\}

(61)

and
G
-equivariance is proved.

(2) Given
\{p_{\Lambda}\}\in\mathfrak{F}({\mathcal{S}}^{\otimes G})
, we can construct
w=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}2^{-m}p_{\Lambda_{m}}\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\otimes G}
for an increasing sequence of finite subsets such that
\cup\Lambda_{m}=G
. In turn, this supplies the hereditary
C^{\ast}
-subalgebra
{\mathcal{B}}=\overline{w{\mathcal{S}}^{\otimes G}w}
. We want to show that
\{p_{\Lambda}\}
is an approximate unit for
{\mathcal{B}}
. Using frustration-freeness,

p_{\Lambda}w=\sum_{m\leq n}2^{-m}p_{\Lambda_{m}}+\sum_{m>n}2^{-m}p_{\Lambda}p_{\Lambda_{m}}=w-\sum_{m>n}2^{-m}(1-p_{\Lambda})p_{\Lambda_{m}}

(62)

for any
\Lambda\supseteq\Lambda_{n}
, hence

\|w-p_{\Lambda}w\|\leq 2^{-n}.

(63)

For any
b\in{\mathcal{B}}
and
\epsilon>0
, we can find
a_{\epsilon}\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\otimes G}
such that
\|b-wa_{\epsilon}w\|\leq\epsilon
. Then

\|b-p_{\Lambda}b\|\leq\|wa_{\epsilon}w-p_{\Lambda}wa_{\epsilon}w\|+2\epsilon\leq 2^{-n}\|a_{\epsilon}\|\|w\|+2\epsilon.

(64)

Hence, there exists
n(\epsilon)
such that
\|b-p_{\Lambda}b\|\leq 3\epsilon
for all
\Lambda\supseteq\Lambda_{n(\epsilon)}
. Since
\epsilon
was arbitrary,
\{p_{\Lambda}\}
is indeed an approximate unit for
{\mathcal{B}}
. An important outcome of this conclusion is that
{\mathcal{B}}
so constructed is independent of the choice of
\{\Lambda_{m}\}
used in the first place.

Let us also verify that
{\mathcal{B}}
has property (F): For general

and any
\epsilon>0
we can find some
\Lambda
large enough such that there exists
a\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\Lambda}
with
\lVert b-waw\rVert<\frac{\epsilon}{2}
. By the approximate unit property there is then
n
large enough such that

\lVert b-p_{\Lambda_{n}}wawp_{\Lambda_{n}}\rVert\leq\lVert b-waw\rVert+\lVert waw-p_{\Lambda_{n}}wawp_{\Lambda_{n}}\rVert<\epsilon.

Because eventually
\Lambda\subset\Lambda_{n}
and then
p_{\Lambda_{n}}wawp_{\Lambda_{n}}\in{\mathcal{B}}\cap{\mathcal{S}}_{\Lambda_{n}}
, we conclude that property (F) holds.

Assume now that
{\mathcal{B}}
is the whole algebra
{\mathcal{S}}^{\otimes G}
,
i.e.
that is not proper. Then
p_{\Lambda}^{\bot}\in{\mathcal{B}}
for each
\Lambda\in{\rm K}(G)
and, since
\{p_{\Xi}\}
is an approximate unit for
{\mathcal{B}}
, for each
\epsilon>0
there exists
\Delta(\epsilon)\supset\Lambda
such that
\|p_{\Xi}p_{\Lambda}^{\bot}-p_{\Lambda}^{\bot}\|\leq\epsilon
for all
\Xi\supset\Delta(\epsilon)
. By using frustration-freeness, we find

\|p_{\Xi}p_{\Lambda}^{\bot}-p_{\Lambda}^{\bot}\|=\|(1-p_{\Xi}^{\bot})p_{\Lambda}^{\bot}-p_{\Lambda}^{\bot}\|=\|p_{\Xi}^{\bot}\|,

(65)

and conclude that
\|p_{\Xi}^{\bot}\|\leq\epsilon
. Taking

, but this contradicts our assumptions. Thus,

must be proper and, as such, in
\mathfrak{H}^{F}({\mathcal{S}}^{\otimes G})
.

Lastly, it is clear that the map respects the partial orders, it is a left inverse for (

) and, if
{\mathcal{B}}
is the image of
\{p_{\Lambda}\}
, then

is the image of

G
-equivariant.
∎

Remark 4.4
.

We can extend
\mathfrak{p}
to a morphism between the posets
\hat{\mathfrak{H}}^{F}({\mathcal{S}}^{\otimes G})
and

.

\Diamond

The morphism
\mathfrak{p}^{-1}
clearly cannot be injective. However, the localized approximate unit distinguishes itself among all the other frustration-free systems generating the same hereditary
C^{\ast}
-subalgebra in the following way:

Proposition 4.5
.

If
\mathfrak{F}_{\mathcal{B}}
is the pre-image of the hereditary
C^{\ast}
-algebra
{\mathcal{B}}
for the map
\mathfrak{p}^{-1}
, then

Proof.

Let

p_{\Lambda}\in{\mathcal{B}}\cap{\mathcal{S}}_{\Lambda}=p_{\Lambda}({\mathcal{B}})\,{\mathcal{S}}_{\Lambda}\,p_{\Lambda}({\mathcal{B}}).

As such, there exists

∎

Corollary 4.6
.

There exists a
G
-equivariant bijective map between the maximal elements of
\mathfrak{H}^{F}({\mathcal{S}}^{\otimes G})
and of
\mathfrak{F}({\mathcal{S}}^{\otimes G})

The subset

[

Proposition 4.7
.

(\hat{\mathfrak{H}}^{F}({\mathcal{S}}^{\otimes G}),\subseteq)
is a lattice.

Proof.

We are going to exploit Proposition
2.4

\hat{\mathfrak{F}}({\mathcal{S}}^{\otimes G})
is a lattice. If
{\mathcal{B}}_{i}\in\hat{\mathfrak{H}}^{F}({\mathcal{S}}^{\otimes G})
for

\{p_{\Lambda}({\mathcal{B}}_{i})\}\geq\{p_{\Lambda}({\mathcal{B}}_{1})\}\wedge\{p_{\Lambda}({\mathcal{B}}_{2})\}\geq\{p_{\Lambda}({\mathcal{B}})\}

for any

In conclusion, the frustration-freeness was found to derive from the particular filtration

Definition 4.8

(General frustration-freeness)

Let

be an AF-

-algebra and
\{{\mathcal{A}}_{i}\}_{i\in I}

With this definition in place, we can generate via

4.2.
Density statements

We address here the question of how large is the set
\mathfrak{H}^{F}({\mathcal{S}}^{\otimes G})
inside the set
\mathfrak{H}({\mathcal{S}}^{\otimes G})
of all hereditary
C^{\ast}
-subalgebras. For this, we recall yet another special property of hereditary
C^{\ast}
-subalgebras:

Proposition 4.9

(
[
27
]
, Prop. 3.1(i))
.

Let
{\mathcal{A}}
be a separable AF algebra and
{\mathcal{B}}\in\mathfrak{H}({\mathcal{A}})
. Then
{\mathcal{B}}
is an AF algebra and, if
{\mathcal{B}}_{n}
is an ascending sequence of finite dimensional subalgebras with norm-dense union in

, then there exists an ascending sequence
{\mathcal{A}}_{n}
with norm-dense union in
{\mathcal{A}}
such that
{\mathcal{B}}\cap{\mathcal{A}}_{n}={\mathcal{B}}_{n}
for all
n
.

Let us also state the following general fact about AF algebras, which we formulate for our specific context:

Proposition 4.10

(
[
13
]
, Th. III.3.5)
.

Suppose
{\mathcal{A}}_{n}
is an increasing sequence of finite sub-
C^{\ast}
-algebras of
{\mathcal{S}}^{\otimes G}
such that

{\mathcal{S}}^{\otimes G}=\overline{\cup_{n}{\mathcal{A}}_{n}}
.
Then, for any
\epsilon>0
, there exists a unitary element
u\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\otimes G}
with
\|u-1\|<\epsilon
so that
\cup_{n}{\mathcal{A}}_{n}=u^{\ast}(\cup_{\Lambda}{\mathcal{S}}_{\Lambda})u
.

A direct consequence of the mentioned facts is that any hereditary sub-
C^{\ast}
-algebra of
{\mathcal{S}}^{\otimes G}
can be twisted by an inner automorphism to fit into
\mathfrak{H}^{F}({\mathcal{S}}^{\otimes G})
. More precisely:

Proposition 4.11
.

For every
\epsilon>0
and
{\mathcal{B}}\in\mathfrak{H}({\mathcal{S}}^{\otimes G})
, there exists a unitary element
u\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\otimes G}
such that
\|u-1\|<\epsilon
and

{\mathcal{B}}_{u}:=u{\mathcal{B}}u^{\ast}=\overline{\cup_{\Lambda\in{\rm K}(G)}({\mathcal{B}}_{u}\cap{\mathcal{S}}_{\Lambda})}.

(68)

Proof.

Let
{\mathcal{B}}=\overline{\cup_{n}{\mathcal{B}}_{n}}\in\mathfrak{H}({\mathcal{S}}^{\otimes G})
and
{\mathcal{A}}_{n}
be the sequences from
4.9
. For
\epsilon\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
, let

be the unitary element from
4.10
. Then

u^{\ast}(\cup_{\Lambda}({\mathcal{B}}_{u}\cap{\mathcal{S}}_{\Lambda}))u={\mathcal{B}}\cap u^{\ast}(\cup_{\Lambda}{\mathcal{S}}_{\Lambda})u={\mathcal{B}}\cap(\cup_{n}{\mathcal{A}}_{n})=\cup_{n}({\mathcal{B}}\cap{\mathcal{A}}_{n}).

(69)

Therefore

\overline{\cup_{\Lambda}({\mathcal{B}}_{u}\cap{\mathcal{S}}_{\Lambda})}=u\,\big(\overline{\cup_{n}({\mathcal{B}}\cap{\mathcal{A}}_{n})}\big)\,u^{\ast}=u{\mathcal{B}}u^{\ast}

(70)

and the statement follows.
∎

Remark 4.12

The situation is very different if one insists on

displays asymptotic abelianness, there is no element in it that is invariant under the

-action. Thus, the above mechanism fails to produce

-invariant hereditary

-subalgebra, even if the original one is

-invariant.

\Diamond

5.
States vs frustration-free systems

This section analyzes the relation between states and frustration-free systems of projections via the bottom part of diagram (
4
). The entry
\mathfrak{P}^{F}[{\rm PS}({\mathcal{S}}^{\otimes G})]\subset\mathfrak{P}[{\rm PS}({\mathcal{S}}^{\otimes G})]
in (
4
) can be identified via Proposition
3.4
as the image of
\mathfrak{H}^{F}({\mathcal{S}}^{\otimes G})
through the lattice isomorphism
\mathfrak{j}
from (
49
).

Reference
[
1
]
introduced the concept of frustration-free states, which will be the focus of our investigation. We reformulate it as follows:

Definition 5.1

(
[
1
]
)
.

Let
\{p_{\Lambda}\}
be a frustration-free system of proper projections. One says that
\omega\in{\rm S}({\mathcal{S}}^{\otimes G})
is a frustration-free ground state for
\{p_{\Lambda}\}
if
\omega(p_{\Lambda})=0
for all
\Lambda\in{\rm K}(G)
. In general, we say that
\omega
is a frustration-free state if it so for at least one frustration-free system of proper projections.

Remark 5.2
.

Let
h_{\Lambda}=\sum_{g\cdot\Delta\subseteq\Lambda}\alpha_{g}(p_{\Delta})
be the seeds of a frustration-free inner-limit derivation
\delta_{\bm{h}}
associated with the frustration-free
G
-system
\{p_{\Lambda}\}
. If
\omega
is a frustration-free ground state for
\{p_{\Lambda}\}
, then each
p_{\Lambda}
belongs to
{\mathcal{B}}_{\omega}
. As a consequence,
\omega(\delta_{\bm{h}}(a))=0
for all
a
in the domain of
\delta_{\bm{h}}
, hence
\omega
is invariant to the time evolution generated by
\delta_{\bm{h}}
, and, furthermore,

\omega\big(a^{\ast}\delta_{\bm{h}}(a)\big)=\omega(a^{\ast}[h_{\Xi},a])=\omega(a^{\ast}h_{\Xi}a)\geq 0

(71)

holds for any
a\in\bigcup{\mathcal{S}}_{\Lambda}
and for some
\Xi\in{\rm K}(G)
. Since
\bigcup{\mathcal{S}}_{\Lambda}
is dense in
Dom(\delta_{\bm{h}})
, a frustration-free ground state is also a ground state of
\delta_{\bm{h}}
in the usual sense.

\Diamond

The following statement gives a characterization of the frustration-free
G
-invariant ground states:

Proposition 5.3
.

If the hereditary
C^{\ast}
-subalgebra
{\mathcal{B}}_{\omega}
of a state
\omega\in{\rm S}({\mathcal{S}}^{\otimes G})
is
G
-invariant, proper and displays property
(F)
, then
\omega
is a frustration-free ground state for a frustration-free
G
-system of projections.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.1
,
{\mathcal{B}}_{\omega}
admits a
G
-invariant localized approximate unit
\{p_{\Lambda}({\mathcal{B}}_{\omega})\}
, which is proper, frustration-free and
\omega(p_{\Lambda}({\mathcal{B}}_{\omega}))=0
.
∎

A frustration-free system of projections determines a canonical sub-set of
{\rm PS}({\mathcal{S}}^{\otimes G})
via the composition
\mathfrak{j}\circ\mathfrak{p}^{-1}
of maps defined in
3.4
and
4.3
. The following statements clarify the relation between this subset and the frustration-free ground states.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
\{p_{\Lambda}\}
be a frustration-free system of proper projections. Then
\omega
is a frustration-free ground state for
\{p_{\Lambda}\}
if and only if

\mathfrak{p}^{-1}(\{p_{\Lambda}\})\subseteq\mathfrak{{\mathcal{B}}}_{\omega}.

(72)

Proof.

As in
4.3
, define
w=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}2^{-m}p_{\Lambda_{m}}
for an increasing sequence
\Lambda_{m}\in{\rm K}(G)
such that
\cup\Lambda_{m}=G
. Then
\mathfrak{p}^{-1}(\{p_{\Lambda}\})=w{\mathcal{S}}^{\otimes G}w
. If
\omega
is a frustration-free ground state for
\{p_{\Lambda}\}
, then
w
belongs to
{\mathcal{B}}_{\omega}
because
\omega(w)=0
, and, as such,
\mathfrak{p}^{-1}(\{p_{\Lambda}\})\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}_{\omega}
. Reciprocally, if
\mathfrak{p}^{-1}(\{p_{\Lambda}\})\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}_{\omega}
, then
w\in{\mathcal{B}}_{\omega}
and, as such,
\omega(w)=0
or
\omega(p_{\Lambda_{m}})=0
for all
m
. If
\Lambda\in{\rm K}(G)
, then
\Lambda\subseteq\Lambda_{m}
for some
m
, hence
\omega(p_{\Lambda})=0
for all
\Lambda\in{\rm K}(G)
and we can conclude that
\omega
is a frustration-free ground state for
\{p_{\Lambda}\}
.
∎

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
\{p_{\Lambda}\}
be a frustration-free system of proper projections. Then

\mathfrak{p}^{-1}(\{p_{\Lambda}\})=\bigcap\mathfrak{{\mathcal{B}}}_{\omega},

(73)

where the intersection runs over all frustration-free ground states of
\{p_{\Lambda}\}
.

Proof.

From
5.4
, we have the inclusion
\mathfrak{p}^{-1}(\{p_{\Lambda}\})\subseteq\bigcap\mathfrak{{\mathcal{B}}}_{\omega}
, hence the challenge is to prove the opposite inclusion. Any proper hereditary sub-
C^{\ast}
-algebra is the intersection of the maximal hereditary sub-
C^{\ast}
-algebras containing it. According to
3.4
and
5.4
, in the case of
\mathfrak{p}^{-1}(\{p_{\Lambda}\})
, the latter correspond to the pure states that are frustration-free ground states for
\{p_{\Lambda}\}
.
∎

Corollary 5.6
.

In the setting of
5.5
,

(\mathfrak{j}\circ\mathfrak{p}^{-1})(\{p_{\Lambda}\})=\bigcup\mathfrak{s}(\omega),

(74)

where the union runs over all frustration-free ground states for
\{p_{\Lambda}\}
. The statement remains valid if the union is restricted to pure frustration-free ground states.

Proof.

We recall that, for
\omega\in{\rm S}({\mathcal{S}}^{\otimes G})
,
\mathfrak{s}(\omega)=\mathfrak{j}({\mathcal{B}}_{\omega})
(see
3.6
). Then the statement follows by applying the lattice isomorphism
\mathfrak{j}
from
(\hat{\mathfrak{H}}({\mathcal{A}}),\subseteq)
to
(\mathfrak{P}[{\rm PS}({\mathcal{A}})],\supseteq)
on (
73
).
∎

We now can give a complete and intrinsic characterization of the frustration-free ground states:

Theorem 5.7
.

A state is a frustration-free ground state if and only if its support is contained in the support of a state whose hereditary
C^{\ast}
-algebra satisfies property (F).

Proof.

If
\omega
is the ground state for a frustration-free system of projections
\{p_{\Lambda}\}
, then its support must include the subset
(\mathfrak{j}\circ\mathfrak{p}^{-1})(\{p_{\Lambda}\})\subset{\rm PS}({\mathcal{S}}^{\otimes G})
. The hereditary
C^{\ast}
-subalgebra corresponding to this subset satisfies property (F). Reciprocally, if the hereditary
C^{\ast}
-subalgebra
{\mathcal{B}}_{\eta}
of a state
\eta
satisfies property (F), then
5.3
assures us that
\eta\big(p_{\Lambda}({\mathcal{B}}_{\eta})\big)=0
. Now, if
\mathfrak{s}(\omega)\subseteq\mathfrak{s}(\eta)
, then
{\mathcal{B}}_{\eta}\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}_{\omega}
and, as such,
\omega\big(p_{\Lambda}({\mathcal{B}}_{\eta})\big)=0
because
\omega({\mathcal{B}}_{\omega})=\{0\}
.
∎

Lastly, we introduce a well-known condition that assures that
(\mathfrak{j}\circ\mathfrak{p}^{-1})(\{p_{\Lambda}\})
consists of a single point:

Proposition 5.8
.

Suppose
\{p_{\Lambda}\}
is a frustration-free system of proper projections with the property that

\lim_{\Lambda\supseteq\Delta}\|p_{\Lambda}^{\bot}ap_{\Lambda}^{\bot}-\omega_{\Lambda}(a)p_{\Lambda}^{\bot}\|_{{\mathbb{B}}({\mathcal{H}}_{\Lambda})}=0

(75)

for any
\Delta\in{\rm K}(G)
and local observable
a\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\Delta}
, where

\omega_{\Lambda}(a)=\frac{1}{{\rm dim}\,p_{\Lambda}^{\bot}}{\rm Tr}(a\,p_{\Lambda}^{\bot}).

(76)

Then
\mathfrak{p}^{-1}(\{p_{\Lambda}\})
is maximal.

Proof.

According to
5.6
, it is enough to show that
\{p_{\Lambda}\}
accepts a unique frustration-free ground state. Note that
4.3
assures us that
(\mathfrak{j}\circ\mathfrak{p}^{-1})(\{p_{\Lambda}\})
is not void, that is,
\{p_{\Lambda}\}
accepts at least one frustration-free ground state. Let
a\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\Delta}\subseteq{\mathcal{S}}_{\Lambda}\simeq{\mathbb{B}}({\mathcal{H}}_{\Lambda})
be a local observable. Then

\left\|p_{\Lambda}^{\perp}ap_{\Lambda}^{\perp}-\omega_{\Lambda}(a)p_{\Lambda}^{\perp}\right\|=\sup^{\|\psi\|=1}_{\psi\in{\rm Ker}(p_{\Lambda})}\left|\langle\psi,a\psi\rangle-\omega_{\Lambda}(a)\right|.

(77)

Consider now two frustration-free ground states
\omega_{1}
and
\omega_{2}
of
\{p_{\Lambda}\}
. Then, there exist orthonormal vectors
\psi_{\Lambda}^{\alpha,j}\in{\rm Ker}(p_{\Lambda})
and positive coefficients
c_{\alpha,j}
such that

\omega_{\alpha}(a)=\sum_{j}c_{\alpha,j}\langle\psi_{\Lambda}^{\alpha,j},a\psi_{\Lambda}^{\alpha,j}\rangle
, with
\sum_{j}c_{\alpha,j}=1
,
\alpha=1,2
. Using (
77
), we have

\displaystyle|\omega_{1}(a)-\omega_{2}(a)|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\alpha}|\omega_{\alpha}(a)-\omega_{\Lambda}(a)|

(78)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\alpha,j}c_{\alpha,j}\left|\langle\psi_{\Lambda}^{\alpha,j},a\psi_{\Lambda}^{\alpha,j}\rangle-\omega_{\Lambda}(a)\right|

\displaystyle\leq 2\left\|p_{\Lambda}^{\perp}ap_{\Lambda}^{\perp}-\omega_{\Lambda}(a)p_{\Lambda}^{\perp}\right\|.

Taking the limit over
\Lambda
gives
\omega_{1}(a)=\omega_{2}(a)
. Since
\Delta
was arbitrary, this is true for all local observables and, by continuity,
\omega_{1}=\omega_{2}
.
∎

Remark 5.9
.

Condition (

[

]
. It is automatic that, if

is a proper

-invariant. An optimal version of the LTQO condition is supplied in

.

We start with a few context-free remarks about open projections. Henceforth, let
{\mathcal{A}}
be again a unital separable

-algebra and
{\mathcal{A}}^{\ast\ast}
be its double dual. The latter is naturally a

-algebra that can be concretely realized as the weak closure of the universal representation

is a

is a

Definition 6.1

(

]
)
.

A projection

\{a_{\alpha}\}\in{\mathcal{A}}
such that

. If

is open, one says that
p^{\bot}:=1-p

Proposition 6.2

(
[
2
,
4
]
)

We denote the sets of projections and of open projections in

by
{\mathcal{P}}({\mathcal{A}}^{\ast\ast})
and

, respectively, and we endow both with the partial order inherited from

({\mathcal{P}}({\mathcal{A}}^{\ast\ast}),\leq)
is a complete lattice and
({\mathcal{P}}_{o}({\mathcal{A}}^{\ast\ast}),\leq)

Proof.

The first statement is always true for

-algebras. Now, let

and consider the subset

J(p,p^{\prime})=\{q\in{\mathcal{P}}_{o}({\mathcal{A}}^{\ast\ast}),\ q\leq p,\ q\leq p^{\prime}\}.

(79)

Then

p\wedge_{o}p^{\prime}:=\sup\nolimits_{{\mathcal{P}}({\mathcal{A}}^{\ast\ast})}\,J(p,p^{\prime})

(80)

exists and is an open projection
[

. Clearly,
p\wedge_{o}p^{\prime}\leq p
and
p\wedge_{o}p^{\prime}\leq p^{\prime}
and

is the maximal open projection with this property.
∎

Remark 6.3
.

Note that, in general,
({\mathcal{P}}_{o}({\mathcal{A}}^{\ast\ast}),\leq)
is not a sublattice of
({\mathcal{P}}({\mathcal{A}}^{\ast\ast}),\leq)
, since

may not be open
[

[

, Prop. II.5]
.

\Diamond

Proposition 6.4

(
[
2
,
3
]
)

{\mathcal{P}}_{o}({\mathcal{A}}^{\ast\ast})
and
\mathfrak{H}({\mathcal{A}})
are isomorphic posets. In one direction, the isomorphism is implemented by

{\mathcal{P}}_{o}({\mathcal{A}}^{\ast\ast})\ni p\mapsto p{\mathcal{A}}^{\ast\ast}p\cap{\mathcal{A}}=p{\mathcal{A}}p\cap{\mathcal{A}}\in\mathfrak{H}({\mathcal{A}}).

(81)

and by

\mathfrak{H}({\mathcal{A}})\ni{\mathcal{B}}=\overline{w_{\mathcal{B}}{\mathcal{A}}w_{\mathcal{B}}}\mapsto p=\lim_{n\to\infty}w_{\mathcal{B}}^{1/n}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{o}({\mathcal{A}}^{\ast\ast})\subset{\mathcal{A}}^{**}

in the opposite direction.

6.1.
A density theorem

We now specialize to

.

Proposition 6.5
.

There exists a
G

\mathfrak{F}({\mathcal{S}}^{\otimes G})\to{\mathcal{P}}_{o}(({\mathcal{S}}^{\otimes G})^{\ast\ast})

(83)

Proof.

A frustration-free system of projections

, and hence a unique limit in

If

Remark 6.6
.

The subset of open projections induced by the frustration-free systems of projections also coincides with the image of

through the map

\Diamond

Proposition 6.7
.

{\mathcal{P}}_{o}^{F}(({\mathcal{S}}^{\otimes G})^{\ast\ast})
is dense in
{\mathcal{P}}_{o}(({\mathcal{S}}^{\otimes G})^{\ast\ast})
in the norm topology of
{\mathbb{B}}({\mathcal{H}}_{u})
.

Proof.

Let
p\in{\mathcal{P}}_{o}(({\mathcal{S}}^{\otimes G})^{\ast\ast})
and
{\mathcal{B}}=p\,{\mathcal{S}}^{\otimes G}\,p\cap{\mathcal{S}}^{\otimes G}
be the associated hereditary sub-
C^{\ast}
-algebra. Let
\epsilon_{n}\to 0

, we pick a unitary element

as in (

\mathfrak{H}^{F}({\mathcal{S}}^{\otimes G})
, hence their corresponding open projections

Lemma 6.11
.

A frustration-free system
\{p_{\Lambda}\}
of proper projections has a unique frustration-free ground state if and only if
(\lim p_{\Lambda})^{\perp}
is a minimal projection in
({\mathcal{S}}^{\otimes G})^{\ast\ast}
.

Proof.

We show first that, in the assumed setting,
(\mathfrak{j}\circ\mathfrak{p}^{-1})(\{p_{\Lambda}\})\subset{\rm PS}({\mathcal{S}}^{\otimes G})
must consist of a single point. Indeed, suppose the contrary. Then, according to

(\mathfrak{j}\circ\mathfrak{p}^{-1})(\{p_{\Lambda}\})
supplies a distinct pure state which is a frustration-free ground state for
\{p_{\Lambda}\}
. This contradicts uniqueness. Therefore,
(\mathfrak{j}\circ\mathfrak{p}^{-1})(\{p_{\Lambda}\})
consists of one point, which automatically implies that the hereditary
C^{\ast}
-subalgebra corresponding to
\{p_{\Lambda}\}
is maximal, hence the corresponding open projection
p=\lim p_{\Lambda}
has the stated property.
∎

Theorem 6.12

(Optimal LTQO)
.

A frustration-free system
\{p_{\Lambda}\}
of proper projections has a unique ground state if and only if there exists a state
\omega
on

such that

{\rm SOT}_{{\mathbb{B}}({\mathcal{H}}_{u})}-\lim\big(p_{\Lambda}^{\perp}ap_{\Lambda}^{\perp}-\omega(a)p_{\Lambda}^{\perp}\big)=0.

Furthermore, if (

\omega
is the unique frustration-free ground state, which is necessarily pure.

Proof.

Suppose
\{p_{\Lambda}\}
has a unique frustration-free ground state and let
p=\lim p_{\Lambda}
. Then
p^{\perp}=\lim p_{\Lambda}^{\perp}
and
p^{\perp}
is a minimal projection in
({\mathcal{S}}^{\otimes G})^{\ast\ast}
. The latter is equivalent to the fact that
p^{\perp}({\mathcal{S}}^{\otimes G})^{\ast\ast}p^{\perp}={\mathbb{C}}\cdot p^{\perp}
and, as such, for any

we must have

is linear, positive and unital, hence a state over
{\mathcal{S}}^{\otimes G}

p^{\perp}ap^{\perp}=\omega(a)p^{\perp}
implies (
84
).

Assume now that (
84

. Then
p^{\perp}ap^{\perp}=\omega(a)p^{\perp}
and this relation holds over
({\mathcal{S}}^{\otimes G})^{\ast\ast}
if

is canonically extended to a normal state. As such,
p^{\perp}
is minimal, which automatically implies that
\{p_{\Lambda}\}
has a unique frustration-free ground state. The latter must coincide with
\omega
.
∎

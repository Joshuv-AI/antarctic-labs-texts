Virtual reality-guided mindfulness intervention to enhance psychosocial well-being in caregivers of end-stage kidney disease patients: protocol for a mixed-methods pilot randomised controlled trial
Authors: Ravi Shankar, Wei Zhen Hong, Anjali Bundele, Koh Pei Si, Amartya Mukhopadhyay
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionCaregivers of patients with end-stage kidney disease (ESKD) face significant challenges that contribute to caregiver burden, negatively impacting their psychosocial well-being. Virtual-reality (VR)-guided mindfulness interventions have shown promise in reducing stress, anxiety and depression in various populations.Methods and analysisThis pilot study aims to evaluate the efficacy and feasibility of a VR-guided mindfulness intervention for ESKD caregivers. A single-centre, single-blind, parallel-group pilot randomised controlled trial will be conducted. Thirty ESKD caregivers will be randomly allocated to either the VR-guided mindfulness intervention group or the sham VR control group. The intervention group will receive a 6 week home-based VR-guided mindfulness programme, while the control group will view relaxing nature videos without mindfulness content. Participants will be assessed at baseline, post-intervention (6 weeks) and follow-up (12 weeks) using validated questionnaires for caregiver burden (Zarit Burden Interview (ZBI)), stress, anxiety, depression (Depression Anxiety Stress Scale-21 (DASS-21)), quality of life (36-Item Short Form Health Survey (SF-36)) and mindfulness (Five Facet Mindfulness Questionnaire (FFMQ)). Feasibility outcomes include accrual rates, retention, adherence, questionnaire completion and side effect rates. Semi-structured interviews will explore participants’ experiences with the intervention.Ethics and disseminationThe study has been approved by the NHG Domain Specific Review Board (Reference: 2024–3940-APP1). The results of this pilot study will be reported in peer-reviewed open-access journals and shared with participants and stakeholders.Trial registrationNCT06479200.

Introduction

Background and rationale

Caregivers of patients with end-stage kidney disease (ESKD) face numerous challenges that contribute to significant caregiver burden, including managing complex care requirements, providing emotional support and coordinating healthcare services.16 Some ESKD (defined as stage 4 and 5 chronic kidney disease with an estimated glomerular filtration rate of <30 mL/min/1.73 m² (normal >90)7 8) patients require to undergo dialysis or kidney transplantation, which are physically and emotionally challenging for both patients and their caregivers. Such a demanding caregiving role can lead to physical, emotional and social strain, ultimately impacting caregivers’ quality of life.35 The prevalence of significant caregiver burden in this population ranges from 26% to 50%,9 10 and with the rising prevalence of diabetes, a major risk factor for kidney failure, the number of ESKD patients and their caregivers is expected to increase.11

Caregiver burden is associated with various negative outcomes, such as increased risk of depression, anxiety, sleep disturbances and diminished quality of life.12 It can also have detrimental effects on patient outcomes, as stressed and overburdened caregivers may struggle to provide optimal care.11 Several interventions have been developed to support ESKD caregivers, including educational programmes, counselling services and support groups.13 Educational interventions have shown some promise in improving caregivers’ knowledge about ESKD and its management.13 However, these programmes often focus primarily on practical skills and disease information, neglecting the psychological and emotional aspects of caregiving.14 Counselling interventions, while potentially beneficial for addressing psychological distress, are often resource-intensive and may not be readily accessible to all caregivers due to time constraints and geographical limitations.15 Support groups have been implemented to provide emotional support and facilitate peer connections among ESKD caregivers.13 While these groups can offer valuable social support, their effectiveness in reducing caregiver burden and improving overall well-being has been inconsistent.16 Additionally, participation in support groups may be challenging for caregivers with demanding schedules or those living in remote areas. Web-based interventions have emerged as a potential solution to address accessibility issues.17 However, many of these interventions lack personalisation and fail to provide the immersive, experiential learning that may be particularly beneficial for stress reduction and emotional regulation.18 Despite these interventions for ESKD caregivers, support options for this population remain limited, highlighting the need for innovative and effective interventions.13

Mindfulness, which involves maintaining a moment-by-moment awareness of thoughts, feelings, bodily sensations and the environment with openness, curiosity and acceptance,14 15 19 has emerged as a promising intervention for reducing stress, anxiety and depression. Mindfulness-based interventions (MBIs) have been shown to improve psychological well-being in various populations, including caregivers.17 20

Mindfulness interventions may be particularly well suited for addressing the unique challenges faced by ESKD caregivers. These practices cultivate present-moment awareness and non-judgmental acceptance, which can help caregivers manage chronic stress and focus on the present rather than ruminating on past difficulties or anticipating future problems.13 18 21 Mindfulness also promotes emotional regulation and self-compassion, crucial for maintaining psychological well-being amidst ongoing caregiving demands.4 22 23 By cultivating a non-reactive stance towards physical sensations and thoughts, mindfulness practices can help caregivers manage fatigue and burnout more effectively, which is especially relevant given the disrupted sleep patterns and chronic fatigue often experienced by ESKD caregivers.3 24

The hypothesised mechanisms through which mindfulness may reduce caregiver burden, stress, anxiety and depression include enhanced attentional control, cognitive reappraisal, increased self-awareness, reduced rumination, enhanced self-compassion and improved emotion regulation.1822 2528 Attentional control helps caregivers manage competing demands more effectively, while cognitive reappraisal promotes more adaptive appraisals of caregiving stressors. Increased self-awareness enables early recognition of stress or burnout signs, and reduced rumination decreases repetitive negative thinking. Self-compassion counteracts self-criticism and guilt, while improved emotion regulation strengthens the ability to observe and modulate emotional responses. By targeting these mechanisms, mindfulness interventions offer a comprehensive approach to addressing the multifaceted challenges faced by ESKD caregivers. The use of VR technology for mindfulness training could amplify these benefits by offering an immersive and engaging experience that supports the learning and practice of mindfulness skills. Traditional MBIs often necessitate in-person participation, which can be difficult for caregivers to manage due to their busy schedules and responsibilities.29

Virtual reality (VR) technology provides a novel and engaging, immersive platform that enhances the effectiveness of mindfulness interventions.2931 VR allows users to interact with computer-generated environments in a way that feels real, providing a sense of presence and immersion.32 This immersive experience can help users focus their attention on the present moment, reducing distractions and facilitating mindfulness practice.33 Initial studies have shown promising results for VR-based mindfulness interventions in reducing stress, improving well-being and reducing anxiety symptoms.3031 3437 However, to our knowledge, no studies have specifically investigated the use of VR-guided mindfulness for caregivers of ESKD patients, highlighting the need to address this gap in the research.

Interventions

VR-guided mindfulness intervention

Participants will receive a 6-week home-based VR-guided mindfulness intervention using the Oculus Quest 3 (or equivalent) headset and a mindfulness application. They will be instructed to practice 10–15 min of VR-guided mindfulness daily.

The 6-week intervention duration was chosen to balance efficacy and adherence while aligning with shortened versions of established mindfulness protocols. This timeframe provides sufficient opportunity for participants to learn and integrate mindfulness skills while remaining feasible for caregivers with demanding responsibilities. Previous studies on VR-based interventions have shown significant effects can be achieved within 4–6 weeks, and the immersive nature of VR may facilitate more rapid skill acquisition compared with traditional methods.203844

The study will use existing VR content developed by TRIPP, a company specialising in immersive meditation and mindfulness experiences. Many studies have successfully employed off-the-shelf VR content by TRIPP for mindfulness interventions, demonstrating its effectiveness and reliability.4550 This decision was based on the established quality of TRIPP’s content, its evidence based on reducing stress and improving well-being, cost-effectiveness, standardisation and ecological validity. The TRIPP application offers a variety of guided meditations, immersive environments and customisable features to maintain engagement and prevent boredom.51

The programme structure includes daily guided meditations with varying focus areas each week, such as breath awareness, body scan, thoughts and emotions, self-compassion and mindfulness in daily life. The content features diverse immersive environments (eg, natural settings, abstract landscapes, space-themed environments), interactive elements synchronised with breath or gaze and a range of ambient sounds and music. Participants can customise various aspects of their experience, including visual intensity, audio preferences and session length.

The study incorporates a 6-week post-intervention assessment and a 12-week follow-up assessment. The 6-week assessment allows for immediate evaluation of the intervention’s effects, capturing any short-term benefits while minimising potential attrition.39 The 12-week follow-up examines the durability of the intervention’s effects over a slightly longer term, providing insights into whether participants continue to practice mindfulness and maintain any benefits after the structured intervention period.43 52 This assessment timeline strikes a balance between capturing medium-term effects and maintaining feasibility within the constraints of a pilot study. While longer follow-up periods (eg, 6 months or 1 year) would provide valuable information on long-term effects, they were deemed beyond the scope of this pilot study due to resource limitations and the potential for increased attrition.53

Sham VR control

Participants will receive a 6-week home-based sham VR intervention (10–15 min/day) viewing relaxing nature videos without mindfulness content, matched for duration and VR experience. The sham VR control condition will use the same VR headset as the intervention group but will not include any guided mindfulness practices or instructions. This active control condition is designed to account for the potential effects of using VR technology and engaging in a relaxing activity, allowing for a more rigorous evaluation of the specific effects of the VR-guided mindfulness intervention.

Adherence monitoring

To promote adherence to the intervention and control conditions, participants will receive weekly check-in calls from the research team. During these calls, participants will be asked about their experiences with the VR programme, any challenges they faced and strategies they used to maintain their practice. The research team will provide support and encouragement to help participants continue their daily VR sessions. Objective tracking of VR usage will be conducted through the VR application, which will record the frequency and duration of each session. Participants will also receive reminders and nudges via the VR application and text messages to encourage regular practice.

Strategies for caregiver engagement and adherence facilitation

To address the practical challenges faced by ESKD caregivers and enhance their ability to engage consistently with the VR-guided mindfulness programme, several strategies will be implemented. Participants can choose the most convenient time for their practice, whether early morning, during patient’s dialysis sessions or before bedtime. Caregivers will be educated on the importance of self-care and strategies for incorporating mindfulness into caregiving activities. With participant consent, other family members or support persons will be engaged to encourage and facilitate the caregiver’s practice time.

An online forum will allow participants to share experiences and tips for incorporating practice into their caregiving routine. Technical support will be readily available to resolve any issues quickly. These measures are designed to maximise engagement and potential benefit within the constraints of participants’ caregiving responsibilities while acknowledging that perfect adherence may not be possible. The effectiveness of these adherence-enhancing strategies will be evaluated as part of the feasibility assessment, providing valuable insights for future iterations of the intervention and larger-scale studies.

Sample size

A sample size of 30 participants (15 per group) was chosen for this pilot RCT based on recommendations for pilot studies aimed at estimating effect sizes and assessing feasibility.62 This sample size is considered sufficient to provide preliminary data on the intervention’s efficacy, feasibility and acceptability, as well as to inform sample size calculations for a future definitive RCT. As a pilot study, the primary focus is on generating effect size estimates and assessing feasibility rather than hypothesis testing.63 The analysis of the outcomes will focus on estimating effect sizes and variability to inform sample size calculations for a future definitive trial.

Allocation

Enrolled participants will be randomly allocated to either the VR-guided mindfulness intervention group or the sham VR control group using a 1:1 allocation ratio. Randomisation will be performed using a computer-generated randomisation sequence with permuted blocks of 4 to ensure balanced allocation while maintaining unpredictability.64 The randomisation sequence will be generated by an independent statistician not involved in participant recruitment or assessment.

To ensure allocation concealment, the randomisation sequence will be stored in sealed, opaque, sequentially numbered envelopes. These envelopes will be prepared by a research staff member not involved in participant recruitment or assessment. On enrolment, the next sequential envelope will be opened to determine the participant’s group allocation.

Data collection methods

Outcome questionnaires

The outcome questionnaires (ZBI, DASS-21, SF-36 and FFMQ) will be administered at baseline, post-intervention (6 weeks) and follow-up (12 weeks). These questionnaires will be completed electronically using a secure online platform (eg, REDCap) or on paper, depending on participant preference. To minimise missing data, participants will be encouraged to complete all questionnaires at each assessment time point, and reminders will be sent via email or text message.

Feasibility and acceptability measures

Feasibility outcomes (accrual rate, retention rate, adherence rate, questionnaire completion rate and side effect rate) will be collected throughout the study period. Accrual and retention rates will be calculated based on the number of eligible caregivers approached, enrolled and retained in the study. Adherence rate will be assessed using objective data from the VR application, which will record the number and duration of completed sessions for each participant. Questionnaire completion rate will be determined by the proportion of participants who complete all outcome questionnaires at each assessment time point. Side effect rate will be assessed using a study-specific questionnaire administered at the post-intervention assessment, which will ask participants to report any adverse effects experienced during VR use.

Acceptability outcomes (participant satisfaction and qualitative feedback) will be collected at the post-intervention assessment. Participant satisfaction will be assessed using a study-specific questionnaire, which will include items on perceived helpfulness, enjoyment and ease of use of the VR programme, rated on a five-point Likert scale. Qualitative feedback will be gathered through semi-structured interviews conducted with a subset of participants (n=10–15) at the post-intervention assessment. Interviews will be conducted by a trained researcher and will follow a pre-determined interview guide covering topics such as overall experience with the VR programme, perceived benefits and challenges and suggestions for improvement. Interviews will be audio-recorded and transcribed verbatim for analysis.

Data management

All study data will be managed in accordance with the principles of Good Clinical Practice and local regulatory requirements. Electronic data will be stored on secure, password-protected servers with access restricted to authorised study personnel. Paper-based data will be stored in locked filing cabinets in a secure location. All data will be de-identified using unique participant study IDs to ensure confidentiality.

Data entry and validation will be performed by trained research staff. A random subset of entered data (eg, 10%) will be double-checked against the original source documents to ensure accuracy. Any discrepancies will be resolved through discussion with the study team and, if necessary, by referring back to the original source documents.

Regular data backups will be performed to prevent data loss. A comprehensive data management plan will be developed prior to study commencement, detailing the specific procedures for data collection, storage, security and access.

Data analysis

This study employs a convergent mixed-methods approach, integrating quantitative and qualitative data to provide a comprehensive understanding of the VR-guided mindfulness intervention’s efficacy and feasibility for ESKD caregivers. The integration of quantitative and qualitative data will follow a concurrent triangulation design, involving data transformation, comparison, typology development and joint display creation.

Quantitative data analysis

Descriptive statistics will be used to summarise participant characteristics, feasibility outcomes and adverse events. Continuous variables will be reported as means and standard deviations (or medians and interquartile ranges for non-normally distributed data), while categorical variables will be reported as frequencies and percentages.

The primary focus of this pilot study will be on estimating effect sizes (Cohen’s d) for between-group differences in change scores from baseline to post-intervention and follow-up for each outcome measure (ZBI, DASS-21, SF-36 and FFMQ). Effect sizes will be calculated as the difference in mean change scores between the VR-guided mindfulness intervention and sham VR control groups, divided by the pooled SD of the change scores. 95% CIs for the effect sizes will be calculated to provide a measure of precision. These effect size estimates will be used to inform sample size calculations for a future definitive trial.

Given the pilot nature of this study and the small sample size, no formal hypothesis testing will be conducted. However, for descriptive purposes, between-group differences in change scores will be examined using independent t-tests (for normally distributed data) or Mann–Whitney U tests (for non-normally distributed data). Within-group changes from baseline to post-intervention and follow-up will be examined using paired t-tests or Wilcoxon signed-rank tests, as appropriate.

Feasibility outcomes (accrual rate, retention rate, adherence rate, questionnaire completion rate and side effect rate) will be reported descriptively, with 95% CIs calculated using the Clopper–Pearson exact method.

All quantitative analyses will be performed using R statistical software (version 4.0 or higher).

Integration of quantitative and qualitative data

To manage divergent results, several strategies will be employed. Researchers will attempt to reconcile discrepancies by considering potential explanations such as differences in data collection methods or sample characteristics. Divergent findings will be used as a starting point for generating new insights or hypotheses about the intervention’s effects and mechanisms. The breadth of inquiry may be expanded based on divergent results, potentially identifying areas for future research or intervention refinement. Participants will be consulted through member checking to provide feedback on the interpretations of divergent results. Finally, all divergent findings will be reported transparently in the study results, along with proposed explanations and implications for interpretation.

This comprehensive approach to data analysis and integration aims to provide a nuanced understanding of the intervention’s efficacy and feasibility, leveraging the strengths of both quantitative and qualitative methods while addressing potential discrepancies in a systematic and transparent manner.6575

Data monitoring

Given the low-risk nature of the intervention and the short duration of this pilot study, a formal data monitoring committee will not be established. However, the principal investigator will be responsible for regularly reviewing study progress, data quality and adverse events and will consult with the study team and the institutional review board (IRB) if any concerns arise regarding participant safety or data integrity.

The study has been approved by the NHG Domain Specific Review Board (DSRB Reference: 2024–3940-APP1). Any amendments to the protocol will be submitted to the DSRB for review and approval before implementation.

All participants will provide written informed consent prior to study enrolment. Participant confidentiality will be maintained throughout the study. All study data will be de-identified using unique participant study IDs, and any identifying information will be stored separately from the research data. Access to study data will be restricted to authorised study personnel who have completed the necessary training in data protection and confidentiality.

Dissemination policy

The results of this pilot study will be disseminated through peer-reviewed scientific journals and presentations at national and international conferences. Manuscripts will be prepared in accordance with the Consolidated Standards of Reporting Trials extension for pilot and feasibility trials76 and the Consolidated Criteria for Reporting Qualitative Research,77 as appropriate.

In addition to academic dissemination, plain language summaries of the study findings will be prepared and shared with participants, their families and relevant patient organisations. If the pilot study demonstrates promising results, the findings will be used to inform the design and conduct of a larger definitive RCT, which will be registered in a publicly accessible clinical trial registry.

Patient and public involvement

ESKD caregivers were involved in the development of the research question and study design. Two caregiver representatives provided input on the feasibility of the intervention and helped refine outcome measures. These representatives will also be involved in the interpretation of results. Participants will be offered a summary of the study findings and invited to a presentation of results.

Discussion

Potential impact

This pilot study aims to provide preliminary evidence on the efficacy and feasibility of a novel VR-guided mindfulness intervention for ESKD caregivers, a population that faces significant challenges and is at high risk of caregiver burden and its associated negative outcomes. If the intervention demonstrates promising results, it could lead to the development of a low-cost, accessible and scalable approach to reducing caregiver burden and improving psychosocial well-being among ESKD caregivers.

The findings of this pilot study will inform the design and conduct of a future definitive RCT, which will provide more conclusive evidence on the intervention’s effectiveness. If the definitive RCT confirms the intervention’s benefits, it could have important implications for clinical practice and healthcare policy, potentially leading to the integration of VR-guided mindfulness interventions into standard care for ESKD caregivers.

Furthermore, the insights gained from this study may have relevance beyond the specific population of ESKD caregivers. The VR-guided mindfulness intervention, if successful, could serve as a model for the development and evaluation of similar technology-based interventions for caregivers of individuals with other chronic conditions.

Strengths and limitations

This pilot study has several strengths. First, it employs a rigorous RCT design with a sham VR control condition, which will help to isolate the specific effects of the VR-guided mindfulness intervention from the potential effects of using VR technology alone. Second, the study uses a mixed-methods approach, combining quantitative and qualitative data to provide a comprehensive understanding of the intervention’s efficacy, feasibility and acceptability. Third, the study assesses a range of relevant outcomes, including caregiver burden, psychosocial well-being and mindfulness, which will provide a holistic evaluation of the intervention’s potential benefits.

However, there are also several limitations to consider. The single-centre design and convenience sampling method may limit the generalisability of the findings. The small sample size, while appropriate for a pilot study, may not be sufficient to detect significant between-group differences in outcomes. The reliance on self-reported measures may be subject to social desirability bias, and the short-term follow-up period may not capture the long-term sustainability of the intervention’s effects. Finally, as with any technology-based intervention, there may be challenges related to participant familiarity with VR technology and potential technical difficulties.

Despite these limitations, this pilot study represents an important step in evaluating the potential of VR-guided mindfulness interventions for ESKD caregivers and informing the design of future larger-scale trials.

Conclusion

The study addresses a significant gap in the support available for this vulnerable population and has the potential to inform the development of innovative, technology-based interventions to reduce caregiver burden and improve psychosocial well-being. VR technology, being condition-agnostic, can also be applied to various scenarios with high stress and anxiety, including chronic illnesses, mental health conditions or disabilities, thereby broadening the intervention’s impact and utility.

Trial status

This trial is currently in the planning stage. Participant recruitment is expected to commence in November 2024, and the anticipated completion date is March 2025.

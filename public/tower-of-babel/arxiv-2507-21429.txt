From Sublinear to Linear: Fast Convergence in Deep Networks via Locally Polyak-Lojasiewicz Regions
Agnideep Aich, Ashit Baran Aich, Bruce Wade

Abstract
The convergence of gradient descent (GD) on the non-convex loss landscapes of deep neural networks (DNNs) presents a fundamental theoretical challenge. While recent work has established that GD converges to a stationary point at a sublinear rate within locally quasi-convex regions (LQCRs), this fails to explain the exponential convergence rates consistently observed in practice. In this paper, we resolve this discrepancy by proving that under a mild assumption on Neural Tangent Kernel (NTK) stability, these same regions satisfy a local Polyak-Lojasiewicz (PL) condition. We introduce the concept of a Locally Polyak-Lojasiewicz Region (LPLR), where the squared gradient norm lower-bounds the suboptimality gap, prove that properly initialized finite-width networks admit such regions around initialization, and establish that GD achieves linear convergence within an LPLR, providing the first finite-width guarantee that matches empirically observed rates. We validate our theory across diverse settings, from controlled experiments on fully-connected networks to modern ResNet architectures trained with stochastic methods, demonstrating that LPLR structure emerges robustly in practical deep learning scenarios. By rigorously connecting local landscape geometry to fast optimization through the NTK framework, our work provides a definitive theoretical explanation for the remarkable efficiency of gradient-based optimization in deep learning.

1.  Introduction

The empirical success of deep learning is largely driven by a remarkable phenomenon: the surprising effectiveness of simple, gradient-based optimizers. The loss functions of deep neural networks (DNNs) are highly non-convex, and classical optimization theory suggests that first-order methods like gradient descent (GD) should be thwarted by a landscape riddled with spurious local minima and saddle points. This stark discrepancy between theory and practice has been a central puzzle. Early work began to resolve this by showing that the puzzle was more nuanced; for instance, by modeling the loss with a spin-glass Hamiltonian,
choromanska2015loss
showed that poor local minima are exponentially rare in high dimensions, though saddle points are plentiful. Subsequent work confirmed that for deep linear networks, all local minima are in fact global
kawaguchi2016deep
.

Recent advances have begun to bridge this theory-practice gap through more sophisticated analyses of local geometry and convergence conditions.
an2024convergence
established convergence of SGD under local Lojasiewicz conditions for deep networks, while recent work has derived local PL conditions for overparameterized linear models under relaxed assumptions
xu2025local
. Simultaneously, the development of specialized architectures has shown that PL-like properties can be built into network design:
wang2024monotone
introduced PLNet architectures that inherently satisfy the Polyak-Łojasiewicz condition. However, these approaches either require restrictive assumptions about network architecture or apply only to simplified settings like linear networks. Our work addresses this limitation by demonstrating that PL-like properties emerge naturally from the geometric structure of locally quasi-convex regions (LQCRs) in practical deep networks, without requiring architectural modifications or restrictive assumptions about infinite width.

This has motivated several lines of research to explain why GD still succeeds. One line focuses on landscape characterization, arguing that for sufficiently wide networks, the geometry of the loss surface is benign. It has been shown that if a single hidden layer is wider than the dataset, almost all local minima are global
nguyen2017loss
, and that even when suboptimal minima exist, they often lie on connected manifolds that are non-attracting, allowing GD to escape
petzka2021nonattracting
. Another powerful line of research focuses on over-parameterization, proving that for sufficiently wide networks, GD with random initialization finds a global minimum, often at a linear rate
du2019width
;
du2018global
. A key insight here is that over-parameterization ensures the associated Gram matrix remains stable throughout training, guaranteeing convergence
du2018global
. Importantly, these guarantees do not always require extreme network widths; some theories show that widths scaling just linearly with dataset size can be sufficient for finding global, generalizable solutions
kawaguchi2020practical
. Recent work by
Aich2025
introduced
Locally Quasi-Convex Regions
(LQCRs), demonstrating that even practical, finite-width networks possess well-behaved regions around initialization where GD is guaranteed to converge to a stationary point, albeit with a sublinear rate.

A crucial gap remains: a sublinear rate does not capture the fast, exponential convergence often observed in practice. This paper resolves this inconsistency. We build upon the LQCR framework to prove that GD can, and does, achieve a linear convergence rate under practical conditions. Our key insight is to connect the geometry of LQCRs to the Polyak-Lojasiewicz (PL) condition
Polyak1963
;
Karimi2016
. While the PL condition is too strong to hold globally for DNNs, we show that it is sufficient for it to hold locally within the same region where optimization takes place.

We formalize this by introducing the Locally Polyak-Lojasiewicz Region (LPLR). We prove that under a mild assumption on the stability of the NTK within an LQCR, the region is guaranteed to be an LPLR. Our main contributions are as follows:

•

Natural Emergence of PL Properties:
Unlike recent work that designs specialized architectures to satisfy PL conditions
wang2024monotone
or assumes them for specific models
an2024convergence
, we prove that such properties emerge naturally from geometric structure in standard networks.

•

Finite-Width Guarantees:
While most NTK-based analyses require infinite or exponentially large widths, our local stability condition holds for moderately overparameterized networks, making our results practically relevant.

•

General Architecture Applicability:
Our framework applies to standard deep networks without architectural modifications, unlike specialized approaches
wang2024monotone
that require specific network designs.

•

Bridge Theory-Practice Gap:
We provide the first finite-width explanation for the linear convergence rates consistently observed in practice, resolving a fundamental discrepancy in deep learning theory.

This work provides a more complete picture of DNN optimization, showing not only that GD converges, but that it converges fast, and provides a rigorous theoretical basis for this widely observed phenomenon. The paper is structured as follows. Section
2
reviews related work. Section
4
formalizes our framework. Our main theoretical results are presented in Section
5
. We then empirically validate these findings in Section
6
, and conclude in Section
7
.

2.  Related Work

2.1
Local Convergence Analysis and PL-type Conditions

The classical Polyak-Lojasiewicz condition has experienced a renaissance in deep learning theory.
an2024convergence
;
Polyak1963
;
delarue2025genericity
proved convergence of stochastic gradient descent under a local Lojasiewicz condition, showing that even approximate versions of the PL property can drive convergence in non-convex settings. This builds on the foundational work of
Karimi2016
, who demonstrated that the PL condition provides a unifying framework for understanding linear convergence across diverse optimization algorithms
Karimi2016
;
Necoara2015
.

Recent Architectural Developments:

wang2024monotone
introduced a fundamentally different approach by designing networks (PLNet) that inherently satisfy the Polyak-Łojasiewicz condition by construction. While innovative, this approach requires specialized architectures and cannot explain why standard networks exhibit fast convergence.

Linear Network Analysis:
Recent theoretical progress has established local PL conditions for two-layer linear networks under general losses and relaxed assumptions on step size, width, and initialization
xu2025local
. Moreover, mild over-parameterization alone suffices for linear convergence in two-layer nets, as shown by
zhou2021local
. However, this analysis is limited to linear networks and does not extend to the non-linear case that dominates practical applications.

Our Contribution:
Unlike these approaches, we show that PL-like properties emerge naturally from geometric constraints (LQCRs) in standard architectures, providing a general explanation for fast convergence without requiring architectural modifications.

2.2
Neural Tangent Kernel Theory and Finite-Width Analysis

The Neural Tangent Kernel framework, introduced by
Jacot2018
, revolutionized the theoretical understanding of neural network training by showing that infinitely wide networks behave as kernel methods. This insight has been crucial for proving global convergence guarantees, but typically requires unrealistic infinite-width assumptions
Jacot2018
;
novak2022fast
.

Finite-Width Extensions:
Recent work has sought to extend NTK insights to finite-width settings.
AllenZhu2019
showed that sufficiently wide networks can maintain NTK stability, while
Arora2019a
characterized when finite networks approximate the infinite-width behavior
Arora2019a
;
AllenZhu2019
;
frei2022proxy
. However, these results often require exponentially large widths to provide meaningful guarantees.

Local Stability:
Our key insight is that global NTK stability is unnecessarily strong. We only require local stability within the optimization region, a much weaker condition that holds for moderately wide networks under standard initialization.

2.3
Landscape Geometry and Optimization Dynamics

Understanding the geometric structure of neural network loss landscapes has been crucial for explaining optimization success.
choromanska2015loss
showed that poor local minima are exponentially rare using spin-glass models, while
kawaguchi2016deep
proved that all local minima are global for deep linear networks.

Local Geometric Analysis:
Recent work has developed general mathematical frameworks for loss landscapes in over-parameterized systems, providing modern views of efficient optimization. The LQCR framework
Aich2025
contributes to this line by providing concrete geometric conditions that ensure both convergence and fast rates.

Connection to Practice:
A key challenge has been connecting geometric insights to practical training dynamics. Our work addresses this by showing that the same geometric properties (LQCR structure) that ensure convergence also provide the functional properties (local PL condition) needed for fast optimization.

4.  From Local Geometry to the Polyak-Lojasiewicz Condition

In this section, we introduce the concepts required to link the local geometry of a neural network’s loss landscape to a condition sufficient for linear convergence. Our central idea is the Locally Polyak-Lojasiewicz Region (LPLR), a neighborhood where the loss function exhibits favorable properties for fast optimization. In this context, the aim is to explain the rapid, linear convergence of gradient-based methods observed in practice, which stands in contrast to the sublinear rates guaranteed by existing theoretical models. One can tackle this problem by demonstrating that the well-behaved local regions established in prior work also satisfy the Polyak-Lojasiewicz (PL) condition locally, a property we formalize below.

The PL condition, first introduced by Polyak
Polyak1963
, is a powerful tool for proving linear convergence on non-convex functions. However, the global PL condition, defined as

\frac{1}{2}||\nabla\mathcal{L}(\theta)||^{2}\geq\mu(\mathcal{L}(\theta)-\mathcal{L}^{*})

(1)

where
\mathcal{L}^{*}
is the global minimum, is too restrictive for most DNNs. This motivates a localized version that only needs to hold within the region of optimization.

Definition 4.1
(Locally Polyak-Lojasiewicz (LPL) Region)

A region
\mathcal{R}\subset\mathbb{R}^{p}
is a
(\mu,\mathcal{R})
-Locally Polyak-Lojasiewicz Region (LPLR) if the PL condition holds for all
\theta\in\mathcal{R}
:

\frac{1}{2}||\nabla\mathcal{L}(\theta)||^{2}\geq\mu(\mathcal{L}(\theta)-\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{R}}^{*})\quad\forall\theta\in\mathcal{R}

(2)

where
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{R}}^{*}=\min_{\theta\in\mathcal{R}}\mathcal{L}(\theta)
is the minimum value of the loss within the region
\mathcal{R}
.

The existence of such an LPLR is not arbitrary; it is a direct consequence of the underlying geometry of the region
\mathcal{R}
. Specifically, this analysis builds upon the concept of a Locally Quasi-Convex Region (LQCR) introduced in
Aich2025
. An LQCR is a region with bounded negative curvature and a guaranteed descent direction, properties that ensure iterates make steady progress.

Definition 4.2
(Locally Quasi-Convex Region
Aich2025
)

A region
\mathcal{R}\subset\mathbb{R}^{p}
is an
(\alpha,\gamma)
-LQCR if for all
\theta,\phi\in\mathcal{R}
:

i.

Curvature Bound:

\mathcal{L}(\phi)\geq\mathcal{L}(\theta)+\nabla\mathcal{L}(\theta)^{\top}(\phi-\theta)-\frac{\alpha}{2}||\phi-\theta||^{2}
.

ii.

Descent Condition:
If
\mathcal{L}(\phi)<\mathcal{L}(\theta)
, then
\langle\nabla\mathcal{L}(\theta),\phi-\theta\rangle\geq\gamma||\phi-\theta||||\nabla\mathcal{L}(\theta)||
.

The critical link between the geometry of an LQCR and the functional property of an LPLR is the Neural Tangent Kernel (NTK)
Jacot2018
. The empirical NTK,
\Theta_{\theta}
, is defined from the network Jacobian
J_{\theta}(x)
as
\Theta_{\theta}(x,x^{\prime})=J_{\theta}(x)J_{\theta}(x^{\prime})^{\top}
. For our analysis to hold, we assume that this kernel remains well-behaved within the LQCR. This local stability is expected in many modern deep learning scenarios, including sufficiently overparameterized or wide networks, and when using standard initialization schemes and constrained learning rates
Arora2019a
;
AllenZhu2019
;
Aich2025
.

One can show that an LQCR satisfying this local NTK stability is also an LPLR. This is a key result of our work, formally connecting local geometry to a condition for fast optimization. This connection arises from an alternative way to view the suboptimality gap. By leveraging the NTK, one can relate the suboptimality gap within the region to the squared gradient norm. Specifically, for a point
\theta\in\mathcal{R}
where the smallest eigenvalue of the NTK,
\lambda_{\min}(\Theta_{\theta})
, is positive, the following inequality holds:

\mathcal{L}(\theta)-\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{R}}^{*}\leq\frac{1}{2\lambda_{\min}(\Theta_{\theta})}||\nabla\mathcal{L}(\theta)||^{2}

(3)

This relationship directly gives rise to the local PL condition. By rearranging the inequality, we find that the PL constant
\mu
is precisely the smallest eigenvalue of the NTK,
\lambda_{\min}
, within the stable region
\mathcal{R}
.

5.  Main Theoretical Results

In this section, we present our main theorems, which provide formal guarantees for the existence of Locally Polyak-Lojasiewicz Regions (LPLRs) and prove the resulting linear convergence of gradient descent. The aim is to formally connect the geometry of the local loss landscape, characterized by Locally Quasi-Convex Regions (LQCRs), to the rapid convergence rates observed in practice. One can tackle this problem by proving that under a mild assumption, these same regions satisfy the local PL condition, which is sufficient to guarantee linear convergence for gradient descent. Our central result establishes that an LQCR is also an LPLR.

Theorem 5.1
(Existence of LPLRs)

Under the same setup as prior work on LQCRs
Aich2025
and holding Assumption
5.2
, the LQCR
\mathcal{R}
is also a Local Polyak-Lojasiewicz Region (LPLR). Specifically, the loss
\mathcal{L}
satisfies the PL condition within
\mathcal{R}
for a PL constant
\mu
satisfying:

\mu\geq\lambda_{\min}

(4)

where
\lambda_{\min}
is the lower bound on the smallest eigenvalue of the NTK from Assumption
5.2
.

The key component of Theorem
5.1
is the local stability of the Neural Tangent Kernel (NTK), which provides the crucial link between the geometric properties of the LQCR and the functional properties required for the PL condition to hold. We state this formally below.

Assumption 5.2
(Enhanced Local NTK Stability)

Let
\mathcal{R}
be the
(\alpha,\gamma)
-LQCR established in
Aich2025
. We assume that for all
\theta\in\mathcal{R}
, the empirical Neural Tangent Kernel
\mathbf{\Theta}_{\theta}
satisfies:

•

Positive Definiteness:

\mathbf{\Theta}_{\theta}\succ 0
with smallest eigenvalue
\lambda_{\min}(\mathbf{\Theta}_{\theta})\geq\lambda_{\min}>0
.

•

Stability Condition:
For any
\theta,\phi\in\mathcal{R}
:

\|\mathbf{\Theta}_{\theta}-\mathbf{\Theta}_{\phi}\|\leq L_{\Theta}\|\theta-\phi\|

(5)

where
L_{\Theta}
is the NTK Lipschitz constant within
\mathcal{R}
.

Sufficient Conditions:
This assumption holds when the network width
m
, initialization, learning rate
\eta
, and region radius
R_{\mathcal{R}}
satisfy certain criteria, such as those outlined in recent theoretical work on over-parameterized models.

Proof of Theorem
5.1
.

Our goal is to show that the PL inequality,
\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla\mathcal{L}(\theta)\|^{2}\geq\mu(\mathcal{L}(\theta)-\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{R}}^{*})
, holds for all
\theta\in\mathcal{R}
.

For a neural network with empirical loss
\mathcal{L}(\theta)=\frac{1}{2n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(f_{\theta}(x_{i})-y_{i})^{2}
, the gradient is
\nabla\mathcal{L}(\theta)=\frac{1}{n}J_{\theta}^{T}(f_{\theta}-y)
, where
J_{\theta}
is the network Jacobian. The squared norm of the gradient is
\|\nabla\mathcal{L}(\theta)\|^{2}=\frac{1}{n^{2}}\|J_{\theta}^{T}(f_{\theta}-y)\|^{2}
.

The empirical Neural Tangent Kernel (NTK) is defined as
\mathbf{\Theta}_{\theta}=\frac{1}{n}J_{\theta}J_{\theta}^{T}
. The smallest non-zero eigenvalue of
J_{\theta}^{T}J_{\theta}
is equal to that of
J_{\theta}J_{\theta}^{T}
. This property, combined with Assumption
5.2
which guarantees
\lambda_{\min}(\mathbf{\Theta}_{\theta})\geq\lambda_{\min}>0
, gives us a bound on the squared gradient:

\|J_{\theta}^{T}(f_{\theta}-y)\|^{2}\geq\lambda_{\min}(J_{\theta}^{T}J_{\theta})\|f_{\theta}-y\|^{2}=n\lambda_{\min}(\mathbf{\Theta}_{\theta})\|f_{\theta}-y\|^{2}\geq n\lambda_{\min}\|f_{\theta}-y\|^{2}.

(6)

Substituting this back into the expression for the gradient norm, we get:

\|\nabla\mathcal{L}(\theta)\|^{2}\geq\frac{1}{n^{2}}\left(n\lambda_{\min}\|f_{\theta}-y\|^{2}\right)=\frac{\lambda_{\min}}{n}\|f_{\theta}-y\|^{2}.

(7)

By the definition of our loss function, the suboptimality gap relates to the residual as
2n(\mathcal{L}(\theta)-\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{R}}^{*})\leq\|f_{\theta}-y\|^{2}
. Combining these gives:

\|\nabla\mathcal{L}(\theta)\|^{2}\geq\frac{\lambda_{\min}}{n}\left(2n(\mathcal{L}(\theta)-\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{R}}^{*})\right)=2\lambda_{\min}(\mathcal{L}(\theta)-\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{R}}^{*}).

(8)

Dividing by two yields the Polyak-Lojasiewicz (PL) condition:

\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla\mathcal{L}(\theta)\|^{2}\geq\lambda_{\min}(\mathcal{L}(\theta)-\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{R}}^{*}).

(9)

This establishes the result with a local PL constant
\mu=\lambda_{\min}
.

Remark 5.3

While global NTK stability requires unrealistic infinite-width networks, our assumption is substantially weaker, requiring only that the kernel remains well-conditioned within the local optimization region
\mathcal{R}
. This aligns with the "lazy training" phenomenon, where parameters in sufficiently overparameterized networks stay close to initialization
Jacot2018
. Our contribution is to show that this empirically observed
local
stability is the critical ingredient that transitions the convergence rate from sublinear to linear.

A direct consequence of the existence of LPLRs is an alternative characterization of the convergence dynamics: specifically, that gradient descent achieves a linear rate of convergence within the region. This property makes the LPLR a powerful tool for explaining the efficiency of deep learning optimizers.

Theorem 5.4
(Linear Convergence of Gradient Descent)

Assume the loss function
\mathcal{L}
is L-smooth and the conditions of Theorem
5.1
hold. Consider the gradient descent update
\theta^{(t+1)}=\theta^{(t)}-\eta\nabla\mathcal{L}(\theta^{(t)})
with a step size
\eta\leq\frac{1}{L}
. If the iterates remain within the LPLR
\mathcal{R}
, they converge to the regional minimum loss
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{R}}^{*}
at a linear rate:

\mathcal{L}(\theta^{(t)})-\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{R}}^{*}\leq(1-\eta\lambda_{\min})^{t}(\mathcal{L}(\theta^{(0)})-\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{R}}^{*})

(10)

Proof of Theorem
5.4
.

The proof is a direct application of the standard result for gradient descent on a function satisfying the PL condition, as shown by Polyak in 1963
Polyak1963
and reviewed in 2016 by Karimi et al.
Karimi2016
.

The standard descent lemma for an L-smooth function states that for a step size
\eta=1/L
:

\mathcal{L}(\theta^{(t+1)})\leq\mathcal{L}(\theta^{(t)})-\frac{1}{2L}||\nabla\mathcal{L}(\theta^{(t)})||^{2}.

(11)

From Theorem
5.1
, we know the local PL condition holds with constant
\mu=\lambda_{\min}
:

||\nabla\mathcal{L}(\theta^{(t)})||^{2}\geq 2\lambda_{\min}(\mathcal{L}(\theta^{(t)})-\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{R}}^{*}).

(12)

Substituting (
12
) into (
11
) gives:

\mathcal{L}(\theta^{(t+1)})\leq\mathcal{L}(\theta^{(t)})-\frac{\lambda_{\min}}{L}(\mathcal{L}(\theta^{(t)})-\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{R}}^{*}).

(13)

Subtracting the optimal loss
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{R}}^{*}
from both sides and rearranging provides the recursive expression for one step:

\mathcal{L}(\theta^{(t+1)})-\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{R}}^{*}\leq(1-\frac{\lambda_{\min}}{L})(\mathcal{L}(\theta^{(t)})-\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{R}}^{*}).

(14)

Applying this inequality recursively from
t=0
yields the final result. For a general step size
\eta\leq 1/L
, the rate becomes
(1-\eta\lambda_{\min})^{t}
.

The aim is to first verify our core claims in a controlled setting using a fully-connected network with full-batch Gradient Descent, and then to test the robustness of our theory in a more complex scenario involving a ResNet-style CNN trained with stochastic methods.

, there is a clear and consistent trend: as network width increases, the final training loss achieved by the optimizer decreases. This finding aligns with established theories of over-parameterization, which suggest that wider models have smoother, more favorable loss landscapes. While our main theorems guarantee a linear convergence rate once inside an LPLR, this ablation study suggests that wider models may converge to "better" LPLRs, i.e., those surrounding a lower-loss minimum. This provides an important empirical link between the global effects of over-parameterization and the local convergence dynamics central to our work.

7.  Discussion and Conclusion

This paper provides a definitive answer to one of the most fundamental questions in deep learning theory: why do gradient-based methods converge so rapidly in practice, despite the highly non-convex nature of neural network loss landscapes? Our work closes the critical gap between the sublinear convergence rates guaranteed by prior geometric analyses and the exponential rates consistently observed in practice. We accomplish this by demonstrating that the Locally Quasi-Convex Regions (LQCRs)

that ensure convergence also exhibit a local Polyak-Lojasiewicz property

under a mild and realistic assumption about Neural Tangent Kernel stability

.

7.1
Comparison with Recent Advances

Architectural Approaches:
Recent work has shown that networks can be designed to satisfy PL conditions by construction (PLNet)

. While this guarantees fast convergence, it requires abandoning standard architectures. Our approach explains why standard architectures already exhibit these favorable properties.

Stochastic Analysis:

provided convergence analysis for SGD under local Lojasiewicz conditions, but their analysis requires stronger assumptions about the local landscape. Our geometric approach provides a path toward understanding when these conditions hold.

Linear Network Insights:
Recent progress on linear networks

shows that local PL properties can be established under relaxed assumptions. Our work extends these insights to the non-linear case that dominates practical applications.

7.2
Theoretical Contributions and Significance

Our central theoretical contribution is the introduction and rigorous analysis of the Locally Polyak-Lojasiewicz Region (LPLR). This concept represents a significant advance in understanding neural network optimization by showing that:

•

Local geometry implies fast optimization:
The same well-behaved regions that guarantee monotonic progress also satisfy a condition sufficient for linear convergence.

•

Finite-width networks can achieve linear rates:
Unlike prior work requiring infinite width, our theory applies to practically-sized networks under realistic conditions.

•

The NTK provides the crucial link:
Local stability of the Neural Tangent Kernel, a much weaker condition than global stability, is sufficient to induce the PL property that drives fast convergence.

Our theoretical framework elegantly unifies three previously disparate lines of research in deep learning theory:

•

Local Landscape Analysis (LQCRs):
Provides the structural guarantee that optimization remains within a well-behaved region.

•

Neural Tangent Kernel Theory:
Contributes the mechanism (local kernel stability) that endows this region with favorable optimization properties.

•

Fast Optimization Theory (PL Condition):
Supplies the analytical tools to translate geometric properties into convergence rate guarantees.

This synthesis represents a qualitative advance in our theoretical understanding, moving beyond existence proofs to provide concrete, quantitative explanations for the efficiency of deep learning optimization.

7.3
Empirical Validation and Practical Relevance

Our empirical validation demonstrates that the LPLR framework is not merely a theoretical construct but captures genuine phenomena occurring in practical deep learning. We validated our theory across multiple settings:

•

Controlled validation:
Full-batch gradient descent on fully-connected networks provided exact confirmation of our theoretical predictions.

•

Realistic scenarios:
ResNet-style CNNs trained with mini-batch SGD on CIFAR-10 showed that the same principles govern modern architectures and stochastic optimization.

•

Initialization sensitivity:
A comparison of initialization schemes highlighted the critical impact of initial weight variance on convergence speed, demonstrating the practical importance of landscape geometry at the start of training.

The robustness of our findings across these diverse settings strongly suggests that LPLRs capture a fundamental organizing principle of neural network optimization, rather than an artifact of specific architectures or training procedures.

7.4
Implications for Deep Learning Practice

Our work has several important implications for practitioners:

•

Initialization Design:
Our theory provides a principled framework for analyzing initialization schemes. The goal is to find methods that improve the local PL constant
\mu
, as this should translate to faster training. However, as our comparative experiment shows, the relationship between a specific strategy and the resulting landscape geometry is complex and architecture-dependent, making this a critical area for future investigation.

•

Architecture Design:
Understanding that local NTK stability drives fast convergence can inform architectural choices. Designs that promote better conditioning of the local kernel (through appropriate width, normalization, or connectivity patterns) should exhibit superior optimization properties.

•

Learning Rate Selection:
Our linear convergence guarantee provides theoretical backing for aggressive learning rate schedules, as long as they respect the smoothness constraints and maintain iterates within the LPLR.

•

Optimization Algorithm Development:
The local PL property suggests that algorithms specifically designed to maintain or enhance this property could achieve superior performance.

7.5
Limitations and Scope

While our work represents a significant advance, we acknowledge several limitations that define important directions for future research:

•

Stochastic Analysis:
Our main theoretical results are established for full-batch gradient descent. Although our empirical results strongly suggest the theory extends to stochastic settings, a formal analysis of SGD convergence to a noise ball within an LPLR remains an important open problem.

•

NTK Assumption:
Our theory relies on local NTK stability, which we validate empirically but do not fully characterize theoretically. Understanding precisely when this assumption holds across different architectures and training regimes would strengthen our framework.

•

Generalization Connection:
While our work explains training efficiency, the connection between LPLR properties and final generalization performance remains unexplored. This represents a promising avenue for understanding implicit regularization.

7.6
Future Research Directions

Our work opens several exciting research directions:

•

Stochastic Extension:
Developing a rigorous theory for mini-batch SGD within LPLRs, characterizing both the convergence rate and the size of the noise ball, building on prior analyses of stochastic methods under PL-like conditions

•

Adaptive Optimization:
Developing optimization algorithms that dynamically estimate and exploit the local PL constant could lead to more efficient training procedures.

•

Generalization Theory:
Connecting LPLR properties to generalization bounds could provide new insights into why certain training trajectories lead to better test performance.

•

Empirical Scaling Laws:
Characterizing how the size and properties of LPLRs scale with network width, depth, and dataset size could inform resource allocation decisions in large-scale training.

7.7
Broader Impact

Our work contributes to the theoretical foundations of deep learning by providing a principled explanation for one of its most important empirical phenomena. By showing that local geometric properties naturally give rise to favorable optimization conditions, we advance the field’s understanding of why deep learning works so well in practice. This theoretical clarity can guide both algorithm development and architectural design, potentially leading to more efficient training procedures and better-performing models.
Furthermore, by demonstrating that finite-width networks can achieve the same favorable convergence guarantees as their infinite-width counterparts under realistic conditions, our work helps bridge the gap between theoretical analysis and practical implementation. This has important implications for the design of resource-efficient training procedures and for understanding the trade-offs between model size and optimization efficiency.

7.8
Conclusion

This paper resolves a longstanding puzzle in deep learning theory by showing that the rapid convergence observed in practice emerges naturally from the local geometric structure of neural network loss landscapes. Our introduction of Locally Polyak-Lojasiewicz Regions provides both a theoretical framework for understanding this phenomenon and practical insights for improving optimization in deep learning. The convergence of geometric, kernel-based, and optimization-theoretic perspectives in our analysis represents a significant step toward a more complete understanding of why deep learning optimization is so remarkably effective.
The validation of our theory across both controlled and realistic settings, combined with its connection to fundamental concepts in optimization and deep learning theory, positions this work as a significant contribution to the field. As deep learning continues to scale to ever-larger models and datasets, theoretical frameworks like ours that explain and predict the behavior of optimization algorithms become increasingly valuable for both understanding current successes and guiding future developments.

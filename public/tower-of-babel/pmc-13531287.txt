To the Editor:

Pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH) remains a progressive, incurable condition often associated with poor outcomes despite advances in therapy [1]. Selexipag, an oral selective prostacyclin receptor agonist, is associated with reduced hospitalisations and slower disease progression when added to an endothelin receptor antagonist (ERA) and/or a phosphodiesterase-5 inhibitor (PDE5i) in patients with PAH [2]. Current recommendations suggest the addition of selexipag for patients with an intermediate–low risk of mortality while receiving ERA/PDE5i therapy [3]. By contrast, for patients at intermediate–high or high risk of mortality despite oral combination therapy, current guidelines recommend the addition of intravenous or subcutaneous prostacyclin analogues to improve risk status and long-term outcomes [4]. While transition from selexipag to i.v./s.c. prostacyclin analogues is recommended for patients not achieving treatment goals [4], outcome data for this approach remain scarce. We aimed to explore the effect of such transitions on the 2022 European Society of Cardiology/European Respiratory Society (ESC/ERS) risk stratification model and patient survival.

This retrospective cohort included all patients with PAH who transitioned from oral selexipag to i.v. epoprostenol or i.v. or s.c. treprostinil between January 2016 and November 2024 at the University of British Columbia and Laval University in Canada. Patients were selected using established PAH diagnostic criteria per the 2022 ESC/ERS guidelines, with right heart catheterisation demonstrating pre-capillary PAH, age ≥19 years and selexipag treatment for ≥3 months before the transition to an i.v./s.c. prostacyclin analogue. There was no standardised transition protocol, and the decision was individualised and clinician directed.

Prognostic variables, including World Health Organization (WHO) functional class, 6-min walk distance (6MWD), serum brain natriuretic peptide (BNP) levels, tricuspid annular plane systolic excursion/systolic pulmonary artery pressure (TAPSE/sPAP) measurement on echocardiography, and haemodynamic measures from right heart catheterisation, were obtained at baseline (within a month pre-transition) and at the first follow-up, scheduled between 3 and 12 months post-transition. This timeframe captures early- to intermediate-term effects of the transition, providing relevant insights for clinical decision-making and management adjustments. Risk assessment at baseline and at the first follow-up was based on the 2022 ESC/ERS guidelines’ four-stratum risk model, using WHO functional class, 6MWD and BNP according to cut-off levels proposed in the refined Comparative, Prospective Registry of Newly Initiated Therapies for Pulmonary Hypertension 2.0 approach [5].

Paired Student's t-test and Wilcoxon signed-rank test were used to compare baseline and post-transition values for normally and abnormally distributed continuous variables, respectively. McNemar's test or the nonlinear mixed model was used for paired nominal variables. Univariate logistic regression was performed to identify variables associated with achieving a low-risk status. No correction for multiple comparisons was applied.

The study population included 21 patients (mean±sd age 51±16 years, 67% female) treated between 2016 and 2024. Eight (38%) patients had connective tissue disease (CTD)-associated PAH. Before the transition, all patients were receiving triple oral combination therapy, including a PDE5i, an ERA and selexipag. The mean selexipag dose before transition was 2619±663 μg·day−1. Patients were transitioned to i.v. epoprostenol (n=14) or s.c. treprostinil (n=7) at a mean of 56±47 months from PAH diagnosis. Two patients underwent transition due to intolerance of selexipag and 19 patients transitioned as they did not maintain low/intermediate-low risk status or did not attain the haemodynamic goals of therapy. The mean duration between the transition and the first complete reassessment was 4±1 months (range 3–12 months). At 3–12 months after transition, the mean i.v. epoprostenol dose was 19.8±6.4 ng·kg−1·min−1 and the mean s.c. treprostinil dose was 39.1±3.7 ng·kg−1·min−1. No unexpected safety signals were observed and no transitions were discontinued due to severe or life-threatening adverse events.

Post-transition, significant improvement was observed in haemodynamic indices, including cardiac index, stroke volume index, pulmonary vascular resistance and mixed venous saturation (table 1). Although no significant change was observed in either the WHO functional class or 6MWD, five (23.8%) patients showed an improvement of >33 m in 6MWD. Among patients with paired measurements, median BNP levels decreased numerically following the transition, with three (14%) patients having BNP levels <50 ng·L−1 after the transition; however, this change did not reach statistical significance (table 1). On echocardiography, a significant improvement in TAPSE/sPAP was observed, with a mean change of 0.10±0.10 mm·mmHg−1 (p=0.010). Only six patients had pre- and post-transition EmPHasis-10 questionnaire documentation, with mean scores of 30.7±9.1 pre-transition and 27.5±11.2 post-transition (p=0.537).

Overall, the risk category improved from baseline to follow-up in nine (42.9%) patients, remained unchanged in 10 (47.5%) patients and deteriorated in two (9.5%) patients. Only four (19.0%) patients achieved a low risk status at follow-up; notably, none had CTD-PAH (table 1). All four patients who achieved low risk status remained transplant-free at 3 years, whereas patients who did not achieve low risk status experienced poor transplant-free survival, with a median of 2.1 years. In exploratory univariable analyses, only lower baseline pulmonary vascular resistance was associated with achieving low risk status at follow-up (OR 1.430 per 1-Wood unit decrease, 95% CI 1.032–1.981; p=0.032).

Since the introduction of selexipag, several studies on the transition from parenteral therapies to the new oral medication have emerged [6, 7]. However, only case reports of safe and successful transitions from selexipag to parenteral prostacyclin analogues exist [8, 9]. To our knowledge, this is the first observational cohort to describe standardised risk stratification and survival in a selected population of patients with PAH who transitioned from oral selexipag to parenteral prostacyclin analogues as part of triple combination therapy.

While haemodynamic improvements were observed, only a minority (five patients, 23.8%) achieved a significant improvement (>33 m) in 6MWD. The advanced disease stage, long disease duration before transition, short follow-up interval and potential ceiling effects may explain the discordance between hemodynamic improvement and functional measures. The lack of statistical significance in the paired analysis of median BNP likely reflects the small sample size and interindividual variability in BNP values in advanced PAH.

A substantial proportion of patients demonstrated improvement in risk status; however, this did not reach statistical significance (p=0.098). This likely reflects the limited cohort size and heterogeneous response in advancing disease. The observation in this cohort highlights the role of parenteral prostacyclin analogues in patients who do not achieve therapeutic goals with oral medications and aligns with prior cohorts, reinforcing low-risk status as a key prognostic determinant [10, 11]. The longer disease duration from diagnosis to the introduction of parenteral prostacyclin analogues in our cohort (median 43 months) may explain the less pronounced haemodynamic and functional gains and lower 3-year survival (42.7% versus 67%) compared with those reported by Bartolome et al. [10]. Compared with that of Olsson et al. [11], our cohort had a higher proportion of patients with CTD-PAH (38%) and was uniformly treated with triple therapy before initiation of parenteral prostacyclin, whereas most patients in their cohort received dual therapy or less.

Selexipag, as part of the initial triple combination therapy, has been shown to reduce PAH progression and escalation to parenteral prostacyclin analogues [12, 13]. Conversely, triple combination initial therapy, including parenteral therapy, was associated with a greater long-term survival compared to monotherapy or dual therapy in a large retrospective cohort [14]. More recently, a trial demonstrated that adding sotatercept, an activin signalling inhibitor, to optimised background therapy in high-risk PAH patients resulted in a lower risk of mortality and morbidity [15]. These findings contextualise the impact of an intensified sequencing strategy in advanced or progressive disease. The poor outcomes observed among patients who failed to reach low risk status underscore the need for earlier identification and transplant evaluation.

The small cohort size and the study's retrospective design are limitations that preclude robust conclusions. The noncomparative nature prevented a confident association between changes in the measured variables and the transition. Changes in BNP levels may have been influenced by contemporaneous management, such as diuretic escalation and fluid restriction, which we could not reliably capture retrospectively. The transition approaches were heterogeneous, limiting inference on an optimal protocol and reducing generalisability. The risk at diagnosis and the disease trajectory before the transition are not indicated. However, this likely represents a worsening disease course, given the initiation of selexipag (usually started in nonsevere forms of PAH) and then the transition to parenteral prostacyclin analogues.

In conclusion, this study highlights the utility of parenteral prostacyclin analogues in patients with PAH who do not meet the goals of triple oral combination therapy that includes selexipag. Significant improvements were observed in certain haemodynamic and echocardiographic parameters, alongside favourable but nonsignificant trends in risk assessment based on functional and biomarker measures. Prognosis appears to be extremely poor in those who do not achieve a low risk status post-transition. These data may prompt early assessment and listing for lung transplantation. A prospective evaluation of long-term outcomes comparing different third-line strategies, particularly in patients with intermediate–low risk, is needed to identify safer and optimal treatment options.

Targetoid, Arciform, and Annular Lesions on the Trunk and Extremities
Authors: Yan J. Chen, Lin Wang
Published: 2026

Case Presentation

A 52-year-old female patient presented with 30 years of multiple, recurrent, annular and arciform erythema. The patient had no systemic symptoms. She had a gastric stromal tumour and underwent surgery 3 years ago with an otherwise unremarkable medical history. She had no regular medications or family history of similar conditions. Physical examination revealed multiple oedematous papules, plaques, and erythema with targetoid, arciform, and annular configurations distributed on the trunk and extremities. No tenderness was found. Scratch marks, scaling, and pigmentation were noted [Figure 1a-f]. Laboratory tests revealed positive herpes simplex virus (HSV) 1/2 IgM, anti-nuclear antibody (1:100), and elevated serum cancer antigen 153. Chest computed tomography identified a 2.2 × 1.8 cm solid nodule in the left lung, confirmed as lung adenocarcinoma on biopsy. Gastrointestinal endoscopy findings were unremarkable. Skin histopathology revealed intraepidermal neutrophils and a dermal neutrophil-rich infiltrate without vasculitis [Figure 2a and b].

Question

What is your diagnosis?

Answer

Diagnosis: Neutrophilic figurate erythema (NFE).

Discussion

Neutrophilic figurate erythema is a rare, benign inflammatory condition, predominantly affecting females, that manifests as annular erythematous lesions on the trunk or limbs and features a neutrophil-rich infiltrate without histological signs of vasculitis.[1] Skin lesions of NFE lack specific predilection for specific sites, and systemic symptoms are typically absent.[1] This case highlights diverse clinical presentations during flare-ups and remission stages of NFE.

The aetiology of figurate erythema remains unclear, but it is hypothesised to result from a hypersensitivity reaction to an unidentified antigen.[2] The release of proinflammatory cytokines and chemokines may trigger the activation and recruitment of neutrophils.[3] Adult-onset NFE has been associated with various conditions, including systemic lupus erythematosus, and underlying malignancies (hematologic malignancies like Hodgkin lymphoma).[1] Neither solid tumours nor HSV infection have been previously associated with NFE. In our case, we propose a possible connection between the flares of NFE and the persistent HSV infection.

NFE may occur seasonally and resolve spontaneously.[1] Lesions typically begin as targetoid oedematous papules that progressively evolve into annular or semicircular reddish erythema.[1] Subtle variations in clinical and histopathological manifestations were observed between the adult and infants, supporting the hypothesis of a unified disease entity.[4] Histopathologically, NFE is characterised by perivascular and interstitial neutrophilic infiltrates, with potential findings such as leukocytoclasis, papillary dermal oedema, or erythrocyte extravasation, but lacks evidence of vasculitis.[1] Diagnosing NFE can be challenging, especially when skin lesions are atypical. The differential diagnoses include Sweet’s syndrome, cutaneous lupus erythematosus, erythema annulare centrifugum, urticarial vasculitis, and granuloma annulare.[1]

Management options include colchicine, dapsone, antihistamines, topical steroids, prednisolone, potassium iodide, or expectant care, as spontaneous resolution is possible.[5] In this case, treatment with thiamphenicol (1.5 g/day) and famciclovir (1 g/day) was initiated, leading to resolution of the skin lesions within 2 weeks. During an 11-month follow-up, the lesions recurred three times and resolved without intervention. This case notably marks the first reported use of thiamphenicol for NFE, highlighting its potential effectiveness. However, associated adverse effects should be carefully monitored. The chronic, recurrent nature of NFE results in atypical lesion presentations, highlighting the need for meticulous long-term follow-up.

Complete genome sequence and functional analysis of a multidrug-resistant Flavobacterium bizetiae strain isolated from farmed Atlantic salmon (Salmo salar)
Authors: Sungjae Ko, Lyu Jin Jun, Suhee Hong
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundSpecies of the genus Flavobacterium are ubiquitous in aquatic environments and play key roles in nutrient cycling. However, their taxonomic classification is often complicated by high phenotypic similarity, and their potential as reservoirs for antimicrobial resistance (AMR) in aquaculture is a growing concern. This study aimed to resolve the taxonomic status and characterize the resistome and metabolic potential of a Flavobacterium strain isolated from a diseased Atlantic salmon (Salmo salar).MethodsWhole-genome sequencing (WGS) was performed using the PacBio HiFi platform. Taxonomic identification was rigorously confirmed via multiple metrics, including JSpeciesWS (ANI) and the TYGS platform (dDDH and whole-genome phylogenomics). High-quality functional annotation was performed using the state-of-the-art Bakta pipeline, while antimicrobial resistance genes (ARGs) and mobile genetic elements (MGEs) were analyzed using CARD, ISfinder, and IntegronFinder. To validate the genomic findings, phenotypic antimicrobial susceptibility testing (AST) was conducted using disk diffusion and Minimum Inhibitory Concentration (MIC) panels, alongside conventional PCR for targeted resistance gene detection.ResultsThe complete circular chromosome of 6,402,102 bp was definitively identified as Flavobacterium bizetiae AS-HAN-256023, supported by 100% phylogenomic clustering with the type strain, 95.36% ANIb, and 68.3% dDDH. Phenotypic analysis revealed an extensive multidrug-resistant (MDR) profile (e.g., ampicillin and doxycycline MICs > 128 µg/mL). The resistome comprised 21 ARGs. Crucially, MGE analysis revealed that the “last-resort” carbapenemase JOHN-1 is stably integrated, whereas multiple copies of tet(X2) are actively driven by IS1595-family transposases within an MDR island. Furthermore, the strain possesses an intact Type IX secretion system (T9SS) lacking MGE interruptions, coupled with host-barrier-degrading chitinases (GH18 and GH20), and a robust 6 mA-dominated epigenetic defense system. Notably, while targeted PCR only detected the erm(F) gene, WGS provided a much more comprehensive view of the resistome, capturing 20 additional ARGs missed by the PCR approach. The complete genome sequence of F. bizetiae AS-HAN-256023 has been deposited in GenBank under the accession number JBYJWJ000000000.ConclusionsThis study demonstrates that seemingly non-pathogenic environmental isolates like F. bizetiae act as active, functional reservoirs for high-risk, “last-resort” resistance genes in aquaculture settings. The discrepancy between PCR and WGS results underscores the limitations of targeted diagnostic methods and highlights the urgent need for non-targeted, high-resolution genomic surveillance to monitor the dissemination of AMR in the global fishing industry.

Background

The genus Flavobacterium, a member of the phylum Bacteroidota, comprises Gram-negative, aerobic bacteria that are ubiquitously distributed in diverse habitats, including soil, freshwater, and marine environments [1]. These organisms are recognized as key players in global biogeochemical cycles, primarily due to their remarkable capacity to degrade complex organic polymers through the secretion of various carbohydrate-active enzymes (CAZymes) [1, 2]. Their metabolic versatility allows them to occupy diverse ecological niches and contribute significantly to nutrient cycling in aquatic ecosystems [1, 3].

Beyond their ecological significance, several Flavobacterium species have emerged as serious pathogens in aquaculture, causing substantial economic losses in the fishing industry worldwide [3, 4]. A distinguishing feature of many species within this genus is the Type IX secretion system (T9SS), which is essential for both gliding motility and the extracellular delivery of virulence factors [5]. Despite their impact, the taxonomic identification of isolates obtained from Atlantic salmon (Salmo salar) remains notably challenging due to high phenotypic similarity and extensive genomic diversity within the genus [6]. Traditional biochemical and physiological characterizations often fail to provide definitive species-level resolution, leading to ambiguity in diagnostic outcomes and inconsistent species assignment [6].

To overcome these taxonomic limitations, whole-genome sequencing (WGS) has emerged as an essential tool, providing high-resolution genomic data required for precise species identification through metrics such as Average Nucleotide Identity (ANI) [6, 7]. While initial screening of certain isolates may suggest a lack of traditional clinical pathogenicity, recent genomic insights suggest that the pathogenicity of this genus may extend beyond traditional clinical toxins [2, 3]. Specifically, the presence of chitin-degrading enzyme families (GH18, GH19, and GH20) is increasingly recognized as a key factor in host-pathogen interactions [2, 8]. When delivered via the T9SS, these chitinases can biochemically degrade the complex polysaccharide barriers of the fish host, including the skin, scales, and mucus [5]. This enzymatic degradation of the protective external layers likely functions as a specialized strategy for bacterial adhesion and penetration, allowing even strains lacking acute toxins to act as opportunistic pathogens [2, 5].

The observation of multidrug resistance (MDR) raises significant concerns. Environmental isolates can act as significant reservoirs for antibiotic resistance genes (ARGs), potentially facilitating the horizontal gene transfer (HGT) of resistance determinants to pathogenic bacteria within the same ecosystem [9, 10]. The phenotypic observation of multidrug resistance (MDR) in this isolate raised immediate concerns regarding its role as a potential reservoir for antimicrobial resistance (AMR) in aquaculture. To address this, we employed an integrative approach combining whole-genome sequencing (WGS) with antimicrobial susceptibility testing. This allowed us to not only resolve the taxonomic position of the isolate but also to clarify the genomic context of its expressed resistance profile, offering essential data for monitoring the environmental dimensions of AMR.

Furthermore, to fully understand bacterial adaptation and survival strategies, it is essential to analyze epigenetic modifications alongside primary sequence information. In particular, N6-methyladenine (6 mA) is a major epigenetic marker in many prokaryotes and plays a pivotal role in maintaining genomic integrity as a core component of the Restriction-Modification (R-M) system [11]. While research on the genetic characteristics of the genus Flavobacterium has been extensive, little is currently known about the methylome landscape of F. bizetiae isolated from aquaculture environments. Therefore, in this study, we characterized 6 mA-based methylation patterns in parallel with whole-genome analysis to provide an in-depth investigation into the genomic defense strategies this strain employs against invasive genetic elements, such as bacteriophages.

In this study, we utilized PacBio HiFi sequencing to generate a complete, circularized genome of an unidentified Flavobacterium strain isolated from a diseased Atlantic salmon. By integrating high-fidelity genomic assembly with a comprehensive resistome and epigenetic analysis, we aimed to define the taxonomic position of this isolate and characterize the genomic context of its multidrug resistance. Our findings demonstrate that non-pathogenic F. bizetiae can serve as a vital environmental reservoir for clinically relevant resistance determinants, providing a high-quality reference for monitoring antimicrobial resistance in aquaculture settings.

Materials and methods

Bacterial isolation and genomic DNA extraction

The Flavobacterium strain used in this study was isolated from a diseased Atlantic salmon (Salmo salar). Detailed information on its cultivation on Cytophaga agar and Tryptic Soy Agar (TSA), as well as its colony characteristics, has been described previously [12]. For pure culture recovery, the isolate was cultured on TSA and incubated at 25 °C for 48 h. Genomic DNA (gDNA) was extracted from the pure culture using the Maxwell® RSC Tissue DNA Kit (Promega, USA) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. The integrity and quality of the extracted gDNA were assessed using the Femto Pulse system (Agilent Technologies, USA), and the concentration was adjusted to 70 ng/µl for downstream library preparation.

Genome sequencing and assembly

Whole-genome sequencing was performed using the PacBio HiFi platform, which provides high-fidelity long reads essential for accurate taxonomic and resistome characterization [13]. The raw PacBio HiFi reads were assembled de novo using the Microbial Genome Analysis tool within the SMRT Link v25.1.0.257715 suite [13]. To generate a high-quality consensus sequence, the initial assembly was polished and corrected for errors using Inspector v1.0.1 [14]. The genome sequence of F. bizetiae strain AS-HAN-256023 has been deposited in DDBJ/ENA/GenBank under the WGS accession number JBYJWJ000000000. The associated accession information is as follows: BioProject PRJNA1441167 and BioSample SAMN56644663.

Genome validation and taxonomic identification

Genome completeness and contamination were rigorously evaluated using Benchmarking Universal Single-Copy Orthologs (BUSCO v5.1.3) against the high-resolution Flavobacterium lineage, alongside CheckM analysis [15]. The assembly was further validated through self-mapping analysis by aligning raw HiFi reads back to the assembled contig using pbmm2 v12.1. Taxonomic assignment was performed by calculating the Average Nucleotide Identity (ANIb and ANIm) using the JSpeciesWS Online Service. The assembled genome was compared against reference genomes in the NCBI database, applying an ANI threshold of ≥ 95% as the standard criterion for prokaryotic species demarcation [6]. Furthermore, comprehensive taxonomic profiling, including digital DNA-DNA hybridization (dDDH, formula d4), Mash distance, and the construction of a whole-genome phylogenomic tree based on the Genome BLAST Distance Phylogeny (GBDP) approach, was performed using the Type (Strain) Genome Server (TYGS) platform [16].

Genomic features and functional annotation

Functional annotation of the assembled genome was performed using the state-of-the-art Bakta v1.12.0 pipeline (database v6.0) to identify protein-coding sequences (CDS), tRNA, and rRNA genes, ensuring high-quality functional characterization. Further functional classification (including COG categories) and orthology assignments were conducted using eggNOG-mapper v2.1.10 against the eggNOG database v5.0.2 [17]. Protein domains and motifs were identified using InterProScan v5.34-73.0 [18]. Additionally, the structural and functional components of the T9SS, including the core spr and gld gene clusters, were manually identified and extracted based on the high-confidence Bakta annotations, cross-validated with eggNOG-mapper, and visually examined using Proksee [16, 19, 20].

Additionally, to investigate the pathogenic potential, the core structural and functional components of the T9SS, including the essential spr and gld gene clusters, were manually identified and extracted based on the high-confidence Bakta. The syntenic organization and genomic context of these functional clusters were subsequently visually examined and mapped using the Proksee platform [19, 20].

Enzymatic repertoire and resistome analysis

The enzymatic repertoire for the degradation of complex organic polymers was characterized by screening the Bakta-annotated CDS against the Carbohydrate-Active Enzymes (CAZy) database [2, 8]. This was performed using the dbCAN3 server employing the HMMER search algorithm, applying strict filtering thresholds of an E-value < 1e-15 and coverage > 0.35 for high-confidence family assignment. To characterize the multidrug resistance profile, antimicrobial resistance genes (ARGs) were identified using the Resistance Gene Identifier (RGI v6.0.3) tool against the Comprehensive Antibiotic Resistance Database (CARD v3.2.9) [21]. Hits with < 50% amino acid identity were stringently classified as putative homologues. To assess the horizontal gene transfer (HGT) potential of key ARGs, a comprehensive Mobile Genetic Element (MGE) analysis was conducted. Integron structures were screened using IntegronFinder v2.0.2 [22], and the flanking genomic neighborhoods of ARGs were analyzed for insertion sequences and transposons using ISfinder [23].

Antimicrobial susceptibility testing and detection of antibiotic resistance genes

The antimicrobial susceptibility of the isolate was evaluated using the disk diffusion method and MIC panel analysis [24]. For quantitative analysis, MICs were determined using KRAQ3 and KRAQ4 panels (TRECK Diagnostic System Ltd, East Grinstead, UK), covering 19 antimicrobial agents. The phenotypic results were cross-referenced with the ARGs identified via WGS using the RGI/CARD database. Primer sets used for the detection of antibiotic resistance genes are listed in Table 1. Using targeted PCR, we screened a total of nine tetracycline (Tc) resistance genes and four macrolide resistance genes. PCR conditions were carried out according to a previously described method [25–27]. Amplification consisted of 30 cycles at 94 °C for 30 s, 55 °C for 30 s, and 72 °C for 30 s in 0.2 ml thin-walled tubes.

Epigenetic and visualization analysis

DNA base modifications, including N6-methyladenine (6 mA) and N4-methylcytosine (4mC), were predicted using ipdSummary and motifMaker within the SMRT Link v25.1 suite [11]. CpG methylation (5mC) probabilities were calculated using pb-CpG-tools v1.1.0. Finally, the circular genome map, incorporating genomic features such as CDS, tRNA/rRNA, GC content, and GC skew, was visualized using Circos v0.69.9 [20].

Results

Genome sequencing and complete assembly

The whole-genome sequencing of the Flavobacterium isolate generated a total of 57,970 HiFi reads, resulting in a high-quality dataset of 457,048,870 bp. The sequencing process achieved an average read quality of Q38 and a mean number of 16 passes, ensuring the high fidelity required for precise taxonomic and resistome characterization. De novo assembly yielded a complete, circularized chromosome represented by a single contig of 6,402,102 bp. The final assembly exhibited a GC content of 34.3% and was achieved at a high sequencing depth of 71.39x (Table 2; Fig. 1).

Assembly validation and taxonomic identification

The quality of the assembled genome was validated through multiple highly stringent metrics. BUSCO analysis was performed against the high-resolution Flavobacterium lineage, demonstrating an exceptional 99.7% completeness. The detailed breakdown revealed 99.7% complete (99.3% single-copy, 0.4% duplicated), 0.2% fragmented, and 0.1% missing orthologs. Furthermore, CheckM analysis confirmed the robustness of the assembly, yielding a near-perfect completeness of 99.65% with a low contamination rate of 1.55%. Self-mapping analysis confirmed 100% coverage, with raw HiFi reads mapping perfectly back to the assembly (Table 2).

Taxonomic assignment was rigorously confirmed through multiple whole-genome metrics (Table 3). The isolate exhibited the highest genomic similarity to F. bizetiae strain HJ-32-4, with an ANIb value of 96.60%. Furthermore, comprehensive taxonomic profiling against the official type strain, F. bizetiae CIP 105534ᵀ, yielded an ANIb of 95.36%, a digital DNA-DNA hybridization (dDDH, formula d4) value of 68.3%, and a low Mash distance of 0.0316. These metrics confidently satisfy the widely accepted thresholds for species demarcation. Comparisons with other closely related species, such as F. piscisymbiosum F-30 and F. piscis CCUG 60,099, clearly distinguished our isolate from neighboring taxa (Table 3). To further validate the taxonomic assignment, a whole-genome phylogenomic tree was constructed using the TYGS platform. Strain AS-HAN-256023 formed a robust monophyletic clade exclusively with the F. bizetiae type strain (CIP 105534ᵀ) with 100% bootstrap support, irrefutably confirming its species-level identification and clearly distinguishing it from phylogenetically related neighbors (Fig. 2). Consequently, the isolate was definitively identified and designated as F. bizetiae AS-HAN-256023 (Fig. 2).

Genomic features and functional annotation

Annotation of the complete genome revealed a total of 5,429 genes, comprising 5,336 protein-coding sequences (CDS), 76 tRNAs, 18 rRNAs, 1 tmRNA, and 212 ncRNAs. Additionally, 17 pseudogenes and 296 hypothetical proteins were identified. Functional categorization of the 5,336 CDS based on the Clusters of Orthologous Groups (COG) classification revealed a highly specialized genomic repertoire. Excluding CDS with unknown functions (Category S; 296 genes, 5.55%), the most abundantly assigned functional categories were Transcription (Category K; 392 genes, 7.35%), Cell wall/membrane/envelope biogenesis (Category M; 389 genes, 7.29%), and Carbohydrate transport and metabolism (Category G; 344 genes, 6.45%). Additionally, a significant proportion of the genome was dedicated to Amino acid transport and metabolism (Category E; 297 genes, 5.57%) and Inorganic ion transport and metabolism (Category P; 295 genes, 5.53%) (Table 4; Fig. 3). The genomic features, including CDS, RNA genes, and GC skew, were visualized in a circular map (Fig. 1).

Carbohydrate-active enzymes (CAZymes) and metabolic potential

The genome was screened against the CAZy database using the dbCAN3 server, identifying a total of 273 enzymes involved in the degradation and modification of complex polysaccharides (Table 5). The Glycoside Hydrolases (GH) class was the most diverse (158 hits), with high prevalence in the GH43, GH2, GH3, and GH13 families, suggesting a robust capacity for starch, cellulose, and hemicellulose degradation. Notably, the repertoire included chitin-degrading enzymes from the GH18 and GH20 families, which may facilitate host barrier penetration. Other identified classes included 54 Glycosyl Transferases (GT), 30 Carbohydrate Esterases (CE), 20 Carbohydrate-Binding Modules (CBM), 7 Polysaccharide Lyases (PL), and 4 Auxiliary Activities (AA). The extensive diversity of these CAZymes, particularly the newly identified CE and AA classes, significantly expands the known metabolic versatility of the strain.

In addition, the core structural and functional components of the T9SS, which is crucial for gliding motility and the extracellular delivery of CAZymes, were identified. Manual extraction and cross-validation using Bakta and eggNOG-mapper confirmed the presence of essential spr and gld gene clusters (Table 6). Characterization of the resistome

The identified antimicrobial resistance (AMR) profile comprises three primary mechanisms: antibiotic inactivation, efflux pumps, and target alteration/replacement (Table 7). Resistance via enzymatic inactivation was evidenced by the presence of beta-lactamases (OXA-347, JOHN-1), the tetracycline-inactivating enzyme tet(X), and macrolide esterases (ereD). Additionally, efflux-mediated resistance was represented by the putative disinfectant resistance gene qacG and the putative adeF pump, which may confer resistance to fluoroquinolones and tetracyclines. Finally, mechanisms of target alteration and replacement were characterized by the detection of sul2 (sulfonamides), ermF (macrolides/lincosamides), and the putative homologue vanW (glycopeptides).

Epigenetic landscape and methylation motifs

The epigenetic analysis of the F. bizetiae strain identified 11 methyltransferase recognition motifs (Table 10). Among these, six 6 mA-modified motifs exhibited exceptionally high specificity and frequency. Motifs such as CCANNNNNRTAYG (Mean QV: 237.61), GCYANNNNNTCR (Mean QV: 229.41), and GNNGANNNNNNTTC (Mean QV: 222.68) were detected at ~ 100% of their genomic occurrences. The exceptionally high Mean Quality Values (Mean QV > 220) for these six motifs indicate that the corresponding methyltransferases are constitutively active and highly specific within the R-M system. In contrast, motifs with a low detection fraction (< 25%) and low Mean QV, including three minor 6 mA motifs and two 4mC motifs (VNCTSVNYRDC and CHBRNNDDC), were identified as probable bioinformatic noise from the sequencing platform rather than active secondary epigenetic systems. Additionally, CpG analysis identified 201,552 sites, but only 8 sites showed a methylation probability of ≥ 50%, confirming that CpG methylation (5mC) is not a dominant feature in this strain.

Discussion

The complete genome assembly of F. bizetiae AS-HAN-256023 strain into a single circular chromosome of 6,402,102 bp represents a high-fidelity genomic resource for understanding the ecological and clinical dimensions of this species [7, 11, 14]. The genome demonstrated 99.7% BUSCO completeness, ensuring the high quality of the assembly and subsequent functional annotations demarcation [6, 15]. Crucially, the taxonomic identity was definitively resolved using multiple whole-genome metrics, in accordance with established standards for species demarcation. The isolate exhibited an ANIb of 95.36%, an ANIm of 96.45%, and a dDDH value of 68.3% against the official type strain, F. bizetiae CIP 105534ᵀ. Furthermore, whole-genome phylogenomic analysis (GBDP) robustly placed the isolate in a monophyletic clade with the type strain with 100% bootstrap support (Fig. 2), irrefutably confirming its species assignment and resolving the taxonomic ambiguity often associated with environmental Flavobacterium isolates. The functional landscape of this strain reveals a complex genetic architecture optimized for robust environmental adaptation and metabolic versatility in the aquaculture ecosystem [1, 5, 17].

The functional landscape of this strain, annotated using the state-of-the-art Bakta pipeline, reveals a complex genetic architecture optimized for robust environmental adaptation [19]. A significant portion of the genome is dedicated to transcription (COG category K, transcription; 7.35%) and cell wall/membrane/envelope biogenesis (COG category M, cell wall/membrane biogenesis; 7.29%), suggesting a highly responsive system that allows F. bizetiae to rapidly modulate its physiology in fluctuating aquatic conditions. The enrichment of genes involved in carbohydrate and amino acid transport underscores its ability to utilize a diverse range of organic substrates [17]. Specifically, the abundance of GH13 and GH3 families supports the hypothesis that this strain efficiently utilizes organic matter derived from unconsumed feed and fish waste in Atlantic salmon aquaculture [2, 8].

The identification of a diverse repertoire of CAZymes, specifically chitin-degrading GH18, and GH20 families, provides a critical genomic link to its putative potential for opportunistic pathogenicity [8, 10]. The extracellular delivery of these large enzymes is typically mediated by the T9SS [5]. In F. johnsoniae, chitinase ChiA is required for chitin utilization and is secreted through the T9SS, demonstrating a direct functional connection between T9SS-mediated secretion and extracellular polysaccharide degradation [28]. More broadly, the T9SS is a Bacteroidetes-associated secretion system involved in the export of adhesins, gliding motility proteins, peptidases, and extracellular enzymes, and it has been linked to surface colonization, extracellular activity, and virulence in fish-pathogenic Flavobacterium species [5, 29–31].

Consistent with this functional potential, genomic context analysis showed that essential T9SS components, including the gldK-L-M-N motor operon, the large sprA translocon, and the sprE component, are present and organized within stable chromosomal regions (Fig. 4). Notably, no detectable MGEs, such as insertion sequences, transposases, integrases, or prophage-associated elements, were observed within or adjacent to these T9SS loci. Because MGEs are major drivers of bacterial horizontal gene transfer and genomic plasticity, the absence of MGE signatures around the T9SS regions suggests that these loci are not part of recently mobilized genomic islands but are instead embedded within the conserved chromosomal framework of strain AS-HAN-256023 [32–34]. Therefore, the intact organization of T9SS components, together with genes encoding putative secreted CAZymes, may support extracellular substrate degradation, surface attachment, and interaction with fish mucosal barriers during host association.

While these chitinases are ecologically significant for the turnover of organic matter—such as crustacean shells prevalent in fish farm settings—they concurrently serve as specialized tools for host barrier penetration. The external surfaces of Atlantic salmon (Salmo salar), including the skin, scales, and mucus, constitute the primary physical defense against microbial invasion. The coordinated action of T9SS-mediated secretion likely allows the extracellular release of GH18 and GH20 enzymes, which biochemically degrade these complex polysaccharide barriers [2, 5]. This enzymatic breakdown facilitates bacterial adhesion and subsequent penetration into host tissues, providing a mechanism by which F. bizetiae can act as a genetically equipped opportunistic pathogen even in the absence of traditional acute toxins. This metabolic and secretory flexibility, integrated with the T9SS, underscores the strain’s ability to transition from a resilient environmental inhabitant to a potential threat to fish health under favorable conditions.

A cornerstone of this study is the strong congruency between the observed MDR phenotype and the genomic resistome. The remarkably high MIC values for ampicillin and doxycycline (> 128 µg/mL) underscore the functional potency of the OXA-347, JOHN-1, and tet(X) genes within this environmental isolate [21, 27, 35, 36]. The ability of WGS to provide a non-targeted, high-resolution view of the resistome is essential for capturing the full spectrum of mobile resistance determinants that pose a threat to public health through horizontal gene transfer (HGT) [7, 10, 11].

Indeed, JOHN-1 has been characterized as a zinc-dependent Ambler class B carbapenem-hydrolyzing β-lactamase originally described in F. johnsoniae, with activity against penicillins, cephalosporins, and carbapenems [37]. Similarly, tet(X)-type genes encode flavin-dependent monooxygenases that inactivate tetracycline antibiotics through enzymatic modification [38, 39]. However, the clinical interpretation of tet(X2) should be made carefully: while tet(X2) is associated with resistance to older tetracyclines, including doxycycline, high-level tigecycline resistance is more strongly associated with recently evolved tet(X) variants such as tet(X3), tet(X4), and related derivatives [35, 40].

The genomic context of JOHN-1 and tet(X2) suggests different evolutionary implications. JOHN-1 lacked adjacent insertion sequences, transposases, or other MGE signatures, indicating stable chromosomal integration rather than immediate mobilization potential. Although carbapenems are not used in aquaculture, the presence of a chromosomally encoded carbapenemase-like determinant in an environmental Flavobacterium species remains relevant from a One Health perspective because environmental bacteria can serve as reservoirs of resistance diversity. In contrast, multiple copies of tet(X2) were located within multidrug resistance regions containing additional antimicrobial resistance genes and IS1595-family transposases (Fig. 5). Since insertion sequences and transposons are important vehicles for the capture, rearrangement, and dissemination of antimicrobial resistance genes, the association of tet(X2) with IS1595-family transposases suggests a higher potential for local genetic rearrangement and possible horizontal transfer than JOHN-1 [33, 34]. These findings indicate that strain AS-HAN-256023 may represent an environmental reservoir of clinically relevant resistance determinants, with tet(X2)-associated MDR regions posing a greater mobility concern than the chromosomally stabilized JOHN-1 locus.

The epigenomic characterization of strain AS-HAN-256023 further suggests the presence of an active restriction–modification system dominated by N6-methyladenine (6 mA) [11]. Notably, the detection of specific motifs such as CCANNNNNRTAYG and GCYANNNNNTCR at 100% frequency across the genome, combined with exceptionally high Mean Quality Values (Mean QV > 220), suggests that the corresponding methyltransferases are highly specific and constitutively active [11]. This robust defense mechanism works in synergy with the 93 identified defense-related genes (Category V) to effectively discriminate and destroy foreign DNA, serving as a vital means of maintaining genetic stability within the competitive aquaculture environment [11, 17]. Furthermore, the negligible level of CpG methylation (5mC) confirms that 6 mA-mediated modification is the true cornerstone of the epigenetic and defensive landscape in this strain.

In conclusion, the complete genome of F. bizetiae strain AS-HAN-256023 represents a specialized genetic toolkit for survival in competitive aquaculture environments. The combination of metabolic flexibility, an active T9SS-linked enzymatic repertoire, and an extensive resistome positions this species as a resilient inhabitant and a significant potential source of antimicrobial resistance dissemination.

Conclusion

In conclusion, this study provides a high-fidelity complete genomic characterization of a multidrug-resistant Flavobacterium bizetiae strain AS-HAN-256023 isolated from Atlantic salmon (Salmo salar). The application of PacBio HiFi sequencing enabled the assembly of a single circular chromosome of 6,402,102 bp with high technical precision, as evidenced by an average read quality of Q38 and a near-perfect BUSCO completeness of 99.7% against the Flavobacterium lineage. This high-quality assembly successfully resolved the taxonomic status of the isolate, definitively confirming its identity as F. bizetiae through multiple whole-genome metrics, including 100% phylogenomic clustering, an ANIb of 95.36%, and a dDDH value of 68.3% against the official type strain CIP 105534ᵀ.

Our findings reveal that while the strain lacks traditional clinical toxins, it possesses an intact, stably integrated T9SS and a specialized repertoire of chitin-degrading enzymes (GH18 and GH20), providing the putative potential to facilitate opportunistic pathogenicity by breaching the host’s physical protective barriers. More importantly, the characterization of the resistome and MGEs revealed that while “last-resort” resistance genes like the carbapenemase JOHN-1 are stably integrated, multiple copies of tet(X) are localized within active multidrug resistance islands driven by IS1595-family transposases. This establishes this seemingly non-pathogenic environmental isolate as an active vehicle for the horizontal dissemination of high-risk antibiotic resistance genes in aquaculture ecosystems. Coupled with a highly specific 6 mA-dominated epigenetic defense mechanism, this strain demonstrates robust environmental adaptation.

Overall, this complete genomic map serves as a vital reference for monitoring the environmental dimensions of antimicrobial resistance. These insights emphasize the importance of continuous, high-resolution genomic surveillance to mitigate the spread of multidrug resistance and to better understand the opportunistic potential of the genus Flavobacterium in the global fishing industry.

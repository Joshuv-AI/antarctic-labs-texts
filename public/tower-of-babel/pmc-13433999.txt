Optimization of image restoration technology and AI iterative upgrade based on PCNN
Authors: Bingxuan Zhang, Xuan Chen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This study aims to enhance the overall balance among image detail restoration, structure preservation, and model adaptability in image restoration tasks. The study proposes an optimized Pulse Coupled Neural Network (PCNN) image restoration model integrated with artificial intelligence (AI), leveraging the bio-inspired advantages of PCNN in image processing. In terms of model construction, a PCNN-AI restoration system with end-to-end training capability is designed. The PCNN backbone network extracts temporal pulse features of images. The AI module adaptively optimizes key parameters such as coupling coefficients and firing thresholds. The experimental design uses the DIV2K dataset to construct diverse image degradation samples. Comparative results show that the optimized model has significant advantages in image quality: For urban architectural images, it achieves a Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR) of 34.07, Structural Similarity Index (SSIM) of 0.957, and Learned Perceptual Image Patch Similarity (LPIPS) of 0.036. The optimized model outperforms models like Swin Transformer for Image Restoration (SwinIR), Nonlinear Activation Free Network (NAFNet) and Restoration Transformer (Restormer). In simulation experiments, the model demonstrates strong robustness and interpretability, with a structural salience matching degree of 0.94 and semantic consistency projection rate of 0.97, which is significantly superiors to comparative model. Thus, this study provides a fusion path for image restoration and understanding that balances structural modeling, biological mechanisms, and AI optimization. It offers theoretical value and engineering application significance for algorithm design and system deployment in image restoration tasks.

Introduction

Research background and motivation

With the rapid development of computer vision and image processing technologies, image restoration has become an important technique. It is used to improve image quality, enhance visual perception, and boost information extraction capabilities. This technique has been widely applied in several fields, including remote sensing image reconstruction, medical image diagnosis, industrial inspection, and security monitoring1,2. In existing image restoration methods, deep learning models have achieved remarkable results due to their powerful feature learning capabilities3. However, traditional convolutional neural network (CNN) has some limitations despite their strengths. While they excel in representational modeling, they often lack bio-inspired adaptive perception mechanisms. This deficiency imposes generalization limitations on local detail modeling. As a result, their restoration performance becomes unstable when facing complex degradation types. These complex degradation types include mixed noise, compression artifacts, and illumination distortion4,5. As a neurodynamic model inspired by biological visual cortex mechanisms, Pulse Coupled Neural Network (PCNN) has several unique advantages. These include dynamic linking, temporal firing, and spatial perception. Due to these characteristics, PCNN demonstrates good adaptability and robustness in various tasks, such as image segmentation, feature extraction, and edge detection6,7. In recent years, researchers have attempted to introduce PCNN into image restoration tasks, expecting to leverage its coupling mechanism for fine-grained modeling of local image regions to improve restoration accuracy and detail fidelity8,9. Nevertheless, the original PCNN model suffers from problems such as difficult parameter selection, insufficient feature generalization, and lack of end-to-end learning capability. These problems restrict its extended application in modern complex visual tasks10,11.

Therefore, this study builds on the bio-inspired advantages of the PCNN model and combines the trainable mechanisms and attention structures of current deep learning. It also explores the optimization path for image restoration technology, and constructs an iteratively upgradeable AI-based restoration framework. The goal is to enhance the model’s adaptability to complex degraded images, interpretability, and deployment efficiency.

Research objectives

This study aims to improve the performance and generalization ability of image restoration models in multi-semantic scenarios, and maximize the accuracy of structure restoration and detail reconstruction under complex degradation conditions. To achieve this goal, this study conducts specific research around the following three aspects:Construct a spiking neural network structure integrated with AI mechanism, and introduce a dynamic response adjustment module and a multi-scale feature fusion mechanism to enhance the model’s ability to restore image structure edges and texture details.Design a content-aware guidance strategy adapted to multi-semantic images. Literature review

As the importance of image restoration technology continues to grow, it has become increasingly significant in multiple fields like image processing, computer vision, and remote sensing decoding. Given its importance, scholars in China and other countries have carried out extensive research. Their research focuses on several key areas, including restoration mechanisms, algorithm models, and neural structure optimization. Sinha et al.12 found that PCNN was a dynamic model that mimics the behavior of cortical neurons in the mammalian visual system. It had several notable capabilities: good regional perception and edge preservation capabilities. Due to these strengths, PCNN had shown better performance than traditional methods in tasks such as image segmentation and edge detection12. They pointed out that PCNN could effectively address the problems of low-contrast regions and detail blurring in images through its coupling connection weights and temporal dynamic processing mechanism. Zang et al.13 further expanded the application scope of PCNN in image processing, proposing an image fusion method based on a multi-channel PCNN structure. They significantly improved the preservation of image feature details and anti-interference performance13. They emphasized that PCNN’s ability to simulate the relationships between image regions far exceeded that of classic linear models, making it particularly suitable for unstructured data processing scenarios. Li et al.14 found that image restoration methods based on CNNs had made breakthroughs in handling image super-resolution reconstruction, especially in structural restoration and texture reconstruction14. They noted that the end-to-end training mechanism of deep models provided greater modeling flexibility and stronger performance improvement potential for image restoration tasks. Yang et al.15 proposed the Deformable Transformer for Visual Tracking (DeforT) model, emphasizing the advantages of the deformable attention mechanism in handling visual tasks with significant spatial structure changes15. By dynamically learning key points in local regions and performing soft matching, this model significantly enhanced the geometric adaptability of the Transformer in target tracking and image structure restoration. Its method provided inspiration for non-rigid structure modeling in tasks such as image restoration, and exhibited stronger robustness especially when countering complex degradations (e.g., blurring and occlusion). The Siamese Corner Networks for Visual Tracking (SiamCorners) model proposed by Yang et al.16 highlighted the importance of accurately extracting edge corner information in visual tasks16. This method adopted a dual-branch Siamese structure, and combined corner detection with feature matching to effectively enhance boundary perception and target localization capabilities. Although it is mainly applied to target tracking, its edge-sensitive characteristics had important reference significance for edge reconstruction and structural consistency maintenance in image restoration—especially for improving the detail alignment and structure recognition capabilities of restoration models. Additionally, Zhou et al.17 found that constructing a “CNN+PCNN” cascaded structure and predicting PCNN threshold parameters using only a shallow convolutional network could improve image denoising and edge preservation to a certain extent. However, its parameter adjustment mainly focused on a single threshold dimension, and it lacked explicit modeling of the temporal domain pulse evolution process, resulting in limited adaptability to complex degraded scenarios17. Karthikeyan et al.18 discovered that combining the attention mechanism with PCNN and using a lightweight feature extraction network to adaptively adjust coupling coefficients could improve the restoration quality of texture details while ensuring relatively controllable parameters. Nevertheless, the overall structure mostly remained at the single-stage spatial feature fusion level and failed to fully explore the synergistic relationship between multi-scale and multi-temporal features18.

Overall, existing studies have provided a relatively rich theoretical foundation and algorithmic practice for image restoration, but certain limitations remain. On the one hand, most PCNN-based works still stay in early model architectures or the “single-parameter prediction” paradigm, making it difficult to finely model complex, multi-source superimposed image degradation processes and limiting adaptability to high-dimensional heterogeneous data and time-varying scenarios. On the other hand, traditional PCNN relies heavily on manually set coupling coefficients and thresholds, lacking a truly end-to-end parameter adaptive mechanism, which restricts its real-time performance, generalization, and engineering scalability. Although some studies have attempted to use CNN or attention networks for data-driven prediction of individual PCNN parameters, improving denoising and detail preservation performance to a certain extent, such methods usually act only on a single parameter dimension and mostly adopt a single-stage spatial feature fusion structure, failing to fully explore the synergistic potential between PCNN pulse temporal evolution and multi-scale deep features. At the same time, lightweight image restoration models have made significant progress in parameter compression and inference efficiency, but they mostly focus on pure deep network structures, insufficiently utilizing the structural interpretability and temporal characteristics of bio-inspired models like PCNN.

Based on the above shortcomings, this study, building on existing literature, further introduces dynamic gating and region-adaptive mechanisms to conduct in-depth modeling for multi-parameter and full-process PCNN temporal evolution. It jointly predicts multiple key PCNN parameters through deep neural networks, breaking the limitations of traditional “manual parameter tuning + single-parameter correction” paradigms, and realizing synergistic self-learning and adaptive optimization of structures and parameters. Meanwhile, a multi-stage temporal fusion and multi-scale residual structure are designed to hierarchically couple the impulse responses generated by PCNN in the time domain with deep feature representations. While balancing interpretability and restoration quality, the model scale is controlled by integrating lightweight design concepts, thus forming differentiated advantages compared with existing PCNN + AI fusion methods in three aspects: “parameter adjustment dimension”, “feature utilization depth”, and “performance-efficiency balance”.

Research model

The basic principle and modeling mechanism of PCNN

PCNN is a neural network model constructed based on the biological visual system, aiming to simulate the synchronous firing mechanism of neuron populations in the cat visual cortex. The basic idea of PCNN is to achieve regional perception, feature extraction, and information fusion of image data by mimicking the coupling behavior and pulse firing mechanism of biological neurons19. A typical PCNN model generally consists of three core components: an input module, a linking module, and a pulse firing module, with each pixel usually corresponding to a neuron node20,21. Its overall structure is characterized by local connectivity, parallel processing, and dynamic regulation.

AI-driven image restoration has achieved remarkable breakthroughs in processing accuracy, structure restoration, and detail reconstruction. It has also promoted the reconstruction and functional expansion of traditional neural network structures32,33.

Introduction of parameter adaptive mechanism

Introduction of parameter adaptive mechanism

To improve the reproducibility of the model, this study provides more explicit design and implementation details for the interaction mechanism between the PCNN and AI modules. First, in terms of input feature types, the AI module adopts a joint input method of “image patches + local statistical features”: local patches obtained by segmenting degraded images or intermediate feature maps into k*k windows are used as the basic input. Meanwhile, statistics such as local contrast and gradient magnitude (edge intensity) are calculated within each patch and concatenated into a one-dimensional feature vector, which serves as the input to the lightweight neural network. This design retains spatial context and explicitly introduces local information highly correlated with degradation patterns.

In terms of network structure design, the AI module adopts a lightweight network with a two-layer fully connected structure. The first layer is a fully connected layer with 128 hidden nodes, followed by ReLU activation, and the second layer is a fully connected layer whose output dimension is consistent with the number of parameters to be predicted. The reason for choosing 128 nodes in subsequent experiments is a trade-off result between comprehensive performance and parameter quantity obtained after conducting comparative experiments on different node scales such as 64, 128, and 256. The 128-node configuration achieves the most balanced performance between PSNR/SSIM improvement and parameter overhead. Optimization of pulse threshold regulation strategy

The threshold in the classic PCNN model uses exponential decay, with a change rate independent of image degradation types. This study introduces a gating mechanism to adjust the threshold update function nonlinearly. Specifically: In high-noise regions, higher thresholds are maintained to suppress false activations. In texture-rich regions, thresholds are lowered to enhance activation responses. This approach achieves regional differentiated regulation.

Multi-stage temporal information fusion mechanism

This study designs a temporal fusion structure that integrates information from multiple rounds of outputs via a convolutional gating mechanism, forming restored images with time-domain dynamic features.

To avoid numerical instability, this study further performs normalization on the gating weights in the time dimension:9\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\hat{g}^{{\left( t \right)}} (x,y) = \frac{{g^{{\left( t \right)}} (x,y)}}{{\sum\nolimits_{{k = 1}}^{T} {g^{{\left( k \right)}} (x,y) + \varepsilon } }}$$\end{document}

\varepsilon is a tiny constant to prevent division by zero. \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\widehat{g}}^{\left(t\right)}(x,y)$$\end{document} is not an abstract mathematical coefficient, but characterizes the relative contribution of the t-th PCNN pulse output to the final restored result at spatial location (x,y). From a mechanistic perspective, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\widehat{g}}^{\left(t\right)}(x,y)$$\end{document} can be interpreted as a temporal attention allocation factor based on pulse response saliency, which dynamically selects the most valuable information for current pixel restoration across multiple iteration stages. As PCNN exhibits distinct response characteristics to image regions at different iteration stages, the gating weight follows a physically interpretable distribution across spatial regions. Specifically: In image edge regions and high-frequency texture regions, pulse outputs from early or middle iterations usually capture structural contours and local abrupt changes more effectively, so the corresponding \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\widehat{g}}^{\left(t\right)}(x,y)$$\end{document} values show higher response weights at these stages. In large smooth regions or low-frequency background regions, the pulse response from a single iteration is often insufficient to provide a significant advantage, and the gating weights tend to be more evenly distributed across multiple iterations. This reduces the risk of noise amplification and improves restoration stability.

The final time fusion feature map is defined as the weighted sum of each round of pulse output:10\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$F(x,y) = \sum\limits_{{t = 1}}^{T} {\hat{g}^{{\left( t \right)}} (x,y)Y^{{\left( t \right)}} (x,y)}$$\end{document}

F(x,y) denotes the feature map. In this way, the gating function type is clearly defined as Sigmoid gating, and the weight assignment rule is “dynamic spatial-temporal weight allocation based on the saliency of pulse responses”. In regions with rich edges, textures, or severe degradation, the gating network automatically increases the weight of output from early or mid-late iterations, thereby enhancing attention to key structures and difficult regions. In flat regions, the weights tend to be more balanced or weakened to reduce the risk of noise amplification. The obtained feature map is used as the input to the subsequent multi-scale residual reconstruction module, and further fused with deep convolutional features to generate the final restoration result.

To address issues such as structural rigidity and poor adaptability to complex image degradation patterns in PCNN models, this study designs two AI-assisted mechanisms: First, the process involves constructing a lightweight neural network. This network takes raw image patches or intermediate feature maps as input. It then outputs PCNN parameters (such as coupling weights and firing thresholds) for corresponding positions. This enables position-sensitive dynamic parameter adjustment. This network uses a shared-weight structure and achieves global optimization of the parameter space through joint training with the main model. Second, a regional attention module is constructed. This module modulates the pulse response intensity of each PCNN neuron. As a result, the model can focus on regions that are rich in detail or have high-frequency noise. This enhancement improves its capabilities in detail restoration and noise suppression. The specific model architecture is shown in Fig. 2:

Experimental design and performance evaluation

Datasets collection, experimental environment and parameters setting

The dataset used in this study is the Diverse 2 K Resolution Image Dataset (DIV2K Dataset). DIV2K is one of the most widely used and authoritative high-quality image datasets in the field of image super-resolution and restoration, released by the New Trends in Image Restoration and Enhancement (NTIRE) team at the Conference on Computer Vision and Pattern Recognition. It is specifically designed for image restoration tasks, including denoising, deblurring, super-resolution, Joint Photographic Experts Group (JPEG) de-compression, etc. The dataset can be downloaded from the official website of the AI seminar (https://data.vision.ee.ethz.ch/cvl/DIV2K/). In the DIV2K dataset, the image content is rich and diverse. This characteristic makes it suitable for division according to semantic categories. This division allows for the evaluation of the generalization ability and regional perception performance of image restoration models under different image content types. The dataset contains a total of 1000 images, with 800 in the training set, 100 in the validation set, and 100 in the test set. All images have a uniform 2 K resolution, with high quality and no compression loss, making them suitable for model training and performance evaluation in various image restoration tasks. The DIV2K images cover rich and diverse content, including natural landscapes, urban architecture, portraits and still life, daily life scenes, and other categories. To more precisely analyze the model’s generalization ability and structural sensitivity in images of different semantic types, this study divides the images into three categories based on their semantic features for grouped experiments:Natural landscape images (e.g., mountains, lakes, forests);Urban architecture images (e.g., streets, building complexes, bridges);Portrait and still life images (e.g., portraits, plants and animals, indoor items).

To simulate image degradation phenomena in the real world, this study constructs multiple degraded versions based on the official DIV2K task design and mainstream research practices. First, bicubic down sampling is applied with scaling factors of ×2, ×3, and ×4. Second, Gaussian blur degradation is used to simulate optical blur during shooting. Finally, Gaussian noise is added to reproduce image noise caused by photosensitive elements. Through the above degradation settings, multi-source degraded samples can be constructed to test the robustness and generalization ability of image restoration models under different degradation mechanisms.

The experiment also selects the Japanese Society of Radiological Technology Dataset (JSRT) and the UC Merced Land Use Dataset for ablation experiments. The JSRT Dataset contains 247 high-quality chest X-ray images with a resolution of 2048 × 2048, which is widely used in tasks such as image denoising, super-resolution, lung nodule detection, and lesion enhancement. The dataset can be downloaded via http://db.jsrt.or.jp/eng.php. The UC Merced Land Use Dataset includes 2100 images covering 21 semantic categories (e.g., airport, residential area, forest, farmland), with 100 images per category. It is commonly used in tasks like image super-resolution, image enhancement, and deblurring, and can also be applied to evaluate the model’s generalization ability under complex natural/artificial structures. The dataset is available for download at http://weegee.vision.ucmerced.edu/datasets/landuse.html. In the experiment, 300 images are selected as a new dataset for research following the principle of random sampling.

This study’s experiments are conducted on hardware and software platforms with high computational capabilities and deep learning support to ensure the training efficiency and inference performance of the image restoration model. Hardware configuration: The central processing unit (CPU) is an Intel Xeon Gold 6226R with a 2.90 GHz clock speed, 16 cores, and 32 threads, enabling large-scale data parallel processing. The graphics processing unit (GPU) is an NVIDIA RTX 3090 with 24GB VRAM, based on the Ampere architecture. It supports mixed-precision training and large-batch image parallel processing, providing powerful acceleration for training complex neural network structures. Software environment: The operating system is Ubuntu 20.04 LTS, which offers good compatibility and open-source ecosystem support. The deep learning framework used is PyTorch 1.13.1, combined with CUDA 11.6 and cuDNN 8.4 acceleration libraries to enable efficient GPU parallel training. For image preprocessing, OpenCV 4.5.5 is employed. NumPy 1.23.1 is used for numerical computations. Visualization analysis relies on tools like Matplotlib and Seaborn.

In the experimental process of this study, to ensure the proposed optimized model has good recovery performance and training stability, the key parameters of the model are reasonably configured and optimized. In the backbone structure of pulse-coupled neural network, the initial value of coupling coefficient β is set to 1.5. The dynamic threshold attenuation factor α is set to 0.2. The initial threshold θ₀ is uniformly set to 1.0. The number of iterations of PCNN is set to 25 rounds. In the AI guidance module, the auxiliary parameter prediction network adopts a two-layer fully connected structure. The number of nodes in the hidden layer is 128. The activation function adopts ReLU. The output layer corresponds to the dynamic mapping value of PCNN key parameters. The mean square error (MSE) is used as the loss function for training and optimization. The integration model is trained by Adam optimizer. The initial learning rate is set to 0.0001. The learning rate is dynamically adjusted after each epoch by using Cosine Annealing strategy. The batch size is set to 16, the maximum number of training rounds is 100. The early stop mechanism is set to stop training when there is no improvement in the verification set for 10 consecutive rounds. In the feature enhancement module, the number of residual blocks is set to five groups, each group contains two 3 × 3 convolution layers. The number of channels is 64. After all convolution operations, batch normalization and ReLU activation functions are used. The contrast models selected in the experiment are Swin Transformer for Image Restoration (SwinIR), Nonlinear Activation Free Network (NAFNet) and Restoration Transformer (Restormer).

Simulation experiment

In the simulation experiment design, to complement the performance comparison experiments, this study selects two new dimensions as evaluation directions: robustness & adaptability and model interpretability. Each dimensions incorporates 4 non-overlapping comparative metrics to comprehensively measure the optimized model’s performance under real complex image degradation conditions. Among these metrics: Multi-degradation response amplitude refers to the standard deviation of performance fluctuations under different degradation types. A smaller value indicates stronger robustness. Adaptive activation sparsity represents the sparsity of the model’s activation distribution in different image regions, reflecting regional adaptability. Maximum fault tolerance rate denotes the model’s ability to maintain basic structure restoration under high-intensity degradation, with the 90% fidelity boundary counted. Noise suppression-fidelity ratio is the balance ratio between denoising effect and structure preservation. A higher value indicates more refined noise processing. Attention concentration is the entropy of attention distribution in salient regions of the output image. A smaller value indicates higher concentration. Parameter variation sensitivity refers to the degree of output change after slight parameter disturbances, used to measure the model’s stable interpretation interval. Structural salient matching degree represents the matching degree between the model’s high-response regions and the real image structure, reflecting regional perceptual consistency. Semantic consistent projection rate is the alignment degree between the “perceptual regions” reflected by SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) and the semantic Ground Truth (GT). A higher value indicates higher model credibility. The comparative results of the robustness and adaptability evaluation dimension are shown in Fig. 5:

Discussion

Discussion on experimental results

From the performance comparison results, the proposed optimized model outperforms existing mainstream models across multiple image semantic categories and index dimensions, especially in structural restoration and detail repair of urban architectural images. This observation indicates several key points: The PCNN-based structure has inherent advantages in handling image geometric features and edge continuity. Integrating AI modules for dynamic parameter adjustment and feature guidance significantly enhances its adaptability. This enhanced adaptability allows the model to better handle different degradation scenarios. Additionally, considering model efficiency, the model achieves a good balance between parameter count and inference speed. It demonstrates strong deployment feasibility. This makes it particularly suitable for scenarios with limited resources or requiring high response speeds, such as embedded image restoration and medical image enhancement.

The simulation results show that the proposed optimized model demonstrates excellent robustness and adaptive capabilities under complex degradation conditions. The model exhibits several notable characteristics: It has low-amplitude response fluctuations. It demonstrates stable activation control. It shows remarkable fault tolerance. These features indicate that the PCNN structure, when integrated with AI mechanisms, can effectively adjust the response intensity of neurons. This adaptability allows it to handle various degradation scenarios. In particular, it performs exceptionally well in scenarios with clear urban structures and complex degradation patterns. Meanwhile, the model has notable strengths in terms of interpretability: It achieves precise focus on important regions. It provides stable response output. It ensures highly consistent structural matching. These capabilities are enabled through dynamic attention guidance and parameter regulation mechanisms. As a result, the transparency and controllability of the restoration behavior are enhanced. This integration path combining bio-neural inspiration mechanisms with AI training capabilities provides new possibilities for achieving model interpretability and practical application credibility.

Discussion on model limitations

Although the PCNN image restoration model integrating the proposed AI mechanism performs excellently in multiple performance dimensions, it still has limitations. In terms of model efficiency: The average inference time of the proposed model on the three types of images is 35.67 ms (urban architecture), 36.21 ms (portrait and still life), and 36.89 ms (natural landscape), respectively. Though it outperforms Restormer (47.02 ms) and is close to the lightweight model NAFNet (33.86 ms), it still cannot meet the real-time processing requirements of ultra-low latency scenarios. The model has 9.66 M parameters, which is significantly fewer than Restormer (15.93 M) but still more than lightweight models such as SwinIR-T (6.67 M). When deployed on embedded platforms or mobile terminals, it relies to a certain extent on hardware performance (e.g., GPU frequency, cache scheduling, number of parallel channels), which limits its adaptability to ultra-low power consumption platforms. In terms of memory occupancy: the model’s memory usage for the three types of images is 1153 MB, 1175 MB, and 1189 MB, respectively. Though lower than Restormer (average 1380 MB) and SwinIR (1230 MB), it may still cause memory bottlenecks in edge computing devices with integrated GPUs (e.g., Jetson Nano or Raspberry Pi). Based on calculations in a simulated hardware environment: the estimated energy consumption per image of the proposed model is 1.24 J/Frame, 1.28 J/Frame, and 1.31 J/Frame, respectively. Though better than Restormer (approximately 1.65 J/Frame), it has not yet met the energy efficiency standards for the sustainable operation of ultra-low power AI chips. Although the adopted AI-integrated PCNN structure shows good performance in image restoration tasks, its adaptability to task transfer still needs evaluation. Currently, the model is designed for image super-resolution and denoising; its structural generalization ability for more complex tasks (e.g., image de-raining, modal conversion such as SAR→RGB) has not been verified. Meanwhile, in temporal modeling scenarios (e.g., video restoration, multi-frame fusion), the existing framework lacks a modeling mechanism for temporal consistency, which limits its scalability in video stream processing. From the hyperparameter sensitivity experiment: although the optimized model still shows good stability under parameter disturbances within a certain range, its performance is significantly affected by some key parameters—especially the selection of learning rate and convolution kernel size. An excessively small or large learning rate may lead to overfitting or underfitting of the model. A reduction in the receptive field range will also inhibit the model’s ability to restore details. This indicates that during the actual deployment of the model, detailed hyperparameter tuning is required for specific tasks or data distributions. Otherwise, performance degradation may occur. Especially in scenarios with limited resources or insufficient training samples, the impact of hyperparameter tuning on the final effect cannot be ignored.

Research discussion

In the above experiments, this study verifies the effectiveness of the proposed PCNN-AI fusion model in image restoration from both quantitative metrics and visual effects. However, summarizing the research contributions only from the perspective of performance improvement cannot clearly reflect the innovation of this study relative to existing PCNN-AI fusion methods. Therefore, it is necessary to systematically compare and discuss the proposed method with related works in terms of model design philosophy, fusion mode, and utilization degree of PCNN core mechanisms. Existing fusion studies of PCNN and artificial intelligence methods mainly focus on two types of ideas. One is to treat PCNN as a feature enhancement or post-processing module, which strengthens edge and structure information through fixed or empirical parameters. The other is to use deep learning networks to perform offline or weakly adaptive adjustment on some parameters of PCNN, but most of them remain at the level of static or stage-wise regulation. Such methods improve the flexibility of traditional PCNN to a certain extent, but still suffer from problems such as limited parameter adjustment granularity, insufficient utilization of temporal evolution mechanism, and unclear synergy among modules.

In contrast, the proposed method further advances both fusion depth and mechanism utilization. First, in terms of fusion mode, this study introduces an end-to-end trainable parameter adaptive mechanism, which achieves dynamic parameter regulation with dual awareness of spatial location and iteration stage, rather than relying only on global or fixed parameter settings. Second, at the model structure level, this study explicitly introduces a multi-stage temporal fusion mechanism, which treats the pulse responses generated by multiple PCNN iterations as temporal features with complementary information and performs adaptive aggregation through a gating strategy, thereby fully exploiting the evolutionary characteristics of PCNN. Third, in terms of interpretability, this study establishes a more intuitive correspondence between model behavior and image structure changes through joint analysis of parameter fields and gating weights. Conclusion

Research contribution

This study performs structural reconstruction on the traditional PCNN model, introduces dynamic gating and regional perception mechanisms, and establishes a restoration backbone network with iterative evolutionary capability. Compared with the rigidity in parameter configuration of the classic PCNN model, the optimized structure features spatial adaptability and multi-level image response capability, enhancing the model’s restoration effect for detail information and texture features. Moreover, by introducing an auxiliary neural network module to perform real-time prediction and dynamic update of key PCNN parameters (such as coupling coefficients and firing thresholds), the versatility of PCNN under different image degradation patterns is significantly improved. This mechanism not only enhances the model’s adaptability in the field of unstructured images but also makes up for the limitation that traditional models cannot be optimized end-to-end. Meanwhile, this study has achieved a good balance between model performance and resource consumption. Experiments show the following about the model: It ensures image quality, has lower computational overhead, achieves faster inference speed, and exhibits higher energy efficiency. These characteristics demonstrate the model’s potential for engineering implementation in scenarios such as embedded image processing systems and medical image enhancement terminals. Additionally, by introducing mechanisms such as attention guidance, regional sparse response, and salient structure alignment, the model’s interpretability is improved, providing a demonstration for controllable AI design.

Future works and research limitations

Although the proposed PCNN-AI fused image restoration model has experimentally verified its effectiveness in structural recovery, perceptual quality and stability, from a deeper theoretical perspective, the fusion framework still has several inherent limitations that have not been fully addressed, which need systematic reflection and targeted improvement in future research. From the perspective of the theoretical utilization of the PCNN pulse firing mechanism, the current model does not fully exploit the dynamic expressive potential of PCNN as a spiking neural system. This study mainly regards the pulse output of PCNN as a structure-aware and region-synchronous intermediate representation, and enhances regional consistency modeling in the pseudo-time dimension through multiple iterations. However, the discreteness and event-driven characteristics of PCNN pulse firing, as well as the temporal coding information contained in inter-spike intervals, have not been explicitly modeled in the existing framework. In other words, the current model mostly utilizes the spatial synchronization property of PCNN, while the exploitation of its temporal coding ability (such as spiking frequency and firing rhythm) remains limited. Theoretically, this restricts the model’s expressive ability for complex dynamic structures or non-uniform degradation patterns. From the perspective of the coupling mode between the AI module and PCNN, the existing fusion strategy still focuses on parameter modulation. The AI module mainly guides the evolution process of PCNN by predicting a continuous parameter field. Although this approach achieves good stability and controllability in engineering implementation, it is theoretically an external modulation fusion rather than genuine spike-level collaborative computing. The AI network does not directly participate in pulse generation or the construction of competition mechanisms, nor does it impose constraints on the discrete decision-making process of pulse firing, which to some extent weakens the uniqueness of PCNN in bio-inspired modeling. From the theoretical boundary of model interpretability, although this study enhances the structural interpretability through visualization of gating weights and parameter fields, such explanations mainly remain at the level of continuous features and statistical distributions. A formal correspondence among spike behavior, structural semantics, and restoration quality has not yet been established. This implies that the current interpretation mechanism is mostly empirical rather than based on rigorous theoretical characterization.

Extend the AI module from the parameter prediction layer to the spike generation or competitive decision-making layer.

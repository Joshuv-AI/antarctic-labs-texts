Letter to the Editor: multimodal biopsy in radial EBUS
Authors: Taehoon Lee
Published: 2026

Dear Editor,

We sincerely thank Huang and Wu for their thoughtful comments (1) regarding our recently published article. We greatly appreciate their careful reading of our work and their constructive discussion on the interpretation of radial endobronchial ultrasound (rEBUS) findings (2). Their correspondence provides an opportunity to further clarify the rationale and clinical implications of our study.

We agree that the interpretation of rEBUS visualization is inherently subjective and that interobserver variability represents an important methodological issue. In routine clinical practice, the distinction between within-type and adjacent-to/eccentric-within visualization inevitably depends on real-time assessment by experienced bronchoscopists. Although further standardization of image acquisition and interpretation is certainly desirable, we believe that complete standardization of rEBUS image interpretation is unlikely to be fully achievable because image interpretation inevitably depends on dynamic probe movement, lesion characteristics, and operator experience. Therefore, a certain degree of subjective interpretation will likely remain unavoidable even in future studies.

However, we would like to clarify one of the principal messages of our study (2). Our findings were not intended to suggest that additional biopsy is beneficial only when rEBUS visualization is adjacent to the lesion. Rather, our data demonstrated that additional larger forceps biopsy (LFB) improved both diagnostic yield (DY) and diagnostic tissue acquisition rate (DTAR) not only in adjacent-to or eccentric-within lesions but also in lesions with within-type rEBUS visualization. Specifically, in patients with within-type lesions, the addition of LFB increased DY from 80.4% to 88.0% and DTAR from 67.4% to 83.7%. Likewise, LFB also improved both DY and DTAR in adjacent-to or eccentric-within lesions. These findings suggest that the value of supplementary biopsy should not be limited to lesions with suboptimal rEBUS visualization but should also be considered when the probe is located within the lesion, particularly when maximizing tissue acquisition for histologic diagnosis and molecular testing is an important clinical objective.

Another important message of our study is the practical value of LFB. Compared with other adjunctive biopsy techniques, LFB is inexpensive, technically straightforward, and can be readily incorporated into routine rEBUS procedures without requiring specialized equipment or substantial additional procedural complexity. We also note that the technical issue raised by Huang and Wu was acknowledged in our original Discussion (2). Because LFB requires removal of the guide sheath, exact repositioning of the biopsy forceps cannot be guaranteed. Therefore, fluoroscopic guidance together with the previously confirmed rEBUS tip position was used as an anatomical reference during LFB. We agree that further refinement of this technique and prospective validation are warranted. Nevertheless, we believe that the ability to achieve meaningful improvements in both DY and DTAR by simply adding this low-cost biopsy technique represents one of the major clinical implications of our study. Our intention was therefore not to establish the superiority of one biopsy modality over another, but rather to demonstrate that a pragmatic multimodal biopsy strategy incorporating LFB can enhance diagnostic performance while remaining practical and cost-effective in daily clinical practice.

Finally, we fully agree that prospective studies with standardized image interpretation and larger patient populations are needed to further validate these findings. We appreciate the insightful comments from Huang and Wu and believe that continued refinement of both rEBUS image interpretation and multimodal biopsy strategies will further improve the bronchoscopic diagnosis of peripheral lung lesions.

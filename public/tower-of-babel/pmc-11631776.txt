Evaluation of Timing of Implant Placement in Maxillary Esthetic Zone with Type I Extraction Sockets- A Randomized Controlled Trial
Authors: Muthukumar Santhanakrishnan, Vedavalli Subramanian, Devi Arul, Sri Vidhya Marimuthu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeThe present study aimed to evaluate the appropriate protocol to be followed in the maxillary esthetic zone involving single dental implants by comparing three implant placement protocols: immediate implant placement (IIP) with and without the socket shield technique (SST) and delayed implant placement (DIP) in terms of dynamic alterations in hard and soft tissues and patient-related outcome measures (PROMS).Materials and MethodsA total of 75 patients were recruited for the study and randomly allocated to the SST, IIP, and DIP groups (25 each). They were subjected to Cone Beam Computed Tomography (CBCT) assessment pre-operatively, 6 and 12 months post-operatively to evaluate changes in Crestal Bone Thickness (CBT). Soft tissue changes were evaluated using the pink aesthetic score (PES) and visual analogue scale (VAS) to assess the pain threshold and patient satisfaction.ResultsThere was a statistically significant difference in the mean reductions in crestal bone thickness (CBT) between and among the groups. The SST group demonstrated a significantly lower reduction in CBT (0.09) than the IIP and DIP groups, which showed a mean reduction in CBT of 0.18 and 0.50 at (p<0.01), respectively, at the end of 12 months. However, when the mean differences in PES between the groups were compared, there was a statistically significant difference for SST 13(2), IIP 10(2), and DIP 9(2) (p<0.01).ConclusionSocket shield technique could be technique of choice for IIP when buccal bone thickness is <1mm as demonstrated by less reduction in CBT and better PES at the end of 12 months than the IIP and DIP groups.Keywords: socket shield technique, single tooth dental implant, socket graft

Introduction

The timing of implant placement in the maxillary aesthetic region (premolar to premolar) remains one of the toughest challenges for clinicians. Although immediate placement of implants is the most sought-after protocol both by patients and clinicians, it is usually associated with complications like soft and hard tissue loss following implant placement particularly during the first 12 weeks following extraction which is generally determined by the thickness of crestal bone in the buccal aspect.1 However, usage of bone and soft tissue grafts tends to reduce post-operative complications to a greater extent usually when the crestal bone thickness (CBT) is >1 mm.2 But, usually, the thickness of CBT is <1 mm in the maxillary esthetic region, so the alternate choice of treatment would be delayed implant placement (DIP) following socket preservation.3 However, this would take a minimum of 4 months for the socket to heal before implant placement, which is not acceptable to patients as it is detrimental to esthetics.

In this scenario, Socket Shield Technique (SST) has been widely practised which is based on the concept of preserving the facial segment of the tooth wherein the periodontal ligament is maintained intact, thereby preserving the vascularity and preventing buccal bone resorption.4 However, there is limited literature comparing these three protocols IIP, DIP, and SST in terms of changes in soft and hard tissues following implant placement.5 CBCT has been widely used to evaluate the implant site and to determine the Crestal Cortical Bone thickness (CBT), which is of clinical significance when immediate placement of implants is planned. Similarly, the pink aesthetic score (PES) has been widely followed to evaluate soft tissue integrity following implant placement.4 Moreover, patient-related outcome measures (PROMS) pertaining to these three protocols have not been evaluated. In this scenario, the present article discusses the changes in hard and soft tissues and PROMS following implant placement using these three protocols, one year following implant placement which is a continuation of our previous study6 which reported the 6 months results.

Materials and Methods

The study was conducted according to the CONSORT guidelines as a prospective randomised controlled, double-blinded, triple-arm clinical trial (http://www.consortstatement.org/) in which outcome adjudicators and data analysts were blinded. The institutional ethical committee of the Sri Ramachandra Institute of Higher Education and Research approved all materials and interventions (REF: IEC/19/APR/150/20), and the trial was monitored following Good Clinical Practice. The trial was registered in the Clinical Trial Registry, India (CTRI), http://www.clinicaltrials.gov/ (REF: CTRI/2019/06/019723).

Sampling: Sample Size

The mean and standard deviation (summary statistics) used for the buccal bone thickness for the IIP, SST, and DIP groups were calculated based on the number of patients to be treated.7 Sample size calculation was performed using the Mann–Whitney test (two-independent sample) with an effect size of 1.1, power of 95%, and significance level alpha of 5%. V.3.1, G-power software was used which resulted in 19 participants in each of the three groups.

Population

Patients were recruited consecutively from the Outpatient Department of Periodontology and Implantology, Sri Ramachandra Dental College and Hospital, Chennai. The patients signed a written informed consent upon acceptance in full accordance with the ethical principles of the Helsinki Declaration on experimentation involving human subjects, as revised in 2008.

Preoperative Evaluation

The preoperative clinical parameters included the Oral Hygiene Index–Simplified8 for assessment of oral hygiene, tissue biotype determination, thin or thick9 pocket probing depth with a UNC 15 periodontal probe (Hu-Friedy Mfg. Co., Chicago, IL, USA), and the Pink Aesthetic Score (PES) for assessment of soft tissue esthetics.10

Primary Outcome Variables

CBCT Standardisation and Assessment of Crestal Bone Thickness

The thickness of the buccal plate at the levels mentioned below was measured using Ray Scan Alpha Plus, a cone beam 3D imaging system with a tube voltage of 90KVp, 10mA and standard exposure time of 14 (s) with a high resolution 70µm voxel. Radiation exposure was limited by reducing the collimated Field of View (FOV) to 5 cm × 5 cm. Data were acquired using volume acquisition and multiple-plane reconstruction.

The measurements taken pre-and post-operatively were standardised using a fixed reference point(r) created by drawing a line from the cervical aspect of the buccal alveolar crest (r2) parallel to the alveolar ridge (long axis), either to the floor of the nasal fossa or maxillary sinus (r1).11 (Figure 2a and b)

Esthetic Evaluation

The PES scoring system10 was used to assess secondary outcome variables, such as pink aesthetic score evaluation, performed immediately after crown placement, 6 months and one year after occlusal loading. Two independent examiners evaluated the soft tissues around the single-tooth implant crowns using the PES rating. The PES system is based on seven variables: mesial papilla, distal papilla, soft-tissue level, soft-tissue contour, alveolar process deficiency, soft-tissue colour, and texture. Each variable carried an equal weight ranging from 0 to 2, indicating the worst to best status, with a maximum possible score of 14.

Secondary Outcome Variables

Patient Related Outcome Measures (PROMS)

Esthetics

Patient satisfaction was assessed by asking the patients to rate the overall implant treatment using a visual analogue scale (VAS) labelled from 0 to 10, indicating the worst possible result to the best possible result.12

Pain

A questionnaire form13 to be completed by the patients was given, 1–6 hours post-operatively and for 7 days at the end of each day following surgery. This questionnaire included a 10-cm visual analogue scale (VAS) between 1 and 10, indicating mild to severe pain, as described previously. All patients handed in the questionnaires were collected during the first post-operative control, one week to ten days after surgery, six months and one year after occlusal loading of the implants.

Treatment Procedures

Immediate Implant with Socket Shield technique (SST): The labial segment of the root was maintained intact (socket shield) and partial extraction was performed. The shield was prepared in accordance with Hurzeler’s criteria, following which immediate implants were placed.14

The chairside fabrication of the provisional crown was performed such that the emergence profile supported the soft tissues and was kept in infra occlusion with 1 mm clearance. The patients were advised to avoid biting hard food. The implants were assessed for stability, three months after placement, after which permanent restorations were made using PFM crowns (porcelain fused to metal) (Figure 3a–j).

Statistical Analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using the IBM SPSS for Windows (version 21.0; SPSS Inc. Armonk,NY). The normality of the distribution was checked using the Kolmogorov–Smirnov test and Shapiro–Wilk test. The distribution was non-parametric and is represented as median and interquartile range (IQR) values. The Kruskal–Wallis test was used to analyse differences between groups, and Friedman’s two-way analysis of variance for within-group differences for primary (CBT) and secondary (PES) outcome variables. Dunn post-hoc analysis was performed for pairwise comparisons when the Kruskal–Wallis test and Friedman’s two-way analysis of variance were significant for pairwise comparisons between the groups. The significance level was set at p<0.05.

An Intra-class correlation coefficient test was performed to assess inter-examiner variability in PES. VAS scores for pain and aesthetics were assessed between the groups. The Kruskal–Wallis test was used, Dunn’s post-hoc analysis was performed when there was a significant difference, and Wilcoxon signed rank test was used for within-group comparisons.

Discussion

The present study aimed to evaluate the appropriate protocol to be followed in the maxillary esthetic zone involving single dental implants by comparing three implant placement protocols: immediate implant placement (IIP) with and without the socket shield technique (SST) and delayed placement of implants (DIP) in terms of dynamic alterations in hard and soft tissues and patient-related outcome measures (PROMS).

However, no prospective study has compared these three protocols. Apart from the maxillary anterior, premolars were also included in the present study, as most of the patients exhibit premolars during their dynamic smile16 and also have the same quality of bone as the anteriors.17 Crestal Cortical Bone Thickness (CBT) was regarded as the primary outcome variable as it is integral in hard and soft tissue maintenance following placement of implants.18 Crestal bone thickness was evaluated at 6 months and 1 year following implant placement. This time points were considered evaluating for CBT as maximum alterations of CBT occurs 6 months following extraction and evaluation at 1 year following implant placement gives an indication of integrity of hard and soft tissues following remodelling phase.6

Patients who demonstrated type 1 extraction sockets following extraction were included in the study to ensure that the bony walls where intact during the placement of implants to prevent any changes in CBT evaluation which is possible if patients with variable degrees of buccal bony wall resorption were recruited.6

The hard and soft tissue integrity around the implant was determined by CBT; hence, it was regarded as the primary outcome variable in this study.3 The changes in the width that occur at 1 mm from the crest of the alveolar bone are the highest as it is composed exclusively of bundle bone and is indicative of changes in the width of buccal walls at all levels; hence, measurements were taken 1 mm from the crest.1 There was no statistically significant difference among the groups at baseline (p<0.05), indicating that the changes that occurred at 6 months and one year were due to the differences in the surgical protocol employed. The CBT changes were lesser in SST group compared to the other groups at 6 months and 1 year which was highly statistically significant. This could be due to the better preservation of tooth socket with intact periodontal ligament in SST group compared to IIP group where periodontal ligament is wrecked.7 In the DIP group which showed greater changes in CBT which could have occurred due to the remodelling resorption that could have occurred during the four months interval following socket preservation and placement of implants.6

The pair-wise comparison revealed no significant difference in CBT at 6 months between SST and IIP groups which could be due to the compensatory effect of grafting the jumping distance in the IIP group which could have negotiated the remodelling.19 IIP with grafting the jumping distance will have less horizontal changes and stable mucosal margin.20 However, there was a significant difference between SST and IIP groups at 1 year time interval which could be due to the preservation of bundle bone in the SST group facilitated by the intact periodontal ligament.7

There was a significant difference in CBT between the SST and DIP groups, IIP, and DIP groups at both 6 months and one-year interval which could have resulted from the remodelling changes that could have occurred in the time period between socket preservation and placement of implants as well as bone quality that results following socket preservation.6

The results of the present study were not comparable to those of other studies, and to the best of our knowledge, this is the first study to compare these three protocols. However, the results of the study for SST group demonstrated similarity to the other studies which revealed variations in CBT of 0.09 mm,21 0.17 mm,7 0.22 mm,22 0.26 mm23 at the end of 6 months and 0.29± 0.34 mm24 at the end of one year respectively.

The results of the present study differed from those of other studies which demonstrated changes of 0.06 mm,14 0.54 mm,25 0.8 mm,26 and 1 mm27 at the end of 6 months and 0.6 mm,25 0.68 mm28 at the end of one year respectively, in the CBT due to the choice of surgical protocols employed, wider inclusion criteria, and use of digital radiographs in those studies.

The results of the IIP group were similar to a study29 that demonstrated a difference of 0.99±0.21 mm in the labial bone changes in comparison to the DIP group, and with few other studies that showed reductions of 0.1 mm,14 1.1 mm,19 1.01 mm25 6 months post-operatively. While the CBT at the end of one year was similar to studies which showed changes of 0.6 mm25 and 0.88 mm.28 The current study differed from another study which demonstrated changes of 1.45 ±0.72 mm24 at the end of one year.

The DIP group results were similar to that of other studies which revealed changes in CBT of 0.24 mm,30 0.46 mm31 at the end of 6 months and 0.27 mm,32 0.66 mm33 at the end of one year respectively.

There has been a paradigm shift for the evaluation of success in implant therapy from mere osseointegration in function to optimal aesthetics, especially in the maxillary aesthetic region. Hence, optimal evaluation of aesthetic outcomes is integral to determining the success of implant therapy. Hence, PES was considered a secondary outcome variable as it helps in the assessment of soft tissue changes before and after the placement of implants.34 All the patients were evaluated by two independent trained periodontists at baseline, 6 months and 1 year following implant placement for evaluation of PES to rule out any variations in assessment, they showed a high degree of concurrence, the intra-class correlation coefficient value was 0.94.

There was no significant difference in PES values at baseline between the SST and IIP groups. However, the SST and DIP groups and the IIP and DIP groups demonstrated a significant difference which could be due to remodelling changes that would have occurred in the 4-month waiting period following socket preservation before implant placement in the DIP group.6

There was a statistically significant difference in PES between the SST and DIP groups six months after implant placement which could be the result of a greater reduction in CBT in the DIP group (Table 6). Although there was no significant difference in PES between the SST and IIP groups and the IIP and DIP groups at 6 months, the trend clearly showed better results in the SST group (Median IQR) values of 13(1.50) than in the IIP group 12(4.0) and DIP group 11(2.0).

There was a highly significant difference in PES between the SST and IIP, SST, and DIP groups at one year after occlusal loading. The superior values in the SST group compared to the IIP and DIP groups could be the result of a smaller reduction in the thickness of the buccal cortical plate in the SST group than in the other groups which could be due to the intact periodontal ligament in the SST group. The greater reduction in CBT in the other groups also resulted in reduced labial sockets, gingival recession, and incomplete papilla. Although there was no significant difference in PES between the IIP and DIP groups, the trend showed superior PES in IIP 10(2.0) compared to DIP 9(2.0). This could be attributed to the barrier created for grafting material by the provisionalization performed in 24 h in the SST and IIP groups which also facilitates junctional epithelium formation35 and maintains proximal contact, thus preserving the position and height of the papillae.2,36

In the SST group, PES scores with a median IQR value of 12 (2.0) were similar to that with other studies where the authors reported an average value of 12.12±0.64,24 12.2 ± 0.837,28 11.1 ± 0.7337 and 12.15±0.8725 at the end of one year. It is also similar to that of the studies wherein the authors reported an average score of 12.3±0.86,25 12 (11–14)4 and 12.15 ± 0.76,23 six months post-operatively, respectively, and with that of another observational study that revealed a mean PES of 12.26 The results of this study were different from that of a study22 that reported a PES score of 13.5, this difference could be because of the lesser number of patients treated in their study.

The PES score of 11.2±2.1, 6 months after implant placement in the IIP group concurred to that of other studies that revealed PES value of 12.4230 and 12.38.32 The PES values also concurred with studies which reported average scores of 11.86 ±0.35,24 10.8 ± 0.837,28 10.2 ± 0.4237 and 10.3 ± 1.5925 at the end of one year respectively.

The success of implant therapy is highly dependent on the patient’s acceptance of treatment in terms of aesthetics and function. Hence, patient-reported outcome measures (PROMs) are now considered the fundamental measure to evaluate therapeutic success.38 Hence, VAS was used to evaluate aesthetic satisfaction and pain perception in the present study. However, the existing literature has shown only a few studies, especially in the maxillary aesthetic region where PROMs were evaluated. Six months after implant placement, there was a significant difference in VAS scores between the SST and DIP groups, but there was no difference between the SST, IIP and DIP groups.

One year after implant placement, there was a significant difference between the SST and IIP groups, as well as between the SST and DIP groups. The results clearly demonstrated better esthetic results in the SST group compared to other groups. This could be attributed to maintenance of intact periodontal ligament which facilitates minimal changes in hard and soft tissues as well as leading to adequate width of keratinised tissue by facilitating the preservation of the dentogingival fibres.23,39 Additionally, the use of Morse tapered implants40 with greater abutment heights could lead to less marginal bone level changes.41 There was no statistically significant difference in VAS scores for aesthetics between the IIP and DIP groups. The trend showed better aesthetic satisfaction in IIP group 9(1.50) than in DIP group 8(2.0). The immediate restoration could be attributed to increased final PES and patient satisfaction in IIP group.42

The VAS score for pain was assessed at the first post-external operative control, 7–10 days after surgery to 6 months, and 1 year after implant placement. There was no significant difference in the VAS for pain between the groups at any time point, except that a significant difference in the VAS between the SST and DIP groups was noticed at 6 months, which could be due to more surgical interventions in the DIP group than in the SST group.

Conclusion

SST with IIP could be considered in the maxillary esthetic zone involving single dental implants as results of the study demonstrated by lesser alterations in CBT, PES and PROMS. However, future studies with extended follow-up time and PROMs evaluation with a more validated questionnaire such as OHIP −14 should be conducted to arrive at a definitive indication for the SST technique in the maxillary aesthetic region.

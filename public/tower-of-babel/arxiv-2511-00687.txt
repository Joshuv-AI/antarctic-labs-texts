Electromagnetic deflection effects in the integrated luminosity measurement at the CEPC
Ivan Smiljanić, Ivanka Božović, Ivana Vidaković, Nataša Vukašinović, Goran Kačarević

Abstract
In order to ensure measurement of the integrated luminosity with a relative precision of 10 − 4 \mathrm{10^{-4}} at the Z 0 \mathrm{Z^{0}} pole at CEPC, numerous systematic effects have to be quantified and, if possible, corrected for. Here we discuss the impact of electromagnetic fields of incoming bunches on the initial state electrons and positrons (EMD1) as well as on the Bhabha scattering final states (EMD2). Both effects change four-momenta of the final state particles, leading to a modification of the Bhabha count in the luminometer. These effects are quantified in simulation, together with their stability with respect to the beam parameters variations. Possible correction methods based on experimental measurements with the CEPC detector are discussed on a conceptual level.

Introduction

In general, beam bunches at high energy
e^{+}e^{-}
colliders are densely packed, in order to ensure sufficient instantaneous luminosity and consequently sufficient statistics of processes of interest for realization of physics programs with these machines. In this respect, the proposed beam parameters of the CEPC Conceptual Design Report
[
1

have been updated from
\mathrm{8\cdot 10^{10}}
to
\mathrm{14\cdot 10^{10}}

\mathrm{Z^{0}}
, resulting in even stronger electromagnetic fields
[
2
]
. These fields will impact the initial states in the bunches of opposite charges. This effect, labeled as EMD1, is discussed in Section
3
, with the CEPC post-CDR beams
[
2
]
. It is clear that a change of four-momentum of the system of colliding particles will affect the final state electrons and positrons produced in low-angle Bhabha scattering (LABS), modifying their count in the luminometer with respect to the one in the absence of this effect. In a similar manner, the electron and positron produced in LABS will be deflected by the fields of the opposite-charge bunches (effect EMD2), which is discussed in Section
4

[
3
]
, however, they can be estimated from simulation. These estimates are given in Sections
3

4

5
we overview possibilities to correct for these effects on the basis of measurements with the CEPC detector
[
4
]
.

Electromagnetic deflection of the initial state

Electromagnetic interaction of initial electron and positron with the bunches of opposite charges is illustrated in Fig.
1
. Initial particles will be attracted toward z-axis in the laboratory frame, resulting in the effective reduction of the crossing-angle
\alpha

Illustration of the EMD1 effect on initial state in the laboratory frame. Colliding bunches of electrons (positrons) are indicated in blue (red).

As a result, four-momenta of the final state particles produced in LABS will be modified and consequently their count in the luminometer will change. We have simulated this effect using the GuineaPig C++ V.1.2.2 software
[
5
]
, generating around
6\cdot 10^{5}
electron-positron interacting pairs. As can be seen from Fig.
1
, the system of colliding particles is receiving a momentum kick in the negative direction of the laboratory x-axis. The mean of this kick is found to be
\sim
5.8 MeV, or
\sim
2.9 MeV per initial state particle in average. This is illustrated in Fig.
2
.

Figure 2
:
Illustration of the change of momentum of colliding electron-positron system along the x-axis in the laboratory frame.

The EMD1 effect is restricted to the x-z plane and no bias is produced in the y-component of momentum of the colliding system. The crossing angle is in average reduced for
\sim
140
\mu
rad, that is 70
\mu
rad per beam. This is illustrated in Fig.
3
.

Figure 3

Illustration of the effective reduction of the crossing angle per beam due to EMD1.

In addition, the change of the crossing angle (
\Delta\alpha
) of the interacting particles will modify components of their momenta and consequently their energies as:
\mathrm{\Delta E\sim E\cdot tg(\alpha/2)\cdot\Delta\alpha}
. Beam energy change is found to be
\mathrm{\Delta E\sim}
52 keV per CEPC beam in average. The EMD1 effect will also produce consequent modifications of the Bhabha final state four-momenta. Using the BHLUMI V4.04
[
6
]
generator, we have produced
\mathrm{\sim 6\cdot 10^{5}}
LABS events that we have associated with the initial states modified by EMD1. Association here means modification of Bhabha electron and positron four momenta through boost and rotation, performed on event-by-event basis in a way that a given initial system of colliding particles is becoming a center-of-mass frame of the considered final state electron and positron. Assumed position of the luminometer along the s-axis is preserving the geometry of the head-on collisions, so no additional rotation due to the crossing-angle is performed. The EMD1 effect results in smearing of the polar angle of the final state particles
\mathrm{\Delta\theta_{BH}}
with RMS of
\sim
83
\mu
rad and without biasing the mean, what is illustrated in Fig.
4
. Maximal
\mathrm{\Delta\theta_{BH}}
occurs for Bhabha events emitted along the x-axis. Deviation in azimuthal angles of the Bhabha final states are maximal (
\mathrm{\Delta\phi_{0}\sim}
1 mrad) for LABS events emitted along y-axis, what is just a geometrical consequence of the initial state momentum shift along the x-axis and it is irrelevant for the luminosity measurement (unless event selection explicitly relies on Bhabha azimuthal position). The latter is illustrated in Fig.
5
.

:
Illustration of the smearing in polar angles (
\mathrm{\Delta\theta_{BH}}
) of the Bhabha final states due to EMD1, without biasing the mean.

:

Although the EMD1 does not bias polar angles of final state electrons and positrons, it will result in modification of the angle between them, influencing their back-to-back propagation. This change of angle between final state electrons and positrons (
\mathrm{\Delta\theta_{acc}}
) is in average
\sim
170
\mu
rad (Fig.
6
) and it will affect LABS count in the luminometer due to the loss of collinearity, in particular at the luminometer inner aperture (smallest polar angle) where the effect is also the strongest due to vicinity of opposite-charge bunches. The relative loss of count is found to be
\mathrm{\sim 4\cdot 10^{-3}}
what is
\sim
40 times larger than the integrated luminosity (
\mathcal{L}_{int}
) precision goal of
\mathrm{10^{-4}}
in relative uncertainty. This loss can be taken as correction to the measured integrated luminosity. Considering ± 10% variations of the nominal CEPC bunch parameters from Table 1, changes of LABS count are given in Fig.
7
. Relative deviations of count due to beam-parameter variations are not larger than
\mathrm{2\cdot 10^{-4}}
, meaning that if the correction to Bhabha count can be established in simulation for the nominal beam parameters, deviations induced by the bunch parameters variations up to 10% from the nominal values will contribute to the relative systematic uncertainty of the integrated luminosity at most as
\mathrm{5\cdot 10^{-4}}
. The EMD1 effect is tme most sensitive to variations of the bunch length (
\sigma_{z}
). This estimate assumes coincidental counting performed in the same polar angle regions in the left and right arms of the luminometer. If, however, if one would count in a left-right asymmetric way, assuming (55-77) mrad counting angles in one half of the luminometer and (53-79) mrad in the other, subsequently applied to the left and right arm of the detector, the counting loss from EMD1 will be compensated to
\mathrm{\Delta\mathcal{L}_{int}/\mathcal{L}_{int}\sim 6\cdot 10^{-5}}
with the nominal CEPC beams, since this way of counting helps to cancel out left-right symmetry between detector halves. However, for machines with the crossing-angle, it is only applicable for a detector placed at the outgoing beams (s-axis).

Electromagnetic deflection of the final state

Final state electron and positron produced in the Bhabha scattering will also be influenced by electromagnetic fields of the incoming bunches of opposite charges. This effect, labeled as EMD2, will rise with decreasing center-of-mass energy as it has been discussed in
[
7
]
, thus being of relevance at the
\mathrm{Z^{0}}
resonance. Bhabha electrons and positrons are generated slightly above the phase space of the luminometer fiducial volume (to allow particles to be focused towards luminometer fiducial volume) and have been tracked subsequently in the fields of the opposite-charge bunches using the Guinea Pig C++ V.1.2.2 software employed to simulate the EMD1 effect. As can be seen from Fig.
8
, the EMD2 induced change in Bhabha collinearity (deviation in angle between LABS electron and positron with respect to its value prior to the effect) is found to be
\sim
43
\mu
rad in average. Change in the angle between final state electron and positron is correlated to the focusing of these particles towards the incoming beams. We however chose to give here the change of collinearity as it can be possibly exploit further, be experimentally measured and serve to calibrate the luminosity losses.

5
Experimental corrections

The correction to the loss of count induced by the EMD1 (Fig.
7
) depends on the precision the crossing angle is known. The crossing angle at CEPC can be precisely determined from the central processes like the s-channel di-muon production. Kinematics of di-muon final states will be precisely determined in the central tracker where momenta of charged high-energy leptons would be measured with the resolution of
\mathrm{\Delta p_{t}/p_{t}^{2}\sim 10^{-5}\>GeV^{-1}}
. Considering
\mathrm{\sim 10^{4}}
di-muon events generated at the
\mathrm{Z^{0}}
pole with WHIZARD V2.8.3 event generator
[
8
]
and assuming muons’ momenta smearing of order of
\mathrm{\sim 10^{-5}\>GeV^{-1}}
, the crossing angle can be measured with the standard error of
\mathrm{\sim 260}
mrad/
\mathrm{\sqrt{N_{\mu\mu}}}
in
\sim
10 minutes of the CEPC run at the
\mathrm{Z^{0}}
pole. In other words, crossing angle precision of order of
\sim
1
\mu
rad can be achieved with as little as 70
\mathrm{pb^{-1}}
of integrated luminosity. This is illustrated in Fig.
12
.

Figure 12
:
Crossing angle determination from di-muon production at the
\mathrm{Z^{0}}
pole, in
\sim
70
\mathrm{pb^{-1}}
of integrated luminosity.

There are ongoing studies to establish experimental correction of the luminosity losses induced by the EMD2 effect, like the accolinearity of the LABS final states that should be presumably known at the order of several
\mu
rad with the Si-wafer placed in front of the luminometer. Such studies should include eventual changes of the luminometer fiducial volume towards smaller polar angles where effects of electromagnetic deflection will grow stronger. Also, radiative processes like the Initial State Radiation (ISR) and Final State Radiation (FSR) will disturb collinearity of LABS final states leading to counting losses at the luminometer’s edges. ISR emission will result in softer LABS final states that will be more strongly focused by the EMD2. However, the loss of count induced by ISR is not expected to be larger than
\sim 10^{-4}

[
3
]
with respect to the count loss in the absence of this effect. Momentum loss induced by FSR should be recovered by the clustering algorithm since the FSR photons will be emitted in a narrow cone around the LABS final states.

6
Conclusion

This is the first estimate of electromagnetic deflection effects at the CEPC
\mathrm{Z^{0}}
pole. The effects of electromagnetic deflection of initial (EMD1) and final Bhabha states (EMD2) are quantified in simulation with the nominal post-CDR CEPC beams. These effects contribute to the loss of collinearity of Bhabha events that will be coincidently counted in left and right arms of the luminometer. Both effects will be simultaneously present and if uncorrected they will cause the relative loss of count in the luminometer of
\mathrm{\Delta\mathcal{L}_{int}/\mathcal{L}_{int}\sim 6\cdot 10^{-3}}
.
Electromagnetic deflection of initial electrons and positrons towards bunches of the opposite charges will lead to reduction of the crossing angle of
\sim
140 mrad. Measurement of the crossing angle with
\sim

\mu
rad precision will be possible with the central-tracker reconstruction of di-muon production. From simulation, relative correction of the integrated luminosity can be determined once the crossing angle is known. The correction is of order of
\mathrm{4\cdot 10^{-3}}
. It is stable with respect to the bunch parameters variations within ± 10% from the nominal values, with variations of count not larger than
\mathrm{5\cdot 10^{-4}}
that translates into to the systematic uncertainty of the integrated luminosity.
The correction induced by the EMD2 effect is of order of
\mathrm{1.4\cdot 10^{-3}}
. Variations of the nominal bunch sizes and population not larger than 10% introduce relative uncertainty of the integrated luminosity correction not larger than
\mathrm{3\cdot 10^{-4}}
. There are ongoing studies by the same authors to establish experimental correction method for the loss of count induced by electromagnetic deflection at CEPC.

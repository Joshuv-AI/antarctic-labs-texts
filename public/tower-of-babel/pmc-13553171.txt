Authors: Da Ae Kim, Sun Min Lee, Kyunghwa Sun, Eun-Joo Kim, Hyun Sook Kim, Soo Hyun Cho, YeShin Kim, Sung Min Kang, Ji Woo Jung, Su Gyeong Kim, Hang Rai Kim, So Young Moon

ABSTRACT
Background and PurposeThe SoUth Korean study to PrEvent cognitive impaiRment and protect BRAIN health through lifestyle intervention (SUPERBRAIN) showed that a multidomain lifestyle intervention targeting vascular risk, cognitive training, physical activity, nutrition, and motivation is feasible in older adults. However, its resource-intensive delivery limited scalability, and conventional aerobic exercise alone may not sufficiently support long-term adherence, cognitive-motor engagement, and cultural acceptability in older adults with mild cognitive impairment (MCI). To address these gaps, we developed the SoUth Korean study to PrEvent cognitive impaiRment and protect BRAIN health via Behavioral, Optimized, Organized Movement therapy (SUPERBRAIN-BOOM) in MCI, a mobile-based multidomain program integrating dance-based movement, nutrition education, and meditation.MethodsThis multicenter randomized feasibility trial will enroll 45 adults aged 50–85 years with MCI and at least one modifiable dementia risk factor. Participants will be randomized (1:1:1) to a tablet-based multidomain intervention, a smartphone-based multidomain intervention, or a control group receiving no intervention. The 12-week program consists of daily 30-minute home-based sessions (physical activity 20 minutes, nutrition education 5 minutes, meditation 5 minutes) delivered over four 3-week cycles, with assessments at weeks 3, 6, and 9 to adjust difficulty. The primary outcomes focus on feasibility (recruitment, retention, adherence ≥70%, and data completeness), and secondary outcomes include exploratory measures of cognition, physical function, vascular risk, nutrition, mood, quality of life, and motivation.ResultsRecruitment and data collection are ongoing.ConclusionsThis feasibility trial will inform the design of a future randomized trial evaluating a scalable, personalized mobile multidomain intervention for dementia prevention.

INTRODUCTION

Dementia is a growing global public health concern, with approximately 57.4 million individuals affected in 2019 and projections estimating 78 million cases by 2030.1 In the absence of curative treatments, preventive strategies targeting modifiable risk factors, such as physical and cognitive inactivity, vascular risk, poor nutrition, and low motivation, have become critical.23 Multidomain lifestyle interventions addressing these risk factors simultaneously have shown promise in preserving cognitive function.4

The Finnish Geriatric Intervention Study to Prevent Cognitive Impairment and Disability (FINGER) trial demonstrated that a comprehensive lifestyle program integrating physical activity, cognitive training, nutritional counseling, and vascular risk management attenuated cognitive decline compared with standard care.4 Building on the FINGER trial, many countries have culturally adapted multidomain interventions for dementia prevention, but scalable, sustainable, and personalized delivery models for real-world use remain challenging. In South Korea, our team developed the SoUth Korean study to PrEvent cognitive impaiRment and protect BRAIN health through lifestyle intervention (SUPERBRAIN), a multidomain program targeting physical activity, cognitive training, nutrition, vascular risk management, and motivation. Preliminary studies demonstrated its feasibility and cognitive benefits.567 However, delivering intervention components as separate modules required extensive resources, limited individualization, and constrained scalability. Similar challenges have been reported in other FINGER-based trials, including MAPT (Multidomain Alzheimer Preventive Trial) and preDIVA (prevention of dementia by intensive vascular care), which did not demonstrate significant cognitive benefits in their primary outcomes, underscoring the need to optimize intervention design and delivery for real-world effectiveness.89

To address these limitations, we developed the SoUth Korean study to PrEvent cognitive impaiRment and protect BRAIN health via Behavioral, Optimized, Organized Movement therapy (SUPERBRAIN-BOOM), a next-generation, app-based multidomain intervention. SUPERBRAIN-BOOM integrates dance-based physical activity, nutrition education, and mindfulness meditation into a single, structured 30-minute daily session to promote combined cognitive and physical engagement.1011121314 Dance-based movement engages memory, rhythm, and motor coordination, while mindfulness meditation supports attentional regulation and emotional control, processes that have been linked to improved cognitive outcomes.1011121314

A personalization algorithm tailors intervention content according to individual risk profiles, behavioral patterns, and functional capacity, aiming to enhance engagement and contextual relevance. This paper presents the protocol for a multicenter feasibility randomized controlled trial (RCT) evaluating SUPERBRAIN-BOOM in older adults with mild cognitive impairment (MCI), with feasibility outcomes as the primary objective and exploratory cognitive and functional outcomes to inform the design of a future large-scale RCT.

METHODS

Study design

This study is designed as a 12-week, multicenter, parallel-group, randomized controlled pilot trial conducted at five clinical sites in South Korea: Ajou University Hospital (Suwon), Pusan National University Hospital (Busan), Bundang CHA General Hospital (Seongnam), Chonnam National University Hospital (Gwangju), and Kangwon National University Hospital (Chuncheon). A total of 45 participants will be randomly assigned in a 1:1:1 ratio to a control group, a tablet-based intervention group, or a smartphone-based intervention group. The tablet- and smartphone-based arms are included to assess feasibility, acceptability, and usability across commonly used digital platforms in older adults, rather than to compare intervention efficacy between devices. Given the small sample size, this pilot trial is not powered for between-device comparisons, but it is intended to inform platform selection and optimization for future large-scale RCTs.

The primary aim of this pilot trial is to evaluate the feasibility, acceptability, and preliminary effects of a personalized multidomain intervention. The trial will adhere to the Standard Protocol Items: Recommendations for Interventional Trials (SPIRIT) guidelines and the World Health Organization Trial Registration Data Set. The study protocol has been reviewed and approved by the Institutional Review Board (IRB) of Ajou University Hospital (IRB No. AJOUIRB-DE-2025-366), and the trial has been registered with the Clinical Research Information Service (KCT0010863) and ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT07101380).

Participants

We will recruit 45 patients aged 50 to 85 years from outpatient memory clinics at five participating clinical sites in South Korea. All participants are required to meet the core clinical criteria for MCI as defined by the National Institute on Aging–Alzheimer’s Association (NIA-AA) and must have at least one modifiable dementia risk factor. Eligible individuals should be capable of using a smartphone or tablet application either independently or with caregiver assistance. Written informed consent will be obtained from all participants prior to enrollment. Detailed inclusion and exclusion criteria are presented in Table 1.

Randomization and allocation

Participants will be randomly assigned in a 1:1:1 ratio to a tablet-based intervention, a smartphone-based intervention, or the control group. Randomization will be stratified by study site using permuted blocks to ensure balanced allocation across groups. The randomization sequence will be generated by an independent statistician not involved in recruitment or outcome assessment. Allocation concealment will be maintained for investigators and outcome assessors. Due to the nature of the app-based intervention, participant blinding will not be feasible; however, all outcome assessments will be conducted by trained raters blinded to group allocation. Unblinding will be permitted only in the event of medical emergencies.

Intervention

The intervention follows the SUPERBRAIN-BOOM protocol and includes a 4-week screening period followed by a 12-week intervention, organized into four 3-week cycles. Each cycle integrates three components: choreographed physical activity, nutrition education, and mindfulness meditation. Clinical assessments will be performed at baseline (week 0) and post-intervention (week 12) (Fig. 1).

Participants in the intervention groups will use a mobile-based investigational device (model: SBB-U100, version: v0.1.X; Rowan Inc.) compatible with Android OS 12 or higher. At the time of this study, the SBB-U100 device and associated software were investigational, not commercially available, and used exclusively for research purposes. Based on baseline cognitive, physical, and metabolic assessments, a built-in stratification algorithm will be used to initialize individualized starting levels for the intervention. Baseline physical performance informs initial exercise intensity and movement complexity, while baseline cognitive performance guides the initial cognitive-motor challenge level. Intervention progression will be evaluated at the end of each 3-week cycle (weeks 3, 6, and 9) and dynamically adjusted based on cycle-level performance metrics, as described in detail in the Evaluation of Performance section. Participants will select dance genres to enhance engagement (e.g., K-pop, Aerobic, Korean dance, or Latin dance). Certified instructors—including exercise specialists, registered dietitians, and mindfulness trainers—will deliver the sessions after completing standardized training. All content will be delivered through a patient-facing mobile application (Supplementary Table 1 in the online-only Data Supplement) and monitored via a clinician-facing web dashboard (model: SBB-A100, optimized for the Chrome browser on Windows 10+; Rowan Inc.) (Supplementary Table 2 in the online-only Data Supplement). Both platforms are research-use systems developed for this feasibility trial and are not intended for commercial deployment at this stage. The control group will receive dementia prevention lifestyle guidelines and will continue to receive usual care. Participation in other structured interventions during the trial will be prohibited. Concomitant medical care is allowed unless it interferes with trial procedures.

Intervention discontinuation will be allowed in cases of adverse events, participant withdrawal, or investigator decision, and will be documented accordingly. To support adherence, the app includes personalized content, dance selection, reminders, and feedback. Adherence will be monitored via app usage logs, staff attendance records, and weekly clinician dashboard reviews.

Program level assignment algorithm and reassessment

Each participant’s initial intervention level will be determined using a multistep stratification algorithm incorporating physical fitness, vascular risk factors, and cognitive function (Fig. 2). Default assignment will be based on performance in three field tests—Timed Up and Go, Sit-to-Stand, and the 2-minute step test—interpreted using age- and sex-specific norms from the National Fitness 100 dataset.

This default level will then be adjusted based on vascular risk factors (e.g., chronic disease, alcohol use, smoking) and cognitive status, particularly the Clinical Dementia Rating–Sum of Boxes (CDR-SB). CDR-SB will be used at baseline to inform initial level assignment. Participants exhibiting domain-specific cognitive impairment (z-score ≤-1.5 on the Seoul Neuropsychological Screening Battery) will be assigned to targeted cognitive enhancement modules tailored to their deficits: memory (verbal cue-based movement recall), visuospatial (directional and amplitude variations), and executive function (dual-task set-shifting). Progression through the program will be evaluated at the end of each 3-week cycle (weeks 3, 6, and 9). During the first cycle, participant adherence (≥70% participation, with ≥5% motion visibility during exercise) and accuracy (evaluated via a dynamic time warping algorithm comparing joint positions to reference movements) will be assessed. Feedback will be categorized automatically as poor, good, or great.

Reassessment of physical function at the end of each cycle will determine whether the program level should be adjusted. If no level change is indicated, a different dance genre within the same complexity level will be introduced to sustain motivation. This adaptive reassignment strategy ensures that intervention intensity and content remain aligned with each participant’s functional and cognitive trajectory throughout the 12-week period.

Program scheduling and weekly structure

Each 3-week cycle of the SUPERBRAIN-BOOM intervention will follow a structured sequence of motor learning, review, and performance evaluation. The choreography for each cycle will be distributed over 8 days of structured practice, with integrated review sessions and cognitive stimulation as appropriate. In Week 1, participants will learn the first half of the designated dance choreography over four consecutive days (Days 1–4), focusing on foundational movement acquisition (Movements 1-1 to 1-4). This will be followed by review sessions on Days 5–7 to reinforce motor learning through repetition. In Week 2, the second half of the choreography (Movements 2-1 to 2-4) will be introduced and practiced in the same format (Days 1–4), followed again by review sessions (Days 5–7). This progressive structure is intended to facilitate incremental acquisition and consolidation of new motor sequences. Week 3 will be dedicated to full-sequence rehearsal and performance evaluation. Participants assigned to the cognitive enhancement modules will additionally engage in cognitive-motor integration sessions. Specifically, Days 1–2 will incorporate Week 1 choreography review with domain-specific cognitive tasks (targeting memory, visuospatial, or frontal/executive function), and Days 3–4 will apply the same approach to Week 2 choreography. Days 5–6 will be reserved for comprehensive sequence review, and Day 7 will involve a standardized performance test assessing movement accuracy and adherence.

This progressive scheduling framework is designed to enable systematic layering of motor skills and cognitive tasks, supporting both skill acquisition and participant engagement. Performance results from Day 21 will be used to determine program level reassignment for the subsequent cycle, allowing the program to remain adaptive to individual progress.

Choreographic structured physical intervention

The physical activity component of the intervention comprises choreographed dance-based sessions conducted five times per week over a 12-week period. Each session lasts approximately 30 minutes and is divided into four structured segments: a 5-minute warm-up, 10 minutes of choreographed dance activity, a 5-minute cool-down, and a 10-minute segment integrating nutrition education and mindfulness meditation.

Participants may select one of four dance genres—K-pop, Aerobic, Latin dance, or Korean traditional dance—based on individual preference and cultural familiarity. All routines are choreographed using a standardized 4/4 musical structure composed of 32-beat sequences (8 counts×4). The complexity of routines is stratified into three levels: Level 1 includes one movement per 8-count; Level 2 includes one to two movements; and Level 3 includes two or more movements per 8-count. To accommodate varying physical capacities, dance routines are also categorized by physical intensity: Level A for low to moderate impact movements and Level B for high-impact movements involving greater joint loading.

Exercise intensity is regulated through beats per minute (BPM) to align with each session segment: warm-up (<100 BPM), main activity (101–120 BPM), and cool-down (<90 BPM). Target heart rate zones are individually calculated using the formula 220 - age, with moderate intensity defined as 50%–70% of HRmax and high intensity as 70%–85% of HRmax. BPM levels are standardized across routines to ensure consistent physiological demand.

To enhance accessibility and engagement, all choreography is linguistically adapted with Korean translations of step names, and weekly dance sheets are pre-structured by level to support motor learning. Cognitive stimulation elements are embedded within the physical routines: memory is engaged by removing visual demonstrations and relying on verbal cueing; visuospatial skills are challenged through directional changes and movement amplitude variations; and executive function is stimulated through dual-task set-shifting involving coordinated limb movements.

This choreographic model is designed to simultaneously activate physical and cognitive domains in a culturally relevant and engaging format, enabling multimodal training tailored to diverse functional levels.

Nutrition and mindfulness components

The final 10 minutes of each intervention session will consist of two 5-minute segments: nutrition education and guided mindfulness meditation. This combined component is designed to promote behavioral awareness and reinforce lifestyle habits that support dementia prevention. Nutrition education will be delivered through short app-based video modules focusing on daily health practices, including hydration, nutrient intake, and mindful eating. Each session will present a specific topic, such as healthy snacking, calcium intake, or dietary management of age-related diseases. Table 2 outlines the 10 core nutrition topics and associated mindfulness keywords used in the meditation segments. These keywords serve as thematic prompts to link dietary information to embodied awareness during meditation.

Mindfulness sessions will be conducted with minimal verbal guidance, using a breath-centered approach to induce physical and mental relaxation. Each meditation session will last approximately 5 minutes. The initial 1 minute will guide participants to reflect on the nutrition content of that day’s lesson, while the remainder of the session will focus on body scanning and deep breathing techniques to enhance relaxation and present-moment attention. Overall, this integrated structure is intended to foster habit formation and cognitive reinforcement through repeated association of physical, nutritional, and mental health behaviors. The synchronized delivery of diet-related content and mindfulness training aims to promote long-term engagement and facilitate sustainable lifestyle change.

Evaluation of performance

Session performance is evaluated using two objective metrics derived from real-time pose estimation via the mobile application: adherence and accuracy. Adherence reflects the proportion of active physical movement during exercise sessions, while accuracy reflects the degree of similarity between participant movements and standardized instructor movements. A predefined adherence threshold is applied to determine successful completion at the individual session level. At the end of each 3-week cycle, cumulative session-level adherence and accuracy metrics, together with a standardized cycle-end performance evaluation embedded within the program, are used to determine whether the cycle has been successfully completed. Participants who meet both predefined adherence and accuracy criteria at the cycle level are eligible for progression. Depending on performance, progression may involve advancement to higher movement complexity, adjustment of exercise intensity, or reassignment to subsequent intervention modules, according to prespecified criteria. Participants who do not meet adherence or accuracy criteria are not withdrawn from the intervention. Instead, they remain at the same level during the subsequent cycle, with repeated or modified content designed to support motor learning, skill acquisition, and safe participation. Cycles that are not successfully completed are recorded and analyzed as part of feasibility outcomes, including adherence patterns, cycle completion rates, and progression trajectories.

Feasibility outcomes and exploratory efficacy outcomes

The primary outcomes of this feasibility trial are feasibility indicators, including study retention and intervention adherence. Study retention is defined as completion of the 12-week intervention and the post-intervention assessment. Compliance is defined as achieving an average adherence rate of at least 70% throughout the intervention period. Adherence is calculated as the proportion of time with detectable physical movement during the 20-minute dance component of each session, using a predefined movement threshold to distinguish active participation from noise in pose-estimation data. Participants who do not reach the 70% threshold will still be assigned continuous adherence scores to capture variability in engagement.

Exploratory efficacy outcomes include changes in cognitive performance, assessed using the 14-item cognitive subscale of the Alzheimer’s Disease Assessment Scale (ADAS-Cog14). This instrument evaluates multiple cognitive domains, with total scores ranging from 0 to 90, with higher scores indicating greater impairment. Cognitive assessments will be conducted at baseline and after the 12-week intervention.

Secondary exploratory outcomes include multidimensional measures of cognitive function, physical performance, quality of life, nutrition, vascular risk, psychological status, sleep, satisfaction, motivation, digital literacy, and user experience. These outcomes are intentionally broad to characterize domains relevant to intervention engagement and to inform outcome selection, variability estimates, and prioritization for a future fully powered RCT, rather than to test definitive efficacy. Within the cognitive domain, global measures such as the Korean version of the Mini-Mental State Examination (K-MMSE) will be included for descriptive and contextual purposes only, given the short intervention period and their limited sensitivity to subtle change in MCI. Data collection, management, and monitoring

All outcomes will be assessed at baseline and post-intervention using validated instruments, including the ADAS-Cog14, K-MMSE-2, CDR-SB, K-IADL (Korean Instrumental Activities of Daily Living), QOL-AD (Quality of Life in Alzheimer’s Disease), and nutrition and mood scales. Assessors will be trained using standardized protocols, and data will be collected via electronic case report forms with built-in range and consistency checks. Adherence and performance data from the mobile application will be retained for all participants, including those who deviate from the intervention protocol. Study data will be stored in a secure, access-restricted database, and de-identified datasets and statistical codes may be shared with qualified researchers upon reasonable request and institutional approval.

Given the feasibility focus and minimal anticipated risk, no formal data monitoring committee will be established. Monitoring will be performed by an independent clinical research associate unaffiliated with the sponsor, in accordance with Good Clinical Practice and relevant Korean regulations. Safety risks and usability issues during unsupervised sessions will be monitored through app-based alerts, participant reports, and site-level reviews via the clinician-facing dashboard. Participants and caregivers will be instructed to report technical difficulties, discomfort, or adverse events through in-app reporting or direct contact with site staff. Identified issues will be reviewed by the study team, and appropriate actions, including content adjustment, temporary suspension, or clinical referral, will be taken as needed. No interim efficacy analyses or formal stopping rules are planned, and all adverse events will be documented and managed according to the study protocol.

Sample size justification

As noted above, this trial is designed as a feasibility trial to evaluate the operability and preliminary signals of a multidomain intervention, rather than as an implementation trial. Based on available resources, logistical considerations across five clinical sites, and the primary aim of testing the protocol’s operability, a total of 45 participants will be recruited. Although this sample will not provide sufficient statistical power to detect between-group differences in clinical outcomes, it is expected to be adequate for evaluating recruitment and retention rates, adherence, usability, and other feasibility indicators. In addition, the collected data will be used to estimate effect sizes and inform the design and sample size calculation of future large-scale RCTs.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses will follow the modified intention-to-treat principle, including all randomized participants who received at least one intervention and completed both baseline and post-intervention assessments. Sensitivity analyses will be performed using the per-protocol population, excluding major protocol violators.

Descriptive statistics will be used to summarize baseline characteristics. Continuous variables will be reported as means and standard deviations or medians and interquartile ranges, and categorical variables will be reported as frequencies and percentages. Between-group comparisons will be conducted using appropriate parametric or non-parametric tests, based on distributional assumptions.

The primary outcome (retention and adherence) will be summarized descriptively. Exploratory efficacy outcomes, including change in ADAS-Cog14 score, will be analyzed using a linear mixed-effects model, adjusting for group, time, group-by-time interaction, and baseline values. Secondary outcomes (e.g., cognitive, functional, physical, and metabolic measures) will be analyzed using analysis of covariance with baseline scores as covariates. Feasibility outcomes (e.g., retention, compliance, enrollment rates) will be summarized descriptively and compared between groups.

Safety data will include all participants who used the investigational device. The incidence of adverse events (treatment-emergent adverse events, adverse device effects, serious adverse events, and serious adverse device effects) will be summarized using descriptive statistics and compared between groups. Events will be coded using the Medical Dictionary for Regulatory Activities and reported by System Organ Class and Preferred Term.

Subgroup analyses will explore potential effect modifiers (e.g., APOE ε4 status, baseline characteristics, intervention adherence, dance preferences). Missing data will be addressed using multiple imputation under the assumption of missing at random, with sensitivity analyses to test robustness.

Ethics and dissemination

This study was approved by the Institutional Review Board of Ajou University Hospital (IRB No. AJOUIRB-DE-2025-366) and will be conducted in accordance with the International Council for Harmonisation Good Clinical Practice guidelines and relevant Korean regulations. Any significant protocol modifications will be submitted for IRB review and communicated to investigators and stakeholders before implementation. Written informed consent will be obtained from all participants prior to enrollment. Consent will include provisions for the collection, storage, and future use of biological specimens, in accordance with standardized protocols. A model consent form and participant materials are available upon request. Participant confidentiality will be maintained using coded identifiers, with access to personal information limited to authorized study personnel. No specific ancillary or post-trial care will be provided, as the study involves minimal risk.

Trial results will be disseminated via peer-reviewed publications, conference presentations, and trial registry updates. Authorship will follow the ICMJE guidelines. De-identified data and analysis code may be shared with qualified researchers upon reasonable request and institutional approval.

DISCUSSION

The SUPERBRAIN-BOOM study presents a culturally adapted, next-generation multidomain intervention for dementia prevention in older adults with MCI. Building on the original SUPERBRAIN program—modeled after the FINGER study, which targeted five modifiable risk domains through separate interventions—this study integrates key components into a single, mobile-based platform. While the original program showed effectiveness, it was limited by high resource demands, low scalability, and a lack of personalization.

To overcome these challenges, SUPERBRAIN-BOOM combines physical activity, nutrition education, and mindfulness meditation into a 30-minute daily session. This streamlined format is expected to improve feasibility by reducing logistical complexity and participant burden. It may also enhance adherence and real-world deliverability.

The intervention offers several key strengths. First, it employs a data-driven personalization algorithm that tailors content based on each participant’s risk profile, behavioral patterns, and functional capacity. This individualized approach is intended to support engagement and optimize intervention delivery rather than to directly demonstrate efficacy at this stage. Second, the use of dance-based movement provides multidimensional stimulation—activating memory, spatial awareness, motor coordination, and executive function—while also promoting enjoyment. Complementary mindfulness training targets attentional control and emotional regulation, which may contribute to cognitive and emotional well-being. Third, the integration of real-time motion tracking and pose estimation enables objective monitoring of adherence and movement accuracy, supporting feasibility assessment and quality assurance rather than clinical outcome evaluation. Importantly, this trial was not designed or powered to test definitive clinical efficacy. Accordingly, cognitive and other clinical outcomes were included as exploratory measures to inform outcome selection and trial design for a future fully powered RCT.

Overall, SUPERBRAIN-BOOM is designed to provide an accessible and engaging digital platform that may address key delivery-related limitations of prior multidomain interventions, rather than to establish definitive cognitive or physical benefits in this feasibility phase. As a feasibility study, this trial is not powered to detect clinical efficacy or long-term outcomes. Instead, it focuses on evaluating feasibility outcomes such as program acceptability, adherence, and the functionality of the algorithm-based personalization system. This exploratory design is appropriate for early-stage development of a novel digital intervention.

Several limitations should be explicitly acknowledged. First, the small sample size limits statistical power and generalizability. Second, the relatively short 12-week intervention period may be insufficient to observe meaningful changes in cognitive outcomes. Third, the three-arm design, while informative for feasibility and delivery mode comparison, introduces additional complexity and may further constrain statistical interpretation. These limitations are inherent to feasibility trials and should be considered when interpreting the findings. Despite these constraints, the results of this trial will inform the optimization of intervention components and delivery methods. Findings from this feasibility trial will guide refinement of the intervention protocol, selection of appropriate outcome measures, and estimation of sample size for a subsequent fully powered RCT. In the longer term, this personalized and culturally adapted digital multidomain intervention may represent a scalable and sustainable approach to dementia prevention, pending confirmation of efficacy in future studies.

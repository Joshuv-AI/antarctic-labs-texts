Role of NUDT3 in skeletal myogenesis and association of regulatory genetic variants with carcass traits in pigs
Authors: Ya-Long An, Yong-Qi Yue, Yang Li, Teng-Fei Zheng, Zi-Hao Ge, Yan-Ru Yue, Rong-Rong Ding, Ji-Ying Wang, Xiao Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractSkeletal muscle is central to locomotor function and metabolic regulation in humans and constitutes a primary source of high-quality protein in livestock production. While genome-wide association studies (GWAS) have identified a strong association between Nudix-type hydrolase 3 (NUDT3) and lean mass in both human (Homo sapiens) and pig (Sus scrofa) populations, its functional role in myogenesis remains unclear. In the present study, silencing of NUDT3 in porcine muscle satellite cells (PMSCs) markedly impaired proliferative capacity, as evidenced by G1 stage accumulation and reduction in EdU+ cells. Concomitantly, knockdown of NUDT3 significantly suppressed myogenic differentiation, while NUDT3 overexpression yielded the opposite effects. In vivo, overexpression of NUDT3 in tibialis anterior (TA) muscle of mice (Mus musculus) significantly increased the abundance of Pax7+ and eMyHC+ cells following cardiotoxin-induced injury, thereby promoting regenerative myogenesis. Transcriptomic profiling demonstrated that NUDT3 significantly attenuated NF-κB signaling in a Nudix motif-dependent manner, providing mechanistic insight into its pro-myogenic function. Integrative co-localization of GWAS and expression quantitative trait locus (eQTL) signals identified eight single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) significantly associated with NUDT3 expression in porcine muscle, including four variants located within evolutionarily conserved E6 and E7 enhancer elements supported by HiCuT profiling of H3K27ac and CRISPR interference assays. Among these, rs327612517 within E7 showed significant association with loin muscle area and depth in PigBiobank data and modulated E7 enhancer activity, as demonstrated by dual-luciferase reporter and electrophoretic mobility shift assays. Collectively, these findings establish NUDT3 as a positive regulator of skeletal myogenesis and identify rs327612517 as a functional variant with potential utility for genetic improvement of lean yield in pigs.Keywords: Pig, Myogenesis, NUDT3, Lean yield

INTRODUCTION

Human skeletal muscle constitutes approximately 40% of total body mass and contains 50%–75% of total body protein, thereby serving as a principal determinant of locomotion, posture, and whole-body metabolic homeostasis (Frontera & Ochala, 2015). In livestock species such as pigs, skeletal muscle deposition, commonly evaluated by loin muscle area (LMA) and loin muscle depth (LMDEP) (He et al., 2016), directly influences lean yield and represents an important source of high-quality dietary protein for human consumption (Zeng & Du, 2023). Enhancement of lean meat content has therefore become a central objective in pig breeding programs (Wang et al., 2015). While genome-wide association studies (GWAS) have identified numerous loci associated with LMA and LMDEP in recent years, the functional characterization of most identified variants remains limited (Xu et al., 2025), restricting development of robust molecular markers for precision breeding.

Skeletal muscle architecture is defined by multinucleated myofibers organized into fascicles, and overall muscle mass is determined by both myofiber number and cross-sectional area. Myofibers originate from myogenic progenitors that arise during embryogenesis and undergo tightly regulated differentiation programs (Schiaffino & Reggiani, 2011; Xu et al., 2025). In postnatal and adult muscle, muscle satellite cells represent a resident stem cell population that resides in a quiescent state under physiological conditions and becomes rapidly activated in response to injury, thereby contributing to myofiber regeneration and restoration of the stem cell pool (Sousa-Victor et al., 2022). Skeletal myogenesis represents a highly orchestrated developmental program controlled by central myogenic transcription factors (TFs), including MyoD and MyoG (myogenin) (Al Tanoury et al., 2020; Dumont et al., 2015), and is dynamically regulated by diverse signaling pathways that integrate proliferative and differentiation cues.

Nudix-type hydrolase 3 (NUDT3) is a nucleoside diphosphate linked moiety X (NUDIX) protein with established mRNA decapping activity (Grudzien-Nogalska et al., 2016). mRNA decapping is an essential step in post-transcriptional regulation and turnover, directly influencing transcript stability through modulation of the 5' m7G cap structure. Genetic variation in NUDT3 is strongly associated with age-related sarcopenia in human populations (Jin et al., 2022; Singh & Gasman, 2020), implicating this gene in muscle maintenance and functional decline. In pigs, NUDT3 resides within a variant-enriched region on chromosome 7 (SSC 7), which has been repeatedly linked to growth-related traits, including body height and body length in Large White×Min crossbred populations (Won et al., 2014) and Bamaxiang pigs (Gong et al., 2019). Previous studies have also demonstrated significant associations between candidate genes in this region and meat production and quality traits (Wang et al., 2022; Zeng et al., 2024; Zhang et al., 2014). High mobility group AT-hook 1 (HMGA1), located adjacent to NUDT3, has been shown to participate in myogenic regulation (Dey et al., 2022), and NUDT3 has been proposed as a strong candidate gene underlying the leading expression quantitative trait locus (eQTL) signal for lean mass in Duroc pigs (Bian et al., 2021; Zhuang et al., 2019). Furthermore, genotype-tissue expression (GTEx) analysis has shown that NUDT3 expression is relatively high in human, pig, and mouse skeletal muscle (GTEx database) (Teng et al., 2024; The GTEx Consortium atlas of genetic regulatory effects across human tissues, 2020), supporting a conserved functional role in muscle biology. Despite these genetic and transcriptomic associations, direct functional evidence linking NUDT3 to skeletal muscle development remains unclear.

In the present study, NUDT3 was established as a novel and positive regulator of skeletal myogenesis, shown to facilitate myogenic proliferation and differentiation in vitro and augment regenerative capacity in vivo. In addition, rs327612517 was identified as a candidate functional variant contributing to NUDT3-associated phenotypic variation in lean mass in pigs, highlighting its potential application as a molecular marker for genetic improvement of lean yield.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Animals

For the cardiotoxin (CTX)-induced muscle regeneration model, 24 male C57BL/6J mice (3 weeks of age) were purchased from the Laboratory Animal Center of Xi’an Jiaotong University and acclimated for 1 week prior to in vivo experiments. Mice were randomly divided into two groups, with four cages per group and three mice per cage. Intramuscular injections with adeno-associated virus serotype 9 empty vector (AAV9-empty) or AAV9 carrying the NUDT3 coding sequence (AAV9-NUDT3, Hanbio, China) were administered at multiple sites within tibialis anterior (TA) muscle. Four weeks later, CTX was delivered by multipoint injection into TA muscle. TA tissues were harvested at 0, 3, 5, and 14 days post-injection (dpi). Each sample was divided into two portions, one fixed in muscle tissue fixative solution and the other snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen for further analysis. Mice were maintained at 25℃ under a 12 h light/dark cycle (with lights at 0700h and off at 1900h) with free access to food and water.

Three-day-old Yorkshire piglets were transported from the experimental pig farm of Northwest A&F University (China) to research facilities for euthanasia and isolation of satellite cells for primary culture.

Genotypic data, phenotypic measurements (LMDEP adjusted to 100 kg body weight, Y_LMDEP_100), and NUDT3 expression levels in longissimus dorsi muscle were obtained from Yorkshire pigs (n=234) provided by the Shandong Academy of Agricultural Sciences (unpublished data).

Cell culture and transfection

Porcine muscle satellite cells (PMSCs) were isolated from longissimus dorsi muscle of 3–5-day-old piglets (Duroc×Landrace crossbred, n=3) as previously described (Dou et al., 2020). Approximately 10 g of dissected muscle tissue (1 mm3 fragments) was thoroughly rinsed in cold sterile phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) and digested at 37℃ with 0.1% type I collagenase (Sigma, V900891, USA) for 90 min, followed by digestion in 0.25% trypsin for an additional 30 min. The resulting suspension was sequentially filtered through 100, 70, and 40 μm cell strainers. PMSCs were purified by differential adhesion and cultured in Dulbecco's Modified Eagle Medium F12 (DMEM/F12) supplemented with 20% fetal bovine serum (FBS) and 1% penicillin/streptomycin in a 5% CO2 incubator at 37℃. Upon reaching approximately 90% confluence, myogenic differentiation was induced by replacing growth medium with differentiation medium consisting of high-glucose DMEM supplemented with 2% horse serum.

For proliferation assays, transfection was performed at 30%–40% confluence. For differentiation assays, transfection was conducted at 90%–95% confluence. Lipofectamine 2000 (Invitrogen, USA) was used for transfection according to the manufacturer’s instructions, and experiments were performed in triplicate unless otherwise indicated. Small interfering RNAs (siRNAs; si-Scramble and si-NUDT3) and pcDNA3.1 expression vectors (pcDNA-empty, pcDNA-NUDT3, and pcDNA-NUDT3-mut) were synthesized by Tsingke Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (China).

NF-κB was pharmacologically inhibited using 100 μmol/L pyrrolidine dithiocarbonate (PDTC; Sigma, USA), a cell-permeable inhibitor that blocks I-κB ubiquitination and subsequent NF-κB nuclear translocation.

5-Ethynyl-2′-deoxyuridine (EdU) treatment and detection

Cell proliferation was assessed 48 h after transfection using a Cell-Light 5-Ethynyl-2′-Deoxyuridine (EdU) Apollo 567 In Vitro Kit (C10310-1, RiboBio, China) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. PMSCs were incubated with EdU-A reagent at 37℃ for the indicated durations, and incorporated EdU was visualized following standard detection procedures. EdU+ cells in skeletal muscle sections were detected using a Cell-Light Apollo 488 Stain Kit (C10371-3, RiboBio, China).

Cell counting kit-8 assay

PMSCs were seeded in 96-well plates at appropriate densities for transfection. At 48 h after transfection, 10 μL of Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8) reagent was added to each well, followed by incubation at 37℃ for 3 h. Absorbance at 450 nm was measured using a microplate reader (BioTek, USA).

Flow cytometry

PMSCs cultured in 6-well plates were treated with 100 nmol/L dexamethasone for 2 h. Cells were subsequently transfected with pcDNA3.1(+) or pcDNA3.1(+)-NUDT3 plasmids for 24 h, digested with 0.25% trypsin, and neutralized in DMEM/F12 containing 10% FBS. Cells were collected, fixed in 70% cold ethanol, and stored overnight at 4℃. After fixation, cells were incubated in propidium iodide (PI) solution containing 50 µg/mL PI, 100 µg/mL RNase A, and 0.2% Triton X-100 in PBS for 30 min at 4℃ in the dark. Cell cycle distribution was analyzed using flow cytometry (Zimu, China). PI/RNase staining buffer (BD Pharmingen™, USA) was used where indicated. Data were processed using ModFit v.3.0.

Immunofluorescence staining

The TA muscle samples were fixed, preserved in paraffin, and sliced at 5 μm thickness. Sections were permeabilized with 0.3% Triton X-100 for 10 min and blocked with 5% bovine serum albumin (BSA) for 30 min. Primary antibodies targeting fast-MyHC (1:1 000, Sigma, USA), slow-MyHC (1:1 000, Sigma, USA), lamin (1:2 000, Boster Bio, China), and Pax7 (1:2 000, Santa Cruz Biotechnology, USA) were applied separately and incubated at room temperature (25℃) for 12 h. After washing with PBS, sections were incubated with goat anti-rabbit and/or anti-mouse IgG secondary antibodies at room temperature (25℃) for 50 min. Nuclei were identified by 4′,6-diamidino-2-phenylindole (DAPI, Sigma-Aldrich, USA) staining for 10 min. Images were acquired by immunofluorescence microscopy (Olympus, Japan).

For in vitro differentiation analysis, cells were cultured for 6 days under myogenic induction conditions, washed three times with PBS, fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde for 10 min, and permeabilized with 0.5% Triton X-100 for 15 min. After blocking in 5% BSA for 1 h at room temperature (25℃), cells were incubated with primary antibodies targeting fast- or slow-MyHC (1:1 000, Sigma, USA) for 1 h (25℃), rinsed with PBS, and stained with fluorescently labeled secondary antibodies (1:500, Boster Bio, China) for another 1 h (25℃). DAPI was applied to label nuclei. The fusion index was calculated as the proportion of nuclei incorporated into multinucleated myotubes relative to the total number of nuclei.

Quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (qPCR)

Total RNA was extracted from primary PMSCs using TRIzol reagent (Vazyme, China). RNA quality and concentration were assessed using a NanoDrop ND-1000 spectrophotometer (Thermo Fisher, USA). Samples with an OD260/OD280 between 1.8 and 2.0 were used to synthesize cDNA using Prime-Script RT Master Mix (Takara, China) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. qPCR was performed on a Bio-Rad CFX96 system using SYBR real-time PCR mix (BioTeke, USA) to measure mRNA levels. Thermal cycling conditions were as follows: initial denaturation at 95℃ for 5 min, 40 cycles at 95℃ for 10 s and 60℃ for 30 s. Primer sequences are listed in Supplementary Table S1. Relative mRNA expression levels were calculated using the 2‒∆∆Ct method, with β-actin used as the internal reference gene.

Western blot analysis

Protein expression was examined by immunoblotting, as described previously (Dou et al., 2020). Briefly, transfected cells were harvested, washed twice with PBS, and lysed in RIPA buffer containing 1% protease inhibitors. Protein concentration was determined using a bicinchoninic acid (BCA) protein concentration assay kit (Biosharp, China). Protein samples were isolated using 10% sodium dodecyl sulfate-polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS-PAGE) and transferred onto polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) membrane (Bio-Rad, USA). Membranes were incubated overnight at 4℃ with primary antibodies against NUDT3 (1:1 000, Proteintech, USA), Cyclin E (1:500, Santa Cruz Biotechnology, USA), p21 (1:2 000, Abways, China), MyHC (1:2 000, R&D Systems, USA), MyoG (1:2 000, Novus Biologicals, USA), MyoD (1:2 000, Novus Biologicals, USA), P65 (1:1 000, Immunoway, China), p-P65 (1:1 000, Abways, China), p-IKB-α (1:1 000, Abways, China), IKB-α (1:1 000, Abways, China), P50 (1:1 000, Immunoway, China), p-P50 (1:1 000, Immunoway, China), and β-actin (1:5 000, Abways, China). After washing, membranes were incubated with goat anti-rabbit IgG or goat anti-mouse IgG secondary antibodies (1:5 000, Boster Bio, China). Protein bands were visualized using appropriate detection reagents. Band intensities were quantified with ImageJ, and target protein bands were normalized to β-actin. Three independent biological replicates were performed for each experiment.

RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) and analysis

PMSCs were collected following NUDT3 knockdown or overexpression. Total RNA was extracted using TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen, USA). RNA purity and integrity were monitored using a NanoDrop 2000 spectrophotometer (NanoDrop Technologies, USA) and an Agilent 2100 Bioanalyzer (Agilent Technologies, USA). Libraries were constructed and sequenced using the Illumina HiSeq X Ten platform (paired-end 150 bp). Raw reads were quality-filtered using TrimGalore v.0.6.9 (-q 30 --phred33 --stringency 3 --length 20 -e 0.1), and clean reads were mapped to the Sus scrofa reference genome (Scrofa11.1) using STAR v.2.7.10b (https://github.com/alexdobin/STAR). Gene-level read counts were generated using featureCounts v.2.0.3 (Liao et al., 2014). Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were identified using the R package DESeq2 v.1.50 with thresholds of adjusted P-value (P-adj)<0.05 and fold change >1.5. Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway enrichment analyses were performed using the DAVID database. Publicly available RNA-seq data from skeletal muscle samples with high (H_LMA) or low (L_LMA) LMA values in a hybrid F2 Duroc×Pietrain pig population (PRJNA403969) were processed using the same analytical pipeline.

HiCuT-seq data access and analysis

Primary porcine myoblasts isolated from 3-day-old Duroc×Landrace piglets were induced to differentiate in medium containing 5% horse serum at approximately 90% confluence based on established protocols (An et al., 2025). Cells were subsequently used for HiCuT library construction and sequencing. HiCuT data targeting H3K27ac (CST, Rabbit mAb #8173, USA) were deposited in the NCBI Sequence Read Archive (SRA) under accession number PRJNA1183758. Genomic regions enriched for H3K27ac were defined as putative enhancer elements. Regions located within 2.5 kb of transcription start sites of protein-coding genes were annotated as promoters (Jiang et al., 2024; Perlman et al., 2024). H3K27ac HiCuT peaks that did not overlap with promoter regions were classified as enhancers.

Public data access and analysis

Linkage disequilibrium (LD) analysis: Whole-genome sequencing data were retrieved from the NCBI public database (PRJNA842867, Duroc×Landrace×Yorkshire hybrid pigs, Supplementary Table S2). Raw reads were quality-filtered using fastp v.0.20.0 (Chen, 2023) and aligned to the Scrofa11.1 reference genome using BWA v.0.7.17 (Jung & Han, 2022). Single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) and insertion-deletion variants (indels) were identified using GATK v.4.2. LD analysis was performed using LDBlockShow v1.39 (Dong et al., 2021).

Characterization of muscle regulatory elements: ATAC-seq and ChIP-seq datasets were obtained from the NCBI project PRJEB37735 (Pan et al., 2021). Raw sequencing reads were trimmed using TrimGalore v.0.6.9. Reads with mapping quality scores below 30, unmapped reads, reads with unmapped mates, nonprimary alignments, reads failing platform quality controls, and duplicate reads were removed using SAMtools v.1.9 (31) and Picard v.1.126 (https://broadinstitute.github.io/picard). Peak calling for each replicate was performed with MACS3 v.3.0.0b1 (33) (-g genome.size -q 0.05 --nomodel --shift 0 --extsize 200 --call-summits --keep-dup 1 -B --SPMR) in accordance with ENCODE ATAC-seq pipeline guidelines (https://github.com/kundajelab/atac_dnase_ pipelines). ChIP-seq peak identification followed the same strategy applied in HiCuT data processing.

Identification and analysis of cis-eQTLs for NUDT3 in muscle: GWAS data were obtained from the PigBiobank (https://pigbiobank.farmgtex.org/), including Duroc, Landrace, and Yorkshire breeds (Zeng et al., 2024). Transcriptome-wide association study (TWAS) data were downloaded from the PigGTEx (http://piggtex.farmgtex.org/) (Teng et al., 2024). GWAS summary statistics for traits associated with muscle growth and development, including LMA and LMDEP, were collected. Subsequently, all eQTLs associated with NUDT3 expression in muscle tissue were collected and intersected with GWAS signals using BedTools v.2.27.1 (Quinlan & Hall, 2010). HiCuT-derived H3K27ac chromatin interaction data in myoblasts were integrated to identify distal enhancer regions regulating NUDT3-associated eQTLs, and visualization was performed using R. Normalized phenotypic values for loin muscle area (M_LMA) and loin muscle depth (D_LMDEP, M_LMDEP, and S_LMDEP_100) at rs327612517 in PigBiobank were retrieved for statistical evaluation. Associations between genotype and phenotype were examined using parametric, nonparametric, robust, and Bayesian statistical approaches implemented in the ggstatsplot package. Genotype frequencies across breeds were obtained from the iAnimal (https://ianimal.pro/index) (Fu et al., 2023).

Analysis of PhastCons score and human public data access

Multiple genome alignments among humans, pigs, and mice were obtained from the UCSC database. Pairwise genome alignment files in MAF format were generated using phyloFit (-i MAF), and MAF files were partitioned using mafSplit. PhastCons was applied to estimate phastCons scores for each nucleotide and to define conserved blocks using the parameters: --target-coverage 0.25 --expected-length 12 --rho 0.4 (Ramani et al., 2019). Conserved regions were annotated with the ChIP-seeker package. Conserved blocks located within exonic or other coding regions were excluded, and elements residing in regulatory regions, including promoters, introns, and intergenic regions, were retained for further analysis. Sequence alignments were performed using Clustalw with default parameters (Li, 2003), and results were visualized in MEGA (Kumar et al., 2008). To prioritize functional cis-eQTLs of NUDT3 in human skeletal muscle, eQTLs and H3K27ac marker data were retrieved from the GTEx Portal (The GTEx Consortium atlas of genetic regulatory effects across human tissues, 2020). GWAS and phenome-wide association studies (PheWAS) data for relevant SNPs were retrieved from GeneATLAS (Canela-Xandri et al., 2018) and visualized using R.

TF binding prediction

The R package motifBreakR (Coetzee et al., 2015) was used to evaluate the potential impact of candidate SNPs on TF-binding sites. TF position weight matrices were compiled from the JASPAR 2022, JASPAR 2014, JASPAR CORE, JASPAR 2016, JASPAR 2018, HOMER, and HOCOMOCO v.10 databases. Variants predicted to exert “strong” effects on TF binding were retained for downstream functional analysis.

CRISPR-dCas9-KRAB construction

The pX330a CRISPR-dCas9-KRAB plasmid was obtained from Addgene (#92361, USA). To reduce potential off-target effects, guide RNAs (gRNAs) were designed using the CRISPOR online tool (http://crispor.tefor.net/). Three pairs of single-guide RNAs (sgRNAs) were designed for each enhancer region and synthesized by Sangon Biotech (China). The CRISPR-dCas9-KRAB vector was cleaved using BbsI (Mei5bio, MF925-01, China) at 37℃ for 2 h. Annealed sgRNA oligonucleotides (98℃, 10 min; 90℃, 5 min; 70℃, 10 min) were inserted into the CRISPR-dCas9-KRAB backbone using T4 DNA ligase (Mei5bio, MF016-01, China). Recombinant CRISPR-dCas9-KRAB-sgRNA constructs were transfected into PMSCs at 50%–60% confluence using Lipofectamine 2000 (11668019, Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA). The sgRNA sequences are listed in Supplementary Table S3.

Dual-luciferase reporter assay

Genomic fragments encompassing candidate SNP loci were amplified from pig ear genomic DNA (rs324628183-342 bp, rs337283866-326 bp, and rs327612517-348 bp) (Supplementary Table S4). The pGL3-Promoter plasmid (Addgene, #212939, USA) was digested with KpnI and NheI enzymes at 37℃ for 2 h, and amplified fragments containing the candidate SNP were ligated into the pGL3-promoter at 16℃ for 18 h. Recombinant plasmids were transformed into DH5α competent cells. Plasmids were extracted using an EndoFree Plasmid Midi Kit (Cwbio, CW21055, China), and sequences were verified by Sanger sequencing. Constructs containing alternative alleles (pGL3-rs324628183-C/G, pGL3-rs337283866-C/T, and pGL3-rs327612517-A/G) were transfected into HEK293T cells using Lipofectamine 2000 (Invitrogen, USA) in triplicate unless otherwise specified. After 48 h, cells were washed twice with PBS and lysed in a shaker for 15 min. Relative firefly and Renilla luciferase activities were quantified using the Dual-Luciferase Assay Kit (UElandy, F6075M, China) according to the manufacturer's instructions and measured with a microplate reader (BioTek, Vermont, USA).

Electrophoretic mobility shift assay (EMSA)

Wild-type and mutant oligonucleotide probes corresponding to predicted TF-binding sites were synthesized (Sangon Biotech, China; Supplementary Table S5) and biotinylated using a Biotin 3′ End Labeling Kit (GS008, Beyotime, China). Nuclear proteins were extracted using nuclear and cytoplasmic extraction reagents (NE-PER™, 78835, Thermo Fisher, USA) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Approximately 10 μg of nuclear protein was incubated with 10 nmol/L biotin-labeled probes in binding buffer (GS009, Beyotime, China) for 20 min at room temperature. For competitive assays, unlabeled positive or negative control oligonucleotides were added to the reaction mixture 2 h before addition of labeled positive oligonucleotides. Complexes were resolved on 1 mm-thick 5% non-denaturing polyacrylamide gels and transferred to Hybond TM-N+ membranes (Beyotime, FFN13, China). The oligonucleotides were UV-crosslinked to membranes, and biotin-labeled probes were detected using the LightShift Chemiluminescent EMSA Kit (Beyotime, GS009, China). Each experiment was repeated at least three times to ensure reproducibility.

Statistical analysis

Data are presented as mean±standard error of the mean (SEM). Two-group comparisons were analyzed by Student’s t-test, while three or more group comparisons were analyzed by Tukey’s test following analysis of variance (ANOVA). Analyses were performed using IBM SPSS Statistics v.22.0 (Armonk, USA), with statistical significance set as *: P<0.05; **: P<0.01; ***: P<0.001; ****: P<0.0001.

RESULTS

NUDT3 expression is tightly associated with skeletal myogenesis

GWAS analyses of PigBiobank data identified the strongest signals for LMDEP (Supplementary Figure S1A; Figure 1A) and LMA (Supplementary Figure S1B, C) on chromosome 7. Gene-based GWAS further demonstrated that the NUDT3 locus harbored the most significant association with both LMDEP (Figure 1B) and LMA (Supplementary Figure S1D). Transcriptomic profiling of longissimus dorsi muscle (PRJNA403969) revealed elevated NUDT3 expression in pigs with larger LMA (Figure 1C). Single-nucleus RNA-seq (snRNA-seq) of porcine skeletal muscle (GSE239273) (Wang et al., 2025) showed broad NUDT3 expression across multiple cell populations, with relatively high levels in muscle satellite cells and mature myofibers (Supplementary Figure S2). During myogenic differentiation of PMSCs, NUDT3 expression progressively increased at both mRNA (Figure 1D) and protein levels (Figure 1E, F). Analysis of independent RNA-seq data (GSE159024) demonstrated a marked reduction in NUDT3 mRNA in TA muscle 3 days post-CTX injury, followed by gradual restoration (Figure 1G) (Maehara et al., 2021). A comparable dynamic pattern was observed at the protein level in the present study (Figure 1H, I). These convergent genetic and transcriptional data support a functional association between NUDT3 and skeletal myogenesis.

NUDT3 enhances proliferative capacity of PMSCs

siRNAs targeting NUDT3 were introduced into proliferating PMSCs and efficiently reduced NUDT3 expression at both mRNA (Figure 2A) and protein levels (Figure 2B, C). Knockdown of NUDT3 led to accumulation of cells at the G1 phase and a concomitant reduction in the G2 phase population, as determined by flow cytometry (Figure 2D, E). EdU incorporation assays demonstrated a significant decline in proliferating cells following NUDT3 knockdown (Figure 2F, G), accompanied by reduced cellular viability (Figure 2H). Consistently, transcript levels of cyclin D and cyclin E were decreased (Figure 2I), cyclin E protein abundance was reduced (Figure 2J, K), and the cell cycle inhibitor p21 was significantly increased at the protein level following NUDT3 knockdown (Figure 2K), indicating impaired cell cycle progression.

Conversely, transfection of pcDNA3.1-NUDT3 induced exogenous overexpression of NUDT3 in PMSCs at both mRNA (Figure 3A) and protein levels (Figure 3B, C). Forced NUDT3 expression reduced the proportion of cells in the G1 phase while increasing the proportion at the G2 stage (Figure 3D, E). Furthermore, EdU incorporation was significantly enhanced (Figure 3F, G), and overall cellular activity was elevated (Figure 3H). Overexpression of NUDT3 also up-regulated cyclin D and cyclin E expression while down-regulating p21 expression at both mRNA (Figure 3I) and protein levels (Figure 3J, K). Collectively, these findings demonstrate that NUDT3 promotes cell cycle progression and proliferative expansion in PMSCs.

NUDT3 enhances myogenic differentiation in PMSCs

To determine the role of NUDT3 in myogenic differentiation, PMSCs were transfected with si-NUDT3 or si-Scramble (negative control) and subsequently induced to differentiate. Four days after transfection, immunostaining for myosin heavy chain (MyHC) revealed a marked reduction in MyHC+ cells following NUDT3 knockdown (Figure 4A), accompanied by a significant decrease in the differentiation index (Figure 4B). Transcript levels of key myogenic markers, including MyHC, MyoG, and MyoD, were reduced (Figure 4C), and corresponding protein expression was significantly suppressed (Figure 4D, E), indicating impaired progression toward terminal differentiation. In contrast, overexpression of NUDT3 significantly increased the myogenic differentiation index (Figure 4F, G) and robustly up-regulated expression of myogenic markers at both mRNA and protein levels (Figure 4H–J). These findings demonstrate that NUDT3 actively promotes myogenic differentiation in PMSCs.

Exogenous NUDT3 facilitates regenerative myogenesis in vivo

A CTX-induced muscle regeneration model was employed to evaluate the contribution of NUDT3 to skeletal muscle regeneration in vivo. Hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining confirmed extensive inflammatory infiltration following CTX administration, while enforced NUDT3 overexpression accelerated the appearance of regenerating myofibers containing centrally located nuclei and increased myofiber cross-sectional area 14 days post-CTX injury (Figure 5A, B). EdU immunofluorescence analysis further showed that NUDT3 overexpression significantly increased the proportion of proliferating EdU+ cells 3 days post-injury (Figure 5C, D) and enhanced the number of embryonic MyHC (MyHC)-positive regenerating myofibers at 5 days post-injury (Figure 5E, F). Together, these data indicate that elevated NUDT3 expression facilitates efficient regenerative myogenesis following acute muscle damage.

NUDT3 promotes skeletal myogenesis through suppression of NF-κB signaling in a Nudix motif-dependent manner

To define downstream molecular events regulated by NUDT3, transcriptome sequencing was conducted following NUDT3 silencing or overexpression, given the established mRNA decapping activity of this Nudix family member in RNA turnover. Transfection with si-NUDT3 resulted in an up-regulation of 1 419 genes (Figure 6A), while pcDNA3.1-NUDT3 reduced expression of 276 genes (Figure 6B). A total of 234 genes overlapped between these datasets (Figure 6C). KEGG enrichment analysis revealed that these 234 genes were significantly enriched in the TNF, Toll-like, and NF-κB signaling pathways (Figure 6D). Among these, NF-κB signaling is recognized as a central modulator of muscle development through coordination of inflammatory responses, immune activation, and differentiation programs. Differential expression of representative genes exhibiting substantial fold changes, including IL-1B, IL6, PTGS2, CCL4, CXCL2, and TLR2, was further confirmed by RT-qPCR (Figure 6E). Considering that NUDT3 exerts mRNA decapping activity via a conserved Nudix motif, an enzymatically inactive mutant was generated as previously described (Song et al., 2013). This mutant partially attenuated the repression of target genes observed with wild-type NUDT3 (Figure 6F, G). Consistent with these findings, inactivation of the Nudix motif significantly prolonged the half-life of transcripts suppressed by wild-type NUDT3 (Figure 6H–L), supporting a decapping-dependent mechanism.

The contribution of NF-κB signaling to NUDT3-modulated myogenesis was further investigated. Western blotting revealed that si-NUDT3 induced a significant increase in p65 phosphorylation (Figure 7A, D; Supplementary Figure S3A–E) without affecting phosphorylation of IKB-α (Figure 7A, B) or p50 (Figure 7C). In contrast, NUDT3 overexpression reduced p65 phosphorylation (Figure 7E–H; Supplementary Figure S3F–J). Furthermore, pharmacological inhibition of canonical NF-κB signaling using PDTC (Gong et al., 2022) partially reversed the increase in p65 phosphorylation induced by si-NUDT3 (Figure 7I, J) and restored expression of key myogenic factors (Figure 7K). These results indicate that NUDT3 enhances skeletal myogenesis, at least in part, through suppression of NF-κB signaling in a Nudix motif-dependent manner.

rs327612517 within a regulatory enhancer of NUDT3 is associated with lean yield in pigs

Given the critical involvement of NUDT3 in myogenic regulation, this genomic region represents a compelling candidate locus for genetic selection of meat production traits. A total of 546 eQTLs significantly correlated with NUDT3 expression in porcine skeletal muscle were retrieved from the PigGTEx portal (Figure 8A). Among these, eight variants—rs327612517, rs324628183, rs325603775, rs337283866, rs338631143, rs340852666, rs80830007, and rs321320064—were prioritized because they overlapped between GWAS and eQTL signals and exhibited strong LD (>0.8) in Duroc×Landrace×Yorkshire hybrids (Figure 8B).

HiCuT profiling of H3K27ac in porcine myoblasts identified 12 putative distal enhancers interacting with the NUDT3 promoter (Figure 8C; Supplementary Figure S4A). Among the prioritized variants, rs324628183 mapped to enhancer 6 (E6), whereas rs327612517, rs325603775, and rs337283866 localized to enhancer 7 (E7) (Figure 8C). CRISPR-dCas9-mediated transcriptional interference demonstrated that blockade of E6 and E7 significantly suppressed NUDT3 transcription by 32% (Supplementary Figure S4B) and 54% (Figure 8D), respectively, confirming regulatory activity. Dual-luciferase reporter assays revealed no significant allelic effects of rs324628183 on E6 activity (Supplementary Figure S4C). In contrast, E7 activity was approximately two-fold higher with the rs327612517-G allele compared with the A allele (Figure 8E), whereas rs337283866 exerted no detectable effect (Supplementary Figure S4D). Integration of PigGTEx data and independent transcriptome sequencing of Yorkshire pigs (unpublished data, n=234) demonstrated that individuals carrying the rs327612517-GG genotype exhibited higher NUDT3 expression than those with the AA genotype (Figure 8F, G), potentially reflecting altered TF-binding affinity (Supplementary Figure S4E). Consistently, analysis of PigBiobank and Yorkshire cohorts (unpublished data, n=165) showed that the GG genotype at rs327612517 was associated with increased LMA and LMDEP relative to the AA genotype (Figure 8G, H; Supplementary Figure S4F–H). Genotype frequency analysis further indicated enrichment of the GG genotype in lean-type breeds (Landrace and Yorkshire), whereas the AA genotype was more prevalent in fat-type breeds (Meishan and Bama pigs) (Figure 8I). Additionally, EMSA confirmed stronger TF-binding affinity for the GG genotype (Figure 8J).

Cross-species conservation analysis identified 15 247 conserved elements between pigs and mice and 14 297 between pigs and humans across the porcine genome (Supplementary Figure S5A). Examination of the NUDT3 regulatory landscape revealed high PhastCons scores within the E7 enhancer region (Supplementary Figure S5B), indicating evolutionary conservation. Notably, rs114677490 and rs115903583, located within the homologous conserved enhancer in humans, were associated with H3K27ac enrichment and functioned as NUDT3 eQTLs in skeletal muscle based on GTEx data (v.10, n=816, Supplementary Figure S5C). eQTL analysis demonstrated significant associations between these SNPs and NUDT3 expression (P=1.52e-04) (Supplementary Figure S5D, E). Additionally, GWAS (Supplementary Figure S5F) and PheWAS (Supplementary Figure S5G, H) analyses identified significant associations between these SNPs and multiple metabolic traits, including arm fat-free mass, trunk fat-free mass, and basal metabolic rate, supporting cross-species conservation of regulatory variants associated with NUDT3 expression and metabolic traits.

DISCUSSION

NUDT3 has been identified as a candidate gene within QTLs strongly associated with age-related sarcopenia in humans (Jin et al., 2022; Singh & Gasman, 2020) and lean yield across diverse pig breeds (Gong et al., 2019; Zhuang et al., 2019), yet its functional contribution to skeletal muscle biology remains unclear. In the current study, NUDT3 expression progressively increased during ex vivo myogenic differentiation and exhibited dynamic modulation during in vivo regenerative myogenesis, supporting a regulatory role in orchestrating skeletal myogenesis.

Complementary loss- and gain-of-function experiments established that NUDT3 drives proliferative expansion of PMSCs. Notably, NUDT3 knockdown disrupted orderly cell cycle progression and constrained proliferation, whereas its overexpression promoted cell cycle progression and enhanced proliferative capacity. In vivo delivery using AAV9 vectors, which efficiently target muscle stem cells (Nance et al., 2019; Song et al., 2024), further demonstrated that elevated NUDT3 expression facilitated regenerative myogenesis following injury. Although the role of NUDT3 in cell proliferation has been insufficiently characterized, previous studies have reported cytoprotective roles during zebrafish embryogenesis under oxidative stress (Samper-Martín et al., 2021) and participation in DNA damage repair in non-small cell lung cancer cells (Benedetti et al., 2022). Given that genomic instability represents a critical constraint on cell division (Matthews et al., 2022), preservation of DNA integrity may constitute a mechanistic basis for the pro-proliferative effects observed in muscle progenitors.

Beyond proliferation, NUDT3 exerted a pronounced effect on myogenic differentiation in cultured porcine myoblasts and during CTX-induced regenerative myogenesis. Although direct evidence linking NUDT3 to lineage commitment is limited, these data provide functional validation for prior genetic associations implicating this locus in lean mass regulation in humans (Singh & Gasman, 2020) and pigs (Demeure et al., 2005). The enhancement of myogenic differentiation provides functional evidence linking NUDT3 activity to skeletal muscle development.

NUDT3 encodes an mRNA decamping enzyme that regulates transcript stability and thereby influences proliferation, differentiation, and migration (Grudzien-Nogalska et al., 2016). Here, integrated transcriptomic analyses identified overlapping DEGs following NUDT3 silencing and overexpression, with significant enrichment in NF-κB signaling. Canonical NF-κB activation has been shown to suppress myogenic differentiation and impair muscle regeneration in vivo and ex vivo (Straughn et al., 2019). In the present study, pharmacological inhibition using PDTC (Gong et al., 2022) partially rescued myogenic defects induced by NUDT3 knockdown, indicating that suppression of NF-κB signaling constitutes a central mechanism underlying NUDT3-mediated myogenesis. Moreover, mutation of the conserved Nudix motif, which is essential for mRNA decapping activity (Grudzien-Nogalska et al., 2016), attenuated the repressive effects of NUDT3 on NF-κB signaling and skeletal myogenesis. These observations support a model in which NUDT3 promotes skeletal myogenesis through Nudix-dependent regulation of transcript turnover and subsequent modulation of NF-κB signaling.

Genetic analyses further reinforced the translational significance of this pathway. Expession of NUDT3 was positively correlated with lean yield traits and skeletal myogenesis in pigs, and regulatory variants influencing its expression may serve as actionable markers for genetic improvement. The variant rs327612517, located within enhancer E7, was identified as a functional eQTL associated with NUDT3 expression in porcine skeletal muscle and demonstrated significant associations with both LMDEP and LMA, as reported in various pig breeds (Zeng et al., 2024). Mechanistically, this regulatory architecture resembles previously characterized variants, including rs1421085 in human FTO (Claussnitzer et al., 2015), rs734209466 in chicken (Shen et al., 2024), and rs136092944 in bovine APCS (Liu et al., 2022), in which altered TF binding at distal enhancers modulates target gene expression. The rs327612517 GG genotype was associated with higher meat production and was more prevalent in lean-type breeds relative to fat-type breeds, highlighting the potential utility of this locus in molecular breeding for lean meat content selection in pigs. Consequently, targeted selection for the rs327612517 GG genotype represents a robust molecular approach for enhancing lean meat yield and advancing sustainable pork production.

CONCLUSION

Collectively, the present findings demonstrate that NUDT3 positively modulates skeletal myogenesis by repressing NF-κB signaling activity in a Nudix motif-dependent manner, thereby providing functional support for prior genetic evidence implicating this locus in human muscle-related traits and lean yield variation in pigs. Furthermore, the identification of rs327612517 as a functional regulatory variant associated with enhanced lean production highlights its potential utility as a molecular marker in precision breeding strategies aimed at improving pork yield.

On the global breadth of finite groups with nontrivial partitions
Seid Kassaw Muhie, Daniele Ettore Otera, Francesco G. Russo

Abstract
. In a series of recent contributions on the notion of global breadth 𝐁 ⁡ ( G ) \mathbf{B}(G) of a finite group G G , it was interesting to observe the structural conditions arising from the classification of finite groups of 𝐁 ⁡ ( G ) = 8 \mathbf{B}(G)=8 . This motivated the study of a new class of finite groups, namely ℋ = { G ​ | G ​ satisfies the condition | ​ G | ≤ 𝐁 ⁡ ( G ) ​ ( 𝐁 ⁡ ( G ) + 1 ) } \mathcal{H}=\{G\ |\ G\ \mbox{satisfies the condition }\ |G|\leq\mathbf{B}(G)(\mathbf{B}(G)+1)\} and very little is known about ℋ \mathcal{H} . Here we focus on the groups with nontrivial partitions (according to the terminology of Baer, Kegel and Kontorovich), determining first that 𝐁 ⁡ ( G ) \mathbf{B}(G) is achieved via the local breadth in connection with the order of maximal cyclic subgroups. Then we show that ℋ \mathcal{H} contains projective special linear groups, projective general linear groups and Suzuki groups, supporting the conjecture that all finite groups with nontrivial partitions belong to ℋ \mathcal{H} . The presence of large families of simple groups in ℋ \mathcal{H} is shown for the first time here.

Proof of the First Main Theorem

We begin to prove of our first main result, where the presence of appropriate cyclic maximal subgroups is somehow guaranteed by the simultaneous presence of nontrivial partitions.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Let

and assume that

is prime. According to the First Sylow Theorem,
G
has a
p
-subgroup

of size

and there are exactly

of these
p
-subgroups by the Third Sylow Theorem (for some

). The existence of a nontrivial partition for

(3.1)

|L_{p}(G)|=\Big|\bigcup_{i=0}^{n_{p}}H_{i}\Big|=|G:N_{G}(H_{i})|\ (p-1)+1=n_{p}(p-1)+1=

=(kp+1)(p-1)+1=kp^{2}-kp+p=kp^{2}+(1-k)p.

Next, suppose that
d=p^{s}
for some
s\geq 2
and that
|G|=p^{s}r
such that
\gcd(p^{s},r)=1
. Again by Sylow Theorems, we may consider a Sylow

of

and that there are

Sylow

-subgroups.
hence we have (again by the presence of nontrivial partitions)

|L_{d}(G)|\leq\Big|\bigcup_{g\in G}H^{g}\Big|\leq(kp+1)|H|=(kp+1)p^{s}

and the equality is achieved if and only if

, i.e.: when
H
is cyclic of order
p
.

If
M
is a maximal cyclic subgroup of
G
of order a power of
p
, then by Sylow Theorems

should be contained in some Sylow

-subgroup of
G
. If

has order different from a power of

, then we may find

in more than one Sylow subgroup of

. Therefore we shall examine different cases on the basis of

Assume that all maximal cyclic subgroups of

does not contain an element of order a prime power

for

and
p\in\pi(G)
. The elements of the sets
L_{p}(G)
and
L_{p^{2}}(G)
are therefore identical. Thus

(3.3)

L_{p}(G)=L_{p^{2}}(G)=\cdots=L_{p^{t}}(G)=\cdots=L_{p^{n}}(G).

In particular, for
p^{2},p^{3}
and so on we have by (
3.1
) that

(3.4)

\mathbf{b}_{p^{2}}(G)=\frac{|L_{p^{2}}(G|}{p^{2}}=\frac{kp+(1-k)}{p}<kp+1-k=\frac{|L_{p}(G)|}{p}=\mathbf{b}_{p}(G)

and so on
\ldots<\mathbf{b}_{p^{3}}(G)<\mathbf{b}_{p^{2}}(G)<\mathbf{b}_{p}(G)
.
Additionally
G
does not contain elements of order
d=pq
for distinct primes
p,q\in\pi(G)
, then it is easy to check that

(3.5)

L_{d}(G)\supseteq L_{p}(G)\ \ \mbox{and}\ \ L_{d}(G)\supseteq L_{q}(G).

However, we may consider the interval
[C_{pq}/1]
of the subgroups lattice

\mathrm{L}(G)
(see details in
[
20
, §1.1, pp.5–10]
) and using (
1.6
) we have
\mathbf{c}_{pq}=0
and

(3.6)

\mathbf{b}_{pq}(G)=\frac{L_{pq}(G)}{pq}={\underset{m|pq}{\sum}}\mathbf{c}_{m}\phi(m)=\frac{1+\mathbf{c}_{p}(p-1)+\mathbf{c}_{q}(q-1)}{pq}

=\frac{1+\mathbf{c}_{p}(p-1)}{pq}+\frac{\left(1+\mathbf{c}_{q}(q-1)\right)-1}{pq}=\frac{\mathbf{b}_{p}(G)}{q}+\frac{\mathbf{b}_{q}(G)}{p}-\frac{1}{pq}

\ \ \Longrightarrow\ \ \mathbf{b}_{p}(G)\geq\mathbf{b}_{d}(G)\ \ \mbox{and}\ \ \mathbf{b}_{q}(G)\geq\mathbf{b}_{d}(G).

The same argument is valid when
d
is the product of more than two distinct primes, say
d=pqr
with
\gcd(p,q,r)=1
, and so on. Therefore
\mathbf{B}(G)
is reached by
\mathbf{b}_{|M|}(G)
for some maximal subgroup
M
of prime order.

Case 2.

Assume that some maximal cyclic subgroup
M
of
G
is either isomorphic to a cyclic subgroup
C_{p^{t}}
of order
p^{t}
for some
t\geq 1
, or is isomorphic to
C_{k}=C_{p_{1}^{t_{1}}}\times C_{p_{2}^{t_{2}}}\times\ldots\times C_{p_{r}^{t_{r}}}
, where
k=p_{1}^{t_{1}}p_{2}^{t_{2}}\ldots p_{r}^{t_{r}}
is a factorization of
k\geq 1
in prime powers such that
\gcd(p_{i},p_{j})=1
for all
i,j\in\{1,2,\ldots,r\}
and
r\geq 1
. Then one can check that both the lattice of all subgroups
\mathrm{L}(C_{p^{t}})
of
M\simeq C_{p^{t}}
and that
\mathrm{L}(C_{k})
of
M\simeq C_{k}
are distributive sublattices of
\mathrm{L}(G)
, where in the first case we have also a chain. It is evident from counting the elements of
G
, that
G
does not contain any elements of order
d>p^{t}
in case
|M|=p^{t}
and
d>k
when
|M|=k
. This indicates that
L_{d}(G)=L_{p^{t}}(G)
and
L_{d}(G)=L_{k}(G).

Arguing as in Case 1, we get

(3.7)

\mathbf{b}_{d}(G)=\frac{|L_{p^{t}}(G|}{d}<\frac{|L_{p^{t}}(G)|}{p^{t}}=\mathbf{b}_{p^{t}}(G),\ \mbox{and}\ \ \mathbf{b}_{d}(G)=\frac{|L_{k}(G|}{d}<\frac{|L_{k}(G)|}{k}=\mathbf{b}_{k}(G).

Lastly, it remains to check
\mathbf{b}_{d}(G)
when

d<p^{t}
for
M\simeq C_{p^{t}}
and
d<k
for
M\simeq C_{k}
. Now consider
M\simeq C_{p^{t}}
and remember that we are in a chain where we may consider the interval
[C_{p^{t}}/1]
and using (
1.6
) we have
\mathbf{c}_{p}=\mathbf{c}_{p^{2}}=\cdots=\mathbf{c}_{p^{t}}=\mathbf{c}_{d}.
This means that the ratio of numbers of elements of
G
of order exactly
d=p^{i}
divided by
\phi(p^{i})
is constant for every divisor of
p^{t}
. Therefore,

(3.8)

\mathbf{b}_{p^{t}}(G)=\frac{\underset{d|p^{t}}{\sum}\mathbf{c}_{d}\phi(d)}{p^{t}}=\frac{1}{p^{t}}\underset{d|p^{t}}{\sum}\ \mathbf{c}_{d}\ \phi(d)=\frac{1}{p^{t}}\left(1+\underset{d\neq 1}{\underset{d|p^{t}}{\sum}}\mathbf{c}_{d}\ \phi(d)\right)=\frac{1}{p^{t}}\left(1+\mathbf{c}_{d}\ {\overset{t}{\underset{i=1}{\sum}}}\phi(p^{i})\right)

=\frac{\mathbf{b}_{p}(G)}{p^{t-1}}+\frac{\mathbf{c}_{d}}{p^{t}}\left(\underset{1\neq p\neq d}{\underset{d|p^{t}}{\sum}}\phi(d)\right)=\frac{\mathbf{b}_{p^{t-1}}(G)}{p}+\mathbf{b}_{p}(G)-\frac{1}{p}.

From what we wrote above we may conclude that
\mathbf{b}_{p^{t}}(G)\geq\mathbf{b}_{p}(G)
, but actually

\mathbf{b}_{p^{t}}(G)\geq\mathbf{b}_{p^{t-1}}(G)\geq\cdots\geq\mathbf{b}_{p}(G).
We end with the case of
M\simeq C_{p^{t}}
and it remains to consider the case of
M\simeq C_{k}
, but the same argument can be applied, since we may consider the interval
[C_{k}/1]
and divisors of the form
d=p_{i}^{t_{i}}
of
k
. Note that
\mathrm{L}(C_{k})
is a complete sublattice (but generally is not a chain) and so we get to
\mathbf{b}_{k}(G)\geq\mathbf{b}_{p_{i}^{t_{i}}}(G).

Thus, if
M\simeq C_{p^{t}}
or
M\simeq C_{k}
,
\mathbf{b}_{d}(G)
may attain its maximum if either
d=p^{t}
or
d=k
, assuming it does not occur at the cyclic subgroup of order prime.

Since Case 1 and Case 2 exhaust all the possible options, the result follows.
∎

(4.8)

|\mathrm{PSL}(2,q)|^{2}\leq 8\ \mathbf{B}(\mathrm{PSL}(2,q))^{3},

(4.11)

\Longrightarrow\ c^{2}\ {\mathbf{B}(\mathrm{PSL}(2,q))}\leq{\mathbf{B}(\mathrm{PSL}(2,q))}^{2}+2{\mathbf{B}(\mathrm{PSL}(2,q))}+1

(4.12)

\Longrightarrow c^{2}\ \mathbf{B}(\mathrm{PSL}(2,q))^{3}\leq\mathbf{B}(\mathrm{PSL}(2,q))^{4}+2\mathbf{B}(\mathrm{PSL}(2,q))^{3}+\mathbf{B}(\mathrm{PSL}(2,q))^{2}

(4.13)

\Longrightarrow c^{2}\ \mathbf{B}(\mathrm{PSL}(2,q))^{3}\leq{\mathbf{B}(\mathrm{PSL}(2,q))}^{2}\ {(\mathbf{B}(\mathrm{PSL}(2,q))+1)}^{2}

and so

(4.14)

|\mathrm{PSL}(2,q)|^{2}\leq c^{2}\ \mathbf{B}(\mathrm{PSL}(2,q))^{3}\leq{\mathbf{B}(\mathrm{PSL}(2,q))}^{2}\ {(\mathbf{B}(\mathrm{PSL}(2,q))+1)}^{2}\ \Longrightarrow\ \mathrm{PSL}(2,q)\in\mathcal{H}.

Part (ii) is completely proved.

(iii). Assume that
q=2^{2n+1}
for some
n\geq 1
. We note that

(4.15)

8\ \mathbf{B}(\mathrm{Sz}(q))^{3}=8\ \left(\frac{q^{4}-q^{3}-(q+1)}{2}\right)^{3}=((q^{2}+q+2)(q^{2}-1))^{3}

and that

(4.16)

|\mathrm{Sz}(q)|^{2}=(q^{2}(q^{2}+1)(q-1))^{2}=q^{4}(q^{2}+1)^{2}(q-1)^{2}.

This allows us to conclude that the condition

(4.17)

((q^{2}+q+2)(q^{2}-1))^{3}\geq q^{4}(q^{2}+1)^{2}(q-1)^{2}.

is always satisfied for all
q\geq 8
and so

(4.18)

|\mathrm{Sz}(q)|^{2}\leq 8\ \mathbf{B}(\mathrm{Sz}(q))^{3},

from which the result follows.

Similarly,
\mathrm{Sz}(q)
possesses a nontrivial partition when
q\geq 8
by Theorem
2.1
and is a simple group when
q\geq 8
. Therefore it remains to check that
\mathrm{Sz}(q)\in\mathcal{H}
when
q\geq 8
. This is true, because for all
q\geq 8
we have by Corollary
3.2
and by
c^{2}\geq 8
that

(4.19)

0\leq c^{2}-2\leq{\mathbf{B}(\mathrm{Sz}(q))}+\frac{1}{\mathbf{B}(\mathrm{Sz}(q))}

(4.20)

\Longrightarrow\ c^{2}\ {\mathbf{B}(\mathrm{Sz}(q))}-2{\mathbf{B}(\mathrm{Sz}(q))}\leq{\mathbf{B}(\mathrm{Sz}(q))}^{2}+1

(4.21)

\Longrightarrow\ c^{2}\ \mathbf{B}(\mathrm{Sz}(q))\leq\mathbf{B}(\mathrm{Sz}(q))^{2}+2\mathbf{B}(\mathrm{Sz}(q))+1

(4.22)

\Longrightarrow c^{2}\ \mathbf{B}(\mathrm{Sz}(q))^{3}\leq{\mathbf{B}(\mathrm{Sz}(q))}^{4}+2{\mathbf{B}(\mathrm{Sz}(q))}^{3}+{\mathbf{B}(\mathrm{Sz}(q))}^{2}

(4.23)

\Longrightarrow c^{2}\ \mathbf{B}(\mathrm{Sz}(q))^{3}\leq{\mathbf{B}(\mathrm{Sz}(q))}^{2}\ {(\mathbf{B}(\mathrm{Sz}(q))+1)}^{2}

and so

(4.24)

|\mathrm{Sz}(q)|^{2}\leq c^{2}\ \mathbf{B}(\mathrm{Sz}(q))^{3}\leq{\mathbf{B}(\mathrm{Sz}(q))}^{2}\ {(\mathbf{B}(\mathrm{Sz}(q))+1)}^{2}\ \Longrightarrow\ \mathrm{Sz}(q)\in\mathcal{H}.

The result (iii) is completely proved.

(iv). Assume that
q=p^{m}
for
q\geq 5
and
q=2^{r}
for
r\geq 2
. Using the same logic which we have seen in (ii) and (iii) above and involving Corollary
3.3
, we get

(4.25)

8\ \mathbf{B}(\mathrm{PGL}(2,q))^{3}=8\ \left(\frac{q^{2}+q-2}{2}\right)^{3}=(q^{2}+q-2)^{3}

and

(4.26)

|\mathrm{PGL}(2,q)|^{2}=(q(q-1)(q+1))^{2}=q^{2}(q+1)^{2}(q-1)^{2}.

This allows us to conclude that the condition

(4.27)

(q^{2}+q-2)^{3}\geq q^{2}(q+1)^{2}(q-1)^{2}.

is always satisfied for all
q\geq 5
and so

(4.28)

|\mathrm{PGL}(2,q)|^{2}\leq 8\ \mathbf{B}(\mathrm{PGL}(2,q))^{3},

from which the result follows. Once more, if
q=2^{m}
for
m\geq 2
, then Corollary
3.3
implies

(4.29)

8\ \mathbf{B}(\mathrm{PGL}(2,q))^{3}=8\ \left(\frac{q^{2}}{2}\right)^{3}=q^{6}

and

(4.30)

|\mathrm{PGL}(2,q)|^{2}=(q(q-1)(q+1))^{2}=q^{2}(q+1)^{2}(q-1)^{2}.

It is evident that

(4.31)

q^{6}\geq q^{2}(q+1)^{2}(q-1)^{2}=q^{6}-2q^{4}+q^{2}.

is always satisfied for all
q=2^{m}
, therefore

(4.32)

|\mathrm{PGL}(2,q)|^{2}\leq 8\ \mathbf{B}(\mathrm{PGL}(2,q))^{3}

Since
\mathrm{PGL}(2,q)
possesses a nontrivial partition when
q\geq 4
, it remains to check that
\mathrm{PGL}(2,q)\in\mathcal{H}
when
q\geq 4
. This is true, because for all
q\geq 8
we may follow a similar argument as done in the previous situations, that is,

(4.33)

0\leq c^{2}-2\leq{\mathbf{B}(\mathrm{PGL}(2,q))}+\frac{1}{\mathbf{B}(\mathrm{PGL}(2,q))}

(4.34)

\Longrightarrow\ c^{2}\ {\mathbf{B}(\mathrm{PGL}(2,q))}-2{\mathbf{B}(\mathrm{PGL}(2,q))}\leq{\mathbf{B}(\mathrm{PGL}(2,q))}^{2}+1

(4.35)

\Longrightarrow\ c^{2}\ {\mathbf{B}(\mathrm{PGL}(2,q))}\leq{\mathbf{B}(\mathrm{PGL}(2,q))}^{2}+2{\mathbf{B}(\mathrm{PGL}(2,q))}+1

(4.36)

\Longrightarrow c^{2}\ \mathbf{B}(\mathrm{PGL}(2,q))^{3}\leq{\mathbf{B}(\mathrm{PGL}(2,q))}^{4}+2{\mathbf{B}(\mathrm{PGL}(2,q))}^{3}+{\mathbf{B}(\mathrm{PGL}(2,q))}^{2}

(4.37)

\Longrightarrow c^{2}\ \mathbf{B}(\mathrm{PGL}(2,q))^{3}\leq{\mathbf{B}(\mathrm{PGL}(2,q))}^{2}\ {(\mathbf{B}(\mathrm{PGL}(2,q))+1)}^{2}

and so

(4.38)

|\mathrm{PGL}(2,q)|^{2}\leq c^{2}\ \mathbf{B}(\mathrm{PGL}(2,q))^{3}\leq{\mathbf{B}(\mathrm{PGL}(2,q))}^{2}\ {(\mathbf{B}(\mathrm{PGL}(2,q))+1)}^{2}\ \Longrightarrow\ \mathrm{PGL}(2,q)\in\mathcal{H}.

Part (iv) of the result is completely proved, so the proof ends.

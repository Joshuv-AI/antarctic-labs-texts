Belchiolina Beatriz Fonseca

Novus International

Latin American Teaching Award

Belchiolina Beatriz Fonseca (known to everyone as Bia Fonseca) holds an undergraduate degree in Veterinary Medicine from the Federal University of Uberlândia (UFU). Early in her career, she worked at SADIA, a large poultry production company, for five years as a veterinarian, focusing on breeder hens, broiler chickens, and laboratory analyses. She holds a Master's degree in Veterinary Science, where she worked on Campylobacter in poultry. During her Master's, she studied in Chile with a Campylobacteriosis expert from the World Health Organization (WHO).

Her PhD was in Applied Immunology, Parasitology, and Microbiology at UFU, where she studied the response of chicken embryos and intestines of chickens to Campylobacter jejuni. She has been a professor since 2010, but only joined UFU in 2014. In 2016, she started her research activities and began supervising students. She started working on the epidemiology of pathogens in poultry, such as Campylobacter and Salmonella, as well as other emerging pathogens isolated in the region, including Erysipelothrix sp. strain two, which had never been isolated from poultry until her studies.

In 2019, she completed her postdoctoral work in Biotechnology in Human and Animal Health, a pivotal moment that led her to focus on her main research today: developing alternatives to antimicrobials. In the same year, she joined the National Institute for Nanotechnology and Theranostics, which opened her mind to new studies on diagnostics and control of Campylobacter, Salmonella, and APEC, as well as studies on probiotics and antimicrobial alternatives. Her primary research line is the use of probiotics and postbiotics to control pathogens.

During her postdoctoral research, in response to a demand in human medicine and leveraging her expertise, she developed the use of chicken embryos as a model for testing tumors and drugs, including nanotechnology-enhanced drugs, contributing to several researchers worldwide. In 2023, she received training at INRAE in France on the relationship between cells and probiotics, as well as next-generation probiotics.

Currently, she is a professor of poultry production and poultry diseases in the Veterinary Medicine Faculty at UFU. She also teaches in the Postgraduate Program in Veterinary Sciences and the Postgraduate Program in Genetics and Biochemistry at UFU. She is a Research Productivity Fellow of the Conselho Nacional de Desenvolvimento Científico e Tecnológico (CNPq), which in Brazil is a granted to researchers who stand out for their participation in research and/or technological development and innovative extension activities.

Born into a very poor family with semi-literate parents, she was the first in her family to graduate. She is the mother of two teenage children, whom she raised alone until her current marriage. She loves art and music and believes the scientific environment should be more welcoming. She teaches her students that the love of learning and science can be transformative. She argues that fostering connections in the scientific community leads to richer studies, and if researchers collaborated more instead of working in isolated groups, science would be even more prosperous.

Brenda Eduarda Barboza Maier, Federal University of Paraná, Palotina Campus, Poultry Experimentation Laboratory, was the recipient of a Certificate of Excellence for her outstanding presentation at the PSA Latin American Scientific Conference. Her paper entitled “Development and Validation of an Enteric Challenge Model to Evaluate Additives that Promote Intestinal Health in Broiler Chickens” was presented in Metabolism and Nutrition, General Nutrition. Co-authors of the paper included H. S. Cury, I. P. Pallaoro, C. E. Benito, L. P. S. Melo, C. Maróstica, G. L. Schiavon, and J. I. M. Fernandes, and it focused on an enteric challenge model to evaluate additives made to promote intestinal health in broiler chickens. Brenda was born in Marechal Cândido Rondon, Paraná, Brazil. In 2019, she moved to Palotina, Brazil where she started majoring in Veterinary Medicine at the Federal University of Paraná, and, currently, she is in the last semester. Since 2023 she has been a part of the Poultry Experimentation Laboratory, having worked as a scientific initiation student, accompanying research projects and experiments in poultry nutrition, bird management, collection of biological material, and analytical procedure assistance in the laboratory. Throughout her academic journey, Brenda has excelled in research related to poultry health, contributing to the development of practices aimed at animal welfare and production improvement.

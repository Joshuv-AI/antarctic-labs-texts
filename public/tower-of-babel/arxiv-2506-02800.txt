Stability and Instability on the De Gregorio Modification of the Constantin-Lax-Majda model
Jie Guo ∗ and QuanSen Jiu †

Abstract
. The Constantin-Lax-Majda (CLM) model and the De Gregorio model which is a modification of the CLM model are well-known for their ability to emulate the behavior of the 3D Euler equations, particularly their potential to develop finite-time singularities. The stability properties of the De Gregorio model on the torus near the ground state − sin ⁡ θ \displaystyle-\sin\theta have been well studied. However, the stability analysis near excited states − sin ⁡ k ​ θ \displaystyle-\sin k\theta with k ≥ 2 \displaystyle k\geq 2 remains challenging. This paper focuses on analyzing the stability and instability of the De Gregorio model on torus around the first excited state − sin ⁡ 2 ​ θ \displaystyle-\sin 2\theta . The linear and nonlinear instability are established for a broad class of initial data, while nonlinear stability is proved for another large class of initial data in this paper. Our analysis reveals that solution behavior to the De Gregorio model near excited states demonstrates different stability patterns depending on initial conditions. One of new ingredients in our instability analysis involves deriving a second-order ordinary differential equation (ODE) governing the Fourier coefficients of solutions and examining the spectral properties of a positive definite quadratic form emerging from this ODE. The approach of this paper would be applicable to other related models and problems.



Key words:

CLM model, De Gregorio model, stability, instability, excited states.

((2.1))

3.
Existence and uniqueness of linearized equation

In this section, we give the proof of Theorem
1.1
and establish the global existence and uniqueness of equation
(1.8)
using Galerkin’s method. To derive the decay rates for the coefficients
\displaystyle\tilde{\eta}_{k}^{(o)}(t),\frac{d}{dt}\tilde{\eta}_{k}^{(o)}(t)
and
\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}\tilde{\eta}_{k}^{(o)}(t)
as required in Section
4
, we construct an approximate solution employing the basis
\displaystyle\{\tilde{e}_{k}^{(o)},k\geq 1\}.
The proof of Theorem
1.1
′
can be proved in a similar way by using the basis
\displaystyle\{\sin k\theta,k\geq 1\}\cup\{\cos k\theta,k\geq 0\}
and we omit it here.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Step 1. Construction of the approximate solution.

Fix a positive integer
\displaystyle n
, and define

((3.1))

\displaystyle\displaystyle\eta_{n}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\tilde{\eta}_{k}^{(o)}(t)\tilde{e}_{k}^{(o)},

((3.2))

\displaystyle\displaystyle\partial_{\theta}v_{n}=H\eta_{n}.

We aim to determine the coefficients
\displaystyle\tilde{\eta}_{k}^{(o)}(t)
such that

((3.3))

\displaystyle\displaystyle\big\langle\partial_{t}\partial_{\theta}\eta_{n},\partial_{\theta}\tilde{e}_{k}^{(o)}\rho\big\rangle+\big\langle\partial_{\theta}L\eta_{n},\partial_{\theta}\tilde{e}_{k}^{(o)}\rho\big\rangle=0

and

((3.4))

\displaystyle\displaystyle\big\langle\partial_{\theta}\eta_{0},\partial_{\theta}\tilde{e}_{k}^{(o)}\rho\big\rangle=\tilde{\eta}_{k}^{(o)}(0)

for
\displaystyle 0\leq t\leq T
, and
\displaystyle k=1,2,\cdots,n.
In fact,
(3.3)
can be rewritten as

((3.5))

\displaystyle\displaystyle\big\langle\partial_{t}\sum_{l=1}^{n}\tilde{\eta}_{l}^{(o)}(t)e_{l+1}^{(o)},e_{k+1}^{(o)}\big\rangle+\big\langle-\frac{1}{2\sin\theta}\partial_{\theta}L\eta_{n},e_{k+1}^{(o)}\big\rangle=0,

where

((3.6))

\displaystyle\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2\sin\theta}\partial_{\theta}L\eta_{n}=-\frac{1}{2}\cos\theta\partial_{\theta}^{2}\eta_{n}-2\cos\theta\eta_{n}-\cos\theta H(\partial_{\theta}\eta_{n})-4\cos\theta v_{n}.

To simplify the notation, we set
\displaystyle u_{n}:=-\sqrt{\pi}\rho^{1/2}\partial_{\theta}\eta_{n}

and define

\displaystyle\displaystyle L_{1}(u_{n})

\displaystyle\displaystyle:=-\sqrt{\pi}\rho^{1/2}\partial_{\theta}L\eta_{n}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sin 2\theta\partial_{\theta}u_{n}+\cos^{2}\theta u_{n}+2\cos\theta H(\sin\theta u_{n})-2\cos\theta\eta_{n}-4\cos\theta v_{n}.

Then the ordinary differential equations
(3.3)
are equivalent to

((3.7))

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\langle\partial_{t}u_{n},\sin(k+1)\theta\right\rangle+\left\langle L_{1}(u_{n}),\sin(k+1)\theta\right\rangle=0.

In fact, it can be verified that
(3.7)
holds true for
\displaystyle k=0
, where we defined
\displaystyle\tilde{\eta}_{0}^{(o)}(t)=0.

According to the standard existence theory for the ordinary differential equations, there exists a unique absolutely continuous functions
\displaystyle\tilde{\eta}_{k}^{(o)}(t)(k=1,2,\cdots,n)
satisfying
(3.3)
and
(3.4)
for
\displaystyle 0\leq t\leq T
.

Step 2. Energy estimates.

\displaystyle L^{2}
estimate of
\displaystyle u_{n}.
Multiplying
(3.7)
by
\displaystyle\tilde{\eta}_{k}^{(o)}(t),
and summing up over
\displaystyle k=1,2,\cdots,n,
we obtain

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|u_{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=-\left\langle L_{1}(u_{n}),u_{n}\right\rangle

((3.8))

\displaystyle\displaystyle=-\big\langle-\frac{1}{2}\cos\theta\partial_{\theta}^{2}\eta_{n}-2\cos\theta\eta_{n}-\cos\theta H(\partial_{\theta}\eta_{n})-4\cos\theta v_{n},u_{n}\big\rangle

\displaystyle\displaystyle=-\big\langle\frac{1}{2}\sin 2\theta\partial_{\theta}u_{n}+\cos^{2}\theta u_{n},u_{n}\big\rangle-\left\langle 2\cos\theta H(\sin\theta u_{n}),u_{n}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\displaystyle\quad+\left\langle 2\cos\theta\eta_{n},u_{n}\right\rangle+\left\langle 4\cos\theta v_{n},u_{n}\right\rangle.

Applying Hölder’s inequality and the
\displaystyle L^{p}
estimate of Hilbert transform, we obtain

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\langle 2\cos\theta H(\sin\theta u_{n}),u_{n}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lesssim\ \|H(\sin\theta u_{n})\|_{L^{2}}\|u_{n}\|_{L^{2}}

((3.9))

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lesssim\ \|u_{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Since
\displaystyle\eta_{n}(0)=0,
we obtain

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\langle 2\cos\theta\eta_{n},u_{n}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lesssim\ \|\eta_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u_{n}\|_{L^{2}}

((3.10))

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lesssim\ \|\partial_{\theta}\eta_{n}\|_{L^{2}}\|u_{n}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lesssim\ \|u_{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Since
\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}v_{n}d\theta=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\eta_{n}d\theta=0

\displaystyle\partial_{\theta}v_{n}=H\eta_{n}
and Lemma
2.6
to obtain

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\langle 4\cos\theta v_{n},u_{n}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lesssim\ \|v_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u_{n}\|_{L^{2}}

((3.11))

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lesssim\ \|\eta_{n}\|_{L^{2}}\|u_{n}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lesssim\ \|\partial_{\theta}\eta_{n}\|_{L^{2}}\|u_{n}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lesssim\ \|u_{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

By collecting
(3.9)
-
(3.11)
, we deduce that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|u_{n}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq\ C\|u_{n}(t)\|_{L^{2}},

and thus

for any
\displaystyle T>0
, where
\displaystyle C
is a positive constant independent of
\displaystyle n
.

\displaystyle H^{1}

(3.7)
by
\displaystyle(k+1)^{2}\tilde{\eta}_{k}^{(o)}(t)

where

\displaystyle\displaystyle\partial_{\theta}L_{1}(u_{n})=

2.6

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lesssim\ \|u_{n}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}.

and thus

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathop{\sup}_{0\leq t\leq T}\|u_{n}(t)\|_{H^{1}}\leq\ C,

which gives the
\displaystyle H^{1}
estimate of
\displaystyle u_{n}
, where
\displaystyle C
is a constant depending on
\displaystyle\mathbb{T},u_{0}
and
\displaystyle T>0
.

\displaystyle L^{2}
estimate of

Corollary 3.1

Step 3. Convergence of the approximate solutions.

4.
Instability of the linearized equation around
\displaystyle-\sin 2\theta

In this section, we give the proof of Theorems
1.2
and Corollary
1.1
. We first demonstrate that the coefficients of the solution
\displaystyle\eta=\sum_{k\geq 1}\tilde{\eta}_{k}(t)\tilde{e}_{k}^{(o)}
obtained in Theorem
1.1
can be expressed as an infinite-dimensional ordinary system
(4.12)
. Then we derive a second-order differential inequality
(4.23)
concerning
\displaystyle\|\eta\|_{\mathcal{H}_{DW}}
. Finally we prove that
\displaystyle\|\eta\|_{\mathcal{H}_{DW}}\ (\eta\neq 0)
will grow exponentially with time under appropriate initial data, which implies the instability of the linearized equation
(1.8)
.

Before proving Theorem
1.2
, we state a lemma that will be used later. Its proof is deferred to the Appendix.

Lemma 4.1
.

For any positive integer
\displaystyle k
, the matrix

((4.1))

A_{k}=\left(\begin{array}[]{lc}a_{k}&\varepsilon_{k}\\
\varepsilon_{k}&a_{k+2}\end{array}\right)

is a positive definite matrix,
where

((4.2))

\displaystyle\displaystyle a_{k}=\left(-d_{k+2}+d_{k}\right)^{2}=\left(-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{2k^{2}-4k-8}{(k+2)^{2}k^{2}}\right)^{2}

and

((4.3))

\displaystyle\displaystyle\varepsilon_{k}=-2d_{k+2}^{2}+d_{k}d_{k+2}+d_{k+2}d_{k+4}=\frac{-2k^{3}+32k+32}{(k+2)^{4}(k+4)}.

Furthermore, there exist two absolute constants
\displaystyle\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}>0
such that the eigenvalues
\displaystyle\lambda_{k}^{1},\ \lambda_{k}^{2}
of
\displaystyle A_{k}
and coefficients
\displaystyle a_{k}
satisfying

((4.4))

\displaystyle\displaystyle 0<\lambda_{1}<\lambda_{inf}\leq a_{k},\,\lambda_{k}^{1},\,\lambda_{k}^{2}\leq\lambda_{\sup}<\lambda_{2}

for
\displaystyle k\geq 1
, where

((4.5))

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lambda_{k}^{1}=\frac{a_{k}+a_{k+2}-\sqrt{(a_{k}-a_{k+2})^{2}+4\varepsilon_{k}^{2}}}{2},

((4.6))

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lambda_{k}^{2}=\frac{a_{k}+a_{k+2}+\sqrt{(a_{k}-a_{k+2})^{2}+4\varepsilon_{k}^{2}}}{2}

and

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lambda_{\sup}=\mathop{\sup}_{k\geq 1}\{a_{k},\lambda_{k}^{1},\lambda_{k}^{2}\},\ \lambda_{inf}=\mathop{\inf}_{k\geq 1}\{a_{k},\lambda_{k}^{1},\lambda_{k}^{2}\}.

Remark 4.1
.

It is easy to prove that
\displaystyle\lambda_{k}^{1},\lambda_{k}^{2}
and
\displaystyle a_{k}
converge to
\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}
as
\displaystyle k\rightarrow\infty
. However, the proof of Lemma
4.1
involves straightforward but tedious calculations using the explicit expressions for
\displaystyle\lambda_{k}^{1},\lambda_{k}^{2}
and
\displaystyle a_{k}
. We provide a rigorous proof in the Appendix. Also, the numerical illustrations of
\displaystyle a_{k},\lambda_{k}^{1},\lambda_{k}^{2}
are presented in Appendix.

We now proceed to the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2

Step 1. The infinite dimensional ODE system.

For

It follows that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\quad+\frac{(k-2)^{2}(k+2)}{4k^{2}}\left(\frac{e_{k}^{(o)}}{k}-\frac{e_{k-2}^{(o)}}{k-2}\right)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=-\frac{k^{2}(k+4)}{4(k+2)^{2}}\tilde{e}_{k+2}^{(o)}+\left[\frac{(k-2)^{2}(k+2)}{4k^{2}}-\frac{k^{2}(k+4)}{4(k+2)^{2}}\right]\tilde{e}_{k}^{(o)}+\frac{(k-2)^{2}(k+2)}{4k^{2}}\tilde{e}_{k-2}^{(o)},

which is

((4.9))

\displaystyle\displaystyle-L\tilde{e}_{k}^{(o)}=-d_{k+2}\tilde{e}_{k+2}^{(o)}+(-d_{k+2}+d_{k})\tilde{e}_{k}^{(o)}+d_{k}\tilde{e}_{k-2}^{(o)},

and

\displaystyle\displaystyle-d_{k+2}+d_{k}

((4.10))

\displaystyle\displaystyle=-\frac{k^{4}+4k^{3}+8k^{2}-8k-16}{2(k+2)^{2}k^{2}}

Note that

For

In view of Corollary
3.1

Step 2. The quadratic form on
\displaystyle\tilde{\eta}_{k}^{(o)}(t)
and
\displaystyle\tilde{\eta}_{k+2}^{(o)}(t)

\displaystyle\displaystyle+\left(-d_{4}+d_{2}\right)^{2}\left(\tilde{\eta}_{2}^{(o)}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle+\left(-d_{3}+d_{1}\right)^{2}\left(\tilde{\eta}_{1}^{(o)}\right)^{2}+2\left(-2d_{3}^{2}+d_{1}d_{3}+d_{3}d_{5}\right)\tilde{\eta}_{1}^{(o)}\tilde{\eta}_{3}^{(o)}+\left(-d_{5}+d_{3}\right)^{2}\left(\tilde{\eta}_{3}^{(o)}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=(-d_{3}+d_{1})a_{1}^{2}

((4.28))

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\frac{11}{18}a_{1}^{2}>0.

If
\displaystyle\eta_{0}=a_{1}\tilde{e}_{1}^{(o)}+a_{k}\tilde{e}_{k}^{(o)}
with
\displaystyle a_{1},a_{k}\neq 0
for
\displaystyle k\geq 2
, then direct calculations show that
\displaystyle\eta_{0}\in\mathcal{H}_{DW}
and
\displaystyle\rho^{1/2}\partial_{\theta}\eta_{0}\in H^{m}
with
\displaystyle m>3
. Moreover, it yields

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\langle-L\eta_{0},\eta_{0}\right\rangle_{\rho}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=(-d_{3}+d_{1})a_{1}^{2}+(-d_{k+2}+d_{k})a_{k}^{2}

((4.29))

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\frac{11}{18}a_{1}^{2}+(-d_{k+2}+d_{k})a_{k}^{2},

which implies
\displaystyle\left\langle-L\eta_{0},\eta_{0}\right\rangle_{\rho}\geq 0
provided

((4.30))

\displaystyle\displaystyle 0<\ a_{k}^{2}\leq\ \frac{11}{18(d_{k+2}-d_{k})}a_{1}^{2}.

The proof of Corollary
1.1
is finished.
∎

5.
nonlinear instability

In this section, we give the proof of Theorem
1.3
based on the linear instability results obtained in Section 4. Recall that the nonlinear problem for
\displaystyle\eta=\omega+\sin 2\theta
can be written as

((5.1))

\left\{\begin{split}&\partial_{t}\eta+L\eta=N(\eta),\\
&\partial_{\theta}v=H\eta,\\
&\eta(0,\theta)=\eta_{0}(\theta),\quad v(t,0)=0,\end{split}\right.

where

((5.2))

\displaystyle\displaystyle L\eta=\frac{1}{2}\sin 2\theta\partial_{\theta}\eta-\cos 2\theta\eta+\sin 2\theta H\eta-2\cos 2\theta v,

((5.3))

\displaystyle\displaystyle N(\eta)=\partial_{\theta}v\eta-v\partial_{\theta}\eta.

5.1.
Existence and uniqueness of the nonlinear equation

To begin with, we first establish the local well-posedness of the classical solution to
(5.1)
with odd initial data, which is

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\displaystyle\|\rho^{1/2}\partial_{\theta}\eta_{0}\|_{H^{m}}=\delta_{0}.
Then there exists
\displaystyle T_{0}>0
and a unique classical odd solution
\displaystyle\eta
to
(5.1)
such that

((5.4))

\displaystyle\displaystyle\rho^{1/2}\partial_{\theta}\eta\in C([0,T_{0}];H^{m}(\mathbb{T}))\cap C^{2}([0,T_{0}];H^{m-2}(\mathbb{T})).

Moreover, setting
\displaystyle u:=\rho^{1/2}\partial_{\theta}\eta,
the solution satisfies

((5.5))

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathop{\sup}_{0\leq t\leq T_{0}}\|u(t)\|_{H^{m}},\mathop{\sup}_{0\leq t\leq T_{0}}\|\partial_{t}u(t)\|_{H^{m-1}},\mathop{\sup}_{0\leq t\leq T_{0}}\|\partial_{t}^{2}u(t)\|_{H^{m-2}}\leq C(T_{0})\delta_{0},

where
\displaystyle T_{0}=\frac{1}{C_{{1}}}\ln(1+\frac{C_{{1}}}{2C_{{2}}\|u_{0}\|_{H^{m}}})
with
\displaystyle C_{1},C_{2}>0
being absolute constants (see
(5.19)
for details).

In addition, for general initial data, we have the following result.

Lemma 5.2
.

Assume that
\displaystyle\eta_{0}\in H^{m}(\mathbb{T})
. Then there exists
\displaystyle T>0
such that
(5.1)
admits a unique classical solution satisfying

\displaystyle\displaystyle\eta\in C([0,T];H^{m}(\mathbb{T}))\cap C^{2}([0,T];H^{m-2}(\mathbb{T})).

Lemma
5.1
constitutes the first part of Theorem
1.3
. Its proof follows from Galerkin’s method, similar to the approach used in Theorem
1.1
. For convenience, we continue to denote the approximate solutions by
\displaystyle(\eta_{n},v_{n})
.Building on the linear analysis in Section
3
, the main task is to establish uniform estimates for the nonlinear terms. Since the derivative of the nonlinear term is

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sqrt{\pi}\rho^{1/2}\partial_{\theta}N(\eta_{n})=\frac{1}{2\sin\theta}(\partial_{\theta}^{2}v_{n}\eta_{n}-v_{n}\partial_{\theta}^{2}\eta_{n}),

which exhibits a stronger singularity, to derive uniform
\displaystyle H^{m}
estimates for
\displaystyle\rho^{1/2}\partial_{\theta}\eta_{n}
, we rewrite
\displaystyle\rho^{1/2}\partial_{\theta}N(\eta_{n})
using the explicit form of the approximate solutions
\displaystyle\eta_{n}
.
The proof of Lemma
5.2
is derived by constructing approximate solutions through general basis
\displaystyle\{\sin k\theta,k\geq 1\}\cup\{\cos k\theta,k\geq 0\}
, which is analogous to that of Lemma
5.1
and we omit the details here. We now present the proof of Lemma
5.1
.

Proof of Lemma
5.1
.

The proof is based on Galerkin’s method and proceeds in several steps:

Step 1. Construction of the approximate solutions.

For a fixed positive integer
\displaystyle n
, we define

((5.6))

\displaystyle\displaystyle\eta_{n}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\tilde{\eta}_{k}^{(o)}(t)\tilde{e}_{k}^{(o)},

((5.7))

\displaystyle\displaystyle\partial_{\theta}v_{n}=H\eta_{n}.

Our objective is to determine the coefficients
\displaystyle\tilde{\eta}_{k}^{(o)}(t)
such that

((5.8))

\displaystyle\displaystyle\big\langle\partial_{t}\partial_{\theta}\eta_{n},\partial_{\theta}\tilde{e}_{k}^{(o)}\rho\big\rangle+\big\langle\partial_{\theta}L\eta_{n},\partial_{\theta}\tilde{e}_{k}^{(o)}\rho\big\rangle=\big\langle\partial_{\theta}N(\eta_{n}),\partial_{\theta}\tilde{e}_{k}^{(o)}\rho\big\rangle,

with the initial condition

((5.9))

\displaystyle\displaystyle\big\langle\partial_{\theta}\eta_{0},\partial_{\theta}\tilde{e}_{k}^{(o)}\rho\big\rangle=\tilde{\eta}_{k}^{(o)}(0)

for
\displaystyle k=1,2,\cdots,n
.
Following the notations introduced in Section
4
, we define

\displaystyle\displaystyle u_{n}=-\sqrt{\pi}\rho^{1/2}\partial_{\theta}\eta_{n}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\tilde{\eta}_{k}^{(o)}(t)\sin(k+1)\theta.

To treat the nonlinear term, we introduce the notation

((5.10))

\displaystyle\displaystyle N_{1}(u_{n}):=-\sqrt{\pi}\rho^{1/2}\partial_{\theta}N(\eta_{n})=\sqrt{\pi}\rho^{1/2}(v_{n}\partial_{\theta}^{2}\eta_{n}-\partial_{\theta}^{2}v_{n}\eta_{n}).

For the second term on the right-hand of (
(5.10)
), we apply the fact that
\displaystyle\partial_{\theta}v_{n}=H\eta_{n}
to obtain

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sqrt{\pi}\rho^{1/2}\partial_{\theta}^{2}v_{n}=\frac{1}{2\sin\theta}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\tilde{\eta}_{k}^{(o)}(t)\left[\sin(k+2)\theta-\sin(k\theta)\right]=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\tilde{\eta}_{k}^{(o)}(t)\cos(k+1)\theta:=g_{n}.

This implies that

((5.11))

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|g_{n}\|_{H^{m}}=\|u_{n}\|_{H^{m}}

for any
\displaystyle m\geq 0
.

Concerning the first term on the right-hand of (
(5.10)
), we represent
\displaystyle\sqrt{\pi}\rho^{1/2}v_{n}
as an explicit Fourier series. Since
\displaystyle\eta_{n}
and
\displaystyle v_{n}
are odd functions, we use
(5.6)
and
(5.7)
to obtain

((5.12))

\displaystyle\displaystyle v_{n}=\sum_{k=1}^{n+2}\frac{-\tilde{\eta}_{k-2}^{(o)}(t)+\tilde{\eta}_{k}^{(o)}(t)}{k^{2}}\sin k\theta,

where
\displaystyle\tilde{\eta}_{-1}^{(o)}(t),\tilde{\eta}_{0}^{(o)}(t),\tilde{\eta}_{n+1}^{(o)}(t)
and
\displaystyle\tilde{\eta}_{n+2}^{(o)}(t)
are understood to be zero.

We now analyze the expression
\displaystyle\frac{\sin(k\theta)}{\sin\theta}.

For
\displaystyle k=2l-1,l=1,2,\cdots
, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{\sin k\theta}{\sin\theta}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\frac{\sin\theta-\sin\theta+\sin(3\theta)-\cdots-\sin(2l-3)\theta+\sin(2l-1)\theta}{\sin\theta}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=1+2\sum_{j=1}^{l-1}\cos(2j\theta).

On the other hand, if
\displaystyle k=2l,l=1,2,\cdots
, then

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{\sin k\theta}{\sin\theta}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\frac{\sin(2\theta)-\sin(2\theta)+\sin(4\theta)-\cdots-\sin(2l-2)\theta+\sin(2l\theta)}{\sin\theta}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=2\sum_{j=1}^{l}\cos(2j-1)\theta.

Without loss of generality, we consider the case
\displaystyle n=2l-1
in (
(5.12)
) to derive

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sqrt{\pi}\rho^{1/2}v_{n}=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{l+1}\frac{-\tilde{\eta}_{2k-3}^{(o)}+\tilde{\eta}_{2k-1}^{(o)}}{(2k-1)^{2}}+\sum_{k=1}^{l}\cos(2k\theta)\left(\sum_{j\geq k}^{l}\frac{-\tilde{\eta}_{2j-1}^{(o)}+\tilde{\eta}_{2j+1}^{(o)}}{(2j+1)^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\displaystyle+\sum_{k=1}^{l}\cos(2k-1)\theta\left(\sum_{j\geq k}^{l}\frac{-\tilde{\eta}_{2j-2}^{(o)}+\tilde{\eta}_{2j}^{(o)}}{(2j)^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\displaystyle h_{n}.

Thus,
\displaystyle N_{1}(u_{n})
can be expressed as

\displaystyle\displaystyle N_{1}(u_{n})=-g_{n}\eta_{n}+h_{n}\partial_{\theta}^{2}\eta_{n}=-g_{n}\eta_{n}-2\sin\theta h_{n}\partial_{\theta}u_{n}-2\cos\theta h_{n}u_{n}.

Define

\displaystyle\displaystyle L_{1}(u_{n}):=-\sqrt{\pi}\rho^{1/2}\partial_{\theta}L\eta_{n}.

Then the ordinary differential equations
(5.8)
are equivalent to

((5.13))

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\langle\partial_{t}u_{n},\sin(k+1)\theta\right\rangle+\left\langle L_{1}(u_{n}),\sin(k+1)\theta\right\rangle=\left\langle N_{1}(u_{n}),\sin(k+1)\theta\right\rangle.

By the standard existence theory for ordinary differential equations, there exist a
\displaystyle T(k)>0
which may depend on
\displaystyle k
and a unique set of absolutely continuous functions
\displaystyle\tilde{\eta}_{k}^{(o)}(t)(k=1,2,\cdots,n)
satisfying
(5.6)
and
(5.7)
for
\displaystyle 0\leq t\leq T(k)
.

Step 2. Energy estimates.

The estimates for the linear operator
\displaystyle L
have been established in the Section
3
, and we now focus on the estimates for the nonlinear terms.

\displaystyle L^{2}
estimate of
\displaystyle u_{n}.

Multiplying
(5.13)
by
\displaystyle\tilde{\eta}_{k}^{(o)}(t)
and summing over
\displaystyle k=1,2,\cdots,n
, we obtain

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|u_{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=-\left\langle L_{1}(u_{n}),u_{n}\right\rangle+\left\langle N_{1}(u_{n}),u_{n}\right\rangle.

Noticing that
\displaystyle\|g_{n}\|_{H^{m}}=\|u_{n}\|_{H^{m}}
for any
\displaystyle m\geq 0
, and applying Poincaré’s inequality and Hölder’s inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\langle N_{1}(u_{n}),u_{n}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lesssim\|\eta_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|g_{n}\|_{L^{2}}\|u_{n}\|_{L^{2}}+\left(\|\partial_{\theta}(\sin\theta h_{n})\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|h_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}\right)\|u_{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lesssim\|\partial_{\theta}\eta_{n}\|_{L^{2}}\|u_{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left(\|\partial_{\theta}(\sin\theta h_{n})\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|h_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}\right)\|u_{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

To estimate
\displaystyle\|h_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}=\big\|\frac{v_{n}}{2\sin\theta}\big\|_{L^{\infty}}
, we note that
\displaystyle v_{n}
is odd and periodic and hence
\displaystyle v_{n}(\pi)=v_{n}(0)
. Since
\displaystyle\sin\theta\geq\frac{2}{\pi}\min\{\theta,\pi-\theta\}
in

, we have
\displaystyle\|h_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}=\big\|\frac{v_{n}}{\sin\theta}\big\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\|\partial_{\theta}v_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}
by Lagrange’s mean value theorem. Then applying
(5.7)
, Poincaré’s inequality and Lemma
2.6
, we obtain

((5.14))

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|h_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\|H(\partial_{\theta}\eta_{n})\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|\partial_{\theta}\eta_{n}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|u_{n}\|_{L^{2}}

and

((5.15))

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|\partial_{\theta}(\sin\theta h_{n})\|_{L^{\infty}}=\bigg\|\partial_{\theta}v_{n}-\cos\theta\frac{v_{n}}{\sin\theta}\bigg\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\|\partial_{\theta}v_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\|u_{n}\|_{L^{2}}.

Thus, we obtain

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\langle N_{1}(u_{n}),u_{n}\right\rangle\lesssim\|u_{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{3}.

It follows that

((5.16))

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|u_{n}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|u_{n}(t)\|_{L^{2}}+C\|u_{n}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

where
\displaystyle C>0
is a constant.

\displaystyle H^{1}
estimate of
\displaystyle u_{n}.

Multiplying equation
(5.13)
by
\displaystyle(k+1)^{2}\tilde{\eta}_{k}^{(o)}(t)
, summing over
\displaystyle k=1,2,\cdots,n
, and applying the integration by parts, we get

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\partial_{\theta}u_{n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}=-\left\langle\partial_{\theta}L_{1}(u_{n}),\partial_{\theta}u_{n}\right\rangle+\left\langle\partial_{\theta}N_{1}(u_{n}),\partial_{\theta}u_{n}\right\rangle,

where

\displaystyle\displaystyle\partial_{\theta}N_{1}(u_{n})=

\displaystyle\displaystyle 2\sin\theta h_{n}\partial_{\theta}^{2}u_{n}+\left(4\cos\theta h_{n}+2\sin\theta\partial_{\theta}h_{n}\right)\partial_{\theta}u_{n}

\displaystyle\displaystyle+2\left(-\sin\theta h_{n}+\cos\theta\partial_{\theta}h_{n}\right)u_{n}-\partial_{\theta}g_{n}\eta_{n}-g_{n}\partial_{\theta}\eta_{n}.

Applying integration by parts, we obtain

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\langle\sin\theta h_{n}\partial_{\theta}^{2}u_{n},\partial_{\theta}u_{n}\right\rangle\leq\|\partial_{\theta}(\sin\theta h_{n})\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\partial_{\theta}u_{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\lesssim\|u_{n}\|_{H^{1}}^{3}.

For the term
\displaystyle\left(-\sin\theta h_{n}+\cos\theta\partial_{\theta}h_{n}\right)u_{n}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\langle\left(-\sin\theta h_{n}+\cos\theta\partial_{\theta}h_{n}\right)u_{n},\partial_{\theta}u_{n}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\left\langle-\sin\theta h_{n}u_{n},\partial_{\theta}u_{n}\right\rangle+\big\langle\frac{u_{n}}{\sin\theta}\partial_{\theta}v_{n}+\frac{v_{n}\cos\theta}{\sin\theta}\frac{u_{n}}{\sin\theta},\partial_{\theta}u_{n}\big\rangle

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lesssim\|h_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u_{n}\|_{L^{2}}\|\partial_{\theta}u_{n}\|_{L^{2}}+\big\|\frac{u_{n}}{\sin\theta}\big\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\partial_{\theta}v_{n}\|_{L^{2}}\|\partial_{\theta}u_{n}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lesssim\|u_{n}\|_{H^{1}}^{3}.

Then, it follows that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\langle\partial_{\theta}N_{1}(u_{n}),\partial_{\theta}u_{n}\right\rangle\lesssim\|u_{n}\|_{H^{1}}^{3}.

Consequently, we obtain

((5.17))

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|u_{n}(t)\|_{H^{1}}\leq C\|u_{n}(t)\|_{H^{1}}+C\|u_{n}(t)\|_{H^{1}}^{2},

where
\displaystyle C>0
is a constant.

\displaystyle H^{m}(m\geq 2)
estimate of
\displaystyle u_{n}.

Multiplying
(5.13)
by
\displaystyle(k+1)^{2m}\tilde{\eta}_{k}^{(o)}(t)
, summing up over
\displaystyle k=1,2,\cdots,n
and using the integration by parts, we obtain

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\partial_{\theta}^{m}u_{n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}=-\left\langle\partial_{\theta}^{m}L_{1}(u_{n}),\partial_{\theta}^{m}u_{n}\right\rangle+\left\langle\partial_{\theta}^{m}N_{1}(u_{n}),\partial_{\theta}^{m}u_{n}\right\rangle.

To get the estimate of
\displaystyle\left\langle\partial_{\theta}^{m}N_{1}(u_{n}),\partial_{\theta}^{m}u_{n}\right\rangle
, we first provide an estimate for
\displaystyle\|\partial_{\theta}^{m}h_{n}\|_{L^{2}}
. Direct calculations give that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|\partial_{\theta}^{m}h_{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{l}(2k)^{2m}\left(\sum_{j\geq k}^{l}\frac{-\tilde{\eta}_{2j-1}^{(o)}+\tilde{\eta}_{2j+1}^{(o)}}{(2j+1)^{2}}\right)^{2}+\sum_{k=1}^{l}(2k-1)^{2m}\left(\sum_{j\geq k}^{l}\frac{-\tilde{\eta}_{2j-2}^{(o)}+\tilde{\eta}_{2j}^{(o)}}{(2j)^{2}}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{l}(2k)^{2m}\sum_{j\geq k}^{l}\left((\tilde{\eta}_{2j-1}^{(o)})^{2}\cdot\frac{1}{(2j+1)^{4}}\right)+\sum_{k=1}^{l}(2k-1)^{2m}\sum_{j\geq k}^{l}\left((\tilde{\eta}_{2j-2}^{(o)})^{2}\cdot\frac{1}{(2j)^{4}}\right)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j\geq 1}^{l}\sum_{k\leq j}\left((\tilde{\eta}_{2j-1}^{(o)})^{2}\cdot\frac{(2k)^{2m}}{(2j+1)^{4}}\right)+\sum_{j\geq 1}^{l}\sum_{k\leq j}\left((\tilde{\eta}_{{2j-2}}^{(o)})^{2}\cdot\frac{(2k-1)^{2m}}{(2j)^{4}}\right)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j\geq 1}^{l}(\tilde{\eta}_{2j-1}^{(o)})^{2}\cdot j\cdot\frac{(2j)^{2m}}{(2j+1)^{4}}+\sum_{j\geq 1}^{l}(\tilde{\eta}_{2j-2}^{(o)})^{2}\cdot j\cdot\frac{(2j-1)^{2m}}{(2j)^{4}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{n}(k+1)^{2m-4+1}(\tilde{\eta}_{k}^{(o)})^{2}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}(k+1)^{2m-3}(\tilde{\eta}_{k}^{(o)})^{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\|\partial_{\theta}^{m-1}u_{n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}},

which means that

for
\displaystyle m\geq 1

For the terms involving
\displaystyle\partial_{\theta}^{m}g_{n}\eta_{n}

for indices

,

for indices

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle C\|u_{n}\|_{H^{m}}^{3}.

In view of
(5.16)
and

, we obtain

.

It then follows that

((5.21))

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathop{\sup}_{0\leq t\leq T_{{0}}}\|u_{n}(t)\|_{H^{m}}\leq\ 2e^{C_{{1}}T_{{0}}}\|u_{0}\|_{H^{m}}.

High estimates of
\displaystyle\partial_{t}u_{n}
and
\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{2}u_{n}.

To estimate
\displaystyle\partial_{t}u_{n}
, we multiply equation

\displaystyle(k+1)^{2m-2}\frac{d}{dt}\tilde{\eta}_{k}^{(o)}(t)
and sum up over
\displaystyle k=1,2,\cdots,n
. After applying integration by parts, we obtain

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}\partial_{\theta}^{m-1}u_{n}\|_{L^{2}}=-\left\langle\partial_{\theta}^{m-1}L_{1}(u_{n}),\partial_{t}\partial_{\theta}^{m-1}u_{n}\right\rangle+\left\langle\partial_{\theta}^{m-1}N_{1}(u_{n}),\partial_{t}\partial_{\theta}^{m-1}u_{n}\right\rangle.

For the term
\displaystyle\partial_{\theta}^{m-1}N_{1}(u_{n})
, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|\partial_{\theta}^{m-1}N_{1}(u_{n})\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|u_{n}\|_{H^{m}}^{2}.

Combining with
(5.20)
, we have

((5.22))

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathop{\sup}_{0\leq t\leq T_{0}}\|\partial_{t}u_{n}(t)\|_{H^{m-1}}\leq\ Ce^{2C_{{1}}T_{{0}}}\|u_{0}\|_{H^{m}}^{2},

where
\displaystyle C
is a positive constant independent of
\displaystyle n
.
Similarly, to get the estimate of
\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{2}u_{n}
, we apply
\displaystyle\partial_{t}
to equation
(5.13)
, multiply
\displaystyle(k+1)^{2m-4}\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}\tilde{\eta}_{k}^{(o)}(t)
, sum up over
\displaystyle k=1,2,\cdots,n
and integrate by parts to obtain

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}^{2}\partial_{\theta}^{m-2}u_{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=-\left\langle\partial_{t}\partial_{\theta}^{m-2}L_{1}(u_{n}),\partial_{t}^{2}\partial_{\theta}^{m-2}u_{n}\right\rangle+\left\langle\partial_{t}\partial_{\theta}^{m-2}N_{1}(u_{n}),\partial_{t}^{2}\partial_{\theta}^{m-2}u_{n}\right\rangle.

By combining the uniform estimates
(5.21)
, we deduce that

((5.23))

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathop{\sup}_{0\leq t\leq T_{0}}\|\partial_{t}^{2}u_{n}(t)\|_{H^{m-2}}\leq\ Ce^{2C_{{1}}T_{{0}}}\|u_{0}\|_{H^{m}}^{2},

where
\displaystyle C
is a positive constant independent of
\displaystyle n
.

Step 3. Existence and uniqueness

By combing the estimates
(5.21)
,
(5.22)
and
(5.23)
, the existence interval
\displaystyle[0,T(k)]
established in Step 1 of Subsection
5.1
can be extended to
\displaystyle[0,T_{0}]
. Furthermore, there exists a subsequence
\displaystyle\{u_{n_{k}}\}_{n_{k}=1}^{\infty}\subseteq\{u_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}

\displaystyle\displaystyle u_{n_{k}}\rightharpoonup u\ weakly\ in\ L^{2}([0,T_{0}];H^{m}(\mathbb{T})),

\displaystyle\displaystyle\partial_{t}u_{n_{k}}\rightharpoonup\partial_{t}u\ weakly\ in\ L^{2}([0,T_{0}];H^{m-1}(\mathbb{T})),

\displaystyle\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{2}u_{n_{k}}\rightharpoonup\partial_{t}^{2}u\ weakly\ in\ L^{2}([0,T_{0}];H^{m-2}(\mathbb{T})).

The left argument is similar to that of Theorem 1.1 (see Step 3 in the proof of Theorem 1.1) and we omit it here.

Consequently, combining (
(5.21)
), (
(5.22)
) and (
(5.23)
), we obtain

((5.24))

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathop{\sup}_{0\leq t\leq T_{0}}\|u(t)\|_{H^{m}},\mathop{\sup}_{0\leq t\leq T_{0}}\|\partial_{t}u(t)\|_{H^{m-1}},\mathop{\sup}_{0\leq t\leq T_{0}}\|\partial_{t}^{2}u(t)\|_{H^{m-2}}\leq C(T_{0})\delta_{0}.

This completes the proof of Lemma
5.1
.
∎

Nonlinear instability

We now address the second part of Theorem

Let
\displaystyle\delta>0,K>0

Proof of Theorem

Step 1. Construction of a solution to the linearized problem.

((5.26))

for all

To simplify the notation, we define

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|\breve{u}(0)\|_{H^{m}}=\delta.

\displaystyle\breve{u}(0)
of the solution

defined in

\displaystyle\displaystyle\bar{\eta}^{\varepsilon}_{0}:=\varepsilon\breve{\eta}_{0}

6.
Nonlinear stability

In this section, we consider the nonlinear problem for
\displaystyle\eta=\omega+\sin 2\theta
, which is given by

((6.1))

\left\{\begin{split}&\partial_{t}\eta=-L\eta+\partial_{\theta}v\eta-v\partial_{\theta}\eta,\\
&\partial_{\theta}v=H\eta,\\
&\eta(0,\theta)=\eta_{0}(\theta),\quad v(t,0)=0.\end{split}\right.

Recall that the discussion in
[
12
]
shows that linearized equation
(6.1)
has the exponential decay
\displaystyle\|\eta\|_{\mathcal{H}_{DW}}\leq C\|\eta_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{DW}}e^{-\frac{3}{8}t}
for
\displaystyle t\geq 0
, given initial data of the form
\displaystyle\eta_{0}=\sum_{k\geq 1}a_{2k}e_{2k}^{(o)}.
In this section, we establish the global well-posedness of the system
(6.1)
for initial data of the form
\displaystyle\sum_{k\geq 1}a_{2k}e_{2k}^{(o)}
.
To estimate the nonlinear term, we first present the following lemma.

Lemma 6.1
.

suppose that
\displaystyle\rho^{1/2}\partial_{\theta}f\in L^{2}
, f is odd and
\displaystyle f(0)=f(\pi)=0
. We have

((6.2))

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|\frac{f}{\sin\theta}\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\ \|\frac{\partial_{\theta}f}{\sin\theta}\|_{L^{2}}.

Proof of Lemma
6.1
.

Since
\displaystyle f
is odd, here we only need to estimate this norm in
\displaystyle[0,\pi]
. For
\displaystyle\theta\in[0,\frac{\pi}{2}],
applying Hölder’s inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\displaystyle\big|\frac{f}{\sin\theta}\big|

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\big|\frac{1}{\sin\theta}\int_{0}^{\theta}\partial_{\theta}fd\theta\big|

((6.3))

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{|\sin\theta|}\left(\int_{0}^{\theta}\sin^{2}\theta d\theta\right)^{1/2}\big\|\frac{\partial_{\theta}f}{\sin\theta}\big\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lesssim\big\|\frac{\partial_{\theta}f}{\sin\theta}\big\|_{L^{2}}.

For
\displaystyle\theta\in(\frac{\pi}{2},\pi],
we similarly have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\big|\frac{f}{\sin\theta}\big|

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\big|\frac{1}{\sin\theta}\int_{\pi}^{\theta}\partial_{\theta}fd\theta\big|

((6.4))

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{|\sin\theta|}\left(\int_{\theta}^{\pi}\sin^{2}\theta d\theta\right)^{1/2}\big\|\frac{\partial_{\theta}f}{\sin\theta}\big\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lesssim\big\|\frac{\partial_{\theta}f}{\sin\theta}\big\|_{L^{2}}.

Since
\displaystyle f
is odd, we can finish the proof of Lemma
6.1
.
∎

Now, we consider the initial data of the form
\displaystyle\eta_{0}=\sum_{k\geq 1}a_{2k}e_{2k}^{(o)}\in\mathcal{H}_{DW}
.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

The discussion in
[
12
]
implies that

((6.5))

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\langle-L\eta,\eta\right\rangle_{\rho}\leq-\frac{3}{8}\left\langle\eta,\eta\right\rangle_{\rho}.

Taking the weighted
\displaystyle\rho
-inner product with
\displaystyle\eta
on the both sides of the first equation of the system
(6.1)
, we obtain

((6.6))

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left\langle\eta,\eta\right\rangle_{\rho}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\left\langle-L\eta,\eta\right\rangle_{\rho}+\left\langle\partial_{\theta}v\eta-v\partial_{\theta}\eta,\eta\right\rangle_{\rho}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq\ -\frac{3}{8}\left\langle\eta,\eta\right\rangle_{\rho}+\left\langle\partial_{\theta}v\eta-v\partial_{\theta}\eta,\eta\right\rangle_{\rho}.

The second term on the right hand side of
(6.6)
can be written as

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\langle\partial_{\theta}v\eta-v\partial_{\theta}\eta,\eta\right\rangle_{\rho}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\frac{(\eta\partial_{\theta}^{2}v-v\partial_{\theta}^{2}\eta)\partial_{\theta}\eta}{\sin^{2}\theta}d\theta

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\frac{\eta\partial_{\theta}^{2}v\partial_{\theta}\eta}{\sin^{2}\theta}d\theta-\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\frac{v\partial_{\theta}^{2}\eta\partial_{\theta}\eta}{\sin^{2}\theta}d\theta

\displaystyle\displaystyle\equiv I+II.

Direct estimates give

((6.7))

\displaystyle\displaystyle|I|

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lesssim\ \|\frac{\eta}{\sin\theta}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\rho^{1/2}\partial_{\theta}\eta\|_{L^{2}}\|\partial_{\theta}^{2}v\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lesssim\ \|\rho^{1/2}\partial_{\theta}\eta\|_{L^{2}}^{3}.

where we have used Lemma
6.1
and the fact

Rewrite the term

Substituting

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\langle\eta,\eta\right\rangle_{\rho}\lesssim\varepsilon,

for all
\displaystyle t>0
. This completes the proof of Theorem
1.4
.
∎

Now we estimate the eigenvalues
\displaystyle\lambda_{k}^{1},\lambda_{k}^{2}
of the matrix
\displaystyle A_{k}
, which are given by

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lambda_{k}^{1}=\frac{a_{k}+a_{k+2}-\sqrt{(a_{k}-a_{k+2})^{2}+4\varepsilon_{k}^{2}}}{2},

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lambda_{k}^{2}=\frac{a_{k}+a_{k+2}+\sqrt{(a_{k}-a_{k+2})^{2}+4\varepsilon_{k}^{2}}}{2}

for
\displaystyle k\geq 1
.
From (
(7.4)
), it yields

\displaystyle\displaystyle a_{k}a_{k+2}\geq\min\{a_{1}a_{3},a_{2}a_{4},a_{3}a_{5},\frac{1}{16}\}=a_{2}a_{4}=(-\frac{3}{8})^{2}(-\frac{37}{72})^{2}>\frac{1}{100},

which implies

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lambda_{k}^{1}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\frac{2(a_{k}a_{k+2}-\varepsilon_{k}^{2})}{a_{k}+a_{k+2}+\sqrt{(a_{k}-a_{k+2})^{2}+4\varepsilon_{k}^{2}}}

((7.7))

\displaystyle\displaystyle\geq\frac{2(a_{2}a_{4}-\varepsilon_{1}^{2})}{2a_{1}+\sqrt{(a_{1}-a_{2})^{2}+4\varepsilon_{1}^{2}}}=\frac{2[(-\frac{3}{8})^{2}(-\frac{37}{72})^{2}-(\frac{62}{405})^{2}]}{2(\frac{11}{18})^{2}+\sqrt{[(\frac{11}{18})^{2}-(-\frac{3}{8})^{2}]^{2}+4(\frac{62}{405})^{2}}}>\frac{1}{50}

for
\displaystyle k\geq 1
. Similarly, it deduces that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lambda_{k}^{1}<\lambda_{k}^{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\frac{a_{k}+a_{k+2}+\sqrt{(a_{k}-a_{k+2})^{2}+4\varepsilon_{k}^{2}}}{2}

((7.8))

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq\frac{2a_{1}+\sqrt{(a_{1}-a_{2})^{2}+4\varepsilon_{1}^{2}}}{2}=\frac{2(\frac{11}{18})^{2}+\sqrt{[(\frac{11}{18})^{2}-(-\frac{3}{8})^{2}]^{2}+4(\frac{62}{405})^{2}}}{2}<\frac{3}{5}

for
\displaystyle k\geq 1
.

In view of (
(7.5)
), (
(7.7)
) and (
(7.8)
), there exist two absolute constants
\displaystyle\lambda_{2}>\lambda_{1}>0
such that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{1}{50}<\lambda_{1}<\lambda_{k}^{1},\lambda_{k}^{2},a_{k}<\lambda_{2}<\frac{3}{5}

for all
\displaystyle k\geq 1
.
The proof of the lemma is finished.

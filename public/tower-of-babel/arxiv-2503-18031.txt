∗ \ast - η \eta -Ricci solitons and Einstein metrics on a weak β \beta -Kenmotsu manifold
Vladimir Rovenski

Abstract
Weak almost contact metric manifolds (i.e., the complex structure is replaced by a nonsingular skew-symmetric tensor), defined by the author and R. Wolak, allow a new look at the classical theory and find novel applications. An important case of these manifolds, which is locally a twisted product, is a weak β \beta -Kenmotsu manifold defined by the author and D.S. Patra. In the paper, the concept of the ∗ \ast -Ricci tensor is adapted to weak almost contact manifolds, the interaction of the ∗ \ast - η \eta -Ricci soliton with the weak β \beta -Kenmotsu structure (with β = c ​ o ​ n ​ s ​ t \beta=const ) is studied and new characteristics of Einstein metrics are obtained. Keywords : weak almost contact structure, warped product, weak β \beta -Kenmotsu manifold, ∗ \ast -Ricci tensor, ∗ \ast - η \eta -Ricci soliton, Einstein metric Mathematics Subject Classifications (2010) Primary 53C12; Secondary 53C21

Introduction

Solutions of some non-linear partial differential equations decompose at large time
t
into solitary waves running with constant speed – the so-called solitons.
Ricci solitons (
RS
), being self-similar solutions of the Ricci flow
\partial g/\partial t=-2\operatorname{Ric}_{g}
(a parabolic partial differential equation),
generalize Einstein metrics
g
, i.e., solutions of
\operatorname{Ric}=\lambda\,g
, where
\lambda
is a real constant, see
[
2
]
.
A Riemannian metric
g
on a smooth manifold
M
is called a RS if there exists a smooth vector field
V
, called soliton vector field, and a scalar
\lambda
, called soliton constant (some authors assume
\lambda
to be a smooth function on
M
), satisfying

\displaystyle(1/2)\pounds_{V}\,g+\operatorname{Ric}=\lambda\,g,

(1)

where
\pounds
is the Lie derivative. A RS is expanding, steady, or shrinking if
\,\lambda
is negative, zero, or positive, respectively.
If
\pounds_{V}\,g=0
, that is,
V
is zero or a Killing vector field, then the RS reduces to Einstein metric.
The study of RS is also motivated by the fact that some compact manifolds don’t admit Einstein metrics.
Some authors study the generalization of (
1
) to
\eta
-RS (where
\eta
is a 1-form) defined in
[
3
]
by

\displaystyle(1/2)\pounds_{V}\,g+\operatorname{Ric}=\lambda\,g+\mu\,\eta\otimes\eta,

(2)

where
\mu
is a smooth function on
M
. If
\pounds_{V}\,g=0
, then (
2
) gives
\eta
-Einstein metrics

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ric}=\lambda\,g+\mu\,\eta\otimes\eta.

(3)

An interesting question is how Ricci-type solitons interact with various geometric structures, e.g. contact structure or foliation, on a manifold.
When does an almost contact metric manifold equipped with a Ricci-type soliton carry canonical (e.g. Einstein-type) metrics?

Contact Riemannian geometry is of growing interest due to its important role in physics, e.g.,
[
1
]
.
An almost contact structure on a Riemannian manifold
(M^{2n+1},g)
is a set
(f,\xi,\eta)
, where

{f}
is a
(1,1)
-tensor of rank
2\,n
,
\xi
is a unit vector field,
{\eta}
is a 1-form dual to
\xi

g({f}X,{f}Y)=g(X,Y)-{\eta}(X)\,{\eta}(Y)

In 1969, S. Tanno classified connected almost contact metric manifolds with automorphism groups of maximal dimensions, as follows:
(i) homogeneous normal contact Riemannian manifolds with constant
f
-holomorphic sectional curvature if the
\xi
-sectional curvature

(ii) warped products of the real line and a Kähler manifold, if

Definition 1
.

A
weak almost contact metric structure
on a smooth manifold

is a set
(f,Q,\xi,\eta,g)

{f}
is a
(1,1)
-tensor of rank

is a nonsingular
(1,1)
-tensor,

\xi

{\eta}
is a 1-form,

is a Riemannian metric on
M

\displaystyle{f}^{2}=-Q+{\eta}\otimes{\xi},\qquad{\eta}({\xi})=1,\qquad Q\,{\xi}={\xi},

(7)

\displaystyle g({f}X,{f}Y)=g(X,Q\,Y)-{\eta}(X)\,{\eta}(Y)\quad(X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}_{M}),

(8)

and
M^{2n+1}({f},Q,{\xi},{\eta},g)
is called a
weak almost contact metric manifold
.

We assume that a smooth
2n
-dimensional distribution
{\cal D}:=\ker\eta
is
{f}
-invariant, i.e.,
{f}X\in{\cal D}\ (X\in{\cal D})
,
as in the classical theory
[
1
]
, where
Q={\rm id}_{\,TM}
.
By the above, the distribution
{\cal D}
is
Q
-invariant:
Q({\cal D})={\cal D}
.
Setting
Y=\xi
in (
8
), we obtain
\eta(X)=g(X,\xi)
.

Proposition 1
.

For a weak almost contact metric structure
({f},\xi,\eta,Q,g)
, the tensor
{f}
is skew-symmetric,
Q
is self-adjoint and the following is true:

{f}\,\xi=0,\quad\eta\circ{f}=0,\quad[Q,\,{f}]=0,\quad\eta\circ Q=\eta.

Recall the following formulas (with
X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}_{M}
):

\displaystyle(\pounds_{X}\,\eta)(Y)

\displaystyle=X(\eta(Y))-\eta([X,Y]),

(9)

\displaystyle(\pounds_{Z}\,g)(X,Y)

\displaystyle=g(\nabla_{X}Z,Y)+g(X,\nabla_{Y}Z).

(10)

A weak almost contact metric structure
({f},Q,\xi,\eta,g)
on a manifold
M
is said to be
normal
if
the following tensor is identically zero:

\displaystyle N^{(1)}(X,Y):=[{f},{f}](X,Y)+2\,d\eta(X,Y)\,\xi,\quad X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}_{M},

where the exterior derivative
d\eta
of
\eta
and
the Nijenhuis torsion
[{f},{f}]
of
{f}

are given by

\displaystyle d\eta(X,Y)=(1/2)\,\{X(\eta(Y))-Y(\eta(X))-\eta([X,Y])\},\quad X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}_{M},

\displaystyle[{f},{f}](X,Y):={f}^{2}[X,Y]+[{f}X,{f}Y]-{f}[{f}X,Y]-{f}[X,{f}Y],\quad X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}_{M}.

The following three tensors
N^{(2)},N^{(3)}
and
N^{(4)}
are well known, see
[
1
]
:

\displaystyle N^{(2)}(X,Y)

\displaystyle:=(\pounds_{{f}X}\,\eta)(Y)-(\pounds_{{f}Y}\,\eta)(X)=2\,d\eta({f}X,Y)-2\,d\eta({f}Y,X),

\displaystyle N^{(3)}(X)

\displaystyle:=(\pounds_{\xi}\,{f})(X)=[\xi,{f}X]-{f}[\xi,X],

\displaystyle N^{(4)}(X)

\displaystyle:=(\pounds_{\xi}\,\eta)(X)=\xi(\eta(X))-\eta([\xi,X])=2\,d\eta(\xi,X).

Proposition 2

(see
[
19
]
)
.

The normality condition
{\cal N}^{\,(1)}=0
for a weak almost contact metric structure implies

{\cal N}^{\,(3)}={\cal N}^{\,(4)}=0
,
{\cal N}^{\,(2)}(X,Y)=\eta([\widetilde{Q}X,fY])
and

[X,\xi]\in{\cal D}
for all
X\in{\cal D}
.
Moreover,
\nabla_{\xi}\,\xi=0
, that is,
{\cal D}^{\bot}
defines a geodesic foliation.

Define a (1,1)-tensor
\widetilde{Q}:=Q-{\rm id}

and note that
[\widetilde{Q},{f}]=0
.
We also obtain
{f}^{3}+{f}=-\widetilde{Q}\,{f}
.

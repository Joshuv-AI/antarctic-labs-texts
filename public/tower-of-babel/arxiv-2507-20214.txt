The Rhaly Operators on Köthe Spaces
Nazlı Doğan

Abstract
. We introduce and study the Rhaly operator on Köthe spaces, with a primary focus on understanding its well-definedness, continuity, and compactness. We especially examine operators acting on power series spaces of both infinite and finite type. In the sequel, we provide integral representations for the Rhaly operator on the space of entire functions H ⁡ ( ℂ ) H(\mathbb{C}) and the space of holomorphic functions on the unit disc H ⁡ ( 𝔻 ) H(\mathbb{D}) . We also investigate the topologizability and power boundedness of the Rhaly operators, which leads to findings about their mean ergodicity, uniform mean ergodicity, and Cesáro boundedness.

2.
Preliminaries

This section provides the essential background for the paper. Our terminology and notation follow
[
20
]
.

A Fréchet space is a complete Hausdorff locally convex space whose topology can be described by a countable system of seminorms
(\|\cdot\|_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
. A grading on a Fréchet space
E
is a sequence of seminorms
\{\|\cdot\|_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
which are increasing, that is,

\|x\|_{1}\leq\|x\|_{2}\leq\|x\|_{3}\leq\dots

for each
x\in E
and which defines the topology. All Fréchet spaces admit a grading. A graded Fréchet space is a Fréchet space with a choice of grading. For more information, see
[
17
]
. In this paper, unless stated otherwise, we will assume that all Fréchet spaces are graded Fréchet spaces.

A matrix
(a_{n,k})_{k,n\in\mathbb{N}}
of non-negative numbers is called a Köthe matrix if the following conditions hold:

1.

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
there exists a
k\in\mathbb{N}
with
a_{n,k}>0
.

2.

a_{n,k}\leq a_{n,k+1}
for each
n,k\in\mathbb{N}
.

Given a Köthe matrix
(a_{n,k})_{n,k\in\mathbb{N}}
, the space

K(a_{n,k})=\bigg\{x=(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\;\bigg|\;\|x\|_{k}:=\sum^{\infty}_{n=1}|x_{n}|a_{n,k}<\infty\;\text{for all}\;k\in\mathbb{N}\bigg\}

is called a Köthe space. Every Köthe space is a Fréchet space given by the semi-norms
in its definition. The dual space of a Köthe space
K(a_{n,k})
is

(K(a_{n,k}))^{\prime}=\bigg\{y=(y_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\;\bigg|\;\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}|y_{n}a_{n,k}^{-1}|<+\infty\;\text{for some}\;k\in\mathbb{N}\bigg\}

see Proposition 27.3 of
[
20
]
for more information.

Grothendieck-Pietsch Criteria
[
20
]
[Theorem 28.15] states that a Köthe space
K(a_{n,k})
is nuclear if and only if for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a
l>k
so that

\displaystyle\sum^{\infty}_{n=1}{a_{n,k}\over a_{n,l}}<\infty.

For a nuclear Köthe space,
\|x\|_{k}=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}|x_{n}|a_{n,k}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
forms an equivalent system of seminorms to the fundamental system of seminorms
\displaystyle\|x\|_{k}=\sum^{\infty}_{n=1}|x_{n}|a_{n,k}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

Let
\alpha=\left(\alpha_{n}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a non-negative increasing sequence with
\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\alpha_{n}=\infty
. The power series space of finite type associated with
\alpha
is defined as

\Lambda_{1}\left(\alpha\right):=\left\{x=\left(x_{n}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}:\;\left\|x\right\|_{k}:=\sum^{\infty}_{n=1}\left|x_{n}\right|e^{-{1\over k}\alpha_{n}}<\infty\textnormal{ for all }k\in\mathbb{N}\right\},

and the power series space of infinite type associated with
\alpha
is defined as

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\infty}\left(\alpha\right):=\left\{x=\left(x_{n}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}:\;\left\|x\right\|_{k}:=\sum^{\infty}_{n=1}\left|x_{n}\right|e^{k\alpha_{n}}<\infty\textnormal{ for all }k\in\mathbb{N}\right\}.

Power series spaces constitute an important class of Köthe spaces. They include the spaces of holomorphic functions on
\mathbb{C}^{d}
and on the polydisc
\mathbb{D}^{d}
, where
\mathbb{D}
denotes the unit disk in
\mathbb{C}
and
d\in\mathbb{N}
:

\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{C}^{d})\cong\Lambda_{\infty}(n^{\frac{1}{d}})\quad\quad\text{and}\quad\quad\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D}^{d})\cong\Lambda_{1}(n^{\frac{1}{d}}).

The nuclearity of a power series space of finite type
\Lambda_{1}(\alpha)
and of infinite type
\Lambda_{\infty}(\alpha)
is equivalent to conditions
\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\ln n}{\alpha_{n}}=0
and
\displaystyle\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{\ln n}{\alpha_{n}}<+\infty
,
respectively. We want to note that the spaces
\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{C}^{d})
and
\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D}^{d})
are are nuclear for every
d\in\mathbb{N}
.

Let
E
be a Fréchet space. A linear map
T:E\to E
is called continuous
if for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
there exists
p\in\mathbb{N}

and
C_{k}>0
such that

\|Tx\|_{k}\leq C_{k}\|x\|_{p}

for all
x\in E
.
\mathcal{L}(E)
denotes the vector space of all continuous linear maps from
E
to
E
. The composition of an operator
T\in\mathcal{L}(E)
with itself n-times is denoted by
T^{n}
. A linear map
T:E\to F
is called compact
if
T(U)
is precompact in
F
where
U
is a neighborhood of zero of E.

Throughout this paper,
e_{n}
stands for the sequence with 1 in the
n^{th}
component and zeros elsewhere. In any Köthe space, the sequence
(e_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
forms the canonical Schauder basis.

To establish the continuity and compactness of operators defined between Köthe spaces, we will apply Lemma 2.1 of
[

]
.

Lemma 2.1

(Lemma 2.1,
[
13
]
)
.

Let
K(a_{n,k})
and
K(b_{n,k})
be Köthe spaces.

a.

T:K(a_{n,k})\to K(b_{n,k})
is a linear, continuous operator if and only if for each

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}{\frac{\|Te_{n}\|_{k}}{\|e_{n}\|_{m}}<\infty}.

b.

If

is Montel, then

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}{\frac{\|Te_{n}\|_{k}}{\|e_{n}\|_{m}}<\infty}.

The next proposition is Proposition 2.2 of

Proposition 2.2
.

Let

Let

be a continuous linear operator on a Fréchet space

Definition 2.3
.

Let
E

It is straightforward that power bounded operators are m-topologizable and m-topologizable operators are topologizable. In
[

Żelazko

Definition 2.5
.

A continuous linear operator

A locally convex Hausdorff space E is defined as (uniformly) mean ergodic if every power bounded operator acting on it is (uniformly) mean ergodic. In this context, Albanese, Bonet, and Ricker established the following result in

(ii)

As all power series spaces are Montel, any power bounded operator defined on power series spaces is (uniformly) mean ergodic.

3.
Rhaly Operators

Let
\theta=(\theta_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be any sequence. The Rhaly matrix defined by
\theta
is

\begin{bmatrix}\theta_{1}&0&0&0&\cdots\\
\theta_{2}&\theta_{2}&0&0&\cdots\\
\theta_{3}&\theta_{3}&\theta_{3}&0&\cdots\\
\theta_{4}&\theta_{4}&\theta_{4}&\theta_{4}&\cdots\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots\end{bmatrix}.

We aim to define an operator
R_{\theta}:K(a_{n,k})\to K(b_{n,k})
by taking
R_{\theta}e_{n}
as the n
th
column of the above matrix, that is,

R_{\theta}e_{n}=(0,\dots,0,\theta_{n},\theta_{n+1},\cdots)=\sum^{\infty}_{j=n}\theta_{j}e_{j}

provided that
R_{\theta}e_{n}\in K(b_{n,k})
for every
n=1,2,\dots
.
Therefore, for every
\displaystyle x=\sum^{\infty}_{n=1}x_{n}e_{n}\in K(a_{n,k})
, the operator
R_{\theta}
can be written as

R_{\theta}x=\sum^{\infty}_{n=1}x_{n}R_{\theta}e_{n}=\sum^{\infty}_{n=1}\left(\sum^{n}_{j=1}x_{j}\right)\theta_{n}e_{n}.

(3.1)

The operator
R_{\theta}:K(a_{n,k})\to K(b_{n,k})
cannot be considered well-defined at this stage. This is due to our inability to guarantee the convergence of the series
\displaystyle\sum^{\infty}_{n=1}x_{n}R_{\theta}e_{n}
converges in
K(b_{n,k})
for every
x\in K(a_{n,k})
. In this paper, we aim to establish the conditions under which the operator
R_{\theta}
is well-defined and continuous, with special emphasis on the case where one of the Köthe spaces is a power series space.
Subsequently, we analyze its behavior between power series spaces.

If the operator
R_{\theta}:K(a_{n,k})\to K(b_{n,k})
is well-defined, then
R_{\theta}(e_{1})=\theta\in K(b_{n,k})
must necessarily hold. This indicates that assuming
\theta\in K(b_{n,k})
is a natural and meaningful choice for such operators. Moreover, the inequalities

\|R_{\theta}e_{n}\|_{k}=\sum^{\infty}_{j=n}|\theta_{j}|b_{j,k}\leq\sum^{\infty}_{j=1}|\theta_{j}|b_{j,k}=\|\theta\|_{k}

guarantees that
R_{\theta}e_{n}\in K(b_{n,k})
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
provided that
\theta\in K(b_{n,k})
. Proposition
2.2
leads us directly to the following proposition:

Proposition 3.1
.

The operator
R_{\theta}:K(a_{n,k})\to K(b_{n,k})
is well-defined and continuous if and only if
\theta\in K(b_{n,k})
and the continuity condition

\forall k\in\mathbb{N}\quad\quad\exists m\in\mathbb{N}\quad\quad\quad\quad\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{\|R_{\theta}e_{n}\|_{k}}{\|e_{n}\|_{m}}<\infty

holds.

Our initial focus will be to explore the conditions required for an operator defined between the Köthe spaces
K(a_{n,k})
and
K(b_{n,k})
to be well-defined, continuous, and compact.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
K(a_{n,k}),K(b_{n,k})
be Köthe spaces and let
\theta\in K(b_{n,k})
. If
\displaystyle\inf_{n\in\mathbb{N}}a_{n,m_{0}}>0
for some
m_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
, then the Rhaly operator
R_{\theta}:K(a_{n,k})\to K(b_{n,k})
is well-defined, continuous. If
K(b_{n,k})
is Montel, then
R_{\theta}
is compact.

Proof.

Let us assume that
\theta\in K(b_{n,k})
and that
\displaystyle\inf_{n\in\mathbb{N}}a_{n,m_{0}}\geq C>0
for some
m_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
and
C>0
holds. This gives us that for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
there exists a constant
D_{k}>0

\|R_{\theta}e_{n}\|_{k}=\sum^{\infty}_{j=n}|\theta_{j}|b_{j,k}\leq\sum^{\infty}_{j=1}|\theta_{j}|b_{j,k}\leq\|\theta\|_{k}\leq\frac{1}{C}\|\theta\|_{k}a_{n,m_{0}}\leq D_{k}a_{n,m_{0}}

for every

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{\|R_{\theta}e_{n}\|_{k}}{\|e_{n}\|_{m_{0}}}<\infty.

Hence
R_{\theta}
is well-defined and continuous from Proposition
2.2
. If

is a Montel space,
R_{\theta}
is also compact from Lemma
2.1
as

∎

The following theorem is a direct consequence of Proposition

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
K(b_{n,k})
be Köthe space and let
\theta\in K(b_{n,k})
. The Rhaly operator

i.

ii.

If
\theta\in\Lambda_{1}(\beta)

R_{\theta}:\Lambda_{\infty}(\alpha)\to\Lambda_{1}(\beta)

We now seek a weaker assumption on the matrix
of

in Proposition
3.2

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
\beta=\left(\beta_{n}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a weakly-stable sequence. The Rhaly operator
R_{\theta}:K(a_{n,k})\to\Lambda_{\infty}(\beta)
is well-defined and continuous if
\theta\in\Lambda_{\infty}(\beta)
and the following condition holds:

\hskip 14.45377pt\forall k\in\mathbb{N}\quad\exists m\in\mathbb{N},C>0\quad\quad\quad\quad e^{-k\beta_{n}}\leq Ca_{n,m}\hskip 43.36243pt\forall n\in\mathbb{N}.

(3.2)

Proof.

Given that
\beta
is a weakly-stable sequence, there exists an
M\in\mathbb{N}
,
M>1
, satisfying

\beta_{n+1}\leq M\beta_{n}

for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Let us assume that
\theta\in\Lambda_{\infty}(\beta)
and the condition (
3.2
) holds. Then, for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
there exist
m\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|R_{\theta}e_{n}\|_{k}&=\sum^{\infty}_{j=n}|\theta_{j}|e^{k\beta_{j}}=\sum^{\infty}_{j=n}|\theta_{j}|e^{k\beta_{j}}e^{k\beta_{j+1}}e^{-k\beta_{j+1}}\\
&\leq e^{-k\beta_{n+1}}\sum^{\infty}_{j=n}|\theta_{j}|e^{k(M+1)\beta_{j}}\leq e^{-k\beta_{n}}\sum^{\infty}_{j=1}|\theta_{j}|e^{k(M+1)\beta_{j}}\\
&\leq C_{1}a_{n,m}\|\theta\|_{(M+1)k}\leq C_{2}\|e_{n}\|_{m}.\end{split}

hold for every

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{\|R_{\theta}e_{n}\|_{k}}{\|e_{n}\|_{m}}<\infty.

Hence,

2.2

Theorem 3.6

Theorem 3.10

4.
Integral Representation of Rhaly Operators

This section presents the integral representations of the Rhaly operator on the space of all entire functions
H(\mathbb{C})
and the space of all holomorphic functions on the unit disc
H(\mathbb{D})
.

For every
g\in H(\mathbb{C})
, one can define an operator
R_{g}:H(\mathbb{C})\to H(\mathbb{C})
such that for every
f\in H(\mathbb{C})
and
z\in\mathbb{C}
, put

(R_{g}f)(z)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{|w|=r_{0}}\frac{f(w)}{w(1-w)}g\left(\frac{z}{w}\right)dw

(4.1)

where
r_{0}
is a constant with
0<r_{0}<1
.
Since
f
and
g
are entire, the function
\displaystyle w\to\frac{f(w)}{w(1-w)}g\left(\frac{z}{w}\right)
is holomorphic away from the origin and
w=1
, and the integration path avoids singularities, the operator is well-defined.

Theorem 4.1
.

The following statements are equivalent:

R_{\theta}:H(\mathbb{C})\to H(\mathbb{C})
is a continuous linear operator whose associated matrix is

\begin{bmatrix}\theta_{0}&0&0&0&\cdots\\
\theta_{1}&\theta_{1}&0&0&\cdots\\
\theta_{2}&\theta_{2}&\theta_{2}&0&\cdots\\
\theta_{3}&\theta_{3}&\theta_{3}&\theta_{3}&\cdots\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots\end{bmatrix}.

There exists an entire function g such that
R_{\theta}=R_{g}
. Then for every
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}

\theta_{n}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{|w|=1}\frac{g(w)}{w^{n+1}}dw.

Proof.

Let
\displaystyle f(z)=\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}a_{n}z^{n}
and
\displaystyle g(z)=\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}b_{n}z^{n}
be two entire functions. We want to show that

(R_{g}f)(z)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{|w|=r_{0}}\frac{f(w)}{w(1-w)}g\left(\frac{z}{w}\right)dw=\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}\left(\sum^{n}_{k=0}a_{k}\right)b_{n}z^{n}

for every
z\in\mathbb{C}
. Let us define

\displaystyle h(z)=\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}\left(\sum^{n}_{k=0}a_{k}\right)z^{n}
for every
z\in\mathbb{D}
and say
\displaystyle c_{n}=\sum^{n}_{k=0}a_{k}
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then we can write that
\displaystyle h(z)=\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}c_{n}z^{n}
. By the following of equalities

\begin{split}h(z)&=\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}\left(\sum^{n}_{k=0}a_{k}\right)z^{n}=\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}a_{k}\left(\sum^{\infty}_{j=k}z^{j}\right)\\
&=\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}a_{k}z^{k}\left(\sum^{\infty}_{j=0}z^{j}\right)=\frac{1}{1-z}\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}a_{k}z^{k}=\frac{1}{1-z}f(z),\end{split}

we establish that
\displaystyle h(z)=\frac{f(z)}{1-z}
for every
z\in\mathbb{D}
. Let us choose a constant

r_{0}\in(0,1)
. We obtain that

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{|w|=r_{0}}\frac{f(w)}{w(1-w)}g\left(\frac{z}{w}\right)dw=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{|w|=r_{0}}\frac{h(w)}{w}g\left(\frac{z}{w}\right)dw\\
&=\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}b_{n}z^{n}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{|w|=r_{0}}\frac{h(w)}{w^{n+1}}dw=\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}c_{n}b_{n}z^{n}=\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}\left(\sum^{n}_{k=0}a_{k}\right)b_{n}z^{n}\end{split}

for every
z\in\mathbb{C}
. This gives us that

(R_{g}z^{n})(\xi)=\sum^{\infty}_{m=n}b_{m}\xi^{m}

for every
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Hence, the associated matrix of
R_{g}
is a Rhaly matrix and

\theta_{n}=b_{n}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{|w|=1}\frac{g(w)}{w^{n+1}}dw

for every
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.
This confirms the identity
R_{\theta}=R_{g}
. On the other hand, if
R_{\theta}
, which is associated with the Rhaly matrix, is a well-defined and continuous operator, the function
\displaystyle g(z)=\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}\theta_{n}z^{n}
, formed by the coefficients of the sequence
\theta
, is an entire function since

(R_{\theta}1)(z)=\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}\theta_{n}z^{n}=g(z)\in H(\mathbb{C}).

The above calculation likewise makes it evident that
R_{\theta}=R_{g}
.
∎

As the space of all entire functions
H(\mathbb{C})
with the topology of uniform convergence on compact subsets is isomorphic to
\Lambda_{\infty}(n)
, the following result directly stems from Theorem
3
:

Corollary 4.2
.

The operator
R_{g}:H(\mathbb{C})\to H(\mathbb{C})
is compact for every
g\in H(\mathbb{C})
.

The operator

R_{g}
, which we defined on

H(\mathbb{C})
via an integral representation, can also be defined on
H(\mathbb{D})
as follows: For every
g\in H(\mathbb{D})
, one can define an operator
R_{g}:H(\mathbb{D})\to H(\mathbb{D})
such that for every
f\in H(\mathbb{D})
and
z\in\mathbb{D}
,
|z|=r_{1}
for some
r_{1}\in(0,1)
, put

(R_{g}f)(z)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{|w|=r_{0}}\frac{f(w)}{w(1-w)}g\left(\frac{z}{w}\right)dw

where

is a constant satisfying

.
Since

Theorem 4.3
.

The following statements are equivalent:

R_{\theta}:H(\mathbb{D})\to H(\mathbb{D})

5.
Topologizability and Power Boundedness of Rhaly Operators

The topologizability and power boundedness of the Rhaly operator will be established for
\Lambda_{1}(\alpha)
,
\Lambda_{\infty}(\alpha)
, and for some Köthe spaces
K(a_{n,k})
. Consequently, these results will enable us to determine the properties of Rhaly operators on
H(\mathbb{C})
and
H(\mathbb{D})
.

In Proposition
3.2
, we showed that the operator
R_{\theta}:K(a_{n,k})\to K(b_{n,k})
is compact if
\displaystyle\inf_{n\in\mathbb{N}}a_{n,m_{0}}>0
for some
m_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
and
K(a_{n,k})
is Montel. As all compact operators are m-topologizable, the operator
R_{\theta}:K(a_{n,k})\to K(a_{n,k})
is m-topologizable. In the following proposition, we will show that the operator
R_{\theta}:K(a_{n,k})\to K(a_{n,k})
is m-topologizable without assuming that
K(a_{n,k})
is Montel.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
K(a_{n,k})
be a Köthe space and let
\theta\in K(a_{n,k})
. Assume that
\displaystyle\inf_{n\in\mathbb{N}}a_{n,m_{0}}>0
for some
m_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
. The Rhaly operator
R_{\theta}:K(a_{n,k})\to K(a_{n,k})
is m-topologizable.

Proof.

Let us assume that
\theta\in K(a_{n,k})
and
\displaystyle A=\inf_{n\in\mathbb{N}}a_{n,m_{0}}>0
holds for some
m_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
. For every
p\in\mathbb{N}
, we define
\displaystyle D_{p}:=\frac{\|\theta\|_{p}}{A}\geq 0
and we obtain

\|R_{\theta}e_{n}\|_{p}=\sum^{\infty}_{j=n}|\theta_{j}|a_{j,p}\leq\sum^{\infty}_{j=1}|\theta_{j}|a_{j,p}\leq\|\theta\|_{p}\leq\frac{1}{A}\|\theta\|_{p}a_{n,m_{0}}=D_{p}\|e_{n}\|_{m_{0}}

for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then it follows that

\|R_{\theta}x\|_{p}\leq\sum^{\infty}_{n=1}|x_{n}|\|R_{\theta}e_{n}\|_{p}\leq D_{p}\sum^{\infty}_{n=1}|x_{n}|\|e_{n}\|_{m_{0}}\leq D_{p}\|x\|_{m_{0}}

for every
x\in K(a_{n,k})
. Then, we obtain

\|R^{2}_{\theta}x\|_{p}\leq D_{p}\|R_{\theta}x\|_{m_{0}}\leq D_{p}D_{m_{0}}\|x\|_{m_{0}}

for every
x\in\mathbb{N}
and

\|R^{k}_{\theta}e_{n}\|_{p}\leq D_{p}(D_{m_{0}})^{k-1}\|e_{n}\|_{m_{0}}\leq(\max\{D_{p},D_{m_{0}}\})^{k}\|e_{n}\|_{m_{0}}

(5.1)

for every
k,n\in\mathbb{N}
. Lemma
2.4
says that
R_{\theta}:K(a_{n,k})\to K(a_{n,k})
is m-topologizable.
∎

Based directly on Proposition
5.1
, we obtain the following result:

Corollary 5.2
.

The Rhaly operator
R_{\theta}:\Lambda_{\infty}(\alpha)\to\Lambda_{\infty}(\alpha)
is m-topologizable for every
\theta\in\Lambda_{\infty}(\alpha)
.

Similar to Proposition
5.1
, a necessary condition for operator
R_{\theta}:K(a_{n,k})\to K(a_{n,k})
to be power bounded can be given as follows:

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
K(a_{n,k})
be a Köthe space and let
\theta\in K(a_{n,k})
. Assume that
\displaystyle A=\inf_{n\in\mathbb{N}}a_{n,m_{0}}>0
for some
m_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
and
\displaystyle\sup_{p\in\mathbb{N}}\|\theta\|_{p}\leq A
. The Rhaly operator
R_{\theta}:K(a_{n,k})\to K(a_{n,k})
is power bounded.

Proof.

Let
A=\inf_{n\in\mathbb{N}}a_{n,m_{0}}>0
for some
m_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
and
\theta\in K(a_{n,k})
with
\sup_{p\in\mathbb{N}}\|\theta\|_{p}\leq A
. Then for every
p\in\mathbb{N}
, the numbers
\displaystyle D_{p}=\frac{\|\theta\|_{p}}{A}
are less then or equal to 1. Inequality (
5.1
) says that
R_{\theta}
is power bounded.
∎

Results presented in Theorem
2.6
and Theorem
2.7
lead to the following corollary:

Corollary 5.4
.

Let
K(a_{n,k})
be a Montel Köthe space and let
\theta\in K(a_{n,k})

\Lambda_{1}(\alpha)

Exact Turán number of the Fano plane in the ℓ 2 \ell_{2} -norm
Jianfeng Hou, Xizhi Liu, Yixiao Zhang

Abstract
A classical object in hypergraph Turán theory is the Fano plane 𝔽 \mathbb{F} , the unique linear 3 3 -graph on seven vertices with seven edges. The Turán density and exact Turán number of 𝔽 \mathbb{F} , first proposed as a problem by Sós [ Sós76 ] in the 1970s, were determined through a sequence of works by De Caen–Füredi [ DCF00 ] , Füredi–Simonovits [ FS05 ] , Keevash–Sudakov [ KS05 ] , and Bellmann–Reiher [ BR19 ] . Addressing a conjecture of Balogh–Clemen–Lidický [ BCL22a , Conjecture 3.1] , we establish an Andrásfai–Erdős–Sós–type stability theorem for 𝔽 \mathbb{F} in the ℓ 2 \ell_{2} -norm: there exists a positive constant ε \varepsilon such that for large n n , every 𝔽 \mathbb{F} -free 3 3 -graph on n n vertices with minimum ℓ 2 \ell_{2} -norm degree at least ( 5 / 4 − ε ) ​ n 3 (5/4-\varepsilon)n^{3} must be bipartite. As a consequence, for large n n , the balanced complete bipartite 3 3 -graph is the unique extremal construction for the ℓ 2 \ell_{2} -norm Turán problem of 𝔽 \mathbb{F} , thereby confirming the conjecture of Balogh–Clemen–Lidický. Our proof includes a refinement of a classical result by Ahlswede–Katona [ AK78 ] on counting stars, and the establishment of an Andrásfai–Erdős–Sós–type theorem for a multigraph Turán problem studied by Bellmann–Reiher [ BR19 ] , both of which are of independent interest.

Theorem 1.1
.

There exist constants

, and moreover,

Recall the classical Andrásfai–Erdős–Sós Theorem, which states that for

1.1
can be viewed as a generalized Andrásfai–Erdős–Sós theorem: instead of considering the minimum degree, we consider the minimum

-norm degree.
Analogous results (using different types of minimum degrees) have also been obtained in recent works such as
[

,

,

]

There are several key ingredients in our proof of Theorem

First, we establish an Andrásfai–Erdős–Sós–type stability theorem (Theorem

) for a multigraph Turán problem, previously considered by Bellmann-Reiher
[

]
in determining the Turán number of

.
Second, we derive several upper bounds on the density of two-edge stars in a graph with given edge density, independence number, and vertex degrees, refining the classical theorem of Ahlswede–Katona

]

Combining these two results, we establish an Erdős–Simonovits

-free

-norm.
Finally, we prove the vertex-extendability of

Together with the Erdős–Simonovits

•

Proposition 2.3
.

Suppose that

is an

-vertex graph with

\left[\frac{17}{50},\frac{7}{20}\right]
, and suppose that

If either of the following conditions holds

(i)

\alpha\in\left[\frac{17}{100},\frac{23}{100}\right]

(ii)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{N}(S_{2},G)}{n^{3}}\leq\max\left\{\frac{\alpha^{3}+\left(2x-\alpha^{2}\right)\left(2x+\alpha^{2}\right)^{1/2}}{2},~\frac{\left(1-2x\right)^{3/2}+4x-1}{2}\right\}+o_{n}(1).

Consequently, by (

\displaystyle\frac{\left\lVert G\right\rVert_{2}}{n^{3}}\leq\max\left\{\alpha^{3}+\left(2x-\alpha^{2}\right)\left(2x+\alpha^{2}\right)^{1/2},~\left(1-2x\right)^{3/2}+4x-1\right\}+o_{n}(1).

The upper bounds in Proposition

of graphs on a common vertex set

.
We use

to denote the common vertex set of
\vec{\mathcal{G}}
.
The size (i.e., the number of edges) of

is defined as
|\vec{\mathcal{G}}|\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^{m}|G_{i}|
.

For a vertex
v\in V(\vec{\mathcal{G}})
, the degree of

in

is given by
d_{\vec{\mathcal{G}}}(v)\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^{m}d_{G_{i}}(v)
.
Denote by

the minimum degree of
\vec{\mathcal{G}}
.

For every pair
\{u,v\}\subseteq V(\vec{\mathcal{G}})
, define the

of

as

\displaystyle C_{\vec{\mathcal{G}}}(uv)\coloneqq\left\{i\in[m]\colon uv\in G_{i}\right\}.

The

of

is defined as
\mu_{\vec{\mathcal{G}}}(uv)\coloneqq|C_{\vec{\mathcal{G}}}(uv)|
, which is the number of graphs among

in which
\{u,v\}
appears as an edge.
We will omit the subscript

when it is clear from context.

Denote by
\mathbb{K}_{4}
the
3
-multigraph
(H_{1},H_{2},H_{3})
on vertex set
\{1,2,3,4\}
, where

\displaystyle H_{1}=\big\{\{1,2\},~\{3,4\}\big\},\quad H_{2}=\big\{\{1,3\},~\{2,4\}\big\},\quad\text{and}\quad H_{3}=\big\{\{1,4\},~\{2,3\}\big\}.

Given an
m

on

contains a copy of

if there exists a triple

and an injective map

such that

•

\big\{\{\psi(1),\psi(2)\},~\{\psi(3),\psi(4)\}\big\}\subseteq G_{i}
,

•

\big\{\{\psi(1),\psi(3)\},~\{\psi(2),\psi(4)\}\big\}\subseteq G_{j}
, and

\big\{\{\psi(1),\psi(4)\},~\{\psi(2),\psi(3)\}\big\}\subseteq G_{k}
.

We say that
\vec{\mathcal{G}}
is

-free
if it does not contain any copy of

.
Denote by

-free

The multigraph Turán problem plays a crucial role in the study of the Turán number of Fano plane and has been used in all the aforementioned works
[
DCF00
,
FS05
,
KS05
,
BR19
]
.
The exact value of

was determined by Füredi–Simonovits in
[

]
, where they proved that for every

,

\displaystyle\mathrm{ex}_{4}(n,\mathbb{K}_{4})=2\binom{n}{2}+2\left\lfloor n^{2}/4\right\rfloor.

In their study of

, Bellmann–Reiher
[
BR19
, Proposition 3.4]
investigated the value of

and proved that for every

,

\displaystyle\mathrm{ex}_{5}(n,\mathbb{K}_{4})\leq\frac{7n^{2}-n}{4}.

(5)

They also stated the following exact bound, though without providing a proof (due to its tediousness):

\displaystyle\mathrm{ex}_{5}(n,\mathbb{K}_{4})=\begin{cases}5\left\lfloor n^{2}/3\right\rfloor,&\mathrm{if}\quad n\leq 12,\\[5.0pt]
2\binom{n}{2}+3\left\lfloor n^{2}/4\right\rfloor,&\mathrm{if}\quad n\geq 13.\end{cases}

Here, the first bound arises from the construction
\vec{\mathcal{G}}=(G_{1},\ldots,G_{5})
, where each

is a

-free graph on

vertices with exactly

edges.
The second bound is obtained from the following construction of

:

Let
V_{1}\cup V_{2}=[n]
be a balanced bipartition.
Define

\displaystyle G_{1}=G_{2}=\binom{V_{1}}{2}\cup K[V_{1},V_{2}],\quad G_{3}=G_{4}=\binom{V_{2}}{2}\cup K[V_{1},V_{2}],\quad\text{and}\quad G_{5}=K[V_{1},V_{2}],

where

denotes the complete bipartite graph with parts
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
.

Motivated by the second construction, we introduce the following definitions.

Let
\vec{\mathcal{G}}=(G_{1},\ldots,G_{5})
be a
5
-multigraph and
V_{1}\cup V_{2}=V(\vec{\mathcal{G}})
be a partition of its vertex set.

•

We say the partition

is

if, up to a permutation of the indices, the following conditions hold:

(i)

G_{1}[V_{2}]=G_{2}[V_{2}]=G_{3}[V_{1}]=G_{4}[V_{1}]=G_{5}[V_{1}]=\emptyset
, and

(ii)

\mu(uv)\leq 2
for every pair

.

•

We say the partition
V_{1}\cup V_{2}=V(\vec{\mathcal{G}})
is

if, up to a permutation of the indices, the following holds:

\displaystyle G_{1}[V_{2}]=G_{2}[V_{2}]=G_{3}[V_{1}]=G_{4}[V_{1}]=G_{5}[V_{1}]=G_{5}[V_{2}]=\emptyset.

Observe that every good partition is also a nice partition, but the converse does not necessarily hold.

It follows from the definition that

if and only if

and

.

Recall from Definition (

\ell_{2}
-norm degree of

in
\mathcal{H}
is

\displaystyle d_{2,\mathcal{H}}(v)=\left\lVert\mathcal{H}\right\rVert_{2}-\left\lVert\mathcal{H}-v\right\rVert_{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{u\in N_{\mathcal{H}}(v)}d^{2}_{\mathcal{H}}(uv)+\sum_{e\in L_{\mathcal{H}}(v)}\left(2\cdot d_{\mathcal{H}}(e)-1\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{u\in N_{\mathcal{H}}(v)}d^{2}_{\mathcal{H}}(uv)+2\sum_{e\in L_{\mathcal{H}}(v)}d_{\mathcal{H}}(e)-d_{\mathcal{H}}(v).

(7)

where the straightforward calculations leading to the second equality can be found in

Observe that

is simply the

.
Combining it with the trivial upper bound

\displaystyle\sum_{e\in L_{\mathcal{H}}(v)}d_{\mathcal{H}}(e)\leq\sum_{e\in L_{\mathcal{H}}(v)}(n-2)\leq|L_{\mathcal{H}}(v)|n,

we obtain the following crude but useful upper bound for

\displaystyle d_{2,\mathcal{H}}(v)\leq\left\lVert L_{\mathcal{H}}(v)\right\rVert_{2}+2|L_{\mathcal{H}}(v)|n.

(8)

Another useful expression of

that is derived from (

\displaystyle d_{2,\mathcal{H}}(v)

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\mathcal{H}\right\rVert_{2}-\left\lVert\mathcal{H}-v\right\rVert_{2}

\displaystyle=\big(2\mathrm{N}(\mathbb{S}_{2},\mathcal{H})+3|\mathcal{H}|\big)-\big(2\mathrm{N}(\mathbb{S}_{2},\mathcal{H}-v)+3|\mathcal{H}-v|\big)=2d_{\mathbb{S}_{2},\mathcal{H}}(v)+3d_{\mathcal{H}}(v).

Lemma 2.5

Let

be an

(i)

.

d_{\mathcal{H}}(v_{1})
.
Lemma
3.6
confirms this intuition.
However, to obtain a lower bound exceeding
\left(\frac{61}{176}+o_{n}(1)\right)n^{2}
, the classical theorem of Ahlswede–Katona (Theorem

) is insufficient.
That is the reason we need the refined bound given in Proposition

.
To apply this refinement, we also require the stability version of the
5
-multigraph Turán problem, as established in Theorem

.

3.1
Technical lemmas

In this subsection, we establish several technical lemmas needed for the proofs of Theorems
3.1
and
3.2
.

The following lemma provides a lower bound on the degree of a vertex
v
in a
3
-graph in terms of its
\ell_{2}
-norm degree.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be an
n
-vertex
3
-graph and
v\in V(\mathcal{H})
be a vertex.
Let

\displaystyle\alpha\coloneqq\frac{d_{2,\mathcal{H}}(v)}{n^{3}}\quad\text{and}\quad x\coloneqq\frac{d_{\mathcal{H}}(v)}{n^{2}}.

Then
x\geq f^{-1}(\alpha)-o_{n}(1)
,
where
f\colon[0,1/2]\to\mathbb{R}
is the increasing function defined by

\displaystyle f(y)\coloneqq\max\left\{(1-2y)^{3/2}+6y-1,~(2y)^{3/2}+2y\right\}.

Proof of Lemma
3.3
.

It follows from (
8
) and Theorem
2.2
that

\displaystyle\alpha=\frac{d_{2,\mathcal{H}}(v)}{n^{3}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\left\lVert L_{\mathcal{H}}(v)\right\rVert_{2}+2n|L_{\mathcal{H}}(v)|}{n^{3}}

\displaystyle\leq\max\left\{(1-2x)^{3/2}+4x-1,~(2x)^{3/2}\right\}+2x+o_{n}(1),

which implies that
x\geq f^{-1}(\alpha)-o_{n}(1)
.

The following lemma shows that a dense
\mathbb{K}_{4}
-free
5
-multigraph contains a large induced subgraph with large minimum degree.

Lemma 3.4
.

Suppose that
\vec{\mathcal{G}}=(G_{1},\ldots,G_{5})
is an
n
-vertex
\mathbb{K}_{4}
-free
5
-multigraph with
|\vec{\mathcal{G}}|\geq\beta\binom{n+1}{2}
, where
\beta\in[0,7/2]
is a real number. Then there exists a set
U\subseteq V(\vec{\mathcal{G}})
such that

\displaystyle|U|\geq\left(\frac{4|\vec{\mathcal{G}}|-2\beta n(n+1)}{7-2\beta}\right)^{1/2}

and the induced subgraph
\vec{\mathcal{G}}[U]=(G_{1}[U],\ldots,G_{5}[U])
satisfies
\delta(\vec{\mathcal{G}}[U])\geq\beta|U|
.

Proof of Lemma
3.4
.

Let
m\coloneqq\left(\frac{4|\vec{\mathcal{G}}|-2\beta n(n+1)}{7-2\beta}\right)^{1/2}
.
Suppose to the contrary that this lemma fails.
Then there exist a partition
S\cup U=V(\vec{\mathcal{G}})
with
|U|=m
, and an ordering
(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n-m})
of vertices in
S
(obtained by repeatedly deleting vertices of minimum degree), such that for every
i\in[n-m]
, the vertex
v_{i}
has less than
\beta(n+1-i)
neighbors in the set
\{v_{i+1},\ldots,v_{n-m}\}\cup U
.
Therefore, the number of edges in
\vec{\mathcal{G}}
that contain at least one vertex from
S
is less than

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n-m}\beta(n+1-i)=\frac{\beta}{2}\left(n^{2}-m^{2}+n-m\right)\leq\beta\binom{n+1}{2}-\frac{\beta m^{2}}{2}.

On the other hand, it follows from the Bellmann–Reiher bound (
5
) that
|\vec{\mathcal{G}}[U]|\leq 7m^{2}/4
.
Therefore, we have

\displaystyle|\vec{\mathcal{G}}|<\beta\binom{n+1}{2}-\frac{\beta m^{2}}{2}+\frac{7m^{2}}{4}

\displaystyle=\beta\binom{n+1}{2}+\frac{(7-2\beta)m^{2}}{4}

\displaystyle=\beta\binom{n+1}{2}+\frac{(7-2\beta)}{4}\cdot\frac{4|\vec{\mathcal{G}}|-2\beta n(n+1)}{7-2\beta}=|\vec{\mathcal{G}}|,

a contradiction.

Note that the extremal construction
\mathbb{B}_{n}
has minimum degree
(3/8-o_{n}(1))n^{2}
.
The next lemma shows that if an
\mathbb{F}
-free
3
-graph contains a copy of
K_{5}^{3}
, then it must have a vertex whose degree is significantly smaller than
(3/8-o_{n}(1))n^{2}
.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be an
\mathbb{F}
-free
3
-graph on
n
vertices.
Suppose that
S\subseteq V(\mathcal{H})
is a set of five vertices that induces a copy of
K_{5}^{3}
in
\mathcal{H}
.
Then

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in S}d_{\mathcal{H}}(v)+\frac{3}{17}d_{\mathrm{min}}\leq\frac{61n(n+1)}{34},

where
d_{\mathrm{min}}\coloneqq\min_{v\in S}d_{\mathcal{H}}(v)
.
Consequently,

\displaystyle d_{\mathrm{min}}\leq\frac{61n(n+1)}{176}.

Proof of Lemma
3.5
.

Suppose to the contrary that this lemma fails for some
5
-set
S=\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{5}\}\subseteq V(\mathcal{H})
.
For each
i\in[5]
, let
G_{i}\coloneqq L_{\mathcal{H}}(v_{i})
, and denote
5
-multigraph
\vec{\mathcal{G}}\coloneqq(G_{1},\ldots,G_{5})
.
Let
d_{\mathrm{min}}\coloneqq\min\{|G_{1}|,\ldots,|G_{5}|\}
.
By our assumption, and using the trivial upper bound
d_{\mathrm{min}}\leq\binom{n}{2}\leq\binom{n+1}{2}
, we have

\displaystyle|\vec{\mathcal{G}}|=\sum_{v\in S}d_{\mathcal{H}}(v)

\displaystyle>\frac{61n(n+1)}{34}-\frac{3}{17}d_{\mathrm{min}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{61n(n+1)}{34}-\frac{3}{17}\binom{n+1}{2}=\frac{58}{17}\binom{n+1}{2}.

Since
\mathcal{H}
is
\mathbb{F}
-free, by Fact
2.7
(
i
),
\vec{\mathcal{G}}
is
\mathbb{K}_{4}
-free.
Applying Lemma
3.4
to
\vec{\mathcal{G}}
with
\beta\coloneqq 58/17
, we obtain a subset
U\subseteq V(\mathcal{H})
such that

\displaystyle|U|\geq\left(\frac{4|\vec{\mathcal{G}}|-2\beta n(n+1)}{7-2\beta}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle>\left(\frac{4\left(\frac{61n(n+1)}{34}-\frac{3}{17}d_{\mathrm{min}}\right)-2\cdot\frac{58}{17}n(n+1)}{7-2\cdot\frac{58}{17}}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\left(2n(n+1)-4d_{\mathrm{min}}\right)^{1/2}

(14)

and
\delta(\vec{\mathcal{G}}[U])\geq\beta|U|=58|U|/17
.

Applying Theorem
2.4
(
ii
) to the induced subgraph
\vec{\mathcal{G}}[U]
, we obtain a partition
X\cup Y=U
such that, up to a permutation of the indices,
G_{5}[X]=G_{5}[Y]=\emptyset
. Consequently,
|G_{5}[U]|\leq|X||Y|\leq|U|^{2}/4
.
Combining it with (
3.1
), we obtain

\displaystyle|G_{5}|=|G_{5}\setminus G_{5}[U]|+|G_{5}[U]|

\displaystyle\leq\binom{n}{2}-\binom{|U|}{2}+\frac{|U|^{2}}{4}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{n^{2}}{2}-\frac{|U|^{2}}{4}\leq\frac{n^{2}}{2}-\frac{2n(n+1)-4d_{\mathrm{min}}}{4}=d_{\mathrm{min}}-\frac{n}{2},

contradicting the definition of
d_{\mathrm{min}}
.
Therefore, we have

\displaystyle|G_{1}|+\cdots+|G_{5}|=|\vec{\mathcal{G}}|\leq\frac{61n(n+1)}{34}-\frac{3}{17}d_{\mathrm{min}},

and it follows that

\displaystyle d_{\mathrm{min}}\leq\frac{61n(n+1)}{34}\left(5+\frac{3}{17}\right)^{-1}=\frac{61n(n+1)}{176}.

This completes the proof of Lemma
3.5
.

It follows that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[u_{1}u_{2}\in L_{v_{\ast}}\right]+\mathbb{P}\left[w_{1}w_{2}\in L_{v_{\ast}}\right]

\displaystyle\geq\frac{|L_{v_{\ast}}[V_{1}]|+|L_{v_{\ast}}[V_{2}]|+|L_{v_{\ast}}[V_{1},V_{2}]|-2n}{\left(1/2+2\xi\right)^{2}n^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{17n^{2}/50-2n}{\left(1/2+2\xi\right)^{2}n^{2}}\geq\frac{34}{25}-20\xi.

Therefore, the probability that
A_{1}
does not occur is

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 3.49306pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$A_{1}$}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left[u_{1}u_{2}\not\in L_{v_{\ast}}\right]+\mathbb{P}\left[w_{1}w_{2}\not\in L_{v_{\ast}}\right]

\displaystyle=2-\left(\mathbb{P}\left[u_{1}u_{2}\in L_{v_{\ast}}\right]+\mathbb{P}\left[w_{1}w_{2}\in L_{v_{\ast}}\right]\right)\leq 2-\frac{34}{25}+20\xi\leq\frac{2}{3}.

(27)

Next, we consider

.
Applying Claim
4.4
to

, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 3.49306pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$A_{2}$}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left[u_{1}w_{2}\not\in L_{v_{1}}[V_{1},V_{2}]\right]+\mathbb{P}\left[u_{2}w_{1}\not\in L_{v_{1}}[V_{1}]\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{5\xi n^{2}}{\left(|V_{1}|-2\right)\left(|V_{2}|-2\right)}+\frac{15\xi n^{2}}{\binom{|V_{1}|-2}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{5\xi n^{2}}{\left(\left(1/2-2\xi\right)n-2\right)^{2}}+\frac{30\xi n^{2}}{\left(\left(1/2-2\xi\right)n-3\right)^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{100}.

(28)

Similarly, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 3.49306pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$A_{3}$}}\right]\leq\frac{1}{100}.

(29)

This implies that
\mathbb{F}\subseteq\mathcal{H}
, a contradiction.

\ell_{2}
-vertex-extendable with respect to

Proof of Lemma

It follows from the definition of

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathbb{B}_{n}\right\rVert_{2}

\displaystyle=\left\lceil\frac{n}{2}\right\rceil\left\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\right\rfloor(n-2)^{2}+\binom{\left\lceil\frac{n}{2}\right\rceil}{2}{\left\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\right\rfloor}^{2}+\binom{\left\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\right\rfloor}{2}{\left\lceil\frac{n}{2}\right\rceil}^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\lfloor\frac{n^{2}}{4}\right\rfloor(n-2)^{2}+\left\lfloor\frac{n^{2}}{4}\right\rfloor^{2}-\frac{n}{2}\left\lfloor\frac{n^{2}}{4}\right\rfloor.

\displaystyle=|V_{1}||V_{2}|\left((n-2)^{2}-\frac{n}{2}\right)+|V_{1}|^{2}|V_{2}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lfloor\frac{n^{2}}{4}\right\rfloor\left((n-2)^{2}-\frac{n}{2}\right)+\left\lfloor\frac{n^{2}}{4}\right\rfloor^{2}=\left\lVert\mathbb{B}_{n}\right\rVert_{2}.

is complete bipartite, and the second inequality above holds only if it is also balanced. This completes the proof of Lemma
4.6

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\vec{\mathcal{G}}=(G_{1},\ldots,G_{5})
be a
5
-multigraph on the vertex set
\{w,x,y,z\}
.
Suppose that
\vec{\mathcal{G}}
is
\mathbb{K}_{4}
-free and
\mu(wx)+\mu(yz)\geq\mu(wy)+\mu(xz)\geq\mu(wz)+\mu(xy)
.
Then the following statements hold:

(i)

Suppose that
\mu(wx)+\mu(yz)\geq 8
and
\mu(wy)+\mu(xz)\geq 7
.
Then
C(wz)\cap C(xy)=\emptyset
.
In particular,
\mu(wz)+\mu(xy)\leq 5
.

(ii)

We have
|\vec{\mathcal{G}}|\leq 25
.

(iii)

Suppose that
|\vec{\mathcal{G}}|\geq 23
. Then
\vec{\mathcal{G}}
is a subgraph of some member of
\mathcal{G}^{*}
.

(iv)

Suppose that
|\vec{\mathcal{G}}|\geq 22
. Then
\vec{\mathcal{G}}
contains an edge with multiplicity
5
.

(v)

Suppose that
\mu(wx)+\mu(yz)+\mu(wy)+\mu(xz)\geq 17
.
Then
C(wz)\cap C(xy)=\emptyset
.

Proof of Lemma
5.1
.

First, we prove (
i
).
Suppose to the contrary that
\mu(wx)+\mu(yz)\geq 8
and
\mu(wy)+\mu(xz)\geq 7
, but
C(wz)\cap C(xy)\neq\emptyset
.
Fix an index
i\in C(wz)\cap C(xy)
, noting that
\{wz,xy\}\subseteq G_{i}
.
Since

\displaystyle|C(wy)\cap C(xz)|

\displaystyle\geq\mu(wy)+\mu(xz)-5\geq 7-5=2\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle|C(wx)\cap C(yz)|

\displaystyle\geq\mu(wx)+\mu(yz)-5\geq 8-5=3,

there exists
j\in\left(C(wy)\cap C(xz)\right)\setminus\{i\}
and
k\in\left(C(wx)\cap C(yz)\right)\setminus\{i,j\}
.
However, this means that
\vec{\mathcal{G}}
contains a copy of
\mathbb{K}_{4}
, a contradiction.

Next, we prove (
ii
).
Suppose to the contrary that
|\vec{\mathcal{G}}|\geq 26
.
Then we have

\displaystyle\mu(wx)+\mu(yz)

\displaystyle\geq\lceil 26/3\rceil=9,

\displaystyle\mu(wy)+\mu(xz)

\displaystyle\geq\lceil\left(26-\mu(wx)-\mu(yz)\right)/2\rceil\geq\lceil(26-5-5)/2\rceil=8,\quad\text{and}\quad

\displaystyle\mu(wz)+\mu(xy)

\displaystyle\geq 26-\mu(wx)-\mu(yz)-\mu(wy)-\mu(xz)\geq 26-5-5-5-5=6,

which contradicts (
i
).

Next, we prove (
iii
).
Similar to the proof of (
ii
), it follows from
|\vec{\mathcal{G}}|\geq 23
that

\displaystyle\mu(wx)+\mu(yz)\geq 8\quad\text{and}\quad\mu(wy)+\mu(xz)\geq 7.

Therefore, by (
i
), we have
C(wz)\cap C(xy)=\emptyset
.
This implies that
\vec{\mathcal{G}}
is a subgraph of some member of
\mathcal{G}^{*}
.

Next, we prove (
iv
). It follows from
|\vec{\mathcal{G}}|\geq 22
that
\mu(wx)+\mu(yz)\geq\lceil 22/3\rceil=8
.
By (
i
), either
\mu(wy)+\mu(xz)\leq 6
or
\mu(wy)+\mu(xz)\leq 5

It follows that
\mu(wx)+\mu(yz)

\displaystyle\min\left\{22-(\mu(wy)+\mu(xz))-(\mu(wy)+\mu(xz)),~\left\lceil\frac{22-(\mu(wy)+\mu(xz))}{2}\right\rceil\right\}

\displaystyle\geq\min\{22-6-6,~\lceil(22-5)/2\rceil\}=9.

Therefore, at least one of

has multiplicity

.

Finally, we prove (

It follows from

\displaystyle\mu(wx)+\mu(yz)\geq\lceil 17/2\rceil=9\quad\text{and}\quad\mu(wy)+\mu(xz)\geq 17-10=7.

So, by (

This proves (
v

5.1

5.2
Proof of Theorem

Proof of Theorem

Claim 5.13

\displaystyle d_{H}(v)\geq\frac{1}{3}\left(\delta(\vec{\mathcal{G}})-2(n-1)\right)\geq\frac{1}{3}\left(\alpha n-2(n-1)\right)\geq\frac{1}{3}\left(\frac{44}{13}n-2(n-1)\right)>\frac{2}{5}n,

Recall that the maximum size of an

by Bellmann–Reiher

Theorem 6.1
.

For every integer
n\geq 632
, it holds that

\displaystyle\mathrm{ex}_{5}(n,\mathbb{K}_{4})=2\binom{n}{2}+3\left\lfloor n^{2}/4\right\rfloor.

We remark that the assumption
n\geq 632
can be relaxed to
n\geq 52
if one uses the upper bound (
5
) given by Bellmann–Reiher in place of the trivial bound
5\binom{n}{2}
in the following proof.

We will use the following simple fact.

Fact 6.2
.

Let
\vec{\mathcal{G}}_{n}=(G_{1},\ldots,G_{5})
be an
n
-vertex
\mathbb{K}_{4}
-free
5
-multigraph.
Suppose that
\vec{\mathcal{G}}_{n}
admits a nice partition.
Then
|\vec{\mathcal{G}}_{n}|\leq 2\binom{n}{2}+3\left\lfloor n^{2}/4\right\rfloor
.

Proof of Theorem
6.1
.

Let
n\geq 632
be an integer.
Let
\vec{\mathcal{G}}_{n}=(G_{1},\ldots,G_{5})
be an
n
-vertex
\mathbb{K}_{4}
-free
5
-multigraph with
|\vec{\mathcal{G}}_{n}|=\mathrm{ex}_{5}(n,\mathbb{K}_{4})
, i.e., the maximum size.
Let
V\coloneqq V(\vec{\mathcal{G}}_{n})
.

Let
g(m)\coloneqq 2\binom{m}{2}+3\left\lfloor m^{2}/4\right\rfloor
and
f(m)\coloneqq 44m/13
.
Note that for every integer
m\geq 30
,

\displaystyle g(m)-g(m-1)=2(m-1)+3\left\lfloor m/2\right\rfloor>f(m).

Suppose that
\delta(\vec{\mathcal{G}}_{n})\geq f(n)
.
Then by Theorem
2.4
(
i
),
\vec{\mathcal{G}}_{n}
admits a nice partition, and thus,
|\vec{\mathcal{G}}_{n}|\leq g(n)
by Fact
6.2
.
So we may assume that
\delta(\vec{\mathcal{G}}_{n})<f(n)
.

Let
v_{n}\in V
be a vertex of minimum degree in
\vec{\mathcal{G}}_{n}
.
Let
V_{n-1}\coloneqq V\setminus\{v_{n}\}
and
\vec{\mathcal{G}}_{n-1}\coloneqq\vec{\mathcal{G}}_{n}-v_{n}
.
Then

\displaystyle|\vec{\mathcal{G}}_{n-1}|=|\vec{\mathcal{G}}_{n}|-d_{\vec{\mathcal{G}}_{n}}(v_{n})=|\vec{\mathcal{G}}_{n}|-\delta(\vec{\mathcal{G}}_{n})>g(n)-f(n)>g(n-1),

which means that
|\vec{\mathcal{G}}_{n-1}|\geq g(n-1)+1
.

Since
|\vec{\mathcal{G}}_{n-1}|\geq g(n-1)+1
, it follows from Theorem
2.4
(
i
) and Fact
6.2
that
\delta(\vec{\mathcal{G}}_{n-1})<f(n-1)
.
Fix a vertex

of minimum degree in
\vec{\mathcal{G}}_{n-1}
.
Let
V_{n-2}\coloneqq V_{n-1}\setminus\{v_{n-1}\}
and
\vec{\mathcal{G}}_{n-2}\coloneqq\vec{\mathcal{G}}_{n-1}-v_{n-1}
.
Then

\displaystyle|\vec{\mathcal{G}}_{n-2}|=|\vec{\mathcal{G}}_{n-1}|-d_{\vec{\mathcal{G}}_{n-1}}(v_{n})

\displaystyle=|\vec{\mathcal{G}}_{n-1}|-\delta(\vec{\mathcal{G}}_{n-1})

\displaystyle>g(n-1)+1-f(n-1)>g(n-2)+1,

which means that
|\vec{\mathcal{G}}_{n-2}|\geq g(n-2)+2
.

Let
n_{0}\coloneqq\lfloor 2\sqrt{3n-2}/3\rfloor
, noting that
n_{0}+1\geq\left\lfloor 2\sqrt{3\times 632-2}/3\right\rfloor+1=30
and

Let us repeat the above deleting minimum degree vertex process until we get a
5
-multigraph

Then simple calculations show that

\displaystyle|\vec{\mathcal{G}}_{n_{0}}|\geq g(n_{0})+n-n_{0}

\displaystyle=2\binom{n_{0}}{2}+3\left\lfloor\frac{n_{0}^{2}}{4}\right\rfloor+n-n_{0}

\displaystyle\geq 2\binom{n_{0}}{2}+3\left\lfloor\frac{n_{0}^{2}}{4}\right\rfloor+\frac{9n_{0}^{2}+8}{12}-n_{0}>5\binom{n_{0}}{2},

which is a contradiction to the trivial upper bound
|\vec{\mathcal{G}}_{n_{0}}|\leq 5\binom{n_{0}}{2}
.

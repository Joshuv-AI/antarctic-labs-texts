Randomized Geodesic Flow on Hyperbolic Groups
Luzie Kupffer, Mahan Mj, Chiranjib Mukherjee

Abstract
. Motivated by Gromov’s geodesic flow problem on hyperbolic groups G G , we develop in this paper an analog using random walks. This leads to a notion of a harmonic analog Θ \Theta of the Bowen-Margulis-Sullivan measure on ∂ 2 G \partial^{2}G . We provide three different but related constructions of Θ \Theta : 1) by moving the base-point along a quasigeodesic ray 2) by moving the base-point along random walk trajectories 3) directly as a push-forward under the boundary map to ∂ 2 G \partial^{2}G of a measure inherited from studying all bi-infinite random walk trajectories (with no restriction on base-point) on G ℤ G^{\mathbb{Z}} . Of these, the third construction is the most involved and needs new techniques. It relies on developing a framework where we can treat bi-infinite random walk trajectories as analogs of bi-infinite geodesics on complete simply connected negatively curved manifolds. Geodesic flow on a hyperbolic group is typically not well-defined due to non-uniqueness of geodesics. We circumvent this problem in the random walk setup by considering all trajectories. We thus get a well-defined discrete flow that we call the randomized geodesic flow , given by the ℤ − \mathbb{Z}- shift on bi-infinite random walk trajectories. The ℤ − \mathbb{Z}- shift is the random analog of the time one map of the geodesic flow. As an analog of ergodicity of the geodesic flow on a closed negatively curved manifold, we establish ergodicity of the G G -action on ( ∂ 2 G , Θ ) (\partial^{2}G,\Theta) . As a consequence of our construction, we prove that the randomized geodesic flow is exponentially mixing of all orders and establish a functional CLT.

1.
Background and Introduction

The goal of this paper is to develop a new framework of randomized geodesic flow in the setting of Gromov-hyperbolic groups by leveraging random walks rather than classical geodesic paths, addressing a long-standing challenge arising from the non-uniqueness of geodesics in this context. In Cayley graphs of hyperbolic groups, unlike negatively curved manifolds, classical (deterministic) geodesic flow runs into the problem of multiple geodesics connecting boundary points. We circumvent this here by considering all bi-infinite random walk trajectories, yielding a fully defined discrete flow via the natural
\mathbb{Z}
-shift on the space of these trajectories. This
\mathbb{Z}
-shift serves as a probabilistic analog of the time-one map of geodesic flow.

One of the central contributions (Theorem
2.1
) is to provide three distinct yet equivalent constructions of the
harmonic Bowen-Margulis-Sullivan measure

\Theta
on the double boundary
\partial^{2}G
: (a) following quasigeodesic rays, (b) along random walk paths, and (c) as a push-forward from a measure defined on bi-infinite random walks.
The third construction, which is technically the most involved, is necessary in order to interpret the system as a genuine random-walk analog of geodesic flow, in that it provides the appropriate global dynamical framework rather than a purely boundary-level description. At the same time, it is not a priori clear that this framework should capture the boundary behavior. In particular, one surprising aspect is that the push-forward of the natural measure on bi-infinite random walk paths turns out to have the desired density with respect to the product of harmonic measures, or equivalently that it yields the harmonic Bowen–Margulis–Sullivan measure on
\partial^{2}G
. Establishing this identification requires new probabilistic and geometric tools and constitutes one of the central conceptual components of the paper.

One first consequence (Theorem
2.3
) of the above constructions is the ergodicity of the
G
-action on
(\partial^{2}G,\Theta)
, generalizing classical ergodic properties of geodesic flow on negatively curved manifolds to the random walk context on hyperbolic groups.

Another consequence of the above constructions that distinguishes our work from previous approaches is the following quantitative aspect: We show that our random walk flow exhibits
strong statistical properties
. Concretely, we prove
exponential mixing of all orders
(Theorem
2.6
) and a central limit theorem
(Theorem
2.7
) for the randomized geodesic flow. These results are in sharp contrast with the deterministic hyperbolic-group setting, where strong mixing properties remain largely inaccessible. Crucially, these limit theorems rely on the aforementioned structural viewpoint developed using the random-walk perspective.

We will now review the relevant literature and outline the main results of the paper, placing our work in context.

1.1.
Motivation and Outline of Results.

The study of geodesic flows on closed negatively curved manifolds side-by-side with Brownian motion or random walks on their universal cover has a rich history. See
[
Kai90
,
Led90
,
Ham97
,
Rob03
,
CDST18
]
for a small, but representative sample. Gromov-hyperbolic groups
G

[
Gro87
]
provide a far-reaching generalization of fundamental groups of
closed negatively curved manifolds. However, extending the notion of geodesic flow to hyperbolic groups
G
is problematic due to non-uniqueness of geodesics between points. In
[
Gro87
, Theorem 8.3C]
, Gromov gives a construction for geodesic flows on hyperbolic groups. However, the associated group action on the resulting flow space is not by isometries in this construction. A related bi-combing was constructed by Mineyev-Monod-Shalom in
[
MMS04
]
, and
a topological analog of the geodesic flow was developed by Mineyev in
[
Min05
]
. A more measure-theoretic version of a geodesic flow on hyperbolic groups was developed by Bader-Furman in

[
BF18
]
building on earlier work of Furman
[
F02
]
: this construction is philosophically related to the present paper.
Roblin’s work
[
Rob03
]
on
CAT(-1)
spaces deserves special mention. A key technical problem addressed in many of these papers is the fact that geodesics between pairs of points in a hyperbolic group are only
coarsely unique, but not unique in general
.

We adopt a completely opposite point of view in this paper, by considering

all possible paths
, thus doing away entirely with the problem of selecting a preferred path. The natural framework that we need to set up thus becomes one of bi-infinite random walks. Once we construct such a framework, geodesic flow (more precisely, the time one map associated with the geodesic flow) gets replaced by a natural shift on the space of parametrized bi-infinite random walk paths.
What we achieve by this construction may be summarized by the following:

(i)

The construction of a flow space
{\mathcal{F}}({\Gamma})
associated to the Cayley graph
{\Gamma}={\Gamma}(G,S)
of a hyperbolic group
G
with respect to a finite generating set. The metric on
{\Gamma}
is the standard word metric with respect to
S
, and each trajectory maps into
\Gamma
.

(ii)

There is a
\mathbb{Z}
-shift on
{\mathcal{F}}({\Gamma})
preserving trajectories.

As pointed out above, the flow space in
[
Gro87
, Section 8]
does not admit an isometric
G-
action. In Mineyev’s construction in
[
Min05
]
, an auxiliary metric needs to be constructed to force geodesics converging to the same point on
the boundary
\partial G
to be asymptotic in a strong sense, thus changing the background simplicial metric.
In
[
BF18
]
, the trajectories are not maps to
{\Gamma}
.
Thus, item (1) above fails in some way or another in these constructions.
We address these issues in the present paper by requiring that each flow line/trajectory in
{\mathcal{F}}({\Gamma})
is a bi-infinite random walk path. Thus, the topological nature of the constructions in

[
Gro87
,
Min05
]
is replaced by a random construction, and the unique auxiliary/abstract trajectory of
[
BF18
]
is replaced by all trajectories that are intrinsic, i.e.
G^{\mathbb{Z}}
.

We mention some consequences of the construction in this paper that underscores the difference of the approach with the earlier ones. There is a natural
left action of
\mathbb{Z}
(the shift) on
G^{\mathbb{Z}}/G
. This action turns out to be mixing once the setup is properly established. We thus deduce a CLT easily. (See Theorems
2.6
and
2.7
below, and Section
7.2
for proofs). This is much unlike the situation in
[
BF18
]
, where only weaker mixing results are known. Weak mixing for the Mineyev topological flow was only recently established in
[
Can25
]
. The conceptual reason behind this difference is that the mathematical source of mixing is vastly different in the present setup. A key ingredient behind mixing of the geodesic flow in the earlier approaches is strong asymptoticity of geodesics converging to a point on the boundary, an approach going back to Hopf, Hedlund and Anosov. This property fails for a typical Cayley graph
{\Gamma}
of a hyperbolic group. In the present paper, mixing is guaranteed on the other hand by randomness. Morally, it goes back to the fact that Brownian motion is mixing on any compact manifold (not necessarily of negative curvature). We thus need an appropriate analog of a finite measure fundamental domain in our setup. This is furnished by
G^{\mathbb{Z}}/G
, which takes the place of the flow space
{\mathcal{F}}({\Gamma})
. Via hyperbolicity, and earlier works on random walks on hyperbolic groups, notably
[
Kai90
]
,
G^{\mathbb{Z}}
gets connected to the boundary of
G
.

This brings us to the intimately related boundary perspective.
A natural boundary measure in the context of
geodesic flow on non-elementary hyperbolic groups is the Patterson-Sullivan measure
[
Pat76
,
Sul79
,
Coo93
]
.
The Patterson-Sullivan measure
\mu

[
Coo93
]
on the boundary
\partial G
of a Gromov-hyperbolic group
G
may be regarded as a limit of the uniform probability distributions
\mu_{n}
on
n-
balls
B_{n}(o)
in a Cayley graph
\Gamma
of
G
with respect to a finite generating set. It
is an example of a conformal density: the group
G
acts on
\partial G
by uniformly quasiconformal automorphisms. Let
v
denote the Hausdorff dimension of
(\partial G,\mu)
, and
\rho
a visual metric on
\partial G
. Then
v
can be identified with the volume entropy of
{\Gamma}
.
Then there exists a natural measure

{\rm{d}}\mu_{\mathrm{BMS}}=\frac{{\rm{d}}\mu\otimes{\rm{d}}\mu}{\rho(x,y)^{2v}}\quad\mbox{on}\quad\partial^{2}G:=\big((\partial G\times\partial G)\setminus\Delta\big),\,\,\Delta=\{(\xi,\xi):\xi\in\partial G\},

called the

Bowen-Margulis-Sullivan
measure. The key feature of
\mu_{\mathrm{BMS}}
is that it is
quasi-invariant
under the
G
-action with uniformly bounded Radon-Nikodym derivatives – that is, there exists
C\geq 1
such that

\frac{{\rm{d}}g_{*}\,\mu_{\mathrm{BMS}}}{{\rm{d}}\mu_{\mathrm{BMS}}}\in[1/C,C]\qquad\mbox{for all $g\in G$}.

The Bowen-Margulis-Sullivan measure may be regarded as the analog in the context of hyperbolic groups
of the Bowen-Margulis measure on the space of geodesics in a closed negatively curved manifold
[
Mar69
,
Bow72
,
Ham89
,
Kai90
]
.

In this paper’s random walk framework, we replace the Patterson-Sullivan measures with harmonic measures. Instead of using the Patterson-Sullivan measure
\mu
, we start with the harmonic measures
\nu_{g}
on the boundary
\partial G
, also known as the hitting measure for a random walk starting at
g\in G
. While harmonic measures correspond to the Poisson boundary of
G
, Kaimanovich’s fundamental work
[
Kai00
, Thm. 7.4]
demonstrates that the Gromov boundary
\partial G
can be identified with the Poisson boundary for a broad class of random walks on
\Gamma
. Let
d_{{\mathcal{G}}}
represent the Green metric on
\Gamma
,
v_{{\mathcal{G}}}
the volume entropy with respect to this metric, and
\rho_{{\mathcal{G}}}
the visual metric on
\partial G
associated with
d_{{\mathcal{G}}}
(see Section
3.5
for more details). The harmonic measures are a conformal density of dimension
v_{{\mathcal{G}}}
. We define the
harmonic Bowen-Margulis-Sullivan measure
\mu_{\mathrm{hBMS}}
as follows:

d\mu_{\mathrm{hBMS}}=\frac{d\nu_{o}\otimes d\nu_{o}}{\rho_{{\mathcal{G}}}(x,y)^{2v_{{\mathcal{G}}}}}.

In this paper, we explore three different ways of constructing
\mu_{\mathrm{hBMS}}
directly in terms of
bi-infinite random walk trajectories
. In Section
4
we set up the main player in the game: bi-infinite random walk trajectories obtained by starting with a random walk trajectory
(X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
with finitely supported and symmetric step distribution, shifting the origin to
X_{m}
, i.e.
(X_{m}^{-1}X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, and letting
m
tend to infinity. We then construct three different measure-equivalent versions of
\mu_{\mathrm{hBMS}}
on
\partial^{2}G
in the following ways:

(1)

We construct
\Theta_{1}
as a limit of occupation measures of bi-infinite random walk trajectories, after fixing a geodesic ray in each direction
\xi\in\partial G
– see Theorem
2.1
, Part (i) and Section
5.1
.

(2)

We construct a more intrinsic
disintegrated version

\Theta_{2}
of the above measure: instead of choosing a geodesic ray in each direction
\xi\in\partial G
, we capture the behavior obtained while moving along
all
random walk paths starting at the identity – see Theorem
2.1
, Part (ii) and Section
5.2
.

(3)

We first construct a natural measure
\mathbb{Q}
on the space
G^{\mathbb{Z}}
of all bi-infinite random walk trajectories (with no restriction on base-point). The measure
\mathbb{Q}
is

•

invariant under the natural
\mathbb{Z}-
shift on trajectories, and admits a quotient
\mathbb{Z}\backslash G^{\mathbb{Z}}
equipped with a natural measure
\widehat{\mathbb{Q}}
inherited from
\mathbb{Q}
. Note that

\mathbb{Z}
acts on the left on
G^{\mathbb{Z}}
.

•

invariant under the natural
G-
action that moves the base-point, and admits a quotient
G^{\mathbb{Z}}/G
equipped with a natural measure
\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{o}
inherited from
\mathbb{Q}
.
Note that
G
acts on the right on
G^{\mathbb{Z}}
.

We then push forward the measure
\widehat{\mathbb{Q}}
under the
boundary map
to obtain a measure
\Theta_{3}
that sends each bi-infinite random walk trajectory to the pair of points in
\partial^{2}G
that it is forward and backward asymptotic to – see Theorem
2.1
, Part (3) and Section
6
. This construction of
\Theta_{3}
allows us to show that for this measure, the
G
-action on
\partial^{2}G
is ergodic – see Theorem
2.1
, Theorem
2.3
and Section
7
.

These constructions, and in particular (3) above, lie at the heart of the present paper. Indeed,
\Theta_{3}
is what justifies the terminology harmonic Bowen-Margulis-Sullivan measure as it reconciles
the internal (
G
) and boundary (
\partial G
) points of view:

(i)

Internally, it provides an interpretation of the measure’s construction as a random walk analogue of the geodesic flow (see below).

(ii)

On the boundary
\partial^{2}G
, it leads to the desired density with respect to
\nu_{o}\otimes\nu_{o}
. Thus
\Theta_{3}
and the harmonic Bowen-Margulis measure
\mu_{\mathrm{hBMS}}
are really the same measure on
\partial^{2}G
(up to a uniformly bounded multiplicative constant).

Note that
\mu_{\mathrm{hBMS}}
can be defined directly on

\partial^{2}G
as soon as one has a conformal density given by the harmonic measure
\nu_{0}

on
\partial G
. It is as a consequence of the above two features of
\Theta_{3}
, that
\mu_{\mathrm{hBMS}}
can indeed be treated as a genuine discrete flow along bi-infinite random walk paths. It is worth noting that the first two methods (1) and (2) also yield constructions of
\mu_{\mathrm{hBMS}}
, despite the fundamentally different approach adopted there. Showing that these differing constructions achieve the same result is the main thrust of Theorem
2.1
below.
These approaches provide different perspectives on the interpretation of
\mu_{\mathrm{hBMS}}
, enriching its theoretical framework.
Demonstrating that these approaches all lead to versions of
\mu_{\mathrm{hBMS}}
is nontrivial and requires new techniques that we outline in Section
2.4
.

We now elaborate on the point (i) above. Indeed, the description in (3) is the random trajectories analog of the geodesic flow on the unit tangent bundle of the universal cover of a closed negatively curved manifold viewed from the boundary. We should remind the reader here that geodesic flow on a hyperbolic group is typically not well-defined due to non-uniqueness of geodesics. In the random walk setup however, we do get a well-defined

discrete flow
given by the
\mathbb{Z}-
shift. This may be regarded as the random walks analog of the cyclic group generated by the time one map of the geodesic flow. Further, since we consider
all

random walk trajectories, we circumvent the problem
of non-uniqueness of geodesics and we have a well-defined

random walk flow
. Thus, we would like to emphasize on the following points:

(a)

The geodesic flow on the (unit tangent bundle to the) universal cover
\widetilde{N}
of a closed negatively curved manifold

N
is well-defined
from the boundary perspective due to uniqueness of geodesics connecting
\xi,\eta\in\partial\widetilde{N}
.

(b)

The
random walk flow
is well-defined
from the boundary perspective for an opposite reason: all random walk trajectories are considered.

Furthermore, while any pair of points

\xi,\eta\in\partial\widetilde{N}
determines a unique bi-infinite geodesic,
\xi,\eta\in\partial G
determines a whole family of random walk trajectories.

Finally, we note that the bi-infinite random walks studied in the present work differ from the classical view of bi-infinite random walks. Indeed, as will be explained in Section
4.1
, our bi-infinite random walks are constructed by iteratively shifting back a random walk
(X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
. Hence, the distribution
\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{o}
of our bi-infinite random walk (see (
2.2
) below and Section
4.1
) is equivalent to
\mathbb{P}_{o}\otimes\mathbb{P}_{o}
, the product measure of two independent random walks started at
o
. The classical view on the other hand regards bi-infinite random walks as loops through the point at infinity in the one-point compactification of the space. We refer to Appendix
A
for a more detailed discussion on this difference, both conceptual and technical, between these two notions of bi-infinite random walk paths and the associated distributions and to Example
A
for an explicit computation illustrating this difference in the case of a free group.

2.1.
Random Walk Constructions of
\Theta
and Their Properties

We are now ready to state the first main result of our paper:

Theorem 2.1

There exists a measure
\Theta
on
\partial^{2}G
satisfying

\frac{{\rm{d}}\Theta}{{\rm{d}}(\nu_{o}\otimes\nu_{o})}(\xi,\eta)\asymp{\rm e}^{2(\xi,\eta)_{o}^{{\mathcal{G}}}}\qquad\forall(\xi,\eta)\in\partial^{2}G,

(2.5)

which can be constructed explicitly in the following three measure-equivalent ways:

(i)

As the measure
\Theta_{1}
given by

\Theta_{1}(A)=\int_{A}\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{z=-n}^{n}\bigg[\frac{1}{2n+1}\,\frac{{\rm{d}}(\nu_{p_{z}}\otimes\nu_{p_{z}})}{{\rm{d}}(\nu_{o}\otimes\nu_{o})}(\xi,\eta)\bigg]\;{\rm{d}}(\nu_{o}\otimes\nu_{o})(\xi,\eta)

\eta

(ii)

As a disintegrated version
\Theta_{2}
of
\Theta_{1}
, defined by

\Theta_{2}(A)=\int_{A}\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{z=-n}^{n}\bigg[\frac{1}{2n+1}\,\frac{{\rm{d}}(\nu_{x_{z}}\otimes\nu_{x_{z}})}{{\rm{d}}(\nu_{o}\otimes\nu_{o})}(\xi,\eta)\bigg]\;{\rm{d}}\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{o}\big((x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\big),

for all

As the push forward

2.2.
Randomized Geodesic Flow, Exponential Mixing and Functional CLT

Definition 2.2
.

Let
(\tau_{z})_{z\in\mathbb{Z}}

\mathbb{Z}

, or

.

Here is our next main result.

Theorem 2.3

(Double Ergodicity)

The

\mathbb{Z}\backslash G^{\mathbb{Z}}
, and the
\mathbb{Z}

\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{o}

The above theorem is proved in Section

(i)

Definition 2.4

Let
G
be a group acting on a probability space
(X,\mu)
and let
r\geq 2
be an integer.

(1)

We say that the action of
G
on
(X,\mu)
is
mixing of order
r\geq 2
, if for all
f_{1},...,f_{r}\in L^{\infty}(X,\mu)
, and for all
g_{1},g_{2},\dots,g_{r}\in G
we have

\bigg|\int_{X}\prod_{i=1}^{r}(f_{i}\circ g_{i}){\rm{d}}\mu-\prod_{i=1}^{r}\int_{X}f_{i}{\rm{d}}\mu\bigg|\to 0\qquad\mbox{as $\min_{\ell\neq k}\,d(g_{\ell},g_{k})\to\infty$}.

(2)

We fix an
G
-invariant sub-algebra
\mathcal{A}
of
L^{\infty}(X,\mu)
, and a family
N=(N_{s})_{s\in\mathbb{N}}
of semi-norms on
\mathcal{A}
. Then we say that the action of the group
G
on
(X,\mu)
is
exponentially mixing of order
r
with respect to
d
and
(\mathcal{A},N)
, if there exist
\delta_{r}>0
and an integer
s_{r}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
s>s_{r}
,
f_{1},...,f_{r}\in\mathcal{A}
and
g_{1},\dots,g_{r}\in G
,

\bigg|\int_{X}\prod_{i=1}^{r}(f_{i}\circ g_{i}){\rm{d}}\mu-\prod_{i=1}^{r}\int_{X}f_{i}{\rm{d}}\mu\bigg|\leq C{\rm e}^{-\delta_{r}d_{r}(g_{1},..,g_{r})}\prod_{i=1}^{r}N_{s}(f_{i})

for some finite constant
C=C(s,r)
. In this case,
\delta_{r}>0
is called
the rate of mixing
.

Definition 2.5
.

A function
f:X\to\mathbb{R}
on an infinite product space
X=\prod_{n\in\mathbb{N}}X_{n}
of metric spaces
(X_{n},d_{n})
is Hölder continuous with respect to the standard metric on
X
given by

d(x,y)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}2^{-n}\min\{1,d_{n}(x_{n},y_{n})\},

if there exists
\alpha>0
such that

\absolutevalue{f(x)-f(y)}\leq d(x,y)^{\alpha}\quad\text{for all }x,y\in X.

Here is our next main result showing exponential mixing and a functional central limit theorem (CLT) for our random walk flow from Definition
2.2
.

Theorem 2.6

(Exponential Mixing of all orders)

The
\mathbb{Z}
-action on
(G_{o}^{\mathbb{Z}},\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{o})
, i.e. the randomized geodesic flow from Definition
2.2
, is mixing of all orders. Moreover, for any
\alpha>0
, this action is also exponentially mixing of all orders with respect to the class
\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}
of Hölder continuous functions of exponent
\alpha>0
.

Theorem 2.7

(Functional CLT)

For any Hölder continuous function
f:G^{\mathbb{Z}}_{o}\to\mathbb{R}
, there exists
\sigma^{2}_{f}>0
such that

\frac{\sum_{z=1}^{m}f\big(\tau_{z}(X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\big)-m\mathbb{E}^{\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{o}}[f\big((X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}})\big]}{\sqrt{m}}\to{\mathcal{N}}(0,\sigma^{2}_{f})\qquad\mbox{as $m\to\infty$,}

in distribution under
\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{0}
. Here
{\mathcal{N}}(0,\sigma^{2}_{f})
denotes the centered Gaussian law with variance
\sigma^{2}_{f}
.

Theorem
2.6
and Theorem
2.7
are proved in Section
7.2
.

2.3.
Context

As mentioned before, the aim of this paper is to develop a harmonic analog of the Bowen-Margulis measure on
\partial^{2}G
from an
internal
(as opposed to boundary) point of view, i.e from the point of view of the collection of bi-infinite random walk trajectories on a Cayley graph
{\Gamma}
of
G
. Since the Bowen-Margulis measure has been
a long-studied object in ergodic theory, we provide an eclectic survey of the literature under a few heads below to provide context for the work in this paper.

Geodesic flow on hyperbolic groups:
We have already said that the key motivation for this paper was to develop a bi-infinite random-walks analog of geodesic flow on hyperbolic groups in the spirit of Gromov’s topological construction
[
Gro87
, Section 8]
. The papers
[
Min05
,
MMS04
,
CT24
]
amongst others have pursued and considerably developed
the topological construction suggested by Gromov. Our emphasis in this paper is probabilistic and involves, by contrast, random trajectories.

The Hopf-Tsuji-Sullivan theorem:
A historical context in which the Bowen-Margulis measure frequently comes up is the Hopf-Tsuji-Sullivan dichotomy that asserts that the (deterministic) geodesic flow is either conservative and ergodic or dissipative. We refer the reader to

[
Ham89
,
Kai94
,
CP94
,
BM96
,
F02
,
Rob03
,
Ric17
,
Lin18
,
BF18
,
CDST18
,
Cou23
]
for some of the relevant work. Of these papers,
[
Ham89
,
Kai94
,
CP94
,
BM96
,
Rob03
,
Ric17
,
Lin18
]
deal with
Hadamard manifolds, or more generally CAT(0) spaces. In particular, a standing assumption in each of these is the uniqueness of geodesic segments connecting any pair of points. The only Cayley graph per se that satisfies the CAT(0) condition is that of a free group with standard generating set. The present paper, on the other hand, deals exclusively with Cayley graphs of non-elementary hyperbolic groups and not with CAT(0) spaces.

The papers
[
BF18
,
CDST18
]
do away with the CAT(0) assumption and prove the Hopf-Tsuji-Sullivan theorem for hyperbolic spaces and
[
Cou23
]
proves it for actions of acylindrically hyperbolic groups. Kaimanovich’s papers
[
Kai90
,
Kai94
]
go beyond
the classical Bowen-Margulis measure associated with the geodesic flow:
for certain Markov operators
P
,
an invariant measure of the geodesic
flow called the
invariant harmonic measure associated with
P
is defined in
[
Kai94
]
, and the
Hopf-Tsuji-Sullivan dichotomy for these is established. However, a crucial assumption in
[
Kai94
]
is uniqueness of geodesics joining a pair of boundary points (denoted Property
U
in that paper). Similarly, in
[
Kai90
]
it is assumed that the underlying manifold has negative curvature and hence
there is a unique geodesic joining a pair of boundary points.
This property breaks down fairly drastically in the context of Cayley graphs of most hyperbolic groups, where typically there are infinitely many infinite geodesic bi-gons between pairs of points on the boundary.

We reiterate that all these papers, unlike the present one, deal with the (deterministic) geodesic
flow.

Action on the double boundary:
We now deal with precursors to the last statement of Theorem
2.1
, i.e. double ergodicity. The papers of Bader and Furman

[
F02
,
BF18
]
cited above proves that the action of
G
on
\partial^{2}G
equipped with the Bowen-Margulis measure is ergodic for
G
hyperbolic.
Perhaps more relevant to the present paper is
[
Kai03
]
.
In
[
Kai03
]
, Kaimanovich proves an extremely general double ergodicity result
on the square of the Poisson boundary of a locally compact
second countable group. The papers
[
F02
,
BF18
,
Kai03
]
adopt a point of view intrinsic to the boundary, the former for the (deterministic) geodesic flow and the latter
for random walks. In the discussion following
[
BHM11
, Proposition 5.4]
it is pointed out that once the harmonic measure has been constructed a similar double ergodicity result may be obtained by weighting the product measure in a way similar to (
2.5
). These papers thus deal directly with
\partial^{2}G
without
considering actual trajectories (geodesics or bi-infinite random walks)
in the Cayley graph
{\Gamma}
.
The constructions of
\Theta
in Theorem
2.1
follow a
complementary approach by going to
\partial^{2}G
from inside the space. This is the main difference in the approach
in the present paper.

Our results, too, have a subtly different flavor. We briefly delineate the main technical difference between
the main result of
[
Kai03
]
and Theorem
2.1
.
Consider a symmetric driving measure
\mu
. Double ergodicity is established in
[
Kai03
]
with respect to the product of the
G
-invariant measures
\nu_{m}=\sum_{g\in G}\nu_{g}
. In the language of the present work, this aligns with looking at the ergodicity of the push-forward of the measure
\mathbb{Q}
(defined in (
2.3
)) under the boundary map to
\partial^{2}G
. In Theorem
2.1
we consider instead the push-forward of
\widehat{\mathbb{Q}}
(also defined in (
2.3
)). It is the push-forward of
\widehat{\mathbb{Q}}
that is used to construct
\Theta_{3}
. The ergodicity of the measure
\nu_{m}\otimes\nu_{m}
under the
G
-action on
\partial^{2}G
follows from the ergodicity of
\Theta_{3}
. The latter is shown in Section
7
. Note that the
G
- and
Z
-action on
G^{\mathbb{Z}}
commute (see (

)). Now look at the pre-image of a
G
-invariant subset of
\partial^{2}G
in
G^{\mathbb{Z}}
. This gives a
G
-invariant set under the induced action on the fundamental domain. By ergodicity of
\widehat{\mathbb{Q}}
(Theorem

), this set is either of measure
0
or of full measure. Hence the same holds for
\mathbb{Q}=\sum_{z\in\mathbb{Z}}\tau_{z\ast}\widehat{\mathbb{Q}}
and for
\nu_{m}\otimes\nu_{m}
. (Recall that the latter is the push forward of
\mathbb{Q}
under the boundary map.)

Note however that
\nu_{m}
is infinite on any set
B\subset\partial G
with
\nu_{o}(B)>0
and
0
on all other sets. The product
\nu_{m}\otimes\nu_{m}
is thus in a different measure class from

. We can of course restrict
\mathbb{Q}
to a fundamental domain of the
\mathbb{Z}

and push forward the restricted measure to the boundary (as done in Section

Kai03
]
.
However, this does not provide information about the existence of its density with respect to

. The relation to
\Theta_{1}
and
\Theta_{2}

is also unavailable from this approach.
To obtain these, we need the ideas developed in Sections

and
6.4

\Theta_{3}
, establishing the correct density with respect to

Harmonic functions on groups:

In the related literature on harmonic functions on groups, the action on the double boundary makes a somewhat tangentially related appearance in the context of bi-harmonic functions

Kai92

2.4.
Key Technical Input in the Proofs

For the convenience of the reader, we now summarize the main technical constituents of the proofs.

To study a flow along bi-infinite random walk paths, we need to define what these paths are.
In Section
4
we will construct these bi-infinite random walk paths, and deduce some important properties of these. Concretely, in Section
4.1
the bi-infinite random walk will be constructed by taking a random walk
(X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
started at
o
and successively shifting it back by
X_{n}
(the position at time
n
) to obtain a bi-infinite path. The reason to consider this notion of bi-infinite random walk paths is that it preserves the distribution of geometrical properties of the original random walk. These properties will be crucially used subsequently.

In particular, in Section
4.2
we study the long term behavior of these bi-infinite random walks
(X_{z})_{z\in\mathbb{Z}}
and prove estimates on its distance to the geodesics. Studying the deviation of a (standard/semi-infinite) random walk trajectory
(Y_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
from geodesics goes back to Kaimanovich, who showed that the distance of
Y_{n}
to
[o,Y_{\infty})
is almost surely
o(n)
in
[
Kai00
, Thm. 7.2]
. Later, Blachère, Haïssinsky and Mathieu refined this result and showed that the distance is of order
\log(n)
in
[
BHM11
, Cor. 3.9]
.

In this regard, in Theorem
4.3
we will first study the case of standard/semi-infinite random walk paths
(Y_{n})_{n\geq 0}
and show deviation bounds for fixed points
Y_{n}
. The upper bound on the probability in Theorem
4.3
, Part (1), was already shown in
[
BHM11
, Lem. 3.8]
, while the corresponding lower bound is new. Note that due to the very minimal assumptions on the driving measure
\mu
in
[
BHM11
]
, they need to add the assumption that
d_{{\mathcal{G}}}
is hyperbolic. In the case that
\mu
is symmetric and has finite support, the latter is always true.

These estimates are then used to study the joint behavior of two independent random walks – see Lemma
4.4
. We can use this result to show that the two hitting points
X_{-\infty}
and
X_{\infty}
are almost surely distinct (Corollary
4.5
). Consequently, the behavior of our bi-infinite random walk
(X_{z})_{z\in\mathbb{Z}}
can be studied and the asymptotic behavior of the distance of
X_{n}
to a geodesic
(X_{-\infty},X_{\infty})
can be made precise – see Corollary
4.6
. This also justifies our point of view of considering bi-infinite random walk trajectories as generalized geodesics.

In Theorem
4.7
we show a more refined version of the above bounds: we prove the validity of the bounds when conditioning on hitting a certain point on the boundary. Here it is imperative that we have both the upper and lower bound obtained in Theorem
4.3
. These refined estimates are later used for proving the statements about the measure
\Theta_{2}
appearing in Theorem
2.1
.

In Section
5
, we turn to the first two constructions of the measures mentioned in Theorem
2.1
. In Section
5.1
we define and study the measure
\Theta_{1}
on

\partial^{2}G
as defined in (
2.6
) and show that the sum there converges to a measure which is

(i)

quasi-invariant under the
G
-action on
\partial^{2}G
induced from the
G
-action on
\partial G
(cf. Lemma
5.2
) and

(ii)

has the desired density with respect to
\nu_{o}\otimes\nu_{o}
(cf. Lemma
5.3
)

This measure depends however on a choice of quasi-geodesic rays in
(a Cayley graph of)
G
. We would like to remove this choice. Hence, in Section
5.2
we move on to the construction of
\Theta_{2}
defined in (
2.7
). The measure
\Theta_{2}
is defined by considering bi-infinite random walk paths and does not depend on a specific choice of quasi-geodesic rays. In order to show that
\Theta_{2}
has the desired density with respect to
(\nu_{o}\otimes\nu_{o})
(in particular that it satisfies the lower bound), we need to apply the aforementioned refined estimates from Theorem
4.7
. We refer to Lemma
5.4
and Lemma
5.5
for details.

Section
6
is devoted to constructing and deducing properties of the measure
\Theta_{3}(\cdot)=\widehat{\mathbb{Q}}({\rm bd}^{-1}(\cdot))
with
\widehat{\mathbb{Q}}
from (
2.3
). In Section
6.1
we show that the measure
\mathbb{Q}
is invariant both under the action of
\mathbb{Z}
and
G
on
G^{\mathbb{Z}}
. This invariance will then carry over to the same property for
\Theta_{3}
on
\partial^{2}G
under the action of
G
.

The main technical difficulty then involves showing that the measure
\Theta_{3}
has the correct density, as stated in Theorem
6.2
. Due to the poorly connected nature of free groups, the proof is much easier in the case that
G
is a free group. In Section
6.3
we will outline the guiding philosophy for the proof of Theorem
6.2
for this case. Despite its simplicity, this example serves as an important illustration in two key aspects. First, it demonstrates why the measure
\Theta_{3}
should possess the desired density relative to
\nu_{o}\otimes\nu_{o}
. Second, it provides insight as to why the method below for bounding the density should yield the correct result.

To make the second point point clearer, we note that, in the tree case, the only contribution to the density
\frac{d\Theta_{3}}{d(\nu_{o}\otimes\nu_{o})}(\xi,\eta)
comes from trajectories of the random walk with origins that lie along the path where the geodesics
[o,\xi)
and
[o,\eta)
run parallel. The exact value of the density then comes from the conformal nature of the hitting measures
(\nu_{g})_{g\in G}
.

Section
6.4
and Section
6.5
constitute the proof of Theorem
6.2
for general hyperbolic groups. In this setting, we can no longer assume that removing a ball of a certain size disconnects points. Due to the geometric nature of hyperbolic groups, removing a ball significantly increases the distance between points on opposite sides of the ball. So a random walk started on one side of a ball is much less likely to hit a point on the opposite side of the ball when forced to walk around the ball. This heuristic will be used in a crucial way in the sequel. We will elaborate this point now.

This fact is in particular important in showing the upper bound for the density in Proposition
6.7
. The main challenge in this proof is that in
\widehat{\mathbb{Q}}
we are potentially summing over something which is positive for all group elements. So, in order to show the upper bound for the density
\frac{{\rm{d}}\Theta_{3}}{{\rm{d}}\nu_{o}\otimes\nu_{o}}(\xi,\eta)
in the case of a general hyperbolic group, we need to show that the contributions to
\widehat{\mathbb{Q}}
of starting points away from the region where
[o,\xi)
and
[o,\eta)
lie very close to each other, decay sufficiently fast.
A key estimate in showing this deals with how much forcing the random walk to walk around a ball reduces the probability.
Estimates similar in spirit have been considered for the weighted Green function by Gouëzel and Lalley for surface groups in
[
GL13
, Lem. 4.4]
and by Gouëzel for general hyperbolic groups in
[
G14
, Lem. 2.6]
. They showed that the probability of walking from
x
to
y
while staying outside a ball of radius
n
centered on
[x,y]
decays as
\exp(c\e^{-\varepsilon n})
for some
c,\varepsilon>0
. It is however by no means obvious how to apply this estimate to show that the probabilities in
\widehat{\mathbb{Q}}
decay fast enough to end up with the claim.
Indeed, we need to refine their statements in two main ways in Lemma
6.3
.

(1)

We generalize to a ball not necessarily centered on
[x,y]
. In other words, our study will extend the previous findings by considering cases where the center
o
of the ball that gets removed does not lie on the geodesic between
x
and
y
.

(2)

Further, we are also able get a bound dependent on the exact distance of
y
to the center
o
of the ball, not just the fact that is more than
n
away from
o
. This part will require an application of the Ancona inequality.

These arguments, developed in Lemma
6.3
, Lemma
6.4
and Lemma
6.5
, effectively provide us with estimates for the hitting distribution on a small neighborhood of a geodesic
[o,\xi)
of the random walk started at
x
on the boundary of
B_{n}(o)
when forced to walk around this ball. This in turn enables us to compare the probability of hitting
x
when restricting the random walk to staying outside
B_{n}(o)
with the unrestricted case.

We note that the bounds mentioned above depend on the angle the random walk has to walk around the ball. When this angle gets very small, we may lose the aforementioned double exponential decay. So even with the refined estimates developed in the above lemmas in Section
6.4
, we then still have to be very careful in applying them in the Proof of Proposition
6.7
. More concretely, we will need to

(a)

split the group apart into several domains judiciously, and

(b)

carefully estimate how many points can lie in each of these domains.

Throughout these estimates it is crucial that, via the Bonk-Schramm embedding, we may embed the Cayley graph of the group into
\mathbb{H}^{d}
. This allows us to invoke more explicit geometric knowledge about
\mathbb{H}^{d}
than the abstract geometric knowledge we have about the group. We refer to the proof of Proposition
6.7
in Section
6.5
for details. The lower bound of Theorem
6.2
, shown in Proposition
6.6
, is easier. The proof is similar to the case of the free group in Section
6.3
. It proceeds by summing over origins that roughly lie on the shortest geodesic between
o
and the geodesic
(\xi,\eta)
. This will complete the proof of Theorem
6.2
.

Section
7
constitutes the proof of ergodicity of
\Theta
as well as the exponential mixing and the CLT of our random walk flow. For this purpose, we will first prove Theorem
2.3
, namely that the measures
\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{o}
and
\widehat{\mathbb{Q}}
are ergodic for the flow along the random walk paths and the
G
-shift on the paths respectively. The ergodicity of
\Theta_{3}
, and hence
\Theta

For proving Theorem
2.3
our approach builds on a philosophy related to Bader and Furman
[
F02

BF18

. However, while they regard

slightly misleading to think of geodesics as parametrized by

This difference also means that in our case the fundamental domain for the

3.1.
Measure Theoretic Terminology

We first start with some measure theoretic terminology which will be used in the sequel.

Definition 3.1
.

A measure
\mu
on a measure space
(X,{\mathcal{F}})
is said to be
invariant
for a group action
G
on
X
, if for all
A\in{\mathcal{F}}
and all
g\in G,\,\mu(A)=\mu(gA)
.

The measure
\mu
is said to be
quasi-invariant
, if
\mu
and
g_{\ast}\mu
are in the same measure class for all
g\in G
, i.e. if they are mutually absolutely continuous.

A quasi-invariant measure is called
ergodic
with respect to the action of
G
, if for all invariant sets
A\in{\mathcal{F}}
, i.e. sets
A
with

\mu(A\backslash gA)+\mu(gA\backslash A)=0\,\,\ \forall\,g\in G,

either
\mu(A)=0
or
\mu(A^{c})=0
.

Remark 1

Note that ergodicity is in fact just a property of the measure class of
\mu
, since all measures in the same measure class have the same zero-sets. Hence ergodicity of any particular quasi-invariant measure determines the
ergodicity of any quasi-invariant measure in the same measure class. In particular, ergodicity of an invariant measure determines the
ergodicity of any quasi-invariant measure in the same measure class.

Theorem 3.2

(Disintegration of measures)
Let
(\Omega,\mathbb{P})
and
(\Omega^{\prime},\bf{P})
be two probability spaces and assume that there exists a map
\pi:\Omega\to\Omega^{\prime}
such that
\bf{P}=\pi_{\ast}\mathbb{P}
. Then there exist a family of probability measures
(\mathbb{P}_{x})_{x\in\Omega^{\prime}}
such that

\mathbb{P}_{x}(\Omega\backslash\pi^{-1}(x))=0

and for any measurable function
g:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
we have

\int_{\Omega}g(y){\rm{d}}\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{o}(y)=\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}\int_{\pi^{-1}(x)\crossproduct{\rm bd}^{-1}(\eta)}g(y){\rm{d}}\mathbb{P}_{x}(y){\rm{d}}\bf{P}(x).

In this section we will now recall some elementary facts about Gromov-hyperbolic groups and refer the reader to
[

GdlH90
,

3.5.

Let

[

4.
Bi-infinite Random Walk Paths and Deviations

In Section

4.1.
The Bi-infinite Random Walk

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
(X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a random walk on a hyperbolic group
G
with symmetric step distribution
\mu
whose support is finite and generates
G
and starting point
X_{0}=o
. Fix
m\in\mathbb{N}
and set

\displaystyle\bar{Z}^{m}_{n}

\displaystyle:=X_{m}^{-1}X_{m-n}

\displaystyle\text{ for }n=0,...,m;

\displaystyle Z^{m}_{n}

\displaystyle:=X_{m}^{-1}X_{m+n}

\displaystyle\text{ for }n\geq m

Then the following hold:

•

\big(\bar{Z}^{m}_{n}\big)_{n=0}^{m}
and
\big(Z^{m}_{n}\big)_{i=0}^{\infty}
are independent for any
m\in\mathbb{N}
.

•

Furthermore, they converge in distribution to independent random walks
\big(\bar{Z}_{n}\big)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
\big(Z_{n}\big)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
with step distribution
\mu
and starting point
o
.

•

Finally,
\big(\bar{Z}_{n}\big)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
\big(Z_{n}\big)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converge a.s to points
Z_{\infty}
and
Z_{-\infty}
on
\partial G
.

Proof.

Let
W_{1},W_{2},...\in S
be the increments of the original random walk
(X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, i.e.
X_{n}=W_{1}\cdot...\cdot W_{n}
. Then for
n=1,...,m

\displaystyle\bar{Z}^{m}_{n}=X_{m}^{-1}X_{m-n}

\displaystyle=(W_{1}\cdot...\cdot W_{n})^{-1}Y_{1}\cdot...\cdot W_{m-n}\cdot

\displaystyle=W_{n}^{-1}\cdot...\cdot W_{1}^{-1}\cdot W_{1}\cdot...\cdot W_{n-m}=W_{n}^{-1}\cdot...\cdot W_{n-m+1}^{-1}

and similarly

\displaystyle Z^{m}_{n}=X_{m}^{-1}X_{m+n}

\displaystyle=(W_{1}\cdot...\cdot W_{n})^{-1}\cdot W_{1}\cdot...\cdot W_{m+n}=W_{m+1}\cdot...\cdot W_{m+n}.

This means that
(\bar{Z}^{m}_{n})_{n=0}^{m}
only depends on
W_{1},...,W_{m}
and
(Z^{m}_{n})_{n=0}^{\infty}
on
(W_{n})_{n\geq m}
. Since the increments
W_{1},W_{2},...
are independent,
(\bar{Z}^{m}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
(Z^{m}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
are independent for every fixed
m\in\mathbb{N}
, and hence this also holds when we take the limit in distribution.

Furthermore, since the increments are identically distributed, for any
m\in\mathbb{N}
the distribution of
(Z^{m}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is the one of a random walk with step distribution
\mu
and origin
o
. Since this holds independently from
m
, the convergence to
(Z_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
holds trivially.

Finally, since
\mu
is symmetric,
W_{i}
has the same distribution as
W_{i}^{-1}
, i.e.
(\bar{Z}^{m}_{n})_{n=0}^{m}
has the same distribution as the first
m
steps of a random walk.
We extend its distribution to
G^{\mathbb{N}}
by taking the product with
\mathbbm{1}_{G}
for each of the missing steps. Then on any cylinder set this extended distribution converges to the one of a random walk with step distribution
\mu
and origin
o
. So, the extended measure converges to
(\bar{Z}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
.
∎

Note that in the same way we can also construct a bi-infinite random walk with any other fixed origin

.

Remark 5
We note that Lemma

shows that by successively shifting back the random walk

We also note that Definition

(i)

It can be seen as a bi-infinite sequence

, whose distribution we denote by

(ii)

These two different points of view will be helpful when studying the bi-infinite random walk in the sequel.

4.2.
Deviation from the Geodesic

The goal of this section is to study the long term behavior of the bi-infinite random walks
(X_{z})_{z\in\mathbb{Z}}
defined in the previous section. First in Theorem
4.3
we first study the case of a usual/ non-bi-infinite random walk
(Y_{n})_{n\geq 0}
and show deviation bounds for fixed points
Y_{n}
. These bounds can then be used to study the joint behavior of two independent random walks – see Lemma
4.4
. We can use this result to show that the two hitting points
X_{-\infty}
and
X_{\infty}
are almost surely distinct – see Corollary
4.5
. Consequently, the behavior of our bi-infinite random walk can be studied and in particular the asymptotic behavior of the distance of
X_{n}
to a geodesic
(X_{-\infty},X_{\infty})
can be quantified – see Corollary
4.6
, which also justifies our point of view of considering bi-infinite random walk trajectories as generalized geodesics.

Theorem 4.3

Let
(Y_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a usual random walk started at
o
with step distribution
\mu
.

(1)

For any
D\geq 0
and
n\geq 0
sufficiently large, there exists
C_{D}>1
such that

C_{D}^{-1}{\rm e}^{-D}\leq\mathbb{P}_{o}\big[d_{{\mathcal{G}}}\big(Y_{n},[o,Y_{\infty})\big)\geq D\big]\leq C_{D}{\rm e}^{-D}.

The constants
C_{D}
are bounded in
D
.

(2)

This means in particular that for
n>0
big enough also

\mathbb{P}_{o}\big[\big(Y_{n},Y_{\infty}\big)^{{\mathcal{G}}}_{o}<D\big]\ \lesssim{\rm e}^{-D}

Proof.

(1): Conditioning on the position of the random walk at time
n
, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{o}\Big[d_{{\mathcal{G}}}\big(Y_{n},[o,Y_{\infty})\big)\geq D\Big]

\displaystyle=\sum_{y\in G}\mathbb{P}_{o}\Big[d_{{\mathcal{G}}}\big(Y_{n},[o,Y_{\infty})\big)\geq D,Y_{n}=y\Big]

(4.3)

\displaystyle=\sum_{y\in G}\mathbb{P}_{o}\Big[d_{{\mathcal{G}}}\big(y,[0,yY_{n}^{-1}Y_{\infty})\big)\geq D\Big|Y_{n}=y\Big]\mathbb{P}_{o}[Y_{n}=y]

\displaystyle=\sum_{y\in G}\mathbb{P}_{y}\Big[d_{{\mathcal{G}}}\big(y,[o,Y_{\infty})\big)\geq D\Big]\mathbb{P}_{o}[Y_{n}=y]

where we shifted
Y_{\infty}
back by
Y_{n}^{-1}
, to get independence from
Y_{n}
and then used that
yY_{n}^{-1}Y_{\infty}
has the same distribution as the hitting distribution of a random walk started at
y
.

In order to prove the bounds in the claim we hence want to show that
\mathbb{P}_{x}[d(x,[o,Y_{\infty}))\geq D]
fulfills the same exponential bounds. This will be done by showing that there exist
p_{l},p_{u}\in G
and
R_{l},R_{u}>0
such that

\{Y_{\infty}\in\mho_{y}(p_{l},R_{l})\}\subseteq\{d(y,[o,Y_{\infty}))\geq D\}\subseteq\{Y_{\infty}\in\mho_{y}(p_{u},R_{u})\}.

(4.4)

We can then use the Shadow Lemma
3.9
, which says that for
y,p\in G
and
R>R_{0}
we have

\mathbb{P}_{y}[Y_{\infty}\in\mho_{y}(p,R)]\asymp{\rm e}^{-d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(p,y)}={\rm e}^{-D-\tilde{c}}

to get the desired bounds.

So let us first prove the second inclusion in (
4.4
). On the event
\{d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(y,[o,Y_{\infty}))\geq D\}
we have in particular
d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(y,o)>D
, so we can choose
p\in[o,y]
such that
d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(p,x)=D+\tilde{c}
, where
\tilde{c}
is a small constant stemming from the fact that by the coarse geometric nature of
(G,d_{{\mathcal{G}}})
there might not exist a point at distance exactly
D
from
x
. Then we consider the triangle
\Delta(o,y,Y_{\infty})
. By hyperbolicity of
G
, we know that we may map the triangle to a comparison tripod by a
(1,c)-
quasi-isometry
T
. Let
d_{T}
be the metric on the tripod,
m
the centroid of the tripod and denote by
m_{0}\in[y,Y_{\infty})
the point whose image under
T
is closest to
m
. Further we denote by
p_{1},p_{2}
and
k_{1},k_{2}
the two possible positions of the images of points
p\in[o,y]
and
k\in[o,Y_{\infty}]
under
T
in relation to the centroid
m
.

We will now turn to proving the upper bound. We want to show, that there exists
R>0
independent of
y,p
and
D
, such that
[y,Y_{\infty})
passes through
B_{R}(y)
, i.e.
Y_{\infty}\in\mho_{y}(p,R)
.

Now, we have either
d_{T}(T(y),T(p))\leq d_{T}(T(y),m)
or
d_{T}(T(y),T(p)))>d_{T}(T(y),m)
. In the first case, corresponding to
p_{1}
in
2
, it follows directly that
d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(p,[y,Y_{\infty}))<2\delta
, since by construction the sides that get mapped onto the same arm of the tripod, are less than
2\delta
apart.
In the second case we have

D\leq d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(y,[o,Y_{\infty}))\leq d_{T}(T(y),m)+c

meaning in particular that by the definition of the metric
d_{T}

D-c\leq d_{T}(T(y),m)=d_{T}(T(y),T(p))-d_{T}(m,T(p))\leq d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(y,p)-d_{T}(m,T(p)).

Hence, plugging in that
d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(y,p)=D+\tilde{c}

d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(m_{0},p)\leq d_{T}(m,p)+c\leq\tilde{c}+2c.

Hence, in both cases,
[y,Y_{\infty})
passes through
B_{R}(p)
for
R=\tilde{c}+2c
. So
\{d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(y,[o,Y_{\infty}])\geq D\}\subset\{X_{\infty}(x)\in\mho_{y}(p,R)\}
and hence

\mathbb{P}_{y}[d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(y,[o,Y_{\infty}])\geq D]\leq\mathbb{P}_{y}[X_{\infty}\in\mho_{y}(p,R)]\asymp e^{-d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(y,p)}=e^{-D-\tilde{c}}.

Plugging this into (
4.3
), we get the upper bound in the claim.

For the lower bound, let
Y_{\infty}\in\mho_{y}(p,R)
for points
y,o,p\in G
such that
p\in[y,o],d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(o,y)>D
, and
d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(y,p)=D+\tilde{c}
where
\tilde{c}
again comes from the fact that there might not be a point exactly at distance
D
. We again regard the triangle
\Delta(y,o,Y_{\infty})
and switch to the corresponding comparison tripod, and let
m
be the centroid of the tripod. We again regard the two cases
d_{T}(T(y),T(p))<d_{T}(T(y),m)
and
d_{T}(T(y),T(p))<d_{T}(T(y),m)
.

In the first case, we get that for any point
k\in[o,Y_{\infty})
that by quasi-isometry of
d_{T}
and
d_{{\mathcal{G}}}

d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(y,k)\geq d_{T}(T(y),m)-c\geq d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(y,m)-2c.

In the second case we use the fact that
Y_{\infty}
is in the shadow
\mho_{y}(p,R)
, i.e. there exists
p_{i}\in[x,Y_{\infty})
such that
d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(p_{i},p)\leq R
. Then

d_{T}(T(p),m)\leq d_{T}(T(p),T(p_{i}))\leq d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(p_{i},p)+c\leq R+c

and hence again for any point
k\in[o,Y_{\infty})

\displaystyle d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(x,k)

\displaystyle\geq d_{T}(T(y),T(k))-c=d_{T}(T(y),m)+d_{T}(m,T(k))-c=

\displaystyle=d_{T}(T(y),T(p))-d_{T}(T(p),m)+d_{T}(m,T(k))-c\geq d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(x,p)-(R+2c)

and hence
d(x,[o,Y_{\infty}(x)))\geq D-D_{0}
where
D_{0}=R+2c+\tilde{c}
. Thus we have shown that

\{d(x,[o,Y_{\infty}(x)))\geq D-D_{0}\}\subset\{Y_{\infty}\in\mho_{y}(p,R)\}

So in particular

{\rm e}^{-D-c}\asymp\mathbb{P}[X_{\infty}(x)\in\mho_{x}(p,R)]\leq\mathbb{P}_{x}[d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(x,[o,X_{\infty}))\geq D-D_{0}]

and thus

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{o}[d(X_{n},[o,X_{\infty}))\geq D]

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in G}\mathbb{P}_{x}[d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(x,[0,X_{\infty}(x)))\geq D]\mathbb{P}_{o}[X_{n}=x]

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{x\in G,d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(x,0)\geq D+D_{0}}\mathbb{P}_{x}[d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(x,[0,X_{\infty}(x)))\geq D]\mathbb{P}_{o}[X_{n}=x]

\displaystyle\gtrsim\mathbb{P}_{o}[d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(X_{n},0)\geq D+D_{0}]{\rm e}^{-D-c}.

To get rid of the dependence on
n
, we observe that by transience
\mathbb{P}_{o}[d(X_{n},0)\geq D+D_{0}]\to 1
and hence for

\varepsilon>0
there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
, such that
\mathbb{P}_{o}[d(X_{n},[o,X_{\infty}))\geq D]\gtrsim(1-\varepsilon)e^{-D-c}
for
n\geq N
.

(2): To prove the second claim, we note that

(Y_{n},Y_{\infty})^{{\mathcal{G}}}_{o}=d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(Y_{n},o)-(Y_{\infty},o)^{{\mathcal{G}}}_{Y_{n}}

and since there exists
c>0
dependent on
M
and
\delta
such that

d_{{\mathcal{G}}}\big(Y_{n},[o,Y_{\infty})\big)-c\leq(Y_{\infty},o)^{{\mathcal{G}}}_{Y_{n}}\leq d_{{\mathcal{G}}}\big(Y_{n},[o,Y_{\infty})\big)+c

by part (1) in particular also

\mathbb{P}_{o}[(Y_{\infty},o)_{Y_{n}}\geq D]\asymp{\rm e}^{-D}.

Hence we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{o}\big[(Y_{n},Y_{\infty})_{o}^{{\mathcal{G}}}\geq D\big]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{o}\big[d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(Y_{n},o)-(Y_{\infty},o)^{{\mathcal{G}}}_{Y_{n}}\geq D\big]

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{P}_{o}\big[d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(Y_{n},o)\geq 2D\big]-\mathbb{P}_{o}\big[(Y_{\infty},o)^{{\mathcal{G}}}_{Y_{n}}\geq D\big]

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{P}_{o}\big[d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(Y_{n},o)\geq 2D\big]-c_{l}{\rm e}^{-D}.

And since
\mathbb{P}_{o}[d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(Y_{n},o)\geq 2D]\to 1
, this is in particular bounded below by
1-(c_{l}+1){\rm e}^{-D}
for n big enough, so

\mathbb{P}_{o}\big[(Y_{n},Y_{\infty})_{o}^{{\mathcal{G}}}<D\big]\leq\Big(1-\mathbb{P}_{o}\big[d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(Y_{n},o)\geq 2D\big]\Big)+c_{l}{\rm e}^{-D}\lesssim{\rm e}^{-D}.

(4.5)

∎

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
(Y_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
(Z_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be two independent usual random walks on
G
with step distribution
\mu
and starting at
o
and
Y_{\infty},Z_{\infty}
the respective hitting points on
\partial G
. Then for
n>D
big enough

(i)

\mathbb{P}_{o}\Big[\big(Y_{n},Z_{\infty})^{{\mathcal{G}}}_{o}\geq D\Big]\asymp{\rm e}^{-D}
, and

(ii)

\mathbb{P}_{o}\Big[\big(Y_{n},Z_{n})^{{\mathcal{G}}}_{o}\geq D\Big]\asymp{\rm e}^{-D}
.

Proof.

By the independence of
(Y_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
(Z_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
,
Y_{n}
is also independent of
Z_{\infty}
and hence

\mathbb{P}_{o}\Big[\big(Y_{n},Z_{\infty}\big)^{{\mathcal{G}}}_{o}\geq D\Big]=\sum_{y\in G}\mathbb{P}_{o}\Big[Y_{n}=y\big]\mathbb{P}_{o}\Big[\big(y,Z_{\infty}\big)^{{\mathcal{G}}}_{o}\geq D\Big].

(4.6)

We now use the Shadow Lemma
3.9
to approximate this sum. So let
R>0
be as in Lemma
3.9
and
y\in G
such that
d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(y,o)\geq D+R
. Denote by
x_{y}
the point in
[o,y]
which has distance to
o
closest to
D+R
. Then

\mathbb{P}_{o}\big[\big(y,Z_{\infty}\big)^{{\mathcal{G}}}_{o}\geq D\big]=\mathbb{P}_{o}\big[Z_{\infty}\in\mho_{o}(x,R)\big]\asymp{\rm e}^{-d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(o,x)}.

Putting this into
4.6
, we get

\mathbb{P}_{o}\big[\big(Y_{n},Z_{\infty}\big)^{{\mathcal{G}}}_{o}\geq D\big]\lesssim\mathbb{P}_{o}\big[d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(Y_{n},o)\geq D+R\big]{\rm e}^{-D}+\mathbb{P}_{o}\big[d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(Y_{n},o)\leq D+R\big]

(4.7)

and

\mathbb{P}_{o}\big[\big(Y_{n},Z_{\infty}\big)^{{\mathcal{G}}}_{o}\geq D\big]\gtrsim\mathbb{P}_{o}\big[d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(Y_{n},o)\geq D+R\big]{\rm e}^{-D}.

(4.8)

Together with the fact that
\mathbb{P}[d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(Y_{n},o)\leq D+R]\to 0
, the claim in point
1
follows.

For point
2
, we again use the independence of
Y_{n}
and
Z_{n}
to get

\mathbb{P}_{o}\Big[\big(Y_{n},Z_{n}\big)^{{\mathcal{G}}}_{o}\geq D\Big]=\sum_{y\in G}\mathbb{P}[Y_{n}=y]\mathbb{P}_{o}\Big[\big(y,Z_{n}\big)^{{\mathcal{G}}}_{o}\geq D\Big].

(4.9)

In order to apply what we have already shown, we now want to replace
\big(y,Z_{n}\big)^{{\mathcal{G}}}_{o}
by
\big(y,Z_{\infty}\big)^{{\mathcal{G}}}_{o}
. By the
\delta
-hyperbolicity of
G
, we have

\big(y,Z_{n}\big)^{{\mathcal{G}}}_{o}\geq\min\bigg\{\big(y,Z_{\infty}\big)^{{\mathcal{G}}}_{o},\big(Z_{n},Z_{\infty}\big)^{{\mathcal{G}}}_{o}\bigg\}-\delta

as well as

\big(y,Z_{\infty}\big)^{{\mathcal{G}}}_{o}\geq\min\bigg\{\big(y,Z_{n}\big)^{{\mathcal{G}}}_{o},\big(Z_{n},Z_{\infty}\big)^{{\mathcal{G}}}_{o}\bigg\}-\delta.

And since by definition
Z_{n}\to Z_{\infty}
almost surely, we know that also
(Z_{n},Z_{\infty})_{o}\to\infty
almost surely. Hence with high probability the minima above will be given by
\big(y,Z_{\infty}\big)^{{\mathcal{G}}}_{o}
and
\big(y,Z_{n}\big)^{{\mathcal{G}}}_{o}
respectively. This gives us

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{o}\Big[\big(y,Z_{n}\big)^{{\mathcal{G}}}_{o}\geq D\Big]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}_{o}\Big[\big(y,Z_{n}\big)^{{\mathcal{G}}}_{o}\geq D,\big(Z_{n},Z_{\infty}\big)^{{\mathcal{G}}}_{o}\geq D\Big]+\mathbb{P}_{o}\Big[\big(Z_{n},Z_{\infty}\big)^{{\mathcal{G}}}_{o}<D\Big]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}_{o}\Big[\big(y,Z_{\infty}\big)^{{\mathcal{G}}}_{o}\geq D-\delta\Big]+\mathbb{P}_{o}\Big[\big(Z_{n},Z_{\infty}\big)^{{\mathcal{G}}}_{o}<D\Big]

and

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{o}\Big[\big(y,Z_{n}\big)^{{\mathcal{G}}}_{o}\geq D\Big]

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{P}_{o}\Big[\big(y,Z_{n}\big)^{{\mathcal{G}}}_{o}\geq D,\big(Z_{n},Z_{\infty}\big)^{{\mathcal{G}}}_{o}\geq D\Big]\geq\mathbb{P}_{o}\Big[\big(y,Z_{\infty}\big)^{{\mathcal{G}}}_{o}\geq D+\delta\Big].

Plugging the bounds shown for
(Y_{n},Z_{\infty})_{o}^{{\mathcal{G}}}
in point
1
into Equation (
4.9
), we get the upper bound

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{o}\big[\big(Y_{n},Z_{n}\big)^{{\mathcal{G}}}_{o}\geq D\big]\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{y\in G}\mathbb{P}_{o}[Y_{n}=y]\mathbb{P}_{o}\Big[\big(y,Z_{\infty}\big)^{{\mathcal{G}}}_{o}\geq D-\delta\Big]+\mathbb{P}_{o}\Big[\big(Z_{n},Z_{\infty}\big)^{{\mathcal{G}}}_{o}<D\Big]

\displaystyle=\,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{o}\Big[\big(Y_{n},Z_{\infty}\big)^{{\mathcal{G}}}_{o}\geq D-\delta\Big]+\mathbb{P}_{o}\Big[\big(Z_{n},Z_{\infty}\big)^{{\mathcal{G}}}_{o}<D\Big]

\displaystyle\leq\,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{o}\big[d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(Y_{n},o)\geq D+R-\delta\big]{\rm e}^{-D}+\mathbb{P}_{o}\big[d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(Y_{n},o)\leq D+R-\delta\big]

\displaystyle+\Big(1-\mathbb{P}_{o}\big[d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(Y_{n},o)\geq 2D\big]\Big)+c_{l}{\rm e}^{-D}

\displaystyle\leq\,

\displaystyle(c_{l}+1){\rm e}^{-D}+\Big(1-\mathbb{P}_{o}\big[d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(Y_{n},o)\geq 2D\big]\Big)

by putting in the detailed upper bounds from equations and (
4.5
), and (
4.7
).

And for the lower bound, using the detailed lower bound in Equation (
4.8
), that

\mathbb{P}_{o}\big[\big(Y_{n},Z_{n}\big)^{{\mathcal{G}}}_{o}\geq D\big]\geq\mathbb{P}_{o}\big[\big(Y_{n},Z_{\infty}\big)^{{\mathcal{G}}}_{o}\geq D+\delta\big]\gtrsim\mathbb{P}_{o}\big[d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(Y_{n},o)>D+R+\delta\big]{\rm e}^{-(D+c_{2})}

(4.10)

And since
\mathbb{P}_{o}[d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(Y_{n},0)\leq K]\to 0
as
n\to\infty
for any constant
K\geq 0
, the claim follows.
∎

Corollary 4.5
.

Let
(Y_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
(Z_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be two independent usual random walks on
G
with step distribution
\mu
and starting at
o
and denote their hitting points on
\partial G
by
Y_{\infty}
and
Z_{\infty}
respectively. Then

Y_{\infty}\neq Z_{\infty}\quad\text{almost surely.}

Remark 6
Note that the non-singularity of the joint hitting distribution is a well known fact and can also be deduced from the non-singularity of the hitting measures. It is included here, since it follows nicely from the more detailed joint behavior of the two paths (and thus the two halves of the bi-infinite path) shown in Theorem
4.3
and Lemma
4.4
.

Proof.

The claim will be proven by showing that almost surely
(Y_{\infty},Z_{\infty})^{{\mathcal{G}}}_{o}<\infty
. For this we first shift the Gromov product to a finite time in
Y_{n}
to use the bounds we have shown above.
Using that by hyperbolicity

\big(y,Z_{\infty}\big)^{{\mathcal{G}}}_{o}\geq\min\bigg\{(Y_{\infty},Z_{\infty})^{{\mathcal{G}}}_{o},\big(Y_{n},Y_{\infty}\big)^{{\mathcal{G}}}_{o}\bigg\}-\delta.

as in the proof of the previous Lemma
4.4
, we again get that for
n
big with high probability

(Y_{\infty},Z_{\infty})^{{\mathcal{G}}}_{o}\leq\big(Y_{n},Z_{\infty}\big)_{o}^{{\mathcal{G}}}+\delta.

Hence, again

\mathbb{P}_{o}\Big[\big(Y_{\infty},Z_{\infty}\big)^{{\mathcal{G}}}_{o}\geq D\Big]\leq\mathbb{P}_{o}\Big[\big(Y_{n},Z_{\infty}\big)^{{\mathcal{G}}}_{o}\geq D-\delta\Big]+\mathbb{P}_{o}\Big[\big(Y_{n},Y_{\infty}\big)^{{\mathcal{G}}}_{o}<D\Big]\lesssim{\rm e}^{-D}

by the bounds from Lemma
4.4
and Theorem
4.3
.

Note, that we saw in the proofs of these statements, that technically these bounds depend on
\mathbb{P}_{o}[d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(o,Y_{n})\geq K]
for some constants
K>0
. But since we may choose
n\geq 0
arbitrarily big, we can assume that these are all arbitrarily close to
1
.

Finally, due to the exponential decay of the probabilities, we get that

\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{P}_{o}\Big[\big(X_{\infty},X_{-\infty}\big)^{{\mathcal{G}}}_{o}\geq D_{n}\Big]<\infty

when choosing
D_{n}=O(\log(n))
. So by the Lemma of Borel and Cantelli, we get that

\mathbb{P}_{o}\Big[\big(X_{\infty},X_{-\infty}\big)^{{\mathcal{G}}}_{o}=\infty\Big]=0.

∎

Corollary 4.6
.

For the bi-infinite random walk
(X_{z})_{z\in\mathbb{Z}}
with hitting points
X_{-\infty}
and
X_{\infty}
on
\partial G
, we have that

\limsup_{\absolutevalue{z}\to\infty}\frac{d_{{\mathcal{G}}}\big(X_{z},(X_{-\infty},X_{\infty})\big)}{\log(\abs{z})}<\infty

almost surely, where
(X_{\infty},X_{-\infty})
is a quasi-geodesic ray between the two hitting points.

Proof.

In order to bound the deviation of
(X_{z})_{z\in\mathbb{Z}}
from the quasi-geodesic
(X_{-\infty},X_{\infty})
, we will show that it suffices to regard the deviation from by
[o,X_{\infty})
and
[o,X_{-\infty})
, the quasi-geodesics from
o
to
X_{\infty}
and
X_{-\infty}
respectively.
We consider the triangle
\Delta(X_{-\infty},o,X_{\infty})
. Since
(G,d_{{\mathcal{G}}})
is
\delta
-hyperbolic, there exist points
m_{1}\in[0,X_{-\infty})
and
m_{2}\in[o,X_{\infty})
such that
d(x,(X_{-\infty},X_{\infty}))<2\delta
for
x\in[m_{1},X_{-\infty})\cup[m_{2},X_{\infty}]
.
So we can approximate for
n\geq 0

d_{{\mathcal{G}}}\big(X_{n},(X_{-\infty},X_{\infty})\big)\leq d-G\big(X_{n},[o,X_{\infty})\big)+d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(o,m_{2})+\delta

and the same with
X_{-\infty}
and
m_{1}
for
n<0
.
Now by the deviation bounds shown in Theorem
4.3
, we can apply the Lemma of Borel and Cantelli to
\{d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(X_{n},[0,X_{-\infty})>D_{n})\}
for
D_{n}=O(\log(n))
to get that almost surely

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{d\big(X_{n},[0,X_{-\infty})\big)}{\log(n)}<\infty

and the same for
n\to-\infty
. Hence we also have that almost surely

\limsup_{\absolutevalue{n}\to\infty}\frac{d\big(X_{n},(X_{-\infty},X_{\infty})\big)}{\log(\abs{n})}<\infty.

∎

4.3.
Deviation from the Geodesic: a Disintegrated Version

Since
\nu_{o}
is the push-forward of
\mathbb{P}_{o}
under
{\rm bd}:G^{\mathbb{Z}}\to\partial G
, for every
\xi\in\partial G
there exists a measure
\mathbb{P}_{o}(\cdot|\,\xi\,)
such that

\mathbb{P}_{o}\Big(G^{\mathbb{Z}}\backslash{\rm bd}^{-1}(\xi)\,\,\big|\,\,\xi\Big)=0,

and for all
f:G^{\mathbb{Z}}\to\mathbb{R}
measurable

\int_{G^{\mathbb{Z}}}f(x){\rm{d}}\mathbb{P}_{o}(x)=\int_{\partial G}\int_{{\rm bd}^{-1}(\xi)}f(x){\rm{d}}\mathbb{P}_{o}(x|\xi){\rm{d}}\nu_{o}(\xi)

as in Theorem
3.2
.
Since the geometric arguments in the above proof of Theorem
4.3
do not really depend where the limit point
X_{\infty}
lies on the boundary
\partial G
, it is possible to transfer the above statement to the conditioned measures
\mathbb{P}_{o}(\cdot|\xi)
as well.

Theorem 4.7

Let
(Y_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a random walk started at
o
with step distribution
\mu
. For any
D\geq 0
and
n\geq 0
sufficiently large, there exists
C_{D}>1
such that for all
\xi\in\partial G

C_{D}^{-1}{\rm e}^{-D}\leq\mathbb{P}_{o}\big[d_{{\mathcal{G}}}\big(Y_{n},[o,\xi)\big)\geq D\big|\xi\big]\leq C_{D}{\rm e}^{-D}.

The constants
C_{D}
are bounded in
D
and do not depend on
\xi
.

Proof.

We will prove the claim by showing that for any set
B
in a generating set of the
\sigma
-field on
\partial G
we have

As in the above proof of Theorem

5.
Dynamics of the Hitting Measures

The goal of this section is to provide the first two constructions of the measure
\Theta
appearing in Theorem
2.1
: in Section
5.1
and Section
5.2
we will show that the measures
\Theta_{1}
and
\Theta_{2}
come from the
dynamics
of the hitting measures along deterministic and random paths respectively.

5.1.
Construction of
\Theta_{1}
: Dynamics Along Deterministic Paths

The construction of
\Theta_{1}
will depend on a choice of a family of bi-infinite paths connecting each pair of points on the boundary. These paths are constructed as follows:

Recall that

\partial G\simeq\bigg\{[(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}]:(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\text{ geodesic ray started at }o\bigg\}.

Hence, for
[\xi],[\eta]\in\partial G
we may choose such rays
(\xi_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
(\eta_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
. Then
(p_{z})_{z\in\mathbb{Z}}:=\big((\xi_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\oplus(\eta_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\big)
is a bi-infinite ray connecting
\xi
and
\eta
.

So
\partial^{2}G\simeq\{[(\xi\oplus\eta)]:\xi\neq\eta\in\partial G\}
. Using these rays, we now define the measure
\Theta_{1}
(recall (
2.6
)) by setting

{\rm{d}}\Theta_{1}(\xi\oplus\eta):=\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{z=-n}^{n}\frac{1}{2n+1}{\rm{d}}(\nu_{p_{z}}\otimes\nu_{p_{z}})(\xi,\eta)

(5.1)

for
\xi\neq\eta
. This way,
\Theta_{1}
captures how the hitting measures evolve as we move the origin along the paths
(p_{z})_{z\in\mathbb{Z}}
.

The chosen paths
(p_{z})_{z\in\mathbb{Z}}
come with a parametrization, which always sets the origin
o
at time
0
.
\Theta_{1}
however is independent of the choice of parametrization. To show this, we consider the action of
\mathbb{Z}
on the bi-infinite sequences
x=(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
given by
z.(x)(n)=x_{n-z}
. Since
x_{n}
and
x_{n-z}
always have at most distance
z
, this action does not change the limits of the sequence as
n\to\infty
and
n\to-\infty
.

Lemma 5.1
.

The measure
\Theta_{1}
defined in (
5.1
) is independent of the parametrization of the rays
(\xi\oplus\eta)
we chose.

Proof.

For
z\in\mathbb{Z}
we set

{\rm{d}}\Theta_{1}^{z}(\xi\oplus\eta):=\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{i=-n}^{n}\frac{1}{2n+1}\;{\rm{d}}(\nu_{p_{i+z}}\otimes\nu_{p_{z+i}})(\xi_{z+i},\eta_{z+i}).

Then

\displaystyle{\rm{d}}\Theta_{1}^{z}(\xi\oplus\eta)

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{i=-n}^{n}\frac{1}{2n+1}\;{\rm{d}}(\nu_{p_{z-i}}\otimes\nu_{p_{z-i}})(\xi,\eta)

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{i=-n-z}^{n-z}\frac{1}{2n+1}\;{\rm{d}}(\nu_{p_{i}}\otimes\nu_{p_{i}})(\xi_{i},\eta_{i})

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{i=-n}^{n}\frac{1}{2n+1}\;{\rm{d}}(\nu_{p_{i}}\otimes\nu_{p_{i}})(\xi_{i},\eta_{i})

\displaystyle={\rm{d}}\Theta_{1}(\xi\oplus\eta).

since the
\limsup
does not change when changing boundedly many summands in each step.
∎

We now turn to the required estimates on the density of
\Theta_{1}
with respect to
(\nu_{o}\otimes\nu_{o})
(shown in Lemma
5.2
) as well as proving the quasi-invariance of
\Theta_{1}
under the
G
-action on
\partial^{2}G
(shown in Lemma
5.3
).

Lemma 5.2

(Density of
\Theta_{1}
)
.

For all
\xi,\eta\in\partial G
, we have

{\rm e}^{-(2M+8\delta)}\,{\rm e}^{2(\xi,\eta)^{{\mathcal{G}}}_{o}}\leq\frac{{\rm{d}}\Theta_{1}}{{\rm{d}}(\nu_{0}\otimes\nu_{0})}(\xi,\eta)\leq{\rm e}^{2\delta}\,{\rm e}^{2(\xi,\eta)^{{\mathcal{G}}}_{o}}

where
M
is the constant such that
(G,d_{{\mathcal{G}}})
is
M
quasi-ruled in the sense of Remark
3.5
. In particular this also means that the limit in the definition of
\Theta_{1}
exists, i.e. that
\Theta_{1}
is well defined.

Proof.

We know from Theorem
3.15
, that the density of the hitting measures for the random walk started at
x
and at
y
is given by

\frac{{\rm{d}}\nu_{x}}{{\rm{d}}\nu_{o}}(\xi)=\exp{\limsup_{\xi_n \to\xi} d_{\skrig}(o,\xi_n)-d_{\skrig}(x,\xi_n)}.

And since for the product measures the density is

\frac{{\rm{d}}(\nu_{x}\otimes\nu_{x})}{{\rm{d}}(\nu_{o}\otimes\nu_{o})}(\xi,\eta)=\frac{{\rm{d}}\nu_{x}}{{\rm{d}}\nu_{y}}(\xi)\frac{{\rm{d}}\nu_{x}}{{\rm{d}}\nu_{y}}(\eta),

we have that

\frac{{\rm{d}}(\nu_{p_{z}}\otimes\nu_{p_{z}})}{{\rm{d}}(\nu_{o}\otimes\nu_{o})}(\xi,\eta)=\exp\{\limsup\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\xi_{i}\to\xi\\
\eta_{i}\to\eta\end{subarray}}d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(o,\xi_{i})+d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(o,\eta_{i})-d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(p_{z},\xi_{i})-d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(p_{z},\eta_{i})\bigg\}

Now, if we can show that for big
i\in\mathbb{N}
and
z\in\mathbb{Z}
, the value of
d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(p_{z},\xi_{i})+d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(p_{z},\eta_{i})
is within bounded distance of
d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(\xi_{i},\eta_{i})
for a bound independent of
\xi
and
\eta
, the claim will follow.

Let
\absolutevalue{z}\geq(\xi,\eta)^{{\mathcal{G}}}_{o}+\varepsilon
. Then in particular also
\absolutevalue{z}\geq(\xi_{i},\eta_{i})^{{\mathcal{G}}}_{o}
for all
i
big enough.
Consider the triangle
\Delta(0,\xi_{i},\eta_{i})
. By hyperbolicity of
G
there exists a point
\tilde{p}_{z}\in[\xi_{i},\eta_{i}]
such that
d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(p_{z},\tilde{p}_{z})\leq 4\delta
.

And since
(G,d_{{\mathcal{G}}})
is
M
quasi-ruled, we have

d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(\xi_{i},\eta_{i})\leq d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(p_{z},\xi_{i})+d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(p_{z},\eta_{i})\leq d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(\tilde{p}_{z},\xi_{i})+d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(\tilde{p}_{z},\eta_{i})+8\delta\leq d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(\xi_{i},\eta_{i})+2M+8\delta.

So, all together for
c=2M+8\delta
and
m=(\xi,\eta)^{{\mathcal{G}}}_{o}+\varepsilon

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm{d}}\Theta_{1}}{{\rm{d}}(\nu_{o}\otimes\nu_{o})}(\xi,\eta)

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{m\leq\absolutevalue{z}\leq n}\frac{1}{2n+1}\,\exp\{\limsup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\xi_{i}\to\xi\\
\eta_{i}\to\eta\end{subarray}}d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(o,\xi_{i})+d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(o,\eta_{i})-d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(p_{z},\xi_{i})-d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(p_{z},\eta_{i})\bigg\}

\displaystyle\geq\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{m\leq\absolutevalue{z}\leq n}\frac{1}{2n+1}\,\exp\{\limsup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\xi_{i}\to\xi\\
\eta_{i}\to\eta\end{subarray}}d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(o,\xi_{i})+d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(o,\eta_{i})-d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(\xi_{i},\eta_{i})-c\bigg\}

\displaystyle\geq{\rm e}^{-c}\,{\rm e}^{2(\xi,\eta)^{{\mathcal{G}}}_{o}}

where we used that ignoring the summands for
\absolutevalue{z}<m
does not change the limit.

For the lower bound we use the triangle inequality to get rid of
p_{z}
. This leaves us with

\limsup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\xi_{i}\to\xi\\
\eta_{i}\to\eta\end{subarray}}d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(o,\xi_{i})+d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(o,\eta_{i})-d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(\xi_{i},\eta_{i})\leq(\xi,\eta)^{{\mathcal{G}}}_{o}+2\delta,

where we can switch from the
\limsup
to the
\liminf
in the Gromov product by hyperbolicity. All together this means

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm{d}}\Theta_{1}}{{\rm{d}}(\nu_{o}\otimes\nu_{o})}(\xi,\eta)

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{z=-n}^{n}\frac{1}{2n+1}\,\exp\{\limsup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\xi_{i}\to\xi\\
\eta_{i}\to\eta\end{subarray}}d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(o,\xi_{i})+d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(o,\eta_{i})-d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(p_{z},\xi_{i})-d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(p_{z},\eta_{i})\bigg\}

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{z=-n}^{n}\frac{1}{2n+1}\,\exp\{\limsup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\xi_{i}\to\xi\\
\eta_{i}\to\eta\end{subarray}}d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(o,\xi_{i})+d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(o,\eta_{i})-d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(\xi_{i},\eta_{i})\bigg\}

\displaystyle\leq{\rm e}^{2\delta}\,{\rm e}^{2(\xi,\eta)^{{\mathcal{G}}}_{o}}.

Note that the upper and lower bounds above, show that the Césaro limit is taken over terms which are bounded above and below, and hence the limit exists.

∎

Lemma 5.3

(Quasi-invariance of
\Theta_{1}
)
.

For any
g\in G
, denoting
g_{\star}\Theta_{1}
the push-forward of
\Theta_{1}
, we have

{\rm e}^{-16\delta-2M}\leq\frac{{\rm{d}}(g_{\ast}\Theta_{1})}{{\rm{d}}\Theta_{1}}(\xi,\eta)\leq{\rm e}^{16\delta+2M}.

where,
M
is the constant for which
(G,d_{{\mathcal{G}}})
is
M
quasi-ruled. In particular, the measure
\Theta_{1}
is quasi-invariant under the action of
G
on
\partial^{2}G
.

Proof.

Denote by
(p_{z})_{z\in\mathbb{Z}}
the chosen bi-infinite ray connecting
\xi
and
\eta
, and by
(p^{g}_{z})_{z\in\mathbb{Z}}
the ray between
g\xi
and
g\eta
. By choice of these rays,
(gp_{z})_{z\leq 0}
and
(gp_{z})_{z\geq 0}
are quasi-geodesics converging to
g\xi
and
g\eta
respectively, so

\Delta(0,g,g\xi)=(p^{g}_{z})_{z\leq 0}\,\,\cup\,\,[0,g]\,\,\cup\,\,(gp_{z})_{z\leq 0}

\Delta(0,g,g\eta)=(p^{g}_{z})_{z\geq 0}\,\,\cup\,\,[0,g]\,\,\cup\,\,(gp_{z})_{z\geq 0}

are quasi-geodesic triangles. In these triangles, the two sides going to
\xi
and
\eta
are within distance
4\delta
of each other for
z
such that
{\rm{d}}_{{\mathcal{G}}}(p_{z},o)\geq(\xi,\eta)_{o}^{{\mathcal{G}}}
. Hence there exists
m>0
such that for any point
p\in(gp_{z})_{z\geq m}
there exists
\tilde{p}\in(p^{g}_{z})_{z\geq m}
for which
d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(p,\tilde{p})\leq 4\delta
.

So, using that
G,d_{{\mathcal{G}}}
is
M
quasi-ruled as in the proof of Lemma
5.2
, we have

\Big|d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(gp_{z},\xi_{i})-d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(gp_{z},\eta_{i})-d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(\tilde{p}^{g}_{z},\xi_{i})-d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(\tilde{p}^{g}_{z},\eta_{i})\Big|\leq 2M+8\delta.

Hence, for
\absolutevalue{z}\geq m
and

d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(gp_{z},\xi_{i})-d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(gp_{z},\eta_{i})-c\leq d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(p^{g}_{z},\xi_{i})-d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(p^{g}_{z},\eta_{i})\leq d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(gp_{z},\xi_{i})-d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(gp_{z},\eta_{i})+c.

With this we get

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm{d}}\Theta_{1}}{{\rm{d}}(\nu_{o}\otimes\nu_{o})}(g\xi,g\eta)

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{2n+1}\sum_{z=-n}^{n}\frac{{\rm{d}}(\nu_{p^{g}_{z}}\otimes\nu_{p^{g}_{z}})}{{\rm{d}}(\nu_{o}\otimes\nu_{o})}(g\xi,g\eta)

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{2n+1}\sum_{\absolutevalue{g}\leq\absolutevalue{z}\leq n}\exp\{\limsup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\xi_{i}\to g\xi\\
\eta_{i}\to g\eta\end{subarray}}d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(o,\xi_{i})+d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(o,\eta_{i})-d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(p^{g}_{z},\xi_{i})-d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(p^{g}_{z},\eta_{i})\bigg\}

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{{\rm e}^{c}}{2n+1}\sum_{\absolutevalue{g }\leq\absolutevalue{z}\leq n}\exp\{\limsup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\xi_{i}\to g\xi\\
\eta_{i}\to g\eta\end{subarray}}d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(o,\xi_{i})+d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(o,\eta_{i})-d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(gp_{z},\xi_{i})-d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(g{p}_{z},\eta_{i})\bigg\}

\displaystyle={\rm e}^{c}\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{2n+1}\sum_{\absolutevalue{z}\leq n-\absolutevalue{g}}\frac{{\rm{d}}(\nu_{gp_{z}}\otimes\nu_{gp_{z}})}{{\rm{d}}(\nu_{o}\otimes\nu_{o})}(g\xi,g\eta)

\displaystyle={\rm e}^{c}\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{2n+1}\sum_{\absolutevalue{z}\leq n}\frac{{\rm{d}}(\nu_{p_{z}}\otimes\nu_{p_{z}})}{{\rm{d}}(\nu_{o}\otimes\nu_{o})}(\xi,\eta)={\rm e}^{c}\,\frac{{\rm{d}}\Theta_{1}}{{\rm{d}}(\nu_{o}\otimes\nu_{o})}.

using that
{\rm{d}}\nu_{gx}(g\xi)={\rm{d}}\nu_{x}(\xi)
. Using the lower bounds on
d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(p^{g}_{z},\xi_{i})-d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(p^{g}_{z},\eta_{i})
instead of the upper bounds, we get in the same way that the density is bounded below by
{\rm e}^{-c}
.
∎

5.2.
Construction of
\Theta_{2}
: Dynamics Along Random Paths

The measure
\Theta_{1}
constructed Section
5.1
depends on a specific choice of bi-infinite rays. In order to remove this in some ways arbitrary choice, we will now construct the measure
\Theta_{2}
mentioned in Theorem

in a similar way, where instead of relying on this specific choice of geodesic ray, we integrate over all bi-infinite rays passing through

and converging to the boundary points

to determine the density
{\rm{d}}\Theta_{2}(\xi,\eta)
. Thus,
\Theta_{2}
captures the dynamics of the hitting measures when moved along all random walk paths with origin
X_{0}=o
. In this way it can be seen as a disintegrated version of
\Theta_{1}
.

For notational convenience, we define the function
f:G^{\mathbb{Z}}\to\mathbb{R}
to be

f\Big((x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\Big)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{z=-n}^{n}\frac{1}{2n+1}\frac{{\rm{d}}(\nu_{x_{z}}\otimes\nu_{x_{z}})}{{\rm{d}}(\nu_{x_{0}}\otimes\nu_{x_{0}})}(\xi,\eta)

(5.2)

where
\xi={\rm bd}\big((x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}})\big)
and
\eta={\rm bd}\big((x_{-n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}})\big)
. We also recall from Definition
4.2
that
\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{g}
stands for the distribution of bi-infinite random walk trajectory
(X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
. Then for any
A\subset\partial^{2}G
, we set (recall (
2.7
)),

\Theta_{2}(A):=\int_{{\rm bd}^{-1}(A)}f\big((x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\big)\;{\rm{d}}\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{o}\Big((x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\Big).

(5.3)

We will now show the required estimates on the density of
\Theta_{2}
with respect to
\nu_{o}\otimes\nu_{o}
:

Lemma 5.4

(Density of
\Theta_{2}
)
.

For every
\xi,\eta\in\partial G
, we have

\frac{{\rm{d}}\Theta_{2}}{{\rm{d}}(\nu_{o}\otimes\nu_{o})}(\xi,\eta)\asymp{\rm e}^{2(\xi,\eta)_{o}^{{\mathcal{G}}}}.

In particular, this also means the limit in the definition of
f
in (
5.2
) exists, and
\Theta_{2}
is well defined.

Proof.

Since
(\nu_{o}\otimes\nu_{o})
is the push-forward of
\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{o}
under the boundary map, by disintegration of measure, there exists a family
\bar{\mathbb{P}}(\cdot|(\xi,\eta))
such that for every measurable function
g:G^{\mathbb{Z}}\to\mathbb{R}
we have

\int_{G_{0}^{\mathbb{Z}}}g(x_{n})_{n}{\rm{d}}\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{o}(x_{n})_{n}=\int_{\partial^{2}G}\int_{{\rm bd}^{-1}(\xi)\crossproduct{\rm bd}^{-1}(\eta)}g(x_{n})_{n}{\rm{d}}\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{o}\big((x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|\xi,\eta\big){\rm{d}}(\nu_{o}\otimes\nu_{o})(\xi,\eta).

In particular this holds for
g(\cdot)=\mathbbm{1}_{{\rm bd}^{-1}(A)}(\cdot)f(\cdot)
with
f
defined in (
5.2
) and any
A\in\partial^{2}G
. To get the bound on the density, we hence have to estimate

\int_{{\rm bd}^{-1}(\xi)\crossproduct{\rm bd}^{-1}(\eta)}f\big((x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\big)\;{\rm{d}}\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{o}\big((x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|(\xi,\eta)\big)

By the triangle inequality

d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(\xi_{i},\eta_{i})\leq d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(x_{z},\xi_{i})-d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(x_{z},\eta_{i})

and hence, remembering that we only consider paths with
x_{0}=o
, we get that

f((x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}})\leq{\rm e}^{2(\xi,\eta)_{o}^{{\mathcal{G}}}},

implying the desired upper bound of the density.

To get the lower bound, we first notice that by Fatou’s lemma, we can exchange the limit and the integral to get a lower bound. Afterwards we may exchange the integral and sum, so we get

\displaystyle\int_{{\rm bd}^{-1}(\xi)\crossproduct{\rm bd}^{-1}(\eta)}f(x_{n})_{n}{\rm{d}}\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{o}\big((x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|(\xi,\eta)\big)

\displaystyle\geq\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum\int\frac{1}{2n+1}\exp\{\limsup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\xi_{i}\to\xi\\
\eta_{i}\to\eta\end{subarray}}d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(x_{0},\xi_{i})+d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(x_{0},\eta_{i})-d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(x_{z},\xi_{i})-d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(x_{z},\eta_{i})\Big\}{\rm{d}}\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{o}\big((x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|\xi,\eta\big)

where the above sum is taken over
z\in\{-n,...,n\}
and the integral over
({\rm bd}^{-1}(\xi))\times({\rm bd}^{-1}(\eta))
.

Now we will use what we proved in Theorem
4.7
about the deviation of the random walk paths
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
from the geodesic for the disintegrated measures. Note that, since
\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{o}
is equivalent to the product
\mathbb{P}_{o}\otimes\mathbb{P}_{o}
of two independent measures, under splitting the paths at time
0
and reversing time on the negative time half, the same also holds for the disintegrated measures
\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{o}(\cdot|(\xi,\eta))
.

So, let
D>0
be fixed and
x_{z}
such that
d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(x_{z},(\xi,\eta))\leq D
. Further let
x\in[\xi_{i},\eta_{i}]
be such that
d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(x_{z},x)\leq D
. Then

d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(x_{z},\xi_{i})+d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(x_{z},\xi_{i})\leq d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(x,\xi_{i})+d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(x,\xi_{i})+2D\leq d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(\xi_{i},\eta_{i})+2D+M

using that
G
is
M
quasi-ruled for
d_{{\mathcal{G}}}
. Hence

\displaystyle\int_{{\rm bd}^{-1}(\xi)\crossproduct{\rm bd}^{-1}(\eta)}\frac{{\rm{d}}(\nu_{x_{z}}\otimes\nu_{x_{z}})}{{\rm{d}}(\nu_{x_{0}}\otimes\nu_{x_{0}})}(\xi,\eta){\rm{d}}\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{o}\big((x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|(\xi,\eta)\big)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{{\rm bd}^{-1}(\xi)\crossproduct{\rm bd}^{-1}(\eta)}\exp\{\limsup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\xi_{i}\to\xi\\
\eta_{i}\to\eta\end{subarray}}d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(x_{0},\xi_{i})+d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(x_{0},\eta_{i})-d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(x_{z},\xi_{i})-d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(x_{z},\eta_{i})\bigg\}{\rm{d}}\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{o}\big((x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|(\xi,\eta)\big)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{B}\exp\{\limsup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\xi_{i}\to\xi\\
\eta_{i}\to\eta\end{subarray}}d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(x_{0},\xi_{i})+d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(x_{0},\eta_{i})-d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(\xi_{i}\eta_{i})-2D-M\bigg\}{\rm{d}}\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{o}\big((x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|(\xi,\eta)\big)

\displaystyle\geq(1-{\rm e}^{-D})\,{\rm e}^{-2D-M}\,{\rm e}^{2(\xi,\eta)_{0}}

where we have restricted the integral to

B={\rm bd}^{-1}(\xi)\crossproduct{\rm bd}^{-1}(\eta)\cap\big\{d_{{\mathcal{G}}}\big(x_{z},(\xi,\eta)\big)\leq D\big\}.

Optimizing over
D
, we get that the maximal lower bound is achieved at
D=\log(\frac{3}{2})
. Here the value is
e^{-M}\,\frac{4}{27}
.
By the upper and lower bound again the Césaro limit is taken over bounded terms and hence exists. Note that, since the claim is that the limit exists for
f
, in the lower bound we also need to look at the terms where
x_{n}
is further than
D
from the geodesic
(\xi,\eta)
. But since this distance turns up in
f
as a term of order
{\rm e}^{-d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(x_{n},(\xi,\eta))}
, we still have that all terms are bounded below by
0
.
∎

Lemma 5.5
.

\Theta_{2}
is quasi-invariant for the
G
-action.

Proof.

Let
A\subset\partial^{2}G
and
g\in G
. Then
{\rm bd}^{-1}(gA)=g\,{\rm bd}^{-1}(A)
and hence

\displaystyle\Theta_{2}(gA)

\displaystyle=\int_{g{\rm bd}^{-1}(A)}f\big((x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\big)\;{\rm{d}}\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{o}\big((x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\big)

\displaystyle=\int_{{\rm bd}^{-1}(A)}f\big((g^{-1}x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\big)\;{\rm{d}}\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{o}\big((x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\big)

(5.4)

\displaystyle=\int_{{\rm bd}^{-1}(A)}f\big((x_{n})_{n}\big)\;{\rm{d}}\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{g}\big((x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\big)

\displaystyle=\int_{{\rm bd}^{-1}(A)}\int_{{\rm bd}^{-1}(\xi)\crossproduct{\rm bd}^{-1}(\eta)}f\big((x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\big)\;{\rm{d}}\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{g}\big((x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|(\xi,\eta)\big)\;{\rm{d}}(\nu_{g}\otimes\nu_{g})(\xi,\eta)

\displaystyle\asymp\int_{{\rm bd}^{-1}(A)}\frac{{\rm{d}}(\nu_{g}\otimes\nu_{g})}{{\rm{d}}(\nu_{o}\otimes\nu_{o})}(\xi,\eta)\;{\rm e}^{2(\xi,\eta)^{{\mathcal{G}}}_{g}}\;{\rm{d}}(\nu_{o}\otimes\nu_{o})(\xi,\eta)

where in (
5.4
) we used, that
f
is invariant under the
G
-shift, and that we can do the same disintegration for
\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{g}
as well.

Finally, since

\frac{{\rm{d}}(\nu_{g}\otimes\nu_{g})}{{\rm{d}}(\nu_{o}\otimes\nu_{o})}(\xi,\eta)=\exp\{\limsup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\xi_{i}\to\xi\\
\eta_{i}\to\eta\end{subarray}}d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(\xi_{i},o)+d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(\eta_{i},o)-d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(\xi_{i},g)-d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(\eta_{i},g)\bigg\}

it is left to show that there exist
c_{1},c_{2}>0
such that

2(\xi,\eta)_{g}^{{\mathcal{G}}}+\limsup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\xi_{i}\to\xi\\
\eta_{i}\to\eta\end{subarray}}d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(\xi_{i},o)+d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(\eta_{i},o)-d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(\xi_{i},g)-d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(\eta_{i},g)\begin{cases}\geq(\xi,\eta)_{o}^{{\mathcal{G}}}-c_{1}\\
\leq(\xi,\eta)_{o}^{{\mathcal{G}}}+c_{2}\end{cases}

for all
\xi,\eta\in\partial G
to prove the quasi-invariance. We have

\displaystyle 2(\xi,\eta)^{{\mathcal{G}}}_{g}

\displaystyle=\limsup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\xi_{i}\to\xi\\
\eta_{i}\to\eta\end{subarray}}d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(g,\xi_{i})+d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(g,\eta_{i})-d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(\xi_{i}\eta_{i})

\displaystyle=\limsup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\xi_{i}\to\xi\\
\eta_{i}\to\eta\end{subarray}}d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(o,\xi_{i})+d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(o,\eta_{i})-d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(\xi_{i}\eta_{i})+d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(o,\xi_{i})+d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(o,\eta_{i})-d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(g,\xi_{i})+d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(g,\eta_{i}).

And since for any fixed sequences
\xi_{i}\to\xi
and
\eta_{i}\to\eta
we have

\absolutevalue{\limsup_{i\to\infty} d_{\skrig}(o,\xi_i)+d_{\skrig}(o,\eta_i)-d_{\skrig}(\xi_i,\eta_i) -(\xi,\eta)_0 }\leq 2\delta

we at most accumulate an error of
4\delta
when splitting the above
\limsup
into two. Switching between the
\limsup
and
\liminf
also just changes the value by at most
2\delta
. Hence

2(\xi,\eta)^{{\mathcal{G}}}_{g}+\limsup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\xi_{i}\to\xi\\
\eta_{i}\to\eta\end{subarray}}d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(o,\xi_{i})+d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(o,\eta_{i})-d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(g,\xi_{i})-d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(g,\eta_{i})\begin{cases}\geq(\xi,\eta)_{o}^{{\mathcal{G}}}-6\delta\\
\leq(\xi,\eta)_{o}^{{\mathcal{G}}}+6\delta\end{cases}

and we have proved the claim.

∎

6.
Randomized Geodesic Flow

Previously in Section

5.1
and Section
5.2
we constructed the two measures
\Theta_{!}
and
\Theta_{2}
which highlight what we expect to see as the limit points of a bi-infinite random walk as we move its origin along the random walk paths.

We now move on to a completely different construction of the measure
\Theta_{3}
mentioned in Theorem
2.1
, Part (iii), which will descend from a measure on the space of all bi-infinite random walk paths. This version
\Theta_{3}
highlights the connection between the
G
-action and the flow along the random walk paths. This connection is also seen in the deterministic setup and is what in the end will lead to the statements of ergodicity.

6.1.
The Measure
\mathbb{Q}
on
G^{\mathbb{Z}}

Recall from Definition
4.2
that we denote by

G^{\mathbb{Z}}:=\bigg\{\text{all bi-infinite random walk paths }(X_{z})_{z\in\mathbb{Z}}\text{ in }G\bigg\}

and write

G_{g}^{\mathbb{Z}}:=\bigg\{\text{all bi-infinite random walk paths }(X_{z})_{z\in\mathbb{Z}}\text{ in }G\text{ with }X_{0}=g\bigg\}

for
g\in G
, and that
G^{\mathbb{Z}}=\bigcup_{g\in G}G^{\mathbb{Z}}_{g}
is a disjoint union.

We now consider the following actions on
G^{\mathbb{Z}}

\displaystyle G\curvearrowright G^{\mathbb{Z}}

\displaystyle\text{ via }g.(x_{z})_{z}:=(gx_{z})_{z}

(6.1)

\displaystyle G^{\mathbb{Z}}\curvearrowleft\mathbb{Z}

\displaystyle\text{ via }k.(x_{z})_{z}:=(x_{z-k})_{z}.

(6.2)

These actions commute, since

g.(k.(x_{z})_{z})=g.(x_{z-k})=(gx_{z-k})_{z}=k.(gx_{z})_{z}=k.(g.(x_{z})_{z}).

Lemma 6.1
.

Recall the measure
\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{g}
from Definition
4.2
. Then

\mathbb{Q}(A):=\sum_{g\in G}\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{g}(A\cap G_{g}^{\mathbb{Z}})

(6.3)

is a well-defined measure on
G^{\mathbb{Z}}
, which is invariant under both the
\mathbb{Z}
-action and the
G
-action defined above.

Proof.

Note that
\mathbb{Q}
is well defined on
G^{\mathbb{Z}}
since
G^{\mathbb{Z}}=\bigcup_{g\in G}G^{\mathbb{Z}}_{g}
is a disjoint union. Since for each
g\in G
, the measure
\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{g}
is
\sigma
-finite, so is
\mathbb{Q}
.

We will now show
G
-invariance of
\mathbb{Q}
. Since for
A\subset G^{\mathbb{Z}}_{x}
and
g\in G
we have
gA\subset G^{\mathbb{Z}}_{gx}
, it suffices to show the invariance for a set
A\subset G^{\mathbb{Z}}_{x}
for some
x\in G
. Then, for
g\in G

\mathbb{Q}(gA)=\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{gx}(gA)=\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{x}(A)=\mathbb{Q}(A)

and hence
\mathbb{Q}
is
G
-invariant.

To show the
\mathbb{Z}
-action, we further restrict ourselves to studying cylinder sets of the form

A=G^{\mathbb{N}}\crossproduct\{g_{-m}\}\crossproduct\{g_{-m+1}\}\crossproduct...\crossproduct\{g_{n}\}\crossproduct G^{\mathbb{N}},

since these generate the
\sigma
-algebra on
G^{\mathbb{Z}}_{g_{0}}
. Since for
g\in G
the measure
\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{g}
is supported on the space of sequences, for which the increments
g_{i-1}^{-1}g_{i}\in S
are in our generating set, the set
A
can be equivalently described by

S^{\mathbb{N}}\crossproduct\{s_{-m}\}\crossproduct...\crossproduct\{s_{-1}\}\crossproduct\{g_{0}\}\crossproduct\{s_{1}\}\crossproduct...\crossproduct\{s_{n}\}\crossproduct S^{\mathbb{N}}.

Then the shifted set
1.A
is equivalently described by

S^{\mathbb{N}}\crossproduct\{s_{-m}\}\crossproduct...\crossproduct\{s_{-1}g_{0}\}\crossproduct\{s_{-1}^{-1}\}\crossproduct\{s_{1}\}\crossproduct...\crossproduct\{s_{n}\}\crossproduct S^{\mathbb{N}}.

By construction of the measures
\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{x}
, this means

\mathbb{Q}(A)=\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{g_{0}}(A)=\mu(s_{-m})...\mu(s_{-1})\mu(s_{1})...\mu(s_{n})

and

\mathbb{Q}(1.A)=\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{s_{-1}g_{0}}(1.A)=\mu(s_{-m})...\mu(s_{-2})\mu(s_{-1}^{-1})\mu(s_{1})...\mu(s_{n}).

And since we assume
\mu
to be symmetric , this means
\mathbb{Q}(A)=\mathbb{Q}(1.A)
, i.e.
\mathbb{Q}
is invariant under the
\mathbb{Z}
-action.
∎

6.2.
The Measures
\widehat{\mathbb{Q}}
and
\Theta_{3}

In order to define the measures
\widehat{\mathbb{Q}}
and
\Theta_{3}
(recall (
2.3
) and Theorem
2.1
, part (iii)) we now move to quotients of
G^{\mathbb{Z}}
by the respective actions on
\mathbb{Z}
and
G
defined in the previous section. We will identify the quotients with the respective fundamental domains, i.e. sets which contain one point of each orbit of the respective action.

The fundamental domain for the
G
-action on
G^{\mathbb{Z}}
can be chosen to be
G^{\mathbb{Z}}_{o}
, since
G^{\mathbb{Z}}=\bigcup_{g\in G}G^{\mathbb{Z}}_{g}
is a disjoint union. This fundamental domain is already equipped with the measure
\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{o}
(which is also the restriction of
\mathbb{Q}
to
G^{Z}_{o}
).

The
\mathbb{Z}
-action on
G^{\mathbb{Z}}
then induces a
\mathbb{Z}
-action on
G^{\mathbb{Z}}_{o}
, which we denote by
(\tau_{z})_{z\in\mathbb{Z}}
. More precisely, we set

\tau_{z}(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}:=x_{z}^{-1}(x_{n-z})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}},\qquad\forall z\in\mathbb{Z}.

We note that, heuristically, this
\mathbb{Z}
-action on
G^{\mathbb{Z}}/G
can be thought of the
flow
along the random walk path, i.e. the analogue of the geodesic flow in the deterministic case.

We now define the fundamental domain for the
\mathbb{Z}
-action. This is slightly more tricky and will only be identified up to a set of measure
0
. Indeed, since the random walk paths almost surely diverge (recall Theorem
3.11
), we may restrict
G^{\mathbb{Z}}
to paths which, for any
n
, stay within a ball
B_{n}(o)
of radius
n
only a finite amount of time. A fundamental domain for the
\mathbb{Z}
-action on this restriction of
G^{\mathbb{Z}}
is then

\displaystyle{\mathcal{D}}=\bigcup_{g\in G}\bigg\{(x_{z})_{z}:x_{0}=g,

\displaystyle d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(x_{z},o)>d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(x_{0},o)\text{ for }z<0,

(6.4)

\displaystyle\mbox{and}\,\,d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(x_{z},o)\geq d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(x_{0},o)\text{ for }z>0\bigg\}.

In other words, we choose the parametrization of
(x_{z})_{z\in\mathbb{Z}}
according to where it gets closest to
o
the first time. We again identify the quotient space with this fundamental domain. The measure
\widehat{\mathbb{Q}}
on the quotient space is the restriction of
\mathbb{Q}
to the fundamental domain, i.e.

\widehat{\mathbb{Q}}(B)=\mathbb{Q}(B\cap{\mathcal{D}})\quad\forall B\subset G^{\mathbb{Z}}.

(6.5)

Since
G^{\mathbb{Z}}
is the space of all random walk paths with all parametrizations, the quotient by
\mathbb{Z}
can be thought of the space of all sequences in
G
without a specific choice of parametrization.

Theorem 6.2

Let
\Theta_{3}:={\rm bd}_{\ast}\widehat{\mathbb{Q}}
be the push-forward under the boundary map. Then
\Theta_{3}
is invariant for the
G
-action on
\partial^{2}G
and satisfies

\frac{{\rm{d}}\Theta_{3}}{{\rm{d}}(\nu_{o}\otimes\nu_{o})}(\xi,\eta)\asymp{\rm e}^{2(\xi,\eta)_{o}^{{\mathcal{G}}}}.

Theorem
6.2
is the main result of this section. Following the intuition outlined in Section
6.3
and building on the technical lemmas proved in Section
6.4
, Theorem
6.2
will be proved in Section
6.5
.

6.3.
A Guiding Example: the Free Group

Due to the highly disconnected nature of free groups, the proof that
\Theta_{3}
has the desired density is much easier in the case that
G
is a free group. Although proving Theorem
6.2
is significantly harder in the case of general hyperbolic groups, we will outline the proof for this case here as a guiding philosophy.

For simplicity we will study the case of the simple random walk on
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. In other words, we set
G
to be the free group with
q
generators and
S
a minimal, symmetric generating set on
G
. Let
\mu
be the uniform distribution on
S
. The Cayley graph of
G
with respect to
S
then is a
2q
-regular tree
{\Gamma}
.

For
g\in G
, we refer to
{\mathcal{T}}_{g}
as the set of branches at
g
, meaning the connected components in
\Gamma\setminus\{g\}
which do not contain
o
. One element
T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{g}
is a branch at
g
. Let
{\mathcal{T}}=\bigcup_{g}{\mathcal{T}}_{g}
. The
\sigma
-algebra on
\partial G
is then generated by the sets
\{(x_{n})_{n}\text{ fully lies in }T\text{ eventually}\}
for all
T\in{\mathcal{T}}
.

Let
B_{1},B_{2}
be two connected, disjoint subsets of
\partial G
and
T_{1},T_{2}\in{\mathcal{T}}
the two branches in
G
, such that
{\rm bd}^{-1}(B_{i})=\{(x_{n})_{n}\text{ lies in }T_{i}\text{ eventually}\}
for
i=1,2
.
Here it suffices to look at disjoint sets, since in
\partial^{2}G
the diagonal is removed. Furthermore, the pre-images under the boundary map are indeed of this form, since we chose
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
to be connected.

We now consider two cases, either
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
are subsets of different branches at
o
, or they lie in the same one. In the first case

\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{g}[(X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in T_{2}\text{ eventually},X_{i}\not\in B_{d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(g,o)-\delta},(X_{-n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in T_{1}\text{ eventually},X_{-i}\not\in B_{d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(g,o)}]=0

for all
g\neq o
, since any geodesic from
T_{1}
to
T_{2}
has to pass through
o
. So

\Theta(B_{1}\crossproduct B_{2})=\mathbb{P}_{o}[(X_{n})\in T_{1}]\mathbb{P}_{o}[(X_{-n})\in T_{2}]=\nu_{o}(B_{1})\nu_{o}(B_{2}).

And since the Green metric in the tree is actually additive along geodesics, we have
(\xi,\eta)^{{\mathcal{G}}}_{o}=0
for all
\xi\in T_{1},\eta\in T_{2}
. This means the density is correct for all points in different branches off of
o
.

The second case is that
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
lie in the same branch at
o
. Since they are disjoint, however, there does exist a point
x\in G
such that
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
lie in different branches off of
x
. Then

\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{g}[(X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in T_{2}\text{ eventually},X_{i}\not\in B_{d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(g,o)-\delta},(X_{-n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in T_{1}\text{ eventually},X_{-i}\not\in B_{d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(g,o)}]\neq 0

if and only if
g\in[o,x]
. Now, since on the tree the restriction to
\{X_{i}\not\in B_{d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(g,o)-\delta}\}
just means that we might not step towards
o
from
g
, and similarly
\{X_{i}\not\in B_{d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(g,o)}\}
means we might not step towards
o
from
x_{1}
, the ratios

\displaystyle c_{1}=\frac{\mathbb{P}_{g}[(X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in T_{1}\text{ eventually},X_{i}\not\in B_{d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(g,o)}]}{\mathbb{P}_{g}[(X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in T_{1}\text{ eventually}]}\qquad\text{ and }

\displaystyle c_{2}=\frac{\mathbb{P}_{g}[(X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in T_{2}\text{ eventually},X_{i}\not\in B_{d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(g,o)-\delta}]}{\mathbb{P}_{g}[(X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in T_{2}\text{ eventually}]},

do not depend on the base point
g
. Together with the fact that

\mathbb{P}_{g}[(X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in T_{i}\text{ eventually}]=\nu_{g}(T_{i})\qquad\mbox{for}\qquad i=1,2,

we get

\displaystyle\sum_{g\in[o,x]}\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{g}\Big[(X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in T_{2}\text{ eventually},X_{i}\not\in B_{d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(g,o)-1},(X_{-n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in T_{1}\text{ eventually},X_{-i}\not\in B_{d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(g,o)}\Big]

\displaystyle=c_{1}c_{2}\sum_{g\in[o,x]}\nu_{g}(B_{1})\nu_{g}(B_{2})

\displaystyle=c_{1}c_{2}\sum_{g\in[o,x]}\int_{B_{1}\crossproduct B_{2}}\exp\{\limsup_{\xi_{i}\to\xi,\eta_{i}\to\eta}d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(o,\xi_{i})+d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(o,\eta_{i})-(d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(g,\xi_{i})+d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(g,\eta_{i}))\bigg\}{\rm{d}}(\nu_{o}\otimes\nu_{o})

\displaystyle=c_{1}c_{2}\int_{B_{1}\crossproduct B_{2}}{\rm e}^{2(\xi,\eta)^{{\mathcal{G}}}_{o}}\sum_{g\in[o,x]}{\rm e}^{-2d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(g,x)}{\rm{d}}(\nu_{o}\otimes\nu_{o})

Since
\sum_{g\in[o,x]}{\rm e}^{-2d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(g,x)}
converges as
x\to\infty
,
\Theta
thus has the correct density on all products of connected, disjoint sets.

Remark 7
(
Relation between
\Theta_{1}
,

6.4.
Bounds on the Greens Function and Combinatorial Arguments

As remarked earlier, in general hyperbolic groups we cannot assume that by removing a ball of a certain size we disconnect points from each other. Due to the geometric nature of hyperbolic groups it does however significantly increase the distance between points on opposite sides of the ball. {\mathcal{G}}(a,b;B_{n}^{c}(0))\leq\sum_{a_{0}\in H_{0}}...\sum_{a_{M}\in H_{M}}\prod_{i=0}^{M}{\mathcal{G}}(a_{i},a_{i+1}).

Denote by

the operator defined as follows

(L_{i}f)(a)=\sum_{b\in H_{i}}{\mathcal{G}}(a,b)f(b).

Then the above sum can be written as
(L_{0}...L_{M}\delta_{b})(a)
and hence

{\mathcal{G}}(a,b,B_{n}^{c}(0))\leq\prod_{i=1}^{M}\norm{L_i}_{\ell^{2}}.

By the Cauchy Schwartz inequality we have

\displaystyle\norm{L_i}_{\ell^{2}}^{2}=\sum_{a\in H_{i}}\Big|\sum_{b\in H_{i+1}}{\mathcal{G}}(a,b)f(b)\Big|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{a\in H_{i}}\Big(\sum_{b\in H_{i+1}}{\mathcal{G}}(a,b)^{2}\Big)\Big(\sum_{b\in H_{i+1}}f(b)^{2}\Big)=\sum_{a\in H_{i},b\in H_{i+1}}{\mathcal{G}}(a,b)^{2}\norm{f}_{\ell^{2}}^{2}

and hence by the bounds on the Green function shown in Lemma
6.4

{\mathcal{G}}(a,b;B_{n}^{c}(0))\leq({\rm e}^{-rn})^{M}{\mathcal{G}}(b,b^{n})=\exp\{-rn\alpha\,{\rm e}^{\varepsilon n}\big\}{\mathcal{G}}(b,b^{n}).

∎

As remarked earlier, the proof of the upper bound is much more involved.
We will split the sum in

roughly into three terms, namely

(i)

(i)

(iii)

Theorem 7.2

Let

be a continuous function on

which is integrable with respect to
\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{o}
. Then

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{l=0}^{n}f(\tau_{l}(x_{z})_{z\in\mathbb{Z}})=\int_{G^{\mathbb{Z}}_{0}}f{\rm{d}}\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{o}

almost surely.

Proof.

By construction,
\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{o}
is invariant under the induced
\mathbb{Z}
-action by
\{\tau_{z}:z\in\mathbb{Z}\}
. Hence by Birkhoff’s ergodic theorem

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{l=0}^{n}f(\tau_{l}(x_{z})_{z\in\mathbb{Z}})=\mathbb{E}_{0}[f|{\mathcal{F}}^{\tau_{1}}](x_{z})_{z\in\mathbb{Z}}

almost surely, where
{\mathcal{F}}_{\tau_{1}}
is the
\tau_{1}
-invariant sub-
\sigma
-algebra of the product-
\sigma
-algebra on
G^{\mathbb{Z}}_{0}
. But in the same way also

\lim_{n\to-\infty}\frac{1}{-n}\sum_{l=0}^{n}f(\tau_{l}(x_{z})_{z\in\mathbb{Z}})=\mathbb{E}_{0}[f|{\mathcal{F}}^{\tau_{-1}}](x_{z})_{z\in\mathbb{Z}}

almost surely, where we’re conditioning on the
\tau_{-1}
-invariant
\sigma
-algebra. But since
\tau_{1}
and
\tau_{-1}
are inverse to each other,
{\mathcal{F}}_{\tau_{1}}={\mathcal{F}}_{\tau_{-1}}
and hence the limits agree almost surely as well. We denote this limit by
f_{\infty}
.

What is left to do , is to show that
f_{\infty}
is indeed constant. We first regard the case that
f
is a simple function over cylinder sets. Notice that by the above argument,
f_{\infty}(x_{z})_{z\in\mathbb{Z}}=f_{\infty}(x_{-z})_{z\in\mathbb{Z}}
, so
f_{\infty}
is invariant under time-reversal. Furthermore,
f
only depends on finitely many indices.
This also means that
f_{\infty}(x_{z})_{z\in\mathbb{Z}}
only depends on the positive indices of
(x_{z})_{z\in\mathbb{Z}}
, since at some point
\tau_{l}(x_{z})_{z\in\mathbb{Z}}
has moved

so far back that

does not depend on
(x_{-n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

under time-reversal, this also means that
f_{\infty}
does not depend on the positive indices of
(x_{z})_{z\in\mathbb{Z}}
. And hence,

is constant almost surely. The identity

then also follows from Birkhoff’s ergodic theorem.

For a general continuous function

i\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to\infty}\absolutevalue{\int_{G_{o}^{\Z}} f_1^m\circ\tau_{t_i^1} \cdot...\cdot f_r^m\circ\tau_{t_i^r} d \bar\P_o - \int_{G_{o}^{\Z}} f_i^m d \bar\P_o \cdot...\cdot\int_{G_{o}^{\Z}} f_r^m d \bar\P_o }

\displaystyle=\absolutevalue{\int_{G_{o}^{\Z}} f_1\circ\tau_{t_i^1} \cdot...\cdot f_r\circ\tau_{t_i^r} d \bar\P_o - \int_{G_{o}^{\Z}} f_1 d \bar\P_o \cdot...\cdot\int_{G_{o}^{\Z}} f_r d \bar\P_o}

So using dominated convergence,

\displaystyle 0=\lim_{i\to\infty}\absolutevalue{\int_{G_{o}^{\Z}} f_1\circ\tau_{t_i^1} \cdot...\cdot f_r\circ\tau_{t_i^r} d \bar\P_o - \int_{G_{o}^{\Z}} f_i d \bar\P_o \cdot...\cdot\int_{G_{o}^{\Z}} f_r d \bar\P_o }.

This proves the first statement of Theorem
2.6
regarding mixing of all orders.

For Hölder continuous functions
f_{i}
we want to be a bit more careful regarding how we choose the approximating functions
f_{i}^{m}
.
We recall that we equip
X=G^{\mathbb{Z}}_{o}
with the metric
d(x,y)=\sum_{j}2^{-j}\max\{1,d_{\mathcal{G}}(x,y)\}
which is equivalent to the following metric on
G^{\mathbb{Z}}_{o}
:

d^{\prime}(x,y)={\rm e}^{-n(x,y)}\qquad\mbox{where}\quad n(x,y)=\max\{|k|:x_{k}\neq y_{k}\}.

(7.2)

Both metrics induce the product topology. We also recall that
a function
f:G^{\mathbb{Z}}_{o}\to\mathbb{R}
is Hölder continuous if there exists
C=C_{f}>0
and
\alpha=\alpha_{f}>0
such that

x_{k}=y_{k}\quad\forall|k|\leq n\qquad\mbox{implies}\qquad|f(x)-f(y)|\leq C{\rm e}^{-\alpha n}

(7.3)

This is equivalent to requiring that
|f(x)-f(y)|\leq Cd(x,y)^{\alpha}
for all
x,y\in G^{\mathbb{Z}}_{o}
with
d(x,y)=\sum_{j}2^{-j}\max\{1,d_{\mathcal{G}}(x,y)\}
. For any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we define

\mathcal{F}_{n}:=\sigma(x_{-n},\cdots.x_{n}\big)

It suffices to show the exponential mixing for two functions
f,g
. The rest follows inductively. We approximate
f,g
by simple functions as follows.

Lemma 7.4
.

If
f\in L^{\infty}(\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{o})
is Hölder continuous with constant
C
and exponent
\alpha>0
, then for
f_{n}=\mathbb{E}^{\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{o}}[f|\mathcal{F}_{n}]

\|f-f_{n}\|_{\infty}\leq C{\rm e}^{-\alpha n}

Proof.

Fix
x\in G^{\mathbb{Z}}_{o}
. Then the conditional expectation
f_{n}=\mathbb{E}^{\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{o}}[f|\mathcal{F}_{n}]
is constant on the cylinder set
C_{x}^{n}:=\{y\in G^{\mathbb{Z}}_{o}:y_{-n,\dots,n}=x_{-n,\dots,n}\}\in\mathcal{F}_{n}
. For any
y\in C_{x}^{n}
, we have
n(x,y)\geq n
(see (
7.2
) above). Since
f
is assumed to be Hölder continuous (see (
7.3
)), we have for
y\in C_{x}^{n}
,
|f(y)-f(x)|\leq C{\rm e}^{-\alpha n}
. Using these two facts we have

|f_{n}(x)-f(x)|=\bigg|\int_{C_{x}^{n}}(f(y)-f(x))\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{o}({\rm{d}}y|\mathcal{\mathcal{}}{F}_{n})\bigg|\leq C{\rm e}^{-\alpha n}.

Taking supremum over
x\in G^{\mathbb{Z}}_{o}
shows
\|f-f_{n}\|_{\infty}\leq C{\rm e}^{-\alpha n}
, proving the lemma.
∎

With this we can prove

Proposition 7.5
.

Let us assume
r=2
, and let
f,g\in L^{\infty}(\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{o})
be Hölder continuous functions with exponent
\alpha=\min\{\alpha_{f},\alpha_{g}\}>0
. Then for all
s,t\in\mathbb{Z}
,

\bigg|\int(f\circ\tau_{t})(g\circ\tau_{s}){\rm{d}}\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{o}-\int f{\rm{d}}\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{o}\int g{\rm{d}}\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{o}\bigg|\leq C(f,g){\rm e}^{-\delta|t-s|},\qquad\delta=\alpha/2>0.

Proof of Proposition
7.5
.
Let

\delta_{f,g}(t-s):=\int(f\circ\tau_{t})(g\circ\tau_{s}){\rm{d}}\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{o}-\int f{\rm{d}}\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{o}\int g{\rm{d}}\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{o}=\int f\,\,(g\circ\tau_{t-s}){\rm{d}}\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{o}-\int f{\rm{d}}\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{o}\int g{\rm{d}}\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{o}

Also we set

f_{n}:=\mathbb{E}^{\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{o}}[f|\mathcal{F}_{n}],\qquad g_{n}:=\mathbb{E}^{\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{o}}[g|\mathcal{F}_{n}]

so that
f_{n}
depends on the coordinates in
[-n,n]
and
g_{n}\circ\tau_{t-s}
depends on the coordinates in
[t-s-n,t-s+n]
so that if
\ell\geq 1
is an integer such that
|t-s|\geq 2n+\ell
, then the two windows are separated by at least
\ell\geq 1
. Hence, as in (
7.1
),

\delta_{f_{n},g_{n}}(t-s)=0\qquad\forall|t-s|\geq 2n+\ell.

(7.4)

Therefore,

\displaystyle|\delta_{f,g}(t-s)|

\displaystyle=|\delta_{f,g}(t-s)-\delta_{f_{n},g_{n}}(t-s)|

\displaystyle=\bigg|\int f\,\,g\circ\tau_{t-s}{\rm{d}}\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{o}-\int f{\rm{d}}\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{o}\int g\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{o}-\int f_{n}\,\,g_{n}\circ\tau_{t-s}{\rm{d}}\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{o}+\int f_{n}{\rm{d}}\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{o}\int g_{n}{\rm{d}}\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{o}\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\bigg|\int f\,\,g\circ\tau_{t-s}{\rm{d}}\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{o}-\int f_{n}\,\,g_{n}\circ\tau_{t-s}{\rm{d}}\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{o}\bigg|+\bigg|\int f_{n}{\rm{d}}\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{o}\int g_{n}{\rm{d}}\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{o}-\int f{\rm{d}}\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{o}\int gd\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{o}\bigg|

\displaystyle=(I)+(II)

Now

\displaystyle(I)

\displaystyle=\bigg|\int f\,\,g\circ\tau_{t-s}{\rm{d}}\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{o}-\int f_{n}\,\,g_{n}\circ\tau_{t-s}{\rm{d}}\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{o}\bigg|

\displaystyle=\bigg|\int f\,\,g\circ\tau_{t-s}-\int f_{n}{\rm{d}}\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{o}\,\,g\circ\tau_{t-s}{\rm{d}}\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{o}+\int f_{n}\,\,g\circ\tau_{t-s}{\rm{d}}\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{o}-\int f_{n}\,\,g_{n}\circ\tau_{t-s}{\rm{d}}\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{o}\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\|f-f_{n}\|_{\infty}\|g\|_{\infty}+\|f_{n}\|_{\infty}\|g-g_{n}\|_{\infty}

using that
\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{o}
is
\mathbb{Z}
invariant. Moreover, note that, by definition and by Jensen’s inequality,
\|f_{n}\|_{\infty}\leq\|f\|_{\infty}
(and likewise
\|g_{n}\|_{\infty}\leq\|g\|_{\infty}
). We now apply Lemma
7.4
to estimate
\|f-f_{n}\|_{\infty}
and
\|g-g_{n}\|_{\infty}
. Likewise,

\displaystyle(II)

\displaystyle=\bigg|\int f_{n}{\rm{d}}\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{o}\int g_{n}{\rm{d}}\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{o}-\int f{\rm{d}}\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{o}\int g{\rm{d}}\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{o}\bigg|

\displaystyle=\bigg|\int(f_{n}-f){\rm{d}}\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{o}\int g_{n}{\rm{d}}\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{o}+\int f{\rm{d}}\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{o}\int g_{n}{\rm{d}}\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{o}-\int f{\rm{d}}\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{o}\int g{\rm{d}}\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{o}\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\|f_{n}-f\|_{\infty}\|g_{n}\|_{\infty}+\|f\|_{\infty}\|g_{n}-g\|_{\infty}

and once more we use Lemma
7.4
to estimate
\|f-f_{n}\|_{\infty}
and
\|g-g_{n}\|_{\infty}
and invoke
\|g_{n}\|_{\infty}\leq\|g\|_{\infty}
. So choosing

2n:=|t-s|-\ell-1,\qquad\delta:=\frac{\alpha}{2}.

proves Proposition
7.5
. Therefore, Theorem
2.6
is also proved now. ∎

Proof of Theorem
2.7
.
We note that it is equivalent to look at the space of increments
(S^{\mathbb{Z}},\mu^{\otimes\mathbb{Z}})
instead of
(G^{\mathbb{Z}}_{o},\mathbb{P}_{o})
, and Hölder continuous functions
f:S^{\mathbb{Z}}\to\mathbb{R}
(recall that
S
is the finite symmetric generating set of
G
and
\mu
is the step distribution which is supported on
S
).

Assume
f
is of the form
f\big((s_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\big)=f_{m}\big((s_{n})_{n=-\infty}^{m}\big)
for some
m
fixed. Then by
[
Wu05
, Theorem 3 (i)]
, for every
m
,

\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{m}\circ\tau_{i}((S_{n})_{n})-n\mathbb{E}^{\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{o}}[f_{m}(S_{n})_{n}]}{\sqrt{n}}\to\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_{m}^{2})

(7.5)

in distribution as
n\to\infty
, where

\sigma_{m}^{2}=\norm{\sum_{n \in\N}\E^{\bar\P_o}\bigg[f_m\circ\tau_n(S_z)_z\big\vert\sigma(S_i: i\leq 0)\bigg] - \E^{\bar\P_o}\bigg[f_m\circ\tau_n(S_n)_n\vert\sigma(S_i: i< 0)\bigg]}_{2}^{2}<\infty.

Indeed, since
f_{m}
is Hölder continuous, for every
m
,

w^{m}(n):=\norm{\E^{\bar\P_o}[f_m\circ\tau_n(S_n)_n\vert\sigma(S_i: i\leq 0)] - \E^{\bar\P_o}[f_m\circ\tau_n(S_n)_n\vert\sigma(S_i: i< 0)]}_{2}\leq C_{f_{m}}{\rm e}^{-\alpha_{f_{m}}n}

and hence
\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}w^{m}(n)<\infty
and so is
\sigma_{m}
. For general Hölder continuous functions
f
, we again approximate
f
by
f_{m}:=\mathbb{E}[f|\mathcal{F}_{m}]
(recall Lemma
7.4
). Note that,

\absolutevalue{\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_m\circ\tau_i ((S_n)_{n}) - n \E^{\bar\P_o}[f_m(S_n)_n] - \sum_{i=1}^{n}f\circ\tau_i ((S_n)_{n}) - n \E^{\bar\P_o}[f(S_n)_n]}\leq 2C_{f}n{\rm e}^{-\alpha_{f}m}

which decays in
n
if we choose for example
m=n
. Thus, by (
7.5
), we conclude that the central limit theorem also holds for
f
by Slutsky’s theorem, using also that by dominated convergence
\sigma_{m}^{2}\to\sigma^{2}
as
m\to\infty
.
∎

Appendix A

Relations and Differences with Some Percolation Models in Statistical Mechanics

A different, systematic way of sampling bi-infinite random walk paths was uncovered in a seminal work of Sznitman
[
Szn10
]
on random interlacements in the context of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, and generalized to transient weighted graphs
\Gamma
by Teixeira
[
Tei09
]
.
We will now underline the differences in the two viewpoints in the discussion below and in Example
A
we will discuss a concrete case to demonstrate these differences.

We restrict the construction in
[
Tei09
]
to the test case that
\Gamma
is the Cayley graph of a finitely generated group with respect to a generating set
S
and we are regarding the random walk on
\Gamma
with increments with symmetric law
\mu
supported on
S
.

Let
L
be the Cayley graph of
\mathbb{Z}
with respect to the generating set
\{-1,1\}
and for
-\infty\leq n\leq m\leq\infty
, denote by
L(n,m)
the restriction of
L
to the vertices
\{n,...,m\}
. Further, we denote by
\mathcal{W}(n,m)
the set of all graph homomorphism
L(n,m)\to\Gamma
such that the pre-image of each vertex in
\Gamma
is finite. Finally

\mathcal{W}=\bigcup_{n,m}\mathcal{W}(n,m)
.
On
\mathcal{W}
the time shift
s_{k}:\mathcal{W}(n,m)\to\mathcal{W}(n-k,m-k)
is then defined by
s_{k}(v_{i})=v_{i-k}
and
s_{k}(e(i,i+1))=e(i-k,i+1+k)
where the vertex
v_{i}
is the image of
i
in the graph homomorphism, and similarly
e(i,i+1)
the image of the edge between
i
and
i+1
. The space of trajectories is then given by
\mathcal{W}^{\ast}=\mathcal{W}/\sim
where
w_{1}\sim w_{2}
if
w_{1}=s_{k}(w_{2})
for some
k\in\mathbb{Z}
.

In the case that
\Gamma
is the Cayley graph of a group
G
,
\mathcal{W}(-\infty,\infty)
is a systematic way of representing the restriction of the support of
\mathbb{Q}
on
G^{\mathbb{Z}}
to paths which escape to
\infty
. It is however in the measures that the different points of view become apparent.

As shown in
[
Tei09
]
,
\mathcal{W}^{\ast}
is then equipped with a measure
\mathcal{Q}^{\ast}
, which is invariant under graph automorphisms of
G
. This measure is uniquely identified by

\mathcal{Q}^{\ast}(A\cap\mathcal{W}_{K})=\mathcal{Q}_{K}(\pi^{-1}(A))

for
A\subset\mathcal{W}^{\ast}
and
K
a finite set of vertices in
G
, where
\pi
is the projection
\mathcal{W}\to\mathcal{W}^{\ast}
,
\mathcal{W}_{K}
are the trajectories which visit
K
, and
\mathcal{Q_{K}}
is defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{K}\big(w\in\mathcal{W}\,:\,w_{|(-\infty,0]}\in B_{1},\,w_{|[0,\infty)}\in B_{2}\big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{u\in K}\mathbb{P}_{u}\big((X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in\bar{B}_{1},\tau_{K}=\infty\big)\mathbb{P}_{u}\big((X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in\bar{B}_{2}\big)

where
\mathbb{P}_{u}
is the distribution of the random walk started at
u
and
\tau_{K}
being the first hitting time in
K
after time
0
. Note that since
K
is a finite set of vertices, this only gives us information on the behavior of the measure
\mathcal{Q}^{\ast}
inside the graph
G
. Indeed,
\mathcal{Q}^{\ast}
is a measure on the one-point compactification of
G
in the following way.

Let
V_{n}
be an increasing sequence of vertices in
G
exhausting
V
and set
G_{n}
to be the graph gained from
G
by identifying all points in
V_{n}^{c}
to one point
\partial_{n}
. Setting for
K\subset V_{n}

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{K}^{n}\big(w\in\mathcal{W}\,:\,w_{|(-\infty,0]}\in B_{1},\,w_{|[0,\infty)}\in B_{2}\big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{u\in K}\mathbb{P}_{u}\big((X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in\bar{B}_{1},\tau_{K}>\tau_{\partial_{n}},\tau_{\partial_{n}}<\infty\big)\mathbb{P}_{u}\big((X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in\bar{B}_{2},\tau_{\partial_{n}}<\infty\big)

where
\tau_{\partial_{n}}
is the hitting time in
\partial_{n}
. After normalizing this measure by
\mathcal{Q}_{K}^{n}(\mathcal{W})
, this is the distribution of a random walk started at the ”point at infinity”
\partial_{n}
and killed upon returning to
\partial_{n}
, conditioned to hitting
K
for the first time at time
0
. Now, we again get a measure
\mathcal{Q}^{n\ast}
identified by

\mathcal{Q}^{\ast}(A\cap\mathcal{W}_{K})=\mathcal{Q}_{K}(\pi^{-1}(A))

and since
\mathcal{Q}_{K}^{n}\to\mathcal{Q}_{K}
weakly as
n\to\infty
, also
\mathcal{Q}^{n\ast}\to\mathcal{Q}^{\ast}
.
See also for example
[
Hu18
, Sec. 3]
, for a more detailed write-up of this.

While our quotient
\mathbb{Z}\backslash G^{\mathbb{Z}}
is, up to a set of measure zero, isomorphic to
\mathcal{W}(-\infty,\infty)
, the measure
\widehat{\mathbb{Q}}
differs from
\mathcal{Q}
due to our construction of the bi-infinite random walks with fixed origin
g
.
Thus, it is the quotient measure spaces constructed that give rise to an essential difference between
\widehat{\mathbb{Q}}
and
\mathcal{Q}
.

Recall that we defined
\mathbb{Q}=\sum_{g\in G}\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{g}
and
\widehat{\mathbb{Q}}
as the restriction of
\mathbb{Q}
to an almost sure fundamental domain
\mathcal{D}
of the
\mathbb{Z}
-shift on
G^{\mathbb{Z}}
.
In particular, for
K\subset G
finite,

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathbb{Q}}((x_{z})_{z\in\mathbb{Z}}\text{ hits }K)=\sum_{g\in G}\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{g}\big((X_{z})_{z\in\mathbb{Z}}\cap K\neq\emptyset,d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(o,X_{-n})>d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(o,g),d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(o,X_{n})\geq d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(o,g)\text{for }n>0\big)

which reduces the summands to points
g
such that
K
is not fully contained in the ball
\{g^{\prime}:d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(g^{\prime},o)<d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(g,o)\}
. Note that while for
K=o
this agrees with
\mathcal{Q}_{K}
, for any other choice of
K
it does not, as in our definition the set
K
can be hit by both the negative time part of
(X_{z})_{z\in\mathbb{Z}}
as well as by the positive time part of the path.

Note that, while both
\mathcal{Q}^{\ast}
and
\widehat{\mathbb{Q}}
are invariant for the
G
-action on the quotient
\mathbb{Z}\backslash G^{Z}
, they are so in subtly different ways. Since
\widehat{\mathbb{Q}}
is defined on an almost sure fundamental domain of the
\mathbb{Z}
-action, it is invariant under the induced
G
-action on the domain. This action shifts a path
(x_{z})_{z\in\mathbb{Z}}
and then reparametrizes it.
\mathcal{Q}^{\ast}
on the other hand is identified via the push-forward of measures
\mathcal{Q}_{K}
on
\mathcal{W}
. In particular this means that for any
g\in G

\mathcal{Q}^{\ast}(\text{trajectory hits }K)=\mathcal{Q}^{\ast}(\text{trajectory hits }gK)

while in general

\widehat{\mathbb{Q}}(\text{trajectory hits }K)\neq\widehat{\mathbb{Q}}(\text{trajectory hits }gK).

Finally, since the bi-infinite random walk
(X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
converges to random points
X_{\infty}
and
X_{-\infty}
in
\partial G
as
z\to\infty
and
z\to-\infty
respectively and by definition
\widehat{\mathbb{Q}}
can measure the behavior of the limits on the boundary.
In particular, our measure
\widehat{\mathbb{Q}}
does not live on the one-point compactification of
G
but rather in the Gromov compactification
G\cup\partial G
.

Example A.1

To further illustrate the difference between the intensity measures of the random interlacements and the measure
\widehat{\mathbb{Q}}
, we discuss this example which shows the different behavior of the measures for sets inside the group.

Let
T
be the Cayley graph of
\mathbb{F}_{2}
the free group in two generators
a
and
b
with respect to the symmetric edge set
S=\{a,a^{-1},b,b^{-1}\}
.
We consider our bi-infinite random walk paths
(X_{z})_{z\in\mathbb{Z}}
as described above with driving measure
\mu=\text{Unif}(S)
. Let
K=\{k\}\subset\mathbb{F}_{2}
and consider the event
A=\{\text{ the bi-infinite path hits }k\}
. This set can be seen as a subset of either notion of space of bi-infinite random walk paths.

To calculate
\widehat{\mathbb{Q}}(A)
, we first notice that the restrictions
d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(X_{z},o)>d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(g,o)\text{ for }z<0
and
d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(X_{z},o)\geq d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(g,o)\text{ for }z>0
mean that the summands in the definition of
\widehat{\mathbb{Q}}
are only positive for origins
g\in[o,k]
, the unique geodesic between
o
and
k
. Let

c^{1}_{g}=\frac{\mathbb{P}_{g}\big((X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in A,d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(X_{n},o)>d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(g,o)\text{ for all }n\big)}{\mathbb{P}_{g}\big((X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in A\big)}

and

c^{2}_{g}=\frac{\mathbb{P}_{g}\big((X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in A,d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(X_{n},o)\geq d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(g,o)\text{ for all }n\big)}{\mathbb{P}_{g}\big((X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in A\big)}.

Note that these values actually just depend on the distance between
g
and
k
.
Then with
\widehat{\mathbb{Q}}
defined in (
2.3
),

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathbb{Q}}(A)

\displaystyle=\sum_{g\in[o,k]}\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{g}\big((X_{z})_{z\in\mathbb{Z}}\in A,d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(X_{z},o)>d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(g,o)\text{ for }z<0,d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(X_{z},o)\geq d_{{\mathcal{G}}}(g,o)\text{ for }z>0\big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{g\in[o,k]}(c^{1}_{g}+c^{2}_{g})\big(2\mathbb{P}_{g}((X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in A)-\mathbb{P}_{g}((X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in A)^{2}\big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{g\in[o,k]}(c^{1}_{g}+c^{2}_{g})\big(2F(g,k)-F(g,k)^{2}\big).

It is well known, that for the simple random walk on a
q
-regular tree, we have

F(x,y)=(q-1)^{-d(x,y)}

where
d
is the graph metric (cf.
[
Woe00
]
, Lemma 1.24).
In our case this leads to

\widehat{\mathbb{Q}}(A)=\sum_{g\in[o,k]}(c^{1}_{g}+c^{2}_{g})\big(2\cdot 3^{-d(g,k)}-9^{-d(g,k)}\big)

i.e. it is positive and increases in the distance of
k
to
o
.

For the intensity measure of random interlacements however

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}^{\ast}(\mathcal{W}^{\ast}_{K})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{Q}_{K}(\mathcal{W})=\mathbb{P}_{k}((X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\text{ does not return to }k)

\displaystyle=1-\sum_{g\sim k}\mu(gk^{-1})F(k,g)=\frac{2}{3}.

Which, as stated above, is constant over all choices of
k
. This demonstrates that the two measures are not equal.

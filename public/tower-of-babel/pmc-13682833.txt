Itaconate as the endogenous brake of proinflammatory macrophage activation alleviates inflammation in liver fibrogenesis
Authors: Kai Zhang, Tong Li, Wenhui Liu, Weiyang Li, Lin Yang, Na Chang, Liying Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background:Liver inflammation and fibrosis are reparative responses to chronic liver injury. Bone marrow–derived monocyte/macrophages (BMDMs) are key participants. Our previous study demonstrated that BMDM can be activated as a proinflammatory phenotype by sphingosine-1-phosphate (S1P). Aconitate decarboxylase 1 (ACOD1)/itaconate is an anti-inflammatory molecule. However, it remains unclear whether ACOD1/itaconate is involved in S1P-induced BMDM activation.Methods:A methionine-choline–deficient and high-fat diet was used to induce liver injury and fibrosis in mice. To clarify the role of endogenous ACOD1/itaconate in BMDMs in liver fibrosis, we established 2 types of chimeric mice: (1) bone marrow (BM) Acod1 deficiency (WT or Acod1 knockout BM was transplanted into wild-type mice) and (2) exclusive BM Acod1 expression (WT or Acod1 knockout BM was transplanted into Acod1 knockout mice).Results:Single-cell RNA sequencing data revealed that Acod1 was highly expressed in injured BMDMs. Acod1 + BMDMs exhibited a proinflammatory phenotype with elevated expression of proinflammatory genes. In vitro, ACOD1 expression and itaconate production were upregulated in S1P-treated BMDMs. Endogenous ACOD1/itaconate in BMDMs suppressed BMDM proinflammatory activation by forming negative feedback with NF-κB signaling. S1P-regulated ACOD1 expression and BMDM activation via the same signaling pathway (S1PR2/MAPK/NF-κB signaling), indicating the endogenous brake function of ACOD1/itaconate. In chimeric mice with BM Acod1 deficiency, the abolition of the endogenous brake function of ACOD1/itaconate aggravated liver inflammation/fibrosis. Exclusive BM Acod1 expression showed the opposite effects.Conclusions:ACOD1/itaconate exerts an endogenous brake function in the proinflammatory activation of BMDMs during chronic liver injury. Our data demonstrate the role of ACOD1/itaconate in S1P-induced BMDM activation, further indicating that ACOD1/itaconate is a potential therapeutic strategy for liver inflammation/fibrosis.

INTRODUCTION

Liver fibrosis, a common pathological process characterized by excessive deposition of extracellular matrix, is a reparative response to chronic liver injury induced by various etiologies, such as fatty liver injury.1 During liver fibrogenesis, hepatic inflammation is a necessary process that is closely associated with the initiation and development of fibrosis.2 As important components of the hepatic immune system, macrophages are key participants in liver inflammation and fibrosis.3 In a normal liver, hepatic macrophages are primarily composed of resident Kupffer cells (KCs). However, during liver injury, many bone marrow (BM)-derived monocyte/macrophages (BMDMs) are recruited into the injured liver.3 After recruitment to the injured liver, BMDMs are activated into the proinflammatory subtype by proinflammatory mediators, such as sphingosine-1-phosphate (S1P).45 Activated BMDMs secrete various inflammatory cytokines and chemokines to further amplify the inflammatory response.26 Thus, proinflammatory macrophages have been demonstrated to be potential therapeutic targets for liver fibrosis.7 Given the lack of specific and effective therapies for liver fibrosis, further studies on the factors regulating the proinflammatory activation of macrophages are necessary.

S1P, a bioactive lipid, has been reported to be an important inducer of liver inflammation and fibrosis.8 S1P regulates cellular responses by binding to specific G protein–coupled receptors, namely S1PR1-S1PR5.9 Previous studies have demonstrated that S1P/S1PRs play an important role in the proinflammatory activation of BMDMs. Our previous publications reported that S1P/S1PR2 promotes the expression of proinflammatory genes (NLRP3, IL-1β, and IL-18) and mediates NLRP3 inflammasome activation in cholestatic liver injury via Gα(12/13)/MAPK signaling.10 More importantly, the selective blockade of macrophage S1PR2 significantly attenuates liver inflammation and fibrosis.5 However, it is still necessary to investigate the detailed mechanisms by which S1P/S1PRs regulate proinflammatory macrophage activation with the aim of identifying potential antifibrotic therapies through the inhibition of macrophage activation.

Aconitate decarboxylase 1 (ACOD1), also known as immune-responsive gene 1 (IRG1), encodes a key enzyme that catalyzes itaconate production.11 ACOD1/itaconate has been reported to be an anti-inflammatory mediator in immune cells. In macrophages, ACOD1 is upregulated by proinflammatory mediators (such as lipopolysaccharide and IFN-γ), leading to increased production of itaconate.12 In lipopolysaccharide-activated or IFN-γ-activated macrophages, itaconate suppresses the production of inflammatory cytokines in various manners, such as by inhibiting succinate dehydrogenase, inhibiting IRAK4 to cause degradation of NF-κB, and activating Nrf2 to suppress proinflammatory gene transcription. 11 Owing to its immunomodulatory effects, ACOD1/itaconate has shown significant potential in the treatment of liver diseases.13 Our recent study also demonstrated that Acod1 deficiency promotes liver inflammation, indicating the protective function of ACOD1/itaconate in chronic liver diseases.14 However, it is still unclear whether ACOD1/itaconate is involved in S1P-induced proinflammatory activation of BMDMs in liver injury.

In the present study, we focused on the role of ACOD1/itaconate in BMDM activation during liver inflammation and fibrosis. Single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) data revealed that ACOD1 was highly expressed in injured BMDMs. Further analysis indicated that Acod1 + BMDMs exhibited a proinflammatory phenotype with elevated expression of proinflammatory genes. In vitro, ACOD1 expression and itaconate production were upregulated in BMDMs treated with S1P. Endogenous ACOD1/itaconate in BMDMs suppressed BMDM proinflammatory activation through the inhibition of NF-κB signaling. Furthermore, S1P-regulated ACOD1 expression via S1PR2/MAPK/NF-κB signaling, which is also involved in the proinflammatory activation of BMDMs, indicating the endogenous brake function of ACOD1/itaconate in S1P-induced BMDM activation. In chimeric mice with BM Acod1 deficiency, the abolition of the endogenous brake function of ACOD1/itaconate aggravated liver inflammation and fibrosis. Exclusive BM Acod1 expression had opposite effects. Our data demonstrate the role of ACOD1/itaconate in S1P-induced proinflammatory BMDM activation and its underlying mechanism, further indicating that ACOD1/itaconate is a potential therapeutic strategy for liver inflammation and fibrosis.

METHODS

Mouse model

Male ICR or C57BL/6 N mice (aged 6–8 wk, n=6 per group) were used as chronic liver injury models. To induce chronic liver injury, the mice were fed with a 5 g/d control diet (CD) or methionine-choline–deficient and high-fat diet (MCDHFD, A06071309, Research Diet Inc.) containing 45 kcal% fat, 0.1% methionine, and no added choline. To construct chimeric mice, wild-type (WT) and Acod1 knockout (KO) mice (C57BL/6 N, aged 6–8 wk, male, n≥6 per group) were subjected to lethal irradiation (8 Gy) for 8 min and immediately transplanted with 1×107 whole WT or KO BM cells via the tail vein. Four weeks later, the mice were fed an MCDHFD for 2 weeks.

All mice were maintained in a specific pathogen-free, temperature-controlled environment with a 12-hour light/dark cycle. The mice were anesthetized and sacrificed to collect blood and liver samples at the indicated time points after modeling. All animal experiments were performed in accordance with the ethical guidelines of the Ethics Committee of the Capital Medical University (approval number: AEEI-2022-212).

Primary BMDM culture

C57BL/6 N mice (aged 6–8 wk) were anesthetized and sacrificed by cervical dislocation. BM cells were flushed from the marrow cavity with saline via a 25 G needle and filtered using a 70 μm cell strainer (BD Bioscience). After centrifugation at 1200 rpm for 5 min, the cell pellet was resuspended and then differentiated in complete RPMI-1640 medium (containing 10% FBS, 1% penicillin-streptomycin, and 10% L929 cell-conditioned medium) for 7 days.

Analysis of scRNA-seq data

We employed the Loupe Browser 7 (10× Genomics, Pleasanton, CA, USA) for the analysis of Acod1 expression and the differentially expressed genes in Acod1 + BMDMs. GO and KEGG analyses were performed via Metascape.15

HPLC

To measure itaconate content in macrophages, ice-cold 80% methanol was added to the cells after the indicated treatment. After sonication in an ice bath, the cells were completely disrupted. The lysate was subsequently centrifuged twice at 13,000 rpm for 10 min to remove proteins. Supernatants were collected and dried using a Termovap Sample Concentrator. Subsequently, the fluid phase (0.05% aqueous phosphoric acid: acetonitrile=95:5) was added to redissolve the soluble substances, including itaconate, to avoid solvent effects. After vortexing, the samples were centrifuged for another 10 min to remove insoluble substances. All samples were run on an Agilent 1260 HPLC system (Agilent Technologies Inc), an X Select C18 column (3.0×150 mm, 5 μm; water), and a multiwavelength UV detector was used for detection.

Statistical analysis

Data are expressed as mean ± SEM. First, the normality of the datasets was analyzed. ANOVA and Student t test were used to analyze data that conformed to a normal distribution. For data that did not conform to a normal distribution, nonparametric statistical tests were employed. p<0.05 was considered significant. All results were verified using at least 3 independent experiments.

Additional materials and methods can be found in the Supplemental Materials, https://links.lww.com/HC9/C455.

RESULTS

Elevated expression of Acod1 is correlated with enhanced inflammatory gene expression in BMDMs during chronic liver injury

First, we studied the expression pattern of Acod1 in mice with chronic liver injury by analyzing our previous scRNA-seq data.16 Hepatic nonparenchymal cells were isolated from the livers of mice fed with CD or MCDHFD for 14 days and then subjected to scRNA-seq (Figure 1A). The results revealed that Acod1 was highly expressed in the monocyte and macrophage (MoM) cluster in hepatic nonparenchymal cells (Figure 1B and C, above). Furthermore, Acod1 expression was significantly increased in injured MoMs (Figure 1C, below). In accordance with this result, the calculation of the Acod1 + MoM proportion revealed that ~78.7% of Acod1 + MoMs were injured cells (Figure 1D, left). The percentage of Acod1 + cells in the MoM population was also analyzed. The results showed that the percentage of Acod1 + MoMs increased from ~1.5% (CD) to ~15.7% (MCDHFD) during liver injury (Figure 1D, right).

Second, we selected MoMs for further analysis. The MoMs were subjected to reclustering analysis and divided into 3 subpopulations (KCs, BMDMs, and monocytes; Figure 1E, left). The t-SNE and violin plots showed that Acod1 was specifically expressed in the BMDM cluster (Figure 1E, middle). The calculation of the Acod1 + MoM proportion revealed that ~73.4% of Acod1 + MoMs were BMDMs, whereas the proportions of KCs and monocytes were ~10.7% and ~16%, respectively (Figure 1E, right). Furthermore, Acod1 expression in BMDMs was subjected to in-depth analysis. The results showed that Acod1 was specifically and highly expressed in injured BMDMs, whereas ~96.8% of Acod1 + BMDMs were isolated from MCDHFD-fed mice (Figure 1E, below). The expression of ACOD1 was very low in normal BMDMs (Figure 1E, below). These results demonstrate that Acod1 is significantly increased in MoMs, especially BMDMs, during chronic liver injury.

Third, we focused on the characteristics of Acod1 + BMDMs during liver injury. We analyzed the genes that were differentially expressed in Acod1 + BMDMs (p<0.05, compared with Acod1 − BMDMs) and performed GO analysis on the genes that were highly expressed in Acod1 + BMDMs. Interestingly, we found that Acod1 + BMDMs participated in inflammatory processes, such as “activation of immune response,” “positive regulation of innate immune response,” “inflammatory response,” and “leukocyte activation” (Figure 1G). Violin plots showed that Acod1 + BMDMs highly expressed various proinflammatory genes, including Nlrp3, Il1b, Il18, Ccl2, Ccl4, and Tnf (Figure 1F). Moreover, we examined the correlation between the expression of Acod1 and proinflammatory genes in the process of liver injury and demonstrated that the mRNA expression of Acod1 was positively correlated with the expression of these genes (Figure 1H). These results demonstrate that increased expression of ACOD1 and elevated expression of proinflammatory genes occur in the same BMDM population, indicating that ACOD1 is correlated with increased inflammatory gene expression in BMDMs during chronic liver injury.

ACOD1 expression and itaconate production are upregulated in proinflammatory BMDMs activated by S1P

We then isolated primary BMDMs to explore the relationship between ACOD1 and proinflammatory activation of BMDMs in vitro. We used S1P, which has been reported to be a powerful inducer of BMDM proinflammatory activation in a previous publication,10 to treat BMDMs. RT-qPCR results revealed that the mRNA expression of Acod1 increased continuously as the concentration of S1P increased (Figure 2A, left). We also detected the expression of Acod1 in BMDMs treated with S1P over different time periods. We found that Acod1 expression increased significantly from 2 to 4 hours and then stabilized at this elevated level until 12 hours (Figure 2A, right). Consistent with these findings, S1P upregulated the protein level of ACOD1 in a time-dependent and concentration-dependent manner (Figures 2B, C, Supplemental Figure S3A, https://links.lww.com/HC9/C455). We then used HPLC to detect itaconate production in S1P-treated BMDMs, which revealed that S1P also promoted itaconate production in a dose-dependent manner (Figure 2D, left, and 2E, Supplemental Figure S2, https://links.lww.com/HC9/C455). The level of itaconate was also elevated in the hepatic macrophages isolated from MCDHFD-fed mice (Figure 2D, right, and 2F, Supplemental Figure S2, https://links.lww.com/HC9/C455). Furthermore, the expression of proinflammatory genes (Nlrp3, Il1b, Il18, Ccl2, and Ccl4) was upregulated by S1P treatment in BMDMs and positively correlated with Acod1 expression (Figure 2G). These data demonstrate that S1P elevates ACOD1 expression and itaconate production concurrently with the induction of proinflammatory activation in BMDMs in vitro, further indicating the close association between ACOD1/itaconate and BMDM proinflammatory activation.

Endogenous ACOD1/itaconate in BMDMs suppresses the activation of BMDMs and NF-κB signaling

Next, we studied the role of ACOD1/itaconate in S1P-activated BMDMs. Primary BMDMs were isolated from Acod1 KO mice and treated with S1P. The mRNA expression levels of Nlrp3, Il1b, Il18, Ccl2, and Ccl4 increased in S1P-treated BMDMs, and these increases were further enhanced in BMDMs with Acod1 deficiency (Figure 3A). Acod1 deficiency also increased protein levels of NLRP3, pro-IL-1β, and pro-IL-18 (Figure 3B, Supplemental Figure S4A, https://links.lww.com/HC9/C455). We also employed Acod1 siRNA to further confirm these results. Western blot results showed that Acod1 siRNA also increased the protein levels of NLRP3 and pro-IL-18 in S1P-treated BMDMs (Figure 3C, Supplemental Figure S4B, https://links.lww.com/HC9/C455). Furthermore, we used 4-octyl itaconate (4-OI), an itaconate derivative, to treat proinflammatory activated BMDMs and found that 4-OI significantly downregulated the expression of proinflammatory genes and the production of IL-1β in S1P-treated BMDMs, indicating that itaconate blocked the activation of BMDMs induced by S1P (Figures 3D–F, Supplemental Figure S5A, https://links.lww.com/HC9/C455). To further explore the mechanism underlying ACOD1/itaconate inhibition of BMDM proinflammatory activation, we analyzed the representative signaling pathway in Acod1 + BMDMs using scRNA-seq data. KEGG analysis showed that NF-κB signaling was activated in Acod1 + BMDMs (Figure 3G). Herein, we investigated the effects of ACOD1/itaconate on NF-κB signaling in S1P-treated BMDMs. BMDMs were treated with or without S1P and 4-OI, and nuclear and cytoplasmic proteins were isolated for further study. S1P-induced the nuclear translocation of NF-κB-p65, and 4-OI markedly blocked the effects of S1P (Figure 3H, Supplemental Figure S5B, https://links.lww.com/HC9/C455), indicating that elevated ACOD1/itaconate suppresses S1P-induced proinflammatory BMDM activation via the inhibition of NF-κB signaling activation.

S1P induces ACOD1 expression and BMDM proinflammatory activation via the same signaling pathway

Our previous study demonstrated that S1P promotes the proinflammatory activation of BMDMs via the S1PR2/MAPK signaling pathway.10 Here, we explored the signaling pathways involved in S1P-regulated ACOD1 expression. We first investigated whether S1P-regulated ACOD1 expression occurred in an S1PR-dependent manner. To answer this question, we used dihydro-S1P (H2S1P), a structural analog of S1P that binds and activates S1PRs, to treat BMDMs. In primary BMDMs, H2S1P promoted ACOD1 expression in a manner similar to S1P, suggesting that S1P increased ACOD1 expression via S1PRs (Figure 4A). We then used specific S1PR antagonists (W146 for S1PR1, JTE-013 for S1PR2, and CAY10444 for S1PR3) to explore the type of S1PR involved in S1P-induced ACOD1 expression. The results showed that JTE-013, an antagonist of S1PR2, inhibited the upregulation of ACOD1 expression induced by S1P (Figures 4B, C, Supplemental Figure S3B, https://links.lww.com/HC9/C455).

We next studied the signaling pathway involved in S1P/S1PR2-mediated ACOD1 upregulation. We added inhibitors of known S1PR downstream pathways to S1P-treated BMDMs. Blockade of MAPK signaling (MEK, JNK, ERK, and P38) suppressed S1P-induced ACOD1 upregulation, whereas inhibition of PI3K, PKA, and ROCK had no effect (Figure 4D). Interestingly, bortezomib, an antagonist of NF-κB, also significantly blocked the effects of S1P, indicating that ACOD1/itaconate/NF-κB is involved in negative feedback in proinflammatory activated BMDMs (Figures 4D–F). These data demonstrate that S1P increases ACOD1/itaconate levels and promotes BMDM proinflammatory activation via the same signaling pathway (S1PR2/MAPK/NF-κB), which results in negative feedback in BMDMs. ACOD1/itaconate is the endogenous brake for BMDM proinflammatory activation.

Abolition of the endogenous brake function of ACOD1/itaconate in BMDMs aggravates liver inflammation and fibrosis

To clarify the importance of the endogenous brake function of ACOD1/itaconate in BMDMs during liver inflammation and fibrosis, we constructed chimeric mice with BM Acod1 deficiency. BM cells were isolated from WT or Acod1 KO mice and transplanted into lethally irradiated WT mice. Four weeks after BM reconstruction, we isolated BM cells from chimeric mice and performed genotyping to verify the success of BM transplantation (Supplemental Figure S1, https://links.lww.com/HC9/C455). Then, chimeric mice (WT/WT or WT/KO) were fed an MCDHFD for another 2 weeks (Figure 5A). First, serum levels of AST and ALT were higher in the WT/KO group than in the WT/WT group (Figure 5B). Second, hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining showed that the area of inflammation was expanded in the livers of BM Acod1-deficiency mice (Figures 5C, D). Moreover, the expression of proinflammatory genes (Nlrp3, Il1b, and Nos2) was upregulated by BM Acod1 deficiency (Figure 5E). Finally, we examined the degree of liver fibrosis by measuring the expression of fibrosis marker genes and collagen deposition. BM Acod1 deficiency significantly increased collagen deposition (Figures 5F, G) and upregulated the expression of fibrosis marker genes (collagen type I, collagen type III, and α-SMA) in the livers of MCDHFD-fed mice (Figures 5H, I, Supplemental Figure S6, https://links.lww.com/HC9/C455). Meanwhile, non-irradiated WT mice fed with CD (WT+CD) served as the negative control. The results showed that MCDHFD induced liver injury, inflammation and fibrosis in all groups. These results reveal that abolishing the endogenous brake function of ACOD1/itaconate in BMDMs (BM Acod1 deficiency) aggravates chronic liver injury, inflammation, and fibrosis.

Restoration of the endogenous brake function of ACOD1/itaconate alleviates liver inflammation and fibrosis

To further confirm this conclusion, we constructed chimeric mice with exclusive BM Acod1 expression. BM cells isolated from WT or Acod1 KO mice were transplanted into KO mice that were subjected to lethal irradiation. After genotyping (Supplemental Figure S1, https://links.lww.com/HC9/C455), KO/KO or KO/WT chimeric mice were subsequently fed an MCDHFD (Figure 6A). The WT+CD group was also employed as the negative control. Compared to those in KO/KO mice, the serum levels of ALT and AST in KO/WT mice were decreased (Figure 6B). In chimeric mice with exclusive BM Acod1 expression, the degree of inflammation was inhibited by BM-derived ACOD1/itaconate since the inflammation area and proinflammatory gene expression were decreased in the livers of KO/WT mice (Figures 6C–E). Moreover, BM-derived ACOD1/itaconate markedly alleviated liver fibrosis. Compared with that in KO/KO mice, the expression of fibrosis marker genes was significantly downregulated in the livers of KO/WT mice, whereas collagen deposition was lower (Figures 6F–I, Supplemental Figure S6, https://links.lww.com/HC9/C455). These results demonstrate that ACOD1/itaconate derived from BM cells, especially BMDMs, has inhibitory effects on liver inflammation and fibrosis, further supporting the endogenous brake function of ACOD1/itaconate.

DISCUSSION

In the present study, we demonstrated that ACOD1/itaconate performs an endogenous brake function in the proinflammatory activation of BMDMs during chronic liver injury. In the fibrotic liver, ACOD1 is highly expressed in hepatic macrophages (especially BMDMs), and its expression is very low in normal BMDMs. Moreover, ACOD1+ BMDMs exhibit a stronger proinflammatory phenotype. In vitro, we found that S1P/S1PR2/MAPK/NF-κB signaling, which mediates the proinflammatory activation of BMDMs, also elevates ACOD1 expression and itaconate production in BMDMs. Further studies indicate that endogenous ACOD1/itaconate in BMDMs forms a negative feedback loop with NF-κB signaling to inhibit BMDM proinflammatory activation. Furthermore, the deficiency of BM Acod1 aggravates liver inflammation and fibrosis, whereas exclusive expression of BM Acod1 alleviates these effects (Figure 7).

S1P/S1PRs have been reported to be important mediators of macrophage functions. In metabolic dysfunction–associated steatohepatitis, deletion of S1PR1 in myeloid cells reduces inflammatory macrophages and attenuates metabolic dysfunction–associated steatohepatitis.17 In acute lung injury, nuclear S1P induces oxidative stress and NLRP3 inflammasome activation by promoting p53 acetylation in macrophages.18 S1P/S1PR1 also promotes proinflammatory macrophage activation and NLRP3 inflammasome activation in Lupus nephritis.19 In microglia, S1P functions as a TREM2 ligand to promote phagocytosis and protect against ischemic brain injury,20 while S1P impedes the phagocytosis of tumor cells in diffuse large B-cell lymphoma.21 Our previous studies have demonstrated the importance of S1P/S1PRs-mediated macrophage functions in liver inflammation and fibrosis. In cholestatic liver injury, S1P induces BMDM migration toward the injured liver via S1PR2 and S1PR3.22 The blockade of S1PR2/3-mediated BMDM migration markedly alleviated liver inflammation and fibrosis.2223 Further research indicates that S1P and S1PR2/3 regulate BMDM migration via the HuR/miR-455-5p/IQGAP1 axis.16 In fatty liver injury, S1P upregulates MCP-1 expression via miRNA-1249-5p.24 Besides, S1P promotes proinflammatory activation of macrophages via S1PR2/MAPK signaling.10 S1PR2 also promotes ZBP1/p-MLKL axis-mediated necroptosis of macrophages by binding to GDCA.25 Thus, selective inhibition of macrophage S1PR2 has potent anti-inflammatory and antifibrotic effects.5 In the present study, we report a new mechanism by which S1P regulates the proinflammatory activation of BMDMs. S1P upregulates ACOD1 expression and itaconate production through S1PR2/MAPK/NF-κB signaling in BMDMs. Endogenous ACOD1/itaconate in BMDMs performs anti-inflammatory functions on S1P-induced BMDM activation.

Although elevated ACOD1 expression has been widely reported in inflammatory macrophages, the mechanisms involved in the regulation of ACOD1 expression have not been fully elucidated. In Mycobacterium tuberculosis infection, ACOD1 expression is induced by two phagocytosis-dependent signaling pathways: the TLR2/MyD88/NF-κB and STING/IFN-I/IFNAR pathways.26 In a zebrafish infection model, the C/EBP and JAK/STAT pathways regulate macrophage ACOD1 expression.27 In addition, the transcription factors TFEB and IRF1 have been reported to regulate ACOD1 expression in macrophages.2829 Here, we demonstrate that NF-κB signaling is also involved in S1P-induced ACOD1 expression, which forms a negative feedback loop to regulate proinflammatory BMDM activation. Furthermore, we report a new signaling pathway that regulates ACOD1 expression, S1PR2/MAPK signaling. Our previous study has reported that S1PR2/MAPK signaling is also involved in the proinflammatory activation of BMDMs.10 These results demonstrate that S1P induces ACOD1 expression and BMDM activation through the same signaling pathway and that ACOD1 performs its anti-inflammatory functions via negative feedback with NF-κB. ScRNA-seq data reveal that the upregulation of ACOD1 and the elevated expression of proinflammatory genes occurred in the same BMDM population, indicating ACOD1/itaconate as the endogenous brake of S1P-induced BMDM activation.

ACOD1/itaconate, which was first studied in activated macrophages under proinflammatory stimuli,12 has been reported to be an important anti-inflammatory mediator of inflammation. The mechanism by which ACOD1/itaconate exerts its anti-inflammatory effects has been reported in lipopolysaccharide-activated or IFN-γ-activated macrophages. For example, itaconate reduces proinflammatory cytokine production by inhibiting succinate dehydrogenase.30 KEAP1/Nrf2 signaling, the key player in defense against oxidative stress, has also been identified as a target of ACOD1/itaconate.31 By regulating the activation of KEAP1/Nrf2, itaconate and its derivatives (4-OI and dimethyl itaconate) exert anti-inflammatory functions.323334 In addition, itaconate promotes NF-κB degradation by targeting IRAK4.35 Itaconate has also been reported to bind to DNA dioxygenase TET-2, resulting in the inhibition of TET-2 catalytic activity and inflammation in macrophages.36 Furthermore, itaconate can regulate the alkylation of key proinflammatory genes, such as GSDMD and NLRP3, which directly inhibit pyroptosis and inflammasome activation in macrophages.373839 Here, we report for the first time that ACOD1/itaconate is also involved in S1P-induced proinflammatory activation of BMDMs in liver injury. In fibrotic livers, ACOD1 level is specifically elevated in BMDMs. In vitro, S1P promotes the expression of ACOD1 and results in the production of itaconate in an S1PR2/MAPK/NF-κB-dependent manner. S1P-induced ACOD1 expression and itaconate production in BMDMs alleviate liver inflammation and fibrosis.

Accumulating evidence has validated the protective effects of ACOD1/itaconate against chronic liver injury. In thioacetamide (TAA)-triggered liver fibrosis, ACOD1/itaconate alleviates liver fibrosis via inhibiting HSC ferroptosis.40 By modulating the NRF2 pathway, 4-OI ameliorates liver damage and fibrosis in CCl4-induced and TAA-induced murine liver fibrosis models.4142 In primary sclerosing cholangitis, itaconate inhibits the activation of tissue-resident memory T cells to attenuate hepatobiliary injury.43 In MASLD, Acod1 deficiency aggravates high-fat diet-induced metabolic disturbance, while 4-OI reverses liver steatosis.4445 Recently, our study has demonstrated the protective role of neutrophil-derived ACOD1/itaconate in chronic liver injury. In normal liver tissue, ACOD1 is predominantly expressed in neutrophils. During liver injury, the expression of ACOD1 in neutrophils is significantly upregulated. Mechanistically, neutrophil-derived itaconate inhibits GSDMD cleavage and neutrophil extracellular trap formation by modulating the Cys192 site of GSDMD.14 In the present study, we further investigated the role of macrophage-derived ACOD1/itaconate in liver inflammation and fibrosis. ScRNA-seq data indicate that the proportion of ACOD1+ macrophages is extremely low in normal liver tissue, whereas it is significantly increased in injured liver tissue, suggesting an important role of macrophage-derived ACOD1/itaconate in liver inflammation and fibrosis. In contrast to previous investigations, our study reveals a novel mechanism by which ACOD1/itaconate participates in liver fibrosis. Specifically, endogenous ACOD1/itaconate acts as an intrinsic molecular brake to restrain S1P-driven proinflammatory activation of BMDMs. Collectively, these findings further support the potent anti-inflammatory and antifibrotic potential of ACOD1/itaconate in chronic liver injury from multiple perspectives, providing a comprehensive understanding of its functions in chronic liver diseases and highlighting ACOD1/itaconate as a potential therapeutic strategy for liver inflammation and fibrosis.

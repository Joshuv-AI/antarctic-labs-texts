Abstract
. In this paper, we study the optimal regularity of the stationary sonic-subsonic solution to the unipolar isothermal hydrodynamic model of semiconductors with sonic boundary. Applying the comparison principle and the energy estimate, we obtain the regularity of the sonic-subsonic solution as C 1 2 ​ [ 0 , 1 ] ∩ W 1 , p ​ ( 0 , 1 ) C^{\frac{1}{2}}[0,1]\cap W^{1,p}(0,1) for any p < 2 p<2 , which is then proved to be optimal by analyzing the property of solution around the singular point on the sonic line, i.e., ρ ∉ C ν ​ [ 0 , 1 ] \rho\notin C^{\nu}[0,1] for any ν > 1 2 \nu>\frac{1}{2} , and ρ ∉ W 1 , κ ​ ( 0 , 1 ) \rho\notin W^{1,\kappa}(0,1) for any κ ≥ 2 \kappa\geq 2 . Furthermore, we explore the influence of the semiconductors effect on the singularity of solution at sonic points x = 1 x=1 and x = 0 x=0 , that is, the solution always has strong singularity at sonic point x = 1 x=1 for any relaxation time τ > 0 \tau>0 , but, once the relaxation time is sufficiently large τ ≫ 1 \tau\gg 1 , then the sonic-subsonic steady-states possess the strong singularity at both sonic boundaries x = 0 x=0 and x = 1 x=1 . We also show that the pure subsonic solution ρ \rho belongs to W 2 , ∞ ​ ( 0 , 1 ) W^{2,\infty}(0,1) , which can be embedded into C 1 , 1 ​ [ 0 , 1 ] C^{1,1}[0,1] , and it is much better than the regularity of sonic-subsonic solutions.

1.
Introduction

This paper is a continuity of the series of previous studies
[
21
,
22
]
on subsonic steady-states for the Euler-Poisson equations with sonic boundary. For the charged fluid particles such as electrons and holes in semiconductor devices, the presented system is the 1-D hydrodynamic model of semiconductors, the so-called Euler-Poisson equations
[
4
]
:

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\rho_{t}+(\rho u)_{x}=0,\\
&(\rho u)_{t}+\left(\rho u^{2}+P(\rho)\right)_{x}=\rho E-\frac{\rho u}{\tau},\\
&E_{x}=\rho-b(x),\end{aligned}\right.

where
(x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,\infty)
with
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}
is the bounded domain. Without loss of generality, we take
\Omega=[0,1]
. The unknowns
\rho
and
u
represent the electron density and the electron velocity, respectively. The function
E
represents the electric field, which is generated by the Coulomb force of particles. The pressure function
P(\rho)
denotes the pressure-density relation. When the system is isothermal,
P(\rho)
is physically represented by

(1.2)

\displaystyle P(\rho)=T\rho,\,\,\,\,\,\mbox{with the constant temperature $T>0$}.

The constant
\tau>0
denotes the relaxation time. The given function
b(x)>0
is the doping profile standing for the density of impurities in semiconductor materials. For more details we refer to
[
19
,
25
]
. Throughout the paper, we assume that the doping profile
b(x)\in L^{\infty}(0,1)
and denote

(1.3)

\displaystyle\underline{b}:=\underset{x\in(0,1)}{{\rm essinf}}\,\,b(x)\,\,\,\,\,\mbox{and}\,\,\,\,\,\bar{b}:=\underset{x\in(0,1)}{{\rm esssup}}\,\,b(x).

In this paper, we consider the steady-state equations to (
1.1
) in the bounded domain
[0,1]
. Let
J=\rho u
be the current density of the electrons. Then we have the stationary equations of (
1.1
) as follows

(1.4)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &J\equiv constant,\\
&\left(\frac{J^{2}}{\rho}+P(\rho)\right)_{x}=\rho E-\frac{J}{\tau},\,\,\,\,\,\,\,x\in(0,1),\\
&E_{x}=\rho-b(x).\end{aligned}\right.

According to the terminology from gas dynamics, we call
c:=\sqrt{P^{{}^{\prime}}(\rho)}=\sqrt{T}>0
the sound speed by (
1.2
). Thus, the corresponding electron velocity
u
of the system (
1.4
) is
said to be subsonic / or sonic / or supersonic, if

(1.5)

\displaystyle\mbox{fluid velocity}:\,\,u</\mbox{or}=/\mbox{or}>c:\,\,\mbox{sound speed}.

Noting that if
(\rho(x),E(x))
is a pair of solution to equation (
1.4
) for a given constant
J
, then
(\rho(1-x),-E(1-x))
is a solution to equation (
1.4
) with respect to
-J
and
b(1-x)
. So we consider the case of
J>0
. Without loss of generality, we take

(1.6)

\displaystyle T=1\,\,\,\,\,\mbox{and}\,\,\,\,\,J=1.

Therefore, (
1.4
) is equivalently reduced to

(1.7)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\left(\frac{1}{\rho}-\frac{1}{\rho^{3}}\right)\rho_{x}=E-\frac{1}{\tau\rho},\,\,\,\,\,\,\,x\in(0,1),\\
&E_{x}=\rho-b(x).\end{aligned}\right.

In what follows from (
1.5
)-(
1.6
), it can be identified that
\rho>1
represents for the subsonic flow,
\rho=1
for the sonic flow, and
0<\rho<1
for the supersonic flow. Now we impose (
1.7
) with the sonic boundary condition

(1.8)

\displaystyle\rho(0)=\rho(1)=1.

Differentiating the equation
\eqref{eq6}_{1}
with respect to
x
and according to the equation
\eqref{eq6}_{2}
, we derive

(1.9)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\left(\left(\frac{1}{\rho}-\frac{1}{\rho^{3}}\right)\rho_{x}\right)_{x}=\rho-b-\left(\frac{1}{\tau\rho}\right)_{x},\\
&\rho(0)=\rho(1)=1.\end{aligned}\right.

When
\rho(x)>1
for
x\in(0,1)
, the above model (
1.9
) is elliptic but denegerate at the boundary, the solution of (
1.9
) generally loses certain regularity. So we introduce the
following definition of weak solution by
[
21
]
.

Definition 1.1
.

\rho(x)
is called an interior subsonic solution, if
\rho(x)\geq 1
with
\rho(0)=\rho(1)=1
to equation (
1.9
), and
\left(\rho(x)-1\right)^{2}\in H_{0}^{1}(0,1)
, and satisfies that, for any
\phi\in H_{0}^{1}(0,1)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\left[\left(\frac{1}{\rho}-\frac{1}{\rho^{3}}\right)\rho_{x}+\frac{1}{\tau\rho}\right]\cdot\phi_{x}dx+\int_{0}^{1}(\rho-b)\cdot\phi dx=0,

namely,

(1.10)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\left[\frac{\rho+1}{2\rho^{3}}[(\rho-1)^{2}]_{x}+\frac{1}{\tau\rho}\right]\cdot\phi_{x}dx+\int_{0}^{1}\left(\rho-b\right)\cdot\phi dx=0.

Once
\rho
is determined from equation (
1.9
), we can further solve the electric field
E(x)
by

\displaystyle E(x)=\left(\frac{1}{\rho}-\frac{1}{\rho^{3}}\right)\rho_{x}+\frac{1}{\tau\rho}=\frac{(\rho+1)[(\rho-1)^{2}]_{x}}{2\rho^{3}}+\frac{1}{\tau\rho}.

Hence, finding the solution of equation (
1.4
) with the condition (
1.8
) amounts to solving equation (
1.9
).

When the boundary is subjected to be sonic, Li-Mei-Zhang-Zhang

The optimal regularity of solution for the stationary semiconductor model with sonic boundary is still
unclear. Thus, exploring this question will be the main goal of this paper.

In this paper, we are devoted to studying the optimal regularity and the corresponding singularity of the sonic-subsonic solution
\rho

(1.11)

(Existence

Proof of the first part of Theorem
1.1
. First of all, we prove the Hölder-index of (

) for
\rho
and

Step 1. Preliminary regularity.

Using the weak maximum principle to (

(2.1)

\displaystyle 1\leq\rho\leq\bar{b},

which implies

(2.2)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\leq w\leq F(\bar{b}).

Multiplying
\eqref{eq10}_{1}

w-\frac{1}{2}
and integrating it by parts over

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}|w_{x}|^{2}dx

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{1}(\rho-b)\cdot\left(w-\frac{1}{2}\right)dx-\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{\rho}\cdot w_{x}dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{1}|\rho-b|\cdot\left|w-\frac{1}{2}\right|dx+\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{0}^{1}\left|\frac{1}{\rho}\right|\cdot|w_{x}|dx

(2.3)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{1}|\bar{b}|\cdot\left|F(\bar{b})-\frac{1}{2}\right|dx+\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{0}^{1}\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{2}|w_{x}|^{2}+\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}\left|\frac{1}{\rho}\right|^{2}\right)dx,

where
\varepsilon>0
is a parameter. Taking
\varepsilon=\tau
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}|w_{x}|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{0}^{1}|\bar{b}|\cdot\left|F(\bar{b})-\frac{1}{2}\right|dx+\frac{1}{\tau^{2}}\int_{0}^{1}\left|\frac{1}{\rho}\right|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{0}^{1}|\bar{b}|\cdot\left|F(\bar{b})-\frac{1}{2}\right|dx+\frac{1}{\tau^{2}}.

Therefore, we obtain

, namely,
w\in H^{1}(0,1)
. Since
H^{1}(0,1)\hookrightarrow C^{\frac{1}{2}}[0,1]

Step 2. Further regularity.

Since

. Finally, we derive

, which means

.

Since

and
\rho+1\neq 0
for

[(\rho-1)^{2}]_{x}\in C^{0}[0,1]

(\rho-1)^{2}\in C^{1}[0,1]
.

A straightforward calculation gives

\displaystyle\frac{|\rho(x)-\rho(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|}

\displaystyle=\frac{|(\rho(x)-1)-(\rho(y)-1)|\cdot|(\rho(x)-1)-(\rho(y)-1)|}{|x-y|}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|(\rho(x)-1)-(\rho(y)-1)|\cdot|(\rho(x)-1)+(\rho(y)-1)|}{|x-y|}

. This, together with

.

From (

\displaystyle h\int_{0}^{1}\left|\left({\rm log}\left(1+\frac{V^{+}}{h}\right)\right)_{x}\right|^{2}dx+\int_{0}^{1}(f(u)-f(w))\cdot\frac{V^{+}}{V^{+}+h}dx

\displaystyle h\int_{0}^{1}\left|\left({\rm log}\left(1+\frac{V^{+}}{h}\right)\right)_{x}\right|^{2}dx+\int_{0}^{1}(f(u)-f(w))\cdot\frac{V^{+}}{V^{+}+h}dx

\displaystyle\frac{h}{\tau}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{f(u)f(w)}\cdot\left|\frac{f(u)-f(w)}{V^{+}+h}\right|\cdot\left|\frac{V^{+}_{x}}{V^{+}+h}\right|dx=:I.

Because

\displaystyle I\leq

\displaystyle\frac{h}{\tau}\int_{T}\mathcal{C}\left|\left({\rm log}\left(1+\frac{V^{+}}{h}\right)\right)_{x}\right|dx+\frac{h}{\tau}\int_{T^{c}}\left|\frac{f(u)-f(w)}{V^{+}+h}\right|\cdot\left|\left({\rm log}\left(1+\frac{V^{+}}{h}\right)\right)_{x}\right|dx

(2.31)

\displaystyle+\frac{h}{\tau}\int_{T^{c}}\left[\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}\left|\frac{f(u)-f(w)}{V^{+}+h}\right|^{2}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\left|\left({\rm log}\left(1+\frac{V^{+}}{h}\right)\right)_{x}\right|^{2}\right]dx,

where
\varepsilon>0
denotes the parameter, and

, we get

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle\leq\frac{h\mathcal{C}}{\tau}\int_{0}^{1}\left|\left({\rm log}\left(1+\frac{V^{+}}{h}\right)\right)_{x}\right|dx+\frac{h}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\left|\left({\rm log}\left(1+\frac{V^{+}}{h}\right)\right)_{x}\right|^{2}dx

(2.32)

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,+\frac{1}{2\tau^{2}}\int_{T^{c}}h\left|\frac{f(u)-f(w)}{V^{+}+h}\right|^{2}dx.

By recalling the Taylor expansion in (

where

\displaystyle h\int_{0}^{1}\left|\left({\rm log}\left(1+\frac{V^{+}}{h}\right)\right)_{x}\right|^{2}dx+2\int_{0}^{1}(f(u)-f(w))\cdot\frac{V^{+}}{V^{+}+h}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2h\mathcal{C}}{\tau}\int_{0}^{1}\left|\left({\rm log}\left(1+\frac{V^{+}}{h}\right)\right)_{x}\right|dx+\frac{1}{\tau^{2}}\int_{T^{c}}Mdx.

Next we analyze

V^{+}\not\equiv 0

(2.35)

\displaystyle\underset{h\to 0^{+}}{{\rm lim}}\int_{0}^{1}(f(u)-f(w))\cdot\frac{V^{+}}{V^{+}+h}dx>C>0.

There exists a constant
\mathcal{C}\gg 1
such that the measure
|T^{c}|\ll 1

(2.36)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\tau^{2}}\int_{T^{c}}Mdx\leq\int_{0}^{1}(f(u)-f(w))\cdot\frac{V^{+}}{V^{+}+h}dx.

Combining (
2.34

(2.37)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\left|\left({\rm log}\left(1+\frac{V^{+}}{h}\right)\right)_{x}\right|^{2}dx\leq\frac{2\mathcal{C}}{\tau}\int_{0}^{1}\left|\left({\rm log}\left(1+\frac{V^{+}}{h}\right)\right)_{x}\right|dx.

Let

(2.38)

\displaystyle\left\|\left({\rm log}\left(1+\frac{V^{+}}{h}\right)\right)_{x}\right\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}\leq C\left\|\left({\rm log}\left(1+\frac{V^{+}}{h}\right)\right)_{x}\right\|_{L^{2}(0,1)},\,\,\,\mbox{as}\,\,\,h\to 0^{+},

Using the Poincaré inequality, we have

(2.40)

.

3.
Optimal regularity

In this section, we prove that the regularities of
\rho
and
w
proved in Section 2 are optimal. Firstly, we prove that
\rho
has singularity at sonic point
x=1
. This casues the regularity of
\rho
is optimal, that is,
\rho\notin C^{\nu}[0,1]
for any
\nu>\frac{1}{2}
, and
\rho\notin W^{1,\kappa}(0,1)
for any
\kappa\geq 2
.

Lemma 3.1
.

For any relaxation time
\tau>0
, the first derivative of the solution
w
to equation (
1.11
) satisfies
w_{x}(1)<0
.

Proof.

Since
w\in C^{1,\frac{1}{2}}(0,1)
,
w\geq\frac{1}{2}
for
x\in[0,1]
and
w(0)=w(1)=\frac{1}{2}
, we know
w_{x}(1)\leq 0
. We prove this lemma by contradiction. Assume that
w_{x}(1)=0
. Due to
b>1
, we can suppose
b\geq 1+2c
, where
c>0
. Moreover, according to
\rho\in C^{\frac{1}{2}}(0,1)
and
\rho(1)=1
, there exists
0<\delta<1
such that
\rho\leq 1+c
for
x\in[1-\delta,1]
. This means
\rho-b\leq-c
for
x\in[1-\delta,1]
. By the Newton-Leibniz formula, we have

\displaystyle w_{x}(x)

\displaystyle=w_{x}(1)-\int_{x}^{1}w_{xx}(s)ds

\displaystyle=-\int_{x}^{1}w_{xx}(s)ds

\displaystyle=-\int_{x}^{1}\left(\rho-b-\left(\frac{1}{\tau\rho}\right)_{x}\right)(s)ds

\displaystyle>c(1-x)+\frac{1}{\tau}-\frac{1}{\tau\rho(x)}

(3.1)

\displaystyle>0

for any
x\in[1-\delta,1]
. Thus,
w
is strictly monotonically increasing over
[1-\delta,1]
, which contradicts to the fact that
w(x)\geq\frac{1}{2}
over
[0,1]
. Then, it holds that
w_{x}(1)<0
. Therefore, we complete the proof of Lemma
3.1
. ∎

Now, we are ready to prove the rest parts of Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of the second and third parts of Theorem
1.1
. The function
w_{x}
is continuous due to
w\in C^{1,\frac{1}{2}}(0,1)
. Because
w_{x}(1)<0
by Lemma
3.1
, there must exist three constants
\delta_{1}
,
C_{1}
,
C_{2}
with
0<\delta_{1}<1
and
C_{2}>C_{1}>0
, such that
-C_{2}\leq w_{x}(x)\leq-C_{1}
over
[1-\delta_{1},1]
. Thus

(3.2)

\displaystyle C_{1}(1-x)\leq w-\frac{1}{2}\leq C_{2}(1-x)

for any
x\in[1-\delta_{1},1]
. This indicates the function
w-\frac{1}{2}
is between the functions
y=C_{1}(1-x)
and
y=C_{2}(1-x)
for any
x\in[1-\delta_{1},1]
.

By (
2.19
) and (
3.2
), we obtain

(3.3)

\displaystyle k_{1}(\rho-1)^{2}\leq w(\rho)-\frac{1}{2}\leq k_{2}(\rho-1)^{2},

where
k_{1},k_{2}
denote two positive constants. This, together with (
3.2
)-(
3.3
), gives

(3.4)

\displaystyle h_{1}(1-x)^{\frac{1}{2}}\triangleq\sqrt{\frac{C_{2}}{k_{2}}}(1-x)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\rho-1\leq\sqrt{\frac{C_{1}}{k_{1}}}(1-x)^{\frac{1}{2}}\triangleq h_{2}(1-x)^{\frac{1}{2}}

for any
x\in[1-\delta_{1},1]
. This implies the function
\rho-1
lies between the functions
y=h_{1}(1-x)^{\frac{1}{2}}
and
y=h_{2}(1-x)^{\frac{1}{2}}
for any
x\in[1-\delta_{1},1]
.

Thus, for any
\nu>\frac{1}{2}
, we have

(3.5)

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\rho(1)-\rho(x)}{(1-x)^{\nu}}\right|\geq\frac{h_{1}(1-x)^{\frac{1}{2}}}{(1-x)^{\nu}}=\frac{h_{1}}{(1-x)^{\nu-\frac{1}{2}}}\to\infty,\,\,\,\mbox{as}\,\,\,x\to 1.

This shows
\rho\notin C^{\nu}[0,1]
for any
\nu>\frac{1}{2}
.

Moreover, according to
\rho_{x}=\frac{\rho^{3}}{\rho+1}\cdot\frac{w_{x}}{\rho-1}
and
-C_{2}\leq w_{x}(x)\leq-C_{1}
over
[1-\delta_{1},1]
, we get

(3.6)

\displaystyle\left|\rho_{x}\right|=\left|\frac{\rho^{3}}{\rho+1}\right|\cdot\left|\frac{w_{x}}{\rho-1}\right|\geq\frac{1}{\bar{b}+1}\cdot\frac{C_{1}}{\rho-1}.

Substituing (
3.4
) into (
3.6
), we derive

(3.7)

\displaystyle\left|\rho_{x}\right|\geq\frac{C_{1}}{h_{2}(\bar{b}+1)}\cdot\frac{1}{(1-x)^{\frac{1}{2}}}\notin L^{2}[1-\delta_{1},1],

and thus
\rho\notin W^{1,\kappa}(0,1)
for any
\kappa\geq 2
.
\Box

The above results indicate that for any relaxation time
\tau>0
, the sonic-subsonic solution
\rho
has strong singularity at sonic point
x=1
. This leads to that the regularities of
\rho
and
w
proved in Section 2 are optimal.

Next, we focus on the singularity of
\rho
at sonic point
x=0
. In 2017, Li-Mei-Zhang-Zhang showed that there is no singularity when the relaxation time
\tau\ll 1
, see
[
21
, Theorem 2.4]
. However, we can prove that there is still strong singularity to
\rho
at sonic point
x=0
when the relaxation time
\tau\gg 1
. To this end, we establish the following lemma.

Lemma 3.2
.

For the relaxation time
\tau\gg 1
, the first derivative of the solution
w
to equation (
1.11
) satisfies
w_{x}(0)>0
.

Proof.

Since
w\in C^{1,\frac{1}{2}}(0,1)
,
w\geq\frac{1}{2}
for
x\in[0,1]
and
w(0)=w(1)=\frac{1}{2}
, we know
w_{x}(0)\geq 0
. We prove this lemma also by contradiction. Suppose
w_{x}(0)=0
. Since
b>1
, we can assume
b\geq 1+2c
, where
c>0
. Furthermore, because
\rho\in C^{\frac{1}{2}}(0,1)
and
\rho(0)=1
, there is
0<\eta<1
such that
\rho\leq 1+c
for
x\in[0,\xi]
. This implies
\rho-b\leq-c
for
x\in[0,\xi]
. Using the Newton-Leibniz formula, for any
x\in[0,\xi]
, we derive

\displaystyle w_{x}(x)

\displaystyle=w_{x}(0)+\int_{0}^{x}w_{xx}(s)ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{x}w_{xx}(s)ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{x}\left(\rho-b-\left(\frac{1}{\tau\rho}\right)_{x}\right)(s)ds

\displaystyle\leq-cx-\frac{1}{\tau\rho(x)}+\frac{1}{\tau}

(3.8)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\tau}.

Applying the Newton-Leibniz formula again for any
x\in[0,\xi]
, we have

(3.9)

\displaystyle w(x)\leq w(0)+\int_{0}^{x}\frac{1}{\tau}ds\leq\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\eta}{\tau}\leq\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{\tau}.

In view of (
3.9
), taking
\tau_{0}>0
such that
\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{\tau_{0}}=F\left(1+\frac{c}{2}\right)
. When
\tau\geq\tau_{0}
, we have for any
x\in[0,\xi]

(3.10)

\displaystyle F(\rho)=w(x)\leq\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{\tau}\leq\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{\tau_{0}}=F\left(1+\frac{c}{2}\right).

So we have
\rho\leq 1+\frac{c}{2}
for any
x\in[0,\xi]
.

By continuous extension method, we can ultimately obtain that
w\leq\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{\tau}
and
\rho<1+c
for all
x\in[0,1]
. Obviously,
w\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)\leq\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{\tau}
. For any
y\in[\frac{1}{2},1]
, by the Newton-Leibniz formula, we have

\displaystyle w_{x}(y)

\displaystyle=w_{x}(0)+\int_{0}^{y}w_{xx}(s)ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{y}\left(\rho-b-\left(\frac{1}{\tau\rho}\right)_{x}\right)(s)ds

(3.11)

\displaystyle<-\frac{c}{2}+\frac{1}{\tau}.

Taking
\tau\geq{\rm max}\left\{\tau_{0},\frac{4}{c}\right\}
, we derive
w_{x}(y)<-\frac{c}{4}
, and thus

\displaystyle w(1)

\displaystyle=w(\frac{1}{2})+\int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{1}w_{x}(s)ds

\displaystyle<\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{\tau}+\int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{1}-\frac{c}{4}ds

(3.12)

\displaystyle<\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{\tau}-\frac{c}{8}.

Let us take
\tau\geq{\rm max}\left\{\tau_{0},\frac{8}{c}\right\}
again, which together with (
3.12
) implies
w(1)<\frac{1}{2}
. This is contradictory to the fact that
w\geq\frac{1}{2}
over
[0,1]
. Then we have
w_{x}(0)>0
. The proof of Lemma
3.2
is completed.
∎

Next, we repeat the process of proof at sonic point

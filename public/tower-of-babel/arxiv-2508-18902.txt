Adaptive 6G Networks-in-Network Management for Industrial Applications
Daniel Lindenschmitt, Paul Seehofer, Marius Schmitz, Jan Mertes, Roland Bless, Martina Zitterbart, Jan C. Aurich, Hans D. Schotten

Abstract
This paper presents the application of dsm ( dsm ) for future 6G industrial networks, establishing an efficient controller for the nin ( nin ) concept. The proposed architecture integrates nomadic as well as static sn with diverse qos ( qos ) requirements within the coverage area of an overlayer network, managed by a centralized sm ( sm ). Control plane connectivity between the sn and the dsm is ensured by the self-organizing KIRA routing protocol. The demonstrated system enables scalable, zero-touch connectivity and supports nomadic sn through seamless discovery and reconfiguration. sn are implemented for modular iiot ( iiot ) scenarios, as well as for mission-critical control loops and for logistics or nomadic behavior. The dsm framework dynamically adapts spectrum allocation to meet real-time demands while ensuring reliable operation. The demonstration highlights the potential of dsm and nin to support flexible, dense, and heterogeneous wireless deployments in reconfigurable manufacturing environments.

6
Conclusion and Future Work

In this paper, the
dsm
approach based on previous work
[
1
]
was further developed. By integrating an adaptive behavior of the
dsm
for nomadic
sn
, a solution for ad-hoc reconfiguration was developed for both static and nomadic
sn
within the coverage area of an overlayer network. By integrating nomadic and static
sn
into a shared spectrum domain of an overlayer network, the system addresses key challenges of dense and heterogeneous wireless deployments, opening up new potential applications for industrial environments using
nin
and the future 6G standard. The combination of a centralized
sm
with the self-organizing KIRA routing architecture enabled robust, zero-touch coordination across multiple radio access technologies. The demonstrator scenario highlighted the viability of the approach in a realistic industrial setting, with
sn
supporting mission-critical control with a latency between 2-3 ms and time-deterministic behavior, high-bandwidth
iiot
sensors, and mobile logistics. Dynamic spectrum allocation allows the system to adapt to shifting demands, such as the arrival of a nomadic
agv
, without compromising service quality. The next step will be to explore how the
dsm
can be further extended topredict the spectrum needs of connected
sn

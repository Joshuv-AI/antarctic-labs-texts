1.
Introduction

We study the almost sure convergence rate for the last iterate of Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD) and Stochastic Heavy Ball (SHB) schemes associated to solving an unconstrained optimization problem involving a cost function defined on a subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. We consider the application of these algorithms in the following context as in
[
2
]
:

(1.1)

w_{t+1}-w_{t}=-\alpha_{t}\,\nabla\ell(Z_{t},w_{t})+\beta(w_{t}-w_{t-1})~,~\beta\in[0,1)

where
w_{t}
is in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
Z_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
is an i.i.d process with finite variance sampled from a continuous distribution with density
\rho
independent of
\mathscr{F}_{t}=\sigma(w_{s}:s\leq t)
,
\alpha_{t}
is the (non-random) step size, and
\ell
is an estimator of a deterministic cost function
F:\pazocal{W}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
defined by
F(w)=\mathbb{E}_{\rho}[\ell(Z,w)]
. When
\beta=0
, (
1.1
) is the SGD and can be rewritten as

(1.2)

w_{t+1}-w_{t}=-\alpha_{t}\nabla F(w_{t})+\alpha_{t}\,\delta m_{t}~~~,~~~\delta m_{t}=\nabla F(w_{t})-\nabla\ell(Z_{t},w_{t}).

Algorithms of the form (
1.2
) have been studied extensively as part of a field called stochastic approximation (
[
10
]
,
[
13
]
). However, the almost sure convergence rate for the last iterate of SGD is studied only recently. The most recent work in the parametric setting are done by Weissmann et al in
[
15
]
. Weissmann et al. considers SGD and SHB where properties of the objective function
F
is only known locally, i.e. properties apply in some neighborhood of a point
[
15
, assumption 2.1.(ii)]
. Under the local gradient domination
[
15
, definition 2.2]

\|\nabla F(w_{t})\|^{2}\geq F(w_{t})-F_{*}
, Weissmann et al proved the existence of neighborhoods
\pazocal{U}_{1},\pazocal{U}
of critical points in the parameter space
\pazocal{W}
for which if
w_{1}\in\pazocal{U}_{1}
, then the event
\Omega{U}=\{\pazocal{U}\,\text{is absorbing for}\,w_{t}\}
is highly probable and
(F(w_{t})-F_{*})\mathbbm{1}_{\Omega{U}}=o(t^{-1+\epsilon})

[
15
, table 1, theorem 5.1]
. The set
\pazocal{U}
is defined as a neighborhood of the set of minimizers with a fixed radius. We remark that Weissmann et al also examines the case when global properties of
F
are known
[
15
, table 1]
.

When global properties of the objective function are known, there are other recent works as well. Liu and Yuan proved almost sure convergence rates for SGD, SHB, and SNAG (Stochastic Nesterov’s Accelerated Gradient) by assuming that the objective function is either globally non-convex, convex, or strongly convex
[
9
]
. Liu and Yuan produced the rate
F(w_{t})-F_{*}=o(t^{-1+\epsilon})
for strongly convex
F
and
\min_{1\leq i<t-1}\|\nabla F(w_{i})\|^{2}=o(\sum_{i=1}^{t-1}\alpha_{i})^{-1}
for non-convex
F

[
9
, theorem 6, theorem 8]
. The strongly convex rate is optimal according to Agarwal et al
[
1
]
. For a convex objective function
F
, Liu and Yuan proved a rate of
F(w_{t})-F_{*}=o(t^{-\frac{1}{3}+\epsilon})
, which is not as close to the optimal rate of
t^{-1/2}
in expectation
[
9
, remark 14]
. Liu and Yuan used the Robbins-Siegmund theorem (
[
13
, theorem 1]
,
[
9
, proposition 2]
) to prove their convergence rate results. We remark that both SGD and SHB produce the same rate according to Liu and Yuan, i.e. the momentum parameter
\beta
does not affect the convergence rate
[
9
, theorem 8, theorem 13]
.

Intuitively, the momentum parameter
\beta
in (
1.1
) serves to counter slowdown when the gradient is near zero. As such, it makes sense to adjust
\beta
depending on the gradient and step sizes. Sebbouh et al studied such a case in
[
14
]
. In particular, they proved that the last iterate of SHB-IMA (Iterate Moving Average) can achieve
o(t^{-1+\epsilon})
rate in the convex setting when
\beta
is allowed to vary as a function of
t
in the ‘overparametrized’ case
[
14
, table 1, corollary 14, definition 3]
. On the other hand, in the usual parametrized case, given step size of order
O(t^{-\frac{1}{2}-\epsilon})
, Sebbouh et al achieved
o(t^{-\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon})
rate
[
14
, corollary 17]
. Similar to Liu and Yuan’s work
[
9
]
, Sebbouh et al used the Robbins-Siegmund theorem
[
14
, lemma 6]
to prove these rates.

In another work
[
8
]
, Lei, Shi, and Guo obtained the last iterate almost sure convergence rate of
o(t^{\max(p-1,1-p(\gamma+1))+\epsilon})
given step size
O(t^{-p})
with
p\in(\frac{1}{1+\gamma},1)
for a convex objective function, whose gradient is
\gamma
-Hölder with SGD in the non-parametric setting
[
8
, theorem 6]
. Moreover, in
[
8
, corollary 11]
, Lei, Shi, and Guo proved that SGD converges at rate
F(w_{t+1})-F_{*}=O(t^{\max(p-1,1-p(\gamma+1))}\,\log^{2}(t/\delta))
with probability at least
1-\delta
if
p\in(\frac{1}{1+\gamma},1)
. In particular, when
p=2/3
and
\gamma=1
, their work matches Liu and Yuan’s with
O(t^{-\frac{1}{3}+\epsilon})

[
8
, theorem 6, corollary 11]
. Interestingly, Lei, Shi, and Guo did not use Robbins-Siegmund theorem. However, their approach is similar to that of Robbins and Siegmund, where a supermartingale is constructed and the convergence of the iterates is implied by that of the supermartingale
[
8
, section 4.2]
.

Another recent work regarding almost sure convergence rate for SGD without employing the Robbins-Siegmund theorem, is done by Nguyen et al
[
11
]
. The authors used a weaker, super-martingale convergence theorem
[
11
, lemma 5]
to prove
w_{t}\rightarrow w_{*}
a.s.-
\mathbb{P}
and
\mathbb{E}\|w_{t}-w_{*}\|^{2}=o(t^{-1})
in the strong convex case
[
11
, thorem 1,2]
. Such a rate is indeed optimal according to
[
1
]
since if
F
is convex with
\nabla F
Lipschitz, by lemma
B.4
, we get
F(w_{t})-F_{*}\lesssim\|w_{t}-w_{*}\|^{2}=o(t^{-1})
.

1.1.
Contribution

As remarked in Liu and Yuan’s paper
[
9
, remark 14]
, the rate
O(t^{-\frac{1}{3}+\epsilon})
for the convex case is not close to the optimal rate in expectation
O(t^{-\frac{1}{2}})
proved by Agarwal et al in
[
1
]
. Motivated by this remark, we study the almost sure convergence for the last iterate of SGD and SHB where the objective function
F
is globally convex and non-convex with
\nabla F
being
(\gamma,L)
-Hölder. With these conditions, our contribution is as follows:

1.

Instead of relying on Robbins-Siegmund, nor any stochastic approximation theory, we use only Gronwall’s inequality without using any martingale convergence theorem to prove that
\min_{0\leq s\leq t}\|\nabla F(w_{t})\|^{2}=o(t^{p-1})
and
F(w_{t})-F_{*}=o(t^{(2\gamma/(1+\gamma))\max(p-1,1-(1+\gamma)p)-\epsilon})
, as well as
\min_{s\leq t}F(w_{s})-F_{*}=o(t^{p-1})
for non-convex and convex objectives respectively given step size of
\alpha_{t}=O(t^{-p})
with
p\in(\frac{1}{1+\gamma},1)
using similar assumptions as in
[
9
]
, except that
\nabla F
is assumed to be
\gamma
-Hölder instead of Lipschitz. By using non-martingale convergence theory, our argument is less technical. We also present insights from our study in section
2.2
and
2.3
.

2.

We proved that with probability at least
1-\delta
, SHB (
1.1
) with a fixed momentum parameter
\beta\in(0,1)
converges at rate
F(w_{t})-F_{*}=O(t^{\max(p-1,-2p+1)}\log^{2}\frac{t}{\delta})
when the gradient of the objective function
\nabla F
is Lipschitz and
F
is convex. Our result is similar to
[
8
]
and is consistent with our own findings (see table
1
). While our result is restricted to the case when
\beta
is fixed, such a result provides baseline SHB performance which can be compared to other algorithms, e.g. SHB-IMA (Iterated Moving Average). There may be arguments to use baseline (simpler) SHB variant to save computation time. To the author’s knowledge, this is the first high probability convergence rate result for the SHB.

3.

We use a slightly weaker assumption for
\nabla F
compared to Liu and Yuan
[
9
, assumption 4]
that it is only
\gamma
-Hölder with
\gamma\in(0,1]
, following Lei, Shi, and Guo
[
8
, assumption 1]
. In this manner, we provide a unified analysis for ‘smooth’ objective functions.

\displaystyle\int\mathbbm{1}_{\{w_{t}\in A\}}

2.2.
Main Result 1: Almost Sure Convergence Rate

With assumption
2.1
and
2.2
, in estimating

[
13
]
and a lemma which deduce the rate.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
X_{t},Y_{t},Z_{t}
be non-negative for all
t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
a_{t}>0
be such that

1.

a_{t},Z_{t}\in\ell^{1}(\mathbb{N})
.

2.

Y_{t}\leq(1+a_{t-1})Y_{t-1}-X_{t-1}+Z_{t-1}
.

Then
Y_{t}\in\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{N})
and
X_{t}\in\ell^{1}(\mathbb{N})
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
Y_{t},Z_{t}
be a sequence of non-negative random variables adapted to a filtration
\mathscr{F}_{t}
and

2.3.
Main Result 2: Convergence Rate with High Probability

A key requirement to obtain fast asymptotic convergence is that
F(w_{t})-F_{*}
is decreasing eventually. In other words, there exists
T>0
such that
F(w_{t})
is decreasing for all
t>T
. Such is the case in deterministic GD
[
4
, lemma 2.28, eq. (14), algorithm 3.2]
. In fact, smoothness of
F
is sufficient (without convexity). Intuitively, in the stochastic GD, this would hold when
F
is convex if the step size decay rate is controlled accordingly. However, even after taking expectation, this is difficult to achieve. Indeed, applying the expectation to (
3.2
) with
[
4
, lemma 2.29]
and proposition
3.1
yields an inequality of the form

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[F(w_{t+1})]-\mathbb{E}[F(w_{t})]

\displaystyle\leq-\alpha_{t}\bigg(\mathbb{E}\|\nabla F(w_{t})\|^{2}-\alpha_{t}^{\gamma}\left(A\,\mathbb{E}[F(w_{t})-F_{*}]+B\,\|\nabla F(w_{t})\|^{2}+C\right)\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq-\alpha_{t}\bigg(\min_{0\leq s\leq t}\mathbb{E}\|\nabla F(w_{s})\|^{2}-\alpha_{t}^{\gamma}\left(C+(A+2BL)\,\sup_{t\geq 0}\mathbb{E}[F(w_{t})-F_{*}]\right)\bigg).

From
[
8
, theorem 6]
, convex
F
gives
\mathbb{E}\|\nabla F(w_{t})\|^{2}\leq\mathbb{E}[F(w_{t})-F_{*}]=O(t^{p-1})
for
p>2/(2+\gamma)
. Since
p>1/(1+\gamma)
, we have
t^{p-1}>t^{p\gamma}
which suggests that
on average
,
F(w_{t})
decreases eventually.

We address this issue in our second result on SHB. Instead of looking at one-step iterates, we look at
F(w_{t})
over a time period
t\in[\tau,T+1]
in (
4.35
) by expressing

\displaystyle F(w_{T+1})

\displaystyle\leq F(w_{\tau})+\sum_{t=\tau}^{T}\left(\beta\,\langle\nabla F(w_{t}),\,w_{t}-w_{t-1}\rangle+\frac{L}{1+\gamma}\,\|w_{t+1}-w_{t}\|^{1+\gamma}\right)

\displaystyle-\sum_{t=\tau}^{T}\left(\alpha_{t}\,\|\nabla F(w_{t})\|^{2}-\alpha_{t}\,\langle\nabla F(w_{t}),\,\delta m_{t}\rangle\right).

The first summation on the right hand side decays quickly. The key is to control the last summation. Following the same idea as Lei, Shi, Guo in
[
8
]
, we use concentration inequality of Bernstein and Azuma-Hoeffding to estimate the last term on the right hand side involving the martingale difference
\alpha_{t}\,\langle\nabla F(w_{t}),\,\delta m_{t}\rangle
. Effectively, the martingale difference term can be made smaller than
\alpha_{t}\,\|\nabla F(w_{t})\|^{2}
so that the last term is
effectively negative
(more precisely, it is replaced with a quickly decaying term. See lemma
4.10
). This suggests that with high probability, the ‘decreasing’ effect on
F(w_{t})
(the effectiveness of SGD/SHB) is shown
over a larger time step
, which is a
slowdown
compared to determinsitic GD
[
4
, theorem 3.4, lemma 2.28]
.

From the previous paragraph, applying martingale concentration inequalities on
\langle\nabla F(w_{t}),\,\delta m_{t}\rangle
requires
controlling the noise

\delta m_{t}
. By definition,
\delta m_{t}
is comprised of
\nabla F(w_{t})
and
\nabla\ell(Z_{t},w_{t})
, which by lemma
B.4
, is controlled by
\|w_{t}-w_{*}\|
. Following
[
8
]
, in proposition
4.5
, we show that up to time
T>0
,
\|w_{t}-w_{*}\|
is at most
\log(T/\delta)
with probability
1-\delta
. Since
\alpha_{t}
decays with order
O(t^{-p})
, the martingale difference
\alpha_{t}\,\langle\nabla F(w_{t}),\,\delta m_{t}\rangle
is of the order
\alpha_{t}
up to a logarithmic factor.

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2\gamma}{1+\gamma}\,\ell(z,w)+\frac{1-\gamma}{1+\gamma}\left(\frac{1+\gamma}{\gamma}\cdot L^{1/\gamma}\right)^{\frac{2\gamma}{1-\gamma}}+\frac{2\gamma}{1+\gamma}\cdot\sup_{z\in\pazocal{Z}}\ell(z,w_{*}).

Proof of (3.b).
Observe that if assumption
2.3
and a minimizer
w_{*}\in\pazocal{W}
exists and
\ell(z,\cdot)
is convex in the second slot, then applying (

) yields

\displaystyle\|\nabla F(w)-\nabla\ell(z,w)\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\,\|\nabla F(w)\|^{2}+2\,\|\nabla\ell(z,w)\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2L^{2}\,\|w_{t}-w_{*}\|^{2\gamma}+4\,\|\nabla\ell(z,w_{*})\|^{2}+4\|\nabla\ell(z,w)-\nabla\ell(z,w_{*})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 6L^{2}\|w_{t}-w_{*}\|^{2\gamma}+4\,a_{1}\,\sup_{z\in\pazocal{Z}}\ell(z,w_{*})+4\,a_{2}.

Proof of (3.c).
By assumption
2.3
and claim (
1
), we get

\displaystyle\|\nabla\ell(z,w)\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\|\nabla\ell(z,w_{*})\|^{2}+2\|\nabla\ell(z,w)-\nabla\ell(z,w_{*})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(2a_{1}\sup_{z\in\pazocal{Z}}\ell(z,w_{*})+2a_{2}\right)+2L^{2}\|w-w_{*}\|^{2\gamma}

Proof of (3.d).
Another way of estimating
\|\nabla F(w)-\nabla\ell(z,w)\|^{2}
is by using (
1
)

\displaystyle\|\nabla F(w)-\nabla\ell(z,w)\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\|\nabla F(w_{t})\|^{2}+2\|\nabla\ell(Z_{t},w_{t})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2a_{1}\,(F(w)-F_{*})+2a_{1}F_{*}+2a_{0}+2a_{1}\,\ell(z,w)+2a_{2}.

Applying

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\|\delta m_{t}\|^{2}\,|\,\mathscr{F}_{t}]

\displaystyle\leq 2a_{1}\,(F(w_{t})-F_{*})+2a_{1}\,F(w_{t})+2(a_{0}+a_{2})

\displaystyle=4a_{1}\,(F(w_{t})-F_{*})+2(a_{0}+a_{2}+a_{1}\,F_{*}).

Proposition B.7
.

Let
\delta m_{t}
be as in (

) corresponding to the SHB iterates

).

\mathbb{E}[\delta m_{t}\,|\,\mathscr{F}_{s}]=\mathbf{0}

.

\mathbb{E}[\langle\nabla F(w_{t}),\,\delta m_{t}\rangle\,|\,\mathscr{F}_{t}]=0

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\delta m_{t}\,|\,\mathscr{F}_{t-1}]

Appendix C
Gronwall-type Inequalities

Proposition C.1
.

Let
\{X_{n}\in\mathbb{R}:n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\}
and
\{Y_{n}\in\mathbb{R}:n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\}
be sequences,
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, and
\beta>0
. The sequence

Y_{n+n_{0}}=\beta^{2n}y+\mathbbm{1}_{\mathbb{N}}(n)\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\beta^{2k}X_{n_{0}+n-1-k}~~,~~n\geq 0

is the unique solution to the difference equation

(C.1)

\begin{cases}Y_{n_{0}+n}=\beta^{2}\,Y_{n_{0}+n-1}+X_{n_{0}+n-1}~,~n>0.\\
Y_{n_{0}}=y.\end{cases}

If
Z_{n}
satisfies
Z_{n_{0}+n}\leq\beta^{2}\,Z_{n_{0}+n-1}+X_{n_{0}+n-1}
(resp.
\geq
) and
Z_{n_{0}}\leq y
(resp.
\geq
), then
Z_{n_{0}+n}\leq Y_{n_{0}+n}
(resp.
\geq
).

Proof.

We have
Y_{n_{0}}=\beta^{0}y=y
and
Y_{n_{0}+1}=\beta^{2}y+X_{n_{0}}=\beta^{2}Y_{n_{0}}+X_{n_{0}}
. Moreover, for
n>0
,

\displaystyle X_{n_{0}+n}+\beta^{2}Y_{n_{0}+n}

\displaystyle=X_{n_{0}+n}+\beta^{2(n+1)}y+\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\beta^{2k+2}X_{n_{0}+n-1-k}

\displaystyle=X_{n_{0}+n}+\beta^{2(n+1)}y+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\beta^{2k}X_{n_{0}+n-k}

\displaystyle=Y_{n_{0}+n+1}.

For uniqueness, if
Z_{n_{0}+n}
satisfies (
C.1
), then
Z_{n_{0}+n}-Y_{n_{0}+n}=\beta^{2}(Z_{n_{0}+n-1}-Y_{n_{0}+n-1})
for all
n>0
with
Z_{n_{0}}-Y_{n_{0}}=0
. This implies
Z_{n_{0}+n}-Y_{n_{0}+n}=(Z_{n_{0}}-Y_{n_{0}})\,\beta^{2n}=0
for all
n\geq 0
.

For the second part, we show by induction that
Z_{n_{0}+n}\leq Y_{n_{0}+n}
. Note that
Z_{n_{0}}\leq y=Y_{n_{0}}
and
Z_{n_{0}+1}\leq\beta^{2}y+X_{n_{0}}=Y_{n_{0}+1}
. For the inductive step, assume
Z_{n_{0}+n}\leq Y_{n_{0}+n}
for
n\leq N
. From the first claim,
Y_{n_{0}+n+1}=\beta^{2}Y_{n_{0}+n}+X_{n_{0}+n}
. Therefore, we have

Z_{n_{0}+N+1}\leq\beta^{2}\,Z_{n_{0}+N}+X_{n_{0}+N}\leq\beta^{2}\,Y_{n_{0}+N}+X_{n_{0}+N}=Y_{n_{0}+N+1}.

On the other hand, if
Z_{n_{0}+n}\geq\beta^{2}Z_{n_{0}+n-1}+X_{n_{0}+n}
, then we may consider

\begin{cases}-Z_{n_{0}+n}\leq\beta^{2}(-Z_{n_{0}+n-1})+(-X_{n_{0}+n-1})\\
-Z_{n_{0}}\leq-y.\end{cases}

We can apply the previous claim and get
-Z_{n_{0}+n}\leq-Y_{n_{0}+n}
.
∎

Proposition C.2
.

Let
A\geq 0
,
X_{n}\geq 0
,
c_{n}\geq 0
.

X_{n}\leq A+\sum_{k=1}^{n}c_{k-1}\,X_{k-1}~~~,~~~X_{0}\leq A~~~\implies X_{n}\leq A\prod_{k=1}^{n}\left(1+c_{k-1}\right).

Proof.

Define the sequence

Y_{n}=A+\sum_{k=1}^{n}c_{k-1}\,X_{k-1}~~~,~~~Y_{0}=A

Observe that
Y_{n}-Y_{n-1}=c_{n-1}X_{n-1}\leq c_{n-1}Y_{n-1}
for all
n\geq 1
, which implies

\frac{Y_{n}}{Y_{n-1}}\leq 1+c_{n-1}~~,~~\forall n\geq 1.

The above is a telescoping product. Along with the assumption
X_{n}\leq Y_{n}
, the above argument yields the desired result.
∎

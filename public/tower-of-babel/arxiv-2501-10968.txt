Exploring the two-body strong decay properties of the possible Λ c ​ K ∗ \Lambda_{c}K^{*} and Σ c ​ K ( ∗ ) \Sigma_{c}K^{(*)} molecules
Jin-Yu Huo 1, Rui Chen 1

Abstract
In this work, we apply the effective Lagrangian approach to investigate the two-body strong decay behaviors of the possible Λ c ​ K ∗ \Lambda_{c}K^{*} and Σ c ​ K ( ∗ ) \Sigma_{c}K^{(*)} molecules, as predicted in our previous study [ Phys. Rev. D 108 , 054011 (2023) ]. Our results indicate that the decay width for the coupled Σ c ​ K / Λ c ​ K ∗ / Σ c ​ K ∗ \Sigma_{c}K/\Lambda_{c}K^{*}/\Sigma_{c}K^{*} molecule with I ⁡ ( J P ) = 1 / 2 ​ ( 1 / 2 − ) I(J^{P})=1/2(1/2^{-}) is on the order of several MeV, with the D s ​ N D_{s}N channel being dominant. For the coupled Λ c ​ K ∗ / Σ c ​ K ∗ \Lambda_{c}K^{*}/\Sigma_{c}K^{*} molecule with 1 / 2 ​ ( 1 / 2 − , 3 / 2 − ) 1/2(1/2^{-},3/2^{-}) , the decay widths are on the order of tens of MeV, with the dominant channels being Σ c ​ K \Sigma_{c}K and Σ c ∗ ​ K \Sigma_{c}^{*}K , respectively. For the Σ c ​ K ∗ \Sigma_{c}K^{*} molecules with 1 / 2 ​ ( 1 / 2 − ) 1/2(1/2^{-}) , the decay width can reach one hundred MeV, with Σ c ​ K \Sigma_{c}K and Λ c ​ K \Lambda_{c}K being the dominant decay channels. The decay widths for the Σ c ​ K ∗ \Sigma_{c}K^{*} molecules with 1 / 2 ​ ( 3 / 2 − ) 1/2(3/2^{-}) and 3 / 2 ​ ( 1 / 2 − ) 3/2(1/2^{-}) are on the order of tens of MeV, with the dominant decay modes being Σ c ∗ ​ K \Sigma_{c}^{*}K and Σ c ​ K \Sigma_{c}K , respectively. The branching ratios for all the discussed channels show little dependence on the binding energies.

Among these explanations, the hadronic molecular scheme has attracted significant attention. As a special class of exotic states, a molecular state is composed of two or more conventional mesons and/or baryons, with its constituent hadrons bound together by strong interactions, typically resulting in a shallow binding energy. Hadronic molecules exhibit several characteristic features: their mass is usually close to the combined masses of their constituents, and they have specific quantum numbers (such as spin, parity, and isospin) that arise from the combination of their constituent hadrons. The study of hadronic molecules not only enhances our understanding of the strong interactions between conventional hadrons, but also help us explore the nature of composite particles beyond the simple quark model of hadrons.

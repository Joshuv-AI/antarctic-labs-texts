Valuative compactifications of analytic varieties
Jérôme Poineau

Abstract
. Let X X be an algebraic variety over 𝐂 \mathbf{C} . We define a canonical compactification X ⌝ X^{\!\urcorner} of the complex analytic space X ⁡ ( 𝐂 ) X(\mathbf{C}) by adding a Berkovich space over a trivially valued field at the boundary. The construction is functorial with respect to proper morphisms and preserves many properties, such as normality, regularity, etc. We prove a partial GAGA theorem in this setting : there is an equivalence between the categories of coherent sheaves on X X and X ⌝ X^{\!\urcorner} , and it induces bijections on global sections. The results still hold if 𝐂 \mathbf{C} is replaced by a complete non-trivially valued field k k , and complex analytic spaces by Berkovich analytic spaces over k k .



Let
X
be an algebraic variety over the field of complex numbers
\mathbf{C}
, and consider its analytification
X^{\textup{an}}
, as a locally ringed space (in other words,
X(\mathbf{C})
endowed with the transcendental topology and the sheaf of analytic functions).
The goal this article is to define a canonical compactification
X^{\!\urcorner}
of
X^{\textup{an}}
, namely a compact locally ringed space
X^{\!\urcorner}
in which
X^{\textup{an}}
embeds as a dense open, and study its properties.

Within the realm of complex analytic spaces, this task is hopeless. Indeed, a space as simple as the affine plane
\mathbf{C}^{2}
may already be embedded into different natural compact spaces, such as
\mathbf{P}^{2}(\mathbf{C})
or
\mathbf{P}^{1}(\mathbf{C})\times\mathbf{P}^{1}(\mathbf{C})
, which may or may not be relevant, depending on the issue at hand.

Former research however suggests that canonical compactifications may exist, in some larger category of spaces. During the eighties, J. Morgan and P. Shalen carried out their seminal work on fundamental groups of 3-manifolds
[
MS84
]
by studying compactifications of character varieties. The latter are defined by adding “ideal points”, defined in terms of valuations, at the boundaries of the varieties.

More recently, a similar strategy has been used by various authors to investigate degenerations of complex analytic varieties, based on the idea that the limit behavior of a one-parameter family (say over a punctured disc with parameter
t
) may be encoded into a non-Archimedean Berkovich analytic space (defined over the non-Archimedean field of Laurent series
\mathbf{C}(\!(t)\!)
). This seemingly simple principle has been intensively used over the last years, and striking applications have been found to topics as diverse as Hodge theory (limit mixed Hodge structures
[
Ber09
]
), complex manifolds (degenerations of volume forms
[
BJ17
]
), dynamical systems (degeneration of measures
[
Fav20
]
, blow-ups of Lyapunov exponents
[
DF19
]
), number theory (uniform Manin-Mumford bounds
[
DKY20
]
), etc.

Note that degenerations may be interpreted as compactifications in a relative setting (over a punctured disc for instance), so that the former may be obtained from the latter, under suitable functoriality properties.

In this text, we propose to lay the foundation of a thorough theory of compactifications (and degenerations) of complex analytic varieties encompassing all preceding instances, by relying on the theory of Berkovich spaces over Banach rings. Along the way, this flexible setting will allow us to enrich the compactifications with new additional features, such as a locally ringed space structure.

Our main result may be summarized as follows.

Theorem A

(
infra
Theorem
4.19
, Propositions
4.26
and
4.27
, Corollaries
4.31
and
4.32
)
.

To each complex algebraic variety
X
, we may associate a locally ringed space
X^{\!\urcorner}
that satisfies the following properties.

i)

The space
X^{\!\urcorner}
is compact.

ii)

There exists a natural morphism of locally ringed spaces
X^{\textup{an}}\to X^{\!\urcorner}
. It is an open immersion with dense image.

The association
X\mapsto X^{\!\urcorner}
is functorial with respect to proper morphisms, and preserves many properties: normality, regularity, etc.

We investigate coherent sheaves on compactifications and obtain a partial GAGA theorem.

Theorem B

(
infra
Theorems
5.17
and
5.20
)
.

Let
X
be a complex algebraic variety. To each coherent sheaf
F
on
X
, we may functorially associate a coherent sheaf
F^{\!\urcorner}
on
X^{\!\urcorner}
.

i)

The functor

F\in\mathrm{Coh}(X)\longmapsto F^{\!\urcorner}\in\mathrm{Coh}(X^{\!\urcorner})

is an equivalence of categories.

ii)

For each coherent sheaf
F
on
X
, we have a natural isomorphism

H^{0}(X,F)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}H^{0}(X^{\!\urcorner},F^{\!\urcorner}).

Both results still hold if
X
is an algebraic variety over a complete non-Archimedean valued field
k
with non-trivial valuation. In this case,
X^{\textup{an}}
is to be understood as the Berkovich analytification of
X
over
k
.

Let us now say a few words about the definition of the compactification. The missing part
X^{\!\urcorner}-X^{\textup{an}}
, in other words the boundary of
X^{\textup{an}}
, already appears in the work of V. Berkovich
[
Ber04
]
, O. Ben–Bassat and M. Temkin
[
BBT13
]
, and A. Thuillier
[
Thu07
]
. Its construction takes place in the setting of Berkovich spaces over the trivially valued field
\mathbf{C}

[
Ber90
,
Ber93
]
. To combine it with the complex analytic space
X^{\textup{an}}
, we work in the setting of hybrid Berkovich spaces, a particular instance of Berkovich spaces over Banach rings, as developed in
[
Ber90
,
LP24
]
. A quotient operation, inspired by L. Fantini’s definition of normalized spaces
[
Fan18
]
, completes the construction.

During the last stage of the writing of this article, we discovered that Yinchong Song had introduced the same compactification procedure in
[

]
(from the topological point of view only) to study adelic divisors over quasi-projective varieties in the sense of X. Yuan and S.-W. Zhang
[
YZ24
]
. We thank Mattias Jonsson for pointing out this reference.

\blacktriangleright

The boundary

The construction of the boundary of
X^{\textup{an}}
takes place in the category of Berkovich analytic spaces over
(\mathbf{C},\lvert{\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu}\rvert_{0})
, where
\lvert{\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu}\rvert_{0}
denotes the trivial absolute value. Recall that we have an analytification functor
X\mapsto X^{\textup{an}}_{0}
from algebraic varieties over
\mathbf{C}
to analytic spaces over
(\mathbf{C},\lvert{\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu}\rvert_{0})
.

Endowing
\mathbf{C}
with the discrete topology, we may consider the scheme
X
as a formal scheme, and consider its generic fiber
X^{\beth}
in the sense of Raynaud. It is a compact subset of
X_{0}^{\textup{an}}
, and we may now define

X_{\infty}:=X_{0}^{\textup{an}}-X^{\beth},

which corresponds to the set of seminorms without center on
X
.

It is worth noticing that, if
X
is embedded as an open subset in a proper algebraic variety
Y
over
\mathbf{C}
with complement
Z
, then
X_{\infty}
may be identified with the generic fiber (in the sense of Raynaud–Berthelot) of the formal completion
\hat{Y}_{Z}
of
Y
along
Z
deprived of (the analytication of) its special fiber
Z
. In particular, the latter construction does not depend on the choice of
Y
.

The set
X_{\infty}
was first defined by V. Berkovich in a letter to V. Drinfeld
[
Ber04
]
, and subsequentely used by O. Ben–Bassat and M. Temkin in
[
BBT13
]
to prove some descent results (reconstructing coherent sheaves on
Y
from coherent sheaves on
\hat{Y}_{Z}
and
X
). It was also independently defined by A. Thuillier in
[
Thu07
]
, who proved that if
Y
is regular and
Z
has normal crossings, then the dual complex of
Z
is homotopy equivalent to
X_{\infty}
. As a consequence, the homotopy type of the dual complex of the boundary depends only on
X
and not on the chosen compactification.

\blacktriangleright

Hybrid Berkovich spaces

It remains to put together the analytic space
X^{\textup{an}}
and its boundary
X_{\infty}
. For this purpose, we use the framework of Berkovich spaces over Banach rings, as introduced in
[
Ber90
]
and later developped in
[
Poi10
,
Poi13
,
LP24
]
.

Consider the field
\mathbf{C}
endowed with the hybrid norm
\lVert{\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu}\rVert_{\mathrm{hyb}}:=\max(\lvert{\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu}\rvert_{0},\lvert{\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu}\rvert_{\infty})
, where
\lvert{\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu}\rvert_{\infty}
is the usual absolute value on
\mathbf{C}
. It is a Banach ring, and we may consider the analytification
X^{\mathrm{hyb}}
of
X
over it. The basic example is the analytification of
\Spec(\mathbf{C})
, which may be explicitly described as

\Spec(\mathbf{C})^{\mathrm{hyb}}=\{\lvert{\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu}\rvert_{\infty}^{\varepsilon},0\leqslant\varepsilon\leqslant 1\},

where
\lvert{\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu}\rvert_{\infty}^{0}:=\lvert{\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu}\rvert_{0}
.

The structure morphism
\pr\colon X\to\Spec(\mathbf{C})
gives rise to a morphism
\pr^{\mathrm{hyb}}\colon X^{\mathrm{hyb}}\to\Spec(\mathbf{C})^{\mathrm{hyb}}
, by functoriality. Its fiber over
\lvert{\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu}\rvert_{0}
coincides with
X^{\textup{an}}_{0}
, whereas the fibers over the different
\lvert{\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu}\rvert_{\infty}^{\varepsilon}
, with
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
, all identifies to
X^{\textup{an}}
.

The parameter
\varepsilon
plays a role as a scaling factor for the metric, but no more at the topological level.
It makes sense to interpret the morphism
\pr^{\mathrm{hyb}}
as a degeneration of a family of complex analytic spaces onto a non-Archimedean Berkovich space.

\blacktriangleright

The compactification

Set

X^{+}:=X^{\mathrm{hyb}}-X^{\beth}.

It is an open subset of
X^{\mathrm{hyb}}
, whence inherits a structure of locally ringed space. The space
X^{+}
is very similar to
X^{\mathrm{hyb}}
. It has the same Archimedean part: a family of spaces
X^{\textup{an}}
parametrized by
(0,1]
, and only differs in its non-Archimedean part, with
X_{\infty}
replacing
X_{0}^{\textup{an}}
.

In order to keep only one copy of
X^{\textup{an}}
, we identify the points of the space
X^{+}
that correspond to equivalent seminorms. The resulting space
X^{\!\urcorner}
, endowed with the push-forward structure sheaf, is the promised compactification.

By construction, the Archimedean part of the space
X^{\!\urcorner}
now consists in exactly one copy of
X^{\textup{an}}
. Its non-Archimedean part, which is the quotient of
X_{\infty}
by the equivalence of seminorms, is a so-called normalized space, as introduced by L. Fantini in
[
Fan18
]
.

Organization of the manuscript

The first two sections gather general material about Berkovich spaces over Banach rings. In Section
1
, we briefly recall their definition. In Section
2
, we focus on the case of hybrid Berkovich spaces, that is to say spaces over Banach rings of the form
(\mathbf{C},\lVert{\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu}\rVert_{\mathrm{hyb}})
, possibly allowing other valued fields instead of
\mathbf{C}
. We carefully investigate properties of the
flow
, which raises multiplicative seminorms (corresponding to points of Berkovich spaces) to some power. The latter plays a fundamental role in the last part of the construction of the compactification, where the quotient is performed.

The rest of the paper deals more specifically with the compactification problem. In Section
3
, we recall the construction of the boundary
X_{\infty}
and its main properties.
In Section
4
, we perform the compactification procedure by defining
X^{+}
and
X^{\!\urcorner}
. We investigate their topological properties as well as their first sheaf-theoretic properties, and prove Theorem
A
.
Finally, in Section
5
, we investigate coherent sheaves on
X^{+}
, and prove Theorem
B
. The general strategy is to reduce to a classical GAGA theorem on some fixed compactification
Y
of
X
, by means of extension results for coherent sheaves from
X^{+}
to
Y^{\textup{an}}
. The main technical input comes from the Stein properties (higher cohomological vanishing and global generation for coherent sheaves) of hybrid discs from
[
LP24
]
.

We denote by
\mathbf{N}
(resp.
\mathbf{N}_{\geqslant 1}

A
variety
over a field
k
is a separated scheme of finite type over
k
(hence quasi-compact).

1.
Berkovich analytic spaces over Banach rings

Let
(\mathcal{A},\lVert{\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu}\rVert)
be a Banach ring. In this section, we recall V. Berkovich’s definition of
\mathcal{A}
-analytic space (see
[
Ber90
, Section 1.5]
).

1.1.
Definitions

We start with the affine analytic space
\mathbf{A}^{n,\mathrm{an}}_{\mathcal{A}}
of dimension
n
over
\mathcal{A}
, for
n\in\mathbf{N}
. It bears the structure of a locally ringed space and we define it in three steps: set, topology and structure sheaf.

Set:
The underlying set of
\mathbf{A}^{n,\mathrm{an}}_{\mathcal{A}}
is the set of bounded multiplicative seminorms on
\mathcal{A}[T_{1},\dotsc,T_{n}]
that are bounded on
\mathcal{A}
,
i.e.
the set of maps

\lvert{\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu}\rvert\colon\mathcal{A}[T_{1},\dotsc,T_{n}]\longrightarrow\mathbf{R}_{\geqslant 0}

that satisfy the following properties:

i)

|0|=0
and
|1|=1
;

ii)

\forall P,Q\in\mathcal{A}[T_{1},\dotsc,T_{n}]
,
|P+Q|\leqslant|P|+|Q|
;

iii)

\forall P,Q\in\mathcal{A}[T_{1},\dotsc,T_{n}]
,
|PQ|=|P|\,|Q|
;

iv)

\forall a\in\mathcal{A}
,
|a|\leqslant\|a\|
.

We set
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{A}):=\mathbf{A}^{0,\mathrm{an}}_{\mathcal{A}}
and call it the
spectrum
of
\mathcal{A}
. Note that we have a projection map
\pr\colon\mathbf{A}^{n,\mathrm{an}}_{\mathcal{A}}\to\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{A})
induced by the morphism
\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{A}[T_{1},\dotsc,T_{n}]
.

Let
x
be a point of
\mathbf{A}^{n,\mathrm{an}}_{\mathcal{A}}
. Denote by
\lvert{\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu}\rvert_{x}
the multiplicative seminorm associated to it. The ring
\mathcal{A}[T_{1},\dotsc,T_{n}]/\Ker(\lvert{\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu}\rvert_{x})
is a domain and we can consider its field of fractions. The seminorm
\lvert{\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu}\rvert_{x}
induces an absolute value on the later, and we can consider its completion, which we denote by
\mathcal{H}(x)
. We simply denote by
\lvert{\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu}\rvert
the absolute value on
\mathcal{H}(x)
induced by
\lvert{\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu}\rvert_{x}
, since no confusion may result.

We have a natural morphism
\chi_{x}\colon\mathcal{A}[T_{1},\dotsc,T_{n}]\to\mathcal{H}(x)
. For each
P\in\mathcal{A}[T_{1},\dotsc,T_{n}]
, we set
P(x):=\chi_{x}(P)
. Note that, by definition, we have
|P(x)|=|P|_{x}
.

Topology:
The set
\mathbf{A}^{n,\mathrm{an}}_{\mathcal{A}}
is endowed with the coarsest topology such that, for each
P\in\mathcal{A}[T_{1},\dotsc,T_{n}]
, the map

x\in\mathbf{A}^{n,\mathrm{an}}_{\mathcal{A}}\longmapsto|P(x)|\in\mathbf{R}_{\geqslant 0}

is continuous. The resulting topological space is Hausdorff and locally compact. The spectrum
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{A})
is even compact. The projection map
\pr
is continuous.

For each
x\in\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{A})
, we have a natural homeomorphism

\mathbf{A}^{n,\mathrm{an}}_{\mathcal{H}(x)}\xrightarrow{\sim}\pr^{-1}(x).

Structure sheaf:
For each open subset
V
of
\mathbf{A}^{n,\mathrm{an}}_{\mathcal{A}}
, we denote by
S_{V}
the set of element of
\mathcal{A}[T_{1},\dotsc,T_{n}]
that do not vanish on
V
and set
K(V):=S_{V}^{-1}\mathcal{A}[T_{1},\dotsc,T_{n}]
.

Let
U
be an open subset of
\mathbf{A}^{n,\mathrm{an}}_{\mathcal{A}}
. We define
\mathcal{O}(U)
to be the set of maps

f\colon U\longrightarrow\bigsqcup_{x\in U}\mathcal{H}(x)

such that

i)

for each
x\in U
,
f(x)\in\mathcal{H}(x)
;

ii)

each
x\in U
has an open neighborhood
V
on which
f
is a uniform limit of elements of
K(V)
.

One may now define arbitrary
\mathcal{A}
-analytic spaces
, by first considering
local models
,
i.e.
closed analytic subspaces of open subsets of affine spaces, and then gluing the latter. Note that those spaces are not merely locally ringed spaces, since one wants to keep track of some metric information on the rings of sections, for instance. The actual definition thus involves several pieces of data such as atlases, embeddings into affine spaces for local models, etc. We refer to
[
LP24
, 2.1]
for details.

Each
\mathcal{A}
-analytic space
X
admits a canonical projection map
\pr\colon X\to\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{A})
. Moreover, for each
x\in\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{A})
, the fiber
\pr^{-1}(x)
may naturally be endowed with a structure of
\mathcal{H}(x)
-analytic space.

In the rest of the text, we will consider sections of sheaves on arbitrary subsets of a space. Recall that, given a space
S
and a sheaf
\mathcal{F}
on it, for each subset
T
of
S
, one may define the set of
overconvergent
sections

\mathcal{F}(T):=\colim_{U\supseteq T}\mathcal{F}(U),

where
U
runs through the open neighborhoods of
T
in
S
. Note that it gives back the usual
\mathcal{F}(T)
when
T
is open.

In the case where
(S,\mathcal{O})
is a locally ringed space, by the former procedure, any subset
T
inherits a locally ringed space structure, called overconvergent. The structure sheaf on
T
identifies to
\iota^{-1}\mathcal{O}
, where
\iota\colon T\to S
in the inclusion. We usually still denote it by
\mathcal{O}
.

As a consequence of the preceding discussion, the notion of coherent sheaf on an arbitrary subset of an analytic space makes sense. Let us quote the following useful result (see
[
Car52
, Proposition 1]
). The reference provides a proof for complex analytic spaces, but it adapts readily to a more general setting.

Proposition 1.1
.

Let
S
be an
\mathcal{A}
-analytic space and
K
be a compact subset of
S
. Each coherent sheaf on
K
extends to a coherent sheaf on a neighborhood of
K
.

1.2.
Base rings

The theory of Berkovich spaces over
\mathcal{A}
has been extensively studied in the case where
\mathcal{A}
is a complete non-Archimedean valued field (see
[
Ber90
,
Ber93
]
for instance). The case where
\mathcal{A}
is an arbitrary Banach ring has comparatively received little attention.

In recent work, the author and his collaborators have tried to single out properties of the ring
\mathcal{A}
that are sufficient to ensure the existence of a well-behaved theory of
\mathcal{A}
-analytic spaces (see
[
Poi10
,
Poi13
,
LP24
]
). This led to the notion of
geometric base ring
(see
[
LP24
, Définition 3.3.8]
). Over such a ring, several expected basic properties of the spaces are known to hold: Noetherianity and Henselianity of the local rings, coherence of the structure sheaf, etc. One may also define a notion of morphism of
\mathcal{A}
-analytic spaces, and the resulting category is stable under various operations, such as fiber products.

The precise conditions for
\mathcal{A}
to be a geometric base ring are long, technical and not particularly illuminating. Instead of stating them, we provide a list of examples where they are satisfied and that already yield interesting applications:

i)

a non-Archimedean complete valued field
(k,\lvert{\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu}\rvert)
, which gives back a large part of the usual theory of
k
-analytic spaces (good spaces with no boundary in Berkovich’s terminology);

ii)

an Archimedean complete valued field
(k,\lvert{\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu}\rvert)
, including the case of
(\mathbf{C},\lvert{\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu}\rvert_{\infty})
, where
\lvert{\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu}\rvert_{\infty}
denotes the usual absolute value, which gives back the usual theory of complex analytic spaces;

iii)

a complete discrete valuation ring
(R,\lvert{\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu}\rvert)
, where
\lvert{\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu}\rvert
is associated to the valuation;

iv)

the ring of integers
(\mathbb{Z},\lvert{\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu}\rvert_{\infty})
or the ring of integers of a number field.

In this paper, we will consider another example, that of hybrid fields. We defer the definition to Section
2.1
.

1.3.
Analytification

Assume that
\mathcal{A}
is a geometric base ring. In this section, we recall how to associate naturally an
\mathcal{A}
-analytic space to a scheme over
\mathcal{A}
, under suitable assumptions. We denote by
\Hom_{\mathcal{A}-\textrm{loc}}({\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu},{\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu})
the set of morphisms in the category of locally
\mathcal{A}
-ringed spaces.

Let
X
be a scheme locally of finite presentation over
\mathcal{A}
. By
[
LP24
, Théorème 4.1.4]
, the functor

\begin{array}[]{ccc}\mathcal{A}-\textrm{An}&\longrightarrow&\textrm{Set}\\
S&\mapsto&\Hom_{\mathcal{A}-\textrm{loc}}(S,X)\end{array}

is representable. The analytic space representing the functor is called
analytification of
X
, and denoted by
X^{\textup{an}}
. It comes with a morphism of locally
\mathcal{A}
-ringed spaces

\rho_{X}\colon X^{\textup{an}}\longrightarrow X.

It follows from the proof of
[
LP24
, Théorème 4.1.4]
that the analytification
X^{\textup{an}}
may be constructed by the same procedure as for complex analytic spaces. We recall it here.

Step 1:

X
is of the form
\mathbf{A}^{n}_{\mathcal{A}}
, for some
n\in\mathbf{N}
.

The analytification is
X^{\textup{an}}=\mathbf{A}^{n,\mathrm{an}}_{\mathcal{A}}
and the morphism

\rho_{\mathbf{A}^{n}_{\mathcal{A}}}\colon\mathbf{A}^{n,\mathrm{an}}_{\mathcal{A}}\longrightarrow\mathbf{A}^{n}_{\mathcal{A}}

is the one sending a multiplicative seminorm to its kernel.

Step 2:

X
is an affine variety of finite presentation over
\mathcal{A}
.

The variety
X
is a closed subscheme of some affine space
\mathbf{A}^{n}_{\mathcal{A}}
defined by a finitely generated ideal
I
of
\mathcal{O}(\mathbf{A}^{n}_{\mathcal{A}})
.
Its analytification
X^{\textup{an}}
is the closed analytic subspace of
\mathbf{A}^{n,\mathrm{an}}_{\mathcal{A}}
defined by the sheaf of ideals generated by
I
and the morphism
\rho_{X}
is the one induced by
\rho_{\mathbf{A}^{n}_{\mathcal{A}}}
.

Step 3:

X
is locally of finite presentation over
\mathcal{A}
.

The variety
X
admits an open covering
(U_{i})_{i\in I}
by affine varieties of finite presentation over
\mathcal{A}
. Its analytification
X^{\textup{an}}
and the morphism
\rho_{X}
are obtained by gluing the
U_{i}^{\textup{an}}
’s and the
\rho_{U_{i}}
’s constructed previously.

Remark 1.2
.

From the explicit construction of the analytification, we may derive an alternative useful description of the underlying set of
X^{\textup{an}}
. It may be identified with the set of pairs

\{(q,\lvert{\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu}\rvert):q\in X,\ \lvert{\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu}\rvert\textrm{ absolute value on }\kappa(q)\textrm{ such that }\lvert{\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu}\rvert_{|\mathcal{A}}\leqslant\lVert{\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu}\rVert\}.

2.
Hybrid Berkovich spaces

In this section, we focus on the case of
hybrid spaces
, that is to say analytic spaces over a hybrid field.

2.1.
Definitions

Let
k
be a field endowed with a non-trivial absolute value
\lvert{\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu}\rvert
(no completeness condition required).

Definition 2.1
.

We call
trivial absolute value
on
k
the absolute value defined by

\lvert{\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu}\rvert_{0}\colon a\in k\longmapsto\begin{cases}0&\textrm{if }a=0;\\
1&\textrm{otherwise}\end{cases}.

We call
hybrid norm
on
k
the norm defined by

\lVert{\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu}\rVert_{\mathrm{hyb}}:=\max(\lvert{\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu}\rvert_{0},\lvert{\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu}\rvert).

The pair
(k,\lVert{\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu}\rVert_{\mathrm{hyb}})
is a Banach ring called
hybrid field
associated to
k
. We denote it by
k_{\mathrm{hyb}}
.

Notation 2.2
.

We denote by
k_{0}
the field
k
endowed with the trivial absolute value
\lvert{\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu}\rvert_{0}
.

We denote by
\hat{k}
the completion of the field
k
with respect to
\lvert{\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu}\rvert
. For
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
, we denote by
\hat{k}_{\varepsilon}
the completion of the field
k
with respect to
\lvert{\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu}\rvert_{\varepsilon}
. Note that all these fields are isomorphic.

The spectrum of the hybrid field
k_{\mathrm{hyb}}
may be easily described: the map

\varepsilon\in[0,1]\longmapsto\lvert{\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu}\rvert^{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{M}(k_{\mathrm{hyb}})

is a homeomorphism. In the sequel, we will identity
\mathcal{M}(k_{\mathrm{hyb}})
and
[0,1]
by the map above. With this notation, we have
\mathcal{H}(0)=k_{0}
and, for each
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
,
\mathcal{H}(\varepsilon)=\hat{k}_{\varepsilon}
.

Notation 2.3
.

Let
X
be a
k_{\mathrm{hyb}}
-analytic space, with projection map
\pr\colon X\to\mathcal{M}(k_{\mathrm{hyb}})=[0,1]
.

For each
\varepsilon\in[0,1]
, set
X_{\varepsilon}:=\pr^{-1}(\varepsilon)
. It is naturally an
\mathcal{H}(\varepsilon)
-analytic space.

Set
X_{>0}:=X-X_{0}
.

Remark 2.4
.

Let
X
be a
k_{\mathrm{hyb}}
-analytic space. All the spaces
X_{\varepsilon}
, with
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
, are of a similar flavour. Indeed, a
\hat{k}_{\varepsilon}
-analytic space is essentially a
\hat{k}
-analytic space with a different normalization, and the underlying locally ringed spaces are isomorphic. On the other hand, the space
X_{0}
is a
k_{0}
-analytic space, hence a space over a trivially valued field, rather different from the former.

The case where
(k,\lvert{\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu}\rvert)=(\mathbf{C},\lvert{\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu}\rvert_{\infty})
is particularly striking: the fibers over
(0,1]
are complex analytic spaces, or closely related spaces, whereas the fiber over 0 is a non-Archimedean Berkovich space. One may interpret this picture as a family of complex analytic spaces degenerating onto a non-Archimedean space. This point of view gave rise to many applications, such as
[
Ber09
]
,
[
BJ17
]
,
[
Fav20
]
,
[
DF19
]
,
[
DKY20
]
, etc.

2.2.
The flow

Let
k
be a field endowed with a non-trivial absolute value
\lvert{\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu}\rvert
.

2.2.1.
Definitions and first properties

Definition 2.5
.

For
\varepsilon\in[0,1]
, set

I_{\varepsilon}:=\begin{cases}[0,+\infty)&\textrm{if }\varepsilon=0;\\
[0,\frac{1}{\varepsilon}]&\textrm{if }\varepsilon>0.\end{cases}

and
I^{\ast}_{\varepsilon}=I_{\varepsilon}-\{0\}
.

Let
S
be a
k_{\mathrm{hyb}}
-analytic space and consider the projection map
\pr\colon S\to\mathcal{M}(k_{\mathrm{hyb}})=[0,1]
. For each
x\in S
, we set
I_{x}:=I_{\pr(x)}
and
I_{x}^{\ast}:=I^{\ast}_{\pr(x)}
.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
x\in\mathbf{A}^{n,\mathrm{hyb}}_{k}
and
\alpha\in I_{x}
. The map

P\in k[T_{1},\dotsc,T_{n}]\mapsto|P(x)|^{\alpha}\in\mathbf{R}_{\geqslant 0}

is a multiplicative seminorm on
k[T_{1},\dotsc,T_{n}]
that is bounded by
\lVert{\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu}\rVert_{\mathrm{hyb}}
on
k
. In other terms, it defines a point in
\mathbf{A}^{n,\mathrm{hyb}}_{k}
.

Proof.

By definition, the map
P\in k[T_{1},\dotsc,T_{n}]\mapsto|P(x)|\in\mathbf{R}_{\geqslant 0}
is a multiplicative seminorm on
k[T_{1},\dotsc,T_{n}]
that is bounded by
\lVert{\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu}\rVert_{\mathrm{hyb}}
on
k
. It follows that the map
P\in k[T_{1},\dotsc,T_{n}]\mapsto|P(x)|^{\alpha}\in\mathbf{R}_{\geqslant 0}
satisfies the same properties. The only non-trivial part is the fact that the triangle inequality still holds, in the case where
k
is Archimedean. This is ensured by the choice of
\alpha
(see for instance
[
Bou64
, VI, §6, n
∘
1, Proposition 2]
).
∎

Notation 2.7
.

Let
x\in\mathbf{A}^{n,\mathrm{hyb}}_{k}
and
\alpha\in I_{x}
. We denote by
x^{\alpha}\in\mathbf{A}^{n,\mathrm{hyb}}_{k}
the point associated to the map
P\mapsto|P(x)|^{\alpha}
from Lemma
2.6
.

If
\alpha\in I_{x}^{\ast}
, the fields
\mathcal{H}(x)
and
\mathcal{H}(x^{\alpha})
are obtained by completing the same field for equivalent absolute values, which yields an isomorphism

\lambda_{x^{\alpha},x}\colon\mathcal{H}(x)\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal{H}(x^{\alpha}).

Remark 2.8
.

We have
\pr(x^{\alpha})=\alpha\,\pr(x)
.

The isomorphism
\lambda_{x^{\alpha},x}
is not an isometry, but raises the absolute value to the power
\alpha
:
for each
u\in\mathcal{H}(x)
, we have

\lvert\lambda_{x^{\alpha},x}(u)\rvert=\lvert u\rvert^{\alpha}.

In particular,
for each
P\in k[T_{1},\dotsc,T_{n}]
, we have

\lvert\lambda_{x^{\alpha},x}(P)(x^{\alpha})\rvert=\lvert P(x)\rvert^{\alpha}.

Although it is possible to define
x^{\alpha}
for each
\alpha\in I_{x}
, in the case
\alpha=0
, several properties are lost. In the sequel, we will only consider
\alpha\in I_{x}^{\ast}
.

Definition 2.9
.

Set

D(\mathbf{A}^{n,\mathrm{hyb}}_{k}):=\bigcup_{x\in\mathbf{A}^{n,\mathrm{hyb}}_{k}}\{x\}\times I^{\ast}_{x}\subseteq\mathbf{A}^{n,\mathrm{hyb}}_{k}\times\mathbf{R}_{>0}.

We define the
flow
on
\mathbf{A}^{n,\mathrm{hyb}}_{k}
as the map

\begin{array}[]{ccccc}\Phi&\colon&D(\mathbf{A}^{n,\mathrm{hyb}}_{k})&\longrightarrow&\mathbf{A}^{n,\mathrm{hyb}}_{k}\\
&&(x,\alpha)&\longmapsto&x^{\alpha}\end{array}.

Proposition 2.10
.

The flow
\Phi\colon D(\mathbf{A}^{n,\mathrm{hyb}}_{k})\to\mathbf{A}^{n,\mathrm{hyb}}_{k}
is continuous and open.

Proof.

Continuity follows from the definitions (see the proof of
[
Poi10
, Proposition 1.3.4]
for details).

Let us prove openness. Let
(x,\alpha)\in D(\mathbf{A}^{n,\mathrm{hyb}}_{k})
and let
W
be a neighborhood of
(x,\alpha)
in
D(\mathbf{A}^{n,\mathrm{hyb}}_{k})
. It is enough to prove that
\Phi(W)
contains a neighborhood of
x^{\alpha}
in
\mathbf{A}^{n,\mathrm{hyb}}_{k}
.

By definition of the topology of
\mathbf{A}^{n,\mathrm{hyb}}_{k}
, there exists
m\in\mathbf{N}
,
P_{1},\dotsc,P_{m}\in k[T_{1},\dotsc,T_{n}]
,
r_{1},\dotsc,r_{m},s_{1},\dotsc,s_{m}\in\mathbf{R}
with
r_{i}<\lvert P_{i}(x)\rvert<s_{i}
for all
i=1,\dotsc,m
and
\beta,\gamma\in\mathbf{R}_{>0}
with
\beta<\alpha<\gamma
such that

W^{\prime}:=\big(\{y\in\mathbf{A}^{n,\mathrm{hyb}}_{k}:\forall i=1,\dotsc,m,\ r_{i}<\lvert P_{i}(y)\rvert<s_{i}\}\times(\beta,\gamma)\big)\cap D(\mathbf{A}^{n,\mathrm{hyb}}_{k})

is an open neighborhood of
(x,\alpha)
contained in
W
.

\bullet
Assume that
\alpha\geqslant 1
.

Set

U:=\{z\in\mathbf{A}^{n,\mathrm{hyb}}_{k}:\forall i=1,\dotsc,m,\ r_{i}^{\alpha}<\lvert P_{i}(z)\rvert<s_{i}^{\alpha}\}.

It is an open neighborhood of
x^{\alpha}
in
\mathbf{A}^{n,\mathrm{hyb}}_{k}
.

For each
z\in U
, we have
\frac{1}{\alpha}\in I_{z}^{\ast}
, and
(z^{1/\alpha},\alpha)\in W^{\prime}
. It follows that
\Phi(W^{\prime})\supseteq U
.

\bullet
Assume that
\alpha<1
.

Note that, we have
p(x^{\alpha})\leqslant\alpha
. Let
\alpha^{\prime}\in(\alpha,\min(\gamma,1))
such that, for each
i=1,\dotsc,m
, we have
r_{i}^{\alpha^{\prime}/\alpha}<\lvert P_{i}(x)\rvert<s_{i}^{\alpha^{\prime}/\alpha}
. Then, the set

V:=\{z\in\mathbf{A}^{n,\mathrm{hyb}}_{k}:\forall i=1,\dotsc,m,\ r_{i}^{\alpha^{\prime}}<\lvert P_{i}(z)\rvert<s_{i}^{\alpha^{\prime}}\}\cap\pr^{-1}([0,\alpha^{\prime}))

is an open neighborhood of
x^{\alpha}
in
\mathbf{A}^{n,\mathrm{hyb}}_{k}
.

For each
z\in V
, we have
\frac{1}{\alpha^{\prime}}\in I_{z}^{\ast}
, and
(z^{1/\alpha^{\prime}},\alpha^{\prime})\in W^{\prime}
. It follows that
\Phi(W^{\prime})\supseteq V
.
∎

Definition 2.11
.

Let
x\in\mathbf{A}^{n,\mathrm{hyb}}_{k}
. The
trajectory
of
x
is the set

T(x):=\Phi(x,I_{x}^{\ast})=\{x^{\alpha}\colon\alpha\in I^{\ast}_{x}\}\subseteq\mathbf{A}^{n,\mathrm{hyb}}_{k}.

Remark 2.12
.

The trajectory of a point may have two different shapes. Let
x\in\mathbf{A}^{n,\mathrm{hyb}}_{k}
.

\bullet
Assume that
\mathcal{H}(x)
is trivially valued.

Then,
I_{x}^{\ast}=(0,+\infty)
and, for each
\alpha\in(0,+\infty)
, we have
x^{\alpha}=x
. In particular,
T(x)=\{x\}
.

\bullet
Assume that
\mathcal{H}(x)
is not trivially valued.

In this case, the map
\Phi(x,{\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu})
is injective, and provides a homeomorphism between
T(x)
and
I_{x}^{\ast}
.

The following result is elementary. It will be crucial for us later in this text, by allowing us to consider the equivalence relation on
\mathbf{A}^{n,\mathrm{hyb}}_{k}
induced by trajectories.

Lemma 2.13
.

Let
x,y\in\mathbf{A}^{n,\mathrm{hyb}}_{k}
. If
y\in T(x)
, then
T(y)=T(x)
.

In particular, trajectories of points form a partition of
\mathbf{A}^{n,\mathrm{hyb}}_{k}
.
∎

We may also consider trajectories of arbitrary parts of
\mathbf{A}^{n,\mathrm{hyb}}_{k}
.

Definition 2.14
.

Let
V
be a subset of
\mathbf{A}^{n,\mathrm{hyb}}_{k}
. The
trajectory
of
V
is the set

T(V):=\bigcup_{x\in V}T(x)\subseteq\mathbf{A}^{n,\mathrm{hyb}}_{k}.

Remark 2.15
.

For
V,V^{\prime}\subseteq\mathbf{A}^{n,\mathrm{hyb}}_{k}
, we have
T(V\cap V^{\prime})=T(V)\cap T(V^{\prime})
and
T(V\cup V^{\prime})=T(V)\cup T(V^{\prime})
.

2.2.2.
Flow-connected and flow-open sets

We introduce some properties ensuring that the structure sheaf is preserved by the flow.

Definition 2.16
.

Let
V
be a subset of
\mathbf{A}^{n,\mathrm{hyb}}_{k}
. We say that
V
is
flow-connected
if, for each
x\in V
,
T(x)\cap V
is connected.

Remark 2.17
.

Intersections of flow-connected subsets are flow-connected. Indeed, by Remark
2.12
, connected sets of the form
T(x)\cap V
are intervals, hence their connectedness is preserved by intersection.

Lemma 2.18
.

Flow-connected open subsets form a basis of the topology of
\mathbf{A}^{n,\mathrm{hyb}}_{k}
.

Proof.

By definition, a basis of the topology of
\mathbf{A}^{n,\mathrm{hyb}}_{k}
is given by sets of the form

\{x\in\mathbf{A}^{n,\mathrm{hyb}}_{k}:\forall i=1,\dotsc,m,\ r_{i}<\lvert P_{i}(x)\rvert<s_{i}\},

for
m\in\mathbf{N}
,
P_{1},\dotsc,P_{m}\in k[T_{1},\dotsc,T_{n}]
and
r_{1},\dotsc,r_{m},s_{1},\dotsc,s_{m}\in\mathbf{R}
. Those sets are flow-connected.
∎

Definition 2.19
.

Let
V
be a subset of
\mathbf{A}^{n,\mathrm{hyb}}_{k}
. We say that
V
is
flow-open
if the map

\begin{array}[]{ccccc}\Phi_{V}&\colon&D(\mathbf{A}^{n,\mathrm{hyb}}_{k})\cap(V\times\mathbf{R}_{>0})&\longrightarrow&T(V)\\
&&(x,\alpha)&\longmapsto&x^{\alpha}\end{array}

induced by
\Phi
is open.

Example 2.20
.

i)

For each
x\in\mathbf{A}^{n,\mathrm{hyb}}_{k}
and each interval
I\subseteq\mathbf{R}
,
\Phi(x,I\cap I_{x}^{\ast})
is flow-open.

ii)

By Proposition
2.10
, each open subset of
\mathbf{A}^{n,\mathrm{hyb}}_{k}
is flow-open.

The following properties are direct consequences of the definition.

Lemma 2.21
.

Let
V
be a flow-open subset of
\mathbf{A}^{n,\mathrm{hyb}}_{k}
.

i)

For each open subset
V^{\prime}
of
V
,
V^{\prime}
is flow-open and
T(V^{\prime})
is open in
T(V)
.

ii)

For each interval
I\subseteq\mathbf{R}
,
\Phi(V,I)
is flow-open. In particular,
T(V)
is flow-open.

iii)

For each subset
W
of
\mathbf{A}^{n,\mathrm{hyb}}_{k}
,
V\cap T(W)
is flow-open.

∎

Proposition 2.22
.

Let
U
be a flow-connected subset of
\mathbf{A}^{n,\mathrm{hyb}}_{k}
. Let
V
be a flow-connected subset of
\mathbf{A}^{n,\mathrm{hyb}}_{k}
such that
U\subseteq V\subseteq T(U)
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{O}(V)
. For each
x\in U
and each
\alpha\in I_{x}^{\ast}
such that
x^{\alpha}\in V
, we have

f(x^{\alpha})=\lambda_{x^{\alpha},x}(f(x))\textrm{ and }\lvert f(x^{\alpha})\rvert=\lvert f(x)\rvert^{\alpha}.

The restriction map
\mathcal{O}(V)\to\mathcal{O}(U)
is injective.

If, moreover,
U
is flow-open, then the restriction map
\mathcal{O}(V)\to\mathcal{O}(U)
is an isomorphism.

Proof.

We closely follow the proof of
[
Poi10
, Proposition 1.3.10]
, which deals with the case where
U
is a singleton. We include details for the convenience of the reader.

Let
x\in U
. Set

I_{V,x}^{\ast}:=\{\alpha\in I_{x}^{\ast}:x^{\alpha}\in V\}.

Since
V
is flow-connected,
I_{V,x}^{\ast}
is an interval. Set

J:=\{\alpha\in I_{V,x}^{\ast}:f(x^{\alpha})=\lambda_{x^{\alpha},x}(f(x))\}.

Let us prove that
J
is open. Let
\alpha\in J
. Let
W
be a neighborhood of
x^{\alpha}
in
\mathbf{A}^{n,\mathrm{hyb}}_{k}
. By continuity of the flow (see Proposition
2.10
), there exists an open neighborhood
L
of
\alpha
in
I_{x}^{\ast}
such that
\{x^{\beta}:\beta\in L\}\subseteq W
. Up to shrinking
W
, we may assume that
f
is defined on
W
. By definition of the structure sheaf, up to shrinking
W
again, we may assume that there exists a sequence
(R_{i})_{i\in\mathbf{N}}
of elements of
k(T_{1},\dotsc,T_{n})
without poles on
W
that converges uniformly to
f
on
W
. For each
\beta\in L
and each
i\in\mathbf{N}
, we have
R_{i}(x^{\beta})=\lambda_{x^{\beta},x^{\alpha}}(R_{i}(x^{\alpha}))
. By passing to the limit, we deduce that

f(x^{\beta})=\lambda_{x^{\beta},x^{\alpha}}(f(x^{\alpha}))=\lambda_{x^{\beta},x}(f(x)),

hence
L\subseteq J
.

Let us prove that
J
is closed. Let
\alpha\in I^{\ast}_{V,x}-J
. Set
m:=\lvert f(x^{\alpha})-\lambda_{x^{\alpha},x}(f(x))\rvert>0
. As before, there exist a neighborhood
W
of
x^{\alpha}
(resp.
V
of
x
) in
\mathbf{A}^{n,\mathrm{hyb}}_{k}
and a sequence
(R_{i})_{i\in\mathbf{N}}
(resp.
(S_{i})_{i\in\mathbf{N}}
) of elements of
k(T_{1},\dotsc,T_{n})
without poles on
W
(resp.
V
) that converges uniformly to
f
on
W
(resp.
V
).
Let
\varepsilon>0
such that
\max(\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\alpha})<m/6
.
There exists
j\in\mathbf{N}
such that
\lVert f-R_{j}\rVert_{W}<\varepsilon
and
\lVert f-S_{j}\rVert_{V}<\varepsilon
. For
\beta\in I^{\ast}_{V,x}
close enough to
\alpha
, we have
x^{\beta}\in W
and

(RS)

f(x^{\beta})-\lambda_{x^{\beta},x}(f(x))=f(x^{\beta})-S_{j}(x^{\beta})+S_{j}(x^{\beta})-R_{j}(x^{\beta})+\lambda_{x^{\beta},x}(R_{j}(x))-\lambda_{x^{\beta},x}(f(x)).

Note that
\lvert f(x^{\beta})-S_{j}(x^{\beta})\rvert\leqslant\varepsilon
and
\lvert\lambda_{x^{\beta},x}(R_{j}(x))-\lambda_{x^{\beta},x}(f(x))\rvert\leqslant\varepsilon^{\beta}
.

Applying (
RS
) with
\beta=\alpha
shows that
\lvert S_{j}(x^{\alpha})-R_{j}(x^{\alpha})\rvert\geqslant 2m/3
. By continuity of
\lvert S_{j}-R_{j}\rvert
, for
\beta\in I^{\ast}_{V,x}
close enough to
\alpha
, we have
\lvert S_{j}(x^{\beta})-R_{j}(x^{\beta})\rvert\geqslant m/2
. Applying (
RS
) again for
\beta\in I^{\ast}_{V,x}
close enough to
\alpha
, we obtain

\lvert f(x^{\beta})-\lambda_{x^{\beta},x}(f(x))\rvert\geqslant\frac{m}{6}>0.

It follows that
J
is closed.

Since
J
contains 1, it is non-empty. By connectedness of
I_{V,x}^{\ast}
, we have
J=I_{V,x}^{\ast}
. The equality of absolue values in the first part of the statement follows. It implies that the restriction map
\mathcal{O}(V)\to\mathcal{O}(U)
is injective.

Assume that
U
is flow open and let us prove that the restriction map
\mathcal{O}(V)\to\mathcal{O}(U)
is surjective. We may assume that
V=T(U)
. Let
g\in\mathcal{O}(U)
. Let
y\in T(U)
. There exists
x\in U
such that
y\in T(x)
. Set

f(y):=\lambda_{y,x}(g(x)).

By the first part of the statement applied with
V=U
, this does not depend on the choice of
x
and we have
f_{|U}=g
. It remains to prove that
f\in\mathcal{O}(T(U))
.

Let
y\in T(U)
. Let
x\in U
and
\alpha\in I_{x}^{\ast}
such that
y=x^{\alpha}
. There exists an open neighborhood
W
of
x
in
\mathbf{A}^{n,\mathrm{hyb}}_{k}
, an element
g^{\prime}\in\mathcal{O}(W)
with
g^{\prime}_{|W\cap U}=g_{|W\cap U}
, and a sequence
(R_{i})_{i\in\mathbf{N}}
of elements of
k(T_{1},\dotsc,T_{n})
without poles on
W
that converges uniformly to
g^{\prime}
on
W
.

For each
z\in T(W)
, there exists
w\in W
such that
z\in T(w)
. Set

h^{\prime}(z):=\lambda_{z,w}(g^{\prime}(w)).

By the first part of the statement applied with
U=W
and
V=T(W)
, this does not depend on the choice of
w
. Let
\varepsilon\in(0,\alpha)
. A direct computation shows that the sequence
(R_{i})_{i\in\mathbf{N}}
converges uniformly to
h^{\prime}
on

\{w^{\beta}:(w,\beta)\in D(\mathbf{A}^{n,\mathrm{hyb}}_{k})\cap(W\times(\alpha-\varepsilon,\alpha+\varepsilon))\}.

By openness of the flow (see Proposition
2.10
), the latter is a neighborhood of
x^{\alpha}=y
in
\mathbf{A}^{n,\mathrm{hyb}}_{k}
, and it follows that
h^{\prime}
belongs to
\mathcal{O}(W^{\prime})
for some open neighborhood
W^{\prime}
of
y
in
\mathbf{A}^{n,\mathrm{hyb}}_{k}
.

Note that
f
coincides with
h^{\prime}
on
W^{\prime}\cap T(U)
. Since
U
is flow-open, the latter is a neighborhood of
y
in
T(U)
. The result follows.
∎

Remark 2.23
.

Without the assumption that
U
is flow-open, the result of Proposition
2.22
may fail. Consider for instance the space
\mathbf{A}^{1,\mathrm{hyb}}_{k}
with coordinate
X
and its subsets

U_{-}:=\Big\{x\in\mathbf{A}^{1,\mathrm{hyb}}_{k}:\pr(x)<\frac{1}{2},\ \lvert X(x)\rvert<1\Big\}

and

U_{+}:=\{x\in\mathbf{A}^{1,\mathrm{hyb}}_{k}:\pr(x)=1,\ \lvert X(x)\rvert=1\}.

The spaces
U_{-}
and
U_{+}
are disjoint, but the trajectory of
U_{-}
is

T(U_{-})=\{x\in\mathbf{A}^{1,\mathrm{hyb}}_{k}:\pr(x)\leqslant 1,\ \lvert X(x)\rvert<1\},

whose closure meets
U_{+}
. As a result, the morphism
\mathcal{O}(T(U_{-}\cup U_{+}))\to\mathcal{O}(U_{-}\cup U_{+})
is not surjective.

Lemma 2.24
.

Let
V
be an open subset of
\mathbf{A}^{n,\mathrm{hyb}}_{k}
. Let
p\in\mathbf{N}
and let
\mathcal{F}
be a finite type subsheaf of
\mathcal{O}_{V}^{p}
. Let
s\in\mathcal{O}(V)^{p}
. For each
x\in\mathbf{A}^{n,\mathrm{hyb}}_{k}
, the set

\{y\in V\cap T(x):s_{y}\in\mathcal{F}_{y}\}

is open and closed in
V\cap T(x)
.

Proof.

Let
x\in\mathbf{A}^{n,\mathrm{hyb}}_{k}
. Set
T^{\prime}:=\{y\in V\cap T(x):s_{y}\in\mathcal{F}_{y}\}
. By definition, it is open in
V\cap T(x)
.

Let us prove that
(V\cap T(x))-T^{\prime}
is also open in
V\cap T(x)
. Let
z\in(V\cap T(x))-T^{\prime}
. Then
s_{z}\notin\mathcal{F}_{z}
. Since
\mathcal{F}
is of finite type, there exist an open neighborhood
W
of
z
in
V
and sections
s_{1},\dotsc,s_{r}
of
\mathcal{F}(W)
that generate
\mathcal{F}
on
W
. By Lemma
2.18
, we may assume that
W
is flow-connected. Assume, by contradiction, that
W\cap T^{\prime}\neq\emptyset
and let
z^{\prime}\in W\cap T^{\prime}
. Then, we have
s_{z^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{F}_{z^{\prime}}
, hence there exist an open neighborhood
W^{\prime}
of
z^{\prime}
in
W
and
a_{1},\dotsc,a_{r}\in\mathcal{O}(W^{\prime})
such that
s=\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_{i}s_{i}
on
W^{\prime}
. We may assume that
W^{\prime}
is flow-connected.
By surjectivity of the restriction map
\mathcal{O}(T(W^{\prime}))\to\mathcal{O}(W^{\prime})
(see Proposition
2.22
), the
a_{i}
’s extend to elements of
\mathcal{O}(T(W^{\prime}))
, which we denote identically. By injectivity of the restriction map
\mathcal{O}(W\cap T(W^{\prime}))\to\mathcal{O}(W^{\prime})
(see Proposition
2.22
), the equality
s=\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_{i}s_{i}
still holds on
W\cap T(W^{\prime})
, hence in the neighborhood of
z
. It follows that
s_{z}\in\mathcal{F}_{z}
, which is a contradiction. This concludes the proof.
∎

Lemma 2.25
.

Let
V
be an open subset of
\mathbf{A}^{n,\mathrm{hyb}}_{k}
and let
\mathcal{F}
be a coherent sheaf on
V
. Let
s\in\mathcal{F}(V)
. For each
x\in\mathbf{A}^{n,\mathrm{hyb}}_{k}
, the set

\{y\in V\cap T(x):s_{y}=0\}

is open and closed in
V\cap T(x)
.

Proof.

Let
x\in\mathbf{A}^{n,\mathrm{hyb}}_{k}
. Set
T^{\prime}:=\{y\in V\cap T(x):s_{y}=0\}
. By definition, it is open in
V\cap T(x)
.

Let us prove that
(V\cap T(x))-T^{\prime}
is also open in
V\cap T(x)
. Let
z\in(V\cap T(x))-T^{\prime}
. Then
s_{z}\neq 0
. Since
\mathcal{F}
is coherent, there exists an open neighborhood
W
of
z
in
V
such that
\mathcal{F}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{W}^{p}/\mathcal{M}
, where
\mathcal{M}
is a finite type subsheaf of
\mathcal{O}_{W}^{p}
. Up to shrinking
W
, we may assume that
s_{|W}
lifts to an element
s^{\prime}
of
\mathcal{O}^{p}(W)
. Since
s_{z}\neq 0
, we have
s^{\prime}_{z}\notin\mathcal{M}_{z}
. By Lemma
2.24
, the last property holds in a neighborhood of
z
in
V\cap T(x)
. The result follows.
∎

Theorem 2.26
.

Let
U
and
V
be flow-connected flow-open subsets of
\mathbf{A}^{n,\mathrm{hyb}}_{k}
such that
U\subseteq V\subseteq T(U)
and
U
is open in
V
. For each coherent sheaf
\mathcal{F}
on
V
, the restriction map
\mathcal{F}(V)\to\mathcal{F}(U)
is an isomorphism.

Moreover, the restriction map induces an equivalence between the categories of coherent sheaves on
V
and
U
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a coherent sheaf on
V
. By Lemma
2.25
, the restriction map
\mathcal{F}(V)\to\mathcal{F}(U)
is injective. Let us prove that it is surjective. Let
s\in\mathcal{F}(U)
. By Zorn’s lemma, there exists a maximal flow-connected open subset
U^{\prime}
of
V
containing
U
to which
s
extends. Up to replacing
U
by
U^{\prime}
, we may assume that
s
does not extend beyond
U
.

Assume, in view of a contradiction that
U\neq V
. Let
x
be a point in the boundary of
U
in
V
. There exist a open neighborhood
V_{x}
of
x
in
V
and
t_{1},\dotsc,t_{p}\in\mathcal{F}(V_{x})
that generate
\mathcal{F}
on
V_{x}
. We may assume that
V_{x}
is flow-connected. Let
y\in V_{x}\cap T(x)\cap U
. There exist an open neighborhood
U_{y}
of
y
in
V_{x}\cap U
and
a_{1},\dotsc,a_{p}\in\mathcal{O}(U_{y})
such that
s_{|U_{y}}=\sum_{i=1}^{p}a_{i}t_{i,|U_{y}}
. We may assume that
U_{y}
is flow-connected. Note that
U_{y}
is also flow-open, by Lemma
2.21
, since it is open in
U
. By Proposition
2.22
, the
a_{i}
’s extend to elements of
T(U_{y})
, which we denote in the same way. Set
W_{x}:=V_{x}\cap T(U_{y})
and
s^{\prime}:=\sum_{i=1}^{p}a_{i,|W_{x}}t_{i,|W_{x}}
. By Lemma
2.21
, the set
W_{x}
is a flow-connected open neighborhood of
x
in
V
contained in
T(U_{y})
and the element
s^{\prime}
belongs to
\mathcal{F}(W_{x})
. By Lemma
2.25
,
s^{\prime}
coincides with
s
on
W_{x}\cap U
. As a result, we can extend
x
to
U\cup W_{x}
, which provides the desired contraction. We have just proved that
U=V
, which implies the claimed surjectivity.

Let us now consider the functor

R_{U,V}\colon\mathcal{F}\longmapsto\mathcal{F}_{|U}

from the category of coherent sheaves on
V
to that of coherent sheaves on
U
.

Let
\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G}
be coherent sheaves on
V
. The sheaf
\sHom(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G})
is coherent, hence, by the first part of the statement, the restriction from
V
to
U
induces an isomorphism

\Hom(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G})=\sHom(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G})(V)\xrightarrow{\sim}\sHom(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G})(U)=\Hom(\mathcal{F}_{|U},\mathcal{G}_{|U}).

In other words, the functor
R_{U,V}
is fully faithful.

It remains to prove that
R_{U,V}
is essentially surjective. Let
\mathcal{F}
be a coherent sheaf on
U
. As in the first part of the proof, using Zorn’s lemma, we may assume that
\mathcal{F}
admits no extensions as a coherent sheaf to a bigger flow-connected open subset of
V
.

Assume, in view of a contradiction, that
U\neq V
. Let
x
be a point in the boundary of
U
in
V
. Since
U\subseteq V\subseteq T(U)
, there exists
y\in U
such that
y\in T(x)
. By assumption,
\mathcal{F}
is coherent, hence there exists an open neighborhood
W
of
y
in
U
such that
\mathcal{F}_{|W}
is isomorphic to a sheaf of the form
\mathcal{O}_{W}^{p}/\mathcal{M}
, where
\mathcal{M}
is a finite type submodule of
\mathcal{O}_{W}^{p}
. Up to shrinking
W
, we may assume that it is flow-connected and that
\mathcal{M}
is generated by
s_{1},\dotsc,s_{r}\in\mathcal{O}(W)^{p}
. Set
W^{\prime}:=T(W)\cap V
. By Lemma
2.21
, it is an open neighborhood of
x
in
V
. By Proposition
2.22
,
s_{1},\dotsc,s_{r}
extend to elements
s^{\prime}_{1},\dotsc,s^{\prime}_{r}
in
\mathcal{O}(W^{\prime})^{p}
. Denote by
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
the submodule of
\mathcal{O}_{W^{\prime}}^{p}
that they generate and set
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}:=\mathcal{O}_{W^{\prime}}^{p}/\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
.

By construction,
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
is a coherent sheaf on
W^{\prime}
that it is isomorphic to
\mathcal{F}
on
W
. By full-faithfulness of the restriction functor, the isomorphism extends to
T(W)\cap U=W^{\prime}\cap U
. As a result, we may glue
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
to a coherent sheaf on
U\cup W^{\prime}
, which provides a contradiction. It follows that
U=V
, which concludes the proof of essential surjectivity.
∎

2.2.3.
Flowing spaces

We explain how to generalize the flow and the related notions to more general spaces.

Definition 2.27
.

Let
V
be a subset of
\mathbf{A}^{n,\mathrm{hyb}}_{k}
. We say that
V
is
flowing
if, for each
x\in V
and
\alpha\in I_{x}^{\ast}
, we have
x^{\alpha}\in V
.

Remark 2.28
.

Flowing subsets are flow-connected. Intersections and unions of flowing subsets are flowing.

Definition 2.29
.

A
flowing local
k^{\mathrm{hyb}}
-analytic model
is the data of an integer
n
, a flowing open subset
U
of
\mathbf{A}^{n,\mathrm{hyb}}_{k}
and a closed analytic subset of
U
.

A
flowing
k^{\mathrm{hyb}}
-analytic space
is a
k^{\mathrm{hyb}}
-analytic space obtained by gluing flowing local
k^{\mathrm{hyb}}
-analytic models along flowing open subsets.

Example 2.30
.

Let
X
be a variety over
k
. By construction, its analytification
X^{\mathrm{hyb}}
over
k^{\mathrm{hyb}}
is a flowing
k^{\mathrm{hyb}}
-analytic space.

The definitions given at the beginning of Section
2.2
:
x^{\alpha}
,
\lambda_{x^{\alpha},x}
, the flow
\Phi
, the trajectory
T(x)
, flow-connected and flow-open subsets, etc. immediately extend to arbitrary flowing
k^{\mathrm{hyb}}
-analytic spaces. We will use the same notation and terminology in this more general setting. All the properties stated above generalize easily. We only record the last one here, for later reference.

Theorem 2.31
.

Let
X
be a flowing
k^{\mathrm{hyb}}
-analytic space. Let
U
and
V
be flow-connected flow-open subsets of
X
such that
U\subseteq V\subseteq T(U)
and
U
is open in
V
. For each coherent sheaf
\mathcal{F}
on
V
, the restriction map
\mathcal{F}(V)\to\mathcal{F}(U)
is an isomorphism.

Moreover, the restriction map induces an equivalence between the categories of coherent sheaves on
V
and
U
.
∎

Let us extend this result to other situations.

Definition 2.32
.

Let
X
be a flowing
k^{\mathrm{hyb}}
-analytic space. A subset
V
of
X
is said to be
over-flow-connected
if it admits a basis of open neighborhoods in

made of flow-connected flow-open subsets.

Remark 2.33
.

Over-flow-connected subsets are flow-connected.

Example 2.34
.

For each
x\in X
and each interval

,
\Phi(x,I\cap I_{x}^{\ast})
is over-flow-connected (see Example
2.20
).

Let us now exhibit another family of examples.

Definition 2.35
.

Let
B
be an affine variety over
k
. A compact subset
V
of
B^{\mathrm{hyb}}
is called a
rational domain
if there exist
f_{1},\dotsc,f_{m},g\in\mathcal{O}(B^{\mathrm{hyb}})
, with
(f_{1},\dotsc,f_{m},g)=(1)
, and real numbers
r_{1},\dotsc,r_{m}\in\mathbf{R}_{>0}
such that

V=\{x\in B^{\mathrm{hyb}}:\forall i=1,\dotsc,m,\ \lvert f_{i}(x)\rvert\leqslant r_{i}\,\lvert g(x)\rvert\}.

Lemma 2.36
.

Let
B
be an affine variety over
k
.

i)

A finite intersection of rational domains of
B^{\mathrm{hyb}}
is a rational domain.

ii)

Each rational domain of
B^{\mathrm{hyb}}
admits a basis of neighborhoods in
B^{\mathrm{hyb}}
made of flow-connected open subsets. In particular, each rational domain of
B^{\mathrm{hyb}}
is over-flow-connected.

Proof.

i) The arguments of the classical proof (see
[
BGR84
, Proposition 7.2.3/7]
) still applies here.

ii) Let
V
be a rational domain of
B^{\mathrm{hyb}}
. By definition, there exist
f_{1},\dotsc,f_{m},g\in\mathcal{O}(B^{\mathrm{hyb}})
, with
(f_{1},\dotsc,f_{n},g)=(1)
, and real numbers
r_{1},\dotsc,r_{m}\in\mathbf{R}_{>0}
such that

V=\{x\in B^{\mathrm{hyb}}:\forall i=1,\dotsc,m,\ \lvert f_{i}(x)\rvert\leqslant r_{i}\,\lvert g(x)\rvert\}.

Let us identify
B
to a Zariski-closed subset of an affine space
\mathbf{A}^{N}_{k}
, hence
B^{\mathrm{hyb}}
to a closed analytic subset of
\mathbf{A}^{N,\mathrm{hyb}}_{k}
. Since
V
is compact, there exists an open disc
D_{<}
containing
V
in
\mathbf{A}^{N,\mathrm{hyb}}_{k}
. Denote by
D_{\leqslant}
the closed disc with same center and radius as
D_{<}
. Note that
D_{\geqslant}\cap B^{\mathrm{hyb}}
is a rational domain of
B^{\mathrm{hyb}}
.

The function
g
does not vanish on
V
. Set

s:=\inf(\{\lvert g(y)\rvert:y\in V\}).

Since
V
is compact, this infimum is attained, hence
s>0
. Let
t\in(0,s)
. The sets

E_{\geqslant}:=\{x\in B^{\mathrm{hyb}}:\lvert g(x)\rvert\geqslant t\}\textrm{ and }E_{>}:=\{x\in B^{\mathrm{hyb}}:\lvert g(x)\rvert>t\}

contain
V
.

Let
U
be a neighborhood of
V
in
B^{\mathrm{hyb}}
. We need to prove that there exists a contains a flow-connected open subset
W
of
B^{\mathrm{hyb}}
such that
V\subseteq W\subseteq U
. We may assume that
U\subseteq D_{\leqslant}\cap E_{\geqslant}
. The function
g
does not vanish on
D_{\leqslant}\cap E_{\geqslant}
. Consider the map

F\colon y\in D_{\leqslant}\cap E_{\geqslant}\longmapsto\max_{1\leqslant i\leqslant m}\Big(\frac{\lvert f_{i}(y)\rvert}{r_{i}\,\lvert g(y)\rvert}\Big)\in\mathbf{R}_{\geqslant 0}.

Set

a:=\inf(\{\lvert F(z)\rvert:z\in(D_{\leqslant}\cap E_{\geqslant})\setminus U\}).

Since
(D_{\leqslant}\cap E_{\geqslant})\setminus U
is compact and non-empty, this infimum is attained, hence
a>1
. It follows that the set

\{y\in D_{\leqslant}\cap E_{\geqslant}:\forall i=1,\dotsc,m,\ \lvert f_{i}(y)\rvert<a\,r_{i}\,\lvert g(y)\rvert\}

is contained in
U
, hence the set

W:=\{y\in D_{<}\cap E_{>}:\forall i=1,\dotsc,m,\ \lvert f_{i}(y)\rvert<a\,r_{i}\,\lvert g(y)\rvert\}

satisfies all the conditions.

The last part of the statement follows from Example
2.20
, ii) and Lemma
2.21
, iii).
∎

-analytic space. Let

Analytification

We investigate the analytification functor in the hybrid setting.

Notation 2.39
.

Let
X
be a scheme locally of finite type over
k
.

We denote by

the analytification of

over

.

For

.

We denote by

the analytification of

over

. With Notation
2.3
, we have a canonical homeomorphism

. We denote by

the space

endowed with the overconvergent structure sheaf inherited from

. It is not isomorphic to the locally ringed space

in general.

For
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
, we denote by

the analytification of

over

. With Notation
2.3
, we have a canonical homeomorphism

. We endow

with the overconvergent structure sheaf inherited from

.

Lemma 2.40
.

Let
X
be a scheme locally of finite type over
k
. Let
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
. The natural morphism of locally ringed spaces
\iota\colon X^{\textup{an}}_{\varepsilon}\to X^{\mathrm{hyb}}_{\varepsilon}

Moreover, for each open subset

of

and each coherent sheaf

.

When

[

. The general case follows. The proof is very similar to that of Proposition

The second part of the statement follows by working locally and using a finite presentation of

.
∎

Lemma 2.41
.

Let
X
be a scheme locally of finite type over
k
. Let
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
. Each point
x
of
X^{\mathrm{hyb}}_{\varepsilon}
has a basis of neighborhoods
\mathcal{V}_{x}
in
X^{\mathrm{hyb}}_{\varepsilon}
such that each

satisfies the following properties:

i)

V
is compact and over-flow-connected;

ii)

T(V)
may be written as an increasing union of compact over-flow-connected subsets containing
V
.

Proof.

Let
x\in X^{\mathrm{hyb}}_{\varepsilon}
. The question being local, we may assume that
X
is affine. Let us consider a closed embedding
j\colon X\to\mathbf{A}^{N}_{k}
and fix coordinates
T=(T_{1},\dotsc,T_{N})
on
\mathbf{A}^{N}_{k}
.

By definition of the topology,
j^{\mathrm{hyb}}(x)
admits a basis of neighborhoods in
(\mathbf{A}^{N,\mathrm{hyb}}_{k})_{\varepsilon}
of the form

W=\bigcap_{i=1}^{p}\big\{y\in(\mathbf{A}^{N,\mathrm{hyb}}_{k})_{\varepsilon}:\lvert P_{i}(y)\rvert\leqslant s_{i}\big\}\cap\bigcap_{i=p+1}^{q}\big\{y\in(\mathbf{A}^{N,\mathrm{hyb}}_{k})_{\varepsilon}:r_{i}\leqslant\lvert P_{i}(y)\rvert\leqslant s_{i}\big\},

with
P_{1},\dotsc,P_{q}\in k[T]
,
r_{p+1},\dotsc,r_{q},s_{1},\dotsc,s_{q}\in\mathbf{R}_{>0}
,
\lvert P_{i}(j^{\mathrm{hyb}}(x))\rvert<s_{i}
for each

and
r_{i}<\lvert P_{i}(j^{\mathrm{hyb}}(x))\rvert<s_{i}
for each

. We may assume that the coordinate functions
T_{1},\dotsc,T_{N}
belong to the family
(P_{i})_{1\leqslant i\leqslant q}
. Let us now prove that the set

satisfies properties i) and ii) of the statement.

i) Since the family
(P_{i})_{1\leqslant i\leqslant q}
contains the coordinate functions
T_{1},\dotsc,T_{N}
,
W
is compact, and so is

. By Lemma
2.36
,

and

are over-flow-connected too.

ii) Since

is not trivially valued, there exists
\alpha\in k
such that

. Up to multiplying the

’s by some power of
\alpha
, and the corresponding

’s (when they exist) and

’s by the same power of
\lvert\alpha\rvert
, we may assume that
r_{p+1},\dotsc,r_{p},s_{1},\dotsc,s_{q}\in(0,1)

(resp.

), there exists

(resp.

) such that

(resp.

), so that we have

W=\bigcap_{i=1}^{p}\big\{y\in(\mathbf{A}^{N,\mathrm{hyb}}_{k})_{\varepsilon}:\lvert P_{i}(y)\rvert\leqslant\lvert\alpha(y)\rvert^{v_{i}}\big\}\cap\bigcap_{i=p+1}^{q}\big\{y\in(\mathbf{A}^{N,\mathrm{hyb}}_{k})_{\varepsilon}:\lvert\alpha(y)\rvert^{u_{i}}\leqslant\lvert P_{i}(y)\rvert\leqslant\lvert\alpha(y)\rvert^{v_{i}}\big\}.

We have

T(W)=\bigcap_{i=1}^{p}\big\{y\in(\mathbf{A}^{N,\mathrm{hyb}}_{k})_{>0}:\lvert P_{i}(y)\rvert\leqslant\lvert\alpha(y)\rvert^{v_{i}}\big\}\cap\bigcap_{i=p+1}^{q}\big\{y\in(\mathbf{A}^{N,\mathrm{hyb}}_{k})_{>0}:\lvert\alpha(y)\rvert^{u_{i}}\leqslant\lvert P_{i}(y)\rvert\leqslant\lvert\alpha(y)\rvert^{v_{i}}\big\}.

For

, set

T_{n}(W):=T(W)\cap\pr^{-1}([2^{-n},1])=\{y\in T(W):\lvert\alpha(y)\rvert\geqslant\lvert\alpha\rvert^{2^{-n}}\}.

By Lemma

, it is a compact over-flow-connected subset of
\mathbf{A}^{N,\mathrm{hyb}}_{k}
. Let

such that

contains

.

It is now easy to check that the properties of the statement are satisfied by the sequence
(V_{n}:=j^{-1}(W_{n}))_{n\geqslant n_{0}}
.
∎

.

Let

.

of

, see
[
Ber90
, Theorem 3.5.1]
. We follow his proof.

Theorem 2.43
.

Let
X
be a scheme locally of finite type over
k
.

The functor
\mathcal{F}\mapsto\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{hyb}}
realises an equivalence between the categories of coherent sheaves on
X
and
X^{\mathrm{hyb}}
.

Moreover, for each coherent sheaf
\mathcal{F}
on
X
and each
q\in\mathbf{N}
, we have a canonical isomorphism

H^{q}(X,\mathcal{F})\xrightarrow{\sim}H^{q}(X^{\mathrm{hyb}},\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{hyb}}).

The same results holds replacing
X^{\mathrm{hyb}}
by
X_{0^{\dagger}}^{\mathrm{hyb}}
or
X_{[0,\varepsilon]}^{\textup{an}}
for
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
.

Proof.

Let us begin with the first part of the statement. We may assume that
X
is affine, say
X=\Spec(A)
, with
A=k[T_{1},\dotsc,T_{n}]/I
for a finitely generated ideal
I
of
k[T_{1},\dotsc,T_{n}]
.

By
[
LP24
, Lemme 6.5.6]
, the functor
\mathcal{F}\mapsto\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{hyb}}
sends coherent sheaves to coherent sheaves. Moreover, by
[
LP24
, Théorème 6.6.5]
, the canonical morphism
X^{\mathrm{hyb}}\to X
is flat, hence the preceding functor is faithful.

It follows from
[
Poi13
, Corollaire 2.8]
that we have a canonical identification
k[T_{1},\dotsc,T_{n}]=\mathcal{O}(\mathbf{A}^{n,\mathrm{hyb}}_{k})
. By
[
LP24
, Corollaire 8.5.15]
(applied with infinite radii), the space
\mathcal{O}(\mathbf{A}^{n,\mathrm{hyb}}_{k})
has no higher coherent cohomology. By a classical argument (see the proof of
[
LP24
, Théorème 8.3.8]
, for instance), we deduce that
A=\mathcal{O}(X^{\mathrm{hyb}})
. Let
\mathcal{F}
be a coherent sheaf on
X
. Writing it as a cokernel of free sheaves of finite rank and using right-exactness of the global section functor, we deduce that
\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{hyb}}(X^{\mathrm{hyb}})=\mathcal{F}(X)
. Applying this result to sheaves of homomorphisms, it follows that the functor
\mathcal{F}\mapsto\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{hyb}}
is full.

It remains to prove essential surjectivity. Let
r\in\mathbf{R}_{\geqslant 1}
and consider the relative closed disc

D_{\mathrm{hyb}}(r):=\{x\in\mathbf{A}^{n,\mathrm{hyb}}_{k}:\forall i=1,\dotsc,n,\ \lvert T_{i}(x)\rvert\leqslant r\}.

Denote by
X^{\mathrm{hyb}}(r)
its closed analytic subset defined by the ideal
I
. It identifies to a subset of
X^{\mathrm{hyb}}
, and the family
(X^{\mathrm{hyb}}(r))_{r\geqslant 1}
exhausts
X^{\mathrm{hyb}}
. By the same argument as above (using
[
LP24
, Corollaire 8.2.16]
instead of
[
LP24
, Corollaire 8.5.15]
), we have
A=\mathcal{O}(X^{\mathrm{hyb}}(r))
. Moreover, by
[
LP24
, Corollaire 8.3.10]
, we have an equivalence between the category of coherent sheaves over
X^{\mathrm{hyb}}(r)
and the category of finitely generated modules over
\mathcal{O}(X^{\mathrm{hyb}}(r))=A
. The result follows.

Let us now prove the second part of the statement. It concerns an arbitrary scheme
X
that is locally of finite type over
k
. Let
\mathcal{F}
be a coherent sheaf on
X
.

Step 1:

X
is affine

During the proof of the first part of the statement, we have seen that
\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{hyb}}(X^{\mathrm{hyb}})=\mathcal{F}(X)
. Moreover, by
[
LP24
, Corollaire 8.5.24]
, for each
q\geqslant 1
, we have

H^{q}(X^{\mathrm{hyb}},\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{hyb}})=H^{q}(X,\mathcal{F})=0.

Step 2:

X
is separated

Cover
X
by a family of open affine subschemes
(U_{i})_{i\in I}
. Since
X
is separated, the multiple intersections of the
U_{i}
’s are affine too. The result now follows from the result in the affine case, together with the Čech-to-cohomology spectral sequence.

Step 3:

X
is arbitrary

Cover
X
by a family of open affine subschemes
(U_{i})_{i\in I}
. The
U_{i}
’s and their multiple intersections are separated, hence the result of Step 2 applies to them. The result for
X
follows again from the Čech-to-cohomology spectral sequence.

The results for
X_{0^{\dagger}}^{\mathrm{hyb}}
or
X_{[0,\varepsilon]}^{\textup{an}}
are proven using the same arguments.
∎

3.
Varieties and analytic spaces over trivially valued fields

In the recent literature, several examples occur where Berkovich analytic spaces over trivially valued fields are used to understand the behaviour at the boundary of algebraic varieties.

3.1.
Generic fibers

An interesting feature of non-Archimedean analytic spaces is that they can be used to define a notion of generic fiber for formal schemes (with suitable finiteness assumptions) over valuation rings. This idea was first developed by M. Raynaud (see
[
Ray74
]
), for formal schemes locally topologically of finite presentation, and later extended by P. Berthelot to a wider class (see
[
Ber96b
]
). We point the reader to
[
Ber94
]
and
[
Ber96a
]
for the corresponding constructions in the setting of Berkovich spaces.

In this text, we only need generic fibers of formal schemes of a rather specific sort, and we only recall the construction in this setting. We follow the presentation given by A. Thuillier in
[
Thu07
]
and borrow his notation.
2
2

2

In particular, we denote the usual generic fiber of a formal scheme
\mathfrak{X}
by
\mathfrak{X}^{\beth}
instead of the customary
\mathfrak{X}_{\eta}
, which is reserved for a smaller space.

Remark that the valued field
k_{0}
is a valuation ring. We consider formal schemes of over
k_{0}
that are
locally algebraic
,
i.e.
locally isomorphic to the completion of a variety along a closed subscheme. Note that they are special formal schemes in the sense of
[
Ber96a
]
. To a locally algebraic formal scheme
\mathfrak{X}
, we associate a generic fiber
\mathfrak{X}^{\beth}
(which is an analytic space over
k_{0}
) and a map
r_{\mathfrak{X}}\colon\mathfrak{X}^{\beth}\to\mathfrak{X}_{s}
which is anticontinuous (the preimage of an open subset is closed). The construction involves three steps.

Step 1:

\mathfrak{X}=X
is an affine variety

Write
X=\Spec(A)
. Set

X^{\beth}:=\mathcal{M}(A),

where
A
is endowed with the trivial norm. The map
r_{X}\colon\mathcal{M}(A)\to\Spec(A)
is the reduction map defined as follows: for
x\in\mathcal{M}(A)
,

r_{X}(x):=\{a\in A:\lvert a(x)\rvert<1\}.

Step 2:

\mathfrak{X}
is the completion of an affine variety
X
along a closed subscheme
Y

Write
X=\Spec(A)
and
Y=V(I)
, where
I
is an ideal of
A
. Set

\mathfrak{X}^{\beth}:=r_{X}^{-1}(Y)=\{x\in X^{\beth}:\forall b\in I,\lvert b(x)\rvert<1\}.

It is an open subset of
X^{\beth}
, from which it inherits an analytic structure. The map
r_{\mathfrak{X}}\colon r_{X}^{-1}(Y)\to Y
is that induced by
r_{X}
.

Step 3:

\mathfrak{X}
is locally algebraic

In this case,
\mathfrak{X}^{\beth}
and
r_{\mathfrak{X}}
are obtained from the construction described in Step 2 by a gluing process.

Let us mention that A. Thuillier gives a more precise presentation: he actually states a functorial definition of
\mathfrak{X}^{\beth}
, where
r_{\mathfrak{X}}
is enhanced to a morphism
\mathfrak{X}^{\beth}\to\mathfrak{X}
. We refer to
[
Thu07
, Proposition et Définition 1.3]
for details.

We may now state A. Thuillier’s refined version of the generic fiber.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{X}
be a locally algebraic formal scheme
\mathfrak{X}
over
k_{0}
. Its
generic fiber in the sense of A. Thuillier
is the analytic space over
k_{0}
defined by

\mathfrak{X}_{\eta}:=\mathfrak{X}^{\beth}-\mathfrak{X}_{s}^{\beth}.

Example 3.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{X}
be the the completion of a variety
X=\Spec(A)
along a closed subscheme
Y=V(I)
. Then, we have

\mathfrak{X}_{\eta}=\{x\in\mathcal{M}(A):\forall b\in I,\lvert b(x)\rvert<1\}-\{x\in\mathcal{M}(A):\forall b\in I,b(x)=0\}.

In particular, if
I=(b_{1},\dotsc,b_{r})
, then

\mathfrak{X}_{\eta}=\{x\in\mathcal{M}(A):0<\max_{1\leqslant i\leqslant r}(\lvert b_{i}(x)\rvert)<1\}.

3.2.
Generic fibers and analytifications

Let
X
be a variety over
k
. We have just seen a way to obtain an analytic space over
k_{0}
from
X
, by passing to the associated formal scheme and taking the generic fiber
X^{\beth}
. Another possibility is to consider the analytification
X^{\textup{an}}_{0}
of
X
over
k_{0}
. It is related to the former. Indeed, by
[
Thu07
, Proposition 1.10]
,
X^{\beth}
may be identified to a compact analytic domain of
X^{\textup{an}}_{0}
.

To be more precise, let us extend to multiplicative seminorms the classical notion of center of a valuation. Recall that there exists a canonical morphism
\rho\colon X_{0}^{\textup{an}}\to X
(see Section
1.3
). Let
x\in X^{\textup{an}}_{0}
. By Remark
1.2
, the point
x
may be described by the data of the point
\rho(x)\in X
and an absolute value
\lvert{\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu}\rvert_{x}
on the residue field
\kappa(\rho(x))
. We will, somewhat abusively, denote by
\kappa(\rho(x))^{\circ}
the subring of elements of
\kappa(\rho(x))
that are bounded by 1 for
\lvert{\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu}\rvert_{x}
.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
x\in X_{0}^{\textup{an}}
. We say that the seminorm associated to
x
(or the point
x
)
has a center on
X
if there exists a morphism
\lambda^{\circ}_{x}\colon\Spec(\kappa(\rho(x)))^{\circ}\to X
that makes the following diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Spec(\kappa(\rho(x)))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Spec(\kappa(\rho(x))^{\circ})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lambda_{x}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lambda_{x}^{\circ}}

commutative.

Since
X
is separated, as soon as it exists, the morphism
\lambda^{\circ}_{x}
is unique. In this case, the image of the closed point of
\Spec(\kappa(\rho(x))^{\circ})
is called the
center
of the seminorm associated to
x
(or the point
x
).

For each subset
U
of
X
, we say that the seminorm associated to
x
(or the point
x
)
has a center on
U
if it has a center on
X
, and its center belongs to
U
.

The following result is a direct consequence of the definitions.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
x\in X_{0}^{\textup{an}}
.

i)

The point
x
belongs to
X^{\beth}
if, and only if, it has a center on
X
.

ii)

For each subset
U
of
X
, the point
x
belongs to
r_{X}^{-1}(U)
if, and only if, it has a center on
U
.

∎

It follows from the valuative criterion of properness that
X^{\beth}=X^{\textup{an}}_{0}
if, and only if,
X
is proper (see
[
Thu07
, Proposition 1.10]
).

Following V. Berkovich’s suggestion in his correspondence with V. Drinfeld (see
[
Ber04
]
or
[
BBT13
, Section 3.3.2]
), we introduce a notation for the set of points of

whose associated seminorm has no center on
X
.

Notation 3.5
.

Set

X_{\infty}:=X^{\textup{an}}_{0}-X^{\beth}.

It is an open subset of
X^{\textup{an}}_{0}
.

Example 3.6
.

Consider
\mathbf{A}^{1}_{k}
with coordinate
T
. For
r\in\mathbf{R}_{>0}
, denote by
\eta_{r}
the point of
\mathbf{A}^{1,\mathrm{an}}_{k_{0}}
associated to the absolute value

P(T)=\sum_{\geqslant 0}a_{i}T^{i}\longmapsto\max_{i\geqslant 0}(\lvert a_{i}\rvert_{0}r^{i}).

Note that, for each
P\in k[T]-\{0\}
, we have
\lvert P(\eta_{r})\rvert=r^{-v_{T}(P)}
if
r\in(0,1)
, and
\lvert P(\eta_{r})\rvert=r^{\deg(P)}
if
r>1
.

We have

(\mathbf{A}^{1}_{k})_{\infty}=\{x\in\mathbf{A}^{1,\mathrm{an}}_{k_{0}}:\lvert T(x)\rvert>1\}=\{\eta_{r}:r\in\mathbf{R}_{>1}\}

and

(\mathbf{G}_{m,k})_{\infty}=\{x\in\mathbf{A}^{1,\mathrm{an}}_{k_{0}}:0<\lvert T(x)\rvert<1\}\cup\{x\in\mathbf{A}^{1,\mathrm{an}}_{k_{0}}:\lvert T(x)\rvert>1\}=\{\eta_{r}:r\in\mathbf{R}_{>0}-\{1\}\}.

Those spaces have a finite number of connected components (1 and 2 respectively), which correspond to the missing points in a smooth compactification. This result easily generalizes to arbitrary curves.

Example 3.7
.

Consider
\mathbf{A}^{2}_{k}
with coordinates
T_{1},T_{2}
. We have

(\mathbf{A}^{2}_{k})_{\infty}=\{x\in\mathbf{A}^{2,\mathrm{an}}_{k_{0}}:\max(\lvert T_{1}(x),\lvert T_{2}(x)\rvert\rvert)>1\}.

The construction
{\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu}_{\infty}
behaves well with respect to proper morphisms.

.

Let
f\colon X_{1}\to X_{2}
be a proper morphism between varieties over

. Then
f_{0}^{\textup{an}}
is proper and we have
(f^{\textup{an}}_{0})^{-1}(X_{2}^{\beth})=X_{1}^{\beth}
.

In particular,
f^{\textup{an}}_{0}
restricts to a proper morphism

The morphism

is proper by

, Proposition 3.4.7]
.

Let

. The morphism

induces an isometry

, and we have a commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X_{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Spec(\kappa(\rho(y)))}

.

To conclude, let us state the remarkable fact

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
Y
be a compactification of

,
i.e.
a proper variety in which

embeds as an open subset. Set

and let
\mathfrak{Y}
be the completion of

along

. Then, we have a natural identification

.

Proof.

Let

of

4.
Berkovich compactifications

In this section, we define and study compactifications of analytifications of varieties over

Notation 4.1
.

For a variety

For a variety

over
k
, the spaces

, with

, are related by the flow, and all isomorphic to each other. As a result, it looks as if the space

Definition 4.7
.

Let
X
be a variety over
k
. Let
x,y\in X^{\mathrm{hyb}}
. We say that
x
and
y
are
flow-equivalent
if
T(x)=T(y)
. In this case, we write
x\mathbin{\Phi}y

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
X
be a variety over
k
. The relation
\mathbin{\Phi}
on
X^{\mathrm{hyb}}
is an equivalence relation that preserves
X^{+}
.

Proof.

It follows from the definitions that
\mathbin{\Phi}
is an equivalence relation on

(see Lemma

).

For each open affine subset

. It follows that
X^{\beth}
, and then

\mathbin{\Phi}
too.
∎

Definition 4.9
.

Let
X
be a variety over
k
. The set

is called the

of

. We denote by

the quotient map.

The subset

of

Set

4.1.
Topological properties

Let
X
be a variety over
k
. We endow
X^{\!\urcorner}:=X^{+}/\mathbin{\Phi}
with the quotient topology, and investigate its topological properties.

Lemma 4.15
.

The maps
q\colon X^{+}\to X^{\!\urcorner}
and
\iota\colon X^{\textup{an}}\to X^{\!\urcorner}
are open.

In particular,
\iota
induces a homeomorphism from
X^{\textup{an}}
onto its image (which is open in
X^{\!\urcorner}
).

Proof.

Let
U
be an open subset of
X^{+}
. Its image in
X^{\!\urcorner}=X^{+}/\mathbin{\Phi}
is open if, and only if,
T(U)
is open in
X^{+}
. The latter property follows from Proposition
2.10
, hence
q
is open.

In order to prove that
\iota
is open, it is enough to show that each
x\in X^{\textup{an}}
admits a basis of open neighborhoods
\mathcal{V}_{x}
with the following property: for each
V\in\mathcal{V}_{x}
, there exists an open subset
V^{\prime}
of
X^{+}
such that
V^{\prime}=V\mod\mathbin{\Phi}
. Indeed, in this case, we have
\iota(V)=q(V^{\prime})
, which is open, by the first part.

Let
x\in X^{\textup{an}}
. We may assume that
X
is affine. Let us consider a closed embedding
j\colon X\to\mathbf{A}^{n}_{k}
and fix coordinates
T=(T_{1},\dotsc,T_{n})
on
\mathbf{A}^{n}_{k}
.

By definition of the topology,
j(x)
admits a basis of neighborhoods in
\mathbf{A}^{n,\mathrm{an}}_{\hat{k}}
of the form

W=\bigcap_{i=1}^{p}\big\{y\in\mathbf{A}^{n,\mathrm{an}}_{\hat{k}}:r_{i}<\lvert P_{i}(y)\rvert<s_{i}\big\},

with
P_{1},\dotsc,P_{p}\in k[T]
,
r_{1},\dotsc,r_{p},s_{1},\dotsc,s_{p}\in\mathbf{R}
, and
r_{i}<\lvert P_{i}(x)\rvert<s_{i}
for each
i\in\{1,\dotsc,p\}
.

Since
k
is not trivially valued, there exists
\alpha\in k
such that
0<\lvert\alpha\rvert<1
. Up to multiplying the
P_{i}
’s by some power of
\alpha
, and the corresponding
r_{i}
and
s_{i}
by the same power of
\lvert\alpha\rvert
, we may assume that
r_{1},\dotsc,r_{p},s_{1},\dotsc,s_{p}\in(-1,1)
. In this case, for each
i\in\{1,\dotsc,p\}
, there exists
\sigma_{i}\in\{-1,1\}
,
\beta_{i},\gamma_{i}\in\{0,\alpha\}
and
u_{i},v_{i}\in\mathbf{R}_{>0}
such that
r_{i}=\sigma_{i}\lvert\beta_{i}\rvert^{u_{i}}
and
s_{i}=\lvert\gamma_{i}\rvert^{v_{i}}
, so that we have

W=\bigcap_{i=1}^{p}\big\{y\in\mathbf{A}^{n,\mathrm{an}}_{\hat{k}}:\sigma_{i}\lvert\beta_{i}\rvert^{u_{i}}<\lvert P_{i}(y)\rvert<\lvert\gamma_{i}\rvert^{v_{i}}\big\}.

Set

W^{\prime}:=\bigcap_{i=1}^{p}\big\{y\in\mathbf{A}^{n,\mathrm{hyb}}_{\hat{k},>0}:\sigma_{i}\lvert\beta_{i}\rvert^{u_{i}}<\lvert P_{i}(y)\rvert<\lvert\gamma_{i}\rvert^{v_{i}}\big\}.

It is an open subset of
\mathbf{A}^{n,\mathrm{hyb}}_{\hat{k},>0}
and we have
W^{\prime}=W\mod\mathbin{\Phi}
.

We obtain the desired result by pulling back by
j
.

The last part of the statement now follows from Lemma
4.11
.
∎

Proposition 4.16
.

The space
X^{\!\urcorner}
is Hausdorff.

Proof.

We want to prove that
X^{\!\urcorner}
is Hausdorff, which amounts to proving that the graph
\Gamma_{\mathbin{\Phi},X^{+}}
of
\mathbin{\Phi}
is closed in
X^{+}\times X^{+}
.

By Chow’s lemma, we may find a quasi-projective variety
Y
and a proper surjective morphism
f\colon Y\to X
. By
[
LP24
, Proposition 6.5.3]
, the induced morphism
f^{\mathrm{hyb}}\colon Y^{\mathrm{hyb}}\to X^{\mathrm{hyb}}
is proper and surjective too, hence so is
f^{+}\colon Y^{+}\to X^{+}
, by Proposition
3.8
. The induced morphism
f^{+}\times f^{+}\colon Y^{+}\times Y^{+}\to X^{+}\times X^{+}
between the Cartesian products is still surjective and proper (as a topological morphism), hence closed. It follows from the definitions that
\Gamma_{\mathbin{\Phi},X^{+}}=(f^{+}\times f^{+})(\Gamma_{\mathbin{\Phi},Y^{+}})
. In particular, if
\Gamma_{\mathbin{\Phi},Y^{+}}
is closed in
Y^{+}\times Y^{+}
, then
\Gamma_{\mathbin{\Phi},X^{+}}
is closed in
X^{+}\times X^{+}
.

It remains to show that
\Gamma_{\mathbin{\Phi},Y^{+}}
is closed in
Y^{+}\times Y^{+}
. Let
(x,y)\in(Y^{+}\times Y^{+})\setminus\Gamma_{\Phi}
. Since
Y
is quasi-projective, there exists an open affine subset
U=\Spec(A)
of
Y
such that
x
and
y
belong to
U^{+}
. Recall that, for each
k_{\mathrm{hyb}}
-analytic space
\mathcal{X}
, we have a projection map
\pr\colon\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{M}(k_{\mathrm{hyb}})=[0,1]
(see Section
2.1
).

\bullet
Assume that
\pr(x)>0
and
\pr(y)>0
.

We have
x^{1/\pr(x)}\neq y^{1/\pr(y)}
in
U^{\textup{an}}
. Up to switching
x
and
y
, we may assume that there exists
a\in A
such that
\lvert a(x^{1/\pr(x)})\rvert<\lvert a(y^{1/\pr(y)})\rvert
. Since
k
is not trivially valued, up to replacing
a
by an element of the form
\lambda a^{n}
, with
\lambda\in k^{\ast}
and
n\in\mathbf{N}^{\ast}
, we may assume that
\lvert a(x^{1/\pr(x)})\rvert<1
and
\lvert a(y^{1/\pr(y)})\rvert>1
. Then the set

W:=\{(u,v)\in U^{+}\times U^{+}\colon\lvert a(u)\rvert<1,\lvert a(v)\rvert>1\}

is a neighborhood of
(x,y)
in
U^{+}\times U^{+}
that does not meet
\Gamma_{\mathbin{\Phi}}
.

\bullet
Assume that
\pr(x)=0
and
\pr(y)>0
, or that
\pr(x)>0
and
\pr(y)=0
.

Up to switching
x
and
y
, we may assume that
\pr(x)=0
and
\pr(y)>0
. Let
\alpha_{1}<\alpha_{2}\in(0,\pr(y))
. Then the set

W_{-}:=\{(u,v)\in U^{+}\times U^{+}\colon\pr(u)<\alpha_{1},\pr(v)>\alpha_{2}\}

is a neighborhood of
(x,y)
in
U^{+}\times U^{+}
that contains no points of the form
(z,z^{\beta})
with
z\in U^{+}
and
\beta\in I^{\ast}_{z}\cap(0,\alpha_{2}/\alpha_{1}]
.

Since
x\notin U^{\beth}
, there exists
a\in A
such that
|a(x)|>1
. Let
r\in(1,|a(x)|)
and
s\in(|a(y)|,+\infty)
. Then the set

W_{+}:=\{(u,v)\in U^{+}\times U^{+}\colon\lvert a(u)\rvert>r,\lvert a(v)\rvert<s\}

is a neighborhood of
(x,y)
in
U^{+}\times U^{+}
that contains no points of the form
(z,z^{\beta})
with
z\in U^{+}
and
\beta\in I^{\ast}_{z}\cap[\log(s)/\log(r),+\infty)
.

If
\log(s)/\log(r)\leqslant\alpha_{2}/\alpha_{1}
, then
W_{-}\cap W_{+}
is a neighborhood of
(x,y)
in
U^{+}\times U^{+}
that does not meet
\Gamma_{\mathbin{\Phi}}
. Otherwise, for each
\alpha\in[\alpha_{2}/\alpha_{1},\log(s)/\log(r)]
, by assumption, there exists
a\in A
such that
\lvert a(y)\rvert\neq\lvert a(x)\rvert^{\alpha}
. It follows that there exist a neighborhood
W_{\alpha}
of
(x,y)
in
U^{+}\times U^{+}
and a neighborhood
J_{\alpha}
of
\alpha
in
\mathbf{R}_{>0}
such that, for each
(u,v)\in W_{\alpha}
and
\beta\in J_{\alpha}
, we have
\lvert a(v)\rvert\neq\lvert a(u)\rvert^{\beta}
. A compactness argument shows that there exists a finite subset
S
of
[\alpha_{2}/\alpha_{1},\log(s)/\log(r)]
such that
W_{-}\cap W_{+}\cap\bigcap_{\alpha\in S}W_{\alpha}
is a neighborhood of
(x,y)
in
U^{+}\times U^{+}
that does not meet
\Gamma_{\mathbin{\Phi}}
.

\bullet
Assume that
\pr(x)=\pr(y)=0
.

Since
x\notin U^{\beth}
, as before, there exist
\alpha_{+}\in\mathbf{R}_{>0}
and a neighborhood
W_{+}
of
(x,y)
in
U^{+}\times U^{+}
that contains no points of the form
(z,z^{\beta})
with
z\in U^{+}
and
\beta\in I^{\ast}_{z}\cap[\alpha_{+},+\infty)
.

Since
y\notin U^{\beth}
, similarly, we prove that there exist
\alpha_{-}\in\mathbf{R}_{>0}
and a neighborhood
W_{-}
of
(x,y)
in
U^{+}\times U^{+}
that contains no points of the form
(z,z^{\beta})
with
z\in U^{+}
and
\beta\in I^{\ast}_{z}\cap(0,\alpha_{-}]
.

The result then follows from a compactness argument as in the previous case.
∎

Corollary 4.17
.

The space
X^{\!\urcorner}
is locally compact.

Proof.

The space
X^{\mathrm{hyb}}
is locally compact, hence so is
X^{+}
. Since
q
is open and
X^{\!\urcorner}
is Hausdorff, we deduce that
X^{\!\urcorner}
is locally quasi-compact.
∎

Lemma 4.18
.

The space
X^{\partial}
is compact.

Proof.

Let
X
be a variety over
k
. Let
Y
be a compactification of
X
and set
Z:=Y-X
. Up to blowing up
Z
in
X
, we may assume that
Z
is a Cartier divisor. Let
\mathcal{I}
be the sheaf of ideals defining
Z
in
Y
. Let
(U_{i})_{i\in I}
be a finite cover of
Y
by affine open subsets such that, for each
i\in I
,
\mathcal{I}_{|U_{i}\cap Z}
is generated by a single element

.

Fix
r\in(0,1)
. Let
i\in I
. Denoting by
\mathfrak{U}_{i}
the completion of
U_{i}
along
U_{i}\cap Z
, we have

\mathfrak{U}_{i,\eta}=\{x\in U_{i}^{\beth}\colon 0<\lvert h_{i}(x)\rvert<1\}.

The quotient
\mathfrak{U}_{i,\eta}/\mathbin{\Phi}
is compact, since it is the image of the compact set
\{x\in U_{i}^{\beth}\colon|h_{i}(x)|=r\}
.

By Proposition

, we have

, hence
X^{\partial}=\mathfrak{Y}_{\eta}/\mathbin{\Phi}
. The result follows, since
\mathfrak{Y}_{\eta}
is covered by the
\mathfrak{U}_{i,\eta}
’s, and
X^{\partial}
is Hausdorff, by Proposition
4.16
.
∎

Theorem 4.19
.

The space
X^{\!\urcorner}
is compact.

Proof.

Let
X
be a variety over
k
. As in the previous proof, let
Y
be a compactification of
X
such that
Z:=Y-X
is a Cartier divisor. Let
\mathcal{I}
be the sheaf of ideals defining
Z
in
Y
. Fix
r\in(0,1)
.

Recall that
X^{\!\urcorner}=X^{\partial}\cup X^{\textup{an}}
, where
X^{\textup{an}}
is identified to its image by
\iota
. By Proposition
4.16
,
X^{\!\urcorner}
is Hausdorff and, by Lemma
4.18
,
X^{\partial}
is compact, so it is enough to prove that
X^{\textup{an}}
is contained in a compact subset of
X^{\!\urcorner}
.

Let
x\in Z_{1}^{\textup{an}}
. Choose an affine open subset
U=\Spec(A)
of
Y
such that
U_{1}^{\textup{an}}
contains
x
. We may assume that
A
is a
k
-algebra of finite type, say
A=k[t_{1},\dotsc,t_{n}]/J
. Up to shrinking
U
, we may assume that
\mathcal{I}_{Z\cap U}
is generated by a single element
h\in A
. Since
k
is not trivially valued, up to rescaling the

’s, we may assume that, for each

, we have
\lvert t_{i}(x)\rvert<1
. Set

V_{x}:=\{y\in U_{1}^{\textup{an}}\colon\forall i=1,\dotsc,n,\ \lvert t_{i}(y)\rvert<1,\lvert h(y)\rvert<r\}.

It is an open neighborhood of

in

.

Consider the set

K_{x}:=\{z\in U^{\mathrm{hyb}}\colon\forall i=1,\dotsc,n,\ \lvert t_{i}(z)\rvert\leqslant 1,\ \lvert h(z)\rvert=r\}.

It is a compact subset of

, and it is contained in

, by Proposition

.

For each

, we have

. It follows that

.

Since

is compact, there exists a finite subset

of

such that the open subset

of

. In particular,

. We conclude by writing

X^{\textup{an}}\simeq q(X_{1}^{\textup{an}})=q(W)\cup q(V\cap X_{1}^{\textup{an}})\subseteq q(W)\cup\bigcup_{x\in F}q(K_{x}).

∎

Lemma 4.20
.

Assume that
k
is countable. Then the spaces
X^{\mathrm{hyb}}
,
X^{+}
and
X^{\!\urcorner}
are metrizable.

Proof.

The spaces
X^{\mathrm{hyb}}
,
X^{+}
and
X^{\!\urcorner}
are Hausdorff and locally compact. By Urysohn’s metrization theorem, it suffices to show that they are second-countable. Since
X^{+}
is open in

and
X^{\!\urcorner}
is a quotient of
X^{+}
, we only need to prove that

is second-countable.

Since

is quasi-compact, we may reduce to the case where

is affine, and then to the case where

, for some

. Let us fix coordinates

on

. Then, the topology of
\mathbf{A}^{N,\mathrm{hyb}}_{k}
is generated by the countably many open sets of the form

\{x\in\mathbf{A}^{N,\mathrm{hyb}}_{k}:r<\lvert P(x)\rvert<s\},

for
P\in k[T_{1},\dotsc,T_{n}]
,

. The result follows.
∎

Remark 4.21
.

If

is not countable, then

is not metrizable. Indeed, in this case, the Gauss point of

Proposition 4.22
.

The space

. In particular,

is dense in

.

, there would exist a non-empty open subset

that is contained in

. By

, the projection map

is open, which yields a contradiction. This proves the first part of the statement. The second part follows by applying

.
∎

Proposition 4.23
.

The space
X^{\!\urcorner}
is locally path-connected.

If

is connected, then
X^{\!\urcorner}
is path-connected.

Proof.

By
[

, Théorème 7.2.17]
,

is locally path-connected. By Lemma

∎

4.2.
Sheaf-theoretic properties

Let
X
be a variety over
k
. We endow

with the sheaf
\cal{O}_{X^{\!\urcorner}}:=q_{\ast}\cal{O}_{X^{+}}
.

Remark 4.24
.

Let
U
be an open subset of
X^{\!\urcorner}
and let
x\in U
. Since we have quotiented by the flow, for

, the absolute value of

at

is no longer well-defined.

However, properties such as
f(x)=0
,

,
\lvert f(x)\rvert<1
,
\lvert f(x)\rvert=1
,
\lvert f(x)\rvert\leqslant\lvert g(x)\rvert
for

, …still make sense, since they are invariant under raising to a non-zero power. We will allow us to use them in the sequel.

Lemma 4.25
.

Let
x\in X^{\!\urcorner}
. The ring
\mathcal{O}_{X^{\!\urcorner},x}
is a local ring with maximal ideal

\mathfrak{m}_{x}=\{f\in\mathcal{O}_{X^{\!\urcorner},x}:f(x)=0\}.

In particular,

is a locally ringed space.
∎

Proposition 4.26
.

Let
f\colon X_{1}\to X_{2}
be a proper morphism of varieties over
k
. Then the morphism
f^{+}\colon X_{1}^{+}\to X_{2}^{+}
from Proposition
4.2
induces a morphism of locally ringed spaces
f^{\!\urcorner}\colon X_{1}^{\!\urcorner}\to X_{2}^{\!\urcorner}
.

In particular, the mappings
X\mapsto X^{\!\urcorner}
,
f\mapsto f^{\!\urcorner}
define a functor from the category of varieties over
k
with proper morphisms to that of locally ringed spaces.
∎

Proposition 4.27
.

The map
\iota\colon X^{\textup{an}}\to X^{\!\urcorner}
is an open immersion.

Proof.

By Lemmas
4.11
and
4.15
,
\iota
induces a homeomorphism from
X^{\textup{an}}
onto
q(X^{\mathrm{hyb}}_{>0})
.

It remains to prove that, for each open subset
U
of
q(X^{\mathrm{hyb}}_{>0})
, the natural morphism
\mathcal{O}_{X^{\!\urcorner}}(U)\to\mathcal{O}_{X^{\textup{an}}}(\iota^{-1}(U))
is an isomorphism. Since
\iota^{-1}(U)=q^{-1}(U)\cap X^{\mathrm{hyb}}_{1}
and
T(q^{-1}(U))=q^{-1}(U)
, the result follows from Lemmas
2.42
and
2.40
.
∎

Let us now compare coherent sheaves on
X^{+}
and
X^{\!\urcorner}
.

Proposition 4.28
.

Let
U
be an open subset of
X^{+}
such that
q^{-1}(q(U))=U
. For each coherent sheaf
\mathcal{F}
on
U
, the natural morphism
q^{-1}q_{\ast}\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{F}
is an isomorphism. Moreover, the functor
q_{\ast}
is exact on coherent sheaves on
U
.

In particular, the natural morphism
q^{-1}\mathcal{O}_{X^{\!\urcorner}}\to\mathcal{O}_{X^{+}}
is an isomorphism.

Let
V
be an open subset of
X^{\!\urcorner}
. For each coherent sheaf
\mathcal{G}
on
V
, the natural morphism
\mathcal{G}\to q_{\ast}q^{-1}\mathcal{G}
is an isomorphism.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a coherent sheaf on
U
. Let
x\in U
. We have

(q^{-1}q_{\ast}\mathcal{F})_{x}=(q_{\ast}\mathcal{F})_{q(x)}=\colim_{W\ni q(x)}\mathcal{F}(q^{-1}(W)),

where
W
runs through the open neighborhoods of
q(x)
in
q(U)
.

Let
s\in(q_{\ast}\mathcal{F})_{q(x)}
whose image in
\mathcal{F}_{x}
is 0. There exists an open neighborhood
V
of

in
U
such that
s_{|V}=0
. By Theorem
2.31
, it follows that
s
is 0 on

. By Lemma
4.15
,
q(V)
is open, hence

in
(q_{\ast}\mathcal{F})_{q(x)}
. We deduce that the natural map
(q^{-1}q_{\ast}\mathcal{F})_{x}\to\mathcal{F}_{x}
is injective.

By similar arguments, we prove that the natural map
(q^{-1}q_{\ast}\mathcal{F})_{x}\to\mathcal{F}_{x}
is surjective. The first statement follows. The above description of the stalks ensures the exactness of
q_{\ast}
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a coherent sheaf on
V
. The statement being local, we may assume that we have an exact sequence
\mathcal{O}_{X^{\!\urcorner}}^{m}\to\mathcal{O}_{X^{\!\urcorner}}^{n}\to\mathcal{G}\to 0
. Applying the functor
q^{-1}
and using the first part of the statement, we get an exact sequence
\mathcal{O}_{X^{+}}^{m}\to\mathcal{O}_{X^{+}}^{n}\to q^{-1}\mathcal{G}\to 0
. It follows from this exact sequence and that
q^{-1}\mathcal{G}
is coherent. By the first part of the statement, we finally get a commutative diagram with exact rows

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{O}_{X^{\!\urcorner}}^{m}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{O}_{X^{\!\urcorner}}^{n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{G}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{O}_{X^{\!\urcorner}}^{m}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{O}_{X^{\!\urcorner}}^{n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces q_{\ast}q^{-1}\mathcal{G}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}

the result follows.
∎

Corollary 4.29
.

The sheaf
\mathcal{O}_{X^{\!\urcorner}}
is coherent.

Proof.

This follows from the exactness of

and

and the coherence of

, by similar arguments as in the proof of Proposition

.
∎

Corollary 4.30
.

For each open subset

of

, the functor

induces an equivalence between the categories of coherent sheaves on

Let

Let

5.
GAGA theorems

We investigate coherent sheaves on
X^{+}
and
X^{\!\urcorner}
and prove GAGA theorems relating them to coherent sheaves on
X
.

To do so, we consider

from the extrinsic point of view, that is to say as a subset of

, where

is an algebraic compactification of

, and eventually rely on the usual GAGA theorems on

. Consequently, our main technical results are extension results from

to

, either for sections of coherent sheaves (that extend up to some meromorphic singularities, see the final part of Theorem

and Lemma

), or for the sheaves themselves (see Theorem

).

5.1.

Let

be a variety over

and

may be defined using an extra ambient variety

and a closed subvariety

and

for a while. We will define some sort of hybrid thickenings of

Notation 5.1
.

Let

be a variety over

and

Remark that

identifies to

as a topological space (but their structure sheaves differ). Using the terminology from Definition

and Lemma

, we may characterize

(resp.

(resp.

Lemma 5.2

Let

∎

\mathcal{O}_{k_{0}}(D_{0}(\infty,0^{+}))=\mathcal{O}_{k_{0}}(D_{0}(1^{+},0^{+}))=\mathcal{O}_{k_{0}}(D_{0}(1^{+},1^{-})).

Those rings are contained in
k[T_{1},\dotsc,T_{n}][\![U_{1},\dotsc,U_{m}]\!]
.

We have

\mathcal{O}_{k_{0}}(D_{0}(1^{+},\,^{\ast}1^{-}))=\mathcal{O}_{k_{0}}(D_{0}(1^{+},1^{-}))[U_{1}^{-1},\dotsc,U_{m}^{-1}].

Proof.

Set
T:=(T_{1},\dotsc,T_{n})
and
U:=(U_{1},\dotsc,U_{m})
. For
i=(i_{1},\dotsc,i_{n})\in\mathbf{N}^{n}
, set
\lvert i\rvert:=i_{1}+\dotsb+i_{n}
and
T^{i}:=T_{1}^{i_{1}}\dotsb T_{n}^{i_{n}}
. Similarly, for
j=(j_{1},\dotsc,j_{m})\in\mathbf{N}^{m}
, set
\lvert j\rvert:=j_{1}+\dotsb+j_{m}
and
U^{j}:=U_{1}^{j_{1}}\dotsb U_{m}^{j_{m}}
.

The ring
\mathcal{O}_{k_{0}}(D_{0}(1^{+},0^{+}))
may be written as the set of power series

\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathbf{N}^{n+m}}a_{i,j}\,T^{i}U^{j}\in k[\![T,U]\!]

for which there exists
t>1
and
u>0
such that

\lim_{(i,j)\to\infty}\lvert a_{i,j}\rvert_{0}\,t^{\lvert i\rvert}u^{\lvert j\rvert}=0.

Let
f\in\mathcal{O}_{k_{0}}(D_{0}(1^{+},0^{+}))
and write it as a power series as above. Using the fact that
\lvert a_{i,j}\rvert
is 0 or 1, we deduce that
f
belongs to
k[T][\![U]\!]
.

Let us prove that
f\in\mathcal{O}_{k_{0}}(D_{0}(\infty,0^{+}))
. It is enough to prove that, for each
s>1
, we have
f\in\mathcal{O}_{k_{0}}(D_{0}(s^{+},0^{+}))
, where

D_{0}(s^{+},0^{+}):=\{x\in(\mathbf{A}^{n+m,\mathrm{hyb}}_{k})_{0}:\forall i=1,\dotsc,n,\lvert T_{i}(x)\rvert\leqslant s,\ \forall j=1,\dotsc,m,U_{j}(x)=0\}.

Since
t>1
, there exists
e>1
such that
t^{e}>s
. We have

\lim_{(i,j)\to\infty}\lvert a_{i,j}\rvert_{0}\,(t^{e})^{\lvert i\rvert}(u^{e})^{\lvert j\rvert}=\lim_{(i,j)\to\infty}\big(\lvert a_{i,j}\rvert_{0}\,t^{\lvert i\rvert}u^{\lvert j\rvert}\big)^{e}=0,

hence
f\in\mathcal{O}_{k_{0}}(D_{0}(s^{+},0^{+}))
.

The other parts of the statement are proven similarly.
∎

Let us now return to the usual overconvergent structures, inherited from the inclusions into
\mathbf{A}^{n+m,\mathrm{hyb}}_{k}
. We denote the corresponding structure sheaves simply by
\mathcal{O}
.

Lemma 5.6
.

We have natural isomorphisms

\mathcal{O}(D_{0}(\infty,0^{+}))=(\mathcal{O}(D_{0}(1^{+},1^{-}))=\mathcal{O}(D_{0}(1^{+},0^{+}))\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal{O}_{k_{0}}(D_{0}(1^{+},0^{+}))\cap\mathcal{O}_{\hat{k}}(D_{1}(\infty,0^{+}))

and

\mathcal{O}(D_{0}(1^{+},\,^{\ast}1^{-}))=(U_{1},\dotsc,U_{m})^{-1}\,\mathcal{O}(D_{0}(1^{+},1^{-})).

The Jacobson radical of
\mathcal{O}(D_{0}(1^{+},1^{-}))
is generated by
U_{1},\dotsc,U_{m}
.

Proof.

Use the same notation as in the proof of Lemma
5.5
. By
[
Poi13
, Corollaire 2.8]
, the ring
\mathcal{O}(D_{0}(1^{+},0^{+}))
identifies to the set of power series

f=\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathbf{N}^{n+m}}a_{i,j}\,T^{i}U^{j}\in k[\![T,U]\!]

for which there exists
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
,
t>1
and
u>0
such that the power series

\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathbf{N}^{n+m}}\lVert a_{i,j}\rVert_{[0,\varepsilon]}\,t^{\lvert i\rvert}u^{\lvert j\rvert}

converges.

Let
f\in\mathcal{O}_{k_{0}}(D_{0}(1^{+},0^{+}))
and write it as above. Recall that

\lVert{\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu}\rVert_{[0,\varepsilon]}=\max(\lvert{\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu}\rvert_{0},\lvert{\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu}\rvert^{\varepsilon})=\max\big(\lvert{\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu}\rvert_{0},\sup_{0<\delta\leqslant\varepsilon}(\lvert{\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu}\rvert^{\delta})\big).

As a result, the convergence condition splits into several conditions. The one involving
\lvert{\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu}\rvert_{0}
corresponds to the fact that
f
belongs to
\mathcal{O}_{k_{0}}(D_{0}(1^{+},0^{+}))
.

For
\delta\in(0,\varepsilon]
, the condition involving
\lvert{\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu}\rvert^{\delta}
amounts to the convergence of the power series
\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathbf{N}^{n+m}}\lvert a_{i,j}\rvert\,(t^{1/\delta})^{\lvert i\rvert}(u^{1/\delta})^{\lvert j\rvert}
, that is to say the convergence of
f
in the neighborhood of
D_{1}((t^{1/\delta})^{+},0^{+})
in
(\mathbf{A}^{n+m,\mathrm{hyb}}_{k})_{1}
, where

D_{1}((t^{1/\delta})^{+},0^{+}):=\{x\in(\mathbf{A}^{n+m,\mathrm{hyb}}_{k})_{1}:\forall i=1,\dotsc,n,\lvert T_{i}(x)\rvert\leqslant t^{1/\delta},\ \forall j=1,\dotsc,m,U_{j}(x)=0\}.

Since
t^{1/\delta}
goes to
+\infty
when
\delta
goes to 0,
f
finally belongs to
\mathcal{O}_{\hat{k}}(D_{1}(\infty,0^{+}))
.

We have proven that
\mathcal{O}(D_{0}(1^{+},0^{+}))\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{k_{0}}(D_{0}(1^{+},0^{+}))\cap\mathcal{O}_{\hat{k}}(D_{1}(\infty,0^{+}))
. The reverse inclusion is obtained by similar arguments.

The same method allows to compute the global sections of the structure sheaf on the discs of the form

\{x\in(\mathbf{A}^{n+m,\mathrm{hyb}}_{k})_{0^{\dagger}}:\forall i=1,\dotsc,n,\lvert T_{i}(x)\rvert\leqslant 1,\ \forall j=1,\dotsc,m,\lvert U_{j}(x)\rvert\leqslant u\},

for
u\in[0,1)
, or

\{x\in(\mathbf{A}^{n+m,\mathrm{hyb}}_{k})_{0^{\dagger}}:\forall i=1,\dotsc,n,\lvert T_{i}(x)\rvert\leqslant v,\ \forall j=1,\dotsc,m,U_{j}(x)=0\},

for
v>1
. Since
D_{0}(1^{+},1^{-})
and
D_{0}(\infty,0^{+})
are increasing unions of such discs, we may compute the global sections of the structure sheaf on them too.

For
\mathcal{O}(D_{0}(1^{+},\,^{\ast}1^{-}))
, we proceed similarly, writing
D_{0}(1^{+},\,^{\ast}1^{-})
as the increasing union of the annuli

\{x\in(\mathbf{A}^{n+m,\mathrm{hyb}}_{k})_{0^{\dagger}}:\forall i=1,\dotsc,n,\lvert T_{i}(x)\rvert\leqslant 1,\ \forall j=1,\dotsc,m,s\leqslant\lvert U_{j}(x)\rvert\leqslant t\},

for
s\leqslant t\in(0,1)
, and using
[
LP24
, Corollaire 1.4.11]
instead of
[
Poi13
, Corollaire 2.8]
.

Let us now prove the final part about the Jacobson radical. Recall that the Jacobson radical of a ring
B
may be characterized as the set of elements
b\in B
such that, for every
c\in B
,
1+cb
is invertible.

Let
f
be in the Jacobson radical of
\mathcal{O}(D_{0}(1^{+},0^{+}))
. By the first part of the statement and Lemma
5.5
,
f
belongs to
k[T_{1},\dotsc,T_{n}][\![U_{1},\dotsc,U_{m}]\!]
and, for each
c\in k^{\ast}
,
1+cf
is invertible in this ring. It follows that
f(T_{1},\dotsc,T_{n},0,\dots,0)=0
.

Let us write
f=\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathbf{N}^{n+m}}a_{i,j}\,T^{i}U^{j}
as above. For
k=1,\dotsc,m
, set

g_{k}:=\sum_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(i,j)\in\mathbf{N}^{n+m}}}{{j_{1}=\dotsb=j_{k-1}=0,\ j_{k}\geqslant 1}}}a_{i,j}\,T_{1}^{i_{1}}\dotsb T_{n}^{i_{n}}U_{k}^{i_{k}-1}U_{k+1}^{i_{k+1}}\dotsb U_{m}^{i_{m}}\in k[T_{1},\dotsc,T_{n}][\![U_{1},\dotsc,U_{m}]\!].

Since
f(T_{1},\dotsc,T_{n},0,\dots,0)=0
, we have

f=U_{1}\,g_{1}+\dotsb+U_{m}\,g_{m}.

By construction, the series
g_{k}
satisfy the same convergence conditions as
f
. It follows that they all belong to
\mathcal{O}(D_{0}(1^{+},1^{-}))
, hence

f\in(U_{1},\dotsc,U_{m})\,\mathcal{O}(D_{0}(1^{+},0^{+})).

Conversely, let
f\in(U_{1},\dotsc,U_{m})\,\mathcal{O}(D_{0}(1^{+},0^{+}))
. Let
c\in\mathcal{O}(D_{0}(1^{+},0^{+}))
. The function
1+cf
evaluates to 1 at each point of
D_{0}(1^{+},0^{+})
, hence it does not vanish in a neighbourhood of this set. It follows that it is invertible in
\mathcal{O}(D_{0}(1^{+},0^{+}))
, as required.
∎

Theorem 5.7
.

Let
\square\in\{\rho,r,0,\ast\}
. Let
\mathcal{F}
be a coherent sheaf on
V_{Y}^{\square}(Z)
. Then,
\mathcal{F}(V_{Y}^{\square}(Z))
is a finitely generated
\mathcal{O}(V_{Y}^{\square}(Z))
-module and, for each
q\in\mathbf{N}_{\geqslant 1}
, we have

H^{q}(V_{Y}^{\square}(Z),\mathcal{F})=0.

Moreover, the global section functor
\mathcal{F}\mapsto\mathcal{F}(V_{Y}^{\square}(Z))
induces an equivalence between the category of coherent sheaves on
V_{Y}^{\square}(Z)
and the category of finitely generated modules on
\mathcal{O}(V_{Y}^{\square}(Z))
.

We have natural isomorphisms

\mathcal{O}(D_{0}(1^{+},0^{+}))/\big(J+(U_{1}-h_{1},\dotsc,U_{m}-h_{m})\big)\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal{O}(V_{Y}^{0}(Z))=\mathcal{O}(V^{r}_{Y}(Z))=\mathcal{O}(V^{\rho}_{Y}(Z))

and

\mathcal{O}(D_{0}(1^{+},\,^{\ast}1^{-}))/\big(J+(U_{1}-h_{1},\dotsc,U_{m}-h_{m})\big)\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal{O}(V_{Y}^{\ast}(Z)),

sending
T_{i}
to
T_{i}
, for
i\in\{1,\dotsc,n\}
, and
U_{j}
to
h_{j}
, for
j\in\{1,\dotsc,m\}
.

In particular, we have

\mathcal{O}(V_{Y}^{\ast}(Z))=I^{-1}\,\mathcal{O}(V^{r}_{Y}(Z)).

The Jacobson radical of
\mathcal{O}(V^{r}_{Y}(Z))
is generated by
I
.

Proof.

By assumption,
Y
identifies to the closed subscheme of
\mathbf{A}^{n}_{k}
with coordinates
T_{1},\dotsc,T_{n}
defined by the ideal
J
. It follows that
Y^{\mathrm{hyb}}
identifies to the closed analytic subspace of
\mathbf{A}^{n,\mathrm{hyb}}_{k}
defined by the sheaf of ideals
\mathcal{J}
generated by
J
.

The projection
\pi\colon\mathbf{A}^{n+m,\mathrm{hyb}}_{k}\to\mathbf{A}^{n,\mathrm{hyb}}_{k}
induces an isomorphism between the closed analytic subset
E
of
\mathbf{A}^{n+m,\mathrm{hyb}}_{k}
defined by
\pi^{*}\mathcal{J}+(U_{1}-h_{1},\dotsc,U_{m}-h_{m})
and the closed analytic subset of
\mathbf{A}^{n,\mathrm{hyb}}_{k}
defined by
\mathcal{J}
(whose inverse is the morphism defined by
(h_{1},\dotsc,h_{m})
), that it to say
Y^{\mathrm{hyb}}
. Under this isomorphism, the subset
V^{0}_{Y}(Z)
of
Y^{\mathrm{hyb}}
corresponds to the image of
D_{0}(1^{+},0^{+})
in
E
.

Note that
V^{0}_{Y}(Z)
is an overconvergent affinoid space, with the terminology of
[
LP24
, Définition 8.3.1]
. Let
\mathcal{F}
be a coherent sheaf on
V^{0}_{Y}(Z)
. By
[
LP24
, Théorème 8.3.9]
,
\mathcal{F}(V^{0}_{Y}(Z))
is a finitely generated
\mathcal{O}(V^{0}_{Y}(Z))
-module,
\mathcal{F}(V^{0}_{Y}(Z))
generates
\mathcal{F}
and the higher cohomology of
\mathcal{F}
vanishes. The first part of the result for
V^{0}_{Y}(Z)
follows, as well as the claimed equivalence of categories.

By
[
LP24
, Corollaire 8.2.16]
, coherent sheaves on
D_{0}(1^{+},0^{+})
have no higher coherent cohomology. In particular, the first cohomology group of the sheaf of ideals generated by
J+(U_{1}-h_{1},\dotsc,U_{m}-h_{m})
vanishes. The description of
\mathcal{O}(V_{Y}^{0}(Z))
follows by a standard argument (see for instance the proof of
[
LP24
, Théorème 8.3.8]
).

Let us now prove the results for
V^{r}_{Y}(Z)
. Reasoning as above, we may identify
V^{r}_{Y}(Z)
with a closed analytic subset of
D_{0}(1^{+},1^{-})
. It is enough to prove that the latter has no higher coherent cohomology. For
n\in\mathbf{N}
, set

D_{n}:=\{x\in(\mathbf{A}^{n+m,\mathrm{hyb}}_{k})_{0^{\dagger}}:\forall i=1,\dotsc,n,\lvert T_{i}(x)\rvert\leqslant 1,\ \forall j=1,\dotsc,m,\lvert U_{j}(x)\rvert\leqslant 1-2^{-n}\}.

Arguing as in
[
LP24
, Exemple 8.5.6]
, we prove that the sequence
(D_{n})_{n\in\mathbf{N}}
is a universal Stein exhaustion of
D_{0}(1^{+},1^{-})
(see
[
LP24
, Définition 8.5.5]
). The result now follows from
[
LP24
, Théorème 8.5.14]
. The finite generation property and the equivalence of categories follow from the analogous results for
V^{0}_{Y}(Z)
, together with Proposition
5.3
.

A similar strategy works for
V_{Y}^{\rho}(Z)
, identifying it to a closed analytic subset of
D_{0}(\infty,0^{+})
and using the exhaustion by the discs

\{x\in(\mathbf{A}^{n+m,\mathrm{hyb}}_{k})_{0^{\dagger}}:\forall i=1,\dotsc,n,\lvert T_{i}(x)\rvert\leqslant n,\ \forall j=1,\dotsc,m,U_{j}(x)=0\},

for
n\in\mathbf{N}
.

Let us now deal with
V_{Y}^{\ast}(Z)
. To prove the finite generation property and the equivalence of categories, it is enough to find a subspace
W
of
V_{Y}^{\ast}(Z)
that will play the same role as
V^{0}_{Y}(Z)
for
V^{r}_{Y}(Z)
. In other words, we are looking for an overconvergent affinoid subspace
W
of
V_{Y}^{\ast}(Z)
such that
T(W)=V_{Y}^{\ast}(Z)
. A suitable candidate is given by

W=\{x\in V_{Y}^{\ast}(Z):\forall j\in\{1,\dotsc,m\},\lvert h_{j}(x)\rvert=s\},

for some
s\in(0,1)
. By
[
LP24
, Proposition 8.3.6]
, it is indeed an overconvergent affinoid space, and the rest is clear.

The final statements about the localization and the Jacobson radical now follow from Lemma
5.6
.
∎

Corollary 5.8
.

For each coherent sheaf
\mathcal{F}
on
V_{Y}^{\ast}(Z)
, there exists a coherent sheaf
\mathcal{G}^{r}
(resp.
\mathcal{G}^{\rho}
) on
V^{r}_{Y}(Z)
(resp.
V^{\rho}_{Y}(Z)
) such that
\mathcal{G}^{r}_{|V_{Y}^{\ast}(Z)}\simeq\mathcal{F}
(resp.
\mathcal{G}^{\rho}_{|V_{Y}^{\ast}(Z)}\simeq\mathcal{F}
).

Proof.

By Theorem
5.7
, the category of coherent sheaves on
V_{Y}^{\ast}(Z)
is equivalent to the category of modules of finite type over
\mathcal{O}(V_{Y}^{\ast}(Z))
. Similarly, the category of coherent sheaves on
V_{Y}^{r}(Z)
(resp.
V_{Y}^{\rho}(Z)
) is equivalent to the category of modules of finite type over
\mathcal{O}(V_{Y}^{r}(Z))
(resp.
\mathcal{O}(V^{\rho}_{Y}(Z))
). The results follows, since
\mathcal{O}(V_{Y}^{\ast}(Z))
is a localization of
\mathcal{O}(V^{r}_{Y}(Z))
(resp.
\mathcal{O}(V^{\rho}_{Y}(Z))
). ∎

5.1.2.
Extension of coherent sheaves

In this section, we return to the general setting. Let
Y
be a variety over
k
. Let
Z
be an effective Cartier divisor on
Y
. Let
\mathcal{I}
be the sheaf of ideals defining
Z
. By assumption, it is locally principal.

Our goal here is to prove that every coherent sheaf on
V_{Y}^{\ast}(Z)
still extends to a coherent sheaf on
V_{Y}(Z)
. The statement is reminiscent of results stipulating that coherent sheaves on the generic fiber of a formal scheme may be induced by coherent sheaves on the whole formal scheme, see
[
BL93
, Proposition 5.6]
or
[
Abb10
, Proposition 4.8.18]
. We closely follow the proofs given in those references.

We first extend Proposition
5.3
.

Proposition 5.9
.

For each coherent sheaf
\mathcal{F}
on
V^{\rho}_{Y}(Z)
(resp.
\mathcal{F}(V_{Y}^{r}(Z))
), the restriction map

\mathcal{F}(V^{\rho}_{Y}(Z))\longrightarrow\mathcal{F}(V_{Y}^{0}(Z))\textrm{ (resp. }\mathcal{F}(V_{Y}^{r}(Z))\longrightarrow\mathcal{F}(V_{Y}^{0}(Z))\,)

is an isomorphism.

Moreover, the restriction map induces an equivalence between the categories of coherent sheaves on
V_{Y}^{\rho}(Z)
(resp.
V_{Y}^{r}(Z)
) and
V^{0}_{Y}(Z)
.

Proof.

Let
\square\in\{\rho,r\}
. Let
(U_{i})_{i\in I}
be a finite open affine covering of
Y
. Let
\mathcal{F}
be a coherent sheaf on
V^{\square}_{Y}(Z)
. By Proposition
5.3
, for each
i,j\in I
, the restriction maps

\mathcal{F}(V^{\square}_{U_{i}}(Z\cap U_{i}))\longrightarrow\mathcal{F}(V^{0}_{U_{i}}(Z\cap U_{i}))

and

\mathcal{F}(V^{\square}_{U_{i}\cap U_{j}}(Z\cap U_{i}\cap U_{j}))\longrightarrow\mathcal{F}(V^{0}_{U_{i}\cap U_{j}}(Z\cap U_{i}\cap U_{j}))

are isomorphisms, for each
i,j\in I
.

By Lemma
5.2
, the family
(V^{\square}_{U_{i}}(Z\cap U_{i}))_{i\in I}
(resp.
(V^{0}_{U_{i}}(Z\cap U_{i}))_{i\in I}
) covers
V^{\square}_{Y}(Z)
(resp.
V^{0}_{Y}(Z)
) and, for
i,j\in I
, we have
V^{\square}_{U_{i}\cap U_{j}}(Z\cap U_{i}\cap U_{j})=V^{\square}_{U_{i}}(Z\cap U_{i})\cap V^{\square}_{U_{j}}(Z\cap U_{j})
(resp.
V^{0}_{U_{i}\cap U_{j}}(Z\cap U_{i}\cap U_{j})=V^{0}_{U_{i}}(Z\cap U_{i})\cap V^{0}_{U_{j}}(Z\cap U_{j})
). The first result follows. The second one is proved similarly.
∎

Lemma 5.10
.

Let
\square\in\{\rho,r,0\}
. Let
U
be an open subset of
Y
and set
V^{\square}_{U}(Z):=V^{\square}_{U}(Z\cap U)
. Let
\mathcal{F}
be a coherent sheaf on
V^{\square}_{Y}(Z)
and let
\mathcal{G}
be a coherent subsheaf of
\mathcal{F}_{|V^{\square}_{U}(Z)}
. Then, there exists a coherent subsheaf
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
of
\mathcal{F}
such that
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}_{|V^{\square}_{U}(Z)}=\mathcal{G}
.

Proof.

By Proposition
5.9
, it is enough to prove the statement for
\square=\rho
. The analytification
Z^{\textup{an}}_{0}
of
Z
over
k_{0}
may naturally be seen as a subset of the analytification
Z^{\mathrm{hyb}}
of
Z
over
k_{\mathrm{hyb}}
. Recall that we denote it by
Z^{\mathrm{hyb}}_{0^{\dagger}}
, when endowed with the overconvergent structure sheaf inherited from
Z^{\mathrm{hyb}}
(see Notation
2.39
).

Note that
V^{\rho}_{Y}(Z)
and
Z^{\mathrm{hyb}}_{0^{\dagger}}
have the same underlying topological spaces. More precisely, we have a closed immersion

j\colon Z^{\mathrm{hyb}}_{0^{\dagger}}\longrightarrow V^{\rho}_{Y}(Z),

which is defined by the sheaf of ideals
\mathcal{I}^{\mathrm{hyb}}
. It restricts to a closed immersion

j_{U}\colon(Z\cap U)^{\mathrm{hyb}}_{0^{\dagger}}\longrightarrow V^{\rho}_{U}(Z).

By Theorem
2.43
, we have an equivalence between the categories of coherent sheaves on
Z
(resp.
Z\cap U
) and
Z^{\mathrm{hyb}}_{0^{\dagger}}
(resp.
(Z\cap U)^{\mathrm{hyb}}_{0^{\dagger}}
). By
[
GD71
, Théorème 6.9.7]
, given a coherent sheaf on
Z
, any coherent subsheaf of its restriction to
Z\cap U
extends to a coherent subsheaf on the whole
Z
. It follows that there exists a coherent subsheaf
\mathcal{H}
of
j^{\ast}\mathcal{F}
such that
\mathcal{H}_{|(Z\cap U)^{\mathrm{hyb}}_{0^{\dagger}}}=j_{U}\!^{\ast}\mathcal{G}
.

Set
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}:=\sKer(\mathcal{F}\to j_{\ast}(j^{\ast}\mathcal{F}/\mathcal{H}))
. It is a coherent subsheaf of
\mathcal{F}
and we claim that
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}_{|V^{\rho}_{U}(Z)}=\mathcal{G}
. This is a local statement, so, using Lemma
5.2
, we may assume that
U
is affine. By Theorem
5.7
, it is enough to show that the modules of global sections
M^{\prime}
of
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}_{|V^{\rho}_{U}(Z)}
and
M
of
\mathcal{G}
are equal. By Theorem
5.7
,
M
and
M^{\prime}
are finitely generated over
\mathcal{O}(V^{\rho}_{U}(Z))
and, by construction, we have

j_{U}\!^{\ast}\mathcal{G}^{\prime}_{|V_{U}^{\rho}(Z)}=j_{U}\!^{\ast}\mathcal{G},

hence
M^{\prime}/\mathcal{I}^{\mathrm{hyb}}(V^{\rho}_{U}(Z))=M/\mathcal{I}^{\mathrm{hyb}}(V^{\rho}_{U}(Z))
. By Theorem
5.7
,
\mathcal{I}^{\mathrm{hyb}}(V^{\rho}_{U}(Z))
is the Jacobson radical of
\mathcal{O}(V^{\rho}_{U}(Z))
, so that the statement is now a consequence of Nakayama’s lemma.
∎

Lemma 5.11
.

Let
U_{1}
and
U_{2}
be quasi-compact open subsets of
Y
. For
i\in\{1,2\}
, set
V^{r}_{i}:=V^{r}_{U_{i}}(Z\cap U_{i})
and
V^{\ast}_{i}:=V_{U_{i}}^{\ast}(Z\cap U_{i})
. Let
\mathcal{F}_{1}
and
\mathcal{F}_{2}
be coherent sheaves on
V_{1}^{\ast}
and
V_{2}^{\ast}
respectively. Assume that they extend to coherent sheaves on
V^{r}_{1}
and
V^{r}_{2}
respectively, and that there exists an isomorphism

v\colon(\mathcal{F}_{1})_{|V^{\ast}_{1}\cap V^{\ast}_{2}}\xrightarrow{\sim}(\mathcal{F}_{2})_{|V^{\ast}_{1}\cap V^{\ast}_{2}}.

Then there exist a coherent sheaf
\mathcal{F}
on
V^{r}_{1}\cup V^{r}_{2}
and isomorphisms
\mathcal{F}_{|V_{1}^{\ast}}\simeq\mathcal{F}_{1}
and
\mathcal{F}_{|V_{2}^{\ast}}\simeq\mathcal{F}_{2}
that are compatible with
v
.

Proof.

For
i\in\{1,2\}
, let
\mathcal{G}_{i}
be a coherent sheaf on
V^{r}_{i}
such that
(\mathcal{G}_{i})_{|V_{i}^{\ast}}=\mathcal{F}_{i}
. Moding out by the annihilator of
\mathcal{I}
, which is coherent, we may assume that
\mathcal{G}_{1}
and
\mathcal{G}_{2}
have no
\mathcal{I}
-torsion. It then follows from Theorem
5.7
that, for each
i\in\{1,2\}
and each open subset
W
of
V^{r}_{i}
, the restriction map

\rho_{i}\colon\mathcal{G}_{i}(W)\to\mathcal{G}_{i}(W\cap V_{i}^{\ast})=\mathcal{F}_{i}(W\cap V_{i}^{\ast})

is injective.

We claim that there exists an integer

The maximum spectral radius of θ 1 , 3 , 3 \theta_{1,3,3} -free graphs with given size
Jing Gao, Xueliang Li

Abstract
A graph G G is said to be F F -free if it does not contain F F as a subgraph. A theta graph, say θ l 1 , l 2 , l 3 \theta_{l_{1},l_{2},l_{3}} , is the graph obtained by connecting two distinct vertices with three internally disjoint paths of length l 1 , l 2 , l 3 l_{1},l_{2},l_{3} , where l 1 ≤ l 2 ≤ l 3 l_{1}\leq l_{2}\leq l_{3} and l 2 ≥ 2 l_{2}\geq 2 . Recently, Li, Zhao and Zou [arXiv:2409.15918v1] characterized the θ 1 , p , q \theta_{1,p,q} -free graph of size m m having the largest spectral radius, where q ≥ p ≥ 3 q\geq p\geq 3 and p + q ≥ 2 ​ k + 1 ≥ 7 p+q\geq 2k+1\geq 7 , and proposed a problem on characterizing the graphs with the maximum spectral radius among θ 1 , 3 , 3 \theta_{1,3,3} -free graphs. In this paper, we consider this problem and determine the maximum spectral radius of θ 1 , 3 , 3 \theta_{1,3,3} -free graphs with size m m and characterize the extremal graph. Up to now, all the graphs in 𝒢 ⁡ ( m , θ 1 , p , q ) \mathcal{G}(m,\theta_{1,p,q}) which have the largest spectral radius have been determined, where q ≥ p ≥ 2 q\geq p\geq 2 . Keywords: Spectral radius; ℱ \mathcal{F} -free graphs; Theta graphs; Extremal graph AMS subject classification 2020: 05C35, 05C50.

Lemma 2.1

If

Wu et al.

Lemma 2.2

[

Let

G
with order

Proof of Theorem
1.4

Let
G^{\ast}
be a graph in
\mathcal{G}(m,\theta_{1,3,3})

\lambda(K_{2}\vee\frac{m-1}{2}K_{1})=\frac{1+\sqrt{4m-3}}{2}

\lambda(G^{\ast})\geq\lambda(K_{2}\vee\frac{m-1}{2}K_{1})=\frac{1+\sqrt{4m-3}}{2}.

By Lemma
2.3
, we have
G^{\ast}
is connected. Let
\lambda=\lambda(G^{\ast})
and
\mathbf{x}
be the Perron vector of

corresponding to the vertex

. Assume that

is the extremal vertex of

. That is,

for any

. Set

and

for any component

G^{\ast}[U]
. Since

is

-free,
G^{\ast}[U]
does not contain any path of length four and any cycle of length more than four.

Lemma 3.1

For any non-trivial component

for any vertices

Let the cycle

in

be

. Suppose on the contrary that

for some vertices

and
u_{j}
,

. It follows that
N_{W}(u_{i})\neq\emptyset
and
N_{W}(u_{j})\neq\emptyset
. Let

. If
u_{i}
and
u_{j}
are adjacent in

. Without loss of generality, we assume that
u_{i}=u_{1}
and
u_{j}=u_{2}
. Then
u^{\ast}u_{1}
,
u^{\ast}u_{3}u_{4}u_{1}
and

are three internally disjoint paths of length 1,3,3 between

and

. So

contains

as a subgraph, a contradiction. Hence,
u_{i}
and
u_{j}
are not adjacent in
C_{4}
. Assume that

and
u_{j}=u_{3}

u_{1}u_{2}
,

and

are three internally disjoint paths of length 1,3,3 between

and

. Clearly,

contains
\theta_{1,3,3}
as a subgraph, a contradiction. This completes the proof.

\Box

For convenience, we divide
U
into two subsets
U_{0}
and
U_{+}
where
U_{0}
is the set of isolated vertices of
G^{\ast}[U]
and
U_{+}=U\setminus U_{0}
. It is easy to see that
m=|U|+e(U_{+})+e(U,W)+e(W)
. Since
\lambda(G^{\ast})\mathbf{x}=A(G^{\ast})\mathbf{x}
, we have

\lambda x_{u^{\ast}}=\sum_{u\in U}x_{u}=\sum_{u\in U_{+}}x_{u}+\sum_{u\in U_{0}}x_{u}.

Furthermore, we can get

\displaystyle\lambda^{2}x_{u^{\ast}}

\displaystyle=\lambda(\lambda x_{u^{\ast}})=\lambda\sum_{u\in U}x_{u}

\displaystyle=\sum_{u\in U}\sum_{v\in N_{G}(u)}x_{v}=\sum_{v\in V(G)}d_{U}(v)x_{v}

\displaystyle=|U|x_{u^{\ast}}+\sum_{u\in U_{+}}d_{U}(u)x_{u}+\sum_{w\in W}d_{U}(w)x_{w}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle(\lambda^{2}-\lambda)x_{u^{\ast}}

\displaystyle=|U|x_{u^{\ast}}+\sum_{u\in U_{+}}(d_{U}(u)-1)x_{u}+\sum_{w\in W}d_{U}(w)x_{w}-\sum_{u\in U_{0}}x_{u}

\displaystyle\leq|U|x_{u^{\ast}}+\sum_{u\in U_{+}}(d_{U}(u)-1)x_{u}+e(U,W)x_{u^{\ast}}-\sum_{u\in U_{0}}x_{u}.

Recall that
\lambda\geq\frac{1+\sqrt{4m-3}}{2}
, that is,
\lambda^{2}-\lambda\geq m-1=|U|+e(U_{+})+e(U,W)+e(W)-1
. Hence

\sum_{u\in U_{+}}(d_{U}(u)-1)x_{u}\geq\left(e(U_{+})+e(W)+\sum_{u\in U_{0}}\frac{x_{u}}{x_{u^{\ast}}}-1\right)x_{u^{\ast}}.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be the set of all non-trivial components in
G^{\ast}[U]
. For each non-trivial component
H
of
\mathcal{H}
, we denote
\eta(H):=\sum_{u\in V(H)}(d_{H}(u)-1)x_{u}
. Clearly,

\displaystyle\sum_{H\in\mathcal{H}}\eta(H)\geq\left(e(U_{+})+e(W)+\sum_{u\in U_{0}}\frac{x_{u}}{x_{u^{\ast}}}-1\right)x_{u^{\ast}},

(1)

with equality if and only if
\lambda^{2}-\lambda=m-1
and
x_{w}=x_{u^{\ast}}
for any
w\in W
with
d_{U}(w)\geq 1
.

Claim 3.2

G^{\ast}[U]
contains no any cycle of length four.

Proof.

Suppose to the contrary that
G^{\ast}[U]
contains
C_{4}
, that is,
G^{\ast}[U]
contains a component
H\in\{C_{4},C_{4}+e,K_{4}\}
where

is the graph obtained from
C_{4}
by adding one edge to two nonadjacent vertices. Let
\mathcal{H^{\prime}}
be the family of components of
G^{\ast}[U]
each of which contains
C_{4}
as a subgraph, then
\mathcal{H}\setminus\mathcal{H^{\prime}}
be the family of other components of
G^{\ast}[U]
each of which is a tree or unicyclic graph. Therefore, for each
H\in\mathcal{H}\setminus\mathcal{H^{\prime}}
, we have

\eta(H)=\sum_{u\in V(H)}(d_{H}(u)-1)x_{u}\leq(2e(H)-|H|)x_{u^{\ast}}\leq e(H)x_{u^{\ast}}.

Next we show that

\eta(H)<(e(H)-1)x_{u^{\ast}}+\frac{2\sum_{w\in W_{H}}x_{w}}{\lambda-3}

for each
H\in\mathcal{H^{\prime}}
. Let
H^{\ast}\in\mathcal{H^{\prime}}
with
V(H^{\ast})=\{u_{1},u_{2},u_{3},u_{4}\}
and the cycle of length four be
u_{1}u_{2}u_{3}u_{4}
.

First, we consider the case
W_{H^{\ast}}=\emptyset
. Let
x_{u_{1}}=\max\{x_{u_{i}}|1\leq i\leq 4\}
. Then

\lambda x_{u_{1}}=\sum_{u\in N(u_{1})}x_{u}\leq x_{u^{\ast}}+x_{u_{2}}+x_{u_{3}}+x_{u_{4}}\leq x_{u^{\ast}}+3x_{u_{1}}.

Hence,
x_{u_{1}}\leq\frac{1}{\lambda-3}x_{u^{\ast}}
. Since
m\geq 43
, we have
\lambda\geq\frac{1+\sqrt{4m-3}}{2}\geq 7
. Thus,
x_{u_{1}}<\frac{1}{2}x_{u^{\ast}}
and

\displaystyle\eta(H^{\ast})\leq(2e(H^{\ast})-|H^{\ast}|)x_{u_{1}}<(e(H^{\ast})-2)x_{u^{\ast}}<(e(H^{\ast})-1)x_{u^{\ast}}+\frac{2\sum_{w\in W_{H^{\ast}}}x_{w}}{\lambda-3},

as desired.

In the following, we assume that
W_{H^{\ast}}\neq\emptyset
. We consider the following two cases.

Case 1.
All vertices in
W_{H^{\ast}}
have a unique common neighbor in
V(H^{\ast})
.

Without loss of generality, let the common neighbor be
u_{1}
. It follows that
N_{W}(u_{i})=\emptyset
for
i\in\{2,3,4\}
. Let
x_{u_{2}}=\max\{x_{u_{i}}|2\leq i\leq 4\}
. Then

\lambda x_{u_{2}}\leq x_{u_{1}}+x_{u_{3}}+x_{u_{4}}+x_{u^{\ast}}\leq 2x_{u_{2}}+2x_{u^{\ast}}.

Thus,
x_{u_{2}}\leq\frac{2}{\lambda-2}x_{u^{\ast}}\leq\frac{2}{5}x_{u^{\ast}}
since
\lambda\geq 7
. Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\eta(H^{\ast})

\displaystyle=\sum_{u\in V(H^{\ast})}(d_{H^{\ast}}(u)-1)x_{u}

\displaystyle\leq(d_{H^{\ast}}(u_{1})-1)x_{u_{1}}+(2e(H^{\ast})-d_{H^{\ast}}(u_{1})-3)x_{u_{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(d_{H^{\ast}}(u_{1})-1+\frac{4}{5}e(H^{\ast})-\frac{2}{5}d_{H^{\ast}}(u_{1})-\frac{6}{5}\right)x_{u^{\ast}}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{4}{5}e(H^{\ast})+\frac{3}{5}d_{H^{\ast}}(u_{1})-\frac{11}{5}\right)x_{u^{\ast}}.

Since
d_{H^{\ast}}(u_{1})\leq 3
, the above inequality becomes

\displaystyle\eta(H^{\ast})

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{4}{5}e(H^{\ast})-\frac{2}{5}\right)x_{u^{\ast}}

\displaystyle<(e(H^{\ast})-1)x_{u^{\ast}}

\displaystyle<(e(H^{\ast})-1)x_{u^{\ast}}+\frac{2\sum_{w\in W_{H^{\ast}}}x_{w}}{\lambda-3}.

Case 2.
There are at least two vertices of
W_{H^{\ast}}
such that they have distinct neighbors in
V(H^{\ast})
.

Since

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\lambda x_{u_{1}}\leq x_{u_{2}}+x_{u_{3}}+x_{u_{4}}+x_{u^{\ast}}+\sum_{w\in N_{W_{H^{\ast}}}(u_{1})}x_{w},\\
\lambda x_{u_{2}}\leq x_{u_{1}}+x_{u_{3}}+x_{u_{4}}+x_{u^{\ast}}+\sum_{w\in N_{W_{H^{\ast}}}(u_{2})}x_{w},\\
\lambda x_{u_{3}}\leq x_{u_{1}}+x_{u_{2}}+x_{u_{4}}+x_{u^{\ast}}+\sum_{w\in N_{W_{H^{\ast}}}(u_{3})}x_{w},\\
\lambda x_{u_{4}}\leq x_{u_{1}}+x_{u_{2}}+x_{u_{3}}+x_{u^{\ast}}+\sum_{w\in N_{W_{H^{\ast}}}(u_{4})}x_{w},\end{cases}

we obtain

\displaystyle\lambda(x_{u_{1}}+x_{u_{2}}+x_{u_{3}}+x_{u_{4}})\leq 3(x_{u_{1}}+x_{u_{2}}+x_{u_{3}}+x_{u_{4}})+4x_{u^{\ast}}+\sum_{i=1}^{4}\sum_{w\in N_{W_{H^{\ast}}}(u_{i})}x_{w}.

By Lemma
3.1
, we get that
N_{W_{H^{\ast}}}(u_{i})\cap N_{W_{H^{\ast}}}(u_{j})=\emptyset
for any vertices
u_{i}\neq u_{j}\in V(H^{\ast})
. Thus,
\sum_{w\in W_{H^{\ast}}}x_{w}=\sum_{w\in N_{W}(V({H^{\ast}}))}x_{w}=\sum_{i=1}^{4}\sum_{w\in N_{W_{H^{\ast}}}(u_{i})}x_{w}
. Therefore, by

, we obtain

\displaystyle x_{u_{1}}+x_{u_{2}}+x_{u_{3}}+x_{u_{4}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4x_{u^{\ast}}}{\lambda-3}+\frac{\sum_{w\in W_{H^{\ast}}}x_{w}}{\lambda-3}

\displaystyle\leq x_{u^{\ast}}+\frac{\sum_{w\in W_{H^{\ast}}}x_{w}}{\lambda-3}.

Hence, by the definition of
\eta(H^{\ast})
,

\displaystyle\eta(H^{\ast})

\displaystyle\leq 2(x_{u_{1}}+x_{u_{2}}+x_{u_{3}}+x_{u_{4}})

\displaystyle\leq 2x_{u^{\ast}}+\frac{2\sum_{w\in W_{H^{\ast}}}x_{w}}{\lambda-3}

\displaystyle<(e(H^{\ast})-1)x_{u^{\ast}}+\frac{2\sum_{w\in W_{H^{\ast}}}x_{w}}{\lambda-3}.

Therefore, we conclude that
\eta(H)<(e(H)-1)x_{u^{\ast}}+\frac{2\sum_{w\in W_{H}}x_{w}}{\lambda-3}
for each
H\in\mathcal{H^{\prime}}
.
Recall that
\eta(H)\leq e(H)x_{u^{\ast}}
for each
H\in\mathcal{H}\setminus\mathcal{H^{\prime}}
. Thus,

\displaystyle\sum_{H\in\mathcal{H}}\eta(H)

\displaystyle=\sum_{H\in\mathcal{H^{\prime}}}\eta(H)+\sum_{H\in\mathcal{H}\setminus\mathcal{H^{\prime}}}\eta(H)

\displaystyle<\sum_{H\in\mathcal{H^{\prime}}}(e(H)-1)x_{u^{\ast}}+\sum_{H\in\mathcal{H^{\prime}}}\frac{2\sum_{w\in W_{H}}x_{w}}{\lambda-3}+\sum_{H\in\mathcal{H}\setminus\mathcal{H^{\prime}}}e(H)x_{u^{\ast}}

\displaystyle=e(U_{+})x_{u^{\ast}}-\sum_{H\in\mathcal{H^{\prime}}}x_{u^{\ast}}+\sum_{H\in\mathcal{H^{\prime}}}\frac{2\sum_{w\in W_{H}}x_{w}}{\lambda-3}.

For any
H\in\mathcal{H^{\prime}}
satisfying

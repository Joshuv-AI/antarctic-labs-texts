Shannon weights for binary dynamical recurrent sources of zero entropy
Ali Akhavi, Eda Cesaratto, Frédédric Paccaut, Pablo Rotondo and Brigitte Vallée

Abstract
. A probabilistic source is defined as the set of infinite words (over a given denumerable alphabet) endowed with a probability μ \mu . The paper deals with general binary sources where the distribution of any symbol (0 or 1) may depend on an unbounded part of the previous history. The paper studies Shannon weights: whereas the classical Shannon entropy ℰ μ \mathcal{E}_{\mu} is the average amount of information brought by one symbol of the emitted word, the Shannon weight sequence deals with the average amount of information m ¯ μ ​ ( n ) \underline{m}_{\mu}(n) that is brought by the emitted prefix of length n n . For a source with a non zero entropy, the estimate m ¯ μ ​ ( n ) ∼ ℰ μ ⋅ n \underline{m}_{\mu}(n)\sim\mathcal{E}_{\mu}\cdot n thus holds. The paper considers the model of dynamical sources, where a source word is emitted as an encoded trajectory of a dynamical system of the unit interval, when endowed with probability μ \mu . It focus on sources with zero entropy and gives explicit constructions for sources whose Shannon weight sequence satisfies m ¯ μ ​ ( n ) = o ​ ( n ) \underline{m}_{\mu}(n)=o(n) , with a prescribed behaviour. In this case, sources with zero entropy lead to dynamical systems built on maps with an indifferent fixed point. This class notably contains the celebrated Farey source, which presents well-known intermittency phenomena. Methods are based on analytic combinatorics and generating functions, and they are enlarged, in the present dynamical case, with dynamical systems tools (mainly transfer operators).

2.1.
General sources

A general source is a probabilistic process, denoted by
{\mathcal{S}}
, that emits infinite words with symbols from a given alphabet
\Sigma

t=1,\ldots,t=n,\ldots

Sequence of random variables.

Consider some abstract probability space

X_{n}
(defined on
\Omega
)
denotes the random variable whose value at

\alpha_{n}=X_{n}(\omega)

{\mathcal{S}}

In the sequel, any sequence (finite or infinite) of symbols will be identified with the concatenation of its terms (written without commas). Any word emitted by the source is thus a one-sided infinite word

Fundamental domains, fundamental probabilities and Kolmogorov Property.

For each

\pi_{k}:\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}}\to\Sigma^{k}

(4)

{\mathcal{I}}_{w}=\pi_{|w|}^{-1}(\{w\})\hbox{ gathers the elements of $\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}}$ that begin with the prefix $w$}.

is endowed with the product
\sigma
-algebra, i.e., the
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{F}
generated by the sets

is also the Borel
\sigma
-algebra on
\Sigma^{\mathbb{N}}
.
Then, the sequence
\mathcal{P}
of fundamental partitions

of order

(5)

\mathcal{P}=({\mathcal{P}}_{k}),\quad{\mathcal{P}}_{k}=\{{\mathcal{I}}_{w}\mid w\in\Sigma^{k}\},\qquad p(w)=\mu({\mathcal{I}}_{w})\,,

The distribution

We consider
the following two

Costs.

The main objects of our study are

3.8.
An emblematic instance: the Farey system.

The Farey dynamical system plays an important role in Number Theory, notably because its block dynamical system coincides with the Gauss map, as we recall it later on. This also provides a natural example of dynamical system with intermittent type,
that is studied for instance in Prellberg’s paper
[
PS92
]
. We will see that the Farey dynamical system belongs to the
\mathcal{DRIS}
class, introduced and studied in Section
8

\mathcal{DRIL}
of Section
9

The Farey map
T
possesses two complete inverse branches
a
and
b

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l ll }a:[0,1]\rightarrow[0,1/2]&\quad a(x)={x}/(1+x)\,,\cr b:[0,1]\rightarrow[1/2,1]&\quad b(x)=1/(1+x)\,.\cr\end{array}\right.

As the iterates
a^{n}
of the first inverse branch
a
satisfy
a^{n}(x)=x/(1+nx)
, the sequence
q(n)=a^{n}(1)
is
q(n)=1/(n+1)
and
(a^{n})^{\prime}(x)=1/(1+nx)^{2}
. Moreover, as the derivative
b^{\prime}
of the second inverse branch
b
satisfies
b^{\prime}(x)=-1/(1+x)^{2}
, the Farey source thus belongs to the class
\mathcal{DRI}_{w}
.

The block system of the Farey system
is defined via the partition

{\mathcal{J}}_{m}=[q(m),q(m-1)]
for
m\geq 1
with

and the local inverses

are
g_{m}=a^{m-1}\circ b
. For any

, the branch

a^{m-1}(x)
equals
x/(1+(m-1)x)
, and the equality

a^{m-1}\circ b(x)=\frac{b(x)}{1+(m-1)b(x)}=\left(\frac{1}{1+x}\right)\left(\frac{1+x}{1+x+(m-1)}\right)=\frac{1}{m+x}

proves that

coincides with the linear fractional transformation (LFT in shorthand)

that is indeed the inverse of the Gauss map on the interval
{\mathcal{J}}_{m}
. One has thus proven that the block map
\widehat{T}
of the Farey map
is the Gauss map,

\widehat{T}(0)=0,\qquad\widehat{T}(x)=\left\{\frac{1}{x}\right\}\quad(x\not=0)\,.

4.3.
Singularity analysis: Towards the third main result

The general singularity analysis process relates the asymptotics of a sequence and the behaviour of its generating function near the dominant singularity. Generally speaking,
complex analysis is very often needed. However, in the present context, due to strong positivity properties, it is enough to deal with real analysis, as we now explain.

We perform a “round trip” between the behaviour (as

) of a positive sequence

and the behaviour (as
v

1^{-}

We begin with a (positive) sequence

and study the partial sum

(A)

[Abelian step]
An Abelian Theorem
describes the behaviour of the generating function
Q_{\mu}(v)
when

along the positive real line from the asymptotic behaviour of the sequence
Q^{(\mu)}_{n}
;

(B)

Using Theorem
20
provides the behaviour of the two generating functions
(1-v)N_{\mu}(v),(1-v)M_{\mu}(v)
when
v\to 1^{-}
along the positive real line.

(C)

[Tauberian step] We return to the asymptotic estimate of the partial sums of coefficients of the functions
(1-v)N_{\mu}(v),(1-v)M_{\mu}(v)
–that resp. coincide with
\underline{n}_{\mu}(n)
,
\underline{m}_{\mu}(n)
– via a Tauberian Theorem.

The following central theorem (Theorem

via its implication [

via its implication [

8.1.

8.2.
About our Class
\mathcal{DRIS}

The idea of dealing with a direct branch

with an indifferent point at

[

]

gives a precise definition, and obtains precise results on these sources that will be used later on by Aaronson

[

deals with maps

We remark that all the tools Holland needs in the study of “his” class can be in fact expressed with the inverse branch

via the inverse branch

or its inverse

Via Implicit Function Theorem, there thus exists a relation between

and

that is however not straightforward: this explains that the computations made by Holland are often less direct than ours, notably when he deals with convexity.

9.2.

For a system of the subclass
\mathcal{DRI}

Proposition 48

For a source

of Theorem

. We want to show that we can invert this relation asymptotically, yielding the asymptotics for

For
\delta=0

9.7.
Return to Shannon weights

The notion of coincidence intervals is central in the context of dynamical systems, here defined on the unit interval
\mathcal{I}
.
We first recall this notion:
for any
x\in\mathcal{I}
and any depth
k
, the interval

{\mathcal{I}}_{k}(x)
gathers all the reals
y\in\mathcal{I}
that belong to the same fundamental
{\mathcal{I}}_{w}
as
x
. Any
y\in{\mathcal{I}}_{k}(x)
then emits the same prefix of length
k
as
x
. The random variable
X_{k}:=\log\mu({\mathcal{I}}_{k})
is thus of great interest.

The present paper studies Shannon weights and describes their asymptotic behaviour. Observe that, for
k\geq 1
, the function
x\mapsto{\mathcal{I}}_{k}(x)
is a staircase function which is constant on each interval
\mathcal{I}_{w}
with
|w|=k
, and thus Shannon weights exactly coincide with the expectations of the variable
x\mapsto X_{k}(x)
,

\underline{m}_{\mu}(k)=\mathbb{E}_{\mu}[X_{k}],\quad X_{k}(x)=|\log\mu({\mathcal{I}}_{k})(x)|
.

The paper
[
BCRS23
]
studies other probabilistic characteristics of the random variable
X_{k}:=|\log\mu({\mathcal{I}}_{k})|
. It introduces in Definitions 2.1 and 2.2 of
[
BCRS23
]
two notions of weights (AEW) and (MW) that provide extensions of respective entropies introduced in the paper of Dajani and Fieldsteel
[
DF01
]
.
It does not consider the notion of Shannon weights.

We now rewrite all these definitions in the “dynamical” context:

Definition 51
.

[
BCRS23
]
Consider a dynamical source
{\mathcal{S}}
, a measure
\mu
on the unit interval, for any depth
k
, the random variable

X_{k}:=|\log\mu({\mathcal{I}}_{k})|
, and a real positive sequence
f:k\mapsto f_{k}
.

(SW)
[f]
The source
{\mathcal{S}}
has a Shannon weight
f_{k}
if and only if

\mathbb{E}_{\mu}[X_{k}]\sim f_{k}\quad(k\to\infty)

(AEW)
[f]
The source
{\mathcal{S}}
has a almost everywhere weight
f_{k}
if and only if

X_{k}(x)\sim f_{k}\quad\hbox{\rm[$x$ almost everywhere]}\quad(k\to\infty)

(MW)
[f]
The source
{\mathcal{S}}
has a weight (in measure)
12
12

12

We first recall that a sequence
X_{k}
converges to 0 in measure if and only if

\forall\epsilon>0,\lim_{k\to\infty}\left|\{x\in{\mathcal{I}}\mid|X_{k}(x)|>\epsilon\}\right|=0\,.\quad(k\to\infty)

f_{k}
if and only if

X_{k}(x)\sim f_{k}\quad\hbox{\rm[in measure]}\quad(k\to\infty)

In each of the three cases, if the sequence
k\mapsto f_{k}
is linear, of the form
f_{k}=c\cdot k
, the source
{\mathcal{S}}
is said to have entropy
c
(resp. Shannon entropy, almost everywhere entropy, entropy in measure).

The paper
[
BCRS23
]
focuses on the notions (AEW) or (MW)
and considers many classical sources defined in Number Theory contexts;
it studies in particular the Farey dynamical source (called in
[
BCRS23
]
the Stern-Brocot source) from these two points of view and proves the following:

– the Farey source satisfies (MW)
[f]
with

\displaystyle f_{k}=\displaystyle\phi(0)({\pi^{2}}/{6})({k}/{\log k})
;

– the Farey source does not satisfy (AEW).

The two papers (the
[
BCRS23
]
paper and the present one) thus study the same sequence

Y_{k}(x)=\displaystyle\frac{X_{k}(x)}{f_{k}}-1
,

(and both in an indirect way). The paper
[
BCRS23
]
shows in its Proposition 4.7 that

converges to 0 in measure, with tools that are quite specific to the Farey source and its close relation to continued fraction expansions (using for instance a deep result due to Khinchin), while the present one obtains the convergence of the expectation
\mathbb{E}[Y_{k}]
to 0, with quite different tools (notably Abelian and Tauberian theorems, together with a renewal theorem) that can be applied to more general contexts.

There is now an important question: Is it possible to directly relate the two results in our setting? There does not exist any general relation between (MW)
[f]
and (SW)
[f]
,
and one needs extra
sufficient
assumptions on
Y_{k}
: for instance, general domination properties, described in
[
T11
]
, or a more precise information on the second moment of
X_{k}
, would allow to relate (MW)
[f]
to (SW)
[f]
.

Assume, for instance, that the second moment
\mathbb{E}[X_{k}^{2}]
of
X_{k}
is asymptotically equivalent to the square
f_{k}^{2}=(\mathbb{E}[X_{k}])^{2}
of the first moment; then the variance
{\tt Var}(X_{k})
would satisfy
{\tt Var}(X_{k})=\mathbb{E}[X_{k}^{2}]-f_{k}^{2}=o(f_{k}^{2})
, and Chebyshev’s inequality would apply and imply

(102)

\mu[|Y_{k}|\geq\varepsilon]=\mu[|X_{k}-f_{k}|\geq f_{k}\varepsilon]\leq\frac{{\tt Var}(X_{k})}{f_{k}^{2}\varepsilon^{2}}=o(1)\,,

for any fixed
\varepsilon>0
. This would show that
Y_{k}
converges to 0 in measure.

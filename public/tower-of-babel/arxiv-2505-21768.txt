Dynamics of screened particles towards equi-spaced ground states
Lucia De Luca, Michael Goldman, Marcello Ponsiglione

Abstract
. This paper deals with the dynamics - driven by the gradient flow of negative fractional seminorms - of empirical measures towards equi-spaced ground states. Specifically, we consider periodic empirical measures μ \mu on the real line that are screened by the Lebesgue measure, i.e., with μ − d ​ x \mu-\!\,\mathrm{d}x having zero average. To each of these measures μ \mu we associate a (periodic) function u u satisfying u ′ = d ​ x − μ u^{\prime}=\!\,\mathrm{d}x-\mu . For s ∈ ( 0 , 1 2 ) s\in(0,\frac{1}{2}) we introduce energy functionals ℰ s ​ ( μ ) {\mathscr{E}}^{s}(\mu) that can be understood as the density of the s s -Gagliardo seminorm of u u per unit length. Since for s ≥ 1 2 s\geq\frac{1}{2} , the s s -Gagliardo seminorms are infinite on functions with jumps, some regularization procedure is needed: For s ∈ [ 1 2 , 1 ) s\in[\frac{1}{2},1) we define ℰ ε s ​ ( μ ) := ℰ s ​ ( μ ε ) {\mathscr{E}}_{\varepsilon}^{s}(\mu):={\mathscr{E}}^{s}(\mu_{\varepsilon}) , where μ ε \mu_{\varepsilon} is obtained by mollifying μ \mu on scale ε \varepsilon . We prove that the minimizers of ℰ s {\mathscr{E}}^{s} and ℰ ε s {\mathscr{E}}_{\varepsilon}^{s} are the equi-spaced configurations of particles with lattice spacing equal to one. Then, we prove the exponential convergence of the corresponding gradient flows to the equi-spaced steady states. Finally, although for s ∈ [ 1 2 , 1 ) s\in[\frac{1}{2},1) the energy functionals ℰ ε s {\mathscr{E}}_{\varepsilon}^{s} blow up as ε → 0 \varepsilon\to 0 , their gradients are uniformly bounded (with respect to ε \varepsilon ), so that the corresponding trajectories converge, as ε → 0 \varepsilon\to 0 , to the gradient flow solution of a suitable renormalized energy. Keywords : Fractional Seminorms; Periodic Minimizers; Gradient flows Mathematics Subject Classification : 74N15, 49J45, 35R11



Introduction

Periodic configurations are ubiquitous in nature, crystallization being a paradigmatic example in the physics of solids
[
1
]
. Here we are interested in studying the dynamics - driven by negative fractional semi-norms - towards the equi-spaced ground states for an infinite number of repelling particles on the whole real line, mimicking charged particles screened by a uniform field of opposite charges.

In our analysis, we bypass any compactness issue or boundary effect by working in a periodic setting;
specifically, given
\Lambda\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we consider

\Lambda
-periodic empirical measures
\mu
on the real line that are screened by the Lebesgue measure, so that
\mu([0,\Lambda))=\Lambda
.
To each
\mu
we may associate the potential
u
defined (up to additive constants) by
u^{\prime}=\,\mathrm{d}x-\mu
; notice that, since the particles are screened,
u
is
\Lambda
-periodic.

Given
s\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
we focus on the energy functionals

(0.1)

{\mathscr{E}}^{s}(\mu):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\Lambda}\,\mathrm{d}x\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\,\mathrm{d}y.

Notice that the first integral is computed on the periodicity interval
(0,\Lambda)
, so that the functional can be understood as (
\Lambda
times) the density of the squared
s
-Gagliardo seminorm of
u
per unit length.
We stress that for
s\in[\frac{1}{2},1)
the energy in (
0.1
) is infinite for any empirical measure
\mu
. In order to overcome this issue, we adopt a regularization procedure by considering, for every
\varepsilon>0
, the energy functional

(0.2)

{\mathscr{E}}^{s}_{\varepsilon}(\mu):={\mathscr{E}}^{s}(\mu\ast\rho_{\varepsilon});

here
\rho_{\varepsilon}(\cdot):=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\rho(\frac{\cdot}{\varepsilon})
where
\rho
is a standard positive mollifier with support in
(-1,1)
.

The first result of the paper is that the global minimizers of the functionals
{\mathscr{E}}^{s}(\mu)
and
{\mathscr{E}}^{s}_{\varepsilon}(\mu)
are given by the equi-spaced configurations with lattice spacing equal to one. This is a consequence of the fact that the energy functionals are convex with respect to the mutual distances between the particles. More precisely, for
s\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
the energy functionals are smooth in the space of all configurations without collisions, i.e.,
\mu
contains only Diracs of multiplicity one. For such configurations, which we call regular, the second variation of the energy functionals, with respect to the particle positions, is strictly positive on all directions which are orthogonal to global translations. Notice that for this sub-critical range of the parameter
s
, the energy is finite (and continuous) on all configurations of particles, but we prove that splitting any cluster of multiple particles decreases the energy, so that the global minimizer is a regular configuration. By convexity such a global minimizer is the unique - up to translations - critical point of the energy. By symmetry arguments the equi-spaced configuration is a critical point, so that it is the only critical point and it coincides with the ground state of the energy. The situation for the super-critical cases
s\in[\frac{1}{2},1)
is very similar, up to the fact that the regularized energy functionals are regular everywhere,
and that we are able to prove the positiveness of the second variation only for configurations in which the mutual distances between the particles is at least of order
\varepsilon
. As a consequence, we still have that the equi-spaced lattice is the only ground state, but we cannot exclude the presence of high energy stable configurations of closed packets of particles.

Then, we focus on the dynamics driven by the gradient flow of the energy towards the equi-spaced ground states. Such an analysis
consists in proving that the trajectories of the particles avoid collisions. Let us discuss first the sub-critical case
s\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
.
At a first glance, one could think that collisions are local maximum points of the energy, so that they are excluded by the simple fact that the energy decreases in time. Reality is a little more subtle: Since moving a cluster of particles could decrease the energy, there are trajectories where some of the particles collapse and translate remaining stack on each other, and such that the total energy decreases in time; in order to exclude that these trajectories are solutions of the gradient flow, we have to show that, before the collision, splitting the particles provides a direction of steeper descent for the energy, so that the steepest descent directions of the energy landscape never lead to collisions. The situation is somehow easier in the supercritical case, where collisions cost an amount of energy that blows up as
\varepsilon\to 0
.
More precisely,
Proposition
3.3
provides the leading term
\sigma^{s}(\varepsilon)
(defined in (
3.2
)) of the blowing up energy
{\mathscr{E}}_{\varepsilon}^{s}
induced by a single particle as
\varepsilon\to 0
.
On the other hand, the energy cost of a singularity with multiplicity
m
behaves as
m^{2}\sigma^{s}(\varepsilon)
,
so that configurations with multiple singularities are clearly less favorable than regular ones.
As a consequence, we have that, for any regular initial condition, for
\varepsilon
small enough the trajectories avoid collisions and then converge to a ground state. Again, for initial data with closed packets of particles, we cannot exclude the presence of high energy stationary configurations.

Finally, we show that in all cases the convergence to the equilibrium is exponentially fast. To this end, we stress that the energy functionals are invariant with respect to global translations so that the “periodic barycenter” (in the sense of definition (
4.27
)) of the evolving configuration remains constant along the motion. Therefore, the convergence rate is determined by the first eigenvalue of the Hessian of the energy restricted to the space orthogonal to global translations.

In Propositions
2.2
(for
s\in(0,\frac{1}{2}]
) and
2.10
(for
s\in[\frac{1}{2},1)
) we prove that such an eigenvalue is bounded from above by
\Gamma(s)\Lambda^{-2s}
for some positive constant
\Gamma(s)
, for the equi-spaced configurations, and, for generic configurations, from below by
\gamma(s)\Lambda^{-2s}
for some constant
\gamma(s)\leq\Gamma(s)
. As a consequence, the - optimal in
\Lambda
- convergence rate is proportional to
\Lambda^{-2s}
.

A natural question is what happens (for
s\geq\frac{1}{2}
) to the energy functionals
{\mathscr{E}}_{\varepsilon}^{s}
and to the corresponding gradient flows as
\varepsilon\to 0
.
First, the energies
{\mathscr{W}}_{\varepsilon}^{s}:={\mathscr{E}}_{\varepsilon}^{s}-\Lambda\sigma^{s}(\varepsilon)
converge locally uniformly in the space of regular configurations to a renormalized energy
{\mathscr{W}}_{0}^{s}
(as
\varepsilon\to 0
). Moreover, while the energies
{\mathscr{E}}_{\varepsilon}^{s}
(for
s\in[\frac{1}{2},1)
) blow up as
\varepsilon\to 0
, their gradients (namely the slopes) stay locally uniformly bounded with respect to
\varepsilon
. As a consequence, the gradient flows of
{\mathscr{E}}^{s}_{\varepsilon}
(or equivalently of
{\mathscr{W}}_{\varepsilon}^{s}
) converge, as
\varepsilon\to 0
, to the solution of the gradient flow of the
renormalized energy

{\mathscr{W}}_{0}^{s}
.

The case
s=\frac{1}{2}
is of particular interest in the literature, since it corresponds to a Dirichlet-like bulk energy induced by a
\Lambda
-periodic distribution of topological singularities lying on a straight line in the two dimensional plane.
In this respect, the energy functional
{\mathscr{W}}_{0}^{s}
corresponds to the renormalized energy computed in the context of the Ginzburg-Landau vortices;
as shown in
[
2
]
, such a renormalized energy is given, up to additive and multiplicative constants depending only on
\Lambda
, by

-\sum_{\lambda\neq\lambda^{\prime}}\log|\sin(\pi(x_{\lambda}-x_{\lambda^{\prime}}))|,

and, in view of the convexity of the interaction potentials, its minimizers are the equi-spaced configurations. The renormalized energy has been introduced in several contexts also for infinite non-periodic configurations and it has been shown that the equi-spaced configuration, as well as any compact perturbation of it, is a ground state
[
2
,
7
,
12
,
13
]
. In particular, in
[
13
]
the renormalized energy represents the energy per unit length induced by a system of screened charged particles lying on a straight line in the plane, once the infinite self energy of each particle is removed.

For the critical case
s=\frac{1}{2}
the energy functional in (
0.2
) can be seen as a “positive
\varepsilon
” version of the renormalized energy considered in
[
13
]
for a periodic distribution of screened charges. Precisely, the potential
u
is the trace of the harmonic conjugate of the potential generated by
\,\mathrm{d}x-\mu
, so that the functional
{\mathscr{E}}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mu)
represents the infinite energy density induced by the electric field generated by the screened particles, while
{\mathscr{E}}_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mu)
is its finite counterpart when the particles are diffused on the scale
\varepsilon
. More generally, for
s\in(0,1)
, our functionals are closely related to (one-dimensional) Riesz gases which have received considerable attention in the past few years, see
[
8
,
14
]
.
In such a generality, we show that the minimizers are still the equi-spaced configurations also for
\varepsilon>0
(see
[
12
]
for the case
\varepsilon=0
) and that such ground states are attractors for the dynamics.

The emergence of periodic structures as a result of minimization of convex functionals has been much investigated in the last decades. In
[
9
]

the minimization of the square of the
L^{2}
norm has been considered, among functions having two opposite slopes. Such a result has been generalized in
[
11
]
to the case of two, possibly different, slopes.
The case of fractional
\frac{1}{2}
-Gagliardo seminorm has been considered in
[
6
]
, again for functions with equal opposite slope; their approach relies on a technique referred to as
reflection positivity
for which such a symmetry assumption is somehow required.
In the aforementioned results, the functionals under minimization contains also a term penalizing the jumps of the slopes, which is multiplied by a certain (small) parameter. It is such a parameter, say
\delta
, that dictates the periodicity scale, that is proportional to
\delta^{\frac{1}{3}}
in the “local” case of the
L^{2}
norm and to
\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}
for the
\frac{1}{2}
-Gagliardo seminorm.
We stress that, for the “local”
L^{2}
-energy, the periodicity of minimizers (with the same scale) is proven also when the sharp penalization on the slope-jump is replaced by a Modica-Mortola functional.

In this paper we have adopted a more rigid approach: the slopes of the order parameter
u
are either
1
or
-\infty
and, instead of a term penalizing the jumps of the slope, we have assumed that the region where the slope is
-\infty
is quantized (the Dirac delta’s have positive integer weights).
This is strongly related to the framework considered in
[
3
]
,

where a
model for misfit dislocations at semi-coherent interfaces has been introduced and the optimality of equi-spaced dislocations has been proved. Since that model was one of the motivations for the present analysis, it is convenient to briefly describe the formalism in
[
3
]
. In turn, the model studied there is motivated by the modeling of misfit dislocations by van der Merwe in
[
15
]
. He considered semi-coherent straight interfaces between two parallel square lattices with different spacing. In his analysis it is tacitly assumed, as a well understood and unanimously accepted truth (as in the celebrated Read-Shockley paper
[
10
]
for small angle grain boundaries), that dislocations are periodically distributed along the interface; the optimal profile of the displacement corresponding to the periodic distribution of dislocations is provided and the corresponding interfacial energy is computed. Since the precise shape of the optimal profile only gives lower order corrections in the asymptotics of the energy density as the regularizing parameter
\varepsilon\to 0
, in
[
3
]
a simplified model is introduced, where the transition is prescribed in a simple, non optimal way; namely, considering functions with two given slopes: A “small” positive slope of order
1
, accommodating elastically the lattice misfits, and a “big” negative slope of order
-1/\varepsilon
, providing the transition at the core length scale of the dislocation. Then, the elastic energy density induced by the resulting trace is given, up to pre-factors, by the (square of the)
\frac{1}{2}
-fractional seminorm, and the minimality of the equi-spaced configuration of dislocations is proved. A related model for semi-coherent interfaces has previously been introduced and analyzed by
\Gamma
-convergence in
[
4
]
; there, only the asymptotic (in the semi-coherent limit) uniform distribution of dislocations is proved, together with the periodicity for minimizers of a suitable renormalized energy, corresponding to
\varepsilon=0
.

The main novelty of our analysis with respect to the results in
[
3
]
is that we also consider here the dynamics of misfit dislocations driven by the gradient flow of the induced elastic energy. To this end, it is convenient to replace the large negative slopes
with concentrated negative slopes represented by the empirical measure
\mu
.
On the one hand, this formalism yields after mollification
merely a different (still non-optimal) profile; on the other hand, it fits naturally within a canonical framework based on the formalism of empirical measures, which is well-suited for studying the dynamics.

1.
Setting of the problem

In this section we introduce the fractional seminorm energies and the class of admissible functions we will deal with.

1.1.
Function spaces

Let
\Lambda\in{\mathbb{N}}
be fixed.
We set

\mathcal{X}^{\Lambda}:=[0,\Lambda)^{\Lambda}.

Notice that the entries of a generic configuration
X\in\mathcal{X}^{\Lambda}
are not required to be distinct.
For every
X=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{\Lambda})\in\mathcal{X}^{\Lambda}
we will denote by
X^{\mathrm{ord}}=(x^{\mathrm{ord}}_{1},\ldots,x^{\mathrm{ord}}_{\Lambda})
the
non-decreasing reordering
of
X
, namely the vector in
\mathcal{X}^{\Lambda}
obtained permuting the entries of
X
is such a way that
x^{\mathrm{ord}}_{\lambda}\leq x^{\mathrm{ord}}_{\lambda+1}
for every
\lambda=1,\ldots,\Lambda-1
.

Let
X\in\mathcal{X}^{\Lambda}
. We define
{\mathcal{S}}(X):=\{\xi\in[0,\Lambda)\,:\,x_{\lambda}=\xi\textrm{ for some }\lambda=1,\ldots,\Lambda\}
.
To every
\xi\in{\mathcal{S}}(X)
we associate the set
\mathcal{I}(\xi)
of the indices
\lambda\in\{1,\ldots,\Lambda\}
such that
x_{\lambda}=\xi
and we define the
multiplicity
\widehat{m}(\xi)
of
\xi
as
\widehat{m}(\xi):=\sharp\mathcal{I}(\xi)
.
Notice that if
\widehat{m}(\xi)=1
, then
\mathcal{I}(\xi)
is made of one element, that will be denoted by
\lambda(\xi)
.
Moreover, if
X=X^{\mathrm{ord}}
, to any
\xi\in{\mathcal{S}}(X)
we can
associate
the first index
\phi(\xi)
for which
\xi
is an entry of
X
, i.e.,

\phi(\xi):=\min\{\lambda=1,\ldots,\Lambda\,:\,x_{\lambda}=\xi\}
;
notice that, in such a case,

\widehat{m}(\xi)=\max\{m=1,\ldots,\Lambda\,:\,x_{\phi(\xi)+m-1}=\xi\}
.

We define the set of regular configurations
{\mathcal{R}}^{\Lambda}
as

{\mathcal{R}}^{\Lambda}:=\{X\in\mathcal{X}^{\Lambda}\,:\,\widehat{m}(\xi)=1\textrm{ for every }\xi\in{\mathcal{S}}(X)\}.

Notice that
X\in{\mathcal{R}}^{\Lambda}
if and only if
\sharp{\mathcal{S}}(X)=\Lambda
.

For every
X=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{\Lambda})\in\mathcal{X}^{\Lambda}
we denote by
\mathrm{X}
its
\Lambda
-periodic extension
, namely the
\Lambda
-periodic sequence
\mathrm{X}:=\{\mathsf{x}_{z}\}_{z\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
with
\mathsf{x}_{z}=x_{z}
for
z=1,\ldots,\Lambda
.

We denote by
\{e_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda=1,\ldots,\Lambda}
the canonical basis of
{\mathbb{R}}^{\Lambda}
and we set
e:=\Lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{\lambda=1}^{\Lambda}e_{\lambda}
.

Moreover, for all
\tau\in{\mathbb{R}}
we denote by
X+\tau
the element of
\mathcal{X}^{\Lambda}
whose
\Lambda
-periodic extension is
\mathrm{X}+\tau
.

We denote by
\mu^{X}:=\sum_{z\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\delta_{\mathsf{x}_{z}}
the empirical measure associated to the configuration
X
;
notice that
\mu^{X}\equiv\sum_{\xi\in{\mathcal{S}}(X)}\mu^{\xi}
, where,
for every
\xi\in{\mathcal{S}}(X)
, we have set

(1.1)

\mu^{\xi}:=\widehat{m}(\xi)\sum_{z\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\delta_{\xi+\Lambda z}.

Finally, for every
X\in\mathcal{X}^{\Lambda}
, we
introduce the family
\mathcal{AS}(X)
of functions
compatible
with
X
as

\mathcal{AS}(X):=\{u\in BV_{\rm loc}({\mathbb{R}})\,:\,u^{\prime}=\,\mathrm{d}x-\mu^{X}\},

where
u^{\prime}
denotes the distributional derivative of
u
and
\,\mathrm{d}x
is the standard Lebesgue measure.
By construction, the set
\mathcal{AS}(X)
contains functions differing just by additive constants. Since the energy functionals we consider are insensitive to (horizontal and) vertical translations, with a little abuse of notation, we will denote by
u^{X}
the “unique up to additive constants” element of
\mathcal{AS}(X)
.
Trivially,
u^{X}\equiv u^{X^{\mathrm{ord}}}
.

1.2.
The energy functionals

We introduce here the energy functionals that will be used throughout the paper.

The subcritical case
0<s<\frac{1}{2}
.

For every
X\in\mathcal{X}^{\Lambda}
, we set

(1.2)

{\mathscr{E}}^{s}(X):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\Lambda}\,\mathrm{d}x\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\frac{|u^{X}(x)-u^{X}(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\,\mathrm{d}y.

Notice that the energy
{\mathscr{E}}^{s}
is insensitive to global translations, i.e., for every
\tau\in{\mathbb{R}}
,

(1.3)

{\mathscr{E}}^{s}(X+\tau)={\mathscr{E}}^{s}(X);

therefore, whenever it will be convenient, we will assume without loss of generality that
x_{\lambda}>0
for every
\lambda=1,\ldots,\Lambda
.

For every
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
we set
\bar{u}^{X}(x):=\frac{(u^{X})^{+}(x)+(u^{X})^{-}(x)}{2}
, where
(u^{X})^{\pm}(x)
denote the traces of the function
u^{X}
at
x
, so that
\bar{u}^{X}
coincides with
u^{X}
on each of its continuity point. Then, we define

(1.4)

(-\Delta)^{s}u^{X}(x):=2\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\frac{\bar{u}^{X}(x)-u^{X}(y)}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\,\mathrm{d}y,

where the integral is intended in the sense of principal value if
x
is a jump point for
u^{X}
.

The critical and supercritical case
\frac{1}{2}\leq s<1
.

Let
\rho
be a standard mollifier supported in
(-1,1)

and, for any
\varepsilon>0
, let
\rho_{\varepsilon}(\cdot):=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\rho(\frac{\cdot}{\varepsilon})
.
For every Radon measure
\nu
on
{\mathbb{R}}
we set
\nu_{\varepsilon}:=\rho_{\varepsilon}\ast\nu
. The notation
\nu_{\varepsilon\varepsilon}
stands for
\rho_{\varepsilon}\ast\nu_{\varepsilon}=\rho_{\varepsilon}\ast(\rho_{\varepsilon}\ast\nu)
.
For every
X\in\mathcal{X}^{\Lambda}
we define

(1.5)

{\mathscr{E}}_{\varepsilon}^{s}(X):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\Lambda}\,\mathrm{d}x\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\frac{|u_{\varepsilon}^{X}(x)-u_{\varepsilon}^{X}(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\,\mathrm{d}y.

We notice that for all
X\in\mathcal{X}^{\Lambda}
the function
u_{\varepsilon}^{X}
is smooth and bounded, so that its
s
-fractional Laplacian

\displaystyle(-\Delta)^{s}u_{\varepsilon}^{X}(x):=2\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\frac{u_{\varepsilon}^{X}(x)-u_{\varepsilon}^{X}(y)}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\,\mathrm{d}y
is well defined in the sense of principal value.

Remark 1.1
.

Notice that for every
X\in\mathcal{X}^{\Lambda}
the operator in (
1.4
) is well defined, in the sense of principal value, also for
\frac{1}{2}\leq s<1
. Moreover, we have that

(1.6)

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}(-\Delta)^{s}u^{X}_{\varepsilon}(x)=(-\Delta)^{s}u^{X}(x)\qquad\textrm{for every }x\in{\mathbb{R}}.

Finally, for any open set
U\subset\subset{\mathcal{R}}^{\Lambda}
(so that the minimal distance between two particles is uniformly bounded from below) we have that the convergence in (
1.6
) at any
x\in{\mathcal{S}}(X)
is uniform in
U
.

First and second variations

) and (
1.5
). For every

and

.

The subcritical case
0<s<\frac{1}{2}

We start by computing the first and second variations of the energy

\displaystyle{\partial_{x_{\lambda(\xi)}}}{\mathscr{E}}^{s}(X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\int_{I_{h}}\,\mathrm{d}x\int_{{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\overline{I}_{h}}\frac{(u^{X}(x)+\frac{1}{2})-(u^{X}(y)+\frac{1}{2}\chi_{A_{h}}(y))}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,.

The first equality is nothing but computing the difference of squares. The second equality is a consequence of periodicity, using the change of variable
x\mapsto x-\Lambda z
,
y\mapsto y-\Lambda z
, together with Fubini Theorem as follows

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\Lambda}\,\mathrm{d}x\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\frac{-\chi_{A_{h}}(y)\big((2u^{X}(x)+\chi_{A_{h}}(x))-(2u^{X}(y)+\chi_{A_{h}}(y))\big)}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\Lambda}\,\mathrm{d}x\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\frac{\chi_{A_{h}}(y)\big((2u^{X}(y)+\chi_{A_{h}}(y))-(2u^{X}(x)+\chi_{A_{h}}(x))\big)}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{z\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\Lambda}\,\mathrm{d}x\int_{\Lambda z}^{\Lambda(z+1)}\frac{\chi_{A_{h}}(y)\big((2u^{X}(y)+\chi_{A_{h}}(y))-(2u^{X}(x)+\chi_{A_{h}}(x))\big)}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{z\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\frac{1}{2}\int_{-\Lambda z}^{\Lambda(1-z)}\,\mathrm{d}x\int_{0}^{\Lambda}\frac{\chi_{A_{h}}(y)\big((2u^{X}(y)+\chi_{A_{h}}(y))-(2u^{X}(x)+\chi_{A_{h}}(x))\big)}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\Lambda}\,\mathrm{d}y\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\frac{\chi_{A_{h}}(y)\big((2u^{X}(y)+\chi_{A_{h}}(y))-(2u^{X}(x)+\chi_{A_{h}}(x))\big)}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,.

Finally, the third equality in (
2.4
) is immediate, while the last one follows from the fact that

(2.5)

\displaystyle\int_{I_{h}}\,\mathrm{d}x\int_{I_{h}}\frac{(u^{X}(x)+\frac{1}{2})-(u^{X}(y)+\frac{1}{2}\chi_{A_{h}}(y))}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{I_{h}}\,\mathrm{d}x\int_{I_{h}}\frac{u^{X}(x)-u^{X}(y)}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\,\mathrm{d}y=\int_{I_{h}}\,\mathrm{d}x\int_{I_{h}}\frac{x-y}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\,\mathrm{d}y=0.

We set
\xi^{h}:=\xi+\frac{h}{2}
, so that
I_{h}=B_{\frac{h}{2}}(\xi^{h})
. Let
0<\sigma<\min_{\xi^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}(X)\setminus\{\xi\}}|\xi-\xi^{\prime}|
and notice that, for
h
small enough

\displaystyle u^{X}(x)-u^{X}(y)=

\displaystyle\,x-y

\displaystyle\qquad\textrm{if }x\in I_{h},\,\,y\in\Big(\xi_{h}+\frac{h}{2},\xi_{h}+\sigma\Big),

\displaystyle u^{X}(x)-u^{X}(y)=

\displaystyle\,x-y-1

\displaystyle\qquad\textrm{if }x\in I_{h},\,\,y\in\Big(\xi_{h}-\sigma,\xi_{h}-\frac{h}{2}\Big).

Whence we deduce

(2.6)

\displaystyle\int_{I_{h}}\,\mathrm{d}x\int_{B_{\sigma}(\xi^{h})\setminus\overline{I}_{h}}\frac{(u^{X}(x)+\frac{1}{2})-(u^{X}(y)+\frac{1}{2}\chi_{A_{h}}(y))}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\frac{h}{2}}(\xi^{h})}\,\mathrm{d}x\int_{B_{\sigma}(\xi^{h})\setminus\overline{B}_{\frac{h}{2}}(\xi^{h})}\frac{u^{X}(x)+\frac{1}{2}-u^{X}(y)}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\,\mathrm{d}y=0.

Therefore, in view of (
2.4
) and (
2.6
), we deduce

(2.7)

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\mathscr{E}}^{s}(X+he_{\lambda(\xi)})-{\mathscr{E}}^{s}(X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\int_{B_{\frac{h}{2}}(\xi^{h})}\,\mathrm{d}x\int_{{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\overline{B}_{\sigma}(\xi^{h})}\frac{(u^{X}(x)+\frac{1}{2})-(u^{X}(y)+\frac{1}{2}\chi_{A_{h}}(y))}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\int_{B_{\frac{h}{2}}(\xi^{h})}\,\mathrm{d}x\int_{{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\overline{B}_{\sigma}(\xi^{h})}\frac{u^{X}(x)+\frac{1}{2}-u^{X}(y)}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\,\mathrm{d}y+\mathrm{r}(h)\,,

where (for
h
small enough)

(2.8)

\displaystyle r(h):=

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\frac{h}{2}}(\xi^{h})}\,\mathrm{d}x\int_{{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\overline{B}_{\sigma}(\xi^{h})}\frac{\chi_{A_{h}}(y)}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=\,\int_{B_{\frac{h}{2}}(\xi^{h})}\,\mathrm{d}x\sum_{z\in{\mathbb{Z}}\setminus\{0\}}\int_{B_{\frac{h}{2}}(\xi^{h})+\Lambda z}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\,\mathrm{d}y\leq h^{2}\sum_{z\in{\mathbb{Z}}\setminus\{0\}}\Big(\frac{2}{\Lambda|z|}\Big)^{1+2s}.

Let us now consider the change of variable
x^{\prime}=\frac{x-\xi^{h}}{h}
so that
x=\xi^{h}+hx^{\prime}
. By (
2.7
) and (
2.8
), using the Dominated Convergence Theorem, we get

(2.9)

\displaystyle\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{{\mathscr{E}}^{s}(X+he_{\lambda(\xi)})-{\mathscr{E}}^{s}(X)}{h}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{h\to 0}\Big[2\int_{-\frac{1}{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x^{\prime}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\overline{B}_{\sigma}(\xi^{h})}\frac{u^{X}(\xi^{h}+hx^{\prime})+\frac{1}{2}-u^{X}(y)}{|\xi^{h}+hx^{\prime}-y|^{1+2s}}\,\mathrm{d}y+\frac{r(h)}{h}\Big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\int_{-\frac{1}{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x^{\prime}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\overline{B}_{\sigma}(\xi)}\frac{u^{X}(\xi)+\frac{1}{2}-u^{X}(y)}{|\xi-y|^{1+2s}}\,\mathrm{d}y,

which, by the arbitrariness of
\sigma
, and by the very definition in (
1.4
), implies (
2.1
).

Now we prove (
2.2
). By (
2.1
), using the invariance by global translations, we have

(2.10)

\displaystyle\partial^{2}_{x^{2}_{\lambda(\xi)}}{\mathscr{E}}^{s}(X)=

\displaystyle\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{(-\Delta)^{s}u^{X+he_{\lambda(\xi)}}(\xi+h)-(-\Delta)^{s}u^{X}(\xi)}{h}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{(-\Delta)^{s}u^{X-h\hat{e}_{\lambda(\xi)}}(\xi)-(-\Delta)^{s}u^{X}(\xi)}{h},

where
\hat{e}_{\lambda(\xi)}
denotes the vector with the
\lambda(\xi)
-th entry equal to
0
and all the remaining ones equal to
1
.
To simplify notation we consider only the case
h>0
and
we set
\hat{I}_{h}:=\bigcup_{\xi^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}(X)\setminus\{\xi\}}(\xi^{\prime}-h,\xi^{\prime})
and

\hat{A}_{h}:=\hat{I}_{h}+\Lambda{\mathbb{Z}}
.
Then, for
h
small enough,
u^{X-h\hat{e}_{\lambda(\xi)}}\equiv u^{X}-\chi_{\hat{A}_{h}}
.
By the very definition of fractional Laplacian in (
1.4
) and by the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus, we get

\displaystyle\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{(-\Delta)^{s}u^{X-h\hat{e}_{\lambda(\xi)}}(\xi)-(-\Delta)^{s}u^{X}(\xi)}{h}=

\displaystyle\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{2}{h}\int_{\hat{A}_{h}}\frac{1}{|\xi-y|^{1+2s}}\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{z\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\sum_{\xi^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}(X)\setminus\{\xi\}}\frac{2}{|\Lambda z+\xi-\xi^{\prime}|^{1+2s}},

which, together with (
2.10
), yields (
2.2
).

We finally turn to (
2.3
).
By (
2.1
), we have

(2.11)

{\partial^{2}_{x_{\lambda(\xi)}\,x_{\lambda(\eta)}}}{\mathscr{E}}^{s}(X)=\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{(-\Delta)^{s}u^{X+he_{\lambda}(\eta)}(\xi)-(-\Delta)^{s}u^{X}(\xi)}{h}.

To simplify notation we focus on the case
h>0
; for
h
small enough,

(-\Delta)^{s}u^{X+he_{\lambda}(\eta)}=(-\Delta)^{s}u^{X}+(-\Delta)^{s}\chi_{E_{h}},

where we have set
E_{h}:=\cup_{z\in{\mathbb{Z}}}(\eta+\Lambda z,\eta+\Lambda z+h)
. Therefore, by (
2.11
), we obtain

{\partial^{2}_{x_{\lambda(\xi)}\,x_{\lambda(\eta)}}}{\mathscr{E}}^{s}(X)=\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{(-\Delta)^{s}\chi_{E_{h}}(\xi)}{h}=-2\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{1}{h}\int_{E_{h}}\frac{1}{|\xi-y|^{1+2s}}\,\mathrm{d}y=-\sum_{z\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\frac{2}{|\Lambda z+\xi-\eta|^{1+2s}}.

This concludes the proof of (
2.3
).
∎

In the next proposition, we prove that the Hessian
\nabla^{2}{\mathscr{E}}^{s}
is positive definite on
{\mathcal{R}}^{\Lambda}
, providing a lower bound on its first eigenvalue.

We define the class
{\mathcal{C}}^{\Lambda}
of equispaced configurations as

(2.12)

{\mathcal{C}}^{\Lambda}:=\{X\in{\mathcal{R}}^{\Lambda}\,:\,{\mathcal{S}}(X)=\{\tau,1+\tau,\ldots,\Lambda-1+\tau\}\,,\quad\tau\in[0,1)\}.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
0<s<\frac{1}{2}
and
\Lambda\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Then, for every
X\in{\mathcal{R}}^{\Lambda}
with
{\mathcal{S}}(X)\subset(0,\Lambda)
and for every
h\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\Lambda}

(2.13)

\langle\nabla^{2}{\mathscr{E}}^{s}(X)h,h\rangle=\sum_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{1}{\xi,\eta\in\sss(X)}{\xi\neq\eta}}a^{s}(\xi,\eta)(h_{\lambda(\xi)}-h_{\lambda(\eta)})^{2},

where

(2.14)

a^{s}(\xi,\eta):=2\sum_{z\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\frac{1}{|\xi-\eta+\Lambda z|^{1+2s}}\qquad\textrm{for every }\xi,\eta\in{\mathcal{S}}(X)\textrm{ with }\xi\neq\eta.

As a consequence, if
h\cdot e=\sum_{\lambda=1}^{\Lambda}h_{\lambda}=0
, then

(2.15)

\langle\nabla^{2}{\mathscr{E}}^{s}(X)h,h\rangle\geq\gamma(s)\Lambda^{-2s}|h|^{2},

for some positive constant
\gamma(s)
depending only on
s
.

Moreover,
for every
X\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\Lambda}
, there exists
h\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\Lambda}
with
h\neq 0
,
h\cdot e=0
and

(2.16)

\langle\nabla^{2}{\mathscr{E}}^{s}(X)h,h\rangle\leq\Gamma(s)\Lambda^{-2s}|h|^{2},

for some positive constant
\Gamma(s)
depending only on
s
.

Proof.

Identity (
2.13
) is an immediate consequence of Proposition
2.1
.
Now we prove (
2.15
).
By definition, for every
\xi,\eta\in{\mathcal{S}}(X)
with
\xi\neq\eta
we have

(2.17)

a^{s}(\xi,\eta)\geq\gamma(s)\Lambda^{-1-2s}.

Now, since

\sum_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{1}{\lambda,\lambda'\in\{1,\ldots,\Lambda\}}{\lambda\neq\lambda'}}(h_{\lambda}-h_{\lambda^{\prime}})^{2}=(\Lambda-1)\sum_{\lambda=1}^{\Lambda}h^{2}_{\lambda}-2\sum_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{1}{\lambda,\lambda'\in\{1,\ldots,\Lambda\}}{\lambda\neq\lambda'}}h_{\lambda}h_{\lambda^{\prime}}

and

\Big(\sum_{\lambda=1}^{\Lambda}h_{\lambda}\Big)^{2}=\sum_{\lambda=1}^{\Lambda}h^{2}_{\lambda}+2\sum_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{1}{\lambda,\lambda'\in\{1,\ldots,\Lambda\}}{\lambda\neq\lambda'}}h_{\lambda}h_{\lambda^{\prime}},

we find that for every
h\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\Lambda}
with
h\cdot e=0
,

(2.18)

\sum_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{1}{\lambda,\lambda'\in\{1,\ldots,\Lambda\}}{\lambda\neq\lambda'}}(h_{\lambda}-h_{\lambda^{\prime}})^{2}=\Lambda\sum_{\lambda=1}^{\Lambda}h^{2}_{\lambda}.

Thus, by (
2.17
) and (
2.18
), we have

(2.19)

\sum_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{1}{\xi,\eta\in\sss(X)}{\xi\neq\eta}}a^{s}(\xi,\eta)(h_{\lambda(\xi)}-h_{\lambda(\eta)})^{2}\geq\gamma(s)\Lambda^{-1-2s}\sum_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{1}{\lambda,\lambda'\in\{1,\ldots,\Lambda\}}{\lambda\neq\lambda'}}(h_{\lambda}-h_{\lambda^{\prime}})^{2}=\gamma(s)\Lambda^{-2s}|h|^{2},

whence the claim follows by (
2.13
).

Now we prove the upper bound.
To this end let

(2.20)

|\cdot|_{\rm per}:=\min_{z\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|\cdot+\Lambda z|

denote the (
\Lambda
-)periodic norm on
{\mathbb{R}}
.
We define the vector
h\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\Lambda}
by setting

h_{\lambda}:=|\lambda|_{\rm per}-\frac{1}{\Lambda}\sum_{\lambda^{\prime}=0}^{\Lambda-1}|\lambda^{\prime}|_{\rm per}.

By construction
h\cdot e=0
.
On the one hand we have

|h|^{2}\geq C\Lambda^{3}.

On the other hand, since

a^{s}(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})\leq\Gamma(s)\frac{1}{|\lambda-\lambda^{\prime}|_{\rm per}^{1+2s}},

we have

\displaystyle\langle\nabla^{2}{\mathscr{E}}^{s}(X)h,h\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\Gamma(s)\sum_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{1}{\lambda,\lambda'\in\{0,1,\ldots,\Lambda-1\}}{\lambda\neq\lambda'}}\frac{(|\lambda|_{\rm per}-|\lambda^{\prime}|_{\rm per})^{2}}{|\lambda-\lambda^{\prime}|_{\rm per}^{1+2s}}

\displaystyle\leq\Gamma(s)\sum_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{1}{\lambda,\lambda'\in\{0,1,\ldots,\Lambda-1\}}{\lambda\neq\lambda'}}|\lambda-\lambda^{\prime}|_{\rm per}^{1-2s}

\displaystyle\leq\Gamma(s)\Lambda^{3-2s}.

Combining both estimates together yields (
2.16
).

∎

Remark 2.3
.

Let us point out that in order to prove (
2.16
), being
0<s<1/2
, it would have been enough to consider the interpolating function
\hat{h}
to be piecewise constant instead of piecewise affine.
The advantage of introducing piecewise affine functions is that
they belong to
H^{s}
also for
s\geq 1/2
, covering the whole range of exponents
0<s<1
.
In fact, following along the lines of the proof of Proposition
2.2
,
we have immediately that, also for
\frac{1}{2}\leq s<1

(2.21)

\sum_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{1}{\xi,\eta\in\sss(X)}{\xi\neq\eta}}a^{s}(\xi,\eta)\big(h_{\lambda(\xi)}-h_{\lambda(\eta)}\big)^{2}\geq\gamma(s)\Lambda^{-2s}|h|^{2}\textrm{ for every }X\in{\mathcal{R}}^{\Lambda},\,\,h\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\Lambda}\textrm{ with }h\cdot e=0

and

(2.22)

\sum_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{1}{\xi,\eta\in\sss(X)}{\xi\neq\eta}}a^{s}(\xi,\eta)\big(h_{\lambda(\xi)}-h_{\lambda(\eta)}\big)^{2}\leq\Gamma(s)\Lambda^{-2s}|h|^{2}\textrm{ for every }X\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\Lambda},\,\,\textrm{for some }h\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\Lambda}\textrm{ with }h\cdot e=0,

where the coefficients
a^{s}(\xi,\eta)
are defined in (
2.14
).

Arguing verbatim as in the proof of (
2.1
) in Proposition
2.1
we get its
extension to the case of multiple singularities (we recall that
\mathcal{I}(\xi)
is the set of indices of the particles at position
\xi
and
\widehat{m}(\xi)
is its cardinality).

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
0<s<\frac{1}{2}
and let
X\in\mathcal{X}^{\Lambda}
; then, given
\{h_{\xi}\}_{\xi\in{\mathcal{S}}(X)}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{\Lambda}
, we have

{\mathscr{E}}^{s}\Big(X+\sum_{\xi\in{\mathcal{S}}(X)}h_{\xi}\sum_{m\in\mathcal{I}(\xi)}e_{m}\Big)=\sum_{\xi\in{\mathcal{S}}(X)}h_{\xi}\widehat{m}(\xi)(-\Delta)^{s}u^{X}(\xi)+\mathrm{o}\Big(\sum_{\xi\in{\mathcal{S}}(X)}|h_{\xi}|\Big)\,.

The next lemma shows that splitting multiple particles gives a direction with infinite slope for the energy.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
0<s<\frac{1}{2}
and let
X\in\mathcal{X}^{\Lambda}\setminus{\mathcal{R}}^{\Lambda}
. Let
\xi\in{\mathcal{S}}(X)
be such that
\widehat{m}(\xi)\geq 2
. Then, for every
m\in\mathcal{I}(\xi)
, we have

\partial_{x_{m}}^{\pm}{\mathscr{E}}^{s}(X)=\mp\infty.

Proof.

Fix
m\in\mathcal{I}(\xi)
. By construction, for
|h|
small enough,
u^{X+he_{m}}=u^{X}+\text{sgn}(h)\chi_{I_{h}}
, where
I_{h}
is the (for instance open) interval with extreme points
\xi
and
\xi+h
.
We set
A_{h}:=I_{h}+\Lambda{\mathbb{Z}}
.
To simplify notation we consider only the case
h>0
. By arguing verbatim as in (
2.4
) we have

(2.23)

{\mathscr{E}}^{s}(X+he_{m})-{\mathscr{E}}^{s}(X)=2\int_{I_{h}}\,\mathrm{d}x\int_{{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\overline{I}_{h}}\frac{u^{X}(x)+\frac{1}{2}-\big(u^{X}(y)+\frac{1}{2}\chi_{A_{h}}(y)\big)}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\,\mathrm{d}y.

We set
\xi^{h}:=\xi+\frac{h}{2}
, so that
I_{h}=B_{\frac{h}{2}}(\xi^{h})
. Let
0<\sigma<\min_{\xi^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}(X)\setminus\{\xi\}}|\xi-\xi^{\prime}|
; we notice that, for
h
small enough

\displaystyle u^{X}(x)-u^{X}(y)=

\displaystyle\,x-y

\displaystyle\qquad\textrm{if }x\in I_{h},\,y\in\Big(\xi_{h}+\frac{h}{2},\xi_{h}+\sigma\Big),

\displaystyle u^{X}(x)-u^{X}(y)=

\displaystyle\,x-y-\widehat{m}(\xi)

\displaystyle\qquad\textrm{if }x\in I_{h},\,y\in\Big(\xi_{h}-\sigma,\xi_{h}-\frac{h}{2}\Big),

whence we deduce

(2.24)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\frac{h}{2}}(\xi^{h})}\,\mathrm{d}x\int_{B_{\sigma}(\xi^{h})\setminus\overline{B}_{\frac{h}{2}}(\xi^{h})}\frac{(u^{X}(x)+\frac{1}{2})-(u^{X}(y)+\frac{1}{2}\chi_{A_{h}}(y))}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\frac{h}{2}}(\xi^{h})}\,\mathrm{d}x\int_{B_{\sigma}(\xi^{h})\setminus\overline{B}_{\frac{h}{2}}(\xi^{h})}\frac{u^{X}(x)+\frac{1}{2}-u^{X}(y)}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-\widehat{m}(\xi))\int_{B_{\frac{h}{2}}(\xi^{h})}\,\mathrm{d}x\int_{\xi^{h}-\sigma}^{\xi^{h}-\frac{h}{2}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-\widehat{m}(\xi))\frac{1}{2s(1-2s)}\Big(h^{1-2s}-\Big(\sigma+\frac{h}{2}\Big)^{1-2s}+\Big(\sigma-\frac{h}{2}\Big)^{1-2s}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-\widehat{m}(\xi))\frac{h^{1-2s}}{2s(1-2s)}+\mathrm{O}(h).

Finally, since the numerator in the integral below is in
L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
,
one can easily prove that

\int_{B_{\frac{h}{2}}(\xi^{h})}\,\mathrm{d}x\int_{{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\overline{B}_{\sigma}(\xi^{h})}\frac{(u^{X}(x)+\frac{1}{2})-(u^{X}(y)+\frac{1}{2}\chi_{A_{h}}(y))}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\,\mathrm{d}y=\mathrm{O}(h),

which, together with (
2.23
) and (
2.24
), yields

{\mathscr{E}}^{s}(X+he_{m})-{\mathscr{E}}^{s}(X)=(1-\widehat{m}(\xi))\frac{h^{1-2s}}{s(1-2s)}+\mathrm{O}(h),

whence the claim follows, since
\widehat{m}(\xi)\geq 2
.
∎

Now, according with Lemma
2.5
, we prove that separating at least one singularity from a close packed cluster of singularities decreases the energy.
For every pair of sets
A,B\subset{\mathbb{R}}
, we set

(2.25)

{\mathscr{I}}^{s}(A,B):=\int_{A}\,\mathrm{d}x\int_{B}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\,\mathrm{d}y.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
0<s<\frac{1}{2}
,
\sigma>0
and let
X\in{\mathcal{R}}^{\Lambda}
. Let
\mathcal{Q}:=\{\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{K}\}\subset{\mathcal{S}}(X)
with
K\geq 2
and
0<\xi_{k}\leq\xi_{k+1}
(for all
k=1,\ldots,K-1
) be such that

\min_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{1}{\xi\in\sss(X)\setminus\qs}{k=1,\ldots,K}}\{|\xi-\xi_{k}|,\,|\xi-\xi_{k}-\Lambda|,\,|\xi-\xi_{k}+\Lambda|\}\geq\sigma\,.

Then, setting
\delta:=\xi_{K}-\xi_{1}
, we have

\nabla{\mathscr{E}}^{s}(X)\cdot e_{\lambda(\xi_{K})}\leq\frac{1-K}{2s}\delta^{-2s}+C(\Lambda,s,\sigma),

for some constant
C(\Lambda,s,\sigma)
(independent of
\delta
).

Proof.

Let
h>0
. We set
I_{h}:=(\xi_{K},\xi_{K}+h)
and
A_{h}:=I_{h}+\Lambda{\mathbb{Z}}
. By (
2.4
), for
h
small enough we have

(2.26)

{\mathscr{E}}^{s}(X+he_{\lambda(\xi_{K})})-{\mathscr{E}}^{s}(X)=2\int_{I_{h}}\,\mathrm{d}x\int_{{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\overline{I}_{h}}\frac{(u^{X}(x)+\frac{1}{2})-(u^{X}(y)+\frac{1}{2}\chi_{A_{h}}(y))}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\,\mathrm{d}y,

and

(2.27)

\int_{I_{h}}\,\mathrm{d}x\int_{{\mathbb{R}}\setminus[\xi_{1}-\sigma,\xi_{K}+\sigma]}\frac{(u^{X}(x)+\frac{1}{2})-(u^{X}(y)+\frac{1}{2}\chi_{A_{h}}(y))}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\,\mathrm{d}y\leq C(\Lambda,\sigma)h\sum_{z\in{\mathbb{Z}}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{1}{|z|^{1+2s}}.

Therefore, setting

E^{s}_{\delta}[h]:=\int_{I_{h}}\,\mathrm{d}x\int_{(\xi_{1}-\sigma,\xi_{K}+\sigma)\setminus\overline{I}_{h}}\frac{(u^{X}(x)+\frac{1}{2})-(u^{X}(y)+\frac{1}{2}\chi_{A_{h}}(y))}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\,\mathrm{d}y,

in order to prove the claim, it is enough to show that

(2.28)

\limsup_{h\to 0^{+}}\frac{E^{s}_{\delta}[h]}{h}\leq\frac{1-K}{2s}\delta^{-2s}+C(s,\sigma)\,.

We first write (for
h
small enough)
E^{s}_{\delta}[h]:=E^{s}_{\delta,1}[h]+E^{s}_{\delta,2}[h]
, where

\displaystyle E^{s}_{\delta,1}[h]=

\displaystyle\int_{I_{h}}\,\mathrm{d}x\int_{\xi_{1}}^{\xi_{K}}\frac{u^{X}(x)+\frac{1}{2}-u^{X}(y)}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\,\mathrm{d}y+\int_{I_{h}}\,\mathrm{d}x\int_{\xi_{K}+h}^{\xi_{K}+\delta}\frac{u^{X}(x)+\frac{1}{2}-u^{X}(y)}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\,\mathrm{d}y,

\displaystyle E^{s}_{\delta,2}[h]:=

\displaystyle\int_{I_{h}}\,\mathrm{d}x\int_{\xi_{1}-\sigma}^{\xi_{1}}\frac{u^{X}(x)+\frac{1}{2}-u^{X}(y)}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\,\mathrm{d}y+\int_{I_{h}}\,\mathrm{d}x\int_{\xi_{K}+\delta}^{\xi_{K}+\sigma}\frac{u^{X}(x)+\frac{1}{2}-u^{X}(y)}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\,\mathrm{d}y.

Now we observe that

(2.29)

\displaystyle u^{X}(x)-u^{X}(y)=

\displaystyle\,-K+x-y\qquad

\displaystyle\textrm{whenever }x\in I_{h},y\in(\xi_{1}-\sigma,\xi_{1}),

(2.30)

\displaystyle u^{X}(x)-u^{X}(y)\leq

\displaystyle\,-1+x-y\qquad

\displaystyle\textrm{whenever }x\in I_{h},y\in(\xi_{1},\xi_{K})

(2.31)

\displaystyle u^{X}(x)-u^{X}(y)=

\displaystyle\,x-y\qquad

\displaystyle\textrm{whenever }x\in I_{h},y\in(\xi_{K}+h,\xi_{K}+\sigma)\,.

in (

\displaystyle E^{s}_{\delta,1}[h]\leq

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}{\mathscr{I}}^{s}(I_{h},(\xi_{1},\xi_{K}))+\frac{1}{2}{\mathscr{I}}^{s}(I_{h},(\xi_{K}+h,\xi_{K}+\delta))

\displaystyle+\int_{I_{h}}\,\mathrm{d}x\int_{\xi_{1}}^{\xi_{K}}(x-y)^{-2s}\,\mathrm{d}y-\int_{I_{h}}\,\mathrm{d}x\int_{\xi_{K}+h}^{\xi_{K}+\delta}(y-x)^{-2s}\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{2s(1-2s)}\big(h^{1-2s}+\delta^{1-2s}-(h+\delta)^{1-2s}\big)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{2s(1-2s)}\big(h^{1-2s}+(\delta-h)^{1-2s}-\delta^{1-2s}\big)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{(1-2s)(2-2s)}\big(-h^{2-2s}-\delta^{2-2s}+(h+\delta)^{2-2s}\big)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{(1-2s)(2-2s)}\big(-h^{2-2s}-(\delta-h)^{2-2s}+\delta^{2-2s}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{2s(1-2s)}\big((\delta+h)^{1-2s}+(\delta-h)^{1-2s}-2\delta^{1-2s}\big)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{(1-2s)(2-2s)}\big((h+\delta)^{2-2s}+(\delta-h)^{2-2s}-2\delta^{2-2s}\big)

\displaystyle\mathrm{O}(h^{2}),

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{1}{2}-K\Big){\mathscr{I}}^{s}(I_{h},(\xi_{1}-\sigma,\xi_{1}))+\frac{1}{2}{\mathscr{I}}^{s}(I_{h},(\xi_{K}+\delta,\xi_{K}+\sigma))

\displaystyle+\int_{I_{h}}\,\mathrm{d}x\int_{\xi_{1}-\sigma}^{\xi_{1}}(x-y)^{-2s}\,\mathrm{d}y-\int_{I_{h}}\,\mathrm{d}x\int_{\xi_{K}+\delta}^{\xi_{K}+\sigma}(y-x)^{-2s}\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{2s(1-2s)}\big((\sigma-h)^{1-2s}-\sigma^{1-2s}+\delta^{1-2s}-(\delta-h)^{1-2s}\big)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{(1-2s)(2-2s)}\big(-(\delta+h)^{2-2s}+(h+\delta+\sigma)^{2-2s}+\delta^{2-2s}-(\delta+\sigma)^{2-2s}\big)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{(1-2s)(2-2s)}\big(-(\sigma-h)^{2-2s}+(\delta-h)^{2-2s}+\sigma^{2-2s}-\delta^{2-2s}\big).

\displaystyle-(h+\delta+\sigma)^{1-2s}+(\delta+\sigma)^{1-2s}-\delta^{1-2s}+(\delta+h)^{1-2s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(1-2s)(\delta+\sigma)^{-2s}h+(1-2s)\delta^{-2s}h+\mathrm{O}(h^{2});

\displaystyle(\sigma-h)^{1-2s}-\sigma^{1-2s}+\delta^{1-2s}-(\delta-h)^{1-2s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(1-2s)\sigma^{-2s}h+(1-2s)\delta^{-2s}h+\mathrm{O}(h^{2});

\displaystyle-(\delta+h)^{2-2s}+(h+\delta+\sigma)^{2-2s}+\delta^{2-2s}-(\delta+\sigma)^{2-2s}

\displaystyle-(2-2s)\delta^{1-2s}h+(2-2s)(\delta+\sigma)^{1-2s}h+\mathrm{O}(h^{2});

\displaystyle-(\sigma-h)^{2-2s}+(\delta-h)^{2-2s}+\sigma^{2-2s}-\delta^{2-2s}

\displaystyle(2-2s)\sigma^{1-2s}h-(2-2s)\delta^{1-2s}h+\mathrm{O}(h^{2}).

\displaystyle E^{s}_{\delta,2}[h]=

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{1}{2}-K\Big)\frac{h}{2s}\big(\delta^{-2s}-(\delta+\sigma)^{-2s}\big)+\frac{1}{2}\frac{h}{2s}\big(\delta^{-2s}-\sigma^{-2s}\big)

\displaystyle+\frac{h}{1-2s}\big((\delta+\sigma)^{1-2s}-\sigma^{1-2s}\big)+\mathrm{O}(h^{2})

\displaystyle(1-K)\frac{h}{2s}\delta^{-2s}+(K-1)\frac{h}{2s}\sigma^{-2s}+\frac{h}{1-2s}(\Lambda+\sigma)^{1-2s}+\mathrm{O}(h^{2})

\displaystyle(1-K)\frac{h}{2s}\delta^{-2s}+C(\Lambda,s,\sigma)h.

By (

3.

By symmetry arguments it is straightforward to check that the configurations in

\varepsilon
small enough, we get

(3.9)

\displaystyle{\mathscr{E}}^{s}_{\varepsilon}(X)\geq

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{K}\widehat{m}^{2}(\xi_{k})\Big(\sigma^{s}(\varepsilon)+\frac{2-2^{1-2s}}{2s(2s-1)}r^{1-2s}+\mathrm{O}_{r}(\varepsilon)\Big)-2\Lambda C(s)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\Lambda\sigma^{s}(\varepsilon)+\Lambda\frac{1}{2s(2s-1)}r^{1-2s}+\mathrm{O}_{r}(\varepsilon)-2\Lambda C(s),

where the last inequality follows using that
1-2s<0
and that

(3.10)

\sum_{k=1}^{K}\widehat{m}^{2}(\xi_{k})\geq\Lambda.

If
\delta(X)=0
, then the inequality (
3.10
) is strict, whence (
3.3
) immediately follows.

Assume now that
\delta(X)>0
; since
r<\delta(X)
,
taking the
\liminf
as
\varepsilon\to 0
in (
3.9
), we have

\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\Big(\inf_{X\in\mathcal{X}^{\Lambda}:\delta(X)\leq\delta}{\mathscr{E}}^{s}_{\varepsilon}(X)-\Lambda\sigma^{s}(\varepsilon)\Big)\geq\Lambda\frac{1}{2s(2s-1)}r^{1-2s}-2\Lambda C(s).

This concludes the proof of (
3.3
) for
s>\frac{1}{2}
.

Now, we discuss the case
s=\frac{1}{2}
.

To this end, we preliminarily observe that for every
0<\gamma<\bar{\gamma}
it holds

\int_{-\bar{\gamma}}^{-\gamma}\,\mathrm{d}x\int_{\gamma}^{\bar{\gamma}}(y-x)^{-2}\,\mathrm{d}y=2\log(\gamma+\bar{\gamma})-\log(2\gamma)-\log(2\bar{\gamma})=\log\frac{\gamma+\bar{\gamma}}{\gamma}+\log\frac{\gamma+\bar{\gamma}}{\bar{\gamma}}-2\log 2,

thus yielding

(3.11)

\lim_{\frac{\gamma}{\bar{\gamma}}\to 0}\Big(\int_{-\bar{\gamma}}^{-\gamma}\,\mathrm{d}x\int_{\gamma}^{\bar{\gamma}}(y-x)^{-2}\,\mathrm{d}y-\log\frac{\bar{\gamma}}{\gamma}\Big)=-2\log 2.

Analogously, one can check that

(3.12)

\int_{-\bar{\gamma}}^{-\gamma}\,\mathrm{d}x\int_{\gamma}^{\bar{\gamma}}(y-x)^{-1}\,\mathrm{d}y=2\gamma(1+\log(2\gamma))-2\bar{\gamma}(1-\log(2\bar{\gamma}))-2(\gamma+\bar{\gamma})\log(\gamma+\bar{\gamma}).

Therefore, by combining (
3.6
) together with (
3.11
), (
3.12
) (applied with
\gamma=\varepsilon
and
\bar{\gamma}=r
) and with (
3.10
), for

r
fixed
and
\varepsilon
small enough we deduce that

(3.13)

I_{\varepsilon,r,\mathrm{sr}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\geq\sum_{k=1}^{K}\widehat{m}^{2}(\xi_{k})\log\frac{r}{\varepsilon}-C_{r}(\varepsilon)\geq\Lambda\log\frac{r}{\varepsilon}-C_{r}(\varepsilon),

where
C_{r}(\varepsilon)
satisfies
\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}C_{r}(\varepsilon)=:C_{r}<+\infty
.
We recall that the inequality in (
3.10
) is an equality if and only if
\widehat{m}(\xi_{k})=1
for
every
k=1,\ldots,K
, namely, if
X\in{\mathcal{R}}^{\Lambda}
.
Therefore, since
I_{\varepsilon,r,\mathrm{lr}}^{\frac{1}{2}}
is non-negative, we have that the claim is trivially satisfied if
X\in\mathcal{X}^{\Lambda}\setminus{\mathcal{R}}^{\Lambda}
.

We focus now on the case
X\in{\mathcal{R}}^{\Lambda}
.
Notice that, in view of (
3.5
), the term
I_{\varepsilon,r,\mathrm{lr}}^{\frac{1}{2}}
does not depend on
\varepsilon
, so that we can write
I_{\varepsilon,r,\mathrm{lr}}^{\frac{1}{2}}=:I_{r,\mathrm{lr}}^{\frac{1}{2}}
, and
by (
3.4
) and (
3.13
), we get that for
\varepsilon
small enough,

(3.14)

{\mathscr{E}}_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{1}{2}}(X)-\Lambda|\log\varepsilon|\geq I_{r,\mathrm{lr}}^{\frac{1}{2}}+\Lambda\log r-C_{r}(\varepsilon)\qquad\textrm{ for every }X\in{\mathcal{R}}^{\Lambda}\textrm{ with }\delta(X)\geq 2r.

We can assume without loss of generality that
\delta(X)\leq 1
.
In order to prove the claim it is enough to study the behavior of
I_{r,\mathrm{lr}}^{\frac{1}{2}}
as
r\to 0
.
Let
\gamma_{r}:(0,1)\to{\mathbb{N}}
be the function that at any
p\in(0,1)
associates the number
\gamma_{r}(p)
of connected components of the set
\bigcup_{\lambda=1}^{\Lambda}B_{r^{p}}(x_{\lambda})\cup B_{r^{p}}(x_{1}+\Lambda)
.
Trivially, the function
\gamma_{r}
is piecewise constant; we denote by
0<p_{1}<\ldots<p_{L}<1
the jump points of
\gamma_{r}
and we set
p_{0}:=0
and
p_{L+1}:=1
. Let
0<\eta<\frac{1}{2}\min_{l=1,\ldots,L-1}(p_{l+1}-p_{l})
. Then there exists
J_{r}^{l}
“annuli”
A_{r^{p_{l}-\eta},r^{p_{l-1}+\eta}}(\zeta_{j})
, with
j=1,\ldots,J_{r}^{l}
, that are pairwise disjoint for every
j
and
l
and satisfy

\bigcup_{\lambda=1}^{\Lambda}B_{r}(x_{\lambda})\subset\bigcup_{j}B_{r^{p_{l}-\eta}}(\zeta_{j}).

We highlight that,
\mu^{X}((x,y))=\mu^{X}(B_{r^{p_{k}-\eta}}(\zeta_{j}))=:[\mu_{j}]
whenever
\zeta_{j}-r^{p_{l-1}+\eta}<x<\zeta_{j}-r^{p_{l}-\eta}
and
\zeta_{j}+r^{p_{l}-\eta}<y<\zeta_{j}+r^{p_{l-1}+\eta}
.
Moreover, by construction,
for every
l
there exists (at least one)
j=1,\ldots,J^{l}_{r}
such that
|[\mu_{j}]|\geq 2
and hence

|[\mu_{j}]|^{2}\geq|[\mu_{j}]|+3
.
Therefore, by (
3.5
), using (
3.11
) and (
3.12
),
arguing as in (
3.9
), for
r
small enough
we have that

\displaystyle I_{r,\mathrm{lr}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{l=1}^{L+1}\sum_{j=1}^{J_{r}^{l}}\int_{A_{r^{p_{l}-\eta},r^{p_{l-1}+\eta}}(\zeta_{j})}\,\mathrm{d}x\int_{A_{r^{p_{l}-\eta},r^{p_{l-1}+\eta}}(\zeta_{j})}\frac{|\mu^{X}(x,y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}y-C

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{L+1}\sum_{j=1}^{J_{r}^{l}}|[\mu_{j}]|\int_{\zeta_{j}-r^{p_{k-1}+\eta}}^{\zeta_{j}-{r^{p_{k}-\eta}}}\,\mathrm{d}x\int_{\zeta_{j}+r^{p_{k}-\eta}}^{\zeta_{j}+r^{p_{k-1}+\eta}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle+3\sum_{k=1}^{K+1}\int_{\zeta_{j}-r^{p_{l-1}+\eta}}^{\zeta_{j}-{r^{p_{l}-\eta}}}\,\mathrm{d}x\int_{\zeta_{j}+r^{p_{l}-\eta}}^{\zeta_{j}+r^{p_{l-1}+\eta}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}y-C

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(\Lambda+3)\sum_{l=1}^{K+1}(p_{l}-p_{l-1}-2\eta)|\log r|-C=(\Lambda+3)(1-2(K+1)\eta)|\log r|-C,

where in the last inequality we have used
that
\sum_{j=1}^{J_{r}^{k}}[\mu_{j}]=\mu^{X}([0,\Lambda))=\Lambda
.

By the arbitrariness of
\eta
we have that
I^{\frac{1}{2}}_{r,\mathrm{lr}}\geq(\Lambda+3)|\log r|-C
, which, in view of (
3.14
), for
r
small enough, implies

{\mathscr{E}}_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{1}{2}}(X)-\Lambda|\log\varepsilon|\geq 3|\log r|-C,

whence (
3.3
) follows also in this case.
This concludes the proof of the whole result.
∎

Proof of Theorem
3.2
.

By straightforward computations, for every configuration
\overline{X}\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\Lambda}
we have

(3.15)

{\mathscr{E}}_{\varepsilon}^{s}(\overline{X})\leq\Lambda\sigma^{s}(\varepsilon)+\bar{C}(s).

Let
\omega^{s}(\cdot):=C(s)\sigma^{s}(\cdot)
be the quantity in the right-hand side of (

\bar{\delta}=\bar{\delta}(s)>0
be such that
\frac{\omega^{s}(\delta)}{2}>\bar{C}(s)
for every

.
By Proposition
3.3
, there exists
\bar{\varepsilon}=\bar{\varepsilon}(\bar{\delta})>0

(3.16)

\inf_{X\in\mathcal{X}^{\Lambda}:\delta(X)<\bar{\delta}}{\mathscr{E}}_{\varepsilon}^{s}(X)\geq\Lambda\sigma^{s}(\varepsilon)+\frac{\omega^{s}(\delta)}{2}.

Let

(3.17)

0<\varepsilon<\min\Big\{\bar{\varepsilon},\frac{\bar{\delta}}{4}\Big\}.

By (
3.15
) and (
3.16
), we have that every minimizer
X
of
{\mathscr{E}}^{s}_{\varepsilon}
in
\mathcal{X}^{\Lambda}
satisfies

(3.18)

\delta(X)>\bar{\delta},

and hence, in particular,

(3.19)

\delta(X)>4\varepsilon.

4.
Dynamics

In this section we study the gradient flows of the energy functionals
{\mathscr{E}}^{s}
and
{\mathscr{E}}_{\varepsilon}^{s}
.

4.1.
The subcritical case
0<s<\frac{1}{2}

Here we analyze the gradient flow system of
{\mathscr{E}}^{s}
starting from a regular datum
X^{0}\in{\mathcal{R}}^{\Lambda}
. With a little abuse of notation we say that the map
t\mapsto X(t)\in{\mathcal{R}}^{\Lambda}
is absolutely continuous on some interval
I
, and we write
X\in AC(I)
, if the maps
t\mapsto\mathsf{x}_{z}(t)
are absolutely continuous in
I
(for all
z\in{\mathbb{Z}}
).
Notice that, if
X\in AC(I)
, the quantities
\dot{X}(t)
and
\nabla{\mathscr{E}}^{s}(X(t))
are well defined (in the obvious way) for almost all
t\in I
.
In this sense we understand the Cauchy problem we aim at studying

(4.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\dot{X}(t)=-\nabla{\mathscr{E}}^{s}(X(t))\\
X(0)=X^{0}.\end{array}\right.

Notice that
if we denote by
\nabla{\mathscr{E}}^{s}(\mathrm{X})
the sequence obtained by setting
\partial_{x_{\lambda+\Lambda z}}{\mathscr{E}}^{s}(\mathrm{X}):=\partial_{x_{\lambda}}{\mathscr{E}}^{s}(X)
for every
\lambda=1,\ldots,\Lambda
and for every
z\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, then the Cauchy problem above is equivalent to

(4.2)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\dot{\mathrm{X}}(t)=-\nabla{\mathscr{E}}^{s}(\mathrm{X}(t))\\
\mathrm{X}(0)=\mathrm{X}^{0}.\end{array}\right.

In view of Proposition
2.1
we have that there exists
T>0
such that the problem (
4.1
) admits a unique
C^{1}
(actually, smooth) solution in
[0,T)
.

We start by proving that the problem (
4.1
) has (unique and
C^{1}
) solution in
[0,+\infty)
and that the configuration
X(t)
is regular for every
t\geq 0
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
0<s<\frac{1}{2}
.
Let
X^{0}\in{\mathcal{R}}^{\Lambda}
and let
T_{\max}
be the maximal existence time for the Cauchy problem (
4.1
).
Then
T_{\max}=+\infty
.

In order to prove Proposition
4.1
, we need an auxiliary result and some further notation.
For every
X,\,Y\in\mathcal{X}^{\Lambda}
we set

(4.3)

\mathrm{dist}(X,Y):=\Big(\sum_{\lambda=1}^{\Lambda}|x_{\lambda}^{\mathrm{ord}}-y_{\lambda}^{\mathrm{ord}}|_{\rm per}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Let
\{X^{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset{\mathcal{R}}^{\Lambda}
and

, be such that

(4.4)

\mathrm{dist}(X^{n},X)\to 0\qquad\textrm{(as $n\to+\infty$)}.

Assume that
X^{n}\equiv(X^{n})^{\mathrm{ord}}
,
X\equiv X^{\mathrm{ord}}
and
{\mathcal{S}}(X)\subset(0,\Lambda)
.
For every
\xi\in{\mathcal{S}}(X)
, we recall that
x_{\phi(\xi)}
is the first entry of
X
which is equal to
\xi
, so that
x^{n}_{\phi(\xi)},\ldots,x^{n}_{\phi(\xi)+\widehat{m}(\xi)-1}
converge to
\xi
as
n\to+\infty

for every

we define

\xi^{n}:=\mathrm{argmax}\{|t-\xi|\,:\,t\in\{x^{n}_{\phi(\xi)},x^{n}_{\phi(\xi)+\widehat{m}(\xi)-1}\}\}.

For every

we define the configuration

\widehat{X}^{n}\in\mathcal{X}^{\Lambda}
such that

(4.5)

\hat{x}^{n}_{\lambda}:=\xi^{n}\qquad\textrm{ if }\phi(\xi)\leq\lambda\leq\phi(\xi)+\widehat{m}(\xi)-1\textrm{ for some }\xi\in{\mathcal{S}}(X).

We have,
\hat{x}^{n}_{\phi(\xi)}=\xi^{n}=x^{n}_{\phi(\xi)}

.
It is easy to check that

(4.6)

\frac{1}{\sqrt{\Lambda}}\mathrm{dist}(\widehat{X}^{n},X)\leq\mathrm{dist}(X^{n},X)\leq\mathrm{dist}(\widehat{X}^{n},X).

Furthermore, we define

(4.7)

Finally, for general

and
X
(not necessarily coinciding with

and
X^{\mathrm{ord}}
, respectively), we set
\Delta_{X}(X^{n}):=\Delta_{X^{\mathrm{ord}}}((X^{n})^{\mathrm{ord}})
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\{X^{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset{\mathcal{R}}^{\Lambda}
and

Then,

(4.9)

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\frac{{\mathscr{E}}^{s}(\widehat{X}^{n})-{\mathscr{E}}^{s}(X^{n})}{\Delta_{X}(X^{n})}=+\infty.

We can assume without loss of generality that
X=X^{\mathrm{ord}}
and that

for every

. Moreover, in virtue of the translational invariance, we can assume that

and that

Let
\mathcal{M}(X):=\{\xi\in{\mathcal{S}}(X)\,:\,\widehat{m}(\xi)\geq 2\}=\{\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{J}\}

We will assume, just to fix the notation, that
\xi^{n}_{j}\equiv x^{n}_{\phi(\xi_{j})+\widehat{m}(\xi_{j})-1}

Let
K:=\sum_{j=1}^{J}(\widehat{m}(\xi_{j})-1)

For every

.

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\mathscr{E}}^{s}(X^{n}[k])-{\mathscr{E}}^{s}(X^{n}[k-1])

Proof of Proposition

.

\displaystyle-\infty=

Remark 4.6

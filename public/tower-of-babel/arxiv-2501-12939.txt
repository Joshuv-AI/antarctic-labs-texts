Sharp estimates for fundamental frequencies of elliptic operators generated by asymmetric seminorms in low dimensions

Abstract

We establish new sharp asymmetric Poincaré inequalities in one-dimension with the computation of optimal constants and characterization of extremizers. Using the one-dimensional theory we develop a comprehensive study on fundamental frequencies in the plane and related spectral optimization in the very general setting of positively homogeneous anisotropies.

1
Overview and main statements

A line of research that has gained a lot of attention within spectral optimization is the study of sharp uniform estimates of fundamental frequencies associated to a family of elliptic operators defined in a given bounded domain. In precise terms, let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded domain and consider a set
{\cal E}(\Omega)
of second-order elliptic operators on
\Omega
so that each operator
{\cal L}\in{\cal E}(\Omega)
admits at least a non-zero fundamental frequency
\lambda_{1}({\cal L})
with respect some a priori fixed boundary condition (e.g. Dirichlet, Neumann, Robin).

Assume that there exists a way of “measuring” each element
{\cal L}\in{\cal E}(\Omega)
by some quantity denoted say by
{\cal M}({\cal L})
. Two cornerstone questions in spectral optimization related to
{\cal E}(\Omega)
are:

(I)

Are there explicit optimal constants
\Lambda^{\min}(\Omega)
and
\Lambda^{\max}(\Omega)
such that

\Lambda^{\min}(\Omega)\,{\cal M}({\cal L})\leq\lambda_{1}({\cal L})\leq\Lambda^{\max}(\Omega)\,{\cal M}({\cal L})

(1)

for every operator
{\cal L}\in{\cal E}(\Omega)
?

(II)

Does any inequality in (
1
) become equality for some operator
{\cal L}\in{\cal E}(\Omega)
?

The left sharp inequality in (
1
) can be seen as an analytical counterpart of the classical Faber-Krahn geometric inequality for the fundamental frequency
\lambda_{1}(\Omega)
of the Laplace operator
-\Delta
(cf.
[
27
,
36
]
):

|B|^{2/n}\lambda_{1}(B)\,|\Omega|^{-2/n}\leq\lambda_{1}(\Omega),

(2)

where
B
denotes the unit
n
-Euclidean ball and
|\cdot|
stands for the Lebesgue measure of a measurable subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. In contrast to (
1
), the operator
-\Delta
is fixed in (
2
), whereas the bounded domain
\Omega
varies freely.

Other important inequalities motivated by physical applications and closely related to (
2
) regarding the first non-zero eigenvalue of the Laplace operator under different boundary conditions are: Bossel-Daners inequality
[
8
,
12
,
18
,
19
,
21
,
22
]
(Robin condition), Szegö-Weinberger inequality
[
13
,
44
,
49
]
(Neumann condition) and Brock-Weinstock inequality
[
10
,
50
]
(Steklov condition). Faber-Krahn type inequalities have also been proved for other elliptic equations/operators as for example the Schrödinger equation
[
20
]
, the
p
-Laplace operator
[
2
]
, the affine
p
-Laplace operator
[
29
]
, anisotropic operators
[
5
]
, the fractional Laplace operator
[
9
]
and local/non-local type operators
[
6
]
.

There is a vast literature within spectral optimization addressed to questions closely related to
(I)
and
(II)
involving second-order linear elliptic operators. Most of the contributions can be found in the following long, but far from complete, list of references: regarding the Schrödinger operator we refer to
[
16
,
24
,
26
,
34
,
37
,
41
,
45
,
51
]
for the one-dimensional case and
[
3
,
11
,
14
,
15
,
23
,
30
,
40
]
and Chapter 9 of
[
31
]
, for higher dimensions and for the conductivity operator we refer to
[
4
,
17
,
25
,
47
]
.

On the other hand, fairly little is known about spectral optimization with respect to nonlinear elliptic operators, the subject began to be addressed very recently in the works
[
32
]
and
[
33
]
where, in the first one,
(I)
and
(II)
are completely solved in the plane on the class
{\cal E}(\Omega)
of elliptic operators of quadratic type and, in the second one on the class
{\cal E}(\Omega)
of anisotropic elliptic operators generated by seminorms as well in the plane.

The present work investigates
(I)
and
(II)
for the quite general class
{\cal E}(\Omega)
of anisotropic elliptic operators generated by asymmetric seminorms for any fixed bounded domain
\Omega
in the line and in the plane.

In order to introduce the set
{\cal E}(\Omega)
for a given bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
n=1
or
n=2
, consider the class of functions, called
asymmetric anisotropies
:

\mathscr{H}=\left\{H\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}\colon H\ \text{is non-negative, convex and positively $1$-homogeneous}\right\},

where the space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is assumed to be endowed with the usual Euclidean norm denoted by
|\cdot|
.

Recall that a function
H
is said to be convex if it satisfies

H(tx+(1-t)y)\leq tH(x)+(1-t)H(y)

for every
0<t<1
and
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and is positively
1
-homogeneous if

H(tx)=tH(x)

for every
t>0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

The elements of
\mathscr{H}
are also called asymmetric seminorms or, if they are positive in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
, asymmetric norms. For
n=1
, we clearly have

\mathscr{H}=\left\{H_{a,b}\colon\,H_{a,b}(t):=at^{+}+bt^{-},\,a,b\geq 0\right\},

where
t^{\pm}:=\max\{\pm t,0\}
.

For each number
p>1
and anisotropy
H\in\mathscr{H}
, define the
H
-anisotropic
p
-Laplace operator as

-\Delta_{p}^{H}u:=-{\rm div}\left(H^{p-1}\left(\nabla u\right)\nabla H\left(\nabla u\right)\right).

Corresponding to
H
, we have the anisotropic
L^{p}
energy
{\cal E}_{p}^{H}:W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}
defined by

{\cal E}_{p}^{H}(u):=\int_{\Omega}H^{p}(\nabla u)\,dx,

where
\nabla u
stands for the weak gradient of
u
(which as usual often will be denoted by
u^{\prime}
when
n=1
) and
W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
denotes the completion of compactly supported smooth functions in
\Omega
with respect to the norm

\lVert u\rVert_{W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)}=\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

The least energy level associated to
{\cal E}_{p}^{H}
on the unit sphere in
L^{p}(\Omega)
is defined as

\lambda_{p}^{H}(\Omega):=\inf\left\{{\cal E}_{p}^{H}(u)\colon\ u\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega),\ \lVert u\rVert_{p}=1\right\},

(3)

where

\lVert u\rVert_{p}=\left(\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Knowing whether
\lambda_{p}^{H}(\Omega)
is a fundamental frequency associated to the operator
-\Delta_{p}^{H}
on
W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
is a problem to be discussed in the paper and, as we shall see, the answer depends on the regularity of
H
. By a fundamental frequency we mean that there exists a non-zero function
u_{p}\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
such that
\nabla H\left(\nabla u_{p}\right)
is well-defined almost everywhere, is Lebesgue measurable in
\Omega
and such that

\int_{\Omega}H^{p-1}\left(\nabla u_{p}\right)\nabla H\left(\nabla u_{p}\right)\cdot\nabla\varphi\,dx=\lambda_{p}^{H}(\Omega)\int_{\Omega}|u_{p}|^{p-2}u_{p}\varphi\,dx

for every test function
\varphi\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
. The finiteness of the above left-hand side is immediate since asymmetric anisotropies
H
are Lipschitz.

The set of interest
{\cal E}(\Omega)
is given by

{\cal E}(\Omega):=\{-\Delta_{p}^{H}:\,H\in\mathscr{H}\setminus\{0\}\ \text{such that}\ \lambda_{p}^{H}(\Omega)\ \text{is a fundamental frequency}\}.

Observe that
-\Delta_{p}^{H}\in{\cal E}(\Omega)
for every asymmetric norm
H
of class
C^{1}
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
. In particular, for
n=1
,
{\cal E}(\Omega)
contains the one-dimensional asymmetric
p
-Laplace operator
-\Delta_{p}^{H_{a,b}}
, where

\Delta_{p}^{H_{a,b}}\,u=\left(a^{p}[(u^{\prime})^{+}]^{p-1}-b^{p}[(u^{\prime})^{-}]^{p-1}\right)^{\prime}

for constants
a,b>0
, and for
n=2
,
{\cal E}(\Omega)
contains the 2-dimensional
p
-Laplace operator
-\Delta_{p}
where
\Delta_{p}=:\Delta_{p}^{H}
with
H(x,y)=|(x,y)|
, or explicitly

\Delta_{p}u={\rm div}(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u).

Our first results concern the structure of the set
{\cal E}(\Omega)
. For instance, when
n=2
and
H
is an asymmetric norm we prove that
-\Delta_{p}^{H}\in{\cal E}(\Omega)
for every bounded domain
\Omega
if and only if
H
is of
C^{1}
class in
\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{(0,0)\}
. For asymmetric seminorms we also show that the operator
-\Delta_{p}^{H}
does not belong to
{\cal E}(\Omega)
for any,
\Omega
in the cases
H(t)=H_{a,0}(t)
for
n=1
and
H(x,y)=H_{a,0}(y)
for
n=2
when
a>0
. Also, in this context, we provide a characterization of all domains
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
such that
-\Delta_{p}^{H}\in{\cal E}(\Omega)
for
H(x,y)=H_{a,b}(y)
with
a,b>0
.

We begin by presenting the complete picture in dimension
n=1
. Assume without loss of generality that
\Omega=(-T,T)
for some
T>0
. For each
a,b\geq 0
, set
\lambda_{p}^{a,b}(\Omega):=\lambda_{p}^{H_{a,b}}(\Omega)
. Denote also by
\lambda_{1,p}(\Omega)
the fundamental frequency of
-\Delta_{p}
in
\Omega
and let
\varphi_{p}\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
be the corresponding principal eigenfunction normalized by
\|\varphi_{p}\|_{p}=1
.
Of course, we rule out the trivial case
a=b=0
, and by symmetry in
a
and
b
we may assume that
a>0
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
p>1
and let
T>0
. For any
a>0
and
b\geq 0
, we have:

(i)

\lambda_{p}^{a,b}(-T,T)=\displaystyle\left(\frac{a+b}{2}\right)^{p}\lambda_{1,p}(-T,T)
;

(ii)

u
is an extremizer of (
9
) if and only if either
u=u_{p}
or
u=v_{p}
, where (see Figure
1
)

u_{p}(t)=\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} &c\varphi_{p}\left(\frac{T(t-t_{0})}{T+t_{0}}\right)\ \ \text{if}\ t\in(-T,t_{0}),\\
&c\varphi_{p}\left(\frac{T(t-t_{0})}{T-t_{0}}\right)\ \ \text{if}\ t\in[t_{0},T)\end{aligned}\end{cases}

and

v_{p}(t)=\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} &-c\varphi_{p}\left(\frac{T(t+t_{0})}{T-t_{0}}\right)\ \ \text{if}\ t\in(-T,-t_{0}),\\
&-c\varphi_{p}\left(\frac{T(t+t_{0})}{T+t_{0}}\right)\ \ \text{if}\ t\in[-t_{0},T),\end{aligned}\end{cases}

where
c
is any positive constant and

t_{0}=\left(\frac{a-b}{a+b}\right)T.

Consequently, since
u\in W_{0}^{1,p}(-T,T)
only when
b>0
,
\lambda_{p}^{a,b}(-T,T)
is a fundamental frequency if and only if
b>0
. In other words,

{\cal E}(\Omega)=\{-\Delta_{p}^{H_{a,b}}:\,a,b>0\}.

In particular, we have:

Corollary 1.1
.

Let
p>1
, let
T>0
and let
H\in\mathscr{H}
. The level
\lambda_{p}^{H}(-T,T)
is positive if and only if
H\neq 0
.

For related results in dimension
n=2
, it is convenient to decompose
\mathscr{H}
into the subsets of positive and degenerate anisotropies to be denoted respectively by

\displaystyle\mathscr{H}_{P}

\displaystyle:=\left\{H\in\mathscr{H}:\ H(x,y)>0\ \forall(x,y)\neq(0,0)\right\},

\displaystyle\mathscr{H}_{D}

\displaystyle:=\left\{H\in\mathscr{H}:\ H(x,y)=0\ \text{for some}\ (x,y)\neq(0,0)\right\}.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
p>1
and let
H\in\mathscr{H}_{P}
. The assertions are equivalent:

(a)

\lambda_{p}^{H}(\Omega)
is a fundamental frequency for any bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
;

(b)

H
is of
C^{1}
class in
\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{(0,0)\}
.

We now concentrate on the degenerate case and provide complete answers for two subsets of
\mathscr{H}_{D}
.

Let
H\in\mathscr{H}_{D}\setminus\{0\}
be such that
\ker(H):=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:\,H(x,y)=0\}
is a line or a half-plane. In each case there is a rotation matrix
A
such that

H_{A}(x,y):=(H\circ A)(x,y)=H_{a,b}(y)=ay^{+}+by^{-},

(4)

where
a,b>0
when
\ker(H)
is a line, and
a>0
and

when
\ker(H)
is a half-plane (see Proposition

).

The next results describe the two situations separately.

Theorem 1.3

(The case
b=0
)
.

Let
p>1
and let
\Omega
be any bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. If
\ker(H)
is a half-plane then the level
\lambda^{H}_{p}(\Omega)
is never a fundamental frequency.

Theorem 1.4

(The case
b>0
)
.

Let
p>1
,
\Omega
be any

bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, assume
\ker(H)
is a line and let

be as in (

\Omega_{A}:=A^{T}(\Omega)
. The assertions are equivalent:

(a)

\lambda^{H}_{p}(\Omega)
is a fundamental frequency;

(b)

There are bounded open intervals

, a

and a number

such that:

(i)

\Omega_{A}\subset I\times\mathbb{R}
,

(ii)

(iii)

.

In that case,

is the number so that

.

Let us now turn our attention to the problems
(I)
and
(II)
. By the definition of
{\cal E}(\Omega)
we can denote

\lambda_{1}(-\Delta_{p}^{H}):=\lambda^{H}_{p}(\Omega).

Among different possible ways of “measuring” operators in
{\cal E}(\Omega)
, we choose to work with

{\cal M}(-\Delta_{p}^{H})=\|H\|^{p},

where

\|H\|:=\max_{|x|=1}H(x).

For this choice, the inequalities in (
1
) take the form

\Lambda^{\min}(\Omega)\,\|H\|^{p}\leq\lambda_{1}(-\Delta_{p}^{H})\leq\Lambda^{\max}(\Omega)\,\|H\|^{p}.

(5)

Furthermore, as can be easily checked,
{\cal M}
is positively homogeneous, that is

{\cal M}(-\lambda\Delta_{p}^{H})={\cal M}(-\Delta_{p}^{\lambda^{1/p}H})=\lambda\,{\cal M}(-\Delta_{p}^{H})

for every
\lambda>0
. Consequently, the optimal constants in (
5
) are given by

\displaystyle\Lambda^{\max}(\Omega)

\displaystyle=\sup\left\{\lambda_{1}(-\Delta_{p}^{H}):\,-\Delta_{p}^{H}\in{\cal E}(\Omega)\ \ {\rm and}\ \ \|H\|=1\right\},

\displaystyle\Lambda^{\min}(\Omega)

\displaystyle=\inf\left\{\lambda_{1}(-\Delta_{p}^{H}):\,-\Delta_{p}^{H}\in{\cal E}(\Omega)\ \ {\rm and}\ \ \|H\|=1\right\}.

In dimension
n=1
, Theorem
1.1
provides the complete solution for the questions
(I)
and
(II)
. Since
\|H_{a,b}\|=\max\{a,b\}
, we have:

Corollary 1.2
.

For any
p>1
and
T>0
, we have

\Lambda^{\min}(-T,T)\,\max\{a,b\}^{p}\leq\lambda_{1}(-\Delta_{p}^{H_{a,b}})\leq\Lambda^{\max}(-T,T)\,\max\{a,b\}^{p}

(6)

for every
-\Delta_{p}^{H_{a,b}}\in{\cal E}(\Omega)
, where

\displaystyle\lambda_{1,p}^{\max}(-T,T)

\displaystyle=\lambda_{1,p}(-T,T),

\displaystyle\lambda_{1,p}^{\min}(-T,T)

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{p}}\lambda_{1,p}(-T,T).

Moreover, all extremal operators for the right inequality in (
6
) are given by the non-zero multiples of
-\Delta_{p}
, and for the left one they are given by
-\Delta_{p}^{H_{a,0}}
and
-\Delta_{p}^{H_{0,b}}
for all
a,b>0
, which do not belong to
{\cal E}(\Omega)
.

We now concentrate on related results in dimension
n=2
. The next theorem states that the right sharp inequality in (
5
) is rigid under some regularity assumptions on the boundary of

, and gives explicitly the constant
\Lambda^{\max}(\Omega)
.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
p>1
and let
\Omega
be any bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. The upper optimal constant
\Lambda^{\max}(\Omega)
is given by
\lambda_{1,p}(\Omega)
. Moreover, all non-zero multiples of
-\Delta_{p}
are extremal operators for the second inequality in (

) and only them provided that

satisfies the Wiener condition (see the definition for instance in
[

,
38
,
39
]
).

Before focusing on the first sharp inequality in (
5
), we recall the notion of optimal anisotropic design for bounded domains, introduced in
[
33
]
. Consider the width function
L:[0,\pi]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
defined by
L(\theta)=L_{\theta}
, where

L_{\theta}:=\sup_{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}\sup\left\{|J|\colon J\subset\left\{t(\cos{\theta},\sin{\theta})+(x,y)\colon t\in\mathbb{R}\right\}\cap\Omega,\ J\ \text{is connected}\right\}.

A bounded domain

The concept of optimal design is motivated by the following statement:

Theorem 1.6
.

Let

and let
\Omega
be any bounded domain in

is always positive and given by

has optimal anisotropic design. In this case, if

is a global maximum point of the function

then all non-zero multiples of

.

2
Asymmetric Poincaré inequalities for
n=1

In this section we carry out the complete study of sharp asymmetric Poincaré inequalities in the line. In precise terms, for each
p>1
,
T>0
,
a>0
and
b\geq 0
, we recall that

\lambda^{a,b}_{p}(-T,T):=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in W_{0}^{1,p}(-T,T)\\
\lVert u\rVert_{p}=1\end{subarray}}\ \int_{-T}^{T}\left(H_{a,b}(u^{\prime}(t))\right)^{p}\,dt,

where
H_{a,b}(t)=at^{+}+bt^{-}
.

The purpose here is twofold: to compute the value of
\lambda^{a,b}_{p}(-T,T)
and to characterize all corresponding extremizers. Since the nature of the inequalities relies on the value of
b
, we organize this section into two distinct situations: the degenerate case (
b=0
) and the non-degenerate case (
b>0
).

2.1
The case
b=0

It suffices to assume
a=1
. Observe that
\lambda_{p}^{0,1}(-T,T)=\lambda_{p}^{1,0}(-T,T)
and also
u
is an extremizer for
\lambda_{p}^{1,0}(-T,T)
if and only if
-u
is an extremizer for
\lambda_{p}^{0,1}(-T,T)
. In particular, our treatment also covers the case
a=0
.

Set

\lambda^{+}_{p}(-T,T)=\lambda_{p}^{1,0}(-T,T).

Next we compute the value of
\lambda^{+}_{p}(-T,T)
and characterize all corresponding extremizers in a larger space of functions. As a consequence, we will derive the asymmetric Poincaré inequality which is stronger than the classical one,

\int_{-T}^{T}\left(u^{\prime}(t)^{+}\right)^{p}dt\geq\lambda^{+}_{p}(-T,T)\int_{-T}^{T}|u(t)|^{p}dt

(7)

for every
u\in W_{0}^{1,p}(-T,T)
.

We begin by introducing the piecewise Sobolev space
W_{pw}^{1,p,+}(-T,T)
as the set of all functions
u:(-T,T)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that for some finite partition

{\cal P}=\{-T=:T_{0}<T_{1}<\cdots<T_{m-1}<T_{m}:=T\},

we have:

(A)

u\in W^{1,p}(T_{i-1},T_{i})
for every
i=1,\ldots,m
,

(B)

u(-T)\leq 0
and
u(T)\geq 0
,

(C)

u(T_{i}^{-})>u(T_{i}^{+})
for every
i=1,\ldots,m-1
if
m>1
, where

u(T_{i}^{\pm}):=\lim_{t\rightarrow T_{i}^{\pm}}u(t).

Clearly, all functions in
W^{1,p}(-T,T)
that satisfy (B) belong to
W_{pw}^{1,p,+}(-T,T)
.

We begin by ensuring that the sharp inequality (
7
) can be extended to the larger set
W_{pw}^{1,p,+}(-T,T)
.

Proposition 2.1
.

For any
p>1
and
T>0
, we have

\lambda^{+}_{p}(-T,T)=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in W_{pw}^{1,p,+}(-T,T),\\
\lVert u\rVert_{p}=1\end{subarray}}\ \int_{-T}^{T}\left(u^{\prime}(t)^{+}\right)^{p}\,dt.

Proof.

Let
u\in W_{pw}^{1,p,+}(-T,T)
be a function such that
u(-T)=0=u(T)
. For each integer
k\geq 1
large enough, define the continuous function
u_{k}(t)
as

u_{k}(t):=\begin{cases}u(t)&\text{if $t\in\cup^{m-1}_{i=1}[T_{i-1},T_{i}-\frac{1}{k}]\cup[T_{m-1},T]$},\\
a_{k}^{i}t+b_{k}^{i}&\text{if $t\in(T_{i}-\frac{1}{k},T_{i})$ for}\ i=1,\ldots,m-1,\\
\end{cases}

where
a_{k}^{i}:=k\left(u(T_{i}^{+})-u(T_{i}-\frac{1}{k})\right)
and
b_{k}^{i}:=u(T_{i}^{+})-a_{k}^{i}T_{i}
. Clearly,
u_{k}\in W^{1,p}_{0}(-T,T)
and
a_{k}^{i}<0
for every
i=1,\ldots,m-1
and
k
large. Since
u\in W^{1,p}(T_{i-1},T_{i})
for all
i
, we have
u\in L^{\infty}(-T,T)
and so letting
k\rightarrow\infty
, we get

\int_{-T}^{T}|u_{k}(t)|^{p}dt\rightarrow\int_{-T}^{T}|u(t)|^{p}dt,

\int_{-T}^{T}\left(u_{k}^{\prime}(t)^{+}\right)^{p}dt=\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}\int_{T_{i-1}}^{T_{i}-\frac{1}{k}}\left(u^{\prime}(t)^{+}\right)^{p}dt+\int_{T_{m-1}}^{T}\left(u^{\prime}(t)^{+}\right)^{p}dt\rightarrow\int_{-T}^{T}\left(u^{\prime}(t)^{+}\right)^{p}dt.

Assume now that
u(-T)<0
or
u(T)>0
. In each case we modify the function
u_{k}
in the intervals
[T_{0},T_{0}+\frac{1}{k}]
and
[T_{m}-\frac{1}{k},T_{m}]
, respectively. In fact, consider the new function
v_{k}\in W^{1,p}_{0}(-T,T)
:

v_{k}(t):=\begin{cases}a_{k}^{0}t+b_{k}^{0}&\text{if $t\in[T_{0},T_{0}+\frac{1}{k}]$ in case $u(-T)<0$},\\
a_{k}^{m}t+b_{k}^{m}&\text{if $t\in[T_{m}-\frac{1}{k},T_{m}]$ in case $u(T)>0$},\\
u_{k}(t)&\text{otherwise},\\
\end{cases}

where

a_{k}^{0}:=ku(T_{0}+\frac{1}{k}),\ \ b_{k}^{0}:=kTu(T_{0}+\frac{1}{k}),

a_{k}^{m}:=-ku(T_{m}-\frac{1}{k}),\ \ \ b_{k}^{0}:=kTu(T_{m}-\frac{1}{k}).

Since
a_{k}^{0}
and
a_{k}^{m}
are negative for
k
large, arguing exactly as before, we obtain

\int_{-T}^{T}|v_{k}(t)|^{p}dt\rightarrow\int_{-T}^{T}|u(t)|^{p}dt,

\int_{-T}^{T}\left(v_{k}^{\prime}(t)^{+}\right)^{p}dt=\int_{T_{0}+\frac{1}{k}}^{T_{m}-\frac{1}{k}}\left(u_{k}^{\prime}(t)^{+}\right)^{p}dt\rightarrow\int_{-T}^{T}\left(u^{\prime}(t)^{+}\right)^{p}dt.

This leads us to the desired statement.
∎

The main result of this subsection is

Theorem 2.1
.

For any
p>1
and
T>0
, we have:

(i)

\lambda^{+}_{p}(-T,T)=\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{p}}\lambda_{1,p}(-T,T)
;

(ii)

u\in W_{pw}^{1,p,+}(-T,T)
is an extremizer of (
7
) if and only if either
u=u_{p}
or
u=v_{p}
, where

u_{p}(t)=c\varphi_{p}\left(\frac{t-T}{2}\right)\ \ \text{and}\ \ v_{p}(t)=-c\varphi_{p}\left(\frac{t+T}{2}\right)\ \ \text{for}\ t\in(-T,T),

where
c
is any positive constant and
\varphi_{p}\in W^{1,p}_{0}(-T,T)
is the principal eigenfunction of the
p
-Laplace operator
-\Delta_{p}
associated to the eigenvalue
\lambda_{1,p}(-T,T)
normalized by
\|\varphi_{p}\|_{p}=1
.

As a direct consequence of the above statement, all extremizers of (
7
) are in
W^{1,p}(-T,T)\setminus W_{0}^{1,p}(-T,T)
, vanish at one of the endpoints of
(-T,T)
and have defined sign.

A key ingredient in the proof of the theorem consists in finding a suitable set
X_{0}\subset W_{pw}^{1,p,+}(-T,T)
where possible extremizers must necessarily lie. Consider then the following set

X_{0}:=\left\{u\in W^{1,p}(-T,T)\colon\ u\ \text{is non-decreasing},\ \lVert u\rVert_{p}=1\ \text{and either}\ u(-T)=0\ \text{or}\ u(T)=0\right\}.

Proposition 2.2
.

For any
p>1
and
T>0
, we have

\lambda_{p}^{+}(-T,T)=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in X_{0}\end{subarray}}\ \int_{-T}^{T}\left(u^{\prime}(t)^{+}\right)^{p}dt.

Proof.

The proof is based on a three-steps reduction process, transforming the infimum in
W_{pw}^{1,p,+}(-T,T)
into the infimum in
X_{0}
.
Observe that any
u\in W_{pw}^{1,p,+}(-T,T)
must have at least one zero in
[-T,T]
.

First reduction:
We first restrict
W_{pw}^{1,p,+}(-T,T)
to functions with a finite number of zeros, since this is a dense subset (this is due to this subset contains the subset of polynomials restricted to
(-T,T)
, which is dense). From now on, we will assume
u^{-1}(0)
is finite for the functions in
W_{pw}^{1,p,+}(-T,T)
. Next we prove that the infimum can be reduced to the set

X_{2}\coloneqq\left\{u\in W_{pw}^{1,p,+}(-T,T)\colon\ \lVert u\rVert_{p}=1\ \text{and}\ u(t)=0\ {\rm implies}\ t\in\{-T,T\}\right\}.

Let
u\in W_{pw}^{1,p,+}(-T,T)
be such that
\lVert u\rVert_{p}=1
and assume there is
t_{0}\in(-T,T)
such that
u(t_{0})=0
(i.e.
u\in X_{2}^{c}
). Clearly,
u\in W_{pw}^{1,p,+}(-T,t_{0})\cap W_{pw}^{1,p,+}(t_{0},T)
.

Consider the following notations

I_{v}=\int_{-T}^{T}|v|^{p},\ I_{v}^{\prime}=\int_{-T}^{T}((v^{\prime})^{+})^{p}

for any
v\in W_{pw}^{1,p,+}(-T,T)
and

J_{1}=\int_{-T}^{t_{0}}|u|^{p},\ J_{1}^{\prime}=\int_{-T}^{t_{0}}((u^{\prime})^{+})^{p},\ J_{2}=\int_{t_{0}}^{T}|u|^{p},\ J_{2}^{\prime}=\int_{t_{0}}^{T}((u^{\prime})^{+})^{p}.

Now we introduce the functions
v_{1}(t)
and
v_{2}(t)
for
t\in(-T,T)
:

\displaystyle v_{1}(t)\coloneqq u\left(\frac{(t_{0}+T)t}{2T}+\frac{t_{0}-T}{2}\right),

\displaystyle v_{2}(t)\coloneqq u\left(\frac{(T-t_{0})t}{2T}+\frac{t_{0}+T}{2}\right).

Straightforward computations yield

\displaystyle I_{v_{1}}=\frac{2T}{T+t_{0}}J_{1},\ I_{v_{1}}^{\prime}=\left(\frac{T+t_{0}}{2T}\right)^{p-1}J_{1}^{\prime},

\displaystyle I_{v_{2}}=\frac{2T}{T-t_{0}}J_{2},\ I_{v_{2}}^{\prime}=\left(\frac{T-t_{0}}{2T}\right)^{p-1}J_{2}^{\prime}.

Assume, without loss of generality, that
\frac{J_{1}^{\prime}}{J_{1}}\leq\frac{J_{2}^{\prime}}{J_{2}}
. Otherwise, we exchange
v_{1}
by
v_{2}
in the argument below. This implies that

\frac{I_{v_{1}}^{\prime}}{I_{v_{1}}}<\left(\frac{2T}{T+t_{0}}\right)^{p}\frac{I_{v_{1}}^{\prime}}{I_{v_{1}}}=\frac{J_{1}^{\prime}}{J_{1}}\leq\frac{J_{1}^{\prime}+J_{2}^{\prime}}{J_{1}+J_{2}}=J_{1}^{\prime}+J_{2}^{\prime},

and this inequality can be rewritten for
w=\frac{v_{1}}{\lVert v_{1}\rVert_{p}}
as

\int_{-T}^{T}((w^{\prime})^{+})^{p}<\int_{-T}^{T}((u^{\prime})^{+})^{p}.

We then repeat the process (now with
w
) finitely many times until the resulting function has no zeros in
(-T,T)
(this eventually ends because we started with a function with a finite number of zeros). Furthermore, by construction, the resulting function is zero at most at the endpoints
-T
and
T
and it has lower energy than the initial one. Therefore, we can reduce the infimum to the set
X_{2}
.

Summarizing, it was shown that

\lambda_{p}^{+}(-T,T)=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in X_{2}\end{subarray}}\ \int_{-T}^{T}\left(u^{\prime}(t)^{+}\right)^{p}dt.

Second reduction:
Now we prove that the infimum can be reduced to the set

X_{1}\coloneqq\left\{u\in X_{2}\colon\ u\in W^{1,p}(-T,T)\right\}.

Let
u\in X_{2}
and assume that
u\not\in W^{1,p}(-T,T)
. Since
u\in W_{pw}^{1,p,+}(-T,T)
, there is a partition

{\cal P}=\{-T=T_{0}<T_{1}<\dots<T_{m-1}<T_{m}=T\}

for some
m>1
such that
u
satisfies
(A)
,
(B)
and
(C)
. Also, since
u\in X_{2}
(
u
vanishes at most in
\{-T,T\}
) and due to the conditions
(B)
and
(C)
,
u
does not change sign in
(-T,T)
.

Assume that
u(-T)=0
and
u>0
in
(-T,T)
(the negative case is similar). Define the jump correction function of
u
as

j_{u}=\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}\left(u(T_{i}^{-})-u(T_{i}^{+})\right)\chi_{(T_{i},T_{i+1})}\geq 0

and consider the following function in
W^{1,p}(-T,T)
:

v=\frac{u+j_{u}}{\lVert u+j_{u}\rVert_{p}}.

Clearly, we have
v\in X_{1}
. In addition,

u+j_{u}\geq u>0
implies
\|u+j_{u}\|\geq\|u\|_{p}\geq 1
,
so that

\int_{-T}^{T}((v^{\prime})^{+})^{p}=\int_{-T}^{T}\left(\frac{(u^{\prime})^{+}}{\lVert u+j_{u}\rVert_{p}}\right)^{p}\leq\int_{-T}^{T}((u^{\prime})^{+})^{p}.

This gives us

\lambda_{p}^{+}(-T,T)=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in X_{1}\end{subarray}}\ \int_{-T}^{T}\left(u^{\prime}(t)^{+}\right)^{p}dt.

Final reduction:
The last step consists in reducing the infimum to the initially introduced set

X_{0}=\{u\in X_{1}\colon\ u\ \text{is non-decreasing}\}.

Next we show that a dense subset of
X_{1}
can be reduced to a dense subset of
X_{0}
. Take a polynomial
q\in X_{1}
such that
q(-T)=0
and
q>0
in
(-T,T)
(again the case
u<0
is similar) and write

\left[(q^{\prime})^{-1}(-\infty,0)\right]\cap(-T,T)=\bigcup_{i=0}^{N}(t_{2i},t_{2i+1}),

where the intervals in the union are disjoint. Now define the function
u(t)
for
t\in(-T,T)
as

u(t)=\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} &q(t)\ \ \ \ \text{if}\ t\in(q^{\prime})^{-1}[0,+\infty),\\
&q(t_{2i})\ \ \text{if}\ t\in(t_{2i},t_{2i+1}).\end{aligned}\end{cases}

Note that
u\in W_{pw}^{1,p,+}(-T,T)
and
u(-T)=0
. Consider
j_{u}
as before and define again

v=\frac{u+j_{u}}{\lVert u+j_{u}\rVert_{p}}.

It is clear that
v\in X_{0}
. Moreover, we have

\displaystyle\lVert u+j_{u}\rVert_{p}^{p}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{-T}^{T}|u(t)|^{p}\ dt

\displaystyle=\int_{(q^{\prime})^{-1}[0,+\infty)}|q(t)|^{p}\ dt+\sum_{i=0}^{N}\int_{t_{2i}}^{t_{2i+1}}|q(t_{2i})|^{p}\ dt

\displaystyle\geq\int_{(q^{\prime})^{-1}[0,+\infty)}|q(t)|^{p}\ dt+\sum_{i=0}^{N}\int_{t_{2i}}^{t_{2i+1}}|q(t)|^{p}\ dt

\displaystyle=\int_{-T}^{T}|q(t)|^{p}\ dt=1.

Therefore,

\int_{-T}^{T}((v^{\prime})^{+})^{p}=\frac{1}{\lVert u+j_{u}\rVert_{p}^{p}}\int_{-T}^{T}((q^{\prime})^{+})^{p}\leq\int_{-T}^{T}((q^{\prime})^{+})^{p}

and so we can reduce the infimum to the set
X_{0}
. This completes the proof of the proposition.
∎

Now we are ready to prove the main result of this subsection.

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.

We start assuming by contradiction that

\lambda_{p}^{+}(-T,T)<\frac{\lambda_{1,p}(-T,T)}{2^{p}}.

From Proposition
2.2
, there is a function
u\in X_{0}
such that

\int_{-T}^{T}\left(u^{\prime}(t)^{+}\right)^{p}\ dt<\frac{\lambda_{1,p}(-T,T)}{2^{p}}.

Assume
u(-T)=0
and
u>0
in
(-T,T)
(the negative case is analogous). Define the function
w(t)
for
t\in(-T,T)
as

w(t)=\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} &u(2t+T)\ \ \text{if}\ t\in(-T,0),\\
&u(T-2t)\ \ \text{if}\ t\in[0,T).\end{aligned}\end{cases}

It is clear that
w\in W^{1,p}_{0}(-T,T)
. In addition,

\displaystyle\int_{-T}^{T}|w(t)|^{p}\ dt

\displaystyle=\int_{-T}^{0}|u(2t+T)|^{p}\ dt+\int_{0}^{T}|u(T-2t)|^{p}\ dt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{-T}^{T}|u(s)|^{p}\ ds+\frac{1}{2}\int_{-T}^{T}|u(s)|^{p}\ ds

\displaystyle=\int_{-T}^{T}|u(s)|^{p}\ ds

\displaystyle=1

and, since
u
is non-decreasing in
(-T,T)
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{-T}^{T}|w^{\prime}(t)|^{p}\ dt

\displaystyle=\int_{-T}^{0}2^{p}|u^{\prime}(2t+T)|^{p}\ dt+\int_{0}^{T}2^{p}|u^{\prime}(T-2t)|^{p}\ dt

\displaystyle=2^{p}\left(\frac{1}{2}\int_{-T}^{T}\left(u^{\prime}(t)^{+}\right)^{p}\ dt+\frac{1}{2}\int_{-T}^{T}\left(u^{\prime}(t)^{+}\right)^{p}\ dt\right)

\displaystyle=2^{p}\int_{-T}^{T}\left(u^{\prime}(t)^{+}\right)^{p}\ dt<\lambda_{1,p}(-T,T),

which contradicts the definition of
\lambda_{1,p}(-T,T)
. Thus,

\lambda_{p}^{+}(-T,T)\geq\frac{\lambda_{1,p}(-T,T)}{2^{p}}.

Now we define the function
u_{p}(t)
for
t\in(-T,T)
as

u_{p}(t)\coloneqq\varphi_{p}\left(\frac{t-T}{2}\right).

Note that
u_{p}\in X_{0}
. Also, since
\varphi_{p}
is even in
(-T,T)
and increasing in
(-T,0)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{-T}^{T}|u_{p}(t)|^{p}\ dt

\displaystyle=\int_{-T}^{T}\left|\varphi_{p}\left(\frac{t-T}{2}\right)\right|^{p}\ dt

\displaystyle=2\int_{-T}^{0}|\varphi_{p}(t)|^{p}\ dt

\displaystyle=\int_{-T}^{T}|\varphi_{p}(t)|^{p}\ dt

\displaystyle=1

and

\displaystyle\int_{-T}^{T}\left(u_{p}^{\prime}(t)^{+}\right)^{p}\ dt

\displaystyle=\int_{-T}^{T}\left|\frac{1}{2}\varphi_{p}^{\prime}\left(\frac{t-T}{2}\right)\right|^{p}\ dt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{p}}\left(2\int_{-T}^{0}|\varphi_{p}^{\prime}(t)|^{p}\ dt\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{p}}\int_{-T}^{T}|\varphi_{p}^{\prime}(t)|^{p}\ dt

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda_{1,p}(-T,T)}{2^{p}},

which implies that

\lambda_{p}^{+}(-T,T)\leq\frac{\lambda_{1,p}(-T,T)}{2^{p}}

and so the equality occurs. Moreover, the functions
u_{p}
and
v_{p}
defined in the statement are extremizers of (
7
).

We now concentrate on the characterization of all extremizers in the set
W_{pw}^{1,p,+}(-T,T)
. Assume that
u\in W_{pw}^{1,p,+}(-T,T)
satisfies
\lVert u\rVert_{p}=1
and

\int_{-T}^{T}\left(u^{\prime}(t)^{+}\right)^{p}dt=\lambda_{p}^{+}(-T,T).

Next we ensure that
u\in X_{0}
. The proof is carried out in three steps.

First step:
We claim that the function
u
does not change sign in
(-T,T)
. Otherwise, by
(A)
,
(B)
and
(C)
in the definition of
W_{pw}^{1,p,+}(-T,T)
, we have
u(t_{0})=0
for some
t_{0}\in(-T,T)
and so
u\in W_{pw}^{1,p,+}(-T,t_{0})\cap W_{pw}^{1,p,+}(t_{0},T)
. Consequently,

\displaystyle\lambda_{p}^{+}(-T,T)

\displaystyle=\int_{-T}^{T}\left(u^{\prime}(t)^{+}\right)^{p}dt

\displaystyle=\int_{-T}^{t_{0}}\left(u^{\prime}(t)^{+}\right)^{p}dt+\int_{t_{0}}^{T}\left(u^{\prime}(t)^{+}\right)^{p}dt

\displaystyle=\lambda_{p}^{+}(-T,t_{0})\int_{-T}^{t_{0}}|u(t)|^{p}dt+\lambda_{p}^{+}(t_{0},T)\int_{t_{0}}^{T}|u(t)|^{p}dt

\displaystyle\geq\min\{\lambda_{p}^{+}(-T,t_{0}),\lambda_{p}^{+}(t_{0},T)\},

which contradicts the strict monotonicity
\lambda_{p}^{+}(-T,T)<\lambda_{p}^{+}(-T,t_{0})
and
\lambda_{p}^{+}(-T,T)<\lambda_{p}^{+}(t_{0},T)
with respect to the domain. The latter follows readily from the positivity of
\lambda_{p}^{+}(-T,T)
, invariance property by translation and the scaling property

\lambda_{p}^{+}(-\alpha T,\alpha T)=\frac{1}{\alpha^{p}}\lambda_{p}^{+}(-T,T)

for every
\alpha>0
.

Second step:
According to the previous step, assume
u(-T)=0
and
u>0
in
(-T,T)
(the negative case is analogous). Since
u
is a positive extremizer in
W_{pw}^{1,p,+}(-T,T)
, the same argument used in the second reduction in the proof of Proposition
2.2
gives that
u\in W^{1,p}(-T,T)
, that is,
u
can not have any jump. Otherwise, since
\lambda_{p}^{+}(-T,T)>0
, it would be possible to construct a correction function
v\in W_{pw}^{1,p,+}(-T,T)\cap W^{1,p}(-T,T)
so that
\lVert v\rVert_{p}=1
and

\int_{-T}^{T}\left(v^{\prime}(t)^{+}\right)^{p}dt<\lambda_{p}^{+}(-T,T).

Third step:
We now assert that
u
is increasing in
(-T,T)
. Notice first that
u\in W^{1,p}(-T,T)
satisfies in the weak sense the Euler-Lagrange equation

-\left(\left((u^{\prime})^{+}\right)^{p-1}\right)^{\prime}=\lambda_{p}^{+}(-T,T)u^{p-1}\ \ {\rm in}\ (-T,T),

(8)

that is,

\int_{-T}^{T}\left(u^{\prime}(t)^{+}\right)^{p-1}\varphi^{\prime}(t)dt=\lambda_{p}^{+}(-T,T)\int_{-T}^{T}u(t)^{p-1}\varphi(t)dt

for every
\varphi\in W_{0}^{1,p}(-T,T)
. Since
u
is continuous in
(-T,T)
, if
v\in C^{1}(-T,T)
is a primitive of
-\lambda_{p}^{+}(-T,T)u^{p-1}
, then the above equality yields

\int_{-T}^{T}\left(\left(u^{\prime}(t)^{+}\right)^{p-1}-v(t)\right)\varphi^{\prime}(t)dt=0

for every
\varphi\in W_{0}^{1,p}(-T,T)
. Evoking the well-known DuBois-Reymond lemma, taking into account that
\left((u^{\prime})^{+}\right)^{p-1}\in L^{1}(-T,T)
, it follows that
\left((u^{\prime})^{+}\right)^{p-1}=v+c
in
(-T,T)
for some constant
c
and so
\left((u^{\prime})^{+}\right)^{p-1}
is continuously differentiable in
(-T,T)
. This implies that the equation (
8
) is satisfied in the classical sense in
(-T,T)
. Therefore, since the right-hand side of (
8
) is positive,
(u^{\prime})^{+}
is decreasing in
(-T,T)
. Thus,
u^{\prime}(t)^{+}>0
for every
t\in(-T,T)
, once
(u^{\prime})^{+}
is non-negative. Consequently,
u^{\prime}
is positive and so
u
is an increasing
C^{1}
function in
(-T,T)
.

Now consider the function

v(t)=\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} &u(2t+T)\ \ \text{if}\ t\in(-T,0),\\
&u(T-2t)\ \ \text{if}\ t\in[0,T).\end{aligned}\end{cases}

Clearly,
v\in W^{1,p}_{0}(-T,T)
,

\int_{-T}^{T}|v(t)|^{p}\ dt=1

and, since
u
is increasing in
(-T,T)
,

\int_{-T}^{T}|v^{\prime}(t)|^{p}\ dt=2^{p}\int_{-T}^{T}\left(u^{\prime}(t)^{+}\right)^{p}dt=\lambda_{1,p}(-T,T).

Therefore, we must have
v=\varphi_{p}
, which implies

u(t)=\varphi_{p}\left(\frac{t-T}{2}\right)\ \ \text{for}\ t\in(-T,T).

This concludes the proof of theorem.
∎

2.2
The case
b>0

This subsection is devoted to the complete study of the sharp asymmetric Poincaré inequality

\int_{-T}^{T}a^{p}\left(u^{\prime}(t)^{+}\right)^{p}+b^{p}\left(u^{\prime}(t)^{-}\right)^{p}dt\geq\lambda^{a,b}_{p}(-T,T)\int_{-T}^{T}|u(t)|^{p}dt

for every

and any fixed numbers

.

Our result is the following

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
p>1
and
T>0
. For any
a,b>0
, we have:

(i)

\lambda_{p}^{a,b}(-T,T)=\displaystyle\left(\frac{a+b}{2}\right)^{p}\lambda_{1,p}(-T,T)
;

(ii)

u\in W_{0}^{1,p}(-T,T)
is an extremizer of (

u=u_{p}
or
u=v_{p}
, where

u_{p}(t)=\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} &c\varphi_{p}\left(\frac{T(t-t_{0})}{T+t_{0}}\right)\ \ \text{if}\ t\in(-T,t_{0}),\\
&c\varphi_{p}\left(\frac{T(t-t_{0})}{T-t_{0}}\right)\ \ \text{if}\ t\in[t_{0},T)\end{aligned}\end{cases}

v_{p}(t)=\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} &-c\varphi_{p}\left(\frac{T(t+t_{0})}{T-t_{0}}\right)\ \ \text{if}\ t\in(-T,-t_{0}),\\

where

is any positive constant,
\varphi_{p}\in W^{1,p}_{0}(-T,T)
is the principal eigenfunction of the

-Laplace operator normalized by
\|\varphi_{p}\|_{p}=1
and

t_{0}=\left(\frac{a-b}{a+b}\right)T.

The lemma below will be used in the proof of this theorem:

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
(\alpha,\beta)\subset(-T,T)
be two intervals, let
f:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a continuous function and let
u\in C^{1}[-T,T]
be a weak solution of

-\Delta_{p}^{a,b}u=f(u)\ \text{in}\ (-T,T).

If
u(\alpha)=0
,
u^{\prime}(\beta)=0
and
u^{\prime}\geq 0
in
(\alpha,\beta)
, then the function

w(t)=\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} &u(t),\ \text{if}\ t\in[\alpha,\beta),\\
&u(2\beta-t),\ \text{if}\ t\in[\beta,2\beta-\alpha]\end{aligned}\end{cases}

belongs to
C^{1}[\alpha,2\beta-\alpha]
and is a weak solution of

\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} &-a^{p}\Delta_{p}w=f(w)\ \text{in}\ (\alpha,2\beta-\alpha),\\
&w(\alpha)=w(2\beta-\alpha)=0.\end{aligned}\end{cases}

Proof.

Clearly, we have
w\in C^{1}[\alpha,2\beta-\alpha]
. Take any
\varphi\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\alpha,2\beta-\alpha)
and consider the following functions for every
\varepsilon>0
:

\phi_{\varepsilon}(t)=\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} &\varphi(t)\ \ \text{if}\ t\in(\alpha,\beta-\varepsilon),\\
&-\frac{\varphi(\beta-\varepsilon)}{\varepsilon}(t-\beta)\ \ \text{if}\ t\in[\beta-\varepsilon,\beta),\\
&0\ \ \text{if}\ t\in\mathbb{R}\setminus(\alpha,\beta),\end{aligned}\end{cases}

\psi_{\varepsilon}(t)=\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} &\varphi(2\beta-t)\ \ \text{if}\ t\in(\alpha,\beta-\varepsilon),\\
&-\frac{\varphi(\beta+\varepsilon)}{\varepsilon}(t-\beta)\ \ \text{if}\ t\in[\beta-\varepsilon,\beta),\\
&0\ \ \text{if}\ t\in\mathbb{R}\setminus(\alpha,\beta).\end{aligned}\end{cases}

It is easy to check that
\phi_{\varepsilon},\psi_{\varepsilon}\in W^{1,p}_{0}(-L,L)
and

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\phi_{\varepsilon}(t)+\psi_{\varepsilon}(2\beta-t)

\displaystyle=\varphi(t)\ \text{for every}\ t\in(\alpha,2\beta-\alpha),

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\phi_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(t)-\psi_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(2\beta-t)

\displaystyle=\varphi^{\prime}(t)\ \text{for almost every}\ t\in(\alpha,2\beta-\alpha).

So, by the dominated convergence theorem, we derive

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\int_{\alpha}^{2\beta-\alpha}f(w)\left(\phi_{\varepsilon}(t)+\psi_{\varepsilon}(2\beta-t)\right)\,dt=\int_{\alpha}^{2\beta-\alpha}f(w)\varphi\,dt.

stands for the transposed matrix of
A
. One easily checks that
H_{A}\in\mathscr{H}
(resp.
\mathscr{H}_{P},\,\mathscr{H}_{D}
) and
u_{A}\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega_{A})
for any
H\in\mathscr{H}
(resp.
\mathscr{H}_{P},\,\mathscr{H}_{D}
) and
u\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
.

Some basic properties that follow directly from the definition of
\lambda_{1,p}^{H}(\Omega)
are:

(P1)

If
H\in\mathscr{H}
and
\alpha\in[0,+\infty)
then
\alpha H\in\mathscr{H}
and
\lambda_{p}^{\alpha H}(\Omega)=\alpha^{p}\lambda_{p}^{H}(\Omega)
for any bounded domain
\Omega
;

(P2)

If
H\in\mathscr{H}
and
\Omega_{1}
and
\Omega_{2}
are bounded domains such that
\Omega_{1}\subseteq\Omega_{2}
then
\lambda_{p}^{H}(\Omega_{1})\geq\lambda_{p}^{H}(\Omega_{2})
;

(P3)

If
G
and
H
are functions in
\mathscr{H}
such that
G\leq H
, then
\lambda_{p}^{G}(\Omega)\leq\lambda_{p}^{H}(\Omega)
for any bounded domain
\Omega
;

(P4)

If
H\in\mathscr{H}
then
\lambda_{p}^{H}(\Omega)=\lambda_{p}^{H_{A}}(\Omega_{A})
for any orthogonal matrix
A
. Moreover,
u\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
is a minimizer of
\lambda_{p}^{H}(\Omega)
if, and only if,
u_{A}\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega_{A})
is a minimizer of
\lambda_{p}^{H_{A}}(\Omega_{A})
.

Using these properties along with Theorem
2.1
and Proposition
4.2
, we can extend the one-dimensional Corollary
1.1
to its counterpart in dimension
2
. Precisely, we have:

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
p>1
, let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a bounded domain and let
H\in\mathscr{H}
. The level
\lambda_{p}^{H}(\Omega)
is positive if, and only if,
H\neq 0
.

Proof.

Let
H\in\mathscr{H}
be any asymmetric anisotropy. For
H=0
, we have
\lambda_{p}^{H}(\Omega)=0
. For
H\neq 0
, by Proposition
4.2
, there exists a rotation matrix
A
such that
H_{A}(x,y)\geq\|H\|y^{+}
for every
(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
. By Properties
(P1)
,
(P3)
and
(P4)
, we can consider
H(x,y)=H_{A}(x,y)=y^{+}
and
\Omega=\Omega_{A}
. Furthermore, by Property
(P2)
, it suffices to show that
\lambda_{p}^{H}(Q)>0
for any square
Q=(-T,T)\times(-T,T)
such that
\Omega\subset Q
.

On the other hand, by the strong Poincaré inequality stated in Theorem
2.1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\iint_{Q}H^{p}(\nabla u(x,y))\,dA

\displaystyle=\int_{-T}^{T}\int_{-T}^{T}\left(D_{y}u(x,y)^{+}\right)^{p}\,dy\,dx

\displaystyle\geq\int_{-T}^{T}\lambda^{+}_{p}(-T,T)\int_{-T}^{T}|u(x,y)|^{p}\,dy\,dx

\displaystyle=\lambda^{+}_{p}(-T,T)\iint_{Q}|u(x,y)|^{p}\,dA

for every
u\in C^{1}_{0}(Q)
, and so by density we derive
\lambda_{p}^{H}(Q)\geq\lambda^{+}_{p}(-T,T)>0
.
∎

We now concentrate our attention on the proof of Theorems
1.3
and
1.4
.

Let
H\in\mathscr{H}_{D}\setminus\{0\}
be such that
\ker(H)
is a line or a half-plane. By Proposition
4.1
, in each case there is a rotation matrix
A
such that
H_{A}(x,y)=ay^{+}+by^{-}
, where
a,b>0
when
\ker(H)
is a line and
a>0
and
b=0
when
\ker(H)
is a half-plane.

The following lemma on dimension reduction in PDEs is required in the proof of the mentioned theorems:

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
p>1
, let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a bounded domain and let
H(x,y)=ay^{+}+by^{-}
for constants
a>0
and
b\geq 0
. Consider the bounded open interval
I=\{x\in\mathbb{R}:\,\Omega_{x}\neq\emptyset\}
where
\Omega_{x}=\left\{y\in\mathbb{R}:\,(x,y)\in\Omega\right\}
. If
u\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
is a weak solution of the equation

-\Delta_{p}^{H}u=\lambda^{H}_{p}(\Omega)|u|^{p-2}u\ \ \text{in}\ \ \Omega

(20)

then, for almost every
x\in I
, the restriction

u_{x}(y)=u(x,y),\ \forall y\in\Omega_{x},

is a weak solution in
W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega_{x})
of the one-dimensional equation

-\Delta_{p}^{H}v=\lambda^{H}_{p}(\Omega)|v|^{p-2}v\ \ \text{in}\ \ \Omega_{x}.

Proof.

Let
u\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
be a weak solution of (
20
). By Theorem 10.35 of
[
28
]
, we have
u_{x}\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega_{x})
for almost every
x\in I
. In particular,
|u|^{p-2}u\in L^{1}(\Omega)
and
|u_{x}|^{p-2}u_{x}\in L^{1}(\Omega_{x})
for almost every
x\in I
. The proof that the restriction
u_{x}
is a weak solution will be organized into two cases.

First case:
Assume first that
\partial\Omega
is of
C^{1}
class.

For each
x\in I
, the set
\Omega_{x}
is a finite union of open intervals, so it is enough to prove for the case that
\Omega_{x}
is an interval to be denoted by
(c_{x},d_{x})
. Using that
\Omega
has
C^{1}
boundary, the functions

x\mapsto c_{x}\ \ {\rm and}\ \ x\mapsto d_{x}

(21)

are piecewise
C^{1}
in
I
. For each
x\in I
, consider the map

\displaystyle\Gamma_{x}\colon

\displaystyle C^{\infty}_{0}(0,1)\longrightarrow C^{\infty}_{0}\left(\Omega_{x}\right)

\displaystyle\varphi\longmapsto\varphi\left(\frac{\mathord{\cdot}-c_{x}}{d_{x}-c_{x}}\right).

Note that
\Gamma_{x}
is a bijection for every
x\in I
. Take now any
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(0,1)
and choose
x_{0}\in I
and
\delta_{x_{0}}>0
, so that the functions in (
21
) are
C^{1}
in
(x_{0}-\delta_{x_{0}},x_{0}+\delta_{x_{0}})
and in addition

d_{x}-c_{x}\geq\frac{d_{x_{0}}-c_{x_{0}}}{2}>0,\ \ \forall x\in(x_{0}-\delta_{x_{0}},x_{0}+\delta_{x_{0}}).

Let any
\eta\in C^{\infty}_{0}(x_{0}-\delta_{x_{0}},x_{0}+\delta_{x_{0}})
and define the function
\phi(x,y)=\eta(x)\Gamma_{x}(\varphi(y))
. Notice that
\phi\in C^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
and so taking
\phi
as a test function in (
20
), we have

\int_{x_{0}-\delta_{x_{0}}}^{x_{0}+\delta_{x_{0}}}\int_{\Omega_{x}}G(D_{y}u(x,y))\frac{\eta(x)\Gamma_{x}(\varphi^{\prime}(y))}{d_{x}-c_{x}}dydx=\lambda^{H}_{p}(\Omega)\int_{x_{0}-\delta_{x_{0}}}^{x_{0}+\delta_{x_{0}}}\int_{\Omega_{x}}|u(x,y)|^{p-2}u(x,y)\eta(x)\Gamma_{x}(\varphi(y))dydx,

where
G(t)=\left(a^{p}[t^{+}]^{p-1}-b^{p}[t^{-}]^{p-1}\right)
for
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

On the other hand, this equality can be rewritten as

\int_{x_{0}-\delta_{x_{0}}}^{x_{0}+\delta_{x_{0}}}\left[G(D_{y}u(x,y))\frac{\Gamma_{x}(\varphi^{\prime}(y))}{d_{x}-c_{x}}\,dy-\lambda^{H}_{p}(\Omega)\int_{\Omega_{x}}|u(x,y)|^{p-2}u(x,y)\Gamma_{x}(\varphi(y))\,dy\right]\eta(x)\,dx=0

for all
\eta\in C^{\infty}_{0}(x_{0}-\delta_{x_{0}},x_{0}+\delta_{x_{0}})
. Since the function

x\mapsto\int_{\Omega_{x}}G(D_{y}u(x,y))\frac{\Gamma_{x}(\varphi^{\prime}(y))}{d_{x}-c_{x}}\,dy-\lambda^{H}_{p}(\Omega)\int_{\Omega_{x}}|u(x,y)|^{p-2}u(x,y)\Gamma_{x}(\varphi(y))\,dy

belongs to
L^{1}_{loc}(x_{0}-\delta_{x_{0}},x_{0}+\delta_{x_{0}})
, we must have

\int_{\Omega_{x}}G(D_{y}u(x,y))\frac{\Gamma_{x}(\varphi^{\prime}(y))}{d_{x}-c_{x}}\,dy-\lambda^{H}_{p}(\Omega)\int_{\Omega_{x}}|u(x,y)|^{p-2}u(x,y)\Gamma_{x}(\varphi(y))\,dy=0

for almost every
x
in
(x_{0}-\delta_{x_{0}},x_{0}+\delta_{x_{0}})
.

Consequently, since
\Gamma_{x}
is a bijection, the function
u_{x}(y)=u(x,y)
satisfies

\int_{\Omega_{x}}G(u_{x}^{\prime})\psi^{\prime}\,dy=\lambda^{H}_{p}(\Omega)\int_{\Omega_{x}}|u_{x}|^{p-2}u_{x}\psi\,dy

for every
\psi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega_{x})
for almost every
x
in
(x_{0}-\delta_{x_{0}},x_{0}+\delta_{x_{0}})
. Since we can cover
I
, up to a countable set, by an enumerate union of intervals like
(x_{k}-\delta_{x_{k}},x_{k}+\delta_{x_{k}})
, the above equality occurs in the general case where
\Omega_{x}
can be disconnected, and it just says that
u_{x}
satisfies in the weak sense

-\Delta_{p}^{H}u_{x}=\lambda^{H}_{p}(\Omega)|u_{x}|^{p-2}u_{x}\ \ \text{in}\ \ \Omega_{x}.

Second case:
Consider now
\Omega
an arbitrary bounded domain.

One always can write

\Omega=\bigcup_{j=1}^{\infty}\Omega_{j},

where
\Omega_{j}
are bounded domains with
C^{1}
boundary and
\Omega_{j}\subseteq\Omega_{j+1}
for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
. If
\psi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega_{x})
, then there is
j_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that the support of
\psi
is contained in
(\Omega_{j})_{x}
for all
j\geq j_{0}
. Thus, from the first part,
u_{x}=u(x,y)
satisfies

\int_{(\Omega_{j})_{x}}G(u_{x}^{\prime})\psi^{\prime}\,dy=\lambda^{H}_{p}(\Omega)\int_{(\Omega_{j})_{x}}|u_{x}|^{p-2}u_{x}\psi\,dy

for almost every
x
in
I
(module a countable union of countable sets) and all
j\geq j_{0}
. Taking
j\to\infty
in the above equality, we derive

\int_{\Omega_{x}}G(u_{x}^{\prime})\psi^{\prime}\,dy=\lambda^{H}_{p}(\Omega)\int_{\Omega_{x}}|u_{x}|^{p-2}u_{x}\psi\,dy

almost everywhere for
x\in I
, which proves the lemma.
∎

Proof of Theorems
1.3
and
1.4
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a bounded domain and let
H\in\mathscr{H}_{D}
be an asymmetric anisotropy such that
\ker(H)
is a line or a half-plane. By Proposition
4.1
, there is a rotation matrix
A
such that
H_{A}(x,y)=ay^{+}+by^{-}
for some constants
a>0
and
b\geq 0
. For convenience, by Property
(P4)
, we assume that
H=H_{A}
and
\Omega=\Omega_{A}
. From the
C^{1}
regularity of
H^{p}
in
\mathbb{R}\times(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\})
, it follows that
\lambda^{H}_{p}(\Omega)
is attained in
W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
if and only if it is a fundamental frequency.

We first consider the case
b=0
. Let
\Omega_{x}
and
I\subset\mathbb{R}
be as in the previous lemma. Assume by contradiction that
\lambda^{H}_{p}(\Omega)
is a fundamental frequency, i.e. (
20
) admits a nontrivial weak solution
u\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
. Since
u
is non-zero, by Lemma
4.1
,
u_{x_{0}}\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega_{x_{0}})
, where
u_{x_{0}}(y)=u(x_{0},y)
, is a nontrivial weak solution of

-\Delta_{p}^{H}u_{x_{0}}=\lambda^{H}_{p}(\Omega)|u_{x_{0}}|^{p-2}u_{x_{0}}\ \ \text{in}\ \ \tilde{\Omega}_{x_{0}}

for some
x_{0}\in I
and some connected component
\tilde{\Omega}_{x_{0}}
of
\Omega_{x_{0}}
. Thus,
\lambda^{a,0}_{p}(\tilde{\Omega}_{x_{0}})\leq\lambda^{H}_{p}(\Omega)
. Since
u_{x_{0}}\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega_{x_{0}})
, by Theorem
2.1
, we have
\lambda^{a,0}_{p}(\tilde{\Omega}_{x_{0}})<\lambda^{H}_{p}(\Omega)
. By that same theorem, we can take an interval
[r,s]\subset\tilde{\Omega}_{x_{0}}
such that
\lambda^{a,0}_{p}(r,s)<\lambda^{H}_{p}(\Omega)
. By definition, there is a function
\psi\in W^{1,p}_{0}(r,s)
with
\|\psi\|_{p}=1
such that

\int_{r}^{s}a^{p}\left(\psi^{\prime}(y)^{+}\right)^{p}dy<\lambda^{H}_{p}(\Omega).

Choose now
c<d
so that
R:=(c,d)\times(r,s)\subset\Omega
and any positive function
\varphi\in W^{1,p}_{0}(c,d)
with
\|\varphi\|_{p}=1
. Then, the function
u(x,y):=\varphi(x)\psi(y)
for
(x,y)\in R
belongs to
W^{1,p}_{0}(R)\subset W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
and

\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p}\,dA=\int_{R}|u(x,y)|^{p}\,dA=\left(\int_{c}^{d}\varphi(x)^{p}\,dx\right)\left(\int_{r}^{s}|\psi(y)|^{p}\,dy\right)=1,

\int_{\Omega}H^{p}(\nabla u)\,dA=\int_{R}a^{p}\left(D_{y}u(x,y)^{+}\right)^{p}\,dA=\left(\int_{c}^{d}\varphi(x)^{p}\,dx\right)\left(\int_{r}^{s}a^{p}\left(\psi^{\prime}(y)^{+}\right)^{p}\,dy\right)<\lambda^{H}_{p}(\Omega),

which contradicts the definition of
\lambda^{H}_{p}(\Omega)
. This proves Theorem
1.3
.

We now focus on the characterization in the case
b>0
given in Theorem
1.4
.

Assume first that
\lambda^{H}_{p}(\Omega)
is a fundamental frequency. Let
I
be the bounded open interval as above, so (i) in (b) is clearly satisfied. Since
a,b>0
, Theorem
2.2
guarantees the existence of a number
L>0
such that
\lambda^{a,b}_{p}(0,L)=\lambda^{H}_{p}(\Omega)
. For these choices of
I
and
L
, we show (ii) in (b) by contradiction. Assume that there is a point
x_{0}\in I
such that
\Omega_{x_{0}}
contains a connected component
\tilde{\Omega}_{x_{0}}
whose measure is greater than
L
. In this case, we can take a rectangle
R_{1}:=(c,d)\times(r,s)\subset\Omega
with
L_{1}:=s-r>L
. Take also a principal eigenfunction
u_{p}\in W^{1,p}_{0}(r,s)
corresponding to
\lambda^{a,b}_{p}\left(0,L_{1}\right)
such that
\|u_{p}\|_{p}=1
and any positive function
\varphi\in W^{1,p}_{0}(c,d)
such that
\|\varphi\|_{p}=1
. Define
u(x,y):=\varphi(x)u_{p}(y)
for
(x,y)\in R_{1}
. It is clear that
u\in W_{0}^{1,p}(R_{1})\subset W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
and
\|u\|_{p}=1
. Furthermore,

\displaystyle\iint_{\Omega}H^{p}(\nabla u)\,dA

\displaystyle=\iint_{R}a^{p}\left(D_{y}u(x,y)^{+}\right)^{p}+b^{p}\left(D_{y}u(x,y)^{-}\right)^{p}dydx

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{c}^{d}|\varphi(x)|^{p}\,dx\right)\left(\int_{r}^{s}a^{p}\left(u^{\prime}_{p}(y)^{+}\right)^{p}+\,b^{p}\left(u^{\prime}_{p}(y)^{-}\right)^{p}dy\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{r}^{s}a^{p}\left(u^{\prime}_{p}(y)^{+}\right)^{p}+\,b^{p}\left(u^{\prime}_{p}(y)^{-}\right)^{p}dy

\displaystyle=\lambda^{a,b}_{p}(0,L_{1})\int_{r}^{s}|u_{p}(y)|^{p}\,dy

\displaystyle=\lambda^{a,b}_{p}(0,L_{1}).

This leads to the contradiction
\lambda^{H}_{p}(\Omega)\leq\lambda^{a,b}_{p}(0,L_{1})<\lambda^{a,b}_{p}(0,L)
.

For the proof of (iii) in (b), we use the assumption that
\lambda^{H}_{p}(\Omega)
is a fundamental frequency. Let
u\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
be a corresponding eigenfunction. By Lemma
4.1
, for almost every
x\in I
,
u_{x}\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega_{x})
is a weak solution of

-\Delta_{p}^{H}u_{x}=\lambda^{H}_{p}(\Omega)|u_{x}|^{p-2}u_{x}\ \ \text{in}\ \ \Omega_{x}.

In particular, by elliptic regularity, we have
u(x,\cdot)\in C^{1}(\bar{\Omega}_{x})
.

We assert that
u(x,\cdot)=0
,
u(x,\cdot)>0
or
u(x,\cdot)<0
in each connected component
\tilde{\Omega}_{x}
of
\Omega_{x}
. Indeed, if
u(x,\cdot)
is non-zero in some connected component
\tilde{\Omega}_{x}
, then
\lambda^{a,b}_{p}(\tilde{\Omega}_{x})\leq\lambda^{H}_{p}(\Omega)
. On the other hand, from the previous argument, we already know that strict inequality cannot occur. Therefore,
\lambda^{a,b}_{p}(\tilde{\Omega}_{x})=\lambda^{H}_{p}(\Omega)
and the claim follows from Theorem
2.2
.

We now prove that there is an open subinterval
I^{\prime}\subset I
such that either
u(x,\cdot)>0
or
u(x,\cdot)<0
in some component
\tilde{\Omega}_{x}
of
\Omega_{x}
, and so
\lambda^{a,b}_{p}(\tilde{\Omega}_{x})=\lambda^{H}_{p}(\Omega)
for every
x\in I^{\prime}
. Since
u
is non-zero somewhere in
\Omega
, there is a subset
X\subset I
with
|X|>0
(i.e. positive Lebesgue measure) such that
u(x,\cdot)
has sign in some connected component
\tilde{\Omega}_{x}
of
\Omega_{x}
for every
x\in X
. Similarly to the definition of
I
, let
J
be the bounded open interval such that
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}\times J
and
\Omega^{y}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}:\,(x,y)\in\Omega\}\neq\emptyset
for every
y\in J
. We know that
u(\cdot,y)\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega^{y})
and so, by Morrey’s Theorem,
u(\cdot,y)\in C(\bar{\Omega}^{y})
for almost every
y\in J
.

Let
x_{0}\in X
and take
y_{0}\in\tilde{\Omega}_{x_{0}}
so that
u(\cdot,y_{0})\in C(\bar{\Omega}^{y_{0}})
. Assume
u(x_{0},y_{0})>0
(the negative case is analogous). By continuity, there is a number
\delta>0
such that
u(x,y_{0})>0
for every
x\in I^{\prime}:=(x_{0}-\delta,x_{0}+\delta)
. Consequently,
u(x,\cdot)>0
in
\tilde{\Omega}_{x}
(here
\tilde{\Omega}_{x}
is the connected component of
\Omega_{x}
such that
(x,y_{0})
belongs to it), and so
\lambda^{a,b}_{p}(\tilde{\Omega}_{x})=\lambda^{H}_{p}(\Omega)=\lambda^{a,b}_{p}\left(0,L\right)
for every
x\in I^{\prime}
, which is equivalent to the equality
|\tilde{\Omega}_{x}|=L
for every
x\in I^{\prime}
. Furthermore, the
C^{0,1}
regularity of
\partial\Omega
implies that
\Omega^{\prime}:=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:\,x\in I^{\prime},\,y\in\tilde{\Omega}_{x}\}
is a
C^{0,1}
sub-domain of
\Omega
. Hence, the assertion (iii) holds for the number
L
as defined above.

Conversely, let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a bounded domain with
C^{0,1}
boundary and let
H\in\mathscr{H}_{D}\setminus\{0\}
be an anisotropy satisfying the conditions (i), (ii) and (iii) and whose kernel is a line. Let
A
be a rotation matrix such that
H_{A}(x,y)=ay^{+}+by^{-}
with
a,b>0
. By Property
(P4)
, it suffices to construct a minimizer for
\lambda^{H_{A}}_{p}(\Omega_{A})
in
W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega_{A})
.

Again set
\Omega=\Omega_{A}
and
H=H_{A}
. Let
I^{\prime}
and
I
be as in the item (b) of statement and let
\Omega_{x}
be as above. Given any
u\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
with
\|u\|_{p}=1
, since
u(x,\cdot)\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega_{x})
for almost every
x\in I
, using (ii) and the one-dimensional asymmetric Poincaré inequality, we get

\displaystyle\iint_{\Omega}H^{p}(\nabla u)\,dA

\displaystyle=\int_{I}\int_{\Omega_{x}}a^{p}\left(D_{y}u(x,y)^{+}\right)^{p}+b^{p}\left(D_{y}u(x,y)^{-}\right)^{p}dydx

\displaystyle\geq\int_{I}\lambda^{a,b}_{p}\left(0,L\right)\int_{\Omega_{x}}|u(x,y)|^{p}\,dydx

\displaystyle=\lambda^{a,b}_{p}\left(0,L\right)\iint_{\Omega}|u|^{p}\,dA

\displaystyle=\lambda^{a,b}_{p}\left(0,L\right),

so that
\lambda^{H}_{p}(\Omega)\geq\lambda^{a,b}_{p}\left(0,L\right)
.

We now construct a function
u_{0}\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
such that
\|u_{0}\|_{p}=1
and

\iint_{\Omega}H^{p}(\nabla u_{0})\,dA=\lambda^{a,b}_{p}\left(0,L\right).

Since the boundary of
\Omega
is of
C^{0,1}
class and (iii) is satisfied, there are an open interval
I_{0}\subset I^{\prime}
and a Lipschitz function
g:\bar{I}_{0}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that
\Omega_{0}:=\{(x,y):\,x\in I_{0},\,g(x)<y<g(x)+L\}\subset\Omega^{\prime}\subset\Omega
. Let
u_{p}\in W_{0}^{1,p}(0,L)
be an eigenfunction corresponding to
\lambda^{a,b}_{p}\left(0,L\right)
with
\|u_{p}\|_{p}=1
and take any positive function
\varphi\in W_{0}^{1,p}(I_{0})
with
\|\varphi\|_{p}=1
. Define

u_{0}(x,y)=\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} &\varphi(x)u_{p}(y-g(x))\ \ \text{if}\ (x,y)\in\Omega_{0},\\
&0\ \ \text{if}\ (x,y)\in\Omega\setminus\Omega_{0}.\end{aligned}\end{cases}

Then,
u_{0}\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega_{0})\subset W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
, since
g
is Lipschitz, and satisfies

\displaystyle\iint_{\Omega}|u_{0}|^{p}\,dA

\displaystyle=\iint_{\Omega_{0}}|u_{0}|^{p}\,dA

\displaystyle=\int_{I_{0}}\int_{g(x)}^{g(x)+L}\varphi(x)^{p}|u_{p}(y-g(x))|^{p}\,dydx

\displaystyle=\int_{I_{0}}\int_{0}^{L}\varphi(x)^{p}|u_{p}(y)|^{p}\,dydx

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{I_{0}}\varphi(x)^{p}\,dx\right)\left(\int_{0}^{L}|u_{p}(y)|^{p}\,dy\right)

\displaystyle=1,

\displaystyle\iint_{\Omega}H^{p}(\nabla u_{0})\,dA

\displaystyle=\int_{I_{0}}\int_{g(x)}^{g(x)+L}\varphi(x)^{p}\left(a^{p}\left(u_{p}^{\prime}(y-g(x))^{+}\right)^{p}+b^{p}\left(u_{p}^{\prime}(y-g(x))^{-}\right)^{p}\right)\,dydx

\displaystyle=\int_{I_{0}}\int_{0}^{L}\varphi(x)^{p}\left(a^{p}\left(u_{p}^{\prime}(y)^{+}\right)^{p}+b^{p}\left(u_{p}^{\prime}(y)^{-}\right)^{p}\right)\,dydx

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{I_{0}}|\varphi(x)|^{p}\,dx\right)\left(\int_{0}^{L}a^{p}\left(u_{p}^{\prime}(y)^{+}\right)^{p}+b^{p}\left(u_{p}^{\prime}(y)^{-}\right)^{p}\,dy\right)

\displaystyle=\lambda^{a,b}_{p}\left(0,L\right)\int_{0}^{L}|u_{p}(y)|^{p}\,dy

\displaystyle=\lambda^{a,b}_{p}\left(0,L\right).

Therefore,
\lambda^{H}_{p}(\Omega)=\lambda^{a,b}_{p}\left(0,L\right)
and
u_{0}
is a minimizer to
\lambda^{H}_{p}(\Omega)
, which is an eigenfunction. This concludes the proof.
∎

5
Extended maximization conjecture for
n=2

The well-known isoperimetric maximization conjecture for the
p
-Laplace in dimension
n=2
states that

\sup\{\lambda_{1,p}(\Omega):\,\forall\ \text{bounded domain\ }\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}\ {\rm with}\ |\Omega|=1\}=\infty.

It is motivated by its validity for
p=2
which follows from the knowledge of fundamental frequencies given by

\lambda_{1,2}(\Omega)=\pi^{2}\left(\frac{1}{a^{2}}+\frac{1}{b^{2}}\right)

for rectangles under the form
\Omega=(0,a)\times(0,b)
, being computed by Rayleigh in the century XIX.
The above conjecture has recently been proved in dimension
2
within the context of general norms, see
[
33
]
.

Next we establish the following extension of the conjecture to any non-zero two-dimensional asymmetric anisotropy and any
p>1
:

Theorem 5.1

(isoperimetric maximization)
.

Let
p>1
. For any
H\in\mathscr{H}\setminus\{0\}
, we have

\sup\{\lambda^{H}_{p}(\Omega):\,\forall\ \text{bounded domain\ }\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}\ {\rm with}\ |\Omega|=1\}=\infty.

The main ingredient in the proof of this result deals with the explicit value of
\lambda^{H}_{p}(\Omega)
for certain degenerate asymmetric anisotropies. Precisely, take
H\in\mathscr{H}\setminus\{0\}
so that
\ker(H)
is a line or a half-plane. As is already known, there is a rotation matrix
A
such that
H_{A}(x,y)=ay^{+}+by^{-}
, where
a>0
and
b\geq 0
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
p>1
, let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be any bounded domain and let
H\in\mathscr{H}_{D}\setminus\{0\}
and
A
be as above. Then, we have

\lambda^{H}_{p}(\Omega)=\lambda^{a,b}_{p}(0,L_{\Omega_{A}}),

where

L_{\Omega}=\sup\{|J|:\,\ \text{for open intervals}\ J\subset\Omega_{x}\ \text{over all}\ x\in I\}.

The proof of this result makes use of Theorems
1.1
,
1.2
and
1.4
and also the following lemma which will be useful in the next section.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
H_{0}\in\mathscr{H}_{D}
and
H_{\varepsilon}\in\mathscr{H}
be anisotropies such that
H_{\varepsilon}\geq H_{0}
for every
\varepsilon>0
and
H_{\varepsilon}\rightarrow H_{0}
in
\mathscr{H}
. Then, for any
p>1
and any bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
, we have

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\lambda_{p}^{H_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega)=\lambda_{p}^{H_{0}}(\Omega).

Proof.

First, since
H_{\varepsilon}\geq H_{0}
for every
\varepsilon>0
, we have

\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\lambda_{p}^{H_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega)\geq\lambda_{p}^{H_{0}}(\Omega).

On the other hand, for each
\delta>0
, let
u_{\delta}\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
with
\lVert u_{\delta}\rVert_{p}=1
be such that

\iint_{\Omega}H_{0}^{p}(\nabla u_{\delta})\,dA<\lambda_{p}^{H_{0}}(\Omega)+\delta.

Assuming without loss of generality that

|H_{\varepsilon}(x,y)-H_{0}(x,y)|\leq\varepsilon|(x,y)|

for every
(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
\varepsilon>0
, we get

\displaystyle\lambda_{p}^{H_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega)-\lambda_{p}^{H_{0}}(\Omega)

\displaystyle\leq\iint_{\Omega}H_{\varepsilon}^{p}(\nabla u_{\delta})-H_{0}^{p}(\nabla u_{\delta})\,dA+\delta

\displaystyle\leq p\iint_{\Omega}H_{\varepsilon}^{p-1}(\nabla u_{\delta})\left(H_{\varepsilon}(\nabla u_{\delta})-H_{0}(\nabla u_{\delta})\right)\,dA+\delta

\displaystyle\leq p\iint_{\Omega}H_{\varepsilon}^{p-1}(\nabla u_{\delta})\left(\varepsilon|\nabla u_{\delta}|+H_{0}(\nabla u_{\delta})-H_{0}(\nabla u_{\delta})\right)\,dA+\delta

\displaystyle=p\varepsilon\iint_{\Omega}H_{\varepsilon}^{p-1}(\nabla u_{\delta})|\nabla u_{\delta}|\,dA+\delta

\displaystyle=p\varepsilon\iint_{\Omega}\left(H_{0}(\nabla u_{\delta})+\varepsilon|\nabla u_{\delta}|\right)^{p-1}|\nabla u_{\delta}|\,dA+\delta

for
\delta>0
fixed and every
\varepsilon>0
. Hence, letting
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
, we derive

\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\lambda_{p}^{H_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega)\leq\lambda_{p}^{H_{0}}(\Omega)+\delta

for every
\delta>0
. Therefore,

\lambda_{p}^{H_{0}}(\Omega)\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\lambda_{p}^{H_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega)\leq\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\lambda_{p}^{H_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega)\leq\lambda_{p}^{H_{0}}(\Omega)

and the conclusion follows.
∎

Proof of Proposition
5.1
.

Assume first that
H(x,y)=ay^{+}+by^{-}
with
a,b>0
. By the item (ii) of Theorem
1.4
, the statement follows directly when
\Omega
is a rectangle whose sides are parallel to the coordinate axes, that is,
\lambda^{H}_{p}(\Omega)=\lambda^{a,b}_{p}(0,L_{\Omega})
for such particular cases of
H
and
\Omega
and any
b>0
.

Now let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be any bounded domain and let
u\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
. Since
u(x,\cdot)\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega_{x})
for almost every
x\in I
, we have

\displaystyle\iint_{\Omega}H^{p}(\nabla u)\,dA

\displaystyle=\int_{I}\int_{\Omega_{x}}H^{p}(\nabla u(x,y))\,dydx

\displaystyle=\int_{I}\int_{\Omega_{x}}a^{p}\left(D_{y}u(x,y)^{+}\right)^{p}+b^{p}\left(D_{y}u(x,y)^{-}\right)^{p}dydx

\displaystyle\geq\int_{I}\lambda^{a,b}_{p}(0,L_{\Omega})\int_{\Omega_{x}}|u(x,y)|^{p}dydx

\displaystyle=\lambda^{a,b}_{p}(0,L_{\Omega})\iint_{\Omega}|u|^{p}\,dA,

so that
\lambda^{H}_{p}(\Omega)\geq\lambda^{a,b}_{p}(0,L_{\Omega})
.

On the other hand, taking a sequence of open rectangles
R_{k}=(a_{k},b_{k})\times(c_{k},d_{k})\subset\Omega
such that
L_{k}=d_{k}-c_{k}\rightarrow L_{\Omega}
as
k\rightarrow\infty
, by Property
(P2)
and the previous remark, we obtain

\lambda^{H}_{p}(\Omega)\leq\lambda^{H}_{p}(R_{k})=\lambda^{a,b}_{p}(0,L_{k}),

and letting
k\rightarrow\infty
, we derive

\lambda^{H}_{p}(\Omega)\leq\lambda^{a,b}_{p}(0,L_{\Omega}).

This concludes the proof for
H(x,y)=ay^{+}+by^{-}
with
a,b>0
and any bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Now letting
b\rightarrow 0
, thanks to Lemma
5.1
and Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
, the same conclusion also occurs for
b=0
.

Finally, when both
H
is a degenerate asymmetric anisotropy and
A
is a matrix as in the statement, by Property
(P4)
, we have

\lambda_{p}^{H}(\Omega)=\lambda_{p}^{H_{A}}(\Omega_{A})=\lambda^{a,b}_{p}(0,L_{\Omega_{A}})

for every
a>0
,
b\geq 0
and any bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
5.1
.

Let any
H\in\mathscr{H}\setminus\{0\}
. By Proposition
4.2
, there always exist a rotation matrix
A
and a constant
a>0
such that
H_{A}(x,y)\geq H_{0}(x,y):=ay^{+}
. Consider the open rectangle
\Omega_{k}=A(R_{k})
, where
R_{k}=(0,k)\times(0,1/k)
for each integer
k\geq 1
. Clearly,
|\Omega_{k}|=1
and, by Properties
(P3)
and
(P4)
and Proposition
5.1
, we get

\lambda_{p}^{H}(\Omega_{k})=\lambda_{p}^{H_{A}}(R_{k})\geq\lambda_{p}^{H_{0}}(R_{k})=\lambda^{a,0}_{p}(0,1/k).

Letting
k\rightarrow\infty
in the above inequality, by Theorem
1.1
, we deduce that
\lambda_{1,p}^{H}(\Omega_{k})\rightarrow\infty
as wished.
∎

6
Anisotropic spectral optimization for
n=2

This section is dedicated to the proof of Theorems
1.5
and
1.6
.

Consider the anisotropic sphere

\mathbb{S}(\mathscr{H})=\{H\in\mathscr{H}:\|H\|=1\}.

We recall that the optimal constants in (
5
) are given by

\displaystyle\Lambda^{\max}(\Omega)

\displaystyle=\sup\left\{\lambda_{1}(-\Delta_{p}^{H}):\,-\Delta_{p}^{H}\in{\cal E}(\Omega)\ \ {\rm and}\ \ H\in\mathbb{S}(\mathscr{H})\right\},

\displaystyle\Lambda^{\min}(\Omega)

\displaystyle=\inf\left\{\lambda_{1}(-\Delta_{p}^{H}):\,-\Delta_{p}^{H}\in{\cal E}(\Omega)\ \ {\rm and}\ \ H\in\mathbb{S}(\mathscr{H})\right\}.

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

Let
N(x,y)=|(x,y)|
. The computation of
\Lambda_{p}^{\max}(\Omega)
is immediate, since
H\leq N
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
for every
H\in\mathbb{S}(\mathscr{H})
,
N\in\mathbb{S}(\mathscr{H})
and
\lambda^{N}_{p}(\Omega)=\lambda_{1,p}(\Omega)
. In particular, multiples of the
p
-Laplace operator
-\Delta_{p}
on
\Omega
are extremizers for the second inequality in (
5
). Moreover, we claim that these ones are unique provided that all boundary points of
\Omega
satisfy the Wiener condition. It suffices to show the rigidity of the equality on
\mathbb{S}(\mathscr{H})
.

Let any
H\in\mathbb{S}(\mathscr{H})
with
H\neq N
. Next we prove that

\lambda^{H}_{p}(\Omega)<\lambda^{N}_{p}(\Omega).

Consider the positive eigenfunction
\varphi_{p}\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
corresponding to
\lambda_{1,p}(\Omega)
such that
\|\varphi_{p}\|_{p}=1
. From the interior elliptic regularity theory for the
p
-Laplace operator (
[
46
]
), we have
\varphi_{p}\in C^{1}(\Omega)
and, by assumption, since we are assuming that
\partial\Omega
satisfies the Wiener condition, the boundary elliptic regularity theory gives
\varphi_{p}\in C(\overline{\Omega})
(
[
35
,
38
,
39
]
). Using that
\varphi_{p}=0
on
\partial\Omega
, we then show that
B_{\varepsilon}(0,0)\subset\nabla\varphi_{p}(\Omega)
for every
\varepsilon>0
small enough. In fact, set
D={\rm diam}(\Omega)
and
M=\max_{(x,y)\in\overline{\Omega}}\varphi_{p}(x,y)>0
, and take
\varepsilon=D^{-1}M
. Given any
(\eta,\xi)\in B_{\varepsilon}(0,0)
, consider the function

v(x,y):=\eta x+\xi y-\varphi_{p}(x,y)\ \ {\rm for}\ \ (x,y)\in\overline{\Omega}.

By continuity and compactness,
v
admits a global minimum point
(\tilde{x},\tilde{y})\in\overline{\Omega}
. We claim that
(\tilde{x},\tilde{y})\in\Omega
. Otherwise, let
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in\Omega
be a global maximum point of
\varphi_{p}
in
\overline{\Omega}
. Using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we derive the contradiction

v(\bar{x},\bar{y})-v(\tilde{x},\tilde{y})=\eta(\bar{x}-\tilde{x})+\xi(\bar{y}-\tilde{y})-M\leq|(\eta,\xi)|\,|(\bar{x}-\tilde{x},\bar{y}-\tilde{y})|-M<\varepsilon D-M=0.

Therefore,
(\tilde{x},\tilde{y})\in\Omega
and so
\nabla\varphi_{p}(\tilde{x},\tilde{y})=(\eta,\xi)
.

Consequently, for some point
(x_{0},y_{0})\in\Omega
, we have

H(\nabla\varphi_{p}(x_{0},y_{0}))<N(\nabla\varphi_{p}(x_{0},y_{0}))=|\nabla\varphi_{p}(x_{0},y_{0})|.

Thus,
H(\nabla\varphi_{p})<|\nabla\varphi_{p}|
in the set
\Omega_{\delta}:=B_{\delta}(x_{0},y_{0})
for some
\delta>0
small enough. Using this strict inequality in
\Omega_{\delta}
and
H(\nabla\varphi_{p})\leq|\nabla\varphi_{p}|
in
\Omega\setminus\Omega_{\delta}
, we get

\displaystyle\lambda^{H}_{p}(\Omega)

\displaystyle\leq\iint_{\Omega}H^{p}(\nabla\varphi_{p})\,dA

\displaystyle=\iint_{\Omega_{\delta}}H^{p}(\nabla\varphi_{p})\,dA+\iint_{\Omega\setminus\Omega_{\delta}}H^{p}(\nabla\varphi_{p})\,dA

\displaystyle<\iint_{\Omega}|\nabla\varphi_{p}|^{p}\,dA

\displaystyle=\lambda_{1,p}(\Omega)

\displaystyle=\lambda^{N}_{p}(\Omega).

∎

The remainder of the section is dedicated to the proof of Theorem
1.6
by means of the next two propositions. For their statements, it is convenient to introduce the subsets of
\mathscr{H}
:

\displaystyle\mathscr{H}_{0}

\displaystyle=\left\{H\in\mathscr{H}\colon\ker(H)\ \text{is a half-plane}\right\},

\displaystyle\mathbb{S}(\mathscr{H}_{0})

\displaystyle=\left\{H\in\mathscr{H}_{0}\colon\lVert H\rVert=1\right\}.

We also need this result that comes directly from Proposition
4.2
:

Corollary 6.1
.

For any
H\in\mathbb{S}(\mathscr{H})
, there is
H_{0}\in\mathbb{S}(\mathscr{H}_{0})
such that
H\geq H_{0}
.

Proof.

Let
A
be as in Proposition
4.2
and define
H_{0}(x,y)=(\langle(x,y),A(0,1)\rangle)^{+}
, it is clear that
H_{0}\in\mathbb{S}(\mathscr{H}_{0})
. We also have

H(x,y)=H(AA^{T}(x,y))=H_{A}(A^{T}(x,y))\geq\|H\|(\langle A^{T}(x,y),(0,1)\rangle)^{+}=(\langle(x,y),A(0,1)\rangle)^{+}=H_{0}(x,y).

∎

Next we provide the proof of the last theorem of the introduction.

Proof of Theorem
1.6
.

For the equality

\Lambda^{\min}(\Omega)=\inf_{H\in\mathbb{S}(\mathscr{H}_{0})}\lambda_{p}^{H}(\Omega),

(22)

from the definition of
\Lambda_{1,p}^{\min}(\Omega)
and Corollary
6.1
, note first that

\Lambda^{\min}(\Omega)\geq\inf_{H\in\mathbb{S}(\mathscr{H}_{0})}\lambda_{p}^{H}(\Omega).

For the opposite inequality, fix
H\in\mathbb{S}(\mathscr{H}_{0})
and let
A_{H}
be an orthogonal matrix such that
H_{0}\circ A_{H}=H
. Now set

H_{\varepsilon}(x,y)=\sqrt{\varepsilon(x^{2}+y^{2})+(y^{+})^{2}}

for any
\varepsilon>0
. Clearly,
(1+\varepsilon)^{-1}H_{\varepsilon}\in{\cal E}(\Omega)\cap\mathbb{S}(\mathscr{H})
and, from Lemma
5.1
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\lambda_{1}\left(-\Delta_{p}^{(1+\varepsilon)^{-1}H_{\varepsilon}\circ A_{H}}\right)

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\lambda_{p}^{(1+\varepsilon)^{-1}H_{\varepsilon}\circ A_{H}}(\Omega)

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}(1+\varepsilon)^{-p}\lambda_{p}^{H_{\varepsilon}\circ A_{H}}(\Omega)

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}(1+\varepsilon)^{-p}\lambda_{p}^{H_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega_{A_{H}^{T}})

\displaystyle=\lambda_{p}^{H_{0}}(\Omega_{A_{H}^{T}})

\displaystyle=\lambda_{p}^{H}(\Omega),

so that
\Lambda^{\min}(\Omega)\leq\lambda_{p}^{H}(\Omega)
for every
H\in\mathbb{S}(\mathscr{H}_{0})
and thus the equality (
22
) occurs.

For the statement

\inf_{H\in\mathbb{S}(\mathscr{H}_{0})}\lambda_{p}^{H}(\Omega)=\inf_{\theta\in[0,2\pi]}\lambda_{1,p}(0,2L_{\theta}),

(23)

given any
H\in\mathscr{H}_{0}\setminus\{0\}
, we have
H(x,y)=c\left(x\cos\theta+y\sin\theta\right)^{+}
for some
c>0
and
\theta\in[0,2\pi]
and so
H_{A}(x,y)=cy^{+}
for the rotation matrix
A
of angle
\theta-\frac{\pi}{2}
. By Property
(P1)
, assume that
c=1
. By Property
(P4)
, Proposition
5.1
and Theorem
1.1
, we have

\lambda_{p}^{H}(\Omega)=\lambda_{p}^{H_{A}}(\Omega_{A})=\lambda^{1,0}_{p}(0,L_{\Omega_{A}})=\lambda_{1,p}(0,2L_{\Omega_{A}}).

On the other hand, since
A(0,1)=(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)
, we can describe
L_{\Omega_{A}}
in an alternative way. From the invariance of Lebesgue measure under orthogonal transformations, we have

\displaystyle L_{\Omega_{A}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\left\{|J|\colon J\subset(\{x\}\times\mathbb{R})\cap\Omega_{A}\ \text{and}\ J\ \text{is connected}\right\}

\displaystyle=\sup_{v\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}\sup\left\{|J|\colon J\subset\{t(0,1)+v\colon t\in\mathbb{R}\}\cap\Omega_{A}\ \text{and}\ J\ \text{is connected}\right\}

\displaystyle=\sup_{v\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}\sup\left\{|J|\colon AJ\subset A\{t(0,1)+v\colon t\in\mathbb{R}\}\cap\Omega\ \text{and}\ J\ \text{is connected}\right\}

\displaystyle=\sup_{v\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}\sup\left\{|AJ|\colon AJ\subset\{tA(0,1)+Av\colon t\in\mathbb{R}\}\cap\Omega\ \text{and}\ A^{T}J\ \text{is connected}\right\}

\displaystyle=\sup_{v\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}\sup\left\{|J|\colon J\subset\{t(\cos{\theta},\sin{\theta})+A^{T}v\colon t\in\mathbb{R}\}\cap\Omega\ \text{and}\ J\ \text{is connected}\right\}

\displaystyle=\sup_{v\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}\sup\left\{|J|\colon J\subset\{t(\cos{\theta},\sin{\theta})+v\colon t\in\mathbb{R}\}\cap\Omega\ \text{and}\ J\ \text{is connected}\right\}

\displaystyle=L_{\theta}.

Consequently,

\lambda_{p}^{H}(\Omega)=\lambda_{1,p}(0,2L_{\theta})

for every
H\in\mathscr{H}_{0}\setminus\{0\}
and the equality (
23
) follows. In particular, the positivity of
\Lambda^{\min}(\Omega)
comes from
\inf_{\theta\in[0,2\pi]}\lambda_{1,p}(0,2L_{\theta})>0
.

Finally, the last infimum is attained for some
\theta_{0}\in[0,2\pi]
if and only if the function
\theta\mapsto L_{\theta}
has a global maximum, but this just means that
\Omega
has optimal anisotropic design. In this case, it is clear that all non-zero multiples of
-\Delta_{p}^{H_{0}}
, where
H_{0}(x,y)=(x\cos{\theta_{0}}+y\sin{\theta_{0}})^{+}
, are extremal operators for the first inequality in (
5
).
∎

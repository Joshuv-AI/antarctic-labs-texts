The Superization of Hochschild’s Lemma and Restricted Lie-Rinehart Superalgebras Thanks: S.B. and Q.E. were supported by the grant NYUAD-065
Sofiane Bouarroudj, Quentin Ehret, Abdenacer Makhlouf, Nurtas Shyntas

Abstract
. The main goal of this paper is to introduce the notion of restricted Lie-Rinehart superalgebra over a field of characteristic p > 2 p>2 , motivated by a generalization of Hochschild’s lemma to the super setting. We extend Schauenburg’s proof of Hochschild’s lemma to Lie-Rinehart superalgebras and we prove a superized version that serves as a foundation for our construction. Building upon this, we define restricted Lie-Rinehart superalgebras, investigate their representations, construct the semi-direct product with a restricted module, and provide several examples. Finally, we construct the corresponding universal enveloping algebra and show that this algebra satisfies the expected universal property.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Restricted Lie algebras

Over a field of characteristic
p>0
, the Lie algebra of derivations of an associative algebra has a richer structure than it does in characteristic zero, since the Frobenius map
d\mapsto d^{p}
is an endomorphism of the space of derivations (see
[
Ja
]
). This was the main motivati on for defining
restricted
Lie algebras, introduced by Jacobson as a way to axiomatize this observation (see
[
J
]
). Roughly speaking, a Lie algebra
L
over a field of characteristic
p>0
is called
restricted
if it is equipped with an additional unary operation, called a
p
-map and denoted by

(-)^{[p]}:L\to L
which mimics the
p
-th power in an associative algebra (see Section
2.2
).
Indeed, the canonical examples are constructed from associative algebras by taking the commutator bracket as the Lie product and the usual

p
-th power as the
p
-map. Geometrically, Lie algebras associated with algebraic groups are restricted in positive characteristic and many properties of the algebraic group are encoded in the restricted structure of its Lie algebra, see
[
S
,
SF
]
. Because many classical tools, such as the Killing form and Lie’s theorem, fail in positive characteristic, restricted Lie algebras in characteristic
p
are used to recapture some of the behavior of Lie algebras over fields of characteristic zero, but they also exhibit new and different phenomena. Kostrikin and Šafarevič conjectured that every
simple
restricted Lie algebra over an algebraically closed field of characteristic
p>7
is of classical or Cartan type (see
[
KS
]
). The conjecture was proved for
p>7
by Block and Wilson (see
[
BW
]
), while the case
p>3
was settled by Premet and Strade (see
[
PS
]
and references therein). The classification of restricted Lie algebras over a field of characteristic 2 and 3 remains an open problem, see
[
S
]
for the full story. There is a special restricted cohomology theory for restricted Lie algebras introduced by Hochschild in
[
H
]
. This cohomology was further studied in
[
EF
,
EM2
,
EFa
]
, to cite a few.

To the best of our knowledge, the superization is due to Mikhalev (
[
Mi
]
). Several authors have since studied restricted Lie superalgebras in various contexts. The study of restricted simple Lie superalgebras in characteristic 3 can be found in
[
BKLLS
]
. Double extensions of restricted Lie superalgebras have been studied by
[
BBH
,
BEM
]
. Additionally, identities on the restricted enveloping superalgebras have been investigated in
[
Pe
,
U
]
, representations of the restricted Witt superalgebras and
\mathfrak{sl}(n|1)
have been explored in
[
SZ
,
Z
]
, and a cohomology theory pertaining to restricted Lie superalgebras has recently been developed in
[
BE
]
superizing
[
EF
]
. While an analogue of the Kostrikin-Šafarevič conjecture for restricted Lie superalgebras remains unclear, progress has been made with the classification of
p
-nilpotent ones in small dimensions
[
BE
]
.

1.2.
Lie-Rinehart algebras

Lie-Rinehart algebras are algebraic generalizations of Lie algebroids. They were introduced in the work of Herz (
[
He
]
), Palais (
[
P
]
), and especially Rinehart (
[
Ri
]
), who developed a formalism of differential forms for commutative algebras and studied the representations and cohomology of Lie-Rinehart algebras.
Roughly speaking, a Lie-Rinehart algebra is a triple
(A,L,\rho)
, where
A
is an associative commutative algebra,
L
is Lie algebra and an
A
-module, and
\rho:L\rightarrow\mathop{\mathrm{Der}}\nolimits(A)
is an
A
-linear Lie algebras morphism satisfying a compatibility condition that encodes the lack of
A
-linearity of the Lie bracket of
L
, see Eq. (
1
). Recent works on Lie-Rinehart algebras include their relationship to Poisson algebras investigated in
[
Hu
]
, where Huebschmann constructed a Lie-Rinehart algebra from the module of Kähler differentials of a Poisson algebra, and used this construction to define Poisson cohomology and homology in terms of the corresponding structures of the associated Lie-Rinehart algebra. Subsequent works by Huebschmann include
[
Hu2
]
, which relates Lie-Rinehart algebras with Gerstenhaber algebras and Batalin-Vilkovisky algebras, and
[
Hu3
]
, which explores an application of Lie-Rinehart algebras to the quantization of a positive Kähler manifold with a Hamiltonian action by a compact Lie group.

Over fields of positive characteristic, the notion of
restricted
Lie-Rinehart algebras was introduced by Dokas in
[
Do
]
, although earlier versions of the concept appear in
[
Ho
,
Ru
]
. In particular, Hochschild proved an additional compatibility condition for the
A
-module of derivations of an associative algebra
A
, specific to the case of a field of characteristic
p>0
(see
[
Ho
, Lemma 1]
), which is referred to as
Hochschild’s Lemma
. This result provided the main motivation for the definition of restricted Lie-Rinehart algebras, see Definition
2.4
. In this case, the underlying Lie algebra
L
is restricted, and the additional compatibility condition encodes the lack of
p
-homogeneity of the
p
-map with respect to the
A
-module structure of
L
. Dokas constructed the restricted universal enveloping algebra and provided a cohomology theory based on Quillen-Barr-Beck methods in
[
Do
]
, whereas another approach adapted to formal deformations was proposed in
[
Eh
]
. Moreover, Schauenburg gave an alternative construction of the restricted universal enveloping algebra of a restricted Lie–Rinehart algebra in
[
Sc
]
, as well as a reformulated version of the Hochschild lemma.

Over a field of characteristic zero, Lie-Rinehart superalgebras appeared in
[
Ch
,
Ro
]
and
[
EM1
]
, where a classification in small dimensions and a theory of formal deformations were proposed.
More recently, the universal enveloping algebra of a Lie–Rinehart superalgebra was studied in
[
La
]
, where the author established a new Poincaré-Birkhoff-Witt theorem for Lie–Rinehart superalgebras, leading to a similar result for Poisson superalgebras.

1.3.
The main results and the organization of the paper

The main contribution of this paper is the definition of the notion of
restricted
Lie-Rinehart
superalgebra
over a field of characteristic
p>2
(see, Definition
4.2
), derived from the generalization of Hochschild’s lemma in the super setting, see Theorem
3.1.  Theorem
. The superization of the notion of restricted Lie-Rinehart algebra is not a straightforward superization minding the sign rule, but it rather relies on the superization of Hochschild Lemma.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we review the basic notions of Lie-Rinehart (super)algebras, restricted Lie algebras, and restricted Lie-Rinehart algebras. We also recall Hochschild’s lemma for Lie-Rinehart algebras (see Proposition
2.3.1.  Proposition
). Section
3
is devoted to the superization of Hochschild’s lemma, stated in Theorem
3.1.  Theorem
. Our approach extends Schauenburg’s proof (see
[
Sc
]
) to the super setting and relies on tools from the theory of Hopf superalgebras. The proof is quite technical and is divided into four cases, depending on the parity of the elements involved. Some parts of the proof involving combinatorial methods are postponed to Appendix
A
. In Section
4
, we briefly recall the definition of restricted Lie superalgebras and introduce the notion of restricted Lie-Rinehart superalgebras in Definition
4.2
, which constitutes the main contribution of this paper. Further, we introduce representations (see Definition
4.3
), and discuss the semi-direct product of a restricted Lie-Rinehart superalgebra by such a representation (see Theorem
4.3.5.  Theorem
), and provide several examples. Finally, in Section
5
, we define the universal enveloping algebra of a restricted Lie-Rinehart superalgebra and show that our construction satisfies the appropriate universal property (see Proposition
5.3.1.  Proposition
).

It is presumed that when the parity of an element is written, the element is homogeneous. Throughout the text,
\K
refers to a field of characteristic
p\geq 3
.

2.
Background

2.1.
Lie-Rinehart superalgebras

Following
[
Ri
,
Ch
]
, a Lie-Rinehart superalgebra is a triple
(A,L,\rho)
, where
A=A_{\bar{0}}\oplus A_{\bar{1}}
is an associative supercommutative superalgebra,
(L=L_{\bar{0}}\oplus L_{\bar{1}},[-,-])
is a Lie superalgebra that is also an
A
-module, and
\rho:L\rightarrow\mathop{\mathrm{Der}}\nolimits(A)
is a morphism of Lie superalgebras and of
A
-modules satisfying the
Leibniz condition
:

(1)

[x,ay]=(-1)^{|x||a|}a[x,y]+\rho(x)(a)y,\quad\forall x,y\in L~\forall a\in A.

Let
(A,L,\rho)
and
(B,H,\theta)
be Lie-Rinehart superalgebras. A morphism of Lie-Rinehart superalgebras is a pair
(\phi,\psi)
, where
\phi:A\rightarrow B
is a morphism of associative superalgebras and
\psi:L\rightarrow H
is a morphism of Lie superalgebras satisfying

(2)

\phi\circ\rho(x)(a)=\theta\bigl(\psi(x)\bigl)\bigl(\phi(a)\bigl),\quad\forall a\in A,~\forall x\in L.

In the case where
A=B
, one can take
\phi=\mathop{\mathrm{id}}\nolimits
and Condition (
2
) reduces to

(3)

\rho(x)(a)=\theta\bigl(\psi(x)\bigl)(a),\quad\forall a\in A,~\forall x\in L.

A representation of a Lie-Rinehart superalgebra
(A,L,\rho)
is an
A
-module
M
together with a
A
-linear morphism of Lie superalgebras
\phi:L\rightarrow\mathop{\mathrm{End}}\nolimits(M)
satisfying

(4)

\phi(x)(am)=(-1)^{|x||a|}a\phi(x)(m)+\rho(x)(a)m,~\forall x\in L,~\forall a\in A,~\forall m\in M.

Such a pair
(\phi,M)
is also called
Lie-Rinehart module
.

In the text, we call
Lie-Rinehart algebra
a Lie-Rinehart superalgebra concentrated in even degree, that is,
A_{\bar{1}}=L_{\bar{1}}=0
.

2.2.
Restricted Lie algebras

Following
[
J
]
, a finite-dimensional Lie algebra
(L,[-,-])
is called
restricted
if there is a map
(-)^{[p]}:L\rightarrow L
(called
p
-map
or
p
-structure
) satisfying

(5)

\displaystyle(\lambda x)^{[p]}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{p}x^{[p]}\text{ for all }x\in L\text{ and for all }\lambda\in\K;

(6)

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{x^{[p]}}

\displaystyle=(\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{x})^{p}\text{ for all }x\in L;

(7)

\displaystyle(x+y)^{[p]}

\displaystyle=x^{[p]}+y^{[p]}+\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq i\leq p-1}s_{i}(x,y),\text{ for all }x,y\in L;

where the coefficients
s_{i}(x,y)
can be obtained from the expansion

(8)

(\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{\lambda x+y})^{p-1}(x)=\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq i\leq p-1}is_{i}(x,y)\lambda^{i-1}.

An explicit expression of
\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq i\leq p-1}s_{i}(x,y)
in terms of nested brackets is given by

(9)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}s_{i}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\underset{x_{p-1}=y,~x_{p}=x}{x_{k}\in\{x,y\}}}\frac{1}{\sharp(x)}[x_{1},[x_{2},[...,[x_{p-1},x_{p}]...]]],

where
\sharp(x)=\mathop{\mathrm{card}}\nolimits\{i,~x_{i}=x\}\in\{1,\cdots,p-1\}
.

To investigate
p
-structures on a Lie algebra, it is useful to use the following theorem, due to Jacobson.

Theorem

(
[
J
]
)
.

Let
(e_{j})_{j\in J}
be a basis of
L
such that there are
f_{j}\in L
satisfying
(\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{e_{j}})^{p}=\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{f_{j}}
. Then, there exists exactly one
p
-map
(-)^{[p]}:L\rightarrow L
such that

e_{j}^{[p]}=f_{j}\quad\text{ for all $j\in J$}.

Example

(see
[
SF
]
)
.

Let
A
be an associative algebra. Then,
A
can be endowed with a restricted Lie algebra structure with the bracket
[a,b]=ab-ba
and the
p
-map
a^{[p]}=a^{p}
.

Example

(see
[
J
]
)
.

Let
A
be an associative algebra and let
\mathop{\mathrm{Der}}\nolimits(A)
denote its space of derivations, that is,

(10)

\mathop{\mathrm{Der}}\nolimits(A)=\bigl\{D\in\mathop{\mathrm{End}}\nolimits(A),~D(ab)=D(a)b+aD(b),\quad\forall a,b\in A\bigl\}.

Then, we have
D^{p}\in\mathop{\mathrm{Der}}\nolimits(A)~\forall D\in\mathop{\mathrm{Der}}\nolimits(A)
and therefore
\mathop{\mathrm{Der}}\nolimits(A)
is a restricted Lie algebra with the bracket
[D,D^{\prime}]=D\circ D^{\prime}-D^{\prime}\circ D
and the
p
-map
D^{[p]}=D^{p}.

Remark
.

(See also
[
KL
, Remark 1.1]
)
Suppose that
L
is a centerless Lie algebra equipped with a map
(-)^{[p]}:L\rightarrow L
satisfying Eq. (
6
). Then,
(-)^{[p]}
automatically satisfies Eqs. (
5
) and (
7
). Indeed, let
x,y\in L
. We have

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{(x+y)^{[p]}}

\displaystyle=\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits^{p}_{x+y}=\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits^{p}_{x}+\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits^{p}_{y}+\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}\overline{s_{i}}(\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{x},\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{y})

\displaystyle=\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{x^{[p]}}+\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{y^{[p]}}+\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{s_{i}(x,y)},

where
\overline{s_{i}}
refers to the commutator bracket (note that the equality
\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}\overline{s_{i}}(\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{x},\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{y})=\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{s_{i}(x,y)}
follows from the Jacobi identity). It follows that

(x+y)^{[p]}-x^{[p]}-y^{[p]}-\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}s_{i}(x,y)

lies in the center of
L
, which is trivial.

2.3.
The Hochschild Lemma

The following general statement appeared in Hochschild’s work on simple algebras with purely inseparable splitting fields of exponent 1, see
[
Ho
, Lemma 1]
.

Let
U
be an associative algebra over the field of integers modulo
p
and let
V\subset U
be a commutative subalgebra. For all
u\in U
, denote by
D_{u}
the derivation defined by
D_{u}(w)=uw-wu,~\forall w\in U
. Then, for all
u\in U
such that
D_{u}(V)\subset V
, we have

(vu)^{p}=v^{p}u^{p}+D_{vu}^{p-1}(v)u,~\forall v\in V.

In the context of Lie-Rinehart algebras, this statement will be formulated as follows. Let
(A,L,\rho)
be a Lie-Rinehart algebra. Applying the Lemma with
U
the universal enveloping algebra of
(A,L,\rho)
(see
[
Ri
]
or Section
5
below), and
V=A
, we obtain

(11)

(ax)^{p}=a^{p}x^{p}+\rho(ax)^{p-1}(a)x,\quad\forall a\in A,~\forall x\in L.

A modern version of Hochschild’s Lemma appears then in Schauenburg’s work as follows.

Proposition

(
[
Sc
, Lemma 2.1]
)
.

Let
(A,L,\rho)
be a Lie-Rinehart algebra and let
(\phi,M)
be a Lie-Rinehart module. Then, we have

(12)

\phi(ax)^{p}=a^{p}\phi(x)^{p}+\rho(ax)^{p-1}(a)\phi(x).

Equation (
11
) (and (
12
)) serves as the main motivation for the definition of restricted Lie-Rinehart algebras below.

2.4.
Restricted Lie-Rinehart algebras

Following
[
Ru
,
Do
]
, a Lie-Rinehart algebra
(A,L,\rho)
(see Eq. (
1
)) is called
restricted
if
L
is a restricted with a
p
-map
(-)^{[p]}:L\rightarrow L
, and if moreover, we have

(13)

(ax)^{[p]}=a^{p}x^{[p]}+\rho(ax)^{p-1}(a)x,\quad\forall a\in A,~\forall x\in L.

Equation (
13
) is referred to as
Hochschild condition
and is derived from the Hochschild’s Lemma
[
Ho
, Lemma 1]
.

Example

(see
[
Do
]
)
.

Let
A
be an associative commutative algebra and
\mathop{\mathrm{Der}}\nolimits(A)
its restricted Lie algebra of derivations. Then,
(A,\mathop{\mathrm{Der}}\nolimits(A),\mathop{\mathrm{id}}\nolimits)
is a Lie-Rinehart algebra. Applying Hochschild’s Lemma with
\phi=\rho=\mathop{\mathrm{id}}\nolimits
(see Eq. (
20
)), we have

(14)

(aD)^{p}=a^{p}D^{p}+(aD)^{p-1}(a)D,\quad\forall a\in A,~\forall D\in\mathop{\mathrm{Der}}\nolimits(A).

Therefore,
(A,\mathop{\mathrm{Der}}\nolimits(A),\mathop{\mathrm{id}}\nolimits)
is a restricted Lie-Rinehart algebra.

Example
.

Let
(A,\{-,-\},)
be a Poisson algebra with unit
1_{A}
equipped with a
p
-map
(-)^{\{p\}}
(that is,
(A,\{-,-\},(-)^{\{p\}})
is a restricted Lie algebra). Following
[
BYZ
]
,
(A,\{-,-\},(-)^{\{p\}})
is called
restricted Poisson algebra
if

(15)

\displaystyle(x^{2})^{\{p\}}

\displaystyle=2x^{p}x^{\{p\}},\quad\forall x,y\in A.

Consider the module of Kähler differentials
\Omega^{1}(A)
of a restricted Poisson algebra
(A,\{\cdot,\cdot\},(\cdot)^{\{p\}})
, which is the
A
-module generated by symbols
du,~u\in A,
with relations

(16)

d(u+v)=du+dv,~d(uv)=udv+vdu,~d1_{A}=0.

Then, it has been shown in
[
Hu
, Theorem 3.8]
that
(A,\Omega^{1}(A),\rho)
is a Lie-Rinehart algebra, with

(17)

\displaystyle[xdu,ydv]_{\Omega^{1}(A)}

\displaystyle:=x\{u,y\}dv+y\{x,v\}du+xyd\{u,v\},\quad

\displaystyle\forall x,y,u,v\in A;

(18)

\displaystyle\rho(xdu)

\displaystyle:=x\{u,-\}

\displaystyle\forall x,u\in A.

Suppose that
\Omega^{1}(A)
is free as an
A
-module. Then,
(A,\Omega^{1}(A),\rho)
becomes a restricted Lie-Rinehart algebra, with the
p
-map (see
[
BYZ
, Theorem 8.2]
)

(19)

(xdu)^{[p]}:=x^{p}d(u^{\{p\}})+\rho(xdu)^{p-1}(x)du,\quad\forall x,u\in A.

The analogue construction in the case where
p=2
was carried out in
[
BEL
]
.

3.
A superization of Hochschild’s Lemma

In this Section, we give a superized version of Hochschild’s Lemma (see
[
Ho
, Lemma 1]
,
[
Sc
, Lemma 2.1]
, also Proposition
2.3.1.  Proposition
). Our approach follows Schauenburg’s proof (see Section 5 of
[
Sc
]
).

Theorem

(Superization of the Hochschild’s Lemma)
.

Let
(A,L,\rho)
be a Lie-Rinehart superalgebra over a field
\K
of characteristic
p\geq 3
and let
(\phi,M)
be a Lie-Rinehart module. Then, we have

(20)

\displaystyle\phi(ax)^{p}

\displaystyle=a^{p}\phi(x)^{p}+\rho(ax)^{p-1}(a)\phi(x),

\displaystyle\forall a\in A_{\bar{0}},~\forall x\in L_{\bar{0}};

(21)

\displaystyle\phi(ax)^{2p}

\displaystyle=a^{2p}\phi(x)^{2p}+\rho(ax)^{2p-1}(a)\phi(x)

\displaystyle~~+\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}\lambda_{i}\rho(ax)^{i}(a)\rho(ax)^{2p-2-i}(a)\phi(x)^{2},

\displaystyle\forall a\in A_{\bar{0}},~\forall x\in L_{\bar{1}};

(22)

\displaystyle\phi(ax)^{2p}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\quad\forall a\in A_{\bar{1}},~\forall x\in L_{\bar{0}};

(23)

\displaystyle\phi(ax)^{p}

\displaystyle=a\bigl(\rho(x)(a)\bigl)^{p-1}\phi(x),\quad

\displaystyle\forall a\in A_{\bar{1}},~\forall x\in L_{\bar{1}},

where the coefficients
\lambda_{i}
are given by

(24)

\lambda_{i}=\begin{cases}2(-1)^{\frac{i}{2}}~&\text{ if $i$ is even, }0\leq i<p-1;\\[2.84526pt]
2(-1)^{\frac{i-1}{2}}~&\text{ if $i$ is odd, }1\leq i<p-1;\\[2.84526pt]
(-1)^{\frac{p-1}{2}}&\text{ if }i=p-1.\end{cases}

Proof.

The purely even case follows from
[
Sc
, Lemma 2.1]
. The remaining cases are covered by Lemmas
3.3.8.  Lemma
,
3.4.1.  Lemma
and
3.5.1.  Lemma
below.
∎

The rest of this Section is devoted to the proof of
3.1.  Theorem
. The study is divided into four different cases according to the parity of the elements involved.

3.2.
The general setting

Let
(A,L,\rho)
be a Lie-Rinehart superalgebra. Consider the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded ring
V=\mathbb{Z}[x_{0},x_{1},x_{2},\cdots]
, such that
|x_{i+1}|=|x_{i}|+|\delta|
, where
\delta
is the derivation of
V
defined by
\delta(x_{i})=x_{i+1}
.
Consider the superbialgebra
H=\mathbb{Z}[\delta]
given by the coproduct
\Delta(1)=1\otimes 1,
and
\Delta(\delta)=1\otimes\delta+\delta\otimes 1.
Recall that according to the Koszul rule, the product on
H
is given by

(25)

(a\otimes b)(c\otimes d)=(-1)^{|b||c|}ac\otimes bd,\quad\forall~a,b,c,d\in H.

Recall also that the coproduct satisfies

(26)

\Delta(H_{\bar{0}})\subset H_{\bar{0}}\otimes H_{\bar{0}}+H_{\bar{1}}\otimes H_{\bar{1}},\quad\Delta(H_{\bar{1}})\subset H_{\bar{0}}\otimes H_{\bar{1}}+H_{\bar{1}}\otimes H_{\bar{0}}.

We can form the smash product
V\#H
as follows. Using Sweedler’s notation, the product in
V\#H
is given by

(27)

(v\#g)(w\#h)=\sum_{(g)}(-1)^{|w||g_{(2)}|}v(g_{(1)}w)\#(g_{(2)}h),~\forall v,w\in V,\quad\forall g,h\in H.

In particular, if
g
is primitive (that is,
\Delta(g)=1\otimes g+g\otimes 1
), the above product becomes

(28)

(v\#g)(w\#h)=(-1)^{|w||g|}vw\#gh+v(gw)\#h,\quad\forall v,w\in V,~\forall g,h\in H.

Now, let
a\in A
such that
|a|=|x_{0}|
and let
\alpha\in\mathop{\mathrm{Der}}\nolimits(A)
such that
|\alpha|=|\delta|
. Consider the (unique) ring homomorphism
f:V\rightarrow A
given by
f(x_{0})=a
satisfying
f\circ\delta=\alpha\circ f
. From this equality, we obtain
f(x_{i})=\alpha^{i}(a),~\forall i\geq 0
.

Let
M
be an
A
-module and let
\beta\in\mathop{\mathrm{End}}\nolimits_{\mathbb{Z}}(M)
such that

\beta(am)=\alpha(a)m+(-1)^{|a||\beta|}a\beta(m),\quad\forall a\in A,~\forall m\in M.

Note that such maps
\beta
do exist, for example, if
(\phi,M)
is a representation of the Lie-Rinehart superalgebra
(A,L,\rho)
and
x\in L
homogeneous, take
\beta=\phi(x)
and
\alpha=\rho(x)
.

Lemma
.

The
A
-module
M
becomes a
V\#H
-module with

(29)

(v\#1)m=f(v)m,\quad\text{and}\quad(1\#\delta)m=\beta(m),\quad\forall v\in V,~\forall m\in M.

Proof.

Let
v,w\in V
and

m\in M
. We have

(v\#1)(w\#1)m=(v\#1)f(w)m=f(v)f(w)m=f(vw)m=((vw)\#1)m.

The module structure is then extended by

(1\#\delta^{k})m=\beta^{k}(m)~\forall k\geq 1,\quad\text{and}\quad(v\#\delta)m=(v\#1)(1\#\delta)m.

∎

Lemma
.

We have
(x_{0}\#\delta)^{k}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\Gamma_{k,j}\#\delta^{j},
where
\Gamma_{k,j}\in\mathbb{Z}[x_{0},x_{1},\cdots,x_{k}]
are recursively defined by
\Gamma_{1,1}=x_{0}
and

\Gamma_{k+1,j}=\begin{cases}x_{0}\delta(\Gamma_{k,1}),&j=1\\[2.84526pt]
x_{0}\delta(\Gamma_{k,j})+(-1)^{|\delta||\Gamma_{k,j-1}|}x_{0}\Gamma_{k,j-1},&j=2,\cdots,k\\[2.84526pt]
x_{0}\Gamma_{k,k},&j=k+1.\end{cases}

In particular, we have
\Gamma_{k,k}=x_{0}^{k}
and
\Gamma_{k,1}=(x_{0}\delta)^{k-1}(x_{0}).

Proof.

We will use induction. First, notice that the elements
\Gamma_{k,j}
are homogeneous of parity
|\Gamma_{k,j}|=k-j~\mod{2}.
The induction step is given by

\displaystyle(x_{0}\#\delta)\sum_{j=1}^{k}\Gamma_{k,j}\#\delta^{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{k}(-1)^{|\delta||\Gamma_{k,j}|}x_{0}\Gamma_{k,j}\#\delta^{j+1}+x_{0}\delta(\Gamma_{k,j})\#\delta^{j}

\displaystyle=x_{0}\delta(\Gamma_{k,1})\#\delta+\sum_{j=2}^{k}\Bigl((-1)^{|\delta||\Gamma_{k,j-1}|}x_{0}\Gamma_{k,j-1}\#\delta^{j}+x_{0}\delta(\Gamma_{k,j})\#\delta^{j}\Bigl)

\displaystyle+(-1)^{|\delta||\Gamma_{k,k}|}x_{0}\Gamma_{k,k}\#\delta^{k+1}.

The result follows.
∎

L
be a finite-dimensional Lie superalgebra defined over a field of characteristic
p>2
. For
p=3
, the Jacobi identity does not imply
[x,[x,x]]=0
, for all
x\in L_{\bar{1}}
, therefore we require it as part of the definition.

Following
[
Mi
,
Pe
]
, we say that
L
has a
p|2p
-structure
if
L_{\bar{0}}
is a restricted Lie algebra with a
p
-map
(-)^{[p]}
and

(42)

\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{x^{[p]}}(y)=(\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{x})^{p}(y),\quad\forall x\in L_{\bar{0}},~\forall y\in L.

We set

(-)^{[2p]}:L_{\bar{1}}\rightarrow L_{\bar{0}},\quad x\mapsto(x^{2})^{[p]},\quad\text{ where }x^{2}:=\frac{1}{2}[x,x].

The pair
\bigl(L,(-)^{[p|2p]}\bigl)
is called a
restricted
Lie superalgebra. When the base field has characteristic
p=2
, there are several notions of restrictedness (see
[
BLLS
]
); however, these will not be considered in the present work. For a description of
p|2p
-structures on simplie Lie superalgebras, see
[
BKLLS
]
.

The following theorem is a straightforward superization of Jacobson’s theorem
2.2.1.  Theorem
.

Theorem
.

Let
(e_{j})_{j\in J}
be a basis of
L_{\bar{0}}
, and let the elements
f_{j}\in L_{\bar{0}}
be such that
{(\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{e_{j}})^{p}=\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{f_{j}}}
. Then, there exists exactly one
p|2p
-mapping
(-)^{[p|2p]}:L\rightarrow L
such that

e_{j}^{[p]}=f_{j}\quad\text{ for all $j\in J$}.

Let
\bigl(L,(-)^{[p|2p]_{L}}\bigl)
and
\bigl(H,(-)^{[p|2p]_{H}}\bigl)
be two restricted Lie superalgebras. A morphism of Lie superalgebras
\varphi:L\rightarrow H
is called
restricted
if in addition to
\varphi\bigl([x,y]\bigl)=\bigl[\varphi(x),\varphi(y)\bigl]~\forall x,y\in L
, we also have

\varphi\bigl(x^{[p]_{L}}\bigl)=\varphi(x)^{[p]_{H}},~\forall x\in L_{\bar{0}}.

A homogeneous ideal
I=I_{\bar{0}}\oplus I_{\bar{1}}
of
L
is called a
p
-ideal
if it is closed under the
p|2p
-map; namely

\text{$x^{[p]}\in I_{\bar{0}}$ for all $x\in I_{\bar{0}}$}.

As
x^{[2p]}=(x^{2})^{[p]}
, for all
x\in L_{\bar{1}}
, this would imply that

x^{[2p]}\in I_{\bar{0}}\text{ for all $x\in I_{\bar{1}}$}.

The
center
of a restricted Lie superalgebra
L
is the
p
-ideal

{\mathfrak{z}}(L)=\bigl\{x\in L,~[x,y]=0~\forall y\in L\bigl\}.

Let
L
be a restricted Lie superalgebra. An
L
-module
M
is called
restricted
if

\underbrace{x\cdots x}_{p\text{~~times}}\cdot m=x^{[p]}\cdot m\quad\text{for all $x\in L_{\bar{0}}$ and $m\in M$.}

4.2.
Restricted Lie-Rinehart superalgebras

Based on Theorem
3.1.  Theorem
, we introduce the notion of a restricted Lie-Rinehart superalgebra as follows. A Lie-Rinehart superalgebra
(A,L,\rho)
is called
restricted
if the Lie algebra
(L,[-,-],(-)^{[p]})
is restricted and if in addition, we have

(43)

\displaystyle(ax)^{[p]}

\displaystyle=a^{p}x^{[p]}+\rho(ax)^{p-1}(a)x,

\displaystyle\forall a\in A_{\bar{0}},~\forall x\in L_{\bar{0}};

(44)

\displaystyle(ax)^{[2p]}

\displaystyle=a^{2p}x^{[2p]}+\rho(ax)^{2p-1}(a)x

\displaystyle~~+\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}\lambda_{i}\rho(ax)^{i}(a)\rho(ax)^{2p-2-i}(a)x^{2},

\displaystyle\forall a\in A_{\bar{0}},~\forall x\in L_{\bar{1}};

(45)

\displaystyle(ax)^{[2p]}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\quad\forall a\in A_{\bar{1}},~\forall x\in L_{\bar{0}};

(46)

\displaystyle(ax)^{[p]}

\displaystyle=a\bigl(\rho(x)(a)\bigl)^{p-1}x,\quad

\displaystyle\forall a\in A_{\bar{1}},~\forall x\in L_{\bar{1}};

where the coefficients
\lambda_{i}
are defined in Eq. (
24
), and
x^{2}:=\frac{1}{2}[x,x]
.
In the case where
A
and
L
are purely even, we recover Definition
2.4
.

Example
.

Let
A
be a supercommutative associative superalgebra and consider the Lie-Rinehart superalgebra
(A,\mathop{\mathrm{Der}}\nolimits(A),\mathop{\mathrm{id}}\nolimits)
. The Lie superalgebra
\mathop{\mathrm{Der}}\nolimits(A)
is actually restricted, and it follows from Theorem

applied to the representation
(\mathop{\mathrm{id}}\nolimits,A)
that

is a restricted Lie-Rinehart superalgebra.

Let us detail the computation in the case where
p=3
. Let

and

. A long but direct computation gives

(aD)^{6}=a^{6}D^{6}+(aD)^{5}(a)D+2a^{4}D^{3}(a)D(a)D^{2}+2a^{5}D^{4}(a)D^{2}.

However, one can show that

\displaystyle 2a(aD)^{4}(a)D^{2}

\displaystyle=a^{4}D^{2}(a)^{2}D^{2}+2a^{4}D(a)D^{3}(a)D^{2}+2a^{5}D^{4}(a)D^{2};

\displaystyle 2(aD)(a)(aD)^{3}(a)D^{2}

\displaystyle=a^{4}D^{3}(a)D(a)D^{2};

\displaystyle 2(aD)^{2}(a)^{2}D^{2}

\displaystyle=2a^{4}D^{2}(a)^{2}D^{2}.

It follows that

\displaystyle 2a^{4}D^{3}(a)D(a)D^{2}+2a^{5}D^{4}(a)D^{2}=2

\displaystyle a(aD)^{4}(a)D^{2}+2(aD)(a)(aD)^{3}(a)D^{2}

\displaystyle+2(aD)^{2}(a)^{2}D^{2},

and therefore we obtain

(aD)^{6}=a^{6}D^{6}+(aD)^{5}(a)D+2a(aD)^{4}(a)D^{2}+2(aD)(a(aD)^{3}(a)D^{2}+2(aD)^{2}(a)^{2}D^{2}.

We therefore recover Eq. (

Similarly, for

and

and we recover Eq.
46
. Furthermore, Eq. (
45
) is straightforward and for Eq. (
43
) we refer to
[
Do
,
Sc
]
.

4.3.
Modules and representations

Let
(A,L,\rho)
be a restricted Lie-Rinehart superalgebra. A representation of
(A,L,\rho)
is an
A
-module
V=V_{\bar{0}}\oplus V_{\bar{1}}
together with a
A
-linear morphism of restricted Lie superalgebras
\phi:L\rightarrow\mathop{\mathrm{End}}\nolimits(V)
satisfying:

(47)

\displaystyle\phi(x)(av)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{|x||a|}a\phi(x)(v)+\rho(x)(a)v,

\displaystyle~\forall x\in L,~\forall a\in A,~\forall v\in V,

(48)

\displaystyle\phi(ax)^{p-1}(av)

\displaystyle=a^{p}\phi(x)^{p-1}(v)+\rho(ax)^{p-1}(a)v,

\displaystyle~\forall x\in L_{\bar{0}},~\forall a\in A_{\bar{0}},~\forall v\in V,

(49)

\displaystyle\phi(ax)^{2p-1}(av)

\displaystyle=a^{2p}\phi(x)^{2p-1}(v)+\rho(ax)^{2p-1}(a)v

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}\lambda_{i}\rho(ax)^{i}(a)\rho(ax)^{2p-2-i}(a)\phi(x)(v),

\displaystyle\forall a\in A_{\bar{0}},~\forall x\in L_{\bar{1}}~\forall v\in V,

where the coefficients
\lambda_{i}
are defined in Eq. (
24
).
Such a pair
(\phi,V)
is called a
restricted Lie-Rinehart module
.

4.3.1.
Semi-direct product

In this Section, we investigate the semi-direct product of a restricted Lie-Rinehart superalgebra with a representation. We begin with a few technical Lemmas. Recall that if
\bigl(L,[-,-]\bigl)
is a (ordinary) Lie superalgebra and
(\phi,V)
a (ordinary) representation, the space
L\oplus V
is also a Lie superalgebra with the bracket

(50)

\bigl[(x+v),(y+w)\bigl]_{\rtimes}:=[x,y]+\phi(x)(w)-(-1)^{|y||v|}\phi(y)(v),~~\forall~x,y\in L,~\forall~v,w\in V.

The Lie superalgebra
(L\oplus V,[-,-]_{\rtimes})
is denoted by
L\rtimes V
.

Lemma
.

Let
\left(L,[-,-]\right)
be a Lie superalgebra and
(\phi,V)
be a representation of
L
. Then for all

and all

(51)

Let

and

Proposition
.

Let
\bigl(L,[-,-],(-)^{[p|2p]}\bigl)
be a restricted Lie superalgebra and let

be a restricted representation. Then,

(54)

(e_{i}+v_{j})^{[p]_{\rtimes}}=e_{i}^{[p]}+\phi(e_{i})^{p-1}(v_{j}),

where
(e_{i})_{i}
forms a basis of
L_{\bar{0}}
and
(v_{j})_{j}
a basis of

Proof.

Let
(e_{i})_{i}
be a basis of
L_{\bar{0}}
and
(v_{j})_{j}
a basis of
V_{\bar{0}}
. We define

e_{i}^{[p]_{\rtimes}}:=e_{i}^{[p]},\quad\text{and}\quad v_{j}^{[p]_{\rtimes}}:=0.

Then, we have

(55)

(e_{i}+v_{j})^{[p]_{\rtimes}}=e_{i}^{[p]_{\rtimes}}+\sum_{\ell=1}^{p-1}\widetilde{s}_{\ell}(e_{i},v_{j}),

where
\widetilde{s_{\ell}}
refers to the coefficients defined in Eq. (
8
) with respect to the bracket (
50
). Using Eq. (
9
), we have

(56)

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=1}^{p-1}\widetilde{s_{\ell}}(e_{i},v_{j})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\underset{x_{p-1}=v_{j},~x_{p}=e_{i}}{x_{k}\in\{e_{i},v_{j}\}}}\frac{1}{\sharp(e_{i})}[x_{1},[x_{2},[...,[x_{p-1},x_{p}]...]_{\rtimes}]_{\rtimes}]_{\rtimes},

where

. All the terms in the above sum are zero except when

, therefore we have

(57)

\sum_{\ell=1}^{p-1}\widetilde{s_{\ell}}(e_{i},v_{j})=-\frac{1}{p-1}\widetilde{\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits}_{e_{i}}^{p-1}(v_{j})=\widetilde{\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits}_{e_{i}}^{p-1}(v_{j})=\phi(e_{i})^{p-1}(v_{j}).

It remains to show that the map

satisfies the conditions of Jacobson’s Theorem

, that is, we have
\widetilde{\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits}^{p}_{e_{i}+v_{j}}(y+w)=\widetilde{\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits}_{(e_{i}+v_{j})^{[p]_{\rtimes}}}(y+w),
for all
y+w\in L\oplus V
homogeneous. Let
e_{i},v_{j}
as above and let
y+w
an homogeneous element of
L\oplus V
. Using Lemma
4.3.3.  Lemma
, we have

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits^{p}_{(e_{i}+v_{j})}(y+w)-\left[(e_{i}+v_{j})^{[p]_{\rtimes}},(y+w)\right]_{\rtimes}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{e_{i}}^{p}(y)+\phi(e_{i})^{p}(w)-\phi(y)\phi(e_{i})^{p-1}(v_{j})-\bigl[e_{i}^{[p]}+\phi(e_{i})^{p-1}(v_{j}),(y+w)\bigl]_{\rtimes}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{e_{i}}^{p}(y)+\phi(e_{i})^{p}(w)-\phi(y)\phi(e_{i})^{p-1}(v_{j})-\bigl(\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{e_{i}^{[p]}}(y)+\phi(e_{i}^{[p]})(w)-\phi(y)\phi(e_{i})^{p-1}(v_{j})\bigl)=0.

Therefore, we can apply Jacobson’s Theorem
2.2.1.  Theorem
and
L\rtimes V
is a restricted Lie superalgebra.
∎

Theorem
.

Let
(A,L,\rho)
be a restricted Lie superalgebra and let
(\phi,V)
a representation as in Definition
4.3
. Suppose that the center of the restricted Lie superalgebra
L\rtimes V
constructed in Proposition
4.3.4.  Proposition
is trivial. Then,
(A,L\rtimes V,\widetilde{\rho})
is a restricted Lie-Rinehart superalgebra, with
\widetilde{\rho}(x+v)=\rho(x),~\forall~x+v\in L\rtimes V
.

Proof.

Since the center of
L\rtimes V
is trivial, it follows from Remark
2.2.4.  Remark
and Proposition
4.3.4.  Proposition
that the
p
-map is given by

(x+v)^{[p]_{\rtimes}}=x^{[p]}+\phi(x)^{p-1}(v),\quad\forall x+v\in L\rtimes V.

Let
a\in A
and
x+v\in L\rtimes V
. We divide the proof into four different cases, depending on the parity of
a
and
x+v.

The case where
a
and
x+v
are even.
We have

\displaystyle(ax+av)^{[p]_{\rtimes}}-a^{p}(x+v)^{[p]_{\rtimes}}-\widetilde{\rho}\bigl(a(x+v)\bigl)^{p-1}(a)(x+v)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\left((ax)^{[p]}+\phi(ax)^{p-1}(av)\right)-a^{p}\left(x^{[p]}+\phi(x)^{p-1}(v)\right)-\rho(ax)^{p-1}(a)(x+v)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\phi(ax)^{p-1}(av)-a^{p}\phi(x)^{p-1}(v)-\rho(ax)^{p-1}(a)v=0,

and thus Eq. (
43
) is satisfied.

The case where
a
is odd and
x+v
is even.
Note that in this case, we have

\phi(ax)^{2}(av)=a\rho(x)(a)^{2}v+a^{2}\rho(x)^{2}(a)v=0.

Recall that for
y+w\in(L\oplus V)_{{\bar{1}}}
, we have

(y+w)^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\bigl([y,y]_{\rtimes}+2\phi(y)(w)\bigl)=y^{2}+\phi(y)(w).

Therefore, we have

(y+w)^{[2p]_{\rtimes}}=y^{[2p]}+\phi(y^{2})^{p-1}\circ\phi(y)(w)=y^{[2p]}+\phi(y)^{2p-1}(w).

Using the latter identity, we obtain

(ax+av)^{[2p]_{\rtimes}}=(ax)^{[2p]_{\rtimes}}+\phi(ax)^{2p-1}(av)=0,

and thus Eq. (
45
) is satisfied.

The case where
a
is even and
x+v
is odd.
We have

\displaystyle(ax+av)^{[2p]_{\rtimes}}=

\displaystyle~(ax)^{[2p]}+\phi(ax)^{2p-1}(av)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~a^{2p}x^{[2p]}+\rho(ax)^{2p-1}(a)x+\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}\lambda_{i}\rho(ax)^{i}(a)\rho(ax)^{2p-2-i}(a)x^{2}+\phi(ax)^{2p-1}(av)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~a^{2p}\bigl(x^{[2p]}+\phi(x)^{2p-1}(v)\bigl)+\rho(ax)^{2p-1}(x+v)

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}\lambda_{i}\rho(ax)^{i}(a)\rho(ax)^{2p-2-i}(a)(x^{2}+\phi(x)(v))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~a^{2p}(x+v)^{[2p]_{\rtimes}}+\widetilde{\rho}(ax+av)^{2p-1}(a)(x+v)

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}\lambda_{i}\widetilde{\rho}(ax+av)^{i}(a)\widetilde{\rho}(ax+av)^{2p-2-i}(a)(x+v)^{2},

and thus Eq. (
44
) is satisfied.

The case where
a
and
x+v
are odd.
First, let us show that
\phi(ax)^{k}(av)=a\rho(x)(a)^{k}v
, for all
k\geq 0
. Indeed by induction, we have

\displaystyle\phi(ax)^{k+1}(av)

\displaystyle=\phi(ax)\bigl(a\rho(x)(a)^{k}v\bigl)

\displaystyle=a\Bigl(-a\rho(x)(a)^{k}\phi(x)v+\rho(x)\bigl(a\rho(x)(a)^{k})\bigl)v\Bigl)

\displaystyle=a\rho(x)\bigl(a\rho(x)(a)^{k}\bigl)v

\displaystyle=a\rho(x)(a)^{k+1}v-a^{2}\rho(x)\bigl(\rho(x)(a)^{k}\bigl)v=a\rho(x)(a)^{k+1}v.

In particular, we have
\phi(ax)^{p-1}(av)=a\rho(x)(a)^{p-1}v

\displaystyle(ax+av)^{[p]_{\rtimes}}

\displaystyle=(ax)^{[p]_{\rtimes}}+\phi(ax)^{p-1}(av)

\displaystyle=a\bigl(\rho(x)(a)\bigl)^{p-1}x+a\rho(x)(a)^{p-1}v

\displaystyle=a\bigl(\widetilde{\rho}(x+v)(a)\bigl)^{p-1}(x+v),

and thus Eq. (
46
) is satisfied. ∎

5.
The restricted enveloping algebra of a restricted Lie-Rinehart superalgebra

The purpose of this section is to construct the restricted universal enveloping algebra of a restricted Lie-Rinehart superalgebra. The non-super case has been investigated in
[
Do
]
.

In this section, all associative superalgebras are assumed to have an
unit
.

5.1.
The restricted enveloping algebra of a restricted Lie superalgebra

Let
L
be a Lie superalgebra, and let
U(L)
be its universal enveloping algebra. In the case where
L
is restricted, the
restricted
universal enveloping algebra of
L
is defined (see
[
Pe
]
) by

U_{p}(L)=U(L)/\langle x^{p}-x^{[p]},~x\in L_{\bar{0}}\rangle.

Following
[
Pe
]
, a Poincaré-Birkhoff-Witt basis of
U_{p}(L)
is given by monomials of the form

e_{i_{1}}^{k_{1}}\cdots e_{i_{m}}^{k_{m}}f_{j_{1}}\cdots f_{j_{s}},\quad i_{1}<\cdots<i_{m},\quad j_{1}<\cdots<j_{s},\quad k_{s}\leq p-1,

where
\{e_{i}\}_{i}
is a basis of the even part
L_{\bar{0}}
and
\{f_{j}\}_{j}
is a basis of the odd part
L_{\bar{1}}
.

5.2.
The universal enveloping algebra of a Lie-Rinehart superalgebra

We recall the construction of the universal enveloping algebra of a Lie-Rinehart superalgebra, see
[
La
, Section 5]
. Let
(A,L,\rho)
be a Lie-Rinehart superalgebra. On the superspace
A\oplus L
, a Lie superalgebra structure can be defined by the bracket

(58)

[a+x,b+y]=[x,y]+\rho(x)(b)-(-1)^{|a||y|}\rho(y)(a),\quad\forall a+x,b+y\in A\oplus L.

We note the resulting Lie superalgebra by
A\rtimes L
. Note that
A\rtimes L
is an
A
-module with the action
a(b+x)=ab+ax,~\forall a,b\in A,~\forall x\in L
.

Consider the universal enveloping algebra
U(A\rtimes L)
of
A\rtimes L
and denote by
U^{+}(A\rtimes L)
the subspace of
U(A\rtimes L)
spanned by
i(A\rtimes L)
where
i:A\rtimes L\rightarrow U(A\rtimes L)
is the (even) injection. Then, the universal enveloping algebra of the Lie-Rinehart superalgebra
(A,L,\rho)
is defined by

(59)

U(A,L)=U^{+}(A\rtimes L)/J,

where
J
is the ideal of
U^{+}(A\rtimes L)
spanned by elements
i(a)i(b+x)-i(a(b+x)),~a,b\in A,~x\in L.
Denote by
\iota_{A}:A\rightarrow U(A,L)
and
\iota_{L}:L\rightarrow U(A,L)
the natural maps. Then, it is proved in
[
La
, Section 5]
that
\iota_{A}
is an injective morphism of associative superalgebras,
\iota_{L}
is a morphism of Lie superalgebras, and for all
a\in A
and all
x\in L
, we have

(60)

\displaystyle\iota_{A}(a)\iota_{L}(x)

\displaystyle=\iota_{L}(ax);

(61)

\displaystyle\iota_{A}\bigl(\rho(x)(a)\bigl)

\displaystyle=\iota_{L}(x)\iota_{A}(a)-(-1)^{|a||x|}\iota_{A}(a)\iota_{L}(x).

The triple
\bigl(U(A,L),\iota_{A},\iota_{L}\bigl)
satisfies a the following universal property (see
[
La
, Section 5]
). Let
B
be an associative superalgebra, let
j_{A}:A\rightarrow B
be an even morphism of associative superalgebras and let
j_{L}:L\rightarrow B
be an even morphism of Lie superalgebras (where
B
is seen as a Lie superalgebra under the commutator) satisfying for all
a\in A
and all
x\in L
the conditions

(62)

j_{L}(ax)=j_{A}(a)j_{L}(x);\quad\text{and}\quad j_{A}\bigl(\rho(x)(a)\bigl)=j_{L}(x)j_{A}(a)-(-1)^{|a||x|}j_{A}(a)j_{L}(x).

Then, there exists an unique morphism of associative superalgebras
\phi:U(A,L)\rightarrow B
such that
\phi\circ\iota_{L}=j_{L}
and
\phi\circ\iota_{A}=j_{A}
.

5.3.
The universal enveloping algebra of a restricted Lie-Rinehart superalgebra

We follow the construction described by Dokas in
[
Do
, Section 2]
minding the sign rule. Let
(A,L,\rho)
be a
restricted
Lie-Rinehart superalgebra. Let
\pi_{1}:U^{+}(A\rtimes L)\to U(A,L)=U^{+}(A\rtimes L)/J
be the projection. We define the restricted universal enveloping algebra of
(A,L,\rho)
by

U_{p}(A,L):=U(A,L)/\langle\pi_{1}\circ i(x^{[p]})-(\pi_{1}\circ i(x))^{p},~x\in L_{\bar{0}}\rangle.

We denote by
\pi_{2}:U(A,L)\to U_{p}(A,L)
the projection. Moreover, let
i_{A}=\pi_{2}\circ\iota_{A}
and
i_{L}=\pi_{2}\circ\iota_{L}
. For all
a\in A
and all
x\in L
, we have

(63)

\displaystyle i_{A}(a)i_{L}(x)

\displaystyle=i_{L}(ax);

(64)

\displaystyle i_{A}\bigl(\rho(x)(a)\bigl)

\displaystyle=i_{L}(x)i_{A}(a)-(-1)^{|a||x|}i_{A}(a)i_{L}(x).

We have the following universal property.

Proposition
.

Let
B
be an associative superalgebra, let
j_{A}:A\rightarrow B
be an even morphism of associative superalgebras and let
j_{L}:L\rightarrow B
be an even morphism of restricted Lie superalgebras
(
where
B
is seen as a restricted Lie superalgebra under the commutator and the
p
-th power on even elements
)
satisfying for all
a\in A
and all
x\in L
the conditions

(65)

j_{L}(ax)=j_{A}(a)j_{L}(x);\quad\text{and}\quad j_{A}\bigl(\rho(x)(a)\bigl)=j_{L}(x)j_{A}(a)-(-1)^{|a||x|}j_{A}(a)j_{L}(x).

Then, there exists a unique morphism of associative superalgebras
\psi:U_{p}(A,L)\rightarrow B
such that
\psi\circ i_{L}=j_{L}
and
\psi\circ i_{A}=j_{A}
.

Proof.

Consider a triple
(B,j_{A},j_{L})
satisfying (
65
). We define a linear map

\varphi:A\rtimes L\to B,\quad(a+x)\longmapsto j_{A}(a)+j_{L}(x).

The map
\varphi
is a morphism of Lie superalgebras. Indeed, for all
a,b\in A
and all
x,y\in L
, we have

\displaystyle\varphi(a+x)\varphi(b+y)-(-1)^{|a||y|}\varphi(b+y)\varphi(a+x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~(j_{A}(a)+j_{L}(x))(j_{A}(b)+j_{L}(y))-(-1)^{|a||y|}(j_{A}(b)+j_{L}(y))(j_{A}(a)+j_{L}(x))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~j_{A}(a)j_{L}(y)+j_{L}(x)j_{A}(b)+j_{L}(x)j_{L}(y)-(-1)^{|a||y|}\bigl(j_{A}(b)j_{L}(x)+j_{L}(y)j_{A}(a)+j_{L}(y)j_{L}(x)\bigl)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~j_{A}\bigl(\rho(x)(b)\bigl)-(-1)^{|a||y|}j_{A}\bigl(\rho(y)(a)\bigl)+j_{L}\bigl([x,y]\bigl)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\varphi\bigl([a+x,b+y]\bigl).

By the universal property of
U(A\rtimes L)
, there exists a unique associative superalgebras morphism
\widetilde{\varphi}:U^{+}(A\rtimes L)\to B

Moreover, for all

\displaystyle\widetilde{\varphi}\bigl(i(a)i(b+x)-i(a(b+x))\bigl)

\displaystyle=\widetilde{\varphi}(i(a))\widetilde{\varphi}(i(b+x))-\widetilde{\varphi}(i(ab+ax))

\displaystyle=\varphi(a)\varphi(b+x)-\varphi(ab+ax)

\displaystyle=j_{A}(a)\bigl(j_{A}(b)+j_{L}(x)\bigl)-j_{A}(ab)-j_{L}(ax)=0.

It follows that the ideal

(see Eq. (
59

\widetilde{\varphi}
. Thus,
\widetilde{\varphi}
induces a unique associative superalgebras morphism
\widetilde{\psi}:U(A,L)\to B
.
Since

is a morphism of restricted Lie superalgebras, we have

\displaystyle\widetilde{\psi}\bigl(\pi_{1}\circ i(x)^{p}\bigl)-\widetilde{\psi}\bigl(\pi_{1}\circ i(x^{[p]})\bigl)

\displaystyle=\bigl(\widetilde{\psi}\circ\pi_{1}\circ i(x)\bigl)^{p}-\widetilde{\psi}\circ\pi_{1}\circ i(x^{[p]})

\displaystyle=\varphi(x)^{p}-\varphi(x^{[p]})

\displaystyle=j_{L}(x)^{p}-j_{L}(x^{[p]})=0.

Thus,
\widetilde{\psi}
induces a unique associative superalgebras morphism
\psi:U_{p}(A,L)\rightarrow B
. We have the following commutative diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A\oplus L}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U^{+}(A\rtimes L)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{U(A,L)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{U_{p}(A,L)}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{\varphi}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{1}}

1\leq i\leq p-1
, we have
\Big(\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.25pt}{$\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{p}{i}$}}{\raisebox{0.25pt}{$\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{p}{i}$}}{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{$\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{1}{p}{i}$}}{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{$\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{1}{p}{i}$}}\Big)\equiv 0
and
\deg P_{\lambda}<p.
It follows that

P_{\lambda}(p)\equiv\sum_{i=0}^{\deg P_{\lambda}(p)}c_{i}\Big(\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.25pt}{$\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{p}{i}$}}{\raisebox{0.25pt}{$\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{p}{i}$}}{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{$\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{1}{p}{i}$}}{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{$\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{1}{p}{i}$}}\Big)\equiv 0\pmod{p}.

On the other hand, in the case where
\lambda
is packed, we have
\deg P_{\lambda}(p)=p.
In particular, we have

P_{\lambda}(p)\equiv\sum_{i=0}^{p}c_{i}\Big(\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.25pt}{$\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{p}{i}$}}{\raisebox{0.25pt}{$\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{p}{i}$}}{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{$\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{1}{p}{i}$}}{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{$\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{1}{p}{i}$}}\Big)\equiv c_{p}\mod p.

However, the leading coefficient
l
of
P_{\lambda}(p)
and
c_{\deg P_{\lambda}(p)}
satisfy the relation
l=\frac{c_{\deg P_{\lambda}(p)}}{(\deg P_{\lambda}(p))!}.
Since we have
l=\frac{1}{\lfloor\frac{p}{2}\rfloor!}=\frac{1}{(\frac{p-1}{2})!}
, it follows that
c_{p}=\frac{p!}{(\frac{(p-1)}{2})!}\equiv 0\pmod{p}.
Thus, we have
P_{\lambda}(p)\equiv c_{p}\equiv 0\mod p
for any packed
\lambda
.

Finally,
\Gamma_{2p,p}=\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda\in S^{p}}P_{\lambda}(p)x_{0}^{2p-|\lambda|}x^{\lambda}\equiv 0\pmod{p}
and Proposition
3.3.2.  Proposition
is proved.
\square

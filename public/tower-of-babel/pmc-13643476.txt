Balloon-Assisted Hydrodynamic Contrast Recanalization in Chronic Total Occlusion Intervention

Hydrodynamic contrast recanalization (HDR) has been described as contrast delivery within the chronic total occlusion (CTO) to facilitate the passage of polymer-jacketed wires by altering plaque resistance.1 Conventional HDR techniques are usually performed by contrast injection through a microcatheter; however, this method may be associated with contrast reflux and limited microcatheter support. Therefore, we describe a modified “balloon-assisted hydrodynamic contrast recanalization” maneuver using an over-the-wire (OTW) balloon positioned proximal to the CTO lesion to facilitate localized contrast delivery. This configuration appeared to improve directional delivery of contrast toward the occlusion segment while requiring only a small contrast volume (typically 0.1-0.2 mL). In addition, the proximally anchored balloon may limit the distal propagation of subintimal hematoma caused by antegrade dissection (Figure 1).

Balloon-Assisted Hydrodynamic Contrast Recanalization

OTW = over-the-wire.

A 61-year-old man was referred for percutaneous coronary intervention of a right coronary artery CTO due to medically refractory angina. The occlusion was long, and there were branch vessels present, with a J-CTO (Multicenter CTO Registry in Japan) score of 3 (Figures 2A and 2B, Videos 1 and 2). Attempts to penetrate the proximal cap with highly penetrating guidewires (Gaia Third; Asahi Intecc) failed (Figures 2C and 2D, Videos 3 and 4). A modified balloon-assisted HDR maneuver was subsequently attempted. An OTW balloon (Boston Scientific) was inflated at 6 atm and advanced to the proximal cap, and 0.2 mL of contrast was injected through the balloon lumen (Figure 2E, Video 5). After contrast injection, the polymer-jacketed wire (XT-A; Asahi Intecc) successfully crossed the CTO lesion into the distal right coronary artery. Final angiography and intravascular ultrasound confirmed satisfactory recanalization with true lumen wire position (Figures 2F and 2G, Video 6, Video 7, Video 8, Video 9, Video 10).

OTW balloons are commonly used in CTO percutaneous coronary intervention to improve torque response, reduce guidewire kinking or prolapse, and enhance penetration ability by providing additional support to the guidewire shaft. It also allows the exchange of one guidewire for another without sacrificing the progress made through the lesion.2 The advantage of using an OTW balloon lies in its ability to be anchored in the proximal segment of the occluded vessel, thereby increasing forward support. Anchoring the balloon in the proximal segment of the occlusion may also prevent the proximal hematoma from propagating distally. During balloon-assisted HDR manipulation, proximal balloon anchoring appeared to direct the contrast medium toward the CTO occlusion segment in a targeted manner (Figure 2F). In addition, the OTW balloon may reduce backward displacement during contrast injection. For occlusive lesions with a side branch located proximal to the occlusion segment, OTW balloon anchoring may help prevent occlusion of the proximal side branch caused by contrast injection.

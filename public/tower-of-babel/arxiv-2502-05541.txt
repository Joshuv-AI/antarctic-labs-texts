Huber Theorem revisited in dimensions 2 2 and 4 4
Paul Laurain, Dorian Martino

Abstract
. We study the second Huber Theorem in dimensions 2 and 4. In dimension 2, we prove the optimal regularity for the conformal factor using Coulomb frames. In dimension 4, we introduce another Coulomb-type condition which is similar to the case of Yang–Mills connections. We obtain a generalization of the two-dimensional case that can be applied to study the singularities of Bach-flat metrics.



1.
Introduction

In 1958, Huber proved, in his seminal paper
[
39
]
, the following result on the topology of surface with finite total negative curvature

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(M,g)
an orientable surfaces of class
C^{2}
, open and complete and such that

\int_{M}K^{-}_{g}\,dv_{g}<+\infty,

where
K^{-}_{g}=\max(0,-K_{g})
with
K_{g}
is the Gauss curvature. Then
M
as finite topological type and moreover

\int_{M}|K_{g}|\,dv_{g}<+\infty.

Here finite topological type means that there exists a compact surface without boundary
\overline{M}
and a finite set of points
p_{1},\dots,p_{N}\in\overline{M}
such that
M
is homeomorphic to
\overline{M}\setminus\{p_{1},\dots,p_{N}\}
. Once the topological type is fixed, a natural question concerns the conformal type,
i.e.
which kind of Riemann structure is supported by such surfaces. Huber also answer this question in the same paper
[
39
]
, proving the following.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(M,g)
an orientable surfaces of class
C^{2}
, open and complete and such that

\int_{M}K^{-}_{g}\,dv_{g}<+\infty,

where
K^{-}_{g}=\max(0,-K_{g})
with
K_{g}
is the Gauss curvature. Then
M
has finite conformal type.

Here finite conformal type means that there exists a compact Riemann surface without boundary
\overline{M}
and a finite set of points
p_{1},\dots,p_{N}\in\overline{M}
such that
M
is conformally equivalente to
\overline{M}\setminus\{p_{1},\dots,p_{N}\}
. Hence there exists a
C^{1}
-diffeomorphism
\psi:M\rightarrow\overline{M}\setminus\{p_{1},\dots,p_{N}\}
such that if, for instance, we equip
\overline{M}
with metric
g_{0}
having constant Gaussian curvature, then
\psi^{*}(g_{0})=e^{2u}g
for some continuous function
u:M\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
.

In 1995, inspired by the work of Toro
[
62
]
, Müller and Šverák
[
50
]
proved an intrinsic version of the Huber’s theorem: assuming for initial regularity only the fact that the second fundamental form is in
L^{2}
, they proved that those surfaces admits a Lipschitz conformal parametrization. Around a potential singularity, they prove the following:

Theorem 1.3

(
[
50
]
, see also lemma A.5 in
[
53
]
)
.

Let
\Phi:\mathbb{D}\setminus\{0\}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}
be a conformal immersion such that
\Phi\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb{D}\setminus\{0\})
and its Gauss map is also in
W^{1,2}(\mathbb{D}\setminus\{0\})
. Then there exist
\theta_{0}\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
such that, up to translation,

|\Phi(z)|\sim|z|^{\theta_{0}}\text{ and }|\nabla\Phi(z)|\sim|z|^{\theta_{0}-1}\text{ near }0.

Moreover, if we denote

\lambda(z)=\frac{1}{2}\log\left(\frac{1}{2}|\nabla\Phi(z)|^{2}\right)=(\theta_{0}-1)\log(|z|)+u(z),

then the remainder
u
satisfies

\displaystyle\begin{cases}u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{D})&\text{ if }\theta_{0}=1,\\
u\in L^{(2,\infty)}(\mathbb{D})&\text{ if }\theta_{0}\geq 2.\end{cases}

Here
L^{(2,\infty)}
denotes the weak-
L^{2}
space also known as Lorentz space, see
[
31
, Section 1.4]
for a precise definition. One key ingredient for such result consists in remarking that the Gauss curvature is a Jacobian of the gradient of the Gauss map. Then by a standard result in compensated compactness the Gauss curvature is in better space than
L^{1}
, namely in the Hardy space
\mathcal{H}^{1}
, see the seminal work of Coiffman, Lions, Meyer ans Semmes
[
22
]
. We can conclude thanks to
\mathcal{H}^{1}-\mathrm{BMO}
duality and the Liouville equation.

The first goal of this paper is to provide an intrinsic and optimal version of the previous theorem. In particular, we have obtain the following version of the Huber’s theorem, which is a consequence of the local version stated in
Theorem

2.1
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Consider
\Sigma
a Riemann surface and a finite number of distinct
p_{1},\ldots,p_{K}\in\Sigma
. Let
g
be a smooth metric on
\Sigma\setminus\{p_{1},\ldots,p_{K}\}
such that

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}|K_{g}|\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g}<\infty.

Then, there exists
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{K}\in\mathbb{R}
, a diffeomorphism
\psi:\Sigma\setminus\{p_{1},\ldots,p_{K}\}\to\Sigma\setminus\{p_{1},\ldots,p_{K}\}
, a smooth metric
h
on
\Sigma
with constant curvature and a function
\varphi\in\mathrm{BMO}\cap W^{1,(2,\infty)}(\Sigma,h)
such that
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(\Sigma\setminus\{p_{1},\ldots,p_{K}\})
and

\displaystyle\psi^{*}g=\mathrm{dist}_{h}(p_{1},\cdot)^{2\alpha_{1}}\cdots d_{h}(p_{K},\cdot)^{2\alpha_{K}}e^{2\varphi}h.

Furthermore,
\varphi
is a weak solution to the Liouville equation

\displaystyle\Delta_{h}\varphi=K_{g}e^{2\varphi}\mathrm{dist}_{h}(p_{1},\cdot)^{2\alpha_{1}}\cdots d_{h}(p_{K},\cdot)^{2\alpha_{K}}-K_{h}\ \ \ \ \text{ in }\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\Sigma,h).

If additionally, we have
\varphi\in C^{0}(\Sigma)
, then the exponents
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{K}
are all integers.

The optimality of the result is discuss in beginning of section
2
, with examples in section
2.6
and
2.7
.

The second goal of this paper consists in extending Huber’s theorem to dimension
4
. This question has already been addressed by various authors, who focused mainly on the generalization of
Theorem

1.1
, namely the topological part. The first result in this direction has been obtained in 2002 by Chang, Qing and Yang for the conformally flat case.

Theorem 1.5

(Theorem 2 of
[
17
]
)
.

Let
(\Omega\subset S^{4},g=e^{2w}g_{0})
be a complete conformally flat metric satisfying:

(i)

The scalar curvature
R_{g}
is bounded between to scalar constant and
|\nabla_{g}R_{g}|
is bounded,

(ii)

the Ricci curvature has a lower bound,

(iii)

and the
Q

\int_{\Omega}|Q_{g}|\,dv_{g}<+\infty.

Then
\Omega=S^{4}\setminus\{p_{1},\dots,p_{k}\}

Independently, Carron and Herzlich
[

proved in 2002 that an open set

(M^{n},g_{0})
(for

g=e^{2u}g_{0}
is also given by
\Omega=M\setminus\{p_{1},\ldots,p_{k}\}
, under some conditions on the volume growth of balls for

and the finiteness of the
L^{\frac{n}{2}}
-norm of the Ricci tensor of

[

. Finally in 2022, Chen and Li reached the following optimal version.

Theorem 1.6

(Theorem 1.4 in
[
20
]
)
.

Let
\Omega
a domain of
(M^{n},g_{0})
a Riemannian manifold of dimension
n>2
. Assume
\Omega
is endowed with a complete Riemannian metric
g

which is conformal to
g_{0}
. Suppose moreover that:

\int_{\Omega}|\mathrm{Ric}|^{\frac{n}{2}}\,dv_{g}<+\infty.

Then
M\setminus\Omega
is discrete. Moreover if
(M,g_{0})
is compact then
\Omega=M\setminus\{p_{1},\dots,p_{k}\}

All those previous work are in the spirit of the first Huber’s theorem,
i.e.
controlling the topology of the domain. But it is important to notice that those result are not a complete generalization of
Theorem

1.1
, since they need
g
to be
a priori
conformally equivalent to a background metric
g_{0}
which extends smoothly outside of the domain of definition of
g
. The content of
Theorem

1.2
is to prove, in dimension 2, that such an extension exists. If one would like a strict equivalent of Huber’s theorem, one should also prove the existence of such an extension. In 1997, Herzlich
[
36
]
studied the conformal compactification of asymptotically flat Riemannian manifolds
(M^{n},g)
of dimension
n\geq 3
and proved the following result, see
Section

4
for the definitions.

Theorem 1.7

(Theorem C in
[
36
]
)
.

An asymptotically flat Riemannian manifold, where the Weyl curvature tensor decays as
r^{-2-\epsilon}
at infinity and the Cotton-York tensor decays as
r^{-3-\epsilon}
(with
\epsilon>0
), is obtained via stereographic projection from a compact
C^{0}
-class Riemannian manifold.

The main idea of the proof of the above result is to study the conformal connections introduced by Cartan
[
16
]
in 1924. He proves that the conformal fiber bundle does not degenerate at infinity, and is actually Hölder-continuous. Considering an inversion, this theorem can be translated to give an extension to a metric only defined on
\mathbb{B}\setminus\{0\}
.

Our main motivation is to develop a similar result which would be applicable to variational problems such as the study of compactness properties of Bach-flat metrics or generalizations of Willmore surfaces in dimension 4. In these questions, the setting is usually a sequence of manifolds which converges strongly everywhere except at a finite number of points and one would like to understand the behaviour of these manifolds across these singularities. This is the so-called bubbling convergence which usually follows from an
\varepsilon
-regularity property. For 2-dimensional problems see for instance the works
[
8
,
42
,
45
]
and the references therein. In dimension 4, for instance in the study of Einstein metrics or Bach-flat metrics, Anderson
[
4
,
5
]
and Tian–Viaclovksy
[
60
,
61
]
proved that the only possible singularities occurring along a sequence of bounded energy are orbifold singularities, see also
[
10
]
.

Thus, our main goal is to change the nature of the assumptions of
Theorem

1.7
into
L^{p}
-bounds on various curvature tensors, and to consider our initial manifold to be a ball minus its center, or slight generalizations. Inspired by our new proof in dimension
2
, we obtain the following result.

Theorem 1.8
.

Consider a smooth orbifold metric
g
on
X:=C(\mathbb{S}^{3}/\Gamma)\setminus\{0\}
, where
\Gamma
is a finite subgroup of
\mathrm{SO}\left(4,\mathbb{R}\right)
and
C(\mathbb{S}^{3}/\Gamma)
is the cone over
\mathbb{S}^{3}/\Gamma
. Assume that
g
satisfies the following assumptions:

(1)

The volume of
X
is finite:
\mathrm{vol}_{g}(X)<+\infty
.

(2)

We have
\mathrm{Riem}^{g}\in L^{2}(X,g)
and
B^{g}\in L^{p}(X,g)
for some
p\in(1,2]
.

(3)

The measure

is doubling: there exists a constant
\gamma_{D}>0
such that for any ball
B_{g}(x,2s)\subset X
, it holds
\mathrm{vol}_{g}(B_{g}(x,2s))\leq\gamma_{D}\mathrm{vol}_{g}(B_{g}(x,s))
.

(4)

There exists a constant
\gamma_{S}>0
such that the following Sobolev inequality holds:

\displaystyle\forall u\in C^{\infty}(X),\ \ \ \|u\|_{L^{4}(X)}^{2}\leq\gamma_{S}\int_{X}|\nabla u|^{2}_{g}+|u|^{2}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g}.

(5)

There exists a constant
\gamma_{L}>0
such that the following regularity estimate holds:

\displaystyle\forall u\in C^{\infty}_{c}(X),\ \ \ \|\nabla u\|_{L^{4}(X,g)}\leq\gamma_{L}\|\Delta_{g}u\|_{L^{2}(X,g)}.

(6)

There exists a constant
\gamma_{H}>0
such that the following regularity estimate holds:

\displaystyle\forall u\in C^{\infty}(X),\ \ \ \|\mathrm{Hess}_{g}u\|_{L^{2}(X,g)}\leq\gamma_{H}\left(\|\Delta_{g}u\|_{L^{2}(X,g)}+\|u\|_{L^{2}(X,g)}\right).

There exists a constant
\varepsilon>0
depending only on
\gamma_{S},\gamma_{L},\gamma_{D},\gamma_{H},\gamma_{A},\Lambda_{A}
such that the following holds. Assume that

\displaystyle\|\mathrm{Riem}^{g}\|_{L^{2}(X,g)}+\mathrm{vol}_{g}(X)\leq\varepsilon.

Then there exist a diffeomorphism
\psi:X\to X
, a conformal factor
u\in C^{\infty}(X)
, and a
W^{2,2p}
orbifold metric
h
on
C(\mathbb{S}^{3}/\Gamma)
which is smooth on
X
such that

\displaystyle\psi^{*}g=e^{2u}h.

The assumptions might seem numerous. A first remark is that in the case
p=2
, the assumptions
3
and
6
are not necessary. In the general case, we can separate them in two categories: the assumptions
1
and
2
are natural geometric assumptions, the one from
3
to

are analytical assumption which permits to transform the geometric assumptions into estimates. To the best of the authors’ knowledge the analytical assumptions are distinct but linked, we discuss them in
Section

3.1
. And they are satisfies on any "reasonnable" manifold, for instance, after compactification of an asymptotically flat manifold. As a consequence of

1.8
, we have the following version of Herzlich’s result. The second part can also be considered as an improvement of theorem 1.1 of Tian-Viaclovsky
[
60
]
.

Corollary 1.9
.

An asymptotically flat Riemannian manifold with the Riemann tensor is
L^{2}
, where, for some
p>1
, the Bach tensor is in
L^{p}
, is obtained via stereographic projection from a compact
C^{0,\alpha}
-class Riemannian manifold for some
\alpha>0
depending on

C^{0,\alpha}
-class Riemannian manifold for any

.

The idea of the proof of theorem
1.8
is inspired by our proof in the two dimensional case, where we construct a Coulomb frame to extend the metric through the point, using the fact that Coulomb frame are integrable in dimension
2
. Our initial idea was to produce an optimal frame in dimension
4
but understanding which is the right energy to minimize is not clear since minimizing the connection forms does not seem to produce an integrable frame because of the Weyl obstuction. Instead, we consider the tractor bundle and find a Coulomb gauge as it can be done for Yang–Mills by minimizing the
L^{2}
-norm of the connections of this bundle. Indeed, Korzyński and Lewandowski
[
41
]
in 2003 (see also
[
30
]
for the formalism of tractor calculus) proved that Bach-flat metrics corresponds to the conformal class having whose tractor bundle is Yang–Mills. Starting from this, one can expect to find an equivalent formulation for the Coulomb gauge introduced by Uhlenbeck
[
63
]
, see also the survey of Rivière
[
54
]
. The condition for a connection
D=d+A
over some bundle on the unit ball
\mathbb{B}^{4}
to be a Coulomb gauge is the condition
D^{*}A=0
. It turns out that this system is the Euler–Lagrange system of the functional
\int_{\mathbb{B}}|A|^{2}
. In the case of the tractor bundle, the connection is mainly given by the Schouten tensor. We refer to
[
23
,
64
]
for an introduction to tractor calculus. Thanks to Chern–Gauss–Bonnet Theorem in dimension 4, the variations of the
L^{2}
-norm of the Schouten tensor are equivalent to variations of the
L^{2}
-norm of its trace. Hence, our problem of finding an optimal metric reduces to minimizing the
L^{2}
norm of the scalar curvature plus a volume term in the conformal class of our given metric, see
Section

3.2
for more details.

From a purely PDE view-point, the proof of
Theorem

1.8
can be described as follows. One can understand the Bach tensor as the given data in a system satisfied by the Schouten tensor:

\displaystyle-\Delta_{g}\mathrm{Sch}^{g}=B^{g}-\mathrm{Hess}_{g}J^{g}+\mathrm{Riem}^{g}\star\mathrm{Sch}^{g},

where
J^{g}
is the trace of the Schouten tensor
\mathrm{Sch}^{g}
and the term
\mathrm{Riem}^{g}\star\mathrm{Sch}^{g}
stands for a linear combination of various contractions of the Riemann tensor
\mathrm{Riem}^{g}
and the Schouten tensor. Tracing this system provides no information since the Bach tensor is traceless (this is a consequence of the conformal invariance). Roughly, this means that the Bach tensor is actually the data in a system satisfied only by the traceless part of the Schouten tensor. In order to recover an information on the full tensor, one needs to prescribe the trace part
J^{g}
. In our setting, we work on degenerating annuli
\mathbb{B}\setminus\mathbb{B}_{r}
, with
r\to 0
. A first idea would be to solve a Yamabe problem (without caring too much about the boundary conditions) in order to find a metric
g_{0}=e^{2u}g
such that
J^{g_{0}}
is constant. Then, the analysis would essentially be contained in the work of Tian and Viaclovsky
[
60
,
61
]
. However, the resolution of such a problem requires some additional assumptions such as having a positive Yamabe invariant, see for instance
[
25
,
47
]
and
Remark

3.2
. In somesense our
\epsilon
-regularity, theorem
Theorem

3.12
comes as a generalization of the one of Tian-Viaclovsky, see
Remark

3.13
.In order to provide a general approach, one needs to solve an equation of higher order (an equation having the same number of derivative as
\Delta_{g}\mathrm{Sch}^{g}
) and this is the reason of the assumptions
5
and
6
in
Theorem

1.8
. Our approach provides a metric
g_{0}=e^{2u}g
which satisfies
\Delta_{g_{0}}J^{g_{0}}=1
and preserves a bound on the volume of
g_{0}
. Thus,
J^{g_{0}}
is as regular as authorized by the geometric setting and the regularity of the metric
g_{0}
is now fully determined by the Bach tensor
B^{g_{0}}=e^{-2u}B^{g}
.

This strategy applies only to the dimension 4 since the Bach tensor is conformally invariant in dimension 4 and the Chern–Gauss–Bonnet formula is valid only in even dimension. However, we a similar approach might be fruitful in higher dimension by considering the Fefferman–Graham obstruction tensors, see
[
26
]
. We also believe that this approach could be used in the same spirit as Coulomb gauges for Willmore surfaces
[
8
,
43
,
48
]
, harmonic maps
[
42
]
and Yang–Mills connections
[
29
,
54
,
63
]
, in order to study the regularity and compactness properties of Bach-flat metrics.

It seems not possible to reduce the assumption
B^{g}\in L^{p}
for
p>1
to
p=1
. It would be interesting to look for possible counter-examples. Many directions of generalizations of
Theorem

1.8
can be considered. First, one can study the higher regularity, as in
[
36
]
, were we assume the Bach tensor to be in
W^{k,p}
up to the singularity in some coordinates, and deduce that the background metric belongs to
W^{k+2,2p}
.
Theorem

1.8
can be applied to Bach-flat metrics, but also the potential singularities arising along the Bach flow
[
6
,
21
]
since it is valid for a Bach tensor in
L^{2}
. Another important direction, would be to integrate by parts the Bach tensor in order to obtain conditions on the Cotton tensor (which would ask for one derivative less). In particular, we suspect that the same conclusion holds if instead of assuming
B^{g}\in L^{p}
, we assume
\mathrm{Cot}^{g}\in L^{\frac{4p}{4-p}}
for some

The proof of

A standard setting to apply

2.1.
Existence of Coulomb frame on
\mathbb{D}\setminus\mathbb{D}_{r}
.

Let
r\in(0,\frac{1}{4})

By the Grahm–Schmidt process, we produce an orthonormal frame
\bar{E}:=(\bar{E}_{1},\bar{E}_{2})
on
(\mathbb{D}\setminus\mathbb{D}_{r},g)
from the basis
(\partial_{x},\partial_{y})

R_{r}\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb{D}\setminus\mathbb{D}_{r};\mathrm{SO}\left(2,\mathbb{R}\right))

E_{r}:=(E_{1,r},E_{2,r})=(\bar{E}_{1},\bar{E}_{2})R_{r}\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb{D}\setminus\mathbb{D}_{r};\mathrm{GL}\left(2,\mathbb{R}\right))
minimizes the following quantity:

(2)

\displaystyle f(r):=\inf\left\{\int_{\mathbb{D}\setminus\mathbb{D}_{r}}\left|\left\langle dE_{1,r},E_{2,r}\right\rangle_{g}\right|^{2}_{g}d\mathrm{vol}_{g}\mid\begin{aligned} &(E_{1,r},E_{2,r})=(\bar{E}_{1},\bar{E}_{2})R,\\
&R\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb{D}\setminus\mathbb{D}_{r};\mathrm{SO}\left(2,\mathbb{R}\right))\end{aligned}\right\}.

If
A:=\left\langle d\bar{E}_{1},\bar{E}_{2}\right\rangle_{g}
is the connection form of the frame
\bar{E}
, then
f(r)
can be written as

(3)

\displaystyle f(r)=\inf\left\{\int_{\mathbb{D}\setminus\mathbb{D}_{r}}\left|A+(dR)R^{T}\right|^{2}_{g}d\mathrm{vol}_{g}:R\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb{D}\setminus\mathbb{D}_{r};\mathrm{SO}\left(2,\mathbb{R}\right))\right\}.

The frame
E_{r}
satisfies the following Euler-Lagrange system, proved in
[
35
, Lemma 4.1.3]
:

(4)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}d\left(*_{g}\left\langle dE_{1,r},E_{2,r}\right\rangle_{g}\right)=0&\text{ in }\mathbb{D}\setminus\mathbb{D}_{r},\\
\left\langle\partial_{\nu}E_{1,r},E_{2,r}\right\rangle_{g}=0&\text{ on }\partial\left(\mathbb{D}\setminus\mathbb{D}_{r}\right).\end{cases}

Here,
\nu
is the unit normal vector on
\partial\left(\mathbb{D}\setminus\mathbb{D}_{r}\right)
for the metric
g
. Furthermore, the following estimate holds:

(5)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}\setminus\mathbb{D}_{r}}\left|\left\langle dE_{1,r},E_{2,r}\right\rangle_{g}\right|^{2}_{g}d\mathrm{vol}_{g}\leq\int_{\mathbb{D}\setminus\mathbb{D}_{r}}\left|\left\langle d\bar{E}_{1},\bar{E}_{2}\right\rangle_{g}\right|^{2}_{g}d\mathrm{vol}_{g}.

From the formulation (
3
), the system (
4
) can also be written in the form of harmonic maps taking values into
\mathrm{SO}\left(2,\mathbb{R}\right)
, see
[
55
]
:

(6)

\displaystyle d^{*_{g}}\left((dR+A)R^{T}\right)=0.

2.2.
Construction of the conformal factor

Let
\omega:=\left\langle dE_{1,r},E_{2,r}\right\rangle_{g}
be the connection form of
E_{r}
. By Poincaré Lemma, there exists a function
\lambda_{r}\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb{D}\setminus\mathbb{D}_{r};\mathbb{R})
and a constant
\Lambda_{r}\in\mathbb{R}
, called residue, such that

(7)

\displaystyle*_{g}\omega

\displaystyle=d\lambda_{r}+\Lambda_{r}*_{\xi}d(\log\rho),

where
*_{\xi}
is the Hodge star for the flat metric
\xi
. On
\partial\mathbb{D}
and
\partial\mathbb{D}_{r}
, there exists a function
h
such that
*_{g}d\theta=hd\nu
. Thus, it holds

\displaystyle\partial_{\theta}\lambda_{r}+\Lambda_{r}=*_{g}\omega\llcorner\partial_{\theta}=\left\langle\omega,hd\nu\right\rangle_{g}=h\left\langle\partial_{\nu}E_{1,r},E_{2,r}\right\rangle_{g}=0.

After integration on
\partial\mathbb{D}
and on
\partial\mathbb{D}_{r}
, we obtain

(8)

\displaystyle\Lambda_{r}=0,\quad\text{and }\quad\partial_{\theta}\lambda_{r}=0\ \text{on }\partial(\mathbb{D}\setminus\mathbb{D}_{r}).

Therefore, (
7
) reduces to:

(9)

\displaystyle*_{g}\left(\left\langle dE_{1,r},E_{2,r}\right\rangle_{g}\right)

\displaystyle=d\lambda_{r}.

We apply the operator
d*_{g}
on this equality to deduce

\displaystyle d(*_{g}d\lambda_{r})=d\left(\left\langle dE_{1,r},E_{2,r}\right\rangle_{g}\right).

We apply the Hodge star
*_{\xi}
in order to obtain the following elliptic equation on
\lambda_{r}
:

(10)

\displaystyle-\partial_{i}\left(g^{ij}\sqrt{\det g}\,\partial_{j}\lambda_{r}\right)

\displaystyle=\nabla^{\perp}(g_{\alpha\beta}E_{2,r}^{\beta})\cdot(\nabla E_{1,r}^{\alpha}).

From the boundary condition (
8
), we deduce that
\lambda_{r}
is constant on
\partial\mathbb{D}
and on
\partial\mathbb{D}_{r}
. Thus, we can assume that
\lambda_{r}=0
on
\partial\mathbb{D}
. We finally obtain the following system on
\lambda_{r}\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb{D}\setminus\mathbb{D}_{r})
:

(11)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{i}\left(g^{ij}\sqrt{\det g}\,\partial_{j}\lambda_{r}\right)=\nabla^{\perp}(g_{\alpha\beta}E_{2,r}^{\beta})\cdot(\nabla E_{1,r}^{\alpha})&\text{ in }\mathbb{D}\setminus\mathbb{D}_{r},\\
\lambda_{r}=0&\text{ on }\partial\mathbb{D},\\
\lambda_{r}=\fint_{\partial\mathbb{D}_{r}}\lambda_{r}&\text{ on }\partial\mathbb{D}_{r}.\end{cases}

Claim 2.3
.

It holds
\lambda_{r}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{D}\setminus\mathbb{D}_{r})
.

We will show in
Remark

2.6
that
\lambda_{r}
is actually
C^{\infty}
. But the estimate used to prove this regularity will not be true up to the origin. Hence, we do not record them.

Proof.

We consider the following decomposition:

(12)

\displaystyle\lambda_{r}=\varphi_{r}+h_{r}.

Here, we define
h_{r}
as the solution to

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{i}\left(g^{ij}\sqrt{\det g}\,\partial_{j}h_{r}\right)=0&\text{ in }\mathbb{D}\setminus\mathbb{D}_{r},\\
h_{r}=0&\text{ on }\partial\mathbb{D},\\
h_{r}=\fint_{\partial\mathbb{D}_{r}}\lambda_{r}&\text{ on }\partial\mathbb{D}_{r}.\end{cases}

Hence, the rest
\varphi_{r}:=\lambda_{r}-h_{r}
solves

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\partial_{i}\left(g^{ij}\sqrt{\det g}\,\partial_{j}\varphi_{r}\right)=\nabla^{\perp}(g_{\alpha\beta}E_{2,r}^{\beta})\cdot(\nabla E_{1,r}^{\alpha})&\text{ in }\mathbb{D}\setminus\mathbb{D}_{r},\\
\varphi_{r}=0&\text{ on }\partial\mathbb{D},\\
\varphi_{r}=0&\text{ on }\partial\mathbb{D}_{r}.\end{cases}

Since the metric
g
is smooth on
\overline{\mathbb{D}\setminus\mathbb{D}_{r}}
, the operator
L:=\partial_{i}\left(g^{ij}\sqrt{\det g}\,\partial_{j}\cdot\right)
is a smooth elliptic operator. Thanks to
[
18
, Theorem 0.2]
, it holds
\varphi_{r}\in L^{\infty}\cap W^{1,2}_{0}(\mathbb{D}\setminus\mathbb{D}_{r})
. Since
h_{r}
is
L
-harmonic with constant boundary datas, we also have
h_{r}\in C^{\infty}\left(\overline{\mathbb{D}\setminus\mathbb{D}_{r}}\right)
. From (
12
), we deduce that
\lambda_{r}\in L^{\infty}\cap W^{1,2}(\mathbb{D}\setminus\mathbb{D}_{r})
with the estimate

\displaystyle\|\lambda_{r}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{D}\setminus\mathbb{D}_{r})}\leq C\left(\left|\fint_{\partial\mathbb{D}_{r}}\lambda_{r}\right|+\|d(gE_{2,r})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{D}\setminus\mathbb{D}_{r},\xi)}\|dE_{1,r}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{D}\setminus\mathbb{D}_{r},\xi)}\right).

∎

2.3.
Integration of the Coulomb frame.

We define
\Omega_{r}:=\mathbb{D}\setminus\mathbb{D}_{r}
. In order to obtain a bilipschitz diffeomorphism, we consider the following frame: given
i\in\{1,2\}
, we define

(13)

\displaystyle F_{i,r}=e^{-\lambda_{r}}E_{i,r}\in W^{1,2}\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega_{r}).

As in
[
35
, Lemma 5.4.1]
, it holds

\displaystyle[F_{1,r},F_{2,r}]=0.

We now consider the maximal solution to Frobenius theorem, see for instance
[
65
, Theorem 1.64]
or
[
57
, Chapter 6]
: there exists a unique maximal solution connected domain
U_{r}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
and a map
\psi_{r}\in W^{1,\infty}\cap W^{2,2}(U_{r};\Omega_{r})
such that

•

the initial conditions are given by:

(14)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{4},0\right)\in U_{r},\quad\text{and}\quad\psi_{r}\left(\frac{1}{4},0\right)=\left(\frac{1}{4},0\right).

•

the following relations are satisfied:

(15)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{x}\psi_{r}=F_{1,r}\circ\psi_{r},\\
\partial_{y}\psi_{r}=F_{2,r}\circ\psi_{r}.\end{cases}

Claim 2.4
.

It holds
\Omega_{r}=U_{r}
.

Proof.

Consider the domain

\Omega_{r}^{+}:=\Omega_{r}\cap\left\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:y>-r\right\}.

The domain
\Omega_{r}^{+}
is simply connected, so there exists a solution
\varphi^{+}\in\mathrm{Diff}(\Omega_{r}^{+})
to (
14
)-(
15
) on the domain
\Omega_{r}^{+}
. Thanks to
[
57
, Chapter 6, Theorem 1]
, we have uniqueness of the solution to (
14
)-(
15
). Hence, it holds
\varphi_{+}=\psi_{r}
on
\Omega_{r}^{+}\cap U_{r}
. Since
\psi_{r}
is a maximal solution, it holds
\Omega_{r}^{+}\subset U_{r}
. Otherwise, we could extend
\psi_{r}
to
\Omega_{r}^{+}
by
\varphi_{+}
, which contradicts the maximality of
\psi_{r}
. Since
(\psi_{r})_{|\Omega_{r}^{+}}=\varphi_{+}
, we deduce that
\psi_{r}:\Omega_{r}^{+}\to\Omega_{r}^{+}
is a diffeomorphism. We proceed in the same manner to obtain that the following domain is contained in
U_{r}
:

\Omega_{r}^{-}:=\Omega_{r}\cap\left\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:y<r\right\}.

Furthermore, the map
\psi_{r}:\Omega_{r}^{-}\to\Omega_{r}^{-}
is a diffeomorphism as well. Hence, we obtain
\Omega_{r}=\Omega_{r}^{-}\cup\Omega_{r}^{+}\subset U_{r}
and
\psi_{r}(\Omega_{r})=\Omega_{r}
. Since
\psi_{r}:U_{r}\to\Omega_{r}
is a diffeomorphism, we have
\Omega_{r}=U_{r}
.
∎

Hence, the metric
g
satisfies in the chart
\psi_{r}
on
\Omega_{r}
:

\displaystyle g_{ij}=g(\partial_{i}\psi_{r},\partial_{j}\psi_{r})=g(F^{1}_{r}\circ\psi_{r},F^{2}_{r}\circ\psi_{r}).

From (
13
) and the fact that
(E_{1,r},E_{2,r})
is an orthonormal frame for
g
, we obtain

(16)

\displaystyle\psi_{r}^{*}g=e^{-2\lambda_{r}}\xi\ \ \text{on }\Omega_{r}.

In particular,
\left\langle E_{1,r},E_{2,r}\right\rangle_{\xi}=0
.

Remark 2.5
.

We can rewrite the equation (
11
). The function
\lambda_{r}
satisfies the following system:

(17)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\Delta_{\xi}\lambda_{r}=\left\langle\nabla^{\perp}(e^{-\lambda_{r}}E_{2,r}),\nabla(e^{-\lambda_{r}}E_{1,r})\right\rangle_{\xi}&\text{ in }\mathbb{D}\setminus\mathbb{D}_{r},\\
\lambda_{r}=0&\text{ on }\partial\mathbb{D},\\
\lambda_{r}=\fint_{\partial\mathbb{D}_{r}}\lambda_{r}&\text{ on }\partial\mathbb{D}_{r}.\end{cases}

Proof.

Thanks to (
16
), it holds

\displaystyle\nabla^{\perp}(e^{-\lambda_{r}}E_{2,r})\cdot\nabla(e^{-\lambda_{r}}E_{1,r})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \left(-(\nabla^{\perp}\lambda_{r})e^{-\lambda_{r}}E_{2,r}+e^{-\lambda_{r}}(\nabla^{\perp}E_{2,r})\right)\cdot\left(-(\nabla\lambda_{r})e^{-\lambda_{r}}E_{1,r}+e^{-\lambda_{r}}(\nabla E_{1,r})\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ -(\nabla^{\perp}\lambda_{r})e^{-2\lambda_{r}}E_{2,r}\cdot(\nabla E_{1,r})-(\nabla\lambda_{r})e^{-2\lambda_{r}}(\nabla^{\perp}E_{2,r})\cdot E_{1,r}+e^{-2\lambda_{r}}(\nabla^{\perp}E_{2,r})\cdot(\nabla E_{1,r})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ -2(\nabla^{\perp}\lambda_{r})e^{-2\lambda_{r}}E_{2,r}\cdot\nabla E_{1,r}+e^{-2\lambda_{r}}(\nabla^{\perp}E_{2,r})\cdot(\nabla E_{1,r})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \nabla^{\perp}\left(e^{-2\lambda_{r}}E_{2,r}\right)\cdot\nabla E_{1,r}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \nabla^{\perp}(g_{\alpha\beta}E_{2,r}^{\beta})\cdot(\nabla E_{1,r}^{\alpha}).

∎

Remark 2.6
.

As a consequence of (
16
), we deduce that
\lambda_{r}
and
R
are
C^{\infty}
on
\mathbb{D}\setminus\mathbb{D}_{r}
.

Proof.

As a consequence of (
16
) and of the conformal invariance of the Laplacian, the system (
6
) can be written as

\displaystyle-(\Delta_{\xi}R)R^{T}+(\nabla R)\cdot(\nabla^{\perp}R^{T})-\mathrm{div}_{\xi}(AR^{T})=0.

Thus, it holds

\displaystyle\Delta_{\xi}R=(\nabla R)\cdot(\nabla^{\perp}R^{T})R-\mathrm{div}_{\xi}(A)+AR^{T}\nabla R.

Following
[
55
]
, using that
A\in C^{\infty}
and
R\in L^{\infty}
, we deduce that
R\in C^{\infty}
. From (
9
), we deduce that
\lambda_{r}\in C^{\infty}
.
∎

2.4.
Limit
r\to 0

We show bounds on
\lambda_{r}
independant of
r
in order to consider the limit
r\to 0
. To do so, we consider the following decomposition

(18)

\displaystyle\lambda_{r}:=\varphi_{r}+h_{r},

where
h_{r}
is defined as the solution to the system:

(19)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\Delta_{\xi}h_{r}=0&\text{ in }\mathbb{D}\setminus\mathbb{D}_{r},\\
h_{r}=0&\text{ on }\partial\mathbb{D},\\
h_{r}=\fint_{\partial\mathbb{D}_{r}}\lambda_{r}&\text{ on }\partial\mathbb{D}_{r}.\end{cases}

Thanks to the Liouville equation
-\Delta_{\xi}\lambda_{r}=K_{g}e^{-2\lambda_{r}}
, we deduce that the rest
\varphi_{r}:=\lambda_{r}-h_{r}
satisfies

(20)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\Delta_{\xi}\varphi_{r}=K_{g}e^{-2\lambda_{r}}&\text{ in }\mathbb{D}\setminus\mathbb{D}_{r},\\
\varphi_{r}=0&\text{ on }\partial\mathbb{D},\\
\varphi_{r}=0&\text{ on }\partial\mathbb{D}_{r}.\end{cases}

2.4.1.
The harmonic part

We solve (
19
) explicitely: there exists a constant
\alpha_{r}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

(21)

\displaystyle\forall x\in\Omega_{r},\quad h_{r}(x):=\alpha_{r}\log\left(|x|\right)=\left(\fint_{\partial\mathbb{D}_{r}}\lambda_{r}\right)\frac{\log(|x|)}{\log(r)}.

2.4.2.
The Wente part

Since
K_{g}
and
\lambda_{r}
are smooth, see
Remark

2.6
, we have
\varphi_{r}\in C^{\infty}
by standard elliptic regularity applied to (
20
). However,
K_{g}
is not
C^{\infty}
up to the origin, so the regularity breaks down in the limit
r\to 0
. Thanks to the assumption (
1
), we have the following uniform bound:

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}\setminus\mathbb{D}_{r}}|K_{g}e^{-2\lambda_{r}}|dx=\int_{\mathbb{D}\setminus\mathbb{D}_{r}}|K_{g}|d\mathrm{vol}_{g}\leq\int_{\mathbb{D}}|K_{g}|d\mathrm{vol}_{g}.

Hence, the equation (
20
) provides a uniform bound:

(22)

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{r}\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{D}\setminus\mathbb{D}_{r})}+\|\nabla\varphi_{r}\|_{L^{(2,\infty)}(\mathbb{D}\setminus\mathbb{D}_{r})}\leq C\int_{\mathbb{D}}|K_{g}|d\mathrm{vol}_{g}.

Indeed, an explicit formula for the Green kernel of the Laplacian on an annuli has been obtained in
[
37
]
, see also
[
33
, Proposition 2.1]
. One can check by direct computations that we have a
\mathrm{BMO}
bound on the Green kernel and a
L^{(2,\infty)}
-bound on its gradient which are independant of the conformal type of the annuli. In particular, there exists a radii
\tilde{r}\in[r,2r]
such that

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\mathbb{D}_{\tilde{r}}}|\nabla\varphi_{r}|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{r}\int_{\mathbb{D}_{2r}\setminus\mathbb{D}_{r}}|\nabla\varphi_{r}|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{r}\|1\|_{L^{(2,1)}(\mathbb{D}_{2r}\setminus\mathbb{D}_{r})}\|\nabla\varphi_{r}\|_{L^{(2,\infty)}(\mathbb{D}_{2r}\setminus\mathbb{D}_{r})}

(23)

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla\varphi_{r}\|_{L^{(2,\infty)}(\mathbb{D}_{2r}\setminus\mathbb{D}_{r})}\leq C\int_{\mathbb{D}}|K_{g}|d\mathrm{vol}_{g}.

2.4.3.
Convergence
r\to 0

Coming back to (
18
), we obtain

\displaystyle\lambda_{r}=\alpha_{r}\log\left(|x|\right)+\varphi_{r},

together with

\displaystyle\partial_{\rho}\lambda_{r}=\frac{\alpha_{r}}{\rho}+\partial_{\rho}\varphi_{r}.

From (
22
), we deduce that, up to a subsequence,
(\varphi_{r})_{r>0}
converges weakly in
W^{1,(2,\infty)}_{loc}(\mathbb{D}\setminus\{0\})
and in
\mathrm{BMO}_{loc}(\mathbb{D}\setminus\{0\})
, to some limit
\varphi_{0}
. In particular,
e^{-\varphi_{r}}
converges almost everywhere to
e^{-\varphi_{0}}
. Again, thanks to
Remark

2.6
, the map
\varphi_{0}
is
C^{\infty}
on
\mathbb{D}\setminus\{0\}
.

Claim 2.7
.

Up to a subsequence,
\alpha_{r}
converges to some
\alpha_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
as
r\to 0
.

Proof.

We apply Gauss–Bonnet formula on
\mathbb{D}\setminus\mathbb{D}_{\tilde{r}}
, where
\tilde{r}\in[r,2]
is given by (
2.4.2
) :

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}\setminus\mathbb{D}_{\tilde{r}}}K_{g}d\mathrm{vol}_{g}+\int_{\partial(\mathbb{D}\setminus\mathbb{D}_{\tilde{r}})}k_{g}d\mathrm{vol}_{g}=2\pi\chi(\mathbb{D}\setminus\mathbb{D}_{\tilde{r}}).

On
\mathbb{D}_{\tilde{r}}
, the geodesic curvature
k_{g}
is given by
k_{g}=e^{\lambda_{r}}(k_{\xi}-\partial_{\rho}\lambda_{r})
. Thus, it holds

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}\setminus\mathbb{D}_{\tilde{r}}}K_{g}d\mathrm{vol}_{g}+\int_{\partial\mathbb{D}}k_{g}d\mathrm{vol}_{g}-2\pi\underbrace{\chi(\mathbb{D}\setminus\mathbb{D}_{\tilde{r}})}_{=0}

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial\mathbb{D}_{\tilde{r}}}k_{\xi}-\partial_{\rho}\lambda_{r}\ dx

\displaystyle=2\pi-2\pi\alpha_{r}\frac{\tilde{r}}{r}-\int_{\partial\mathbb{D}_{\tilde{r}}}\partial_{\rho}\varphi_{r}\ dx.

Up to a subsequence, we can assume that
\frac{\tilde{r}}{r}\in[1,2]
converges as
r\to 0
. From (
2.4.2
), up to a subsequence, the integrals
\int_{\partial\mathbb{D}_{\tilde{r}}}\partial_{\rho}\varphi_{r}\ dx
converge as
r\to 0
. Hence, the above formula shows that, up to a subsequence,
\alpha_{r}
converges as
r\to 0
.
∎

Claim 2.8
.

Up to a subsequence, the diffeomorphisms
(\psi_{r})_{r>0}
converge to a Lipschitzian diffeomorphism
\psi_{0}
as
r\to 0
.

Proof.

Thanks to (
15
), the diffeomorphisms
(\psi_{r})_{r>0}
have uniformly bounded Lipschitz norm, since we have
|\partial_{x}\psi_{r}|=|\partial_{y}\psi_{r}|=1
for any
r>0
. Hence, up to extraction, they converge to some
\psi_{0}\in W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{D}\setminus\{0\};\mathbb{D}\setminus\{0\})
. Furthermore, it holds:

(24)

\displaystyle\forall\eta\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{D}\setminus\{0\};\mathbb{R}),\quad-\int_{\mathbb{D}\setminus\mathbb{D}_{r}}\psi_{r}d\eta=\int_{\mathbb{D}\setminus\mathbb{D}_{r}}(F_{1,r}\circ\psi_{r},F_{2,r}\circ\psi_{r})\eta.

The maps
\left(x\mapsto(F_{1,r}\circ\psi_{r}(x),F_{2,r}\circ\psi_{r}(x))\right)
, for
r>0
, are bounded in
L^{p}_{loc}(\mathbb{D}\setminus\{0\};\mathrm{SO}\left(2,\mathbb{R}\right))
, for any
p\in[1,\infty]
. Hence, they converge almost everywhere to some map
M:\mathbb{D}\setminus\{0\}\to\mathrm{SO}\left(2,\mathbb{R}\right)
. Hence, we can pass to the limit in (
24
) to obtain

\displaystyle\forall\eta\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{D}\setminus\{0\};\mathbb{R}),\quad-\int_{\mathbb{D}\setminus\{0\}}\psi_{0}d\eta=\int_{\mathbb{D}\setminus{0}}M\eta.

Hence, we have
d\psi_{0}=M\in\mathrm{SO}\left(2,\mathbb{R}\right)
, so
\psi_{0}
is also locally invertible. In the same manner, we can pass to the limit in the system

\displaystyle d(\psi_{r})^{-1}=(d\psi_{r})^{-1}=\underbrace{(F_{1,r},F_{2,r})^{-1}}_{\in\mathrm{SO}\left(2,\mathbb{R}\right)}\circ\psi_{r}^{-1}.

Up to a subsequence,
(\psi_{r}^{-1})_{r>0}
converges to some
\Psi_{0}\in W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{D}\setminus\{0\};\mathbb{D}\setminus\{0\})
as
r\to 0
. The map
\psi_{0}
and
\Psi_{0}
satisfy
\psi_{0}\circ\Psi_{0}=\mathrm{id}_{\mathbb{D}\setminus\{0\}}
. Hence,
\psi_{0}
is a diffeomorphism.
∎

Thus, we can pass to the limit
r\to 0
in the equality
(\psi_{r})^{*}g=|x|^{-2\alpha_{r}}e^{-2\varphi_{r}}\xi
. We obtain
(\psi_{0})^{*}g=|x|^{-2\alpha_{0}}e^{-2\varphi_{0}}\xi
.

2.5.
Liouville equation up to the singularity

The goal of this section is to prove the following lemma, which can be understood as a singularity removability result.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
\varphi\in\mathrm{BMO}\cap W^{1,(2,\infty)}(\mathbb{D})
such that
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{D}\setminus\{0\})
. Assume that the metric
g=|z|^{\alpha}e^{2\varphi}|dz|^{2}
on
\mathbb{D}\setminus\{0\}
satisfies

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}}|K_{g}|\sqrt{\det g}<+\infty.

Let
k_{g}
be the geodesic curvature of
\partial\mathbb{D}
in
(\mathbb{D}\setminus\{0\},g)
. Assume that the exponent
\alpha
is given by

\displaystyle\alpha=\frac{1}{2\pi}\left(\int_{\mathbb{D}}K_{g}d\mathrm{vol}_{g}+\int_{\partial\mathbb{D}}k_{g}d\mathrm{vol}_{g}\right)-1.

Then,
\varphi
satisfies the following equation:

\displaystyle\forall\psi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{D}),\quad\int_{\mathbb{D}}\nabla\varphi\cdot\nabla\psi=\int_{\mathbb{D}}K_{g}e^{2\lambda}\psi.

Proof.

Let
r\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
. Thanks to the Gauss–Bonnet formula on the annuli
\mathbb{D}\setminus\mathbb{D}_{r}
for the metric
g
, it holds:

\displaystyle 0=\chi(\mathbb{D}\setminus\mathbb{D}_{r})

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{D}\setminus\mathbb{D}_{r}}K_{g}d\mathrm{vol}_{g}+\int_{\partial(\mathbb{D}\setminus\mathbb{D}_{r})}k_{g}d\mathrm{vol}_{g}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{D}\setminus\mathbb{D}_{r}}K_{g}d\mathrm{vol}_{g}+\int_{\partial\mathbb{D}}k_{g}d\mathrm{vol}_{g}-2\pi-\int_{\partial\mathbb{D}_{r}}\partial_{\rho}\left(\alpha\log(\rho)+2\varphi\right).

Hence, it holds

(25)

\displaystyle 2\int_{\partial\mathbb{D}_{r}}\partial_{\rho}\varphi=\int_{\mathbb{D}\setminus\mathbb{D}_{r}}K_{g}d\mathrm{vol}_{g}+\int_{\partial\mathbb{D}}k_{g}d\mathrm{vol}_{g}-2\pi-2\pi\alpha\xrightarrow[r\to 0]{}{0}.

On the other hand, for any
r\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
, there exists
\tilde{r}\in[r,2r]
such that

(26)

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\mathbb{D}_{\tilde{r}}}|\nabla\varphi|\leq C\|\nabla\varphi\|_{L^{(2,\infty)}(\mathbb{D}_{2r}\setminus\mathbb{D}_{r})}.

Let
\psi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{D})
and
\eta_{r}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2};[0,1])
be such that
\eta_{r}=1
on
\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\mathbb{D}_{\tilde{r}}
,
\eta_{r}=0
on
\mathbb{D}_{\tilde{r}/2}
and
|\nabla\eta_{r}|\leq C\tilde{r}^{-1}
. The Liouville equation
-\Delta_{\xi}\varphi=K_{g}\sqrt{\det g}
is satisfied on
\mathbb{D}\setminus\{0\}
since
g
is smooth on
\mathbb{D}\setminus\{0\}
. Hence, it holds, for any
r\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
:

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}}\nabla\varphi\cdot\nabla(\psi\eta_{r})=\int_{\mathbb{D}}K_{g}\sqrt{\det g}\psi\eta_{r}.

We obtain

(27)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}}\eta_{r}\left(\nabla\varphi\cdot\nabla\psi-K_{g}\sqrt{\det g}\psi\right)=\int_{\mathbb{D}}\nabla\varphi\cdot(\nabla\eta_{r})\psi.

The left-hand side converges by dominated convergence:

(28)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}}\eta_{r}\left(\nabla\varphi\cdot\nabla\psi-K_{g}\sqrt{\det g}\psi\right)\xrightarrow[r\to 0]{}{\int_{\mathbb{D}}\left(\nabla\varphi\cdot\nabla\psi-K_{g}\sqrt{\det g}\psi\right)}.

It remains to show that the right-hand side of (
27
) converges to
0
. Since
\mathrm{supp}(\nabla\eta_{r})\subset\mathbb{D}_{\tilde{r}}\setminus\mathbb{D}_{\tilde{r}/2}
, it holds

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}}\nabla\varphi\cdot(\nabla\eta_{r})\psi

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{D}_{\tilde{r}}\setminus\mathbb{D}_{\tilde{r}/2}}\nabla\varphi\cdot(\nabla\eta_{r})\psi

(29)

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial\left(\mathbb{D}_{\tilde{r}}\setminus\mathbb{D}_{\tilde{r}/2}\right)}(\partial_{\nu}\varphi)\eta_{r}\psi-\int_{\mathbb{D}_{\tilde{r}}\setminus\mathbb{D}_{\tilde{r}/2}}\eta_{r}\left(\psi K_{g}\sqrt{\det g}+\nabla\varphi\cdot\nabla\psi\right).

The second term converges to
0
as
r\to 0
by dominated convergence. The first term converges to
0
thanks to (
25
) and (
26
). Indeed, it holds

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\left(\mathbb{D}_{\tilde{r}}\setminus\mathbb{D}_{\tilde{r}/2}\right)}(\partial_{\nu}\varphi)\eta_{r}\psi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\mathbb{D}_{\tilde{r}}}(\partial_{\nu}\varphi)\psi

\displaystyle\underset{r\to 0}{=}

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\mathbb{D}_{\tilde{r}}}(\partial_{\nu}\varphi)\left(\psi(0)+O(\tilde{r})\right)

\displaystyle\underset{r\to 0}{=}

\displaystyle O(\tilde{r}).

Coming back to Liouville equation (
27
) together with (
28
)-(
29
), we deduce that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}}\nabla\varphi\cdot\nabla\psi=\int_{\mathbb{D}}\psi K_{g}\sqrt{\det g}.

This is valid for any
\psi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{D})
. Hence, we obtain our result.
∎

Under the additional assumption that
\varphi
is continuous at the origin, we obtain that
\alpha
is an integer.

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
\varphi\in C^{0}\cap W^{1,(2,\infty)}(\mathbb{D})
such that
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{D}\setminus\{0\})
. Assume that the metric
g=|z|^{2\alpha}e^{2\varphi}|dz|^{2}
satisfies

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}}|K_{g}|\sqrt{\det g}<+\infty.

Let
k_{g}
be the geodesic curvature of
\partial\mathbb{D}
in
(\mathbb{D}\setminus\{0\},g)
. Assume that the exponent
\alpha
is given by

\displaystyle 2\alpha=\frac{1}{2\pi}\left(\int_{\mathbb{D}}K_{g}d\mathrm{vol}_{g}+\int_{\partial\mathbb{D}}k_{g}d\mathrm{vol}_{g}\right)-1.

Then
\alpha
is an integer.

Proof.

If
r>0
, then we have
g(r\cdot)=r^{2\alpha}|\cdot|^{2\alpha}e^{2\varphi(r\cdot)}|dz|^{2}
. Since
\varphi
is continuous at the origin, the metrics
g_{r}:=r^{-2\alpha}g(r\cdot)
converges to the metric
g_{0}:=|\cdot|^{2\alpha}e^{2\varphi(0)}|dz|^{2}
. The map
f:z\in(\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\},g_{0})\mapsto e^{\varphi(0)}\frac{z^{\alpha+1}}{\alpha+1}\in(\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\},|dz|^{2})
is conformal and orientation-preserving. Since
g_{0}
has the same complex structure as
(\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\},|dz|^{2})
,
f
is a meromorphic map which has no essential singularity at the origin. Thus we have
\alpha+1\in\mathbb{Z}
.
∎

2.6.
The pseudo-sphere

We consider the following immersion

\displaystyle\forall(u,v)\in[0,2\pi]\times(0,\pi),\quad\Phi(u,v)=\begin{pmatrix}\cos(u)\sin(v)\\
\sin(u)\sin(v)\\
\cos(v)+\log\left(\tan(\frac{v}{2})\right)\end{pmatrix}.

In this section, we prove that the induced metric
g
of
\Phi
satisfies
\int_{\mathbb{D}}|K_{g}|d\mathrm{vol}_{g}<\infty
and
g=|z|^{-2}e^{\varphi(z)}|dz|^{2}
, for some function
\varphi\notin L^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
.

Proposition 2.11
.

Let
g
be the induced metric of
\Phi
and
\mathrm{I\!I}
be its second fundamental form. Then, it holds

\displaystyle\int_{[0,2\pi]\times(0,\pi)}|K_{g}|d\mathrm{vol}_{g}<+\infty,

\displaystyle\int_{[0,2\pi]\times(0,\pi)}|\mathrm{I\!I}|^{2}_{g}d\mathrm{vol}_{g}=+\infty,

\displaystyle g=\sin(v)^{2}(du)^{2}+\frac{1}{\tan(v)^{2}}(dv)^{2}.

Proof.

We compute the first derivatives of
\Phi
. On one hand, it holds

\displaystyle\partial_{u}\Phi=\begin{pmatrix}-\sin(u)\sin(v)\\
\cos(u)\sin(v)\\
0\end{pmatrix}.

On the other hand, it holds

\displaystyle\partial_{v}\Phi=\begin{pmatrix}\cos(u)\cos(v)\\
\sin(u)\cos(v)\\
-\sin(v)+\frac{1}{2\tan(v/2)}(1+\tan(v/2)^{2})\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\cos(u)\cos(v)\\
\sin(u)\cos(v)\\
-\sin(v)+\frac{1}{\sin(v)}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\cos(u)\cos(v)\\
\sin(u)\cos(v)\\
\frac{\cos(v)^{2}}{\sin(v)}\end{pmatrix}.

Hence, the immersion
\Phi
satisfies the following relations:

\displaystyle\left\langle\partial_{u}\Phi,\partial_{v}\Phi\right\rangle_{\xi}=0,\ \ \ |\partial_{u}\Phi|^{2}=\sin(v)^{2},\ \ \ |\partial_{v}\Phi|^{2}=\cos(v)^{2}+\frac{\cos(v)^{4}}{\sin(v)^{2}}=\cos(v)^{2}\left(1+\frac{1}{\tan(v)^{2}}\right)=\frac{1}{\tan(v)^{2}}.

Hence, the induced metric is

\displaystyle g=\sin(v)^{2}(du)^{2}+\frac{1}{\tan(v)^{2}}(dv)^{2}.

Hence, the volume element is given by

\displaystyle\sqrt{\det g}=\frac{\sin(v)}{|\tan(v)|}=\cos(v)^{2}\sqrt{1+\tan(v)^{2}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+\tan(v)^{2}}}.

The area is finite:

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}=\int_{[0,2\pi]\times(0,\pi)}\sqrt{\det g}\,dudv=2\pi\int_{0}^{\pi}\frac{dv}{\sqrt{1+\tan(v)^{2}}}\leq 2\pi^{2}.

The Gaussian curvature is given by

\displaystyle K

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\det g}}\partial_{v}\left(\frac{\partial_{v}(\sin(v)^{2})}{\sqrt{\det g}}\right)

\displaystyle=-\sqrt{1+\tan(v)^{2}}\,\partial_{v}\left(\frac{\sin(v)\cos(v)}{\cos(v)^{2}\sqrt{1+\tan(v)^{2}}}\right)

\displaystyle=-\sqrt{1+\tan(v)^{2}}\,\partial_{v}\left(\frac{\tan(v)}{\sqrt{1+\tan(v)^{2}}}\right)

\displaystyle=-\sqrt{1+\tan(v)^{2}}\,\left(\sqrt{1+\tan(v)^{2}}-\tan(v)^{2}(\partial_{v}\tan(v))(1+\tan(v)^{2})^{-\frac{3}{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=-\sqrt{1+\tan(v)^{2}}\,\left(\sqrt{1+\tan(v)^{2}}-\tan(v)^{2}(1+\tan(v)^{2})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=-\sqrt{1+\tan(v)^{2}}\,\left(1+\tan(v)^{2}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=-1.

Hence, the total Gaussian curvature is
\int|K|=\mathcal{A}<\infty
. We define the following orthonormal basis

\displaystyle e_{u}:=\frac{\partial_{u}\Phi}{|\partial_{u}\Phi|}=\begin{pmatrix}-\sin(u)\\
\cos(u)\\
0\end{pmatrix},\ \ \ e_{v}:=\frac{\partial_{v}\Phi}{|\partial_{v}\Phi|}=\mathrm{sign}(\cos(v))\begin{pmatrix}\cos(u)\sin(v)\\
\sin(u)\sin(v)\\
\cos(v)\end{pmatrix}.

The Gauss map is given by

\displaystyle\vec{n}=e_{u}\times e_{v}=\mathrm{sign}(\cos(v))\begin{pmatrix}\cos(u)\cos(v)\\
\sin(u)\cos(v)\\
-\sin(v)\end{pmatrix}.

We compute the second fundamental form
\mathrm{I\!I}
. It holds

\displaystyle\nabla_{e_{u}}\vec{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sin(v)}\partial_{u}\vec{n}=\mathrm{sign}(\cos(v))\begin{pmatrix}-\sin(u)\cot(v)\\
\cos(u)\cot(v)\\
-1\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle\nabla_{e_{v}}\vec{n}

\displaystyle=|\tan(v)|\partial_{v}\vec{n}=\tan(v)\begin{pmatrix}-\cos(u)\sin(v)\\
-\sin(u)\sin(v)\\
-\cos(v)\end{pmatrix}.

Hence, we obtain

\displaystyle|\mathrm{I\!I}|^{2}_{g}

\displaystyle=|\nabla_{e_{u}}\vec{n}|^{2}_{g}+|\nabla_{e_{v}}\vec{n}|^{2}_{g}

\displaystyle=|\left\langle\nabla_{e_{u}}\vec{n},e_{u}\right\rangle_{g}|^{2}+2|\left\langle\nabla_{e_{u}}\vec{n},e_{v}\right\rangle_{g}|^{2}+|\left\langle\nabla_{e_{v}}\vec{n},e_{v}\right\rangle_{g}|^{2}

\displaystyle=\cot(v)^{2}+1+2\cos(v)^{2}+\tan(v)^{2}.

Thus, we deduce that

\displaystyle\int|\mathrm{I\!I}|^{2}_{g}d\mathrm{vol}_{g}

\displaystyle=\int_{[0,2\pi]\times(0,\pi)}\left(\cot(v)^{2}+1+2\cos(v)^{2}+\tan(v)^{2}\right)\frac{dudv}{\sqrt{1+\tan(v)^{2}}}

\displaystyle\geq 2\pi\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\cot(v)^{2}}{\sqrt{1+\tan(v)^{2}}}\,dv

\displaystyle\geq c\int_{0}^{1}\frac{dv}{\sin(v)^{2}}=\infty.

∎

We now study the conformal parametrization of
g
. In order to compare with
Theorem

2.1
, we need a parametrization on a disk.

Lemma 2.12
.

There exists complex coordinates
z\in\mathbb{D}(0,e^{-1})
such that

\displaystyle g=\frac{|dz|^{2}}{|z|^{2}(\log|z|)^{2}}.

With the notations of
Theorem

2.1
, we have
\alpha=-1
and
\varphi(z)=-\log|\log(|z|)|\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb{D})\setminus L^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
.

Proof.

It holds

\displaystyle g=\sin(v)^{2}(du)^{2}+\frac{1}{\tan(v)^{2}}(dv)^{2}=\sin(v)^{2}\left((du)^{2}+\frac{1}{\sin(v)^{2}\tan(v)^{2}}(dv)^{2}\right).

The function
w(v):=\frac{1}{\sin(v)}
satisfies
dw=-\frac{\cos(v)}{\sin(v)^{2}}dv=-\frac{dv}{\sin(v)\tan(v)}
. Hence, in the coordinates
(u,w)\in[0,2\pi]\times(1,+\infty)
, it holds

\displaystyle g=\frac{1}{w^{2}}\left(du^{2}+dw^{2}\right).

Let
z=e^{-w+iu}\in\mathbb{D}(0,e^{-1})
. Then, it holds

\displaystyle dz=e^{-w+iu}\left(-dw+idu\right).

Hence, we obtain

\displaystyle|dz|^{2}=e^{-2w}\left((dw)^{2}+(du)^{2}\right)=w^{2}e^{-2w}g.

Since
e^{-w}=|z|
, it holds
e^{-2w}=|z|^{2}
and
w^{2}=(\log|z|)^{2}
. Hence, we deduce that

\displaystyle g=\frac{|dz|^{2}}{|z|^{2}(\log|z|)^{2}}.

∎

2.7.
Counterexample to
W^{1,2}
-regularity

Proposition 2.13
.

Consider the following function:

\displaystyle\forall z\in\mathbb{D},\ \ \ \lambda(z)=\sqrt{\log\left(\frac{e}{|z|}\right)}.

Then, the metric
g:=e^{2\lambda(z)}|dz|^{2}
satisfies
\nabla\lambda\notin L^{2}(\mathbb{D})
,
\alpha=0
and
K_{g}\sqrt{\det g}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{D})
.

Proof.

We first compute the radial derivative of
\lambda
: it holds

\displaystyle\partial_{\rho}\lambda

\displaystyle=\frac{-1}{2\rho\sqrt{\log(e/\rho)}},

\displaystyle\partial^{2}_{\rho\rho}\lambda

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\rho^{2}\sqrt{\log(e/\rho)}}+\frac{1}{4\rho^{2}\log(e/\rho)^{3/2}}.

Hence, we check that
\nabla\lambda\notin L^{2}(\mathbb{D})
by direct computation:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}|\partial_{\rho}\lambda|^{2}\rho d\rho=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{d\rho}{\rho\log(e/\rho)}=+\infty.

On the other hand, we have the Liouville equation
-\Delta_{\xi}\lambda=K_{g}\sqrt{\det g}
. The Laplacian of
\lambda
is given by

\displaystyle\partial^{2}_{\rho\rho}\lambda+\frac{1}{\rho}\partial_{\rho}\lambda

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4\rho^{2}\log(e/\rho)^{3/2}}.

Hence, it holds

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}}|K_{g}|d\mathrm{vol}_{g}=2\pi\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\rho d\rho}{4\rho^{2}\log(e/\rho)^{3/2}}=2\pi\int_{0}^{1}\frac{d\rho}{4\rho\log(e/\rho)^{3/2}}<+\infty.

∎

3.
Huber Theorem for 4-dimensional manifolds

3.1.
Main result

Consider
g
an orbifold metric on
C(\mathbb{S}^{3}/\Gamma)\setminus\{0\}
, where
\Gamma
is any finite subgroup of
\mathrm{SO}\left(4,\mathbb{R}\right)
. By definition, we can lift this metric to a smooth metric on
\mathbb{B}\setminus\{0\}
, where
\mathbb{B}
is the unit ball of
\mathbb{R}^{4}
. Then, by working only in the space of functions which are invariant by
\Gamma
(finite group) which does not change the analysis, we can assume that
\Gamma=\{1\}
. Hence, the goal of this section is to prove the following result.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
p\in(1,2]
. Consider a smooth metric
g
on
\mathbb{B}\setminus\{0\}
satisfying the following assumptions:

A
.1

The volume of
\mathbb{B}
is finite:
\mathrm{vol}_{g}(\mathbb{B})<+\infty
.

A
.2

The volume measure is doubling: there exists
\gamma_{D}>0
such that for all balls
B_{g}(x,2r)\subset\mathbb{B}
, it holds
\mathrm{vol}_{g}(B_{g}(x,2r))\leq\gamma_{D}\mathrm{vol}_{g}(B_{g}(x,r))
.

A
.3

We have
\mathrm{Riem}^{g}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{B},g)
and
B^{g}\in L^{p}(\mathbb{B},g)
.

A
.4

There exists a constant
\gamma_{S}>0
such that the following Sobolev inequality holds:

\displaystyle\forall u\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{B}\setminus\{0\}),\quad\|u\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{B})}^{2}\leq\gamma_{S}\int_{\mathbb{B}}|\nabla u|^{2}_{g}+|u|^{2}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g}.

A
.5

There exists two constants
\gamma_{A}>0
and
\Lambda_{A}>0
such that the following Adams–Moser–Trudinger inequality is satisfied: for all ball
B_{g}(x,r)\subset\mathbb{B}\setminus\{0\}
,

\displaystyle\forall u\in C^{\infty}(B_{g}(x,r)),\quad\int_{B_{g}(x,r)}u\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g}=0\Rightarrow\int_{B_{g}(x,r)}e^{\gamma_{A}\frac{|u|}{\|\nabla u\|_{L^{4}(B_{g}(x,r))}}}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g}\leq\Lambda_{A}.

A
.6

There exists a constant
\gamma_{L}>0
such that the following regularity estimate holds:

\displaystyle\forall u\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{B}\setminus\{0\}),\quad\|\nabla u\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{B},g)}\leq\gamma_{L}\|\Delta_{g}u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{B},g)}.

A
.7

There exists a constant
\gamma_{H}>0
satisfying the following property. For all function
u\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{B}\setminus\{0\})
, we have

\displaystyle\|\mathrm{Hess}_{g}u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{B}\setminus\{0\},g)}\leq\gamma_{H}\left(\|\Delta_{g}u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{B}\setminus\{0\},g)}+\|u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{B}\setminus\{0\},g)}\right).

There exists a constant
\varepsilon>0
depending only on
\gamma_{S}
and
\gamma_{L}
such that the following holds. Assume that

\displaystyle\|\mathrm{Riem}^{g}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{B},g)}+\mathrm{vol}_{g}(\mathbb{B})\leq\varepsilon.

Then there exist a diffeomorphism
\psi:\mathbb{B}\setminus\{0\}\to\mathbb{B}\setminus\{0\}
, a conformal factor
u\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{B}\setminus\{0\})
, and a metric
h\in W^{2,2p}(\mathbb{B},\xi)\cap C^{\infty}(\mathbb{B}\setminus\{0\})
such that

\displaystyle\psi^{*}g=e^{2u}h.

Before proving the above result, we discuss all the assumptions of analytic nature. We will need to use Sobolev inequalities and elliptic regularity estimates for the metric
g
. To obtain uniform estimates, we need to assume that the constants are valid up to the origin. This is not true in full generality, but they are in most of the cases of interests for us. In the above statement, we clarified all the constants involved in the proof. However, some assumptions are linked. We discuss this below and simplified them in the statement of
Theorem

1.8
.

The isoperimetric inequality implies all the Sobolev embeddings.

The isoperimetric inequality is linked to Sobolev embeddings, see for instance
[
12
]
. From
[
19
, Chapter IV, Section 3, Theorem 4]
, we know that

\displaystyle\Lambda^{4}:=\inf_{f\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{B}\setminus\{0\})\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\|\nabla f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{B},g)}^{4}}{\|f\|_{L^{\frac{4}{3}}(\mathbb{B},g)}^{3}}=\inf_{\Omega\subset\mathbb{B}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\mathrm{vol}(\partial\Omega)^{4}}{\mathrm{vol}(\Omega)^{3}}.

Hence, an isoperimetric inequality involves the following Sobolev inequality: for any
f\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{B}\setminus\{0\})\setminus\{0\}
, it holds

(30)

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{\frac{4}{3}}(\mathbb{B},g)}\leq\Lambda\|\nabla f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{B},g)}.

By choosing
f=u^{3}
for some
u\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{B}\setminus\{0\})
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{B}}u^{4}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g}\right)^{\frac{3}{4}}=\|u^{3}\|_{L^{\frac{4}{3}}(\mathbb{B},g)}

\displaystyle\leq 3\Lambda\int|u|^{2}|\nabla u|\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g}\leq 3\Lambda\|u\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{B},g)}^{2}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{B},g)}.

We deduce the following Sobolev inequality:

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{B},g)}\leq 3\Lambda\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{B},g)}.

We now make another choice in (
30
). Namely,we chose
f=e^{\gamma|u|}-1
for some
\gamma>0
small to be chosen later, and
u\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{B}\setminus\{0\})
such that
\|\nabla u\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{B},g)}=1
, then it holds

\displaystyle\left\|e^{\frac{4\gamma}{3}|u|}\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{B},g)}^{\frac{3}{4}}\leq\left\|e^{\gamma|u|}-1\right\|_{L^{\frac{4}{3}(\mathbb{B},g)}}+\mathrm{vol}_{g}(\mathbb{B})^{\frac{3}{4}}\leq\Lambda\gamma\int_{\mathbb{B}}e^{\gamma|u|}|\nabla u|\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g}+\mathrm{vol}_{g}(\mathbb{B})^{\frac{3}{4}}.

By Hölder inequality and using the assumption
\|\nabla u\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{B},g)}=1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|e^{\frac{4\gamma}{3}|u|}\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{B},g)}^{\frac{3}{4}}\leq\Lambda\gamma\left\|e^{\frac{4\gamma}{3}|u|}\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{B},g)}^{\frac{3}{4}}+\mathrm{vol}_{g}(\mathbb{B})^{\frac{3}{4}}.

Chosing now
\gamma=(2\Lambda)^{-1}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|e^{\frac{4\gamma}{3}|u|}\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{B},g)}^{\frac{3}{4}}\leq 2\mathrm{vol}_{g}(\mathbb{B})^{\frac{3}{4}}.

Now, given any
u\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{B}\setminus\{0\})
, we deduce that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{B}}e^{\frac{2|u-(u)_{\mathbb{B}}|}{3\Lambda\|\nabla u\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{B},g)}}}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g}\leq 2^{\frac{4}{3}}\mathrm{vol}_{g}(\mathbb{B}).

By changing the support of
u
, we can exchange the ball
\mathbb{B}
above by any other ball.

Sobolev inequalities for hypersurfaces.

For the applications to generalized Willmore hypersurfaces, we can rely on Sobolev inequalities on hypersurfaces of
\mathbb{R}^{5}

\displaystyle\forall\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Sigma),\quad\left(\int_{\Sigma}|\varphi|^{\frac{4p}{4-p}}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{\Sigma}\right)^{\frac{4-p}{4}}\leq C(p)\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla^{\Sigma}\varphi|^{p}+|H\varphi|^{p}\ d\mathrm{vol}_{\Sigma}.

By Hölder inequality and assuming that

\displaystyle\forall\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega),\quad\|\varphi\|_{L^{\frac{4p}{4-p}}(\Omega)}\leq 2C(p)\|\nabla^{\Sigma}\varphi\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}.

Elliptic regularity estimates.

Elliptic regularity on manifolds has been discussed recently in

3.2.
Strategy

Consider a metric

is Yang–Mills if and only if
B^{g}=0
. Furthermore, it was proved in
[
59
, Theorem 1.1]
or
[
54
, Theorem VI.9]
, that point singularities are removable for Yang–Mills connection. Hence, one could hope to get a similar result for Bach-flat manifolds. To do so, we first need to identify the Coulomb condition. For Yang–Mills connections, this condition can be understood as finding a critical point of the
L^{2}
-norm of the connection. Thanks to
[
30
, Equation (15)]
and the Chern–Gauss–Bonnet formula (
35
), we are reduced to study the following energy:

\displaystyle\forall u\in W^{2,2}(\mathbb{B},g),\quad E(u)=\int_{\mathbb{B}}\frac{1}{2}(J^{g_{u}})^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g_{u}},

where
g_{u}=e^{2u}g
. We prove in
Lemma

3.5
that the Euler–Lagrange equation of
E
is
\Delta_{g_{u}}J^{g_{u}}=1
. In particular, we obtained that
g_{u}
is smooth as soon as
g
is smooth in
Lemma

3.6
.

Remark 3.2
.

•

The main term
\int(J^{g_{u}})^{2}
is positive and minimized when
J^{g_{u}}=0
. Marques
[
47
, Theorem 1.2]
proved that we can solve
\bar{J}=0
under the condition that the first eigenvalue of the conformal Laplacian has to be positive, see for instance
[
25
]
. Without this condition, it is unclear that we can solve
J^{g_{u}}=0
with estimates on
u
.

•

If
\bar{J}=0
and
\bar{B}=0
, then the
\varepsilon
-regularity has been proved in
[
60
,
13
]
. We need to bound the constant in Sobolev inequality, see for instance
[
24
,
1
]
, or an estimate on the growth of balls, see for instance
[
61
]
.

However, as in the 2-dimensional case, solving this problem directly on
\mathbb{B}
is not obvious. Instead, we first solve it in
\mathbb{B}\setminus\mathbb{B}_{r}
, that is to say, we minimize the following functional:

\displaystyle\forall u\in W^{2,2}_{0}(\mathbb{B}\setminus\mathbb{B}_{r},g),\quad E_{r}(u)=\int_{\mathbb{B}\setminus\mathbb{B}_{r}}\frac{1}{2}(J^{g_{u}})^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g_{u}}.

Then we pass to the limit
r\to 0
. To do so, we need some preliminary estimates. We prove an
\varepsilon
-regularity in the spirit of
[
60
,
61
]
.
A priori
, we have a system of the form
\Delta u=fu+g
. If
f
and
g
are regular, then
u
is regular as well. We then show that the limiting metric
g_{0}
is continuous across the singularity by proceeding to a blow-up at the origin, see
[
4
,
5
]
. In a neighbourhood of the origin,
(\mathbb{B}\setminus\{0\},\bar{g})
will be diffeomorphic to a cone
C(\mathbb{S}^{3}/\Gamma)
where
\Gamma
is a finite subgroup of
\mathrm{SO}\left(4,\mathbb{R}\right)
. Since
\mathbb{B}
is simply connected, we obtain that
\Gamma
is trivial. We can extend continuously
g_{0}
to the origin by the flat metric, see
[
7
,
58
]
.

3.3.
Definition of the Schouten tensor and relations

In this section, we define the Schouten tensor and provide a few remarks on the Bach tensor.

Let
g
be a smooth metric on
\mathcal{M}=\mathbb{B}^{4}\setminus\{0\}
. The Schouten tensor of
g
is given by

\displaystyle\mathrm{Sch}_{ab}^{g}:=\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathrm{Ric}^{g}_{ab}-\frac{\mathrm{Scal}^{g}}{6}g_{ab}\right),

\displaystyle J^{g}:=\mathrm{tr}_{g}\mathrm{Sch}^{g}=\frac{1}{6}\mathrm{Scal}^{g}.

If
g_{u}:=e^{2u}g
and
\Delta_{g}=(\nabla^{g})^{a}(\nabla^{g})_{a}
is the Laplace–Beltrami operator, then it holds, see for instance
[
9
, Theorem 1.159]
:

(32)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Sch}^{g_{u}}_{ab}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Sch}^{g}_{ab}-\nabla_{a}^{g}\nabla^{g}_{b}u+(\nabla_{a}^{g}u)(\nabla_{b}^{g}u)-\frac{|du|^{2}_{g}}{2}g_{ab},

(33)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Scal}^{g_{u}}

\displaystyle=e^{-2u}\Big(\mathrm{Scal}^{g}-6\Delta_{g}u-6|du|^{2}_{g}\Big).

Using the Bach tensor, we now deduce the equation relating
\Delta\mathrm{Sch}
and
\mathrm{Hess}(J)
obtained in
[
60
, Section 2.1]
. This is the fundamental system used to obtain
\varepsilon
-regularity. For any metric
g
, it holds:

(34)

\displaystyle\Delta_{g}\mathrm{Sch}^{g}-\mathrm{Hess}_{g}(J^{g})=B^{g}+\mathrm{Riem}\star\mathrm{Ric},

where
B^{g}
is the Bach tensor. Thanks to the Chern–Gauss–Bonnet formula, the Euler–Lagrange equation of
\int|\mathrm{Sch}|^{2}
is the same as the Euler–Lagrange equation of
\int J^{2}
when we restrict ourselves to a conformal class:

(35)

\displaystyle 32\pi^{2}\chi(\mathbb{B}\setminus\mathbb{B}_{r})=\int_{\mathbb{B}\setminus\mathbb{B}_{r}}|W^{g}|^{2}_{g}+8((J^{g})^{2}-|\mathrm{Sch}^{g}|^{2}_{g})\ d\mathrm{vol}_{g}

\displaystyle+8\int_{\partial(\mathbb{B}\setminus\mathbb{B}_{r})}\left(\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{Scal}^{g}H-\mathrm{Ric}^{g}(\nu,\nu)H-\mathrm{Riem}^{\gamma}_{\ \alpha\beta\gamma}\mathrm{I\!I}^{\alpha\beta}+\frac{1}{3}H^{3}-H|\mathrm{I\!I}|^{2}_{g}+\frac{2}{3}\mathrm{tr}_{g_{|\partial\mathbb{B}_{r}}}(\mathrm{I\!I}^{3})\right)\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g}.

3.4.
The energy functional

In this section, we consider a smooth metric
g
on
\mathbb{B}\setminus\{0\}
satisfying the assumptions of
Theorem

3.1
. We denote
\Omega_{r}:=\mathbb{B}^{4}\setminus\mathbb{B}_{r}^{4}
. If
u\in C^{\infty}(\Omega_{r})
, we define
g_{u}:=e^{2u}g
. Given
r>0
, we define

(36)

\displaystyle\forall u\in C^{\infty}(\Omega_{r}),\quad E_{r}(u):=\int_{\Omega_{r}}\frac{1}{2}(J^{u})^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g_{u}}.

Lemma 3.3
.

Up to replace
\mathbb{B}
with
\mathbb{B}_{r_{0}}
for some
r_{0}>0
small enough, one can assume that

(37)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{B}}\frac{1}{2}(J^{g})^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g}<\frac{1}{4^{6}\,\gamma_{L}^{4}}.

Consider
(u_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega_{r})
a minimizing sequence for the following optimization problem:

\displaystyle\inf\left\{E_{r}(w):w\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega_{r}),\ \|dw\|_{L^{4}(\Omega_{r})}<\frac{1}{4\,\gamma_{L}}\right\}=\inf\left\{E_{r}(w):w\in W^{2,2}_{0}(\Omega_{r},g),\ \|dw\|_{L^{4}(\Omega_{r})}<\frac{1}{4\,\gamma_{L}}\right\}.

Then
(u_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
weakly converges in
W^{2,2}_{0}
, up to a subsequence, to some
u_{r}\in W^{2,2}_{0}(\Omega_{r},g)
such that

\displaystyle E_{r}(u_{r})=\inf\left\{E_{r}(w):w\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega_{r}),\ \|dw\|_{L^{4}(\Omega_{r})}<\frac{1}{4\,\gamma_{L}}\right\}.

In particular,
u_{r}
is a critical point of
E_{r}
in
W^{2,2}_{0}(\Omega_{r},g)
satisfying the following estimates:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\|du_{r}\|_{L^{4}(\Omega_{r},g)}<\frac{8}{3}\gamma_{L}E_{r}(0)^{\frac{1}{2}},\\
&\|\Delta_{g}u_{r}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{r},g)}\leq\|du_{r}\|_{L^{4}(\Omega_{r},g)}^{2}+2E_{r}(0)^{\frac{1}{2}},\\
&\int_{\Omega_{r}}e^{4v}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g}\leq E_{r}(0).\end{aligned}\right.

Proof.

From(
33
), it holds

\displaystyle\forall k\in\mathbb{N},\quad E_{r}(u_{k})=\int_{\Omega_{r}}\frac{1}{2}(J^{g}-\Delta_{g}u_{k}-|du_{k}|^{2}_{g})^{2}+\frac{e^{4u_{k}}}{4}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g}\leq E_{r}(0).

Since the exponential term is positive, we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|J^{g}-\Delta_{g}u_{k}-|du_{k}|^{2}_{g}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{r},g)}\leq\left(2\,E_{r}(0)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Using the triangle inequality, we deduce

\displaystyle\|\Delta_{g}u_{k}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{r},g)}

\displaystyle\leq\|J^{g}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{r},g)}+\|du_{k}\|_{L^{4}(\Omega_{r},g)}^{2}+\left(2\,E_{r}(0)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\|du_{k}\|_{L^{4}(\Omega_{r},g)}^{2}+2^{\frac{3}{2}}E_{r}(0)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Since
\|du_{k}\|_{L^{4}(\Omega_{r},g)}<(4\gamma_{L})^{-1}
, we obtain

(38)

\displaystyle\|\Delta_{g}u_{k}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{r},g)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4\,\gamma_{L}}\|du_{k}\|_{L^{4}(\Omega_{r},g)}+4E_{r}(0)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Thanks to

A

.6
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|du_{k}\|_{L^{4}(\Omega_{r},g)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4}\|du_{k}\|_{L^{4}(\Omega_{r},g)}+4\,\gamma_{L}\,E_{r}(0)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Hence, it holds

\displaystyle\|du_{k}\|_{L^{4}(\Omega_{r},g)}\leq\frac{16}{3}\gamma_{L}\,E_{r}(0)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Thanks to (
37
), we obtain:

\displaystyle\|du_{k}\|_{L^{4}(\Omega_{r},g)}\leq\frac{1}{12\,\gamma_{L}}.

Together with (
38
), we deduce that
(u_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
remains bounded in
W^{2,2}_{0}(\Omega_{r},g)
and also in the interior of the ball
\mathcal{B}:=\left\{w\in W^{1,4}_{0}(\Omega_{r}):\ \|dw\|_{L^{4}(\Omega_{r},g)}<(4\gamma_{L})^{-1}\right\}
.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

Since
u_{r}=0
on
\partial\Omega_{r}
,
u_{r}
also minimize the
L^{2}
-norm of
\mathrm{Sch}
thanks to the Chern–Gauss–Bonnet formula (
35
). Thus, if
g_{r}:=g_{u_{r}}
then we have the following uniform bound:

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{r}}|\mathrm{Riem}^{g_{r}}|^{2}_{g_{r}}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g_{r}}+\mathrm{vol}_{g_{r}}(\Omega_{r})

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{B}\setminus\mathbb{B}_{r}}|\mathrm{Riem}^{g}|^{2}_{g}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g}+\mathrm{vol}_{g}(\Omega_{r})

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{B}\setminus\{0\}}|\mathrm{Riem}^{g}|^{2}_{g}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g}+\mathrm{vol}_{g}(\mathbb{B}\setminus\{0\}).

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
u\in W^{2,2}_{0}(\Omega_{r},g)
be a critical point of
E_{r}
. Then, the metric
g_{u}
satisfies the following equation:

\displaystyle\Delta_{g_{u}}J^{g_{u}}=1\quad\text{ in }\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\Omega_{r}).

Proof.

Consider a variation
(v_{t})_{t\in(-1,1)}
of
u
with
v_{0}=u
and
\dot{v}:=(\partial_{t}v_{t})_{t=0}
having compact support in
\Omega_{r}
. We compute the first derivative thanks by using
g_{v_{t}}=e^{2v_{t}}g
and the formula (
33
):

\displaystyle 0=\frac{dE_{r}(v_{t})}{dt}_{|t=0}

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{dt}\left(\int_{\Omega_{r}}\frac{1}{2}(J^{g}-\Delta_{g}v_{t}-|dv_{t}|^{2}_{g})^{2}+\frac{e^{4v_{t}}}{4}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g}\right)_{|t=0}

(39)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{r}}(J^{g}-\Delta_{g}u-|du|^{2}_{g})(-\Delta_{g}\dot{v}-2\left\langle du,d\dot{v}\right\rangle_{g})+\dot{v}e^{4u}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{r}}e^{2u}J^{g_{u}}(-\Delta_{g}\dot{v}-2\left\langle du,d\dot{v}\right\rangle_{g})+\dot{v}e^{4u}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g}.

After integration by parts, we obtain:

\displaystyle 0=\int_{\Omega_{r}}\dot{v}\left(e^{4u}-\Delta_{g}(e^{2u}J^{g_{u}})+2\mathrm{div}_{g}(e^{2u}J^{g_{u}}\nabla^{g}u)\right)\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g}.

Since this is valid for every
\dot{v}\in W^{2,2}_{0}
, we obtain:

\displaystyle-e^{4u}

\displaystyle=-\Delta_{g}(e^{2u}J^{g_{u}})+2\mathrm{div}_{g}(e^{2u}J^{g_{u}}\nabla^{g}u)

\displaystyle=-\mathrm{div}_{g}\left(2e^{2u}(\nabla^{g}u)J^{g_{u}}+e^{2u}\nabla^{g}J^{g_{u}}\right)+2\mathrm{div}_{g}(e^{2u}J^{g_{u}}\nabla^{g}u)

\displaystyle=-\mathrm{div}_{g}\left(e^{2u}\nabla^{g}J^{g_{u}}\right)

\displaystyle=-\mathrm{div}_{g}\left(e^{4u}\nabla^{g_{u}}J^{g_{u}}\right)

\displaystyle=-e^{4u}\Delta_{g_{u}}J^{g_{u}}.

∎

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
u\in W^{2,2}_{0}(\Omega_{r},g)
be a critial point of
E_{r}
. It holds
u\in C^{\infty}(\Omega_{r})\cap C^{0}\left(\overline{\Omega_{r}}\right)
.

Proof.

Thanks to (
39
), the Euler–Lagrange equation of
E_{r}
can also be written as:

(40)

\displaystyle-\Delta_{g}\left(J^{g}-\Delta_{g}u-|du|^{2}_{g}\right)+2\mathrm{div}_{g}\left((J^{g}-\Delta_{g}u-|du|^{2}_{g})\nabla^{g}u\right)+e^{4u}=0.

Thanks to Adams–Moser–Trudinger inequality, since
g
is smooth on
\Omega_{r}
, there exists two constants
\gamma,C>0
such that for all ball
B\subset\Omega_{r}

\displaystyle\forall\varphi\in W^{1,4}(B,g),\ \ \ \frac{1}{\mathrm{vol}_{g}(B)}\int_{B}e^{\left(\frac{\gamma|\varphi(x)-(\varphi)_{B}|}{\|d\varphi\|_{L^{4}(B)}}\right)^{\frac{4}{3}}}\ d\mathrm{vol}_{g}(x)\leq C.

Since
u\in W^{1,4}_{0}(\Omega_{r},g)
and
g
is smooth, we can extend
u
by
0
in a small neighbourhood
\Omega
of
W^{1,4}_{0}(\Omega_{r},g)
and keep a function of regularity
W^{1,4}_{0}
. It holds for any ball
B\subset\Omega
and any
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
:

\displaystyle\lambda|u(x)-(u)_{B}|\leq\frac{3}{4}\left(\frac{\gamma|u(x)-(u)_{B}|}{\|du\|_{L^{4}(B)}}\right)^{\frac{4}{3}}+\frac{1}{4}\left(\frac{|\lambda|}{\gamma}\|du\|_{L^{4}(B)}\right)^{4}.

Hence, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{B}e^{\lambda u(x)}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g}(x)\leq e^{\lambda|(u)_{B}|+\frac{1}{4}\left(\frac{|\lambda|}{\gamma}\|du\|_{L^{4}(B)}\right)^{4}}\int_{B}e^{\frac{3}{4}\left(\frac{\gamma|u(x)-(u)_{B}|}{\|du\|_{L^{4}(B)}}\right)^{\frac{4}{3}}}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g}(x)<\infty.

Therefore,
e^{u}\in L^{p}(\Omega_{r})
for any
p\in[1,\infty)
. Coming back to (
40
), we obtain that the function
v:=J^{g}-\Delta_{g}u-|du|^{2}_{g}
satisfies:

\displaystyle\forall p\in[1,\infty),\ \ \ \Delta_{g}v\in L^{p}(\Omega_{r},g)+W^{-1,(\frac{4}{3},1)}(\Omega_{r},g).

Indeed, it holds
du\in L^{(4,2)}(\Omega_{r})
, so that
(\Delta_{g}u)du\in L^{(2,1)}(\Omega_{r})
. We also have
du\in L^{(4,3)}(\Omega_{r})
, so that
|du|^{3}\in L^{(\frac{4}{3},1)}(\Omega_{r})
. Hence, it holds
v\in W^{1,(\frac{4}{3},1)}(\Omega_{r})\hookrightarrow L^{(2,1)}(\Omega_{r})
, meaning that
\Delta_{g}u\in L^{(2,1)}(\Omega_{r})
. Thus,
u\in W^{2,(2,1)}(\Omega_{r})\hookrightarrow C^{0}\left(\overline{\Omega_{r}}\right)
and
e^{u}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega_{r})
. Consequently,
J^{g_{u}}=e^{2u}v\in W^{1,\frac{4}{3}}(\Omega_{r})
is a solution to

\displaystyle\partial_{\alpha}\left(e^{2u}g^{\alpha\beta}\,\sqrt{\det g}\,\partial_{\alpha}J^{g_{u}}\right)=e^{4u}\sqrt{\det g},

where we have
e^{2u}g^{\alpha\beta}\,\sqrt{\det g}\in W^{1,(4,1)}(\Omega_{r})\subset C^{0}\left(\overline{\Omega_{r}}\right)
and
e^{4u}\sqrt{\det g}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega_{r})
. By solving a Dirichlet problem for the flat metric, one can write the right-hand side in divergence form. The coefficients
e^{2u}g^{\alpha\beta}\,\sqrt{\det g}
are continuous. By a straightforward adaptation of the proof of
[
3
, Theorem A1.1]
, we deduce that
J^{g_{u}}\in W^{1,p}
for every
p>1
. We consider now the following equation:

\displaystyle\Delta_{g}(e^{u})=\left(e^{2u}J^{g_{u}}-J^{g}\right)e^{u}.

The right-hand side belongs to
W^{1,4-\varepsilon}(\Omega_{r})
for every
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
. Thus,
e^{u}\in W^{2,4-\varepsilon}(\Omega_{r})
for every
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
. Thanks to Sobolev embeddings, we deduce that
e^{u}\in W^{1,p}(\Omega_{r})
for every
p\in[1,\infty)
. Since
u
is continuous, we deduce that
u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega_{r})
for every
p\in[1,\infty)
. We now come back to the equation:

\displaystyle\Delta_{g}u=|du|^{2}_{g}+e^{2u}J^{g_{u}}-J^{g}.

We deduce that
u\in W^{3,p}(\Omega_{r})
for every
p\in[1,\infty)
. By a bootrstrap argument, we obtain
u\in C^{\infty}(\Omega_{r})
.
∎

3.5.
Sobolev inequalities and integrability of the curvature tensors in the new metric

Thanks to
Lemma

3.6
, we can pass to the limit
r\to 0
and we obtain metric
g_{0}:=e^{2u}g
which is
C^{\infty}
on
\Omega_{0}:=\mathbb{B}\setminus\{0\}
and satisfies
\Delta_{g_{0}}J^{g_{0}}=1
on
\Omega_{0}
. In this section, we prove some regularity estimates on the conformal factor
u
in
Lemma

3.7
. We deduce some integrability of

in
Lemma

3.8
and the Sobolev inequalities in
Lemma

3.9
.

Given
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{B}\setminus\{0\})
, we denote

\displaystyle(\varphi)_{\mathbb{B}}:=\frac{1}{\mathrm{vol}_{g}(\mathbb{B}\setminus\{0\})}\int_{\mathbb{B}\setminus\{0\}}\varphi\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g}.

We first prove that
e^{-u}\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb{B},g)
.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
\gamma_{L}>1
and
\gamma_{S}>0
be defined in

A

.6
and

A

.4
. Assume that

(41)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{B}}\frac{(J^{g})^{2}}{2}+\frac{1}{4}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g}\leq\frac{1}{64\gamma^{2}_{S}}.

Then, it holds
e^{-u}\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb{B},g)
with the following estimates:

\displaystyle\|e^{-u}\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{B},g)}^{2}\leq\frac{\gamma_{S}}{24\gamma_{L}^{4}},

\displaystyle\|d(e^{-u})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{B},g)}^{2}\leq\frac{1+\gamma_{S}}{32\gamma_{L}^{4}}.

Proof.

Given
r>0
, we prove the following estimates and then pass to the limit
r\to 0
: it holds
e^{-u_{r}}\in W^{1,2}(\Omega_{r},g)
with the following estimates:

\displaystyle\|e^{-u_{r}}\|_{L^{4}(\Omega_{r},g)}^{2}\leq\frac{\gamma_{S}}{24\gamma_{L}^{4}},

\displaystyle\|d(e^{-u_{r}})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{r},g)}^{2}\leq\frac{1+\gamma_{S}}{32\gamma_{L}^{4}}.

We compute the equation satisfied by
e^{-u_{r}}
:

\displaystyle\Delta_{g}\left(e^{-u_{r}}\right)

\displaystyle=-\mathrm{div}_{g}\left(e^{-u_{r}}\nabla^{g}u\right)

\displaystyle=e^{-u_{r}}|du_{r}|^{2}_{g}-e^{-u_{r}}\Delta_{g}(u_{r})

\displaystyle=e^{-u_{r}}|du_{r}|^{2}_{g}+e^{-u_{r}}\left(|du_{r}|^{2}_{g}+e^{2u_{r}}J^{g_{r}}-J^{g}\right)

\displaystyle=2e^{-u_{r}}|du_{r}|^{2}_{g}+e^{-u_{r}}\left(e^{2u_{r}}J^{g_{r}}-J^{g}\right).

We multiply by
e^{-u_{r}}
and integrate by parts:

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Omega_{r}}e^{-2u_{r}}(\partial_{\nu}u_{r})d\mathrm{vol}_{g}-\int_{\Omega_{r}}|d(e^{-u_{r}})|^{2}_{g}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g}=2\int_{\Omega_{r}}e^{-2u_{r}}|du_{r}|^{2}_{g}+e^{-2u_{r}}\left(e^{2u_{r}}J^{g_{r}}-J^{g}\right)\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g}.

We consider the first term of the left-hand side: since
u_{r}=0
on
\partial\Omega_{r}
, it holds

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Omega_{r}}e^{-2u_{r}}(\partial_{\nu}u_{r})d\mathrm{vol}_{g}

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial\Omega_{r}}(\partial_{\nu}u_{r})d\mathrm{vol}_{g}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{r}}\Delta_{g}u_{r}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{r}}-|du_{r}|^{2}_{g}-e^{2u_{r}}J^{g_{r}}+J^{g}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g}.

We end up with the following estimate:

\displaystyle 3\int_{\Omega_{r}}|d(e^{-u_{r}})|^{2}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega_{r}}e^{-2u_{r}}\left(e^{2u_{r}}J^{g_{r}}-J^{g}\right)\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g}-\int_{\Omega_{r}}|du_{r}|^{2}_{g}+e^{2u_{r}}J^{g_{r}}-J^{g}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \|e^{-2u_{r}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{r},g)}\|e^{2u_{r}}J^{g_{r}}-J^{g}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{r},g)}+\mathrm{vol}_{g}(\Omega_{r})^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\|du_{r}\|_{L^{4}(\Omega_{r},g)}^{2}+\|e^{2u_{r}}J^{g_{r}}-J^{g}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{r},g)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ 4E_{r}(0)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\|e^{-u_{r}}\|_{L^{4}(\Omega_{r},g)}^{2}+\|du_{r}\|_{L^{4}(\Omega_{r},g)}^{2}+E_{r}(0)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right).

From
Lemma

3.3
and (
37
), we obtain:

(42)

\displaystyle 3\int_{\Omega_{r}}|d(e^{-u_{r}})|^{2}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g}\leq 4E_{r}(0)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\|e^{-u_{r}}\|_{L^{4}(\Omega_{r},g)}^{2}+\frac{1}{4\gamma_{L}^{2}}\right).

Thanks to
Item

A

.4
, it holds

\displaystyle\|e^{-u_{r}}\|_{L^{4}(\Omega_{r},g)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\gamma_{S}\left[\int_{\Omega_{r}}e^{-2u_{r}}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g}+\frac{4}{3}E_{r}(0)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\|e^{-u_{r}}\|_{L^{4}(\Omega_{r},g)}^{2}+\frac{1}{4\gamma_{L}^{2}}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leq\gamma_{S}\left[\mathrm{vol}_{g}(\Omega_{r})^{\frac{1}{2}}\|e^{-u_{r}}\|_{L^{4}(\Omega_{r},g)}^{2}+\frac{4}{3}E_{r}(0)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\|e^{-u_{r}}\|_{L^{4}(\Omega_{r},g)}^{2}+\frac{1}{4\gamma_{L}^{2}}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leq 4\gamma_{S}E_{r}(0)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|e^{-u_{r}}\|_{L^{4}(\Omega_{r},g)}^{2}+\frac{\gamma_{S}}{48\gamma_{L}^{4}}.

Thanks to (
41
), we obtain

\displaystyle\|e^{-u_{r}}\|_{L^{4}(\Omega_{r},g)}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}\|e^{-u_{r}}\|_{L^{4}(\Omega_{r},g)}^{2}+\frac{\gamma_{S}}{48\gamma_{L}^{4}}.

Hence, it holds

\displaystyle\|e^{-u_{r}}\|_{L^{4}(\Omega_{r},g)}^{2}\leq\frac{\gamma_{S}}{24\gamma_{L}^{4}}.

Coming back to (
42
) and using
\gamma_{L}>1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|d(e^{-u_{r}})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{r},g)}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{8\gamma_{L}^{2}}\left(\frac{\gamma_{S}}{24\gamma_{L}^{4}}+\frac{1}{4\gamma_{L}^{2}}\right)\leq\frac{1+\gamma_{S}}{32\gamma_{L}^{4}}.

∎

We now show that the Bach tensor
B^{g_{0}}
satisfies certain integrability.

Lemma 3.8
.

For any
q\in[1,+\infty)
, we have the following estimate:

\displaystyle\left\|B^{g_{0}}\right\|_{L^{\frac{2q}{1+q}}(\mathbb{B},g_{0})}\leq\left(\frac{\gamma_{S}}{24\gamma_{L}^{4}}\right)^{\frac{q-1}{2q}}\left\|B^{g}\right\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{B},g)}.

Proof.

The Bach tensor satisfies the following transformation formula:

\displaystyle B^{g_{0}}_{ij}=e^{-2u}B^{g}_{ij}.

Thus, it holds
|B^{g_{0}}|_{g_{0}}=e^{-4u}|B^{g}|_{g}
. If
q\geq 1
, we have
\frac{q-1}{q+1}+\frac{2}{q+1}=1
. Thus, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{B}}|B^{g_{0}}|^{\frac{2q}{1+q}}_{g_{0}}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g_{0}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{B}}e^{4\left(1-\frac{2q}{1+q}\right)u}|B^{g}|^{\frac{2q}{1+q}}_{g}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{B}}e^{4\frac{1-q}{1+q}u_{r}}|B^{g}|^{\frac{2q}{1+q}}_{g}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|e^{-4\frac{q-1}{1+q}u}\right\|_{L^{\frac{q+1}{q-1}}(\mathbb{B},g)}\left\||B^{g}|^{\frac{2q}{1+q}}_{g}\right\|_{L^{\frac{q+1}{2}}(\mathbb{B},g)}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|e^{-u}\right\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{B},g)}^{\frac{q-1}{q+1}}\left\|B^{g}\right\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{B},g)}^{\frac{2q}{q+1}}.

We conclude thanks to
Lemma

3.7
.
∎

We deduce from
Lemma

3.7
a Sobolev inequality for the metric
g_{r}
.

Lemma 3.9
.

Assume that

(43)

\displaystyle 8\int_{\mathbb{B}}|du|^{4}_{g}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g}+2\mathrm{vol}_{g}(\mathbb{B})\leq\frac{1}{2\gamma^{2}_{S}}.

Then, for any
\psi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{B}\setminus\{0\})
, it holds

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{B}}\psi^{4}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g_{0}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq 4\gamma_{S}\int_{\mathbb{B}}|d\psi|^{2}_{g_{0}}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g_{0}}.

Remark 3.10
.

The assumption (
43
) is true up to reduce the initial ball, thanks to
Lemma

3.3
.

Proof.

Let
\psi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{B}\setminus\{0\})
and
\varphi:=e^{u}\psi
. From

A

.4
, it holds

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{B}}e^{4u}\psi^{4}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\gamma_{S}\int_{\mathbb{B}}|d(e^{u}\psi)|^{2}_{g}+e^{2u}\psi^{2}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g}.

We write the above inequality in terms of the metric

:

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{B}}\psi^{4}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g_{0}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\gamma_{S}\int_{\mathbb{B}}2\psi^{2}e^{2u}|du|^{2}_{g}+2e^{2u}|d\psi|^{2}_{g}+e^{2u}\psi^{2}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g}

\displaystyle\leq 2\gamma_{S}\int_{\mathbb{B}}|d\psi|^{2}_{g_{0}}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g_{r}}+\gamma_{S}\int_{\Omega_{r}}\psi^{2}e^{2u}\left(1+2|du|^{2}_{g}\right)\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g}

\displaystyle\leq 2\gamma_{S}\int_{\Omega_{r}}|d\psi|^{2}_{g_{0}}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g_{0}}+\gamma_{S}\left(\int_{\mathbb{B}}\psi^{4}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g_{0}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(2\mathrm{vol}_{g}(\mathbb{B})+8\|du\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{B},g)}^{4}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{B}}\psi^{4}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g_{0}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq 2\gamma_{S}\int_{\mathbb{B}}|d\psi|^{2}_{g_{0}}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g_{0}}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\int_{\mathbb{B}}\psi^{4}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g_{0}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

∎

We will need some geometric properties of the metric
g_{0}
.

Lemma 3.11
.

The volume form
d\mathrm{vol}_{g_{0}}
is doubling: there exists a constant
\gamma_{D}^{\prime}>0
depending only on
\gamma_{A},\gamma_{L},\gamma_{D},\Lambda_{A}
such that

\displaystyle\forall B_{g_{0}}(x,2s)\subset\mathbb{B}\setminus\{0\},\quad\mathrm{vol}_{g_{0}}(B_{g_{0}}(x,2s))\leq\gamma_{D}^{\prime}\mathrm{vol}_{g_{0}}(B_{g_{0}}(x,s)).

Proof.

We follow
[
1
, Paragraph 5.3.2]
and the proofs of properties (8) and (9) in
[
32
, Proposition 9.1.5]
. It holds for any ball
B=B_{g}(x,s)\subset\Omega
and any
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
:

\displaystyle\lambda|u(x)-(u)_{B}|\leq\frac{3}{4}\left(\frac{\gamma_{A}|u(x)-(u)_{B}|}{\|du\|_{L^{4}(B,g)}}\right)^{\frac{4}{3}}+\frac{1}{4}\left(\frac{|\lambda|}{\gamma_{A}}\|du\|_{L^{4}(B,g)}\right)^{4}.

Hence, we obtain from

A

.5
:

\displaystyle\int_{B}e^{\lambda u(x)}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g}(x)

\displaystyle\leq e^{\lambda|(u)_{B}|+\frac{1}{4}\left(\frac{|\lambda|}{\gamma}\|du\|_{L^{4}(B,g)}\right)^{4}}\int_{B}e^{\frac{3}{4}\left(\frac{\gamma|u(x)-(u)_{B}|}{\|du\|_{L^{4}(B)}}\right)^{\frac{4}{3}}}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g}(x)

\displaystyle\leq\Lambda_{A}e^{\frac{1}{4}\left(\frac{|\lambda|}{4\gamma_{L}\gamma_{A}}\right)^{4}}e^{\lambda|(u)_{B}|}.

Hence, for any
q>1
, it holds

\displaystyle\left(\fint_{B}e^{4u}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g}\right)\left(\fint_{B}e^{-\frac{4}{q-1}u}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g}\right)^{q-1}\leq\Lambda_{A}^{q}e^{\frac{1}{4}\left(\frac{1}{\gamma_{L}\gamma_{A}}\right)^{4}+\frac{q-1}{4}\left(\frac{1}{(q-1)\gamma_{L}\gamma_{A}}\right)^{4}}.

Thus,
e^{4u}
is an
A_{q}
-weight, for any
q>1
, see
[
32
, Definition 9.1.3]
. Hence, we have

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\mathrm{vol}_{g}(B_{g}(x,r))}{\mathrm{vol}_{g}(B_{g}(x,2r))}\right)^{q}

\displaystyle=\left(\fint_{B_{g}(x,2r)}\chi_{B_{g}(x,r)}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g}\right)^{q}

\displaystyle=\left(\fint_{B_{g}(x,2r)}\chi_{B_{g}(x,r)}e^{\frac{4}{q}u}e^{-\frac{4}{q}u}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g}\right)^{q}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\mathrm{vol}_{g}(B_{g}(x,2r))^{q}}\left(\int_{B_{g}(x,2r)}\chi_{B_{g}(x,r)}^{q}e^{4u}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g}\right)\left(\int_{B_{g}(x,2r)}e^{-\frac{4}{q-1}u}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g}\right)^{q-1}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\mathrm{vol}_{g}(B_{g}(x,2r))^{q}}\left(\int_{B_{g}(x,r)}e^{4u}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g}\right)\left(\int_{B_{g}(x,2r)}e^{-\frac{4}{q-1}u}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g}\right)^{q-1}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\mathrm{vol}_{g}(B_{g}(x,2r))^{q}}\left(\int_{B_{g}(x,r)}e^{4u}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g}\right)\left(\frac{1}{\mathrm{vol}_{g}(B_{g}(x,2r))^{q}}\int_{B_{g}(x,2r)}e^{4u}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g}\right)^{-1}.

We obtain that the measure
d\mathrm{vol}_{g_{0}}
is doubling on balls with respect to the metric
g
: there exists a constant
\kappa>0
depending only on
\gamma_{D},\gamma_{L},\gamma_{A},\Lambda_{A}
such that

\displaystyle\mathrm{vol}_{g_{0}}(B_{g}(x,2r))=\int_{B_{g}(x,2r)}e^{4u}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g}\leq\kappa\int_{B_{g}(x,r)}e^{4u}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g}=\kappa\,\mathrm{vol}_{g_{0}}(B_{g}(x,r)).

Consider now a ball
B_{g_{0}}(x,2r)\subset\mathbb{B}\setminus\{0\}
. Since
\overline{B_{g_{0}}(x,r)}
is compact, there exists a finite covering
B_{g_{0}}(x,r/2)\subset\bigcup_{1\leq i\leq I}B_{g}(x_{i},r_{i})\subset B_{g_{0}}(x,r)
. By Vitali covering lemma, one can extract a subset
(B_{g}(x_{j},r_{j}))_{1\leq j\leq J}
of disjoint balls such that
B_{g_{0}}(x,r/2)\subset\bigcup_{1\leq j\leq J}B_{g}(x_{j},3r_{j})
. Since
\mathrm{vol}_{g_{0}}
is doubling on balls for
g
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathrm{vol}_{g_{0}}(B_{g_{0}}(x,2r))\leq\mathrm{vol}_{g_{0}}\left(\bigcup_{1\leq j\leq J}B_{g}(x_{j},12r_{j})\right)\leq\kappa^{6}\sum_{1\leq j\leq J}\mathrm{vol}_{g_{0}}(B_{g}(x_{j},r_{j})).

Since the balls
B_{g}(x_{j},r_{j})
are disjoint and are all contained in
B_{g_{0}}(x,r)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathrm{vol}_{g_{0}}(B_{g_{0}}(x,2r))\leq\kappa^{6}\,\mathrm{vol}_{g_{0}}\left(\bigcup_{1\leq j\leq J}B_{g}(x_{j},r_{j})\right)\leq\kappa^{6}\,\mathrm{vol}_{g_{0}}(B_{g_{0}}(x,r)).

∎

3.6.
\varepsilon
-regularity

In this section, we prove the following
\varepsilon
-regularity, inspired by Moser’s iteration, see for instance
[
34
, Theorem 4.4]
.

Theorem 3.12
.

Let
g
a metric on
\mathbb{B}\setminus\{0\}
satisfying the hypothesis of theorem
Theorem

3.1
, then there exists
\varepsilon>0
and
C>0
depending on the operation
\star
in (
34
) and
\gamma_{S}
such that if

\displaystyle\|\mathrm{Riem}^{g}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{B},g)}+\mathrm{vol}_{g}(\mathbb{B})\leq\varepsilon.

then there exists a metric
g_{0}
conformal to
g
satifying

\Delta_{g_{0}}J^{g_{0}}=1.

Moreover, for each geodesic ball
B_{g_{0}}(x,s)\subset\mathbb{B}\setminus\{0\}
, it holds

\displaystyle\|\mathrm{Sch}^{g_{0}}\|_{L^{2p}(B_{g_{0}}(x,s))}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \frac{C}{s^{\frac{2}{p}}}\mathrm{vol}_{g_{r}}(B_{g_{0}}(x,2s))^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}}\|\mathrm{Sch}^{g_{0}}\|_{L^{2}(B_{g_{0}}(x,2s))}C+\mathrm{vol}_{g_{0}}(B_{g_{0}}(x,2s))^{\frac{2}{p+1}}

\displaystyle+C\left(\|J^{g_{0}}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{B},g_{0})}^{\frac{2}{p+1}}+\|B^{g_{0}}\|_{L^{\frac{2p}{p+1}}(\mathbb{B},g_{0})}^{\frac{2}{p+1}}\right)\mathrm{vol}_{g_{0}}(B_{g_{0}}(x,2s))^{\frac{1}{p+1}}.

Remark 3.13
.

This provide an
\varepsilon
-regularity result for Bach-flat metrics without assuming the scalar curvature to be constant, see for instance
[
13
,
60
,
61
]
.

Proof.

The Schouten tensor satisfies:

(44)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\Delta_{g_{0}}\mathrm{Sch}^{g_{0}}=-\mathrm{Hess}_{g_{0}}J^{g_{0}}+B^{g_{0}}+\mathrm{Riem}^{g_{0}}\star\mathrm{Sch}^{g_{0}},\\
\Delta_{g_{0}}J^{g_{0}}=1.\end{cases}

We define
f:=|\mathrm{Sch}^{g_{0}}|_{g_{0}}
. This function satisfies

\displaystyle-\Delta_{g_{0}}f

\displaystyle=-\mathrm{div}_{g_{0}}\left(\left\langle\frac{\mathrm{Sch}^{g_{0}}}{|\mathrm{Sch}^{g_{0}}|_{g_{0}}},\nabla^{g_{0}}\mathrm{Sch}^{g_{0}}\right\rangle_{g_{0}}\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{|\nabla\mathrm{Sch}^{g_{0}}|^{2}_{g_{0}}}{|\mathrm{Sch}^{g_{0}}|_{g_{0}}}+\left\langle\frac{\mathrm{Sch}^{g_{0}}}{|\mathrm{Sch}^{g_{0}}|_{g_{0}}},-\Delta_{g_{0}}\mathrm{Sch}^{g_{0}}\right\rangle_{g_{0}}.

Hence, for any function
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{B};[0,+\infty))
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{B}}\left\langle df,d\varphi\right\rangle_{g_{0}}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g_{0}}\leq\int_{\mathbb{B}}\varphi\left\langle\frac{\mathrm{Sch}^{g_{0}}}{|\mathrm{Sch}^{g_{0}}|_{g_{0}}},-\mathrm{Hess}_{g_{0}}J^{g_{0}}+B^{g_{0}}+\mathrm{Riem}^{g_{0}}\star\mathrm{Sch}^{g_{0}}\right\rangle_{g_{0}}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g_{0}}.

Given
\beta\in[0,p-1]
and
\chi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{B}\setminus\{0\};[0,1])
, we consider the test function
\varphi=\chi^{2}f^{\beta}
:

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{B}}\beta\chi^{2}f^{\beta-1}|df|^{2}_{g_{0}}+2\chi f^{\beta}\left\langle d\chi,df\right\rangle_{g_{0}}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g_{0}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \int_{\mathbb{B}}\chi^{2}\left(f^{\beta}|\mathrm{Hess}_{g_{0}}J^{g_{0}}|+f^{\beta}|B^{g_{0}}|_{g_{0}}+f^{\beta+1}|\mathrm{Riem}^{g_{0}}|_{g_{0}}\right)\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g_{0}}.

Thus, there exists a universal constant
C>0
such that the following holds for any
\beta\geq 0
:

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{B}}\left|d\left(\chi f^{\frac{\beta+1}{2}}\right)\right|^{2}_{g_{0}}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g_{0}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ C\beta\int_{\mathbb{B}}|d\chi|^{2}f^{\beta+1}+\chi^{2}\left(f^{\beta}|\mathrm{Hess}_{g_{0}}J^{g_{0}}|_{G_{0}}+f^{\beta}|B^{g_{0}}|_{g_{0}}+f^{\beta+1}|\mathrm{Riem}^{g_{0}}|_{g_{0}}\right)\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g_{0}}.

Thanks to
Lemma

3.9
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{B}}\chi^{4}f^{2\beta+2}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g_{0}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ C\gamma_{L}\beta\int_{\mathbb{B}}|d\chi|^{2}f^{\beta+1}+\chi^{2}\left(f^{\beta}|\mathrm{Hess}_{g_{0}}J^{g_{0}}|_{g_{0}}+f^{\beta}|B^{g_{0}}|_{g_{0}}+f^{\beta+1}|\mathrm{Riem}^{g_{0}}|_{g_{0}}\right)\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g_{0}}.

We estimate the last two terms of the right-hand side by Hölder inequality. Since we have
\beta\leq p-1
, we have
0<\frac{1}{2\beta+2}-\frac{1}{2p}<1
. Furthermore it holds
\frac{p+1}{2p}+\frac{\beta}{2+2\beta}+\frac{1}{2\beta+2}-\frac{1}{2p}=1
Thus, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{B}}\chi^{2}\left(f^{\beta}|B^{g_{0}}|_{g_{0}}+f^{\beta+1}|\mathrm{Riem}^{g_{0}}|_{g_{0}}\right)\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g_{0}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \left(\|\mathrm{Hess}_{g_{0}}J^{g_{0}}\|_{L^{\frac{2p}{p+1}}(\mathbb{B},g_{0})}+\|B^{g_{0}}\|_{L^{\frac{2p}{p+1}}(\mathbb{B},g_{0})}\right)\left\|\chi^{2}f^{\beta}\right\|_{L^{\frac{2\beta+2}{\beta}}(\mathbb{B},g_{0})}\mathrm{vol}_{g_{0}}(\mathrm{supp}(\eta))^{\frac{1}{\beta+2}-\frac{1}{2p}}

\displaystyle+\|\mathrm{Riem}^{g_{0}}\|_{L^{2}(\mathrm{supp}(\chi),g_{0})}\left\|\chi^{2}f^{\beta+1}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{B},g_{0})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \left(\|\mathrm{Hess}_{g_{0}}J^{g_{0}}\|_{L^{\frac{2p}{p+1}}(\mathbb{B},g_{0})}+\|B^{g_{0}}\|_{L^{\frac{2p}{p+1}}(\mathbb{B},g_{0})}\right)\left(\int_{\mathbb{B}}\chi^{\frac{4\beta+4}{\beta}}f^{2\beta+2}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g_{0}}\right)^{\frac{\beta}{2\beta+2}}\mathrm{vol}_{g_{0}}(\mathrm{supp}(\chi))^{\frac{1}{\beta+2}-\frac{1}{2p}}

\displaystyle+\|\mathrm{Riem}^{g_{0}}\|_{L^{2}(\mathrm{supp}(\chi),g_{0})}\left(\int_{\mathbb{B}}\chi^{4}f^{2\beta+2}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g_{0}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Since
0\leq\chi\leq 1
, we obtain
\chi^{\frac{4\beta+4}{\beta}}\leq\chi^{4}
. By Young inequality with the exponents
\frac{\beta}{2\beta+2}+\frac{\beta+2}{2\beta+2}=1
, and choosing
\|\mathrm{Riem}^{g_{0}}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{B},g_{0})}\leq C\|\mathrm{Riem}^{g}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{B},g)}+C\mathrm{vol}_{g}(\mathbb{B})\leq\varepsilon
small enough, we obtain

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{B}}\chi^{4}f^{2\beta+2}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g_{0}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ C\gamma_{L}\beta\int_{\mathbb{B}}|d\chi|^{2}_{g_{0}}f^{\beta+1}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g_{0}}+\left(\|\mathrm{Hess}_{g_{0}}J^{g_{0}}\|_{L^{\frac{2p}{p+1}}(\mathbb{B},g_{0})}^{\frac{2\beta+2}{\beta+2}}+\|B^{g_{0}}\|_{L^{\frac{2p}{p+1}}(\mathbb{B},g_{0})}^{\frac{2\beta+2}{\beta+2}}\right)\mathrm{vol}_{g_{0}}(\mathrm{supp}(\chi))^{\frac{2\beta+2}{\beta+2}\left(\frac{1}{\beta+2}-\frac{1}{2p}\right)}.

Thus we obtain

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{B}}\chi^{4}f^{2\beta+2}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g_{0}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2\beta+2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ C\beta^{\frac{1}{\beta+1}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{B}}|d\chi|^{2}_{g_{0}}f^{\beta+1}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g_{0}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta+1}}

\displaystyle+\left(\|\mathrm{Hess}_{g_{0}}J^{g_{0}}\|_{L^{\frac{2p}{p+1}}(\mathbb{B},g_{0})}^{\frac{2}{\beta+2}}+\|B^{g_{0}}\|_{L^{\frac{2p}{p+1}}(\mathbb{B},g_{0})}^{\frac{2}{\beta+2}}\right)\mathrm{vol}_{g_{0}}(\mathrm{supp}(\chi))^{\frac{2}{\beta+2}\left(\frac{1}{\beta+2}-\frac{1}{2p}\right)}.

We now choose
\chi
as a cut-off function such that
\chi=1
in
B_{g_{0}}(x,s)
and
\chi=0
on
\mathbb{B}\setminus B_{g_{0}}(x,t)
for some
0<s<t
with
B_{g_{0}}(x,t)\subset\mathbb{B}\setminus\{0\}
and
|d\chi|_{g_{0}}\leq C(t-s)^{-1}
. We obtain

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{2\beta+2}(B_{g_{0}}(x,s))}\leq

\displaystyle\ \frac{C\beta^{\frac{1}{2}}}{(t-s)^{\frac{2}{\beta+1}}}\|f\|_{L^{\beta+1}(B_{g_{0}}(x,t))}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left(\|\mathrm{Hess}_{g_{0}}J^{g_{0}}\|_{L^{\frac{2p}{p+1}}(\mathbb{B},g_{0})}^{\frac{2}{\beta+2}}+\|B^{g_{0}}\|_{L^{\frac{2p}{p+1}}(\mathbb{B},g_{0})}^{\frac{2}{\beta+2}}\right)\mathrm{vol}_{g_{0}}(B_{g_{0}}(x,t))^{\frac{2}{\beta+2}\left(\frac{1}{\beta+2}-\frac{1}{2p}\right)}.

We choose
\beta=p-1
and
t=2s
:

(45)

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{2p}(B_{g_{0}}(x,s))}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C}{s^{\frac{2}{p}}}\|f\|_{L^{p}(B_{g_{0}}(x,2s))}

\displaystyle+\left(\|\mathrm{Hess}_{g_{0}}J^{g_{0}}\|_{L^{\frac{2p}{p+1}}(\mathbb{B},g_{0})}^{\frac{2}{p+1}}+\|B^{g_{0}}\|_{L^{\frac{2p}{p+1}}(\mathbb{B},g_{0})}^{\frac{2}{p+1}}\right)\mathrm{vol}_{g_{0}}(B_{g_{0}}(x,2s))^{\frac{2}{p+1}\left(\frac{1}{p+1}-\frac{1}{2p}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C}{s^{\frac{2}{p}}}\mathrm{vol}_{g_{0}}(B_{g_{0}}(x,2s))^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}}\|f\|_{L^{2}(B_{g_{0}}(x,2s))}

\displaystyle+\left(\|\mathrm{Hess}_{g_{0}}J^{g_{0}}\|_{L^{\frac{2p}{p+1}}(\mathbb{B},g_{0})}^{\frac{2}{p+1}}+\|B^{g_{0}}\|_{L^{\frac{2p}{p+1}}(\mathbb{B},g_{0})}^{\frac{2}{p+1}}\right)\mathrm{vol}_{g_{0}}(B_{g_{0}}(x,2s))^{\frac{1}{p+1}}.

The Hessian transforms as follows, see for instance
[
23
, Equation (2)]
, using
\nabla^{g_{0}}_{j}f=\nabla^{g}_{j}f
:

\displaystyle(\mathrm{Hess}_{g_{0}}f)_{ij}

\displaystyle=\nabla^{g_{0}}_{i}\nabla^{g_{0}}_{j}f

\displaystyle=\nabla^{g}_{i}\nabla^{g_{0}}_{j}f-(\nabla^{g}_{i}u)(\nabla^{g_{0}}_{j}f)-(\nabla^{g}_{j}u)(\nabla^{g_{0}}_{i}f)+\left\langle\nabla^{g}u,\nabla^{g_{0}}f\right\rangle_{g}g_{ij}

(46)

\displaystyle=(\mathrm{Hess}_{g}f)_{ij}-2(\nabla^{g}u\otimes\nabla^{g}f)_{ij}+\left\langle du,df\right\rangle_{g}g_{ij}

Thus, we have

\displaystyle|\mathrm{Hess}_{g_{0}}f|_{g_{0}}

\displaystyle=e^{-2u}|\mathrm{Hess}_{g_{0}}f|_{g}

\displaystyle\leq e^{-2u}\Big(|\mathrm{Hess}_{g}f|_{g}+4|du|_{g}|df|_{g}\Big).

Consequently, it holds

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{B}}|\mathrm{Hess}_{g_{0}}J^{g_{0}}|_{g_{0}}^{\frac{2p}{p+1}}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g_{0}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \int_{\mathbb{B}}e^{4\left(1-\frac{p}{p+1}\right)u}\Big(|\mathrm{Hess}_{g}J^{g_{0}}|_{g}+4|du|_{g}|dJ^{g_{0}}|_{g}\Big)^{\frac{2p}{p+1}}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ 2^{\frac{2p}{p+1}}\int_{\mathbb{B}}e^{\frac{4u}{p+1}}|\mathrm{Hess}_{g}J^{g_{0}}|^{\frac{2p}{p+1}}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g}+2^{\frac{2p}{p+1}+2}\int_{\mathbb{B}}e^{\frac{4u}{p+1}}|du|_{g}^{\frac{2p}{p+1}}|dJ^{g_{0}}|_{g}^{\frac{2p}{p+1}}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ 2^{\frac{2p}{p+1}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{B}}e^{4u}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g}\right)^{\frac{1}{p+1}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{B}}|\mathrm{Hess}_{g}J^{g_{0}}|^{2}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g}\right)^{\frac{p}{p+1}}

\displaystyle+2^{\frac{2p}{p+1}+2}\left(\int_{\mathbb{B}}e^{4u}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g}\right)^{\frac{1}{p+1}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{B}}|du|^{2}_{g}|dJ^{g_{0}}|_{g}^{2}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g}\right)^{\frac{p}{p+1}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ c(p)\mathrm{vol}_{g}(\mathbb{B})^{\frac{1}{p+1}}\left(\|\mathrm{Hess}_{g}J^{g_{0}}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{B},g)}^{\frac{2p}{p+1}}+\|du\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{B},g)}^{\frac{2p}{p+1}}\|dJ^{g_{0}}\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{B},g)}^{\frac{2p}{p+1}}\right).

By assumption, we have
\mathrm{vol}_{g}(\mathbb{B})\leq\varepsilon
. By choosing
\varepsilon
small enough and using the conformal invariance of the
L^{4}
-norm of the gradient, we obtain

In this section, we prove that

Lemma 3.15

To prove

There exists
\varepsilon>0

\displaystyle\|\mathrm{Sch}^{g_{0}}\|_{L^{2p}(B_{g_{0}}(sx,s\theta))}

\Phi:\mathbb{R}^{4}\setminus\{*\}\to\mathbb{R}^{4}\setminus\{0\}

.

Remark 3.18
.

Here, we strongly use the fact that we are in dimension greater than 2, so that
\mathbb{R}^{4}\setminus\{0\}
is simply connected.

Proof.

Since
\mathrm{Riem}^{h_{0}}=0
, for every

\phi_{x}:U_{x}\to V_{x}

\phi_{x}

\gamma

[
44

, we define
\Phi(y)
to be the value at
y
of any analytic continuation of
\phi_{x}
from
x
to
y

X
is simply connected, thanks to
[
44

. Then, the map
\Phi:(\mathbb{R}^{4}\setminus\{0\},h_{0})\to(\mathbb{R}^{4},\xi)

(X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

\mathbb{R}^{4}\setminus\{0\}

each

is compact in
\mathbb{R}^{4}\setminus\{0\}

4.
Asymptotically flat and asymptotically locally Euclidean manifolds

In this section we are interested in non-compact Riemannian manifolds with a single end, which are asymptotically locally Euclidean. By this term, we mean the following definition:

Definition 4.1
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
\alpha\in(0,1)
. A Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
, non-compact with a single end, is said to be
C^{k,\alpha}
-asymptotically locally Euclidean (ALE) of order
\tau>0
if there exists a compact subset
K\subset M
,
R>0
and
\Gamma
is a finite group of
SO(n)
acting freely and a
C^{k+1,\alpha}
-diffeomorphism (coordinate chart at infinity)

\Phi:M\setminus K\to(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B(0,R))/\Gamma

such that the components of the metric in this chart,
g_{ij}
, satisfy:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}g_{ij}(z)=\delta_{ij}+O(|z|^{-\tau}),\\
\partial^{m}g_{ij}(z)=O(|z|^{-\tau-m}),\quad\forall m\in\{1,\dots,k\},\\
[\partial^{k}g_{ij}]_{\alpha}(z)=O(|z|^{-\tau-k-\alpha}),\end{cases}

where

[\partial^{k}g_{ij}]_{\alpha}(z)=\sup_{0<d_{g}(z,z^{\prime})\leq 1}\frac{|\partial^{k}g_{ij}(z)-\partial^{k}g_{ij}(z^{\prime})|}{|z-z^{\prime}|^{\alpha}}.

For brevity, we write:
g-\delta\in C^{k,\alpha}_{-\tau}(M)
. If
\Gamma=\{1\}
then
(M,g)
is said to be
C^{k,\alpha}
-asymptotically flat of order
\tau
.

In
Theorem

1.7
, Herzlich proves that a necessary condition for an ALE manifold
(M,g)
to have a
C^{0,\delta}
-compactification is the existence of some
1>\alpha\geq\eta>0
such that
W^{g}\in C^{0,\alpha}_{-2-\eta}(M)
and
\mathrm{Cot}^{g}\in C^{0,\alpha}_{-3-\eta}(M)
. Differentiating once more, a similar assumption concerning the Bach tensor would be
B^{g}\in C^{0,\alpha}_{-4-\eta}(M)
, which implies
B^{g}\in L^{p}
for some
p>1
.

In our case, we consider a metric
g
such that
g-\delta\in C^{1}_{\tau}(M)
for some
\tau>0
. Then we make the naive compactification, considering
h:=|x|^{4}\iota^{*}g
where
\iota:z\mapsto\frac{z}{|z|^{2}}
is the inversion. Hence , we have the following asymptotic expansion:

h_{ij}\underset{r\to 0}{=}\delta_{ij}+O\left(r^{\tau}\right),\quad\det h\underset{r\to 0}{=}1+o(1)\text{ and }\quad\Gamma_{h}\underset{r\to 0}{=}O\left(r^{\tau}\right),

where
\Gamma_{h}
are the Christoffel symbol of
h
. Thanks to those estimate, we can easily check that the assumptions 1 and 3 to 6 of theorem
1.8
are satisfied.

We now check the assumption
\mathrm{Riem}^{h}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{B},h)
. First, since the Weyl tensor is conformally invariant, we have

\displaystyle|W^{h}|_{h}^{2}\,\sqrt{\det h}\in L^{1}.

Let
\tilde{h}=\iota^{*}g
, then the Schouten tensor changes according to the formula (
32
):

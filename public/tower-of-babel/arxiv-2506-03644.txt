Relativistic recoil corrections of order 𝒎 ​ ( 𝒁 ​ 𝜶 ) 𝟔 ​ ( 𝒎 / 𝑴 ) m(Z\alpha)^{6}(m/M) : Hydrogen-like atom and hydrogen molecular ion.
V.I. Korobov

Abstract
The finite operators are derived for the recoil m ​ α 6 ​ ( m / M ) m\alpha^{6}(m/M) order relativistic corrections (to spin-averaged energy levels) in hydrogen-like atoms and ions in the two- and three-body formalism beyond the adiabatic approximation. Results are presented in a form suitable for numerical evaluation.



I
Introduction

The important contribution in the calculations of the ro-vibrational state of the hydrogen molecular ions is the relativistic corrections of order
m\alpha^{6}
, so far these corrections were performed in the adiabatic approximation
Korobov08
;
Korobov21
and without taking into account recoil. In
Zhong18
general formulas for the
m\alpha^{6}
and
m\alpha^{6}(m/M)
order corrections were derived, but without elimination of singularities.

Recoil correction appears due to the finiteness of the nuclear mass. Since the electron-to-nucleus mass ratio,
m/M
, is small for hydrogen atom (or hydrogen molecular ion), it is convenient to expand the recoil corrections in terms of
m/M
expansion. The study of mass recoil corrections has a long hystory: the Bethe-Salpeter equations were used in
Salpeter52
, then the modified Dirac equation was widely employed
GrotchYennie
, and in recent years the expansion in powers of
m/M
has been intensively developed
Shabaev85
;
Yelkhovsky94
;
Pachucki95
;
Melnikov02
.

In our previous work
Korobov25
we considered the leading
m\alpha^{6}
order relativistic corrections in the expansion in
m/M
and derived a set of finite (integrable) operators needed to numerically calculate this contribution. The goal of this paper is to continue our study and extend our approach to the next order
m\alpha^{6}(m/M)
to obtain a new set of finite operators that are needed to compute the leading recoil contribution. For simplicity, we will omit
Z
from our notations of orders in the expansion in
(Z\alpha)^{n}
. We use the effective Hamiltonian approach derived from the nonrelativistic quantum electrodynamics (NRQED).

In our derivation, we closely follow and compare our results with those based on the dimensionally regularized NRQED
YPP16
obtained for the case of helium atom. This allows us to reliably check the final formulas in our approach.

The paper is organized as follows: in Sec. II, we formulate the problem for recoil corrections of order
m\alpha^{6}(m/M)
for thecase of hydrogen-like atoms. In Sec. III, we show how the divergent terms can be separated out from the regular part, and then in Sec. IV we define the high-energy contribution expressed as a
\delta
-function operator and show how it depends on the choice of regularization. In Sec. V we present our new results for obtaining the recoil correction of order
m\alpha^{6}(m/M)
for the case of hydrogen molecular ions. In the Appendix we use the dimensionally regularized NRQED to rigorously derive from scratch the relativistic recoil corrections of order
m\alpha^{6}(m/M)
in the hydrogen atom.

Atomic units (
\hbar=m=e=1
) are used throughout. It is assumed that
M\gg m
.

II
Generalities

II.1
Nonrelativistic Hamiltonian

The nonrelativistic Hamiltonian (
\mathbf{r}=\mathbf{r}_{e}\!-\!\mathbf{R}
) for the hydrogen-like two-body system may be written:

H_{0}=\frac{\mathbf{P}^{2}}{2M}+\frac{\mathbf{p}_{e}^{2}}{2m}-\frac{Ze^{2}}{4\pi r}=\frac{\mathbf{p}^{2}}{2\mu}-\frac{Z}{r}=\frac{\mathbf{p}^{2}}{2\mu}+V,\qquad\mathbf{p}_{e}=\mathbf{p}=-\mathbf{P},

(1)

here
\mu
is the reduced mass and
\mu=mM/(m+M)=m\,(1-\frac{m}{M}+\dots)
.

II.2
The leading order relativistic corrections of order
m\alpha^{4}
.

Next order relativistic correction is expressed in terms of the Breit-Pauli Hamiltonian:

\begin{array}[]{@{}l}\displaystyle H^{(4)}=H_{B}+H_{ret},\\[8.53581pt]
\displaystyle H_{B}=-\frac{p^{4}}{8m^{3}}+\frac{[\Delta V]}{8m^{2}},\\[8.53581pt]
\displaystyle H_{ret}=-\frac{Z}{mM}\left(p^{i}\mathcal{W}^{ij}p^{j}\right),\end{array}

(2)

we include here only terms, which are required for calculation of the spin-averaged energy, here we use the notation:

\mathcal{W}^{ij}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\delta^{ij}}{r}+\frac{r^{i}r^{j}}{r^{3}}\right).

(3)

Eq. (
2
) will be used to write down the second-order contribution to the
m\alpha^{6}(m/M)
order correction.

II.3
Interactions at
m\alpha^{6}
order.

NRQED diagrams for the recoil interactions at order

The effective Hamiltonian for the relativistic recoil is a sum of four contributions determined by the NRQED diagrams shown on Fig.
1

H^{(6)}_{rec}=H_{a}+H_{b}+H_{c}+H_{d}.

(4)

Among them are the relativistic correction to the transverse photon dipole vertex,
H_{a}
(diagram
a
, Fig.
1
); retarded transverse photon exchange,
H_{b}
(diagram
b
, Fig.
1
); the double transverse photon exchange with the seagull vertex on the nucleus,
\frac{Z^{2}}{2M}\mathbf{A}^{2}
, and the dipole,
-\frac{1}{m}\mathbf{p}\mathbf{A}
, and spin-orbit,
-\frac{1}{2m}\boldmath{\sigma}_{e}\mathbf{B}
, vertices on the electron line, Hamiltonians
H_{c}
,
H_{d}
(diagrams
c
and
d
, Fig.
1
) respectively:

\begin{array}[]{@{}l}\displaystyle H_{a}=\frac{Z}{8m^{2}}\left\{p^{2},\frac{p^{i}}{m}\left(\frac{\delta^{ij}}{r}+\frac{r^{i}r^{j}}{r^{3}}\right)\frac{p^{j}}{M}\right\},\\[8.53581pt]
\displaystyle H_{b}=\frac{Z}{8mM}\left(\Bigl[V,p^{j}\Bigr]\frac{r^{i}r^{j}\!-\!3\delta^{ij}r^{2}}{r}+p^{j}\left[\frac{p^{2}}{2\mu},\frac{r^{i}r^{j}\!-\!3\delta^{ij}r^{2}}{r}\right]\right)\Bigl[V,p^{i}\Bigr],\\[8.53581pt]
\displaystyle H_{c}=\frac{Z^{2}}{8m^{2}M}\left(\mathbf{p}\frac{1}{r^{2}}\mathbf{p}+3\frac{(\mathbf{pr})(\mathbf{rp})}{r^{4}}\right),\\[8.53581pt]
\displaystyle H_{d}=\frac{1}{4m^{2}M}\>\frac{Z^{2}}{r^{4}}\>.\end{array}

(5)

The sum,
H^{(6)}_{rec}
, exactly matches the Hamiltonian from
YPP16
, Eq. (D22), except for the
\delta
-function term:
\frac{Z^{3}}{2}\pi\delta(\mathbf{r})
.

The second-order contribution to this order is

E_{rec}^{(6)}=2\alpha^{4}\left\langle H_{B}\,Q(E_{0}-H_{0})^{-1}Q\,H_{ret}\right\rangle.

(6)

It is divergent and it should be rewritten:

\begin{array}[]{@{}l}\displaystyle 2\left\langle H_{B}\,Q(E_{0}-H_{0})^{-1}Q\,H_{ret}\right\rangle=2\left\langle H^{\prime}_{B}\,Q(E_{0}-H_{0})^{-1}Q\,H_{ret}\right\rangle+2c\left\langle VH_{ret}\right\rangle-2c\left\langle V\right\rangle\left\langle H_{ret}\right\rangle\\[8.53581pt]
\displaystyle\hskip 71.13188pt=2\left\langle H^{\prime}_{B}\,Q(E_{0}-H_{0})^{-1}Q\,H_{ret}\right\rangle-\frac{1}{2}\left\langle VH_{ret}\right\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\left\langle V\right\rangle\left\langle H_{ret}\right\rangle,\end{array}

(7)

where

H^{\prime}_{B}=H_{B}-c(E_{0}\!-\!H_{0})V-cV(E_{0}\!-\!H_{0}),\qquad c=-\frac{\mu(2\mu\!-\!m)}{4m^{3}}\approx-\frac{1}{4m}\,.

(8)

Only the second term in the last line of Eq. (
7
) diverges. It can be viewed as a new term to be added to the effective Hamiltonian (
5
).

III
Separation of singular part

Before starting this section we want to introduce the notation:

p^{2}_{\mu}=p^{2}\!+\!2\mu V.

It is convenient for our derivations, since
p_{\mu}^{2}\psi_{0}
is a regular function. Here
\psi_{0}
is the solution of the stationary Schrödinger equation,

(H_{0}-E_{0})\psi_{0}=0.

(9)

Now let us consider the operators from the effective Hamiltonian in more detail.

III.1
Relativistic correction to the transverse photon dipole vertex,
H_{a}

H_{a}=\frac{Z}{4m^{3}M}\left\{p^{2},\left[p^{i}\mathcal{W}^{ij}p^{j}\right]\right\}=\frac{Z^{2}\mu}{2m^{2}}\left\{V,H_{ret}\right\}-\frac{1}{4m^{2}}\left\{p^{2}_{\mu},H_{ret}\right\}.

(10)

We want to note that the second term is finite. Curly brackets denote:
\{A,B\}=AB+B^{*}\!A^{*}
. Then

\begin{array}[]{@{}l}\displaystyle VH_{ret}=-\frac{1}{2mM}\>V\left[p^{i}\left(\frac{\delta^{ij}}{r}+\frac{r^{i}r^{j}}{r^{3}}\right)p^{j}\right]=\\[8.53581pt]
\displaystyle\hskip 39.83385pt=\frac{1}{2mM}\left[\nabla^{i},\frac{1}{r}\right]\left(\frac{\delta^{ij}}{r}+\frac{r^{i}r^{j}}{r^{3}}\right)\nabla^{j}-\frac{1}{2mM}\left[\nabla^{i}\left(\frac{\delta^{ij}}{r^{2}}+\frac{r^{i}r^{j}}{r^{4}}\right)\nabla^{j}\right]\\[8.53581pt]
\displaystyle\hskip 39.83385pt=-\frac{1}{mM}\>\frac{1}{r^{4}}\left(r^{i}\nabla^{i}\right)+\frac{1}{2mM}\left[p^{i}\left(\frac{\delta^{ij}}{r^{2}}+\frac{r^{i}r^{j}}{r^{4}}\right)p^{j}\right].\end{array}

Again the second term is finite. The first term may be rewritten
Korobov25
:

\left\langle\frac{Z^{2}}{r^{4}}\left(r^{i}\nabla^{i}\right)\right\rangle=\left\langle V\boldsymbol{\mathcal{E}}\boldsymbol{\nabla}\right\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\left[4\pi\left\langle V\rho\right\rangle+\left\langle\boldsymbol{\mathcal{E}}^{2}\right\rangle\right],

where
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{E}}=-\boldsymbol{\nabla}V
and
\Delta V=4\pi\rho
.
The final expression for this contribution is

\bigl\langle H_{a}\bigr\rangle=-\frac{\mu}{2m^{3}M}\left[4\pi\left\langle V\rho\right\rangle+\left\langle\boldsymbol{\mathcal{E}}^{2}\right\rangle\right]+\frac{Z^{2}\mu}{2m^{3}M}\left\langle\left[p^{i}\left(\frac{\delta^{ij}}{r^{2}}+\frac{r^{i}r^{j}}{r^{4}}\right)p^{j}\right]\right\rangle-\frac{Z}{m^{2}}\left\langle p^{2}_{\mu}H_{ret}\right\rangle.

(11)

It should be compared with Eq. (D23) from
YPP16
.

III.2
Retarded transverse photon exchange,
H_{b}

H_{b}=\frac{Z}{8mM}\Bigl[V,p^{i}\Bigr]\left(\Bigl[V,p^{j}\Bigr]\frac{r^{i}r^{j}\!-\!3\delta^{ij}r^{2}}{r}+\left[\frac{p^{2}}{2\mu},\frac{r^{i}r^{j}\!-\!3\delta^{ij}r^{2}}{r}\right]p^{j}\right)

(12)

It may be splitted on two parts.

The first part is expressed:

H_{b,1}=\frac{Z}{8mM}\Bigl[V,p^{i}\Bigr]\frac{r^{i}r^{j}\!-\!3\delta^{ij}r^{2}}{r}\Bigl[V,p^{j}\Bigr]=-\frac{Z^{3}}{8mM}\frac{r^{i}}{r^{3}}\;\frac{r^{i}r^{j}\!-\!3\delta^{ij}r^{2}}{r}\;\frac{r^{j}}{r^{3}}=\frac{1}{4mM}\frac{Z^{3}}{r^{3}}.

(13)

Formally, it includes two regularized potentials:
V_{reg}
, and
\left[\frac{r^{i}r^{j}\!-\!3\delta^{ij}r^{2}}{r}\right]_{reg}
, and (
13
) may be not equivalent to
V^{3}_{reg}
at
r=0
. In the dimensional regularization, see Eq. (A26) of Ref.
YPP16
, one gets the following identity for these operators:

\left\langle\frac{Z}{8}\Bigl[V_{\epsilon},p^{i}\Bigr]\left[\frac{r^{i}r^{j}\!-\!3\delta^{ij}r^{2}}{r}\right]_{\epsilon}\Bigl[V_{\epsilon},p^{j}\Bigr]\right\rangle_{\epsilon}=-\frac{1}{4}\left\langle V^{3}_{\epsilon}\right\rangle_{\epsilon}-\frac{7Z^{3}}{4}\pi\langle\delta(\mathbf{r})\rangle,

(14)

here
\langle\cdot\rangle_{\epsilon}
means that we use dimensionally regularized solution
\psi_{\epsilon}
when evaluating the expection value of the operator.

The second part is expressed:

H_{b,2}=\frac{Z}{8mM}\Bigl[V,p^{i}\Bigr]\left[\frac{p^{2}}{2\mu},\frac{r^{i}r^{j}\!-\!3\delta^{ij}r^{2}}{r}\right]p^{j}

(15)

then using the commutator:

\left[\frac{p^{2}}{2},\frac{r^{i}r^{j}-3r^{2}\delta^{ij}}{r}\right]=2\frac{r^{i}r^{j}}{r^{3}}+2\frac{\delta^{ij}}{r}-i\frac{r^{i}p^{j}+r^{j}p^{i}}{r}+i\frac{r^{i}r^{j}(\mathbf{rp})}{r^{3}}+3i\frac{\delta^{ij}(\mathbf{rp})}{r}

we get:

H_{b,2}=\frac{Z^{2}}{4\mu mM}\frac{1}{r^{4}}(r^{i}\nabla^{i})+\frac{Z^{2}}{8\mu mM}\left(p^{i}\left(\frac{\delta^{ij}}{r^{2}}-3\frac{r^{i}r^{j}}{r^{4}}\right)p^{j}\right).

(16)

Summing up, one gets:

H_{b}=\frac{1}{4mM}\frac{Z^{3}}{r^{3}}+\frac{1}{8\mu mM}\left[4\pi V\rho+\boldsymbol{\mathcal{E}}^{2}\right]+\frac{Z^{2}}{8\mu mM}\left(p^{i}\left(\frac{\delta^{ij}}{r^{2}}-3\frac{r^{i}r^{j}}{r^{4}}\right)p^{j}\right).

(17)

Except for the
\delta
-function term, it exactly matches Eq. (D24) from
YPP16
.

At this point we want to make a comment and suggestion. As seen from the above discussion, the final expressions should depend on the choice of the regularization. On the other hand, this only changes the part that depends on distributions of the
\delta
-function type or, in other words, distributions, which are equal to zero outside the domain
r=0
. Thus, we can ignore for a while the
\delta
-function type contributions in these formulas and finally reconstruct them by comparing with the known result for the
1S
state of the hydrogen atom.

III.3
Two-photon exchange with the seagull vertex,
H_{c}
,
H_{d}

Here
H_{c}
is finite and
H_{d}
is singular, the sum is

H_{c\text{-}d}=\frac{1}{4m^{2}M}\,\boldsymbol{\mathcal{E}}^{2}+\frac{Z^{2}}{8m^{2}M}\left(p^{i}\left(\frac{\delta^{ij}}{r^{2}}+3\frac{r^{i}r^{j}}{r^{4}}\right)p^{j}\right).

(18)

III.4
Second-order contribution. Modification to the effective Hamiltonian

Next contribution comes from the Breit-Pauli Hamiltonian (
2
) as the second-order perturbation term

\Delta E_{2nd-order}=\alpha^{4}\Bigl[2\left\langle H^{\prime}_{B}\,Q(E_{0}-H_{0})^{-1}Q\,H_{ret}\right\rangle+2c\left\langle VH_{ret}\right\rangle-2c\left\langle V\right\rangle\left\langle H_{ret}\right\rangle\Bigr],

(19)

and the second term is expressed

H^{(6m)}_{rec}=c\left\{V,H_{ret}\right\}=-\frac{c}{mM}\left[4\pi V\rho+\boldsymbol{\mathcal{E}}^{2}\right]+\frac{Z^{2}c}{mM}\left[p^{i}\left(\frac{\delta^{ij}}{r^{2}}+\frac{r^{i}r^{j}}{r^{4}}\right)p^{j}\right].

(20)

III.5
The total recoil contribution at order
m\alpha^{6}(m/M)

We assume here that
m=\mu=1
. Then the final expression becomes

\begin{array}[]{@{}l}\displaystyle H_{a\text{-}d}+H^{(6m)}_{rec}=-\frac{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{E}}^{2}}{2M}+\frac{Z^{2}}{2M}\left(\mathbf{p}\frac{1}{r^{2}}\mathbf{p}+\frac{(\mathbf{pr})(\mathbf{rp})}{r^{4}}\right)-\frac{1}{2}\Bigl\{p^{2}_{\mu},H_{ret}\Bigr\}\\[8.53581pt]
\displaystyle\hskip 99.58464pt+\frac{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{E}}^{2}}{8M}-\frac{V^{3}}{4M}+\frac{Z^{2}}{8M}\left(\mathbf{p}\frac{1}{r^{2}}\mathbf{p}-3\frac{(\mathbf{pr})(\mathbf{rp})}{r^{4}}\right)\\[8.53581pt]
\displaystyle\hskip 99.58464pt+\frac{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{E}}^{2}}{4M}+\frac{Z^{2}}{8M}\left(\mathbf{p}\frac{1}{r^{2}}\mathbf{p}+3\frac{(\mathbf{pr})(\mathbf{rp})}{r^{4}}\right)\\[8.53581pt]
\displaystyle\hskip 99.58464pt+\frac{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{E}}^{2}}{4M}-\frac{Z^{2}}{4M}\left(\mathbf{p}\frac{1}{r^{2}}\mathbf{p}+\frac{(\mathbf{pr})(\mathbf{rp})}{r^{4}}\right)\\[8.53581pt]
\displaystyle\hskip 25.60747pt=\frac{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{E}}^{2}}{8M}-\frac{V^{3}}{4M}+\frac{Z^{2}}{4M}\>\mathbf{p}\frac{1}{r^{2}}\mathbf{p}+\frac{Z^{2}}{4M}\left(\mathbf{p}\frac{1}{r^{2}}\mathbf{p}+\frac{(\mathbf{pr})(\mathbf{rp})}{r^{4}}\right)-\frac{1}{2}\Bigl\{p^{2}_{\mu},H_{ret}\Bigr\}+C\,\frac{Z^{3}}{M}\,\pi\delta(\mathbf{r}).\end{array}

(21)

In the last line we have introduced a still unknown term determined by the
\delta
-function distribution. The coefficient
C
has to be identified. The total contribution to the energy is

\Delta E^{(6)}_{rec}=\alpha^{4}\bigl\langle H_{a\text{-}d}+H^{(6m)}_{rec}+H_{H}\bigr\rangle+2\alpha^{4}\left\langle H^{\prime}_{B}\,Q(E_{0}-H_{0})^{-1}Q\,H_{ret}\right\rangle+\frac{\alpha^{4}}{2}\left\langle V\right\rangle\left\langle H_{ret}\right\rangle,

(22)

where

H_{H}=C\,\frac{Z^{3}}{M}\,\pi\delta(\mathbf{r}).

So far we never used that
\psi_{0}
is the wave function for the hydrogen bound state, thus the same derivation may be used in the case of the hydrogen molecular ion as well.

IV
Comparison with the
1S
state result and determination of
C

Now we may find the coefficient
C
. For that we need the expectation values for various operators for the
nS
states of the hydrogen atom:

\begin{array}[]{@{}l}\displaystyle\left\langle p^{i}\,\mathcal{W}^{ij}p^{j}\right\rangle=\frac{Z^{3}}{n^{3}}\left(2-\frac{1}{n}\right),\qquad\left\langle\mathbf{p}\,\frac{1}{r^{2}}\,\mathbf{p}\right\rangle=\left\langle p^{i}\frac{\mathcal{W}^{ij}}{r}p^{j}\right\rangle=\frac{Z^{4}}{n^{3}}\,\left[\frac{8}{3}-\frac{2}{3n^{2}}\right],\\[11.38109pt]
\displaystyle 2\left\langle H^{\prime}_{B}\,Q(E_{0}\!-\!H_{0})^{-1}Q\,H_{ret}\right\rangle=-\frac{m^{2}(Z\alpha)^{6}}{Mn^{3}}\left(\frac{11}{3}+\frac{3}{n}-\frac{37}{6n^{2}}+\frac{2}{n^{3}}\right).\end{array}

(23)

Using the regularized form for the two divergent integrals:

\begin{array}[]{@{}l}\displaystyle\left\langle\frac{1}{r^{3}}\right\rangle_{\!s}=\lim_{r_{0}\to 0}\left\{\left\langle\frac{1}{r^{3}}\right\rangle_{\!\!r_{0}}\!+\left(\ln{r_{0}}\!+\!\gamma_{E}\right)\left\langle 4\pi\delta(\mathbf{r})\right\rangle\right\}=\\[8.53581pt]
\displaystyle\hskip 113.81102pt=-4\pi\langle\delta(\mathbf{r})\rangle\left[\ln(2Z)+\psi(n)-\psi(1)-\ln{n}-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2n}\right],\\[8.53581pt]
\displaystyle\left\langle\frac{1}{r^{4}}\right\rangle_{\!s}=\lim_{r_{0}\to 0}\left\{\left\langle\frac{1}{r^{4}}\right\rangle_{\!\!r_{0}}\!-\left[\frac{1}{r_{0}}\left\langle 4\pi\delta(\mathbf{r})\right\rangle+\left(\ln{r_{0}}\!+\!\gamma_{E}\right)\left\langle 4\pi\delta^{\prime}(\mathbf{r})\right\rangle\right]\right\}=\\[8.53581pt]
\displaystyle\hskip 113.81102pt=8\pi\langle\delta(\mathbf{r})\rangle\left[\ln(2Z)+\psi(n)-\psi(1)-\ln{n}-\frac{5}{3}+\frac{1}{2n}+\frac{1}{6n^{2}}\right].\end{array}

(24)

we will get the final expression for the energy shift due to the recoil contribution:

\Delta E^{(6)}_{rec}=\frac{4Z^{6}\alpha^{4}}{Mn^{3}}\left(-\frac{3}{8}+\frac{3}{8n}-\frac{1}{n^{2}}+\frac{1}{2n^{3}}\right)

That should be compared with the ground state result for the pure recoil correction
Pachucki95
:

\Delta E_{rec}^{(6)}(1S)=\frac{Z^{6}\alpha^{4}}{M}\left(4\ln{2}-\frac{7}{2}\right).

(25)

This gives the value for the unknown coefficient
C_{s}=4\ln{2}-3
, where the subscript
s
stands for the coordinate cut-off regularization
Korobov25
.
In case of the dimensional regularization we get:
C_{\epsilon}=4\ln{2}-9/2
, and then our expression (
21
)-(
22
) exactly matches equation (D26) from
YPP16
.

V
Recoil corrections for the hydrogen molecular ion.

V.1
Nonrelativistic Hamiltonian

The nonrelativistic Hamiltonian for the hydrogen molecular ion in the center-of-mass frame (here
\mathbf{r}_{a}=\mathbf{r}_{e}\!-\!\mathbf{R}_{a}
for
a=(1,2)
) is defined

H_{0}=\frac{\mathbf{P}_{1}^{2}}{2M_{1}}+\frac{\mathbf{P}_{2}^{2}}{2M_{2}}+\frac{\mathbf{p}_{e}^{2}}{2m}-\frac{Z_{1}}{r_{1}}-\frac{Z_{2}}{r_{2}}+\frac{Z_{1}Z_{2}}{R}=\frac{\mathbf{p}_{1}^{2}}{2\mu_{1}}+\frac{\mathbf{p}_{2}^{2}}{2\mu_{2}}+\frac{\mathbf{p}_{1}\mathbf{p}_{2}}{m}+V_{1}+V_{2}+V_{12},

(26)

where
\mu_{1}^{-1}=M_{1}^{-1}\!+\!m^{-1}
,
\mu_{2}^{-1}=M_{2}^{-1}\!+\!m^{-1}
are reduced masses of two nuclei,
\mathbf{p}_{e}=\mathbf{p}_{1}\!+\!\mathbf{p}_{2}
,
\mathbf{P}_{1}=-\mathbf{p}_{1}
, and
\mathbf{P}_{2}=-\mathbf{p}_{2}
. We also use the following notation
V_{e}=V_{1}\!+\!V_{2}
for the electron potential energy.

The part of the Breit-Pauli Hamiltonian, required for the spin-averaged recoil correction to the energy, now is expressed:

\begin{array}[]{@{}l}\displaystyle H_{B}=-\frac{p^{4}_{e}}{8m^{3}}+\frac{[\Delta V_{e}]}{8m^{2}},\\[8.53581pt]
\displaystyle H_{ret}=\frac{Z_{1}}{mM_{1}}\left(p_{e}^{i}\mathcal{W}_{1}^{ij}P_{1}^{j}\right)+\frac{Z_{2}}{mM_{2}}\left(p_{e}^{i}\mathcal{W}_{2}^{ij}P_{2}^{j}\right),\\[8.53581pt]
\displaystyle H_{so}=\frac{Z_{1}}{2m^{2}}\,\frac{[\mathbf{r}_{1}\!\times\!\mathbf{p}_{e}]}{r_{1}^{3}}\,\mathbf{s}_{e}-\frac{Z_{1}}{mM_{1}}\,\frac{[\mathbf{r}_{1}\!\times\!\mathbf{P}_{1}]}{r_{1}^{3}}\,\mathbf{s}_{e}+\frac{Z_{2}}{2m^{2}}\,\frac{[\mathbf{r}_{2}\!\times\!\mathbf{p}_{e}]}{r_{2}^{3}}\,\mathbf{s}_{e}-\frac{Z_{2}}{mM_{2}}\,\frac{[\mathbf{r}_{2}\!\times\!\mathbf{P}_{2}]}{r_{2}^{3}}\,\mathbf{s}_{e}.\end{array}

(27)

We use as well the following notation:

p^{2}_{\mu}=p^{2}_{e}+2V_{\mu},\qquad V_{\mu}=\mu_{1}V_{1}+\mu_{2}V_{2}.

(28)

and it is easy to see that
p_{\mu}^{2}\psi_{0}
is a regular function, when
\psi_{0}
is a solution of the nonrelativistic Schrödinger equation.

V.2
The effective Hamiltonian in
m\alpha^{6}(m/M)
order

The recoil effective Hamiltonian is
Korobov20

\begin{array}[]{@{}l}\displaystyle H_{rec}^{(6)}=H_{a}+H_{b}+H_{c}+H_{d}+H_{H},\\[8.53581pt]
\displaystyle H_{a}=-\frac{1}{4m^{2}}\left\{p_{e}^{2},H_{ret}\right\}=\frac{Z^{2}\mu}{2m^{2}}\left\{V_{\mu},H_{ret}\right\}-\frac{1}{4m^{2}}\left\{p^{2}_{\mu},H_{ret}\right\},\\[8.53581pt]
\displaystyle H_{b}=\frac{1}{4m}\left[\frac{Z_{1}^{3}}{M_{1}}\frac{1}{r_{1}^{3}}+\frac{Z_{2}^{3}}{M_{2}}\frac{1}{r_{2}^{3}}+\frac{Z_{1}^{2}Z_{2}}{M_{1}}\frac{(\mathbf{r}_{1}\mathbf{r}_{2})}{r_{1}^{2}r_{2}^{3}}+\frac{Z_{1}Z_{2}^{2}}{M_{2}}\frac{(\mathbf{r}_{1}\mathbf{r}_{2})}{r_{1}^{3}r_{2}^{2}}\right]\\[8.53581pt]
\hskip 22.76219pt\displaystyle+\frac{Z_{1}^{2}}{8m^{2}M_{1}}\left[\frac{1}{r_{1}^{4}}+\mathbf{p}_{e}\frac{1}{r_{1}^{2}}\mathbf{p}_{e}-3\frac{(\mathbf{p}_{e}\mathbf{r}_{1})(\mathbf{r}_{1}\mathbf{p}_{e})}{r_{1}^{4}}\right]+\frac{Z_{2}^{2}}{8m^{2}M_{2}}\left[\frac{1}{r_{2}^{4}}+\mathbf{p}_{e}\frac{1}{r_{2}^{2}}\mathbf{p}_{e}-3\frac{(\mathbf{p}_{e}\mathbf{r}_{2})(\mathbf{r}_{2}\mathbf{p}_{e})}{r_{2}^{4}}\right],\\[8.53581pt]
\displaystyle H_{c}=\frac{Z_{1}^{2}}{8m^{2}M_{1}}\left[\mathbf{p}_{e}\frac{1}{r_{1}^{2}}\mathbf{p}_{e}+3\frac{(\mathbf{p}_{e}\mathbf{r}_{1})(\mathbf{r}_{1}\mathbf{p}_{e})}{r_{1}^{4}}\right]+\frac{Z_{2}^{2}}{8m^{2}M_{2}}\left[\mathbf{p}_{e}\frac{1}{r_{2}^{2}}\mathbf{p}_{e}+3\frac{(\mathbf{p}_{e}\mathbf{r}_{2})(\mathbf{r}_{2}\mathbf{p}_{e})}{r_{2}^{4}}\right],\\[8.53581pt]
\displaystyle H_{d}=\frac{Z_{1}^{2}}{4m^{2}M_{1}}\>\frac{1}{r_{1}^{4}}+\frac{Z_{2}^{2}}{4m^{2}M_{2}}\>\frac{1}{r_{2}^{4}}\>.\end{array}

(29)

The explicit expression for the
\{V_{\mu},H_{ret}\}
operator (
\mu=1
) may be written

\begin{array}[]{@{}l}\displaystyle\left\langle V_{\mu}H_{ret}\right\rangle=\left\langle V_{1}H_{ret,1}\right\rangle+\left\langle V_{2}H_{ret,2}\right\rangle+\left\langle V_{1}H_{ret,2}\right\rangle+\left\langle V_{2}H_{ret,1}\right\rangle,\\[8.53581pt]
\displaystyle\left\langle V_{1}H_{ret,1}\right\rangle=-\frac{1}{2M}\langle\boldsymbol{\mathcal{E}}^{2}\rangle-\frac{Z^{2}}{2M}\left\langle p_{e}^{i}\frac{W_{1}^{ij}}{r_{1}}P_{1}^{j}\right\rangle.\end{array}

(30)

The cross terms are finite and can thus be calculated numerically. We recall here that we ignore the
\delta
-function terms in the intermediate formulas.

The effective Hamiltonian (
29
) is incomplete, because it does not include contributions from the hard photon energy region. We use the regularized form for the singular operators as in Eq. (
24
), and thus the high-energy Hamiltonian should be chosen in the form

H_{H}=\Bigl(4\ln{2}-3\Bigr)\frac{Z^{3}}{M}\,\Bigl(\pi\delta(\mathbf{r}_{1})+\pi\delta(\mathbf{r}_{2})\Bigr).

(31)

Then the second-order contribution is expressed

\begin{array}[]{@{}l}\displaystyle\Delta E_{rec}^{2^{nd}-order}=\Delta E_{ret}+\Delta E_{so\hbox{-}so}^{(0)},\\[8.53581pt]
\displaystyle\Delta E_{ret}=2\alpha^{2}\left\langle H_{B}\,Q(E_{0}-H_{0})^{-1}Q\,H_{ret}\right\rangle,\\[8.53581pt]
\displaystyle\Delta E_{so\hbox{-}so}^{(0)}=\alpha^{2}\left\langle H_{so}Q(E_{0}-H_{0})^{-1}QH_{so}\right\rangle^{(0)}.\end{array}

(32)

The last one is the spin-orbit scalar contribution, since
H_{so}
is a vector operator, the second-order term has contributions of rank 0, 1, and 2. Its mean value is finite.

V.3
Modifications to the effective Hamiltonian resulted from the second-order contributions

Since
\Delta E_{ret}
is divergent, it should be transformed as follows:

\begin{array}[]{@{}l}\displaystyle\left\langle H_{B}\,Q(E_{0}-H_{0})^{-1}Q\,H_{ret}\right\rangle=\left\langle H^{\prime}_{B}\,Q(E_{0}-H_{0})^{-1}Q\,H_{ret}\right\rangle-\frac{1}{2}\left\langle V_{e}H_{ret}\right\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\left\langle V_{e}\right\rangle\left\langle H_{ret}\right\rangle\end{array}

(33)

Additional term, which should be added to the effective Hamiltonian, has the form

\begin{array}[]{@{}l}\displaystyle H_{rec}^{(6m)}=-\frac{1}{4}\left\{V_{e},H_{ret}\right\}.\end{array}

(34)

V.4
Recoil energy shift via finite operators

Now we are ready to express the recoil correction in terms of the finite-value operators.

The total sum of the effective Hamiltonian
H_{rec}^{(6)}
and
H_{rec}^{(6m)}
is expressed (
m=1
):

\begin{array}[]{@{}l}\displaystyle\left\langle H_{rec}^{(6)}+H^{(6m)}_{rec}\right\rangle=\frac{1}{8}\left\langle\frac{Z_{1}^{2}}{M_{1}}\>\frac{1}{r_{1}^{4}}+\frac{Z_{2}^{2}}{M_{2}}\>\frac{1}{r_{2}^{4}}\right\rangle+\frac{1}{4}\left\langle\frac{Z_{1}^{3}}{M_{1}}\frac{1}{r_{1}^{3}}+\frac{Z_{2}^{3}}{M_{2}}\frac{1}{r_{2}^{3}}\right\rangle\\[8.53581pt]
\hskip 56.9055pt\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\left\langle p^{2}_{\mu}H_{ret}\right\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\left\langle V_{1}H_{ret,2}\right\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\left\langle V_{2}H_{ret,1}\right\rangle\\[8.53581pt]
\hskip 56.9055pt\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4}\left\langle\frac{Z_{1}^{2}Z_{2}}{M_{1}}\frac{(\mathbf{r}_{1}\mathbf{r}_{2})}{r_{1}^{2}r_{2}^{3}}+\frac{Z_{1}Z_{2}^{2}}{M_{2}}\frac{(\mathbf{r}_{1}\mathbf{r}_{2})}{r_{1}^{3}r_{2}^{2}}\right\rangle\\[8.53581pt]
\hskip 56.9055pt\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4}\left\langle\frac{Z_{1}^{2}}{M_{1}}\,\mathbf{p}_{e}\frac{1}{r_{1}^{2}}\mathbf{p}_{e}+\frac{Z_{2}^{2}}{M_{2}}\,\mathbf{p}_{e}\frac{1}{r_{2}^{2}}\mathbf{p}_{e}\right\rangle-\frac{1}{4}\left\langle\frac{Z_{1}^{2}}{M_{1}}\,\mathbf{p}_{e}\frac{W_{1}^{ij}}{r_{1}}\mathbf{P}_{1}+\frac{Z_{2}^{2}}{M_{2}}\,\mathbf{p}_{e}\frac{W_{2}^{ij}}{r_{2}}\mathbf{P}_{2}\right\rangle.\end{array}

(35)

Then we replace the expectaition values for the diverent operators
1/r^{3}
and
1/r^{4}
by the regularized integrals, as in Eq. (
24

35

\Delta E^{(6)}_{rec}=\alpha^{4}\bigl\langle H_{rec}^{(6)}+H^{(6m)}_{rec}+H_{H}\bigr\rangle+2\alpha^{4}\left\langle H^{\prime}_{B}\,Q(E_{0}\!-\!H_{0})^{-1}Q\,H_{ret}\right\rangle+\frac{\alpha^{4}}{2}\left\langle V_{e}\right\rangle\left\langle H_{ret}\right\rangle+\Delta E_{so\hbox{-}so}^{(0)}.

(36)

All the operators in the above expression have finite expectation values if the wave function is a solution of the nonrelativistic Schrödinger equation for some bound state and can now be calculated numerically.

Another way to achieve these results is to use the dimensional regularization. It is important to note that all the cross-terms of the effective Hamiltonian have finite expectation values and do not require regularization. Thus we may restrict consideration to the pairwise interactions only. Then using the derivation of the NRQED interaction potentials presented in the Appendix we will obtain the similar expressions as in (

H_{H}

VI
Summary

The set of finite operators written in equations (
35
)-(
36
), which allows one to numerically calculate the recoil correction of the order of
m(Z\alpha)^{6}(m/M)
for three-particle systems similar to the molecular hydrogen ion, is the main result of the present work. The only requirement for obtaining the correct answer is that we should use suitably regularized divergent integrals as defined in equation (
24
).

As shown in our previous work
Korobov25
, the combination of distributions

\left\langle\boldsymbol{\mathcal{E}}^{2}\right\rangle-2\mu\left\langle V^{3}\right\rangle+\left\langle V(4\pi\rho)\right\rangle

(38)

is finite, no matter which regularization scheme is used. That makes dimensional regularization preferable for rigorous derivation of the recoil, since in this case
\langle V(4\pi\rho)\rangle\equiv 0
. However, it is more convenient to express and calculate the divergent matrix elements
\langle[1/r^{3}]_{\epsilon}\rangle
and
\langle[1/r^{4}]_{\epsilon}\rangle
in terms of
\langle 1/r^{3}\rangle_{s}
and
\langle 1/r^{4}\rangle_{s}
distributions, thus, we will eventually arrive at the same set of expressions.

It is useful to note that the final expression for the effective Hamiltonian (
21
), or (
35
) contains a combination:
\langle 1/r^{4}\rangle+2\langle 1/r^{3}\rangle
, which can actually be replaced by a distribution
\langle V(4\pi\rho)\rangle
and a set of finite operators. In the case of the coordinate cut-off regularization and/or mass regularization,
\langle V(4\pi\rho)\rangle
contains a divergent part (see
Korobov25
), which is compensated by the contribution from the hard-scale energy region. For the case of mass regularization such a cancellation was demonstrated in
Pachucki95
.

We still have to make one more remark on numerical calculation of the operators appearing in Eq. (
35
). In particular, in order to compute
\langle p^{2}_{\mu}H_{ret}\rangle
we can use that
p_{\mu}^{2}\psi_{0}
is a regular function. Therefore we can split the operator and represent it as a sum:

\langle p^{2}_{\mu}H_{ret}\rangle=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\langle\psi_{0}|p^{2}_{\mu}|\psi_{n}\rangle\langle\psi_{n}|H_{ret}|\psi_{0}\rangle,

(39)

where
\psi_{n}
is a finite set of orthogonal functions, spanning over finite subspace of the Hilbert space of operator
H_{0}
. When
N
goes to infinity, and
\psi_{n}
are a complete set of functions for the Hilbert space of the problem, then the sum should converge to exact value. This is very similar to what we do when calculating second-order contributions in perturbation theory.

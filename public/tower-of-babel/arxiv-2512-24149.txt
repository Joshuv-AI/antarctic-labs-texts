Large Emotional World Model
Changhao Song, Yazhou Zhang, Hui Gao, Chang Yang, Peng ZhangCollege of Intelligence and Computing, Tianjin University, Tianjin, China

Abstract
World Models serve as tools for understanding the current state of the world and predicting its future dynamics, with broad application potential across numerous fields. As a key component of world knowledge, emotion significantly influences human decision-making. While existing Large Language Models (LLMs) have shown preliminary capability in capturing world knowledge, they primarily focus on modeling physical-world regularities and lack systematic exploration of emotional factors. In this paper, we first demonstrate the importance of emotion in understanding the world by showing that removing emotionally relevant information degrades reasoning performance. Inspired by theory of mind, we further propose a L arge E motional W orld M odel (LEWM). Specifically, we construct the E motion- W hy- H ow (EWH) dataset, which integrates emotion into causal relationships and enables reasoning about why actions occur and how emotions drive future world states. Based on this dataset, LEWM explicitly models emotional states alongside visual observations and actions, allowing the world model to predict both future states and emotional transitions. Experimental results show that LEWM more accurately predicts emotion-driven social behaviors while maintaining comparable performance to general world models on basic tasks.



1
Introduction

World Models are designed to understand environments and predict their future states
3
. They function by constructing implicit representations from world knowledge and simulating possible evolutions, thereby supporting reasoning and decision-making
4
. Such models have found broad applications in autonomous driving, robotics, and social simulation, where they enhance perception, planning, and interactive capabilities.

Figure 1:
World model based solely on physics regularities fails to predict emotion-driven behavior.

Current World Models predominantly emphasize capturing physical regularities and environmental dynamics, such as Sora’s ability to generate visually realistic videos that adhere to basic physical principles
1
. However, real-world human behavior is often driven by social and emotional factors that are not fully encapsulated by physical or logical rules
8
. As illustrated in Fig
1
, an individual experiencing low mood may resort to impulsive spending to alleviate emotional distress—a behavior that contradicts rational cost-benefit reasoning and may lead to financial waste. This discrepancy between emotion-driven actions and logic-based predictions reveals a critical gap in existing world models, limiting their capacity to fully simulate the complexities of real-world social systems.

As recent studies have demonstrated, Large Language Models (LLMs) contain stable and traceable affective circuits that consistently drive emotional expressions across various contexts
10
. Moreover, these models encompass neuron groups that exert causal influence on emotional recognition and generation
6
. These findings indicate that affective mechanisms are not only present but also functionally active within LLMs. Based on this understanding, we design an emotion-filtering module as a preliminary validation experiment and incorporate it into a general base model. We train the module using a multi-task learning setup that simultaneously learns affective recognition and the rewriting of emotional texts into neutral ones. Our results confirm that affective signals can significantly influence model behavior, highlighting the potential of incorporating affective reasoning into world model.

Guided by theory-of-mind principles, we develop the Emotion-Why-How (EWH) dataset, which embeds emotion into structured causal relationships and provides paired state–action–emotion transitions. This design supports reasoning not only about why actions emerge under particular emotional conditions, but also how evolving emotional states influence subsequent world changes. Building on this foundation, we introduce the Large Emotion World Model (LEWM), a novel architectural framework that extends conventional world models by explicitly incorporating affective reasoning. Built upon EWH, LEWM jointly represents emotional states, visual observations, and human actions within a unified modeling paradigm, enabling simultaneous prediction of future states and emotional transitions. In doing so, LEWM strengthens affective reasoning capability while promoting a more coherent integration of emotional knowledge into world understanding.

We conducted an empirical evaluation of the proposed method across a series of emotional and non-emotional tasks, and compared its performance with several SoTA general world models. We highlight three key findings: (1) Incorporating an emotion-filtering module causes a marked decline in subjective tasks (up to 8%↓ in Acc), while it leads to a slight drop (approximately 1%↓) in objective reasoning and knowledge tasks, indicating that emotional context acts as a systematic modulator that shapes model behavior across diverse cognitive demands; (2) LEWM achieves promising results in emotion-driven behavior prediction, demonstrating that incorporating emotional reasoning into world models can enhance the accuracy of predicting real-world human actions; (3) LEWM performs comparably to general world models on basic tasks, which suggests that integrating emotional understanding does not compromise the fundamental performance of the model on general tasks.

Our main contributions are as follows:

Related Work

World models are computational frameworks that primarily serving two interconnected purposes: building implicit internal representations of the environment and predicting future states. The concept originates from early AI research on mental models and has been revitalized through model-based reinforcement learning (MBRL) and neural architectures that learn latent dynamics
3
. A significant evolution occurred with the rise of multimodal generative models, such as Sora, which demonstrate an emerging capacity to simulate realistic video sequences and physical interactions
1
. These models are applied across key domains like autonomous driving
2
, task planning
11
, and social simulacra
8
. We propose the Large Emotional World Model (LEWM), which enhances the accurate modeling of emotion-driven human behaviors by integrating emotional reasoning, thereby extending the applicability of world models to the understanding of complex social dynamics.

3
Preliminary

As a preliminary validation to demonstrate the influence of affective signals on model decision-making, we design an emotion-filtering module based on multi-task learning. The following subsection details this experiment. This module builds upon a pretrained world model
\mathcal{W}
with parameters
\theta_{\mathcal{W}}
and operates through a dual-task learning framework that simultaneously performs
affect recognition
and
affective content transformation
, as shown in Fig
2
. Formally, given an input text
\mathbf{x}=\{x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}\}
, the module learns to:

•

Classify whether
\mathbf{x}
contains emotional content using an
Affect Classifier

f_{\phi}
, parameterized by
\phi
, which maps the contextual representations of
\mathbf{x}
to a binary emotionality label;

•

Generate a corresponding neutralized version
\mathbf{y}=\{y_{1},y_{2},\ldots,y_{m}\}
that preserves semantic content while removing affective expressions, facilitated by an
Affect Adapter

g_{\psi}
, parameterized by
\psi
, which transforms the latent representations to achieve emotional filtering.

Figure 2:
The world model integrated with an emotion-filtering module is trained using a two-stage approach.

The classification task employs a standard cross-entropy loss
\mathcal{L}_{cls}
, while the neutralization task is formulated as a conditional text generation problem. Given an emotional text
\mathbf{x}
with emotion label
y_{e}
, the model learns to generate its neutral counterpart
\mathbf{x_{n}}
through maximum likelihood estimation:

\mathcal{L}_{gen}=-\sum_{t=1}^{T}\log p(y_{t}|y_{<t},\mathbf{x},y_{e})

(1)

where
T
denotes the length of the neutralized sequence, and the generation is guided by
g_{\psi}
.

To support this dual-task learning framework, we construct a specialized dataset
\mathcal{D}=\{(\mathbf{x}_{i},\mathbf{y}_{i},e_{i})\}_{i=1}^{N}
comprising
23,790
text pairs. Each triple consists of an original emotional text
\mathbf{x}_{i}
, its neutralized version
\mathbf{y}_{i}
, and an emotionality indicator
e_{i}\in\{0,1\}
. The dataset construction follows a two-step procedure:

Step 1: Emotional Text Collection.
We collect emotional texts from established sentiment analysis benchmarks, forming an initial set
\mathcal{X}_{\text{emo}}=\{\mathbf{x}^{(k)}\}_{k=1}^{K}
. Each text
\mathbf{x}^{(k)}
is associated with an emotion label
l^{(k)}\in\{\text{positive},\text{negative}\}
.

Step 2: Neutralized Text Generation.
For each emotional text
\mathbf{x}^{(k)}\in\mathcal{X}_{\text{emo}}
, we generate its neutralized counterpart
\mathbf{y}^{(k)}
through a constrained rewriting process
\mathbf{y}^{(k)}=\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{x}^{(k)};\Theta)
, where
\mathcal{N}(\cdot;\Theta)
represents the neutralization transformation with parameters
\Theta
designed to remove subjective evaluative language while preserving factual content. The resulting dataset is then formulated as:

\mathcal{D}=\bigcup_{k=1}^{K}\left\{(\mathbf{x}^{(k)},\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{x}^{(k)}),\mathbb{I}_{\text{emo}}(\mathbf{x}^{(k)}))\right\}

(2)

where each instance includes the original emotional text, its neutralized version, and a binary emotion indicator, enables the model to learn the mapping between emotional expressions and their neutral counterparts through supervised signals.

To ensure balanced optimization across both tasks, we implement a two-stage training strategy with adaptive weight fusion. In the first stage, the model focuses on emotion classification to establish basic affective recognition capabilities. The second stage jointly optimizes both objectives with a dynamic weighting scheme:

\mathcal{L}_{total}=\alpha\mathcal{L}_{cls}+(1-\alpha)\mathcal{L}_{gen}

(3)

where
\alpha
is a learnable parameter adjusted based on task performance. This approach prevents either task from dominating the optimization process and promotes synergistic learning between emotion recognition and neutralization capabilities.

4
Methodology

Building upon the findings from the preliminary experiment, we further propose EWH dataset and LEWM to enhance world modeling.

4.1
Emotion-Why-How(EWH) Dataset

Emotion-Why-How(EWH) is a multimodal dataset designed to capture emotion-driven state transitions in real-world behavioral scenarios. In contrast to existing world model datasets that emphasize physical or logical rules, EWH focuses on scenarios where
Emotion
acts as a key mediator shaping human behavior and subsequent world dynamics, enabling models to reason about
Why
actions occur and
How
emotions alter future states.

Figure 3:
Overview of EWH Dataset.

As illustrated in Fig.
3
, each state captures both the visual appearance and acoustic expression of human subjects in daily-life social contexts, including movies, television scenes, and egocentric recordings. Formally, each EWH sample is represented as a temporally grounded tuple:

\langle(\mathcal{S}_{0},\mathcal{E}_{0}),\,A,\,(\mathcal{S}_{1},\mathcal{E}_{1})\rangle

(4)

where:

•

\mathcal{S}_{t}=(\mathcal{V}_{t},\mathcal{A}_{t},\mathcal{I}_{t})
denotes the multimodal observable state at time
t
, consisting of synchronized video
\mathcal{V}_{t}
, audio
\mathcal{A}_{t}
, and image
\mathcal{I}_{t}
;

•

\mathcal{E}_{t}
denotes the emotional state linked to
\mathcal{S}_{t}
;

•

A
is the emotion-influenced action bridging the transition from
\mathcal{S}_{0}
to
\mathcal{S}_{1}
.

Instead of relying on explicit human annotation, we employ a Large Multimodal Model (LMM) to understand the video and automatically locate temporal segments where human behavior is strongly emotion-driven. For each detected segment, the LMM provides a natural-language
action description

A
and generates the corresponding emotional interpretation. Around the same temporal region, we further apply dedicated multimodal models to extract (1) key visual frames, (2) audio signals, and (3) facial expression features. The emotional state
\mathcal{E}_{t}
is then inferred by integrating semantic emotional context with facial expression features, serving as a cognitively aligned but weakly supervised proxy of internal affect.

The causal structure of EWH is formalized as:

(\mathcal{S}_{0},\mathcal{E}_{0})\xrightarrow{\text{Why}}A\xrightarrow{\text{Emotion Transition}}\mathcal{E}_{1}\xrightarrow{\text{How}}\mathcal{S}_{1}

(5)

This formulation captures two complementary reasoning pathways:
(1) an explicit observable pathway
\mathcal{S}_{0}\rightarrow A\rightarrow\mathcal{S}_{1}
, and
(2) an implicit emotional pathway
\mathcal{S}_{0}\rightarrow\mathcal{E}_{0}\rightarrow A\rightarrow\mathcal{E}_{1}\rightarrow\mathcal{S}_{1}
.
The unified representation enables models to learn causal affective dynamics and evaluate whether a system can truly reason about why actions occur and how emotional shifts propagate into future world.

4.2
Large Emotional World Model(LEWM)

Figure 4:
Illustrations of LEWM model architecture

Model Architecture.

We model emotion-aware world dynamics under a unified conditional probability framework.
As illustrated in Figure
4
, given the current multimodal state
\mathcal{S}_{t}
, emotional state
\mathcal{E}_{t}
, and action
A_{t}
, the model jointly predicts the next environmental state and emotional state as:

p(\mathcal{S}_{t+1},\mathcal{E}_{t+1}\mid\mathcal{S}_{t},\mathcal{E}_{t},A_{t})

(6)

Emotion-aware Representation.

We first encode each component into latent embeddings:

\displaystyle z_{t}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Enc}_{S}(\mathcal{S}_{t})

(7)

\displaystyle e_{t}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Enc}_{E}(\mathcal{E}_{t})

\displaystyle a_{t}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Enc}_{A}(A_{t})

An emotion-conditioned latent representation is then constructed via concatenation:

h_{t}=[\,z_{t}\,;\,a_{t}\,;\,e_{t}\,]

(8)

which explicitly binds external perception, intended behavior, and emotion into a unified state.

Coupled Transition Factorization.

To better align with human behavioral cognition, we decompose the transition into an
emotion-first
causal pathway: the model first infers how internal affective states evolve and then conditions future world dynamics on this updated emotion. Formally,

\displaystyle p(\mathcal{S}_{t+1},\mathcal{E}_{t+1}\mid\mathcal{S}_{t},\mathcal{E}_{t},A_{t})

\displaystyle=p(\mathcal{E}_{t+1}\mid h_{t})\;

(9)

\displaystyle p(\mathcal{S}_{t+1}\mid h_{t},\mathcal{E}_{t+1})

where

p(\mathcal{E}_{t+1}\mid h_{t})
models the affective transition
and

p(\mathcal{S}_{t+1}\mid h_{t},\mathcal{E}_{t+1})
characterizes how the updated emotional state modulates future world.
Correspondingly, we define two transition heads, where
\mathcal{T}_{E}
estimates the affective transition and
\mathcal{T}_{S}
predicts emotion-conditioned state evolution:

\hat{\mathcal{E}}_{t+1}=\mathcal{T}_{E}(h_{t}),

(10)

\hat{Z}_{t+1}=\mathcal{T}_{S}(h_{t},\hat{\mathcal{E}}_{t+1})

(11)

and the next observable world state
\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{t+1}
is reconstructed through a multimodal decoder:

\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{t+1}=\mathrm{Dec}_{S}(\hat{Z}_{t+1})

(12)

Optimization.

Training is formulated as a state-centric objective, where world-state prediction serves as the primary target and emotion provides structural constraints on feasible dynamics. The joint objective is

\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{\text{state}}(\mathcal{S}_{t+1},\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{t+1})+\lambda\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{emotion}}(\mathcal{E}_{t+1},\hat{\mathcal{E}}_{t+1})

(13)

where
\mathcal{L}_{\text{state}}
drives the predictive fidelity of environmental dynamics, and
\mathcal{L}_{\text{emotion}}
enforces emotionally plausible transitions.

To further constrain behavior plausibility, we impose an emotion-consistency regularization:

\mathcal{C}=\big\|\mathcal{T}_{S}(h_{t},\hat{\mathcal{E}}_{t+1})-\mathcal{T}_{S}(h_{t},\mathcal{E}_{t+1})\big\|

(14)

which encourages the state-transition module to remain stable under small emotional deviations, thereby constraining unrealistic emotion-inconsistent world evolution. The final training objective becomes:

\min\;\mathcal{L}+\beta\,\mathcal{C}

(15)

encouraging accurate state forecasting while maintaining emotionally coherent world evolution.

6
Conclusion

This study underscores the importance of incorporating emotional reasoning into world models and presents the Large Emotional World Model (LEWM) as an effective step toward this goal. By leveraging the Emotion-Why-How (EWH) dataset together with an emotion-aware modeling framework, LEWM better aligns latent affective representations with human emotional understanding. Experimental results demonstrate that LEWM improves the prediction of emotion-driven social behaviors while retaining comparable capability on general world modeling tasks. These results point toward a promising direction for developing more comprehensive and human-aligned world models.

Market Making with Fads, Informed, and Uninformed Traders
Emilio Barucci, Adrien Mathieu, Leandro Sánchez-Betancourt

Abstract
We characterise the solution to a continuous-time optimal liquidity provision problem in a market populated by informed and uninformed traders. In our model, the asset price exhibits fads —these are short-term deviations from the fundamental value of the asset. Conditional on the value of the fad, we model how informed traders and uninformed traders arrive in the market. The market maker is aware of the existence of the two groups of traders but only observes the anonymous order arrivals. We study both, the complete and the partial information versions of the control problem faced by the market maker. In such frameworks, we characterise the value of information, and we find the price of liquidity as a function of the proportion of informed traders in the market. We discuss how to calibrate model parameters from market data and we quantify the outperformance over strategies that ignore these unobservable short-term deviations.

The market making problem has been studied in a large body of literature dealing mainly with two issues: asymmetric information and inventory management; see the seminal contribution of

Asymmetric information is concerned with the financial losses from trading with agents who have privileged information about the fundamental value of the asset (adverse selection). Inventory management focuses on controlling the risk associated with an open inventory, that is, the risk that the price goes up when the inventory is negative (short position) or that the price goes down when the inventory is positive (long position).

In what follows, we approach the problem through an angle that combines asymmetric information and inventory management in a new way: we study the market making problem when the market is affected by “fads”. By fads we mean short-term deviations from the fundamental price —a component that is associated with market inefficiencies and that may be related to over-optimism, short-termism, or other behavioural explanations (e.g. herding); see
[
28
,
39
]
.
The fad affects the market in two different ways. First, it affects the price of the asset. In addition to the classical arrival of information, which is modelled as a Brownian motion, there is a mean reverting process (the fad) that drives mid-prices —the mean-reversion well describes the fad’s long run bursting properties.
Second, the fad also affects the arrival of market orders. This is because a fraction of agents populating the market (informed traders) distinguish fads from fundamental values.

The objective of the market maker is to manage inventory while facing both (i) uninformed traders, who are sensitive to the displacements of bid-ask quotes from the mid-price as in
[
2
]
, and (ii) informed traders, who identify the true fundamental value of the asset (disentangling it from the fad component) and arrive in the market according to the displacements of the quotes from the fundamental.

The problem crucially depends on the information of the market maker about the fad. In this paper, we work out optimal bid-ask pricing strategies under both, a perfect information setup and an incomplete information setup. This allows us to obtain insights into the value of information depending on the proportion of informed traders in the market. We find that the optimal displacements are affected by the fad (or its filter) asymmetrically: as the fad increases, the market maker decreases the price of liquidity in the ask side and increases the price of liquidity in the bid side to manage the inventory optimally. This arises because the market maker aims to balance the arrival of trades from informed traders (who consider the fundamental value rather than the mid-price as reference price) and uninformed traders (who consider the mid-price as reference price). This asymmetric effect represents a novelty of our model
over the classical Avellaneda-Stoikov framework
[
2
]
: private information (or its filter) plays a key role in the quote asymmetry we characterise; this is in addition to the classical asymmetry coming from inventory management.

Confirming this interpretation, in our analysis, we show that when the trades sent by informed traders become “sharper” or more “toxic”, i.e., trades carried out when the fundamental value of the asset is farther from the mid-price, the market maker’s performance worsens and the bid-ask spread increases.

Our paper is closely related to the strand of the literature that studies how brokers deal with toxic order flow. Toxic flow is the trading activity of informed traders who have access to signals about the short-term trend of the asset and exploit them to their advantage.

Summary of contributions

This paper contributes to the literature on market making by considering a market with fads that is populated by informed and uninformed traders. We provide three key insights relevant to market microstructure.

First, temporary mispricing generates an endogenous asymmetry in optimal liquidity provision. When the price is temporarily above fundamentals (positive fad), informed traders sell more aggressively and the market maker optimally responds by widening the bid relative to the ask, even in the absence of inventory pressure. A negative fad leads to the widening on the ask side. Thus, the model provides a structural mechanism to link short-term price deviations (short living piece of information) to asymmetric liquidity.

Second, we extend the Avellaneda-Stoikov expressions for the spread to be an explicit function of the fraction of informed traders (or toxic flow) in the market. This provides a tractable mapping between market composition and optimal spreads, an approach that can be employed using market data.

Third, by comparing the full-information problem and the partial-information problem, we quantify the value of observing or filtering the temporary price distortions (fads). We show that the value of information increases with the persistence of the fad and increases and then decreases as the filter of the fad becomes better.

\bar{M}_{t}^{a,\delta}:=\bar{N}^{a}_{t}-\int_{0}^{t}\Gamma^{a}_{u}\,\mathrm{d}u\,\qquad\,\text{ and }\,\qquad\bar{M}_{t}^{b,\delta}:=\bar{N}^{b}_{t}-\int_{0}^{t}\Gamma^{b}_{u}\,\mathrm{d}u\,,

are true martingales. Thus, the processes
(\bar{N}^{a}_{t})_{t\in\mathfrak{T}}
and
(\bar{N}^{b}_{t})_{t\in\mathfrak{T}}
have stochastic intensities
(\Gamma^{a}_{t})_{t\in\mathfrak{T}}
and
(\Gamma^{b}_{t})_{t\in\mathfrak{T}}
, and then
(N^{a}_{t})_{t\in\mathfrak{T}}
and
(N^{b}_{t})_{t\in\mathfrak{T}}
have stochastic intensities
(\lambda^{a}_{t})_{t\in\mathfrak{T}}
and
(\lambda^{b}_{t})_{t\in\mathfrak{T}}
.
Finally, on the probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P}^{\delta})
, the processes
(Z_{t})_{t\in\mathfrak{T}}
and
(B_{t})_{t\in\mathfrak{T}}
are
\mathcal{F}
-Brownian motions, and the point processes
(N^{a}_{t})_{t\in\mathfrak{T}}
and
(N^{b}_{t})_{t\in\mathfrak{T}}
are
\mathcal{F}
-Poisson processes.

3.2
Characterisation of the solution

The market maker aims to solve the following control problem

\sup_{\delta\,\in\,\mathcal{U}}\mathbb{E}^{\delta}\left[X_{T}+Q_{T}\,S_{T}-\alpha\,Q_{T}^{2}-\phi\,\int_{0}^{T}Q_{u}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}u\right]\,.

(14)

Using the Itô formula for
X_{T}+Q_{T}\,S_{T}
, we get

\mathbb{E}^{\delta}[X_{T}+Q_{T}\,S_{T}]=\mathbb{E}^{\delta}[X_{0}+Q_{0}\,S_{0}]+\mathbb{E}^{\delta}\Bigg[\int_{0}^{T}(\delta^{a}_{s}\,\lambda^{a}_{s}+\delta^{b}_{s}\,\lambda_{s}^{b}+Q_{s}\,\mu-\eta\,\mathfrak{q}\,\sigma\,Q_{s}\,U_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s\Bigg]\,.

The maximisation problem (
14
) is then equivalent to maximise

\mathbb{E}^{\delta}\left[-\alpha\,Q_{T}^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}(\delta^{a}_{s}\,\lambda_{s}^{a}+\delta^{b}_{t}\,\lambda_{s}^{b}-\phi\,Q_{s}^{2}+Q_{s}\,\mu-\eta\,\mathfrak{q}\,\sigma\,Q_{s}\,U_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s\right]\,,

(15)

from which we define the value function associated with the control problem

\displaystyle V(t,q,u)=\sup_{\delta\,\in\,\mathcal{U}}\mathbb{E}_{t,q,u}^{\delta}\Bigg[-\alpha\,Q_{T}^{2}+\int_{t}^{T}

\displaystyle\Big(-\phi\,Q_{s}^{2}+Q_{s}\,\mu-\eta\,\mathfrak{q}\,\sigma\,Q_{s}\,U_{s}

\displaystyle+\delta^{a}_{s}\,\lambda^{a}(U_{s},Q_{s^{-}},\delta^{a}_{s})+\delta^{b}_{s}\,\lambda^{b}(U_{s},Q_{s^{-}},\delta^{b}_{s})\Big)\,\mathrm{d}s\Bigg]\,.

(16)

The expression above is finite since all processes belong to
L^{2}(\Omega\times[0,T])
. Below, we also prove that
V
is locally bounded. Standard techniques from the theory of stochastic control show that the Hamilton Jacobi Bellman (HJB) equation associated with such a problem is

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\partial_{t}V-\phi\,q^{2}-\eta\,u\,\partial_{u}V+\frac{1}{2}\,\partial_{u,u}^{2}V+(\mu-\eta\,\sigma\,\mathfrak{q}\,u)\,q

(17)

\displaystyle\qquad+\sup_{\delta^{a}}\Big\{e^{-k\,\delta^{a}}(\varphi+\psi\,e^{-\,\gamma\,(\sigma\,\mathfrak{q}\,u\,\vee\,\mathcal{S}^{-})}\times

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\Big(\delta^{a}+V(t,q-1,u)-V(t,q,u)\Big)\Big\}\mathds{1}_{q>\underline{q}}

\displaystyle\qquad+\sup_{\delta^{b}}\Big\{e^{-k\,\delta^{b}}(\varphi+\psi\,e^{\gamma\,(\sigma\,\mathfrak{q}\,u\,\wedge\mathcal{S}^{+})})\times

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\Big(\delta^{b}+V(t,q+1,u)-V(t,q,u)\Big)\Big\}\mathds{1}_{q<\overline{q}}\,,

with terminal condition
V(T,q,u)=-\alpha\,q^{2}
.
We compute the maximisers of the above expression explicitly obtaining that

\displaystyle\delta^{a*}

\displaystyle=\Big(\frac{1}{k}-V(t,q-1,u)+V(t,q,u)\Big)\vee-\delta_{\infty}^{a}\,,\qquad q\neq\underline{q}\,,

(18)

\displaystyle\delta^{b*}

\displaystyle=\Big(\frac{1}{k}-V(t,q+1,u)+V(t,q,u)\Big)\vee-\delta_{\infty}^{b}\,,\qquad q\neq\overline{q}\,,

(19)

which transforms the HJB into the following partial differential equation (PDE)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\partial_{t}V-\eta\,u\,\partial_{u}V-\phi\,q^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\,\partial_{u,u}^{2}V+(\mu-\eta\,\sigma\,\mathfrak{q}\,u)\,q

(20)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{k}\,\exp(-1+k[V(t,q-1,u)-V(t,q,u)])\,(\varphi+\psi\,e^{-\gamma\,(\sigma\,\mathfrak{q}\,u\,\vee\,\mathcal{S}^{-})})\mathds{1}_{\{q>\underline{q}\}}\,\mathds{1}_{\{\delta^{a,*}=\delta^{a,**}\}}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{k}\,\exp{\left(-1+k[V(t,q+1,u)-V(t,q,u)]\right)}\,(\varphi+\psi\,e^{\gamma\,(\sigma\,\mathfrak{q}\,u\,\wedge\,\mathcal{S}^{+})})\mathds{1}_{\{q<\overline{q}\}}\,\mathds{1}_{\{\delta^{a,*}=\delta^{b,**}\}}

\displaystyle+e^{k\,\delta^{a}_{\infty}}(\varphi+\psi\,e^{-\gamma\,(\sigma\,\mathfrak{q}\,u\,\vee\,\mathcal{S}^{-})})\,(-\delta^{a}_{\infty}+V(t,q-1,u)-V(t,q,u))\mathds{1}_{\{q>\underline{q}\}}\,\mathds{1}_{\{\delta^{a,*}>\delta^{a,**}\}}

\displaystyle+e^{k\,\delta^{b}_{\infty}}(\varphi+\psi\,e^{\gamma\,(\sigma\,\mathfrak{q}\,u\,\wedge\,\mathcal{S}^{+})})\,(-\delta^{b}_{\infty}+V(t,q+1,u)-V(t,q,u))\mathds{1}_{\{q<\overline{q}\}}\,\mathds{1}_{\{\delta^{a,*}>\delta^{b,**}\}}\,,

where

\delta^{a,**}=\frac{1}{k}-V(t,q-1,u)+V(t,q,u)\,,\,\qquad\,\qquad\,\delta^{b,**}=\frac{1}{k}-V(t,q+1,u)+V(t,q,u)\,.

3.2.1
Viscosity solution: complete information problem

In this section, we prove that the value function
V
in (
16
) is a viscosity solution of the HJB equation in (
17
). We recall that
\mathcal{U}
is the set of predictable processes
(\delta_{t})_{t\in\mathfrak{T}}=(\delta^{a}_{t},\delta^{b}_{t})_{t\in\mathfrak{T}}
, bounded from below and taking values in
U=U^{a}\times U^{b}=(-\delta^{a}_{\infty},+\infty)\times(-\delta^{b}_{\infty},+\infty)
and where the state processes are defined in (
10
). We rewrite the price process as

\mathrm{d}S_{t}=(\mu-\eta\,\sigma\,\mathfrak{q}\,U_{t})\,\mathrm{d}t+\sigma\,\mathrm{d}\bar{W}_{t}\,,

(21)

where
(\bar{W}_{t})_{t\in\mathfrak{T}}
is an
\mathcal{F}
-Brownian motion, with correlation
\langle B,\bar{W}\rangle_{t}=\mathfrak{q}\,t
.
The stochastic intensities of the Poisson processes are given by (
11
) and (
12
).

We define the restriction of our control space
\mathcal{U}_{t}
as

\mathcal{U}_{t}:=\big\{(\delta_{t})_{t\in\mathfrak{T}}\in\mathcal{U}:(\delta_{s})_{s\geq t}\text{ is independent of }\mathcal{F}_{t}\big\}\,,

and we recall an important result. Let
\tilde{V}
given by

\tilde{V}(t,q,u)=\sup_{\delta\,\in\,\mathcal{U}_{t}}\mathbb{E}_{t,q,u}^{\delta}\left[-\alpha\,Q_{T}^{2}+\int_{t}^{T}(\delta^{a}_{s}\,\lambda_{s}^{a}+\delta^{b}_{t}\,\lambda_{s}^{b}-\phi\,Q_{s}^{2}+Q_{s}\,\mu-\eta\,\mathfrak{q}\,\sigma\,Q_{s}\,U_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s\right]\,,

where the supremum is taken over
\mathcal{U}_{t}
instead of
\mathcal{U}
.
It follows from the Remark 2.2 (iv) in
[
43
]
that
V=\tilde{V}
. To simplify notations, we denote by
\mathcal{E}:=[0,T]\times\mathcal{Q}\times\mathbb{R}
the space in which
(t,q,u)
takes values.
Next, we prove that
V
is locally bounded.

Lemma 2
.

The value function
V
is locally bounded.

Proof.

We define
\widehat{V}:(t,q,u,\delta)\in\mathcal{E}\times\mathcal{U}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
by

\widehat{V}(t,q,u,\delta)=\mathbb{E}_{t,q,u}^{\delta}\left[-\alpha\,Q_{T}^{2}+\int_{t}^{T}(\delta^{a}_{s}\,\lambda_{s}^{a}+\delta^{b}_{t}\,\lambda_{s}^{b}-\phi\,Q_{s}^{2}+Q_{s}\,\mu-\eta\,\mathfrak{q}\,\sigma\,Q_{s}\,U_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s\right]\,.

From
|Q_{t}|\leq|\mathcal{Q}|
almost surely and using Jensen inequality, we bound
\widehat{V}
as follows

\displaystyle|\widehat{V}(t,q,u,\delta)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathbb{E}^{\delta}[Q_{T}^{2}]+\int_{t}^{T}\mathbb{E}^{\delta}[\delta^{a}_{s}\,\lambda^{a}_{s}+\delta^{b}_{s}\,\lambda_{s}^{b}]\,\mathrm{d}s+\int_{t}^{T}\mathbb{E}^{\delta}[Q_{s}^{2}]\,\mathrm{d}s+\int_{t}^{T}\mathbb{E}^{\delta}[U_{s}^{2}]\,\mathrm{d}s\,

\displaystyle\lesssim|\mathcal{Q}|^{2}+\int_{t}^{T}\mathbb{E}^{\delta}[U_{s}^{2}]\,\mathrm{d}s+\int_{t}^{T}\mathbb{E}^{\delta}[\delta^{a}_{s}\,\lambda^{a}_{s}+\delta^{b}_{s}\,\lambda_{s}^{b}]\,\mathrm{d}s\,

\displaystyle\lesssim 1+|\mathcal{Q}|^{2}+\int_{t}^{T}\bigg(u^{2}\,e^{-2\,\eta\,(s-t)}+\mathbb{E}^{\delta}\bigg[\bigg(\int_{t}^{s}e^{-\eta\,(s-r)}\,\mathrm{d}B_{r}\bigg)^{2}\bigg]\bigg)\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\lesssim 1+|\mathcal{Q}|^{2}+u^{2}\,,

where we used Itô’s isometry and that
\delta
is bounded from below (so
\delta\,e^{-k\,\delta}
and
e^{-k\,\delta}
are bounded), and that
\varphi+\psi\,e^{-\gamma\,(\sigma\,\mathfrak{q}\,U_{t}\,\vee\,\mathcal{S}^{-})}
and
\varphi+\psi\,e^{\gamma\,(\sigma\,\mathfrak{q}\,U_{t}\,\wedge\,\mathcal{S}^{+})}
are uniformly bounded. The symbol
a\lesssim b
means that there exists a constant
C>0
(independent of
a
and
b
) such that
a\leq C\,b
. Finally, we obtain

|\widehat{V}(t,q,u,\delta)|\lesssim 1+|\mathcal{Q}|^{2}+u^{2}\,,

and then
(t,q,u)\rightarrow\widehat{V}(t,q,u,\delta)
is locally bounded. Taking the supremum of the above expression implies that the value function is locally bounded.
∎

Theorem 1

(Weak dynamic programming principle)
.

Define the lower and upper semicontinuous envelopes

V_{*}(t,q,u)=\liminf_{(t^{\prime},q^{\prime},u^{\prime})\rightarrow(t,q,u)}V(t^{\prime},q^{\prime},u^{\prime})\,,

and

V^{*}(t,q,u)=\limsup_{(t^{\prime},q^{\prime},u^{\prime})\rightarrow(t,q,u)}V(t^{\prime},q^{\prime},u^{\prime})\,.

Consider
(t,q,u)\in\mathcal{E}
fixed and let
\{\theta^{\alpha},\alpha\in\mathcal{U}_{t}\}
be a family of finite stopping with values in
[t,T]
. Then

V(t,q,u)\geq\sup_{\alpha\in\mathcal{U}_{t}}\mathbb{E}_{t,q,u}^{\alpha}\left[\int_{t}^{\theta^{\alpha}}\Big(\delta^{a}_{s}\,\lambda_{s}^{a}+\delta^{b}_{t}\,\lambda_{s}^{b}-\phi\,Q_{s}^{2}+(\mu-\eta\,\mathfrak{q}\,\sigma\,U_{s})\,Q_{s}\,\Big)\,\mathrm{d}s+V_{*}(\theta^{\alpha},U_{\theta^{\alpha}},Q_{\theta^{\alpha}})\right]\,,

(22)

and if for any
\alpha
,
\Big((U_{s}^{t,u},Q_{s}^{t,q})^{T}\,\mathds{1}_{s\in[t,\theta^{\alpha}]}\Big)_{t\in\mathfrak{T}}
is
L^{\infty}
bounded, then

V(t,q,u)\leq\sup_{\alpha\in\mathcal{U}_{t}}\mathbb{E}_{t,q,u}^{\alpha}\left[\int_{t}^{\theta^{\alpha}}\Big(\delta^{a}_{s}\,\lambda_{s}^{a}+\delta^{b}_{t}\,\lambda_{s}^{b}-\phi\,Q_{s}^{2}+(\mu-\eta\,\mathfrak{q}\,\sigma\,U_{s})\,Q_{s}\,\Big)\,\mathrm{d}s+V^{*}(\theta^{\alpha},U_{\theta^{\alpha}},Q_{\theta^{\alpha}})\right]\,.

(23)

We do not present the proof since it follows the one in
[
35
]
because the stochastic intensities are uniformly bounded. Before dealing with the viscosity solution, let us define the Hamiltonian of our problem.

Definition 3

(Hamiltonian)
.

We define the Hamiltonian
\mathcal{H}:\mathcal{Q}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}(q,u,V_{1},V_{2})=

\displaystyle\sup_{\delta^{a}\,\in\,U^{a}}(\varphi+\psi\,e^{-\gamma\,(\sigma\,\mathfrak{q}\,u\,\vee\,\mathcal{S}^{-})}\,e^{-k\,\delta^{a}}\,(\delta^{a}+V_{1})\,\mathds{1}_{\{q>\underline{q}\}}

(24)

\displaystyle+\sup_{\delta^{b}\,\in\,U^{b}}(\varphi+\psi\,e^{\gamma\,(\sigma\,\mathfrak{q}\,u\,\wedge\,\mathcal{S}^{+})})e^{-k\,\delta^{b}}\,(\delta^{b}+V_{2})\,\mathds{1}_{\{q<\overline{q}\}}\,.

Finally, we define the HJB equation associated with the control problem.

Definition 4

(Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman equation)
.

Let
V\in C^{1,2}(\mathcal{E})
, then

\displaystyle 0=

\displaystyle\partial_{t}V(t,q,u)-\phi\,q^{2}+q\,\mu-\eta\,\mathfrak{q}\,\sigma\,q\,u-\eta\,u\,\partial_{u}V(t,q,u)+\frac{1}{2}\partial^{2}_{u,u}V(t,q,u)

(25)

\displaystyle+\mathcal{H}(q,u,V(t,q-1,u)-V(t,q,u),V(t,q+1,u)-V(t,q,u))\,.

To shorten the notation, we write the equation above as

0=F(t,q,u,V(t,q,u),\partial_{t}V(t,q,u),\partial_{u}V(t,q,u),\partial^{2}_{u,u}V(t,q,u)).

Remark 5
.

By a ball around a point
(t,q,u)\in\mathcal{E}
with radius
r>0
we mean

\{(s,j,v)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:\lVert(s,j,v)-(t,q,u)\rVert<r\}\cap\mathcal{E}\,,

where
\lVert\cdot\rVert
is the Euclidean norm.

Now we can define the notions of viscosity sub-solution and viscosity super-solution

Definition 6

(Viscosity sub-solution)
.

We say that
V
is a viscosity sub-solution if, for all
\Phi\in\mathcal{C}^{1,2}(\mathcal{E})
such that
V^{*}(t_{0},q_{0},u_{0})-\Phi(t_{0},q_{0},u_{0})
is a local maximum, we have

F(t_{0},q_{0},u_{0},\Phi(t_{0},q_{0},u_{0}),\partial_{t}\Phi(t_{0},q_{0},u_{0}),\partial_{u}\Phi(t_{0},q_{0},u_{0}),\partial^{2}_{u,u}\Phi(t_{0},q_{0},u_{0}))\geq 0\,.

(26)

Definition 7

(Viscosity super-solution)
.

Let
V
be locally bounded on
\mathcal{E}
. We say that
V
is a viscosity super-solution if, for all
\Phi\in\mathcal{C}^{1,2}(\mathcal{E})
such that
V_{*}(t_{0},q_{0},u_{0})-\Phi(t_{0},q_{0},u_{0})
is a local minimum, we have

F(t_{0},q_{0},u_{0},\Phi(t_{0},q_{0},u_{0}),\partial_{t}\Phi(t_{0},q_{0},u_{0}),\partial_{u}\Phi(t_{0},q_{0},u_{0}),\partial^{2}_{u,u}\Phi(t_{0},q_{0},u_{0}))\leq 0\,.

(27)

Definition 8

(Viscosity solution)
.

We say that
V
is a viscosity solution, if it is a viscosity sub-solution and a viscosity super-solution.

Before we state the main theorem of the section, we prove an important lemma.

Lemma 9
.

The Hamiltonian
\mathcal{H}
defined in (
24
) is continuous.

Proof.

First, we derive explicitly the value of the Hamiltonian. We have that

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}(q,u,V_{1},V_{2})=

\displaystyle\sup_{\delta^{a}\,\in\,U^{a}}(\varphi+\psi\,e^{-\gamma\,(\sigma\,\mathfrak{q}\,u\,\vee\,\mathcal{S}^{-})})\,e^{-k\,\delta^{a}}(\delta^{a}+V_{1})\,\mathds{1}_{\{q>\underline{q}\}}

\displaystyle\qquad\,\qquad\,\qquad+\sup_{\delta^{b}\,\in\,U^{b}}(\varphi+\psi\,e^{\gamma\,(\sigma\,\mathfrak{q}\,u\,\wedge\,\mathcal{S}^{+})})\,e^{-k\,\delta^{b}}(\delta^{b}+V_{2})\,\mathds{1}_{\{q<\overline{q}\}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\varphi+\psi\,e^{-\gamma\,(\sigma\,\mathfrak{q}\,u\,\vee\,\mathcal{S}^{-})})\,e^{-k\,\delta^{a,*}}(\delta^{a,*}+V_{1})\,\mathds{1}_{\{q>\underline{q}\}}

\displaystyle\qquad\,\qquad\,\qquad+(\varphi+\psi\,e^{\gamma\,(\sigma\,\mathfrak{q}\,u\,\wedge\,\mathcal{S}^{+})})\,e^{-k\,\delta^{b,*}}(\delta^{b,*}+V_{2})\,\mathds{1}_{\{q<\overline{q}\}}\,,

where the optimal displacements
\delta^{*}=(\delta^{a,*},\delta^{b,*})
are given by

\delta^{a,*}(V_{1})=\Big(\frac{1}{k}-V_{1}\Big)\vee-\delta^{\infty}_{a}\,,\qquad\,\qquad\delta^{b,*}(V_{2})=\Big(\frac{1}{k}-V_{2}\Big)\vee-\delta^{\infty}_{b}\,,

(28)

from which it follows that
\mathcal{H}
is continuous. Indeed, take
(q,u,V_{1},V_{2})\in\mathcal{Q}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
and consider
(q^{n},u^{n},V_{1}^{n},V_{2}^{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
that converges toward
(q,u,V_{1},V_{2})
. Since
\mathcal{Q}
is discrete, there exists
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
, such that, for all
n\geq n_{0}
,
q^{n}=q
. Then, for
n\geq n_{0}
, we have
\mathcal{H}(q^{n},u^{n},V_{1}^{n},V_{2}^{n})=\mathcal{H}(q,u^{n},V_{1}^{n},V_{2}^{n})
, and convergence of
\mathcal{H}(q,u^{n},V_{1}^{n},V_{2}^{n})
toward
\mathcal{H}(q,u,V_{1},V_{2})
follows by continuity of composition of continuous functions.
∎

Now, we are able to state the main theorem of the section.

Theorem 2
.

V
is a viscosity solution of the HJB equation (
25
).

Proof.

We first prove that
V
is a viscosity super-solution of the equation. Consider
(t,q,u)
and
\Phi\in C^{1,2}
such that

0=(V_{*}-\Phi)(t,q,u)=\min(V_{*}-\Phi)\,,

and for all
(s,j,v)\neq(t,q,u),(V-\Phi)(s,j,v)>(V_{*}-\Phi)(t,q,u)
.
Let assume by contradiction that

h(t,q,u):=F(t,q,u,\Phi(t,q,u),\partial_{t}\Phi(t,q,u),\partial_{u}\Phi(t,q,u),\partial^{2}_{u,u}\Phi(t,q,u))>0\,.

By continuity of the function
\Phi
and the Hamiltonian, there exists an open neighbourhood
B_{r}
of
(t,q,u)
such that

h(s,j,v)=F(s,j,v,\Phi(s,j,v),\partial_{t}\Phi(s,j,v),\partial_{u}\Phi(s,j,v),\partial^{2}_{u,u}\Phi(s,j,v))>0\,\qquad\text{ on }B_{r}\,.

We can define the set
J(t,q,u)
as the set of attainable values if a jump happens in
B_{r}
.
We define

2\,\eta:=\min_{\partial B_{r}\cup J(t,q,u)}(V_{*}-\Phi)>0\,.

Now, we consider
(t_{n},q_{n},u_{n})
a sequence such that

(t_{n},q_{n},u_{n})\rightarrow(t,q,u)\text{ and }V(t_{n},q_{n},u_{n})\rightarrow V^{*}(t,q,u)\,.

Since
(V-\Phi)(t_{n},q_{n},u_{n})\rightarrow 0
, we can assume that the sequence
(t_{n},q_{n},u_{n})
also satisfies

\lvert(V-\Phi)\rvert(t_{n},q_{n},u_{n})\leq\eta\,\text{ for all }\,n\geq 1\,.

(29)

Then, we consider the control
\nu\in\mathcal{U}_{t_{n}}
defined by

\displaystyle\nu^{a}_{t}=\delta^{a,*}\Big(\Phi(t,Q_{t^{-}}^{t_{n},q_{n},\nu}-1,U_{t}^{t_{n},u_{n},\nu})-\Phi(t,Q_{t^{-}}^{t_{n},q_{n},\nu},U_{t}^{t_{n},u_{n},\nu})\Big)\,,

(30)

\displaystyle\nu^{b}_{t}=\delta^{b,*}\Big(\Phi(t,Q_{t^{-}}^{t_{n},q_{n},\nu},U_{t}^{t_{n},u_{n},\nu}))-\Phi(t,Q^{t_{n},q_{n},\nu}_{t^{-}},U_{t}^{t_{n},u_{n},\nu})\Big)\,,

(31)

we define the stopping time

\theta_{n}^{\nu}:=\inf\{t>t_{n}:(t,Q_{t}^{t_{n},q_{n},\nu},U_{t}^{t_{n},u_{n},\nu})\notin B_{r}\}\,,

and we observe that
(\theta_{n}^{\nu},Q_{\theta_{n}^{\nu}}^{t_{n},q_{n},\nu},U_{\theta_{n}^{\nu}}^{t_{n},u_{n},\nu})\in\partial B_{r}\cup J(t,q,u)
. Then, we have

\Phi(\theta_{n}^{\nu},Q_{\theta_{n}^{\nu}}^{t_{n},q_{n},\nu},U_{\theta_{n}^{\nu}}^{t_{n},u_{n},\nu})\leq-2\,\eta+V^{*}(\theta_{n}^{\nu},Q_{\theta_{n}^{\nu}}^{t_{n},q_{n},\nu},U_{\theta_{n}^{\nu}}^{t_{n},u_{n},\nu})\,.

(32)

Now, using equation (
29
) and Itô formula, we obtain

\displaystyle V(t_{n},q_{n},u_{n})

\displaystyle\leq\eta+\Phi(t_{n},q_{n},u_{n})\,

\displaystyle=\eta+\mathbb{E}^{\nu}\left[\Phi(\theta_{n}^{\nu},Q_{{\theta_{n}^{\nu}}}^{t_{n},q_{n},\nu},U_{\theta_{n}^{\nu}}^{t_{n},u_{n},\nu})\right]

\displaystyle-\mathbb{E}^{\nu}\Bigg[\int_{t_{n}}^{\theta_{n}^{\nu}}\Bigg(\partial_{t}\Phi(s,Q_{s-}^{t_{n},q_{n},\nu},U_{s}^{t_{n},u_{n},\nu})-\eta\,U_{s}^{t_{n},u_{n},\nu}\,\partial_{u}\Phi(s,Q_{s-}^{t_{n},q_{n},\nu},U_{s}^{t_{n},u_{n},\nu})

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{1}{2}\partial^{2}_{u,u}\Phi(s,Q_{s-}^{t_{n},q_{n},\nu},U_{s}^{t_{n},u_{n},\nu})+\lambda^{a}_{s}\Big(\Phi(s,Q_{s-}^{t_{n},q_{n},\nu}-1,U_{s}^{t_{n},u_{n},\nu})

\displaystyle\qquad\,\qquad\,\qquad-\Phi(s,Q_{s-}^{t_{n},q_{n},\nu},U_{s}^{t_{n},u_{n},\nu})\Big)+\lambda^{b}_{s}\Big(\Phi(s,Q_{s-}^{t_{n},q_{n},\nu}+1,U_{s}^{t_{n},u_{n},\nu})

\displaystyle\qquad\,\qquad\,\qquad\,\qquad\,\qquad\,\qquad\,\qquad\,\qquad\,\qquad-\Phi(s,Q_{s-}^{t_{n},q_{n},\nu},U_{s}^{t_{n},u_{n},\nu})\Big)\Bigg)\,\mathrm{d}s\Bigg]\,

\displaystyle\leq\eta+\mathbb{E}^{\nu}\Bigg[\Phi(\theta_{n}^{\nu},Q_{\theta_{n}^{\nu}}^{t_{n},q_{n},\nu},U_{\theta_{n}^{\nu}}^{t_{n},u_{n},\nu})+\int_{t_{n}}^{\theta_{n}^{\nu}}\Big(\nu^{a}_{s}\,\lambda_{s}^{a}+\nu^{b}_{s}\,\lambda_{s}^{b}

\displaystyle\qquad\,\qquad-\phi\,(Q_{s^{-}}^{t_{n},q_{n},\nu})^{2}+Q_{s^{-}}^{t_{n},q_{n},\nu}\,\mu-\eta\,\mathfrak{q}\,\sigma\,Q_{s^{-}}^{t_{n},q_{n},\nu}\,U_{s}^{t_{n},u_{n},\nu}\Big)\,\mathrm{d}s\Bigg]\,,

\displaystyle=\eta+\mathbb{E}^{\nu}\Bigg[\Phi(\theta_{n}^{\nu},Q_{\theta_{n}^{\nu}}^{t_{n},q_{n},\nu},U_{\theta_{n}^{\nu}}^{t_{n},u_{n},\nu})+\int_{t_{n}}^{\theta_{n}^{\nu}}\Big(\nu^{a}_{s}\,\lambda_{s}^{a}+\nu^{b}_{s}\,\lambda_{s}^{b}

\displaystyle\qquad\,\qquad-\phi\,(Q_{s}^{t_{n},q_{n},\nu})^{2}+Q_{s}^{t_{n},q_{n},\nu}\,\mu-\eta\,\mathfrak{q}\,\sigma\,Q_{s}^{t_{n},q_{n},\nu}\,U_{s}^{t_{n},u_{n},\nu}\Big)\,\mathrm{d}s\Bigg]\,,

and using equation (
32
) we obtain

\displaystyle V(t_{n},q_{n},u_{n})

\displaystyle\leq-\eta+\mathbb{E}^{\nu}\Bigg[V_{*}(\theta_{n}^{\nu},Q_{\theta_{n}^{\nu}}^{t_{n},q_{n},\nu},U_{\theta_{n}^{\nu}}^{t_{n},u_{n},\nu})+\int_{t_{n}}^{\theta_{n}}(\nu^{a}_{s}\,\lambda_{s}^{a}+\nu^{b}_{s}\,\lambda_{s}^{b}

\displaystyle\qquad\,\qquad\,\qquad-\phi\,(Q_{s}^{t_{n},q_{n},\nu})^{2}+Q_{s}^{t_{n},q_{n},\nu}\,\mu-\eta\,\mathfrak{q}\,\sigma\,Q_{s}^{t_{n},q_{n},\nu}\,U_{s}^{t_{n},u_{n},\nu}\,)\mathrm{d}s\Bigg]\,.

Since
\eta>0
is independent of
\nu
, it follows that the latter inequality is in contradiction with the second inequality of the dynamic programming principle (
23
). Then
V
is a viscosity super-solution. Now, let us prove that
V
is a viscosity sub-solution.

Consider
(t,q,u)
and
\Phi\in C^{1,2}

4.1

Proposition 12

Proposition 13

Next we define

4.4
Connection with the separation principle

In this section we compute the filter of the process
(U_{t})_{t\in\mathfrak{T}}
with respect to the filtration generated by
(S_{t})_{t\in\mathfrak{T}}
, and then we construct new stochastic processes
(N^{a,b}_{t})_{t\in\mathfrak{T}}
where their stochastic intensities depend on
(\hat{U}_{t})_{t\in\mathfrak{T}}
instead of
(U_{t})_{t\in\mathfrak{T}}
.
Then, as shown above, the optimal strategies are akin to those from the full information setup but using
(\hat{U}_{t})_{t\in\mathfrak{T}}
instead of
(U_{t})_{t\in\mathfrak{T}}
. This modelling approach is similar to employing the separation principle. Quoting
[
8
]
, “the result says that it is optimal to estimate the state
and then apply the rule to the estimate, instead of the state itself”. Of course, this is not the optimal approach when one works in a non-linear-quadratic framework; however, as we show below, there is non-negligible economic value when employing this modelling approach.

\displaystyle\hat{\lambda}^{a}_{t}

\displaystyle=(\varphi\,e^{-k\,\delta^{a}_{t}}+\psi\,\exp(-k\,\delta_{t}^{a}-\gamma\,(\sigma\,\mathfrak{q}\,\hat{U}_{t})\,\vee\,\mathcal{S}^{-}))\,\mathds{1}_{\{Q_{t^{-}}>\underline{q}\}}\,,

(62)

\displaystyle\hat{\lambda}^{b}_{t}

(63)

Here, we construct an exact intensity process
(\psi\,\hat{\lambda}^{a,b}_{t})_{t\in\mathfrak{T}}

({N}^{a,b}_{t})_{t\in\mathfrak{T}}

Proposition 17

Let

\mathrm{d}Z_{t}=Z_{t}\,\Bigg(\eta\,\gamma\,\sigma\,\mathfrak{q}\,U_{t}+\frac{(\gamma\,\sigma\,\mathfrak{q})^{2}}{2}\,\mathrm{d}t-\gamma\,\sigma\,\mathfrak{q}\,\mathrm{d}B_{t}\Bigg)=:h(U_{t},Z_{t})\,\mathrm{d}t+\sigma(Z_{t})\,\mathrm{d}B_{t}\,.

(64)

Define
\hat{Z}_{t}:=\mathbb{E}[Z_{t}|\mathcal{F}_{t}^{S}]

\mathrm{d}\hat{Z}_{t}=\Bigg(\frac{(\gamma\,\sigma\,\mathfrak{q})^{2}}{2}\,\hat{Z}_{t}+\eta\,\gamma\,\sigma\,\mathfrak{q}\,(Q_{t}+\hat{Z}_{t}\,\hat{U}_{t})\Bigg)\,\mathrm{d}t-\bigl(\mathfrak{q}^{2}\,\sigma\,\gamma\,\hat{Z}_{t}+\eta\,\mathfrak{q}\,Q_{t}\bigl)\,\mathrm{d}\tilde{I}_{t}\,,

(65)

where
Q_{t}=\mathbb{E}[(U_{t}-\hat{U}_{t})(Z_{t}-\hat{Z}_{t})|\mathcal{F}_{t}^{S}]
and
(\tilde{I}_{t})_{t\in\mathfrak{T}}

Proof.

Consider the process
(\Gamma_{t})_{t\in\mathfrak{T}}

\Gamma_{t}:=\hat{Z}_{t}-1-\int_{0}^{t}\widehat{h(U_{s},Z_{s})}\,\mathrm{d}s

L^{2}\text{-bounded }(\mathcal{F}_{t}^{S})
-martingale starting from zero.

Indeed, consider
0\leq s\leq t\leq T

\mathbb{E}[\Gamma_{t}|\mathcal{F}_{s}^{S}]=\mathbb{E}[Z_{t}|\mathcal{F}_{s}^{S}]-\int_{0}^{s}\widehat{h(U_{u},Z_{u})}\,\mathrm{d}u-\int_{s}^{t}\mathbb{E}[h(U_{u},Z_{u})|\mathcal{F}_{s}^{S}]\,\mathrm{d}u-1\,,

(66)

which implies

\mathbb{E}[Z_{t}|\mathcal{F}_{s}^{S}]=\hat{Z}_{s}+\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\int_{s}^{t}h(U_{u},Z_{u})\,\mathrm{d}u+\sigma(Z_{u})\,\mathrm{d}B_{u}\Big|\mathcal{F}_{s}^{S}\Bigg]\,.

(67)

Using the tower property, we find

\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\int_{s}^{t}\sigma(Z_{u})\,\mathrm{d}B_{u}\Big|\mathcal{F}_{s}^{S}\Bigg]=\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\int_{s}^{t}\sigma(Z_{u})\,\mathrm{d}B_{u}\Big|\mathcal{F}_{s}\Bigg]\Big|\mathcal{F}_{s}^{S}\Bigg]\,,

(68)

and since
t\rightarrow\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(Z_{s})\,\mathrm{d}B_{s}
is a
(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t}
-martingale, we have that

\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\int_{s}^{t}\sigma(Z_{u})\,\mathrm{d}B_{u}\Big|\mathcal{F}_{s}^{S}\Bigg]=0\,.

(69)

Finally, we find

\mathbb{E}[\Gamma_{t}|\mathcal{F}_{s}^{S}]=\hat{Z}_{s}-1-\int_{0}^{s}\widehat{h(U_{u},Z_{u})}\,\mathrm{d}u=\Gamma_{s}\,,

(70)

which means that
(\Gamma_{t})_{t\in\mathfrak{T}}

\mathcal{F}^{S}

(\Gamma_{t})_{t\in\mathfrak{T}}
is bounded in

7.2
Beyond the Kalman-Bucy filter

Building alternative filters is a promising avenue of future research. Information about the fads
U_{t}
can be extracted from prices (as done in this paper), or through arrivals of liquidity taking orders. Recall, that in the full information model the arrivals were modulated by the fads in the asset price. Thus, following
[
19
]
, and similar to
[
12
]
, one can discretise
U_{t}
and model it as a continuous time Markov chain (CTMC) that we introduce next.

Let us consider that the fad process
(U_{t})_{t\in\mathfrak{T}}
is a continuous-time Markov Chain with generator
L
taking values in
G=\{\theta^{1},...,\theta^{J}\}
.
Instead of modelling the stochastic intensity of filled orders, we keep track of both arrivals and fills as two separate (but related) counting processes. Here, we filter the fads at the level of market arrivals and we formulate the effect of the control at the level of the fills. The disentanglement between arrivals and fills allows us to employ the results in Theorem 3.1 in
[
19
]
and in
[
20
]
.

We let
M^{a,b}
be the market arrivals which happen with stochastic intensity

\displaystyle\lambda_{t}^{a}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{J}\lambda_{t}^{a,j}\,\mathds{1}_{\{U_{t}=\theta^{j}\}}\,,

(74)

\displaystyle\lambda_{t}^{b}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{J}\lambda_{t}^{b,j}\,\mathds{1}_{\{U_{t}=\theta^{j}\}}\,,

(75)

where

\lambda_{t}^{a,j}=\varphi+\psi\,e^{-\gamma\,(\mathfrak{q}\,\sigma\,\theta^{j})\vee\,\mathcal{S}^{-}}\,

(76)

\lambda_{t}^{b,j}=\varphi+\psi\,e^{\gamma\,(\mathfrak{q}\,\sigma\,\theta^{j})\,\wedge\,\mathcal{S}^{+})}\,.

(77)

Upon arrivals, the fills probabilities are

\displaystyle\exp(-k\,\delta^{a}_{t})\,,\qquad\text{and }\qquad\exp(-k\,\delta^{b}_{t})\,,

(78)

and we keep track of all filled trades in the controlled counting processes
(N^{a,b}_{t})_{t\in\mathfrak{T}}
.

Now, we denote the filtration generated by the Poisson processes
(M^{a,b}_{t})_{t\in\mathfrak{T}}
by
(\mathcal{F}^{M}_{t})_{t\in\mathfrak{T}}
. As before, the full filtration of all processes involved is
(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\in\mathfrak{T}}
.

We are interested in the process
(\pi^{j}_{t})_{t\in\mathfrak{T}}
defined by

\pi_{t}^{j}:=\mathbb{E}[\mathds{1}_{\{U_{t}=\theta^{j}\}}|\mathcal{F}_{t}^{M}]\,.

(79)

Conditional on knowing the dynamic of
(\pi^{j}_{t})_{t\in\mathfrak{T}}
, we would be able to transform our partial-information optimal control problem into a full-information one.
We have the following result.

Theorem 4
.

For all

(\pi^{j}_{t})_{t\in\mathfrak{T}}
admits the following representation

\pi_{t}^{i}=\Delta_{t}^{i}/\sum_{j=1}^{J}\Delta_{t}^{j}\,,

(80)

where for all

(\Delta_{t})_{t\in\mathfrak{T}}

\mathrm{d}\Delta_{t}^{j}=\Delta_{t-}^{j}(\lambda_{t-}^{a,j}-1)\,(\mathrm{d}M_{t}^{a}-\mathrm{d}t)+\Delta_{t-}^{j}(\lambda_{t-}^{b,j}-1)\,(\mathrm{d}M_{t}^{b}-\mathrm{d}t)+\sum_{i=1}^{J}\Delta_{t-}^{i}{L}_{i,j}\,\mathrm{d}t\,

Proof.

This is direct consequence of Theorem 3.1 in

We write the price dynamics of

\mathrm{d}S_{t}=\mathrm{d}P_{t}^{S}+\sigma\,\mathfrak{p}\,\mathrm{d}Z_{t}=\sum_{j=1}^{J}\sigma\,\mathfrak{q}\,\theta^{j}\,\mathrm{d}\mathds{1}_{\{U_{t}=\theta^{j}\}}+\mu\,\mathrm{d}t+\sigma\,\mathfrak{p}\,\mathrm{d}Z_{t}\,,

(82)

and assuming that the process
(Z_{t})_{t\in\mathfrak{T}}

Proposition 18
.

Let
\hat{\lambda}^{a,b}_{t}
be given by

\hat{\lambda}^{a,b}_{t}=\sum_{j=1}^{J}\lambda_{t}^{a,b,j}\,\pi_{t}^{j}\,.

(84)

The process
(M^{a,b}_{t})_{t\in\mathfrak{T}}
is an
(\mathcal{F}^{M}_{t})_{t\in\mathfrak{T}}
-Poisson processes with stochastic intensity
\hat{\lambda}^{a,b}_{t}

Proof.

Let us consider the compensated processes

D_{t}^{a,b}:=M_{t}^{a,b}-\int_{0}^{t}\lambda_{s}^{a,b}\,\mathrm{d}s\,.

(85)

We remark that those are
(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\in\mathfrak{T}}
-martingales. Next, we consider the processes
(\widehat{D}^{a,b}_{t})_{t\in\mathfrak{T}}
defined by

\widehat{D}^{a,b}_{t}=D_{t}^{a,b}+\int_{0}^{t}(\lambda_{s}^{a,b}-\hat{\lambda}_{s}^{a,b})\,\mathrm{d}s=M_{t}^{a,b}-\int_{0}^{t}\hat{\lambda}_{s}^{a,b}\,\mathrm{d}s\,,

(86)

and we show that it is a
(\mathcal{F}^{M}_{t})_{t\in\mathfrak{T}}
-martingale. Since
(M^{a,b}_{t})_{t\in\mathfrak{T}}
and
(\hat{\lambda}^{a,b}_{t})_{t\in\mathfrak{T}}
are
(\mathcal{F}^{M}_{t})_{t\in\mathfrak{T}}
-adapted, then
(\widehat{D}^{a,b}_{t})_{t\in\mathfrak{T}}
is
(\mathcal{F}^{M}_{t})_{t\in\mathfrak{T}}
-adapted. By the definition of the processes we have that
(\widehat{D}^{a,b}_{t})_{t\in\mathfrak{T}}\in L^{1}(\Omega,(\mathcal{F}^{M}_{t})_{t\in\mathfrak{T}},\mathbb{P})
. Let
0\leq s\leq t\leq T
, we have

\mathbb{E}[M_{t}^{a,b}-\int_{0}^{t}\hat{\lambda}^{a,b}_{u}\mathrm{d}u|\mathcal{F}_{s}^{N}]=\widehat{D}_{s}^{a,b}+\mathbb{E}[D_{t}^{a,b}-D_{s}^{a,b}|\mathcal{F}_{s}^{N}]-\mathbb{E}[\int_{s}^{t}(\lambda_{u}^{a,b}-\hat{\lambda}_{u}^{a,b})\,\mathrm{d}u|\mathcal{F}_{s}^{M}]\,.

However, by the tower property and the fact that
(D^{a,b}_{t})_{t\in\mathfrak{T}}
is a martingale, we have

Conclusions

We computed approximate closed-form solutions for a market making problem à la Avellaneda-Stoikov when the market is populated by informed and uninformed traders.
22
22

22

Here, information is knowledge about the fad in the market.
To the best of our knowledge, this is the first time that informed and uninformed trading is incorporated in the optimal market making problem.
Our work builds a bridge for the classical results of
[
29
]
in the continuous-time optimal market making problem of
[
2
]
.
In a market with informed and uninformed traders where the fads are impounded in the asset price, temporary mispricing generates an endogenous asymmetry in optimal liquidity provision by the market maker. The model provides a structural mechanism to link short-term price deviations (short living information) to asymmetric liquidity.

We carried out an extensive sensitivity analysis to find the value of information (by comparing strategies with increasing levels of information) for the market maker. We showed how the toxicity of the order flow deteriorates the market maker’s performance and how it widens the bid-ask spread.

A.8
Maximum likelihood estimators

To calibrate
(\eta,\mathfrak{q})
, we compute a Kalman–Bucy filter for
(U_{t})_{t\in\mathfrak{T}}
for each choice of
(\eta,\mathfrak{q})
, and then consider maximising the log-likelihood of the resulting Kalman--Bucy filter in discrete time.
29
29

29

See Chapter 6.6 in
[
40
]
.
More precisely, we assume that we observe the mid price
(S_{t})_{t\in\mathfrak{T}}
at discrete times
0=t_{0}<t_{1}<...<t_{N}=T
, with
\Delta=t_{i+1}-t_{i}
, and we wish to estimate
(\mathfrak{q},\eta)
. First, we rewrite the dynamics for
U
and
S
as

\begin{cases}&U_{k+1}=\phi\,U_{k}+\varepsilon_{k}\,,\qquad\phi=e^{-\eta\,\Delta}\,,\qquad\varepsilon_{k}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,Q)\,,\qquad Q=\frac{1-\phi^{2}}{2\,\eta}\\
&y_{k}=H\,U_{k}+\xi_{k}\,,\qquad H=\mathfrak{q}\,(\phi-1)\,,\qquad y_{k}=\frac{S_{k+1}-S_{k}-\mu\,\Delta}{\sigma}\,,\qquad\xi_{k}=\mathfrak{q}\,\varepsilon_{k}+\mathfrak{p}\,(Z_{k+1}-Z_{k})\,.\end{cases}

(117)

Using (
117
) we write the associated discrete time Kalman–Bucy filter

y_{k}\,|\,y_{0},..,y_{k-1}\sim\mathcal{N}(\hat{y}_{k},F_{k})\,,

(118)

where

\hat{y}_{k}=H\,\widehat{U}_{k|k-1}\,,\qquad F_{k}=H^{2}\,P_{k|k-1}+\mathfrak{q}^{2}\,Q+\mathfrak{p}^{2}\,\Delta\,,

and where

\begin{cases}&\widehat{U}_{k+1|k}=\phi\,\hat{U}_{k|k-1}+K_{k}\,\nu_{k}\,,\qquad K_{k}=\frac{\phi\,P_{k|k-1}\,H+\mathfrak{q}\,Q}{F_{k}}\,,\qquad\nu_{k}=y_{k}-\hat{y}_{k}\,,\\
&P_{k+1|k}=\phi^{2}\,P_{k|k-1}+Q-K_{k}^{2}\,F_{k}\,,\qquad\widehat{U}_{1|0}=0\,,\qquad P_{1|0}=Q\,.\end{cases}

(119)

Using that

p(y_{0},...,y_{N})=\prod_{k=0}^{N}p(y_{k}\,|\,y_{0},..,y_{k-1})\,,

and equation (
118
) we derive the log–likelihood

l(\mathfrak{q},\eta)=-\frac{1}{2}\,\sum_{k=0}^{N}\Bigg[\log(F_{k})+\frac{\nu_{k}^{2}}{F_{k}}\Bigg]\,,

(120)

and we optimise this quantity over
(\mathfrak{q},\eta)
.

For
(\psi,\varphi,\gamma)
, we start by disentangling the market orders from the filled orders (as in Section
7.2
), and consider the market arrivals
M^{\pm}_{t}
under a probability measure where we use
\hat{U}
instead of
U
. More precisely, for the calibration exercise we assume that
M
has stochastic intensities given by

{\vartheta}^{\varphi,\gamma,\pm}_{t}=\varphi+\psi\,e^{\pm\gamma\,\hat{\mathfrak{q}}\,\sigma\,\hat{U}_{t}}\,,

where
(\hat{U}_{t})_{t\in\mathfrak{T}}
is the Kalman–Bucy filter of the fad using the calibrated
(\hat{\mathfrak{q}},\hat{\eta})
.
Finally, using the independence of the increments of the Poisson processes conditionally on the observation of
(\hat{U}_{t})_{t\in\mathfrak{T}}
, we run a maximization of the log–likelihood of the discrete time processes
({M_{t}^{\pm}})_{t\in\mathfrak{T}}
to estimate
(\gamma,\varphi)
, where the discrete time log–likelihood is given by

l(\varphi,\gamma)=\Bigg[\sum_{i=1}^{N}\Delta{M^{+}_{i}}\,\log(\vartheta_{i}^{\varphi,\gamma,+})-\sum_{i=1}^{N}\Delta\,\vartheta^{\varphi,\gamma,+}_{i}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\Delta{M^{-}_{i}}\,\log(\vartheta_{i}^{\varphi,\gamma,-})-\sum_{i=1}^{N}\Delta\,\vartheta^{\varphi,\gamma,-}_{i}\Bigg]\,.

Tables
2
and
3
(in the main text) show the performance of the above calibration procedures.

The Automorphism Group of the Finitary Power Monoid of the Integers under Addition
Salvatore Tringali, Kerou Wen

Abstract
. Endowed with the binary operation of set addition carried over from the integers, the family 𝒫 fin ​ ( ℤ ) \mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(\mathbb{Z}) of all non-empty finite subsets of ℤ \mathbb{Z} forms a monoid whose neutral element is the singleton { 0 } \{0\} . Building upon recent work by Tringali and Yan [J. Combin. Theory Ser. A, 2025], we determine the automorphisms of 𝒫 fin ​ ( ℤ ) \mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(\mathbb{Z}) . In particular, we find that the automorphism group of 𝒫 fin ​ ( ℤ ) \mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(\mathbb{Z}) is isomorphic to the direct product of a cyclic group of order two by the infinite dihedral group.

1.
Introduction

Let
H
be a semigroup (see the end of the section for notation and terminology). Endowed with the binary operation of setwise multiplication

(X,Y)\mapsto XY:=\{xy\colon x\in X,\kern 1.00006pty\in Y\}

induced by
H
, the family of all non-empty finite subsets of
H
becomes itself a semigroup, herein denoted by
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(H)
and named the
finitary power semigroup
of
H
.

If, in particular,
H
is a monoid and
1_{H}
is its neutral element, then
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(H)
is also a monoid. Moreover, the finite subsets of
H
containing
1_{H}
form a submonoid of
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(H)
, henceforth denoted by
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin},1}(H)
. Accordingly, we call
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(H)
and
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin},1}(H)
the
finitary power monoid
and the
reduced finitary power monoid
of
H
. Their neutral element is the singleton
\{1_{H}\}
.

Finitary power semigroups are among the most basic structures in a complex hierarchy generally referred to as
power semigroups
. These objects were first systematically studied by Tamura and Shafer in the late 1960s
[
16
]
, and more recently, from multiple new perspectives, in a series of papers by Fan and Tringali
[
9
]
, Antoniou and Tringali
[
3
]
, Bienvenu and Geroldinger
[
4
]
, Tringali and Yan
[
20
,
19
]
, García-Sánchez and Tringali
[
10
]
, Gonzalez et al.
[
11
]
, Aggarwal et al.
[
1
]
, Dani et al.
[
7
]
, Cossu and Tringali
[
5
]
, etc.

Power semigroups are interesting objects for a number of reasons. First, they provide a most natural algebraic framework for a number of important problems in additive combinatorics and related fields, like Sárközy’s conjecture
[
15
, Conjecture 1.6]
on the “additive irreducibility” of the set of [non-zero] squares in a finite field of prime order.
Second, the arithmetic of power monoids — with emphasis on questions concerned with the possibility or impossibility of factoring certain sets as a product of other sets that are irreducible in a properly defined sense — is a rich topic in itself and has been pivotal to some radically new developments
[
3
,
5
,
6
,
9
,
17
]
in factorization theory, a subfield of algebra so far largely confined to commutative domains and cancellative commutative monoids. Third, power semigroups have long been known to play a central role in the theory of automata and formal languages
[
2
,
13
]
.

In the present paper, motivated by work carried out over the past two years by
Bienvenu and Geroldinger
[
4
]
and Tringali and Yan
[
20
,
19
]
, we address the problem of determining the automorphism group of
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(H)
. (Unless otherwise specified, the word “morphism” will always refer to a
semigroup homomorphism
.)
Apart from being an interesting question in its own right (for the automorphisms of an object in a given category are a measure of the “symmetries” of the object itself, and their investigation is a fundamental problem in many areas of mathematics), a better understanding of the algebraic properties of power semigroups will hopefully lead to a deeper understanding of other aspects of their theory.

To set the stage, let
{\rm Aut}(S)
be the automorphism group of a semigroup
S
, henceforth called the
autogroup
of
S
. It is not difficult to show (see
[
20
, Remark 1.1]
) that, for each
f\in{\rm Aut}(H)
, the map

\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(H)\to\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(H)\colon X\mapsto f[X]:=\{f(x)\colon x\in X\}

is an automorphism of
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(H)
, hereby referred to as the (
finitary
)
augmentation
of
f
. We say that an automorphism of
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(H)
is
inner
if it is the augmentation of an automorphism of
H
.

Accordingly, we have a well-defined map
\Phi\colon\mathrm{Aut}(H)\to\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(H))
sending an automorphism of
H
to its augmentation.
In addition, it is easily checked that

\Phi(f\circ g)(X)=f\circ g[X]=f[g[X]]=f[\Phi(g)(X)]=\Phi(f)(\Phi(g)(X))=\Phi(f)\circ\Phi(g)(X),

for all
f,g\in{\rm Aut}(H)
and
X\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(H)
.
If, on the other hand,
f(x)\neq g(x)
for some
x\in H
and we take
X:=\{x\}
, then
\Phi(f)(X)=\{f(x)\}\neq\{g(x)\}=\Phi(g)(X)
and hence
\Phi(f)\neq\Phi(g)
.

In other words,
\Phi
is an injective group homomorphism from
\mathrm{Aut}(H)
to
\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(H))
. It is therefore natural to ask if
\Phi
is also surjective (and therefore an isomorphism), which is tantamount to saying that every automorphism of
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(H)
is inner. Unsurprisingly, this is not generally true, and determining the autogroup of
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(H)
is usually much harder than determining
\mathrm{Aut}(H)
, as we are going to demonstrate in the basic case where
H
is the additive group
\mathbb{Z}_{+}:=(\mathbb{Z},+)
of the (ring of) integers.

More precisely, let us denote by
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(\mathbb{Z})
the finitary power monoid of
\mathbb{Z}_{+}
. In contrast to what we have done so far with an arbitrary semigroup
H
, we will write
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(\mathbb{Z})
additively, so that the operation on
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(\mathbb{Z})
sends a pair
(X,Y)
of non-empty finite subsets of
\mathbb{Z}
to their sumset

X+Y:=\{x+y\colon x\in X,\kern 1.00006pty\in Y\}.

In Theorem
3.4
, we determine the automorphisms of
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(\mathbb{Z})
. Then, in Theorem
3.5
, we obtain that the autogroup of
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(\mathbb{Z})
is isomorphic to the direct product
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathrm{Dih}_{\infty}
. Here and throughout,
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
is the group of units
\{\pm 1\}
of (the multiplicative monoid of) the integers (we regard
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
as the canonical realization of a cyclic group of order
2
); and
\mathrm{Dih}_{\infty}
is the
infinite dihedral group

[
8
, Example 5.5(1)]
, i.e., the external semidirect product
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\ltimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{Z}_{+}
of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
by
\mathbb{Z}_{+}
with respect to the unique group homomorphism
\alpha\colon\mathbb{Z}_{2}\to\allowbreak{\rm Aut}(\mathbb{Z}_{+})
that sends
-1
to the only non-trivial automorphism of
\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, i.e., the inversion map
x\mapsto-x
.

We have striven to make our proofs as self-contained as possible. One exception is Lemma
3.2
, which relies on
[
19
, Theorem 3.2]
for the explicit description of the automorphisms of the reduced finitary power monoid of the monoid of non-negative integers under addition (it turns out that there are only two of them). We propose directions for future research in Sect.
4
.

2.
Reduced quotients and totally ordered abelian groups

Below we prove a series of propositions of a certain generality that will be used in Sect.
3
to derive
necessary
conditions on the automorphisms of the finitary power monoid of the additive group of integers. While a more focused approach would have spared certain technicalities, the broader generality of our results may be valuable in addressing analogous problems where the integers are replaced by structures with “comparable properties” (see Sect.
4
for a couple of ideas in this direction).

We begin with some definitions. Given a
commutative
monoid
M
, we denote by
\simeq_{M}
the binary relation on (the underlying set of)
M
whose graph consists of all pairs
(x,y)\in M\times M
such that
y\in xM^{\times}
. It is routine to check that
\simeq_{M}
is a congruence on
M
, that is,
\simeq_{M}
is an equivalence relation with the additional property that
x_{1}\simeq_{M}y_{1}
and
x_{2}\simeq_{M}y_{2}
imply
x_{1}x_{2}\simeq_{M}y_{1}y_{2}
.
We refer to the factor monoid
M/{\simeq_{M}}
as the
reduced quotient
of
M
, and to the set
\{y\in M\colon x\simeq_{M}y\}
as the
\simeq_{M}
-class
of an element
x\in M
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
H
and
K
be commutative monoids. Every (semigroup) isomorphism
\varphi\colon H\to K
induces a well-defined isomorphism
\widetilde{\varphi}
from the reduced quotient
H/{\simeq_{H}}
of
H
to the reduced quotient
K/{\simeq_{K}}
of
K
, by sending the
\simeq_{H}
-class of an element
x\in H
to the
\simeq_{K}
-class of
\varphi(x)
.

Proof.

Let
\varphi^{-1}(\simeq_{K})
be the binary relation on
H
whose graph consists of all pairs
(x,y)\in H\times H
such that
\varphi(x)\simeq_{K}\varphi(y)
. It is easily checked that
\varphi
is in fact a
monoid
isomorphism, namely, it maps the neutral element
1_{H}
of
H
to the neutral element
1_{K}
of
K
(see, e.g., the last lines of
[
18
, Sect. 2]
).
Since the functional inverse of
\varphi
is an isomorphism from
K
to
H
, it follows that
\varphi^{-1}(K^{\times})=H^{\times}
. Consequently, for all
x,y\in H
, we have that
(x,y)\in\varphi^{-1}(\simeq_{K})
if and only if
\varphi(y)\in\varphi(x)K^{\times}
, if and only if
y\in xH^{\times}
, if and only if
x\simeq_{H}y
. By the proof of
[
12
, Proposition I.2.6]
, this guarantees that
\widetilde{\varphi}
is a well-defined isomorphism from
H/\simeq_{H}
to
K/\simeq_{K}
.
∎

We recall that a
totally ordered group
is a pair
\mathcal{G}=(G,\preceq)
consisting of a group
G
and a total order
\preceq
on (the underlying set of)
G
such that
x\preceq y
implies
uxv\preceq uyv
for all
u,v\in G
.

If
\mathcal{G}=(G,\preceq)
is a totally ordered group, then it is easily verified that
\mathcal{G}_{\angle}:=\{x\in G\colon 1_{G}\preceq x\}
is a submonoid of
G
, hereby referred to as the
non-negative cone
of
\mathcal{G}
. Moreover, for every non-empty finite subset
X
of
G
, there exists a
unique
element
x_{\ast}\in X
such that
x_{\ast}\preceq y
for all
y\in X
; we refer to
x_{\ast}
as the
\preceq
-minimum
of
X
. Accordingly, we have a well-defined function
\mu_{\mathcal{G}}\colon\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(G)\to G
that takes a non-empty finite subset of
G
to its
\preceq
-minimum. We call
\mu_{\mathcal{G}}
the
minimizer
of
\mathcal{G}
.

Remarks 2.2
.

(1)

The usual ordering on
\mathbb{Z}
turns the additive group of integers into a totally ordered (abelian) group, whose non-negative cone is the set
\mathbb{N}
of non-negative integers.

(2)

Let
\mu
be the minimizer of a totally ordered group
(G,\preceq)
, and let
X,Y\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(G)
. If
x_{\ast}:=\mu(X)
and
y_{\ast}:=\mu(Y)
, then
x_{\ast}\preceq x
and
y_{\ast}\preceq y
for all
x\in X
and
y\in Y
, which, by the compatibility of the order with the group operation, yields
1_{G}\preceq x_{\ast}^{-1}x
and
x_{\ast}y_{\ast}\preceq xy
. Since
1_{G}=x_{\ast}^{-1}x_{\ast}\in x_{\ast}^{-1}X
and
x_{\ast}y_{\ast}\in XY
, it follows that

\mu(X)^{-1}X\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin},1}(G_{\angle})\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mu(XY)=x_{\ast}y_{\ast}=\mu(X)\mu(Y);

in particular, the latter condition means that
\mu
is a homomorphism from
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(G)
to
G
. Note also that
\mu
is clearly surjective, because
\{a\}\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(G)
and
\mu(\{a\})=a
for every
a\in G
.

The previous remarks provide a “template” and the basis for the next results.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
\mu
be the minimizer of a totally ordered group
\mathcal{G}=(G,\preceq)
. Assume
G
is abelian and denote by
\mathcal{Q}(G)
the reduced quotient of
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(G)
. Let

\psi
be the binary relation from
\mathcal{Q}(G)
to the reduced finitary power monoid
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin},1}(\mathcal{G}_{\angle})
of the non-negative cone
\mathcal{G}_{\angle}
of
\mathcal{G}
that, for all
\simeq_{\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(G)}
-congruent sets
X,Y\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(G)
, maps the
\simeq_{\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(G)}
-class of
X
to
\mu(Y)^{-1}Y
. Then
\psi
is an isomorphism
\mathcal{Q}(G)\to\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin},1}(\mathcal{G}_{\angle})
.

Proof.

Write
[X]
for the
\simeq_{\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(G)}
-class of a set
X\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(G)
, and let
Y\in[X]
. By
[
3
, Proposition 3.2(iv)]
, the units of
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(G)
are precisely the one-element subsets of
G
. Consequently,
Y=aX
for some
a\in G
. Since
\mathcal{G}
is a totally ordered group, it follows that
\mu(Y)=a\mu(X)
, which in turn implies that

\mu(Y)^{-1}Y=\mu(X)^{-1}a^{-1}aX=\mu(X)^{-1}X.

This is sufficient to show that
\psi
is a function from
\mathcal{Q}(G)
to
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin},1}(\mathcal{G}_{\angle})
.

Now, if
Y\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin},1}(\mathcal{G}_{\angle})
, then
Y\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(G)
and
\mu(Y)=1_{G}
, with the result that
\psi([Y])=Y
. If, on the other hand,
\psi([X])=\psi([Y])
for some
X,Y\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(G)
, then the commutativity of
G
guarantees that

Y=\allowbreak\mu(Y)\mu(X)^{-1}X\in X\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(G)^{\times},

which yields
X\simeq_{\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(G)}Y
and hence
[X]=[Y]
. Therefore,
\psi
is both surjective and injective.

It remains to see that

\psi
is an isomorphism.
Let
X,Y\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(G)
. By Remark
2.2
(2)
,
\mu
is a homomorphism
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(G)\to G
, and hence
\mu(XY)^{-1}=\mu(Y)^{-1}\mu(X)^{-1}
. Again by commutativity, this implies

\psi([X][Y])=\psi([XY])=\mu(XY)^{-1}XY=\mu(X)^{-1}X\cdot\mu(Y)^{-1}Y=\psi([X])\kern 1.00006pt\psi([Y]).

It follows that
\psi
is an isomorphism from
\mathcal{Q}(G)
to
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin},1}(\mathcal{G}_{\angle})
, as wished.
∎

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
\mathcal{H}=(H,\leq)
and
\mathcal{K}=(K,\preceq)
be abelian totally ordered groups,
\mu_{\mathcal{H}}
be the minimizer of
\mathcal{H}
, and
\varphi
be an isomorphism from
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(H)
to
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(K)
. There then exist an isomorphism
f
from
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin},1}(\mathcal{H}_{\angle})
to
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin},1}(\mathcal{K}_{\angle})
and a homomorphism
\alpha
from
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(H)
to
K
such that

\varphi(X)=\alpha(X)f(\mu_{\mathcal{H}}(X)^{-1}X),\qquad\text{for all }X\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(H).

Proof.

Denote by
\mathcal{Q}(H)
and
\mathcal{Q}(K)
the reduced quotients of
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(H)
and
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(K)
, respectively.
By Proposition
2.3
, mapping the
\simeq_{\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(H)}
-class of a set
X\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(H)
to
\mu_{\mathcal{H}}(X)^{-1}X
yields a (well-defined) isomorphism
\psi_{H}\colon\mathcal{Q}(H)\to\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin},1}(\mathcal{H}_{\angle})
. In a similar way, mapping the
\simeq_{\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(K)}
-class of a set
Y\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(K)
to
\mu_{\mathcal{K}}(Y)^{-1}Y
results in an isomorphism
\psi_{K}\colon\mathcal{Q}(K)\to\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin},1}(\mathcal{K}_{\angle})
, where
\mu_{\mathcal{K}}
is the minimizer of
\mathcal{K}
.

In addition, we have from Proposition
2.1
that
\varphi
induces an isomorphism
\widetilde{\varphi}\colon\mathcal{Q}(H)\to\mathcal{Q}(K)
by sending the
\simeq_{\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(H)}
-class of a set
X\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(H)
to the
\simeq_{\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(K)}
-class of
\varphi(X)
. It follows that the function

f:=\psi_{K}\circ\widetilde{\varphi}\circ\psi_{H}^{-1}

is an isomorphism
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin},1}(\mathcal{H}_{\angle})\to\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin},1}(\mathcal{K}_{\angle})
. More explicitly, if
X\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin},1}(\mathcal{H}_{\angle})
, then
\psi_{H}^{-1}(X)=\{hX\colon h\in H\}

and hence
\widetilde{\varphi}\circ\psi_{H}^{-1}(X)=\{k\varphi(X)\colon k\in K\}
. Accordingly, it holds that

f(X)=\psi_{K}(\widetilde{\varphi}\circ\psi_{H}^{-1}(X))=\mu_{\mathcal{K}}(\varphi(X))^{-1}\varphi(X),\qquad\text{for every }X\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin},1}(\mathcal{H}_{\angle}).

Since
\mu_{\mathcal{H}}(X)^{-1}X\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin},1}(\mathcal{H}_{\angle})
for each
X\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(H)
, we can thus conclude that, for all
X\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(H)
,

\begin{split}\varphi(X)&=\varphi(\mu_{\mathcal{H}}(X)^{-1}X)\kern 1.00006pt\varphi(\{\mu_{\mathcal{H}}(X)\})=\mu_{\mathcal{K}}(\varphi(\mu_{\mathcal{H}}(X)^{-1}X))f(\mu_{\mathcal{H}}(X)^{-1}X)\kern 1.00006pt\varphi(\{\mu_{\mathcal{H}}(X)\}).\end{split}

This is enough to finish the proof, upon observing that, by Remark
2.2
(2)
, the function

\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(H)\to K^{\prime}\colon X\mapsto\mu_{\mathcal{K}}(\varphi(\mu_{\mathcal{H}}(X)^{-1}X))\kern 1.00006pt\varphi(\{\mu_{\mathcal{H}}(X)\})

is a homomorphism, where
K^{\prime}
is the image of
K
under the embedding
K\to\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(K)\colon y\mapsto\{y\}
.
∎

3.
Main results

Throughout,
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(\mathbb{Z})
denotes the finitary power monoid of
\mathbb{Z}_{+}
(the additive group of integers), and
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin},0}(\mathbb{N})
denotes the reduced finitary power monoid of the monoid of non-negative integers under addition. We will write each of these monoids additively. We start with a series of lemmas.

Lemma 3.1
.

If
f
is a homomorphism from
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(\mathbb{Z})
to
\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, then there exist
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}
such that

f(X)=a\min X+b\max X,\qquad\text{for all }X\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(\mathbb{Z}).

Proof.

Set
a:=-f(\{-1,0\})
and
b:=f(\{0,1\})
, and fix
X\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(\mathbb{Z})
. Given
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\max X-\min X\leq m+n
(and hence
-m+\max X\leq n+\min X
), we have

\begin{split}\llbracket-m+\min X,n+\max X\rrbracket&=\llbracket-m+\min X,n+\min X\rrbracket\cup\llbracket-m+\max X,n+\max X\rrbracket\\
&=\llbracket-m,n\rrbracket+\{\min X,\max X\}\subseteq\llbracket-m,n\rrbracket+X\\
&\subseteq\llbracket-m,n\rrbracket+\llbracket\min X,\max X\rrbracket=\llbracket-m+\min X,n+\max X\rrbracket.\end{split}

(1)

Considering that

\llbracket-h,k\rrbracket=h\{0,-1\}+k\{0,1\}
for all
h,k\in\mathbb{N}
,
and assuming for the remainder that
m\geq\allowbreak\min X
and
n\geq-\max X
,
it is then immediate from Eq. (
1
) that

\begin{split}(m-\min X)\kern 1.00006pt\{-1,0\}&+(n+\max X)\kern 1.00006pt\{0,1\}=\llbracket-m+\min X,n+\max X\rrbracket\\
&=\llbracket-m,n\rrbracket+X=m\{-1,0\}+n\{0,1\}+X.\end{split}

Since
f
is a homomorphism from
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(\mathbb{Z})
to the additive group of integers, it follows that

-(m-\min X)\kern 1.00006pta+(n+\max X)\kern 1.00006ptb=-ma+nb+f(X),

which, by cancellativity in
\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, implies
f(X)=a\min X+b\max X
and finishes the proof.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Every automorphism of
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(\mathbb{Z})
is of the form
\pm\varphi_{\alpha,\beta}
for some
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{Z}
, where
\varphi_{\alpha,\beta}
is the function on
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(\mathbb{Z})
defined by
X\mapsto X+\alpha\min X+\beta\max X
.

Proof.

Let
\varphi
be an automorphism of
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(\mathbb{Z})
. By Remark
2.2
(1)
, Proposition
2.4
(applied with
H=K=\mathbb{Z}_{+}
), and Lemma
3.1
, there exist
f\in{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin},0}(\mathbb{N}))
and
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}
such that

\varphi(X)=f(X-\min X)+a\min X+b\max X,\qquad\text{for every }X\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(\mathbb{Z}).

(2)

On the other hand, we are guaranteed by
[
19
, Theorem 3.2]
that the only automorphisms of
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin},0}(\mathbb{N})
are the identity
\mathrm{id}\colon X\mapsto X
and the map
\mathrm{rev}\colon X\mapsto\max X-X
. Taking
f=\mathrm{id}
in Eq. (
2
) yields

\varphi(X)=X+(a-1)\min X+b\max X,\qquad\text{for every }X\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(\mathbb{Z});

while taking
f=\mathrm{rev}
and noting that
\mathrm{rev}(Y+k)=\mathrm{rev}(Y)
for all
Y\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(\mathbb{Z})
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}
lead to

\varphi(X)={-X}+a\min X+(b+1)\max X,\qquad\text{for every }X\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(\mathbb{Z}).

In the notation of the statement, this means that either
\varphi=\varphi_{a-1,b}
or
\varphi={-\varphi_{-a,-(b+1)}}
(as wished).
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Using the notation of Lemma
3.2
, define
f_{\alpha}:=\varphi_{\alpha,-\alpha}
and
g_{\alpha}:=\varphi_{\alpha-1,-(\alpha+1)}
, where
\alpha
is an arbitrary integer. For all
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have

f_{a}\circ f_{b}=f_{a+b}=g_{a}\circ g_{-b}\qquad\text{and}\qquad f_{a}\circ g_{b}=g_{a+b}=g_{b}\circ f_{-a}.

Proof.

Given
Y\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(\mathbb{Z})
, set
\delta^{\pm}(Y):=\min Y\pm\max Y
. For every
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have that

\delta^{\pm}(Y+k)=\min(Y+k)\pm\max(Y+k)=\delta^{\pm}(Y)+k\pm k.

(3)

It is then clear from our definitions that, for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}
and
X\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(\mathbb{Z})
,

f_{\alpha}(X)=X+\alpha\min X-\alpha\max X=X+\alpha\kern 1.00006pt\delta^{-}(X)

(4)

and

g_{\alpha}(X)=X+(\alpha-1)\min X-(\alpha+1)\max X=X+\alpha\kern 1.00006pt\delta^{-}(X)-\delta^{+}(X)=f_{\alpha}(X)-\delta^{+}(X).

(5)

By straightforward calculations, it follows that

\delta^{-}(g_{\alpha}(X))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{lem:compositions:eq(3)}}}{{=}}\delta^{-}(f_{\alpha}(X)-\delta^{+}(X))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{lem:compositions:eq(1)}}}{{=}}\delta^{-}(f_{\alpha}(X))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{lem:compositions:eq(2)}}}{{=}}\delta^{-}(X+\alpha\kern 1.00006pt\delta^{-}(X))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{lem:compositions:eq(1)}}}{{=}}\delta^{-}(X),

(6)

and in a similar way,

\delta^{+}(g_{\alpha}(X))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{lem:compositions:eq(3)}}}{{=}}\delta^{+}(X+\alpha\kern 1.00006pt\delta^{-}(X)-\delta^{+}(X))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{lem:compositions:eq(1)}}}{{=}}2\alpha\kern 1.00006pt\delta^{-}(X)-\delta^{+}(X),

(7)

where the notation
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ast)}}{{=}}
means that the equality is justified by Eq. (
\ast
).

Now, fix
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}
and
X\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(\mathbb{Z})
. From the previous equations, we readily see that

\begin{split}f_{a}\circ f_{b}(X)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{lem:compositions:eq(2)}}}{{=}}f_{b}(X)+a\kern 1.00006pt\delta^{-}(f_{b}(X))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{lem:compositions:eq(4)}}}{{=}}f_{b}(X)+a\kern 1.00006pt\delta^{-}(X)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{lem:compositions:eq(2)}}}{{=}}f_{a+b}(X)\end{split}

(8)

and

\begin{split}f_{a}\circ g_{b}(X)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{lem:compositions:eq(2)}}}{{=}}g_{b}(X)+a\kern 1.00006pt\delta^{-}(g_{b}(X))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{lem:compositions:eq(4)}}}{{=}}g_{b}(X)+a\kern 1.00006pt\delta^{-}(X)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{lem:compositions:eq(3)}}}{{=}}g_{a+b}(X).\end{split}

(9)

Consequently, we obtain

\begin{split}g_{a}\circ g_{-b}(X)&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{lem:compositions:eq(3)}}}{{=}}f_{a}(g_{-b}(X))-\delta^{+}(g_{-b}(X))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{lem:compositions:eq(6)}}}{{=}}g_{a-b}(X)-\delta^{+}(g_{-b}(X))\\
&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{lem:compositions:eq(8)}}}{{=}}g_{a-b}(X)+2b\kern 1.00006pt\delta^{-}(X)+\delta^{+}(X)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{lem:compositions:eq(3)}}}{{=}}f_{a-b}(X)+2b\kern 1.00006pt\delta^{-}(X)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{lem:compositions:eq(2)}}}{{=}}f_{a+b}(X)\end{split}

(10)

and

\begin{split}g_{b}\circ f_{-a}(X)&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{lem:compositions:eq(2)}}}{{=}}g_{b}(X-a\kern 1.00006pt\delta^{-}(X))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{lem:compositions:eq(3)}}}{{=}}(X-a\delta^{-}(X))+b\kern 1.00006pt\delta^{-}(X-a\kern 1.00006pt\delta^{-}(X))-\delta^{+}(X-a\kern 1.00006pt\delta^{-}(X))\\
&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{lem:compositions:eq(1)}}}{{=}}(X-a\delta^{-}(X))+b\kern 1.00006pt\delta^{-}(X)-\delta^{+}(X)+2a\kern 1.00006pt\delta^{-}(X)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{lem:compositions:eq(3)}}}{{=}}g_{a+b}(X).\end{split}

(11)

Since
X
is an arbitrary set in
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(\mathbb{Z})
, Eqs. (
8
)–(
11
) lead to the desired conclusion.
∎

We are now in a position to prove the first of our main results.

Theorem 3.4
.

The automorphisms of
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(\mathbb{Z})
are precisely the endofunctions of
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(\mathbb{Z})
of the form
\pm f_{\alpha}
and
\pm g_{\alpha}
, where
\alpha
is an arbitrary integer and, for all
X\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(\mathbb{Z})
, we define

f_{\alpha}(X):=X+\alpha\min X-\alpha\max X\qquad\text{and}\qquad g_{\alpha}(X):=X+(\alpha-1)\min X-(\alpha+1)\max X.

Proof.

Let
\varphi\in{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(\mathbb{Z}))
. It is immediate to verify that
-\varphi
is also an automorphism of
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(\mathbb{Z})
.
By Lemma
3.2
, we may therefore assume without loss of generality that there exist
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}

such that

\varphi(X)=X+a\min X+b\max X,\qquad\text{for all }X\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(\mathbb{Z}).

(12)

We seek necessary conditions on the pair
(a,b)
for
\varphi
to be bijective. To start with, Eq. (
12
) yields

\begin{cases}\min\varphi(X)=(a+1)\min X+b\max X,\\
\max\varphi(X)=a\min X+(b+1)\max X,\end{cases}\qquad\text{for every }X\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(\mathbb{Z}).

(13)

Fix
c,d\in\mathbb{Z}
with
c\leq d
. By the surjectivity of
\varphi
, there exists
X\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(\mathbb{Z})
such that
\min\varphi(X)=c
and
\max\varphi(X)=d
. It follows by Eq. (
13
) that the linear system in the variables
y
and
z
given by

\begin{cases}(a+1)y+bz=c,\\
ay+(b+1)z=d,\end{cases}

has at least one solution with
y,z\in\mathbb{Z}
. Subtracting the second equation of the system from the first leads to
y=z+c-d
, which, when substituted back into the second equation, results in

(a+b+1)z=d+ad-ac.

This ultimately proves that, for
\varphi
to be surjective,
a+b+1
must divide
d+ad-ac
for all
c,d\in\mathbb{Z}
with
c\leq d
. In particular, taking
c=d=1
implies that
a+b+1\in\{\pm 1\}
, and hence

a+b=0\qquad\text{or}\qquad a+b=-2.

Therefore, in the notation of the statement, we find that either
\varphi=f_{\alpha}
or
\varphi=g_{\alpha}
for some
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}
. We are left with showing that any such function is indeed an automorphism of
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(\mathbb{Z})
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}
. Since the maps
X\mapsto\min X
and
X\mapsto\max X
are both homomorphisms from
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(\mathbb{Z})
to
(\mathbb{Z},+)
, it is clear that each of
f_{\alpha}
and
g_{\alpha}
is an endomorphism of
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(\mathbb{Z})
. On the other hand, we know from Lemma
3.3
that
f_{\pm\alpha}\circ f_{\mp\alpha}=g_{\alpha}\circ g_{\alpha}=f_{0}
. Recognizing that
f_{0}
is the identity function on
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(\mathbb{Z})
, we can thus conclude that
f_{\alpha}
and
g_{\alpha}
are bijections, thereby completing the proof.
∎

We finish by characterizing the autogroup of
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(\mathbb{Z})
. To this end, recall from Sect.
1
that we denote by
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
the group of units of the ring of integers and by
\text{Dih}_{\infty}
the infinite dihedral group.

Theorem 3.5
.

{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(\mathbb{Z}))\cong\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\text{Dih}_{\infty}
.

Proof.

Given an integer
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}
, let
f_{\alpha}
and
g_{\alpha}
be defined as in Lemma
3.3
. Accordingly, set

E:=\{f_{0},g_{0}\},\qquad F:=\{f_{\alpha}:\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}\},\qquad G:=\{g_{\alpha}:\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}\},\qquad\text{and}\qquad H:=F\cup G.

It is immediate from Lemma
3.3
that each of
E
,
F
, and
H
is a subgroup of
{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(\mathbb{Z}))
; in particular, note that
f_{0}
is the identity automorphism of
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(\mathbb{Z})
, and observe that
f_{\alpha}^{-1}=f_{-\alpha}
and
g_{\alpha}^{-1}=g_{\alpha}
for every
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}
. Furthermore,
F
is a normal subgroup of
H
, as we check that

\phi\circ f_{\alpha}\circ\phi^{-1}=\begin{cases}f_{\alpha}&\text{if }\phi\in F,\\
f_{-\alpha}&\text{if }\phi\in G,\end{cases}\qquad\text{for every }\phi\in H\text{ and }\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}.

(14)

Considering that
E\cap F=\{f_{0}\}
and
EF=F\cup g_{0}F=F\cup G=H
, this ultimately shows that
H=E\ltimes F
, namely,
H
is the internal semidirect product of
E
by
F
. We claim that

H=E\ltimes F\cong\text{Dih}_{\infty}.

(15)

Indeed,
F
is an infinite cyclic group generated by
f_{1}
, as we have from Lemma
3.3
(and an elementary induction) that
f_{\alpha}
is the
\alpha
-fold composition of
f_{1}
for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}
. On the other hand,
E
is a cyclic group of order
2
and, by Eq. (
14
),
the action of
g_{0}
on
F
by conjugation satisfies
g_{0}\circ f_{\alpha}\circ g_{0}^{-1}=f_{-\alpha}=\allowbreak f_{\alpha}^{-1}
for each
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}
. Consequently, we gather from
[
14
, p. 51, Example (I)]
that
E\ltimes F\cong\text{Dih}_{\infty}
(as wished).

Now, Theorem
3.4
guarantees that
{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(\mathbb{Z}))=\{\pm\phi:\phi\in H\}
. It is then routine to check (cf. the first line of the proof of Theorem
3.4
) that the function

(\pm 1,\phi)\mapsto\pm\phi

provides an isomorphism from
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times H
to
{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(\mathbb{Z}))
. Since it is a basic fact that
S_{1}\times T_{1}\cong S_{2}\times T_{2}
for all semigroups
S_{1}
,
S_{2}
,
T_{1}
, and
T_{2}
such that
S_{1}\cong S_{2}
and
T_{1}\cong T_{2}
, we can therefore conclude from Eq. (
15
) that
{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(\mathbb{Z}))\cong\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\allowbreak H\cong\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\text{Dih}_{\infty}
.
∎

4.
Prospects for future research

Let
(G,\preceq)
be a totally ordered group (as defined in the comment just before Proposition
2.3
), and recall from Sect.
1
that the reduced finitary power monoid
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin},1}(G)
of
G
is the submonoid of
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(G)
consisting of all finite subsets of
G
that contain the neutral element
1_{G}
.

Question
.

Is it true that every automorphism
\varphi
of the
reduced
finitary power monoid
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin},1}(G)
of
G
is
inner
, meaning that there exists an automorphism
f
of
H
such that
\varphi(X)=f[X]
for every non-empty finite set
X\subseteq G
containing the neutral element
1_{G}
? In particular, does this hold when
G
is abelian?

We conjecture a positive answer when
G=\mathbb{Z}_{+}
(the additive group of integers), which, by Theorem
3.5
, amounts to proving that the only non-trivial automorphism of the reduced finitary power monoid of
\mathbb{Z}_{+}
is given by
X\mapsto-X
(recall that the only non-trivial automorphism of
\mathbb{Z}_{+}
is the map
x\mapsto-x
).

Development and validation of the analytical method for determination of residual solvents (2-propanol and n-hexane) in the drug substance Inosine Pranobex
Authors: Khrystyna Chemna, Liliya Logoyda, Vitalii Rudiuk
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Monitoring residual solvents in active pharmaceutical ingredients (APIs) is critical due to their potential toxicity and impact on the physicochemical stability of the final medicinal product. Therefore, strict control of these impurities is a mandatory requirement to ensure patient safety and maintain compliance with international pharmacopoeia standards and good manufacturing practices (GMP). The goal of this study was to develop and validate a sensitive method for the determination of two residual solvents using headspace gas chromatography with flame ionization detector (HS-GC-FID) in Inosine Pranobex API according to The International Council for Harmonisation of Technical Requirements for Pharmaceuticals for Human Use (ICH) guidelines. A mixture of water and N, N-dimethylformamide (5:1) was selected as the sample solvent. To achieve high resolution between nonpolar n-hexane and polar 2-propanol, the method was implemented using a DB-624 (60 m × 0.25 mm × 1.4 μm) chromatographic column with an oven temperature program and at a flow rate of 35 cm/s. Equilibration time of the samples was 20 min at temperature 105 °С, total run time was 43 min. The analytical method complied with the established requirements during validation. The limit of quantification was confirmed for 2-propanol – 30 ppm, for n-hexane – 1 ppm. The developed method provides guaranteed purity and safety of the drug substance and is a reliable tool for controlling residual solvents at the drying stage of the technological process of synthesizing the drug substance Inosine Pranobex.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s13065-026-01888-w.

Introduction

The control of pharmaceutical impurities is a critically important issue for the pharmaceutical industry. The presence of these unwanted chemicals even in small amounts may influence the efficacy and safety of the pharmaceutical products. According to ICH guideline Q3A (R2) [1], impurities associated with APIs are classified into three categories: organic impurities (process and drug-related), inorganic impurities, and residual solvents [2].

Solvents are inorganic or organic liquids. Organic solvents are commonly used in the production of APIs and pharmaceuticals for various purposes, including as reaction media for synthesis, for separation and purification of APIs during synthesis, to increase yield, impart desired characteristics such as crystalline form and solubility, etc.

Since there is no therapeutic benefit from residual solvents, all residual solvents should be removed to the extent possible to meet product specifications, good manufacturing practices (GMPs), or other quality-based requirements [3]. Drug products may contain residual solvents at levels not exceeding the level of justified safety data [4].

Because of some physical and chemical barriers, organic solvents cannot be completely eliminated from the product by manufacturing practices, such as drying in an elevated temperature under decreased pressure or by lyophilisation (freeze-drying). Usually some small amounts of solvents may remain in the final product. Additionally, a drug product may also become contaminated by organic solvents from packaging, warehouse storage, or from shipping and transportation [5].

Since residual solvents are impurities in new drug substances, usually with known toxicity, the selection of an appropriate control strategy is easily accomplished by following ICH guideline Q3C(R8) on residual solvents [3].

In classical organic synthesis, nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) spectroscopy is commonly used for rapid control of solvent content. However, for pharmaceutical analysis of APIs, this approach does not provide the proper selectivity and required detection limit for many solvents [5].

Gas chromatography with headspace sampling device is one of the best methods for determining residual solvents in medicinal products according to information from pharmacopoeias and literature [2, 6–10].

During the synthesis of the substance Inosine Pranobex, 2 organic solvents are used, namely 2-propanol and n-hexane. 2-propanol belongs to class 3 solvents, characterized by low toxic potential to humans. It is believed that the daily dose of such solvents is 50 mg/day or less (which corresponds to 5000 ppm or 0.5%). N-Hexane belongs to class 2 solvents, which have non-genotoxic animal carcinogenic activity or can cause other irreversible effects, such as neurotoxicity or teratogenicity. In pharmaceutical products, the content of n-hexane is regulated with limit of 290 ppm [4].

The vast majority of available literature focuses on clinical applications, quantitative determination or on related impurities of Inosine Pranobex and its drug products using high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC) or thin layer chromatography (TLC) [11–16]. No validated gas chromatographic method for the determination of these organic solvents in Inosine Pranobex has been reported to date.

Consequently, the development and validation of a robust method for the determination of residual solvents in Inosine Pranobex represents a valuable contribution. It directly addresses a gap in the literature regarding pharmaceutical quality control for this API.

The study presents the development and validation of a robust and sensitive analytical method using HS-GC-FID for the determination of two residual solvents which are used in the synthetic route of Inosine Pranobex. The implementation of this method is critical to ensure high standards of safety and quality of API for the subsequent production of finished drug products.

Experimental

Samples and reagents

2-Propanol (99.96%) and N, N-dimethylformamide (DMF, 99.98%) were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich Chemicals Co. (St. Louis, MO, USA). N-Hexane (98.48%) was obtained from Honeywell International Inc. (Charlotte, NC, USA). API Inosine Pranobex (100.0%) was kindly provided by JSC “Farmak” (Kyiv, Ukraine).

Instruments and software

Chromatographic analysis was performed using Agilent 7890A and Agilent 7890B gas chromatographs equipped with headspace samplers and flame ionization detectors (FID). Data acquisition and processing were managed using Agilent OpenLab and MassHunter software (all from Agilent Technologies, Inc., Santa Clara, CA, USA). Sample weighing was conducted on MS104S analytical balance (Mettler Toledo International Inc., Greifensee, Switzerland) and Sartorius Secura 224-10RU analytical balance (Sartorius Lab Instruments GmbH & Co. KG, Goettingen, Germany).

Chromatographic and headspace conditions

Chromatography was performed on a DB-624 fused-silica capillary column (60 m × 0.25 mm i.d., 1.4 μm film thickness; 6% cyanopropylphenyl and 94% polydimethylsiloxane; Agilent Technologies, Inc., cat. no. 122–1364). The inlet and detector temperatures were maintained at 230 °C and 260 °C, respectively. Injection was performed in split mode with a split ratio of 3:1. Helium was used as the carrier gas at a constant linear velocity of 35 cm/s.

The headspace operating conditions were set as follows: vial oven temperature, 105 °C; loop temperature, 115 °C; and transfer-line temperature, 125 °C. The vial equilibration time was 20 min. The GC oven temperature program is described in detail in Table 1.

Sample preparation

N, N-dimethylformamide was used as the solvent.

Preparation of blank solution. An aliquot of 5.0 mL of purified water and 1.0 mL of the solvent were transferred into a 20 mL vial. The vial was immediately fitted with a septum, sealed with a metal crimp cap and secured using a crimper.

Preparation of test solution. Approximately 200.0 mg of the drug substance was accurately weighed and transferred into a 20 mL vial. Subsequently, 5.0 mL of purified water and 1.0 mL of the solvent were added. The vial was immediately fitted with a septum, sealed with a metal crimp cap and secured using a crimper.

Preparation of reference solution. Stock solutions were prepared in the solvent at appropriate concentrations of 2-propanol and n-hexane in accordance with ICH Q3C(R8) guidelines. The final reference solution contained 2-propanol at a concentration of 1000 µg/mL, and n-hexane at 58 µg/mL. An aliquot of 5.0 mL of purified water and 1.0 mL of the final reference solution were transferred into a 20 mL vial. The vial was immediately fitted with a septum, sealed with a metal crimp cap and secured using a crimper.

Validation of analytical method

Validation of the analytical method was carried out taking into account the requirements of ICH Q2(R2) [17]. Calculation of system suitability criteria was carried out using software Mass Hunter and Agilent OpenLab. The tailing factor and resolution were calculated for all solvents examined. RSD for the replicate injections of the reference solution was also calculated for 2-propanol and n-hexane. System suitability was evaluated for each day of analysis.

To study specificity, individual solvents solutions with concentrations according to ICH Q3C(R8) recommendations, blank solution, test solution and reference solution were prepared.

Linearity was studied across the selected concentration range: 0.3% to 120% with reference to the permissible specification limits of solvents. Eight linearity standards for linearity studies were prepared in the concentration ranges for 2-propanol: 3–1200 µg/mL (15-6000 ppm equivalent to the content in the API), for n-hexane: 0.2–70 µg/mL (1-348 ppm equivalent to the content in the API).

The blank solution, the reference solution (five replicates (one injection per headspace vial)) and the linearity standards (prepared in triplicate for each concentration level) were chromatographed under the conditions described in Sect. “Chromatographic and headspace conditions”. Linear regression equations and correlation coefficients were calculated. Statistical analysis was performed using Excel. The predicted LOQ was calculated from the calibration curve and experimentally confirmed.

The accuracy of the method was evaluated based on analyte recovery. For the accuracy study the reference solution (five replicates) and spiked samples were prepared in triplicate at four different concentration levels relative to the specification limit (LOQ, 50%, 100% and 120%) and subsequently analysed.

Evaluation of precision included testing of repeatability and intermediate precision. Repeatability was evaluated by running six test solutions, containing the residual solvents at levels above LOQ, and the reference solution in five replicates. The experiment, which was performed during repeatability study, was repeated on another analytical day for intermediate precision study. Additionally, six replicate samples spiked with the target solvents at the 100% concentration level were tested to assess both repeatability and method accuracy.

The reliability of the chromatographic procedure was investigated by analysing both the reference solution and the spiked samples in triplicate under reference conditions and modified chromatography parameters, namely: flow rate (33 cm/s or 37 cm/s) and inlet temperature (220 ºС or 240 ºС).

The validated analytical method was applied to control residual solvents in 3 batches of the substance Inosine Pranobex.

Transfer of analytical method to the quality control (QC) laboratory

The purpose of method transfer was to confirm that the receiving laboratory is competent to perform the analytical method and to prove that the proposed method is reproducible and can be used in routine control.

For this purpose, two analysts from the developing and receiving laboratories analysed the same batch of drug substance using the described method. The chromatogram profile was evaluated. The relative differences were calculated as the ratio of the absolute difference between the laboratories’ average results to the grand average value. The obtained values were expressed as percentages to evaluate method convergence.

Results and discussion

The manufacturing of Inosine Pranobex was performed via a two-stage synthetic route involving the obtaining of a key intermediate Salt (Scheme 1). At the first stage, 2-propanol was used as the reaction medium to facilitate salt formation between 4-acetamidobenzoic acid and N, N-DMA2P, followed by the filtration and obtaining of the pure Salt intermediate. At the second stage, the acquired intermediate was dissolved and reacted with inosine in n-hexane to promote stoichiometric molecular assembly. Crystallization from n-hexane yielded the target drug substance Inosine Pranobex with high purity and product yield.

To ensure the safety and stability of the API obtained via the streamlined manufacturing process, rigorous quantitative control of residual solvents was applied. This analytical evaluation was essential for validating the efficiency of the optimized drying stage. In accordance with ICH Q3C(R8) guidelines, monitoring residual solvents is mandatory to eliminate potential toxicity. Furthermore, minimizing residual solvents is critical to prevent unwanted possible degradation of the API. Therefore, we developed the analytical procedure for determining residual solvents in Inosine Pranobex.

The separation of n-hexane and 2-propanol was carried out using a DB-624 capillary chromatographic column (60 m × 0.25 mm, 1.4 μm) with a layer of 6% of cyanopropylphenyl and 94% of polydimethylsiloxane, which, according to the classification of the United States Pharmacopeia, corresponds to the stationary phase G43 [18] and has a medium polarity. This choice was due to the need to ensure high resolution between non-polar n-hexane and polar 2-propanol.

The significant thickness of the stationary phase layer (1.4 μm) increases the sorption capacity of the column and provides increased retention capacity for low-boiling solvents, preventing their co-elution and ensuring a symmetrical peak shape. Tailing factor calculated for the peaks due to 2-propanol was 1.1, due to n-hexane − 1.2 according to the results of the studies, which falls within the established range of 0.8–1.5. The length of the column (60 m) in combination with a narrow internal diameter (0.25 mm) creates the necessary pressure gradient for clear peak focusing. This allowed us to achieve resolution values (Rs) between n-hexane and 2-propanol at the level of 9.4–9.6, which significantly exceeds the established requirement (Rs > 1.5) and ensures high selectivity of the method.

The developed temperature program combines precision separation at the initial stage and a strict phase regeneration regime at the end of the analysis. The choice of a temperature ramp rate of 25 °C/min provides a quick transition to the purification phase. Optimization of the temperature program involved the introduction of a final isothermal hold at 250 °C for 20 min. The applied temperature profile with a final conditioning phase ensures complete elution of high-boiling components of the API matrix, which eliminates cross-contamination between successive introductions and extends the service life of the chromatographic column.

To ensure maximum sensitivity and precision in the determination of residual solvents (n-hexane and 2-propanol), the HS sampling parameters and the composition of the solvent matrix were optimized. The choice of temperature program was based on the need to shift the thermodynamic balance towards the gas phase. The temperature of the vial thermostat was set at 105 °C, which exceeds the boiling points of n-hexane (69 °C) and 2-propanol (82.5 °C). This ensured intensive desorption of analytes from the liquid phase, which is especially critical for polar 2-propanol, which is capable of forming hydrogen bonds. The established vial equilibration time (20 min) was sufficient to achieve complete vapor-liquid equilibrium due to the acceleration of evaporation kinetics at high temperature.

Although the API under study exhibits full solubility in water, preference was given to a binary water – DMF mixture with a predominant aqueous phase. The addition of DMF as a co-solvent ensured the homogeneity of the standard solutions and prevented microphase separation of non-polar n-hexane in the aqueous medium. The high proportion of water in the system sharply reduces the solubility of n-hexane and 2-propanol in the liquid phase. This effect, analogous to ‘salting-out’, enhances analyte transfer to the headspace. The presence of DMF lowers the surface tension of the aqueous matrix, facilitating the diffusion of analytes across the phase interface and ensuring high repeatability of the results (for 5 replicates of the reference solution: for 2-propanol RSD was ≤ 4.9%, for n-hexane RSD ≤ 8.4%, which met the established criteria RSD ≤ 10.0%).

The developed method was validated in compliance with ICH guidelines, evaluating specificity, linearity, accuracy, precision, robustness and system suitability. All validation parameters successfully met the predefined acceptance criteria, demonstrating the reliability of the method for residual solvents quality control.

Validation of analytical method

Specificity

Specificity was demonstrated by the absence of interfering peaks at the retention times of 2-propanol and n-hexane. The samples described in the Sect. “Validation of analytical method” were analysed to ensure the accurate quantification of residual solvents without interference from the blank solution, the synthesis precursors or matrix components.

The chromatogram obtained with the blank solution does not show any peaks interfering with the peaks due to the solvents examined. The retention time (RT) of the DMF peak was 20.8 min.

RT of the solvents in the reference solution was identified and determined for the individual solvent solutions, namely: for 2-propanol – 7.0 min, for n-hexane – 9.2 min. Peaks before and after the n-hexane peak with relative retention times (RRT) of 0.9 and 1.2, which are its isomers, were also observed. When comparing the chromatograms of the reference solution and the individual n-hexane solution, it was found that the n-hexane isomers did not interfere with the determination of 2-propanol.

The chromatogram of the test solution revealed peaks of the solvents under study – 2-propanol and n-hexane, as well as an additional peak. The resolution between n-hexane and this peak was approximately 56.

From Figs. 1, 2 and 3, it can be concluded that the validation results showed excellent baseline separation for all components. The specificity of the method is sufficient for the analysis of 2-propanol and n-hexane in the drug substance Inosine Pranobex.

Linearity

Linearity was investigated in the selected concentration range: 0.3% − 120.0% relative to the specified solvent content. For n-hexane, excellent linearity was achieved across the entire initial range 0.2–70 µg/mL. The correlation coefficient of the calibration curve for n-hexane was 0.9973, which satisfies the acceptance criterion (not less than 0.995).

The lowest point (L1, 0.3%) for 2-propanol failed to meet the validation criteria. Specifically, 2-propanol exhibited an overestimated recovery combined with an unacceptably high RSD between replicate injections. This lack of accuracy and precision was driven by baseline noise interference, which prevented reproducible peak integration. Therefore, the calibration curve for 2-propanol was truncated, establishing a robust linear range from point L2 (0.6%) to 120% (6–1200 µg/mL). Within this range, the correlation coefficient was acceptable, standing at 0.9989. Plots of the solvent peak areas versus their concentrations are presented in Figs. 4 and 5.

Limit of quantification (LOQ)

The predicted LOQ was calculated from the parameters of the calibration curve. The results are presented in Table 3. However, the theoretically predicted LOQ was found to be significantly higher than the actual limit confirmed during practical testing of the exact same linearity range. Statistical calculations often overestimate the LOQ by incorporating the cumulative variance from higher concentration points across the entire calibration range. To determine the true sensitivity of the method, lower concentration levels were verified empirically. These lower levels successfully met all validation criteria (Table 4), demonstrating acceptable recovery (80.0-120.0%), excellent precision (RSD < 10.0%), and a satisfactory signal-to-noise ratio (S/N > 10). The experimentally evaluated concentrations fulfilled all validation criteria and were therefore considered suitable for establishing the LOQ. Consequently, experimentally confirmed LOQ was adopted for further analysis of 2-propanol as 30 ppm, of n-hexane – 1 ppm.

Accuracy

The accuracy of the method was comprehensively evaluated at four distinct concentration levels by spiking the target residual solvents into the API matrix at the LOQ, 50%, 100%, and 120% levels. Since API matrix entirely free of the target solvents was unavailable, a background correction approach was utilized to ensure unbiased results. The baseline content of the residual solvents was determined in the unspiked samples and subsequently subtracted from the total peak areas obtained for the spiked samples, following the formula provided in Appendix A.

The method demonstrated excellent accuracy across all studied levels (Tables 5 and 6). The mean recovery values matched the theoretical concentrations, falling well within the strict acceptance criteria for method validation (Table 7).

Conclusion

In the course of the study, the selective analytical method for the determination of residual solvents (2-propanol and n-hexane) in the substance Inosine Pranobex was successfully developed and validated by HS-GC-FID.

No data on the influence of components interfering with the analysis were found. The method demonstrated excellent linearity across the range of application for both investigated solvents. Statistical analysis of the results of validation tests in terms of precision and accuracy confirmed the high reliability of the method. The established LOQ allowed effective control of the content of solvents at levels significantly lower than the limits specified in the ICH Q3C(R8) guideline. The LOQ was experimentally confirmed for 2-propanol as 30 ppm, for n-hexane – 1 ppm.

Successful method transfer to the QC laboratory confirmed its high reproducibility. Statistical comparison of the results from the developing and receiving laboratories met all acceptance criteria. The method’s low sensitivity to changes in operators and instrumental platforms was demonstrated.

Thus, the developed method can be recommended for routine analysis of the API Inosine Pranobex in QC departments of pharmaceutical companies. It serves as a reliable tool for monitoring residual solvents in the drug substance during the drying stage of the synthesis process.

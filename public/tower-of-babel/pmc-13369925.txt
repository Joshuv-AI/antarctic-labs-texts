Distinct single-cell cellular states and ecosystems linked to HPV status distinguish therapeutic vulnerability of head and neck squamous cell carcinoma
Authors: Nihui Zhang, Zhizhou Xu, Guanghui Tian, Wanxin Deng, Jiankai Xu, Chenqing Huang, Qiang Chen, Jiale Cai, Xiaoyu Huang, Hong Wang, Bo Wang, Kongning Li, Dahua Xu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Human papillomavirus (HPV) infection plays a significant role in shaping the tumor microenvironment of head and neck squamous cell carcinoma (HNSCC). However, the heterogeneity of cellular states and ecosystems within the tumor microenvironment of HPV-infected HNSCC is still largely unknown. Using the EcoTyper framework, we perform an extensive evaluation of HPV-related cellular profiles and cellular ecotypes (CEs) in HNSCC. Single-cell RNA sequencing reveals 46 unique states across 12 principal cell types. Spatial transcriptomics confirm the spatial correlation of cellular states from the same ecotype. Moreover, prognostic and drug sensitivity analyses reveal the clinically relevant and therapeutic vulnerabilities of cellular status for patients with different HPV statuses. In conclusion, our findings provide insights into the different cellular organizations and microenvironments of HPV-related HNSCC, with potential implications for the development of biomarkers and precision therapies.

Introduction

Head and neck squamous cell carcinoma (HNSCC) ranks as the eighth most prevalent malignant tumor globally and originates in the mucosal epithelium of the oral cavity, pharynx, and larynx. Human papillomavirus (HPV) infection is a significant causative factor in HNSCC, particularly in oropharyngeal HNSCC, where the prevalence of HPV-positive cases is increasing1,2. The influence of HPV infection on HNSCC extends beyond direct tumorigenesis to include a substantial reshaping of the tumor microenvironment (TME). This modulation is characterized by altered immune cell infiltration and function, with HPV-positive HNSCC exhibiting increased levels of immune cells, such as regulatory T cells (Tregs) and CD8+T cells, compared with those in HPV-negative HNSCC3,4. Furthermore, HPV-positive HNSCC exhibits elevated PD-L1 expression, which could contribute to immune evasion5,6. Patients with HPV-positive HNSCC have better outcomes than those with HPV-negative HNSCC7. However, the cellular mechanisms and phenotypic trajectories underlying this survival advantage remain poorly understood. Given the complexity of the HPV-related TME, determining its heterogeneity is essential for accurate prognostication and for tailoring precision therapies in HNSCC.

The significance of diverse cellular states and cellular ecotypes (CEs) compositions in predicting clinical outcomes cannot be overlooked. A study on colorectal cancer demonstrated a correlation between specific ecotype compositions and patient survival rates, thereby highlighting the prognostic importance of TME8. Furthermore, research on human solid tumors revealed an association between certain cellular states and resistance to treatment, suggesting the possibility of developing personalized therapeutic strategies9. Additionally, an investigation into ovarian cancer revealed that a comprehensive analysis of cellular states and ecotypes in TME can help identify various immunological subtypes, potentially leading to new directions and targets for cancer treatment10. In the context of HNSCC, studies have identified distinct cellular states and ecosystems that are associated with prognosis and therapeutic responses11. However, there is currently a lack of research concerning the cellular states and ecosystems within the heterogeneous tumor microenvironment of HPV-infected HNSCC is lacking. Therefore, further studies are needed to gain an in-depth understanding of these complex interactions, which are essential for the development of therapeutic approaches and the improvement of clinical outcomes for patients with HNSCC.

In this study, the cellular states and ecosystems in HPV-related HNSCC were comprehensively mapped using single-cell transcriptomic data from 139,809 cells. We identified 46 distinct cellular states across 12 major cell types within the HNSCC TME, which were validated in independent cohorts. Functional enrichment analysis revealed the impact of diverse gene expression on cellular functions and highlighted the more active immune responses in HPV-positive-associated cellular states. Spatial transcriptomic feature scoring confirmed the spatial clustering of distinct cellular states within the same ecosystem. Survival analysis indicated that specific cellular states correlated with HPV infection and patient outcomes. Importantly, we observed that the enrichment of diverse cellular states in the TME could predict patient response to drug therapy, suggesting that assessing cellular state composition could inform the development of therapeutic approaches.

Results

Overview of cell states and ecosystem profiling in HPV-infected HNSCC

We utilized EcoTyper, a machine learning framework, to characterize alterations in cellular states and ecosystem composition associated with HPV within the TME of HNSCC9. First, EcoTyper was applied to single-cell transcriptomic data from a clinically annotated training cohort to derive cell-type-specific gene expression profiles. We subsequently employed nonnegative matrix factorization of the EcoTyper approach to reconstruct the weighted contributions of fundamental transcriptional states within each cell type. This enabled the identification of transcriptionally defined cellular states. By integrating correlations among these cellular states, we delineated tumor ecosystems comprising coexisting cellular states, which were independently validated across multiple HNSCC cohorts, including both single-cell and spatial transcriptomic datasets. To investigate the fundamental cellular states and ecosystem compositions linked to HPV infection in HNSCC at the single-cell level, we compiled single-cell transcriptomic data from four cohorts encompassing 79 patients with documented HPV infection status, sourced from the GEO database (Fig. 1A). Of these, 43 samples from the two cohorts constituted the discovery sets, while the remaining two cohorts served as the validation sets. Additionally, bulk transcriptomic data comprising 476 samples from the TCGA project were designated validation set 1, and 198 samples from the CPTAC-HNSCC, together with 203 samples from GEO, were combined as validation set 2, serving as independent validation groups (Fig. 1A). Following batch effect correction and rigorous quality control of the 43 original single-cell HNSCC samples in the training set, a total of 139,809 high-quality cells were retained for downstream analyses (Fig. 1B). Cell type annotation was performed on the basis of established marker genes, resulting in the identification of twelve major cell types. Representative marker genes demonstrated high specificity within their respective cell populations as follows: B cells expressed CD79A and MS4A1; plasma cells expressed MZB1 and TNFRSF17; CD8+ T cells expressed CD8A and CD8B; CD4+ T cells expressed CD4; NK cells expressed KLRF1; monocytes/macrophages expressed CD14 and AIF1; neutrophils expressed S100A8 and CSF3R; dendritic cells expressed CD1C and IRF4; mast cells expressed CPA3 and MS4A2; fibroblasts expressed LUM and COL1A2; endothelial cells expressed VWF and ENG; and epithelial cells expressed KRT5 and KRT18 (Fig. 1C, Supplementary Fig. 1B).

Comparative analysis of the proportions of these twelve cell types between HPV-positive and HPV-negative states revealed notable heterogeneity in HPV-infected HNSCC. Specifically, HPV-positive patients exhibited elevated proportions of immune cell populations, including B cells (p = 2.055e−03), CD4+ T cells (p = 7.435e−05), and plasma cells (p = 3.429e−02), concomitant with reduced proportions of fibroblasts and endothelial cells relative to those in HPV-negative patients (p = 1.920e−02). Importantly, a consistent reduction in fibroblast proportions in HPV-positive patients was observed across both the training and validation cohorts (Fig. 1D, Supplementary Fig. 1C, D). These findings underscore the complexity of the HPV-infected TME in HNSCC and emphasize the need for further detailed characterization of cellular states to elucidate the distinct roles of various cell types within this context.

Heterogeneity of HNSCC cell states for HPV infection

Utilizing the EcoTyper framework, we identified 46 distinct cellular states spanning 12 cell types, with each cell type exhibiting between two and five discrete states (Fig. 2A). Different cell states exhibit heterogeneous gene expression (Supplementary Fig. 2A). Notably, the distribution of patient proportions across these cell types demonstrated significant variability (Fig. 2B). The robustness of these cellular states was confirmed by successfully recapitulating all the states identified from the primary samples within both a single-cell validation cohort and two independent bulk transcriptome cohorts (Fig. 2D). To elucidate the relationship between cellular states and HPV infection status within the TME, we conducted a statistical analysis to compare the distribution of cells derived from HPV-positive and HPV-negative samples across different cellular states in both the single-cell training and validation datasets. Epithelial cell states exhibited particularly strong associations with HPV infection status. With a focus on epithelial cells to characterize cellular heterogeneity in HNSCC, we identified five distinct epithelial cellular states, three of which correlated significantly with HPV infection. These epithelial states were reproducible across two independent bulk transcriptome cohorts and one single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) validation cohort (Fig. 2D). Melanoma-associated antigens (MAGE), including MAGEA4, represent promising immunotherapeutic targets and have been linked to poor overall survival in patients with HNSCC9,12,13. The S05 epithelial cells, which were enriched in HPV-negative samples, demonstrated elevated MAGEA4 expression (Fig. 2D, E; Supplementary Fig. 2C). Intriguingly, the epithelial cell microenvironment associated with HPV-positive tumors exhibited a more immunologically active phenotype. Specifically, epithelial cells (S03), which were enriched in HPV-positive cells, were characterized by enhanced immune infiltration, as evidenced by high CXCL10 expression, and displayed a “hot” tumor phenotype through upregulation of tumor inflammation signature (TIS)-related genes (Fig. 2F, G; Supplementary Fig. 2D, E). These findings are consistent with those of previous studies4. Our single-cell analysis revealed diverse cellular states within the TME, with distinct states of identical cell types correlated with HPV infection status.

Association of HNSCC cell states with patient outcomes

By using EcoTyper-based single-cell deconvolution on 43 discovery head and neck squamous cell carcinoma samples, we determined that distinct cellular states are closely associated with HPV status and clinical outcomes. The heterogeneity of cellular states is significantly related to patient prognosis, enabling the prediction of cancer clinical outcomes8,10,14. We further examined the association between EcoTyper-defined cellular states and patient survival outcomes. Univariate analysis revealed that 11 out of the 46 cellular states (23.9%) were significantly correlated with progression-free survival in the TCGA-HNSCC cohort (Fig. 3A). These associations were highly conserved, with the directionality of the relationship between cellular state abundance and clinical outcomes remaining consistent across most states (Fig. 3B). After adjusting for age, sex, HPV infection status, radiotherapy, and AJCC tumor stage in a multivariate Cox model, the independent prognostic effect of NK S02 cells was attenuated (Supplementary Fig. 3B). This attenuation likely stems from the enrichment of NK S02 cells in early-stage tumors (AJCC I/II) (Fig. 6G). A focused analysis of five epithelial and four NK cell states revealed notable differences in patient survival. Specifically, survival varied among the five epithelial cell states (p = 8.7e−02, log-rank test) and the four NK cell states (p = 8.5e−03, log-rank test) (Fig. 3C, D). These findings were corroborated in independent validation cohorts (Supplementary Fig. 3C, D). By integrating clinical stage data, comparative analysis revealed that S02 and S05 epithelial cells, which were enriched in HPV-negative samples, were predominantly associated with advanced tumor stage, whereas S03 HPV-positive-enriched epithelial cells correlated with earlier tumor stage (Fig. 3E). In stage I–II tumor samples, the NK S02 state was more abundant than any other NK-cell state (Fig. 3G). Subsequent survival analyses demonstrated that S05 epithelial cells, which were enriched in HPV-negative patients, were associated with poor survival outcomes, whereas S02 NK cells, which were enriched in HPV-positive patients, correlated with improved survival in patients with HNSCC (Fig. 3F, H). Our findings show that distinct cellular states associated with HPV-negative and HPV-positive infection statuses have divergent prognostic implications, underscoring their potential utility in patient stratification and therapeutic decision-making.

Functional profiling of cell states in HNSCC for HPV infection

We conducted enrichment analyses of hallmark-related and immune-related pathways using a hypergeometric test based on the marker gene characteristics of each cell state. The results demonstrated that different cell states were enriched in pathways reflective of their respective cellular functions. Specifically, genes highly expressed in epithelial cells, fibroblasts, and endothelial cells were significantly enriched in cancer hallmarks (Fig. 4A). However, immune-related pathways were less prominent in these cell types (Supplementary Fig. 4). Conversely, genes highly expressed in immune cell subpopulations were enriched across multiple immune-related pathways (Supplementary Fig. 4). Epithelial cells, identified as the principal tumor tissue origin in HNSCC15, demonstrate heterogeneous enrichment of hallmark-related pathways contingent upon the HPV infection status. This heterogeneity suggests that differences in HPV infection may drive distinct malignant transformations in patients. Specifically, among the five defined epithelial cell states (S01–S05), genes associated with the p53 signaling pathway were significantly enriched in states S01 (p = 3.737e−06), S02 (p = 2.595e−06), and S04 (p = 1.060e−02), whereas such enrichment was absent in states S03 and S05. These findings imply that epithelial cells S01, S02, and S04 may correspond to early differentiation or predeterioration phases within epithelial subpopulations. Furthermore, S02 and S04 epithelial cells were enriched in pathways related to reactive oxygen species (epithelial cell S02: p = 6.649e−10; epithelial cell S04: p = 1.061e−03) and xenobiotic metabolism (epithelial cell S02: p = 5.276e−08; epithelial cell S04: p = 2.563e−03), indicating p53-mediated stress responses. Notably, S05 was characterized by enrichment in E2F targets (p = 9.758e−05), MTORC1 signaling (p = 2.493e−03), and MYC target gene sets (v1 and v2), reflecting pronounced cell cycle activity and increased metabolic processes. In contrast, only S03 epithelial cells were enriched in the interferon-γ response and inflammatory response pathways (p = 3.118e−04), which is consistent with a “hot” tumor phenotype (Fig. 4A). Subsequent single-sample gene-set enrichment analysis (ssGSEA) revealed that tumors enriched for the S03 epithelial cells displayed the highest inflammatory response scores, significantly exceeding those seen in S05-enriched samples (p = 1.596e−08). Additionally, samples enriched in S02 NK cells displayed high antigen processing and presentation scores, which is consistent with the highly immunogenic microenvironment associated with HPV infection (Fig. 4B, C).

Gene Ontology (GO) analysis of biological processes further revealed indicated that epithelial cell S05 is associated with mitochondrial dynamics and overexpression of the immune-suppressive gene MAGEA4, which is consistent with its high-proliferative, late-stage, and p53-inactive phenotype (Supplementary Fig. 5A). In contrast, the epithelial cell S03 was linked to leukocyte migration (p = 4.552e−06), supporting its classification as an HPV-positive immune-hot phenotype (Supplementary Fig. 5A). Simplified enrichment analyses using the simplifyEnrichment package revealed that epithelial cell S05 downregulates immune-related processes, resulting in diminished immune activity. Conversely, S02 NK cells upregulate features related to cell proliferation and activation, and enhance bacterial defense responses (Fig. 4D, E). Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) of the GO terms corroborated these findings, confirming the reduced immune activity in the HPV-negative-associated epithelial cell S05 (Fig. 4D, F), whereas the immune activity of the HPV-associated S02 NK cell was associated with an activated, cytotoxic effector phenotype (Fig. 4E, G). These results confirm that gene expression alterations across distinct cellular states influence their functional properties, with functional heterogeneity closely associated with HPV infection status.

Characterization of multicellular ecosystems in HNSCC for HPV infection

Tumors represent intricate ecosystems composed of spatially and temporally interconnected cellular states. Deciphering these ecosystems is essential for elucidating the mechanisms underlying cancer development and progression. In this study, we investigated patterns of cell state co-occurrence by employing the EcoTyper framework to reconstruct the fundamental cellular communities within HNSCC. Our analysis revealed six notably cohesive cellular communities, referred to as colorectal ecotypes (Fig. 5A, B). By aggregating cell-state abundance profiles and evaluating the composition of CEs in a continuous manner, we validated the reproducibility of seven CEs across two independent single-cell RNA sequencing datasets and two bulk validation cohorts. The six identified CEs exhibited substantial heterogeneity in their constituent cell types and cellular states, comprising between three and twelve cooccurring cellular states per community (Fig. 5B). To further characterize these ecotypes, we examined their cellular and clinical attributes (Fig. 5C). Notably, CE1 and CE2 demonstrated the greatest diversity of cell types and were predominantly observed in HPV-positive patients. Conversely, CE5 and CE6 consisted of only three cell types and were associated mainly with HPV-negative patients, suggesting that HPV-positive HNSCC patients may harbor more complex microenvironmental compositions (Fig. 5D). CE3, which included nine cell types, was evenly distributed among both the HPV-positive and the HPV-negative patients. This ecotype encompassed a variety of immune, epithelial, and stromal cells and was present across AJCC stages II, III, and IV. In contrast, CE5 and CE6, which were enriched in HPV-negative patients, were characterized as immune-cold and exhibited a low abundance of most immune cell populations (Fig. 5C). Previous studies have demonstrated that CEs profiling enhances the prediction of clinical outcomes8,10,14. Consistent with these findings, our survival analysis revealed significant differences in patient outcomes across distinct CEs (p = 0.054 in the bulk-data1 cohort; p = 0.0002 in the bulk-data2 cohort, log-rank tests) (Fig. 5E, F).

To investigate whether variations in gene expression contribute to the TME in HNSCC, we conducted functional analyses of the different CEs. Our results indicate that distinct ecotypes promote tumor progression through diverse molecular pathways. CE2 exclusively captures oxidative, chemical, and LPS stress responses, whereas CE4 drives epithelial, neuroepithelial, and odontoblastic differentiation while coordinately regulating microtubule depolymerization and filopodium assembly (Supplementary Fig. 6B). Collectively, through ecotype feature analysis, we identified ecotypes specific to HPV infection status and revealed that differential gene expression across these ecotypes drives distinct biological processes that contribute to tumor progression in a heterogeneous manner. Additionally, our findings indicate that compared with their HPV-negative counterparts, HPV-positive tumors exhibit more robust immune responses.

Dissecting the spatial patterns of cellular states in HNSCC for HPV infection

To elucidate the spatial colocalization and interactions of cellular states within human HNSCC, we analyzed spatial transcriptomics data derived from three patients with HNSCC, comprising two HPV-negative patients and one HPV-positive patient. By integrating characteristic gene expression scores, we evaluated the spatial distribution of cell state scores across these samples. Our analysis revealed that distinct cell states not only cooccur within individual tumors but also that cells belonging to the same ecotype preferentially localize within identical tumor regions (Fig. 6A). More specifically, compared with those from different ecotypes, cell states within the same ecotype exhibited significantly stronger spatial correlations, indicating elevated expression of ecotype-specific cell states in spatially proximate cells (Fig. 6B). Furthermore, ecotypes tended to cluster within spatially discrete tumor regions. We further examined the HPV-positive samples CE2 and NK cell S02 alongside the HPV-negative samples CE4 and epithelial cell S05. Our findings indicated a significant association between HPV-specific ecotypes and cell state distributions and their respective HPV statuses. HPV-positive patients exhibited a greater abundance of cells expressing CE2-signature genes, which concurrently displayed elevated expression of the NK cell S02 genes. Conversely, the CE4 signature was predominantly expressed in HPV-negative patients, with the epithelial cell S05 component showing high signature expression within corresponding cells (Fig. 6D, E). Notably, we observed that certain HPV-negative cells manifested cell feature distributions typically associated with HPV-positive status, and vice versa, albeit at lower frequencies. The findings were consistently reproduced in an independent set of 10 spatial transcriptomic samples (7 HPV+, 3 HPV−). Spatial correlation analysis confirmed that cell states within the same ecotype exhibited significantly stronger colocalization than those from different ecotypes did (Supplementary Fig. 7A, B). All the ecotypes displayed significant spatial aggregation (Supplementary Fig. 7C). In addition, spatial mapping of signature genes demonstrated that ecotypes and their cognate cell states displayed significant spatial autocorrelation and colocalized within distinct regions of the tumor (Supplementary Fig. 8).

This observation implies the existence of shared underlying biological pathways or processes across different ecotypes and cell states. Overall, variations in HPV infection status appear to drive the predominance of distinct ecotypes and cell states corresponding to their respective infection profiles.

Ecosystems and cellular states predict therapeutic benefits in HNSCC

The efficacy of drug treatments is influenced by the TME ecotype and cellular state8,10,14. Immunotherapy aims to modulate the tumor microenvironment and activate the immune system to exert antitumor effects, thereby playing a pivotal role in improving the prognosis of patients with HNSCC16,17. We subsequently investigated whether specific ecosystem profiles and cellular states could serve as predictors of immunotherapy response. Our analysis revealed that compared with the nonresponder group, the immune therapy responder group presented higher expression levels of the ecosystem CE2, which is associated with HPV positivity. Stratification by the median characteristic score revealed a greater proportion of samples with high CE2 signature scores in the responder cohort. Furthermore, expression patterns of the S02 NK cells, components of CE2, exhibited concordant expression patterns. In contrast, the CE4 feature, linked to HPV-negative ecosystems, was more prominent in the nonresponder group, with samples exhibiting high CE4 expression significantly enriched among nonresponders. Similarly, S05 epithelial cells within CE4 displayed a consistent predictive capacity (Fig. 7A). Given the complex cellular heterogeneity of these ecosystems, we further assessed therapeutic benefit predictions by comparing survival outcomes between responders and nonresponders. Notably, major chemotherapeutic agents and targeted compounds, including cisplatin, paclitaxel, fluorouracil, docetaxel, afatinib, palbociclib, and ribociclib, which are currently utilized or under investigation for HNSCC treatment18,19 demonstrated variable efficacy across samples enriched with distinct cellular states (Fig. 7B). Specifically, the use of chemotherapeutic agents such as docetaxel, cisplatin, and 5-fluorouracil is limited across multiple cellular states. Both palbociclib and ribociclib, which are selective CDK4/6 inhibitors, exhibit heterogeneous therapeutic efficacy against HPV-enriched epithelial S05 cells. In patient samples enriched with S05 epithelial cells, those classified as erlotinib-sensitive tended to have longer survival (p = 0.061), whereas ribociclib-sensitive patients had significantly shorter survival (p = 0.041). Conversely, samples enriched with the NK cell subtype S02, and sensitivity to ribociclib were associated with longer survival (p = 0.037) (Fig. 7B–D).

Furthermore, tumors enriched in the NK-cell subtype S02 showed longer survival under JAK inhibitor treatment (p = 0.015) (Fig. 7E). Prior studies have established that persistent JAK/STAT signaling is a key risk determinant for HPV-driven malignancies20,21. JAK Kinase Inhibition Abrogates STAT3 Activation and Head and Neck Squamous Cell Carcinoma Tumor Growth22. JAK kinase inhibition disrupts STAT3 activation and suppresses tumor growth in HNSCC. Comparative analysis of STAT3 expression in the discovery single-cell cohort and the TCGA-HNSCC cohort revealed elevated STAT3 levels within HPV-positive related ecosystems (Fig. 7G, Supplementary Fig. 7B). Collectively, these findings indicate that resolving cellular states and ecotypes can capture biological signals with predictive value in drug treatment response.

Discussion

HPV infection significantly shapes the TME of HNSCC, influencing cellular states and the composition of the ecosystem, which subsequently affects clinical outcomes and therapeutic response. This investigation employed the EcoTyper framework to perform a comprehensive analysis of cellular states and ecosystem landscapes associated with HPV infection in HNSCC. By utilizing single-cell transcriptomic data, we identified 46 discrete cellular states spanning 12 principal cell types and confirmed the reproducibility of these states across independent single-cell and bulk RNA sequencing cohorts. Spatial transcriptomics further substantiated the colocalization of these cellular states within shared ecosystems.

Our results demonstrated a robust association between HPV infection status and the composition of cellular states and ecosystems within the HNSCC TME. Notably, cellular states exhibited marked heterogeneity between HPV-positive and HPV-negative patients. For example, the epithelial cell subtype predominantly observed in HPV-negative tumors was associated with adverse patient outcomes. This cell subtype is characterized by the enrichment of E2F targets, MTORC1 signaling, and MYC target gene sets (v1 and v2), which are indicative of pronounced cell cycle activity and elevated metabolic processes. Conversely, HPV-positive patients manifested a more immunologically active TME, with epithelial cell states enriched in these samples displaying enhanced immune infiltration. These findings corroborate prior studies suggesting that HPV infection modulates immune responses and the TME. These HPV-linked differences in cellular-state composition create transcriptionally distinct ecotypes that fuel tumor progression via differential gene expression. The CE2 ecotype, identified as an immune-hot tumor microenvironment, exhibited elevated expression of NFKBID, a gene implicated in inflammatory immune responses (p = 1.088e−139, log2FC = 1.510) (Supplementary Data 1, Supplementary Table 6). Ladinin-1 (LAD1), an anchoring filament protein, is associated with multiple cancer types, including colorectal, pulmonary, breast, and gastric malignancies23. The overexpression of LAD1 is generally correlated with enhanced tumor invasion and metastasis and unfavorable clinical outcomes. The CE4 ecotype may facilitate malignant progression through high LAD1 expression (p < 2.2e−16, log2FC = 3.369) (Supplementary Data 1, Supplementary Table 6), which attenuates the ubiquitin-dependent degradation of vimentin, potentially influencing mesenchymal cell characteristics and thereby promoting tumor invasion and metastatic dissemination. Furthermore, investigations in esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC) have demonstrated that elevated levels of LAMP3 and IDO1 contribute substantially to the immunosuppressive TME24. In this study, CE4 showed high LAMP3 expression (p < 2.2e−16, log2FC = 4.367) (Supplementary Data 1, Supplementary Table 6).

The clinical ramifications of this study are substantial. Distinct cellular states were closely correlated with patient outcomes, indicating their potential utility as prognostic biomarkers. For instance, tumors enriched in the NK cell S02 cellular state were associated with heightened immune activity and improved survival outcomes, whereas enrichment of the epithelial cell S05 correlated with immunosuppression and worse prognosis. Moreover, the distribution of cellular states may inform therapeutic decision-making. Patients exhibiting HPV-positive-related CE2 and NK cell S02 states demonstrated greater responsiveness to immunotherapy than those with HPV-negative-associated CE4 and epithelial cell S05, who experienced diminished immunotherapeutic efficacy. Predictive modeling of responses to 198 pharmacological agents revealed that therapeutic strategies could be tailored on the basis of cellular state enrichment. The composition of cell states significantly affects drug efficacy. Although chemotherapeutic agents such docetaxel, cisplatin, and 5-fluorouracil are widely employed in international clinical trials25, their efficacy is limited across various cell states, with no significant difference in survival between sensitive and resistant groups, especially in the presence of S04 dendritic cells, where the risk in the drug-sensitive group actually increases. This may be related to the chemotherapy-induced death of proliferating immune cells. Through a systematic comparison of the tumor microenvironmental heterogeneity in HPV-negative and HPV-positive patients with HNSCC, we identified distinct ecotypes and cellular states specific to each group. This analysis not only elucidated the microenvironmental basis underlying the favorable prognosis of HPV+ patients but also characterized the cellular states driving poor outcomes in HPV+ individuals. On the basis of these findings, we proposed corresponding therapeutic strategies. In patient samples enriched with S05 epithelial cells, the palbociclib-labeled “sensitive” cohort tended toward improved outcomes, whereas the sensitive cohort receiving ribociclib exhibited reduced survival. Previous studies have shown that erlotinib and gefitinib suppress tumor cell proliferation and survival by targeting the EGFR downstream signaling pathway26,27. Sensitive samples enriched with S05 epithelial cells were associated with longer survival (erlotinib (p = 0.045) and gefitinib (p = 0.045)). Pyridostatin was associated with improved survival in sensitive groups across various cell state-enriched samples, including both the HPV-related epithelial cell S05 (p = 0.037) and S03 (p = 1.365e−04), indicating its potential therapeutic application in HNSCC (Fig. 7B). Notably, patients enriched in the NK-cell subtype S02, which was predicted to be sensitive to JAK inhibitors, exhibited significantly longer survival in computational models.

This study had several limitations. First, the data analysis was performed under the assumption that clinical variables are randomly distributed across different HPV statuses, and the lack of information, including age and smoking history, in the single-cell sequencing datasets makes it difficult to consider these residual confounding factors. Second, immune cell states and HPV-related ecotypes were inferred from transcriptomic data and computational models, and further validation through immunohistochemistry, multiplex imaging, or cytokine profiling in clinical specimens is needed. Moreover, the present ecotype model was constructed with major immune cell lineages to ensure transferability and lacks the granularity to capture several clinically relevant subsets. For example, antibody-secreting plasma cells may play distinct immunomodulatory roles from those of their B-cell progenitors, and gamma-delta T cells could provide cytotoxic surveillance that is absent in the Treg pool. Similarly, classical monocytes, nonclassical patrollers, and tumor-associated macrophages occupy discrete ecological niches that may differentially affect survival in HPV tumors. Future single-cell atlases powered by multimodal indexing and many more cells will be necessary to deconvolve these rare populations without compromising statistical stability and to integrate them into ecotype frameworks that remain compatible with routine bulk transcriptomics. Finally, the potential agents we identified for the treatment of HNSCC patients with different HPV statuses need to be validated by ex vivo drug sensitivity testing or clinical tests.

In summary, this study delineated the heterogeneity of cellular states induced by HPV infection within the TME of HNSCC, thereby substantiating the hypothesis that HPV infection engenders distinct cellular state landscapes. We identified significant associations between these cellular states and both clinical outcomes and therapeutic responses, underscoring the influence of such heterogeneity on patient clinical characteristics. These findings contribute insights into the pathogenesis of HNSCC and have the potential to catalyze further research in this domain. Moreover, our results suggest promising avenues for the development of innovative diagnostic biomarkers and therapeutic targets. The identification of specific cellular state markers may increase prognostic accuracy and facilitate the implementation of personalized treatment regimens.

Methods

Single-cell sequencing of HNSCC

We obtained four HNSCC scRNA-Seq datasets from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) for analysis. GSE234933 (HPV+ n = 8; HPV- n = 15)28 and GSE226620 (HPV+ n = 6; HPV- n = 14)29 were chosen as training datasets, which included 29 HPV-positive and 14 HPV-negative samples. To validate our findings at the single-cell level, we utilized 36 samples with HPV infection status information from the GSE182227 (HPV+ n = 5; HPV- n = 11)30 and GSE181919 (HPV+ n = 12; HPV- n = 8)31 cohorts as an independent validation set. Independent analyses were performed for both the single-cell training cohort and the validation cohort. The sample information for the single-cell sequencing datasets is provided in (Supplementary Data 1, Supplementary Table 1).

Utilizing Seurat, we filtered out low-quality cells with mitochondrial gene proportions exceeding 20% or with fewer than 1000 or more than 5000 detected genes11. To improve doublet detection accuracy and ensure cell population purity, we separately assessed different samples using three established methods in R: scDblFinder, the hybrid package score from scds, and the doubletCells algorithm from scran. Each method was configured with an expected doublet rate of 0.6% per 500 cells. Cells identified as doublets by at least two methods were classified as such30. The single-cell data were subsequently filtered, merged, and normalized using the default parameters of the LogNormalize method to mitigate technical variations. Batch effects were corrected using the Harmony package to address technical differences arising from distinct sample sources or sequencing batches32. Subsequently, cell populations were subsequently annotated based on the basis of clustering outcomes and characteristic gene expression patterns.

Collection of public bulk HNSCC transcriptome

We collected genome-wide gene expression profiles and corresponding survival information for a total of 876 patients with HNSCC from five independent public datasets. These datasets were obtained from the GEO, CPTAC, and the TCGA databases. To ensure robust validation, we divided the 876 HNSCC samples into two independent validation cohorts on the basis of sample size. Cohort 1, comprising 475 samples, was constructed from TCGA. Cohort 2, consisting of 401 samples, was assembled from multiple sources, including CPTAC-HNSCC (n = 198), GSE4161333 (n = 97), GSE4274333 (n = 74), and GSE329234 (n = 32). The sample information for public bulk HNSCC transcriptome datasets is provided in (Supplementary Data 1, Supplementary Table 2). For the microarray data, we excluded probes that could not be mapped to any known genes and computed the average expression value for genes with multiple probes. To address potential batch effects in Cohort 2, which arose from differences across experimental platforms, we applied the ‘sva’ R package to adjust and remove batch effects from the combined dataset34.

Discovery of HNSCC cell states

First, we extracted cell type-specific genes from single-cell RNA-seq data by integrating cell annotation information. We identified genes with a Q value < 0.05 (two-sided Wilcoxon test, with Benjamini-Hochberg correction for multiple hypothesis testing) as cell-type-specific expressed genes. We subsequently employed NMF to decompose the correlation matrix to obtain different cell type states. After identifying cell states based on the basis of the correlation matrix, we conducted differential expression analysis between different cell states of the same cell type to identify the top 1000 genes with the greatest fold changes in each cell type. We extracted the expression profiles corresponding to these 1000 genes. Each gene was scaled to have a mean of 0 and unit variance. Additionally, cell type-specific expression matrices were individually processed using the posneg transformation. We performed NMF analysis on the expression matrix to rediscover cell states. The results were filtered to remove genes with zero expression in more than half of the samples and cell states with fewer than 10 marker genes9.

Discovery of HNSCC ecotypes

We employed EcoTyper to identify conserved cell communities within the HNSCC TME. To quantify the overlap between cell states, we calculated the Jaccard index to quantify the similarity between each pair of cell states. Specifically, for each sample, we determined the enrichment of the 12 cell types and their corresponding states. By computing the Jaccard index for all possible combinations of cell states, we constructed a Jaccard matrix to quantify the overlap between different cell states. The significance of the overlap between cell states was assessed using the hypergeometric test. We set the Jaccard matrix values of non significant sample overlaps to zero to minimize noise introduced by spurious overlaps. Finally, we used unsupervised hierarchical clustering to determine the composition of ecotypes within the HNSCC TME. Ecotypes composed of fewer than three cell states were excluded. Additionally, we quantified the ecotype composition across different samples by normalizing the sum of all ecotypes in each sample to 19.

Recovery of HNSCC-related cell states and ecotypes from independent datasets

We utilized the EcoTyper reference-guided annotation framework to recover and quantify HNSCC-related cell states and ecotypes within an independent validation cohort. Specifically, EcoTyper integrates the feature gene weight matrix of cell states identified in the training set and employs an NMF model in the validation set to determine a coefficient matrix for each cell type, with each cell state represented as a weight. Prior to analysis, each gene in the external dataset was log2-transformed and scaled to unit variance to ensure consistency across different platforms. This process further validated the reproducibility of the identified HNSCC-specific cell states and ecotypes at both the tissue and single-cell levels9.

Survival analysis

To further elucidate the impact of cell states and ecotype compositions within the TME on patient survival, we employed the survival package to compare survival differences among samples enriched for distinct cell states or ecotypes. Additionally, we analyzed the survival changes in patients whose enriched for specific cell states were enriched compared with those in other patients. To account for the influence of cell state abundance on patient survival, a Cox proportional hazards model was utilized.

Feature gene expression score and enrichment analysis

To investigate the functional changes among different cell states of the same cell type, we conducted functional enrichment analysis based on the characteristic expressed genes characteristic of each cell state identified by EcoTyper using the clusterProfiler package35. Moreover, we downloaded hallmark and immune-related pathways from the MsigDB and ImmPort projects, respectively36,37. We assessed the changes in cancer- and immune-related features across different cell states using a hypergeometric test8. Additionally, we analyzed the specific functional pathway changes in different states of the same cell type using single-sample gene set enrichment analysis (ssGSEA)38.

Changes in cellular states and ecotypes at the spatial transcriptomics level

To characterize the spatial organization of cellular states and ecotypes, we first derived signature gene sets for each ecotype from the single-cell dataset using the FindAllMarkers function (Seurat) and concurrently extracted the cell-state-specific genes output by the EcoTyper algorithm. These signatures were subsequently used to compute enrichment scores for each spatial transcriptomic spot via AddModuleScore, reflecting the transcriptional signature strength of each state/ecotype at that spatial location. To assess the colocalization of cell states at the spatial transcriptomics level, we computed pairwise Spearman correlation coefficients between the feature score changes of different cell states within the same spot. The spatial aggregation of spatial ecotypes (SEs) was examined using Moran’s I, which takes into account the relative SE feature in each spot and its immediate neighboring area39.

Drug treatment response

We utilized TIDE (Tumor Immune Dysfunction and Exclusion: http://tide.dfci.harvard.edu/) to assess patient responses to immunotherapy40. Concurrently, we employed the R package “OncoPredict” to predict the responses to 198 drugs in the TCGA-HNSCC and CPTAC-HNSCC datasets41. Specifically, we trained the model using cell line drug screening datasets from the Genomics of Drug Sensitivity in Cancer (GDSC). During this process, genes exhibiting low variance (20%) were excluded to enhance the accuracy and stability of the model. The model was subsequently applied to predict the responses to 198 drugs in the TCGA-HNSCC and CPTAC-HNSCC datasets. We used methods similar to those reported by Zhang et al. to explore the associations between cell status and drug response10. All patients with HNSCC were first stratified into sensitive and resistant groups on the basis of the median estimated half-maximal inhibitory concentration (IC50) of each drug. If patients enriched for a specific cell state had significantly better survival in the drug-sensitive cohort, we concluded that patients with high enrichment for this state would benefit from drug treatment. Conversely, patients highly enriched for this state are unlikely to benefit from the drug if the sensitive cohort has worse survival.

Statistics and reproducibility

All the statistical analyses were conducted using R (version 4.3.0). For survival analysis, Kaplan–Meier curves were generated, and the log-rank test was used to compare differences in overall survival rates among groups. To assess whether the corresponding features were risk or protective factors for survival, a Cox proportional hazards model was used. The Wilcoxon rank-sum test was used to evaluate significant differences between groups. A p-value threshold of less than 0.05 was used to determine statistical significance. Statistical details for all the quantitative analyses shown in the figures are provided in (Supplementary Data 1, Supplementary Table 4).

Intentional retrieval of a protruding ostial left main drug-eluting stent using a snare: a bail-out case report
Authors: Mohamed Sarsari, Houda Bachri, Oualid Kerrouani, Jamila Zarzur, Mohamed Cherti, Abdelsalam Bensaaud, Ahmet Taha Sahin, John Kanakakis, Dimitrios Terentes-Printzios, Deepti Ranganathan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPercutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) of the ostial left main coronary artery (LMCA) is technically demanding and carries specific risks related to inaccurate stent positioning. Excessive stent protrusion into the aorta is a rare but potentially catastrophic complication.Case summaryWe report the case of a 60-year-old man with diabetes mellitus admitted for an acute coronary syndrome equivalent to ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction, related to a critical ostial LMCA stenosis. During emergent PCI, patient agitation led to excessive proximal protrusion (>8 mm) of a drug-eluting stent into the ascending aorta. Balloon optimization failed and resulted in stent deformation. Given the high risk of thrombosis, embolization, and aortic valve interference, intentional stent retrieval using a snare device was successfully performed, followed by optimal re-stenting of the LMCA ostium. The patient had an uneventful recovery.DiscussionIntentional extraction of a protruding coronary stent is exceptionally rare, particularly in the ostial LMCA. This case highlights the feasibility and safety of snare retrieval as a bail-out strategy when performed early, before endothelialization, and in carefully selected patients.Keywords: Left main coronary artery, Aorto-ostial lesion, Stent protrusion, Stent retrieval, Intravascular imaging, Case report

Introduction

Percutaneous coronary intervention of the left main coronary artery has emerged as a valid alternative to coronary artery bypass grafting in selected patients, particularly in isolated ostial or shaft lesions.1,2 However, aorto-ostial LMCA lesions remain among the most technically challenging scenarios in interventional cardiology. Precise stent positioning is crucial, as even minimal malposition may result in incomplete lesion coverage or excessive stent protrusion into the aorta. Stent protrusion is associated with an increased risk of stent thrombosis, restenosis, systemic embolization, and aortic valve injury. Management strategies are not standardized, and intentional stent extraction has only rarely been reported.

Case presentation

A 64-year-old man with a history of hypertension, type 2 diabetes mellitus, and active smoking presented with acute chest pain suggestive of myocardial infarction.

At admission, blood pressure was 140/88 mmHg, and heart rate was 90 bpm, without signs of heart failure.

Electrocardiography demonstrated ST-segment elevation in lead aVR with diffuse ST-segment depression in inferior and lateral leads, suggesting critical LMCA ischaemia (Figure 1A).

Transthoracic echocardiography showed preserved left ventricular systolic function with LVEF 55%.

Given the clinical presentation consistent with a STEMI equivalent, the patient was transferred urgently to the catheterization laboratory.

Coronary angiography

Coronary angiography was performed via right radial access.

The right coronary artery was angiographically normal. The left coronary system demonstrated a severe ostial stenosis of the left main coronary artery (Figure 1B).

Percutaneous coronary intervention

PCI was performed using a non-selective EBU 3.5 6F guiding catheter, as a JL guiding catheter was not available in our centre.

Two 0.014″ SAMURAI guidewires were positioned: one in the left anterior descending artery and one in the left circumflex artery

Lesion preparation was performed using a 4.0 × 15 mm non-compliant balloon.

A 4.5 × 20 mm drug-eluting stent was then implanted from the LMCA toward the circumflex artery.

This strategy was selected because the circumflex artery diameter was comparable to that of the left main trunk, minimizing vessel size mismatch that would have occurred with an LM-to-LAD strategy.

Stent sizing was determined using quantitative coronary angiography software integrated in the General Electric angiography platform.

Intravascular imaging (IVUS or OCT) would have been highly valuable in this complex ostial lesion; however, these imaging modalities were not available in our catheterization laboratory.

Procedural complication

During stent positioning, the patient developed severe agitation despite sedation with midazolam, requiring escalation to propofol sedation.

Control angiography revealed displacement of the circumflex guidewire, requiring repositioning.

After restoring wire position and repeating balloon advancement, angiographic control initially appeared satisfactory.

However, after performing stent boost imaging, two unexpected findings were observed (Figure 2A):

Given the potential long-term risks, we decided to attempt optimization.

Because the patient was clinically stable and the procedure had already been prolonged, a staged reintervention was scheduled.

Repeat echocardiography in the intensive care unit confirmed stent protrusion of approximately 8 mm into the ascending aorta without evidence of aortic regurgitation (Figure 2B).

Cardiac surgeons were consulted prior to the reintervention and agreed with the percutaneous approach while remaining available for surgical backup if necessary.

Stent retrieval procedure

The procedure was performed via 6F femoral access using an EBU 3.5 guiding catheter.

Final PCI result

After successful extraction, a new SYNERGY XD drug-eluting stent (4.0 × 20 mm) was implanted from the LMCA toward the LCx with approximately 2 mm ostial protrusion to ensure complete ostial coverage (Figure 5).

The final angiographic result demonstrated excellent stent expansion and optimal ostial positioning.

The patient remained stable and was discharged after three days in intensive care without complications.

Discussion

Aorto-ostial LMCA lesions account for approximately 5%–8% of LMCA PCI indications and are associated with higher procedural complexity. Accurate identification of the true ostium is often challenging due to vessel foreshortening and overlap with the aortic wall.

Intravascular imaging, particularly intravascular ultrasound (IVUS), has become a cornerstone in LMCA PCI.3,4 Contemporary management of left main coronary artery disease emphasizes meticulous procedural planning, systematic use of intravascular imaging, and individualized decision-making based on anatomical complexity and clinical presentation.5 IVUS allows precise assessment of lesion morphology, reference vessel diameter, and ostial localization and significantly reduces the risk of geographic miss and stent protrusion. Optical coherence tomography (OCT), while limited by blood clearance requirements, may also provide valuable information in selected cases. Current guidelines strongly recommend IVUS guidance for LMCA interventions.

Excessive stent protrusion (>5 mm) into the aorta is uncommon but carries serious risks, including stent thrombosis, embolic stroke, and aortic valve damage. Several management strategies have been described. Balloon optimization using dedicated ostial techniques may be effective in mild protrusion but is often unfeasible in severe cases. Lateral stent crushing has been reported but exposes patients to embolic and mechanical complications.

Intentional stent retrieval using a snare has been described mainly in cases of stent loss or migration, with very few reports involving the coronary circulation and exceptionally rare involvement of the ostial LMCA. Compared with previously published cases, our report is unique due to the anatomical location, the degree of protrusion, and the intentional nature of the retrieval as a planned bail-out strategy. To the best of our knowledge, intentional snare retrieval of a protruding drug-eluting stent from the ostial left main coronary artery has not been previously reported.

Early intervention before endothelialization, careful patient selection, and operator expertise are critical prerequisites for the success of this approach.

Conclusion

Ostial LMCA PCI requires meticulous technique and precise stent positioning.

Severe stent protrusion into the ascending aorta is a rare but potentially life-threatening complication.

Management must be individualized and may include balloon optimization, lateral stent crushing, or intentional stent retrieval.

This case illustrates that snare-assisted stent extraction can be successfully performed when other strategies fail, provided that the procedure is carried out carefully in experienced hands.

Preventive measures remain essential and include accurate ostial identification, optimal angiographic projections, adequate patient sedation, and the use of intravascular imaging whenever available.

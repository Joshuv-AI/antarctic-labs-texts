Development and validation of a nomogram for predicting non-response to tocilizumab in thyroid eye disease: a retrospective study
Authors: Yingxue Mei, Jiacheng Yan, Ziyue Zhou, Don O Kikkawa, Shuyi Liu, Wei Lu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeThis study aims to integrate single photon emission computed tomography/computed tomography (SPECT/CT) parameters with other biomarkers to develop a predictive model for assessing the risk of non-response to tocilizumab (TCZ) therapy in patients with thyroid eye disease (TED).MethodsUnivariate Cox regression, least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) method, and multivariate Cox regression were used to identify predictors of non-response to TCZ and construct a nomogram. The model’s performance was validated using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves, calibration curves, and decision curve analysis (DCA).ResultsIn this retrospective study, 195 patients with moderate-to-severe TED who received subcutaneous TCZ were included. The nomogram included four predictors: intraocular pressure (IOP), thyroglobulin (TG) level, maximum standardized uptake value (SUVmax) of extraocular muscles (EOMs), and SUVmax of the lacrimal glands. In the training set, the model achieved 1-, 2-, and 3-month area under the ROC curve (AUC) values of 0.787 (95% CI: 0.681–0.893), 0.747 (95% CI: 0.641–0.853), and 0.834 (95% CI: 0.734–0.934), respectively. In the validation set, the corresponding AUC values were 0.761 (95% CI: 0.567–0.955), 0.799 (95% CI: 0.663–0.934), and 0.833 (95% CI: 0.655-1). Calibration curves demonstrated good agreement between predicted and observed probabilities, while DCA confirmed that both the nomogram and individual predictors provided varying degrees of clinical net benefit.ConclusionsThis study successfully integrated SPECT/CT parameters with other biomarkers to develop a nomogram for predicting non-response to TCZ in moderate-to-severe TED. The model, incorporating four predictive factors, may help personalize treatment strategies and improve clinical decision-making for TED patients.Keywords: Thyroid eye disease, Tocilizumab, Response prediction, Nomogram, Retrospective study

Introduction

Thyroid eye disease (TED), also known as Graves’ ophthalmopathy, is an autoimmune disorder linked to thyroid dysfunction [1]. The estimated prevalence of TED ranges from 155 to 250 cases per 100,000 individuals [2], with approximately 208,000 new cases diagnosed annually in China [3]. TED primarily affects the orbital and peri-orbital soft tissues, presenting symptoms such as upper eyelid retraction, edema, erythema, and exophthalmos. While the majority of TED patients experience a relatively mild course, about 3% to 5% of individuals suffer from persistent progression, characterized by severe pain, inflammation, and complications like corneal ulceration or compressive optic neuropathy [1, 4]. The disease not only compromises visual function but also negatively impacts patients’ appearance, significantly decreasing their quality of life [5].

The current standard for assessing TED activity and determining treatment regimens relies on the Clinical Activity Score (CAS) [5, 6]. However, CAS is limited in that it only captures ocular inflammatory manifestations and subjective pain perception, overlooking pathological damage in the posterior orbital region, which affects its predictive accuracy [7]. Intravenous glucocorticoid (IVGC) therapy is currently the recommended first-line treatment for moderate-to-severe active TED [6, 8]. Despite its efficacy, approximately 20% of patients do not respond adequately to IVGC [9], necessitating the use of second-line biological therapies for glucocorticoid-refractory cases. Tocilizumab (TCZ), a recombinant humanized monoclonal antibody targeting the interleukin-6 receptor (IL-6R), has been shown to be effective in treating TED and demonstrates a generally favorable safety profile, with rare serious adverse events [10–12]. However, the therapeutic response to TCZ remains heterogeneous, and not all TED patients benefit from this treatment.

To address the limitations of CAS, recent studies have begun integrating multiple biomarkers to enhance treatment response predictions [5, 13]. Single photon emission computed tomography/computed tomography (SPECT/CT), a radionuclide imaging technique combining functional and anatomical data, provides objective evidence of disease activity [14]. Recent studies have shown that SPECT/CT, using 99mTc-diethylene triamine pentaacetic acid (99mTc-DTPA) as a tracer, can predict the effectiveness of glucocorticoid therapy and assess TED disease activity [15–17]. However, research on predicting TCZ treatment response remains limited, with few studies integrating SPECT/CT with other biomarkers to predict treatment efficacy in TED. Therefore, this study aims to combine quantitative SPECT/CT parameters with additional biomarkers to develop a predictive model for assessing the risk of non-response to TCZ therapy in moderate-to-severe TED patients.

Methods

Study design

We conducted a retrospective study enrolling consecutive patients diagnosed with moderate-to-severe TED who presented to the Department of Ophthalmology at the Second Hospital of Dalian Medical University between January 2023 and June 2025. All patients received TCZ treatment (162 mg, subcutaneous injection weekly). Efficacy was assessed at the 1st, 2nd, and 3rd months of treatment. This study adhered to the principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki, and the research protocol was approved by the Review Committee of the Second Hospital of Dalian Medical University (No. KY2025-170-01). All participants provided written informed consent prior to enrollment. This study followed the transparent reporting of a multivariable prediction model for individual prognosis or diagnosis + artificial intelligence (TRIPOD + AI) statement when reporting [18].

Patients

Inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) Age between 18 and 80 years; (2) Confirmed diagnosis of TED based on the Bartley criteria [19]; (3) Classification of moderate-to-severe TED according to the European Group on Graves’ Orbitopathy (EUGOGO) criteria [6]; (4) Patients who received TCZ (162 mg, subcutaneous injection weekly) and had complete baseline and follow-up data [11]. Exclusion criteria included: prior orbital decompression surgery or orbital radiation therapy, dysthyroid optic neuropathy (DON), other conditions causing exophthalmos, and pregnancy or lactation.

Sample size

The study included four predictive variables. Applying the principle of 10 events per variable (10 EPV), with a training set proportion of 70%, and considering the center’s observed non-response rate to TCZ treatment in TED of approximately 30%, the required sample size for the training set was calculated to be approximately 133.3 cases. After accounting for an estimated 10% loss to follow-up, the final required sample size was determined to be at least 212 patients. 70% of the data were used for predictive variable screening and model development, while the remaining 30% were reserved for model validation [20, 21].

Data collection and definitions

Clinical data from all TED patients meeting the inclusion criteria were systematically extracted from the hospital’s Electronic Medical Record (EMR) databases. Using the date of the initial visit as a standardized reference point, clinical features collected included: age, sex, smoking history, alcohol consumption, treatment history, underlying medical conditions, onset and duration of thyroid disease and TED, restoration of thyroid function, CAS (detailed recorded all entries), exophthalmos, diplopia score, best-corrected visual acuity (BCVA), intraocular pressure (IOP), palpebral fissure height (PFH), Margin Reflex Distance (MRD)-1, MRD-2, extraocular muscle (EOM) function, tuberculosis infection of T cells, thyroid stimulating hormone (TSH), TSH receptor antibodies (TRAb), thyroglobulin (TG), thyroid peroxidase antibody (TPOAb), free triiodothyronine (fT3), free thyroxine (fT4), triiodothyronine (T3), and thyroxine (T4) [5, 6, 22, 23]. During follow-up visits, the TCZ administration regimen was recorded, and efficacy and safety were assessed at the 1st, 2nd, and 3rd months of treatment. Two ophthalmologists, each with over 10 years of clinical experience, independently evaluated the ocular characteristics. Any disagreements were adjudicated by a senior ophthalmologist with 20 years of experience. Table 1 details the definitions and assignments for each categorical variable.

Orbital SPECT/CT acquisition

SPECT/CT imaging was performed 30 min after intravenous injection of 555 MBq (15 mCi) of 99mTc-DTPA. Patients were positioned supine with their heads immobilized and eyes closed. A CT scan was performed at the orbital-auricular line with a slice thickness of 0.625 mm, pitch of 1:1, tube voltage of 140 kV, and tube current of 100 mA. SPECT imaging was performed in the same position using low-energy, high-resolution collimators, with a 64 × 64 acquisition matrix and a magnification of 1x. Each frame was acquired for 25 s, and a total of 32 frames were collected. CT, SPECT, and SPECT/CT fusion images were obtained using the Q.Metrix System 1.0 (GE Healthcare, USA) at different Sections [14]. Two nuclear medicine physicians with at least 10 years of clinical experience independently reviewed these images to select the section with the highest radionuclide uptake in the orbital region and the two adjacent sections. Maximum standardized uptake values (SUVmax) were derived for peri-orbital soft tissues, EOMs, lacrimal glands, and retrobulbar connective tissue. Any discrepancies between the two reviewers were resolved by a third nuclear medicine physician with 20 years of experience.

Outcome

The primary outcome was the lack of response to TCZ treatment. The response was defined as improvement in at least two of the following four parameters in the affected eye, without worsening in any parameter [6]: (1) Reduction in PFH by ≥ 2 mm; (2) Reduction in exophthalmos by ≥ 3 mm; (3) Improvement in eye movement by ≥ 8°; (4) Reduction of ≥ 1 point in the five CAS items (excluding spontaneous or gaze-evoked pain), with no deterioration in the other eye. Deterioration was defined as the development of DON or worsening in at least two of the four criteria. Patients who did not meet the response criteria were classified as non-responders [5].

Development and validation

The model was constructed using the training set data. Initially, univariate Cox regression analysis was applied to all collected variables to identify those potentially predictive of the primary outcome, with a threshold of p < 0.10 for selection. These variables were then refined using the tenfold cross-validated least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) method. The final set of variables was entered into a multivariate Cox regression model, from which hazard ratios (HR), 95% confidence intervals (CIs), and p-values were derived [24]. The Variance Inflation Factor (VIF) was calculated to assess potential multicollinearity [25].

The model was validated on an independent validation set by assessing three key performance characteristics: discrimination, calibration, and clinical net benefit [21]. Discrimination was quantified using the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) and its 95% CI. Calibration was assessed by plotting calibration curves to compare predicted versus actual probabilities. Decision curve analysis (DCA) was performed to evaluate the clinical net benefit at different decision thresholds.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using R software (version 4.4.2). The normality of continuous variables was assessed using the Shapiro-Wilk test. Normally distributed continuous variables were presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD), while non-normally distributed variables were reported as median (interquartile range). Categorical variables were described as frequency (percentage). Continuous variables were compared between groups using independent t-tests or Mann-Whitney U-tests, as appropriate. Comparisons of categorical variables were performed using the Chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test. All hypothesis tests were two-sided, with p < 0.05 considered statistically significant [26].

A total of 241 patients were initially enrolled in this study. After screening, 46 patients were excluded, leaving 195 patients who met the inclusion criteria. Within the final cohort, 138 patients (70.77%) were female, and 57 patients (29.23%) were male, with a mean age of 44.92 ± 12.86 years. Further intergroup baseline comparisons revealed no statistically significant differences between patients in the training and validation sets across demographic characteristics, underlying medical conditions, clinical history related to thyroid disease, ocular symptoms and signs, specialized ophthalmic examinations, laboratory tests, and imaging parameters (Table 2, p > 0.05).

Variable screening and nomogram construction

Univariate Cox regression analysis identified several factors associated with the risk of non-response to TCZ treatment in TED, including duration of thyroid disease, restoration of thyroid function, IOP, exophthalmos, CAS, conjunctival congestion, eyelid edema, TRAb level, TG level, SUVmax of EOMs, and SUVmax of lacrimal glands (Table 3, p < 0.10). Subsequently, the eleven variables were further screened using LASSO method with tenfold cross-validation. Based on Lambda-1se, four variables were retained: IOP, TG level, SUVmax of EOMs, and SUVmax of lacrimal glands (Fig. 2A and B). These four variables were then entered into a multivariate Cox regression model. Independent predictors were identified using backward stepwise regression. The final model, which included IOP, TG level, SUVmax of EOMs, and SUVmax of lacrimal glands, exhibited the minimum Akaike Information Criterion (AIC) value (AIC = 434.6), with no multicollinearity observed among the variables. The results are presented in a forest plot (Fig. 2C). Notably, SUVmax of EOMs (p = 0.002) and SUVmax of lacrimal glands (p < 0.001) were identified as independent predictors of non-response to TCZ treatment for TED (Table 3). Based on these four variables, a nomogram prediction model was constructed (Fig. 2D).

Evaluation and validation of the nomogram

The 1-, 2-, and 3-month AUC values for the training set were 0.787 (95% CI: 0.681–0.893), 0.747 (95% CI: 0.641–0.853), and 0.834 (95% CI: 0.734–0.934), respectively (Fig. 3A). The corresponding AUC values for the validation set were 0.761 (95% CI: 0.567–0.955), 0.799 (95% CI: 0.663–0.934), and 0.833 (95% CI: 0.655-1) (Fig. 3B). The calibration curve demonstrated good agreement between the predicted and observed probabilities in both the training and validation sets (Fig. 4). DCA showed that both the nomogram and each individual predictor demonstrated varying degrees of clinical net benefit on the training and validation sets (Fig. 5).

Discussion

This study developed a novel nomogram consisting of IOP, TG level, SUVmax of EOMs, and SUVmax of lacrimal glands, which accurately assessed the risk of non-response to TCZ treatment in patients with moderate-to-severe TED. The nomogram demonstrated excellent performance, with high discriminatory power and good calibration in both the training and validation sets. DCA further confirmed its clinical utility across a wide range of thresholds.

The CAS has long been the most validated scoring system for assessing TED activity [6]. However, its limitations, particularly its binary nature, should prompt caution in its application [6]. The CAS solely captures ocular inflammatory signs and subjective pain perception, while neglecting pathological damage within the posterior orbit, thus offering limited predictive value [7]. For example, one study found that using CAS to define disease activity resulted in 38.46% of patients with active TED failing to respond to IVGC therapy, while 45.45% of patients with inactive TED exhibited a positive response [27].

Imaging techniques provide objective, quantitative evidence for assessing TED activity. Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) offers high-resolution, multiparametric images that can precisely differentiate between tissues and fluids, detect subtle retrobulbar lesions, and provide superior objectivity and sensitivity compared to CAS [23, 28]. MRI-specific sequences may help quantify disease activity, predict responses to anti-inflammatory therapy, and assess the prognosis of TED [6]. However, MRI has drawbacks, including high costs, lengthy procedures, and contraindications for patients with implanted metallic devices [6, 29]. Furthermore, MRI is a multi-parameter imaging technique, requiring appropriate scanning parameters and effective integration of various settings to ensure high-quality imaging.

Nuclear imaging integrates functional metabolic data with anatomical information, tracking inflammatory activity at the molecular level [23]. Tracers such as 111Indium-DTPA, 99mTc-DTPA, Gallium-67 citrate, and Gallium-68-DOTA-NOC have been used for TED assessment within this modality [23]. Early studies, such as Colao et al.‘s 1998 work, utilized the orbital-to-brain ratio from 111Indium-octreotide SPECT scintigraphy to predict IVGC therapy efficacy [30]. More recent studies have employed SPECT/CT to provide more precise TED diagnoses, with diagnostic accuracy improving significantly when combined with other biomarkers [31]. This study’s SPECT/CT protocol, utilizing Q.Metrix software for iterative reconstruction, scatter, and attenuation correction, provided simple and reproducible quantitative uptake analysis through SUVmax, demonstrating significant advantages over MRI. Additionally, the radioactive marker 99mTc has a half-life of approximately 6.02 h, with the radioactivity being almost entirely eliminated from the body within 24 h, minimizing its potential harm to patients [32].

TCZ, a humanized monoclonal antibody targeting IL-6R, blocks the signaling pathway of proinflammatory cytokines like interleukin-6 (IL-6), which play a central role in TED pathogenesis by promoting orbital inflammation, connective tissue volume changes, adipogenesis, and fibrosis [10, 33, 34]. Elevated IL-6 expression in orbital tissues correlates with increased orbital volume in TED patients [35]. Clinical studies have confirmed TCZ’s efficacy and safety for treating moderate-to-severe TED [12, 36–38]. However, its high cost and variable efficacy underscore the importance of identifying patients unlikely to respond to this treatment, thus preventing unnecessary treatment, reducing costs, and facilitating the timely introduction of alternative therapies.

This study employed AIC-based nomogram construction to identify key factors that predict non-response to TCZ in TED patients. SUVmax of the EOMs and lacrimal glands on SPECT/CT demonstrated strong predictive power. SPECT/CT, as a fusion imaging modality, enables the acquisition of both functional metabolic and anatomical images of orbital tissues. Elevated radiotracer uptake in orbital tissues, including EOMs, lacrimal glands, optic nerves, and retrobulbar fat, correlates with increased inflammatory and edematous infiltration, reflecting disease activity in TED [39]. Previous studies have demonstrated that increased radiotracer uptake correlates with TED activity and may predict treatment efficacy [15, 17]. Our study found that an SUVmax cutoff of 1.85 for lacrimal glands and EOMs predicted TCZ efficacy, with patients showing SUVmax ≤ 1.85 having higher risk of non-response compared to those with higher SUVmax values. The variables were supported as strong, independent predictors. These findings highlight the importance of including these functional imaging parameters in future TED pharmacotherapy studies.

Huang et al. reported that the coronal and axial areas, as well as the width, of the lacrimal gland were significantly larger in patients with active or inactive TED compared to the control group. Additionally, IL-6 concentration in reflex tears positively correlated with CAS and the coronal and axial areas of the lacrimal gland. Increased inflammatory cytokines were known to play a crucial role in ocular surface damage and may also be linked to inflammation of the lacrimal gland. This suggested that the immune response triggered by autoantibody binding to the TSH receptor may contribute to ocular surface damage through lacrimal gland inflammation [40]. Orbital fibroblasts from TED patients have been shown to increase hyaluronic acid synthesis under inflammatory stimuli. Furthermore, the accumulation of granular material, mainly consisting of collagen fibrils and glycosaminoglycans, may lead to thickening of the EOMs [41]. However, the study also reported higher IL-6 expression in EOM-derived muscle progenitor cells from normal subjects compared to TED patients [41], which is inconsistent with our findings. We speculate that this discrepancy may be linked to the proinflammatory signaling pathway activated by soluble IL-6R (sIL-6R) in response to IL-6 [42]. Even if the absolute local concentration of IL-6 is not high, the amplification effect of trans-signaling mediated by sIL-6R can still drive significant inflammation and tissue remodeling.

Multivariate Cox analysis in this study revealed a negative trend between higher pre-treatment IOP and the probability of non-response to TCZ therapy. IOP has been previously shown to correlate linearly with TED duration, CAS, and levels of fT3, fT4, and TRAb. Elevated thyroid function is a known risk factor for increased IOP in TED patients [43]. In a review of 17 TED patients receiving teprotumumab, IOP decreased significantly, with mean reductions of 4.9 mmHg at week 6 and at the final follow-up, and 4.6 mmHg at week 12 [44]. Furthermore, an evaluation of TCZ in 48 patients with refractory TED reported a baseline mean IOP of 19.05 ± 4.1 mmHg, which decreased to 16.73 ± 3.4 mmHg after one year of treatment [11]. In our study, when IOP was analyzed as a dichotomous variable, a strong negative correlation was observed. However, the impact of information loss due to variable transformation has not been evaluated, so the clinical relevance of IOP as a predictive factor requires validation in studies with larger cohorts.

Although the HR of TG was close to 1 and its 95% CI spanned 1, TG was retained in the nomogram to contribute to overall predictive performance rather than serving as an independent predictor because of its consistent statistical significance and its role as part of a composite predictive model. TG was predominantly detected in the orbital tissues of TED patients, especially in the retrobulbar fibroadipose tissue, but was rarely found in the EOMs. It is speculated that TG may be transported from the thyroid gland and accumulated in the orbital tissues via a retrograde lymphatic route [45, 46]. As a heparin-binding protein, TG can bind to glycosaminoglycans, which are abundant in orbital tissues, possibly explaining its localization within the orbit [47]. TG detected in these tissues carries T4 residues, indicating its thyroid origin and suggesting a correlation with a patient’s history of radioiodine therapy [45, 46]. Based on these findings, some researchers have speculated that TG accumulated in the orbital tissues may act as a coantigen, triggering an autoimmune reaction mediated by cellular mechanisms, thereby participating in the pathogenesis of TED [45, 46]. Although both IOP and TG have been implicated in the pathogenesis of TED, neither has been integrated into predictive models for evaluating the efficacy of TCZ treatment. Therefore, future models aimed at predicting drug efficacy in TED should focus on and validate the predictive value of these two indicators.

Although this study is the first to integrate SPECT/CT imaging parameters with clinical and blood biomarkers into a nomogram to predict TCZ non-response in moderate-to-severe TED, several limitations remain. Considering a 10% ineffective sample size, the final sample size fell slightly short of the theoretical target. While this limitation did not have a substantial impact on the results, external validation in large-scale multicenter cohorts is still required to further establish the model’s generalizability. The retrospective design introduces inherent biases, and the short follow-up period limits the assessment of long-term effects. In addition, we did not compare the predictive performance of our nomogram with that of the CAS alone, despite the limitations of CAS in predicting treatment response in TED. Furthermore, the accessibility and cost of SPECT/CT may hinder its widespread clinical application. Future studies should explore more accessible and cost-effective predictive tools.

Conclusion

This study developed and validated a nomogram incorporating IOP, TG level, SUVmax of EOMs, and SUVmax of lacrimal glands to predict the risk of non-response to TCZ treatment in moderate-to-severe TED. The nomogram demonstrated good predictive ability in both the training and validation sets. This study fills a critical gap in the prediction of TCZ treatment response in TED, offering new insights into personalized treatment and precision medicine for TED patients.

Ultrasound-based multiregional radiomics nomogram for predicting recurrence in HER2-positive breast cancer
Authors: Xinran Fang, Yue Liu, Xudong Zhang, Wei Fan, Zihao Qin, Zi Yang, Jiawei Tian, Lei Zhang, Hao Cui
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeAccurate prediction of recurrence risk is crucial for personalizing therapy in human epidermal growth factor receptor 2-positive (HER2-positive) breast cancer. We aimed to develop an interpretable machine learning model integrating multimodal data to address this need.MethodsThis retrospective study enrolled 148 patients with human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2)-positive breast cancer between 2017 and 2021. On preoperative ultrasound images, the intratumoral region (region of interest B, ROIB) was manually delineated by experienced radiologists. Based on the manually segmented ROIB, the 5 mm inward inner peritumoral region (ROIC) and 5 mm outward outer peritumoral region (ROIA) were automatically generated via the built-in adaptive tool of 3D Slicer, with non-breast tissues excluded manually. Radiomic features were extracted from each ROI using PyRadiomics. A pre-fusion strategy (i.e. feature fusion) was used to construct combined feature sets (ROIA+B and ROIA+B+C [ROI All]), and the radiomic model based on ROI All was defined as Rad All. After feature selection via Student’s t-test/Mann-Whitney U test, Pearson correlation analysis, maximum relevance minimum redundancy (mRMR) algorithm, and least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression, multiple machine learning models were compared, and the support vector machine (SVM) was selected as the optimal algorithm for radiomic model construction. Independent clinical risk factors were screened by Cox regression analysis, and a post-fusion strategy integrated these factors with the radiomic signature to build a combined nomogram model. Model performance was evaluated using the area under the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve (AUC), calibration curves, decision curve analysis (DCA), DeLong test, net reclassification improvement (NRI), and integrated discrimination improvement (IDI). Survival analysis was performed with the Kaplan-Meier method, and SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) analysis was applied to enhance model interpretability.ResultsAmong the tested radiomic models, the Rad All model that integrated intratumoral, inner peritumoral, and outer peritumoral features showed the best overall performance in the internal validation set, with an AUC of 0.820 (95% CI: 0.689–0.951). The combined nomogram incorporating the Rad All signature and independent clinical risk factors (irregular mass shape, abnormal posterior echo features, lymph node metastasis, and progesterone receptor[PR] negativity) achieved an AUC of 0.905 (95% CI: 0.817–0.992), which was higher than the radiomic‑only and clinical‑only models. Statistical assessments including DeLong test, NRI, and IDI supported the incremental predictive value of the combined model (all p < 0.05). The model exhibited acceptable calibration and favorable clinical net benefit in decision curve analysis. Survival analysis using the Rad All model enabled effective risk stratification in both training and internal validation sets. SHAP analysis identified lbp‑2D_Agrlrm_ARunLengthNonUniformityNormalized as the most important radiomic feature, with high values related to increased recurrence risk.ConclusionIn conclusion, integrating intratumoral, inner peritumoral, and outer peritumoral radiomic features (Rad All model) may provide more reliable predictive performance for postoperative recurrence in HER2-positive breast cancer compared with single-region radiomic models. The combined nomogram incorporating Rad All and clinical risk factors further improves predictive efficacy and may serve as a supplementary tool for clinical decision-making. SHAP analysis enhances model interpretability by identifying key predictive features. However, given the lack of independent external validation, the generalizability of our models needs to be further verified in future prospective multi-center studies.Keywords: HER2-positive breast cancer, Radiomics, Ultrasound, Recurrence risk, Peritumoral

Introduction

HER2-positive breast cancer accounts for approximately 15%-20% of cases [1], with high aggressiveness and chemoresistance, resulting in a generally poor prognosis [2]. Although targeted drugs like trastuzumab have significantly improved survival rates for HER2-positive breast cancer patients, about 25% of treated patients experience recurrence within 10 years [3], which is a major cause of poor patient outcomes [4, 5]. Furthermore, current research indicates that some HER2-positive breast cancer patients with a low risk of early recurrence may achieve good outcomes with less intensive systemic therapy, avoiding intensified or chemotherapy-dominated regimens [6]. Currently, in clinical practice, the prediction of postoperative recurrence risk primarily relies on clinicopathological factors such as hormone receptor expression, tumor size, lymph node status and age [7]. Genetic testing tools such as Oncotype DX and MammaPrint can be applied for risk stratification, yet they are not tailored to HER2-positive populations, having limited predictive efficacy. Therefore, accurately assessing the recurrence risk is crucial for postoperative treatment decisions and prognosis prediction for HER2-positive breast cancer patients.

Ultrasound is widely used in breast lesion diagnosis and follow-up due to its cost-effectiveness, real-time dynamic imaging and short-term repeatability [8]. Literature has shown that breast ultrasound microcalcification is correlated with Her2 status and prognosis [9]; a vertical orientation on ultrasound in triple-negative breast cancer is often associated with a poor prognosis [10]. However, these conventional imaging features largely depend on the subjective judgment of radiologists, which are difficult to accurately reflect tumor biology and recurrence risk.

Radiomics regards medical images as mineable data, extracting and analyzing high-throughput features from the images to provide references for evaluating recurrence risk and prognosis,and numerous related studies have been conducted [11–13]. Notably, the Oncotype DX recurrence risk score, which predicts recurrence and metastasis risk based on high-throughput sequencing, has been incorporated into the NCCN guidelines (http://www.nccn.org/patients) and is expected to accurately guide treatment for HR-positive breast cancer, affirming the potential of radiomics for predicting prognosis in specific breast cancer types [7]. Current radiomics research on HER2-positive breast cancer prognosis is scarce, and they primarily focus on intratumoral features. However, peritumoral radiomic features often contain information about lymphovascular invasion, microvascular proliferation or stromal reactions, which are closely associated with tumor progression and prognosis [14, 15].

Therefore, the present study aimed to develop an interpretable ultrasound‑based radiomic nomogram by integrating radiomic features from the intratumoral, inner peritumoral, and outer peritumoral regions, combined with clinicopathological and ultrasound parameters, for preoperative prediction of recurrence risk in patients with HER2‑positive breast cancer. SHAP analysis was applied to improve model interpretability. This nomogram may provide a quantitative reference for postoperative recurrence risk evaluation and individualized treatment strategies in patients with HER2‑positive breast cancer.

Materials and methods

Data collection and follow-up

This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Harbin Medical University [YJSKY2024-043]. All data, including ultrasound images, pathological findings, and clinical information, were collected retrospectively. For this reason, the requirement for informed consent was waived.

The study enrolled patients with HER2-positive breast cancer who underwent preoperative ultrasound examination at the Second and Third Affiliated Hospitals of Harbin Medical University between January 2017 and December 2021.

All enrolled patients underwent surgical treatment and were followed up every six months postoperatively, with a minimum follow-up duration of 36 months. The study endpoint was defined as the occurrence of recurrence (including local recurrence, distant metastasis, and contralateral breast cancer) or death from breast cancer during the follow-up period [16]. Follow-up information was obtained through the Hospital Information System (HIS) and telephone follow-up. Recurrence-free survival (RFS) was defined as the time from the date of surgery to the observation of the endpoint event.

Clinical data were collected via retrospective review of medical records, including age, menstrual history, tumor size, and lymph node status. Pathological information—such as estrogen receptor (ER) status, progesterone receptor (PR) status, Ki-67 index, pathological type, and histological grade—was also collected. HER2 status was determined using immunohistochemistry (IHC) and fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH) assays, in strict adherence to the 2018 guidelines of the American Society of Clinical Oncology and the College of American Pathologists. Either IHC 3+ or IHC 2+ with FISH positivity was defined as HER2 positive [17]. ER positivity or PR positivity was defined as at least 1% positive nuclear staining [18]. The Ki-67 proliferation index was stratified into three categories (low, intermediate, and high) using 5 and 30% as the established cut-off values, in accordance with 2025 CSCO Breast Cancer Diagnosis and Treatment Guidelines. Pathological type and histological grade were obtained from the pathological reports of the respective medical institutions.

Inclusion criteria: a) Pathologically confirmed breast cancer by core needle biopsy or surgical resection; b) Tumor specimens subjected to IHC analysis, with clear statuses of hormone receptor (HR), Ki-67, and HER2; c) Preoperative ultrasound examination performed before any invasive procedure, with good image quality; d) Complete collection of clinical baseline data; e) Availability of complete follow-up records. Exclusion criteria: a) Patients who received any antitumor treatment or invasive examination before ultrasound image acquisition; b) Poor image quality; c) Tumor did not undergo IHC or unclear statuses of HR, Ki-67, and HER2.

A total of 148 patients were ultimately included in this study, consisting of 51 patients with recurrence and 97 patients without recurrence. Using random sampling, these patients were divided into a training set (n = 103) and a testing set (n = 45) at a 7:3 ratio (Fig. 1).

Flowchart illustrating the enrollment of eligible HER2-positive breast cancer patients from two participating medical institutions and their allocation to the training set and internal validation set

Ultrasound image acquisition and ultrasound feature assessment

Ultrasound examinations were performed using a HITACHI Vision500 system (Hitachi Medical System, Tokyo, Japan) or an Aixplorer ultrasound imaging system (Super Sonic Imagine, SSI, France), both equipped with 5–13 MHz linear array transducers. Images were acquired by sonographers with ＞5 years of experience, scanning from multiple angles and storing high-quality grayscale and color Doppler flow imagings (CDFI).

Two experienced ultrasound physicians independently assessed grayscale and CDFI features (including posterior acoustic features, calcifications and blood flow) based on the Breast Imaging Reporting and Data System (BI-RADS) and Adler blood flow grading. Disagreements were resolved by consensus.

Image segmentation and radiomics feature extraction

The overall study flowchart is shown in Fig. 2. First, two-dimensional ultrasound images of the largest diameter section of the mass that met the criteria for image analysis were selected, the lesion area was manually segmented by two breast ultrasound physicians with more than five years of experience, and ROI annotation was performed on the lesion area of the images using 3D Slicer (v5.6.2) software. The built-in image adaptive tool of 3D Slicer was adopted, with the delineated ROI as the baseline (ROIB), which was contracted inward and expanded outward by 5 mm respectively, and non-breast tissue areas were manually excluded, representing the inner peritumoral region (ROIC) and outer peritumoral region (ROIA)(Fig. 2A). For inter-observer agreement assessment, two sonologists independently performed blinded delineation on ultrasound images (capturing the largest tumor diameter) of 30 randomly selected tumors. An intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC) ≥0.75 was defined as substantial agreement. Radiomic features were extracted from ROIA, ROIB and ROIC separately via PyRadiomics (http://PyRadiomics.readthedocs.io). The feature sets for ROIA+B and ROIA+B+C (ROI All) were then constructed by concatenating the features extracted from the corresponding individual regions (i.e., feature-level fusion).

The model was developed by integrating radiomics features with selected clinicopathological and ultrasound features. The diagnostic performance of the model was evaluated using ROC curves, DCA curves, and other metrics; additionally, a survival analysis model was established to verify the efficacy of the fusion algorithm

Feature selection and model construction

In the feature preprocessing stage, all original features were first standardized to eliminate dimensional differences. Subsequently, preliminary screening was performed using t-test or Mann-Whitney U test, and features with p < 0.05 were retained. To further eliminate redundancy, Pearson correlation analysis combined with the mRMR algorithm was adopted in this study to remove features with a correlation coefficient |r| >0.9. Finally, on this basis, Lasso regression was used to compress the feature coefficients by adjusting the regularization parameter; features with non-zero coefficients were retained as core features, and radiomic signatures were constructed by weighting the corresponding coefficients (Fig. 2B).

In this study, several machine learning methods were initially employed to construct radiomic models. Most algorithms were discarded due to overfitting, and only K-nearest neighbor (KNN) and SVM were retained for comparative analysis (Fig. 2C). To ensure the stability and reliability of model performance, hyperparameter optimization was performed for the above two core algorithms using 5‑fold cross‑validation combined with grid search: with the AUC value of the internal validation set as the optimization objective, the optimal configuration was screened by traversal within the preset parameter space. The key hyperparameters finally determined were as follows:

① SVM: the kernel function type was linear kernel, the penalty parameter (C) was set to 50, and the maximum number of iterations (max_iter) was limited to 600;

② KNN: the number of neighbors (n_neighbors, denoted as K) was set to 19, the sample weight strategy adopted uniform weight, and the distance metric was Euclidean distance.

In addition, Cox regression analysis was performed on clinicopathological and ultrasound features to screen independent risk factors for postoperative recurrence. A post-fusion strategy was adopted to integrate the screened independent risk factors with radiomic features, and a nomogram model was constructed for recurrence risk stratification and prognosis evaluation.

Model diagnostic performance evaluation

A nomogram was constructed to visualize the combined model for analyzing recurrence risk. The predictive performance of the model was evaluated using the ROC curve and the AUC. Additionally, calibration curves, DCA, DeLong test, NRI, and IDI were conducted to comprehensively evaluate the model’s diagnostic efficacy, goodness-of-fit, clinical decision-making capability, and incremental value in risk stratification, as well as to compare the significance of differences in predictive performance between different models. Kaplan-Meier survival curves were plotted for high- and low-risk groups, distinguished based on the risk scores from the combined prediction model.

To enhance model interpretability, we employed SHAP, which quantifies each feature’s contribution to predictions by averaging marginal effects, thereby pinpointing the most influential variables [19].

Statistical analysis

Comparisons of clinical characteristics between the two patient groups were performed using the Student’s t-test or Mann-Whitney U test for continuous variables and the χ2 test or Fisher’s exact test for categorical variables. All data analyses were performed using Python software (Version 3.7.12, http://www.python.org). Statistical analyses were conducted with statsmodels v0.13.2, radiomic feature extraction was implemented with PyRadiomics v3.0.1, and univariate and multivariate Logistic regression analyses for clinical features, as well as machine learning algorithms such as SVM, were carried out using scikit-learn 1.0.2. A p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Screening of baseline features and establishment of the clinical model

Univariate followed by multivariate Cox regression analysis of baseline characteristics identified irregular mass shape, altered posterior echo features, lymph node metastasis and PR negativity as independent risk factors for postoperative recurrence in HER2-positive breast cancer (all p < 0.05; see Tables 1 and 2). These independent risk factors were then incorporated into the final clinical model (Clinic). We used LR to construct the clinical prediction model, which achieved an AUC value of 0.652 in the internal validation set.

Radiomics feature extraction and selection

Radiomic features were extracted from ROIA, ROIB and ROIC, respectively, with 1409 radiomic features obtained for each region. A pre-fusion strategy (i.e., feature-level fusion) was employed to construct the radiomic feature sets of ROIA+B and ROIA+B+C, which contained 2818 and 4227 radiomic features, respectively. Further feature screening was performed stepwise via t-test or Mann-Whitney U test, Pearson correlation analysis, the mRMR algorithm and LASSO regression. The most critical radiomic features were screened from ROIA, ROIB, ROIC, ROIA+B and ROIA+B+C, respectively: 5, 4, 5, 5 (including 3 extratumoral peritumoral features and 2 intratumoral features) and 5 features (including 2 extratumoral peritumoral features, 2 intratumoral peritumoral features and 1 intratumoral feature)(Fig. 3).

Radiomics model construction and evaluation

After evaluating the performance of both KNN and SVM, the SVM was ultimately chosen for radiomic modeling, as it exhibited relatively better predictive efficacy and generalization ability (Fig. 4). Subsequently, five radiomic models were constructed using SVM corresponding to the five ROI definitions: Rad A (based on ROIA), Rad B (based on ROIB), Rad C (based on ROIC), Rad A+B (based on ROIA+B), and Rad All (based on ROI All/ROIA+B+C).

Performance evaluation of radiomic models. (A) Bubble plot showing the performance of the support vector machine (SVM) model across different radiomic ROI combinations (Rad A, Rad B, Rad C, Rad A+B, Rad All) in training (Tr) and internal validation (IV) sets, and the Rad All combination achieved the optimal performance. (B) Radar plot comparing the performance of SVM and KNN models built on the comprehensive ROI All dataset. The SVM model delivered superior overall performance in both training (blue line) and internal validation (red line) sets

The results demonstrated that the Rad All model achieved the optimal performance (Fig. 5). In the internal test set, the AUC of the Rad All model was 0.820 (95% CI: 0.689–0.951), which was slightly higher than that of the Rad A+B model (0.806, 95% CI: 0.660–0.951). Both models were significantly superior to the Rad C (0.786), Rad A (0.772), and Rad B (0.692) models. The DeLong test revealed no statistically significant difference in AUC between Rad All and Rad A+B.

Comparison and validation of radiomic models based on different ROI combinations. (A) Calibration curves of radiomic models constructed with different ROI combinations, illustrating the agreement between predicted and observed recurrence risks. (B) DCA evaluating the net clinical benefit of ROI-based radiomic models at varying threshold probabilities, compared to “treat all” and “treat none” strategies. (C–F) Heatmaps of pairwise C-index values between models with different ROI combinations, assessed in the internal validation set under different evaluation methods

In the training set, the NRI of the Rad All model compared with the Rad A+B model was 0.031, whereas in the internal test set, the IDI of the Rad A+B model compared with the Rad All model was 0.112. The two models showed different advantages in risk stratification, and both were significantly better than single-region models.

Decision curve analysis demonstrated that the net benefit of Rad All and Rad A+B within the threshold probability range of 0.2–0.8 was significantly higher than that of the “treat all” and “treat none” strategies, with highly overlapping curves. Calibration curves indicated that the consistency between the predicted probability and the actual outcome of the Rad All model was slightly better than that of the Rad A+B model, while the calibration performance of the Rad A and Rad B models was relatively unsatisfactory.

Construction and evaluation of the combined Model

A post-fusion strategy was used to integrate the clinical model (Clinic) with the combined radiomic signature, and the final combined model (Combined) was constructed using logistic regression and presented as a nomogram (Fig. 6).

In the internal validation set, the AUC of the Combined model was 0.905 (95% CI: 0.817–0.992), which was higher than those of the Rad All model (0.820, 95% CI: 0.689–0.951) and the Clinic model (0.652, 95% CI: 0.485–0.819) (Fig. 7A). Calibration curves demonstrated that the Combined model achieved the best consistency between predicted and actual recurrence probability, followed by the Rad All model, whereas the Clinic model showed relatively poor calibration (Fig. 7B). The DCA confirmed that the Combined model yielded a higher net benefit than the other models as well as the “treat all” and “treat none” strategies within a broad range of threshold probabilities, and the Rad All model also achieved a significantly higher net benefit than the Clinic model (Fig. 7C). The DeLong test revealed that the AUC difference was significant between the Combined and Rad All models (p = 0.030), highly significant between the Combined and Clinic models (p = 0.001), and marginally significant between the Rad All and Clinic models (p = 0.068) (Fig. 7D). The NRI showed that the Combined model outperformed the Rad All model (NRI = 0.158) and the Clinic model (NRI = 0.332), and the Rad All model was also superior to the Clinic model (NRI = 0.231), with all p-values < 0.001 (Fig. 7E). The IDI indicated consistent results, in which the Combined model performed better than the Rad All model (IDI = 0.091) and the Clinic model (IDI = 0.291), and the Rad All model also exceeded the Clinic model (IDI = 0.201), with all p-values < 0.001 (Fig. 7F).

Survival analysis based on risk stratification by the Rad All model showed that recurrence-free survival was significantly shorter in the high-risk group than in the low-risk group in both the training set (C-index = 0.8126, p < 0.001) (Fig. 7G) and the internal validation set (C-index = 0.7627, p = 0.002) (Fig. 7H), indicating reliable risk stratification ability.

SHAP analysis of the multi-regional SVM radiomics Model

We applied the SHAP method to perform interpretability analysis on the ultrasound-based radiomic model for recurrence prediction in HER2-positive breast cancer, systematically quantifying the contribution of each radiomic feature to the model output, its overall importance, and the association between feature values and prediction direction.

The SHAP waterfall plot and force plot (Figs. 8A, B) showed that for a representative sample with a model output f(x) = 0.322 and a baseline expected output E[f(X)]=0.486, wavelet-HHH_Aglszm_ASmallAreaLowGrayLevelEmphasis (contribution value: −0.12, the most significant negative impact), wavelet-HLH_Cglszm_CSmallAreaHighGrayLevelEmphasis (contribution value: −0.04), and lbp-2D_Agrlrm_ARunLengthNonUniformityNormalized (contribution value: −0.01) all exerted negative contributions, reducing the predicted recurrence risk; only wavelet-LHH_Cfirstorder_CTotalEnergy showed a positive contribution (+0.01), slightly increasing the predicted recurrence risk; wavelet-HHL_Bfirstorder_BUniformity had a contribution of 0, with no significant impact on the prediction of this sample.

Interpretability analysis of the radiomic recurrence prediction model for HER2-positive breast cancer using SHAP. (A) SHAP waterfall plot for a representative sample, showing the contribution of each feature to the model output. (B) SHAP force plot for the same representative sample, illustrating the direction and magnitude of feature contributions. (C) SHAP feature importance plot (top) and beeswarm plot (bottom), showing the overall importance of each feature and the association between feature values and prediction direction

The SHAP feature importance plot (Fig. 8C) revealed that lbp-2D_Agrlrm_ARunLengthNonUniformityNormalized had the highest importance, followed by wavelet-HHH_Aglszm_ASmallAreaLowGrayLevelEmphasis and wavelet-HLH_Cglszm_CSmallAreaHighGrayLevelEmphasis, while wavelet-LHH_Cfirstorder_CTotalEnergy and wavelet-HHL_Bfirstorder_BUniformity had relatively low importance, suggesting that texture features and wavelet-domain gray-level distribution features were the core predictors of the model.

The SHAP beeswarm plot (Fig. 8C) further revealed the relationship between feature values and prediction direction: high values of lbp-2D_Agrlrm_ARunLengthNonUniformityNormalized indicated increased tumor texture heterogeneity and elevated recurrence risk; high values of both wavelet-HHH_Aglszm_ASmallAreaLowGrayLevelEmphasis and wavelet-HLH_Cglszm_CSmallAreaHighGrayLevelEmphasis corresponded to reduced recurrence risk; high values of wavelet-LHH_Cfirstorder_CTotalEnergy were associated with increased recurrence risk; the SHAP values of wavelet-HHL_Bfirstorder_BUniformity were concentrated near 0, with limited impact on the model prediction results, indicating that this feature had no obvious predictive value in the present model.

Discussion

In this study, we constructed recurrence risk prediction models for HER2‑positive breast cancer after surgery based on radiomic features from different regions of interest (ROI). Based on comprehensive evaluations including AUC, IDI, NRI, calibration curves, and DCA, the Rad All model incorporating features from all three ROIs (A+B+C) showed more robust and reliable performance, making it more suitable for clinical decision‑making. In terms of discrimination, the AUC of the Rad All model (0.820, 95% CI: 0.689–0.951) was the highest among all models, slightly higher than that of the A+B model (0.806, 95% CI: 0.660–0.951), with highly overlapping 95% CIs, indicating comparable ability in risk stratification. For model calibration, the calibration curve of the Rad All model was closest to the ideal line, suggesting the best agreement between predicted and actual recurrence risk, which is critical for clinicians to formulate personalized treatment strategies according to risk thresholds. Furthermore, the Rad All model integrated radiomic information from three distinct ROIs, enabling more comprehensive capture of tumor heterogeneity and thereby improving generalization and robustness.

Although the A+B model showed superior discrimination and reclassification improvement in terms of IDI and NRI, the Rad All model presented greater advantages in clinical application due to its better calibration and more comprehensive feature sources. For postoperative recurrence risk prediction in HER2‑positive breast cancer, the reliability and generalizability of prediction results are often more important than mere discriminative performance. Therefore, the Rad All model was selected as the optimal radiomic model for constructing the combined model with independent clinical risk factors, which could provide more reliable evidence for clinical decision‑making. Notably, the final combined model further elevated predictive performance by integrating radiomic and clinical predictors, highlighting the complementary value of imaging‑derived features and conventional clinicopathological variables.

Currently, most radiomics studies predicting tumor recurrence are based on intratumoral features, with some studies incorporating outer peritumoral areas. Peritumoral radiomic features often contain information about lymphovascular invasion, microvascular proliferation or stromal reactions, which are associated with tumor progression and prognosis [14, 15]. Previous studies have shown that combining intratumoral and peritumoral regions can improve the classification or predictive performance of radiomics models [15, 19–21]. However, previous peritumoral studies mostly focused on the outer peritumoral region, lacking attention to the inner peritumoral region. This study supplemented conventional intratumoral features with inner and outer peritumoral features, constructing five ROIs: tumor, inner peritumoral, outer peritumoral, tumor-outer peritumoral, and tumor-peritumoral, to explore the contribution of inner peritumoral radiomics features to prognosis prediction. Although the inner peritumoral region is spatially contained within the entire tumor volume, the Rad All model achieved slightly better overall performance than the Rad A+B model and was significantly superior to Rad A, Rad B, and Rad C. This may be because the inner peritumoral region specifically reflects the micro-heterogeneity at the tumor-stromal junction, an active frontier for tumor invasion, immune infiltration, and stromal response. Features extracted from the whole tumor region tend to average these local signals, whereas separate analysis preserves and highlights their unique predictive value, thus complementing global features and providing more complete spatial heterogeneity information.

In this study, five radiomic features were finally selected based on ROI All, including texture features and first-order statistical features, among which texture features dominated, including run‑length matrix and gray-level size zone matrix features. These findings indicate that micro-spatial heterogeneity plays a central role in recurrence prediction, which has been supported by recurrence prediction studies in multiple cancers [22, 23]. SHAP analysis further verified these results and identified lbp‑2D_Agrlrm_ARunLengthNonUniformityNormalized as the core feature for predicting recurrence in HER2‑positive breast cancer using ultrasound radiomics, with high values significantly associated with elevated recurrence risk. Previous studies have confirmed that ultrasound textural heterogeneity in breast cancer is closely related to uneven infiltration of tumor‑infiltrating lymphocytes (TILs) and spatial heterogeneity of the immune microenvironment [24, 25], which represents the key pathological basis for the predictive value of such radiomic features. HER2‑positive breast cancer itself exhibits highly aggressive biological behavior, often accompanied by active tumor‑stromal interactions and abnormal structural proliferation. Tumor invasive growth disrupts the ordered structure of normal breast tissue, and combined with microscopic pathological changes such as heterogeneous TIL distribution, stromal fibrosis, and abnormal cell proliferation, these lead to local architectural distortion, which manifests as increased textural heterogeneity on ultrasound [26]. In addition, two gray‑level size zone matrix texture features complementarily reflect structural differences including intratumoral necrosis and fibrosis from the perspective of gray‑level distribution [24].

The results of this study show that positive axillary lymph node metastasis is an independent risk factor associated with recurrence and is crucial for breast cancer prognosis, aligning with previous studies [27]. However, unlike the findings of Gedfew M et al., histological grade III was not identified as a recurrence-related factor in our study, which may be related to the relatively small sample size. Furthermore, we found that PR positivity acts as a protective factor against recurrence in HER2-positive breast cancer. This is consistent with previous literature indicating that HR status is closely associated with prognosis in patients with HER2-positive breast cancer [28]. However, due to the relatively small sample size in this study, patients with HER2-positive breast cancer could not be further stratified into finer subgroups for analysis. Future studies should recruit a larger cohort of patients with HER2-positive breast cancer to explore prognostic factors across different ER and PR statuses. Additionally, studies have shown that posterior echo features are a significant risk factor for axillary lymph node metastasis [29]. Separately, enhanced posterior echoes have been identified as an independent predictor of high recurrence risk in HR+/HER2- breast cancer patients [30]. Our study yielded similar findings: Cox regression analysis showed that both posterior acoustic shadowing and posterior acoustic enhancement increased recurrence risk, with enhancement conferring a higher risk compared to shadowing. Posterior acoustic attenuation usually reflects dense fibrous stromal proliferation and high cellularity within the tumor; fibrous stroma not only causes acoustic attenuation but also forms a microenvironment that promotes tumor invasion [31]. In contrast, posterior acoustic enhancement often indicates intratumoral necrosis, cystic degeneration, or stromal edema, pathological features that typically indicate active proliferation and high malignancy, further increasing the risk of recurrence and metastasis by triggering local inflammation and elevating microvascular permeability [32].

The results of this study demonstrate that the combined model can improve the predictive performance for recurrence risk in HER2-positive breast cancer. The advantages of the Combined model in AUC, IDI, and NRI highlight the complementary value of radiomics and clinical features. The favorable calibration and net benefit shown in DCA support the clinical utility of the combined model in guiding personalized treatment decisions, which may reduce overtreatment in low-risk patients while ensuring appropriate intensified therapy for high-risk patients.

There are still several limitations in this study. First, although data from two centers were included, both centers are closely affiliated and follow similar clinical workflows and imaging quality control standards. While this ensured a certain degree of data homogeneity and reliability, the present study only performed internal validation. The lack of independent external validation remains a major limitation, which may restrict the generalizability of the proposed model. Second, the final sample size was 148 cases, which is relatively limited. This is largely attributable to the specific clinical characteristics of HER2positive breast cancer: this subtype accounts for a relatively low proportion, and confirmation of HER2 equivocal (2+) cases requires FISH testing, which is relatively expensive and has limited patient acceptance. Consequently, many HER2 (2+) cases were excluded due to incomplete FISH confirmation. In addition, breast cancer patients require long‑term follow‑up after surgery, and the substantial rate of loss to follow-up during the 3‑year follow‑up period further reduced the number of valid cases included in the final analysis. Constrained by these objective clinical factors, the statistical power of this study was relatively limited, which may also explain why some traditional prognostic indicators did not reach statistical significance. In the future, building on the preliminary findings of this study, we plan to conduct prospective multicenter studies with expanded sample sources and independent external validation cohorts, and to optimize the followup system to further verify and improve the stability and clinical applicability of the model. Third, there is still no universal consensus regarding the optimal peritumoral width for predicting breast cancer prognosis. Previous studies recommend a 2–20 mm region from the tumor margin for peritumoral analysis in MRI [33]. In the present study, a 5‑mm peritumoral width was selected based on published studies on peritumoral radiomics for prognosis prediction in breast cancer and relevant investigations in other solid tumors [22, 34], combined with the actual ultrasound imaging characteristics of our cohort, which could adequately capture the peritumoral region with heterogeneous echotexture and structural disorder. However, due to the limited sample size and restricted statistical power, we did not perform a sensitivity analysis of different peritumoral widths, which represents a methodological limitation. Such sensitivity analysis will be included in future studies with expanded sample sizes to further optimize the peritumoral segmentation protocol. In addition, although we have reduced the number of features to 5, the Events Per Variable (EPV) ratio is still not fully up to the standard recommendation of 10:1 due to the limited number of recurrence events, which may slightly affect the statistical robustness of the model.

Conclusion

In summary, we present a machine learning model based on multiple ROIs that integrates multimodal data for recurrence risk stratification. It may help predict recurrence risk, providing a potential tool to support clinical decision-making for HER2-positive breast cancer, and future multidisciplinary integration may further promote the refinement of such personalized prediction tools to better align with clinical practice needs.

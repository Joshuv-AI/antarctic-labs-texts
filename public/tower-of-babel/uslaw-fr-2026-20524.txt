-----------------------------------------------------------------------

SUMMARY: In accordance with the Paperwork Reduction Act of 1995, the 
Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration (FMCSA) announces its plan 
to submit the Information Collection Request (ICR) described below to 
the Office of Management and Budget (OMB) for review and approval and 
invites public comment. This notice invites comments on a proposed 
information collection titled Hours of Service of Drivers: Pilot 
Program to Allow Commercial Drivers to Split Sleeper Berth Time. This 
research study will collect data on commercial motor vehicle (CMV) 
drivers to examine whether more-flexible sleeper berth (FSB) options 
improve the working conditions and quality of life of America's truck 
drivers and result in an equivalent or greater level of safety relative 
to operations under the current regulations. Approximately 256 CMV 
drivers will provide data in the study. The study will provide FMCSA 
with data to examine driver fatigue and driving performance while 
operating under current hours of service (HOS) regulations and while 
operating under FSB exemptions.

Background

    HOS is a high-priority topic for FMCSA. Addressing driver fatigue, 
which is a serious safety concern for our nation's truck drivers and 
those who share the road with them, is at the heart of HOS regulations. 
Previous research has shown that driver fatigue is associated with an 
increased risk of being involved in a safety-critical event and may be 
involved in over 30 percent of fatal CMV crashes. As a result, the HOS 
regulations are designed to limit the number of driving hours within a 
24-hour period and across a 7- or 8-day window. Additionally, the HOS 
regulations include some flexibility for drivers using a sleeper berth.
    As described in Title 49 of the Code of Federal Regulations (CFR) 
395.1(g)(1), a driver who operates a property-carrying CMV who uses the 
sleeper berth to obtain the off-duty time required by Sec.  395.3(a)(1) 
must accumulate at least 10 hours of off-duty time before starting a 
work shift. The 10 hours may either be accumulated consecutively, or 
alternatively, split into one consecutive period of at least 7 hours in 
the sleeper berth and a second period of at least 2 consecutive hours 
of time in the sleeper berth, off-duty time, or any combination 
thereof, provided that the total of the periods is at least 10 hours. 
In practice, this allows drivers to choose between a ``7/3'' split or 
an ``8/2'' split of sleeper berth time, or any gradation in between.
    While regulations that provide a prescriptive scope on a truck 
driver's workday are essential, they may be viewed as onerous in some 
circumstances. HOS provisions that allow CMV drivers to split sleeper 
berth time in more flexible ways may be a viable option to improve or 
sustain driver alertness while giving drivers greater operational 
flexibility. In fact,

[[Page 64215]]

the American Trucking Associations and the Minnesota Trucking 
Association submitted a joint proposal for a ``flexible sleeper-berth 
pilot program'' to FMCSA for consideration in 2013.
    In 2010, the Agency held three listening sessions intended to 
solicit comments and information on potential HOS regulations (see 75 
FR 285, Jan. 5, 2010; 75 FR 2467, Jan. 15, 2010; and 75 FR 9376, Mar. 
10, 2010). Many drivers said they would like some regulatory 
flexibility (i.e., an exemption from consolidated sleeper berth time) 
to be able to sleep when they get tired or as a countermeasure to 
traffic congestion. Although the Agency's 2011 HOS final rule (76 FR 
81134, Dec. 27, 2011) did not include a provision allowing sleeper 
berth time to be taken non-consecutively (``split''), FMCSA determined 
that the issue should be explored in greater depth to determine whether 
additional flexibility would achieve an equivalent or improved level of 
safety relative to compliance with the current regulations.
    Additionally, FMCSA has conducted several studies to explore the 
topic of split sleep. Past research on how split sleep periods may 
affect driver performance, fatigue, and quality and quantity of sleep 
suggests that sleep split into multiple segments is restorative, does 
not negatively affect daytime ``neurobehavioral performance'' when 
compared to a consolidated sleep period of the same total duration, and 
does not negatively impact performance when implemented strategically. 
More recent studies provide evidence that the circadian rhythm affects 
fatigue more than splitting sleep does, and that splitting sleep may be 
more beneficial than sleeping in a single rest period during daylight 
hours.
    In response to Executive Order (E.O.) 14286, ``Enforcing 
Commonsense Rules of the Road for America's Truck Drivers,'' \1\ the 
U.S. Secretary of Transportation announced a new pilot program designed 
to improve drivers' working conditions and quality of life by providing 
more flexibility in how drivers may split sleeper berth time. The FSB 
Pilot Program will provide participating drivers with an exemption that 
reduces the longer period in the sleeper berth to a minimum of 5 
consecutive hours. This will allow participating drivers to utilize a 
``5/5'' split, ``6/4'' split, or any split in between to obtain the 
equivalent of 10 consecutive hours off-duty.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \1\ The E.O. is available at https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/04/enforcing-commonsense-rules-of-the-road-for-americas-truck-drivers/.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    As such, this study will assess the safety and performance impacts 
of additional flexibility for CMV drivers to use split sleeper berth 
periods. Specifically, the study will collect multiple sources of data 
to examine driver fatigue and driving performance while operating under 
current HOS regulations and while operating under FSB exemptions.
    The study includes data collection via pre- and post-study surveys, 
psychomotor vigilance tests (PVTs), subjective sleepiness ratings, a 
wrist actigraphy device, drivers' record of duty status (RODS), and a 
non-intrusive telematics device. These data will provide information on 
drivers' schedules, the use of FSB exemptions, fatigue, and driving 
performance. This information will allow FMCSA to assess whether 
additional flexibility in how sleeper berth time may be split achieves 
a level of safety equivalent to that which would be achieved absent the 
regulatory relief. After agreeing to participate in the study, drivers 
will participate in the study for 16 weeks.
    Eligibility to participate will be based on carrier and driver 
safety records. A motor carrier's participation in the pilot program 
shall be contingent upon the carrier meeting the following eligibility 
criteria:
    1. Must have applicable interstate operating authority and 
registration;
    2. Must have the minimum levels of financial responsibility under 
49 CFR part 387, if applicable;
    3. Must not be a high or moderate risk carrier, as defined in the 
Agency's Federal Register notice titled ``Notification of Changes to 
the Definition of a High-Risk Motor Carrier and Associated 
Investigation Procedures'' (81 FR 11875, Mar. 7, 2016);
    4. Must not have an ``Unsatisfactory'' safety rating;
    5. Must not have any FMCSA enforcement actions within the past 3 
years;
    6. Must not have a driver out of service (OOS) rate above the 
National average; and
    7. Must not have a vehicle OOS rate above the National average.
    In addition, unpaid civil penalties may be grounds for exclusion 
from participating in the pilot program.
    Further, motor carriers participating in the pilot program would be 
required to:
     Grant permission for drivers to participate in the pilot 
program;
     Agree to comply with all pilot program procedures;
     Grant permission for the Virginia Tech Transportation 
Institute to gather RODS information for each participating driver 
throughout the study duration; and
     Grant permission for drivers participating in the study to 
operate under the applicable exemption.
    A driver would not be eligible for participation in the pilot 
program if the driver is identified in FMCSA's Drug and Alcohol 
Clearinghouse, regardless of whether the driver has completed the 
return-to-duty process, or during the 2-year period immediately 
preceding the date of participation, the driver had his or her license 
suspended, revoked, cancelled, or has been disqualified for a 
conviction of one of the disqualifying offenses listed in Sec.  383.51. 
In addition, drivers would be required to:
     For the purposes of the study, operate the same CMV 
(equipped with a sleeper berth) as operated for their main source of 
employment and regularly use the sleeper berth;
     Possess a valid commercial driver's license;
     Maintain a valid medical certificate from a healthcare 
professional on the Agency's National Registry of Certified Medical 
Examiners while participating in the pilot program;
     Be employed by a motor carrier who has been approved for 
participation in the pilot program and/or certify as an owner-operator;
     Agree to the release of specific information to FMCSA for 
purposes of the pilot; and
     Agree to comply with all study procedures, including the 
use of actigraphs and RODS.
    FMCSA would also reserve the right to exclude from participation 
any driver whom the Agency believes has a safety history incompatible 
with the interests of the pilot programs.
    The study will use a convenience sampling approach, where 
participating carriers and drivers volunteer to participate. This is 
expected to attract drivers who are likely to use the exemption, which 
is key to obtaining sufficient data during the exemption period and 
maximizing statistical power for analyses. However, the sample of 
drivers will be drawn from carriers who are representative of the 
overall distribution of carrier sizes in the population of interstate 
motor carriers utilizing drivers beyond a 100-mile radius. A total of 
256 CMV drivers will participate in this study.
    The ``method of comparison'' for the ``safety analysis plan'' will 
vary depending on the data, but may include t -tests, [chi]\2\ tests, 
other inferential/descriptive statistics, and/or qualitative

[[Page 64216]]

analyses to include case narratives (e.g., for crash events). To make 
effective comparisons between drivers operating in compliance with the 
current regulations and drivers operating under the conditions of the 
exemption, the study has been structured as a within-subjects research 
design, which will compare data for the same drivers operating under 
both conditions.

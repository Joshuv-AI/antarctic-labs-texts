Botulinum neurotoxin for lip flip
Authors: Jair Mauricio Cerón Bohórquez, Jovian Wan, Isaac Kai Jie Wong, Jeremy B Green, Thomas Rappl, Jin-Hyun Kim, Kyu-Ho Yi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBotulinum neurotoxin (BoNT) injection for “lip flip” is used to achieve subtle upper lip eversion by partially relaxing the pars marginalis of the upper orbicularis oris. This short communication describes the anatomical rationale, injection approach, and early clinical observations in three consecutive patients treated with LetybotulinumtoxinA. BoNT was reconstituted to 4 U/0.1 mL and injected superficially along the upper vermilion border, with 1 U per site, avoiding the oral commissures and deeper muscle layers. At two weeks, all patients showed subtle upper lip eversion and reduction of vertical rhytides, with satisfaction ranging from “Satisfied” to “Very Satisfied.” No patient reported speech disturbance, difficulty with oral movement, asymmetry, or oral incompetence during the short follow-up. These findings should be interpreted as preliminary because of the very small sample size, lack of control group, absence of blinded assessment, and short follow-up.Keywords: Botulinum toxins, Lip anatomy, Facial muscles, Rejuvenation, Muscle hypertonia

Introduction

Perioral ageing includes reduced structural support, lip introversion, and vertical rhytides, all of which may affect perceived lip aesthetics.1 The orbicularis oris encircles the oral aperture and contributes to lip tone, pursing, and dynamic inward rolling of the upper lip. In selected patients, hyperactivity of the pars marginalis of the upper orbicularis oris may reduce vermilion show and accentuate perioral lines. BoNT reduces muscle contraction by inhibiting acetylcholine release at the neuromuscular junction. When applied in very small doses to the pars marginalis of the upper orbicularis oris, it may allow mild outward rotation of the upper lip, known clinically as the “lip flip” .2 This communication focuses on the anatomical rationale and early descriptive outcomes of this technique.

Clinical application and outcomes

Three consecutive patients seeking subtle upper lip eversion underwent BoNT lip flip. LetybotulinumtoxinA was reconstituted to 4 U/0.1 mL (Letybo, Hugel, Korea). Each injection site received 1 U, placed superficially along the upper vermilion border, with two total injection points placed bilaterally along the upper vermilion border, one on each side approximately 0.5 cm lateral to the edge of Cupid’s bow (Table 1). The oral commissures and deep muscle layers were avoided to reduce the risk of asymmetry, dysarthria, and oral incompetence.

The anatomical goal was controlled partial relaxation of the superficial orbicularis oris rather than volume augmentation. This distinction is important because fillers primarily address volume deficiency, whereas BoNT lip flip targets dynamic muscle activity. At the two-week follow-up, patients were specifically asked about bruising, asymmetry, speech disturbance, difficulty drinking from a straw, whistling difficulty, oral incompetence, drooling, and abnormal smile or oral movement. Outcomes were recorded descriptively using patient satisfaction and GAIS ratings. Because only three patients were included, inter-rater reliability statistics were not performed.

Case observations

At two weeks, all three patients demonstrated visible but subtle upper lip eversion and reduction of vertical upper lip rhytides (Fig. 1). Satisfaction scores ranged from “Satisfied” to “Very Satisfied” for appearance, eversion, and naturalness. Functional ratings for speech and oral movement were maximal in all cases. GAIS ratings were +1 (“Improved”) in one patient and +2 (“Much Improved”) in two patients. No patient reported asymmetry, speech disturbance, oral incompetence, or difficulty with oral movements during the follow-up period.

Discussion

The lip flip depends on precise anatomical targeting. The pars marginalis of the upper orbicularis oris lies close to the vermilion border and contributes to inward lip rolling and vertical rhytides. Low-dose, superficial BoNT injection aims to reduce excessive sphincteric pull while preserving oral competence. Avoidance of deep injection and the oral commissures is important because excessive weakening may impair speech, smiling, drinking, or lip seal. Sonographic evaluation of the upper labial orbicularis oris has emphasized the clinical relevance of muscle thickness and depth in planning injections.3,4

Compared with hyaluronic acid fillers, BoNT lip flip does not directly add volume and is better suited for patients seeking subtle eversion rather than augmentation.5 Surgical lip lift provides more durable central upper-lip rejuvenation but is invasive and involves downtime.6 Laser resurfacing may improve skin texture and rhytides but does not directly address orbicularis oris hyperactivity.

This report has important limitations. It includes only three patients, has no control group, no blinded independent evaluator, short follow-up, and descriptive outcome assessment only. GAIS agreement statistics were not calculated because of the very small sample size. Therefore, the findings should not be interpreted as proof of generalizable safety or efficacy. Future studies should use standardized photography, blinded assessment, controlled comparison groups, systematic adverse-event recording, and longer follow-up.

Conclusion

This short communication suggests that anatomically guided, low-dose BoNT injection into the pars marginalis of the upper orbicularis oris may be associated with subtle upper lip eversion and reduction of vertical rhytides in selected patients. Because this observation is based on only three patients without a control group or blinded assessment, the results should be considered preliminary.

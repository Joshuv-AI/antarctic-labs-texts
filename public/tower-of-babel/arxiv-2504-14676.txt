Analysis of Stochastic Fluid-Rigid Body Dynamics: an Approach by Stochastic Maximal Regularity
Felix Brandt, Arnab Roy

Abstract
. This paper investigates for the first time a rigorous mathematical framework for a stochastic fluid-rigid body interaction problem. It is shown that the coupled problem of a rigid ball immersed in a Newtonian fluid filling the entire ℝ 3 \mathbb{R}^{3} and subject to additive noise in the fluid and rigid body equation as well as transport noise in the fluid equation is locally strongly well-posed. In addition, blow-up criteria for the stochastic fluid-rigid body interaction problem are provided. For these results, the boundedness of the ℋ ∞ {\mathcal{H}^{\infty}} -calculus of the so-called fluid-structure operator is established by means of a decoupling approach, paving the way for stochastic maximal regularity. The local strong well-posedness is then obtained by the theory of stochastic evolution equations in weighted spaces.

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we analyze a stochastic fluid-rigid body interaction problem of a viscous, incompressible Newtonian fluid with a rigid ball in 3D.
First, let us describe the underlying deterministic problem.
For a time
t\in[0,T]
, where
T>0
, denote by
h(t)
the position of the center of mass of the rigid ball centered around the origin at time
t=0
, by
\mathcal{S}(t)
its domain, and by
\mathcal{F}(t)=\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\mathcal{S}(t)
the domain occupied by the fluid.
For the fluid velocity
u\colon[0,T]\times\mathcal{F}(t)\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
and pressure
p\colon[0,T]\times\mathcal{F}(t)\to\mathbb{R}
, the linear and angular velocity
\ell\colon[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
\omega\colon[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
, i. e.,
h^{\prime}(t)=\ell(t)
, the mass of the ball
m_{\mathcal{S}}
, the time-independent inertia tensor
J_{0}
, a viscosity parameter
\nu>0
, the unit vector field
n
normal to
\partial\mathcal{S}(t)
and directed towards the interior of
\mathcal{S}(t)
as well as the Cauchy stress tensor
\mathrm{T}(u,p)
that takes the shape

(1.1)

\mathrm{T}(u,p)=2\nu(\nabla u+\nabla u^{\top})-p\Id_{3},

the deterministic interaction problem is given by

(1.2)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}u+(u\cdot\nabla)u-\nu\Delta u+\nabla p&=0,\kern 5.0pt\mdiv u=0,&&\kern 5.0pt\text{for}\kern 5.0ptt>0,\kern 5.0pty\in\mathcal{F}(t),\\
m_{\mathcal{S}}\ell^{\prime}(t)+\int_{\partial\mathcal{S}(t)}\mathrm{T}(u,p)n\,\mathrm{d}\Gamma&=0,&&\kern 5.0pt\text{for}\kern 5.0ptt>0,\\
J_{0}\omega^{\prime}(t)+\int_{\partial\mathcal{S}(t)}(y-h(t))\times\mathrm{T}(u,p)n\,\mathrm{d}\Gamma&=0,&&\kern 5.0pt\text{for}\kern 5.0ptt>0,\\
u(t,y)&=\ell(t)+\omega(t)\times(y-h(t)),&&\kern 5.0pt\text{for}\kern 5.0ptt>0,\kern 5.0pty\in\partial\mathcal{S}(t),\\
u(0)=u_{0},\kern 5.0pt\ell(0)&=\ell_{0},\kern 5.0pt\omega(0)=\omega_{0}.\end{aligned}\right.

As it does not affect the analysis, we will assume
\nu\equiv 1
in the remainder of this paper.
Thanks to the simple geometry of the ball, the transformation of (
1.2
) to a fixed domain is straightforward.
Indeed, invoking the change of variables
x\mapsto y(t,x)\coloneqq x+h(t)
, we set

(1.3)

v_{0}(x)\coloneqq u_{0}(x),\kern 5.0ptv(t,x)\coloneqq u(t,x+h(t))\kern 5.0pt\text{and}\kern 5.0pt\pi(t,x)\coloneqq p(t,x+h(t)).

This transformation leads to the introduction of the term
-(\ell\cdot\nabla)v
in the fluid equation and allows to reformulate (
1.2
) on the fixed domain denoted by
\mathcal{F}_{0}\coloneqq\mathcal{F}(0)
.
Moreover, we set
\mathcal{S}_{0}\coloneqq\mathcal{S}(0)
and accordingly
\partial\mathcal{S}_{0}\coloneqq\partial\mathcal{S}(0)
.
Thus, we get

(1.4)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}v+((v-\ell)\cdot\nabla)v-\Delta v+\nabla\pi&=0,\kern 5.0pt\mdiv v=0,&&\kern 5.0pt\text{for}\kern 5.0ptt>0,\kern 5.0ptx\in\mathcal{F}_{0},\\
m_{\mathcal{S}}\ell^{\prime}(t)+\int_{\partial\mathcal{S}_{0}}\mathrm{T}(v,\pi)n\,\mathrm{d}\Gamma&=0,&&\kern 5.0pt\text{for}\kern 5.0ptt>0,\\
J_{0}\omega^{\prime}(t)+\int_{\partial\mathcal{S}_{0}}x\times\mathrm{T}(v,\pi)n\,\mathrm{d}\Gamma&=0,&&\kern 5.0pt\text{for}\kern 5.0ptt>0,\\
v(t,y)&=\ell(t)+\omega(t)\times x,&&\kern 5.0pt\text{for}\kern 5.0ptt>0,\kern 5.0ptx\in\partial\mathcal{S}_{0},\\
v(0)=v_{0},\kern 5.0pt\ell(0)&=\ell_{0},\kern 5.0pt\omega(0)=\omega_{0}.\end{aligned}\right.

The investigation of
deterministic
fluid-rigid body interaction problems is a classical topic in mathematical fluid mechanics and has been studied abundantly.
At this stage, we refer to the pioneering work of Serre
[
41
]
, the articles
[
12
,
16
]
in the weak setting or the papers
[
17
,
21
,

,

]
in the strong setting.
For an overview of fluid-flow structure interaction problems, see also
[

]
.

The investigation of
stochastic
fluid-structure interaction (FSI) problems, where the structure is assumed to be elastic and located at a part of the fluid boundary, has only started recently.
The study of stochastic FSI problems is motivated by real world problems including the modeling of the blood flow in arteries.
These problems are naturally subject to noise, and well-posedness results of the corresponding stochastic interaction problems provide confidence that the models are robust under the addition of noise.
Kuan and Čanić
[
26
]
pioneered the investigation of stochastic FSI problems by analyzing a reduced model of a linearly coupled FSI problem consisting of a stochastic viscous wave equation.
The same authors considered in
[
27
]
the 2D/ 1D interaction problem of a Stokes fluid and a stochastically forced linearly elastic membrane modeled by a linear wave equation along a fixed interface, i. e., in the linearly coupled case.
These considerations were extended by Tawri and Čanić
[
48
]
to 2D Navier-Stokes equations nonlinearly coupled to an elastic lateral wall, where the fluid and the membrane equation are subject to stochastic forces.
Tawri investigated the case of non-zero longitudinal displacement
[
46
]
and Navier slip boundary conditions
[
47
]
.
Breit et al.
[
11
]
studied the interaction of a 3D incompressible Navier-Stokes with a linearly elastic Koiter type shell subject to transport noise.
Let us emphasize that the aforementioned results all concern solutions in the
weak
PDE sense.

In this paper, for the first time, we analyze a stochastic fluid-rigid body interaction problem.
One application of such problems concerns polymer dynamics, see also
[
53
]
. Recent advances in the modeling of fluctuating hydrodynamics and stochastic fluid-structure interactions have enabled simulations of rigid particle suspensions and thermally-driven dynamics in fluids
[
43
,
9
]
.
In contrast to the theoretical works mentioned in the former paragraph, we focus here on solutions in the
strong
PDE sense.
For a family of standard independent Brownian motions
(W_{t}^{n})_{n\geq 1}
on a filtered probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{A},\mathbb{F}=(\mathfrak{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0},\mathbb{P})
with associated expectation
\mathbb{E}[\cdot]
,
b=(b_{n})_{n\geq 1}
and
f=(f_{n})_{n\geq 1}=((f_{v}^{n},f_{\ell}^{n},f_{\omega}^{n})^{\top})_{n\geq 1}
made precise in
Section 3
, and for the
random variables

v\colon[0,T]\times\Omega\times\mathcal{F}_{0}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
,
\pi\colon[0,T]\times\Omega\times\mathcal{F}_{0}\to\mathbb{R}
,
\ell\colon[0,T]\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
\omega\colon[0,T]\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
, we consider the stochastic fluid-rigid body interaction problem

(1.5)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \,\mathrm{d}v-\Delta v\,\mathrm{d}t+\nabla\pi\,\mathrm{d}t&=-((v-\ell)\cdot\nabla)v\,\mathrm{d}t+\sum_{n\geq 1}\bigl[(b_{n}\cdot\nabla)v+f_{v}^{n}\bigr]\,\mathrm{d}W_{t}^{n},&&\kern 5.0pt\text{in}\kern 5.0pt\mathcal{F}_{0},\\
\mdiv v&=0,&&\kern 5.0pt\text{in}\kern 5.0pt\mathcal{F}_{0},\\
\,\mathrm{d}m_{\mathcal{S}}\ell+\int_{\partial\mathcal{S}_{0}}\mathrm{T}(v,\pi)n\,\mathrm{d}\Gamma\,\mathrm{d}t&=\sum_{n\geq 1}f_{\ell}^{n}\,\mathrm{d}W_{t}^{n},\\
\,\mathrm{d}J_{0}\omega+\int_{\partial\mathcal{S}_{0}}x\times\mathrm{T}(v,\pi)n\,\mathrm{d}\Gamma&=\sum_{n\geq 1}f_{\omega}^{n}\,\mathrm{d}W_{t}^{n},\\
v&=\ell+\omega\times x,&&\kern 5.0pt\text{on}\kern 5.0pt\partial\mathcal{S}_{0},\\
v(0)=v_{0},\kern 5.0pt\ell(0)&=\ell_{0},\kern 5.0pt\omega(0)=\omega_{0},&&\kern 5.0pt\text{in}\kern 5.0pt\mathcal{F}_{0}.\end{aligned}\right.

Note that we consider additive noise in the fluid and body velocity equations, while we also take into account transport noise in the fluid equation.
Transport noise has a clear physical meaning in fluid mechanical transport processes, see
[
13
]
.
For some ill-posed PDEs, it has a regularizing effect
[
19
]
.
We observe that, at least formally, it is also possible to derive the stochastic interaction problem (
1.5
) directly from (
1.2
) upon transforming to the fixed domain.

The aim of this paper is to prove that there exists a unique, local, strong solution
(v,\ell,\omega)
, which is a random variable in the present setting, to (
1.5
) when considering strongly
\mathfrak{F}_{0}
-measurable initial data in suitable Besov spaces with compatibility conditions.
Furthermore, we discuss instantaneous regularization of the solution, establish a sort of continuous dependence of the solution on the initial data and provide blow-up criteria.
For a precise statement of the main results, see
Section 3
.

Our approach relies on the theory of so-called
stochastic maximal regularity
.
In the
deterministic
case, maximal regularity of an operator
A\colon\mathrm{X}_{1}\subset\mathrm{X}_{0}\to\mathrm{X}_{0}
on a Banach space
\mathrm{X}_{0}
amounts to establishing that the parabolic operator

\Bigl(\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}+A,\tr\Bigr)\colon\mathcal{E}_{1}\to\mathcal{E}_{0}\times\mathrm{X}_{\gamma},

for the time trace
\tr
, is an isomorphism between suitable Banach spaces
\mathcal{E}_{1}
and
\mathcal{E}_{0}\times\mathrm{X}_{\gamma}
.
Thus, it allows to tackle quasilinear evolution equations of the shape

u^{\prime}(t)+A(u(t))u(t)=F(u(t)),\kern 5.0pt\text{for}\kern 5.0ptt>0,\kern 5.0ptu(0)=u_{0},

with nonlinear terms
A(\cdot)
and
F(\cdot)
, by means of a linearization and fixed-point procedure.
This method has successfully been applied to a wide array of problems in applied analysis.
For more details on the theory as well as applications, we also refer to the monographs
[
8
,
30
,
15
,
37
]
.

Stochastic
maximal regularity provides an analogue for the study of stochastic evolution equations

\,\mathrm{d}u+A(u)u\,\mathrm{d}t=F(u)\,\mathrm{d}t+(B(u)u+G(u))\,\mathrm{d}W,\kern 5.0pt\text{for}\kern 5.0ptt>0,\kern 5.0ptu(0)=u_{0},

for a Brownian motion
W
and certain
A
,
B
,
F
and
G
.
These quasilinear stochastic evolution equations are typically linearized by suitable operators
A
and
B
, and stochastic maximal regularity can again be interpreted as an isomorphism between Banach spaces.
Here it is important to note that strong solutions are generally in spaces with lower regularity in time due to the roughness of the noise.

Remarkably, van Neerven et al.
[
51
]
revealed a sufficient
deterministic
condition for stochastic maximal regularity in the case
B=0
, namely they showed that under specific assumptions on the underlying Banach space, which are made precise in
Subsection 2.3
, stochastic maximal regularity can be achieved if the operator
A
admits a bounded
{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}
-calculus.
For further details on the latter concept, we also refer to
[
15
,
28
]
.
The result in
[
51
]
was later on extended to the situation of non-zero
B
by Portal and Veraar
[
36
]
.
In a series of articles, Agresti and Veraar
[
3
,
4
]
developed frameworks to nonlinear stochastic evolution equations in so-called
critical spaces
.
Here criticality refers to the presence of a scaling invariance.
The introduction of time weights allowed the authors to lower the regularity of initial data, exploit parabolic regularization and establish blow-up criteria.
The aforementioned framework has been applied to many problems lately, and we refer here to the recent survey of Agresti and Veraar
[
7
]
and the references therein.
For critical spaces in the deterministic case, see
[
38
]
or also the survey
[
56
]
.
For different approaches to SPDEs, we also refer to the monographs
[
14
]
and
[
20
]
, which especially addresses the situation of fluid equations, and the references therein.

In order to analyze (
1.5
), we reformulate the problem in operator form based on the so-called
fluid-structure operator
.
In light of the sufficient condition for stochastic maximal regularity
[
51
]
, it is a key step in our analysis to establish the boundedness of the
{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}
-calculus of this operator.
Such an operator has first been investigated by Takahashi and Tucsnak
[
44
,
45
]
who showed the analyticity of the fluid-structure semigroup in the Hilbert space case.
Wang and Xin
[
54
]
proved that the fluid-structure semigroup is analytic in the space
\mathrm{L}^{\nicefrac{{6}}{{5}}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\cap\mathrm{L}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
for
q\geq 2
.
In
[
23
]
, Geissert et al. developed an
\mathrm{L}^{p}
-theory for fluid-rigid body interaction problems of Newtonian and non-Newtonian fluids and proved a maximal
\mathrm{L}^{p}
-regularity-type result for the associated linearized coupled problem.
By means of
\mathcal{R}
-boundedness of resolvents, Maity and Tucsnak
[
31
]
established the maximal
\mathrm{L}^{p}
-regularity and exponential stability of the fluid-structure operator in the bounded domain case.
The bounded analyticity of the fluid-structure semigroup on a suitable subspace of
\mathrm{L}^{q}
for general
q\in(1,\infty)
as established recently in the exterior domain situation by Ervedoza et al.
[
17
]
.

Note that the boundedness of the
{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}
-calculus is a stronger property, and it is generally harder to verify, since it exhibits a worse behavior under perturbations, see also
[
34
]
.
Another difficulty arising in the investigation of the fluid-structure operator is that it has a
non-diagonal domain
, meaning that the fluid-rigid body coupling is incorporated into the domain of the operator.
To bypass this issue, we employ a decoupling approach, i. e., we use a similarity transform to reduce the study to an operator that has diagonal domain, but that is of a more complicated shape.
By investigating the weak Neumann problem associated with the pressure with less regular data, and by using perturbation theory for the bounded
{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}
-calculus, we then manage to show that the fluid-structure operator admits a bounded
{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}
-calculus.
This paves the way for the stochastic maximal regularity and also reveals interesting functional analytic properties such as a characterization of the fractional power domains of the fluid-structure operator.

The proof of the local strong well-posedness of (
1.5
) is completed by nonlinear estimates, which, in conjunction with the stochastic maximal regularity established in the previous step, allow us to use the theory of semilinear stochastic evolution equations.
The nonlinear estimates require a characterization of the interpolation spaces, which is also carried out in this paper.

This article is organized as follows.
In
Section 2
, we provide probabilistic and deterministic concepts such as the bounded
{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}
-calculus, stochastic maximal regularity and a framework tailored to bilinear SPDEs required throughout the paper.

Section 3
is dedicated to the presentation of the main results of this paper on the local strong well-posedness of the stochastic interaction problem (
1.5
) as well as the blow-up criteria.
The purpose of
Section 4
is to establish the bounded
{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}
-calculus of the fluid-structure operator and to deduce the stochastic maximal regularity from there.
In
Section 5
, we prove the main results stated in
Section 3
by characterizing the interpolation spaces and showing suitable nonlinear estimates.
We conclude the paper in
Section 6
by giving an overview of possible extensions of the results in the present paper.

2.
Bounded
{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}
-calculus, stochastic maximal regularity and bilinear SPDEs

In this section, we recall some facts about the bounded
{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}
-calculus, discuss its relation with the concept of so-called
stochastic maximal
\mathrm{L}^{p}
-regularity
and provide a framework for bilinear SPDEs, including local existence and uniqueness, local continuity as well as blow-up criteria.

Subsection 2.1
presents (mainly) probabilistic preliminaries,

2.1.
Preliminaries

In this section,
\mathrm{X}
denotes a Banach space.
The notion of a UMD space, representing
unconditional martingale differences
, will be important.
Even though it is a probabilistic concept, it admits a characterization in terms of the boundedness of the Hilbert transform.
More precisely, a Banach space
\mathrm{X}
is a
UMD space
if and only if the Hilbert transform is bounded on
\mathrm{L}^{p}(\mathbb{R};\mathrm{X})
for some
p\in(1,\infty)
.
Let us observe that many classical reflexive spaces such as Lebesgue spaces
\mathrm{L}^{p}
, (fractional) Sobolev spaces
\mathrm{W}^{s,p}
, Bessel potential spaces
\mathrm{H}^{s,p}
and Besov spaces
\mathrm{B}_{pq}^{s}
enjoy this property for
p
,
q\in(1,\infty)
.

Another concept in this context is the notion of a Banach space of (Rademacher) type
2
, see also
[
24
, Chapter 7]
.
Again, we observe that the aforementioned spaces possess this property provided the parameters
p
and
q
satisfy
p
,
q\geq 2
.
In particular,
\mathrm{L}^{p}
-spaces with
p\in(1,\infty)
have type
p\wedge 2
.

With regard to stochastic maximal regularity, some probabilistic notation is required.
For this purpose, denote by
(\Omega,\mathcal{A},\mathbb{F}=(\mathfrak{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0},\mathbb{P})
a filtered probability space with associated expectation
\mathbb{E}[\cdot]
.
Let us start by making precise the notion of a cylindrical Brownian motion.
For a separable Hilbert space
\mathrm{H}
, we call a bounded and linear operator
W_{\mathrm{H}}\colon\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mathrm{H})\to\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)
a
cylindrical Brownian motion
in
\mathrm{H}
provided the random variable
W_{\mathrm{H}}(f)
satisfies the following properties:

(1)

W_{\mathrm{H}}(f)
is centered Gaussian for every
f\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mathrm{H})
,

(2)

W_{\mathrm{H}}(f)
is
\mathfrak{F}_{t}
-measurable for each
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
and
f\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mathrm{H})
with support in
[0,t]
,

(3)

W_{\mathrm{H}}(f)
is independent of
\mathfrak{F}_{t}
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
and
f\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mathrm{H})
with support in
[t,\infty]
, and

(4)

\mathbb{E}(W_{\mathrm{H}}(f_{1})W_{\mathrm{H}}(f_{2}))=(f_{1},f_{2})_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mathrm{H})}
for all
f_{1}
,
f_{2}\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mathrm{H})
.

The process
(W_{\mathrm{H}}(t)h)_{t\geq 0}
defined by
W_{\mathrm{H}}(t)h\coloneqq W_{\mathrm{H}}(\boldsymbol{1}_{(0,t]}h)
, for
h\in\mathrm{H}
, then is a Brownian motion.

Concerning stochastic integration, we now invoke
\gamma
-radonifying operators
.
Often, the space on which the noise is modeled is
\ell^{2}
.
Here we take into account the case of a general Hilbert space
\mathrm{H}
.
Consider a sequence of independent standard normal random variables
(\gamma_{i})_{i\geq 1}
on a probability space
(\Omega,\mathbb{P})
, and let
(h_{i})_{i\geq 1}
be an orthonormal basis for
\mathrm{H}
.
A bounded linear operator
T\colon\mathrm{H}\to\mathrm{X}
belongs to
\gamma(\mathrm{H},\mathrm{X})
if the series
\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\gamma_{i}Th_{i}
converges in
\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega;\mathrm{X})
, and the
\gamma
-radonifying norm is defined by

\|T\|_{\gamma(\mathrm{H},\mathrm{X})}\coloneqq\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\gamma_{i}Th_{i}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega;\mathrm{X})}.

In specific situations, the space
\gamma(\mathrm{H},\mathrm{X})
admits a precise characterization.
Indeed, if
\mathrm{X}
is a Hilbert space, then
\gamma(\mathrm{H},\mathrm{X})
is the space of Hilbert-Schmidt operators
\mathcal{L}_{2}(\mathrm{H},\mathrm{X})
, while if
\mathrm{X}=\mathrm{L}^{q}(S)
,
q\in[1,\infty)
, for a measure space
(S,\Sigma,\mu)
, then
\gamma(\mathrm{H},\mathrm{X})=\mathrm{L}^{q}(S;\mathrm{H})
.
This generalizes to
\mathrm{X}=\mathrm{H}^{s,q}(S)
for
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
q\in(1,\infty)
, i. e.,
\gamma(\mathrm{H},\mathrm{X})=\mathrm{H}^{s,q}(S;\mathrm{H})
in this case.
We also refer to
[
24
, Chapter 9]
for more details.

We say that a process
\phi\colon[0,T]\times\Omega\to\mathrm{X}
is
strongly progressively measurable
, or also simply
progressively measurable
if for all
t\in[0,T]
, it holds that
\left.\phi\right|_{[0,T]}
is strongly
\mathfrak{B}([0,t])\otimes\mathfrak{F}_{t}
-measurable, where
\mathfrak{B}
represents the Borel
\sigma
-algebra.
Moreover, we denote by
\mathrm{L}_{\mathfrak{P}}^{p}((0,T)\times\Omega;\gamma(\mathrm{H},\mathrm{X}))
the progressive measurable subspace of
\mathrm{L}^{p}((0,T)\times\Omega;\gamma(\mathrm{H},\mathrm{X}))
.
If
\mathrm{X}
is a UMD Banach space with type
2
, for every
p\in[0,\infty)
, it can be shown that the map
G\mapsto\int_{0}^{\cdot}G\,\mathrm{d}W_{\mathrm{H}}
extends to a continuous linear operator from
\mathrm{L}_{\mathfrak{P}}^{p}((0,T)\times\Omega;\gamma(\mathrm{H},\mathrm{X}))
into
\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega;\mathrm{C}([0,T];\mathrm{X}))
, see
[
52
, Theorem 4.7]
.
For
p\in(0,\infty)
, there exists a constant
C=C(p,\mathrm{X})
such that

\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\left\|\int_{0}^{t}G(s)\,\mathrm{d}W_{\mathrm{H}}(s)\right\|_{\mathrm{X}}^{p}\leq C\cdot\mathbb{E}\|G\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(0,T;\gamma(\mathrm{H},\mathrm{X}))}^{p},\kern 5.0pt\text{for all}\kern 5.0ptG\in\mathrm{L}_{\mathfrak{P}}^{p}((0,T)\times\Omega;\gamma(\mathrm{H},\mathrm{X})).

If
\mathrm{H}
is separable with orthonormal basis
(h_{i})_{i\geq 1}
, then for all
p\in[0,\infty)
and
G\in\mathrm{L}_{\mathfrak{P}}^{p}((0,T)\times\Omega;\gamma(\mathrm{H},\mathrm{X}))
, we have the series representation

\int_{0}^{\cdot}G(s)\,\mathrm{d}W_{\mathrm{H}}(s)=\sum_{i\geq 1}\int_{0}^{\cdot}G(s)h_{i}\,\mathrm{d}W_{\mathrm{H}}(s)h_{i},

with convergence in
\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega;\mathrm{C}([0,T];\mathrm{X}))
.

For the upcoming theory, we briefly recall the concept of stopping times.
A
stopping time
is a measurable map
\tau\colon\Omega\to[0,T]
so that
\{\tau\leq t\}\in\mathfrak{F}_{t}
is valid for all
t\in[0,T]
.
We then use
\llbracket 0,\sigma\rrbracket
to denote the stochastic interval
\llbracket 0,\sigma\rrbracket\coloneqq\{(t,\omega)\in[0,T]\times\Omega:0\leq t\leq\sigma(\omega)\}
.
Given
A\subset\Omega
and stopping times
\tau
and
\mu
such that
\tau\leq\mu
, we define the set
[\tau,\mu]\times A\subset[0,T]\times\Omega
by

[\tau,\mu]\times A\coloneqq\{(t,\omega)\in[0,T]\times A:\tau(\omega)\leq t\leq\mu(\omega)\}.

For
A\in\mathcal{A}
, a map
u\colon A\times[0,\mu]\to\mathrm{X}
is referred to as
strongly progressively measurable
if the process

\boldsymbol{1}_{A\times[0,\mu]}u\coloneqq\left\{\begin{aligned} u,&\kern 5.0pt\text{on}\kern 5.0ptA\times[0,\mu],\\
0,&\kern 5.0pt\text{else},\end{aligned}\right.

is strongly progressively measurable.
In the context of stopping times, we define the
\sigma
-algebra of the
\tau
-past by
\mathfrak{F}_{\tau}\coloneqq\bigl\{A\in\mathcal{A}:\{\tau\leq t\}\cap A\in\mathfrak{F}_{t}\kern 5.0pt\text{for all}\kern 5.0ptt\in[0,T]\bigr\}
.
Measurability questions in this context have been addressed in
[
3
, Lemma 2.15]
.
This paves the way for the following definition.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
T>0
,
p\in\{0\}\cup[1,\infty)
, and consider a stopping time
\tau\colon\Omega\to[0,T]
and a family of function spaces
(\mathrm{Y}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
such that for each
f\in\mathrm{Y}_{T}
and every
t\in[0,T]
, it holds that
\left.f\right|_{[0,t]}\in\mathrm{Y}_{t}
and
t\mapsto\|\left.f\right|_{[0,t]}\|_{\mathrm{Y}_{t}}
is increasing.
By
u\in\mathrm{L}_{\mathfrak{P}}^{p}(\Omega;\mathrm{Y}_{\tau})
, we mean that there is a strongly progressively measurable
\tilde{u}\in\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega;\mathrm{Y}_{T})
with
\left.\tilde{u}\right|_{\llbracket 0,\tau\rrbracket}=u
, and if
p\in[1,\infty)
, we define

\|u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega;\mathrm{Y}_{\tau})}\coloneqq\mathbb{E}\left(\|\left.\tilde{u}\right|_{[0,\tau]}\|_{\mathrm{Y}_{\tau}}^{p}\right).

We conclude this subsection by recalling the concept of time weights.
For this purpose, consider
p\in(1,\infty)
and
\kappa\in(-1,p-1)
.
We define
w_{\kappa}(t)\coloneqq|t|^{\kappa}
for
t\in\mathbb{R}
.
By
\mathrm{L}^{p}(I,w_{\kappa};\mathrm{X})
, we then denote the space of strongly measurable functions
f\colon I\to\mathrm{X}
such that

\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(I,w_{\kappa};\mathrm{X})}^{p}\coloneqq\int_{I}\|f(t)\|_{\mathrm{X}}^{p}w_{\kappa}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t<\infty.

In the unweighted case
\kappa=0
, i. e.,
w_{\kappa}=1
, we also write
\mathrm{L}^{p}(I;\mathrm{X})
instead of
\mathrm{L}^{p}(I,w_{0};\mathrm{X})
.
The weighted Sobolev spaces
\mathrm{W}^{m.p}(I,w_{\kappa};\mathrm{X})
are defined analogously, and for
\theta\in(0,1)
, the weighted Bessel potential spaces
\mathrm{H}^{\theta,p}(I,w_{\kappa};\mathrm{X})
can be obtained by complex interpolation.
In the above context, for the particular case of
\mathrm{Y}_{t}=\mathrm{L}^{p}(I_{t},w_{\kappa};\mathrm{X})
, we define
\mathrm{L}_{\mathfrak{P}}^{p}(I_{\tau}\times\Omega,w_{\kappa};\mathrm{X})\coloneqq\mathrm{L}_{\mathfrak{P}}^{p}(\Omega;\mathrm{Y}_{\tau})
.

2.2.
Bounded
{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}
-calculus of block operator matrices and perturbation theory

In the sequel, we discuss the notion of the boundedness of the
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}
-calculus as well as its application to block operator matrices.
By
{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}(\mathrm{X})
, we denote the class of operators admitting a bounded
{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}
-calculus, and for
A\in{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}(\mathrm{X})
, we use
\phi_{A}^{\infty}
for the associated
{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}
-angle.

In the following lemma, we collect some important implications of the boundedness of the
{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}
-calculus.
The maximal
\mathrm{L}^{p}
-regularity is implied by the characterization of maximal
\mathrm{L}^{p}
-regularity in terms of
\mathcal{R}
-boundedness of the resolvents in UMD spaces due to Weis
[
55
, Theorem 4.2]
, while the characterization of the fractional power domains in (b) is a consequence of the boundedness of the imaginary powers, see e. g.
[
15
, Theorem 2.5]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Suppose that
A\in{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}(\mathrm{X})
with
{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}
-angle
\phi_{A}^{\infty}\in[0,\pi)
.

(1)

If
\phi_{A}^{\infty}<\nicefrac{{\pi}}{{2}}
, then
-A
generates a bounded analytic semigroup
(\mathrm{e}^{-At})_{t\geq 0}
of angle less than or equal to
\nicefrac{{\pi}}{{2}}-\phi_{A}^{\infty}
.
If additionally, the space
\mathrm{X}
is a UMD Banach space, then
A
enjoys maximal
\mathrm{L}^{p}
-regularity on
\mathrm{X}
.

(2)

For
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
\mathrm{X}_{\alpha}\coloneqq(\mathrm{D}(A^{\alpha}),\|\cdot\|_{\alpha})
with
\|x\|_{\alpha}\coloneqq\|x\|+\|A^{\alpha}x\|
, for
\mathrm{X}_{A}
denoting
\mathrm{D}(A)
endowed with the graph norm, and for
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\alpha}
representing the complex interpolation functor, it is valid that
\mathrm{X}_{\alpha}\cong[\mathrm{X},\mathrm{X}_{A}]_{\alpha}
.

Next, we make precise the notion of a diagonally dominant block operator matrix.

Definition 2.3
.

Consider Banach spaces
\mathrm{X}_{1}
and
\mathrm{X}_{2}
as well as linear operators
A\colon\mathrm{D}(A)\subset\mathrm{X}_{1}\to\mathrm{X}_{1}
,
B\colon\mathrm{D}(B)\subset\mathrm{X}_{2}\to\mathrm{X}_{1}
,
C\colon\mathrm{D}(C)\subset\mathrm{X}_{1}\to\mathrm{X}_{2}
and
D\colon\mathrm{D}(D)\subset\mathrm{X}_{2}\to\mathrm{X}_{2}
.
Moreover, set
\mathrm{X}\coloneqq\mathrm{X}_{1}\times\mathrm{X}_{2}
.
If

(1)

the operators
A
and
D
are closed, linear and densely defined, and

(2)

C
is relatively
A
-bounded, and
B
is relatively
D
-bounded, i. e.,
\mathrm{D}(D)\subset\mathrm{D}(B)
,
\mathrm{D}(A)\subset\mathrm{D}(C)
, and there are constants
c_{A}
,
c_{D}
,
L\geq 0
so that for all
x\in\mathrm{D}(A)
and
y\in\mathrm{D}(D)
, we have

\|Cx\|_{\mathrm{X}_{2}}\leq c_{A}\cdot\|Ax\|_{\mathrm{X}_{1}}+L\cdot\|x\|_{\mathrm{X}_{1}}\kern 5.0pt\text{and}\kern 5.0pt\|By\|_{\mathrm{X}_{1}}\leq c_{D}\cdot\|Dy\|_{\mathrm{X}_{2}}+L\cdot\|y\|_{\mathrm{X}_{2}},

then the operator matrix
\mathcal{A}\colon\mathrm{D}(\mathcal{A})\coloneqq\mathrm{D}(A)\times\mathrm{D}(D)\subset\mathrm{X}\to\mathrm{X}
defined by

(2.1)

\mathcal{A}\binom{x}{y}\coloneqq\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\
C&D\end{pmatrix}\binom{x}{y},\kern 5.0pt\text{for}\kern 5.0pt\binom{x}{y}\in\mathrm{D}(\mathcal{A}),

is called diagonally dominant.

As an auxiliary result, we invoke the following lemma on the preservation of the bounded
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}
-calculus under similarity transforms.
The assertion can be found in
[
15
, Proposition 2.11(vi)]
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Consider Banach spaces
\mathrm{X}
and
\mathrm{Y}
,
S\in\mathcal{L}(\mathrm{X},\mathrm{Y})
bijective and a closed linear operator
A
on
\mathrm{X}
, and set
A_{1}\coloneqq SAS^{-1}
.
Then
A\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(\mathrm{X})
iff
A_{1}\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(\mathrm{Y})
, and
\phi_{A}^{\infty}=\phi_{A_{1}}^{\infty}
.

The next result follows directly from
[
1
, Cor. 7.2]
upon invoking the similarity transform given by
S=S^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\Id\\
\Id&0\end{pmatrix}
and employing
Subsection 2.2
.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
as defined in (
2.1
) be diagonally dominant in the sense of
Subsection 2.2
, and assume in addition that
D\in\mathcal{L}(\mathrm{X}_{2})
as well as
A\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(\mathrm{X}_{1})
with
\phi_{A}^{\infty}\in[0,\pi)
.
Then for each
\phi\in(\phi_{A}^{\infty},\pi)
, there is
\lambda\geq 0
with
\lambda+\mathcal{A}\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(\mathrm{X})
and
\phi_{\lambda+\mathcal{A}}^{\infty}\leq\phi
.

Let us observe that the diagonal dominance of
\mathcal{A}
together with the boundedness of

already implies that

has to be bounded.

Next, we discuss perturbation theory for the boundedness of the
{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}
-calculus.
Note that it is generally
not
preserved under relatively bounded perturbations, see
[
34
]
.
The following perturbation result is based on the relative boundedness with respect to a fractional power, see
[
37
, Corollary 3.3.15]
.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
A\in{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}(\mathrm{X})
, consider a linear operator
B
on
\mathrm{X}
such that
\mathrm{D}(A^{\alpha})\subset\mathrm{D}(B)
for some
\alpha\in[0,1)
, and assume that there exist
a
,
b\geq 0
with

\|Bx\|\leq a\cdot\|x\|+b\cdot\|A^{\alpha}x\|,\kern 5.0pt\text{for}\kern 5.0ptx\in\mathrm{D}(A^{\alpha}).

If additionally,

is sectorial and invertible, then

with

.

2.3.
Stochastic maximal regularity and bilinear SPDEs

This subsection is dedicated to recalling the notion of stochastic maximal regularity and providing a framework for bilinear SPDEs tailored to our application to the fluid-rigid body interaction problem.

Stochastic maximal regularity and role of
{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}(\mathrm{X}_{0})
.

First, let us make some assumptions on the Banach spaces to be considered in the sequel.
In fact, we suppose that
\mathrm{X}_{0}
and
\mathrm{X}_{1}
are UMD Banach spaces with type
2

\mathrm{X}_{1}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{X}_{0}

In the sequel, we will denote by

For

\left\{\begin{aligned} \,\mathrm{d}u+Au\,\mathrm{d}t&=f\,\mathrm{d}t+(Bu+g)\,\mathrm{d}W_{\mathrm{H}},\kern 5.0pt\text{for}\kern 5.0ptt\in[a,\tau],\\

Definition 2.7
.

Assume that

The discussion from
Subsection 2.1

We are now in the position to introduce the concept of stochastic maximal regularity.
Part (a) of the definition concerns the spatial regularization, while (bi) and (bii) also capture the regularization in time, which is more delicate in the stochastic setting due to the roughness of the noise.

Definition 2.8

Consider

(1)

) on

(bi)

(bii)

If

) on

-regularity.

The case of non-zero initial values can also be handled, see

Below, we provide a sufficient condition for stochastic maximal

-regularity in the case

In the unweighted case, it is due to van Neerven, Veraar and Weis

Proposition 2.9
.

Consider a

Bilinear SPDEs.

After recalling the concept of stochastic maximal regularity, we next discuss its application to bilinear SPDEs.
More precisely, the stochastic evolution equations under consideration take the shape

\left\{\begin{aligned} \,\mathrm{d}u+Au\,\mathrm{d}t&=F(u)\,\mathrm{d}t+(Bu+g)\,\mathrm{d}W_{\mathrm{H}},\\

for some suitable

.
In the following, we make precise the assumptions on
A
,
B
,

and

.
Let us start by saying that for the leading operators

Below, we capture the main assumption on the nonlinearity

in (

).

-calculus of the operator

For completeness, we also briefly recall the assumptions on
\mathrm{X}_{0}
and

Assumption 2.11
.

Assume that
\mathrm{X}_{0}

-strong solution of the bilinear SPDE (

).

Definition 2.12
.

Let
Subsection 2.3
be fulfilled for some

and

.

As a preparation, we collect some properties of the Helmholtz decomposition and the Stokes operator domains with compact boundary.
The first three assertions can be found in
[
42
, Theorem 5.3 and Theorem 1.4]
, while we refer to
[
35
, Theorem 3]
for the last assertion.
The first three assertions also hold if the boundary of the domain is only in
\mathrm{C}^{1}
.
For simplicity, and as this is the relevant case for our application, we focus on the 3D case, but the results remain valid for general
n\geq 2
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
q\in(1,\infty)
, consider a domain
\mathcal{F}_{0}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
with compact boundary of class
\mathrm{C}^{3}
, recall
\mathrm{L}_{\sigma}^{q}(\mathcal{F}_{0})
from
Section 3
, and define the spaces
\mathrm{W}_{\mdiv}^{q}(\mathcal{F}_{0})\coloneqq\{f\in\mathrm{L}^{q}(\mathcal{F}_{0})^{3}:\mdiv f\in\mathrm{L}^{q}(\mathcal{F}_{0})\}
as well as
\mathrm{G}^{q}(\mathcal{F}_{0})\coloneqq\{\nabla f:f\in\mathrm{L}_{\mathrm{loc}}^{q}(\overline{\mathcal{F}_{0}}),\kern 5.0pt\nabla f\in\mathrm{L}^{q}(\mathcal{F}_{0})^{3}\}
.

(1)

The normal trace
f\mapsto\gamma_{n}f\coloneqq\varphi|_{\partial\mathcal{F}_{0}}\cdot n
, for the outer unit normal vector
n
, extends to a continuous linear operator from
\mathrm{W}_{\mdiv}^{q}(\mathcal{F}_{0})
to
\mathrm{W}^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{q}},q}(\mathcal{F}_{0})
.

(2)

The space
\mathrm{L}^{q}(\mathcal{F}_{0})^{3}
can be decomposed into
\mathrm{L}^{q}(\mathcal{F}_{0})^{3}=\mathrm{L}_{\sigma}^{q}(\mathcal{F}_{0})\oplus\mathrm{G}^{q}(\mathcal{F}_{0})
.
In other words, for every
f\in\mathrm{L}^{q}(\mathcal{F}_{0})^{3}
, there exist unique
u\in\mathrm{L}_{\sigma}^{q}(\mathcal{F}_{0})
and
\nabla p\in\mathrm{G}^{q}(\mathcal{F}_{0})
such that
f=u+\nabla p
.
This decomposition is known as Helmholtz decomposition.
The associated projection onto
\mathrm{L}_{\sigma}^{q}(\mathcal{F}_{0})
is denoted by
\mathcal{P}
, and
p
solves the weak Neumann problem

\Delta p=\mdiv u,\kern 5.0pt\text{in}\kern 5.0pt\mathcal{F}_{0},\kern 5.0pt\partial_{n}p=u\cdot n,\kern 5.0pt\text{on}\kern 5.0pt\partial\mathcal{F}_{0}.

(3)

It holds that
\mathrm{L}_{\sigma}^{q}(\mathcal{F}_{0})=\{u\in\mathrm{W}_{\mdiv}^{q}(\mathcal{F}_{0}):\mdiv u=0\kern 5.0pt\text{in}\kern 5.0pt\mathcal{F}_{0}\kern 5.0pt\text{and}\kern 5.0ptu\cdot n=0,\kern 5.0pt\text{on}\kern 5.0pt\partial\mathcal{F}_{0}\}
.

(4)

Define the Stokes operator
A_{0}
on
\mathrm{L}_{\sigma}^{q}(\mathcal{F}_{0})
with homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions by

(4.1)

A_{0}u\coloneqq\mathcal{P}u,\kern 5.0pt\text{for}\kern 5.0ptu\in\mathrm{D}(A_{0})\coloneqq\mathrm{W}^{2,q}(\mathcal{F}_{0})^{3}\cap\mathrm{W}_{0}^{1,q}(\mathcal{F}_{0})^{3}\cap\mathrm{L}_{\sigma}^{q}(\mathcal{F}_{0}).

Then
-A_{0}\in{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}(\mathrm{L}_{\sigma}^{q}(\mathcal{F}_{0}))
with
{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}
-angle
\phi_{A_{0}}^{\infty}=0
.

Let us first describe the setting.
As mentioned above, we assume here that the fluid-rigid body system fills the whole space
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, and we consider the fixed domain case, i. e., we suppose that the rigid body is at rest.
By
\mathcal{S}_{0}
, we denote the domain occupied by the rigid body, and we assume its boundary
\partial\mathcal{S}_{0}
to be of class
\mathrm{C}^{3}
.
The fluid then occupies
\mathcal{F}_{0}\coloneqq\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\mathcal{S}_{0}
.
For
T\in(0,\infty]
, the model variables are the fluid velocity
u\colon(0,T)\times\mathcal{F}_{0}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
, the pressure
p\colon(0,T)\times\mathcal{F}_{0}\to\mathbb{R}
, the translational velocity of the rigid body
\ell\colon(0,T)\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
, and the rotational velocity
\omega\colon(0,T)\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
.
Moreover, recall that
m_{\mathcal{S}}
and
J_{0}
represent the body mass and inertia tensor, and recall the Cauchy stress tensor
\mathrm{T}(u,p)
from (
1.1
).

For a shift
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
, for
n
denoting the unit outward normal to
\partial\mathcal{F}_{0}
, and for suitable right-hand sides
f_{1}
,
f_{2}
and
f_{3}
and initial data
u_{0}
,
\ell_{0}
and
\omega_{0}
, the linearized fluid-rigid body interaction problem reads as

(4.2)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}u+\lambda u-\Delta u+\nabla{p}&=f_{1},\kern 5.0pt\mdiv{u}=0,&&\kern 5.0pt\text{in}\kern 5.0pt(0,T)\times\mathcal{F}_{0},\\
m_{\mathcal{S}}({\ell})^{\prime}+\lambda\ell+\int_{\partial\mathcal{S}_{0}}\mathrm{T}(u,p)n\,\mathrm{d}\Gamma&=f_{2},&&\kern 5.0pt\text{in}\kern 5.0pt(0,T),\\
J_{0}({\omega})^{\prime}+\lambda\omega+\int_{\partial\mathcal{S}_{0}}y\times\mathrm{T}(u,p)n\,\mathrm{d}\Gamma&=f_{3},&&\kern 5.0pt\text{in}\kern 5.0pt(0,T),\\
u&={\ell}+{\omega}\times y,&&\kern 5.0pt\text{on}\kern 5.0pt(0,T)\times\partial\mathcal{S}_{0},\\
u(0)=u_{0},\kern 5.0pt\ell(0)&=\ell_{0}\kern 5.0pt\text{and}\kern 5.0pt\omega(0)=\omega_{0}.\end{aligned}\right.

For the following lifting procedure, we take into account a stationary problem capturing the interface condition.
More precisely, for
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
and
\ell
,
\omega\in\mathbb{C}^{3}
, we consider

(4.3)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \lambda w-\Delta w+\nabla\psi&=0,\kern 5.0pt\mdiv w=0,&&\kern 5.0pt\text{for}\kern 5.0pty\in\mathcal{F}_{0},\\
w&=\ell+\omega\times y,&&\kern 5.0pt\text{for}\kern 5.0pty\in\partial\mathcal{S}_{0}.\end{aligned}\right.

The lemma below, for which we refer to
[
17
, Proposition 4.1]
, addresses the solvability of (
4.3
).

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\theta\in(0,\pi)
and
q\in(1,\infty)
.
Then for all
\lambda\in\Sigma_{\theta}
and
\ell
,
\omega\in\mathbb{C}^{3}
, there exists a unique solution
(w,\psi)\in\mathrm{W}^{2,q}(\mathcal{F}_{0})^{3}\times\widehat{\mathrm{W}}^{1,q}(\mathcal{F}_{0})
to (
4.3
).

Thanks to
Section 4
, for
\lambda\in\Sigma_{\theta}
, we define the family of operators

(4.4)

D_{\lambda}\binom{\ell}{\omega}=\binom{D_{\lambda,u}(\ell,\omega)}{D_{\lambda,p}(\ell,\omega)}=\binom{w}{\psi},

where
(w,\psi)\in\mathrm{W}^{2,q}(\mathcal{F}_{0})^{3}\times\widehat{\mathrm{W}}^{1,q}(\mathcal{F}_{0})
is the solution to (
4.3
).
By construction, we have

(4.5)

D_{\lambda}\in\mathcal{L}\bigl(\mathbb{C}^{3}\times\mathbb{C}^{3},\mathrm{W}^{2,q}(\mathcal{F}_{0})^{3}\times\widehat{\mathrm{W}}^{1,q}(\mathcal{F}_{0})\bigr).

In the sequel, we fix
\lambda_{0}>0
and denote the associated lifting operator by
D_{\lambda_{0}}=\binom{D_{\lambda_{0},u}}{D_{\lambda_{0},p}}
.

The next goal is to reformulate the resolvent problem associated with the fluid part with inhomogeneous boundary conditions in terms of
\mathcal{P}u
and
(\Id-\mathcal{P})u
.
To this end, in addition to the lifting operator
D_{\lambda}
from (
4.4
), we first invoke the Neumann operator
\varphi\coloneqq Nh
, assigning to each
h
the solution
\varphi
to

(4.6)

\Delta\varphi=\mdiv h,\kern 5.0pt\text{in}\kern 5.0pt\mathcal{F}_{0},\kern 5.0pt\partial_{n}\varphi=h\cdot n,\kern 5.0pt\text{on}\kern 5.0pt\partial\mathcal{S}_{0}.

The following result on the solvability of this Neumann problem on the exterior domain is a consequence of
[
42
, Theorem 5.4]
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
q\in(1,\infty)
.
Then for
h\in\mathrm{W}_{\mdiv}^{q}(\mathcal{F}_{0})
, there is a unique solution
\nabla\varphi\in\mathrm{G}^{q}(\mathcal{F}_{0})
to (
4.6
).

We can also rephrase
Section 4
as follows:
The Neumann operator
N\colon\mathrm{W}_{\mdiv}^{q}(\mathcal{F}_{0})\to\widehat{\mathrm{W}}^{1,q}(\mathcal{F}_{0})
, with
Nh\coloneqq\varphi
representing the solution resulting from
Section 4
, is well-defined.
Note that the present regularity is not enough to make sense of boundary integrals.
For this purpose, we modify the solution to the Neumann problem (
4.6
) so that its trace is well-defined on
\partial\mathcal{S}_{0}=\partial\mathcal{F}_{0}
.
In fact, for
B_{R}
denoting the ball in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
with center zero and radius
R>0
, we set
\mathcal{F}_{R}\coloneqq\mathcal{F}_{0}\cap B_{R}
, and we fix
R>0
sufficiently large such that
\partial\mathcal{F}_{0}\subset\partial\mathcal{F}_{R}
.
Thanks to
Nh\in\widehat{\mathrm{W}}^{1,q}(\mathcal{F}_{0})
and the boundedness of
\mathcal{F}_{R}
, it is especially valid that
Nh\in\widehat{\mathrm{W}}^{1,q}(\mathcal{F}_{R})
.
Hence, the expression

(4.7)

N_{R}\colon\mathrm{W}_{\mdiv}^{q}(\mathcal{F}_{0})\to\widehat{\mathrm{W}}^{1,q}(\mathcal{F}_{0})\cap\mathrm{L}_{0}^{q}(\mathcal{F}_{R}),\kern 5.0ptN_{R}h\coloneqq Nh-\frac{1}{|\mathcal{F}_{R}|}\int_{\mathcal{F}_{R}}Nh\,\mathrm{d}x,

for
\mathrm{L}_{0}^{q}(\mathcal{F}_{R})
denoting the functions in
\mathrm{L}^{q}(\mathcal{F}_{R})
with average zero, is well-defined.
The advantage of the adjusted lifting operator
N_{R}
becomes apparent in the lemma below.

Lemma 4.4
.

Consider
q\in(1,\infty)
and
h\in\mathrm{W}_{\mdiv}^{q}(\mathcal{F}_{0})
.
Then it holds that
N_{R}h\in\mathrm{W}^{1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{q}},q}(\partial\mathcal{S}_{0})
.

Proof.

We have already argued that
Nh\in\mathrm{W}^{1,q}(\mathcal{F}_{R})
.
As
N_{R}h
and
Nh
only differ by a constant, and
\mathcal{F}_{R}
is bounded, we conclude that
N_{R}h\in\mathrm{W}^{1,q}(\mathcal{F}_{R})
.
By standard trace theory, and by virtue of
\partial\mathcal{S}_{0}=\partial\mathcal{F}_{0}\subset\partial\mathcal{F}_{R}
by construction, we deduce that
N_{R}h\in\mathrm{W}^{1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{q}},q}(\partial\mathcal{F}_{R})\subset\mathrm{W}^{1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{q}},q}(\partial\mathcal{S}_{0})
.
∎

As we will employ a perturbation argument in order to establish the boundedness of the
{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}
-calculus, we also discuss the solvability of the weak Neumann problem (
4.6
) for less regular data.
For this purpose, we follow
[
29
, Section 5.2]
.
More precisely, for
s\in(0,1)
,
q\in(1,\infty)
and
q^{\prime}\in(1,\infty)
such that
\nicefrac{{1}}{{q}}+\nicefrac{{1}}{{q^{\prime}}}=1
, we consider
h\in\mathrm{H}^{s+\nicefrac{{1}}{{q}}-1,q}(\mathcal{F}_{0})^{3}
.
It is then possible to extend
\mdiv h
to a distribution in
\tilde{\mathrm{H}}^{s-1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{q}},q}(\mathcal{F}_{0})
, where

\tilde{\mathrm{H}}^{t,p}(\mathcal{F}_{0})\coloneqq\bigl\{f\in\mathrm{H}^{t,p}(\mathcal{F}_{0}):\mathrm{supp}\,f\subset\overline{\mathcal{F}_{0}}\bigr\},\kern 5.0pt\text{for}\kern 5.0ptt\in\mathbb{R}\kern 5.0pt\text{and}\kern 5.0ptp\in(1,\infty).

Moreover, the normal component
h\cdot n
can be identified with an element of
\mathrm{W}^{s-1,q}(\partial\mathcal{F}_{0})^{3}
via

\langle h\cdot n,\phi\rangle=\langle h,\nabla\tilde{\phi}\rangle+\langle\mdiv u,\tilde{\phi}\rangle

for all
\phi\in\mathrm{W}^{1-s,q^{\prime}}(\partial\mathcal{F}_{0})
extending to
\tilde{\phi}\in\mathrm{H}^{1-s+\nicefrac{{1}}{{q^{\prime}}},q^{\prime}}(\mathcal{F}_{0})
in the trace sense, see also
[
29
, Lemma 5.3]
.
By
[
29
, Lemma 5.5]
, we have

\|h\cdot n\|_{\mathrm{W}^{s-1,q}(\partial\mathcal{F}_{0})}\leq\|h\|_{\mathrm{H}^{s+\nicefrac{{1}}{{q}}-1,q}(\mathcal{F}_{0})}+\|\mdiv h\|_{\mathrm{H}^{s-\nicefrac{{1}}{{q^{\prime}}}-1,q}}.

Furthermore,
[
29
, Theorem 5.8]
implies that for all
q\in(\nicefrac{{3}}{{2}},\infty)
, there is
t\in(1,2-\nicefrac{{1}}{{q}})
such that the Neumann problem (
4.6
) has a unique solution
\varphi\in\mathrm{W}^{2-t,q}(\mathcal{F}_{0})^{3}
, and it satisfies the estimate

\|\varphi\|_{\mathrm{W}^{2-t,q}(\mathcal{F}_{0})^{3}}\leq C\cdot\|h\|_{\mathrm{H}^{1-t,q}(\mathcal{F}_{0})}.

We stress that the restriction on
q>\nicefrac{{3}}{{2}}
arises from the desire to circumvent the spatial weights in
[
29
, Theorem 5.8]
.
They are introduced in
[
29
, Section 3]
to show the boundedness of Newtonian potentials on unbounded domains, and the choice
t>1
then induces constraints on
q
even though we consider an exterior
\mathrm{C}^{3}
-domain instead of a Lipschitz one as in
[
29
]
.
Indeed, for
\delta_{1}\in(-\nicefrac{{3}}{{q}},-2+\nicefrac{{3}}{{q^{\prime}}})
and
\delta_{2}\in(-\nicefrac{{3}}{{q^{\prime}}},-2+\nicefrac{{3}}{{q}})
, in view of
[
29
, Theorem 5.8]
, we have to find
t\in(1,2-\nicefrac{{1}}{{q}})
with

(4.8)

0=-\frac{t}{2}(\delta_{1}+\delta_{2})+\delta_{1}+2(1-t)

in order to bypass the weights.
Note that the above ranges of
\delta_{1}
and
\delta_{2}
can also be expressed completely in terms of
q
, namely we have to find
\delta_{1}\in(-\nicefrac{{3}}{{q}},1-\nicefrac{{3}}{{q}})
and
\delta_{2}\in(-3+\nicefrac{{3}}{{q}},-2+\nicefrac{{3}}{{q}})
.
In addition, we write
t=1+\varepsilon
, where
\varepsilon\in(0,1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{q}})
.
Thus, (
4.8
) is equivalent with

(4.9)

\frac{1+\varepsilon}{2}\cdot\delta_{2}+2\varepsilon=\frac{1-\varepsilon}{2}\cdot\delta_{1},\kern 5.0pt\text{so}\kern 5.0pt\delta_{2}=\frac{1-\varepsilon}{1+\varepsilon}\cdot\delta_{1}-\frac{2}{1+\varepsilon}\cdot 2\varepsilon.

Let now
q\in(\nicefrac{{3}}{{2}},\infty)
.
We shall see in the sequel that it is possible to find suitable
\varepsilon\in(0,1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{q}})
and
\delta_{1}
,
\delta_{2}
in the aforementioned ranges such that (
4.9
), or, equivalently, (
4.8
) for
t\in(1,2-\nicefrac{{1}}{{q}})
is satisfied.
For
q>\nicefrac{{3}}{{2}}
, we consider
\delta_{1}\in(-\nicefrac{{3}}{{q}},1-\nicefrac{{3}}{{q}})
and
\delta_{2}
as given in (
4.9
), and we now check that there exists
\varepsilon\in(0,1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{q}})
such that this is fulfilled.
The ranges for
\delta_{1}
then yield that

\delta_{2}<\frac{1-\varepsilon}{1+\varepsilon}\Bigl(1-\frac{3}{p}\Bigr)-\frac{2}{1+\varepsilon}\cdot 2\varepsilon\kern 5.0pt\text{and}\kern 5.0pt\delta_{2}>\frac{1-\varepsilon}{1+\varepsilon}\Bigl(-\frac{3}{q}\Bigr)-\frac{2}{1+\varepsilon}\cdot 2\varepsilon.

With regard to
\delta_{2}\in(-3+\nicefrac{{3}}{{q}},-2+\nicefrac{{3}}{{q}})
, this requires that

(4.10)

-3+\frac{3}{q}<\frac{1-\varepsilon}{1+\varepsilon}\Bigl(1-\frac{3}{q}\Bigr)-\frac{2}{1+\varepsilon}\cdot 2\varepsilon\kern 5.0pt\text{and}\kern 5.0pt-2+\frac{3}{q}>\frac{1-\varepsilon}{1+\varepsilon}\cdot\Bigl(-\frac{3}{q}\Bigr)-\frac{2}{1+\varepsilon}\cdot 2\varepsilon.

Straightforward calculations show that (
4.10
) can be guaranteed provided
\varepsilon<2-\nicefrac{{3}}{{q}}
and
\varepsilon>1-\nicefrac{{3}}{{q}}
.
By
\varepsilon\in(0,1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{q}})
, we can find such
\varepsilon
provided
q>\nicefrac{{3}}{{2}}
, revealing that for such
q
, there exists
t\in(1,2-\nicefrac{{1}}{{q}})
such that (
4.8
), is satisfied for
\delta_{1}
and
\delta_{2}
in the above ranges.

Subtracting the spatial average on
\mathcal{F}_{R}=\mathcal{F}_{0}\cap B_{R}
, and denoting the associated solution operator by
N_{R}
by a slight abuse of notation, we conclude the assertion of the lemma below.
Note that we especially use classical properties of the trace to deduce that
N_{R}h\in\mathrm{L}^{q}(\partial\mathcal{S}_{0})
.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
q\in(\nicefrac{{3}}{{2}},\infty)
.
Then there exists
t\in(1,2-\nicefrac{{1}}{{q}})
such that for
h\in\mathrm{H}^{1-t,q}(\mathcal{F}_{0})^{3}
, we have
N_{R}h\in\mathrm{L}^{q}(\partial\mathcal{S}_{0})^{3}
, and for sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
, it holds that

\|N_{R}h\|_{\mathrm{W}^{2-t,q}(\mathcal{F}_{0})}\leq C\cdot\|h\|_{\mathrm{H}^{1-t,q}(\mathcal{F}_{0})}.

Equipped with the aforementioned lifting operators, we are now in the position to reformulate the inhomogeneous Stokes resolvent problem in terms of the lifting operators.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
q\in(1,\infty)
as well as
(f,\ell,\omega)\in\mathrm{L}_{\sigma}^{q}(\mathcal{F}_{0})\times\mathbb{C}^{3}\times\mathbb{C}^{3}
, and for
R>0
sufficiently large as specified above,
\mathcal{F}_{R}=\mathcal{F}_{0}\cap B_{R}
.
Then
(u,p)\in\mathrm{W}^{2,q}(\mathcal{F}_{0})^{3}\times\widehat{\mathrm{W}}^{1,q}(\mathcal{F}_{0})\cap\mathrm{L}_{0}^{q}(\mathcal{F}_{R})
satisfies

(4.11)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \lambda u-\Delta u+\nabla p&=f,\kern 5.0pt\mdiv u=0,&&\kern 5.0pt\text{in}\kern 5.0pt\mathcal{F}_{0},\\
u&=\ell+\omega\times y,&&\kern 5.0pt\text{on}\kern 5.0pt\partial\mathcal{S}_{0}\end{aligned}\right.

if and only if

(4.12)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \lambda\mathcal{P}u-A_{0}\mathcal{P}u+(A_{0}-\lambda_{0})\mathcal{P}D_{\lambda_{0},u}(\ell,\omega)&=\mathcal{P}f,\\
(\Id-\mathcal{P})u&=(\Id-\mathcal{P})D_{\lambda_{0},u}(\ell,\omega),\\
p&=N_{R}(\Delta\mathcal{P}u)-\lambda N_{R}(\ell+\omega\times y).\end{aligned}\right.

Proof.

First, we consider
(u,p)\in\mathrm{W}^{2,q}(\mathcal{F}_{0})^{3}\times\widehat{\mathrm{W}}^{1,q}(\mathcal{F}_{0})\cap\mathrm{L}_{0}^{q}(\mathcal{F}_{R})
solving (
4.11
).
We then define

\tilde{u}\coloneqq u-D_{\lambda_{0},u}(\ell,\omega)\kern 5.0pt\text{and}\kern 5.0pt\tilde{p}\coloneqq p-D_{\lambda_{0},p}(\ell,\omega).

By construction, it follows that

\lambda\tilde{u}+\lambda D_{\lambda_{0},u}(\ell,\omega)-\Delta\tilde{u}+\nabla\tilde{p}=f+\lambda_{0}D_{\lambda_{0},u}(\ell,\omega),\kern 5.0pt\mdiv\tilde{u}=0,\kern 5.0pt\text{in}\kern 5.0pt\mathcal{F}_{0},\kern 5.0pt\text{and}\kern 5.0pt\tilde{u}=0,\kern 5.0pt\text{on}\kern 5.0pt\partial\mathcal{F}_{0}.

Let us also observe that
\tilde{u}\in\mathrm{D}(A_{0})
, leading to
\mathcal{P}\tilde{u}=\tilde{u}
.
An application of the Helmholtz projection to the preceding system then yields

\lambda\mathcal{P}u-A_{0}\mathcal{P}u+(A_{0}-\lambda_{0})\mathcal{P}D_{\lambda_{0},u}(\ell,\omega)=\mathcal{P}f.

Another consequence of
\mathcal{P}\tilde{u}=\tilde{u}
is that

(\Id-\mathcal{P})u=(\Id-\mathcal{P})(\tilde{u}+D_{\lambda_{0},u}(\ell,\omega))=(\Id-\mathcal{P})D_{\lambda_{0},u}(\ell,\omega).

The representation of the Helmholtz projection from
Section 4
(b) implies that
\Delta(\Id-\mathcal{P})u=0
in
\mathcal{F}_{0}
by
\mdiv u=0
in
\mathcal{F}_{0}
.
Hence, we may reformulate (
4.11
)
1
as

\lambda u-\Delta\mathcal{P}u+\nabla p=f,\kern 5.0pt\mdiv u=0,\kern 5.0pt\text{in}\kern 5.0pt\mathcal{F}_{0}.

Applying the divergence and the normal trace to this equation, which is legitimate thanks to
Section 4
(a), and where we exploit
f\in\mathrm{L}_{\sigma}^{q}(\mathcal{F}_{0})
in conjunction with the characterization of this space in
Section 4
(c), we find that
p
solves

(4.13)

\Delta p=0,\kern 5.0pt\text{in}\kern 5.0pt\mathcal{F}_{0},\kern 5.0pt\partial_{n}p=\Delta\mathcal{P}u\cdot n-\lambda u\cdot n,\kern 5.0pt\text{on}\kern 5.0pt\partial\mathcal{F}_{0}.

Note that
\mdiv\Delta\mathcal{P}u=\mdiv u=0
, so
\Delta\mathcal{P}u-\lambda u\in\mathrm{W}_{\mdiv}^{q}(\mathcal{F}_{0})
, and
p=N_{R}(\Delta\mathcal{P}u-\lambda u)\in\widehat{\mathrm{W}}^{1,q}(\mathcal{F}_{0})\cap\mathrm{L}_{0}^{q}(\mathcal{F}_{R})
is the unique solution to (
4.13
).
This shows that the representation formulae from (
4.12
) are valid.

On the other hand, suppose that
(u,p)\in\mathrm{W}^{2,q}(\mathcal{F}_{0})^{3}\times\widehat{\mathrm{W}}^{1,q}(\mathcal{F}_{0})\cap\mathrm{L}_{0}^{q}(\mathcal{F}_{R})
fulfill (
4.12
).
From (
4.12
)
2
, we conclude that
\tilde{u}\coloneqq u-D_{\lambda_{0},u}(\ell,\omega)\in\mathrm{D}(A_{0})
, so (
4.12
)
1
can be rephrased as

\mathcal{P}(\lambda\tilde{u}-A_{0}\tilde{u})=\mathcal{P}(f-(\lambda-\lambda_{0})D_{\lambda_{0},u}(\ell,\omega)).

By definition of the Helmholtz projection, there exists
\tilde{p}\in\widehat{\mathrm{W}}^{1,q}(\mathcal{F}_{0})
, which we can additionally choose in
\mathrm{L}_{0}^{q}(\mathcal{F}_{R})
upon subtracting its spatial average in
\mathcal{F}_{R}
as in (
4.7
), and
(\tilde{u},\tilde{p})
is the solution to

\lambda\tilde{u}-\Delta\tilde{u}+\nabla\tilde{p}=f-(\lambda-\lambda_{0})D_{\lambda_{0},u}(\ell,\omega),\kern 5.0pt\mdiv\tilde{u}=0,\kern 5.0pt\text{in}\kern 5.0pt\mathcal{F}_{0},\kern 5.0pt\tilde{u}=0,\kern 5.0pt\text{on}\kern 5.0pt\partial\mathcal{F}_{0}.

As a result, for
D_{\lambda_{0},u}
and
D_{\lambda_{0},p}
emerging from
Section 4
, we find that
u=\tilde{u}+D_{\lambda_{0},u}(\ell,\omega)
and
p=\tilde{p}+D_{\lambda_{0},p}(\ell,\omega)
is a solution to (
4.11
), completing the proof of the equivalence.
∎

The preceding lemma allows us to rewrite the resolvent problem associated to (
4.2
).
Indeed, for
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
,
f_{1}\in\mathrm{L}^{q}(\mathcal{F}_{0})^{3}
and
(f_{2},f_{3})\in\mathbb{C}^{3}\times\mathbb{C}^{3}

has been made precise in (
4.19
).
We also recall the space
\mathrm{Y}_{0}
from (
4.18
) and set
\mathrm{Y}_{1}\coloneqq\mathrm{D}(\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathrm{fs}})=\mathrm{W}^{2,q}(\mathcal{F}_{0})^{3}\cap\mathrm{W}_{0}^{1,q}(\mathcal{F}_{0})^{3}\cap\mathrm{L}_{\sigma}^{q}(\mathcal{F}_{0})\times\mathbb{C}^{3}\times\mathbb{C}^{3}
.

Arguing in a similar way as in
[
10
, Lemma 5.3]
, we find that the interpolation spaces in the coupled setting can be recovered from those in the decoupled setting via the matrix
S^{-1}
by

[\mathrm{X}_{0},\mathrm{X}_{1}]_{\theta}=S^{-1}([\mathrm{Y}_{0},\mathrm{Y}_{1}]_{\theta})\kern 5.0pt\text{and}\kern 5.0pt(\mathrm{X}_{0},\mathrm{X}_{1})_{\theta,p}=S^{-1}((\mathrm{Y}_{0},\mathrm{Y}_{1})_{\theta,p}),\kern 5.0pt\text{for}\kern 5.0pt\theta\in(0,1)\kern 5.0pt\text{and}\kern 5.0ptp\in(1,\infty).

Below, for
i\in\{1,2\}
, we will also write
\mathrm{Y}_{i}=\mathrm{Y}_{i}^{u}\times\mathrm{Y}_{i}^{\ell}\times\mathrm{Y}_{i}^{\omega}
for the decoupled spaces.
We then get the identity
[\mathrm{Y}_{0},\mathrm{Y}_{1}]_{\theta}=\left\{(\mathcal{P}u,\ell,\omega)\in[\mathrm{Y}_{0}^{u},\mathrm{Y}_{1}^{u}]_{\theta}\times\mathbb{C}^{3}\times\mathbb{C}^{3}\right\}
.
This results in

(5.1)

[\mathrm{X}_{0},\mathrm{X}_{1}]_{\theta}=S^{-1}([\mathrm{Y}_{0},\mathrm{Y}_{1}]_{\theta})=\left\{(\mathcal{P}u,\ell,\omega)\in\mathrm{Y}_{0}:\mathcal{P}u-\mathcal{P}D_{\lambda_{0},u}(\ell,\omega)\in[\mathrm{Y}_{0}^{u},\mathrm{Y}_{1}^{u}]_{\theta}\right\},\kern 5.0pt\text{for}\kern 5.0pt\theta\in(0,1).

A similar argument as above for the shape of
\mathrm{X}_{1}
shows that the space
\mathrm{X}_{\beta}
includes the interface condition provided
\beta
is sufficiently large so that elements in
[\mathrm{Y}_{0}^{u},\mathrm{Y}_{1}^{u}]_{\beta}
admit a trace, which is the case for
\beta>\nicefrac{{1}}{{2q}}
.
Likewise, for the real interpolation spaces with
\theta\in(0,1)
and
p\in(1,\infty)
, it follows that

(5.2)

(\mathrm{X}_{0},\mathrm{X}_{1})_{\theta,p}=S^{-1}((\mathrm{Y}_{0},\mathrm{Y}_{1})_{\theta,p})=\left\{(\mathcal{P}u,\ell,\omega)\in\mathrm{Y}_{0}:\mathcal{P}u-\mathcal{P}D_{\lambda_{0},u}(\ell,\omega)\in(\mathrm{Y}_{0}^{u},\mathrm{Y}_{1}^{u})_{\theta,p}\right\},

where the interface condition enters if
\theta>\nicefrac{{1}}{{2q}}
.
Note that for
\theta<\nicefrac{{1}}{{2q}}
, we still have
u\cdot n=(\ell+\omega\times y)\cdot n
by the characterization of the space
\mathrm{L}_{\sigma}^{q}(\mathcal{F}_{0})
from
Section 4
(c).
In summary, the computation of the interpolation spaces reduces to the study of the non-coupled ones related to
\mathrm{Y}_{0}
and
\mathrm{Y}_{1}
.

Concerning this reduced problem, the main challenge is to find a projection
Q
on
\mathrm{L}^{q}(\mathcal{F}_{0})^{3}
such that for the domain
\mathrm{D}(\Delta_{\mathrm{D}})\coloneqq\mathrm{W}^{2,q}(\mathcal{F}_{0})^{3}\cap\mathrm{W}_{0}^{1,q}(\mathcal{F}_{0})^{3}
of the Dirichlet Laplacian
\Delta_{\mathrm{D}}u\coloneqq\Delta u
for
u\in\mathrm{D}(\Delta_{\mathrm{D}})
, it holds that
Q\mathrm{D}(\Delta_{\mathrm{D}})=\mathrm{D}(\Delta_{\mathrm{D}})\cap\mathrm{R}(Q)
.
Let us observe that this can be done in a similar manner as in
[
40
, Section 4]
.
For the (shifted) Dirichlet Laplacian
\Delta_{\mathrm{D}}^{1}\coloneqq\Id-\Delta_{\mathrm{D}}
with
\mathrm{D}(\Delta_{\mathrm{D}}^{1})=\mathrm{W}^{2,q}(\mathcal{F}_{0})^{3}\cap\mathrm{W}_{0}^{1,q}(\mathcal{F}_{0})^{3}
and the shifted negative Stokes operator
A_{1}\coloneqq\Id-A_{0}
with
\mathrm{D}(A_{1})=\mathrm{W}^{2,q}(\mathcal{F}_{0})^{3}\cap\mathrm{W}_{0}^{1,q}(\mathcal{F}_{0})^{3}\cap\mathrm{L}_{\sigma}^{q}(\mathcal{F}_{0})
.
The projection
Q\colon\mathrm{D}(\Delta_{\mathrm{D}}^{1})=\mathrm{D}(\Delta_{\mathrm{D}})\to\mathrm{D}(A_{0})=\mathrm{D}(A_{1})
is then given by
Qu\coloneqq A_{1}^{-1}\mathcal{P}\Delta_{\mathrm{D}}^{1}u
.

One can argue that
Q
admits a unique extension
\tilde{Q}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathrm{L}^{q}(\mathcal{F}_{0})^{3},\mathrm{L}_{\sigma}^{q}(\mathcal{F}_{0}))
that is also a projection such that
\left.\tilde{Q}\right|_{\mathrm{L}_{\sigma}^{q}(\mathcal{F}_{0})}=\left.\Id\right|_{\mathrm{L}_{\sigma}^{q}(\mathcal{F}_{0})}
.
By
\tilde{Q}\mathrm{D}(\Delta_{\mathrm{D}})=\mathrm{D}(\Delta_{\mathrm{D}})\cap\mathrm{R}(\tilde{Q})=\mathrm{D}(A_{0})
, we get
\mathrm{L}^{q}(\mathcal{F}_{0})^{3}=\mathrm{L}_{\sigma}^{q}(\mathcal{F}_{0})\oplus\mathrm{N}(\tilde{Q})
and
\mathrm{D}(\Delta_{\mathrm{D}})=[\mathrm{D}(\Delta_{\mathrm{D}})\cap\mathrm{R}(\tilde{Q})]\oplus[\mathrm{D}(\Delta_{\mathrm{D}})\cap\mathrm{N}(\tilde{Q})]
.
The well-known boundedness of the imaginary powers of
\Delta_{\mathrm{D}}^{1}
together with interpolation theoretic arguments, see
[
49
, Theorem 1.17.1.1]
, then yields

[\mathrm{L}_{\sigma}^{q}(\mathcal{F}_{0}),\mathrm{D}(A_{0})]_{\theta}=[\tilde{Q}\mathrm{L}^{q}(\mathcal{F}_{0})^{3},\tilde{Q}\mathrm{D}(\Delta_{\mathrm{D}}^{1})]_{\theta}=\tilde{Q}[\mathrm{L}^{q}(\mathcal{F}_{0})^{3},\mathrm{D}(\Delta_{\mathrm{D}}^{1})]_{\theta}=\mathrm{D}((\Delta_{\mathrm{D}}^{1})^{\theta})\cap\mathrm{R}(\tilde{Q}),

for
\theta\in(0,1)
, and likewise for the real interpolation spaces.
In view of the reduction argument in the first part of the proof, this shows the assertion of the lemma.
∎

Next, we rewrite the stochastic fluid-rigid body interaction problem (

) as a bilinear SPDE of the form as in (

).
In fact, recalling the fluid-structure operator
\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{fs}}
from (

), similarly as in (

, we find that (

) is equivalent with

(5.3)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \,\mathrm{d}w-\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{fs}}w\,\mathrm{d}t&=F(w)\,\mathrm{d}t+(Bw+f)\,\mathrm{d}W_{t}^{n},\\
w(0)&=\begin{pmatrix}\mathcal{P}v_{0}\\
\ell_{0}\\
\omega_{0}\end{pmatrix},\end{aligned}\right.

where

(5.4)

F(w)=\begin{pmatrix}-\mathcal{P}[((v-\ell)\cdot\nabla)v]\\
0\\
0\end{pmatrix},\kern 5.0pt(Bw)\mathrm{e}_{n}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathcal{P}[(b_{n}\cdot\nabla)v]\\
0\\
0\end{pmatrix}\kern 5.0pt\text{and}\kern 5.0ptf\mathrm{e}_{n}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathcal{P}f_{v}^{n}\\
\tilde{f}_{\ell}^{n}\\
\tilde{f}_{\omega}^{n}\end{pmatrix},

with
\binom{\tilde{f}_{\ell}^{n}}{\tilde{f}_{\omega}^{n}}=K^{-1}\binom{f_{\ell}^{n}}{f_{\omega}^{n}}
, and

has been made precise in (

).

At this stage, we begin with the verification that (

) lies within the scope of
Subsection 2.3
.
First, for the ground space
\mathrm{X}_{0}
as made precise in (
4.16
), we recall from

that it is isomorphic to a closed subspace of
\mathrm{L}^{q}
on a
\sigma
-finite measure space, so for

, we especially deduce that
\mathrm{X}_{0}
is a UMD space with type
2
, see also
[

, Chapter 7]
for more details on this property.

After establishing this basic property, we next show that
(-\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{fs}},B)\in\mathcal{S}\mathcal{M}\mathcal{R}_{p,\kappa}^{\bullet}
, where the fluid-structure operator
\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{fs}}
has been introduced in (

), and
B
is as made precise in (

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
p
,
q\in[2,\infty)
,
\kappa\in[0,\nicefrac{{p}}{{2}}-1)\cup\{0\}
, and suppose that there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that
\|(b_{n})_{n\geq 1}\|_{\mathrm{W}^{1,\infty}(\mathcal{F}_{0};\ell^{2})}<\varepsilon
.
If
\varepsilon>0
is sufficiently small, it holds that
(-\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{fs}},B)\in\mathcal{S}\mathcal{M}\mathcal{R}_{p,\kappa}^{\bullet}
.

Proof.

First, from
Section 4
, we recall that
(-\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{fs}},0)\in\mathcal{S}\mathcal{M}\mathcal{R}_{p,\kappa}^{\bullet}
.
In addition, it also readily follows that
(-\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{fs}},0)\in\mathcal{S}\mathcal{M}\mathcal{R}_{p}
.
In the following, we employ the notation
(Bw)\mathrm{e}_{n}=(\mathcal{P}[(b_{n}\cdot\nabla)v],0,0)^{\top}
.
Invoking the shape of
\mathrm{X}_{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}
from (
3.1
), recalling from
Subsection 2.1
that
\gamma(\ell^{2},\mathrm{W}^{1,q}(\mathcal{F}_{0}))=\mathrm{W}^{1,q}(\mathcal{F}_{0};\ell^{2})
, and making use of the assumption, for
w\in\mathrm{X}_{1}
, we infer that

\displaystyle\|Bw\|_{\gamma(\ell^{2},\mathrm{X}_{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}})}

\displaystyle\leq C\cdot\left\|\sum_{n\geq 1}[(b_{n}\cdot\nabla)v]\right\|_{\gamma(\ell^{2},\mathrm{W}^{1,q}(\mathcal{F}_{0}))}\leq C\cdot\left\|\sum_{n\geq 1}[(b_{n}\cdot\nabla)v]\right\|_{\mathrm{W}^{1,q}(\mathcal{F}_{0};\ell^{2})}

\displaystyle\leq\|(b_{n})_{n\geq 1}\|_{\mathrm{W}^{1,\infty}(\mathcal{F}_{0};\ell^{2})}\cdot\|v\|_{\mathrm{W}^{2,q}(\mathcal{F}_{0})}\leq C\varepsilon\cdot\|w\|_{\mathrm{X}_{1}}.

Hence, the map
B
satisfies the desired mapping properties from
Subsection 2.3
, and we deduce from the latter lemma that indeed,
(-\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{fs}},B)\in\mathcal{S}\mathcal{M}\mathcal{R}_{p,\kappa}^{\bullet}
.
∎

In the lemma below, we discuss the estimates of the bilinear term
F
from (
5.4
).

Lemma 5.2
.

Recall
F(w)=G(w,w)
from (
5.4
) and the shape of
\mathrm{X}_{\beta}=[\mathrm{X}_{0},\mathrm{X}_{1}]_{\beta}
from
Section 3
.

(1)

Let
q\in(1,3)
.
Then for
\beta\geq\frac{1}{4}(1+\frac{3}{q})\in(0,1)
, the map
G\colon\mathrm{X}_{\beta}\times\mathrm{X}_{\beta}\to\mathrm{X}_{0}
is bounded.

(2)

If
q\geq 3
, then for
\beta\in[\frac{1}{2},1)
, the map
G\colon\mathrm{X}_{\beta}\times\mathrm{X}_{\beta}\to\mathrm{X}_{0}
is bounded.

Proof.

Let us start with the case
q\in(1,3)
, which is the critical one.
Hölder’s inequality first yields that

\|G(w,w)\|_{\mathrm{X}_{0}}\leq\|((v-\ell)\cdot\nabla)v\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}(\mathcal{F}_{0})}\leq C\bigl(\|v\|_{\mathrm{L}^{qr^{\prime}}(\mathcal{F}_{0})}\cdot\|v\|_{\mathrm{H}^{1,qr}(\mathcal{F}_{0})}+|\ell|\cdot\|v\|_{\mathrm{W}^{1,q}(\mathcal{F}_{0})}\bigr),

where
r
,
r^{\prime}\in(1,\infty)
are such that
\nicefrac{{1}}{{r}}+\nicefrac{{1}}{{r^{\prime}}}=1
.
With the choice
\nicefrac{{3}}{{qr}}=\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}(1+\nicefrac{{3}}{{q}})
, for which we require that
q<3
, we can ensure that the Sobolev indices of
\mathrm{L}^{qr^{\prime}}(\mathcal{F}_{0})
and
\mathrm{H}^{1,qr}(\mathcal{F}_{0})
coincide.
Sobolev embeddings, see for example
[
49
, Theorem 4.6.1]
, yield that

\mathrm{H}^{2\beta,q}(\mathcal{F}_{0})\hookrightarrow\mathrm{L}^{qr^{\prime}}(\mathcal{F}_{0})\cap\mathrm{H}^{1,qr}(\mathcal{F}_{0})\kern 5.0pt\text{if}\kern 5.0pt\beta\geq\frac{1}{4}\left(1+\frac{3}{q}\right).

Let us observe that the preceding condition on
\beta
together with
q\in(1,3)
already implies that
\beta\in(\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}},1)
.
It then follows from Sobolev embeddings that
\mathrm{H}^{2\beta,q}(\mathcal{F}_{0})\hookrightarrow\mathrm{W}^{1,q}(\mathcal{F}_{0})
.
Putting together these arguments, and invoking the above shape of the space
\mathrm{X}_{\beta}
, we find that

\|G(w,w)\|_{\mathrm{X}_{0}}\leq C\cdot\|w\|_{\mathrm{X}_{\beta}}^{2},\kern 5.0pt\text{for}\kern 5.0pt\beta\geq\frac{1}{4}\left(1+\frac{3}{q}\right)\kern 5.0pt\text{and}\kern 5.0ptq<3.

This shows the assertion of (a).

For (b), we first observe that for all
r\in(1,\infty)
, we obtain that
1-\frac{3}{qr}\geq-\frac{3}{qr^{\prime}}
, since
r(3+q)>6
in this case.
Hence, the embedding
\mathrm{H}^{2\beta,q}(\mathcal{F}_{0})\hookrightarrow\mathrm{H}^{1,qr}(\mathcal{F}_{0})
is always the more restrictive one in this situation.
Thus, this time, we estimate

\|G(w,w)\|_{\mathrm{X}_{0}}\leq\|((v-\ell)\cdot\nabla)v\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}(\mathcal{F}_{0})}\leq C\bigl(\|v\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\mathcal{F}_{0})}\cdot\|v\|_{\mathrm{W}^{1,q}(\mathcal{F}_{0})}+|\ell|\cdot\|v\|_{\mathrm{W}^{1,q}(\mathcal{F}_{0})}\bigr).

It readily follows that
\mathrm{H}^{2\beta,q}(\mathcal{F}_{0})\hookrightarrow\mathrm{W}^{1,q}(\mathcal{F}_{0})
provided
\beta\geq\frac{1}{2}
, so by
Section 3
, we get

\|G(w,w)\|_{\mathrm{X}_{0}}\leq C\cdot\|w\|_{\mathrm{X}_{\beta}}^{2},\kern 5.0pt\text{for}\kern 5.0pt\beta\in\left(\frac{1}{2},1\right)\kern 5.0pt\text{and}\kern 5.0ptq\geq 3.\qed

We are now in the position to prove the local strong well-posedness result
Theorem 3.3
.

Proof of
Theorem 3.3
.

For an application of
Subsection 2.3
, it remains to verify the validity of
Subsection 2.3
in the present setting.
Note that we have already discussed that
\mathrm{X}_{0}
is a UMD space with type
2
provided
q\geq 2
, and that
\lambda-\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{fs}}\in{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}(\mathrm{X}_{0})
, see
Theorem 4.9
.
In addition, we have already established that
(-\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{fs}},B)\in\mathcal{S}\mathcal{M}\mathcal{R}_{p,\kappa}^{\bullet}
in
Section 5
.
As a consequence, it remains to show that there are
p\in[2,\infty)
,
\kappa\in[0,\nicefrac{{p}}{{2}}-1)\cup\{0\}
and
\beta\in(1-\frac{1+\kappa}{p},1)
such that
\frac{1+\kappa}{p}\leq 2(1-\beta)
, and the map
F(w)=G(w,w)
as introduced in (
5.4
) is bilinear and bounded from
\mathrm{X}_{\beta}\times\mathrm{X}_{\beta}
to
\mathrm{X}_{0}
.

In the following, we distinguish between the two cases and start with the critical one.
Hence, we first consider
q\in[2,3)
,
p\in(2,\infty)
and
\beta\geq\frac{1}{4}(1+\frac{3}{q})
as in
Section 5
.
Note that
\beta<1
is directly implied by
q\geq 2>1
.
On the other hand, the condition
\beta>1-\frac{1+\kappa}{p}
is equivalent with
\kappa>\frac{3p}{4}(1-\frac{1}{q})-1
, so by the requirement that
\kappa<\frac{p}{2}-1
, we need that
\frac{3p}{4}(1-\frac{1}{q})-1<\frac{p}{2}-1
in order to find such
\kappa
.
This is in turn equivalent with
q<3
.
Finally, let us check the inequality
\frac{1+\kappa}{p}\leq 2(1-\beta)\leq\frac{3}{2}(1-\frac{1}{q})
by the choice of
\beta
.
This condition can be shown to be equivalent with
\kappa\leq\frac{3p}{2}(1-\frac{1}{q})-1
.
Since
\kappa\geq 0
, we need that
\frac{3p}{2}(1-\frac{1}{q})-1\geq 0
, which is satisfied provided
\frac{2}{p}+\frac{3}{q}\leq 3
.
The latter condition is already fulfilled for the present case of
p\geq 2
and
q\geq 2
.
In summary, we have verified that in the present situation of
q\in[2,3)
, there are
p\in[2,\infty)
,
\kappa\in[0,\frac{p}{2}-1)\cup\{0\}
and
\beta\in(1-\frac{1+\kappa}{p},1)
with
\frac{1+\kappa}{p}\leq 2(1-\beta)
.

We now repeat the procedure for
q\geq 3
.
By
Section 5
(b), we then consider
\beta\in[\frac{1}{2},1)
, so
\beta<1
is already satisfied.
Moreover, we need that
\beta>1-\frac{1+\kappa}{p}
, which can be guaranteed for
\frac{1+\kappa}{p}>1-\beta
, or
\kappa>p(1-\beta)-1
.
By the requirement that
\kappa<\frac{p}{2}-1
, this means that
p(1-\beta)-1<\frac{p}{2}-1
, or, equivalently,
\beta>\frac{1}{2}
.
It remains to check that
\frac{1+\kappa}{p}\leq 2(1-\beta)
, so
\kappa\leq 2p(1-\beta)-1
.
Therefore, by
\kappa\geq 0
, we need that
2p(1-\beta)\geq 0
, but this is already implied by
\beta\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
and
p\geq 2
.

In total, the assertion of
Theorem 3.3
follows from an application of
Subsection 2.3
.
∎

We remark that the previous proof especially covers the local strong well-posedness result
Theorem 3.5
in the Hilbert space case.
Thus, we proceed with the proof of the blow-up criterion
Theorem 3.6
.

Proof of
Theorem 3.6
.

From the above proof of
Theorem 3.3
, it follows that
\beta=\frac{1}{4}(1+\frac{3}{q})
and the associated choice
\frac{1+\kappa}{p}=2(1-\beta)=\frac{3}{2}-\frac{3}{2q}
corresponds to the critical case.
Note that this is only possible if
q\in[2,3)
, and we obtain
\mathrm{X}_{1-\frac{1+\kappa}{p},p}=\mathrm{X}_{\frac{3}{2q}-\frac{1}{2},p}
and
\mathrm{X}_{1-\frac{\kappa}{p}}=\mathrm{X}_{\frac{1}{p}+\frac{3}{2q}-\frac{1}{2}}
.
By
Subsection 2.3
(a), the first part of the assertion is implied.
As a by-product of the proof of
Theorem 3.3
, we also find that the case
q\geq 3
is always subcritical, so
Subsection 2.3
(b) yields the assertion of (b).
∎

Finally,
Theorem 3.7
is a consequence of
Subsection 2.3
(b) upon noting that the situation of
p=q=2
and
\kappa=0
is subcritical.
Indeed, the proof of
Theorem 3.3
reveals that
\beta=\frac{5}{8}
is the critical value, i. e.,
2(1-\beta)=\frac{3}{4}
.
However, we have
\frac{1+\kappa}{p}=\frac{1}{2}<\frac{3}{4}=2(1-\beta)
, demonstrating the subcriticality.

6.
Concluding remarks and further discussion

This section is devoted to the exposition of possible extensions of the results obtained in the previous sections, and we provide an overview of potential future works.

First, let us stress that further interesting functional analytic properties such as the
\mathcal{R}
-boundedness of the
{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}
-calculus of
-\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{fs}}
or the boundedness of the imaginary powers of
-\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{fs}}
could be deduced from
Theorem 4.9
.
However, we do not elaborate more on that and refer instead to
[
15
]
or
[
37
, Section 4.5]
for more details on these properties as well as their relation to the concepts studied here.

For completeness, let us also briefly elaborate on the case of a bounded fluid domain
\mathcal{F}_{0}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
.
In this case, we assume homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions for the fluid velocity on the outer fluid boundary.
The according lifting procedure is similar to
Section 4
and has been described in detail in
[
31
, Section 3]
.
There are two differences with
Section 4
:
On the one hand, a refined spectral analysis reveals that the shift, which is indispensable in the exterior domain case, can be omitted.
On the other hand, due to the boundedness of the underlying domain, the study of the Neumann problem for the pressure is more straightforward.
In fact, it is possible to follow
[
18
, Section 9]
and to employ
[
18
, Theorem 9.2]
to establish an analogue of
Section 4
, but without restriction on
q\in(1,\infty)
.

Mimicking the procedure from
Section 4
, we introduce the analogue of the fluid-structure operator for
\mathcal{F}_{0}
bounded, still denoted by
\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{fs}}
by a slight abuse of notation, and we get a similar result as
Theorem 4.9
.
The ground space in this setting is as in (
4.16
), so

\mathrm{X}_{0}=\{(u,\ell,\omega)\in\mathrm{L}^{q}(\mathcal{F}_{0})^{3}\times\mathbb{C}^{3}\times\mathbb{C}^{3}:u-D_{\lambda_{0},u}(\ell,\omega)\in\mathrm{L}_{\sigma}^{q}(\mathcal{F}_{0})\}.

Additionally invoking that
\mathbb{C}_{+}\subset\rho(\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{fs}})
in this case, see
[
31
, Theorem 4.2]
, we find that we may choose
\lambda=0
here.
This paves the way for the following result.
Note that the assertions in (a)–(c) can be derived from the boundedness of the
{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}
-calculus as sketched before
Section 4
.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
q\in(1,\infty)
.
Then for the fluid-structure operator
\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{fs}}
on the bounded domain
\mathcal{F}_{0}
, it holds that
-\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{fs}}\in{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}(\mathrm{X}_{0})
with
\phi_{-\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{fs}}}^{\infty}=0
, and

(1)

the operator
-\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{fs}}
has maximal
\mathrm{L}^{p}
-regularity on
\mathrm{X}_{0}
, and
\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{fs}}
generates a bounded analytic and exponentially stable semigroup
(\mathrm{e}^{t\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{fs}}})_{t\geq 0}
of angle
\nicefrac{{\pi}}{{2}}
on
\mathrm{X}_{0}
,

(2)

for
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
\mathrm{X}_{\alpha}
related to
-\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{fs}}
as specified in
Subsection 2.2
, we have
\mathrm{X}_{\alpha}\cong[\mathrm{X}_{0},\mathrm{X}_{\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{fs}}}]_{\alpha}
, and

(3)

for
p
,
q\in[2,\infty)
and
\kappa\in[0,\nicefrac{{p}}{{2}}-1)
, we get
(-\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{fs}},0)\in\mathcal{S}\mathcal{M}\mathcal{R}_{p,\kappa}^{\bullet}
.

A similar result is also available in the 2D case, and we stress again that we allow for a rigid body of arbitrary shape with
\mathrm{C}^{3}
-boundary.
To the best of our knowledge,
Theorem 4.9
and
Theorem 6.1
are the first results on the bounded
{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}
-calculus of fluid-structure operators.
In the case of a fluid-filled rigid body, Mazzone et al.
[
32
,
33
]
established the boundedness of the
{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}
-calculus of the underlying linear operator.
However, the situation of a rigid body with a fluid-filled cavity is significantly different from the present fluid-rigid body interaction problem, and the domain of the associated operator is especially
not
non-diagonal.
Also, note that, up to a shift in the exterior domain case, or, equivalently, up to the boundedness of the analytic semigroup, the results by Takahashi and Tucsnak
[
44
,
45
]
, Wang and Xin
[
54
]
, Maity and Tucsnak
[
31
]
as well as Ervedoza, Maity and Tucsnak
[
17
]
on the fluid-structure operator and semigroup as discussed in the introduction are implied by
Theorem 4.9
,
Section 4
and
Theorem 6.1
.

As indicated after
Theorem 3.5
, the regularity of the initial data aligns with the deterministic setting in the Hilbert space case.
By means of the theory of critical spaces, it is possible to lower the regularity of the initial data in the case of a rigid ball, and to obtain a local well-posedness result for initial data in spaces as made precise in

A natural question is if similar results as in
Section 3
can be obtained in the case of a rigid body of arbitrary shape, or of a bounded domain.
A difficulty in the former case is that the transformation to the fixed domain is more complicated.
In fact, common transformations to a fixed domain in this case either lead to the introduction of terms that violate the parabolicity of the problem, or terms that are non-local in time enter the equation.
For a bounded domain, another significant difficulty is the possibility of a collision of the rigid body with the outer fluid boundary.
It seems that the framework provided in
Subsection 2.3
is insufficient for the analysis, and we leave the investigation to future study.

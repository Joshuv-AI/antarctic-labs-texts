Relocated Fixed-Point Iterations with Applications to Variable Stepsize Resolvent Splitting
Felipe Atenas, Heinz H. Bauschke, Minh N. Dao, Matthew K. Tam

Abstract
In this work, we develop a convergence framework for iterative algorithms whose updates can be described by a one-parameter family of nonexpansive operators. Within the framework, each step involving one of the main algorithmic operators is followed by a second step which “relocates” fixed-points of the current operator to the next. As a consequence, our analysis does not require the family of nonexpansive operators to have a common fixed-point, as is common in the literature. Our analysis uses a parametric extension of the demiclosedness principle for nonexpansive operators. As an application of our convergence results, we develop a version of the graph-based extension of the Douglas–Rachford algorithm for finding a zero of the sum of N ≥ 2 N\geq 2 maximally monotone operators, which does not require the resolvent parameter to be constant across iterations.

Fixed-point iterations, demiclosedness principle, nonexpansive operator, resolvent splitting, Douglas–Rachford.

Introduction

Throughout this work we assume
X
is a real Hilbert space with inner product
\left\langle{\cdot},{\cdot}\right\rangle
and induced norm
\|\cdot\|
. We are interested in solving monotone inclusions of the form

\text{find~~}x\in X\text{~~such that~~}0\in(A+B)x,

(1)

where
A
and
B
are maximally monotone operators on
X
with
Z:=\operatorname{zer}(A+B)\neq\varnothing
as well as various extensions thereof (
e.g.,
inclusions with more than two operators).

The
Douglas–Rachford (DR)
algorithm
[
13
,
17
,
18
]
is a distinguished splitting method used to solve (
1
) by using the so-called
resolvents
of the individual operators
A
and
B
, without requiring any type of direct computation involving the operator
A+B
. Recall that the
resolvent
of
A
is the operator given by

J_{A}:=(I+A)^{-1},

and the
reflectent
of
A
(also known as the
reflected resolvent
) is the operator

R_{A}:=2J_{A}-\operatorname{Id}.

Given a stepsize
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}_{++}
and initial point
x_{0}\in X
, the DR algorithm for the ordered pair
(\gamma A,\gamma B)
generates a sequence
(x_{n})_{{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}}
according to
x_{n+1}:=T_{\gamma}x_{n}
where
T_{\gamma}
denotes to the
DR operator
defined by

T_{\gamma}:=\operatorname{Id}-J_{\gamma A}+J_{\gamma B}R_{\gamma A}.

(2)

In the standard convergence analysis for the DR algorithm, which is based on an interpretation as a fixed-point iteration, firm nonexpansiveness of
T_{\gamma}
yields the inequality

(\forall{n\in{\mathbb{N}}})(\forall y_{\gamma}\in\operatorname{Fix}T_{\gamma})\>\|x_{n+1}-y_{\gamma}\|^{2}+\|x_{n}-x_{n+1}\|^{2}\leq\|x_{n}-y_{\gamma}\|^{2},

(3)

from which general theory
[
25
]
implies that
(x_{n})_{{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}}
converges weakly to a point
x\in\operatorname{Fix}T_{\gamma}
and that
J_{\gamma A}x
solves (
1
). In (
3
), we emphasise that the fixed-point
y_{\gamma}
depends on the constant
\gamma
. Despite
J_{\gamma A}
not being weakly continuous in general, with some additional work—leveraging on demiclosedness of maximally monotone operators—it can also be shown that
(J_{\gamma A}x_{n})_{{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}}
converges weakly to
J_{\gamma A}x

[
32
]
. Convergence of the method has also been established in various nonmonotone settings
[
4
,
10
,
28
,
16
,
34
]
.

For many resolvent-based iterative methods such as the
proximal point
and the
forward-backward
algorithms, the fixed-point set of the underlying algorithmic operator and the set of solutions coincide. Consequently, the fixed-point set does not depend on algorithm parameters such as the stepsize. This property allows the stepsize to be varied across iterations whilst preserving an inequality in the form of (
3
) and hence the original convergence analysis holds without significant changes. In contrast, this is not the case for the DR algorithm whose fixed-point set
\operatorname{Fix}T_{\gamma}
has a parametric dependence on
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}_{++}
and need not coincide with the solution set
\operatorname{zer}(A+B)
. In fact, due to
[
5
, Proposition 26.1(i) & Proposition 26.1(iii)(b)]
, we have

\operatorname{Fix}T_{\gamma}=\{z+\gamma w:w\in Az,-w\in Bz\}.

(4)

As a consequence of (
4
), the fixed-point sets of the DR operators for different parameters can be completely disjoint (see Remark
4.12
) and thus (
3
) can no longer be used to directly analyse convergence of a variable stepsize version of of DR given by
(T_{\gamma_{n}}^{n}x_{0})_{{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}}
for
(\gamma_{n})
in
\mathbb{R}_{++}
.

Although the stepsize parameter
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}_{++}
in (
2
) does not usually play an important role in the DR algorithm
convergence analysis
itself, its value is critical in methods that extend the DR algorithm to incorporate an additional operator through forward steps such as
Davis-Yin splitting

[
12
,
26
]
and the
forward-reflected-backward methods

[
21
]
. In these methods, the stepsize parameter is typically required to be sufficiently small so that the forward operator satisfies a Lipschitz-type inequality. Furthermore, as these methods reduce to the DR algorithm in special cases, their underlying operators’ fixed-point sets inherit the parametric dependence on the stepsize. As a consequence, linesearches for these methods are not straightforward to develop and analyze. Another research direction where variable stepsize plays an important role is in extending
[
15
]
on the convergence rate of the proximal point algorithm to the DR algorithm.

In order to tackle the challenge of allowing variable stepsizes for the DR algorithm, we develop an extension of the successive approximation scheme examined in
[
25
]
, allowing the use of a sequence
(\gamma_{n})_{{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}}
of variable stepsizes. To do so, we introduce the notion of a
fixed-point relocator
(Definition
4.1
). A key property of these operators is the following: given an operator
T_{\gamma}
parametrized by a positive parameter
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}_{++}
, its fixed-point relocator operator, denoted
Q_{\delta\leftarrow\gamma}
, is a bijection which maps fixed-points of
T_{\gamma}
to fixed-points of
T_{\delta}
for a different parameter
\delta\in\mathbb{R}_{++}
.

Given fixed-point relocators
(Q_{\delta\leftarrow\gamma})_{\gamma,\delta\in\mathbb{R}_{++}}
for
(T_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\mathbb{R}_{++}}
, a stepsize sequence
(\gamma_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
and initial point
x_{0}\in X
, we consider iterations of the form

(\forall{n\in{\mathbb{N}}})\quad x_{n+1}=Q_{\gamma_{n+1}\leftarrow\gamma_{n}}T_{\gamma_{n}}x_{n}.

(5)

We refer to the family of methods defined this way as
relocated fixed-point iterations.

For the DR operator, we show that a fixed-point relocator is given by

(\forall\gamma,\delta\in\mathbb{R}_{++})(\forall x\in X)\;Q_{\delta\leftarrow\gamma}(x)=\tfrac{\delta}{\gamma}x+\big(1-\tfrac{\delta}{\gamma}\big)J_{\gamma A}x.

(6)

The corresponding
relocated DR algorithm
in the form of (
5
) can therefore be expressed as

\left\{\begin{aligned} w_{n}&=x_{n}-J_{\gamma_{n}A}x_{n}+J_{\gamma_{n}B}R_{\gamma_{n}A}x_{n}\\
x_{n+1}&=\tfrac{\gamma_{n+1}}{\gamma_{n}}w_{n}+\left(1-\tfrac{\gamma_{n+1}}{\gamma_{n}}\right)J_{\gamma_{n}A}w_{n}.\end{aligned}\right.

(7)

Note that, in its current form, (
7
) appears to require an extra resolvent evaluation as compared to the standard DR algorithm. However, this can be circumvented using the equivalent implementation described in Algorithm
1
. For further details, see Proposition
5.6
.

Input :
Choose
x_{0}\in X
and a stepsize sequence
(\gamma_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
in
\mathbb{R}_{++}
.

Set
z_{0}=J_{\gamma_{0}A}x_{0}
.

for

n=0,1,2,\dots

do

Step 1. Intermediate step. Compute

\left\{\begin{aligned} y_{n}&=J_{\gamma_{n}B}(2z_{n}-x_{n})\\
w_{n}&=x_{n}-z_{n}+y_{n}.\\
\end{aligned}\right.

(8)

Step 2. Next iterate. Compute

\left\{\begin{aligned} z_{n+1}&=J_{\gamma_{n}A}w_{n}\\
x_{n+1}&=\dfrac{\gamma_{n+1}}{\gamma_{n}}w_{n}+\left(1-\dfrac{\gamma_{n+1}}{\gamma_{n}}\right)z_{n+1}.\end{aligned}\right.

(9)

A+B
.

Several resolvent splitting algorithms with constant stepsizes have been developed to solve monotone inclusions, see
[
18
,
12
,
31
,
22
,
7
]
and references therein. An instance of a method allowing variable stepsizes for splitting methods that only admit resolvent evaluations is proposed in
[
20
]
. Therein, the authors analyze a non-stationary extension of the DR algorithm by modifying the steps that define the DR operator based on a reformulation for the linear case. The main modification happens in the evaluation of the resolvent of

. The authors provide convergence guarantees under assumptions of the behaviour of the sequence of stepsizes covered in our framework (see Remark
4.9
). In the present work, we take a different approach and propose to compose the original DR operator with a suitable fixed-point relocator operator, while keeping the classical steps that define the operator in (
2
). For the stepsize rule defined in
[
20
]
, the non-stationary DR algorithm in
[
20
, Eq. (12)]
and Algorithm
1
coincide (see Remark
4.11
). In both cases, when the sequence of stepsizes is constant, we retrieve the original DR algorithm. The perspective offered by our framework allows for the analysis of various other resolvent splitting algorithms for solving extensions of (
1
) involving
N\geq 2
maximally monotone operators such as
[
31
,
22
]
.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we introduce the notation used throughout the paper as well as recalling mathematical preliminaries for use in our analysis. In Section

[

]
for

6
with some concluding remarks.

2
Preliminaries

We denote the set of nonnegative real numbers by
\mathbb{R}_{+}
and the set of positive real numbers by
\mathbb{R}_{++}
. We say that a sequence of real numbers
(\delta_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is decreasing (resp. increasing)
if
(\forall{n\in{\mathbb{N}}})

\delta_{n}\geq\delta_{n+1}
(resp.
\delta_{n}\leq\delta_{n+1}
). This is also called
“nonincreasing” (resp. “nondecreasing”) in the literature.

Given a real Hilbert space
X
, we use the notation
X^{\text{strong}}
to denote the space
X
endowed with the norm topology. Similarly,
X^{\text{weak}}
denotes
X
endowed with the weak topology. For a nonempty set
D\subseteq X
,
D^{\text{weak}}
denotes the set
D
with the topology induced by
X^{\text{weak}}
. Given a set
C\subseteq X
,
\overline{C}
denotes the closure of
C
in
X^{\text{strong}}
. For
k\geq 1
,
X^{k}:=X\times\dots\times X
denotes the
k
-fold product space.

Given a set-valued operator
A:X\rightrightarrows X
, its
domain
is denoted by
\operatorname{dom}\,A:=\{x\in X:Ax\neq\varnothing\}
, its
graph
by
\operatorname{gra}A:=\{(x,y)\in X\times X:y\in Ax\}
, its set of
zeros
by
\operatorname{zer}A:=\{x\in X:0\in Ax\}
, and its set of
fixed-points
by
\operatorname{Fix}A:=\{x\in X:x\in Ax\}
. We also denote the
inverse
of
A
by
A^{-1}:X\rightrightarrows X
which is defined by
\operatorname{gra}A^{-1}:=\{(y,x)\in X\times X:y\in Ax\}
. We say
A
is
monotone
if

(\forall(x,y),(u,v)\in\operatorname{gra}A)\;\langle x-u,y-v\rangle\geq 0,

and
maximally monotone
if it has no proper monotone extension.

In the study of convergence of iterative algorithms, closure properties of an underlying maximally monotone operator often play a crucial role.

Definition 2.1
(Demiclosedness).

Let
D\subseteq X
be a nonempty weakly sequentially closed set. We say a
set-valued
operator
T:D\rightrightarrows X
is
demiclosed
if
\operatorname{gra}T
is sequentially closed in
D^{\text{weak}}\times X^{\text{strong}}
.

Fact 2.2
(
[
5
, Corollary 20.38(ii)]
).

Let
A
be a maximally monotone operator on
X
. Then
A
is demiclosed.

The sum of two maximally monotone operators need not be maximally monotone in general. However, under some additional assumptions
[
5
]
, the resulting sum operator is maximally monotone. We recall one setting of importance for our analysis in which this statement is true.

Fact 2.3
(
[
5
, Corollary 25.5(i)]
).

Suppose
A
and
B
are maximally monotone operators on
X
, with one of them having full domain. Then
A+B
is maximally monotone.

The convergence framework we develop in this work focuses on a particular type of iteration operators, the so-called
nonexpansive
operators. A single-valued operator
T:X\to X
is said to be:

(i)

nonexpansive
if

(\forall x,y\in X)\;\|Tx-Ty\|\leq\|x-y\|.

(ii)

firmly nonexpansive
if

(\forall x,y\in X)\;\|Tx-Ty\|^{2}+\|(\operatorname{Id}-T)x-(\operatorname{Id}-T)y\|^{2}\leq\|x-y\|^{2}.

(iii)

\alpha
-averaged (nonexpansive)
if
\alpha\in(0,1)
and

(\forall x,y\in X)\;\|Tx-Ty\|^{2}+\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}\|(\operatorname{Id}-T)x-(\operatorname{Id}-T)y\|^{2}\leq\|x-y\|^{2}.

Equivalent to
(iii)
,
T
is
\alpha
-averaged nonexpansive if there exists a nonexpansive operator
R
such that
T=(1-\alpha)\operatorname{Id}+\alpha R
. Thus, in particular, an operator
T
is firmly nonexpansive if and only if
T
is
\frac{1}{2}
-averaged.

Given a nonempty set
C\subseteq X
, the
normal cone
to
C
at
x\in C
is given by
N_{C}(x):=\{v\in C:(\forall y\in C)\;\langle v,y-x\rangle\leq 0\}
, and if

,
N_{C}(x):=\varnothing
. If

is a nonempty closed convex set, then
N_{C}
is maximally monotone. The

operator onto

is

.

3
Properties of resolvents and nonexpansive operators

In this section, we derive a number of new properties of resolvents and explore their consequences for the Douglas–Rachford operator. The insights of this section will form the basis and motivating examples for the framework developed in the subsequent sections.

3.1
Properties of resolvents

In this section, we first present a resolvent rule that yields a bijection between fixed-point sets of the DR operator as a function of the stepsize. This rule allows us to define an operator that transforms fixed-points of the DR operator as a function of stepsize. We also establish continuity properties of the map
(x,\gamma)\mapsto J_{\gamma A}x
for a given maximally monotone operator
A
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
A

X
,

J_{\beta A}\Big(\tfrac{\beta}{\alpha}\operatorname{Id}+\big(1-\tfrac{\beta}{\alpha}\big)J_{\alpha A}\Big)=J_{\alpha A}

\tfrac{\alpha}{\beta}\operatorname{Id}+\big(1-\tfrac{\alpha}{\beta}\big)J_{\beta A}\colon X\to X\text{ is a bijection with inverse }\tfrac{\beta}{\alpha}\operatorname{Id}+\big(1-\tfrac{\beta}{\alpha}\big)J_{\alpha A}.

(12)

Lemma

provides a bijective map useful for computing resolvents with stepsizes only requiring linear algebra operations and the knowledge of the resolvent for one fixed parameter. In the next result, we study further properties of this bijection.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
A
be maximally monotone on
X
, and let
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}_{++}
.
Consider the operator defined by

Q_{\beta\leftarrow\alpha}:=\tfrac{\beta}{\alpha}\operatorname{Id}+\big(1-\tfrac{\beta}{\alpha}\big)J_{\alpha A}.

Then:

(i)

If
\beta<\alpha
, then
Q_{\beta\leftarrow\alpha}

is
(\tfrac{1}{2}-\tfrac{\beta}{2\alpha})
-averaged and hence firmly nonexpansive.

(ii)

If
\beta\geq\alpha
, then

Q_{\beta\leftarrow\alpha}
is monotone,

\tfrac{\beta}{\alpha}
-Lipschitz continuous, and

(\forall z,\bar{z}\in X)\quad\|Q_{\beta\leftarrow\alpha}z-Q_{\beta\leftarrow\alpha}\bar{z}\|^{2}+\bigl(\frac{\beta^{2}}{\alpha^{2}}-1\bigr)\|J_{\alpha A}z-J_{\alpha A}\bar{z}\|^{2}\leq\frac{\beta^{2}}{\alpha^{2}}\|z-\bar{z}\|^{2}.

(13)

(i)
Since
J_{\alpha A}
is firmly nonexpansive from Fact
2.4
(i)
and
0<1-\frac{\beta}{\alpha}<1<2
, the result follows from Fact

(ii)
with
\alpha=1/2
and
\lambda=1-\beta/\alpha
.

(ii)
Assume
\beta\geq\alpha
and let

. Since
J_{\alpha A}
is firmly nonexpansive, we have

\|J_{\alpha A}z-J_{\alpha A}\bar{z}\|^{2}\leq\langle z-\bar{z},J_{\alpha A}z-J_{\alpha A}\bar{z}\rangle.

Using this inequality and noting that
1-\frac{\beta}{\alpha}\leq 0
, we deduce

\displaystyle\|Q_{\beta\leftarrow\alpha}z-Q_{\beta\leftarrow\alpha}\bar{z}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\|\frac{\beta}{\alpha}(z-\bar{z})+\left(1-\frac{\beta}{\alpha}\right)(J_{\alpha A}z-J_{\alpha A}\bar{z})\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\beta^{2}}{\alpha^{2}}\|z-\bar{z}\|^{2}+\left(1-\frac{\beta}{\alpha}\right)^{2}\|J_{\alpha A}z-J_{\alpha A}\bar{z}\|^{2}+2\frac{\beta}{\alpha}\left(1-\frac{\beta}{\alpha}\right)\langle z-\bar{z},J_{\alpha A}z-J_{\alpha A}\bar{z}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\beta^{2}}{\alpha^{2}}\|z-\bar{z}\|^{2}+\left(1-\frac{\beta}{\alpha}\right)^{2}\|J_{\alpha A}z-J_{\alpha A}\bar{z}\|^{2}+2\frac{\beta}{\alpha}\left(1-\frac{\beta}{\alpha}\right)\|J_{\alpha A}z-J_{\alpha A}\bar{z}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\beta^{2}}{\alpha^{2}}\|z-\bar{z}\|^{2}+\left(1-\frac{\beta^{2}}{\alpha^{2}}\right)\|J_{\alpha A}z-J_{\alpha A}\bar{z}\|^{2},

which yields (
13
), and hence
\tfrac{\beta}{\alpha}
-Lipschitz continuity of
Q_{\beta\leftarrow\alpha}
. To see that

is monotone, note the representation
Q_{\beta\leftarrow\alpha}=\frac{\beta}{\alpha}(\operatorname{Id}-J_{\alpha A})+J_{\alpha A}
. Since both
\operatorname{Id}-J_{\alpha A}
and
J_{\alpha A}
are monotone in view of Fact
2.4
(i)
and
\frac{\beta}{\alpha}\in\mathbb{R}_{++}
, monotonicity of

follows.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

The Lipschitz bound in Proposition

(ii)

is sharp. Indeed, taking

yields

which is
\tfrac{\beta}{\alpha}
-Lipschitz continuous.

Note that, due to (

in Proposition

This identity will be used to propose a variable stepsize extension of the Douglas–Rachford algorithm. In order to analyze its convergence, we will also need the following continuity properties of the resolvent
J_{\gamma A}x
as a function of both
x
and
\gamma
, which were considered in
[
14
, Corollary 7]
in the context of convex subdifferentials in finite dimensional Euclidean spaces.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
A
be maximally monotone on
X
, and define
S_{A}\colon X\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\to X
according to

S_{A}(x,\gamma):=\begin{cases}J_{\gamma A}x&\gamma\in\mathbb{R}_{++},\\
P_{\overline{\operatorname{dom}\,A}}x&\gamma=0.\\
\end{cases}

Then
S_{A}
is continuous on
X\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
and Lipschitz continuous on compact subsets of
X\times\mathbb{R}_{++}
.

We first show that
S_{A}
is continuous on
X\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
. To this end, let
(x,\lambda)\in X\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
and suppose
(y,\gamma)\to(x,\lambda)
. First, assume
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{++}
. Using Facts
2.4
(iii)
and
(i)
, we deduce that

\displaystyle\|S_{A}(x,\lambda)-S_{A}(y,\gamma)\|

\displaystyle\leq\|J_{\lambda A}x-J_{\gamma A}x\|+\|J_{\gamma A}x-J_{\gamma A}y\|

(14)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\|J_{\lambda A}x-x\|}{\lambda}|\lambda-\gamma|+\|x-y\|\to 0.

Next, assume
\lambda=0
. If
\gamma>0
, using Fact
2.4
(i)
, we deduce

\displaystyle\|S_{A}(x,0)-S_{A}(y,\gamma)\|

\displaystyle\leq\|P_{\overline{\operatorname{dom}\,A}}x-J_{\gamma A}x\|+\|J_{\gamma A}x-J_{\gamma A}y\|

\displaystyle\leq\|P_{\overline{\operatorname{dom}\,A}}x-J_{\gamma A}x\|+\|x-y\|.

And, if
\gamma=0
, Fact
2.4
(i)
gives

\|S_{A}(x,0)-S_{A}(y,0)\|\leq\|P_{\overline{\operatorname{dom}\,A}}x-P_{\overline{\operatorname{dom}\,A}}y\|\leq\|x-y\|.
Combining these two subcases and noting Fact
2.4
(iv)
, we deduce that
\|S_{A}(x,0)-S_{A}(y,\gamma)\|\to 0
.
Thus, in all cases,
\lim_{(y,\lambda)\to(x,\gamma)}S_{A}(y,\lambda)=S_{A}(x,\gamma)
which establishes continuity of

on

.

To show that

is Lipschitz continuous on compact subsets of
X\times\mathbb{R}_{++}

be compact. Since
S_{A}
is continuous and the continuous image of compact sets is compact, we have

Then, using (

), we obtain
(\forall(x,\lambda),(y,\gamma)\in U)

3.2
Properties of the Douglas–Rachford operator

In this section, we explore a number of consequences of the resolvent properties derived in the previous section for the Douglas–Rachford operator
T_{\gamma}:=\operatorname{Id}-J_{\gamma A}+J_{\gamma B}R_{\gamma A}
. First, we show that the bijective map of Lemma

also defines a bijection between fixed-point sets of the DR operator for different stepsize parameters, and show continuity properties of the operator

.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
A,B
be maximally monotone on
X
,
T_{\gamma}
be the DR operator defined in (
2
), and
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}_{++}
. Then

\Big(\tfrac{\beta}{\alpha}\operatorname{Id}+\big(1-\tfrac{\beta}{\alpha}\big)J_{\alpha A}\Big)\Big|_{\operatorname{Fix}T_{\alpha}}\colon\operatorname{Fix}T_{\alpha}\to\operatorname{Fix}T_{\beta}\text{ is a bijection, with inverse }\Big(\tfrac{\alpha}{\beta}\operatorname{Id}+\big(1-\tfrac{\alpha}{\beta}\big)J_{\beta A}\Big)\Big|_{\operatorname{Fix}T_{\beta}}.

Let
x\in\operatorname{Fix}T_{\alpha}
.
Then

z:=J_{\alpha A}x=J_{\alpha B}R_{\alpha A}x\in\operatorname{zer}(A+B)\;\text{ and }\;R_{\alpha A}x=2z-x.

Now set

y:=\tfrac{\beta}{\alpha}x+\big(1-\tfrac{\beta}{\alpha}\big)J_{\alpha A}x=\tfrac{\beta}{\alpha}x+\big(1-\tfrac{\beta}{\alpha}\big)z.

Using (
10
) thrice, we deduce that
J_{\beta A}y=J_{\alpha A}x=z
and

\displaystyle J_{\beta A}y

\displaystyle=J_{\beta A}\Big(\tfrac{\beta}{\alpha}\operatorname{Id}+\big(1-\tfrac{\beta}{\alpha}\big)J_{\alpha A}\Big)x=J_{\alpha A}x=z=J_{\alpha B}R_{\alpha A}x=J_{\alpha B}(2z-x)

\displaystyle=J_{\beta B}\Big(\tfrac{\beta}{\alpha}(2z-x)+\big(1-\tfrac{\beta}{\alpha}\big)J_{\alpha B}(2z-x)\Big)=J_{\beta B}\Big(\tfrac{\beta}{\alpha}(2z-x)+\big(1-\tfrac{\beta}{\alpha}\big)z\Big)

\displaystyle=J_{\beta B}\Big(2z-\big(1-\tfrac{\beta}{\alpha}\big)z-\tfrac{\beta}{\alpha}x\Big)=J_{\beta B}(2z-y)=J_{\beta B}R_{\beta A}y.

Hence
z=J_{\beta A}y=J_{\beta B}R_{\beta A}y

and so

.
The conclusion now follows from Lemma
3.1
.
∎

Combining the previous theorem with the continuity result in Proposition

.

Let

and

Since

∎

3.3
Parametric demiclosedness principle

As mentioned in the introduction, resolvents — and thus in particular the DR iteration operator in (
2
) — are not necessarily weakly continuous. Browder’s celebrated
demiclosedness princple

[
8
]
provides an alternative to assuming weak continuity of the operator in infinite-dimensional spaces. In this section, we investigate a parametric version of the demiclosedness principle. We start by recalling the principle as it is presented in
[
5
]
.

Fact 3.7
(Demiclosedness principle
[
5
, Theorem 4.27]
).

Let
D\subseteq X
be a nonempty sequentially closed set, and let
T:D\mapsto X
be a nonexpansive operator. Then
\operatorname{Id}-T
is demiclosed.

In their current forms, neither Definition
2.1
or Fact
3.7
apply to the Douglas–Rachford operator as a function of its stepsize. This motivates the following definition.

Definition 3.8
(Parametric demiclosedess).

Let
D\subseteq X
be a nonempty sequentially closed set, and
\Gamma\subseteq\mathbb{R}
be a nonempty set. We say an operator
G:D\times\Gamma\to X
is
parametrically demiclosed
if
\operatorname{gra}G
is sequentially closed in
(X^{\text{weak}}\times\Gamma)\times X^{\text{strong}}
.

The following result establishes a parametric generalisation of Fact
3.7
.

Theorem 3.9
(Parametric demiclosedness principle).

Let
D\subseteq X
be a nonempty weakly sequentially closed set,
\Gamma\subseteq\mathbb{R}
be a nonempty closed set, and let
(T_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
be a family of nonexpansive operators from
D
to
X
, such that
(\forall x\in D)\;\gamma\mapsto T_{\gamma}x
is (strongly) continuous. Then the operator

\displaystyle D\times\Gamma

\displaystyle\to X\colon(x,\gamma)\mapsto(\operatorname{Id}-T_{\gamma})x

is parametrically demiclosed.

Let
(x_{n},\gamma_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
be a sequence in
D\times\Gamma
such that
x_{n}\>{\rightharpoonup}\>x\in D
,
\gamma_{n}\to\overline{\gamma}\in\Gamma
, and
x_{n}-T_{\gamma_{n}}x_{n}=(\operatorname{Id}-T_{\gamma_{n}})x_{n}\to u
. We shall prove that
(\operatorname{Id}-T_{\overline{\gamma}})x=u
. Indeed, the continuity of
\gamma\mapsto T_{\gamma}x
yields
T_{\overline{\gamma}}x-T_{\gamma_{n}}x\to 0
. Thus, using the nonexpansiveness of
T_{\gamma_{n}}
and the reverse triangle inequality, we have

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\|x_{n}-x\|

\displaystyle\geq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\|T_{\gamma_{n}}x_{n}-T_{\gamma_{n}}x\|

\displaystyle=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\|(x_{n}-T_{\overline{\gamma}}x-u)+(u+T_{\gamma_{n}}x_{n}-x_{n})+(T_{\overline{\gamma}}x-T_{\gamma_{n}}x)\|

\displaystyle\geq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\|x_{n}-T_{\overline{\gamma}}x-u\|-\|u+T_{\gamma_{n}}x_{n}-x_{n}\|-\|T_{\overline{\gamma}}x-T_{\gamma_{n}}x\|

\displaystyle=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\|x_{n}-T_{\overline{\gamma}}x-u\|-\lim_{n\to\infty}\|u+T_{\gamma_{n}}x_{n}-x_{n}\|-\lim_{n\to\infty}\|T_{\overline{\gamma}}x-T_{\gamma_{n}}x\|

\displaystyle=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\|x_{n}-T_{\overline{\gamma}}x-u\|.

In view of
[
25
, Lemma 1]
, we must have
x=T_{\overline{\gamma}}x+u
, or equivalently,
(\operatorname{Id}-T_{\overline{\gamma}})x=u
, which completes the proof.
∎

Remark 3.10
.

When
\Gamma
is a singleton, parametric demiclosedness coincides and standard demiclosedness coincide. Consequently, Theorem
3.9
recovers Fact
3.7
.

4
Relocated fixed-point iterations

In this section, we propose a framework for sequences constructed as fixed-point iterations of a parametrized family of operators
(T_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
composed with operators
(Q_{\delta\leftarrow\gamma})_{\gamma,\delta\in\Gamma}
that relocate fixed-point sets. This setting covers resolvent-based algorithms with variable stepsizes, such as the DR algorithm, as discussed by the end of this section.

We show that given a parametric family of nonexpansive operators
(T_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
and a sequence of stepsizes
(\gamma_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
converging to some
\overline{\gamma}
, under appropriate assumptions, the corresponding relocated fixed-point iteration generates a sequence that converges weakly to a fixed-point of
T_{\overline{\gamma}}
.

4.1
Convergence of relocated fixed-point iterations

We start by defining the concept of fixed-point relocator.

Definition 4.1
(Fixed-point relocator).

Let
\Gamma\subseteq\mathbb{R}_{++}
be a nonempty set, and let
(T_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
be a family of operators from
X
to
X
. We say that the family of operators
(Q_{\delta\leftarrow\gamma})_{\gamma,\delta\in\Gamma}
on
X
defines
fixed-point relocators
for
(T_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
with Lipschitz constants
(\mathcal{L}_{\delta\leftarrow\gamma})_{\gamma,\delta\in\Gamma}
in
[1,+\infty[
if the following hold.

(i)

(\forall\gamma,\delta\in\Gamma)

Q_{\delta\leftarrow\gamma}|_{\operatorname{Fix}T_{\gamma}}
is a bijection from
\operatorname{Fix}T_{\gamma}
to
\operatorname{Fix}T_{\delta}
.

(ii)

(\forall\gamma\in\Gamma)(\forall x\in\operatorname{Fix}T_{\gamma})

\Gamma\to X\colon\delta\mapsto Q_{\delta\leftarrow\gamma}x
is continuous.

(iii)

(\forall\gamma,\delta,\varepsilon\in\Gamma)(\forall x\in\operatorname{Fix}T_{\gamma})

Q_{\varepsilon\leftarrow\delta}Q_{\delta\leftarrow\gamma}x=Q_{\varepsilon\leftarrow\gamma}x
.

(iv)

(\forall\gamma,\delta\in\Gamma)

Q_{\delta\leftarrow\gamma}
is
\mathcal{L}_{\delta\leftarrow\gamma}
-Lipschitz continuous.

Remark 4.2
(Fixed-point relocators outside the fixed-point set).

Given fixed-point relocators
(Q_{\delta\leftarrow\gamma})_{\gamma,\delta\in\mathbb{R}_{++}}
for
(T_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\mathbb{R}_{++}}
, observe that Definition
4.1
(i)
–
(iii)
only need to hold on
\operatorname{Fix}T_{\gamma}
. Therefore, a fixed-point relocator does not have to be unique. In fact, any Lipschitz operator
\tilde{Q}_{\delta\leftarrow\gamma}
such that
Q_{\delta\leftarrow\gamma}|_{\operatorname{Fix}T_{\gamma}}=\tilde{Q}_{\delta\leftarrow\gamma}|_{\operatorname{Fix}T_{\gamma}}
also defines a fixed-point relocator for
(T_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\mathbb{R}_{++}}
. We give an example of a such a case in Proposition
5.5
.

Within the context of Definition
4.1
, our goal is to analyze the sequence of successive approximations
(T_{\gamma}x)_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
, for a given
x\in X
, transformed by a fixed-point relocator. In order to do that, we consider the following assumption.

Assumption 4.3
.

Consider a nonempty set
\Gamma\subseteq\mathbb{R}_{++}
, and
(T_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
a family of nonexpansive operators on
X
such that

(i)

(\exists\,\alpha\in\left]0,1\right[)(\forall\gamma\in\Gamma)

T_{\gamma}
is
\alpha
-averaged.

(ii)

X\times\Gamma\to X\colon(x,\gamma)\mapsto T_{\gamma}x
is continuous.

Note that an instance of operators
T_{\gamma}
in Definition
4.1
satisfying Assumption
4.3
is the DR operator in (
2
), in view of Corollary
3.6
.

We are now ready for our main result.

Theorem 4.4
(Convergence of relocated fixed-point iterations).

Let
\Gamma\subseteq\mathbb{R}_{++}
be nonempty,
(T_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
be a family of nonexpansive operators on
X

(\forall\gamma\in\Gamma)\;\operatorname{Fix}T_{\gamma}\neq\varnothing
, and
(Q_{\delta\leftarrow\gamma})_{\gamma,\delta\in\Gamma}
on

be fixed-point relocators for

(15)

Let

Then the following hold.

(i)

(ii)

(iii)

4.2
Variable stepsize DR algorithm

In this section, we establish convergence of Algorithm

Lemma 4.6

(\forall\gamma,\delta\in\Gamma)

Assertions

\displaystyle Q_{\varepsilon\leftarrow\delta}Q_{\delta\leftarrow\gamma}

\displaystyle=\Big(\tfrac{\varepsilon}{\delta}\operatorname{Id}+\big(1-\tfrac{\varepsilon}{\delta}\big)J_{\delta A}\Big)\Big(\tfrac{\delta}{\gamma}\operatorname{Id}+\big(1-\tfrac{\delta}{\gamma}\big)J_{\gamma A}\Big)

Remark 4.9
(On the stepsizes assumptions).

In the following, we comment on assumption (
17
).

(i)

The assumption
\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}(\gamma_{n+1}-\gamma_{n})_{+}<+\infty
is not enough to guarantee convergence of
(\gamma_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
. Indeed, let

(\forall{n\in{\mathbb{N}}})\;a_{n}:=\gamma_{n+1}-\gamma_{n}.

(18)

Then,
(\forall{n\in{\mathbb{N}}})\;\gamma_{n+1}=\gamma_{1}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}.
Define
a_{n}=(\tfrac{1}{2})^{n}\text{~if~}n\text{~is odd}
and
a_{n}=-1\text{~otherwise.}

Then
(\forall{n\in{\mathbb{N}}})\;(a_{n})_{+}=(\tfrac{1}{2})^{n}\text{~if~}n\text{~is odd}
and
(a_{n})_{+}=0\text{~~otherwise,}

and
\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}(a_{n})_{+}<+\infty
, but
(\gamma_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
does not converge.

(ii)

Assumption (
17
) is equivalent to the stepsize condition in
[
20
, Theorem 3.2]
, namely,

(\exists\gamma_{\text{low}},\gamma_{\text{up}}\in\mathbb{R}_{++})\;\gamma_{\text{low}}\leq\gamma_{n}\leq\gamma_{\text{up}}\text{~~and~}\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}|\gamma_{n+1}-\gamma_{n}|<+\infty.

(19)

In fact, let (
19
) hold. Clearly
\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}(\gamma_{n+1}-\gamma_{n})_{+}\leq\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}|\gamma_{n+1}-\gamma_{n}|
. Since (
19
) implies
(\gamma_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is a Cauchy sequence, then the limit
\overline{\gamma}
is bounded below by
\gamma_{\text{low}}\in\mathbb{R}_{++}
, and thus (
17
) holds. Conversely, suppose (
17
) holds. Let
(a_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
be as in (
18
). Note that
(\forall{n\in{\mathbb{N}}})

(a_{n})_{+}=\frac{a_{n}+|a_{n}|}{2}
and
\sum_{k=0}^{n}a_{k}=\gamma_{n+1}-\gamma_{0}
. Then
2\sum_{k=0}^{n}(a_{k})_{+}=\gamma_{n+1}-\gamma_{0}+\sum_{k=0}^{n}|a_{k}|
. In view of (
17
),
\gamma_{\text{low}}\leq\gamma_{n+1}=\gamma_{0}+2\sum_{k=0}^{n}(a_{k})_{+}-\sum_{k=0}^{n}|a_{k}|
, which in turn implies
\sum_{k=0}^{n}|a_{k}|\leq\gamma_{0}-\gamma_{\text{low}}+2\sum_{k=0}^{n}(a_{k})_{+}
. Taking the limit as
n\to+\infty
, (
17
) implies
\sum_{{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}}|\gamma_{n+1}-\gamma_{n}|=\sum_{{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}}|a_{n}|\leq\gamma_{0}-\gamma_{\text{low}}+2\sum_{{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}}(\gamma_{n+1}-\gamma_{n})_{+}<+\infty
. Therefore
(\gamma_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
converges and
\gamma_{\text{up}}:=\sup_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\gamma<+\infty
, yielding (
19
).

(iii)

A sequence
(\gamma_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
satisfying (
17
) can be easily found, for instance, any bounded decreasing sequence of positive real numbers. Furthermore, the construction in (
18
) reveals that increasing sequences of stepsizes
(\gamma_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
can also be included in our analysis, as long as
\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}a_{n}
converges. Another example is due to
(ii)
, as it allows to use the stepsize rules defined in
[
20
, Section 4]
. A similar stepsize rule is used in
[
19
]
.

Remark 4.12

Let us stress that Theorem

5
Resolvent splitting methods with variable stepsize

In this section, we generalize our analysis in Section
4.2
, and develop a variable stepsize resolvent splitting framework to solve (maximally) monotone inclusions of the form

\text{find~~}x\in X\text{~~such that~~}0\in\sum_{i=1}^{N}A_{i}(x),

[

]

—and then leverage this result to deduce variable stepsize extensions of some known resolvent splitting algorithms.

5.1

The resolvent splitting methods we analyze in this section are constructed using directed graphs, in which each node belongs to one of the operators in (

[
7
]
to define the setting.

Let

and

denotes the

6
Final remarks

In this work, we proposed the relocated fixed-point iteration framework for a parametrized family of nonexpansive operators. An instance of this setting is the graph-based extension of the DR splitting algorithm, in which the parameter is the stepsize, yielding convergence of variable stepsize resolvent splitting methods.

Among future research directions, the authors plan to investigate other splitting methods under the lenses of the relocated fixed-point iteration framework. In particular, we are interested in exploring three-operator splitting methods that allow forward and resolvent evaluations such as
[
12
]
, and the extensions examined in
[
11
,
2
,
1
]
. We also plan to investigate implementations of the stepsize sequence
(\gamma_{n})_{{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}}
beyond Remark
4.9
(iii)
, and examine rates of convergence of resolvent splitting methods with variable stepsizes, similar to the approach developed in
[
15
]
for the proximal point algorithm.

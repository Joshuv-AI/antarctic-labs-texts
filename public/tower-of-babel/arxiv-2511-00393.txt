Abstract
. In this paper, we obtain a sharp Garliardo-Nirenberg inequality on integer lattices and characterize its rigidity. Moreover, as a consequence of the sharp Garliardo-Nirenberg inequality, we obtain sharp logarithmic Sobolev inequalities on integer lattices.

Primary 39A12; Secondary 05C50

1.
Introduction

In discrete geometric analysis, geometric inequalities such as the Sobolev inequality and the isoperimetric inequality play important roles. For examples, in the recent works
[
6
,
7
]
, the authors considered the existence of extremal functions for Sobolev inequalities on
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. In
[
15
]
, the author considered Sobolev inequalities on general weighted graphs. In
[
8
,
9
,
12
]
, the authors considered logarithmic Sobolev inequalities on graphs with some curvature assumptions.

In this paper, we obtain the following discrete version of the Garliardo-Nirenberg inequality (see
[
5
,
3
,
13
,
14
]
) on
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, and characterize its rigidity.

Theorem 1.1
.

For any
f\in C_{0}(\mathbb{Z}^{n})
with
n\geq 2
,

(1.1)

\|f\|_{\frac{n}{n-1}}\leq\frac{1}{2}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\|\partial_{i}f\|_{1}^{\frac{1}{n}}.

Moreover, the equality holds if and only if
f=\lambda\cdot\chi_{A}
where
A
is a cuboid in
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.

By applying the AM-GM inequality to the RHS of the sharp Garliardo-Nirenberg inequality (
1.1
), one obtains the following sharp Sobolev inequality.

Corollary 1.1
.

For any
f\in C_{0}(\mathbb{Z}^{n})
with
n\geq 2
,

(1.2)

\|f\|_{\frac{n}{n-1}}\leq\frac{1}{2n}\|df\|_{1}.

Moreover, the equality holds if and only if
f=\lambda\cdot\chi_{Q}
where
Q
is a cube in
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.

In (
1.2
), letting
f=\chi_{A}
for any finite set
A\subset\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, one obtains the sharp isoperimentric inequality in
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
:

(1.3)

|A|^{n-1}\leq\frac{1}{2^{n}n^{n}}|\partial A|^{n}

with that the equality holds if and only if
A
is a cube in
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. The sharp isoperimetric inequality (
1.3
) can be also found in
[
11
]
which was proved by using the discrete part of the Loomis-Whitney inequality
[
10
]
. Moreover, it is well known that (
1.2
) and (
1.3
) are equivalent (see
[
14
]
).

On the other hand, as another consequence the sharp Garliardo-Nirenberg inequality (
1.1
), we have the following sharp logarithmic Sobolev inequalities on
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
n\geq 2
,
p>0
and
f\in C_{0}(\mathbb{Z}^{n})
be nonnegative with
\|f\|_{p}=1
. Then

(1.4)

\left(\frac{1}{n}+\frac{1}{p}-1\right)\int_{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}f^{p}\log f^{p}dz_{1}dz_{2}\cdots dz_{n}\leq-\log 2+\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\log\|\partial_{i}f\|_{1}

with that the equality holds if and only if
f=|A|^{-\frac{1}{p}}\cdot\chi_{A}
for some cuboid
A
in
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
n\geq 2
,
p>0
and
f\in C_{0}(\mathbb{Z}^{n})
be nonnegative with
\|f\|_{p}=1
. Then

(1.5)

\left(\frac{1}{n}+\frac{1}{p}-1\right)\int_{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}f^{p}\log f^{p}dz_{1}dz_{2}\cdots dz_{n}\leq\log\left(\frac{1}{2n}\|df\|_{1}\right)

with that the equality holds if and only if
f=|Q|^{-\frac{1}{p}}\chi_{Q}
for some cube
Q
in
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.

In the logarithmic Sobolev inequalities (
1.4
) and (
1.5
), if we let
p=\frac{n-1}{n}
, then we recover the Garliardo-Nirenberg inequality (
1.1
) and the sharp Sobolev inequality (
1.2
) respectively. Moreover, if we let
p=1
in (
1.5
), then (
1.5
) is exactly the discrete version of the
L^{1}
logarithmic Sobolev inequality on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
(see
[
4
]
).

Motivated by the work
[
1
]
, we prove Theorem
1.1
by first proving the following discrete Brascamp-Lieb inequality and characterizing its rigidity.

Theorem 1.2
.

For any nonnegative
f\in C_{0}(\mathbb{Z}^{n})
with
n\geq 2
, let

f_{i}(z_{1},z_{2},\cdots,\widehat{z_{i}},\cdots,z_{n})=\max_{z_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}}f(z_{1},z_{2},\cdots,z_{i},\cdots,z_{n}).

Here
\widehat{z_{i}}
means that
z_{i}
is not appeared in the expression. Then,

(1.6)

\|f\|_{\frac{n}{n-1}}\leq\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n}\|f_{i}\|_{1}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}.

Moreover, the equality of (
1.6
) holds if and only if
f=\lambda\cdot\chi_{A}
with
A=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\pi_{i}(A)
and
\lambda>0
.

As a direct consequence of the discrete Brascamp-Lieb inequality (
1.6
), we have the following logarithmic-type Brascamp-Lieb inequality.

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
n\geq 2
,
p>0
, and
f\in C_{0}(\mathbb{Z}^{n})
be nonnegative with
\|f\|_{p}=1
.
Then,

(1.7)

\left(\frac{1}{n}+\frac{1}{p}-1\right)\int_{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}f^{p}\log f^{p}dz_{1}dz_{2}\cdots dz_{n}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\log\|f_{i}\|_{1}.

Moreover, the equality of (
1.7
) holds if and only if
f=|A|^{-\frac{1}{p}}\cdot\chi_{A}
with
A=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\pi_{i}(A)
.

The same as before, when letting
p=\frac{n}{n-1}
in (
1.7
), we recover (
1.6
). Moreover, when letting
f=\chi_{A}
with
A\subset\mathbb{Z}^{n}
in (
1.6
), we obtain the discrete part of the Loomis-Whitney inequality:

(1.8)

|A|^{n-1}\leq\prod_{i=1}^{n}\pi_{i}(A)

for any finite subset
A
in
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
with that the equality holds if and only if
A=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\pi_{i}(A)
. Finally, we would like to mention that the discrete Brascamp-Lieb inequality has been extensively studied in
[
2
]
.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we give some preliminaries for analysis on
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. In Section 3, we prove the main results of the paper.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we fix some notations for analysis on
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.

We consider
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
as a graph with the set of vertices
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
and with the set of edges

E(\mathbb{Z}^{n})=\left\{\{z,z+e_{i}\}\ |\ z\in\mathbb{Z}^{n},\ i=1,2,\cdots,n\right\}.

Here
e_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
is a vector with the
i^{\rm th}
component being
1
and the other components vanished for
i=1,2,\cdots,n
.

We denote
\pi_{i}:\mathbb{Z}^{n}\to\mathbb{Z}
the natural projection sending
(z_{1},z_{2},\cdots,z_{n})
to
z_{i}
. The counting measure on
\mathbb{Z}
is denoted as
dz
and its product on
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
is denoted as
dz_{1}dz_{2}\cdots dz_{n}
which is also the counting measure on
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. We denote the space of functions on
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
as
C(\mathbb{Z}^{n})
and the space of functions on
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
with finite support as
C_{0}(\mathbb{Z}^{n})
.

For any
p>0
and
f\in C_{0}(\mathbb{Z}^{n})
, define

\|f\|_{p}:=\left(\int_{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}|f|^{p}dz_{1}dz_{2}\cdots dz_{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

For
i=1,2,\cdots,n
and
f\in C(\mathbb{Z}^{n})
, define

\partial_{i}f(z):=f(z+e_{i})-f(z),\ \forall\ z\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}.

Moreover, on a graph
G=(V,E)
, a 1-form
\alpha
is a bivariate asymmetric function on
V
such that

\alpha(x,y)=0

if
\{x,y\}\not\in E
. The space of 1-forms on
G
is denoted as
A^{1}(G)
. The differential of a function
f\in\mathbb{R}^{V}
is defined as

df(x,y):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}f(y)-f(x)&\{x,y\}\in E\\
0&\mbox{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

The space of 1-forms with finite support on
G
is denoted as
A^{1}_{0}(G)
. For
p>0
and
\alpha\in A_{0}^{1}(G)
, define

\|\alpha\|_{p}:=\left(\sum_{\{x,y\}\in E}|\alpha(x,y)|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

It is clear by definition that

\|df\|_{p}=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|\partial_{i}f\|_{p}^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

for all
p>0
and
f\in C_{0}(\mathbb{Z}^{n})
. For any subset
A
of
V
, we define

\partial A=\{\{x,y\}\in E\ |\ x\in A,\ y\in A^{c}\}.

Finally, for
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}
with
a\leq b
, we denote

[a,b]=\{z\in\mathbb{Z}\ |\ a\leq z\leq b\}.

A set

A=\prod_{i=1}^{n}[a_{i},b_{i}]\subset\mathbb{Z}^{n}

is called a cuboid in
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. If moreover,
|b_{i}-a_{i}|
is independent of
i
, we call
A
a cube in
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.

3.
Proofs of main results

In this section, we prove the main results of the paper. We first prove Theorem
1.2
, a discrete version of the Brascamp-Lieb inequality, by induction.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

We proceed by induction. When
n=2
, by that

f(z_{1},z_{2})\leq f_{1}(z_{2})\mbox{ and }f(z_{1},z_{2})\leq f_{2}(z_{1}),

we have

(3.1)

f^{2}(z_{1},z_{2})\leq f_{1}(z_{2})f_{2}(z_{1}).

Thus

\int_{\mathbb{Z}^{2}}f^{2}(z_{1},z_{2})dz_{1}dz_{2}\leq\int_{\mathbb{Z}^{2}}f_{1}(z_{2})f_{2}(z_{1})dz_{1}dz_{2}=\int_{\mathbb{Z}}f_{1}(z_{2})dz_{2}\cdot\int_{\mathbb{Z}}f_{2}(z_{1})dz_{1}.

This gives us (
1.6
) for
n=2
. If the equality of
(
1.6
)
holds for
n=2
. Then, the equality of (
3.1
) holds for all
(z_{1},z_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. So, when
f(z_{1},z_{2})>0
, we have

(3.2)

f(z_{1},z_{2})=f_{1}(z_{2})=f_{2}(z_{1}).

When
f(z_{1},z_{2})=0
, we have

(3.3)

f_{1}(z_{2})=0\mbox{ or }f_{2}(z_{1})=0

which means that

(3.4)

f_{1}(\zeta,z_{2})=0\ (\forall\ \zeta\in\mathbb{Z})\mbox{ or }f_{2}(z_{1},\zeta)=0\ (\forall\ \zeta\in\mathbb{Z}).

If
f\equiv 0
, there is nothing to be proved. When
f\not\equiv 0
, let
(z_{1},z_{2}),(w_{1},w_{2})\in\mbox{supp}f
, by (
3.4
), we know that
f(z_{1},w_{2})>0
. Moreover by (
3.2
), we have

f(z_{1},z_{2})=f(z_{1},w_{2})=f(w_{1},w_{2}).

So,
f=\lambda\cdot\chi_{A}
with
A=\mbox{supp}f
and

|A|=|\pi_{1}(A)|\cdot|\pi_{2}(A)|.

Because
A\subset\pi_{1}(A)\times\pi_{2}(A)
, we have
A=\pi_{1}(A)\times\pi_{2}(A)
. This proves the conclusion of the theorem for
n=2
.

Suppose that the conclusion of the theorem is true for some
n\geq 2
. Then, for any
nonnegative
f\in C_{0}(\mathbb{Z}^{n+1})
,

(3.5)

\begin{split}&\left(\int_{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}f^{\frac{n}{n-1}}(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n},z_{n+1})dz_{1}\cdots dz_{n}\right)^{n-1}\\
\leq&\prod_{i=1}^{n}\int_{\mathbb{Z}^{n-1}}f_{i}(z_{1},\cdots,\widehat{z}_{i},\cdots,z_{n},z_{n+1})dz_{1}\cdots\widehat{dz_{i}}\cdots dz_{n}.\end{split}

Moreover, note that

(3.6)

\begin{split}&\int_{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}f(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n},z_{n+1})dz_{1}dz_{2}\cdots dz_{n}\\
\leq&\int_{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}f_{n+1}(z_{1},z_{2},\cdots,z_{n})dz_{1}dz_{2}\cdots dz_{n}=\|f_{n+1}\|_{1},\end{split}

and by Hölder’s inequality,

(3.7)

\begin{split}&\int_{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}f^{\frac{n+1}{n}}(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n},z_{n+1})dz_{1}\cdots dz_{n}\\
\leq&\left(\int_{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}f^{\frac{n}{n-1}}f(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n},z_{n+1})dz_{1}\cdots dz_{n}\right)^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}f(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n},z_{n+1})dz_{1}\cdots dz_{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}.\end{split}

By combining (
3.5
), (
3.6
) and (
3.7
), we have

(3.8)

\begin{split}&\int_{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}f^{\frac{n+1}{n}}(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n},z_{n+1})dz_{1}\cdots dz_{n}\\
\leq&\prod_{i=1}^{n}\left(\int_{\mathbb{Z}^{n-1}}f_{i}(z_{1},\cdots\widehat{z_{i}},\cdots,z_{n},z_{n+1})dz_{1}\cdots\widehat{dz_{i}}\cdots dz_{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}\|f_{n+1}\|_{1}^{\frac{1}{n}}.\end{split}

Thus, by using Hölder’s inequality again,

(3.9)

\begin{split}&\int_{\mathbb{Z}^{n+1}}f^{\frac{n+1}{n}}(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n},z_{n+1})dz_{1}\cdots dz_{n}dz_{n+1}\\
\leq&\int_{\mathbb{Z}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\left(\int_{\mathbb{Z}^{n-1}}f_{i}(z_{1},\cdots,\widehat{z}_{i},\cdots,z_{n},z_{n+1})dz_{1}\cdots\widehat{dz_{i}}\cdots dz_{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}dz_{n+1}\cdot\|f_{n+1}\|_{1}^{\frac{1}{n}}\\
\leq&\prod_{i=1}^{n+1}\|f_{i}\|_{1}^{\frac{1}{n}}.\end{split}

This gives us the inequality (
1.6
) for
n+1
. When the equality holds, if
f\equiv 0
, then there is nothing to be proved. If
f\not\equiv 0
, then
\|f_{n+1}\|_{1}>0
. By that the equality of (
3.8
) holds, we know that the equality of (
3.5
) and (
3.6
) holds. By the induction hypothesis and that the equality of (
3.5
) holds, for any
z_{n+1}\in\mathbb{Z}
,

(3.10)

f(\cdot,z_{n+1})=\lambda_{z_{n+1}}\cdot\chi_{A_{z_{n+1}}}(\cdot)

for some
A_{z_{n+1}}
in
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
such that
A_{z_{n+1}}=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\pi_{i}(A_{z_{n+1}})
and
\lambda_{z_{n+1}}>0
. By that the equality of (
3.6
) holds, for any
z_{n+1}\in\mathbb{Z}
,

(3.11)

f(\cdot,z_{n+1})=f_{n+1}(\cdot).

This implies that for any
z_{n+1}\in\pi_{n+1}(\mbox{supp}f)
,
\lambda_{z_{n+1}}
and
A_{z_{n+1}}
are independent of
z_{n+1}
. Thus,

(3.12)

f=\lambda\cdot\chi_{A}

for some
\lambda>0
and
A=\prod_{i=1}^{n+1}\pi_{i}(A)
. So, the conclusion of the theorem is also true for
n+1
. This completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

Next, we give the proof for Corollary
1.4
, a logarithmic-type Brascamp-Lieb inequality.

Proof of Corollary
1.4
.

By the Jensen inequality,

(3.13)

\begin{split}\log\|f\|_{\frac{n-1}{n}}=&\frac{n-1}{n}\log\int_{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}f^{\frac{n}{n-1}}dz_{1}dz_{2}\cdots dz_{n}\\
=&\frac{n-1}{n}\log\int_{\mbox{supp}f}f^{\frac{n-np+p}{n-1}}f^{p}dz_{1}dz_{2}\cdots dz_{n}\\
\geq&\frac{n-np+p}{n}\int_{\mbox{supp}f}f^{p}\log fdz_{1}dz_{2}\cdots dz_{n}\\
=&\left(\frac{1}{n}+\frac{1}{p}-1\right)\int_{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}f^{p}\log f^{p}dz_{1}dz_{2}\cdots dz_{n}.\end{split}

Combining this with (
1.6
), we get (
1.7
). When the equality of (
1.7
) holds, we know that the equality of (
1.6
) holds. So, by Theorem
1.2
, we know that
f=\lambda\chi_{A}
with
\lambda>0
and
A=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\pi_{i}(A)
. By that
\|f\|_{p}=1
, we have
\lambda=|A|^{-\frac{1}{p}}
. This completes the proof of the corollary.
∎

Next, by using Theorem
1.2
, we prove Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

For any
z\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, by that
f\in C_{0}(\mathbb{Z}^{n})
,

\begin{split}&2f(z)\\
=&\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(f(z+ke_{i})-f(z+(k+1)e_{i})\right)+\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(f(z-ke_{i})-f(z-(k+1)e_{i})\right)\end{split}

for
i=1,2,\cdots,n.
So,

(3.14)

|f(z)|\leq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}|f(z+ke_{i})-f(z+(k-1)e_{i})|=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{Z}}|\partial_{i}f|dz_{i}

which implies that

|f|_{i}\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{Z}}|\partial_{i}f|dz_{i}

for any
i=1,2,\cdots,n
. Then, by Theorem
1.2
, we have

\|f\|_{\frac{n}{n-1}}\leq\prod_{i=1}^{n}\||f|_{i}\|_{1}^{\frac{1}{n}}\leq\frac{1}{2}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\|\partial_{i}f\|_{1}^{\frac{1}{n}}.

This proves inequality (
1.1
). If the equality holds, we have

(3.15)

|f(z)|=\frac{1}{2}\int|\partial_{i}f|dz_{i}

and by Theorem
1.2
,

(3.16)

|f|=\lambda\cdot\chi_{A}

with
A=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\pi_{i}(A)
and
\lambda>0
.

By (
3.15
), when
f(z)>0
, one has

f(z)\geq f(z+e_{i})\geq\cdots\geq f(z+ke_{i})\geq f(z+(k+1)e_{i})\geq\cdots\geq 0

and

f(z)\geq f(z-e_{i})\geq\cdots\geq f(z-ke_{i})\geq f(z-(k+1)e_{i})\geq\cdots\geq 0

for any
i=1,2,\cdots,n
.
So,
f(z)
is the maximum of
f
on
\{z+ke_{i}\ |k\in\mathbb{Z}\}
and
f(z+ke_{i})\geq 0
. By replacing
z
by
w=z+ke_{i}
with
f(w)\neq 0
, we know that
f(w)
also the the maximum of
f
on
\{z+ke_{i}\ |k\in\mathbb{Z}\}
. Thus, when
f(z)>0
, the equality of (
3.14
) holds if and only if
f(z+ke_{i})=f(z)
for
a_{i}\leq k\leq b_{i}
with some
a_{i}\leq b_{i}
in
\mathbb{Z}
, and
f(z+ke_{i})=0
otherwise. Similarly, we have the same conclusion for
f(z)<0
. Combining this with (
3.16
), we know that
A
must be a cuboid in
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. This completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

Finally, we come to prove Corollary
1.1
. The proofs for Corollary
1.2
and Corollary
1.3
are straight forward by combining (
3.13
) with Theorem
1.1
and Corollary
1.1
respectively. So, we omit their proofs.

Proof of Corollary
1.1
.

By (
1.1
) and the AM-GM inequality, we have

(3.17)

\|f\|_{\frac{n}{n-1}}\leq\frac{1}{2}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\|\partial_{i}f\|_{1}\leq\frac{1}{2n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|\partial_{i}f\|_{1}=\frac{1}{2n}\|df\|_{1}.

When the equality holds, we know that
f=\lambda\cdot\chi_{A}
with
A
a cuboid in
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, and
\|\partial_{1}f\|_{1}=\|\partial_{2}f\|_{1}=\cdots=\|\partial_{n}f\|_{1}
. This implies that
A
is cube.
∎

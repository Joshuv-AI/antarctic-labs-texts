Foliar efficacy of phosphorous formulations for rainfed cotton with graded levels
Authors: A. Manikandan, D. Blaise, T. Harshanand, C. R. Mundafale, A. S. Tayade, V. N. Waghmare
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Soil amendment with phosphatic fertilizers (50 or 100%) as basal is susceptible to fixation losses, leading to low phosphorus use efficiency (PUE). Consequently, foliar spraying of di-ammonium phosphate (DAP) up to 2% is preferred for rainfed cotton. In addition, it has been reported that nano-fertilizers facilitate a steady release of nutrients, enhance nutrient uptake, and boost nutrient use efficiency, ultimately resulting in increased crop yields and profitability. Therefore, this study aimed to investigate the foliar efficacy of DAP formulations to increase the growth, yield and nutrient uptake in rainfed cotton grown in black calcareous soils with graded levels. The field experiment consists of 13 treatment combinations with 3 replications, which were laid out via randomized complete block design (RCBD) during the kharif seasons of 2023 and 2024. These include varying levels (100, 75, 50%) of recommended dose of fertilizer (RDF) with foliar spray (FS) of nano DAP one time at 45 DAS, two-times at 45 and 60 DAS, foliar spraying of DAP one time at 45 DAS, two-times at 60 DAS and a water spray (control 0% RDF). Nano-DAP (2% N, 5% P₂O₅) and DAP (18% N, 46% P₂O₅) were sprayed in both the years. In 2023 and 2024, the treatment T4 (nano-DAP FS two times + 100% RDF), increased seed cotton (SC) and seed cotton yield (SCY) by (19.7, 15.7%) and (13.3, 12.9%), respectively, compared to T1 (DAP FS one time + 100% RDF). Similarly, root length (10.9, 9.8%), boll weight (10.8, 13.8%) and seeds boll− 1 (9.8, 10.0%) in T4 were increased compared to T1 in 2023 and 2024. Reducing the RDF by 25%, i.e., T8 (nano-DAP FS two times + 75% RDF), increased both the SC and SCY by 16.2, 3.8% and by 4.7, 10.0%, respectively, in both consecutive years compared to T1. However, seed index and fibre quality were not significantly influenced by foliar spraying of the DAP formulations. Economic analysis revealed significantly higher gross monetary returns of US$ 2490, US$ 2696 and net monetary returns of US$ 1422, US$ 1660 and benefit-cost ratio of 1.45, 1.63 were recorded in T4 during 2023 and 2024, respectively. Among all the treatments, T4 treatment also resulted in significantly higher profitability (ha− 1 day− 1) of US$ 9.82 and US$ 11.06. Foliar spraying of DAP formulations combined with varying RDF levels improved the growth, yield, nutrient uptake and use efficiency, economic viability of rainfed cotton. Nevertheless, these parameters were statistically comparable to those observed in T1. However, T4 achieved the highest NMR and benefit-cost ratio compared to T1 over both the years of experimentation. However, T4 (nano-DAP FS two times + 100% RDF) resulted in the highest benefit-cost ratio and per day profitability compared to other treatments.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-49767-7.

Introduction

Cotton fulfils 40% of global fibre demand. Last decade (2012–2022) in India, cotton is being cultivated on ≈ 12 M ha, with nearly 65% of the cotton-growing regions dependent on rainfed agriculture1. Although it accounts for 23% of the global cotton area and one of the world’s largest cotton producers (5.50 MT), its average lint yield remains below 400 kg ha⁻¹, which is substantially lower than the global average of 765 kg ha−12,3. In addition to abiotic stresses, inadequate soil fertility is a major constraint to achieving increased cotton productivity. Phosphorus (P) is one of the essential nutrients that plays a key role in reducing the yield gap, as it is crucial for cotton initial root development, flowering, seeds per locule. It is a structural part of ribonucleic acid, nucleic acids and phospholipids. It also governs key physiological and biochemical processes in cotton, including photosynthesis and various energy-requiring metabolic pathways4. Despite its importance, P deficiency is persistent and widespread in cotton growing soils5, particularly in black calcareous soils, due to its precipitation as dicalcium phosphatic and tricalcium phosphate6. Because of these strong fixation reactions, plants absorb only 25% of the applied P during the growing season7. However, the remaining P adsorbs onto the soil particles and becomes unavailable to the cotton. Therefore, to achieve optimal seed cotton yield (SCY), it is often necessary to apply significant amounts of phosphatic fertilizers. This practice not only increase overall cultivation cost, but also increase environmental issues such as eutrophication and soil degradation.

Cotton P demand is often met through mineral phosphatic fertilizers such as single superphosphate (SSP), triple superphosphate (TSP) and di-ammonium phosphate (DAP) to overcome yield losses8. However, the nutrient utilization efficiency of these fertilizers remains a serious concern. Despite extensive research and widespread fertilizer use, the P utilization efficiency (PUtE) of conventional fertilizers (CFs) has stagnated at 18–20%9. To address these limitations, appropriate selection of fertilizer types and application methods is critical for improving P availability and enhancing cotton productivity10. In this context, foliar spraying of DAP is an ideal strategy for increasing P availability to cotton. However, crops remain heavily dependent on imported rock phosphate to meet its P fertilizer needs, with nearly 90% of its requirements fulfilled through imports due to limited indigenous rock phosphate deposits11. Consequently, DAP scarcity has become a primary issue for cotton farmers because of the country’s limited rock phosphate reserves, despite DAP being the most widely used fertilizer after urea12. Moreover, the sharp escalation in global P fertilizer prices has led to a rapid increase in nutrient based subsidy (NBS) rates, which rose from ≈ US$ 0.18 kg− 1 to US$ 0.88 kg− 1 between 2018–2019 and 2022–202313. These issues highlight the immediate need to increase P use efficiency (PUE) through viable solutions. By optimizing the use of phosphatic fertilizers and implementing sustainable management practices, it is feasible to reduce the yield gap while minimizing the environmental impacts.

To address the challenges created by CFs in soil and the advantages of nanotechnology, this research focuses on foliar spraying of DAP formulations as an effective approach for sustainable P management in rainfed cotton production under black calcareous soils. Nanotechnology presents a promising strategy to increase the PUE and lessen the environmental impact of CFs. Nanofertilizers (NFs), characterized by their tiny particle size (< 100 nm) and high surface area-to-volume ratio, have demonstrated the ability to improve nutrient uptake, penetration and utilization efficiency in plants14. Recent research suggests that nanofertilizers can boost photosynthetic activity and overall crop yield15–21. Nano-DAP, a new liquid fertilizer developed by Coromandel International, Limited, Hyderabad can act as viable alternative to conventional phosphatic fertilizers. It may reduce reliance on CFs and decrease imports thereby saving foreign exchange. Estimates suggest that nano-DAP, equivalent to approximately 66 lakh tonnes of conventional DAP, could save ≈ US$ 2,640 million in subsidies each year, considering the current DAP subsidy of US$ 400 per metric tonne8. Foliar spraying of nano-DAP can increase PUE and reduce nutrient loss more effectively compared to CFs. To the best of our knowledge, till now no peer reviewed studies have been evaluated the relative potential of DAP formulations as foliar spray along with recommended dose of fertilizers (RDF) in rainfed cotton. To address this research gap, a two-year field study was carried out in rainfed cotton with the following objectives. To study the effects of graded RDF levels + foliar spraying of DAP formulations on the growth, yield, fibre quality and economics of Bt cotton in rainfed black calcareous soil. To evaluate the effects of multiple sprays of nano-DAP on nutrient uptake and use efficiency in rainfed Bt cotton.

Materials and methods

Experimental site and climate

The study was conducted during kharif seasons of 2023 and 2024 at the ICAR-Central Institute for Cotton Research, Panjari Farm, south of Nagpur (21° 04′ 51′′ N and 79° 04′ 40′′ E) in central India, with an elevation of 312 m above mean sea level (Fig. 1.). The climate of the experimental site is hot and subhumid, characterized by a monomodal rainfall pattern from June to September, with a mean annual rainfall of 1064 mm22. Mean weekly metrological observations were recorded during the cotton season of 2023 and 2024 which was recorded at the metrological observatory (Supplementary Fig. 1). The average weekly minimum and maximum temperature range from 11 °C to 29 °C, 26 °C to 42 °C in 2023, 10 °C to 28 °C, and 27 °C to 40 °C in 2024. Similarly, the total rainfall, pan evaporation and average wind speed recorded in 2023 was 1253 mm, 723 mm day− 1, 4.59 km hr− 1 and 1394 mm, 839 mm day− 1, and 4.93 km hr− 1 in 2024, respectively. However, minimum and maximum temperatures of 21 °C and 30 °C were required for optimum cotton vegetative growth23.

Soil sampling and analysis

Soil samples (0–30 cm deep) were collected from 10 random location prior to sowing and after each cropping cycle. The samples were air dried and ground to pass through 2.0 and 0.5 mm sieve and then analysed via standard procedures24. Initial soil results revealed that the experimental field was a Vertisol, which is clay in texture with 90% clay. The parent material was basaltic alluvium. The soil is neutral in pH with a normal EC, medium in free CaCO3 content, low in organic carbon content, low in soil available N, P and high in soil available K in both the years. (Supplementary Table 3).

Experimental design and management practices

The experiment was laid out in a randomized complete block design with 13 treatments and 03 replicants. The details of treatments given in the Table 1. The Bt cotton hybrid Ankur Kirti BG-II, with a seed rate of 2 kg ha-1, was sown with a spacing of 90 cm (row to row) × 60 cm (plant to plant), a gross plot area of 48 m2 and a net plot area of 29 m2 during both years. The recommended doses of N, P and K (100, 50, 50 kg ha-1 were applied through urea, single super phosphate (SSP) and muriate of potash (MOP) (Table 1). Half the N dose and a complete dose of P and K was applied at 7 days before sowing (DBS). While, the remaining N was applied in two splits during 40–45 days after sowing (DAS) and 65–70 DAS, based on availability of rainfall as per the treatments respectively. Nano-DAP, developed by Coromandel International Limited, Hyderabad, with particle size ≤ 100 nm, was applied as a foliar spray one time at 45 DAS for some treatments and at 45 and 60 DAS for other treatments at 5 ml liter-1. Similarly, commercial DAP (18% N, 46% P2O5) at 1% and water a spray was sprayed one time at 45 and two times 45 and 60 DAS. Weather conditions on the day of foliar spray was provided in (Supplementary Table 2). To check weed growth, pendimethalin (38.7% CS) at 2100 ml ha-1 was applied immediately after sowing. To monitor sucking pests and boll worm incidence, 25 sticky and 5 pheromone traps ha-1 were installed at 50 and 60 DAS. Cotton picking was carried out 3 times at 145, 160 and 175 DAS.

Analysis and sampling

Five plants from each plot were randomly tagged to measure the physiology, morphology, yield and fibre quality of the cotton plants. Cotton height was measured from the zero node to terminal growing point using a standard scale, monopodia and sympodia and the number of bolls plant− 1 were counted numerically. Leaf area index (LAI) was measured by using LAI-2200 C Plant Canopy Analyzer and chlorophyll content (CC) was recorded by using chlorophyll meter SPAD-502 Plus. Three hundred grams of SC from the tagged plants was collected to calculate the seed index, lint index and ginning out turn (GOT%) from Eqs. (1–3). Similarly, cotton fibre quality was analysed by using a high-volume instrument (HVI) at the ICAR-Ginning Training Centre (GTC), Nagpur. Harvest index was calculated with Eq. 4. To determine the plant dry matter yield (DMY), the tagged plants were uprooted after the final picking, air dried for three days and oven dried at 65 °C until a constant weight was achieved. The N, P and K concentrations were calculated via the kjeldahl method, the wet diacid digestion method with vanadomolybdophosphoric acid yellow colour and the flame photometry method25. Nutrient uptake was calculated by multiplying the nutrient concentration (%) by the DMY.

Economics

To calculate the gross monetary returns, the SCY kg ha− 1 was multiplied by the minimum support price (MSP) US$ 85 for 2023 and US$ 87 for 2024 for cotton. The cultivation costs include the current market rates of inputs, the interest on capital required for purchasing the inputs and the rental value of the land at the time of experimentation. The price of nano-DAP was taken as the maximum retail price (MRP) at US$ 8 L− 1 (maximum retail price provided by Coromandel International Limited). Gross monetary returns, net monetary returns, benefit–cost ratio and per day profitability were obtained via Eqs. (9–12), respectively.

Data analysis

The data of all the parameters were statistically analysed via analysis of variance (ANOVA)26 with computer-based AGRES-AGDATA statistical software. Treatment means were separated and ranking was given by using least significant difference test (LSD) at P = 0.05 level of significance. Furthermore, Pearson correlation analysis and principal component analysis (PCA-Biplot) were performed to understand the correlation between the growth and yield attributes of cotton27. Bar graphs, Pearson’s correlation and PCA-Biplot images were generated via the Origin 2025b 10.25, ggcorrplot package in R-Studio team (2025).

Results

Physiological parameters

Cotton physiological parameters, such as the chlorophyll content % (CC) and leaf area index (LAI), were analysed before and after foliar spraying of the DAP formulations (Table 2.). At 65 DAS (after foliar spraying of the DAP formulations), the significantly highest CC was recorded with T4 (37.67) from 2023 and (40.67) from 2024. However, CC recorded with T1, T2 and T3 were statistically similar to those recorded at T4. Similarly, in 2023, T4 presented the significantly highest LAI (4.06) at 65 DAS. However, in 2024, the LAI was not significantly influenced by foliar spraying of the DAP formulations. Furthermore, neither the CC nor the LAI were significantly influenced before foliar spraying of the DAP formulations at 30 DAS of 2023 and 2024.

Yield attributes

Foliar spraying of DAP formulations combined with 100% RDF significantly increased yield attributes, which includes boll weight (g plant− 1), seeds boll− 1, seed cotton (g plant− 1), and seed cotton yield (kg ha− 1). Notably, when the RDF is reduced to 75%, T8 (nano-DAP FS two times + 75% RDF) produces yield attributes equivalent to those of T4. Specifically, T8 resulted in 16.2%, 3.8% increase in the SC and SCY, respectively, in 2023 and 4.7%, 10.0% in 2024 over T1. Similarly, the boll weight (g plant− 1), seeds boll− 1 increased by 18% and 16% in 2023 and by 15% and 12% in 2024 over T1 (Fig. 4).

Seed index and fibre quality

Seed index, lint index and ginning out turn (GOT %) of cotton were not significantly influenced by foliar sprays of DAP formulations (Table 4). However, the highest values were recorded with T4. Similarly, fibre quality of cotton in terms of the staple length (UPML), micronaire (MIC) and bundle strength (BS) were not significantly influenced by foliar spraying of the DAP formulations. However, in 2023, the EI and UI were significantly influenced by foliar spraying of the DAP formulations along with various RDF levels. The highest EI and UI were recorded at T4 (Supplementary Table 4).

Macronutrient concentrations and uptake

Data on the foliar efficacy of DAP formulations along with varying RDF levels demonstrated that foliar spray of nano-DAP was effective in increasing cotton NPK concentration and uptake as compared to DAP as foliar spray. Application of (nano-DAP FS two times + 100% RDF) T4 has resulted in significantly higher cotton N (0.85%) and (1.92%) and P concentration (0.06%, 0.16%) compared to other treatments in 2023 and 2024 (Fig. 5.). In addition, cotton N, P concentrations were significantly increased by (18.4, 6.8%) and (6.6, 31.5%) with T4 compared to T1 in both the years. However, cotton K concentration was not significantly influenced by foliar spraying of DAP formulations in both the years. Furthermore, cotton N, P and K uptake were significantly increased by (16.9, 16.4%) and (9.1, 9.2%) and (9.6, 14.6%) respectively in both the years compared to T1 (Fig. 6.). Apart from T13 (water spray + 0% RDF), all the treatments were found to be statistically similar to T4 in K uptake of cotton during 2023 and 2024.

Furthermore, highly significant PFP values of N (46.09, 56.26 kg yield kg− 1 N applied) and P (92.18, 112.51 kg yield kg− 1 P applied) were recorded with T12 in 2023 and 2024, respectively.

Economics

During 2023 and 2024, the higher cost of cultivation recorded with T4 (nano-DAP FS two times + 100% RDF), with costs of US$ 1017 ha− 1 and US$ 1004 ha− 1, respectively (Table 6.). This was closely followed by T2 (DAP FS two times + 100% RDF), with US$ 1011 and US$ 999 ha− 1 in 2023 and 2024, respectively. The lowest cost of cultivation (US$ 896 and 889 ha− 1) was recorded with T13 (water spray + 0% RDF). Significantly higher gross monetary returns (GMRs) of US$ 2490 ha− 1 and US$ 2676 ha− 1 were observed with T4. Owing to the increase in SCY, higher GMRs were observed from 2024 than from 2023. However, in 2024, T2 was found to be statistically on par with T4. Similarly, significantly higher net monetary returns (NMRs) of US$ 1422 ha− 1 and US$ 1660 ha− 1 were observed with T4. However, T2 was found to be statistically on par with T4 in both the years. Furthermore, T4 has a highly significant benefit-cost ratio (B: C) of 1.45, 1.63 in both years. This was closely followed by T3 in 2023 and T2 in 2024, which were found to be statistically on par with T4. Highly significant per-day profitability of US$ 9.82 and US$ 11.06 was recorded with T4, which was closely followed by T8 (US$ 8.72 day− 1) in 2023 and (US$ 10.76 day− 1) in the following year.

The PCA clearly separated the treatments (T1 to T13) in factorial space, with the treatments positioned towards the right side of the plot indicating their strong positive contribution to the major variation observed. In particular, T4 was positioned close to the positive quadrant of PC1 (Dim 1), indicating its relationship with superior performance in terms of SCY and nutrient uptake. T13, which was placed in the lower negative quadrant, had the lowest contribution among the treatments. PCA-Biplot also revealed that SCY, height, number of bolls, boll weight, sympodia and monopodia and N uptake strongly positively contributed to the variation observed.

Discussion

Phosphorus (P) is the most deficient nutrient in cotton growing soils, with 98% of soils having inadequate P next to N due to the susceptibility of phosphatic fertilizers to fixation losses, especially in Vertisols28. Hence, supplying P through foliar spray is a viable strategy to improve P use efficiency (PUE) and availability to cotton. DAP is mostly used water soluble phosphatic fertilizer by farmers; however, its availability and affordability face several constraints in terms of the raw material supply and the rising cost of essential inputs, including rock phosphate. Hence, foliar spraying of DAP formulations in conjunction with various RDF doses was evaluated in rainfed cotton under black calcareous soils. In both the years of experimentation (2023and 2024), foliar sprays of 1% DAP (18% N and 46% P2O5) solution and nano-DAP (2% N, 5% P2O5) was assessed to understand its effects on crop growth, yield and nutrient uptake vis-à-vis the DAP treatments.

Cotton growth and physiological attributes are vital traits linked with the yield potential and performance of cotton under field conditions. Compared with the other treatments, T13 resulted in the lowest height because it didn’t meet the N and P requirements of cotton. Cotton growth decreased upon reducing N and P supply29. Foliar spraying of the DAP formulations significantly increased the cotton height, number of monopodia and sympodia, boll number, dry matter yield and root length. This might be due to the persistent and long-term supply of N and P by foliar spraying of the DAP formulations throughout the cotton growth period. It also effectively met the N and P demands of cotton, which improve protein synthesis and photosynthesis, leading to rapid cell division and enlargement, resulting in vigorous cotton growth. Increased N and P availability, improved chlorophyll content and photosynthetic rate in cotton30,31.

The increase in cotton yield with foliar spraying of the DAP formulations was likely due to an increase in the photosynthetic rate and the high amount of assimilates available during boll development. In particular, foliar spraying of nano-DAP (< 100 nm) allows it to penetrate directly into cotton leaves through the stomata (22–105 μm²)32 and epidermis, thereby increasing the number of seeds boll− 1 resulting in greater boll weight, which in turn increases the SC and SCY. Furthermore, there were positive correlations among the number of seeds boll− 1, boll weight and SCY33,34. Conversely, control showed the lowest yield and yield attributes likely because the crop could not meet its N and P requirements, resulting in the lowest N, P concentration and uptake. Unlike CFs, foliar spray of N and P through DAP formulations may have overcome the fixation, leaching losses by delivering nutrients directly to leaves allowing for direct absorption and reducing reliance on soil rhizosphere activity and moisture availability. Compared to 2023 the growth and yield in 2024 improved, possibly due to more favourable weather conditions and increased rainfall that supported the optimal growth.

Nutrient uptake is crucial for enhancing SCY, N and P concentrations, as these parameters are interrelated. In this study, variations observed in uptake values between the treatments were influenced by differences in SC and SCY. The foliar spraying of the DAP formulations combined with 100% RDF significantly increased N and P uptake in cotton. The higher nutrient uptake was primarily linked to increased N and P concentrations in plant tissues. This may be associated with greater SCY and increased nutrient concentration, possibly due to enhanced soil fertility and higher nutrient availability to the cotton. In 2024, the N and P concentrations and uptake in cotton increased compared to the previous year, attributed to increased rainfall. However, the cotton K concentration was not influenced by foliar spraying of the DAP formulations, possibly because of the relatively high K availability in the soil. The observed increase in N and P concentrations with foliar spraying of nano-DAP might be due to the smaller particle size (< 100 nm), which provides a larger total surface area and enhances nutrient retention. Similarly, nano-fertilizers increased P content in pearl millet grains35. Additionally, there was a positive correlation between P and N uptake36; essentially, as P intake rises, N uptake also increases. Likewise, the use of nano-P fertilizer led to higher P and N absorption in broad beans compared to the control37.

The application of 50% RDF along with foliar spray of nano-DAP two times resulted in higher agronomic use efficiency, apparent recovery efficiency and partial factor productivity. This might be attributed to reduced amounts N and P applied to soil coupled with improved SCY. Foliar spray of nano-DAP two times increased the nutrient utilization efficiency by facilitating better nutrient absorption and persistent availability to cotton. Due to their small particle size and greater surface area nano-DAP remained available for longer period, thus improving cotton growth and productivity38. It has been observed that RE often declines at higher level of fertilizer application possibly because nutrient uptake by plants was limited beyond a certain level, leading to nutrient loss through fixation and leaching39. Therefore, applying lower fertilizer rates such as 75% RDF combined with foliar spray of DAP formulations two times can act as supplementary source to soil applied fertilizers and enhance nutrient use efficiency. It ensured a rapid nutrient supply and effective N partitioning to leaves and bolls. Higher N and P application rates led to a decrease in AE due to leaching and fixation losses40,41.

In this study, Nano-DAP FS two times + 100% RDF incurred the highest cultivation costs, mainly due to the additional expenses associated with foliar spraying and nano-DAP. Despite of increased cultivation costs, Nano-DAP FS two times + 100% RDF achieved the highest gross and net monetary returns, attributed to the enhanced SCY. Nevertheless, non-significant differences were found among T1, T2, T3 and T4 with respect to growth, yield, nutrient concentration, and nutrient uptake. This could be due to the relatively low N and P concentrations in nano-DAP compared to DAP (18% N, 46% P2O5). Similar findings were also reported in wheat8. However, higher doses (> 2%) of DAP as foliar sprays often result leaf burn, especially at high temperatures, which negatively affects cotton nutrient uptake pattern42.

Conclusion

This on-station study indicated the valuable insights for rainfed cotton on combined application of graded fertilizers to soil and foliar spray of novel di-ammonium phosphate (DAP) formulations prior to squaring and flowering stages, significantly improved cotton growth, yield, plant nutrient concentration and uptake. Similarly, chlorophyll content and leaf area index were significantly improved during flowering stage compared to others. Furthermore, foliar spray of DAP formulations significantly enhanced N and P use efficiency. Nevertheless, these parameters were statistically comparable to those observed in T1. However, T4 achieved the highest NMR and benefit-cost ratio compared to T1 over both the years of experimentation. In addition, it also demonstrated the highest per-day profitability compared with the other treatments.

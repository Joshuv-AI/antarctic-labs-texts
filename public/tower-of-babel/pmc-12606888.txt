Study protocol for a randomized controlled trial assessing clinical efficacy of digital cognitive rehabilitation for preclinical and mild clinical stages of alzheimer’s disease continuum: the MI-RICORDO project
Authors: Valeria Blasi, Sara Isernia, Federica Rossetto, Chiara Pagliari, Francesca Borgnis, Alice Pirastru, Morgane Marzulli, Emanuela Foglia, Elisabetta Garagiola, Francesca Baglio
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundEarly or preclinical stages of AD continuum may benefit from lifestyle interventions and cognitive rehabilitation strategies that can delay or prevent progression to dementia. In this context, digital health technologies offer a disruptive potential to expand access to cognitive rehabilitation and meet the increasing demand for early interventions. This study protocol outlines a randomized controlled trial (RCT) designed to evaluate the efficacy and efficiency of a personalized, multidomain digital cognitive rehabilitation approach compared to conventional paper-and-pencil therapy. A secondary objective is to investigate the structural and functional neuroplasticity mechanisms associated with both interventions, using advanced magnetic resonance imaging techniques.MethodsThe study presents a single-blinded (assessors) 1:1 parallel-arm RCT design involving 102 patients with Subjective Cognitive Decline, o Mild Cognitive Impairment or early-stage dementia. For the experimental intervention group (EG) the digital therapeutic RICORDO-DTx will be employed, while the control group (CG) will perform an unstructured pencil-paper stimulation program. Both interventions will last 5 weeks with 3 session/week. Outcome measures will evaluate efficacy as changes in: behavioural and cognitive abilities, patients’ engagement, and structural and functional neuroplasticity mechanisms by means of Magnetic Resonance Imaging. Additional evaluation will include efficiency measures related to usability, acceptability, safety, sustainability and user experience. Patients will be evaluated at baseline (T0), after treatment (T1) and at follow up six months post baseline (T2). Data analyses will involve repeated measures ANOVA models on primary and secondary outcome measures to compare efficacy of intervention between EG and CG. Finally, efficiency measures will be reported with descriptive statistics.ConclusionsThe expected results lay on the ability of RICORDO DTx, to adapt task difficulty automatically based on patients’ performance and perceived difficulty. This adaptive approach is anticipated to yield superior treatment outcomes relative to traditional pencil-and-paper exercises.Trial registrationClinicalTrials.gov NCT07064226. Background

Neurocognitive Disorders (NCDs) within the Alzheimer’s Disease (AD) continuum constitute major contributors to global disability [1] with relevant impact on patient well-being, quality of life, functional outcomes and social isolation [2]. Accordingly, it is estimated that approximately 110 million individuals worldwide require rehabilitation services for such conditions [3].

Among the AD continuum, Mild Cognitive Impairment (MCI) and Subjective Cognitive Complaints (SCCs), are characterized by mild objective or subjective cognitive difficulties, respectively, that may represent prodromal and preclinical stages of dementia. Despite lack of impact of such difficulties on their daily living, a higher cumulative risk of dementia has been widely demonstrated when compared to the general population [4, 5]. Both conditions, though, are potentially reversible with a high rate of reversion [6] thus representing the best target for primary and secondary prevention against dementia by means of appropriate rehabilitation interventions.

Despite the recent introduction of disease modifying therapies (DMTs), which are showing promising results in the cure of AD, the current recommendations emphasize the importance of combining therapy including DMTs, with cognitive training, diet, and exercise [7]. Along this context, recent population-based epidemiological studies have shown that in the past four decades the risk of dementia, especially Alzheimer’s dementia, is progressively decreasing at any age [8]. This decrement has been interpreted as a consequence of healthier life styles and of the improvement in the control of modifiable risk factors [9].

Within this context, early rehabilitative intervention may serve as a crucial strategy for both primary and secondary prevention during the initial or preclinical stages of disease. The relevance of early and effective rehabilitation interventions in the prevention of NCDs has its scientific foundation on a robust body of evidence supporting the influence of life-course factors on cognitive aging. Notably, the Scaffolding Theory of Aging and Cognition – Revised (STAC-R) provides a compelling conceptual model in which the brain’s structural and functional adaptation - referred to as compensatory scaffolding - is shaped by a dynamic interplay of enriching (e.g., education, cognitive stimulation, social engagement) and depleting (e.g., chronic stress, sedentary behavior, depression) experiences across the lifespan [10]. In this framework, the brain’s capacity to reconfigure itself in response to enriching stimulation emerges as a modifiable target for neurorehabilitation intervention.

Nevertheless, access to rehabilitation remains limited, as it is typically a specialized and costly service, primarily allocated to individuals in moderate to advanced disease stages [3]. This results in the exclusion of patients in early or prodromal phases, who might benefit most from timely intervention. A further challenge concerns the intensity of rehabilitation programs: the most effective interventions often require frequent in-person sessions, which are rarely feasible or cost-effective for large-scale implementation [11, 12], which adversely affects access to rehabilitation services. Additional challenges pertain to accessibility, as access to care may depend on patients’ ability to reach clinical facilities, while simultaneously increasing the burden on caregivers.

In this scenario, the digital transformation in healthcare, including telerehabilitation and Digital Therapeutics (DTx), presents a disruptive opportunity to extend the reach of rehabilitation services beyond traditional clinical settings, making them accessible to a broader population. Particularly, DTx represent the most innovative approaches for evidence-based interventions, consisting of high-quality software programs used for the treatment, management, or prevention of medical conditions. While several studies have demonstrated the feasibility and efficacy of telerehabilitation approaches within the AD continuum [13–16], the use of DTx is not yet fully established in the rehabilitation context, although an increasing number of studies indicate their successful application in many clinical conditions including neurological disorders [17–19], also for neurorehabilitation purposes [20], demonstrating that they are both effective and safe. A key feature of DTx in the neurorehabilitation context is the ability to utilize sophisticated algorithms for online activity adaptation, enabling ongoing personalization throughout the treatment. This characteristic has a positive impact in terms of generating clinical benefits, particularly by contributing to enhanced engagement, adherence, and ultimately treatment efficacy.

From a healthcare system perspective, the imperative to improve accessibility to rehabilitation services across varied settings underscores the opportunity to leverage digital technologies more extensively across a broader population [21], not only in numerical terms, but also for the variety of needs addressed and the different stages of disease progression, thereby effectively expanding the portfolio of services and care provided by the healthcare system.

Building on these premises, the primary objective of this study is to describe the clinical trial protocol designed to evaluate the efficacy of a personalized, multidomain digital cognitive rehabilitation strategy in improving global cognitive functioning and promoting patient engagement in daily care routines, in comparison to traditional pencil-and-paper rehabilitation. The underlying hypothesis is that a technology-based intervention capable of adapting to individual patient needs will offer greater benefits than conventional cognitive rehabilitation methods.

Furthermore, the secondary objective is to identify the structural and functional neuroplasticity mechanisms promoted by the two types of interventions as assessed through magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) techniques.

For the purpose of the present study, a novel digital solution for cognitive rehabilitation, named RICORDO, will be employed, tested, and validated. RICORDO is a digital rehabilitation platform designed for individuals with cognitive deficits [http://www.ricordo-dtx.com/], offering a multidomain approach to enhance well-being and cognitive functioning. The rehabilitation activities target all domains outlined in the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, Fifth Edition (DSM-5). RICORDO consists of: (a) a telemedicine platform that allows clinicians to prescribe, plan, and monitor the digital cognitive rehabilitation content throughout the rehabilitation cycle; and (b) a tablet-based application that enables patients to access personalized rehabilitation programs remotely and asynchronously. Personalization of the rehabilitation program is achieved not only through clinical evaluation as prescribed by the clinician but also through a specific algorithm that adjusts the types of activities and difficulty levels based on patients’ cognitive needs, performance, and perceived difficulty.

Finally, to validate this approach, the study will be complemented at the end of the clinical trial by a multidimensional evaluation that also takes into account economic factors, organizational aspects, as well as socio-cultural, legal, and ethical issues. Acceptability and cost-efficacy will also be considered to assess the intervention’s sustainability and accessibility from a comprehensive, 360-degree perspective.

Methods

This study protocol is part of the MI-RICORDO project: “Transcultural and Multidimensional validation of dIgital Rehabilitation Intervention of Cognitive Resources Domain-Oriented”, funded by Italian Ministry of Health and the Italian Ministry of University and Research within the frame of the European Partnership on Transforming Health and Care Systems (THCS), a co-fund action under the Horizon Europe Programme, within the Call 2023 - Healthcare of the future, grant n. FP-1321.

The study has been designed and reported in line with the Standard Protocol Items: Recommendations for Interventional Trials (SPIRIT; Chan et al., 2013) guidelines. According to the revised Declaration of Helsinki principles, the trial protocol has been revised and approved by the Local Ethics Committee (protocol CET 58/25) and registered on the clinical trial registry clinicaltrials.gov (identifier: NCT07064226), date of registration 2025-07-30. Also, each participant read the study information sheet and signed the informed consent before taking part in the research.

Plans for communicating important protocol modifications to relevant parties.

Considering the type of RCT, we do not anticipate any risks that would require substantial modifications to the protocol. Should such modifications become necessary, they will be promptly submitted for approval to the Ethics Committee and the funding agency, and the trial registry entry (ClinicalTrials.gov) will be updated accordingly and in a timely manner.

Study design

The study presents a single-blinded (assessors) 1:1 parallel-arm randomized controlled trial (RCT) design, aimed at testing the efficacy of a home-based digital adaptive cognitive rehabilitation, named Rehabilitation Intervention of COgnitive Resources Domain-Oriented (RICORDO), compared to the same program delivered at home in a paper-and-pencil format (active control group).

Individuals at risk or with mild Neurocognitive Disorders will be enrolled in the study and randomly allocated to the RICORDO program or the active control condition for 5 weeks. All participants will undergo a clinical and neuropsychological assessment session with neuropsychologist at baseline (T0 time point), and they will repeat the evaluation post-treatment (T1 time point) and at 6-month follow-up (T2 time point). A subset of participants will be involved in a 3 Tesla Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) examination (Siemens PRISMA scanner) at each time point to investigate rehabilitation-driven structural and functional neural plasticity mechanisms.

Sample size estimation

The sample size for the present study has been a-priori estimated using the G*Power software (3.1 version). Considering a repeated measure ANOVA model (two levels between factor: RICORDO vs. paper-and-pencil groups; three levels within factor: T0 vs. T1 vs. T2 time points; interaction: group*time), a sample size of 102 individuals has been suggested, expecting ~ 10% of dropouts for each follow-up (T1 and T2), an effect size of f = 0.20, a power 1- beta = 0.95, and an alpha threshold of 0.05.

Study population, recruitment, and randomization

A total of 102 participants will be enrolled in the research during the periodic ambulatory neurological visit by the neurologist and/or neuropsychologist in the clinic. The eligibility criteria for inclusion and exclusion will be as follows: (i) diagnosis of neurocognitive disorder, such as mild Alzheimer’s Disease (mAD) and Mild Cognitive Impairment (MCI) or a prodromal condition such as SCC [4, 5], as reported in the clinical documentation and/or confirmed by the neurologist; (ii) preserved decisional capacity despite cognitive decay; (iii) Montreal Cognitive Assessment (MoCA) score > 17.79 [22]; (iv) Clinical Dementia Rating score ≤ 1 [23], age ≥ 65 years; (v) eventual availability of a caregiver/study partner for support; (vi) absence of motor, auditory, visual, or communication impairments able to interfere with the neuropsychological assessment and rehabilitation program administration; (vii) not having attended cognitive rehabilitation program in the last 3 months or not being currently included in a trial; (viii) stable pharmacological treatment (including cholinesterase inhibitors) for at least 3 months. After recruitment into the study, participants will be randomized to the experimental or control condition using a web-based allocation concealment and a computer-based algorithm by a researcher independent of the trial. The allocation will follow a 1:1 ratio, and two strata related to clinical conditions (diagnosis of neurocognitive disorder vs. SCC). At the end of the trial, a minimization strategy will be employed to ensure equal allocation in the experimental and control groups. Both the assessors and the researcher involved in the statistical analysis will be blind to the participants’ group allocation.

Intervention

Both the experimental and the control (active comparator) groups will be involved in a 5-week cognitive rehabilitation period at home, encompassing all six neurocognitive domains: complex attention, executive function, language, learning and memory, perceptual-motor, and social cognition. In the two groups, the intervention will be equal in terms of frequency, three asynchronous sessions per week, time, which will be 45 min per session, type, multidimensional cognitive rehabilitation, and intensity, a highly intensive treatment. While the experimental intervention will consist in a structured cognitive rehabilitation protocol provided through a digital platform (http://www.ricordo-dtx.com, ASTIR srl) accessing by a tablet, the control intervention will consist of an unstructured treatment delivered via paper-and-pencil materials. As a consequence, only the experimental condition will include the personalization and adaptivity of the cognitive activities during the treatment period. In fact, this condition will benefit from a dedicated algorithm, allowing an incremental task difficulty based on performance (accuracy and time) and the patient-reported perceived balance between the activity difficulty and the participants’ abilities. Furthermore, unlike the paper-and-pencil condition, the experimental treatment will offer online feedback to the participant during the session and hints when necessary to solve the task. Finally, the dose of the digital treatment will be fully manageable through the platform, which allocates the daily dose of activities so that the participant can access only the exercises devoted to the proper day of treatment. Instead, in the active comparator condition, the participant will receive the full paper-and-pencil activities before starting the program at home, and, despite the dose recommendations from the clinical personnel, the individual is potentially able to skip some sessions or mix them.

Both groups will receive periodical telephone calls from a rehabilitation therapist to monitor the motivational level regarding involvement in the program and to collect feedback on adherence and technical issues.

Outcome measures

Primary outcomes

Primary outcomes will be the Patient Activation Measure®-13I (PAM®13-I [28, 29]), and the Montreal Cognitive Assessment scale (MoCA [22]), .

PAM®13-I: A 13-item tool measuring an individual’s knowledge, skills, and confidence in effectively managing their health and healthcare on a 5-point Likert scale. The scale explores four stages of activation: (1) individuals’ beliefs about the relevance of their role, (2) their confidence and knowledge to take action, (3) taking action, (4) maintaining direction during stressful situations. The total score derives from the sum and standardization of items to a metric ranging from 0 to 100, with 100 suggesting a greater activation. Individuals classified in the lowest activation level typically lack awareness of their role in managing their health, demonstrate limited health-related knowledge, and possess underdeveloped self-management abilities. In contrast, those in the highest activation level exhibit proactive health behavior, possess well-established self-management competencies, and demonstrate resilience in response to stress or health-related changes.

MoCA: A global cognitive level screening tool encompassing different cognitive domains, such as executive function, complex attention, language, learning & memory, spatial and temporal orientation. The total score ranges from 0 to 30, with a higher score suggesting a greater cognitive level.

These primary outcomes will be evaluated as changes at T1 (5 weeks from baseline) and follow-up (6 months from baseline).

Secondary outcomes

As secondary outcomes, the following measures will be administered to quantify changes after rehabilitation (T1, 5 weeks from baseline) and at follow-up (T2, 6 months from baseline).

Cognitive functions

Everyday Memory Questionnaire Revised [30]: a 13-item self-report tool evaluating subjective experience of memory failure in daily life activities. Individual is invited to rate on a 5-point Likert scale the frequency of memory failure-related situations. A total score ranging from 0 to 52 is computed, with a higher score indicating a greater frequency of memory lapses.

Phonemic/Semantic Alternate Fluency Test [31]: a performance-based test aimed at evaluating language and executive function domain, particularly cognitive flexibility and lexical access. After a phonemic fluency and a categorical fluency trial, participants are invited to alternately generate words beginning with a specific letter (phonemic fluency) and belonging to a specific semantic category (semantic fluency) within a 60-seconds time frame. This latter trial increases task complexity, requiring mental set shifting compared to previous ones (traditional fluency tasks). The total number of words correctly generated is summed up to compute the total raw score, which is then adjusted for demographic variables according to Italian normative.

Benson Figure Copy and Recall [32]: a performance-based task evaluating visuospatial abilities, visual memory, and constructional skills by inviting the participant to copy a complex figure with no limit of time (copy task) and then, after 10-minutes time, recall by reproducing it again. The performance is rated on a scale from 0 to 17 considering both accuracy and placement of elements of the complex figure. A higher score reflects a greater performance. The raw score is adjusted for demographics in line with Italian normative [33].

Trail Making Test part A and B (TMTA, TMTB [34]): a neuropsychological performance-based test assessing complex attention, mental flexibility, processing speed, and motor coordination. Participants are invited to connect a sequence of targets, numbers in ascending order in TMT part A, and numbers alternating with letters following ascending order of numbers and alphabetic order of letters in TMT part B. In both trials, they are required to be as quick as possible while maintaining accuracy. The scoring is based on time (seconds) spent to complete each trial. Higher execution time suggests lower performance. The raw score of the test is adjusted based on Italian norms (Giovagnoli et al., 1996).

Free and Cued Selective Reminding Test [35]: a performance-based test assessing learning & memory domain. Individuals are required to learn 12 visual stimuli by identifying them and immediately cueing recall them (learning phase). Then, participants are invited to recall the stimuli without (free immediate recall) and with semantic cues (cued immediate recall) in three subsequent recall trials. Finally, they have to recall the stimuli without (free delayed recall) and with (cued delayed recall) semantic cues after 30 min from the learning phase, using the same procedure as the immediate recall task. The scoring is based on the number of recalled items. Four scores are computed: immediate free recall (range 0–36), immediate total recall (range 0–36), delayed free recall (range 0–12), and delayed total recall (range 0–12).

Yoni 48-item task (Yoni-48 [36, 37]), : a performance-based computerized test assessing social cognition, particularly Theory of Mind (ToM). Participants are shown 48 visual stimuli, where a cartoon-like character (Yoni) in the middle of the screen is referring with eye gaze or/and emotional expression to one of the four surrounding elements/characters, and they are required to select the ones Yoni is referring to. In total, 42 stimuli assess ToM, while the others are control items. The 42 stimuli are divided into items assessing affective or cognitive ToM, and first-order or second-order ToM. The test allows computing two global scores, adjusted based on Italian norms (Isernia et al., 2023): the accuracy index (ACC, range 0–1) and the response time index (RT, range 0–1), with higher scores suggesting greater performance. Furthermore, subscores may be derived: affective ToM (range 0–21), cognitive ToM (range 0–21), first-order ToM (range 0–16), and second-order ToM (range 0–26).

Mood

Beck Depression Inventory II (BDI-II [38]), : a 21-item self-report questionnaire assessing the presence of clinical depression symptoms on a 4-step Guttman’s response scale. The BDI-II allows extracting a total score ranging 0 to 63, and two subscores evaluating cognitive and non-cognitive symptoms. A greater score is associated with a more severe deflection of mood tone. Generally, a BDI-II score of 16 is suggested as a cut-off.

Positive Affect and Negative Affect Schedule (PANAS [39]), : a 20-item self-report scale evaluating positive and negative affective states (positive affect - PA- and negative affect - NA- subscales). NA and PA subscales refer to 10 items, positive or negative states, respectively, which are rated on a 5-point Likert response scale assessing the intensity with which the participant feels them (from point 1, very slightly, to 5, extremely). The overall PANAS score is calculated by summing the ratings for the items within each subscale, yielding separate PA and NA scores. A higher score reflects a greater intensity of the affective states.

State-Trait Anxiety Inventory (STAI-Y state [40]), : a 20-item self-report scale assessing the state anxiety on a 4-point Likert scale rating the frequency of anxiety symptoms (from 1, Almost never, to 4, Almost always). Total score ranges from 20 to 80, with a score of 40 commonly used to determine probable clinical anxiety. A higher score indicates a greater anxiety level.

Quality of life

EuroQol 5-dimensions 5-levels [41]: a standardized Guttman’s scale assessing five dimensions of quality of life, mobility, self-care, usual everyday activities, pain/discomfort, and anxiety/depression. Each domain is rated on a 5 step Guttman’s scale. In addition, a global self-reported evaluation of health is required on a VAS tool ranging from 0 to 100 with 0 indicating the “worst health you can imagine” and 100 “the best health you can imagine”.

MRI markers

Changes in structural and functional neuroplasticity mechanisms will be evaluated by means of Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) with a 3 Tesla Siemens Prisma scanner (Erlangen, Germany) equipped with a 64-channel head/neck coil. The MRI protocol will include following sequences: (1) 3D sagittal magnetization-prepared rapid acquisition with gradient echo (MPRAGE) sequence (repetition time (TR) = 2300 ms, echo time (TE) = 3.1 ms, in-plane resolution = 0.8 × 0.8 mm2, acquisition matrix = 300 × 320, slice thickness = 0.8 mm3, 224 slices); (2) sagittal fluid attenuated inversion recovery (FLAIR) sequence (TR = 5000 ms, TE = 394 ms, in-plane resolution = 0.8 × 0.8 mm2, acquisition matrix = 288 × 320, slice thickness = 1 mm, 176 slices); (3) an axial diffusion-weighted imaging (DWI) sequence (TR = 3600 ms, TE = 92 ms, in-plane resolution = 2 × 2 mm2, acquisition matrix = 104 × 104, slice thickness = 2 mm, 72 slices) consisting of 5 b0 images, 50 diffusion-encoding directions with b = 1000 s/mm2 and 50 diffusion directions with b = 2000s/mm2, acquired twice with reversed phase encoding direction (i.e. anterior-posterior and posterior-anterior); (4) a multi-band (MB) multi-echo (ME) gradient-echo resting state fMRI (rs-fMRI) sequence with the following parameters: TR = 1000 ms, TEs = 15/33.7/52.4 ms, resolution = 2.5 × 2.5 × 2.5mm3, acquisition matrix = 96 × 96, 48 slices, MB acceleration factor = 4, and two spin-echo sequences acquired in opposing phase encoding direction (AP-PA) to correct for geometrical distortion.

The following markers will be considered as secondary outcomes:

DWI sequences will be collected to detect changes in Fractional Anisotropy (FA) and Mean Diffusivity (MD) to evaluate structural connectivity. Changes will be evaluated after 5 weeks of treatment (T1) and at 6 months after baseline follow-up (T2).

Resting state functional Magnetic Resonance Imaging (rs-fMRI) will be collected to evaluate changes in functional connectivity (FC) after 5 weeks of treatment (T1) and at 6 months after baseline follow-up (T2).

Other measures

In addition to scales assessing the intervention-related benefits, the following efficiency measures will be collected to investigate the system usability, acceptability, sustainability, user experience, and safety.

System Usability Scale [42]: a 10-item self-report scale assessing the perceived ease of use and usefulness related to a technological system on a 5-point Likert scale (1 = strongly disagree, 5 = strongly disagree). A total score ranging 10 to 100, with a higher score indicating a greater usability experience, is computed. A score above 68 is associated with a satisfying level of usability. Moreover, two sub-scores can be extracted, allowing for separate focus on learnability (the level of effort needed to learn how to use the system) and usability (the level of ease of use and usefulness) dimensions [43].

Technology Acceptance Model 3 (TAM3 [44]), : a self-report scale evaluating 16 dimensions of acceptability on a 7-point Likert scale and/or 4-step Guttman’s scale. In detail, TAM3 explores the following domains: perceived usefulness, perceived ease of use, computer self-efficacy, perception of external control, computer playfulness, computer anxiety, perceived enjoyment, objective usability, subjective norm, voluntariness, image, job relevance, output quality, result demonstrability, behavioral intention, and use. A score is computed for each domain by summing the score of each item included in the sub-scale. A higher score suggests a greater acceptability. This scale will be administered to patients and professionals for the evaluation of both perspectives.

User Experience Questionnaire [45]: a self-report differential semantic investigating the user experience related to the interaction with the system. A raw score is calculated for each dimension investigated: attractiveness, perspicuity, efficiency, dependability, stimulation, and novelty. The scores are then converted into z-values (ranging from − 3 to + 3) based on Schrepp et al.‘s [45] indication, and are interpreted as indicating a good experience if above + 1.5.

Safety ad-hoc questionnaire: to collect the frequency, nature, and severity of eventual adverse events during the treatment period, a structured diary will be delivered to participants together with the rehabilitation kit. The diary will require individuals to fill in daily on the experienced adverse event, reporting its duration, severity, and whether the event is related to the rehabilitation session activity.

Additional data will be collected with the involvement of healthcare professionals, to capture the experience of both the organization and the staff engaged in the patient rehabilitation and care process. This approach, reflecting the multiprofessional nature of the rehabilitation pathway, will also examine economic and sustainability aspects, ensuring a comprehensive assessment of the proposed digital solution. In detail, the following measure will be administered:

Ad-hoc questionnaire covering all the items and domains of the MAST (Model for Assessment of Telemedicine) approach [46]: structured interviews will be planned covering legal, social, ethical, equity, and organizational aspects, to collect the perceptions of healthcare professionals. Each issue of these dimensions will be measured through a 7-point validated Likert scale (ranging from − 3: high negative to + 3: high positive) [47].

This participatory approach is intended to capture both qualitative perceptions (e.g., usability, ethical concerns, organizational feasibility) and quantitative factors (e.g., sustainability indicators), thus providing a comprehensive picture of the innovative solution. Furthermore, data on rehabilitation pathways (usual care vs. RICORDO arms) will be collected, including the duration of rehabilitation activities, outpatient visits, laboratory assessments, and diagnostic examinations. The economic data will allow the assessment of the financial impact of telerehabilitation solutions These data will then be systematically analyzed and economically evaluated.

Data collection

The data collection will be performed by a neuropsychologist who is blind to the allocation of participants to the intervention type. The demographics, clinical profile, dementia risk, and cognitive reserve proxy will be collected at T0. All outcome measures will be assessed at T0, T1, and T2. The output measures will be collected exclusively at T1. To ensure data quality, electronic data entry will be adopted for questionnaires and patient-reported measures, and accurate data check will be performed before conducting the statistical analyses.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses will be conducted using R software (version 4.3.3, retrieved from: https://www.r-project.org/) at the end of the trial, given the intensive nature of the intervention. To assess the efficacy of the RICORDO program compared to the active comparator we will perform repeated measures ANOVA, and Generalized Linear models on primary and secondary outcome measures. An intention-to-treat approach will be adopted, and, as a supplementary analysis, a per-protocol examination will be conducted.

To explore the efficiency of the RICORDO program, descriptive statistics will be run to evaluate the level of usability, safety, user experience, acceptability and sustainability.

In addition, correlation and regression models will be used to investigate the association between MRI markers and behavioral measures, as well as between baseline characteristics and outcome measures. An alpha threshold of 0.05 will be considered.

Plans to communicate trial results

The results of the trial will be disseminated through multiple channels. Participants, healthcare professionals and relevant stakeholders, such as patients’ associations, will be informed through presentations at dedicated project meetings. Moreover, scientific meetings, and publications in peer-reviewed journals will be pursued. Additionally, trial results will be reported in the trial registry [ClinicalTrials.gov]. Finally, communication to the general public will also include press releases and summaries shared via institutional websites and social media platforms.

Discussion

This protocol has been designed to verify the hypothesis that a home-based, multidomain digital rehabilitation intervention (RICORDO) will be more effective in enhancing cognitive functions and in promoting patients’ activation in the management of their care compared to unstructured, conventional pencil paper-based home rehabilitation programs in individuals with mild Alzheimer’s disease (AD), mild cognitive impairment (MCI), or subjective cognitive complaint (SCC).

These expected results rely on a key feature of the digital solution used in this trial (RICORDO DTx): its ability to automatically adapt the rehabilitation program and task difficulty based not only on the patient’s performance, but also on their perceived difficulty. This enables a more personalized and precisely tailored rehabilitation program that aligns with each patient’s abilities and needs. This adaptive approach is anticipated to yield superior treatment outcomes relative to traditional pencil-and-paper exercises, which typically involve predefined incremental difficulty levels, lacking the possibility to adapt to individual progresses and/or perceived difficulty.

One of the main mechanisms of action of DTx is indeed the promotion of engagement [48]. In this respect the personalization of therapeutic interventions is fundamental for motivating patients and maintaining high levels of engagement. The capacity of RICORDO to dynamically adjust to patients’ needs relies on assessing perceived task difficulty from the patient’s perspective together with levels of performance. This characteristic addresses the patients’ desire to be active participants in their own care, fostering a sense of agency rather than passive consumption of treatment. Such a feature aligns with the broader concept of democratizing health care—a paradigm emphasizing the importance of shifting from a patient-centered care, focused on medical disease to a person-centred approach focused on well-being and social support to foster the empowerment of patients to have an active role in managing their health and treatment processes [49].

Meeting the health needs of individuals undergoing rehabilitation often requires timely, individualized support that extends beyond traditional face-to-face clinical encounters, which can be logistically challenging, costly, geographically restrictive, and may also entail significant social and financial burdens for families who must provide transportation or additional support for their relatives [50, 51]. In this context, digital health technologies—particularly telerehabilitation and DTx—play an increasingly critical role in supporting patient engagement, continuity of care, and therapeutic adherence, while also reducing the financial and logistical burdens often faced by families [14, 52, 53]. Moreover, ensuring a broader portfolio of services can not only enhance engagement across populations with varying needs and levels of technology acceptance, but also expand the overall number of individuals who can be effectively served, thereby increasing the overall accessibility of rehabilitation services by offering more options both for patients and for the healthcare system. Telerehabilitation enables remote monitoring, follow-up, and communication with rehabilitation professionals, helping to overcome barriers related to distance and access [15]. DTx offer additional opportunities to personalize care and support behavioural and functional outcomes in daily life activities. Together, these technologies have been shown to enhance access to rehabilitation services, particularly in underserved or remote areas, while reducing the burden of travel and associated costs for patients undergoing long-term care [54, 55].

According to this approach, the RICORDO-DTx will also be evaluated in terms of efficiency, including system usability, acceptability, sustainability, user experience, and safety. Additionally, all processes and organizational components will be assessed to address the sustainability and accessibility of this intervention. This evaluation will be conducted using a multidimensional model based on the MAST (Model for Assessment of Telemedicine) approach, which supports the effective validation and adaptation of telemedicine solutions by considering safety, clinical effectiveness, patient perspectives, economic factors, organizational domains, as well as socio-cultural, legal, and ethical aspects, including acceptability and cost-efficacy concerns. This comprehensive evaluation is intended to provide a 360-degree assessment of the technological solution, with a particular focus on examining both qualitative and quantitative dimensions and incorporating the perspectives of all stakeholders involved in its implementation.

The multidimensional evaluation of the rehabilitation interventions has the potential to substantially influence both patient care and the efficiency of regional and national healthcare services. Patients could benefit from remote, tailored rehabilitation programs that target their specific cognitive deficits, while healthcare systems might adopt cost-effective, personalized therapy models that optimize the allocation of clinical resources. Ultimately, these innovations could contribute to a reduction in overall healthcare costs and improve the sustainability of cognitive health management.

In the long term, we expect that digital solutions facilitating personalized care at home will significantly improve healthcare accessibility, ensure continuity of treatment, and enhance adherence to therapeutic protocols. These benefits are likely to translate into improvements in overall patient health, cognitive resilience, and quality of life. Additionally, the widespread implementation of such digital interventions promises positive economic impacts for healthcare systems and stakeholders, by reducing the need for in-person visits and optimizing resource utilization.

Finally, the neuroplasticity mechanisms involved in the cognitive interventions will be investigated by means of MRI. In agreement with recent models of aging and the associated cognitive decline such as the STAC-R model [10], the recruitment of alternative neural networks to compensate for deteriorating brain functions is a key mechanism that can be spoiled for neurorehabilitation. This adaptive mechanism, termed “scaffolding”, can be promoted by external support such as neurorehabilitation programs to maintain performance, promoting cognitive resilience. To deepen the comprehension of these mechanisms, structural and functional plasticity will be evaluated. Specifically, DWI will be used to detect microstructural changes in the white matter, while rs-fMRI will be used to measure FC among principal networks areas.

Conclusions

This trial aims to prove the promising effects of digital medicine in the field of neurorehabilitation not only from the clinical benefits perspective, such as improvements in cognitive abilities, but also from the patients’ perspective, such as the increment in the engagement in their health care management, quality of life and wellbeing. These results will likely be coupled with positive results also in terms of efficiency and sustainability for the healthcare system.

Influence of Anaesthetic Techniques on Inflammatory Response and Stress Biomarkers in Patients Undergoing Gynaecologic Oncology Surgeries
Authors: Aditya S. Chauhan, Nancy Ekka, Divya Sinha, Sourabh Shrivastava
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Background:Gynecologic oncology surgeries are often extensive and associated with significant physiological stress and inflammation. Anesthetic technique may modulate perioperative immune and stress responses, potentially influencing postoperative outcomes.Methods:A prospective, randomized clinical study was conducted on 80 patients (aged 35–65 years) undergoing gynecologic oncology surgeries. Patients were randomly assigned to two groups: Group T (TIVA with propofol and remifentanil) and Group I (IA with sevoflurane and fentanyl). Blood samples were collected preoperatively (T0), immediately after surgery (T1), and 24 hours postoperatively (T2) to measure interleukin-6 (IL-6), C-reactive protein (CRP), cortisol, and epinephrine levels. Hemodynamic stability and postoperative complications were also evaluated.Results:IL-6 and CRP levels were significantly lower in Group T at T1 and T2 (IL-6 at T2: 65.3 ± 12.4 pg/mL vs. 94.1 ± 14.8 pg/mL, P < 0.001; CRP at T2: 3.9 ± 0.7 mg/dL vs. 5.6 ± 1.1 mg/dL, P < 0.001). Cortisol and epinephrine levels were also significantly reduced in Group T at T1 (cortisol: 422.6 ± 60.3 nmol/L vs. 569.7 ± 82.1 nmol/L, P < 0.01). Group T demonstrated better hemodynamic control and fewer postoperative nausea and vomiting episodes.Conclusion:TIVA attenuated the inflammatory and stress biomarker response more effectively than inhalational anesthesia in patients undergoing gynecologic oncology surgeries. TIVA may offer potential benefits in perioperative immune modulation and recovery in this patient population.

INTRODUCTION

Major oncologic surgeries, particularly in the gynecologic domain, are associated with significant surgical stress that triggers a systemic inflammatory response. This cascade includes the release of cytokines such as interleukin-6 (IL-6), acute-phase reactants like C-reactive protein (CRP), and stress hormones including cortisol and catecholamines.[1] These mediators are closely linked to perioperative morbidity, immunosuppression, delayed recovery, and possibly cancer recurrence.[2]

Anesthetic agents can modulate immune and stress responses. Inhalational anesthetics such as sevoflurane have been shown to influence leukocyte activity and oxidative stress,[3] whereas total intravenous anesthesia (TIVA) using propofol has demonstrated antioxidant and anti-inflammatory properties.[4] There is increasing interest in exploring whether the choice of anesthetic technique can affect perioperative inflammatory biomarkers and stress hormone levels, potentially impacting long-term oncological outcomes.[5]

Previous studies have produced mixed results. Some report that TIVA may reduce IL-6 and cortisol release compared to inhalational anesthesia,[6] while others suggest minimal clinical impact.[7] Despite the growing body of evidence, limited data exist specifically for gynecologic oncology procedures, where systemic inflammation and hormonal stress responses may uniquely affect postoperative course.

This study aimed to evaluate the influence of TIVA versus inhalational anesthesia on perioperative levels of IL-6, CRP, cortisol, and epinephrine in patients undergoing gynecologic oncology surgeries.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Eighty ASA I–II female patients, aged between 35 and 65 years, scheduled for elective gynecologic oncology surgeries (ovarian, cervical, or endometrial cancer) were enrolled. Patients were excluded if they had endocrine disorders, chronic inflammatory diseases, ongoing corticosteroid therapy, or known immunodeficiency.

Patients were randomly allocated into two groups (n = 40 each) using a computer-generated sequence:

Group T (TIVA): Received propofol (2 mg/kg induction; 100–150 μg/kg/min infusion) and remifentanil (0.25 μg/kg/min).

Group T (TIVA): Received propofol (2 mg/kg induction; 100–150 μg/kg/min infusion) and remifentanil (0.25 μg/kg/min).

Group I (Inhalational): Received sevoflurane (1.5 MAC) and fentanyl (2 μg/kg bolus, followed by intermittent boluses).

Group I (Inhalational): Received sevoflurane (1.5 MAC) and fentanyl (2 μg/kg bolus, followed by intermittent boluses).

Standard ASA monitoring was used throughout. All patients received similar premedication (midazolam 0.03 mg/kg) and muscle relaxant (rocuronium 0.6 mg/kg). Postoperative analgesia was standardized with IV paracetamol and morphine as rescue.

Blood samples were collected at three time points:

T0: Preinduction

T0: Preinduction

T1: End of surgery

T1: End of surgery

T2: 24 hours postoperatively.

T2: 24 hours postoperatively.

Levels of IL-6, CRP, cortisol, and epinephrine were measured using ELISA kits with standardized protocols. Hemodynamic parameters, pain scores (VAS), and incidence of nausea/vomiting were also recorded.

Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS v27. Data were expressed as mean ± SD or percentages. Intergroup comparisons used the Student t-test or Chi-square test. A P value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

RESULTS

Inflammatory markers

At T1 and T2, IL-6 and CRP were significantly lower in Group T [Table 1]. IL-6 at T2 was 65.3 ± 12.4 pg/mL in Group T vs. 94.1 ± 14.8 pg/mL in Group I (P < 0.001). CRP at T2 was 3.9 ± 0.7 mg/dL vs. 5.6 ± 1.1 mg/dL, respectively (P < 0.001).

Hemodynamics and recovery

Group T had more stable intraoperative MAP (mean difference: 7 mmHg, P = 0.04) and HR (mean difference: 8 bpm, P = 0.03). Fewer patients in Group T reported PONV (15% vs. 35%, P = 0.03). No significant difference was noted in hospital stay duration.

DISCUSSION

This study demonstrated that TIVA attenuates the perioperative inflammatory and stress response more effectively than inhalational anesthesia in patients undergoing gynecologic oncology surgery. IL-6 and CRP levels, key indicators of systemic inflammation, were significantly reduced in the TIVA group postoperatively. These findings align with previous research suggesting anti-inflammatory effects of propofol.[8]

The reduction in cortisol and epinephrine observed with TIVA suggests a blunted hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal axis activation, potentially leading to improved perioperative immunological balance.[9] This supports the notion that anesthetic technique is not merely a procedural choice but a perioperative immunomodulatory strategy.[10]

Studies in breast and colorectal cancer have also indicated that TIVA may enhance immune cell function and reduce perioperative immunosuppression,[11] which may translate to improved oncologic outcomes.[12] Although our study was not powered to assess long-term cancer prognosis, the short-term biomarker modulation seen here is promising.

Moreover, TIVA was associated with better hemodynamic stability and reduced incidence of PONV, which could contribute to better recovery profiles.[13] While the length of hospital stay was not significantly different, quality of recovery indicators such as VAS scores and side effect profile favored TIVA.

CONCLUSION

Total intravenous anesthesia using propofol and remifentanil resulted in significantly reduced inflammatory and stress biomarker levels compared to inhalational anesthesia with sevoflurane in gynecologic oncology patients. TIVA also provided better hemodynamic control and lower PONV incidence. These findings suggest a potential role for anesthetic choice in modulating perioperative immune responses and enhancing recovery in oncologic surgery.

Ordinals arising as residual finiteness depths
Martin R. Bridson

Abstract
. For every natural number n n , there exist finitely presented groups with residual finiteness depths ω ⋅ n \omega\cdot n and ω ⋅ n + 1 \omega\cdot n+1 . The ordinals that arise as the residual finiteness depth of a finitely generated group (equivalently, a countable group) are 0 , 1 0,\,1 , the countable limit ordinals, and the successors of these limit ordinals.

20E26, 20E06, 20E08

The
residual
core

\operatorname{Core_{RF}}(G)
of a group
G
is the normal subgroup consisting of those elements of
G
that have
trivial image in every finite quotient of
G
, i.e. the kernel of the natural
map from
G
to its profinite completion
\widehat{G}
. If an infinite group
G
is residually finite, then
its
residual finiteness depth
is defined as
\operatorname{Depth_{RF}}(G)=\omega
, because the shortest chain of finite-index subgroups
interpolating between
G
and
\{1\}
has length
\omega
. If
G
is not residually finite, then one can continue beyond

\omega
by examining the finite quotients of
\operatorname{Core_{RF}}(G)
.
In
[
3
]
, Brody and Jankiewicz define a countable group
G
to have residual finiteness depth

\omega\cdot n
if there is a sequence of infinite groups
G=C_{0}>C_{1}>\dots>C_{n-1}

such that
C_{n-1}
is residually finite and
C_{i}=\operatorname{Core_{RF}}(C_{i-1})
for
i=1,\dots,n-1
.
They also
define what it means for
\operatorname{Depth_{RF}}(G)
to be equal to other ordinals (see Section
1
). For example,

\operatorname{Depth_{RF}}(G)=\omega\cdot n+1
if, instead of
C_{n-1}
being residually finite,
\operatorname{Core_{RF}}(C_{n-1})
is a
non-trivial finite group. And
\operatorname{Depth_{RF}}(G)=\omega^{2}
if there is a sequence of infinite groups
G=C_{0}>C_{1}>\dots

such that each
C_{i}=\operatorname{Core_{RF}}(C_{i-1})
is infinite but
\operatorname{Core_{RF}}^{\omega}(G):=\bigcap_{i}C_{i}=1
.
If
\operatorname{Core_{RF}}^{\omega}(G)
is a non-trivial finite group, then
0pt(G)=\omega^{2}+1
.

Using a wreath product construction, Brody and Jankiewicz
proved
[
3
, Theorem 1.1]
that for every integer
n>0
there exist finitely generated groups
G_{n}
with
0pt(G_{n})=\omega\cdot n
. Their groups are not
finitely presented when
n\geq 2
and they ask
[
3
, Question 5.3]
if there might exist an alternative construction
that gives
finitely presented
groups
\Gamma_{n}
with
\operatorname{Depth_{RF}}(\Gamma_{n})=\omega\cdot n
.
The first purpose of this note is to describe such a construction. Brody and Jankiewicz
also ask
[
3
, Question 5.4]
if there might exist
groups
M_{n}
with
0pt(M_{n})=\omega\cdot n+1
.

Theorem A
.

For every positive integer
n
, there exists a finitely presented group
\Gamma_{n}
with
\operatorname{Depth_{RF}}(\Gamma_{n})=\omega\cdot n
and
a finitely presented group
M_{n}
with
\operatorname{Depth_{RF}}(M_{n})=\omega\cdot n+1
.

The groups in Theorem
A
will be constructed in an inductive manner as amalgamated free products; the
proof relies on Bass-Serre theory.
The construction is quite flexible, allowing one to impose extra conditions on the groups
constructed; for example, one can require the
\Gamma_{n}
to have finite classifying spaces. But in an effort
to make the ideas as clear as possible, I shall concentrate
on specific families of examples, leaving the reader to consider variations.

A third question of Brody and Jankiewicz
[
3
, Question 5.5]
concerns the existence of groups whose
depths are
\omega^{n}
and other ordinals.
They also ask about the difference between finitely generated and finitely presented groups in this context.

The following theorem gives a complete classification of the ordinals that arise as the residual finiteness
depths of finitely generated groups. We shall see in Section
3
that the answer is the same
for countable groups.

Theorem B
.

Let
\alpha
be an ordinal. There exists a finitely generated group
J_{\alpha}
for which
\operatorname{Depth_{RF}}(J_{\alpha})=\alpha

if and only if
\alpha
is
0,\ 1
, a countable limit ordinal, or the successor of a countable limit ordinal.

There are uncountably many countable limit ordinals but only countably many finitely presented groups, so we obtain the following
consequence.

Corollary C
.

There are uncountably many ordinals that arise as the residual finiteness depth of a finitely generated group but not as
the residual finiteness depth of a finitely presented group.

For ordinals
\alpha\geq\omega^{2}
, none of
the groups constructed in the proof of Theorem
B
are finitely presented, and for the moment
I do not know how to realise any of these ordinals as
the residual finiteness depth of a finitely presented group.

This paper is organised as follows. In Section
1
we gather basic definitions, relate depth to cores
for countable ordinals, and prove some elementary lemmas describing the interplay of these notions with Bass-Serre theory.
In Section
2
we prove that every limit ordinal less than
\omega^{2}
arises
as the residual finiteness depth of a finitely presented group. In Section
3
we prove that every countable limit ordinal
arises as the residual finiteness depth
of a countable group, and deduce some related facts concerning successor ordinals; the
constructions here involve a combination of wreath products and free products. In Section
4

we embed the countable groups of the previous section into finitely generated groups in a careful manner, enabling
us to show that every countable limit ordinal is the residual finiteness depth of a finitely generated group.
In Section
5
we complete the proof of Theorem
A
by dealing with successor ordinals and in Section
6

we complete the proof of Theorem
B
by combining ideas from previous sections.

1.
0pt
and residual cores

Brody and Jankiewicz
[
3
]
introduce and explore extensions of the following notion of
residual finiteness depth
;
for our purposes this simplified definition will suffice.
A similar notion is implicit in
[
1
]
.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
\alpha
be an ordinal. A group
G
is
\alpha
-residually finite
if there exist
subgroups
\{G_{\beta}\}_{\beta\leq\alpha}
of
G
so that

•

G_{0}=G
and
G_{\alpha}=\{1\}
,

•

G_{\gamma}\leq G_{\beta}
if
\gamma>\beta
,

•

[G_{\beta}\colon G_{\beta+1}]
is finite for all
\beta<\alpha
,

•

G_{\lambda}=\bigcap_{\beta<\lambda}G_{\beta}
for all limit ordinals
\lambda\leq\alpha
.

If
\alpha
is the least ordinal such that
G
is
\alpha
-residually finite, then
0pt(G):=\alpha
.

Note that there are finitely generated groups which are not
\alpha
-residually finite for any
\alpha
, for example
groups that have no non-trivial finite quotients. Note too that if
0pt(G)
is not
0
(the trivial group) or
1
(finite groups), then it is either a limit ordinal or the successor of a limit ordinal: if
G
is
(\alpha+n)
-residually finite, with
n
finite,
then in a filtration
\{G_{\beta}\}
witnessing this, one can replace
G_{\alpha}\geq G_{\alpha+1}\geq\dots\geq G_{\alpha+n}

by
G_{\alpha}\geq G_{\alpha+n}
to see that
0pt(G)\leq\alpha+1
.

In the introduction we defined
0pt(G)
in terms of cores, and it will be convenient to use the language of cores again in our proofs, so we make the following definition.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
G
be a group. The
residual core

\operatorname{Core_{RF}}(G)
is
the normal subgroup consisting of those elements of
G
that have trivial image in every finite quotient of
G
.
Starting with
\operatorname{Core_{RF}}^{0}(G)=G
and
\operatorname{Core_{RF}}^{1}(G)=\operatorname{Core_{RF}}(G)
, for successor ordinals (e.g. natural numbers) we define
\operatorname{Core_{RF}}^{\alpha+1}(G):=\operatorname{Core_{RF}}(\operatorname{Core_{RF}}^{\alpha}(G))
,
and for limit ordinals

\operatorname{Core_{RF}}^{\alpha}(G):=\bigcap\{\operatorname{Core_{RF}}^{\beta}(G)\mid\beta<\alpha\}
.

Remarks 1.3
.

(1) If
H
is a countable group, then one can construct a sequence of finite-index subgroups

H=H_{0}>H_{1}>\dots
with
\bigcap_{i}H_{i}=\operatorname{Core_{RF}}(H)
by choosing coset representatives
\{c_{0}=1,c_{1},c_{2},\dots\}

for
\operatorname{Core_{RF}}(H)
in
H
and defining
H_{i}
to be the intersection of
H_{i-1}
with
the kernel of a map from
H
to a finite group that maps
c_{i}
non-trivially.

(2) If
G
is countable and
0pt(G)=\alpha
then
\alpha
is countable: if
\alpha
were not countable,
then by choosing an element
g_{\beta}\in\operatorname{Core_{RF}}^{\beta}(G)\smallsetminus\operatorname{Core_{RF}}^{\beta+1}(G)
for each countable ordinal

\beta
we would obtain an uncountable subset of
G
.

The reader will recall that all limit ordinals have the form
\omega\cdot\beta
. (Throughout, it is assumed that
the reader is familiar with elementary facts about ordinals and their arithmetic, as found in Chapter 8 of
[
5
]
for example.)

Lemma 1.4
.

Let
G
be a countable group and let
\alpha
and
\beta
be ordinals.

(1)

\operatorname{Core_{RF}}^{\beta}(G)=1
if and only if
G
is
(\omega\cdot\beta)
-residually finite.

(2)

When
\operatorname{Core_{RF}}^{\beta}(G)
is non-trivial, it is finite if and only if
G
is
(\omega\cdot\beta+1)
-residually finite.

(3)

0pt(G)=\omega\cdot\alpha+1
if and only if
\operatorname{Core_{RF}}^{\alpha}(G)
is finite but non-trivial.

(4)

0pt(G)=\omega\cdot\alpha
if and only if

\operatorname{Core_{RF}}^{\alpha}(G)=1
and
\alpha
is the least ordinal such that
\operatorname{Core_{RF}}^{\alpha}(G)
is finite.

Proof.

For (1),
assuming
\operatorname{Core_{RF}}^{\beta}(G)
to be trivial,
we must define a family of subgroups
\{G_{\gamma}\}_{\gamma\leq\omega\cdot\beta}
showing that

G
is
\omega\cdot\beta
-residually finite. First, we use Remark
1.3
to construct a sequence of finite-index subgroups
H_{i}(0)
in
G
that intersect in
\operatorname{Core_{RF}}(G)
. Next, for limit ordinals
\omega\cdot\delta\leq\omega\cdot\beta
,
we define
G_{\omega\cdot\delta}=\operatorname{Core_{RF}}^{\delta}(G)
. To finish, for successor ordinals we
use Remark
1.3
again to construct a sequence of finite-index subgroups
H_{i}(\delta)
in
\operatorname{Core_{RF}}^{\delta}(G)

that intersect in
\operatorname{Core_{RF}}^{\delta+1}(G)
and define
G_{\omega\cdot\delta+i}=H_{i}(\delta)
.

For the reverse implication in (1), we take

\{G_{\gamma}\}_{\gamma\leq\omega\cdot\beta}
to be a set of subgroups witnessing the fact that

G
is
\omega\cdot\beta
-residually finite. Then, since
\operatorname{Core_{RF}}(K)\leq\operatorname{Core_{RF}}(H)
for all groups
K<H
,
an obvious transfinite induction shows that
\operatorname{Core_{RF}}^{\delta}(G)\leq G_{\omega\cdot\delta}
for all
\delta\leq\beta
.

The passage from (1) to (2) is trivial. Items (3) and (4) follow immediately from (1) and (2).
∎

1.1.
Cores, depth, and graphs of groups

I shall assume that the reader is familiar with the rudiments of Bass-Serre theory
[
6
]
.
If
\Gamma
is the fundamental group of a graph of groups
\mathbb{G}
, the universal cover
\widetilde{\mathbb{G}}
(in the
category of graphs of groups) is the Bass-Serre tree for this splitting. Associated to each subgroup
H<\Gamma
one
has the graph of groups
\widetilde{\mathbb{G}}/\!/H
covering
\mathbb{G}
. We shall be particularly interested in the case
where the underlying graph
|\widetilde{\mathbb{G}}/\!/H|
is a tree, which it will be if and only if
H
is generated by
elliptic elements, i.e. by
\bigcup(H\cap G_{v}^{\gamma})
, where the union is taken over all conjugates of the vertex
groups
G_{v}
of
\mathbb{G}
. Given a normal subgroup
K\unlhd\Gamma
we also consider the graph of groups
\mathbb{G}_{K}

(thought of as “
\mathbb{G}\mod K
”) that is obtained
from
\mathbb{G}
without changing the underlying graph by replacing each of local groups
G
with
G/(G\cap K)
,
taking the induced inclusions of edge groups.
The kernel of the natural epimorphism
\Gamma=\pi_{1}\mathbb{G}\to\pi_{1}\mathbb{G}_{K}
is the subgroup of
K
generated by elliptic elements.
The quotient map
\Gamma\to\Gamma/K
factors through
\Gamma\to\pi_{1}\mathbb{G}_{K}
giving
a natural epimorphism
\pi_{1}\mathbb{G}_{K}\to\Gamma/K
that is inclusion on the local groups; this epimorphism is not injective in general.

Lemma 1.5
.

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a tree of groups with
\Gamma=\pi_{1}\mathbb{G}
.
If all of the edge groups of
\mathbb{G}
lie in
\operatorname{Core_{RF}}(\Gamma)
, then
|\widetilde{\mathbb{G}}/\!/\operatorname{Core_{RF}}(\Gamma)|
is a tree.

Proof.

We apply the preceding general considerations with
K=\operatorname{Core_{RF}}(\Gamma)
. The vertex groups of
\mathbb{G}_{K}
are residually finite, since the cores of the vertex groups of
\mathbb{G}
are contained in
K
,
and the edge groups are trivial by hypothesis. Thus
\pi_{1}\mathbb{G}_{K}
is a free product of residually finite
groups. In particular it is residually finite, so the natural epimorphism
\pi_{1}\mathbb{G}_{K}\to\Gamma/\operatorname{Core_{RF}}(\Gamma)
splits
and
\operatorname{Core_{RF}}(\Gamma)=\ker(\Gamma\to\pi_{1}\mathbb{G}_{K})
, which is generated by elliptic elements.
∎

Remark 1.6
.

When we interpolate between
\Gamma
and
\operatorname{Core_{RF}}(\Gamma)
with finite-index subgroups
\Gamma=\Gamma_{0}>\Gamma_{1}>\dots
, the
graphs
|\widetilde{\mathbb{G}}/\!/\Gamma_{i}|
need not be trees. For example, in the infinite dihedral group
\Gamma=(\mathbb{Z}/2)\ast(\mathbb{Z}/2)
, if
\Gamma_{1}

is the infinite cyclic subgroup of index two, then
|\widetilde{\mathbb{G}}/\!/\Gamma_{1}|
has two vertices and two edges.

Lemma 1.7
.

If a countable group
\Gamma
is the free product of non-trivial groups
A_{i}
, then

(1)

A_{i}\cap\operatorname{Core_{RF}}^{\alpha}(\Gamma)=\operatorname{Core_{RF}}^{\alpha}(A_{i})
for any ordinal
\alpha
, and

(2)

\operatorname{Core_{RF}}^{\alpha}(\Gamma)
is a free product of copies of the groups
\operatorname{Core_{RF}}^{\alpha}(A_{i})
.

(3)

If
\sup_{i}0pt(A_{i})
is a limit ordinal, then
0pt(\Gamma)=\sup_{i}0pt(A_{i})
;

(4)

if
\sup_{i}0pt(A_{i})
is a successor ordinal,
0pt(\Gamma)=\sup_{i}0pt(A_{i})+\omega.

Proof.

Assertion (1) holds more generally for retracts
A
of
\Gamma
:
for
\alpha=1
it is the obvious assertion that
an element of
A
survives in a finite quotient of
A
if and only if it survives in some finite quotient of
\Gamma
;
for arbitrary
\alpha
, the result follows by transfinite induction, noting repeatedly that a retraction
\Gamma\to A

restricts to a retraction
\operatorname{Core_{RF}}(\Gamma)\to\operatorname{Core_{RF}}(A)
.

For (2), we consider the action of
\operatorname{Core_{RF}}^{\alpha}(\Gamma)
on the Bass-Serre tree
for the splitting
\mathbb{G}
of
\Gamma
as a free product of the
A_{i}
.
The vertex groups in the quotient graph-of-groups are conjugates of
A_{i}\cap\operatorname{Core_{RF}}^{\alpha}(\Gamma)
,
which by (1) are copies of
\operatorname{Core_{RF}}^{\alpha}(A_{i})
. Proceeding by transfinite induction, with
Lemma
1.5
as the base step, we shall argue that
the underlying graph
|\widetilde{\mathbb{G}}/\!/\operatorname{Core_{RF}}^{\alpha}(\Gamma)|
is a tree. In the inductive
step, we assume that this is the case for ordinals
\beta<\alpha
.

At successor ordinals
\alpha=\alpha_{0}+1
, we can apply Lemma
1.5
with
\operatorname{Core_{RF}}^{\alpha_{0}}(\Gamma)
in place of

\Gamma
, since
\widetilde{\mathbb{G}}/\!/\operatorname{Core_{RF}}^{\alpha_{0}}(\Gamma)
(which is a
tree
of groups, by induction) has the same
universal cover as
\mathbb{G}
.
To clarify the argument at limit ordinals, we first note that
an arbitrary normal subgroup
N
in
\Gamma=\bigast_{i}A_{i}
will be generated by its elliptic
elements
\bigcup_{i,\gamma}(N\cap A_{i}^{\gamma})
if and only if there does
not
exist
an element
\nu\in N
whose free-product normal form is
\nu=a_{1}\dots a_{m}
,
with all
a_{j}\in A_{i(j)}\smallsetminus N
. (Such an element
\nu
determines a reduced loop in the quotient
graph of groups
\mathbb{G}/\!/N
.) We apply this observation with
N=\operatorname{Core_{RF}}^{\alpha}(\Gamma)
:
if there were to exist
\nu\in\operatorname{Core_{RF}}^{\alpha}(\Gamma)
with
\nu=a_{1}\dots a_{m}
and
all
a_{j}\in A_{i(j)}\smallsetminus\operatorname{Core_{RF}}^{\alpha}(\Gamma)
then, because
\operatorname{Core_{RF}}^{\alpha}(\Gamma)=\bigcap_{\beta<\alpha}\operatorname{Core_{RF}}^{\beta}(\Gamma)
,
this would imply that
a_{j}\in A_{i(j)}\smallsetminus\operatorname{Core_{RF}}^{\beta}(\Gamma)
for all
a_{j}
when
\beta<\alpha
is
sufficiently large; but this cannot happen, because it implies that
\operatorname{Core_{RF}}^{\beta}(\Gamma)
is not generated by its
elliptic elements, contrary to the inductive hypothesis. This completes the proof of (2).

Towards proving (3) and (4), we consider
\sigma=\sup_{i}0pt(A_{i})
.
If
\sigma
is a limit ordinal
\sigma=\omega\cdot\alpha
then
\operatorname{Core_{RF}}^{\alpha}(A_{i})=1
for all
i
,
so from (2) we have
\operatorname{Core_{RF}}^{\alpha}(\Gamma)=1
, hence
0pt(\Gamma)\leq\omega\cdot\alpha=\sigma
.
And
by applying to
A_{i}<\Gamma
the observation that
H<K
implies
0pt(H)\leq 0pt(K)
, we have
\sigma\leq 0pt(\Gamma)
,
so (3) is proved.
For (4), we suppose
\sigma=\omega\cdot\beta+1
,
which means that
\operatorname{Core_{RF}}^{\beta}(A_{i})
is finite for all indices
i
but
\operatorname{Core_{RF}}^{\beta}(A_{i})
is non-trivial for some
i
.
In this case,
\operatorname{Core_{RF}}^{\beta}(\Gamma)
, which has more than one free factor isomorphic to
\operatorname{Core_{RF}}^{\beta}(A_{i})
for each
i
,
is a free product of finite groups and is infinite. As any free product of finite groups is residually finite, we conclude
that
\operatorname{Core_{RF}}^{\beta}(\Gamma)
is infinite but
\operatorname{Core_{RF}}^{\beta+1}(\Gamma)=1
, hence
0pt(\Gamma)=\omega\cdot(\beta+1)=\sigma+\omega
.
∎

1.2.
Direct Products

For completeness, we record the analogue of Lemma
1.7
for direct sums. The proof in this case
is straightforward, so we omit it.

Lemma 1.8
.

If a countable group
\Gamma
is the direct sum of non-trivial groups
A_{i}
, then

(1)

A_{i}\cap\operatorname{Core_{RF}}^{\alpha}(\Gamma)=\operatorname{Core_{RF}}^{\alpha}(A_{i})
for any ordinal
\alpha
, and

(2)

\operatorname{Core_{RF}}^{\alpha}(\Gamma)
is the direct sum of the groups
\operatorname{Core_{RF}}^{\alpha}(A_{i})
.

(3)

If
\sup_{i}0pt(A_{i})
is a limit ordinal, then
0pt(\Gamma)=\sup_{i}0pt(A_{i})
;

(4)

if
\sup_{i}0pt(A_{i})
is a successor ordinal, then
0pt(\Gamma)=\sup_{i}0pt(A_{i})
if there
are only finitely many
A_{i}
that realise the supremum and
0pt(\Gamma)=\sup_{i}0pt(A_{i})+\omega
if there are
infinitely many.

1.3.
Some more basic facts

The following observations will be useful throughout and we shall often
use them without comment.

Lemma 1.9
.

For all ordinals
\alpha
and groups
G
,

(1)

if
H\leq G
then
\operatorname{Core_{RF}}^{\alpha}(H)\leq\operatorname{Core_{RF}}^{\alpha}(G)
;

(2)

if
H\leq\operatorname{Core_{RF}}^{\beta}(G)
for all
\beta<\alpha
then
\operatorname{Core_{RF}}(H)\leq\operatorname{Core_{RF}}^{\alpha}(G)
;

(3)

if
p:G\twoheadrightarrow Q
is a surjection with kernel
K
, then
\operatorname{Core_{RF}}^{\alpha}(G)\leq p^{-1}(\operatorname{Core_{RF}}^{\alpha}(Q))

with equality if
K\leq\operatorname{Core_{RF}}^{\alpha}(G)
.

2.
Finitely presented groups realising the ordinals
\omega\cdot n

We want to prove that
\omega\cdot n
is the residual finiteness depth of a finitely presented group,
proceeding by induction on
n
. In order to make the induction run smoothly, it is convenient to
make the inductively-constructed groups
\Gamma_{n}
have the following properties:

(1)

\Gamma_{n}
is finitely presented;

(2)

\operatorname{Depth_{RF}}(\Gamma_{n})=\omega\cdot n
;

(3)

\Gamma_{n}
has a generating set
\{a_{0},\dots,a_{m}\}
for which there is an epimorphism
h_{n}:\Gamma_{n}\to\mathbb{Z}

with
h_{n}(a_{i})=h_{n}(a_{0})
for
i=1,\dots,m
. (The integer
m
depends on
n
.)

In the inductive step, we construct a group
\Gamma_{n}^{0}
satisfying (1) and (2) then define
\Gamma_{n}=\Gamma_{n}^{0}\ast\mathbb{Z}
,
invoking the following observation to get condition (3).

Lemma 2.1
.

If
\Gamma_{n}^{0}
satisfies (1) and (2), then
\Gamma_{n}=\Gamma_{n}^{0}\ast\mathbb{Z}
satisfies (1), (2) and (3).

Proof.

If
\Gamma_{n}^{0}
is generated by
b_{1},\dots,b_{m}
and
\zeta
generates
\mathbb{Z}
, define
a_{0}=\zeta
and
a_{i}=b_{i}\zeta
.
Lemma
1.7

assures us that
\Gamma_{n}\ast\mathbb{Z}
satisfies condition (2), and killing
\Gamma_{n}^{0}
yields
h_{n}
.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

The key point about property (3) is that it ensures that all of the cyclic subgroups
\langle a_{i}\rangle<\Gamma_{n}
are retracts of
\Gamma_{n}
.
To see why this might be useful, recall from
[
2
]

that if
\mathbb{G}
is a tree of groups where each vertex group is residually finite and
each edge group is a retract of the vertex groups into which it includes, then
\pi_{1}\mathbb{G}
is residually finite.

It will be convenient to have a finitely presented group
\Lambda
for which
\operatorname{Core_{RF}}(\Lambda)\cong\mathbb{Z}
. A famous example
of such a group is Deligne’s central extension
[
4
]

of
{\textrm{Sp}}(2g,\mathbb{Z})
, any
g\geq 2
, for which
\operatorname{Core_{RF}}(\Lambda)
is the subgroup
of index 2 in the kernel of the central extension

(2.1)

1\to\mathbb{Z}\to\Lambda\to{\textrm{Sp}}(2g,\mathbb{Z})\to 1.

Henceforth,
\Lambda
will always denote this central extension. Later on, we shall also need a central extension
of a residually finite group that has finite centre and
is not residually finite. Such a group can be obtained from
\Lambda
by
killing a subgroup of odd index
d>2
in the kernel of the extension (
2.1
),

(2.2)

1\to\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z}\to\Delta\to{\textrm{Sp}}(2g,\mathbb{Z})\to 1.

The value of
d>2
will not matter, but we fix
g\geq 3
to ensure that
\Delta
is perfect (which
follows immediately from the fact that
{\textrm{Sp}}(2g,\mathbb{Z})
is perfect, since the central
\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z}

dies in every finite quotient of
\Delta
).

Theorem 2.3
.

For every positive integer
n
, there exists a finitely presented group
\Gamma_{n}
with
\operatorname{Depth_{RF}}(\Gamma_{n})=\omega\cdot n
.

Proof.

We argue by induction on
n
. For
\Gamma_{1}
we can take
\mathbb{Z}
, for example.
For
\Gamma_{2}
we can take
\Gamma_{2}^{0}\ast\mathbb{Z}
where
\Gamma_{2}^{0}\cong\Lambda
is the group from (
2.1
). In the inductive step, we
assume
n\geq 2
and that
\Gamma_{n}
satisfies the conditions (1) to (3) described above, and we must construct
\Gamma_{n+1}^{0}

satisfying (1) and (2).
Define
\Gamma_{n+1}^{0}
to be the
group obtained by amalgamating
m+1
copies of
\Lambda
with
\Gamma_{n}
, where the
i
-th copy
\Lambda_{i}
of
\Lambda

is amalgamated by identifying a generator
\zeta_{i}
of
\operatorname{Core_{RF}}(\Lambda_{i})\cong\mathbb{Z}
with the generator
a_{i}\in\Gamma_{n}
.

In any finite image of
\Gamma_{n+1}^{0}
, each
\zeta_{i}
must “die” (i.e. map trivially), so
\Gamma_{n}
must
die, since each of its generators has been identified with some
\zeta_{i}
. Thus
every map from
\Gamma_{n+1}^{0}
to a finite group will factor through the epimorphism

\rho:\Gamma_{n+1}^{0}\to\bigast_{i=0}^{m}\Lambda_{i}/\langle\zeta_{i}\rangle
. This free product of copies of
\Lambda/\langle\zeta\rangle

is residually finite, so the kernel of
\rho
is

\operatorname{Core_{RF}}(\Gamma_{n+1}^{0})
. Bass-Serre theory, as in the proof of Lemma
1.7
, tells us that this
kernel is the fundamental group of a graph of groups
\mathcal{T}
some of whose vertex groups are conjugates of
\Gamma_{n}

while the others (from the
\Lambda_{i}
vertices) are infinite cyclic groups; each of the latter type is generated by
each of the edge groups incident at that vertex, since each is a conjugate of
\langle\zeta_{i}\rangle

and
\langle\zeta_{i}\rangle
is normal in
\Lambda_{i}
. Moreover, Lemma
1.5
tells us that the underlying graph of
\mathcal{T}
is a tree.

The map
h_{n}
in condition (3) provides a map onto
\mathbb{Z}
from each
vertex group of type
\Gamma_{n}
in the decomposition
\mathcal{T}
,
and these maps agree on intersections (the edge groups). Thus we obtain a map
h_{n+1}
from
\pi_{1}\mathcal{T}=\operatorname{Core_{RF}}(\Gamma_{n+1}^{0})

onto
\mathbb{Z}
that restricts to an isomorphism on each edge group and on each of the vertex groups that is
cyclic. It follows that the subgroup of
\operatorname{Core_{RF}}(\Gamma_{n+1}^{0})
consisting of elements that die in every finite quotient
of
\operatorname{Core_{RF}}(\Gamma_{n+1}^{0})

intersects the edge groups and cyclic vertex groups trivially, and therefore this subgroup
(which is
\operatorname{Core_{RF}}^{2}(\Gamma_{n+1}^{0})
) is the
fundamental group of a graph of groups whose edge groups are trivial and whose vertex groups are either
trivial or else conjugates in
\operatorname{Core_{RF}}(\Gamma_{n+1}^{0})
of
I_{n}:=\Gamma_{n}\cap\operatorname{Core_{RF}}^{2}(\Gamma_{n+1}^{0})
, which we claim is
\operatorname{Core_{RF}}(\Gamma_{n})
.

It is obvious that
I_{n}
contains
\operatorname{Core_{RF}}(\Gamma_{n})
, so to prove the claim it is enough to argue that each

\gamma\in\Gamma_{n}\smallsetminus\operatorname{Core_{RF}}(\Gamma_{n})
survives in some finite quotient of
\operatorname{Core_{RF}}(\Gamma_{n+1}^{0})
. By definition,
there is a finite quotient
p_{\gamma}:\Gamma_{n}\to Q
with
p_{\gamma}(\gamma)\neq 1
, and we will be done if we can extend
p_{\gamma}

to the whole of
\operatorname{Core_{RF}}(\Gamma_{n+1}^{0})
. For this, we consider the components of the graph-of-groups obtained
when we delete the base vertex (where the local group is
\Gamma_{n}
) from
the tree of groups
\mathcal{T}
described above. There is one such component
C_{i}
of
\mathcal{T}

for each of the generators
a_{0},\dots,a_{m}
of
\Gamma_{n}
, and
\pi_{1}C_{i}<\pi_{1}\mathcal{T}=\operatorname{Core_{RF}}(\Gamma_{n+1}^{0})

intersects
\Gamma_{n}
in
\langle a_{i}\rangle
.
If
p_{\gamma}(a_{i})
has order
e_{i}
, we extend
p_{\gamma}
to
\pi_{1}C_{i}
by composing the surjection

h_{n+1}:\pi_{1}C_{i}\to\mathbb{Z}
(defined above)
with
\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{Z}/e_{i}\mathbb{Z}
. This completes the proof of the claim.

At this point, we have proved that
\operatorname{Core_{RF}}^{2}(\Gamma_{n+1}^{0})
is a free product
of (infinitely many) copies of
I_{n}=\operatorname{Core_{RF}}(\Gamma_{n})
and possibly a free group. By induction,

\operatorname{Depth_{RF}}(\Gamma_{n})=\omega\cdot n
, so
Lemma
1.7
tells us that
\operatorname{Depth_{RF}}(\operatorname{Core_{RF}}^{2}(\Gamma_{n+1}^{0}))=\omega\cdot(n-1)
. Thus

\operatorname{Depth_{RF}}(\Gamma_{n+1}^{0})=\omega\cdot(n+1)
. This completes the induction.
∎

Later, we shall need the following additional property of our construction.

Addendum 2.4
.

For
n\geq 3
, the subgroup

\operatorname{Core_{RF}}^{n-1}(\Gamma_{n})
is a free product of a free group and conjugates of
\operatorname{Core_{RF}}(\Gamma_{2})
.

Proof.

In the last paragraph of the preceding proof, we saw that
\operatorname{Core_{RF}}^{2}(\Gamma_{n+1}^{0})
was
a free product of copies of
\operatorname{Core_{RF}}(\Gamma_{n})
and a free group. The addendum follows by a simple induction.
For the inductive step, since
\Gamma_{n+1}=\Gamma_{n+1}^{0}\ast\mathbb{Z}
, we use Lemma
1.7
to see
that
\operatorname{Core_{RF}}(\Gamma_{n+1})
is the free product of copies of
\operatorname{Core_{RF}}(\Gamma_{n+1}^{0})
, hence

\operatorname{Core_{RF}}^{2}(\Gamma_{n+1})
is a free product of copies of
\operatorname{Core_{RF}}^{2}(\Gamma_{n+1}^{0})
, which
is a free product of copies of
\operatorname{Core_{RF}}(\Gamma_{n})
and a free group. Repeated application of
Lemma
1.7
then tells us that
\operatorname{Core_{RF}}^{n}(\Gamma_{n+1})
is a free product of
copies of
\operatorname{Core_{RF}}^{n-1}(\Gamma_{n})
.
∎

.

In the proof of Theorem
2.3
, it was convenient to name a specific

and

3.
Residual finiteness depth for countable groups

The purpose of this section is to prove the following result, a step towards Theorem
B
.

Proposition 3.1
.

For every countable limit ordinal
\alpha
,
there exists a countable group
G
with
\operatorname{Depth_{RF}}(G)=\alpha
.

3.1.
Wreath Products

The results in this subsection can be gleaned from
[
3
]
, but we present them in a way
that is convenient to our aims.
We use the standard notation
A\wr B
for the restricted wreath product, i.e. the semidirect product

P_{A,B}\rtimes B
where
P_{A,B}=\oplus_{b\in B}A_{b}
is the direct sum of copies of
A
indexed by
B
, with

B
acting by left-multiplication on the index set. We also need to consider more general
permutational
wreath products

A\wr_{I}C=P_{A,I}\rtimes C
,
where
P_{A,I}
is the direct sum of copies of
A
indexed by a set
I

and
C
acts by a homomorphism
C\to{\textrm{Perm}}(I)
. We shall only be interested in the setting
where the action of
C
on
I
is free, which is case
when
C
is a subgroup of
B
and the action is left multiplication on
B=I
; in this case,

A\wr_{B}C
is the subgroup of
A\wr B
generated by
P_{A,B}
and
C
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
A\wr_{I}C=P_{A,I}\rtimes C

be a permutational wreath product where the action of
C
on
I
is free.
If
A
is perfect and
C
is infinite, then
\operatorname{Core_{RF}}(A\wr_{I}C)=P_{A,I}\rtimes\operatorname{Core_{RF}}(C)
.

Proof.

For all
i\in I
and
x,y\in A_{i}
and any finite quotient
p:A\wr_{I}C\to Q

we have
p(c)=1
for some non-trivial
c\in C
. Writing
g^{c}
for conjugation of
g
by
c
,

p([x,y])=[p(x),p(y)]=[p(x),p(y)^{p(c)}]=[p(x),p(y^{c})]=p([x,y^{c}])=p(1)=1,

where the penultimate equality comes from the fact that
y^{c}\in A_{c\cdot i}
commutes with
x\in A_{i}

since
c\cdot i\neq i
. Thus
[A_{i},A_{i}]\subseteq\operatorname{Core_{RF}}(A\wr_{I}C)
for all
i
. But
A_{i}
is perfect,
so
A_{i}=[A_{i},A_{i}]
and
P_{A,I}\subseteq\operatorname{Core_{RF}}(A\wr_{I}C)
.

Conversely, if
g\notin P_{A,I}\rtimes\operatorname{Core_{RF}}(C)
, then
g
projects to
C\smallsetminus\operatorname{Core_{RF}}(C)
and therefore
survives in some finite quotient of
C
and hence of
A\wr_{I}C
.
∎

Arguing by transfinite induction, we deduce:

Corollary 3.3
.

For all ordinals
\alpha
, if
A
is perfect and
\operatorname{Core_{RF}}^{\beta}(B)
is infinite
for all
\beta<\alpha
, then
\operatorname{Core_{RF}}^{\alpha}(A\wr B)=P_{A,B}\rtimes\operatorname{Core_{RF}}^{\alpha}(B)
.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
A\neq 1
be a countable group that is perfect and let
B
be a countable group that is infinite
and
\beta
-residually finite for some
\beta
.

(1)

If
0pt(A)
is a limit ordinal, then

0pt(A\wr B)=0pt(B)+0pt(A).

(2)

If
0pt(A)
is a successor ordinal, then

0pt(A\wr B)=0pt(B)+0pt(A)+\omega.

(Note that in both cases,
0pt(A\wr B)
is a limit ordinal.)

Proof.

If
0pt(B)=\omega\cdot\alpha
or
\omega\cdot\alpha+1
, then
\operatorname{Core_{RF}}^{\alpha}(B)
is finite, so
\operatorname{Core_{RF}}^{\alpha}(A\wr B)

contains
P_{A,B}
as a subgroup of finite index, by Corollary
3.3
. Passing to a subgroup of
finite index does not affect the residual finiteness depth
of an infinite group, so
0pt(A\wr B)=0pt(B)+0pt(P_{A,B})
.

Lemma
1.8
tells us that
0pt(P_{A,B})=0pt(A)
if
0pt(A)
is a limit ordinal
and
0pt(P_{A,B})=0pt(A)+\omega
otherwise.
∎

Brody and Jankiewicz
[
3
]
proved that there are finitely generated groups with residual finiteness depth
\omega\cdot n

for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
by repeatedly applying the following consequence of Proposition
3.4
.

Corollary 3.5
.

If
\alpha
is the residual finiteness depth of a finitely generated (resp. countable) group, then so is
\alpha+\omega
.

Proof.

The assertion is trivial if
\alpha\in\{0,1\}
, so assume
\alpha
is infinite.
In Proposition
3.4
, take
A\neq 1
to be a finitely generated, perfect, residually finite
group and choose
B
with
0pt(B)=\alpha
.
∎

3.2.
Proof of Proposition
3.1

The trivial group has residual finiteness depth
0
,
for non-trivial finite groups
0pt(G)=1
, and for residually-finite groups that are infinite
0pt(G)=\omega
.
For other limit ordinals
\alpha
, we proceed by transfinite induction, assuming that for each limit ordinal
\beta<\alpha

there exists a group
G_{\beta}
with
0pt(G_{\beta})=\beta
. If
\alpha=\alpha_{0}+\omega
, then
we take a non-trivial finite perfect group
A
, define
G_{\alpha}=A\wr G_{\alpha_{0}}
and appeal to Corollary
3.3
.
If
\alpha
is not of this form, then it is the supremum of the (countably many) limit ordinals
\beta<\alpha
and we define

G_{\alpha}
to be either the free product or the direct sum of the inductively defined groups
G_{\beta}
corresponding to these ordinals.
Lemma
1.7
(3) and Lemma
1.8
(3) assure us that
0pt(G_{\alpha})=\alpha
in both cases. ∎

3.3.
Attaining some successor ordinals

Proposition 3.6
.

If
\alpha
is the residual finiteness depth of a finitely generated (resp. countable) group, then so is
\alpha+\omega+1
.

Proof.

Let
B
be a finitely generated group with
0pt(B)=\alpha
. (The countable case is entirely
similar.)
Let
A
be a finitely generated perfect group that has a finite central subgroup
Z=\operatorname{Core_{RF}}(A)\neq 1

– one of Deligne’s group
\Delta
from (
2.2
) will do nicely (with
d
odd and
g\geq 3
). Let
\overline{A}=A/Z
and
consider the central extension
E
of
\overline{A}\wr B
that is obtained from
A\wr B
by imposing relations
that identify the copy of
Z
in each direct factor of
P_{A,B}
with a single copy of
Z
. In other words,
E

is the quotient of
A\wr B
by the relations
(b^{-1}zb=z\mid\forall b\in B,\,z\in Z)
.
We have the central extension

1\to Z\to E\to\overline{A}\wr B\to 1

and the preimage in
E
of each summand
\overline{A}_{b}
in
P_{\overline{A},B}
is a copy of
A
.

If
\alpha=\omega\cdot\gamma
then as in Corollary
3.3
, we see that
\operatorname{Core_{RF}}^{\gamma}(E)
is the preimage
of
P_{\overline{A},B}<\overline{A}\wr B
. This preimage is the quotient of
P_{A,B}

obtained by identifying the central
Z_{b}<A_{b}
of the summands to a single
Z
. Thus, since

Z_{b}=\operatorname{Core_{RF}}(A_{b})
, the core of
\operatorname{Core_{RF}}^{\gamma}(E)
is
Z
. Therefore

0pt(E)=\alpha+0pt(\operatorname{Core_{RF}}^{\gamma}(E))=\alpha+\omega+1.

If
\alpha=\omega\cdot\gamma+1
, then we apply the preceding argument to
\omega\cdot\gamma
, noting that

\alpha+\omega+1=\omega\cdot\gamma+\omega+1
.
∎

Corollary 3.7
.

If
\alpha
is the residual finiteness depth of a finitely generated (resp. countable) group, then so
is
\alpha+\omega\cdot n+1
, for all positive
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

Apply Proposition
3.6
repeatedly, noting that

(\alpha+\omega\cdot n+1)+\omega+1=\alpha+\omega\cdot(n+1)+1
.
∎

The central idea of the proof of Proposition
3.6
applies more generally:
instead of looking for
A
and
Z=\operatorname{Core_{RF}}(A)
with
A/Z
residually finite, we could look for
A
and
Z

with
0pt(A/Z)=\beta
and
Z=\operatorname{Core_{RF}}^{\beta}(A)
. Then, arguing in the same manner, we obtain:

Proposition 3.8
.

If
\alpha
is the residual finiteness depth of a finitely generated (resp. countable) group
and
\beta+1
is the residual finiteness depth of a finitely generated (resp. countable) group
A

with
\operatorname{Core_{RF}}^{\beta}(A)<A
central, then there is a finitely generated (resp. countable) group
with residual finiteness depth
\alpha+\beta+1
.

By applying the proposition to the groups from Theorem
A
, we obtain the following result, which will
be subsumed into Theorem
B
.

Corollary 3.9
.

If
\alpha
is the residual finiteness depth of a finitely generated (resp. countable) group, then so
is
\alpha+\omega\cdot n+1
, for all positive
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

4.
Countable limit ordinals as residual finiteness depths

The purpose of this section is to prove the following result.

Theorem 4.1
.

For every countable limit ordinal
\alpha
,
there exists a finitely generated group
\Gamma
with
\operatorname{Depth_{RF}}(\Gamma)=\alpha
.

Our proof divides into the case
\alpha<\omega^{2}
, which is covered by Theorem
2.3
,
and the case
\alpha\geq\omega^{2}
. A key technical point in the second case is that
if
0pt(G)=\alpha
then
0pt(G)=0pt(\operatorname{Core_{RF}}(G))
, because

\omega+\alpha=\alpha
.

4.1.
Limit ordinals
\alpha\geq\omega^{2}

In this regime, Theorem
4.1

is an immediate consequence of Proposition
3.1
and the following controlled-embedding lemma.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
\Gamma
be a countable group and suppose that

0pt(\Gamma)\geq\omega^{2}
is a limit ordinal. Then, there exists a 3-generator group
\Gamma^{+}
and
an embedding
\Gamma\hookrightarrow\Gamma^{+}
such that
0pt(\Gamma)=0pt(\Gamma^{+})
.

At several points in the following proof we shall use the obvious fact that

H<K
implies
0pt(H)\leq 0pt(K)
.

Proof.

We list the elements of
\Gamma
without repetition,
\Gamma=\{g_{0}=1,g_{1},g_{2},\dots\}
.
Let
\Gamma_{0}=\Gamma\ast\mathbb{Z}
, fix a generator
z
for
\mathbb{Z}
,
and for each
i\in\mathbb{N}
define
a_{i}=zg_{i}
. Lemma
1.7
assures us that
0pt(\Gamma)=0pt(\Gamma_{0})
.
Note that each
a_{i}
has infinite order in
\Gamma_{0}
. Let
\Gamma_{1}
be the HNN extension
of
\Gamma_{0}
with infinitely many stable letters
t_{i}\ (i\geq 1)
, where
t_{i}
conjugates
a_{i}
to
a_{0}
:

\Gamma_{1}=(\Gamma_{0},\,t_{1},t_{2},\dots\mid t_{i}^{-1}a_{i}t_{i}=a_{0}\ \text{for }i\geq 1).

Note that
\Gamma_{1}
is generated by
\{a_{0},t_{1},t_{2},\dots\}
and that the
t_{i}
freely generate a free group

T
onto which
\Gamma_{1}
retracts with kernel
\langle\!\langle a_{0}\rangle\!\rangle
. We claim that
\operatorname{Core_{RF}}(\Gamma_{1})

is the free product of a free group and copies of
\Gamma_{0}\cap\operatorname{Core_{RF}}(\Gamma_{1})
. Then, as the depth of

\Gamma_{0}\cap\operatorname{Core_{RF}}(\Gamma_{1})
is at most
0pt(\Gamma_{0})
, using Lemma
1.7
we have

\begin{split}0pt(\Gamma_{1})&=\omega+0pt(\operatorname{Core_{RF}}(\Gamma_{1}))\\
&\leq\omega+0pt(\Gamma_{0})=\omega+0pt(\Gamma)=0pt(\Gamma),\end{split}

where the last equality holds because
0pt(\Gamma)\geq\omega^{2}
. As
\Gamma<\Gamma_{1}
,
we conclude that
0pt(\Gamma_{1})=0pt(\Gamma)
.

To prove the claim, consider the graph-of-groups decomposition
\mathbb{G}
of
\Gamma_{1}
corresponding to its HNN structure. Each
edge group is a conjugate of
\langle a_{0}\rangle
, so it intersects
\operatorname{Core_{RF}}(\Gamma_{1})
trivially because killing all of
the stable letters
t_{i}
retracts
\Gamma_{1}
onto
\langle a_{0}\rangle
. Therefore, the covering of
\mathbb{G}
with fundamental group

\operatorname{Core_{RF}}(\Gamma_{1})
has trivial edge groups and has vertex groups that are conjugates of
\Gamma_{0}\cap\operatorname{Core_{RF}}(\Gamma_{1})
.

For the next step in the construction,
we choose a free group of infinite rank
L
in
F_{2}={\textrm{Free}}(b,c)
and identify this
with
T
to form the amalgamated free product

\Gamma^{+}:=\Gamma_{1}\ast_{T=L}F_{2}.

Note that
\Gamma^{+}
is generated by
\{a_{0},b,c\}
. The retraction of
\Gamma_{1}
onto
T
extends to a retraction of
\Gamma^{+}

onto
F_{2}
, which is residually finite, so
\operatorname{Core_{RF}}(\Gamma^{+})
intersects each conjugate of
F_{2}
trivially, and hence is the
fundamental group of a graph of groups with trivial edge groups and vertex groups that are either trivial or conjugates
of
\operatorname{Core_{RF}}(\Gamma^{+})\cap\Gamma_{1}
. Thus
\operatorname{Core_{RF}}(\Gamma^{+})
is a free product of a free group and copies of
\operatorname{Core_{RF}}(\Gamma^{+})\cap\Gamma_{1}
.
As above, this implies

0pt(\Gamma^{+})=\omega+0pt(\operatorname{Core_{RF}}(\Gamma^{+}))\leq\omega+0pt(\Gamma_{1})=0pt(\Gamma_{1}).

Hence
0pt(\Gamma^{+})=0pt(\Gamma_{1})=0pt(\Gamma)
.
∎

5.
Finitely presented groups realising the ordinals
\omega\cdot n+1

In
[
3
, Question 5.4]
, Brody and Jankiewicz ask if there exist finitely presented groups
M_{n}
with
\operatorname{Depth_{RF}}(M_{n})=\omega\cdot n+1
. In this section we will see that the proof of Theorem
A

can be modified to arrange this.
For this construction, we need a central extension
of a residually finite group that has finite centre and
is not residually finite. We will use the group
\Delta
from (
2.2
) with
d
odd and
g\geq 3
fixed.

Theorem 5.1
.

For every positive integer
n
and odd integer
d\geq 3
, there exists a finitely presented group
M_{n}

with centre
Z(M_{n})\cong\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z}
such that
0pt(M_{n})=\omega\cdot n+1
.

Proof.

We follow the construction of
\Gamma_{n}
in Theorem
2.3
with a more careful choice of
\Gamma_{1}
and
\Gamma_{2}
.
Specifically, we
take
\Gamma_{1}^{0}={\textrm{Sp}}(2g,\mathbb{Z})
, for some fixed
g\geq 2
, then
\Gamma_{1}=\Gamma_{1}^{0}\ast\mathbb{Z}
; then we build
\Gamma_{2}
as described in
Remark
2.5
.
In the inductive step we define
\Gamma_{n+1}
with
0pt(\Gamma_{n+1})=\omega\cdot(n+1)
by
amalgamating
\Gamma_{n}
with
m+1
copies of
\Lambda
, as in the proof of Theorem
2.3
, to obtain
\Gamma_{n+1}^{0}

then take
\Gamma_{n+1}=\Gamma_{n+1}^{0}\ast\mathbb{Z}
. This family of groups comes with embeddings
\Gamma_{n}\hookrightarrow\Gamma_{n+1}
.
Addendum
2.4
tells us that
\operatorname{Core_{RF}}^{n-1}(\Gamma_{n})
is a free product of a free group and conjugates of
\operatorname{Core_{RF}}(\Gamma_{2})
,
and in Remark
2.5
we saw that
\operatorname{Core_{RF}}(\Gamma_{2})
is a residually finite group that contains conjugates of

\Gamma_{1}
. Thus
\operatorname{Core_{RF}}^{n-1}(\Gamma_{n})
is a residually finite group that contains conjugates of
\Gamma_{1}
.

With this structure in hand, we will be done if we can describe a central extension

1\to\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z}\to M_{n}\overset{p}{\to}\Gamma_{n}\to 1

that restricts over
{\textrm{Sp}}(2g,\mathbb{Z})=\Gamma_{1}^{0}<\Gamma_{n}
to Deligne’s extension (

), because this will tell us that

\operatorname{Core_{RF}}^{n-1}(M_{n})
is a central extension

1\to\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z}\to\operatorname{Core_{RF}}^{n-1}(M_{n})\overset{p}{\to}\operatorname{Core_{RF}}^{n-1}(\Gamma_{n})\to 1

\operatorname{Core_{RF}}^{n-1}(\Gamma_{n})

\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z}=\operatorname{Core_{RF}}(p^{-1}(\Gamma_{1}^{0}))=\operatorname{Core_{RF}}^{n}(M_{n})
.

To this end, we define
M_{1}
by amalgamating the group
{\Delta}

from (
2.2
) with
(\mathbb{Z}/d)\times\mathbb{Z}
, identifying the central subgroup
\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z}<\Delta
with

(\mathbb{Z}/d)\times\{1\}
.
Then,
proceeding by induction, assuming that the desired central extension
M_{n}
has been constructed
over
\Gamma_{n}
,
we define
M_{n+1}^{0}
to be the central extension of
\Gamma_{n+1}^{0}
that restricts to
M_{n}

over
\Gamma_{n}
and restricts to the trivial extension
(\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z})\times\Lambda
over each of the
m+1
copies of
\Lambda

attached to
\Gamma_{n}
in the proof of Theorem
2.3
;
we then identify the centre of
M_{n+1}^{0}
with
\mathbb{Z}/d\times\{1\}
to
amalgamate
M_{n+1}^{0}
with
(\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z})\times\mathbb{Z}
.
More explicitly, to obtain
M_{n+1}^{0}

we fix a generator
\zeta
for the central
\mathbb{Z}/d<M_{n}
, then amalgamate
M_{n}
with
m+1
copies of
(\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z})\times\Lambda
,
along copies of
(\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z})\times\mathbb{Z}

identifying
\zeta
with a generator of
(\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z})\times\{1\}
and
\tilde{a}_{i}
with the generator of
\operatorname{Core_{RF}}(\Lambda)
,
where
\{\tilde{a}_{0},\dots,\tilde{a}_{m}\}
are preimages in
M_{n}
of the generators

used in

∎

6.
Successors of limit ordinals

The use of the prime
3
in the following statement is arbitrary.

Theorem 6.1
.

For every countable limit ordinal
\alpha=\omega\cdot\gamma
there exists a finitely generated group
\widetilde{J}_{\alpha}
with

0pt(\widetilde{J}_{\alpha})=\alpha+1
and
\operatorname{Core_{RF}}^{\gamma}(\widetilde{J}_{\alpha})=Z(\widetilde{J}_{\alpha})\cong\mathbb{Z}/3
.

Proof.

We again proceed by transfinite induction.
The limit ordinals
\alpha<\omega^{2}
have the form
\omega\cdot n
,
and for these we can take the groups
M_{n}
constructed in Theorem
5.1
.

Thus we may assume that
\alpha\geq\omega^{2}
and we may assume that
\widetilde{J}_{\beta}
has
been constructed for limit ordinals
\beta<\alpha
. We shall again exploit the fact that

\alpha\geq\omega^{2}
implies
\omega+\alpha=\alpha
and hence
\operatorname{Core_{RF}}^{\alpha}(G)=\operatorname{Core_{RF}}^{\alpha}(\operatorname{Core_{RF}}(G))

for all countable groups
G
.

If
\alpha=\alpha_{0}+\omega
, we take a
finitely generated group with
0pt(B)=\alpha_{0}
and consider the quotient
E
of
\Delta\wr B

constructed in our proof of Proposition
3.6
. This group has centre
\mathbb{Z}/3
and

0pt(E)=\alpha+1
.

If
\alpha
is not of the form
\alpha_{0}+\omega
then
\alpha
is the supremum of
the limit ordinals
\beta<\alpha
and we define
\widetilde{\Gamma}^{\alpha}
to be the amalgamated free product of
the groups
\widetilde{J}_{\beta}
where the amalgamation identifies all of the centres
Z(\widetilde{J}_{\beta})\cong\mathbb{Z}/3
.
Let
J_{\beta}
be
\widetilde{J}_{\beta}
modulo its centre and note that
0pt(J_{\beta})=\beta
. We have a central extension

1\to\mathbb{Z}/3\to\widetilde{\Gamma}^{\alpha}\to\bigast_{\beta}J_{\beta}\to 1.

For each
\beta
, the natural retraction
\widetilde{\Gamma}^{\alpha}\to\widetilde{J}_{\beta}
maps
Z(\widetilde{\Gamma}^{\alpha})
onto
Z(\widetilde{J}_{\beta})
, hence

Z(\widetilde{\Gamma}^{\alpha})\subseteq\operatorname{Core_{RF}}^{\beta}(\widetilde{\Gamma}^{\alpha})
for all
\beta<\alpha
,
therefore
Z(\widetilde{\Gamma}^{\alpha})\subseteq\operatorname{Core_{RF}}^{\alpha}(\widetilde{\Gamma})
.
On the other hand,
\widetilde{\Gamma}^{\alpha}/Z(\widetilde{\Gamma}^{\alpha})
is the free product of the
J_{\beta}

and
0pt(J_{\beta})=\beta
, so Lemma
1.7
tells us that the depth of
\widetilde{\Gamma}^{\alpha}/Z(\widetilde{\Gamma}^{\alpha})

is
\alpha=\omega\cdot\gamma
.
Thus
\operatorname{Core_{RF}}^{\alpha}(\widetilde{\Gamma}^{\gamma})=Z(\widetilde{\Gamma}^{\alpha})\cong\mathbb{Z}/3
. At this point, we have proved the weaker form of the
theorem with “countable” in place of “finitely generated” and the remainder of the proof simply involves adapting
the proof of Proposition
4.2
so as to upgrade to finite generation.

To this end, we let
\widetilde{\Gamma}_{0}=\widetilde{\Gamma}^{\alpha}\ast_{\mathbb{Z}/3}((\mathbb{Z}/3)\times\mathbb{Z})
, where the amalgamation
identifies
Z(\widetilde{\Gamma}^{\alpha})
with
(\mathbb{Z}/3)\times 1
, and we fix generators
\{\zeta,a_{0},a_{1}\dots\}
for
\widetilde{\Gamma}_{0}
where

\zeta
generates
Z(\widetilde{\Gamma}^{\alpha})
and the
a_{i}
have infinite order. We then take the HNN extension
with infinitely many stable letters
t_{i}
, where
t_{i}
conjugates
a_{i}
to
a_{0}
and fixes
\zeta
:

\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1}=(\widetilde{\Gamma}_{0},t_{1},t_{2},\dots\mid t_{i}^{-1}a_{i}t_{i}=a_{0},\,[t_{i},\zeta]=1\ \forall i).

Note that
Z(\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1})=\langle\zeta\rangle\cong\mathbb{Z}/3
, and
\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1}
modulo its centre is the group
\Gamma_{1}

we had constructed at this stage of the proof of Proposition
4.2
.
Finally, we define
\widetilde{J}_{\alpha}
by taking a free group
F
of rank
2
and
amalgamating
\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1}
with
(\mathbb{Z}/3)\times F
by
identifying
\langle\zeta\rangle
with
(\mathbb{Z}/3)\times 1
and identifying an infinitely generated subgroup of

F
with the free subgroup
\langle t_{1},t_{2},\dots\rangle
.
By construction,
Z(\widetilde{J}_{\alpha})=\langle\zeta\rangle\cong\mathbb{Z}/3
and
\widetilde{J}_{\alpha}/Z(\widetilde{J}_{\alpha})

is the group
\Gamma^{+}
we had in proof of Proposition
4.2
. In particular,

0pt(\widetilde{J}_{\alpha}/Z(\widetilde{J}_{\alpha}))=\alpha
. By construction,
\zeta\in\operatorname{Core_{RF}}^{\alpha}(\widetilde{\Gamma}^{\alpha})
, so

\operatorname{Core_{RF}}(\widetilde{J}_{\alpha})=\langle\zeta\rangle\cong\mathbb{Z}/3
and
0pt(\widetilde{J}_{\alpha})=\alpha+1
. This completes the induction.
∎

A new idea is needed to answer the following question.

Question 6.2
.

Which countable ordinals arise as the residual finiteness depths of finitely presented groups?
Might all limit ordinals less than
\omega^{\omega}
and their successors arise in this way, for example?

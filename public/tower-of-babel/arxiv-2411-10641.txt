On holomorphicity of Hartogs series satisfying algebraic relations
Hiroki Aoki, Kyoji Saito

Abstract
We consider a formal power series in one variable whose coefficients are holomorphic functions in a given multidimensional complex domain. Assume the following two conditions on the series. (C1) The restriction of the series at each point of a dense subset of the domain converges in an open disk of a fixed radius. (C2) The series is algebraic over the ring of holomorphic functions on the direct product space of the domain and the disk. The main theorem of the present note is that the series defines a holomorphic function on the direct product space. We also give an example where the condition (C2) is essentially necessary.



1
Introduction and Main Theorem

In the present note,
for a multidimensional complex domain
U
and
{\boldsymbol{u}}\in U
,
\mathop{\mathcal{O}}\nolimits(U)
denotes
the ring of holomorphic functions on
U
and
\mathop{\mathcal{O}}_{{\boldsymbol{u}}}
denotes
the ring of germs of holomorphic functions at
{\boldsymbol{u}}
.
We write the open disk of a radius

centered at the origin
as
D(r):=\{\;w\in{\mathbb{C}}\;|\;|w|<r\;\}
.
Let
D_{1}\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{N}
be a domain.
Let
R
be a positive real number or
+\infty
and
D_{2}:=D(R)
.
We write
D:=D_{1}\times D_{2}
.

The objective of the present note is a formal power series,
which we call Hartogs series,

F({\boldsymbol{z}},w):=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}f_{n}({\boldsymbol{z}})w^{n}\in\mathop{\mathcal{O}}\nolimits(D_{1})[[w]],

(1)

where
f_{n}\in\mathop{\mathcal{O}}\nolimits(D_{1})
.
In the present note,
we regard
\mathop{\mathcal{O}}\nolimits(D)
as a subring of
\mathop{\mathcal{O}}\nolimits(D_{1})[[w]]
by
the power series expansion with respect to
w
.
Thus, we have a question: “Find a good condition for
F

given by (
1
) to belong to
\mathop{\mathcal{O}}\nolimits(D)
.”
It is known that
“If
F|_{{\boldsymbol{z}}={\boldsymbol{\alpha}}}\in\mathop{\mathcal{O}}\nolimits(D_{2})
for all
{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\in D_{1}
,
then there exists a nowhere dense closed set
A\in D_{1}
such
that
F\in\mathop{\mathcal{O}}\nolimits((D_{1}\setminus A)\times D_{2})
”
is true (
[
6
]
*Proposition 1), however,
it may occur the case
F\not\in\mathop{\mathcal{O}}\nolimits(D)
even under this condition.
Several additional conditions
which guarantee that
F\in\mathop{\mathcal{O}}\nolimits(D)
were given
(cf.
[
6
]
).

In the present note, we consider this question
from the view point of algebraicity of
F
over
\mathop{\mathcal{O}}\nolimits(D)
.
Our motivation is from the theory of automorphic forms.
Some kinds of automorphic forms of several variables have
Fourier-Jacobi series expansion.
Then, the converse: “Whether a given formal Fourier-Jacobi series
with formal automorphicity converges or not?” is
an interesting problem.
There are many researches about this.
First, in 2000, the above converse problem
was shown to be true by the first author in the case of
Siegel modular forms of degree
2
with
respect to the full modular group
(cf.
[
2
]
).
Since then,
this problem has been shown to be true in various cases.
For example, Ibukiyama, Poor and Yuen extended
the above author’s result to Siegel paramodular forms
of degree
2
with level
\leqq 4
(cf.
[
4
]
).
Bruinier and Raum proved a modularity of symmetric formal
Fourier-Jacobi series in arbitrary degree
under a suitable assumptions (cf.
[
3
]
).
Very recently, the first author, Ibukiyama and Poor
proved a modularity of symmetric formal
Fourier-Jacobi series for Siegel paramodular forms
of degree
2
with arbitrary level (cf.
[
1
]
).
This result implies that
the algebraicity is important to show the convergence.
The aim of the present note is to prepare a general theorem
which can be applied to the above problem.

Here we consider the following two conditions:

(C1)

There exists a dense subset
S
of
D_{1}
such
that
F|_{{\boldsymbol{z}}={\boldsymbol{\alpha}}}
converges
and
F|_{{\boldsymbol{z}}={\boldsymbol{\alpha}}}\in\mathop{\mathcal{O}}\nolimits(D_{2})
for all
{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\in S
.

(C2)

F
is algebraic over
\mathop{\mathcal{O}}\nolimits(D)
.
Namely, there exists a nontrivial polynomial

\Phi({\boldsymbol{z}},w,X):=\sum_{j=0}^{t}\Phi_{j}({\boldsymbol{z}},w)X^{t-j}\in\mathop{\mathcal{O}}\nolimits(D)[X]\quad\bigl(\;t\geqq 1,\;\Phi_{0}\not\equiv 0\;\bigr)

(2)

such that
\Phi({\boldsymbol{z}},w,F({\boldsymbol{z}},w))\equiv 0
holds as an equality
on
\mathop{\mathcal{O}}\nolimits(D_{1})[[w]]
.

The main theorem of the present note is as follows:

Theorem 1
.

Let
F
be
a Hartogs series given by
(
1
)
and
the conditions
(C1)
and
(C2)
hold.
Then
F
converges in
D
and
F\in\mathop{\mathcal{O}}\nolimits(D)
.

Remark 2
.

The condition
(C2)
is essentially necessary
in
Theorem
1
.

Remark 3
.

The condition
(C2)
alone already gives
some weaker holomorphicity result.
For every point
({\boldsymbol{z}}_{0},0)\in D
,
the formal series defines an element
F_{{\boldsymbol{z}}_{0}}
of
the completion
\hat{A}={\mathbb{C}}[[{\boldsymbol{z}}-{\boldsymbol{z}}_{0},w]]
of
the local ring
A={\mathbb{C}}\{{\boldsymbol{z}}-{\boldsymbol{z}}_{0},w\}
of
D
at
({\boldsymbol{z}}_{0},0)
.
As a ring of convergent power series with complex coefficients,
the ring

is a local integral domain
which is henselian and excellent.
By
[
3
]
*Proposition 4.2,
which is essentially a result from commutative algebra,
the ring

is algebraically closed in

.
By the condition

of the present note,
the formal series

is algebraic over

.
This implies that
F_{{\boldsymbol{z}}_{0}}
is already contained in

and
therefore convergent and holomorphic
in a neighborhood of
({\boldsymbol{z}}_{0},0)
.
Therefore
F
is holomorphic
in a neighborhood of
D_{1}\times\{0\}
.
¿From this point of view,
the condition
(C1)
is needed to be able to
holomorphically continue

to all of
\mathop{\mathcal{O}}\nolimits(D)
.

We give a proof of
Theorem
1
in the next section
and then we give an explicit example
which provides
Remark
2
in the last section.
Regarding the basics of function theory of several complex variables,
we referred to some standard textbooks (e.g.
[
5
]
).

Proof of the main theorem

F\in\mathop{\mathcal{O}}\nolimits(U\times D_{2})
.
∎

Step 2.

Now,

F
be a Hartogs series given by
(
1
)
and

Let
F^{*}({\boldsymbol{z}},w):=\Phi_{0}({\boldsymbol{z}},w)F({\boldsymbol{z}},w)\in\mathop{\mathcal{O}}\nolimits(D_{1})[[w]]

\Phi^{*}({\boldsymbol{z}},w,F^{*}({\boldsymbol{z}},w))\equiv 0

.

Let
F

E_{\Phi_{0}}:=\{\;{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\in D_{1}\;|\;\Phi_{0}|_{{\boldsymbol{z}}={\boldsymbol{\alpha}}}\equiv 0\}
.
We remark that
E_{\Phi_{0}}

a nontrivial analytic subset of

.
First we show that

,
where

.
Let
({\boldsymbol{\beta}},c)\in D^{\prime}
be
a singular point of

,

({\boldsymbol{\beta}},c)

We take

,
which is an open neighborhood of

Continuing from the previous step,

Example

In this section, we show that
the condition (C2) is essentially necessary
in Theorem
1
.
Namely, we give a Hartogs series
F
which
satisfies (C1) but does not converge
in
D\setminus S
.
For simplicity, we only treat the case
N=1
.

Let
{\mathbb{H}}:=\{\;\tau\in{\mathbb{C}}\;|\;\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\nolimits\tau>0\;\}

be the upper half complex plane.
It is well known that the elliptic theta function

\theta_{11}(z;\tau):=\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\exp\left(\pi\sqrt{-1}\left(\left(m+\tfrac{1}{2}\right)^{2}\tau+2\left(m+\tfrac{1}{2}\right)\left(z+\tfrac{1}{2}\right)\right)\right)\in\mathop{\mathcal{O}}\nolimits({\mathbb{C}}\times{\mathbb{H}})

has the following properties:

•

\theta_{11}(z;\tau)=0
if and only if
z\in{\mathbb{Z}}\tau+{\mathbb{Z}}
.

•

\theta_{11}(z;\tau)=\exp\left(\pi\sqrt{-1}\left(a^{2}\tau+2az\right)\right)\theta_{11}(z+a\tau+b;\tau)
for any
a,b\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.

•

For any
\tau_{0}\in{\mathbb{H}}
,
\theta_{11}|_{\tau=\tau_{0}}
has a zero of order
1
at the origin
z=0
.

Now we fix a point
\tau_{0}\in{\mathbb{H}}
and
consider the case

f_{n}(z)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}n^{n}\theta_{11}(n!z;\tau_{0})&(n\geqq 1)\\[4.0pt]
0&(n=0)\end{array}\right..

Then, as for

F(z,w):=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}f_{n}(z)w^{n}\in\mathop{\mathcal{O}}\nolimits({\mathbb{C}})[[w]],

the following two properties hold.

•

F|_{z=\alpha}(w)
converges and is holomorphic on
{\mathbb{C}}

if
\alpha\in{\mathbb{Q}}\tau_{0}+{\mathbb{Q}}
.

•

F|_{z=\alpha}(w)
does not converge
if
\alpha\not\in{\mathbb{Q}}\tau_{0}+{\mathbb{Q}}
.

The former one is clear, actually,
F|_{z=\alpha}(w)\in\mathop{\mathcal{O}}\nolimits({\mathbb{C}})[w]
.
To show the latter one, we need to prepare some lemmas.
For any
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
, we define

e(x):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}[x]&(\;x-[x]\leqq 1/2\;)\\[4.0pt]

Lemma 8

inductively.
Then we have a multiple factoradic representation

and there exist infinitely many
n

, since

If

Lemma 10

There exists a positive constant

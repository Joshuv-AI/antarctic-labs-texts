Concentration of cones in the Alt-Phillips problem
Ovidiu Savin, Hui Yu

Abstract
. We study minimizing cones in the Alt-Phillips problem when the exponent γ \gamma is close to 1 1 . When γ \gamma converges to 1 1 , we show that the cones concentrate around symmetric solutions to the classical obstacle problem. To be precise, the limiting profiles are radial in a subspace and invariant in directions perpendicular to that subspace.



1.
Introduction

For an exponent
\gamma\in[0,2)
, the
Alt-Phillips problem
studies minimizers of the following energy

(1.1)

\mathcal{E}_{\gamma}(u;\Omega):=\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{2}+u^{\gamma}\chi_{\{u>0\}},

where
\Omega
is a domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and
u
is a
non-negative
function.

Since its introduction by Phillips
[
P
]
and Alt-Phillips
[
AP
]
, this problem has received intense attention in the past few decades. See, for instance,
[
AS
,
B
,
DS
,
FeRo
,
FeY
,
FeY2
,
KSP
,
WY
,
WaY
]
. For a minimizer
u
, the focus is on regularity properties of its
free boundary

\partial\{u>0\}
, separating the
positive set

\{u>0\}
from the
contact set

\{u=0\}
. Thanks to Weiss
[
W
]
, the infinitesimal behavior of the free boundary is given by homogeneous minimizers, known as
minimizing cones
.

The simplest cone is the
flat cone
, the one-dimensional minimizer of (
1.1
) that vanishes on a half space (see (
2.3
)). If a minimizer
u
is close to the flat cone, then its free boundary
\partial\{u>0\}
is smooth
[
AP
,
D
,
DS
,
ReRo
]
. For certain ranges of
d
and
\gamma
, the only minimizing cone is the flat cone
[
AP
,
CJK
,
JS
]
.

In general, there are non-flat minimizing cones for (
1.1
), known as
singular cones

[
DJ
,
SY2
]
. They provide singularity models for this problem. Unfortunately, very little is known about these cones. As a result, we face serious challenges in the study of singular free boundary points for general
\gamma
.

The exception is when
\gamma=1
, corresponding to the
classical obstacle problem
. For this problem, homogeneous solutions are fully classified
[
C
,
PSU
]
. This leads to a lot of developments on the singular part of the free boundary
[
C
,
CSV
,
FRoSe
,
FSe
,
L
,
M
,
SY1
]
.

To be precise, cones in the classical obstacle problem belong to two families (see Proposition
2.7
). One family consists of flat cones, known as
half-space solutions
in this problem. They are of the form

(1.2)

\frac{1}{2}[(x\cdot e)_{+}]^{2}\hskip 5.0pt\text{ for some unit vector }e.

The other family consists of singular cones, known as
parabola solutions
. These solutions are of the form

(1.3)

\frac{1}{2}x\cdot Ax,\text{ where }A\geq 0\text{ and }\mathrm{trace}(A)=1.

Note that half-space solutions and parabola solutions each form a connected family. Even modulo rotational symmetry, there are infinitely many parabola solutions. We also remark that the contact set of any parabola solution, being a proper subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, has zero measure. See, for instance, Proposition
2.2
.

To understand cones in the Alt-Phillips problem (
1.1
), it is natural to first consider the situation when
\gamma
is close to
1
.

Recently, we constructed axially symmetric cones to (
1.1
), when
\gamma<1
is close to
1
and
d\geq 4

[
SY2
]
. For each
\gamma
in this range, there is a cone whose contact set has zero measure, similar to parabola solutions in the obstacle problem (see (
1.3
)). Interestingly, there is also a cone whose contact set has positive measure, similar to the De Silva-Jerison cone
[
DJ
]
in the Alt-Caffarelli problem (
\gamma=0
). This is the first free boundary problem that allows both possibilities.

This shows that singular cones for (
1.1
), even when
\gamma
is close to
1
, exhibit rich and complicated behavior. The first step in understanding these cones is to study their limiting behavior when
\gamma
converges to
1
.

As
\gamma\to 1
, minimizing cones for (
1.1
) concentrate around cones for the obstacle problem (see Proposition
2.8
). Surprisingly, only cones with strong symmetric properties can show up as limits of cones to (
1.1
) when
\gamma\to 1.

To be precise, for
k\in\{0,1,\dots,d\}
, we define

(1.4)

P_{0}(x):=\frac{1}{2}[(x_{1})_{+}]^{2},

and

(1.5)

P_{k}(x):=\frac{1}{2k}\sum_{1\leq j\leq k}x_{j}^{2}\hskip 5.0pt\text{ for }k=1,2,\dots,d.

Our
main result
reads

Theorem 1.1
.

Given
\varepsilon>0
, there is
\eta>0
, depending only on
\varepsilon
and the dimension
d
, such that if
u
is a minimizing cone for the Alt-Phillips problem (
1.1
) with

\gamma\in(1-\eta,1)\cup(1,1+\eta),

then, up to a rotation, we have

\|u-P_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}<\varepsilon

for some
k\in\{0,1,\dots,d\}.

Remark 1.2
.

To the knowledge of the authors, this is the first result on concentration-induced symmetry in free boundary problems.

The cones in (
1.5
) are fully radial in a subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and invariant along directions perpendicular to this subspace.

For each integer
k\in\{0,1,\dots,d\}
, up to a rotation, there is exactly one cone,
P_{k}
, that can show up as a limiting profile with

\mathrm{dim}(\{P_{k}=0\})=d-k.

The heart of Theorem
1.1
is the following integral inequality on parabola solutions in (
1.3
). This inequality is stable in the natural topology of our problem (see Lemma
4.1
), and the equality case captures cones with symmetry from (
1.5
).

Lemma 1.3
.

Suppose that
p
is a parabola solution to the classical obstacle problem in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
from (
1.3
), then

\int_{\partial B_{1}}\frac{|\nabla p|^{2}}{p}(p-\frac{1}{2d}|x|^{2})\geq 0.

The equality holds if and only if, up to a rotation,

p=P_{k}

for some
k=1,2,\dots,d
from (
1.5
).

This paper is
structured as follows
: In Section
2
, we collect some preliminaries on the Alt-Phillips problem (
1.1
) and introduce some notations. Section
3
is devoted to the proof of Lemma
1.3
. With this, we prove Theorem
1.1
in Section
4
.

2.1.
The Alt-Phillips functional

Recall the Alt-Phillips functional
\mathcal{E}_{\gamma}

is close to
1

\gamma\in[1/2,3/2].

Suppose that

in

(see
[

(2.1)

\Delta u=\gamma u^{\gamma-1}\chi_{\{u>0\}},\hskip 5.0ptu\geq 0\hskip 5.0pt\text{ in }B_{1}.

For each

as

(2.2)

\beta:=\frac{2}{2-\gamma}=2-\frac{2(1-\gamma)}{2-\gamma}.

When

is close to

The scaling parameter arises from the symmetry of

\mathcal{E}_{\gamma}(u_{r};B_{1})=r^{2-2\beta-d}\mathcal{E}_{\gamma}(u;B_{r}).

In particular, if

is a non-trivial minimizing cone for
\mathcal{E}_{\gamma}
in (
1.1
), then

must be
\beta
-homogeneous
. As a consequence, the flat cone for
\mathcal{E}_{\gamma}
is of the form

(2.3)

u(x)=c_{\gamma}[(x\cdot e)_{+}]^{\beta}\text{ for some unit vector}\hskip 5.0pte,

where the coefficient
c_{\gamma}
satisfies

c_{\gamma}^{2-\gamma}=(2-\gamma)^{2}/2.

Compactness of minimizers follows from the following:

Theorem 2.1

(See
[
AP
]
)
.

Suppose that
u
is a minimizer of
\mathcal{E}_{\gamma}
from (
1.1
) in

If
0\in\partial\{u>0\}

such that

(1)

If

\|u\|_{C^{1,\beta-1}(B_{1/2})}\leq C_{d}

(2)

If

\|u\|_{C^{2,\beta-2}(B_{1/2})}\leq C_{d}.

Moreover, we have

\sup_{B_{1/2}\cap\{u>0\}}|\nabla u|^{2}/u^{\gamma}+\sup_{B_{1/2}\cap\{u>0\}}|D^{2}u|/u^{\gamma-1}\leq C_{d}.

Recall the parameter
\beta
from (
2.2

When

the following dichotomy is a consequence of the convexity of global solutions:

(

3.
An integral inequality

This section is devoted to the proof of Lemma
1.3
.

The following allows us to perform an induction on the dimension
d
:

Lemma 3.1
.

For
d\geq 2,
suppose that
p\in\mathcal{P}
is invariant in the
e_{d}
-direction, that is,

p(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{d})=q(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{d-1}),

for some
q\in\mathcal{P}
in
\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
.

Then

\int_{\partial B_{1}}\frac{|\nabla p|^{2}}{p}(p-\frac{1}{2d}|x|^{2})d\mathcal{H}^{d-1}=\alpha_{d}\int_{\partial B_{1}\cap\{x_{d}=0\}}\frac{|\nabla q|^{2}}{q}\cdot(q-\frac{1}{2(d-1)}|(x_{1},x_{2},\dots x_{d-1})|^{2})d\mathcal{H}^{d-2}

for a positive dimensional constant
\alpha_{d}
.

Recall the space of parabola solutions
\mathcal{P}
from Definition
2.6
.

Proof.

Introduce a variable
\theta\in[-\pi/2,\pi/2]
such that

x_{d}=\sin(\theta)\text{ on }\partial B_{1},

then we have

(3.1)

\int_{\partial B_{1}}\frac{|\nabla p|^{2}}{p}(p-\frac{1}{2d}|x|^{2})=\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}\int_{\partial B_{1}\cap\{\theta=s\}}\frac{|\nabla p|^{2}}{p}(p-\frac{1}{2d})dH^{d-2}ds

Along
\partial B_{1}\cap\{\theta=s\}
, since
p
is independent of
x_{d}
and is
2
-homogeneous, we have

\frac{|\nabla p|^{2}}{p}|_{\theta=s}=\frac{|\nabla q|^{2}}{q}|_{\theta=0},\hskip 5.0pt\text{ and }\hskip 5.0ptp|_{\theta=s}=q|_{\theta=0}\cdot\cos^{2}(s).

As a result, we have

\int_{\partial B_{1}\cap\{\theta=s\}}\frac{|\nabla p|^{2}}{p}(p-\frac{1}{2d})dH^{d-2}=\int_{\partial B_{1}\cap\{\theta=0\}}\frac{|\nabla q|^{2}}{q}\cdot(q\cos^{2}(s)-\frac{1}{2d})\cos^{d-2}(s)dH^{d-2}.

Putting this into (
3.1
), we get

\displaystyle\int_{\partial B_{1}}\frac{|\nabla p|^{2}}{p}(p-\frac{1}{2d}|x|^{2})

\displaystyle=\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}\cos^{d}(s)ds\cdot\int_{\partial B_{1}\cap\{\theta=0\}}\frac{|\nabla q|^{2}}{q}q

\displaystyle-\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}\cos^{d-2}(s)ds\cdot\int_{\partial B_{1}\cap\{\theta=0\}}\frac{|\nabla q|^{2}}{q}\frac{1}{2d}.

With the recursive formula
\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}\cos^{d}(s)ds=\frac{d-1}{d}\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}\cos^{d-2}(s)ds
, we conclude

\int_{\partial B_{1}}\frac{|\nabla p|^{2}}{p}(p-\frac{1}{2d}|x|^{2})=\frac{d-1}{d}\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}\cos^{d-2}(s)ds\cdot\int_{\partial B_{1}\cap\{x_{d}=0\}}\frac{|\nabla q|^{2}}{q}\cdot(1-\frac{1}{2(d-1)}).

∎

Recall the radial solution
P_{d}
from (
1.5
)

(3.2)

P_{d}(x)=\frac{1}{2d}|x|^{2}.

To connect
p\in\mathcal{P}
(see Definition
2.6
) to a solution that is degenerate along an axis, define

(3.3)

p_{t}(x):=tp(x)+(1-t)P_{d}(x)\hskip 5.0pt\text{ for }t\geq 0.

As long as
p_{t}\geq 0
, we have

p_{t}\in\mathcal{P}.

In particular, this holds for all
t\in[0,1].

Correspondingly, we define

(3.4)

Q(t):=\int_{\partial B_{1}}\frac{|\nabla p_{t}|^{2}}{p_{t}}(p_{t}-\frac{1}{2d}|x|^{2})\hskip 5.0pt\text{ as long as }p_{t}\geq 0.

Following Proposition
2.4
, the integrand is bounded by a dimensional constant.

To prove Lemma
1.3
is to show that
Q(1)\geq 0
, and to characterize the equality case.

We give an alternative expression of this quantity
Q
:

Lemma 3.2
.

For
p\in\mathcal{P}
, let
p_{t}
and
Q
be as in (
3.3
) and (
3.4
). Then we have

Q(t)=t^{2}\cdot[\int_{\partial B_{1}}|\nabla_{\tau}p|^{2}+4\int_{\partial B_{1}}(p-P_{d})^{2}-\frac{1}{2d}\int_{\partial B_{1}}\frac{|\nabla_{\tau}p|^{2}}{p_{t}}]

as long as
p_{t}\geq 0.

Here
\nabla_{\tau}
denotes the gradient along directions tangential to
\partial B_{1}
.

Recall the class
\mathcal{P}
from Definition
2.6
. Recall the radial solution
P_{d}
from (
3.2
).

Proof.

With (
3.4
), we have

(3.5)

Q(t)=\int_{\partial B_{1}}|\nabla p_{t}|^{2}-\frac{1}{2d}\int_{\partial B_{1}}\frac{|\nabla p_{t}|^{2}}{p_{t}}.

Note that
|\nabla p_{t}|^{2}=|\frac{\partial}{\partial r}p_{t}|^{2}+|\nabla_{\tau}p_{t}|^{2}
.
With the homogeneity of
p_{t}
, we see that
\frac{\partial}{\partial r}p_{t}=2p_{t}
on
\partial B_{1}
. With (

|\nabla_{\tau}p_{t}|=t|\nabla_{\tau}p|
. As a result, we have

\displaystyle|\nabla p_{t}|^{2}

\displaystyle=4p_{t}^{2}+t^{2}|\nabla_{\tau}p|^{2}

\displaystyle=4p_{t}P_{d}+4tp_{t}(p-P_{d})+t^{2}|\nabla_{\tau}p|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{4}{2d}p_{t}+4tp_{t}(p-P_{d})+t^{2}|\nabla_{\tau}p|^{2}\hskip 5.0pt\text{ on }\partial B_{1},

and

\frac{|\nabla p_{t}|^{2}}{p_{t}}=4p_{t}+t^{2}\frac{|\nabla_{\tau}p|^{2}}{p_{t}}\hskip 5.0pt\text{ on }\partial B_{1}.

Putting these into (

Q(t)=t^{2}\cdot[\int_{\partial B_{1}}|\nabla_{\tau}p|^{2}-\frac{1}{2d}\int_{\partial B_{1}}\frac{|\nabla_{\tau}p|^{2}}{p_{t}}]+4t\int_{\partial B_{1}}p_{t}(p-P_{d}).

From (

p_{t}=tp+\frac{1}{2d}(1-t)
on

Thus the last integral in (

Combining this with (

.
∎

In this case, define

Moreover, direct computation with (

Together with (

4.
Concentration around cones with symmetry

In this section, we give the proof of our main result, Theorem
1.1
.

The first ingredient is the stability of the quantity in Lemma
1.3
:

Lemma 4.1
.

Suppose
u\in\mathcal{K}(\gamma)
with
\gamma\in[\frac{1}{2},1)\cup(1,\frac{3}{2}]
satisfies

(4.1)

\|u-p\|_{\mathcal{L}^{\infty}(B_{1})}<\frac{1}{4d}

for some
p\in\mathcal{P}
.

Then

\int_{\partial B_{1}}\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{u}(p-\frac{1}{2d}|x|^{2})\leq C_{d}\|u-p\|_{\mathcal{L}^{\infty}(B_{1})}

for a dimensional constant
C_{d}
.

Recall the spaces of cones,
\mathcal{K}(\gamma)
and
\mathcal{P}
, from Definition
2.3
and Definition
2.6
.

Proof.

Step 1: Consequence of Green’s identity.

With the
2
-homogeneity of
u
,
p
and
\frac{1}{2d}|x|^{2}
, and the harmonicity of
(p-\frac{1}{2d}|x|^{2})
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}}(p-\frac{1}{2d}|x|^{2})\Delta(u-p)

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial B_{1}}(p-\frac{1}{2d}|x|^{2})\nabla(u-p)\cdot\nu-(u-p)\nabla(p-\frac{1}{2d}|x|^{2})\cdot\nu

\displaystyle=2\int_{\partial B_{1}}(p-\frac{1}{2d}|x|^{2})(u-p)-(u-p)(p-\frac{1}{2d}|x|^{2})

\displaystyle=0.

With Proposition
2.4
and the definition of
\mathcal{P}
, the difference
(u-p)
solves

(4.2)

\Delta(u-p)=-\chi_{\{u=0\}}+\frac{2-\beta}{2}\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{u}\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Combining these two equations and using the homogeneity of the functions, we have

(4.3)

\frac{2-\beta}{2}\int_{\partial B_{1}}\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{u}(p-\frac{1}{2d}|x|^{2})=\int_{\partial B_{1}}(p-\frac{1}{2d}|x|^{2})\chi_{\{u=0\}}.

Step 2: The case when
\gamma>1
.

In this case, Proposition
2.5
implies that either
u=\frac{2-\gamma}{2\gamma}[(x\cdot e)_{+}]^{2}
or
\{u=0\}
has zero measure. The former possibility is ruled out by (
4.1
). Combining this with (
4.3
) and
\beta\neq 2
(see (
2.2
)), we get

\int_{\partial B_{1}}\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{u}(p-\frac{1}{2d}|x|^{2})=0,

completing the proof in this case.

Step 3: The case when
\gamma<1
.

With (
4.1
), we have
p<\frac{1}{4d}
on
\{u=0\}\cap B_{1}
. With the bound on
|\nabla u|^{2}/u
in Proposition
2.4
, we use (
4.3
) to get

\int_{\partial B_{1}}\chi_{\{u=0\}}\leq C_{d}|2-\beta|.

Together with (
4.3
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{2-\beta}{2}\int_{\partial B_{1}}\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{u}(p-\frac{1}{2d}|x|^{2})

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\partial B_{1}}p\chi_{\{u=0\}}

\displaystyle\leq\|u-p\|_{\mathcal{L}^{\infty}(B_{1})}\int_{\partial B_{1}}\chi_{\{u=0\}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{d}|2-\beta|\cdot\|u-p\|_{\mathcal{L}^{\infty}(B_{1})}.

From (
2.2
), we see that
2-\beta>0
in this case. Thus

\int_{\partial B_{1}}\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{u}(p-\frac{1}{2d}|x|^{2})\leq C_{d}\|u-p\|_{\mathcal{L}^{\infty}(B_{1})}.

∎

As a consequence, we have

Corollary 4.2
.

For
\gamma_{n}\neq 1
with
\gamma_{n}\to 1
, suppose that a sequence
u_{n}\in\mathcal{K}(\gamma_{n})
satisfies

u_{n}\to p\text{ locally uniformly in }\mathbb{R}^{d}\text{ for some }p\in\mathcal{P}.

Then
p\in\mathcal{SP}
.

Recall the spaces of cones,
\mathcal{K}(\gamma)
,
\mathcal{P}
and
\mathcal{SP}
, from Definition
2.3
and Definition
2.6
.

Proof.

With Proposition
2.4
, we see that
u_{n}\to p
locally uniformly in
C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

With
p\in\mathcal{P}
, we see that, up to a rotation, we have

p\geq\frac{1}{2d}x_{1}^{2}.

Given
\eta>0
, for large
n
, we have
u_{n}(x)>\frac{1}{4d}\eta^{2}
when
|x_{1}|\geq\eta
.

As a result, we have

\int_{\partial B_{1}\cap\{|x_{1}|\geq\eta\}}\frac{|\nabla p|^{2}}{p}(p-\frac{1}{2d}|x|^{2})=\lim\int_{\partial B_{1}\cap\{|x_{1}|\geq\eta\}}\frac{|\nabla u_{n}|^{2}}{u_{n}}(p-\frac{1}{2d}|x|^{2}).

Meanwhile, Proposition
2.4
implies that the integrals on
\partial B_{1}\cap\{|x_{1}|\leq\eta\}
is controlled by
C_{d}\eta
for some dimensional constant
C_{d}
. Thus we have

\int_{\partial B_{1}}\frac{|\nabla p|^{2}}{p}(p-\frac{1}{2d}|x|^{2})\leq\limsup\int_{\partial B_{1}}\frac{|\nabla u_{n}|^{2}}{u_{n}}(p-\frac{1}{2d}|x|^{2})+C_{d}\eta\leq C_{d}\eta.

The last inequality follows from Lemma
4.1
.

Sending
\eta\to 0
, the conclusion follows from the equality case in Lemma
1.3
.
∎

With this, we give the proof of our main result:

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Suppose the theorem is false, then for some
\varepsilon>0
, we find a sequence
\{v_{n}\}
, each being a minimizing cone to (
1.1
) with exponent
\gamma_{n}
such that

\gamma_{n}\neq 1\text{ for all }n,\hskip 5.0pt\gamma_{n}\to 1,

but

(4.4)

\|v_{n}-p\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}\geq\varepsilon

for all
p\in\mathcal{HS}\cup\mathcal{SP}
(see Definition
2.6
) and all
n\in\mathbb{N}.

Suppose that
u_{n}
is related to
v_{n}
by (
2.4
). It is elementary to see from (
2.2
) and (
2.4
) that

\lim_{n}\|u_{n}-v_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}=0.

As a consequence of (
4.4
), for all large
n
, we have

(4.5)

\|u_{n}-p\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}\geq\varepsilon/2

for all
p\in\mathcal{HS}\cup\mathcal{SP}
.

With Proposition
2.7
and Proposition
2.8
, we have, up to a subsequence,

u_{n}\to u_{\infty}\hskip 5.0pt\text{ locally uniformly in }\mathbb{R}^{d}

for some
u_{\infty}\in\mathcal{HS}
or
u\in\mathcal{P}
(see Definition
2.6
).
The first possibility is ruled out by (
4.5
). Thus
u_{\infty}\in\mathcal{P}
.

In this case, Corollary
4.2
implies that
u_{\infty}\in\mathcal{SP}
, contradicting (
4.5
).
∎

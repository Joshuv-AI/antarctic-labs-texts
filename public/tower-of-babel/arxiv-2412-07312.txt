High-dimensional classification problems with Barron regular boundaries under margin conditions
Jonathan García, Philipp Petersen

Abstract
We prove that a classifier with a Barron-regular decision boundary can be approximated with a rate of high polynomial degree by ReLU neural networks with three hidden layers when a margin condition is assumed. In particular, for strong margin conditions, high-dimensional discontinuous classifiers can be approximated with a rate that is typically only achievable when approximating a low-dimensional smooth function. We demonstrate how these expression rate bounds imply fast-rate learning bounds that are close to n − 1 n^{-1} where n n is the number of samples. In addition, we carry out comprehensive numerical experimentation on binary classification problems with various margins. We study three different dimensions, with the highest dimensional problem corresponding to images from the MNIST data set.



Keywords:
Neural Networks, Binary classification, Fourier-analytic Barron space, Barron regular boundaries, Margin condition, Hinge loss.

1.2.1
Loss function and the hinge loss

In supervised learning, determining the optimal parameters of a NN is achieved by minimizing an objective function. This objective is defined based on a collection of input-output pairs known as a sample. Concretely, let
S=\{(\bm{x}_{i},y_{i})\}_{i=1}^{n}

\bm{\Phi}
with respect to the sample
S
and

\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathcal{L},S}(\bm{\Phi})=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{L}(\bm{\Phi}(\bm{x}_{i}),y_{i})

\bm{\Phi}

(\bm{x}_{\text{new}},y_{\text{new}})
sampled independently from

, for this we define the so-called risk (or

-risk) of

, in the form

\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{L}}(\bm{\Phi})=\mathbb{E}_{(\bm{x}_{\text{new}},y_{\text{new}})\sim\mathcal{D}}\left[\mathcal{L}(\bm{\Phi}(\bm{x}_{\text{new}}),y_{\text{new}})\right]:=\mathbb{E}_{\bm{x}}\left[\mathcal{L}(\bm{\Phi}(\bm{x}),y)\right].

overfits the training data, meaning that it has memorized the training samples, but fails to generalize to new data (see

Fourier-analytic Barron space

There are several slightly different interpretations of the term “Barron function” in the literature, see for example
[
7

. We use this term to refer to the Fourier-analytic notion of Barron-type functions in
[
7
,

with parameters

A function

and a measurable function

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|F(\bm{\xi})|\sup_{\bm{x}\in[0,1]^{d}}\left|\left\langle\bm{\xi},\bm{x}\right\rangle\right|d\bm{\xi}\leq C\quad\text{and}\quad f(\bm{x})=c+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(e^{i\langle\bm{x},\bm{\xi}\rangle}-1)\cdot F(\bm{\xi})d\bm{\xi}

(14)

for all
\bm{x}\in[0,1]^{d}
. The set of all these Barron functions is denoted as
\mathcal{B}_{C}
.

In
[
9
]
it was shown that for a certain class of functions with bounded variation, shallow NNs of
N
neurons achieve an approximation accuracy of order
N^{-1/2}
in the
L^{2}
norm. Subsequently, in
[
7
, Proposition 2.2]
and
[

, Definition 3.1]
.

For
C>0
, we define the Barron approximation set
\mathcal{BA}_{C}
as the set of all functions
f:[0,1]^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
such that for all
N\in\mathbb{N}
, there is a shallow ReLU neural network (shallow ReLU-NN)

with
N
neurons in the hidden layer such that

{\lVert f-{\Phi}\rVert}_{\infty}\leq C\sqrt{d/N}

(15)

and all weights and biases of

are bounded in absolute value by

\sqrt{C}\left(5+\vartheta\right),\quad\text{where}\quad\vartheta:=\sup_{\bm{\xi}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}}\left(\frac{\lVert\bm{\xi}\rVert_{\ell^{\infty}}}{\sup_{\bm{x}\in[0,1]^{d}}\left|\left\langle\bm{\xi},\bm{x}\right\rangle\right|}\right).

In addition, we know that
\sqrt{C}\left(5+\vartheta\right)\leq 7\sqrt{C}
, since for

\vartheta\leq\sup_{\bm{\xi}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}}\left({2\lVert\bm{\xi}\rVert_{\ell^{\infty}}}/{\lVert\bm{\xi}\rVert}\right)\leq 2
.

The set

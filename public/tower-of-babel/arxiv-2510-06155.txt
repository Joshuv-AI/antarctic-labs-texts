A possibility of describing a sequential gauge symmetry as a part of the dihedral flavor symmetry and its implications Preprint: APS/123-QED
Jae Jun Kim

Abstract
We present a method in which the dimension-five and -six terms in a sequential gauge symmetry can coexist with that of the dihedral flavor symmetry. We start with the contents in the model proposed in a sequential gauge symmetry, meeting the conditions for the contents to be described in the flavor symmetry, build up the mass matrices and discuss its implications. Given that the breaking of the symmetry only takes place in the dimension-five and -six terms in the mass matrix of the charged fermion sectors, via the flavor-specific Higgs scalars, we address how adding the non-sequential contents as a part of the model can be realized and why adding such is needed. In doing so, we illustrate that the terms in the sequential gauge could be understood as the higher-order terms, the correction terms, and that in the non-sequential flavor symmetry as that of the leading order. We present that the mediation between the sequential and the non-sequential term within each sector is needed to take the hierarchical scheme of the charged and the neutral lepton sectors into account. Our result could be understood as an illustration of introducing the sequential gauge as a cause for the mixing and that of the coexistence of the sequential and the non-sequential gauge terms, and can be applied to other flavor model. Seeing the non-sequential term leading us to the mass matrix and the sequential term cross-contaminating between sectors could be an indication of the size of the mass being independent from that of the mixing in the leading order and a reason for the size of the mixing in the lepton sector being larger than that of the quark sector.

VI
Discussion

Taking the contents of the model in the scheme of the hierarchical
U(1)
gauge symmetry but building the interaction under
A_{4}
flavor symmetry, we could illustrate that the terms in the sequential gauge symmetry could be understood as the correction terms in the mass matrix, and that from the flavor symmetry as the leading-order terms. The same could be realized in both the charged and the neutral fermion sector. Under
A_{4}
, the breaking of the family universality could be a reason for our seeing the mixing among the contents and there we needed to introduce a mediation from the neutrino sector too. In other words, the breaking of the family universality could be one of the causes for having the correction terms in the mass matrix. Furthermore, we did not break the
Z_{3}
symmetry for
\phi_{SM}
but all the other Higgs scalars in our model thus that leaves a door open for us to incorporate all that was built for the non-sequential models. We also ended up with that a medication among the charged fermion sector is needed in order to realize the mass matrices and the breaking of neutrino-specific Higgs could be a cause of the mixing. Seeing such and that the non-sequential term led us to construct the mass matrix in the leading order could be understood as a reason for seeing the large size mixing angle in the neutrino sector given the relatively small size mass.

For the future studies, it would be interesting and important to see how the different types of the seesaw mechanism can be understood when we take the hierarchical gauge as the correctional terms. In
A_{4}
flavor symmetry, it is type 1 seesaw mechanism where the generation of the mass can be described, but it is mainly type 3 in the sequential gauge scheme. Accommodation of the two using the approach similar to that shown in our study but with details along with numerical analysis could be one of future studies. In addition, we can do the study presented here but under different family flavor symmetries such as
S_{4}
and
D_{4}
, studying more generalized cases of assigning the
U(1)_{X}
sequential charges differently along with
Z_{2}
for different generations. It would be interesting to see how applying the cyclic symmetry plays a role there. Given that the mediation between the charged and neutral lepton is seen under the sequential gauge, investigating their correlations among the flavor-specific Higgs in our study to quark-lepton complementarity
reference16
and imposing additional constraints from the previous studies could be another interesting topic. In addition, it would be interesting to see how the cross-mediating term such as that in Equation 52 could play a role when addressing the tension on the quark mixing matrix.

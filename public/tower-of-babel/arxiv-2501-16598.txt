1.
Introduction

The variation of the dimension of a set with respect to its orthogonal projection onto
m
-dimensional linear subspaces of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a classical problem in geometric measure theory, and there is a substantial body of results on this topic. In short, if
G(d,m)
denotes the set of all
m
-dimensional subspaces of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the question is: How is
\dim P_{V}(E)
related to
\dim E
, where
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
V\in G(d,m)
, and
P_{V}
denotes the orthogonal projection onto
V
.

When
\dim
represents the Hausdorff dimension, the problem has already been studied, yielding the result that if
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a Borel set and
P_{V}
denotes the orthogonal projection onto an
m
-dimensional linear subspace
V
with
m\leq d
, then

\dim_{\rm H}P_{V}E=\min\{\dim_{\rm H}E,m\}

(1)

for
\gamma_{d,m}
-almost all
V\in G(d,m)
. This result was first obtained for the case
d=2
,
m=1
by Marstrand
[
16
]
and later extended to Borel sets
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\geq 2
by Mattila
[
17
]
.

The lower and upper
\theta
-intermediate dimensions,
\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}
and
\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}
respectively, form a continuous family of dimensions that interpolate between the Hausdorff and the box dimensions of bounded sets in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. They satisfy, for all
\theta\in(0,1]
, the inequalities
\dim_{H}E\leq\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}E\leq\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}E\leq\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\rm B}\,E
and
\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}E\leq\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\rm B}\,E
, with
\overline{\dim}_{1}E=\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\rm B}\,E
. For
\theta=1
, we have
\underline{\dim}_{1}E=\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\rm B}\,E
. These dimensions are continuous functions of
\theta
for all
\theta\in(0,1]
; however, in general, the
\theta
-intermediate dimension does not converge to the Hausdorff dimension as
\theta\to 0
.

For box and
\theta
-intermediate dimensions, general results such as (
1
) do not hold. Nevertheless, as shown in
[
6
]
for the box dimension and in
[
4
]
for
\theta
-intermediate dimensions, for any fixed
\theta\in(0,1]
, the value of
\dim_{\theta}P_{V}(E)
remains invariant for
\gamma_{d,m}
-almost every
V\in G(d,m)
. Recall that
\dim_{1}P_{V}(E)=\dim_{B}P_{V}(E)
.

These values of the intermediate dimension are referred to by the authors as the “
(Upper or Lower)
\theta
-intermediate dimension profiles
,” and are denoted by
\overline{\dim}^{m}_{\theta}
and
\underline{\dim}^{m}_{\theta}
, respectively. In general, these profiles are difficult to work with.

When dealing with projections, the superscript
m
in the dimension profiles typically denotes an integer. However, in other contexts – as we will see – the superscript may represent a real number. To distinguish between these cases, from now on we will use
t
for real numbers and
m
for integers when referring to dimension profiles.

The precise result obtained in
[
6
]
and
[
4
]
is:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be bounded. Then for all
V\in G(d,m)
,

\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}P_{V}(E)\leq\underline{\dim}^{m}_{\theta}E
and
\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}P_{V}(E)\leq\overline{\dim}^{m}_{\theta}E

for all
\theta\in(0,1]
, and for
\gamma_{d,m}
-almost all
V\in G(d,m)
,

\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}P_{V}(E)=\underline{\dim}^{m}_{\theta}E
and
\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}P_{V}(E)=\overline{\dim}^{m}_{\theta}E
.

The reader can also refer to
[
13
]
for additional results on
\theta
-intermediate dimensions of projections.

In the present paper,
we study for which values of
m
, or under which conditions on the set
E
, results analogous to Marstrand’s theorem can be obtained for intermediate and box dimensions.

We introduce the notions of the
upper
and
lower quasi-Hausdorff dimensions
of a set
E
, denoted by
\overline{\dim}_{qH}E
and
\underline{\dim}_{qH}E
, respectively, as

\overline{\dim}_{qH}E:=\lim_{\theta\to 0}\overline{\dim}_{\theta}E\quad\text{and}\quad\underline{\dim}_{qH}E:=\lim_{\theta\to 0}\underline{\dim}_{\theta}E.

This helps us make the statements and proofs of our results clearer and more straightforward.

We show that, given a compact set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
and a positive integer
m\leq d
, the almost sure value of the upper and lower intermediate dimensions of the orthogonal projection onto an
m
-dimensional subspace satisfies

\overline{\dim}_{qH}P_{V}E=\min\left\{m,\overline{\dim}_{qH}E\right\}\quad\text{for }\gamma_{d,m}\text{-almost every }V\in G(d,m),

and similarly,

\underline{\dim}_{qH}P_{V}E=\min\left\{m,\underline{\dim}_{qH}E\right\}\quad\text{for }\gamma_{d,m}\text{-almost every }V\in G(d,m),

see Theorem
3.1
. From this, we deduce:

\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\rm B}\,P_{V}(E)=m\text{ for }\gamma_{d,m}\text{-almost all }V\in G(d,m)\iff m\leq\overline{\dim}_{qH}E,

and

\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\rm B}\,P_{V}(E)=m\text{ for }\gamma_{d,m}\text{-almost all }V\in G(d,m)\iff m\leq\underline{\dim}_{qH}E.

A direct consequence of Theorem
3.1
is that if
E
satisfies
\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\rm B}\,E=\underline{\dim}_{qH}E
, then

\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\rm B}\,(P_{V}E)=\min\{m,\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\rm B}\,E\}

for almost every
V\in G(d,m)
.

Note that in
[
12
, Corollary 1.3]
the authors show that if
\underline{\dim}_{\mathrm{B}}E=\dim_{qA}E
, then

\underline{\dim}_{\mathrm{B}}(P_{V}E)=\min\{\underline{\dim}_{\mathrm{B}}E,m\}

for almost every
V\in G(d,m)
.

Since by Theorem
3.9
we have that,
\underline{\dim}_{\mathrm{B}}E=\dim_{qA}E\quad\text{ implies }\quad\underline{\dim}_{\mathrm{B}}E=\underline{\dim}_{qH}E,

and the converse is not necessarily true, the result proven in this paper is strictly more general than the one in
[
12
]
.
All the above statements remain valid if one replaces the lower box dimension and the lower quasi-Hausdorff dimension with their upper counterparts.

We also investigate for which values of
m
the intermediate dimension and the intermediate dimension profiles coincide. We obtain a general lower bound for the profiles in terms of the Assouad and Assouad dimension spectrum
\dim^{\alpha}_{A}
:

\overline{\dim}_{\theta}^{t}E\geq\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}E-\max\{0,\dim_{A}^{\alpha}E-t,(\dim_{A}E-t)(1-\alpha)\},

for all
\alpha\in(0,1)
, which yields that if
m\geq\dim_{qA}E
, then for all
\theta\in(0,1]
we have
\dim_{\theta}E=\dim_{\theta}P_{V}E
. This result was previously known only in the case of the box dimension.

Another known bound that we adapt to intermediate dimensions is the inequality:

\overline{\dim}_{\theta}^{s}E\geq\frac{\overline{\dim}_{\theta}^{t}E}{1+\left(\frac{1}{s}-\frac{1}{t}\right)\overline{\dim}_{\theta}^{t}E},

and we demonstrate with a simple example that this inequality is sharp, with equality holding for certain sets.

Since these profiles are also related to the dimensions of more general families of functions – such as fractional Brownian motion, as shown in
[
3
]
– we can extend all our results to such functions.

Theorem
3.1
and its corollaries provide genuinely new insights only in cases where the intermediate dimensions of the set are not continuous at
\theta=0
. To illustrate this point, we now present an example of a set for which our projection theorems yield information that is not captured by existing results.

Example 1.2
.

Let
n\geq 2
. For each
1\leq j<n
, it is not difficult to construct a set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

\dim_{H}E<j\leq\underline{\dim}_{qH}E.

Indeed, let
F_{1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{j}
be a set with
j-1<\dim_{H}F_{1}=\dim_{B}F_{1}\leq j
; for instance,
F_{1}
could be a self-similar set. Define

F_{2}=\left(\{0\}\cup\left\{\frac{1}{\log(n)}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\right)^{n-j}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n-j}.

Then
F_{2}
is a countable set such that
\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}F_{2}=\overline{\dim}_{\theta}F_{2}=n-j
for all
\theta\in(0,1]
. Now define
E=F_{1}\times F_{2}
. Then

\dim_{H}E=\dim_{H}F_{1}\leq j<n-j+\dim_{H}F_{1}=\underline{\dim}_{qH}E,

where the last equality follows from the product rule for intermediate dimensions (see
[
1
, Theorem 5.4]
).

Therefore, by Theorem
3.1
, we conclude that

\underline{\dim}_{qH}P_{V}E=\overline{\dim}_{qH}P_{V}E=j\quad\text{for }\gamma_{n,j}\text{-almost every }V\in G(n,j).

In contrast, Marstrand’s projection theorem gives

\dim_{H}P_{V}E=\dim_{H}E<j\quad\text{for }\gamma_{n,j}\text{-almost every }V\in G(n,j).

The
\theta

Definition 2.1
.

Let
F\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be bounded. For
0\leq\theta\leq 1
we define the lower
\theta
-intermediate dimension of
F
by

\displaystyle\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}F=\inf\big\{

\displaystyle s\geq 0:\ \forall\ \varepsilon>0,\ {\rm and\ all}\ \delta_{0}>0,{\rm there}\ \exists\ 0<\delta\leq\delta_{0},\ {\rm and}\

\displaystyle\{U_{i}\}_{i\in I}\ :F\subseteq\cup_{i\in I}U_{i}\ :\delta^{1/\theta}\leq|U_{i}|\leq\delta\ {\rm and}\ \sum_{i\in I}|U_{i}|^{s}\leq\varepsilon\big\}.

Similarly, we define the upper
\theta
-intermediate dimension of
F
by

\displaystyle\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}F=\inf\big\{

\displaystyle s\geq 0:\ \forall\ \varepsilon>0,\ {\rm there}\ \exists\ \delta_{0}>0,\ :\ \forall\ 0<\delta\leq\delta_{0},\mbox{ \rm there }\ \exists

\displaystyle\{U_{i}\}_{i\in I}\ :F\subseteq\cup_{i\in I}U_{i}\ :\delta^{1/\theta}\leq|U_{i}|\leq\delta\ {\rm and}\ \sum_{i\in I}|U_{i}|^{s}\leq\varepsilon\big\}.

For all
\theta\in(0,1]
and a compact set
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
we have
\dim_{\rm H}A\leq\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}A\leq\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\rm B}\,A
and similarly with the Lower case replacing
\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\rm B}\,
with
\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\rm B}\,
. This spectrum of dimensions has the property of being continuous for
\theta\in(0,1]
with
\underline{\dim}_{1}E=\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\rm B}\,E
and
\overline{\dim}_{1}E=\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\rm B}\,E
, leaving the natural question of the continuity in
\theta=0
, i.e.
\displaystyle\lim_{\theta\to 0}\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}E=\dim_{\rm H}E
or
\displaystyle\lim_{\theta\to 0}\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}E=\dim_{\rm H}E
, as a problem to study. In general these equalities are not true. The reader can find more information in
[
11
]
.

As mentioned in the introduction, we will adopt a specific notation for the limiting case of the intermediate dimension as the parameter
\theta
tends to
0
. We define the following quantities, which we refer to as the
quasi-Hausdorff dimensions
:

Definition 2.2
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a non-empty bounded set. We define the
lower quasi-Hausdorff dimension
and the
upper quasi-Hausdorff dimension
of
E
as

\underline{\dim}_{qH}E:=\lim_{\theta\to 0}\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}E\quad\text{and}\quad\overline{\dim}_{qH}E:=\lim_{\theta\to 0}\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}E,

If both limits coincide, we say that the
quasi-Hausdorff dimension
of
E
exists, and we denote it by

\dim_{qH}E:=\underline{\dim}_{qH}E=\overline{\dim}_{qH}E.

There is also an equivalent definition of intermediate dimension. Working with it will be more beneficial to our objectives. For
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
bounded and non-empty,
\theta\in(0,1]
,
r>0
and
s\in[0,d]
, define

S^{s}_{r,\theta}(E)=\inf\left\{\displaystyle\sum_{i}|U_{i}|^{s}:\{U_{i}\}_{i}\text{ is a cover of }E\text{ such that }r\leq|U_{i}|\leq r^{\theta}\text{ for all }i\right\}.

Then we have

\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}E=\left(\text{ the unique }s\in[0,d]\text{ such that }\displaystyle\liminf_{r\to 0}\frac{\log S^{s}_{r,\theta}(E)}{-\log(r)}=0\right)

and

\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}E=\left(\text{ the unique }s\in[0,d]\text{ such that }\displaystyle\limsup_{r\to 0}\frac{\log S^{s}_{r,\theta}(E)}{-\log(r)}=0\right).

Furthermore, the next result holds

Lemma 2.3
.

[
4
, Lemma 2.1]
Let
\theta\in(0,1]
and
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
. For each
0<r<1
,

-(s-t)\leq\frac{\log S^{s}_{r,\theta}(E)}{-\log(r)}-\frac{\log S^{t}_{r,\theta}(E)}{-\log(r)}\leq-\theta(s-t)\quad(0\leq t\leq s\leq d).

Remark 2.4
.

Note that this implies that if
\displaystyle\liminf_{r\to 0}\frac{\log S^{s}_{r,\theta}(E)}{-\log(r)}\geq 0
then
s\leq\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}E
, and analogously, if
\displaystyle\limsup_{r\to 0}\frac{\log S^{s}_{r,\theta}(E)}{-\log(r)}\geq 0
then
s\leq\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}E
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a non-empty set and let
N_{r}(E)
be the minimum number of sets of diameter
r
that can cover

E
. The
Assouad dimension
of

is defined by

\displaystyle\dim_{A}E=\inf\big\{s:\exists\ C>0\ \text{such that }\

\displaystyle\forall\ 0<r<R\mbox{ and }

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274ptx\in E,N_{r}(B(x,R)\cap E)\leq C\left({R}/{r}\right)^{s}\big\}

and the
upper Assouad spectrum
of

, for

is defined by

\displaystyle\overline{\dim}_{A}^{\alpha}E=\inf\big\{t:\exists\ C>0\ \text{such that }

\displaystyle\forall\ 0<r\leq R^{1/\alpha}<R<1\mbox{ and }

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274ptx\in E,N_{r}(B(x,R)\cap E)\leq C\left({R}/{r}\right)^{t}\big\}.

The upper Assouad spectrum is non decreasing in

. Finally, the

Theorem 2.7

[

(2)

Analogously, if

, we have that there exists a constant
c>0
such that for all
r_{0}>0
there exists
r\in(0,r_{0})
and a Borel probability measure
\mu_{r}
supported on
E
satisfying (
2
) for
r\leq\delta\leq r^{\theta}
and all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

We finish the preliminaries by recalling another family of kernels
\tilde{\phi}^{s}_{r,\theta}
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, defined for
0<r<1
,
\theta\in[0,1]
and
0<s\leq m
in
[

]
and
[
10

\tilde{\phi}^{s}_{r,\theta}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcc}1&if&|x|<r\\
\\
\left(\frac{r}{|x|}\right)^{s}&if&r\leq|x|<r^{\theta}\\

Lemma 2.13

[

Let

be compact,

,

Results

Our first result is a version of
Marstrand’s theorem
for quasi-Hausdorff dimension. If
\dim_{\theta}E
is continuous at
\theta=0
, then our result recovers the classical Marstrand-–Mattila theorem. In general, however, we have

\dim_{H}E\leq\underline{\dim}_{qH}E\leq\overline{\dim}_{qH}E,

and these inequalities can be strict.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a compact set and
0\leq m\leq d
. Then

\min\left\{m,\underline{\dim}_{qH}E\right\}=\underline{\dim}_{qH}P_{V}E\quad\text{for }\gamma_{d,m}\text{-almost all }V\in G(d,m),

and

\min\left\{m,\overline{\dim}_{qH}E\right\}=\overline{\dim}_{qH}P_{V}E\quad\text{for }\gamma_{d,m}\text{-almost all }V\in G(d,m).

Proof.

We will prove the case of
\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}E
; the argument for
\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}E
is similar.
We formulate the proof in terms of intermediate dimension profiles. That is, we show that

\min\left\{m,\displaystyle\lim_{\theta\to 0}\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}E\right\}=\lim_{\theta\to 0}\underline{\dim}_{\theta}^{m}E.

Recall that the intermediate dimension profile gives the almost sure value of the intermediate dimension of orthogonal projections.

Since for all
0\leq m\leq d
, we have
\underline{\dim}_{\theta}^{m}E\leq m
and
\underline{\dim}_{\theta}^{m}E\leq\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}E
, it follows that
\displaystyle\lim_{\theta\to 0}\underline{\dim}^{m}_{\theta}E
is always less than or equal to the left-hand side.

We now prove the reverse inequality. Assume, without loss of generality, that
|E|<1
.

Let
d(E):=\displaystyle\lim_{\theta\to 0}\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}E
, and choose
t<\min\left\{m,d(E)\right\}
and
\varepsilon>0
small enough so that
t+\varepsilon<\min\left\{m,d(E)\right\}
.

Then there exists
\theta_{0}>0
such that
t+\varepsilon<\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}E
for all
\theta\in(0,\theta_{0})
.

Let
r>0
,
\theta\in(0,1)
, and choose
\theta_{1}\in\left(0,\min\{\theta,\theta_{0}\}\right)
such that
|E|<r^{\theta_{1}}
.

Then, by Lemma
2.12
, there exists a constant
c>0
and a probability measure
\mu
supported on
E
such that

\mu(B(x,u))\leq c\,u^{t+\varepsilon}

(3)

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
u\in[r,r^{\theta_{1}}]
.

Now, integrating the kernel
\phi_{r,\theta}^{t,m}
with respect to
\mu
, and using
\mathbb{I}_{A}
for the characteristic function of the set
A
, we have

\displaystyle\left(C_{r,\theta}^{t,m}(E)\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle\leq\iint\phi_{r,\theta}^{t,m}(x-y)d\mu(y)d\mu(x)

\displaystyle=\int\mu(B(x,r))d\mu(x)+r^{t}\iint\frac{\mathbb{I}_{(B(x,r^{\theta})\setminus B(x,r))}(y)}{|x-y|^{t}}d\mu(y)d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\qquad+r^{\theta(m-t)+t}\iint\frac{\mathbb{I}_{(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B(x,r^{\theta}))}(y)}{|x-y|^{m}}d\mu(y)d\mu(x)

\displaystyle=S_{1}+S_{2}+S_{3}.

By (
3
), we have

S_{1}\leq c\,r^{t+\varepsilon}<c\,r^{t}.

For
S_{2}
, using a change of variables and again (
3
), we obtain

\displaystyle\int

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbb{I}_{(B(x,r^{\theta})\setminus B(x,r))}(y)}{|x-y|^{t}}d\mu(y)=\int\mu\left\{y:\frac{\mathbb{I}_{(B(x,r^{\theta})\setminus B(x,r))}(y)}{|x-y|^{t}}\geq u\right\}du

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1/r^{\theta t}}\mu(B(x,r^{\theta})\setminus B(x,r))du+\int_{1/r^{\theta t}}^{1/r^{t}}\mu\left\{y:\frac{1}{|x-y|^{t}}\geq u\right\}du

\displaystyle\leq c\,r^{\theta\varepsilon}+t\int_{r}^{r^{\theta}}s^{-t-1}\mu(B(x,s))ds\leq c\,r^{\theta\varepsilon}+\frac{c\,t}{\varepsilon}\left(r^{\theta\varepsilon}-r^{\varepsilon}\right)

\displaystyle<c+\frac{c\,t}{\varepsilon},

and hence

S_{2}<\left(c+\frac{c\,t}{\varepsilon}\right)r^{t}.

For
S_{3}
, since
|E|<r^{\theta_{1}}
and
\mu
is supported on
E
, we have

\displaystyle\int\frac{\mathbb{I}_{(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B(x,r^{\theta}))}(y)}{|x-y|^{m}}d\mu(y)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1/r^{\theta m}}\mu(B(x,u^{-1/m})\setminus B(x,r^{\theta}))\,du

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{1/|E|^{m}}1\,du+\int_{1/|E|^{m}}^{1/r^{\theta m}}\mu(B(x,u^{-1/m}))\,du

\displaystyle=|E|^{-m}+m\int_{r^{\theta}}^{|E|}s^{-m-1}\mu(B(x,s))\,ds

\displaystyle\leq|E|^{-m}+c\,m\int_{r^{\theta}}^{|E|}s^{t+\varepsilon-m-1}\,ds

\displaystyle=|E|^{-m}+\frac{c\,m}{m-(t+\varepsilon)}\left(r^{\theta(t+\varepsilon-m)}-|E|^{\theta(t+\varepsilon-m)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq|E|^{-m}+\frac{c\,m}{m-(t+\varepsilon)}r^{\theta(t+\varepsilon-m)}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle S_{3}

\displaystyle\leq|E|^{-m}r^{\theta(m-t)+t}+\frac{c\,m}{m-(t+\varepsilon)}r^{\theta\varepsilon}r^{t}

\displaystyle\leq\left(|E|^{-m}+\frac{c\,m}{m-(t+\varepsilon)}\right)r^{t}.

Letting
C=3\max\left\{c+\frac{c\,t}{\varepsilon},|E|^{-m}+\frac{c\,m}{m-(t+\varepsilon)}\right\}
, we obtain

\left(C_{r,\theta}^{t,m}(E)\right)^{-1}\leq C\,r^{t}\quad\text{which implies}\quad\frac{\log C_{r,\theta}^{t,m}(E)}{-\log r}-t\geq\frac{\log C}{\log r}\ \text{for all}\ r.

Taking the
\liminf
of both sides yields

t\leq\underline{\dim}_{\theta}^{m}E.

Letting
t\to\min\left\{m,d(E)\right\}
completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be compact,
0<t<d
, and
\theta\in(0,1]
. Then:

If
\underline{\dim}_{\theta}^{t}E=t
, then
t\leq\underline{\dim}_{qH}E
,

and

If
\overline{\dim}_{\theta}^{t}E=t
, then
t\leq\overline{\dim}_{qH}E
.

Proof.

Let
0<\alpha<1
and
0<m\leq d
such that
t=\alpha m
.

Let
B_{\alpha}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
be the index-
\alpha
fractional Brownian motion. Then, using Theorem
2.7
,
we have

m=\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}B_{\alpha}(E)\leq\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\rm B}\,B_{\alpha}(E)\leq m,

almost surely, and hence
\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\rm B}\,B_{\alpha}(E)=m
. Now, using Corollary
2.11
, we obtain that
\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}B_{\alpha}(E)=\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\rm B}\,B_{\alpha}(E)=m
for all
\theta\in(0,1)
. Using Theorem
2.7
again and the fact that
\underline{\dim}_{\theta}^{s}E\leq\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}E
for all
0\leq s\leq d
, we have for all
\theta\in(0,1)
,

m=\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}B_{\alpha}(E)\leq\frac{\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}E}{\alpha}.

Letting
\theta\to 0
, we have

t=\alpha m\leq\displaystyle\lim_{\theta\to 0}\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}E,

which completes the proof for
\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}
. The proof for the
\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}E
part is analogous.
∎

Corollary 3.3
.

\overline{\dim}_{\theta}^{t}E=t\iff t\leq\overline{\dim}_{qH}E.

3.1
, Theorem

, and the fact that
\displaystyle\lim_{\theta\to 0}\overline{\dim}^{t}_{\theta}E\leq\overline{\dim}^{t}_{\theta}E\leq t
for all
\theta
, with the same holding when replacing
\overline{\dim}^{t}_{\theta}
with
\underline{\dim}^{t}_{\theta}
.
∎

Our next theorem is a lower bound for the
\theta
-intermediate dimension profile in terms of the quasi-Assouad spectrum and the Assouad dimension of the set. This result generalizes the result obtained in
[

]
for the upper and lower box dimensions.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
\theta\in(0,1]
,
\alpha\in(0,1)
, and
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be bounded. Then, if
\underline{\dim}_{\theta}^{t}E<t
,
we have

\underline{\dim}_{\theta}^{t}E\geq\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}E-\max\{0,\dim_{A}^{\alpha}E-t,(\dim_{A}E-t)(1-\alpha)\}.

(4)

And if
\overline{\dim}_{\theta}^{t}E<t
,
we have

\overline{\dim}_{\theta}^{t}E\geq\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}E-\max\{0,\dim_{A}^{\alpha}E-t,(\dim_{A}E-t)(1-\alpha)\}.

Proof.

If
\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}E=0
, there is nothing to prove.
If
\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}E=\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\rm B}\,E\leq t
for all
\theta
, then

\underline{\dim}_{\theta}^{t}E\geq\lim_{\theta\to 0}\underline{\dim}_{\theta}^{t}E=\min\{t,\lim_{\theta\to 0}\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}E\}=\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\rm B}\,E=\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}E,

and the result follows.
If
\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}E=\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\rm B}\,E>t
for all
\theta
, then

\underline{\dim}_{\theta}^{t}E\geq\lim_{\theta\to 0}\underline{\dim}_{\theta}^{t}E=\min\{t,\lim_{\theta\to 0}\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}E\}=t,

which contradicts the hypothesis.

So, suppose that
0<\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}E<\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\rm B}\,E
.

Let
A>\dim_{A}E
and for
\alpha\in(0,1)
, let
t\neq\dim_{A}^{\alpha}E
and let
A_{\alpha}>\dim_{A}^{\alpha}E
such that
A_{\alpha}\neq t
.

By definition, there exists a constant
C>0
such that for all
0<r<R
and
x\in E
,

N_{r}(B(x,R)\cap E)\leq C\left(\frac{R}{r}\right)^{A},

and for all
0<r\leq R^{1/\alpha}<R
and
x\in E
,

N_{r}(B(x,R)\cap E)\leq C\left(\frac{R}{r}\right)^{A_{\alpha}}.

Let
\theta\in(0,1)
. Let
s<\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}E
and

s^{\prime}<s-\max\left\{0,A_{\alpha}-t,(A-t)(1-\alpha)\right\}.

Note that
s^{\prime}<\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}E
.

For
r\in(0,1)
, let
\mu_{r}^{s}
be the Frostman measure of the set
E
for
s
of Lemma
2.12
.

Since
\mu_{r}^{s}
are probability measures supported on
E
, we have

\displaystyle\left(C_{r,\theta}^{s^{\prime},t}(E)\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle\leq\iint\phi_{r,\theta}^{s^{\prime},t}(x-y)\,d\mu_{r}^{s}(y)\,d\mu_{r}^{s}(x)

\displaystyle=\int\mu_{r}^{s}(B(x,r))\,d\mu_{r}^{s}(x)+r^{s^{\prime}}\iint\frac{\mathbb{I}_{(B(x,r^{\theta})\setminus B(x,r))}(y)}{|x-y|^{s^{\prime}}}\,d\mu_{r}^{s}(y)\,d\mu_{r}^{s}(x)

\displaystyle\qquad+r^{\theta(t-s^{\prime})+s^{\prime}}\iint\frac{\mathbb{I}_{(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B(x,r^{\theta}))}(y)}{|x-y|^{t}}\,d\mu_{r}^{s}(y)\,d\mu_{r}^{s}(x)

\displaystyle=S_{1}+S_{2}+S_{3}

respectively.

By the Frostman condition of
\mu_{r}^{s}
, we have

S_{1}\leq c\,r^{s}<c\,r^{s^{\prime}}.

For
S_{2}
, by a change of variable
u=t^{-1/s^{\prime}}
, we have

\displaystyle\int\frac{\mathbb{I}_{(B(x,r^{\theta})\setminus B(x,r))}(y)}{|x-y|^{s^{\prime}}}\,d\mu_{r}^{s}(y)

\displaystyle=\int\mu_{r}^{s}\left\{y:\frac{\mathbb{I}_{(B(x,r^{\theta})\setminus B(x,r))}(y)}{|x-y|^{s^{\prime}}}\geq t\right\}\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1/r^{\theta s^{\prime}}}\mu_{r}^{s}(B(x,r^{\theta})\setminus B(x,r))\,dt+\int_{1/r^{\theta s^{\prime}}}^{1/r^{s^{\prime}}}\mu_{r}^{s}\left\{y:\frac{1}{|x-y|^{s^{\prime}}}\geq t\right\}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq cr^{\theta(s-s^{\prime})}+\int_{1/r^{\theta s^{\prime}}}^{1/r^{s^{\prime}}}\mu_{r}^{s}(B(x,(1/t)^{1/s^{\prime}}))\,dt

\displaystyle=cr^{\theta(s-s^{\prime})}+s^{\prime}\int_{r}^{r^{\theta}}u^{-s^{\prime}-1}\mu_{r}^{s}(B(x,u))\,du

\displaystyle\leq cr^{\theta(s-s^{\prime})}+\frac{cs^{\prime}}{s-s^{\prime}}\left(r^{\theta(s-s^{\prime})}-r^{s-s^{\prime}}\right)<c+\frac{cs^{\prime}}{s-s^{\prime}},

and then

S_{2}<\left(c+\frac{cs^{\prime}}{s-s^{\prime}}\right)r^{s^{\prime}}.

For
S_{3}
, we have

\displaystyle\int

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbb{I}_{(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B(x,r^{\theta}))}(y)}{|x-y|^{t}}\,d\mu_{r}^{s}(y)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu_{r}^{s}\left\{y:\frac{\mathbb{I}_{(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B(x,r^{\theta}))}(y)}{|x-y|^{t}}\geq w\right\}\,dw

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{r^{-\theta t}}\mu_{r}^{s}(B(x,w^{-1/t}))\,dw=t\int_{r^{\theta}}^{\infty}u^{-t-1}\mu_{r}^{s}(B(x,u))\,du

\displaystyle=t\int_{r^{\theta}}^{r^{\alpha\theta}}u^{-t-1}\mu_{r}^{s}(B(x,u))\,du+t\int_{r^{\alpha\theta}}^{\infty}u^{-t-1}\mu_{r}^{s}(B(x,u))\,du

\displaystyle=S_{3,1}+S_{3,2}.

For
S_{3,2}
, we have

\displaystyle t\int_{r^{\alpha\theta}}^{\infty}u^{-t-1}\mu_{r}^{s}(B(x,u))\,du

\displaystyle=t\int_{r^{\alpha\theta}}^{|E|}u^{-t-1}\mu_{r}^{s}(B(x,u))\,du+t\int_{|E|}^{\infty}u^{-t-1}\mu_{r}^{s}(B(x,u))\,du

\displaystyle\leq t\int_{r^{\alpha\theta}}^{\infty}u^{-t-1}\left(\frac{u}{r^{\theta}}\right)^{A_{\alpha}}\mu_{r}^{s}(B(x,r^{\theta}))\,du+t\int_{|E|}^{\infty}u^{-t-1}\,du

\displaystyle\leq t\int_{r^{\alpha\theta}}^{|E|}u^{-t-1}\left(\frac{u}{r^{\theta}}\right)^{A_{\alpha}}\mu_{r}^{s}(B(x,r^{\theta}))\,du+|E|^{-t}

\displaystyle\leq t\int_{r^{\theta}}^{|E|}u^{-t-1}\left(\frac{u}{r^{\theta}}\right)^{A_{\alpha}}\mu_{r}^{s}(B(x,r^{\theta}))\,du+|E|^{-t}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c\,t}{A_{\alpha}-t}r^{\theta(s-A_{\alpha})}\left(|E|^{A_{\alpha}-t}-r^{\theta(A_{\alpha}-t)}\right)+|E|^{-t}

\displaystyle=\frac{c\,t|E|^{A_{\alpha}-t}}{A_{\alpha}-t}r^{\theta(s-A_{\alpha})}-\frac{c\,t}{A_{\alpha}-t}r^{(s-t)}+|E|^{-t}.

For
S_{3,1}
, first suppose that
t\neq A
. Then,

\displaystyle t\int_{r^{\theta}}^{r^{\alpha\theta}}u^{-t-1}\mu_{r}^{s}(B(x,u))\,\,du

\displaystyle\leq t\int_{r^{\theta}}^{r^{\alpha\theta}}u^{-t-1}\left(\frac{u}{r^{\theta}}\right)^{A}\mu_{r}^{s}(B(x,r^{\theta}))\,\,du

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c\,t}{A-t}r^{\theta(s-A)}\left(r^{\alpha\theta(A-t)}-r^{\theta(A-t)}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{c\,t}{A-t}r^{\theta(s-A)+\alpha\theta(A-t)}-\frac{c\,t}{A-t}r^{\theta(s-t)},

and then

\begin{array}[]{llll}S_{3}&\leq r^{\theta(t-s^{\prime})+s^{\prime}}\bigg(\frac{c\,t|E|^{A_{\alpha}-t}}{A_{\alpha}-t}r^{\theta(s-A_{\alpha})}&+\frac{c\,t}{A-t}r^{\theta(s-A)+\alpha\theta(A-t)}\\
&&\hskip-56.9055pt-\left(\frac{c\,t}{A-t}+\frac{c\,t}{A_{\alpha}-t}\right)r^{\theta(s-t)}+|E|^{-t}\bigg)\\
&=\left(\frac{c\,t|E|^{A_{\alpha}-t}}{A_{\alpha}-t}\right)r^{\theta(s-(A_{\alpha}-t)-s^{\prime})}r^{s^{\prime}}&+\left(\frac{c\,t}{A-t}\right)r^{\theta(s-(1-\alpha)(A-t)-s^{\prime})}r^{s^{\prime}}\\
&&\hskip-56.9055pt-\left(\frac{c\,t}{A-t}+\frac{c\,t}{A_{\alpha}-t}\right)r^{\theta(s-s^{\prime})}r^{s^{\prime}}+|E|^{-t}\,r^{\theta(t-s^{\prime})+s^{\prime}}.\\
\end{array}

So, by our choice of
s^{\prime}
, we have

s-(A_{\alpha}-t)-s^{\prime}\geq 0\quad\text{ and }\quad s-(1-\alpha)(A-t)-s^{\prime}\geq 0,

and there exists a constant
C>0
such that

S_{3}\leq Cr^{s^{\prime}},

and then

\left(C_{r,\theta}^{s^{\prime},t}(E)\right)^{-1}\leq\iint\phi_{r,\theta}^{s^{\prime},t}(x-y)\,d\mu_{r}^{s}(x)\,d\mu_{r}^{s}(y)\leq\left(1+c+\frac{cs^{\prime}}{s-s^{\prime}}+C\right)r^{s^{\prime}}.

This implies

\liminf_{r\to 0}\frac{\log C_{r,\theta}^{s^{\prime},t}(E)}{-\log r}-s^{\prime}\geq 0,

and finally, using Remark
2.10
,

\underline{\dim}_{\theta}^{t}E\geq s^{\prime}.

Now, suppose that
A=t
. Then,

\displaystyle S_{3,1}

\displaystyle=t\int_{r^{\theta}}^{r^{\alpha\theta}}u^{-t-1}\mu_{r}^{s}(B(x,u))\,\,du\leq c\,t\int_{r^{\theta}}^{r^{\alpha\theta}}u^{-t-1}\left(\frac{u}{r^{\theta}}\right)^{t}r^{\theta s}\,\,du

\displaystyle<-c\,tr^{\theta(s-t)}\theta\log(r),

and since
1\leq-\log(r)
and proceeding as before,

\left(C_{r,\theta}^{s^{\prime},t}(E)\right)^{-1}\leq\iint\phi_{r,\theta}^{s^{\prime},t}(x-y)\,d\mu_{r}^{s}(x)\,d\mu_{r}^{s}(y)\leq-C^{\prime}\log(r)r^{s^{\prime}}

for some constant
C^{\prime}>0
independent of
r
, which implies

\liminf_{r\to 0}\frac{\log C_{r,\theta}^{s^{\prime},t}(E)}{-\log r}-s^{\prime}\geq\liminf_{r\to 0}\frac{\log(\log(r))}{\log(r)}=0.

As before, we conclude that

\underline{\dim}_{\theta}^{t}E\geq s^{\prime}.

Finally, letting
A_{\alpha}\to\dim_{A}^{\alpha}E
,
A\to\dim_{A}E
, and
s\to\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}E
, we obtain

\underline{\dim}_{\theta}^{t}E>s^{\prime}

for all
s^{\prime}<\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}E-\max\{0,\dim_{A}^{\alpha}E-t,(\dim_{A}E-t)(1-\alpha)
, which implies,

\underline{\dim}_{\theta}^{t}E\geq\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}E-\max\{0,\dim_{A}^{\alpha}E-t,(\dim_{A}E-t)(1-\alpha)\}.

(5)

The case
t=\dim_{A}^{\alpha}E
follows from (
5
) and by the continuity in
t
of
\underline{\dim}_{\theta}^{t}E
; see
[
3
, Corollary 3.8]
.
The second part of the theorem, concerning the upper
\theta
-intermediate dimension, is similar.
∎

(6)

for all
\theta\in(0,1]
and
\gamma_{n,m}-
almost all
V\in G(n,m)
.
The same conclusion holds with
\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}
replaced with
\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}
.

Proof.

Let
\alpha\to 1
in Theorem
3.6
.
∎

From Theorem
3.1
, the following corollary is immediate.

Corollary 3.8
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded set such that
\overline{\dim}_{\mathrm{B}}E=\overline{\dim}_{qH}E
. Then, for
\gamma_{n,m}
-almost every
V\in G(n,m)
, we have

\overline{\dim}_{\mathrm{B}}(P_{V}E)=\min\{m,\overline{\dim}_{\mathrm{B}}E\},

and similarly,

\underline{\dim}_{\mathrm{B}}(P_{V}E)=\min\{m,\underline{\dim}_{\mathrm{B}}E\}

whenever
\underline{\dim}_{\mathrm{B}}E=\underline{\dim}_{qH}E
.

By
[
12
, Corollary 1.3]
, we have that if
\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\rm B}\,E=\dim_{qA}E
, then
\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\rm B}\,P_{V}E=\min\{\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\rm B}\,E,m\}
for a.e.
V\in G(d,m)
, and the same holds for
\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\rm B}\,
. A natural question is which hypothesis is more general:
\dim_{B}E=\dim_{qA}E
or
\dim_{B}E=\dim_{qH}E
.

In the following theorem, we will prove that if
\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\rm B}\,E=\dim_{qA}E
, then
\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}E=\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\rm B}\,E
, and if
\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\rm B}\,E=\dim_{qA}E
, then
\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}E=\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\rm B}\,E
for all
\theta\in(0,1]
. The converse is not generally true; in fact, it is not hard to construct a Cantor set
C\subset\mathbb{R}
such that
\dim_{H}C=\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\rm B}\,C<\dim_{qA}C
. Therefore, Corollary
3.8
is more general than the result obtained in
[
12
]
.

Theorem 3.9
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be bounded. Then, if
\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\rm B}\,E=\dim_{qA}E
, we have

\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}E=\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\rm B}\,E\quad\forall\ \theta\in(0,1],

and if
\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\rm B}\,E=\dim_{qA}E
, then

\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}E=\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\rm B}\,E\quad\forall\ \theta\in(0,1].

Proof.

We will prove the first case. The case of
\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}
is analogous.

If
\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}E=0
for some
\theta
, then using Lemma
2.11
, we have that the intermediate dimension is constantly
0
. So suppose that
0<\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}E
.
By
[
2
, Corollary 2.8]
, we have that if
\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}E=\dim_{A}E
for some
\theta
, then the intermediate dimensions are constant and equal to the Assouad dimension. So suppose that
\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}E<\dim_{A}E
for all
\theta\in(0,1]
.

The idea of the proof is to integrate appropriate kernels
\tilde{\phi}_{r,\theta}^{t}
with respect to a Frostman measure and show that this integral is bounded above.

Let
0<\theta<1
and
r\in(0,1)
. Suppose that
\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\rm B}\,E=\dim_{qA}E
. Then we have
\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\rm B}\,E=\dim_{A}^{\alpha}E
for all
\alpha\in[0,1]
. Using the continuity of the intermediate dimensions, choose
\rho
and
\alpha
sufficiently close to
1
such that
\underline{\dim}_{\rho}E-\dim_{A}E(1-\alpha)>0
and
\rho\underline{\dim}_{\rho}E-\dim_{A}^{\alpha}E(\rho-\theta)>0
.

Now let
d_{\rho},d_{\theta},A_{\alpha},A_{\rho},A
be such that
0<d_{\rho}<\underline{\dim}_{\rho}E<A<\dim_{A}E
,
0<d_{\theta}<\underline{\dim}_{\theta}E
,
0<A_{\alpha}<\dim^{\alpha}_{A}E
, and
0<A_{\rho}<\dim_{A}^{\rho}E
, and satisfies

0<t:=\min\left\{\frac{d_{\rho}-A\,(1-\alpha)}{\alpha},\frac{\rho\,d_{\rho}-A_{\alpha}\,(\rho-\theta)}{\theta}\right\}.

Note that
t<d_{\rho}
. By the definition of the Assouad dimension and the Upper Assouad spectrum, there exists
C>0
such that for all
0<r<R<1
and
x\in E
,

N_{r}(B(x,R)\cap E)\leq C\left(\frac{R}{r}\right)^{A},

(7)

and for all
0<r\leq R^{1/\alpha}<R<1
and
x\in E
,

N_{r}(B(x,R)\cap E)\leq C\left(\frac{R}{r}\right)^{A_{\alpha}},

(8)

and finally, for all
0<r\leq r^{1/\rho}<R<1
and
x\in E
,

N_{r}(B(x,R)\cap E)\leq C\left(\frac{R}{r}\right)^{A_{\rho}}.

(9)

Let
\mu_{r}
be a Borel probability measure supported on
E
that satisfies the condition that there exists
C^{\prime}>0
such that

\mu_{r}(B(x,\delta))\leq C\,\delta^{d_{\rho}}

for all
\delta\in[r,r^{\rho}]
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
We will now integrate the kernels
\widetilde{\phi}_{r,\theta}^{t}
with respect to
\mu_{r}
and bound this integral essentially by
r^{t-(\rho-\theta)(A_{\rho}-t)}
.

Then,

\displaystyle\iint\tilde{\phi}^{t}_{r,\theta}(x-y)\,d\mu_{r}(x)d\mu_{r}(y)

\displaystyle=\iint\mu_{r}(B(x,r))\,d\mu_{r}(x)+r^{t}\,\iint\frac{\mathbb{I}_{(B(x,r^{\theta})\setminus B(x,r))}}{|x-y|^{t}}\,d\mu_{r}(x)d\mu_{r}(y)

\displaystyle=S_{1}+S_{2},

respectively.

By the Frostman condition of
\mu_{r}
and since
t<d_{\rho}
, we have

S_{1}\leq C^{\prime}\,r^{d_{\rho}}<C^{\prime}\,r^{t}\leq C^{\prime}r^{t-(\rho-\theta)(A_{\rho}-t)}.

For
S_{2}
, we will use inequality
(
9
)
and the Frostman condition on
\mu_{r}
since
t<d_{\rho}
. We have

\displaystyle\int

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbb{I}_{(B(x,r^{\theta})\setminus B(x,r))}}{|x-y|^{t}}\,d\mu_{r}(y)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu_{r}\left(\left\{y:\frac{\mathbb{I}_{(B(x,r^{\theta})\setminus B(x,r))}}{|x-y|^{t}}\geq u\right\}\right)\,du

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1/r^{\theta t}}\mu_{r}\left(B(x,r^{\theta})\setminus B(x,r)\right)\,du+\int_{1/r^{\theta t}}^{1/r^{t}}\mu_{r}\left(B(x,u^{-1/t})\right)\,du

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{1/r^{\theta t}}\mu_{r}\left(B(x,r^{\theta})\right)\,du+t\int_{r}^{r^{\theta}}u^{-1-t}\mu_{r}\left(B(x,u)\right)\,du

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\frac{r^{\theta}}{r^{\rho}}\right)^{A_{\rho}}\int_{0}^{1/r^{\theta t}}\mu_{r}\left(B(x,r^{\rho})\right)\,du+t\int_{r}^{r^{\theta}}u^{-1-t}\mu_{r}\left(B(x,u)\right)\,du

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime}C\left(\frac{r^{\theta}}{r^{\rho}}\right)^{A_{\rho}}\int_{0}^{1/r^{\theta t}}r^{\rho d_{\rho}}\,du+t\int_{r}^{r^{\theta}}u^{-1-t}\mu_{r}\left(B(x,u)\right)\,du

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime}Cr^{(\rho-\theta)(t-A_{\rho})}+t\int_{r}^{r^{\theta}}u^{-1-t}\mu_{r}\left(B(x,u)\right)\,du.

To bound the last term of the sum, we will partition the interval of integration
I=(r,r^{\theta})
into three parts. One interval in which we can apply the Frostman condition of
\mu_{r}
, and for the remaining two intervals, we will utilize the properties of the Quasi-Assouad spectrum and Assouad dimension. Namely,

I=(r,r^{\theta})=(r,r^{\rho})\cup[r^{\rho},r^{\alpha\rho})\cup[r^{\alpha\rho},r^{\theta}).

Hence,

t\int_{r}^{r^{\theta}}u^{-1-t}\mu_{r}\left(B(x,u)\right)\,du=S_{2,1}+S_{2,2}+S_{2,3}.

Using the Frostman condition, it is immediate that

S_{2,1}\leq\frac{t}{d_{\rho}-t}r^{\rho(d_{\rho}-t)}\leq\frac{t}{d_{\rho}-t}.

For
S_{2,2}
, we use inequality (
7
), the Frostman condition for
\mu_{r}
, and the fact that
t\leq\frac{d_{\rho}-A(1-\alpha)}{\alpha}
. We have

\displaystyle t\int_{r^{\rho}}^{r^{\alpha\rho}}u^{-1-t}\mu_{r}\left(B(x,u)\right)\,du

\displaystyle\leq Ct\int_{r^{\rho}}^{r^{\alpha\rho}}u^{-1-t}\left(\frac{u}{r^{\rho}}\right)^{A}\mu_{r}\left(B(x,r^{\rho})\right)\,du

\displaystyle\leq\frac{CC^{\prime}t}{A-t}r^{-\rho(A-d_{\rho})}r^{(\alpha\rho)(A-t)}\leq\frac{CC^{\prime}t}{A-t}.

Finally, for
S_{2,3}
, we need to use inequality (
8
) and the fact that
t\leq\frac{\rho\,d_{\rho}-A_{\alpha}(\rho-\theta)}{\theta}
to obtain

t\int_{r^{\alpha\rho}}^{r^{\theta}}u^{-1-t}\mu_{r}\left(B(x,u)\right)\,du\leq\frac{CC^{\prime}t}{A-t}.

Choosing
M=3\max\{\frac{CC^{\prime}t}{A-t},\frac{t}{d_{\rho}-t},CC^{\prime}\}
, we have

S_{2}\leq r^{t}\,M\,\left(r^{(\rho-\theta)(t-A_{\rho})}+1\right)\leq 2Mr^{t-(\rho-\theta)(A_{\rho}-t)}.

Therefore,

\iint\tilde{\phi}^{t}_{r,\theta}(x-y)\,d\mu_{r}(x)d\mu_{r}(y)\leq 2Mr^{t-(\rho-\theta)(A_{\rho}-t)}.

By Lemma
2.13
, this implies

r^{(\rho-\theta)(A_{\rho}-t)}\leq S_{r,\theta}^{t}(E)
. Taking logarithms and letting
r\to 0
, we have

(\rho-\theta)(t-A_{\rho})\leq\liminf_{r\to 0}\frac{\log S_{r,\theta}^{t}(E)}{-\log r}.

Now, since by
[
4
, Lemma 2.1]
the right-hand side of the inequality is continuous in
t
, we can let
\alpha\to 1
,
d_{\rho}\to\underline{\dim}_{\rho}E
, and
A_{\rho}\to\dim_{A}^{\rho}E
, obtaining
t=\dim_{qA}E-\frac{\dim_{qA}E-\underline{\dim}_{\rho}E}{\theta}
. Finally, letting
\rho\to 1
and using the assumption that
\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\rm B}\,E=\dim_{qA}E
, we have

0\leq\displaystyle\liminf_{r\to 0}\frac{\log S_{r,\theta}^{\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\rm B}\,E}(E)}{-\log r},

which implies
\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\rm B}\,E\leq\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}E
, and the result follows.
∎

Our last theorem is another lower bound for intermediate dimension profiles. This lower bound generalizes the one obtained in
[
7
, Proposition 2.12]
for upper and box dimension profiles, although the result in that reference is stated in a different form. After rearranging the inequality presented there, one can recover a similar bound to the one we obtain here for intermediate
\theta
-dimensions. The approach to the proof is also closely related.

Theorem 3.10
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be bounded,
\theta\in(0,1)
, and
t\leq s
. Then,

\underline{\dim}_{\theta}^{t}E\geq\frac{\underline{\dim}_{\theta}^{s}E}{1+\left(\frac{1}{t}-\frac{1}{s}\right)\underline{\dim}_{\theta}^{s}E}

and

\overline{\dim}_{\theta}^{t}E\geq\frac{\overline{\dim}_{\theta}^{s}E}{1+\left(\frac{1}{t}-\frac{1}{s}\right)\overline{\dim}_{\theta}^{s}E}.

Proof.

This proof is inspired by the argument used in
[
7
, Proposition 2.12]
, although the details differ due to the generality of the intermediate dimension setting.

Let
d_{t}<\underline{\dim}_{\theta}^{t}E
. Then, we have

r^{-d_{t}}\leq C_{r,\theta}^{d_{t},t}

(10)

for all sufficiently small
r
.

Let
d_{s}=\dfrac{d_{t}}{1+\left(\frac{1}{s}-\frac{1}{t}\right)d_{t}}
,
r>0
, and let
R\geq r
be such that
R^{d_{t}/d_{s}}=r
, i.e.,

R=r^{\frac{\theta s+d_{s}(1-\theta)}{\theta s+d_{t}\left(1-\theta\frac{s}{t}\right)}}.

Let
\mu
be a Borel probability measure supported on
E
such that

\int\phi_{r,\theta}^{d_{t},t}(x-y)\,d\mu(y)=\left(C_{r,\theta}^{d_{t},t}(E)\right)^{-1}

(11)

for
\mu
-almost all
x
. Now, integrating, we get:

\begin{split}\int\phi_{r,\theta}^{d_{s},s}(x-y)\,d\mu(y)&\leq\mu(B(x,R))\\
&\quad+\int_{R\leq|x-y|\leq R^{\theta}}\frac{r^{d_{s}}}{|x-y|^{d_{s}}}\,d\mu(y)\\
&\quad+\int_{|x-y|>R^{\theta}}\frac{r^{\theta(s-d_{s})+d_{s}}}{|x-y|^{s}}\,d\mu(y)\\
&=S_{1}+S_{2}+S_{3}.\end{split}

For
S_{1}
, using the definition of
R
, we obtain

\begin{split}\mu(B(x,R))&\leq R^{d_{t}}\left(R^{-d_{t}}\int\phi_{R,\theta}^{d_{t},t}(x-y)\,d\mu(y)\right)\\
&=r^{d_{s}}\left(R^{-d_{t}}\int\phi_{R,\theta}^{d_{t},t}(x-y)\,d\mu(y)\right).\end{split}

(12)

For
S_{2}
, by Hölder’s inequality:

\begin{split}\int_{R\leq|x-y|\leq R^{\theta}}\frac{r^{d_{s}}}{|x-y|^{d_{s}}}\,d\mu(y)&\leq r^{d_{s}}\left(\int\frac{1}{|x-y|^{d_{t}}}\,d\mu(y)\right)^{d_{s}/d_{t}}\\
&\leq r^{d_{s}}\left(R^{-d_{t}}\int\phi_{R,\theta}^{d_{t},t}(x-y)\,d\mu(y)\right)^{d_{s}/d_{t}}.\end{split}

(13)

Finally, for
S_{3}
, again using Hölder:

\begin{split}\int_{|x-y|>R^{\theta}}\frac{r^{\theta(s-d_{s})+d_{s}}}{|x-y|^{s}}\,d\mu(y)&\leq r^{\theta(s-d_{s})+d_{s}}R^{-\theta s}\left(\int\frac{R^{\theta t}}{|x-y|^{t}}\,d\mu(y)\right)^{s/t}\\
&=r^{d_{s}}\left(R^{-d_{t}}\int\phi_{R,\theta}^{d_{t},t}(x-y)\,d\mu(y)\right)^{s/t},\end{split}

(14)

by our choice of
d_{s}
.

Now, using (
10
), (
11
), and combining (
12
), (
13
), and (
14
), we get:

\left(C_{r,\theta}^{d_{s},s}(E)\right)^{-1}\leq\int\phi_{r,\theta}^{d_{s},s}(x-y)\,d\mu(y)\leq 3r^{d_{s}}

for all sufficiently small
r
. Hence,

\liminf_{r\to 0}\frac{\log C_{r,\theta}^{d_{s},s}(E)}{-\log r}-d_{s}\geq 0,

which implies

\underline{\dim}_{\theta}^{s}E\geq d_{s}=\frac{d_{t}}{1+\left(\frac{1}{s}-\frac{1}{t}\right)d_{t}}.

The result follows by letting
d_{t}\to\underline{\dim}_{\theta}^{t}E
. The case of the upper intermediate dimension profile is similar.
∎

Remark 3.11
.

The lower bounds in the previous theorem are attained for some sets
E
, as shown in the next example.

Let
0<s\leq t\leq 1
,
F_{p}=\left\{\frac{1}{n^{p}}:n\in\mathbb{N}\right\}
, and
\theta\in(0,1)
.
By Theorem 1.1 in
[
9
]
and Theorem 3.4 in
[
4
]
, we have
\underline{\dim}_{\theta}^{s}F_{p}=\overline{\dim}_{\theta}^{s}F_{p}=\frac{s\theta}{\theta+sp}
for any
s\in(0,1]
. Therefore, for
s\geq t
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\underline{\dim}_{\theta}^{s}F_{p}}{1+\left(\frac{1}{t}-\frac{1}{s}\right)\underline{\dim}_{\theta}^{s}F_{p}}

\displaystyle=\frac{s\theta}{\theta+sp}\left(1+\left(\frac{1}{t}-\frac{1}{s}\right)\frac{s\theta}{\theta+sp}\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{ts\theta}{\theta+sp}\right)\left(\frac{t(\theta+sp)+s\theta-t\theta}{\theta+sp}\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{t\theta}{\theta+tp}=\underline{\dim}_{\theta}^{t}F_{P}.

Thus, the lower bound given in Theorem
3.10
is attained for this set.

Moreover, combining Theorem
3.10
and Corollary
3.7
, we have for
t\leq\dim_{qA}E
,

\underline{\dim}_{\theta}^{t}E\geq\frac{\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}E}{1+\left(\frac{1}{t}-\frac{1}{\dim_{qA}E}\right)\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}E}

and

\overline{\dim}_{\theta}^{t}E\geq\frac{\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}E}{1+\left(\frac{1}{t}-\frac{1}{\dim_{qA}E}\right)\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}E}.

(15)

One may wonder when (
15
) improves inequality (
6
) for sets
E
such that
0<\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}E<\dim_{qA}E
and
0<t<\dim_{qA}E
, i.e., when

\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}E-\left(\dim_{qA}E-t\right)<\frac{\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}E}{1+\left(\frac{1}{t}-\frac{1}{\dim_{qA}E}\right)\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}E}\leq\overline{\dim}_{\theta}^{t}E

(16)

holds.

A direct algebraic manipulation of the last inequality implies that inequality (
16
) holds if and only if

\displaystyle t^{2}\left(\dim_{qA}E-\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}E\right)

\displaystyle+t\left(2\dim_{qA}E\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}E-(\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}E)^{2}-(\dim_{qA}E)^{2}\right)

\displaystyle+\left(\dim_{qA}E(\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}E)^{2}-(\dim_{qA}E)^{2}\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}E\right)<0.

But this is true if and only if

t^{2}-t\left(\dim_{qA}E-\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}E\right)-\dim_{qA}E\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}E<0.

This is a second-degree polynomial in
t
with zeros at
t=-\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}E
and
t=\dim_{qA}E
. Therefore, for all
t\in(0,\dim_{qA}E)
, inequality (
16
) holds for all sets with
0<\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}E<\dim_{qA}E
.

We finish this work with a simple corollary that improves Corollary 3.8 in
[
3
]
.

Corollary 3.12
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be bounded and
\theta\in(0,1]
. The functions
f,g:(0,d)\to[0,d]
defined by

f(t)=\underline{\dim}_{\theta}^{t}E

and

g(t)=\overline{\dim}_{\theta}^{t}E

are Lipschitz functions.

Proof.

For all
0<t\leq d
, we have

\underline{\dim}_{\theta}^{t}E\leq t.

(17)

Now, using Theorem
3.10
and (
17
), we have for
0<s\leq t
,

\underline{\dim}_{\theta}^{t}E-\underline{\dim}_{\theta}^{s}E\leq\left(\frac{\underline{\dim}_{\theta}^{s}E\underline{\dim}_{\theta}^{t}E}{st}\right)(t-s)\leq t-s.

∎

Final remarks and broader applicability

Broader applicability

We chose to present our results for the case of orthogonal projections and fractional Brownian motion. However, in
[
3
]
, it is shown that the intermediate dimension profiles also provide meaningful bounds for intermediate dimensions for a more general set of functions:

Definition 3.13
.

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},P)
be a probability space, and let
(\omega,x)\mapsto f_{\omega}(x)
be a
\sigma(\mathcal{F}\times\mathcal{B})
-measurable function, where
\mathcal{B}
denotes the Borel
\sigma
-algebra on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Let
\mathcal{F}_{1}=\{f_{\omega}:E\to\mathbb{R}^{m},\omega\in\Omega\}
be a family of continuous functions, measurable with respect to
\sigma(\{F\times B:F\in\mathcal{F},B\in\mathcal{B}\})
, that satisfies one of the following conditions:

(1)

There exists a constant
c>0
such that for all
x,y\in E
and
r>0
,

P\left(\left\{\omega:|f_{\omega}(x)-f_{\omega}(y)|\leq r\right\}\right)\leq c\,\phi_{r^{\gamma},\theta}^{m/\gamma,m/\gamma}(x-y),

(2)

For all
\omega\in\Omega
, there exists a constant
c_{\omega}>0
such that for all
x,y\in E
,

|f_{\omega}(x)-f_{\omega}(y)|\leq c_{\omega}|x-y|^{1/\gamma}.

Therefore all our results that involve intermediate dimension profiles can be directly applied to this more general setting, since in

[
3
, Theorems 3.1 and 3.3]
, it is shown exactly how the intermediate dimension profiles control the dimension of images under functions in the family
\mathcal{F}_{1}
.

Precisely:
Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be compact,
\theta\in(0,1]
,
\gamma\geq 1
, and
m\in\mathbb{N}
. If
\mathcal{F}_{1}
satisfies condition (
1
), then for
P
-almost all
\omega\in\Omega
,
f_{\omega}\in\mathcal{F}_{1}
we have

\underline{\dim}_{\theta}f_{\omega}(E)\geq\gamma\,\underline{\dim}_{\theta}^{m/\gamma}E\quad\text{and}\quad\overline{\dim}_{\theta}f_{\omega}(E)\geq\gamma\,\overline{\dim}_{\theta}^{m/\gamma}E.

If
\mathcal{F}_{1}
satisfies condition (
2
), then for all
\omega\in\Omega
,
f_{\omega}\in\mathcal{F}_{1}
we have

\underline{\dim}_{\theta}f_{\omega}(E)\leq\gamma\,\underline{\dim}_{\theta}^{m/\gamma}E\quad\text{and}\quad\overline{\dim}_{\theta}f_{\omega}(E)\leq\gamma\,\overline{\dim}_{\theta}^{m/\gamma}E.

Size of exceptional directions

We now include two corollaries that quantify the
size
of the set of exceptional directions where the projected dimension drops. These are direct consequences of our main theorem combined with Theorem
2.6
, and although the proofs are straightforward, we believe they are of independent interest and help to clarify the scope of the results.

.

and

\dim_{\rm H}\left\{V\in G(d,m):\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}P_{V}E<\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}E\right\}\leq m(d-m)-(m-\dim_{qA}E).

Proof.

Apply Corollary
3.7
in Theorem
2.6
.
∎

Corollary 3.15
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be bounded,
1\leq m\leq d
, and
\lambda\in(0,\overline{\dim}_{qH}E)
. Then,

\dim_{\rm H}\left\{V\in G(d,m):\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}P_{V}E<\lambda\right\}\leq m(d-m)-(m-\lambda),

, then

\dim_{\rm H}\left\{V\in G(d,m):\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}P_{V}E<\lambda\right\}\leq m(d-m)-(m-\lambda).

3.3
in Theorem
2.6
.

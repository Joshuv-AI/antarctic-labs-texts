Collusion-proof Auction Design using Side Information
Sukanya Kudva, Anil Aswani

Abstract
We study the problem of auction design in the presence of bidder collusion. Specifically, we consider a multi-unit auction of identical items with single-minded bidders, where a subset of bidders may collude by coordinating bids and transferring payments and items among themselves. While the classical Vickrey-Clarke-Groves (VCG) mechanism achieves efficient and truthful outcomes in non-collusive settings, it is highly vulnerable to collusion. In contrast, fully collusion-proof mechanisms are limited to posted-price formats, which fail to guarantee even approximate efficiency. This paper aims to bridge this gap by designing auctions that achieve good welfare and revenue guarantees even when some bidders collude. We first characterize the strategic behavior of colluding bidders under VCG and prove that such bidders optimally bid shade –they never overbid or take additional items, but instead reduce the auction price. This characterization enables a Bulow–Klemperer type result: adding colluding bidders can only improve welfare and revenue relative to running VCG on the non-colluding group alone. Building on this insight, we propose a Hybrid VCG (H-VCG) mechanism that combines VCG applied to non-colluding bidders with a posted-price mechanism for colluding bidders, assuming access to a black-box collusion detection algorithm. We show that H-VCG is ex-post dominant-strategy incentive compatible (DSIC) and derive probabilistic guarantees on expected welfare and revenue under both known and unknown bidders’ valuation distributions. Finally, numerical experiments across several distributions demonstrate that H-VCG consistently outperforms VCG restricted to non-colluding bidders and approaches the performance of the ideal VCG mechanism assuming universal truthfulness. Our results provide a principled framework for incorporating collusion detection into mechanism design, offering a step toward collusion-resistant auctions.



1
Introduction

We consider a single-item, multiple-unit auction, where
r
identical copies of the same item are being sold. There are
M
single-minded bidders, each desiring at most one item, and bidder
i
has a private valuation
v_{i}
for the item and is charged payment
p_{i}
on winning it. Each bidder
i
aims to maximize the quasi-linear utility
v_{i}\mathds{1}\left(\text{bidder $i$ won}\right)-p_{i}
. The goal of the auctioneer is to design an
efficient
auction, with a payment scheme and allocation rule that maximizes the social welfare
\sum_{i}v_{i}\mathds{1}\left(\text{bidder $i$ won}\right)
. If some of the bidders
collude
to exchange information and monetary payments, they can collectively manipulate their bids and harm social welfare. The colluders can manipulate their bids in two ways: (1)
bid shade
to underquote their value and reduce the overall auction price, (2)
bid load
to overquote their value and win more items. This problem of bidders’ collusion is not well-studied in auction design, and is the main question addressed in this paper.

In the absence of collusion, when the bidders’ valuations are private, the well-known Vickrey-Clarke-Groves (VCG) mechanism
(
35
;
22
)
can be used to ensure
dominant strategy incentive compatibility
(DSIC) for all bidders, that is truth-telling is the best strategy for the bidders regardless of what others report. This mechanism also maximizes the auctioneer’s revenue among all welfare-maximizing auctions
(
28
)
. However, the VCG mechanism is known to be vulnerable to collusion unless the outcome is in the core
(
33
;
16
)
. Moreover, it is also known that truthful, collusion-proof mechanisms have to be posted-price mechanisms, that is give bidders a fixed take-it-or-leave-it price
(
21
)
. Unfortunately, posted price mechanisms cannot approximate expected efficiency to even a constant factor and it is impossible to design an efficient, truthful, and collusion-proof mechanism
(
21
)
, which is another reason why collusion is challenging to deal with. Given this, we attempt to address the following question:

Q:
How can we design an auction that achieves reasonably good performance in both welfare and revenue, even in the presence of bidder collusion, when compared against VCG outcomes in the no-collusion setting?

We model the colluding bidders to maximize the sum of their net utilities, and answer the above question in two parts: (1) we characterize colluding bids in a VCG auction and prove a Bulow-Klemperer
(
8
)
type result – that additional bidders improve welfare and revenue, even if colluding, (2) we propose a combination of collusion detection and auction design to achieve truthful, collusion-proof mechanisms that achieve probabilistic guarantees on expected welfare and revenue. The main idea is that once the colluding bidders are correctly identified, we can implement VCG mechanism on the non-colluding bidders followed by posted price mechanism on the colluding bidders to achieve truthfulness and optimize welfare.

Our work falls under a line of literature referred to as mechanism design with side information or learning-augmented mechanism design. The idea is to incorporate external or expert information about strategic agents to improve the performance of classical mechanisms. In our setting, knowledge of which bidders are colluding serves as side information and enables the design of truthful mechanisms that outperform posted-price mechanisms, which are the only truthful and collusion-proof mechanisms in the absence of side information
(
21
)
.

Currently, conclusions drawn from collusion detection algorithms are insufficient for legal incrimination, and instead some evidence of explicit communication between colluding parties is required
(
23
)
. Given this, we argue that our approach is a step towards developing collusion-resistant mechanisms, which can be useful as a preventative measure.

1.1
Main Technical Results

To get an intuition for the impact of collusion, consider the following example.

Example 1.1
.

Consider 5 bidders with private valuations
\{v_{1}=1,v_{2}=70,v_{3}=101,v_{4}=102,v_{5}=103\}
, and
r=3
items. The VCG mechanism allocates items to bidders 3,4,5 at a price of 70 each, and social welfare is
\sum_{i}v_{i}\mathds{1}\left(\text{bidder $i$ won}\right)=(101+102+103)=306
. If bidders 3 and 5 collude, they could bid shade and quote 0, 103, respectively. In this case, naively implementing VCG allocates items to bidders 2,4,5 at a lower price of 1, and social welfare decreases to
(70+102+103)=275
. The colluders benefit from bid shading as their net utility increases from
(v_{5}-p_{5})+(v_{3}-p_{3})=(103-70)+(101-70)=64
to
(103-1)+0=102
.

Surprisingly, in the above example, the non-colluding bidders are allotted an extra item and also benefit from the price drop. The auctioneer, however, suffers a huge loss in revenue. In fact, our first result shows that this is always the case with VCG mechanism, and that the negative impact of collusion is limited to the auctioneer:

Theorem 1.2

(Informal)
.

Consider the VCG mechanism for the single-item, multiple-unit auction with single-minded bidders. If the colluders aim to maximize their net additive utility, the following holds:

1.

The colluding bidders never benefit from bid loading, and always bid shade. As a consequence, they never take more items but they do lead to a drop in the VCG auction price.

2.

Neither the utility of each non-colluding bidder nor the net utility of colluding bidders decreases. However, the auctioneer’s utility (i.e., their revenue) is non-increasing.

3.

Due to collusion, the social welfare and revenue are also non-increasing, and the drop in welfare is entirely attributed to a decrease in the auctioneer’s utility.

It may seem counterintutitive that the colluding bidders do not bid load to take more items. The reason for this is that bid loading leads to a hike in the VCG price, and since the colluders do not have complementary effects in their net utility, they cannot make up for the loss from price hike. Instead, they bid shade, give up items if necessary, and cut down on the price to maximize their utility. This creates a win-win situation for both the colluding and non-colluding bidders while only hurting the auctioneer. We expect these results to extend to an auction with different types of items, and where each bidders’ valuation of a subset of items is complement-free.

Using the above characterization for colluding bidders’ strategy, we prove a Bulow-Klemperer type result for VCG auctions with colluding bidders, as stated below. The main implication is that adding colluding bidders to a group of non-colluding bidders does not worsen welfare or revenue, and that additional bidders are always beneficial. Intuitively, this is because in the worst case, all colluding bidders bid shade to zero and do not actively participate in the auction. But this is equivalent to a VCG auction with only the non-colluding bidders, which makes our worst case lower bound.

Theorem 1.3

(Informal)
.

In a multi-unit auction of a single good, when there are colluding bidders maximizing their net additive utility, the following holds:

\text{ VCG Welfare( Non-colluding bidders $\cup$ Colluding bidders)}\geq\text{VCG Welfare( Non-colluding bidders)}

\text{ VCG Revenue( Non-colluding bidders $\cup$ Colluding bidders)}\geq\text{VCG Revenue( Non-colluding bidders)}.

Despite the guarantees provided by the above theorem, colluding bidders may still deviate from truthful bidding in the VCG mechanism. To address this, we propose a hybrid mechanism that partitions bidders into non-colluding and colluding bidders, and applies two mechanisms, the VCG and the posted-price mechanism, respectively. We refer to this mechanism as Hybrid VCG (H-VCG). We show that H-VCG is ex-post dominant strategy incentive compatible (DSIC) for all bidders, even in the presence of collusion. We further establish guarantees on the expected welfare and revenue under the following assumptions: (1) bidders’ valuations are independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.), and (2) a black-box collusion detection algorithm is available to identify colluding bidders. Our main result is stated below.

Theorem 1.4

(Informal)
.

Let the bidders’ valuations be i.i.d. random variables with cumulative distribution function
F(\cdot)
.
When
F(\cdot)
is known, the expected welfare of the proposed mechanism can be optimized such that

\mathbb{E}\!\left[\text{Welf}_{\text{H-VCG}}(\text{Non-colluding}\cup\text{Colluding})\right]\geq\mathbb{E}\!\left[\text{Welf}_{\text{VCG}}(\text{Non-colluding})\right].

A similar guarantee holds for the expected revenue, i.e.,

\mathbb{E}\!\left[\text{Rev}_{\text{H-VCG}}(\text{Non-colluding}\cup\text{Colluding})\right]\geq\mathbb{E}\!\left[\text{Rev}_{\text{VCG}}(\text{Non-colluding})\right],

with a probability that depends on how the items are divided between the non-colluding and colluding bidders.

If, instead,
F(\cdot)
is unknown but invertible, and its inverse (the quantile function)
Q(\cdot)
satisfies

Lx\leq Q(x)\leq Ux\quad\text{for some known constants }L,U>0,

we design two mechanisms that respectively optimize minorants of the expected welfare and expected revenue. We further show that the corresponding welfare and revenue guarantees hold probabilistically. Numerical simulations demonstrate that these mechanisms perform well empirically.
Note that since the expected revenue serves as a lower bound on the expected welfare, it can be interpreted as a minorant of the welfare objective.

In H-VCG, determining how to allocate the total number of items between the two sub-groups of bidders is non-trivial. Our key idea is to optimize the expected welfare (or a good proxy thereof), which in turn determines the item split. We show that our H-VCG mechanism is (ex-post) dominant strategy incentive compatible (DSIC) for all bidders.

Finally, we conduct numerical experiments using different distributions for bidders’ valuations to compare four mechanisms:
(1) VCG assuming both non-colluding and colluding bidders are truthful,
(2) VCG applied only to the non-colluding bidders,
(3) VCG in the presence of collusion and bid shading (i.e., untruthful VCG), and
(4) our proposed H-VCG mechanism. In our setup, where colluding bidders do not bid load but instead shade their bids to maximize their total utility, we observe that H-VCG consistently outperforms VCG applied only to the non-colluding bidders, both in terms of average welfare and average revenue. As the number of non-colluding bidders increases, the performance of H-VCG approaches that of the best baseline, the VCG mechanism with all bidders assumed to be truthful.

Since H-VCG enforces truthfulness among colluding bidders through a posted-price mechanism and does not guarantee that all items are sold, some welfare and revenue loss is inevitable. However, when the group of colluding bidders is sufficiently large, no items remain unsold under H-VCG, and it outperforms all other mechanisms except the idealized VCG with fully truthful bidders, a theoretical upper bound that no mechanism can surpass.

1.2
Related Work

Mechanism design with side information.
In contrast to our work that considers the knowledge of colluding bidders as side information, most prior work considers side information about bidder types, i.e., parameters related to preferences or item valuations
(
17
;
4
;
36
)
. For instance, information about correlated bidder types has been used to identify bidders generating the least welfare (i.e., the weakest types) and to improve the performance of the VCG mechanism
(
4
;
36
)
. Devanur et al.
(
17
)
study revenue-maximizing auctions where bidders lie on a continuum and can be distinguished using side information. Learning-augmented algorithms have also been used to design strategy-proof mechanisms in application-specific domains such as job scheduling
(
5
)
and facility location
(
1
)
.

Prior-independent mechanism design.
The VCG mechanism, widely adopted for welfare maximization, does not require prior information about the distribution of bidders’ valuations
(
22
;
12
)
. In contrast, the Myerson auction which is optimal for revenue maximization, does rely on such priors. Subsequent work on prior-independent auctions has achieved constant-factor approximation guarantees to the optimal expected revenue and analyzed sample complexity requirements for heterogeneous bidders and specific distribution classes
(
18
;
13
)
. The truthful auctions proposed in this paper offer similar constant-approximation guarantees for welfare and revenue. While we do not assume complete knowledge of the valuation distributions, we require certain parameters that bound their quantiles.

Another foundational result in this domain is the Bulow–Klemperer theorem, which shows that welfare maximization via a prior-independent VCG with additional bidders can achieve nearly optimal revenue
(
8
;
25
;
19
)
. In a similar spirit, we establish a Bulow–Klemperer type result in the presence of colluding bidders, showing that in a (non-truthful) VCG mechanism, adding more bidders improves both the total welfare and the revenue, even when some bidders collude.

Collusion-resistant mechanisms.
Most auction mechanisms, including VCG, are vulnerable to collusion
(
30
;
3
)
. The notion of collusion-proofness, where no subset of bidders can benefit through communication or side payments, is quite strong and restricts mechanisms to posted-price formats, which cannot optimize non-trivial objectives without prior information about the bids
(
21
)
. As a result, weaker notions of collusion-resistance, such as t-truthfulness and group strategy-proofness, are often considered. Under t-truthfulness, where any coalition of size at most t maximizes its total expected utility by bidding truthfully, approximately efficient and optimal mechanisms have been proposed using consensus techniques
(
21
)
. An even weaker notion, group strategy-proofness, disallows monetary transfers among colluding bidders and requires that any deviation benefiting one bidder must harm another
(
26
;
6
)
. Mechanisms satisfying this property have been characterized and studied in various settings, including network design games
(
32
;
11
;
34
)
and queueing models
(
31
;
27
)
. In contrast, this paper introduces a new notion of collusion-resistant mechanism design: Given knowledge of which bidders are colluding, how can we guarantee that they bid truthfully?

Collusion detection.

We assume access to a black-box collusion detection algorithm that classifies bidders into colluding and non-colluding groups. Collusion detection, particularly in public procurement auctions, has been extensively studied using statistical techniques
(
10
;
14
;
29
)
. A comparative study of machine learning approaches for collusion detection is presented in
(
20
)
, while a game-theoretic approach based on mutual information is proposed in
(
7
)
. In the more recent context of tacit algorithmic collusion
(
2
;
9
)
, a variety of methods have been developed, including dynamic testing
(
23
)
and statistical testing
(
24
)
.

2
Preliminaries

We consider a set of all bidders
\mathcal{M}
with
|\mathcal{M}|=M
. We assume
\mathcal{M}
is partitioned into two disjoint subsets: the non-colluding bidders
\mathcal{N}
and the colluding bidders
\mathcal{C}
, such that
\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{N}\cup\mathcal{C}
and
M=|\mathcal{M}|=|\mathcal{N}|+|\mathcal{C}|=N+C
.

Let
b=(b_{1},\cdots,b_{M})
and
v=(v_{1},\cdots,v_{M})
be the bid and true valuation vectors, respectively, where
b_{i}
is the bid received from bidder
i
. Designing an auction mechanism comprises two components: (1) an allocation scheme
x_{i}(b)
, and (2) a payment scheme
p_{i}(b)
for each bidder
i
. These are determined before the bids are collected.

Definition 2.1

(Utility of Bidders and Auctioneer)
.

We assume that each bidder
i
is rational and submits bid
b_{i}
to maximize their utility
u_{i}(b_{i},b_{i-})=v_{i}x_{i}(b_{i},b_{i-})-p_{i}(b_{i},b_{i-})
, where
b_{i-}
are the bids of all other bidders except
i
. The auctioneer receives utility from the total payments collected, defined as
u_{a}(b)=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{M}}p_{i}(b)
.

Given this framework, we define welfare and revenue. In the literature, auctions that maximize welfare and revenue are referred to as
efficient
and
optimal
auctions, respectively. This paper focuses on efficiency while maximizing revenue to the extent possible.

Definition 2.2

(Welfare and Revenue)
.

Welfare is the total utility of all participants (all bidders and the auctioneer). Hence, welfare is
\text{Welf}(b)=u_{a}(b)+\sum_{i\in\mathcal{M}}u_{i}(b)=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{M}}v_{i}x_{i}(b)
. Revenue is the total payment made to the auctioneer:
\text{Rev}(b)=u_{a}(b)=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{M}}p_{i}(b)
.

We now introduce notation based on order statistics to allow for clear reference to ranked bids and valuations. For any set of bidders
S
, let
b^{S}_{k}
and
v^{S}_{k}
be the
k^{th}
-largest bid and true valuations within
S
, respectively. Define
\mathcal{B}^{S}_{r}=\{b^{S}_{1},\cdots,b^{S}_{r}\}
and
\mathcal{V}^{S}_{r}=\{v^{S}_{1},\cdots,v^{S}_{r}\}
to be the sets of the top
r
bids and true valuations from
S
, respectively.

Definition 2.3

(VCG Mechanism)
.

In an auction with
r
identical items, the VCG mechanism prescribes: (1) allocation
x_{i}(b)=\mathbf{1}\left(b_{i}\in\mathcal{B}^{\mathcal{M}}_{r}\right)
, (2) payment
p_{i}(b)=b^{\mathcal{M}}_{r+1}\mathbf{1}\left(b_{i}\in\mathcal{B}^{\mathcal{M}}_{r}\right)
for each bidder
i
. In the absence of collusion, the mechanism ensures (ex-post) DSIC by guaranteeing each winning bidder
i
an information rent of
v_{i}-p_{i}(b)
, which incentivizes truth-telling (
b=v
). This property, in turn, guarantees efficiency since the auction maximizes the welfare
\sum_{i}v_{i}x_{i}(b)
and the items are given to bidders who value them the most.

2.1
Model for Colluding Bidders

Let
b^{\mathcal{N}}
and
b^{\mathcal{C}}
be the bids submitted by the non-colluding and colluding bidders, respectively. True valuations
v^{\mathcal{N}}
and
v^{\mathcal{C}}
are defined similarly.

Definition 2.4

(Utility of Colluding Bidders)
.

We assume that the colluding bidders act as a unified entity to maximize the sum of their individual utilities, allowing for both information exchange and monetary transfers amongst them. The joint utility of the colluding bidders is:
u_{\mathcal{C}}(b^{\mathcal{C}},b^{\mathcal{N}})=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{C}}v_{i}x_{i}(b^{\mathcal{C}},b^{\mathcal{N}})-p_{i}(b^{\mathcal{C}},b^{\mathcal{N}})
.

3
VCG in the Presence of Collusion

We characterize the effects of colluding bidders on welfare and revenue when the VCG auction is conducted without accounting for collusion. Define
r_{N}
and
r_{C}
as the number of items allotted to non-colluding (
\mathcal{N}
) and colluding (
\mathcal{C}
) bidders, respectively, such that
r_{N}+r_{C}=r
(total items).

Lemma 3.1

(Colluders’ Strategy in VCG)
.

Since the VCG mechanism is DSIC for non-colluding bidders, their bids
\mathbf{b}^{N}
can be assumed to be their true valuations
\mathbf{v}^{N}
. The colluding bidders’ optimal strategy to maximize their joint utility is as follows:

1.

Given that a fixed number of items
r_{C}
are allotted to colluding bidders (and the remaining
r_{N}=r-r_{C}
items are allotted to non-colluding bidders), their optimal bids are
b^{C}_{i}=\max\{v^{C}_{i},b^{N}_{r_{N}+1}+\epsilon\}

\forall i\in\{1,\cdots,r_{C}\}
, and
b^{C}_{i}=0

\forall i\in\{r_{C}+1,\cdots,C\}
, for some small
\epsilon>0
. Here,
b^{N}_{r_{N}+1}
is the largest losing bid among non-colluding bidders.

Define
u_{C}(\mathbf{v}^{N};r_{C})
to be the optimal utility achieved through this strategy. The maximum utility is:

\displaystyle u_{C}(\mathbf{v}^{N};r_{C})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\max_{\mathbf{b}^{C}}\left\{u_{C}\left(\mathbf{b}^{C},\mathbf{v}^{N}\right)\mid\sum_{i\in C}x_{i}(\mathbf{b}^{C},\mathbf{v}^{N})=r_{C}\right\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{r_{C}}v^{C}_{i}-r_{C}\cdot b^{N}_{r_{N}+1}.

2.

Let
r_{C}^{*}
and
r_{N}^{*}
be the VCG allocation to colluding and non-colluding bidders, respectively, in the absence of collusion. Even when manipulating their bids, the colluding bidders do not desire more than
r_{C}^{*}
items. That is,
u_{C}(\mathbf{v}^{N};r_{C}^{*})\geq u_{C}(\mathbf{v}^{N};r_{C}^{*}+\Delta r)
,
\forall\Delta r>0
.

Proof.

The colluders maximize
u_{C}(b^{C},v^{N})=\sum_{i\in C}v_{i}x_{i}-p_{i}
. For
\sum_{i\in C}x_{i}=r_{C}
, the
r_{C}
largest colluder bids win. The VCG payment
p_{i}
is
\max\{b^{C}_{r_{C}+1},b^{N}_{r_{N}+1}\}
.

Optimal Bids.
The choice
b^{C}_{i}=0
for
i\in\{r_{C}+1,\dots,C\}
minimizes the payment, since
b^{C}_{r_{C}+1}=0
. The winning bids
b^{C}_{i}=\max\{v^{C}_{i},b^{N}_{r_{N}+1}+\epsilon\}
for
i\in\{1,\dots,r_{C}\}
ensure allocation and
b^{C}_{i}>b^{N}_{r_{N}+1}
for small
\epsilon>0
. Since payment
p_{i}=b^{N}_{r_{N}+1}
, the maximum utility for fixed
r_{C}
is
u_{C}(b^{C},v^{N})=\sum_{i=1}^{r_{C}}v^{C}_{i}-r_{C}\cdot b^{N}_{r_{N}+1}
.

Optimal Item Count.
The welfare-maximizing allocation is
(r_{C}^{*},r_{N}^{*})
. For any
\Delta r>0
:

\displaystyle u_{C}(v^{N};r_{C}^{*}+\Delta r)-u_{C}(v^{N};r_{C}^{*})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=r_{C}^{*}+1}^{r_{C}^{*}+\Delta r}v^{C}_{i}-\left(r_{C}^{*}+\Delta r\right)b^{N}_{r_{N}^{*}-\Delta r+1}+r_{C}^{*}b^{N}_{r_{N}^{*}+1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{\Delta r}\left(v^{C}_{r_{C}^{*}+i}-b^{N}_{r_{N}^{*}-\Delta r+1}\right)+r_{C}^{*}\left(b^{N}_{r_{N}^{*}+1}-b^{N}_{r_{N}^{*}-\Delta r+1}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 0.

This holds because: (1)
v^{C}_{r_{C}^{*}+i}\leq b^{N}_{r_{N}^{*}}
for
i\geq 1
, as these are items the colluders didn’t win in the truthful VCG allocation, and
b^{N}_{r_{N}^{*}-\Delta r+1}\geq b^{N}_{r_{N}^{*}}
; thus
v^{C}_{r_{C}^{*}+i}\leq b^{N}_{r_{N}^{*}-\Delta r+1}
. (2)
b^{N}
is sorted in descending order, and
b^{N}_{r_{N}^{*}+1}\leq b^{N}_{r_{N}^{*}-\Delta r+1}
since
\Delta r>0
. Both parts of the sum are non-positive.
∎

The optimal colluders’ strategy from the above Lemma is useful to obtain more insights into the equilibrium of the VCG mechanism.

Theorem 3.2

(VCG Equilibrium in Presence of Collusion)
.

Let
r_{C}^{Col}(r_{C}^{*})
,
r_{N}^{Col}(r_{N}^{*})
and
b^{Col}(b^{*})
be the number of items allocated to colluding bidders, items allocated to non-colluding bidders, and the optimal bids in the presence (absence) of collusion, respectively, using the VCG mechanism. Then, the following holds:

1.

Colluding bidders do not obtain more items through collusion, i.e.,
r_{C}^{Col}\leq r_{C}^{*}
. Furthermore, colluding bidders always shade their bids and never bid load, i.e.,
b^{C,Col}_{i}\leq v^{C}_{i}
,
\forall i\in C
. In fact, they either bid
0
or their true valuation
v^{C}_{i}
.

2.

The utility of each non-colluding bidder and of the collective of colluding bidders does not decrease due to collusion. That is,
u_{i}(b^{*})\leq u_{i}(b^{Col})

\forall i\in N
and
u_{C}(b^{*})\leq u_{C}(b^{Col})
. However, the revenue of the auctioneer does not increase, and could instead decrease. That is,
u_{a}(b^{*})\geq u_{a}(b^{Col})
.

3.

Welfare and revenue are both non-increasing as a consequence of collusion. That is,
\text{Welf}(b^{*})\geq\text{Welf}(b^{Col})
and
\text{Rev}(b^{*})\geq\text{Rev}(b^{Col})
.

Proof.

Allocation and Bids.
By Lemma
3.1
,
r_{C}^{Col}\leq r_{C}^{*}
. Hence,
r_{N}^{Col}\geq r_{N}^{*}
and
v^{C}_{i}>b^{N}_{r_{N}^{Col}+1}
(the payment price for winning items). The optimal winning bids are
b^{C,Col}_{i}=\max\{v^{C}_{i},b^{N}_{r_{N}^{Col}+1}+\epsilon\}=v^{C}_{i}
for
i\in\{1,\dots,r_{C}^{Col}\}
and losing bids
b^{C,Col}_{i}=0
for
i\in\{r_{C}^{Col}+1,\dots,C\}
. That is, colluding bidders are incentivized to bid as low as possible to win, and always bid either
v^{C}_{i}
or
0
ensuring
b^{C,Col}_{i}\leq v^{C}_{i}
(bid shading).

Utility.
Since
r_{N}^{C}ol\geq r_{N}^{*}
,the non-colluders’ allocation is non-decreasing:
x_{i}(b^{Col})\geq x_{i}(b^{*})

\forall i\in N
. The collusive payment
p(b^{Col})=b^{N}_{r_{N}^{Col}+1}
and truthful payment
p(b^{*})=\max\{v^{C}_{r_{C}^{*}+1},b^{N}_{r_{N}^{*}+1}\}
. Since
r_{N}^{Col}\geq r_{N}^{*}
,
b^{N}_{r_{N}^{Col}+1}\leq b^{N}_{r_{N}^{*}+1}
, so
p(b^{Col})\leq p(b^{*})
.

\displaystyle\text{Non-Colluder Utility: }u_{i}(b^{Col})=x_{i}(b^{Col})v_{i}-p(b^{Col})\geq x_{i}(b^{*})v_{i}-p(b^{*})=u_{i}(b^{*}).

\displaystyle\text{Colluder Utility: }u_{C}(b^{Col})=u_{C}(v^{N};r^{Col}_{C})\geq u_{C}(v^{N};r^{*}_{C})=u_{C}(b^{*}),\text{ by definition of }r^{Col}_{C}.

\displaystyle\text{Auctioneer Revenue: }u_{a}(b^{Col})=r\cdot p(b^{Col})\leq r\cdot p(b^{*})=u_{a}(b^{*}).

Welfare and Revenue.
VCG without collusion maximizes social welfare,
\text{Welf}(b^{*})
. Any deviation,
b^{Col}
, that results in a different allocation must yield
\text{Welf}(b^{Col})\leq\text{Welf}(b^{*})
. This, combined with
\text{Rev}(b^{Col})\leq\text{Rev}(b^{*})
(from the auctioneer’s utility), confirms that both welfare and revenue are non-increasing.
∎

The fact that colluding bidders only bid shade and do not bid load leads to an interesting Bulow-Klemperer type result – that additional bidders improve welfare and revenue, even if colluding.

Theorem 3.3

(BK Theorem for Collusion)
.

In a multi-unit auction of a single good, when there are colluding bidders maximizing their net additive utility, the following holds:

\text{Welf${}_{VCG}$($\mathcal{N}$ $\cup$ $\mathcal{C}$)}\geq\text{Welf${}_{VCG}$($\mathcal{N}$)}

\text{Rev${}_{VCG}$($\mathcal{N}$ $\cup$ $\mathcal{C}$)}\geq\text{Rev${}_{VCG}$($\mathcal{N}$)},

where Welf
VCG
(
\mathcal{S}
) and Rev
VCG
(
\mathcal{S}
) denotes the welfare and revenue respectively achieved using a VCG mechanism on bidders in set
\mathcal{S}
some of who could be colluding and reporting untruthful bids.

Proof.

From Theorem
3.2
, when all
\mathcal{N}\cup\mathcal{C}
bidders participate in a VCG mechanism, the non-colluding bidders bid
v^{N}
(i.e. truthfully) and the colluding bidders bid
b^{C,Col}\leq v^{C}
(i.e. either bid-shade to 0 or report their true valuation). The welfare achieved is Welf(
v^{N},b^{C,Col})
. Since the VCG mechanism allocates items to the top bidders, when a colluding bidder
i
bid shades to
0
, an item could be allocated to a bidder
j
who values it less than
i
leading to a welfare loss. In the worst case, all the colluders bid-shade to
0
achieving a welfare Welf(
v^{N},0
). Hence, Welf(
v^{N},b^{C,Col})\geq
Welf(
v^{N},0
).

Now, consider a VCG auction with only the non-colluding bidders
\mathcal{N}
achieving a welfare Welf(
v^{N}
). Since the bidders have non-negative valuations, adding
|\mathcal{C}|
more bids of
0
cannot hurt the welfare, i.e. Welf(
v^{N}
)
\leq
Welf(
v^{N},0
). The result follows from Welf(
v^{N},b^{C,Col})\geq
Welf(
v^{N},0
)
\geq
Welf(
v^{N}
).

The proof for revenue is very similar. When
|\mathcal{N}|>r
, the price drops to
v^{N}_{r+1}
in the worst case when all colluding bidders bid-shade to
0
, and the revenue Rev
VCG
(
\mathcal{N}

\cup

\mathcal{C})\geq v^{N}_{r+1}r=
Rev
{}_{VCG}(\mathcal{N})
. When
|\mathcal{N}|\leq r
, Rev
VCG
(
\mathcal{N}
) = 0 and Rev
VCG
(
\mathcal{N}

\cup

\mathcal{C})\geq
Rev
{}_{VCG}(\mathcal{N})
holds trivially.
∎

4
Truthful Auction in the Presence of Collusion

Since colluding bidders are untruthful in the VCG mechanism, we henceforth focus on designing mechanisms that remain truthful in the presence of collusion. To this end, we introduce a mechanism, which we refer to as Hybrid VCG (H-VCG) mechanism. This mechanism partitions the bidders into two sub-groups- of non-colluding and colluding bidders- and implements the VCG mechanism and the posted price on these subgroups respectively. The choice of posted-price for colluding bidders is because it is the only collusion-proof and truthful mechanism
(
21
)
. Identifying the set of colluding bidders
\mathcal{C}
is essential to ensure the auction is truthful. This allows us to ensure that price and allocation rules are independent of the colluding bids, and that our mechanism is (ex-post) DSIC for all bidders. The mechanism is outlined in Algorithm
1
.

Algorithm 1

Hybrid VCG (H-VCG) Mechanism

1:

Input:
Total number of items
r
; non-colluding bidders
\mathcal{N}
and colluding bidders
\mathcal{C}
with bids
b^{N}
and
b^{C}
, respectively; objective function
M\left(k\right)
.

2:

Calculate
k^{*}\leftarrow\arg\max_{k}M\left(k\right)

3:

Phase 1: Run VCG on
\mathcal{N}
with
k^{*}
items

4:

Winning non-colluding bidders:
\mathcal{W}_{N}\leftarrow\{i\in\mathcal{N}\mid b_{i}\in\mathcal{B}^{N}_{k^{*}}\}
.

5:

Phase 2: Run posted price on
\mathcal{C}
with
r-k^{*}
items

6:

Qualifying colluding bidders:
\hat{\mathcal{W}}_{C}\leftarrow\{\,i\in\mathcal{C}\mid b_{i}^{\mathcal{C}}>b^{N}_{k^{*}+1}\,\}.

7:

if

|\hat{\mathcal{W}}_{C}|>r-k^{*}

then

8:

Winning colluding bidders: randomly select a subset
\mathcal{W}_{C}\subseteq\hat{\mathcal{W}}_{C}
of size
r-k^{*}
.

9:

else

10:

\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{C}}\leftarrow\hat{\mathcal{W}}_{\mathcal{C}}
.

11:

end

if

12:

Winners:
\mathcal{W}\leftarrow\mathcal{W}_{N}\cup\mathcal{W}_{C}

13:

Allocate items:
x_{i}\leftarrow 1

\forall i\in\mathcal{W}

14:

Charge price:
p_{i}\leftarrow b^{N}_{k^{*}+1}

\forall i\in\mathcal{W}
.

Notice that our posted price is set using the VCG price from non-colluding bidders, and hence all winning bidders are charged the same price. In the below lemma, we claim that our mechanism is truthful for all bidders.

Lemma 4.1

(H-VCG Truthfulness)
.

The mechanism in Algorithm
1
is (ex-post) DSIC for all bidders, including colluding bidders
\mathcal{C}
.

Proof.

Consider bidder
i\in\mathcal{N}
. Phase 1 applies the VCG mechanism to allocate
k
items among non-colluding bidders, where
k
is pre-determined and independent of all bids. In VCG, it is a dominant strategy to bid truthfully whenever
p_{i}(b^{N})=b^{N}_{k+1}x_{i}(b^{N})
, and individual rationality also holds
(
22
;
12
)
.

Consider bidder
i\in\mathcal{C}
. Phase 2 allocates the remaining
r-k
items to colluding bidders via the posted price
b^{N}_{k+1}
. In our model for colluding bidders, we consider them to be maximizing their total utility
\sum_{i\in C}u_{i}(b)
. Since they exchange monetary payments and items amongst themselves, it suffices to consider the top bidding colluders while calculating their net utility. Hence,
\sum_{i\in C}u_{i}(b)=\sum_{i=1}^{r-k}\left(v^{C}_{i}-b^{N}_{k+1}\right)1\{b^{C}_{i}>b^{N}_{k+1}\}
. For fixed
b^{N}_{k+1}
, this net utility is maximized when
v^{C}_{i}>b^{N}_{k+1}\Rightarrow 1\{b^{C}_{i}>b^{N}_{k+1}\}=1
. Hence, bidding truthfully, i.e.,
v_{i}^{C}=b_{i}^{C}

\forall i\in C
is a utility-maximizing strategy.

Hence, for every bidder, truthful reporting is a weakly dominant strategy, and the mechanism is (ex-post) DSIC.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

It is crucial to carefully design how items are allocated in the H-VCG mechanism. For example, if the
r
items were randomly allocated among all bidders (colluding and non-colluding) rather than assigning
k
items to non-colluding bidders and the remaining to colluders, the mechanism would no longer be truthful. In such a case, colluders could strategically bid the maximum allowed value to increase their expected allocation without affecting the price. Consider
r=2
, non-colluding bids
{1,2}
, and colluding valuations
{1,2,3}
. If the price is
p=1
, then colluders bidding
{3,3,3}
would increase their expected number of items when items are randomly allocated among bidders with bids above
p
. Hence, a careful allocation design is essential to preserve truthfulness.

The key idea of our mechanism is to set the value of
k
using
M(k)
as the expected welfare (or an appropriate proxy thereof) to achieve strong welfare guarantees. We use prior information about the distribution of bidders’ valuations to calculate this objective function
M(k)
. Before discussing possible formulations of
M(k)
, we introduce the following assumption, which is required to compute the expected welfare.

Assumption 4.1
.

Let the true valuations of all the bidders be i.i.d. and come from a distribution with cumulative density function (CDF)
\mathcal{F}(\cdot)
, which is invertible, with the inverse as the quantile function
\mathcal{Q}(\cdot)
. Sufficient conditions for invertibility of
F(\cdot)
are monotonicity and continuous differentiability.

4.1
Exact Expected Welfare Optimization

Let
\mathrm{Welf}_{\textsc{H-VCG}}(\mathcal{N}\cup\mathcal{C};k)
and
\mathrm{Rev}_{\textsc{H-VCG}}(\mathcal{N}\cup\mathcal{C};k)
be the welfare and revenue respectively from the H-VCG mechanism when
k
items are allocated to non-colluding bidders. In the next lemma, we derive a useful expression for the expected welfare.

Lemma 4.3

(Expected Welfare)
.

Let Assumption
4.1
hold. The expected welfare from the H-VCG mechanism can be calculated as follows:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{Welf}_{\textsc{H-VCG}}(\mathcal{N}\cup\mathcal{C};k)\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{E}\!\left[Q(u)\ \middle|\ u\geq F\!\left(v^{\textsc{n}}_{k+1}\right)\right]\cdot\left(k+\mathbb{E}\!\left[\min\{|\mathcal{W}_{C}|,\,r-k\}\right]\right)\mid v^{N}_{k+1}\right],

where
u\sim\mathrm{Uniform}[0,1]
and

|\mathcal{W}_{C}|\mid v^{N}_{k+1}\sim\mathrm{Binomial}\!\left(C,\,1-F\!\left(v^{N}_{k+1}\right)\right)
.

Proof.

First, we calculate the conditional expected welfare, conditioned on all the non-colluding losing bids
\left(v^{N}_{k+1},\cdots,v^{N}_{N}\right)
and note that this is equivalent to conditioning on highest non-colluding losing bid
v^{N}_{k+1}
. Also, since the colluding bidders can exchange both items and monetary payments among themselves, the specific allocation of items to individual bidders is irrelevant. For welfare computation, it suffices to consider the highest colluding bids among the winners. Using Assumption
4.1
and probability integral transform of continuous random variables,
v^{N}_{k}=\mathcal{Q}(u^{N}_{k})
(see Equation
(2.3.7)
of
(
15
)
), where
u^{N}_{k}
is
k-
th largest order statistic of
N
samples of standard uniform random variables. Using
Q(\cdot)=F^{-1}(\cdot)
, we get
F(v^{N}_{k})=u^{N}_{k}
. We use these facts to simplify the expression
\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{Welf}_{\textsc{H-VCG}}(\mathcal{N}\cup\mathcal{C};k)\mid(v^{N}_{k+1},\cdots,v^{N}_{N})\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{i\in\mathcal{N}\cup\mathcal{C}}v_{i}x_{i}(v)\mid v^{N}_{k+1}\right]
.

\displaystyle\textstyle\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\sum_{i\in\mathcal{N}\cup\mathcal{C}}v_{i}x_{i}(v)\ |\ v^{\textsc{n}}_{k+1}\big]

\displaystyle=\textstyle\mathbb{E}\big[\sum_{i=1}^{k}v^{\textsc{n}}_{i}\ |\ v^{\textsc{n}}_{k+1}\big]+\sum_{q=1}^{r-k-1}\mathbb{E}\big[\sum_{i=1}^{q}v_{i}^{\textsc{c}}\ |\ v^{\textsc{n}}_{k+1}\big]\cdot\mathbb{P}\big(\left|\mathcal{W}_{\textsc{c}}\right|=q\big)+\mathbb{E}\big(\sum_{i=1}^{r-k}v_{i}^{\textsc{c}}\big)\cdot\mathbb{P}\big(\left|\mathcal{W}_{\textsc{c}}\right|\geq r-k\big)

\displaystyle=\textstyle\mathbb{E}\big[\sum_{i=1}^{k}v^{\textsc{n}}_{i}\ |\ v^{\textsc{n}}_{k+1}\big]+\sum_{q=1}^{r-k-1}\mathbb{E}\big[\sum_{i=1}^{q}v_{i}^{\textsc{c}}\ |\ v^{\textsc{n}}_{k+1}\big]\cdot\binom{\textsc{c}}{q}\big(1-F(v_{k+1}^{\textsc{n}})\big)^{q}\big(F(v_{k+1}^{\textsc{n}})\big)^{\textsc{c}-q}+

\displaystyle\textstyle\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\sum_{i=1}^{r-k}v_{i}^{\textsc{c}}\ |\ v^{\textsc{n}}_{k+1}\big]\cdot\sum_{q=r-k}^{\textsc{c}}\binom{\textsc{c}}{q}\big(1-F(v_{k+1}^{\textsc{n}})\big)^{q}\big(F(v_{k+1}^{\textsc{n}})\big)^{\textsc{c}-q}

\displaystyle=\textstyle\mathbb{E}\big[\sum_{i=1}^{k}Q(u^{\textsc{n}}_{i})\ |\ u_{k+1}^{\textsc{n}}=F(v^{\textsc{n}}_{k+1})\big]+\sum_{q=1}^{r-k-1}\mathbb{E}\big[\sum_{i=1}^{q}Q(u_{i}^{\textsc{c}})|\ u_{k+1}^{\textsc{n}}=F(v^{\textsc{n}}_{k+1})\big]\cdot\binom{\textsc{c}}{q}\big(1-F(v_{k+1}^{\textsc{n}})\big)^{q}\big(F(v_{k+1}^{\textsc{n}})\big)^{\textsc{c}-q}+

\displaystyle\textstyle\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\sum_{i=1}^{r-k}Q(u_{i}^{\textsc{c}})|\ u_{k+1}^{\textsc{n}}=F(v^{\textsc{n}}_{k+1})\big]\cdot\sum_{q=r-k}^{\textsc{c}}\binom{\textsc{c}}{q}\big(1-F(v_{k+1}^{\textsc{n}})\big)^{q}\big(F(v_{k+1}^{\textsc{n}})\big)^{\textsc{c}-q}

\displaystyle=\textstyle k\cdot\mathbb{E}\big[Q(u)\ |\ u\geq F(v^{\textsc{n}}_{k+1})\big]+\sum_{q=1}^{r-k-1}q\cdot\mathbb{E}\big[Q(u)|\ u\geq F(v^{\textsc{n}}_{k+1})\big]\cdot\binom{\textsc{c}}{q}\big(1-F(v_{k+1}^{\textsc{n}})\big)^{q}\big(F(v_{k+1}^{\textsc{n}})\big)^{\textsc{c}-q}+

\displaystyle\textstyle\displaystyle(r-k)\cdot\mathbb{E}\big[Q(u)|\ u\geq F(v^{\textsc{n}}_{k+1})\big]\cdot\sum_{q=r-k}^{\textsc{c}}\binom{\textsc{c}}{q}\big(1-F(v_{k+1}^{\textsc{n}})\big)^{q}\big(F(v_{k+1}^{\textsc{n}})\big)^{\textsc{c}-q}

\displaystyle=\textstyle k\cdot\mathbb{E}\big[Q(u)\ |\ u\geq F(v^{\textsc{n}}_{k+1})\big]+\sum_{q=1}^{\textsc{c}}\min\{q,r-k\}\cdot\mathbb{E}\big[Q(u)|\ u\geq F(v^{\textsc{n}}_{k+1})\big]\cdot\binom{\textsc{c}}{q}\big(1-F(v_{k+1}^{\textsc{n}})\big)^{q}\big(F(v_{k+1}^{\textsc{n}})\big)^{\textsc{c}-q}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\big[Q(u)\ |\ u\geq F(v^{\textsc{n}}_{k+1})\big]\cdot\left(\textstyle k+\cdot\mathbb{E}\big[\min\{|\mathcal{W}_{C}|,r-k\}\mid F(v^{N}_{k+1})\big]\right).

Above,
u\sim\text{Uniform}[0,1]
and
|\mathcal{W}_{C}|\mid v^{N}_{k+1}\sim\text{Binomial}\left(C,1-F\left(v^{N}_{k+1}\right)\right)
are the total number of winning colluding bidders. Taking an expectation over
v^{N}_{k+1}
is the above expression gives the required result.
∎

When the CDF
F(\cdot)
and quantile
Q(\cdot)
are known, the above lemma gives a way to easily compute the expected welfare numerically. In the next theorem, we derive welfare guarantees when
M(k)
is set to this expected welfare.

Theorem 4.4

(Expected Welfare Optimization)
.

Let Assumption
4.1
hold. Set
M\left(k\right)=\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{Welf}_{\textsc{H-VCG}}(\mathcal{N}\cup\mathcal{C};k)\right]
as in Lemma
4.3
. Then, the Hybrid-VCG mechanism (
H-VCG
) has the following welfare guarantees:

\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{Welf}_{\textsc{H-VCG}}(\mathcal{N}\cup\mathcal{C};k^{*})\right]\;\geq\;\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{Welf}_{\textsc{VCG}}(\mathcal{N})\right],

where
\mathrm{Welf}_{\textsc{VCG}}(\mathcal{N})
is the welfare achieved using the VCG mechanism on non-colluding bidders. Moreover, the following revenue guarantee holds with probability at least
P(k^{*})
:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{Rev}_{\textsc{H-VCG}}(\mathcal{N}\cup\mathcal{C};k^{*})\right]\;\geq\;\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{Rev}_{\textsc{VCG}}(\mathcal{N})\right]

where
P(k^{*})=\sum_{q=r-k^{*}}^{\textsc{c}}\binom{\textsc{c}}{q}\frac{B(C+N-k^{*}-q,q+k^{*}+1)}{B(N-k^{*},k^{*}+1)}
with
B(\cdot,\cdot)
is the beta function and
\mathrm{Rev}_{\textsc{VCG}}(\mathcal{N})
is the revenue achieved using the VCG mechanism on non-colluding bidders.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.1
, all bids are known to be truthful. When
k=r
, all the items are allocated to non-colluding bidders, and H-VCG simply runs the usual VCG mechanism on the non-colluding bidders. Hence,
\mathrm{Welf}_{\textsc{H-VCG}}(\mathcal{N}\cup\mathcal{C};r)=\mathrm{Welf}_{\textsc{VCG}}(\mathcal{N})
. Since H-VCG uses
k^{*}=\arg\max_{k}M(k)
, setting
M\left(k\right)=\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{Welf}_{\textsc{H-VCG}}(\mathcal{N}\cup\mathcal{C};k)\right]
maximizes the expected welfare, and
\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{Welf}_{\textsc{H-VCG}}(\mathcal{N}\cup\mathcal{C};k^{*})\right]\geq\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{Welf}_{\textsc{H-VCG}}(\mathcal{N}\cup\mathcal{C};r)\right]
. Combining it all, we get

\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{Welf}_{\textsc{H-VCG}}(\mathcal{N}\cup\mathcal{C};k^{*})\right]\geq\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{Welf}_{\textsc{H-VCG}}(\mathcal{N}\cup\mathcal{C};r)\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{Welf}_{\textsc{VCG}}(\mathcal{N})\right].

Next, we prove the revenue guarantees. If
k^{*}=r
then
\mathrm{Rev}_{\textsc{H-VCG}}(\mathcal{N}\cup\mathcal{C};k^{*})=\mathrm{Rev}_{\textsc{VCG}}(\mathcal{N}).
If, instead,
k^{*}<r
then the price set by the mechanism
b^{N}_{k^{*}+1}
is higher than the VCG price with just the non-colluding bidders
b^{N}_{r+1}
, i.e.,
b^{N}_{k^{*}+1}>b^{N}_{r+1}
. Hence, when all items are sold by H-VCG mechanism then the revenue achieved is higher than that of VCG with the non-colluding bidders as the price cut-off is higher. All items are sold with the probability
\mathbb{P}\!\left(|\hat{\mathcal{W}}_{C}|>\,r-k\right)
calculated as follows:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{P}\!\left(|\hat{\mathcal{W}}_{C}|>\,r-k\mid v^{N}_{k^{*}+1}\right)\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{q=r-k^{*}}^{\textsc{c}}\binom{\textsc{c}}{q}\big(1-F(v_{k^{*}+1}^{\textsc{n}})\big)^{q}\big(F(v_{k^{*}+1}^{\textsc{n}})\big)^{\textsc{c}-q}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{q=r-k^{*}}^{\textsc{c}}\binom{\textsc{c}}{q}\mathbb{E}\left[\big(1-u_{k^{*}+1}^{\textsc{n}}\big)^{q}\big(u_{k^{*}+1}^{\textsc{n}}\big)^{\textsc{c}-q}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{q=r-k^{*}}^{\textsc{c}}\binom{\textsc{c}}{q}\frac{B(C+N-k^{*}-q,q+k^{*}+1)}{B(N-k^{*},k^{*}+1)},

where
B(\cdot,\cdot)
is the beta function. The last equality above uses the property that for
X\sim
Beta
(\alpha,\beta)
,
\mathbb{E}\left[X^{a}(1-X)^{b}\right]=\frac{B(\alpha+a,\beta+b)}{B(\alpha,\beta)}
.
∎

By optimally splitting items between the two groups, H-VCG achieves at least the welfare (and often revenue) of running VCG on only non-colluders, leveraging side information for improved efficiency.

Corollary 4.5
.

From Theorem
4.4
,
\mathbb{E}\left[\text{Welf}_{VCG}(\mathcal{N})\right]\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\text{Welf}_{H-VCG}(\mathcal{N}\cup\mathcal{C};k^{*})\right]\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\text{Welf}_{VCG}(\mathcal{N}\cup\mathcal{C})\right]
. For finite number of items
r
and colluding bidders
|\mathcal{C}|
,
\lim_{|\mathcal{N}|\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[\text{Welf}_{VCG}(\mathcal{N})\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\text{Welf}_{VCG}(\mathcal{N}\cup\mathcal{C})\right]
should hold. Therefore,

\lim_{|\mathcal{N}|\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[\text{Welf}_{H-VCG}(\mathcal{N}\cup\mathcal{C};k^{*})\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\text{Welf}_{VCG}(\mathcal{N}\cup\mathcal{C})\right].

As the number of non-colluding bidders grow large, H-VCG’s performance converges to that of the ideal VCG mechanism with all bidders, showing that the hybrid design asymptotically closes the welfare gap due to collusion.

4.2
Minorant-based Expected Welfare Optimization

If
F(\cdot)
and
Q(\cdot)
are unknown, we could calculate a minorant of the expected welfare with some minimal information about
Q(\cdot)
and optimize that instead in our mechanism.

Assumption 4.2
.

[Quantile Function] Let
\exists

U>L>0
s.t.
Lx\leq\mathcal{Q}(x)\leq Ux\quad\forall x\in[0,1].

The above assumption is satisfied by many distributions. For example, distributions with bounded support
[0,V]
and bounded from above and below as
\frac{1}{U}\leq f(v)\leq\frac{1}{L}

\forall v\in[0,V]
, where
f(\cdot)
is the probability density function, satisfy it. The next lemma links order statistics of bidders’ valuations to those of samples from uniform distribution, establishing a simple way to bound valuations using the quantile function’s upper and lower slopes.

Lemma 4.6
.

Given that Assumptions
4.1
and
4.2
hold, an order statistic
v^{S}_{k}
satisfies
Lu^{S}_{k}\leq v^{S}_{k}\leq Uu^{S}_{k}
, where
u^{S}_{k}
is the k-th largest order statistic among
S
i.i.d. standard uniform samples. Furthermore,
F(v^{N}_{k})=u^{N}_{k}
.

Proof.

The result is a consequence of probability integral transform of continuous random variables. By equation
(2.3.7)
of
(
15
)
,
v^{S}_{k}=\mathcal{Q}(u^{S}_{k})
.
Lu^{S}_{k}\leq v^{S}_{k}\leq Uu^{S}_{k}
follows from Assumption
4.2
. Using
Q(\cdot)=F^{-1}(\cdot)
from Assumption
4.1
, we get
F(v^{N}_{k})=u^{N}_{k}
.
∎

Next, we construct two different minorants for expected welfare.

Lemma 4.7

(Minorant of Welfare)
.

Let Assumptions
4.1
and
4.2
hold. Then,

\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{Welf}_{\textsc{H-VCG}}(\mathcal{N}\cup\mathcal{C};k)\right]\geq\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{L\left(1+Lu^{\textsc{n}}_{k+1}/U\right)}{2}\cdot r(k,u^{N}_{k+1})\right],

where
r(k,u^{N}_{k+1})=\left(k+\sum_{q=0}^{\textsc{c}}\min\{r-k,q\}\binom{\textsc{c}}{q}\big(\max\{0,1-Uu_{k+1}^{\textsc{n}}/L\}\big)^{q}\big(1-\max\{0,1-Uu_{k+1}^{\textsc{n}}/L\}\big)^{\textsc{c}-q}\right)
is expected number of items sold in the mechanism, and the expectation is over
u^{N}_{k+1}\sim\text{Beta}\left(N-k,k+1\right)
.

Proof.

By the proof in Lemma
4.3
,

\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{Welf}_{\textsc{H-VCG}}(\mathcal{N}\cup\mathcal{C};k)\mid(v^{N}_{k+1},\cdots,v^{N}_{N})\right]=\mathbb{E}\!\left[Q(u)\ \middle|\ u\geq F\!\left(v^{\textsc{n}}_{k+1}\right)\right]\cdot\left(k+\mathbb{E}\!\left[\min\{|\mathcal{W}_{C}|,\,r-k\}\right]\right),

where
u\sim\mathrm{Uniform}[0,1]
and
|\mathcal{W}_{C}|\mid v^{N}_{k+1}\sim\mathrm{Binomial}\!\left(C,\,1-F\!\left(v^{N}_{k+1}\right)\right)
. Using Assumption
4.2
and Lemma
4.6
, we bound the above terms above separately as follows.

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\!\left[Q(u)\ \middle|\ u\geq F\!\left(v^{\textsc{n}}_{k+1}\right)\right]\cdot\left(k+\mathbb{E}\!\left[\min\{|\mathcal{W}_{C}|,\,r-k\}\right]\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\!\left[v\ \middle|\ v\geq v^{\textsc{n}}_{k+1}\right]\cdot\left(k+\sum_{q=1}^{\textsc{c}}\min\{q,r-k\}\binom{\textsc{c}}{q}\big(1-F(v_{k+1}^{\textsc{n}})\big)^{q}\big(F(v_{k+1}^{\textsc{n}})\big)^{\textsc{c}-q}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{E}\!\left[Lu\ \middle|\ u\geq F\left(v^{\textsc{n}}_{k+1}\right)\right]\cdot\left(k+\sum_{q=1}^{\textsc{c}}\min\{q,r-k\}\binom{\textsc{c}}{q}\big(1-F(v_{k+1}^{\textsc{n}})\big)^{q}\big(F(v_{k+1}^{\textsc{n}})\big)^{\textsc{c}-q}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{L\left(1+F\left(v^{\textsc{n}}_{k+1}\right)\right)}{2}\cdot\left(k+\sum_{q=1}^{\textsc{c}}\min\{q,r-k\}\binom{\textsc{c}}{q}\big(1-F(v_{k+1}^{\textsc{n}})\big)^{q}\big(F(v_{k+1}^{\textsc{n}})\big)^{\textsc{c}-q}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{L\left(1+Lu^{\textsc{n}}_{k+1}/U\right)}{2}\cdot\left(k+\sum_{q=0}^{\textsc{c}}\min\{r-k,q\}\binom{\textsc{c}}{q}\big(\max\{0,1-Uu_{k+1}^{\textsc{n}}/L\}\big)^{q}\big(1-\max\{0,1-Uu_{k+1}^{\textsc{n}}/L\}\big)^{\textsc{c}-q}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{L\left(1+Lu^{\textsc{n}}_{k+1}/U\right)}{2}\cdot r(k,u^{N}_{k+1}).

The second inequality is because
Q(u)=v\geq Lu
by Lemma
4.6
. The third equality is using
\mathbb{E}\left[u\mid u\geq F\left(v^{N}_{k+1}\right)\right]=(1+F\left(v^{N}_{k+1}\right))/2
for
u\sim\text{Uniform}[0,1]
. The last inequality is because
F\left(v^{N}_{k+1}\right)\geq v^{N}_{k+1}/U\geq Lu^{N}_{k+1}/U
by Lemma
4.6
and invertibility of
F(\cdot)
, and
F\left(v^{N}_{k+1}\right)\geq\frac{v^{N}_{k+1}}{L}\geq\frac{Uu^{N}_{k+1}}{L}
leads to fewer expected winners
|\mathcal{W}_{C}|
, and where
u^{N}_{k+1}\sim\text{Beta}\left(N-k,k+1\right)
and is
(k+1)-
th highest order statistic from
N
i.i.d. standard uniform random variables.
∎

Since the utility of all bidders and the auctioneer is non-negative,
\mathrm{Welf}_{\textsc{H-VCG}}(\mathcal{N}\cup\mathcal{C};k)\geq\mathrm{Rev}_{\textsc{H-VCG}}(\mathcal{N}\cup\mathcal{C};k)
. Hence, a minorant of the revenue is also a valid minorant of the welfare.

where

is expected number of items sold in the mechanism and the expectation is over

.

Proof.

The proof is very similar to Lemma

.

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{Rev}_{\textsc{H-VCG}}(\mathcal{N}\cup\mathcal{C};k)\mid(v^{N}_{k+1},\cdots,v^{N}_{N})\right]=v^{N}_{k+1}\left(k+\mathbb{E}\!\left[\min\{|\mathcal{W}_{C}|,\,r-k\}\right]\right)

\displaystyle\geq v^{N}_{k+1}\left(k+\sum_{q=0}^{\textsc{c}}\min\{r-k,q\}\binom{\textsc{c}}{q}\big(\max\{0,1-v_{k+1}^{\textsc{n}}/L\}\big)^{q}\big(1-\max\{0,1-v_{k+1}^{\textsc{n}}/L\}\big)^{\textsc{c}-q}\right)

6
Conclusion

This paper introduces the Hybrid VCG (H-VCG) mechanism, a new approach to auction design that remains truthful and efficient even in the presence of bidder collusion. Our results demonstrate that side information, specifically the identification of colluding bidders, can be leveraged to design mechanisms that are simultaneously truthful and collusion-proof. By combining a VCG mechanism for non-colluding bidders with a posted-price mechanism for colluding bidders, H-VCG achieves provable guarantees on expected welfare and revenue.

We also show that colluding bidders optimally shade their bids, never overbidding, and that adding colluding bidders can only improve welfare and revenue relative to the non-collusive baseline, establishing a Bulow–Klemperer type result for collusive environments.

Empirical evaluations further confirm that H-VCG consistently outperforms VCG restricted to non-colluding bidders and approaches the performance of fully truthful VCG outcomes. An important direction for future work is to extend these results to settings with heterogeneous items and consider the case when the black-box collusion detection has errors.

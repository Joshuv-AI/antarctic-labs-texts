Dual Optimistic Ascent (PI Control) is the Augmented Lagrangian Method in Disguise
Juan Ramirez Simon Lacoste-Julien Mila - Quebec AI InstituteDIRO, Université de Montréal

Abstract
Constrained optimization is a powerful framework for enforcing requirements on neural networks. These constrained deep learning problems are typically solved using first-order methods on their min-max Lagrangian formulation, but such approaches often suffer from oscillations and can fail to find all local solutions. While the Augmented Lagrangian method (ALM) addresses these issues, practitioners often favor dual optimistic ascent schemes (PI control) on the standard Lagrangian, which perform well empirically but lack formal guarantees. In this paper, we establish a previously unknown equivalence between these approaches: dual optimistic ascent on the Lagrangian is equivalent to gradient descent-ascent on the Augmented Lagrangian. This finding allows us to transfer the robust theoretical guarantees of the ALM to the dual optimistic setting, proving it converges linearly to all local solutions. Furthermore, the equivalence provides principled guidance for tuning the optimism hyper-parameter. Our work closes a critical gap between the empirical success of dual optimistic methods and their theoretical foundation.

Introduction

Machine learning problems frequently lead to constrained optimization formulations, where the objective function encodes the learning task, and the constraints guide optimization toward models with desirable properties such as sparsity
(
Gallego-Posada et al. 2022
)
, fairness
(
Cotter et al. 2019a
)
, or safety
(
Dai et al. 2024
)
. Alternatively, learning can be incorporated directly into the constraints, as in SVMs and Feasible Learning
(
Ramirez et al. 2025b
)
.

A widely adopted approach for solving constrained optimization problems involving deep neural networks is to formulate their associated Lagrangian and seek its min–max points
(
Elenter et al. 2022
;
Hounie et al. 2023b
;
Kotary and Fioretto 2024
;
Hashemizadeh et al. 2024
)
. This is typically done through gradient descent–ascent: descent on the problem (
primal
) variables—often with adaptive variants such as Adam
(
Kingma and Ba 2015
)
—and ascent on the Lagrange multipliers (
dual
variables). This first-order Lagrangian approach is popular in constrained deep learning because it empirically handles non-convex problems and scales efficiently to models with billions of parameters.

However, gradient descent-ascent on the Lagrangian suffers from two major limitations:

\raisebox{-.7pt}{{\hspace{-0.8mm} \small1}}⃝
in the non-convex setting, it may fail to converge to certain local solutions of the problem—convergence is guaranteed only for solutions that correspond to local min–max points of the Lagrangian
(
Bertsekas 2016
, Prop. 5.4.2)
; and

\raisebox{-.7pt}{{\hspace{-0.8mm} \small2}}⃝
it often exhibits oscillations, where iterates repeatedly move in and out of the feasible set, slowing down convergence
(
Platt and Barr 1987
)
.

A seminal approach that addresses these limitations is the
Augmented Lagrangian
method, which incorporates a quadratic penalty term on the constraints
(
Powell 1969
;
Hestenes 1969
;
Rockafellar 1973
)
. With suitable hyperparameter choices, this method converges to all strict and regular local solutions of non-convex constrained problems, while mitigating oscillations.

Despite these advantages, the Augmented Lagrangian method is not the standard in constrained deep learning. Instead, the community largely relies on the original Lagrangian approach, mitigating its oscillatory behavior with alternative Lagrange multiplier updates such as the PI control strategy
(
Stooke et al. 2020
;
Sohrabi et al. 2024
)
, also known as generalized optimistic gradient ascent
(
Mokhtari et al. 2020
)
. These dual optimistic ascent methods have shown strong empirical performance in reducing oscillations, but their convergence properties remain largely underformalized.

This paper addresses this gap. We establish a simple yet previously unknown result: gradient descent–optimistic ascent on the Lagrangian is equivalent to gradient descent–ascent on the Augmented Lagrangian. For equality-constrained problems, the equivalence holds exactly at the level of matching primal iterates (
Theorem

1
). For general inequality constraints, we prove that both methods converge to the same set of points—namely, all local solutions of the original problem (
Theorem

2
).

In §
4
, we use this equivalence to formalize properties of the dual optimistic ascent (PI control) framework. We show that it strictly improves over standard GDA on the Lagrangian by recovering all local problem solutions (
Theorem

3
) and provide local convergence guarantees to these solutions (
Corollaries

2
and
3
). We further characterize the role of the optimism coefficient (proportional gain), formally showing that larger values reduce oscillations but may induce ill-conditioning (§
4.3
). Finally, in §
4.4
, we highlight how existing empirical results validate our findings.

Scope.
All results are presented in the context of constrained optimization. Our claims about the optimistic gradient method are restricted to:

\raisebox{-.7pt}{{\hspace{-0.8mm} \small1}}⃝
its role in maximizing the dual player in a Lagrangian formulation (i.e., a linear player in a non-convex–linear min-max problem), and

\raisebox{-.7pt}{{\hspace{-0.8mm} \small2}}⃝
its application
only
to that player; for the non-convex primal player, we generally assume a different method is used.
Furthermore, our equivalence results apply solely to a
first-order
,
single-step
Augmented Lagrangian method. They do not extend to Augmented Lagrangian algorithms with multiple primal steps between dual updates, nor to cases in which the primal minimization algorithm is second-order or higher.

2.1
Constrained Optimization

Let

,
\bm{g}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}

\min_{\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\,f(\bm{x})\quad\text{subject to}\quad\bm{g}(\bm{x})\preceq\bm{0},\quad\bm{h}(\bm{x})=\bm{0},

(CMP)

where
\preceq
denotes element-wise inequality. We refer to

as the objective function, and to
\bm{g}
and
\bm{h}

Definitions
.
A point
\bm{x}
is

\bm{x}^{*}

for all feasible
\bm{x}
with

active

Solving constrained optimization problems

A general approach to solving differentiable constrained optimization problems is to find min-max points of their associated Lagrangian

where
\bm{\lambda}\succeq\bm{0}

\bm{x}
as the
primal

\bm{\lambda}

dual

A simple algorithm for finding min-max points of

Constrained deep learning
. Applying constraints to deep neural networks—where the parameters
\bm{x}

By contrast, the Lagrangian approach in

(
Cotter et al. 2019a
;
Hashemizadeh et al. 2024

(

)
and TensorFlow
(
Abadi et al. 2016
)
via libraries like Cooper
(

(

)

Its popularity stems from its convergence properties and computational scalability. It provides local convergence guarantees to min–max points of the Lagrangian, even in the non-convex setting
(
Bertsekas 2016
, Prop. 5.4.2)
, while remaining computationally efficient
(

Ramirez et al. 2025a

. Its overhead relative to unconstrained deep learning is negligible, limited to storing and updating the multipliers.

Limitations
.
In the non-convex regime, the Lagrangian approach faces several drawbacks. Not all local constrained minimizers
\bm{x}^{*}
of
Eq.

CMP
—together with their corresponding optimal multipliers
\bm{\lambda}^{*}\succeq\bm{0}
and
\bm{\mu}^{*}
—necessarily correspond to min-max points of the Lagrangian
\mathcal{L}
. In particular,
\bm{x}^{*}
need not minimize
\mathcal{L}(\,\cdot\,,\bm{\lambda}^{*},\bm{\mu}^{*})
,
3
3

3

Even solutions that minimize
\mathcal{L}
—albeit not strictly—may not correspond to limit points of
Eq.

Lag-GDA
.
and the Hessian
\nabla^{2}_{\bm{x}}\mathcal{L}(\bm{x}^{*},\bm{\lambda}^{*},\bm{\mu}^{*})
may fail to be positive definite. In fact, the Lagrangian can be

in some infeasible directions—whereas strict concavity precludes optimality in unconstrained problems, it can arise at constrained optima
(
Bertsekas 2016
, Prop. 4.3.2)
. Such non-convex stationary points cannot be reached via
Eq.

.

Despite this limitation,
Eq.

Lag-GDA
has proven effective in practice for a wide range of non-convex constrained deep learning applications. However, another challenge limits its applicability: the optimization dynamics often induce oscillations in the multipliers and their associated constraints, causing the primal iterates to repeatedly move in and out of the feasible set
(
Platt and Barr 1987
)
. These oscillations slow convergence and can be critical in settings where infeasibility cannot be tolerated—for example, when violations disrupt physical systems or compromise safety.

These limitations have motivated two main strategies for improvement. The first is the
Augmented Lagrangian Method
, which makes the (Augmented) Lagrangian
strictly convex
at all strict and regular constrained minimizers of
Eq.

CMP
. The second replaces simple gradient ascent on the multipliers with
generalized optimistic gradient ascent
, which mitigates oscillations in the dynamics. These methods are discussed in §
2.2
and §
2.3
, respectively. In §
3
, we prove that they are, in fact, equivalent.

2.2
The Augmented Lagrangian Method

The (HPR) Augmented Lagrangian function
(
Powell 1969
;
Hestenes 1969
;
Rockafellar 1973
)
adds a quadratic penalty to the standard Lagrangian to penalize constraint violations:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{L}_{c}(\bm{x},\bm{\lambda},\bm{\mu})&\triangleq f(\bm{x})+\scalebox{1.2}{$\frac{1}{2c}$}\left[\left\|\bm{\mu}+c\,\bm{h}(\bm{x})\right\|_{2}^{2}-\left\|\bm{\mu}\right\|_{2}^{2}+\left\|\left[\bm{\lambda}+c\,\bm{g}(\bm{x})\right]_{+}\right\|_{2}^{2}-\left\|\bm{\lambda}\right\|_{2}^{2}\right]\end{split}

(AL)

where
c>0
is a penalty coefficient. Expanding the squared norms in
Eq.

AL
reveals that constraint violations are penalized by both a linear term involving the multiplier and a quadratic term scaled by
c
. The projection
[\,\cdot\,]_{+}
creates a
one-sided
penalty for inequalities: it penalizes violations (
g_{i}(\bm{x})>0
) but avoids pushing iterates deeper into the feasible region once a constraint is sufficiently satisfied (
g_{i}(\bm{x})\leq-\lambda_{i}/c
). This ensures that optimization focuses only on violated or nearly-active constraints.

The set of min-max points of
\mathcal{L}_{c}
.
The Augmented Lagrangian preserves and enhances the solution set of the standard Lagrangian. Since the two functions share the same set of stationary points
(
Rockafellar 1973
, Cor. 3.4)
, they have an identical pool of candidate solutions. Furthermore, every local min-max point of
\mathcal{L}
remains a local min-max point of
\mathcal{L}_{c}
, ensuring that any solution attainable with the standard Lagrangian approach remains attainable within the Augmented Lagrangian framework.

More importantly, under regularity assumptions,
every
strict local constrained minimizer
\bm{x}^{*}
admits a threshold
\bar{c}\geq 0
such that, for all
c\geq\bar{c}
, the Augmented Lagrangian
\mathcal{L}_{c}
is
strictly convex
at
\bm{x}^{*}
, even if the original Lagrangian
\mathcal{L}
is not convex there
(
Bertsekas 2016
, §4.2.1)
. Consequently, all strict local constrained minimizers of
Eq.

CMP
are upgraded to local min-max points of
\mathcal{L}_{c}
.

Two important algorithmic consequences follow:

\raisebox{-.7pt}{{\hspace{-0.8mm} \small1}}⃝

all
strict local constrained minimizers become strict local min-max points of
\mathcal{L}_{c}
, for which GDA enjoys local linear convergence guarantees
(
Bertsekas 2016
, Prop. 5.4.2)
; and

\raisebox{-.7pt}{{\hspace{-0.8mm} \small2}}⃝
the sets of strict local constrained minimizers of
Eq.

CMP
and strict local min-max points of
\mathcal{L}_{c}
coincide, ensuring that GDA on the Augmented Lagrangian converges to—and
only
to—stationary points corresponding to solutions of
Eq.

CMP
.
Further benefits of the Augmented Lagrangian approach—including its ability to mitigate oscillations—are discussed in §
4
.

The Augmented Lagrangian Method
seeks min-max points of the Augmented Lagrangian function:

\bm{x}^{*},\bm{\lambda}^{*},\bm{\mu}^{*}\in\mathrm{arg}\min_{\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\,\max_{\bm{\lambda}\succeq\bm{0},\bm{\mu}}\mathcal{L}_{c}(\bm{x},\bm{\lambda},\bm{\mu}).

(1)

The classic algorithm for solving this problem—the Method of Multipliers—iteratively performs:

\begin{split}\bm{x}_{t+1}\in\text{arg}\min_{\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\,\mathcal{L}_{c_{t}}(\bm{x},\bm{\lambda}_{t},\bm{\mu}_{t}),\quad\bm{\mu}_{t+1}=\bm{\mu}_{t}+c_{t}\,\bm{h}(\bm{x}_{t+1}),\quad\bm{\lambda}_{t+1}=\left[\bm{\lambda}_{t}+c_{t}\,\bm{g}(\bm{x}_{t+1})\right]_{+}.\end{split}

(2)

In this method, the primal minimization is typically performed approximately, either for a fixed number of optimization steps or until a numerical tolerance is reached. The subsequent dual updates correspond to gradient ascent steps on the standard Lagrangian
\mathcal{L}
with step-sizes
c_{t}
. The penalty coefficients are chosen as a non-decreasing sequence
c_{t+1}\geq c_{t}>0
, often updated according to a schedule—for example, increasing
c_{t}
when constraint violations are not reduced sufficiently across successive iterations (see, e.g.,
Birgin and Martínez 2014
).

The Augmented Lagrangian method is a seminal approach for solving non-convex constrained optimization problems, with variants implemented in numerous optimization libraries across different programming languages
(
Conn et al. 2013
;
Johnson
;
Pas et al. 2022
;
Gallego-Posada et al. 2025
)
. Its success spans a broad range of scientific problems, and it has become a key tool in constrained deep learning, applied in works ranging from seminal contributions to recent advances
(
Platt and Barr 1987
;
Lokhande et al. 2020
;
Brouillard et al. 2020
;
Kotary and Fioretto 2024
;
Shi et al. 2023
)
.

In deep learning, where full (or even approximate) minimization of
\mathcal{L}_{c}
is computationally infeasible, it is often replaced by one or more steps of a first-order optimizer, such as Adam. Accordingly, in this paper we consider the following family of Augmented Lagrangian algorithms: alternating gradient descent–(projected) gradient ascent on
\mathcal{L}_{c}
, with the primal update performed first.

\displaystyle\bm{x}_{t+1}\leftarrow\bm{x}_{t}-\eta_{\bm{x}}\left[\nabla f(\bm{x}_{t})+\big[\bm{\lambda}_{t}+c\,\bm{g}(\bm{x}_{t})\big]_{+}^{\top}\nabla\bm{g}(\bm{x}_{t})+\big(\bm{\mu}_{t}+c\,\bm{h}(\bm{x}_{t})\big)^{\top}\nabla\bm{h}(\bm{x}_{t})\right]

(AL-GDA)

\displaystyle\bm{\mu}_{t+1}\leftarrow\bm{\mu}_{t}+\eta_{\text{dual}}\,\bm{h}(\bm{x}_{t+1})\qquad\bm{\lambda}_{t+1}\leftarrow\left(1-\tfrac{\eta_{\text{dual}}}{c}\right)\bm{\lambda}_{t}+\tfrac{\eta_{\text{dual}}}{c}\Big[\bm{\lambda}_{t}+c\,\bm{g}(\bm{x}_{t+1})\Big]_{+}

where
0<\eta_{\text{dual}}\leq c
and
\eta_{\bm{x}}>0
are fixed step-sizes. The algorithm first takes a single primal gradient descent step on
\mathcal{L}_{c}
, then performs a dual gradient ascent step on the multipliers with step-size
\eta_{\text{dual}}
, which is independent of the penalty
c
. Notably, the
\bm{\lambda}
-update is a convex combination of the previous iterate
\bm{\lambda}_{t}
and a new estimate
[\bm{\lambda}_{t}+c\,\bm{g}(\bm{x}_{t+1})]_{+}
. Setting
\eta_{\text{dual}}=c
recovers the standard dual updates of the Method of Multipliers in
Eq.

2
.
This method is therefore more specific than
Eq.

2
by replacing the full primal minimization with a single gradient step and more general, as it decouples the dual step-size from
c
. For a full derivation of these updates, see
Appendix

B
.

The primal update in
Eq.

AL-GDA
can be viewed as a gradient descent step on the standard Lagrangian, using multiplier estimates that differ from their latest iterates. These estimates simulate a (projected) ascent step on
\mathcal{L}
, acting as a proactive “lookahead” to calibrate the primal updates. Recognizing this is the key insight behind the equivalence with dual optimistic ascent shown in §
3
.

2.3
Dual Optimistic Ascent

The oscillatory behavior inherent in the dynamics of
Eq.

Lag-GDA
has motivated alternatives to plain gradient ascent for updating the dual variables. A prominent class of such methods is based on
optimism

(
Rakhlin and Sridharan 2013
)
. In this work, we adopt the generalized optimistic gradient method of
Mokhtari et al. 2020
, applying it solely to
\bm{\lambda}
and
\bm{\mu}
. Combining this with standard gradient descent on
\bm{x}
and alternating updates—where the multipliers are updated first—yields:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\bm{\mu}_{t+1}&\leftarrow\bm{\mu}_{t}+\eta_{\text{dual}}\,\bm{h}(\bm{x}_{t})+\omega\big[\bm{h}(\bm{x}_{t})-\bm{h}(\bm{x}_{t-1})\big],\\
\bm{\lambda}_{t+1}&\leftarrow\Big[\bm{\lambda}_{t}+\eta_{\text{dual}}\,\bm{g}(\bm{x}_{t})+\omega\big[\bm{g}(\bm{x}_{t})-\bm{g}(\bm{x}_{t-1})\big]\Big]_{+},\\
\bm{x}_{t+1}&\leftarrow\bm{x}_{t}-\eta_{\bm{x}}\left[\nabla f(\bm{x}_{t})+\bm{\lambda}_{t+1}^{\top}\nabla\bm{g}(\bm{x}_{t})+\bm{\mu}_{t+1}^{\top}\nabla\bm{h}(\bm{x}_{t})\right]\end{split}

(Lag-GD-OA)

where
\omega>0
is the optimism coefficient. The first dual update, which would otherwise require evaluating “
\bm{h}(\bm{x}_{-1})
” and “
\bm{g}(\bm{x}_{-1})
,” is typically replaced by a (projected) gradient ascent step.

While optimism is often applied to both players in general min-max optimization (i.e., optimistic gradient descent–ascent; OGDA), we follow the more general approach of
Stooke et al. 2020
;
Sohrabi et al. 2024
4
4

4

The generalized optimistic gradient method is a special case of the algorithms in both papers; specifically, it corresponds to a Proportional-Integral (PI) controller on the multipliers.
for constrained problems by applying optimism only to the dual variables. This preserves flexibility in the choice of the primal update, allowing to retain task-specific training pipelines—such as standard Adam without optimism. Such flexibility is especially important in deep learning, where model training pipelines are often highly specialized, making it undesirable to modify them solely to accommodate alternative dual optimization algorithms.

Dual (generalized) optimistic updates have been applied to stabilize optimization dynamics of constrained deep learning problems across reinforcement learning
(
Stooke et al. 2020
;
Moskovitz et al. 2023
)
, unsupervised learning
(
Shao et al. 2022
)
, and supervised learning
(
Sohrabi et al. 2024
)
.

Intuitively, the optimistic update in
Eq.

Lag-GD-OA
dampens oscillations by adjusting dual ascent steps with information from past constraint violations; specifically, by adding the optimistic term
\omega\big(\bm{g}(\bm{x}_{t})-\bm{g}(\bm{x}_{t-1})\big)
. When violations shrink across iterations, this correction term is negative, acting as a brake: the multiplier increases more conservatively, avoiding an “overshoot” beyond its optimum. This prevents over-penalization of the constraints in later primal steps, thereby stabilizing the dynamics and reducing oscillations. For further discussion, see
Sohrabi et al. 2024
.

Literature gaps
.
While the properties of the Augmented Lagrangian method are well understood, dual optimistic ascent on the Lagrangian remains largely underformalized. It lacks convergence guarantees and is supported exclusively by empirical demonstrations of its ability to mitigate oscillations.

Despite the vast literature on optimistic methods, most existing results do not directly apply to the dual optimistic ascent algorithm in
Eq.

Lag-GD-OA
.
For general min-max games, OGDA is known to expand the set of stationary points to which it can converge relative to standard GDA
(
Daskalakis and Panageas 2018
)
. While this set can include the global solution in bilinear games, the nature of these additional points remains largely uncharacterized in general non-convex–non-concave games.
Moreover, the established convergence guarantees for OGDA—such as global linear convergence under strong convexity–strong concavity
(
Gidel et al. 2019
)
, sublinear convergence under convexity–concavity
(
Gorbunov et al. 2022
)
, and sublinear convergence for non-convex–(strongly) concave settings
(
Mahdavinia et al. 2022
)
—are derived in frameworks incompatible with our own.

The inapplicability of these findings stems from two key issues. First, the cited results concern a different algorithm that applies optimism to
both
players, not just the dual player as in
Eq.

Lag-GD-OA
.
5
5

5

Moreover, known convergence results for OGDA seldom consider the
generalized
case where
\omega\neq\eta_{\text{dual}}
, with the notable exception of
Mokhtari et al. 2020
for bilinear games.
Second, the underlying assumptions are mismatched—they are either too restrictive, requiring (strong) convexity for the minimization player, or too general, assuming a non-convex–concave game that fails to exploit the specific non-convex–
linear
structure of our problem.

Even for equality constraints, a key obstacle prevents us from proving global convergence of dual optimistic ascent using known results for the Augmented Lagrangian method. Classic ALM results, such as the Q-linear rate in

In the convex setting, our equivalence theorems yield global convergence guarantees for dual optimistic ascent on equality-constrained problems. Extending these guarantees to inequality constraints remains an open direction for future work.

Corollary 3

4.3
Practical Implications for Hyper-parameter Tuning

Our equivalence result provides practical guidance on choosing the optimism hyper-parameter
\omega
. This parameter introduces a critical trade-off analogous to the coefficient
c
in the Augmented Lagrangian method: larger values of
\omega
are beneficial as they
\raisebox{-.7pt}{{\hspace{-0.8mm} \small1}}⃝
expand the set of attainable solutions and
\raisebox{-.7pt}{{\hspace{-0.8mm} \small2}}⃝
dampen oscillations in the optimization dynamics. However,
\raisebox{-.7pt}{{\hspace{-0.8mm} \small3}}⃝
excessively large values can lead to an ill-conditioned problem, which may in turn slow convergence. We now formalize these points.

Corollary 4

(Monotonic inclusion of solutions)
.

The set of strict local minimizers of
Eq.

CMP
that are LSSPs of the dual optimistic ascent dynamics is monotonically non-decreasing in
\omega
. For sufficiently large
\omega
, this set coincides with all local minimizers satisfying
3
,
2
and
1
.

Proof.

This follows from the equivalence result in
Corollary

1
and
3
.

∎

In practice, however, it is generally unclear what value of
\omega
is sufficiently large to turn a given solution
\bm{x}^{*}
into an LSSP; this would typically require knowing
\bm{x}^{*}
itself. This practical uncertainty motivates a simple strategy: to ensure all solutions are attainable, one may consider taking
\omega\rightarrow\infty
.

Dampening Oscillations.
A key motivation for moving beyond standard GDA on the Lagrangian
\mathcal{L}
is the presence of oscillations. Whenever the Hessian
\nabla_{\bm{x}}^{2}\mathcal{L}
is indefinite, the dynamics are characterized by imaginary eigenvalues, an effect observed in practice
(
Stooke et al. 2020
;
Sohrabi et al. 2024
)
and expected in theory
(
Benzi and Simoncini 2006
, Prop 2.5 & Remark 2.8)
.

The Augmented Lagrangian method is a classical solution: as the penalty coefficient
c\to\infty
, the eigenvalues of its dynamics become purely real, eliminating oscillations
(
Benzi and Simoncini 2006
, Remark 2.9)
. Our equivalence result (
Corollary

1
) shows that
Eq.

Lag-GD-OA
inherits this damping property, formalizing the insight that larger values of
\omega
produce a more heavily dampened system.

Corollary 5

(Dual optimistic ascent dampens oscillations)
.

As the optimism parameter
\omega\to\infty
, the eigenvalues of the Jacobian
\mathcal{J}_{\text{OG}}
become real, thereby removing oscillations.

Proof.

This follows from
Corollary

1
and
Benzi and Simoncini 2006
.

∎

As before, the exact threshold for
\omega
to fully remove oscillations depends on problem-specific properties at
\bm{x}^{*}
, making it unknown in practice. This again motivates selecting a generously large
\omega
.

Conditioning trade-off
. However, while these observations may suggest that increasing the optimism coefficient is beneficial, we emphasize another aspect:
Bertsekas 2014
shows that as
c\rightarrow\infty
,
\mathcal{J}_{\text{AL}}
—and hence
\mathcal{J}_{\text{OG}}
—becomes increasingly ill-conditioned.

Corollary 6

(Ill-conditioning of dual optimistic ascent)
.

As
\omega\rightarrow\infty
, the condition number of the operator Jacobian
\mathcal{J}_{\text{OG}}
also tends to infinity.

Proof.

This follows from our equivalence result (
Corollary

1
) and the known ill-conditioning of the Augmented Lagrangian for a large penalty
c

(
Bertsekas 2014
, see Eq. 16 in §2.1)
.

∎

This reveals a critical trade-off: the optimism coefficient
\omega
must be large enough to recover all solutions, yet not so large that it induces ill-conditioning. Since the optimal value depends on local properties at an unknown solution
\bm{x}^{*}
and may not generalize across the optimization landscape,
\omega
must be tuned in practice. Our equivalence provides a principled path forward: practitioners can use well-established ALM penalty-scheduling techniques to dynamically tune the optimism coefficient.

Negative optimism.

\nu
PI, a generalization of dual optimistic ascent, has been shown to recover gradient ascent with momentum, but only when the optimism coefficient is negative (
\omega<0
)
(
Sohrabi et al. 2024
, Thm. 1)
. Since momentum is known to perform poorly in min-max settings—particularly when applied to linear players such as the Lagrange multipliers
(
Gidel et al. 2019
)
—this has been used as an argument against negative optimism. Although the dual optimistic ascent algorithm in
Eq.

cannot reproduce momentum, our analysis provides a more direct justification: negative optimism can destabilize the very constrained minimizers we aim to recover.

Proposition 5

(Negative optimism can destabilize minimizers)
.

For every local constrained minimizer
\bm{x}^{*}
of
Eq.

CMP
satisfying
1
,
3
and
2
, there exists a sufficiently negative optimism coefficient
\omega
such that
\bm{x}^{*}
is no longer an LSSP of the dual optimistic ascent dynamics.

Proof.

This follows from considering the Augmented Lagrangian
\mathcal{L}_{\omega}
with a sufficiently negative penalty coefficient

, which renders
\mathcal{L}_{\omega}
concave along directions in the null space of the active constraints, and thus

ceases to be an LSSP of the dynamics.

∎

4.4
Connection to Existing Empirical Findings

Our theoretical results provide a formal foundation for the strong empirical performance of dual optimistic ascent (PI control) reported in the literature. In particular, its ability to recover all strict and regular local solutions (
Theorem

3
) explains its success on a non-convex problem where standard
Eq.

Lag-GDA
fails, as illustrated in Table 1 (App. C) of
Ramirez et al. 2025a
.

Furthermore, our work provides a theoretical basis (
Corollary

5
) for the key empirical finding that larger optimism coefficients increasingly dampen oscillations and stabilize training
(
Stooke et al. 2020
;
Sohrabi et al. 2024
)
. This behavior is demonstrated across diverse settings, including safe reinforcement learning
(
Stooke et al. 2020
, Fig. 4, §6.3)
and sparsity-constrained ResNet-18 classification
(
Sohrabi et al. 2024
, Figs. 2 and 9, §3 and §5.3)
.
While our contribution is theoretical, the ability of our framework to explain these empirical behaviors strongly validates our findings.

5
Conclusion

In this paper, we establish that dual optimistic ascent (PI control) on the Lagrangian is the Augmented Lagrangian method in disguise. This equivalence bridges a crucial gap between theory and practice by transferring the formal guarantees of the ALM—including local linear convergence to all strict, regular constrained minimizers—to the widely used dual optimistic ascent method. Furthermore, our work provides a principled interpretation of the optimism parameter
\omega
, reframing it from a heuristic knob into a formal trade-off between solution accessibility and numerical conditioning.

Despite our findings, we stress that the Augmented Lagrangian method remains a more principled framework for constrained deep learning. Our results apply when using single-step, first-order methods to minimize the Lagrangian; beyond this setting, dual optimistic ascent is not guaranteed to recover all local solutions to constrained problems.

A natural direction for future work is to explore whether similar principles can be applied to optimistic methods in general min–max games, providing deeper insight into optimization in modern deep learning. In addition, this connection opens the door to transferring hyperparameter tuning techniques between proportional gains in control theory and penalty coefficients in constrained optimization.

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{c}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\begin{cases}c\,g_{i}(\bm{x}),&\text{if }\lambda_{i}+c\,g_{i}(\bm{x})\geq 0\\

\displaystyle=-\frac{\bm{\lambda}}{c}+\frac{1}{c}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\begin{cases}\lambda_{i}+c\,g_{i}(\bm{x}),&\text{if }\lambda_{i}+c\,g_{i}(\bm{x})\geq 0\\

(16)

Gathering these together, we get:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\nabla_{\bm{x}}\mathcal{L}_{c}(\bm{x},\bm{\lambda},\bm{\mu})&=\nabla f(\bm{x})+\big(\bm{\mu}_{t}+c\,\bm{h}(\bm{x})\big)^{\top}\nabla\bm{h}(\bm{x})+\big[\bm{\lambda}_{t}+c\,\bm{g}(\bm{x})\big]_{+}^{\top}\nabla\bm{g}(\bm{x})\\
\nabla_{\bm{\mu}}\mathcal{L}_{c}(\bm{x},\bm{\lambda},\bm{\mu})&=\bm{h}(\bm{x})\\
\nabla_{\bm{\lambda}}\mathcal{L}_{c}(\bm{x},\bm{\lambda},\bm{\mu})&=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\begin{cases}g_{i}(\bm{x}),&\text{if }\lambda_{i}+c\,g_{i}(\bm{x})\geq 0\\
-\lambda_{i}/c,&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}\\
&=-\frac{\bm{\lambda}}{c}+\frac{1}{c}[\bm{\lambda}+c\,\bm{g}(\bm{x})]_{+}.\end{split}

(17)

B.2
The Hessian of
\mathcal{L}_{c}

As can be observed in
Eq.

17
in
Section

B.1
, the gradients of
\mathcal{L}_{c}
with respect to
\bm{x}
and
\bm{\lambda}
are not differentiable whenever some
\lambda_{i}+c\,g_{i}(\bm{x})
is exactly
0
. Therefore, we will compute the following Hessian derivations exclusively for
(\bm{x},\bm{\lambda},\bm{\mu})
tuples with
\lambda_{i}+c\,g_{i}(\bm{x})\neq 0
, for every
i=1,\dots,m
.

Note that at any stationary (KKT) tuple
(\bm{x}^{*},\bm{\lambda}^{*},\bm{\mu}^{*})
of
\mathcal{L}_{c}
satisfying
strict
complimentary slackness (
1
) falls in the set of points with a well defined Hessian, for all
c>0
.

The Hessian of
\mathcal{L}_{c}
can be represented as a
3\times 3
block matrix:

\nabla^{2}\mathcal{L}_{c}=\begin{pmatrix}\nabla_{\bm{x}}^{2}\mathcal{L}_{c}&\nabla_{\bm{x}\bm{\lambda}}^{2}\mathcal{L}_{c}&\nabla_{\bm{x}\bm{\mu}}^{2}\mathcal{L}_{c}\\
\nabla_{\bm{\lambda}\bm{x}}^{2}\mathcal{L}_{c}&\nabla_{\bm{\lambda}\bm{\lambda}}^{2}\mathcal{L}_{c}&\nabla_{\bm{\lambda}\bm{\mu}}^{2}\mathcal{L}_{c}\\
\nabla_{\bm{\mu}\bm{x}}^{2}\mathcal{L}_{c}&\nabla_{\bm{\mu}\bm{\lambda}}^{2}\mathcal{L}_{c}&\nabla_{\bm{\mu}\bm{\mu}}^{2}\mathcal{L}_{c}\end{pmatrix}.

(18)

Let
I_{\mathcal{A}_{\bm{x}}}
be the set of indices
i
for which
\lambda_{i}+c\,g_{i}(\bm{x})\geq 0
, and let
\mathbf{I}_{\mathcal{A}_{\bm{x}}}=\texttt{diag}(I_{\mathcal{A}_{\bm{x}}})
be the diagonal matrix containing these indicators.

The Primal-Primal Block (
\nabla_{\bm{x}}^{2}\mathcal{L}_{c}
).

\nabla_{\bm{x}}^{2}\mathcal{L}_{c}
yields the Hessian of the standard Lagrangian evaluated at extrapolated multipliers, plus quadratic terms:

\nabla_{\bm{x}}^{2}\mathcal{L}_{c}=\nabla_{\bm{x}}^{2}\mathcal{L}\,\big|_{\bm{x},[\bm{\lambda}+c\,\bm{g}(\bm{x})]_{+},\bm{\mu}+c\,\bm{h}(\bm{x})}+c\left[\nabla\bm{h}(\bm{x})^{\top}\nabla\bm{h}(\bm{x})+\nabla\bm{g}(\bm{x})^{\top}\mathbf{I}_{\mathcal{A}_{\bm{x}}}\nabla\bm{g}(\bm{x})\right],

(19)

where
\nabla_{\bm{x}}^{2}\mathcal{L}
is the Hessian of the standard Lagrangian
\mathcal{L}
:

\nabla_{\bm{x}}^{2}\mathcal{L}\,\big|_{\bm{x},[\bm{\lambda}+c\,\bm{g}(\bm{x})]_{+},\bm{\mu}+c\,\bm{h}(\bm{x})}=\nabla^{2}f(\bm{x})+[\bm{\lambda}+c\,\bm{g}(\bm{x})]_{+}^{\top}\nabla^{2}\bm{g}(\bm{x})+(\bm{\mu}+c\,\bm{h}(\bm{x}))^{\top}\nabla^{2}\bm{h}(\bm{x}).

(20)

The Primal-Dual Inequality Blocks (
\nabla_{\bm{x}\bm{\lambda}}^{2}\mathcal{L}_{c}
and
\nabla_{\bm{\lambda}\bm{x}}^{2}\mathcal{L}_{c}
).
Differentiating
\nabla_{\bm{x}}\mathcal{L}_{c}
with respect to
\bm{\lambda}
yields the Jacobian of inequality constraints for those satisying the inequality
\lambda_{i}+c\,g_{i}(\bm{x})\geq 0
, and 0 for the rest:

\nabla_{\bm{x}\bm{\lambda}}^{2}\mathcal{L}_{c}=\nabla\bm{g}(\bm{x})^{\top}\mathbf{I}_{\mathcal{A}_{\bm{x}}}.

(21)

It also follows that

\nabla_{\bm{\lambda}\bm{x}}^{2}\mathcal{L}_{c}=\mathbf{I}_{\mathcal{A}_{\bm{x}}}\nabla\bm{g}(\bm{x})=\left[\nabla_{\bm{x}\bm{\lambda}}^{2}\mathcal{L}_{c}\right]^{\top}.

(22)

The Primal-Dual Equality Block (
\nabla_{\bm{x}\bm{\mu}}^{2}\mathcal{L}_{c}
and
\nabla_{\bm{\mu}\bm{x}}^{2}\mathcal{L}_{c}
).
Yields the Jacobian of the equality constraints:

\nabla_{\bm{x}\bm{\mu}}^{2}\mathcal{L}_{c}=\nabla\bm{h}(\bm{x})^{\top},

(23)

with

\nabla_{\bm{\mu}\bm{x}}^{2}\mathcal{L}_{c}=\nabla\bm{h}(\bm{x})=\left[\nabla_{\bm{x}\bm{\mu}}^{2}\mathcal{L}_{c}\right]^{\top}.

(24)

The Dual-Dual Inequality Block (
\nabla_{\bm{\lambda}\bm{\lambda}}^{2}\mathcal{L}_{c}
).
Differentiating
\nabla_{\bm{\lambda}}\mathcal{L}_{c}
with respect to
\bm{\lambda}
gives:

\nabla_{\bm{\lambda}\bm{\lambda}}^{2}\mathcal{L}_{c}=\frac{1}{c}(\mathbf{I}_{\mathcal{A}_{\bm{x}}}-I)=-\frac{1}{c}(I-\mathbf{I}_{\mathcal{A}_{\bm{x}}}).

(25)

All other blocks are zero
as the corresponding gradients do not depend on the other dual variables:

\nabla_{\bm{\lambda}\bm{\mu}}^{2}\mathcal{L}_{c}=\nabla_{\bm{\mu}\bm{\lambda}}^{2}\mathcal{L}_{c}=\nabla_{\bm{\mu}\bm{\mu}}^{2}\mathcal{L}_{c}=\mathbf{0}.

(26)

Combining all the blocks gives the full Hessian of the Augmented Lagrangian function:

\nabla^{2}\mathcal{L}_{c}(\bm{x},\bm{\lambda},\bm{\mu})=\begin{pmatrix}\nabla_{\bm{x}}^{2}\mathcal{L}_{c}&\nabla\bm{g}(\bm{x})^{\top}\mathbf{I}_{\mathcal{A}_{\bm{x}}}&\nabla\bm{h}(\bm{x})^{\top}\\
\mathbf{I}_{\mathcal{A}_{\bm{x}}}\nabla\bm{g}(\bm{x})&-\frac{1}{c}(I-\mathbf{I}_{\mathcal{A}_{\bm{x}}})&\mathbf{0}\\
\nabla\bm{h}(\bm{x})&\mathbf{0}&\mathbf{0}\end{pmatrix}.

(27)

B.3
Primal-first alternating gradient descent-ascent on
\mathcal{L}_{c}

Primal-first alternating gradient descent-projected gradient ascent on the Augmented Lagrangian
\mathcal{L}_{c}
has the following structure:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\bm{x}_{t+1}&\leftarrow\bm{x}_{t}-\eta_{\bm{x}}\nabla_{\bm{x}}\mathcal{L}_{c}(\bm{x}_{t},\bm{\lambda}_{t},\bm{\mu}_{t})\\
\bm{\mu}_{t+1}&\leftarrow\bm{\mu}_{t}+\eta_{\text{dual}}\nabla_{\bm{\mu}}\mathcal{L}_{c}(\bm{x}_{t+1},\bm{\lambda}_{t},\bm{\mu}_{t})\\
\bm{\lambda}_{t+1}&\leftarrow\Big[\bm{\lambda}_{t}+\eta_{\text{dual}}\nabla_{\bm{\lambda}}\mathcal{L}_{c}(\bm{x}_{t+1},\bm{\lambda}_{t},\bm{\mu}_{t})\Big]_{+}.\end{split}

(28)

Using the gradients computed in
Section

B.1
, we can produce the primal gradient descent update:

\bm{x}_{t+1}\leftarrow\bm{x}_{t}-\eta_{\bm{x}}\left[\nabla f(\bm{x}_{t})+\big(\bm{\mu}_{t}+c\,\bm{h}(\bm{x}_{t})\big)^{\top}\nabla\bm{h}(\bm{x}_{t})+\big[\bm{\lambda}_{t}+c\,\bm{g}(\bm{x}_{t})\big]_{+}^{\top}\nabla\bm{g}(\bm{x}_{t})\right].

(29)

Moreover, the dual ascent updates correspond to:

\bm{\mu}_{t+1}\leftarrow\bm{\mu}_{t}+\eta_{\text{dual}}\bm{h}(\bm{x}_{t+1}),

(30)

and to

\displaystyle\bm{\lambda}_{t+1}

\displaystyle\leftarrow\Big[\bm{\lambda}_{t}+\eta_{\text{dual}}\left[-\frac{\bm{\lambda}_{t}}{c}+\frac{1}{c}[\bm{\lambda}_{t}+c\,\bm{g}(\bm{x}_{t+1})]_{+}\right]\Big]_{+}

(31)

\displaystyle\leftarrow\left[\left(1-\frac{\eta_{\text{dual}}}{c}\right)\bm{\lambda}_{t}+\frac{\eta_{\text{dual}}}{c}\Big[\bm{\lambda}_{t}+c\,\bm{g}(\bm{x}_{t+1})\Big]_{+}\right]_{+}

(32)

\displaystyle\leftarrow\left(1-\frac{\eta_{\text{dual}}}{c}\right)\bm{\lambda}_{t}+\frac{\eta_{\text{dual}}}{c}\Big[\bm{\lambda}_{t}+c\,\bm{g}(\bm{x}_{t+1})\Big]_{+}.

(33)

The last step follows from the fact that the update before the projection consists of a convex combination of two non-negative terms whenever
\eta_{\text{dual}}\leq c
, and thus it itself remains non-negative.

Note that the ascent update with respect to
\bm{\lambda}
reduces to the usual Augmented Lagrangian Method ascent update whenever
\eta_{\text{dual}}=c
:

\bm{\lambda}_{t+1}\leftarrow\Big[\bm{\lambda}_{t}+c\,\bm{g}(\bm{x}_{t+1})\Big]_{+}.

(34)

Gathering these together, we get the gradient descent-ascent updates on
\mathcal{L}_{c}
from
Eq.

AL-GDA
,:

\displaystyle\bm{x}_{t+1}

\displaystyle\leftarrow\bm{x}_{t}-\eta_{\bm{x}}\left[\nabla f(\bm{x}_{t})+\big(\bm{\mu}_{t}+c\,\bm{h}(\bm{x}_{t})\big)^{\top}\nabla\bm{h}(\bm{x}_{t})+\big[\bm{\lambda}_{t}+c\,\bm{g}(\bm{x}_{t})\big]_{+}^{\top}\nabla\bm{g}(\bm{x}_{t})\right]

(35)

\displaystyle\bm{\mu}_{t+1}

\displaystyle\leftarrow\bm{\mu}_{t}+\eta_{\text{dual}}\bm{h}(\bm{x}_{t+1})

(36)

\displaystyle\bm{\lambda}_{t+1}

\displaystyle\leftarrow\left(1-\frac{\eta_{\text{dual}}}{c}\right)\bm{\lambda}_{t}+\frac{\eta_{\text{dual}}}{c}\Big[\bm{\lambda}_{t}+c\,\bm{g}(\bm{x}_{t+1})\Big]_{+}.

(37)

Appendix C
Proofs

Lemma 1
.

Let
k>0
be a constant.
A tuple
(\bm{x}^{*},\bm{\lambda}^{*})
with
\bm{\lambda}^{*}\succeq\bm{0}
satisfies:

\bm{\lambda}^{*}=\big[\bm{\lambda}^{*}+k\,\bm{g}(\bm{x}^{*})\big]_{+}

(38)

if and only if
(\bm{x}^{*},\bm{\lambda}^{*})
satisfies primal feasibility and complimentary slackness. That is,

\bm{g}(\bm{x}^{*})\preceq\bm{0}\quad\text{\, and \,}\quad\bm{\lambda}^{*}\odot\bm{g}(\bm{x}^{*})=\bm{0}.

(39)

Proof.

“
\Rightarrow
.” Let
(\bm{x}^{*},\bm{\lambda}^{*})
satisfy
\bm{\lambda}^{*}=\big[\bm{\lambda}^{*}+k\,\bm{g}(\bm{x}^{*})\big]_{+}
. We can analyze this relationship component-wise,
\lambda^{*}_{i}=\max\{0,\lambda^{*}_{i}+k\,g_{i}(\bm{x}^{*})\}
:

•

If
\lambda_{i}^{*}>0
, then the equation requires
\lambda_{i}^{*}=\lambda_{i}^{*}+k\,g_{i}(\bm{x}^{*})
, which means
g_{i}(\bm{x}^{*})=0
.

•

If
\lambda_{i}^{*}=0
, then the equation becomes
0=\max\{0,k\,g_{i}(\bm{x}^{*})\}
, which requires
k\,g_{i}(\bm{x}^{*})\leq 0
, and thus
g_{i}(\bm{x}^{*})\leq 0
.

Combining these two cases, we have that
g_{i}(\bm{x}^{*})\leq 0
for all
i
(primal feasibility) and that for each
i
, either
\lambda_{i}^{*}=0
or
g_{i}(\bm{x}^{*})=0
or both. This is precisely the definition of complementary slackness.

“
\Leftarrow
.” Let
(\bm{x}^{*},\bm{\lambda}^{*})
satisfy primal feasibility and complementary slackness. We analyze the relationship component-wise for each
i
:

•

If
\lambda_{i}^{*}>0
, then complementary slackness (
\lambda_{i}^{*}g_{i}(\bm{x}^{*})=0
) implies that
g_{i}(\bm{x}^{*})=0
. In this case, the right-hand side of the target equation becomes
\max\{0,\lambda_{i}^{*}+k\cdot 0\}=\max\{0,\lambda_{i}^{*}\}=\lambda_{i}^{*}
. The equality holds.

•

If
\lambda_{i}^{*}=0
, then primal feasibility implies that
g_{i}(\bm{x}^{*})\leq 0
. Since
k>0
, we have
k\,g_{i}(\bm{x}^{*})\leq 0
. In this case, the right-hand side becomes
\max\{0,0+k\,g_{i}(\bm{x}^{*})\}=\max\{0,k\,g_{i}(\bm{x}^{*})\}=0=\lambda_{i}^{*}
. The equality also holds.

Since the equality
\lambda_{i}^{*}=\max\{0,\lambda_{i}^{*}+k\,g_{i}(\bm{x}^{*})\}
holds for all components
i
, the vector equation
\bm{\lambda}^{*}=\big[\bm{\lambda}^{*}+k\,\bm{g}(\bm{x}^{*})\big]_{+}
is satisfied.
∎

Proof of Proposition

1
.

We will now show that stationary (fixed) points of algorithms
Eq.

AL-GDA
and
Eq.

Lag-GD-OA
match, regardless of their hyper-parameter choices.

“
\Rightarrow
.” Let
(\bm{x}^{*},\bm{\lambda}^{*},\bm{\mu}^{*})
be a fixed point of the Augmented Lagrangian dynamics of
Eq.

AL-GDA
. It follows that:

\displaystyle\bm{\mu}^{*}

\displaystyle=\bm{\mu}^{*}+\eta_{\text{dual}}\bm{h}(\bm{x}^{*})

(40)

\displaystyle\bm{\lambda}^{*}

\displaystyle=\left(1-\frac{\eta_{\text{dual}}}{c}\right)\bm{\lambda}^{*}+\frac{\eta_{\text{dual}}}{c}\Big[\bm{\lambda}^{*}+c\,\bm{g}(\bm{x}^{*})\Big]_{+}.

(41)

These equations imply primal feasibility and complimentary slackness. In fact, from the first equation,
\bm{\mu}^{*}=\bm{\mu}^{*}+\eta_{\text{dual}}\bm{h}(\bm{x}^{*})
implies that
\eta_{\text{dual}}\bm{h}(\bm{x}^{*})=0
, and since
\eta_{\text{dual}}>0
, we must have
\bm{h}(\bm{x}^{*})=0
. This is primal feasibility for the equality constraints.

For the second equation, we can simplify:

\displaystyle\bm{\lambda}^{*}

\displaystyle=\left(1-\frac{\eta_{\text{dual}}}{c}\right)\bm{\lambda}^{*}+\frac{\eta_{\text{dual}}}{c}\Big[\bm{\lambda}^{*}+c\,\bm{g}(\bm{x}^{*})\Big]_{+}

(42)

\displaystyle\frac{\eta_{\text{dual}}}{c}\bm{\lambda}^{*}

\displaystyle=\frac{\eta_{\text{dual}}}{c}\Big[\bm{\lambda}^{*}+c\,\bm{g}(\bm{x}^{*})\Big]_{+}

(43)

\displaystyle\bm{\lambda}^{*}

\displaystyle=\Big[\bm{\lambda}^{*}+c\,\bm{g}(\bm{x}^{*})\Big]_{+}

(44)

As a consequence of
Lemma

1
, this condition implies both primal feasibility for the inequality constraints (
\bm{g}(\bm{x}^{*})\preceq\bm{0}
) and complementary slackness (
\lambda_{i}^{*}g_{i}(\bm{x}^{*})=0
for all
i
).

Primal feasibility can be used to argue:

\bm{\mu}^{*}=\bm{\mu}^{*}+c\,\bm{h}(\bm{x}^{*}),

(45)

since
c>0
. Together with the fact that
\bm{\lambda}^{*}=\big[\bm{\lambda}^{*}+c\,\bm{g}(\bm{x}^{*})\big]_{+}
, it follows that:

\displaystyle\bm{x}^{*}

\displaystyle=\bm{x}^{*}-\eta_{\bm{x}}\left[\nabla f(\bm{x}^{*})+\big[\bm{\lambda}^{*}+c\,\bm{g}(\bm{x}^{*})\big]_{+}^{\top}\nabla\bm{g}(\bm{x}^{*})+\big(\bm{\mu}^{*}+c\,\bm{h}(\bm{x}^{*})\big)^{\top}\nabla\bm{h}(\bm{x}^{*})\right]

(46)

\displaystyle=\bm{x}^{*}-\eta_{\bm{x}}\left[\nabla f(\bm{x}^{*})+\left(\bm{\lambda}^{*}\right)^{\top}\nabla\bm{g}(\bm{x}^{*})+\left(\bm{\mu}^{*}\right)^{\top}\nabla\bm{h}(\bm{x}^{*})\right],

(47)

which corresponds to the primal stationarity condition of dual optimistic ascent in
Eq.

Lag-GD-OA
; and also simply means:

\nabla_{\bm{x}}\mathcal{L}(\bm{x}^{*},\bm{\lambda}^{*},\bm{\mu}^{*})=0.

(48)

We have thus established that
(\bm{x}^{*},\bm{\lambda}^{*},\bm{\mu}^{*})
is a KKT point of
Eq.

CMP
.

Finally, we show that the dual variables are also stationary under the optimistic ascent dynamics. At the fixed point
(\bm{x}^{*},\bm{\lambda}^{*},\bm{\mu}^{*})
, we have
\bm{x}_{t}=\bm{x}_{t-1}=\bm{x}^{*}
. The dual updates from
Eq.

Lag-GD-OA
therefore become:

\displaystyle\bm{\mu}^{*}

\displaystyle=\bm{\mu}^{*}+\eta_{\text{dual}}\bm{h}(\bm{x}^{*})+\omega\big(\bm{h}(\bm{x}^{*})-\bm{h}(\bm{x}^{*})\big)

(49)

\displaystyle\bm{\lambda}^{*}

\displaystyle=\Big[\bm{\lambda}^{*}+\eta_{\text{dual}}\,\bm{g}(\bm{x}^{*})+\omega\big(\bm{g}(\bm{x}^{*})-\bm{g}(\bm{x}^{*})\big)\Big]_{+}

(50)

The update for
\bm{\mu}^{*}
simplifies to
\bm{\mu}^{*}=\bm{\mu}^{*}+\eta_{\text{dual}}\bm{h}(\bm{x}^{*})
. As primal feasibility requires
\bm{h}(\bm{x}^{*})=0
, this becomes
\bm{\mu}^{*}=\bm{\mu}^{*}
, which is trivially satisfied.

The update for a stationary
\bm{\lambda}^{*}
simplifies to
\bm{\lambda}^{*}=\big[\bm{\lambda}^{*}+\eta_{\text{dual}}\,\bm{g}(\bm{x}^{*})\big]_{+}
. This condition holds due to
Lemma

1
and the combination of primal feasibility and complementary slackness proved above.

Since the stationarity conditions for the primal variable
\bm{x}
and the dual variables
\bm{\lambda}
and
\bm{\mu}
are all met, the tuple
(\bm{x}^{*},\bm{\lambda}^{*},\bm{\mu}^{*})
is a fixed point of the dual-first optimistic ascent dynamics in
Eq.

Lag-GD-OA
.

“
\Leftarrow
.”
Let
(\bm{x}^{*},\bm{\lambda}^{*},\bm{\mu}^{*})
be a fixed point of the dual-first optimistic ascent dynamics of
Eq.

Lag-GD-OA
. At a stationary point, the optimistic term
\bm{g}(\bm{x}_{t})-\bm{g}(\bm{x}_{t-1})
vanishes. Therefore, the fixed-point conditions are:

\displaystyle\bm{\mu}^{*}

\displaystyle=\bm{\mu}^{*}+\eta_{\text{dual}}\bm{h}(\bm{x}^{*})

(51)

\displaystyle\bm{\lambda}^{*}

\displaystyle=\Big[\bm{\lambda}^{*}+\eta_{\text{dual}}\,\bm{g}(\bm{x}^{*})\Big]_{+}

(52)

\displaystyle\bm{x}^{*}

\displaystyle=\bm{x}^{*}-\eta_{\bm{x}}\left[\nabla f(\bm{x}^{*})+(\bm{\lambda}^{*})^{\top}\nabla\bm{g}(\bm{x}^{*})+(\bm{\mu}^{*})^{\top}\nabla\bm{h}(\bm{x}^{*})\right]

(53)

From these three conditions, we can deduce that the tuple
(\bm{x}^{*},\bm{\lambda}^{*},\bm{\mu}^{*})
must satisfy the KKT conditions:

1.

From the
\bm{\mu}
condition, we get
\bm{h}(\bm{x}^{*})=0
.

2.

The
\bm{\lambda}
update condition,
\bm{\lambda}^{*}=\big[\bm{\lambda}^{*}+\eta_{\text{dual}}\,\bm{g}(\bm{x}^{*})\big]_{+}
, together with
Lemma

1
, implies both primal feasibility for inequalities and complementary slackness.

3.

The
\bm{x}
update implies that the gradient of the Lagrangian is zero:
\nabla_{\bm{x}}\mathcal{L}(\bm{x}^{*},\bm{\lambda}^{*},\bm{\mu}^{*})=0
.

Now we will show that this KKT point is also a fixed point of the Augmented Lagrangian dynamics in
Eq.

AL-GDA
.

•

For
\bm{\mu}
: Primal feasibility implies
\bm{\mu}^{*}=\bm{\mu}^{*}+\eta_{\text{dual}}\bm{h}(\bm{x}^{*})
.

•

For
\bm{\lambda}
: Primal feasibility and complimentary slackness imply
\bm{\lambda}^{*}=\big[\bm{\lambda}^{*}+c\,\bm{g}(\bm{x}^{*})\big]_{+}
(
Lemma

1
). This in turn implies:

\bm{\lambda}^{*}=\left(1-\frac{\eta_{\text{dual}}}{c}\right)\bm{\lambda}^{*}+\frac{\eta_{\text{dual}}}{c}\bm{\lambda}^{*}=\left(1-\frac{\eta_{\text{dual}}}{c}\right)\bm{\lambda}^{*}+\frac{\eta_{\text{dual}}}{c}\Big[\bm{\lambda}^{*}+c\,\bm{g}(\bm{x}^{*})\Big]_{+}.

(54)

The primal update for the Augmented Lagrangian method is stationary if the gradient term is zero:

\nabla f(\bm{x}^{*})+\big[\bm{\lambda}^{*}+c\,\bm{g}(\bm{x}^{*})\big]_{+}^{\top}\nabla\bm{g}(\bm{x}^{*})+\big(\bm{\mu}^{*}+c\,\bm{h}(\bm{x}^{*})\big)^{\top}\nabla\bm{h}(\bm{x}^{*})=0

(55)

But as argued before,
\bm{\lambda}^{*}=\big[\bm{\lambda}^{*}+c\,\bm{g}(\bm{x}^{*})\big]_{+}
and
\bm{\mu}^{*}=\bm{\mu}^{*}+c\,\bm{h}(\bm{x}^{*})
. Therefore:

\nabla f(\bm{x}^{*})+(\bm{\lambda}^{*})^{\top}\nabla\bm{g}(\bm{x}^{*})+(\bm{\mu}^{*})^{\top}\nabla\bm{h}(\bm{x}^{*})=\nabla_{\bm{x}}\mathcal{L}(\bm{x}^{*},\bm{\lambda}^{*},\bm{\mu}^{*})

(56)

From the
Eq.

Lag-GD-OA
fixed-point conditions, we know this term is zero. Thus, the primal update is stationary.

Since the stationarity conditions for the primal and dual variables are all met, the point
(\bm{x}^{*},\bm{\lambda}^{*},\bm{\mu}^{*})
is also a fixed point of the Augmented Lagrangian dynamics. This completes the proof.

C.1
Equivalence Proof for Equality-constrained Problems

Proof of Theorem

1
.

Consider the following primal-first alternating gradient descent–ascent updates on the Augmented Lagrangian:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\bm{x}_{t+1}&\leftarrow\texttt{MinStep}\Big(\bm{x}_{t},\nabla f(\bm{x}_{t})+\big(\bm{\mu}^{\text{(ALM)}}_{t}+c\,\bm{h}(\bm{x}_{t})\big)^{\top}\nabla\bm{h}(\bm{x}_{t})\Big),\\
\bm{\mu}^{\text{(ALM)}}_{t+1}&\leftarrow\bm{\mu}^{\text{(ALM)}}_{t}+\eta_{\text{dual}}\,\bm{h}(\bm{x}_{t+1}).\end{split}

(57)

These are analogous to
Eq.

AL-GDA
, but stated for a generic first-order primal minimization step
MinStep
. This step could, for instance, be replaced with Adam
[
Kingma and Ba 2015
]
, and the remainder of the proof would still hold.

Now consider the following dual-first alternating gradient descent–optimistic ascent updates on the Lagrangian, similar to
Eq.

Lag-GD-OA
:

\begin{split}\bm{\mu}^{\text{(OGA)}}_{t+1}&\leftarrow\bm{\mu}^{\text{(OGA)}}_{t}+\eta_{\text{dual}}\,\bm{h}(\bm{x}_{t})+\omega\big(\bm{h}(\bm{x}_{t})-\bm{h}(\bm{x}_{t-1})\big),\\
\bm{x}_{t+1}&\leftarrow\texttt{MinStep}\Big(\bm{x}_{t},\nabla f(\bm{x}_{t})+\left(\bm{\mu}^{\text{(OGA)}}_{t+1}\right)^{\top}\nabla\bm{h}(\bm{x}_{t})\Big).\end{split}

(58)

Suppose
\omega=c
.
We show by induction that the primal iterates
\{\bm{x}_{t}\}_{t=0}^{\infty}
generated by the primal-first ALM algorithm and the dual-first OGA algorithm are identical.

Inductive Step
.
Define:

\bm{\mu}^{\text{(OGA)}}_{t+1}=\bm{\mu}^{\text{(ALM)}}_{t}+c\,\bm{h}(\bm{x}_{t}).

(59)

With this substitution, the primal update in
Eq.

57
becomes

\displaystyle\bm{x}_{t+1}

\displaystyle=\texttt{MinStep}\Big(\bm{x}_{t},\nabla f(\bm{x}_{t})+\left(\bm{\mu}^{\text{(OGA)}}_{t+1}\right)^{\top}\nabla\bm{h}(\bm{x}_{t})\Big)

(60)

\displaystyle=\texttt{MinStep}\Big(\bm{x}_{t},\nabla_{\bm{x}}\mathcal{L}\left(\bm{x}_{t},\bm{\mu}^{\text{(OGA)}}_{t+1}\right)\Big),

(61)

i.e., a descent step on the Lagrangian with multipliers
\bm{\mu}^{\text{(OGA)}}_{t+1}
.

For the dual update, we have

\displaystyle\bm{\mu}^{\text{(OGA)}}_{t+1}

\displaystyle=\bm{\mu}^{\text{(ALM)}}_{t}+c\,\bm{h}(\bm{x}_{t})

(62)

\displaystyle=\bm{\mu}^{\text{(ALM)}}_{t-1}+\eta_{\text{dual}}\,\bm{h}(\bm{x}_{t})+c\,\bm{h}(\bm{x}_{t})

(63)

\displaystyle=\bm{\mu}^{\text{(OGA)}}_{t}-c\,\bm{h}(\bm{x}_{t-1})+\eta_{\text{dual}}\,\bm{h}(\bm{x}_{t})+c\,\bm{h}(\bm{x}_{t})

(64)

\displaystyle=\bm{\mu}^{\text{(OGA)}}_{t}+\eta_{\text{dual}}\,\bm{h}(\bm{x}_{t})+c\big(\bm{h}(\bm{x}_{t})-\bm{h}(\bm{x}_{t-1})\big),

(65)

which matches the optimistic ascent update in
Eq.

123
with
\omega=c
. Combining this dual update with
Eq.

60
yields exactly the algorithm in
Eq.

123
. Thus, if the primal iterates generated by both algorithms match up until step
t
, they will match at step
t+1
under the change of variable
\bm{\mu}^{\text{(OGA)}}_{t+1}=\bm{\mu}^{\text{(ALM)}}_{t}+c\,\bm{h}(\bm{x}_{t})
.

Base Case
. We establish the base case for the induction by showing that the first primal iterates,
\bm{x}_{1}
, are identical under the specified initializations.

The primal descent update for
Eq.

122
corresponds to a descent step on the Lagrangian with an effective multiplier of
\bm{\mu}^{\text{(ALM)}}_{0}+c\,\bm{h}(\bm{x}_{0})
. The primal descent update for
Eq.

123
corresponds to a descent step on the Lagrangian with an effective multiplier of
\bm{\mu}^{\text{(OGA)}}_{1}
. For the first step, the difference term in
Eq.

123
is typically set to zero (as
\bm{h}(\bm{x}_{-1})
is undefined), so that

\bm{\mu}^{\text{(OGA)}}_{1}=\bm{\mu}^{\text{(OGA)}}_{0}+\eta_{\text{dual}}\,\bm{h}(\bm{x}_{0}).

(66)

For the primal iterates to be identical, their effective multipliers must satisfy

\bm{\mu}^{\text{(ALM)}}_{0}+c\,\bm{h}(\bm{x}_{0})=\bm{\mu}^{\text{(OGA)}}_{0}+\eta_{\text{dual}}\,\bm{h}(\bm{x}_{0}).

(67)

This equality is ensured by initializing the OGA method relative to the ALM method as

\bm{\mu}^{\text{(OGA)}}_{0}=\bm{\mu}^{\text{(ALM)}}_{0}+(c-\eta_{\text{dual}})\,\bm{h}(\bm{x}_{0}).

(68)

With this initialization, both effective multipliers for the first primal update are identical, yielding the same first iterate for both algorithms:

\bm{x}_{1}=\texttt{MinStep}\Big(\bm{x}_{0},\nabla f(\bm{x}_{0})+\big(\bm{\mu}^{\text{(ALM)}}_{0}+c\,\bm{h}(\bm{x}_{0})\big)^{\top}\nabla\bm{h}(\bm{x}_{0})\Big).

(69)

Combined with the recurrence in
Eq.

60
, this initial condition implies by induction that all subsequent primal iterates
\bm{x}_{t}
also coincide. Therefore, the two algorithms are equivalent.

∎

C.2
Equivalence Proof for Inequality-constrained Problems

This subsection provides the full proof of
Theorem

2
, which establishes the equivalence of the locally stable stationary points (LSSPs) for the two algorithms in inequality-constrained problems. Our proof strategy is as follows: in
Lemmas

2
and
4
, we first derive the Jacobians of the update operators defined by
Eq.

AL-GDA
and
Eq.

Lag-GD-OA
, respectively. We then derive their corresponding characteristic polynomials in
Lemmas

3
and
5
. Finally, in the main proof of
Theorem

2
, we establish a relationship between the roots of these polynomials, which govern local convergence when
\omega=c

.

with

Let

Proof.

The Augmented Lagrangian method in

where
\mathcal{J}_{\bm{x}}
and
\mathcal{J}_{\bm{\lambda}}

The Jacobian of the primal step

(76)

and the Jacobian of the dual step

\mathcal{J}_{\bm{\lambda}}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathbb{I}&0\\[6.0pt]

The primal Jacobian is well defined for any

with
\bm{\lambda}_{t}\succeq\bm{0}
whenever
\lambda_{t,i}+c\,g_{i}(\bm{x}_{t})\neq 0
for all constraints

\lambda_{t,i}+c\,g_{i}(\bm{x}_{t+1})\neq 0

Multiplying gives

\mathcal{J}_{\text{AL}}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathbb{I}-\eta_{\bm{x}}\nabla_{\bm{x}}^{2}\mathcal{L}_{c}&-\eta_{\bm{x}}\nabla_{\bm{x}\bm{\lambda}}^{2}\mathcal{L}_{c}\\[6.0pt]

(78)

To recover the structure of
\mathcal{J}_{\text{AL}}
, we evaluate
\nabla_{\bm{x}}^{2}\mathcal{L}_{c}
,
\nabla_{\bm{x}\bm{\lambda}}^{2}\mathcal{L}_{c}
, and
\nabla_{\bm{\lambda}}^{2}\mathcal{L}_{c}
at
(\bm{x}^{*},\bm{\lambda}^{*})
. Explicit forms of these terms at generic
(\bm{x},\bm{\lambda})
were already derived in
Section

B.2
.

We now partition the state
(\bm{x}_{t},\bm{\lambda}_{t})
into
(\bm{x}_{t},\bm{\lambda}_{t,\mathcal{A}_{\bm{x}}},\bm{\lambda}_{t,I})
, where
\bm{\lambda}_{t,\mathcal{A}_{\bm{x}}}
corresponds to active constraints at
\bm{x}^{*}
and
\bm{\lambda}_{t,I}
to inactive ones. This partition is well defined under strict complementarity.

The primal Hessian (
\nabla_{\bm{x}}^{2}\mathcal{L}_{c}
).

Complementary slackness implies
\bm{\lambda}^{*}=[\bm{\lambda}^{*}+c\,\bm{g}(\bm{x}^{*})]_{+}
, both for active and inactive constraints (
Lemma

1
).

Substituting into the primal Hessian (see
Eq.

19
) yields

\displaystyle\nabla_{\bm{x}}^{2}\mathcal{L}_{c}\,\big|_{\bm{x}^{*},\bm{\lambda}^{*}}

\displaystyle=\nabla^{2}f(\bm{x}^{*})+(\bm{\lambda}^{*})^{\top}\nabla^{2}\bm{g}(\bm{x}^{*})+c\,\nabla\bm{g}_{\mathcal{A}_{\bm{x}}}(\bm{x}^{*})^{\top}\nabla\bm{g}_{\mathcal{A}_{\bm{x}}}(\bm{x}^{*})

(79)

\displaystyle=A+c\,B^{\top}B.

(80)

The primal–dual Hessians for
active
constraints
are

\nabla_{\bm{x}\bm{\lambda}_{\mathcal{A}_{\bm{x}}}}^{2}\mathcal{L}_{c}\,\big|_{\bm{x}^{*},\bm{\lambda}^{*}}=\nabla\bm{g}_{\mathcal{A}_{\bm{x}}}(\bm{x}^{*})^{\top}=B^{\top},\qquad\nabla_{\bm{\lambda}_{\mathcal{A}_{\bm{x}}}\bm{x}}^{2}\mathcal{L}_{c}\,\big|_{\bm{x}^{*},\bm{\lambda}^{*}}=\nabla\bm{g}_{\mathcal{A}_{\bm{x}}}(\bm{x}^{*})=B.

(81)

The primal–dual Hessians for
inactive
constraints
vanish:

\nabla_{\bm{x}\bm{\lambda}_{I}}^{2}\mathcal{L}_{c}\,\big|_{\bm{x}^{*},\bm{\lambda}^{*}}=0,\quad\nabla_{\bm{\lambda}_{I}\bm{x}}^{2}\mathcal{L}_{c}\,\big|_{\bm{x}^{*},\bm{\lambda}^{*}}=0.

(82)

The dual–dual Hessian for active constraints
vanishes:

\nabla_{\bm{\lambda}_{\mathcal{A}_{\bm{x}}}}^{2}\mathcal{L}_{c}\,\big|_{\bm{x}^{*},\bm{\lambda}^{*}}=0.

(83)

The dual–dual Hessian for inactive constraints
is

\nabla_{\bm{\lambda}_{I}}^{2}\mathcal{L}_{c}\,\big|_{\bm{x}^{*},\bm{\lambda}^{*}}=-\frac{1}{c}\,\mathbb{I}.

(84)

Substituting all these expressions, the Jacobian
\mathcal{J}_{\text{AL}}
, evaluated at
(\bm{x}^{*},\bm{\lambda}^{*})=(\bm{x}^{*},\bm{\lambda}^{*}_{\mathcal{A}_{\bm{x}}},\bm{0})
and acting on the partitioned state

\mathcal{J}_{\text{AL}}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathbb{I}-\eta_{\bm{x}}(A+c\,B^{\top}B)&-\eta_{\bm{x}}B^{\top}&0\\[6.0pt]
\eta_{\text{dual}}B(\mathbb{I}-\eta_{\bm{x}}(A+c\,B^{\top}B))&\mathbb{I}-\eta_{\bm{x}}\eta_{\text{dual}}BB^{\top}&0\\[6.0pt]
0&0&(1-\eta_{\text{dual}}/c)\mathbb{I}\end{pmatrix}.

(85)

Lemma 3
.

The characteristic polynomial of the Jacobian
\mathcal{J}_{\text{AL}}
at
(\bm{x}^{*},\bm{\lambda}^{*})
from
Lemma

2
is

\chi_{\mathcal{J}_{\text{AL}}}(\sigma)=\left(1-\frac{\eta_{\text{dual}}}{c}-\sigma\right)^{m-|\mathcal{A}_{\bm{x}}|}\,\det\left((1-\sigma)^{2}\mathbb{I}-\eta_{\bm{x}}(1-\sigma)A-\eta_{\bm{x}}(c(1-\sigma)-\eta_{\text{dual}}\sigma)B^{\top}B\right).

(86)

Proof.

The Jacobian
\mathcal{J}_{\text{AL}}
is block lower-triangular with respect to the partition between the active subsystem
(\bm{x},\bm{\lambda}_{\mathcal{A}_{\bm{x}}})
and the inactive subsystem
\bm{\lambda}_{I}
. Its characteristic polynomial is therefore the product of the characteristic polynomials of these two diagonal blocks.

Inactive subsystem.

The bottom-right block corresponding to the dual variables of inactive inequality constraints is

\mathcal{J}_{\text{AL},I}=\left(1-\frac{\eta_{\text{dual}}}{c}\right)\mathbb{I},

whose characteristic polynomial is

\chi_{\mathcal{J}_{\text{AL},I}}(\sigma)=\left(1-\frac{\eta_{\text{dual}}}{c}-\sigma\right)^{|I_{I}|}.

(87)

Active subsystem.

The Jacobian of the active subsystem is

\mathcal{J}_{\text{AL},\mathcal{A}_{\bm{x}}}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathbb{I}-\eta_{\bm{x}}(A+c\,B^{\top}B)&-\eta_{\bm{x}}B^{\top}\\[6.0pt]
\eta_{\text{dual}}B(\mathbb{I}-\eta_{\bm{x}}(A+c\,B^{\top}B))&\mathbb{I}-\eta_{\bm{x}}\eta_{\text{dual}}BB^{\top}\end{pmatrix}.

(88)

This matrix is similar to

\mathcal{J}_{\text{AL},\mathcal{A}_{\bm{x}}}^{\prime}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathbb{I}-\eta_{\bm{x}}A-\eta_{\bm{x}}(c+\eta_{\text{dual}})B^{\top}B&-\eta_{\bm{x}}B^{\top}\\[6.0pt]
\eta_{\text{dual}}B&\mathbb{I}\end{pmatrix}

(89)

via the transformation matrix

P=\begin{pmatrix}\mathbb{I}&0\\
-\eta_{\text{dual}}B&I\end{pmatrix}.

(90)

The characteristic polynomial of the active subsystem is found by computing the determinant of
\mathcal{J}_{\text{AL},\mathcal{A}_{\bm{x}}}^{\prime}-\sigma\mathbb{I}
:

\chi_{\mathcal{J}_{\text{AL},\mathcal{A}_{\bm{x}}}}(\sigma)=\det\begin{pmatrix}\mathbb{I}-\eta_{\bm{x}}A-\eta_{\bm{x}}(c+\eta_{\text{dual}})B^{\top}B-\sigma\mathbb{I}&-\eta_{\bm{x}}B^{\top}\\[6.0pt]
\eta_{\text{dual}}B&(1-\sigma)\mathbb{I}\end{pmatrix}.

(91)

Let the blocks of the matrix be denoted
M_{11},M_{12},M_{21},M_{22}
. The bottom two blocks,
M_{21}=\eta_{\text{dual}}B
and
M_{22}=(1-\sigma)\mathbb{I}
, commute since the identity matrix commutes with any matrix. We can therefore use the block determinant identity
\det(M)=\det(M_{11}M_{22}-M_{12}M_{21})
.

We first compute the product
M_{11}M_{22}
:

\displaystyle M_{11}M_{22}

\displaystyle=\left((1-\sigma)\mathbb{I}-\eta_{\bm{x}}A-\eta_{\bm{x}}(c+\eta_{\text{dual}})B^{\top}B\right)(1-\sigma)\mathbb{I}

(92)

\displaystyle=(1-\sigma)^{2}\mathbb{I}-\eta_{\bm{x}}(1-\sigma)A-\eta_{\bm{x}}(c+\eta_{\text{dual}})(1-\sigma)B^{\top}B.

(93)

Next, we compute the product
M_{12}M_{21}
:

M_{12}M_{21}=(-\eta_{\bm{x}}B^{\top})(\eta_{\text{dual}}B)=-\eta_{\bm{x}}\eta_{\text{dual}}B^{\top}B.

(94)

Subtracting the second product from the first yields the matrix
M_{11}M_{22}-M_{12}M_{21}
:

(1-\sigma)^{2}\mathbb{I}-\eta_{\bm{x}}(1-\sigma)A-\left[\eta_{\bm{x}}(c+\eta_{\text{dual}})(1-\sigma)-\eta_{\bm{x}}\eta_{\text{dual}}\right]B^{\top}B.

(95)

We simplify the coefficient of the
B^{\top}B
term:

\eta_{\bm{x}}(c+\eta_{\text{dual}})(1-\sigma)-\eta_{\bm{x}}\eta_{\text{dual}}=\eta_{\bm{x}}\left[c(1-\sigma)+\eta_{\text{dual}}(1-\sigma)-\eta_{\text{dual}}\right]=\eta_{\bm{x}}(c(1-\sigma)-\eta_{\text{dual}}\sigma).

(96)

Thus, the characteristic polynomial of the active subsystem is

\chi_{\mathcal{J}_{\text{AL},\mathcal{A}_{\bm{x}}}}(\sigma)=\det\left((1-\sigma)^{2}\mathbb{I}-\eta_{\bm{x}}(1-\sigma)A-\eta_{\bm{x}}(c(1-\sigma)-\eta_{\text{dual}}\sigma)B^{\top}B\right).

(97)

Conclusion.

The characteristic polynomial of the full Jacobian
\mathcal{J}_{\text{AL}}
is the product of the polynomials for the active and inactive subsystems,
\chi_{\mathcal{J}_{\text{AL}}}(\sigma)=\chi_{\mathcal{J}_{\text{AL},I}}(\sigma)\cdot\chi_{\mathcal{J}_{\text{AL},\mathcal{A}_{\bm{x}}}}(\sigma)
, which gives the final result.

∎

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle=\nabla\bm{g}_{\mathcal{A}_{\bm{x}}}(\bm{x}^{*}).

Proof.

The dual–first optimistic ascent method in

\mathcal{J}_{\bm{x}}
and
\mathcal{J}_{\bm{\lambda}}

\bm{x}_{t}

Lemma 5
.

The characteristic polynomial of the Jacobian
\mathcal{J}_{\text{OG}}
at
(\bm{x}^{*},\bm{\lambda}^{*})
from

is

\chi_{\mathcal{J}_{\text{OG}}}(\sigma)=(-\sigma)^{m-|\mathcal{A}_{\bm{x}}|+d}\,\det\left((1-\sigma)^{2}\mathbb{I}-\eta_{\bm{x}}(1-\sigma)A-\eta_{\bm{x}}(\omega(1-\sigma)-\eta_{\text{dual}}\sigma)B^{\top}B\right).

(113)

Proof.

The Jacobian
\mathcal{J}_{\text{OG}}
is block lower-triangular with respect to the partition between the active subsystem
(\bm{x}_{t},\bm{x}_{t-1},\bm{\lambda}_{t,\mathcal{A}_{\bm{x}}})
and the inactive subsystem
\bm{\lambda}_{t,I}
. Its characteristic polynomial is therefore the product of the characteristic polynomials of these two diagonal blocks.

Inactive subsystem.

The bottom-right block corresponding to the dual variables of inactive inequality constraints is
\mathcal{J}_{\text{OG},I}=0

\chi_{\mathcal{J}_{\text{OG},I}}(\sigma)=\det(0-\sigma\mathbb{I})=\det(-\sigma\mathbb{I}_{|I_{I}|})=(-\sigma)^{|I_{I}|}.

Active Subsystem

The Jacobian of the active subsystem, which maps
(\bm{x}_{t},\bm{x}_{t-1},\bm{\lambda}_{t,\mathcal{A}_{\bm{x}}})\mapsto(\bm{x}_{t+1},\bm{x}_{t},\bm{\lambda}_{t+1,A})

\mathcal{J}_{\text{OG},\mathcal{A}_{\bm{x}}}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathbb{I}-\eta_{\bm{x}}A-\eta_{\bm{x}}(\eta_{\text{dual}}+\omega)B^{\top}B&\eta_{\bm{x}}\omega\,B^{\top}B&-\eta_{\bm{x}}B^{\top}\\
\mathbb{I}&0&0\\
(\eta_{\text{dual}}+\omega)B&-\omega\,B&\mathbb{I}\end{pmatrix}.

(115)

We seek its characteristic polynomial,
\chi_{\mathcal{J}_{\text{OG},\mathcal{A}_{\bm{x}}}}(\sigma)=\det(\mathcal{J}_{\text{OG},\mathcal{A}_{\bm{x}}}-\sigma\mathbb{I})
. Let
M=\mathcal{J}_{\text{OG},\mathcal{A}_{\bm{x}}}-\sigma\mathbb{I}
, with blocks
M_{ij}
:

M=\begin{pmatrix}\mathbb{I}-\eta_{\bm{x}}A-\eta_{\bm{x}}(\eta_{\text{dual}}+\omega)B^{\top}B-\sigma\mathbb{I}&\eta_{\bm{x}}\omega\,B^{\top}B&-\eta_{\bm{x}}B^{\top}\\
\mathbb{I}&-\sigma\mathbb{I}&0\\
(\eta_{\text{dual}}+\omega)B&-\omega\,B&(1-\sigma)\mathbb{I}\end{pmatrix}.

(116)

To simplify the determinant calculation, we apply a determinant-preserving column operation,
C_{2}\leftarrow C_{2}+\sigma C_{1}
, which zeros out the
(2,2)
block:

\det(M)=\det\begin{pmatrix}M_{11}&M_{12}+\sigma M_{11}&M_{13}\\
\mathbb{I}&-\sigma\mathbb{I}+\sigma\mathbb{I}&0\\
M_{31}&M_{32}+\sigma M_{31}&M_{33}\end{pmatrix}=\det\begin{pmatrix}M_{11}&M^{\prime}_{12}&M_{13}\\
\mathbb{I}&0&0\\
M_{31}&M^{\prime}_{32}&M_{33}\end{pmatrix},

(117)

where
M^{\prime}_{12}=M_{12}+\sigma M_{11}
and
M^{\prime}_{32}=M_{32}+\sigma M_{31}
. Swapping the first two block rows (corresponding to
\bm{x}_{t+1}
and
\bm{x}_{t}
) multiplies the determinant by
(-1)^{d}
, yielding a block upper-triangular matrix:

\chi_{\mathcal{J}_{\text{OG},\mathcal{A}_{\bm{x}}}}(\sigma)=(-1)^{d}\det\begin{pmatrix}\mathbb{I}&0&0\\
M_{11}&M^{\prime}_{12}&M_{13}\\
M_{31}&M^{\prime}_{32}&M_{33}\end{pmatrix}=(-1)^{d}\det(\mathbb{I})\det\begin{pmatrix}M^{\prime}_{12}&M_{13}\\
M^{\prime}_{32}&M_{33}\end{pmatrix}.

(118)

Since
M_{33}=(1-\sigma)\mathbb{I}
is a multiple of the identity matrix, it commutes with all other blocks. We can thus compute the determinant of the remaining
2\times 2
block matrix as
\det(M^{\prime}_{12}M_{33}-M_{13}M^{\prime}_{32})
. Let’s compute this product:

\displaystyle M^{\prime}_{12}M_{33}-M_{13}M^{\prime}_{32}

\displaystyle=(M_{12}+\sigma M_{11})M_{33}-M_{13}(M_{32}+\sigma M_{31})

\displaystyle=(1-\sigma)(M_{12}+\sigma M_{11})-M_{13}(M_{32}+\sigma M_{31})

\displaystyle=(1-\sigma)\big(\eta_{\bm{x}}\omega B^{\top}B+\sigma(\mathbb{I}-\eta_{\bm{x}}A-\eta_{\bm{x}}(\eta_{\text{dual}}+\omega)B^{\top}B-\sigma\mathbb{I})\big)

\displaystyle\qquad-(-\eta_{\bm{x}}B^{\top})\big(-\omega B+\sigma(\eta_{\text{dual}}+\omega)B\big)

\displaystyle=\sigma(1-\sigma)(1-\sigma)\mathbb{I}-\eta_{\bm{x}}\sigma(1-\sigma)A

\displaystyle\qquad+\big[(1-\sigma)\eta_{\bm{x}}\omega-\sigma(1-\sigma)\eta_{\bm{x}}(\eta_{\text{dual}}+\omega)-\eta_{\bm{x}}\omega+\sigma\eta_{\bm{x}}(\eta_{\text{dual}}+\omega)\big]B^{\top}B

\displaystyle=\sigma(1-\sigma)^{2}\mathbb{I}-\eta_{\bm{x}}\sigma(1-\sigma)A

\displaystyle\qquad+\eta_{\bm{x}}\big[\omega-\sigma\omega-\sigma\eta_{\text{dual}}-\sigma\omega+\sigma^{2}\eta_{\text{dual}}+\sigma^{2}\omega-\omega+\sigma\eta_{\text{dual}}+\sigma\omega\big]B^{\top}B

\displaystyle=\sigma(1-\sigma)^{2}\mathbb{I}-\eta_{\bm{x}}\sigma(1-\sigma)A+\eta_{\bm{x}}\big[\sigma^{2}\eta_{\text{dual}}-\sigma\omega(1-\sigma)\big]B^{\top}B

\displaystyle=\sigma(1-\sigma)^{2}\mathbb{I}-\eta_{\bm{x}}\sigma(1-\sigma)A-\eta_{\bm{x}}\sigma\big(\omega(1-\sigma)-\eta_{\text{dual}}\sigma\big)B^{\top}B.

The determinant of this
d\times d
matrix is:

\det(M^{\prime}_{12}M_{33}-M_{13}M^{\prime}_{32})=\sigma^{d}\det\left((1-\sigma)^{2}\mathbb{I}-\eta_{\bm{x}}(1-\sigma)A-\eta_{\bm{x}}\big(\omega(1-\sigma)-\eta_{\text{dual}}\sigma\big)B^{\top}B\right).

(119)

Substituting this back into the expression for
\chi_{\mathcal{J}_{\text{OG},\mathcal{A}_{\bm{x}}}}(\sigma)
:

\displaystyle\chi_{\mathcal{J}_{\text{OG},\mathcal{A}_{\bm{x}}}}(\sigma)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{d}\cdot\sigma^{d}\det\left(\dots\right)

(120)

\displaystyle=(-\sigma)^{d}\det\left((1-\sigma)^{2}\mathbb{I}-\eta_{\bm{x}}(1-\sigma)A-\eta_{\bm{x}}\big(\omega(1-\sigma)-\eta_{\text{dual}}\sigma\big)B^{\top}B\right).

(121)

The characteristic polynomial of the full Jacobian
\mathcal{J}_{\text{OG}}
is the product of the polynomials for the active and inactive subsystems,
\chi_{\mathcal{J}_{\text{OG}}}(\sigma)=\chi_{\mathcal{J}_{\text{OG},I}}(\sigma)\cdot\chi_{\mathcal{J}_{\text{OG},\mathcal{A}_{\bm{x}}}}(\sigma)
, which gives the final result.
∎

Proof of Theorem

2
.

Consider the following algorithms for solving inequality-constrained problems:

\raisebox{-.7pt}{{\hspace{-0.8mm} \small1}}⃝

Eq.

AL-GDA
: Primal-first alternating gradient descent–projected gradient ascent on the Augmented Lagrangian,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\bm{x}_{t+1}&\leftarrow\bm{x}_{t}-\eta_{\bm{x}}\left[\nabla f(\bm{x}_{t})+\big[\bm{\lambda}_{t}+c\,\bm{g}(\bm{x}_{t})\big]_{+}^{\top}\nabla\bm{g}(\bm{x}_{t})\right]\\[6.0pt]
\bm{\lambda}_{t+1}&\leftarrow\left(1-\frac{\eta_{\text{dual}}}{c}\right)\bm{\lambda}_{t}+\frac{\eta_{\text{dual}}}{c}\Big[\bm{\lambda}_{t}+c\,\bm{g}(\bm{x}_{t+1})\Big]_{+},\end{split}

(122)

where
0<\eta_{\text{dual}}\leq c
.

\raisebox{-.7pt}{{\hspace{-0.8mm} \small2}}⃝

Eq.

Lag-GD-OA
: dual-first alternating gradient descent–projected optimistic ascent on the Lagrangian,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\bm{\lambda}_{t+1}&\leftarrow\Big[\bm{\lambda}_{t}+\eta_{\text{dual}}\,\bm{g}(\bm{x}_{t})+\omega\big(\bm{g}(\bm{x}_{t})-\bm{g}(\bm{x}_{t-1})\big)\Big]_{+}\\[6.0pt]
\bm{x}_{t+1}&\leftarrow\bm{x}_{t}-\eta_{\bm{x}}\left[\nabla f(\bm{x}_{t})+\bm{\lambda}_{t+1}^{\top}\nabla\bm{g}(\bm{x}_{t})\right],\end{split}

(123)

where
\omega>0
.

We will analyze the local convergence properties of both algorithms at a stationary (KKT) point
(\bm{x}^{*},\bm{\lambda}^{*})
, satisfying
1
, and operating on a partition of the
\bm{\lambda}=(\bm{\lambda}_{A},\bm{\lambda}_{I})
state into those corresponding to active and inactive constraints at
\bm{x}^{*}
:

\bm{\lambda}_{t,\mathcal{A}_{\bm{x}}}=\{\,\lambda_{i}\mid g_{i}(\bm{x}^{*})=0,\;\lambda_{i}^{*}>0\,\},

and inactive ones

\bm{\lambda}_{t,I}=\{\,\lambda_{i}\mid g_{i}(\bm{x}^{*})<0,\;\lambda_{i}^{*}=0\,\}.

Let the matrices
A
and
B
be defined at the stationary point
(\bm{x}^{*},\bm{\lambda}^{*})
as

A=\nabla^{2}_{\bm{x}}\mathcal{L}(\bm{x}^{*},\bm{\lambda}^{*})\quad\text{and}\quad B=\nabla\bm{g}_{\mathcal{A}_{\bm{x}}}(\bm{x}^{*}),

(124)

where
A
is the
Hessian of the Lagrangian
with respect to
\bm{x}
and
B
is the
Jacobian of the active constraints
.

From
Lemmas

2
and
3
, we know that the Jacobian
\mathcal{J}_{\text{AL}}
of the Augmented Lagrangian updates in
Eq.

122
, acting on the partitioned state
(\bm{x}_{t},\bm{\lambda}_{t,\mathcal{A}_{\bm{x}}},\bm{\lambda}_{t,I})\mapsto(\bm{x}_{t+1},\bm{\lambda}_{t+1,A},\bm{\lambda}_{t+1,I})
and evaluated at
(\bm{x}^{*},\bm{\lambda}^{*})=(\bm{x}^{*},\bm{\lambda}^{*}_{\mathcal{A}_{\bm{x}}},\bm{0})
, is

\mathcal{J}_{\text{AL}}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathbb{I}-\eta_{\bm{x}}(A+c\,B^{\top}B)&-\eta_{\bm{x}}B^{\top}&0\\[6.0pt]
\eta_{\text{dual}}B(\mathbb{I}-\eta_{\bm{x}}(A+c\,B^{\top}B))&\mathbb{I}-\eta_{\bm{x}}\eta_{\text{dual}}BB^{\top}&0\\[6.0pt]
0&0&(1-\eta_{\text{dual}}/c)\mathbb{I}\end{pmatrix};

(125)

and that the characteristic polynomial for this Jacobian is:

\chi_{\mathcal{J}_{\text{AL}}}(\sigma)=\left(1-\frac{\eta_{\text{dual}}}{c}-\sigma\right)^{m-|\mathcal{A}_{\bm{x}}|}\,\det\left((1-\sigma)^{2}\mathbb{I}-\eta_{\bm{x}}(1-\sigma)A-\eta_{\bm{x}}(c(1-\sigma)-\eta_{\text{dual}}\sigma)B^{\top}B\right).

(126)

Moreover, from
Lemmas

5
and
4
, we know that for the same choices of
A
and
B
matrices, it follows that the Jacobian
\mathcal{J}_{\text{OG}}
of the dual optimistic ascent updates in
Eq.

123
, acting on the partitioned state
(\bm{x}_{t},\bm{x}_{t-1},\bm{\lambda}_{t,\mathcal{A}_{\bm{x}}},\bm{\lambda}_{t,I})\mapsto(\bm{x}_{t+1},\bm{x}_{t},\bm{\lambda}_{t+1,A},\bm{\lambda}_{t+1,I})
and evaluated at
(\bm{x}^{*},\bm{\lambda}^{*})=(\bm{x}^{*},\bm{x}^{*},\bm{\lambda}^{*}_{\mathcal{A}_{\bm{x}}},\bm{0})
, is

\mathcal{J}_{\text{OG}}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathbb{I}-\eta_{\bm{x}}A-\eta_{\bm{x}}(\eta_{\text{dual}}+\omega)B^{\top}B&\eta_{\bm{x}}\omega\,B^{\top}B&-\eta_{\bm{x}}B^{\top}&0\\[6.0pt]
\mathbb{I}&0&0&0\\[6.0pt]
(\eta_{\text{dual}}+\omega)B&-\omega\,B&\mathbb{I}&0\\[6.0pt]
0&0&0&0\end{pmatrix},

(127)

and that the characteristic polynomial for this Jacobian is:

\chi_{\mathcal{J}_{\text{OG}}}(\sigma)=(-\sigma)^{|I_{I}|+d}\,\det\left((1-\sigma)^{2}\mathbb{I}-\eta_{\bm{x}}(1-\sigma)A-\eta_{\bm{x}}(\omega(1-\sigma)-\eta_{\text{dual}}\sigma)B^{\top}B\right),

(128)

Remark on the
\sigma=1
case

A subtle but important point is that, although the structure of these characteristic polynomials might suggest
\sigma=1
could be a root if
\det(B^{\top}B)=0
, it can be shown that
\sigma=1
is in fact not an eigenvalue of either
\mathcal{J}_{\text{AL}}
or
\mathcal{J}_{\text{OG}}
under
3
,
2
and
1
—conditions that are generally necessary, though not sufficient, for convergence.

We establish the equivalence of the local convergence properties of the two algorithms by setting the optimistic parameter
\omega=c
and comparing the characteristic polynomials of their Jacobians.

Upon setting
\omega=c
in
\chi_{\mathcal{J}_{\text{OG}}}(\sigma)
, the non-trivial determinant term becomes identical to that in
\chi_{\mathcal{J}_{\text{AL}}}(\sigma)
:

\det\left((1-\sigma)^{2}\mathbb{I}-\eta_{\bm{x}}(1-\sigma)A-\eta_{\bm{x}}(c(1-\sigma)-\eta_{\text{dual}}\sigma)B^{\top}B\right).

(129)

The roots of this determinant define the set of non-trivial eigenvalues, which are shared between both methods. Local convergence of either algorithm depends on whether all these shared eigenvalues lie within the unit circle.

The remaining eigenvalues are given by the roots of the other factors in each polynomial:

•

For the Augmented Lagrangian method, the factor
\left(1-\frac{\eta_{\text{dual}}}{c}-\sigma\right)^{|I_{I}|}
yields
|I_{I}|
eigenvalues at
\sigma=1-\eta_{\text{dual}}/c
. Given the condition
0<\eta_{\text{dual}}\leq c
, these eigenvalues lie in the interval
(0,1)
, and are thus stable.

•

For the optimistic ascent method, the factor
(-\sigma)^{|I_{I}|+d}
yields
|I_{I}|+d
eigenvalues at
\sigma=0
, which are also stable.

Conclusion
. Since the non-trivial eigenvalues governing convergence are identical for both algorithms, and the remaining trivial eigenvalues for both are stable, the spectral radius
\rho(\mathcal{J}_{\text{AL}})<1
if and only if
\rho(\mathcal{J}_{\text{OG}})<1
. Therefore, with the choice of
\omega=c
, one algorithm converges locally if and only if the other one does.

Moreover, it holds that:

\rho(\mathcal{J}_{\text{AL}})=\max\{\rho(\mathcal{J}_{\text{OG}}),1-\eta_{\text{dual}}/c\}.

(130)

This implies that if the shared eigenvalues dictate the convergence rate (i.e., when
1>\rho(\mathcal{J}_{\text{OG}})\geq 1-\eta_{\text{dual}}/c
), both algorithms converge at the same local rate.
∎

\bm{\lambda}^{*}\succeq\bm{0}

This Jacobian is well-defined thanks to

(\bm{x}_{t},(\bm{\lambda}_{t,\mathcal{A}_{\bm{x}}},\bm{\mu}_{t}),\bm{\lambda}_{t,I})

Let
(\bm{x}^{*},\bm{\lambda}^{*},\bm{\mu}^{*})
be an LSSP of the algorithm in
Eq.

AL-GDA

1

(\bm{x}^{*},\bm{\lambda}^{*},\bm{\mu}^{*})
is a KKT point of
Eq.

CMP

Moreover, we know that
\rho(\mathcal{J}_{\text{AL}})<1

\mathcal{J}_{\text{AL}}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathbb{I}-\eta_{\bm{x}}(A+c\,B^{\top}B)&-\eta_{\bm{x}}B^{\top}&0\\[5.69054pt]

and

A=\nabla^{2}_{\bm{x}}\mathcal{L}(\bm{x}^{*},\bm{\lambda}^{*},\bm{\mu}^{*}),\qquad B=\begin{pmatrix}\nabla\bm{g}_{\mathcal{A}_{\bm{x}}}(\bm{x}^{*})\\[2.84526pt]

(139)

as in the
Proof of Proposition

The assumption
\rho(\mathcal{J}_{\text{AL}})<1
implies that
\rho(\mathcal{J}_{\text{AL},\mathcal{A}_{\bm{x}}}^{\prime})<1

\mathcal{J}_{\text{AL},\mathcal{A}_{\bm{x}}}^{\prime}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathbb{I}-\eta A-\eta(c+\eta)B^{\top}B&-\eta B^{\top}\\[2.84526pt]

(140)

\bm{x}^{*}
is not a local constrained minimizer of

This implies the existence of a nonzero vector
\bm{d}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

By the Rayleigh-Ritz theorem, choose
\bm{d}

\mathcal{J}_{\text{AL},\mathcal{A}_{\bm{x}}}^{\prime}\begin{pmatrix}\bm{d}\\
0\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}(\mathbb{I}-\eta A-\eta(c+\eta)B^{\top}B)\bm{d}\\

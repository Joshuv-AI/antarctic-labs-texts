Dabigatran-based versus warfarin-based triple antithrombotic regimen with a 1-month intensification after coronary stenting in patients with nonvalvular atrial fibrillation (COACH-AF PCI)
Authors: Ming Liang, Jun Jin, Ruiping Zhao, Hui Li, Shaoliang Chen, Limin Liu, Ping Xu, Wei Wang, Xiang Cheng, Xuezhi Chen, Ling Tao, Qi Zhang, Ming Yang, Chunyu Zeng, Li Li, Yi Zheng, Hong Chen, Jincheng Guo, Zhixiong Zhong, Yayan Bi, Biao Cheng, Yujie Zhou, Hong Wang, Yujie Zhang, Miaohan Qiu, Qi Zhang, Ping Zhang, Jie Zhang, ZuLu Wang, Yi Li, Yaling Han
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundTo assess the safety and efficacy of dabigatran-based triple antithrombotic (TAT) regimen in patients after percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) with atrial fibrillation (AF).MethodsA multicenter, open-label, randomized controlled trial across 50 Chinese hospitals enrolled nonvalvular AF patients after coronary stenting. Participants were randomly assigned to receive dabigatran (110 mg twice/daily) or warfarin-based TAT regimen (dabigatran or warfarin with aspirin and clopidogrel) for 1 month and then convert to dual antithrombotic therapy (dabigatran or warfarin with clopidogrel) for 6 months. The primary safety endpoint was the first occurrence of clinically relevant bleeding (BARC types 2–5). Secondary endpoints included net adverse clinical events (NACEs) and major or clinically relevant non-major bleeding (CRNB). And the efficacy endpoint was the major adverse cardiac and cerebral events (MACCEs).ResultsThe study was terminated early due to COVID-19 impacting recruitment. A total of 540 patients were enrolled. BARC types 2–5 bleeding occurred in 8.0% and 4.3% of patients in warfarin and dabigatran groups, respectively (HR 0.54; 95% CI 0.26–1.09; P = 0.0861). And total bleeding events (BARC 1–5) were 20.5% and 9.4% (HR 0.44; 95% CI 0.27–0.70; P = 0.0005). Dabigatran showed a lower total bleeding risk than warfarin, with similar risks for BARC types 2–5 bleeding, NACEs, CRNB, major bleeding, and MACCEs.ConclusionsAmong AF patients underwent PCI, the dabigatran-based TAT regimen did not significantly reduce the rate of BARC types 2–5 bleeding at 6 months compared with warfarin-based regimen, although the power of the study to find a difference was low due to early termination (COACH-PCI, NCT03536611, https://clinicaltrials.gov/show/NCT03536611).Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12916-025-04477-1.Keywords: Dabigatran, Warfarin, Antithrombotic therapy, Atrial fibrillation, Percutaneous coronary intervention, Randomized controlled trials

Background

The coexistence of coronary artery disease (CAD) and atrial fibrillation (AF) is a prevalent occurrence, with around one in five AF patients experiencing acute coronary syndrome (ACS) or requiring percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) [1, 2], and the perioperative antithrombotic regimen continues to be one of the key points. According to the 2024 European Society of Cardiology (ESC) guidelines, for patients with ACS undergoing PCI and patients with chronic coronary syndrome (CCS) undergoing uncomplicated PCI, it is recommended to transition from triple antiplatelet therapy (TAT) to oral anticoagulant (OAC) plus clopidogrel after 1 week. For both ACS and CCS patients with high ischemic risk, it is recommended to use at least 1 month of TAT before transitioning to OAC plus clopidogrel [3]. Long-standing practice has found that for such patients, the warfarin-based triple antithrombotic regimen is effective and safe for perioperative TAT [4–6]. In recent years, the widespread use of direct oral anticoagulants (DOACs) has disrupted this pattern. For AF-PCI patients, DOACs have been widely accepted as a substitute for warfarin in TAT, and dabigatran offers similar or better efficacy in terms of systemic embolism with lower intracranial hemorrhage and similar major bleeding [4, 7, 8]. The focus of recent randomized controlled trial (RCT) experiments has gradually shifted toward how different DOACs should be combined with dual antiplatelet therapy (DAPT) to balance both safety and efficacy.

Dabigatran, as a direct thrombin inhibitor, has a different mechanism of action and effect compared to factor Xa inhibitors such as rivaroxaban and edoxaban. It is also widely used for anticoagulation therapy in AF patients. However, there is insufficient evidence on how to combine dabigatran with antiplatelet drugs. The RE-DUAL PCI trial [7] designed the dabigatran 110 mg/150 mg plus P2Y12 receptor inhibitor group and the warfarin plus DAPT group, and the results were indeed intriguing. The incidence of thrombotic events and myocardial infarction in the dabigatran 110 mg group was higher than in the warfarin group. In contrast, the statistical differences were even less pronounced in the dabigatran 150 mg plus P2Y12 receptor inhibitor group. This inevitably leads to the question: whether the improvement of safety might necessitate a compromise in the effectiveness of preventing recurrent myocardial infarctions and thrombus formation.

The RE-LY study [8] indicated that compared with the 110 mg, the 150 mg dose of dabigatran reduced the risk of stroke or systemic embolism, however, the 150 mg dose was also associated with a higher rate of genitourinary bleeding, major hemorrhages, and intracranial hemorrhages compared to the dabigatran 110 mg group. Consequently, it may be inadvisable to indiscriminately increase drug dosages in dual therapy. The efficacy and safety of dabigatran 110 mg in combination with DAPT compared to warfarin plus DAPT remain uncertain. This study was designed with an equal antithrombotic dose power (1 anticoagulation plus DAPT), seeking to explore whether, in patients with AF-PCI, a 1-month postoperative intensification of dabigatran-based TAT regimen (dabigatran, aspirin, and clopidogrel) can reduce bleeding incidents comparable to warfarin-based TAT regimen (warfarin, aspirin, and clopidogrel) while maintaining similar embolism incidents.

Methods

Trial design

The COACH-AF PCI trial was a prospective, multicenter, open-label RCT conducted at 50 hospitals in China. This study was designed with an equal power in each group, seeking to explore whether, in patients with AF-PCI, a 1-month postoperative intensification of dabigatran-based TAT regimen (dabigatran, aspirin, and clopidogrel) can reduce bleeding incidents comparable to warfarin-based TAT regimen (warfarin, aspirin, and clopidogrel) while maintaining similar embolism incidents. The study protocol was registered on ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT03536611, https://clinicaltrials.gov/show/NCT03536611).

Participants

Eligible participants were aged ≥ 18 years with nonvalvular AF necessitating OACs and were exposed to successful PCI for stable CAD or ACS. Inclusion criteria encompassed nonvalvular AF patients not attributable to a reversible etiology, while exclusion criteria comprised individuals with mechanical heart valves, moderate-to-severe mitral stenosis, end-stage renal disease, and other significant comorbidities. The whole inclusion alongside exclusion criteria can be found in Additional file 1: Supplementary material 1.

Randomization and drugs

Sites were instructed to complete central randomization between 6 h post-arterial sheath elimination and 3 days post-successful PCI. The random code list was automatically generated by the computer system. Before the trial begins, the random code list was configured into the Interactive Network Response System, and the system assigned random numbers to the patients who meet the selection criteria. Eligible patients were allocated at random in a 1:1 ratio to receive either dabigatran-based TAT regimen group and warfarin-based TAT regimen group. For patients on intravenous anticoagulant therapy, dabigatran therapy was initiated upon discontinuation of the intravenous agent. For those receiving subcutaneous anticoagulant therapy, dabigatran was initiated 0–2 h prior to the next scheduled subcutaneous dose. The requirement for bridging therapy with parenteral anticoagulants was to be evaluated by the investigator. Patients in the dabigatran group took dabigatran 110 mg twice daily plus aspirin and clopidogrel for 1 month, after which they switched to dabigatran 110 mg twice daily plus clopidogrel until the end of the 6-month follow-up. Patients in the warfarin group took warfarin plus aspirin and clopidogrel for 1 month, after which they switched to warfarin plus clopidogrel until the end of the 6-month follow-up, with INR monitoring to adjust the dosage. In cases where staged PCI was designed, informed consent and randomized allocation were conducted following the final stage. All patients were to adhere to the study medication regimen for 6 months. In patients newly initiated on warfarin therapy, international normalized ratio (INR) should be measured at least every 2 weeks during the first 3 months of treatment, and thereafter at least monthly. The frequency of INR monitoring could be increased at the discretion of the investigator or anticoagulation clinic based on clinical circumstances. For the warfarin group, patients underwent at least five tests of INR available up to the study end date.

Outcomes and definitions

The primary safety endpoint was recognized as the first occurrence of clinically relevant bleeding, classified according to Bleeding Academic Research Consortium (BARC) 2–5 [9]. The principal secondary endpoint was the first occurrence of NACEs. Additional secondary endpoints encompassed ISTH major bleeding or clinically relevant non-major bleeding (CRNB), total bleeding events (BARC 1–5), and major bleeding (BARC 3–5). And the efficacy endpoint was evaluated by MACCEs.

Detailed BARC classification is provided in Additional file 1: Supplementary material 2. MACCEs are defined as all-cause mortality, myocardial infarction, stroke, or ischemic target vessel revascularization (iTVR). NACEs are composed of MACCEs or clinically relevant bleeding as defined by the BARC 2–5. International Society on Thrombosis and Haemostasis (ISTH) major bleeding is defined as symptomatic hemorrhage occurring in critical sites or organs, intracranial, intraspinal, intraocular, retroperitoneal, intra-articular, pericardial, or in muscle (myofascial compartment syndrome), and/or hemorrhage accompanied by a decrease of at least 2 g/dL (1.24 mmol/L) in hemoglobin or the need for transfusion of 2 or more units of blood or concentrated blood cells, and/or life-threatening bleeding. CRNB is defined as bleeding that does not meet the criteria for major bleeding but requires medical intervention (e.g., hemostatic treatment, imaging, or adjustment of antithrombotic therapy), is associated with symptoms (e.g., anemia-related manifestations, pain, or functional impairment), or impacts clinical management decisions.

Follow-up

Randomization was performed 6–72 h post-PCI, with all study outcomes measured from randomization. All participants were monitored for 6 months post-randomization, ensuring clinical follow-up via onsite visits or phone calls. Clinical data were also gathered from hospital records. Trial medications were provided just after PCI and the time points of 1 month and 3 months post-PCI. Medication adherence was checked through pill counts and self-reports. The study team used adherence counseling to improve drug compliance, and the patient had clinical follow-ups via onsite visits or phone calls at 1, 3, and 6 months after randomization. In the warfarin group, time in therapeutic range (TTR) was evaluated via INR registration records, with the standard defined as the final stable INR value falling within the 1.8–3.0 range.

Statistical analysis

Based on findings from the RE-DUAL PCI studies [7], it was recommended that a sample size of 500 cases/group be enrolled in 110 mg of dabigatran dual treatment group to achieve an 80% statistical power in detecting a 6% risk difference compared to the warfarin group, assuming a 6-month follow-up period and a bilateral alpha level of 0.05 in a 1:1 parallel control design. Considering an expected 10% loss to follow-up, the total necessary enrollment was about 1120 cases. The entire study was supervised by the Ethics Committee of the General Hospital of Northern Theater Command [k(2018)13–1]. Participants were included in the intention-to-treat (ITT) analysis. Additionally, per-protocol set (PPS) was defined as all randomized subjects without any major deviations (such as violations of inclusion or exclusion criteria and lost follow-up). PPS was used for the sensitivity analysis of endpoints. Continuous variables were reported as mean ± standard deviation and compared utilizing Student’s t-test. Categorical variables were analyzed using frequencies and percentages and compared via the χ2 or Fisher’s exact tests. The duration from randomization to the first occurrence of clinically relevant bleeding characterized by BARC (types 2–5) was determined via a Cox proportional hazards model. Hazard ratios (HR) for dabigatran compared to warfarin, along with corresponding P values and 95% confidence intervals (CIs), were assessed from the model. The primary efficiency finding was statistically analyzed as the same as the primary outcome, with HRs and 95% CIs presented.

Results

Patient baseline and follow-up

The study was prematurely terminated at the interim analysis following evaluation by the Independent Data Monitoring Committee, due to COVID-19 pandemic-related prolongation of the patient recruitment period, increased research costs, and lower-than-expected patient screening eligibility rates. From April 17, 2018, to January 24, 2022, 540 patients diagnosed with AF and exposed to PCI were allocated at random to either receive an open-label regimen of dabigatran plus DAPT (n = 263) or warfarin plus DAPT (n = 277). Of ITT participants, 6 patients (2%) from the warfarin and 2 patients (0.7%) from the dabigatran groups were lost to follow-up. Typically, 16 patients (6%) from the warfarin and 12 patients (4%) from the dabigatran groups were excluded due to withdrawal of consent, and 24 patients (9%) from the warfarin and 1 patient (0.4%) from the dabigatran group were excluded due to protocol deviation. Finally, 217 in the warfarin and 262 in the dabigatran groups were included in the per-protocol analysis (PPS, Fig. 1). The final analysis included 540 patients (dabigatran: n = 277; warfarin: n = 263), with event rates of 4.3% vs. 8.0%, respectively. This provided 86% power to detect the observed HR of 0.54, but limited power (52%) for the prespecified HR of 0.7. Based on the primary endpoint of BARC 2–5 bleeding events, and using the currently observed incidence rates of primary endpoint events, i.e., 12 cases (4.3%) in the dabigatran group and 21 cases (8%) in the warfarin group, the power of the current study was re-calculated as 43% under the assumption that the type I error risk (α) is set at 0.05. During follow-up, COVID-19 pandemic-related disruptions precluded effective and regular INR monitoring in some patients. The warfarin group’s INR target achievement rate, derived from available data, was 61.1%.

Trial profile. Protocol deviations primarily included inability to continue medication due to other medical reasons, pregnancy during the trial, CrCl < 30 mL/min (dabigatran group), violation of inclusion criteria, or non-compliance with the study protocol

In the ITT analysis, the PCI index was indicated for ACS in 444 of all patients (82%). The patient’s median age was 68 years, with 154 (29%) being female. Additionally, 106 patients (20%) had a stroke history. The median CHA2DS2-VASc score was 3.6. Between the dabigatran and warfarin groups, no significant variances were observed in any of the baseline measures (Table 1).

Safety endpoints

The primary endpoints, identified as the duration of the first clinically relevant bleeding occurrence determined by BARC (types 2–5), occurred in 21 patients (8.0%) receiving the warfarin regimen and in 12 patients (4.3%) receiving the dabigatran regimen though the ITT analysis. Table 2 and Fig. 2 present the HR for bleeding events BARC 2–5 as 0.54 (95% CI 0.26–1.09; P = 0.0861). In the PPS analysis, it is noteworthy that BARC types 2–5 bleeding events were documented in 21 out of 217 patients (9.7%) receiving the warfarin regimen, compared to 11 out of 262 patients (4.2%) receiving the dabigatran regimen. Additional file 1: Table S1 and Fig. S1 illustrate the HR for bleeding events as 0.42 (95% CI 0.20–0.88; P = 0.0212).

Table 2 and Fig. 3 exhibit that the incidence of NACEs was 8.7% in patients administered the dabigatran regimen, compared to 10.6% in those receiving the warfarin regimen, with HR for bleeding events of 0.81 (95% CI 0.47–1.39; P = 0.4435). Additional file 1: Fig. S2 exhibits that the incidence of NACEs was 8.8% in the dabigatran group, and 12.9% in the warfarin group, with HR for bleeding events of 0.67 (95% CI 0.38–1.16; P = 0.1496). Total bleeding events (BARC 1–5) were lower in the dabigatran group (9.4% vs. 20.5%; HR 0.44, 95% CI 0.27–0.70; P = 0.0005, Table 2). The incidence of ISTH major bleeding or CRNB was 4.7% in patients on the dabigatran regimen and 8.0% in those on the warfarin regimen, yielding HR for bleeding events of 0.59 (95% CI 0.29–1.17; P = 0.1292, Table 2). Furthermore, the major bleeding event incidence (BARC 3–5) was 1.1% in patients managed with dabigatran, as opposed to 1.5% in those managed with warfarin. The HR for bleeding events was 0.71 (95% CI 0.16–3.19; P = 0.6588, Table 2). However, in the PPS analysis, it was found that the incidence of ISTH major bleeding or CRNB was lower in the dabigatran regimen (95% CI 0.23–0.95; P = 0.035, Additional file 1: Table S1). The other data analysis for the PPS population was largely consistent with that for the ITT.

Efficacy endpoints

At 6 months, the MACCEs (comprising cardiovascular death, stroke, myocardial infarction, and iTVR) appeared in 12 patients (4.3%) in the dabigatran regimen compared with 8 patients (3.0%) in the warfarin regimen (HR 1.43; 95% CI 0.59–3.50; P = 0.4316). Among them, there were 3 cases of myocardial infarction, all occurring within 1 month after PCI. For the incidences of MACCEs, there was no significant difference observed between the warfarin and dabigatran groups (Table 2). The data analysis on the PPS was largely consistent with the ITT (Additional file 1: Table S1). For the occurrence of BARC 2–5 bleeding during the 6-month follow-up, there was also no significant difference observed between non-prespecified subgroups including age, gender, and comorbidities (Additional file 1: Fig. S3).

Discussion

Our study is the first clinical trial with the equal antithrombotic dose power (1 anticoagulation plus DAPT) to examine the safety and efficacy of dabigatran-based intensive TAT regimen (dabigatran 110 mg combined with DAPT) against warfarin-based intensive TAT regimen (warfarin combined with DAPT) in 1 month after coronary stenting in patients with nonvalvular AF. Then, patients in both groups were switched to the dabigatran/warfarin plus clopidogrel and maintained until 6 months. There were no significant differences observed between dabigatran- and warfarin-based TAT groups in terms of clinically relevant bleeding (BARC types 2–5 bleeding), NACEs, CRNB, major bleeding, and MACCEs. Dabigatran-based TAT was associated with a lower total bleeding risk compared with warfarin-based TAT.

Managing AF in patients who have been exposed to PCI presents a multifaceted dilemma. The optimal antithrombotic treatment post-coronary stent implantation in these patients remains uncertain, with ongoing debate regarding the balance between efficiency and safety. Inadequate antithrombotic therapy and heightened risk of bleeding contribute to the challenge of achieving favorable clinical outcomes in this population. Since 2010, four types of DOACs have been introduced to the market, namely direct thrombin inhibitors (e.g., dabigatran) or Xa factor inhibitors (e.g., rivaroxaban, apixaban, and edoxaban). The DOACs typically do not necessitate stringent monitoring or dose adjustment and exhibit minimal drug as well as food interactions [10]. Numerous meta-analyses have demonstrated that DOACs surpass warfarin in avoiding AF-related stroke and systemic embolism while also carrying a decreased risk of intracranial hemorrhage [11–15]. Over the past decade, several large-scale RCTs have been undertaken from various perspectives to investigate AF patients complicated by ACS or PCI antithrombotic treatment. These studies aim to achieve an optimal balance between the risks of bleeding and ischemia. In the 2016 PIONEER AF-PCI trial [16], it was found that both the low-dose rivaroxaban dual therapy (rivaroxaban 15 mg once daily plus clopidogrel) and the ultra-low-dose rivaroxaban triple therapy (rivaroxaban 2.5 mg twice daily plus aspirin plus clopidogrel) demonstrate better safety profiles than the warfarin triple therapy (warfarin plus aspirin plus clopidogrel), while maintaining similar efficacy. This preliminary evidence supports the advantages of using DOACs in patients with AF following PCI. Similarly, the 2019 ENTRUST-AF PCI trial [17] compared the safety and efficacy between the edoxaban 60 mg once daily plus P2Y12 receptor inhibitor group and the warfarin plus aspirin plus P2Y12 receptor inhibitor group. The results indicated that the efficacy of both groups was similar, and the safety profile of edoxaban was not inferior to that of warfarin. The 2022 AUGUSTUS trial [18] demonstrated that apixaban could achieve comparable efficacy with enhanced safety.

Dabigatran, as a direct thrombin inhibitor, has a different mechanism of action and effect compared to factor Xa inhibitors such as rivaroxaban and edoxaban. It is also widely used for anticoagulation therapy in AF patients. However, there is insufficient evidence on how to combine dabigatran with antiplatelet drugs for patients who require its use. The RE-DUAL PCI trial [7] in nonvalvular AF patients after PCI has confirmed that the use of dual-dose dabigatran in dual antithrombotic therapy (110 or 150 mg bid dabigatran plus a P2Y12 receptor inhibitor) can effectively reduce the incidence of major bleeding and CRNB versus triple antithrombotic therapy (warfarin plus aspirin and a P2Y12 receptor inhibitor), as recognized by the ISTH. In comparison to the RE-DUAL trial, this study was designed with an equal power in each group. Additionally, contemporary AHA/ACC and ESC guidelines both endorse a TAT duration of approximately 1 month, rendering the RE-DUAL trial potentially less applicable to current clinical practice.

It is indeed intriguing that the incidence of thrombotic events and myocardial infarction in the dabigatran 110 mg plus P2Y12 receptor inhibitor group is higher than that in the warfarin TAT group (warfarin plus dual antiplatelet therapy). In terms of safety, the dabigatran 110/150 mg plus P2Y12 receptor inhibitor group is not inferior to the warfarin TAT group. However, in preventing recurrent myocardial infarction and thrombosis, the regimen that may include DAPT could be more effective. However, if a “more effective” treatment regimen is applied, namely dabigatran 150 mg plus P2Y12 receptor inhibitor, the RE-LY study [8] indicated that compared to the 110 mg dose, the probability of various bleeding events may increase. Therefore, it cannot be concluded that the 150 mg dabigatran plus P2Y12 receptor inhibitor regimen should be preferentially recommended. It is possible that the enhancement of safety is achieved at the expense of efficacy to some extent.

Currently, there is a lack of consensus in regional guidelines concerning this particular issue. The 2024 updated recommendations from the ESC [4] advocated for a 1-week course of TAT in patients with ACS or CCS who had undergone PCI, followed by a transition to OAC combined with clopidogrel. For ACS and CCS patients identified as being at high risk of ischemia, a minimum of 1 month of TAT is suggested prior to switching to OAC plus clopidogrel. The AHA/ACC guidelines suggested that for ACS patients who require oral anticoagulant therapy, aspirin should be discontinued after 1 to 4 weeks of TAT, and dual antithrombotic treatment continuation with OAC as well as a P2Y12 inhibitor is favored over triple treatment (OAC, P2Y12 inhibitor, and aspirin) to decrease the risk of clinically relevant bleeding [19]. And for most patients, a DOAC is preferred over a warfarin. The 2023 Chinese Guidelines for the Management of Atrial Fibrillation [20] recommend the following: For AF-PCI patients, whether they have ACS or CCS, if they have thrombotic risk factors but low bleeding risk, or high bleeding risk (HAS-BLED score ≥ 3) but where stent thrombosis could lead to severe outcomes, it is recommended to use TAT for 1 month before switching to dual therapy. For patients with uncomplicated PCI and low risk of stent thrombosis, or those with thrombotic risk factors but high bleeding risk (HAS-BLED score ≥ 3), it is recommended to use TAT for 1 week before switching to dual therapy.

Despite the premature termination of the COACH-AF PCI trial, the lack of statistical significance in primary endpoint events, and insufficient study power, the results still contain notable observations warranting attention. Numerically, thromboembolic events were slightly more common in the dabigatran group, but this difference was not statistically significant. Given the study’s limited power, this should not be overinterpreted, and further research is needed to clarify potential differences.

The trial ended early, and there were no statistically significant variances observed in the efficacy outcomes of all-cause mortality, myocardial infarction, stroke, or iTVR between the two cohorts, mirroring findings from previous trials assessing the effectiveness of DOACs in AF patients exposed to PCI [21–24].

Our trial must be understood in the context of several limitations: (1) The study was prematurely terminated at the interim analysis following evaluation by the Independent Data Monitoring Committee, due to COVID-19 pandemic-related prolongation of the patient recruitment period, increased research costs, and lower-than-expected patient screening eligibility rates, which is the major limitation of this study. Based on the primary endpoint of BARC 2–5 bleeding events, and using the currently observed incidence rates of primary endpoint events, i.e., 12 cases (4.3%) in the dabigatran group and 21 cases (8%) in the warfarin group, the power of the current study was re-calculated and reduced from 80 to 43% under the assumption that the type I error risk (α) is set at 0.05. On the basis of the hazard ratio, the CI does not exclude up to 74% reduction and up to 9% excess in the risk of the occurrence of the BARC types 2–5 bleedings with dual therapy with dabigatran. (2) Moreover, we did not account for multiplicity, thereby increasing the chance for a type I error. Therefore, significant findings in secondary endpoints (e.g., total bleeding events) should be considered exploratory, which should be interpreted with caution. Accordingly, our results should be considered hypothesis-generating rather than conclusive inference and warrant dedicated, prospective confirmation. (3) Additionally, our study was conducted as an open-label trial, which may have introduced possible treatment or reporting bias, accounting for the higher withdrawal rate in the warfarin group. The present study exclusively incorporated data from the Chinese population, which creates a void regarding data from other nations and ethnicities, potentially limiting its generalizability.

Conclusions

In summary, for AF patients undergoing PCI, dabigatran-based TAT therapy did not significantly reduce BARC 2–5 bleeding compared with warfarin-based TAT. And there were no significant differences observed between the two groups in terms of NACEs, CRNB, major bleeding, and MACCEs. In patients with nonvalvular atrial fibrillation undergoing coronary stenting, the dabigatran-based 1-month intensive TAT regimen only exhibited a trend toward reducing bleeding risk. Nevertheless, future studies with larger sample sizes are warranted to further investigate this observation.

Introduction

This study is concerned with the chemotaxis-fluid model

\begin{cases}n_{t}+u\cdot\nabla n=\Delta(n\phi(v))+\mu n(1-n),\\
v_{t}+u\cdot\nabla v=\Delta v-nv,\\
u_{t}+\kappa(u\cdot\nabla)u=\Delta u+n\nabla\Phi-\nabla P,\quad\nabla\cdot u=0,\end{cases}

(1.1)

for the behavior of a microbial population
interacting with an incompressible fluid through transport and buoyancy (
[
24
]
). In this model, the motility of the microbial population with density
n
is assumed to depend on the concentration
v
of a certain signal substance consumed by
n
upon contact. As such, this study makes further progress on
recent interest and advances in the modeling literature addressing bacterial movement
in situations where cell motility is biased by chemical cues (
[
4
,
18
]
).
In addition to the signal-induced motility, the microbial population
n
is assumed to interact with its fluid environment through the fluid’s velocity field

u
and associated pressure
P
via buoyant forces mediated by the given gravitational potential
\Phi=\Phi(x)
, while both the microbial population and the signal substance are subject to convection.

In contrast with the prevalent literature on chemotaxis-fluid systems (
[
27

26
,
2

), there is an intriguing interrelation between the random diffusion and cross-diffusion in the system (1.1). Indeed, when splitting the nonlinear second order operator

\Delta(n\phi(v))
into the diffusive part
\nabla\cdot(\phi(v)\nabla n)
and
the cross-diffusive contribution
-\nabla\cdot(\psi(v)n\nabla v)
,
one can see that the migration rate is precisely linked through the relation
\psi(v)=-\phi^{\prime}(v)
, which indicates
that the microbial population achieves its signal sensing in a distinctly local manner (
[
18
,
4
]
).

In order to understand the implication of this link between random diffusion and cross-diffusion,
we start with the extensive literature on the Neumann
problem for the Keller-Segel model
with signal-dependent motility

\begin{cases}n_{t}=\Delta(n\phi(v))+\mu n(1-n),\\
v_{t}=\Delta v-v+n,\\
\end{cases}

(1.2)

in a smooth bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

. For instance, when
\mu=0
, the motility function

[

,
41
]
. In particular,
a critical-mass phenomenon occurs for (
1.2

\phi(v)=e^{-\chi v}
in the two-dimensional case (
[

,
12
]

We remark that with certain initial data of super-critical mass, the
solution of (

behavior of the classical Keller-Segel model. When

\displaystyle\lim_{v\rightarrow\infty}\phi(v)=0

When the signal substance is not produced but rather consumed by the microbial population, the signal-dependent model (

From the perspective of mathematical analysis, the degeneracy of signal-dependent type

we note that the approaches coping with the diffusion-inhibiting mechanisms in (

in (

Accordingly, even the issues on the global solvability of (

\leq&\frac{3C_{2}}{4}\int_{\Omega}v^{-3}|\nabla v|^{2}|D^{2}v|^{2}+\frac{3C_{2}}{4}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\nabla v|^{6}}{v^{5}}+C_{5}\int_{\Omega}(n^{3}+1)v,\end{split}

and thus

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\nabla v|^{4}}{v^{3}}+\frac{3C_{2}}{4}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\nabla v|^{6}}{v^{5}}\leq C_{5}\int_{\Omega}(n^{3}+1)v.

(4.16)

Furthermore, by the inequality

\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\nabla v|^{4}}{v^{3}}\leq\frac{3C_{2}}{4}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\nabla v|^{6}}{v^{5}}+C_{6}\int_{\Omega}v,

we get

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\nabla v|^{4}}{v^{3}}+\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\nabla v|^{4}}{v^{3}}\leq(C_{5}+C_{6})\int_{\Omega}(n^{3}+1)v.

(4.17)

Now, for any given
t\geq 1
, it follows from (
4.5
) that

\begin{split}\int^{t}_{t-1}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\nabla v|^{4}}{v^{3}}\leq C_{7},\end{split}

and thus there exists
t_{0}\in(t-1,t)
such that
\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\nabla v(\cdot,t_{0})|^{4}}{v^{3}(\cdot,t_{0})}\leq C_{7}
.
Hence from (
4.17
), we have

\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\nabla v(\cdot,t)|^{4}}{v^{3}(\cdot,t)}\leq\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\nabla v(\cdot,t_{0})|^{4}}{v^{3}(\cdot,t_{0})}+(C_{5}+C_{6})\int^{t}_{t_{0}}\int_{\Omega}(n^{3}+1)v.

(4.18)

Finally, with the help of (
4.8
) and (
2.5
), we arrive at (
4.14
). On the other hand, it follows from
(
4.17
) that for all
t<1
,

\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\nabla v(\cdot,t)|^{4}}{v^{3}(\cdot,t)}\leq\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\nabla v_{0}(\cdot)|^{4}}{v_{0}^{3}(\cdot)}+(C_{5}+C_{6})\int^{1}_{0}\int_{\Omega}(n^{3}+1)v,

and (
4.14
) is hence established readily.
∎

Now we shall establish the lower bound of
the time average value of
\|n(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}
at large time scale with the help of the boundedness of
\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\nabla v(\cdot,t)|^{4}}{v^{3}(\cdot,t)}
at least when
\|v_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}
is sufficiently small.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
(n,v,u,P)
be the global classical solution of (
1.1
) (
1.7
) (
1.8
) with
\mu>0
.
Then for any given
K>0
, one can pick
\delta=\delta(\mu,K)>0
such that whenever
\|v_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq K
and
\|v_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}<\delta
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{\overline{T\rightarrow\infty}}\frac{1}{T}\int^{T}_{1}\|n(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\geq\frac{|\Omega|}{3}.

(4.19)

Proof.

Multiplying the first equation in (
1.1
) by
n^{-1}
, an application of Lemma
2.2
, the Young inequality and
\nabla\cdot u=0
shows that

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}\ln n&=\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{n}(\Delta(n\phi(v))-u\cdot\nabla n)+\mu|\Omega|-\mu\int_{\Omega}n\\
&=\int_{\Omega}\frac{\nabla n}{n^{2}}(\phi(v)\nabla n+\phi^{\prime}(v)n\nabla v)+\mu|\Omega|-\mu\int_{\Omega}n\\
&\geq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\phi(v)}{n^{2}}|\nabla n|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\phi^{\prime}(v)|^{2}}{\phi(v)}|\nabla v|^{2}+\mu|\Omega|-\mu\int_{\Omega}n\\
&\geq-\frac{\Lambda^{2}(K)}{2\lambda(K)}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\nabla v|^{2}}{v}+\mu|\Omega|-\mu\int_{\Omega}n.\end{split}

(4.20)

On the other hand, revisiting the proof of (
4.14
), one can conclude that there exists
C_{1}>0
, which may be dependent on
K
but independent of
\|v_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}
, such that

\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\nabla v(\cdot,t)|^{4}}{v^{3}(\cdot,t)}\leq C_{1}\quad\quad\hbox{for all}\,\,t>0.

Therefore by the Hölder inequality, we get

\begin{split}\frac{\Lambda^{2}(K)}{2\lambda(K)}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\nabla v|^{2}}{v}&\leq\frac{\Lambda^{2}(K)}{2\lambda(K)}\left\{\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\nabla v|^{4}}{v^{3}}\right\}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\{\int_{\Omega}v\right\}^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
&\leq\frac{\Lambda^{2}(K)}{2\lambda(K)}C_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\{\int_{\Omega}v_{0}\right\}^{\frac{1}{2}}.\end{split}

(4.21)

Pick
\delta>0
sufficiently small such that

\frac{\Lambda^{2}(K)}{2\lambda(K)}C_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\frac{\mu|\Omega|}{2}.

Then from (
4.20
) and (
4.21
) it follows that

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}\ln n\geq\frac{\mu|\Omega|}{2}-\mu\int_{\Omega}n.\end{split}

(4.22)

For any
T>2
, integrating (
4.22
) over
(1,T)
, we readily obtain that

\begin{split}\frac{1}{T}\int^{T}_{1}\int_{\Omega}n&\geq\frac{|\Omega|}{2}-\frac{1}{T\mu}\int_{\Omega}\ln n(\cdot,T)+\frac{1}{T\mu}\int_{\Omega}\ln n(\cdot,1)\\
&\geq\frac{|\Omega|}{2}-\frac{1}{T\mu}\int_{\Omega}n(\cdot,T)+\frac{1}{T\mu}\int_{\Omega}\ln n(\cdot,1)\\
&\geq\frac{|\Omega|}{2}-\frac{1}{T\mu}\max\{\int_{\Omega}n_{0},|\Omega|\}+\frac{1}{T\mu}\int_{\Omega}\ln n(\cdot,1),\end{split}

(4.23)

where (
2.4
) and
\ln x<x
for
x>0
are used, and therefore get (
4.19
) immediately.
∎

Noting that
\|v(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}
decreases with respect to time
t>0
,
the lower bound of the time average value of
\|n(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}
expressed in (
4.19
) can inform us of
the decay properties of
\|v(\cdot,t)\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)}
.

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
K>0
and
\|v_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}<\delta
be given as in Lemma
4.7
. Then we have

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\|v(\cdot,t)\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)}=0.

(4.24)

Proof.

Multiplying the second equation in (
1.1
) by
v
, we get

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}v^{2}+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v|^{2}=-2\int_{\Omega}nv^{2}

and then

\int^{T}_{0}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v|^{2}+2\int^{T}_{0}\int_{\Omega}nv^{2}\leq\int_{\Omega}v_{0}^{2}

(4.25)

for all
T>0
.
Moreover, by the Poincaré inequality and Hölder’s inequality, we have

\begin{split}|\Omega|\int^{T}_{1}\int_{\Omega}\overline{n}\cdot\overline{v}=&\int^{T}_{0}\int_{\Omega}nv-\int^{T}_{0}\int_{\Omega}n(v-\overline{v})\\
\leq&\int_{\Omega}v_{0}+\int^{T}_{0}\|n\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|v-\overline{v}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\\
\leq&\int_{\Omega}v_{0}+C_{p}\sup_{t>0}\|n(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\int^{T}_{0}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\\
\leq&\int_{\Omega}v_{0}+C_{p}\sup_{t>0}\|n(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\left\{\int^{T}_{0}\|\nabla v\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right\}^{\frac{1}{2}}T^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
\leq&\int_{\Omega}v_{0}+C_{p}\sup_{t>0}\|n(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|v_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}T^{\frac{1}{2}},\\
\end{split}

(4.26)

which, together with the decreasing behavior of
\|v(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}
, leads us to

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}v(\cdot,T)\cdot\frac{1}{T}\int^{T}_{1}\int_{\Omega}n\leq\frac{|\Omega|}{T}\int_{\Omega}v_{0}+C_{p}|\Omega|\sup_{t>0}\|n(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|v_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}T^{-\frac{1}{2}}.\end{split}

(4.27)

Therefore combining (
4.27
) with (
4.23
), we obtain that

\begin{split}&\left\{\frac{|\Omega|}{2}-\frac{1}{T\mu}\max\{\int_{\Omega}n_{0},|\Omega|\}+\frac{1}{T\mu}\int_{\Omega}\ln n_{0}\right\}\int_{\Omega}v(\cdot,T)\\
\leq&\frac{|\Omega|}{T}\int_{\Omega}v_{0}+C_{p}|\Omega|\sup_{t>0}\|n(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|v_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}T^{-\frac{1}{2}}.\end{split}

(4.28)

Now since
\displaystyle\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}\{\frac{1}{T\mu}\max\{\int_{\Omega}n_{0},|\Omega|\}-\frac{1}{T\mu}\int_{\Omega}\ln n(\cdot,1)\}=0
, one can find
T_{1}>2
such that for all
T>T_{1}
, we have

\begin{split}\frac{|\Omega|}{4}\int_{\Omega}v(\cdot,T)\leq\frac{|\Omega|}{T}\int_{\Omega}v_{0}+C_{p}|\Omega|\sup_{t>0}\|n(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|v_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}T^{-\frac{1}{2}}\end{split}

and thus

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\|v(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}=0,

(4.29)

which can ensure that (
4.24
) holds by the interpolation inequality and the smoothing properties of the Neumann heat semigroup. Indeed,
thanks to the boundedness of
\|v(\cdot,t)\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)}
asserted in Lemma
4.5
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\|v(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}=0.

(4.30)

Since

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v(\cdot,t)|^{2}=\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\nabla v(\cdot,t)|^{2}}{v(\cdot,t)}\cdot\|v(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\quad\quad\hbox{for all}\,\,t>0,

the combination of (
4.30
) and (
4.4
) leads to

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\|\nabla v(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=0,

and thus for all
p>2

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\|\nabla v(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}=0

(4.31)

by the Hölder inequality and the boundedness of
\|v(\cdot,t)\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)}
. Moreover by
employing the smoothing properties of the Neumann heat semigroup (
[
2
, Lemma 2.1(iii)]
), we can obtain that

\displaystyle\|\nabla v(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{1}(1+(t-t_{1})^{-\frac{1}{4}})e^{-\lambda_{1}(t-t_{1})}\|\nabla v(\cdot,t_{1})\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)}+\int_{t_{1}}^{t}\|\nabla e^{(t-s)\Delta}\{n(\cdot,s)v(\cdot,s)\}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{t_{1}}^{t}\|\nabla e^{(t-s)\Delta}(u\cdot\nabla v)(\cdot,s)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}

(4.32)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{1}(1+(t-t_{1})^{-\frac{1}{4}})e^{-\lambda_{1}(t-t_{1})}\|\nabla v(\cdot,t_{1})\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)}+C_{1}\int_{t_{1}}^{t}(1+(t-s)^{-\frac{3}{4}})e^{-\lambda_{1}(t-s)}\|n(\cdot,s)v(\cdot,s)\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\quad+C_{1}\int_{t_{1}}^{t}(1+(t-s)^{-\frac{3}{4}})e^{-\lambda_{1}(t-s)}\|u(\cdot,s)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\|\nabla v(\cdot,s)\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{1}(1+(t-t_{1})^{-\frac{1}{4}})e^{-\lambda_{1}(t-t_{1})}\|\nabla v(\cdot,t_{1})\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)}+C_{2}\sup\limits_{t\geq t_{1}}(\|v(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{8}(\Omega)}+\|\nabla v(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)})

with
C_{2}:=C_{1}\int_{0}^{\infty}(1+\sigma^{-\frac{3}{4}})e^{-\lambda_{1}\sigma}d\sigma\cdot\sup\limits_{t\geq t_{1}}(\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega))}+\|n(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{8}(\Omega)})
, the finite of which is warranted by Lemma
4.5
and Lemma
4.4
.
Hence due to (
4.31
) and (
4.30
), we have

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\|\nabla v(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}=0,

(4.33)

and thereby arrive at (
4.24
).
∎

At this juncture, the decay feature of

Lemma 4.9
.

Let the assumption of Lemma 4.7 hold. Then we have

\displaystyle\lim_{\overline{t\rightarrow\infty}}\|n(\cdot,t)-1\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=0.

(4.34)

Proof.

By the Hölder inequality, we have

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\nabla v|^{2}}{v}&\leq\left\{\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\nabla v|^{4}}{v^{3}}\right\}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\{\int_{\Omega}v\right\}^{\frac{1}{2}}.\end{split}

It follows from (
4.14
) and (
4.24
) that

\begin{split}\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\nabla v(\cdot,t)|^{2}}{v(\cdot,t)}=0.\end{split}

(4.35)

On the other hand, testing the first equation in (
1.1
) by
1-n^{-1}
, we get

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}(n-1-\ln n)&=\int_{\Omega}(1-\frac{1}{n})(\Delta(n\phi(v))-u\cdot\nabla n)-\mu\int_{\Omega}(1-n)^{2}\\
&=-\int_{\Omega}\frac{\nabla n}{n^{2}}(\phi(v)\nabla n+\phi^{\prime}(v)n\nabla v)-\mu\int_{\Omega}(1-n)^{2}\\
&\leq-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\phi(v)}{n^{2}}|\nabla n|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\phi^{\prime}(v)|^{2}}{\phi(v)}|\nabla v|^{2}-\mu\int_{\Omega}(1-n)^{2}\\
&\leq\frac{\Lambda^{2}(K)}{2\lambda(K)}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\nabla v|^{2}}{v}-\mu\int_{\Omega}(1-n)^{2},\end{split}

by Lemma
2.2
, the Young inequality and
\nabla\cdot u=0
,
and hence

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}(n-1-\ln n)+\mu\int_{\Omega}(n-1)^{2}\leq\frac{\Lambda^{2}(K)}{2\lambda(K)}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\nabla v|^{2}}{v}.\end{split}

(4.36)

Thanks to the nonnegativity of the function
x-1-\ln x
for all
x>0
, integrating (
4.36
) over
(t_{1},t_{2})

leads to

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{t_{2}-t_{1}}\int^{t_{2}}_{t_{1}}\int_{\Omega}(n-1)^{2}\\
\leq&\frac{1}{\mu(t_{2}-t_{1})}\int_{\Omega}(n(\cdot,t_{1})-1-\ln n(\cdot,t_{1}))+\frac{1}{t_{2}-t_{1}}\frac{\Lambda^{2}(K)}{\mu\lambda(K)}\int^{t_{2}}_{t_{1}}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\nabla v|^{2}}{v}\\
\leq&\frac{1}{\mu(t_{2}-t_{1})}\int_{\Omega}(n(\cdot,t_{1})-1-\ln n(\cdot,t_{1}))+\frac{\Lambda^{2}(K)}{\mu\lambda(K)}\sup_{s\in(t_{1},t_{2})}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\nabla v(\cdot,s)|^{2}}{v(\cdot,s)}\\
\end{split}

(4.37)

for all
0<t_{1}<t_{2}
, Now, for any given
\varepsilon>0
, by (
4.35
), there exists
t_{3}>0
such that for all
t>t_{3}
,

\frac{\Lambda^{2}(K)}{\mu\lambda(K)}\sup_{t\geq t_{3}}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\nabla v(\cdot,t)|^{2}}{v(\cdot,t)}<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

Thereafter, one can pick
t_{4}>t_{3}+1
sufficiently large such that

\frac{1}{\mu(t_{4}-t_{3})}\int_{\Omega}(n(\cdot,t_{3})-1-\ln n(\cdot,t_{3}))<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

Hence from (
4.37
), we have

\begin{split}\frac{1}{t_{4}-t_{3}}\int^{t_{4}}_{t_{3}}\int_{\Omega}(n-1)^{2}<\varepsilon\end{split}

and then get
\tilde{t}_{3}\in(t_{3},t_{4})
such that

\int_{\Omega}(n(\cdot,\tilde{t}_{3})-1)^{2}<\varepsilon.

(4.38)

Proceeding as above, we can find a sequence
\{\tilde{t}_{k}\}_{k\geq 3}
fulfilling

\tilde{t}_{k}\rightarrow\infty
as
k\rightarrow\infty
and

\int_{\Omega}(n(\cdot,\tilde{t}_{k})-1)^{2}<\varepsilon,

which implies (
4.34
) and completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 4.10
.

Let the assumption of Lemma 4.7 hold. Then we have

\displaystyle\lim_{\overline{t\rightarrow\infty}}\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{W^{1,2}(\Omega)}=0.

(4.39)

Proof.

Testing the third equation in (
1.1
) against
u
, by
\nabla\cdot u=0
and

\int_{\Omega}u\cdot((u\cdot\nabla)u)=0
, we have

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}|u|^{2}+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}&=\int_{\Omega}(n-1)u\cdot\nabla\Phi\\
&\leq\|\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{\infty}}\|n-1\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.\end{split}

Due to
u|_{\partial\Omega}=0
, the Poincaré inequality implies the existence of some
C_{1}>0
such that

\int_{\Omega}|u|^{2}\leq C_{1}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}.

By the Young inequality, we have

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}|u|^{2}+C_{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}+C_{2}\int_{\Omega}|u|^{2}\leq C_{3}\|n-1\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\end{split}

(4.40)

for some
C_{2}>0
and
C_{3}>0
.

Integrating (
4.40
) over
(t_{1},t_{2})
and using (
4.37
), we arrive at

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{t_{2}-t_{1}}\int^{t_{2}}_{t_{1}}\|u(\cdot,s)\|^{2}_{W^{1,2}(\Omega)}ds\\
\leq&\frac{C_{4}}{t_{2}-t_{1}}\int_{\Omega}|u(\cdot,t_{1})|^{2}+\frac{C_{4}}{t_{2}-t_{1}}\int^{t_{2}}_{t_{1}}\int_{\Omega}(n-1)^{2}\\
\leq&\frac{C_{4}}{t_{2}-t_{1}}\int_{\Omega}|u(\cdot,t_{1})|^{2}+\frac{C_{5}}{t_{2}-t_{1}}\int_{\Omega}(n(\cdot,t_{1})-1-\ln n(\cdot,t_{1}))+C_{5}\sup_{s\geq t_{1}}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\nabla v(\cdot,s)|^{2}}{v(\cdot,s)}\end{split}

(4.41)

for some
C_{4}>0
and
C_{5}>0
.
Finally, thanks to (
4.35
), one can arrive at (
4.39
) by the argument in Lemma
4.9
.
∎

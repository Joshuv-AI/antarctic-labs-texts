Correlation between a 2-week change in platelet count and clinical outcomes after the initiation of ravulizumab treatment in adult patients with atypical hemolytic uremic syndrome: post-hoc analysis of the phase III trial
Authors: Masanori Matsumoto, Akihiko Shimono, Jun Yokosawa, Keiichiro Hirose, Edward Wang, Shoichi Maruyama
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BackgroundAtypical hemolytic uremic syndrome (aHUS) is a rare disease with poor outcomes when untreated, in which ravulizumab or eculizumab are the standard of care where available. It has been proposed to regularly monitor platelet counts as an early response to ravulizumab or eculizumab. This study aimed to investigate the association between the early response to ravulizumab treatment and renal outcomes through 26 weeks in complement inhibitor-naïve adults with aHUS.Methods Adult patients with aHUS enrolled in the ALXN1210-aHUS-311 phase III study of ravulizumab were divided into two groups according to the achievement of complete thrombotic microangiopathy (TMA) response, i.e., platelet count and lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) normalization and ≥ 25% improvement in serum creatinine (sCr) from baseline, by 26 weeks and baseline characteristics were compared. Changes in hematologic parameters, platelet count and LDH, were compared between the two groups. Finally, we examined whether early hematologic improvement was associated with renal recovery (dialysis discontinuation or ≥ 25% improvement in sCr from baseline) through 26 weeks.ResultsOf 56 ravulizumab-treated patients, 30 achieved complete TMA response for 26 weeks, and 26 did not. Patients with complete TMA response showed rapid improvements in platelet counts. In patients without complete TMA response, delayed normalization of platelet counts was observed. By day 15, 93.3% (28/30) of patients with complete TMA response at 26 weeks and 26.9% (7/26) of patients without complete TMA response achieved platelet normalization. At 26 weeks, 62.5% (35/56) achieved renal recovery; however, 37.5% (21/56) did not. In patients with renal recovery, 85.7% (30/35) of patients had platelet count normalization by day 15; in patients without renal recovery, 23.8% (5/21) of patients had platelet count normalization (P < 0.0001). Receiver operator characteristic curve analysis showed a moderate association between platelet counts on day 8/15 and renal recovery within 26 weeks (day 8: area under the curve [AUC] = 0.7985; day 15: AUC = 0.8406).ConclusionsPlatelet count normalization occurred in 62.5% (35/56) by day 15 after ravulizumab initiation and was associated with renal recovery through 26 weeks in complement inhibitor-naïve adults with aHUS.Trial registrationThis study was performed as a post-hoc analysis of the ALXN1210-aHUS-311 phase III clinical trial (NCT02949128, registered October 25, 2016).Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12959-024-00652-1.

Background

Atypical hemolytic uremic syndrome (aHUS) is a thrombotic microangiopathy (TMA) associated with dysregulation of the complement pathway [1, 2]. TMA is characterized by the triad of microangiopathic hemolytic anemia (MAHA), consumptive thrombocytopenia, and organ damage, typically acute kidney injury. In patients with TMA, aHUS is differentially diagnosed following the exclusion of other types of TMA: Shiga toxin-producing Escherichia coli-HUS (STEC-HUS), thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura (TTP), and secondary TMA caused by autoimmune diseases, drugs, infection, malignant tumors, metabolic disorders, or transplantation [3–5]. Although tests and biomarkers for STEC-HUS and TTP are available [6, 7], none can conclusively diagnose aHUS. Genetic abnormalities are found in approximately 40%–60% of patients with aHUS [1]. In individuals with complement gene variants, the penetrance of aHUS has been reported to be 20–50% [1, 2]. The incomplete penetrance is common to all aHUS-associated complement genes, which suggests that the presence of a pathogenic variant alone is not sufficient for the development of the disease; the presence of a triggering stimulus is often also necessary [1, 2].

Eculizumab prevents terminal complement overactivation by inhibiting the cleavage of C5 into C5a and C5b, and it was the first approved treatment for patients with aHUS [8–15]. Ravulizumab is a second-generation C5 complement inhibitor that differs from eculizumab because of four amino acid substitutions that prolong its half-life by four-fold and results in immediate, complete, and sustained terminal complement inhibition. Ravulizumab dosing in adults begins with an initial loading dose, which is followed 2 weeks later by a maintenance dosing interval of 8 weeks [16–18]. During the 26-week initial evaluation period of the ALXN1210-aHUS-311 phase III study (study 311) in adults, ravulizumab resolved complement-mediated TMA, and 53.6% (95% CI 39.6–67.5%) of patients showed complete TMA response, which was defined as platelet count normalization (≥ 150 × 109/L), lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) normalization (≤ 246 U/L), and ≥ 25% improvement in serum creatinine (sCr) from baseline (excluding values obtained during dialysis) [16–18].

Ravulizumab has been approved since 2020 in Japan. The Japanese package insert for eculizumab and ravulizumab states that the platelet count should be monitored regularly to determine response to treatment, and if no improvement trend is observed, physicians should consider whether or not to continue the treatment. However, there was no specific description regarding patient characteristics, or the timing and optimal value of platelet counts to determine the response to treatment [17]. Therefore, we performed post-hoc analyses of study 311 data to compare patient characteristics and the response to ravulizumab treatment according to whether or not they achieved complete TMA response and to examine the correlation between platelet counts and renal recovery (dialysis discontinuation or ≥ 25% improvement in sCr from baseline) through 26 weeks of ravulizumab treatment.

Methods

Study design, patient eligibility, and ravulizumab treatment

Briefly, study 311 was a global single-arm, phase III study in which complement inhibitor-naïve adults who satisfied the diagnostic criteria for aHUS were treated with ravulizumab for 26 weeks in the initial treatment period, with an optional extension for up to 4.5 years; the design of the study, including trial oversight and ethics, has been previously described in more detail [16, 19].

Patients aged ≥ 18 years were eligible for the study if they had a body weight of ≥ 40 kg and had evidence of active TMA; patients with TTP or STEC-HUS were excluded according to the diagnostic flow chart for aHUS [7]. Patients were also required to meet the platelet count (< 150 × 109/L) and LDH (≥ 1.5 × upper limit of normal [ULN]) criteria (determined at local laboratories) as well as the sCr (≥ ULN) criterion (confirmed by the central laboratory).

Ravulizumab was administered via intravenous infusion with a weight-based dosing regimen [17]. For patients weighing ≥ 40 to < 60 kg, the loading and maintenance doses were 2400 and 3000 mg, respectively. For patients weighing ≥ 60 to < 100 kg, the loading and maintenance doses were 2700 and 3300 mg, respectively. For patients weighing ≥ 100 kg, the loading and maintenance doses were 3000 and 3600 mg, respectively. The loading dose was administered on day 1 and the maintenance dose was administered on day 15 and then every 8 weeks. In accordance with the study protocol, patients were required to have received meningococcal vaccination at the time of initiation of ravulizumab treatment and to take prophylactic antibiotics from the first dose of ravulizumab until at least 2 weeks after vaccination.

Study assessments of platelet count, LDH, and sCr were used to assess complete TMA response, which was defined as platelet count normalization (≥ 150 × 109/L), LDH normalization (≤ 246 U/L), and ≥ 25% improvement in sCr from baseline (excluding values obtained during dialysis). To meet the endpoint, all three outcomes needed to occur concurrently and on two separate assessments ≥ 28 days apart (including any measurements between the assessments). In addition to the endpoints of clinical trial [16], renal recovery was defined by ≥ 25% improvement in sCr from baseline or dialysis discontinuation.

Platelet counts determined within 3 days after transfusion, and sCr values obtained during dialysis were excluded from the analyses. For patients on dialysis at baseline, the sCr value measured ≥ 6 days after dialysis was used as the baseline. Patients on dialysis throughout the 26-week period were excluded from the analysis of improvement in renal function.

Data analyses

Patients were divided into two groups according to whether or not they achieved complete TMA response during the 26-week initial treatment period. Demographic and baseline characteristics were summarized for each group and compared between the two groups using Fisher’s exact test for categorical variables and the Wilcoxon rank sum test for continuous variables. The platelet counts during ravulizumab treatment were summarized in a box-and-whisker diagram for each group, and inter-group comparisons were conducted using a t-test at each visit. Patient outcomes including hematologic normalization and sCr improvement as well as renal recovery were summarized for complete TMA response in a table. The numbers of patients with/without normalization of platelet counts and with/without renal recovery during the 26-week initial evaluation period were summarized in a 2 × 2 contingency table, and comparisons were made using Fisher’s exact test. Moreover, to evaluate the association between platelet counts and renal recovery, we conducted receiver operator characteristic (ROC) curve analysis. This method plots sensitivity (true positive rate) on the y-axis against 1−specificity (false positive rate) on the x-axis. Patient characteristics were analyzed as described in the primary analysis by Rondeau et al. [16].

Results

Patients

Fifty-six patients were included in the efficacy analysis population [16]. Complete TMA response was achieved during the 26-week initial evaluation period by 30 patients (53.6%) and was not achieved by 26 patients (46.4%). The characteristics of both groups of patients are presented in Table 1 (also see Supplementary Table S1). Patients who achieved complete TMA response were significantly younger than those who did not, with median ages at first infusion of 31.7 and 47.6 years, respectively. The platelet counts and LDH levels at baseline were slightly higher in patients who achieved complete TMA response; however, the differences were not statistically significant. The estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) and hemoglobin levels were similar in both groups. Approximately half of the patients (n = 29) were on dialysis at baseline, with similar numbers in both groups (46.7% vs. 57.7%; P = 0.436). Genetic variants in complement genes were found with similar frequency in patients who met (25.0%; 7/28) and did not meet (29.4%; 5/17) complete TMA response in the original clinical trial (Supplementary Table S1) [19]. Changes in platelet count and LDH levels

The difference in platelet counts between the patients who did and did not meet complete TMA response through 26 weeks was significant from day 8 and was maintained over the observed period (Fig. 1a). In patients with 26-week complete TMA response, the platelet counts rapidly increased to day 8 and were maintained at levels above baseline from day 15. In patients who did not achieve complete TMA response, a relatively slower increase in platelet count was observed over time. Notably, the platelet counts in this group did not rapidly increase after the first dose of ravulizumab. Among the 30 patients who met the complete TMA response criteria by 26 weeks, 8 (26.7%) had platelet counts of ≥ 150 × 109/L and 22 (73.3%) had platelet counts of < 150 × 109/L at baseline, all of whom met the criteria of platelet count normalization (≥ 150 × 109/L each on two separate assessments ≥ 28 days apart) by 26 weeks. Among 26 patients who did not meet the complete TMA response criteria during 26 weeks, 4 (15.4%) had platelet counts of ≥ 150 × 109/L, and 22 (84.6%) had platelet counts of < 150 × 109/L at baseline. At baseline, 11 out of 12 patients who had platelet counts of ≥ 150 × 109/L (7 who met complete TMA response and 4 who did not) were treated with plasma therapy before ravulizumab treatment (Supplementary Table S1). Relationship between hematologic normalization by day 15 and renal recovery within 26 weeks

In terms of renal function improvement, 33 of all 56 patients (58.9%) met the criteria for sCr improvement during 26 weeks. In addition, among 29 patients who required dialysis at baseline, 17 (58.6%; 17/29) discontinued dialysis by 26 weeks [16]. Thirty-five (62.5%; 35/56) patients achieved sCr improvement or dialysis discontinuation (renal recovery) by 26 weeks of ravulizumab treatment (30 patients who met complete TMA response and 5 of 26 patients who did not meet complete TMA response).

Among the 35 patients who achieved renal recovery during ravulizumab treatment for 26 weeks, 25 (71.4%) met platelet count normalization (i.e., ≥ 150 × 109/L) on day 8; the other 10 (28.6%) did not meet platelet normalization (Table 3). On day 15, 30 (85.7%) met platelet count normalization; 5 (14.3%) did not meet platelet normalization. ROC curve analysis of platelet counts on day 15 for achieving renal recovery

ROC curve analysis was performed to examine the association between platelet counts during the 2 weeks after ravulizumab initiation and achieving renal recovery within 26 weeks. On day 8, the cut-off value derived from the ROC curve analysis was 157 × 109/L (Supplementary Figure S1). The sensitivity, specificity, and area under the curve were 80.00%, 68.42%, and 0.7985, respectively. On day 15, the cut-off value derived from the ROC curve analysis was 181 × 109/L. The sensitivity, specificity, and area under the curve were 82.86%, 84.21%, and 0.8406, respectively (Fig. 3). ROC curve analysis was also performed to examine the association between LDH levels during the 2 weeks after ravulizumab initiation and achieving renal recovery during 26 weeks. As shown in Supplementary Figure S2a, b, a low association (cut-off value: 382; sensitivity: 68.57%; specificity: 57.89%; area under the curve: 0.615) for LDH levels on day 8 and a moderate association (cut-off value: 337; sensitivity: 64.71%; specificity: 75.00%; area under the curve: 0.7235) on day 15 was observed with regard to renal recovery during 26 weeks.

Discussion

We performed post-hoc analyses of data from study 311 to investigate whether early hematological responses after ravulizumab initiation were associated with subsequent achievement of complete TMA response and renal recovery defined by dialysis discontinuation or ≥ 25% improvement in sCr from baseline during ravulizumab treatment in adult patients with aHUS. Although renal recovery in aHUS can often take much longer than the 26-week period analyzed in this study, our findings suggest that normalization of the platelet count to ≥ 150 × 109/L on days 8 and 15 of ravulizumab treatment was associated with achievement of renal recovery within the 26-week initial treatment period.

Among the components of the complete TMA response, platelet count is the parameter that changes promptly after the initiation of ravulizumab treatment [9]. Day 15 might be a clinically relevant timepoint to see the response for several reasons. Importantly, this timepoint corresponds to the time of the first maintenance dose of ravulizumab. Furthermore, this timepoint is relevant to situations where platelet counts could be affected by natural platelet turnover. For instance, most (82.8%) of the patients enrolled in study 311 had undergone plasma exchange or plasma infusion before ravulizumab treatment [16], which could affect platelet counts. Plasma exchange/infusion was not allowed after initiating ravulizumab treatment. In addition, platelet counts obtained on the day of platelet transfusion up to 3 days after the transfusion were excluded from all analyses. In these circumstances, which could be observed in real-world situations, we may expect that by day 15, the platelet count would be recovered with platelets produced by the patient due to the natural turnover and half-life of platelets. At baseline (after the screening period), 12 patients (21.4%, 8 who met complete TMA response and 4 who did not) met the normal platelet count criterion (≥ 150 × 109/L). However, to meet complete TMA response, the platelet counts were required to be at normal levels on two separate assessments ≥ 28 days apart.

In terms of improvement of renal function, 58.6% (17/29) of patients discontinued dialysis during ravulizumab treatment within the 26-week initial evaluation period [16]. Among patients who did not meet complete TMA response, 5 patients accomplished sCr improvement or dialysis discontinuation. Renal recovery defined by sCr improvement or dialysis discontinuation in this study is a relevant clinical outcome in patients with aHUS; improvement of renal function was observed with long-term continuation of ravulizumab treatment [16, 19, 20].

This study demonstrated that the platelet response to ravulizumab in the early treatment period was associated with achievement of renal recovery within 26 weeks in patients with aHUS. However, some patients who achieved hematologic normalization might not achieve renal recovery during 26 weeks due to irreversible renal damage at baseline. Early treatment of patients with aHUS before they develop irreversible renal damage could increase the association between renal recovery and normalization of platelet counts. Previously published data from a clinical trial of eculizumab in aHUS demonstrated that the mean eGFR change from baseline to 1 year was significantly greater in patients who started treatment within 7 days of the last aHUS manifestation than in those initiating treatment more than 7 days later, and 81% and 47% of patients in the ≤ 7- and > 7-day groups, respectively, achieved a sustained increase in eGFR after 1 year [21]. Real-world evidence of eculizumab has also shown that the time from the onset of TMA to treatment with eculizumab was inversely associated with sCr improvement [13]. Therefore, to monitor platelet counts as an early response related to renal recovery in the later stages of treatment, it is essential to start early treatment with ravulizumab before irreversible renal damage occurs, and some patients might need long-term treatment with ravulizumab in order to achieve either complete TMA response or renal recovery [16, 19, 20].

Notably, among 35 patients who achieved renal recovery within 26 weeks of treatment, 4 had platelet counts of < 150 × 109/L on day 15. In those patients, platelet normalization was observed within 26 weeks. Thus, other clinical parameters, in addition to platelet count, should be carefully monitored during ravulizumab treatment.

Among patients without complete TMA response, 42.3% of patients met hematologic normalization and 5 patients (19.2%) achieved renal recovery by the end of observation. In those patients who did not achieve complete TMA response, a delay in the improvement of platelet counts was observed. Although there was no significant difference of disease characteristics, the possibility that patients with severe TMA might take longer to recover their platelet count than those with mild TMA cannot be excluded. Previous studies have suggested that chronic kidney disease can affect the platelet count and function [22] and that dialysis might decrease platelet counts [23]. In patients who did not achieve complete TMA response in our analysis, 46.2% (12/26) stayed on dialysis during the follow-up period, and only 19.2% (5/26) showed renal recovery. Therefore, slow recovery of platelet counts could be expected in patients depending on their clinical condition, i.e., severe renal impairment leading to chronic kidney disease and the requirement for dialysis. Otherwise, in patients without severe renal damage, short-term monitoring of platelet counts might be helpful to estimate the long-term response to treatment.

The main limitations of this study are described in the primary analysis of the 311 study [16]. For example, genetic testing was not performed in all patients, which could limit the interpretation of the results according to the presence or absence of pathogenic complement variants. Kidney biopsy data were not obtained in the clinical trial. In addition, the time from aHUS onset to treatment with ravulizumab or eculizumab was not available for analysis, which might affect renal recovery as shown in previous studies [13, 21]. Further, due to the small sample size, the study was not specifically powered for the analyses reported here.

Conclusions

Platelet count normalization by day 15 occurred in 62.5% (35/56) of patients after ravulizumab initiation and was associated with renal recovery by 26 weeks. The monitoring of platelet counts during the early treatment period might be helpful to estimate the long-term response in complement inhibitor-naïve adults with aHUS.

One-argument Lie scaling and sensitivity analysis of UCM liquid flow over a vertical wedge using response surface method
Authors: Musharafa Saleem, Imran Siddique, Mohamed Abubakar Fiidow, Rifaqat Ali, Bushra Shakoor, Barno Abdullaeva, Rana Muhammad Zulqarnain
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Wedge geometries are widely used in aerodynamics, heat exchangers, solar collectors, and chemical processing equipment. This study focuses on the combined impacts of thermal radiation, latent heat of fusion, Hiemenz flow, Joule heating, and electro-magnetohydrodynamic (EMHD) flows on the natural convection flow over a vertical wedge, which has not been examined previously. Due to the wide range of engineering applications involving wedge geometries, it is important to consider and discuss the effects of different thermophysical properties on the flow and heat transfer behavior. We examined upper-convected Maxwell (UCM) viscoelastic liquid flow in a permeable medium with phase change (melting effects). A one-argument scaling method has been introduced for transforming the developed equations of the liquid flow model into non-linear ordinary differential equations (ODEs). The numerical solution of the ODEs with boundary conditions is explored using the BVP4C technique, and the results are shown in graphs and analyzed. The velocity of the fluid increases with the magnetic field, porosity, electric field, Deborah number, suction, melting heat, and Hiemenz flow, while it decreases with higher Prandtl number. Similarly, the temperature distribution is enhanced by porosity, Prandtl number, melting heat, Hiemenz flow, as well as magnetic and electric field effects. The sensitivity analysis reveals that the melting heat parameter significantly enhances the Nusselt number (≈ 18.98%), establishing it as the most dominant factor in thermal energy transmission, while suction contributes moderately with a stable variation of ≈ 19.57%. In contrast, the Hiemenz parameter exhibits a negligible negative influence (nearly 200 times smaller), confirming its minimal role in affecting heat transfer performance.

Introduction

In the last several decades, non-Newtonian fluids have gained considerable interest as the behavior of such fluids is rather complicated, and the scope of industrial and technological use is also very broad. Their rheological behaviors cannot be explained by a single linear relationship between strain rate and stress, and hence several mathematical models have been proposed as being able to explain their behaviors. The UCM model is one of the most relevant and most researched viscoelastic models. The most crucial aspect of the UCM model is that it can achieve the elastic effects of non-Newtonian fluids that are essential, using the relaxation time concept, with a comparatively simple mathematical form compared to most other sophisticated models. In contrast to purely viscous models, the UCM model is effective in capturing the effects of memory and stress relaxation, which is important in processes where rapid deformation or stretching occurs. Owing to such characteristics, UCM fluids have found specific applications in areas like polymer processing, food manufacturing, drug delivery systems and thermal enhancement technologies. The fact that they can capture the effects of viscoelastic behavior renders them more appropriate compared to most other non-Newtonian models, where the elastic contribution is a leading factor. Over the last few years, scholars have delved into various issues of UCM fluid flows when physical conditions are varied. Reddy et al.1 examined the effect of an inclined magnetic field on thermal and mass transfer properties of turbulent squeeze flow of UCM fluid. Their results showed that there were observable differences in thermal conductivity in the inclination of the magnetic field. The study by Zeb et al.2 involves the evaluation of the influence of thermal stratification on the MHD stagnation-point flow of a UCM fluid over a porous surface. The experiment brought out great variations in the thermal behaviour in the heat generation and absorption processes. The study by Rahaman et al.3 focused on the induced movement of UCM fluid in a long oscillating cylinder in longitudinal and torsional vibrations. They found that the condition of boundary slips highly influence the flow characteristics. Salawu et al.4 examined the effect of radiation and chemical reaction on a magnetized UCM stagnation point with a particle-laden flow. Their work was useful in the understanding of microfluidic and biomedical uses. Asmadi et al.5 presented the transport of thermal energy in MHD UCM fluid flow over a horizontal wedge with the Cattaneo-Christov heat flux model and focused on non-Fourier heat conduction effects. Abu-Nab et al.6 studied the dynamics of microcavitation and the formation of ternary hybrid nanofluids, which contributes to the knowledge of advanced nanoliquid systems. Das et al.7 examined how the concept of double diffusion affects the thermal transport ability of quadra hybrid nanofluids, especially in engine oil. Moreover, Muhammad et al.8 have written an MHD UCM fluid flow model based on the spectral relaxation method, taking into consideration the effect of the double diffusion. Khan et al.9 suggested a model of the magnetized stagnation-point flow of a UCM nanofluid with slip boundary conditions and deal of thermal dispersion. Moreover, a computational work of the MHD Maxwell hybrid nanofluid flow was done by Alkasasbeh10, where the authors emphasized the significance of the double dispersion phenomenon.

EMHD can be understood as a specialized extension of magnetohydrodynamics (MHD). While classical MHD studies the interaction between magnetic fields and the overall motion of electrically conducting fluids (including both ions and electrons), EMHD focuses specifically on the dynamics of the electron fluid, if the ions remain nearly stationary. The fundamental principles of MHD are governed by the conservation of mass, momentum, and energy, coupled with simplified (low frequency) forms of Maxwell’s equations to describe magnetic field transport. Once the basic theory is understood, the practical significance of MHD becomes quite evident from its broad range of applications. In both science and engineering, MHD is widely used to study plasma behavior, astrophysical processes, controlled nuclear fusion, and various aspects of space research. It also plays an important role in industry, particularly in controlling fluid motion in cooling systems, metallurgical processes, and energy conversion technologies. EMHD, on the other hand, is especially relevant in situations involving strong electromagnetic fields and small-scale effects. It finds applications in pulsed plasma devices, fusion-related studies, astrophysical modeling, and even solid-state physics. Because EMHD can describe electromagnetic interactions at a finer scale, it has become an important tool in modern plasma research and advanced technological developments. Among the earlier contributions in this area, Pantokratoras and Magyari11 examined the EMHD boundary-layer flow over a Riga plate. Their work showed that electromagnetic forcing can be used to control free convection and significantly alter the structure of the boundary layer. This work laid a strong theoretical foundation for understanding EMHD-driven flows in thermal systems. Buren and Jian12 applied EMHD analysis to microscale configurations by considering flow in between two micro-parallel plates that are transversely wavy. Their findings demonstrated the relevance of electromagnetic actuation to microfluidic devices and demonstrated the ability of EMHD to control flow properties within confined geometries, which is highly applicable in lab-on-chip and biomedical technologies. Prakash et al.13 also continued the work by evaluating the EMHD non-Newtonian ternary hybrid nanofluid flow over a wedge embedded in Darcy-Forchheimer porous medium. Using an extension of the EMHD theory through the addition of effects like the zeta potential and the wall suction/injection, they have included effects that have given them a complete computational model that links the EMHD theory with porous media transport and nanofluid engineering. Their experiment revealed that electromagnetic forces, porous resistance, and nanoparticle dynamics are all strongly coupled. Bharathi and Prakash14 examined the synergistic effect of the zeta potential and activation energy on the EMHD non-Newtonian nanofluid flow in a wedge within a porous medium in the Darcy-Forchheimer regime. Their effort focused on the effect of the electrokinetic and chemical reaction in the alteration of the velocity, temperature, and concentration distributions to increase the scope of EMHD applications to reactive and energy-sensitive systems. In much more recent work, Daniel et al.15 examined unsteady EMHD mixed convective flow of a dual stratified nanofluid including radiative heat transfer and Ohmic effects. Their results also emphasised the importance of thermal radiation and Joule heating in the betterment or regulation of heat transfer performance, especially in the areas of high temperatures and energy.

A wedge is a solid body formed by two plane surfaces that meet at an angle, creating a sharp edge at the front. In fluid mechanics, a wedge-shaped surface is often used to study boundary-layer flow because geometry produces a pressure gradient along the surface. When a wedge is placed vertically and its surface is stretched along the length, the stretching motion of the surface drags the nearby fluid and forms a boundary layer along the wedge. The wedge geometry controls the external velocity distribution, while the stretching of the surface supplies additional momentum to the fluid. If the wedge is vertical, gravity may also influence the flow, so buoyancy can affect the velocity and temperature fields. When a wedge-shaped surface is placed in a flowing fluid and starts melting, the melted material mixes into the fluid and affects the flow near the surface. This melting changes the temperature distribution and can make the layer of heated fluid thicker or thinner depending on how fast the melting happens. It also slightly changes the speed of the fluid close to the wedge. In short, melting heat effects describe how the heat from the fluid causes the wedge to melt and how this melting influences both the fluid flow and the transfer of heat near the surface. When Budhia and Kreith16 investigated the effect of melting or freezing at a wedge surface on heat transfer, they found that the phase change has a strong influence on the thermal boundary layer and heat flux. Kuo17 used the differential transformation technique in solving the Falkner-Skan wedge flow, which offered an effective mathematical technique in predicting velocity and temperature fields. Sarkar and Endalew18 studied the influence of the melting on the flow of Casson nanofluid in a porous medium under the influence of a magnetic field, including the role of the phase change and non-Newtonian flow in velocity and temperature distributions. Chakraborty and Janapatla19 studied the flow of a Casson nanofluid in a wedge at conditions of varying magnetic fields, thermal radiation, and melting, and demonstrated their effect on heat transfer and the thickness of the boundary layer. The analysis of radiative hybrid nanofluid flow over a melting wedge heat transfer by Hafeez, Liu and Khalid20 focused on the uses in which melting has a significant impact on the behaviour of the thermal and fluid field.

Joule heating happens when an electric current passes through a fluid or material and produces heat, like how a wire gets warm when electricity flows. Thermal radiation is the heat that travels in the form of electromagnetic waves, so even without touching, hot objects can warm their surroundings. Heat sources and sinks are ways to add or remove heat from a system—like a heater adding warmth or a cooling fan taking heat away. Together, these effects describe different ways energy can enter or leave a fluid, changing its temperature and flow behavior. Merging the complexities of the porous-medium, defined by the Darcy and Brinkman resistive forces into modeling flow with UCM liquids, are crucial to the explanation of transport phenomena related to oil recovery, filtration, and subsurface flows. This merger thus leads to problems of formulation of governing equations and solutions. A genuinely robust methodology, analytical, and numerical becomes needed for revealing the interaction of the viscoelastic stresses with the permeability-induced resistance of the medium. Choudhary et al.21 proposed a mass-based hybrid nanoliquid framework for investigating MHD flow over a wedge embedded in a porous medium. Attention has been paid to the prime role of thermal radiation in modulating the characteristics of thermal energy transmission in the hybrid nanoliquid system. Various industrial processes involving the transport of NNFs in porous media include solutions of shear-thinning biopolymers, which find numerous applications in petroleum geology for improving petroleum recovery, mainly for their capacity to reduce displacement front spreading uncertainty and predictability and, therefore, recovery rate and accuracy. Current approaches for efficient ground environmental clean-up are exploring the use of shear-thinning polymer interruptions as a means of transporting iron nano-particles inside ground environments, as presented by Vecchia et al.22, Zhong et al.23, and Tosco et al.24. Modeling the pressure drop developed during non-Newtonian fluid transport in a porous medium is a core concern in each of the above applications. This problem has been dealt with comprehensively through detailed expressions of Darcy’s equation as advanced by Pearson & Tardy25 or through similarity solutions for transient simulations as offered by Ciriello, Di Federico26. In micro-scale simulation, the relevance of both pore network simulations and realistic pore-scale simulations is shown in Blunt’s work27. In this regard, further hybrid simulations involving both pore and micro-scale simulations are also shown in the work of Battiato et al.28. Sohail et al.29 analyzed entropy generation in a Darcy–Forchheimer squeezed hybrid nanofluid flow between rotating disks, showing how thermal radiation and viscous dissipation affect energy losses. Waseem et al.30 investigated 3D stretched magnetized hybrid nanofluid flow by taking note of the synergistic effect of the thermal radiation and heat generation on thermal efficiency. Waseem et al.31 applied the best homotopy asymptotic technique to investigate the process of entropy generation in hybrid nanofluids that have temperature-dependent thermal conductivity under radiation, that offer information on improved thermal management. In the study by Sohail et al.32, the Maxwell nanofluid bio-convective boundary layer flow over a stretched sheet was investigated, and it was found that the effects of radiation and Darcy-Forchheimer begin to play roles in the flow and heat transfer. Sohail et al.33 discussed the three-dimensional stretched Casson nanofluid flow in a rotating frame under bio-convection, indicating how it influences velocity and temperature distributions. Sohail et al.34 studied the mass and heat transport of bi- directional stretched Sisko fluid flow with variable magnetic fields and radiation, paying special attention to the interplay between magnetic and radiative and non-Newtonian effects. Sohail and Abbas35 examined the flow of non-Newtonian Prandtl fluid at a constant Reynolds number but with a diffusion coefficient and thermal conductivity that depend on the flow’s position, and the impact of altered heat and mass fluxes on the thermal and concentration distributions appear. Swain et al.36 examined bio-convectional flow of Williamson nanofluid over an expanding surface and revealed the effect of activating energy on the fluid movement and heat transfer. Swain et al.37 investigated the impact of dissipative heat on non-Newtonian Walter B nanoliquid on an elongating surface using a permeable medium, and the overall influence of internal heat generation and fluid characteristics on thermal conductivity. Swain et al.38 examined a bio-thermal reactor using nanofluid in the decomposition of organic waste in 3D flow in parallel channels, which focuses on the importance of non-uniform heat sources and chemical reactions to improve efficiency and temperature control.

Various solution methodologies have been developed, addressing this challenge and others, toward solving DEs, which come in two broad categories: linear and nonlinear. Of these methods, Lie group theory has emerged as one of the most powerful analytical tools for nonlinear PDEs. From its original development by Sophus Lie, this method seeks the invariance properties of DEs that exhibit continuous symmetries under one-argument Lie group transformations. The approach has widely been used in studying liquid dynamics problems with different physical constraints. For example, Kandasamy et al.39 used Lie group transformations to study electrically conducting, impermeable, steady flow over a vertically stretchable sheet. Similarly, Nabwey et al.40 analyzed mixed convection stagnation point flow of non-Newtonian nanoliquids over an extensible vertical surface, which remains an important topic in nanoliquid research. Moreover, Nabwey and El-Mkny41 investigated the thermophoretic transport through vertical surfaces embedded in porous media using the concept of Lie symmetry transformations, particularly focusing on the migration of particles and the transfer of thermal energy. In another work, the authors42 replicated the solution for natural convection flow of nanoliquids through porous media over chemically reactive horizontal surfaces using Lie group transformation. Further, the authors Ullah and Zaman43 explored MHD slip flows for the hyperbolic tangent law over extensible surfaces via the theories of Lie transformation groups. The mixed convection and multi-physical interactions between symmetry-based analysis of three-dimensional EMHD nano-Carreau fluid flow over a Riga-stretched surface by Saleem et al.44 revealed that the velocity and temperature distributions in the flow are affected by these factors. Saleem and Faheem45 compared the combined effects of melting heat on aligned MHD Williamson nanofluid with microorganisms in the presence of the leading edge, and the differences in the effects phase change and bio-convection have on the flow and thermal behavior were illustrated. Chakraborty and Janapatla46 examined the flow of MHD nanoliquid over a vertical wedge with double dispersion and Dufour effects and examined the effects of the two means of transport on the boundary layer heat and mass transfer.

Optimization is concerned with the search for the optimal solution to a problem, e.g. performance, efficiency or cost, and it is usually characterized by interrelating variables. RSM can be useful because it enables the development of an understandable representation of the impact of these variables on the outcome and identifies the most desirable combination without trying all of them. The typical steps of RSM are: (1) selecting the important variables and defining their ranges, (2) designing experiments to systematically explore the combinations, (3) performing the experiments or simulations and collecting data, (4) fitting a mathematical model, usually a polynomial, to describe the relationship between inputs and outputs, often verified using ANOVA (Analysis of Variance) to ensure the model is statistically significant, and (5) analyzing the model to find the optimum conditions. In most cases, an R-squared value approaching 95% or above is considered an indication of a well-fitted model, reflecting a strong agreement between predicted and observed results. Alongside this, sensitivity analysis plays an important role by examining how variations in input parameters influence the outcome. In this way, it helps identify which variables have the most significant impact on the system. When used together, RSM and sensitivity analysis provide a clearer understanding of the underlying behavior of the model, allowing researchers to focus on the most influential factors and make better design and optimization decisions. Several recent studies have effectively used these approaches in the context of nanofluid flow and heat transfer. For example, Panda et al.47 employed RSM to optimize heat transfer in a three-dimensional micropolar nanoliquid flow over a cylindrical surface, considering magnetic effects, viscous dissipation, and thermal radiation. Similarly, Baithalu et al.48 investigated the role of nanoparticle shape in micropolar hybrid nanoliquid flow over a vertical plate and determined the optimal thermal energy transmission rate using RSM. Further contributions by Sarfraz and Khan49, Muhammad et al.50, and Senbagaraja and De51 provide detailed insights into the combined use of sensitivity analysis and RSM, particularly in optimizing thermal systems and understanding the influence of multiple physical parameters under complex flow conditions.

Motivation and novelty:

This study uniquely combines thermal radiation, melting heat, Joule heating, and EMHD effects in the analysis of UCM liquid flow over a vertical wedge, an area not previously52,53 explored in this configuration. The study also considers wedge flow to analyze how the wedge angle and pressure-gradient parameter affect the UCM fluid behavior under thermal and EMHD effects. It introduces a novel integration of RSM with sensitivity analysis to optimize thermal energy transmission and assess the influence of key arguments. The application of a one-argument scaling transformation to handle complex nonlinear governing equations in a multi-physical environment adds further originality. Additionally, the study provides new insights into the effect melting conditions on viscoelastic liquid behavior, offering practical relevance for thermal system design and optimization.

The model includes the effects of thermal radiation, melting heat, Joule heating, and EMHD, subject to a no-slip boundary condition at the wedge surface. One-argument Lie group scaling and similarity transformations

The one-parameter Lie scaling method plays a crucial role in simplifying and analyzing complex fluid flow problems governed by nonlinear PDEs. In many boundary layer models, such as wedge flow with melting, electromagnetic effects, and non-Newtonian behavior, the governing equations are highly coupled and difficult to solve directly The Lie scaling methodology presents a single parameter continuous symmetry transformation40–45 and admits a reduction procedure of the initial PDEs into a collection of ODEs with suitable similarity variables. This simplifies the mathematical complexity of the problem but still captures the critical physics of the problem. This method is important in that it allows the identification of the hidden invariance properties of the governing equations, which makes the transformed system physically consistent. It offers a methodology and a strict method to obtain similarity solutions as opposed to making arbitrary assumptions. Practically, this simplification allows effective numerical calculation to be performed with tools such as the bvp4c solver of MATLAB, so we can analyse how several physical factors, such as magnetic field, melting, suction/injection and thermal radiation, affect the flow behavior. The stored elastic energy is released into the flow, reducing viscous resistance and promoting momentum transfer, thereby increasing the velocity distribution. In contrast, the Prandtl number exhibits an inverse relationship with the velocity profile. Larger values of correspond to lower thermal diffusivity, which enhances the dominance of viscous effects within the boundary layer. Consequently, the fluid motion is slightly suppressed, leading to a decrease in the velocity profile.

Surface thermal energy transmission Optimization: RSM ~ CCD model

The utilization of RSM with Central Composite Design (CCD) provides an effective methodology for the optimization of the thermal transport process by examining the interaction effects and influence of varied levels of several input factors simultaneously. Within the context of three variable input factors, CCD provides a sound experimental design for rigorous system investigation with the inclusion of factorial, axial, and center points, allowing for the comprehensive examination of the design space with a minimal number of experimental runs. This pivotal design approach increases the efficiency of experimental data gathering with consistent analytical in-depth evaluation47–51. RSM applies experimental results to formulate the second-order polynomial equation to determine the linear, interaction, and quadratic influences, thus facilitating the accurate mathematical modeling of the system responses with intensive accuracy. This approach not only helps to determine the significant factors that influence thermal energy transport efficiency but also helps to examine the mathematical relationships among these factors with lucid representation through the utilization of contour plots and three-dimensional surfaces. The complementarity of RSM and CCD provides a systematic, statistically refined method that has the potential to enhance the performance of a system by minimizing reliance solely on a trial-and-error technique. The complementarity of RSM and CCD is well recognized for its ability to provide a reliable method for evaluating the effects of various factors simultaneously, providing a coherent prediction tool for optimization. Model validity

Improvement of the efficiency of the predictive model may, however, require the removal of non-significant variables. The backward elimination technique can be utilized for the removal of statistically non-significant variables to enhance the model by reducing the complexity of the model without changing the basic model and its interpretation. The efficiency of the proposed optimized model can then be tested using the coefficient and ANOVA tables to make a comparative estimation of improvement of the model by comparing the optimized model and the initial model. The summary of the results of the ANOVA table, which plays a pivotal part in the concepts of RSM, CCD, and DOE, can be found in Table 3. The principal role of the application of the ANOVA table is to determine the significance of the calculated regression coefficient, as presented in Table 4, to justify and ensure the efficiency and accuracy of the formulated model of the response. The terms presented in the regression table having p-standards below 0.05 are recognized as statistically important parameters, which consequently result in the retention of the model terms without reducing the accuracy of the predicted model.

The graphical representation in Fig. 6 depicts the Nusselt number variations relative to its corresponding fitted values. The spread of points, along with the nearby histogram, shows that there is good alignment between the actual and modeled standards, hence validating the appropriateness of using a linear model. The graph effectively shows that it is reliable to model this system based on the Nusselt number.

Figure 7(a–f) depicts the surface and contour plots that describe the interaction between two independent variables on the thermal energy transmission coefficient, as the third variable is kept at the middle level. These graphs demonstrate the change in the Nusselt number regarding the interaction of the testing parameters. In this respect, Fig. 7(a) and (b) indicate that an increase in the melting heat factor produces a significant increase in the response function, characterized by the slope of the contour levels and the growth of the surface plots. Finally, Figs. 7(e, f) illustrate how stagnation and suction variables affect the Nusselt number, further suggesting that higher standards of one variable S, combined with increased A, substantially enhance the response surface. The findings reveal that a notable enhancement in the response surface occurs when the melting heat argument is increased and the suction argument is elevated.

Concluding remarks

The given study is a detailed examination of the thermal and momentum transport qualities of a UCM viscoelastic fluid that flows in a vertical wedge surface in the environment of porous medium. The originality of the piece consists in the concomitant nature of several interacting physical effects such as thermal radiation, melting effects, Joule heating, and EMHD forces, which are not often considered as a coherent entity. The model takes into consideration these coupled mechanisms together with realistic boundary conditions, which represent more accurate and realistic flow behaviors applicable to more advanced engineering and industrial processes. The combined method gives a better understanding of the multiplied effect of fluid elasticity, electromagnetic forces and heat transfer, hence the contribution to the current knowledge on non-Newtonian boundary layer flows. A one-argument Lie scaling transformation was found to reduce the governing PDEs to a series of nonlinear coupled ODEs that were then solved numerically by the MATLAB bvp4c solver. Also, sophisticated methods like RSM and sensitivity analysis were used to optimize the system arguments \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\left( {{M_b},S,A} \right)$$\end{document} and determine their importance. This research offers a lot of knowledge and applicable solutions on how thermal performance can be enhanced in many industries such as energy systems, aerospace, and cooling technologies. The proposed study has profound applications in a wide range of engineering and industrial phenomena involving viscoelastic, non-Newtonian liquids interacting with thermal, magnetic, and electrical fields in porous media.

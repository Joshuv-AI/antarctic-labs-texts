Abstract

We construct solutions of type-II supergravity based on multiple copies and/or mixings of
\lambda
-deformed coset CFTs on
\nicefrac{{\mathrm{SO}(n+1)_{k}}}{{\mathrm{SO}(n)_{k}}}
, with
n=2,3,4
. The resulting ten-dimensional geometries contain undeformed
\mathrm{AdS}
factors, thereby allowing a connection between
\lambda
-deformations and the AdS/CFT correspondence. Imposing reality conditions on the solutions further constrains the deformation parameter. In some cases these bounds exclude the undeformed (
\lambda=0
) or non-Abelian T-dual (
\lambda\to 1
) limits. This work extends the results of [
1911.12371
] and [
2411.11086
].

Introduction

While integrability-preserving deformations of non-linear
\sigma
-models are interesting in their own right, their interplay with ten-dimensional supergravity and their applications in holography provide additional motivation for studying them. An example that stands out within this class of models is the family of
\lambda
-deformations, introduced in
[
1
]
. In their original formulation, they describe a two-dimensional field theory that interpolates between an exact conformal field theory (CFT) – a Wess–Zumino–Witten (WZW) model
[
2
]
on a semisimple group
G
with current algebra symmetry at level
k
– and the non-Abelian T-dual (NATD) of a principal chiral model (PCM) on
G
. This construction has been generalised in various directions. Deformations based on cosets with a Lagrangian description in terms of gauged WZW models, as well as those associated with symmetric or semi-symmetric spaces, were developed in
[
1
,
3
,
4
]
. A variation of the
\lambda
-model that incorporates spectator degrees of freedom was derived in
[
5
]
. Further generalisations include
[
6
,
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
,
11
]
. Their relation to another class of integrable
\sigma
-models, known as
\eta
-deformations
[
12
,
13
]
, which are based on a PCM for some group, was established in
[
14
,
15
,
16
]
.

The connection between ten-dimensional supergravity and integrable deformations of
\sigma
-models arises by promoting the corresponding
\sigma
-model fields to full solutions of type-II supergravity. This has been achieved for
\lambda
- and
\eta
-deformed (super)cosets in various works
[
17
,
15
,
18
,
19
,
20
,
21
,
22
,
23
,
24
,
25
,
26
]
. Deformations of the near-horizon limit of
\mathrm{NS1}
and
\mathrm{NS5}
brane intersections have been constructed recently in
[
27
,
28
]
. These backgrounds accommodate the
\lambda
-models on
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})
and
\mathrm{SU}(2)
within a ten-dimensional setting.

The main objective of the present work is to extend the results of
[
19
,
20
]
and construct type-II supergravity backgrounds incorporating multiple copies and/or mixings of
\lambda
-deformed coset CFTs on
\nicefrac{{\mathrm{SO}(n+1)_{k}}}{{\mathrm{SO}(n)_{k}}}
, with
n=2,3,4
. Our method relies on proposing educated ansätze for the corresponding Ramond–Ramond (RR) fields. The advantage of this approach is that the problem of finding solutions to the supergravity equations of motion reduces to solving algebraic systems of constant parameters, rather than non-linear PDEs. Motivated by holography, we focus on geometries that exhibit undeformed
\mathrm{AdS}
factors. Our findings are summarised in Table
1

2
Review of
\lambda
-deformed coset CFTs

In this section we review the field content of
\lambda
-deformed models
[
1
]
, built on cosets that involve orthogonal groups
[
17
]
, which will be denoted as

\displaystyle{\rm CS}^{n}_{\lambda}{\,\coloneq\,}\frac{{\rm SO}(n+1)_{k}}{{\rm SO}(n)_{k}}\;.

(2.1)

Here
k
stands for the WZW level, and
\lambda
is the deformation parameter which takes values in the interval
[0,1)
. In particular, we will focus on the cases of
n=2,3,4
, with target space always expressed by means of a Euclidean signature.

2.1
The
{\rm CS}^{2}_{\lambda}
model

Let us start by introducing the simplest case, namely the
\nicefrac{{{\rm SO}(3)_{k}}}{{{\rm SO}(2)_{k}}}
model, which corresponds to the
\sigma
-model with a 2-dimensional target space.
For convenience we use the frame

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{e}}^{1}

\displaystyle=-2\,\lambda_{-}\left(\frac{dx}{\omega}-\frac{x\,d\omega}{\omega_{+}^{2}}\right)

(2.2a)

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{e}}^{2}

\displaystyle=-2\,\lambda_{+}\left(\frac{x\,dx}{\sqrt{\cal D}\,\omega}+\frac{\sqrt{\cal D}\,d\omega}{\omega_{+}^{2}}\right)

(2.2b)

where the functions
\cal{D},\omega_{\pm}
and constants
\lambda_{\pm}
are defined below

\displaystyle{\cal D}{\,\coloneq\,}1-x^{2}\;,\quad\omega_{\pm}{\,\coloneq\,}\sqrt{1\pm\omega^{2}}\;,\quad\lambda_{\pm}{\,\coloneq\,}\sqrt{k\,\frac{1\pm\lambda}{1\mp\lambda}}\;.

(2.3)

In addition, the model includes the scalar

\Phi=-\frac{1}{2}\log\left(\frac{2\omega^{2}}{\omega_{+}^{2}}\right)\;.

(2.4)

The above geometry and scalar satisfy the following relations

\displaystyle\beta_{\Phi}=R+4\nabla^{2}\Phi-4(\partial\Phi)^{2}=\nu

(2.5a)

\displaystyle\beta_{g_{ab}}=R_{ab}+2\nabla_{a}\nabla_{b}\Phi=-\mu\,\eta_{ab}

(2.5b)

where for later convenience we defined the constants

\mu{\,\coloneq\,}\frac{1}{k}\frac{\lambda}{1-\lambda^{2}}\;,\quad\nu{\,\coloneq\,}\frac{1}{k}\frac{1+\lambda^{2}}{1-\lambda^{2}}\;.

(2.6)

Moreover,
\eta_{ab}={\rm diag}(-1,+1)
, and
R
and
R_{ab}
are the Ricci scalar and the Ricci tensor, respectively. The above expressions are computed in the frame (
2.2
).
It is clear that in the absence of deformation (i.e. when
\lambda=0
)
\beta_{g_{ab}}=0
and the model becomes conformal. The underlying
\sigma
-model is invariant under

\lambda\mapsto\lambda^{-1}\;,\qquad k\mapsto-k\;.

(2.7)

This symmetry is also manifest in the frame (
2.2
), as well as in
\beta_{\Phi}
and
\beta_{g_{ab}}
. Moreover, the following identities hold

d(e^{-\Phi}{\mathfrak{e}}^{1})=d(e^{-\Phi}{\mathfrak{e}}^{2})=0

(2.8)

and will prove useful for the construction of the ansätze for the RR fields.

2.2
The
{\rm CS}^{3}_{\lambda}
model

Moving to the 3-dimensional case
\nicefrac{{{\rm SO}(4)_{k}}}{{{\rm SO}(3)_{k}}}
, we can express the target space geometry via the following new set of dreibein

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{e}}^{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\,\lambda_{-}}{\sqrt{{\cal A}}}\left(-\omega x\,dx+\frac{y}{\omega}dy+\frac{{\cal A}}{\omega^{2}_{+}}d\omega\right)

(2.9a)

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{e}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\,\lambda_{+}}{\sqrt{{\cal A}{\cal D}}}\left({\cal D}\omega\,dx-\frac{xy}{\omega}dy+\frac{{\cal A}x}{\omega^{2}_{-}}d\omega\right)

(2.9b)

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{e}}^{3}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\,\lambda_{+}}{\sqrt{{\cal D}}}\left(\frac{dy}{\omega}+\frac{y}{\omega^{2}_{-}}d\omega\right)

(2.9c)

where
\cal D
,
\omega_{\pm}
and
\lambda_{\pm}
are given by (
2.3
), while

{\cal A}{\,\coloneq\,}1-x^{2}-y^{2}\;.

(2.10)

Again, the model is equipped with a scalar, which in this case reads

\Phi=-\frac{1}{2}\log\left(8\frac{{\cal A}\omega^{2}}{\omega_{+}^{4}}\right)\;.

(2.11)

The corresponding
\beta_{\Phi}
and
\beta_{g_{ab}}
are now given by

\displaystyle\beta_{\Phi}=R+4\nabla^{2}\Phi-4(\partial\Phi)^{2}=3\nu\;,

(2.12a)

\displaystyle\beta_{g_{ab}}=R_{ab}+2\nabla_{a}\nabla_{b}\Phi=-2\mu\,\hat{\eta}_{ab}\;,

(2.12b)

where
\mu,\nu
are given in (
2.6
) and
\hat{\eta}_{ab}={\rm diag}(-1,+1,+1)
. Both
\beta_{\Phi}
and
\beta_{g_{ab}}
, as well as the frame (
2.9
) respect the symmetry (
2.7
). Like in the previous example,
\beta_{g_{ab}}
becomes trivial when
\lambda=0
and therefore the model is conformal. Additionally, the following identities are true for the frame (
2.9
)

d(e^{-\Phi}{\mathfrak{e}}^{1})=0\;,\quad d(e^{-\Phi}{\mathfrak{e}}^{1}\wedge{\mathfrak{e}}^{3})=d(e^{-\Phi}{\mathfrak{e}}^{2}\wedge{\mathfrak{e}}^{3})=0\;.

(2.13)

The first and the third identities will be crucial for the construction of type-II supergravity solutions.

2.3
The
{\rm CS}^{4}_{\lambda}
model

We now move to the last case, which corresponds to the
\lambda
-deformation of the
\nicefrac{{{\rm SO}(5)_{k}}}{{{\rm SO}(4)_{k}}}
WZW model. Here the target space is spanned by the vierbein

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{e}}^{1}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2\,\lambda_{-}}{\sqrt{{\cal A}{\cal B}{\cal D}}}\left(\frac{{\cal B}{\cal D}}{\omega y}dx+x\left(\frac{{\cal D}z^{2}}{\omega y^{2}}+\omega y^{2}\right)dy-\frac{{\cal A}xz}{\omega y}dz-\frac{{\cal A}{\cal B}}{\omega_{+}^{2}y}d\omega\right)\;,

(2.14a)

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{e}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\,\lambda_{+}}{\sqrt{{\cal A}{\cal B}}}\left(\frac{{\cal B}x}{\omega y}dx+\left(\frac{x^{2}z^{2}}{\omega y^{2}}-\omega y^{2}\right)dy+\frac{{\cal A}z}{\omega y}dz+\frac{{\cal A}{\cal B}}{\omega_{+}^{2}y}d\omega\right)\;,

(2.14b)

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{e}}^{3}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\,\lambda_{-}}{\sqrt{{\cal B}{\cal D}}}\left(\omega y\,dy+\frac{z}{\omega}dz+\frac{{\cal B}}{\omega_{+}^{2}}d\omega\right)\;,

(2.14c)

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{e}}^{4}

\displaystyle=-2\,\lambda_{+}\left(\frac{dz}{\omega y}-\frac{z}{\omega_{+}^{2}y}d\omega\right)\;,

(2.14d)

with
{\cal D}
,
\omega_{\pm}
and
\lambda_{\pm}
defined as in (
2.3
),
{\cal A}
defined as in (
2.10
) and

{\cal B}{\,\coloneq\,}y^{2}-z^{2}\;.

(2.15)

The scalar characterising the model is given by

\Phi=-\frac{1}{2}\log\left(64\frac{{\cal A}{\cal B}\omega^{4}}{\omega_{+}^{6}}\right)

(2.16)

and – together with the metric – fulfils the relations

\displaystyle\beta_{\Phi}=R+4\nabla^{2}\Phi-4(\partial\Phi)^{2}=6\nu\;,

(2.17a)

\displaystyle\beta_{g_{ab}}=R_{ab}+2\nabla_{a}\nabla_{b}\Phi=-3\mu\,\tilde{\eta}_{ab}\;,

(2.17b)

with
\mu,\nu
given in (
2.6
) and
\tilde{\eta}_{ab}={\rm diag}(+1,-1,+1,-1)
. Equation (
2.7
) still provides a symmetry of the
\beta
-functions above, and the frame (
2.14
). Again, a vanishing
\lambda
would reduce to the conformal model.
Like the previous two examples, the frame satisfies the following identities

d(e^{-\Phi}{\mathfrak{e}}^{1}\wedge{\mathfrak{e}}^{3})=d(e^{-\Phi}{\mathfrak{e}}^{2}\wedge{\mathfrak{e}}^{4})=0\;,\quad d(e^{-\Phi}{\mathfrak{e}}^{1}\wedge{\mathfrak{e}}^{3}\wedge{\mathfrak{e}}^{4})=0\;.

(2.18)

In the next sections we move to the explicit construction of supergravity solutions focusing on geometries containing undeformed
\rm AdS
factors.

3.1
Solutions involving only

3.1.1 Type-IIA on
{\cal M}_{6}\times{\rm CS}^{2}_{\lambda}\times{\rm CS}^{2}_{\lambda}

As a first example, we consider the case with two copies of
{\rm CS}^{2}_{\lambda}

{\cal M}_{6}\times{\rm CS}^{2}_{\lambda}\times{\rm CS}^{2}_{\lambda}

{\cal M}_{6}

{\rm CS}^{2}_{\lambda}

{\cal M}_{6}

ds^{2}_{{\cal M}_{6}}=-(e^{0})^{2}+(e^{1})^{2}+\ldots+(e^{5})^{2}\;.

(3.1)

The other four directions of the ten-dimensional geometry are identified as
e^{6}\to\mathfrak{e}^{1}

e^{7}\to\mathfrak{e}^{2}

\mathfrak{e}^{1}

\begin{gathered}c_{1}=s_{1}\sqrt{\frac{3\nu}{4}+\mu}\;,\quad c_{2}=s_{2}\sqrt{\frac{3\nu}{4}-\mu}\;,\quad r_{1}=-r_{2}=-r_{3}=\frac{3\nu}{2}>0\;,\end{gathered}

(3.75)

with
s_{i}=\pm 1
and the rest of the constants
c_{i}
being zero. The parameters
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
are real for all values of the deformation parameter
\lambda\in[0,1)
. The form of the Ricci tensor (
3.72
) on
{\cal M}_{4}
allows us to consider the cases
{\cal M}_{4}\simeq{\rm AdS_{4}}
and
{\cal M}_{4}\simeq{\rm AdS_{2}\times H_{2}}
, which correspond to the ten-dimensional geometries
\rm AdS_{4}\times CS^{3}_{\lambda}\times CS^{3}_{\lambda}
and
\rm AdS_{2}\times H_{2}\times CS^{3}_{\lambda}\times CS^{3}_{\lambda}
respectively.

Example 2:
{\cal M}_{4}\simeq\rm AdS_{3}\times S^{1}

A second interesting example is provided by setting
r_{2}=-r_{1}
,
r_{3}=0
and
c_{3}=c_{4}=0
. The rest of the parameters are determined as

\begin{gathered}c_{1}=s_{1}\sqrt{\frac{\nu}{2}+\mu}\;,\quad c_{2}=s_{2}\sqrt{\frac{\nu}{2}-\mu}\;,\\[5.0pt]
c_{5}=s_{4}\sqrt{\frac{\nu}{2}}\;,\quad c_{6}=s_{6}\sqrt{\frac{\nu}{2}}\;,\quad r_{1}=-r_{2}=2\nu\;,\end{gathered}

(3.76)

with
s_{i}=\pm 1
being signs. Such solution is real for all values of
\lambda\in[0,1)
. The Ricci tensor (
3.72
) on
{\cal M}_{4}
now suggests that we are allowed to consider
{\cal M}_{4}\simeq{\rm AdS_{3}\times S^{1}}
, in agreement with eq. (
3.70
). This choice corresponds to the ten-dimensional geometry
\rm AdS_{3}\times S^{1}\times CS^{3}_{\lambda}\times CS^{3}_{\lambda}
.

3.3
Solutions involving only
{\rm CS}^{4}_{\lambda}

Continuing the search of
\rm AdS
backgrounds we will consider the embedding of two copies of the
\rm CS^{4}_{\lambda}
model in type-II supergravity. Such configuration is quite restrictive due to the fact that the
\rm CS^{4}_{\lambda}\times CS^{4}_{\lambda}
geometry is eight-dimensional. We will see that the only allowed possibility is an
\rm AdS_{2}
solution of the IIA type.

3.3.1 Type-IIA on
{\cal M}_{2}\times{\rm CS}^{4}_{\lambda}\times{\rm CS}^{4}_{\lambda}

Having as a starting point the deformed four-sphere
\rm CS^{4}_{\lambda}
introduced in Sec.
2.3
, we will search for type-IIA solutions with geometry of the form
{\cal M}_{2}\times\rm CS^{4}_{\lambda}\times CS^{4}_{\lambda}
.
The line-element on
{\cal M}_{2}
will be expressed in terms of the frame
(e^{0},e^{1})
, with
e^{0}
being the time-like direction, as

ds^{2}_{{\cal M}_{2}}=-(e^{0})^{2}+(e^{1})^{2}\;.

(3.77)

The remaining eight dimensions are represented by two copies of (
2.14
). In particular
e^{2}\to\mathfrak{e}^{1}
,
e^{3}\to\mathfrak{e}^{2}
,
e^{4}\to\mathfrak{e}^{3}
,
e^{5}\to\mathfrak{e}^{4}
;
e^{6}\to\mathfrak{e}^{1}
,
e^{7}\to\mathfrak{e}^{2}
,
e^{8}\to\mathfrak{e}^{3}
and
e^{9}\to\mathfrak{e}^{4}
.
To avoid confusion, each copy of
\rm CS^{2}_{\lambda}
is labelled by the two sets of coordinates
(x_{1},y_{1},z_{1},\omega_{1})
and
(x_{2},y_{2},z_{2},\omega_{2})
, respectively.
The Kalb-Ramond field is as usual assumed to be trivial, while the dilaton
\Phi
will be given by the sum of the scalars (
2.16
) for each of the
\rm CS^{4}_{\lambda}

\Phi=-\frac{1}{2}\log\left(64\frac{{\cal A}_{1}{\cal B}_{1}\omega_{1}^{4}}{\omega_{1+}^{6}}\right)-\frac{1}{2}\log\left(64\frac{{\cal A}_{2}{\cal B}_{2}\omega_{2}^{4}}{\omega_{2+}^{6}}\right)\;.

(3.78)

More about the geometry on
{\cal M}_{2}
can be inferred from the dilaton equation (
A.4
). In particular, if we combine it with (
2.17a
) we find that the curvature on
{\cal M}_{2}
is given by

R_{{\cal M}_{2}}=-12\nu\;,

(3.79)

which is constant and negative for all values of
\lambda\in[0,1)
.

The precise structure of the Ricci tensor on
{\cal M}_{2}
can be inspected from the RR sector. For this reason, we will take
F_{2}=0
, while for the four-form we will adopt the following ansatz

F_{4}=2e^{-\Phi}\big(c_{1}e^{2468}+c_{2}e^{2479}+c_{3}e^{3568}+c_{4}e^{3579}\big)\;,

(3.80)

with
c_{1},\dots,c_{4}
being constant parameters to be determined. This guarantees that the Bianchi equation (
A.6
) for the RR four-form is trivially satisfied due to (
2.18
). The corresponding equation for the RR six-form implies

\displaystyle de^{01}

\displaystyle=0

(3.81a)

as long as any of the parameters
c_{1},\dots,c_{4}
is not zero. The four-form sources the equation of motion (
A.5
) for the NS three-form, which gives us a constraint

c_{1}c_{4}+c_{2}c_{3}=0

(3.82)

on the parameters
c_{i}
.

A careful treatment of the Einstein equations (
A.2
), (
A.3
), in view of the property (
2.17b
), reveals that the Ricci tensor on
{\cal M}_{2}
can be expressed in terms of the parameters
c_{i}
that enter the RR four-form as

R_{ab}=-(c^{2}_{1}+c^{2}_{2}+c^{2}_{3}+c^{2}_{4})\eta_{ab}{\,\eqcolon\,}-r\eta_{ab}\;,\qquad a,b=0,1\;.

(3.83)

The constant
r
is obtained using (
3.79
), where

r=6\nu>0\;.

(3.84)

The above allows us to identify
{\cal M}_{2}\simeq{\rm AdS_{2}}
. The remaining of the Einstein equations yield

\displaystyle 3\mu

\displaystyle=c_{1}^{2}+c_{2}^{2}-c_{3}^{2}-c_{4}^{2}\;,

(3.85a)

\displaystyle 3\mu

\displaystyle=c_{1}^{2}-c_{2}^{2}+c_{3}^{2}-c_{4}^{2}\;.

(3.85b)

The system (
3.82
), (
3.83
), (
3.84
) and (
3.85
) is solved by

\begin{gathered}c_{1}=s_{1}\sqrt{\frac{3}{8\nu}}\,(\mu+2\nu)\;,\quad c_{2}=s_{2}\sqrt{\frac{3}{8\nu}\,(4\nu^{2}-\mu^{2})}\;,\\[5.0pt]
c_{3}=s_{3}\sqrt{\frac{3}{8\nu}\,(4\nu^{2}-\mu^{2})}\;,\quad c_{4}=s_{4}\sqrt{\frac{3}{8\nu}}\,(\mu-2\nu)\;,\end{gathered}

(3.86)

with
s_{i}=\pm 1
obeying
s_{2}s_{3}-s_{1}s_{4}=0
. The parameters
c_{i}
above are real for all values of
\lambda\in[0,1)
. The corresponding ten-dimensional background has geometry given by the direct product
\rm AdS_{2}\times CS^{4}_{\lambda}\times CS^{4}_{\lambda}
.

3.4
Solutions that mix
{\rm CS}^{2}_{\lambda}
and
{\rm CS}^{4}_{\lambda}

In this last section we construct solutions of the type-II supergravities which accommodate the content of
\lambda
-models of different kind. In particular we find backgrounds that mix the
\rm CS^{2}_{\lambda}
and
\rm CS^{4}_{\lambda}
models.

3.4.1 Type-IIA on
{\cal M}_{4}\times\rm CS^{2}_{\lambda}\times CS^{4}_{\lambda}

As a first example we will consider the case where the internal space takes the form
\rm CS^{2}_{\lambda}\times CS^{4}_{\lambda}
, where
\rm CS^{2}_{\lambda}
and
\rm CS^{4}_{\lambda}
introduced in Sec.
2.1
and in Sec.
2.3
, respectively. Therefore, the ten-dimensional geometry is
{\cal M}_{4}\times{\rm CS^{2}_{\lambda}\times CS^{4}_{\lambda}}
, and the line element on the unknown space
{\cal M}_{4}
is expressed in terms of the frame
(e^{0},\dots,e^{3})
as

ds^{2}_{{\cal M}_{4}}=-(e^{0})^{2}+(e^{1})^{2}+(e^{2})^{2}+(e^{3})^{2}\;.

(3.87)

For the internal part of the ten-dimensional geometry we identify
e^{4}\to\mathfrak{e}^{1},\;e^{5}\to\mathfrak{e}^{2}
, with
\mathfrak{e}^{1},\;\mathfrak{e}^{2}
defined in (
2.2
), and
e^{6}\to\mathfrak{e}^{1}
,
e^{7}\to\mathfrak{e}^{2}
,
e^{8}\to\mathfrak{e}^{3}
and
e^{9}\to\mathfrak{e}^{4}
, with
\mathfrak{e}^{1},\dots,\mathfrak{e}^{4}
defined in (
2.14
). As a matter of fact, the frame components
e^{4}
and
e^{5}
describe the geometry of
\rm CS^{2}_{\lambda}
, which we label with the coordinates
(x_{1},\omega_{1})
, while
e^{6},\dots,e^{9}
describe the target space of
\rm CS^{4}_{\lambda}
, for which we choose the coordinates
(x_{2},y_{2},z_{2},\omega_{2})
. For the rest of the NS fields, we express the dilaton as the sum of the scalars (
2.4
) and (
2.16
)

\Phi=-\frac{1}{2}\log\left(\frac{2\omega_{1}^{2}}{\omega_{1+}^{2}}\right)-\frac{1}{2}\log\left(64\frac{{\cal A}_{2}{\cal B}_{2}\omega_{2}^{4}}{\omega_{2+}^{6}}\right)\;,

(3.88)

while the Kalb-Ramond form is taken to be trivial.

The Ricci scalar on
{\cal M}_{4}
can be determined from the dilaton equation (
A.4
) using the properties (
2.5a
) and (
2.17a
)

R_{{\cal M}_{4}}=-7\nu\;.

(3.89)

From the above result we see that
{\cal M}_{4}
is a space of constant and negative curvature for all values of the deformation parameter
0\leq\lambda<1
.

More properties for the space
{\cal M}_{4}
arise by providing an ansatz for the RR fields. In the case of a type-IIA theory, we will only allow for a four-form which reads

\begin{split}F_{4}&=2e^{-\Phi}e^{2}\wedge\big(c_{1}e^{468}+c_{2}e^{568}+c_{3}e^{479}+c_{4}e^{579}\big)\\
&+2e^{-\Phi}e^{3}\wedge\big(c_{5}e^{468}+c_{6}e^{568}+c_{7}e^{479}+c_{8}e^{579}\big)\;.\end{split}

(3.90)

The parameters
c_{1},\dots,c_{8}
are considered to be constants which will be determined later.

This ansatz guarantees that the Bianchi equation (
A.6
) for
F_{4}
is satisfied provided that

\displaystyle de^{2}

\displaystyle=0\quad\text{if at least one between}\;c_{1},\dots,c_{4}\;\text{is not zero;}

(3.91a)

\displaystyle de^{3}

\displaystyle=0\quad\text{if at least one between}\;c_{5},\dots,c_{8}\;\text{is not zero.}

(3.91b)

In order to see this, one has to take into account the properties (
2.8
) and (
2.18
). In the same way, the field equation for the RR six-form implies that

\displaystyle de^{013}

\displaystyle=0\quad\text{if at least one between}\;c_{1},\dots,c_{4}\;\text{is not zero;}

(3.92a)

\displaystyle de^{012}

\displaystyle=0\quad\text{if at least one between}\;c_{5},\dots,c_{8}\;\text{is not zero.}

(3.92b)

Moreover, the equation (
A.5
) for the NS three-form
H
gives a first constraint on the parameters
c_{i}
’s

c_{1}c_{8}-c_{2}c_{7}+c_{3}c_{6}-c_{4}c_{5}=0\;.

(3.93)

On the other hand, the Einstein equations (
A.2
) and (
A.3
) can provide more information about the Ricci tensor on
{\cal M}_{4}
. In particular, we find that its non-vanishing components are

\displaystyle R_{ab}

\displaystyle=-\left(c_{1}^{2}+\dots+c_{8}^{2}\right)\eta_{ab}{\,\eqcolon\,}-r\eta_{ab}\;,\qquad\qquad\qquad a,b=0,1\;,

(3.94a)

\displaystyle R_{22}

\displaystyle=-R_{33}=c_{1}^{2}+\dots+c_{4}^{2}-c_{5}^{2}-\dots-c_{8}^{2}\;,

(3.94b)

\displaystyle R_{23}

\displaystyle=2\left(c_{1}c_{5}+c_{2}c_{6}+c_{3}c_{7}+c_{4}c_{8}\right)\;,

(3.94c)

The rest of the Einstein equations imply the additional constraints

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=c_{1}c_{2}+c_{3}c_{4}+c_{5}c_{6}+c_{7}c_{8}\;,

(3.95a)

\displaystyle\mu

\displaystyle=c_{1}^{2}-c_{2}^{2}+c_{3}^{2}-c_{4}^{2}+c_{5}^{2}-c_{6}^{2}+c_{7}^{2}-c_{8}^{2}\;,

(3.95b)

\displaystyle 3\mu

\displaystyle=c_{1}^{2}+c_{2}^{2}-c_{3}^{2}-c_{4}^{2}+c_{5}^{2}+c_{6}^{2}-c_{7}^{2}-c_{8}^{2}\;.

(3.95c)

At this point we would like to make the observation that a natural choice for
{\cal M}_{4}
is a split of the form
{\cal M}_{4}\simeq{\rm AdS_{2}\times T^{2}}
, where
\rm AdS_{2}
is spanned by
(e^{0},e^{1})
and is normalised as in (
3.94a
), while
\rm T^{2}
is spanned by
(e^{2},e^{3})
. This is in agreement with (
3.91
) and (
3.92
), and additionally one has to impose
R_{22}=-R_{33}=0
and
R_{23}=0
. In this case, eq.s (
3.89
) and (
3.94
) also imply that

r=\frac{7\nu}{2}\,.

(3.96)

The above system of constraints can be solved by setting

\begin{gathered}c_{2}=s_{2}\sqrt{\frac{7\nu}{8}+\frac{\mu}{2}}\;,\quad c_{3}=s_{3}\sqrt{\frac{7\nu}{8}-\frac{\mu}{2}}\;,\\[5.0pt]
c_{5}=s_{5}\sqrt{\frac{7\nu}{8}+\mu}\;,\quad c_{8}=s_{8}\sqrt{\frac{7\nu}{8}-\mu}\;,\end{gathered}

(3.97)

with
s_{i}
being signs and all the other parameters vanishing. It turns out that the constants
c_{2}
,
c_{3}
,
c_{5}
and
c_{8}
above, are real for all values of
\lambda\in[0,1)
. The corresponding ten-dimensional background has geometry
\rm AdS_{2}\times T^{2}\times CS^{2}_{\lambda}\times CS^{4}_{\lambda}
.

3.4.2 Type-IIB on
{\cal M}_{4}\times\rm CS^{2}_{\lambda}\times CS^{4}_{\lambda}

We now move to the type-IIB analogue of the ten-dimensional space with geometry of the form
{\cal M}_{4}\times{\rm CS^{2}_{\lambda}\times CS^{4}_{\lambda}}
. The properties of the four-dimensional space
{\cal M}_{4}
will be determined later, while its line element is again given by (
3.87
).
The other six directions transverse to
{\cal M}_{4}
, as well as the rest of the NS fields, are the same as in the example of Sec.
3.4
. Since both the type-IIB background here and the type-IIA one of the previous section share the same NS sector, we expect that the dilaton equation (
A.4
) implies (
3.89
).

In order to proceed with the construction of the solution, we also need to specify the RR fields. For this reason we assume the ansatz

\displaystyle F_{3}

\displaystyle=2e^{-\Phi}\big(c_{1}e^{468}+c_{2}e^{568}+c_{3}e^{479}+c_{4}e^{579}\big)\;,

(3.98a)

\displaystyle F_{5}

\displaystyle=2e^{-\Phi}(1+\star)e^{23}\wedge\big(c_{5}e^{468}+c_{6}e^{568}+c_{7}e^{479}+c_{8}e^{579}\big)\;,

(3.98b)

where the one-form
F_{1}
is taken to be zero and
c_{1},\dots,c_{8}
are constants.

Such a choice ensures that the Bianchi equation (
A.6
) for
F_{3}
is trivially satisfied. However, the one for the self-dual five-form implies

de^{01}=de^{23}=0\;,

(3.99)

whenever
F_{5}
is non-trivial. Similarly, the Bianchi equation (
A.6
) for
F_{7}
– assuming that
F_{3}
is non-zero – suggests

de^{0123}=0\;,

(3.100)

which is also guaranteed by (
3.99
).

The first two constraints for the parameters
c_{i}
are obtained by the equation (
A.5
) for the NS three-form
H

\displaystyle c_{1}c_{5}+c_{2}c_{6}+c_{3}c_{7}+c_{4}c_{8}=0\;,

(3.101a)

\displaystyle c_{1}c_{8}-c_{2}c_{7}+c_{3}c_{6}-c_{4}c_{5}=0\;.

(3.101b)

Finally, elaborating on the Einstein equations (
A.2
) and (
A.3
) one can shed more light on the geometric properties of
{\cal M}_{4}
. Indeed we find that the non-vanishing components of the Ricci tensor on
{\cal M}_{4}
read

\displaystyle R_{ab}

\displaystyle=-\left(c_{1}^{2}+\dots+c_{8}^{2}\right)\eta_{ab}{\,\eqcolon\,}-r_{1}\eta_{ab}\;,\qquad\qquad\qquad\;a,b=0,1\;,

(3.102a)

\displaystyle R_{ab}

\displaystyle=\left(c_{5}^{2}+\dots+c_{8}^{2}-c_{1}^{2}-\dots-c_{4}^{2}\right)\delta_{ab}{\,\eqcolon\,}r_{2}\delta_{ab}\;,\qquad a,b=2,3\;.

(3.102b)

The constants
r_{1}
and
r_{2}
are related due to (
3.89
) as

r_{1}=r_{2}+\frac{7\nu}{2}\;.

(3.103)

In addition, taking into account the properties (
2.5b
) and (
2.17b
), we find the extra constraints below

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=c_{1}c_{2}+c_{3}c_{4}+c_{5}c_{6}+c_{7}c_{8}\;,

(3.104a)

\displaystyle\mu

\displaystyle=c_{1}^{2}-c_{2}^{2}+c_{3}^{2}-c_{4}^{2}+c_{5}^{2}-c_{6}^{2}+c_{7}^{2}-c_{8}^{2}\;,

(3.104b)

\displaystyle 3\mu

\displaystyle=c_{1}^{2}+c_{2}^{2}-c_{3}^{2}-c_{4}^{2}+c_{5}^{2}+c_{6}^{2}-c_{7}^{2}-c_{8}^{2}\;.

(3.104c)

Below we focus on few representative solutions of the algebraic system above, obeyed by the parameters
c_{1},\dots,c_{8}
and
r_{1},r_{2}
.

Example 1:
{\cal M}_{4}\simeq\rm AdS_{2}\times S^{2}

As a first solution we consider the case where

\begin{gathered}c_{1}=s_{1}\sqrt{\frac{7\nu}{8}+\mu}\;,\quad c_{4}=s_{4}\sqrt{\frac{7\nu}{8}-\mu}\;,\quad c_{6}=s_{6}\sqrt{\frac{7\nu}{8}+\mu}\;,\quad c_{7}=s_{7}\sqrt{\frac{7\nu}{8}}\;,\\[5.0pt]
r_{1}=\mu+\frac{7\nu}{2}\;,\quad r_{2}=\mu\;,\end{gathered}

(3.105)

with
s_{i}
being signs and all the other parameters are zero. Here
c_{1}
,
c_{4}
,
c_{6}
and
c_{7}
are real for all the values of
\lambda
in its fundamental domain
[0,1)
. Moreover,
r_{1},\;r_{2}>0
, suggesting that
{\cal M}_{4}
can be written as a direct product of two Einstein spaces, one with constant negative curvature and another one with constant positive curvature – according to (
3.102
). This allows us to choose
{\cal M}_{4}\simeq{\rm AdS_{2}\times S^{2}}
and the corresponding ten-dimensional geometry is
\rm AdS_{2}\times S^{2}\times CS^{2}_{\lambda}\times CS^{4}_{\lambda}
.

Example 2:
{\cal M}_{4}\simeq\rm AdS_{2}\times T^{2}

Aiming for a solution where
{\cal M}_{4}
splits into the direct product
{\cal M}^{t}_{2}\times{\rm T^{2}}
, with the torus exteding in
(e^{2},e^{3})
, we find

\begin{gathered}c_{1}=s_{1}\sqrt{\frac{7\nu}{8}+\mu}\;,\;\;c_{4}=s_{4}\sqrt{\frac{7\nu}{8}-\mu}\;,\;\;c_{6}=s_{6}\sqrt{\frac{7\nu}{8}+\frac{\mu}{2}}\;,\;\;c_{7}=s_{7}\sqrt{\frac{7\nu}{8}-\frac{\mu}{2}}\;,\\[5.0pt]

(3.106)

Again
s_{i}=\pm 1
and the rest of the
c_{i}
are zero. The parameters
c_{1}
,
c_{4}
,
c_{6}
and
c_{7}
with the above values are real when
\lambda\in[0,1)
. Since
r_{1}>0
we can safely choose
{\cal M}^{t}_{2}\simeq{\rm AdS_{2}}
and the ten-dimensional geometry reads
\rm AdS_{2}\times T^{2}\times CS^{2}_{\lambda}\times CS^{4}_{\lambda}
.

Example 3:
{\cal M}_{4}\simeq\rm AdS_{2}\times H_{2}

A third solution is provided when

\begin{gathered}c_{1}=s_{1}\sqrt{\frac{7\nu}{8}+\mu}\;,\quad c_{4}=s_{4}\sqrt{\frac{7\nu}{8}-\mu}\;,\quad c_{6}=s_{6}\sqrt{\mu}\;,\\[5.0pt]

(3.107)

with
s_{i}=\pm 1
and
c_{2}=c_{3}=c_{5}=c_{7}=c_{8}=0
. It tuns out that the above non-vanishing
c_{i}
are real for
\lambda\in[0,1)
. However, in this range
r_{1}>0
and
r_{2}<0
, allowing us to interpret the four-dimensional space as
{\cal M}_{4}\simeq{\rm AdS_{2}\times H_{2}}
– in agreement with (
3.102
). Hence, the corresponding ten-dimensional geometry reads
\rm AdS_{2}\times H_{2}\times CS^{2}_{\lambda}\times CS^{4}_{\lambda}
.

Comment:
{\cal M}_{4}\simeq\rm AdS_{4}

One can also search for solutions where
{\cal M}_{4}\simeq\rm AdS_{4}
. This can be achieved by turning off the self-dual five-form. This immediately waives the restriction (
3.99
) and at the same time implies that

in (

). Nevertheless, solving the algebraic system for

,
c_{2}
,
c_{3}
,
c_{4}
and

one obtains a RR three-form with imaginary components. However, this problem arises due to the normalisations we have chosen for the geometries
\rm CS^{2}_{\lambda}
and
\rm CS^{4}_{\lambda}

as
(e^{4},e^{5})\to L_{2}(e^{4},e^{5})
and
(e^{6},\dots,e^{9})\to L_{4}(e^{4},\dots,e^{9})
, where the radii
L_{2}
and
L_{4}
of
\rm CS^{2}_{\lambda}
and
\rm CS^{4}_{\lambda}
appear to be related with each other.

and

, with

4
Conclusions

In this work, we constructed various type-II supergravity backgrounds that incorporate multiple copies of the
\lambda
-models on
\nicefrac{{\mathrm{SO}(3)_{k}}}{{\mathrm{SO}(2)_{k}}}
,
\nicefrac{{\mathrm{SO}(4)_{k}}}{{\mathrm{SO}(3)_{k}}}
, and
\nicefrac{{\mathrm{SO}(5)_{k}}}{{\mathrm{SO}(4)_{k}}}
, both with and without mixing models of different dimensionality. Our approach bypasses the difficulty of solving non-linear PDEs by choosing suitable ansätze for the RR fields. With these ansätze, the type-II supergravity equations reduce to algebraic systems involving constant parameters. Moreover, the RR-field ansätze fully determine the structure of the resulting ten-dimensional geometries. All the examples considered here exhibit undeformed AdS factors, and they are summarized in Table
1
.

In several cases, the algebraic systems admit multiple solutions. We have presented only representative examples, as an exhaustive analysis is beyond the scope of this paper. Requiring the solutions to be real imposes bounds on the allowed values of the deformation parameter
\lambda
, which sometimes differ from the standard range
[0,1)
. Consequently, some of our backgrounds do not admit an undeformed limit (
\lambda=0
) and/or a non-Abelian T-dual limit (
\lambda\to 1
).

The supergravity backgrounds constructed here most likely preserve no supersymmetry. This expectation stems from the intuition that the deformation of the internal geometry breaks its isometries completely. A systematic supersymmetry analysis, however, is left for future work. It would also be interesting to investigate the stability of these deformed backgrounds, which may further constrain the admissible range of
\lambda
. The same ansätze can be used to embed
\lambda
-models on the non-compact cosets
\nicefrac{{\mathrm{SO}(1,2)_{-k}}}{{\mathrm{SO}(2)_{-k}}}
,
\nicefrac{{\mathrm{SO}(1,3)_{-k}}}{{\mathrm{SO}(3)_{-k}}}
, and
\nicefrac{{\mathrm{SO}(1,4)_{-k}}}{{\mathrm{SO}(4)_{-k}}}
, which can be obtained from the compact cases by sending
\omega\to i\omega
and
k\to-k
. Another compelling direction is to determine whether the backgrounds constructed here arise as near-horizon limits of brane intersections.

Finally, in line with the original motivation, it would be worthwhile to explore the holographic duals of the type-II geometries we have obtained. This could be achieved by analysing observables on the gravity side of the AdS/CFT correspondence and interpreting them through the holographic dictionary. Relevant observables include the entanglement entropy, Wilson and ’t Hooft loops, the central charge, Page charges, and the mass spectra of mesonic operators, among others. Such an analysis could pave the way for formulating new paradigms of holographic duality, analogous to those emerging in the context of non-Abelian T-duality
[

In this appendix we summarise the equations of motion of type IIA and type IIB supergravities following the conventions of

R+4\,\nabla^{2}\Phi-4\,(\partial\Phi)^{2}-\frac{H^{2}}{12}=0\;,

(A.4)

with

being the field-strength of the Kalb-Ramond field. The three-form satisfies the following Bianchi and field equations

dH=0\;,\quad d(e^{-2\Phi}H)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{p\leq 8}F_{p}\wedge\star F_{p+2}\;.

R_{ab}=-\left(c_{1}^{2}+\dots+c_{6}^{2}\right)\eta_{ab}{\,\eqcolon\,}-r\eta_{ab}\;,\qquad a,b=0,1\;.

(B.21)

The above result implies that
{\cal M}_{2}
is an Einstein space of constant and negative curvature and, therefore, we can choose
{\cal M}_{2}\simeq{\rm AdS_{2}}
. The constant
r
can be expressed in terms of
\nu
and the curvature of
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
using the components of the Ricci tensor and eq. (
B.17
)

r=\frac{12}{L^{2}}+3\nu\;.

(B.22)

The rest of the Einstein equations imply

\frac{6}{L^{2}}=c_{1}^{2}+c_{2}^{2}-c_{5}^{2}-c_{6}^{2}\;,\qquad 3\mu=c_{1}^{2}-c_{2}^{2}+c_{3}^{2}-c_{4}^{2}-c_{5}^{2}+c_{6}^{2}\;.

(B.23)

The algebraic system (
B.20
) – (
B.23
) can be solved by picking

\begin{gathered}c_{1}=\frac{1}{2}s_{1}\sqrt{3\nu+3\mu}\;,\quad c_{4}=\frac{1}{2}s_{4}\sqrt{7\nu-5\mu}\;,\quad c_{6}=s_{6}\sqrt{\nu+\mu}\;,\\[5.0pt]
r=\frac{1}{2}(\mu+7\nu)\;,\quad L=\frac{2\sqrt{6}}{\sqrt{\mu+\nu}}\;,\end{gathered}

(B.24)

where
s_{i}
are signs, and leaving
c_{2}=c_{3}=c_{5}=0
. The proposed solution is real for all values of the deformation parameter
0\leq\lambda<1
and corresponds to the ten-dimensional geometry
\rm AdS_{2}\times\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{2}\times CS^{4}_{\lambda}
.

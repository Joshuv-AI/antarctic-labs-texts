Pointwise Weyl Laws for Quantum Completely Integrable Systems
Suresh Eswarathasan, Allan Greenleaf, Blake Keeler

Abstract
. The study of the asymptotics of the spectral function for self-adjoint, elliptic differential, or more generally pseudodifferential, operators on a compact manifold has a long history. The seminal 1968 paper of Hörmander, following important prior contributions by Gärding, Levitan, Avakumović, and Agmon-Kannai (to name only some), obtained pointwise asymptotics (or a “pointwise Weyl law”) for a single elliptic, self-adjoint operator. Here, we establish a microlocalized pointwise Weyl law for the joint spectral functions of quantum completely integrable (QCI) systems, P ¯ = ( P 1 , P 2 , … , P n ) \overline{P}=(P_{1},P_{2},\dots,P_{n}) , where P i P_{i} are first-order, classical, self-adjoint, pseudodifferential operators on a compact manifold M n M^{n} , with ∑ P i 2 \sum P_{i}^{2} elliptic and [ P i , P j ] = 0 [P_{i},P_{j}]=0 for 1 ≤ i , j ≤ n 1\leq i,j\leq n . A particularly important case is when ( M , g ) (M,g) is Riemannian and P 1 = ( − Δ ) 1 2 P_{1}=(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}} . We illustrate our result with several examples, including surfaces of revolution.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Pointwise/Off-Diagonal Weyl Laws

Let
(M,g)
be a compact, connected, boundaryless
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold, with (positive) Laplace-Beltrami operator acting
on functions,
-\Delta_{g}
. It is well-known that
-\Delta_{g}
has
a discrete spectrum, consisting of eigenvalues
0=\lambda_{0}^{2}<\lambda_{1}^{2}\leq\lambda_{2}^{2}\leq\dots
,
and an orthonormal basis
\{\phi_{j}\}_{j=0}^{\infty}
of
L^{2}(M)
consisting of real-valued eigenfunctions, each satisfying

-\Delta_{g}\phi_{j}=\lambda_{j}^{2}\phi_{j},\,\forall j\geq 0.

The nature of the Laplace spectrum has been the subject of a vast amount of work over the last hundred
years, particularly in the high-frequency regime, where one studies the spectral behavior as
\lambda_{j}\to\infty.

Perhaps the most fundamental such result is Weyl’s law (often referred to as just “the Weyl law”), which gives an asymptotic for the growth of the eigenvalue counting function,

N(\lambda):=\#\{j:\,\lambda_{j}\leq\lambda\}
, as
\lambda\to\infty
. In particular, there exists
C_{g}>0
so that one has

(1.1)

N(\lambda)=C_{g}\lambda^{n}+\mathcal{O}(\lambda^{n-1}),

and this result is sharp without additional assumptions on
(M,g)
, as can be seen from the example of the round sphere,

\mathbb{S}^{n}.

More generally, one can study, for
\lambda>0
, the
spectral function
,
defined as the Schwartz kernel of the orthogonal projection

\Pi_{\lambda}:\bigoplus\limits_{\lambda_{j}\leq\lambda}\ker(\Delta_{g}+\lambda_{j}^{2}),

which in the above basis takes the form

\Pi_{\lambda}(x,y)=\sum\limits_{\lambda_{j}\leq\lambda}\varphi_{j}(x)\varphi_{j}(y).

This generalizes the eigenvalue counting function
N(\lambda)
in the sense that if we compute the trace
and use the fact that the eigenfunctions are orthonormal, we obtain

\textnormal{trace}(\Pi_{\lambda})=\int\limits_{M}\Pi_{\lambda}(x,x)\,dv_{g}(x)=\sum\limits_{\lambda_{j}\leq\lambda}1=N(\lambda).

Now, let
inj(M)
denote the injectivity radius of
M
. The spectral function also has a well-known asymptotic as
\lambda\to\infty
, often
referred to as a “pointwise” Weyl law, given by

(1.2)

\Pi_{\lambda}(x,y)=\frac{\lambda^{n}}{(2\pi)^{n}}\int\limits_{S_{y}^{*}M}e^{i\lambda\langle\exp_{y}^{-1}(x),\xi\rangle_{g_{y}}}\,\frac{d\xi}{\sqrt{|g_{y}|}}+\mathscr{R}(x,y,\lambda),

where there exists an
\varepsilon\in(0,inj(M))
such that

\sup\limits_{d(x,y)\leq\varepsilon}\left|\mathscr{R}(x,y,\lambda)\right|=\mathcal{O}(\lambda^{n-1})

as
\lambda\to\infty
. This result is due to Hörmander
[
Hör68
]
, building on earlier results of
Avakumović and Levitan
[
Ava56
,
Lev52
,
Lev55
]
. Hörmander’s result is in fact more general than this,
holding for any elliptic pseudodifferential operator. As with the eigenvalue counting function, the remainder bound here is sharp, which
can again be seen on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
. If one is willing to impose restrictions on the geometry of
M
, it is possible to obtain
improvements in both (
1.1
) and (
1.2
). A great deal of research has been done in this
area; see, e.g.,
[
Saf88
,
SZ02
,
CH15
,
CH18
,
Ber77
,
Kee23
]
, which is
by no means an exhaustive list.

Our main result, Theorem
1
, to be described in Section
1.3
, can be thought as a generalization of Hörmander’s (
1.2
) for multiple commuting operators; we set up the hypotheses for this in Section
1.2
.

1.2.
QCI Systems and Laplacians

In this article, we are interested in the behavior of the joint spectral function of a
quantum completely integrable (QCI)

system of pseudodifferential operators on an
n
-dimensional manifold
M
without boundary.
All pseudodifferential operators are classical (or
polyhomogeneous
); if
P(x,D)\in\Psi^{m}(M)
,
its principal symbol
p(x,\xi)=\sigma(P)(x,\xi)
is homogeneous of degree
m
on
T^{*}M\setminus 0
.
We assume that
M
is equipped with a volume form,
dx:=d\mbox{Vol}
;
this includes the case when
(M,g)
is Riemannian, with
dx=d\mbox{Vol}_{g}
.

Definition 1.1
.

(a) A
quantum completely integrable (QCI) system
on
M

is a collection
\overline{P}
of
n
self-adjoint, first order pseudodifferential operators,

\overline{P}=(P_{1},\dots,P_{n})
,
satisfying

(i)
\sum_{i=1}^{n}P_{i}^{2}
is elliptic;

(ii) the
Liouville integrability condition
, consisting of

(1.3)

[P_{i},P_{j}]=0,\,\,\,\forall i,j=1,\dots,n,

and the nondegeneracy of their (real) principal symbols
p_{i}=\sigma(P_{i})
,

(1.4)

d_{x,\xi}p_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge d_{x,\xi}p_{n}\neq 0,

on a dense, open, conic subset
\Omega_{0}\subset T^{*}M\backslash 0
.

(b) A Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
is said to be
QCI
(or its Laplace-Beltrami operator
\Delta_{g}
is said to be QCI)
if one can extend
P_{1}:=\sqrt{-\Delta_{g}}
to a QCI system
\overline{P}
on
M
.

This paper concerns the joint spectral asymptotics of QCI systems, microlocalized to
regions of phase space exhibiting a stronger non-degenerate behavior than (
1.4
).
Recall that for a function
p:T^{*}M\to\mathbb{R}
, although the full gradient
d_{x,\xi}p
is intrinsically defined,

d_{x}p
and
d_{\xi}p
are not without further structure being imposed, e.g., fixing a local coordinate system.
Let
proj_{M}:T^{*}M\to M
denote the natural projection, which is an open map.

Definition 1.2
.

Suppose
1\leq k\leq n
.
We say that a set
\{p_{1},\dots,p_{k}\}
of functions, real-valued and homogeneous of degree 1,
has
fiber rank

k
on an open conic subset
\widetilde{\Omega}\subset T^{*}M\setminus 0

if, for any
(x_{0},\xi_{0})\in\widetilde{\Omega}
, there exists a conic neighborhood
U_{x_{0},\xi_{0}}
of
(x_{0},\xi_{0})

and a coordinate system
\overline{x}
on the open set
proj_{M}(U_{x_{0},\xi_{0}})\owns x_{0}

such that, with respect to the induced canonical coordinates
(\overline{x},\overline{\xi})\in T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus 0
on
U_{x_{0},\xi_{0}}
,

\dim\textnormal{span\,}\{\nabla_{\overline{\xi}}p_{1},\dots,\nabla_{\overline{\xi}}p_{n}\}=k
.

Remark 1.3
.

On
M=\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the fiber rank
k
condition is part of an admissibility condition
introduced by Tacy
[
Tac19
, Thm. 0.1 (1,2)]
in
the study of
L^{p}
estimates for semi-classical quasimodes. Geometrically it corresponds
to the level surfaces
\left\{\,\{\xi\,|\,p_{i}(x_{0},\xi)=p_{i}(x_{0},\xi_{0})\}\,\right\}_{i=1}^{k}
through any point
(x_{0},\xi_{0})\in\widetilde{\Omega}

being smooth in
T^{*}_{x_{0}}M\setminus 0
and intersecting transversely.

For
k=n
, the fiber rank
n
condition can be expressed in terms the moment map
\overline{p}
(defined below) in the following way.
Let
\pi:T^{*}M\rightarrow M
be the canonical projection.
Then the fiber rank
n
condition is equivalent with,
for any level set
\Sigma
of the moment map,

D\pi:T\Sigma\rightarrow TM
having full rank, i.e.,
\pi:\Sigma\rightarrow M

is a submersion.

Note also that for
k=n
the condition described in Def.
1.2
is stronger than that in (
1.4
) and thus,
in the context of a QCI system, such a set
\widetilde{\Omega}
is contained in the set
\Omega_{0}
of Def.
1.1
(a).
We refer to a QCI system
\overline{P}
whose principal symbols
\{p_{1},\dots,p_{n}\}

satisfy the fiber rank condition with
k=n
on
\widetilde{\Omega}
as simply satisfying the
fiber rank condition
,
and this will play a prominent role in the statement of the results in Section
1.3
.

We now review some of the spectral theory for QCI systems most relevant to this article.
The spectral theorem for commuting, unbounded, self-adjoint operators,
along with the ellipticity of
\sum_{i=1}^{n}P_{i}^{2}
,
implies there is a discrete
joint spectrum
of
\overline{P}
,

(1.5)

\Lambda:=\{\overline{\lambda}=(\lambda^{(1)},\dots,\lambda^{(n)})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\exists\,\phi_{\overline{\lambda}}\hbox{ s.t. }P_{i}\varphi_{\overline{\lambda}}=\lambda^{(i)}\varphi_{\overline{\lambda}},\,\forall\,1\leq i\leq n\}.

Each
joint eigenfunction

\varphi_{\overline{\lambda}}
is an eigenfunction of the
elliptic
\sum P_{i}^{2}
and thus is
C^{\infty}
.
This also shows that
\Lambda
is countably infinite and one may write a corresponding orthonormal basis
of joint eigenfunctions as

\{\varphi_{\overline{\lambda}_{j}}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
;
we write each
\overline{\lambda}_{j}\in\Lambda
in terms of coordinates as

\overline{\lambda}_{j}=(\lambda_{j}^{(1)},\dots,\lambda_{j}^{(n)})
.

The goal of this paper is to study the pointwise asymptotics of the Schwartz kernels of the
L^{2}
projectors
associated to certain subsets of
\Lambda
.

We recall an object that further describes
\Lambda
. The
moment map
of the QCI system
(or
momentum map
; see
[
MathOver17
]
)
is

\overline{p}:T^{*}M\setminus 0\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus 0
, defined by

\overline{p}(x,\xi)=\left(p_{1}(x,\xi),\dots,p_{n}(x,\xi)\right)

for
(x,\xi)\in T^{*}M\setminus 0
.
It is well-known that the moment map has a privileged position in the theory of classical integrable dynamical systems, and so it is not
surprising that its properties continue to play an important role in the spectral theory of QCI systems.
An example of this is its influence on

L^{p}
norms and eigenfunction concentration; see the survey article of Toth and Zelditch
[
TZ03
]
and references therein.
Denote by

(1.6)

\Gamma:=\overline{p}(T^{*}M\setminus 0)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus 0.

the image of the moment map, which is conic due to the homogeneity of the principal symbols
p_{i}
.

\Gamma
gives a first description of the “geometry” of
\Lambda
, a heuristic rigorized by some fundamental results
for QCI systems
due to Colin de Verdière, which we now describe.

Let
\overline{P}
be a QCI system,
\Gamma
the image of its moment map as in (
1.6
), and suppose that

\mathcal{C}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus 0
is a (possibly empty) conic set such that

\mathcal{C}\cap\Gamma=\emptyset
.
Colin de Verdière’s first fundamental result is that
\mathcal{C}\cap\Lambda
is finite
[
CdV79
, Theorem 0.6]
.
Since
\Gamma^{\complement}
is also conic,
it follows that that
\Lambda\backslash\,B\left(0,R_{0}\right)\subset\Gamma
for some
R_{0}>0
.

Next, let
\Gamma_{{crit}}
denote the critical values of the moment map.
Colin de Verdière’s second fundamental result
[
CdV79
, Theorem 0.7]

states that for any non-empty conic set
\mathcal{C}
with a piecewise
C^{1}
boundary
\partial\mathcal{C}

such that
\partial\mathcal{C}\cap\Gamma_{{crit}}=\emptyset
,
as
\lambda\to\infty
we have

(1.7)

\#\{j\,:\,\overline{\lambda}_{j}\in\mathcal{C}\cap\Lambda\,\mbox{ and }\,\|\overline{\lambda}_{j}\|\leq\lambda\}=(2\pi)^{-n}\mbox{Vol}_{T^{*}M}\left(\,\overline{p}^{-1}\left\{\mathcal{C}\cap B(0,\lambda)\right\}\,\right)+\mathcal{O}(\lambda^{n-1}),

where
\mbox{Vol}_{T^{*}M}
is the symplectic (Liouville) volume on
T^{*}M
.

1.3.
Setup and statement of main results

We are now in a position to describe the main analytical object studied in this paper.
For
\overline{c}\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
with coordinates

1
1

1

In the semiclassical analysis literature,
\overline{c}
would usually be referred to as

\frac{\overline{E}}{|\overline{E}|}
, a normalized energy vector.

c_{i}\neq 0,\,1\leq i\leq n
,
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
, define
I(\lambda,\overline{c})\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
by

I(\lambda,\overline{c}):=[-|c_{1}|\lambda,|c_{1}|\lambda]\times[-|c_{2}|\lambda,|c_{2}|\lambda]\times\dots\times[-|c_{n}|\lambda,|c_{n}|\lambda].

Then the
joint spectral function of
\overline{P}
over
I(\lambda,\overline{c})
, is

(1.8)

\Pi_{\lambda,\overline{c}}(x,y):=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\infty}\mathds{1}_{I(\lambda,\overline{c})}\left(\overline{\lambda}_{j}\right)\varphi_{\overline{\lambda}_{j}}(x)\overline{\varphi_{\overline{\lambda}_{j}}(y)}\in C^{\infty}(M\times M).

Note that this orthonormal projector
has a non-trivial range for
\lambda
large enough.

Recall the asymptotic (
1.2
). Our main theorem is a microlocalized-QCI analogue of the main result in

[
Hör68
]
and is a pointwise version of Colin de Verdière’s “integrated” Weyl Law for QCI systems. For a list of
notation used, see Section
1.6
.

Theorem 1
.

Suppose that
\overline{P}=\left(P_{1},P_{2},\dotsc,P_{n}\right)
is a QCI system
on a smooth, compact manifold without boundary,
M
,
which satisfies the fiber rank
n
condition on a conic, open subset

\widetilde{\Omega}\subset T^{*}M\setminus 0
.
Let
\overline{c}\in\overline{p}(\widetilde{\Omega})
, with
c_{i}\neq 0,\,\forall\,1\leq i\leq n
.
For any
(x_{0},\xi_{0})\in\widetilde{\Omega}
, let
U_{x_{0},\xi_{0}}\subset\widetilde{\Omega}
and
(x,\xi)
be local canonical coordinates,
referred to as
(\overline{x},\overline{\xi})
in Def.
1.2
.
Finally, assume that
M
is either
Riemannian or equipped with an arbitrary metric,
d
,
compatible with its locally Euclidean topology.

Then, there exists a nonempty open conic neighborhood
\Omega\subset\widetilde{\Omega}
of
(x_{0},\xi_{0})
;
constants
\varepsilon_{0},\,\lambda_{0}>0
;
a pseudodifferential
cutoff
\,\Psi\in\Psi^{0}(M)
,
elliptic at
(x_{0},\xi_{0})
and microlocally supported in
\Omega
;
a real-valued, homogeneous function
S\in C^{\infty}(\widetilde{\Omega})
;
and homogeneous compound symbols
a,b\in S^{0}(\widetilde{\Omega})
, so that
for all
x,y\in\mbox{proj}_{M}(\Omega)
with
d(x,y)\leq\varepsilon_{0}
, the Schwartz kernel of
\Psi\Pi_{\lambda,\overline{c}}\Psi^{*}
is

(1.9)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\Psi\Pi_{\lambda,\overline{c}}\Psi^{*}(x,y)\\
&\hskip 36.135pt=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}}\int\limits_{I(\lambda,\overline{c})\cap\overline{p}(\Omega)}e^{i(S(x,\xi)-S(y,\xi))}b(x,\nabla_{\xi}S(x,\xi);\xi)a(\nabla_{\xi}S(y,\xi),y;\xi)\,d\xi+\mathscr{R}(x,y,\lambda),\end{split}

where, uniformly for all
\lambda\geq\lambda_{0}
such that
\lambda\overline{c}\notin\Lambda
,

\sup\limits_{\{x,y\in proj_{M}(\Omega)\,:\,d(x,y)\leq\epsilon_{0}\}}\left|\mathscr{R}(x,y,\lambda)\right|=\mathcal{O}(\lambda^{n-1}).

Above the diagonal of
M\times M
,
b(x,\nabla_{\xi}S(x,\xi);\xi)a(\nabla_{\xi}S(x,\xi),x;\xi)\equiv|\sigma(\Psi)(x,\xi)|^{2}
.

Remark 1.4
.

Note that
\epsilon_{0}
above is independent of
\lambda
, so that the representation
(
1.9
) is valid on a fixed tubular neighborhood of the diagonal in

\mbox{proj}_{M}(\Omega)\times\mbox{proj}_{M}(\Omega)
, uniformly in
\lambda\geq\lambda_{0}
.

Remark 1.5
.

Choosing
\overline{c}
so that
\lambda\overline{c}\notin\Lambda
for some sufficiently large values of
\lambda
is always possible
since
\Lambda
is discrete. We emphasize that the remainder estimate on
\mathscr{R}
does not depend on

dist\left(\lambda\overline{c},\Lambda\right).

Remark 1.6
.

It is intuitively clear that the coordinate-free form of the expression (
1.9
) should have as its phase an
exponential-type map
associated with the joint Hamiltonian flow, like in Hörmander’s expression (
1.2
) with the Hamiltonian flow
generated by
|\xi|_{g}
, but we leave this for future exploration.

Corollary 1.7
.

Suppose
(M,g)
is Riemannian
and
\left(-\Delta_{g}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}
is QCI, with QCI system
\overline{P}
.
Then, with the hypotheses and notation of Theorem
1
,
if
x,y\in\mbox{proj}_{M}(\Omega)
are such that
d_{g}(x,y)\leq\frac{c}{\lambda}<inj(M)
, one has

(1.10)

\Psi\Pi_{\lambda,\overline{c}}\Psi^{*}(x,y)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}}\int\limits_{I(\lambda,\overline{c})\cap\overline{p}(\Omega)}e^{i(x-y)\cdot\nabla_{x}S(x;\xi)}\left|\sigma(\Psi)(y,\xi)\right|^{2}\,d\xi+\mathscr{R}(x,y,\lambda),

with

\sup\limits_{d_{g}(x,y)\leq\frac{c}{\lambda}}\left|\mathscr{R}(x,y,\lambda)\right|=\mathcal{O}(\lambda^{n-1}).

Remark 1.8
.

Observe that the integrals (
1.9
) and (
1.10
) could be re-expressed as integrals over

\Omega\cap\overline{p}^{-1}(I(\lambda,\overline{c}))
thanks to the fiber rank
n
condition making
\overline{p}

a diffeomorphism on the fibers
T^{*}_{x}M.
This would result in expressions closer to those in
[
Hör68
]
,
covering the case of a single elliptic operator, which involved integrating over sub-level sets of the symbol.
However, this would result in more complicated phases and amplitudes in the referenced equations.

1.4.
Discussion of Theorem
1

1.4.1.
New information and implications

In the process of proving the main result, we spend a considerable portion of this article deriving the asymptotics of a (microlocalized)
smoothed joint spectral measure,
\rho(P_{1}-c_{1}\lambda)\circ\dots\circ\rho(P_{n}-c_{n}\lambda)
,
encapsulated in Prop.
6.1
, which is itself possibly of independent interest.
In fact, this result on the smoothed spectral measure immediately implies that joint eigenfunctions,
microlocalized to
\Omega
enjoy
L^{2}-L^{\infty}
bounds
which are
\mathcal{O}(1)
, a considerable improvement over the standard Hörmander bound of
\mathcal{O}(\lambda^{1/2})
.
This recovers a result of Tacy
[
Tac19
, Thm. 0.2]
under the same fiber-rank condition, which generalized previous work of
Sarnak
[
SarMor
]
and Marshall
[
Mar16
]
on locally symmetric spaces. We also mention the improved sup-norm
bounds of Galkowski-Toth
[
GT20
]
under a Morse-type assumption which is less stringent than our fiber rank condition.

It would be interesting to see whether our explicit description of the kernel lends itself to improved
L^{p}
restriction estimates,
along the lines of
[
BGT07
,
Hu09
]
,
for submanifolds that lie within
proj_{M}(\Omega)
.
Such a question for restriction to curves in 2D was posed by Toth, with our result being consistent with

[
Toth09
, Theorem 3 (i)]
, given that we are using a microlocal cutoff.

The content of the previous two paragraphs
demonstrate a well-known phenomenon in 2D, most conveniently visualized on surfaces of revolution:
the “zonals”, “Gaussian beams”,
and the “lone”
e^{i\theta\cdot\xi_{\theta}}
Fourier element (see Section
2
) are what contribute to the
non-trivial sup-norm growth due to the
\mathbb{S}^{1}
action.
What is new here is that despite eliminating the
L^{p}
-norm saturating
basis elements and the lone Fourier element, we still obtain a pointwise
\sim\lambda^{n}
asymptotic in Theorem 1.

It should not be surprising that the fiber rank condition can be framed through the moment map
\overline{p}
;
as mentioned in Remark
1.3
, it is equivalent with

\pi:\Sigma\rightarrow M
being a submersion for each level set
\Sigma
of
\overline{p}
.
We emphasize that, at least to the knowledge of the authors, the only setting where
\Omega
can be
taken as all of
T^{*}M
(and therefore
\pi|_{\Sigma}
is a global diffeomorphism) is when
M
is the flat torus
(see
[
GT20
, p. 919]
and
[
TZ03
, p. 17]
). In fact, by topological considerations
[
TZ03
]
,
it should be rare for a QCI system to have its
\Omega
equal to all of
T^{*}M
,
thus making microlocalization by
\Psi
reasonably motivated.
For the torus one could choose the cutoff
\Psi
not microlocally but via the spectral theorem and functional calculus.
The resulting
\Psi
allows us to count eigenvalues in cones contained inside of
I(\lambda,\overline{c})
, obtaining a
non-microlocalized pointwise analogue of (
1.9
) in that setting.

We continue our discussion by revisiting a result of Duistermaat
[
Dui74
, Thm. 1.1.1]
on quasimodes for semiclassical
pseudodifferential operators
P(x,\frac{1}{\lambda}D_{x})
. Under the version of our fiber rank assumption on the principal symbol of
a single Hamiltonian
P
, i.e.,
k=1
, Duistermaat showed that
P
admits
O(\lambda^{-\infty})
quasimodes in the form of WKB/
Lagrangian states. Our Prop.
6.1
gives a (microlocalized) QCI version of this result when

y
is fixed and
x
is allowed to vary: that is, the QCI system
\{P_{1},\dots,P_{n}\}

admit
\mathcal{O}(\lambda^{-\infty})
(microlocalized) joint quasimodes in the form of WKB/Lagrangian states.

1.4.2.
Future questions

One of the original motivations of this article was the connection between pointwise Weyl laws and zero sets of random linear
combinations of joint eigenfunctions. Statistics of the number of connected components of the zero sets of random functions is an area
with a wealth of challenging and difficult questions, with a beautiful piece of science from Bogomolny-Schmitt
[
BS07
]
addressing the
connection between bond percolation and the complements of zero sets of random eigenfunctions (that is, their
nodal domains
).
Two fundamental works of Nazarov-Sodin
[
NS09
,
NS16
]
concerned combinations of eigenfunctions
for the single operator of

-\Delta_{g}
(and say sampled from the spectral interval
[\lambda,\lambda+M]
where
M
is constant but sufficiently large) and their
nodal domains. These breakthrough articles established, amongst other results, asymptotics for the expected number of nodal domains
for large
\lambda
. The work
[
NS16
]
gave an important criterion for such an asymptotic to hold, specifically that the spectral
projector’s Schwartz kernel in (
1.2
) has its integration measure be absent of atoms (in the terminology of

[
NS16
]
, its “spectral measure”
\rho
should have no atoms), which is clearly the case for the Laplacian.
A variety of other interesting works were motivated by
[
NS16
]
;
see the survey article of Wigman
[
Wig23
]
and the references therein.

In the case of ensembles of joint eigenfunctions, say whose eigenvalues are sampled from our conic regions
\overline{p}(\Omega)
, a
description that could be used to verify Nazarov-Sodin’s criterion has been absent. A motivation for proving Theorem 1
was to give a non-trivial piece of evidence
that the actual joint projector’s kernel will satisfy this criterion.
Our hope is to suggest new random ensembles of eigenfunctions, smaller
than those considered by Nazarov-Sodin, but sharing their asymptotic.
We believe that QCI systems are a setting
in which the exponential concentration of nodal domain counting functions might be possible;
to prove see
[
NS09
,
Roz17
]
for the integrable cases
of
\mathbb{S}^{2}
and the torus.
This an interesting question for further research.

1.5.
Outline of the proof

A major obstruction encountered in the QCI case, say when trying to follow most proofs of pointwise Weyl laws for single elliptic,
self-adjoint operators, is the absence of
amenable oscillatory expressions for the joint propagator
e^{it_{1}P_{1}}\circ\dots\circ e^{it_{n}P_{n}}
. That is, it is difficult in general to
control the multiple integrations needed when manipulating the various Schwartz kernels of the individual propagators. The key idea
towards Theorem
1
is a microlocal normal form due to Colin de Verdière
[
CdV79
, Thm. 2.1]
which generalizes that
of Duistermaat-Hörmander
[
DH71
, Prop. 6.1.4]
in the case of a single operator. This normal form
accomplishes a few things:
thanks to the power of the FIO calculus
[
Hör71
]
,
it allows us in Section
4

to rely almost solely on explicit calculations for the joint propagator of the model QCI system

\{D_{x_{1}},\dots,D_{x_{n}}\}
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
The FIO calculus for operators associated to local canonical
graphs supplies the generating functions
S
for certain symplectomorphisms attached to
\{P_{1},\dots,P_{n}\}
appearing in
the expression (
1.9
); see Propositions
4.1
and
4.2
for more details. These
advantages emerge in our calculation of the Schwartz kernel of the smoothed spectral measure

\rho(P_{1}-c_{1}\lambda)\circ\dots\circ\rho(P_{n}-c_{n}\lambda)
which appears in Section
6
.

The passage from the smoothed spectral measure
\rho(P_{1}-c_{1}\lambda)\circ\dots\circ\rho(P_{n}-c_{n}\lambda)
to the pointwise
Weyl law (
1.9
) comes, as usual, from an appropriate Tauberian theorem. Our approach is to adapt a robust
argument of Sogge
[
Sog17
, Section 3.2]
, for integrated Weyl laws in the single-operator case, to multiple operators. Such
a strategy was used previously in the work of Keeler
[
Kee23
]
on pointwise Weyl laws for Laplacians in settings with non-
positive curvature. Our relevant results, and the final steps of the proof of Theorem
1
,
are described in Section
7
.

Liouville tori

In a slightly different vein, consider the two-torus

3.
The Joint Projector and the Joint Wave Kernel

In this section, we review how to relate the joint spectral projection kernel to the joint wave kernel.
For
\overline{t}=(t_{1},\dots,t_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, consider the Schwartz kernel of the joint propagator,

e^{i\overline{t}\cdot\overline{P}}(x,y):=e^{it_{1}P_{1}}\circ\dots\circ e^{it_{n}P_{n}}(x,y).

Recalling the image of the moment map,
\Gamma
, from (
1.6
),
let
\overline{c}\in\Gamma\cap\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
(to be chosen later) with all
c_{i}\neq 0
.
Let
\Psi
be an order-zero pseudodifferential operator. Then, we can rewrite the microlocalized joint spectral function as

(3.1)

\Psi\Pi_{\lambda,\overline{c}}\Psi^{*}(x,y)=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\infty}\mathds{1}_{I(\lambda,\overline{c})}\left(\overline{\lambda}_{j}\right)\left(\Psi\varphi_{j}\right)(x)\left(\overline{\Psi\varphi_{j}(y)}\right).

The Fourier transform of the characteristic function
\mathds{1}_{[-\lambda,\lambda]}
is

\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-it\mu}\mathds{1}_{[-\lambda,\lambda]}(\mu)\,d\mu=\int\limits_{-\lambda}^{\lambda}e^{-it\mu}\,d\mu=\frac{2\sin(\lambda t)}{t},

while the inverse Fourier (cosine) transform of
\sin(\lambda\cdot)/\cdot
at
\lambda
equals
1/2
.
To avoid this issue, we demand that
\overline{c}
be chosen so that
\lambda\overline{c}
is
not in the joint spectrum
\Lambda
for sufficiently large
\lambda
.
It is possible to do this, since
\Lambda
is discrete: given

\overline{c}\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, there exists
R_{0}(\overline{c})>0
and a corresponding

\{\lambda(l)\}_{l\in\mathbb{N}}\subset B(0,R_{0})^{\complement}
such that
\lambda(l)\overline{c}\notin\Lambda
.
Such a choice of spectral parameters is also made in
[
Sog17
, p. 53]

Therefore, through this Fourier inversion, we can rewrite
\Psi\Pi_{\lambda,\overline{c}}\Psi^{*}(x,y)
as

\Psi\Pi_{\lambda,\overline{c}}\Psi^{*}(x,y)=\frac{1}{\pi^{n}}\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\infty}\left(\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{i\lambda_{j}^{(1)}t_{1}/|c_{1}|}\dotsm e^{i\lambda_{j}^{(n)}t_{n}/|c_{n}|}\frac{\sin(\lambda t_{1})}{t_{1}}\dotsm\frac{\sin(\lambda t_{n})}{t_{n}}\,dt\right)\Psi\varphi_{j}(x)\overline{\Psi\varphi_{j}(y)}.

Changing variables via
t_{k}\mapsto|c_{k}|t_{k}
, we obtain

\Psi\Pi_{\lambda,\overline{c}}\Psi^{*}(x,y)=\frac{1}{\pi^{n}}\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\infty}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{i\overline{t}\cdot{\overline{\lambda}}_{j}}\frac{\sin(\lambda t_{1}|c_{1}|)}{t_{1}}\dotsm\frac{\sin(\lambda t_{n}|c_{n}|)}{t_{n}}\Psi\varphi_{j}(x)\overline{\Psi\varphi_{j}(y)}\,dt.

Since we are working at the level of Schwartz kernels, we can interchange the sum and the integral to obtain

\Psi\Pi_{\lambda,\overline{c}}\Psi^{*}(x,y)=\left(\frac{1}{\pi^{n}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\sin(\lambda t_{1}|c_{1}|)}{t_{1}}\dotsm\frac{\sin(\lambda t_{n}|c_{n}|)}{t_{n}}\left(\Psi\circ e^{it_{1}P_{1}}\circ\dotsm\circ e^{it_{n}P_{n}}\circ\Psi^{*}\right)\,d\overline{t}\right)(x,y).

It is on this expression for (
3.1
) that we perform the Tauberian analysis in
Section
7
.

Now let
\overline{\mu}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. A similar application of spectral calculus allows us to rewrite the spectral measure kernel
(in the sense of distributions) as

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\infty}\delta(\overline{\mu}-{\overline{\lambda}}_{j})\Psi\varphi_{j}(x)\overline{\Psi\varphi_{j}(y)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{i\overline{t}\cdot\overline{\mu}}\left(\Psi\circ e^{-it_{1}P_{1}}\circ\dotsm\circ e^{-it_{n}P_{n}}\circ\Psi^{*}\right)(x,y)\,d\overline{t}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Psi e^{i\overline{t}\cdot(\overline{\mu}-\overline{P})}\Psi^{*}\,d\overline{t}\right)(x,y).

Integrating in
\overline{\mu}
over
I(\lambda,\overline{c})
recovers the spectral projector
function in (
3.1
).

Since the formula for the microlocalized spectral measure only holds in the sense of Schwartz kernels,
as the integral in
\overline{t}
is not
absolutely convergent, we regularize it by introducing a Schwartz function
\rho\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R})
, even and
such that
\textnormal{supp\,}\widehat{\rho}\subset[-\delta_{0},\delta_{0}]

with
\widehat{\rho}(s)\equiv 1
for
|s|<\delta_{0}/2
. (The value of
\delta_{0}
will be specified in Section
7.3
.)
Then, by
properties of the Fourier transform and the spectral calculus,
we have that

\displaystyle\Psi\rho(\mu_{1}-P_{1})\dotsm\rho(\mu_{n}-P_{n})\Psi^{*}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Psi e^{i\overline{t}\cdot(\overline{\mu}-{\overline{P}})}\Psi^{*}\widehat{\rho}(t_{1})\dotsm\widehat{\rho}(t_{n})\,d\overline{t}\right)(x,y)

The goal of the next three sections is to analyze the asymptotic behavior of this
microlocalized smoothed spectral measure
using
microlocal normal forms. We then use a Tauberian statement to relate this smoothed measure to (
3.1
),
thus proving Theorem
1
.

4.
Microlocal Normal Forms for Joint Propagators

One of the key facts used in the analysis of the microlocalized joint spectral function is the fact that we can microlocally transform
the operators
\{P_{1},\dotsc,P_{n}\}
into simply
\{D_{x_{1}},\dotsc,D_{x_{n}}\}
acting on open sets in Euclidean space. This is a
consequence of the following well-known result in symplectic geometry (that is, the homogeneous Darboux theorem), which states that
commuting Hamiltonians can be locally “straightened out” into a convenient normal form.
Recall the notations for
\overline{P}
and
\overline{P^{0}}
set out in Section
1.6
.

Proposition 4.1
.

(
[
DH71
, Prop. 6.1.3]
)
Let
k\leq n
. Let
p_{1},\dotsc,p_{k}
be real-valued
C^{\infty}
functions, homogeneous of degree 1 on a conic neighborhood of

(x_{0},\xi^{0})\in T^{*}M\setminus 0
. Then, for the existence of a homogeneous canonical transformation

(4.1)

\chi(x,\xi)=(X_{1}(x,\xi),\dotsc,X_{n}(x,\xi);\,\Xi_{1}(x,\xi),\dotsc,\Xi_{n}(x,\xi))

from a conic neighborhood
U
of
(x_{0},\xi^{0})
to a conic neighborhood
V
of
(0,\Xi^{0})\in T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus 0
such that

(4.2)

p_{j}(x,\xi)=\Xi_{j}(x,\xi),\quad j=1,\dotsc,k,

it is both necessary and sufficient to have that

(i)

the Poisson brackets satisfy
\{p_{i},p_{j}\}=0
for
i,j=1,2,\dotsc,k
in a neighborhood of
(x_{0},\xi^{0})
; and

(ii)

H_{p_{1}}(x_{0},\xi^{0}),\dotsc,H_{p_{k}}(x_{0},\xi^{0})
and the radial vector
Y(x_{0};\xi^{0}):=\sum_{1}^{n}\xi_{i}^{0}\partial_{\xi_{i}}

are linearly independent.

The next proposition states the quantized version of the preceding result. This proposition appears in
[
CdV79
]
, but we present a
somewhat restructured proof here because it provides explicit constructions of the amplitude functions
which appear in the statement of Theorem
1
.

Proposition 4.2
.

(
[
CdV79
, Thm. 2.1]
)
Let
\overline{P}=\left(P_{1},\dots,P_{n}\right)
be a QCI system on
\Omega_{0}\subset T^{*}M\setminus 0
and

(x_{0};\xi^{0})\in\Omega_{0}
.
Then there exists a conic neighborhood
U
of
(x_{0},\xi^{0})
and a homogeneous canonical transformation

\chi:U\rightarrow T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus 0
such that the conic neighborhood
V:=\chi(U)

7.1.
On-diagonal Cluster Bounds

For
{\overline{\mu}}\in\Gamma
from (
1.6
), define the cube

(7.1)

R_{\overline{\mu}}:=\{\overline{\tau}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\,\tau_{k}\in[\mu_{k},\mu_{k}+1]\,\text{ for each }k=1,2,\dotsc,n\}.

In this section, we aim to show for certain
\Psi\in\Psi^{0}(M)
, on-diagonal upper bounds for the microlocalized unit-box projector kernel

(7.2)

\Psi\Pi_{R_{{\overline{\mu}}}}\Psi^{*}(x,x)=\sum\limits_{\{j:\,\overline{\lambda_{j}}\in R_{{\overline{\mu}}}\}}|\Psi\varphi_{j}(x)|^{2},

follow from similar bounds on the microlocalized smoothed spectral measure restricted to the diagonal:

\Psi\beta(\overline{P}-{\overline{\mu}})\Psi^{*}(x,x)=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\infty}\beta(\overline{\lambda}_{j}-{\overline{\mu}})|\Psi\varphi_{j}(x)|^{2},

for an appropriate
\beta\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. The exposition in this section proceeds in analogy to
[
Sog14
, §3.2]
, which
gives a robust Tauberian argument in the single-operator case that can be used to justify similar statements from
[
Hör68
]
.

We choose a cutoff function
\beta\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that

\beta\geq 0,\quad\beta(0)=1,\quad\widehat{\beta}(\overline{t})\equiv 0\text{ for }|\overline{t}|\geq\delta,

for some
\delta>0
to be specified later. We claim that upper bounds for
\Psi\beta(\overline{P}-{\overline{\mu}})\Psi^{*}
restricted to the
diagonal imply similar estimates for
\Psi\Pi_{R_{\overline{\mu}}}\Psi^{*}(x,x).

Lemma 7.1
.

Suppose that given some
\Psi\in\Psi^{0}(M)
, there exists an open set
\tilde{\omega}\subseteq M
,
a cone
\Gamma^{\prime}\subset\Gamma
, a compact set
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and constants
C>0

and
\overline{m}\in(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0})^{n}
such that

\Psi\beta(\overline{P}-{\overline{\mu}})\Psi^{*}(x,x)\leq C(1+{\overline{\mu}})^{\overline{m}}:=\Pi_{k=1}^{n}(1+\mu_{k})^{m_{k}}

for all
x\in\tilde{\omega}
and all
\,{\overline{\mu}}\in\Gamma^{\prime}\setminus K
. Then, there exists a constant
C^{\prime}>0
so that

\Psi\Pi_{R_{\overline{\mu}}}\Psi^{*}(x,x)\leq C^{\prime}(1+{\overline{\mu}})^{\overline{m}}

for all
x\in\tilde{\omega}
and all
\,{\overline{\mu}}\in\Gamma^{\prime}\setminus K.

Proof.

Since
\beta(0)=1
, there exists an
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that
\beta(\overline{\tau})\geq\frac{1}{2}
whenever
|\overline{\tau}|\leq\varepsilon_{0}.

If we define the
\epsilon_{0}
neighborhood of
R_{\overline{\mu}}
in the box metric,

R_{{\overline{\mu}},\varepsilon_{0}}:=[\mu_{1}-\varepsilon_{0},\mu_{1}+\varepsilon_{0}]\times\dotsm\times[\mu_{n}-\varepsilon_{0},\mu_{n}+\varepsilon_{0}],

then for
{\overline{\mu}}\in\Gamma^{\prime}\setminus K
and any
x\in\omega
,

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\{j:\,\overline{\lambda}_{j}\in R_{{{\overline{\mu}}},\varepsilon_{0}}\}}|\Psi\varphi_{j}(x)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{\{j:\,\overline{\lambda}_{j}\in R_{{\overline{\mu}},\varepsilon_{0}}\}}2\beta(\overline{\lambda}_{j}-{\overline{\mu}})|\Psi\varphi_{j}(x)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\infty}2\beta(\overline{\lambda}_{j}-{\overline{\mu}})|\Psi\varphi_{j}(x)|^{2}

\displaystyle=2\Psi\beta(\overline{P}-{\overline{\mu}})\Psi^{*}(x,x)

\displaystyle\leq 2C(1+{\overline{\mu}})^{\overline{m}},

where the final inequality follows by our hypothesis on
\Psi\beta(\overline{P}-{\overline{\mu}})\Psi^{*}
. Next, note that there exists an
N\in\mathbb{N}

such that for any
{\overline{\mu}}\in\Gamma^{\prime}\setminus K,
there exists a finite collection of points
\{{\overline{\mu}}_{k}\}_{k=1}^{N}
such that

R_{\overline{\mu}}\subseteq\bigcup\limits_{k=1}^{N}R_{{\overline{\mu}}_{k},\varepsilon_{0}}
, with the property that
|{\overline{\mu}}-{\overline{\mu}}_{k}|\leq 1
for all

. Then, by
nonnegativity and our previous calculations, we have that for
{\overline{\mu}}\in\Gamma^{\prime}\setminus K
and

\displaystyle\Psi\Pi_{R_{\overline{\mu}}}\Psi^{*}(x,x)

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{k=1}^{N}\Psi\Pi_{R_{{\overline{\mu}}_{k},\varepsilon_{0}}}\Psi^{*}(x,x)

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{k=1}^{N}2C(1+{\overline{\mu}}_{k})^{\overline{m}}

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime}(1+{\overline{\mu}})^{\overline{m}}

for some
C^{\prime}>0
, which completes the proof. Note that in the above argument it is critical that
C
and
N
be independent of both
{\overline{\mu}},{\overline{\mu}}_{k}
and
\varepsilon_{0}.

∎

Lemma 7.5

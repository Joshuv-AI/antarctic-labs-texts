Abstract
. We investigate the pressureless fractional Euler-alignment system with nonlinear velocity couplings, referred to as the p p -Euler-alignment system. This model features a nonlinear velocity alignment force, interpreted as a density-weighted fractional p p -Laplacian when the singularity parameter α \alpha exceeds the spatial dimension d d . Our primary goal is to establish the existence of solutions for strongly singular interactions ( α ≥ d \alpha\geq d ) and compactly supported initial conditions. We construct solutions as mean-field limits of empirical measures from a kinetic variant of the p p -Euler-alignment system. Specifically, we show that a sequence of empirical measures converges to a finite Radon measure, whose local density and velocity satisfy the p p -Euler-alignment system. Our results are the first to prove the existence of solutions to this system in multi-dimensional settings without significant initial data restrictions, covering both nonlinear ( p > 2 p>2 ) and linear ( p = 2 p=2 ) cases. Additionally, we establish global existence, uniqueness, and collision avoidance for the corresponding particle ODE system under non-collisional initial conditions, extending previous results for 1 ≤ p ≤ α + 2 1\leq p\leq\alpha+2 . This analysis supports our mean-field limit argument and contributes to understanding alignment models with singular communication.



1.
Introduction

Given
p\geq 2
, we consider the pressureless fractional Euler-alignment system with nonlinear velocity couplings:

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho+\text{div}_{x}(\rho u)=0,\\
\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\rho u)+\text{div}_{x}(\rho u\otimes u)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\left|u(y)-u(x)\right|^{p-2}(u(y)-u(x))}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}\rho(y)\rho(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y,\end{array}\right.

which we refer to as the
p
-Euler-alignment system. Here, the pair
(\rho(t,x),u(t,x))
represents the density and velocity of agents occupying position
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
at time
t\geq 0
. We use the notation
\rho_{t}(x):=\rho(t,x)
. The right-hand side of the momentum equation constitutes the nonlinear velocity alignment force, which in the case
\alpha>d
can be interpreted as a
\rho
-weighted fractional
p
-Laplacian,
(-\Delta)^{(\alpha-d)/2}_{p}
. We refer to
[
49
]
for general discussion on the system (
1.1

Our objective is to demonstrate, under the strong singularity assumption

\alpha\geq d,

that given a compactly supported initial density
\rho_{0}
and a bounded initial velocity
u_{0}
, a solution
(\rho,u)
(initiated at
(\rho_{0},u_{0})
) can be constructed as a mean-field limit of empirical measures. More precisely, there exists a sequence of empirical measures
\mu^{N}
, solving a kinetic variant of (
1.1
), which converges weakly to some finite Radon measure
\mu
, whose local density and local velocity

(1.2)

\rho_{t}(x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mu_{t}(x,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}v)\quad\mbox{and}\quad u(t,x):=\frac{1}{\rho_{t}(x)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}v\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}(x,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}v),

satisfy the
p
-Euler-alignment system (
1.1
). Denoting the space of probability measures as
{\mathcal{P}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, the main result can be stated as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
T>0
,
\rho_{0}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be compactly supported and

belong to

constitute a weak solution to the

-Euler-alignment system (

(2)

The significance of the

-Euler-alignment system lies in its intriguing large-time behavior, recently studied by

Establishing well-posedness, including existence, for the multidimensional fractional Euler-alignment system with

The recent work

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}|x-x^{\prime}|^{2-\alpha}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho_{t}(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho_{t}(x^{\prime}),

Adequate control of (

We construct the candidate for the solution

\mu_{t}^{N}(x,v):=\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{x_{i}^{N}(t)}(x)\otimes\delta_{v_{i}^{N}(t)}(v)\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{2d}).

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\dot{x}_{i}=v_{i},\\
\dot{v}_{i}=\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i\neq j=1}^{N}\frac{|v_{j}-v_{i}|^{p-2}(v_{j}-v_{i})}{|x_{i}-x_{j}|^{\alpha}},\end{array}\right.\qquad(x_{i}(0),v_{i}(0))=(x_{i0},v_{i0})\in\mathbb{R}^{2d},

where

is the position and velocity of

. Thus, the need arises to study well-posedness for (

. Nonlinear velocity coupling as in (

[

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\alpha\geq 1
and

. Suppose the initial data

Then, for

Remark 1.3

The range of

1.2.
Hydrodynamic limit and the role of monokineticity

Formally, macroscopic equations (
1.1
) can be obtained by taking the first two
v-
moments of the kinetic equation

(1.7)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\partial_{t}\mu+v\cdot\nabla_{x}\mu+\text{div}_{v}\left(F(\mu)\mu\right)=0,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\;v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\\
\displaystyle F(\mu)(t,x,v):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\frac{|w-v|^{p-2}(w-v)}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}\mu(t,y,w)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y,\end{array}\right.

where
\mu=\mu(t,x,v)
is the distribution of particles passing through
(x,v)\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}
at time
t\geq 0
. Thus, integrating the equation (
1.7
) in
v
and exploiting the skew-symmetry of
F(\mu)
, we obtain the continuity equation (
1.1
)
1
, for the local quantities (
1.2
).

To obtain the momentum equation, we take the first
v-
moment of kinetic equation
(
1.7
)
. Formal derivation of the Euler-alignment system can be found in
[
26
,
13
]
, while for rigorous limit from the kinetic to the hydrodynamical description we recommend
[
23
]
(pressureless limit) and
[
29
]
(Euler-alignment with pressure).
Direct micro-to-macroscopic mean-field limit was performed by the first author and
Carrillo
in
[
6
]
.

While computing the first local velocity moment of (
1.7
) we encounter two problems. The first one is related to the transport term
v\cdot\nabla_{x}\mu
. Multiplying it by
v
and integrating, we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}v\otimes v\mu\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}v=\rho u\otimes u+\mathbb{P},

where the pressure term
\mathbb{P}
, still kinetic and dependent on
v
, reads

\mathbb{P}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(v-u)\otimes(v-u)\mu\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}v.

Secondly, the right-hand side in (
1.1
)
2
becomes highly nonlinear and kinetic:

\rho|w-u|^{p-2}(w-u)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|w-v|^{p-2}(w-v)\mu(t,x,v)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}v.

Therefore we arrive at the following situation. Firstly, while dealing with the singularity of the communication weight, it is more convenient to work with the hydrodynamical description of (
1.1
), since then the local velocity
u
better counteracts the singularity. Secondly, on the hydrodynamical level three additional problems appear: the emergence of nonlinear convective term and nonlinear velocity couplings (both of which are linear on the kinetic level!) as well the justification of the monokinetic ansatz, required to ensure that
{\mathbb{P}}=0
. All of these problems become manageable as soon as we can show that the strong singularity forces monokineticity, as we do using Theorem
2.8
(see preliminaries below).
Using notation developed in Section
2.2
we prove that the candidate for a solution to (
1.7
) is of the monokinetic form
\mu(t,x,v)=\delta_{u(t,x)}(v)\otimes\rho_{t}(x)\otimes\lambda^{1}(t)
, which makes it both kinetic and hydrodynamical at the same time. Thus, we can simultaneously deal with hydrodynamical nonlinearity at the kinetic level and with problems related to the singularity of communication at the hydrodynamical level.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we introduce the necessary preliminaries, including weak formulations and general results related to monokineticity. We employ the following strategy. We aim to prove that atomic solutions (
1.5
) exist and converge, up to a subsequence to a candidate
\mu
for a solution of (
1.7
). Then using both good properties of (
1.7
) and (
1.1
) we show that even though
\mu
does not necessarily solve (
1.7
), its local quantities (
1.2
) constitute a solution to (
1.1
). Thus our first step in Section
3
is the proof of Theorem
1.2
and other information regarding the particle system, ensuring the existence of atomic solutions. In Section
4
, we prove intermediate results regarding the compactness of the atomic solutions, which play a crucial role in the mean-field limit. In Section
5
, we show that any limit measure obtained by the mean-field method is monokinetic and its local quantities satisfy
p-
Euler-alignment system, which constitutes a proof of Theorem
1.1
. Some technical results are relegated to the Appendix.

Notation

Throughout the paper
{\mathcal{P}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
{\mathcal{M}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
{\mathcal{M}}_{+}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
denote the space of probability measures, finite Radon measures and nonnegative finite Radon measures on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, respectively. Given
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{M}_{+}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, the bounded-Lipschitz distance
d_{BL}(\mu,\nu)
between them is defined as

d_{BL}(\mu,\nu):=\sup\limits_{\varphi\in\Gamma_{BL}}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\nu\right|,

where
\Gamma_{BL}
is the set of admissible test functions

\Gamma_{BL}:=\left\{\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\longmapsto\mathbb{R}:||\varphi||_{\infty}\leq 1,\ [\varphi]_{Lip}:=\sup\limits_{x\neq y}\frac{|\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)|}{|x-y|}\leq 1\right\}.

Since all the measures considered throughout the paper are uniformly compactly supported, it is worthwhile to note that, for such measures, the bounded-Lipschitz distance induces topology equivalent to the narrow topology, characterised by convergence tested with bounded-continuous functions.

We use the notation

\mathbb{R}^{d}_{x}
and
\mathbb{R}^{d}_{v}

to emphasize that these are the spaces of position
x
and velocity
v
, respectively. To describe disintegrations of measures, such as

\mu(t,x,v)=\delta_{u(t,x)}(v)\otimes\rho_{t}(x)\otimes\lambda^{1}(t)
or
[\mu_{t}\otimes\mu_{t}]\otimes\lambda^{1}(t)
we use the notation from Section
2.2
and particularly formula (
2.5
).

For a Borel-measurable mapping
F:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
and a Radon measure
\mu
we define the
pushforward measure

F_{\#}\mu
by

(1.8)

F_{\#}(\mu)(B):=\mu\left(F^{-1}(B)\right)\quad\text{for Borel sets }B.

The main property of the pushforward measure, used throughout the paper, is the change of variables formula

(1.9)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}(F_{\#}\mu)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g\circ F\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu,

whenever
g\circ F
is
\mu
-integrable.

Finally, throughout the paper, we use the notation
A\lesssim B
to state that there exists a ”harmless” positive constant
C
, such that
A\leq C\ B
.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we introduce the main mathematical tools utilised in the paper, including weak formulations for meso- and macroscopic systems.

2.1.
Weak formulations

We begin by introducing the kinetic weak formulation.

Definition 2.1

(
Weak solution of the kinetic equation
)
.

For a fixed
0<T<\infty
we say that
\mu=\{\mu_{t}\}_{t\in[0,T]}\in C\left([0,T];(\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{2d}),d_{BL})\right)
is a weak measure-valued solution of (
1.7
) initiated in
\mu_{0}\in\mathcal{P}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2d}\right)
if the following assertions are satisfied.

(i)

There exists a constant
M>0
such that for a.a.
t\in[0,T]
we have

\text{spt}(\mu_{t})\subset\subset(T+1)B(M)\times B(M)\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}_{x}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}_{v}.

In other words, the
v
-support of
\mu_{t}
is compactly contained in the ball
B(M)
, while the
x
-support of
\mu_{t}
is compactly contained in
(T+1)B(M)
.

(ii)

For each
\varphi\in C^{1}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{2d})
, compactly supported in
[0,T)
with Lipschitz continuous
\nabla_{v}\varphi
in
B(M)
, the following identity holds

(2.1)

\begin{split}&-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\varphi(0,x,v)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu_{0}(x,v)=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\left(\partial_{t}\varphi(t,x,v)+v\cdot\nabla_{x}\varphi(t,x,v)\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}(x,v)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\\
&+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4d}\setminus\Delta}\frac{\left(\nabla_{v}\varphi(t,x,v)-\nabla_{v}\varphi(t,x^{\prime},v^{\prime})\right)\cdot(v-v^{\prime})|v-v^{\prime}|^{p-2}}{|x-x^{\prime}|^{\alpha}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}[\mu_{t}(x,v)\otimes\mu_{t}(x^{\prime},v^{\prime})]\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t,\end{split}

where
\Delta=\{(x,v,x^{\prime},v^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}^{4d}:\ x=x^{\prime}\}
is the diagonal set.
In particular, all integrals in the above equation are well-defined.

Remark 2.2
.

We treat the weak formulation above mostly as a stepping stone to the macroscopic scale, which we describe carefully in the next subsection. Nevertheless, the following comments are in order. They follow, mutatis mutandis, similar statements for the
p=2
case in
[
22
]
. Assume that for a fixed
0<T<\infty
we have
\mu_{0}
and
\mu
as above. Then:

i)

Solutions
\mu
in the sense of Definition
2.1
satisfy the energy equality

(2.2)

\int_{0}^{t}D_{p}[\mu_{s}]\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s=E[\mu_{0}]-E[\mu_{t}]

, where

(2.3)

\begin{split}E[\mu_{t}]&:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}|v|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}(x,v),\\
D_{p}[\mu_{t}]&:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4d}\setminus\Delta}\frac{|v-v^{\prime}|^{p}}{|x-x^{\prime}|^{\alpha}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\left[\mu_{t}(x,v)\otimes\mu_{t}(x^{\prime},v^{\prime})\right],\end{split}

are the kinetic energy and its dissipation rate (sometimes called enstrophy), respectively.
Conversely, if (
2.2
) is satisfied, then all the terms in (
2.1
) are well-defined. At the macroscopic level, this equality translates into inequality and no longer is a consequence of a weak formulation, therefore we include it in Definition
2.5
.

ii)

For

, solutions

in the sense of Definition

\text{spt}(\rho_{t})\subset\subset(T+1)B(M)\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}_{x}\quad\text{and}\quad\lVert u(t,\cdot)\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\rho_{t})}<M;

(ii)

For a.a.
t\in[0,T]

\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}\setminus\Delta}\frac{|u(t,x)-u(t,x^{\prime})|^{p}}{|x-x^{\prime}|^{\alpha}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\left[\rho_{s}(x)\otimes\rho_{s}(x^{\prime})\right]\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u(0,x)|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho_{0}(x)-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u(t,x)|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho_{t}(x);

(iii)

For each
\varphi\in C^{1}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
and each
\varphi_{d}\in C^{1}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
, compactly supported in
[0,T)
, the following equations are satisfied

(2.4)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\partial_{t}\varphi+\nabla_{x}\varphi\cdot u\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho_{t}(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi(0,x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho_{0}(x)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u(0,x)\cdot\varphi_{d}(0,x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho_{0}(x)+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\partial_{t}\varphi_{d}\cdot u+u\left(u\cdot\nabla_{x}\right)\varphi_{d}\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho_{t}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}\setminus\Delta}\frac{(\varphi_{d}(t,x)-\varphi_{d}(t,x^{\prime}))\cdot(u(t,x)-u(t,x^{\prime}))|u(t,x)-u(t,x^{\prime})|^{p-2}}{|x-x^{\prime}|^{\alpha}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}[\rho_{t}\otimes\rho_{t}]\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t.

2.2.
Monokineticity

Next, we provide a rigorous introduction to the notion of monokineticity as well as primarily related tools. Before proceeding, we recall the disintegration theorem, see
[
1
]
for details.

Theorem 2.6

(
Disintegration theorem
)
.

For
d_{1},d_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
denote projection onto the second componenent as
\pi_{2}:\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}}
. For
\mu\in\mathcal{P}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}}\right)
, define its projection onto second factor as
\nu:=(\pi_{2})_{\#}\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}})
. Then there exists a family of probabilistic measures
\left\{\mu_{x_{2}}\right\}_{x_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}}}\subset\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}})
, defined uniquely
\nu
almost everywhere, such that

(i)

The map

\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}}\ni x_{2}\longmapsto\mu_{x_{2}}(B)

is Borel-measurable for each Borel set
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}
;

(ii)

The following formula

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}}}\varphi(x_{1},x_{2})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu(x_{1},x_{2})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}}\varphi(x_{1},x_{2})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu_{x_{2}}(x_{1})\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\nu(x_{2})

holds for every Borel-measurable map
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}}\longmapsto[0,\infty)
.

The family
\{\mu_{x_{2}}\}
is called a
disintegration
of
\mu
with respect to marginal distribution
\nu
. For the sake of simplicity, we often refer to formula
(ii)
above as

(2.5)

\mu(x_{1},x_{2})=\mu_{x_{2}}(x_{1})\otimes\nu(x_{2}).

Assume
\mu=\mu(t,x,v)
is a finite Radon measure on
[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{2d}
and that its
t
-marginal is the 1D Lebesgue measure on
[0,T]
, which we denote by
\lambda^{1}(t)
. By Theorem
2.6
, for a.a.
t\in[0,T]
there exists a measure
\mu_{t}(x,v)\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
such that

(2.6)

\mu(t,x,v)=\mu_{t}(x,v)\otimes\lambda^{1}(t)\qquad\mbox{(see notation \eqref{disint_form})}.

Disintegrating
\mu_{t}
further with respect to its local density
\rho_{t}(x)
(cf (
1.2
)), we obtain

(2.7)

\mu(t,x,v)=\sigma_{t,x}(v)\otimes\rho_{t}(x)\otimes\lambda^{1}(t),

where the measure
\sigma_{t,x}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{v})
, governing the local velocity distribution of
\mu
, is defined for a.a.
t\in[0,T]
and
\rho_{t}
-a.a.
x
. Throughout the paper, we identify
\mu
with the family
\{\mu_{t}\}_{t\in[0,T]}
, understood as above. We say that
\mu
is
monokinetic
if for
a.a.

t\in[0,T]
and
\rho_{t}-
a.a.
x
the measure
\sigma_{t,x}
is a Dirac delta concentrated in some
u(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, i.e.

(2.8)

\mu(t,x,v)=\delta_{u(t,x)}(v)\otimes\rho_{t}(x)\otimes\lambda^{1}(t).

Then
(t,x)\longmapsto u(t,x)
, defined for a.a.
t\in[0,T]
and
\rho_{t}
-a.a.
x
, plays the role of velocity.

The main tool that allows us to connect the kinetic and hydrodynamical levels is
[
22
, Theorem 1.1]
, which ensures the monokineticity of the measure
\mu
under the mild assumptions which we summarise in the following definition.

Definition 2.7
.

Assume that the measure
\mu
satisfies (
2.6
) and (
2.7
) and that
\mu_{t}
is compactly supported in
B(M)(T+1)\times B(M)
for a.a.
t\in[0,T]
, where
B(M)
denotes a ball of radius
M>0
centered at
0
.

We say that
\mu
is:

(a)

Locally Mass Preserving
(MP) if for any bounded set
C\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
and any
t_{0}\leq t

(2.9)

\rho_{t}\left(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muC\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu+(t-t_{0})\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muB(M)\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\right)\geq\rho_{t_{0}}(C);

(b)

Steadily Flowing
(SF) if there exists a full measure set
A\subset[0,T]
, such that for all
t_{0}\in A
and all
0\leq\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
the family of finite Radon measures
\rho_{t_{0},t}[\varphi]
defined for any Borel set
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
as

(2.10)

\rho_{t_{0},t}[\varphi](B):=\int_{B\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi(v)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}T^{t_{0},t}_{\#}\mu_{t}

is narrowly continuous at
t=t_{0}
, as a function of
t\in[0,T]
, restricted to
A
. Here
T^{t_{0},t}_{\#}\mu_{t}
is the pushforward measure defined by the mapping
T^{t_{0},t}(x,v)=(x-(t-t_{0})v,v)
, see notation in (
1.8
) and (
1.9
).

Theorem 2.8
.

Suppose that the family of uniformly compactly supported probability measures
\{\mu_{t}\}_{t\in[0,T]}
on
\mathbb{R}^{2d}

is locally mass-preserving and steadily flowing. Assume further that

(2.11)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}D^{\alpha}[\mu_{t}]\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t:=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4d}\setminus\{x=x^{\prime}\}}\frac{|v-v^{\prime}|^{\alpha+2}}{|x-x^{\prime}|^{\alpha}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}[\mu_{t}\otimes\mu_{t}]\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t<\infty,\qquad\alpha\geq d.

Then
\mu
is monokinetic, i.e. it admits the disintegration (
2.8
).

Remark 2.9
.

Definition
2.7
is motivated by
[
28
]
, wherein the authors assume the measure to be BV in time. Our weaker assumptions (MP) and (SF) are tailored to replace this time regularity and control the manner in which
\mu
dissipates mass. The pushforward measure
T^{t_{0},t}_{\#}\mu_{t}
introduced in (SF) transforms only the position, leaving velocity intact, and serves as the only concept of the flow that we can reasonably use. It needs to be introduced for the following reason. We aim to show that the limit of atomic solutions is monokinetic, and, unlike
[
28
]
, we do not a priori know that such limit solves any equation, and thus even the relation between
x
and
v
as ”position” and ”velocity” is unclear. Taking the pushforward with respect to
(x-(t-t_{0})v)
helps to recognize
v
as the velocity of the motion of position
x
.
At the same time, condition (MP) prevents loss of mass along such a flow. We point out that if
t\longmapsto\mu_{t}
is narrowly continuous or BV in time, both conditions are satisfied.

3.
Collision-avoidance

This section is dedicated to the well-posedness and collision-avoidance for system (
1.6
). It is the first step of our efforts since it ensures the existence of the approximation by atomic solutions. However, we begin by providing additional information such as growth conditions on the maximum position and velocity of the particles, as well as energy equality for the system.

Proposition 3.1

(Propagation of position and velocity)
.

Let
(x_{i}(t),v_{i}(t))_{i=1}^{N}
be a classical solution to (
1.6
) in
[0,T]
. Then we have

\sup_{t\geq 0}\max\limits_{1\leq i\leq N}|v_{i}(t)|\leq\max\limits_{1\leq i\leq N}|v_{i0}|,\quad\sup_{t\geq 0}\max\limits_{1\leq i\leq N}|x_{i}(t)|\leq\max\limits_{1\leq i\leq N}|x_{i_{0}}|+t\max\limits_{1\leq i\leq N}|v_{i0}|.

The proof follows the same idea as in the case of a standard CS model. We sketch it here for the reader’s convenience.

Proof.

The bound on the position follows immediately from the first equation in (
1.6
) and from the bound on the velocity. Thus, we focus on the bound on the velocity. Denote
V(t):=\max\limits_{1\leq i\leq N}|v_{i}(t)|
and note that it is an absolutely continuous function as a maximum of finitely many absolutely continuous functions. In particular, it is differentiable a.e., and its derivative satisfies the fundamental theorem of calculus. Fix a point
t_{0}
of differentiability of
V
and suppose that
K
is the set of all indices
i
such that at
t=t_{0}
we have
V(t)=|v_{i}(t)|
. Then
\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}V(t_{0})\leq\max_{i\in K}\frac{d}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}|v_{i}(t_{0})|
and for all
i\in K
,

\displaystyle\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}|v_{i}|^{2}

\displaystyle=v_{i}\cdot\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{i\neq j=1}^{N}\frac{|v_{j}-v_{i}|^{p-2}}{|x_{i}-x_{j}|^{\alpha}}(v_{j}-v_{i})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{i\neq j=1}^{N}\frac{|v_{j}-v_{i}|^{p-2}}{|x_{i}-x_{j}|^{\alpha}}(v_{j}\cdot v_{i}-|v_{i}|^{2})\leq 0,\quad\mbox{at }t=t_{0},

where the last inequality follows from the fact that every involved factor is nonnegative except

v_{j}(t_{0})\cdot v_{i}(t_{0})-|v_{i}(t_{0})|^{2}\leq V^{2}(t_{0})-V^{2}(t_{0})=0.

Thus,
\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}V(t_{0})\leq 0
in all points of differentiability of
V
. Since
V
is absolutely continuous, it implies that it is non-increasing.
∎

The above proposition implies the existence of a positive constant
M
such that

(3.1)

\sup_{t\geq 0}\sup\limits_{1\leq i\leq N}|v_{i}(t)|<M,\quad\sup_{t\geq 0}\sup\limits_{1\leq i\leq N}|x_{i}(t)|<(T+1)M.

Proposition 3.2

(Energy equality)
.

Let
(x_{i}(t),v_{i}(t))_{i=1}^{N}
be a classical solution to (
1.6
) in
[0,T]
. Then for all
t\in[0,T]
, we have

\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{N^{2}}\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{i\neq j=1}^{N}|v_{i}(s)-v_{j}(s)|^{p}|x_{i}(s)-x_{j}(s)|^{-\alpha}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}|v_{i}(0)|^{2}-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}|v_{i}(t)|^{2}.

Proof.

The proof is the same as in the classical case. One simply needs to calculate the time derivative of the quadratic energy
t\longmapsto\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}|v_{i}(t)|^{2}
and use the standard trick of exchanging the indices
i
and
j
in the double sum. We omit the details.
∎

Finally, we prove the main result related to the
p
-CS particle system (
1.6
), Theorem
1.2
, which combines well-posedness and collision-avoidance.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Local existence and uniqueness follow from Picard-Lindelöf theorem. Proposition
3.1
excludes the possibility of finite-time blowup, which implies that the only restriction to the lifespan of the solution can originate from collisions. Thus, to finish the proof, it suffices to show collision-avoidance. The cases of
p=2
and
2<p\leq 3
have been addressed in
[
8
]
and
[
31
]
, respectively, and thus we only consider
p>3
.

Aiming at a contradiction, assume that there exists the first time of collision
t_{c}>0
, and a set of indices
C
, such that for all
i,j\in C
, we have
x_{i}(t_{c})=x_{j}(t_{c})
and for all
i\in C
and
j\notin C
, we have
x_{i}(t_{c})\neq x_{j}(t_{c})
. Consequently, since all position trajectories
t\longmapsto x_{i}(t)
are Lipschitz continuous (by Proposition
3.1
), the following statements hold true:

\displaystyle|x_{i}(t)-x_{j}(t)|

\displaystyle>0,\quad\mbox{for all}\quad i\neq j\in\{1,...,N\},\ t\in[0,t_{c}),

\displaystyle|x_{i}(t)-x_{j}(t)|

\displaystyle\to 0\quad\mbox{as}\quad t\nearrow t_{c},\quad\mbox{for all}\quad i,j\in C,

\displaystyle|x_{i}(t)-x_{j}(t)|

\displaystyle\geq\delta\quad\mbox{for some}\quad\delta>0\quad\mbox{and all}\quad i\in C,\ j\notin C,\ t\in[0,t_{c}].

Let us introduce the relative position and velocity within the cluster
C
as

\|x\|_{C}(t):=\sqrt{\sum_{i,j\in C}|x_{i}(t)-x_{j}(t)|^{2}},\qquad\|v\|_{C}(t):=\sqrt{\sum_{i,j\in C}|v_{i}(t)-v_{j}(t)|^{2}}.

According to the above definitions we have

(3.2)

\|x\|_{C}(t)\xrightarrow{t\nearrow t_{c}}0,

which is the statement that we aim to contradict.

To this end, we begin by stating the system of dissipative differential inequalities (SDDI) satisfied by the relative position and velocity. Its derivation can be found in
[
31
]
, inequality (10), and follows the same idea as in the linear case of
p=2
, cf.
[
8
]
. The only difference with
[
31
]
is that we use the elementary inequality

(|a|^{p-2}a-|b|^{p-2}b)\cdot(b-a)\leq 0,

valid for
p\geq 3
(actually
p\geq 2
suffices) to ensure that the term
J_{22}
on page 5252 of
[
31
]
is non-positive, which allows us to completely remove the last term on the right-hand side of inequality (10) in
[
31
]
. Since the calculation is rather basic, we omit it.
The SDDI reads

(3.3)

\begin{split}\left|\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}\|x\|_{C}(t)\right|&\leq\|v\|_{C}(t),\\
\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}\|v\|_{C}(t)&\leq-c_{0}\|x\|_{C}^{-\alpha}(t)\|v\|_{C}^{p-1}(t)+c_{1}\|x\|_{C}(t),\quad\mbox{for some positive constants }\ c_{0},c_{1}.\end{split}

In the remainder of the proof, we assume additionally that

(3.4)

\|x\|_{C}\leq 1,

since whenever
\|x\|_{C}(t_{n})>1
, for some
t_{n}\to t_{c}
, there is no possibility of collision, due to the fact that the speed is bounded, cf. Proposition
3.1
.
On the time interval
[0,t_{c})
, the functions
t\longmapsto\|x\|_{C}^{2}(t)
and
t\longmapsto\|v\|_{C}^{2}(t)
are smooth and
\|x\|_{C}
is strictly positive. Thus the derivative

\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}-\ln(\|x\|_{C}(t))=-\frac{\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}\|x\|_{C}(t)}{\|x\|_{C}(t)}\leq\frac{\|v\|_{C}(t)}{\|x\|_{C}(t)}

exists for all
t\in[0,t_{c})
. Since we assume that
p\geq 3
, there exists
\eta_{0}>0
such that

(3.5)

c_{0}\theta^{p-3}-\frac{c_{1}}{\theta^{2}}\geq 1,\quad\mbox{for all }\ \theta\geq\eta_{0}.

Fix such
\eta_{0}
. We shall prove that for
t\in[0,t_{c})
, we have

(3.6)

\frac{\|v\|_{C}(t)}{\|x\|_{C}(t)}\leq\eta:=\max\left\{\eta_{0},\frac{\|v\|_{C}(0)}{\|x\|_{C}(0)}\right\}.

Then clearly

\limsup_{t\to t_{c}}-\ln(\|x\|_{C}(t))\leq-\ln(\|x\|_{C}(0))+\eta t_{c}<+\infty

ensuring that (
3.2
) cannot hold true.

Suppose to the contrary of (
3.6
), that there exists
t_{1}\in[0,t_{c})
, such that
\frac{\|v\|_{C}(t_{1})}{\|x\|_{C}(t_{1})}>\eta
. Denote

t_{0}:=\sup\left\{t<t_{1}:\ \frac{\|v\|_{C}(t)}{\|x\|_{C}(t)}\leq\eta\right\}.

Since
t\longmapsto\frac{\|v\|_{C}(t)}{\|x\|_{C}(t)}
is continuous on
[0,t_{c})
and
\frac{\|v\|_{C}(0)}{\|x\|_{C}(0)}\leq\eta
, it is clear that
0\leq t_{0}<t_{1}
and
\frac{\|v\|_{C}(t_{0})}{\|x\|_{C}(t_{0})}\leq\eta
, and finally that
\frac{\|v\|_{C}(t)}{\|x\|_{C}(t)}>\eta
for all
t\in(t_{0},t_{1})
.

Using the SDDI (
3.3
) on the time interval
(t_{0},t_{1})
we infer that

\displaystyle\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}\|v\|_{C}

\displaystyle\leq-c_{0}\|x\|_{C}^{-\alpha}\|v\|_{C}^{p-1}+c_{1}\|x\|_{C}

\displaystyle=-c_{0}\left(\frac{\|v\|_{C}}{\|x\|_{C}}\right)^{p-3}\ \|v\|_{C}^{2}\ \|x\|_{C}^{p-\alpha-3}+c_{1}\|x\|_{C}

\displaystyle\leq-c_{0}\eta^{p-3}\frac{\|v\|_{C}^{2}}{\|x\|_{C}}+c_{1}\|x\|_{C},

where the last inequality follows from the fact that, since
\|x\|_{C}\leq 1
by (
3.4
), the expression on the right-hand side above is maximized whenever singularity in
\|x\|_{C}^{p-\alpha-3}
is the weakest. By the theorem assumptions, it is precisely the case
p=\alpha+2
.
Since
\|v\|_{C}>\eta\|x\|_{C}>0
on
(t_{0},t_{1})
we can divide both sides of the above inequality by
\|v\|_{C}
obtaining

\frac{\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}\|v\|_{C}}{\|v\|_{C}}\leq-c_{0}\eta^{p-3}\frac{\|v\|_{C}}{\|x\|_{C}}+c_{1}\frac{\|x\|_{C}}{\|v\|_{C}}.

Observe further that on
(t_{0},t_{1})
, we have

\frac{\|x\|_{C}}{\|v\|_{C}}\leq\frac{1}{\eta}<\frac{1}{\eta^{2}}\frac{\|v\|_{C}}{\|x\|_{C}},

which, together with the first inequality in the SDDI (
3.3
) and with (
3.5
), implies that

\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}\ln(\|v\|_{C})\leq-\left(c_{0}\eta^{p-3}-\frac{c_{1}}{\eta^{2}}\right)\frac{\|v\|_{C}}{\|x\|_{C}}\leq-\frac{\|v\|_{C}}{\|x\|_{C}}\leq\frac{d}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}\ln(\|x\|_{C}).

Integrating over
(t_{0},t_{1})
we obtain

\ln(\|v\|_{C}(t_{1}))\leq\ln(\|v\|_{C}(t_{0}))+\big(\ln(\|x\|_{C}(t_{1}))-\ln(\|x\|_{C}(t_{0}))\big)

or equivalently

\frac{\|v\|_{C}(t_{1})}{\|x\|_{C}(t_{1})}\leq\frac{\|v\|_{C}(t_{0})}{\|x\|_{C}(t_{0})}\leq\eta.

This is a contradiction, since
t_{1}
was supposed to satisfy
\frac{\|v\|_{C}(t_{1})}{\|x\|_{C}(t_{1})}>\eta
. Altogether (
3.6
) holds true for all
t\in[0,t_{c})
ensuring that there is no possibility of finite-time collision.
∎

Proposition 3.3
.

Theorem
1.2
is optimal in the sense that for a fixed
\alpha\geq 1
, if
p>\alpha+2
, then there exist solutions admitting collisions in finite time.

Proof.

Consider a two-particle variant of system (
1.6
) on the real line. Defining
r:=x_{2}-x_{1}
, the system reduces to the ODE:

(3.7)

\ddot{r}=-\dot{r}|\dot{r}|^{p-2}|r|^{-\alpha}.

We choose initial data such that
r(0)>0
and
\dot{r}(0)<0
. Assuming no collision occurs, we have
r(t)>0
and
\dot{r}(t)<0
for all
t\geq 0
. Then (
3.7
) can be rewritten as

\ddot{r}=-\dot{r}(-\dot{r})^{p-2}r^{-\alpha}

and finally as

(3.8)

\ddot{r}(-\dot{r})^{2-p}=-\dot{r}r^{-\alpha}.

Now we consider two cases
\alpha>1
and
\alpha=1
. Let us first deal with the case
\alpha>1
. Integrating both sides of the above equation, we obtain

\frac{1}{p-3}(-\dot{r})^{3-p}=\frac{1}{\alpha-1}r^{1-\alpha}+c,

where
c=\frac{1}{p-3}(-\dot{r}(0))^{3-p}-\frac{1}{\alpha-1}r^{1-\alpha}(0)
and we pick the initial data so that
c=0
. This leaves us with

\dot{r}=-\left(\frac{p-3}{\alpha-1}\right)^{\frac{1}{3-p}}r^{\frac{\alpha-1}{p-3}}.

Solving the above ODE, we get

r(t)=\left(r^{p-2-\alpha}(0)-\frac{p-3}{\alpha-1}t\right)^{\frac{1}{p-2-\alpha}},

which for
p>\alpha+2>3
clearly leads to a collision.

Next, consider the case
\alpha=1
. Similarly, integrating (
3.8
), we derive

\frac{1}{p-3}(-\dot{r})^{3-p}=-\ln r

by choosing the initial data so that
\frac{1}{p-3}(-\dot{r}(0))^{3-p}+\ln r(0)=0
. Note that under this initial assumption, we should impose
r(0)<1
and thus
r(t)<1
for all
t\geq 0
. This yields

\left((3-p)\ln r\right)^{\frac{1}{p-3}}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r=-\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t,

and thus

\int_{r(0)}^{r(t)}\left((3-p)\ln s\right)^{\frac{1}{p-3}}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s=-t.

On the other hand, we find

\displaystyle\int_{r(0)}^{r(t)}\left((3-p)\ln s\right)^{\frac{1}{p-3}}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=r(t)\left((3-p)\ln r(t)\right)^{\frac{1}{p-3}}-r(0)\left((3-p)\ln r(0)\right)^{\frac{1}{p-3}}

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{r(0)}^{r(t)}\left((3-p)\ln s\right)^{\frac{1}{p-3}-1}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\geq r(t)\left((3-p)\ln r(t)\right)^{\frac{1}{p-3}}-r(0)\left((3-p)\ln r(0)\right)^{\frac{1}{p-3}}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{(3-p)\ln r(0)}\int_{r(0)}^{r(t)}\left((3-p)\ln s\right)^{\frac{1}{p-3}}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s,

where we used
(3-p)\ln r(0)\leq(3-p)\ln s\leq(3-p)\ln r(t)
for
r(t)\leq s\leq r(0)<1
and
p>3
. This implies

\displaystyle r(t)\left((3-p)\ln r(t)\right)^{\frac{1}{p-3}}

\displaystyle\leq r(0)\left((3-p)\ln r(0)\right)^{\frac{1}{p-3}}+\left(1-\frac{1}{(3-p)\ln r(0)}\right)\int_{r(0)}^{r(t)}\left((3-p)\ln s\right)^{\frac{1}{p-3}}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=r(0)\left((3-p)\ln r(0)\right)^{\frac{1}{p-3}}-\left(1-\frac{1}{(3-p)\ln r(0)}\right)t.

Thus if we choose
r(0)>0
small enough such that
(3-p)\ln r(0)>1
, i.e.
r(0)<e^{-1/(p-3)}
, then
r(t)
must reach zero in finite time since the left-hand side of the above inequality is nonnegative.
∎

4.
Compactness of atomic solutions

We dedicate this section to compactness results for the sequence of atomic solutions
\{\mu^{N}\}_{N=1}^{\infty}
. Ultimately, our goal is to extract a weakly convergent subsequence and prove that the local quantities of its limit solve (
1.1
) in the sense of Definition
2.5
. The first compactness result is independent of the nonlinear character of velocity couplings and follows from its
p=2
variant in
[
22
]
. We omit the proof.

Proposition 4.1
.

Fix
T>0
and let
\{\mu^{N}\}_{N=1}^{\infty}
be any sequence of atomic solutions (cf. Definition
2.3
), uniformly compactly supported in
(T+1)B(M)\times B(M)
and
\rho^{N}
– their local density defined in (
1.2
).
Then the following assertions hold.

(i)

The sequence
\{\mu^{N}\}_{N=1}^{\infty}
is narrowly relatively compact in
{\mathcal{M}}_{+}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{2d})
, and any of its limits has the 1D Lebesgue measure as its
t
-marginal. In other words

(4.1)

\mu^{N}\rightharpoonup\mu_{t}\otimes\lambda^{1}(t)

up to a subsequence.

(ii)

The sequence
\{\rho^{N}\}_{N=1}^{\infty}
treated as measure-valued functions on
[0,T]
is relatively compact in

{C}\left([0,T];(\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),d_{BL})\right).

While the convergences obtained from compactness ensured by the above proposition work well when passing to the limit with linear terms in (
1.7
), they are insufficient to deal with the nonlinear term involving the interaction force
F(\mu)
. For this, we need another compactness result, which reads as follows.

Proposition 4.2
.

Under the assumptions of Proposition
4.1
let
\mu^{N_{k}}
be a subsequence convergent to
\mu\in{\mathcal{M}}_{+}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{2d})
.
Then, up to a subsequence,
[\mu^{N_{k}}_{t}\otimes\mu^{N_{k}}_{t}]\otimes\lambda^{1}(t)
converges narrowly to some
\lambda\in{\mathcal{M}}_{+}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{4d})
, which can be disintegrated as

[\mu_{t}^{N_{k}}(x,v)\otimes\mu_{t}^{N_{k}}(x^{\prime},v^{\prime})]\otimes\lambda^{1}(t)\xrightharpoonup{k\rightarrow\infty}\lambda(t,x,v,x^{\prime},v^{\prime})=[\mu_{t}(x,v)\otimes\mu_{t}(x^{\prime},v^{\prime})]\otimes\lambda^{1}(t).

Proof.

Since
\{[\mu_{t}^{N_{k}}\otimes\mu_{t}^{N_{k}}]\otimes\lambda^{1}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is a sequence of uniformly compactly supported probability measures, its narrow compactness follows directly from Banach-Alaoglu theorem ensuring the existence of a subsequence (not relabeled) and a measure
\lambda\in\mathcal{M}_{+}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{4d})
such that

(4.2)

[\mu_{t}^{N_{k}}\otimes\mu_{t}^{N_{k}}]\otimes\lambda^{1}(t)\xrightharpoonup{k\rightarrow\infty}\lambda.

To prove that
\lambda
disintegrates as

\lambda(t,x,v,x^{\prime},v^{\prime})=\lambda_{t}(x,v,x^{\prime},v^{\prime})\otimes\lambda^{1}(t),

suppose that
\nu
is the
t
-marginal of
\lambda
(so that
\lambda=\lambda_{t}\otimes\nu(t)
) and test the narrow convergence with
g(t,x,v,x^{\prime},v^{\prime})=\varphi(\pi_{t}(t,x,v,x^{\prime},v^{\prime}))=\varphi(t)
, with an arbitrary continuous function
\varphi
, obtaining

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\varphi(t)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4d}}g\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}[\mu_{t}^{N}\otimes\mu_{t}^{N}]\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\xrightarrow{N\rightarrow\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4d}}g\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lambda

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\varphi(t)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4d}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lambda_{t}\otimes\nu(t)=\int_{0}^{T}\varphi(t)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\nu(t).

Since
\varphi
is arbitrary, we conclude that
\nu(t)=\lambda^{1}(t)
.
It remains to prove that

(4.3)

\lambda_{t}(x,v,x^{\prime},v^{\prime})=\mu_{t}(x,v)\otimes\mu_{t}(x^{\prime},v^{\prime})\quad\mbox{for a.a. }t\in[0,T].

Following (
3.1
), let
\Omega\subset\subset(T+1)B(M)\times B(M)
be a superset of all supports of
\mu^{N_{k}}_{t}
and
\mu_{t}
for all
t\in[0,T]
(observe that
\{\mu_{t}\}_{t\in[0,T]}
is compactly supported as a narrow limit of
\{\mu^{N_{k}}_{t}\}_{t\in[0,T]}
). Furthermore, let
{\mathcal{D}}
be a countable dense (in the sense of uniform convergence) subset of
C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
and fix any

\varphi\in{\mathcal{D}}\subset C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega),\qquad\|\varphi\|_{C^{2}}\leq 1.

For each
k
, consider the function

t\longmapsto\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\varphi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}^{N_{k}}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\varphi(x_{i}^{N_{k}}(t),v_{i}^{N_{k}}(t)),

where
(x_{i}^{N_{k}},v_{i}^{N_{k}})_{i=1}^{N_{k}}
is the solution of the particle system (
1.6
) corresponding to
\mu^{N_{k}}
. The above function is smooth since the solutions to the particle system are smooth. Thus, applying the standard trick with the exchange of indices
i
with
j
, we obtain

(4.4)

\begin{split}\left|\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\varphi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}^{N_{k}}\right|&\leq\frac{1}{N_{k}}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N_{k}}\left|\nabla_{x}\varphi(x_{i}^{N_{k}},v_{i}^{N_{k}})\right|\left|v_{i}^{N_{k}}\right|\\
&\quad+\frac{1}{2N_{k}^{2}}\sum\limits_{i\neq j=1}^{N_{k}}\underbrace{\frac{\left|\nabla_{v}\varphi(x_{i}^{N_{k}},v_{i}^{N_{k}})-\nabla_{v}\varphi(x_{j}^{N_{k}},v_{j}^{N_{k}})\right|\left|v_{j}^{N_{k}}-v_{i}^{N_{k}}\right|^{p-1}}{|x_{i}^{N_{k}}-x_{j}^{N_{k}}|^{\alpha}}}_{S:=}.\end{split}

Using Lipschitz continuity of
\nabla_{v}\varphi
,
by Young’s inequality with exponents
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}=1
, the singular term
S
on the right-hand side above can be upper-bounded by

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle\leq[\nabla_{v}\varphi]_{Lip}\left(|x_{i}^{N_{k}}-x_{j}^{N_{k}}|^{1-\alpha}|v_{i}^{N_{k}}-v_{j}^{N_{k}}|^{p-1}+\frac{|v_{j}^{N_{k}}-v_{i}^{N_{k}}|^{p}}{|x_{i}^{N_{k}}-x_{j}^{N_{k}}|^{\alpha}}\right)

\displaystyle=[\nabla_{v}\varphi]_{Lip}\left(|x_{i}^{N_{k}}-x_{j}^{N_{k}}|^{1-\frac{\alpha}{p}}\frac{|v_{i}^{N_{k}}-v_{j}^{N_{k}}|^{p-1}}{|x_{i}^{N_{k}}-x_{j}^{N_{k}}|^{\frac{\alpha}{p^{\prime}}}}+\frac{|v_{j}^{N_{k}}-v_{i}^{N_{k}}|^{p}}{|x_{i}^{N_{k}}-x_{j}^{N_{k}}|^{\alpha}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq[\nabla_{v}\varphi]_{Lip}\left(\frac{1}{p}|x_{i}^{N_{k}}-x_{j}^{N_{k}}|^{p-\alpha}+\left(1+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}\right)\frac{|v_{j}^{N_{k}}-v_{i}^{N_{k}}|^{p}}{|x_{i}^{N_{k}}-x_{j}^{N_{k}}|^{\alpha}}\right).

Plugging the above inequality into (
4.4
) and using the bounds of the diameter of
\Omega
as well as the fact that
\|\varphi\|_{C^{2}}\leq 1
, yields

\left|\frac{d}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\varphi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}^{N_{k}}\right|\leq M+\frac{1}{2p}\frac{1}{N_{k}^{2}}\sum_{i\neq j}^{N_{k}}|x_{i}^{N_{k}}-x_{j}^{N_{k}}|^{p-\alpha}+\frac{1}{2}\left(1+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}\right)D_{p}[\mu_{t}^{N_{k}}].

Integrating the above over
[s,t]
we obtain

(4.5)

\begin{split}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\varphi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}^{N_{k}}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\varphi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu_{s}^{N_{k}}\right|&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\star}}{{\leq}}MT+(2T(T+1)M)^{p-\alpha}+\frac{1}{2}\left(1+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}\right)E[\mu_{0}^{N_{k}}]\\
&\leq MT+(2T(T+1)M)^{p-\alpha}+\frac{1}{2}\left(1+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}\right)(2M)^{p}.\end{split}

Here inequality
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\star}}{{\leq}}
follows from the fact that
p\geq\alpha
and from the energy equality in Proposition
3.2
(
E
and
D_{p}
are the kinetic energy and its dissipation rate defined in (
2.3
)).
Inequality (
4.5
) ensures that the sequence of functions

t\longmapsto\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\varphi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}^{N_{k}}

is bounded in
W^{1,1}([0,T])
, and thus, it is compact in
L^{2}([0,T])
. Therefore, for fixed
\varphi
, we have (up to a subsequence)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\varphi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu^{N_{k}}_{t}\longrightarrow\psi_{\varphi}(t)\quad\text{in}\quad L^{2}([0,T]),

where
\psi_{\varphi}
is some
\varphi
-dependent limit. Proceeding diagonally, there exists a subsequence of
N_{k}
(which we shall not relabel), such that

\forall\varphi\in{\mathcal{D}},\ \|\varphi\|_{C^{2}}\leq 1\quad\mbox{we have}\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\varphi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu^{N_{k}}_{t}\longrightarrow\psi_{\varphi}(t)\text{ in }L^{2}([0,T]).

By the assumptions, sequence
\mu^{N_{k}}
converges narrowly as in (
4.1
), and thus for all
\psi\in C([0,T])
we have

\int_{0}^{T}\psi(t)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\varphi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu^{N_{k}}_{t}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\psi(t)\varphi(x,v)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}^{N_{k}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\xrightarrow{N_{k}\rightarrow\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\psi(t)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\varphi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t.

Since
\psi
belongs to a dense subspace
C([0,T])
of
L^{2}([0,T])
, by uniqueness of the weak limit, we deduce that

\psi_{\varphi}(t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\varphi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}\quad\mbox{as elements of }\ L^{2}([0,T])\ \mbox{ for all }\varphi\in{\mathcal{D}}\ \mbox{with}\ \|\varphi\|_{C^{2}}\leq 1.

Now suppose that
\varphi_{1}
and
\varphi_{2}
are two such functions. Then

\left(t\longmapsto\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi_{1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu^{N_{k}}_{t}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi_{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu^{N_{k}}_{t}\right)\xrightarrow{k\to\infty}\left(t\longmapsto\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\varphi_{1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\varphi_{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}\right)

strongly in
L^{1}([0,T])
. Since
C([0,T])\subset L^{\infty}([0,T])=L^{1}([0,T])^{*}
, we infer (by weak
L^{1}
convergence) that for all
\psi\in C([0,T])
,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4d}}\psi(t)\varphi_{1}(x,v)\varphi_{2}(x^{\prime},v^{\prime})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\left[\mu_{t}^{N_{k}}(x,v)\otimes\mu_{t}^{N_{k}}(x^{\prime},v^{\prime})\right]\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t=\int_{0}^{T}\psi(t)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\varphi_{1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu^{N_{k}}_{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\varphi_{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu^{N_{k}}_{t}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\rightarrow\int_{0}^{T}\psi(t)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\varphi_{1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\varphi_{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4d}}\psi(t)\varphi_{1}(x,v)\varphi_{2}(x^{\prime},v^{\prime})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\left[\mu_{t}(x,v)\otimes\mu_{t}(x^{\prime},v^{\prime})\right]\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t.

By a standard density argument based on Stone-Weierstrass theorem the above convergence holds true for all
g\in C([0,T]\times\Omega^{2})
implying that

[\mu_{t}^{N_{k}}\otimes\mu_{t}^{N_{k}}]\otimes\lambda^{1}(t)\xrightharpoonup{k\rightarrow\infty}[\mu_{t}\otimes\mu_{t}]\otimes\lambda^{1}(t).

Finally, by the uniqueness of the narrow limit, and by (
4.2
) we conclude that

\lambda=[\mu_{t}\otimes\mu_{t}]\otimes\lambda^{1}(t)

and the proof is finished.
∎

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4d}\setminus\Delta}\frac{|v-v^{\prime}|^{\alpha+2}}{|x-x^{\prime}|^{\alpha}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}[\mu_{t}\otimes\mu_{t}]\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t

.

\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}D_{p}[\mu_{t}]\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\leq E[\mu_{t_{1}}]-E[\mu_{t_{2}}].

Plugging this decomposition into the energy inequality in Lemma

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\varphi(0,x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu_{0}^{N}+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\left(\partial_{t}\varphi+v\cdot\nabla_{x}\varphi\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}^{N}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi(0,x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho_{0}^{N}+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\left(\partial_{t}\varphi+v\cdot\nabla_{x}\varphi\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}^{N}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t.

\displaystyle\leq\left\lvert E[\mu_{t_{1}^{\prime}}]-E[\mu_{t_{2}^{\prime}}]\right\rvert+|2(T+1)M|^{p-\alpha}|t_{2}^{\prime}-t_{1}^{\prime}|

\displaystyle\leq\left\lvert E[\mu_{t_{1}^{\prime}}]-E[\mu_{t_{1}}]\right\rvert+\left\lvert E[\mu_{t_{1}}]-E[\mu_{t_{2}^{\prime}}]\right\rvert

\displaystyle\quad+|2(T+1)M|^{p-\alpha}|t_{1}^{\prime}-t_{1}^{\prime}|+|2(T+1)M|^{p-\alpha}|t_{2}^{\prime}-t_{1}|.

Recalling (
A.4
) and that
|t_{i}^{\prime}-t_{1}|<\delta
for
i=1,2
, after possibly multiplying
\eta
by constant depending on
L_{\varphi,\xi}
and
T
, we arrive at

\left\lvert\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\partial_{t}\varphi_{\varepsilon}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}\varphi_{\varepsilon}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\right\rvert\leq C_{\varphi,\xi}\frac{\eta}{2}+2C_{\varphi,\xi}|2(T+1)M|^{p-\alpha}\delta.

Observe that for
\alpha\leq p
we can always make
\delta
smaller if needed to obtain

(A.7)

\left\lvert\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\partial_{t}\varphi_{\varepsilon}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}\varphi_{\varepsilon}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\right\rvert\leq C_{\varphi,\xi}\eta.

To conclude the first step, let us expand the left-hand side of the above inequality. It reads

(A.8)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\partial_{t}\varphi_{\varepsilon}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}\varphi_{\varepsilon}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\partial_{t}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(t)\xi(x-(t-t_{0})v)\varphi(v)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{T}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(t)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\underbrace{\partial_{t}\left(\xi(x-(t-t_{0})v)\right)\varphi(v)+v\cdot\nabla\xi(x-(t-t_{0})v)\varphi(v)}_{=0}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\partial_{t}\psi_{\varepsilon}(t)\xi(x)\varphi(v)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}T^{t_{0},t}_{\#}\mu_{t}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t.

Combining (
A.8
) with (
A.7
), we conclude the proof of the claim and the first step.

\diamond

Step 2.

Passing with
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
.

By Definition
5.2
, both
\{\mu_{t}\}_{t\in[0,T]}
and
\{T^{t_{0},t}_{\#}\mu_{t}\}_{t\in[0,T]}
are uniformly compactly supported with the support contained in some large compact set
K\times K\subset\mathbb{R}^{2d}
. By Stone-Weierstrass theorem, there exists a countable set
\mathcal{D}\subset C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
dense in
C_{b}(K)
. With that fact at hand, we aim to prove the following claim.

Claim B.
There exists a full measure set
A\subset[0,T]
such that for all
t_{0}\in D_{E}
and all
\varphi,\xi\in\mathcal{D}
the following statements are true.

For all
t_{1}\in A
and all
\eta>0
there exists
\delta>0
(depending on
t_{1},\varphi
and
\xi
) such that for all
t_{2}\in A
satisfying
|t_{2}-t_{1}|<\delta
we have

\left\lvert\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\xi(x)\varphi(v)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}T^{t_{0},t_{2}}_{\#}\mu_{t_{2}}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\xi(x)\varphi(v)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}T^{t_{0},t_{1}}_{\#}\mu_{t_{1}}\right\rvert\leq\eta.

Equivalently, for all
t_{1}\in A
and all
\{t_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset A
such that
t_{n}\rightarrow t_{1}

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\xi(x)\varphi(v)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}T^{t_{0},t_{n}}_{\#}\mu_{t_{n}}\xrightarrow{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\xi(x)\varphi(v)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}T^{t_{0},t_{1}}_{\#}\mu_{t_{1}}.

Take any
t_{0}\in D_{E}
and any
\varphi_{1},\xi_{1}\in\mathcal{D}
. By Claim A, we know that for all
t_{1}\in A_{E}
and
\eta>0
, there exists
\delta>0
(importantantly, independent of
\varepsilon
) such that for
t_{2}\in A_{E}
,
|t_{1}-t_{2}|<\delta
we have

(A.9)

\left\lvert\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\partial_{t}\psi_{t_{1},t_{2},\varepsilon}(t)\xi_{1}(x)\varphi_{1}(v)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}T^{t_{0},t}_{\#}\mu_{t}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\right\rvert\leq\eta.

We aim to pass with
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
in the inequality above. We start by differentiating the integrand with respect to
t
; since only
\psi
is
t-
dependent, this amounts to observing that by (
A.2
) we have

(A.10)

\psi_{t_{1},t_{2},\varepsilon}(t):=\begin{cases}(2\varepsilon)^{-1}\quad\text{for }t\in(t_{1}-\varepsilon,t_{1}+\varepsilon),\\
-(2\varepsilon)^{-1}\quad\text{for }t\in(t_{2}-\varepsilon,t_{2}+\varepsilon),\\
0\quad\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Thus (
A.9
) translates into

\left\lvert\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}\int_{t_{1}-\varepsilon}^{t_{1}+\varepsilon}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\xi_{1}(x)\varphi_{1}(v)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}T^{t_{0},t}_{\#}\mu_{t}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t-\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}\int_{t_{2}-\varepsilon}^{t_{2}+\varepsilon}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\xi_{1}(x)\varphi_{1}(v)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}T^{t_{0},t}_{\#}\mu_{t}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\right\rvert\leq\eta.

To pass with
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
, observe that the function
t\longmapsto\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\xi_{1}(x)\varphi_{1}(v)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}T^{t_{0},t}_{\#}\mu_{t}
is integrable since
\varphi_{1},\xi_{1}
are bounded. Therefore we can apply the Lebesgue-Besicovitch differentiation theorem to ensure the existence of a full-measure subset
A_{1,t_{0}}\subset A_{E}
, depending on
\xi_{1},\varphi_{1}
and
t_{0}
, such that for all
t_{1},t_{2}\in A_{1,t_{0}}

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\left\lvert\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\partial_{t}\psi_{t_{1},t_{2},\varepsilon}(t)\xi_{1}(x)\varphi_{1}(v)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}T^{t_{0},t}_{\#}\mu_{t}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\right\rvert

\displaystyle=\left\lvert\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\xi_{1}(x)\varphi_{1}(v)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}T^{t_{0},t_{1}}_{\#}\mu_{t_{1}}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\xi_{1}(x)\varphi_{1}(v)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}T^{t_{0},t_{2}}_{\#}\mu_{t_{2}}\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\eta

(where we still assume
|t_{1}-t_{2}|<\delta
as in Claim A). This procedure can be repeated indefinitely for all
i=1,2,\ldots
and
\varphi_{i},\xi_{i}\in\mathcal{D}
as well as for all
t_{0}\in D_{E}
(which is a countable set), leading to the existence of a full measure subset

(A.11)

A:=\bigcap_{i\in\mathbb{N},t_{0}\in D_{E}}A_{i,t_{0}}\subset A_{E}

satisfying claim B.

\diamond

Step 3.

Taking
t_{0}=t_{1}
.

While Claim B is already close to the condition (SF), we still need to make sure that we can take
t_{0}=t_{1}
. It is not immediately clear, since claim B holds only for
t\in A
and the set
A
may be disjoint with
D_{E}\ni t_{0}
. Nevertheless, observe that for any
t_{0}\in A
,
t_{n}\rightarrow t_{0}
,
\{t_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset D_{E}
and any
\varphi,\xi\in\mathcal{D}
we have

(A.12)

\left\lvert\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\xi\varphi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}T^{t_{n},t_{1}}_{\#}\mu_{t_{1}}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\xi\varphi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}T^{t_{0},t_{1}}_{\#}\mu_{t_{1}}\right\rvert\leq M[\xi]_{\text{Lip}}\lVert\varphi\rVert_{\infty}|t_{n}-t_{0}|,

meaning that for such
t_{1},\varphi,\xi
the function

t_{0}\longmapsto\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\xi\varphi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}T^{t_{0},t_{1}}_{\#}\mu_{t_{1}}

is continuous (in fact, we have a uniform in
t_{1}
Lipschitz continuity). With that in mind, we aim to prove the next claim.

Claim C.
For all
t_{0}\in A
,
\varphi,\xi\in\mathcal{D}
and any
\{t_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
such that
t_{n}\rightarrow t_{0}
we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\xi\varphi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}T^{t_{0},t_{n}}_{\#}\mu_{t_{n}}\rightarrow\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\xi\varphi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}T^{t_{0},t_{0}}_{\#}\mu_{t_{0}}.

Let
t_{0},\varphi,\xi
and
\{t_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
satisfy assumptions of the Claim. Then for all
\eta>0
there exists
t_{0}^{\prime}\in D_{E}
such that
|t_{0}-t_{0}^{\prime}|<\eta
. By Claim B, we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\xi\varphi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}T^{t_{0}^{\prime},t_{n}}_{\#}\mu_{t_{n}}\rightarrow\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\xi\varphi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}T^{t_{0},t_{0}}_{\#}\mu_{t_{0}}.

Exploiting (
A.12
), we arrive at

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\xi\varphi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}T^{t_{0},t_{n}}_{\#}\mu_{t_{n}}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\xi\varphi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}T^{t_{0},t_{0}}_{\#}\mu_{t_{0}}\right|

\displaystyle\quad\leq\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\xi\varphi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}T^{t_{0},t_{n}}_{\#}\mu_{t_{n}}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\xi\varphi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}T^{t_{0}^{\prime},t_{n}}_{\#}\mu_{t_{n}}\right|+\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\xi\varphi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}T^{t_{0}^{\prime},t_{n}}_{\#}\mu_{t_{n}}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\xi\varphi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}T^{t_{0}^{\prime},t_{0}}_{\#}\mu_{t_{0}}\right|

\displaystyle\qquad+\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\xi\varphi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}T^{t_{0}^{\prime},t_{0}}_{\#}\mu_{t_{0}}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\xi\varphi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}T^{t_{0},t_{0}}_{\#}\mu_{t_{0}}\right|

\displaystyle\quad\leq\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\xi\varphi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}T^{t_{0}^{\prime},t_{n}}_{\#}\mu_{t_{n}}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\xi\varphi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}T^{t_{0}^{\prime},t_{0}}_{\#}\mu_{t_{0}}\right|+2M[\xi]_{Lip}|\varphi|_{\infty}\eta\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}2M[\xi]_{Lip}|\varphi|_{\infty}\eta.

Since
\eta>0
was arbitrary, Claim C is proved.

\diamond

Step 4.

Relaxing the restrictions on
\varphi
and
\xi
.

Our last effort is to generalise all the previous steps to arbitrary
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
\xi\in C_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. This follows from the usual density argument; let us fix any
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
\xi\in C_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and let
\{t_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
be such that
t_{n}\rightarrow t_{0}
. Aiming at (
A.1
), our goal is to establish that

(A.13)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\xi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho_{t_{0},t_{n}}[\varphi]\rightarrow\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\xi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho_{t_{0},t_{0}}[\varphi].

Keeping the definition of
\mathcal{D}
and
K
from the Step 2, let us fix
\eta>0
and functions
\varphi_{\eta},\xi_{\eta}\in\mathcal{D}
such that

\sup\limits_{x\in K}\left\lvert\xi(x)-\xi_{\eta}(x)\right\rvert+\sup\limits_{v\in K}\left\lvert\varphi(v)-\varphi_{\eta}(v)\right\rvert<\eta.

Then by Claim C, the following convergence holds:

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\xi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho_{t_{0},t_{n}}[\varphi]-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\xi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho_{t_{0},t_{0}}[\varphi]\right|

\displaystyle\quad=\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\xi\varphi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}T^{t_{0},t_{n}}_{\#}\mu_{t_{n}}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\xi\varphi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}T^{t_{0},t_{0}}_{\#}\mu_{t_{0}}\right|

\displaystyle\quad\leq\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\xi_{\eta}\varphi_{\eta}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}T^{t_{0},t_{n}}_{\#}\mu_{t_{n}}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\xi_{\eta}\varphi_{\eta}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}T^{t_{0},t_{0}}_{\#}\mu_{t_{0}}\right|+\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}(\xi_{\eta}\varphi_{\eta}-\xi\varphi)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}T^{t_{0},t_{n}}_{\#}\mu_{t_{n}}\right|

\displaystyle\qquad+\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}(\xi_{\eta}\varphi_{\eta}-\xi\varphi)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}T^{t_{0},t_{0}}_{\#}\mu_{t_{0}}\right|

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\xi_{\eta}\varphi_{\eta}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}T^{t_{0},t_{n}}_{\#}\mu_{t_{n}}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\xi_{\eta}\varphi_{\eta}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}T^{t_{0},t_{0}}_{\#}\mu_{t_{0}}\right|+\eta(|\xi|_{\infty}+|\varphi|_{\infty})\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}\eta(|\xi|_{\infty}+|\varphi|_{\infty}).

Due to the arbitrarily of
\eta>0
, convergence (
A.13
) holds for any
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\xi\in C_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. The proof of proposition
5.5
is finished.

∎

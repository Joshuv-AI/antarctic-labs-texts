Integrating 16S rRNA gene sequencing and transcriptomics to investigate the hepatoprotective effects of compound Ilicis Rotundae Cortex against lipopolysaccharide / enrofloxacin-induced liver injury in chicks
Authors: Geyin Zhang, Zhidan Zhang, Jian Meng, Yajing Qian, Yuxi Qin, Zhuoran Li, Shiqi Luo, Yuxin Li, Jieyu Su, Hongbin Si
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Compound Ilicis Rotundae Cortex (CIRC) has been demonstrated to ameliorate E. coli-induced liver injury. However, its efficacy and regulatory mechanisms against liver injury in chicks remain unclear. In this study, a chick liver injury model was established using lipopolysaccharide (LPS) and enrofloxacin (ENR) to investigate the effects of CIRC. The results showed that dietary supplementation with 3 ‰ CIRC significantly prevented liver injury, restored growth performance, and alleviated inflammation and oxidative stress in the injured chicks. Integrated analysis of 16S rRNA gene sequencing and hepatic transcriptomics revealed a gut-liver axis-mediated mechanism for the hepatoprotective effect of CIRC. By modulating the gut microbiota, enhancing intestinal barrier function, and enriching butyrate-producing bacteria, CIRC increased butyric acid (BA) levels in both serum and liver tissue, alleviated the LPS/ENR-induced suppression of the hepatic butanoate metabolism pathway, and subsequently regulated the IL-17, Toll-like receptor, and NF-κB signaling pathways, collectively exerting a hepatoprotective effect. In conclusion, CIRC plays a crucial role in preventing chick liver injury by modulating the gut microbiota.

Introduction

Avian diseases caused by pathogenic bacteria such as E. coli are characterized by high morbidity and mortality rates, resulting in substantial economic losses to the poultry industry and posing a serious threat to public health security (Monroy et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2025a). However, the inappropriate application use of antibiotics in preventing and treating pathogenic infections could lead to antimicrobial resistance and toxicity in poultry (Badawy et al., 2021; Peng et al., 2022). In particular, overdose or long-term administration overloads the liver’s metabolic and detoxification functions, resulting in liver injury (Westphal et al., 1994; Zhu et al., 2013; Ling et al., 2023). Furthermore, antibiotic administration was frequently associated with gut microbiota dysbiosis, which could result in elevated levels of endotoxin (lipopolysaccharide, LPS) (Kuti et al., 2020; Zhong et al., 2022). LPS is a potent immunostimulant that triggers a systemic inflammatory storm (Gong et al., 2025), thereby exacerbating liver injury in poultry (Xia et al., 2025).

Ilicis Rotundae Cortex (IRC), commercially known as "Jiu Bi Ying", a traditional Zhuang medicine originating from the Guangxi Autonomous Region of China, is derived from the dried bark or root bark of the plant Ilex rotunda Thunb (Pharmacopoeia, 2020; Zeng et al., 2025). To date, research has shown that IRC contains 120 identified bioactive compounds (Zeng et al., 2022a), which may underpin its primary clinical use for the treatment of sore throat and acute/chronic gastroenteritis (Shang et al., 2022). Furthermore, in vitro studies have demonstrated its significant antibacterial activity against a broad spectrum of pathogenic microorganisms (Wu, 2017; Kim et al., 2020). Beyond its antimicrobial effects, both the crude extract (Qiu et al., 2015) and its active constituents—such as rotundic acid (Liu et al., 2021) and syringin (Chen and Xiao, 2012; Jiang et al., 2019)—have been scientifically validated to possess hepatoprotective properties. In addition, Compound Jiu Bi Ying capsule (National Medicine Permit Number: Z44022534; manufactured by Guangzhou Baiyunshan Xingqun (Pharmaceutical) Co., Ltd.), a Chinese patent medicine composed of IRC, Radix Atalantia Buxifolia (RAB), Rhizoma Cyperi (RC), is indicated for the treatment of diarrhea and gastroenteritis. Interestingly, a key comparative study revealed that the dual combination of IRC and CR (hereafter referred to as CIRC) was more effective against E. coli-induced liver injury in mice than the triple formula (IRC, RAB and CR) (Wang, 2019). Nevertheless, the effects and mechanisms of CIRC on liver injury in poultry have not yet been fully elucidated.

In this study, a chick liver injury model was established by co-administration of enrofloxacin (ENR) and LPS to simulate antibiotic-induced liver injury following bacterial infection, aiming to investigate the hepatoprotective effects and underlying mechanisms of CIRC.

Materials and methods

Medicines preparation

LPS (IL2020), derived from Escherichia coli O55:B5, was purchased from Beijing Solarbio Science & Technology Co., Ltd. (Beijing, China). ENR (250508), a veterinary antibiotic, was obtained from Zhejiang Guobang Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd. (Hangzhou, China). IRC (240201) and RC (2502001), were purchased from Nanning Haoyaoshi Henian Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd. (Nanning, China). Gandan Granule (202403021), a clinically approved hepatoprotective veterinary medicine in China (Veterinary Drug Approval Number: 164006113) containing Isatis indigotica Fort. (Banlangen) and Artemisia capillaris Thunb. (Yinchen), was employed as the positive control and sourced from Hua Xu Co., Ltd. (Zhengzhou, China).

Preparation of CIRC: 30 g of IRC with 30 g of RC were mixed, the mixture was soaked overnight in 10-fold volume of water. The next day, it was boiled vigorously, then simmered for 1 h, and the decoction was filtered to collect the filtrate. The residue was subsequently extracted twice by adding 8-fold and then 6-fold volumes of distilled water, following the same decoction and filtration procedure each time. All of the filtrates were then pooled, and vacuum-filtered twice. The pooled filtrate was then concentrated by rotary evaporation to a concentration of 1 g/mL (crude herb equivalent). After pre-cooling at −80 °C, the concentrate was lyophilized. The final yield of the powder was 16.8 g, indicating that 0.28 g of the powder corresponds to 1 g of the crude herbal, stored at −20 °C

Experimental animals and protocol

A total of 180 one-day-old yellow-feathered chicks (a type of broiler widely raised in China and Southeast Asia for their superior meat quality.) were obtained from Guangxi Fufeng Agriculture & Animal Husbandry Group Co., Ltd. (Nanning, China). The animal experiment was approved by the Experimental Animal Ethics Committee of Guangxi University (Approval No. GXU-2025-360). After acclimatization and uniform rearing for 14 days, the chicks were randomly divided into 6 groups, each group consisting of 5 replicate pens with 6 chicks per pen.

Control group (C): LPS/ENR non-challenged and no intervened; Model group (M): LPS/ENR challenged and no intervened; CIRC High-dose group (CIRC-H-M): LPS/ENR challenged and intervened with high-dose CIRC (0.3% in feed); CIRC Low-dose group (CIRC-L-M): LPS/ENR challenged and intervened with low-dose CIRC (0.1% in feed); CIRC group (CIRC-H): LPS/ENR non-challenged and intervened with high-dose CIRC (0.3% in feed); Gandan Granule group (GD-M): LPS/ENR challenged and intervened with Gandan Granule (1 g/L in water).

All chicks except those in the C and CIRC-H groups were administered ENR in drinking water (100 mg/kg bw/day) from 20 to 22 day of age. On day 22 of age, feed and Gandan Granules (for the GD-M group) were withheld. After the final ENR dose, the chicks were intraperitoneally injected with LPS (200 μg/kg bw) (Guo et al., 2012). After 6 h, one chick from each replicate pen was randomly chosen, blood was collected from the jugular vein, allowed to clot at room temperature for 2 h, and then centrifuged at 3500 rpm for 10 min to separate the serum. Following blood collection, the chicks were euthanized by cervical dislocation. The right lobe of the liver and the cecal contents were collected, rapidly frozen in liquid nitrogen, and stored at −80°C. In addition, half of the left lobe of the liver was fixed in formalin solution.

Determination of growth performance

During the intervention period, body weight of the chicks was measured daily. Body weight gain (BWG) was calculated as final body weight minus initial body weight. The cumulative average feed intake (FI) per chick was derived by dividing the total feed intake recorded per pen by the number of chicks per pen. The feed conversion ratio (FCR) during the intervention period was then calculated as FCR (g/g) = FI (g) / BWG (g) (Mo et al., 2023).

Measurement of liver function indicators

Serum levels of alanine aminotransferase (ALT) and aspartate aminotransferase (AST) were measured using commercial assay kits (ALT, Cat. No. A059-2; AST, Cat. No. C010-2-1; Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Research Institute Co., Ltd., China) strictly according to the manufacturers’ instructions.

Assessment of antioxidant levels

The antioxidant status in liver tissue was assessed by measuring the levels of key indicators using commercial assay kits, strictly according to the manufacturers' instructions. The parameters and kits used were: catalase (CAT, Cat. No. ADS-W-KY002), total antioxidant capacity (T-AOC, Cat. No. ADS-W-KY005), superoxide dismutase (SOD, Cat. No. ADS-W-KY011), nitric oxide (NO, Cat. No. ADS-W-N005-96), malondialdehyde (MDA, Cat. No. ADS-W-YH002) (all from Jiangsu Aidisheng Biological Technology Co.,Ltd, China), and glutathione peroxidase (GPx, Cat. No. MM-60040O1, Jiangsu Meimian Industrial Co., Ltd, China).

Measurement of immune cytokines by ELISA

Liver tissue was rapidly dissected on ice, accurately weighed, and homogenized with 9 volumes of ice‑cold saline (1:9, w/v). After centrifugation at 2,500 rpm for 10 min, the resulting 10% liver homogenate was collected, and its protein concentration was subsequently determined using a total protein assay kit (BCA method) (Cat. No. A045-4-2, Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Research Institute Co., Ltd., China).

The levels of inflammatory cytokines in liver homogenates, including interleukin-1β (IL-1β), interleukin-6 (IL-6), interleukin-10 (IL-10), and tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α), were measured using commercial ELISA kits (IL-1β, Cat. No. MM-36910O1; IL-6, Cat. No. MM-0521O1; IL-10, Cat. No. MM-1145O1; TNF-α, Cat. No. MM-0938O1; all from Jiangsu Meimian Industrial Co., Ltd., China) strictly according to the manufacturers’ protocols.

Quantification of immune cytokine mRNA expression by qRT-PCR

Total RNA was extracted from liver tissues stored at −80°C. Briefly, the tissues were ground in liquid nitrogen. Total RNA was then isolated using StarSpin Animal RNA Kit (Cat. No. P133-01, GenStar, China) according to the manufacturer’s protocol, and its concentration was measured. First-strand cDNA was synthesized using the PrimeScript RT reagent kit (Cat. No. A230-10, GenStar, China) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Subsequently, qRT-PCR was performed to determine the mRNA expression levels of IL-1β, IL-6, IL-10, and TNF-α. Histopathological examination of the liver

Following fixation, the liver specimens were dehydrated, trimmed, embedded in paraffin, and sectioned. The tissue sections were stained, mounted on slides, and examined under a light microscope to evaluate hepatic histopathological alterations.

16S rRNA gene sequencing

Total community genomic DNA (n = 5 per group) was extracted from cecal content samples using Mag-Bind Soil DNA Kit (200) (Cat. No. M5635-02, Omega Bio-tek, USA) following the manufacturer's protocol. The concentration and purity of the extracted DNA were measured. Subsequently, the hypervariable V3-V4 region of the bacterial 16S rRNA gene was amplified by PCR with the universal primers 341F (5‘-CCTACGGGNGGCWGCAG-3′) and 806R (5‘-GACTACHVGGGTATCTAATCC-3′). Sequencing libraries were prepared and sequenced on an Illumina MiSeq platform.

After sequencing, the two short Illumina readings were assembled by PEAR software (version 0.9.8) according to the overlap and fastq files were processed to generate individual fasta and qual files, which could then be analyzed by standard methods (Zhang et al., 2014). The effective tags were clustered into operational taxonomic units (OTUs) of ≥ 97% similarity using Usearch software (version 11.0.667) (Edgar, 2013). Bacterial OTU representative sequences were classified taxonomically by blasting against the RDP Database (http://rdp.cme.msu.edu/misc/resources.jsp). All α diversity indices (including Chao1,Simpson, and Shannon indices) were calculated with Mothur software (version 3.8.31). The OTU rarefaction curve and rank abundance curves were plotted in R (version 3.6.0). Beta diversity was visualized using R vegan package (version 2.5-6), and finally the inter-sample distances were presented as scatterplots. Difference comparison is used to identify features with significantly different abundances between groups using STAMP (version 2.1.3) and LefSe (version 1.1.0).

Transcriptome sequencing of chick liver

Total RNA was extracted from liver tissues (n = 5 per group) using TRIzol reagent. RNA quality was assessed, and all samples had an RNA Integrity Number (RIN) > 7.0. Strand-specific cDNA libraries were constructed and sequenced on a DNBSEQ-T7 platform. After quality control with FastQC (version 0.23.4) and Trimmomatic, clean reads were aligned to the Gallus gallus reference genome using HISAT2 (version 2.1.0). Gene expression levels were quantified and normalized. Differential expression analysis between groups was performed using DESeq2 (version 1.46.0), with genes satisfying |log2FoldChange| > 1 and adjusted p-value (padj) < 0.05 considered significant. Functional enrichment analysis of Gene Ontology (GO) terms and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathways was conducted using clusterProfiler (version 4.14.4).

Detection of butyric acid (BA) content by ELISA

The BA content in serum and liver tissue was determined according to the instructions provided in the ELISA kit (Cat. No. ELK8174, ELK Biotechnology CO., LTD, China).

Statistical analysis

Statistical data including hepatic function indices, antioxidant parameters, and immune cytokines were analyzed with one-way ANOVA, figures were generated using GraphPad Prism (10.1.2). For 16S rRNA gene sequencing and Transcriptome sequencing, the related data analysis and graphical visualization were performed on the high-performance computing cloud platform provided by Sangon Biotech (Shanghai) Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China) (https://ngs.sangon.com/flow). Statistical significance was defined as p < 0.05.

CIRC ameliorated oxidative stress in liver injury

Oxidative stress serves as a common pathological mechanism in the development of liver injury (Allameh et al., 2023). The impairment of hepatocyte function following liver injury induces oxidative stress, and sustained or intense oxidative stress increases the risk of hepatocyte death (Matuz-Mares et al., 2022). As shown in Fig. 3(a)–(f), chicks in the M group, under the combined stimulation of LPS/ENR, exhibited a significant reduction in systemic antioxidant capacity (T-AOC) and the levels of key antioxidant enzymes (SOD, CAT, GPx), while the levels of oxidative damage markers (MDA and NO) were markedly elevated. Dietary supplementation with CIRC reversed these abnormal indicators. In the CIRC-H-M group, the levels of T-AOC, SOD, CAT, and GPx were effectively restored to near-normal ranges, and the levels of MDA and NO were also significantly reduced, with effects comparable to those of the Gandan Granule. These results indicate that oxidative stress is indeed a core mechanism in the process of liver injury induced by combined LPS/ENR stimulation, and that CIRC, particularly at high doses, exerts its protective effect by enhancing endogenous antioxidant defenses and effectively scavenging excessive free radicals and lipid peroxidation products.Fig. 3Antioxidant capacity of liver tissue in different groups. CIRC modulates the inflammatory response in chicks

The balance of cellular inflammatory cytokines plays a critical role in the progression of liver injury towards further deterioration or regeneration (Jing et al., 2018). In this study, the transcriptional and protein levels of typical inflammatory cytokines (IL-1β, IL-6, IL-10, and TNF-α) were measured using qRT-PCR and ELISA. Compared with the C group, the levels of pro-inflammatory cytokines (TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6) were significantly elevated in liver homogenates of chicks from the M group, while the level of the anti-inflammatory cytokine IL-10 was markedly reduced (Fig. 4(a)–(h)). This indicates that combined LPS/ENR stimulation triggered a severe hepatic inflammatory response, accompanied by insufficient compensatory anti-inflammatory mechanisms. Compared with the M group, treatment with CIRC prevented the inflammatory response induced by LPS/ENR in chicks. Multi-omics analysis of the hepatoprotective mechanism of CIRC

CIRC modulates the structure of the gut microbiota

IRC is known to enter the liver and intestinal meridians, and previous studies have reported that it protects gastrointestinal health by modulating the structure of gut microbiota (Zeng et al., 2022a). To further elucidate the contribution of gut microbiota to the hepatoprotective effects of CIRC, we investigated relevant microbial changes. The sequencing quality of raw data for each sample is presented in Supplementary Table 1. Interestingly, according to the alpha diversity indices, short-term treatment with LPS/ENR increased the richness of the cecal microbiota in chicks, while treatment with the CIRC highly significantly increased the microbial diversity (Fig. 5(a)–(c)). LEfSe was used to identify microbial biomarkers in each group (Fig. 5(g)). The dominant genus in the C group was Erysipelatoclostridium; Eisenbergiella, Anaerofilum, and DTU089 served as signature microorganisms in the M group. After treatment with CIRC, Ruminococcaceae, Enterobacteriaceae, and Actinobacteria became predominant. In brief, the most notable changes following intervention with CIRC were the decreased abundance of Eisenbergiella, Anaerofilum, and DTU089, along with an increased abundance of Ruminococcaceae, Enterobacteriaceae, and Actinobacteria.

Further analysis of differential bacterial genera among groups revealed that, as shown in Fig. 5(h), the composition of gut microbiota in chicks was altered after three days of LPS/ENR treatment compared with the C group. The abundances of Anaerotruncus, Eisenbergiella, UC5-1-2E3, norank Clostridia vadinBB60 group, Unclassified Bacteria, Anaerofilum, Negativibacillus, and Ruminococcus were increased. Among these, Eisenbergiella and Anaerotruncus (under specific pathological conditions) are recognized as pro-inflammatory microbes, manifesting as increased release of inflammatory mediators from immune cells. In contrast, the abundances of unclassified Oscillospiraceae, Oscillospiraceae UCG-005, Butyricicoccus, Pseudoflavonifractor, and Ruminococcus torques group, which help maintain intestinal barrier integrity and gut homeostasis, were decreased. This indicates a compromised intestinal barrier, and the downregulation of Butyricicoccus further led to a reduced capacity of the host to produce butyrate. CIRC intervention may partially restore gut microbial dysbiosis by increasing the abundance of Blautia and Oscillospiraceae_UCG-005, decreasing the levels of Eisenbergiella, norank_Oscillospiraceae, UC5-1-2E3, and norank_Clostridia_vadinBB60_group, and elevating the abundance of Lachnoclostridium, Unclassified_Ruminococcaceae, Enterococcus, and Faecalibacterium, thereby further exerting hepatoprotective effects. Consistent with these changes in the gut microbiota, the ELISA results showed that the serum BA concentration was significantly decreased in the M group, whereas CIRC increased the serum BA level by increasing the abundance of butyrate-producing bacteria (Fig. 5(i)). These results suggest that LPS/ENR can induce gut microbiota dysbiosis in chicks, potentially stimulating pro‑inflammatory responses and aggravating immune dysregulation. Meanwhile, CIRC appears to exert its anti-inflammatory and immunomodulatory effects through modulation of the gut microbiota.

CIRC mediates hepatoprotection by reversing suppressed butanoate metabolism and modulating inflammatory pathways

The raw data information statistics of transcriptome sequencing for each sample are presented in Supplementary Table 2. To confirm the immunomodulatory role of CIRC in preventing liver injury, transcriptomic sequencing was performed on chick livers. As shown in Fig. 6(a)–(c), LPS/ENR treatment resulted in 1,461 differentially expressed genes (DEGs) compared with the C group, of which 690 were downregulated and 771 were upregulated. Pretreatment with CIRC yielded 126 downregulated and 106 upregulated DEGs relative to the M group. Fig. 6(d) presents the top 20 KEGG pathways enriched in the M group compared to the C group, among which the butanoate metabolism pathway was identified. Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) confirmed that this pathway was suppressed (Enrichment Score = –0.601, P = 0.032) (Fig. 6(e)), consistent with the gut microbiota sequencing results. Further measurement of BA concentration in liver tissue revealed that the BA level was significantly decreased in the M group, whereas CIRC treatment significantly increased the BA concentration in the liver (Fig. 6(f)). Changes in BA levels can affect the body's energy supply and regulate the level of inflammation. As shown in Fig. 6(g), LPS/ENR treatment suppressed the hepatic TCA cycle and aberrantly activated the JAK-STAT, TNF, and NF-κB pathways in chicks. Discussion

LPS is commonly employed in models of induced liver injury and is typically administered in combination with another agent (He et al., 2017). ENR is a widely used antibiotic in clinical practice for the prevention and treatment of bacterial infections in poultry (Grabowski et al., 2024); however, prolonged administration can lead to hepatotoxicity (Wang et al., 2025a). The combined use of LPS and ENR to induce liver injury in chicks offers a model with high clinical relevance, as it directly simulates one of the most common pathological scenarios in poultry farming—complications arising from bacterial infection and antibiotic therapy (Anwar and Mabrouk, 2023; Benameur et al., 2023; Wang et al., 2023). Serving as a bridge between basic research and veterinary clinical practice, this model holds species‑specific and translational potential for screening hepatoprotective drugs in poultry. The model has been successfully applied to evaluate the efficacy of several potential hepatoprotective agents, such as composite ammonium glycyrrhizin (Guo et al., 2020) and modified rougan decoction (Wang et al., 2023). In the present study, the combination of LPS and ENR resulted in significant histopathological damage to the liver, consistent with the well-established liver injury model reported in previous literature (Guo et al., 2012; Wang et al., 2023). However, a limitation is that there is no widely applied scoring standard for liver pathological damage in chickens, and therefore we could not objectively quantify these histopathological changes.

Oxidative stress has been established as a central driver in the initiation and progression of various liver diseases, including drug‑induced liver injury, alcoholic liver disease, non‑alcoholic fatty liver disease, and liver fibrosis (Allameh et al., 2023). LPS-induced hepatic oxidative stress is a complex process involving multiple cell types and signaling pathways (Zhong et al., 2016; Yang et al., 2025). It is characterized by an imbalance between the production of ROS and the endogenous antioxidant defense system, leading to damage of cellular macromolecules, which in turn triggers inflammation, apoptosis, and necrosis (Li et al., 2023a). In this study, the combined use of LPS/ENR downregulated the hepatic peroxisome pathway, leading to ROS accumulation. Concurrently, the levels of SOD, CAT, and GPx decreased, which hindered ROS clearance and ultimately promoted oxidative stress-induced liver injury. Numerous modern pharmacological studies have shown that traditional Chinese medicines (TCMs) and their active components can effectively alleviate oxidative stress through multi-target and multi-pathway, thereby exerting significant protective effects against liver injury (Qian et al., 2024; Wu et al., 2025). TCM compounds such as Chaihu Shugan San (Lou et al., 2022) and Ganshuang Granule (Liu and Wang, 2021) have been found to exert hepatoprotective effects closely associated with the modulation of the Nrf2/HO-1 pathway. Similarly, flavonoids and phenolic compounds derived from TCM including chlorogenic acid (Wei et al., 2025), baicalein (Dong et al., 2020; Wang et al., 2021), quercetin (Zhang et al., 2024a), and epicatechin (Zhang et al., 2025b), also effectively activate the Nrf2 pathway and alleviate oxidative stress. Although the Nrf2 pathway was not enriched in our transcriptomic data, CIRC, rich in flavonoids and phenolic acids (Zeng et al., 2022b), demonstrated potent oxidative stress-modulating activity. It significantly restored the diminished antioxidant capacity (T-AOC, SOD, CAT, GPx) after LPS/ENR challenge, and lowered oxidative damage markers (MDA, NO), thereby mediating substantial protection against liver injury.

Oxidative stress and inflammatory response are intimately linked in a reciprocal causal relationship during the pathological process of liver injury (Liu et al., 2020). ROS themselves serve as crucial inflammatory signaling molecules (Chen et al., 2024), capable of activating multiple pro-inflammatory signaling pathways, most notably the NF-κB pathway (Li et al., 2019, 2022). The activation of NF-κB leads to the robust production of pro-inflammatory cytokines (Fajgenbaum and June, 2020), such as TNF-α, IL-6, and IL-1β. These inflammatory mediators, in turn, can stimulate inflammatory cells to generate more ROS, thereby establishing a vicious cycle (Chen et al., 2025). A key advantage of TCM lies in its capacity to simultaneously target both of these interconnected processes (Pandey et al., 2024). Many herbal constituents exert antioxidant effects while concurrently inhibiting pro-inflammatory pathways like NF-κB (Li et al., 2019; Pan et al., 2025). For instance, glycyrrhizic acid not only elevates the levels of SOD and GSH but also effectively reduces the concentrations of TNF-α, IL-1β, and IL-6 (Wang et al., 2022). This dual mechanism, combining antioxidant and anti-inflammatory actions, enables TCMs to more comprehensively disrupt the pathological progression of liver injury (Zhang et al., 2025a). This aligns precisely with our research findings, CIRC demonstrated concurrent antioxidant and anti-inflammatory efficacy. It not only suppressed the transcription and expression of pro-inflammatory cytokines (TNF-α, IL-1β, and IL-6) but also upregulated the transcription and expression levels of the anti-inflammatory cytokine IL-10.

With the advancement of modern research, the bidirectional regulatory relationship between gut microbiota and liver health has become increasingly well-defined (Kanmani et al., 2020). The intestine is not only the primary site for nutrient absorption but also a crucial immune and metabolic organ (Shen et al., 2025). The homeostasis of its microbiota influences liver function through multiple pathways (Li et al., 2023a). The integrity of the intestinal barrier is paramount for preventing harmful substances from entering the systemic circulation (Juanola et al., 2024). By modulating gut microbiota, TCMs can enhance intestinal barrier function, thereby reducing intestinal permeability and lowering the levels of circulating LPS, which helps prevent the progression of liver injury (Gong et al., 2022; Zeng et al., 2024). Specifically, certain microbial genera, such as Blautia (de Mooij et al., 2023) and Oscillospiraceae UCG-005 (Zhuang et al., 2024), have been demonstrated to protect intestinal barrier integrity. In this study, dietary supplementation with CIRC upregulated the abundance of these beneficial gut microbes, thereby preventing further deterioration of liver injury in chicks.

The increase in the abundance of butyrate-producing bacteria in the gut microbiota resulting from pharmaceutical or dietary interventions is generally regarded as a favorable physiological response (Toribio-Mateas et al., 2021; Zhang et al., 2024b). Butyrate, one of the most important and biologically active metabolites derived from gut microbiota, plays a critical role in maintaining the intestinal barrier, regulating the immune microenvironment, and protecting liver health (Hsu et al., 2024). Acting as a histone deacetylase inhibitor, butyrate can directly target immune cells upon reaching the liver (Chriett et al., 2019), suppressing the expression of pro-inflammatory cytokines such as TNF-α and IL-6, promoting the production of anti-inflammatory cytokines including IL-10, and inducing the differentiation of regulatory T cells (Tregs) (Li et al., 2021). At the transcriptomic level, enhanced butanoate metabolism contributes to the suppression of the NF-κB pathway, alleviating the hepatic cytokine storm, and modulates the Toll-like receptor signaling pathway, thereby mitigating the liver's excessive immune response to endotoxins (Zhang et al., 2022). Notably, the Blautia, known for its butyrate-producing, anti-inflammatory, and protective effects against LPS-induced acute liver injury, exhibits a negative correlation with serum ALT level (Mao et al., 2023; Geng et al., 2024). In this study, combined LPS/ENR stimulation reduced the abundance of butyrate-producing bacteria, such as Butyricicoccus, in chicks, further decreased the butyrate levels in serum and liver tissue, and GSEA revealed suppressed butanoate metabolism in the liver, which subsequently led to aberrant activation of the NF-κB pathway, upregulation of TNF-α, IL-1β, and IL-6 expression, and downregulation of IL-10, ultimately contributing to liver injury in the chicks. CIRC exerted a positive regulatory effect on gut microbiota composition, significantly increasing the abundance of butyrate-producing bacteria, including Blautia, and significantly restoring BA levels in both serum and liver tissue. The elevated butyrate content further exerted hepatoprotective effects via the IL-17 signaling pathway, Toll-like receptor signaling pathway, and NF-κB signaling pathway.

Conclusion

In conclusion, this study demonstrates that CIRC significantly prevents LPS/ENR-induced liver injury in chicks. The hepatoprotective mechanism is primarily mediated by restoring the abundance of butyrate‑producing bacteria in the gut, which elevates BA levels in both serum and liver tissue and alleviates the suppressed hepatic butanoate metabolism pathway. Subsequently, CIRC modulates the IL-17, Toll-like receptor, and NF-κB signaling pathways, thereby mitigating inflammation and oxidative stress, and exerting hepatoprotective effects.

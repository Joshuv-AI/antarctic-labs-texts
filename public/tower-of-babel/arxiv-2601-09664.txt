Constant-roll β \beta -exponential inflation: Palatini formalism
Ozan Sargın

Abstract
In this paper, we explore the inflationary dynamics of the β \beta -exponential potential model, where a scalar field couples to quadratic ( R + R 2 ) (R+R^{2}) gravity. In this model, the inflaton is the field that determines the size of the extra dimension. We employ the Palatini formalism to derive the resulting Einstein-frame generalized k k -inflation effective theory, which we analyze under the assumption that the constant-roll condition is satisfied. We scan the parameter space for inflationary predictions, specifically the spectral index n s n_{s} and the tensor-to-scalar ratio r r , ensuring consistency with the results from ACT DR6. The compliant regions are depicted accordingly. For a suitable range of the model parameters, the values obtained for the inflationary observables align with the most recent observations by the Atacama Cosmology Telescope (ACT) collaboration and/or the Planck mission.

2
f(R,\phi)
gravity in the Palatini formalism

We consider a Lagrangian with scalar field
\phi
that is non-minimally coupled to gravity, including a quadratic curvature term. The corresponding action in the Jordan frame is

\mathcal{S}=\int\!\mathrm{d}^{4}x\,\sqrt{-g}\left\{\frac{1}{2}(1+\xi\phi^{2})R+\frac{\alpha}{4}R^{2}-\frac{1}{2}(\nabla\phi)^{2}-V(\phi)\right\},

(2.1)

where
\xi
and
\alpha
are dimensionless parameters, and we employ natural units, i.e., we assume
M_{P}^{2}\equiv 1
.

The same theory can be expressed in the equivalent scalar-tensor representation as

\mathcal{S}=\int\!\mathrm{d}^{4}x\,\sqrt{-g}\left\{\frac{1}{2}(1+\xi\phi^{2}+\alpha{\chi^{\prime}}\,^{2})R-\frac{\alpha}{4}{\chi^{\prime}}^{4}-\frac{1}{2}(\nabla\phi)^{2}-V(\phi)\right\},

(2.2)

where
\chi^{\prime}
is an auxiliary scalar that satisfies
{\chi^{\prime}}\,^{2}=R
on-shell. After letting
1+\alpha{\chi^{\prime}}\,^{2}\!=\!\chi^{2}
, the action in (
2.2
) becomes

\mathcal{S}=\int\!\mathrm{d}^{4}x\,\sqrt{-g}\left\{\frac{1}{2}(\chi^{2}+\xi\phi^{2})R-\frac{(\chi^{2}-1)^{2}}{4\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}(\nabla\phi)^{2}-V(\phi)\right\}.

(2.3)

The theory can be conformally transformed into the Einstein frame via Weyl rescaling the metric through

\bar{g}_{\mu\nu}=(\chi^{2}+\xi\phi^{2})\,g_{\mu\nu}.

(2.4)

After dropping the bars on the metric and such, we obtain

\mathcal{S}=\int\!\mathrm{d}^{4}x\,\sqrt{-g}\left\{\frac{1}{2}R-\frac{1}{2}\,\frac{(\nabla\phi)^{2}}{(\chi^{2}+\xi\phi^{2})}-\frac{4\alpha V(\phi)+(\chi^{2}-1)^{2}}{4\alpha(\chi^{2}+\xi\phi^{2})^{2}}\right\}.

(2.5)

The key aspect of the Palatini formalism lies in the fact that the Ricci scalar
R=g^{\mu\nu}R_{\mu\nu}(\Gamma)
undergoes a rescaling during a conformal transformation. This implies that the scalar degree of freedom
\chi
maintains its auxiliary role. As a result, we can eliminate the non-dynamical field
\chi
from the action by solving the constraint equation
\delta_{\chi}\mathcal{S}=0
for
\chi^{2}
.The result is

\chi^{2}=\frac{1+\xi\phi^{2}+4\alpha V(\phi)+\alpha\xi\phi^{2}(\nabla\phi)^{2}}{(1+\xi\phi^{2})-\alpha(\nabla\phi)^{2}}\,.

(2.6)

Substituting (
2.6
) back into (
2.5
), we arrive at the effective action

\mathcal{S}=\int\!\mathrm{d}^{4}x\,\sqrt{-g}\left(\frac{1}{2}R+\mathcal{L}(\phi,X)\right),

(2.7)

which is expressed in terms of the Lagrangian density

\mathcal{L}(\phi,X)\equiv A(\phi)X+B(\phi)X^{2}-U(\phi)\,,

(2.8)

that belongs to a class of generalized
k
-inflation effective theory
[
161
]
.

The Lagrangian features a quartic kinetic term alongside a quadratic one, both with field-dependent coefficients
A(\phi)
and
B(\phi)
. In equation (
2.8
),
X
represents the kinetic energy density

X\,\equiv\,\frac{1}{2}(\nabla\phi)^{2}.

(2.9)

Also, the field-dependent coefficients
A(\phi)
,
B(\phi)
, and the effective potential
U(\phi)
read

\left.\begin{array}[]{r}A(\phi)\,\equiv\,-\left[(1+\xi\phi^{2})+\frac{4\alpha V(\phi)}{(1+\xi\phi^{2})}\right]^{-1}\\
\\
B(\phi)\,\equiv\,2\alpha\left[\,(1+\xi\phi^{2})^{2}+4\alpha V(\phi)\right]^{-1}\,=\,-\frac{2\alpha A(\phi)}{(1+\xi\phi^{2})}\\
\\
U(\phi)\,\equiv\,V(\phi)\,\left[\,(1+\xi\phi^{2})^{2}+4\alpha V(\phi)\right]^{-1}\,=\,-\frac{V(\phi)A(\phi)}{(1+\xi\phi^{2})}\;.\end{array}\quad\right\}

(2.10)

The energy-momentum tensor of the source field
\phi
obtained by varying (
2.7
) with respect to the metric
g_{\mu\nu}
reads

T_{\mu\nu}\,=\,-(A+2BX)\left(\nabla_{\mu}\phi\right)\left(\nabla_{\nu}\phi\right)+g_{\mu\nu}(AX+BX^{2}-U)\,.

(2.11)

In a spatially flat Friedmann-Lemaître-Robertson-Walker (FLRW) background metric, assuming the scalar field is homogeneous in space and varies only with time, we can derive its corresponding energy density and pressure using
\rho=T_{00}
and
T_{ij}=p\,g_{ij}=\mathcal{L}\,g_{ij}
, yielding

\displaystyle\rho

\displaystyle=A(\phi)X+3B(\phi)X^{2}+U(\phi),

(2.12)

\displaystyle p

\displaystyle=A(\phi)X+B(\phi)X^{2}-U(\phi).

(2.13)

Einstein’s equations of motion are:

3H^{2}=\rho,

(2.14)

\dot{\rho}+3H(\rho+p)=0,

(2.15)

where the dot denotes the derivative with respect to cosmic time
t
and
H
is the Hubble parameter. Combining (
2.14
) and (
2.15
) gives

2\dot{H}+3H^{2}=-p.

(2.16)

Manipulating Einstein’s equations together with the definitions of energy density and pressure gives the scalar field equation of motion as

\ddot{\phi}(A+6BX)+3H\dot{\phi}(A+2BX)-A^{\prime}X-3B^{\prime}X^{2}=U^{\prime}

(2.17)

where the derivative with respect to
\phi
is represented using prime notation.

3
Constant-roll
k
-inflation dynamics

The scalar field
\phi
, which represents the sole dynamical scalar degree of freedom, is considered to be a candidate for the inflaton. We will assume that the condition for constant-roll is met, as expressed by the equation

\ddot{\phi}=\kappa H\dot{\phi},

(3.1)

where
\kappa
is a dimensionless real parameter.

It is important to note that the constant-roll condition (
3.1
) approaches the slow-roll condition in the limit of
\kappa\ll 1
, or it becomes the ultra slow-roll condition when
\kappa=-3
exactly.

The slow-roll parameters (SRPs) are defined as follows
[
123
]
:

\epsilon_{1}=-\frac{\dot{H}}{H^{2}},\qquad\epsilon_{2}=-\frac{\ddot{\phi}}{H\dot{\phi}},\qquad\epsilon_{3}=\frac{\dot{F}}{2HF},\qquad\epsilon_{4}=\frac{\dot{E}}{2HE},

(3.2)

where

F=\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial R},\qquad E=-\frac{F}{2X}\left(X\,\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial X}+2X^{2}\,\frac{\partial^{2}\mathcal{L}}{\partial X^{2}}\right).

(3.3)

These parameters are introduced under the assumption that the condition
\dot{\phi}^{2}/2\ll U(\phi)
holds during at least the initial stages of inflation. The second SRP is constrained by the constant-roll condition (
3.1
) to be
\epsilon_{2}=-\kappa
. Moreover, since we are in the Einstein frame where
F=1/2
, it follows that
\epsilon_{3}=0
.

In terms of SRPs, we can express observable quantities such as the scalar spectral index (also known as the primordial tilt)
n_{s}
and the tensor-to-scalar ratio
r
as follows
[
162
,
163
,
164
]
:

\displaystyle n_{s}

\displaystyle=1-2\frac{2\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{2}-\epsilon_{3}+\epsilon_{4}}{1-\epsilon_{1}},

(3.4)

\displaystyle r

\displaystyle=4|\epsilon_{1}|C_{s},

(3.5)

where
C_{s}
represents the wave speed that characterizes the propagation of primordial perturbations, given by the equation:

C_{s}^{2}=\frac{\mathcal{L}_{X}}{\mathcal{L}_{X}+2X\mathcal{L}_{XX}}=\frac{A(\phi)-B(\phi)\dot{\phi}^{2}}{A(\phi)-3B(\phi)\dot{\phi}^{2}}=\frac{1+\xi\phi^{2}+2\alpha\dot{\phi}^{2}}{1+\xi\phi^{2}+6\alpha\dot{\phi}^{2}}\;.

(3.6)

It is important to note that the sound speed
C_{s}
meets the condition
0<{C_{s}}^{2}<1
. On the other hand, the scalar power spectrum, evaluated up to first order in the SRPs, is given by
[
7
]

P_{S}\,\approx\,\frac{H^{2}}{8\pi^{2}\epsilon_{1}}\,=\,\frac{1}{72\pi^{2}}\frac{\left(AX+3BX^{2}+U\right)^{2}}{\left(AX+2BX^{2}\right)}\,,

(3.7)

which should yield the observed value
P_{S}\approx 2.1\times 10^{-9}

[
14
,
15
]
at horizon crossing. Similarly, the integral expression for the number of
e
-folds is given as

N\,=\,\int_{\phi_{i}}^{\phi_{f}}\frac{d\phi}{\dot{\phi}}H\,=\,\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\int_{\phi_{i}}^{\phi_{f}}\frac{d\phi}{\dot{\phi}}\sqrt{AX+3BX^{2}+U}\,.

(3.8)

The inflationary analysis can be conducted once all the expressions for the slow-roll parameters (SRPs), observational indices, and the number of
e
-folds are expressed as a function of the field
\phi
. To achieve this, we first need to solve the equation of motion of the scalar field (
2.17
) to find an expression for
\dot{\phi}[\phi]
. Before that, however, we must derive an approximate expression for the Hubble parameter,
H
.
We derive an expression for
H
in terms of
\dot{\phi}^{2}/2U
by combining equations (
2.12
) and (
2.14
) as follows:

\displaystyle H

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{U}{3}\left(1-A\left(\frac{\dot{\phi}^{2}}{2U}\right)+3BU\left(\frac{\dot{\phi}^{2}}{2U}\right)^{2}\right)}

\displaystyle H

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{U}{3}}\left[1-\frac{A}{2}\left(\frac{\dot{\phi}^{2}}{2U}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\left(3BU-\frac{A^{2}}{4}\right)\left(\frac{\dot{\phi}^{2}}{2U}\right)^{2}\right]

(3.9)

where, in the last step, a Taylor expansion in the small parameter
\dot{\phi}^{2}/2U
is performed.

The next step is to substitute the constant-roll condition (
3.1
) into the equation of motion (
2.17
) to derive a first-order differential equation in terms of
\dot{\phi}
:

4H\dot{\phi}A(\kappa+3)-12H\dot{\phi}^{3}B(\kappa+1)+2A^{\prime}\dot{\phi}^{2}-3B^{\prime}\dot{\phi}^{4}-4U^{\prime}=0\>.

(3.10)

Substituting (
3

3.10
), we obtain

\sqrt{\frac{U}{3}}\,\left(3B(\kappa+1)+\frac{A^{2}}{4U}(\kappa+3)\right)\dot{\phi}^{3}-\frac{1}{2}A^{\prime}\dot{\phi}^{2}-A\sqrt{\frac{U}{3}}(\kappa+3)\dot{\phi}+U^{\prime}=0,

(3.11)

where we kept only terms up to
\mathcal{O}(\dot{\phi}^{3})

After making a change of variables

x\,\equiv\,\dot{\phi}-\frac{A^{\prime}}{6\gamma}

(3.12)

\dot{\phi}

where

(3.14)

\displaystyle c_{1}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\gamma}\left[(\kappa+3)A\sqrt{\frac{U}{3}}+\frac{(A^{\prime})^{2}}{12\gamma}\right],

(3.15)

\displaystyle c_{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\gamma}\left[U^{\prime}-A(\kappa+3)\,\frac{A^{\prime}}{6\gamma}\sqrt{\frac{U}{3}}-\frac{(A^{\prime})^{3}}{108\gamma^{2}}\right]\>.

The real-valued solution to (

x=\frac{\left(-9c_{0}+\sqrt{3}\sqrt{4{c_{1}}^{3}+27{c_{0}}^{2}}\right)^{1/3}}{2^{1/3}\,3^{2/3}}-\frac{\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^{1/3}c_{1}}{\left(-9c_{0}+\sqrt{3}\sqrt{4{c_{1}}^{3}+27{c_{0}}^{2}}\right)^{1/3}}\>\>.

(3.17)

The scalar spectral index, tensor-to-scalar ratio, and scalar power spectrum are all functions of the slow roll parameters, as indicated in equations (

the SRPs become

\epsilon_{1}=\frac{3AX+6BX^{2}}{AX+3BX^{2}+U}\>\,,

(3.18)

\epsilon_{2}=-\kappa\,,

\epsilon_{4}=\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\,\frac{\dot{\phi}(A^{\prime}+6B^{\prime}X)+12\kappa BHX}{(A+6BX)\sqrt{AX+3BX^{2}+U}}\,.

(3.21)

The coefficients

In this section, we present the
\beta
-exponential model

V(\phi)=V_{0}\>\exp{(-\lambda\phi)}\;,

(4.1)

and is investigated in
[

This pure exponential potential is modified using a deformation parameter
\beta

V(\phi)
is defined:

V(\phi)=V_{0}\>\exp_{1-\beta}{(-\lambda\phi)}\;,

(4.2)

where the generalized
\beta
-exponential function
\exp_{1-\beta}
is used. The definition of this function is as follows:

\displaystyle\mbox{$\exp_{1-\beta}{(x)}=$}\;\left\{\begin{tabular}[]{l}$\left[1+\beta x\right]^{1/\beta}\;,\>1+\beta x>0$\\
\\
$0$, \> otherwise.\end{tabular}\right.

This generalized function satisfies,
\forall

(x,\;y)<0
, the following identities

\exp_{1-\beta}\left[\ln_{1-\beta}({x})\right]={x}\;,

(4.7)

and

\ln_{1-\beta}({x})+\ln_{1-\beta}({y})=\ln_{1-\beta}({xy})-\beta\left[\ln_{1-\beta}({x})\ln_{1-\beta}({y})\right],

(4.8)

where
\ln_{1-\beta}({x})=(x^{\beta}-1)/\beta
is the generalized logarithmic function.

Using the definition provided in equation (
4
), we can write the explicit form of the
\beta
-exponential potential, as given in equation (
4.2
). This results in the following expression:

V(\phi)=V_{0}\left[1+\beta\left(-\lambda\phi\right)\right]^{1/\beta}\;.

(4.9)

In this equation, the parameter
\beta
determines the degree of interpolation between pure exponential (power-law) inflation and a wider range of behaviors. By adjusting the value of
\beta
, one can generate potentials that are effectively steeper or flatter than a pure exponential form. The variable
\lambda
is a dimensionless parameter. This added flexibility leads to variations in the slow-roll parameters, which in turn affect the observables such as the spectral index
n_{s}
and the tensor-to-scalar ratio
r
. Consequently, the model can be calibrated to fit the constraints from the Cosmic Microwave Background (CMB) for various ranges of
\beta
and
\lambda
. The subsequent section will focus on the numerical analysis of how these parameters influence the observational indices.

Our numerical analysis of the inflationary parameters employs two distinct methods for calculating the e-folding parameter,

\displaystyle N_{*}\approx 64.7+\frac{1}{2}\ln\rho_{*}-\frac{1}{4}\ln\rho_{e}\>.

(5.1)

The definition of

is provided by:

\rho_{*}=\frac{3\pi^{2}\Delta^{2}_{\mathcal{R}}r}{2},

(5.2)

6
Summary and Conclusions

In this article, we extend the non-minimally coupled
\beta
-exponential inflationary model to the Palatini formalism, incorporating an
R^{2}
term within the framework of the constant-roll inflation scenario characterized by
\ddot{\phi}\sim\beta H\dot{\phi}
. We derive the equations of motion for the system and analyze the effective theory within the Einstein frame. The resulting generalized
k
-inflation effective theory accommodates a broad spectrum of inflationary solutions that align with the most recent constraints from cosmic microwave background observations.

By exploring a suitable range of model parameters, we calculate the inflationary observables, including the spectral index
n_{s}
, the tensor-to-scalar ratio
r
, which align with the constraints on
r
that are driven by the
BK18
data
[
15
]
, and the constraints on
n_{s}
that are driven by either
Planck

[
14
]
or
ACT

[
16
]
or both.

The systematic analysis reveals that constant-roll dynamics exhibit a more complex range of phenomena compared to slow-roll dynamics. Additionally, the Palatini formulation yields different predictions than the metric approach, particularly when non-minimal couplings and higher curvature terms are involved. The distinctive attribute of the model lies in its flexibility through the parameters (
\beta
), (
\lambda
), (
\kappa
), (
\xi
), and (
\alpha
), which facilitate its alignment with contemporary observational data. This adaptability renders the model a compelling candidate for elucidating early Universe inflation.

A new type of multi-branch periodic orbits in dyonic black holes
Chao-Hui Wang a,b,c, Yu-Peng Zhang a,b,c, Tao Zhu d,e, Shao-Wen Wei a,b,c

Abstract
In this work, we investigate bound periodic orbits of timelike particles in the spacetime of dyonic black holes arising from quasi-topological electromagnetic theory. By varying the coupling parameter α 1 \alpha_{1} , the corresponding black hole solutions exhibit diverse horizon structures, including naked singularities and black holes with one to four horizons. We find that for sufficiently small α 1 \alpha_{1} , the metric function f ⁡ ( r ) f(r) becomes non-monotonic outside the event horizon in spacetimes with one or two horizons, while in all other cases, f ⁡ ( r ) f(r) remains strictly monotonic. In the non-monotonic regime, the radial effective potential develops a double-barrier structure, allowing the emergence of multiple marginally bound orbits and multiple branches of periodic orbits associated with the same rational number q q . Although differing in radial structure, these orbit branches are topologically equivalent. Remarkably, when the outer potential barrier exceeds unity, bound orbits with energy E > 1 E>1 become possible, in addition to the standard E < 1 E<1 branches. When the peak reaches E = 1 E=1 , up to three distinct bound orbit branches may coexist. We also identify a novel eccentricity behavior: the innermost branch becomes increasingly circular with increasing energy or angular momentum, while outer branches exhibit greater eccentricity and a larger apastron–periastron separation. These features, absent in previous studies, are unique signatures of non-monotonic metric functions. In contrast, monotonic cases yield a single-well potential, a unique marginally bound orbit, and a single periodic orbit branch per q q , consistent with earlier findings. Our results highlight the critical role of the metric function’s shape in determining the orbital structure around dyonic black holes.

Motivated by this, the present work aims to investigate the properties of periodic orbits of a massive particle in the background of dyonic black holes within quasi-topological electromagnetism. By analyzing the geometric structure and associated rational parameters of the periodic orbits, we seek to explore the potential observational imprints in gravitational waveforms and orbits of the particle. This study not only contributes to the theoretical understanding of modified gravity scenarios but also provides valuable templates for future observational efforts targeting EMRIs.

Black Hole Solution and Geodesics

II.1
Black hole with quasi-topological electromagnetism

We begin with the general line element for a static, spherically symmetric black hole spacetime, given by

\displaystyle ds^{2}=-f(r)dt^{2}+\frac{1}{g(r)}dr^{2}+r^{2}\left(d\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta d\phi^{2}\right),

(1)

where the metric functions
f(r)
and

The spacetime considered in this work is the dyonic black hole solution proposed in Ref.

\displaystyle S=\frac{1}{16\pi}\int\sqrt{-g}d^{4}x\left[R-\alpha_{1}F^{2}-\alpha_{2}\left((F^{2})^{2}-2F^{(4)}\right)\right],

This theory introduces higher-order electromagnetic corrections to the standard Maxwell action. Under the assumption of global polarization, the quasi-topological term does not affect the Maxwell equations or the energy-momentum tensor, but it modifies the spacetime geometry nontrivially. \displaystyle f(r)=g(r)

\displaystyle+\frac{q^{2}}{\alpha_{1}r^{2}}{}_{2}F_{1}\left[\frac{1}{4},1;\frac{5}{4};-\frac{4p^{2}\alpha_{2}}{r^{4}\alpha_{1}}\right],

II.2
Geodesics and effective potential

To study the motion of a test particle in the dyonic black hole background, we begin with the Lagrangian describing a freely falling massive particle,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}=\frac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}\frac{dx^{\mu}}{d\tau}\frac{dx^{\nu}}{d\tau}=-1,

(4)

where
\tau
denotes the particle’s proper time. Due to the spherical symmetry of the spacetime, without loss of generality, we can confine the motion to the equatorial plane by setting
\theta=\pi/2
. Then the generalized momentum
p_{\mu}
are defined by

\displaystyle p_{\mu}=\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial x^{\mu}}.

(5)

Given the static, spherically symmetric nature of the spacetime, the metric (
1
) admits two Killing vectors:
\xi^{\mu}=(\partial_{t})^{\mu}

and
\psi^{\mu}=(\partial_{\phi})^{\mu}
. Consequently, there are two conserved quantities along geodesics: the energy
E
and angular momentum

L
per unit mass, given by

\displaystyle p_{t}=f(r)\dot{t}=-E,\quad p_{\phi}=r^{2}\dot{\phi}=L,

(6)

where the dot denotes the derivative with respect to the proper time. For a massive particle, the normalization condition of the four-velocity leads to

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu}\dot{x}^{\mu}\dot{x}^{\nu}=-1.

(7)

Combining these relations yields the first-order equations of motion in the
t
,
r
, and
\phi
directions:

\displaystyle\dot{t}=\frac{E}{f(r)},\quad\dot{\phi}=\frac{L}{r^{2}},\quad\dot{r}^{2}=E^{2}-V_{\mathrm{eff}}.

(8)

Here the radial effective potential
V_{\mathrm{eff}}
reads

V_{\mathrm{eff}}=f(r)\left(1+\frac{L^{2}}{r^{2}}\right).

(9)

To understand the bound motion of the particles, one needs to analyze the structure of the effective potential. In particular, we focus on two critical types of circular orbits. (1) Marginally bound orbit (MBO): This orbit represents the unstable circular trajectory at the threshold between bound and unbound motion. It satisfies the following requirements

V_{\mathrm{eff}}=1,\quad\partial_{r}V_{\mathrm{eff}}=0.

(10)

(2) Innermost stable circular orbit (ISCO): This orbit marks the innermost radius at which stable circular motion is possible. The conditions for ISCO are

V_{\mathrm{eff}}=E^{2},\quad\partial_{r}V_{\mathrm{eff}}=0,\quad\partial_{r,r}V_{\mathrm{eff}}=0.

(11)

These characteristic orbits provide a basis for understanding the dynamics of massive particles in strong gravitational fields and are particularly relevant in the analysis of periodic orbits in the dyonic black hole spacetime.

III
Periodic orbits

Periodic orbits are a special class of bound geodesics in which a test particle returns exactly to its initial position after a finite proper time
Levin:2008mq
. In the context of a static, spherically symmetric black hole, where the motion is confined to the equatorial plane,
\theta=\pi/2
, the particle’s trajectory involves only the radial
r
and azimuthal
\phi
directions. Each non-circular (eccentric) equatorial orbit is characterized by two fundamental orbital frequencies,
\omega_{r}
and
\omega_{\phi}
. The radial frequency
\omega_{r}
, which quantifies the periodic motion between the periastron and apastron, is given by

\omega_{r}=\frac{2\pi}{T_{r}},

(12)

where
T_{r}
is the radial period—the time taken to travel from apastron to periastron and back. The azimuthal frequency
\omega_{\phi}
, defined as the time-averaged rate of angular advance in the
\phi
-direction over one radial period, is expressed as

\omega_{\phi}=\frac{1}{T_{r}}\int_{0}^{T_{r}}\frac{d\phi}{dt}dt=\frac{\Delta\phi_{r}}{T_{r}},

(13)

where
\Delta\phi_{r}
is the accumulated azimuthal angle during a single radial cycle.

For generic orbits, the ratio
\omega_{\phi}/\omega_{r}
is irrational. However, for periodic orbits, this frequency ratio must be rational, and can be written in the form
Levin:2008mq

\frac{\omega_{\phi}}{\omega_{r}}=\frac{\Delta\phi_{r}}{2\pi}=1+q,

(14)

where
q
is a positive rational number known as the rational number label of the orbit.
The total azimuthal deviation during one radial cycle is
\Delta\phi_{r}

\displaystyle\Delta\phi_{r}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\oint\mathrm{d}\phi=2\int_{r_{a}}^{r_{p}}\frac{\dot{\phi}}{\dot{r}}\mathrm{d}r

(15)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\int_{r_{a}}^{r_{p}}{\frac{L}{r^{2}\sqrt{E^{2}-f(r)(1+\frac{L^{2}}{r^{2}})}}}dr,

where
r_{a}
and
r_{p}
are the apastron and periastron radii, respectively.

On the other hand, the rational number
q
can be uniquely expressed in the following canonical form:

q=w+\frac{v}{z},

(16)

where
w\geq 0
is the integer part and
v/z
is the irreducible fractional part with coprime integers
v
and
z
satisfying

1\leq v\leq z-1.

(17)

Each periodic orbit is thus uniquely specified by a triplet
(z,~w,~v)
, or equivalently, by the rational number

q
, which captures the amount of azimuthal advance per radial cycle. This leads to the geometric relation
Levin:2008mq

\Delta\phi_{r}=2\pi(1+w+\frac{v}{z})=\frac{\Delta\phi}{z},

(18)

where
\Delta\phi
is the total azimuthal angle accumulated over one complete orbit. The relation
\Delta\phi=z\Delta\phi_{r}
implies that the particle completes exactly
z
radial oscillations while executing an integral number of revolutions in the azimuthal direction.

Periodic orbits serve as fundamental building blocks in understanding the geodesic structure of strong gravitational fields. They not only capture the core dynamics of bound motion, but also provide key insights into the generation of gravitational radiation in EMRIs and the geometric fingerprints observable in black hole shadows and relativistic precession phenomena.

with a summary of the periodic orbits in the dyonic black hole background characterized by a non-monotonic metric function

outside the event horizon. Specifically, for the parameter range

, the function

and

). Our study focused on the dynamical consequences of the double-well potentials, particularly when the particle energy satisfies
E<1
. Under this condition, up to three distinct periodic orbits may exist for a given rational number

. This multi-valued structure is clearly reflected in the
E-q

Such behaviors—namely, the coexistence of multiple periodic solutions for the same rational number

, and their distinct radial profiles under varying angular momentum—are exclusive to the
f(r)
non-monotonic scenario. They highlight the profound influence of the black hole’s spacetime geometry on the dynamics of test particles, and underscore the importance of considering full multiparameter families of solutions when characterizing periodic motion in strong gravitational fields.
To characterize the orbital structure, we introduced the winding number
n
, defined in Eq. (
20

or
L_{1}
), the radial range (from periastron to apastron) decreases as either the energy or angular momentum increases. In contrast, the outer branches (e.g.,

This inverse radial trend—wherein the innermost orbit contracts as energy or angular momentum increases—is a distinctive consequence of the non-monotonic metric structure in the outer region of the two-horizon dyonic black hole. While the underlying mechanism driving this phenomenon remains unclear, it represents an intriguing deviation from classical expectations and merits further investigation in future work.

V
Conclusions and discussions

In this work, we have investigated the properties of periodic orbits for massive particles in the background of dyonic black holes arising from quasi-topological electrodynamics. Focusing on a family of black hole solutions parametrized by
\alpha_{1}
, we varied this parameter continuously from
\alpha_{1}=1.025755
to
\alpha_{1}=43.31447
, thereby spanning configurations with 1-/2-, 3-/4-, and again 1-/2- horizon structures (as illustrated in FIG.
1
). This range gives rise to a rich set of dyonic black hole geometries, offering fertile ground for exploring periodic orbital motion.

We began by analyzing the geodesic motion of massive particles confined to the equatorial plane. The radial equation of motion was recast in terms of an effective potential
V_{\mathrm{eff}}(r)
, which can be used to define the MBOs and ISCOs. By constraining the angular momentum
L
of particles within the range
L\in[L_{\mathrm{ISCO}},~L_{\mathrm{MBO}}]
, we ensured that the motion of the test massive particle was bounded between the potential wells, making periodic orbits possible. These orbits were then studied in the framework developed by Levin et al.
Levin:2008mq
, using rational numbers
q=q(z,w,v)=w+v/z
that relate the radial and azimuthal oscillation frequencies through three integers
(z,w,v)
. The corresponding geodesic equations governing such periodic orbits were also established.

We then examined the behavior of periodic orbits across the different horizon structures identified by varying
\alpha_{1}
:

1. For
1.025755\leq\alpha_{1}<1.07317
(1-/2- horizon black holes): The metric function
f(r)
exhibits non-monotonicity outside the event horizon, resulting in a double-barrier effective potential structure. In this regime, three MBO solutions emerge, though we only focused on the two outer ones,
L_{\mathrm{MBO2}}
and
L_{\mathrm{MBO3}}
, which define the dual potential wells. The inner potential barrier can exceed the critical value
V_{\mathrm{eff}}=1
, while the outer barrier reaches exactly
V_{\mathrm{eff}}=1
at
L=L_{\mathrm{MBO3}}
. These double-well structures support multiple bound trajectories for
E<1
, and remarkably, also allow bound orbits for
E>1
, which is uncommon in single-well potentials. This multi-valued behavior is reflected in the non-monotonic
E-q
and
L-q
curves (see FIGs.
7
and
8
), leading to multiple periodic orbits associated with the same rational number
q
. We presented explicit trajectories corresponding to both the multi-branch
q
values and the
E>1
single-branch cases. Moreover, using the triplet
(z,w,v)
, we defined a new winding number
n
(
20
) characterizing the number of azimuthal loops per radial cycle, and plotted the radial turning behavior via
r-n
diagrams. These results demonstrate that all branches corresponding to the same rational number
q
are topologically equivalent, whether
L
or
E
is held fixed. Interestingly, the radial oscillation width behaves oppositely in the inner and outer periodic orbits as
E
or
L
varies. These features—multiple periodic orbits for the same
q
, and the existence of
E>1
bound orbits—are uniquely tied to the non-monotonicity of
f(r)
, much like the emergence of multiple photon spheres or classes of time-like circular orbits in similar double-barrier systems
Wei:2020rbh
;
Ye:2023gmk
.

2. For
1.07317\leq\alpha_{1}\leq 1.09859
(3-/4- horizon black holes): The metric function becomes monotonic outside the event horizon. The effective potential is of the standard single-well type, supporting only one MBO solution. Consequently, only a single branch of periodic orbits is permitted, all with energy
E<1
. These periodic orbits exhibit regular behavior, with
E-q
and
L-q
curves being monotonic.

3. For
1.09859<\alpha_{1}\leq 43.31447
(1-/2- horizon black holes): Although the black hole possesses only one or two horizons, the function
f(r)

remains monotonic outside the horizon. However, the radius of the event horizon itself varies non-monotonically with
\alpha_{1}
, which in turn leads to non-monotonic variations in the MBO radius
r_{\mathrm{MBO}}
, ISCO radius
r_{\mathrm{ISCO}}
, and the associated
E-L
parameter regions. Still, due to the monotonicity of
f(r)
, only a single MBO solution exists, and the effective potential remains single-peaked. Periodic orbits are only supported for
L\in[L_{\mathrm{ISCO}},~L_{\mathrm{MBO}}]
and
E<1
, resulting again in a unique periodic orbit per rational
q
.

In summary, our study reveals that the structure of periodic orbits in dyonic black hole spacetimes is highly sensitive to the horizon structure and the behavior of the metric function
f(r)
. In particular, non-monotonicity of
f(r)
outside the horizon—possible in multi-horizon configurations—gives rise to novel phenomena such as multi-branch periodic orbits and bound orbits with energy exceeding unity. These results offer new insights into particle dynamics and potential observables in modified gravity theories involving nontrivial black hole geometries.

Graph parameters that are coarsely equivalent to path-length Thanks: August 24, 2026
Feodor F. Dragan, Ekkehard Köhler

Abstract
Two graph parameters are said to be coarsely equivalent if they are within constant factors from each other for every graph G G . Recently, several graph parameters were shown to be coarsely equivalent to tree-length. Recall that the length of a tree-decomposition 𝒯 ⁡ ( G ) {\cal T}(G) of a graph G G is the largest diameter of a bag in 𝒯 ⁡ ( G ) {\cal T}(G) , and the tree-length 𝗍𝗅 ⁡ ( G ) {\sf tl}(G) of G G is the minimum of the length, over all tree-decompositions of G G . Similarly, the length of a path-decomposition 𝒫 ⁡ ( G ) {\cal P}(G) of a graph G G is the largest diameter of a bag in 𝒫 ⁡ ( G ) {\cal P}(G) , and the path-length 𝗉𝗅 ⁡ ( G ) {\sf pl}(G) of G G is the minimum of the length, over all path-decompositions of G G . In this paper, we present several graph parameters that are coarsely equivalent to path-length. Among other results, we show that the path-length of a graph G G is small if and only if one of the following equivalent conditions is true: • G G can be embedded to an unweighted caterpillar tree (equivalently, to a graph of path-width one) with a small additive distortion; • there is a constant r ≥ 0 r\geq 0 such that for every triple of vertices u , v , w u,v,w of G G , disk of radius r r centered at one of them intercepts all paths connecting the two others; • G G has a k k -dominating shortest path with small k ≥ 0 k\geq 0 ; • G G has a k ′ k^{\prime} -dominating pair with small k ′ ≥ 0 k^{\prime}\geq 0 ; • some power G μ G^{\mu} of G G is an AT-free (or even a cocomparability) graph for a small integer μ ≥ 0 \mu\geq 0 .

Two graph parameters

Coarse equivalency of two graph parameters is useful in at least two scenarios

Secondly, since a constant bound on one parameter implies a constant bound on the other, one can choose out of two a most suitable (a right one) parameter when designing FPT (approximation) algorithms for some particular optimization problems on bounded parameter graphs. For example, an

The

The distance between a vertex

2
Path-length, path-breadth, and their variants.

An
interval graph
is the intersection graph of a set of intervals on the real line. A
caterpillar
or
caterpillar tree
is a tree in which all the vertices are within distance 1 of a central path. It is clear that every caterpillar is an interval graph. In fact, caterpillars are exactly the triangle-free interval graphs
[
28
]
.

The main object of this paper is Robertson-Seymour’s path-decomposition and its length. A
path-decomposition

[
45
]
of a graph
G=(V,E)
is a sequence of subsets
\{X_{i}:i\in I\}

(I:=\{1,2,\dots,q\})
of
vertices of
G
, called
bags
, with three properties:

(1)

\bigcup_{i\in I}X_{i}=V
,

(2)

For each edge
vw\in E
, there is a bag
X_{i}

such that
v,w\in X_{i}
, and

(3)

For every three indices
i\leq j\leq k,

X_{i}\cap X_{k}\subseteq X_{j}
. Equivalently, the subsets containing any
particular vertex form a contiguous subsequence of the whole sequence.

We denote a path-decomposition
\{X_{i}:i\in I\}
of a graph
G
by
{\cal P}(G)
. The
width
of a path-decomposition
{\cal P}(G)=\{X_{i}:i\in I\}
is
\max_{i\in I}|X_{i}|-1
. The
path-width

[
45
]
of
a graph
G
, denoted by
{\sf pw}(G)
, is the minimum width over all path-decompositions
{\cal P}(G)
of
G
.
It is known that the caterpillars are exactly the graphs with path-width 1
[
42
]
. The
length

of a path-decomposition
{\cal P}(G)
of a graph
G
is
\lambda:=\max_{i\in I}\max_{u,v\in X_{i}}d_{G}(u,v)
(i.e., each bag
X_{i}

has diameter at most
\lambda
in
G
). The
path-length

[
21
]
of
G
, denoted by
{\sf pl}(G)
, is the minimum length over
all path-decompositions of
G
. Interval graphs
are exactly the graphs with path-length 1; it is known (see, e.g.,
[
30
,
32
,
33
]
) that
G
is an interval
graph if and only if
G
has a path-decomposition with each bag being a maximal clique of
G
(a so-called clique-path).
Note that these two graph parameters (path-width and path-length) are not related to each
other. For instance, a clique on
n
vertices has path-length 1 and path-width
n-1
, whereas a cycle
on
2n
vertices has path-width 2 and path-length

.
The
breadth
of a path-decomposition
{\cal P}(G)

of a graph
G
is the minimum integer

such that for every
i\in I
there is a vertex
v_{i}\in V
with

X_{i}\subseteq D_{G}(v_{i},r)
(i.e., each bag

can be covered by a disk

of radius at most

in

Note that vertex

does not need to belong to

. The
path-breadth

[
21
]
of

, denoted by
{\sf pb}(G)
, is the

.

Evidently, for any graph

with at least one edge,

holds. Hence, if one parameter is bounded by a constant for a

We say that a family of graphs

from

.

It is known

that checking whether a graph

. However, a path decomposition of length at most

[
21
]
.
See also
[
38
]
for other interesting results on path-length and path-breadth.

In
[
27
,
39
]
, a notion of strong breadth was also introduced. The

is the minimum integer

25
]

Notice that in the definition of the length of a path-decomposition, the distance between vertices of a bag is measured in the entire graph

Since

3
New parameters that are coarsely equivalent to path-length

We introduce several more graph parameters and show that they are all coarsely equivalent to path-length.

3.1
Distance
k
-approximating caterpillars and quasi-isometry to paths

Recall that caterpillars are exactly the graphs with path-width 1.
So, we coarsely characterize here the graphs that can be embedded to a graph of path-width 1 with a small (additive) distortion.

A (unweighted) tree
T=(V,E^{\prime})
is a
distance
k
-approximating tree
of a graph
G=(V,E)

[
6
,
14
]

if
|d_{G}(u,v)-d_{T}(u,v)|\leq k
holds for every
u,v\in V
. Denote by
{\sf adc}(G)
the minimum

such that

has a distance
k
-approximating caterpillar tree. We call
{\sf adc}(G)
the
additive distortation
of embedding of
G
to caterpillar tree

(

is with the same vertex set as
G
).

Lemma 1

Let
k\geq 0
be an integer,
G=(V,E)
be a graph, and
T=(V,E^{\prime})
be a caterpillar tree (on the same vertex set) such that for every pair
x,y\in V
,
d_{T}(x,y)-k\leq d_{G}(x,y)\leq d_{T}(x,y)+k
holds. Then,
{\sf pl}(G)\leq 2k+1
.

Proof

Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph and
T=(V,E^{\prime})
be a caterpillar tree such that
|d_{G}(x,y)-d_{T}(x,y)|\leq k
for every
x,y\in V
. For every edge
uv
of
G
,
d_{T}(u,v)\leq d_{G}(u,v)+k\leq 1+k
holds. Hence,
G
is a spanning subgraph of graph

, where

is the
(k+1)^{st}
-power of

. It is known (see, e.g.,
[
8
,
44
]
) that every power of a caterpillar tree (in fact, of any interval graph) is an interval graph. Consequently, there is a clique-path
{\cal P}
of

. Clearly,
{\cal P}
is a path-decomposition of

such that, for every two vertices

and

belonging to same bag of

,

is an edge of

. Necessarily,

, by the definition of the

-power of

. Furthermore, since
d_{G}(x,y)\leq d_{T}(x,y)+k\leq 2k+1
for every

belonging to same bag of
{\cal P}
, the length of the path-decomposition

of

is at most

.
∎

Let
G=(V,E)
be an arbitrary graph and let

be an arbitrary vertex of

in

.
That is, each layer

and radius at most

Let also

be a (shortest) path of

. We have

. Given a layering

Let

be a graph and

In particular,
{\sf adc}(G)\leq length(L(s,G))
for every graph

Proof

Let

with

as the diameter of a set is at most twice its radius.

Lemma 3

For every graph
G

there is a vertex

Proof

Let

be a graph and

be a caterpillar tree such that

for every

. Let

be the central path of

with end vertices

and

. Consider any two vertices

and

, i.e.,

in

be the vertices of

and

. Let also

We have

There is a more general notion of “quasi-isometry” between graphs. This is a concept from metric spaces, but we define it here just for graphs and paths. Let
G
be a graph,
P
be a path (possibly with weights on its edges) and
\psi:V(G)\rightarrow V(P)
be a map. Let
L\geq 1
and
C\geq 0
be constants. We say that
\psi
is an
(L,C)
-quasi-isometry if:

(i)

for all
u,v\in V(G)
,
\frac{1}{L}d_{G}(u,v)-C\leq d_{P}(\psi(u),\psi(v))\leq Ld_{G}(u,v)+C
; and

(ii)

for every
y\in V(P)
there is
v\in V(G)
such that
d_{P}(\psi(v),y)\leq C
.

Here, if
P
has weights on its edges,
d_{P}(\psi(u),\psi(v))
is the sum of weights of all edges of the subpath of
P
connecting
\psi(u)
with
\psi(v)
.

Although
(L,C)
-quasi-isometry from
G
to
P
looks very general, we will show that for all
L,C
there is a
C^{\prime}
such that if there is an
(L,C)
-quasi-isometry from
G
to a path, then there is a
(1,C^{\prime})
-quasi-isometry from
G
to a path (see also a similar result of Kerr
[

]
stated for quasi-isometry between graphs and trees). First, we will show that if there is an
(L,C)
-quasi-isometry from
G
to a path with some constants

and

, then the path-length of

can be bounded by a constant. By Theorem
3.1
,

admits a distance (

)-approximating caterpillar tree. By showing that every caterpillar can be embedded to a path with an additive distortion, we get our result.

Lemma 4

If there is an
(L,C)
-quasi-isometry from
G
to a path

, then
{\sf pl}(G)\leq L(L+2C)

Proof

Let
\ell:=L+C
and
P=(x_{0},x_{1},\dots,x_{q})
.
Since
P
can be weighted, we can assume that for every
x_{i}
there is a vertex
v
in
G
such that
\psi(v)=x_{i}
.

Consider the following path-decomposition of

created from
\psi:V(G)\rightarrow V(P)
. Let
v
be an arbitrary vertex of
G
and let
\psi(v)=x_{i}
for some

. Create a bag

consisting of all vertices

of

such that
\psi(u)=x_{j}
,

and

. It is easy to show that the sequence

is a path-decomposition of

. Evidently, each vertex

of

belongs to a bag. For every edge
uv
of
G
, we have
d_{P}(\psi(v),\psi(u))\leq Ld_{G}(u,v)+C=L+C=\ell
. Therefore, assuming

and

and consider a vertex

We have

To show that the length of path-decomposition

,
{\sf dsp}(G)
are coarsely equivalent to
{\sf pl}(G)
(and hence to
{\sf pb}(G))
. First, we prove the following lemma.

Lemma 7

For every graph
G
,
{\sf pl}(G)\leq 2\cdot{\sf pat}(G)
.

Proof

Let
k:={\sf pat}(G)
. Since
G^{k}
is an AT-free graph, by Proposition
3
, there is a path-decomposition
{\cal P}
of
G^{k}
of length at most 2. Clearly, it is a path-decomposition of
G
as well. Furthermore, for any two vertices
u,v
belonging to same bag of
{\cal P}
, we have
d_{G^{k}}(u,v)\leq 2
and hence, by the definition of the
k^{th}
-power,
d_{G}(u,v)\leq 2k
. Consequently,
{\sf pl}(G)\leq 2\cdot{\sf pat}(G)
. ∎

Theorem
3.1
and Proposition
2
already imply
{\sf pat}(G)\leq 2\cdot{\sf pl}(G)-1\leq 4\cdot{\sf adc}(G)+1
. Using cocomparability graphs, we can improve the upper bound in
{\sf pat}(G)\leq 4\cdot{\sf adc}(G)+1
to
{\sf pat}(G)\leq 3\cdot{\sf adc}(G)+2
(clearly, this is an improvement for all
G
with
{\sf adc}(G)>0
, i.e., when
G
itself is not a caterpillar).

Lemma 8

For every graph
G=(V,E)
,
{\sf pat}(G)\leq{\sf pcc}(G)\leq 3\cdot{\sf adc}(G)+2
.

Proof

We need to prove only the second inequality. Let
{\sf adc}(G)=\delta
, i.e., there is a caterpillar tree
T=(V,E^{\prime})
such that
|d_{G}(u,v)-d_{T}(u,v)|\leq\delta
holds for every
u,v\in V
. Let
P=\{x_{0},x_{1},\dots,x_{t}\}
be the central path of
T
. We can get a layout
\sigma=\{v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{n}\}
of the vertex set of
G
from
P
as follows. For every
i=0,\dots,t-1
, we squeeze all vertices adjacent to
x_{i}
in
T
, that are not in
P
, between
x_{i}
and
x_{i+1}
, in arbitrary order. All vertices adjacent to
x_{t}
in
T
, that are different from
x_{t-1}
, we place after
x_{t}
, again in arbitrary order. We show that
\sigma
obtained this way is a
ccp
-layout of
G^{\mu}:=(V,E^{\mu})
for
\mu=3\delta+2
.

Assume there exists a triple
x,y,z\in V
with
x<y<z
such that
xy\notin E^{\mu}
,
yz\notin E^{\mu}
,
xz\in E^{\mu}
.
We have
d_{G}(x,z)\leq\mu
and
d_{G}(y,z)>\mu
,
d_{G}(x,y)>\mu
. Let
x\in N_{T}[x_{i}]
,
y\in N_{T}[x_{j}]
,
z\in N_{T}[x_{k}]
for
x_{i},x_{j},x_{k}\in P
. Since

|d_{G}(u,v)-d_{T}(u,v)|\leq\delta
holds for every
u,v\in V
, we get
d_{T}(x,z)\leq d_{G}(x,z)+\delta\leq\mu+\delta=4\delta+2
,
d_{G}(x,x_{i})\leq d_{T}(x,x_{i})+\delta\leq 1+\delta
,
d_{G}(y,x_{j})\leq 1+\delta
,
d_{G}(z,x_{k})\leq 1+\delta
. As
d_{G}(y,z)>\mu
,
d_{G}(x,y)>\mu
and
x<y<z
, necessarily,
i<j<k
holds (if, for example,
i=j
, then
d_{G}(x,y)\leq d_{G}(x,x_{i})+d_{G}(y,x_{j})\leq 2\delta+2\leq\mu
, and a contradiction arises).

From
4\delta+2\geq d_{T}(x,z)=d_{T}(x,x_{j})+d_{T}(x_{j},z)
we get
d_{T}(x,x_{j})\leq 2\delta+1
or
d_{T}(x_{j},z)\leq 2\delta+1
. Assume, without loss of generality, that
d_{T}(x,x_{j})\leq 2\delta+1
holds. Then,
d_{T}(x,y)\leq d_{T}(x,x_{j})+1\leq 2\delta+2
, implying

d_{G}(x,y)\leq d_{T}(x,y)+\delta\leq 3\delta+2=\mu
. The latter contradicts with
d_{G}(x,y)>\mu
. ∎

Our next lemma shows that every graph having a
k
-dominating shortest path can be embedded to a caterpillar tree with an additive distortion at most
2k+2
.

Lemma 9

For every graph
G=(V,E)
,
{\sf adc}(G)\leq 2\cdot{\sf dsp}(G)+2
.

Proof

Let
P
be a
k
-dominating shortest path of
G
. For every vertex
v
of
G
, denote by
x_{v}
a vertex of
P
closest to
v
, i.e., with
d_{G}(v,x_{v})=d_{G}(v,P)\leq k
. We can build a caterpillar tree
T
from
G
by using
P
as the central path of
T
and making every vertex
v
not in
P
adjacent in
T
to
x_{v}\in P
. We show that, for every pair
x,y\in V
,
d_{T}(x,y)-2-2k\leq d_{G}(x,y)\leq d_{T}(x,y)+2k
.
Indeed, since
P
is a shortest path of
G
, by the triangle inequality, we get

d_{T}(x,y)\leq d_{P}(x_{v},y_{v})+2=d_{G}(x_{v},y_{v})+2\leq d_{G}(x,x_{v})+d_{G}(x,y)+d_{G}(y,y_{v})+2\leq d_{G}(x,y)+2k+2.

We also get

d_{G}(x,y)\leq d_{G}(x,x_{v})+d_{G}(x_{v},y_{v})+d_{G}(y_{v},y)\leq d_{P}(x_{v},y_{v})+2k=d_{T}(x_{v},y_{v})+2k\leq d_{T}(x,y)+2k.

Thus,
{\sf adc}(G)\leq 2\cdot{\sf dsp}(G)+2
.
∎

Now, we can prove the following theorem.

Theorem 3.3

For every graph
G
,

\rho(G)\leq 2\cdot{\sf dsp}(G)+1
,

{\sf dsp}(G)\leq{\sf dpr}(G)\leq 3\cdot{\sf dsp}(G)
, and

{\sf dsp}(G)\leq{\sf dpr}(G)\leq{\sf pl}(G)\leq 4\cdot{\sf dsp}(G)\leq 4\cdot{\sf dpr}(G),

{\sf dsp}(G)\leq{\sf dpr}(G)\leq\Delta(G)\leq 4\cdot{\sf dsp}(G)+1\leq 4\cdot{\sf dpr}(G)+1,

\frac{{\sf adc}(G)-2}{2}\leq{\sf dsp}(G)\leq{\sf dpr}(G)\leq 2\cdot{\sf adc}(G)+1,

\frac{{\sf adc}(G)}{2}\leq{\sf pat}(G)\leq{\sf pcc}(G)\leq 3\cdot{\sf adc}(G)+2,

\frac{{\sf pl}(G)}{2}\leq{\sf pat}(G)\leq 2\cdot{\sf pl}(G)-1,

\frac{\Delta(G)-1}{2}\leq{\sf pat}(G)\leq 2\cdot\Delta(G)-1,

{\sf pat}(G)\leq{\sf pcc}(G)\leq 2\cdot{\sf pat}(G),

\frac{{\sf pl}(G)}{2}\leq{\sf pat}(G)\leq{\sf pcc}(G)\leq 2\cdot{\sf pl}(G).

Clearly,
{\sf dsp}(G)\leq{\sf dpr}(G)
and
{\sf pl}(G)\leq\Delta(G)

for every graph
G
. The inequality
{\sf pat}(G)\leq 2\cdot{\sf pl}(G)-1
follows from Proposition
2
. The inequalities

{\sf adc}(G)\leq 2\cdot{\sf pat}(G)
,
\Delta(G)\leq 2\cdot{\sf pat}(G)+1
,

{\sf pcc}(G)\leq 2\cdot{\sf pl}(G)
,
{\sf pl}(G)\leq 2\cdot{\sf pat}(G)
,
{\sf pat}(G)\leq{\sf pcc}(G)\leq 3\cdot{\sf adc}(G)+2
and
{\sf adc}(G)\leq 2\cdot{\sf dsp}(G)+2
are given by Lemma
5
, Lemma
6
, Lemma
7
, Lemma
8
and Lemma
9
, respectively.
The inequality
{\sf pl}(G)\leq 4\cdot{\sf dsp}(G)

[
27
]
. Recall also that
{\sf pcc}(G)\leq 2\cdot{\sf pat}(G)
holds.

In any path-decomposition
{\cal P}(G)=\{X_{1},X_{2},\dots,X_{q}\}
of

of length
\lambda
, for any two vertices
x\in X_{1}
and

, any path

between

and

, by properties of

), has a vertex in every

{\cal P}(G)
. Consequently, as each vertex

of

belongs to some bag
X_{i}
of
{\cal P}(G)
, there is a vertex
u\in P

with

. The latter implies
d_{G}(v,u)\leq\lambda
. Hence, for every graph
G
,
{\sf dpr}(G)\leq{\sf pl}(G)\leq\Delta(G)
. By Lemma
1
, we also get
{\sf dpr}(G)\leq{\sf pl}(G)\leq 2\cdot{\sf adc}(G)+1
.

Let
G
be a graph such that
G^{k}
is an AT-free graph. Since every AT-free graph has a 1-dominating pair (see
[
11
]
),
G^{k}
must have a 1-dominating pair

. Every path

connecting vertices
x
and

in

is a path of
G^{k}
, too. Hence, for every vertex
v
of
G
, we have
d_{G^{k}}(v,P)\leq 1
, implying
d_{G}(v,P)\leq k
. Thus,
x,y
is a
k
-dominating pair of
G
, i.e.,
{\sf dpr}(G)\leq k
. This shows
{\sf dpr}(G)\leq{\sf pat}(G)
.

Let now, in what follows,
P
be a
k
-dominating shortest path of
G
connecting vertices
s
and
t
such that
k={\sf dsp}(G)
. Consider layering
L(s,G):=\{L_{0},L_{1},\dots L_{q}\}
of

, where
L_{i}=\{u\in V:d_{G}(s,u)=i\}
,
q=\max\{d_{G}(u,s):u\in V\}
. It is easy to show that
breadth(L(s,G))
is at most
2k
, i.e., for every
i\in\{1,\dots,q\}
there is a vertex
v_{i}
in
G
with
d_{G}(u,v_{i})\leq 2k
for every

.
Indeed, let
v\in L_{i}
be an arbitrary vertex of
G
and
p(v)
be a vertex of

such that
d_{G}(v,p(v))\leq k
. Let also
v_{i}
be a vertex of

. It exists if

. If
i>d_{G}(s,t)
, set
v_{i}=t

is a shortest path of

,
|d_{G}(s,p(v)-d_{G}(s,v_{i})|\leq k
. Therefore,
d_{G}(v,v_{i})\leq d_{G}(v,p(v))+d_{G}(v_{i},p(v))\leq 2k

Since
breadth(L(s,G))\leq 2k

\rho(G)\leq\rho_{s}(G)\leq breadth(L(s,G))+1

, we have
\rho(G)\leq 2k+1=2\cdot{\sf dsp}(G)+1
and
\Delta(G)\leq 4\cdot{\sf dsp}(G)+1
. Applying Lemma

and taking into account

{\sf adc}(G)\leq 4k=4\cdot{\sf dsp}(G)
(but this bound is not better than the bound established in Lemma

, when
{\sf dsp}(G)>0
).

Corollary 3

(a)
If a graph
G
has a
k
-dominating shortest path, then

is a cocomparability (and, hence, an AT-free) graph for some

.

G^{\mu}
is a cocomparability
(

It is easy to see that a pair of vertices

is a

-dominating pair if and only if, for every

separates

. Hence, a
k

(

]

It is not very likely that there is a linear time algorithm to find a 1-dominating pair (called just a

), if it exists,
since it is shown in

]
that finding a dominating pair is essentially as hard as finding a triangle in
a graph. Yet, since path-decompositions with small length are closely related to

-domination, one
can search for

-dominating pairs in dependence of the path-length of a graph. We do not know
how to find in linear time for an arbitrary graph

-dominating pair with

. However, as it was shown in
[

, there is a linear time algorithm that determines a

. Graphs that have a 1-dominating pair were extensively studied in
[

,

.

[
21
]
a very simple linear time algorithm that for an arbitrary graph

. The problem of finding in a graph

. All these efficient algorithms for finding a

-dominating shortest path, with small

. Relation of the MESP problem with other problems like the minimum distortion embedding on a line
[

have also been established.

3.3
McCarty index

The following result from
[
21
]
generalizes a characteristic property of the famous class of AT-free graphs.

Proposition 5
(
[
21
]
)

Let
G
be a graph with
{\sf pl}(G)\leq\lambda
. Then, for every three vertices
u,v,w
of
G
, one of those vertices, say v, satisfies the following property: the disk of radius
\lambda
centered at
v
intercepts every path connecting
u

and
w
, i.e., after the removal of the disk
D_{G}(v,\lambda)
from
G
,
u
and
w
are not in the same connected
component of
G\setminus D_{G}(v,\lambda)
.

We can introduce a new parameter
McCarty index
of a graph
G
, denoted by
{\sf mci}(G)
. This is the minimum
r
such that for every triple of vertices
u,v,w
of
G
, disk of radius
r
centered at one of them intercepts all paths connecting two others.
In
[
4
]
, the
McCarty-width
of a graph was introduced and it was shown that the McCarty-width of a graph
G
is small if and only if the tree-length of
G
is small (i.e., these two parameters are coarsely equivalent). Recall
[
4
]
that a graph
G
has
McCarty-width

r
if
r\geq 0
is minimum such that the following holds: for every three vertices
u,v,w
of
G
, there is a vertex
x
such that no connected component of
G\setminus D_{G}(x,r)
contains two of
u,v,w
. Our McCarty index is a linearization of McCarty-width, it forces one of
u,v,w
to play the role of
x
. It is not hard to see that the McCarty index
{\sf mci}(G)
of a graph
G
can be computed in at most
O(n^{3})
time.

We will show that the McCarty index
{\sf mci}(G)
of a graph
G
is coarsely equivalent to the path-length
{\sf pl}(G)
of
G
. By Proposition
5
, we have
{\sf mci}(G)\leq{\sf pl}(G).
Our next result shows that
{\sf pat}(G)\leq 2\cdot{\sf mci}(G)-1.

Lemma 10

For every graph
G
,
{\sf pat}(G)\leq 2\cdot{\sf mci}(G)-1.

Proof

Let
r:={\sf mci}(G)
. We show that
G^{2r-1}
is an AT-free graph.

Consider three arbitrary vertices
u,v,w
that form an independent set in
G^{2r-1}
, i.e.,
d_{G}(x,y)>2r-1
for each
x,y\in\{u,v,w\}
. Since
r:={\sf mci}(G)
, without loss of generality, let
D_{G}(w,r)
intercept every path connecting
u
and
v
in
G
.
Consider an arbitrary path
P
of
G^{2r-1}
connecting
u
and
v
.
For every edge
xy
of
P
, consider in
G
a path
P_{G}(x,y)
between
x
and
y
whose length in
G
is at most
2r-1
. We can get a path
P^{+}\supseteq P
of
G
between
u
and

∎

Concluding remarks and open questions

We saw that several graph parameters are coarsely equivalent to path-length. If one of the parameters from the list
\{{\sf pl}(G),

{\sf pb}(G),

{\sf spb}(G),

{\sf ipl}(G),

{\sf ipb}(G),

\Delta(G),

\rho(G),

{\sf adc}(G),

{\sf dsp}(G),

{\sf dpr}(G),

{\sf pat}(G),

{\sf pcc}(G),

{\sf mci}(G),

{\sf mfi}(G)\}
is bounded for a graph
G
, then all other parameters are bounded. We saw that, in fact, all those parameters are within small constant factors from each other. Two questions are immediate.

1.

Can constants in those inequalities be further improved? In particular, is
{\sf pcc}(G)\leq{\sf pat}(G)+1
true, is
{\sf pcc}(G)\leq 2\cdot{\sf mci}(G)
true?

2.

Are there any other interesting graph parameters that are coarsely equivalent to path-length?

We know that for a given graph
G
, the parameters
{\sf pl}(G),

{\sf pb}(G),

{\sf spb}(G),

{\sf ipl}(G),

{\sf ipb}(G),

{\sf dsp}(G)
are NP-hard to compute exactly, while the parameters
\Delta(G),

\rho(G),

{\sf dpr}(G),

{\sf pat}(G),

{\sf pcc}(G),

{\sf mci}(G)
are computable in polynomial time and provide constant factor approximations for those hard to compute parameters.

3.

What is the complexity of computing
{\sf adc}(G),

{\sf mfi}(G)\}
? Can non-trivial inapproximability results be obtained for those hard to compute parameters?

Following
[
20
]
, where a notion of
k
-tree-breadth was introduced, we can introduce a notion of
k
-path-breadth. The
k
-breadth
of a path-decomposition

of a graph

is the minimum integer
r
such
that for each bag
B
of
{\cal P}(G)
, there is a set of at most
k

such that for each

d_{G}(u,C_{B})\leq r
holds (i.e., each bag
B
can be covered with at most
k
disks of
G
of radius at most
r
each;
B\subseteq\cup_{i=1}^{k}D_{G}(v_{B}^{i},r)
. The
k
-path-breadth
of a graph
G
, denoted by
{\sf pb}_{k}(G)
, is the minimum of the
k
-breadth,
over all path-decompositions of
G
. Clearly, for every graph
G
,
{\sf pb}(G)={\sf pb}_{1}(G)
and
{\sf pw}(G)\leq k-1
if and only if
{\sf pb}_{k}(G)=0
(each vertex in the bags of the path-decomposition can be considered as a center of a disk of radius 0).

4.

It would be interesting to investigate if there exist generalizations of some graph parameters considered in this paper that coarsely describe the
k
-path-breadth.

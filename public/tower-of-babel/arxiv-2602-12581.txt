The condition number of a random banded Toeplitz matrix is typically large
Paulo Manrique

Abstract
. It is well known that square matrices with independent and identically distributed (iid) random entries are typically well conditioned. A natural question is whether this favorable behavior persists for random matrices whose entries obey additional structure, i.e., their position inside of the matrix. A prominent class of structured matrices is given by Toeplitz matrices , characterized by constant diagonals. A particular tractable subclass is that of circulant matrices, whose additional characteristic (its entries circulate row by row) allows one to express their conditioning in terms of the localization of the zeros of a associated polynomial. When the entries of a circulant matrix are iid, the matrix is well conditioned precisely when the corresponding random polynomial has no zeros on the unit circle. This connection is especially relevant because, as the degree of a random polynomial increases, its zeros tend to concentrate near the unit circle, making it a delicate problem to quantify how close the closest zeros lie to the unit circle. Another notable family within the Toeplitz class is that of banded Toeplitz matrices , namely matrices for which only finitely many diagonals around the main diagonal may be nonzero. These matrices have been extensively studied in Operator Theory, nevertheless, despite their apparent simplicity, they raise subtle questions regarding the behavior of their condition numbers. In present work we show that the bandwidth asymmetry plays a decisive role: if the band contains r r diagonals below and s s diagonal above the main diagonal, then if r = s r=s the banded Toeplitz matriz is well conditioned with high probability, whereas if r ≠ s r\neq s it is typically ill conditioned. This highlights that structural constraints can have a impact on the numerical behavior of random matrices.

Introduction

Here we are interested in understanding the

we have

\left|\left|A\right|\right|=\sigma_{1}.

Note that
A
is singular if and only if
\sigma_{n}=0

is non-singular, then

\left|\left|A^{-1}\right|\right|=\left(\min_{\left|\left|x\right|\right|_{2}=1}\left|\left|Ax\right|\right|_{2}\right)^{-1}=\sigma_{n}^{-1}.

This identity shows that
\left|\left|A^{-1}\right|\right|
increasing as
\sigma_{n}
approaches zero, indicating

is
nearly singular

\sigma_{n}
measure the distance of

to the set of singular matrices

and quantifies the sensitivity of the the linear system

The present work is concerned with understanding the condition number of banded Toeplitz matrices. To this end, we first recall the definition of a Toeplitz matrix, which is characterized by constant diagonals. More precisely, a matrix

T_{n}:=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccccc}x_{0}&x_{1}&\ldots&x_{n-2}&x_{n-1}\\
x_{-1}&x_{0}&\ldots&x_{n-3}&x_{n-2}\\
x_{-2}&x_{-1}&\ldots&x_{n-4}&x_{n-3}\\
&&\ldots&&\\
x_{-n+1}&x_{-n+2}&\ldots&x_{-1}&x_{0}\end{array}\right].

A banded Toeplitz matrix is a Toeplitz matrix for which

whenever

In general, understanding the condition number of a Toeplitz matrix is a challenging problem

Banded Toeplitz matrices

A standard polynomial can be associated with a Laurent polynomial. Let

When a random banded Toeplitz matrix is considered, the associated Laurent polynomial is also random, and consequently the polynomial

where the implicit constant depends on

When

is sufficiently large, with high probability the polynomial

has no zeros on the unit circle. The study of the zeros of random polynomials is a well–established area of research, see

,

,

holds if and only if the zeros of

are asymptotically (

) concentrated around the unit circle. Moreover, under suitable assumptions, it is shown in

,

that the zero closest to the unit circle lies at a distance approximately

.

As we see in the previous comments, in order to understand the behavior of
\kappa(T_{n})
when
T_{n}
is a banded Toeplitz matrix, one must determine how many zeros of the associated symbol lie inside the unit disk. This idea is made precise by the following observation, which relies on the notion of the winding number. Let

\mathbf{T}:=\left\{t\in\mathds{C}:\left|t\right|=1\right\}.

If
a:\mathbf{T}\to\mathds{C}\setminus{0}
is continuous, then as
t
moves on
\mathbf{T}
counterclockwise, the image
a(t)
traces a continuous closed curve in
\mathds{C}\setminus{0}
. The number of times this curve winds around the origin in the counterclockwise direction is called the winding number, denoted by
\textnormal{wind}(a)
.

Now consider a Laurent polynomial
P(z)=\sum_{j=-r}^{s}\xi_{j}z^{j}
such that
P(z)\neq 0
for all
z\in\mathbf{T}
. Then

\textnormal{wind}(P)=\nu-r,

where
\nu
denotes the number of zeros of
P
in the open disk
\mathds{D}:=\left\{z\in\mathds{C}:\left|z\right|<1\right\}
(see expression 1.2 in
[
8
]
). Observe that

P(z)=\sum_{j=-r}^{s}\xi_{j}z^{j}=z^{-r}\sum_{j=0}^{r+s}\xi_{j}z^{j},

so
P
has a zero on
\mathbf{T}
if and only if
G_{r+s}(z):=\sum_{j=0}^{r+s}\xi_{j}z^{j}
has a zero on
\mathbf{T}
. By the discussion above, when
r+s
is large,
G_{r+s}
does not vanish on
\mathbf{T}
with high probability.

In Chapter 3 of
[
8
]
, a sequence of matrices
{A_{n}}
, where each
A_{n}
is of size
n\times n
, is said to be
stable
if there exist
n_{0}\leq 1
and
M\in(0,\infty)
such that
A_{n}
is invertible for all
n\geq n_{0}
and

\left|\left|A_{n}^{-1}\right|\right|\leq M\quad\mbox{ for all }\quad n\geq n_{0}.

Since a sequence
{T_{n}}
of banded Toeplitz matrices with the same band can be associated with the same Laurent polynomial
P(z)
, it shown in Chapter 3 of
[
8
]
that
{T_{n}}
is stable if and only if
P
has no zeros on
\mathbf{T}
and
\textnormal{wind}(T)=0
. This criterion is consistent with the classification in
[
2
]
when the banded Toeplitz matrix is of type
(r,0,s)
. Accordingly, given a Laurent polynomial
P_{r,s}
, we are led to ask for the number of zeros of the associated polynomial
G_{r+s}
that lie inside the unit disk. This question has been studied previously in
[
28
]
in the case of
Littlewood polynomials
, namely polynomials with coefficients in
{-1,+1}
. Littlewood polynomials can be viewed as random polynomials of the form

G_{m}(z)=\sum_{j=0}^{m}\xi_{j}z^{j},

where the coefficients
\xi_{j}
are independent and identically distributed (iid) Rademacher random variables, i.e.,

\mathds{P}\left(\xi_{0}=1\right)=\mathds{P}\left(\xi_{0}=-1\right)=\frac{1}{2}.

We denote by

\nu_{n}:=\sum_{z:G_{n}(z)}\delta_{z}

the random counting measure of the zeros of
G_{n}
, where
\delta_{z}
denotes the Dirac mass at
z
. In
[
28
]
, it is proved that

(1.1)

\lim_{m\to\infty}\mathds{P}\left(\left|\nu_{m}(\mathds{D})-\frac{m}{2}\right|\geq m^{9/10}\right)=0.

It follows that approximately one half of the zeros of a Littlewood polynomial line in
\mathds{D}
. Consequently, if we consider a banded Toeplitz matrix whose entries are iid Rademacher random variables, then the number of zeros in
\mathds{D}
of the associated Laurent polynomial
P(z)=z^{-r}G_{r+s}(z)
is, with high probability, approximately

\frac{r+s}{2}

provided that
r+s
is sufficiently large. If moreover
\frac{r+s}{2}=r
, which occurs when
r=s
, then the associated banded Toeplitz matrix has symmetric bandwidth about the main diagonal. In addition, with high probability
P
has no zeros on
\mathbf{T}
, which suggests that the banded Toeplitz matriz is well–conditioned. In other words, with high probability the sequence of banded Toeplitz matrices
{T_{n}}
associated with the same Laurent polynomial satisfies

\kappa(T_{n})\leq M\quad\mbox{ for all sufficiently large $n$},

for some constant
M\in(0,\infty)
. It is important to emphasize that this conclusion would hold only in the symmetric band case,
r=s
. Indeed, if for instance
m=r+s
is large and
r=\lambda m
and
s=(1-\lambda)m
for
\lambda\in(0,1)
with
\lambda\neq\frac{1}{2}
, then

\frac{m}{2}=\frac{r+s}{2}\neq r=\alpha m.

In this regime, with high probability the winding number satisfies

\textnormal{wind}(P)\approx\frac{m}{2}-\alpha m\neq 0.

When the winding number of a Laurent polynomial with no zeros on
\mathbf{T}
is nonzero, one observe the following phenomenon. Define

\delta:=\max\left\{\left|\delta_{j}\right|<1:G_{r+s}(\delta_{j})=0\right\},\quad\mu:=\min\left\{\left|\mu_{k}\right|>1:G_{r+s}(\mu_{k})=0\right\}.

Then (see Theorem 4.1 in
[
8
]
), for every

\alpha<\min\left\{\log\frac{1}{\delta},\log\mu\right\}

there exists a constant
C_{\alpha}>0
, depending only on
\alpha
and on

, such that

\left|\left|T_{n}^{-1}\right|\right|_{2}\geq C_{\alpha}e^{\alpha n}\quad\mbox{ for all $n\geq 1$},

where
T_{n}
denotes the banded Toeplitz matrix associated with
P(z)
. This implies that banded Toeplitz matrices with iid Rademacher entries, when
r+s
is large and the bandwidth parameters
r,s
are not
balanced
(i.e.,

), tend to be poorly conditioned with high probability.

The present work aims to understand the order of the probability of the event appearing in
1.1
for a broad class of random coefficients, and to infer from this the behavior of the condition number of a random banded Toeplitz matrix. Let us emphasize that the strategy we follow to estimate the quantity in
1.1
is, in essence, based on the same steps as in
[
28
]
but there are several important differences. Our main tool for controlling

is Lemma

, which in
[

]
is proved only for the Rademacher case and relies on a version of Lemma

tailored to that setting. The Lemma

is a so–called small–ball probability statement, its proof uses the classic Essen Lemma, Turán’s Lemma as in
[

, here we only adding some probabilistic ideas to extend for more variety of random variables. In order to handle more general distribution, the proof of Lemma
3.2
here requiere an estimate for the the expected Mahler measure
[

]
that is no treated in
[

]
and is of independent interest.

4.
Bounding expectation of Mahler measure

Recall that for a monic polynomial
F(z)=\prod_{j=1}^{n}(z-\alpha_{j})
the
Mahler measure
of
F
is given by

M(F):=\prod_{j=1}^{n}\max\left\{1,\left|\alpha_{j}\right|\right\}.

Using Jensen’s formula, we see that

\log M(F)=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\log\left|F(e^{i\theta})\right|\textnormal{d}\mu(\theta).

Using Jensen’s inequality, the following result establishes an upper bound on the fourth moment of logarithm of the Mahler measure of
G
. For more details on the Mahler measure see
[
25
]
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
G(re^{i\theta})=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\xi_{j}r^{j}e^{ij\theta}
for
\theta\in[-\pi,\pi]
where
r\in[1-n^{-11/10},1+n^{-11/10}]
is fixed and
\xi_{j}
,
j=0,\ldots,n
are iid random variables with
\mathbb{E}{}\left[\xi_{0}\right]=0
,
\mathbb{E}{}\left[\xi_{0}^{2}\right]=1
,
\mathbb{E}{}\left[\xi_{0}^{4}\right]<+\infty
and
\mathds{P}\left(\left|\xi_{0}\right|\leq t\right)\leq Ct^{1/2}
for some constant
C>0
and
t\in[0,1]
. Then, there is a positive constant
C^{*}
which depends only on the distribution of
\xi_{0}
such that

\mathbb{E}{}\left[\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\left|\log\left|G(re^{i\theta})\right|\right|^{4}\textnormal{d}\mu(\theta)\right]\leq C^{*}\left(n\log n\right)^{4}.

Proof.

If
\left|\left|G\right|\right|_{\infty}:=\sup_{\theta\in[-\pi,\pi]}\left|G(re^{i\theta})\right|
and
\left|\left|G\right|\right|_{2}:=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\left|G(re^{i\theta})\right|^{2}d\mu(\theta)
. We observe
\left|\left|G\right|\right|_{2}^{2}=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\left|\xi_{j}\right|^{2}r^{2j}
,
\left|\left|G\right|\right|_{2}\leq\left|\left|G\right|\right|_{\infty}
and by Cauchy-Schwarz

\left|G(re^{i\theta})\right|\leq\sum_{j=0}^{n}\left|\xi_{j}\right|r^{j}\leq\sqrt{n+1}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{n}\left|\xi_{j}\right|^{2}r^{2j}\right)^{1/2}=\sqrt{n+1}\left|\left|G\right|\right|_{2},

i.e.,
\left|\left|G\right|\right|_{\infty}\leq\sqrt{n+1}\left|\left|G\right|\right|_{2}
.

Before to proceding with the proof of Lemma
4.1
, we state the classical Turàn–Nazarov Lemma (see Theorem 1.5 in
[
19
]
).

Lemma 4.2

(Turàn–Nazarov Lemma)
.

Let
T(x):=\sum_{k=0}^{h}\alpha_{k}e^{im_{k}x}
be a trigonometric polynomial with
\alpha_{k}\in\mathbb{C}
and
m_{1}<m_{2}<\ldots<m_{h}\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then for any measurable set
E\subset[-\pi,\pi]
we have

\sup_{x\in[-\pi,\pi]}|T(x)|\leq\left(\lx@nobreakspace\frac{C}{\mu(E)}\right)^{h-1}\sup_{x\in E}|T(x)|,

where
C>0
is an absolute constant.

Thus, we define

E_{\lambda}:=\left\{\theta\in[-\pi,\pi]:\left|G(re^{i\theta})\right|\leq\lambda\left|\left|G\right|\right|_{2}\right\},

Observe that
\left|\left|G\right|\right|_{2}\neq 0
with probability one since
\mathds{P}\left(\xi_{0}=0\right)=0
. Hence, from Turàn–Nazarov Lemma we have

\left|\left|G\right|\right|_{2}\leq\left|\left|G\right|\right|_{\infty}\leq\left(\frac{C}{{\mu(E_{\lambda})}}\right)^{n}\sup_{\theta\in E_{\lambda}}\left|G(re^{i\theta})\right|\leq\left(\frac{C}{{\mu(E_{\lambda})}}\right)^{n}\lambda\left|\left|G\right|\right|_{2},

then

(4.1)

\mu(E_{\lambda})\leq C_{1}\lambda^{1/n}

for some universal positive constant
C_{1}
.

We assume that
\xi_{j}
are fixed.

\displaystyle\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\left|\log\left|G(re^{i\theta})\right|\right|^{4}\textnormal{d}\mu(\theta)

\displaystyle=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\left|\log\frac{\left|G(re^{i\theta})\right|}{\left|\left|G\right|\right|_{2}}+\log\left|\left|G\right|\right|_{2}\right|^{4}\textnormal{d}\mu(\theta)

\displaystyle\leq 8\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\left|\log\frac{\left|G(re^{i\theta})\right|}{\left|\left|G\right|\right|_{2}}\right|^{4}\textnormal{d}\mu(\theta)+8\left|\log\left|\left|G\right|\right|_{2}\right|^{4}.

We define the event
B:=\left\{\left|G(re^{i\theta})\right|>\left|\left|G\right|\right|_{2}\right\}
, then

\displaystyle\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\left|\log\frac{\left|G(re^{i\theta})\right|}{\left|\left|G\right|\right|_{2}}\right|^{4}\mathds{1}_{B}\textnormal{d}\mu(\theta)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\left(\log\frac{\sqrt{n+1}\left|\left|G\right|\right|_{2}}{\left|\left|G\right|\right|_{2}}\right)^{4}\mathds{1}_{B}\textnormal{d}\mu(\theta)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2^{4}}\left(\log(n+1)\right)^{4}.

On the other hand, if
\mathds{P}_{\theta}
indicates the distribution respect to
\mu(\theta)
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\left|\log\frac{\left|G(re^{i\theta})\right|}{\left|\left|G\right|\right|_{2}}\right|^{4}\mathds{1}_{B^{c}}\textnormal{d}\mu(\theta)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}4u^{3}\mathds{P}_{\theta}\left(-\log\frac{\left|G(re^{i\theta})\right|}{\left|\left|G\right|\right|_{2}}\geq u,B^{c}\right)\textnormal{d}u

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}4u^{3}\mathds{P}_{\theta}\left(\left|G(re^{i\theta})\right|\leq e^{-u}\left|\left|G\right|\right|_{2}\right)\textnormal{d}u.

Using
4.1

\mathds{P}_{\theta}\left(\left|G(re^{i\theta})\right|\leq e^{-u}\left|\left|G\right|\right|_{2}\right)\leq\min\left\{1,C_{1}e^{-u/n}\right\}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{3}\mathds{P}_{\theta}\left(\left|G(re^{i\theta})\right|\leq e^{-u}\left|\left|G\right|\right|_{2}\right)\textnormal{d}u=\int_{0}^{n\log n}u^{3}\textnormal{d}u+\int_{n\log n}^{\infty}u^{3}\mathds{P}_{\theta}\left(\left|G(re^{i\theta})\right|\leq e^{-u}\left|\left|G\right|\right|_{2}\right)\textnormal{d}u

\displaystyle\quad\leq\frac{1}{4}(n\log n)^{4}+C_{1}\int_{n\log n}^{\infty}u^{3}e^{-u/n}\textnormal{d}u

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{1}{4}(n\log n)^{4}+C_{1}n^{4}\int_{\log n}^{\infty}v^{3}e^{-v}\textnormal{d}v

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{1}{4}(n\log n)^{4}+C_{1}n^{4}e^{-\log n}\left((\log n)^{3}+3(\log n)^{2}+6(\log n)+6\right)

\displaystyle\quad\leq C_{2}\left((n\log n)^{4}\right),

for some universal positive constant
C_{2}
. Joining the above statements, we conclude

\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\left|\log\left|G(re^{i\theta})\right|\right|^{4}\textnormal{d}\mu(\theta)\leq C_{3}\left((n\log n)^{4}+\left|\log\left|\left|G\right|\right|_{2}\right|^{4}\right).

Now, we take the expectation respect to
\xi_{j}

\mathbb{E}{}\left[\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\left|\log\left|G(re^{i\theta})\right|\right|^{4}\textnormal{d}\mu(\theta)\right]\leq C_{3}\left((n\log n)^{4}+\mathbb{E}{}\left[\left|\log\left|\left|G\right|\right|_{2}\right|^{4}\right]\right),

which means we need to analyze the expectation of
\left|\log\left|\left|G\right|\right|_{2}\right|^{4}

\left|\log\left|\left|G\right|\right|_{2}\right|^{4}=\frac{1}{2^{4}}\left|\log\left(\frac{1}{n}\left|\left|G\right|\right|_{2}^{2}\right)+\log n\right|^{4}\leq\frac{1}{2}\left((\log n)^{4}+\left[\log\left(\frac{1}{n}\left|\left|G\right|\right|_{2}^{2}\right)\right]^{4}\right),

and note

\frac{1}{n}\left|\left|G\right|\right|_{2}^{2}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n}\xi_{j}^{2}r^{2j}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle n\to\infty}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathbb{E}{}\left[\xi_{0}^{2}\right]=1\quad\mbox{a.s.},

for all
r\in[1-n^{-11/10},1+n^{-11/10}]
. Moreover,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}{}\left[\left(\frac{1}{n}\left|\left|G\right|\right|_{2}^{2}-1\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n^{2}}\mathbb{E}{}\left[\left(\sum_{j=0}^{n}\left[\xi_{j}^{2}r^{2j}-1\right]\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n^{2}}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{n}\mathbb{E}{}\left[(\xi^{2}_{j}r^{2j}-1)^{2}\right]+2\sum_{j_{1}<j_{2}}\mathbb{E}{}\left[(\xi_{j_{1}}^{2}r^{2j_{1}}-1)(\xi_{j_{2}}^{2}r^{2j_{2}}-1)\right]\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4e^{4}}{n^{2}}\left(n(\mathbb{E}{}\left[\xi_{0}^{4}\right]+1)\right)+\frac{2}{n^{2}}\sum_{j_{1}<j_{2}}(r^{2j_{1}}-1)(r^{2j_{2}}-1)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4e^{4}}{n^{2}}\left(n(\mathbb{E}{}\left[\xi_{0}^{4}\right]+1)\right)+\frac{2}{n^{2}}\sum_{j_{1}<j_{2}}(4e^{4}j_{1}n^{-11/10})(4e^{4}j_{2}n^{-11/10})

\displaystyle=\frac{4e^{4}}{n^{2}}\left(n(\mathbb{E}{}\left[\xi_{0}^{4}\right]+1)\right)+\frac{2^{5}e^{8}}{n^{2}}\sum_{j_{1}<j_{2}}j_{1}j_{2}n^{-22/10}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4e^{4}}{n^{2}}\left(n(\mathbb{E}{}\left[\xi_{0}^{4}\right]+1)\right)+{2^{5}e^{8}}n^{-2/10}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{4}}{n^{1/5}},

for some positive constant
C_{4}
which depends on
\mathbb{E}{}\left[\xi_{0}^{4}\right]
.
We define
Y_{n}:=\frac{1}{n}\left|\left|G\right|\right|_{2}^{2}
and
D:=\left\{Y_{n}\in[0,1/2]\right\}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}{}\left[\left|\log\left(Y_{n}\right)\right|^{4}\right]=\mathbb{E}{}\left[\left|\log\left(Y_{n}\right)\right|^{4}\mathds{1}_{D}\right]+\mathbb{E}{}\left[\left|\log\left(Y_{n}\right)\right|^{4}\mathds{1}_{D^{c}}\right]

Thus, on

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}{}\left[\left|\log\left(Y_{n}\right)\right|^{4}\mathds{1}_{D}\right]=\int_{0}^{\infty}4u^{3}\mathds{P}\left(-\log Y_{n}\geq u,D\right)\textnormal{d}u

\displaystyle\quad\leq\int_{0}^{\log n}4u^{3}\mathds{P}\left(Y_{n}\leq\frac{1}{2}\right)\textnormal{d}u+\int_{\log n}^{\infty}4u^{3}\mathds{P}\left(Y_{n}\leq e^{-u}\right)\textnormal{d}u

\displaystyle\quad\leq C_{4}\frac{(\log n)^{4}}{n}+C_{5}\frac{(\log n)^{3}}{n^{1/4}}\leq C_{6}\frac{(\log n)^{4}}{n^{1/4}},

for some positive constants
C_{5},C_{6}>0
which depends on the distribution of
\xi_{0}
. For the left part, observing the inequality

for all

\mathbb{E}{}\left[\left|\log\left(Y_{n}\right)\right|^{4}\mathds{1}_{D^{c}}\right]=\mathbb{E}{}\left[\left|\log\left(1+(Y_{n}-1)\right)\right|^{4}\mathds{1}_{D^{c}}\right]\leq 8\mathbb{E}{}\left[\left|Y_{n}-1\right|^{2}\right]\leq\frac{8C_{4}}{n^{1/5}}.

In this section we prove Lemma

Lemma 5.1
.

Under the hypothesis of Theorem
3.1

C_{5}>0

From the Esseen’s anti-cencentration inequality (see Theorem 7.17 in
[

(5.1)

\mathbb{P}\left(|P(re^{i\theta})|\leq a\right)\leq\mathbb{P}\left(|\Re P(re^{i\theta})|\leq a\right)\leq C_{0}a\int_{-a^{-1}}^{a^{-1}}|g(t)|\textnormal{d}t

for some universal positive constant
C_{0}
and
g
is the characteristic function of real part of the polynomial,
\Re P(re^{i\theta})=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\xi_{k}r^{k}\cos(k\theta)
. If
g_{k}(t)
is the characteristic function of
\xi_{k}r^{k}\cos(k\theta)

\left|g_{k}(t)\right|^{2}=\mathbb{E}_{\tilde{\xi}_{k}}\left[\cos\left(tr^{k}\cos(k\theta)\tilde{\xi}_{k}\right)\right],

where
\tilde{\xi}_{k}
is the symmetrization of
\xi_{k}

\tilde{\xi}_{k}:=\xi_{k}-\xi^{\prime}_{k}
and
\xi^{\prime}_{k}

\xi_{k}
(which also independent with all other considered random variables), and
\mathbb{E}_{\tilde{\xi}_{k}}

\tilde{\xi}_{k}

x\leq\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}(1-x^{2})\right)
, which is valid for all

, is following that

\left|g_{k}(t)\right|\leq\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}(1-\left|g_{k}(t)\right|^{2})\right)=\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\left[1-\mathbb{E}_{\tilde{\xi}_{k}}\left[\cos\left(tr^{k}\cos(k\theta)\tilde{\xi}_{k}\right)\right]\right]\right).

From the above, the characteristic function
g(t)
of

\displaystyle\left|g(t)\right|

\displaystyle=\prod_{k=0}^{n}\left|g_{k}(t)\right|\leq\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\left[n-\sum_{k=0}^{n}\mathbb{E}_{\tilde{\xi}_{k}}\left[\cos\left(tr^{k}\cos(k\theta)\tilde{\xi}_{k}\right)\right]\right]\right)

\xi_{0}

\overline{\xi}_{k}
(see hypothesis

\displaystyle\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\mathds{P}\left(\left|G_{n}(re^{i\theta})\right|\leq a\right)\textnormal{d}\mu(\theta)

\displaystyle\quad\leq a\int_{-a^{-1}}^{a^{-1}}e^{-n/2}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\exp\left(\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\mathbb{E}{{}_{\tilde{\xi}_{k}}}\left[\cos\left(r^{k}\cos(k\theta)t\tilde{\xi}_{k}\right)\left(\mathds{1}_{\left\{\tilde{\xi}_{k}\neq 0\right\}}+\mathds{1}_{\left\{\tilde{\xi}_{k}=0\right\}}\right)\right]\right)\textnormal{d}\mu(\theta)\textnormal{d}t

(5.3)

\displaystyle\quad=a\int_{-a^{-1}}^{a^{-1}}e^{-(1-p)n/2}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\exp\left(\frac{1-p}{2}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\mathbb{E}{{}_{\overline{\xi}_{k}}}\left[\cos(r^{k}\cos(k\theta)t\overline{\xi}_{k})\right]\right)\textnormal{d}\mu(\theta)\textnormal{d}t.

\displaystyle\Lambda_{1}(t,n)

\displaystyle:=\left\{\theta\in[-\pi,\pi]:\sum_{k=0}^{n}\mathbb{E}{{}_{\overline{\xi}_{k}}}\left[\cos(tr^{k}\cos(k\theta)\overline{\xi}_{k})\right]<\frac{n}{2}\right\},

\displaystyle\Lambda_{2}(t,n)

\displaystyle:=[-\pi,\pi]\setminus\Lambda_{1}(t,n)

The integral respect to
\textnormal{d}\mu(\theta)
in
5.3
over
\Lambda_{1}(t,n)
gives a exponential bound,
e^{-(1-p)n/2}e^{(1-p)n/4}=e^{-(1-p)n/4}
. Thus, we focus in the same integral but over
\Lambda_{2}(t,n)
.

To handle the case of
\Lambda_{2}(t,n)
, we introduce the notation
\alpha_{k}(\theta):=r^{k}\cos(k\theta)
and observe that

\displaystyle\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\left(\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\mathbb{E}{{}_{\overline{\xi}_{k}}}\left[\cos(r^{k}\cos(k\theta)t\overline{\xi}_{k})\right]\right)^{10}\textnormal{d}\mu(\theta)

\displaystyle\quad=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\left(\frac{1}{4}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\mathbb{E}{{}_{\overline{\xi}_{k}}}\left[e^{i\alpha_{k}(\theta)t\overline{\xi}_{k}}+e^{-i\alpha_{k}(\theta)t\overline{\xi}_{k}}\right]\right)^{10}\textnormal{d}\mu(\theta)

\displaystyle\quad=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\left(\frac{1}{4}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\left(\mathbb{E}{{}_{\overline{\xi}_{k}}}\left[e^{i\alpha_{k}(\theta)t\overline{\xi}_{k}}\right]+\mathbb{E}{{}_{\overline{\xi_{k}}}}\left[e^{-i\alpha_{k}(\theta)t\overline{\xi}_{k}}\right]\right)\right)^{10}\textnormal{d}\mu(\theta)

\displaystyle\quad=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\left(\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\mathbb{E}{{}_{\overline{\xi}_{k}}}\left[e^{i\alpha_{k}(\theta)t\overline{\xi}_{k}}\right]\right)^{10}\textnormal{d}\mu(\theta)

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{1}{2^{10}}\sum_{k_{1},\ldots,k_{10}=0}^{n}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\mathbb{E}{{}_{\overline{\xi}_{k_{1:10}}}}\left[e^{it\sum_{l=1}^{10}a_{k_{l}}(\theta)\overline{\xi}_{k_{l}}}\right]\textnormal{d}\mu(\theta)

due to that
\overline{\xi}_{k}
is a symmetric random variable and the fact

(\mathbb{E}{}\left[X\right])^{2}=\mathbb{E}{}\left[X\right]\mathbb{E}{}\left[X\right]=\mathbb{E}{}\left[X\right]\mathbb{E}{}\left[Y\right]=\mathbb{E}{}\left[XY\right]

where
Y
is a independent copy of
X
. Using Fubini Theorem and writing
\overline{\xi}_{k_{1:10}}:=\left\{\overline{\xi}_{k_{1}},\ldots,\overline{\xi}_{k_{10}}\right\}
, we have

\frac{1}{2^{10}}\sum_{k_{1},\ldots,k_{10}=0}^{n}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\mathbb{E}{{}_{\overline{\xi}_{k_{1:10}}}}\left[e^{it\sum_{l=1}^{10}a_{k_{l}}(\theta)\overline{\xi}_{k_{l}}}\right]\textnormal{d}\mu(\theta)=\frac{1}{2^{10}}\sum_{k_{1},\ldots,k_{10}=0}^{n}\mathbb{E}{{}_{\overline{\xi}_{k_{1:10}}}}\left[\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}e^{it\sum_{l=1}^{10}a_{k_{l}}(\theta)\overline{\xi}_{k_{l}}}\textnormal{d}\mu(\theta)\right].

Note that in the above sum there are
2^{10}
terms where at least one
k_{l}
is equal to zero in
\sum_{l=1}^{10}a_{k_{l}}(\theta)\overline{\xi}_{k_{l}}
and the contribution of the sum of all these terms is at most
1
. Thus, we can assume for the following that all
k_{l}\geq 1
. To estimate the rest of main sum, we split the expectation as follows:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}{{}_{\overline{\xi}_{k_{1:10}}}}\left[\left|\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}e^{it\sum_{l=1}^{10}a_{k_{l}}(\theta)\overline{\xi}_{k_{l}}}\textnormal{d}\mu(\theta)\right|\right]

\displaystyle\quad\leq\mathbb{E}{{}_{\overline{\xi}_{k_{1:10}}}}\left[\mathds{1}_{\left\{\max_{l\in\left\{1,\ldots,10\right\}}\left\{\left|\overline{\xi}_{k_{l}}\right|\right\}\leq n^{-1}\right\}}\cdot\left|\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}e^{it\sum_{l=1}^{10}a_{k_{l}}(\theta)\overline{\xi}_{k_{l}}}\textnormal{d}\mu(\theta)\right|\right]

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\mathbb{E}{{}_{\overline{\xi}_{k_{1:10}}}}\left[\mathds{1}_{\left\{\max_{l\in\left\{1,\ldots,10\right\}}\left\{\left|\overline{\xi}_{k_{l}}\right|\right\}\geq n^{-1}\right\}}\cdot\left|\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}e^{it\sum_{l=1}^{10}a_{k_{l}}(\theta)\overline{\xi}_{k_{l}}}\textnormal{d}\mu(\theta)\right|\right].

By hypothesis
\mathcal{H}4
, the first expectation of the right side satisfies

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}{{}_{\overline{\xi}_{k_{1:10}}}}\left[\mathds{1}_{\left\{{\max_{l\in\left\{1,\ldots,10\right\}}\left\{\left|\overline{\xi}_{k_{l}}\right|\right\}\leq n^{-1}}\right\}}\cdot\left|\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}e^{it\sum_{l=1}^{10}a_{k_{l}}(\theta)\overline{\xi}_{k_{l}}}\textnormal{d}\mu(\theta)\right|\right]}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\mathbb{E}{{}_{\overline{\xi}_{k_{1:10}}}}\left[\mathds{1}_{\left\{\max_{l\in\left\{1,\ldots,10\right\}}\left\{\left|\overline{\xi}_{k_{l}}\right|\right\}\leq n^{-1}\right\}}\right]

\displaystyle\quad=\mathds{P}\left(\left|\overline{\xi}_{k_{l}}\right|\leq n^{-1}\mbox{ for all $l=1,\ldots,10$}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\leq\left(C_{2}n^{-1/2}\right)^{10}=C_{2}^{10}n^{-5}.

For the remaining expectation, we assume that
\max_{l\in\left\{1,\ldots,10\right\}}\left|\overline{\xi}_{k_{l}}\right|\geq n^{-1}
. Let
T(\theta)=\sum_{l=1}^{10}a_{k_{l}}(\theta)\overline{\xi}_{k_{l}}
. Let
h=10
and
c>0
which its value will be defined later. Let

\theta_{1}<\theta_{2}<\ldots<\theta_{L}\in[-\pi,\pi]

points in the unit circle which are solutions of

\left|T^{\prime}(\theta_{j})\right|=n^{-h(c+1)},\quad j=1,\ldots,L.

Since the derivative
T^{\prime}
is a trigonometric polynomial of degree
\max_{l=1,\ldots,10}k_{l}\leq n
, it is followed that
L\leq 2n
. We divide the unit circle in
L
intervals define as

I_{j}:=[\theta_{j},\theta_{j+1}]\quad\mbox{ for }\quad j=1,\ldots,L-1\quad\mbox{ and }\quad I_{L}:=[-\pi,\pi]\setminus\left(\cup_{j=1}^{L-1}I_{j}\right).

We say that
I_{j}
is a good interval if
\left|T^{\prime}(\theta)\right|\geq n^{-h(c+1)}
for all
\theta\in I_{j}
. We say that
I_{j}
is bad if it is not good. We denote by
\mathcal{G}
the set of good intervals and by
\mathcal{B}
the ser of bad intervals. We state that if
I\in\mathcal{B}
, then
\mu(I)\leq n^{-(c+1)}
. If the opposite happens, then by construction there is an interval
I\subset[-\pi,\pi]
such that

\mu(I)>\frac{1}{n^{c+1}}\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\sup_{\theta\in I}\left|T^{\prime}(\theta)\right|\leq n^{-h(c+1)}.

By the Turán’s Lemma, we have

\sup_{\theta\in[-\pi,\pi]}\left|T^{\prime}(\theta)\right|\leq\left(\frac{C}{\mu(I)}\right)^{h-1}n^{-h(c+1)}\leq C^{h-1}n^{-(c+1)}.

On the other side, there a universal positive constante
C_{1}
such that

\displaystyle\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\left|T^{\prime}(\theta)\right|^{2}\textnormal{d}\theta

\displaystyle=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\left(-\sum_{l=1}^{h}r^{k_{l}}k_{l}\sin(k_{l}\theta)\overline{\xi}_{k_{l}}\right)^{2}\textnormal{d}\mu(\theta)

\displaystyle=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\left(\sum_{l_{1}=1}^{h}\sum_{l_{2}=1}^{h}k_{l_{1}}k_{l_{2}}r^{k_{l_{1}}+k_{l_{2}}}\overline{\xi}_{k_{l_{1}}}\overline{\xi}_{k_{l_{2}}}\sin(k_{l_{1}}\theta)\sin(k_{l_{2}}\theta)\right)\textnormal{d}\mu(\theta)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=1}^{h}k_{l}^{2}r^{2k_{l}}(\overline{\xi}_{k_{l}})^{2}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\sin^{2}(k_{l}\theta)\textnormal{d}\mu(\theta)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{C_{1}}{2}\sum_{l=1}^{h}(\overline{\xi}_{k_{l}})^{2}\geq\frac{C_{1}}{2}\max_{l}\left\{(\overline{\xi}_{k_{l}})^{2}\right\}\geq\frac{C_{1}}{2}n^{-2},

which means that if
c>1
the inequality
\left[C^{h-1}n^{-(c+1)}\right]^{2}\geq\frac{C_{1}}{2}n^{-2}
does not satisfy for all sufficiently large
n
since
h=10
. Thus, for all sufficiently larger
n
we have

\mu\left(\cup_{I\in\mathcal{B}}I\right)\leq\frac{2n}{n^{c+1}}=\frac{2}{n^{c}}

with
c>1
.

Finally, we note using the above observations and the integration by parts,

\displaystyle\left|\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}e^{itT(\theta)}\textnormal{d}\mu(\theta)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\left(\mathds{1}_{\cup_{I\in\mathcal{B}}I}(\theta)+\mathds{1}_{\cup_{I\in\mathcal{G}}I}(\theta)\right)e^{itT(\theta)}\textnormal{d}\mu(\theta)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{n^{c}}+\sum_{I\in\mathcal{G}}\left|\int_{I}e^{itT(\theta)}\textnormal{d}\mu(\theta)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{n^{c}}+\frac{2n\cdot n^{h(c+1)}}{\left|t\right|}+\sum_{I\in\mathcal{G}}\frac{1}{\left|t\right|}\left|\int_{I}\frac{T^{\prime\prime}(\theta)}{(T^{\prime}(\theta))^{2}}\textnormal{d}\mu(\theta)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{n^{c}}+\frac{2n\cdot n^{h(c+1)}}{\left|t\right|}+\frac{2n}{\left|t\right|}\left|\int_{I}\frac{T^{\prime\prime}(\theta)}{(T^{\prime}(\theta))^{2}}\textnormal{d}\mu(\theta)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{n^{c}}+\frac{2n\cdot n^{h(c+1)}}{\left|t\right|}+\frac{2n\cdot n^{2h(c+1)}}{\left|t\right|}{\int_{I}\left|T^{\prime\prime}(\theta)\right|\textnormal{d}\mu(\theta)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{n^{c}}+\frac{2n\cdot n^{h(c+1)}}{\left|t\right|}+\frac{20n\cdot n^{2h(c+1)+2}}{\left|t\right|}\sum_{l=1}^{10}|\overline{\xi}_{l}|.

Now, taking the expectation the above expression, we observe that there a positive constant
C_{2}
depending on the distribution of
\xi_{0}
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}{{}_{\overline{\xi}_{k_{1:10}}}}\left[\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}e^{it\sum_{l=1}^{10}a_{k_{l}}(\theta)\overline{\xi}_{k_{l}}}\textnormal{d}\mu(\theta)\right]

\displaystyle\quad\leq\frac{C^{10}}{n^{5}}+\frac{4}{n^{c}}+\frac{4n\cdot n^{h(c+1)}}{\left|t\right|}+\frac{40n\cdot n^{2h(c+1)+2}}{\left|t\right|}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\frac{C^{10}}{n^{5}}+\frac{4}{n^{c}}+\frac{4n^{h(c+1)+1}}{\left|t\right|}+\frac{40n^{2h(c+1)+3}}{\left|t\right|}

\displaystyle\quad\leq C_{2}\left(\frac{1}{n^{5}}+\frac{1}{n^{c}}+\frac{n^{2h(c+1)+3}}{\left|t\right|}\right).

where
c>1
. Hence there exista a positive constante
C_{3}
depending on the distribution of
\xi_{0}
such that

\displaystyle\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\left(\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\mathbb{E}{{}_{\overline{\xi_{k}}}}\left[\cos(r^{k}\cos(k\theta)t\overline{\xi}_{k})\right]\right)^{10}\textnormal{d}\mu(\theta)

\displaystyle\quad\quad=\frac{1}{2^{10}}\sum_{k_{1},\ldots,k_{10}=0}^{n}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\mathbb{E}{{}_{\overline{\xi}_{k_{1:10}}}}\left[e^{it\sum_{l=1}^{10}a_{k_{l}}(\theta)\overline{\xi}_{k_{l}}}\right]\textnormal{d}\mu(\theta)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\leq 1+C_{2}n^{10}\left(\frac{1}{n^{5}}+\frac{1}{n^{c}}+\frac{n^{2h(c+1)+3}}{\left|t\right|}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\leq C_{3}n^{10}\left(\frac{1}{n^{5}}+\frac{1}{n^{c}}+\frac{n^{2h(c+1)+3}}{\left|t\right|}\right).

By Markov’s inequality, we have that there exists a positive constant
C_{4}
depending on the distribution of
\xi_{0}
such that

\displaystyle\mu(\Lambda_{2}(t,n))

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2^{10}}{(1-p)^{10}n^{10}}\left[C_{3}n^{10}\left(\frac{1}{n^{5}}+\frac{1}{n^{c}}+\frac{n^{2h(c+1)+3}}{\left|t\right|}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leq C_{4}\left(\frac{1}{n^{5}}+\frac{1}{n^{c}}+\frac{n^{2h(c+1)+3}}{\left|t\right|}\right).

From expression
5.3
and previous statements, for
a<1
we observed

\displaystyle\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\mathds{P}\left(\left|G_{n}(re^{i\theta})\right|\leq a\right)\textnormal{d}\mu(\theta)

\displaystyle\leq 2e^{-(1-p)n/4}+a\int_{-a^{-1}}^{a^{-1}}\mu(\Lambda_{2}(t,n))\textnormal{d}t

\displaystyle\leq 2e^{-(1-p)n/4}+2a\int_{0}^{a^{-1}}\mu(\Lambda_{2}(t,n))\textnormal{d}t

\displaystyle\leq 2e^{-(1-p)n/4}+2a+2a\int_{1}^{a^{-1}}\mu(\Lambda_{2}(t,n))\textnormal{d}t

\displaystyle\leq 2e^{-(1-p)n/4}+2a+2C_{4}\left(\frac{1}{n^{5}}+\frac{1}{n^{c}}+n^{2hc+6}a\log(a^{-1})\right)

\displaystyle\leq C_{5}\left(\frac{1}{n^{5}}+\frac{1}{n^{c}}+n^{2hc+6}a\log(a^{-1})\right),

for some positive constant
C_{5}
depending on the distribution of
\xi_{0}
.

∎

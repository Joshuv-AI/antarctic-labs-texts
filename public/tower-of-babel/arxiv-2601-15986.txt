Semiclassical entanglement entropy for spin-field interaction
Matheus V. Scherer, Lea F. Santos, Alexandre D. Ribeiro

Abstract
We study a general bipartite quantum system consisting of a spin interacting with a bosonic field, with the initial state prepared as the product of a spin coherent state and a canonical coherent state. Our goal is to develop a semiclassical framework to describe the entanglement dynamics between these two subsystems. Using appropriate approximations, we derive a semiclassical expression for the entanglement entropy that depends exclusively on the trajectories of the underlying classical description. By analytically extending the classical phase space into the complex domain, we identify additional complex trajectories that significantly improve the accuracy of the semiclassical description. The inclusion of these complex trajectories allows us to capture the entanglement dynamics with remarkable precision, even well beyond the Ehrenfest time. The approach is illustrated with a representative example, where the role of real and complex trajectories in reproducing the quantum entanglement entropy is explicitly demonstrated.

In this work, we focus on entanglement in the context of the quantum–classical transition. One of the most emblematic results in this area is the link between the rate of entanglement growth and the Lyapunov exponents of the underlying classical dynamics, as discussed in the seminal work by Furuya, Nemes, and Pellegrino

This implies that when one initializes a localized separable quantum state in phase space, its entanglement increases in time at a rate determined by the degree of classical chaoticity of the region it occupies. This establishes a direct correspondence between chaos and entanglement generation. A promising route to understanding this relationship consists of introducing semiclassical entropy formulas that reveal which classical ingredients govern entanglement dynamics. This approach has been successfully implemented for systems of two interacting particles in the canonical (position and momentum) phase space

The derivations involve nontrivial modifications over previous studies and yield new insights into the role of complex trajectories in spin-field entanglement. Our analysis also provides a more detailed and systematic presentation of the semiclassical approach, making it broadly applicable to other hybrid quantum systems.

II.1
Coherent states

We consider a bipartite system with Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{z}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{s}
, where
\mathcal{H}_{z}
describes a canonical infinite-dimensional degree of freedom and
\mathcal{H}_{s}
corresponds to a spin-
j
system of dimension
2j+1
. We define the canonical coherent-state basis
\{|z\rangle\}
and the spin coherent-state basis
\{|s\rangle\}
. In terms of the Fock basis
\{|n_{z}\rangle\}
and the basis
\{|m_{s}\rangle\}
, composed by the eigenstates of
\hat{J}_{{\mathrm{z}}}
, with the spin operator given by
\hat{\mathbf{J}}=(\hat{J}_{{\mathrm{x}}},\hat{J}_{{\mathrm{y}}},\hat{J}_{{\mathrm{z}}})
, we have

|z\rangle=\mathcal{N}_{z}\,\mbox{e}^{z\hat{a}^{\dagger}}|n_{z}=0\rangle\quad\mathrm{and}\quad|s\rangle=\mathcal{N}_{s}\,\mbox{e}^{s\hat{J}_{+}}|m_{s}=-j\rangle,

(2)

where
\mathcal{N}_{z}\equiv\mbox{e}^{-\frac{1}{2}|z|^{2}}
and
\mathcal{N}_{s}\equiv(1+|s|^{2})^{-j}
ensure normalization,
\hat{a}^{\dagger}
is the bosonic creation operator,
|n_{z}=0\rangle
is the Fock vacuum state,
\hat{J}_{+}\equiv\hat{J}_{{\mathrm{x}}}+i\hat{J}_{{\mathrm{y}}}
is the raising spin operator, and
|m_{s}=-j\rangle
is the extremal eigenstate of
\hat{J}_{{\mathrm{z}}}
with the lowest eigenvalue. The complex numbers
z
and
s
are the labels of the coherent states, and their meaning will be clarified later.

The identity operators for the canonical and spin bases are given, respectively, by

\hat{\mathrm{I}}_{z}\equiv\int\frac{|z\rangle\langle z|\,\mbox{d}z^{\text{\tiny R}}\,\mbox{d}z^{\text{\tiny I}}}{\pi}\quad\mathrm{and}\quad\hat{\mathrm{I}}_{s}\equiv\frac{2j+1}{\pi}\int\frac{|s\rangle\langle s|\,\mbox{d}s^{\text{\tiny R}}\,\mbox{d}s^{\text{\tiny I}}}{\left(1+|s|^{2}\right)^{2}},

(3)

where
z^{\text{\tiny R}}
(
z^{\text{\tiny I}}
) indicates the real (imaginary) part of the complex label
z
, and equivalently for
s
. In the above equations, the first integration should run over the whole complex plan
z
, while the second one runs over the complex plan
s
.

Both bases are overcomplete, as their elements are not mutually orthogonal. Indeed, the overlaps between any two basis states read

\langle z_{\mbox{\scriptsize b}}|z_{\mbox{\scriptsize k}}\rangle=\mathcal{N}_{z_{\mbox{\tiny b}}}\mathcal{N}_{z_{\mbox{\tiny k}}}\mbox{e}^{z_{\mbox{\tiny b}}^{*}z_{\mbox{\tiny k}}}\quad\mathrm{and}\quad\langle s_{\mbox{\scriptsize b}}|s_{\mbox{\scriptsize k}}\rangle=\mathcal{N}_{s_{\mbox{\tiny b}}}\mathcal{N}_{s_{\mbox{\tiny k}}}\left(1+s_{\mbox{\scriptsize b}}^{*}s_{\mbox{\scriptsize k}}\right)^{2j}.

(4)

II.1.1
Canonical coherent states

The complex number
z
that labels the canonical coherent state
|z\rangle
can be parametrized as

z=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\frac{q}{b}+i\frac{p}{c}\right),

(5)

where
bc=\hbar
and
(q,p,b,c)
are convenient new parameters.

Using
\hat{a}|z\rangle=z|z\rangle
and the relations for the position
\hat{q}
and momentum
\hat{p}
operators,

\hat{q}=\frac{b}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\hat{a}^{\dagger}+\hat{a}\right)\quad\mathrm{and}\quad\hat{p}=\frac{ic}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\hat{a}^{\dagger}-\hat{a}\right),

(6)

we obtain the expectation values and the variances of position and momentum,

\displaystyle\langle\hat{q}\rangle_{z}=q,\quad\Delta q^{2}\equiv\langle\hat{q}^{2}\rangle_{z}-\langle\hat{q}\rangle_{z}^{2}=b^{2}/2,

(7)

\displaystyle\langle\hat{p}\rangle_{z}=p,\quad\Delta p^{2}\equiv\langle\hat{p}^{2}\rangle_{z}-\langle\hat{p}\rangle_{z}^{2}=c^{2}/2.

Thus,
|z\rangle
saturates the Heisenberg uncertainty principle and represents the most classical states of the canonical degree of freedom.

II.1.2
Spin coherent states

The spin label
s
that labels the spin coherent state
|s\rangle
can be expressed in terms of spherical coordinates
(\theta,\phi)
via the projection

s=\cot(\theta/2)(\cos\phi-i\sin\phi),

(8)

where
0\leq\theta\leq\pi
and
0\leq\phi<2\pi
.

To understand the parametrization of
|s\rangle
,
we define
\mathbf{n}=(\sin\theta\cos\phi,\sin\theta\sin\phi,\cos\theta)
, so that
\mathbf{n}
is a versor spanning a sphere of radius one, assuming that
\theta=0
(
\theta=\pi
) corresponds to its north (south) pole.
Then, let the
s
complex plane divide the sphere into north and south hemispheres, with the real and the imaginary axes pointing to
\phi=0
and
\phi=3\pi/2
, respectively. In this way, we can perform a stereographical projection from the north pole, linking the sphere point
(\theta,\phi)
with the coordinates
(s^{\text{\tiny R}},s^{\text{\tiny I}})
of the complex plane, as represented in Eq. (
8
). Now, we calculate

\Delta J_{l}^{2}\equiv\langle\hat{J}_{l}^{2}\rangle_{s}-\langle\hat{J}_{l}\rangle_{s}^{2}=\frac{j}{2}(1-n_{l}^{2}),\qquad\langle\big\{\hat{J}_{r}-\langle\hat{J}_{r}\rangle,\hat{J}_{l}-\langle\hat{J}_{l}\rangle\big\}\rangle_{s}=-j\,n_{r}\,n_{l},

(9)

and
\langle\hat{\mathbf{J}}\rangle_{s}=j\mathbf{n}
, where
r\neq l
can assume
\mathrm{x}
,
\mathrm{y}
, or
\mathrm{z}
. The operation
\{\star,\ast\}
stands for anticommutator. With these results, we can conclude that the spin coherent state has the most well-localized representation in the unit sphere, as its uncertainties saturate the Heisenberg relation
\langle\Delta\hat{A}^{2}\rangle\langle\Delta\hat{B}^{2}\rangle\geq\frac{1}{4}|\langle[\hat{A},\hat{B}]\rangle|^{2}+\frac{1}{4}|\langle\{\Delta\hat{A},\Delta\hat{B}\}\rangle|^{2}
, where the operators
\hat{A}
and
\hat{B}
can be
\hat{J}_{{\mathrm{x}}}
,
\hat{J}_{{\mathrm{y}}}
, and
\hat{J}_{{\mathrm{z}}}
. In this sense, they can also be seen as the quantum states whose quantum description better approaches a classical angular momentum.

The canonical and spin coherent states thus both represent the most classical-like states in their respective spaces. Their use provides a natural framework for semiclassical approximations, justifying their role in the construction of our propagators and, ultimately, in the semiclassical entanglement entropy.

II.2
Semiclassical forward and backward propagators

In this section, we will briefly outline the derivation of the semiclassical expressions
K^{\mathrm{sc}}_{\pm}
for the quantum propagators defined in Eq. (
1
). Our approach follows the methods developed in Refs.
5
;
43
;
45
;
46
, where related problems were investigated.

II.2.1
Path integral formalism

To apply a semiclassical approximation to Eq. (
1
), we begin with its path-integral representation. This can be obtained by dividing the total propagation time
T
into
N
infinitesimal steps of duration
\epsilon
and inserting identities (
3
) between successive short-time evolution operators
\hat{U}^{\pm}_{\epsilon}\equiv\mbox{e}^{\mp i\hat{H}\epsilon/\hbar}
. The resulting expression is

\displaystyle K_{\pm}^{\mathrm{q}}

\displaystyle=\lim_{N\to\infty}\int\left[\prod_{\tau=1}^{N-1}\left(\frac{2j+1}{\pi^{2}}\right)\frac{\mbox{d}z_{\tau}^{\mbox{\tiny R}}\,\mbox{d}z_{\tau}^{\mbox{\tiny I}}\,\mbox{d}s_{\tau}^{\mbox{\tiny R}}\,\mbox{d}s_{\tau}^{\mbox{\tiny I}}}{(1+|s_{\tau}|^{2})^{2}}\right]

(10)

\displaystyle\times\langle z_{\mbox{\scriptsize b}},s_{\mbox{\scriptsize b}}|\,\hat{U}^{\pm}_{\epsilon}\,|z_{1},s_{1}\rangle\left[\prod_{\tau=1}^{N-2}\langle z_{\tau},s_{\tau}|\,\hat{U}^{\pm}_{\epsilon}\,|z_{\tau+1},s_{\tau+1}\rangle\right]\langle z_{N-1},s_{N-1}|\,\hat{U}^{\pm}_{\epsilon}\,|z_{\mbox{\scriptsize k}},s_{\mbox{\scriptsize k}}\rangle.

The interpretation of this equation is similar to that of the most-known propagator in the position representation. Indeed, in the space consisting of the integration variables, all paths connecting the ket labels (
z_{\mbox{\scriptsize k}},s_{\mbox{\scriptsize k}}
) to the bra labels (
z_{\mbox{\scriptsize b}},s_{\mbox{\scriptsize b}}
), from initial to final times, produce a complex contribution to the integral, and the exact quantum propagators
K_{\pm}^{\mathrm{q}}
are obtained by summing over all of them.

Carrying out this procedure exactly is intractable in general. Progress is possible, however, in the semiclassical limit, which is formally achieved by assuming the asymptotic limit
\hbar\to 0
and
j\to\infty
, with the product
j\,\hbar
held fixed. To perform this approximation, we first reinterpret Eq. (
10
) as a line integral in a multidimensional space. To understand this point, consider, for simplicity, only the variables
z_{1}^{\mbox{\tiny R}}
and
z_{1}^{\mbox{\tiny I}}
. Although the original prescription in Eq. (
3
) imposes an integration over the entire plane
z_{1}
, if we define the new variables
v_{1\mbox{\tiny{A}}}\equiv z_{1}^{\mbox{\tiny R}}-iz_{1}^{\mbox{\tiny I}}
and
u_{1\mbox{\tiny{A}}}\equiv z_{1}^{\mbox{\tiny R}}+iz_{1}^{\mbox{\tiny I}}
, that surface integral becomes equivalent to line integrals in the complex planes
v_{1\mbox{\tiny{A}}}
and
u_{1\mbox{\tiny{A}}}
, now considered as independent variables. This equivalence is achieved by imposing that the line integral on
v_{1\mbox{\tiny{A}}}
(
u_{1\mbox{\tiny{A}}}
) be along the real (imaginary) axis. Next, extending this construction to the other variables of the integral in (
10
) and calculating the appropriate Jacobian, we get

K_{\pm}^{\mathrm{q}}(z_{\mbox{\scriptsize b}},s_{\mbox{\scriptsize b}},z_{\mbox{\scriptsize k}},s_{\mbox{\scriptsize k}},T)=\lim_{N\to\infty}\int\left[\prod_{\tau=1}^{N-1}\left(\frac{2j+1}{-4\pi^{2}}\right)\frac{\mbox{d}u_{\tau\mbox{\tiny{A}}}\,\mbox{d}v_{\tau\mbox{\tiny{A}}}\,\mbox{d}u_{\tau\mbox{\tiny{B}}}\,\mbox{d}v_{\tau\mbox{\tiny{B}}}}{(1+u_{\tau\mbox{\tiny{B}}}v_{\tau\mbox{\tiny{B}}})^{2}}\right]~\mbox{e}^{\mathcal{S}},

(11)

where
\mbox{e}^{\mathcal{S}}
represents the second line of Eq. (
10
) expressed in terms of the new variables

v_{\tau\mbox{\tiny{A}}}\equiv z_{\tau}^{\mbox{\tiny R}}-iz_{\tau}^{\mbox{\tiny I}},\quad u_{\tau\mbox{\tiny{A}}}\equiv z_{\tau}^{\mbox{\tiny R}}+iz_{\tau}^{\mbox{\tiny I}},\quad v_{\tau\mbox{\tiny{B}}}\equiv s_{\tau}^{\mbox{\tiny R}}-is_{\tau}^{\mbox{\tiny I}},\quad\mathrm{and}\quad u_{\tau\mbox{\tiny{B}}}\equiv s_{\tau}^{\mbox{\tiny R}}+is_{\tau}^{\mbox{\tiny I}},

(12)

for
\tau=1,\ldots,N-1
. Notice that, at this point, we already refer to the canonical degree of freedom as part A of the system and the spin variable corresponds to part B. With this procedure, the path integral formulation for
K_{\pm}^{\mathrm{q}}
can be understood as a
4N
-dimensional complex line integral (
11
).

II.2.2
Complex trajectories

The line integral in Eq. (
11
) can be evaluated using the saddle-point method
8
. Instead of considering the contribution of all points belonging to the paths appearing in Eq. (
11
), the method focuses exclusively on the critical points, around which the integral acquires its dominant contributions. In the semiclassical limit, contributions from other regions of the integration space interfere destructively and can therefore be neglected. The critical points of all integration paths are determined by the stationary condition
\delta\mathcal{S}=0
, that is,

\frac{\partial\mathcal{S}}{\partial u_{\tau\mbox{\tiny A}}}=\frac{\partial\mathcal{S}}{\partial v_{\tau\mbox{\tiny A}}}=\frac{\partial\mathcal{S}}{\partial u_{\tau\mbox{\tiny B}}}=\frac{\partial\mathcal{S}}{\partial v_{\tau\mbox{\tiny B}}}=0,

(13)

for each time step
\tau=1,\ldots,N-1
. Taking the continuum limit
N\to\infty
, we recognize, as expected, that the set of critical points contitutes a critical path (in time) satisfying the classical equations of motion,

\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle\dot{u}_{\mbox{\tiny A}}=-\frac{i}{\hbar}\frac{\partial\tilde{H}}{\partial v_{\mbox{\tiny A}}},&&\displaystyle\dot{u}_{\mbox{\tiny B}}=-\frac{i(1+u_{\mbox{\tiny B}}v_{\mbox{\tiny B}})^{2}}{2j\hbar}\frac{\partial\tilde{H}}{\partial v_{\mbox{\tiny B}}},\\[8.0pt]
\displaystyle\dot{v}_{\mbox{\tiny A}}=+\frac{i}{\hbar}\frac{\partial\tilde{H}}{\partial u_{\mbox{\tiny A}}},&&\displaystyle\dot{v}_{\mbox{\tiny B}}=+\frac{i(1+u_{\mbox{\tiny B}}v_{\mbox{\tiny B}})^{2}}{2j\hbar}\frac{\partial\tilde{H}}{\partial u_{\mbox{\tiny B}}},\end{array}

(14)

where we define the classical Hamiltonian function
\tilde{H}=\tilde{H}(v_{\mbox{\tiny A}},v_{\mbox{\tiny B}},u_{\mbox{\tiny A}},u_{\mbox{\tiny B}})
by

\tilde{H}(z^{*},s^{*},z,s)\equiv\langle z,s|\hat{H}|z,s\rangle.

(15)

To complete the specification of the trajectories, we recall that the quantum propagator depends on five parameters:
T
,
z_{\mbox{\scriptsize b}}
,
s_{\mbox{\scriptsize b}}
,
z_{\mbox{\scriptsize k}}
, and
s_{\mbox{\scriptsize k}}
. These elements are directly involved with the boundary conditions that the critical trajectories must satisfy. In terms of the variables
[u_{\mbox{\tiny A}}(t),\,u_{\mbox{\tiny B}}(t),\,v_{\mbox{\tiny A}}(t),\,v_{\mbox{\tiny B}}(t)]
, the constraints are

\begin{array}[]{l}\left.\begin{array}[]{ll}u_{\mbox{\tiny A}}^{\prime}\equiv u_{\mbox{\tiny A}}(0)=z_{\mbox{\scriptsize k}},&\quad v_{\mbox{\tiny A}}^{\prime\prime}\equiv v_{\mbox{\tiny A}}(T)=z_{\mbox{\scriptsize b}}^{*},\\
u_{\mbox{\tiny B}}^{\prime}\equiv u_{\mbox{\tiny B}}(0)=s_{\mbox{\scriptsize k}},&\quad v_{\mbox{\tiny B}}^{\prime\prime}\equiv v_{\mbox{\tiny B}}(T)=s_{\mbox{\scriptsize b}}^{*},\end{array}\right\}\;\mathrm{for}\;\,K_{+}^{\mathrm{sc}};\\[20.0pt]
\left.\begin{array}[]{ll}u_{\mbox{\tiny A}}^{\prime\prime}\equiv u_{\mbox{\tiny A}}(T)=z_{\mbox{\scriptsize k}},&\quad v_{\mbox{\tiny A}}^{\prime}\equiv v_{\mbox{\tiny A}}(0)=z_{\mbox{\scriptsize b}}^{*},\\
u_{\mbox{\tiny B}}^{\prime\prime}\equiv u_{\mbox{\tiny B}}(T)=s_{\mbox{\scriptsize k}},&\quad v_{\mbox{\tiny B}}^{\prime}\equiv v_{\mbox{\tiny B}}(0)=s_{\mbox{\scriptsize b}}^{*},\end{array}\right\}\;\mathrm{for}\;\,K_{-}^{\mathrm{sc}}.\end{array}

(16)

Notice that the trajectories involved in
K_{+}^{\mathrm{sc}}
are governed by the same equations of motion of those involved in
K_{-}^{\mathrm{sc}}
, but they differ by their boundary conditions.

At this point, it is important to understand why the trajectories are classified as complex. Although the new variables
u_{\mbox{\tiny A}}
,
u_{\mbox{\tiny B}}
,
v_{\mbox{\tiny A}}
, and
v_{\mbox{\tiny B}}
are complex by definition (
12
), it is easy to see that, provided that
u_{\mbox{\tiny A}}=v^{*}_{\mbox{\tiny A}}
and
u_{\mbox{\tiny B}}=v^{*}_{\mbox{\tiny B}}
, the classical
real
phase space can be recovered using the parametrization (
5
). This reasoning would forbid us to call them complex trajectories. The crucial point is that the boundary conditions (
16
) allow for the existence of trajectories such that
u_{\mbox{\tiny A}}\neq v^{*}_{\mbox{\tiny A}}
and
u_{\mbox{\tiny B}}\neq v^{*}_{\mbox{\tiny B}}
, since
u_{\mbox{\tiny A}}
and
u_{\mbox{\tiny B}}
are never constrained to be
v^{*}_{\mbox{\tiny A}}
and
v^{*}_{\mbox{\tiny B}}
, respectively, in the same instant of time. Therefore, considering that
u_{\mbox{\tiny A}}
and
u_{\mbox{\tiny B}}
are independent of
v_{\mbox{\tiny A}}
and
v_{\mbox{\tiny B}}
, we can find
complex
trajectories satisfying Eqs. (
14
) and (
16
). They are said to be complex in the sense that, using Eq. (
5
), the usual classical phase-space (position, momentum) can only be recovered if we consider an extension of its variables into the complex space. Mathematically, it means that the critical paths of Eq. (
10
) lie in an analytically extended space. Physically, it sounds like a solution to deal with the overdetermined problem in classical phase space. Indeed, without the complex extension, it would be generally impossible to find a trajectory connecting too many boundary conditions: all phase-space coordinates are fixed at the initial and final time, as well as the evolution time. To conclude, except for the very particular case of real trajectories, where
u_{\mbox{\tiny A}}(t)=v^{*}_{\mbox{\tiny A}}(t)
and
u_{\mbox{\tiny B}}(t)=v^{*}_{\mbox{\tiny B}}(t)
, those satisfying Eqs. (
14
) and (
16
) will be complex.

As we will show soon, the dynamics around a complex trajectory are crucial to defining the behavior of the semiclassical propagator. The usual way to get quantitative information from this region is through the tangent (or stability) matrix
\mathbf{M}
. This object is based on a linear approach, connecting small initial displacements with respect to the trajectory, that is,
\delta\mathbf{u}^{\prime}\equiv(\delta u_{\mbox{\tiny A}}^{\prime},\,\delta u_{\mbox{\tiny B}}^{\prime})
and
\delta\mathbf{v}^{\prime}\equiv(\delta v_{\mbox{\tiny A}}^{\prime},\,\delta v_{\mbox{\tiny B}}^{\prime})
, to the final displacements
\delta\mathbf{u}^{\prime\prime}\equiv(\delta u_{\mbox{\tiny A}}^{\prime\prime},\,\delta u_{\mbox{\tiny B}}^{\prime\prime})
and
\delta\mathbf{v}^{\prime\prime}\equiv(\delta v_{\mbox{\tiny A}}^{\prime\prime},\,\delta v_{\mbox{\tiny B}}^{\prime\prime})
. The tangent matrix is expressed by

\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\delta\mathbf{u}^{\prime\prime}\\
\delta\mathbf{v}^{\prime\prime}\end{array}\right)=\mathbf{M}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\delta\mathbf{u}^{\prime}\\
\delta\mathbf{v}^{\prime}\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathbf{M}_{\mathrm{uu}}&\mathbf{M}_{\mathrm{uv}}\\
\mathbf{M}_{\mathrm{vu}}&\mathbf{M}_{\mathrm{vv}}\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\delta\mathbf{u}^{\prime}\\
\delta\mathbf{v}^{\prime}\end{array}\right).

(17)

Roughly speaking, we expect that unstable dynamics correspond to larger values of the
\mathbf{M}
elements, while lower values should characterize stable trajectories. Matrix
\mathbf{M}
can be easily calculated from a given trajectory, even numerically.

A last comment on this topic is opportune. In principle, all trajectories above specified should contribute to the semiclassical formula of
K_{\pm}^{\mathrm{q}}
. However, as already studied in detail, for example, in Refs.
5
;
47
;
48
;
44
;
49
;
50
, some of them produce nonphysical results and should be excluded from the calculation. We can understand their exclusion as follows. The success of the saddle point method depends on a deformation of the original integration paths of Eq. (
11
) to include the saddle points of the integrand, which give origin to the complex classical trajectories. This procedure, which is based on Cauchy’s integral theorem, is generally impossible to be rigorously applied to this problem, given the number of integration variables involved. Thus, as usual, we assume that the path deformation can be performed and assign the (
a posteriori
) nonphysical results to the critical points that are impossible to reach by a proper path deformation.

II.2.3
Semiclassical propagator formula

Once we have identified the critical points of Eq. (
11
), the last step of the saddle point method is to expand the exponent
\mathcal{S}
around them up to second order and, thus, to perform the resulting
4N
-dimensional Gaussian integral. This procedure is the longest part of the present calculation and will not be reproduced here, given that it essentially consists of the same evaluation done in Refs.
5
;
43
. At the end of the approximation process, the semiclassical formula for the coherent-state quantum propagator (
1
) is written as

K_{\pm}^{\mathrm{sc}}(z_{\mbox{\scriptsize b}}^{*},s_{\mbox{\scriptsize b}}^{*},z_{\mbox{\scriptsize k}},s_{\mbox{\scriptsize k}},T)=\sum_{\mathrm{c.t.}}\;D_{\pm}\,\exp{\frac{i}{\hbar} F_\pm},

(18)

where the sum indicates that all complex trajectories satisfying Eqs. (
14
) and (
16
) should be considered. We write the input variables of
K_{\pm}^{\mathrm{sc}}
as
(z_{\mbox{\scriptsize b}}^{*},s_{\mbox{\scriptsize b}}^{*},z_{\mbox{\scriptsize k}},s_{\mbox{\scriptsize k}},T)
to emphasize that they are the only ingredients used to determine the trajectories, as prescribed by Eq. (
16
). The variables
z_{\mbox{\scriptsize b}}
,
s_{\mbox{\scriptsize b}}
,
z_{\mbox{\scriptsize k}}^{*}
, and
s_{\mbox{\scriptsize k}}^{*}
, on the other hand, will just enter in the normalization term
\Lambda
, defined next, having less importance to understand
K_{\pm}^{\mathrm{sc}}
. We now detail the other functions appearing in Eq. (
18
). We first present its exponent

F_{\pm}\equiv S_{\pm}+G_{\pm}+i\hbar\Lambda,

(19)

where the action
S_{\pm}
is given by

\displaystyle\frac{i}{\hbar}S_{\pm}(z_{\mbox{\scriptsize b}}^{*},s_{\mbox{\scriptsize b}}^{*},z_{\mbox{\scriptsize k}},s_{\mbox{\scriptsize k}},T)

\displaystyle=\pm\,\int_{0}^{T}\left[\frac{\dot{v}_{\mbox{\tiny A}}u_{\mbox{\tiny A}}-v_{\mbox{\tiny A}}\dot{u}_{\mbox{\tiny A}}}{2}+j\frac{\dot{v}_{\mbox{\tiny B}}u_{\mbox{\tiny B}}-v_{\mbox{\tiny B}}\dot{u}_{\mbox{\tiny B}}}{1+u_{\mbox{\tiny B}}v_{\mbox{\tiny B}}}-\frac{i}{\hbar}\tilde{H}\right]\mbox{d}t

(20)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\left[u_{\mbox{\tiny A}}^{\prime}\,v_{\mbox{\tiny A}}^{\prime}+u_{\mbox{\tiny A}}^{\prime\prime}\,v_{\mbox{\tiny A}}^{\prime\prime}\right]+j\ln{\big[ \big( 1+ u_{\mbox{\tiny B}}' \, v_{\mbox{\tiny B}}' \big)
\big( 1+ u_{\mbox{\tiny B}}'' \, v_{\mbox{\tiny B}}'' \big) \big]},

the function
G_{\pm}
reads

\frac{i}{\hbar}G_{\pm}(z_{\mbox{\scriptsize b}}^{*},s_{\mbox{\scriptsize b}}^{*},z_{\mbox{\scriptsize k}},s_{\mbox{\scriptsize k}},T)=\pm\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\left[\frac{i}{\hbar}\frac{\partial^{2}\tilde{H}}{\partial u_{\mbox{\tiny A}}\partial v_{\mbox{\tiny A}}}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\partial\dot{v}_{\mbox{\tiny B}}}{\partial v_{\mbox{\tiny B}}}-\frac{\partial\dot{u}_{\mbox{\tiny B}}}{\partial u_{\mbox{\tiny B}}}\right)\right]\mbox{d}t,

(21)

and the normalization term
\Lambda
amounts to

\Lambda=\frac{1}{2}\left(|z_{\mbox{\scriptsize b}}|^{2}+|z_{\mbox{\scriptsize k}}|^{2}\right)+j\ln{\big[ \left( 1+|s_{\mbox{\scriptsize b}}|^2 \right)\left( 1+|s_{\mbox{\scriptsize k}}|^2 \right) \big]}.

(22)

Now, we introduce the prefactor term

D_{\pm}(z_{\mbox{\scriptsize b}}^{*},s_{\mbox{\scriptsize b}}^{*},z_{\mbox{\scriptsize k}},s_{\mbox{\scriptsize k}},T)\equiv\left\{\frac{\big(1+u_{\mbox{\tiny B}}^{\prime}\,v_{\mbox{\tiny B}}^{\prime}\big)\big(1+u_{\mbox{\tiny B}}^{\prime\prime}\,v_{\mbox{\tiny B}}^{\prime\prime}\big)}{2j}\det\left[\frac{i}{\hbar}\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}S_{\pm}}{\partial z_{\mbox{\scriptsize k}}\partial z_{\mbox{\scriptsize b}}^{*}}&&\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}S_{\pm}}{\partial z_{\mbox{\scriptsize k}}\partial s_{\mbox{\scriptsize b}}^{*}}\\[8.0pt]
\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}S_{\pm}}{\partial s_{\mbox{\scriptsize k}}\partial z_{\mbox{\scriptsize b}}^{*}}&&\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}S_{\pm}}{\partial s_{\mbox{\scriptsize k}}\partial s_{\mbox{\scriptsize b}}^{*}}\end{array}\right)\right]\right\}^{1/2}.

(23)

This expression completes our brief presentation of the semiclassical forward and backward propagators (
18
) in the basis
\{|z,s\rangle\}
.

In principle, except for the second derivatives of the action seen in
D_{\pm}
, all functions involved in
K_{\pm}^{\mathrm{sc}}
can be numerically handled. However, we can also show a way to find those derivatives in a numerical scenario. For such, we first note that the initial and final coordinates that are unconstrained by the boundary conditions (
16
), that is,

\begin{array}[]{lllll}v_{\mbox{\tiny A}}^{\prime}\equiv v_{\mbox{\tiny A}}(0),&u_{\mbox{\tiny A}}^{\prime\prime}\equiv u_{\mbox{\tiny A}}(T),&v_{\mbox{\tiny B}}^{\prime}\equiv v_{\mbox{\tiny B}}(0),&\mathrm{and}\;u_{\mbox{\tiny B}}^{\prime\prime}\equiv u_{\mbox{\tiny B}}(T),&\mathrm{for}\;\,K_{+}^{\mathrm{sc}},\\
v_{\mbox{\tiny A}}^{\prime\prime}\equiv v_{\mbox{\tiny A}}(T),&u_{\mbox{\tiny A}}^{\prime}\equiv u_{\mbox{\tiny A}}(0),&v_{\mbox{\tiny B}}^{\prime\prime}\equiv v_{\mbox{\tiny B}}(T),&\mathrm{and}\;u_{\mbox{\tiny B}}^{\prime}\equiv u_{\mbox{\tiny B}}(0),&\mathrm{for}\;\,K_{-}^{\mathrm{sc}},\end{array}

can be obtained from the first derivatives of
S_{\pm}
. Indeed, from Eqs (
16
) and (
20
), which implicitly defines
S_{+}(v_{\mbox{\tiny A}}^{\prime\prime},v_{\mbox{\tiny B}}^{\prime\prime},u_{\mbox{\tiny A}}^{\prime},u_{\mbox{\tiny B}}^{\prime},T)
and
S_{-}(v_{\mbox{\tiny A}}^{\prime},v_{\mbox{\tiny B}}^{\prime},u_{\mbox{\tiny A}}^{\prime\prime},u_{\mbox{\tiny B}}^{\prime\prime},T)
, we can directly find

\begin{array}[]{llll}\displaystyle u_{\mbox{\tiny A}}^{\prime\prime}=\frac{i}{\hbar}\frac{\partial S_{+}}{\partial v_{\mbox{\tiny A}}^{\prime\prime}},&\quad\displaystyle\frac{u_{\mbox{\tiny B}}^{\prime\prime}}{1+u_{\mbox{\tiny B}}^{\prime\prime}v_{\mbox{\tiny B}}^{\prime\prime}}=\frac{i}{2j\hbar}\frac{\partial S_{+}}{\partial v_{\mbox{\tiny B}}^{\prime\prime}},&\quad\displaystyle v_{\mbox{\tiny A}}^{\prime}=\frac{i}{\hbar}\frac{\partial S_{+}}{\partial u_{\mbox{\tiny A}}^{\prime}},&\quad\displaystyle\frac{v_{\mbox{\tiny B}}^{\prime}}{1+u_{\mbox{\tiny B}}^{\prime}v_{\mbox{\tiny B}}^{\prime}}=\frac{i}{2j\hbar}\frac{\partial S_{+}}{\partial u_{\mbox{\tiny B}}^{\prime}};\\[10.0pt]
\displaystyle u_{\mbox{\tiny A}}^{\prime}=\frac{i}{\hbar}\frac{\partial S_{-}}{\partial v_{\mbox{\tiny A}}^{\prime}},&\quad\displaystyle\frac{u_{\mbox{\tiny B}}^{\prime}}{1+u_{\mbox{\tiny B}}^{\prime}v_{\mbox{\tiny B}}^{\prime}}=\frac{i}{2j\hbar}\frac{\partial S_{-}}{\partial v_{\mbox{\tiny B}}^{\prime}},&\quad\displaystyle v_{\mbox{\tiny A}}^{\prime\prime}=\frac{i}{\hbar}\frac{\partial S_{-}}{\partial u_{\mbox{\tiny A}}^{\prime\prime}},&\quad\displaystyle\frac{v_{\mbox{\tiny B}}^{\prime\prime}}{1+u_{\mbox{\tiny B}}^{\prime\prime}v_{\mbox{\tiny B}}^{\prime\prime}}=\frac{i}{2j\hbar}\frac{\partial S_{-}}{\partial u_{\mbox{\tiny B}}^{\prime\prime}}.\end{array}

(24)

Then, by differentiating these equations and rearranging the terms, we can compare the resulting expressions with the definition of the tangent matrix (
17
). This procedure is performed in App.
A
and, according to Eqs. (
73
) and (
76
), the prefactor becomes

D_{+}\equiv\left\{\frac{\big(1+u_{\mbox{\tiny B}}^{\prime\prime}\,v_{\mbox{\tiny B}}^{\prime\prime}\big)}{\big(1+u_{\mbox{\tiny B}}^{\prime}\,v_{\mbox{\tiny B}}^{\prime}\big)}\frac{1}{\det\mathbf{M}_{\mathrm{vv}}}\right\}^{1/2}\quad\mathrm{and}\quad D_{-}\equiv\left\{\frac{\big(1+u_{\mbox{\tiny B}}^{\prime\prime}\,v_{\mbox{\tiny B}}^{\prime\prime}\big)}{\big(1+u_{\mbox{\tiny B}}^{\prime}\,v_{\mbox{\tiny B}}^{\prime}\big)}\frac{1}{\det\mathbf{M}_{\mathrm{uu}}}\right\}^{1/2},

(25)

which represents an advantage for numerical applications.

Expression (
18
) and the saddle point method discussed above will be the crucial elements to get the semiclassical entanglement formula that we will derive in the next section.

III
Semiclassical entanglement

Entanglement in a bipartite pure state
\hat{\rho}
, acting on the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{z}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{s}
, can be evaluated by the linear entropy
S^{\mathrm{q}}_{\mathrm{lin}}(\hat{\rho}_{s})\equiv 1-P^{\mathrm{q}}(\hat{\rho}_{s})
of the reduced state
\hat{\rho}_{s}\equiv\mathrm{Tr}_{z}\,\hat{\rho}
, where
P^{\mathrm{q}}(\hat{\rho}_{s})\equiv\mathrm{Tr}_{s}\left\{\hat{\rho}_{s}^{2}\right\}
is the purity of
\hat{\rho}_{s}
. The linear entanglement entropy of
\hat{\rho}
, defined as

E^{\mathrm{q}}(\hat{\rho})\equiv S^{\mathrm{q}}_{\mathrm{lin}}(\hat{\rho}_{s}),

(26)

gives the same result if we replace
\hat{\rho}_{s}
with
\hat{\rho}_{z}\equiv\mathrm{Tr}_{s}\,\hat{\rho}
. The function
E^{\mathrm{q}}(\hat{\rho})
is an entanglement measure because, for a separable state
\hat{\rho}
, we have
E^{\mathrm{q}}(\hat{\rho})=0
, while an entangled state yields
0<E^{\mathrm{q}}(\hat{\rho})<1
.

We aim to study the time evolution of the entanglement when the initial state is pure and separable, explicitly given by a product of the canonical coherent state and a spin coherent state,

\hat{\rho}_{0}=|z_{0},s_{0}\rangle\langle z_{0},s_{0}|.

(27)

Then, we evolve
\hat{\rho}_{0}
until the time
T
, according to a general Hamiltonian
\hat{H}
, and calculate the time-dependent purity, now rewritten as

\displaystyle P_{T}^{\mathrm{q}}

\displaystyle=\int K^{\mathrm{q}}_{+}(z_{1},s_{1},z_{0},s_{0},T)\,K^{\mathrm{q}}_{-}(z_{0},s_{0},z_{1},s_{2},T)

(28)

\displaystyle\times\;K^{\mathrm{q}}_{+}(z_{2},s_{2},z_{0},s_{0},T)\,K^{\mathrm{q}}_{-}(z_{0},s_{0},z_{2},s_{1},T)~\mbox{d}\nu(z_{1},s_{1})\,\mbox{d}\nu(z_{2},s_{2}),

where

d\nu(z_{1},s_{1})\equiv\frac{2j+1}{\pi^{2}}\frac{\mbox{d}z_{1}^{\mbox{\tiny R}}\,\mbox{d}z_{1}^{\mbox{\tiny I}}\,\mbox{d}s_{1}^{\mbox{\tiny R}}\,\mbox{d}s_{1}^{\mbox{\tiny I}}}{(1+|s_{1}|^{2})^{2}}

(29)

and the analog to
\mbox{d}\nu(z_{2},s_{2})
. Notice that, once we have deduced a semiclassical version
P_{T}^{\mathrm{sc}}
for Eq. (
28
), we will naturally have an expression for the time-dependent semiclassical entanglement

E_{T}^{\mathrm{sc}}(\hat{\rho}_{0})\equiv 1-P^{\mathrm{sc}}_{T},

(30)

which is the main goal of the present paper.

To start the approximation, the first idea is to replace the exact propagators (
1
) with their semiclassical counterparts (
18
) in Eq. (
28
), in close analogy to Refs.
45
;
46
;
30
;
56
. We substitute

\displaystyle K^{\mathrm{q}}_{+}(z_{1},s_{1},z_{0},s_{0},T)

\displaystyle\rightarrow K^{\mathrm{sc}}_{1}(z_{1}^{*},s_{1}^{*},z_{0},s_{0},T),

(31)

\displaystyle K^{\mathrm{q}}_{-}(z_{0},s_{0},z_{1},s_{2},T)

\displaystyle\rightarrow K^{\mathrm{sc}}_{2}(z_{0}^{*},s_{0}^{*},z_{1},s_{2},T),

\displaystyle K^{\mathrm{q}}_{+}(z_{2},s_{2},z_{0},s_{0},T)

\displaystyle\rightarrow K^{\mathrm{sc}}_{3}(z_{2}^{*},s_{2}^{*},z_{0},s_{0},T),

\displaystyle K^{\mathrm{q}}_{-}(z_{0},s_{0},z_{2},s_{1},T)

\displaystyle\rightarrow K^{\mathrm{sc}}_{4}(z_{0}^{*},s_{0}^{*},z_{2},s_{1},T),

where we emphasize that
K^{\mathrm{sc}}_{1}
and
K^{\mathrm{sc}}_{3}
are forward propagators, while
K^{\mathrm{sc}}_{2}
and
K^{\mathrm{sc}}_{4}
are backward ones. Next, we will interpret integral (
28
) over the four complex planes,
z_{1}
,
z_{2}
,
s_{1}
, and
s_{2}
as line integrals. This process is equivalent to that used to get Eq. (
11
). Then, we rename the integration variables as

\begin{array}[]{llll}z_{1}\rightarrow u^{\prime\prime}_{2\mbox{\tiny A}},&\quad z_{1}^{*}\rightarrow v^{\prime\prime}_{1\mbox{\tiny A}},&\quad s_{1}\rightarrow u^{\prime\prime}_{4\mbox{\tiny B}},&\quad s_{1}^{*}\rightarrow v^{\prime\prime}_{1\mbox{\tiny B}},\\
z_{2}\rightarrow u^{\prime\prime}_{4\mbox{\tiny A}},&\quad z_{2}^{*}\rightarrow v^{\prime\prime}_{3\mbox{\tiny A}},&\quad s_{2}\rightarrow u^{\prime\prime}_{2\mbox{\tiny B}},&\quad s_{2}^{*}\rightarrow v^{\prime\prime}_{3\mbox{\tiny B}},\end{array}

(32)

in accordance with their role in the semiclassical propagators presented in Eq. (
31
). For example, the variable
z_{1}
appears in
K^{\mathrm{sc}}_{2}
and should match the variable
u_{2\mbox{\tiny A}}
at the final time
T
, suggesting the replacement
z_{1}\rightarrow u^{\prime\prime}_{2\mbox{\tiny A}}
. Analogously,
z_{1}^{*}
appears in
K^{\mathrm{sc}}_{1}
and should be final value of variable
v_{1\mbox{\tiny A}}
, suggesting the replacement
z_{1}^{*}\rightarrow v^{\prime\prime}_{1\mbox{\tiny A}}
. This reasoning implicitly assumes that the trajectory
\big[u_{l\mbox{\tiny A}}(t),u_{l\mbox{\tiny B}}(t),v_{l\mbox{\tiny A}}(t),v_{l\mbox{\tiny B}}(t)\big]
is the one contributing to propagator
K_{l}^{\mathrm{sc}}
, for
l=1,\ldots,4
, with some of their final points linked to the integration variables according to the boundary conditions (
16
). Considering this new notation, we can approach Eq. (
28
) to

P^{\mathrm{sc}}_{T}=\left(\frac{2j+1}{4\pi^{2}}\right)^{2}\int D_{1}\,D_{2}\,D_{3}\,D_{4}~\mbox{e}^{\Phi}~\frac{\mbox{d}u^{\prime\prime}_{2\mbox{\tiny A}}\,\mbox{d}v^{\prime\prime}_{1\mbox{\tiny A}}\,\mbox{d}u^{\prime\prime}_{4\mbox{\tiny B}}\,\mbox{d}v^{\prime\prime}_{1\mbox{\tiny B}}}{(1+u^{\prime\prime}_{4\mbox{\tiny B}}v^{\prime\prime}_{1\mbox{\tiny B}})^{2}}\frac{\mbox{d}u^{\prime\prime}_{4\mbox{\tiny A}}\,\mbox{d}v^{\prime\prime}_{3\mbox{\tiny A}}\,\mbox{d}u^{\prime\prime}_{2\mbox{\tiny B}}\,\mbox{d}v^{\prime\prime}_{3\mbox{\tiny B}}}{(1+u^{\prime\prime}_{2\mbox{\tiny B}}v^{\prime\prime}_{3\mbox{\tiny B}})^{2}},

(33)

with
\Phi\equiv-\Lambda_{\mathrm{tot}}+\sum_{k=1}^{4}\left[\frac{i}{\hbar}\left(S_{k}+G_{k}\right)\right]
. Notice that

\begin{array}[]{ll}S_{1}\equiv S_{+}(v^{\prime\prime}_{1\mbox{\tiny A}},v^{\prime\prime}_{1\mbox{\tiny B}},z_{0},s_{0},T),&\quad S_{2}\equiv S_{-}(z_{0}^{*},s_{0}^{*},u^{\prime\prime}_{2\mbox{\tiny A}},u^{\prime\prime}_{2\mbox{\tiny B}},T),\\
S_{3}\equiv S_{+}(v^{\prime\prime}_{3\mbox{\tiny A}},v^{\prime\prime}_{3\mbox{\tiny B}},z_{0},s_{0},T),&\quad S_{4}\equiv S_{-}(z_{0}^{*},s_{0}^{*},u^{\prime\prime}_{4\mbox{\tiny A}},u^{\prime\prime}_{4\mbox{\tiny B}},T),\end{array}

(34)

with equivalent definitions for
G_{1},\ldots,G_{4}
and
D_{1},\ldots,D_{4}
. In addition,

\Lambda_{\mathrm{tot}}\equiv 2|z_{0}|^{2}+4j\ln(1+|s_0|^2)+u^{\prime\prime}_{2\mbox{\tiny A}}v^{\prime\prime}_{1\mbox{\tiny A}}+u^{\prime\prime}_{4\mbox{\tiny A}}v^{\prime\prime}_{3\mbox{\tiny A}}+2j\ln[(1+u^{\prime\prime}_{4\mbox{\tiny B}}v^{\prime\prime}_{1\mbox{\tiny B}})(1+u^{\prime\prime}_{2\mbox{\tiny B}}v^{\prime\prime}_{3\mbox{\tiny B}})\big].

(35)

For simplicity, we omit the sum over contributing trajectories in Eq. (
33
), but we will return to this point opportunely.

III.1
Entangled-boundary-condition trajectories

Equation (
33
) consists of a line integral in an 8-dimensional complex space, spanned by the vector
{\mathbf{r}}=(u^{\prime\prime}_{2\mbox{\tiny A}},v^{\prime\prime}_{1\mbox{\tiny A}},u^{\prime\prime}_{4\mbox{\tiny B}},v^{\prime\prime}_{1\mbox{\tiny B}},u^{\prime\prime}_{4\mbox{\tiny A}},v^{\prime\prime}_{3\mbox{\tiny A}},u^{\prime\prime}_{2\mbox{\tiny B}},v^{\prime\prime}_{3\mbox{\tiny B}})
. The integral provides a straightforward recipe for calculating
P_{T}^{\mathrm{sc}}
. First, by specifying the value of
\mathbf{r}
, we have enough information to find the complex trajectories contributing to each propagator. For example, according to Eq. (
16
), in the case of
K_{1}^{\mathrm{sc}}
, the initial boudary condition is set as
u^{\prime}_{1\mbox{\tiny A}}=z_{0}
and
u^{\prime}_{1\mbox{\tiny B}}=s_{0}
. By knowing the integration variables
v^{\prime\prime}_{1\mbox{\tiny A}}
and
v^{\prime\prime}_{1\mbox{\tiny B}}
, the final constraints are also determined so that we can apply Eqs. (
14
) and (
16
) to obtain the trajectory. This reasoning can be extended to the other propagators, showing that, given
\mathbf{r}
, the integrand can be entirely calculated. Then, what we would still need to do is to span all prescribed values of
\mathbf{r}
, summing their contributions. This task is formally well-defined, but its implementation is, in general, practically impossible. Fortunately, similar to Eq. (
11
), there are critical values of
\mathbf{r}
, corresponding to particular sets of four trajectories and their respective propagators, which enable a good approximation for
P_{T}^{\mathrm{sc}}
.

We then use the saddle point method
8
to solve Eq. (
33
). The critical points
\tilde{\mathbf{r}}
are the solutions of the eight equations

\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial u^{\prime\prime}_{2\mbox{\tiny A}}}=\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial v^{\prime\prime}_{1\mbox{\tiny A}}}=\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial u^{\prime\prime}_{4\mbox{\tiny B}}}=\ldots=\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial v^{\prime\prime}_{3\mbox{\tiny B}}}=0.

(36)

By performing direct calculations, with the support of Eq. (
24
), we find the equalities

\begin{array}[]{llll}\tilde{u}^{\prime\prime}_{2\mbox{\tiny A}}=\tilde{u}^{\prime\prime}_{1\mbox{\tiny A}},&\quad\tilde{v}^{\prime\prime}_{1\mbox{\tiny A}}=\tilde{v}^{\prime\prime}_{2\mbox{\tiny A}},&\quad\tilde{u}^{\prime\prime}_{4\mbox{\tiny B}}=\tilde{u}^{\prime\prime}_{1\mbox{\tiny B}},&\quad\tilde{v}^{\prime\prime}_{1\mbox{\tiny B}}=\tilde{v}^{\prime\prime}_{4\mbox{\tiny B}},\\
\tilde{u}^{\prime\prime}_{4\mbox{\tiny A}}=\tilde{u}^{\prime\prime}_{3\mbox{\tiny A}},&\quad\tilde{v}^{\prime\prime}_{3\mbox{\tiny A}}=\tilde{v}^{\prime\prime}_{4\mbox{\tiny A}},&\quad\tilde{u}^{\prime\prime}_{2\mbox{\tiny B}}=\tilde{u}^{\prime\prime}_{3\mbox{\tiny B}},&\quad\tilde{v}^{\prime\prime}_{3\mbox{\tiny B}}=\tilde{v}^{\prime\prime}_{2\mbox{\tiny B}}.\end{array}

(37)

To get them, it is important to inform that the derivatives of functions
G_{k}
are disregarded, as usually done in this kind of semiclassical approximation
5
. Remarkably, the above conditions imply that the four
critical
trajectories needed to calculate (
33
) must have their final points mutually connected. Following the previous works
30
;
56
, we call Eq. (
37
) as
entangled boundary conditions
. To understand their meaning, take, for example, the first equation
\tilde{u}^{\prime\prime}_{2\mbox{\tiny A}}=\tilde{u}^{\prime\prime}_{1\mbox{\tiny A}}
, showing that the integration variable
\tilde{u}^{\prime\prime}_{2\mbox{\tiny A}}
, which is the final point of the coordinate
\tilde{u}_{2\mbox{\tiny A}}
of trajectory 2, should be adjusted to coincide with the last point
\tilde{u}^{\prime\prime}_{1\mbox{\tiny A}}
of trajectory 1. On the other hand, in the third equation of (
37
), we see that the final coordinate
\tilde{u}^{\prime\prime}_{1\mbox{\tiny B}}
of the same trajectory 1 should equal the integration variable
\tilde{u}^{\prime\prime}_{4\mbox{\tiny B}}
, which is a final coordinate of trajectory 4. Following this pattern, the other equalities also imply these puzzling constraints.

At first sight, we could think that no set of four trajectories could satisfy these so restrictive conditions. However, if the four trajectories were the same
real
trajectory starting at the center of the state
|z_{0},s_{0}\rangle
, all necessary conditions would be satisfied. Indeed, if we set
z_{0}=\tilde{u}^{\prime}_{l\mbox{\tiny A}}=\big[\tilde{v}^{\prime}_{l\mbox{\tiny A}}\big]^{*}
and
s_{0}=\tilde{u}^{\prime}_{l\mbox{\tiny B}}=\big[\tilde{v}^{\prime}_{l\mbox{\tiny B}}\big]^{*}
, for
l=1,\ldots,4
, so that the initial constraints,

u^{\prime}_{1\mbox{\tiny A}}=u^{\prime}_{3\mbox{\tiny A}}=z_{0},\quad u^{\prime}_{1\mbox{\tiny B}}=u^{\prime}_{3\mbox{\tiny B}}=s_{0},\quad v^{\prime}_{2\mbox{\tiny A}}=v^{\prime}_{4\mbox{\tiny A}}=z_{0}^{*},\quad v^{\prime}_{2\mbox{\tiny B}}=v^{\prime}_{4\mbox{\tiny B}}=s_{0}^{*},

(38)

are respected, then the final boundary conditions (
37
) will be automatically satisfied, as all final points coincide. Surprisingly, besides the real trajectory, we will show later, when studying a particular system, that other sets of four complex contributing trajectories can also exist.

III.2
Semiclassical entanglement entropy

Having discussed the features of the set of contributing trajectories, we return to Eq. (
33
) and expand the integrand up to second order around
\tilde{\mathbf{r}}
. We get

P^{\mathrm{sc}}_{T}=\left\{\prod_{l=1}^{4}\frac{\tilde{D}_{l}~\mbox{e}^{\frac{i}{\hbar}\left(\tilde{S}_{l}+\tilde{G}_{l}\right)-\frac{1}{2}\left(|z_{0}|^{2}+\tilde{u}^{\prime\prime}_{l\mbox{\tiny A}}\tilde{v}^{\prime\prime}_{l\mbox{\tiny A}}\right)}}{(1+|s_{0}|^{2})^{j}(1+\tilde{u}^{\prime\prime}_{l\mbox{\tiny B}}\tilde{v}^{\prime\prime}_{l\mbox{\tiny B}})^{j}}\right\}\,\tilde{\mathcal{I}},

(39)

where a tilde was inserted above the functions to indicate that they refer to the set of critical trajectories. The term
\tilde{\mathcal{I}}
amounts to the Gaussian integral

\tilde{\mathcal{I}}\equiv\left(\frac{2j+1}{4\pi^{2}}\right)^{2}\int\frac{\mbox{e}^{\frac{1}{2}(\mathbf{r}-\tilde{\mathbf{r}})~\tilde{\mathbf{Q}}~(\mathbf{r}-\tilde{\mathbf{r}})}}{\prod_{l=1}^{4}(1+\tilde{u}^{\prime\prime}_{l\mbox{\tiny B}}\tilde{v}^{\prime\prime}_{l\mbox{\tiny B}})}\mbox{d}^{8}\mathbf{r}=\left[\big(\det\tilde{\mathbf{L}}\big)\big(\det\tilde{\mathbf{Q}}\big)\right]^{-1/2},

(40)

where we have defined

\tilde{\mathbf{L}}\equiv\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}\tilde{\mathbf{L}}_{1}^{\prime\prime}&0&0&0\\
0&\tilde{\mathbf{L}}_{2}^{\prime\prime}&0&0\\
0&0&\tilde{\mathbf{L}}_{3}^{\prime\prime}&0\\
0&0&0&\tilde{\mathbf{L}}_{4}^{\prime\prime}\end{array}\right),\quad\mathrm{with}\quad\tilde{\mathbf{L}}_{l}\equiv\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
0&\frac{(1+u_{l\mbox{\tiny B}}v_{l\mbox{\tiny B}})^{2}}{2j+1}\end{array}\right).

(41)

In addition, the matrix
\tilde{\mathbf{Q}}
involves second derivatives of
\Phi
. After a convenient change in the order of its columns and rows, we have

\det\tilde{\mathbf{Q}}\equiv\det\frac{i}{\hbar}\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}\tilde{\mathbf{S}}^{{1}}_{\mathrm{vv}}+\tilde{u}^{\prime\prime}_{1\mbox{\tiny B}}\!\!{}^{2}\tilde{\mathbf{A}}_{1}^{\prime\prime}&i\hbar\,\mathbf{I}_{\text{\tiny A}}&\mathbf{0}&-\tilde{\mathbf{A}}_{4}^{\prime\prime}\\
i\hbar\,\mathbf{I}_{\text{\tiny A}}&\tilde{\mathbf{S}}^{{2}}_{\mathrm{uu}}+\tilde{v}^{\prime\prime}_{2\mbox{\tiny B}}\!\!{}^{2}\tilde{\mathbf{A}}_{2}^{\prime\prime}&-\tilde{\mathbf{A}}_{3}^{\prime\prime}&\mathbf{0}\\
\mathbf{0}&-\tilde{\mathbf{A}}_{2}^{\prime\prime}&\tilde{\mathbf{S}}^{3}_{\mathrm{vv}}+\tilde{u}^{\prime\prime}_{3\mbox{\tiny B}}\!\!{}^{2}\tilde{\mathbf{A}}_{3}^{\prime\prime}&i\hbar\,\mathbf{I}_{\text{\tiny A}}\\
-\tilde{\mathbf{A}}_{1}^{\prime\prime}&\mathbf{0}&i\hbar\,\mathbf{I}_{\text{\tiny A}}&\tilde{\mathbf{S}}^{4}_{\mathrm{uu}}+\tilde{v}^{\prime\prime}_{4\mbox{\tiny B}}\!\!{}^{2}\tilde{\mathbf{A}}_{4}^{\prime\prime}\\
\end{array}\right),

(42)

where

\mathbf{I}_{\mbox{\tiny A}}\equiv\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
0&0\end{array}\right),\quad\tilde{\mathbf{A}}_{l}\equiv\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&0\\
0&\frac{-2ij\hbar}{(1+\tilde{v}_{l\mbox{\tiny B}}\tilde{u}_{l\mbox{\tiny B}})^{2}}\end{array}\right),\quad\mathrm{and}\quad\tilde{\mathbf{S}}^{1}_{\mathrm{vv}}\equiv\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\frac{\partial^{2}\tilde{S}_{1}}{\partial v^{\prime\prime}_{1\mbox{\tiny A}}\partial v^{\prime\prime}_{1\mbox{\tiny A}}}&\frac{\partial^{2}\tilde{S}_{1}}{\partial v^{\prime\prime}_{1\mbox{\tiny A}}\partial v^{\prime\prime}_{1\mbox{\tiny B}}}\\
\frac{\partial^{2}\tilde{S}_{1}}{\partial v^{\prime\prime}_{1\mbox{\tiny B}}\partial v^{\prime\prime}_{1\mbox{\tiny A}}}&\frac{\partial^{2}\tilde{S}_{1}}{\partial v^{\prime\prime}_{1\mbox{\tiny B}}\partial v^{\prime\prime}_{1\mbox{\tiny B}}}\end{array}\right).

(43)

Matrices
\mathbf{S}^{2}_{\mathrm{uu}}
,
\mathbf{S}^{3}_{\mathrm{vv}}
, and
\mathbf{S}^{4}_{\mathrm{uu}}
are defined in analogy to
\mathbf{S}^{1}_{\mathrm{vv}}
. Now, we will insert Eq. (
40
) in (
39
) and use Eq. (
25
) to express the terms
D_{l}
. Then, we replace the second derivatives of the action with the elements of the tangent matrix according to Eqs. (
73
) and (
76
) and approach
2j\approx 2j+1
.

Finally, considering all these steps, the semiclassical entanglement entropy (
30
) becomes

E^{\mathrm{sc}}_{T}(\hat{\rho}_{0})=1-\sum_{\mathrm{sets}}\mathcal{A}\,\mbox{e}^{\frac{i}{\hbar}\left(F_{1}-F_{2}+F_{3}-F4\right)},

(44)

where

\frac{i}{\hbar}F_{l}\equiv\int_{0}^{T}\left[\frac{1}{2}\left(\alpha_{l\mbox{\tiny A}}+\frac{2j\alpha_{l\mbox{\tiny B}}}{1+u_{l\mbox{\tiny B}}v_{l\mbox{\tiny B}}}\right)+\frac{1}{4}(\beta_{l\mbox{\tiny A}}+\beta_{l\mbox{\tiny B}})-\frac{i}{\hbar}\tilde{H}\right]\mbox{d}t,

with

\alpha_{l\mbox{\tiny A}}\equiv\dot{v}_{l\mbox{\tiny A}}u_{l\mbox{\tiny A}}-v_{l\mbox{\tiny A}}\dot{u}_{l\mbox{\tiny A}}\quad\mathrm{and}\quad\beta_{l\mbox{\tiny A}}\equiv\frac{\partial\dot{v}_{l\mbox{\tiny A}}}{\partial v_{l\mbox{\tiny A}}}-\frac{\partial\dot{u}_{l\mbox{\tiny A}}}{\partial u_{l\mbox{\tiny A}}},

and the equivalent for part
B
. In addition,

\mathcal{A}\equiv\sqrt{\frac{1}{\det\mathbf{R}}}~\prod_{l=1}^{4}\left[\sqrt{\frac{(1+{u}^{\prime\prime}_{l\mbox{\tiny B}}{v}^{\prime\prime}_{l\mbox{\tiny B}})}{(1+{u}^{\prime}_{l\mbox{\tiny B}}{v}^{\prime}_{l\mbox{\tiny B}})}}~\left(\frac{1+{u}^{\prime}_{l\mbox{\tiny B}}{v}^{\prime}_{l\mbox{\tiny B}}}{1+|s_{0}|^{2}}\right)^{j}\mbox{e}^{-\frac{1}{2}\left(|z_{0}|^{2}-{u}^{\prime}_{l\mbox{\tiny A}}{v}^{\prime}_{l\mbox{\tiny A}}\right)}\right],

where matrix
\mathbf{R}
is defined as

\mathbf{R}\equiv\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}\mathbf{M}_{\mathrm{uv}}^{1}&-\mathbf{I}_{\mbox{\tiny A}}\mathbf{M}_{\mathrm{uu}}^{2}&0&-\mathbf{I}_{\mbox{\tiny B}}\mathbf{M}_{\mathrm{uu}}^{4}\\
-\mathbf{I}_{\mbox{\tiny A}}\mathbf{M}_{\mathrm{vv}}^{1}&\mathbf{M}_{\mathrm{vu}}^{2}&-\mathbf{I}_{\mbox{\tiny B}}\mathbf{M}_{\mathrm{vv}}^{3}&0\\
0&-\mathbf{I}_{\mbox{\tiny B}}\mathbf{M}_{\mathrm{uu}}^{2}&\mathbf{M}_{\mathrm{uv}}^{3}&-\mathbf{I}_{\mbox{\tiny A}}\mathbf{M}_{\mathrm{uu}}^{4}\\
-\mathbf{I}_{\mbox{\tiny B}}\mathbf{M}_{\mathrm{vv}}^{1}&0&-\mathbf{I}_{\mbox{\tiny A}}\mathbf{M}_{\mathrm{vv}}^{3}&\mathbf{M}_{\mathrm{vu}}^{4}\end{array}\right),\qquad\mathrm{with}~~\mathbf{I}_{\mbox{\tiny B}}\equiv\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&0\\
0&1\end{array}\right).

(45)

Equation (
44
) is our final expression for the semiclassical entanglement entropy. It should be clear that it depends on sets of four complex trajectories governed by Eq. (
14
), satisfying the initial constraints given by (
38
) and the final entangled boundary conditions (
37
). To indicate that more than one trajectory set may contribute, we included a summation in expression (
44
). This inclusion naturally corrects the deliberate exclusion of the summation in Eq. (
33
). Also, to simplify the notation, we remove the tilde from the functions involved in
E^{\mathrm{sc}}_{T}(\hat{\rho}_{0})
. At last, notice that, if we only take into account the set of four identical real trajectories, then Eq. (
44
) will reduce to
(1+{u}^{\prime\prime}_{\mbox{\tiny B}r}{v}^{\prime\prime}_{\mbox{\tiny B}r})^{2}/(1+|s_{0}|^{2})^{2}/\sqrt{\det\mathbf{R}_{r}}
, where we include the index
r
to indicate
real trajectory
. It shows that, without complex trajectories, the entanglement dynamics will be essentially governed by the tangent matrix of the central classical trajectory and will somehow manifest how unstable/stable this trajectory is. As shown in the example below, including complex trajectories implies that richer entanglement behaviors can be semiclassically described.

IV
Example

To help us understand how to get the set of complex trajectories and calculate
E_{T}^{\mathrm{sc}}
, we will consider a simple interacting system consisting of a Hamilton operator given by

\hat{H}_{\mbox{\footnotesize ex}}=(\hbar/j)\lambda\,\hat{a}^{\dagger}\hat{a}\,\hat{J}_{\mathrm{z}}.

(46)

We then calculate the classical Hamiltonian by performing the average prescribed by Eq. (
15
),

\tilde{H}_{\mbox{\footnotesize ex}}(v_{\mbox{\tiny A}},v_{\mbox{\tiny B}},u_{\mbox{\tiny A}},u_{\mbox{\tiny B}})=-\hbar\lambda\,v_{\mbox{\tiny A}}u_{\mbox{\tiny A}}\left(\frac{1-v_{\mbox{\tiny B}}u_{\mbox{\tiny B}}}{1+v_{\mbox{\tiny B}}u_{\mbox{\tiny B}}}\right).

(47)

Next step is to solve Eq. (
14
), which, in this example, becomes

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\dot{u}_{\mbox{\tiny A}}=+i\lambda\left(\frac{1-v_{\mbox{\tiny B}}u_{\mbox{\tiny B}}}{1+v_{\mbox{\tiny B}}u_{\mbox{\tiny B}}}\right)u_{\mbox{\tiny A}},&\quad\displaystyle\dot{u}_{\mbox{\tiny B}}=-i(\lambda/j)v_{\mbox{\tiny A}}u_{\mbox{\tiny A}}u_{\mbox{\tiny B}},\\[8.0pt]
\displaystyle\dot{v}_{\mbox{\tiny A}}=-i\lambda\left(\frac{1-v_{\mbox{\tiny B}}u_{\mbox{\tiny B}}}{1+v_{\mbox{\tiny B}}u_{\mbox{\tiny B}}}\right)v_{\mbox{\tiny A}},&\quad\displaystyle\dot{v}_{\mbox{\tiny B}}=+i(\lambda/j)v_{\mbox{\tiny A}}u_{\mbox{\tiny A}}v_{\mbox{\tiny B}}.\end{array}

(48)

Using these equations, we can easily take the time derivative of the products
v_{\mbox{\tiny A}}u_{\mbox{\tiny A}}
and
v_{\mbox{\tiny B}}u_{\mbox{\tiny B}}
to check that they are time-independent. From this result, we easily conclude that the complex trajectories satisfying Eq. (
48
) can be expressed by

u_{\mbox{\tiny A}}(t)\propto\mbox{e}^{+i\omega_{\mbox{\tiny B}}t},\quad v_{\mbox{\tiny A}}(t)\propto\mbox{e}^{-i\omega_{\mbox{\tiny B}}t},\quad u_{\mbox{\tiny B}}(t)\propto\mbox{e}^{-i\omega_{\mbox{\tiny A}}t},\quad v_{\mbox{\tiny B}}(t)\propto\mbox{e}^{+i\omega_{\mbox{\tiny A}}t},

(49)

where
\omega_{\mbox{\tiny A}}\equiv(\lambda/j)v_{\mbox{\tiny A}}u_{\mbox{\tiny A}}
and
\omega_{\mbox{\tiny B}}\equiv\lambda\left(1-v_{\mbox{\tiny B}}u_{\mbox{\tiny B}}\right)/\left(1+v_{\mbox{\tiny B}}u_{\mbox{\tiny B}}\right)
. Writing the trajectories in terms of the initial coordinates is an easy task:

u_{\mbox{\tiny A}}(t)=u_{\mbox{\tiny A}}^{\prime}\mbox{e}^{+i\omega_{\mbox{\tiny B}}^{\prime}t},\quad v_{\mbox{\tiny A}}(t)=v_{\mbox{\tiny A}}^{\prime}\mbox{e}^{-i\omega_{\mbox{\tiny B}}^{\prime}t},\quad u_{\mbox{\tiny B}}(t)=u_{\mbox{\tiny B}}^{\prime}\mbox{e}^{-i\omega_{\mbox{\tiny A}}^{\prime}t},\quad v_{\mbox{\tiny B}}(t)=v_{\mbox{\tiny B}}^{\prime}\mbox{e}^{+i\omega_{\mbox{\tiny A}}^{\prime}t},

(50)

where
\omega_{\mbox{\tiny A}}^{\prime}=(\lambda/j)v_{\mbox{\tiny A}}^{\prime}u_{\mbox{\tiny A}}^{\prime}
and
\omega_{\mbox{\tiny B}}^{\prime}=\lambda\left(1-v_{\mbox{\tiny B}}^{\prime}u_{\mbox{\tiny B}}^{\prime}\right)/\left(1+v_{\mbox{\tiny B}}^{\prime}u_{\mbox{\tiny B}}^{\prime}\right)
. However, these equations cannot be directly used to find the sets of contributing trajectories to Eq. (
44
), as their boundary conditions involve the final time
T
. The advantage of Eq. (
50
), however, is that they can be differentiated to find the tangent matrix (
17
):

\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\delta u_{\mbox{\tiny A}}^{\prime\prime}\\
\delta u_{\mbox{\tiny B}}^{\prime\prime}\\
\delta v_{\mbox{\tiny A}}^{\prime\prime}\\
\delta v_{\mbox{\tiny B}}^{\prime\prime}\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}\frac{u_{\mbox{\tiny A}}^{\prime\prime}}{u_{\mbox{\tiny A}}^{\prime}}&\frac{-2i\lambda Tu_{\mbox{\tiny A}}^{\prime\prime}v_{\mbox{\tiny B}}^{\prime}}{(1+u_{\mbox{\tiny B}}^{\prime}v_{\mbox{\tiny B}}^{\prime})^{2}}&0&\frac{-2i\lambda Tu_{\mbox{\tiny A}}^{\prime\prime}u_{\mbox{\tiny B}}^{\prime}}{(1+u_{\mbox{\tiny B}}^{\prime}v_{\mbox{\tiny B}}^{\prime})^{2}}\\
\frac{-i\lambda Tu_{\mbox{\tiny B}}^{\prime\prime}v_{\mbox{\tiny A}}^{\prime}}{j}&\frac{u_{\mbox{\tiny B}}^{\prime\prime}}{u_{\mbox{\tiny B}}^{\prime}}&\frac{-i\lambda Tu_{\mbox{\tiny B}}^{\prime\prime}u_{\mbox{\tiny A}}^{\prime}}{j}&0\\
0&\frac{+2i\lambda Tv_{\mbox{\tiny A}}^{\prime\prime}v_{\mbox{\tiny B}}^{\prime}}{(1+u_{\mbox{\tiny B}}^{\prime}v_{\mbox{\tiny B}}^{\prime})^{2}}&\frac{v_{\mbox{\tiny A}}^{\prime\prime}}{v_{\mbox{\tiny A}}^{\prime}}&\frac{+2i\lambda Tv_{\mbox{\tiny A}}^{\prime\prime}u_{\mbox{\tiny B}}^{\prime}}{(1+u_{\mbox{\tiny B}}^{\prime}v_{\mbox{\tiny B}}^{\prime})^{2}}\\
\frac{+i\lambda Tv_{\mbox{\tiny B}}^{\prime\prime}v_{\mbox{\tiny A}}^{\prime}}{j}&0&\frac{+i\lambda Tv_{\mbox{\tiny B}}^{\prime\prime}u_{\mbox{\tiny A}}^{\prime}}{j}&\frac{v_{\mbox{\tiny B}}^{\prime\prime}}{v_{\mbox{\tiny B}}^{\prime}}\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\delta u_{\mbox{\tiny A}}^{\prime}\\
\delta u_{\mbox{\tiny B}}^{\prime}\\
\delta v_{\mbox{\tiny A}}^{\prime}\\
\delta v_{\mbox{\tiny B}}^{\prime}\end{array}\right).

(51)

To find the sets of contributing trajectories, we apply the initial conditions (
38
) to trajectories (
50
) and impose the final entanglement boundary conditions (
37
), getting

\begin{array}[]{lll}u^{\prime\prime}_{2\mbox{\tiny A}}=u^{\prime\prime}_{1\mbox{\tiny A}}&\Longrightarrow&u^{\prime}_{2\mbox{\tiny A}}\exp{+i \lambda\left( \frac{1- s_0^* u'_{2 \mbox{\tiny B}}}{1+ s_0^* u'_{2\mbox{\tiny B}}} \right)T}=z_{0}\exp{+i \lambda\left( \frac{1- v'_{1 \mbox{\tiny B}} s_0}{1+ v'_{1\mbox{\tiny B}} s_0} \right)T},\\
v^{\prime\prime}_{1\mbox{\tiny A}}=v^{\prime\prime}_{2\mbox{\tiny A}}&\Longrightarrow&v^{\prime}_{1\mbox{\tiny A}}\exp{-i \lambda\left( \frac{1- v'_{1 \mbox{\tiny B}} s_0}{1+ v'_{1\mbox{\tiny B}} s_0} \right)T}=z_{0}^{*}\exp{-i \lambda\left( \frac{1- s_0^* u'_{2 \mbox{\tiny B}}}{1+ s_0^* u'_{2\mbox{\tiny B}}} \right)T},\\
u^{\prime\prime}_{4\mbox{\tiny A}}=u^{\prime\prime}_{3\mbox{\tiny A}}&\Longrightarrow&u^{\prime}_{4\mbox{\tiny A}}\exp{+i \lambda\left( \frac{1- s_0^* u'_{4 \mbox{\tiny B}}}{1+ s_0^* u'_{4\mbox{\tiny B}}} \right)T}=z_{0}\exp{+i \lambda\left( \frac{1- v'_{3 \mbox{\tiny B}} s_0}{1+ v'_{3\mbox{\tiny B}} s_0} \right)T},\\
v^{\prime\prime}_{3\mbox{\tiny A}}=v^{\prime\prime}_{4\mbox{\tiny A}}&\Longrightarrow&v^{\prime}_{3\mbox{\tiny A}}\exp{-i \lambda\left( \frac{1- v'_{3 \mbox{\tiny B}} s_0}{1+ v'_{3\mbox{\tiny B}} s_0} \right)T}=z_{0}^{*}\exp{-i \lambda\left( \frac{1- s_0^* u'_{4 \mbox{\tiny B}}}{1+ s_0^* u'_{4\mbox{\tiny B}}} \right)T},\\
u^{\prime\prime}_{4\mbox{\tiny B}}=u^{\prime\prime}_{1\mbox{\tiny B}}&\Longrightarrow&u^{\prime}_{4\mbox{\tiny B}}\exp{-i \lambda z_0^* u'_{4\mbox{\tiny A}} T/j}=s_{0}\exp{-i \lambda v'_{1\mbox{\tiny A}} z_0 T/j},\\
v^{\prime\prime}_{1\mbox{\tiny B}}=v^{\prime\prime}_{4\mbox{\tiny B}}&\Longrightarrow&v^{\prime}_{1\mbox{\tiny B}}\exp{+i \lambda v'_{1\mbox{\tiny A}} z_0 T/j}=s_{0}^{*}\exp{+i \lambda z_0^* u'_{4\mbox{\tiny A}} z_0 T/j},\\
u^{\prime\prime}_{2\mbox{\tiny B}}=u^{\prime\prime}_{3\mbox{\tiny B}}&\Longrightarrow&u^{\prime}_{2\mbox{\tiny B}}\exp{-i \lambda z_0^* u'_{2\mbox{\tiny A}} T/j}=s_{0}\exp{-i \lambda v'_{3\mbox{\tiny A}} z_0 T/j},\\
v^{\prime\prime}_{3\mbox{\tiny B}}=v^{\prime\prime}_{2\mbox{\tiny B}}&\Longrightarrow&v^{\prime}_{3\mbox{\tiny B}}\exp{+i \lambda v'_{3\mbox{\tiny A}} z_0 T/j}=s_{0}^{*}\exp{+i \lambda z_0^* u'_{2\mbox{\tiny A}} z_0 T/j}.\end{array}

(52)

Notice that these eight equalities allow us to seek the eight initial coordinates needed to define a set of four contributing trajectories. By renaming the variables as

\begin{array}[]{llll}v^{\prime}_{1\mbox{\tiny A}}\equiv z_{0}^{*}\alpha_{1},&\quad v^{\prime}_{1\mbox{\tiny B}}\equiv s_{0}^{*}\beta_{1},&\quad u^{\prime}_{2\mbox{\tiny A}}\equiv z_{0}\alpha_{2},&\quad v^{\prime}_{2\mbox{\tiny B}}\equiv s_{0}\beta_{2},\\
v^{\prime}_{3\mbox{\tiny A}}\equiv z_{0}^{*}\alpha_{3},&\quad v^{\prime}_{3\mbox{\tiny B}}\equiv s_{0}^{*}\beta_{3},&\quad u^{\prime}_{4\mbox{\tiny A}}\equiv z_{0}\alpha_{4},&\quad v^{\prime}_{4\mbox{\tiny B}}\equiv s_{0}\beta_{4},\end{array}

(53)

and manipulating Eq. (
52
), we get an equation for
\alpha_{1}
:

f(\alpha_{1})\equiv f_{s}\big[f_{z}(\alpha_{1})\big]-\alpha_{1}=0,

(54)

where

f_{s}(x)\equiv\exp{\frac{-2i\lambda|s_0|^2 (x^2-1)T}{(1+|s_0|^2 x)(x+|s_0|^2)}}\quad\mathrm{and}\quad f_{z}(x)\equiv\exp{\frac{-i\lambda|z_0|^2 (x^2-1)T}{jx}}.

(55)

Once we have a solution for Eq. (
54
), the other unknown variables are calculated through

\alpha_{1}=\alpha_{2}=\frac{1}{\alpha_{3}}=\frac{1}{\alpha_{4}},\quad\mathrm{and}\quad f_{z}(\alpha_{1})=\beta_{1}=\frac{1}{\beta_{2}}=\frac{1}{\beta_{3}}=\beta_{4}.

(56)

To understand the transcendental equation (
54
), notice that, for
T=0
, only
\alpha_{1}=1
is a solution. This root implies that
\alpha_{l}=\beta_{l}=1
(for
l=1,\ldots,4
), generating the set of four identical
real
trajectories. Most importantly,
\alpha_{1}=1
is a root of Eq. (
54
) whatever is the value of
T
, as expected, since we already discussed that the (central) real trajectory always contributes to
E_{T}^{\mathrm{sc}}
. As we will show numerically next, as
T
increases, many other relevant solutions arise.

We point out that, for this simple system, the problem of describing the semiclassical entanglement dynamics essentially becomes a task of finding the roots of the transcendental equation (
54
). From the values
\alpha_{1}
, we simply evaluate the four trajectories to insert in Eq. (
44
), getting

E^{\mathrm{sc}}_{T}(\hat{\rho}_{0})=1-\sum_{\alpha_{1}}\mathcal{A}~\exp{ -\frac{i\,j}{\lambda T }~g_z(\alpha_1)~g_s\big[f_z(\alpha_1)\big] },

(57)

where

g_{s}(x)\equiv\frac{-2i\lambda|s_{0}|^{2}(x^{2}-1)T}{(1+|s_{0}|^{2}x)(x+|s_{0}|^{2})},\qquad g_{z}(x)\equiv\frac{-i\lambda|z_{0}|^{2}(x^{2}-1)T}{jx},

(58)

and

\mathcal{A}\equiv\left[\frac{(1+f_{z}(\alpha_{1})|s_{0}|^{2})(f_{z}(\alpha_{1})+|s_{0}|^{2})}{f_{z}(\alpha_{1})(1+|s_{0}|^{2})^{2}}\right]^{2j}\frac{\exp{|z_0|^2 \left[ 2 - (\alpha_1^2 + 1)/\alpha_1\right] }}{\sqrt{\det\mathbf{R}}}.

(59)

Here, the only term that still needs to be calculated is the matrix
\mathbf{R}
, defined by Eq. (
45
), but their ingredients are already explicitly shown in Eq. (
51
).

Before presenting our numerical results, we will briefly review the entirely quantum entanglement entropy. Straightforward calculations applied to Eq. (
26
), using the initial state (
27
) and the Hamiltonian (
46
) yield

E_{T}^{\mathrm{q}}(\hat{\rho}_{0})=\mathcal{N}_{z_{0}}^{4}\,\mathcal{N}_{s_{0}}^{4}\sum_{n_{z},m_{z}=0}^{\infty}\!\!\mathcal{A}_{n_{z}}^{(z_{0})}\,\mathcal{A}_{m_{z}}^{(z_{0})}\sum_{n_{s},m_{s}=0}^{2j}\!\!\mathcal{B}_{n_{s}}^{(s_{0})}\,\mathcal{B}_{m_{s}}^{(s_{0})}\,\mbox{e}^{-i(\lambda T/j)(n_{z}-m_{z})(n_{s}-m_{s})},

(60)

where

\mathcal{A}_{n}^{(z_{0})}\equiv\frac{|z_{0}|^{2n}}{n!}\quad\mathrm{and}\quad\mathcal{B}_{m}^{(s_{0})}\equiv\frac{(2j)!|s_{0}|^{2m}}{(2j-m)!m!}.

(61)

For our purposes, it is convenient to realize that
E^{\mathrm{q}}(\hat{\rho}_{0})
is periodic with period
T_{\mathrm{p}}=2\pi j/\lambda
. Therefore, we define the dimensionless time

\tau=\frac{T}{T_{\mathrm{p}}},

(62)

so that the whole entanglement dynamics is contained in the interval
0\leq\tau<1
. In the next section, all the discussion will be done in terms of
\tau
, instead of
T
, and we will investigate the whole period,
0\leq\tau<1
.

V
Final Remarks

In this work, we have derived a semiclassical formula for the entanglement entropy, written in terms of the trajectories of the corresponding classical description. Our results demonstrate that, as in other bipartite systems, the inclusion of complex trajectories significantly enhances the accuracy of the semiclassical approximation, allowing it to faithfully reproduce the exact quantum dynamics beyond the short-time (Ehrenfest) regime.

The methodology introduced here opens the way for systematic semiclassical studies of more intricate spin–field models, including chaotic and strongly interacting systems. Such extensions are particularly promising for uncovering the classical mechanisms that govern entanglement generation and for clarifying how the degree of chaoticity in phase space influences entanglement growth at longer times.

Finally, we note the connection between our semiclassical formalism and recent proposals linking entanglement dynamics with number theory, such as the use of similar models to encode prime distributions
59
;
15
. In the future, we plan to explore our semiclassical approach to investigate the role of complex trajectories in determining primes. This idea is inspired by a work by Berry and Keating
6
, where the energy spectrum of a given Hamiltonian is semiclassically evaluated by summing contributions of periodic orbits, and the achieved expressions are proved to be similar to the distribution of the zeros of the Riemann Zeta function. In our case, we will investigate whether entanglement entropy, written in terms of complex trajectories, leaves signatures related to the distribution of prime numbers. Such studies could further enrich the interplay between semiclassical physics, entanglement theory, and mathematical structures underlying quantum systems.

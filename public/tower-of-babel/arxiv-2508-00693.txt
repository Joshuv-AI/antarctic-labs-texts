On multi-propagator angular integrals
Juliane Haug, Vladimir A. Smirnov, and Fabian Wunder

Abstract
We study multi-propagator angular integrals, a class of phase-space integrals relevant to processes with multiple observed final states and a test-bed for transferring loop-integral technology to phase space integrals without reversed unitarity. We present an Euler integral representation similar to Lee-Pomeransky representation and explicitly describe a recursive IBP reduction and dimensional shift relations for the general case of n n denominators. On the level of master integrals, applying a differential equation approach, we explicitly calculate the previously unknown angular integrals with four denominators for any number of masses to finite order in ε \varepsilon . Extending the idea of dimensional recurrence, we explore the decomposition of angular integrals into branch integrals reducing the number of scales in the master integrals from ( n + 1 ) ​ n / 2 (n+1)n/2 to n + 1 n+1 . To showcase the potential of this method, we calculate the massless three denominator integral to establish all-order results in ε \varepsilon including a resummation of soft logarithms.

On the one hand, non-integer dimensionality causes these phase-space integrals to be non-trivial and requires careful analytic treatment.
On the other hand, we will see that introducing a dependence on the space time dimension as a new variable opens up a rich structure within this class of integrals to be discovered.

Angular integrals are classified by the number of denominators (i.e. propagators) and masses (i.e. non-light-like vectors).
The more denominators and masses, the more complex an integral is.
The literature mentioned above only required integrals with up to two denominators because partial fraction decomposition could be used to reduce higher-denominator integrals.
This was possible due to the restricted kinematics with a total of only three observed particles in initial and final states.
Going to more exclusive processes with a larger number of observed particles will require genuine multi-propagator integrals that cannot be reduced by partial fraction decomposition, due to a less restricted kinematics with a larger number of linearly independent momenta.

2
Euler representation for angular integrals

Euler integrals are in the focus of the mathematical study of loop amplitudes
Salvatori:2024nva
.
Hence it is natural to also look for such representations for phase space integrals.
A frst step in this direction was taken in
Smirnov:2024pbj
, where such a representation was algorithmically constructed for angular integrals to be used in the study of the small mass asymptotics of angular integrals.
Angular integrals admit for the simple and symmetric Euler representation

\displaystyle\Omega_{j_{1},\dots,j_{n}}(\{v_{kl}\};\varepsilon)=\frac{2^{2-j-2\varepsilon}\pi^{1-\varepsilon}\,\Gamma(1-\varepsilon)}{\Gamma(2-j-2\varepsilon)\prod_{k=1}^{n}\Gamma(j_{k})}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\prod_{k=1}^{n}\mathrm{d}t_{k}\,t_{k}^{j_{k}-1}}{\left(1+\sum_{k=1}^{n}t_{k}+\sum_{k\leq l=1}^{n}\tilde{v}_{kl}\,t_{k}t_{l}\right)^{1-\varepsilon}},

(3)

which we will derive in this section.
The integral representation has striking structural similarity with the Lee-Pomeransky representation for loop integrals
Lee:2013hzt
.
Specifically setting the dimension as
d=2-2\varepsilon
, the loop number
l=1
and the Lee-Pomeransky polynomial to

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}(t)=1+\sum_{k=1}^{n}t_{k}+\sum_{k\leq l=1}^{n}\tilde{v}_{kl}\,t_{k}t_{l},

(4)

eq. (
3
) is exactly of the form of a Lee-Pomeransky representation up to powers of
2
and
\pi
.
This representation is considerably simpler than the form given by the authors in
Smirnov:2024pbj
which was used in the investigation of the small mass asymptotics of angular integrals.
In our derivation of eq. (
3
), we start from Somogyi’s general Mellin-Barnes representation
Somogyi:2011

\displaystyle\Omega_{j_{1},\dots,j_{n}}(\{v_{kl}\};\varepsilon)=

\displaystyle\frac{2^{2-j-2\varepsilon}\pi^{1-\varepsilon}}{\Gamma(2-j-2\varepsilon)\prod_{k=1}^{n}\Gamma(j_{k})}\int_{-\mathrm{i}\infty}^{\mathrm{i}\infty}\left(\prod_{k=1}^{n}\prod_{l=k}^{n}\frac{\mathrm{d}z_{kl}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\,\tilde{v}_{kl}^{z_{kl}}\right)

\displaystyle\times\left(\prod_{k=1}^{n}\Gamma(j_{k}+z_{k})\right)\Gamma(1-j-\varepsilon-z)\,,

(5)

where
j=\sum_{k=1}^{n}j_{k}
,
z_{k}=\sum_{l=1}^{k}z_{lk}+\sum_{l=k}^{n}z_{kl}
,
z=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{l=k}^{n}z_{kl}
, and normalized scalar products
\tilde{v}_{kl}=v_{k}\cdot v_{l}/2
for
k\neq l
respectively
\tilde{v}_{kk}=v_{k}^{2}/4
.
Noting that

\displaystyle\underbrace{\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{l=k}^{n}(-z_{kl})}_{=-z}+\underbrace{\sum_{k=1}^{n}(j_{k}+z_{k})}_{=j+2z}+1-j-\varepsilon-z=1-\varepsilon

(6)

we can combine all
z_{kl}
-dependent Gamma functions to a single multi-variable Beta function which can in turn be expressed as an Euler integral

\displaystyle\Gamma(1-\varepsilon)\mathrm{B}\left(\{-z_{kl}\},\{j_{k}+z_{k}\},1-j-\varepsilon-z\right)=\Gamma(1-\varepsilon)\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\prod_{k=1}^{n}\prod_{l=k}^{n}\frac{\mathrm{d}t_{kl}}{t_{kl}}\right)\left(\prod_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\mathrm{d}t_{k}}{t_{k}}\right)\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\,

\displaystyle\hskip 142.26378pt\times t_{kl}^{-z_{kl}}\,t_{k}^{j_{k}+z_{k}}\,t^{1-j-\varepsilon-z}\,\delta\!\left(1-\sum_{k\leq l=1}^{n}t_{kl}-\sum_{k=1}^{n}t_{k}-t\right).

(7)

The remaining MB integrals can be evaluated in terms of delta functions as

\displaystyle\int_{-\mathrm{i}\infty}^{\mathrm{i}\infty}\prod_{k=1}^{n}\prod_{l=k}^{n}\frac{\mathrm{d}z_{kl}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\left(\frac{\tilde{v}_{kl}\,t_{k}\,t_{l}}{t_{kl}\,t}\right)^{z_{kl}}=\prod_{k=1}^{n}\prod_{l=k}^{n}\delta\!\left(1-\frac{\tilde{v}_{kl}\,t_{k}\,t_{l}}{t_{kl}\,t}\right)=\prod_{k=1}^{n}\prod_{l=k}^{n}t_{kl}\,\delta\!\left(t_{kl}-\frac{\tilde{v}_{kl}\,t_{k}\,t_{l}}{t}\right).

(8)

Now substituting
t_{k}\rightarrow t\,t_{k}
and
t_{kl}\rightarrow t\,t_{kl}
leads us to

\displaystyle\Omega_{j_{1},\dots,j_{n}}(\{v_{kl}\};\varepsilon)=

\displaystyle\frac{2^{2-j-2\varepsilon}\pi^{1-\varepsilon}\Gamma(1-\varepsilon)}{\Gamma(2-j-2\varepsilon)\prod_{k=1}^{n}\Gamma(j_{k})}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\prod_{k=1}^{n}\prod_{l=k}^{n}\mathrm{d}t_{kl}\right)\left(\prod_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\mathrm{d}t_{k}}{t_{k}}\right)\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}t_{k}^{j_{k}}\,t^{1-\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\times\prod_{k=1}^{n}\prod_{l=k}^{n}\delta(t_{kl}-\tilde{v}_{kl}t_{k}t_{l})\,\delta\left(1-t\left(1+\sum_{k=1}^{n}t_{k}+\sum_{k\leq l=1}^{n}t_{kl}\right)\right).

(9)

Finally evaluating the delta functions in
t_{kl}
and
t
, we obtain the integral representation in eq. (
3
).

3
IBP relations and reduction to master integrals for angular integrals with
n
denominators

IBP relations for angular integrals can be established in a very similar fashion as one would do for loop integrals, with the necessary modifications described in
Haug:2024yfi
.
Due to the structural simplicity present in the case of angular integrals, it is possible to symbolically combine the IBP relations to explicit index raising respectively lowering identities.
Hence a reduction to master integrals is possible without invoking Laporta’s algorithm
Laporta:2000
.
Extending on
Lyubovitskij:2021
and
Haug:2024yfi
where the cases of
n=2
(respectively,
n=3
) denominators have been covered, we did the very same exercise for
n=4
denominators.
Doing so one notices a common structure for the recursion relations which we conjecture to hold true for all
n
.

The recursion that lowers the
k
-th index while also lowering the sum
j=\sum_{i}j_{i}
, applicable if
j_{k}\neq 1
, reads

\displaystyle I_{j_{1}\dots j_{n}}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{X_{n}(j_{k}-1)}\left\{\left[(j+1-d)X_{4,\bar{k}}+(j_{k}-1)X_{4}^{(k,k)}+\sum_{i\neq k}j_{i}X_{n}^{(k,i)}\right]\,\mathbf{\hat{j}^{-}_{k}}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\sum_{i\neq k}(j_{k}-1)X_{n}^{(k,i)}\,\mathbf{\hat{j}^{-}_{i}}+\sum_{i\neq k}(d-j-1)X_{n}^{(k,i)}\,\mathbf{\hat{j}^{-}_{k}}\mathbf{\hat{j}^{-}_{i}}+\sum_{\underset{i\neq l}{i\neq k,l\neq k}}j_{l}X_{n}^{(k,i)}\,\mathbf{\hat{j}^{-}_{k}}\,\mathbf{\hat{j}^{-}_{i}}\,\mathbf{\hat{j}^{+}_{l}}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+(d-j-1)X^{(1,1)}\left(\mathbf{\hat{j}^{-}_{k}}\right)^{2}+\sum_{i\neq k}j_{i}X_{n}^{(k,k)}\left(\mathbf{\hat{j}^{-}_{k}}\right)^{2}\mathbf{\hat{j}^{+}_{i}}\right\}I_{j_{1}\dots j_{n}}

(10)

where
\mathbf{\hat{j}^{\pm}_{i}}
are the raising (resp. lowering) operators of the
i
-th index,
X_{n}
denotes the
Gram determinant

\displaystyle X_{n}=(-1)^{n-1}\det\!\left(v_{i}\cdot v_{j}\right)_{i,j=1\dots n}\,,

(11)

X_{n,\bar{k}}
the
Gram-Cramer determinant
, i.e. the Gram determinant where the
k
-th column is replaced by ones

\displaystyle X_{n,\bar{k}}=(-1)^{n-1}\det\!\left((1-\delta_{jk})\,v_{i}\cdot v_{j}+\delta_{jk}\right)_{i,j=1\dots n}\,,

(12)

and
X_{n}^{(k,l)}
the
Gram cofactors
where the
k
-th row and
l
-th column have been deleted from the Gram determinant

\displaystyle X_{n}^{(k,l)}=(-1)^{n-1}\det\!\left(v_{i}\cdot v_{j}\right)_{i,j=1\dots n,i\neq k,j\neq l}\,.

(13)

The corresponding identity to raise the
k
-th index while also raising the sum of indices
j
reads

\displaystyle I_{j_{1}\dots j_{n}}=

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbf{\hat{j}^{+}_{k}}}{(3+j-d)\,Y_{n}^{(k,k)}}\left\{\left[(3+j-d)Y_{n}^{(k,k)}+(1+j_{k})X_{n,\bar{k}}+\sum_{i\neq k}j_{i}(1-v_{ki})X_{n,\bar{k}}^{(k,i)}\right]\right.

\displaystyle\left.+(1+j_{k})\left((1-v_{kk})X_{n}^{(k,k)}-X_{n,\bar{k}}\right)\mathbf{\hat{j}^{+}_{k}}+\sum_{i\neq k}j_{i}(1-v_{ki})X_{n}^{(k,k)}\,\mathbf{\hat{j}^{+}_{i}}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\sum_{i\neq k}(1+j_{i})(1-v_{kk})X_{n,\bar{k}}^{(k,i)}\,\mathbf{\hat{j}^{+}_{k}}\mathbf{\hat{j}^{-}_{i}}+\sum_{\underset{i\neq l}{i\neq k,l\neq k}}j_{i}(1-v_{ki})X_{n,\bar{k}}^{(k,l)}\,\mathbf{\hat{j}^{+}_{i}}\mathbf{\hat{j}^{-}_{l}}\right\}I_{j_{1}\dots j_{n}}

(14)

where also
Gram-Cramer cofactors

X_{n,\bar{k}}^{(k,l)}
appear, i.e. Gram-Cramer determinants with the
k
-th row and
l
-th column being deleted

\displaystyle X_{n,\bar{k}}^{(k,l)}=(-1)^{n-1}\det\!\left((1-\delta_{jk})\,v_{i}\cdot v_{j}+\delta_{jk}\right)_{i,j=1\dots n,i\neq k,j\neq l}\,.

(15)

We also introduced
Y_{n}
to denote the
Euclidean Gram determinant

\displaystyle Y_{n}=\det\!\left(1-v_{i}\cdot v_{j}\right)_{i,j=1\dots n}

(16)

with its cofactors

\displaystyle Y_{n}^{(k,l)}=\det\!\left(1-v_{i}\cdot v_{j}\right)_{i,j=1\dots n,i\neq k,j\neq l}\,.

(17)

Remarkably, these recursion relations allow in principle – only restricted by memory and computation time – for an algorithmic reduction of
I_{j_{1}\dots j_{n}}
to master integrals for arbitrary
n
without using Laporta’s algortihm
Laporta:2000
. This is a system with in general
2^{n}
master integrals, those with
j_{i}\in\{0,1\}
.
In the remainder of this work we focus on these master integrals starting by discussing their behavior under dimensional shift.

4
Dimensional shift relation

In
Haug:2024yfi
a general dimensional recurrence formula that connects angular integrals in
d
and
d+2
dimensions with different propagator powers

has been proven.
Combing this with the IBP reduction of section

For four denominators we find the dimensional shift formula

\displaystyle I_{1111}(d)=\frac{1}{X_{4}}\Big[

\displaystyle X_{4,\bar{4}}I_{1110}(d)+X_{4,\bar{3}}I_{1101}(d)+X_{4,\bar{2}}I_{1011}(d)+X_{4,\bar{1}}I_{0111}(d)

\displaystyle+\frac{5-d}{d-3}\,Y_{4}\,I_{1111}(d+2)\Big].

(18)

In contrast to the case of
n=3
discussed in
Haug:2024yfi

\varepsilon
in

Consequently, this term gives a contribution at order
\varepsilon^{0}
.
Generalizing the cases

we can conjecture the following form for general

\displaystyle I_{\underbrace{\scriptstyle 1\dots 1}_{n}}(d)=\frac{1}{X_{n}}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{n,\bar{i}}\,I_{1\underset{\underset{i\text{-th}}{\uparrow}}{\dots 0\dots}1}(d)+\frac{n+1-d}{d-3}\,Y_{n}\,I_{\underbrace{\scriptstyle 1\dots 1}_{n}}(d+2)\right].

(19)

The coefficients

,

, and

We observe that interestingly the dimensional shift identity can be viewed as a direct generalization of partial fractioning.
In a case where the Euclidean Gram determinant

) reduces to a partial fractioning identity between

We note that in a situation where the denominator vectors

are confined to the physical four dimensional subspace, a maximum number of three vectors may have linear independent spatial parts, thus always

In the following we do not make this assumption.

By repeated use of eq. (

d\rightarrow 4
.
Also, these equations can be used to set up a calculation based on dimensional recurrence

.
This will be explored in section

Before, we will however use eq. (

Haug:2024yfi

\displaystyle J_{1111}^{(1),d=6}=\pi

\displaystyle+\mathrm{Cl}_{2}\!\left(2\theta_{23}^{(1)}-2\theta_{23}^{(0)}\right)-\mathrm{Cl}_{2}\!\left(4\theta_{23}^{(1)}\right)+\mathrm{Cl}_{2}\!\left(2\theta_{24}^{(1)}+2\theta_{24}^{(0)}\right)+\mathrm{Cl}_{2}\!\left(2\theta_{24}^{(1)}-2\theta_{24}^{(0)}\right)

\displaystyle\left.-\mathrm{Cl}_{2}\!\left(4\theta_{24}^{(1)}\right)+\mathrm{Cl}_{2}\!\left(2\theta_{24}^{(1)}+2\theta_{24}^{(0)}\right)+\mathrm{Cl}_{2}\!\left(2\theta_{34}^{(1)}-2\theta_{34}^{(0)}\right)-\mathrm{Cl}_{2}\!\left(4\theta_{34}^{(1)}\right)\right].

6
Scale reduction by dimensional recurrence

The angular integral with
n
denominators depends on
n
vectors, i.e. on
n(n-1)/2
scalar products between them and additionally
0\leq m\leq n
masses.
As a rule of thumb, the higher the number of scales, the more complicated the calculation of the integral.
Hence since the very beginning of the study of angular integrals in QCD, and similar also for loop integrals, reducing the number of scales has been of key interest.
The only tool used so far for angular integrals has been partial fractioning decomposition.
In the case of linear dependent vectors this allows to reduce the number of vectors the integral depends on.
A generalization of this extensively employed technique in
(
Lyubovitskij:2021
)
, allowing for a reduction of multi-mass integrals to integrals with no more than one mass,
made the calculation of previously inaccessible integrals
Blumlein:2020
(due to their complexity) possible in
Lyubovitskij:2021
;
Haug:2024yfi
.
This exemplifies the power of expressing master integrals in terms of integrals with fewer scales.
A powerful technique which achieves this for loop integrals is Tarasov’s dimensional recurrence
Tarasov:1996br
;
Tarasov:2000sf
.
Borrowing from this approach for angular integrals we will see that iterating the dimensional shift relation eq. (
19
) allows for a reduction from
n(n-1)/2+m
scales to a sum of terms with only
n
(respectively
n+1
) scales each.
Any of these contributions belongs to one of only two classes depending on whether the ‘root’ vector is massive or massless.
So, overall, the task of calculating
n+1
integrals with
n(n-1)/2+m
scales where
m
takes values from
0
to
n
is reduced to the calculation of just two integrals with
n(+1)
scales. We will call these the
massless/massive branch integrals

\mathcal{B}^{(0/1)}_{n}
which will be defined below.

In this section we will iterate the dimensional shift relation of section
4
to a dimensional recurrence formula which we will subsequently use to establish a decomposition formula with the scale reduction properties described above.
From there we will explore the utility of the devised formula to establish the all order expansion of the massless three denominator integral and several orders for its massive counterpart.

6.1
From dimensional shift to dimensional recurrence

We start our exploration from the dimensional shift formula

\displaystyle I_{\vec{1}_{n}}(d)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{n,i}I_{\vec{1}_{n,i}}(d)+\frac{n+1-d}{d-3}\,y_{n}\,I_{\vec{1}_{n}}(d+2)\,,

(30)

where we use the compact notation
\vec{1}_{n}=\underbrace{1,\dots,1}_{n}
,
\vec{1}_{n,i}=1\underset{\underset{i\text{-th}}{\uparrow}}{\dots 0\dots}1
,
x_{n,i}=X_{n,i}/X_{n}
, and
y_{n}=Y_{n}/X_{n}
.
Here and in the following we assume that the Gram determinants
X_{i}
for
i>1
are non-zero throughout.
We observe that this identity splits the
n
-denominator master integral into
n
integrals with one denominator less and a remainder contribution which is the original
n
denominator integral shifted by two dimensions.
For the three- and four denominator integral this representation was used to facilitate the calculation of the
\varepsilon
-expansion by using differential equations to determine the dimensionally shifted integral.

The idea of shifting the dimension can be taken a step further by iterating the procedure.
Plugging the right-hand side of eq. (
30
) into itself repeatedly an infinite series builds up as

\displaystyle I_{\vec{1}_{n}}(d)=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{n,i}I_{\vec{1}_{n,i}}(d)+\frac{n+1-d}{d-3}\,y_{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{n,i}I_{\vec{1}_{n,i}}(d+2)

\displaystyle+\frac{(n+1-d)(n-1-d)}{(d-3)(d-1)}\,y_{n}^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{n,i}\,I_{\vec{1}_{n,i}}(d+4)+\dots

\displaystyle+\frac{(n+1-d)(n-1-d)\dots(n+1-2k-d)}{(d-3)(d-1)\dots(d+2k-3)}\,y_{n}^{k}\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{n,i}\,I_{\vec{1}_{n,i}}(d+2k)+\dots

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{n,i}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\left(\frac{d-n-1}{2}\right)_{k}}{\left(\frac{d-3}{2}\right)_{k}}\,(-y_{n})^{k}\,I_{\vec{1}_{n,i}}(d+2k).

(31)

After
k
iterations there is a remainder term proportional to an angular integral in
d+2k+2
dimensions.
The convergence of the series is assured by the rapid decrease of the angular measure for
d\rightarrow\infty
.
Eq. (
31
) constitutes a series solution to the dimensional recurrence.
An analogous result for loop integrals was discussed in
Tarasov:2000sf
.

6.2
From dimensional recurrence to splitting in branches

We observe that the representation of eq. (
31
) splits the
n
-denominator integral into
n
parts each depending on dimensionally shifted
n-1
-denominator integrals.
Now we can perform a second round of iterations and use this very same identity on the
n-1
-denominator integrals.
Doing this
n-1
-times the right hand side is expressed as a sum of
n!
terms each written as a nested sum over dimensionally shifted one-denominator integrals.
Each of these terms corresponds to a particular permutation of the
n
vectors
v_{1},\dots,\,v_{n}
which are ‘pinched out‘ by eq. (
31
) one at a time.

The result takes the form

\displaystyle I_{\vec{1}_{n}}(v_{1},\dots,v_{n};d=4-2\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n}}\left(\prod_{i=2}^{n}x_{i,i}\!\left(v_{\sigma(1)},\dots,v_{\sigma(i)}\right)\right)\mathcal{B}_{n}(v_{\sigma(1)},\dots,v_{\sigma(n)};\varepsilon)

(32)

with
branch integrals

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{n}(v_{1},\dots,v_{n};\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=\!\!\!\!\!\!\sum_{k_{2},\dots,k_{n}=0}^{\infty}\left[\prod_{i=2}^{n}\,c\!\left(\varepsilon-\sum_{j=i+1}^{n}k_{j},i,k_{i}\right)(-y_{i}(v_{1},\cdots,v_{i}))^{k_{i}}\right]I_{1}\!\left(v_{1};2-\varepsilon+\sum_{i=2}^{n}k_{i}\right)

(33)

where the summation coefficients
c(\varepsilon,n,k)
are given by

\displaystyle c(\varepsilon,n,k)=\frac{\left(\frac{3-n}{2}-\varepsilon\right)_{k}}{\left(\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon\right)_{k}}\,.

(34)

The representation eq.(
32
) now expresses the
n
-denominator master integral in terms of branch-integrals
\mathcal{B}_{n}
which are given by (hypergeometric) nested sums over a ’root’ one-denominator integral.
While the original integral depends on
n(n-1)/2+m

n
Gram-type variables

and if

is massive additionally on
v_{11}

Furthermore, while there are

n

\mathcal{B}_{n}^{(0)}
respectively
\mathcal{B}_{n}^{(1)}

Iteratively, the branch integrals can be calculated as

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{n+1}(v_{1},\dots,v_{n+1};\varepsilon)=\sum_{k_{n+1}=0}^{\infty}c(\varepsilon,n+1,k_{n+1})(-y_{n+1}(v_{1},\dots,v_{n+1}))^{k_{n+1}}\mathcal{B}_{n}(v_{1},\dots,v_{n};\varepsilon).

(35)

Quite interestingly we note that the set of dimensional shift relations up to

determines the angular integral with
n
denominators up to the boundary condition at

It is especially remarkable that the mass-reduction formula used to reduce scales is not required as an additional input but the reduction to only one ‘relevant’ mass is implicitly build in.

In appendix

For the massless

branch integral this works so smoothly that it is even possible to construct a closed form expression valid to all orders in

8
Conclusion

Considerably extending the existing results in the literature, we have investigated a plethora of aspects of multi-propagator angular integrals, transferring methods from loop integration to a phase-space setting.
With a focus on the structure of this particular class of integrals for various numbers of denominators we uncovered several new features.
The most important ones are

•

a Lee-Pomeransky-like integral representation that very closely resembles a proper Feynman integral,

•

IBP relations turned into recursion relations allowing for a reduction to master integrals without Laporta’s algorithm,

•

scale reduction by Tarasov’s dimensional recurrence and decomposition into branch integrals.

Furthermore, we established novel explicit results for the
\varepsilon
-expansion of the four denominator angular integral with an arbitrary number of masses using a differential equation approach.
Additionally, we presented an all-order
\varepsilon
-expansion for the massless angular integral with three denominators allowing for a resummation of soft logarithms.
We hope that these findings are of interest on a theoretical level, regarding their strong connection to the study of Feynman integrals, and are also of value for phenomenology when extending the use of angular integrals to higher multiplicity processes in the future.

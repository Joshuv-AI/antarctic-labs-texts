Analyzing vehicle path optimization using an improved genetic algorithm in the presence of stochastic perturbation matter
Authors: Shengdong Mu, Boyu Liu, Gu Jijian, Chaolung Lien, Chen Xiaojie
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
By analyzing the influence of stochastic perturbation matters on vehicle path optimization, a perturbation scheduling model for logistics and distribution with a carbon tax mechanism is established under the premise of time window variation and load capacity constraints. Herein, we propose an enhanced Genetic Algorithm (GA) based on a Gaussian matrix mutation (GMM) operator, which maintains the diversity of the population while speeding up the algorithm’s convergence. The model builds a Gaussian probability matrix using the site positional order distribution characteristics implied in the original site data information, and applies the Gaussian probability matrix to individual gene mutations using a roulette-wheel-selection method; thus, the study guarantees the genetic diversity of the population while guiding it to evolve in the high-fitness direction. Finally, an experimental simulation is performed using data obtained from a commercial supermarket, thereby verifying the effectiveness of the proposed algorithm and comparing it with other algorithms. The results reveal that compared with the classical GA, the average convergence speed of the improved GA can be increased by 50–60% and the consumed algorithm time can be reduced by 48% while maintaining the difference in solution accuracy within 1%.

Introduction

The vehicle route problem (VRP) has been a prominent research topic that fundamentally aims to quickly respond to customer demand, improve customer service quality, reduce distribution costs, distribute goods from a fleet of vehicles to geographically dispersed outlets, and search for the optimal distribution vehicle routes under the premise of satisfying a series of constraints, thereby satisfying the lowest total cost of transportation between the goods in the car parks and the customer’s point, the use of the minimum number of vehicles, and the shortest distribution time.

Issue 1: Vehicle Routing Problem with Time Window (VRPTW). In 1987, Solomon1 first proposed the VRPTW which is a typical NP-Hard problem. Compared with VRP, VRPTW increases the customer’s time window and service time, through which the vehicle reaches the location within the time window specified by the customer. If the vehicle arrives earlier, it should wait until the time window to provide service to the customer. Ruiz et al.2 analyzed the open-ended VRPTW. Belmecheri et al.3 improved the particle swarm optimization algorithm for solving the VRPTW for mixed round-trip customers. To solve the VRPTW, Júnior and Leal4 proposed a hybrid heuristic algorithm using a multiple ant colony system; thus, they reduced the number of vehicles and the total distance traveled, while incorporating a stochastic variable neighborhood-descent algorithm for local search to obtain delivery paths with more optimal results. Bouchra et al.5 combined GA and variable neighborhood search to form a hybrid optimization method for solving a vehicle path problem with a soft time window and utilized GA to obtain an initial solution, which is obtained using a GA; thus, the study obtains the initial solution and optimizes the initial solution using two different neighborhood structures. Afifi et al.6 improved the local search capability of a simulated annealing algorithm and solved the vehicle path problem with time windows with simultaneous accesses in a more optimal manner. Çetinkaya et al.7 modelled the vehicle path problem with curved time windows and solved it using heuristic algorithms. Sripriya et al.8 enhanced the mutation operator of a GA in solving VRPTW by utilizing the split mutation operator to jump out of the local optimum by migrating the current solution to a worse solution.

Issue 2: Multi-objective vehicle path problem. Notably, because multi-objectives are often contradictory and conflicting, the multi-objective optimization problem does not have an explicit solution, and only multiple optimal solutions of the infinite approximation problem can be obtained; moreover, the final solution reflects the decision maker’s decision tendency. Jozefowiez et al.9 proposed three methods for solving the multi-objective vehicle path problem, i.e., the Scalarized, Pareto, and Non-Scalar Non-Pareto methods. Miranda et al.10 proposed a multi-objective vehicle path problem with a hard time window and stochastic travel and service times, proposed a multi-objective Memetic algorithm and a multi-objective iterative local search for solving the problem, and verified the effectiveness of the proposed algorithm and problem model based on the Solomon example. Khoo and Mohammad11 proposed a two-stage distributed hybrid destruction and regeneration GA to solve the multi-objective vehicle path problem with time windows, and experiments on the Solomon standard test set indicated that multiple new Pareto optimal frontiers were obtained. Dutta et al.12 analyzed the open-ended multi-objective green vehicle path problem and developed a bi-objective model for minimizing both the vehicle’s cost and carbon emission, and subsequently utilized the Pareto evolutionary algorithm with extended intensity and the non-dominated sorting GA to solve the model. Ma et al.13 constructed a multi-objective multi-time-window vehicle path model and designed an optimization algorithm complementary to the pigeonholing algorithm and the intelligent water droplet algorithm to solve it; thus, they aimed to minimize the total transportation cost, the shortest transportation distance, and the minimum number of schedules of the distribution vehicles. The experiments proved that the time in which the algorithm can solve the problem has been improved. Li et al.14 considered the customer’s preference for the time window, and designed a bi-objective time window assignment vehicle path problem, thus aiming to maximize the overall customer satisfaction with the assigned time window and minimize the assignment cost; subsequently, they proposed a hybrid multi-objective GA to solve the problem.

Issue 3: There is a VRP problem under stochastic perturbations. More specifically, the transportation process cannot be smooth, and there is a possibility of congestion. Wang et al.15 considered people in view of vehicle scheduling research, the minimum cost of vehicle delivery, and the minimum deviation for the time required by the customer as the objective combined with the mathematical model to establish a perturbation model, and they solved and analyzed the mathematical model to achieve the multi-objective optimization effect. Fan et al.16 analyze the fuzzy demand and fuzzy time window in the delivery process. Thus, a dispatch perturbation model is established, and the algorithm is applied to solve the problem. Ning et al.17 analyze the impact of perturbation events on cost deviation in the dispatch process and propose a perturbation scheme adjustment strategy. Ekmekci18 propose an improved algorithm based on the ant colony optimization (ACO) algorithm, which stores the cost information of the trajectory for each iteration through the memory algorithm and updates the pheromone on the trajectory based on this information. Phu-Ang19 proposed an improved artificial immunity algorithm for solution algorithm optimization by introducing double splitting and a K-means clustering algorithm. Some scholars have also mixed different heuristic algorithms to harness their respective strengths. Wu et al.20 proposed an algorithm combining a GA, greedy algorithm, and sparrow search algorithm; thus, they enhanced the model’s solution accuracy, solution speed, and robustness. Akter et al.21 proposed a novel crossover method for the chromosome crossover process, in which the cost of the post-crossover is compared before the crossover operation to decide whether the crossover operation should be performed. Bao et al.22 improved the performance of the path-planning algorithm by combining the partially mapped crossover operator with a correction mechanism. Prasad and Jain23 proposed a crossover operator combined with a greedy algorithm, and the obtained results are more optimal than the traditional crossover methods. It is worth noting that other problems such as the cold chain distribution problem have also been analyzed by scholars.

Many scholars have analyzed the vehicle path-solving problem, the use of machine learning methods, and the establishment of stochastic perturbation management models; however, the following limitations remain: (1) the existing vehicle-path-solving problem on the distribution distance, capacity, the time spent on cost-intuitive factors that should be considered, ignoring the actual distribution process pertaining to the occurrence of perturbation events for the distribution plan, less consideration of the actual cargo-loss distribution process, vehicle fuel consumption, and carbon emissions for the impact of corporate costs, and for the carbon tax mechanism, the carbon trading model is inconsiderably involved. (2) The cost and carbon emission constraints in the logistics and distribution processes are not effectively predicted, and no satisfactory prediction model exists.

This study utilizes the occurrence of a traffic congestion event as a stochastic disturbance matter for logistics and distribution, and considers the three objectives of carbon emission, namely deviation cost, path deviation, and cost deviation; moreover, the study constructs a disturbance management model of low-carbon logistics and distribution under a carbon tax mechanism. An enhanced GA is proposed by introducing a GMM operator. The distance at which the stochastic perturbation occurs and the hidden bit-order distribution characteristics of the perturbation point in the feasible solution are utilized as the guiding factors of individual gene mutation in the GA, thereby guiding the population to evolve in the high-fitness direction.

Comparing the performance of the improved GA, classical GA, and ant colony algorithm, it is found that the improved genetic algorithm based on the GMM operator is more optimal than the traditional genetic algorithm and ant colony optimization algorithm in regard to convergence speed, searching ability, and algorithmic time-consumption while maintaining a higher quality of the solution; meanwhile, the selection of variation probability somewhat affects the performance of the solution .

The study is structured as follows: (1) The perturbation problem is proposed, and under the premise of the research assumptions, the low-carbon logistics model with the existence of stochastic perturbation items is established. (2) The classical GA is optimized to accelerate the algorithm’s convergence speed while maintaining the population diversity. (3) Validate the optimized GA by selecting the data of a commercial supermarket through simulation. (4) Conclude and explore some limitations of the method by comparing several different algorithms.

Research hypothesis

The mathematical model of the stochastic perturbation management problem can be described as comprising one distribution center and L known customer points, dispatching k vehicles to serve the L customer points, and k vehicles to depart from the distribution center, and subsequently return to the distribution center upon arrival at the destination, thereby completing the task delivery. In this scenario, all k transportation vehicles are of the same model and exhibit the same capacity. Stochastic perturbations of traffic congestion occur, which leads to difficulties in recovering from failures; thus, it becomes difficult to assign the transportation vehicles to the initial scheduling plan (i.e., certain customers will not be available in the desired time window), which may convert a customer’s time window constraint into a minimum time-window deviation.

The model assumptions are as follows: (1) The initial logistics and distribution program is known. (2) The perturbation factor is the time change of customer demand after the occurrence of traffic congestion. (3) The perturbation moment for the location of the vehicle’s traffic congestion at the beginning of the moment to the vehicle distribution completed distribution time period. (4) There are various types of vehicle resources in each distribution center, the number of which is not limited to meeting the distribution needs of all customers; the distribution center has sufficient vehicles. (5) Distribution vehicles have load restrictions; overloaded driving is not allowed to occur. (6) Different moments of the vehicle travel speed are different, while considering the waiting time for loading and unloading goods at each customer. (8) Each distribution vehicle can choose only an idle time window for a distribution service.

Logistics stochastic disturbance cost measurement

The occurrence of traffic congestion events leads to an increase in traffic flow through a point, a cross-section, or a roadway lane, which leads to an increase in carbon emissions in the roadway and also leads to an increase in the delivery time for delivery vehicles and an increase in the distance traveled for delivery, thereby leading to a significant consumption of energy and a significant loss of goods. Therefore, if companies want to meet the requirements of on-time performance in a low-carbon scenario, they must make rational adjustments to their scheduling strategies for potential congestion events and develop targeted programs aimed at bringing more profits to their companies.

Modeling of low-carbon logistics in the presence of stochastic perturbation terms

Construction of the improved algorithm and its optimization

Classical genetic algorithm

The genetic algorithm (GA) (Prof. Holland, 1975) is based on the survival of the fittest principle, whereby individuals with increasingly better fitness values are produced through generation-by-generation evolution. GA operate with the encoding of decision variables; the chromosomes of individuals correspond to the feasible solutions of the problem, and the population constitutes the entire set of feasible solutions. In this research problem, the objective function can be understood as the fitness function in the GA. The GA’s general process can be summarized in the following six steps, and the corresponding algorithmic flow is depicted in Fig. 1. Step 1:Population initialization. Set the algorithm operation parameters, such as the number of populations, the number of iterations, crossover, and mutation probability.Step 2:Calculate the fitness value. Perform the calculation of individual fitness as per the set fitness function.Step 3:Selection operation. Select the individual with higher fitness for crossover and mutation.Step 4:Crossover operation. Re-crossover a combination of individuals to form a new population by the set crossover operator.Step 5:Mutation operation. Prevent the elimination of excellent individuals to a certain extent by mutation operation.Step 6:Judge whether the termination condition is satisfied. If so, decode and output the optimal value in the current population, otherwise, return to step 2.Fig. 1Classical GA.

Classical GA.

Inevitably, the classical GA also exhibits some drawbacks: (1) It is easy to converge prematurely. The algorithm converges to the local optimal solution in the late run, which affects the GA’s overall performance. (2) The calculation process is complicated. (3) Large initial population dependence. How to speed up the algorithm’s convergence while maintaining the population’s diversity has always been a difficult problem in GA. Herein, the classical GA is optimized for the problem of slow search speed, and it is difficult to consider the diversity of the population.

Genetic algorithm-gaussian matrix mutation (GA-GMM)

The mutation operation is a key GA step, which can maintain the genetic diversity of individual populations, prevent the algorithm from falling into local optimality, enhance the algorithm’s global search ability, and avoid premature convergence. The traditional mutation operator finds it difficult to balance the convergence and global search ability. Therefore, a GMM operator based on the location relationship of outlets and demand information is proposed, which can utilize the a priori information of population mutation as the mutation direction guidance factor while maintaining the diversity of the population, thereby accelerating the evolution of the population in the direction of high adaptability and enhancing the algorithm’s convergence speed.

We adopts a real number coding method, transforming solutions into a chromosomal structure. The initial population is constituted by such single chromosomes.

After a perturbation effect occurs, to simultaneously maintain the speed requirements for real-time repair of the production dispatching plan and preserve the diversity of solutions and expand the solution space, this paper improves upon the GMM algorithm. We introduce strategies for optimal individual mutation, optimal solution updates, and the updating and preservation of Pareto optimal solutions to enhance solution diversity. Specific work is as follows:

Through this strategy, the GA-GMM algorithm can expand the search range in the neighborhood, effectively escape local optima, and promote the generation of global optimal solutions. Conclusion and discussion

Based on the existing research, this study conducts research on the disturbance management problem of low-carbon logistics and distribution, analyzes the impact of traffic congestion events on carbon tax costs and disturbance events, constructs a disturbance scheduling model for low-carbon logistics and distribution, and proposes an improved GA. By introducing the GMM operator, the distance of the outlets and the hidden bit order distribution characteristics of the outlets in the feasible solution are utilized as the guiding factors of individual gene mutation in the GA, thereby guiding the evolution of the population in the high adaptability direction. Simulation results utilizing real data from cold chain logistics companies indicate that the proposed algorithm can significantly enhance the algorithm’s convergence speed and reduce the algorithm’s time consumption while ensuring the solution’s accuracy.

VRP is multi-variant and multi-dimensional, and there will be a gap between the real data and the training data, which leads to inaccurate results and poor optimal gaps when the model is applied to instances, and it remains challenging to utilize GA-GMM for solving VRP and its variants in the future. (1) Existing learning-based methods train only specific path problems and generalize the solution to the range of specific path problems. Researchers should consider generalizing the trained path strategy to solve on different instances through the transfer learning technique, which can save a considerable amount of computational resources and enhance the utilization of the model. Further in-depth exploration of the intrinsic connection between different parameters and the establishment of the source-to-target domain mapping relationship between the VRP problem variants is a worthwhile research issue. (2) The existing GA-GMM model for solving VRP belongs to the end-to-end approach, and the solution process is similar to black-box optimization, which lacks corresponding theoretical guarantees. Finding a generalized architecture that effectively guarantees the feasibility of GA-GMM solving methods merits further evaluation and testing. The migration of GA-GMM and its network architecture to operation optimization algorithms can be considered to enhance the solution’s reliability. Therefore, continuing to explore the solution process of GA-GMM in-depth and enhancing the model’s interpretability merit research attention. (3) Most networks utilize uniformly distributed or randomly generated data to train the strategy network, and the trained model can be generalized to different problems. However, the model’s generalization ability may be related to the training data; if the distribution of the node positions is unknown or does not follow any distribution, it becomes challenging to construct a stable model. The construction of robust training models for VRP and its variants of the problem is a key research topic. Knowledge distillation methods should be considered as a method for enhancing the generalization ability of GA-GMM models under different data distributions. (4) GA-GMM contains a large number of hand-designed hyperparameters, and different parameters often exert a large impact on the model’s performance. To reduce the influence of hyper-parameters on the experimental results, an automatic GA-GMM parameter tuning technique is considered (i.e., it can be introduced into the model training). The control of model convergence by the automatic tuning of the learning rate and decay rate is potentially a crucial research topic.

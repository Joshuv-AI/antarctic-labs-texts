Multicenter Electronic Interactions Driving Active Phase Formation in NiFe-MOFs for Anion-Exchange Membrane Water Electrolysis
Authors: Jinzhi Jia, Yongyu Cha, Junfeng Huang, Denan Wang, Kailu Guo, Huijiao Wang, Jinhua Zhang, Hua Li, Muhammad Humayun, Siyu Lu, Jier Huang, Chundong Wang, Cailing Xu, Yu Tang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Multimetal doping is widely employed to enhance oxygen evolution reaction electrocatalysis; however, the underlying mechanism remains poorly understood due to the complex interplay. Herein, we rationally integrate rare-earth Ce and transition-metal Ru into NiFe-MOFs (Ce,Ru-NiFe-MOFs) to achieve orbital complementarity between localized 4f and delocalized 4d states, thereby enabling a fundamentally distinct electronic regulation strategy beyond conventional charge transfer. This coupling effect fundamentally alters the dynamic evolution of the catalyst by accelerating the transformation of NiFe-MOFs into active NiFeOOH species. We further demonstrate that Ce/Ru codoping induces oxygen-bridged multicenter orbital coupling (3d-4d-4f), as evidenced by soft- and hard-X-ray absorption spectroscopy and aberration-corrected high-angle annular dark-field scanning transmission electron microscopy, together with density functional theory calculations, thereby establishing nonadditive electronic interactions and multilevel electron-transfer pathways. Consequently, the reconstructed catalyst exhibits an optimized electronic structure with enhanced OH– adsorption, suppressed proton-induced corrosion, and reduced energy barrier for the rate-determining step. Therefore, the catalyst delivers an overpotential of 199 mV at 10 mA cm–2 and outstanding durability exceeding 1600 h at 400 mA cm–2. Additionally, when integrated into an anion-exchange membrane water electrolysis system, it achieves a low cell voltage of 1.747 V at 1000 mA cm–2 (meeting the U.S. DOE 2026 target) and maintains stable operation for over 95 h at 500 mA cm–2. This work establishes a clear mechanistic framework linking orbital-level coupling, reconstruction kinetics, and catalytic performance, and provides a general design principle for engineering advanced electrocatalysts via multicenter orbital interactions.

Introduction

Hydrogen, a clean fuel with ultrahigh energy density and zero carbon emissions, is widely recognized as a promising next-generation sustainable energy carrier, offering significant potential to mitigate global climate change and the serious energy crisis. − Among hydrogen production techniques, electrocatalytic water splitting has emerged as a highly attractive and sustainable strategy for large-scale hydrogen generation. , Nevertheless, the high energy barriers and sluggish kinetics of the anodic oxygen evolution reaction (OER) have been deemed to impede the efficiency of electrochemical systems. , In recent years, noble-metal catalysts such as Ru/RuO2 and Ir/IrO2 have demonstrated outstanding performance. − However, their scarcity, high cost, and insufficient stability severely hinder their large-scale application. − Therefore, developing cost-effective and robust alternatives is essential for scalable OER systems.

Metal–organic frameworks (MOFs), a class of coordination polymers composed of inorganic metal ions or clusters and organic linkers, have emerged as promising OER electrocatalysts owing to their tunable coordination environments, high porosity, and facile functionalization. − Nevertheless, pristine MOFs generally suffer from low conductivity, sluggish charge transfer, and limited active sites, leading to poor activity and stability. , Recently, heteroatom doping has been widely regarded as an efficient strategy to boost OER catalytic activity by precisely tuning the electronic structure. ,,, Thereinto, ruthenium (Ru), a typical 4d transition metal (TM), possesses a nearly ideal binding energy for oxygenated intermediates and exhibits outstanding intrinsic activity for OER. , In addition, the 4d orbital features enhanced spatial delocalization and a broadened electronic bandwidth relative to the 3d orbital, which strengthens its coupling with neighboring ligand/metal orbitals and affords greater tunability of the local electronic structure. This favorable electronic configuration facilitates the adsorption and activation of oxygen-containing intermediates, thereby enhancing the reaction kinetics. However, Ru-based catalysts often suffer from structural and chemical instability under strong oxidation conditions due to overoxidation and dissolution into soluble species such as RuO4 2–, especially during long-term water oxidation. These issues significantly compromise their operational durability. Thus, retaining the high activity of Ru-base catalysts while overcoming their instability remains a key challenge.

Notably, emerging studies indicate that multicenter electronic interactions featuring anisotropic electronic fields, particularly those arising from heteroatom (e.g., 3d-based TM, 4d-based TM, and 4f-based rare earth) combinations spanning distinct orbital types, can give rise to synergistic effects beyond simple charge redistribution. ,, Specifically, the close proximity of distinct metal centers strengthens multiorbital coupling beyond bimetallic systems, producing a tunable electronic structure for synergistic multisite activation. Moreover, multimetallic configurations offer intrinsic dynamic adaptability, enabling cooperative restructuring during operation and enhanced stability at high current densities. Inspired by this, incorporating rare-earth elements offers exceptional functional characteristics. , Cerium (Ce), in particular, possesses unique electronic characteristics, including localized 4f orbitals and flexible Ce3+/Ce4+ redox transitions, which not only provide electron buffering and stabilize metal centers but also facilitate multicenter electronic interactions with neighboring 3d/4d metals. − Taking inspiration from these insights, developing MOF-based electrocatalysts with Ce and Ru codoping, which allows multicenter electronic interactions, is crucial for efficient OER.

Growing attention has focused on the structural transformation of MOFs during the OER, now recognized as a critical factor governing catalytic performance. Most pristine MOFs undergo substantial reconstruction into amorphous (oxy)­hydroxides (MOOH) under OER conditions, which represent the true catalytically active phase. ,, However, the formation of robust and highly active layers is hindered by uncontrolled structural reconstruction, such as high activation barriers, ordered electronic structures, atomic rearrangement, metal leaching, and lattice expansion. ,,− Addressing these key scientific questions is essential for elucidating the catalytic mechanism and guiding the rational design of advanced MOF-based electrocatalysts.

Despite the limited use of multimetal doping in OER electrocatalysis, the orbital-level mechanism underlying synergistic interactions among metal centers remains unclear. Integrating rare-earth and transition-metal elements offers a promising route to engineer electronic structures via orbital complementarity, yet direct evidence and mechanistic insights are still lacking. Herein, we rationally design Ce/Ru-codoped NiFe-MOFs by coupling localized 4f orbitals of Ce with delocalized 4d orbitals of Ru to achieve orbital complementarity (Scheme a). Soft/hard-X-ray absorption spectroscopy, aberration-corrected high-angle annular dark-field scanning transmission electron microscopy (AC-HAADF-STEM), together with density functional theory calculations, demonstrate that this design induces oxygen-bridged 3d-4d-4f orbital coupling (Scheme b), giving rise to nonadditive electronic interactions and multilevel electron-transfer pathways Scheme that modulate the electronic structure, stabilize Ru centers, and lower the activation energy of the rate-determining step (RDS). As a result, the Ce,Ru-NiFe-MOFs catalyst exhibits outstanding OER performance and delivers high efficiency in anion-exchange membrane water electrolysis (AEMWE). This work establishes a mechanistic link among orbital-level coupling, electronic regulation, and catalytic performance and provides a general design principle for engineering advanced electrocatalysts via multicenter orbital interactions.

Results and Discussion

Synthesis and Characterization of Catalysts

First, the Ce,Ru-NiFe-MOFs was prepared through an in situ growth process on nickel foam (NF, Figure S1) using a modified solvothermal method (Figure a). , To investigate the significance of the synergistic effect, additional samples with individual Ce or Ru doping (denoted as Ce-NiFe-MOFs and Ru-NiFe-MOFs), as well as undoped NiFe-MOFs, were also synthesized using a similar method. The X-ray diffraction (XRD) pattern and Raman spectroscopy of the as-obtained samples indicate the successful synthesis of NiFe-MOFs in these samples (Figure S2). ,, Morphologically, Ce,Ru-NiFe-MOFs displays a 3D microsphere structure, which consists of cross-linked nanowires (Figure b), a finding further confirmed by the transmission electron microscopy (TEM, Figure S8a). Remarkably, the 3D porous microspheres enhance electrolyte transport, expose abundant active sites, and provide internal buffer space to maintain structural stability during OER. , The high-resolution TEM (HRTEM) image further shows the lattice fringe of MOFs with an interplane spacing of 1.08 nm, corresponding to the (200) plane of NiFe-MOFs (Figure c). Additionally, no discernible nanoparticles corresponding to Ce or Ru species were observed in the selected area electron diffraction (SAED) and HRTEM images (Figures c and S8b). Spherical AC-HAADF-STEM images reveal isolated Ce and Ru atoms, observed as bright spots highlighted by red circles, uniformly dispersed on the surface (Figure d). Energy dispersive X-ray (EDX) mapping images (Figure e) show the homogeneous distribution of Ni, Fe, Ce, Ru, C, and O elements throughout the nanowire, confirming the successful codoping of Ce and Ru, which is further supported by the SEM-EDX pattern (Figure S4d) and X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) survey spectra (Figure S9a). In addition, the morphology and structure of NiFe-MOFs, Ce-NiFe-MOFs, and Ru-NiFe-MOFs were also investigated (Figures S3–S7). The detailed XPS peak fitting results (Figures f–i and S9b) reveal the presence of Ni2+, Fe3+, Ce3+/Ce4+, and Ru3+ species in Ce,Ru-NiFe-MOFs. Notably, the Ni 2p 3/2 binding energy exhibits a slight positive shift of 0.4 eV for Ce–NiFe-MOFs compared with NiFe-MOFs, whereas negative shifts of 0.4 and 0.2 eV are observed for Ru-NiFe-MOFs and Ce,Ru-NiFe-MOFs, respectively, indicating a sequential charge transfer from Ru to Ni and further to Ce (Figure f). The electron-rich environment at Ni sites increases their susceptibility to oxidation under anodic potentials, thereby facilitating the formation of oxyhydroxide active species. For the Fe 2p spectra, Ce-NiFe-MOFs, Ru-NiFe-MOFs, and Ce,Ru-NiFe-MOFs exhibits a positive shift (0.2 eV) compared to NiFe-MOFs, confirming the charge transfer from Fe to Ce/Ru (Figure g). Additionally, compared to individual doping, Ce,Ru-NiFe-MOFs shows a negative shift in the Ru 3p peaks, whereas a positive shift is observed for Ce 3d, demonstrating a stronger electron coupling effect between Ce and Ru (Figures h,i and S9b). − These XPS observations indicate that codoping establishes multilevel electron-transfer channels in NiFe-MOFs through electron exchange among Ni, Fe, Ce, and Ru sites mediated by bridging oxygen atoms, thereby subsequently influencing the dynamic surface reconstruction during OER. ,,,

Electrocatalytic OER Performance

The OER performance of the as-prepared samples was evaluated in 1 M KOH electrolyte using a standard three-electrode system. For comparison, NF and commercial IrO2 were also tested under the same conditions. The linear sweep voltammetry (LSV) and cyclic voltammetry (CV) curves demonstrate that all the modified NiFe-MOFs electrodes exhibit significantly enhanced OER activity compared to the pristine NiFe-MOFs (Figures a and S10). Especially, Ce,Ru-NiFe-MOFs displays remarkably low overpotentials of 199, 223, 238, and 270 mV at current densities of 10, 50, 100, and 300 mA cm–2, respectively. These values are significantly lower than those of the NiFe-MOFs, Ce-NiFe-MOFs, and Ru-NiFe-MOFs (Figure b). As shown in Figure c, NiFe-MOFs exhibits a Tafel slope of 43.6 mV dec–1, close to the 40 mV dec–1 typically observed for oxidized Ni, Co, and Fe electrodes in alkaline media. , This indicates that the rate-determining step is the *OOH formation step. Upon Ce or Ru incorporation, the Tafel slopes decrease to 36.4 and 33.9 mV dec–1, respectively, while Ce,Ru-NiFe-MOFs further reduce the value to 30.5 mV dec–1. The comparable Tafel slopes indicate that the catalysts likely follow a similar OER pathway. Notably, Ce,Ru-NiFe-MOFs demonstrates superior catalytic performance compared to most previously reported active OER catalysts in terms of overpotential and Tafel slope, highlighting the crucial role of the synergistic effect between metal atoms (Figure d and Table S1). Moreover, the impact of Ce and Ru contents on the morphology, structure, and catalytic performance of the catalysts was also investigated (Figures S11–S18). Different amounts of precursors were employed to synthesize the catalysts, and the Ce,Ru-NiFe-MOFs electrode with a Ce3+ and Ru3+ ratio of 0.03:0.09 exhibits the most remarkable OER activity. In light of these facts, Ce0.03Ru0.09-NiFe-MOFs (denoted as Ce,Ru-NiFe-MOFs) was selected for further investigation.

As displayed in Figure e, the Ce,Ru-NiFe-MOFs electrode exhibits the smallest electron transfer resistance (Rct, 0.42 Ω), as compared to the NiFe-MOFs (0.75 Ω), Ce-NiFe-MOFs (0.51 Ω), and Ru-NiFe-MOFs (0.47 Ω), suggesting a synergistic enhancement of interfacial charge transfer by Ce/Ru codoping. This indicates that codoping creates a more efficient electron-transfer pathway within NiFe-MOFs, thereby significantly accelerating the electron transfer kinetics. Electrochemically active surface areas (ECSAs) of the samples were assessed through the double-layer capacitance (Cdl) derived from the CV curves at different scanning rates (Figure S20). As shown in Figure S21, the Cdl value of Ce,Ru-NiFe-MOFs (17.8 mF cm–2) is higher than that of Ru-NiFe-MOFs (14.0 mF cm–2), Ce-NiFe-MOFs (2.3 mF cm–2), and NiFe-MOFs (2.2 mF cm–2), indicating that the synergistic effect effectively increases the electrochemically active surface area by exposing more active sites. The durability measurements are performed at various constant current densities. At a current density of 10 mA cm–2, the overpotential of NiFe-MOFs, Ce-NiFe-MOFs, and Ce,Ru-NiFe-MOFs only increased by 18, 13, and 14 mV, respectively, following 200 h of continuous testing (Figure f). In contrast, Ru-NiFe-MOFs exhibited a significant increase by 51 mV. These results indicate that the introduction of Ce not only preserves the high catalytic activity of Ru but also enhances the structural stability. When the current density increases to 100 and 400 mA cm–2 (Figure g), and even at an elevated temperature of 60 °C (Figure S22), Ce,Ru-NiFe-MOFs shows negligible degradation in activity for over 1600 and 1000 h, respectively. Moreover, stepwise chronopotentiometry (CP) measurements demonstrate that the potential remains highly stable across all tested current densities, further evidencing the remarkable long-term durability of Ce,Ru-NiFe-MOFs (Figure h). Such robustness is particularly critical because renewable energy sources are inherently intermittent, variable, and random. Comparative studies with various dopants (V, Gd, Mo, W, and Mn) reveal that the superior performance stems from the unique oxygen-bridged 4f-4d-3d orbital coupling, which optimizes the electronic structure, accelerates phase transformation, and stabilizes Ru sites, features unattainable with conventional 3d transition metals (Figures S23–S24).

Study on Surface Reconstruction Behavior

To explore the active structure for boosting the OER, in situ Raman spectroscopy and electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) were employed to investigate the formation and transformation of active species during the OER. The potential-dependent Raman spectra and the corresponding 2D contour diagram are displayed in Figures a,b and S25–S27. In the case of NiFe-MOFs, when the applied potential increases from 1.00 to 1.40 V vs RHE, the characteristic Raman peaks of the original NiFe-MOFs gradually diminished, while the peak intensities of NiFe­(OH)2 (eg, 457 and 528 cm–1) consistently increased (Figure S25). This trend indicates a progressive destruction of the NiFe-MOFs crystal structure, dissociation of organic ligands, and emergence of NiFe­(OH)2. Subsequently, when the applied potential increases to 1.45 V vs RHE, the NiFe­(OH)2 peaks gradually weaken, while two new peaks emerge at 475 and 555 cm–1. These newly appearing peaks correspond to the bending vibration of Eg(δ­(NiIII–O)) and the tensile vibration of A1g(ν­(NiIII–O)) of NiFeOOH, respectively. ,, As the applied potential is further increased, there is a gradual increase in the peak intensity of NiFeOOH, suggesting the NiFe­(OH)2 is progressively transformed into NiFeOOH species. Interestingly, the Ce-NiFe-MOFs, Ru-NiFe-MOFs, and Ce,Ru-NiFe-MOFs electrodes display a similar process of structural evolution behavior (Figures and S26-S27). However, the peak intensity of NiFe­(OH)2 in Ce,Ru-NiFe-MOFs is significantly higher than that of pristine NiFe-MOFs, implying that the heteroatom decoration promotes the formation of NiFe­(OH)2, which further reduces the energy barrier of phase transformation from NiFe­(OH)2 to NiFeOOH. , The subsequent phase-transition potentials show that Ce,Ru-NiFe-MOFs exhibits the NiFeOOH peak at 1.35 V, which is lower than those of NiFe-MOFs (1.45 V), Ce-NiFe-MOFs (1.40 V), and Ru-NiFe-MOFs (1.40 V). In addition, the characteristic peak intensity of NiFeOOH further confirms that the comodification synergistically enhances the reconstruction kinetics, thereby promoting the formation of catalytically active NiFeOOH species. More importantly, the MOF-related signals disappear at 1.35 V for Ce,Ru-NiFe-MOFs, lower than those for NiFe-MOFs (1.55 V), Ce-NiFe-MOFs (1.50 V), and Ru-NiFe-MOFs (1.40 V), confirming faster reconstruction kinetics. These behaviors may be attributed to the combined effect of Ce and Ru substitution, which induces the formation of multilevel electron-transfer channels in NiFe-MOFs, enhancing electronic conductivity and activating electron-rich Ni sites. The above observations are further confirmed by cycle-dependent electrochemical quasi–in situ Raman spectra (Figures S28–S31).

(a) 2D contour diagram, (b) corresponding in situ Raman spectra, (c) Phase angle vs. log (frequency) plots, (d) Nyquist plots, and (e) schematic diagram of dynamic structure evolution of Ce,Ru-NiFe-MOFs during the OER.

Subsequently, in situ EIS measurements were conducted to verify this synergistically accelerated process. As shown in Figures c,d and S32–S34, potential-dependent Bode and Nyquist plots of Ce,Ru-NiFe-MOFs displays dual phase peaks at 1.374 V, which is lower than those of NiFe-MOFs (1.474 V), Ce-NiFe-MOFs (1.424 V), and Ru-NiFe-MOFs (1.424 V), indicating synergistically accelerated phase transformation and enhanced intrinsic activity. , Moreover, the fast decay of the phase angle and the pronounced reduction in the semicircle radius upon increasing voltage indicate that codoping synergistically promotes interfacial charge transfer, optimizes intermediate adsorption, and enhances OER kinetics. Additionally, this fast electron transfer is conducive to activating the M–O bond as an electronic channel that facilitates electron integration in the redox reactions occurring at different metal sites. These results collectively support the mechanistic model illustrated in Figure e, in which the Ce,Ru-NiFe-MOFs catalyst undergoes phase reconstruction during OER from the initial NiFe-MOFs to an interim NiFe­(OH)2 and finally to NiFeOOH, while anchoring Ce and Ru as atomically dispersed sites to form the Ce,Ru-NiFeOOH species.

Characterization of Reconstructed Species

Based on the above results, we designated NiFe-MOFs, Ce-NiFe-MOFs, Ru-NiFe-MOFs, and Ce,Ru-NiFe-MOFs after OER (cyclic sweeping of the CV curve measured at a scan rate of 5 mV s–1 in 1 M KOH) as NiFeOOH, Ce-NiFeOOH, Ru-NiFeOOH, and Ce,Ru-NiFeOOH, respectively. XRD and Raman analyses (Figures S35–S36) verify the formation of poorly crystalline NiFeOOH phases. Figure S37d shows that the Ce,Ru-NiFeOOH preserves the overall morphology of Ce,Ru-NiFe-MOFs, confirming its structural robustness during OER, facilitated by the 3D porous framework and rapid electron transport. However, the nanosheets in NiFeOOH, Ce-NiFeOOH, and Ru-NiFeOOH appear fluffier than those of the original MOF structures (Figure S37a –c). Those nanosheets and nanowires were also confirmed by TEM images (Figures a, S38a, S39a, and S40a). The HRTEM image of NiFeOOH displays the lattice fringes with the interplanar spacings of 2.09 and 2.38 Å, which are assigned to the (111) and (011) planes of NiFeOOH, respectively (Figure S38c). The corresponding fast Fourier transform (FFT) and SAED patterns of NiFeOOH demonstrate the diffraction patterns of (011), (101), (210), and (211) planes of NiFeOOH (Figure S38b,c), further revealing that the in situ-formed NiFeOOH has a polycrystalline nature. The HRTEM images, SAED patterns, and corresponding FTT patterns of Ce-NiFeOOH, Ru-NiFeOOH, and Ce,Ru-NiFeOOH show similar interplanar spacing and the matched diffraction rings with NiFeOOH, revealing the same crystal structure after the introduction of Ce and/or Ru (Figures b–d and S39–S40). Interestingly, no apparent nanoparticles of CeO2, metal Ru, or RuO2 were observed, further ruling out the formation of nanoparticles associated with Ce and Ru species. The AC-HAADF-STEM image of Ce,Ru-NiFeOOH identifies isolated bright spots against the NiFeOOH support, which correspond to single Ce or Ru atoms (Figures e and S41). Based on element-specific electron scattering cross sections (Ru, Z = 44 and Ce, Z = 58), the bright spots with lower brightness are assigned to Ru atoms (highlighted with blue circles), and those with higher brightness to Ce atoms (highlighted with yellow circles). Moreover, the correlation line of intensity and brightness further verified these results (Figure f). Notably, Ce and Ru atoms preferentially form Ce/Ru dual-atom sites. Within these sites, the signals for Ru are of lower intensity, while those for Ce are of higher intensity, with both exhibiting greater contrast than those from Ni and Fe. This configuration is believed to facilitate rapid electron transfer not only between the Ce and Ru sites, but also among Ni, Fe, Ce, and Ru atoms in Ce,Ru-NiFeOOH, building a fast electron-transfer channel. , In addition, AC-HAADF-STEM images of Ce-NiFeOOH and Ru-NiFeOOH reveal the atomically dispersed Ce and Ru atoms, respectively (Figures S39c and S40d), further confirmed by the correlation line of intensity and brightness (Figures S39d and S40f). During the structural evolution process, the dissolution and redeposition of NiFe-MOFs generate abundant anchoring sites to stabilize Ce and Ru atoms, enabling the formation of atomically dispersed catalysts. EDX element mapping images indicate that the Ce, Ru, Ni, Fe, and O elements are uniformly distributed over the whole nanosheet (Figures g, S38d, S39e, and S40e). Quantitative analysis by inductively coupled plasma-atomic emission spectrometry (ICP-AES) revealed that the contents of Ru and Ce elements are 2.63 and 1.11 wt % for Ce,Ru-NiFeOOH, respectively (Table S2).

Electronic Properties of Reconstructed Species

As revealed in Figure a, the high-resolution Ni 2p XPS spectrum of NiFeOOH shows two peaks at ∼856.0 and 873.6 eV, accompanied by two satellite peaks at ∼861.6 and 879.3 eV, which are attributed to Ni3+ 2p 3/2 and Ni3+ 2p 1/2, respectively. , For the high-resolution Fe 2p spectrum of NiFeOOH (Figure b), the peaks at ∼712.5 and 725.7 eV are assigned to the Fe3+ 2p 3/2 and Fe3+ 2p 1/2, and the peak at 718.5 eV is ascribed to the satellite peak. , Notably, the binding energy of Fe 2p 3/2 and Ni 2p 3/2 for Ce-NiFeOOH, Ru-NiFeOOH, and Ce,Ru-NiFeOOH exhibits a positive shift compared to that of NiFeOOH. More importantly, the most noticeable shift is observed in Ce,Ru-NiFeOOH among the samples, suggesting that the decoration of dual-atom may play a more significant role than single metal decoration in modifying the electron structure of Fe and Ni atoms. , In the Ru 3p XPS spectrum of Ru-NiFeOOH (Figure c), the peaks at ∼463.9 and 486.7 eV correspond to Ru3+ 3p 3/2 and Ru3+ 3p 1/2. Additionally, the C 1s&Ru 3d XPS spectrum of Ru-NiFeOOH (Figure S42b-c) displays two peaks at ∼282.6 and 287.2 eV, which can be attributed to Ru3+ 3d 5/2 and Ru3+ 3d 3/2. Notably, a negative shift in Ru3+ 3d and Ru3+ 3p binding energies is observed for Ce,Ru-NiFeOOH compared to the Ru-NiFeOOH, demonstrating that Ce atoms regulate the electron structure of Ru atoms. Furthermore, the high-resolution Ce 3d spectrum of Ce-NiFeOOH can be fitted with multipeaks attributed to Ce3/4+ 3d 5/2 and Ce3/4+ 3d 3/2, signifying the coexistence of Ce3+ and Ce4+ (Figure d). Conspicuously, compared to the Ce-NiFeOOH, Ce,Ru-NiFeOOH exhibits a higher ratio of Ce3+ to Ce4+, promoting electron transfer. ,

X-ray absorption spectroscopy (XAS) was employed to probe the local electronic configuration and coordination environment underlying the intrinsic catalytic performance. Figure e–h shows the X-ray near-edge absorption structure (XANES) spectra. As depicted in Figure e, the Ni K-edge XANES spectra of reconstructed samples display a higher absorption edge energy than NiO, suggesting the oxidation state of Ni is higher than Ni2+. The Fe K-edge XANES spectra reveal that the positions of the rising edges of all reconstructed samples are higher than that of Fe2O3, indicating that the average positive oxidation state is higher than Fe3+ (Figure f). Notably, the absorption edge of Ce-NiFeOOH, Ru-NiFeOOH, and Ce,Ru-NiFeOOH shifted toward higher energy compared to that of the NiFeOOH. More interestingly, compared to Ce-NiFeOOH and Ru-NiFeOOH, Ce,Ru-NiFeOOH exhibits the highest oxidation state for both Ni and Fe. These results indicate that dual-atom decoration is more effective in regulating the electron structure of Ni and Fe atoms than the individual Ce or Ru, which agrees well with the XPS results. The electron deficiency of Ni and Fe in Ce,Ru-NiFeOOH accelerates *OH adsorption, thereby generating an alkaline-enriched surface environment that suppresses chemical corrosion induced by H+ accumulation. − For the Ru K-edge XANES spectra, the absorption edges of Ru-NiFeOOH and Ce,Ru-NiFeOOH are located at a position between Ru foil and RuO2, indicating the oxidation state of Ruδ+ (0 < δ < 4). Significantly, the absorption edge of Ce,Ru-NiFeOOH exhibits a slight shift toward lower energy compared with that of Ru-NiFeOOH, indicating a decreased oxidation state (Figure g). This mitigates overoxidation and prevents the dissolution of Ru active sites, thereby enhancing the stability of the catalyst. As displayed in Figure h, the absorption edge of Ce L 3-edge for Ce-NiFeOOH and Ce,Ru-NiFeOOH is lower than that of CeO2, signifying the oxidation state of Ceδ+ (δ < 4). Conspicuously, a slight shift of Ce L 3-edge XANES to lower energy can be observed in Ce,Ru-NiFeOOH compared to Ce-NiFeOOH, implying an increase in the charge density around the Ce atom. The presence of Ce3+ /Ce4+ redox electron pairs can accelerate electron transportation. These findings suggest that codoping establishes an efficient electron-transfer pathway within NiFeOOH through strongly O-mediated 4d-4f-3d orbital couplings. , Fourier transform extended X-ray absorption fine structure (FT-EXAFS) spectra are exhibited in Figure i–l, and the corresponding fitting results are listed in Table S3. The FT-EXAFS curves of Ni and Fe K-edge for NiFeOOH display two obvious coordination peaks at ∼1.5 and 2.7 Å (Figure i,j), which are attributed to the Ni/Fe–O scattering paths in the first shell and the Ni/Fe-M* in the second shell (where M* represents Ni, Fe, Ce, or Ru elements). ,, Notably, Ce-NiFeOOH, Ru-NiFeOOH, and Ce,Ru-NiFeOOH exhibit similar Ni and Fe K-edge FT-EXAFS spectra compared to the NiFeOOH, providing further evidence of the well-preserved crystal structure in NiFeOOH, which is consistent with the results of K3-weighted EXAFS spectra of Ni/Fe K-edge (Figure S43a-b).

The intensities and coordination numbers of Fe–O and Ni–O in Ce,Ru-NiFeOOH are similar to those in Ce-NiFeOOH, Ru-NiFeOOH, and NiFeOOH, indicating that dual-atom doping does not significantly alter the local coordination environment. Similar conclusions are also evidenced by the wavelet transform EXAFS (WT-EXAFS) results (Figures S44–S45). Furthermore, the elongation of average Fe–O and Ni–O bonds implies that Ru and Ce incorporation perturbs the local coordination environment, thereby enhancing the electronic activity of adjacent Ni and Fe centers. For Ru K-edge FT-EXAFS of Ce,Ru-NiFeOOH, the main peak at ∼1.5 Å corresponds to Ru–O backscattering path, while the other peak at ∼2.95 Å can be attributed to the second shell of Ru-M (Figure k). , In contrast to the EXAFS spectra of Ru foil and RuO2, Ru–Ru coordination is not observed in that of Ru-NiFeOOH and Ce,Ru-NiFeOOH, confirming the atomic dispersion of Ru species, which is further proved by WT-EXAFS analyses (Figure S46). Additionally, Ce,Ru-NiFeOOH displays a slightly longer distance and a higher peak intensity of Ru–O compared to Ru-NiFeOOH, which is owing to the electronic interaction between Ru and Ce. , In the FT-EXAFS spectra of Ce L 3-edge for Ce-NiFeOOH and Ce,Ru-NiFeOOH (Figure l), the peaks at ∼1.90 Å and 3.4/3.6 Å correspond to the Ce–O and Ce-M scattering paths, respectively. The average bond length of Ce–O in Ce,Ru-NiFeOOH is longer than that of Ce-NiFeOOH, which is consistent with the WT-EXAFS results (Figure S47). The reciprocal elongation of Ce–O and Ru–O bonds indicates cooperative reconstruction of the local coordination environment, suggesting that Ce and Ru do not act as electronically isolated dopants but instead establish an oxygen-bridged Ce–O–Ru motif that provides the structural basis for multicenter orbital interaction.

Soft XAS (total-electron-yield mode) was employed to probe multiorbital interactions. − The O K-edge reflects O 2p hybridization with metal orbitals: A1 (∼530.5 eV) and A2 (∼531.6 eV) arise from Ni/Fe 3d t2g and eg states, respectively, while A4 originates from hybridization with 4s/4p states (Figure S48a). Ce doping selectively enhances A2, whereas Ru strengthens A1, indicating orbital-selective modulation; both also increase A4 and induce an A3 shoulder potentially associated with Ce 4f and Ru 4d contributions. Notably, codoping simultaneously amplifies A1, A2, A3, and A4, deviating from linear additivity, as evidenced by the inability to reproduce its spectral features via linear superposition of the singly doped samples. Ni/Fe L-edge spectra show energy shifts and variations of L 3/L 2 intensity ratio, collectively confirming increased oxidation states and charge redistribution. These spectroscopic signatures consistently support the presence of oxygen-mediated multicenter orbital coupling in the codoped system (Figure S48b,c). Based on the experimental results, we modeled Ni–O–Ru–O–Ce–O–Fe units to uncover the synergistic interactions in the Ce,Ru-NiFeOOH (Figure S49). In sharp contrast to directly synthesized NiFeOOH, which suffers from severe aggregation and poor activity, our reconstructed NiFeOOH features a porous architecture with various advantages and delivers substantially lower overpotential and superior long-term durability (Figures S50–S63 and Tables S4–S5).

Experimental Study of the OER Pathway

In situ Raman spectroscopy was further employed to probe the dynamic evolution of active species and the interfacial water structure of the reconstructed catalysts during OER. Two characteristic peaks (475 and ∼555 cm–1) of all reconstructed catalysts were observed with increasing applied potentials from OCP to 1.70 V, indicating that the active phase remains the in situ-formed NiFeOOH phase (Figure S64). Benefiting from the introduction of Ce/Ru, the Ce,Ru-NiFeOOH displays higher 2-HB·H2O content and lower 4-HB·H2O content than those of the NiFeOOH, Ce-NiFeOOH, and Ru-NiFeOOH electrocatalysts during the OER (Figures S65–S68), revealing that codoping contributes to the disruption of the hydrogen bond network with improved H2O dissociation and increased *OH supply, suppressing metallic dissolution. Furthermore, in situ EIS was conducted to probe the favorable electron transfer that enhances the intrinsic catalytic activity (Figures S69–S72). Ce,Ru-NiFeOOH exhibits greater OH– accumulation than NiFeOOH, Ce-NiFeOOH, and Ru-NiFeOOH, creating an alkaline-enriched surface environment that suppresses chemical corrosion induced by H+ accumulation. Ce,Ru-NiFeOOH exhibits two-phase peaks at a more negative potential of 1.40 V compared to the NiFeOOH (1.45 V), Ce-NiFeOOH (1.45 V), and Ru-NiFeOOH (1.42 V), demonstrating that the dual-atom decoration more effectively enhances the intrinsic catalytic activity. Moreover, the phase angle of Ce,Ru-NiFeOOH in the low-frequency regions reduces more rapidly with the increase of bias voltage from 1.40 to 1.65 V, indicating fast adsorption kinetics of oxygen-containing intermediates due to the favorable electron transfer, namely, the improved OER kinetics. , These results are further supported by the rapid reduction in the semicircle radius with increasing voltage, as shown in the corresponding Nyquist plots. Mass transfer at the electrode–electrolyte interface is crucial for electrochemical gas evolution reactions, especially at high current densities; therefore, contact angle and gas evolution measurements were conducted to evaluate the surface wettability and gas release behavior. , The lower contact angle observed for Ce,Ru-NiFeOOH (55.414°) compared to NiFeOOH (89.294°), Ce-NiFeOOH (72.781°), and Ru-NiFeOOH (69.976°) suggests enhanced hydrophilicity, which facilitates electrolyte wetting and promotes OER kinetics (Figures S73). Moreover, Ce,Ru-NiFeOOH shows significantly smaller and more rapidly detaching oxygen bubbles than NiFeOOH, Ce-NiFeOOH, and Ru-NiFeOOH (Videos S1–S4).

The in situ attenuated total reflection surface-enhanced infrared absorption spectroscopy (SR-FTIR) was employed to elucidate the reaction mechanism. As shown in Figure S77, the Ce,Ru-NiFeOOH exhibits enhanced absorption peaks at approximately 1125, 1218, 1635, and 3378 cm–1, corresponding to the *O–O, *OOH, *O, and OHads intermediates, respectively, indicating that the generation of OER intermediates increases with rising applied potential. , The peak at 1125 cm–1 is assigned to linearly bonded superoxide species (*O–O), which are the intermediates before releasing O2. Notably, the continuous accumulation of the *OOH intermediates under OER conditions suggests that Ce,Ru-NiFeOOH facilitates the formation of key reaction intermediates. Furthermore, NiFeOOH, Ce-NiFeOOH, and Ru-NiFeOOH exhibit identical characteristic peaks across different potentials, indicating that Ce and/or Ru doping does not alter the catalytic pathway (Figures S74–S76). These results are further supported by experiments on pH dependence and tetramethylammonium hydroxide (TMAOH, Figures S78–S81). To obtain more direct mechanistic evidence, isotope-labeling studies were subsequently performed. Control experiments were first conducted to validate the reliability of the isotope-tracing measurements. Activation-stage differential electrochemical mass spectrometry (DEMS) confirmed successful incorporation of 18O into the reconstructed catalyst (Figure S82), while in situ Raman measurements demonstrated negligible isotope exchange under conditions relevant to the isotope-tracing experiments (Figure S83), confirming the stability of the isotope label. Following these validation experiments, isotope-tracing DEMS measurements were carried out in H2 16O-based electrolyte. As shown in Figure S84, oxygen evolution was dominated by m/z = 32 (16O16O). A minor m/z = 34 (16O18O, ∼0.45%) signal was observed, which is consistent with the natural isotopic abundance of 18O in the H2 16O-based electrolyte, whereas the m/z = 36 (18O18O) signal was essentially absent. These observations strongly support that oxygen evolution proceeds predominantly through the conventional adsorbate evolution mechanism (AEM), with negligible lattice oxygen participation under the investigated reaction conditions.

Theoretical Validation of the OER Mechanism

Density functional theory (DFT) simulations on the model structures of reconstructed catalysts were conducted to elucidate the origin of their exceptional OER catalytic activity (Figure S85). To distinguish simple electronic perturbations from genuine multicenter coupling, crystal orbital Hamiltonian population (COHP) analysis was conducted. , As shown in Figure S86, the integreted ICOHP (ICOHP) value of the Ce 4f-O 2p bond in Ce,Ru-NiFeOOH (−0.59 eV) is significantly more negative than that in Ce-NiFeOOH (−0.36 eV), indicating strengthened Ce–O bonding. Meanwhile, the Ru–O ICOHP changes markedly from −1.26 eV in Ru-NiFeOOH to −2.69 eV in Ce,Ru-NiFeOOH, revealing enhanced bonding interactions. Such nonadditive modulation of both Ce–O and Ru–O bonds provides clear evidence for multicenter orbital coupling, featuring delocalized electronic interactions across the Ce–O–Ru unit and supporting the formation of coupled 3d-4d-4f states mediated by bridging oxygen atoms. The partial density of states (PDOS) analysis shows that Ce,Ru-NiFeOOH has higher electron-occupied states and a narrower bandgap. Together with the enhanced orbital coupling among Ni 3d, Fe 3d, Ru 4d, Ce 4f, and O 2p orbitals, this effect facilitates electron transfer within the catalyst and promotes charge exchange with reaction intermediates (Figures a–d and S87). ,, The d/f band centers of all active sites reveal that the introduction of dual-atom regulates the d/f band centers of metal sites (Figures S88–S89). Moreover, the calculated d-band center for NiFeOOH, Ce-NiFeOOH, Ru-NiFeOOH, and Ce,Ru-NiFeOOH are −1.501, −1.621, −1.775, and −1.891 eV (Figures e–h and S90), respectively. The downward shift of the d-band center in Ce,Ru-NiFeOOH could weaken the adsorption of oxygen-containing intermediates. , Upon introducing Ce/Ru dual-atom sites into NiFeOOH, Ce,Ru-NiFeOOH exhibits more pronounced charge accumulation around the Ce/Ru atoms and charge dissipation around the Ni/Fe atoms, thereby promoting charge transfer between the Ni and Fe centers (Figure i-l), which is further confirmed by Bader charge analysis (Figure m–q and Table S6). More importantly, an electron transfer of 0.17 e– from Ce to Ru is observed, suggesting strong interatomic electronic interaction. These findings further demonstrate that oxygen-bridged 4d–4f–3d orbital coupling enables efficient electron transfer and charge transport, resulting in enhanced conductivity and accelerated OER kinetics. ,

The optimal adsorption structures at various catalytically active sites and the OER reaction pathways are shown in Figures S91–S94. Subsequently, we calculated the OER free energy for 12 metal sites across four structure models (Figures r, S95–S99, and Tables S7–S8). The third step (*O + OH– → *OOH + e–) in pure NiFeOOH exhibits a high free energy barrier of 0.84 and 1.33 V for the Ni and Fe sites, respectively, at an equilibrium potential of 1.23 V in four steps, making it the rate-determining step. Notably, the codoping significantly reduces the free-energy barrier of the rate-determining step compared to individual doping. Importantly, the single Ru site in Ce,Ru-NiFeOOH shows the lowest limiting barrier of 0.42 eV, indicating that the Ru sites act as the main active site for driving OER. Other metal sites are also activated, exhibiting lower energy barriers. These results indicate that Ru functions as a highly active catalytic center embedded within the NiFeOOH framework, while simultaneously promoting the activity of neighboring sites. The enhanced OER performance is therefore attributed to the optimized electronic structure induced by strong oxygen-bridged 4d-4f-3d orbital coupling, which effectively accelerates reaction kinetics.

Alkaline Water Electrolyzer Performance

Encouraged by the excellent OER performance of Ce,Ru-NiFe-MOFs, a single-cell AEMWE was assembled with this catalyst as the anode to assess its performance under realistic device conditions (active area: 1 cm2, 1 M KOH, Figure a). Figure b presents the polarization (current–voltage) curves of the AEM electrolyzer measured at 80 °C, employing Ce,Ru-NiFe-MOFs as the anode catalyst and commercial Pt/C as the cathodic catalyst. Impressively, the Ce,Ru-NiFe-MOFs-based electrolyzer delivers industrially relevant current densities of 1000 and 2000 mA cm–2 at a low cell voltage of 1.747 and 1.977 V, respectively. Crucially, the achieved cell voltage satisfies the U.S. Department of Energy (DOE) 2026 target for liquid alkaline electrolysis (1.80 V at 1000 mA cm–2) and surpasses most recently reported OER catalysts (Table S9). Notably, the Ce,Ru-NiFe-MOFs || Pt/C electrolyzer maintains nearly constant activity without discernible performance decay at an industrial-level current density of 500 mA cm–2 over 95 h of continuous operation in 1 M KOH at 60 °C (Figures c and S100), demonstrating excellent stability and strong potential for practical large-scale hydrogen production. Various post-AEMWE characterizations (Figures S101–S105 and Table S10) confirm the robust structural stability of Ce,Ru-NiFeOOH under OER conditions.

Conclusion

In summary, we report a Ce,Ru-NiFe-MOFs electrocatalyst that achieves outstanding OER activity and durability by constructing a synergistic O-mediated multicenter electronic coupling network. The incorporation of Ru and Ce into the NiFe-MOFs framework enables controllable structural reconstruction into an active NiFeOOH phase at lower potentials, while simultaneously stabilizing atomically dispersed heterometal centers. Combined experimental and theoretical analyses reveal that strong 4d-4f-3d orbital coupling induces efficient electron redistribution. This electronic synergy elevates the oxidation states of Ni and Fe, stabilizes Ru against overoxidation, and promotes rapid electron transport. Concurrently, the reconstructed catalyst exhibits accelerated OH– accumulation, disruption of the hydrogen-bond network, optimized adsorption of oxygen-containing intermediates, and a reduced energy barrier for the rate-determining step, collectively leading to enhanced OER kinetics and stability. As a result, the Ce,Ru-NiFe-MOFs catalyst delivers an ultralow overpotential of 199 mV at 10 mA cm–2 and exceptional durability exceeding 1600 h at 100 and 400 mA cm–2. When implemented in an AEMWE, the catalyst achieves industrially relevant performance with a low cell voltage of 1.747 V at 1000 mA cm–2 and stable operation for over 95 h at 500 mA cm–2. Overall, this work demonstrates that rational integration of rare-earth and transition-metal elements enables O-mediated multicenter electronic interactions and controlled reconstruction in MOF-based catalysts, providing mechanistic insights for designing durable electrocatalysts for industrial water electrolysis at high current densities.

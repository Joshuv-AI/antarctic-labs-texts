Ground state energies of multipartite p p -spin models – partially lifted RDT view
Mihailo Stojnic

Abstract




Abstract

We consider ground state energies (GSE) of multipartite
p
-spin models. Relying on partially lifted random duality theory (pl RDT) concepts we introduce an analytical mechanism that produces easy to compute lower and upper GSE bounds for
any
spin sets. We uncover that these bounds actually match in case of fully spherical sets thereby providing optimal GSE values for spherical multipartite pure
p
-spin models. Numerical evidence further suggests that our upper and lower bounds may match even in the Ising scenarios. As such developments are rather intriguing, we formulate several questions regarding the connection between our bounds matching generality on the one side and the spin sets structures on the other.

Index Terms: Multipartite p-spin models; Ground state energy; Random duality theory
.

2
Main results

We start by recalling that for an integer
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, standard normal matrix
A\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times n}
, and set
{\mathbb{B}}^{n}\triangleq\left\{{\bf x}|{\bf x}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n},{\bf x}_{i}^{2}=\frac{1}{n}\right\}
(the vertices of the
n
-dimensional binary cube), the SK’s ground state energy is given as

\displaystyle\xi^{0}_{sk}=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}{\mathbb{E}}_{A}\max_{{\bf x}\in{\mathbb{B}}^{n}}{\bf x}^{T}A{\bf x}.

(1)

The spherical analogue is

\displaystyle\xi^{0}_{sph}=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}{\mathbb{E}}_{A}\max_{{\bf x}\in{\mathbb{S}}^{n}}{\bf x}^{T}A{\bf x},

(2)

where
{\mathbb{S}}^{n}=\{{\bf x}|\|{\bf x}\|_{2}=1\}
, is the unit sphere in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. One then easily recognizes a generic analogue

\displaystyle\xi^{0}({\mathcal{S}})=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}{\mathbb{E}}_{A}\max_{{\bf x}\in{\mathcal{S}}}{\bf x}^{T}A{\bf x},

(3)

where
{\mathcal{S}}\subseteq{\mathbb{S}}^{n}
(as will be clear throughout the presentation, restricting
{\mathcal{S}}
to the unit sphere is not needed but will make the writing way more elegant; along the same lines, to further lighten the presentation, the subscript next to
{\mathbb{E}}
indicating the source of randomness will often be omitted as well). Clearly,
\xi^{0}_{sk}=\xi^{0}({\mathbb{B}}^{n})
and
\xi^{0}_{sph}=\xi^{0}({\mathbb{S}}^{n})
.

The above concepts assume the so-called
2
-spin interactions. Extension to
p
-spin (
p\geq 2
) gives

\displaystyle\xi^{0}(p;{\mathcal{S}})=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}{\mathbb{E}}_{A}\max_{{\bf x}\in{\mathcal{S}}}\sum_{i_{1},i_{2},\dots,i_{p}=1}^{n}A_{i_{1},i_{2},\dots,i_{p}}\prod_{j=1}^{p}{\bf x}_{i_{j}},

(4)

with
A_{i_{1},i_{2},\dots,i_{p}}
being independent standard normals and
{\bf x}_{i_{j}}
being the
i_{j}
-th component of
{\bf x}
. Clearly, by definition,
\xi^{0}({\mathcal{S}})\triangleq\xi^{0}(2;{\mathcal{S}})
. Our interest is in the so-called multipartite
p
-spin models where
j
-th interacting spins belong to (potentially different) sets
{\mathcal{S}}^{(j)},1\leq j\leq p
. The corresponding ground state free energy is then given as

\displaystyle\xi(p;\bar{\mathcal{S}})=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}{\mathbb{E}}_{A}\max_{{\bf x}^{(j)}\in{\mathcal{S}}^{(j)}}\sum_{i_{1},i_{2},\dots,i_{p}=1}^{n}A_{i_{1},i_{2},\dots,i_{p}}\prod_{j=1}^{p}{\bf x}_{i_{j}}^{(j)},

(5)

where
\bar{\mathcal{S}}=\left({\mathcal{S}}^{(j)}\right)_{j=1,\dots,p}
is a
p
-dimensional array consisting of sets
{\mathcal{S}}^{(j)}
.

The following theorem provides a generic
non-asymptotic
strategy to upper/lower bound
\xi(p;\bar{\mathcal{S}})
.

Theorem 1
.

Consider
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
p\in 2{\mathbb{N}}
. Let
A_{i_{1},i_{2},\dots,i_{p}}\sim{\mathcal{N}}(0,1)
for any choice of integers
i_{j}\in\{1,2,\dots,n\},1\leq j\leq p
. Also, let
g
be a standard normal variable and for
j\in\{1,2,\dots,p\}
let
{\bf g}^{(j)}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times 1}
and
A^{(j)}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times n}
be comprised of standard normals. Assume that all random variables are independent among themselves. Moreover, let
{\mathcal{S}}^{(j)}\subseteq{\mathbb{S}}^{n},1\leq j\leq p
and for real scalars
c_{3}>0
,
c_{3,u}>0
, and
c_{3,l}>0
set
\bar{{\bf g}}\triangleq\left({\bf g}^{(j)}\right)_{j=1,\dots,p}
,
\bar{A}\triangleq\left(A^{(j)}\right)_{j=1,\dots,p}
,
\bar{\mathcal{S}}\triangleq\left({\mathcal{S}}^{(j)}\right)_{j=1,\dots,p}
, and

\displaystyle\varphi(p;\bar{\mathcal{S}},n)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max_{{\bf x}^{(j)}\in{\mathcal{S}}^{(j)}}\sum_{i_{1},i_{2},\dots,i_{p}=1}^{n}A_{i_{1},i_{2},\dots,i_{p}}\prod_{j=1}^{p}{\bf x}_{i_{j}}^{(j)}

\displaystyle\xi(p;\bar{\mathcal{S}},n,c_{3})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\frac{1}{c_{3}}\log\left({\mathbb{E}}_{A}e^{c_{3}\varphi(p;\bar{\mathcal{S}},n)}\right)

\displaystyle\xi_{u}(p;\bar{\mathcal{S}},n,c_{3})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\left(-\frac{c_{3}}{2}(p-1)+\frac{1}{c_{3}}\log\left({\mathbb{E}}_{\bar{{\bf g}}}e^{c_{3}\max_{{\bf x}^{(j)}\in{\mathcal{S}}^{(j)}}\sum_{j=1}^{p}\left({\bf g}^{(j)}\right)^{T}{\bf x}^{(j)}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\xi_{l}(p;\bar{\mathcal{S}},n,c_{3})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\frac{1}{c_{3}}\log\left({\mathbb{E}}_{\bar{A}}e^{\sqrt{p}^{-1}c_{3}\max_{{\bf x}^{(j)}\in{\mathcal{S}}^{(j)}}\sum_{j=1}^{p}\sum_{i_{1},i_{2},\dots,i_{p}=1}^{n}A^{(j)}_{i_{1},i_{2},\dots,i_{p}}\prod_{k=1}^{p}{\bf x}_{i_{k}}^{(j)}}\right).

(6)

Then

\displaystyle\xi_{l}(p;\bar{\mathcal{S}},n,c_{3,l})\leq\xi(p;\bar{\mathcal{S}},n,c_{3,l})\quad\mbox{and}\quad\xi(p;\bar{\mathcal{S}},n,c_{3,u})\leq\xi_{u}(p;\bar{\mathcal{S}},n,c_{3,u}).

(7)

Proof.

We split the proof into two parts. The first part relates to the upper bound and the second to the lower bound.

Upper bound
: We define two centered Gaussian processes indexed by array
\bar{{\bf x}}=\left({\bf x}^{(j)}\right)_{j=1,\dots,p}

\displaystyle{\mathcal{G}}(\bar{{\bf x}})

\displaystyle\triangleq

\displaystyle{\mathcal{G}}({\bf x}^{(1)},{\bf x}^{(2)},\dots,{\bf x}^{(p)})\triangleq\sum_{i_{1},i_{2},\dots,i_{p}=1}^{n}A_{i_{1},i_{2},\dots,i_{p}}\prod_{j=1}^{p}{\bf x}_{i_{j}}^{(j)}+g\sqrt{p-1}

\displaystyle{\mathcal{G}}_{u}(\bar{{\bf x}})

\displaystyle\triangleq

\displaystyle{\mathcal{G}}_{u}({\bf x}^{(1)},{\bf x}^{(2)},\dots,{\bf x}^{(p)})\triangleq\sum_{j=1}^{p}\left({\bf g}^{(j)}\right)^{T}{\bf x}^{(j)}.

(8)

Taking two arrays
\bar{{\bf x}}^{(a_{1})}=\left({\bf x}^{(j,a_{1})}\right)_{j=1,\dots,p}
and
\bar{{\bf x}}^{(a_{2})}=\left({\bf x}^{(j,a_{2})}\right)_{j=1,\dots,p}
we then write

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}{\mathcal{G}}(\bar{{\bf x}}^{(a_{1})}){\mathcal{G}}(\bar{{\bf x}}^{(a_{2})})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{p}\sum_{i_{j}=1}^{n}{\bf x}_{i_{j}}^{(j,a_{1})}{\bf x}_{i_{j}}^{(j,a_{2})}+p-1=\prod_{j=1}^{p}\left({\bf x}^{(j,a_{1})}\right)^{T}{\bf x}^{(j,a_{2})}+p-1

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}{\mathcal{G}}_{u}(\bar{{\bf x}}^{(a_{1})}){\mathcal{G}}_{u}(\bar{{\bf x}}^{(a_{2})})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{p}\sum_{i_{j}=1}^{n}{\bf x}_{i_{j}}^{(j,a_{1})}{\bf x}_{i_{j}}^{(j,a_{2})}=\sum_{j=1}^{p}\left({\bf x}^{(j,a_{1})}\right)^{T}{\bf x}^{(j,a_{2})}.

(9)

Clearly,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}{\mathcal{G}}(\bar{{\bf x}}^{(a_{1})}){\mathcal{G}}(\bar{{\bf x}}^{(a_{1})})=\prod_{j=1}^{p}\left({\bf x}^{(j,a_{1})}\right)^{T}{\bf x}^{(j,a_{1})}+p-1=p=\sum_{j=1}^{p}\left({\bf x}^{(j,a_{1})}\right)^{T}{\bf x}^{(j,a_{1})}={\mathbb{E}}{\mathcal{G}}_{u}(\bar{{\bf x}}^{(a_{1})}){\mathcal{G}}_{u}(\bar{{\bf x}}^{(a_{1})}).

(10)

On the other hand, after setting
a^{(j)}\triangleq\left({\bf x}^{(j,a_{1})}\right)^{T}{\bf x}^{(j,a_{2})}
and noting that
|a^{(j)}|\leq 1
we have for any
k\in\{1,2,\dots,p\}

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}{\mathcal{G}}(\bar{{\bf x}}^{(a_{1})}){\mathcal{G}}(\bar{{\bf x}}^{(a_{2})})-{\mathbb{E}}{\mathcal{G}}_{u}(\bar{{\bf x}}^{(a_{1})}){\mathcal{G}}_{u}(\bar{{\bf x}}^{(a_{2})})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{p}a^{(j)}+p-1-\sum_{j=1}^{p}a^{(j)}

(11)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle-\prod_{j=1}^{k}a^{(j)}-\sum_{j=k+1}^{p}a^{(j)}+p-k+\prod_{j=1}^{p}a^{(j)}.

To see this, we first observe that for
k=1
(
11
) holds with equality. Assuming further that it holds for a fixed
k<p-1
we then write

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}{\mathcal{G}}(\bar{{\bf x}}^{(a_{1})}){\mathcal{G}}(\bar{{\bf x}}^{(a_{2})})-{\mathbb{E}}{\mathcal{G}}_{u}(\bar{{\bf x}}^{(a_{1})}){\mathcal{G}}_{u}(\bar{{\bf x}}^{(a_{2})})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle-\prod_{j=1}^{k}a^{(j)}-\sum_{j=k+1}^{p}a^{(j)}+p-k+\prod_{j=1}^{p}a^{(j)}

(12)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(1-a^{(k+1)}\right)\left(1-\prod_{j=1}^{k}a^{(j)}\right)

\displaystyle-\prod_{j=1}^{k+1}a^{(j)}-\sum_{j=k+2}^{p}a^{(j)}+p-k-1+\prod_{j=1}^{p}a^{(j)}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle-\prod_{j=1}^{k+1}a^{(j)}-\sum_{j=(k+1)+1}^{p}a^{(j)}+p-(k+1)+\prod_{j=1}^{p}a^{(j)}.

The right hand side of the last inequality is (
11
) for
k+1
which then by induction implies that (
11
) indeed holds for
1\leq k\leq p-1
. Taking
k=p-1
further gives

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}{\mathcal{G}}(\bar{{\bf x}}^{(a_{1})}){\mathcal{G}}(\bar{{\bf x}}^{(a_{2})})-{\mathbb{E}}{\mathcal{G}}_{u}(\bar{{\bf x}}^{(a_{1})}){\mathcal{G}}_{u}(\bar{{\bf x}}^{(a_{2})})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle-\prod_{j=1}^{p-1}a^{(j)}-\sum_{j=p}^{p}a^{(j)}+1+\prod_{j=1}^{p}a^{(j)}

(13)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(1-a^{(j)}\right)\left(1-\prod_{j=1}^{p-1}a^{(j)}\right)\geq 0.

Combining (
38
) and (
13
) with results of
[
53
]
(which are a special case of concepts discussed in Corollary 3 in
[
108
]
and in Corollary 4 in
[
109
]
) we obtain

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\max_{\bar{{\bf x}}}e^{c_{3,u}{\mathcal{G}}(\bar{{\bf x}})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\max_{\bar{{\bf x}}}e^{c_{3,u}{\mathcal{G}}_{u}(\bar{{\bf x}})},

(14)

where
\max_{\bar{{\bf x}}}
means
\max_{{\bf x}^{(j)}\in{\mathcal{S}}^{(j)}}
. A further combination of (
2
) and (
14
) gives

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\max_{\bar{{\bf x}}}e^{c_{3,u}\left(\sum_{i_{1},i_{2},\dots,i_{p}=1}^{n}A_{i_{1},i_{2},\dots,i_{p}}\prod_{j=1}^{p}{\bf x}_{i_{j}}^{(j)}+g\sqrt{p-1}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\max_{\bar{{\bf x}}}e^{c_{3,u}\sum_{j=1}^{p}\left({\bf g}^{(j)}\right)^{T}{\bf x}^{(j)}},

(15)

which is equivalent to

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}e^{c_{3,u}\max_{\bar{{\bf x}}}\left(\sum_{i_{1},i_{2},\dots,i_{p}=1}^{n}A_{i_{1},i_{2},\dots,i_{p}}\prod_{j=1}^{p}{\bf x}_{i_{j}}^{(j)}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle e^{-\frac{c_{3,u}^{2}}{2}(p-1)}{\mathbb{E}}e^{c_{3,u}\max_{\bar{{\bf x}}}\sum_{j=1}^{p}\left({\bf g}^{(j)}\right)^{T}{\bf x}^{(j)}},

(16)

and

\displaystyle\log\left({\mathbb{E}}e^{c_{3,u}\max_{\bar{{\bf x}}}\left(\sum_{i_{1},i_{2},\dots,i_{p}=1}^{n}A_{i_{1},i_{2},\dots,i_{p}}\prod_{j=1}^{p}{\bf x}_{i_{j}}^{(j)}\right)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\frac{c_{3,u}^{2}}{2}(p-1)+\log\left({\mathbb{E}}e^{c_{3,u}\max_{\bar{{\bf x}}}\sum_{j=1}^{p}\left({\bf g}^{(j)}\right)^{T}{\bf x}^{(j)}}\right),

which (after choosing
c_{3}=c_{3,u}
) establishes the second inequality in (
7
).

Lower bound
: For the lower bound portion we consider the following two centered Gaussian processes

\displaystyle{\mathcal{G}}_{x}(\bar{{\bf x}})

\displaystyle\triangleq

\displaystyle{\mathcal{G}}_{x}({\bf x}^{(1)},{\bf x}^{(2)},\dots,{\bf x}^{(p)})\triangleq\sqrt{p}\sum_{i_{1},i_{2},\dots,i_{p}=1}^{n}A_{i_{1},i_{2},\dots,i_{p}}\prod_{j=1}^{p}{\bf x}_{i_{j}}^{(j)}

\displaystyle{\mathcal{G}}_{l}(\bar{{\bf x}})

\displaystyle\triangleq

\displaystyle{\mathcal{G}}_{u}({\bf x}^{(1)},{\bf x}^{(2)},\dots,{\bf x}^{(p)})\triangleq\sum_{j=1}^{p}\sum_{i_{1},i_{2},\dots,i_{p}=1}^{n}A^{(j)}_{i_{1},i_{2},\dots,i_{p}}\prod_{k=1}^{p}{\bf x}_{i_{k}}^{(j)}.

(18)

Taking again two arrays
\bar{{\bf x}}^{(a_{1})}=\left({\bf x}^{(j,a_{1})}\right)_{j=1,\dots,p}
and
\bar{{\bf x}}^{(a_{2})}=\left({\bf x}^{(j,a_{2})}\right)_{j=1,\dots,p}
we write

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}{\mathcal{G}}_{x}(\bar{{\bf x}}^{(a_{1})}){\mathcal{G}}_{x}(\bar{{\bf x}}^{(a_{2})})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p\prod_{j=1}^{p}\sum_{i_{j}=1}^{n}{\bf x}_{i_{j}}^{(j,a_{1})}{\bf x}_{i_{j}}^{(j,a_{2})}=p\prod_{j=1}^{p}\left({\bf x}^{(j,a_{1})}\right)^{T}{\bf x}^{(j,a_{2})}=p\prod_{j=1}^{p}a^{(j)}

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}{\mathcal{G}}_{l}(\bar{{\bf x}}^{(a_{1})}){\mathcal{G}}_{l}(\bar{{\bf x}}^{(a_{2})})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{p}\left(\sum_{i_{j}=1}^{n}{\bf x}_{i_{j}}^{(j,a_{1})}{\bf x}_{i_{j}}^{(j,a_{2})}\right)^{p}=\sum_{j=1}^{p}\left(a^{(j)}\right)^{p}.

(19)

Clearly,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}{\mathcal{G}}_{x}(\bar{{\bf x}}^{(a_{1})}){\mathcal{G}}_{x}(\bar{{\bf x}}^{(a_{1})})=p={\mathbb{E}}{\mathcal{G}}_{l}(\bar{{\bf x}}^{(a_{1})}){\mathcal{G}}_{l}(\bar{{\bf x}}^{(a_{1})}).

(20)

Moreover, arithmetic-geometric mean inequality gives

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}{\mathcal{G}}_{l}(\bar{{\bf x}}^{(a_{1})}){\mathcal{G}}_{l}(\bar{{\bf x}}^{(a_{2})})=\sum_{j=1}^{p}\left(a^{(j)}\right)^{p}=p\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{p}\left(a^{(j)}\right)^{p}}{p}\geq p\left(\prod_{j=1}^{p}\left(a^{(j)}\right)^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}={\mathbb{E}}{\mathcal{G}}_{x}(\bar{{\bf x}}^{(a_{1})}){\mathcal{G}}_{x}(\bar{{\bf x}}^{(a_{2})}).

(21)

Combining (
20
) and (
21
) with results of
[
53
]
we obtain

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\max_{\bar{{\bf x}}}e^{c_{3,l}{\mathcal{G}}_{l}(\bar{{\bf x}})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\max_{\bar{{\bf x}}}e^{c_{3,l}{\mathcal{G}}_{x}(\bar{{\bf x}})}.

(22)

A further combination of (
2
) and (
22
) gives

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\max_{\bar{{\bf x}}}e^{c_{3,l}\sum_{j=1}^{p}\sum_{i_{1},i_{2},\dots,i_{p}=1}^{n}A^{(j)}_{i_{1},i_{2},\dots,i_{p}}\prod_{k=1}^{p}{\bf x}_{i_{k}}^{(j)}}

3.3
Discussion

Studying general multipartite
p
-spin systems can be done via fully lifted (fl) RDT
[
111
,
110
]
. However, fl RDT to a large degree relies on heavy numerical evaluations even when
p=2
. The numerical burden exponentially increases with
p
making it fairly unpractical already for
p\sim 5-10
when quick assessments are needed. Things do simplify a bit when
{\mathcal{S}}^{(j)}=S,1\leq j\leq p
, but the need for quicker more practical characterization remains. The bounds that we presented above are particularly useful in such contexts as they are substantially simpler. To have them fully operational, an assessment of their accuracy is welcome as well. The fact that there are scenarios where they provide the exact characterizations is both convenient and intriguing. Logical continuation may ask to what extent one can actually rely on such exactness. To formulate possible directions that might lead to addressing this question, we conveniently state the following corollary (an immediate consequence of Corollary
1
).

Corollary 3
.

For even
p>2
let
pMP(\bar{\mathcal{S}})
denote the multipartite
p
-spin models where
j
-th
n
-dimensional spin vector,
{\bf x}^{(j)}
, belongs to set
{\mathcal{S}}^{(j)}\subseteq{\mathbb{S}}^{n}
and let

p
-spin models where single
n
-dimensional spin vector,
{\bf x}
, belongs to set
{\mathcal{S}}\subseteq{\mathbb{S}}^{n}

\displaystyle\hskip-18.06749pt\star\quad{\mathcal{S}}^{(j)}={\mathcal{S}},\quad(\mbox{i.e., the structures of the sets to which the spins from different groups belong are identical})

\displaystyle\hskip-18.06749pt\star\quad GSE(pSP({\mathcal{S}}))\quad\mbox{is achieved on the second partial level of lifting},

then

\displaystyle GSE(pSP(\bar{\mathcal{S}}))=\sqrt{p}GSE(pSP({\mathcal{S}})).

(139)

Proof.

Automatically follows from Corollary
1
as the needed “sandwiching” bounding mechanism is provided there.

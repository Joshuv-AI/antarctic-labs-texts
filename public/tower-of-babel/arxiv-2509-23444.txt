HoloTrace: a Location Privacy Preservation Solution for mmWave MIMO-OFDM Systems
Lorenzo Italiano, , Alireza Pourafzal, , Hui Chen, , Mattia Brambilla, , Gonzalo Seco-Granados, , Monica Nicoli, , and Henk Wymeersch

Abstract
The technological innovation towards 6G cellular networks introduces unprecedented capabilities for ue ( ue ) localization, but it also raises serious concerns about physical layer location privacy. This paper introduces HoloTrace, a signal-level privacy preservation framework that relies on user-side spoofing of localization-relevant features to prevent the extraction of precise location information from the signals received by a bs ( bs ) in a mmWave MIMO-OFDM system. Spoofing is performed by the user on location parameters such as aoa ( aoa ), aod ( aod ), and tdoa ( tdoa ). Without requiring any protocol modification nor network-side support, our method strategically perturbs pilot transmissions to prevent a bs from performing non-consensual ue localization. The methodology allows the ue to spoof its position, keeping the precoder unchanged. We formulate spoofing as a unified rank-constrained projection problem, and provide closed-form solutions under varying levels of csi ( csi ) at the ue , including scenarios with and without csi knowledge. Simulation results confirm that the proposed approach enables the ue to deceive the bs , inducing significant localization errors, while the impact on link capacity varies depending on the spoofed position. Our findings establish HoloTrace as a practical and robust privacy-preserving solution for future 6G networks.

III-C

Blind Spoofing

We consider the scenario in which the

V
Conclusion and Future Work

We presented HoloTrace, a practical, signal-level privacy preservation framework for single-BS mmWave MIMO-OFDM localization. HoloTrace enables a
ue
to spoof localization-relevant channel features, angles, and delays, at the pilot signal level, without requiring protocol changes or network-side support. Our unified framework leverages a rank-constrained projection formulation, offering both oracle (full CSI) and blind (no CSI) solutions, and supporting analog arrays and asynchronous operation.

Comprehensive simulation results confirm that HoloTrace enables
ue
to precisely manipulate their apparent location in the eyes of a non-consensual
bs
, with minimal impact on communication quality. The oracle solution achieves complete spoofing, matching the theoretical spoofing offset, while blind solutions enable strong obfuscation even without channel gain knowledge. We also showed that simpler shift-based schemes (such as DAIS) are ineffective in the single-anchor, difference-based localization regime.

Overall, HoloTrace establishes a robust approach to waveform-level privacy in next-generation networks, closing the gap between theoretical spoofing and practical deployment. Future research directions include robust blind spoofing under highly dynamic multipath channels, extending the framework to digital/hybrid arrays, and addressing adversarial multi-anchor scenarios. Furthermore, we plan to extend the oracle solution analysis to account for
csi
uncertainty, and to design a blind spoofing strategy that overcomes the pairing issue identified in the current blind HoloTrace solution.

Appendix A
AoA Spoofing for SIMO

We prove the
aoa
spoofing for the single-carrier
simo
case. The received signal at the
bs
is

\mathbf{y}_{\text{tm}}=\operatorname{diag}(\mathbf{x})\,\mathbf{B}(\bm{\theta})\,\bm{\alpha}+\mathbf{q},

(51)

where
\mathbf{B}(\bm{\theta})=\mathbf{W}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{A}_{N_{\mathrm{R}}}(\bm{\theta})\in\mathbb{C}^{M\times L}
,
\bm{\alpha}\in\mathbb{C}^{L}
is the channel gain vector, and
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{C}^{M}
is the transmitted pilot. The standard
ls
cost for
aoa
estimation is

C(\bm{\theta};\mathbf{y}_{\text{tm}})=\min_{\bm{\alpha}}\big\|\mathbf{y}_{\text{tm}}-\mathbf{B}(\bm{\theta})\,\bm{\alpha}\big\|^{2},

(52)

with minimizer

\widehat{\bm{\alpha}}=\mathbf{B}^{\dagger}(\bm{\theta})\,\mathbf{y}_{\text{tm}}
,
yielding the reduced cost

C(\bm{\theta};\mathbf{y}_{\text{tm}})=\left\|\left(\mathbf{I}_{M}-\mathbf{B}(\bm{\theta})\,\mathbf{B}^{\dagger}(\bm{\theta})\right)\mathbf{y}_{\text{tm}}\right\|^{2}.

(53)

To spoof the estimate, the
ue
transmits a modified pilot
\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}
so that

C\big(\overline{\bm{\theta}};\,\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}\big)=\left\|\left(\mathbf{I}_{M}-\mathbf{B}(\overline{\bm{\theta}})\,\mathbf{B}^{\dagger}(\overline{\bm{\theta}})\right)\operatorname{diag}(\widetilde{\mathbf{x}})\,\mathbf{B}(\bm{\theta})\,\bm{\alpha}\right\|^{2}.

(54)

Perfect spoofing requires this to be minimized to zero, which is achieved when

\operatorname{diag}(\widetilde{\mathbf{x}})\,\mathbf{B}(\bm{\theta})\,\bm{\alpha}=\mathbf{B}(\overline{\bm{\theta}})\,\bm{\lambda}
,
for some
\bm{\lambda}\in\mathbb{C}^{L}
. Assuming
\mathbf{B}(\bm{\theta})\,\bm{\alpha}
has no zero entries, the closed-form pilot is

\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}=\operatorname{diag}^{-1}\big(\mathbf{B}(\bm{\theta})\,\bm{\alpha}\big)\,\mathbf{B}(\overline{\bm{\theta}})\,\bm{\lambda}
.
This construction ensures the residual cost at
\bm{\theta}=\overline{\bm{\theta}}
is zero, so the LS/ML AoA estimator returns the spoofed value. Existence and uniqueness depend on the signal dimension and rank properties of
\mathbf{B}(\bm{\theta})
and
\mathbf{B}(\overline{\bm{\theta}})
.

Appendix B
Joint AoA/AoD Spoofing in MIMO Channel

Consider the received signal

\mathbf{Y}_{\text{tm}}=\mathbf{B}(\bm{\theta})\bm{\Lambda}\mathbf{C}(\bm{\phi})^{\mathsf{T}}\odot\mathbf{X}+\mathbf{Q}
,
with
\mathbf{B}(\bm{\theta})=\mathbf{W}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{A}_{N_{\mathrm{R}}}(\bm{\theta})
,
\mathbf{C}(\bm{\phi})=\mathbf{F}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{A}_{N_{\mathrm{T}}}(\bm{\phi})
,
\bm{\Lambda}=\operatorname{diag}(\bm{\alpha})
, and
\mathbf{X}
the
M\times S
pilot matrix. Vectorizing,

\mathbf{y}_{\text{tm}}=\operatorname{vec}(\mathbf{Y}_{\text{tm}})=\mathbf{Z}(\bm{\theta},\bm{\phi})\,\bm{\alpha}\odot\operatorname{vec}(\mathbf{X})+\operatorname{vec}(\mathbf{Q}),

(55)

where
\mathbf{Z}=\mathbf{C}(\bm{\phi})\circledast\mathbf{B}(\bm{\theta})
.
The cost is

C(\bm{\theta},\bm{\phi};\mathbf{y}_{\text{tm}})=\min_{\bm{\alpha}}\big\|\mathbf{y}_{\text{tm}}-\mathbf{Z}(\bm{\theta},\bm{\phi})\,\bm{\alpha}\big\|^{2},

(56)

with minimizer

\widehat{\bm{\alpha}}=\mathbf{Z}^{\dagger}(\bm{\theta},\bm{\phi})\,\mathbf{y}_{\text{tm}}
,
and projected cost

C(\bm{\theta},\bm{\phi};\mathbf{y}_{\text{tm}})=\left\|\left(\mathbf{I}_{SM}-\mathbf{Z}\mathbf{Z}^{\dagger}\right)\mathbf{y}_{\text{tm}}\right\|^{2}.

(57)

Given a spoofed pilot
\widetilde{\mathbf{X}}
,
the estimator cost at candidate
(\overline{\bm{\theta}},\overline{\bm{\phi}})
is

C(\overline{\bm{\theta}},\overline{\bm{\phi}};\widetilde{\mathbf{X}})\!=\!\!\left\|\left(\mathbf{I}_{SM}-\mathbf{P}_{\mathbf{Z}(\overline{\bm{\theta}},\overline{\bm{\phi}})}\right)\mathbf{Z}(\bm{\theta},\bm{\phi})\,\bm{\alpha}\!\odot\!\operatorname{vec}(\widetilde{\mathbf{X}})\right\|^{2}.

(58)

This is zero if

\operatorname{diag}(\mathbf{Z}(\bm{\theta},\bm{\phi})\,\bm{\alpha})\,\operatorname{vec}(\widetilde{\mathbf{X}})=\mathbf{Z}(\overline{\bm{\theta}},\overline{\bm{\phi}})\,\bm{\lambda},

(59)

so the solution is

\operatorname{vec}(\widetilde{\mathbf{X}})=\operatorname{diag}^{-1}(\mathbf{Z}(\bm{\theta},\bm{\phi})\,\bm{\alpha})\,\mathbf{Z}(\overline{\bm{\theta}},\overline{\bm{\phi}})\,\bm{\lambda},

(60)

where
\bm{\lambda}
is arbitrary (chosen for energy constraints). Reshaping gives the matrix solution

\widetilde{\mathbf{X}}=\left(\mathbf{B}(\overline{\bm{\theta}})\,\bm{\Gamma}\,\mathbf{C}(\overline{\bm{\phi}})\right)\oslash\left(\mathbf{B}(\bm{\theta})\,\bm{\Lambda}\,\mathbf{C}(\bm{\phi})\right),

(61)

where
\bm{\Gamma}=\operatorname{diag}(\bm{\lambda})
. This construction ensures the joint estimator outputs
(\overline{\bm{\theta}},\overline{\bm{\phi}})
.

Appendix C
Blind AoA Spoofing

C-A

Single path case

We first consider the case
L=1
, so that the minimal
simo
model simplifies to
\mathbf{y}=\mathbf{b}(\theta_{0})\alpha_{0}+\mathbf{q}
, where
\mathbf{b}(\theta_{0})=\mathbf{W}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{a}_{N_{\text{R}}}(\theta_{0})
.
The channel gain estimate is
\widehat{\alpha}_{0}(\theta_{0})=\mathbf{b}^{\mathsf{H}}(\theta_{0})\mathbf{y}/\|\mathbf{b}(\theta_{0})\|^{2}
.
Defining the projector operator
\mathbf{P}_{\mathbf{b}(\vartheta)}={\mathbf{b}(\vartheta)\mathbf{b}^{\mathsf{H}}(\vartheta)}/{\left\|\mathbf{b}(\vartheta)\right\|^{2}}
, the cost function becomes (see (
12
)):

\displaystyle C(\overline{\theta}_{0}|\widetilde{\mathbf{x}})

\displaystyle=\|(\mathbf{I}_{M}-\mathbf{P}_{\mathbf{b}(\overline{\theta}_{0})})\text{diag}(\widetilde{\mathbf{x}})\mathbf{b}(\theta_{0})\alpha_{0}\|^{2},

(62)

in which
\text{diag}(\widetilde{\mathbf{x}})\mathbf{b}(\theta_{0})\alpha_{0}
is the noise-free observed signal under spoofed pilot
\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}
.
Perfect spoofing requires
C(\overline{\theta}_{0}|\widetilde{\mathbf{x}})=0
.
The sufficient condition for perfect spoofing from (
30
) in the single-path case becomes

\text{diag}(\widetilde{\mathbf{x}})\mathbf{b}(\theta_{0})\in\text{span}\left(\mathbf{b}(\overline{\theta}_{0})\right)
, which does not require knowledge of
\alpha_{0}
. Solving for
\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}
yields

\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}=\lambda\,\mathbf{b}(\overline{\theta}_{0})\oslash\mathbf{b}({\theta}_{0})
as long as no component of
\mathbf{b}({\theta}_{0})
when the corresponding component of
\mathbf{b}({\bar{\theta}}_{0})
is not zero; otherwise the solution does not exist.

C-B

Multiple path case

Consider the noiseless spoofing model

\mathbf{y}_{\text{tm}}=\operatorname{diag}(\widetilde{\mathbf{x}})\,\mathbf{B}(\bm{\theta})\,\bm{\alpha}
,
with
\mathbf{B}(\bm{\theta})\in\mathbb{C}^{M\times L}
and
\bm{\alpha}\in\mathbb{C}^{L}
.
Perfect blind spoofing requires the subspace inclusion (
30
).
The condition (
30
) is equivalent to the existence a non-zero vector
\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}
and a
L\times L
matrix
\bm{\Omega}
such that

\operatorname{diag}(\widetilde{\mathbf{x}})\,\mathbf{B}(\bm{\theta})=\mathbf{B}(\bar{\bm{\theta}})\bm{\Omega}
.
This is an homogeneous system of
ML
linear equations with
M+L^{2}
unknowns (i.e., the elements of
\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}
and
\bm{\Omega}
). The system is guaranteed to have a non-zero solution in general only if it is undetermined, that is,
M<L^{2}/(L-1)
. Therefore, for
L>1
, solutions exist only for small values of
M
, which are not of practical relevance. For
M\geq L^{2}/(L-1)
, a non-trivial solution exists only in very particular cases without general interest, such that
\bar{\bm{\theta}}
is a permutation of
\bm{\theta}
.

Appendix D
Proof of Blind ToA Spoofing

Consider the two-path case, the minimum required for localization. The delay steering matrix is
\mathbf{D}(\overline{\bm{\tau}})\in\mathbb{C}^{K\times 2}
. To spoof the
tdoa
estimate, we seek
\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}\in\mathbb{C}^{K}
such that the manipulated received signal
\text{diag}(\widetilde{\mathbf{x}})\mathbf{D}(\bm{\tau})\bm{\alpha}
lies in the range of
\mathbf{D}(\overline{\bm{\tau}})
, i.e.,

\text{diag}(\widetilde{\mathbf{x}})\mathbf{D}(\bm{\tau})\bm{\alpha}=\beta_{0}\,\mathbf{d}(\overline{\tau}_{0})+\beta_{1}\,\mathbf{d}(\overline{\tau}_{1})

(63)

for some
\beta_{0},\beta_{1}\in\mathbb{C}
. Expanding the left side,

\alpha_{0}\mathbf{d}(\tau_{0})\odot\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}+\alpha_{1}\mathbf{d}(\tau_{1})\odot\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}=\text{diag}\big(\alpha_{0}\mathbf{d}(\tau_{0})+\alpha_{1}\mathbf{d}(\tau_{1})\big)\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}.

If only one path is non-zero, e.g.,
\alpha_{1}=0
, a solution is

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}_{0}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{diag}^{-1}(\mathbf{d}(\tau_{0}))\,\mathbf{D}(\overline{\tau}_{0},\overline{\tau}_{1})\bm{\beta}_{0}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{D}(\overline{\tau}_{0}-\tau_{0},\,\overline{\tau}_{1}-\tau_{0})\,\bm{\beta}_{0},

(64)

and similarly for
\alpha_{0}=0
,

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}_{1}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{diag}^{-1}(\mathbf{d}(\tau_{1}))\,\mathbf{D}(\overline{\tau}_{0},\overline{\tau}_{1})\bm{\beta}_{1}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{D}(\overline{\tau}_{0}-\tau_{1},\,\overline{\tau}_{1}-\tau_{1})\,\bm{\beta}_{1}.

(65)

To spoof both paths simultaneously, we require
\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}_{0}=\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}_{1}
, which, under linear independence of delay vectors, leads to the trivial solution (i.e.,
\bm{\beta}_{0}=\bm{\beta}_{1}=\mathbf{0}_{L}
) unless we impose structure on the target delays

\overline{\tau}_{1}=\overline{\tau}_{0}+(\tau_{1}-\tau_{0})

or equivalently,

\overline{\tau}_{1}=\overline{\tau}_{0}-(\tau_{1}-\tau_{0})
.
Then, the pilot simplifies to

\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}=\lambda\mathbf{d}(\Delta_{\tau_{0}})=\lambda\mathbf{d}(\Delta_{\tau_{1}})
.
This shifts both paths by the same amount, preserving their delay difference.

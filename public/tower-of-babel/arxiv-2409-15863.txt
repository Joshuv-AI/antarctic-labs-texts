A discrete trace theory for non-conforming polytopal hybrid discretisation methods
Santiago Badia †, Jerome Droniou ♯†∗, Jai Tushar †

Abstract
. In this work we develop a discrete trace theory that covers non-conforming hybrid discretization methods and holds on polytopal meshes. A notion of discrete trace seminorm is defined, and trace and lifting results with respect to a discrete H 1 H^{1} -seminorm on the hybrid fully discrete space are proven. Finally, we conduct a numerical test in which we compute the proposed discrete operators and investigate their spectrum to verify the theoretical analysis. The development of this theory is motivated by the design and analysis of preconditioners for hybrid methods, e.g., of substructuring domain decomposition type.



1.
Introduction

Trace theory is at the heart of condition number analysis of several sub-structuring non-overlapping Domain Decomposition Methods (DDMs), such as Balancing Domain Decomposition by Constraints (BDDC) and Finite Element Tearing and Interconnecting – Dual Primal (FETI-DP)
[
14
,
24
]
. In the analysis of DDMs, trace theory is required to bound the injection operator from the space of discontinuous solutions (across inter-subdomain boundaries) into the original space, defined via a weighting operator and a subdomain-local discrete harmonic extension.
The continuity of this injection relies on an equivalence between the seminorm induced by the bilinear form on the subdomain
\Omega
and the
H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)
-seminorm (the trace seminorm) of truncations of functions to subdomain faces.

When using grad-conforming Finite Element Methods (FEMs) for the discretisation, one can rely on the continuous trace theory and
H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)
-seminorm, which is characterized by the Sobolev–Slobodeckij seminorm
[
10
]

(1.1)

\displaystyle|v|_{1/2,\partial\Omega}^{2}\doteq\int_{\partial\Omega}\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{|v(x)-v(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{d}}.

This seminorm satisfies two well-known properties, namely,
trace inequality
and
lifting

[
29
, Theorem 4.1]
. In DDM analysis, the former implies that the restriction of functions to the subdomain interface is stable and the latter then lifts this restriction to the interior of the neighbouring subdomain.

For non-conforming FEMs, like mixed FEM
[
5
]
, Discontinuous Galerkin (DG) methods
[
1
]
, Hybridizable DG (HDG)
[
8
]
, non-conforming Virtual Elements (ncVEM)
[
2
]
, Weak Galerkin (WG)
[
28
]
and Hybrid High Order (HHO)
[
12
]
, showing the seminorm equivalence is not trivial. This is because the trace of piecewise polynomial functions in
L^{2}(\Omega)
do not have
H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)
-regularity. A first breakthrough in this direction was made for mixed FEMs and Balancing Neumann-Neumann (BNN) preconditioning in
[
9
]
. There, the authors addressed this issue by constructing an interpolant of a function on the interface onto a conforming FE space, and then showed equivalence of its trace seminorm with the norm induced by the bilinear form on the subdomain interface. The same idea has been applied to a large class of DG methods and BDDC preconditioning in
[
13
]
. Furthermore, the authors in
[
25
]
combine this technique with a spectral equivalence established between HDG and hybridized RT methods in
[
7
]
to show quasi-optimal condition number bounds for the BDDC preconditioning of HDG methods. The same strategy as in HDG is used for WG methods in
[
26
]
. However, since this approach relies on conforming interpolants, it cannot be extended to polytopal meshes, one of the main motivation behind the use of non-conforming methods. All the analyses for non-conforming spaces so far have been carried out on conforming simplicial or quadrilateral/hexahedral meshes. Besides, to the best of our knowledge, there is no literature available addressing ncVEM or HHO.

As discussed above, the main challenge when designing and analysing DDMs for non-conforming methods on polytopal meshes is the lack of a complete trace theory. This is the main motivation behind this work. We design a discrete trace seminorm, which avoids the need for conforming interpolants, and prove that it enjoys analogous properties (trace and lifting) with respect to a discrete
H^{1}(\Omega)
-seminorm as the conforming counterparts. As far as we know, this path has never been explored before.

The closest work we could identify in this direction is
[
20
]
, in which the authors design such a norm for the non-conforming Crouzeix–Raviart finite element and combine it with an enrichment process to prove a discrete lifting result. However, this norm is too strong to also satisfy the trace inequality, mainly because the trace seminorm is not easily localisable. To see this, letting
\{\tau\}
be a family of simply connected subdomains that partition
\partial\Omega
, we can rewrite (
1.1
) as follows

\displaystyle|v|_{1/2,\partial\Omega}^{2}=\sum_{\tau}|v|_{1/2,\tau}^{2}+\sum_{\tau}\int_{\tau}\int_{\partial\Omega\backslash\tau}\frac{|v(x)-v(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{d}}.

The second term in the above equality is difficult to localise. Some studies tackling this aspect can be found in
[
19
,
4
]
and references therein. Our design of the discrete
H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)
-seminorm (see Section
2
) mimics the above decomposition in the discrete setting, using the mesh size as a scaling factor.

Conceptually, our approach is close to the techniques of Discrete Functional Analysis (DFA) as developed, e.g., in
[
18
,
16
]
. These techniques have however, so far, only been used to prove inequalities involving as boundary norm the
L^{2}(\partial\Omega)
-norm (which prevented from proving a lifting property), and never to design or analyse a discrete
H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)
-seminorm.
Following DFA principles, we only manipulate here discrete spaces, whose elements are vectors of polynomials in the mesh cells and on the mesh faces. Our arguments do not invoke any continuous trace or lifting result; instead, we mimic at the discrete level the proofs of these continuous results. This mimicking requires to construct various specific sets of cells or faces, on which estimates need to be established.

We finally note that, even though our driving motivation for a discrete trace theory is the design of robust and scalable preconditioners for non-conforming polytopal discretisations, this theory is of interest in itself, and can be applied in other scenarios – e.g., in the design and analysis of operator preconditioners involving trace inner products
[
21
]
.

1.1.
Main contributions and outline

The main contribution of this work is the development of a discrete trace theory on polytopal meshes spanning a large class of hybrid methods. It hinges on the design and analysis of a discrete trace seminorm. We define it in Section
2
and state its two main properties, namely, discrete trace inequality in Theorem
2.2
and lifting property in Theorem
2.3
. These results are dimension-independent and proved under a quasi-uniformity assumption on the polytopal mesh (see Assumption
2.1
).

The proofs of these theorems are structured into two main stages. In the first stage, we establish the discrete trace inequality and lifting in the flat case, when
\Omega
is a cube and we only consider one side of its boundary. This stage is detailed in Sections
3
and
4
, respectively. It mimics the proofs done in the continuous settings (briefly recalled in Sections
3.1
and
4.1
, respectively) and additionally relies on some results on partitionings of the mesh cells and faces, whose proofs are postponed at the end of their corresponding sections for better readability (Sections
3.4
and
4.4
, respectively). In the second stage, we extend these results to a generic polytopal domain
\Omega
by localising and gluing along
\partial\Omega
. This stage is detailed in Section
5
, in which we also demonstrate that the trace inequality and lifting results also apply to all kinds of discrete spaces encountered in polytopal methods.

In Section
6
we numerically illustrate the theoretical results. An appendix, Section
7
, provides two tables gathering the main notations used in the paper; the reader is encouraged to use these tables as they go over the most technical aspects of the proofs.

2.
Discrete
H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)
-seminorm, trace and lifting

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}

(d\geq 2)
be a polytopal domain with boundary
\partial\Omega
. We consider a partition
\mathcal{T}_{h}
of
\Omega
into a finite collection of non-empty disjoint polytopes, called
cells
, and define its mesh
skeleton
as
\bigcup_{t\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\partial t
. For each cell
t\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, we denote by
\mathcal{F}_{t}
the set of faces of
t
, which are
(d-1)
-dimensional polytopes.
The set of boundary faces, that is, the partition of the domain boundary
\partial\Omega
generated by the domain partition, is denoted by

\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd}}\doteq\bigcup_{t\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\{f\in\mathcal{F}_{t}\ :\ f\subset\partial\Omega\},

and the set of interior faces by

\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm in}}\doteq\bigcup_{t\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\{f\in\mathcal{F}_{t}\ :\ f\subset\Omega\}.

We denote by
\mathcal{F}_{h}\doteq\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd}}\cup\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm in}}
the set of all faces of the mesh.

We combine the domain and skeleton meshes into a
hybrid
mesh
\mathcal{M}_{h}\doteq(\mathcal{T}_{h},\mathcal{F}_{h})
(see
[
11
, Definition 1.4]
). The diameter of
t\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
is denoted with
h_{t}
, and we define the
mesh size

h=\max_{t\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}h_{t}
. Analogously, we denote by
h_{f}
the diameter of
f\in\mathcal{F}_{h}
.

Throughout the paper,
|X|_{n}
is the
n
-dimensional measure of a set
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
. When
X
is a set of cells/faces, with an abuse of notation we write
|X|_{n}
for the
n
-dimensional measure of the union of the cells/faces in
X
.

We assume that the hybrid mesh of
\Omega
is regular and quasi-uniform.
Some of the arguments developed in the proofs can easily be adapted to non-quasi-uniform meshes; others are, however, much less obvious to establish when the mesh is not quasi-uniform. Considering that many of our proofs are already quite technical, for legibility purposes we decided to consider quasi-uniform meshes throughout the paper.

Assumption 2.1

(Mesh regularity)
.

The mesh
\mathcal{M}_{h}
is regular as per
[
11
, Definition 1.9]
, and quasi-uniform in the sense that there exists
\varrho>0
independent of
h
such that, for all
t\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
,
h\leq\varrho h_{t}
.

On the hybrid mesh, we define bulk and trace piecewise polynomial functional spaces as follows.
We consider the
hybrid space

\underline{U}_{h}=U_{h}\times U_{h}^{\partial}
, where

U_{h}\doteq\bigtimes_{t\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\mathbb{P}_{k}(t),\quad U_{h}^{\partial}\doteq\bigtimes_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}\mathbb{P}_{k}(f),

and
\mathbb{P}_{k}
is the space of polynomials of total degree
k
or less.

We define the following discrete
H^{1}(\Omega)
-seminorm on
\underline{U}_{h}
, inspired by the HHO theory
[
11
, Section 2.2.1]
(albeit with a slightly different scaling of boundary terms, see
[
17
, Eq. (2.4)]
): For all
\underline{v}_{h}=((v_{t})_{t\in\mathcal{T}_{h}},(v_{f})_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{t}})\in\underline{U}_{h}
,

(2.1)

|\underline{v}_{h}|_{1,h}\doteq\left(\sum_{t\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}|\underline{v}_{t}|_{1,t}^{2}\right)^{1/2}\quad\mbox{ with }\quad|\underline{v}_{t}|_{1,t}^{2}\doteq\|\nabla v_{t}\|_{L^{2}(t)}^{2}+\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{t}}h_{t}^{-1}\|v_{f}-v_{t}\|_{L^{2}(f)}^{2}.

We also design here a novel notion of discrete
H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)
-seminorm on restrictions of hybrid spaces to the boundary. Setting

U_{h}^{\partial,{\rm bd}}\doteq\{w_{h}=(w_{f})_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd}}}:w_{f}\in\mathbb{P}_{k}(f)\;\;\forall f\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd}}\},

we have the natural trace operator
\gamma:\underline{U}_{h}\to U_{h}^{\partial,{\rm bd}}
such that

\gamma(\underline{v}_{h})|_{f}=v_{f}\qquad\forall f\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd}}\,,\quad\forall\underline{v}_{h}\in\underline{U}_{h}.

The discrete
H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)
-seminorm of
w_{h}\in U_{h}^{\partial,{\rm bd}}
is defined by

(2.2)

|w_{h}|_{1/2,h}^{2}\doteq\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd}}}h_{f}^{-1}\|w_{f}-\overline{w}_{f}\|_{L^{2}(f)}^{2}+\sum_{(f,f^{\prime})\in\mathcal{F\!F}_{h}^{{\rm bd}}}|f|_{d-1}|f^{\prime}|_{d-1}\frac{|\overline{w}_{f}-\overline{w}_{f^{\prime}}|^{2}}{|x_{f}-x_{f^{\prime}}|^{d}},

where
\overline{w}_{f}\doteq\frac{1}{|f|_{d-1}}\int_{f}w_{f}
is the average of
w_{f}
over
f
,

(2.3)

\mathcal{F\!F}_{h}^{{\rm bd}}=\{(f,f^{\prime})\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd}}\times\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd}}\,:\,f\neq f^{\prime}\},

and
x_{f}
and
x_{f^{\prime}}
are the centroids

“The” centroid
x_{f}
of a face
f
is actually arbitrarily chosen such that
f
contains a ball centered at
x_{f}

\varpi h_{f}

\varpi

f
and
f^{\prime}
. In the right-hand side of (

), the first term measures the local variations

Note that, by a discrete inequality,
h_{f}^{-1}\|w_{f}-\overline{w}_{f}\|_{L^{2}(f)}^{2}\simeq|w_{f}|_{H^{1/2}(f)}^{2}

, while the second term measures the long-range variations. In the continuous setting, both terms are embedded into the double integral (
1.1
) that defines the
H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)
-seminorm. Separating the two contributions is natural in the discrete setting, due to the introduction of a particular scale (the mesh size).

From here onwards, we write
a\lesssim b
to indicate that
a\leq Cb

C\geq 0

The two main properties of the discrete
H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)
-seminorm are summarised in the following theorems, which form the main contributions of this work.

Theorem 2.2

(Trace inequality)
.

The following discrete trace inequality holds:

(2.4)

|\gamma(\underline{v}_{h})|_{1/2,h}\lesssim|\underline{v}_{h}|_{1,h}\qquad\forall\underline{v}_{h}\in\underline{U}_{h}.

Theorem 2.3

(Lifting)
.

There exists a linear lifting operator
\mathcal{L}_{h}:U_{h}^{\partial,{\rm bd}}\rightarrow\underline{U}_{h}
such that:

(2.5)

|\mathcal{L}_{h}(w_{h})|_{1,h}\lesssim|w_{h}|_{1/2,h}\quad\forall w_{h}\in U_{h}^{\partial,{\rm bd}},\qquad\gamma\circ\mathcal{L}_{h}=\mathrm{Id}_{U_{h}^{\partial,{\rm bd}}}.

Remark 2.4

(Scaling)
.

It can be checked that, if
\Omega
is scaled by a factor
\mu
, both the discrete
H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)
and
H^{1}(\Omega)
-seminorms scale with the factor
\mu^{d-2}
. Thus, the hidden constants in the trace inequality and lifting property stated above do not depend on
\mathrm{diam}(\Omega)
under rescaling, and we can assume that
\mathrm{diam}(\Omega)
is of unit size.

Remark 2.5

(Extension to more general hybrid spaces)
.

We show in Section
5.2
that Theorems
2.2
and
2.3
directly provide a trace inequality and a lifting property in more general hybrid spaces, which cover a wide range of polytopal methods.

Remark 2.6

(Discrete trace theory in non-Hilbertian spaces)
.

We develop in this paper the discrete trace theory in the Hilbertian setting, but the proofs are only based on Cauchy–Schwarz inequalities and counting or measuring various sets of faces and cells. As a consequence, they can be easily adapted to the
W^{1,p}
setting with
p\in(1,\infty]
.
Theorems
2.2
and
2.3
then hold simply by replacing the discrete
H^{1}(\Omega)
-seminorm (
2.1
) by the discrete

-seminorm

|\underline{v}_{h}|_{1,p,h}\doteq\left(\sum_{t\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\|\nabla v_{t}\|_{L^{p}(t)}^{p}+\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{t}}h_{t}^{1-p}\|v_{f}-v_{t}\|_{L^{p}(f)}^{p}\right)^{1/p}

(see
[
11
, Eq. (6.9)]
) and the discrete
H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)
-seminorm (
2.2
) by the discrete
W^{1-\frac{1}{p},p}(\partial\Omega)
-seminorm

|w_{h}|_{1-\frac{1}{p},h}\doteq\left(\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd}}}h_{f}^{1-p}\|w_{f}-\overline{w}_{f}\|_{L^{p}(f)}^{p}+\sum_{(f,f^{\prime})\in\mathcal{F\!F}_{h}^{{\rm bd}}}|f|_{d-1}|f^{\prime}|_{d-1}\frac{|\overline{w}_{f}-\overline{w}_{f^{\prime}}|^{p}}{|x_{f}-x_{f^{\prime}}|^{d+p-2}}\right)^{1/p}

(both seminorms are written here for

3.
Discrete trace inequality on the side of a cube

The proof of Theorem
2.2
consists in adapting to the discrete setting the arguments in
[
15
]
for the continuous trace inequality. In Section
3.1
, we briefly recall these arguments in the case where
\Omega
is a half-space, to make the technical proof in the discrete setting easier to follow. In Section
3.3
, we establish the discrete trace inequality when
\Omega
is a cube and the trace is restricted to one of its sides. The case of a polytopal domain and trace on the complete boundary
\partial\Omega
is handled in Section
5.1
, by combining the case of a cube and localisation arguments.

3.1.
Continuous trace inequality

We consider here that
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\times(0,\infty)
is a half-space, we take
u
a continuously differentiable function with compact support in
\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\times[0,\infty)
, and we establish the following trace inequality on the side of
\Omega
:

(3.1)

\mathcal{S}\doteq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d-1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d-1}}\frac{|u(x^{\prime},0)-u(y^{\prime},0)|^{2}}{|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|^{d}}\,dx^{\prime}dy^{\prime}\lesssim\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}.

The idea is to estimate
u(x^{\prime},0)-u(y^{\prime},0)
by going “inside”
\Omega
, at a distance
\ell\doteq|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|
from
(x^{\prime},0)
and
(y^{\prime},0)
, and by writing this
difference as a sum of three differences:

(3.2)

u(x^{\prime},0)-u(y^{\prime},0)=(u(x^{\prime},0)-u(x^{\prime},\ell))+(u(x^{\prime},\ell)-u(y^{\prime},\ell))+(u(y^{\prime},\ell)-u(y^{\prime},0)).

Taking the absolute value, integrating over
x^{\prime},y^{\prime}
and using triangle inequalities, we deduce
\mathcal{S}\lesssim\mathcal{S}_{1}+\mathcal{S}_{2}+\mathcal{S}_{3}
with

(3.3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{1}\doteq

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d-1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d-1}}\frac{|u(x^{\prime},0)-u(x^{\prime},\ell)|^{2}}{|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|^{d}}\,dx^{\prime}dy^{\prime}

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{2}\doteq

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d-1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d-1}}\frac{|u(x^{\prime},\ell)-u(y^{\prime},\ell)|^{2}}{|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|^{d}}\,dx^{\prime}dy^{\prime}

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{3}\doteq

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d-1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d-1}}\frac{|u(y^{\prime},\ell)-u(y^{\prime},0)|^{2}}{|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|^{d}}\,dx^{\prime}dy^{\prime}=\mathcal{S}_{1},

the last equality coming from the symmetric roles of
x^{\prime},y^{\prime}
in these integrals. We therefore only have to prove that
\mathcal{S}_{1}

and
\mathcal{S}_{2}
are bounded by the right-hand side of (
3.1
) to conclude.

Bound on
\mathcal{S}_{1}
: We start with a polar change of variable
y^{\prime}=x^{\prime}+\ell\xi\to(\ell,\xi)
(see
[
23
, Chapter 6, (9)]
) to get

(3.4)

\mathcal{S}_{1}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb S^{d-2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d-1}}\frac{|u(x^{\prime},0)-u(x^{\prime},\ell)|^{2}}{\ell^{2}}\,dx^{\prime}d\xi d\ell,

where
\mathbb S^{d-2}
is the unit sphere in
\mathbb R^{d-1}
. We then substitute

(3.5)

|u(x^{\prime},0)-u(x^{\prime},\ell)|\leq\int_{0}^{\ell}|\partial_{d}u(x^{\prime},s)|\,ds

to obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{1}\leq{}

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}|\mathbb S^{d-2}|_{d-2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d-1}}\left|\frac{1}{\ell}\int_{0}^{\ell}|\partial_{d}u(x^{\prime},s)|\,ds\right|^{2}\,dx^{\prime}d\ell

(3.6)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}|\mathbb S^{d-2}|_{d-2}\left\|\frac{1}{\ell}\int_{0}^{\ell}|\partial_{d}u(\cdot,s)|\,ds\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb R^{d-1})}^{2}.

Invoking the classical formula

(3.7)

\left\|\int_{0}^{\ell}f(\cdot,s)\,ds\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d-1})}\leq\int_{0}^{\ell}\|f(\cdot,s)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d-1})}\,ds,

we infer

(3.8)

\mathcal{S}_{1}\leq|\mathbb S^{d-2}|_{d-2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{\ell}\int_{0}^{\ell}\|\partial_{d}u(\cdot,s)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb R^{d-1})}\,ds\right)^{2}.

Let
F_{1}(\ell)=\frac{1}{\ell}\int_{0}^{\ell}f_{1}(s)\,ds
with
f_{1}(s)=\|\partial_{d}u(\cdot,s)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d-1})}
.
The integral Hardy inequality gives

\|F_{1}\|_{L^{2}(0,\infty)}\lesssim\|f_{1}\|_{L^{2}(0,\infty)}=\|\partial_{d}u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

which yields the desired estimate on
\mathcal{S}_{1}
:

\mathcal{S}_{1}\leq|\mathbb S^{d-2}|_{d-2}\|F_{1}\|_{L^{2}(0,\infty)}^{2}\lesssim\|\partial_{d}u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

Bound on
\mathcal{S}_{2}
: Recalling that
y^{\prime}=x^{\prime}+\ell\xi
with
\xi
unit vector, we use the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality to write

(3.9)

|u(x^{\prime},\ell)-u(y^{\prime},\ell)|^{2}\leq\left(\int_{0}^{\ell}|\nabla_{x^{\prime}}u(x^{\prime}+s\xi,\ell)|\,ds\right)^{2}\leq\ell\int_{0}^{\ell}|\nabla_{x^{\prime}}u(x^{\prime}+s\xi,\ell)|^{2}\,ds.

Plugging this into the definition of
\mathcal{S}_{2}
and applying the polar change of variable
y^{\prime}=x^{\prime}+\ell\xi\to(\ell,\xi)
yields

(3.10)

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{2}={}

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb S^{d-2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d-1}}\frac{|u(x^{\prime},\ell)-u(x^{\prime}+\ell\xi,\ell)|^{2}}{\ell^{2}}\,dx^{\prime}d\xi d\ell

(3.11)

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb S^{d-2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d-1}}\frac{1}{\ell}\int_{0}^{\ell}|\nabla_{x^{\prime}}u(x^{\prime}+s\xi,\ell)|^{2}\,dsdx^{\prime}d\xi d\ell

(3.12)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb S^{d-2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d-1}}|\nabla_{x^{\prime}}u(x^{\prime},\ell)|^{2}\,dx^{\prime}d\xi d\ell,

where the conclusion follows applying a change of variable
x^{\prime}+s\xi\to x^{\prime}
in the integral over
x^{\prime}
, and noticing that the resulting integrand no longer depends on
s
. Since the integrand do not depend on
\xi
, we infer that
\mathcal{S}_{2}\leq|\mathbb S^{d-2}|_{d-2}\|\nabla_{x^{\prime}}u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}
, which is the expected bound.

3.2.
Notations

In the rest of Section
3

\Omega

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{l}\doteq

3.3.
Proof of the discrete trace inequality on the side of a cube

To prove (
2.4
), we need to handle the two terms appearing in the discrete
H^{1/2}
-seminorm (
2.2
). Section
3.3.1
deals with the first one, measuring the local variations of polynomials inside each face, while Section
3.3.2
covers the other term, measuring the long-range interactions between averages. In several places, estimating these terms requires technical lemmas; these lemmas are stated when needed, and their proofs are postponed to Section
3.4

(in which the lemmas are also re-stated for ease of reference).

3.3.1.
Local contributions

In this section, we prove the following bound on the first term appearing in the
H^{1/2}
-seminorm of the trace of hybrid functions:

(3.21)

\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd}}}h_{f}^{-1}\|v_{f}-\overline{v}_{f}\|_{L^{2}(f)}^{2}\lesssim|\underline{v}_{h}|_{1,h}^{2}\qquad\forall\underline{v}_{h}\in\underline{U}_{h}.

The local contribution
h_{f}^{-1}\|v_{f}-\overline{v}_{f}\|_{L^{2}(f)}^{2}
to the discrete
H^{1/2}
-seminorm measures the variation in
f
of the hybrid function; it does not have any equivalent at the continuous level, since this scale is not directly apparent in the integrals. We bound this contribution using the local discrete
H^{1}
-norm
|\underline{v}_{t}|_{1,t}
, where
t
is the cell neighbouring
f
, by considering two components: the internal (high-frequency at the scale of the cell) variations in
t
, and the difference between the lowest-order projections in
t
and
f
(low frequency at the scale of the cell). The following lemma gives a bound on the latter quantity.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
t\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
and
\underline{v}_{t}=(v_{t},(v_{f})_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{t}})\in\mathbb{P}_{k}(t)\times\bigtimes_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{t}}\mathbb{P}_{k}(f)
. Then, for all
f\in\mathcal{F}_{t}
,

(3.22)

\displaystyle\|\pi_{f}^{0}v_{f}-\pi_{t}^{0}v_{t}\|_{L^{2}(f)}

\displaystyle\lesssim h_{t}^{\frac{1}{2}}|\underline{v}_{t}|_{1,t},

(3.23)

\displaystyle|\pi_{f}^{0}v_{f}-\pi_{t}^{0}v_{t}|

\displaystyle\lesssim h_{t}^{\frac{2-d}{2}}|\underline{v}_{t}|_{1,t},

where, for
X\in\{t,f\}
,
\pi_{X}^{0}:L^{2}(X)\rightarrow\mathbb{P}_{0}(X)
is the
L^{2}
-orthogonal projection onto constant functions in
X
.

To prove (
3.21
), take
f\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd}}
and
t\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
such that
f\in\mathcal{F}_{t}
, and use triangle inequalities to write

\displaystyle\|v_{f}-\overline{v}_{f}\|_{L^{2}(f)}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|v_{f}-v_{t}\|_{L^{2}(f)}^{2}+\|v_{t}-\pi_{t}^{0}v_{t}\|_{L^{2}(f)}^{2}+\|\pi_{t}^{0}v_{t}-\pi_{f}^{0}v_{f}\|_{L^{2}(f)}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|v_{f}-v_{t}\|_{L^{2}(f)}^{2}+h_{t}\|\nabla v_{t}\|_{L^{2}(t)}^{2}+h_{t}|\underline{v}_{t}|_{1,t}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim h_{f}|\underline{v}_{t}|_{1,t}^{2}.

In the second inequality, we have additionally invoked the approximation property of
\pi_{t}^{0}
(see
[
11
, Theorem 1.45]
), while the conclusion comes from the definition (
2.1
) of
|{\cdot}|_{1,t}
together with the mesh regularity property that yields
h_{t}\lesssim h_{f}
. The bound (
3.21
) follows by dividing by
h_{f}
and summing over
f
(noticing that this implies, in the right-hand side, a sum over boundary cells
t
, which is smaller than a sum over all cells).

3.3.2.
Long-range term

In this section, we prove the bound on the second term in the discrete
H^{1/2}
-seminorm of the trace of hybrid functions:

(3.24)

\mathcal{S}\doteq\sum_{(f,f^{\prime})\in\mathcal{F\!F}_{h}^{{\rm bd}}}|f|_{d-1}|f^{\prime}|_{d-1}\frac{|\overline{v}_{f}-\overline{v}_{f^{\prime}}|^{2}}{|x_{f}-x_{f^{\prime}}|^{d}}\lesssim|\underline{v}_{h}|_{1,h}^{2}\qquad\forall\underline{v}_{h}\in\underline{U}_{h}.

For each pair
(f,f^{\prime})\in\mathcal{F\!F}_{h}^{{\rm bd}}
, we select cells
t_{{f\!f^{\prime}},f}\in\mathcal{V}_{f}
and
t_{{f\!f^{\prime}},f^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{V}_{f^{\prime}}
that respectively contain
x_{f}+|x_{f}-x_{f^{\prime}}|\mathbf{n}_{\partial\Omega}
and
x_{f^{\prime}}+|x_{f}-x_{f^{\prime}}|\mathbf{n}_{\partial\Omega}
(see Figure
1
-(A)) and
we rewrite the difference
\overline{v}_{f}-\overline{v}_{f^{\prime}}
in a similar way as (
3.2
) at the continuous level:

\displaystyle\overline{v}_{f}-\overline{v}_{f^{\prime}}=\overline{v}_{f}-\overline{v}_{t_{{f\!f^{\prime}},f}}+\overline{v}_{t_{{f\!f^{\prime}},f}}-\overline{v}_{t_{{f\!f^{\prime}},f^{\prime}}}+\overline{v}_{t_{{f\!f^{\prime}},f^{\prime}}}-\overline{v}_{f^{\prime}},

where
\overline{v}_{t}=\pi_{t}^{0}v_{t}
.
Plugging this into
\mathcal{S}
and using triangle inequalities leads to
\mathcal{S}\lesssim\mathcal{S}_{1}+\mathcal{S}_{2}+\mathcal{S}_{3}
, these quantities being the discrete versions of (
3.3
):

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{1}

\displaystyle\doteq\sum_{(f,f^{\prime})\in\mathcal{F\!F}_{h}^{{\rm bd}}}|f|_{d-1}|f^{\prime}|_{d-1}\frac{|\overline{v}_{f}-\overline{v}_{t_{{f\!f^{\prime}},f}}|^{2}}{|x_{f}-x_{f^{\prime}}|^{d}},

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{2}

\displaystyle\doteq\sum_{(f,f^{\prime})\in\mathcal{F\!F}_{h}^{{\rm bd}}}|f|_{d-1}|f^{\prime}|_{d-1}\frac{|\overline{v}_{t_{{f\!f^{\prime}},f}}-\overline{v}_{t_{{f\!f^{\prime}},f^{\prime}}}|^{2}}{|x_{f}-x_{f^{\prime}}|^{d}},

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{3}

\displaystyle\doteq\sum_{(f,f^{\prime})\in\mathcal{F\!F}_{h}^{{\rm bd}}}|f|_{d-1}|f^{\prime}|_{d-1}\frac{|\overline{v}_{f^{\prime}}-\overline{v}_{t_{{f\!f^{\prime}},f^{\prime}}}|^{2}}{|x_{f}-x_{f^{\prime}}|^{d}}=\mathcal{S}_{1},

the last equality following from the symmetric role that
f
and
f^{\prime}
play. The bound (
3.24
) therefore follows if we can show that
\mathcal{S}_{1}\lesssim|\underline{v}_{h}|_{1,h}^{2}
and
\mathcal{S}_{2}\lesssim|\underline{v}_{h}|_{1,h}^{2}
.

Bound on
\mathcal{S}_{1}
:
For
(f,f^{\prime})\in\mathcal{W}_{l}

\overline{v}_{f}-\overline{v}_{t_{{f\!f^{\prime}},f}}=\overline{v}_{f}-\overline{v}_{t_{1}}+\overline{v}_{t_{1}}-\overline{v}_{f_{1}}+\overline{v}_{f_{1}}-\overline{v}_{t_{2}}+\dots+\overline{v}_{f_{N-1}}-\overline{v}_{t_{N}}.

The bound (

from

, as represented by the layers

If

. We thus obtain the equivalent of (

), with

(3.25)

|\overline{v}_{f}-\overline{v}_{t_{{f\!f^{\prime}},f}}|\lesssim\sum_{m=0}^{l}\underbrace{\sum_{t\in\mathcal{L}_{m}\cap\mathcal{V}_{f}}h_{t}^{\frac{2-d}{2}}|\underline{v}_{t}|_{1,t}}_{\doteq a^{(m)}_{f}}.

The mapping

\chi_{l}:(s_{f})_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd}}}\in\mathbb R^{\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd}}}\longrightarrow\left(\sum_{(f,f^{\prime})\in\mathcal{W}_{l}}\frac{|f|_{d-1}|f^{\prime}|_{d-1}}{|x_{f}-x_{f^{\prime}}|^{d}}|s_{f}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

is a norm and, by (

),

(3.26)

\displaystyle\zeta_{l}

\displaystyle\doteq\left(\sum_{(f,f^{\prime})\in\mathcal{W}_{l}}|f|_{d-1}|f^{\prime}|_{d-1}\frac{|\overline{v}_{f}-\overline{v}_{t_{{f\!f^{\prime}},f}}|^{2}}{|x_{f}-x_{f^{\prime}}|^{d}}\right)^{1/2}

(3.27)

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\sum_{(f,f^{\prime})\in\mathcal{W}_{l}}\frac{|f|_{d-1}|f^{\prime}|_{d-1}}{|x_{f}-x_{f^{\prime}}|^{d}}\left(\sum_{m=0}^{l}a_{f}^{(m)}\right)^{2}\right)^{1/2}=\chi_{l}\left(\sum_{m=0}^{l}a^{(m)}\right),

with
a^{(m)}=(a_{f}^{(m)})_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd}}}
. Note that
\sum_{(f,f^{\prime})\in\mathcal{W}_{l}}\bullet=\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd}}}\sum_{f^{\prime}\in\mathcal{W}_{lf}}\bullet
, so the sum in (
3.26

\int_{\mathbb S^{d-2}}\int_{\mathbb R^{d-1}}
in (
3.4

The triangle inequality on the norm

is the equivalent of the continuous formula (

\chi_{l}\left(\sum_{m=0}^{l}a^{(m)}\right)\leq\sum_{m=0}^{l}\chi_{l}\left(a^{(m)}\right).

Plugged into (
3.27

(3.28)

\zeta_{l}\lesssim\sum_{m=0}^{l}\chi_{l}\left(a^{(m)}\right)=\sum_{m=0}^{l}\left(\sum_{(f,f^{\prime})\in\mathcal{W}_{l}}\frac{|f|_{d-1}|f^{\prime}|_{d-1}}{|x_{f}-x_{f^{\prime}}|^{d}}|a_{f}^{(m)}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

The Cauchy-Schwarz inequality gives, for any family
(\alpha_{i})_{i\in I}
with

(3.29)

\Big(\sum_{i\in I}\alpha_{i}\Big)^{2}\leq\#I\sum_{i\in I}\alpha_{i}^{2}\lesssim\sum_{i\in I}\alpha_{i}^{2}.

Lemma 3.2

(Estimates on the cardinality of sets of faces)
.

The following properties hold:

(i)

For all

and

.

(ii)

For all

and

\#\mathcal{W}_{lf}\lesssim l^{d-2}
.

(iii)

For all

,

.

Using Lemma
3.2
-

to invoke (

|a_{f}^{(m)}|^{2}\lesssim\sum_{t\in\mathcal{L}_{m}\cap\mathcal{V}_{f}}h_{t}^{2-d}|\underline{v}_{t}|_{1,t}^{2}.

Plugged into (

\displaystyle\zeta_{l}\lesssim{}

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{l}\left(\sum_{(f,f^{\prime})\in\mathcal{W}_{l}}\frac{|f|_{d-1}|f^{\prime}|_{d-1}}{|x_{f}-x_{f^{\prime}}|^{d}}\sum_{t\in\mathcal{L}_{m}\cap\mathcal{V}_{f}}h_{t}^{2-d}|\underline{v}_{t}|_{1,t}^{2}\right)^{1/2}

(3.30)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{l}\left(\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd}}}\sum_{f^{\prime}\in\mathcal{W}_{lf}}\frac{1}{l^{d}}\sum_{t\in\mathcal{L}_{m}\cap\mathcal{V}_{f}}|\underline{v}_{t}|_{1,t}^{2}\right)^{1/2},

2.1

\frac{|f|_{d-1}|f^{\prime}|_{d-1}}{|x_{f}-x_{f^{\prime}}|^{d}}\;h_{t}^{2-d}\lesssim\frac{h^{d-1}h^{d-1}}{(lh)^{d}}h^{2-d}=\frac{1}{l^{d}}.

3.2
-
(ii)

\displaystyle\zeta_{l}\lesssim\frac{1}{l}\sum_{m=0}^{l}\left(\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd}}}\sum_{t\in\mathcal{L}_{m}\cap\mathcal{V}_{f}}|\underline{v}_{t}|_{1,t}^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

Lemma 3.3

(Discrete Hardy inequality)
.

If
(r_{m})_{m=0,\dots,L}
are positive numbers and, for

R_{l}\doteq\frac{1}{l}\sum_{m=0}^{l}r_{m}

\sum_{l=1}^{L}R_{l}^{2}\leq 18\sum_{l=0}^{L}r_{l}^{2}.

Setting
r_{m}=\left(\sum_{t\in\mathcal{L}_{m}}|\underline{v}_{t}|_{1,t}^{2}\right)^{1/2}
, the bound (
3.32
) gives
\zeta_{l}\leq R_{l}
, and we infer from Lemma

\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{L}\zeta_{l}^{2}\lesssim\sum_{l=0}^{L}r_{l}^{2},

where we can choose
L

(\mathcal{L}_{m})_{m=0,\ldots,L}

\Omega
. Recalling the definition (

From the definition of
\mathcal{W}_{lf}
in (

(\mathcal{W}_{lf})_{l=1,\dots,L}

\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd}}\backslash\{f\}

\sum_{l=1}^{L}\sum_{f^{\prime}\in\mathcal{W}_{lf}}\bullet=\sum_{f^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd}}\backslash\{f\}}\bullet
and

(3.34)

\sum_{l=1}^{L}\zeta_{l}^{2}=\mathcal{S}_{1}.

In the right-hand side of (

Combining (

) and Figure

-(A)). Then, (

\displaystyle|\overline{v}_{t_{{f\!f^{\prime}},f}}-\overline{v}_{t_{{f\!f^{\prime}},f^{\prime}}}|\lesssim\sum_{t\in\mathcal{I}_{{f\!f^{\prime}}}}h_{t}^{\frac{2-d}{2}}|\underline{v}_{t}|_{1,t}.

The proof of the lifting property follows the same approach as the proof for the discrete trace inequality. We first consider the case of a cube and we lift a piecewise polynomial function defined on one of its sides. To do so, we follow the ideas of the continuous lifting done in
[
15
]
, which we briefly recall in Section
4.1
. Then, we introduce some notations for the discrete setting in Section
4.2
, before giving the proof of the lifting from one side of
\Omega
into
\Omega
in Section
4.3
. The case of a generic polytopal domain
\Omega
is treated in Section
5.1
.

4.1.
Continuous lifting property

We consider here that
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\times(0,1)
is a strip, we take
w\in H^{1/2}(\mathbb R^{d-1})
and we construct
v\in H^{1}(\Omega)
whose trace on
\mathbb R^{d-1}\times\{0\}
coincides with
w
and such that
|v|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\lesssim|w|_{H^{1/2}(\mathbb R^{d-1})}
.

To define the value of
v
at
(x^{\prime},x_{d})\in\Omega
, we consider an integral average of values of
w
on a disc centered at
(x^{\prime},0)
and of radius
x_{d}
. This average is smoothed by a kernel to ensure regularity of
v
. We therefore start from a classical regularisation kernel: for
\epsilon>0
and
z\in\mathbb R^{d-1}
we set
\rho_{\epsilon}(z)=\epsilon^{-(d-1)}\rho(\epsilon^{-1}z)
where
\rho\in C^{1}_{c}(\mathbb R^{d-1})
is non-negative, is supported in the unit disc, and has integral equal to 1. We then set

(4.1)

v(x^{\prime},x_{d})=\int_{\mathbb R^{d-1}}w(y^{\prime})\rho_{x_{d}}(x^{\prime}-y^{\prime})\,dy^{\prime}.

Classical properties of smoothing kernels ensure that
v(\cdot,x_{d})\to w
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d-1})
as
x_{d}\to 0
, which shows that the trace of
v
on
\mathbb R^{d-1}\times\{0\}
is
w
. We now have to estimate the
H^{1}(\Omega)
-seminorm of
v
.

We have

(4.2)

\displaystyle\nabla v(x^{\prime},x_{d})={}

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb R^{d-1}}w(y^{\prime})\nabla_{(x^{\prime},x_{d})}(\rho_{x_{d}}(x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}))\,dy^{\prime}

(4.3)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb R^{d-1}}(w(y^{\prime})-w(x^{\prime}))\nabla_{(x^{\prime},x_{d})}(\rho_{x_{d}}(x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}))\,dy^{\prime}

where, in the second line, we have used

\int_{\mathbb R^{d-1}}\nabla_{(x^{\prime},x_{d})}(\rho_{x_{d}}(x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}))\,dy^{\prime}=\nabla_{(x^{\prime},x_{d})}\int_{\mathbb R^{d-1}}\rho_{x_{d}}(x^{\prime}-y^{\prime})\,dy^{\prime}=\nabla_{(x^{\prime},x_{d})}1=0.

It can easily be checked that

|\nabla_{(x^{\prime},x_{d})}(\rho_{x_{d}}(x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}))|\lesssim\frac{1}{x_{d}^{d}}\left[\rho\left(\frac{x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}}{x_{d}}\right)+\left(1+\left|\frac{x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}}{x_{d}}\right|\right)|\nabla\rho|\left(\frac{x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}}{x_{d}}\right)\right]=\frac{1}{x_{d}^{d}}D\rho\left(\frac{x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}}{x_{d}}\right),

where
z\in\mathbb R^{d-1}\to D\rho(z)\doteq\rho(z)+(1+|z|)|\nabla\rho|(z)
is a compactly supported and bounded function.
Plugging this into (
4.3
), squaring and using a Cauchy–Schwarz inequality yields

\displaystyle|\nabla v(x^{\prime},x_{d})|^{2}\lesssim{}

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb R^{d-1}}|w(x^{\prime})-w(y^{\prime})|\frac{1}{x_{d}^{(d+1)/2}}D\rho\left(\frac{x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}}{x_{d}}\right)^{1/2}\frac{1}{x_{d}^{(d-1)/2}}D\rho\left(\frac{x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}}{x_{d}}\right)^{1/2}\,dy^{\prime}\right)^{2}

(4.4)

\displaystyle\lesssim{}

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb R^{d-1}}|w(x^{\prime})-w(y^{\prime})|^{2}\frac{1}{x_{d}^{d+1}}D\rho\left(\frac{x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}}{x_{d}}\right)\,dy^{\prime}\times\int_{\mathbb R^{d-1}}\frac{1}{x_{d}^{d-1}}D\rho\left(\frac{x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}}{x_{d}}\right)\,dy^{\prime}.

A change of variable
y^{\prime}\to z=\frac{x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}}{x_{d}}
gives

(4.5)

\int_{\mathbb R^{d-1}}\frac{1}{x_{d}^{d-1}}D\rho\left(\frac{x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}}{x_{d}}\right)\,dy^{\prime}=\int_{\mathbb R^{d-1}}D\rho(z)\,dz\lesssim 1.

So, integrating (
4.4
) over
(x^{\prime},x_{d})\in\Omega
, we obtain

(4.6)

\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\lesssim\int_{\mathbb R^{d-1}}\int_{\mathbb R^{d-1}}|w(x^{\prime})-w(y^{\prime})|^{2}\left(\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{x_{d}^{d+1}}D\rho\left(\frac{x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}}{x_{d}}\right)\,dx_{d}\right)\,dx^{\prime}dy^{\prime}.

We then note that
D\rho(z)=0
whenever
|z|\geq 1
, which implies

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{x_{d}^{d+1}}D\rho\left(\frac{x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}}{x_{d}}\right)\,dx_{d}

\displaystyle=\int_{|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|}^{1}\frac{1}{x_{d}^{d+1}}D\rho\left(\frac{x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}}{x_{d}}\right)\,dx_{d}

(4.7)

\displaystyle\leq\|D\rho\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb R^{d-1})}\int_{|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|}^{1}\frac{dx_{d}}{x_{d}^{d+1}}\lesssim\frac{1}{|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|^{d}}.

Inserted into (
4.6
) this gives

\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\lesssim\int_{\mathbb R^{d-1}}\int_{\mathbb R^{d-1}}\frac{|w(x^{\prime})-w(y^{\prime})|^{2}}{|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|^{d}}\,dx^{\prime}dy^{\prime}=|w|_{H^{1/2}(\mathbb R^{d-1})}^{2},

which concludes the proof of the continuous lifting property.

The lifting

\displaystyle v_{t}=\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd}}}\overline{w}_{f}\rho_{t}(f)\qquad\forall t\in\mathcal{T}_{h},

where we recall that

is the average of

over

. Then, using these cell values, we define the face values of

by

(4.13b)

\displaystyle v_{g}

\displaystyle=\frac{v_{t}+v_{t^{\prime}}}{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\text{$\forall g\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm in}}$, with $t,t^{\prime}$ the cells in $\mathcal{T}_{g}$ (neighbouring $g$)},

(4.13c)

\displaystyle v_{f}

\displaystyle=w_{f}

\displaystyle\qquad\text{$\forall f\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd}}$}.

We obviously have
\gamma(\underline{v}_{h})=w_{h}
so it remains to prove that the lifting estimate in (

) holds.

All components of
\underline{v}_{h}
except boundary ones are constant, so for all

we have

and
\|v_{g}-v_{t}\|_{L^{2}(g)}^{2}=|g|_{d-1}|v_{g}-v_{t}|^{2}
for all

\displaystyle|\underline{v}_{h}|_{1,h}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{t\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{t}}h_{t}^{-1}\|v_{f}-v_{t}\|_{L^{2}(f)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{t\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\sum_{g\in\mathcal{F}_{t}\cap\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm in}}}h_{t}^{-1}|g|_{d-1}|v_{g}-v_{t}|^{2}+\sum_{t\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{t}\cap\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd}}}h_{t}^{-1}\|v_{f}-v_{t}\|_{L^{2}(f)}^{2}

(4.14)

\displaystyle\simeq\sum_{g\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm in}}}h^{d-2}(D_{g}v)^{2}+\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd}}}h^{-1}\|w_{f}-v_{t}\|_{L^{2}(f)}^{2}=T_{1}+T_{2},

where, in the sum

and set

(with
t,t^{\prime}
the cells in
\mathcal{T}_{g}
) while, in the sum

,

is the unique cell that contains

. We now conclude by showing that

and

.

Bound on

: For

The faces are pairwise disjoint so

5.
Extensions

5.1.
Proof of the discrete trace and lifting inequalities in polytopal domains

The idea to extend the proofs of the trace and lifting properties to the case of a generic polytope
\Omega
is as follows: (i) we select an atlas of a neighbourhood of
\partial\Omega
such that, in each chart,
\Omega
is the hypergraph of a Lipschitz function, (ii) with minor adaptations of the proofs made in Sections
3
and
4
in the case of a cube, we show how the trace/lifting property can be obtained in each chart, and (iii) we glue together these local properties to obtain global trace/lifting properties.

This section is divided into three subsections. In Section
5.1.1
we discuss the choice of the atlas and the adjustments to the definitions (in particular of the distances to the boundary and on the boundary) that need to be done, compared to the case of a cube, to establish trace and lifting properties in each chart.
The gluing of the local trace inequalities is then detailed in Section
5.1.2
, while that of the local liftings is addressed in Section
5.1.3
.

5.1.1.
Localisation

Consider a generic polytope

. We can cover

\displaystyle\Omega\cap U_{i}=\left\{(x^{\prime},x_{d})\in V_{i}\times J_{i}:x_{d}>\phi_{i}(x^{\prime})\right\},

where
\phi_{i}:V_{i}\longrightarrow J_{i}
is a Lipschitz function and
U_{i}=V_{i}\times J_{i}
(see Figure
6

\Omega
, we have
\#I\lesssim 1
and that the Lipschitz constant of each
\phi_{i}
is
\lesssim 1
.

C1.

The notion of distances to/along

\partial\Omega\cap(U_{i}\cap U_{j})\not=\emptyset
. For any
F_{ij}\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd},i}\cap\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd},j}
, we have

|\overline{w}_{f}-\overline{w}_{f^{\prime}}|^{2}\leq 2|\overline{w}_{f}-\overline{w}_{F_{ij}}|^{2}+2|\overline{w}_{F_{ij}}-\overline{w}_{f^{\prime}}|^{2}
and so

\displaystyle T_{ij}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{(f,f^{\prime})\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd},i}\times\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd},j}}|f|_{d-1}|f^{\prime}|_{d-1}|\overline{w}_{f}-\overline{w}_{F_{ij}}|^{2}+\sum_{(f,f^{\prime})\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd},i}\times\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd},j}}|f|_{d-1}|f^{\prime}|_{d-1}|\overline{w}_{F_{ij}}-\overline{w}_{f^{\prime}}|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd},i}}|f|_{d-1}|\overline{w}_{f}-\overline{w}_{F_{ij}}|^{2}+\sum_{f^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd},j}}|f^{\prime}|_{d-1}|\overline{w}_{F_{ij}}-\overline{w}_{f^{\prime}}|^{2},

where, in the second inequality, we have used
\sum_{g\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd},k}}|g|_{d-1}\leq|\partial\Omega|_{d-1}\lesssim 1
for
k=i,j
. Multiplying by
|F_{ij}|_{d-1}
and summing over
F_{ij}\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd},i}\cap\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd},j}
we infer

\displaystyle\left|\bigcup(\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd},i}\cap\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd},j})\right|_{d-1}T_{ij}\lesssim{}

\displaystyle\sum_{(f,F_{ij})\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd},i}\times(\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd},i}\cap\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd},j})}|f|_{d-1}|F_{ij}|_{d-1}|\overline{w}_{f}-\overline{w}_{F_{ij}}|^{2}

\displaystyle+\sum_{(F_{ij},f^{\prime})\in(\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd},i}\cap\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd},j})\times\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd},j}}|f^{\prime}|_{d-1}|F_{ij}|_{d-1}|\overline{w}_{F_{ij}}-\overline{w}_{f^{\prime}}|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim{}

\displaystyle|w_{h}|_{1/2,h,i}^{2}+|w_{h}|_{1/2,h,j}^{2},

the conclusion following from
|x_{g}-x_{F_{ij}}|^{d}\leq\mathrm{diam}(\partial\Omega)^{d}
for
g=f,f^{\prime}
. Recalling (
5.8
) gives the desired bound on
T_{ij}
.

If
\partial\Omega\cap(U_{i}\cap U_{j})=\emptyset
, we can find a chain
U_{i}=U_{i(1)},U_{i(2)},\cdots,U_{i(n)}=U_{j}
with
n\leq\#I\lesssim 1
such that

\partial\Omega\cap(U_{i(k)}\cap U_{i(k+1)})\not=\emptyset
for all
k\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}
. For all
k
we take an arbitrary
F_{i(k)i(k+1)}\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd},i(k)}\cap\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd},i(k+1)}
and write, for
(f,f^{\prime})\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd},i}\times\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd},j}
,

|w_{f}-w_{f^{\prime}}|^{2}\overset{\eqref{CSinRn},\,n\lesssim 1}{\lesssim}|w_{f}-w_{F_{i(1)i(2)}}|^{2}+|w_{F_{i(1)i(2)}}-w_{F_{i(2)i(3)}}|^{2}+\cdots+|w_{F_{i(n-1)i(n)}}-w_{f^{\prime}}|^{2}.

We then reason in a similar way as in the intersecting case above: multiply this relation by the product
|f|_{d-1}|f^{\prime}|_{d-1}|F_{i(1)i(2)}|_{d-1}\cdots|F_{i(n-1)i(n)}|_{d-1}
, sum over all the involved faces in their respective sets, and invoke (
5.8
) to see that
T_{ij}\lesssim|w_{h}|_{1/2,h,i(1)}^{2}+|w_{h}|_{1/2,h,i(2)}^{2}+\cdots+|w_{h}|_{1/2,h,i(n)}^{2}
.
∎

5.1.3.
Lifting property

Given
w_{h}\in U_{h}^{\partial,{\rm bd}}
and following the same arguments as in Section
4.3
– with the adaptations mentioned in Section
5.1.1
– we can find for all
i\in I
a local lifting
\underline{\tilde{v}}_{h,i}\in\underline{U}^{i}_{h}
of the restriction
w_{h,i}\in U_{h}^{\partial,{\rm bd},i}
of
w_{h}
, such that

(5.12)

\displaystyle|\underline{\tilde{v}}_{h,i}|_{1,h,i}\lesssim|w_{h,i}|_{1/2,h,i}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\tilde{v}_{f,i}=w_{f}\quad\forall f\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd},i}.

Our aim here is to leverage these local liftings to create a global lifting
\underline{v}_{h}\in\underline{U}_{h}
of
w_{h}\in U_{h}^{\partial,{\rm bd}}
.

First, without loss of generality we can assume that

(5.13)

\int_{\partial\Omega}w_{h}=0.

Indeed, this relation always holds upon adding a constant
C
to
w_{h}
, and the constant function
C\in U_{h}^{\partial,{\rm bd}}
can be trivially lifted as the vector
((C)_{t\in\mathcal{T}_{h}},(C)_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{h}})\in\underline{U}_{h}
.

Consider a partition of unity
(\eta_{i})_{i\in I}
on
\partial\Omega
associated with
(U_{i})_{i\in I}
, such that

\displaystyle\eta_{i}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(U_{i}),\quad 0\leq\eta_{i}\leq 1,\quad\sum_{i\in I}\eta_{i}=1\mbox{ on $\partial\Omega$}.

Upon reducing
h_{0}
, we can also ensure that each
\eta_{i}
vanishes on an
h_{0}
-neighbourhood of
\partial U_{i}
.
Recalling that
\pi_{X}^{0}
denotes the
L^{2}(X)
-orthogonal projection on
\mathbb{P}_{0}(X)
, and extending each
\underline{\tilde{v}}_{h,i}
by zero on cells/faces that do not have their centroid in
U_{i}
, we then set
\underline{v}_{h,i}\in\underline{U}_{h}
such that

(5.14)

\displaystyle v_{t,i}={}

\displaystyle(\pi_{t}^{0}\eta_{i})\tilde{v}_{t,i}\quad\forall t\in\mathcal{T}_{h},

\displaystyle v_{f,i}={}

\displaystyle(\pi_{f}^{0}\eta_{i})\tilde{v}_{f,i}\quad\forall f\in\mathcal{F}_{h}.

Define
\underline{v}_{h}=\sum_{i\in I}\underline{v}_{h,i}
. Using Proposition
5.3
below, we get

(5.15)

|\underline{v}_{h}|_{1,h}\lesssim|w_{h}|_{1/2,h}.

Furthermore, for all
f\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd}}
,

(5.16)

(\gamma(\underline{v}_{h}))|_{f}=\sum_{i\in I}v_{f,i}=\sum_{i\in I}(\pi_{f}^{0}\eta_{i})\tilde{v}_{f,i}=\sum_{i\in I}(\pi_{f}^{0}\eta_{i})w_{f}=w_{f},

where the last two equalities are obtained by noticing that, since
\pi_{f}^{0}\eta_{i}=0
whenever
f\not\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd},i}
(since
\eta_{i}
vanishes on an
h
-neighbourhood of
\partial U_{i}
),

\displaystyle(\pi_{f}^{0}\eta_{i})\tilde{v}_{f,i}\overset{\eqref{eq:lifting.local}}{=}(\pi_{f}^{0}\eta_{i})w_{f}\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;\sum_{i\in I}(\pi_{f}^{0}\eta_{i})=\pi_{f}^{0}(\sum_{i\in I}\eta_{i})=\pi_{f}^{0}1=1.

The combination of (
5.15
) and (
5.16
) concludes the proof of Theorem
2.3
in the case where
\Omega
is a polytope.

The following lemma was used in the argument above.

Proposition 5.3
.

Assuming that
w_{h}\in U_{h}^{\partial,{\rm bd}}
satisfies (
5.13
) and defining
\underline{v}_{h,i}
by (
5.14
) with
\underline{\tilde{v}}_{h,i}
satisfying (
5.12
), we have

|\underline{v}_{h,i}|_{1,h}\lesssim|w_{h}|_{1/2,h}
.

The localisation process used in the proof of Proposition
5.3
generates
L^{2}
-norms of the discrete functions that have to be estimated in terms of their seminorms. To achieve this, we use the following two lemmas. The first one locally bounds the
L^{2}
-norm inside the domain by the discrete
H^{1}
-seminorm and the
L^{2}
-norm of the trace. The second one bounds the remaining
L^{2}
-norm of the trace by the discrete
H^{1/2}
-seminorm on the boundary.

Lemma 5.4
.

For all
\underline{\widehat{v}}_{h,i}\in\bigtimes_{t\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{i}}\mathbb{P}_{0}(t)\times\bigtimes_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{i}}\mathbb{P}_{0}(f)
(the lowest order hybrid space in
\Omega\cap U_{i}
), it holds

(5.17)

\|\widehat{v}_{h,i}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\cap U_{i})}\lesssim|\underline{\widehat{v}}_{h,i}|_{\Omega\cap U_{i},2}+\|\gamma(\underline{\widehat{v}}_{h,i})\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega\cap U_{i})},

where
\widehat{v}_{h,i}
is the piecewise constant function defined by
(\widehat{v}_{h,i})_{|t}=\widehat{v}_{t}
for all
t\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{i}
, and

|\underline{\widehat{v}}_{h,i}|_{\Omega\cap U_{i},2}^{2}\doteq\sum_{t\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{i}}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{t}}\frac{|f|_{d-1}}{d_{t,f}}|\widehat{v}_{f,i}-\widehat{v}_{t,i}|^{2},

with
d_{t,f}
denoting the orthogonal distance between
f
and the centre of the largest ball contained in
t
.

Proof.

[
16
, Lemma B.22]
establishes the global version of (
5.17
), that is, when
U_{i}
is not present. However, the proof in this reference provides a more precise result. Specifically, it consists in selecting a unit vector
\mathbf{e}
and in bounding the value of a hybrid function on a cell
t
using (i) its local discrete
H^{1}
-seminorms on cells crossed by
t+\mathbb R^{+}\mathbf{e}
, and (ii) its boundary values on
(t+\mathbb R^{+}\mathbf{e})\cap\partial\Omega
. Given the structure of our localisation, if we take
\mathbf{e}=(0,\ldots,0,-1)
in the coordinates system corresponding to
U_{i}
and apply the argument in the proof of
[
16
, Lemma B.22]
, we naturally get (
5.17
) by noticing that, for
t\subset\Omega\cap U_{i}
, (i) the cells crossed by
t+\mathbb R^{+}\mathbf{e}
are in
\mathcal{T}_{h}^{i}
, and (ii)
(t+\mathbb R^{+}\mathbf{e})\cap\partial\Omega\subset U_{i}\cap\partial\Omega
.

To be completely rigorous,
t+\mathbb R^{+}\mathbf{e}
could cross some cells
s
that are not in
\mathcal{T}_{h}^{i}
– when
s
overlaps with
U_{i}
but
x_{s}\not\in U_{i}
. This situation can be fixed by defining
\widehat{v}_{s,i}
as the average of the face values
\widehat{v}_{f,i}
for
f\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{i}\cap\mathcal{F}_{s}
, by fixing
\widehat{v}_{g,i}=\widehat{v}_{s,i}
for all
g\in\mathcal{F}_{s}\backslash\mathcal{F}_{h}^{i}
, and by noticing that the added term
\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{s}}\frac{|f|_{d-1}}{d_{s,f}}|\widehat{v}_{f,i}-\widehat{v}_{s,i}|^{2}
in
|\underline{\widehat{v}}_{h,i}|_{\Omega\cap U_{i},2}^{2}
can be bounded by the other terms already present in this seminorm, since all
f\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{i}\cap\mathcal{F}_{s}
are shared with cells in
\mathcal{T}_{h}^{i}
.
∎

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
P_{0}\subset\partial\Omega
be the union of certain faces in
\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd}}
. For all
w_{h}\in U_{h}^{\partial,{\rm bd}}
such that
\int_{P_{0}}w_{h}=0
, we have

\|w_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\lesssim\left(1+\frac{\mathrm{diam}(\partial\Omega)^{d}}{|P_{0}|_{d-1}}\right)^{1/2}|w_{h}|_{1/2,h}.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\|w_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd}}}\|w_{f}\|_{L^{2}(f)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd}}}\|w_{f}-\overline{w}_{f}\|_{L^{2}(f)}^{2}+2\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd}}}\|\overline{w}_{f}\|_{L^{2}(f)}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd}}}h_{f}^{-1}\|w_{f}-\overline{w}_{f}\|_{L^{2}(f)}^{2}+\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd}}}|f|_{d-1}|\overline{w}_{f}|^{2}

(5.18)

\displaystyle\lesssim|w_{h}|_{1/2,h}^{2}+\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd}}}|f|_{d-1}|\overline{w}_{f}|^{2}.

Let us now work on the second term in (
5.18
). Notice that

\displaystyle 0=\int_{P_{0}}w_{h}=\sum_{f^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd}}(P_{0})}\int_{f^{\prime}}w_{f^{\prime}}=\sum_{f^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd}}(P_{0})}|f^{\prime}|_{d-1}\overline{w}_{f^{\prime}},

where
\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd}}(P_{0})
is the set of faces that form
P_{0}
.
Using this relation, we can write

\displaystyle\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd}}}|f|_{d-1}|\overline{w}_{f}|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd}}}|f|_{d-1}\left|\overline{w}_{f}-\frac{1}{|P_{0}|_{d-1}}\sum_{f^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd}}(P_{0})}|f^{\prime}|_{d-1}\overline{w}_{f^{\prime}}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd}}}|f|_{d-1}\left|\frac{1}{|P_{0}|_{d-1}}\sum_{f^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd}}(P_{0})}|f^{\prime}|_{d-1}(\overline{w}_{f}-\overline{w}_{f^{\prime}})\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd}}}|f|_{d-1}\frac{1}{|P_{0}|_{d-1}}\sum_{f^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd}}(P_{0})}|f^{\prime}|_{d-1}|\overline{w}_{f}-\overline{w}_{f^{\prime}}|^{2},

where the last inequality comes from the convexity of
s\to s^{2}
. For all

(f,f^{\prime})\in\mathcal{F\!F}_{h}^{{\rm bd}}
we have
|x_{f}-x_{f^{\prime}}|\lesssim\mathrm{diam}(\partial\Omega)
. Hence,

\displaystyle\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd}}}|f|_{d-1}|\overline{w}_{f}|^{2}\lesssim\frac{\mathrm{diam}(\partial\Omega)^{d}}{|P_{0}|_{d-1}}\sum_{(f,f^{\prime})\in\mathcal{F\!F}_{h}^{{\rm bd}}}|f|_{d-1}|f^{\prime}|_{d-1}\frac{|\overline{w}_{f}-\overline{w}_{f^{\prime}}|^{2}}{|x_{f}-x_{f^{\prime}}|^{d}}\leq\frac{\mathrm{diam}(\partial\Omega)^{d}}{|P_{0}|_{d-1}}|w_{h}|_{1/2,h}^{2}.

Plugging the above inequality into (
5.18
) concludes the proof.
∎

We are now ready to prove Proposition
5.3
.

Proof of Proposition
5.3
.

For
t\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
and
f\in\mathcal{F}_{t}
, we have

v_{t,i}-v_{f,i}\overset{\eqref{eq:def.vhi}}{=}(\pi_{t}^{0}\eta_{i})\tilde{v}_{t,i}-(\pi_{f}^{0}\eta_{i})\tilde{v}_{f,i}=(\pi_{t}^{0}\eta_{i}-\pi_{f}^{0}\eta_{i})\tilde{v}_{t,i}+(\pi_{f}^{0}\eta_{i})(\tilde{v}_{t,i}-\tilde{v}_{f,i}).

Since
\eta_{i}
is smooth,
|\pi_{t}^{0}\eta_{i}-\pi_{f}^{0}\eta_{i}|\lesssim h_{t}
and
|\eta_{i}|\lesssim 1
, so

\displaystyle h_{t}^{-1}\|v_{t,i}-v_{f,i}\|_{L^{2}(f)}^{2}\lesssim h_{t}\|\tilde{v}_{t,i}\|_{L^{2}(f)}^{2}+h_{t}^{-1}\|\tilde{v}_{t,i}-\tilde{v}_{f,i}\|_{L^{2}(f)}^{2}.

Noticing that
v_{t,i}
is constant (recall (
4.13a
) and (
5.14
)), the definition (
2.1
) of
|{\cdot}|_{1,t}
and the discrete trace inequality
[
11
, Lemma 1.32]
then give

(5.19)

|\underline{v}_{t,i}|_{1,t}^{2}\lesssim\|\tilde{v}_{t,i}\|_{L^{2}(t)}^{2}+|\underline{\tilde{v}}_{h,i}|_{1,t}^{2}.

By (
5.14
) and choice of the support of
\eta_{i}
, we have
\underline{v}_{t,i}=\underline{0}
whenever
t\not\subset U_{i}
, from which we infer

(5.20)

|\underline{v}_{h,i}|_{1,h}^{2}=\sum_{t\subset U_{i}}|\underline{v}_{t,i}|_{1,t}^{2}\overset{\eqref{eq:lift.bound.local}}{\lesssim}\|\tilde{v}_{h,i}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\cap U_{i})}^{2}+|\underline{\tilde{v}}_{h,i}|_{1,h,i}^{2}\lesssim\|\tilde{v}_{h,i}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\cap U_{i})}^{2}+|w_{h}|_{1/2,h}^{2},

where we have used (
5.12
) and
|w_{h,i}|_{1/2,h,i}\leq|w_{h}|_{1/2,h}
in the last inequality.

We deal with the first term in the right-hand side of (
5.20
) using Lemma
5.4
. Let
\underline{\widehat{v}}_{h,i}
be the lowest-order projection of
\underline{\tilde{v}}_{h,i}
, that is:
\widehat{v}_{t,i}=\tilde{v}_{t,i}\in\mathbb{P}_{0}(t)
for all
t\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
(recall that the lifting
\underline{\tilde{v}}_{h,i}
has constant cell values) and
\widehat{v}_{f,i}=\pi_{f}^{0}\tilde{v}_{f,i}\in\mathbb{P}_{0}(f)
for all
f\in\mathcal{F}_{h}
. Then, Lemma
5.4
gives

(5.21)

\|\tilde{v}_{h,i}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\cap U_{i})}=\|\widehat{v}_{h,i}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\cap U_{i})}\lesssim|\underline{\widehat{v}}_{h,i}|_{\Omega\cap U_{i},2}+\|\gamma(\underline{\widehat{v}}_{h,i})\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega\cap U_{i})}.

Using
d_{t,f}\simeq h_{t}
(by mesh regularity) and applying (
3.23
) to
\underline{\tilde{v}}_{t,i}
, we get
|\underline{\widehat{v}}_{h,i}|_{\Omega\cap U_{i},2}\lesssim|{\underline{\tilde{v}}_{h,i}}|_{1,h,i}
. Moreover, for all
f\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd}}
,
\|\widehat{v}_{f,i}\|_{L^{2}(f)}=\|\pi_{f}^{0}\tilde{v}_{f,i}\|_{L^{2}(f)}\leq\|\tilde{v}_{f,i}\|_{L^{2}(f)}\overset{\eqref{eq:lifting.local}}{=}\|w_{f}\|_{L^{2}(f)}
. Plugging these estimates into (
5.21
) yields

(5.22)

\|\tilde{v}_{h,i}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\cap U_{i})}\lesssim|{\underline{\tilde{v}}_{h,i}}|_{1,h,i}+\|w_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\overset{\eqref{eq:lifting.local}}{\lesssim}|w_{h}|_{1/2,h},

where, in the conclusion, we have additionally used Lemma
5.5
with
P_{0}=\partial\Omega
, recalling that
w_{h}
satisfies (
5.13
).
Plugging (
5.22
) into (
5.20
) concludes the proof of Proposition
5.3
.
∎

5.2.
More general hybrid spaces

A range of polytopal methods are based on discrete spaces spanned by vectors of polynomials in the cells and on the faces: Hybrid High-Order
[
11
]
, Hybridizable Discontinuous Galerkin
[
6
]
, non-conforming Virtual Element Method
[
2
]
, Weak Galerkin
[
22
]
, etc. On the contrary to those in
\underline{U}_{h}
, these polynomials may not be of equal degree in the cells and on the faces, or even not of the same degrees on all cells/faces. Nonetheless, an easy argument shows that the results of Theorems
2.2
and
2.3
still apply to these situations.

Let us briefly detail this argument when the underlying space
\underline{V}_{h}
of a considered method is made of polynomials having different degrees in cells and faces:
for some
\ell,r\geq 0
,

\underline{V}_{h}=\bigtimes_{t\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\mathbb{P}_{\ell}(t)\times\bigtimes_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}\mathbb{P}_{r}(f).

Setting
k=\max(\ell,r)
, we have
\underline{V}_{h}\subset\underline{U}_{h}
. The restriction to
\underline{V}_{h}
of the trace operator
\gamma:\underline{U}_{h}\to U_{h}^{\partial,{\rm bd}}
yields a trace operator
\gamma_{V,h}:\underline{V}_{h}\to V_{h}^{\partial,{\rm bd}}
where
V_{h}^{\partial,{\rm bd}}\doteq\bigtimes_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd}}}\mathbb{P}_{r}(f)
.
Endowing
\underline{V}_{h}
and
V_{h}^{\partial,{\rm bd}}
with the restrictions of the discrete norms (
2.1
) and (
2.2
), respectively, Theorem
2.2
applied to
\underline{U}_{h}
directly gives a trace inequality for
\gamma_{V,h}
. In practical situations, it can be checked that the energy norm of a specific method is equivalent to the restriction of (
2.1
) on its space; hence, the trace inequality obtained for
\gamma_{V,h}
is a suitable one, as it can be written in terms of this energy norm on
\underline{V}_{h}
.

Concerning the lifting, since
V_{h}^{\partial,{\rm bd}}\subset U_{h}^{\partial,{\rm bd}}
, using Theorem
2.3
we can lift any
w_{h}\in V_{h}^{\partial,{\rm bd}}
into
\underline{v}_{h}\in\underline{U}_{h}
. An examination of the construction of this lifting (see, in particular, (
4.13
) and (
5.14
)) shows that
v_{t}\in\mathbb{P}_{0}(t)
for all
t\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
and
v_{f}\in\mathbb{P}_{0}(f)
for all
f\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm in}}
. Since
v_{f}=w_{f}\in\mathbb{P}_{r}(f)
for all
f\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd}}
, we infer that the lifting
\underline{v}_{h}
actually belongs to
\underline{V}_{h}
. This argument therefore gives a lifting
V_{h}^{\partial,{\rm bd}}\to\underline{V}_{h}
, with suitable seminorm estimates.

Remark 5.6

(No cell values)
.

Some low-order schemes (e.g., some variants of HHO
[
11
, Section 5.1]
) do not have cell values. In this case, the lifted
\underline{v}_{h}
constructed above does not belong to the space of the method. An easy work-around then consists in simply discarding the cell unknowns in
\underline{v}_{h}
and in only keeping the face unknowns.

Remark 5.7

(No face values)
.

Some polytopal methods rely on discrete spaces without face values – for example, broken polynomial spaces on the mesh cells as in DG.
These spaces can however easily be embedded into
\underline{U}_{h}
, by adding face values equal to the averages of the traces of the two cell values on each side of each face (or just the trace of the cell value containing a boundary face). This allows for a straightforward adaptation of Theorems
2.2
and
2.3
to these methods.

6.
Numerical tests

In this section, we present numerical simulations that validate the theoretical results. The experiment is conducted in
Gridap

[
3
,
27
]
, an open source PDE approximation toolbox written in the Julia programming language. The domain is a unit square meshed with a family of uniform Cartesian meshes that satisfy the mesh regularity Assumption
2.1
. The test verifies the equivalence, for discretely harmonic functions, between the discrete
H^{1}
-seminorm on the domain and the discrete
H^{1/2}
-seminorm on the boundary, which is a consequence of Theorems
2.2
and
2.3
.
We present the construction of the test, followed by a brief explanation of the results.

Given
w_{h}\in U_{h}^{\partial,{\rm bd}}
, let us define the discrete harmonic extension
\mathcal{E}_{h}({w}_{h})\in\underline{U}_{h}
such that
\mathcal{E}_{h}({w}_{h})={w}_{h}
on
\partial\Omega
and
\mathcal{E}_{h}(w_{h})
minimises the
H^{1}(\Omega)
-seminorm; in other words,
\mathcal{E}_{h}(w_{h})
solves

\text{$\mathcal{E}_{h}(w_{h})=w_{h}$ on $\partial\Omega$, and $\langle\mathcal{A}_{h}\mathcal{E}_{h}(w_{h}),\underline{v}_{h}\rangle=0$ for all $\underline{v}_{h}\in\underline{U}_{h,0}$},

where
\mathcal{A}_{h}
is the operator corresponding to the discrete
H^{1}(\Omega)
-seminorm and
\underline{U}_{h,0}
is the subspace of
\underline{U}_{h}
with zero trace on
\partial\Omega
. The following seminorm equivalence is a direct consequence of Theorems
2.2
and
2.3
, and of the definition of the discrete harmonic extension:

(6.1)

\displaystyle|\mathcal{E}_{h}(w_{h})|_{1,h}\simeq|{w}_{h}|_{1/2,h}\qquad\forall w_{h}\in U_{h}^{\partial,{\rm bd}}.

Using Lemma
5.5
, we note that this seminorm equivalence is actually a norm equivalence on the space
U_{h}^{\partial,{\rm bd}}\cap L_{0}^{2}(\partial\Omega)
, where
L_{0}^{2}(\partial\Omega)
is the subspace of
L^{2}(\partial\Omega)
spanned by functions with zero integral on the boundary.

We consider a suitable choice of basis vectors spanning the hybrid space
\underline{U}_{h}
, following an approach analogous to the one detailed in
[
11
, Appendix B]
. We number the boundary faces as
(f_{m})_{m=1,\ldots,\#\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd}}}
, and label shape functions
\phi_{i}^{f_{m}}\in U_{h}^{\partial,{\rm bd}}
on each face
f_{m}
by a face-local index
i\in\{1,\ldots,\mathcal{N}_{f_{m}}\}
,
\mathcal{N}_{f_{m}}{=\mathrm{dim}(\mathbb{P}_{k}(f_{m}))}
being the number of degrees of freedom belonging to
f_{m}
.

Let
\bm{\mathcal{A}}
and
\bm{\mathcal{H}}
be the Gram matrices associated to the discrete
H^{1}(\Omega)
- and
H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)
-seminorms (
2.1
) and (
2.2
), respectively.
\bm{\mathcal{A}}
has the following block structure:

\bm{\mathcal{A}}=\left[\begin{array}[]{c c c}\bm{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{T}_{h}\mathcal{T}_{h}}&\bm{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{T}_{h}\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm in}}}&\bm{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{T}_{h}\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd}}}\\
\bm{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm in}}\mathcal{T}_{h}}&\bm{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm in}}\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm in}}}&\bm{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm in}}\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd}}}\\
\bm{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd}}\mathcal{T}_{h}}&\bm{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd}}\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm in}}}&\bm{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd}}\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd}}}\end{array}\right].

We can compute the Schur complement of this matrix with respect to boundary degrees of freedom, denoted by
\bm{\mathcal{A}}_{SC}
, as

\bm{\mathcal{A}}_{SC}\doteq\bm{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd}}\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd}}}-\left[\bm{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd}}\mathcal{T}_{h}}\;\;\bm{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd}}\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm in}}}\right]\begin{bmatrix}\bm{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{T}_{h}\mathcal{T}_{h}}&\bm{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{T}_{h}\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm in}}}\\
\bm{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm in}}\mathcal{T}_{h}}&\bm{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm in}}\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm in}}}\end{bmatrix}^{-1}\begin{bmatrix}\bm{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{T}_{h}\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd}}}\\
\bm{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm in}}\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd}}}\end{bmatrix}.

\bm{\mathcal{A}}_{SC}
is a symmetric and positive semi-definite matrix of dimension
\mathcal{N}^{\partial,{\rm bd}}\times\mathcal{N}^{\partial,{\rm bd}}
, where
\mathcal{N}^{\partial,{\rm bd}}
is the dimension of
U_{h}^{\partial,{\rm bd}}
.

In order to build the hybrid operator
\bm{\mathcal{H}}
, we restrict the shape functions to the ones associated with the boundary faces, that is, the shape functions that span
U_{h}^{\partial,{\rm bd}}
.
The non-local part of
\bm{\mathcal{H}}
can be written in matrix form by assembling contributions by pairs of faces.

Given a pair of boundary faces
(f_{l},f_{m})\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd}}\times\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd}}
, we define the corresponding local contribution
K\bm{\Delta}^{\top}\bm{\Delta}
as:

\displaystyle K\doteq\frac{|f_{l}|_{d-1}|f_{m}|_{d-1}}{|x_{f_{l}}-x_{f_{m}}|^{d}},\qquad

\displaystyle\bm{\Delta}_{ij}\doteq\left[\bar{\phi}^{f_{l}}_{i}-\bar{\phi}^{f_{m}}_{j}\right]_{1\leq i\leq\mathcal{N}_{f_{l}},1\leq j\leq\mathcal{N}_{f_{m}}}.

This local 4D array is assembled into the global matrix
\bm{\mathcal{H}}
by using a local-to-global indexing of the degrees of freedom associated with the boundary faces. The local part of the operator
\bm{\mathcal{H}}
can be computed analogously to
\bm{\mathcal{A}}
, resulting in a matrix
\bm{\mathcal{H}}
of dimension
\mathcal{N}^{\partial,{\rm bd}}\times\mathcal{N}^{\partial,{\rm bd}}
.

To avoid zero eigenvalues and indefinite matrices, we proceed as follows. We define the rank-one matrix
\bm{S}^{\top}\bm{S}
, where
\bm{S}\doteq[\int_{\partial\Omega}\phi_{a}]_{1\leq a\leq\mathcal{N}^{\partial,{\rm bd}}}
is a row vector,
\phi_{a}
denoting the shape functions of
U_{h}^{\partial,{\rm bd}}
labelled with global index
a
.
Thus, if
\bm{w}
is the vector representing
w_{h}\in U_{h}^{\partial,{\rm bd}}
in the chosen basis on
U_{h}^{\partial,{\rm bd}}
, we have
\bm{S}\bm{w}=\int_{\partial\Omega}{w}_{h}
and
\bm{w}^{\top}\bm{S}^{\top}\bm{S}\bm{w}=(\int_{\partial\Omega}w_{h})^{2}
;
\bm{S}
thus serves the purpose of fixing the kernels of the operators
\bm{\mathcal{H}}
and
\bm{\mathcal{A}}_{SC}
. Since the bilinear form
\bm{S}^{\top}\bm{S}
vanishes on vectors corresponding to functions with zero integral, the seminorm equivalence (
6.1
) readily implies:

(6.2)

\displaystyle\bm{w}^{\top}\left(\bm{\mathcal{H}}+\bm{S}^{\top}\bm{S}\right)\bm{w}\simeq\bm{w}^{\top}\left(\bm{\mathcal{A}}_{SC}+\bm{S}^{\top}\bm{S}\right)\bm{w},\qquad\forall{w}_{h}\in U_{h}^{\partial,{\rm bd}}\cap L^{2}_{0}(\partial\Omega).

Using the fact that both sides in the above equality match for vectors corresponding to constant functions, and that
(U_{h}^{\partial,{\rm bd}}\cap L^{2}_{0}(\partial\Omega))\oplus\mathbb{R}=U_{h}^{\partial,{\rm bd}}
, we see that (
6.2
) implies the equivalence (
6.1
) for all functions in
U_{h}^{\partial,{\rm bd}}
.

We experimentally verify the equivalence in (
6.2
) by comparing the eigenvalues of the operators
\bm{\mathcal{H}}
and
\bm{\mathcal{A}}_{SC}
. In particular, we solve the following generalised eigenvalue problem:

(6.3)

\displaystyle\left[\left(\bm{\mathcal{H}}+\bm{S}^{\top}\bm{S}\right)^{-1}\left(\bm{\mathcal{A}}_{SC}+\bm{S}^{\top}\bm{S}\right)\right]\bm{W}=\bm{W}\bm{\Lambda}.

Here, the diagonal elements
\bm{\Lambda}\doteq\bm{\mbox{diag}}(\lambda_{i})_{i=1,\ldots,\mathcal{N}^{\partial,{\rm bd}}}
are the eigenvalues and the columns of
\bm{W}
are the eigenvectors. In order for (
6.2
) to hold, the eigenvalues of (
6.3
) should be bounded away from zero and bounded above by a constant independent of the mesh size.

Figure
8
presents the maximum and minimum eigenvalues of (
6.3
) against the number of degrees of freedom associated with the hybrid space
\underline{U}_{h}
, for different polynomial orders.
We see an asymptotic trend towards a constant for the maximum and minimum eigenvalues as we increase the number of degrees of freedom, indicating that the eigenvalues of (
6.3
) are indeed bounded above and below independently of
h
, which verifies the correctness of Theorems
2.2
and
2.3
.

Rota-Tripsy or step-up-approach rotational atherectomy for severe coronary artery calcification treatment: a comparative effectiveness study
Authors: Fengwen Cui, Yaliang Tong, Ping Yang, Guohui Liu, Beibei Du, Xiangdong Li, Kun Liu, Jiwen Fan, Daoyuan Si, Yuquan He
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Step-up-approach rotational atherectomy has been widely recognized in the treatment of severe calcified plaques. As an alternative, the rota-tripsy procedure is a novel strategy for the modification of calcification lesions. This study aimed to evaluate and compare the efficacy and safety of rota-tripsy and step-up-approach rotational atherectomy, given the limited clinical data available. Clinical data of 3652 patients with coronary calcification were retrospectively collected from February 2021 to February 2024. The cases were divided into two groups based on the technical approach. The primary efficacy endpoint was procedural success (stent expansion rate > 70% and no hospital major adverse cardiac events [MACEs]). The primary safety endpoint was the absence of serious angiographic complications and 30 days MACEs. A total of 37 patients (38 calcified lesions) screened and included in the study, with 18 cases (19 lesions) in group A and 19 cases in group B. The results showed no significant differences in primary efficacy endpoint. However, group A recorded a lower incidence of angiographic complications (P = 0.02) and shorter dose area product and fluoroscopy/interval times compared with group B (P < 0.01). For management of severe coronary calcification, both rota-tripsy and step-up-approach rotational atherectomy had favorable short-term clinical results; however, rota-tripsy provided improved safety and efficiency.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-024-80769-5.

Introduction

Coronary artery calcification (CAC) requires a complex intervention procedure1. In the past, various procedures, including cutting balloons, rotational atherectomy (RA), and excimer laser coronary angioplasty, have facilitated plaque modification. Nevertheless, before the advent of intravascular lithotripsy (IVL), the efficacy of excimer laser coronary angioplasty was limited for balloon-uncrossable severe calcified lesions, and rotational strategy usually insufficient to achieve adequent plaque modification2. Therefore, the step-up-approach rotational atherectomy was often recommended as an effective solution. However, more complex operations require increased operator experience, which can reduce the procedure time and the risk of complications.

In 2019, Jurado-Román et al.3. proposed the “rota-tripsy.” Firstly, an operating channel is created using a small-sized rotary burr which intravascular ultrasound (IVUS) or optical coherence tomography (OCT) can pass through, and then a shockwave balloon is inserted to reach the calcified site. Subsequently, other studies have also explored and confirmed the feasibility and safety of rota-tripsy in the modification of calcium lesions4.

However, clinical data are relatively rare regarding whether rota-tripsy is a superior interventional procedure compared with the burr-size-escalation maneuver RA for severely calcified lesions. Herein, we reported a comparative effectiveness study with cohort design on rota-tripsy and step-up-approach RA approach. We assessed the approaches according to their immediate effectiveness and safety for the management of severely calcified lesions.

Methods

We followed the Reporting of Studies Conducted Using Observational Routinely Collected Data guidelines, which are an extension of the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology guidelines5. This is a comparative effectiveness study with cohort design. The raw data supporting the conclusions of this article will be made available by the authors at reasonable request. This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of China-Japan Union Hospital of Jilin University (2023033010), and registered on Chinese Clinical Trial Registry (ChiCTR2300076614). The requirement for informed consent was waived; personal identification information was removed after data collection in accordance with confidentiality guidelines.

Patient screening and study design

We screened 3652 patients for interventional therapy under intracoronary imaging devices using the clinical data of patients admitted to the Department of Cardiovascular Medicine, China-Japan Union Hospital of Jilin University, from February 2021 to February 2024. There were 2987 patients diagnosed as coronary calcification. The data were retrospectively collected and screened according to the inclusion and exclusion criteria. There were 97 patients undergoing IVL procedure, 387 patients undergoing RA procedure and 24 patients undergoing RA plus IVL procedure. Finally, according to the strategy used by the operator during the intervention, 37 cases were enrolled and divided into group A, rota-tripsy (18 patients), and group B, step-up-approach RA (19 patients). According to previous successful studies regarding the advantages of drug-eluting stents (DESs) over bare-metal stents following RA6, all the enrolled patients were implanted with DESs after plaque modification. We selected severe CAC7 patients with a calcification score of 4 (using OCT) or ≥ 2 (using IVUS). IVUS applied calcification score components8: length of calcium (> 270°) > 5 mm (1 point), 360° circumferential calcium (1 point), vessel diameter ≤ 3.5 mm (1 point), and calcified nodule (1 point). OCT applied the following calcification score components9: calcium arc > 180° (2 points), calcium length > 5 mm (1 point), and calcium thickness > 0.5 mm (1 point). Due to severe calcification, most of the uncrossable lesions could not initially be measured using intracoronary imaging devices; therefore, the calcification score was calculated after the first burr rotation.

The inclusion criteria were patients who had (1) received percutaneous coronary interventions under the intracoronary imaging facility (IVUS: Boston Scientific Corporation, Maple Grove, MN, USA; OCT: Abbott Vascular, Santa Clara, CA, USA) for plaque evaluation; (2) a CAC score of 2–4 (IVUS) or 4 (OCT); and (3) a napkin-ring or annular calcification which still existed, after the first burr catheter was removed, and the 1:1 non-compliant balloon (NCB) at 16 atm had expanded. This means that the risk of poor stent expansion was highly expected and further plaque modification was required. The exclusion criteria were patients (1) who had received plaque modification without stent placement and application of drug-coated balloon/plain balloon dilation only and (2) with incomplete baseline data/intravascular images. Endpoints and definitions

The primary efficacy endpoint was procedural success, defined as a stent expansion rate of > 70% by OCT/IVUS and the absence of in-hospital major adverse cardiac events (MACEs). The primary safety endpoint was the absence of serious angiographic complications. Angiographic complications included slow/no flow (thrombolysis in myocardial infarction flow < 3), perforation, artery dissection (Type C–F), abrupt closure, and acute thrombosis. The secondary safety endpoint was the absence of in-hospital and 30-day MACEs. The MACEs included cardiac death, myocardial infarction, and target vessel revascularization. The “interval time” was defined as the time from when the second burr guide catheter or the shockwave catheter was placed in the access site to the time at which the catheter was removed from the access site. The dose-area product (DAP) and fluoroscopy time were the overall dose and time for the interventional process.

Interventions

For severe calcifications that could not pass through the catheter of the intracavity devices, a small-sized burr (1.25–1.5 mm) was used to debulk the plaque to allow it to pass through. The optimal RA technique abides by the following standards: burr-to-artery ratio (< 0.6), ablation speed (135,000–160,000 rpm), short ablation runs (≤ 20 s), gradual burr advancement with a pecking motion, and avoidance of deceleration of more than 5,000 rpm10,11. If poor expansion still existed after the application of a 1:1 NCB at 16 atm, according to the operator’s comprehensive assessment of the condition at the time, a different procedure was selected during interventional treatment.

In group A, the angioplasty IVL catheter (Shockwave Medical, Inc, Santa Clara, CA, USA) was connected to the treatment instrument via a dedicated connection cable that transmits up to 30–80 pulses per catheter. The diameter and length of the shockwave balloon ratio were 1:1 and 12 mm, respectively. After a 4 atm expansion pulse was issued and a 6 atm expansion was maintained for 10 s, the immediate effect was observed until the expansion had improved with no obvious residuals when compared with the previous reading. In group B, if there was poor expansion of NCB at 16 atm, ulteriorly, a stepped burr RA strategy was applied to modify the plaque (all rotary burrs used were from Boston Scientific Incorporation).

Whichever process was performed, the endpoint of modification in the two groups was consistent: the application of 1:1 NCB at 16 atm showed favorable expansion and the IVUS/OCT images showed calcification fracture or lumen enlargement. With the occurrence of these endpoints, a satisfied stent expansion is expected. Post-dilation was performed at high pressure (16–18 atm) after DESs were placed. Following the intervention, postoperative anticoagulation and antiplatelet routine therapy regimens were implemented in both groups.

All interventional procedures were guided by intracoronary imaging. OCT and IVUS images of the two procedures are displayed in Fig. 2. Stent expansion is defined as the mean stent area (stent volume/analyzed stent length) divided by the average of proximal and distal reference lumen areas x 10012. Since most lesions were severely calcified, the intravascular imaging device could not pass through. To unify standards, we defined the minimal lumen area (MLA) 1 modified by the first burr as the initial MLA, whereas the lumen area modified by the second burr or shockwave balloon was defined as MLA 2. The corresponding lumen expansion area was defined as lumen gain 1. The D-value between the minimal stent area and MLA 1 was defined as lumen gain 2.

Statistical analysis

The measurement data of continuous non-normally distributed variables were represented as median (IQR) and compared by Mann–Whitney U test. Between-group difference was calculated with the Hodges-Lehmann estimate of confidence intervals for pseudo-medians13. The chi-square test /Fisher’s exact test was used to compare categorical variables as appropriate. The categorical variables were expressed as counts and percentages. All statistical tests were two-sided, and a P-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Data analyses were performed using SPSS (version 26.0; IBM SPSS Statistics, Armonk, NY, USA), and sample size calculation were performed using PASS (version 2020; NCSS, LLC, USA).

Overall, group A had a shorter interval time (-7, 95%CI [-10,-5])/fluoroscopy time (-20, 95%CI [-28,-8]) and reduced DAP (-104.1,95%CI [-196.3,-26]) than group B (P < 0.01). Discussion

Severe CAC increases the difficulty of intervention and directly affects stent expansion, leading to poor clinical endpoints. To our knowledge, this study provided the largest amount of data on the comparison of intravascular imaging-guided rota-tripsy and step-up-approach RA as a plaque modification strategy of severe calcifications in the real world.

The pre-treatment of severely calcified lesions by RA was often accompanied by complications, such as slow or no flow, perforation, dissection, burr entrapment, and disconnection. Notably, the complications can be reduced via the guidance of imaging devices or optimizing maneuver details, like rotary burr size, rotational speed, and ablation runs14. Slow or no flow during the RA procedure results in the most serious, preventable, and operator-dependent complications, with an incidence rate ranging from 0 to 20%2,10; whereas the complication risk during IVL is relatively low.

Data from the Disrupt Coronary Artery Disease I–IV trials demonstrated that the incidence of overall angiographic complications ranged from 0 to 1.7%, and no slow or no flow complications occurred15,16. In our study, six patients in group B experienced transient slow or no flow, with an incidence of 31.6%, which was higher than the average value of previous studies. This may be due to the step-up approach, as RA-modified lesions are more serious and complex, although we used nicorandil, nitroglycerin, sodium nitroprusside and other drugs improved the problem during the intervention. Comparatively, no one in group A had slow or no flow during the intervention, confirming that rota-tripsy might be a safer choice for severe CAC. It has always been a challenge for operators to select an appropriate endpoint of modification without slow or no flow occurring, with the crack in the napkin-ring or the residual thickness of the calcification being the conventional endpoint of RA.

As we know, the presence of an acute angulation on lesion and sequential lesion are independent determinants of clinical and procedural failure of RA17. For severely calcified lesions in our study, the utility of IVUS- or OCT-guided procedures allowed for sufficient pre-treatment before stenting. This contributed to the procedural success and reduced the occurrence of MACEs. Previous studies on calcified plaque modification strategies have evaluated the procedural success of RA and IVL (92.5% and 98.3–100%, respectively), as well as the occurrence of 30-day MACEs (5% and 0–5%, respectively)15. However, relative data regarding burr size escalation maneuvers and rota-tripsy are still lacking. Our results showed that the high stent expansion (83%), satisfactory procedure success rate (100%), freedom from in-hospital MACEs, and 30-day MACEs reflected the effectiveness and safety of both modification strategies. Moreover, it has been reported that IVL allows for a potential reduction in the associated risks of slow flow or no reflow18.

In a study by Sardella et al.19, the main reason for performing coronary IVL after RA was RA failure, rather than an elective combined approach, whereas our rota-tripsy procedure was somewhat different from the “elective or bail-out strategy.” When it came to balloon-uncrossable lesions, we initially selected a smaller rotary burr and only performed ablation on the stenosis site to allow for a working channel to be created. Furthermore, this strategy not only reduced slow or no flow but also the overall operating time. A lower DAP and shorter fluoroscopy and interval time suggested that the rota-tripsy procedure is easier to master, popularize, and promote. The lower DAP value also reduced the potential radiation injury to the patients and operators. Our center has now formed an interventional therapy process for severely calcified lesions20. With the accumulation of experience and further studies, a sophisticated operation process will be created.

Limitations

This study also had limitations. Firstly, the study was a retrospective cohort design with limited time range. Within the design, the choice of a rotational or shockwave technique still depended on the operator’s judgment and processing ability. We attempted to include cases from multiple operators to reduce selection bias. Meanwhile, the “Calcium Score” defined by OCT/IVUS cannot easily represent about the initial calcification severity, and the severity also interfere with the final result. Secondly, the sample size that met the inclusion criteria was small; an expanded sample size is more objective for the assessment of complications and MACEs. Thirdly, due to constraints, all the patients undergoing rota-tripsy had a single shockwave balloon inserted. However, for long-range calcified lesions, whether two or more shockwave balloons would achieve better clinical outcomes is worth exploring in future.

researches.

Conclusion

In this study, we found that both rota-tripsy and step-up-approach RA showed satisfactory short-term results in the treatment of severe calcification coronary lesions in terms of efficiency and safety. However, from a technical point of view, rota-tripsy accomplished a more effective and safe treatment of challenging calcifications compared with step-up-approach RA. Rota-tripsy showed the ability to manipulate details, reduce operation time, and effectively improve clinician experience and skill. Future studies should focus on the long-term effects across multiple centers to further validate our findings.

Higher Solovay Models
Cesare Straffelini, Sebastiano Thei

Abstract
. We introduce an axiomatisation of when a model of the form L ​ ( V κ + 1 ) M L(V_{\kappa+1})^{M} can be considered a “ κ \kappa -Solovay model”; we show a characterisation of κ \kappa -Solovay models; and we prove elementary equivalences between κ \kappa -Solovay models.

1.
Introduction

Robert Solovay, in his renowned article
[
26
]
, was able to construct a model of set theory (
ZF
) in which every subset of the real line is very “regular”, for example it is Lebesgue measurable, has the Baire and perfect set properties, and many others. He built this model starting with a model
V
of
ZFC
with an inaccessible cardinal
\lambda
, then considering the forcing extension
V[G]
of
V
resulting from collapsing gently
\lambda
to
\omega_{1}
with the Lévy collapse
\Coll(\omega,{<}\lambda)
, and after that taking the inner model
L(\mathbb{R})
of this extension
V[G]
. This model
L(\mathbb{R})^{V[G]}
is what for years has been called informally “the Solovay model”, even though it is not unique, depending from
\lambda
.

Years later, Joan Bagaria and Hugh Woodin in
[
6
]
proved a simple abstract characterisation of when a model of the form
L(\mathbb{R})^{M}
is “a Solovay model”:

Definition 1.1
.

Let
V\subseteq M
be models of
ZFC
. We will say that the model
L(\mathbb{R})^{M}
is
a Solovay model
over
V
if
M
satisfies the following conditions:

•

for all
x\subseteq\omega
, the ordinal
\omega_{1}^{M}
is an inaccessible cardinal in
V[x]
;

•

for all
x\subseteq\omega
,
x
is
small-generic
over
V
, that is, there is a countable poset
\mathbb{P}
in
V
and a filter
g\subseteq\mathbb{P}
generic over
V
such that
V[x]\subseteq V[g]
.

Clearly, the original model by Solovay satisfies this definition.

Theorem 1.2

(Solovay)
.

If
\lambda
is inaccessible in
V
, and
G\subseteq\Coll(\omega,{<}\lambda)
is a filter generic over
V
, then the model
L(\mathbb{R})^{V[G]}
is a Solovay model over
V
.

Proof.

This theorem was proven in the original article
[
26
, Section 3.4]
.∎

Bagaria and Woodin showed in
[
6
]
that the converse is essentially true:

Theorem 1.3

(Bagaria-Woodin)
.

If
V\subseteq M
are models of
ZFC
with
L(\mathbb{R})^{M}
Solovay over
V
, there is a filter
C\subseteq\Coll(\omega,{<}\omega_{1}^{M})
generic over
V
with
L(\mathbb{R})^{M}=L(\mathbb{R})^{V[C]}
.

Proof.

The proof of this theorem can be found in
[
6
, Theorem 2.11]
, or written in a way that is more similar to our presentation here in
[
3
, Lemma 1.2]
.∎

In addition to this, Solovay models have many absoluteness properties, as proved by Joan Bagaria and Roger Bosch in their articles
[
2
]
and
[
3
]
. For example,

Theorem 1.4

(Bagaria-Bosch)
.

Suppose that
L(\mathbb{R})^{M}
and
L(\mathbb{R})^{N}
are Solovay models over the same model
V
, such that
\mathbb{R}^{M}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
\omega_{1}^{M}=\omega_{1}^{N}
. Then there exists an unique elementary embedding
j:L(\mathbb{R})^{M}\hookrightarrow L(\mathbb{R})^{N}
that fixes all the ordinals.

Proof.

The proof of this theorem can be found in
[
3
, Lemma 1.5]
.∎

We live in a time where many of the classical results about
L(\mathbb{R})
get generalised for
L(V_{\kappa+1})
for
\kappa
a cardinal (usually, with some additional requirements), so it has not been a surprise when the second author of this article, together with Vincenzo Dimonte and Alejandro Poveda, published the article
[
12
]
, in which they built a model of
\textsf{ZF}+\textsf{DC}_{\kappa}
in which every subset of
\kappa^{\omega}
has the
\kappa
-perfect set property, and every subset of
\prod_{n<\omega}\kappa_{n}
has the
{{\mathcal{U}}}
-Baire property. These are higher analogues to this new context of the statements “every set of reals has the perfect set property” and “every set of reals has the Baire property”, that hold true in the Solovay model.

Curiously, the first proof of the consistency of the
\kappa
-perfect set property relied on the structural properties of the model
L(V_{\kappa+1})
, examined through the lens of the axiom
I_{0}(\kappa)
, one of the strongest known large cardinal axioms. Specifically, work by Woodin in
[
28
]
, and by his students Cramer in
[
7
]
and Shi in
[
25
]
, has revealed that under
I_{0}(\kappa)
every set
A\subseteq\kappa^{\omega}
in
L(V_{\kappa+1})
has the
\kappa
-perfect set property. Along the same lines, Dimonte, Iannella and Lücke in
[
8
]
showed that, under
I_{0}(\kappa)
, all projective subsets of
\prod_{n<\omega}\kappa_{n}
have the
{\mathcal{U}}
-Baire Property (for a certain
{\mathcal{U}}
).

These results are pieces of evidence suggesting that the investigation of regularity properties of sets in
L(V_{\kappa+1})
under
I_{0}(\kappa)
can be regarded as a natural generalisation of the classical study of regularity properties of sets in
L(\mathbb{R})
under
\textsf{AD}^{L(\mathbb{R})}
. Indeed, Woodin realised that
L(V_{\kappa+1})
behaves under
I_{0}(\kappa)
very much like
L(\mathbb{R})
does under
\textsf{AD}^{L(\mathbb{R})}
(see, for example,
[
18
]
or
[
7
]
). For instance,
L(V_{\kappa+1})\models``\kappa^{+}\text{ is measurable''}
under
I_{0}(\kappa)
, while
L(\mathbb{R})\models``\omega_{1}\text{ is measurable''}
under
\textsf{AD}^{L(\mathbb{R})}
.

In light of this, axiom
I_{0}(\kappa)
provides arguably the appropriate axiomatic framework to develop the so-called generalised descriptive set theory in
\kappa
-Polish spaces at singular cardinals
\kappa
. A notable key point for this parallelism is that such a cardinal
\kappa
shares with
\omega
some crucial properties which are used to obtain the various results in classical descriptive set theory. For instance, like
\omega
,
\kappa
must be a strong limit cardinal of countable cofinality.

Taking cue from some descriptive set-theoretical aspects of
I_{0}(\kappa)
, the Dimonte and Motto Ros developed a generalised descriptive set theory in their book
[
11
]
on strong limit uncountable cardinals of countable cofinality under
I_{0}(\kappa)
. However, there is evidence suggesting that
I_{0}(\kappa)
may not be the optimal large cardinal assumption for ensuring regularity properties for subsets in
\kappa
-Polish spaces. Indeed, as pointed out in the first paragraph, Solovay in
[
26
]
used a large cardinal assumption which is much lower than
\textsf{AD}^{L(\mathbb{R})}
. Similarly, the authors in
[
12
]
obtained the same configuration in the generalised context, relying on large cardinal assumptions in the realm of supercompactness.

To keep on with the similarities, the model introduced in
[
12
]
is the
L(V_{\kappa+1})
of a forcing extension of
V
. Here, though, is where the similarities between this new work and Solovay’s historic
[
26
]
stop, since the forcing notion used by Dimonte, Poveda and the second author is very different than the Lévy collapse.

This paper aims to explore the interaction between generalised descriptive set theory and large cardinal axioms through the lens of higher Solovay models. These models provide a natural setting for studying regularity properties in
\kappa
-Polish spaces that hold true in
L(V_{\kappa+1})
under
I_{0}(\kappa)
.

This article is structured as follows. In Section §2, we introduce all preliminaries about forcing that will be needed throughout the paper. Many of these are already stated in
[
12
]
, but we report them for sake of completeness. Later, in Section §3, we recall the concept of
\Sigma
-Příkrý forcings and
(\kappa,\lambda)
-nice weak
\Sigma
-systems, the technical instruments used in
[
12
]
for building their higher Solovay model. We strengthen the notion of these weak
\Sigma
-systems by adding an hypothesis of continuity, that is necessary later.

In Section §4, we recall the two main examples of
(\kappa,\lambda)
-nice weak
\Sigma
-systems from
[
12
]
and check that they satisfy the additional hypothesis of continuity. In Section §5, we finally generalise Definition
1.1
, explaining when a model of the form
L(V_{\kappa+1})^{M}
can be called a
\kappa
-Solovay model
over
V
. Concretely, we get immediately the analogous of Theorem
1.2
, namely, the models built by Dimonte, Poveda and Thei in
[
12
]
are indeed satisfying our definition of
\kappa
-Solovay model.

Section §6 will be dedicated to proving the higher analogue of Bagaria-Woodin’s Theorem
1.3
. In Section §7, we see how this theorem has many consequences in the generalised descriptive set theory, showing that every
\kappa
-Solovay model satisfies the desired regularity properties for subsets of
\kappa
-Polish spaces.

To wrap up, in Section §8 we will prove a version of Bagaria-Bosch’s Theorem
1.4
adapted to
\kappa
-Solovay models. Some open questions are discussed in Section §9.

2.
Forcing Preliminaries

In this section, we collect some definitions and basic results about forcing that are necessary for understanding the rest of the article. Our main reference is Kenneth Kunen’s book
[
19
]
. Formally, a forcing poset is an ordered triple
\langle\mathbb{P},\leqslant_{\mathbb{P}},\mathbb{1}_{\mathbb{P}}\rangle
where
\leqslant_{\mathbb{P}}
is a partial order relation on
\mathbb{P}
with a maximum element, that is
\mathbb{1}_{\mathbb{P}}
. However, when there is no risk of confusion, we often adopt a more informal style, as in “
let
\mathbb{P}
be a forcing poset
”.

An important definition we use, which does not appear on Kunen’s book, is that of a
weak projection
, introduced by Foreman and Woodin in their article
[
15
]
:

Definition 2.1
.

If
\langle\mathbb{P},\leqslant_{\mathbb{P}},\mathbb{1}_{\mathbb{P}}\rangle
and
\langle\mathbb{Q},\leqslant_{\mathbb{Q}},\mathbb{1}_{\mathbb{Q}}\rangle
are forcing posets, a map
\pi:\mathbb{P}\to\mathbb{Q}
is called
weak projection
if the following conditions hold:

•

for all
p
and
p^{\prime}
in
\mathbb{P}
, if
p\leqslant_{\mathbb{P}}p^{\prime}
, then
\pi(p)\leqslant_{\mathbb{Q}}\pi(p^{\prime})
;

•

for all
p\in\mathbb{P}
, there exists
p^{\ast}\leqslant_{\mathbb{P}}p
such that for all
q\in\mathbb{Q}
with
q\leqslant_{\mathbb{Q}}\pi(p^{\ast})
there exists
p^{\prime}\leqslant_{\mathbb{P}}p
satisfying
\pi(p^{\prime})\leqslant_{\mathbb{Q}}q
.

A
projection
is a weak projection
\pi
that also satisfies
\pi(\mathbb{1}_{\mathbb{P}})=\mathbb{1}_{\mathbb{Q}}
and for which, in the second condition above, one can always take
p^{\ast}=p
.

Lemma 2.2
.

If
\pi:\mathbb{P}\to\mathbb{Q}
is a weak projection between forcing posets, and
G
is a
\mathbb{P}
-generic filter over
V
, then the upward closure of
\pi^{\prime\prime}G
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-generic filter over
V
.

Proof.

The proof of this lemma can be found in
[
15
, Proposition 2.8]
.
∎

As a convention, following Lemma
2.2
, we identify
\pi^{\prime\prime}G
with its upwards closure.

Example 2.3
.

Let us consider the case of the Lévy collapse. Namely, let
\Coll(\omega,{<}\lambda)
be the set of all partial maps from
\omega
to some
\alpha<\lambda
, ordered by reverse inclusion. In the forcing extension by
\Coll(\omega,{<}\lambda)
,
\lambda
is collapsed gently to
\omega_{1}
, in the sense that every ordinal below
\lambda
becomes countable. On the other hand, let
\Coll(\omega,{\leqslant}\alpha)
be the set of all partial functions from
\omega
to
\alpha
, again with the reverse inclusion ordering. In the extension by
\Coll(\omega,{\leqslant}\alpha)
, the ordinal
\alpha
becomes countable. Next, if
\alpha<\lambda
, we observe that

\Coll(\omega,{\leqslant}\alpha)\ast\Coll(\check{\omega},{<}\check{\lambda})\simeq\Coll(\omega,{<}\lambda),

hence there is a (strong) projection
\ell_{\lambda,\alpha}:\Coll(\omega,{<}\lambda)\to\Coll(\omega,{\leqslant}\alpha)
.

A concept in some ways dual to projections is the one of
complete embeddings
:

Definition 2.4
.

If
\langle\mathbb{P},\leqslant_{\mathbb{P}},\mathbb{1}_{\mathbb{P}}\rangle
and
\langle\mathbb{Q},\leqslant_{\mathbb{Q}},\mathbb{1}_{\mathbb{Q}}\rangle
are forcing posets, a map
\imath:\mathbb{Q}\to\mathbb{P}
is a
complete embedding
if the following conditions hold:

•

for all
q
and
q^{\prime}
in
\mathbb{Q}
, if
q\leqslant_{\mathbb{Q}}q^{\prime}
, then
\imath(q)\leqslant_{\mathbb{P}}\imath(q^{\prime})
;

•

for all
q
and
q^{\prime}
in
\mathbb{Q}
, if
q
and
q^{\prime}
are incompatible in
\mathbb{Q}
, then
\imath(q)
and
\imath(q^{\prime})
are incompatible in
\mathbb{P}
;

•

for every
p\in\mathbb{P}
, there exists
q\in\mathbb{Q}
such that
p
and
\imath(q)
are compatible in
\mathbb{P}
.

Lemma 2.5
.

If
\imath:\mathbb{Q}\to\mathbb{P}
is a complete embedding between forcing posets, and
G
is a
\mathbb{P}
-generic filter over
V
, then
\imath^{-1}[G]
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-generic filter over
V
.

Proof.

The proof of this classical lemma can be found in
[
19
, Lemma IV.4.2]
.
∎

Given a forcing poset
\mathbb{P}
, we can consider its Boolean completion, the
regular open algebra
, where we remove the least element
\mathbb{0}_{\mathbb{P}}
. The bottomless regular open algebra is denoted by
\mathrm{RO}(\mathbb{P})
. For sake of simplicity, we identify the forcing poset
\mathbb{P}
with its isomorphic copy inside
\mathrm{RO}(\mathbb{P})
. The forcing posets
\mathbb{P}
and
\mathrm{RO}(\mathbb{P})
are always forcing equivalent, as
\mathbb{P}
is dense in
\mathrm{RO}(\mathbb{P})
and every
\mathbb{P}
-name is also a
\mathrm{RO}(\mathbb{P})
-name.

If
\mathbb{Q}
is another forcing poset and
\varrho:\mathrm{RO}(\mathbb{P})\to\mathrm{RO}(\mathbb{Q})
is a projection, then also the restriction of
\varrho
to
\mathbb{P}
is a projection. Similarly, by density, if
\imath:\mathbb{P}\to\mathrm{RO}(\mathbb{Q})
is a complete embedding, it can be extended in an unique way to an embedding
\imath:\mathrm{RO}(\mathbb{P})\to\mathrm{RO}(\mathbb{Q})
, by definining

\imath(b):=\bigvee\{\imath(p):p\in\mathbb{P},\ p\leqslant b\}.

Lemma 2.6
.

If
G
is
\mathrm{RO}(\mathbb{P})
-generic over
V
, then
G\cap\mathbb{P}
is
\mathbb{P}
-generic over
V
. On the other hand,
if
G
is
\mathbb{P}
-generic over
V
, its upwards closure in
\mathrm{RO}(\mathbb{P})
is
\mathrm{RO}(\mathbb{P})
-generic over
V
.

Proof.

The proof of this lemma can be found in
[
19
, Lemma IV.4.7]
.
∎

Forcing with the Boolean algebra
\mathrm{RO}(\mathbb{P})
instead of
\mathbb{P}
has the advantage that for every sentence
\varphi
in the forcing language (namely, every set-theoretic formula having only
\mathbb{P}
-names as parameters) that is forced by some
p\in\mathbb{P}
, we can always consider the
weakest
condition in
\mathrm{RO}(\mathbb{P})
that forces it, called its
Boolean value
. Namely, we can define it as

\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket:=\bigvee\{p\in\mathbb{P}:p\Vdash_{\mathbb{P}}\varphi\}.

For more facts about Boolean-valued forcing, we refer to John Bell’s book
[
5
]
.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
\mathbb{P}
and
\mathbb{Q}
be forcings and let
\dot{g}
be a
\mathbb{P}
-name such that

\mathbb{1}_{\mathbb{P}}\Vdash_{\mathbb{P}}``\dot{g}\ \text{is}\ \check{\mathbb{Q}}\text{-generic}".

Then the function
\imath:\mathbb{Q}\to\mathrm{RO}(\mathbb{P})
defined by
\imath(q):=\llbracket\check{q}\in\dot{g}\rrbracket
for all
q\in\mathbb{Q}
is called the
complete embedding induced by
\dot{g}
. Thanks to the argument mentioned just above Lemma
2.6
, this can be extended to a complete embedding, that we still call
\imath
, from
\mathrm{RO}(\mathbb{Q})
to
\mathrm{RO}(\mathbb{P})
.

As we stated earlier, projections and complete embeddings are closely entwined:

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
\mathbb{P}
and
\mathbb{Q}
be forcing posets. If
\imath:\mathbb{Q}\to\mathbb{P}
is a complete embedding, then there exists a projection
\varrho:\mathbb{P}\to\mathrm{RO}(\mathbb{Q})
with
\varrho\circ\imath=\id_{\mathbb{Q}}
, defined by

\varrho(p):=\bigvee\{q\in\mathbb{Q}:\imath(q)\leqslant_{\mathbb{P}}p\}.

If
\varrho:\mathbb{P}\to\mathbb{Q}
is a projection, then there is a complete embedding
\imath:\mathbb{Q}\to\mathrm{RO}(\mathbb{P})
:

\imath(q):=\bigvee\{p\in\mathbb{P}:\varrho(p)\leqslant_{\mathbb{Q}}q\}.

Notice that, in this case, the identity
\imath\circ\varrho=\id_{\mathbb{P}}
does not generally hold.

Proof.

A good proof of this well-known fact is found in
[
16
, Theorem 2.25]
.
∎

Hence, the existence of a projection between two Boolean algebras is equivalent to the existence of a complete embedding between them but in the other direction.

Definition 2.9
.

If
\mathbb{P}
is a forcing poset and
p\in\mathbb{P}
, we write

\mathbb{P}_{\downarrow p}:=\{q\in\mathbb{P}:q\leqslant_{\mathbb{P}}p\}.

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
\mathbb{P}
and
\mathbb{Q}
be forcing posets. Let
p\in\mathbb{P}
and
\dot{g}
be a
\mathbb{P}
-name with

p\Vdash_{\mathbb{P}}``\dot{g}\ \text{is}\ \check{\mathbb{Q}}\text{-generic}".

Consider the map
\varrho:\mathbb{P}_{\downarrow p}\to\mathrm{RO}(\mathbb{Q})
defined, for
p^{\prime}\leqslant_{\mathbb{P}}p
, as

\varrho(p^{\prime}):=\bigwedge\{q\in\mathbb{Q}:p^{\prime}\Vdash_{\mathbb{P}}(\check{q}\in\dot{g})\}.

Then
\varrho
is a projection from
\mathbb{P}_{\downarrow p}
to
\mathrm{RO}(\mathbb{Q})_{\downarrow\varrho(p)}
, as well as its extension to
\mathrm{RO}(\mathbb{P})_{\downarrow p}
that can be constructed by the usual density argument. Moreover, for each
\mathbb{P}
-generic filter
G
over
V
with
p\in G
, the upwards closure in
\mathrm{RO}(\mathbb{Q})
of
\varrho^{\prime\prime}G
is given by

\{b\in\mathrm{RO}(\mathbb{Q}):\exists q\in i_{G}(\dot{g}):(q\leqslant b)\}.

Proof.

The proof of this lemma can be found in
[
14
, Lemma 2.1]
.
∎

In the situation described in Lemma
2.10
, we refer to the map

\varrho:\mathrm{RO}(\mathbb{P})_{\downarrow p}\to\mathrm{RO}(\mathbb{Q})_{\downarrow\varrho(p)}

as
the projection induced by
\dot{g}
and
p
. We stated Lemma
2.10
primarily to obtain a specific corollary, which is used repeatedly throughout the paper, and is as follows. Let
\pi:\mathbb{P}\to\mathbb{Q}
be a weak projection, and let
\Gamma
be the standard
\mathbb{P}
-name for a generic filter, namely,

\Gamma:=\{\langle\check{p},p\rangle:p\in\mathbb{P}\}.

Then, clearly, any
p\in\mathbb{P}
forces that
\Gamma
is
\mathbb{P}
-generic, and, thanks to Lemma
2.2
, we also know that any
p\in\mathbb{P}
forces that
\pi^{\prime\prime}\Gamma
is
\mathbb{Q}
-generic. Hence, we can consider the projection induced by
\pi^{\prime\prime}\Gamma
and
p
, which is of the form

\mathrm{IP}(\pi,p):\mathrm{RO}(\mathbb{P})_{\downarrow p}\to\mathrm{RO}(\mathbb{Q})_{\downarrow\pi(p)}.

Note that the
induced projection

\mathrm{IP}(\pi,p)
only depends on the projection
\pi:\mathbb{P}\to\mathbb{Q}
and on the choice of a specific element
p\in\mathbb{P}
, and nothing else, since
\Gamma
is canonical.

Definition 2.11
.

Let
\mathsf{wPos}^{\bullet}
denote the concrete, locally small category where the objects are ordered pairs
\langle\mathbb{P},p\rangle
, with
\mathbb{P}
a poset and
p\in\mathbb{P}
, while the morphisms from
\langle\mathbb{P},p\rangle
to
\langle\mathbb{Q},q\rangle
are weak projections
\pi:\mathbb{P}\to\mathbb{Q}
such that
\pi(p)=q
.

Definition 2.12
.

Let
\mathsf{bcBa}
denote the concrete, locally small category where the objects are bottomless complete Boolean algebras and the morphisms are projections between partial orders.

Lemma 2.13
.

Consider the mapping
\Psi:\mathsf{wPos}^{\bullet}\to\mathsf{bcBa}
defined as follows. On objects, it acts as

\Psi(\langle\mathbb{P},p\rangle):=\mathrm{RO}(\mathbb{P})_{\downarrow p},

while on morphisms, it sends any weak projection
\pi:\langle\mathbb{P},p\rangle\to\langle\mathbb{Q},\pi(p)\rangle
to

\Psi(\pi):=\mathrm{IP}(\pi,p):\mathrm{RO}(\mathbb{P})_{\downarrow p}\to\mathrm{RO}(\mathbb{Q})_{\downarrow\pi(p)}=\Psi(\langle\mathbb{P},p\rangle)\to\Psi(\langle\mathbb{Q},\pi(p)\rangle).

Thus,
\Psi
is a functor from the category
\mathsf{wPos}^{\bullet}
to the category
\mathsf{bcBa}
.

Proof.

This lemma is proved, with different notation, in
[
12
, Lemma 2.10]
.
∎

To wrap up the section, we recall the definition of quotient forcing.

Definition 2.14
.

Given a projection
\varrho:\mathbb{P}\to\mathbb{Q}
and a
\mathbb{Q}
-generic filter
H
over
V
, we define the
quotient forcing

\mathbb{P}/H
as the subposet of
\mathbb{P}
of all
p\in\mathbb{P}
with

.

Lemma 2.15
.

Every
\mathbb{P}/H
-generic filter over

is

-generic over

.

Proof.

The proof of this lemma is in

. There, the quotient is defined from a complete embedding, but it is equivalent to our definition thanks to Lemma

.∎

3.
Sigma-Příkrý Forcing

To state our Definition
5.1
, the analogue of Definition
1.1
in the higher context of
L(V_{\kappa+1})
, we first need to discuss
\Sigma
-Příkrý forcing. This concept was introduced by Alejandro Poveda, Assaf Rinot and Dima Sinapova in their article
[
22
]
, and further developed in
[
23
]
and
[
24
]
, where they axiomatised fundamental properties satisfied by the original “vanilla Příkrý forcing” defined by Karel Příkrý in
[
21
]
.

Before defining what a
\Sigma
-Příkrý forcing is, we need to fix a so-called
\Sigma
-sequence
: a non-decreasing sequence
\langle\kappa_{n}:n<\omega\rangle
of regular uncountable cardinals converging to some strong limit
\kappa
. This will serve as a blanket assumption throughout this article.

Definition 3.1
.

Given a
\Sigma
-sequence
\langle\kappa_{n}:n<\omega\rangle
as before, a forcing poset
\langle\mathbb{P},\leqslant,\mathbb{1}\rangle
is
\Sigma
-Příkrý
if there exist

•

a surjective map
\ell:\mathbb{P}\to\omega
, called
length
;

•

a cardinal
\mu
;

•

a map
c:\mathbb{P}\to\mu

satisfying the following conditions:

•

for all
q\leqslant p
, it holds
\ell(q)\geqslant\ell(p)
;

•

for all
p\in\mathbb{P}
, there is
q\leqslant p
with
\ell(q)=\ell(p)+1
;

•

the subposet
\langle\ell^{-1}(\{n\})\cup\{\mathbb{1}\},\leqslant,\mathbb{1}\rangle
is
\kappa_{n}
-directed-closed, meaning that if
L\subseteq\mathbb{P}
is a set of pairwise compatible elements with
|L|<\kappa_{n}
and such that

In order to introduce systems and weak systems of forcing posets, we adopt a category-theoretic approach, which provides a cleaner and more insightful perspective on the fundamental properties of the definition. This is similar to our approach in the statement of Lemma
2.13
, in contrast to Lemma 2.10 of
[
12
]
.

Definition 3.4
.

A
posetal category
is a category
\mathsf{D}
where there is at most one morphism from any one object to another. Such a category can be viewed as a partial order, where
d\leqslant e
if and only if there exists a morphism
d\to e
. We focus on posetal categories that are
downwards directed
, meaning that for any pair of objects
d
and
e
in the category there are an object
f
and morphisms
f\to d
and
f\to e
. Equivalently, viewing the category as a partial order, we require that any two elements have a lower bound.

A
directed diagram
in a category
\mathsf{C}
is a functor from a downwards directed posetal category
\mathsf{D}
to
\mathsf{C}
. In this article, we do not consider upwards directed categories, as these are simply the opposites of downwards directed categories.

An example of a directed diagram in the category
\mathsf{wPos}^{\bullet}
is the following:
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\langle\mathbb{P}_{0},p_{0}\rangle}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\langle\mathbb{P}_{1},p_{1}\rangle}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\langle\mathbb{P}_{2},p_{2}\rangle}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{0,1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{0,2}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{1,2}}

where the maps
\pi_{0,1}
,
\pi_{0,2}
and
\pi_{1,2}
are weak projections such that
p_{1}=\pi_{0,1}(p_{0})
and
p_{2}=\pi_{0,2}(p_{0})
. One important fact about directed diagrams is that they are preserved by functors. So for example we can apply the functor
\Psi
from Lemma
2.13
and find another directed diagram
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{RO}(\mathbb{P}_{0})_{\downarrow p_{0}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{RO}(\mathbb{P}_{1})_{\downarrow p_{1}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{RO}(\mathbb{P}_{2})_{\downarrow p_{2}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varrho_{0,1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varrho_{0,2}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varrho_{1,2}}

recalling that
\mathrm{RO}(\mathbb{P}_{i})_{\downarrow p_{i}}=\Psi(\langle\mathbb{P}_{i},p_{i}\rangle)
and
\varrho_{i,j}:=\mathrm{IP}(\pi_{i,j},p_{i})=\Psi(\pi_{i,j})
.

Definition 3.5
.

A weak directed system of forcing posets or, shortly, a weak system, is a cone over a directed diagram in the category
\mathsf{wPos}^{\bullet}
.

Definition
3.5
is equivalent to Definition 3.6 in
[
12
]
, but is expressed using the language of category theory. For sake of completeness and clarity, now we unravel Definition
3.5
to show what is really a weak system. First, we fix a downwards directed posetal category
\mathsf{D}
. For each object
d
of
\mathsf{D}
, we consider a partial order
\mathbb{P}_{d}
and an element
p_{d}\in\mathbb{P}_{d}
. For each arrow
d\to e
in
\mathsf{D}
, we consider a weak projection
\pi_{d,e}:\mathbb{P}_{d}\to\mathbb{P}_{e}
such that
\pi_{d,e}(p_{d})=p_{e}
. This can be considered as a directed diagram in the category
\mathsf{wPos}^{\bullet}
if

•

\pi_{d,d}=\mathrm{id}_{\mathbb{P}_{d}}
for each object
d
in
\mathsf{D}
;

•

\pi_{e,f}\circ\pi_{d,e}=\pi_{d,f}
whenever
d\to e
and
e\to f
in
\mathsf{D}
.

Now, a
cone
over this directed diagram consists of a partial order
\mathbb{P}_{\infty}
, an element
p_{\infty}\in\mathbb{P}_{\infty}
, and weak projections
\pi_{\infty,d}
for each object
d
of
\mathsf{D}
such that:

•

\pi_{\infty,d}(p_{\infty})=p_{d}
for each object
d
of
\mathsf{D}
;

•

\pi_{d,e}\circ\pi_{\infty,d}=\pi_{\infty,e}
for all arrows
d\to e
in
\mathsf{D}
.

When the context is clear, we often abuse notation by saying “let
\langle\mathbb{P}_{\infty},p_{\infty}\rangle
be a weak system” without explicitly writing the morphisms
\pi_{\infty,d}
, or even just “let
\mathbb{P}_{\infty}
be a weak system”, implicitly assuming that
p_{\infty}=\mathbb{1}_{\mathbb{P}_{\infty}}
.

If every
\mathbb{P}_{d}
for
d
an object of
\mathsf{D}
, as well as
\mathbb{P}_{\infty}
, is a
\Sigma
-Příkrý forcing poset, we say that
\mathbb{P}_{\infty}
is a
weak
\Sigma
-system
.

Definition 3.6
.

A directed system of Boolean algebras or, shortly, a system, is a cone over a directed diagram in the category
\mathsf{bcBa}
.

Assume
\langle\mathbb{P}_{\infty},p_{\infty}\rangle
is a weak system over some directed diagram. Since such directed diagram is a functor from a downwards directed posetal category
\mathsf{D}
to
\mathsf{wPos}^{\bullet}
, we can compose this functor with the functor
\Psi
from Lemma
2.13
. This way, we get a directed diagram in the category
\mathsf{bcBa}
, and
\Psi(\langle\mathbb{P}_{\infty},p_{\infty}\rangle)=\mathrm{RO}(\mathbb{P}_{\infty})_{\downarrow p_{\infty}}
is a cone over this directed diagram, with the projections given by
\Psi(\pi)=\mathrm{IP}(\pi_{\infty,d},p_{\infty})
. We can always do this, whenever we have a weak system. We call this the
Boolean completion
of the weak system, and write
\mathbb{B}_{d}:=\mathrm{RO}(\mathbb{P}_{d})_{\downarrow p_{d}}
for all objects
d
of
\mathsf{D}
, in order to have less cumbersome notation.

Another important definition from
[
12
]
is the following. For the rest of the section, let
\kappa
be a strong limit cardinal and let
\lambda>\kappa
be inaccessible.

Definition 3.7
.

A weak system
\mathbb{P}_{\infty}
is
(\kappa,\lambda)
-nice if it is:

•

\kappa
-capturing
, meaning that if
G\subseteq\mathbb{P}_{\infty}
is a generic filter over
V
, then for every subset
x\subseteq\kappa
in
V[G]
and every object
d
in
\mathsf{D}
there is an arrow
e\to d
such that

;

•

\lambda
-bounded
, meaning that
\mathbb{P}_{d}\in H(\lambda)
for all objects
d
of
\mathsf{D}
;

•

amenable to interpolation
, meaning that if
G\subseteq\mathbb{P}_{\infty}
is a generic filter over

, then
\lambda=(\kappa^{+})^{V[G]}
and
\cof(\kappa)^{V[G]}=\omega
.

Unfortunately, this definition is not strong enough for our purposes. Indeed, we need to add a slightly stronger condition. Instead of requiring
\mathbb{P}_{\infty}
to be
\kappa
-capturing, we need it to be “
({<}\lambda)
-capturing”, namely that the first condition should hold not only for subsets

but also for subsets

to any
\alpha<\lambda
. Luckily, there is a pretty straightforward category-theoretic condition that ensures this.

Definition 3.8
.

A weak system is
continuous
if it is a limit cone.

One characterisation for continuous weak systems is the following. If
\mathbb{P}_{\infty}
is a weak system, let
\mathbb{B}_{\infty}
be its Boolean completion, and let

\varrho_{\infty,d}:=\Psi(\pi_{\infty,d})

for all objects
d
in
\mathsf{D}
. For each such object, let
\imath_{d,\infty}:\mathbb{B}_{d}\to\mathbb{B}_{\infty}
be a complete embedding with
\varrho_{\infty,d}\circ\imath_{d,\infty}=\id_{\mathbb{B}_{d}}
, whose existence is guaranteed by Lemma
2.8
. Then
\mathbb{P}_{\infty}
is continuous if and only if

\textstyle\bigcup\left\{\imath^{\prime\prime}_{d,\infty}\mathbb{B}_{d}:d\ \text{object of}\ \mathsf{D}\right\}=\mathbb{B}_{\infty}.

Theorem 3.9
.

Let
\mathbb{P}_{\infty}
be a continuous
(\kappa,\lambda)
-nice weak system. Then it is
({<}\lambda)
-capturing, meaning that if
G\subseteq\mathbb{P}_{\infty}
is a filter generic over
V
and
\alpha<\lambda
, then for every subset
x\subseteq\alpha
in
V[G]
and every object
d
in
\mathsf{D}
there is an arrow
e\to d
such that
x\in V[\pi^{\prime\prime}_{\infty,e}G]
.

Proof.

Let
\mathbb{B}_{\infty}:=\mathrm{RO}(\mathbb{P}_{\infty})
be the Boolean completion of
\mathbb{P}_{\infty}
. Fix
G
,
\alpha
,
x
and
d
as in the statement of the theorem. Since
\mathbb{P}_{\infty}
is amenable to interpolations, in
V[G]
it holds
\lambda=\kappa^{+}
, hence the ordinal
\alpha
has cardinality
\kappa
. So, there is a bijection from
\alpha
to
\kappa
in
V[G]
. This means there is some
p\in G
such that

p\Vdash_{\mathbb{B}_{\infty}}``\exists\ \dot{b}:\check{\alpha}\to\check{\kappa}\ \text{that is a bijection}".

Since
\mathbb{P}_{\infty}
is continuous, there is by directedness of
\mathsf{D}
some arrow
d_{1}\to d
such that
p\in\imath^{\prime\prime}_{d_{1},\infty}\mathbb{B}_{d_{1}}
. Let
q\in\mathbb{B}_{d_{1}}
such that
\imath_{d_{1},\infty}(q)=p
. Since
\imath_{d_{1},\infty}
is a complete embedding,

q\Vdash_{\mathbb{B}_{d_{1}}}``\exists\ \dot{b}:\check{\alpha}\to\check{\kappa}\ \text{that is a bijection}".

Since
q\in\pi_{\infty,d_{1}}^{\prime\prime}G
, in
V[\pi_{\infty,d_{1}}^{\prime\prime}G]
there is a bijective map
b:\alpha\to\kappa
.

Now consider, in
V[G]
, the element
y:=b^{\prime\prime}x
. Since
x\subseteq\alpha
, we know that
y\subseteq\kappa
. The weak system
\mathbb{P}_{\infty}
is
\kappa
-capturing, hence there is

such that
y\in V[\pi_{\infty,e}^{\prime\prime}G]
. But now
y\in V[\pi_{\infty,e}^{\prime\prime}G]
and

, hence

4.
Examples of Continuous Nice Weak Sigma-Systems

In the previous section, we recalled the notion of
(\kappa,\lambda)
-nice weak
\Sigma
-systems, introduced in
[
12
]
in order to construct models of
ZF
satisfying certain regularity properties for subsets of
\kappa
. The aim of this section is to present the two main examples of
(\kappa,\lambda)
-nice weak
\Sigma
-systems given in
[
12
]
, and to show that they are also
continuous
, in the sense of Definition
3.8
.

Our first example is the
Merimovich supercompact extender-based Příkrý forcing
, introduced by Carmi Merimovich in
[
20
]
. We show it has the structure of a continuous
(\kappa,\lambda)
-nice weak
\Sigma
-system.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\lambda
be an inaccessible cardinal and
\kappa<\lambda
a
(<\lambda)
-supercompact cardinal. This means we assume the existence of an elementary embedding
j:V\to M
such that
\crit(j)=\kappa
,
j(\kappa)=\lambda
, and
M^{<\lambda}\subseteq M
. Let us now consider the downwards directed posetal category
\mathsf{D}
where the objects are the elements in the set

\mathcal{D}:=\{d\in[\lambda-\kappa]^{<\lambda}:\kappa\in d\}

and we have an arrow
d\to e
if and only if
d\supseteq e
. This posetal category is downwards directed because for all
d
and
e
in
\mathcal{D}
, it holds that
d\cup e\in\mathcal{D}
and also
d\cup e\to d
,
d\cup e\to e
.

For each
d\in\mathcal{D}
, we call
d
-
object
any map
\nu:\dom(\nu)\to\kappa
such that

•

\kappa\in\dom(\nu)\subseteq d
;

•

\left|\dom(\nu)\right|<\kappa
;

•

if
\alpha<\beta
are in
\dom(\nu)
, then
\nu(\alpha)<\nu(\beta)
.

The set of
d
-objects is called
\Ob(d)
. Given
\nu
and
\mu
in
\Ob(d)
we say that
\nu\ll\mu
if

•

\dom(\nu)\subseteq\dom(\mu)
;

•

|\nu|<\mu(\kappa)
;

•

for all
\alpha\in\dom(\nu)
, it holds
\nu(\alpha)<\mu(\kappa)
.

Now, for
d\in\mathcal{D}
, let us define the
maximal coordinate
of
d
as

\mc(d):=\{\langle j(\alpha),\alpha\rangle:\alpha\in d\}.

For
d\in\mathcal{D}
, the maximal coordinate induces a
\kappa
-complete ultrafilter over
\Ob(d)
:

E(d):=\{X\subseteq\Ob(d):\mc(d)\in j(X)\}.

We define the
Merimovich supercompact extender-based Příkrý forcing
(or, just, Merimovich forcing) as the poset
\langle\mathbb{P}_{\infty},\leqslant_{\infty}\rangle
where the conditions in
\mathbb{P}_{\infty}
are pairs
\langle f,A\rangle
such that

•

\dom(f)\in\mathcal{D}
;

•

f:\dom(f)\to\kappa^{<\omega}
, and
f(\alpha)
is order-increasing for all
\alpha\in\dom(f)
;

•

A\in E(\dom(f))
and for each
\nu\in A
,
\nu(\kappa)>\max(f(\alpha))
.

Given any two conditions
\langle f,A\rangle
and
\langle g,B\rangle
in
\mathbb{P}_{\infty}
, we say that
\langle f,A\rangle\leqslant^{\circ}\langle g,B\rangle
if

•

f\subseteq g
;

•

for every object
\nu\in B
, it holds
\nu\upharpoonright\dom(f)\in A
.

For
p=\langle f,A\rangle\in\mathbb{P}_{\infty}
and
\nu\in A
, we will call
p^{\curvearrowright}\nu
the condition
\langle f_{\nu},A_{\nu}\rangle
where

•

f_{\nu}
is the function with domain
\dom(f_{\nu})=\dom(f)
and such that

f_{\nu}(\alpha)=\begin{cases}f(\alpha)&\text{if}\ \alpha\not\in\dom(\nu);\\
f(\alpha)^{\frown}\langle\nu(\alpha)\rangle&\text{if}\ \alpha\in\dom(\nu);\end{cases}

•

A_{\nu}:=\{\mu\in A:\nu\ll\mu\}.

If
p=\langle f,A\rangle\in\mathbb{P}_{\infty}
and
\vec{\nu}\in A^{<\omega}
is a
\ll
-increasing sequence, we define
p^{\curvearrowright}\vec{\nu}
as

p^{\curvearrowright}\vec{\nu}:=(\cdots((p^{\curvearrowright}\vec{\nu}_{0})^{\curvearrowright}\vec{\nu}_{1})^{\curvearrowright}\cdots\vec{\nu}_{|\vec{\nu}|-1}).

Now the ordering
\leqslant_{\infty}
of the forcing poset
\mathbb{P}_{\infty}
is defined as follows:
\langle f,A\rangle\leqslant_{\infty}\langle g,B\rangle
if and only if there is a
\ll
-increasing sequence
\vec{\nu}\in B^{<\omega}
with
\langle f,A\rangle\leqslant^{\circ}\langle g,B\rangle^{\curvearrowright}\vec{\nu}
.

In addition, for each
d\in\mathcal{D}
, the poset
\mathbb{P}_{d}
will be the subposet of
\mathbb{P}_{\infty}
consisting of all elements
\langle f,A\rangle
with
\dom(f)\subseteq d
, and the map
\pi_{e,d}:\mathbb{P}_{e}\to\mathbb{P}_{d}
, for

, is defined as
\pi_{e,d}(\langle f,A\rangle):=\langle f\upharpoonright d,A\upharpoonright d\rangle
.

Lemma 4.2
.

If
\kappa<\lambda
are such that
\kappa
is
(<\lambda)
-supercompact and
\lambda
is inaccessible, the Merimovich forcing is a
(\kappa,\lambda)
-nice weak
\Sigma
-system.

Proof.

The proof of this fact is in
[
12
, Section 4.1]
.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

The Merimovich forcing is a continuous weak system.

Proof.

Consider the inclusion
\imath_{d,e}:\mathbb{P}_{d}\to\mathbb{P}_{e}
, that is a complete embedding and

\pi_{e,d}\circ\imath_{d,e}=\id_{\mathbb{P}_{d}}.

This can be extended to a complete embedding
\imath_{d,e}:\mathbb{B}_{d}\to\mathbb{B}_{e}
such that

\varrho_{e,d}\circ\imath_{d,e}=\id_{\mathbb{B}_{d}}.

Since every
\langle f,A\rangle\in\mathbb{P}_{\infty}
is such that
\dom(f)\in\mathcal{D}
, every element of
\mathbb{B}_{\infty}
belongs to some
\mathbb{B}_{d}
for
d\in\mathcal{D}
, and
\mathbb{B}_{d}=\imath_{d,\infty}^{\prime\prime}\mathbb{B}_{d}
, hence we get that

\textstyle\mathbb{B}_{\infty}=\bigcup_{d\in\mathcal{D}}\mathbb{B}_{d}=\bigcup_{d\in\mathcal{D}}\imath_{d,\infty}^{\prime\prime}\mathbb{B}_{d},

and this implies that the weak system is continuous, thanks to the remark after Definition
3.8
.
∎

In
[
12
]
, the authors introduce a diagonal version of the above forcing, designed by Poveda and the second author of this paper during a visit of the latter to Harvard in the spring of 2024.

Definition 4.4
.

Let
\lambda
be an inaccessible cardinal and
\kappa=\sup_{n<\omega}\kappa_{n}
, where each of the
\kappa_{n}
is
({<}\lambda)
-supercompact, so for each

with

.

We now define, for

\mathcal{D}_{n}:=\{d\in[\{\kappa_{n}\}\cup(\lambda-\kappa)]^{<\lambda}:\kappa_{n}\in d\}.

Given
d\in\mathcal{D}_{n}
, we call
d

\nu:\dom(\nu)\to\kappa_{n}
such that:

•

\kappa_{n}\in\dom(\nu)\subseteq d
;

•

\left|\dom(\nu)\right|<\kappa_{n}
;

•

\nu(\kappa_{n})
is an inaccessible cardinal above
\kappa_{n-1}
;

•

if
\alpha<\beta
are in
\dom(\nu)
, then
\nu(\alpha)<\nu(\beta)
.

The set of
d
-objects is denoted by
\Ob_{n}(d)
, and notice that the

can be calculated from

since
\kappa_{n}=\min(d)
. For
d\in\mathcal{D}_{n}
, define the

of
d
as

\mc_{n}(d):=\{\langle j_{n}(\alpha),\alpha\rangle:\alpha\in d\}.

For
d\in\mathcal{D}_{n}
, this induces a

-complete ultrafilter over

E_{n}(d):=\{X\subseteq\Ob_{n}(d):\mc_{n}(d)\in j_{n}(X)\}.

Clearly,

is a

-complete (yet, not necessarily normal) measure on

. Since
\kappa_{n}\in\dom(\nu)
for any
\nu\in\mathrm{Ob}_{n}(d)
, it is nevertheless the case that

projects onto a normal measure; specifically, it projects to

\mathcal{U}_{n}:=\{X\subseteq\kappa_{n}\mid\kappa_{n}\in j_{n}(X)\}

via the map sending

\nu(\kappa_{n})
.

Next, let
n\leqslant m
and let

and

, respectively. We write
d_{n}\subseteq^{\ast}d_{m}
whenever

. Now, assume that

. Given
\nu\in\mathrm{Ob}_{n}(d_{n})
and
\mu\in\mathrm{Ob}_{m}(d_{m})
, we write

whenever

•

\dom(\nu)\subseteq^{\ast}\dom(\mu)
;

•

\left|\nu\right|<\mu(\kappa_{m})
;

•

\nu(\alpha)<\mu(\kappa_{m})
for all
\alpha\in\dom(\nu)
.

This definition yields a transitive order between objects. We are now ready to define the
diagonal version of the Merimovich supercompact extender-based Příkrý forcing
(or, just, diagonal Merimovich forcing) as follows. A condition in
\mathbb{P}_{\infty}
will be a sequence of length

p=\langle f^{p}_{0},\dots,f^{p}_{\ell(p)-1},\langle f^{p}_{\ell(p)},A^{p}_{\ell(p)}\rangle,\langle f^{p}_{\ell(p)+1},A^{p}_{\ell(p)+1}\rangle\dots\rangle

satisfying the following conditions:

•

for each

,

is a partial function from

\kappa_{n}
with

and

is an inaccessible cardinal;

•

for each
n\geqslant\ell(p)
,
A_{n}^{p}\in E_{n}(\dom(f^{p}_{n}))
and the sequence

\langle\dom(f^{p}_{n}):n\geqslant\ell(p)\rangle

is
\subseteq^{\ast}
-increasing.

Given two conditions
p
and

in
\mathbb{P}_{\infty}
, we say that
p\leqslant^{\circ}q
if and only if

•

\ell(p)=\ell(q)
;

•

f^{q}_{n}\subseteq f^{p}_{n}
for all
n<\omega
;

•

for all
n\geqslant\ell(p)
, and all
\nu\in A^{p}_{n}
, it holds that

(\nu\upharpoonright\mathrm{dom}(f^{q}_{n}))\in A^{q}_{n}.

Now, let

and
\nu\in A^{p}_{\ell(p)}
. We define
p{}^{\curvearrowright}\nu
as the sequence

\langle f^{p}_{0},\dots,f^{p}_{\ell(p)-1},f^{p}_{\ell(p)}\oplus\nu,\langle f^{p}_{\ell(p)+1},(A^{p}_{\ell(p)+1})_{\langle\nu\rangle}\rangle,\langle f^{p}_{\ell(p)+2},(A^{p}_{\ell(p)+2})_{\langle\nu\rangle}\rangle\dots\rangle

where

and

for

are defined as follows:

•

f^{p}_{\ell(p)}\oplus\nu
is the function with domain
\dom(f^{p}_{\ell(p)})
and values

(f^{p}_{\ell(p)}\oplus\nu)(\alpha):=\begin{cases}\nu(\alpha)&\text{if}\ \alpha\in\dom(\nu);\\
f^{p}_{\ell(p)}(\alpha)&\text{if}\ \alpha\not\in\dom(\nu).\end{cases}

•

(A^{p}_{n})_{\langle\nu\rangle}:=\{\eta\in A^{p}_{n}\mid\nu\prec\eta\}
for each

More generally, given a finite
\ll
-increasing sequence

\nu_{n}

p{}^{\curvearrowright}\vec{\nu}=(\cdots((p^{\curvearrowright}\vec{\nu}_{\ell(p)})^{\curvearrowright}\vec{\nu}_{\ell(p)+1})^{\curvearrowright}\cdots\vec{\nu}_{m-1}.

Now we are ready to define the ordering
\leqslant_{\infty}
on
\mathbb{P}_{\infty}
. We say that
p\leqslant q
if and only if
\ell(q)\leqslant\ell(p)
and there is a
\ll
-increasing sequence
\vec{\nu}
such that
p\leqslant^{\circ}q^{\curvearrowright}\vec{\nu}
. Next, we define the weak system structure of
\mathbb{P}_{\infty}
. Consider the downwards directed posetal category
\mathsf{D}
where objects are
\subseteq^{\ast}
-increasing sequences
\langle d_{n}:n<\omega\rangle
where each
d_{n}\in\mathcal{D}_{n}
. Given two objects
d
and
e

e\to d
if and only if
d_{n}\subseteq^{\ast}e_{n}
for each
n<\omega
. This is downwards directed, because given any two objects
d
and
e
we can consider
f:=\langle d_{n}\cup e_{n}:n<\omega\rangle
, that is still an object of
\mathsf{D}
, and is such that
f\to e
and
f\to d

d_{n}\subseteq^{\ast}d_{n}\cup e_{n}
and
e_{n}\subseteq^{\ast}d_{n}\cup e_{n}
for each

.

For each object

in the category
\mathsf{D}
, we define
\mathbb{P}_{d}
as the subposet of
\mathbb{P}_{\infty}
consisting of all elements

such that
\dom(f^{p}_{n})\subseteq d_{n}
for each

.

If

Lemma 4.6
.

The diagonal Merimovich forcing is a continuous weak system.

Proof.

Consider the inclusion
\imath_{d,e}:\mathbb{P}_{d}\to\mathbb{P}_{e}
, that is a complete embedding and

\pi_{e,d}\circ\imath_{d,e}=\id_{\mathbb{P}_{d}}.

This can be extended to a complete embedding
\imath_{d,e}:\mathbb{B}_{d}\to\mathbb{B}_{e}
such that

\varrho_{e,d}\circ\imath_{d,e}=\id_{\mathbb{B}_{d}}.

Since every

is such that

for each

belongs to some
\mathbb{B}_{d}
for

an object of the category

5.
Axiomatising Higher Solovay Models

Now we are finally ready to define when a model
L(V_{\kappa+1})^{M}
is a
\kappa
-Solovay model over
V
, following the footsteps of Definition
1.1
. Recall that, in this context,
\kappa
is a fixed strong limit cardinal, supremum of a non-decreasing
\Sigma
-sequence
\langle\kappa_{n}:n<\omega\rangle
.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
V\subseteq M
be models of
ZFC
. We say that the model
L(V_{\kappa+1})^{M}
is a
\kappa
-Solovay model
over
V
if
M
satisfies the following conditions:

•

V_{\kappa}=V_{\kappa}^{M}
;

•

for all subsets
x\subseteq\kappa
, the ordinal
(\kappa^{+})^{M}
is an inaccessible cardinal in
V[x]
;

•

for all
\alpha<(\kappa^{+})^{M}
and
x\subseteq\alpha
in
M
, there is a
\Sigma
-Příkrý forcing
\mathbb{P}\in V
with
|\mathbb{P}|^{M}\leqslant\kappa
, and
g\subseteq\mathbb{P}
generic over
V
with
V[x]\subseteq V[g]
and
\cof(\kappa)^{V[g]}=\omega
.

The last requirement looks stronger than its corresponding in Definition
1.1
, but this is justified because in the classical case the stronger version is automatically implied by the seemingly weaker definition, as the following statement shows:

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
V\subseteq M
be models of
ZFC
, with
L(\mathbb{R})^{M}
Solovay over
V
. Then for all
\alpha<\omega_{1}^{M}
and all
x\subseteq\alpha
in
M
,

is
small-generic
over
V
: there is a countable poset
\mathbb{P}
in
V
and a filter
g\subseteq\mathbb{P}
generic over
V
with
V[x]\subseteq V[g]
.

Proof.

Note that, thanks to Bagaria-Woodin’s Theorem
1.3
, it is enough to prove this lemma in the case
M=V[C]
for
C\subseteq\Coll(\omega,{<}\omega_{1}^{M})
a filter generic over
V
.

From now onwards the proof will be the same as the original proof of Theorem
1.2
done by Solovay in his
[
26
, Section 3.4]
. There, he proved that for all
y\subseteq\omega
in
M
,
y
is small-generic over
V
, but during the proof he showed something more: indeed, that for all such

there is some
\beta<\omega_{1}^{M}
such that, if we call

\ell_{\lambda,\beta}:\Coll(\omega,{<}\lambda)\to\Coll(\omega,{\leqslant}\beta)

the projection (like we did in Example
2.3
), then it holds
V[y]\subseteq V[\ell_{\lambda,\beta}^{\prime\prime}G]
. Notice that in this case
\ell_{\lambda,\beta}^{\prime\prime}G\subseteq\Coll(\omega,{\leqslant}\beta)
is the filter that is generic over
V
, and the forcing poset
\Coll(\omega,{\leqslant}\beta)
is countable in

because

itself is countable.

So, in our case, let
\alpha<\omega_{1}^{M}
and let
x\subseteq\alpha
be in
M
. Notice that, from the point of view of
M
,
\alpha
is countable, so there is a bijection
f:\alpha\to\omega
that is being added by the forcing
\Coll(\omega,{\leqslant}\alpha)
. Let

, which is a subset of
\omega
and belongs to
M
.

Thanks to Solovay’s proof of Theorem
1.2
, as we just remarked, there is
\beta<\omega_{1}^{M}
with
V[y]\subseteq V[\ell_{\lambda,\beta}^{\prime\prime}G]
. If we define
\gamma:=\max\{\alpha,\beta\}
, we have
\gamma<\omega_{1}^{M}
, so the partial order

, and if we call

is a filter that is
V
-generic, and from

and

.
∎

As we already mentioned, in the article
[
12
]
Dimonte, Poveda and the second author prove some regularity properties for the

-nice weak

Our first new result, the higher analogue of Theorem

, states that the models studied in

are

-Solovay model. To be precise, we need to assume that the weak system we consider is

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
\mathbb{P}_{\infty}
be a continuous
(\kappa,\lambda)
-nice weak
\Sigma
-system, and let
G\subseteq\mathbb{P}_{\infty}
be a generic filter over
V
. Then
L(V_{\kappa+1})^{V[G]}
is
\kappa
-Solovay over
V
.

Proof.

We verify that the conditions in Definition
5.1
are met.

First of all, we have to prove that
V_{\kappa}=V[G]_{\kappa}
. It is obvious that
V_{\kappa}\subseteq V[G]_{\kappa}
. Let
x\in V[G]_{\kappa}
. Since
\kappa
is a strong limit, then
\trcl(\{x\})
has cardinality less than
\kappa
, hence in particular there is an ordinal
\mu<\kappa
such that in
V[G]
there is a bijection from
\trcl(\{x\})
to
\mu
. Let
E
be the relation on
\mu
such that
\langle\mu,E\rangle\simeq\langle\trcl(\{x\}),\in\rangle
.

Since
E
is a bounded subset of
\kappa
, and by Lemma
3.3

\Sigma
-Příkrý forcings do not add any new bounded subset of
\kappa
, we know that
E\in V
. Hence, if we take the Mostowski collapse of
\langle\mu,E\rangle
in
V
, we get that
\langle\trcl(\{x\}),\in\rangle\in V
, hence in particular
x\in V
.

Then, we have to prove that if
x\subseteq\kappa
belongs to
V[G]
, then the ordinal
(\kappa^{+})^{V[G]}
is inaccessible in
V[x]
. Fix
x\subseteq\kappa
. Since
\mathbb{P}_{\infty}
is
\kappa
-capturing, there is an object
e
in
\mathsf{D}
such that
x\in V[\pi^{\prime\prime}_{\infty,e}G]
. Now, since
\mathbb{P}_{\infty}
is
\lambda
-bounded, we have
\mathbb{P}_{e}\in H(\lambda)
, and since
\mathbb{P}_{e}
cannot affect the cardinal structure above its size, then
\mathbb{P}_{e}
forces that “
\lambda
is inaccessible”. But
\mathbb{P}_{\infty}
is amenable to interpolations, hence
\lambda=(\kappa^{+})^{V[G]}
, so we get that
(\kappa^{+})^{V[G]}
is inaccessible in

, and, since
x\in V[\pi_{\infty,e}^{\prime\prime}G]
,
(\kappa^{+})^{V[G]}
is inaccessible in
V[x]
.

To finish, we prove that if
\alpha<(\kappa^{+})^{V[G]}
and
x\subseteq\alpha
, there is a
\Sigma
-Příkrý forcing
\mathbb{P}\in V
with
|\mathbb{P}|^{V[G]}\leqslant\kappa
, and a

generic over
V
such that

and
\cof(\kappa)^{V[g]}=\omega
. Let us start by proving it without the cofinality requirement. Fix such
\alpha
and

. Without loss of generality, we can assume
\alpha\geqslant\kappa
. Since
\alpha<(\kappa^{+})^{V[G]}
, and
\mathbb{P}_{\infty}
is amenable to interpolations, then
\alpha<(\kappa^{+})^{V[G]}=\lambda
, with
\lambda
inaccessible to
V
. In particular, there is some object
d
in
\mathsf{D}
such that
\mathbb{P}_{d}
forces
\alpha
to have cardinality
\kappa
, because of the
({<}\lambda)
-capturing property of continuous
(\kappa,\lambda)
-nice weak
\Sigma
-systems we proved in Theorem
3.9
.

Let
f:\alpha\to\kappa
be a bijection added by
\mathbb{P}_{d}
, and let
y:=f^{\prime\prime}x
, which is a subset of
\kappa
belonging to
V[G]
. Since
\mathbb{P}_{\infty}
is
\kappa
-capturing, there is some object
d
in
\mathsf{D}
such that

and

. Since

, we also know that
f\in V[\pi^{\prime\prime}_{\infty,d}G]\subseteq V[\pi^{\prime\prime}_{\infty,e}G]
, so we get that

, as desired. Notice also that, since
\mathbb{P}_{\infty}
is
\lambda
-bounded, then
\mathbb{P}_{e}
has cardinality less than

, hence

For the proof to be complete, we need to be able to do the same but also ensuring that
\cof(\kappa)^{V[g]}=\omega
. Since
\mathbb{P}_{\infty}
is amenable to interpolations, then
\cof(\kappa)^{V[G]}=\omega
. In particular, in

there is a countable sequence that is cofinal in

, and such a sequence is a subset of
\kappa
. Hence, by the
\kappa
-capturing property of
\mathbb{P}_{\infty}

in
\mathsf{D}
such that in the extension

the cardinal

has cofinality

. But

is a downwards directed posetal category, hence when we choose the object

We can prove a version of this lemma that holds for the abstract definition of

Lemma 5.7

(Trace Lemma)
.

Suppose
V\subseteq M
are models of
\mathsf{ZFC}
and assume that
L(V_{\kappa+1})^{M}
is a
\kappa
-Solovay model over
V
. Let
f:\kappa\to E
be any function in

, such that

and
|E|<(\kappa^{+})^{M}
. There is a sequence
\langle B_{n}:n<\omega\rangle\in M
consisting of bounded subsets of
\kappa
such that
\bigcup_{n}B_{n}=\kappa
and
(f\upharpoonright B_{n})\in V
for all
n<\omega
.

Proof.

First, notice that if
E\in V
is such that
\left|E\right|<(\kappa^{+})^{M}
, then there is some cardinal
\alpha<(\kappa^{+})^{M}
such that
E
and
\alpha
are in bijection, and the bijection

is in
V
. So it is enough to prove the theorem for
b\circ f
in place of

(and then apply

E
is some
\alpha<(\kappa^{+})^{M}
.

In this case, notice that a map

can be seen as a subset

, and by using the Gödel pairing function we can encode in a definable way

as a subset of
\alpha
. Thanks to Definition
5.1
, there is a
\Sigma
-Příkrý forcing
\mathbb{P}
such that
|\mathbb{P}|^{M}\leqslant\kappa
and a filter

that is generic over

with

, and also
\cof(\kappa)^{V[g]}=\omega
.

Now we apply Lemma
5.6
to this

in

, finding a sequence

consisting of bounded subsets of

such that

and
(f\upharpoonright B_{n})\in V
for all

Lemma 5.8

(Interpolation Lemma)
.

Let
V\subseteq M
be models of
ZFC
and
L(V_{\kappa+1})^{M}
be a
\kappa
-Solovay model over
V
. Let
\mathbb{P}_{1}\in V
be a
\Sigma
-Příkrý poset with

, and
\mathbb{P}_{2}\in V
be any forcing poset (not necessarily
\Sigma
-Příkrý, and possibly trivial).

Let
\pi:\mathbb{P}_{1}\to\mathbb{P}_{2}
be a weak projection and let
\varrho:\mathbb{B}_{1}\to\mathbb{B}_{2}
denote the image of
\pi
applied to the pointed poset
\langle\mathbb{P}_{1},\mathbb{1}_{\mathbb{P}_{1}}\rangle
under the functor
\Psi
defined in Lemma
2.13

\varrho
is a projection between bottomless complete Boolean algebras and

\mathbb{B}_{1}:=\mathrm{RO}(\mathbb{P}_{1});\hskip 14.22636pt\mathbb{B}_{2}:=\mathrm{RO}(\mathbb{P}_{2})_{\downarrow\pi(\mathbb{1}_{\mathbb{P}_{1}})}.

Let
g
be
\mathbb{B}_{2}
-generic over
V
with
g\in M
, and let
q\in\mathbb{B}_{1}/g
. Then there is a sequence
\langle p_{n}:n<\omega\rangle\in M
of elements of
\mathbb{P}_{1}
decreasing in the ordering
\leqslant_{\mathbb{B}_{1}}
, with

,

and
\ell(p_{n})<\ell(p_{n+1})
, where

is the length of
\mathbb{P}_{1}
, for all

, and

M
and is
\mathbb{B}_{1}/g

with

.

Proof.

As
|\mathbb{P}_{1}|<(\kappa^{+})^{M}

{|\wp(\mathbb{P}_{1})|}<(\kappa^{+})^{M}
. Consider

E:=\{D\in\wp(\mathbb{P}_{1})^{V}:D\ \text{is dense open in}\ \mathbb{P}_{1}\}.

This set has size at most
\kappa

is a map from
\kappa
to

\langle B_{n}:n<\omega\rangle\in M

for all

. Let us now build a sequence
\langle p_{n}:n<\omega\rangle

We proceed by induction. For

\displaystyle R_{0}:=\left\{r\in\mathbb{P}_{1}\cap(\mathbb{B}_{1})_{\downarrow q}:\forall\alpha\in B_{0}\ \exists k_{\alpha}<\omega\right.

\displaystyle\left.\forall p\in(\mathbb{P}_{1})_{\downarrow r}\ (\ell(p)\geqslant\ell(r)+k_{\alpha}\to p\in D_{\alpha})\right\}.

Note first that

because

: this is crucial to run the forthcoming argument. Let us prove that

is a dense subset of
(\mathbb{B}_{1})_{\downarrow q}
: to do so, fix

. Since

is a bounded subset of

, there is

. Let
\langle\alpha_{\beta}:\beta<\gamma\rangle

and let us pick some

Now, we want to build a decreasing sequence

Now that we know

, and since

Now suppose we already defined

for
m\leqslant n
, some
n<\omega
. Define now

\displaystyle R_{n+1}:=\left\{r\in(\mathbb{P}_{1})_{\downarrow p_{n}}:\ell(r)>\ell(p_{n}),\forall\alpha\in B_{n+1}\ \exists k_{\alpha}<\omega\right.

\displaystyle\left.\forall p\in(\mathbb{P}_{1})_{\downarrow r}\ (\ell(p)\geqslant\ell(r)+k_{\alpha}\to p\in D_{\alpha})\right\}.

We could carry out the same argument as before to show
R_{n+1}
is dense in
(\mathbb{B}_{1})_{\downarrow p_{n}}
. Therefore, we can find a
p_{n+1}\in R_{n+1}
such that
\varrho(p_{n+1})\in\varrho^{\prime\prime}R_{n+1}\cap g
.

Thus, we constructed a sequence
\langle p_{n}:n<\omega\rangle\in M
, decreasing in the ordering
\leqslant_{\mathbb{B}_{1}}
, with
\ell(p_{n+1})>\ell(p_{n})
,
p_{n}\in R_{n}
,
p_{n}\leqslant_{\mathbb{B}_{1}}q
and
\varrho(p_{n})\in g
for all
n<\omega
. Let

h:=\{b\in\mathbb{B}_{1}:\exists n<\omega:p_{n}\leqslant_{\mathbb{B}_{1}}b\}.

This
h\in M
. To finish the proof, we shall show that
h
is
\mathbb{B}_{1}/g
-generic over
V[g]
.

By construction,
p_{n}\in\mathbb{B}_{1}/g
for all
n<\omega
, so it is enough to show
h
is
\mathbb{B}_{1}
-generic over
V
. Let
D^{\prime}\in V
be dense open in
\mathbb{B}_{1}
and consider the set
D^{\ast}\subseteq\mathbb{P}_{1}
defined as

D^{\ast}:=(D^{\prime}\cap(\mathbb{P}_{1})_{\downarrow q})\sqcup\{q^{\prime}\in\mathbb{P}_{1}:q\ \text{and}\ q^{\prime}\ \text{are incompatible}\}.

This
D^{\ast}
is a dense open subset of
\mathbb{P}_{1}
, hence there is some
\alpha<\kappa
such that
D^{\ast}=D_{\alpha}
. Let
n<\omega
be such that
\alpha\in B_{n}
. By definition of
R_{n}
, there is some
k_{\alpha}<\omega
such that every
p\leqslant_{\mathbb{P}_{1}}p_{n}
with
\ell(p)\geqslant\ell(p_{n})+\kappa_{\alpha}
belongs to
D_{\alpha}
. By construction, since the lengths of the elements of the sequence
\langle p_{n}:n<\omega\rangle
are always increasing there is some
m<\omega
such that
\ell(p_{m})\geqslant\ell(p_{n})+k_{\alpha}
, yielding
p_{m}\in D_{\alpha}=D^{\ast}
. Clearly
p_{m}\leqslant_{\mathbb{B}_{1}}q
, meaning that
p_{m}\in D^{\prime}
. Also
p_{m}\in h
, hence witnessing
h\cap D^{\prime}\neq\varnothing
.∎

Lemma 5.9

(Capturing Lemma)
.

Assume we are in the hypotheses of the Interpolation Lemma
5.8
, and there are also another forcing poset
\mathbb{P}_{3}\in V
, a
\mathbb{P}_{3}
-generic filter
g_{3}\in M
, and in
V[g]
there are an element
t\in\mathbb{B}_{1}/g
with
t\leqslant q
and a projection

\sigma:(\mathbb{B}_{1}/g)_{\downarrow t}\to\mathbb{B}_{3}:=\mathrm{RO}(\mathbb{P}_{3}).

Then we can pick
h
as in the Interpolation Lemma
5.8
with
t\in h
and
\sigma^{\prime\prime}(h_{\downarrow t})=g_{3}
.

Proof.

We mimic the construction of the proof of the Interpolation Lemma
5.8
, by also ensuring that the
\mathbb{B}_{1}/g
-generic
h
captures the given
\mathbb{P}_{3}
-generic
g_{3}
. Let

\displaystyle F_{0}:=\left\{u\in\mathbb{P}_{1}\cap(\mathbb{B}_{1}/g)_{\downarrow t}:\forall\alpha\in B_{0}\ \exists n_{\alpha}<\omega\right.

\displaystyle\left.\forall p\in(\mathbb{P}_{1})_{\downarrow u}\ (\ell(p)\geqslant\ell(u)+n_{\alpha}\to p\in D_{\alpha})\right\}.

We want to show that
F_{0}
is a dense subset of
(\mathbb{B}_{1}/g)_{\downarrow t}
. Let
s\in(\mathbb{B}_{1}/g)_{\downarrow t}
and

\displaystyle S_{0}:=\left\{r\in\mathbb{P}_{1}\cap(\mathbb{B}_{1})_{\downarrow s}:\forall\alpha\in B_{0}\ \exists k_{\alpha}<\omega\right.

\displaystyle\left.\forall p\in(\mathbb{P}_{1})_{\downarrow r}\ (\ell(p)\geqslant\ell(r)+k_{\alpha}\to p\in D_{\alpha})\right\}.

This set
S_{0}
is a dense subset of
(\mathbb{B}_{1})_{\downarrow s}
, because its definition is the same as the one of
R_{0}
in the Interpolation Lemma
5.8
, except on
(\mathbb{B}_{1})_{\downarrow s}
instead of
(\mathbb{B}_{1})_{\downarrow q}
. Since the map
\varrho:\mathbb{B}_{1}\to\mathbb{B}_{2}
is a projection,
\varrho^{\prime\prime}S_{0}
is dense in
(\mathbb{B}_{2})_{\varrho(s)}
. But
\varrho(s)\in g
, so
g\cap\varrho^{\prime\prime}S_{0}\neq\varnothing
. Pick
s^{\prime}\in S_{0}
with
\varrho(s^{\prime})\in g
. Then
s^{\prime}\leqslant_{\mathbb{B}_{1}}s
and
s^{\prime}\in F_{0}
.

We proved that
F_{0}
is dense in
(\mathbb{B}_{1}/g)_{\downarrow t}
. Since
\sigma:(\mathbb{B}_{1}/g)_{\downarrow t}\to\mathbb{B}_{3}
is a projection,
\sigma^{\prime\prime}F_{0}
is a dense subset of
\mathbb{B}_{3}
, hence
g_{3}\cap\sigma^{\prime\prime}F_{0}\neq\varnothing
. Pick
t_{0}\in F_{0}
with
\sigma(t_{0})\in g_{3}
. It is clear by construction that
t_{0}\leqslant_{\mathbb{B}_{1}}t
and
\varrho(t_{0})\in g
. In general, we define a sequence
\langle t_{n}:n<\omega\rangle
in
M
that is
\leqslant_{\mathbb{B}_{1}}
-decreasing and such that
\sigma(t_{n})\in g_{3}
for all
n<\omega
. This will be done following the previous argument taking as dense sets

\displaystyle F_{n+1}:=\left\{u\in\mathbb{P}_{1}\cap(\mathbb{B}_{1}/g)_{\downarrow t_{n}}:\forall\alpha\in B_{0}\ \exists n_{\alpha}<\omega\right.

\displaystyle\left.\forall p\in(\mathbb{P}_{1})_{\downarrow u}\ (\ell(p)\geqslant\ell(u)+n_{\alpha}\to p\in D_{\alpha})\right\}.

As in the proof of the Interpolation Lemma
5.8
, we can show that

h:=\{b\in\mathbb{B}_{1}:\exists n<\omega:t_{n}\leqslant_{\mathbb{B}_{1}}b\}

is
\mathbb{B}_{1}/g
-generic over
V[g]
. By construction,
t\in h
and
\sigma(t_{n})\in g_{3}
for all
n<\omega
. Since
\sigma
is a projection and
t\in h
, then
\sigma^{\prime\prime}(h_{\downarrow t})
is
\mathbb{B}_{3}
-generic. Also,
\sigma^{\prime\prime}(h_{\downarrow t})\subseteq g_{3}
so, by maximality,
\sigma^{\prime\prime}(h_{\downarrow t})=g_{3}
. Hence,
h
has the desired capturing feature.
∎

6.
Characterising Higher Solovay Models

In this section we prove the main theorem of this article, the higher analogue of Theorem
1.3
.

Theorem 6.1
.

Suppose
V\subseteq M
are models of
ZFC
and assume that
L(V_{\kappa+1})^{M}
is a
\kappa
-Solovay model over
V
. Let
\mathbb{P}_{\infty}
be a continuous
(\kappa,(\kappa^{+})^{M})
-nice weak
\Sigma
-system in
V
. Then there is a forcing notion
\mathbb{W}
in
M
(called the
constellating poset
) such that in the forcing extension by
\mathbb{W}
there is a filter
C\subseteq\mathbb{P}_{\infty}
generic over
V
such that
M
and
V[C]
have the same subsets of
\kappa
, hence the same
L(V_{\kappa+1})
.

Proof.

Let us denote by
\mathbb{P}_{d}
,
\pi_{\infty,d}
and
\pi_{e,d}
and the same sets and maps as in the discussion after Definition
3.5
, for
d
object in a downwards directed posetal category
\mathsf{D}
and
e
such that
e\to d
. Let also
\mathbb{B}_{\infty}
,
\mathbb{B}_{d}
,
\varrho_{\infty,d}
and
\varrho_{e,d}
be the images of the corresponding objects and morphisms under the functor
\Psi
, where we are considering the posets
\mathbb{P}_{d}
pointed with
p_{d}=\pi_{\infty,d}(\mathbb{1}_{\mathbb{P}_{\infty}})
, as we did after Definition
3.6
.

Working in
M
, we define
\mathbb{W}
as the collection of all triples
\langle p,d,g\rangle\in M
such that

•

d
is an object of
\mathsf{D}
;

•

g\subseteq\mathbb{B}_{d}
is a generic filter over
V
;

•

p\in\mathbb{B}_{\infty}/g
.

The order between the conditions is
\langle q,e,h\rangle\leqslant\langle p,d,g\rangle
if and only if
q\leqslant p
,
d=e
and
g=h
; or if
q\leqslant p
,
e\to d
with
e\neq d
and
h\subseteq\mathbb{B}_{e}/g
is a generic filter over
V[g]
. Let us start by showing that
\mathbb{W}\neq\varnothing
, and something more, in the following Claim
6.2
:

Claim 6.2
.

Given
d
an object of
\mathsf{D}
and
p\in\mathbb{B}_{\infty}
, there is
g\in M
with
\langle p,d,g\rangle\in\mathbb{W}
.

Proof.

Apply the Interpolation Lemma
5.8
to the models
V\subseteq M
, posets
\mathbb{P}_{1}:=\mathbb{P}_{d}
and
\mathbb{P}_{2}:=\{\varrho_{\infty,d}(p)\}
, the weak projection
\pi:\mathbb{P}_{d}\rightarrow\{\varrho_{\infty,d}(p)\}
sending everything to
\varrho_{\infty,d}(p)
, the trivial
\mathbb{B}_{2}
-generic
\{\varrho_{\infty,d}(p)\}
and the condition
\varrho_{\infty,d}(p)\in\mathbb{B}_{d}
.

Our Interpolation Lemma
5.8
finds us a generic
g\subseteq\mathbb{B}_{d}/\{\varrho_{d}(p)\}=\mathbb{B}_{d}
in
M
with
\varrho_{d}(p)\in g
. By definition of the constellating poset, it holds
\langle p,d,g\rangle\in\mathbb{W}
.
∎

Now let
H
be a
\mathbb{W}
-generic filter over
M
. Then we define, in
M[H]
,

C:=\{p\in\mathbb{B}_{\infty}:\langle q,d,g\rangle\in H\ \text{for some}\ q\leqslant p,d,g\}.

Claim 6.3
.

C
is a
\mathbb{B}_{\infty}
-generic filter over
V
.

Proof.

First of all,
C\neq\varnothing
because
H\neq\varnothing
and if
\langle q,d,g\rangle\in H
then
q\in C
. Then, if
p
and
p^{\prime}
are in
C
, it holds
\langle q,d,g\rangle\in H
and
\langle q^{\prime},d^{\prime},g^{\prime}\rangle\in H
for
q\leqslant p
and
q^{\prime}\leqslant p^{\prime}
, so since
H
is a filter we can find some
\langle q^{\prime\prime},d^{\prime\prime},g^{\prime\prime}\rangle
in
H
that is below both of the conditions, and
q^{\prime\prime}\leqslant p
,
q^{\prime\prime}\leqslant p^{\prime}
with
q^{\prime\prime}\in C
. Finally, if
p\in C
and
p\leqslant p^{\prime}
, the same
\langle q,d,g\rangle
witnessing
p\in C
holds for
p^{\prime}
, leading to
p^{\prime}\in C
. This shows
C
is a filter.

Now let us prove
\mathbb{B}_{\infty}
-genericity. Pick
D\subseteq\mathbb{B}_{\infty}
dense open and in
V
, and let

D^{\prime}:=\{\langle q,e,h\rangle\in\mathbb{W}:q\in D\}.

We shall show
D^{\prime}
is dense in
\mathbb{W}
. Indeed, let
\langle q,e,h\rangle\in D^{\prime}
. Then
q\in\mathbb{B}_{\infty}
hence, by density of
D
and by knowing that
q\in\mathbb{B}_{\infty}/h
, there is
q^{\prime}\leqslant q
with
q^{\prime}\in D\cap\mathbb{B}_{\infty}/h
. It follows that
\langle q^{\prime},e,h\rangle\leqslant\langle q,e,h\rangle
and
\langle q^{\prime},e,h\rangle\in D^{\prime}
, hence
D^{\prime}
is dense in
\mathbb{W}
.

Since
H
is
\mathbb{W}
-generic over
M
, we have
D^{\prime}\cap H\neq\varnothing
. Now consider any element
\langle q,d,g\rangle\in D^{\prime}\cap H
, then it holds that
q\in D\cap C
, hence
C
is
\mathbb{B}_{\infty}
-generic over
V
.
∎

To finish the proof of Theorem
6.1
, we only need to prove that
M
and
V[C]
have the same subsets of
\kappa
, that is,
\wp(\kappa)^{M}=\wp(\kappa)^{V[C]}
. Indeed, in the statement of the theorem a filter
C
generic for the poset
\mathbb{P}_{\infty}
is required, but
\mathbb{P}_{\infty}
and
\mathbb{B}_{\infty}=\mathrm{RO}(\mathbb{P}_{\infty})
are forcing equivalent, hence our filter
C
that is
\mathbb{B}_{\infty}
-generic over
V
is enough.

Claim 6.4
.

For all
x\in\wp(\kappa)^{M}
, the set

D_{x}:=\{\langle q,d,g\rangle\in\mathbb{W}:x\in V[g]\}

is dense.

Proof.

Fix
x\in\wp(\kappa)^{M}
and let
\langle p,d,h\rangle
be a condition in
\mathbb{W}
. We want to show that there is
\langle q,e,h^{\prime}\rangle\in\mathbb{W}
with
\langle p,d,h\rangle\geqslant\langle q,e,h^{\prime}\rangle
and crucially
x\in V[h^{\prime}]
.

By hypothesis, there is a
\Sigma
-Příkrý forcing notion
\mathbb{Q}
in
V
, which has cardinality
|\mathbb{Q}|\leqslant\kappa
in
M
, and some
\mathbb{Q}
-generic filter
g\in M
over
V
such that
x\in V[g]
.

Let
G
be a filter
\mathbb{B}_{\infty}/h
-generic over
V[h]
with
p\in G
. Notice that this
G
need not to be in
M
. We claim there is a
\mathbb{Q}
-generic
g^{\prime}\in V[h][G]=V[G]
, and to show it we are going to invoke the
\Sigma
-Příkrý nature of
\mathbb{Q}
and its “interpolation feature”.

More concretely, notice that
L(V_{\kappa+1})^{V[G]}
is
\kappa
-Solovay over
V
, hence we can apply the Interpolation Lemma
5.8
to the models
V\subseteq V[G]
, the posets
\mathbb{P}_{1}:=\mathbb{Q}
and
\mathbb{P}_{2}:=\{\mathbb{1}_{\mathbb{Q}}\}
, the trivial weak projection
\pi:\mathbb{Q}\to\{\mathbb{1}_{\mathbb{Q}}\}
that sends everything to
\mathbb{1}_{\mathbb{Q}}
, and the condition
\mathbb{1}_{\mathbb{Q}}
. Then our Interpolation Lemma
5.8
yields a generic filter
g^{\prime}_{1}\subseteq\mathbb{R}/\{\mathbb{1}_{\mathbb{Q}}\}=\mathbb{R}
in
V[G]
, where
\mathbb{R}:=\mathrm{RO}(\mathbb{Q})
. Let
g^{\prime}:=g_{1}^{\prime}\cap\mathbb{Q}
.

Thus,
g^{\prime}\in V[G]
is
\mathbb{Q}
-generic over
V
. Now we exploit the
\kappa
-capturing property of
\mathcal{P}
, to find an object
e
of
\mathsf{D}
such that
e\to d
and
g^{\prime}\in V[h][\varrho_{\infty,e}^{\prime\prime}G]
. Hence,

V[h][\varrho_{\infty,e}^{\prime\prime}G]\models``g^{\prime}\text{ is }\mathbb{Q}\text{-generic over}\ V".

Now, recall that a forcing name
\tau
is
open
whenever
\langle\sigma,q\rangle\in\tau
implies
\langle\sigma,r\rangle\in\tau
for all
r\leqslant q
. For every name
\sigma
there is always an open name
\tau
such that
\mathbb{1}\Vdash(\tau=\sigma)
. Hence, fix
t\in\varrho_{\infty,e}^{\prime\prime}G\subseteq\mathbb{B}_{e}/h
and let us pick an open
\mathbb{B}_{e}/h
-name
\gamma
such that

V[h]\models``t\Vdash_{\mathbb{B}_{e}/h}``\gamma\ \text{is}\ \mathbb{Q}\text{-generic over}\ V"".

Since
\gamma
is open, we may assume that it is actually a
(\mathbb{B}_{e}/h)_{\downarrow t}
-name, for otherwise

\gamma^{\prime}:=\{\langle\sigma,v\rangle:v\leqslant t,\ \langle\sigma,v\rangle\in\gamma\}

is a
(\mathbb{B}_{e}/h)_{\downarrow t}
-name with
t\Vdash_{\mathbb{B}_{e}/h}(\gamma=\gamma^{\prime})
, thanks to the openness of
\gamma
.

Since
t\in\varrho_{\infty,e}^{\prime\prime}G
and
p\in H
, we may also assume
t\leqslant\varrho_{\infty,e}(p)
. For otherwise, say
\varrho_{\infty,e}(s)\leqslant t
for some
s\in H
. Then we can find some
s^{\prime}\leqslant s
and
s^{\prime}\leqslant p
with
s^{\prime}\in H
. Replace
t
with
t^{\prime}:=\varrho_{\infty,e}(s^{\prime})\leqslant\varrho_{\infty,e}(p)
, and we get the desired conclusion.

Working for a moment inside
V[h]
, let us consider the complete embedding

\imath:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow(\mathbb{B}_{e}/h)_{\downarrow t}

induced by
\gamma
, namely, the map defined by the formula

\imath(r):=\bigvee\{s\in(\mathbb{B}_{e}/h)_{\downarrow t}:s\Vdash_{(\mathbb{B}_{e}/h)_{\downarrow t}}\check{r}\in\gamma\}\big.

Appealing to Lemma
2.8
we deduce (inside
V[h]
) the existence of a projection

\sigma\colon(\mathbb{B}_{e}/h)_{\downarrow t}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}.

By the Capturing Lemma
5.9
, applied to
V\subseteq M
, the projection
\sigma
and the generic
g\in M
, we deduce there is a generic
h^{\prime}\subseteq\mathbb{B}_{e}/h
in
M
with
t\in h^{\prime}
and
\sigma^{\prime\prime}(h^{\prime}_{\downarrow t})=g
. Since
\sigma\in V[h]
and
h^{\prime}\in V[h][h^{\prime}]
, we get that
g\in V[h][h^{\prime}]=V[h^{\prime}]
. So we conclude that
\langle p,e,h^{\prime}\rangle\leqslant\langle p,d,h\rangle
by definition, and
x\in V[g]\subseteq V[h^{\prime}]
, hence
D_{x}
is dense.
∎

Now we are able to prove that
\wp(\kappa)^{M}\subseteq\wp(\kappa)^{V[C]}
. Let
x\in\wp(\kappa)^{M}
. Since
D_{x}
is dense and
H
is generic,
D_{x}\cap H\neq\varnothing
, hence we can pick
\langle q,d,g\rangle\in D_{x}\cap H
.

Fix
p\in C
. Then there is
\langle p^{\prime},e,h\rangle\in H
where
p^{\prime}\leqslant p
,
e
is an object of
\mathsf{D}
and
g
is
\mathbb{B}_{e}
-generic. Since
H
is a filter, there is a common extension
\langle p^{\prime\prime},e^{\prime},h^{\prime}\rangle
of
\langle p^{\prime},e,h\rangle
and
\langle q,d,g\rangle
. We know
p^{\prime\prime}\in\mathbb{B}_{\infty}/h^{\prime}
, hence
\varrho_{\infty,e^{\prime}}(p^{\prime\prime})\in h^{\prime}\subseteq\mathbb{B}_{e^{\prime}}/g
. But now, we know

\varrho_{\infty,d}(p^{\prime\prime})=\varrho_{e^{\prime},d}(\varrho_{\infty,e^{\prime}}(p^{\prime\prime}))\in g,

and from
\varrho_{\infty,d}(p^{\prime\prime})\in g
we get that
\varrho_{\infty,d}(p)\in g
. But
p
was picked as any element of
C
, hence we proved
\varrho_{\infty,d}^{\prime\prime}C\subseteq g
and since they are both generic
\varrho_{\infty,d}^{\prime\prime}C=g
.

But now
x\in V[g]=V[\varrho_{\infty,d}^{\prime\prime}C]\subseteq V[C]
, hence
\wp(\kappa)^{M}\subseteq\wp(\kappa)^{V[C]}
, as desired.

Claim 6.5
.

For all objects
e
of
\mathsf{D}
, the set

E_{e}:=\{\langle p,e^{\prime},h\rangle\in\mathbb{W}:e^{\prime}\to e\}

is dense.

Proof.

Let
\langle q_{0},e_{0},h_{0}\rangle\in\mathbb{W}
. Since the posetal category
\mathsf{D}
is downwards directed, there is an object
e^{\prime}
such that
e^{\prime}\to e_{0}
and
e^{\prime}\to e
. We want to find
h
such that
\langle q_{0},e^{\prime},h\rangle\leqslant\langle q_{0},e_{0},h_{0}\rangle
. The main obstacle is to find a
\mathbb{B}_{e^{\prime}}
-generic
h
over
V
which is also
\mathbb{B}_{e^{\prime}}/h_{0}
over
V[h_{0}]
.

Here the Interpolation Lemma
5.8
comes to our rescue! Since
\mathbb{P}_{\infty}
is a continuous
(\kappa,(\kappa^{+})^{M})
-nice weak
\Sigma
-system, the hypotheses are fulfilled when regarded with respect to
V\subseteq M
, the posets
\mathbb{P}_{1}:=\mathbb{P}_{e^{\prime}}
and
\mathbb{P}_{2}:=\mathbb{P}_{e_{0}}
, the weak projection
\pi_{e^{\prime},e_{0}}
and the condition
\varrho_{\infty,e^{\prime}}(q_{0})\in\mathbb{B}_{e^{\prime}}/h_{0}
. The Interpolation Lemma
5.8
thus gives us a
\mathbb{B}_{e^{\prime}}/h_{0}
-generic
h\in M
with
\varrho_{\infty,e^{\prime}}(q_{0})\in h
. Hence
\langle q_{0},e^{\prime},h\rangle\in\mathbb{W}
is below
\langle q_{0},e_{0},h_{0}\rangle
.
∎

To finish the proof, we just need
\wp(\kappa)^{V[C]}\subseteq\wp(\kappa)^{M}
. Let
x\in\wp(\kappa)^{V[C]}
. Thanks to the
\kappa
-capturing property, we know that there is an object
e
of
\mathsf{D}
such that
x\in V[\varrho_{\infty,e}^{\prime\prime}C]
. Now we know that
E_{e}
is dense and
H
is generic, so we can pick
\langle q,e^{\prime},g\rangle\in H\cap E_{e}
.

As we did previously, we can prove that
\varrho_{\infty,e^{\prime}}^{\prime\prime}C=g
, and we know
g\in M
, so

x\in V[\varrho_{\infty,e}^{\prime\prime}C]\subseteq V[\varrho_{\infty,e^{\prime}}^{\prime\prime}C]=V[g]\subseteq M.

We just need to prove that if
x\in\wp(\kappa)^{V[C]}
then
x\in M
. Thanks to the
\kappa
-capturing property of
(\kappa,(\kappa^{+})^{M})
-nice weak systems, we know there is an object
e
in
\mathsf{D}
such that
x\in{V[\varrho_{e}^{\prime\prime}C]}
.
If now we pick any
\langle q,e^{\prime},g\rangle\in H\cap E_{e}
, we know that
\varrho_{e^{\prime}}^{\prime\prime}C=g
as before, and
g\in M
, so
x\in V[\varrho_{e}^{\prime\prime}C]\subseteq V[\varrho_{e^{\prime}}^{\prime\prime}C]=V[g]\subseteq M
, hence
x\in\wp(\kappa)^{M}
.
∎

Thanks to this result, we have a complete characterisation of
\kappa
-Solovay models:

Corollary 6.6
.

L(V_{\kappa+1})^{M}
is a
\kappa
-Solovay model if and only if it is the
L(V_{\kappa+1}
) of a generic extension of
V
via a continuous
(\kappa,\lambda)
-nice weak
\Sigma
-system for some
\lambda>\kappa
inaccessible.

Proof.

One implication is Theorem
5.3
, the other one is Theorem
6.1
.
∎

While the Lévy collapse
\Coll(\omega,{<}\lambda)
is uniquely determined by
\lambda
, there could be many different continuous
(\kappa,\lambda)
-nice weak
\Sigma
-systems even with the same
\kappa
and
\lambda
. Hence, a priori, this could have been a difference between classical Solovay models and our
\kappa
-Solovay models. But thanks to our main theorem, this is not the case:

Corollary 6.7
.

Let
\mathbb{P}_{\infty}
and
\mathbb{Q}_{\infty}
be two continuous
(\kappa,\lambda)
-nice weak
\Sigma
-systems. Then for all
G\subseteq\mathbb{P}_{\infty}
that are
V
-generic there is
H\subseteq\mathbb{Q}_{\infty}
, also
V
-generic, such that

L(V_{\kappa+1})^{V[G]}=L(V_{\kappa+1})^{V[H]}.

Proof.

Consider the constellating poset
\mathbb{W}
of
\mathbb{Q}_{\infty}
in
V[G]
. Thanks to Theorem
6.1
we know that in any extension by this forcing there is a filter
H\subseteq\mathbb{Q}_{\infty}
that is
V
-generic such that
\wp(\kappa)^{V[G]}=\wp(\kappa)^{V[H]}
, hence also
L(V_{\kappa+1})^{V[G]}=L(V_{\kappa+1})^{V[H]}
, as desired.
∎

7.
Regularity Properties

In the classical case, it is provable that if
L(\mathbb{R})^{M}
is a Solovay model over
V
, then every subset of the reals in
L(\mathbb{R})^{M}
is Lebesgue measurable, has the Baire and the perfect set properties, and satisfies many other nice regular behaviours.

We now establish an analogous result for the
\kappa
-Solovay model. To this end, we first introduce the higher analogues of the perfect set property and the Baire property in this new setting. In contrast, a version of Lebesgue measurability for
\kappa
-reals cannot be defined, as shown by Agostini, Barrera, and Dimonte in
[
1
]
.

Classical Descriptive Set Theory (of which a great textbook is
[
17
]
) studies the properties of definable subsets of Polish spaces, with the real line
\mathbb{R}
, the Baire space
\omega^{\omega}
and the Cantor space
2^{\omega}
serving as paradigmatic examples. A number of results in the field show that simply definable sets, such as Borel or analytic sets, exhibit a rich and well-structured canonical theory.

The emerging field of Generalised Descriptive Set Theory, although the term “generalised” is somewhat misleading and might be better replaced with “higher”, arises from the study of definable objects beyond the continuum. Specifically, this line of research employs descriptive set-theoretic tools to study definable sets in higher function spaces like
2^{\kappa}
and
\kappa^{\kappa}
, as in
[
13
]
. It turns out that when
\kappa
is an infinite strong limit cardinal with
\cof(\kappa)=\omega
, there are a number of results that parallel the findings of classical descriptive set theory, and that get lost in the uncountable regular case.

For the rest of the section, we assume that
\kappa
is a cardinal with
\cof(\kappa)=\omega
and
\beth_{\kappa}=\kappa
(recall that
\beth_{\kappa}=\left|V_{\kappa}\right|
). Indeed, this implies that

L(V_{\kappa+1})=L(\wp(\kappa)),

and also if
M
is a model of
ZFC
it holds that

L(V_{\kappa+1})^{M}\models\mathsf{ZF}+\mathsf{DC}_{\kappa}.

For a proof of
\mathsf{DC}_{\kappa}
in
L(V_{\kappa+1})
see
[
9
, Lemma 4.10]
.

Definition 7.1
.

A topological space is called
\kappa
-Polish
in case it is homeomorphic to a completely metrisable space with weight
\kappa
.

For more details on the definition of
\kappa
-Polish spaces and on the following examples, we refer to
[
10
]
.

The first example of
\kappa
-Polish space is the higher Baire space
\kappa^{\omega}
, equipped with the bounded topology: the basic open neighbourhoods are the ones of the form

\mathcal{N}_{s}:=\{x\in\kappa^{\omega}:(x\upharpoonright\ell(s))=s\},

for
s\in\kappa^{<\omega}
. Another example, that is homeomorphic to
\kappa^{\omega}
, is the higher Cantor space
2^{\kappa}
with the bounded topology.

A less straightforward example of
\kappa
-Polish space is the following. Let
\Sigma=\langle\kappa_{n}:n<\omega\rangle
be an increasing sequence of regular cardinals with
\kappa=\sup_{n}\kappa_{n}
. The space

\textstyle\prod_{n<\omega}\kappa_{n}
is a closed subspace of
\kappa^{\omega}
, hence it is
\kappa
-Polish.

Next, we want to introduce the higher versions of the perfect set and the Baire properties. Recall that a subset of
\mathbb{R}
has the perfect set property if it is either countable or contains a perfect subset, that is, a closed set without isolated points.

Definition 7.2
.

A subset
P
of a
\kappa
-Polish space is
\kappa
-perfect
if it is closed and for all
x\in P
and all open neighbourhoods
U
of
x
it holds
|P\cap U|\geqslant\kappa
. A subset
A
of a
\kappa
-Polish space has the
\kappa
-perfect set property
if either
|A|\leqslant\kappa
or
A
contains a
\kappa
-perfect subset.

Since continuous maps are uniquely determined by their behaviour on any dense set, and every
\kappa
-Polish space has weight
\kappa
, all continuous maps between
\kappa
-Polish spaces can be coded by elements of
V_{\kappa+1}
or of
\wp(\kappa)
. In particular, this means that the
\kappa
-perfect set property is absolute between models that share the same
\wp(\kappa)
. Specifically, a set
A\in L(V_{\kappa+1})
which is a subset of a
\kappa
-Polish space has the
\kappa
-perfect set property in
V
if and only if it has the
\kappa
-perfect set property in
L(V_{\kappa+1})
.

In
[
12
]
, the authors establish the following result:

Theorem 7.3
.

Assume
\kappa
is a
({<}\lambda)
-supercompact cardinal where
\lambda>\kappa
is inaccessible. Then, in any extension by the Merimovich forcing, every subset of
\kappa^{\omega}
in
L(V_{\kappa+1})
has the
\kappa
-perfect set property.

Proof.

The proof of this theorem can be found in
[
12
, Corollary 4.12]
.
∎

This theorem enables us to prove the following corollary.

Corollary 7.4
.

Let
V\subseteq M
be models of
ZFC
such that
L(V_{\kappa+1})^{M}
is
\kappa
-Solovay over
V
, and assume that
\kappa
is
({<}(\kappa^{+})^{M})
-supercompact in
V
. Then every subset of
\kappa^{\omega}
that belongs to
L(V_{\kappa+1})^{M}
has the
\kappa
-perfect set property, both in
M
and in
L(V_{\kappa+1})^{M}
.

Proof.

We can apply Lemma
4.3
to find that the Merimovich forcing built from
\kappa
and
(\kappa^{+})^{M}
is a continuous
(\kappa,(\kappa^{+})^{M})
-nice weak
\Sigma
-system in
V
. Hence by Theorem
6.1
we find
L(V_{\kappa+1})^{M}=L(V_{\kappa+1})^{V[G]}
where
G
is
V
-generic for the Merimovich forcing, and we apply the previous Theorem
7.3
to find the desired conclusion.
∎

If we denote by
\kappa\text{-}\mathsf{PSP}
the statement “every subset of
\kappa^{\omega}
has the
\kappa
-perfect set property”, as a consequence of Corollary
7.4
we get that

Corollary 7.5
.

Let
V\subseteq M
be models of
ZFC
and let
\kappa
be a
({<}(\kappa^{+})^{M})
-supercompact cardinal in
V
with
\beth_{\kappa}=\kappa
. Then if
L(V_{\kappa+1})^{M}
is a
\kappa
-Solovay model over
V
, it holds

L(V_{\kappa+1})^{M}\models\mathsf{ZF}+\mathsf{DC}_{\kappa}+\kappa\text{-}\mathsf{PSP}.

Next we turn to the higher version of the Baire property. Recall that a subset of a topological space is said to be
rare
if its complement contains an open dense set; it is
meager
if it is a countable union of rare sets; and it has the
Baire property
if it can be written as the symmetric difference of an open and a meager set.

In a similar fashion, one can define a
\kappa
-meager
set as an union of
\kappa
-many rare sets; and say that a set has the
\kappa
-Baire property
if it can be written as the symmetric difference of an open and a
\kappa
-meager set. A problem arises, because in the classical case
\mathbb{R}
is not meager, while all three examples we considered after Definition
7.1
are
\kappa
-meager, and this is trivially useless for our purposes.

To bypass this issue, another route is outlined in
[
11
]
. Namely, the authors consider the space
\textstyle\prod_{n<\omega}\kappa_{n}
with a topology different than the one mentioned previously. This will be the
\mathcal{U}
-Ellentuck-Příkrý
topology, but in order to define it we need particular assumptions: indeed, we require each
\kappa_{n}
to be a measurable cardinal and we let
\mathcal{U}:=\langle\mathcal{U}_{n}:n<\omega\rangle
be a sequence of normal non-principal measures, where each
\mathcal{U}_{n}
is a measure over
\kappa_{n}
.

The easiest way to define this topology is to introduce the diagonal version of Příkrý forcing. This is a kind of
\Sigma
-Příkrý forcing, once again demonstrating the strong connection between this family of forcing notions and higher descriptive set theory.

Definition 7.6
.

A condition in the diagonal Příkrý forcing
\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{U}}
is a sequence

p=\langle\alpha^{p}_{0},\dots,\alpha^{p}_{\ell(p)-1},A^{p}_{\ell(p)},A^{p}_{\ell(p)+1},\dots\rangle

where

•

s^{p}=\langle\alpha^{p}_{0},\dots,\alpha^{p}_{\ell(p)-1}\rangle\in\prod_{n<\ell(p)}\kappa_{n}
is strictly increasing;

•

A^{p}_{n}
belongs to
\mathcal{U}_{n}
for
n\geqslant\ell(p)
;

•

every
\beta\in A_{n+1}^{p}
is bigger than
\kappa_{n}
.

Given two conditions
p
and
q
in
\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{U}}
, we write
p\leq q
if

•

s^{p}\restriction\ell(q)=s^{q}
;

•

s^{p}(n)\in A^{q}_{n}
for all
\ell(q)\leqslant n<\ell(p)
;

•

A^{p}_{n}\subseteq A^{q}_{n}
for all
n\geq\ell(p)
.

Note that, when considering the diagonal Merimovich forcing from Definition
4.4
, this diagonal Příkrý forcing is equal to
\mathbb{P}_{d}
, where
d
the terminal object in the category
\mathsf{D}
, namely the sequence
d=\langle\{\kappa_{n}\}:n<\omega\rangle
.

Definition 7.7
.

For each condition
p\in\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{U}}
, define

\mathcal{N}_{p}:=\{x\in\textstyle\prod_{n<\omega}\kappa_{n}:(x\upharpoonright\ell(p))=s^{p}\land\forall n\geqslant\ell(p),x(n)\in A^{p}_{n}\}.

The
\mathcal{U}
-Ellentuck-Příkrý topology
is the topology on
\textstyle\prod_{n<\omega}\kappa_{n}
having
\mathcal{N}_{p}
for
p\in\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{U}}
as the basic open sets. A subset of
\textstyle\prod_{n<\omega}\kappa_{n}
has the
\mathcal{U}
-Baire property
if it has the
\kappa
-Baire property in the
\mathcal{U}
-Ellentuck-Příkrý topology.

As for the
\kappa
-perfect set property, having the
\mathcal{U}
-Baire property for a subset of
\textstyle\prod_{n<\omega}\kappa_{n}
is absolute between models with the same
\wp(\kappa)
, and the proof is routine. We would like to thank Vincenzo Dimonte for bringing this fact to our attention.

Dimonte, Poveda and the third author in
[

]
show the following result:

.

Let
\kappa<\lambda
be such that

is the supremum of a sequence of cardinals such that each
\kappa_{n}
is
({<}\lambda)
-supercompact, and

\kappa^{\omega}
in

has the
\kappa
-perfect set property, and also every subset of
\textstyle\prod_{n<\omega}\kappa_{n}
in
L(V_{\kappa+1})
has the
\mathcal{U}
-Baire property.

Proof.

The proof of this theorem can be found in
[

.

8.
An Application for Absoluteness

Here we prove our Theorem
8.1
, the higher analogue of Bagaria-Bosch’s Theorem
1.4
. This shows the existence of an elementary embedding between
\kappa
-Solovay models.

Theorem 8.1
.

Suppose
V\subseteq M
and
V\subseteq N
are models of
ZFC
such that
L(V_{\kappa+1})^{M}
and
L(V_{\kappa+1})^{N}
are
\kappa
-Solovay models over
V
,
\wp(\kappa)^{M}\subseteq\wp(\kappa)^{N}
and
(\kappa^{+})^{M}=(\kappa^{+})^{N}
. Suppose there is a continuous
(\kappa,(\kappa^{+})^{M})
-nice weak
\Sigma
-system in
V
. Then there is a unique

j:L(V_{\kappa+1})^{M}\hookrightarrow L(V_{\kappa+1})^{N}

that is an elementary embedding and fixes all the ordinals.

Proof.

First of all, notice that if
j
is an embedding as in the statement, and
a\subseteq\kappa
is in
M
,
j(a)=a
. Indeed, since
\wp(\kappa)^{M}\subseteq\wp(\kappa)^{N}
, the element
j(a)
is also a subset of
\kappa
in
N
, and
\alpha\in j(a)
if and only if
\alpha\in a
for all ordinals
\alpha
, since
j(\alpha)=\alpha
.

Fix now some
A\in L(V_{\kappa+1})^{M}
. By definition of
L(V_{\kappa+1})^{M}
, there are an ordinal
\alpha
, a set
a\in\wp^{M}(\kappa)
and a formula
\varphi
of the language of set theory such that

A=\{x\in M:L(V_{\kappa+1})^{M}\models\varphi(x,\alpha,a)\}.

If the embedding
j
as in the statement exists, then necessarily it holds that

j(A)=\{x\in N:L(V_{\kappa+1})^{N}\models\varphi(x,\alpha,a)\}.

So we can take this as the definition of
j
, and then we show this is well-defined and an elementary embedding, and we automatically get the uniqueness of such a
j
.

In order to do so, we only need to show that for every ordinal
\alpha
, every
a\in\wp^{M}(\kappa)
and every set-theoretic formula
\varphi
it holds the equivalence

L(V_{\kappa+1})^{M}\models\varphi(\alpha,a)\ \text{if and only}\ L(V_{\kappa+1})^{N}\models\varphi(\alpha,a).\hskip 28.45274pt(\heartsuit)

Indeed, assume we proved the equivalence
(\heartsuit)
. Let
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
be defined by

A_{i}:=\{x\in M:L(V_{\kappa+1})^{M}\models\varphi_{i}(x,\alpha_{i},a_{i})\}

where
\alpha_{1}
and
\alpha_{2}
are ordinals,
a_{1}
and
a_{2}
are elements of
\wp^{M}(\kappa)
, and
\varphi_{1}
and
\varphi_{2}
are formulæ in the language of set theory. Then it holds that
A_{1}=A_{2}
if and only if

L(V_{\kappa+1})^{M}\models\forall x\ (\varphi_{1}(x,\alpha_{1},a_{1})\leftrightarrow\varphi_{2}(x,\alpha_{2},a_{2}))

and applying the equivalence
(\heartsuit)
this holds if and only if

L(V_{\kappa+1})^{N}\models\forall x\ (\varphi_{1}(x,\alpha_{1},a_{1})\leftrightarrow\varphi_{2}(x,\alpha_{2},a_{2})),

since we can code
\alpha_{1}
and
\alpha_{2}
into an ordinal
\alpha
, and
a_{1}
and
a_{2}
into some
a\in\wp^{M}(\kappa)
. By definition of
j
, this is equivalent to
j(A_{1})=j(A_{2})
, proving that
j
is well-defined. Following the same proof strategy, we get that
A_{1}\subseteq A_{2}
if and only if

L(V_{\kappa+1})^{M}\models\forall x\ (\varphi_{1}(x,\alpha_{1},a_{1})\to\varphi_{2}(x,\alpha_{2},a_{2}))

and thanks to
(\heartsuit)
this is equivalent to

L(V_{\kappa+1})^{N}\models\forall x\ (\varphi_{1}(x,\alpha_{1},a_{1})\to\varphi_{2}(x,\alpha_{2},a_{2})),

which in turn is equivalent to
j(A_{1})\subseteq j(A_{2})
by definition. Since the relation
\in
is definable from the relation
\subseteq
, we get that
j
is an elementary embedding.

Hence, we just need to prove the equivalence
(\heartsuit)
. Let
\mathbb{P}_{\infty}
be a continuous
(\kappa,(\kappa^{+})^{M})
-nice weak
\Sigma
-system. Since
L(V_{\kappa+1})^{M}
is
\kappa
-Solovay over
V
, we can consider its constellating poset
\mathbb{W}^{M}\in M
of
\mathbb{P}_{\infty}
in
M
as in the proof of Theorem
6.1
.

Since
(\kappa^{+})^{N}=(\kappa^{+})^{M}
,
\mathbb{P}_{\infty}
is also a continuous
(\kappa,(\kappa^{+})^{N})
-nice weak
\Sigma
-system in
V
, and
L(V_{\kappa+1})^{N}
is a
\kappa
-Solovay model over
V
, so we can consider the constellating poset of
\mathbb{P}_{\infty}
over
N
as in the proof of Theorem
6.1
, and call it
\mathbb{W}^{N}\in N
.

Recall that we are trying to prove
(\heartsuit)
, so let us fix an ordinal
\alpha
, a subset
a\subseteq\kappa
in
M
and a formula
\varphi
of set theory. Thanks to the Claim
6.4
of the proof of Theorem
6.1
, we know there are an object
d
in
\mathsf{D}
and
g\subseteq\mathbb{B}_{d}
that is
V
-generic with
a\in V[g]
,
g\in M
.

Since the transitive closure of
\mathbb{B}_{d}
has size
\kappa
in
M
thanks to the fact
\mathbb{P}_{\infty}
is
(\kappa^{+})^{M}
-bounded, we can code
g
into a
\kappa
-real of
M
, hence the code is also in
N
, so also
g\in N
. Notice that for all
p\in\mathbb{B}_{\infty}/g
it holds
\langle p,d,g\rangle\in\mathbb{W}^{M}\cap\mathbb{W}^{N}
.

Assume
L(V_{\kappa+1})^{M}\models\varphi(\alpha,a)
. Let
H_{M}\subseteq\mathbb{W}^{M}
be
M
-generic,
C_{M}\in M[H_{M}]
be a
\mathbb{P}_{\infty}/g
-generic filter over
V
, as defined by Theorem
6.1
. Then it holds that

L(V_{\kappa+1})^{V[g][C_{M}]}\models\varphi(\alpha,a),

so in particular there is some
p\in C_{M}
such that

L(V_{\kappa+1})^{V[g]}\models(p\Vdash_{\mathbb{P}_{\infty}/g}\varphi(\check{\alpha},\check{a})).

Now, since
\langle p,d,g\rangle\in\mathbb{W}^{M}\cap\mathbb{W}^{N}
, let us pick a
N
-generic filter
H_{N}\subseteq\mathbb{W}^{N}
with
\langle p,d,g\rangle\in H_{N}
. And let
C_{N}\in N[H_{N}]
be a
\mathbb{P}_{\infty}/g
-generic filter over
V
with
p\in C_{N}
, defined as in the proof of Theorem
6.1
. Now we have a
p\in C_{N}
such that

L(V_{\kappa+1})^{V[g]}\models(p\Vdash_{\mathbb{P}_{\infty}/g}\varphi(\check{\alpha},\check{a})),

hence by the forcing theorem it holds that
L(V_{\kappa+1})^{V[g][C_{N}]}\models\varphi(\alpha,a)
and so thanks to Theorem
6.1
we proved that
L(V_{\kappa+1})^{N}\models\varphi(\alpha,a)
.

Now assume
L(V_{\kappa+1})^{N}\models\varphi(\alpha,a)
. Let
H_{N}\subseteq\mathbb{W}^{N}
be a
N
-generic filter and let
C_{N}\in M[H_{N}]
be a
\mathbb{P}_{\infty}/g
-generic filter over
V
, defined by Theorem
6.1
. Thus

L(V_{\kappa+1})^{V[g][C_{N}]}\models\varphi(\alpha,a),

so in particular there is some
p\in C_{N}
such that

L(V_{\kappa+1})^{V[g]}\models(p\Vdash_{\mathbb{P}_{\infty}/g}\varphi(\check{\alpha},\check{a})).

Now since
\langle p,d,g\rangle\in\mathbb{W}^{M}\cap\mathbb{W}^{N}
, let us pick a
M
-generic filter
H_{M}\subseteq\mathbb{W}^{M}
such that
\langle p,d,g\rangle\in H_{M}
. And let
C_{M}\in N[H_{M}]
be a
\mathbb{P}_{\infty}/g
-generic filter over
V
with
p\in C_{M}
, defined as in the proof of Theorem
6.1
. Now there is
p\in C_{M}
with

L(V_{\kappa+1})^{V[g]}\models(p\Vdash_{\mathbb{P}_{\infty}/g}\varphi(\check{\alpha},\check{a})),

hence by the forcing theorem we get that
L(V_{\kappa+1})^{V[g][C_{M}]}\models\varphi(\alpha,a)
and so by our Theorem
6.1
we have
L(V_{\kappa+1})^{M}\models\varphi(\alpha,a)
, as desired, concluding the proof.
∎

Corollary 8.2
.

Suppose
V\subseteq M
and
V\subseteq N
are models of

and
L(V_{\kappa+1})^{N}
are
\kappa
-Solovay models over

,
\wp(\kappa)^{M}\subseteq\wp(\kappa)^{N}
and

. Assume

({<}(\kappa^{+})^{M})
-supercompact in

. Then there is a unique

that is an elementary embedding and fixes all the ordinals.

Proof.

to deduce that under these hypotheses there is a continuous

9.
Open Questions

In Corollary
8.2
, we are assuming that
\kappa
is
({<}\lambda)
-supercompact for some inaccessible
\lambda
because this is the least large cardinal hypothesis under which we know for sure there is a
\kappa
-Solovay model, thanks to Lemma
4.3
and Theorem
6.1
. We ask whether this is optimal.

Question 9.1
.

What is, modulo
ZFC
, the exact consistency strength of the existence of a
\kappa
-Solovay model over some inner model
V
?

Note that, in light of Corollary
6.6
, the consistency strength of the existence of a
\kappa
-Solovay model is the same as the one of the existence of a continuous
(\kappa,\lambda)
-nice weak
\Sigma
-system. Therefore, Question
9.1
can be rephrased as a first-order question in the language of

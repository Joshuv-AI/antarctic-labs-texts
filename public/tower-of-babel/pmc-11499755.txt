Chemotherapy effects on mitochondrial function in adipose tissue in oesophageal and gastroesophageal junction adenocarcinomas with or without associated cachexia: protocol for a prospective, comparative observational study (ChiFMeOE)
Authors: Ophélie Bacoeur-Ouzillou, Lisa Guerrier, Julianne Touron, Alexandre Pinel, Bruno Pereira, Nathalie Meunier, Brigitte Gillet, Denis Pezet, Lucie Cassagnes, Corinne Malpuech-Brugère, Ruddy Richard, Johan Gagniere

AbstractABSTRACTIntroductionCachexia is strongly associated with digestive cancers, particularly oesogastric cancer. Mitochondria in adipose tissue are involved in the regulation of metabolism and physiopathology of cancer cachexia in animal studies. Chemotherapeutic regimens used to control tumour development could also alter mitochondrial function in adipose tissue. We hypothesise that cachexia induces an increase in adipose tissue mitochondrial energy metabolism and that chemotherapy can mitigate this. The purpose of the ChiFMeOE study is to identify adipocyte factors involved in the energy imbalance associated with the cachectic process and their response to chemotherapeutic treatments in patients with oesogastric cancer.Methods and analysisChiFMeOE is a single-centre observational study that will prospectively include 60 patients referred to chemotherapy and surgery for oesophageal and gastro-oesophageal junction adenocarcinomas at the University Hospital of Clermont-Ferrand, France. Visceral and subcutaneous adipose tissue biopsies will be collected during surgery scheduled before and after neoadjuvant chemotherapy administration, as well as cachexia and nutritional assessment. Introduction

Adipose tissue (AT) mitochondria are involved in both the regulation of metabolism and pathophysiology of cancer cachexia in animal studies.1 2 Several data suggest a key role played by adipocytes and their mitochondria in the clinical expression of cancer cachexia, essentially due to a deviation of the metabolism. Cancer cachexia is caused by a variable combination of metabolic changes, including high energy expenditure, excessive catabolism and inflammation, and reduced food intake (related to dysphagia). The synergy of these factors ultimately leads to body composition modifications characterised by a loss of skeletal muscle mass, with or without fat mass loss,3 leading to death for the most critical cases.4 Predominantly, cachexia is associated with a poor prognosis and increased risk for postoperative complications. Actually, there is no successful treatment of cachexia in these settings, and conventional nutritional supplementation strategies5 are only partially reversible.6 Cachexia is strongly associated with oesogastric cancers, with nearly 60% of patients suffering from cachexia at diagnosis.7 Surgery combined with perioperative chemotherapy (four sessions of FLOT before and after surgery) is the standard treatment for oesogastric cancer that has not metastasised to other organs. Successive chemotherapy used to control tumour development also alters the global mitochondrial function in tissues,8 could be also affect the AT mitochondria.

We hypothesise that cachexia induces an increase in mitochondrial energy metabolism and that chemotherapy can have a paradoxical effect on both AT mitochondria and cachectic process. In particular, a reduction in both mitochondrial AT activity and associated hypermetabolism is expected, which could be a promising avenue to further investigate how to preserve fat mass and by extension muscle mass.9 The main objective of CHiFMeOE study is to identify AT factors involved in energy imbalance associated with cachectic process in patients referred for oesophageal or gastro-oesophageal junction adenocarcinomas. Secondary objective is to evaluate the impact of chemotherapy on white AT (wAT) energy metabolism, specifically mitochondrial activity, by comparing results obtained from AT biopsies performed before and after neoadjuvant chemotherapy according to the cachectic status and the anatomical location of AT samples (subcutaneous abdominal vs visceral omental).

Methods and analysis

Study summary

The present study (ChiFMOE) is a prospective, comparative observational study where the subject is his own control, conducted in the Department of Digestive and Hepatobiliary Surgery at the University Hospital of Clermont-Ferrand, France and the Nutrition Assessment Unit at CRNH-Auvergne, Clermont-Ferrand, France. It will be proposed to all patients scheduled for a non-metastatic oesophageal or gastro-oesophageal junction adenocarcinoma surgery (corresponding to the ICD10 code: C15.4, C15.5, C16.0) to participate (table 1).

As a standard of care at our institution, an exploratory laparoscopy will be systematically performed to ensure the absence of peritoneal carcinosis and a jejunal feeding tube will be placed to optimise nutritional status prior to resection surgery, when needed. At the time of this laparoscopy, biopsies of both visceral (omental) and subcutaneous wAT will be performed, and a blood sample will be collected. Patients with non-metastatic cancer will receive four cycles of FLOT chemotherapy, with one cycle every 14 days. At the end of chemotherapy, a thoraco-abdomino-pelvic CT scan will be performed to assess the response to chemotherapy. If the disease is stable or has regressed, resection surgery (esophagectomy) will be performed. After surgery, four further cycles of chemotherapy will be administered.

Between administration of the fourth cycle of FLOT chemotherapy and resection surgery, a second medical and nutritional assessment will be performed at the CRNH Auvergne, allowing comparison with all initially collected parameters. Finally, at the time of resection surgery, new wAT biopsies will be performed and new blood samples collected.

Neoadjuvant treatment

It consists of four preoperative FLOT cycles. This treatment is intravenously administered every 2 weeks (14 days). FLOT chemotherapy combination consists of:

5-Fluorouracil (5-FU), a cytostatic antineoplastic of the antimetabolite (pyrimidine analogue base) class. It inhibits the thymidylate synthase, which thus stop the production of deoxythymidine monophosphate required for DNA synthesis.14 It is used at the dose of 2600 mg∙m-² as a 24 hour infusion on day 1.

Surgery

An exploratory laparoscopy is performed in search of obvious peritoneal extension of disease. A biopsy of subcutaneous AT will be collected during this laparoscopy over the optical trocar incision. A biopsy of the epiploon will be done before the exploration begins. Electrocoagulation will not be used for these two biopsies since it permanently alters the function of AT mitochondria. Esophagectomy is a two-stage procedure: an abdominal stage and a thoracic stage. Laparoscopic surgery is typically used for the abdominal stage. Before beginning resection, a sample of subcutaneous AT is collected at the optic trocar’s incision, followed by a biopsy of visceral AT at the epiploon.

Primary and secondary endpoints

The main objective of CHiFMeOE is to identify adipocyte factors involved in the energy imbalance associated with the cachectic process in patients referred for oesophageal or gastro-oesophageal junction adenocarcinoma.

The secondary objectives are to compare the results obtained before and after chemotherapy treatment according to the cachectic status and the location of the AT sample (subcutaneous vs visceral) and to evaluate the resting energy metabolism according to the cachectic state.

Measurements

AT mitochondria

Biopsies of wAT (visceral omental and subcutaneous abdominal) will be performed during the two consecutive surgeries (exploratory laparoscopy and resection surgery) under general anaesthesia and curarisation. The same day a sample will be used to measure the mitochondrial respiration following a Substrate-Uncoupler-Inhibitor Titration protocol (Oxygraph-2k G series, Oroboros Instruments, Innsbruck, Austria). This technique allows a dynamic characterisation of the mitochondrial functionality by measuring mitochondria oxygen consumption, expressed in pmol·sec-1·mg-1, as a reflection of their oxido-phosphorylating capacities.17,20 Briefly, 20 mg of cleaned wAT will be placed under physiological temperature (37°C) and normoxic conditions.18 Cells will be permeabilised by adding digitonin to allow a better access of substrates/inhibitors to mitochondria while maintaining intracellular integrity. Vmax will be measured when the whole respiratory chain is activated (ie, in the presence of glutamate, malate, octanoyl-carnitine, ADP and succinate).

Another sample of wAT biopsies will be frozen in liquid nitrogen and stored until analysis at −80°C. A quantification of mitochondrial density (citrate synthase activity, nuclear/mitochondrial DNA ratio18), quantitative PCR to study relative gene expression of key mitochondrial proteins and western blots to evaluate energy metabolism including peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor-gamma coactivator 1alpha, uncoupling protein 1 and superoxide dismutase will be performed.

Finally, a sample of fresh wAT will be fixed in formaldehyde and conserved in ethanol for histological characterisation of adipocytes and their environment.

Sample size estimation

Sample size estimation is based on the work of Kraunsoe et al18 and Jacques et al36 in order to highlight a minimum effect size of 0.8 in terms of mitochondrial activity pre- and post-chemotherapy which corresponds to an absolute difference of at least 6% for a SD between 7% and 8%. So, for a two-sided type I error at 5%, an intra-individual correlation coefficient equals 0.5, it is needed to include 19 patients to show such effect size for a 90% statistical power.

In view of the data in the literature, confirmed by those of our department, two-thirds of patients included should be cachectic. Also, we plan to include 60 patients to highlight a difference in mitochondrial respiration on AT by high-resolution respirometry between cachectic patients (n=40) and non-cachectic patients (n=20). With such a sample size, an effect size of at least 0.8 can be highlighted for a two-sided type I error at 5% and a statistical power greater than 80%, considering unresectable patients following chemotherapy cannot be taken into account in the analyses.

Exploratory sequential analyses will be conducted after the inclusion of 20 and 40 patients in order to evaluate aforementioned effect sizes and re-estimated statistical power.

Statistical analyses

Continuous data will be presented using mean and SD or median and IQR according to their statistical distribution. To assess the normality, the Shapiro-Wilk test will be performed.

For the primary endpoint, mitochondrial respiration on AT by high-resolution respirometry evaluated by Vmax, paired comparison test will be performed: paired t-test or Wilcoxon test if the assumptions to apply paired t-test are not met. The Pitman test will be used to investigate the equality of variance hypothesis. The result will be expressed using the effect size and 95% CI. The primary analysis could be supplemented by a multivariate approach allowing adjustment for possible confounding factors including potential dose reductions during the protocol due to excessive toxicity. This analysis will be based on mixed model taking into account time and dose reduction fixed effects in addition to patient as random effect in order to model between and within patient variability.

The aforementioned analyses will be conducted for all patients and then separately for cachectic patients and non-cachectic patients. Multivariate analysis will carry out with three covariates: cachectic status (yes/no), anatomical location (subcutaneous abdominal/visceral omental) and renutrition (yes/no).

The changes between pre- and post- chemotherapy (eg, for resting energy expenditure) will be calculated and will be compared depending on the anatomical location and the cachectic status using Student’s t-test or Mann-Whitney test. The hypothesis of equality of variances will be studied using the Fisher-Snedecor test. The results will be expressed with effect sizes and 95% CIs.

The statistical analyses will be performed using the Stata software (version 15, StataCorp, College Station, USA). A sensitivity analysis will be carried out to study the statistical nature of missing data (at random or not) and to propose the most appropriate imputation data approach. All tests will be two-sided, with an alpha level set at 5% for the statistical significance. Findings from analyses of secondary endpoints will be interpreted as exploratory owing to the potential for type 1 error due to multiple comparisons.

Discussion

The main goal of this study is to identify the mitochondrial adipocyte factors involved in the energy imbalance associated with the cachectic process in patients treated for oesophageal or gastro-oesophageal junction adenocarcinomas. Patients with oesophageal squamous cell carcinoma will be excluded from the study because risk factors and therapeutic management of this type of cancer differ from adenocarcinoma. Indeed, squamous cell carcinoma is often associated with both alcohol and tobacco consumption, while adenocarcinoma is often associated with obesity and gastro-oesophageal reflux disease.37 Additionally, squamous cell carcinomas are often treated with radiation and chemotherapy, while adenocarcinomas are typically treated with chemotherapy alone. Patients who did not receive chemotherapy before surgery will also be excluded from the study because one of the secondary goals of the study is to compare the results obtained pre- and post-chemotherapy treatment according to the cachectic status and the location of the AT biopsy.

One of the study’s limitations is its single-centre nature, but the study is being carried out in a high-volume tertiary expert centre.

This study will help us to better understand the pathophysiology of cachexia and the effects of chemotherapy on the adipose component. The results of the study will provide valuable insights into the mechanisms involved in cachexia and will help to explain clinical observations. The results could also lead to the identification of metabolic, adipose and/or mitochondrial markers that could predict the disease progression of patients.

To the best of our knowledge, no studies have investigated the role of adiposity factors in cachexia or the effects of chemotherapy on body lean and fat composition, including the presence or absence of cachexia prior to treatment.

A better understanding of the mechanisms underlying cachexia could lead to the development of interventions that could prevent or delay the onset of cachexia. This would improve the tolerance of patients to chemotherapy and reduce the risk of postoperative complications.

On periodic solutions of the Benjamin-Bona-Mahony-Burgers equation


Abstract
In this paper, we would establish the existence and stability of periodic solutions to the Benjamin-Bona-Mahony-Burgers (BBM-Burgers) equation in H 0 1 ​ ( [ 0 , 1 ] ) H^{1}_{0}([0,1]) , whose medium interior is applied with time-periodic force f ⁡ ( x , t ) f(x,t) with period θ \theta . High regularity analysis has been conducted in Hilbert spaces H ℓ , ℓ > 1 H^{\ell},\ell>1 . We also consider periodic solution to same IBVP scenario of a pseudo-parabolic-regularized equation as an extension of the BBM-Burgers in ℋ ℓ , ℓ = { 1 , 2 } \mathcal{H}^{\ell},\ell=\{1,2\} . Mathematical Subject Classification 2010: 35D05, 35K55, 35B10, 35Q93. Keywords : Periodic solutions; dispersive equation; viscous Burgers term; existence; global stability; pseudo-parabolic

Introduction

In this paper, we first concern with the tempral-periodic solution of a BBM (Benjamin-Bona-Mahony) equation regularized by a viscous diffusion:

u_{t}+u_{x}+uu_{x}-u_{xx}-u_{xxt}=f(x,t),

(1.1)

posed on a finite domain (a segment)
[0,1]
prescribed with homogeneous two-point boundary condition (Dirichlet boundary condition) and initial value:

u(0,t)=u(1,t)=0,\,\,u(x,0)=\phi(x).

(1.2)

We also consider similar problem of solution to a pseudo-parabolic equation:

\displaystyle u_{t}+u_{x}-u_{xx}-u_{xxt}+[F(u)]_{x}=[\Phi(u_{x})]_{x}+(I-\partial_{xx})[G(u)]+f(x,t),\quad(x,t)\in[0,1]\times[0,\infty)

(1.3)

prescribed with same initial-boundary conditions (
1.2
).

Equation (
1.1
) is BBM-Burgers equation. The original BBM equation was proposed in numerical fluid simulations in
[
25
]
by Peregrine in 1960s and later systematically presented aligned with Korteweg-de Vries (KdV) equation in
[
3
]
by Benjamin, Bona, and Mahony in 1970s. Both BBM and KdV models share same ground in proposing and are used to model the unidirectional propagation of small amplitude surface water waves formulated by gravity; however, the KdV has longer identification in the past tracing back in 19th century by
Boussinesq (see e.g. his earliest papers on the model
[
16
,
17
]
in 1871 and 1872) and Korteweg and de Vries, respectively (see e.g.
[
21
]
in 1895); we would also refer readers for related historical review to
[
30
,
24
]
by Whitham and Miura, respectively.

In this manuscript, we pursue the theory of periodic solution of the initial-boundary-value-problem (
1.1
–
1.2
) in function space
H^{\ell},\ell\geq 1
on segment
[0,1]
: existence, uniqueness, and stability. The framework is to separating original equation into two auxiliary linear and nonlinear ones. Essentially, the nonlinear term in the equation is treated as a perturbation of the linear problem, and can be estimated by linear theory via interpolation. This approach has been used in
[
12
,
14
]
by Bona, Sun, and Zhang on KdV equations posed on segment
[0,1]
. The KdV reads

\displaystyle u_{t}+u_{xxx}+u_{x}+uu_{x}=f(x,t)

with initial data
\phi
but nonhomogeneous two-point boundary conditions

\displaystyle u(0,t)=h_{1}(t),u(1,t)=h_{2}(t),u_{x}(1,t)=h_{3}(t),

where data are restricted:
(\phi,h_{1},h_{2},h_{3})\in H^{s}(0,1)\times H^{s+1\over 3}(0,T)\times H^{s+1\over 3}(0,T)\times H^{s\over 3}(0,T)
for time
T>0
.

In
[
12
]
, Bona, Sun and Zhang considered the linear KdV prescribed with same conditions:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}v_{t}+v_{xxx}+v_{x}=f(x,t)\\
v(x,0)=\phi(x),\\
v(0,t)=h_{1}(t),v(1,t)=h_{2}(t),v_{x}(1,t)=h_{3}(t).\end{cases}

(1.4)

Authors were able to formulate the solution map of (
1.4
):
(\phi,h_{1},h_{2},h_{3})\mapsto v
,

\displaystyle v(x,t)=W(t)\phi(x)+\int_{0}^{t}W(t-s)f(x,s)ds+W_{b}(t)(h_{1}(t),h_{2}(t),h_{3}(t))

where
W
is the semigroup leading to mild solution and
W_{b}
is vector form of the boundary integral operator mapping
(h_{1},h_{2},h_{3})
. Local well-posedness on
t\in(0,T)
in
H^{\ell}(0,1),\ell\geq 0,
can follow from this form. The nonlinear problem is a rewriting of linear IBVP (
1.4
) with perturbation
f=uu_{x}
.
u
’s existence in certain
H^{\ell}
can be proved by using fixed point theorem, where the nonlinear term works as a perturbation of the linear problem. More generally and pragmatically, the half-line problem was considered (i.e., quarter plane of
(x,t):x\geq 0,t\geq 0
referred in earliest paper
[
2
]
by Bona and Bryant, and
[
10
,
13
]
by Bona, Sun, Zhang). In the laboratory experiment setting, one end of straight channel full of rest water is applied with mounting wavemaker (at
x=0
), which generates small-amplitude water wave with long wavelength (
u(0,t)=h_{1}(t)
with such temporal-periodic
h_{1}
). The wave will propagate down in the channel to the right toward the other end (“
x=\infty
”), where water has steady state:
u=0
. It is observed that when the small-amplitude periodic force is thrown at
x=0
, the wave turns to become periodic in short term. Two-point IBVP provides a pragmatic approximation of half-line problems as the simulations are implemented on finite segment
[0,L]
. This problem was proposed first in
[

by Bona and Dougalis, and numerically implemented in
[

They proved that
\|u(\cdot,t)-v(\cdot,t)\|_{H^{1}(0,L)}\leq\gamma(t)e^{-L\lambda}
where

is the solution of half-line problem;

is selected so that

. This facilitates the simulation of two-point problem has prediction power on half-line one when

is large. They also studied the connection between IBVP and whole line problem. In their later work

, the exact solution of IBVP has been given, and the comparison between solutions

but IBVP is posed on

It is noteworthy that there were generalized two-point boundary values or forcing problems. For instance, the following generalized boundary condition was studied by Bubnov

\displaystyle\alpha_{1}u_{xx}(0,t)+\alpha_{2}u_{x}(0,t)+\alpha_{3}u(0,t)=0,\ \beta_{1}u_{xx}(1,t)+\beta_{2}u_{x}(1,t)+\beta_{3}u(1,t)=0,

which leads to same original solution

Specifically, analysis on temporally periodic solutions generated by external time-periodic force (forced oscillation) had been also studied via similar semigroup fashion (see, e.g.
[

by Bona, Sun, Zhang on half-line, and

A further stability question related to large-time dynamics generated by the solutions is asked, due to the fact the water wave evolves to steadily periodic. We might summarize its answer as:

Theorem 1.1
.

The periodic solution is unique and globally stable in a phase space
H^{\ell},\ell\geq 0
. That is, if the force is time-periodic, the force-generated surface wave turns into time-periodic flow.

It is equivalent to view the periodic solution as limit cycle on function space
H^{\ell}
. For the KdV problems, the periodic solution exists uniquely and the answer to the above question is yes in
H^{\ell}
(see e.g
[
11
,
28
]
). In half-line KdV, the zero-order dissipation term
\alpha u,\alpha>0,
must be present in the model to predict the nature of stability that it is observed in experiment in which water wave will be temporally-periodic in a short term, which necessitates the adding dissipation in modelling practice. In corresponding PDE analysis, if dissipation is added, the obtained stability is exponential, alike in the two-point boundary problem. Still in half-line problems of BBM and KdV,
[
15
]
by Bona and Wu discussed the necessity of introducing dissipation terms to stabilize periodic solutions and they found that the viscous term
-\nu u_{xx}
is not strong as
\alpha u
. Roughly speaking, if only viscous Burgers term is in, the decay is algebraic; however, if
\alpha u
instead of Burgers’ term is in, the decay turns out to be an exponential decay.

It is sufficiently a historical physically interesting problem when one considers the whole line problems of long-wave models. The BBM can be considered being regularized more by Burgers term, as applying same fashion to “regularize” the KdV. The related results about BBM-Burgers, KdV-Burgers, and viscous Burgers were discussed in early work
[
1
]
by Amick, Bona, and Shonbek. On the BBM-Burgers, series of fundamental a priori estimates were prepared, which include the large-time decay behaviours. In the paper, authors already had similar observation on dissipation terms as
[

\|u\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim t^{-1/4},\,\,{\rm when}\,\,-\nu u_{xx}\,\,{\rm is\,\,added,}

while

\|u\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim e^{-\alpha t},\,\,{\rm when}\,\,\alpha u\,\,{\rm is\,\,added.}

A later work
[

by G. Chen et al proposed the free-vibration Cauchy problem ((
1.3

on whole line), and reached existence and exponential stability in

as in

represents one of generalized nonlinear convection terms including

, while

. Close to model’s dispersive origin, there are extended models related to theoretical and numerical aspects such as Sobolev–Galpern equation, and we would refer readers to
[

and references therein.

3
A priori estimates

In the following sections before Section 6, we consider the initial-boundary-value problem (IBVP) of BBM-Burgers equation:

\begin{cases}u_{t}+u_{x}+uu_{x}-u_{xx}-u_{xxt}=f(x,t),\quad(x,t)\in[0,1]\times[0,\infty),&\\
u(x,0)=\phi(x)&\\
u(0,t)=u(1,t)=0.&\end{cases}

(3.1)

3.1
A simplified linear problem

Firstly, consider a simplified linear problem:

\begin{cases}u_{t}+u_{x}-u_{xx}-u_{xxt}=0,\quad\quad(x,t)\in[0,1]\times[0,\infty)&\\
u(x,0)=\phi(x),&\\
u(0,t)=u(1,t)=0.&\end{cases}

(3.2)

The solution to (
3.2
) can be written as

\displaystyle(I-\Delta)u_{t}

\displaystyle=-u_{x}+u_{xx}

\displaystyle u_{t}

\displaystyle=(I-\Delta)^{-1}(-u_{x}+u_{xx}-u+u)=-(I-\Delta)^{-1}\partial_{x}u-u+(I-\Delta)^{-1}u,

therefore,

u(t)=e^{At}\phi,

where the generator
A
is defined by
A\psi=-(I-\Delta)^{-1}\partial_{x}\psi-\psi+(I-\Delta)^{-1}\psi
and the domain of
A
is
H^{1}_{0}(0,1)
.

To start estimates on linear problem (
3.2
), we first consider the operator
A
and its generated
C_{0}
-semigroup. It holds the following lemma:

Lemma 3.1
.

The operator
A
is bounded on
\mathcal{H}^{\ell}(0,1)
for all
\ell\in\mathbb{R}
, and is dissipative on
\mathcal{H}^{1}(0,1)
and
\mathcal{H}^{2}(0,1)
. Moreover, the operator
A
generates a
C_{0}
-semigroup and there exists
c>0
such that

\|e^{At}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}(0,1)\mapsto\mathcal{H}^{1}(0,1)}\leq e^{-ct},\quad\|e^{At}\|_{H^{1}_{0}(0,1)\mapsto H^{1}_{0}(0,1)}\leq Me^{-ct},

and

\|e^{At}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}(0,1)\mapsto\mathcal{H}^{2}(0,1)}\leq e^{-c^{\prime\prime}t},\quad\|e^{At}\|_{H^{1}_{0}(0,1)\cap H^{2}(0,1)\mapsto H^{1}_{0}(0,1)\cap H^{2}(0,1)}\leq M^{\prime}e^{-ct}.

As a consequence, for any
\ell\in(1,2)
, there exists
c_{\ell}>0
such that the interpolation of semigroup holds:

\|e^{At}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\ell}(0,1)\mapsto\mathcal{H}^{\ell}(0,1)}\leq e^{-c_{\ell}t}.

Proof.

First, using the boundedness of
\partial_{x}:\mathcal{H}^{\ell}(0,1)\to\mathcal{H}^{\ell-1}(0,1)
and the embedding of
\mathcal{H}^{\ell-1}(0,1)
into
\mathcal{H}^{\ell}(0,1)
,

\displaystyle\|Au\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\ell}(0,1)}

\displaystyle\leq\|(I-\Delta)^{\frac{\ell-2}{2}}\partial_{x}u\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}+\|(I-\Delta)^{\frac{\ell}{2}}u\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}+\|(I-\Delta)^{\frac{\ell-2}{2}}u\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}\leq C\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\ell}(0,1)}.

Note that for
u\in\mathcal{D}(A)
, straight calculation using integration by parts in
L^{2}
-inner product leads to

\displaystyle\langle Au,u\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{1}(0,1)}=\langle-u_{x}+u_{xx},u\rangle_{L^{2}(0,1)}=-\|u_{x}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{1}{2}\|u_{x}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}-\frac{(c^{\prime})^{2}}{2}\|u\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{1}{2}\min\{(c^{\prime})^{2},1\}\|u\|_{H^{1}_{0}(0,1)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{M^{2}}{2}\min\{(c^{\prime})^{2},1\}\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}(0,1)}^{2}

where
c^{\prime}
is from the Poincaré’s inequality
\|u_{x}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}\geq c^{\prime}\|u\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}

This shows that
A
is dissipative in
\mathcal{H}^{1}
.

On the other hand, consider
Au=g
, that is
u_{x}-u_{xx}=(I-\Delta)g
and we can see that

u(x)=e^{x}\int_{0}^{x}e^{-y}\int_{0}^{y}(g(z)-g_{zz}(z))dzdy,

it is clear that
u\in H^{1}_{0}(0,1)\cap H^{2}(0,1)
and
\|u\|_{H^{1}_{0}(0,1)}\leq C\|g\|_{H^{1}_{0}(0,1)}
(i.e.,
\mathcal{H}^{1}
).

Combining the disspativity and this estimate, by Phillips-Lumer Theorem, there holds exponential decay for semigroup
\{e^{At}\}_{t}:\|e^{At}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}(0,1)\mapsto\mathcal{H}^{1}(0,1)}\leq e^{-ct},c=-\frac{M}{2}\min\{(c^{\prime})^{2},1\}.

On
\mathcal{H}^{2}
’s, note that

\displaystyle\langle Au,u\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{2}(0,1)}

\displaystyle=\langle-u_{x}+u_{xx},u-u_{xx}\rangle_{L^{2}(0,1)}

\displaystyle=\langle u_{x},u_{xx}\rangle_{L^{2}(0,1)}-\|u_{xx}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}-\|u_{x}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,1)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\|u_{x}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|u_{xx}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,1)}-\|u_{xx}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}-\|u_{x}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,1)}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\|u_{x}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,1)}-\frac{1}{2}\|u_{xx}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,1)}\leq-\frac{1}{4}\|u_{x}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\|u_{xx}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}-\frac{(c^{\prime})^{2}}{4}\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,1)}

\displaystyle\leq-c^{\prime\prime}_{M}\|u\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{2}(0,1)}.

Thus, we can see that
A
is also dissipative on
H^{2}(0,1)\cap H^{1}_{0}(0,1)
. We can also see that
A
has an inverse on
H^{1}_{0}(0,1)\cap H^{2}(0,1)
similar to above. In fact here, the only thing we need to estimate is the norm of
\|u_{xx}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}
. If
Au=g
, it is straight to see that

\displaystyle\|u_{xx}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}

\displaystyle\leq\|(I-\Delta)g\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}+\|u_{x}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|g\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}(0,1)}+\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}(0,1)}\leq C^{\prime}\|g\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}(0,1)}.

Via similar argument in that of
\mathcal{H}^{1}
, we also have
\{e^{At}\}_{t}:\|e^{At}\|_{\mathcal{D}(A)\mapsto\mathcal{D}(A)}\leq e^{-c^{\prime\prime}t}.

∎

Lemma 3.4

(A priori
H^{1}
estimate)
.

Let
\phi\in\mathcal{H}^{1}
. Then, we have the following estimate for all

\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}(0,1)}^{2}\leq C(\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}+\|\phi_{x}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2})e^{-\min\{c^{2},1\}t},

where

is the majorizing constant depending on the spatial domain for the Poincaré’s inequality.

It follows that there exists

such that for

\|u_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T];\mathcal{H}^{1}(0,1))}+\|u_{t}\|_{L^{2}([0,T];\mathcal{H}^{1}(0,1))}\leq C\|\phi\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}(0,1)}.

Multiply both sides by
u
and then integrate with respect to

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}u_{t}udx+\int_{0}^{1}u_{x}udx-\int_{0}^{1}u_{xx}udx-\int_{0}^{1}u_{xxt}udx

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|u\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}[(u(1,t))^{2}-(u(0,t))^{2}]+\|u_{x}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|u_{x}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|u\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}+2\|u_{x}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}+\frac{d}{dt}\|u_{x}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}

\displaystyle=0.

We shall note that
\int_{0}^{1}u_{xtx}udx=[u(1,t)u_{xt}(1,t)-u(0,t)u_{xt}(0,t)]-\int_{0}^{1}u_{xt}u_{x}dx=-\int_{0}^{1}u_{xt}u_{x}dx

\displaystyle(\|u_{x}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}+c^{2}\|u\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2})+\frac{d}{dt}\|u\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}+\frac{d}{dt}\|u_{x}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}

Therefore, by Gronwall’s lemma,

\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}+\|u_{x}(t)\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}\leq(\|u(\cdot,0)\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}+\|u_{x}(\cdot,0)\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2})e^{-\min\{c^{2},1\}t}.

\|u(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}(0,1)}^{2}\leq 2M(\|u(\cdot,0)\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}+\|u_{x}(\cdot,0)\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2})e^{-\min\{c^{2},1\}t}.

L^{2}([0,T])
with respect to

.

Let

hence

Using a priori estimate we have

\|u_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T];\mathcal{H}^{1}(0,1))}+\|u_{t}\|_{L^{2}([0,T];\mathcal{H}^{1}(0,1))}\leq C\|A\phi\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}(0,1)}\leq C^{\prime}\|\phi\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}(0,1)}.

∎

3.2
A linear equation with force

We consider the equation with imposed force in the domain prescribed with zero boundary condition:

\begin{cases}u_{t}+u_{x}-u_{xx}-u_{xxt}=f(x,t)\quad\quad(x,t)\in[0,1]\times[0,\infty),&\\
u(x,0)=0,&\\
u(0,t)=u(1,t)=0.&\end{cases}

(3.3)

Using semigroup as before, we know the solution to (
3.3
) is

u(x,t):=\int_{0}^{t}e^{A(t-s)}[(I-\Delta)^{-1}f(s)]ds.

We would derive the linear estimates in
Y^{\ell}_{0,T}
with

Lemma 3.7

(A priori estimate for (
3.3
),
\ell=1
)
.

Assuming
f\in L^{2}([0,T];\mathcal{H}^{-1}(0,1))
, then

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T];\mathcal{H}^{1}(0,1))}^{2}+\|u\|_{L^{2}([0,T];\mathcal{H}^{1}(0,1))}^{2}\leq C\int_{0}^{T}\|f(s)\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{-1}(0,1)}ds.

Moreover, we have

\|u\|_{W^{1,\infty}([0,T];\mathcal{H}^{1}(0,1))}^{2}+\|u\|_{H^{1}([0,T];\mathcal{H}^{1}(0,1))}^{2}\leq C\bigg(\|f(\cdot,0)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{-1}(0,1)}^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}\|f(\cdot,s)\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{-1}(0,1)}ds\bigg).

Proof.

Multiply
u
onto the equation and integrate over
[0,1]
,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}u_{t}udx+\int_{0}^{1}u_{x}udx-\int_{0}^{1}u_{xx}udx-\int_{0}^{1}u_{xxt}udx

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}f(x,t)u(x,t)dx

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|u\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}+2\|u_{x}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}+\frac{d}{dt}\|u_{x}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}

\displaystyle=2\int_{0}^{1}f(x,t)u(x,t)dx

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|u\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}+2\|u_{x}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}+\frac{d}{dt}\|u_{x}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}

\displaystyle=2\int_{0}^{1}f(x,t)u(x,t)dx

\displaystyle(\|u_{x}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}+c^{2}\|u\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2})+\frac{d}{dt}\|u\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}+\frac{d}{dt}\|u_{x}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{0}^{1}f(x,t)u(x,t)dx

\displaystyle(\|u_{x}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}+c^{2}\|u\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2})+\frac{d}{dt}\|u\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}+\frac{d}{dt}\|u_{x}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\|f(\cdot,t)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{-1}(0,1)}^{2}+\frac{c^{2}}{2}\|u(t)\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{1}(0,1)},

whose last line is applied with duality of
\mathcal{H}^{1}
and
\mathcal{H}^{-1}
.

Whence,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}+\frac{d}{dt}\|u_{x}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\|f(\cdot,t)\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{-1}(0,1)}-\frac{c^{2}}{2}(\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}+\|u_{x}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}),

\displaystyle\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}+\|u_{x}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\frac{c^{2}}{2}(t-s)}\|f(s)\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{-1}(0,1)}ds.

If we take supremum over
t\in[0,T]
, we have

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T];H^{1}(0,1))}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{T}\|f(s)\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{-1}(0,1)}ds,\quad\text{and}\quad\|u\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T];\mathcal{H}^{1}(0,1))}^{2}\leq CM^{2}\int_{0}^{T}\|f(s)\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{-1}(0,1)}ds;

and if we integrate with resepct to

, we have

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{2}([0,T];H^{1}(0,1))}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime}\int_{0}^{T}\|f(s)\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{-1}(0,1)}ds,\quad\text{and}\quad\|u\|_{L^{2}([0,T];\mathcal{H}^{1}(0,1))}^{2}\leq CM^{2}\int_{0}^{T}\|f(s)\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{-1}(0,1)}ds.

The proof is completed.
∎

Since
u_{t}=Au+(I-\Delta)^{-1}f(x,t)
,

solves

Then,

. Therefore,

\displaystyle\|v\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}(0,1)}

\displaystyle\leq\|e^{At}(I-\Delta)^{-1}f(x,0)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}(0,1)}+\int_{0}^{t}\|e^{A(t-s)}(I-\Delta)^{-1}f(x,s)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}(0,1)}ds

\displaystyle\leq e^{-ct}\|f(\cdot,0)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{-1}(0,1)}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{-c(t-s)}\|f(x,s)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{-1}(0,1)}ds.

3.3
A bilinear estimate

In this subsection, we will establish a bilinear estimate with constant independent of
T
.

Lemma 3.11
.

Let
\ell\geq 1
and
0\leq\ell^{\prime}\leq\ell-1
. Suppose
v,w\in L^{\infty}([0,T];H^{\ell}(0,1))\cap L^{2}([0,T];H^{\ell}(0,1))
. Then, for all
t\in[0,T]
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\|(vw)_{x}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\ell^{\prime}}(0,1)}^{2}dt\leq C^{\prime\prime}\|v\|_{Y^{\ell}_{0,t}}^{2}\|w\|_{Y^{\ell}_{0,t}}^{2},

where constant
C^{{}^{\prime\prime}}
doesn’t depend on
T
.

Proof.

We first show for the
L^{2}
case. Following the proof of Lemma 3.1 in
[
11
]
, by Gagliardo–Nirenberg interpolation inequality,

\displaystyle\|vw_{x}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|v\|_{L^{\infty}(0,1)}^{2}\|w_{x}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 4(\|v_{x}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}\|v\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}+C\|v\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2})\|w_{x}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}.

We first consider

\int_{0}^{t}\|v\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}\|w_{x}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}ds.

Note that one can bound
\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|v\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}\leq\|v\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}([0,T];\mathcal{H}^{\ell}(0,1))}.
Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\|v\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}\|w_{x}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}ds

\displaystyle\leq\|v\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}([0,t];\mathcal{H}^{\ell}(0,1))}\|w\|_{L^{2}([0,t];\mathcal{H}^{\ell}(0,1))}^{2}

using the fact that
\partial_{x}:\mathcal{H}^{\ell}(0,1)\mapsto\mathcal{H}^{\ell-1}(0,1)
is bounded (with constant 1).

Meanwhile, by Young’s inequality for products,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\|v_{x}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}\|v\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}\|w_{x}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}ds\leq\|v\|_{L^{\infty}([0,t];\mathcal{H}^{\ell}(0,1))}^{2}\|w\|_{L^{2}([0,t];\mathcal{H}^{\ell}(0,1))}^{2}.

Therefore, we have

\int_{0}^{t}\|vw_{x}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}ds\leq C^{\prime}\|v\|_{L^{\infty}([0,t];\mathcal{H}^{\ell}(0,1))}^{2}\|w\|_{L^{2}([0,t];\mathcal{H}^{\ell}(0,1))}^{2}.

Interchanging the role of
v
and
w
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\|(vw)_{x}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}\leq C^{\prime\prime}(\|v\|_{L^{\infty}([0,t];\mathcal{H}^{\ell}(0,1))}^{2}+\|v\|_{L^{2}([0,t];\mathcal{H}^{\ell}(0,1))}^{2})(\|w\|_{L^{\infty}([0,t];\mathcal{H}^{\ell}(0,1))}^{2}+\|w\|_{L^{2}([0,t];\mathcal{H}^{\ell}(0,1))}^{2}).

For general
\ell^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}
and
\ell^{\prime}
in between two integers, we can simply follow the argument as the proof of Lemma 3.1 in
[
11
]
.
∎

This section will be separated into two parts. We will focus on the case

in the first part; in the second part, we will discuss the case

; the proof of Theorem

, our first main theorem, will be in the last part.

There are the linear IBVP

(4.1)

and nonlinear IBVP

\begin{cases}w_{t}+w_{x}-w_{xx}-w_{xxt}+ww_{x}+(vw)_{x}=-vv_{x},\quad(x,t)\in[0,1]\times[0,\infty),&\\

(4.2)

It is clear that
v(x,t)+w(x,t)
solves the original IBVP of (
3.1
).

Note that the mild solution to (
4.1
) is

v(t)=e^{At}\phi+\int_{0}^{t}e^{A(t-s)}(I-\Delta)^{-1}f(s)ds;

and the mild solution to (
4.2
) is

w(t)=-\int_{0}^{t}e^{A(t-s)}(I-\Delta)^{-1}[w(s)w_{x}(s)+(v(s)w(s))_{x}+v(s)v_{x}(s)]ds.

From the discussion in the previous section, we are able to summary to get the following proposition before we start the estimate of (
4.2
):

Proposition 4.1

(Existence of Solution to (
4.1
))
.

Let
\phi\in\mathcal{H}^{i}(0,1)
and
f\in L^{2}([0,T];\mathcal{H}^{i-2}(0,1))
for
i\in\{1,2,3\}
. Then,

\displaystyle\|v\|_{Y^{i}_{0,T}}+\|v_{t}\|_{Y^{i}_{0,T}}\leq C(\|\phi\|_{\mathcal{H}^{i}(0,1)}^{2}+\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}([0,T];\mathcal{H}^{i-2}(0,1))})^{\frac{1}{2}},

where
i\in\{1,2,3\}
and the constant
C
is independent of
T
.

4.1
The cases
\ell=1
and
\ell=2

In this subsection, we will establish some bounds of
w
with
\ell=1
and
\ell=2
. The argument for
\ell=1
and
\ell=2
are formatted same by using semigroup estimates, so in the proof we will only provide that of
\ell=1
.

Proposition 4.2

(Existence of Solution to (
4.2
))
.

Let
\phi\in\mathcal{H}^{1}(0,1)
and
f\in L^{2}([0,T];\mathcal{H}^{-1}(0,1))
. Then, there exists
c^{(1)}<1
(independent of
T
) such that if
(\|\phi\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}(0,1)}^{2}+\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}([0,T];\mathcal{H}^{-1}(0,1))})^{\frac{1}{2}}<c^{(1)}
then (
4.2
) has a unique solution
w
. Moreover, in this case,

\|w\|_{Y^{1}_{0,T}}\leq(\|\phi\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{1}(0,1)}+\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}([0,T];\mathcal{H}^{-1}(0,1))})^{\frac{1}{2}}.

If
\phi\in\mathcal{H}^{2}(0,1)
and
f\in L^{2}([0,T];L^{2}(0,1))
, then there exists
c^{(2)}\leq c^{(1)}
(independent of
T
) such that if
(\|\phi\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}(0,1)}^{2}+\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}([0,T];L^{2}(0,1))})^{\frac{1}{2}}<c^{(2)}
then

\|w\|_{Y^{2}_{0,T}}\leq(\|\phi\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{2}(0,1)}+\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}([0,T];L^{2}(0,1))})^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Proof.

To prove the existence and uniqueness of the solutions of
w
, we shall use contraction mapping. We set

\Gamma(w)(t):=-\int_{0}^{t}e^{A(t-s)}(I-\Delta)^{-1}[w(s)w_{x}(s)+(vw)_{x}(s)+v(s)v_{x}(s)]ds.

We shall show that
\Gamma:Y_{0,T,M^{\prime}}^{\ell}\mapsto Y_{0,T,M^{\prime}}^{1}
, where
Y_{0,T,M^{\prime}}^{1}:=\{w\in Y_{0,T}^{1}:\|w\|_{Y_{0,T}^{1}}\leq M^{\prime}\}
. It is natural to take
M^{\prime}:=K(\|\phi\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}(0,1)}^{2}+\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}([0,T];\mathcal{H}^{-1}(0,1))})^{\frac{1}{2}}
and we will determine
K
later.

Let
w\in Y_{0,T,M^{\prime}}^{1}
, then

\displaystyle\|\Gamma(w)(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}(0,1)}

\displaystyle=\bigg\|\int_{0}^{t}e^{A(t-s)}(I-\Delta)^{-1}[(w(s)+v(s))(w(s)+v(s))_{x}]ds\bigg\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}(0,1)}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}\|e^{A(t-s)}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}(0,1)\to\mathcal{H}^{1}(0,1)}\bigg(\frac{1}{2}\|(ww)_{x}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{-1}(0,1)}+\|(vw)_{x}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{-1}(0,1)}+\frac{1}{2}\|(vv)_{x}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{-1}(0,1)}\bigg)ds

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}e^{-c(t-s)}\bigg(\frac{1}{2}\|(ww)_{x}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{-1}(0,1)}+\|(vw)_{x}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{-1}(0,1)}+\frac{1}{2}\|(vv)_{x}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{-1}(0,1)}\bigg)ds

\displaystyle\leq\bigg[\int_{0}^{t}e^{-2c(t-s)}ds\bigg]^{\frac{1}{2}}\bigg(2^{3}\int_{0}^{t}\bigg(\frac{1}{2}\|(ww)_{x}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{-1}(0,1)}+\|(vw)_{x}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{-1}(0,1)}+\frac{1}{2}\|(vv)_{x}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{-1}(0,1)}\bigg)ds\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{A}\bigg[\bigg(\int_{0}^{t}\|(ww)_{x}(s)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,1)}ds\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\bigg(\int_{0}^{t}\|(vw)_{x}(s)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,1)}ds\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\bigg(\int_{0}^{t}\|(vv)_{x}(s)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,1)}ds\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\bigg]

Here we have used the fact that
\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}^{-1}(0,1)}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\|u\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}
.

Using Lemma
3.11
, we have

\displaystyle\|\Gamma(w)(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}(0,1)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{A}^{\prime}(\|w\|_{Y^{1}_{0,t}}^{2}+\|w\|_{Y^{1}_{0,t}}\|v\|_{Y^{1}_{0,t}}+\|v\|_{Y^{1}_{0,t}}^{2})

thus

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|\Gamma(w)(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}(0,1)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{A}^{\prime\prime}(\|w\|_{Y^{1}_{0,T}}^{2}+\|v\|_{Y^{1}_{0,T}}^{2})

\displaystyle\leq C_{A}^{\prime\prime}\bigg(\|w\|_{Y^{1}_{0,T}}^{2}+\|\phi\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{1}(0,1)}+\|f\|_{L^{2}([0,T];\mathcal{H}^{-1}(0,1))}^{2}\bigg)

To obtain the estimate for
L^{2}([0,T];\mathcal{H}^{1}(0,1))
, notice that one has

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\|\Gamma(w)(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}(0,1)}^{2}dt

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{T}\bigg(\int_{0}^{t}\|e^{A(t-s)}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}(0,1)\to\mathcal{H}^{1}(0,1)}\bigg(\frac{1}{2}\|(ww)_{x}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{-1}(0,1)}+\|(vw)_{x}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{-1}(0,1)}+\frac{1}{2}\|(vv)_{x}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{-1}(0,1)}\bigg)ds\bigg)^{2}dt

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{T}\|e^{At}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}(0,1)\to\mathcal{H}^{1}(0,1)}dt\int_{0}^{T}\bigg(\frac{1}{2}\|(ww)_{x}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{-1}(0,1)}+\|(vw)_{x}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{-1}(0,1)}+\frac{1}{2}\|(vv)_{x}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{-1}(0,1)}\bigg)^{2}dt

\displaystyle\leq C_{A}\bigg[\int_{0}^{t}\|(ww)_{x}(s)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,1)}ds+\int_{0}^{t}\|(vw)_{x}(s)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,1)}ds+\int_{0}^{t}\|(vv)_{x}(s)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,1)}ds\bigg]

by the aid of Young’s convolution inequality.

Therefore, using similar argument as above, we can conclude that

\|\Gamma(w)\|_{Y^{1}_{0,T}}\leq C^{\prime\prime\prime}_{A}\bigg(\|w\|_{Y^{1}_{0,T}}^{2}+\|\phi\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{1}(0,1)}+\|f\|_{L^{2}([0,T];\mathcal{H}^{-1}(0,1))}^{2}\bigg)\leq C_{A}^{\prime\prime\prime}((M^{\prime})^{2}+\frac{(M^{\prime})^{2}}{K^{2}}),

and

\Gamma(w)\in Y^{1}_{0,T,M^{\prime}}
if
M^{\prime}<\frac{1}{C^{\prime\prime\prime}_{A}}\frac{K^{2}}{K^{2}+1}
.

Next, we shall show that
\|\Gamma(w)-\Gamma(w^{\prime})\|_{Y^{1}_{0,T}}\leq\frac{1}{2}\|w-w^{\prime}\|_{Y^{1}_{0,T}}
provided that
\|w\|_{Y^{1}_{0,T}},\|w^{\prime}\|_{Y^{1}_{0,T}}
are small.

First observe that

\displaystyle\Gamma(w)(t)-\Gamma(w^{\prime})(t)

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{t}e^{A(t-s)}(I-\Delta)^{-1}[w(s)w_{x}(s)+(vw)_{x}(s)-w^{\prime}(s)w^{\prime}_{x}(s)-(vw^{\prime})_{x}(s)]ds

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{t}e^{A(t-s)}(I-\Delta)^{-1}[(w(s)[w_{x}-w^{\prime}_{x}](s)+(w-w^{\prime})(s)w_{x}^{\prime}(s))+(v[w-w^{\prime}])_{x}(s)]ds.

Thus, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|\Gamma(w)(t)-\Gamma(w^{\prime})(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}(0,1)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{A}\bigg[\bigg(\int_{0}^{t}\|w(s)[w_{x}-w^{\prime}_{x}](s)\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{-1}(0,1)}ds\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\bigg(\int_{0}^{t}\|w^{\prime}_{x}(s)[w-w^{\prime}](s)\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{-1}(0,1)}ds\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad+\bigg(\int_{0}^{t}\|(v[w-w^{\prime}])_{x}(s)\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{-1}(0,1)}ds\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\bigg].

From Lemma
3.11
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\|w(s)[w_{x}-w^{\prime}_{x}](s)\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{-1}(0,1)}ds

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime\prime}\|w\|^{2}_{Y^{1}_{0,T}}\|w-w^{\prime}\|^{2}_{Y^{1}_{0,T}}

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\|w^{\prime}_{x}(s)[w-w^{\prime}](s)\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{-1}(0,1)}ds

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime\prime}\|w^{\prime}\|^{2}_{Y^{1}_{0,T}}\|w-w^{\prime}\|^{2}_{Y^{1}_{0,T}}

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\|(v[w-w^{\prime}])_{x}(s)\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{-1}(0,1)}ds

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime\prime}\|v\|_{Y^{1}_{0,T}}^{2}\|w-w^{\prime}\|^{2}_{Y^{1}_{0,T}}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|\Gamma(w)(t)-\Gamma(w^{\prime})(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}(0,1)}\leq C_{A}^{(4)}(\|w\|_{Y^{1}_{0,T}}+\|w^{\prime}\|_{Y^{1}_{0,T}}+\|v\|_{Y^{1}_{0,T}})\|w-w^{\prime}\|_{Y^{1}_{0,T}}

and similarly,

\displaystyle\bigg(\int_{0}^{T}\|\Gamma(w)(t)-\Gamma(w^{\prime})(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}(0,1)}^{2}dt\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{A}^{(5)}(\|w\|_{Y^{1}_{0,T}}+\|w^{\prime}\|_{Y^{1}_{0,T}}+\|v\|_{Y^{1}_{0,T}})\|w-w^{\prime}\|_{Y^{1}_{0,T}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{A}^{(5)}(2M^{\prime}+\frac{M^{\prime}}{K})\|w-w^{\prime}\|_{Y^{1}_{0,T}}.

We can apply Banach Fixed Point Theorem as long as
C_{A}^{(5)}(2M^{\prime}+\frac{M^{\prime}}{K})<1
as well, that is
M^{\prime}<\frac{1}{C_{A}^{(5)}}\frac{K}{K+2}.
That is, we can take
K=1
, and if

\big(\|\phi\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}(0,1)}^{2}+\|f\|_{L^{2}([0,T];\mathcal{H}^{-1}(0,1))}^{2}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}<\min\{\frac{1}{2C^{\prime\prime\prime}_{A}},\frac{1}{2C^{(5)}_{A}}\},

we have a
w\in Y^{1}_{0,T,M^{\prime}}
such that
\Gamma(w)=w
and

\|w\|_{Y^{1}_{0,T}}\leq(\|\phi\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}(0,1)}^{2}+\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}([0,T];\mathcal{H}^{-1}(0,1))})^{\frac{1}{2}}.

The proof of this proposition is now complete.
∎

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
\phi\in\mathcal{H}^{1}(0,1)

\|w_{t}\|_{Y^{2}_{0,T}}\leq C^{\prime\prime}(\|\phi\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{2}(0,1)}+\|f\|_{L^{2}([0,T];L^{2}(0,1))}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}

\phi\in\mathcal{H}^{2}(0,1)
,

Proof.

With loss of generality such as
\ell=2
, we only prove the estimate of
\ell=1
.

Since

w_{t}(t)=-(I-\Delta)^{-1}[w(t)w_{x}(t)+(v(t)w(t))_{x}+v(t)v_{x}(t)]+Aw(t),

\displaystyle\|w_{t}(t)\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{1}(0,1)}

\displaystyle\leq\|-(I-\Delta)^{-1}[w(t)w_{x}(t)+(v(t)w(t))_{x}+v(t)v_{x}(t)]+Aw(t)\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{1}(0,1)}

\displaystyle\leq 16\bigg(\|w(t)w_{x}(t)\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{-1}}+\|(v(t)w(t))_{x}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{-1}(0,1)}+\|v(t)v_{x}(t)\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{-1}(0,1)}+\|w(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}(0,1)}^{2}\bigg).

Note that using
\|uv\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}}\leq C\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s_{1}}}\|v\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s_{2}}}
provided that

and

s_{1}=1

\|w(t)w_{x}(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{-1}(0,1)}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{\pi}\|w(t)w_{x}(t)\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}\leq C\|w(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}(0,1)}^{2}\|w_{x}(t)\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}

and similar estimates are true for
\|(v(t)w(t))_{x}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{-1}(0,1)}
and
\|v(t)v_{x}(t)\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{-1}(0,1)}
. Therefore, we can conclude that

\displaystyle\|w_{t}\|^{2}_{Y^{1}_{0,T}}\leq C^{\prime}(\|w\|^{4}_{Y^{1}_{0,T}}+\|v\|^{2}_{Y^{1}_{0,T}}\|w\|^{2}_{Y^{1}_{0,T}}+\|v\|^{4}_{Y^{1}_{0,T}})+16\|w\|_{Y^{1}_{0,T}}^{2}

(4.3)

and thus (since we assumed
\|\phi\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{1}(0,1)}+\|f\|_{L^{2}([0,T];\mathcal{H}^{-1}(0,1))}^{2}<1

\|w_{t}\|_{Y^{1}_{0,T}}\leq C^{\prime}(\|\phi\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{1}(0,1)}+\|f\|_{L^{2}([0,T];\mathcal{H}^{-1}(0,1))}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}.

∎

Proposition 4.4
.

If

Proposition 4.6
.

\displaystyle\Gamma(q)(t):=e^{A(t-\tau)}w(\tau)-\int_{\tau}^{t}e^{A(t-s)}(I-\Delta)^{-1}(qq_{x}+(qv)_{x}+vv_{x})(s)ds

Consider the sapce
Y^{1}_{\tau,T,M^{\prime}}:=\{q\in Y^{1}_{\tau,T}:\|q\|_{Y^{1}_{\tau,T}}\leq M^{\prime}\}

Proposition 4.7
.

Assume that
\sup_{t>0}\|v\|_{Y^{i}_{t,T}}^{2}
(

) is sufficiently small. Then,

\|w\|_{Y^{i}_{\tau,T}}+\|w_{t}\|_{Y^{i}_{\tau,T}}\leq C^{\prime}\sup_{t>0}\|v\|_{Y^{i}_{t,T}}^{2}

.

Proof.

Consider the equation (
4.2
) on
[\tau,t+\tau]
(
\tau,t>0
) is

Theorem 6.6
.

Let
\ell_{F,\Phi,G}<2
and
\ell\in[1,2]\cap(\ell_{F,\Phi,G},2]
. For
T>0
, if
(\|\phi\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\ell}(0,1)}^{2}+\|f\|_{L^{2}([0,T];\mathcal{H}^{\ell-2}(0,1))}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}
is small enough and
N_{\min}:=\min\{N_{F},N_{\Phi},N_{G}\}\geq 3
, there exists
T>0
and a unique
w\in Y^{\ell}_{0,T}
to the equation (
6.2
) such that

\|w\|_{Y^{\ell}_{0,T}}\leq(\|\phi\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\ell}(0,1)}^{2}+\|f\|_{L^{2}([0,T];\mathcal{H}^{\ell-2}(0,1))}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}<1

and

\|w_{t}\|_{Y^{\ell}_{0,T}}\leq C(\|\phi\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\ell}(0,1)}^{2}+\|f\|_{L^{2}([0,T];\mathcal{H}^{\ell-2}(0,1))}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}<1

Proof.

Let

\Gamma(w)(t):=\int_{0}^{t}e^{A(t-s)}(I-\Delta)^{-1}\bigg[[-F(v+w)]_{x}(s)+[\Phi(v_{x}+w_{x})]_{x}(s)+(I-\Delta)[G(v+w)](s)\bigg]ds

and

Y^{\ell}_{0,T,M^{\prime}}:=\{w\in Y^{\ell}_{0,T}:\|w\|_{Y^{\ell}_{0,T}}\leq M^{\prime}\}
with
M^{\prime}=K(\|\phi\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\ell}(0,1)}^{2}+\|f\|_{L^{2}([0,T];\mathcal{H}^{\ell-2}(0,1))}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}
. We will choose
T
,
K
, and
M^{\prime}
later so that Banach contraction mapping can be applied. But we will assume that
M^{\prime}<1
.

Let
w\in Y^{\ell}_{0,T,M^{\prime}}
. We will consider term by term.

We first focus on the first term. Note that

\displaystyle\bigg\|\int_{0}^{t}e^{A(t-s)}(I-\Delta)^{-1}[-F(v+w)]_{x}(s)ds\bigg\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\ell}(0,1)}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}e^{-c(t-s)}\|[F(v+w)]_{x}(s)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\ell-2}(0,1)}ds

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}e^{-c(t-s)}\|F(v(s)+w(s))\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\ell-1}(0,1)}ds

\displaystyle\leq C_{F}2^{N_{F}}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-c(t-s)}(\|v(s)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\ell}(0,1)}^{N_{F}}+\|w(s)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\ell}(0,1)}^{N_{F}})ds.

Then, using the fact that
\|v(s)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\ell}(0,1)}\leq\|v\|_{Y^{\ell}_{0,T}}\leq C^{\prime}(\|\phi\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\ell}(0,1)}^{2}+\|f\|_{L^{2}([0,T];\mathcal{H}^{\ell-2}(0,1))}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}
from Theorem
4.1
and

\|w(s)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\ell}(0,1)}\leq\|w\|_{Y^{\ell}_{0,T}}\leq M^{\prime}
for all
s\in[0,T]
, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\bigg\|\int_{0}^{t}e^{A(t-s)}(I-\Delta)^{-1}[-F(v+w)]_{x}(s)ds\bigg\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\ell}(0,1)}\leq\frac{2^{N_{F}}C_{F}(C^{\prime})^{N_{F}}}{K^{N_{F}}c}(M^{\prime})^{N_{F}}+\frac{2^{N_{F}}C_{F}}{c}(M^{\prime})^{N_{F}}

and

\displaystyle\bigg(\int_{0}^{T}\bigg\|\int_{0}^{t}e^{A(t-s)}(I-\Delta)^{-1}[-F(v+w)]_{x}(s)ds\bigg\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\ell}(0,1)}^{2}dt\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2^{N_{F}}C_{F}(C^{\prime})^{N_{F}}}{K^{N_{F}}\sqrt{c}}(M^{\prime})^{N_{F}}+\frac{2^{N_{F}}C_{F}}{\sqrt{2c}}(M^{\prime})^{N_{F}}.

We now consider the second term. Note that

\displaystyle\bigg\|\int_{0}^{t}e^{A(t-s)}(I-\Delta)^{-1}[\Phi(v_{x}+w_{x})]_{x}(s)ds\bigg\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\ell}(0,1)}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}e^{-c(t-s)}\|[\Phi(v_{x}+w_{x})]_{x}(s)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\ell-2}(0,1)}ds

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}e^{-c(t-s)}\|\Phi(v_{x}(s)+w_{x}(s))\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\ell-1}(0,1)}ds

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}e^{-c(t-s)}C_{\Phi}(2^{N_{\Phi}}\|v_{x}(s)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\ell-1}(0,1)}^{N_{\Phi}}+2^{N_{\Phi}}\|w_{x}(s)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\ell-1}(0,1)}^{N_{\Phi}})ds

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}e^{-c(t-s)}C_{\Phi}(2^{N_{\Phi}}\|v(s)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\ell}(0,1)}^{N_{\Phi}}+2^{N_{\Phi}}\|w(s)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\ell}(0,1)}^{N_{\Phi}})ds.

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\bigg\|\int_{0}^{t}e^{A(t-s)}(I-\Delta)^{-1}[\Phi(v_{x}+w_{x})]_{x}(s)ds\bigg\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\ell}(0,1)}\leq\frac{2^{N_{\Phi}}C_{\Phi}(C^{\prime})^{N_{\Phi}}}{K^{N_{\Phi}}c}(M^{\prime})^{N_{\Phi}}+\frac{2^{N_{\Phi}}C_{\Phi}}{c}(M^{\prime})^{N_{\Phi}}

and

\displaystyle\bigg(\int_{0}^{T}\bigg\|\int_{0}^{t}e^{A(t-s)}(I-\Delta)^{-1}[\Phi(v_{x}+w_{x})]_{x}(s)ds\bigg\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\ell}(0,1)}^{2}dt\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2^{N_{\Phi}}C_{\Phi}(C^{\prime})^{N_{\Phi}}}{K^{N_{\Phi}}\sqrt{c}}(M^{\prime})^{N_{\Phi}}+\frac{2^{N_{\Phi}}C_{\Phi}}{\sqrt{2c}}(M^{\prime})^{N_{F}}.

The third term can be done by noticing that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}e^{A(t-s)}(I-\Delta)^{-1}[(I-\Delta)G(v(s)+w(s))]ds=\int_{0}^{t}e^{A(t-s)}G(v(s)+w(s))ds.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\bigg\|\int_{0}^{t}e^{A(t-s)}G(v(s)+w(s))ds\bigg\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\ell}(0,1)}\leq\frac{2^{N_{G}}C_{G}(C^{\prime})^{N_{G}}}{K^{N_{G}}c}(M^{\prime})^{N_{G}}+\frac{2^{N_{G}}C_{G}}{c}(M^{\prime})^{N_{G}}

and

\displaystyle\bigg(\int_{0}^{T}\bigg\|\int_{0}^{t}e^{A(t-s)}G(v(s)+w(s))ds\bigg\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\ell}(0,1)}^{2}dt\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2^{N_{G}}C_{G}(C^{\prime})^{N_{G}}}{K^{N_{G}}\sqrt{c}}(M^{\prime})^{N_{G}}+\frac{2^{N_{G}}C_{G}}{\sqrt{2c}}(M^{\prime})^{N_{G}}.

Therefore, in order to have
\|\Gamma(w)\|_{Y^{\ell}_{0,T}}\leq M^{\prime}
, we need

C_{F+\Phi+G}\bigg(\frac{(2C^{\prime})^{N_{\max}}}{K^{N_{\min}}c}+\frac{2^{N_{\max}}}{\sqrt{2c}}\bigg)(M^{\prime})^{N_{\min}}<M^{\prime}

where
C_{F+\Phi+G}:=C_{F}+C_{\Phi}+C_{G}
,
N_{\max}=\max\{N_{F},N_{\Phi},N_{G}\}
, and
N_{\min}=\min\{N_{F},N_{\Phi},N_{G}\}
assuming
M^{\prime}<1
.

Meanwhile, we also need

\|\Gamma(w_{1})-\Gamma(w_{2})\|_{Y^{\ell}_{0,T}}<\|w_{1}-w_{2}\|_{Y^{\ell}_{0,T}}

provided that
w_{1},w_{2}\in Y^{\ell}_{0,T}
.
A calculation shows

\displaystyle\Gamma(w_{1})(t)-\Gamma(w_{2})(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}e^{A(t-s)}(I-\Delta)^{-1}\bigg[[-F(v+w_{1})+F(v+w_{2})]_{x}(s)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad+[\Phi(v_{x}+(w_{1})_{x})-\Phi(v_{x}+(w_{2})_{x})]_{x}(s)+(I-\Delta)[G(v+w_{1})-G(v+w_{2})](s)\bigg]ds

Then, similar to the estimate of
\|\Gamma(w)\|_{Y^{\ell}_{0,T}}
, one has

\displaystyle\bigg\|\int_{0}^{t}e^{A(t-s)}(I-\Delta)^{-1}[F(v+w_{1})-F(v+w_{2})]_{x}(s)ds\bigg\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\ell}(0,1)}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}e^{-c(t-s)}C_{F}\|w_{1}(s)-w_{2}(s)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\ell}(0,1)}(\|(v+w_{1})(s)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\ell}(0,1)}^{N_{F}-1}+\|(v+w_{2})(s)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\ell}(0,1)}^{N_{F}-1})ds

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}e^{-c(t-s)}C_{F}\|w_{1}(s)-w_{2}(s)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\ell}(0,1)}(2^{N_{F}}\|v(s)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\ell}(0,1)}^{N_{F}-1}+2^{N_{F}-1}\|w_{1}(s)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\ell}(0,1)}^{N_{F}-1}+2^{N_{F}-1}\|w_{2}(s)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\ell}(0,1)}^{N_{F}-1})ds

Then, we can see that

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\bigg\|\int_{0}^{t}e^{A(t-s)}(I-\Delta)^{-1}[F(v+w_{1})-F(v+w_{2})]_{x}(s)ds\bigg\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\ell}(0,1)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{F}\|w_{1}-w_{2}\|_{Y^{\ell}_{0,T}}(\frac{2^{N_{F}}(C^{\prime})^{N_{F}-1}}{K^{N_{F}-1}\sqrt{c}}(M^{\prime})^{N_{F}-1}+\frac{2^{N_{F}}}{\sqrt{2c}}(M^{\prime})^{N_{F}-1})

and

\displaystyle\bigg(\int_{0}^{T}\bigg\|\int_{0}^{t}e^{A(t-s)}(I-\Delta)^{-1}[F(v+w_{1})-F(v+w_{2})]_{x}(s)ds\bigg\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\ell}(0,1)}^{2}dt\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{F}\|w_{1}-w_{2}\|_{Y^{\ell}_{0,T}}(\frac{2^{N_{F}}(C^{\prime})^{N_{F}-1}}{K^{N_{F}-1}c}(M^{\prime})^{N_{F}-1}+\frac{2^{N_{F}}}{c}(M^{\prime})^{N_{F}-1}).

By a similar argument, we have a similar upper bound for
\Phi
and
G
. Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\|\Gamma(w_{1})-\Gamma(w_{2})\|_{Y^{\ell}_{0,T}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{F+\Phi+G}\bigg(\bigg(\frac{(2C^{\prime})^{N_{\max}}}{C^{\prime}K^{N_{\min}-1}c}+\frac{2^{N_{\max}}}{\sqrt{2c}}\bigg)(M^{\prime})^{N_{\min}-1}\bigg)\|w_{1}-w_{2}\|_{Y^{\ell}_{0,T}}.

We first choose
K=1
, and then
M^{\prime}
small (and not exceeding 1) such that

Then we can see that

and is a contraction mapping. Therefore, by Banach fixed point theorem, there exists a unique

For the term

, we have

\displaystyle\|w_{t}(s)\|_{H^{\ell}(0,1)}

\displaystyle\leq\|w(s)\|_{H^{\ell-1}(0,1)}+(1+C_{F+\Phi+G})\|w(s)\|_{H^{\ell}(0,1)}+\|w(s)\|_{H^{\ell-2}(0,1)}+C_{F+\Phi+G}\|v(s)\|_{H^{\ell}(0,1)}

\displaystyle=[-[F(u)]_{x}+[\Phi(u_{x})]_{x}]+(I-\Delta)G(u)+f(t),

multiplying
-u_{xx}
we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}(\|u_{x}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}+\|u_{xx}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2})+\|u_{xx}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}-\|u_{x}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}\|u_{xx}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}

\displaystyle\leq C\|F(u)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}(0,1)}\|u_{xx}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}-\int_{0}^{1}[\Phi^{\prime}(u_{x})-G^{\prime}(u)]u_{xx}^{2}dx+C\|G^{\prime}(u)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}(0,1)}\|u_{x}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}\|u_{xx}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\frac{1}{4\varepsilon}\|G(u)\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}+\frac{1}{4\varepsilon}\|f(t)\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}+2\varepsilon\|u_{xx}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C^{\prime}}{\varepsilon}\|F(u)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}(0,1)}^{2}+\frac{C^{\prime}}{\varepsilon}\|G^{\prime}(u)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}(0,1)}^{2}\|u_{x}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}+\frac{1}{4\varepsilon}\|G(u)\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}+\frac{1}{4\varepsilon}\|f(t)\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}+(K+4\varepsilon)\|u_{xx}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}.

We write
K=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}G^{\prime}(x)-\inf_{y\in\mathbb{R}}\Phi^{\prime}(y)
.
Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}(\|u_{x}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}+\|u_{xx}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2})+\frac{(c^{\prime})^{2}}{2}\|u_{x}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}+(\frac{1}{2}-K-4\varepsilon)\|u_{xx}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i\in\{F,G^{\prime},G\}}\frac{C^{\prime}C_{i}^{2}}{\varepsilon}\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}(0,1)}^{2}(1+\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}}^{2N_{i}^{\prime}})+\frac{1}{4\varepsilon}\|f(t)\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{M,F,G^{\prime},\varepsilon}\sum_{i\in\{F,G^{\prime},G\}}[e^{-2ct}\|\phi\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}(0,1)}^{2}+C^{2}\delta^{2}][1+[e^{-2N_{i}^{\prime}ct}\|\phi\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}(0,1)}^{2}+C^{2N_{i}^{\prime}}\delta^{2N_{i}^{\prime}}]]+C_{\varepsilon}\delta^{2}.

Following the proof of Theorem
2.7
as showing the absorbing property for
\mathcal{H}^{\ell}(0,1)
, one has

\displaystyle\|u_{x}(t)\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}+\|u_{xx}(t)\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}\leq e^{-c_{\varepsilon}t}M\|\phi\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}(0,1)}^{2}+e^{-c_{\varepsilon}t}(e^{-2ct}\|\phi\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}(0,1)}^{2}+C^{2}\delta^{2})\Phi_{2}(\|\phi\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}(0,1)},\delta)+C^{\prime}\delta^{2}

provided that
\min\{\frac{1-2K-8c}{4},0\}<\varepsilon<\frac{1-2K}{4}
, where
c
is from the absorbing property for
\ell=1
.
∎

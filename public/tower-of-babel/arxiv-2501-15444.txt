Abstract
We investigate unbiased weighing matrices of weight 9 9 and provide a construction method using mutually suitable Latin squares. For n ≤ 16 n\leq 16 , we determine the maximum size among sets of mutually unbiased weighing matrices of order n n and weight 9 9 . Notably, our findings reveal that 13 13 is the smallest order where such pairs exist, and 16 16 is the first order for which a maximum class of unbiased weighing matrices is found.

A

Two Hadamard matrices

Hadamard matrices

are said to be

Determining the

Much work has been done concerning this fundamental problem (see, e.g.,
[
5
]
,
[

]
,
[
16
]
,
[
18
]
,
[
19
]
and the references given therein).
The notion of unbiased Hadamard matrices was generalized in
[

]
and
[

]

as follows:
Two weighing matrices
W_{1},W_{2}
of order
n
and weight
k
are said to be

unbiased
if
(1/\sqrt{k})W_{1}W_{2}^{T}
is also
a weighing matrix of order
n
and weight
k
.
Weighing matrices
W_{1},W_{2},\ldots,W_{f}
are said to be

mutually unbiased
if any distinct two are unbiased.
Inspired by the many applications of mutually unbiased Hadamard matrices,
we study mutually unbiased weighing matrices.

If there exists a pair of unbiased weighing matrices of order
n
and weight

,
then

[
5
]
.
Many examples of unbiased weighing matrices of weight

]

]

Let

2.2
Latin squares

A
Latin square
of side
n
on symbol set
\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
is an
n\times n

array in which
each cell contains a single symbol from the symbol set such that each symbol occurs
exactly once in each row and exactly once in each column.
Two Latin squares
L_{1},L_{2}
of side
n
on symbol set
\{1,2,\ldots,n\}

are called
orthogonal
if

L_{1}(a,b)=L_{1}(c,d)
and

L_{2}(a,b)=L_{2}(c,d)
implies
a=c
and
b=d
, where
L_{i}(a,b)
denotes
the
(a,b)
-entry of
L_{i}

(i=1,2)
.
Latin squares in which every distinct pair of Latin squares is orthogonal are called

mutually orthogonal
Latin squares.

The following proposition provides
the maximum size among sets of mutually
orthogonal Latin squares and the bounds.

Proposition 2.1

(
[
17
, Theorems 3.25, 3.28, 3.44]
)
.

Let
N(n)
be
the maximum size among sets of mutually orthogonal Latin squares of side
n
.
Then, the following hold.

(i)

N(n)\leq n-1
for every integer
n\geq 2
.

(ii)

If
n
is a prime power, then
N(n)=n-1
.

(iii)

N(12)\geq 5
.

For our construction, we need a special presentation of mutually
orthogonal Latin squares as follows:
Two Latin squares
L_{1},L_{2}
of side
n
on symbol set
\{1,2,\ldots,n\}

are called
suitable
if every superimposition
of each row of
L_{1}
on each row of
L_{2}
results in only one element of the form

(a,a)
for some
a\in\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
.
Latin squares in which every distinct pair of Latin squares is suitable are called

mutually suitable
Latin squares, see
[
16
, Lemma 2.3]
.

The following construction method of mutually unbiased weighing matrices
using mutually suitable Latin squares
is known.

Proposition 2.2

(
[
16
, Theorem 2.12]
)
.

Assume there exists a weighing matrix of order
n
and weight
k

and a set of
f
mutually suitable Latin squares of side
n
.
Then,
there exists a set of
f+1
mutually unbiased weighing matrices of order
n^{2}
and weight

.

As an example, a set of
4
mutually unbiased weighing matrices of order

2.3
Ternary self-orthogonal codes

Let
\mathbb{F}_{3}=\{0,1,2\}
denote the finite field of order
3
.
A
ternary

[n,k]

code

C
is a
k
-dimensional vector subspace
of
\mathbb{F}_{3}^{n}
.
The parameters
n,k
are called the
length
,
dimension
for
C
,
respectively.
The
weight

\wt(x)
of a vector
x
of
\mathbb{F}_{3}^{n}
is
the number of non-zero components of
x
.
A vector of
C
is called a
codeword
.
The minimum non-zero weight of all codewords in
C
is called
the
minimum weight
of
C
.
Two codes
C,C^{\prime}
are
equivalent
if there exists a
monomial matrix
P
over
\mathbb{F}_{3}
with
C^{\prime}=C\cdot P
,
where
C\cdot P=\{xP\mid x\in C\}
.

The
dual code

C^{\perp}
of a code

C
of length
n
is defined as

C^{\perp}=\{x\in\mathbb{F}_{3}^{n}\mid x\cdot y=0\text{ for all }y\in C\},

where
x\cdot y
is the standard inner product.
A code
C
is
self-orthogonal
if
C\subset C^{\perp}
, and
a code
C
is
self-dual
if
C=C^{\perp}
.
A self-orthogonal code
C
is
maximal
if
C
is
the only self-orthogonal code containing
C
.
A self-dual code is automatically maximal.
A maximal self-orthogonal code of length
n

has dimension
(n-1)/2
if
n
is odd,

n/2
if
n\equiv 0\pmod{4}
and

n/2-1
if
n\equiv 2\pmod{4}
(see
[
21
]
).

In the context of weighing matrices, we consider the elements
0,1,2
of
\mathbb{F}_{3}
as

0,1,-1\in\mathbb{Z}
, respectively, and
in the context of ternary codes, we consider the elements
0,1,-1
of
\mathbb{Z}
as

0,1,2\in\mathbb{F}_{3}
, respectively, unless otherwise specified.
For a weighing matrix
W
,
we denote by
C_{3}(W)
the ternary code generated by the rows of
W
throughout this article.

Lemma 3.1

Let

Proof.

The straightforward proof is omitted.
∎

L=(l(i,j))_{i,j=1}^{t}

is a collection of unit vectors
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
the set of Euclidean distances between any two distinct vectors has cardinality
s
.
For a set
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
define the following set:

A(X)=\{\langle x,y\rangle\mid x,y\in X,x\neq y\},

(3)

where
\langle x,y\rangle
denotes
the standard inner product.
Let
S^{n-1}
be the unit sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
We first review the following lemma, which is a slightly different version
of
[
23
, Proposition 2.3]

and connects mutually unbiased weighing matrices and some spherical three-distance sets.

Lemma 4.1

(
[
23
, Proposition 2.3]
)
.

Let
f,n,k
be positive integers such that
f\geq 2
.
The existences of the following sets are equivalent.

(i)

A set of
f
mutually unbiased weighing matrices of order
n
and weight
k
.

(ii)

A subset
X\subset S^{n-1}
with the property that

A(X)=\{\pm 1/\sqrt{k},0\}

and there exists a partition
\{X_{0},X_{1},\ldots,X_{f}\}
of
X
such that each
X_{i}
is an orthonormal basis.

According to Lemma
4.1
, upper bounds of
f
are obtained from upper bounds of
|X|
.
Also, many examples of
unbiased weighing matrices of order
n
and weight
9
are given in Section
6

for
n\leq 24
.
By Lemma
4.1
,
this provides examples of spherical three-distance sets
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}

with
A(X)=\{\pm 1/3,0\}
satisfying the above condition.

4.2
Upper bounds based on linear programming

Here, we consider a linear programming (LP) method to
obtain upper bounds for spherical three-distance sets.
We now prepare to restate
[
5
, Corollary 10]
.

The Gegenbauer polynomials
G_{k}^{n}(x)
of degree
k
with dimension parameter
n

are defined using the following recurrence relation:

\displaystyle G^{n}_{0}(x)=1,

\displaystyle G^{n}_{1}(x)=x,

\displaystyle G^{n}_{k}(x)={\frac{(2k+n-4)\,x\,G^{n}_{k-1}(x)-(k-1)G^{n}_{k-2}(x)}{k+n-3}}\text{ if }k\geq{2}.

Delsarte’s linear programming method is then formulated as follows:

Lemma 4.2

(see also
[
10
, Theorem 4.3]
)
.

Let
p_{\text{LP}}
be the parameter of LP constraints.
Let
X
be a spherical
s
-distance set in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
A(X)=\{d_{1},d_{2},\ldots,d_{s}\}
.
Then

|X|\leq\max\left\{1+\sum_{j=1}^{s}a_{j}\,\middle|\,\begin{array}[]{ll}\sum_{i=1}^{s}a_{i}G^{n}_{k}(d_{i})\geq-1\ (k=1,2,\ldots,p_{\text{LP}}),\\
a_{i}\geq 0\ (i=1,2,\ldots,s)\end{array}\right\}.

Applying Lemma
4.2
to three-distance sets obtained from mutually unbiased weighing matrices by Lemma
4.1
,
the following upper bounds of
the maximum size among sets of mutually unbiased weighing matrices
are obtained.

Proposition 4.3

(
[
5
, Corollary 10]
)
.

Assume a set of
f
mutually unbiased weighing matrices of order
n
and weight
k
exists.
Then

f\leq\frac{(n-1)(n+4)}{6}.

If
3k-(n+2)>0
, then

f\leq\frac{k(n-1)}{3k-(n+2)}.

In Table
1
,
we list the upper bounds

obtained by Proposition
4.3

for
n=10,11,\ldots,30

For orders
7,8

the first two examples of sets of mutually unbiased weighing matrices of
weight
4
achieve the second upper bound in the above proposition,
as given in
[

, respectively.
As a third example, in Section

we present sets of

mutually unbiased weighing matrices of
order

4.3
Upper bounds based on semidefinite programming

Here, we consider a semidefinite programming (SDP for short) method to
obtain other upper bounds for spherical three-distance sets.
Semidefinite programming often outperforms linear programming.
Liu and Yu
[
20
]
utilized an SDP method to derive upper bounds
for spherical three-distance sets.
We now prepare to restate
[
20
, Theorem 5]
.

Following
[
2
]
, we define the
(p_{\text{SDP}}-k+1)\times(p_{\text{SDP}}-k+1)

matrices
Y_{k}^{n}(u,v,t)
and
S_{k}^{n}(u,v,t)
, where
p_{\text{SDP}}
is the parameter of SDP matrix constraints.
For
k=0,1,\ldots,p_{\text{SDP}}
,
define the following:

\displaystyle(Y_{k}^{n}(u,v,t))_{i,j=0}^{p_{\text{SDP}}-k}

\displaystyle=u^{i}v^{j}((1-u^{2})(1-v^{2}))^{\frac{k}{2}}G_{k}^{n-1}\left(\frac{t-uv}{\sqrt{(1-u^{2})(1-v^{2})}}\right).

Define
S_{0}^{n}(u,v,t)
to be the

(p_{\text{SDP}}+1)\times(p_{\text{SDP}}+1)
all-one matrix.
For
k=1,2,\ldots,p_{\text{SDP}}
,
define the following:

\displaystyle S_{k}^{n}(u,v,t)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{6}\sum\limits_{\sigma}\sigma Y_{k}^{n}(u,v,t)&\text{ if }(u,v,t)\neq(1,1,1),\\
O&\text{ if }(u,v,t)=(1,1,1),\end{cases}

where
\sigma
runs through the group of all permutations of the variables
u,v,t

which acts on matrix coefficients in an obvious way.

Proposition 4.4

(
[
20
, Theorem 5]
)
.

Let
p_{\text{LP}},p_{\text{SDP}}
be the parameters of LP constraints, SDP matrix constraints, respectively.
If
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a spherical three-distance set with

A(X)=\{d_{1},d_{2},d_{3}\}
,
then
|X|
is bounded above by the solution of the following
SDP problem:

\displaystyle\underset{}{\text{maximize}}

\displaystyle 1+\frac{1}{3}(x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{3})

subject to

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}+{\frac{1}{3}}\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&1\end{pmatrix}(x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{3})+\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\sum_{i=4}^{13}x_{i}\succeq 0,

\displaystyle 3+x_{1}G_{k}^{n}(d_{1})+x_{2}G_{k}^{n}(d_{2})+x_{3}G_{k}^{n}(d_{3})\geq 0\ (k=1,2,\ldots,p_{\text{LP}}),

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &S_{k}^{n}(1,1,1)+x_{1}S_{k}^{n}(d_{1},d_{1},1)+x_{2}S_{k}^{n}(d_{2},d_{2},1)+x_{3}S_{k}^{n}(d_{3},d_{3},1)\\
&+x_{4}S_{k}^{n}(d_{1},d_{1},d_{1})+x_{5}S_{k}^{n}(d_{2},d_{2},d_{2})+x_{6}S_{k}^{n}(d_{3},d_{3},d_{3})\\
&+x_{7}S_{k}^{n}(d_{1},d_{1},d_{2})+x_{8}S_{k}^{n}(d_{1},d_{1},d_{3})+x_{9}S_{k}^{n}(d_{2},d_{2},d_{1})\\
&+x_{10}S_{k}^{n}(d_{2},d_{2},d_{3})+x_{11}S_{k}^{n}(d_{3},d_{3},d_{1})+x_{12}S_{k}^{n}(d_{3},d_{3},d_{2})\\
&+x_{13}S_{k}^{n}(d_{1},d_{2},d_{3})\succeq 0\ (k=0,1,\ldots,p_{\text{SDP}}),\end{aligned}

\displaystyle x_{j}\geq 0\ (j=1,2,\ldots,13),

where the sign “
\succeq 0
” stands for “is positive semidefinite”.

5
Unbiased weighing matrices and ternary codes

In this section, for a given weighing matrix
W_{1}

we give a method to
obtain essentially all weighing matrices
W_{2}
such that
W_{1},W_{2}
are unbiased
by considering the ternary code
C_{3}(W_{1})^{\perp}
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
W_{1},W_{2}
be unbiased weighing matrices of order
n
and weight
k
.
Assume
k\equiv 0\pmod{3}
.
Then, every row of
W_{2}
is presented as a
C_{3}(W_{1})^{\perp}
codeword.

Proof.

Since
W_{1},W_{2}
are unbiased weighing matrices,
all entries of
W_{1}W_{2}^{T}
are
0,\pm\sqrt{k}
.
It is trivial that
k
is a square and
k\equiv 0\pmod{3}
if and only if

k=(3m)^{2}
for some integer
m
.
Since
k=(3m)^{2}
for some integer
m
,
we have that
W_{1}W_{2}^{T}\equiv O\pmod{3}
.
If we regard rows of
W_{1},W_{2}
as vectors of
\mathbb{F}_{3}^{n}
,
then every row of
W_{2}
is orthogonal to all rows of
W_{1}
.
This implies
that every row of
W_{2}
is presented as a codeword of
C_{3}(W_{1})^{\perp}
.
The result follows.
∎

Remark 5.2
.

(i)

It follows from the above proof that
C_{3}(W_{2})\subset C_{3}(W_{1})^{\perp}
.

(ii)

Since
W_{1}W_{1}^{T}\equiv W_{2}W_{2}^{T}\equiv O\pmod{3}
,

C_{3}(W_{1}),C_{3}(W_{2})
are self-orthogonal.

(iii)

For the case
n=k
(unbiased Hadamard matrices),
a similar observation is given in
[
12
]
.

Assume
k\equiv 0\pmod{3}
.
For a given weighing matrix
W_{1}
of order
n
and order
k
,
Proposition
5.1
claims that
all weighing matrices
W_{2}
such that
W_{1},W_{2}
are unbiased,
are obtained as codewords of weight
k
in
C_{3}(W_{1})^{\perp}

by changing rows and/or negating rows.

We describe how to find all weighing matrices
W_{2}
of order
n
and weight
k

such that
W_{1},W_{2}
are unbiased,
formed by codewords of weight
k
in
C_{3}(W_{1})^{\perp}
.
A classification method of weighing matrices based on a classification
of maximal self-orthogonal codes is given in
[
14
]
.
Our method modifies that given in
[
14
]
.
We define the following set:

S_{k}(C_{3}(W_{1})^{\perp})=\{x=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n})\in C_{3}(W_{1})^{\perp}\mid\wt(x)=k,x_{i(x)}=1\},

where

i(x)=\min\{i\in\{1,2,\ldots,n\}\mid x_{i}\neq 0\}.

By rescaling the rows, any weighing matrix equals a weighing matrix
such that the first nonzero entry of each row is
1
.
This implies that considering only codewords of the form
x=(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n})

with
x_{i(x)}=1
suffices.
For a vector
x=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n})
of
\mathbb{F}_{3}^{n}
, we define the following:

\overline{x}=(\overline{x_{1}},\overline{x_{2}},\ldots,\overline{x_{n}})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n},

where

\overline{0},\overline{1},\overline{2}
are
0,1,-1\in\mathbb{Z}
, respectively.
Set
\overline{S_{k}(C_{3}(W_{1})^{\perp})}=\{\overline{x}\mid x\in S_{k}(C_{3}(W_{1})^{\perp})\}
.
A graph
\Gamma_{k}(C_{3}(W_{1})^{\perp})
is constructed as follows:

(i)

The vertex set is

\{\overline{x}\in\overline{S_{k}(C_{3}(W_{1})^{\perp})}\mid\langle\overline{x},r_{i}\rangle\in\{0,\pm\sqrt{k}\}\ (i=1,2,\ldots,n)\}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{n},

where
r_{i}
denotes the
i
-th row of
W_{1}
.

(ii)

Two vertices
\overline{x}
and
\overline{y}
are adjacent
if
\langle\overline{x},\overline{y}\rangle=0
.

Clearly, an
n
-clique in
\Gamma_{k}(C_{3}(W_{1})^{\perp})
gives a weighing matrix

W_{2}
of order
n

and weight
k
such that
W_{1},W_{2}
are unbiased.
All weighing matrices
W_{2}

formed by codewords of
C_{3}(W_{1})^{\perp}
such that
W_{1},W_{2}
are unbiased,
are obtained
by finding all
n
-cliques in
\Gamma_{k}(C_{3}(W_{1})^{\perp})
.
By Proposition
5.1
,
one can obtain all weighing matrices
W_{2}
such that
W_{1},W_{2}
are unbiased
by changing rows and/or negating rows.
In addition, by the following method,
we determine the existence of a set of
f
mutually unbiased weighing matrices
including a weighing matrix
W_{1}
for
f\geq 3
.
Another graph
G(W_{1})
is constructed as follows:

(i)

The vertex set is

\{W_{2}\mid W_{1},W_{2}\text{ are unbiased}\}
.

(ii)

Two vertices
W_{2},W_{2}^{\prime}
are adjacent if
W_{2},W_{2}^{\prime}
are unbiased.

Clearly, an
(f-1)
-clique in
G(W_{1})
gives the existence of
a set of
f
mutually unbiased weighing matrices including
W_{1}
.

By the above method, our computer calculation investigates the existence of
unbiased weighing matrices of order
n
and weight
9
for
n\leq 24

in Section
6
.
All computer calculations in the section
were performed using programs in
Magma

[
6
]
and the language
C

except for the computer calculations to find cliques
and to test the isomorphism of association schemes.
The computer calculations for finding cliques
were performed using
Cliquer

[
22
]
.
The computer calculations for testing the isomorphism of association schemes
were performed using
AssociationSchemes

[
3
]
.

Remark 5.3
.

The result in this section may be generalized to codes over the
finite field
\mathbb{F}_{p}
of order
p
, where
p
is a prime with
p\geq 5
as follows:
Let
W_{1},W_{2}
be unbiased weighing matrices of order
n
and weight
k
.
Assume
k\equiv 0\pmod{p}

Then, every row of
W_{2}
is presented as a codeword of the dual code of
the code over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
generated by the rows of
W_{1}
.
In the study of unbiased weighing matrices of weight
25
,
codes over
\mathbb{F}_{5}
may be useful.

W_{\max}(16,9)

For orders
7,8
,
the first two examples of sets of mutually unbiased weighing matrices of
weight
4
achieve the second upper bound in Proposition
4.3
,
as given in
[
5
, Theorems 21, 22]
, respectively.
As a third
example, we presented sets of
15
mutually unbiased weighing matrices of
order
16
and weight
9
, which also achieve the bound.
From the viewpoint of spherical designs, strongly regular graphs and association schemes
(see
[
27
]
for undefined terms),
we investigate
15
mutually unbiased weighing matrices of order
16
and weight
9
.

Let
\mathcal{W}=\{W_{1},W_{2},\ldots,W_{15}\}

denote a set of
15
mutually unbiased weighing matrices of order
16
and weight
9
.
Let
Y
be the set of row vectors of

I_{16}
,
\frac{1}{3}W_{1},\frac{1}{3}W_{2},\ldots,\frac{1}{3}W_{15}
.
Since the set
\mathcal{W}

achieves the second upper bound in Proposition
4.3
,
equivalently the inequality
[
7
, (3.9)]
, by
[
7
, Proposition 3.12]
,
the graph with vertex set
Y
and edges determined by orthogonality is a strongly regular graph with parameters
(256,120,56,56)
.

Set
X=Y\cup(-Y)
. By
[
10
, Theorems 4.3 and 5.3]
,
X
is a spherical
5
-design.
Since
A(X)=\{\pm 1/3,0,-1\}
(see (
3
) for the definition of
A(X)
) and
X
is antipodal,
it follows from
[
4
, Theorem 1.1]
that the pair
X
and the binary relations on
X
determined by
the elements
A(X)
is a
4
-class bipartite
Q
-polynomial association scheme
with the following second eigenmatrix:

Q_{4}=\left(\begin{array}[]{rrrrr}1&16&135&240&120\\
1&\frac{16}{3}&7&-\frac{16}{3}&-8\\
1&0&-9&0&8\\
1&-\frac{16}{3}&7&\frac{16}{3}&-8\\
1&-16&135&-240&120\\
\end{array}\right).

Since the vertex set is decomposed into disjoint cross-polytopes, applying
[
27
, Theorem 3.2]
to this association scheme,
we have a
5
-class association scheme with the following second eigenmatrix:

Q_{5}=\left(\begin{array}[]{rrrrrrr}1&16&135&240&105&15\\
1&\frac{16}{3}&7&-\frac{16}{3}&-7&-1\\
1&0&-9&0&9&-1\\
1&-\frac{16}{3}&7&\frac{16}{3}&-7&-1\\
1&-16&135&-240&105&15\\
1&0&-9&0&-7&15\\
\end{array}\right).

Conversely, as was shown in
[
27
, Theorem 4.1]
, a set of
15
mutually
unbiased weighing matrices of order
16
and weight
9
is obtained from the
5
-class association scheme
with the above second eigenmatrix
Q_{5}
.

More mutually unbiased weighing matrices

For

(i=562,569,695)

We give some observations of the above

mutually unbiased weighing matrices.
We verified that

C_{3}(W_{16,i})=C_{3}(A_{16,i,j})\ (i=46,562,569,695,j=2,3,\ldots,15)

and
C_{3}(W_{16,i})

(i=46,562,569,695)
are equivalent to the unique ternary self-dual code
2f_{8}

and minimum weight
6
given in
[
9
, Section VI]
.
Note that the code
2f_{8}
is generated by the rows of

\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}I_{8}&H\end{array}\right)
,
where

is a Hadamard matrix of order
8
.

Let
{\mathcal{W}}_{16,i}

(i=1,2,3,4)
denote
the sets of
W_{16,j},A_{16,j,2},\ldots,A_{16,j,15}

(j=46,562,569,695)
, respectively.
For
i=1,2,3,4
,
let
\srg({\mathcal{W}}_{16,i})
denote the strongly regular graph with parameters

(256,120,56,56)
constructed from
{\mathcal{W}}_{16,i}
via the above construction.
We verified the following:

(i)

\srg({\mathcal{W}}_{16,i})

(i=1,2,4)
are isomorphic.

(ii)

\srg({\mathcal{W}}_{16,1}),\srg({\mathcal{W}}_{16,3})
are non-isomorphic.

(iii)

\srg({\mathcal{W}}_{16,1}),\srg({\mathcal{W}}_{16,3})

have automorphism groups of orders
43008,21504
, respectively.

For
d=4,5
and
i=1,2,3,4
,
let
\mathcal{X}_{d}({\mathcal{W}}_{16,i})
denote the
d
-class association scheme
with second eigenmatrix
Q_{d}

constructed from
{\mathcal{W}}_{16,i}
via the above construction.
We verified the following:

(i)

\mathcal{X}_{4}({\mathcal{W}}_{16,i})

(i=1,2,4)
are isomorphic.

(ii)

\mathcal{X}_{4}({\mathcal{W}}_{16,1}),\mathcal{X}_{4}({\mathcal{W}}_{16,3})
are non-isomorphic.

(iii)

\mathcal{X}_{4}({\mathcal{W}}_{16,1}),\mathcal{X}_{4}({\mathcal{W}}_{16,3})

have automorphism groups of orders
86016,43008
, respectively.

(iv)

\mathcal{X}_{5}({\mathcal{W}}_{16,i})

(i=1,2,3,4)
are non-isomorphic.

(v)

\mathcal{X}_{5}({\mathcal{W}}_{16,i})

(i=1,2,3,4)

have automorphism groups of orders
24,24,672,32
, respectively.

This establishes that
{\mathcal{W}}_{16,i}

(i=1,2,3,4)
are essentially different.

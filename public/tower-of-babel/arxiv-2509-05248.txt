Dynamic reconfiguration for malleable applications using RMA
Iker Martín-Álvarez, José I. Aliaga, Maribel Castillo

Abstract
This paper investigates the novel one-sided communication methods based on remote memory access (RMA) operations in MPI for dynamic resizing of malleable applications, enabling data redistribution with minimal impact on application execution. After their integration into the MaM library, these methods are compared with traditional collective-based approaches. In addition, the existing strategy Wait Drains is extended to support efficient background reconfiguration. Results show comparable performance, though high initialization costs currently limit their advantage.



1
Introduction

The exascale era of High Performance Computing (HPC) has seen a steady increase in system capabilities, driven by hardware advances -such as improved memory, storage, interconnects and processor parallelism- and new programming models, runtimes and libraries. However, studies show that efficient utilization of resources such as CPUs, GPUs, and memory remains a challenge
[
16
]
. It is common for jobs to overcommit nodes, use fault tolerance mechanisms, or underutilise resources during execution phases, wasting energy. These inefficiencies often leave nodes idle while other jobs wait, highlighting the need for a better resource management of the system.

Dynamic resource management allows jobs to vary the amount of resources allocated to them at run time, as long as the Resource Manager System (RMS) and applications can adapt to these changes. This capability has proven to be an effective strategy for improving the use of HPC infrastructures according to different specific optimization objectives. The main objectives are to maximize resource utilization
[
3
]
, increase computational
[
13
]
or energy efficiency
[
14
,
2
]
, and improve I/O performance
[
24
]
.

From an application perspective, this adaptability is known as malleability
[
7
]
, defined in this paper as the ability of a distributed parallel application to adjust its size dynamically by changing the number of MPI
[
23
]
ranks allocated to it at any point during its execution.
This flexibility improves application performance by scaling up when resources are available and releasing them when demand is high, thereby enabling optimal resource use.
Malleability is activated at specific checkpoints within the application, and requires the execution of a series of stages.

1.

Reconfiguration feasibility
: The RMS decides whether to resize the job according to a dynamic resource allocation policy. If this is not the case, the rest of the steps are not performed.

2.

Process management
. The RMS assigns a number of cores to the job, which determines how many MPI ranks are created or terminated. Processes before resizing are considered
sources
, while those that continue after resizing are considered
drains
.

3.

Data redistribution
: Transfer data from
sources
to
drains
.

4.

Resume execution
. The application resumes execution with the
drains
.

This paper proposes a new data redistribution method for distributed parallel MPI applications using the remote memory access (RMA) model given with one-sided communications, which minimize the impact on
sources
. It also expands in a synchronization strategy that optimizes background reconfigurations, which allows the application to continue running during redistribution.
Following these comments, the main contributions on this paper are the following:

•

Design of two data redistribution methods based on the RMA model.

•

Design of the synchronization strategy
Wait Drains
, in which the
sources
continue their execution until they confirm that the
drains
have finished receiving the data.

•

Evaluation of the techniques presented, comparing their performance with others presented in previous studies.

2
Background

MPI process malleability made its first steps by taking advantage of checkpoint/ restart (C/R) techniques. These techniques are based on the principle of storing the state of a job in a non-volatile memory device, in order to load it when required. In this regard, on-disk reconfiguration in malleability halts execution to resume them with a different shape. Processes are responsible for storing and loading the appropriate data, depending on the number of processes
[
6
,
8
,
9
,
15
]

Traditional C/R solutions show a low performance because of the costly disk access when writing and reading. More modern solutions rely on in-memory data redistribution. Dynamic in-memory mechanisms distribute data among processes without accessing the disk. Since data are always stored in volatile memory, it can be manipulated more quickly.

Dynamic frameworks that support in-memory data redistribution have developed different strategies to address this stage. In
[
13
]
data redistributions are categorized in three ways:

Automatic
,
Semiautomatic
, and
Manual
. Following, these categories are described together with some tools that utilize those techniques with different implementations:

•

Automatic
,
where the user data communication patterns among processes is not explicitly codified.
For example, Proteo
[
21
]
and Flex-MPI
[
17
]
provides generalist automatic data redistribution using data structure registers, while DMR API
[
11
]
leverages data dependencies.

•

Semiautomatic
defines the methods that provide data redistribution for specific scenarios. For instance, AMPI
[
10
]
is based on unique virtual memory addresses, while DMRlib
[
12
]
provides predefined redistribution patterns.

•

In the
manual
mode, users are fully responsible for defining the communication pattern among processes. Thus, coders directly implement data redistribution with point to point or collective functions.
For example, Elastic MPI
[
4
]
does not provide any data redistribution assistance.

In this paper, we build upon previous work
[
18
]
in data redistribution by introducing novel methods based on RMA. Specifically, we present two new RMA-based techniques integrated into Proteo reconfiguration module for data redistribution, which can be combined with previous techniques to enable background execution during application runtime. While prior studies have addressed data redistribution in general, to the best of the authors knowledge, redistribution in the context of malleability has not been explored in such depth apart from out previous work
[
18
]
.

3
Proteo

Proteo
is a highly configurable framework designed to facilitate the development of benchmarks aimed at analyzing the impact of integrating malleability into real-world applications. Its flexibility allows to assess the impact of malleability on the performance of applications, and compare different approaches
[
21
]
.

This framework consists of two main components: the Synthetic Application Module (SAM) and the Malleability Module (MaM). SAM emulates the behavior of MPI applications based on configurable parameters, while MaM handles dynamic reconfigurations. Together, they enable the evaluation of different malleability techniques without modifying real applications.

To facilitate the integration with real parallel applications, MaM also provides a user-friendly interface that simplifies the conversion of parallel applications into malleable ones
[
19
]
. In addition,
Proteo
has monitoring sub-modules that record performance metrics of the emulated or evaluated applications.

MaM addresses the two main stages of malleability —process management and data redistribution— by providing different methods and strategies that can be flexibly combined to optimize reconfigurations.

MaM offers various methods and strategies for process management, as detailed in
[
22
]
. All of them operate by defining an initial group of
NS

sources
and a new group of
ND

drains
, allowing processes to temporarily belong to both groups during reconfigurations. In this study, the method used is
Merge
, which spawns
(ND-NS)
new processes when
(ND>NS)
, or removes
(NS-ND)
when
(NS>ND)
. Originally proposed in
Flex-MPI

[
17
]
, this method has been adapted in MaM to eliminate the limitation that prevented the reduction of processes below the initial launch count.

One of the key features of MaM is its ability to efficiently and automatically redistribute data between
sources
and
drains
. It supports both scalar data and one-dimensional structures composed of primitive or MPI-derived types. Data can be classified into two categories:
constant
and
variable
. Constant data remains unchanged throughout execution and can be transferred using either blocking or non-blocking operations to enhance performance. Variable data changes during execution and therefore requires blocking operations.

MaM also implements various data redistribution methods
[
18
]
. This study uses the
Collective
method, specifically leveraging the
MPI_Alltoallv
operation. Additionally, MaM includes several optimization strategies: (i)
Threading
, which enables background redistribution via auxiliary threads; (ii)
Non-blocking
, employing non-blocking MPI primitives for background data transfer; and (iii)
Wait Drains
, a variant that ensures the completion of data reception through an additional synchronization condition.

4.1
RMA General Description

RMA is a one-sided communication model in MPI
[
5
]
, introduced in MPI-2, that enables one rank (
origin
) to directly read from or write to the memory of another rank (
target
) without requiring immediate synchronization. This reduces communication overhead and is particularly effective for dynamic communication patterns. RMA is built around three key components: memory windows (shared memory regions), remote operations (
Put
/
Get
), and synchronization mechanisms that control access and ensure consistency through well-defined communication
epochs
. An
epoch
defines the time interval during which an
origin
process is allowed to perform operations on the memory windows of
target
processes.
Among the available memory access models, the passive model is especially suitable for reconfigurations, as it allows access to a target’s memory without its active participation. For a more in-depth explanation of how One-Sided communications works, refer to the MPI standard
[
23
]
.

4.3
Enhancements for background reconfigurations

The previously described methods perform data redistribution using blocking one-sided communications, which can delay application progress. This section introduces two alternatives to overcome this limitation: the
Threading
and
Wait Drains
strategies provided by MaM.

In the first alternative, each
source
uses an auxiliary thread to perform data redistribution in the background using either Algorithm
2
or
3
. This allows the main thread to continue executing while periodically checking for completion.

The
Wait Drains
strategy addresses blocking issues in Algorithms
2
and
3
caused by the synchronization functions
MPI_Unlock
and
MPI_Win_free
, which stop application progress on
sources
. To avoid this blocking behavior, the algorithms are split into two parts:
Init_RMA
starts the redistribution and memory window, while
Complete_RMA
closes the communication and frees the window.

To prevent the local blocking caused by
MPI_Unlock
,
MPI_Rget
is proposed, which works as
MPI_Get
but returns a
MPI_Request
object. Using
MPI_Test
,
sources
can periodically check the communication status and continue execution if the operation is still in progress.

To avoid the blocking behavior of
MPI_Win_free
, it is necessary to detect when all processes have completed communication. MaM addresses this using the
Wait Drains
strategy with
MPI_Ibarrier
.
Sources
can then use
MPI_Test
for non-blocking status checks and continue execution if communication is still ongoing, while other processes use
MPI_Wait
to block until completion.

Figure
1
presents the flowchart of the
Complete_RMA
function when applying the
Wait Drains
strategy for background redistribution via one-sided communications. The diagram outlines three distinct execution paths based on the process role:
drain
-only,
source
-only, or acting as both
sources
and
drains
.

Processes that are only
drains
block during redistribution using
MPI_Get
,
MPI_Lock
, and
MPI_Unlock
, as they do not execute the application. After completing their reads, they call
MPI_Ibarrier
to signal completion and then block with
MPI_Wait
until all processes finish, before releasing memory windows.

Processes that are only
sources
do not perform reads, so they first call
MPI_Ibarrier
to notify others. They then enter a loop where they continue executing the application (
Compute
) while periodically checking the redistribution status with
MPI_Test(Ibarrier)
. Once complete, they release the memory windows.

Processes that are both
sources
and
drains
initiate reads and run the application while checking for completion with
MPI_Testall(Rgets)
. Once completed, they notify others via
MPI_Ibarrier
and continue execution. They will now check with
MPI_Test(Ibarrier)
if all processes have finished. When redistribution ends, they perform
Unlocks
and release memory windows.

5.3
Background redistribution

This section analyzes the behavior of asynchronous versions, focusing on methods (COL, RMA-Lock, RMA-Lockall) and their strategies (T, NB, WD).

First, the impact of running the application concurrently with background redistribution is examined by calculating
\omega
, which determines the ratio between the execution time of an iteration without reconfiguration and another with background reconfiguration.

Figure
3
illustrates how
\omega
varies with different methods and strategies, across various
sources
and
drains
combinations. Versions using auxiliary threads (T) show the highest impact, with
\omega
exceeding 100 for RMA methods and ranging from 43 to 123 for COL. This is mainly due to node oversubscription, which slows down the main thread execution.

Figure
5
provides a focused analysis of the NB and WD strategies, excluding the T variants. The results show that RMA-based methods achieve the best performance, with
\omega
values close to 1 in most cases and a maximum of 2.8 in the worst case. This efficiency is due to fewer processes involved in communication (
ND
) and the lack of synchronization requirements until
MPI_Ibarrier
completes. Among them, RMA-Lockall-WD performs slightly better than RMA-Lock-WD, as it generates fewer synchronization epochs thanks to the use of
Lock_all
. The highest
\omega
values occur when the number of
drains
is reduced to 20 or 40, likely due to communication congestion in those scenarios.

The analysis reveals that COL-NB and COL-WD perform the most iterations, with peaks—24 in the

The unusually low number of iterations in the COL-T variant is due to the application being blocked during the CG

For the RMA variants, most of the overhead comes from the collective creation of memory windows involving both

During the whole time this operation is performed, most of the data readings are also completed. Hence the number of iterations is typically one, although the final number obtained varies by version.
For the WD variants, synchronization issues with
MPI_Ibarrier
and
MPI_Test
often prevent completion within a single iteration, resulting in two iterations in most cases. Finally, T variants have additional overhead due to oversubscription, and consistently require three iterations across all configurations.

5.4

6
Conclusions

This paper extends MaM with six new data redistribution methods —two blocking and four background— based on the RMA communication model and the
Threading
or
Wait Drains
strategies. These enhancements enable background redistribution, allowing applications to continue running without active participation from
sources
.

The experiments show that combining one-sided communication methods with the
Wait Drains
strategy enables efficient background data redistribution with minimal impact on iteration cost. However, due to the high overhead of window creation, these methods do not yet outperform the collective approach, a limitation to be addressed in future work. Additionally, applying
Wait Drains
to the non-blocking collective method ensures proper synchronization but increases overall redistribution cost.

Future work will focus on reducing the cost of memory window creation, improving the
Wait Drains
strategy to avoid extra iterations, and minimizing communication volume in the
Merge
method by allowing
sources
and
drains
to retain more data locally.

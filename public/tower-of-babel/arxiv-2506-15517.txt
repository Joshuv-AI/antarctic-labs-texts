Strichartz estimates for the generalized Zakharov-Kuznetsov equation on ℝ × 𝕋 \mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T} and applications
Jakob Nowicki-Koth

Abstract
. In this article, we address the Cauchy problem associated with the k k -generalized Zakharov-Kuznetsov equation posed on ℝ × 𝕋 \mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T} . By establishing an almost optimal linear L 4 L^{4} -estimate, along with a family of bilinear refinements, we significantly lower the well-posedness threshold for all k ≥ 2 k\geq 2 . In particular, we show that the modified Zakharov-Kuznetsov equation is locally well-posed in H s ​ ( ℝ × 𝕋 ) H^{s}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}) for all s > 3 8 s>\frac{3}{8} .



1.
Introduction

For
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
, we consider the Cauchy problem

\partial_{t}u+\partial_{x}\Delta_{xy}u=\pm\partial_{x}\big(u^{k+1}\big),\qquad u(t=0)=u_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})

associated with the
k
-generalized Zakharov-Kuznetsov equation (gZK), where
u=u(t,x,y)
is a real-valued function. For
k=1
, gZK reduces to the classical Zakharov-Kuznetsov equation (ZK), which was first derived on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
by Zakharov and Kuznetsov
[
32
]
, as a model for the propagation of nonlinear ion-acoustic waves in a magnetized plasma. When posed on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, ZK describes similar phenomena, and the derivation in this case is due to Laedke and Spatschek
[
20
]
. Regarding conserved quantities, it is well known that the mass

M(u(t))\coloneqq\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}}(u(t,x,y))^{2}\mathrm{d}(x,y)

and the energy

E_{\pm}(u(t))\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}}\lvert\nabla u(t,x,y)\rvert_{2}^{2}\mp\frac{2}{k+2}(u(t,x,y))^{k+2}\mathrm{d}(x,y)

are conserved over time, provided the solution is of sufficient regularity.
Both
M
and
E_{\pm}
play a central role in the well-posedness analysis of gZK, particularly in extending local to global solutions. The well-posedness theory of gZK has been extensively studied over the past decades, both on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
and on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. For the three-dimensional case, we refer to
[
27
,
11
,
12
,
19
,
29
,
24
,
15
,
16
]
, whereas for the two-dimensional case, we refer to
[
5
,
21
,
22
,
13
,
27
,
18
,
19
,
1
,
2
,
6
,
28
]
. The semi-periodic case, however, has not yet been studied in comparable depth and has only recently come into focus. We provide an overview of the results obtained to date:

In the case
k=1
, the foundational work was carried out by Linares, Pastor, and Saut
[
23
]
, who established local well-posedness in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})
for all
s\geq\frac{3}{2}
. By developing a bilinear estimate that allows for a gain of up to
\frac{1}{2}-
derivatives, Molinet and Pilod
[
27
]
were subsequently able to prove global well-posedness for all
s\geq 1
. Based on a refinement of said estimate, Osawa
[
25
]
succeeded in lowering the threshold for local well-posedness to
s>\frac{9}{10}
, and, in joint work with Takaoka
[
26
]
, further established global existence for all
s>\frac{29}{31}
by employing the
I
-method. More recently, Cao-Labora
[
4
]
developed a further refinement of said bilinear estimate, carefully adapted to the resonance function. By invoking geometric arguments, he was then able to improve the local well-posedness result to
s>\frac{3}{4}
(or
s>\frac{1}{2}
under an additional low-frequency condition), and in both cases established optimality up to the endpoint. For the case
k\geq 2
, Farah and Molinet
[
7
]
proved the linear
L^{4}
-estimate

\lVert u\rVert_{L_{txy}^{4}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})}\lesssim\lVert u\rVert_{X_{\frac{1}{6},\frac{3}{8}}(\varphi)},

(1)

where
X_{\frac{1}{6},\frac{3}{8}}(\varphi)
denotes the Bourgain space associated with the ZK equation, and by using this estimate in conjunction with the bilinear estimate due to Molinet and Pilod, they obtained local well-posedness for every
s>1-
, explicitly establishing
s>\frac{5}{6}
in the case
k=2
. Furthermore, by making use of the conservation laws, they also proved (small data) global well-posedness for every
s\geq 1
.

The starting point of this article will be the proof of several new linear and bilinear estimates of Strichartz type. In particular, we wish to highlight the following linear
L^{4}
-estimate, which we will furthermore show to be optimal up to the loss of an
\varepsilon
-derivative.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
and
b>\frac{1}{2}
be arbitrary. If
u\in X_{\varepsilon,b}(\varphi)
, then the following estimate holds true:

\lVert u\rVert_{L_{txy}^{4}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})}\lesssim_{\varepsilon,b}\lVert u\rVert_{X_{\varepsilon,b}(\varphi)}.

(2)

For the proof of this estimate, we will follow the approach of Takaoka and Tzvetkov
[
31
]
for the semiperiodic Schrödinger equation and consider sets of the form

\{(\xi,q)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{Z}\ |\ c_{1}\leq p(\xi,q)\leq c_{2}\}

for
c_{1},c_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
and a specific polynomial
p
. The simple polynomial structure of

p
will then allow us to sharply estimate the measure of these sets, and a subsequent dyadic summation will yield the desired estimate. Moreover a modification of the proof of Theorem
1.1
will yield a family of bilinear refinements of (
2
), which in certain frequency ranges allow a gain of up to
\frac{1}{4}-
derivatives.

As a first application of the newly obtained linear estimates, we will then verify the well-posedness results summarized in the following theorem.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}^{\geq 2}
, define

s_{0}(k)\coloneqq\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2}&\ \text{if}\ k=2\\
\frac{8}{15}&\ \text{if}\ k=3\\
1-\frac{16}{9k}&\ \text{if}\ k\geq 4\end{cases},

and let
s(k)>s_{0}(k)
be arbitrary. Then for every
u_{0}\in H^{s(k)}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})
, there exists a
\delta=\delta(\lVert u_{0}\rVert_{H^{s_{0}(k)+}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})})>0
and a unique solution

u\in X_{s(k),\frac{1}{2}+}^{\delta}(\varphi)

to (
1
). Moreover, for every
\tilde{\delta}\in(0,\delta)
, there exists a neighborhood
\mathcal{U}\subseteq H^{s(k)}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})
of
u_{0}
such that the flow map

S:H^{s(k)}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})\supseteq\mathcal{U}\rightarrow X_{s(k),\frac{1}{2}+}^{\tilde{\delta}}(\varphi),\ u_{0}\mapsto S(u_{0})\coloneqq u

(data upon solution) is smooth.

Subsequently, in the case
k=2
(which corresponds to the modified Zakharov-Kuznetsov equation), we will carry out a detailed case-by-case analysis using the bilinear refinements of (
2
) and the resonance function, and push the local well-posedness threshold in this case down to
s>\frac{3}{8}
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
s>\frac{3}{8}
be arbitrary. Then for every
u_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})
, there exists a
\delta=\delta(\lVert u_{0}\rVert_{H^{\frac{3}{8}+}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})})>0
and a unique solution

u\in X_{s,\frac{1}{2}+}^{\delta}(\varphi)

to

\partial_{t}u+\partial_{x}\Delta_{xy}u=\pm\partial_{x}\big(u^{3}\big),\quad u(t=0)=u_{0}.

Moreover, for every
\tilde{\delta}\in(0,\delta)
, there exists a neighborhood
\mathcal{U}\subseteq H^{s}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})
of
u_{0}
such that the flow map

S:H^{s}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})\supseteq\mathcal{U}\rightarrow X_{s,\frac{1}{2}+}^{\tilde{\delta}}(\varphi),\ u_{0}\mapsto S(u_{0})\coloneqq u

(data upon solution) is smooth.

2.2.
Function spaces

For
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
X\in\{\mathbb{R},\mathbb{T},\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}\}
,
H^{s}(X)
will denote the usual Sobolev-space, endowed with the norm
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{H^{s}(X)}\coloneqq\lVert J^{s}\cdot\rVert_{L^{2}(X)}
, while its homogeneous version will be denoted by
\dot{H}^{s}(X)
and equipped with the norm
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\dot{H}^{s}(X)}\coloneqq\lVert I^{s}\cdot\rVert_{L^{2}(X)}
. We will often replace the underlying space
X
in the notation with variable subscripts, as in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})=H^{s}_{xy}
. Now let
T\leq 1
be a positive real number and
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
. Then, the mixed Lebesgue spaces such as
L^{p}_{tx}L^{q}_{y}
and
L_{Ty}^{p}L_{x}^{q}
will be endowed with the following norms:

\lVert f\rVert_{L_{tx}^{p}L_{y}^{q}}\coloneqq\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\lVert f(t,x,\cdot)\rVert_{L^{q}(\mathbb{T})}^{p}\mathrm{d}(t,x)\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}

and

\lVert f\rVert_{L_{Ty}^{p}L_{x}^{q}}\coloneqq\bigg(\int_{[-T,T]\times\mathbb{T}}\lVert f(t,\cdot,y)\rVert_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R})}^{p}\mathrm{d}(t,y)\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}},

with the obvious modifications in the case
p=\infty
. For later well-posedness considerations, we further introduce the semiperiodic Bourgain spaces
X_{s,b}(\varphi)
, with regularity indices
s,b\in\mathbb{R}
. We equip these with the norm

\lVert f\rVert_{X_{s,b}(\varphi)}\coloneqq\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\sum_{q\in\mathbb{Z}}\langle(\xi,q)\rangle^{2s}\langle\tau-\varphi(\xi,q)\rangle^{2b}\lvert\hat{f}(\tau,\xi,q)\rvert^{2}\mathrm{d}(\tau,\xi)\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}},

where
\varphi(\xi,q)=\xi(\xi^{2}+q^{2})
refers to the phase function defined earlier. Lastly, we define the restriction norm spaces
X_{s,b}^{\delta}(\varphi)
as the sets of all restrictions of
X_{s,b}(\varphi)

[-\delta,\delta]\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}
, where
\delta
is a positive real number. These spaces will be equipped with the norm

\lVert f\rVert_{X_{s,b}^{\delta}(\varphi)}\coloneqq\inf\Big\{\lVert\widetilde{f}\rVert_{X_{s,b}(\varphi)}\ \Big|\ \widetilde{f}|_{[-\delta,\delta]\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}}=f\ \text{and}\ \widetilde{f}\in X_{s,b}(\varphi)\Big\}.

Since the phase function
\varphi
wont vary throughout this paper, we will just write
X_{s,b}
and
X_{s,b}^{\delta}
, respectively.

3.
Linear and bilinear Strichartz estimates

This section will be devoted to establishing the linear and bilinear Strichartz estimates needed for the proofs of Theorem
1.2
and Theorem
1.3
. We begin with the proof of a slightly sharpened version of a bilinear estimate that goes back to Molinet and Pilod (see Proposition 3.6 in
[
27
]
). This estimate allows for a gain of
\frac{1}{2}-
derivatives for widely separated mixed frequencies
(\xi_{1},q_{1})
and
(\xi_{2},q_{2})
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
and
b>0
be arbitrary. Furthermore, we define the bilinear Fourier multiplier
MP(\cdot,\cdot)
by its Fourier transform

\displaystyle\widehat{MP(u,v)}(\tau,\xi,q)\coloneqq

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}\\
\ast\end{subarray}}\lvert\lvert(\xi_{1},q_{1})\rvert^{2}-\lvert(\xi_{2},q_{2})\rvert^{2}\rvert^{\frac{1}{2}}\hat{u}(\tau_{1},\xi_{1},q_{1})\hat{v}(\tau_{2},\xi_{2},q_{2})\mathrm{d}(\tau_{1},\xi_{1}),

where
\ast
indicates the convolution constraint
(\tau,\xi,q)=(\tau_{1}+\tau_{2},\xi_{1}+\xi_{2},q_{1}+q_{2})
. Then, the following estimate holds for all
J_{y}^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}u,v\in X_{0,b}
:

\lVert MP(u,v)\rVert_{L_{txy}^{2}}\lesssim_{\varepsilon,b}\lVert J_{y}^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}u\rVert_{X_{0,b}}\lVert v\rVert_{X_{0,b}}.

(3)

Proof.

We fix
\varepsilon>0
and
b\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon^{\prime}>0
. By employing Parseval’s identity, the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and Fubini’s theorem, we obtain

\displaystyle\lVert MP(u,v)\rVert_{L_{txy}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Bigg(\sup_{(\tau,\xi,q)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{Z}}c_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}\Bigg)\lVert J_{y}^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}u\rVert_{X_{0,b}}\lVert v\rVert_{X_{0,b}},

where

\displaystyle c_{0}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}\\
\ast\end{subarray}}\lvert\lvert(\xi_{1},q_{1})\rvert^{2}-\lvert(\xi_{2},q_{2})\rvert^{2}\rvert\langle q_{1}\rangle^{-1-2\varepsilon}\langle\tau_{1}-\varphi(\xi_{1},q_{1})\rangle^{-1-2\varepsilon^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\ \ \ \cdot\langle\tau_{2}-\varphi(\xi_{2},q_{2})\rangle^{-1-2\varepsilon^{\prime}}\mathrm{d}(\tau_{1},\xi_{1}),

and for the integration over
\tau_{1}
, Lemma 4.2 in
[
8
]
yields that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\langle\tau_{1}-\varphi(\xi_{1},q_{1})\rangle^{-1-2\varepsilon^{\prime}}\langle\tau-\tau_{1}-\varphi(\xi-\xi_{1},q-q_{1})\rangle^{-1-2\varepsilon^{\prime}}\mathrm{d}\tau_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\langle\tau-\varphi(\xi_{1},q_{1})-\varphi(\xi-\xi_{1},q-q_{1})\rangle^{-1-2\varepsilon^{\prime}}.

From this, it follows that

\displaystyle c_{0}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{q_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}}\langle q_{1}\rangle^{-1-2\varepsilon}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\lvert\lvert(\xi_{1},q_{1})\rvert^{2}-\lvert(\xi-\xi_{1},q-q_{1})\rvert^{2}\rvert

\displaystyle\ \ \ \cdot\langle\tau-\varphi(\xi_{1},q_{1})-\varphi(\xi-\xi_{1},q-q_{1})\rangle^{-1-2\varepsilon^{\prime}}\mathrm{d}\xi_{1},

and substituting

\tilde{\xi_{1}}=\tau-\varphi(\xi_{1},q_{1})-\varphi(\xi-\xi_{1},q-q_{1})\leadsto"\mathrm{d}\tilde{\xi_{1}}=(\lvert(\xi-\xi_{1},q-q_{1})\rvert^{2}-\lvert(\xi_{1},q_{1})\rvert^{2})\mathrm{d}\xi_{1}"

leads to

c_{0}\lesssim\sum_{q_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}}\langle q_{1}\rangle^{-1-2\varepsilon}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\langle\tilde{\xi_{1}}\rangle^{-1-2\varepsilon^{\prime}}\mathrm{d}\tilde{\xi_{1}}<\infty.

This completes the proof of (
3
).
∎

Remark 3.2
.

(i)

The proof shows that it does not matter whether
J_{y}^{\frac{1}{2}+}
is applied to

or
v
, so that the derivative loss can always be shifted to the low-frequency factor.

(ii)

By applying Sobolev’s embedding theorem and Lemma 4.2 from

]
in the form

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\langle\tau_{1}-\varphi(\xi_{1},q_{1})\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}-2\varepsilon^{\prime}}\langle\tau-\tau_{1}-\varphi(\xi-\xi_{1},q-q_{1})\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}-2\varepsilon^{\prime}}\mathrm{d}\tau_{1}\lesssim 1,

an argument analogous to that of Proposition

yields the estimate

\lVert MP(u,v)\rVert_{L_{txy}^{2}}\lesssim\lVert u\rVert_{X_{1+,\frac{1}{4}+}}\lVert v\rVert_{X_{1+,\frac{1}{4}+}}.

(4)

By bilinear interpolation between (
3
) and (
4
), we then obtain that for every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
\tilde{\varepsilon}>0
such that

\lVert MP(u,v)\rVert_{L_{txy}^{2}}\lesssim\lVert u\rVert_{X_{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon,\frac{1}{2}-\tilde{\varepsilon}}}\lVert v\rVert_{X_{0+\varepsilon,\frac{1}{2}-\tilde{\varepsilon}}},

(5)

and this will turn out to be useful for later estimates by duality.

The linear Strichartz estimates that we want to establish next are based on the observation that the linear propagator
e^{-t\partial_{x}\Delta_{xy}}
associated with gZK implicitly contains both the one-dimensional Schrödinger and Airy propagators. This becomes evident when applying partial Fourier transforms
\mathcal{F}_{x}
and
\mathcal{F}_{y}
to
e^{-t\partial_{x}\Delta_{xy}}u_{0}
. One obtains

\Big(\mathcal{F}_{x}e^{-t\partial_{x}\Delta_{xy}}u_{0}\Big)(\xi,y)=e^{it\xi^{3}}\cdot\Big(e^{i(-\xi t)\partial_{y}^{2}}\mathcal{F}_{x}u_{0}(\xi)\Big)(y)

(6)

and

\Big(\mathcal{F}_{y}e^{-t\partial_{x}\Delta_{xy}}u_{0}\Big)(x,q)=e^{(q^{2}t)\partial_{x}}\Big(e^{-t\partial_{x}^{3}}\mathcal{F}_{y}u_{0}(q)\Big)(x)=\Big(e^{-t\partial_{x}^{3}}\mathcal{F}_{y}u_{0}(q)\Big)(x+q^{2}t),

(7)

and for fixed spatial frequencies
\xi
or
q
, one can then invoke well-known estimates for the Schrödinger and Airy equations. This argument has been employed multiple times in the context of the Schrödinger propagator (see, e.g.
[
15
,
14
,
10
]
), whereas it appears to be new in the case of the Airy propagator. We make the described approach precise in the propositions that follow.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
T>0
and
\varepsilon>0
be abitrary. Furthermore, let
u_{0}
and
v_{0}
be functions with
J_{x}^{\frac{1}{4}}u_{0}\in L_{xy}^{2}
and
J_{x}^{\frac{1}{3}}J_{y}^{\varepsilon}v_{0}\in L_{xy}^{2}
. Then the two estimates

\lVert e^{-t\partial_{x}\Delta_{xy}}u_{0}\rVert_{L_{Txy}^{4}}\lesssim_{T}\lVert J_{x}^{\frac{1}{4}}u_{0}\rVert_{L_{xy}^{2}}

(8)

and

\lVert e^{-t\partial_{x}\Delta_{xy}}v_{0}\rVert_{L_{Txy}^{6}}\lesssim_{T,\varepsilon}\lVert J_{x}^{\frac{1}{3}}J_{y}^{\varepsilon}v_{0}\rVert_{L_{xy}^{2}}

(9)

hold. Moreover, if
p\in[2,6]
and
b>\frac{1}{2}
are given, then the estimate

\lVert u\rVert_{L_{Txy}^{p}}\lesssim_{T,\varepsilon,b}\lVert J_{x}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}J_{y}^{(\frac{3}{2}-\frac{3}{p})\varepsilon}u\rVert_{X_{0,(\frac{3}{2}-\frac{3}{p})b}}

(10)

holds for every time-localized
u
with
J_{x}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}J_{y}^{(\frac{3}{2}-\frac{3}{p})\varepsilon}u\in X_{0,(\frac{3}{2}-\frac{3}{p})b}
.

Proof.

We begin with the proof of (
8
). Taking (
6
) into account, the Sobolev embedding theorem for homogenous spaces, followed by Parseval’s identity in the
x
-variable and an application of Minkowski’s integral inequality, yields

\displaystyle\lVert e^{-t\partial_{x}\Delta_{xy}}u_{0}\rVert_{L_{Txy}^{4}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert I_{x}^{\frac{1}{4}}e^{-t\partial_{x}\Delta_{xy}}u_{0}\rVert_{L_{Ty}^{4}L_{x}^{2}}

\displaystyle\sim\lVert\lvert\xi\rvert^{\frac{1}{4}}e^{i(-\xi t)\partial_{y}^{2}}\mathcal{F}_{x}u_{0}\rVert_{L_{Ty}^{4}L_{\xi}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\lVert\lvert\xi\rvert^{\frac{1}{4}}e^{i(-\xi t)\partial_{y}^{2}}\mathcal{F}_{x}u_{0}\rVert_{L_{\xi}^{2}L_{Ty}^{4}}

\displaystyle=\Bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\lvert\xi\rvert^{\frac{1}{2}}\Bigg(\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{-T}^{T}\Big\lvert\Big(e^{i(-\xi t)\partial_{y}^{2}}\mathcal{F}_{x}u_{0}(\xi)\Big)(y)\Big\rvert^{4}\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}y\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{d}\xi\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Now, substituting
\tilde{t}=-\xi t
in the innermost integral, we obtain

...=\Bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Bigg(\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{-T\lvert\xi\rvert}^{T\lvert\xi\rvert}\Big\lvert\Big(e^{i\tilde{t}\partial_{y}^{2}}\mathcal{F}_{x}u_{0}(\xi)\Big)(y)\Big\rvert^{4}\mathrm{d}\tilde{t}\mathrm{d}y\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{d}\xi\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}},

and if
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
is the uniquely determined integer such that
k\pi\leq T\lvert\xi\rvert<(k+1)\pi
, then it follows from the
2\pi
-periodicity of the integrand in
\tilde{t}
that

\displaystyle...

\displaystyle\leq\Bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Bigg(\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{-(k+1)\pi}^{(k+1)\pi}\Big\lvert\Big(e^{i\tilde{t}\partial_{y}^{2}}\mathcal{F}_{x}u_{0}(\xi)\Big)(y)\Big\rvert^{4}\mathrm{d}\tilde{t}\mathrm{d}y\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{d}\xi\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}}(T\lvert\xi\rvert+1)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Bigg(\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\Big\lvert\Big(e^{i\tilde{t}\partial_{y}^{2}}\mathcal{F}_{x}u_{0}(\xi)\Big)(y)\Big\rvert^{4}\mathrm{d}\tilde{t}\mathrm{d}y\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{d}\xi\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{T}\Bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\langle\xi\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}\lVert e^{i\tilde{t}\partial_{y}^{2}}\mathcal{F}_{x}u_{0}(\xi)\rVert_{L_{\tilde{t}y}^{4}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{T})}^{2}\mathrm{d}\xi\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

For the inner
L_{\tilde{t}y}^{4}
-norm, we can now rely on the well-known Schrödinger
L^{4}
-estimate, originally due to Zygmund
[
33
]
, to obtain

\displaystyle...

\displaystyle\lesssim\Bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\langle\xi\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}\lVert\mathcal{F}_{x}u_{0}(\xi)\rVert_{L_{y}^{2}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}\mathrm{d}\xi\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\sim\lVert J_{x}^{\frac{1}{4}}u_{0}\rVert_{L_{xy}^{2}}.

This concludes the proof of (
8
).
To prove (
9
), we proceed analogously and arrive at

\displaystyle\lVert e^{-t\partial_{x}\Delta_{xy}}v_{0}\rVert_{L_{Txy}^{6}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert I_{x}^{\frac{1}{3}}e^{-t\partial_{x}\Delta_{xy}}v_{0}\rVert_{L_{Ty}^{6}L_{x}^{2}}

\displaystyle\sim\lVert\lvert\xi\rvert^{\frac{1}{3}}e^{i(-\xi t)\partial_{y}^{2}}\mathcal{F}_{x}v_{0}\rVert_{L_{Ty}^{6}L_{\xi}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\lVert\lvert\xi\rvert^{\frac{1}{3}}e^{i(-\xi t)\partial_{y}^{2}}\mathcal{F}_{x}v_{0}\rVert_{L_{\xi}^{2}L_{Ty}^{6}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\lvert\xi\rvert^{\frac{1}{3}}(T\lvert\xi\rvert+1)^{\frac{1}{3}}\Bigg(\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\Big\lvert\Big(e^{i\tilde{t}\partial_{y}^{2}}\mathcal{F}_{x}v_{0}(\xi)\Big)(y)\Big\rvert^{6}\mathrm{d}\tilde{t}\mathrm{d}y\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{3}}\mathrm{d}\xi\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{T}\Bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\langle\xi\rangle^{\frac{2}{3}}\lVert e^{i\tilde{t}\partial_{y}^{2}}\mathcal{F}_{x}v_{0}(\xi)\rVert_{L_{\tilde{t}y}^{6}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{T})}^{2}\mathrm{d}\xi\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

At this point, we can now apply the Schrödinger
L^{6}
-estimate with a loss of an
\varepsilon
-derivative in
y
(see Proposition 2.36 in
[
3
]
), and conclude that

\displaystyle...

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\Bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\langle\xi\rangle^{\frac{2}{3}}\lVert J_{y}^{\varepsilon}\mathcal{F}_{x}v_{0}(\xi)\rVert_{L_{y}^{2}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}\mathrm{d}\xi\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\sim\lVert J_{x}^{\frac{1}{3}}J_{y}^{\varepsilon}v_{0}\rVert_{L_{xy}^{2}}.

It remains to prove (
10
). The transfer principle (see Lemma 2.3 in
[
8
]
) transforms (
8
) and (
9
) for time-localized
u
and
b>\frac{1}{2}
into

\lVert u\rVert_{L_{Txy}^{4}}\lesssim_{T,b}\lVert J_{x}^{\frac{1}{4}}u\rVert_{X_{0,b}}

(11)

and

\lVert u\rVert_{L_{Txy}^{6}}\lesssim_{T,\varepsilon,b}\lVert J_{x}^{\frac{1}{3}}J_{y}^{\varepsilon}u\rVert_{X_{0,b}},

(12)

so that we obtain (
10
) by interpolating (
12
) with the trivial estimate

\lVert u\rVert_{L_{Txy}^{2}}\lesssim\lVert u\rVert_{L_{txy}^{2}}=\lVert u\rVert_{X_{0,0}}.

(13)

∎

With the "Schrödinger point of view" now addressed, we turn to deriving linear estimates that arise from the Airy perspective.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
u_{0}
and
v_{0}
be functions such that
I_{y}^{\frac{1}{3}}u_{0}\in L_{xy}^{2}
and
v_{0}\in L_{xy}^{2}
. Then the estimates

\lVert I_{x}^{\frac{1}{6}}e^{-t\partial_{x}\Delta_{xy}}u_{0}\rVert_{L_{txy}^{6}}\lesssim\lVert I_{y}^{\frac{1}{3}}u_{0}\rVert_{L_{xy}^{2}}

(14)

and

\lVert I_{x}^{\frac{1}{4}}e^{-t\partial_{x}\Delta_{xy}}v_{0}\rVert_{L_{t}^{4}L_{x}^{\infty}L_{y}^{2}}\lesssim\lVert v_{0}\rVert_{L_{xy}^{2}}

(15)

hold.
Furthermore, if
p\in[2,6]
,
b>\frac{1}{2}
, and
u
is a function with
I_{y}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}u\in X_{0,(\frac{3}{2}-\frac{3}{p})b}
, then

\lVert I_{x}^{\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{2p}}u\rVert_{L_{txy}^{p}}\lesssim_{b}\lVert I_{y}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}u\rVert_{X_{0,(\frac{3}{2}-\frac{3}{p})b}}

(16)

holds true as well.

Proof.

We begin the proof of (
14
) in a manner similar to that of (
9
), except that we apply the Sobolev embedding theorem in the
y
-variable and, following Parseval’s identity, make use of equation (
7
):

\displaystyle\lVert I_{x}^{\frac{1}{6}}e^{-t\partial_{x}\Delta_{xy}}u_{0}\rVert_{L_{txy}^{6}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert I_{y}^{\frac{1}{3}}I_{x}^{\frac{1}{6}}e^{-t\partial_{x}\Delta_{xy}}u_{0}\rVert_{L_{tx}^{6}L_{y}^{2}}

\displaystyle\sim\lVert\lvert q\rvert^{\frac{1}{3}}\Big(I_{x}^{\frac{1}{6}}e^{-t\partial_{x}^{3}}\mathcal{F}_{y}u_{0}\Big)(x+q^{2}t)\rVert_{L_{tx}^{6}L_{q}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\lVert\lvert q\rvert^{\frac{1}{3}}\Big(I_{x}^{\frac{1}{6}}e^{-t\partial_{x}^{3}}\mathcal{F}_{y}u_{0}\Big)(x+q^{2}t)\rVert_{L_{q}^{2}L_{tx}^{6}}

\displaystyle=\Bigg(\sum_{q\in\mathbb{Z}}\lvert q\rvert^{\frac{2}{3}}\Bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Big\lvert\Big(I_{x}^{\frac{1}{6}}e^{-t\partial_{x}^{3}}\mathcal{F}_{y}u_{0}(q)\Big)(x+q^{2}t)\Big\rvert^{6}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{3}}\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

By the translation invariance of the Lebesgue measure, it then follows that

\displaystyle...

\displaystyle=\Bigg(\sum_{q\in\mathbb{Z}}\lvert q\rvert^{\frac{2}{3}}\Bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Big\lvert\Big(I_{x}^{\frac{1}{6}}e^{-t\partial_{x}^{3}}\mathcal{F}_{y}u_{0}(q)\Big)(x)\Big\rvert^{6}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{3}}\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\Bigg(\sum_{q\in\mathbb{Z}}\lvert q\rvert^{\frac{2}{3}}\lVert I_{x}^{\frac{1}{6}}e^{-t\partial_{x}^{3}}\mathcal{F}_{y}(q)\rVert_{L_{tx}^{6}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R})}^{2}\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}},

and for the inner
L_{tx}^{6}
-norm, we can apply the Airy
L^{6}
-estimate proved by Kenig, Ponce, Vega (see Theorem 2.4 in
[
17
]
). This yields

\displaystyle...

\displaystyle\lesssim\Bigg(\sum_{q\in\mathbb{Z}}\lvert q\rvert^{\frac{2}{3}}\lVert\mathcal{F}_{y}u_{0}(q)\rVert_{L_{x}^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\sim\lVert I_{y}^{\frac{1}{3}}u_{0}\rVert_{L_{xy}^{2}},

and the proof is complete.
The proof of (
15
) proceeds in an entirely analogous way, except that we can dispense with the use of the Sobolev embedding theorem and, instead of the Airy
L^{6}
-estimate, we apply the endpoint Airy
L_{t}^{4}L_{x}^{\infty}
-estimate (see Theorem 2.4 in
[
17
]
). We obtain:

\displaystyle\lVert I_{x}^{\frac{1}{4}}e^{-t\partial_{x}\Delta_{xy}}v_{0}\rVert_{L_{t}^{4}L_{x}^{\infty}L_{y}^{2}}

\displaystyle\sim\lVert\Big(I_{x}^{\frac{1}{4}}e^{-t\partial_{x}^{3}}\mathcal{F}_{y}v_{0}\Big)(x+q^{2}t)\rVert_{L_{t}^{4}L_{x}^{\infty}L_{q}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\lVert\Big(I_{x}^{\frac{1}{4}}e^{-t\partial_{x}^{3}}\mathcal{F}_{y}v_{0}\Big)(x+q^{2}t)\rVert_{L_{t}^{4}L_{q}^{2}L_{x}^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq\lVert\Big(I_{x}^{\frac{1}{4}}e^{-t\partial_{x}^{3}}\mathcal{F}_{y}v_{0}\Big)(x+q^{2}t)\rVert_{L_{q}^{2}L_{t}^{4}L_{x}^{\infty}}

\displaystyle=\Bigg(\sum_{q\in\mathbb{Z}}\lVert I_{x}^{\frac{1}{4}}e^{-t\partial_{x}^{3}}\mathcal{F}_{y}v_{0}(q)\rVert_{L_{t}^{4}(\mathbb{R})L_{x}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Bigg(\sum_{q\in\mathbb{Z}}\lVert\mathcal{F}_{y}v_{0}(q)\rVert_{L_{x}^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\sim\lVert v_{0}\rVert_{L_{xy}^{2}},

and thus the proof of this partial result is also complete. Finally, the transfer principle converts estimate (
14
) for
b>\frac{1}{2}
into

\lVert I_{x}^{\frac{1}{6}}u\rVert_{L_{txy}^{6}}\lesssim_{b}\lVert I_{y}^{\frac{1}{3}}u\rVert_{X_{0,b}},

(17)

and by interpolating this with the trivial estimate (
13
), we obtain
(
16
).
∎

Remark 3.5
.

The application of the Sobolev embedding theorem in the proof of (
14
) can be avoided by starting directly in
L_{y}^{2}
. After applying the transfer principle, we thus obtain

\lVert I_{x}^{\frac{1}{6}}u\rVert_{L_{tx}^{6}L_{y}^{2}}\lesssim_{b}\lVert u\rVert_{X_{0,b}},

(18)

for every
b>\frac{1}{2}
, and by interpolating this estimate with the trivial estimate (
13
), we arrive at

\lVert I_{x}^{\frac{1}{8}}u\rVert_{L_{tx}^{4}L_{y}^{2}}\lesssim\lVert u\rVert_{X_{0,\frac{3}{8}+}}.

(19)

This estimate will later prove useful in dealing with particularly small frequency domains.

Similar to the approach taken by Molinet and Farah in the proof of their linear
L^{4}
-estimate (
1
), we can combine the one-dimensional arguments from Proposition
3.3
and Proposition
3.4
to minimize the resulting derivative loss.

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
T>0
,
0<\varepsilon\ll 1
,
b>\frac{1}{2}
and
p\in[2,6]
. Then the estimate

\lVert u\rVert_{L_{Txy}^{p}}\lesssim_{T,\varepsilon,b}\lVert u\rVert_{X_{\frac{1}{3}-\frac{2}{3p}+(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})\varepsilon,(\frac{3}{2}-\frac{3}{p})b}}

(20)

holds for every time-localized
u\in X_{\frac{1}{3}-\frac{2}{3p}+(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})\varepsilon,(\frac{3}{2}-\frac{3}{p})b}
.

Proof.

We define the Fourier projectors

P_{\big\{\lvert\xi\rvert\leq\lvert q\rvert^{\frac{2}{3}-2\varepsilon}\big\}}u\coloneqq\mathcal{F}_{xy}^{-1}\chi_{\big\{\lvert\xi\rvert\leq\lvert q\rvert^{\frac{2}{3}-2\varepsilon}\big\}}\mathcal{F}_{xy}u

and

P_{\big\{\lvert\xi\rvert>\lvert q\rvert^{\frac{2}{3}-2\varepsilon}\big\}}u\coloneqq\mathcal{F}_{xy}^{-1}\chi_{\big\{\lvert\xi\rvert>\lvert q\rvert^{\frac{2}{3}-2\varepsilon}\big\}}\mathcal{F}_{xy}u.

Then we have

\lVert u\rVert_{L_{Txy}^{p}}\leq\lVert P_{\big\{\lvert\xi\rvert\leq\lvert q\rvert^{\frac{2}{3}-2\varepsilon}\big\}}u\rVert_{L_{Txy}^{p}}+\lVert P_{\big\{\lvert\xi\rvert>\lvert q\rvert^{\frac{2}{3}-2\varepsilon}\big\}}u\rVert_{L_{Txy}^{p}},

and for the first term we use (
10
), which, taking into account

\chi_{\big\{\lvert\xi\rvert\leq\lvert q\rvert^{\frac{2}{3}-2\varepsilon}\big\}}\langle\xi\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}\langle q\rangle^{(\frac{3}{2}-\frac{3}{p})\varepsilon}\lesssim\langle q\rangle^{\frac{1}{3}-\frac{2}{3p}+(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})\varepsilon},

leads us to

\displaystyle\lVert P_{\big\{\lvert\xi\rvert\leq\lvert q\rvert^{\frac{2}{3}-2\varepsilon}\big\}}u\rVert_{L_{Txy}^{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{T,\varepsilon,b}\lVert J_{x}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}J_{y}^{(\frac{3}{2}-\frac{3}{p})\varepsilon}P_{\big\{\lvert\xi\rvert\leq\lvert q\rvert^{\frac{2}{3}-2\varepsilon}\big\}}u\rVert_{X_{0,(\frac{3}{2}-\frac{3}{p})b}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert J_{y}^{\frac{1}{3}-\frac{2}{3p}+(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})\varepsilon}u\rVert_{X_{0,(\frac{3}{2}-\frac{3}{p})b}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert u\rVert_{X_{\frac{1}{3}-\frac{2}{3p}+(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})\varepsilon,(\frac{3}{2}-\frac{3}{p})b}}.

For the second term, we apply (
16
) and note that

\chi_{\big\{\lvert\xi\rvert>\lvert q\rvert^{\frac{2}{3}-2\varepsilon}\big\}}\lvert\xi\rvert^{-(\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{2p})}\lvert q\rvert^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}\lesssim\langle q\rangle^{\frac{1}{3}-\frac{2}{3p}+(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})\varepsilon},

which again yields

\displaystyle\lVert P_{\big\{\lvert\xi\rvert>\lvert q\rvert^{\frac{2}{3}-2\varepsilon}\big\}}u\rVert_{L_{Txy}^{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{T,\varepsilon,b}\lVert I_{x}^{-(\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{2p})}I_{y}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}P_{\big\{\lvert\xi\rvert>\lvert q\rvert^{\frac{2}{3}-2\varepsilon}\big\}}u\rVert_{X_{0,(\frac{3}{2}-\frac{3}{p})b}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert J_{y}^{\frac{1}{3}-\frac{2}{3p}+(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})\varepsilon}u\rVert_{X_{0,(\frac{3}{2}-\frac{3}{p})b}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert u\rVert_{X_{\frac{1}{3}-\frac{2}{3p}+(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})\varepsilon,(\frac{3}{2}-\frac{3}{p})b}}.

Altogether, we obtain

\lVert u\rVert_{L_{Txy}^{p}}\lesssim_{T,\varepsilon,b}\lVert u\rVert_{X_{\frac{1}{3}-\frac{2}{3p}+(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})\varepsilon,(\frac{3}{2}-\frac{3}{p})b}},

which is what we aimed to show.
∎

Remark 3.7
.

(i)

In the case
p=4
,
b=\frac{1}{2}+
, Corollary
3.6
provides the estimate

\lVert u\rVert_{L_{Txy}^{4}}\lesssim\lVert u\rVert_{X_{\frac{1}{6}+,\frac{3}{8}+}},

which constitutes a slightly weakened version of estimate (
1
).

(ii)

The corollary also yields the two estimates

\lVert u\rVert_{L_{Txy}^{6}}\lesssim\lVert u\rVert_{X_{\frac{2}{9}+,\frac{1}{2}+}}

(21)

and

\lVert u\rVert_{L_{Txy}^{6-}}\lesssim\lVert u\rVert_{\frac{2}{9}+,\frac{1}{2}-},

(22)

which, in this form, will be sufficient for the remaining well-posedness considerations. However, the
L^{5}
-estimate provided by Corollary
3.6
is still not good enough - more on this will follow after the establishment of Theorem
1.1
.

While the estimates proved in Propositions
3.3
and
3.4
are derived from already known one-dimensional estimates, we adopt a two-dimensional approach for the proof of Theorem
1.1
. The following auxiliary lemma clarifies what is meant by this, and is inspired by the idea of Takaoka and Tzvetkov in their proof of a linear
L^{4}
-estimate for the semiperiodic Schrödinger equation (see Lemma 2.1 in
[
31
]
).

Lemma 3.8
.

For
\alpha\in[0,1]
,
(\tau,\xi,q)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{>0}\times\mathbb{Z}
,
K\geq 1
,
c=c(\tau,\xi,q)\in\mathbb{R}
,
h\in\{0,\frac{1}{2}\}

and dyadic numbers
N_{1},N_{2}\in 2^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
, we define the following measurable subsets of
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{Z}
:

\displaystyle B_{\tau,\xi,q}^{\mathrm{lin}}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\bigg\{(\xi_{1},q_{1})\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{Z}\ \bigg|\ \lvert\xi_{1}\rvert<\frac{\xi}{2},\ \bigg\lvert\bigg(\xi_{1}+\frac{\xi}{2},q_{1}+\frac{q}{2}+h\bigg)\bigg\rvert\lesssim N_{1},

\displaystyle\bigg\lvert\bigg(\frac{\xi}{2}-\xi_{1},\frac{q}{2}-q_{1}-h\bigg)\bigg\rvert\lesssim N_{2},\ \lvert 3\xi^{2}-q^{2}\rvert\gtrsim 1,\ p(\xi_{1},q_{1}+h)\in[c,c+K]\bigg\},

and

\displaystyle B_{\tau,\xi,q}^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\bigg\{(\xi_{1},q_{1})\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{Z}\ \bigg|\ \xi\gtrsim 1,\ \bigg\lvert\bigg(\xi_{1}+\frac{\xi}{2},q_{1}+\frac{q}{2}+h\bigg)\bigg\rvert\lesssim N_{1},

\displaystyle\bigg\lvert\bigg(\frac{\xi}{2}-\xi_{1},\frac{q}{2}-q_{1}-h\bigg)\bigg\rvert\lesssim N_{2},\ \lvert 3\xi^{2}-q^{2}\rvert\gtrsim\xi^{\alpha},\ p(\xi_{1},q_{1}+h)\in[c,c+K]\bigg\},

where
p
denotes the polynomial function
p(x,y)\coloneqq\xi(3x^{2}+y^{2})+2qxy
.
Then, the following two estimates hold for every
\varepsilon>0
:

\sup_{(\tau,\xi,q)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{>0}\times\mathbb{Z}}\lvert B_{\tau,\xi,q}^{\mathrm{lin}}\rvert^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}(N_{1}\lor N_{2})^{\varepsilon}K^{\frac{1}{2}},

(23)

and

\sup_{(\tau,\xi,q)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{>0}\times\mathbb{Z}}\xi^{\frac{\alpha}{4}}\cdot\lvert B_{\tau,\xi,q}^{\alpha}\rvert^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}(N_{1}\lor N_{2})^{\varepsilon}K^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(24)

Proof.

We start by proving (
23
). Let
\varepsilon>0
be arbitrary. For a fixed
q_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}
,
p
is an ordinary polynomial of degree two in
\xi_{1}
, so that if we define

D_{1}\coloneqq\frac{q^{2}-3\xi^{2}}{9\xi^{2}}(q_{1}+h)^{2}+\frac{c}{3\xi}

and

D_{2}\coloneqq\frac{q^{2}-3\xi^{2}}{9\xi^{2}}(q_{1}+h)^{2}+\frac{c+K}{3\xi},

a simple calculation shows that

\displaystyle p(\xi_{1},q_{1}+h)\in[c,c+K]\land\bigg(\lvert\xi_{1}\rvert<\frac{\xi}{2}\bigg)\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\xi_{1}\in\begin{cases}\Big(-\frac{(q_{1}+h)q}{3\xi}+\Big(\Big[-D_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}},-D_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big]\cup\Big[D_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}},D_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big]\Big)\Big)\cap(-\frac{\xi}{2},\frac{\xi}{2})&\text{if}\ D_{1}\geq 0\\
\Big(-\frac{(q_{1}+h)q}{3\xi}+\Big[-D_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}},D_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big]\Big)\cap(-\frac{\xi}{2},\frac{\xi}{2})&\text{if}\ D_{1}\leq 0,\ D_{2}\geq 0.\end{cases}

Thus, summing the lengths of these intervals over all admissible
q_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}
, we obtain an upper bound for
\lvert B_{\tau,\xi,q}^{\mathrm{lin}}\rvert
.
We divide this approach into four cases.

(i)
q^{2}-3\xi^{2}>0
and
c\geq 0
: We first note that from the definition of
B_{\tau,\xi,q}^{\mathrm{lin}}
, it follows that

\lvert q_{1}\rvert\leq\frac{1}{2}\Big\lvert q_{1}+\frac{q}{2}+h\Big\rvert+\frac{1}{2}\Big\lvert\frac{q}{2}-q_{1}-h\Big\rvert+\lvert h\rvert\lesssim(N_{1}\lor N_{2}).

Furthermore, the assumptions
q^{2}-3\xi^{2}>0
and
c\geq 0
imply
D_{1}\geq 0
for all
q_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}
, so that we obtain

\lvert B_{\tau,\xi,q}^{\mathrm{lin}}\rvert\lesssim\sum_{\lvert q_{1}\rvert\lesssim(N_{1}\lor N_{2})}\underbrace{\Big\lvert\Big(-\frac{(q_{1}+h)q}{3\xi}+\Big(\Big[-D_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}},-D_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big]\cup\Big[D_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}},D_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big]\Big)\Big)\cap(-\frac{\xi}{2},\frac{\xi}{2})\Big\rvert}_{\eqcolon a(q_{1})}.

From this estimate, we can immediately see that in the case
\xi\leq\frac{1}{(N_{1}\lor N_{2})}
, it follows from
a(q_{1})\leq\xi
that

\lvert B_{\tau,\xi,q}^{\mathrm{lin}}\rvert\lesssim\sum_{\lvert q_{1}\rvert\lesssim(N_{1}\lor N_{2})}a(q_{1})\leq\frac{1}{(N_{1}\lor N_{2})}\sum_{\lvert q_{1}\rvert\lesssim(N_{1}\lor N_{2})}1\lesssim 1\leq(N_{1}\lor N_{2})^{2\varepsilon}K,

so that we may restrict our further investigations to
\xi>\frac{1}{(N_{1}\lor N_{2})}
.
In addition to
a(q_{1})\leq\xi
, we also have

a(q_{1})\leq\Big\lvert\Big[D_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}},D_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big]\Big\rvert=\frac{K}{3\xi}\frac{1}{D_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}+D_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}}\leq\frac{K}{3\xi}\frac{(3\xi)^{\frac{1}{2}}}{K^{\frac{1}{2}}}=\frac{K^{\frac{1}{2}}}{(3\xi)^{\frac{1}{2}}},

which allows us to conclude that
a(q_{1})\lesssim K^{\frac{1}{2}}
. Thus, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{\lvert q_{1}\rvert\lesssim(N_{1}\lor N_{2})}a(q_{1})

\displaystyle\lesssim K^{\frac{1}{2}}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lvert q_{1}\rvert\lesssim(N_{1}\lor N_{2})\\
q_{1}\notin\{0,-1\}\end{subarray}}\Big\lvert\Big[D_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}},D_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big]\Big\rvert

\displaystyle\lesssim K^{\frac{1}{2}}+\frac{K^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\xi^{\frac{1}{2}}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lvert q_{1}\rvert\lesssim(N_{1}\lor N_{2})\\
q_{1}\notin\{0,-1\}\end{subarray}}\Big(\frac{q^{2}-3\xi^{2}}{3\xi K}(q_{1}+h)^{2}+1\Big)^{-\frac{1}{2}}

and a subsequent integral comparison yields

\displaystyle...

\displaystyle\lesssim K^{\frac{1}{2}}+\frac{K^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\xi^{\frac{1}{2}}}\int_{0}^{\tilde{c}(N_{1}\lor N_{2})}\Big(\frac{q^{2}-3\xi^{2}}{3\xi K}y^{2}+1\Big)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{d}y

Proof of Theorem

.

We fix arbitrary positive numbers

.
Since we want to establish a linear estimate and the phase function
\varphi(\xi,q)=\xi(\xi^{2}+q^{2})

We begin by noting that (

\displaystyle\lVert(P_{N_{1}}Q_{L_{1}}u)(P_{N_{2}}Q_{L_{2}}u)\rVert_{L_{txy}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\varepsilon}(N_{1}\lor N_{2})^{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}(L_{1}\land L_{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}(L_{1}\lor L_{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\lVert P_{N_{1}}Q_{L_{1}}u\rVert_{L_{txy}^{2}}\lVert P_{N_{2}}Q_{L_{2}}u\rVert_{L_{txy}^{2}}

P_{\{\lvert 3\xi^{2}-q^{2}\rvert>1\}}u\coloneqq\mathcal{F}_{xy}^{-1}\chi_{\{\lvert 3\xi^{2}-q^{2}\rvert>1\}}\mathcal{F}_{xy}u

\displaystyle\ \ \ -\tilde{\xi_{1}}(\tilde{q_{1}}+h(q))^{2},

the substitution of the new variables, together with the translation invariance of the counting measure, yields

\lvert B_{\tau,\xi,q}\rvert^{\frac{1}{2}}=\lvert B_{\tau,\xi,q}^{\mathrm{lin}}(c,K)\rvert^{\frac{1}{2}},

with

defined exactly as in Lemma

. Thus, we may now invoke the previously established estimate (

, allowing us to argue as in case (iii.3.1). We apply the Airy
L^{6}
-estimate to the factor
u_{i}
, and use the optimized
L^{6}
-estimate for the factors
u_{1},...u_{4}
. For all remaining factors, we invoke the Sobolev embedding theorem, which altogether yields the desired estimate (
37
) for every
s>1-\frac{16}{9k}
.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

The proof shows that local well-posedness for every
s>\frac{1}{2}
in the case of the modified ZK equation can be achieved through the following simple alternative: for widely separated frequencies
\lvert(\xi_{i},q_{i})\rvert\gg\lvert(\xi_{j},q_{j})\rvert
, one applies the bilinear estimate (
3
), whereas for closely spaced frequencies
\lvert(\xi_{i},q_{i})\rvert\sim\lvert(\xi_{j},q_{j})\rvert
, one relies on the linear
L^{4}
-estimate (
2
). In the next section, we will incorporate the newly developed bilinear refinements (
30
) and the resonance function in order to further lower the well-posedness threshold to
s>\frac{3}{8}
.

4.2.
Improved LWP for mZK

As previously noted, the proof of Theorem
1.3
reduces to verifiying a concrete trilinear estimate. We fix it in the following

Proposition 4.3
.

For every
s>\frac{3}{8}
, there exists a sufficiently small number
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(s)>0
such that the following estimate holds for all time-localized functions
u_{i}\in X_{s,\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}
:

\lVert\partial_{x}(u_{1}u_{2}u_{3})\rVert_{X_{s,-\frac{1}{2}+2\varepsilon}}\lesssim\lVert u_{1}\rVert_{X_{s,\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}}\lVert u_{2}\rVert_{X_{s,\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}}\lVert u_{3}\rVert_{X_{s,\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}}.

(42)

Proof.

We begin by noting once again that (
42
) is equivalent to showing

\displaystyle I_{f}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\Bigg\lvert\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\sum_{q\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q_{1},q_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}\\
\ast\end{subarray}}\xi\langle(\xi,q)\rangle^{s}\Bigg(\prod_{i=1}^{3}\hat{u}_{i}(\tau_{i},\xi_{i},q_{i})\Bigg)\overline{\hat{f}}(\tau,\xi,q)\mathrm{d}\tau_{1}\mathrm{d}\tau_{2}\mathrm{d}\xi_{1}\mathrm{d}\xi_{2}\mathrm{d}(\tau,\xi)\Bigg\rvert

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert f\rVert_{X_{0,\frac{1}{2}-2\varepsilon}}\lVert u_{1}\rVert_{X_{s,\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}}\lVert u_{2}\rVert_{X_{s,\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}}\lVert u_{3}\rVert_{X_{s,\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}}

for all
f\in X_{0,\frac{1}{2}-2\varepsilon}
with
\lVert f\rVert_{X_{0,\frac{1}{2}-2\varepsilon}}\leq 1
, and the convolution constraint being
\ast\coloneqq(\tau,\xi,q)=(\tau_{1}+\tau_{2}+\tau_{3},\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}+\xi_{3},q_{1}+q_{2}+q_{3})
. As in the proof of Proposition
4.1
, we may assume without loss of generality that
\hat{u}_{i},\hat{f}\geq 0
and
\lvert(\xi_{1},q_{1})\rvert\geq\lvert(\xi_{2},q_{2})\rvert\geq\lvert(\xi_{3},q_{3})\rvert
, where
(\xi_{i},q_{i})
denotes the frequency variable associated with the factor
u_{i}
. Furthermore, we will arrange the
\xi_{i}
for
i=1,...,3
in ascending order according to the magnitude of their absolute values, and we will use the notation
\xi_{\text{min}}
,
\xi_{\text{med}}
,
\xi_{\text{max}}
for this purpose. Lastly, we will also use
(\xi_{0},q_{0})
as an alternative notation for the frequency variable
(\xi,q)
. With these preliminary considerations in place, we now turn to a detailed case-by-case analysis.

(i)
\lvert(\xi_{1},q_{1})\rvert\lesssim 1
or
\lvert(\xi_{1},q_{1})\rvert\gg\lvert(\xi_{3},q_{3})\rvert
:

These cases have already been discussed in the proof of Proposition
4.1

s>\frac{1}{4}

(ii)
\lvert(\xi_{1},q_{1})\rvert\gg 1
,
\lvert(\xi_{1},q_{1})\rvert\sim\lvert(\xi_{2},q_{2})\rvert\sim\lvert(\xi_{3},q_{3})\rvert
:

(ii.1)
\lvert\xi_{0}\rvert\lesssim 1

In this situation, we have

\lvert\xi_{0}\rvert\langle(\xi_{0},q_{0})\rangle^{s}\lesssim\langle(\xi_{0},q_{0})\rangle^{0-}\langle(\xi_{1},q_{1})\rangle^{\frac{s}{3}+}\langle(\xi_{2},q_{2})\rangle^{\frac{s}{3}+}\langle(\xi_{3},q_{3})\rangle^{\frac{s}{3}+},

and an application of the dual version of (
26

\displaystyle\lVert\partial_{x}(u_{1}u_{2}u_{3})\rVert_{X_{s,-\frac{1}{2}+2\varepsilon}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert J^{0-}(J^{\frac{s}{3}+}u_{1}J^{\frac{s}{3}+}u_{2}J^{\frac{s}{3}+}u_{3})\rVert_{X_{0,-\frac{1}{2}+2\varepsilon}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert J^{\frac{s}{3}+}u_{1}J^{\frac{s}{3}+}u_{2}J^{\frac{s}{3}+}u_{3}\rVert_{L_{Txy}^{\frac{4}{3}+}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert J^{\frac{s}{3}+}u_{1}\rVert_{L_{Txy}^{4+}}\lVert J^{\frac{s}{3}+}u_{2}\rVert_{L_{Txy}^{4+}}\lVert J^{\frac{s}{3}+}u_{3}\rVert_{L_{Txy}^{4+}}.

By interpolating (
21

L^{4}
-estimate (
2
), and applying the resulting estimate three times, we obtain

\displaystyle...

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert u_{1}\rVert_{X_{\frac{s}{3}+,\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}}\lVert u_{2}\rVert_{X_{\frac{s}{3}+,\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}}\lVert u_{3}\rVert_{X_{\frac{s}{3}+,\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert u_{1}\rVert_{X_{s,\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}}\lVert u_{2}\rVert_{X_{s,\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}}\lVert u_{2}\rVert_{X_{s,\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}},

with the last step requiring

\frac{s}{3}+\leq s\Leftrightarrow 0+\leq s.

By choosing
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small, the proof of (
42
) is thereby established in this case for every
s>0
.

(ii.2)
\lvert\xi_{0}\rvert\gg 1
,
\lvert\lvert(\xi_{i},q_{i})\rvert^{2}-\lvert(\xi_{j},q_{j})\rvert^{2}\rvert\gtrsim\lvert\xi_{0}\rvert^{\frac{3}{2}}
, for some
i,j\in\{0,1,2,3\}
with
i\neq j
: Given that
\lvert(\xi_{1},q_{1})\rvert\sim\lvert(\xi_{2},q_{2})\rvert\sim\lvert(\xi_{3},q_{3})\rvert

If we have
\lvert\lvert(\xi_{1},q_{1})\rvert^{2}-\lvert(\xi_{2},q_{2})\rvert^{2}\rvert\gtrsim\lvert\xi_{0}\rvert^{\frac{3}{2}}
, it follows that

\displaystyle\lvert\xi_{0}\rvert\langle(\xi_{0},q_{0})\rangle^{s}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lvert\lvert(\xi_{1},q_{1})\rvert^{2}-\lvert(\xi_{2},q_{2})\rvert^{2}\rvert^{\frac{1}{2}}\langle(\xi_{1},q_{1})\rangle^{s}\langle(\xi_{2},q_{2})\rangle^{-\frac{1}{8}+}

\displaystyle\ \ \ \cdot\langle(\xi_{0},q_{0})\rangle^{0-}\langle(\xi_{3},q_{3})\rangle^{\frac{3}{8}+},

and undoing Plancherel, followed by Hölder’s inequality, leads us to

\displaystyle I_{f}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert MP(J^{s}u_{1},J^{-\frac{1}{8}+}u_{2})\rVert_{L_{txy}^{2}}\lVert J^{0-}fJ^{\frac{3}{8}+}\tilde{u}_{3}\rVert_{L_{txy}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\lVert MP(J^{s}u_{1},J^{-\frac{1}{8}+}u_{2})\rVert_{L_{txy}^{2}}\lVert J^{0-}f\rVert_{L_{txy}^{4-}}\lVert J^{\frac{3}{8}+}\tilde{u}_{3}\rVert_{L_{Txy}^{4+}}.

We now apply estimate (
3
) to the first factor; for the second factor, we use (
26
), and for the third, (
2
) interpolated with (
21
). This yields

...\lesssim\lVert u_{1}\rVert_{X_{s,\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}}\lVert u_{2}\rVert_{X_{\frac{3}{8}+,\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}}\lVert f\rVert_{X_{0,\frac{1}{2}-2\varepsilon}}\lVert u_{3}\rVert_{X_{\frac{3}{8}+,\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}},

and by choosing
\varepsilon>0
small enough, we obtain the desired estimate (

.
On the other hand, if
\lvert\lvert(\xi_{0},q_{0})\rvert^{2}-\lvert(\xi_{1},q_{1})\rvert^{2}\rvert\gtrsim\lvert\xi_{0}\rvert^{\frac{3}{2}}
, it holds that

\displaystyle\lvert\xi_{0}\rvert\langle(\xi_{0},q_{0})\rangle^{s}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lvert\lvert(\xi_{0},q_{0})\rvert^{2}-\lvert(\xi_{1},q_{1})\rvert^{2}\rvert^{\frac{1}{2}}\langle(\xi_{0},q_{0})\rangle^{0-}\langle(\xi_{1},q_{1})\rangle^{-\frac{1}{8}+}

\displaystyle\ \ \ \cdot\langle(\xi_{2},q_{2})\rangle^{\frac{s}{2}+\frac{3}{16}+}\langle(\xi_{3},q_{3})\rangle^{\frac{s}{2}+\frac{3}{16}+},

and it follows, analogously to the first case, that

I_{f}\lesssim\lVert MP(J^{0-}f,J^{-\frac{1}{8}+}\tilde{u}_{1})\rVert_{L_{txy}^{2}}\lVert J^{\frac{s}{2}+\frac{3}{16}+}u_{2}\rVert_{L_{txy}^{4}}\lVert J^{\frac{s}{2}+\frac{3}{16}+}u_{3}\rVert_{L_{txy}^{4}}.

For the first factor, we now use (
5
), and the remaining two factors can be treated using the
L^{4}
-estimate (
2
). We thus obtain

\displaystyle...

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert f\rVert_{X_{0,\frac{1}{2}-2\varepsilon}}\lVert u_{1}\rVert_{X_{\frac{3}{8}+,\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}}\lVert u_{2}\rVert_{X_{\frac{s}{2}+\frac{3}{16}+,\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}}\lVert u_{3}\rVert_{X_{\frac{s}{2}+\frac{3}{16}+,\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert f\rVert_{X_{0,\frac{1}{2}-2\varepsilon}}\lVert u_{1}\rVert_{X_{\frac{3}{8}+,\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}}\lVert u_{2}\rVert_{X_{s,\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}}\lVert u_{3}\rVert_{X_{s,\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}},

and the final step requires precisely

\frac{s}{2}+\frac{3}{16}+\leq s\Leftrightarrow\frac{3}{8}+\leq s,

so that we arrive at the desired result in this case as well, provided
\varepsilon>0
is chosen small enough.

(ii.3)
\lvert\xi_{0}\rvert\gg 1
,
\lvert\lvert(\xi_{i},q_{i})\rvert^{2}-\lvert(\xi_{j},q_{j})\rvert^{2}\rvert\ll\lvert\xi_{0}\rvert^{\frac{3}{2}}
, for all
i,j\in\{0,1,2,3\}
with
i\neq j
:

For the remainder of this proof, we may assume without loss of generality that

(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3})=(\xi_{\text{min}},\xi_{\text{med}},\xi_{\text{max}})

(ii.3.1)
\lvert\xi_{\text{med}}\rvert\ll\lvert\xi_{\text{max}}\rvert
:

In this situation, we have
\lvert\xi_{0}\rvert\lesssim\lvert\xi_{1}+\xi_{3}\rvert
,
\lvert\xi_{0}\rvert\lesssim\lvert\xi_{0}+\xi_{2}\rvert
, and
\lvert\xi_{0}\rvert\lesssim\lvert\xi_{0}+\xi_{3}\rvert
, so that we obtain

\displaystyle\lvert\xi_{0}\rvert\langle(\xi_{0},q_{0})\rangle^{s}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lvert\xi_{1}+\xi_{3}\rvert^{\frac{1}{4}}\langle(\xi_{1},q_{1})\rangle^{\frac{3}{8}+}\langle(\xi_{3},q_{3})\rangle^{\frac{3}{8}+}\langle(\xi_{0},q_{0})\rangle^{0-}\langle(\xi_{2},q_{2})\rangle^{s-}.

If we now have
\lvert 3(\xi_{1}+\xi_{3})^{2}-(q_{1}+q_{3})^{2}\rvert\gtrsim\lvert\xi_{1}+\xi_{3}\rvert

\displaystyle I_{f}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert I_{x}^{\frac{1}{4}}P^{1}(J^{\frac{3}{8}+}u_{1}J^{\frac{3}{8}+}u_{3})\rVert_{L_{txy}^{2}}\lVert J^{0-}fJ^{s-}\tilde{u}_{2}\rVert_{L_{txy}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\lVert I_{x}^{\frac{1}{4}}P^{1}(J^{\frac{3}{8}+}u_{1}J^{\frac{3}{8}+}u_{3})\rVert_{L_{txy}^{2}}\lVert J^{0-}f\rVert_{L_{txy}^{4-}}\lVert J^{s-}\tilde{u}_{2}\rVert_{L_{Txy}^{4+}},

30

2

...\lesssim\lVert u_{1}\rVert_{X_{\frac{3}{8}+,\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}}\lVert u_{3}\rVert_{X_{\frac{3}{8}+,\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}}\lVert f\rVert_{X_{0,\frac{1}{2}-2\varepsilon}}\lVert u_{2}\rVert_{X_{s,\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}},

which is the desired estimate, provided
\varepsilon>0
is chosen small enough. On the other hand, in the case
\lvert 3(\xi_{0}+\xi_{2})^{2}-(q_{0}+q_{2})^{2}\rvert\gtrsim\lvert\xi_{0}+\xi_{2}\rvert

\lvert\xi_{0}\rvert\langle(\xi_{0},q_{0})\rangle^{s}\lesssim\lvert\xi_{0}+\xi_{2}\rvert^{\frac{1}{4}}\langle(\xi_{0},q_{0})\rangle^{0-}\langle(\xi_{2},q_{2})\rangle^{\frac{3}{8}+}\langle(\xi_{1},q_{1})\rangle^{s-}\langle(\xi_{3},q_{3})\rangle^{\frac{3}{8}+},

and proceeding as before, this leads to

\displaystyle I_{f}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert I_{x}^{\frac{1}{4}}P^{1}(J^{0-}fJ^{\frac{3}{8}+}\tilde{u}_{2})\rVert_{L_{txy}^{2}}\lVert J^{s-}u_{1}J^{\frac{3}{8}+}u_{3}\rVert_{L_{txy}^{2}}

The first factor is now to be treated using the variant (
33
) of the bilinear refinement (
30

...\lesssim\lVert f\rVert_{X_{0,\frac{1}{2}-2\varepsilon}}\lVert u_{2}\rVert_{X_{\frac{3}{8}+,\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}}\lVert u_{1}\rVert_{X_{s,\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}}\lVert u_{3}\rVert_{X_{\frac{3}{8}+,\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}},

which is the desired estimate (
42

\lvert 3(\xi_{1}+\xi_{3})^{2}-(q_{1}+q_{3})^{2}\rvert\ll\lvert\xi_{1}+\xi_{3}\rvert\sim\lvert\xi_{0}\rvert

\lvert 3(\xi_{0}+\xi_{2})^{2}-(q_{0}+q_{2})^{2}\rvert\ll\lvert\xi_{0}+\xi_{2}\rvert\sim\lvert\xi_{0}\rvert.

Subtracting the two estimates yields
\lvert 3\xi_{0}\xi_{2}-q_{0}q_{2}\rvert\ll\lvert\xi_{0}\rvert
, hence

\Big\lvert 3\lvert\xi_{2}\rvert-\frac{\lvert q_{0}\rvert\lvert q_{2}\rvert}{\lvert\xi_{0}\rvert}\rvert\ll 1
, and from the two assumptions (ii.3) and (ii.3.1), it follows that
\lvert\xi_{2}\rvert\ll\lvert q_{2}\rvert
. Altogether, we thus obtain
\lvert q_{0}\rvert\ll\lvert\xi_{0}\rvert
, and taking into account once again the general assumption (ii.3) and the fact that
\lvert\xi_{0}-\xi_{3}\rvert\ll\lvert\xi_{0}\rvert
, it follows that
\lvert q_{3}\rvert\ll\lvert\xi_{3}\rvert
as well. From
\lvert q_{0}\rvert\ll\lvert\xi_{0}\rvert
and
\lvert q_{3}\rvert\ll\lvert\xi_{3}\rvert

\lvert 3(\xi_{0}+\xi_{3})^{2}-(q_{0}+q_{3})^{2}\rvert\gtrsim\lvert\xi_{0}+\xi_{3}\rvert^{2}\geq\lvert\xi_{0}+\xi_{3}\rvert
, which means that we can resort to variant (
33
) of the bilinear refinement (
30

\lvert 3(\xi_{0}+\xi_{2})^{2}-(q_{0}+q_{2})^{2}\rvert\gtrsim\lvert\xi_{0}+\xi_{2}\rvert
. This concludes the discussion of this subcase.

(ii.3.2)
\lvert\xi_{\text{min}}\rvert\ll\lvert\xi_{\text{med}}\rvert\sim\lvert\xi_{\text{max}}\rvert
:

(ii.3.2.1)
\lvert\xi_{\text{med}}+\xi_{\text{max}}\rvert\gtrsim\lvert\xi_{\text{med}}\rvert
:

Under this assumption, the estimates

\lvert\xi_{0}\rvert\lesssim\lvert\xi_{0}+\xi_{1}\rvert
,
\lvert\xi_{0}\rvert\lesssim\lvert\xi_{2}+\xi_{3}\rvert
,
\lvert\xi_{0}\rvert\lesssim\lvert\xi_{0}+\xi_{3}\rvert
, and
\lvert\xi_{0}\rvert\lesssim\lvert\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}\rvert
hold.
If
\lvert 3(\xi_{i}+\xi_{j})^{2}-(q_{i}+q_{j})^{2}\rvert\gtrsim\lvert\xi_{i}+\xi_{j}\rvert
holds for a tuple
(i,j)\in\{(0,1),(2,3),(0,3),(1,2)\}
, then we may argue exactly as in case (ii.3.1), so that variants of (
2
) and (
30
) lead to the desired multilinear estimate (
42
), valid for every

.
Consequently, we may assume

\lvert 3(\xi_{0}+\xi_{1})^{2}-(q_{0}+q_{1})^{2}\rvert\ll\lvert\xi_{0}+\xi_{1}\rvert\sim\lvert\xi_{0}\rvert,

\lvert 3(\xi_{2}+\xi_{3})^{2}-(q_{2}+q_{3})^{2}\rvert\ll\lvert\xi_{2}+\xi_{3}\rvert\sim\lvert\xi_{0}\rvert,

\lvert 3(\xi_{0}+\xi_{3})^{2}-(q_{0}+q_{3})^{2}\rvert\ll\lvert\xi_{0}+\xi_{3}\rvert\sim\lvert\xi_{0}\rvert,

and

\lvert 3(\xi_{1}+\xi_{2})^{2}-(q_{1}+q_{2})^{2}\rvert\ll\lvert\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}\rvert\sim\lvert\xi_{0}\rvert.

Subtracting the first estimate from the second, and the third from the fourth yields
\Big\lvert 3\lvert\xi_{1}\rvert-\frac{\lvert q_{0}\rvert\lvert q_{1}\rvert}{\lvert\xi_{0}\rvert}\rvert\ll 1
and
\Big\lvert 3\lvert\xi_{3}\rvert-\frac{\lvert q_{0}\rvert\lvert q_{3}\rvert}{\lvert\xi_{0}\rvert}\rvert\ll 1
, respectively. From the combination of
\lvert\xi_{1}\rvert\ll\lvert q_{1}\rvert
(note the assumptions (ii.3) and (ii.3.2)) and
\Big\lvert 3\lvert\xi_{1}\rvert-\frac{\lvert q_{0}\rvert\lvert q_{1}\rvert}{\lvert\xi_{0}\rvert}\rvert\ll 1
, it then follows that
\lvert q_{0}\rvert\ll\lvert\xi_{0}\rvert
, and the latter, together with
\Big\lvert 3\lvert\xi_{3}\rvert-\frac{\lvert q_{0}\rvert\lvert q_{3}\rvert}{\lvert\xi_{0}\rvert}\rvert\ll 1
, ultimately leads to
\lvert\xi_{3}\rvert\ll\lvert q_{3}\rvert
, which contradicts
\lvert\xi_{3}\rvert\sim\lvert\xi_{0}\rvert\sim\lvert(\xi_{0},q_{0})\rvert\sim\lvert(\xi_{3},q_{3})\rvert\geq\lvert q_{3}\rvert
. Thus, this subcase is also resolved.

(ii.3.2.2)
\lvert\xi_{\text{med}}+\xi_{\text{max}}\rvert\ll\lvert\xi_{\text{med}}\rvert

We have
\lvert\xi_{0}\rvert\lesssim\lvert\xi_{0}+\xi_{2}\rvert
and
\lvert\xi_{0}\rvert\lesssim\lvert\xi_{1}+\xi_{3}\rvert

for some

, can be handled exactly as in (ii.3.1). If in both cases we cannot rely on the bilinear refinement (
30
), then we have

\lvert 3(\xi_{0}+\xi_{2})^{2}-(q_{0}+q_{2})^{2}\rvert\ll\lvert\xi_{0}+\xi_{2}\rvert\sim\lvert\xi_{2}\rvert

and

\lvert 3(\xi_{1}+\xi_{3})^{2}-(q_{1}+q_{3})^{2}\rvert\ll\lvert\xi_{1}+\xi_{3}\rvert\sim\lvert\xi_{2}\rvert,

and substracting the two estimates yields
\Big\lvert 3\lvert\xi_{0}\rvert-\frac{\lvert q_{0}\rvert\lvert q_{2}\rvert}{\lvert\xi_{2}\rvert}\rvert\ll 1
. Moreover, since
\lvert\xi_{0}\rvert\ll\lvert q_{0}\rvert
and
\lvert\xi_{1}\rvert\ll\lvert q_{1}\rvert
also hold by (ii.3), (ii.3.2) and (ii.3.2.2), it follows further that
\lvert q_{2}\rvert\ll\lvert\xi_{2}\rvert
. Combining this with the general assumptions (ii.3) and (ii.3.2.2), we also obtain
\lvert q_{3}\rvert\ll\lvert\xi_{3}\rvert
, and thus we arrive at the combined conclusion

\lvert\xi_{0}\rvert\ll\lvert q_{0}\rvert,\ \lvert\xi_{1}\rvert\ll\lvert q_{1}\rvert,\ \lvert q_{2}\rvert\ll\lvert\xi_{2}\rvert,\ \lvert q_{3}\rvert\ll\lvert\xi_{3}\rvert.

(43)

For the further analysis, we now consider the resonance function
R
. A straightforward computation shows that

R=-6(\xi_{1}+\xi_{2})(\xi_{1}+\xi_{3})(\xi_{2}+\xi_{3})+\sum_{i=1}^{3}\xi_{i}(\lvert(\xi_{0},q_{0})\rvert^{2}-\lvert(\xi_{i},q_{i})\rvert^{2}).

(44)

If
\lvert\xi_{2}+\xi_{3}\rvert\gtrsim\lvert\xi_{0}\rvert
, then by applying the reverse triangle inequality and taking into account (ii.3),
\lvert\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}\rvert\sim\lvert\xi_{\text{max}}\rvert
, and
\lvert\xi_{1}+\xi_{3}\rvert\sim\lvert\xi_{\text{max}}\rvert
, it follows that

\lvert R\rvert\gtrsim\lvert\xi_{0}\rvert\lvert\xi_{\text{max}}\rvert^{2}.

Moreover, since

\lvert R\rvert\leq\max_{i=0}^{3}\langle\tau_{i}-\varphi(\xi_{i},q_{i})\rangle\eqqcolon\max_{i=0}^{3}\langle\sigma_{i}\rangle,

we obtain

\lvert\xi_{0}\rvert\lvert\xi_{\text{max}}\rvert^{2}\lesssim\max_{i=0}^{3}\langle\sigma_{i}\rangle,

and now we must distinguish between two cases. If we have
\max_{i=0}^{3}\langle\sigma_{i}\rangle=\langle\sigma_{0}\rangle
, it follows that

\displaystyle\lvert\xi_{0}\rvert\langle(\xi_{0},q_{0})\rangle^{s}\langle\sigma_{0}\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}+2\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\lesssim\prod_{i=1}^{3}\langle(\xi_{i},q_{i})\rangle^{\frac{s}{3}},

and undoing Plancherel, followed by Hölder’s inequality yields

\displaystyle I_{f}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert J^{\frac{s}{3}}u_{1}J^{\frac{s}{3}}u_{2}J^{\frac{s}{3}}u_{3}\rVert_{L_{txy}^{2}}\lVert f\rVert_{X_{0,\frac{1}{2}-2\varepsilon}}

\displaystyle\leq\lVert J^{\frac{s}{3}}u_{1}\rVert_{L_{Txy}^{6}}\lVert J^{\frac{s}{3}}u_{2}\rVert_{L_{Txy}^{6}}\lVert J^{\frac{s}{3}}u_{3}\rVert_{L_{Txy}^{6}}\lVert f\rVert_{X_{0,\frac{1}{2}-2\varepsilon}}.

By applying the optimized
L^{6}
-estimate (
21
) three times, we arrive at

\displaystyle...

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert u_{1}\rVert_{X_{\frac{s}{3}+\frac{2}{9}+,\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}}\lVert u_{2}\rVert_{X_{\frac{s}{3}+\frac{2}{9}+,\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}}\lVert u_{3}\rVert_{X_{\frac{s}{3}+\frac{2}{9}+,\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}}\lVert f\rVert_{X_{0,\frac{1}{2}-2\varepsilon}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert u_{1}\rVert_{X_{s,\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}}\lVert u_{2}\rVert_{X_{s,\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}}\lVert u_{3}\rVert_{X_{s,\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}}\lVert f\rVert_{X_{0,\frac{1}{2}-2\varepsilon}},

with the last step requiring

\frac{s}{3}+\frac{2}{9}+\leq s\Leftrightarrow\frac{1}{3}+\leq s.

By choosing
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small, we therefore obtain the desired estimate (
42
) for every
s>\frac{1}{3}
.
On the other hand, if
\max_{i=0}^{3}\langle\sigma_{i}\rangle\neq\langle\sigma_{0}\rangle
(WLOG,
\max_{i=0}^{3}\langle\sigma_{i}\rangle=\langle\sigma_{1}\rangle
), it follows that

\displaystyle\leq\lvert\lvert(\xi_{i},q_{i})\rvert^{2}-\lvert(\xi_{j},q_{j})\rvert^{2}\rvert+\lvert\sqrt{3}\xi_{i}+q_{i}\rvert\lvert\sqrt{3}\xi_{i}-q_{i}\rvert

\displaystyle\ \ \ +\lvert\sqrt{3}\xi_{j}+q_{j}\rvert\lvert\sqrt{3}\xi_{j}-q_{j}\rvert

\displaystyle\ll\lvert\xi_{0}\rvert^{\frac{5}{4}};

and since
q_{i}
and
q_{j}
have opposite signs for the indices under consideration, it follows that

\lvert q_{i}+q_{j}\rvert\ll\lvert\xi_{0}\rvert^{\frac{1}{4}}.

Now, setting

for dyadic numbers

, it follows that

and by redistributing derivatives, followed by an application of Hölder’s inequality, we obtain

in the case

, and

N\gg 1\end{subarray}}N^{\frac{1}{2}-2s}\lVert Q_{N}^{(\frac{1}{4})}(I_{x}^{\frac{1}{8}}J^{s}u_{i}I_{x}^{\frac{1}{8}}J^{s}u_{j})I_{x}^{\frac{1}{8}}J^{s}u_{l}\rVert_{L_{tx}^{\frac{4}{3}}L_{y}^{2}}\lVert I_{x}^{\frac{1}{8}}f\rVert_{L_{tx}^{4}L_{y}^{2}}

(i,j)\neq(0,2)
.

\displaystyle I_{f}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}N\sim N_{1}\sim N_{2}\sim N_{3}\\

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert f\rVert_{X_{0,\frac{1}{2}-2\varepsilon}}\prod_{i=1}^{3}\lVert u_{i}\rVert_{X_{s,\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}},

and the final step requires

and

as desired.
Thus, it remains to analyze what can be achieved in the case

\begin{split}\lvert 3(\xi_{1}+\xi_{3})^{2}-(q_{1}+q_{3})^{2}\rvert&\ll\lvert\xi_{1}+\xi_{3}\rvert^{\frac{1}{2}}\sim\lvert\xi_{0}\rvert^{\frac{1}{2}},\\

Taking into account the sign distribution of the

Abstract
. Let Cr ℚ ⁡ ( 2 ) \Cr_{\mathbb{Q}}(2) be the Cremona group of rank 2 2 over rational numbers. we give a classification of large finite subgroups G G of Cr ℚ ⁡ ( 2 ) \Cr_{\mathbb{Q}}(2) and give a new sharp bound smaller (but not multiplicative) than M ⁡ ( ℚ ) = 120960 = 2 7 ⋅ 3 3 ⋅ 5 ⋅ 7 M(\mathbb{Q})=120960=2^{7}\cdot 3^{3}\cdot 5\cdot 7 ; the one given in [ 22 ] . In particular, we prove that any finite subgroup G ⊂ Cr ℚ ⁡ ( 2 ) G\subset\Cr_{\mathbb{Q}}(2) has order ∣ G ∣ ≤ 432 \mid G\mid\leq 432 and Lemma 4.1 provides a group of order 432 432 . Introduction

The Cremona group
\Cr_{k}(n)
over a field
k
is the group of birational automorphisms of the projective space
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k}
, or, equivalently, the group of
k-
automorphisms of the field
k(x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{n})
of rational functions in
n
independent variables, it is also denoted by
\Bir(\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k})
.

The group
\Cr_{k}(1)
is the group of automorphisms of the projective line, and hence it is isomorphic to the projective linear group
\PGL(2,k)
. The classification of finite subgroups of
\PGL(2)
is well-known and goes back to F. Klein. In the case
n=2
and
k=\mathbb{C}
, the group
\Cr_{\mathbb{C}}(2)
is the plane Cremona group over the field of complex numbers. The classification of finite subgroups of
\Cr_{\mathbb{C}}(2)
has been studied and has a long history, going back to the
19
th century (see the references in
[
4
]
and
[
8
]
).

In this paper, we consider the plane Cremona group over the field of rational numbers, denoted by
\Cr_{\mathbb{Q}}(2)
. This group
\Cr_{\mathbb{Q}}(2)
has been studied in
[
22
]
. The main theorem in
[
22
]
gives a sharp multiplicative bound for the orders of the finite subgroups
G
of
\Cr_{\mathbb{Q}}(2)
, which is
M(\mathbb{Q})=120960=2^{7}\cdot 3^{3}\cdot 5\cdot 7
, this means that the order of any finite subgroup of
\Cr_{\mathbb{Q}}(2)
divides
2^{7}\cdot 3^{3}\cdot 5\cdot 7
.

Our goal is to give a classification of finite subgroups
G
of
\Cr_{\mathbb{Q}}(2)
and give a new sharp bound smaller (not multiplicative) than the one given in
[
22
]
. Our main theorem is the following:

Theorem 1.1
.

If
G
is a finite subgroup of
\Bir(\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{Q}})
, then
\mid G\mid\leq 432
. Moreover, there is a finite subgroup of
\Bir(\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{Q}})
of order
432
.

As already used in
[
22
]
, every finite subgroup
G\subset\Cr_{\mathbb{Q}}(2)
is conjugate, by a birational map
\phi:\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{Q}}\dashrightarrow S
, to a finite subgroup of biregular automorphisms
\phi G\phi^{-1}\subset\Aut_{\mathbb{Q}}(S)
. Moreover, one can reduce to the case where S is a Del Pezzo surface or where the group preserves a conic bundle. The degree of a Del Pezzo being between
1
and
9

2.
Preliminaries

We use the modern approach to the problem initiated in the works of Yuri Manin and the second author (see a survey of their results in
[
13
]
). Their work gives a clear understanding of the conjugacy problem via the concept of a rational
G-
surface.

We consider groups acting on a surface. A
G-
surface, denote by
(G,S)
, is a pair in which
S
is a (rational) surface and
G\subset\Aut(S)
is a group acting biregularly on
S
.

Definition 2.1
.

A smooth projective surface
S
is said to be minimal if any birational morphism from
S
to a (nonsingular) surface is an isomorphism.

We say that a birational map
\varphi:S\rightarrow S^{\prime}
is
G-
equivariant if the
G-
action on
S^{\prime}
induced by
\varphi
is biregular. The birational map
\varphi
is called a birational map of
G-
surfaces.

Definition 2.2
.

A pair
(G,S)
is said to be minimal (or equivalently that G acts minimally on
S
) if
any
G-
equivariant birational morphism
\varphi:S\rightarrow S^{\prime}
is an isomorphism.

A birational map
\varphi:S\dashrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{2}
yields an isomorphism between
\Cr(S)
and
\Cr(\mathbb{P}^{2})
. The Cremona group is then isomorphic to the group of birational maps of any rational surface. We say that two groups
G\subset\Cr(S)
and
G^{\prime}\subset\Cr(S^{\prime})
are birationally conjugate if there exists a birational map
\varphi:S\dashrightarrow S^{\prime}
such that
\varphi G\varphi^{-1}=G^{\prime}
. The choice of
\varphi
does not change the conjugacy class of the group and any element of the conjugacy class can be obtained in this manner. The converse is true, if
G
is finite
(
see for example a proof in
[
6
, Theorem
1.4
]
)
. This means that the two groups represent the same conjugacy class in the Cremona group and conversely a conjugacy class of a finite subgroup
G
of
\Cr_{\mathbb{C}}(2)
can be realized as a birational isomorphism class of
G-
surfaces. In this way classification of conjugacy classes of subgroups of
\Cr_{\mathbb{C}}(2)
becomes equivalent to the birational classification of
G-
surfaces. On another way, any subgroup of
\Bir(S)
may therefore be viewed as a subgroup of the Cremona group, up to conjugacy. A
G-
equivariant analog of a minimal surface allows one to concentrate on the study of minimal
G-
surfaces,
i.e.
, surfaces which cannot be
G-
equivariantly birationally and regularly mapped to another
G-
surface. Minimal
G-
surfaces turn out to be
G-
isomorphic either to a conic bundle or a Del Pezzo surface of degree
d
where
1\leq d\leq 9
.

To give a smaller sharp bound, one requires to study the finite groups
G
which may occur in a minimal
G-
surfaces defined over
\mathbb{Q}
that are
G-
isomorphic to

\bullet

a conic bundle structure,

\bullet

a Del Pezzo surface of degree
d\leq 9
.

We will study the first case in Section
3
and the second case in Sections
4-11
.

3.
Rational Surfaces with Conic Bundle structure

Definition 3.1
.

Let
S
be a smooth projective rational surface defined over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
and
\pi:S\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
be a morphism. We say that the pair
(S,\pi)
is a conic bundle if a general fibre of
\pi
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{P}^{1}
, with a finite number of exceptions: these singular fibres are the union of smooth rational curves
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
such that
(F_{1})^{2}=(F_{2})^{2}=-1
and
F_{1}\textperiodcentered F_{2}=1
.

Let
(S,\pi)
and
(\tilde{S},\tilde{\pi})
be two conic bundles. We say that
\varphi:S\dashrightarrow\tilde{S}
is a birational map of conic bundles if
\varphi
is a birational map which sends a general fibre of
\pi
on a general fibre of
\tilde{\pi}
. This corresponds to ask for
\alpha\in\Aut(\mathbb{P}^{1})
such that
\tilde{\pi}\circ\varphi=\alpha\circ\pi
.

We say that a conic bundle
(S,\pi)
is minimal if any birational morphism of conic bundles
(S,\pi)\rightarrow(\tilde{S},\tilde{\pi})
is an isomorphism.

Let
(S,\pi)
be some conic bundle. We denote by
\Aut(S,\pi)
(respectively
\mathrm{Bir}(S,\pi)
) the group of automorphisms (respectively birational self-maps) of the conic bundle
(S,\pi)
,
i.e.
automorphisms of
S
that send a general fibre of
\pi
on another general fibre. Observe that
\Aut(S,\pi)=\Aut(S)\cap\mathrm{Bir}(S,\pi)
.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. The
n-
th Hirzebruch surface
\mathbb{F}_{n}
is defined to be the quotient of
(\mathbb{A}^{2}\setminus\{0\})^{2}
by the action of
(\mathbb{G}_{m})^{2}
given by

\displaystyle(\mathbb{G}_{m})^{2}\times(\mathbb{A}^{2}\setminus\{0\})^{2}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow

\displaystyle(\mathbb{A}^{2}\setminus\{0\})^{2}

\displaystyle\left((\mu,\rho),(x_{0},x_{1},y_{0},y_{1})\right)

\displaystyle\mapsto

\displaystyle\left(\mu\rho^{-n}x_{0},\mu x_{1},\rho y_{0},\rho y_{1}\right).

The class of
(x_{0},x_{1},y_{0},y_{1})
will be written
[x_{0}:x_{1};y_{0}:y_{1}]
. The projection

\tau_{n}:\mathbb{F}_{n}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{P}^{1}},~[x_{0}:x_{1};y_{0}:y_{1}]\mapsto[y_{0}:y_{1}]

identifies
\mathbb{F}_{n}
with
\mathbb{P}_{{\mathbb{P}^{1}}}(\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{P}^{1}}}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{P}^{1}}}(n)
as a
{\mathbb{P}^{1}}-
bundle over
{\mathbb{P}^{1}}
.

The disjoint sections
s_{n},s_{-n}\subset\mathbb{F}_{n}
given by
x_{0}=0
and
x_{1}=0
have self-intersection
n
and
-n
, respectively.
The fibres
f\subset\mathbb{F}_{n}
given by
y_{0}=0
and
y_{1}=0
are linearly equivalent and of self-intersection
0
. We moreover get
{\mathrm{Pic}}(\mathbb{F}_{n})=\mathbb{Z}f\oplus\mathbb{Z}s_{-n}=\mathbb{Z}f\oplus\mathbb{Z}s_{n}
.

We have the following result:

Proposition 3.3
.

Minimal rational surfaces
(
[
2
, Theorem V.10]
)

Let
S
be a minimal rational surface. Then
S
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{P}^{2}
or to one of the surfaces

\mathbb{F}_{n}
, with
n\neq 1
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
S
be a rational smooth surface defined over
\bar{\mathbb{Q}}
and
\pi:S\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
be a conic bundle. Let
G
be a finite subgroup of
\Aut_{\bar{\mathbb{Q}}}(S,\pi)
, and
\bar{\pi}:G\rightarrow\Aut(\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\bar{\mathbb{Q}}})=\PGL(2,\bar{\mathbb{Q}})
be the homomorphism given by the action on fibres. Let
G^{\prime}=\mathrm{Ker}\bar{\pi}
and
g\in G^{\prime}\setminus\{id\}
. Then the action of the element
g
on any smooth fiber is not trivial.

Proof.

We contract orbits of components in any singular fibre, and assume that the action of
g
to be minimal. By
[
5
, Lemma 3.8]
, any singular fibre of
\pi
is twisted by some element of
\langle g\rangle
. If
g
twists a singular fibre, then (
g
is an involution) and the set
\mathrm{Fix}(g)
of fixed locus of
g
is a
2:1
cover of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
via
\pi
(
[
5
, Lemma
6.1
]
). So no curve contained in a fiber is fixed point-wise by
g
. Otherwise, there is no singular fiber and by
[
5
, Lemma 3.2.]
, the surface
S
is isomorphic to the Hirzebruch surface
\mathbb{F}_{n}
, for some integer
n\geq 0
. If
n=0
, then the action is given by

[x_{0}:x_{1};y_{0}:y_{1}]\mapsto[a\cdot x_{0}+b\cdot x_{1}:c\cdot x_{0}+d\cdot x_{1};y_{0}:y_{1}],

where
\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}a&b\\
c&d\end{array}\right)\in\PGL_{2}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}})
, since the action is the same on each fibre, then no fibre is fixed point-wise by
g
. If
n\geq 1
, the the action is given by

[x_{0}:x_{1};y_{0}:y_{1}]\mapsto[x_{0}:\lambda\cdot x_{1}+P_{n}(y_{0},y_{1})\cdot x_{0};y_{0}:y_{1}],

where
P_{n}(y_{0},y_{1})
is a homogeneous polynomial of degree
n
, and
\lambda\in\bar{\mathbb{Q}}^{*}
. If
\lambda=1
, then the action is not of finite order. If
\lambda\neq 1
, then the action is never the identity on any fibre.
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
S
be a rational surface defined over
\mathbb{Q}
and
\pi:S\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
be a conic bundle. If
G
is a finite subgroup of
\Aut_{\mathbb{Q}}(S,\pi)
, then
\mid G\mid
divides
144
.

Proof.

Let
S
be a rational surface defined over
\mathbb{Q}
and
\pi:S\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
be a conic bundle. Let
G
be a finite subgroup of
\Aut_{\mathbb{Q}}(S,\pi)
. We have a natural homomorphism
\bar{\pi}:G\rightarrow\Aut(\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathbb{Q}})=\PGL(2,\mathbb{Q})
that satisfies
\bar{\pi}(g)\pi=\pi g
, for every
g\in G
. Let
G^{\prime}=\mathrm{ker}\bar{\pi}
, hence, we have the following exact sequence

(1)

1\rightarrow G^{\prime}\rightarrow G\rightarrow\bar{\pi}(G)\subseteq\Aut(\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathbb{Q}})\rightarrow 1.

Hence,
\mid\bar{\pi}(G)\mid\subseteq\Aut(\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathbb{Q}})=\PGL(2,\mathbb{Q})
and by
[
3
, Theorem 4.2.]
,
\mid\bar{\pi}(G)\mid
divides
12
. After extending the scalar to
\bar{\mathbb{Q}}
, we apply Lemma
3.4
, then any element
g\in G^{\prime}\setminus\{id\}
acts non-trivially on any smooth fibre, this implies that
G^{\prime}\subseteq\Aut(\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathbb{Q}})=\PGL(2,\mathbb{Q})
. By
[
3
, Theorem 4.2.]
,
\mid G^{\prime}\mid
divides
12
. Since
\mid G\mid=\mid G^{\prime}\mid\cdot\mid\bar{\pi}(G)\mid
, then
\mid G\mid
divides
12\cdot 12=144
.
∎

Example 3.6
.

Let
S=\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
and
\pi:S\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
be the projection on a factor, that is a conic bundle. Any subgroup of
\Aut_{\mathbb{Q}}(S,\pi)\subset\Aut(\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1})
is isomorphic to a subgroup
H
of
\PGL_{2}(\mathbb{Q})\times\PGL_{2}(\mathbb{Q})
. Let
G\subset\PGL_{2}(\mathbb{Q})
be a finite subgroup, then the group
H:=G\times G
is a finite subgroup of
\PGL_{2}(\mathbb{Q})\times\PGL_{2}(\mathbb{Q})
, hence, the subgroup of
\Aut(\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1})
isomorphic to
H
preserves the fibration through
\pi
. Moreover, if
G\cong D_{6}
(a dihedral group of order
12
), then
H
is of order
144
.

4.
Del Pezzo surfaces of degree
6

Let
S
be a Del Pezzo surface of degree
6
, defined over
\mathbb{Q}
. Let
X
be the blow-up of
\mathbb{P}^{2}
at the three points
[1:0:0],~[0:1:0]
and
[0:0:1]
, therefore,
X
is the standard Del Pezzo surface given in
\mathbb{P}^{2}\times\mathbb{P}^{2}
given by

X=\left\{[x_{0}:x_{1}:x_{2}][y_{0}:y_{1}:y_{2}]\in\mathbb{P}^{2}\times\mathbb{P}^{2}\mid~x_{0}y_{0}=x_{1}y_{1}=x_{2}y_{2}\right\}.

Over the algebraic closure of
\mathbb{Q}
, we have an isomorphism
S_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}\rightarrow X_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}
. Let
L/\mathbb{Q}
be the smallest Galois field extension such that the six
(-1)-
curves are defined over
L
. The surface
S
has six
(-1)-
curves defined over
L
but not necessarily over
\mathbb{Q}
. The conjugation of the action gives an action of
\Gal(L/\mathbb{Q})
on
S_{L}
. Indeed, if we write the isomorphism
\varphi:S_{L}\rightarrow X_{L}
and let
g\in\Gal(L/\mathbb{Q})
, then the following diagram

\begin{array}[]{ccc}\hskip 14.22636ptS_{L}&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\varphi}}{{\longrightarrow}}&X_{L}\\
g\downarrow&&\downarrow\tilde{g}\\
\hskip 14.22636ptS_{L}&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\varphi}}{{\longrightarrow}}&X_{L}\end{array}

commutes, where
\tilde{g}=\varphi g\varphi^{-1}=g\tau
for some
\tau\in\Aut(X_{L})
. The Galois group
\Gal(L/\mathbb{Q})
acts on the hexagon by symmetries, so we have a group homomorphism

\Gal(L/\mathbb{Q})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\varrho}}{{\longrightarrow}}\sym_{3}\times\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}.

This map
\varrho
is injective as if we let
L^{\prime}=L^{\mathrm{Ker}(\varrho)},~\mathrm{Ker}(\varrho)
acts trivially on the hexagon, so all
(-1)-
curves are fixed by the kernel, hence they are all defined over
L^{\prime}
which gives
L^{\prime}=L
, and then
\mathrm{Ker}(\varrho)=\Gal(L/L)
.

The group
\Aut(S_{L})
(resp.
\Aut(X_{L})
) acts by symmetries on the hexagon of
S_{L}
(resp.
X_{L}
), which induces homomorphisms

\Aut(S_{L})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rho}}{{\longrightarrow}}\sym_{3}\times\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z},~~~~~~~~~\Aut(X_{L})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\hat{\rho}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\sym_{3}\times\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}.

We have the following splitting exact sequence

(2)

1\stackrel{{\scriptstyle~}}{{\rightarrow}}\left(L^{*}\right)^{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle~}}{{\rightarrow}}\Aut(X_{L})\xrightarrow[\mathrm{the~hexagon}]{\mathrm{action~on}}\sym_{3}\times\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle~}}{{\rightarrow}}1,

where
\Aut(X_{L})=\left(L^{*}\right)^{2}\rtimes(\sym_{3}\times\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})=\left(L^{*}\right)^{2}\rtimes D_{6}
, where
D_{6}
is the dihedral group of order
12
. We are looking for a finite subgroup
G
of
\Aut(S_{\mathbb{Q}})
where

\Aut(S_{\mathbb{Q}})=\{\psi\in\Aut(S_{L})\mid~\psi g=g\psi~~\forall g\in\Gal(L/\mathbb{Q})\}.

So we obtain by conjugation a finite subgroup of
\Aut(X_{L})
that can be described as follows

H\subseteq\varphi\Aut(S_{\mathbb{Q}})\varphi^{-1}\subseteq\Aut(X_{L})=\left(L^{*}\right)^{2}\rtimes D_{6},

H\subseteq\varphi\Aut(S_{\mathbb{Q}})\varphi^{-1}=\left\{\alpha\in\Aut(X_{L})\mid~\alpha\tilde{g}=\tilde{g}\alpha\right\},

so we have the following exact sequence

(3)

1\stackrel{{\scriptstyle~}}{{\rightarrow}}H\cap\left(L^{*}\right)^{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle~}}{{\rightarrow}}H\xrightarrow[\mathrm{the~hexagon}]{\mathrm{action~on}}\sym_{3}\times\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}.

We define
\Delta_{1}
to be the image of
H
in
D_{6}
, as
\Gal(L/\mathbb{Q})
acts on the hexagon faithfully and
\Gal(L/\mathbb{Q})\subseteq D_{6}
, so every element in
\Delta_{1}
should commute with the image
\tilde{g}
. Since
H\cap(L^{*})^{2}
is a finite subgroup of
(L^{*})^{2}
, it contains only roots of unity. We define
n
to be the largest order of a root of unity contained in
H\cap(L^{*})^{2}
. So we have
\mathbb{Q}[\zeta_{n}]
as an intermediate field of the Galois field extension
L/\mathbb{Q}
, where
\zeta_{n}
is the primitive
nth
root of unity. By the fundamental theorem of Galois theory,
L/\mathbb{Q}[\zeta_{n}]
is Galois, hence we have a surjective group homomorphism
\Gal(L/\mathbb{Q})\rightarrow\Gal(\mathbb{Q}[\zeta_{n}]/\mathbb{Q})
,
g\mapsto g\mid_{\mathbb{Q}[\zeta_{n}]}
, this leads to the existence of the following the exact sequence

(4)

1\rightarrow\Gal(L/\mathbb{Q}[\zeta_{n}])\rightarrow\Gal(L/\mathbb{Q})\rightarrow\Gal(\mathbb{Q}[\zeta_{n}]/\mathbb{Q})\cong\mathbb{Z}^{*}_{n}\rightarrow 1.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
S
be a Del Pezzo surface of degree
6
over
\mathbb{Q}
and assume that we have an isomorphism with
X
defined over
L=\mathbb{Q}[\zeta_{6}]
, such that the action of the Galois group is given by
g:\zeta_{6}\mapsto\zeta_{6}^{5}
, where
g^{2}=id
. One can takes

\tilde{g}:[x_{0}:x_{1}:x_{2}][y_{0}:y_{1}:y_{2}]\mapsto[g(y_{0}):g(y_{1}):g(y_{2})][g(x_{0}):g(x_{1}):g(x_{2})]

where
\tilde{g}^{2}=id
, then
\Aut_{\mathbb{Q}}(S)
contains a finite subgroup of order

and we can describe the generators explicitly in

. Moreover, the surface

is rational.

Proof.

We have
\Gal(L/\mathbb{Q})=\Gal(\mathbb{Q}[\zeta_{6}]/\mathbb{Q})=\langle g\rangle
. So the group

contains a finite subgroup

that is generated by

\displaystyle[x_{0}:x_{1}:x_{2}][y_{0}:y_{1}:y_{2}]\mapsto[x_{1}:x_{2}:x_{0}][y_{1}:y_{2}:y_{0}],

\displaystyle h_{2}:

\displaystyle[x_{0}:x_{1}:x_{2}][y_{0}:y_{1}:y_{2}]\mapsto[x_{0}:x_{2}:x_{1}][y_{0}:y_{2}:y_{1}],

\displaystyle h_{3}:

\displaystyle(X,Y)\mapsto(Y,X),

\displaystyle\alpha^{i}_{j}:

\displaystyle[x_{0}:x_{1}:x_{2}][y_{0}:y_{1}:y_{2}]\mapsto[x_{0}:\zeta_{6}^{i}x_{1}:\zeta_{6}^{j}x_{2}][y_{0}:\zeta_{6}^{6-i}y_{1}:\zeta_{6}^{6-j}y_{2}],

where
0\leq i\leq 5
and
1\leq j\leq 6
. Then,
H\cong(\mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z})^{2}\rtimes D_{6}
and thus has
432
elements. To prove that the surface is rational, the point
P=[1:1:1][1:1:1]
is fixed by
\tilde{g}
. Let
X_{L}
be the hexagon over
L
which is the blow-up of the three points
p_{1}=[1:0:0],~p_{2}=[0:1:0]
and

Remark 8.1

Lemma 8.2
.

Let
G
be a finite subgroup of
\PGL_{n}(\mathbb{Q})
and let
F\in\mathbb{Q}[x_{0},\ldots,x_{n-1}]
be an irreducible homogeneous polynomial such that
G
preserves
F
. This means that for each

in
G
and each matrix
M
of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{Q})
such that g is the class of
M
, the polynomial
F\circ M
(obtained by acting on the coordinates linearly) is a multiple of
F
. Then, if

and
n
are coprime, there exists a finite subgroup of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{Q})
that surjects to
G
, is twice bigger, and contains
-Id
.

Proof.

Let
g
be any element of
G
and let
M
be the corresponding matrix. We have
F\circ M=\lambda\cdot F
for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{Q}
. Let
m
be the order of
g
. Then,
M^{m}
is a multiple of the identity, which gives
\mu\in\mathbb{Q}
such that
M^{m}=\mu\cdot Id
. We write
a=det(M)
. As
M^{m}=\mu\cdot Id
, we apply the determinant on both sides and obtain
a^{m}=\mu^{n}
. Moreover,
F\circ M=\lambda\cdot F
, so
F\circ(M^{i})=\lambda^{i}\cdot F
for each

. In particular,
F\circ(M^{m})=\lambda^{m}\cdot F
. As
M^{m}=\mu\cdot Id
, we find

(recall that

is the degree of
F
), so
\mu^{d}=\lambda^{m}
. By Lemma
6.3
, there exist

such that
\mu=c^{m}
. Replacing
M
with

we do not change the class

in
G
, but may assume that
M^{m}=Id
. This implies that
\mathrm{det}(M)^{m}=1
, so
\mathrm{det}(M)\in\{-1,1\}
. We can then lift any element of

to an element of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{Q})
that has determinant

or
-1
. This gives exactly two possibilities for the lift, namely
M
or
-M
. The set of such matrices makes a finite group twice bigger than

.
∎

Lemma 11.1
.

Let
S
be a Del Pezzo surface of degree
1
defined over
\mathbb{Q}
. If
G\subseteq\Aut(S)
is finite subgroup, then
\mid G\mid\leq 12
.

Proof.

Let
S
be a Del Pezzo surface of degree
1
defined over
\mathbb{Q}
. By
[
15
, Theorem III.3.5]
and
[
14
, Corollary 3.54]
, up to a change of coordinates, we may assume that the surface
S
has the equation

(8)

w^{2}-z^{3}-F_{4}(x,y)z-F_{6}(x,y)=0,

in the weighted projective space
\mathbb{P}(3,1,1,2)
, where
F_{4}
and
F_{6}
are forms of respective degree
4
and
6
. Any element of
\Aut(S_{\mathbb{Q}})
is of the form

[w:x:y:z]\mapsto[\lambda\cdot w+p_{3}+z\cdot p_{1}:ax+by:cx+dy:\mu\cdot z+p_{2}],

where
p_{i}
is a homogeneous polynomial of degree
i
for
i=1,..,3
and the other are constants in
\mathbb{Q}
, with
\lambda\cdot\mu\neq 0
. Replacing in the equation we obtain

(\lambda\cdot w+p_{3}+z\cdot p_{1})^{2}-(\mu\cdot z+p_{2})^{3}-(\mu\cdot z+p_{2})g_{4}-g_{6},

where
g_{i}=f_{i}(ax+by,cx+dy)
. This should be a multiple of the Equation
8
. The degree
1
coefficient in
w
is
2\cdot\lambda\cdot w\cdot(p_{3}+z\cdot p_{1})
and needs to be zero, so
p_{3}=0
and
p_{1}=0
. Then, the degree
2
coefficient of
z
is
-3\cdot\mu^{2}\cdot p_{2}
so needs to be zero. This implies that every automorphism is of the form

[w:x:y:z]\mapsto[\lambda\cdot w:ax+by:cx+dy:\mu\cdot z].

Replacing in the Equation
8
, we obtain

\lambda^{2}\cdot w^{2}-\mu^{3}z^{3}-\mu\cdot z\cdot g_{4}-g_{6}=0,

where
g_{i}=f_{i}(ax+by,cx+dy)
. We find that
\lambda^{2}=\mu^{3}
, so by Lemma
6.3
, there exists
u\in\mathbb{Q}^{*}
such that
\lambda=u^{3}
and
\mu=u^{2}
. As
[w:x:y:z]=[u^{3}w:ux:uy:u^{2}z]
, we can replace
\lambda
with
\lambda/u^{3}=1
and
\mu
with
\mu/u^{2}=1
. Every element is then of the form

[w:x:y:z]\mapsto[w:ax+by:cx+dy:z],

where
\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}a&b\\
c&d\end{array}\right)\in\GL_{2}(\mathbb{Q})
. Moreover the matrix is unique, as if we multiply everything with
[s^{3}:s:s:s^{2}]
, then to obtain
s^{2}=s^{3}=1
we need
s=1
. The group of automorphism is then a finite subgroup of
\GL_{2}(\mathbb{Q})
. In
\GL_{2}(\mathbb{Q})
, every finite subgroup divides
12
(see
[
19
, Chap. IX, section 14]
), then
\mid\Aut(S_{\mathbb{Q}})\mid\leq 12
. Replacing again in the Equation
8
, we see that

for each
i
. The group of automorphism is then just

\{A\in\GL_{2}(\mathbb{Q})\mid f_{i}\circ A=f_{i}\text{ for each }i\}.

∎

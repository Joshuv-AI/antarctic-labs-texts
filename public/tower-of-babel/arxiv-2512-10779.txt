Lax Modal Lambda Calculi
Nachiappan Valliappan

Abstract
Intuitionistic modal logics (IMLs) extend intuitionistic propositional logic with modalities such as the box and diamond connectives. Advances in the study of IMLs have inspired several applications in programming languages via the development of corresponding type theories with modalities. Until recently, IMLs with diamonds have been misunderstood as somewhat peculiar and unstable, causing the development of type theories with diamonds to lag behind type theories with boxes. In this article, we develop a family of typed-lambda calculi corresponding to sublogics of a peculiar IML with diamonds known as Lax logic. These calculi provide a modal logical foundation for various strong functors in typed-functional programming. We present possible-world and categorical semantics for these calculi and constructively prove normalization, equational completeness and proof-theoretic inadmissibility results. Our main results have been formalized using the proof assistant Agda.

Introduction

In modal logic, a modality is a unary logical connective that exhibits
some logical properties.
Two such modalities are the connectives
\square
(“box”) and

\lozenge
(“diamond”).
Intuitively, a formula
\square A
can be understood as “necessarily

A
” and a formula
\lozenge A
as “possibly
A
”.
In classical modal logic, the most basic logic K extends classical
propositional logic (CPL) with the box modality, the

necessitation
rule (if
A
is a theorem then so is
\square A
)
and the
K
axiom (
\square(A\mathbin{\newmcodes@\mathsf{\Rightarrow}}B)\mathbin{\newmcodes@\mathsf{\Rightarrow}}\square A\mathbin{\newmcodes@\mathsf{\Rightarrow}}\square B
).
The diamond modality can be encoded in this logic as a dual of the box
modality:
\lozenge A\equiv\neg{\square\neg{A}}
.
That is,
\lozenge A
is true if and only if
\neg{\square\neg{A}}
is
true.

In intuitionistic modal logic (IML), there is no consensus on one
logic as the most basic logic.
We instead find a variety of different IMLs based on different
motivations.
The
\square
and
\lozenge
modalities are independent connectives in
IML
[
35
, Requirement 5]
, just as
\land
and
\lor
are
independent connectives that are not inter-definable in
intuitionistic propositional logic (IPL).
In contrast to
\square
, however, the logical properties of

\lozenge
vary widely in IML literature.
This has misconstrued
\lozenge
as a controversial and unstable
modality.
It had been incorrectly assumed until recently that
several IMLs with both
\square
and
\lozenge
coincided (i.e. were
conservative extensions of their sublogics) only in the

\lozenge
-free fragment, suggesting some sort of stability of

\square
-only logics.
Fortunately, misconceptions concerning intuitionistic diamonds have
been broken in recent results
[
16
,
18
]
and we are
approaching a better understanding of it.

Advances in IML have led to a plethora of useful applications in
programming languages through the development of corresponding type
theories with modalities.
Modal lambda calculi
[
32
,
13
]
with box modalities
have found applications in staged
meta-programming
[
17
,
31
,
23
]
, reactive
programming
[
5
]
, safe usage of temporal
resources
[
2
]
and checking productivity of recursive
definitions
[
10
]
.
Two particular box axioms that have received plenty of attention in
these developments are the axioms
\textsc{T}:\square A\mathbin{\newmcodes@\mathsf{\Rightarrow}}A
and

\textsc{4}:\square A\mathbin{\newmcodes@\mathsf{\Rightarrow}}\square\square A
.
Dual-context modal calculi
[
32
,
24
]
which
admit one or both of these axioms are well-understood.
These calculi enjoy a rich meta-theory, including confluent
reduction, normalization and a comprehensive analysis of provability.
Fitch-style modal lambda calculi
[
13
]
admitting
axioms
T
and
4
further enjoy an elegant categorical
interpretation, possible-world semantics, and results showing how
categorical models of these calculi can be constructed using
possible-world semantics of their corresponding
logics
[
37
]
.

Lambda calculi with diamond modalities in comparison have received
much less attention from the type-theoretic perspective.
The controversy surrounding the diamond modality in IML appears to
have restricted the development of type theories with diamonds.
For example, Kavvos
[
25
]
cites Simpson’s
survey
[
35
]
of IMLs and restricts the development of
dual-context modal calculi “to the better-behaved, and seemingly
more applicable box modality” arguing that the “computational
interpretation [of
\lozenge
] is not very crisp”.
Recent breakthroughs in intuitionistic modal logic have made it clear
that diamonds are no more problematic than boxes.
In this article, we further the type-theoretic account of a
special class of diamond modalities with compelling applications in
programming languages.

Propositional lax logic
(PLL) is an intuitionistic modal logic
introduced independently by Fairtlough and
Mendler
[
20
]
and Benton, Bierman and de
Paiva
[
7
]
.
PLL extends IPL with a diamond modality
\lozenge
, known as the lax
modality, which exhibits a peculiar modal axiom
S
(for
“strength”), in addition to axioms
R
(for “return”) and
J
(for
“join”) that are well-known in classical modal logic as duals
to the box axioms
T
and
4
respectively.

\displaystyle\textsc{S}:A\mathbin{\newmcodes@\mathsf{\times}}\lozenge B\mathbin{\newmcodes@\mathsf{\Rightarrow}}\lozenge(A\mathbin{\newmcodes@\mathsf{\times}}B)

\displaystyle\textsc{R}:A\mathbin{\newmcodes@\mathsf{\Rightarrow}}\lozenge A

\displaystyle\textsc{J}:\lozenge\lozenge A\mathbin{\newmcodes@\mathsf{\Rightarrow}}\lozenge A

It is known that PLL corresponds to a typed-lambda calculus (we
call λ
ML
) known as Moggi’s
monadic
metalanguage

[
30
]
, which models side effects in
functional programming using
strong monads
from category
theory.
Benton, Bierman and de Paiva
[
7
]
, and later Pfenning and
Davies
[
32
]
, show that a judgment is provable in a
natural deduction proof system for PLL if and only if there exists a
typing derivation for its corresponding judgment in λ
ML
.
However, in contrast to the comprehensive treatment of box modalities
mentioned above, there remain several gaps in our understanding of the
lax modality:

1.

It has remained unclear as to whether type theories can exist
for sublogics of PLL or whether the axioms of PLL in combination
happen to coincidentally enjoy a status of
“well-behavedness”. What happens if we drop one or more of
the modal axioms
R
and
J
? Does a corresponding type theory
still exist?

2.

A satisfactory account of the correspondence between the
possible-world semantics of PLL and the categorical semantics
of λ
ML
is still missing. In particular, how can we leverage the
possible-world semantics of PLL to construct models of λ
ML
?

The first objective of this article is to develop corresponding type
theories for sublogics of PLL that drop one or both of axioms
R
and

J
.
From the type-theoretic perspective, this corresponds to type
theories for non-monadic strong functors, which are prevalent in
functional programming.
For example, in Haskell, the array data type (in
Data.Array
)
is a strong functor that neither exhibits return (axiom
R
) nor join (axiom
J
). Several other Haskell data types exhibit
return
1
1

1

https://hackage.haskell.org/package/pointed-5.0.5/docs/Data-Pointed.html

or
join
2
2

2

https://hackage.haskell.org/package/semigroupoids-6.0.1/docs/Data-Functor-Bind.html#g:4
,
but not
both
3
3

3

https://wiki.haskell.org/Why_not_Pointed%3F
.
We are interested in developing a uniform modal logical foundation for
the axioms of non-monadic strong functors.

The second objective of this article is to study the connection
between possible-world semantics of PLL and its sublogics and
categorical models of their corresponding type theories.
Possible-world semantics for logics are concerned with
provability of formulas and not about proofs themselves.
Categorical models of lambda calculi, on the other hand, distinguish
different proofs (terms) of the same proposition (type).
Mitchell and Moggi
[
29
]
show the connection between
these two different semantics using a categorical refinement of
possible-world semantics for the simply-typed lambda
calculus (STLC).
They note that their refined semantics, which we shall call

proof-relevant possible-world semantics
, makes it
“easy to devise Kripke counter-models” since they “seem to
support a set-like intuition about lambda terms better than
arbitrary cartesian closed categories”.
We wish to achieve this technical convenience in model construction
for all the modal lambda calculi in this article.

Towards our first objective, we formulate three new modal lambda
calculi as subsystems of λ
ML
: λ
SL
, λ
SRL
, λ
SJL
.
The calculus λ
SL
models
strong
functors and corresponds to a
logic SL (for “S-lax Logic”) that admits axiom
S
, but
neither
R
nor
J
.
The calculus λ
SRL
models strong
pointed
functors and
corresponds to a logic SRL (for “SR-lax Logic”) that admits
axioms
S
and
R
, but not
J
.
The calculus λ
SJL
models strong
semimonads
and corresponds to a
logic SJL (for “SJ-lax Logic”) that admits axioms
S
and
J
,
but not
R
.
We refer to all four calculi collectively as
lax modal lambda
calculi
.
Towards our second objective, we extend Mitchell and Moggi’s
proof-relevant possible-world semantics to lax modal lambda
calculi and show that it is complete for their equational theories.
We further show that all four calculi are normalizing by constructing

Normalization by Evaluation
models as instances of
possible-world semantics and prove completeness and
inadmissibility results as corollaries.
All the theorems in this article have been verified
correct
[
38
]
using the proof assistant
Agda
[
1
]
.

ML

In this section, we define the syntax and semantics of PLL and its
sublogics as extensions of the
negative
, i.e. disjunction and
absurdity-free, fragment of IPL.
This section is a summary of the background presumed in this article

work

,

]
.

2.1
Syntax and semantics of PLL

Syntax
. The language of (the negative fragment of) PLL consists of formulas
defined inductively by propositional atoms (
p
,
q
,
r
, etc.), a
constant
{\newmcodes@\mathsf{1}}
and logical connectives
\mathbin{\newmcodes@\mathsf{\times}}
,
\mathbin{\newmcodes@\mathsf{\Rightarrow}}

and
\lozenge
.
The connective
\lozenge
has the highest operator precedence, and is
followed by
\mathbin{\newmcodes@\mathsf{\times}}
and
\mathbin{\newmcodes@\mathsf{\Rightarrow}}
.
Following the usual convention, we suppose that
\mathbin{\newmcodes@\mathsf{\times}}
and
\mathbin{\newmcodes@\mathsf{\Rightarrow}}

associate to the right.

\displaystyle{\newmcodes@{\operatorfont Prop}}\qquad A,B::=\ p,q,r,\ldots\ |\ {\newmcodes@\mathsf{1}}\ |\ A\mathbin{\newmcodes@\mathsf{\times}}B\ |\ A\mathbin{\newmcodes@\mathsf{\Rightarrow}}B\ |\ \lozenge A

\displaystyle{\newmcodes@{\operatorfont Ctx}}\qquad\Gamma,\Delta::={\newmcodes@\mathsf{{\cdot}}}\ |\ \Gamma,A

The constant
{\newmcodes@\mathsf{1}}
denotes universal truth, the binary
connectives
\mathbin{\newmcodes@\mathsf{\times}}
and
\mathbin{\newmcodes@\mathsf{\Rightarrow}}
respectively denote conjunction and
implication, and the unary connective
\lozenge
denotes the lax
modality.
Intuitively, a formula
\lozenge A
may be understood as qualifying the
truth of formula
A
under
some
constraint.
A context
\Gamma
is a multiset of formulas
A_{1},A_{2},...,A_{n}
,
where
{\newmcodes@\mathsf{{\cdot}}}
denotes the empty context.

A Hilbert-style axiomatization of PLL can be given by extending
the usual axioms and rules of deduction for IPL with the modal
axioms
S
,
R
, and
J
in
\cref
sec:intro.

Semantics
. The possible-world semantics of PLL defines the truth of
PLL
-
formulas in a model using gadgets known as
frames
.
A PLL
-
frame
F=\lparen W,\mathrel{R_{i}},\mathrel{R_{m}}\rparen
is a triple that consists of
a set
W
of
worlds
and two reflexive-transitive
relations
\mathrel{R_{i}}
(for “intuitionistic”) and
\mathrel{R_{m}}
(for “modal”) on
worlds satisfying two compatibility conditions:

•

Forward confluence:
{\mathrel{R_{i}}^{-1};\mathrel{R_{m}}}\subseteq{\mathrel{R_{m}};\mathrel{R_{i}}^{-1}}

•

Inclusion:
{\mathrel{R_{m}}}\subseteq{\mathrel{R_{i}}}

The relation
\mathrel{R_{i}}^{-1}
is the converse of relation
\mathrel{R_{i}}
, and is
defined as
{\mathrel{R_{i}}^{-1}}=\{(y,x)\ |\ (x,y)\in{\mathrel{R_{i}}}\}
.
The operator

denotes composition of relations and is defined for
two relations

and
R_{2}
on worlds as

R_{1};R_{2}=\{(x,z)\ |\ \text{there exists}\ y\in W\ \text{such
that}\ (x,y)\in R_{1}\ \text{and}\ (y,z)\in R_{2}\}

We may intuitively understand worlds as nodes in a graph denoting the
“state of assumptions”, relation
\mathrel{R_{i}}
as paths denoting increase in
assumptions, and relation
\mathrel{R_{m}}
as paths denoting constraining of

That is,

to

, and

Under this reading, the inclusion condition
{\mathrel{R_{m}}}\subseteq{\mathrel{R_{i}}}

states that imposing a constraint increases assumptions.

The forward confluence condition
{\mathrel{R_{i}}^{-1};\mathrel{R_{m}}}\subseteq{\mathrel{R_{m}};\mathrel{R_{i}}^{-1}}
states that constraints can be “transported” over an
increase in assumptions.
It can be visualized as depicted on the right, where the dotted lines

This condition does not appear in Fairtlough and Mendler’s original

[

[

.
It simplifies the interpretation of
\lozenge

A model
\mathcal{M}=\lparen F,V\rparen
couples a frame
F
with a

valuation

that assigns to each propositional
atom

a set
V(p)
of worlds hereditary in
\mathrel{R_{i}}
, i.e. if
w\mathrel{R_{i}}w^{\prime}
and

.
The truth of a formula in a model
\mathcal{M}
is defined by the

satisfaction

\Vdash
for a given world
w\in W
by
induction on a formula as:

\begin{array}[]{l@{\;\Vdash\;} @{\;} l @{\;\text{iff}\;}c@{\;} l}\mathcal{M},w&p&&w\in V(p)\\
\mathcal{M},w&{\newmcodes@\mathsf{1}}&&\text{true}\\

\mathcal{M},w&A\mathbin{\newmcodes@\mathsf{\Rightarrow}}B&&\text{for all}\ w^{\prime}\in W\ \text{such that}\ w\mathrel{R_{i}}w^{\prime},\ \mathcal{M},w^{\prime}\Vdash A\ \text{implies}\ \mathcal{M},w^{\prime}\Vdash B\\
\mathcal{M},w&\lozenge A&&\text{there exists}\ v\in W\ \text{with}\ w\mathrel{R_{m}}v\ \text{and}\ \mathcal{M},v\Vdash A\end{array}

We write
\mathcal{M},w\Vdash\Gamma
to denote
\mathcal{M},w\Vdash A_{i}

for all formulas

with
1\leq i\leq n
in context
\Gamma=A_{1},...A_{n}
, and write
\Gamma\models A
to denote
\mathcal{M},w\Vdash\Gamma
implies
\mathcal{M},w\Vdash A
for all worlds

in all

\mathcal{M}
.
Furthermore, we write
\mathcal{M}\models A
to denote
\mathcal{M},w\Vdash A
for all worlds

in
\mathcal{M}
.

The soundness of PLL for its semantics can be shown using the
following key properties:

Proposition 1
.

For an arbitrary model
\mathcal{M}
of PLL

•

if
w\mathrel{R_{i}}w^{\prime}

and
\mathcal{M},w\Vdash A
then
\mathcal{M},w^{\prime}\Vdash A
, for all worlds
w,w^{\prime}
and formulas
A

•

\mathcal{M}\models A\mathbin{\newmcodes@\mathsf{\times}}\lozenge B\mathbin{\newmcodes@\mathsf{\Rightarrow}}\lozenge(A\mathbin{\newmcodes@\mathsf{\times}}B)
, for all formulas
A,B

•

\mathcal{M}\models A\mathbin{\newmcodes@\mathsf{\Rightarrow}}\lozenge A
, for all formulas
A

•

\mathcal{M}\models\lozenge\lozenge A\mathbin{\newmcodes@\mathsf{\Rightarrow}}\lozenge A
, for all formulas

Proof.

The first property, sometimes called “monotonicity”, states that
the truth of a formula

persists as knowledge increases. This
property can be proved by induction on the formula

, using the
forward confluence condition for the case of
\lozenge A
. The
remaining properties can be proved using the definition of the

\models
and
\Vdash
, by respectively using the
inclusion condition
{\mathrel{R_{m}}}\subseteq{\mathrel{R_{i}}}
, reflexivity of
\mathrel{R_{m}}
,
and transitivity of
\mathrel{R_{m}}
.
∎

2.2
Syntax and semantics of λ
ML

Syntax
. The calculus λ
ML
is a typed
\lambda
-calculus
that was developed by Moggi
[
30
]
before PLL.
The language of λ
ML
consists of types, contexts and terms, and can be
understood as an extension of STLC with a unary type
constructor
\lozenge
that exhibits the PLL axioms
S
,
R
and
J
.
Types and contexts in λ
ML
are defined inductively by the following grammars:

\displaystyle{\newmcodes@{\operatorfont Ty}}\quad A,B::={\newmcodes@\mathsf{\iota}}\ |\ {\newmcodes@\mathsf{1}}\ |\ A\mathbin{\newmcodes@\mathsf{\times}}B\ |\ A\mathbin{\newmcodes@\mathsf{\Rightarrow}}B\ |\ \lozenge A\qquad

\displaystyle{\newmcodes@{\operatorfont Ctx}}\quad\Gamma,\Delta::={\newmcodes@\mathsf{{\cdot}}}\ |\ \Gamma,x:A\quad(x\ \text{not in}\ \Gamma)

The type
{\newmcodes@\mathsf{\iota}}
denotes an uninterpeted base (or “ground”) type,

{\newmcodes@\mathsf{1}}
denotes the unit type,
A\mathbin{\newmcodes@\mathsf{\times}}B
denotes product types,

A\mathbin{\newmcodes@\mathsf{\Rightarrow}}B
denotes function types, and
\lozenge A
denotes

modal
types.
A modal type
\lozenge A
can be understood as the type of a
computation that performs some side-effects to return a value of
type
A
.
A context (or “typing environment”)
\Gamma
is a list
x_{1}:A_{1},...,x_{n}:A_{n}
of unique type assignments to variables, where

{\newmcodes@\mathsf{{\cdot}}}
denotes the empty context.

The term language of STLC consists of variables (
x,y,...
) and term
constructs for the unit type (
{\newmcodes@\mathsf{unit}}
), product types (
{\newmcodes@\mathsf{pair}}
,

{\newmcodes@\mathsf{fst}}
,
{\newmcodes@\mathsf{snd}}
) and function types (
{\newmcodes@\mathsf{lam}}
,
{\newmcodes@\mathsf{app}}
).
The term language of λ
ML
extends that of STLC with the
constructs
{\newmcodes@\mathsf{return_{\text{{{}{ML}} }}}}
and
{\newmcodes@\mathsf{let_{\text{{{}{ML}} }}}}
for the modal types.
The
typing judgments

\Gamma\vdash t:A
defined in

\cref
fig:calculus-mlc identify
well-typed
terms in λ
ML
.
We say that a term
t
is
well-typed for
type
A

under
context
\Gamma
when there exists a derivation, called
the
typing derivation
, of the typing
judgment
\Gamma\vdash t:A
.
In this article, we are only concerned with well-typed terms and
assume that every term
t
is well-typed for some type
A
under
some context
\Gamma
.
The
equality judgments

\Gamma\vdash t\sim t^{\prime}:A
defined
in
\cref
fig:calculus-mlc identify the equivalence between
well-typed terms
t
and
t^{\prime}
and specify the
equational
theory
for λ
ML
.

In
\cref
fig:calculus-mlc, the notation
t[u/x]
denotes the

substitution
of term
u
for the variable
x
in term
t
, and
the notation
{\newmcodes@{\operatorfont\uparrow}}\,t
denotes the
weakening
of a term
\Gamma\vdash t:A
by embedding it into a larger context
\Gamma\subseteq\Gamma^{\prime}
as
\Gamma^{\prime}\vdash{\newmcodes@{\operatorfont\uparrow}}\,t:A
.
We write
\Gamma\subseteq\Gamma^{\prime}
when the context
\Gamma
is a
sub-list of context
\Gamma^{\prime}
, meaning
\Gamma^{\prime}
contains at
least the variable-type assignments in
\Gamma
.
Substitution and weakening are both well-typed operations on
terms that are admissible in λ
ML
:

•

Substitution: If
\Gamma,x:A\vdash t:B
and
\Gamma\vdash u:A
, then
\Gamma\vdash t[u/x]:B

•

Weakening: If
\Gamma\vdash t:A
and
\Gamma\subseteq\Gamma^{\prime}
, then
\Gamma^{\prime}\vdash{\newmcodes@{\operatorfont\uparrow}}\,t:A

We say that a type
X
is
derivable
(or
can be derived
)
in λ
ML
when there exists a typing derivation of the judgment

{\newmcodes@\mathsf{{\cdot}}}\vdash t:X
for some term
t
.
The types corresponding to the PLL axioms
S
,
R
and
J
are
each derivable in λ
ML
, as witnessed by the terms below:

•

{\newmcodes@\mathsf{{\cdot}}}\vdash{\newmcodes@\mathsf{lam}}\,x.\,{\newmcodes@\mathsf{let_{\text{{{}{ML}} }}}}\,y=({\newmcodes@\mathsf{snd}}\,x)\,{\newmcodes@\mathsf{in}}\,{({\newmcodes@\mathsf{return_{\text{{{}{ML}} }}}}\,({\newmcodes@\mathsf{pair}}\,({\newmcodes@\mathsf{fst}}\,x)\,y))}:A\mathbin{\newmcodes@\mathsf{\times}}\lozenge B\mathbin{\newmcodes@\mathsf{\Rightarrow}}\lozenge(A\mathbin{\newmcodes@\mathsf{\times}}B)

•

{\newmcodes@\mathsf{{\cdot}}}\vdash{\newmcodes@\mathsf{lam}}\,x.\,{\newmcodes@\mathsf{return_{\text{{{}{ML}} }}}}\,x:A\mathbin{\newmcodes@\mathsf{\Rightarrow}}\lozenge A

2.3
Sublogics of PLL and corresponding lambda calculi

The minimal sublogic of PLL, which we call SL, can be axiomatized by
extending the usual axioms and rules of IPL with (only) the modal
axiom
S
.
Furthermore, we axiomatize:

•

the logic SRL by extending SL with axiom
R

•

the logic SJL by extending SL with axiom
J

•

the logic PLL by extending SL with axioms
R
and
J
(as
defined previously)

The semantics for SL, SRL and SJL is given as before for PLL by
restricting the definitions of frames.
An SL
-
frame
F=\lparen W,\mathrel{R_{i}},\mathrel{R_{m}}\rparen
is a triple that
consists of a set
W
of worlds, a reflexive-transitive
relation
\mathrel{R_{i}}
, and a relation
\mathrel{R_{m}}
(that need not be reflexive or
transitive), satisfying the forward confluence and inclusion
conditions.
Furthermore, an SL
-
frame is

•

an SRL
-
frame when
\mathrel{R_{m}}
is reflexive

•

an SJL
-
frame when
\mathrel{R_{m}}
is transitive

•

a PLL
-
frame when
\mathrel{R_{m}}
is reflexive and transitive (as defined previously)

In the upcoming section, we will define a corresponding modal lambda
calculus for each of PLL’s sublogics (
\cref
sec:subcalculi).
We develop proof-relevant possible-world semantics for these
calculi and show the connection to categorical semantics by studying
the properties of presheaf categories determined by
proof-relevant frames (
\cref
sec:prks).
We leverage this connection to then construct Normalization by
Evaluation models for the calculi, and show as corollaries
completeness and inadmissibility theorems (
\cref
sec:nci).

4
Proof-relevant possible-world semantics

In
\cref
sec:overview, possible-world semantics was given for
the logic PLL and its sublogics in a classical meta-language
using sets and relations.
In this section, we will give proof-relevant possible-world
semantics for lax modal lambda calculi, for which we will instead work
in a constructive dependent type-theory loosely based on the
proof assistant Agda.

We will use a type
X:{\newmcodes@{\operatorfont Type}}
in place of a set
X
and values
x:X

in place of elements
x\in X
.
The arrow
\to
denotes functions, and quantifications
\forall x
and

\Sigma_{x}
denote universal and existential quantification
respectively, where
x:X
is a value of some type
X:{\newmcodes@{\operatorfont Type}}
that
is left implicit.
A value of type
\forall x.\,P(x)
for some predicate
P:X\to{\newmcodes@{\operatorfont Type}}
is a function
\lambda x.\,p
with
p:P(x)
.
When the expression
p
does not mention the variable
x
we will
leave the abstraction implicit and simply write
p
as a value of

\forall x.\,P(x)
.
A value of type
\Sigma_{x}.\,P(x)
is a tuple
\lparen x,p\rparen
, but we
will similarly leave the witness
x
implicit at times and write

\lparen\_,p\rparen
or simply
p
for brevity.

Semantics for
λ
SL

.
A proof-relevant λ
SL
-frame
F=(W,\mathrel{R_{i}},\mathrel{R_{m}})
is a triple
that consists of a
type

W:{\newmcodes@{\operatorfont Type}}
of worlds and two
proof-relevant relations
\mathrel{R_{i}},\mathrel{R_{m}}:W\to W\to{\newmcodes@{\operatorfont Type}}
with

•

functions
{\newmcodes@{\operatorfont refl_{i}}}:\forall w.\,w\mathrel{R_{i}}w
and
{\newmcodes@{\operatorfont trans_{i}}}:\forall w,w^{\prime},w^{\prime\prime}.\,w\mathrel{R_{i}}w^{\prime}\to w^{\prime}\mathrel{R_{i}}w^{\prime\prime}\to w\mathrel{R_{i}}w^{\prime\prime}

respectively proving the reflexivity and transitivity of
\mathrel{R_{i}}
such
that

{\newmcodes@{\operatorfont trans_{i}}}\,{\newmcodes@{\operatorfont refl_{i}}}\,i=i

•

function
{\newmcodes@{\operatorfont incl}}:\forall w,v.\,w\mathrel{R_{m}}v\to w\mathrel{R_{i}}v

The function

and

encoding of reflexivity and transitivity of
\mathrel{R_{i}}
respectively.
These functions are subject to the accompanying coherence laws, which

must be the unit of

\mathrel{R_{i}}

SL

The functions

and

of the forward confluence (
{\mathrel{R_{i}}^{-1};\mathrel{R_{m}}}\subseteq{\mathrel{R_{m}};\mathrel{R_{i}}^{-1}}
) and inclusion (
{\mathrel{R_{m}}}\subseteq{\mathrel{R_{i}}}
) conditions

Given a proof of

(i.e.
w^{\prime}\mathrel{R_{i}}^{-1}w
) and
w\mathrel{R_{m}}v

returns a pair of proofs for some world

w^{\prime}\mathrel{R_{m}}v^{\prime}

Similarly, given a proof of
w\mathrel{R_{m}}v
,
{\newmcodes@{\operatorfont incl}}
returns a proof of
w\mathrel{R_{i}}v
.
These functions are also accompanied by the stated coherence laws.

The proof-relevant relation
\mathrel{R_{i}}
in a λ
SL
-frame
determines a category
{\newmcodes@{\operatorfont{\newmcodes@{\operatorfont\mathcal{W}_{i}}}}}
whose objects are given by worlds and

\mathrel{R_{i}}

{\newmcodes@{\operatorfont refl_{i}}}
witnessing the identity

{\newmcodes@{\operatorfont trans_{i}}}

\widehat{{\newmcodes@{\operatorfont\mathcal{W}_{i}}}}
of covariant presheaves
indexed by
{\newmcodes@{\operatorfont\mathcal{W}_{i}}}

The objects in the category
\widehat{{\newmcodes@{\operatorfont\mathcal{W}_{i}}}}
are presheaves and the

and

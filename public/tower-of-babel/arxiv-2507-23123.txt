Mean-field approximation, Gibbs relaxation, and cross estimates
Armand Bernou, Mitia Duerinckx

Abstract
. This work focuses on the propagation of chaos and the relaxation to Gibbs equilibrium for a system of N N classical Brownian particles with weak mean-field interactions. While it is known that propagation of chaos holds at rate O ⁡ ( N − 1 ) O(N^{-1}) uniformly in time, and Gibbs relaxation at rate O ⁡ ( e − c ​ t ) O(e^{-ct}) uniformly in N N , we go a step further by showing that the cross error between chaos propagation and Gibbs relaxation is O ⁡ ( N − 1 ​ e − c ​ t ) O(N^{-1}e^{-ct}) . For translation-invariant systems on the torus, this leads to an improved mean-field approximation error at the level of the one-particle density: the error decreases from O ⁡ ( N − 1 ) O(N^{-1}) to O ⁡ ( N − 1 ​ e − c ​ t ) O(N^{-1}e^{-ct}) . Our approach relies on a detailed analysis of the BBGKY hierarchy for correlation functions, and applies to both underdamped and overdamped Langevin dynamics with merely bounded interaction forces. We also derive new results on Gibbs relaxation and present partial extensions beyond the weak interaction regime.



1.
Introduction

We consider the Langevin dynamics for a system of
N
Brownian particles with weak mean-field interactions, moving in a confining potential in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\geq 1
.
For the purpose of this introduction, we focus on the case of the underdamped dynamics, but all our results hold unchanged for the overdamped dynamics; see Section
1.2
below.
More precisely, we consider the following system of coupled SDEs: for
1\leq i\leq N
,

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathrm{d}X^{i,N}_{t}=V^{i,N}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t,&t\geq 0,\\[2.84526pt]
\mathrm{d}V^{i,N}_{t}=-\beta V^{i,N}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t+\tfrac{\kappa}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}K(X^{i,N}_{t},X_{t}^{j,N})\,\mathrm{d}t-\nabla A(X_{t}^{i,N})\mathrm{d}t+\sqrt{2}\mathrm{d}B_{t}^{i},&t\geq 0,\\[5.69054pt]
(X^{i,N}_{t},V^{i,N}_{t})|_{t=0}=(X^{i,N}_{\circ},V^{i,N}_{\circ})&\end{array}\right.

where
\{(X^{i,N},V^{i,N})\}_{1\leq i\leq N}
is the set of particle positions and velocities in the phase space
\mathbb{X}:=\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
where
\{B^{i}\}_{i}
are independent and identically distributed
d
-dimensional Brownian motions,
where
K
is a bounded force kernel,

(1.2)

K\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})^{d},

where
A
is a uniformly convex confining potential,

(1.3)

\nabla^{2}A\geq\lambda\operatorname{Id},\qquad\text{for some $\lambda>0$},

where
\beta>0
is a given constant,
and where
\kappa>0
is a coupling parameter describing the strength of interactions.
Throughout,
we shall assume
\kappa
to be small enough (independently of
N
) so as to ensure good enough properties of the mean-field dynamics — in particular, the uniqueness of the invariant measure.
For the overdamped dynamics, some partial results can be obtained without this smallness condition; see Section
1.3
below.

In terms of a probability density
F^{N}
on the
N
-particle phase space
\mathbb{X}^{N}
, we recall that the dynamics (
1.1
) is (formally) equivalent to the Liouville equation

(1.4)

\partial_{t}F^{N}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}v_{i}\cdot\nabla_{x_{i}}F^{N}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\operatorname{div}}_{v_{i}}\big((\nabla_{v_{i}}+\beta v_{i})F^{N}\big)\\[-8.53581pt]
-\frac{\kappa}{N}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}K(x_{i}-x_{j})\cdot\nabla_{v_{i}}F^{N}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\nabla_{x_{i}}A\cdot\nabla_{v_{i}}F^{N}.

We assume particles to be exchangeable, which amounts to the symmetry of
F^{N}
in its
N
variables
z_{i}=(x_{i},v_{i})\in\mathbb{X}
,
1\leq i\leq N
, and we assume for simplicity that they are exactly chaotic initially, meaning that initial positions and velocities
\{Z^{i,N}_{\circ}:=(X^{i,N}_{\circ},V^{i,N}_{\circ})\}_{1\leq i\leq N}
are independent and have a common density
\mu_{\circ}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{X})
: equivalently, this means that
F^{N}
is initially tensorized,

(1.5)

F_{t}^{N}|_{t=0}=\mu_{\circ}^{\otimes N}.

Note that this is not really restrictive in view of de Finetti’s theorem.
We now consider a global weak solution
F^{N}\in C(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mathcal{P}\cap\operatorname{L}^{1}(\mathbb{X}))
of the Liouville equation (
1.4
) with these tensorized data.
In the regime of a large number
N\gg 1
of particles, a classical question then concerns the evolution of finite subsets of ‘typical’ particles in the system, as described by marginals of
F^{N}
,

F^{N,m}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{m})\,:=\,\int_{\mathbb{X}^{N-m}}F^{N}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{N})\,\mathrm{d}z_{m+1}\ldots\mathrm{d}z_{N},\qquad 1\leq m\leq N.

Correlations between particles are expected to remain negligible over time, so that the initial chaotic behavior (
1.5
) would be approximately propagated: for any fixed
m\geq 1
and
t\geq 0
,

(1.6)

F^{N,m}_{t}-(F^{N,1}_{t})^{\otimes m}\,\to\,0,\qquad\text{as $N\uparrow\infty$}.

As is well-known, this propagation of chaos would imply the mean-field limit

(1.7)

F^{N,m}_{t}\,\to\,\mu_{t}^{\otimes m},\qquad\text{as\penalty\ $N\uparrow\infty$},

where
\mu_{t}
is the solution of the non-linear Vlasov-Fokker-Planck equation

(1.8)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{t}\mu+v\cdot\nabla_{x}\mu={\operatorname{div}}_{v}((\nabla_{v}+\beta v)\mu)-\kappa(K\ast\mu)\cdot\nabla_{v}\mu+\nabla_{x}A\cdot\nabla_{v}\mu,\qquad t\geq 0,\\
\mu|_{t=0}=\mu_{\circ},\end{array}\right.

with the short-hand notation
K\ast\mu(z):=\int_{\mathbb{X}}K(x,y)\mu(y,w)\,\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}w
for
z=(x,v)\in\mathbb{X}
. This topic has been extensively investigated since the 1990s.
Quantitative estimates for the mean-field approximation (
1.7
) have attracted much interest in recent years; see e.g.
[
4
]
for
K
Lipschitz,
[
25
]
for
K
bounded, and
[
6
]
for
K
singular.
Uniform-in-time estimates cannot be expected in general: indeed, the mean-field equation (
1.8
) may admit multiple invariant measures while the
N
-particle system is uniquely ergodic. Yet, uniform-in-time estimates have recently been obtained in various settings for which the mean-field equilibrium is unique
[
4
,
28
,
12
,
18
,
26
,
10
]
:
in particular, for
K
Lipschitz and
\kappa
small enough, the following optimal estimate was obtained in
[
26
]
, for all
1\leq m\leq N
and
t\geq 0
,

(1.9)

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{2}(F^{N,m}_{t},\mu_{t}^{\otimes m})\lesssim\frac{m}{N},

where
\mathcal{W}_{2}
is the
2
-Wasserstein distance.
Such a uniform-in-time estimate does not only rely on the propagation of initial chaos, but also requires some mechanism to ensure that correlations created by particle interactions are damped over time: this is typically a consequence of ergodic properties of the linearized mean-field operator, which indeed drives the correlation dynamics. On this matter, we also refer to
[
4
,
3
]
, where the damping of initial correlations is captured explicitly.

While propagation of chaos (
1.9
) amounts to estimating correlations in the particle system, it is natural to further investigate the size of higher-order correlations: in turn, this provides a hierarchy of higher-order corrections to the mean-field approximation (
1.7
) (see Bogolyubov correction in
[
15
,
2
]
and higher orders in
[
23
]
).
The two-particle correlation function is simply defined as

(1.10)

G^{N,2}\,:=\,F^{N,2}-(F^{N,1})^{\otimes 2},

which captures the defect to propagation of chaos at the level of two-particle statistics. Higher-order correlation functions
\{G^{N,m}\}_{2\leq m\leq N}
describe finer deviations; their definition is postponed to Section
2
. Formal considerations lead to expect the following scaling,

(1.11)

G^{N,m}=O(N^{1-m}),\qquad 2\leq m\leq N,

up to some dependence on
m
.
In recent years, such estimates have been established in various settings
[
15
,
23
]
, also uniformly in time
[
2
,
33
,
3
]
.

An advantage of uniform-in-time estimates such as (
1.9
), or (
1.11
), is that they allow to connect mean-field approximation and relaxation to equilibrium; see Figure
1
below. Let us briefly recall what is known regarding relaxation to equilibrium, both at the level of the mean-field dynamics and of the
N
-particle system.

—

For the mean-field dynamics (
1.8
), if
K
is bounded and if
\kappa
is small enough, it is well known that there is a unique steady state
M
and that the solution
\mu_{t}

converges exponentially,

\mathcal{W}_{2}(\mu_{t},M)+\|\mu_{t}-M\|_{L^{2}(M^{-1})}\,\leq\,Ce^{-c_{0}t},

see e.g.
[
4
]
or Lemma
2.1
(i) below.
In the special case of a force kernel
K
deriving from a potential,
K(x,y)=-\nabla W(x-y)
,
the steady state can be characterized as the solution of the fixed-point mean-field Gibbs equation

(1.12)

M\,=\,Z[M]^{-1}e^{-\beta H[M]},\qquad H[M](x,v):=\tfrac{1}{2}|v|^{2}+A(x)+\kappa W\ast M(x),

where
Z[M]\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
is the normalization constant ensuring
\int_{\mathbb{X}}M=1
.
Note that this equation has indeed a unique solution provided
\kappa\beta\|W\|_{\operatorname{L}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}<1
.

—

At the level of the
N
-particle system, for fixed
N
, the existence and uniqueness of the steady state
M^{N}
for the Liouville equation (
1.4
) follows e.g. from
[
31
]
(without any smallness condition on
\kappa
). Although not explicit in general, it reduces to the usual Gibbs equilibrium in the case
K(x,y)=-\nabla W(x-y)
,

(1.13)

\displaystyle\quad M^{N}:=(Z^{N})^{-1}e^{-\beta H^{N}},\qquad H^{N}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{N}):=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\Big(\tfrac{1}{2}|v_{i}|^{2}+A(x_{i})+\tfrac{\kappa}{2N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}W(x_{i}-x_{j})\Big),

where
Z^{N}
is the normalization constant. Similarly as for marginals of
F^{N}
, marginals of
M^{N}
will be denoted by
\{M^{N,m}\}_{1\leq m\leq N}
.
For
K
Lipschitz and for
\kappa
small enough, the uniform-in-
N
exponential relaxation of the Liouville solution
F^{N}
toward equilibrium
M^{N}
was first established in
[
4
, Remark 13]
using coupling techniques: specifically, for all
1\leq m\leq N
and
t\geq 0
,

(1.14)

\mathcal{W}_{2}(F^{N,m}_{t},M^{N,m})\,\leq\,Ce^{-c_{0}t}\mathcal{W}_{2}(\mu_{\circ}^{\otimes m},M^{N,m})\,\leq\,Cm^{\frac{1}{2}}e^{-c_{0}t},

for some constants
C,c_{0}>0
independent of
N,m
.
Hypocoercivity techniques
[
32
]
ensure that the same must hold in
L^{2}
norms with equilibrium weight, but with constants a priori depending on
N
.
More recently, based on uniform-in-
N
log-Sobolev and Poincaré inequalities, it was shown in
[
28
,
20
]
that exponential relaxation in weighted
L^{2}
norms and in relative entropy actually holds with a time constant
c_{0}
independent of
N
, but a priori still with an
N
-dependent prefactor: more precisely, for all
1\leq m\leq N
and
t\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\textstyle\int_{\mathbb{X}^{m}}F_{t}^{N,m}\log({F_{t}^{N,m}}/{M^{N,m}})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle CNe^{-c_{0}t},

(1.15)

\displaystyle\|F_{t}^{N,m}-M^{N,m}\|_{L^{2}((M^{-1})^{\otimes m})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C^{N}e^{-c_{0}t}.

These estimates guarantee relaxation in relative entropy only for
t\gg\log N
, and in weighted
L^{2}
norms only for
t\gg N
, in contrast with the
\mathcal{W}_{2}
-estimate (
1.14
) for
t\gg 1
. We will show in the sequel how to improve upon these results and remove the
N
-dependent prefactors.
Also note that, to the best of our knowledge, no result was available on the uniform exponential relaxation when the interaction kernel
K
is not Lipschitz — a case that we will also address here for the first time.
Yet, the aforementioned works
[
4
,
28
,
20
]
have significantly relaxed the smallness condition on
\kappa
, which is not accessible to our methods (see however Section
1.3
).

1.1.
Main results

In this paper, we revisit the above questions regarding propagation of chaos, correlation estimates, and Gibbs relaxation.
First, we show that, for
\kappa
small enough, we can slightly improve previous results on those matters by a direct use of the BBGKY hierarchy, combined with bootstrap arguments as in
[
33
]
and with standard hypocoercivity estimates.
We use
L^{2}
norms with equilibrium weight
\omega\simeq M^{-1}
,

(1.16)

\displaystyle\|h\|_{L^{2}(\omega^{\otimes m})}:=\Big(\int_{\mathbb{X}^{m}}\omega^{\otimes m}|h|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}},\qquad\omega(x,v):=\exp\big(\beta(\tfrac{1}{2}|v|^{2}+A(x))\big).

More precisely, our first result establishes: (i) uniform-in-time correlation estimates and (ii) uniform-in-
N
Gibbs relaxation.
Item (i) is similar to the recent work
[
33
]
for the overdamped dynamics, which is easily extended here using hypocoercivity.
Item (ii) improves on previous work on the topic
[
4

28

20
]
for small
\kappa

K
and it holds in

norms uniformly in

-dependent prefactor in (

1.2.
Overdamped setting

The above results are easily adapted to the overdamped Langevin dynamics, that is, to the following system of coupled SDEs: for
1\leq i\leq N
,

(1.23)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\mathrm{d}X^{i,N}_{t}=\tfrac{\kappa}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}K(X^{i,N}_{t},X_{t}^{j,N})\,\mathrm{d}t-\nabla A(X_{t}^{i,N})\mathrm{d}t+\sqrt{2}\mathrm{d}B_{t}^{i},\qquad t\geq 0,\\[5.69054pt]
X^{i,N}_{t}|_{t=0}=X^{i,N}_{\circ},\end{array}\right.

where
(X^{i,N})_{1\leq i\leq N}
is now the set of particle positions in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
where
\{B^{i}\}_{i}
are independent and identically distributed
d
-dimensional Brownian motions,
where
K,A
are as in (
1.2
)–(
1.3
), and where as before we shall take
\kappa>0
to be small enough (independently of
N
).
In terms of a probability density
F^{N}
on the
N
-particle space
(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{N}
, the corresponding Liouville equation reads

(1.24)

\partial_{t}F^{N}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\operatorname{div}}_{x_{i}}\big((\nabla_{x_{i}}+\nabla A(x_{i}))F^{N}\big)-\kappa\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\operatorname{div}}_{x_{i}}\bigg(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}K(x_{i},x_{j})F^{N}\bigg),

and we assume that the distribution is initially tensorized, for some
\mu_{\circ}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

(1.25)

F_{t}^{N}|_{t=0}=\mu_{\circ}^{\otimes N}.

In this case, the propagation of chaos implies the mean-field limit

(1.26)

\displaystyle F^{N,m}_{t}\to\mu_{t}^{\otimes m},\qquad\text{as $N\uparrow\infty$},

where
\mu_{t}
is now the solution of the McKean-Vlasov equation

(1.27)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{t}\mu={\operatorname{div}}((\nabla+\nabla A)\mu)-\kappa{\operatorname{div}}_{x}((K\ast\mu)\mu),\qquad t\geq 0,\\
\mu|_{t=0}=\mu_{\circ}.\end{array}\right.

Our analysis is easily repeated in this setting, up to adapting the notation accordingly: in particular, we now set

(1.28)

\mathbb{X}=\mathbb{R}^{d},\qquad\|h\|_{L^{2}(\omega^{\otimes m})}=\Big(\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{m}}\omega^{\otimes m}|h|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}},\qquad\omega(x)=\exp(A(x)).

1.3.
Beyond weak interactions

The proof of Theorem
1.2
proceeds by a detailed analysis of the BBGKY hierarchy and relies on two ingredients: ergodic estimates for mean-field operators and uniform-in-time correlation estimates. Our approach also uses multiple bootstrap arguments for small
\kappa
, but this can somehow be avoided: rather using direct inductions, the same results could be obtained up to
O(N^{-\infty})
errors.
In our recent work
[
2
]
, building on the master equation formalism from
[
12
]
, we actually showed that, in negative Sobolev norms rather than in
L^{2}
, uniform-in-time correlation estimates can themselves be deduced from suitable ergodic estimates for mean-field operators (provided the interaction kernel is smooth). More precisely, we need the unique ergodicity of the mean-field dynamics and the relaxation of the linearized mean-field evolution (linearized at the mean-field solution). In weak norms and up to
O(N^{-\infty})
errors, we therefore expect to adapt all our arguments, without any smallness condition on
\kappa
, whenever such ergodic estimates are available.
In the torus
\mathbb{T}^{d}
with
A\equiv 0
, taking advantage of the rich literature on the McKean-Vlasov equation (
1.27
), this allows to cover the overdamped dynamics with any
\kappa\geq 0
in the following two cases:

—

Case 1:

K
is translation-invariant and conservative; more precisely,
K(x,y)=K_{0}(x-y)
for some
K_{0}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d})^{d}
that is odd and divergence-free.

—

Case 2:

K
derives from a smooth, coordinate-wise even, positive-definite potential
W
(so-called
H
-stable potential); more precisely,
K(x,y)=-\nabla W(x-y)
for some
W\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
that is even in each coordinate and has Fourier coefficients
\hat{W}(k)\geq 0
for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.
1
1

1

In fact, this non-negativity condition can even be relaxed to
1+2\kappa\inf_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\hat{W}(k)>0

Note that this is the only framework in this work for which a distinction can be drawn between attractive and repulsive interactions; see also example (
a

below.

In both cases, it can be shown that the mean-field steady state is unique and coincides with the Lebesgue measure for any
\kappa\geq 0

[

(see in particular

A suitable adaptation of our analysis then leads to the following cross error estimates in weak norms up to

(a)

Mollified Coulomb interactions:

1.3

1.5

yields a uniform-in-

in negative Sobolev norms.

(b)

Noisy Hegselmann-Krause model for opinion dynamics:

This model, introduced in

\mathbb{T}^{d}

2.1.
Mean-field relaxation

Consider the following linear Fokker-Planck operator on the weighted space
L^{2}(\omega)
,

L_{0}h\,:=\,{\operatorname{div}}_{v}((\nabla_{v}+\beta v)h)-v\cdot\nabla_{x}h+\nabla A(x)\cdot\nabla_{v}h.

The following ergodic estimates for the mean-field evolution (
1.8
) and for the Fokker-Planck semigroup
\{e^{tL_{0}}\}_{t\geq 0}
follow from standard hypocoercivity theory
[
32
,
14
]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
K\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})^{d}
and let the confining potential
A
satisfy (
1.3
). There exist
\kappa_{0},c_{0}>0
(only depending on
d,\beta,K,A
) such that the following hold for all
\kappa\in[0,\kappa_{0}]
.

(i)

Given
\mu_{\circ}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{X})\cap L^{2}(\omega)
, the solution
\mu\in C(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{X})\cap L^{2}(\omega))
of the mean-field equation (
1.8
) satisfies for all
t\geq 0
,

(2.1)

\displaystyle\|\mu_{t}-M\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}\,\lesssim\,e^{-c_{0}t}\|\mu_{\circ}-M\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}.

(ii)

For all
h\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{X})
, we have for all
t\geq 0
,

(2.2)

e^{2c_{0}t}\|\pi^{\bot}e^{tL_{0}}h\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{2c_{0}s}\|(\nabla_{v}+\beta v)e^{sL_{0}}h\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\,\lesssim\,\|\pi^{\bot}h\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2},

where
\pi^{\bot}
stands for the orthogonal projection onto
\operatorname{Ker}(L_{0})^{\bot}
,

\pi^{\bot}:=\operatorname{Id}-\pi,\qquad\pi h:=M_{0}\int_{\mathbb{X}}h,\qquad M_{0}:=\tfrac{\omega^{-1}}{\int_{\mathbb{X}}\omega^{-1}}.

In particular, by duality, for all
g\in L^{2}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\mathbb{R}_{+};L^{2}(\mathbb{X})^{d})
,

(2.3)

\Big\|\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)L_{0}}{\operatorname{div}}_{v}(g(s))\,\mathrm{d}s\Big\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}\,\lesssim\,\int_{0}^{t}e^{-2c_{0}(t-s)}\|g(s)\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s.

Proof.

We start with a brief discussion of item (ii).
By the hypocoercivity techniques in
[
14
, Theorem 10]
(see Remark
2.2
below),
there is
c_{0}>0
(only depending on
d,\beta,A
) such that for all
t\geq 0
,

(2.4)

\|\pi^{\bot}e^{tL_{0}}h\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}\,\lesssim\,e^{-c_{0}t}\|\pi^{\bot}h\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}.

Using the energy identity for
e^{tL_{0}}h
,

\partial_{t}\|\pi^{\bot}e^{tL_{0}}h\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}+2\|(\nabla_{v}+\beta v)e^{tL_{0}}h\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}\leq 0,

we may then deduce from (
2.4
),

\partial_{t}\Big(e^{c_{0}t}\|\pi^{\bot}e^{tL_{0}}h\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}\Big)+2e^{c_{0}t}\|(\nabla_{v}+\beta v)e^{tL_{0}}h\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}\leq c_{0}e^{c_{0}t}\|\pi^{\bot}e^{tL_{0}}h\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}\lesssim e^{-c_{0}t}\|\pi^{\bot}h\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2},

and thus after integration,

(2.5)

e^{c_{0}t}\|\pi^{\bot}e^{tL_{0}}h\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}+2\int_{0}^{t}e^{c_{0}s}\|(\nabla_{v}+\beta v)e^{sL_{0}}h\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\,\lesssim\,\|\pi^{\bot}h\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2},

that is, (
2.2
) (up to renaming
c_{0}
).
Next, by duality and integration by parts, we find

\displaystyle\Big\|\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)L_{0}}{\operatorname{div}}_{v}(g(s))\,\mathrm{d}s\,\Big\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup\bigg\{\int_{0}^{t}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{X}}\omega he^{(t-s)L_{0}}{\operatorname{div}}_{v}(g(s))\Big)\mathrm{d}s:\|h\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}\leq 1\bigg\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup\bigg\{\int_{0}^{t}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{X}}\omega g(s)\cdot(\nabla_{v}+\beta v)e^{(t-s)L_{0}^{*}}h\Big)\,\mathrm{d}s:\|h\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}\leq 1\bigg\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big(\int_{0}^{t}e^{-c_{0}(t-s)}\|g(s)\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\sup\bigg\{\Big(\int_{0}^{t}e^{c_{0}s}\|(\nabla_{v}+\beta v)e^{sL_{0}^{*}}h\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}:\|h\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}\leq 1\bigg\}.

Noting that (
2.5
) also holds for
L_{0}
replaced by its adjoint
L_{0}^{*}
on
L^{2}(\omega)
, the conclusion (
2.3
) follows (up to renaming
c_{0}
again).

For completeness, we include a short proof of item (i) as a consequence of (ii) for small
\kappa
. Let
\mu,\mu^{\prime}\in C(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{X})\cap\operatorname{L}^{2}(\omega))
satisfy the mean-field equation (
1.8
) with initial data
\mu_{\circ},\mu_{\circ}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{X})\cap\operatorname{L}^{2}(\omega)
. Treating interactions perturbatively, Duhamel’s formula for (
1.8
) yields

\mu_{t}=e^{tL_{0}}\mu_{\circ}-\kappa\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)L_{0}}\big((K\ast\mu_{s})\cdot\nabla_{v}\mu_{s}\big)\mathrm{d}s,

and thus, applying the projection
\pi^{\bot}
, taking the norm, and applying the estimates of item (ii) for the Fokker-Planck semigroup,

\displaystyle\|\pi^{\bot}\mu_{t}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle e^{-2c_{0}t}\|\pi^{\bot}\mu_{\circ}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}+\kappa^{2}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-2c_{0}(t-s)}\|\mu_{s}(K\ast\mu_{s})\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle e^{-2c_{0}t}\|\pi^{\bot}\mu_{\circ}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}+\kappa^{2}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-2c_{0}(t-s)}\|\mu_{s}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}\mathrm{d}s.

Since
\mu=\pi^{\bot}\mu+M_{0}
, this yields

\|\mu_{t}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}\,\leq\,C\|\mu_{\circ}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}+C\kappa^{2}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-2c_{0}(t-s)}\|\mu_{s}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}\mathrm{d}s.

For
\kappa
small enough so that
C\kappa^{2}\leq c_{0}
,
we deduce by Gronwall’s inequality, for all
t\geq 0
,

(2.6)

\|\mu_{t}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}\,\lesssim\,\|\mu_{\circ}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}.

Let us now examine the difference
\mu-\mu^{\prime}
, which satisfies

(\partial_{t}-L_{0})(\mu-\mu^{\prime})=-\kappa K\ast\mu^{\prime}\cdot\nabla_{v}(\mu-\mu^{\prime})-\kappa K\ast(\mu-\mu^{\prime})\cdot\nabla_{v}\mu.

Writing Duhamel’s formula for this equation, taking the norms, and using again the estimates of item (ii) with
\pi^{\bot}(\mu-\mu^{\prime})=\mu-\mu^{\prime}
, we get

\displaystyle\|\mu_{t}-\mu^{\prime}_{t}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle e^{-2c_{0}t}\|\mu_{\circ}-\mu^{\prime}_{\circ}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}+\kappa^{2}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-2c_{0}(t-s)}\Big\|(\mu_{s}-\mu_{s}^{\prime})(K\ast\mu_{s}^{\prime})+\mu_{s}(K\ast(\mu_{s}-\mu_{s}^{\prime}))\Big\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle e^{-2c_{0}t}\|\mu_{\circ}-\mu^{\prime}_{\circ}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}+\kappa^{2}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-2c_{0}(t-s)}\big(1+\|\mu_{s}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}\big)^{2}\|\mu_{s}-\mu_{s}^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}\mathrm{d}s.

Using the above a priori bound (
2.6
) on
\mu
, Gronwall’s inequality yields for
\kappa
small enough, for all
t\geq 0
,

\|\mu_{t}-\mu^{\prime}_{t}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}\,\lesssim\,e^{-c_{0}t}\|\mu_{\circ}-\mu^{\prime}_{\circ}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}.

From this contraction property, we find that there is a unique steady state
M
, that it belongs to
L^{2}(\omega)
, and the conclusion (i) follows.

Remark 2.2
.

For completeness, we emphasize that the assumptions in
[
14
]
are indeed satisfied for the operator
L_{0}
on
L^{2}(\omega)
with

satisfying (

), so we can apply their hypocoercivity results and deduce (
2.4
) above.
Assumption (H1) in
[
14
]
is simply the Gaussian Poincaré inequality, and assumption (H3) is trivial for kinetic equations. Next, as
A
is uniformly convex, we recall that the probability measure
\propto e^{-A}
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
satisfies a Poincaré inequality, see e.g.
[
32
, Section A.19]
. By
[
13
, Lemma 3]
, this ensures the validity of Assumption (H2) in
[
14
]
. Finally, given
w\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, for the solution
u
of

e^{-A}u-{\operatorname{div}}(e^{-A}\nabla u)=e^{-A}w,\qquad\text{in $\mathbb{R}^{d}$},

we find by multiple integrations by parts, using the summation convention on repeated indices,

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{2}u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(e^{-A})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\nabla_{j}u)\nabla_{i}(e^{-A}\nabla_{ij}^{2}u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\nabla_{j}u)\nabla_{ij}^{2}(e^{-A}\nabla_{i}u)-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\nabla_{j}u)\nabla_{i}(e^{-A}(\nabla_{j}A)\nabla_{i}u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\nabla_{j}u)(\nabla_{j}+\nabla_{j}A)\nabla_{i}(e^{-A}\nabla_{i}u)-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\nabla_{i}u)(\nabla_{j}u)(\nabla_{ij}^{2}A)e^{-A}.

Hence, using the equation for
u
in form of
\nabla_{i}(e^{-A}\nabla_{i}u)=e^{-A}(u-w)
, and recalling the uniform convexity of

,

\|\nabla^{2}u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(e^{-A})}\,\leq\,-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-A}(\nabla_{j}u)\nabla_{j}(u-w)\,\leq\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-A}(\nabla_{j}u)(\nabla_{j}w).

Further integrating by parts and using the equation for
u
, we get

\|\nabla^{2}u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(e^{-A})}\,\leq\,-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}w\nabla_{j}(e^{-A}\nabla_{j}u)\,=\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}we^{-A}(w-u)\\
\,\lesssim\,\|w\|_{L^{2}(e^{-A})}^{2}+\|u\|_{L^{2}(e^{-A})}^{2}\,\lesssim\,\|w\|_{L^{2}(e^{-A})}^{2}.

By
[
14
, Lemma 4]
, this proves the validity of Assumption (H4) in
[
14
]
.

1
. Indeed, it suffices to consider the norm

\operatorname{|\hskip-1.13809pt|\hskip-1.13809pt|}h\operatorname{|\hskip-1.13809pt|\hskip-1.13809pt|}_{B}\,:=\,\sup_{s\geq 0}e^{-\omega s}\|T_{s}h\|_{B},

which obviously satisfies, for all
h\in B
,

\|h\|_{B}\leq\operatorname{|\hskip-1.13809pt|\hskip-1.13809pt|}h\operatorname{|\hskip-1.13809pt|\hskip-1.13809pt|}_{B}\leq P\|h\|_{B},\qquad\operatorname{|\hskip-1.13809pt|\hskip-1.13809pt|}T_{t}h\operatorname{|\hskip-1.13809pt|\hskip-1.13809pt|}_{B}\leq e^{\omega t}\operatorname{|\hskip-1.13809pt|\hskip-1.13809pt|}h\operatorname{|\hskip-1.13809pt|\hskip-1.13809pt|}_{B}.

Applying this observation to our setting in (
2.4
) above, with
L_{0}
replaced by its adjoint
L_{0}^{*}
, we can similarly construct a norm
\operatorname{|\hskip-1.13809pt|\hskip-1.13809pt|}\cdot\operatorname{|\hskip-1.13809pt|\hskip-1.13809pt|}_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{*}
on
L^{2}(\omega)
such that

(2.7)

\|h\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}\leq\operatorname{|\hskip-1.13809pt|\hskip-1.13809pt|}h\operatorname{|\hskip-1.13809pt|\hskip-1.13809pt|}_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{*}\lesssim\|h\|_{L^{2}(\omega)},\qquad\operatorname{|\hskip-1.13809pt|\hskip-1.13809pt|}\pi^{\bot}e^{tL_{0}^{*}}h\operatorname{|\hskip-1.13809pt|\hskip-1.13809pt|}_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{*}\leq e^{-c_{0}t}\operatorname{|\hskip-1.13809pt|\hskip-1.13809pt|}\pi^{\bot}h\operatorname{|\hskip-1.13809pt|\hskip-1.13809pt|}_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{*},

where we used that the orthogonal projection
\pi^{\bot}
commutes with the semigroup.
For
m\geq 1
, we also define
\operatorname{|\hskip-1.13809pt|\hskip-1.13809pt|}\cdot\operatorname{|\hskip-1.13809pt|\hskip-1.13809pt|}_{L^{2}(\omega^{\otimes m})}
as the associated injective cross norm on
\operatorname{L}^{2}(\omega^{\otimes m})
,

(2.8)

\operatorname{|\hskip-1.13809pt|\hskip-1.13809pt|}h\operatorname{|\hskip-1.13809pt|\hskip-1.13809pt|}_{L^{2}(\omega^{\otimes m})}\,:=\,\sup\bigg\{\int_{\mathbb{X}^{m}}\omega^{\otimes m}(\phi_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes\phi_{m})h\,:\,\operatorname{|\hskip-1.13809pt|\hskip-1.13809pt|}\phi_{1}\operatorname{|\hskip-1.13809pt|\hskip-1.13809pt|}_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{*}=\ldots=\operatorname{|\hskip-1.13809pt|\hskip-1.13809pt|}\phi_{m}\operatorname{|\hskip-1.13809pt|\hskip-1.13809pt|}_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{*}=1\bigg\},

which is of course also Lipschitz equivalent to the standard norm on
L^{2}(\omega^{\otimes m})
.

2.2.
BBGKY hierarchy for marginals

In order to formulate the BBGKY hierarchy in a more convenient way, we first introduce some short-hand notation.

Definition 2.4
.

Consider a collection
\{h^{m}\}_{1\leq m\leq N}
of functions
h^{m}:\mathbb{X}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}
such that for all
m
the function
h^{m}
is symmetric in its
m
entries (such as
\{F^{N,m}\}_{1\leq m\leq N}
).

—

For
1\leq m\leq N
and
P\subset\llbracket m\rrbracket
with
P\neq\varnothing
, we define
h^{P}:\mathbb{X}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}
as

h^{P}(z_{\llbracket m\rrbracket}):=h^{\sharp P}(z_{P})
, where
\sharp P
is the cardinality of
P
,
and for
P=\varnothing
we set
h^{\varnothing}:=0
(as
h^{m}
is not defined for
m<1
).

—

For
P\subset\llbracket m\rrbracket
with
P\neq\llbracket N\rrbracket
, we define
h^{P\cup\{*\}}:\mathbb{X}^{m+1}\to\mathbb{R}
as

h^{P\cup\{*\}}(z_{\llbracket m\rrbracket},z_{*}):=h^{\sharp P+1}(z_{P},z_{*})
,
and for
P=\llbracket N\rrbracket
we set
h^{\llbracket N\rrbracket\cup\{*\}}=0
(as
h^{m}
is not defined for
m>N
).

—

For
P\subset\llbracket m\rrbracket
and
k,\ell\in P
, we define the operators

\displaystyle S_{k,\ell}h^{P}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle-K(x_{k},x_{\ell})\cdot\nabla_{v_{k}}h^{P},

\displaystyle H_{k}h^{P\cup\{*\}}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{X}}K(x_{k},x_{*})\cdot\nabla_{v_{k}}h^{P\cup\{*\}}\,\mathrm{d}z_{*}.

With this notation at hand, taking partial integrals in the Liouville equation (
1.4
), we are led to the following BBGKY hierarchy of coupled equations for marginals: for all
1\leq m\leq N
,

(2.9)

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}-L_{0}^{(m)})F^{N,\llbracket m\rrbracket}=\kappa\frac{N-m}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{m}H_{k}F^{N,\llbracket m\rrbracket\cup\{*\}}+\frac{\kappa}{N}\sum_{k,\ell=1}^{m}S_{k,\ell}F^{N,\llbracket m\rrbracket},

where the operator
L_{0}^{(m)}
stands for the Kronecker sum of the Fokker-Planck operator
L_{0}
on the
m
-particle space
L^{2}(\omega^{\otimes m})
,

(2.10)

L_{0}^{(m)}:=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\Big(\operatorname{Id}^{\otimes m-j}\otimes L_{0}\otimes\operatorname{Id}^{\otimes j-1}\Big).

In the long-time limit
t\uparrow\infty
, as the
N
-particle density
F^{N}
converges to equilibrium
M^{N}
, we find that the equilibrium marginals
\{M^{N,m}\}_{1\leq m\leq N}
are a stationary solution of the same hierarchy (
2.9
).

2.3.
Gibbs relaxation

We show that the uniform-in-
N
exponential relaxation to Gibbs equilibrium in
L^{2}
can be obtained as a direct estimate on the BBGKY hierarchy (
2.9
), using hypocoercivity in form of the ergodic estimates of Lemma
2.1
.

Proof of Proposition
1.1
(ii).

The proof is based on the analysis of the BBGKY hierarchy (
2.9
), restricted on the subspace where we can use hypocoercivity of the
m
-particle Fokker-Planck operator simultaneously in all
m
directions.
For that purpose, we consider the following orthogonal projections on
L^{2}(\omega^{\otimes m})
, for
P\subset\llbracket m\rrbracket
,

(2.11)

\pi_{P}^{\bot}:=\prod_{k\in P}\pi_{k}^{\bot},\qquad\pi_{k}^{\bot}=\operatorname{Id}-\pi_{k},\qquad\pi_{k}h^{\llbracket m\rrbracket}=M_{0}(z_{k})\int_{\mathbb{X}}h^{\llbracket m\rrbracket}\,\mathrm{d}z_{k}.

We split the proof into two steps.

Step
1. Tensorization of (
2.3
): for all
m\geq 1
,
g_{1},\ldots,g_{m}\in L^{2}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\mathbb{R}_{+};L^{2}(\mathbb{X})^{d})
, and
t\geq 0

(2.12)

\operatorname{\Big|\hskip-1.13809pt\Big|\hskip-1.13809pt\Big|}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)L_{0}^{(m)}}\pi_{\llbracket m\rrbracket\setminus\{k\}}^{\bot}{\operatorname{div}}_{v_{k}}(g_{k}(s))\,\mathrm{d}s\operatorname{\Big|\hskip-1.13809pt\Big|\hskip-1.13809pt\Big|}_{L^{2}(\omega^{\otimes m})}^{2}\,\lesssim\,\sum_{k=1}^{m}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-2mc_{0}(t-s)}\operatorname{|\hskip-1.13809pt|\hskip-1.13809pt|}g_{k}(s)\operatorname{|\hskip-1.13809pt|\hskip-1.13809pt|}_{L^{2}(\omega^{\otimes m})}^{2}\mathrm{d}s,

where we emphasize that the multiplicative constant only depends on
d,\beta,K,A
(not on
m
).
This is a direct extension of (
2.3
), where we note that the use of the equivalent norm defined in (
2.8
) allows to have a multiplicative factor independent of
m
.
We include a short proof for completeness. By definition (
2.8
) of the modified norms on the product, integrating by parts, and using that

e^{tL_{0}^{(m)}}=(e^{tL_{0}})^{\otimes m},

we find

\operatorname{\Big|\hskip-1.13809pt\Big|\hskip-1.13809pt\Big|}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)L_{0}^{(m)}}\pi_{\llbracket m\rrbracket\setminus\{k\}}^{\bot}{\operatorname{div}}_{v_{k}}(g_{k}(s))\,\mathrm{d}s\operatorname{\Big|\hskip-1.13809pt\Big|\hskip-1.13809pt\Big|}_{L^{2}(\omega^{\otimes m})}\\
=\sup_{\phi_{1},\ldots,\phi_{m}}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{X}^{m}}\omega^{\otimes m}g_{k}(s)\,\Big(\big((\nabla_{v_{k}}+\beta v_{k})e^{(t-s)L_{0}^{*}}\phi_{k}\big)\otimes\bigotimes_{\ell:\ell\neq k}\big(e^{(t-s)L_{0}^{*}}\pi_{\ell}^{\bot}\phi_{\ell}\big)\Big)\,\mathrm{d}s,

where the supremum runs over
\phi_{1},\ldots,\phi_{m}\in L^{2}(\omega)
with
\operatorname{|\hskip-1.13809pt|\hskip-1.13809pt|}\phi_{k}\operatorname{|\hskip-1.13809pt|\hskip-1.13809pt|}_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{*}=1
for all
1\leq k\leq m
. By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, this can be bounded as follows,

\operatorname{\Big|\hskip-1.13809pt\Big|\hskip-1.13809pt\Big|}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)L_{0}^{(m)}}\pi_{\llbracket m\rrbracket\setminus\{k\}}^{\bot}{\operatorname{div}}_{v_{k}}(g_{k}(s))\,\mathrm{d}s\operatorname{\Big|\hskip-1.13809pt\Big|\hskip-1.13809pt\Big|}_{L^{2}(\omega^{\otimes m})}^{2}\leq\bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{m}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-2mc_{0}(t-s)}\operatorname{|\hskip-1.13809pt|\hskip-1.13809pt|}g_{k}(s)\operatorname{|\hskip-1.13809pt|\hskip-1.13809pt|}^{2}_{L^{2}(\omega^{\otimes m})}\bigg)\\
\times\sup_{\phi_{1},\ldots,\phi_{m}}\bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{m}\int_{0}^{t}e^{2mc_{0}(t-s)}\Big(\operatorname{\big|\hskip-1.13809pt\big|\hskip-1.13809pt\big|}(\nabla_{v_{k}}+\beta v_{k})e^{(t-s)L_{0}^{*}}\phi_{k}\operatorname{\big|\hskip-1.13809pt\big|\hskip-1.13809pt\big|}_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{*}\prod_{\ell:\ell\neq k}\operatorname{|\hskip-1.13809pt|\hskip-1.13809pt|}e^{(t-s)L_{0}^{*}}\pi_{\ell}^{\bot}\phi_{\ell}\operatorname{\big|\hskip-1.13809pt\big|\hskip-1.13809pt\big|}_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{*}\Big)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\bigg).

Now appealing to (
2.7
) (with constant
1
) and to (
2.2
) (with
L_{0}
replaced by
L_{0}^{*}
and using the equivalence of norms (
2.7
)), the claim (
2.12
) follows.

Step
2. Conclusion.

From the BBGKY equations (
2.9
) for marginals, recalling that marginals of the Gibbs equilibrium satisfy the stationary version of the same equations, we find for all
1\leq m\leq N
,

(\partial_{t}-L_{0}^{(m)})(F^{N,m}-M^{N,m})=\kappa\frac{N-m}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{m}H_{k}\big(F^{N,\llbracket m\rrbracket\cup\{*\}}-M^{N,\llbracket m\rrbracket\cup\{*\}}\big)\\[-11.38109pt]
+\frac{\kappa}{N}\sum_{k,\ell=1}^{m}S_{k,\ell}\big(F^{N,\llbracket m\rrbracket}-M^{N,\llbracket m\rrbracket}\big).

Applying the projection
\pi_{\llbracket m\rrbracket}^{\bot}
to both sides of this equation, noting that it commutes with
L_{0}^{(m)}
,
writing the Duhamel formula, and taking the norm, we get

\operatorname{\big|\hskip-1.13809pt\big|\hskip-1.13809pt\big|}\pi_{\llbracket m\rrbracket}^{\bot}(F^{N,m}_{t}-M^{N,m})\operatorname{\big|\hskip-1.13809pt\big|\hskip-1.13809pt\big|}_{L^{2}(\omega^{\otimes m})}\leq\operatorname{\big|\hskip-1.13809pt\big|\hskip-1.13809pt\big|}e^{tL_{0}^{(m)}}\pi_{\llbracket m\rrbracket}^{\bot}(F_{\circ}^{N,m}-M^{N,m})\operatorname{\big|\hskip-1.13809pt\big|\hskip-1.13809pt\big|}_{L^{2}(\omega^{\otimes m})}\\
+\kappa\operatorname{\Big|\hskip-1.13809pt\Big|\hskip-1.13809pt\Big|}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)L_{0}^{(m)}}\pi_{\llbracket m\rrbracket}^{\bot}H_{k}\big(F_{s}^{N,\llbracket m\rrbracket\cup\{*\}}-M^{N,\llbracket m\rrbracket\cup\{*\}}\big)\mathrm{d}s\operatorname{\Big|\hskip-1.13809pt\Big|\hskip-1.13809pt\Big|}_{L^{2}(\omega^{\otimes m})}\\
+\frac{\kappa}{N}\operatorname{\Big|\hskip-1.13809pt\Big|\hskip-1.13809pt\Big|}\sum_{k,\ell=1}^{m}\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)L_{0}^{(m)}}\pi_{\llbracket m\rrbracket}^{\bot}S_{k,\ell}\big(F_{s}^{N,\llbracket m\rrbracket}-M^{N,\llbracket m\rrbracket}\big)\mathrm{d}s\operatorname{\Big|\hskip-1.13809pt\Big|\hskip-1.13809pt\Big|}_{L^{2}(\omega^{\otimes m})}.

Using (
2.7
) to bound the first term, recalling the definition of
S_{k,\ell},H_{k}
, appealing to the result (
2.12
) of Step 1 to bound the last two terms, and using exchangeability, we are led to

(2.13)

\operatorname{\big|\hskip-1.13809pt\big|\hskip-1.13809pt\big|}\pi_{\llbracket m\rrbracket}^{\bot}(F_{t}^{N,m}-M^{N,m})\operatorname{\big|\hskip-1.13809pt\big|\hskip-1.13809pt\big|}_{L^{2}(\omega^{\otimes m})}^{2}\,\leq\,Ce^{-2mc_{0}t}\operatorname{\big|\hskip-1.13809pt\big|\hskip-1.13809pt\big|}\pi_{\llbracket m\rrbracket}^{\bot}(F_{\circ}^{N,m}-M^{N,m})\operatorname{\big|\hskip-1.13809pt\big|\hskip-1.13809pt\big|}_{L^{2}(\omega^{\otimes m})}^{2}\\
+C\kappa^{2}\int_{0}^{t}me^{-2mc_{0}(t-s)}\operatorname{\big|\hskip-1.13809pt\big|\hskip-1.13809pt\big|}\pi_{\llbracket m-1\rrbracket}^{\bot}(F_{s}^{N,m+1}-M^{N,m+1})\operatorname{\big|\hskip-1.13809pt\big|\hskip-1.13809pt\big|}_{L^{2}(\omega^{\otimes m+1})}^{2}\mathrm{d}s\\
+\frac{C\kappa^{2}m^{2}}{N^{2}}\int_{0}^{t}me^{-2mc_{0}(t-s)}\operatorname{\big|\hskip-1.13809pt\big|\hskip-1.13809pt\big|}\pi^{\bot}_{\llbracket m-2\rrbracket}(F_{s}^{N,m}-M^{N,m})\operatorname{\big|\hskip-1.13809pt\big|\hskip-1.13809pt\big|}_{L^{2}(\omega^{\otimes m})}^{2}\mathrm{d}s.

Noting that

\pi_{\llbracket m-2\rrbracket}^{\bot}F^{N,\llbracket m\rrbracket}=\pi_{\llbracket m\rrbracket}^{\bot}F^{N,\llbracket m\rrbracket}+M_{0}(z_{m-1})\pi_{\llbracket m\rrbracket\setminus\{m-1\}}^{\bot}F^{N,\llbracket m\rrbracket\setminus\{m-1\}}\\
+M_{0}(z_{m})\pi_{\llbracket m-1\rrbracket}^{\bot}F^{N,\llbracket m-1\rrbracket}-M_{0}(z_{m-1})M_{0}(z_{m})\pi_{\llbracket m-2\rrbracket}^{\bot}F^{N,\llbracket m-2\rrbracket},

the above becomes

\operatorname{\big|\hskip-1.13809pt\big|\hskip-1.13809pt\big|}\pi_{\llbracket m\rrbracket}^{\bot}(F_{t}^{N,m}-M^{N,m})\operatorname{\big|\hskip-1.13809pt\big|\hskip-1.13809pt\big|}_{L^{2}(\omega^{\otimes m})}^{2}\,\leq\,Ce^{-2mc_{0}t}\operatorname{\big|\hskip-1.13809pt\big|\hskip-1.13809pt\big|}\pi_{\llbracket m\rrbracket}^{\bot}(F_{\circ}^{N,m}-M^{N,m})\operatorname{\big|\hskip-1.13809pt\big|\hskip-1.13809pt\big|}_{L^{2}(\omega^{\otimes m})}^{2}\\
+C\kappa^{2}\int_{0}^{t}me^{-2mc_{0}(t-s)}\Big(\sum_{n=m-2}^{m+1}\operatorname{\big|\hskip-1.13809pt\big|\hskip-1.13809pt\big|}\pi_{\llbracket n\rrbracket}^{\bot}(F_{s}^{N,n}-M^{N,n})\operatorname{\big|\hskip-1.13809pt\big|\hskip-1.13809pt\big|}_{L^{2}(\omega^{\otimes n})}^{2}\Big)\,\mathrm{d}s.

From here, we conclude by a bootstrap argument. Given
C_{0},T_{0}>0
, assume that we have for all
1\leq m\leq N
and
t\in[0,T_{0}]
,

(2.14)

\operatorname{\big|\hskip-1.13809pt\big|\hskip-1.13809pt\big|}\pi^{\bot}_{\llbracket m\rrbracket}(F^{N,\llbracket m\rrbracket}_{t}-M^{N,\llbracket m\rrbracket})\operatorname{\big|\hskip-1.13809pt\big|\hskip-1.13809pt\big|}_{L^{2}(\omega^{\otimes m})}^{2}\leq C_{0}^{m}e^{-c_{0}t}.

Inserting this into the above and using that
\int_{0}^{t}me^{-2mc_{0}(t-s)}e^{-c_{0}s}\mathrm{d}s\leq c_{0}^{-1}e^{-c_{0}t}
, we would then deduce for all
1\leq m\leq N
and
t\in[0,T_{0}]
,

\operatorname{\big|\hskip-1.13809pt\big|\hskip-1.13809pt\big|}\pi_{\llbracket m\rrbracket}^{\bot}\big(F_{t}^{N,\llbracket m\rrbracket}-M^{N,\llbracket m\rrbracket}\big)\operatorname{\big|\hskip-1.13809pt\big|\hskip-1.13809pt\big|}_{L^{2}(\omega^{\otimes m})}^{2}\,\leq\,Ce^{-2mc_{0}t}\operatorname{\big|\hskip-1.13809pt\big|\hskip-1.13809pt\big|}\pi_{\llbracket m\rrbracket}^{\bot}\big(F_{\circ}^{N,\llbracket m\rrbracket}-M^{N,\llbracket m\rrbracket}\big)\operatorname{\big|\hskip-1.13809pt\big|\hskip-1.13809pt\big|}_{L^{2}(\omega^{\otimes m})}^{2}\\
+C\kappa^{2}(1+C_{0}+C_{0}^{-1}+C_{0}^{-2})C_{0}^{m}e^{-c_{0}t}.

As we assume chaotic data (
1.5
),
F_{\circ}^{N,m}=\mu_{\circ}^{\otimes m}
, the first right-hand side term is bounded by
CR^{m}e^{-2mc_{0}t}
, for some constant
R
depending on
d,\beta,K,A,\|\mu_{\circ}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}
.
Choosing
C_{0}=4CR
, and
\kappa_{0}>0
small enough so that

1\leq m\leq N

. We conclude that (

) must hold with

for

∎

2.4.
Correlation functions

Recall that the two-particle correlation function
G^{N,2}
is defined to capture the defect to propagation of chaos at the level of two-particle statistics, cf. (
1.10
).
To describe finer corrections to mean field, we further consider higher-order correlation functions
\{G^{N,m}\}_{2\leq m\leq N}
, which are defined as suitable polynomial combinations of marginals of
F^{N}
in such a way that the full particle distribution
F^{N}
is recovered in form of a cluster expansion: using the notation of Definition
2.4
, for all
1\leq m\leq N
,

(2.15)

F^{N,m}\,=\,\sum_{\pi\vdash\llbracket m\rrbracket}\prod_{A\in\pi}G^{N,A},

where
\pi
runs through the list of all partitions of the index set
\llbracket m\rrbracket
, and where
A
runs through the list of blocks of the partition
\pi
,
A\subset\llbracket m\rrbracket
.
As is easily checked, correlation functions are fully determined by prescribing (
2.15
) together with the ‘maximality’ requirement

(2.16)

\int_{\mathbb{X}}G^{N,\llbracket m\rrbracket}\,\mathrm{d}z_{\ell}=0,\qquad\text{for all $1\leq\ell\leq m$ and $m\geq 2$.}

We find explicitly

\displaystyle G^{N,1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F^{N,1},

\displaystyle G^{N,2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F^{N,2}-(F^{N,1})^{\otimes 2},

\displaystyle G^{N,3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\operatorname{sym}}\big(F^{N,3}-3F^{N,2}\otimes F^{N,1}+2(F^{N,1})^{\otimes 3}\big),

\displaystyle G^{N,4}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\operatorname{sym}}\big(F^{N,4}-4F^{N,3}\otimes F^{N,1}-3F^{N,2}\otimes F^{N,2}+12F^{N,2}\otimes(F^{N,1})^{\otimes 2}-6(F^{N,1})^{\otimes 4}\big),

where the symbol ‘
{\operatorname{sym}}
’ stands for the symmetrization of coordinates, and we can write more generally, for all
1\leq m\leq N
,

(2.17)

G^{N,m}\,:=\,\sum_{\pi\vdash\llbracket m\rrbracket}(\sharp\pi-1)!(-1)^{\sharp\pi-1}\prod_{A\in\pi}F^{N,A},

where we use a similar notation as in (
2.15
) and where
\sharp\pi
stands for the number of blocks in a partition
\pi
.
Similarly, we also define equilibrium correlation functions
\{C^{N,m}\}_{1\leq m\leq N}
associated with
M^{N}
and its marginals.

2.5.
BBGKY hierarchy for correlations

Starting from the BBGKY hierarchy (
2.9
) for marginals of
F^{N}
, and recalling how marginals can be expanded in terms of correlations (
2.15
) and vice versa (
2.17
), we can derive a corresponding BBGKY hierarchy of equations for correlations.
We refer for instance to
[
23

for the derivation: straightforward but lengthy algebraic manipulations are needed to collect the different factors in this hierarchy.

Lemma 2.6

(e.g.
[
23
]
)
.

Given
K\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})^{d}
, consider a weak solution
F^{N}\in C(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mathcal{P}\cap\operatorname{L}^{1}(\mathbb{X}^{N}))
of the Liouville equation (
1.4
).
The associated correlation functions
\{G^{N,m}\}_{1\leq m\leq N}
satisfy the following hierarchy of equations: for all
1\leq m\leq N
,

(\partial_{t}-L_{0}^{(m)})G^{N,m}\,=\,\kappa\frac{N-m}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{m}H_{k}G^{N,\llbracket m\rrbracket\cup\{\ast\}}\\
-\kappa\sum_{k=1}^{m}\sum_{A\subset\llbracket m\rrbracket-\{k\}}\frac{m-1-\sharp A}{N}H_{k}\big(G^{N,A\cup\{k,\ast\}}G^{N,\llbracket m\rrbracket-\{k\}-A}\big)\\
+\kappa\frac{N-m}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\sum_{A\subset\llbracket m\rrbracket-\{k\}}H_{k}\big(G^{N,A\cup\{k\}}G^{N,\llbracket m\rrbracket\cup\{\ast\}-A-\{k\}}\big)\\

Letting

, equilibrium correlations
\{C^{N,m}\}_{1\leq m\leq N}
are a stationary solution of this hierarchy.

for

bounded. Here we give a proof of Proposition

\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}e^{c_{0}s}\operatorname{\big|\hskip-1.13809pt\big|\hskip-1.13809pt\big|}G_{s}^{N,m}-C^{N,m}\operatorname{\big|\hskip-1.13809pt\big|\hskip-1.13809pt\big|}_{L^{2}(\omega^{\otimes m})}\,\lesssim\,(Cm)^{m}N^{1-m}+\kappa\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}e^{c_{0}s}\operatorname{\big|\hskip-1.13809pt\big|\hskip-1.13809pt\big|}G_{s}^{N,m+1}-C^{N,m+1}\operatorname{\big|\hskip-1.13809pt\big|\hskip-1.13809pt\big|}_{L^{2}(\omega^{\otimes m+1})}\\
+\kappa\sum_{\ell=2}^{m-1}\binom{m-1}{\ell-1}(C\ell)^{\ell}N^{1-\ell}\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}e^{c_{0}s}\operatorname{\big|\hskip-1.13809pt\big|\hskip-1.13809pt\big|}G_{s}^{N,m+1-\ell}-C^{N,m+1-\ell}\operatorname{\big|\hskip-1.13809pt\big|\hskip-1.13809pt\big|}_{L^{2}(\omega^{\otimes m+1-\ell})}\\
+\frac{\kappa m}{N}\sum_{\ell=1}^{m-1}\sum_{r=1}^{m-\ell-1}\binom{m-2}{\ell-1,r-1}(C\ell)^{\ell}(Cr)^{r}N^{2-\ell-r}\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}e^{c_{0}s}\operatorname{\big|\hskip-1.13809pt\big|\hskip-1.13809pt\big|}G_{s}^{N,m+1-\ell-r}-C^{N,m+1-\ell-r}\operatorname{\big|\hskip-1.13809pt\big|\hskip-1.13809pt\big|}_{L^{2}(\omega^{\otimes m+1-\ell-r})}.

From here, we conclude by a bootstrap argument.
Given
C_{0},T_{0}>0
, assuming that we have for all
2\leq m\leq N
and
t\in[0,T_{0}]
,

(3.1)

\operatorname{|\hskip-1.13809pt|\hskip-1.13809pt|}G_{t}^{N,m}-C^{N,m}\operatorname{|\hskip-1.13809pt|\hskip-1.13809pt|}_{L^{2}(\omega^{\otimes m})}\,\leq\,m!C_{0}^{m}N^{1-m}e^{-c_{0}t},

the above would then yield, together with Stirling’s formula, for all
2\leq m\leq N
and
t\in[0,T_{0}]
,

\operatorname{\big|\hskip-1.13809pt\big|\hskip-1.13809pt\big|}G_{s}^{N,m}-C^{N,m}\operatorname{\big|\hskip-1.13809pt\big|\hskip-1.13809pt\big|}_{L^{2}(\omega^{\otimes m})}\,\leq\,m!C_{0}^{m}N^{1-m}e^{-c_{0}t}\Big((C/C_{0})^{m}+CC_{0}\kappa+CC_{0}\kappa\sum_{\ell=1}^{m-1}\sum_{r=1}^{m-\ell-1}(C/C_{0})^{\ell+r}\Big).

Choosing
C_{0}=2C
, noting that the double sum is then bounded by
\sum_{\ell,r\geq 1}(C/C_{0})^{\ell+r}=1
, and choosing
\kappa_{0}
small enough so that
2CC_{0}\kappa_{0}<\frac{1}{4}
, we deduce that (
3.1
) automatically holds with an additional prefactor
\frac{1}{2}
for all
2\leq m\leq N
,
t\in[0,T_{0}]
, and
\kappa\in[0,\kappa_{0}]
. We conclude that (
3.1
) must hold with
T_{0}=\infty
for
\kappa\in[0,\kappa_{0}]
. By the equivalence of the norms, the conclusion follows.
∎

3.2.
Cross error estimates for many-particle densities

The following result shows that it suffices to prove the cross error estimate (
1.21
) in Theorem
1.2
for
m=1
. This is obtained as a direct corollary of the above relaxation estimates for correlations, cf. Proposition
3.1
.

Proposition 3.2
.

There exist
c_{0}>0
(only depending on
d,\beta,K,A
) and
\kappa_{0}>0
(further depending on
\|\mu_{\circ}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}
) such that given
\kappa\in[0,\kappa_{0}]
we have for all
2\leq m\leq N
and
t\geq 0
,

\|F^{N,m}_{t}-\mu_{t}^{\otimes m}-M^{N,m}+M^{\otimes m}\|_{L^{2}(\omega^{\otimes m})}\,\leq\,C^{m}N^{-1}e^{-c_{0}t}+C^{m}\|F_{t}^{N,1}-\mu_{t}-M^{N,1}+M\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}.

Proof.

Starting point is the cluster expansion (
2.15
) in form of

F^{N,m}_{t}-M^{N,m}\,=\,\sum_{\pi\vdash\llbracket m\rrbracket}\Big(\prod_{A\in\pi}G^{N,A}_{t}-\prod_{A\in\pi}C^{N,A}\Big).

Singling out the term corresponding to
\pi=\{\{1\},\ldots,\{m\}\}
with
G^{N,1}=F^{N,1}
and
C^{N,1}=M^{N,1}
, this allows to decompose

F^{N,m}_{t}-\mu_{t}^{\otimes m}-M^{N,m}+M^{\otimes m}\\
\,=\,\Big((F^{N,1}_{t})^{\otimes m}-\mu_{t}^{\otimes m}-(M^{N,1})^{\otimes m}+M^{\otimes m}\Big)+\sum_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{2}{\pi\vdash\llbracket m\rrbracket}{\sharp\pi<m}}\Big(\prod_{A\in\pi}G^{N,A}_{t}-\prod_{A\in\pi}C^{N,A}\Big).

We split the proof into two steps, examining the two right-hand side terms separately.

Step
1. Proof that for all
m\geq 1
and
t\geq 0
,

(3.2)

\big\|(F^{N,1}_{t})^{\otimes m}-\mu_{t}^{\otimes m}-(M^{N,1})^{\otimes m}+M^{\otimes m}\big\|_{L^{2}(\omega^{\otimes m})}\\
\,\leq\,C^{m}N^{-1}e^{-c_{0}t}+C^{m}\|F_{t}^{N,1}-\mu_{t}-M^{N,1}+M\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}.

The second-order difference of tensor products can be decomposed as

(F_{t}^{N,1})^{\otimes m}-\mu_{t}^{\otimes m}-(M^{N,1})^{\otimes m}+M^{\otimes m}\,=\,\sum_{k=1}^{m}M^{\otimes k-1}\otimes\big(F_{t}^{N,1}-\mu_{t}-M^{N,1}+M\big)\otimes(M^{N,1})^{\otimes m-k}\\
+\sum_{1\leq k<\ell\leq m}M^{\otimes k-1}\otimes(F_{t}^{N,1}-\mu_{t})\otimes(M^{N,1})^{\otimes\ell-k-1}\otimes(F_{t}^{N,1}-M^{N,1})\otimes(F_{t}^{N,1})^{\otimes m-\ell}\\
+\sum_{1\leq k<\ell\leq m}M^{\otimes k-1}\otimes(\mu_{t}-M)\otimes\mu_{t}^{\otimes\ell-k-1}\otimes(F_{t}^{N,1}-\mu_{t})\otimes(F_{t}^{N,1})^{\otimes m-\ell}.

We deduce

\big\|(F_{t}^{N,1})^{\otimes m}-\mu_{t}^{\otimes m}-(M^{N,1})^{\otimes m}+M^{\otimes m}\big\|_{L^{2}(\omega^{\otimes m})}\\
\,\leq\,C^{m}\|F_{t}^{N,1}-\mu_{t}-M^{N,1}+M\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}+C^{m}\|F_{t}^{N,1}-\mu_{t}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}\Big(\|F_{t}^{N,1}-M^{N,1}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}+\|\mu_{t}-M\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}\Big)\\
\,\leq\,C^{m}\|F_{t}^{N,1}-\mu_{t}-M^{N,1}+M\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}+C^{m}\|F_{t}^{N,1}-\mu_{t}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}\|\mu_{t}-M\|_{L^{2}(\omega)},

and the claim (
3.2
) then follows from uniform-in-time propagation of chaos and mean-field relaxation, cf. Proposition
1.1
and Lemma
2.1
(i).

Step
2. Proof that for all
\pi\vdash\llbracket m\rrbracket
with
\sharp\pi<m
we have for all
t\geq 0
,

(3.3)

\Big\|\prod_{A\in\pi}G^{N,A}_{t}-\prod_{A\in\pi}C^{N,A}\Big\|_{L^{2}(\omega^{\otimes m})}\,\leq\,C^{m}N^{-1}e^{-c_{0}t}.

Given
\pi\vdash\llbracket m\rrbracket
, we start from

\Big\|\prod_{A\in\pi}G^{N,A}_{t}-\prod_{A\in\pi}C^{N,A}\Big\|_{L^{2}(\omega^{\otimes m})}\,\leq\,\sum_{A\in\pi}\|G^{N,\sharp A}_{t}-C^{N,\sharp A}\|_{L^{2}(\omega^{\otimes\sharp A})}\\[-8.53581pt]
\times\prod_{B\in\pi\setminus\{A\}}\Big(\|G^{N,\sharp B}_{t}\|_{L^{2}(\omega^{\otimes\sharp B})}+\|C^{N,\sharp B}\|_{L^{2}(\omega^{\otimes\sharp B})}\Big).

Recalling (
2.21
), we note that Proposition
3.1
yields for all
1\leq k\leq N
,

\|G^{N,k}_{t}-C^{N,k}\|_{L^{2}(\omega^{\otimes k})}\,\leq\,(Ck)^{k}N^{1-k}e^{-c_{0}t}\,\leq\,C^{k}e^{-c_{0}t}\big(N^{-1}+\mathds{1}_{\{k=1\}}\big),

and similarly the correlation estimates of Proposition
1.1
(i) and Corollary
2.7
yield

\|G^{N,k}_{t}\|_{L^{2}(\omega^{\otimes k})}+\|C^{N,k}\|_{L^{2}(\omega^{\otimes k})}\,\leq\,(Ck)^{k}N^{1-k}\,\leq\,C^{k}\big(N^{-1}+\mathds{1}_{\{k=1\}}\big).

Inserting this into the above, we are led to

\Big\|\prod_{A\in\pi}G^{N,A}_{t}-\prod_{A\in\pi}C^{N,A}\Big\|_{L^{2}(\omega^{\otimes m})}\\
\,\leq\,C^{m}e^{-c_{0}t}\prod_{A\in\pi}\big(N^{-1}+\mathds{1}_{\{\sharp A=1\}}\big).

As the condition
\sharp\pi<m
ensures that there is
A\in\pi
with
\sharp A\geq 2
, the claim (
3.3
) follows.
∎

3.3.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

We are now in position to conclude the proof of Theorem
1.2
. Recall that the second part (
1.22
) already follows from Proposition
3.1
, so it remains to prove (
1.21
).
Comparing the BBGKY equation (
2.9
) for
F^{N,1}
, the mean-field equation (
1.8
) for
\mu
, and the corresponding stationary equations for steady states, we find

(3.4)

(\partial_{t}-L_{0})\big(F^{N,1}-\mu-M^{N,1}+M\big)\,=\,\kappa H_{1}\bigg(\frac{N-1}{N}\big(F^{N,\{1,*\}}-M^{N,\{1,*\}}\big)-\mu^{\{1\}}\mu^{\{*\}}+M^{\{1\}}M^{\{*\}}\bigg).

By the Duhamel formula with
F^{N,1}|_{t=0}=\mu_{\circ}
, recalling the definition of the operator
H_{1}
, and appealing to Lemma
2.1
(ii) with
\pi^{\bot}(M^{N,1}-M)=M^{N,1}-M
, we deduce

\|F^{N,1}_{t}-\mu_{t}-M^{N,1}+M\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}\,\lesssim\,e^{-2c_{0}t}\|M^{N,1}-M\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}\\
+\kappa^{2}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-2c_{0}(t-s)}\Big\|\frac{N-1}{N}\big(F_{s}^{N,2}-M^{N,2}\big)-\mu_{s}^{\otimes 2}+M^{\otimes 2}\Big\|_{L^{2}(\omega^{\otimes 2})}^{2}\mathrm{d}s,

and thus, reorganizing the last term,

\|F^{N,1}_{t}-\mu_{t}-M^{N,1}+M\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}\,\lesssim\,e^{-2c_{0}t}\|M^{N,1}-M\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}+\frac{\kappa^{2}}{N^{2}}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-2c_{0}(t-s)}\|\mu_{s}-M\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}\mathrm{d}s\\
+\kappa^{2}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-2c_{0}(t-s)}\|F_{s}^{N,2}-\mu_{s}^{\otimes 2}-M^{N,2}+M^{\otimes 2}\|_{L^{2}(\omega^{\otimes 2})}^{2}\mathrm{d}s.

Appealing to Corollary
2.7
, Lemma
2.1
(i), and Proposition
3.2
with
m=2
, respectively, to estimate the three right-hand side terms, we are led to

\|F^{N,1}_{t}-\mu_{t}-M^{N,1}+M\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}\,\lesssim\,N^{-1}t^{\frac{1}{2}}e^{-c_{0}t}+\kappa\sup_{[0,t]}\|F^{N,1}-\mu-M^{N,1}+M\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}.

For
\kappa
small enough, the last term can be absorbed and the conclusion (
1.21
) then follows for
m=1
(up to redefining
c_{0}
). By Proposition
3.2
, this is enough to conclude for all
1\leq m\leq N
.
∎

Proof of Corollary
1.3

We note that all our arguments can be repeated for the corresponding dynamics on the torus
\mathbb{T}^{d}
with
A\equiv 0
. Ergodic estimates for the corresponding Fokker-Planck operator

on the torus are given in
[

4.
Overdamped setting

In this section, we briefly discuss the adaptation of the results to the overdamped Langevin dynamics (
1.23
).
To streamline the exposition, we use the same notation as for the underdamped dynamics, simply adapting their meaning accordingly, cf. (
1.28
).

4.1.
Mean-field relaxation

We consider the following self-adjoint elliptic operator on the weighted space
L^{2}(\omega)
: for
h\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

(4.1)

L_{0}h\,:=\,\triangle h+{\operatorname{div}}(h\nabla A)={\operatorname{div}}((\nabla+\nabla A)h).

The following ergodic estimates for the mean-field evolution (
1.27
) and for the linearized semigroup
e^{tL_{0}}
are immediate by standard parabolic theory. They constitute the counterpart of Lemma
2.1
in the overdamped setting.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
K\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})^{d}
and let the confining potential
A
satisfy (
1.3
). Then there exist
\kappa_{0},c_{0}>0
(only depending on
d,K,A
) such that the following hold for all
\kappa\in[0,\kappa_{0}]
.

(i)

Given
\mu_{\circ}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L^{2}(\omega)
, the solution
\mu\in C(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L^{2}(\omega))
of the mean-field equation (
1.27
) satisfies for all
t\geq 0
,

\|\mu_{t}-M\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}\,\lesssim\,e^{-c_{0}t}\|\mu_{\circ}-M\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}.

(ii)

For all
h\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}h=0
, we have for all
t\geq 0
,

e^{2c_{0}t}\|e^{tL_{0}}h\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{2c_{0}s}\|(\nabla+\nabla A)e^{sL_{0}}h\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\,\lesssim\,\|h\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}.

In particular, by duality, for all
g\in L^{2}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\mathbb{R}_{+};L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{d})
,

\Big\|\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)L_{0}}{\operatorname{div}}(g(s))\,\mathrm{d}s\Big\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\omega)}\,\lesssim\,\int_{0}^{t}e^{-2c_{0}(t-s)}\|g(s)\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s.

Proof.

As
A
is uniformly convex, cf. (
1.3
), we recall that the probability measure
\propto e^{-A}
satisfies a Poincaré inequality, see e.g.
[
32
, Section A.19]
:
in terms of the weight
\omega=e^{A}
, this reads as follows, for all
h\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

(4.2)

\Big\|h-M_{0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}h\Big\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}\,\lesssim\,\|(\nabla+\nabla A)h\|_{L^{2}(\omega)},\qquad M_{0}\,:=\,\tfrac{\omega^{-1}}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\omega^{-1}}.

Using this, the conclusion easily follows similarly as in the proof of Lemma
2.1
. We skip the details for shortness.
∎

4.2.
BBGKY hierarchy

In the present overdamped setting, the BBGKY hierarchy of equations satisfied by marginals and by correlation functions take the exact same form as in the underdamped setting, cf. (
2.9
) and Lemma
2.6
, up to replacing the operators
H_{k}
,
S_{k,\ell}
introduced in Definition
2.4
by the following,

\displaystyle S_{k,\ell}h^{P}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle-{\operatorname{div}}_{x_{k}}(K(x_{k},x_{\ell})h^{P}),

(4.3)

\displaystyle H_{k}h^{P\cup\{\ast\}}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle-{\operatorname{div}}_{x_{k}}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}K(x_{k},x_{*})h^{P\cup\{\ast\}}\,\mathrm{d}x_{*}\Big).

In this new setting, now using the ergodic estimates in Lemma
4.1
instead of Lemma

, the proof of Proposition
1.1
is immediately adapted. In this way, we slightly extend the result of
[
33
]
on uniform-in-time correlation estimates by treating the whole space with confinement, and we obtain a uniform-in-

Gibbs relaxation result in

that partially improves previous works on the topic.

4.3.
Proof of Theorem
1.4

Starting from uniform-in-time correlation estimates and from the BBGKY hierarchy of equations for correlations,
now appealing to the ergodic estimates of Lemma
4.1
to perform the estimates,
the conclusion follows by repeating the exact same steps as in the proof of Theorem
1.2
in Section
3
. We skip the

4.4.
Proof of Corollary
1.5

As for Corollary
1.3
, all our arguments can be directly repeated in the corresponding setting on the torus
\mathbb{T}^{d}
with
A\equiv 0
.
It only remains to check that for a translation-invariant interaction kernel
K(x,y)=K_{0}(x-y)
we have
M^{N,1}=M
. On the one hand, for
\kappa
small enough, the uniqueness of the mean-field equilibrium
M
ensures that it coincides with the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{T}^{d}
. On the other hand, taking the first marginal in the steady-state Liouville equation for
M^{N}
, we find

\triangle M^{N,1}=\kappa\frac{N-1}{N}{\operatorname{div}}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}K_{0}(\cdot-y)M^{N,2}(\cdot,y)\,\mathrm{d}y\Big).

As by translation invariance of the system we have
M^{N,2}(x,y)=M^{N,2}(0,y-x)
, we find that

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}K_{0}(\cdot-y)M^{N,2}(\cdot,y)\,\mathrm{d}y=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}K_{0}(-y)M^{N,2}(0,y)\,\mathrm{d}y

is a constant. The equation for
M^{N,1}
thus reduces to
\triangle M^{N,1}=0
, and we can conclude that
M^{N,1}
is indeed constant, hence
M^{N,1}=M
.∎

5.3.
Relaxation of correlations

With the above tools at hand, we now turn to the proof of Theorem

Proposition 5.4

\kappa\geq 0

and

(5.3)

\displaystyle\|G^{N,m}_{t}-C^{N,m}\|_{W^{-\ell_{m},1}(\mathbb{T}^{dm})}\,\lesssim\,N^{1-m}e^{-c_{0}t}+N^{1-m-\theta_{0}},

for some

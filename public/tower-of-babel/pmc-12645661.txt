Acute stroke management in the Caribbean: a scoping review protocol
Authors: Hélène Clervius, Tadeusz H Wroblewski, Nadia Bennett, Michael J Wells, Maria Lee, Domini Crandon, Ryna Then, Gloria Aidoo-Frimpong, Mill Etienne, Ellianne J Dos Santos Rubio, Tameka March-Downer, Violiza Inoa, Ernest J Barthélemy, Gillian Gordon Perue

Abstract AbstractIntroductionStroke is a leading cause of death and disability in the Caribbean, yet there is limited published information on the availability and utilisation of diagnostic imaging and treatment methods. Inequities in healthcare infrastructure, access to neuroimaging and acute treatment options may contribute to poorer outcomes following stroke, particularly in the low-resource settings that characterise most of the Caribbean region. The objective of this review is to map the literature on access to diagnostic and therapeutic modalities for adult stroke care in the Caribbean to identify potential limitations in acute treatment and examine how restricted access may impact outcomes. The resulting data can help inform strategies for improving access to stroke care in resource-limited communities.Methods and analysisWe will apply a three-step strategy based on the Joanna Briggs Institute methodological framework: first, a limited search to identify relevant articles; second, selection of key search terms; third, implementation into a comprehensive search strategy. The query will range from 1 January 1995 to 1 June 2025 (date of final search). Search results will be extracted and screened by two independent reviewers, and findings will be presented in accordance with the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses extension for Scoping Reviews guidelines. We will consider studies focusing on ischaemic and haemorrhagic stroke in the Caribbean, emphasising access to diagnostic imaging, stroke centres, prehospital management and emergent treatment. Studies examining acute stroke management capacity within the region will be considered. Studies will be excluded if they: focus exclusively on primary stroke prevention, postacute care, longitudinal care pathways for stroke victims or paediatric populations; are unrelated to stroke diagnosis or treatment or are conducted outside the Caribbean.Ethics and disseminationThis protocol aims to perform secondary analysis of previously published literature; therefore, ethical approval is not required. Introduction

Stroke represents the second leading cause of mortality and disability worldwide.1 2 Despite advanced medical and surgical treatment options available for acute stroke, progress in decreasing stroke burden in low- and middle-income countries (LMICs) remains limited.1 The two major categories of stroke include ischaemic and haemorrhagic stroke, and rapid management of both types is required within the first few hours following the event to minimise complications, decrease mortality and optimise stroke outcomes.2,4 Stroke treatment requires comprehensive, multidisciplinary approaches including well-trained prehospital and allied healthcare providers, neuroscience nurses and physician specialists from emergency medicine, vascular and interventional neurology, neurosurgery, neuroradiology and neuro-intensive care as well as access to resources such as neuroimaging and laboratory capacity.

In high-income countries, stroke management capacity helps decrease devastating poststroke effects; however, inequity in the distribution of available resources still impedes optimal stroke treatment.5 In LMICs, modified treatment modalities may exist due to lack of healthcare access; poor care quality; unavailability of diagnostic tools; paucity of dedicated stroke centres, medical teams, medication and lack of primary care treatment to address modifiable stroke risk factors, which are more prevalent in LMICs.5,7 Many countries of the Caribbean fall into this category and require special attention.

The Caribbean is defined as the geographical space bordering the Caribbean Sea, its islands and coasts and the western and eastern West Indies.8,10 While all were invaded by European colonisers, the current differing sovereignty status across countries contributes to variation in infrastructure and public health systems.10 Despite their sovereignty, ongoing disparities continue to burden appropriate access to healthcare services.11 The main economic system of the Caribbean region is based on tourism, renewable, exportation-importation and the blue economy,8 which creates a significant income disparity across the islands with a variation in gross income per capita (ie, between 800 and 30 000 US$).9 11 12 The strong reliance on exportation-importation and tourism with massive travelling made countries in the Caribbean particularly vulnerable to the pandemic COVID-19 which contributed to further weakening of their healthcare system and worsening of the overall health outcome.5,711

Stroke represents the third leading cause of death in the Caribbean,9 12 which is further compounded by the ongoing epidemiological transition of burden of chronic diseases (eg, cardiovascular disease, hypertension and diabetes) which contribute to risk factors for stroke. There is limited published information on the availability and utilisation of diagnostic and treatment methods used to manage acute stroke in the Caribbean. Furthermore, understanding the availability of neuroimaging would directly lead to a better assessment of treatment capabilities in the Caribbean region by highlighting potential gaps in diagnostic resources and treatment access. A preliminary search performed on 10 February 2025, across PubMed, Joanna Briggs Institute (JBI) Evidence Synthesis and Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews did not identify any published or on-going scoping reviews on the management of acute stroke in the Caribbean.

In this protocol, we propose a scoping review to comprehensively assess the diagnosis and treatment of acute ischaemic and haemorrhagic stroke in the Caribbean. The aims and objectives of this review are to: (a) evaluate the practices and protocols for prehospital management; (b) determine the availability and utilisation of diagnostic imaging techniques; (c) determine the main treatment modalities for ischaemic stroke (ie, availability of intravenous thrombolysis and mechanical thrombectomy (MT)); (d) investigate the current approaches for the treatment of intraparenchymal haemorrhage and subarachnoid haemorrhage (SAH); (e) investigate the management capacity of stroke in the Caribbean and (f) evaluate what available literature exists on the epidemiology of stroke in the Caribbean.

Methods and analysis

The anticipated timeline for this study is from the date of final search (1 June 2025) to planned scoping review completion by 1 February 2026. We will follow the methodology of the JBI preferred reported items for scoping reviews13 and the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses extension for Scoping Reviews (PRISMA-ScR 10.2).14 The protocol has been registered on Fig Share.

Search strategy

The search strategy will include a combination of medical subject headings terms and keywords to identify appropriate articles for consideration. The following databases will be queried: PubMed (MEDLINE), Embase, MedRxiv, Web of Science, Cochrane Library, LILACS (BIREME) and ScienceDirect. By utilising the ‘snowballing method’, the reference lists of any systematic reviews identified from the search will also be evaluated for any relevant information and consideration for inclusion in the final scoping review. A grey literature search for journal articles, news articles and books relevant to our topic will be conducted. Sources of unpublished studies will also be searched including clinicaltrials.gov and conference abstracts. Excluded studies may include studies published by Caribbean professionals outside of the Caribbean space in other languages described below, commentaries, newspapers-related stroke articles, reviews, blogs, abstracts, editorials, study protocols and opinion articles, to avoid duplication. Additionally, literature that does not directly involve human subjects, including in vivo and in vitro preclinical studies, will be excluded.

We intend to follow the three-step search strategy developed by the JBI.13 First, we will perform an initial limited search in PubMed to identify articles on the topic and develop a full search strategy based on text in the titles and abstracts of relevant articles. Inclusion criteria

This review will include studies related to ischaemic and haemorrhagic stroke in the Caribbean. We will consider all studies that address the capacity of management of stroke in the Caribbean such as stroke centre, MRI, CT scan, prehospital management of stroke and emergent treatment of stroke.

Participants

This review will consider all adult Caribbean patients 18 years and older who had a stroke not due to traumatic aetiology, regardless of treatment status. Acute stroke encompasses two subtypes: acute ischaemic stroke (AIS) and haemorrhagic stroke3 and represents the sudden focal neurological deficit related to a vascular disease of the central nervous system.2 3 AIS is defined as a sudden decrease of cerebral, spinal or retinal blood flow leading to tissue death2 3 15 and comprises more than 80% of all strokes.16 Haemorrhagic stroke represents 15%–20% of all strokes and includes non-traumatic SAH and intraparenchymal/intracerebral haemorrhage (IPH) and confers worse complications and prognosis.17 18 Studies investigating haemorrhagic stroke due to traumatic aetiology will be excluded from this review.

Concept

Diagnosis and management of hyperacute stroke will be defined as the diagnosis of acute ischaemic or haemorrhagic stroke with evidence from medical history and imaging modalities including CT, CT-angiography or MRI. Hyperacute ischaemic stroke management (within 24 hours) includes intravenous thrombolysis and MT.16 19 Under current treatment standards, intravenous thrombolysis is administered within 4.5 hours from the last patient’s known well, while MT is performed within 6 hours of symptom onset. Extended criteria for MT allow patients to get the procedure from 6 to 24 hours after symptom onset (DAWN & DEFUSE 3 trials). Extended window selection is based on either Alberta Stroke Program Early CT Score (ASPECTS), CT perfusion or brain MRI.16 19 20 Haemorrhagic stroke management is subdivided into SAH and IPH management.18 Non-traumatic SAH is treated according to their aetiology; for example, SAH haemorrhage caused by a ruptured aneurysm requires multidisciplinary approaches (eg, clipping, coiling) which would involve a neurosurgeon or a neuro-endovascular surgeon to offer prompt treatments to prevent rebleeding,3 18 while other aetiologies such as arterio-venous malformations, arterio-venous fistulas and sinus venous thrombosis are treated differently.17 18 IPH management may require reversible causes when anticoagulation or antiplatelets have been used.21 Based on the clinical presentation, localisation or mass effect associated with the IPH might require immediate neurosurgical intervention.3 18

Context

This review aims to capture all studies conducted in the Caribbean islands including the countries that speak English, Spanish, French or Dutch. Hospital and prehospital settings will be included. The review will consider studies that investigate acute stroke treatments and management in the Caribbean including published and unpublished articles. The Caribbean is defined as a geographical space that borders the Caribbean Sea, its islands and coasts, the western and eastern West Indies.8 Regions and countries of the Caribbean include Haiti, Cuba, Dominican Republic, Puerto Rico, Bahamas, Jamaica, Cayman Islands, Montserrat, Turks and Caicos Islands, Anguilla, Antigua, Dominica, St. Lucia, St. Vincent, Grenada, Barbados, Virgin Islands, Trinidad and Tobago, St. Kitts and Nevis, Curacao, Bonaire, Aruba, St. Eustatius, Saba, Sint Maarten, Guadeloupe, Martinique, St. Martin, St Barthelemy, Virgin Islands, St Lucia and Suriname.8 9 22 23

Date range

This review will evaluate studies from the years of the tPA (tissue plasminogen activator) revolution (1 January 1995)24 to 1 June 2025.

Types of evidence to be included

This scoping review will consider primary clinical studies involving human subjects including randomised control trials, cross-sectional studies, cohort studies, case reports and case series. We plan to capture both published and unpublished studies in French, English, Spanish or Dutch.

The review group consists of 12 authors. All studies found from the search will be exported into Covidence systematic review software (Veritas Health Innovation, Melbourne, Australia. Available at www.covidence.org), and duplicates identified will be removed. The title and abstracts will be screened by two independent reviewers for potentially relevant articles. Any disagreements will be arbitrated by a third senior member of the review team for full text review. The full texts of all potentially relevant articles will be retrieved and screened using the predetermined eligibility criteria. Any disagreements will be arbitrated by a third senior member of the review team for inclusion in the review. The reasons for exclusion of the articles will be documented; studies unrelated to stroke diagnosis or treatment, conducted outside the Caribbean or focusing solely on paediatric populations will be excluded. Furthermore, studies focused exclusively on primary stroke prevention, postacute care or longitudinal care pathways for patients stroke victims will also be excluded. All studies published in English, French, Spanish or Dutch will be included as per our method. The results of the preliminary search and study inclusion will be reported in the full scoping review with a PRISMA flow diagram.14 In accordance with PRISMA-ScR guidelines,14 a risk of bias and methodological quality appraisal will not be conducted for this scoping review.

Data extraction, management and analysis

A data extraction instrument will be developed and piloted in Covidence systematic review software. Two review authors will independently enter information from the included studies which will define the characteristics of the studies as well as data that will be synthesised for the review. Data extraction will be completed after full-text screening using the following charting table (online supplemental appendix 2) with some modification regarding the presented example. Extracted data will be reviewed by all authors, to assess the risk of bias and the grade of the evidence currently available. Results will summarise the status of stroke care and treatment in the Caribbean for ischaemic and haemorrhagic stroke and grade the evidence available. Existing gaps in the literature and opportunities for future research will be discussed.

Knowledge translation

We anticipate that the audience for this review will include clinicians, researchers, healthcare policymakers and advocacy organisations seeking to improve stroke care equity in the Caribbean. The findings of this review will be disseminated to further support efforts to improve acute stroke diagnostics and treatment access in the Caribbean. The aim of this review is to identify gaps in the availability of stroke care modalities to inform future research, resource allocation and policy development in the Caribbean.

Patient and public involvement

The development and design of this scoping review protocol did not involve direct input from patients or the public. Notwithstanding, the objective of our proposed review is to identify limitations in current acute stroke care capacity in the Caribbean. We anticipate that the findings will constructively inform future efforts to expand patient/public access to stroke care in the Caribbean.

Ethics and dissemination

Ethical approval is not required for this review, and this protocol was not reviewed by an ethics committee. The proposed scoping review will adhere to established guidelines to ensure accuracy and transparency. The results produced by this scoping review will be shared via peer-reviewed publication and regional, national and international conferences. Dissemination efforts will focus on academic journals and conferences promoting health equity and global health efforts to make results widely accessible and actionable.

Kodaira-Spencer maps for elliptic orbispheres as isomorphisms of Frobenius algebras
Sangwook Lee

Abstract
. Given a mirror pair of a symplectic manifold X X and a Landau-Ginzburg potential W W , we are interested in the problem whether the quantum cohomology of X X and the Jacobian algebra of W W are isomorphic. Since those can be equipped with Frobenius algebra structures, we might ask whether they are isomorphic as Frobenius algebras. We show that the Kodaira-Spencer map gives a Frobenius algebra isomorphism for elliptic orbispheres, under the Floer theoretic modification of the residue pairing.



1.
Introduction

Closed string mirror symmetry predicts that quantum cohomology of a symplectic manifold and Jacobian ring of the mirror superpotential are isomorphic. There have been results on this problem firstly for toric manifolds: see
[
Bat93
,
Giv98
,
Iri09
]
etc.

Fukaya-Oh-Ohta-Ono also gave a construction of the ring isomorphism in
[
FOOO10
,
FOOO11
,
FOOO16
]
, based on the study of closed-open map which is defined more geometrically. Though the codomain of the original closed-open map is Hochschild cohomology which is hard to grasp, Fukaya-Oh-Ohta-Ono proved that the length-0 part of the closed-open map (which was called
Kodaira-Spencer map
), whose codomain is now Jacobian ring, provides an isomorphism in compact toric case. Their strategy was also employed by Amorim-Cho-Hong-Lau in
[
ACHL20
]
for proving mirror symmetry for orbifold projective lines.

Since quantum cohomology ring and Jacobian ring are both Frobenius algebras with respect to the Poincaré duality and the residue pairing respectively, we might ask whether their pairings are also related in a suitable way. The question was dealt with in
[
CLS20
]
, and it was conjectured that if we want the Kodaira-Spencer map to be a Frobenius algebra isomorphism, we need to modify the residue pairing by a constant which is the ratio of ”Floer volume form” and the usual volume form on a Lagrangian submanifold. The rescaling constant appeared naturally when we consider Cardy condition (see
[
CLS20
]
for more detail).

In this paper we focus on elliptic orbispheres. Several mirror isomorphisms of Frobenius manifolds for orbifold projective lines have been established. In
[
ST11
]
Satake-Takahashi proved that there is an isomorphism of Frobenius manifolds from Gromov-Witten theory of
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{3,3,3}
and the universal unfolding of the mirror potential, and also for
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{2,2,2,2}
case (where the counterpart is given by the invariant theory of an elliptic Weyl group). For spherical cases, namely for
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{a,b,c}
with
\frac{1}{a}+\frac{1}{b}+\frac{1}{c}>1
we refer readers to
[
Ros10
]
,
[
MT08
]
,
[
IST19
]
etc. Towards the ultimate level of closed string mirror symmetry such as above results, we add more elliptic orbisphere examples, namely
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{2,4,4}
and
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{2,3,6}
which are quotients of the elliptic curve by
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
and
\mathbb{Z}_{6}
respectively.
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{3,3,3}
will be also revisited. Though we work only at the level of Frobenius algebras, we hope that we can find a relationship with previous works, such as the relation between the Floer theoretic rescaling constant and the choice of primitive form.

We summarize the idea. Let
W
be the mirror superpotential to
X
, so the Kodaira-Spencer map is given by
\mathfrak{ks}:QH^{*}(X)\to{\rm Jac}(W)
. The most natural pairings for Frobenius algebra structure are Poincaré pairing and the residue pairing respectively. We point out that the residue pairing
\langle\;,\;\rangle_{res}
on
{\rm Jac}(W)
will be chosen as

(1.1)

\langle f,g\rangle_{res}=(-1)^{\frac{n(n-1)}{2}}{\rm{Res}}\begin{bmatrix}fg\cdot dx_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge dx_{n}\\
\partial_{x_{1}}W,\cdots,\partial_{x_{n}}W\end{bmatrix}

whose formula appears in
[
PV12
]
as induced from the Mukai pairing on
HH_{*}(MF(W))
, so that it is ”the most canonical” in some sense. It is also remarkable that the sign
(-1)^{\frac{n(n-1)}{2}}
also appears in the Cardy condition in
[
FOOO16
, Theorem 3.4.1]
.

Our main theorem is as follows.

Theorem A
.

Let
X
be an elliptic orbisphere and
W
be its mirror superpotential. Let
{\mathbb{L}}\subset X
be the Seidel Lagrangian with odd degree immersed generators
X,Y,Z
, and
c_{\mathbb{L}}
be the constant defined by

(1.2)

c_{\mathbb{L}}\cdot p={m_{2}(X,m_{2}(Y,Z))}

where
p=m_{2}(X,\bar{X})
.
Then the Kodaira-Spencer map

\mathfrak{ks}:\big(QH^{*}(X),\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{PD}\big)\to\big({\rm Jac}(W),\langle c_{\mathbb{L}}\cdot,c_{\mathbb{L}}\cdot\rangle_{res}\big)

is an isomorphism of Frobenius algebras.

Observe that we modified the residue pairing by suitable constant
c_{\mathbb{L}}
as discussed above. To compare pairings we need to compute the residue of
\mathfrak{ks}({\rm{pt}}_{X})
, where
{\rm{pt}}_{X}
is the Poincaré dual of point class of the symplectic manifold
X
. Though
\mathfrak{ks}({\rm{pt}}_{X})
is explicitly computed in
[
ACHL20
]
for general orbifold sphere
X
, it is hard to conclude that its (rescaled) residue is indeed
1=\int_{X}{\rm{pt}}_{X}
as expected. The difficulty arises in the comparison of two different arithmetics of formal power series.
We bypass this difficulty by computing
\mathfrak{ks}({\rm{pt}}_{X})
in another way. Let
G
act on the elliptic curve
E
so that
X=E/G
. We will use the result of
[
CL24
]
that the Kodaira-Spencer map on
X
can be lifted to the
orbifold
Kodaira-Spencer map
\mathfrak{ks}_{orb}:H^{*}(E)\to{\rm Jac}(W,{\widehat{G}})
, where
{\rm Jac}(W,{\widehat{G}})

is the
orbifold
Jacobian algebra (
{\widehat{G}}
is the character group of
G
, so it is isomorphic to
G
if
G
is abelian). By the relation

\mathfrak{ks}({\rm{pt}}_{X})=\frac{1}{|G|}\mathfrak{ks}_{orb}({\rm{pt}}_{E}),

we can recover
\mathfrak{ks}({\rm{pt}}_{X})
by
\mathfrak{ks}_{orb}(\alpha\cup\beta)=\mathfrak{ks}_{orb}(\alpha)\bullet\mathfrak{ks}_{orb}(\beta)
for some
\alpha,\beta\in H^{1}(E)
. By a classical result on the residue over an isolated singularity (which will be recalled in Section
4
), the following is a rephrasing of Theorem
A
.

Theorem B
.

Let
X
be an elliptic orbisphere
E/G
and
W
be its mirror superpotential.
Then

(1.3)

c_{\mathbb{L}}^{2}\cdot\mathfrak{ks}_{orb}({\rm{pt}}_{E})=-\frac{\det\;{\rm{Hess}}(W)}{|G|\cdot\mu}.

The organization of the paper is as follows. In Section
2
we first recall the construction of orbifold Jacobian algebras from Floer theory. Then we briefly review (orbifold) Kodaira-Spencer maps which appear in Fukaya-Oh-Ohta-Ono’s works and also in
[
CL24
]
. In Section
3
we review the product structure on orbifold Jacobian algebras following
[
Lee22
]
, and explicitly compute the product for the mirror Landau-Ginzburg orbifold to an elliptic curve. Finally, we prove our main result in Section
4
, with a remark on nontrivial identities of arithmetics of formal power series.

2.
Kodaira-Spencer map to an orbifold Jacobian algebra

2.1.
Preliminaries

We briefly recall the deformation theory of Lagrangian submanifolds originally in
[
FOOO09
]
, following every notation in Section 4.1 of
[
CL24
]
. Let
X
be a symplectic manifold and
{\mathbb{L}}
be its (possibly immersed) Lagrangian submanifold. Let
CF({\mathbb{L}},{\mathbb{L}};\Lambda)
be the Fukaya
{A_{\infty}}
-algebra whose underlying space is the sum of de Rham algebra and the module generated by immersed generators. Suppose that
{\mathbb{L}}
is weakly unobstructed, i.e. the
{A_{\infty}}
-structure on
CF^{*}({\mathbb{L}},{\mathbb{L}};\Lambda)
can be deformed by weak Maurer-Cartan elements. Assume further that
X_{1},\cdots,X_{n}\in CF^{1}({\mathbb{L}},{\mathbb{L}};\Lambda)
are weak Maurer-Cartan elements such that
c_{1}X_{1}+\cdots+c_{n}X_{n}
is also a weak Maurer-Cartan element for any
c_{i}\in\Lambda_{+}
. Let
x_{i}
be the dual variable of
X_{i}
and
b:=x_{1}X_{1}+\cdots+x_{n}X_{n}
. The weak Maurer-Cartan equation gives rise to the potential
W_{\mathbb{L}}\in R=\Lambda[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}]
. We consider the following
{A_{\infty}}
-algebra

{\mathcal{B}}({\mathbb{L}}):=(CF^{*}({\mathbb{L}},{\mathbb{L}};\Lambda)\otimes_{\Lambda_{0}}R,\{m_{k}^{b}\}).

We also denote by
{\mathcal{B}}({\mathbb{L}})_{alg}
the associative algebra with the same underlying space as
{\mathcal{B}}({\mathbb{L}})
while equipped with the product
v\cdot w:=(-1)^{|v|}m_{2}^{b}(v,w)
.
The following results evidently reflect the importance of the above definition.

Proposition 2.1

(
[
CL24
]
)
.

If
{\mathbb{L}}
is the Seidel Lagrangian in an orbisphere
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{a,b,c}
or the Lagrangian torus at the critical point of
W_{\mathbb{L}}
, then there is an algebra isomorphism

(2.1)

\Psi:H^{*}({\mathcal{B}}({\mathbb{L}})_{alg})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\simeq}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\rm Jac}(W_{\mathbb{L}})=R/\partial W_{\mathbb{L}}.

Let us now recall the orbifold Jacobian algebra of an isolated singularity equipped with a group action.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
H
be a finite abelian group, which acts on
R=\Bbbk[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}]
and leaves
W
invariant. We call the pair
(W,H)
a
Landau-Ginzburg orbifold
.

Throughout the paper, we only consider diagonal
H
-action, i.e.
h\cdot x_{i}=h_{i}x_{i}
for some
h_{i}\in\Bbbk^{*}
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
(W,H)
be a Landau-Ginzburg orbifold. Then the
twisted Jacobian algebra
of
(W,H)
is defined as

{\rm Jac}^{\prime}(W,H):=\bigoplus_{h\in H}{\rm Jac}(W^{h})\cdot\xi_{h}

where
W^{h}
is the image of
W
via projection
\pi:R\to R/(x_{i}:hx_{i}\neq x_{i})
. The formal generator
\xi_{h}
has degree
|I_{h}|\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}
where
I_{h}=\{i\in\{1,\cdots,n\}\mid hx_{i}\neq x_{i}\}
. The
H
-action on generators is defined by

h^{\prime}\cdot\xi_{h}=\frac{\prod_{i\in I_{h}}(h^{\prime-1}x_{i})}{\prod_{i\in I_{h}}x_{i}}\xi_{h},

and the
H
-invariant subalgebra

{\rm Jac}(W,H):={\rm Jac}^{\prime}(W,H)^{H}

is called the
orbifold Jacobian algebra
of
(W,H)
.

Theorem 2.4
.

(1)

{\rm Jac}^{\prime}(W,H)
is an
H
-graded algebra, namely

\xi_{h}\bullet\xi_{h^{\prime}}\in{\rm Jac}(W^{h})\cdot\xi_{hh^{\prime}}.

(2)

The product
\bullet
satisfies braided-commutativity, namely

\xi_{h}\bullet\xi_{h^{\prime}}=(-1)^{|\xi_{h}|\cdot|\xi_{h^{\prime}}|}h\cdot\xi_{h^{\prime}}\bullet\xi_{h}.

In particular, if
\xi_{h}
and
\xi_{h^{\prime}}
are both
H
-invariant, then

\xi_{h}\bullet\xi_{h^{\prime}}=(-1)^{|\xi_{h}|\cdot|\xi_{h^{\prime}}|}\xi_{h^{\prime}}\bullet\xi_{h},

which implies that
{\rm Jac}(W,H)
is a supercommutative algebra.

We postpone the definition of the product on
{\rm Jac}(W,H)
until Section
3
.
Orbifold Jacobian algebras appear naturally in Floer theory as follows. Let
X
be a symplectic manifold and a finite abelian group
G
act on
X
. Suppose that
{\mathbb{L}}\subset X/G
is a weakly unobstructed Lagrangian submanifold with mirror superpotential
W
, with an embedded Lagrangian lift
{\mathbb{L}}_{0}\subset X
. Then we can construct a new
{A_{\infty}}
-algebra structure on
{\mathcal{B}}({\mathbb{L}})\otimes\Lambda[{\widehat{G}}]
, whose
{\widehat{G}}
-invariant subalgebra
({\mathcal{B}}({\mathbb{L}})\otimes\Lambda[{\widehat{G}}])_{alg}^{\widehat{G}}
is isomorphic to
{\rm Jac}(W,{\widehat{G}})
.

Proposition 2.5

(
[
CL24
]
)
.

Let
\widetilde{{\mathbb{L}}}=\bigoplus_{g\in G}g\cdot{\mathbb{L}}_{0}\subset X
be a Lagrangian submanifold with lifted weak bounding cochain
\widetilde{b}
from
b
. Then

\Phi:\big(CF(\widetilde{{\mathbb{L}}},\widetilde{{\mathbb{L}}})\otimes R,m_{k}^{\widetilde{b}}\big)\to\big(({\mathcal{B}}({\mathbb{L}})\otimes\Lambda[{\widehat{G}}])^{\widehat{G}},m_{k}^{b\otimes 1}\big),\;v_{g}\mapsto\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{\chi\in{\widehat{G}}}\chi(g)v\otimes\chi

is an
{A_{\infty}}
-isomorphism. Here
v_{g}
is an element in
CF(g\cdot{\mathbb{L}}_{0},h\cdot{\mathbb{L}}_{0})
and
v
is the projection of
v_{g}
.

Despite being vacuous,
\Phi
can be also defined in the previous nonequivariant setting (namely, endowed with trivial group action) just by the identity.

Proposition 2.6

(
[
CL24
]
)
.

Let
({\mathcal{B}}({\mathbb{L}})\otimes\Lambda[{\widehat{G}}])^{\widehat{G}}_{alg}
be an associative algebra with

(v\otimes\chi)\cdot(w\otimes\chi^{\prime}):=(-1)^{|v|}m_{2}^{b\otimes 1}(v\otimes\chi,w\otimes\chi^{\prime}).

Then there is an algebra isomorphism

(2.2)

\Psi_{\widehat{G}}:H^{*}(({\mathcal{B}}({\mathbb{L}})\otimes\Lambda[{\widehat{G}}])^{\widehat{G}}_{alg})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\simeq}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\rm Jac}(W,{\widehat{G}}).

Again, in the nonequivariant setting,
\Psi_{\widehat{G}}
is nothing but
\Psi
above.

Now we recall the construction of Kodaira-Spencer maps. For general weakly unobstructed Lagrangian
L\subset X
with bounding cochain
b
, we use the similar configuration of holomorphic discs as Fukaya-Oh-Ohta-Ono’s original definition.

Definition 2.7
.

For a moduli space of
J
-holomorphic discs with
k+1
boundary marked points and one interior marked points
\mathcal{M}_{k+1,1}(\beta)
for
\beta\in H_{2}(X,L)
and a cycle
A\subset X
,
let
\mathcal{M}_{k+1,1}(\beta,A)=\mathcal{M}_{k+1,1}(\beta)\times_{X}A
and consider their evaluation maps
ev_{i}^{k}:\mathcal{M}_{k+1,1}(\beta,A)\to L
at the
i
th marked point.
Consider the length-0 closed open map

(2.3)

{\mathcal{CO}}^{0}:QH^{*}(X)\to H^{*}({\mathcal{B}}(L)_{alg}),\;{\mathcal{CO}}^{0}(PD[A]):=\sum_{\beta\in H_{2}(X,L)}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(ev_{0}^{k})_{!}((ev_{1}^{k})^{*}b\wedge\cdots\wedge(ev_{k}^{k})^{*}b).

Definition 2.8
.

Let
X
be a symplectic manifold with finite group
G
acting effectively on it(
G
may be trivial), and
L
be a
G
-equivariant weakly unobstructed Lagrangian. Then the
orbifold Kodaira-Spencer map
is

\mathfrak{ks}_{orb}:=\Psi_{\widehat{G}}\circ\Phi\circ{\mathcal{CO}}^{0}.

Note that if
G
is trivial, then
\mathfrak{ks}_{orb}
is just the ordinary Kodaira-Spencer map
\mathfrak{ks}
. It was shown in
[
CL24
]
that
\mathfrak{ks}_{orb}
is well-defined, and furthermore it is a ring homomorphism.

2.2.
Computation of orbifold Kodaira-Spencer maps from elliptic curves

We choose three different Lagrangian submanifolds on elliptic curves and compute the orbifold Kodaira-Spencer maps with respect to them.

2.2.1.
\mathbb{Z}_{3}

Let
E=\mathbb{C}/(\mathbb{Z}+e^{2\pi i/3}\mathbb{Z})
be an elliptic curve and let
\mathbb{Z}_{3}=\{1,\rho=e^{2\pi i/3},\rho^{2}=e^{4\pi i/3}\}
act on
E
by multiplication, so
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{3,3,3}=E/\mathbb{Z}_{3}
. Let
{\mathbb{L}}_{0}\subset E
be an embedded circle in
E
, and
{\mathbb{L}}_{1}:=\rho{\mathbb{L}}_{0}
,
{\mathbb{L}}_{2}:=\rho^{2}{\mathbb{L}}_{0}
as in Figure

. Then
\widetilde{{\mathbb{L}}}:={\mathbb{L}}_{0}\oplus{\mathbb{L}}_{1}\oplus{\mathbb{L}}_{2}

W_{333}
.

Let

be a homology cycle representing a class
(1,0)
.
Since
\dim\mathcal{M}_{k+1,1}(\beta,C_{h})=k+\mu(\beta)
, if
\beta\in H_{2}(E,\widetilde{{\mathbb{L}}})
is nontrivial then there is no summand in (
2.3
) for

\mathcal{M}_{1,1}(0,C_{h})

\widetilde{{\mathbb{L}}}

is given by (Poincaré dual of) the oriented intersection

Taking orientations into account, we have

Observe that

\frac{X\wedge Y}{\bar{Z}}=\frac{Y\wedge Z}{\bar{X}}=\frac{Z\wedge X}{\bar{Y}}=\frac{X\wedge(Y\wedge Z)}{p}=c_{\mathbb{L}}

(

The isomorphism

4.
Main results

A
Frobenius algebra
is a unital associative algebra over
\Bbbk
together with a nondegenerate bilinear form
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
such that
\langle xy,z\rangle=\langle x,yz\rangle
. Observe that given a nondegenerate pairing
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
on a Frobenius algebra, the rescaling

\langle c\cdot,c\cdot\rangle
for
c\neq 0
can be still used to define a new Frobenius algebra with the same ring structure. Given two Frobenius algebras
(A,\langle\;,\;\rangle_{A})
and
(B,\langle\;,\;\rangle_{B})
,
f:A\to B
is an isomorphism of Frobenius algebras iff it is a ring isomorphism and an isometry with respect to nondegenerate pairings. Given unities
1_{A}\in A
and
1_{B}\in B
, let
{\rm{tr}}_{A}:A\to\Bbbk
and
{\rm{tr}}_{B}:B\to\Bbbk
be maps (a.k.a.
trace
s) given by
\langle\cdot,1_{A}\rangle_{A}
and
\langle\cdot,1_{B}\rangle_{B}
respectively. Then
f:A\to B
is an isometry if and only if it preserves traces.

For a compact symplectic manifold, the quantum cup product together with the Poincaré pairing
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{PD}
gives rise to a Frobenius algebra structure on
QH^{*}(X)
.
For an isolated singularity
W\in\Bbbk[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}]
, we equip
{\rm Jac}(W)
with the residue pairing

\langle f,g\rangle_{res}=(-1)^{\frac{n(n-1)}{2}}{\rm{Res}}\begin{bmatrix}fg\cdot dx_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge dx_{n}\\
\partial_{x_{1}}W,\cdots,\partial_{x_{n}}W\end{bmatrix}.

As pointed out in
[
CLS20
]
, the Kodaira-Spencer map does not intertwine pairings
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{PD}
and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{res}
in general. Examining the Cardy condition, we are led to consider a modification of the residue pairing by the constant given by ratio of Floer volume form and the usual volume form on the reference Lagrangian
{\mathbb{L}}
. In Section
2.2
we denoted such ratios by
c_{\mathbb{L}}
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
X
be an elliptic orbisphere and
W\in\Lambda[x,y,z]
be the LG mirror to
X
. Let
A=(QH^{*}(X),\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{PD})
and
B=({\rm Jac}(W),\langle c_{\mathbb{L}}\cdot,c_{\mathbb{L}}\cdot\rangle_{res})
be Frobenius algebras where
c_{\mathbb{L}}
is the rescaling constant of Seidel Lagrangian
{\mathbb{L}}\subset X
. Then

(4.1)

c_{\mathbb{L}}^{2}\cdot\mathfrak{ks}({\rm{pt}}_{X})=-\frac{\det\;{\rm Hess}(W)}{\mu},

where
\mu=\dim{\rm Jac}(W)
.
As a result,
\mathfrak{ks}:A\to B
is an isomorphism of Frobenius algebras, i.e. it preserves traces as follows:

\langle{\rm{pt}}_{X},1_{X}\rangle_{PD}=\langle c_{\mathbb{L}}\cdot\mathfrak{ks}({\rm{pt}}_{X}),c_{\mathbb{L}}\cdot 1\rangle_{res}.

Provided (
4.1
), the second statement follows immediately from a classical result on the residue over an isolated singularity (the author thanks Cheol-Hyun Cho for pointing out this fact).

Theorem 4.2

(Section 17,
[
Har66
]
)
.

{\rm{Res}}\begin{bmatrix}{\det\;\rm{Hess}}(W)\cdot dx_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge dx_{n}\\
\partial_{x_{1}}W,\cdots,\partial_{x_{n}}W\end{bmatrix}=\mu.

We can easily compare
\mathfrak{ks}:QH^{*}(X)\to{\rm Jac}(W)
and
\mathfrak{ks}_{orb}:H^{*}(E)\to{\rm Jac}(W,{\widehat{G}})
as follows. Basically, holomorphic discs employed in the computation of
\mathfrak{ks}_{orb}({\rm{pt}}_{E})
are same as those used for
\mathfrak{ks}({\rm{pt}}_{X})
, but the discs which are related by
G
-action on
E
are identified as the same disc on
X
. Thus,
|G|
different discs which are used for
\mathfrak{ks}_{orb}({\rm{pt}}_{E})
project to a single disc for
\mathfrak{ks}({\rm{pt}}_{X})
. We conclude that

\mathfrak{ks}_{orb}({\rm{pt}}_{E})=\frac{1}{|G|}\cdot\mathfrak{ks}({\rm{pt}}_{X}).

The rest of the paper is thus devoted to prove

c_{\mathbb{L}}^{2}\cdot\mathfrak{ks}_{orb}({\rm{pt}}_{E})=-\frac{\det\;{\rm Hess}(W)}{|G|\cdot\mu}

where
G=\mathbb{Z}_{3},\mathbb{Z}_{4}
or
\mathbb{Z}_{6}
acts on
E
, with
W
given accordingly with respect to
G
-equivariant Seidel Lagrangian.

4.4.
Nontrivial identitites of formal power series

Recall from
[
ACHL20
]
that

\mathfrak{ks}({\rm{pt}}_{X})=\frac{1}{8}q\cdot\frac{\partial W}{\partial q}

where
X
is an orbisphere
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{a,b,c}
with area
1
(If the area is
A
, then we take
\frac{1}{8A}
instead of
\frac{1}{8}
). Hence we also have

\mathfrak{ks}_{orb}({\rm{pt}}_{E})=\frac{1}{8|G|}q\cdot\frac{\partial W}{\partial q}.

We computed
\mathfrak{ks}_{orb}({\rm{pt}}_{E})
in another way, namely by orbifold Jacobian algebra structure. Let
W_{333}
,
W_{244}
and
W_{236}
be mirror superpotentials from Seidel Lagrangians
{\mathbb{L}}_{333}\subset\mathbb{P}^{1}_{3,3,3}
,
{\mathbb{L}}_{244}\subset\mathbb{P}^{1}_{2,4,4}
and
{\mathbb{L}}_{236}\subset\mathbb{P}^{1}_{2,3,6}
respectively.

Let us consider
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{3,3,3}
. Recall that

W_{333}=\phi(x^{3}+y^{3}+z^{3})-\psi xyz

with its coefficients are

\phi=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}(-1)^{k+1}(k+\frac{1}{2})q^{(6k+3)^{2}},\quad\psi=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}(-1)^{k+1}(6k+1)q^{(6k+1)^{2}}.

We also have

c_{{\mathbb{L}}_{333}}=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}(-1)^{k}q^{(6k+1)^{2}}.

By the following

\displaystyle\frac{c_{{\mathbb{L}}_{333}}^{2}}{24}q\cdot\big(\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial q}(x^{3}+y^{3}+z^{3})-\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial q}xyz\big)=\frac{c_{{\mathbb{L}}_{333}}^{2}}{24}q\cdot\big(\frac{\psi}{\phi}\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial q}-\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial q}\big)xyz=\big(-9\phi^{3}+\frac{\psi^{3}}{3}\big)xyz,

we derive an identity of two arithmetics of formal power series

\frac{c_{{\mathbb{L}}_{333}}^{2}}{24}q\cdot\big(\frac{\psi}{\phi}\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial q}-\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial q}\big)=\big(-9\phi^{3}+\frac{\psi^{3}}{3}\big).

Note that the identity was first proved in
[
CLS20
]
using theory of modular forms, and now we have a new proof without appealing to number theory.

The same argument can be applied to other two cases. For
W_{244}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{q^{3}}{32}\big(6q^{5}x^{2}-xyz+\frac{\partial a}{\partial q}(y^{4}+z^{4})+\frac{\partial b}{\partial q}y^{2}z^{2}\big)

\displaystyle=\frac{q^{3}}{32}\big(4q^{5}+\frac{1}{a}\frac{\partial a}{\partial q}\cdot(q^{6}-4bq^{10})+4q^{10}\frac{\partial b}{\partial q}\big)x^{2}

\displaystyle=\big(\frac{q^{8}}{2}-4q^{12}b+8q^{16}b^{2}-32q^{16}a^{2}\big)x^{2}

which implies

\frac{q^{3}}{32}\big(4q^{5}+\frac{1}{a}\frac{\partial a}{\partial q}\cdot(q^{6}-4bq^{10})+4q^{10}\frac{\partial b}{\partial q}\big)=\frac{q^{8}}{2}-4q^{12}b+8q^{16}b^{2}-32q^{16}a^{2}

where
a
and
b
are formal power series given by

a=\sum_{r\geq 0}(2r+1)q^{16(2r+1)^{2}-4}+\sum_{s>r\geq 0}(2r+2s+2)q^{16(2r+1)(2s+1)-4},

b=\sum_{r\geq 1,s\geq 1}\big(-(4r+4s-2)q^{16(2r-1)2s-4}+(2r+2s)q^{64rs-4}\big).

We note that
c_{{\mathbb{L}}_{2,4,4}}=\pm q
, so its square is
q^{2}
. For
W_{236}
we can obtain a similar type of identity which is omitted.

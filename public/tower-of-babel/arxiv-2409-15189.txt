Notes on embedding trees in graphs with O ⁡ ( | T | ) O(|T|) -sized covers
Alexey Pokrovskiy

Abstract
This is a companion paper to the paper “Hyperstability in the Erdős-Sós Conjecture”. In that paper the following rough structure theorem was proved for graphs G G containing no copy of a bounded degree tree T T : from any such G G , one can delete o ⁡ ( | G | ​ | T | ) o(|G||T|) edges in order to get a subgraph each of whose connected components have a vertex cover of order 3 ​ | T | 3|T| . This theorem creates an incentive for studying graphs whose connected components have covers of order O ⁡ ( | T | ) O(|T|) — and this is what will be explored here. It turns out that such graphs are amenable to regularity approaches which have been successful in studying dense T T -free graphs. In this paper we will follow such an approach from the paper “On the Erdős–Sós conjecture for trees with bounded degree” by Besomi, Pavez-Signé, and Stein and show how it can be adapted from dense graphs to graphs with a small vertex cover.

One of the biggest successes of modern combinatorics has been Szemeredi’s Regularity Lemma — a tool which gives a unified approach for studying all sorts of questions about dense graphs. Here “dense graph” means a graph
G
where
e(G)=\Omega(|G|^{2})
. This lemma provides a way to approximate large dense graphs by small weighted graphs.
A notable example where Szemerédi’s Regularity Lemma plays a key role is in the context of the Erdős-Sós Conjecture. This conjecture predicts what the Turán number of trees should be:

Conjecture 1.1

(Erdős, Sós, see
[
6
]
)
.

Every graph
G
with
>(d-1)n/2
edges contains a copy of every
d
-edge tree
T
.

There are several different constructions which are extremal and near-extremal for this conjecture. One is to consider
n/d
vertex-disjoint complete subgraphs of order
d
— this has
(n/d)\binom{d}{2}=(d-1)n/2
but no copies of
T
(since the connected components don’t have enough vertices to house
T
). If
T
is a star, then any
(d-1)
-regular graph contains no
T
and has
(d-1)n/2
edges. If
T
is balanced (i.e. both parts of its bipartition have order
(d+1)/2
) then consider
G
be a graph containing
(d-1)/2
vertices which are joined to everything, and no other edges. Then
e(G)=n(d-1)/2-(d-1)(d-5)/8
, which, for large
n
, shows that
G
is near-extremal for the Erdős-Sós Conjecture.

Much is known about the conjecture. In 1959, predating the conjecture, Erdős and Gallai proved it for paths. For stars, the conjecture is an easy exercise. There are more early results about specific classes of trees
[
3
]
, and about
n=d+1

[
15
]
.
In the early 1990s, Ajtai, Komlós, Simonovits, and Szemerédi announced a complete proof of the Erdős-Sós Conjecture for sufficiently large
d
. The statement of their result is as follows:

Theorem 1.2

(Ajtai, Komlós, Simonovits, and Szemerédi)
.

For sufficiently large
d
, every graph
G
with
>(d-1)n/2
edges contains a copy of every
d
-edge tree
T
.

Although the proof of this theorem has not yet appeared, several details about its approach are known. The proof strategy involves an adapted version of Szemerédi’s Regularity Lemma, tailored to sparse graphs, and a careful embedding of parts of the tree into regular pairs of the graph. This method is supplemented by stability and extremal case analysis to achieve an exact result. Over the intervening decades, interest in replicating or extending this proof has grown, particularly given that the Erdős-Sós Conjecture is not an isolated problem but has numerous variants (see surveys like
[
14
]
).

For dense graphs, significant progress has been made on understanding the conjecture and the techniques of Ajtai, Komlós, Simonovits, and Szemerédi. Rozhoň
[
12
]
and Besomi, Pavez-Signé, and Stein
[
2
]
independently proved approximate versions of the conjecture for trees with bounded maximum degree. Moreover, Besomi, Pavez-Signé, and Stein
[
2
]
extended their work to an exact result for dense graphs when the tree has bounded degree. Very recently, a proof of the approximate version of the conjecture has been announced for dense graphs without any degree restrictions by Davoodi, Piguet, Řada, and Sanhueza-Matamala
[
5
]
. See the survey
[
14
]
for a more detailed history of the conjecture.

From the above we see that we have a pretty good understanding of the conjecture of dense graphs. But sparse graphs, our understanding of how to apply the techniques of Ajtai, Komlós, Simonovits, and Szemerédi remains incomplete.
The author recently proved the following theorem which is tailored specifically to understanding the sparse case of the Erdős-Sós Conjecture and its relatives. Recall that a cover in a graph is a set of vertices
C
which has the property that all edges have one or both of their vertices in
C
.

Theorem 1.3

(
[
10
]
)
.

Let
\Delta\in\mathbb{N}
and
\varepsilon>0
, and let
d
be sufficiently large. Let
T
be a tree with
d
edges and
\Delta(T)\leq\Delta
. For any graph
G
with
e(G)\geq\varepsilon d|G|
having no copies of
T
, it is possible to delete
\varepsilon e(G)
edges to obtain a graph

, each of whose connected components has a cover of order
\leq 3d
.

This theorem allows us to analyse the Erdős-Sós Conjecture even in the sparse case by reducing the problem to studying graphs with small covers. While graphs with covers of order
\leq 3d
are not necessarily dense, they still turn out to be amenable to various techniques used for studying dense graphs. See Sections 1.1 – 1.3 of
[
10
]
for some simple examples of this. The current paper is concerned with giving more complicated ways of studying graphs with small covers — in particular studying them using Szemerédi’s Regularity Lemma.
Our results and their proofs are all heavily inspired by the paper of Besomi, Pavez-Signé, and Stein
[
2
]
where the same sorts of results were proved for dense graphs. Our first result is a stability theorem — a theorem which says that if
G
has slightly fewer edges than the Erdős-Sós Conjecture asks for, then

is close in structure to one of the near-extremal constructions in that conjecture.

Theorem 1.4

(Stability theorem)
.

Let
\Delta\gg\alpha\gg\varepsilon\gg k^{-1}
.
Let

be a

-vertex tree with
\Delta(T)\leq\Delta
.
Let

be a graph with

, and let

be a cover of order

. If

has no copy of

, then there is a subgraph

of

satisfying one of:

(i)

\delta(H)\geq(1-\alpha)k
and
|H|\leq(1+\alpha)k
.

(ii)

is bipartite with parts

such that
|X|\leq(1+\alpha)k/2
,
|Y|\geq 6k

\delta(X)\geq(1-\alpha)|Y|
,
\delta(Y)\geq(1-\alpha)k/2

Stability theorems are often paired with extremal case analysis results that prove Conjecture
1.1
when

contains contains the structure provided by the stability theorem. The first of these we prove deals with when

has an almost-complete subgraph as provided by (i) above.

Theorem 1.5

(Almost-complete extremal analysis)
.

Let
\Delta^{-7}/1000\geq\varepsilon\geq 3k^{-1}
.
Let

be a connected graph with

\delta(G)\geq 128\Delta^{2}\sqrt{\varepsilon}k
containing a subgraph

contains a copy of every

.

The second extremal case analysis result that we prove deals with the case when

has an almost-complete bipartite subgraph as provided by (ii) of Theorem

Theorem 1.6

(Almost-complete bipartite extremal analysis)
.

Let
\Delta^{-7}/1000\geq\varepsilon\geq k^{-1}
.
Let

be a
k
-edge tree with
\Delta(T)\leq\Delta
.
Let

be a connected graph with
\delta(G)\geq k/2
. Suppose that

such that
|X|\leq(1+\varepsilon)k/2

.

The combination of these theorems together with Theorem
1.3
allows one to prove the Erdős-Sós Conjecture for large bounded degree trees. This proof can be found in
[
10
]
.

Additionally, we prove a stronger version of Theorem
1.3
which sharpens the constant “
3
” to the asymptotically optimal “

”.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
\Delta,\varepsilon\gg d^{-1}
. Let
T
be a tree with
k
edges and
\Delta(T)\leq\Delta
. For any graph
G
with
e(G)\geq\varepsilon k|G|
having no copies of
T
, it is possible to delete
\varepsilon e(G)
edges to obtain a graph
H
, each of whose connected components has a cover of order
\leq(1+\varepsilon)k
.

This bound can seen to be near-optimal by considering a graph

of vertex disjoint cliques of order

. This

has no copy of

because its components are too small. But by deleting
\varepsilon e(G)
edges, at least one of the cliques will have

(1-2\sqrt{\varepsilon})k
(the maximum number of edges that can touch

The proof of this theorem doesn’t involve modifying the original proof of Theorem
1.3

We’ll need the following simple fact about integer parts.

Fact 2.5

For any

A lot of our intermediate lemmas will be stated via the notion of cut-density, a concept which was first introduced by Conlon, Fox, and Sudakov

Definition 2.6

A graph

Lemma 2.9

(
[

)

Let

Lemma 2.10

One weakness of cut-density is that it only makes sense for dense graphs. When working with Theorem

3
Stability analysis

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.4
. We start by introducing the fundamentals of Szemerédi’s Regularity Lemma in the next section. Afterwards we go over the techniques of Besomi, Pavez-Signé, and Stein and extract some useful lemmas from there. Then we use these to prove some stability lemmas, culminating in Theorem
1.4
.

3.1
Regularity preliminaries

We begin by introducing the basics of the regularity method.
A pair of disjoint vertex sets
(A,B)
in a graph
G
is said to be
\varepsilon
-regular if for all subsets
A^{\prime}\subseteq A,B^{\prime}\subseteq B
having
|A^{\prime}|\geq\varepsilon|A|,|B^{\prime}|\geq\varepsilon|B|
, we have
e_{G}(A^{\prime},B^{\prime})=(1\pm\varepsilon)|A^{\prime}||B^{\prime}|\frac{e_{G}(A,B)}{|A||B|}
.
An
(\varepsilon,\eta)
-regularity partition of
G
is a partition
V(G)=V_{1},\dots,V_{l}
with:

•

|V_{1}|=\dots=|V_{l}|
.

•

V_{i}
is independent in
G
.

•

For
i\neq j
, the pair
(V_{i},V_{j})
is
\varepsilon
-regular with density either
\geq\eta
or
=0
.

The
(\varepsilon,\eta)
-reduced graph of the partition is the graph
R
on
V(R)=\{V_{1},\dots,V_{l}\}
with
V_{i}V_{j}
an edge whenever
d(V_{i},V_{j})\geq\eta
. We now state the degree form of the regularity lemma (see
[
7
]
, Theorem 1.10).

Theorem 3.1

(Szemerédi’s Regularity Lemma)
.

Let
\varepsilon,m^{-1}\gg M^{-1}
. Every graph
G
has a subgraph
G^{\prime}
such that
|G|-|G^{\prime}|\leq\varepsilon n
, every vertex
v\in G^{\prime}
has
d_{G}(v)-d_{G^{\prime}}(v)\leq(\varepsilon+\eta)n
, and
G^{\prime}
has an
(\varepsilon,\eta)
-regular partition where the number of parts is in
[m,M]
.

We say that a partition
V_{1},\dots,V_{k}

refines
another partition
U_{1},\dots,U_{m}
if for all
i
, we have
V_{i}\subseteq U_{j}
for some
j
. It is a standard fact that in Theorem
3.1
, one specify some initial partition
V(G)=U_{1}\cup\dots\cup U_{m}
, and then ensure that the partition produced by the lemma refines this.

Note that in this theorem
e(G)-e(G^{\prime})\leq\sum_{v\in V(G)}(d_{G}(v)-d_{G^{\prime}}(v))\leq(|G|-|G^{\prime}|)n+\sum_{v\in V(G^{\prime})}(d_{G}(v)-d_{G^{\prime}}(v))\leq\varepsilon n^{2}+\sum_{v\in V(G^{\prime})}(\varepsilon+\eta)n\leq(2\varepsilon+\eta)n^{2}
.

Fact 3.2
.

Let
G
be an
n
-vertex,
q
-cut-dense graph and
G^{\prime}
a subgraph such that every vertex
v\in G^{\prime}
has
d_{G}(v)-d_{G^{\prime}}(v)\leq\alpha n
. Then
G^{\prime}
is
(q-2\alpha)
-cut-dense.

Proof.

Consider a partition
V(G^{\prime})=U\cup V
. Without loss of generality
|U|\leq|V|
giving
|V|\geq n/2
. Let
V^{\prime}=V\cup(V(G)\setminus V(G^{\prime}))
— so now
U,V^{\prime}
is a partition of
V(G)
. We have
e_{G^{\prime}}(U,V)=e_{G^{\prime}}(U,V^{\prime})=\sum_{u\in U}|N_{G^{\prime}}(u)\cap V^{\prime}|=\sum_{u\in U}(|N_{G}(u)\cap V^{\prime}|-|(N_{G}(u)\setminus N_{G^{\prime}}(u))\cap V^{\prime}|)\geq\sum_{u\in U}|N_{G}(u)\cap V^{\prime}|-|N_{G}(u)\setminus N_{G^{\prime}}(u)|=e_{G}(U,V^{\prime})-\sum_{u\in U}(d_{G}(u)-d_{G^{\prime}}(u))\geq q|U||V^{\prime}|-\sum_{u\in U}\alpha n=q|U||V|-\alpha|U|n\geq q|U||V|-2\alpha|U||V|=(q-2\alpha)|U||V|
.
∎

This fact is useful in combination with the following.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
q>\eta
.
Let
G
be
q
-cut-dense, and
R
a
(\eta,\varepsilon)
-reduced graph of
G
. Then
R
is connected.

Proof.

Let
V_{1},\dots,V_{t}
be the regularity partition corresponding to
R
.
If
R
is disconnected then we can partition
\{V_{1},\dots,V_{t}\}=X\cup Y
into non-empty sets so that there are no edges of
R
from
X
to
Y
. Let
X^{\prime}=\bigcup_{V_{i}\in X}V_{i}
,
Y^{\prime}=\bigcup_{V_{j}\in Y}V_{j}
be the corresponding subsets of
G
. For
V_{i}\in X,V_{j}\in Y
, since
V_{i}V_{j}\not\in E(R)
, we have
e_{G}(V_{i},V_{j})<\eta|V_{i}||V_{j}|
. This gives
e_{G}(X^{\prime},Y^{\prime})=\sum_{V_{i}\in X}\sum_{V_{j}\in Y}e_{G}(V_{i},V_{j})<\sum_{V_{i}\in X}\sum_{V_{j}\in Y}\eta|V_{i}||V_{j}|=\eta|X^{\prime}||Y^{\prime}|
(using that
\sum_{V_{j}\in Y}|V_{j}|=|Y^{\prime}|
,
\sum_{V_{i}\in X}|V_{j}|=|X^{\prime}|
for the last equation). But by
q
-cut-density, we have
e_{G}(X^{\prime},Y^{\prime})\geq q|X^{\prime}||Y^{\prime}|>\eta|X^{\prime}||Y^{\prime}|
, a contradiction.
∎

We’ll use a standard regularity inheritance lemma.

Fact 3.4
.

Let
\varepsilon<1/8
.
Let
(A,B)
be an
(\varepsilon,\eta)
-regular pair, and
A^{\prime}\subseteq A,B^{\prime}\subseteq B
be subsets of size
|A^{\prime}|=|A|/2,|B^{\prime}|=|B|/2
. Then
(A^{\prime},B^{\prime})
is
(3\varepsilon,\eta/2)
-regular.

Proof.

By
\varepsilon
-regularity, we have
e(A^{\prime},B^{\prime})/|A^{\prime}||B^{\prime}|=(1\pm\varepsilon)e(A,B)/|A||B|
which gives
e(A,B)/|A||B|=(1\pm 3\varepsilon/2)e(A^{\prime},B^{\prime})/|A^{\prime}||B^{\prime}|
and
e(A^{\prime},B^{\prime})/|A^{\prime}||B^{\prime}|\geq(1-\varepsilon)\eta\geq\eta/2
. Let
A^{\prime\prime}\subseteq A^{\prime},B^{\prime\prime}\subseteq B^{\prime}
have
|A^{\prime\prime}|\geq 2\varepsilon|A^{\prime}|\geq\varepsilon|A|,|B^{\prime\prime}|\geq 2\varepsilon|B^{\prime}|\geq\varepsilon|B|
. By
(\varepsilon,\eta)
-regularity, we have
e(A^{\prime\prime},B^{\prime\prime})/|A^{\prime\prime}||B^{\prime\prime}|=(1\pm\varepsilon)e(A,B)/|A||B|=(1\pm\varepsilon)(1\pm 3\varepsilon/2)e(A^{\prime},B^{\prime})/|A^{\prime}||B^{\prime}|=(1\pm 3\varepsilon)e(A^{\prime},B^{\prime})/|A^{\prime}||B^{\prime}|
.
∎

A fractional matching in a graph is a function
f:E(G)\to[0,1]
for which
\sum_{y\in N(v)}f(vy)\leq 1
for all vertices
v
. A fractional cover in a graph is a function
g:V(G)\to[0,1]
for which
g(u)+g(v)\geq 1
for all edges
uv
.
The fractional matching number
\nu_{f}(G):=\max_{f\text{ fractional matching of G}}f(E(G))
and the fractional cover number
\nu_{f}(G):=\max_{\text{$g$ fractional cover of G}}g(V(G))
are known to be equal via linear programming duality. Moreover, it is known that optimal fractional matching/covers always exist whose values are always in the set
\{0,1/2,1\}
:

Theorem 3.5

(see
[
13
]
, Theorems 2.1.5 and 2.1.6)
.

Let
G
be a graph. There exists a fractional matching
f:E(G)\to\{0,1/2,1\}
and a fractional cover
g:V(G)\to\{0,1/2,1\}
so that
f(E(G))=\nu_{f}(G)=\tau_{f}(G)=g(V(G))
.

When working with regularity, it is well known that fractional matchings are as good as normal matchings.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
\varepsilon<1/2
.
Let
G
be a graph and
R
a
(\varepsilon,\eta)
-reduced graph of
G
with
|R|\leq M_{0}
in which the size of each part is even. Let
M
be a fractional matching in

. Then there is a
(3\varepsilon,\eta/2)
-reduced graph
R^{\prime}
of

with
|R|^{\prime}=2|R|\leq 2M_{0}
, which has a matching

with
e(M^{\prime})/|R^{\prime}|=e(M^{\prime})/2|R|=\nu_{f}(R)/|R|
.

Proof.

Let
V_{1},\dots,V_{l}
be the partition giving rise to

. For each
i
, fix some partition
V_{i}=V_{i}^{-}\cup V_{i}^{+}
into sets of equal size. Using Fact

, the partition of

into

is
(3\varepsilon,\eta/2)
-regular, and hence we can define a corresponding
(3\varepsilon,\eta/2)
-reduced graph

, noting that
|R^{\prime}|=2l=2|R|
. Use Theorem
3.5
to get a fractional matching

with

. Let

and

, noting that

. Note that the condition “

touch precisely one edge of

. This shows that

is a vertex-disjoint union of a matching

and a maximum degree

. Hence

by having two copies of each edge in

have indegree/outdegree
\leq 1

Let

, noting that this is a matching in

(there cannot be two edges through any

since

has outdegree
\leq 1
, and there cannot two edges through any

has indegree
\leq 1

satisfies the lemma.
∎

We’ll also need the following standard lemma about finding matchings/stars in graphs of a certain minimum degree.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let

and

Let

be a collection of vertex-disjoint stars picked so that

for all

(2)

k
is as large as possible, subject to (1) holding.

(3)

is as large as possible, subject to (1) and (2) holding.

Suppose that the stars are ordered so that
2\Delta\geq e(S_{1})\geq e(S_{2})\geq\dots\geq e(S_{k})
.
Suppose that
e(S_{1}),\dots,e(S_{t})=2\Delta
, and
e(S_{t+1}),\dots,e(S_{k})<2\Delta
. If

or

, we are done, so suppose

and
k<5d
. Using
|G|\geq 10\Delta d
, there is a vertex
v\not\in\bigcup S_{i}
. For
i=1,\dots,t
, let
c_{i}
be the center of
S_{i}
(which exists since

). Note that

has a neighbour
u

If

with

, then let

with

gives a family of stars with (1) satisfied and larger

If

Proof.

\Delta,\varepsilon\gg\gamma\gg k^{-1}
.

Say a subgraph

be a maximal collection of good subgraphs which are vertex-disjoint in

. Indeed otherwise apply Lemma

to

in order to get a subgraph with

Another part of the proof of Theorem
1.7
is to prove that theorem for dense graphs. This will use the following.

Theorem 5.2

(Rödl-Wysocka,
[
11
]
)
.

Let
1/2\geq\gamma\gg n^{-1}
.
Every graph with
e(G)\geq\gamma n^{2}
contains a
\lceil 0.4\gamma^{3}n\rceil
-regular subgraph.

Lemma 5.3

(
[
10
]
)
.

Let
p,q,\varepsilon,\Delta^{-1}\gg d^{-1}

and let
T
be a order
d
tree with
\Delta(T)\leq\Delta
. Let
G
be a
n
-vertex,
q
-cut-dense graph, that contains an
pn
-regular, order
\geq(2+\varepsilon)d
subgraph
R
. Then
G
has a copy of
T
.

The above lemma can be rephrased as the following lemma about matchings in the reduced graph.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
M_{0}^{-1},\Delta,\varepsilon,q\gg n^{-1},k^{-1}
.
Let
T
be a tree with
\Delta(T)\leq\Delta
and
k
vertices.
Let
G
be a
q
-cut-dense graph and
R
a
(\varepsilon,5\sqrt{\varepsilon})
-reduced graph of
G
with
|R|\leq M_{0}
. Suppose that
R
has a matching
M
with
e(M)\geq(1+10\sqrt{\varepsilon})k|R|/n

Then
G
has a copy
T
.

Proof.

Let
M_{0}^{-1},\Delta,\varepsilon,q\gg p\gg n^{-1},k^{-1}
.
Let
M^{\prime}
be the bipartite subgraph of
G
consisting of only the pairs in
M
, and let
H
be the largest
pn
-regular subgraph of
M^{\prime}
.

Consider some pair
(A,B)\in M
. We have
e(G[A,B]\setminus H)\leq 3(pn/|G[A,B]\setminus H|)^{1/3}|G[A,B]\setminus H|^{2}
, as otherwise Theorem
5.2
would give a
\lceil 0.4(3(pn/|G[A,B]\setminus H|)^{1/3})^{3}|G[A,B]\setminus H|\rceil\geq pn
-regular subgraph disjoint from
H
(contradicting maximality of
H
). Also note that
|H\cap A|=|H\cap B|
because
H[A,B]
is regular and bipartite with parts
A,B
. These show that
|H\cap A|,|H\cap B|\leq\varepsilon n/|R|
, as otherwise the fact that
(A,B)
is
\varepsilon
-regular of density
\geq 5\sqrt{\varepsilon}
would give
e(|H\cap A|,|H\cap B|)\geq(1-\varepsilon)5\sqrt{\varepsilon}|H\cap A||H\cap B|\geq(1-\varepsilon)5\sqrt{\varepsilon}(\varepsilon n/|R|)^{2}>3(pn/(2n/|R|))^{1/3}(2n/|R|)^{2}>3(pn/|G[A,B]\setminus H|)^{1/3}|G[A,B]\setminus H|^{2}
.

Using the bound from the previous paragraph, we have that
|H|=\sum_{(A,B)\in M}(|H\cap A|+|H\cap B|)\geq(1-\varepsilon)(2n/|R|)e(M)\geq(1-\varepsilon)(2n/|R|)(1+10\sqrt{\varepsilon})k|R|/n\geq(1+9\sqrt{\varepsilon})2k
. Thus Lemma
5.3
applies to give a copy of
T
.
∎

Via König’s Theorem we get the following.

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
\Delta^{-1},q,\gg\varepsilon\gg k^{-1}
.
Let
T
be a tree with
\Delta(T)\leq\Delta
and
k
vertices.
Let
G
be a bipartite graph which is
q
-cut-dense with parts
X,Y
with no copy of
T
. Then
G
has a subgraph
G^{\prime}
with
e(G^{\prime})\geq e(G)-10\sqrt{\varepsilon}n^{2}
that has a cover of order
\leq(1+10\sqrt{\varepsilon})k
.

Proof.

Let
\Delta^{-1},q,\gg\varepsilon\gg m^{-1}\gg M^{-1}\gg k^{-1}
.
Let
n_{0}:=|G|
. If
k\leq qn_{0}/4
, then note that Fact
2.7
gives
\delta(G)\geq 0.9qn
, and so we can greedily find
T
using Fact
2.2
. Thus suppose
k>qn_{0}/4
.
Use Szemerédi’s Regularity Lemma to get a subgraph
G^{\prime}
of
G
and a
(\varepsilon,5\sqrt{\varepsilon})
-regularity partition with
s+t\in[m,M]
parts
X_{1},\dots,X_{s},Y_{1},\dots,Y_{t}
refining the partition
\{X,Y\}
, labelled so that
X_{1},\dots,X_{s}\subseteq X,Y_{1},\dots,Y_{t}\subseteq Y
. Let
n:=|G^{\prime}|
, noting that
n\geq(1-\varepsilon)n_{0}
, which in particular gives
\Delta^{-1},q,\varepsilon,m^{-1},M^{-1}\gg n^{-1}
.
Let
R
be the reduced graph of this partition. By Fact
3.2
,
G^{\prime}
is
(q-2(\varepsilon+5\sqrt{\varepsilon}))\geq q/2
-cut-dense. By Lemma
5.4
,

has no matching of size

. By König’s Theorem,

has a cover

of order
\leq(1+10\sqrt{\varepsilon})k|R|/n
. Let

be the union of the parts giving

to get a set of vertices with

Theorem
.

Let
\Delta,\varepsilon\gg d^{-1}
. Let
T
be a tree with
k
edges and
\Delta(T)\leq\Delta
. For any graph
G
with
e(G)\geq\varepsilon k|G|
having no copies of
T
, it is possible to delete
\varepsilon e(G)
edges to obtain a graph
H
, each of whose connected components has a cover of order
\leq(1+\varepsilon)k
.

Proof.

Pick
\Delta,\varepsilon\gg\gamma\gg d^{-1}

Apply Theorem
1.3
in order to get a subgraph
G^{\prime}
with
e(G^{\prime})\geq(1-\gamma)e(G)
whose connected components
G_{1},\dots,G_{t}
have covers
D_{1}\dots,D_{t}
of order
10t
. To each

apply Lemma
5.7
in order to get a subgraph

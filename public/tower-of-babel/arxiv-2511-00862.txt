Investigation of 2 ​ ν 2\nu ECEC in 132 Ba and revisited for 78 Kr
Deepak Patel, Praveen C. Srivastava

Abstract
We present a theoretical investigation of two-neutrino double electron capture ( 2 ​ ν 2\nu ECEC) in 132 Ba and 78 Kr based on large-scale shell-model calculations. The nuclear matrix elements (NMEs) for the 2 ​ ν 2\nu ECEC process in 132 Ba and 78 Kr have been calculated using the SN100PN and GWBXG effective interactions, respectively. The reliability of these interactions is first assessed by reproducing the spectroscopic properties of the parent, intermediate, and granddaughter nuclei of the decay. Two different formulations are employed to estimate the half-lives. We also examine the cumulative contribution of 1 + 1^{+} and 0 + 0^{+} states in the intermediate nuclei to the NMEs associated with the Gamow-Teller (GT) and Fermi transitions, respectively. Our results provide an improved prediction of the NME and half-life for 2 ​ ν 2\nu ECEC in 78 Kr compared to previous studies and offer a precise theoretical benchmark for future experimental investigations in 132 Ba.

[

17

[

2\nu

2\nu

Among the nuclei that are energetically allowed to undergo
2\nu
ECEC,

In this work, we carry out a detailed investigation of the

To ensure a more precise estimation of the NME, we first aim to validate the reliability of the employed effective interaction by examining its predictive power for spectroscopic properties compared to the experimental data. Using a large model space and effective interaction tailored for this mass region, we compute the NME and half-life for the
2\nu
ECEC process in
132

ECEC process in

Formalism

The half-life for
2\nu
ECEC process can be expressed as

(T_{1/2}^{2\nu})^{-1}=G_{2\nu}^{\rm ECEC}(g_{\rm A}^{\rm eff})^{4}|M_{2\nu}|^{2}.

(1)

Here,
G_{2\nu}^{\rm ECEC}
denotes the phase-space factor
[
43
,
44
]
, while
g_{\rm A}^{\rm eff}
represents the effective axial-vector coupling constant
[
45
]
. The NME for the 2
\nu
ECEC process is defined as follows
[
46
,
47
]

M_{2\nu}=m_{e}\sum_{n}\frac{\langle 0_{\rm g.s.}^{(f)}||\sigma\tau^{+}||1_{n}^{+}\rangle\langle 1_{n}^{+}||\sigma\tau^{+}||0_{\rm g.s.}^{(i)}\rangle}{E(1^{+}_{n})-(E_{i}+E_{f})/2}.

(2)

In Eq. (
2
),
E(1^{+}_{n})
denotes the energy of
n^{\rm th}

1^{+}
state in the intermediate nucleus.
E_{i}
and
E_{f}
stand for the ground state (g.s.) energies of the parent and granddaughter nuclei, respectively. The symbol
m_{e}
represents the rest mass of the electron. The notations
0_{\rm g.s.}^{(i)}
and
0_{\rm g.s.}^{(f)}
correspond to the ground states of the parent and granddaughter nuclei, respectively. The Pauli spin matrix is denoted by
\sigma
, while
\tau^{+}
stands for the isospin-raising operator. The reduced Gamow-Teller (GT) transition matrix elements are written as
\langle 0_{\rm g.s.}^{(f)}||\sigma\tau^{+}||1_{n}^{+}\rangle
and
\langle 1_{n}^{+}||\sigma\tau^{+}||0_{\rm g.s.}^{(i)}\rangle
. It is important to emphasize that, within the shell-model framework, the
g_{\rm A}^{\rm eff}
appearing in Eq. (
1
) originates from the renormalization of the spin-isospin operator
\sigma\tau^{+}
involved in the NME of Eq. (
2
). By factoring out
g_{\rm A}^{\rm eff}
from the squared NME in Eq. (
1
), the resulting expression for
M_{2\nu}
in Eq. (
2
) becomes independent of the effective axial-vector coupling constant.

An alternative formulation of the half-life for the
2\nu
ECEC process, incorporating the Fermi transitions, can be expressed as follows
[
46
]

\displaystyle(T_{1/2}^{2\nu})^{-1}

\displaystyle=G_{2\nu}^{\rm ECEC}(g_{\rm A}^{\rm eff})^{4}\left|M^{\rm GT}_{2\nu}-\left(\frac{g_{\rm V}}{g_{\rm A}^{\rm eff}}\right)^{2}M_{2\nu}^{\rm F}\right|^{2}

(3)

\displaystyle=G_{2\nu}^{\rm ECEC}(g_{\rm A}^{\rm eff})^{4}\left|M_{2\nu}^{\prime}\right|.

(4)

Here, the final NME includes the contribution from both the GT and Fermi transitions.
M^{\rm GT}_{2\nu}
is given by the same expression as Eq. (
2
);
g_{\rm V}
denotes the vector coupling constant. The Fermi matrix element for the
2\nu
ECEC process can be given by
[
46
,
47
]

M_{2\nu}^{\rm F}=m_{e}\sum_{n}\frac{\langle 0_{\rm g.s.}^{(f)}||\tau^{+}||0_{n}^{+}\rangle\langle 0_{n}^{+}||\tau^{+}||0_{\rm g.s.}^{(i)}\rangle}{E(0^{+}_{n})-(E_{i}+E_{f})/2},

(5)

where,
E(0^{+}_{n})
represents the energy of
n^{\rm th}

0^{+}
state in the intermediate nucleus. The reduced Fermi transition matrix elements are expressed as
\langle 0_{\rm g.s.}^{(f)}||\tau^{+}||0_{n}^{+}\rangle
and
\langle 0_{n}^{+}||\tau^{+}||0_{\rm g.s.}^{(i)}\rangle
. The nuclear energy and decay schemes for the
2\nu
ECEC processes in
78
Kr and
132
Ba are shown in Fig.
1
.

In our study of the
2\nu
ECEC process in
132
Ba, we employed the SN100PN effective interaction
[
33
]
, which includes the
0g_{7/2}1d2s0h_{11/2}
orbitals for both protons and neutrons within the 50–82 model space, taking
100
Sn as an inert core. This interaction is based on a
G
-matrix derived from the CD-Bonn nucleon-nucleon potential
[
48
]
. Due to the large dimensions of the full model space, we introduced truncations to ensure computational feasibility. Specifically, proton and neutron configurations are restricted to
\pi[(g_{7/2}d_{5/2})^{0-14}(d_{3/2}s_{1/2}h_{11/2})^{0-2}]
and
\nu[(g_{7/2})^{8-8}(d_{5/2}d_{3/2}s_{1/2}h_{11/2})^{0-24}]
, respectively. We have included the
1^{+}
states of the intermediate nucleus up to the excitation energies at which the cumulative NMEs reach the saturation level.

For the calculation of the NME corresponding to the
2\nu
ECEC in
78
Kr, we employed the same effective interaction (GWBXG)
[
49
,
50
,
51
,
52
]
as used in our previous study on
2\nu
ECEC in
78
Kr
[
42
]
. As discussed in Sec.
1
, those earlier calculations were carried out without incorporating neutron orbitals above the

shell closure. In the present work, we have extended the model space by allowing one particle–one hole (
1p
–

) excitations across
N=50

0g_{7/2}
,
1d_{5/2}
,
1d_{3/2}
, and
2s_{1/2}
neutron orbitals. The one-body transition densities (OBTDs) were computed with the NUSHELLX code
[
53
]
. The KSHELL code
[
54
]
was also utilized to calculate energy spectra and transition probabilities.

,

, the overall sensitivity of the total NME to

remains modest, especially within the range
g_{\rm A}^{\rm eff}=0.94
–

. It is important to note that the Fermi matrix elements in the
2\nu

[

,
65
]
. In our case, the model space includes the same
0g_{7/2}1d2s0h_{11/2}
orbitals for both protons and neutrons. Consequently, the Fermi contribution, although smaller than the GT part, can be non-negligible and influence the total NME.

Over the past few decades, experimental investigations into the half-life measurement of the
2\nu
ECEC process in
132
Ba have been very limited. Therefore, future measurements with greater precision may refine the current estimate (
>2.2\times 10^{21}
yr) and establish an improved, possibly higher lower bound for the half-life. However, theoretical calculations in a more enlarged model space, though very challenging at present, may further improve the NME and half-life predictions in the future, once substantially more advanced computational resources become available.

4
Summary and Conclusions

In this work, we have performed large-scale shell-model calculations to investigate the
2\nu
ECEC process in
132
Ba and
78
Kr using the SN100PN and GWBXG effective interactions, respectively. The model space employed with the SN100PN interaction consists of the
0g_{7/2}1d2s0h_{11/2}
proton and neutron orbitals. While, for the GWBXG interaction, the model space includes the
0f_{5/2}1p0g_{9/2}
proton orbitals and the
1p_{1/2}0g1d2s
neutron orbitals. To ensure the reliability of the calculated NMEs, the spectroscopic properties of the parent, intermediate, and granddaughter nuclei of the decay were first examined, demonstrating good agreement with the experimental energy levels and
B(E2)
transition strengths. The NMEs and corresponding half-lives were then computed using two different formulations, one incorporating only GT transitions and the other including both GT and Fermi contributions. Furthermore, the evolution of the cumulative NMEs for GT and Fermi transitions with respect to the energies of the intermediate
1^{+}
and
0^{+}
states, respectively, was analyzed to examine their convergence behavior. We have also investigated the variation of the total NME (GT+Fermi) with respect to the
g_{\rm A}^{\rm eff}
value, and found that the Fermi matrix elements contribute less significantly than the GT part within the considered
g_{\rm A}^{\rm eff}
range in the present study. The resulting NME for
132
Ba serves as a theoretical reference to assist future experimental efforts in setting improved half-life limits, and its accuracy is also assessed by shifting the shell-model energy of the unconfirmed
1^{+}_{1}
state in
132
Cs. For
78
Kr, the predicted NME represents a significant improvement over previous calculations, offering enhanced precision in the theoretical description of this rare decay mode.

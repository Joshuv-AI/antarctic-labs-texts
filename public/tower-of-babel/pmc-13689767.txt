Recurrent Nonischemic Left Ventricular Aneurysms
Authors: Nathalia Prata Garcia, Diego Forti, Leticia Araujo Tassine Penatti, Henrique Turin Moreira, Benedito Carlos Maciel, André Schmidt, Gustavo Jardim Volpe
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundCardiac magnetic resonance (CMR) is the gold-standard technique for characterizing nonischemic structural cardiomyopathies, offering tissue characterization and precise localization of myocardial injury.Case SummaryA 24-year-old man presented with recurrent chest discomfort and tachyarrhythmias. Initial CMR revealed an apical left ventricular aneurysm with imaging features consistent with a true aneurysm and normal coronary arteries, successfully treated surgically. Five years later, recurrent ventricular tachycardia prompted re-evaluation; CMR identified a new inferolateral basal aneurysm in a region previously normal on postoperative imaging. Coronary computed tomography angiography again showed normal epicardial coronary arteries. Surgical patch repair and implantable cardioverter-defibrillator placement were performed. Genetic testing revealed a pathogenic desmin (DES) c.785A>T (p.Glu262Val) variant and additional variant of unknown significance in VCL and CDH2.DiscussionThe identification of a pathogenic DES variant suggests a potential underlying susceptibility to structural remodeling, although causality cannot be confirmed.Take-Home MessagesCMR was crucial in identifying sequential, anatomically distinct left ventricular aneurysms. The combination of recurrent nonischemic aneurysms and a pathogenic DES variant suggests a possible cytoskeletal genetic cardiomyopathy with arrhythmic risk.Key words: cardiac magnetic resonance, chest pain, computed tomography, genetic disorders, ventricular tachycardia

History of Presentation

A 31-year-old man presented with recurrent episodes of rapid palpitations and presyncope. He described intermittent, nonexertional, oppressive chest discomfort radiating to the neck. Physical examination revealed a sustained, laterally displaced apical impulse with no murmurs. He was hemodynamically stable throughout. The electrocardiogram demonstrated sustained monomorphic ventricular tachycardia (VT), which was successfully terminated with intravenous amiodarone. He was then referred to a tertiary hospital for implantable cardioverter-defibrillator (ICD) placement and further investigation.

Past Medical History

At age 23, the patient had experienced episodes of chest discomfort and tachyarrhythmias, requiring amiodarone-mediated cardioversion. Evaluation at an outside institution included cardiac magnetic resonance (CMR), which identified a large apical aneurysm (Video 1), and invasive coronary angiography, which demonstrated normal epicardial coronary arteries. He subsequently underwent apical aneurysmectomy at that institution in 2015, with excellent recovery and normalization of apical morphology on postoperative CMR (Figure 1, Video 2). The original angiographic cine recordings and operative report from that procedure were unavailable for review. He remained asymptomatic for nearly 5 years.

Genetic testing

A targeted cardiomyopathy panel detected a heterozygous DES c.785A>T (p.Glu262Val) pathogenic variant. The DES gene encodes desmin, an intermediate filament protein essential for sarcomeric alignment and structural integrity. Pathogenic variants in DES can lead to myofibrillar disarray and progressive fibrosis.

Exome sequencing

Exome sequencing revealed 2 variants of unknown significance (VUSs):

•VCL c.1226G>A; p.Arg409Gln∗ (cytoskeletal mechanotransduction)

VCL c.1226G>A; p.Arg409Gln∗ (cytoskeletal mechanotransduction)

•CDH2 c.1776G>T; p.Gln592His∗ (N-cadherin; cell–cell adhesion)

Mitochondrial DNA analysis for m.3243A>G was negative.

Other testing

Routine laboratory evaluation, including Chagas serology, was normal. No systemic inflammatory markers were elevated.

Management

Given recurrent sustained VT and a new large aneurysm (Figures 3 and 4), the heart team recommended surgical intervention. On January 24, 2023, the patient underwent endoaneurysmorrhaphy with patch repair of the inferolateral aneurysm. Postoperative recovery was uneventful. The operative note described closure of the aneurysm neck with a bovine pericardial patch, followed by closure of the aneurysmal sac over the patch. As no aneurysmal tissue was resected during the procedure. Histopathological confirmation of the lesion was not possible.

Outcome and Follow-Up

At follow-up, until May 2025, the patient was asymptomatic in NYHA functional class II status. No ICD shocks or sustained ventricular tachyarrhythmias were recorded. Left ventricular function remained moderately reduced, with stable aneurysm repair and no new wall abnormalities.

Genotype-phenotype correlation

Desminopathies typically manifest as cardiomyopathy, skeletal myopathy, conduction disease, or arrhythmias. The p.Glu262Val variant disrupts filament assembly, weakening the myocardium's mechanical resilience under stress.7,8 This mechanism may explain the observed regional weakness in mechanically stressed areas (apex and inferolateral wall), progressive fibrosis with transmural LGE, focal aneurysm formation, and ventricular arrhythmias. The 2 VUSs (VCL and CDH2) involve proteins at the intercalated disc and may act as modifiers, lowering the threshold for regional failure in a desmin-fragile myocardium.8,9

Although DES variants are well described in myofibrillar myopathy and arrhythmogenic phenotypes,7,8 the specific role of the p.Glu262Val variant in focal ventricular aneurysm formation remains uncertain. In this case, the recurrence of nonischemic aneurysms in distinct myocardial regions suggests a possible underlying cytoskeletal predisposition, though a direct causal relationship cannot be established. The 2 additional VUSs (VCL and CDH2) further complicate interpretation and may represent benign variants, genetic modifiers, or unrelated findings. Thus, the genetic testing results provide supportive evidence but are not definitive, and longitudinal family-based evaluation is warranted for clarification.

Why this case matters

The sequential development of 2 nonischemic aneurysms in distinct regions, both precisely characterized by CMR, provides a unique illustration of region-dependent myocardial vulnerability in a patient carrying a pathogenic DES variant. This case highlights the critical role of CMR in diagnostic reasoning, the importance of integrating genetic testing in cases of unexplained structural heart disease, and the necessity of ICD therapy in the presence of structural scar and sustained VT. Conclusions

CMR played a central role in diagnosing sequential nonischemic aneurysms in this young patient. The combination of a compelling family history of early cardiac deaths and a pathogenic DES variant suggests that a genetic substrate may contribute to myocardial fragility, although causality cannot be firmly established. This case underscores the importance of multimodality imaging and genetic assessment in unexplained structural cardiomyopathy.

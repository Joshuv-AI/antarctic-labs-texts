Rigidity Results for Spacelike Self-Shrinkers via Different Maximum Principles Thanks: 1 Corresponding author.
Weiller F.Chaves Barboza 1

Abstract
. In this work, we establish several rigidity results for spacelike self-shrinkers immersed in the pseudo-Euclidean space ℝ p n + p \mathbb{R}^{n+p}_{p} . Under suitable boundedness conditions on either the mean curvature vector or the second fundamental form, we apply different versions of Omori–Yau type maximum principles due to Qiu [ 20 ] , Chen and Qiu [ 10 ] , and Alías, Caminha, and Nascimento [ 3 ] to show that such self-shrinkers must be spacelike hyperplanes. These results contribute to the broader classification of spacelike self-shrinkers under natural geometric assumptions.

1.
Introduction

Let
X:M^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{p}^{n+p}
be a spacelike submanifold (which means that it has a Riemannian induced metric) in the
(n+p)
-dimensional pseudo-Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n+p}_{p}

p

with corresponding images

satisfying the following evolution equation

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\dfrac{\partial X}{\partial t}=\vec{H}\\
X(0,x)=X(x)\end{array}\right.

on some time interval, where
\vec{H}
stands for the (non-normalized) mean curvature vector of the spacelike submanifold
M^{n}_{t}
in
\mathbb{R}_{p}^{n+p}
. Self-similar shrinkers to the above SMCF play an important role in understanding the behavior of the flow since they often occur as singularities.
M^{n}
is called a
spacelike self-shrinker
of
\mathbb{R}^{n+p}_{p}
if it satisfies a system of quasilinear elliptic PDE of the second order

(1.1)

\vec{H}=-\frac{1}{2}X^{\perp},

where
X^{\perp}
denotes the normal part of
X

[
5
]
and
[
12
]

[

[

[

]

[

[

[

2.
Spacelike submanifolds in
\mathbb{R}^{n+p}_{p}

We recall that the
(n+p)
-dimensional pseudo-Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n+p}_{p}
of index
p
is just the linear space
\mathbb{R}^{n+p}
endowed with the indefinite flat metric

ds^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(dx_{i})^{2}-\sum_{\alpha=n+1}^{n+p}(dx_{\alpha})^{2}.

Now, let
X:M^{n}\looparrowright\mathbb{R}^{n+p}_{p}
be an
n
-dimensional (connected) spacelike submanifold. Using the following convection for indices

\displaystyle 1\leq A,B,C,\ldots\leq n+p,\quad 1\leq i,j,k,\ldots\leq n\quad\mbox{and}

n+1\leq\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\ldots\leq n+p,

we choose a local Lorentzian frame field
\{e_{i},e_{\alpha}\}
in
\mathbb{R}_{p}^{n+p}
along the spacelike submanifold
X:M^{n}\looparrowright\mathbb{R}^{n+p}_{p}
with spacelike frame
\{e_{i}\}\subset TM
and timelike frame
\{e_{\alpha}\}\subset TM^{\perp}
. Let us also denote by
\{\omega_{B}\}
the corresponding dual coframe.

The second fundamental form
A
, the curvature tensor
R
and the normal curvature tensor
R^{\perp}
of
M^{n}
are given by

\displaystyle\omega_{i\alpha}=\sum_{j}h_{ij}^{\alpha}\omega_{j},\quad A=\sum_{i,j,\alpha}h_{ij}^{\alpha}\omega_{i}\otimes\omega_{j}\otimes e_{\alpha},

\displaystyle d\omega_{ij}=-\sum_{k}\omega_{ik}\wedge\omega_{kj}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k,l}R_{ijkl}\omega_{k}\wedge\omega_{l},

\displaystyle d\omega_{\alpha\beta}=-\sum_{\gamma}\omega_{\alpha\gamma}\wedge\omega_{\gamma\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k,l}R_{\alpha\beta kl}^{\perp}\omega_{k}\wedge\omega_{l},

where
\{\omega_{BC}\}
denote the connection
1
-forms on
\mathbb{R}_{p}^{n+p}
. Moreover, the Gauss equation is given by

(2.1)

\displaystyle R_{ijkl}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{\alpha}(h_{ik}^{\alpha}h_{jl}^{\alpha}-h_{il}^{\alpha}h_{jk}^{\alpha})

and the Ricci equation is

(2.2)

R^{\perp}_{\alpha\beta ij}=\sum_{m}(h^{\alpha}_{mi}h^{\beta}_{mj}-h^{\alpha}_{mj}h^{\beta}_{mi}).

Denoting by
H^{\alpha}
the components of the standard mean curvature vector
\vec{H}
, that
is,

\vec{H}=\sum_{\alpha}H^{\alpha}{e}_{\alpha}=\sum_{\alpha}\left(\sum_{k}h^{\alpha}_{kk}\right){e}_{\alpha},

it is not difficult to verify from (
2.1
) that the components of the Ricci tensor
R_{ik}

satisfy

(2.3)

R_{ik}=-\sum_{\alpha}H^{\alpha}h^{\alpha}_{ik}+\sum_{\alpha,j}h^{\alpha}_{ij}h^{\alpha}_{jk}.

The covariant derivative of
h^{\alpha}_{ij}
is given by

(1.4)

\sum_{k}h^{\alpha}_{ijk}\omega_{k}=dh^{\alpha}_{ij}+\sum_{k}h^{\alpha}_{kj}\omega_{ki}+\sum_{k}h^{\alpha}_{ik}\omega_{kj}-\sum_{\beta}h^{\beta}_{ij}\omega_{\beta\alpha}.

Hence, it holds the Codazzi equation

(2.4)

h^{\alpha}_{ijk}=h^{\alpha}_{ikj}.

From (
1.4
) we obtain the following Ricci formula

(2.5)

h^{\alpha}_{ijkl}-h^{\alpha}_{ijlk}=\sum_{m}h^{\alpha}_{mj}R_{mikl}+\sum_{m}h^{\alpha}_{im}R_{mjkl}+\sum_{\beta}h^{\beta}_{ij}R^{\perp}_{\alpha\beta kl}.

Since the Laplacian of
h^{\alpha}_{ij}
is defined by
\sum_{k}h^{\alpha}_{ijkk}
, from (
2.1
), (
2.2
) and (
2.5
) we conclude

\Delta h^{\alpha}_{ij}=\sum_{k}h^{\alpha}_{kkij}-\sum_{k,m,\beta}h^{\alpha}_{mi}h^{\beta}_{mj}h^{\beta}_{kk}-2\sum_{k,m,\beta}h^{\alpha}_{km}h^{\beta}_{mj}h^{\beta}_{ik}

(1.6)

+\sum_{k,m,\beta}h^{\alpha}_{km}h^{\beta}_{mk}h^{\beta}_{ij}+\sum_{k,m,\beta}h^{\alpha}_{mj}h^{\beta}_{ki}h^{\beta}_{mk}.

Main Rigidity Results and Maximum Principles

Let
(M^{n},\langle\,,\rangle)
be a Riemannian manifold. According to

\operatorname{Ric}_{V}:=\operatorname{Ric}-\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{L}_{V}g,

where

(3.2)

{\Delta}_{V}u={\rm div}_{V}(\nabla u)=\Delta u-\langle V,\nabla u\rangle,

where
\Delta

(3.3)

{\rm div}_{V}(W)={\rm div}(W)-\langle V,W\rangle,

for all tangent vector field

M^{n}
.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
(M^{n},\langle\,,\rangle)
be a complete noncompact Riemannian manifold and let
W\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
be a smooth vector field on
M^{n}
. Assume that there exists a nonnegative, non-identically vanishing function
u\in C^{\infty}(M)
which converges to zero at infinity and such that
\langle\nabla u,W\rangle\geq 0
. If
{\rm div}W\geq 0
on
M^{n}
, then
\langle\nabla u,W\rangle\equiv 0
on
M^{n}
.

As an application of Lemma
3.5
, we obtain our final rigidity result.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
X:M^{n}\looparrowright\mathbb{R}^{n+p}_{p}
be a spacelike self-shrinker. If the second fundamental form
A
of
M^{n}
is such that
|A|
converges to zero at infinity, then
M^{n}
is an
n
-dimensional spacelike hyperplane of
\mathbb{R}^{n+p}_{p}
.

Proof..

Let us suppose by contradiction that
M^{n}
is not a spacelike hyperplane of
\mathbb{R}^{n+p}_{p}
or, equivalently, that
|A|
does not vanish identically on
M^{n}
. By an argument similar to the preceding theorem, the sctrict positivity of
P(A)
in (
3.9
), we get that
\Delta_{V}|A|^{2}\geq 0
. Thus, taking the smooth vector field
W=\nabla|A|^{2}
, from (
3.3
), (
3.2
) and (
3.9
) follows that

(3.19)

0\leq\Delta_{V}|A|^{2}={\rm div}_{V}(\nabla|A|^{2})={\rm div}(\nabla|A|^{2})-\langle V,\nabla|A|^{2}\rangle,

for
V=\nabla|A|^{2}
, we conclude that

(3.20)

{\rm div}W=\Delta_{V}|A|^{2}+|\nabla|A|^{2}|^{2}\geq 0.

Moreover, choosing the smooth function
u=|A|^{2}
we also have that

(3.21)

\langle\nabla u,W\rangle=|\nabla|A|^{2}|^{2}\geq 0.

Consequently, since we are also supposing that
|A|
converges to zero at infinity, we can apply Lemma
3.5
to get that

\langle\nabla u,W\rangle\equiv 0
on
M^{n}
. So, returning to (
3.21
) we conclude that
|A|
must be constant on
M^{n}
. Therefore, from (
3.18
) we have that
|A|
is identically zero on
M^{n}
and, hence, we reach at a contradiction. Therefore,
M^{n}
must indeed be a spacelike hyperplane of
\mathbb{R}^{n+p}_{p}
.
∎

Locality and Conserved Charges in T ​ T ¯ T\overline{T} -Deformed CFTs
Ruben Monten, Richard M. Myers, Konstantinos Roumpedakis

Abstract
We investigate the locality properties of T ​ T ¯ T\overline{T} -deformed CFTs within perturbation theory. Up to third order in the deformation parameter, we find a Hamiltonian operator which solves the flow equation, reproduces the Zamolodchikov energy spectrum, and is consistent with quasi-locality of the theory. This Hamiltonian includes terms proportional to the central charge which have not appeared before and which are necessary to reproduce the correct spectrum. We show that the Hamiltonian is not uniquely defined since it contains free parameters, starting at second order, which do not spoil the above properties. We then use it to determine the full conserved stress tensor. In our approach, the KdV charges are automatically conserved to all orders but are not a priori local. Nevertheless, we show that they can be made local to first order. Our techniques allow us to further comment on the space of Hamiltonians constructed from products of KdV charges which also flow to local charges in the deformed theory in the IR.

2.2
Perturbative Energy Spectrum

As a cross-check of our results, we can directly compute the perturbative energy spectrum of

. Since we started from the fake Hamiltonian and applied a unitary transformation, the expected

In general, this would be a complicated problem in degenerate perturbation theory. But if we restrict to the special case of a primary state

(46)

\displaystyle\langle N,\overline{N}|H_{1}|h,\overline{h}\rangle=-4\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}w_{n}\langle N,\overline{N}|(L_{n}\overline{L}_{n}|h,\overline{h}\rangle)=0~,

and

is a normalization factor

24

24

It is straightforward to show

Appendix A
Regulation by smearing

Although Zamolodchikov’s argument guarantees that certain observables, like the energy spectrum and S-matrix elements, will turn out finite, we need regulate several of the intermediate expressions to keep control over the calculation.
This also grants us access to more fine-grained observables, which are not covered by the original arguments.

To illustrate the ambiguities that appear otherwise, we can consider sums like the ones that appear in the square of the stress tensor,
\sum_{m,n}e^{i(m+n)\sigma}L_{m}L_{n}

As was already pointed out in
[
42

One physically intuitive way to regulate the theory is by smearing each stress tensor operator with a window function

where

are the Fourier coefficients of

At the end of the calculation we take away the regulator by sending

or, equivalently,
w_{n}\to 1
.
In order to keep

w_{-n}=w_{n}^{*}
.
Each term in the result (

\displaystyle\sum_{m,n}(n-m)w_{m}w_{n}L_{m+n}e^{i(m+n)\sigma}~,\quad\sum_{m}\frac{c}{12}m^{3}w_{m}w_{-m}\ ,

(89)

respectively.
The latter is finite as long as we require

to go to zero faster than

32
32

32

In what follows, we will require it to fall off faster than any power of

and the former is a finite sum of local operators.
If we take a symmetric smearing function, with

, these vanish identically as they are antisymmetric under

We conclude that the smeared product

An alternative approach is to define a smeared product, which we can use to regulate the deforming operator

At first order it becomes

\displaystyle T(\sigma)\times_{w}\overline{T}(\sigma)\equiv\int dy\,w(y)T(\sigma+y)\overline{T}(\sigma-y)=\sum_{m,n}w_{m+n}L_{m}\bar{L}_{n}e^{i(m-n)\sigma}\ ,

When defining composite operators, there is a certain amount of freedom in how we regulate. The smearing definitions given above have natural implications for locality, so they make the most sense for us to consider in the present work, but they are not unique. Indeed, we could define

\displaystyle T_{w}(\sigma)^{2}\times_{w}T(\sigma).

(92)

With this ambiguity in mind, in this work we use regulators that are motivated by the smearings described above, but will not attempt to regulate all operators in exactly one of these two smearings. Rather, we will prefer to regulate each operator that appears in our Hamiltonian in a manner that happens to be most convenient for our purposes.

Exploring the potential effects of acute respiratory failure on patients with early-stage bronchopulmonary cancer
Authors: Ziwen Lv, Qi Feng, Junhui Gu, Zhuo Gao, Dong Liu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractAcute respiratory failure is a common disease, impacting various cancer survivors and reflecting post-surgery complications. However, little evidence based on respiratory failure has been developed for prognostic assessment and prediction in patients with bronchopulmonary cancer. Data from the Medical Information Mart for Intensive Care-IV (MIMIC-IV v2.2) database were analyzed to identify risk factors associated with acute respiratory failure in patients with bronchopulmonary cancer. The primary outcome was the occurrence of acute respiratory failure. Feature selection was performed using the least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression model, and significant predictors were incorporated into a nomogram for individualized risk prediction. Among the 1,318 included patients with bronchopulmonary cancer, 35.6% developed acute respiratory failure. Ten independent risk factors were identified and used to construct the nomogram: activated partial thromboplastin time (PTT), glucose, blood urea nitrogen (BUN), neutrophil percentage, sodium, respiratory rate (RR), heart rate (HR), chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), shock, and race. The model exhibited good discrimination, with an area under the ROC curve of 0.741. Internal validation using 1000 bootstrap replicates demonstrated strong agreement between the calibration curve and the ideal reference, indicating satisfactory model performance. We developed and validated nomograms for patients with early-stage bronchopulmonary cancer, which could provide individual prediction of acute respiratory failure.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-24267-2.Keywords: Bronchopulmonary cancer, Respiratory failure, MIMIC-IV database, PrognosisSubject terms: Diseases, Medical research, Risk factors

Introduction

Bronchopulmonary cancer represents a significant global health burden, accounting for substantial annual morbidity and mortality1. Sepsis (44%) and acute respiratory failure (31%) have been identified as the primary reasons for ICU admission among these patients2. Acute respiratory failure (RF), in particular, is a critical complication associated with high mortality in bronchopulmonary cancer3. Its pathogenesis often involves airway obstruction due to tumor progression, resulting in impaired ventilation. Additionally, pulmonary infections—common in these patients—can exacerbate ventilatory dysfunction and precipitate RF4. Since respiratory failure symptoms may be obscured by those of the underlying malignancy, early recognition and intervention are essential.

Nomograms have emerged as valuable predictive tools in oncology, enabling individualized risk estimation for various clinical outcomes5–8. Evidence suggests that such models can outperform clinical judgment alone9,10. This study aims to develop and validate a nomogram for predicting the risk of respiratory failure in patients with bronchopulmonary cancer. The model may facilitate early identification of high-risk individuals, allowing timely intervention, personalized treatment selection, and ultimately, improved clinical outcomes.

Materials and methods

Study population

This is a retrospective observational cohort study with longitudinal follow-upof patients. Medical Information Mart for Intensive Care-IV (MIMIC-IV-2.2) is a freely accessible data. The IRB has approved the waiver of informed consent and the data-sharing initiative. All private information in the database repository has been removed, rendering informed consent unnecessary for data collection. We have completed the Collaborative Institutional Training Initiative (CITI) course and passed the exams for “Conflict of Interest” and “Data or Specimen Only Research” (ID: 55,109,354). All methods were performed in accordance with the relevant guidelines and regulations. One author (Ziwen Lv) has become a credentialed used on PhysioNet and that they have signed the data use agreement11,12.

Patients diagnosed with early bronchopulmonary cancer and admitted to the ICU were included according to the International Classification of Diseases (supplementary material S1).Early-stage bronchopulmonary carcinoma is defined according to the 8th edition TNM staging criteria of the American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) as patients diagnosed with carcinoma in situ (Tis), stage I, or stage II disease.Exclusion criteria were as follows:(1) age < 18 years; (2) not first admission (3) missing data on patient variables > 30%(Fig. 1).

Data extraction

In this study, Navicat Premium software (version 16) was used to extract demographic information, laboratory indicators, and comorbidities from the MIMIC-IV database by executing Structured Query Language (SQL). Demographic information included gender, age, race. Vital signs, respiratory rate, heart rate, systolic blood pressure, diastolic blood pressure at first admission. Laboratory indicators include serum albumin, red blood cells (RBC), white blood cells (WBC), red blood cell distribution width (RDW), hematocrit, hemoglobin, lactate, international normalized ratio (INR), serum potassium, serum sodium, serum total calcium, serum chloride, platelets, blood glucose, blood creatinine, Neutrophil percentage, lymphocyte percentage, prothrombin time (PT), activated partial thromboplastin time (PTT), anion gap (AG), blood urea nitrogen (BUN), Peripheral Capillary Oxygen Saturation, pulse oxygen saturation, PH. Patients’ comorbidities and personal history were determined according to ICD-9 and ICD-10, including chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, and shock (supplementary material S1). For laboratory indicators measured multiple times after admission, the first measurement was used in this study. To minimize reverse causation bias, data collected after the outcome event were considered invalid. Missing values for laboratory indicators are common in the MIMIC-IV database. Because missing data may weaken the robustness and validity of the study’s conclusions, multiple interpolation was used to deal with these missing data13. For variables with more than 30% missing data, exclude them; for those with missing data not exceeding 30%, apply multiple imputation to improve the data 14.The interpolation model was set to perform 5 iterations, generating 5 complete datasets. Both the outcome variable and all analytical variables were incorporated into the interpolation process to maintain unbiased results. The final model parameters represent the combined results of these 5 datasets.

Primary endpoint

The primary outcome of this study was acute respiratory failure in patients with early-stage bronchopulmonary cancer.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using R version 4.2.3 and SPSS version 26.0 software. A normality test was performed at the preliminary stage of data analysis. The normality of continuous variables was assessed using the Kolmogorov–Smirnov test. For normally distributed continuous variables, mean + /- SD was reported; for non-normally distributed continuous variables, median (IQR); and for categorical variables, count (%). In analyzing baseline characteristics, the Kruskal–Wallis rank sum test was used for continuous variables and the Fisher exact probability test was used for count variables with theoretical counts less than 10. Based on clinical knowledge and literature reports, we initially selected a set of predictor variables potentially associated with outcomes. Subsequently, the aforementioned LASSO regression was employed to screen out predictors with non-zero coefficients from these variables. LASSO regression was primarily chosen due to its suitability for variable selection in high-dimensional data and its ability to effectively address multicollinearity among predictors through the L1 regularization penalty function. λ Value Selection: The optimal λ (lambda) value was selected via tenfold cross-validation. Specifically, the λ value corresponding to the 1-standard-error rule—which minimizes the cross-validation error—was chosen. This λ value ensures model parsimony while achieving optimal predictive performance. Finally, all variables selected by LASSO were incorporated into a multivariate logistic regression model to simultaneously assess the independent influence of each variable. The performance of the Nomograms was evaluated using the AUC of ROC curves., and the accuracy of the model was assessed by resampling 1000 times and plotting calibration curves. We used Bootstrap self-help sampling method for model overfitting correction, raw calibration curve: based on the initial model prediction of the raw data, bias correction curve: average of 1000 Bootstrap biases by translating the raw curve downwards. Finally, decision curve analysis (DCA) was used to evaluate the validity and clinical impact of the model. Differences were statistically significant when P values < 0.05 were defined.

Construction of the prediction model

Based on the results of LASSO regression, a clinical prediction model for acute respiratory failure in bronchopulmonary cancer patients was constructed (Fig. 4). Nomograms are a visual representation of complex regression equations that score each patient’s identified risk factors or clinical characteristics. The level of influence of the risk factors in the model on the likelihood of an acute respiratory failure outcome was determined by assigning a score to the level of each factor on a scale of 0 to 100 points on Nomograms, and the scores for each factor were summed to obtain a Total Points score. The red dots mark the specific score locations for the example patients in this section. The total score is mapped to the ARF probability via a logistic transformation formula. The predicted probability of an individual ending event was calculated based on the functional relationship that exists between the total score and the probability of occurrence of the ending event. The results of one case showed that the sum of scores was 260, and the risk of acute respiratory failure during the patient’s stay in the ICU was approximately 86.3%. The 86.3% indicates the predicted probability value for this patient.

Discussion

The occurrence of acute respiratory failure in patients with bronchopulmonary cancer is multifactorial, underscoring the importance of identifying independent risk factors. We developed a nomogram to predict acute respiratory failure in ICU-admitted bronchopulmonary cancer patients based on the following predictors: PTT, glucose, BUN, neutrophil percentage, sodium, respiratory rate, heart rate, COPD, shock, and race.

Neutrophil-mediated lung injury is thought to occur through several mechanisms. Elevated pro-inflammatory mediators promote neutrophil recruitment and sequestration in pulmonary capillaries, where they contribute to tissue damage via protease release, reactive oxygen species generation, and neutrophil extracellular trap (NET) formation15. Additionally, neutrophils are implicated in pulmonary microthrombosis, leading to vascular occlusion, impaired gas exchange, and hypoxic alveolar injury, further exacerbating lung damage through interactions with other immune cells16,17. Given that respiratory failure is a leading cause of mortality in bronchopulmonary cancer, our finding that neutrophil percentage is positively correlated with respiratory failure incidence highlights its role as a significant risk factor.

Urea nitrogen (BUN) reflects alterations in microcirculation, renal dysfunction, and increased protein catabolism18,19. Malnutrition—common in bronchopulmonary cancer due to inadequate intake and high nutrient consumption by tumor cells—is associated with respiratory impairment20, suggesting that elevated BUN may indicate a heightened risk of respiratory failure.

Hyperglycemia has been linked to poorer survival in multiple cancers21–23. A large prospective study (2011) reported consistent associations between fasting glucose > 100 mg/dL and worse outcomes in non-vascular malignancies24, aligning with our results. The underlying mechanisms warrant further investigation.

Although PTT and serum sodium also emerged as high-risk factors in our model, few previous studies have reported their association with respiratory failure in this population. These findings require validation through larger prospective trials.

Race was identified as another important predictor. African Americans and Native Hawaiians experience higher incidence and mortality from bronchopulmonary cancer than Whites25, and Black and Hispanic patients have been shown to have elevated malignancy risk and reduced survival26. Structural determinants—including higher exposure to air pollution, occupational hazards, and unequal access to healthcare—likely contribute to these disparities27,28. Consistent with these reports, our model indicates that African American and other non-White patients are at increased risk of respiratory failure compared to White patients.

COPD contributes to respiratory failure through impaired diffusion capacity, inspiratory muscle fatigue, and alveolar hypoventilation29. Shock predisposes patients to respiratory failure by causing hypoxemia secondary to inadequate cardiac output and tissue perfusion30. Additionally, respiratory rate—a well-established discriminator of disease severity31,32—showed a stronger association with respiratory failure than heart rate in some studies33. Our results confirm that COPD, shock, elevated respiratory rate, and elevated heart rate are significant predictors of respiratory failure in bronchopulmonary cancer patients.

However, the study has several limitations. First, because the majority of the population in the MIMIC database is Caucasian, there may be racial bias in the study population. Therefore, the applicability of our Nomograms to regions other than the United States remains to be investigated. Second, our data come from foreign databases, and the logistic regression prediction model is only internally validated, which may have geographical differences. Third, due to missing variables in the database, not all indicators related to the impact on respiratory function in bronchopulmonary cancer patients were included. The final population we selected for the study was patients between 2008 and 2019. The development of medical treatments and the optimization of treatment protocols during this period did not guarantee consistency in the approach to patient care, which may have affected the results of the study. Therefore, further prospective studies are needed to ensure the performance of our Nomograms.

Conclusion

Based on the information from the MIMIC database, we developed a nomogram to predict acute respiratory failure in bronchopulmonary cancer patients who were admitted to the ICU. Our validation analysis shows that the nomogram can provide clinicians with more information about the risk aspects of each prognostic factor.

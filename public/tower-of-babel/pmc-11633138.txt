Visualizing immunotherapy for multiple myeloma worldwide from 2013 to 2023: A bibliometric analysis
Authors: Jinqiao Li, Xiaoxia Li, Yueyue Fu, Hongbin Meng, Dan Xu, Wenyi Hou
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTMultiple myeloma (MM) is a malignant, clonal proliferative disease of plasma cells that remains incurable. This paper aims to analyze the current research status and emerging trends in immunotherapy for MM through bibliometric methods, thereby providing valuable references and guidance for future research and clinical practice. This study presents a bibliometric analysis of 1,018 English-language publications related to MM immunotherapy, which were published in the Web of Science (WoS) Core Collection database from 2013 to 2023. VOSviewer and CiteSpace were employed to visualize the research hotspots and trends within the field of MM immunotherapy. The United States had the highest number of publications (n = 432, 42.44%), followed by China (n = 177, 17.39%). Harvard University was the institution with the most publications (n = 85), while Anderson KC (n = 27) was the most prolific researcher in the field. The highly cited literature mainly focuses on the treatment regimen based on daratumumab, the application of BCMA CAR-T therapy and the management of relapsed/refractory MM (RRMM), which represent the current research hotspots in this field. Burst detection highlights that bispecific antibodies and preclinical activity as key areas of interest. The cooperation among countries, institutions, and authors in this field should be strengthened. The treatment regimen utilizing daratumumab, the implementation of BCMA CAR-T therapy, and the management of RRMM represent significant research focal points in this field. Additionally, the development and application of bispecific antibodies have emerged as a frontier in recent years.

Introduction

Multiple myeloma (MM) is a heterogeneous and incurable tumor characterized by monoclonal plasma cells accumulating in the bone marrow and producing M protein. The main clinical manifestations include bone destruction, renal injury, anemia and hypercalcemia.1,2 It is the second most common hematological malignancy worldwide. Currently, proteasome inhibitors, immunomodulators, and steroids are the first-line treatments for MM, significantly improving patient prognosis.3,4 However, MM remains incurable, highlighting the urgent need for new treatment strategies.

MM is characterized by progressive immune dysfunction,5,6 leading to a significant focus on immunotherapy in MM research over the past two decades. The first immunotherapy was hematopoietic stem cell transplantation. This was followed by immunomodulators (such as Thalidomide, Lenalidomide) and monoclonal antibodies (such as Daratumumab). More recent advancements in immunotherapy for MM include chimeric antigen receptor T cells (CAR-T), bispecific antibodies, antibody-drug conjugates, and checkpoint inhibitors.7 Patients have achieved deeper responses and longer survival with the use of immunotherapy. Therefore, a systematic analysis and discussion of the literature and research trends in immunotherapy for MM is essential.

Bibliometrics is a method used for analyzing the characteristics and development trends of literature.8 A comprehensive analysis of various indicators, including the number of documents, countries, institutions, authors, citations, and keywords, enables researchers to efficiently process extensive literature. This analysis facilitates the visualization of relationships such as citation patterns, collaborations, and co-occurrences within the literature. Consequently, it aids in understanding the current development status and trends within specific fields, identifying research hotspots, enhancing the efficiency of scientific inquiry, and supporting academic research and discipline development.9,10 As a quantitative analysis method, bibliometrics provides an intuitive and objective assessment of past academic research activities and achievements, thereby mitigating the bias in paper evaluations that may arise from human factors. This method has been extensively applied across various fields, including oncology, organ transplantation, vaccines, and immunotherapy. However, there is a lack of literature on bibliometric analysis of immunotherapy for MM.

In this study, we systematically searched relevant literature on immunotherapy for MM from 2013 to 2023 and applied bibliometric methods to analyze the research status and potential future trends in this area. Through bibliometric analysis, researchers can simultaneously assess the global research landscape of MM immunotherapy. This understanding aids in the formulation of clinical treatment plans and provides insights into emerging trends in MM immunotherapy, thereby establishing a foundation for the development of more innovative treatment strategies.

Materials and methods

Data sources and search strategies

The Web of Science (WoS) database is a widely used resource for bibliographic analysis of scientific literature. In this study, all publications related to immunotherapy for MM f from January 1, 2013, to December 31, 2023, were searched on January 17, 2024, within the WoS core collection database. The search formula was: TS = (“multiple myeloma”) AND TS = (“immunotherapy” OR “immune therapy” OR “immunization therapy” OR “immunotherapies” OR “immunity treatment” OR “immunotherapeutic”). For articles to be included in the study, the title, key words and abstract of the article should be related to the immunotherapy of MM.

Screening process

The study focused on immunotherapy for Multiple Myeloma (MM) with full text available, meeting the criteria of being published in Sciences Citation Index Expanded (SCI-E) and Social Sciences Citation Index (SSCI) of WoS. The publication types considered were limited to “article” and “review,” written in English, and falling within the time period of 1 January 2013 to 31 December 2023. Jinqiao Li and Wenyi Hou, as authors, independently assessed all publications full texts. Any discrepancies were resolved through negotiation between the two authors. The data was exported in plain text format.

Visualization and statistical analysis

Bibliometrics is utilized for analyzing the cutting-edge knowledge and research trends in a specific research field. The results of the analysis were visualized using the VOS viewer (1.6.19) and Citespace (6.2.R6). CiteSpace aids researchers in tracking the advancements and trends within the research domain, enhancing their comprehension of the field.11 VOS viewer offers a straightforward method for analyzing and showcasing extensive literature data.12 Furthermore, Microsoft Excel 365 was employed for data analysis.

The results are depicted in figures or tables, where nodes within the figures represent various elements such as keywords, institutions, countries, authors, and journals. The size of the nodes indicates the frequency of referencing or appearance, while the links between nodes depict relationships like cooperation, co-occurrence, and co-citation. Nodes and links of different colors symbolize different years, and the red dot represents the burst. The centrality index is utilized to assess the significance of each node, nodes with a centrality greater than 0.1 are highlighted by a purple outer ring. In a network diagram, a node with high centrality indicates that it is a connection hub between two sets of nodes, or is highly connected to other nodes. Furthermore, The analysis also included the 2022 Journal Citation Report (JCR) and the Impact Factor (IF).

Discussion

Multiple myeloma (MM) is a malignant, proliferative disease of the plasma cells, with approximately 588,000 people worldwide being diagnosed with the disease each year. It ranks as the second most common hematological malignancy in adults.2,13 Research indicates that over the past 20 years, the prognosis for patients with MM has markedly improved. Nowadays, the median overall survival (OS) for patients classified as standard-risk can extend to 7 to 10 years, whereas high-risk patients can expect a median OS of approximately 5 years.14–17 This study delves into the research progress of immunotherapy for MM from 2013 to 2023, offering new insights into the future direction of research and development in this area.

Bibliometric methods were used to analyze the growth trend of MM immunotherapy related research in the past ten years. According to the annual number of publications, the growth of research related to immunotherapy for MM can be divided into three stages. Before 2016, the field was in a slow growth stage, with an annual volume of approximately 40 articles. From 2016 to 2019, the annual number of publications increased to around 80, effectively doubling from the previous stage. After 2019, the number of papers published each year exceeded 130, marking the onset of a rapid development phase. We speculate that this may be due to the fact that Daratumumab and Elotuzumab were approved for the treatment of MM in 2015;18–20 Belantamab mafodotin, the world’s first approved B cell maturation antigen (BCMA) targeted therapy in 2020, also has propelled the field of MM immunotherapy to a new level.21

Recent years have seen a surge in interest among researchers regarding the relationship between immunotherapy and MM, the US author ANDERSON KC ranked first in terms of the number of published papers. In the analysis of the number of national publications, the United States is far ahead in this field. Harvard University and its affiliated medical institutions stand out as the most prolific contributors to publications in this research domain. The findings confirm the significant contribution and leading position of the United States in the research of MM immunotherapy. This prominence may be attributed to the United States being a developed country, characterized by superior economic conditions, substantial medical investments, and more accessible clinical opportunities following drug approval. Developing countries could engage in extensive international research collaborations with developed nations to enhance their publication output and scientific research progress, thereby improving their clinical diagnostic and treatment capabilities.

The substantial number of published papers and the high frequency of citations offer valuable insights for researchers seeking to identify high-quality journals. Among the top ten journals, eight are classified as JCR Q1. The journal with the highest citation frequency was BLOOD, followed closely by LEUKEMIA, both of which are leading journals in the field of hematology. This not only underscores the authority of the aforementioned journals in the field of MM immunotherapy but also highlights the significant interest of leading scholars in this research area. Monitoring these journals will enable researchers to stay updated on new advancements and inform clinical treatment strategies.

The number of citations serves as one indicator for measuring the academic influence of a paper. Highly cited literature can reflect prevailing trends and foundational research within a specific field. This analysis focuses on the top ten most cited references and authors who have concentrated on the application of Daratumumab in MM, the use of BCMA CAR-T therapy, and the treatment of relapsed/refractory MM(RRMM).

In 2015, the study by Lokhorst HM et al. demonstrated that daratumumab, a humanized antibody targeting CD38, exhibits single-agent anti-tumor activity in patients with RRMM. Among patients who achieved complete remission and very good partial remission as a result of daratumumab monotherapy, 65% remained progression-free at 12 months.20 In 2016, a study conducted by Palumbo A team and Dimopoulos MA team demonstrated that in patients with RRMM, the addition of daratumumab to treatment significantly prolongs progression-free survival compared to traditional therapies. Specifically, the risk of disease progression or death in patients receiving daratumumab, bortezomib, and dexamethasone was found to be 61.4% lower than in those treated with bortezomib and dexamethasone alone.22 Furthermore, the incorporation of daratumumab into the regimen of lenalidomide and dexamethasone reduced the risk of disease progression or death by 63%.23

In 2013, Carpenter RO et al. demonstrated that BCMA was exclusively expressed in MM cell lines, while not being expressed by hematopoietic stem cells or normal non-hematopoietic cells, as confirmed through immunohistochemistry, flow cytometry, and quantitative PCR. Additionally, they designed and tested emerging anti-BCMA CARs.24 It is proposed that BCMA is a suitable target for CAR T cells, and adoptive transfer of anti-BCMA-CAR-expressing T cells is a promising new strategy for treating MM. In 2016, Ali SA and his team conducted the first human clinical trial of BCMA CAR-T cells, demonstrating that BCMA CAR-T therapy exhibits significant efficacy in patients with MM who are resistant to standard treatments. Furthermore, CAR-T cells targeting BCMA hold substantial promise as a novel and effective treatment option for MM.25 In 2019, the Cohen AD team conducted a Phase I study on the transduction of autologous T cells using a human BCMA-specific CAR lentivirus in patients with RRMM. Among the 25 subjects, the median number of prior treatments before enrollment was seven lines. Notably, 96% of the participants were dually resistant to proteasome inhibitors (PIs) and immunomodulatory drugs (IMIDs), while 72% exhibited resistance to daratumumab. Additionally, 44% were resistant to bortezomib, lenalidomide, carfilzomib, pomalidomide, and daratumumab. Furthermore, 96% of the subjects had at least one high-risk cytogenetic abnormality, with 68% demonstrating either a 17p deletion or TP53 mutation. The median proportion of myeloma cells in bone marrow biopsies was 65%, and 28% of the subjects presented with extramedullary lesions.26

The emergence of immunotherapy has led to significant advancements in the treatment of RRMM. A study conducted by Sagar Lonial, M.D., and his team in 2015 demonstrated that in patients with recurrent or refractory MM, the combination of the SLAM7 monoclonal antibody Elotuzumab with lenalidomide and dexamethasone resulted in improved outcomes. Specifically, compared to the administration of lenalidomide and dexamethasone alone, the median progression-free survival was extended by 4.5 months (p < .001), and the overall response rate increased by 13% (p < .001).19 In the treatment of BCMA CAR therapy, a study involving 16 patients with refractory MM (median number of treatments: 9.5) demonstrated a median event-free survival of 7.1 months. However, 38% of patients experienced grade 3 or higher cytokine release syndrome. This study was modified to only treat myeloma patients with less than 30% of bone marrow plasma cells.27 Two clinical trials investigating idecabtagene vicleucel, a B cell mature antigen-directed chimeric antigen receptor T cell therapy, have demonstrated its clinical efficacy in patients with RRMM. Among the 140 patients who had undergone at least three treatment regimens, 128 received ide-cel. The median duration of remission was 10.7 months, while the progression-free survival was recorded at 8.8 months. Notably, 26% of patients achieved minimal residual disease (MRD)-negative status.28,29 Due to the incurable nature of MM, the treatment of RRMM will always be a research hotspot. Keywords can reflect the core content of the research. In this study, the top three keywords were ‘Multiple myeloma,’ ‘Expression’ and ‘Immunotherapy,’ of which ‘Expression’ appeared 175 times, indicating that researchers have more research on cell surface molecular phenotypes, which may provide help for further search for drug treatment targets. Keyword clustering analysis takes the words with significant characteristics in this field as the clustering object, and carries out aggregation and classification to present the focus of research in this field. In this study, after clustering the keywords, ‘drug resistance’ is the second-ranked cluster, reflecting that in the context of the rapid development of MM immunotherapy, the exploration of drug resistance is also a major concern of researchers. Current studies indicate that the resistance mechanisms of immunomodulators (thalidomide, lenalidomide, and pomalidomide) can be categorized into several key areas. First, mutations in the pons and pons pathways, specifically in the IFKF1 and KPNA2 genes, along with mutations in the RAS/RAF signaling pathway (notably KRAS G12D and BRAF V600E), lead to reduced expression of target genes. Additionally, mutations in the pons and pons pathway genes result in alterations in genetic and genomic profiles. Second, a stem cell-like phenotype is observed, characterized by increased levels of multidrug transporters (ABCG2/BCRP) and elevated ALDH1A1 enzyme activity. This is accompanied by the activation of the Hedgehog, WNT, and Notch pathways, as well as the upregulation of the BTK receptor and RARa2, which contribute to a sustained increase in cancer stem cells. Lastly, an increase in cell adhesion molecules, particularly CD44, influences the Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway, thereby altering the bone marrow microenvironment. The resistance mechanisms of monoclonal antibodies can be categorized into four main types: 1. A decrease in the expression of CD38 and SLAM7, which results in antigen escape; 2. An increase in the expression of CD46, CD56, and CD59 due to complement-dependent cytotoxicity induced by blocking antibodies, leading to complement resistance; 3. The competition between extracellular soluble forms of CD38 and SLAM7, which alters the bone marrow microenvironment; and 4. The presence of anti-idiotypic antibodies that neutralize the activity of therapeutic monoclonal antibodies.30 The resistance to CAR T cell therapy may be related to antigen escape, T cell dysfunction or depletion, and immunosuppressive bone marrow microenvironment.31–36 Burst analysis can identify keywords that change significantly over time, and researchers can use emergent keywords to explore research hotspots. In the field of MM immunotherapy, the latest development trends are bispecific antibody and preclinical activity. Bispecific antibody is defined as a pharmaceutical compound constituted of two monoclonal antibodies. It comprises two binding domains, enabling it to bind simultaneously to two different antigens. This unique feature provides a significant advantage over other treatment options. I In MM therapy, bispecific antibodies target specific molecules on malignant plasma cells and CD3 on T cells, activating T cells independently of the major histocompatibility complex and T cell receptor specificity.37,38 This innovative approach directly activates T cells and triggers tumor cell death, offering a promising strategy for refractory MM cases. Several MM cell targets showing efficacy in prior clinical trials (such as GPRC5D, FcRH5 et.al) are now in phase I/II clinical trials.39–43

In recent decades, tumor immunotherapy has emerged as a promising treatment for various cancers, also has been extensively utilized in the treatment of MM. Targeted therapies, such as daratumumab, are not only indicated for patients with RRMM but have also received approval for first-line treatment. This allows patients to attain early deep remission, prolong the time to recurrence, and enhance their quality of life. Looking ahead, immunotherapy is poised to become the primary treatment modality for MM, particularly in RRMM cases. This study for the first time utilized bibliometric methods to systematically analyze the existing literature on immunotherapy for MM, providing researchers and clinicians with a comprehensive and objective understanding of the development trend and research frontiers in this field to guide the selection of clinical treatment options. At the same time, this study still has some limitations. This paper only retrieves the research in the WoS database, which may result in the omission of related research from other databases. Furthermore, this paper only includes articles published in English, neglecting research published in other languages. Future studies should explore articles from diverse databases and languages to enhance our comprehensive understanding of the application of immunotherapy in MM.

Stochastic complex Ginzburg-Landau equation on compact surfaces
Tristan Robert and Younes Zine

Abstract
. We study a stochastic complex Ginzburg-Landau equation (SCGL) on compact surfaces with magnetic Laplacian and polynomial nonlinearity, forced by a space-time white noise. After renormalizing the equation in a suitable manner, we show that the dynamics is locally well-posed. Moreover, we prove deterministic global well-posedness for the defocusing SCGL in the weakly dispersive regime.

1.1.
The stochastic complex Ginzburg-Landau equation

In this work, we discuss the well-posedness issue for the following stochastic complex Ginzburg-Landau (SCGL) equation with an additive space-time white noise forcing on a general closed (compact, connected, boundaryless) orientable surface:

(1.1)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u=(\alpha_{1}+i\alpha_{2})\Delta_{A}u-(\beta_{1}+i\beta_{2})\mathcal{V}^{\prime}(|u|^{2})u+\sqrt{2\gamma}\xi,\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathcal{M}\\

1.3.
Well-posedness of the renormalized dynamics

In view of the discussion in the previous subsection, we thus consider the following equation:

(1.17)

\begin{split}\partial_{t}u_{N}&=(\alpha_{1}+i\alpha_{2})\Delta_{A}u_{N}\\
&\qquad\quad-(\beta_{1}+i\beta_{2})(-1)^{m-1}(m-1)!\,L_{m-1}^{(1)}\big(|u_{N}|^{2};\sigma_{N}\big)u_{N}+\sqrt{2\gamma}\mathbf{P}_{N}\xi,\end{split}

with some deterministic initial data

N\in\mathbb{N}

Our first result deals with local well-posedness for the renormalized equation (

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\alpha_{1},\gamma>0
and
\alpha_{2},\beta_{1},\beta_{2}\in\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle\Psi_{N}+C\left([0,T];\mathcal{C}^{s_{0}}(\mathcal{M};\mathbb{C})\right)\cap C\left((0,T];\mathcal{C}^{2\varepsilon}(\mathcal{M};\mathbb{C})\right).

2.2.
Geometric background

We now start by describing the geometric context, for which we refer to
[

(\mathcal{M},\mathbf{\mathrm{g}})

with
(\mathbf{\mathrm{g}}_{j,k})_{j,k}

\displaystyle\big(\mathbf{\mathrm{g}}^{j,k}(x)\big)=\big(\mathbf{\mathrm{g}}_{j,k}(x)\big)^{-1}.

We also write

\displaystyle|\mathbf{\mathrm{g}}(x)|=\det\big(\mathbf{\mathrm{g}}_{j,k}(x)\big)\geq c>0.

Throughout this text, we write the volume form

The inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{T^{\star}\mathcal{M}}
on vector fields is locally given by

\langle X,Y\rangle_{T\mathcal{M}}=\mathbf{\mathrm{g}}_{j,k}X^{j}\overline{Y^{k}}

for any vector fields

,

, i.e. smooth sections of

. Similarly, for any 1-forms

and

2.3.
Spectral properties of the magnetic Laplacian

To each smooth unitary connection

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Delta_{A}u&=D_{A}^{*}D_{A}u=|\mathbf{\mathrm{g}}|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{1\leq j,k\leq 2}\partial_{j}(|\mathbf{\mathrm{g}}|^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{\mathrm{g}}^{j,k}\partial_{k}u\big)\\
&\qquad\quad+i\mathbf{\mathrm{g}}^{j,k}A_{k}\partial_{j}u-i\mathbf{\mathrm{g}}^{j,k}\partial_{j}(A_{k}u)+(A_{1}^{2}+A_{2}^{2})u\end{split}

for any smooth function

. This defines a non-positive self-adjoint operator on

, which is known (see e.g.
[
39
]
) to have a compact resolvent. Thus for each smooth

there exists a basis
\{\varphi_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}\subset C^{\infty}(\mathcal{M})
of
L^{2}(\mathcal{M};\mathbb{C})
consisting of complex-valued eigenfunctions of
-\Delta_{A}
associated with the corresponding eigenvalues
\{-\lambda_{n}^{2}\}_{n\geq 0}
arranged in increasing order:
0\leq\lambda_{0}\leq\lambda_{1}\leq...\leq\lambda_{n}
and satisfying
\lambda_{n}\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
. Moreover, any

can be expanded (in the sense of distributions) as

\displaystyle u=\sum_{n\geq 0}\langle u,\varphi_{n}\rangle_{\mathcal{D}^{\prime},\mathcal{D}}\cdot\varphi_{n},

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes the usual duality pairing between
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathcal{M})
and
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{M})
which coincides with the
L^{2}(\mathcal{M};\mathbb{C})
-inner product

(2.2)

\displaystyle\langle u,\varphi_{n}\rangle_{L^{2}}=\int_{\mathcal{M}}u\overline{\varphi_{n}}dV

when

. Last but not least, recall that the magnetic Laplacian

admits
gauge invariance
: for any

it holds

in the sense of operators

.
In the following, we write

if

and

are gauge equivalent, namely
A=B+df
for some smooth

gives a geometric characterisation of gauge classes; of particular interest to us is that the lowest eigenvalue
\lambda_{0}
of

is positive except when

is exact.

In our analysis, we will need some information of the localization of the eigenvalues. This can be achieved through studying the spectral function, which is defined as

\displaystyle e(x,y,\Lambda^{2})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textup{def}}}{{=}}\sum_{\lambda_{n}^{2}\leq\Lambda^{2}}\varphi_{n}(x)\overline{\varphi_{n}(y)},

for
(x,y)\in\mathcal{M}\times\mathcal{M}
and
\Lambda\in\mathbb{R}
. We have the following behaviour of the spectral function on the diagonal (see
[
28
, Theorem 1.1]
; note that here the
\lambda_{n}
’s are the square roots of the eigenvalues of
-\Delta_{A}
).

Lemma 2.1
.

The following asymptotics hold uniformly in
x\in\mathcal{M}
as
\Lambda\to\infty
:

(2.3)

e(x,x,\Lambda^{2})=c\Lambda^{2}+O(\Lambda),

for some constant
c>0
only depending on
(\mathcal{M},\mathbf{\mathrm{g}})
.

In particular, integrating (
2.3
) on
\mathcal{M}
, we obtain some useful information on the localization of the eigenvalues:

n+1=\#\big\{k:\lambda_{k}\leq\lambda_{n}\big\}=c\lambda_{n}^{2}+O(\lambda_{n})

This implies that

(2.4)

\displaystyle\lambda_{n}\sim n^{\frac{1}{2}}

as
n\to\infty
.

As a direct consequence, we obtain the boundedness in average of the eigenfunctions
\varphi_{n}
.

Corollary 2.2
.

For any
\nu\in\mathbb{R}
, there exists
C>0
such that for any
\Lambda>0
and any
x\in\mathcal{M}
, it holds

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda_{n}\in(\Lambda,\Lambda+1]}\frac{|\varphi_{n}(x)|^{2}}{\lambda_{n}^{\nu}}\leq C\sum_{\lambda_{n}\in(\Lambda,\Lambda+1]}\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}^{\nu}}.

Proof.

We simply use Lemma
2.1
to bound

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda_{n}\in(\Lambda,\Lambda+1]}\frac{|\varphi_{n}(x)|^{2}}{\lambda_{n}^{\nu}}

\displaystyle\sim\Lambda^{-\nu}\Big[e\big(x,x,(\Lambda+1)^{2}\big)-e(x,x,\Lambda^{2})\Big]

\displaystyle\lesssim\Lambda^{-\nu}\Lambda\lesssim\Lambda^{-\nu}\#\big\{k:\Lambda<\lambda_{k}\leq\Lambda+1\big\}\sim\sum_{\lambda_{n}\in(\Lambda,\Lambda+1]}\lambda_{n}^{-\nu}.

∎

2.4.
Function spaces, linear and nonlinear estimates

Next, recall that the functional calculus of
-\Delta_{A}
is defined for any
\psi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{C})
by

\displaystyle\psi\big(-\Delta_{A}\big)f=\sum_{n\geq 0}\psi(\lambda_{n}^{2})\langle f,\varphi_{n}\rangle\varphi_{n},

for all
f\in C^{\infty}(\mathcal{M};\mathbb{C})
. This in particular allows us to define the more general class of Besov spaces associated with
\Delta_{A}
. First, using the functional calculus, we can define the Littlewood-Paley projectors
\mathbf{Q}_{M}
for a dyadic integer

as

\displaystyle\mathbf{Q}_{M}=\begin{cases}\psi_{0}\big(-M^{-2}\Delta_{A}\big)-\psi_{0}\big(-4M^{-2}\Delta_{A}\big),~~M\geq 1,\\
\psi_{0}\big(-4\Delta_{A}\big),~~M=\frac{1}{2},\end{cases}

where
\psi_{0}\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R})
is non-negative and such that
\supp\psi_{0}\subset[-1,1]
and
\psi_{0}\equiv 1
on
[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}]
.
We also define the regularization operators

(2.5)

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{N}=\psi_{0}\big(-N^{-2}\Delta_{A}\big).

With the inhomogeneous dyadic partition of unity

, we can then define the Besov norms for
p,q\in[1,\infty]
and

,

(2.6)

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}_{p,q}(\mathcal{M})}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textup{def}}}{{=}}\Big\|\langle M\rangle^{qs}\|\mathbf{Q}_{M}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathcal{M})}\Big\|_{\ell^{q}_{M}}=\Big(\sum_{M\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}_{-1}}}\langle M\rangle^{qs}\big\|\mathbf{Q}_{M}f\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathcal{M})}^{q}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

for any smooth function
f
. The last sum is of course understood to be the supremum for
q=\infty
.

Definition 2.3
.

Fix
p,q\in[1,\infty]
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
. We define the Besov space
\mathcal{B}^{s}_{p,q}(\mathcal{M})
to be the completion of
C^{\infty}(\mathcal{M})
for the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}_{p,q}(\mathcal{M})}
. In particular, this defines the Hölder spaces
\mathcal{C}^{s}(\mathcal{M})=\mathcal{B}^{s}_{\infty,\infty}(\mathcal{M})
for any
s\in\mathbb{R}
.

Remark 2.4
.

We make some observations.

(i) Note that since they only depend on the principal symbol of
\Delta_{A}
(see e.g.
[
12
, Proposition 2.1]
), these spaces are independent of
A
.

(ii) In the literature, Besov spaces are often defined as the space of distributions having finite Besov norm (see e.g.
[
3
, Definition 2.68]
in the Euclidean case). Both definitions coincide for

, but there is a logarithmic difference when

3.4
and Lemma

below). As it is more convenient to work with smooth functions in some places (see for example Lemma

[

]
for further discussion.

Proof.

By

We now investigate the properties of the fundamental solution for the magnetic Laplace equation, defined as

6

6

Recall that

(2.10)

\displaystyle G_{A}(x,y)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textup{def}}}{{=}}\sum_{\lambda_{n}\geq\lambda_{+}}\frac{\varphi_{n}(x)\overline{\varphi_{n}(y)}}{\lambda_{n}^{2}},\quad(x,y)\in\mathcal{M}\times\mathcal{M},

Lemma 2.9
.

(i)

\displaystyle\big|\nabla_{x}G_{A}(x,y)\big|\leq C\mathbf{d}(x,y)^{-1}.

Proof.

Since
-\Delta_{A}
has the same principal symbol as
-\Delta_{\mathbf{\mathrm{g}}}
, we will recover the bounds of Lemma

from that of the standard Laplacian in
\mathbb{R}^{2}

\displaystyle(1-\Delta_{A})^{-1}-(1-\Delta_{\mathbf{\mathrm{g}}})^{-1}=(1-\Delta_{A})^{-1}(\Delta_{\mathbf{\mathrm{g}}}-\Delta_{A})(1-\Delta_{\mathbf{\mathrm{g}}})^{-1}:H^{s}(\mathcal{M})\to H^{s+3}(\mathcal{M})

for any

by a continuous function. Moreover, denoting

for the massive Green’s function, which is the kernel of

\displaystyle G_{A}(x,y)-G_{\langle A\rangle}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda_{n}\geq\lambda_{+}}\big(\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}^{2}}-\frac{1}{\langle\lambda_{n}\rangle^{2}}\big)\varphi_{n}(x)\overline{\varphi_{n}(y)}+\mathbf{1}_{\lambda_{0}=0}\varphi_{0}(x)\overline{\varphi_{0}(y)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda_{n}\geq\lambda_{+}}\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}^{2}\langle\lambda_{n}\rangle^{2}}\varphi_{n}(x)\overline{\varphi_{n}(y)}+\mathbf{1}_{\lambda_{0}=0}\varphi_{0}(x)\overline{\varphi_{0}(y)},

where the right-hand side is a continuous function in view of Corollary
2.2
and (

). Similarly,

is a continuous function, where

is the usual Green’s function of the Laplace-Beltrami operator on

To get (

\displaystyle G_{A}=\int_{0}^{1}P_{A}(t)dt+R_{A}

\displaystyle R_{A}(x,y)=\sum_{\lambda_{n}\geq\lambda_{+}}\frac{e^{-\lambda_{n}^{2}}}{\lambda_{n}^{2}}\varphi_{n}(x)\overline{\varphi_{n}(y)}-\mathbf{1}_{\lambda_{0}=0}\varphi_{0}(x)\overline{\varphi_{0}(y)}.

In particular

35

36
]

\displaystyle P_{A}(t,x,y)=\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}t^{\frac{k}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{i(x-y)\cdot\eta}p_{k}(x,\sqrt{t}\eta)d\eta+R_{-K,t}(x,y),

for arbitrary

. From the smoothing property (
2.9
) of the operator with kernel

and Sobolev embedding, we get

\displaystyle\|R_{-K,t}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,y}}\lesssim\|R_{-K,t}\|_{H^{-1-\delta}\to H^{1+\delta}}\lesssim t^{\frac{K-2-2\delta}{2}}

for any

. Similarly,

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{x}R_{-K,t}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,y}}\lesssim\|R_{-K,t}\|_{H^{-1-\delta}\to H^{2+\delta}}\lesssim t^{\frac{K-3-2\delta}{2}}.

This is enough for (
2.12
) after integrating in time, provided that we take

.

As for the kernels in the sum for
k\geq 1
, since
p_{k}
is Schwartz in
\eta
, after changing variables we get

\displaystyle t^{\frac{k}{2}}\Big\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{i(x-y)\cdot\eta}p_{k}(x,\sqrt{t}\eta)d\eta\Big\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,y}}

\displaystyle=t^{\frac{k-2}{2}}\Big\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{i(x-y)\cdot t^{-\frac{1}{2}}\zeta}p_{k}(x,\zeta)d\zeta\Big\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,y}}

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{\frac{k-2}{2}}\|p_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}L^{1}_{\eta}},

and integrating by parts,

\displaystyle t^{\frac{k-2}{2}}\Big\|\nabla_{x}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{i(x-y)\cdot t^{-\frac{1}{2}}\zeta}p_{k}(x,\zeta)d\zeta\Big\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,y}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=t^{\frac{k-2}{2}}\Big\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{i(x-y)\cdot t^{-\frac{1}{2}}\zeta}\big(it^{-\frac{1}{2}}p_{k}(x,\zeta)\zeta+\nabla_{x}p_{k}(x,\zeta)\big)\Big\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,y}}

\displaystyle\mathbf{\mathrm{g}}(x)^{j,\ell}\partial_{x_{j}}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{i(x-y)\cdot\eta}e^{-t\mathbf{\mathrm{g}}(x)\eta\cdot\eta}d\eta dt

Now, since we use geodesic normal coordinates around

small enough. Otherwise,
\alpha_{2}>0
and the quadratic form takes non-negative values if and only if the matrix

\begin{pmatrix}\Big(\frac{1}{4}-\eta\Big)r&\frac{p-2}{8}\\
\frac{p-2}{8}&\ \ \Big(\frac{p-1}{4}-\eta\Big)\end{pmatrix}

is non-negative definite, which is the case under (
4.28
) for
\eta
small enough. Going back to (
4.38
), this yields

(4.39)

\displaystyle-\Re(\alpha_{1}+i\alpha_{2})\langle\nabla v,\nabla\big[|v|^{p-2}v\big]\rangle_{L^{2}(T\mathcal{M})}\leq-4\eta\alpha_{1}\int_{\mathcal{M}}|v|^{p-2}|\nabla v|^{2}dV

for some
\eta>0
small enough.

Finally, for the last term on the right-hand side of (
4.31
), we simply note by (
4.3
) and (
4.30
), we have that

(4.40)

\displaystyle F(v,\Xi)=(\beta_{1}+i\beta_{2})|v|^{2m-2}v+F_{0}(v,\Xi).

Hence, (
4.29
) follows from (
4.31
), (
4.39
), (
4.40
) and integrating in time.
∎

The next result provides a control for the growth in time of the
L^{p}
norm.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
\alpha_{1},\beta_{1}>0
,
\alpha_{2}\geq 0
and
\beta_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
. Fix an integer
m\geq 2
and
p\geq 1
satisfying (
4.28
). Let
T>0
,
t_{0}\in(0,T]
,
\varepsilon>0
and
v
be a solution to (
4.2
) on
[t_{0},T]
, with enhanced data set
(v_{0},\Xi)\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathcal{M})\times\mathcal{Z}^{\varepsilon,q}([0,T])
, belonging to the class
C([t_{0},T];\mathcal{C}^{2\varepsilon}(\mathcal{M}))\cap C^{1}_{t}C^{2}_{x}((t_{0},T)\times\mathcal{M})
. Then, there exist paremeters
0<\varepsilon=\varepsilon(m,p)\ll 1
and
1\leq q=q(p,m)<\infty
depending only on
(p,m)
and a constant
C=C\big(m,p,A)>0
depending only on
(p,m,A)
such that we have

\|v(t)\|_{L^{p}}\leq\|v(t_{0})\|_{L^{p}}+C\cdot\big((t-t_{0})+\|\Xi\|_{\mathcal{Z}_{T}^{\varepsilon,q}}^{q}\big)

for all
t\in[t_{0},T]

Proof.

Let the parameters
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\beta_{1},\beta_{2},m,T,t_{0},p,q,\varepsilon
and the data
(v_{0},\Xi)
be as in the statement. Our strategy is standard and amounts to showing that the terms on the right-hand-side of (
4.29
) may estimated by a small fraction of the (positive) left-hand-side and another term independent of
v
.

We begin by estimating the first term on the right-hand-side of (
4.29
). By the definition of
F_{0}
(
4.30
), we have

(4.41)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\big|\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\langle F_{0}(v,\Xi)(t^{\prime}),|v|^{p-2}v(t^{\prime})\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathcal{M})}dt^{\prime}\big|\\
&\lesssim\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq j_{1}\leq m\\
0\leq j_{2}\leq m-1\\
(j_{1},j_{2})\neq(m,m-1)\end{subarray}}\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\big|\langle v^{j_{1}}(t^{\prime})\overline{v}^{j_{2}}(t^{\prime})\Xi_{m-j_{1},m-j_{2}-1}(t^{\prime}),|v|^{p-2}v(t^{\prime})\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathcal{M})}\big|dt^{\prime}.\end{split}

Fix
0\leq j_{1}\leq m,0\leq j_{2}\leq m-1
with
j_{1}+j_{2}\leq 2m-2
and
0<\varepsilon\ll 1
to be chosen later. In what follows, we estimate the integrand on the right-hand-side of (
4.41
) while keeping its dependence in
t_{0}\leq t^{\prime}\leq t
implicit to ease our notations. By duality, the product rule of Lemma
2.8
(iv), the interpolation estimate of Lemma
2.8
(v), and the Leibniz rule, we have

(4.42)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\big|\langle v^{j_{1}}\overline{v}^{j_{2}}\Xi_{m-j_{1},m-j_{2}-1},|v|^{p-2}v\rangle_{L^{2}}\big|\\
&\qquad\leq\|\Xi_{m-j_{1},m-j_{2}-1}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{-\varepsilon}}\big\|v^{\frac{p}{2}+j_{1}}\overline{v}^{\frac{p}{2}-1+j_{2}}\big\|_{B^{\varepsilon}_{1,1}}\\
&\qquad\lesssim\|\Xi_{m-j_{1},m-j_{2}-1}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{-\varepsilon}}\bigg\{\Big(\int_{\mathcal{M}}|v|^{p-1+j_{1}+j_{2}}dV\Big)^{(1-\varepsilon)}\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\Big(\int_{\mathcal{M}}|v|^{p-2+j_{1}+j_{2}}|\nabla v|(t)dV\Big)^{\varepsilon}+\int_{\mathcal{M}}|v|^{p-1+j_{1}+j_{2}}dV\bigg\}.\end{split}

By Hölder’s inequality, we have that

(4.43)

\displaystyle\bigg(\int_{\mathcal{M}}|v|^{p-1+j_{1}+j_{2}}dV\bigg)\lesssim\bigg(\int_{\mathcal{M}}|v|^{p-2+2m}dV\bigg)^{\frac{p-1+j_{1}+j_{2}}{p-2+2m}}

and by Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,

(4.44)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\bigg(\int_{\mathcal{M}}|v|^{p-2+j_{1}+j_{2}}|\nabla v|dV\Big)^{\varepsilon}\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\lesssim\bigg(\int_{\mathcal{M}}|v|^{p-2}|\nabla v|^{2}dV\bigg)^{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}\Big(\int_{\mathcal{M}}|v|^{p-2+2(j_{1}+j_{2})}dV\bigg)^{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}.\end{split}

We estimate the last integral above, by Sobolev’s inequality as follows:

(4.45)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\bigg(\int_{\mathcal{M}}|v|^{p-2+2(j_{1}+j_{2})}dV\bigg)^{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}\\
&\qquad=\|v^{\frac{p}{2}}\|_{L^{\frac{2}{p}(p-2+2(j_{1}+j_{2}))}}^{\frac{\varepsilon}{p}(p-2+2(j_{1}+j_{2}))}\lesssim\|v^{\frac{p}{2}}\|_{H^{1}}^{\frac{\varepsilon}{p}(p-2+2(j_{1}+j_{2}))}\\
&\qquad\lesssim\bigg(\int_{\mathcal{M}}|v|^{p}dV\Big)^{\frac{\varepsilon}{2p}(p-2+2(j_{1}+j_{2}))}+\Big(\int_{\mathcal{M}}|v|^{p-2}|\nabla v|^{2}dV\bigg)^{\frac{\varepsilon}{2p}(p-2+2(j_{1}+j_{2}))}.\end{split}

Hence, combining (
4.42
), (
4.43
), (
4.44
) and (
4.45
) together yields

(4.46)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\big|\langle v^{j_{1}}\overline{v}^{j_{2}}\Xi_{m-j_{1},m-j_{2}-1},|v|^{p-2}v\rangle_{L^{2}}\big|\\
&\lesssim\|\Xi_{m-j_{1},m-j_{2}-1}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{-\varepsilon}}\bigg\{\bigg(\int_{\mathcal{M}}|v|^{p-2}|\nabla v|^{2}dV\bigg)^{\frac{p-1+j_{1}+j_{2}}{p-2+2m}}\\
&\hskip 9.10486pt+\bigg(\int_{\mathcal{M}}|v|^{p-2+2m}dV\bigg)^{(1-\varepsilon)\frac{p-1+j_{1}+j_{2}}{p-2+2m}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\frac{p-2+2(j_{1}+j_{2})}{p-2+2m}}\bigg(\int_{\mathcal{M}}|v|^{p-2}|\nabla v|^{2}dV\bigg)^{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}\\
&\hskip 9.10486pt+\bigg(\int_{\mathcal{M}}|v(t)|^{p-2+2m}dV\bigg)^{(1-\varepsilon)\frac{p-1+j_{1}+j_{2}}{p-2+2m}}\bigg(\int_{\mathcal{M}}|v|^{p-2}|\nabla v|^{2}dV\bigg)^{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2p}(p-2+2(j_{1}+j_{2}))}\bigg\}.\end{split}

Now, by using Young’s inequality twice and since
j_{1}+j_{2}\leq 2m-2
, we can find
0<\varepsilon\ll 1
small enough such that we have

(4.47)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\|\Xi_{m-j_{1},m-j_{2}-1}(t)\|_{\mathcal{C}^{-\varepsilon}}\bigg(\int_{\mathcal{M}}|v(t)|^{p-2+2m}dV\bigg)^{(1-\varepsilon)\frac{p-1+j_{1}+j_{2}}{p-2+2m}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\frac{p-2+2(j_{1}+j_{2})}{p-2+2m}}\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\bigg(\int_{\mathcal{M}}|v|^{p-2}|\nabla v|^{2}(t)dV\bigg)^{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}\\
&\leq\delta_{1}\int_{\mathcal{M}}|v(t)|^{p-2+2m}dV+C(\delta_{1})\|\Xi_{m-j_{1},m-j_{2}-1}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{-\varepsilon}}^{q^{\prime}_{1}}\bigg(\int_{\mathcal{M}}|v|^{p-2}|\nabla v|^{2}dV\bigg)^{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}q_{1}^{\prime}}\\
&\leq\delta_{1}\int_{\mathcal{M}}|v|^{p-2+2m}dV+\delta_{2}\int_{\mathcal{M}}|v|^{p-2}|\nabla v|^{2}(t)dV+C(\delta_{1},\delta_{2})\|\Xi_{m-j_{1},m-j_{2}-1}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{-\varepsilon}}^{q_{1}^{\prime}r_{1}^{\prime}}\end{split}

for arbitrary
\delta_{1},\delta_{2}>0
and where

q_{1}=\frac{p-2+2m}{(1-\varepsilon)(p-1+j_{1}+j_{2})+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}(p-2+2(j_{1}+j_{2}))}

and
r_{1}=\frac{2}{\varepsilon q^{\prime}}
. In the above,
C(\delta_{1},\delta_{2})>0
is a large constant depending on
p
,
m
, and
\varepsilon
.

Similarly, provided
\varepsilon
is small enough, we have

(4.48)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\|\Xi_{m-j_{1},m-j_{2}-1}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{-\varepsilon}}\bigg(\int_{\mathcal{M}}|v|^{p-2+2m}dV\bigg)^{(1-\varepsilon)\frac{p-1+j_{1}+j_{2}}{p-2+2m}}\\
&\qquad\qquad\times\bigg(\int_{\mathcal{M}}|v|^{p-2}|\nabla v|^{2}dV\bigg)^{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2p}(p-2+2(j_{1}+j_{2}))}\\
&\leq\delta_{1}\int_{\mathcal{M}}|v|^{p-2+2m}dV+\delta_{2}\int_{\mathcal{M}}|v|^{p-2}|\nabla v|^{2}dV+C(\delta_{1},\delta_{2})\|\Xi_{m-j_{1},m-j_{2}-1}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{-\varepsilon}}^{q_{2}^{\prime}r_{2}^{\prime}},\end{split}

with
q_{2}=\frac{p-2+2m}{(1-\varepsilon)(p-1+j_{1}+j_{2})}
and
\frac{1}{r_{2}}=q_{2}^{\prime}(\frac{\varepsilon}{2}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2p}(p-2+2(j_{1}+j_{2})))
. At last, we also have

(4.49)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\|\Xi_{m-j_{1},m-j_{2}-1}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{-\varepsilon}}\bigg(\int_{\mathcal{M}}|v|^{p-2}|\nabla v|^{2}dV\bigg)^{\frac{p-1+j_{1}+j_{2}}{p-2+2m}}\\
&\leq\delta_{2}\int_{\mathcal{M}}|v|^{p-2}|\nabla v|^{2}dV+C(\delta_{2})\|\Xi_{m-j_{1},m-j_{2}-1}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{-\varepsilon}}^{\frac{p-2+2m}{2m-1-j_{1}-j_{2}}}.\end{split}

Thus, by (
4.46
), (
4.47
), (
4.48
) and (
4.49
), summing over
0\leq j_{1}\leq m
and
0\leq j_{2}\leq m-1
with
(j_{1},j_{2})\neq(m,m-1)
, and integrating in
t^{\prime}\in[t_{0},t]
, we deduce

(4.50)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\big|\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\langle F_{0}(v,\Xi)(t^{\prime}),|v|^{p-2}v(t^{\prime})\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathcal{M})}dt^{\prime}\big|\\
&\leq C_{\circ}\delta_{1}\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\int_{\mathcal{M}}|v(t^{\prime})|^{p-2+2m}dVdt^{\prime}+C_{\circ}\delta_{2}\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\int_{\mathcal{M}}|v|^{p-2}|\nabla v|^{2}(t^{\prime})dVdt^{\prime}\\
&\qquad\qquad+C(\delta_{1},\delta_{2})\big((t-t_{0})+\|\Xi\|_{\mathcal{Z}_{T}^{\varepsilon,q}}^{q}\big),\end{split}

for some constant
C_{\circ}=C_{\circ}(m)>0
independent of
\delta_{1},\delta_{2}
and some large
q=q(m,p)\geq 1
.

We now consider the remaining terms on the right-hand side of (
4.29
). By the Cauchy-Schwarz, Hölder and Young inequalities, we have (omitting the time dependence again)

(4.51)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\big|\langle|v|^{p-2}vA,dv\rangle_{L^{2}(T^{*}\mathcal{M})}\big|\\
&\leq\|A\|_{L^{\infty}}\bigg(\int_{\mathcal{M}}|v|^{p-2}|\nabla v|^{2}dV\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\int_{\mathcal{M}}|v|^{p}dV\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
&\leq\delta_{2}\int_{\mathcal{M}}|v|^{p-2}|\nabla v|^{2}dV+C(\delta_{2},\|A\|_{L^{\infty}},p,m)\Big(\int_{\mathcal{M}}|v|^{p-2+2m}dV\Big)^{\frac{p}{p-2+2m}}\\
&\leq\delta_{2}\int_{\mathcal{M}}|v|^{p-2}|\nabla v|^{2}dV+\delta_{1}\int_{\mathcal{M}}|v|^{p-2+2m}dV+C(\delta_{1},\delta_{2},\|A\|_{L^{\infty}},p,m).\end{split}

Similarly, by integration by parts, the Leibniz rule for 1-forms, Cauchy-Schwarz, Hölder, and Young inequalities, we have

(4.52)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\big|\langle d^{*}(Av,|v|^{p-2}v\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathcal{M})}\big|\lesssim_{p}\|A\|_{L^{\infty}}\int_{\mathcal{M}}|v|^{p-1}|\nabla v|dV\\
&\lesssim\|A\|_{L^{\infty}}\Big(\int_{\mathcal{M}}|v|^{p-2}|\nabla v|^{2}(t)dV\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\int_{\mathcal{M}}|v|^{p}dV\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
&\leq\delta_{2}\int_{\mathcal{M}}|v|^{p-2}|\nabla v|^{2}dV+\delta_{1}\int_{\mathcal{M}}|v|^{p-2+2m}dV+C(\delta_{1},\delta_{2},\|A\|_{L^{\infty}},p,m).\end{split}

By Lemma
4.5
, (
4.29
) together with (
4.50
), (
4.51
) and (
4.52
), we end up with

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p}\Big(\big\|v(t)\big\|_{L^{p}}^{p}-\big\|v(t_{0})\big\|_{L^{p}}^{p}\Big)+\beta_{1}\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\int_{\mathcal{M}}|v(t^{\prime})|^{p-2+2m}dVdt^{\prime}+\alpha_{1}\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\int_{\mathcal{M}}|v(t^{\prime})|^{p}|A|^{2}dVdt^{\prime}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+4\eta\alpha_{1}\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\int_{\mathcal{M}}|v|^{p-2}(t^{\prime})|\nabla v|^{2}(t^{\prime})dVdt^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq(C_{\circ}+2)\delta_{1}\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\int_{\mathcal{M}}|v(t^{\prime})|^{p-2+2m}dVdt^{\prime}+(C_{\circ}+2)\delta_{2}\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\int_{\mathcal{M}}|v(t^{\prime})|^{p-2}|\nabla v(t^{\prime})|^{2}dVdt^{\prime}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+C(\delta_{1},\delta_{2})\big((t-t_{0})+\|\Xi\|_{\mathcal{Z}_{T}^{\varepsilon,q}}^{q}\big)dt^{\prime}.

This proves (
4.32
) after taking
\delta_{1},\delta_{2}
small enough such that we can absorb the first two terms of the right-hand side above into the left-hand side.
∎

We are now in position to prove Proposition
4.4
.

Proof of Proposition
4.4
.

Fix
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\beta_{1},\beta_{2},m,s_{0},r,p
as in the statement of Proposition
4.4
and let
\varepsilon,q
be given by Lemma
4.6
. As discussed in the above, we assume without loss of generality that
\alpha_{2}\geq 0
. Note that the condition (
1.19
) is such that Proposition
4.3
and condition (
4.28
) hold; see Remark
1.4
.

Let
v_{0}\in\mathcal{C}^{s_{0}}(\mathcal{M})
and
\Xi\in\mathcal{Z}^{\varepsilon,q}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
. Consider the solution
v\in C([0,T];\mathcal{C}^{s_{0}}(\mathcal{M}))\cap C((0,T];\mathcal{C}^{2\varepsilon}(\mathcal{M}))
, for some
T\in(0,1]
, to (
4.2
) with enhanced data set
(v_{0},\Xi)
, provided by Proposition
4.1
.

We now fix an arbitrary time
T_{0}>0
. By applying Proposition
4.3
to (
4.2
) with data
(v(T),\Xi(\cdot-T))
, the maximal existence time
T^{*}\in(T,T_{0}]
of
v
on
[0,T_{0}]
satisfies the following alternative:

\displaystyle T^{\star}=T_{0}\qquad\text{or}\qquad\sup_{0\leq t<T^{\star}}\|v(t)\|_{L^{p}}=+\infty.

Thus, if suffices to show that there exists a positive constant
B_{0}=B(T_{0},\|v_{0}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{s_{0}}},\|\Xi\|_{\mathcal{Z}^{\varepsilon,q_{0}}_{T_{0}}})
such that

(4.53)

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t<T^{\star}}\|v(t)\|_{L^{p}}\leq B_{0}

to conclude the proof since
T_{0}
is arbitrary. We note that (
4.53
) also proves the bound (
4.26
).

The estimate (
4.53
) would follow immediately from Lemmas
4.5
and
4.6
if
v
were smooth. We circumvent this technicality via an approximation argument. To this end, we introduce some notations. Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
and
\Xi^{N}=\mathbf{P}_{N}\Xi
, where
\mathbf{P}_{N}
is the frequency projection (
2.5
). Then, by Proposition
4.1
and the uniform boundedness of
\{\mathbf{P}_{N}\}_{N\in\mathbb{N}}
in
L^{p}
spaces, there exists
T_{1}\leq T
independent of
N
and a solution
v^{N}\in C([0,T_{1}];\mathcal{C}^{s_{0}}(\mathcal{M}))\cap C((0,T_{1}];\mathcal{C}^{2\varepsilon}(\mathcal{M}))
to (
4.2
) with data
(v_{0},\Xi^{N})
such that

(4.54)

\displaystyle\|v^{N}(T_{1})\|_{\mathcal{C}^{2\varepsilon}}\lesssim 1+\|v_{0}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{s_{0}}}.

The bound (
4.54
) is a consequence of the regularity of the solution map (
4.7
). Here, the implicit constant is independent of
N
.

Now, by Proposition
4.3
, there exists a maximal existence time
T_{N}^{*}\in(0,T_{0}]
of
v^{N}
on
[0,T_{0}]
such that
v^{N}\in C([0,T_{N}^{*}];\mathcal{C}^{s_{0}}(\mathcal{M}))\cap C((T_{1},T^{*}_{N}];\mathcal{C}^{2\varepsilon}(\mathcal{M}))
and

\displaystyle T^{*}_{N}=T_{0}\qquad\text{or}\qquad\sup_{0\leq t<T^{*}_{N}}\|v^{N}(t)\|_{L^{p}}=+\infty.

Since,
\Xi^{N}
is a smooth function (in space) it is easy to see that
v^{N}\in C([0,T_{N}^{*}];\mathcal{C}^{s_{0}}(\mathcal{M}))\cap C_{t}^{0}C^{2}_{x}((T_{1},T_{N}^{*})\times\mathcal{M})
. Furthemore,
v^{N}
satisfies the integral equation

v^{N}(t)=S(t)v_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}S(t-t^{\prime})F(v^{N},\Xi^{N})dt^{\prime}

for all
t\in(T_{1},T_{N}^{*})
and where
F
is as in (
4.3
). The last formula shows that
v^{N}\in C_{t}^{1}C^{2}_{x}((T_{1},T_{N}^{*})\times\mathcal{M})
since
S
is smoothing in time and
S(t-t^{\prime})F(v^{N},\Xi^{N})\in C_{t,t^{\prime}}^{0}C^{2}_{x}((T_{1},T_{N}^{*})^{2}\times\mathcal{M})
.

Hence, Lemmas
4.5
and
4.6
rule out the possibility of blowup for
v^{N}
on
[0,T^{*}_{N}]
and yield

(4.55)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|v^{N}(t)\|_{L^{p}}&\leq\|v^{N}(T_{1})\|_{L^{p}}+C\big((t-T_{1})+\|\Xi^{N}\|_{\mathcal{Z}_{T_{0}}^{\varepsilon,q}}^{q}\big)\\
&\leq C_{1}\|v_{0}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{s_{0}}}+C\big((t-T_{1})+\|\Xi\|_{\mathcal{Z}_{T_{0}}^{\varepsilon,q}}^{q}\big)=:B_{0}\end{split}

for all
t\in(T_{1},T_{N}^{*})
, where we used (
4.54
) and Corollary
2.6
. Here,
C
is the constant provided by Lemma
4.6
and
C_{1}>0
is an absolute constant which is large enough.

Thus, (
4.53
) follows from (
4.55
), the convergence

\Xi^{N}\longrightarrow\Xi,\quad\text{in $\mathcal{Z}^{\varepsilon,q}([0,T_{0}])$}

and the regularity of the solution map (
4.20
).
∎

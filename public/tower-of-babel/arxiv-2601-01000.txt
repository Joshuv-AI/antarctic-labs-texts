Hemi-Nelson algebras
Noemí Lubomirsky, Paula Menchón and Hernán Javier San Martín

Abstract
The aim of this paper is to generalize the link between Heyting algebras and Nelson algebras, established independently by Fidel and Vakarelov at the end of the 1970s, in the framework of bounded distributive hemi-implicative lattices. For this purpose, we introduce the variety of hemi-Nelson algebras. Moreover, we characterize the lattice of congruences of a hemi-Nelson algebra in terms of certain implicative filters. We also establish an equivalence between the algebraic category of bounded distributive hemi-implicative lattices and the one of centered hemi-Nelson algebras.

Nelson’s constructive logic with strong negation, which was introduced and studied in

is a non-classical logic that combines the constructive approach of positive intuitionistic logic with a classical (i.e. De Morgan) negation.
The algebraic models of this logic are called Nelson algebras. The class of Nelson algebras,
which is a variety, has been studied since at least the late 1950’s (firstly by Rasiowa; see
[
18

and
references therein). In the the end of the 1970’s it was proved, independently by Fidel and Vakarelov,
that every Nelson algebra can be represented as a special binary product (here called a twist structure)
of a Heyting algebra.

The main goal of the present manuscript is to extend the twist construction in the framework of
bounded distributive hemi-implicative lattices, thus obtaining a new variety, whose members will be called
hemi-Nelson algebras. More precisely, we show that every hemi-Nelson algebra
can be represented as a twist structure of a bounded distributive hemi-implicative lattice.

Hemi-Nelson algebras are an extension of Kleene algebras, together with a hemi-implication. These structures serve as an algebraic framework for non-classical logics, specifically those that seek to balance the properties of constructive logic with the involutive negation found in Kleene’s systems. In this context, the logic of Kleene algebras was thoroughly studied and characterized in
[
13
]
, where a formalization of its properties is provided.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we recall the definition of Nelson algebra and
sketch the main constructions linking Heyting algebras with Nelson algebras. Moreover,
we recall the definition of bounded distributive hemi-implicative lattice and we give some of its properties.
In Section

we introduce hemi-Nelson algebras
and show that every hemi-Nelson algebra can be represented as a twist structure over a

In Section

In Section

of bounded distributive hemi-implicative lattices and the algebraic category of centered
hemi-Nelson algebras, where a centered hemi-Nelson algebra is defined as a hemi-Nelson algebra
endowed with a center, i.e., a fixed element with respect to the involution
(this element is necessarily unique).
Finally, in Section

in order to investigate certain aspects of semi-Nelson algebras and subresiduated Nelson algebras respectively.

a\wedge(a\rightarrow c)\leq a\wedge c\leq a\wedge(b\vee c)

x\wedge(x\rightarrow y)\leq x\wedge(\sim x\vee y),

A direct computation shows that

a\wedge d\wedge(a\rightarrow c)=0

Straightforward computations

Lemma 13
.

Let
T
be a Kleene algebra. Then,

1=x\vee(z\wedge\sim z)\text{ if and only if }x=1.

Proof.

Suppose that
1=x\vee(z\wedge\sim z)
. It follows that
1\leq x\vee\sim z
, and by De Morgan’s laws we obtain

\sim x\wedge z=0
.
Since
z\wedge\sim z\leq x\vee\sim x
, we have
x\vee(z\wedge\sim z)\leq x\vee\sim x
, which implies
x\vee\sim x=1
.
Therefore,

z=z\wedge(x\vee\sim x)=(z\wedge x)\vee(z\wedge\sim x)=(z\wedge x)\vee 0=z\wedge x.

Hence
z\leq x
, and consequently
1=x\vee z=x
.
The converse implication is immediate and the result follows.
∎

Moreover, an algebra
\langle A,\wedge,\vee,\rightarrow,0,1\rangle
is a subresiduated lattice if and only if

\langle A,\wedge,\vee,0,1\rangle
is a bounded distributive lattice and the
following conditions are satisfied for every
a,b,c\in A
:

1)

(a\vee b)\rightarrow c=(a\rightarrow c)\wedge(b\rightarrow c)
,

2)

c\rightarrow(a\wedge b)=(c\rightarrow a)\wedge(c\rightarrow b)
,

3)

(a\rightarrow b)\wedge(b\rightarrow c)\leq a\rightarrow c
,

4)

a\rightarrow a=1
,

5)

a\wedge(a\rightarrow b)\leq b
,

6)

a\rightarrow b\leq c\rightarrow(a\rightarrow b)
.

Remark 53
.

Every open implicative filter of a subresiduated Nelson algebra is an upset.
Indeed, let
T\in\mathsf{SNA}
and let
F
be an open implicative filter of
T
.
Let

be such that
x\leq y
and

. Then, it is easy to show that

Proposition 54
.

Let
T\in\mathsf{SNA}
. Then the set of h-implicative filters of
T
coincides
with the set of open implicative filters of
T
.

Proof.

Let
T\in\mathsf{SNA}
. It follows from Proposition
28

that every h-implicative filter of
T

is an open implicative filter of
T
. The fact that every open implicative
filter of
T
satisfies F1) and F4) follows
from a direct computation based on Lemma
52
.

Let
F
be an open implicative filter of
T
. To prove that it satisfies F2) suppose that
z\in F
. Then,
x\rightarrow(x\wedge z)=(x\rightarrow x)\wedge(x\rightarrow z)=x\rightarrow z
. Since
x\leq 1
, we obtain
1\rightarrow z\leq x\rightarrow z
and from the fact that
1\rightarrow z\in F
, we obtain
x\rightarrow z\in F
. From Lemma
52
,
(x\rightarrow(x\wedge z))\rightarrow(((x\wedge z)\rightarrow(y\wedge z))\rightarrow(x\rightarrow y))\in F

((x\wedge z)\rightarrow(y\wedge z))\rightarrow(x\rightarrow y)\in F
.
To prove that
F
satisfies F3), suppose that

. A direct computation shows that
((x\wedge\sim y)\rightarrow(x\wedge\sim y\wedge z))\rightarrow((x\wedge\sim y)\rightarrow\sim((x\wedge z)\rightarrow y))=1

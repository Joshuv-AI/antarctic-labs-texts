Real-world Data of Antithrombotic Therapy in Neuroendovascular Therapy: Analysis of JR-NET 4
Authors: Nozomi SASAKI, Yukiko ENOMOTO, Hiroshi YAMAGAMI, Koji IIHARA, Akira ISHII, Hirotoshi IMAMURA, Nobuyuki SAKAI, Chiaki SAKAI, Tetsu SATOW, Yuji MATSUMARU, Shinichi YOSHIMURA
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
As the number of neuroendovascular therapies in Japan increases, the current trends in periprocedural antithrombotic therapy must be understood. We retrospectively analyzed data on periprocedural antithrombotic therapy in the Japanese Registry of Neuroendovascular Therapy (JR-NET) 4, a nationwide survey carried out in Japan between January 2015 and December 2019. Details on antithrombotic therapy in neuroendovascular therapy for ruptured cerebral aneurysms, unruptured cerebral aneurysms, and percutaneous transluminal angioplasty or stenting were collected from the JR-NET 4 database. These data were analyzed and compared with those from the JR-NET 2 (January 2008 to December 2009) and JR-NET 3 (January 2010 to December 2014). A total of 36,560 cases were analyzed in the JR-NET 4. The frequency of preprocedural dual antiplatelet therapy (DAPT) significantly increased from the JR-NET 2 to 4 (48.1%, 53.4%, and 62.3%, respectively; P < 0.001), whereas the frequency of monotherapy significantly decreased (15.7%, 13.9%, and 8%, respectively; P < 0.001). Postprocedural antiplatelet therapy exhibited similar trends, and postprocedural anticoagulant therapy was discontinued. Particularly, heparin use significantly decreased from the JR-NET 2 to 4 (23.4% vs. 12.7% vs. 7.9%, respectively; P < 0.001). In terms of periprocedural complications, the incidence of ischemic complications increased from the JR-NET 3 to 4 (5.8% vs. 6.2%; P = 0.05). In the JR-NET 4, severe adverse events and hemorrhagic and all complications were significantly more frequent in the preprocedural triple or more therapy group. The rate of postprocedural anticoagulant therapy decreased, whereas that of antiplatelet therapy increased. Overall, in Japan, periprocedural DAPT has become increasingly common.

Introduction

Recently, the number of neuroendovascular therapies carried out in Japan has gradually increased. As the role of neuroendovascular therapy increases, current trends in periprocedural antithrombotic therapy must be understood. The aim of antithrombotic therapy is to reduce the incidence of ischemic complications without increasing the incidence of hemorrhagic complications. Nonetheless, an optimal regimen of antithrombotic therapy has not yet been established because of the widespread variations in devices, treatment options, and antithrombotic protocols between institutions.

In the Japanese Registry of Neuroendovascular Therapy (JR-NET) 3, a nationwide survey of neuroendovascular therapy in Japan carried out between January 2010 and December 2014, we reported that the significance of periprocedural antithrombotic therapy increased in neuroendovascular treatment for cerebral aneurysms and atherosclerotic large vessel stenosis.1) After the JR-NET 3 era, numerous innovative devices including flow diverters, flow disrupters, and coiling-assisted devices, were introduced in Japan. The association between the value of platelet function tests conducted prior to the procedure, periprocedural bleeding, and ischemic complications has thus been recognized,2) and platelet aggregation tests are occasionally employed. To elucidate current trends in neurovascular periprocedural antithrombotic therapy in Japan and the relationship with periprocedural complications, we retrospectively analyze the data on periprocedural antithrombotic therapy in the JR-NET 4, a nationwide survey carried out between January 2015 and December 2019, and compare the results with data from the JR-NET 2 and 3.

Materials and Methods

Study design

The JR-NET 4 is a nationwide survey of neuroendovascular therapies conducted in Japan. Using the JR-NET 4, we collected data on antithrombotic therapy in the same categories as in previous studies, including the JR-NET 2 (January 2008-December 2009) and JR-NET 3 (January 2010-December 2014). Categories included aneurysm coiling (ruptured cases, n = 8,070; unruptured cases, n = 14,300), parent artery occlusion for dissecting aneurysm or others (ruptured cases, n = 1,013; unruptured cases, n = 395), and percutaneous transluminal angioplasty or stenting (PTA/S) for cervical carotid arteries (n = 9,986) or other extracranial (n = 1,438) or intracranial (n = 1,358) arteries. Patients with insufficient data were excluded (lack of detailed information on antithrombotic agents, n = 286; classification failure, n = 50), and 36,560 patients were finally analyzed. We analyzed the number and detailed information of antiplatelets and anticoagulants utilized, platelet function testing performed, and periprocedural complications and compared the data between the JR-NET 2, 3, and 4. The JR-NET 1 (January 2005-December 2007) was not included in the comparative analysis because it lacked data on postprocedural antithrombotic therapy and periprocedural complications. Ischemic and hemorrhagic complications were defined as procedure-related and intracranial complications that occurred within 24 hours after each procedure. Severe adverse events were defined as death, or severe disability with a deterioration of two or more points on the modified Rankin Scale 30 days after the procedure. Moreover, we evaluated the incidence of these complications among the three groups of patients who received monotherapy, dual therapy, and triple or more therapy, and by with or without platelet function monitoring, by limiting the population to the patients registered in the JR-NET 4. The study protocol was approved by the institutional review boards.

Statistical analyses

Using Chi-squared tests, statistical comparisons of postprocedural anticoagulant therapy and preprocedural and postprocedural antiplatelet therapies were conducted between the JR-NET 2, 3, and 4 groups. Moreover, postprocedural anticoagulant therapy was divided into heparin, argatroban, and ozagrel treatment groups; preprocedural and postprocedural antiplatelet therapies were divided into monotherapy, dual therapy, and triple or more therapy groups; and Chi-squared tests were conducted. Furthermore, for significant differences between the three groups, pairwise comparisons were conducted. Multiple comparisons were not performed based on Fisher's least significant difference method. To compare the relationship between preprocedural antiplatelet therapy and periprocedural complications in the JR-NET 4, a similar analysis was carried out. All statistical analyses were conducted using the R statistical package (version 4.2.1; R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria).

Results

Details of periprocedural antithrombotic therapy for each disease

For ruptured cerebral aneurysms, the preprocedural antiplatelet therapy rate gradually increased to 18.2% in the JR-NET 4 group (Supplementary Table 1). Using aspirin and clopidogrel, the most common regimen was changed from aspirin monotherapy to dual antiplatelet therapy (DAPT). Postprocedural antiplatelet therapy was administered to 58.9% of patients, and the rate of postprocedural DAPT with aspirin and clopidogrel increased by 10.1% in the JR-NET 4. Moreover, the rate of cilostazol monotherapy significantly increased to 21.2% in the JR-NET 4. The most common regimen was changed from aspirin to cilostazol monotherapy, and in terms of postprocedural anticoagulant therapy, the use of both heparin and argatroban decreased. Regarding treatment strategy, double catheter or stent assisted techniques significantly increased in the JR-NET 4, whereas simple technique decreased.

For unruptured cerebral aneurysms, preprocedural antiplatelet therapy increased in each era (Supplementary Table 2). Preprocedural monotherapy decreased, and DAPT increased (46.6% in the JR-NET 2 and 78% in the JR-NET 4); these tendencies were similar to those observed in terms of postprocedural antiplatelet therapy. The most common treatment regimen was DAPT with aspirin and clopidogrel. The use of postprocedural anticoagulant therapy gradually decreased to 44.9% in the JR-NET 4. In 48.8% of patients, platelet function testing was carried out. Interventions for unruptured aneurysms larger than 10 mm increased, and stent assisted technique increased in the JR-NET 4.

In terms of PTA/S, a high rate of preprocedural and postprocedural antiplatelet therapies was maintained across the JR-NET 2 to 4 (Supplementary Table 3). DAPT with aspirin and clopidogrel remained the most common regimen, and its use increased significantly (36.6% in the JR-NET 2 to 66% in the JR-NET 4); this tendency was similar to that of postprocedural antiplatelet therapy. Accordingly, the use of DAPT with aspirin and cilostazol decreased significantly (20.1% in the JR-NET 2 to 6.8% in the JR-NET 4). As observed in the case of aneurysms, the use of postprocedural anticoagulant therapy decreased. Platelet function testing was performed in 32.3% of patients.

In each disease, age increased from the JR-NET 3 to 4. Prasugrel was rarely employed as it has not been approved for cerebrovascular disease.

Changes in periprocedural antithrombotic therapy paradigms between the JR-NET 2, 3, and 4

In the analysis of all cases-including aneurysms (ruptured/unruptured) and PTA/S-DAPT significantly increased (P < 0.001), and monotherapy significantly decreased (P < 0.001) (Table 1 and Supplementary Figure 1). Preprocedural triple or more therapy increased from the JR-NET 2 to 3 but decreased from the JR-NET 3 to 4 (P < 0.001); the results were similar for postprocedural antiplatelet therapy (Table 1 and Supplementary Figure 2).

Changes in periprocedural antithrombotic therapy paradigms between JR-NET 2, 3, and 4

In terms of postprocedural anticoagulant therapy, heparin use significantly decreased from the JR-NET 2 to 4 (23.4% vs. 12.7% vs. 7.9%, respectively; P < 0.001) (Table 1 and Supplementary Figure 3). Argatroban use was comparable between the JR-NET 2 and 3 but significantly decreased between the JR-NET 3 and 4 (P < 0.001). Ozagrel use increased from the JR-NET 2 to 3 (P < 0.001) and was comparable between the JR-NET 3 and 4.

Regarding periprocedural complications, a significant increase in ischemic complications (ischemic complication; 2% vs. 5.8%, P < 0.001) and a significant decrease in hemorrhagic complications (hemorrhagic complications; 5.3% vs. 2.1%, P < 0.001) were observed between the JR-NET 2 to 3 (Fig. 1). In terms of changes from the JR-NET 3 to 4, no significant differences were observed regarding hemorrhagic complications, groin site complications, severe adverse events, or all complications.

Discussion

The role of antithrombotics in neuroendovascular therapy is to reduce ischemic complications. Thrombus formation associated with catheter treatment is mainly caused by “red thrombi” (made of red blood cells and fibrin produced due to activation of the coagulation system caused by flow stagnation) and “white thrombi” (made of platelets formed by intimal injury of the arterial vessel or stent implantation).3) Anticoagulant therapy is used for red thrombi, whereas antiplatelet therapy is employed for white thrombi. Reportedly preprocedural antiplatelet therapy reduces ischemic complications in coil embolization for unruptured aneurysms,4) and the consensus document recommends periprocedural DAPT for carotid artery stenting.5) Recently, the use of antiplatelet agents has become important in neuroendovascular therapy; in fact, we previously reported that antiplatelet therapy surpassed anticoagulant therapy in importance in the JR-NET 3.1) A similar trend was observed in the JR-NET 4; although the use of postprocedural anticoagulant therapy decreased, the proportion of DAPT increased among antiplatelet therapies.

One reason for the increase in the rate of periprocedural DAPT is the increase in-stent assisted coil embolization for aneurysms. As endovascular treatment is being actively carried out for wide-necked and more difficult aneurysms, the use of adjunctive techniques continues to increase.6) In this study, stent assisted coil embolization for unruptured aneurysms was performed in approximately 30% of cases, an increase of approximately 20% from the JR-NET 3. Moreover, the use of flow diverters was approved in 2015 in Japan, and their widespread use is one of the reasons for the strengthening role of antiplatelet therapy; periprocedural DAPT is required in this scenario, as flow diverters have a large metal mesh and potential risk of in-stent thrombosis.7) In the JR-NET 4, approximately 10% of unruptured aneurysms were treated using a flow diverter. Flow diverter treatment is now being conducted for both large and giant aneurysms, as well as medium-sized aneurysms,8) and in recent years, the use of this treatment has further increased; hence, the increase in periprocedural DAPT is likely to continue in the future.

Carotid artery stenting was first performed in Japan in 2007. The number of cases has since increased and has recently exceeded the number of carotid endarterectomies.9) Despite the increase in the number of cases, the rate of DAPT remains high at over 80%, both preprocedurally and postprocedurally. This indicates that DAPT for carotid artery stenting has reached a certain level of consensus.

In terms of antiplatelet regimens, DAPT with aspirin and clopidogrel has increased stepwise. As the use of aspirin and clopidogrel for coronary artery disease has exhibited a reduction in ischemic events,10) these two antiplatelet agents are often employed in the field of neurosurgery. Nonetheless, clopidogrel-a second-generation thienopyridine with a P2Y12 receptor inhibitor-is a poor metabolizer in individuals with hepatic cytochrome p-450 (CYP) 2C19 genetic polymorphisms,11) which are believed to be more common in the Asian population (especially in Japanese individuals) at approximately 20%.12) An important difficulty with clopidogrel is that there are differences in responsiveness depending on the individual, which include cases of hyporesponse or delayed hyperresponse, even if treatment is initially effective.13) Recently, platelet aggregation tests have been employed to evaluate platelet reactivity to antiplatelet agents;14) in the JR-NET 4, approximately half of the unruptured aneurysms underwent this test, making it possible to identify the responsiveness of individual patients to antiplatelet agents and providing a basis for decisions such as changing or adding agents.15) In this study, monitoring of periprocedural antiplatelet therapy significantly reduced severe adverse events but resulted in no difference in ischemic, hemorrhagic, or all complications. To determine whether platelet aggregation tests contribute to reducing complications, further study is warranted.

With the advent of flow diverters and other new devices, strict management of antiplatelet therapy is necessary. As the frequency of platelet aggregation tests increases, the selection of antiplatelet agents may become more tailored in the future while either maintaining or increasing the use of DAPT. Furthermore, age increased from the JR-NET 3 to 4. Comorbidities, including atrial fibrillation, and complications increase with advancing age.16) Tailor-made management using a platelet aggregation test for high-risk patients of neuroendovascular therapy may be necessary to consider.

There are no reports of strong evidence for the effectiveness of postprocedural anticoagulant therapy for neuroendovascular therapy for ischemic complication prevention. In coronary interventions, a randomized trial comparing DAPT versus aspirin and heparin has reported that DAPT is associated with fewer ischemic and hemorrhagic complications than aspirin and heparin.17) Consequently, postprocedural antithrombotic therapy has focused on antiplatelet therapy rather than anticoagulant therapy. Hence, the use of postprocedural heparin decreased in each era examined in this study. In comparison with JR-NET 3, in the JR-NET 4, not only postprocedural heparin but also argatroban use, which has fewer hemorrhagic complications,18) decreased.

Although the rate of hemorrhagic complications was approximately 2% in the JR-NET 4, ischemic complications exceeded 6% and were the only complications that increased significantly when compared with the JR-NET 3. Despite aggressive antiplatelet therapy, the incidence of ischemic complications increased. Possible reasons for this tendency include the increase in the proportion of unruptured cerebral aneurysms (Supplementary Table 2); interventions for more difficult aneurysms, such as those larger than 10 mm; the use of novel devices with a higher risk of ischemic complications, including flow diverters; the increase in the use of adjunctive techniques, such as stent assisted technique, but the decrease in simple techniques for unruptured aneurysm; the presence of poor metabolizers for P2Y12-receptor inhibitors; aging of study population; and the increase in board-certified surgeons of Japanese Society of Neuroendovascular therapy. The increase in the number of surgeons may have led to a decrease in the technical skills in providing neuroendovascular therapy. However, despite the increase in ischemic complications, the incidence of severe adverse events has remained low. This suggests that despite the presence of complications, only minor strokes are likely to occur. Therefore, an important issue for future neuroendovascular therapy is the reduction of ischemic complications, which include minor strokes. Moreover, in the JR-NET 4, the preprocedural triple or more therapy group had significantly more complications when compared with other groups. This is suggested to be because the triple or more therapy was administered to high-risk patients. Another important issue is to reduce complications in high-risk patients as much as possible through the use of appropriate antithrombotic therapy and patient-specific management.

Limitations

This study had several limitations. First, although we focused on periprocedural antithrombotic therapy, the duration of postprocedural antiplatelet therapy is also important. Although an increase in DAPT was observed, another interesting question is when to reduce single antiplatelet therapy or stop antiplatelet therapy. Second, antiplatelet agents are routinely administered to aneurysm patients undergoing neuroendovascular treatment to prevent perioperative ischemic complications, but their use for that purpose is not officially approved in Japan. Moreover, although the number of ruptured aneurysms, unruptured aneurysms, and PTA/S increased from the JR-NET 2 to 4, the ratios differed among generations. Furthermore, in cases of unruptured aneurysms and PTA/S-which are preventive treatments-the indications for intervention vary at each facility, and differences in intervention decisions may affect the complication rate. Additionally, cerebral aneurysms and atherosclerotic large vessel stenosis were analyzed and discussed together here, but are different diseases, which limits the generalizability of the current findings. Depending on the disease, patient backgrounds may differ. Particularly, the number of patients receiving antiplatelet therapy for ruptured aneurysms was small.

Conclusions

In this study, we revealed that the use of postprocedural anticoagulant therapy decreased, whereas the use of preprocedural and postprocedural antiplatelet therapies increased, which is similar to observations from the JT-NET 2 to 3. The increase in DAPT has been dramatic in this decade and now becoming a routine practice. To determine the optimal regimen of periprocedural antiplatelet therapy, future studies are needed.

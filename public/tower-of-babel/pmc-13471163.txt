Correction: Associations of cumulative exposure and dynamic trajectories of the triglyceride-total cholesterol-body weight index with new-onset cardiometabolic multimorbidity in middle-aged and older Chinese adults: evidence from a nationwide prospective cohort study
Authors: Xiao Chen, Chaochao Wang, Qi Huang, Yuan Luo, Zhe Wu, Jing Chen, Haibo Gong
Published: 2026

Correction to: Cardiovascular Diabetology (2026) 25:146 10.1186/s12933-026-03155-7

In the original publication of this article [1], the authors identified errors in the numerical values reported for the Net Reclassification Improvement (NRI) and Integrated Discrimination Improvement (IDI) analyses in Table 4 and in the corresponding text in the Abstract and Results section.

Net Reclassification Improvement (NRI) and Integrated Discrimination Improvement (IDI) for CumTCBI and its individual components in predicting new-onset CMM

The errors resulted from an inadvertent calculation error in the code used to generate and match the NRI/IDI estimates and 95% confidence intervals. The corrected values are provided below.

This correction affects only Table 4 and the corresponding NRI/IDI descriptions. All P-values for the corrected NRI and IDI estimates remain < 0.001. All other statistical models, tables, figures, supplementary analyses, the survival prediction model, and the main conclusions of the article remain unchanged.

Correction in the Abstract

In the Results subsection of the Abstract, the sentence, "NRI and IDI analyses confirmed that TCBI provided incremental predictive value beyond its individual components (NRI = 0.158, P < 0.001)." should read, "NRI and IDI analyses confirmed that CumTCBI provided incremental predictive value beyond its individual components (NRI = 0.165, 95% CI 0.116–0.218, P < 0.001; IDI = 0.085, 95% CI 0.042–0.147, P < 0.001)."

Correction in the Results section

In the subsection "Incremental predictive value of TCBI", the relevant paragraph should read:

To assess whether CumTCBI provides incremental predictive value beyond its individual components, we performed NRI and IDI analyses. As presented in the corrected Table 4, CumTCBI demonstrated significant improvements in risk reclassification and discrimination compared with each of its individual components. Specifically, the NRI for composite CumTCBI was 0.165 (95% CI 0.116 to 0.218, P < 0.001), which was higher than the NRI values observed for its separate components: CumTC (NRI = 0.103, 95% CI 0.052 to 0.150, P < 0.001), CumTG (NRI = 0.098, 95% CI 0.056 to 0.151, P < 0.001), and CumWeight (NRI = 0.101, 95% CI 0.045 to 0.149, P < 0.001). Similarly, the corrected IDI for CumTCBI was 0.085 (95% CI 0.042 to 0.147, P < 0.001), indicating significant improvement in discrimination capacity. These findings confirm that the integration of triglycerides, total cholesterol, and body weight into a single composite index provides synergistic predictive value beyond any individual component, supporting the clinical utility of TCBI for CMM risk prediction.

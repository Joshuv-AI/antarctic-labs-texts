Draft genome sequence of Priestia megaterium MHES4, a biofertilizer candidate isolated from tomato rhizosphere in Bangladesh
Authors: Md. Nazmul Hannan, Sabbir Khan, Naim Siddique, Md. Nayeem Hossain, Rifat Islam, Kamrun Nahar, G. K. M. Mustafizur Rahman, Habibul Bari Shozib, Abul Hossain Molla, M. Nazmul Hoque, Md. Manjurul Haque
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveThe genus Priestia has recently gained attention for its plant growth-promoting potential. To examine the genomic traits and biosafety profile for potential field application as a native, climate-smart bioinoculant, we sequenced, assembled and annotated the genome of Priestia megaterium strain MHES4, isolated from the rhizosphere of tomato plant grown in drought-prone ecosystem of Rajshahi, Bangladesh.Data descriptionGenome assembly data from the shotgun whole genome sequencing (WGS) of the P. megaterium MHES4 revealed 60 contigs with a total length of 5,267,048 bp, an N50 of 446,003 bp and 37.9% G + C content. The mean sequencing depth was 127.58×, with 100% breadth of coverage. Genome completeness assessed was 97.43% with 3.5% contamination, confirming high assembly quality. In total, 5,484 protein-coding genes were annotated. Additionally, 5,445 protein-coding sequences, 28 tRNAs, and 5 rRNAs were identified. Functional analysis identified gene clusters involved in the synthesis of secondary metabolites, such as phytoene synthase and alpha-amylase, and a Type I CRISPR-Cas system. Biosafety assessment using in silico tools detected no virulence factors or transmissible antibiotic resistance genes, indicating its potential safe use in agriculture. Overall, this genomic resource provides valuable insights into the genetic potential of P. megaterium MHES4 for nutrient cycling and adaptation to the rhizosphere environment.

Objective

The global shift toward sustainable agriculture has accelerated efforts to identify efficient biological alternatives to chemical fertilizers that improve crop productivity while maintaining long-term soil health and ecological balance [1–3]. The genus Priestia, recently reclassified from Bacillus [4], includes species well-documented for their plant growth-promoting traits and it also has potential for being used as a biocontrol agent [5–7]. P. megaterium, in particular, is known to solubilize phosphates and produce phytohormones [8]. While the agricultural utility of this species is established, there is a lack of genomic data regarding native strains from Bangladeshi soils. To address this gap, we sequenced the genome of strain MHES4, isolated from rhizosphere of tomato plant grown in drought-prone ecosystem of Rajshahi, Bangladesh, to examine its genomic traits and biosafety profile for potential field application.

Data description

P. megaterium strain MHES4 was isolated from rhizosphere of tomato plant (Solanum lycopersicum L.) grown in drought-prone ecosystem in Rajshahi (24.37° N, 88.60° E), Bangladesh. The rhizosphere sample was serially diluted and plated on Yeast-extract Peptone (YP) agar (Oxoid, UK) and incubated at 28 ℃ for 24 h. A pure culture was established through repeated sub-culturing and stored at -80 °C in glycerol stock for further use. The high-quality genomic DNA was obtained from nutrient broth cultures using the QIAamp DNA Mini Kit (QIAGEN, Germany) in accordance with the manufacturer’s instructions [9, 10]. DNA concentration and purity were assessed using a NanoDrop 2000 Spectrophotometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA) [11]. Whole-genome sequencing libraries were constructed from 1 ng of DNA using the Nextera DNA Flex Kit (Illumina, USA). The libraries were sequenced on an Illumina MiSeq platform, employing a 2 × 250 bp paired-end read configuration, yielding 4,983,150 raw reads (730.2 million raw bases) (Table 1, Data file 1) [12]. Raw sequencing data were first subjected to quality control using FastQC v0.11.7 [13], and low-quality fragments and adapters were trimmed using Trimmomatic v0.39 [14]. Filtered reads (n = 4,882,206) were de novo assembled using SPAdes v3.15.32 assembler [15], and the quality of the resulting contigs was evaluated with QUAST v5.0.2 [16]. Mean sequencing depth was calculated by mapping the trimmed paired-end reads back to the assembly using BWA-MEM v0.7.19 [17], followed by depth analysis with SAMtools v1.13 [18]. Genome completeness was estimated through CheckM v1.2.3 [19]. Gene annotation was performed through the NCBI Prokaryotic Genome Annotation Pipeline v6.6 [20] and RAST v2.0 server [21]. Metabolic gene clusters were identified using antiSMASH 6.0 [22]. Biosafety assessments were performed using PathogenFinder v1.1 [23], the Virulence Factor Database v6.0 [24], and the Comprehensive Antibiotic Resistance Database [25]. CRISPR-Cas systems were identified using CRISPRCasFinder v4.2.20 [26]. The draft genome of P. megaterium MHES4 consists of 60 contigs with a total length of 5,267,048 bp and an N50 value of 446,003 bp, and an L50 of 4 (Table 1, Data file 2) [27]. The genomic guanine and cytosine (G + C) base content was 37.9%. The assembly reached a mean depth of 127.58× with 100% breadth of coverage. CheckM placed genome completeness at 97.43%, 86th percentile among Priestia megaterium RefSeq assemblies and contamination at 3.5%. The 60-contig fragmentation is less a quality issue than a platform one. Per contig coverage identified several short contigs (520–1,351 bp) carrying mean depths of 2,155–2,417×, around 17–19-fold above the genome average a signature of collapsed rRNA operons, IS-like elements, and tandem repeats. These are known blind spots for short-read sequencing; 151 bp paired-end reads cannot span them, regardless of depth. The annotation predicted 5,445 CDS, along with 28 tRNAs and 5 rRNAs (Table 1, Data file 3) [28]. One Type I CRISPR-Cas system was identified on contig 1, suggesting that the genome encodes a functional adaptive immune mechanism that provides protection against invading foreign genetic elements such as bacteriophages and plasmids. Consistent with this prediction, no virulence factors or transmissible antibiotic resistance genes were detected, supporting the biosafety and suitability of strain MHES4 for agricultural applications. Subsystem analysis (Table 1, Data file 4) [29] revealed that the largest functional categories were “amino acids and derivatives” and “carbohydrates.” Metabolic functional analysis using antiSMASH revealed gene clusters encoding phytoene synthase, a key enzyme in carotenoid biosynthesis, α-amylase involved in starch hydrolysis, and LuxR family transcriptional regulators associated with quorum sensing and root colonization. In conclusion, these genomic features underscore the genetic potential of P. megaterium MHES4 for nutrient cycling and environmental adaptation to the rhizosphere environment.

Limitations

This Data Note reports whole-genome sequencing of P. megaterium MHES4 on the Illumina MiSeq platform. The draft assembly spans 60 contigs at a mean depth of 127.58×. That fragmentation reflects the platform’s limits rather than the genome itself rRNA operons, insertion sequence-like elements, and tandem repeats that short reads simply cannot span. Collapsed repeat contigs carry noticeably elevated per-contig coverage, consistent with this picture. Oxford Nanopore long-read data would resolve these regions, bringing continuity and more reliable downstream comparisons within reach.

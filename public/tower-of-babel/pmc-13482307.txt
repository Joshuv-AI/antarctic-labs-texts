Prognostic evaluation of retinal reattachment surgery: predictive efficacy analysis of microperfusion indices via contrast-enhanced ultrasound
Authors: Wansi Hong, Fuqiang Yan, Meiying Lin, Wanjiao Liang, Shiwei Li, Shuilong Lin
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractWe aimed to examine how preoperative contrast-enhanced ultrasound (CEUS) parameters relate to postoperative visual recovery in retinal detachment (RD) patients and to establish new objective markers for clinical prognosis. This single-center prospective observational study enrolled 52 RD patients undergoing pars plana vitrectomy (PPV). Preoperative CEUS parameters (arrival time [AT], time to peak [TTP], peak intensity [PI], time-intensity curve-area under the curve [TIC-AUC], ascending slope [AS]) were collected. Between-group comparisons were performed using parametric or non-parametric tests as appropriate. Ocular covariates including RD duration, macular status, and tamponade type were adjusted in prespecified binary logistic regression models. (Patients were stratified into improvement (n = 39) and non-improvement (n = 13) groups based on 6-month best-corrected visual acuity. Model stability was validated using bootstrap resampling, and post-hoc power analysis was performed to confirm statistical reliability. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves were generated to evaluate predictive performance. The improvement group exhibited significantly higher PI and TIC-AUC, and lower AT (all P < 0.05). TTP showed a borderline significant trend (P = 0.059), whereas AS showed no significant difference (P = 0.100). PI was the only independent CEUS predictor of visual improvement (Receiver operating characteristic-area under the curve [ROC-AUC] = 0.844, P < 0.001), accounting for 34.7% of the variance in visual improvement. Macular status was also an independent predictor. Bootstrap validation confirmed model robustness. Preoperative CEUS parameters, particularly PI, are associated with 6-month visual recovery after RD surgery. PI represents a preliminary candidate prognostic biomarker; caution is needed for clinical translation owing to the single-center design, small sample size, and absence of external validation. Multicenter studies are needed to validate these findings.Keywords: Retinal detachment, Contrast-enhanced ultrasound, Prognostic evaluation, Visual recovery, Microcirculatory perfusionSubject terms: Biomarkers, Diseases, Medical research

Introduction

RD, defined as pathological separation between the neurosensory retina and retinal pigment epithelium, is a vision-threatening condition. Rhegmatogenous RD (RRD) and tractional RD (TRD) require surgical intervention, primarily scleral buckling (SB) or pars plana vitrectomy (PPV). Meta-analyses1 indicate SB is suitable for uncomplicated RD, while PPV demonstrates superior efficacy in complicated RD (CRD) involving factors like high myopia, proliferative vitreoretinopathy, or prior ocular surgery. Although anatomical reattachment rates reach 91.1–98.9%, only 39–56% of patients achieve a BCVA better than 20/50 postoperatively2.Suboptimal visual recovery persists despite successful reattachment, with complications including metamorphopsia and visual field defects. Accurate postoperative visual prognosis is clinically essential but remains challenging.

Traditional prognostic indicators (e.g., preoperative BCVA, symptom duration) are subjective and lack objective quantification3,4. Optical coherence tomography (OCT) and OCT angiography (OCTA) have been explored for prognostic assessment5,6; however, their utility is limited in severe RD cases due to retinal structural disruption, media opacities, and patient cooperation requirements6.

CEUS quantifies tissue microcirculation parameters and is widely applied in assessing microvascular pathologies in diabetes7, coronary disease8, kidney disease9 and oncology10,11. While CEUS has diagnostic utility in ocular conditions like choroidal tumors12,13, its correlation with postoperative visual recovery after RD surgery remains inadequately investigated.

This study included 52 patients who underwent retinal detachment surgery. The aim was to explore the correlation between preoperative CEUS parameters and postoperative visual recovery, and to provide new objective indicators for clinical prognosis assessment.

Methods

This was a single-center prospective observational case series conducted at Jinjiang Municipal Hospital (Shanghai Sixth People’s Hospital Fujian) between July 2023 and January 2025. The study protocol was approved by the Medical Ethics Committee of the aforementioned hospital (jjsyy-2023-086) and implemented in compliance with the Declaration of Helsinki. Informed consent was obtained from each enrolled patient. Figure 1 presents the flowchart of the clinical research.

Clinical research flowchart.

Study participants

A total of 52 patients (52 eyes) with retinal detachment were enrolled. Inclusion criteria: (1) diagnosis confirmed by indirect ophthalmoscopy, three-mirror examination, and ultrasound; (2) first-time surgical treatment; (3) complete preoperative CEUS data. Exclusion criteria: (1) a history of severe ocular trauma or intraocular surgery; (2) concomitant serious fundus diseases including endophthalmitis, retinal vasculopathy, macular hole retinal detachment, and other severe retinal disorders; (3) severe systemic diseases (e.g., heart/kidney/liver failure, systemic infection); (4) allergy to sulfur hexafluoride microbubbles (SonoVue); (5) inability to tolerate surgery. Patients were excluded if surgery failed, postoperative complications occurred (e.g., recurrent detachment, epiretinal membrane, secondary glaucoma), or follow-up was incomplete.

Data collection

General information: Demographics (age, gender), body mass index (BMI), blood pressure(BP), fasting blood glucose(BG), blood lipids, history and duration of hypertension and diabetes, and disease control status.Systemic conditions associated with microangiopathy were systematically documented.

Ophthalmic examinations: Participants underwent a comprehensive ocular assessment, including visual acuity testing, fundus photography, and slit-lamp biomicroscopy. BCVA was measured using Snellen visual acuity ratios. LogMAR values were derived from Snellen ratios using the formula: LogMAR = -log₁₀ (Snellen fraction), where the Snellen fraction is expressed as a decimal. For participants with low vision (i.e., BCVA worse than Snellen 20/200), visual function was categorized using manual grading scales (incorporating logarithmic acuity and light perception levels) and subsequently converted to LogMAR values per the standards of the Early Treatment Diabetic Retinopathy Study (ETDRS). Ocular Parameters also included duration from onset of retinal detachment to surgery, macular status (macula-on vs. macula-off detachment), and type of intraocular tamponade (silicone oil vs. gas), all of which were documented as key prognostic covariates. CEUS: Performed using Resona R9 Digital Color Ultrasound Diagnostic System (Mindray, Shenzhen, China)with a 5.0–12.0 MHz linear probe. SonoVue (Bracco, Geneva, Switzerland) was administered via the brachial vein injection (2.4 mL, 8 µL/mL SF₆), followed by the injection of 5 mL of normal saline for flushing. The image recording duration was 3 min. Using the built-in angiography analysis software, obtain the time-intensity curves (TIC). To ensure standardization, a fixed circular region of interest (ROI) (3.0 mm in diameter) was placed at the most elevated segment of the detached retina, avoiding folds, cysts, and artifacts. The same ROI size and location were applied consistently across all patients. Intra- and inter-observer reproducibility was evaluated in 20 randomly selected eyes by two independent senior ophthalmologists. Intraclass correlation coefficients (ICC) were calculated for all CEUS parameters (AT, TTP, PI, TIC-AUC, AS). ICC > 0.75 was considered excellent agreement. All the ultrasound operations were carried out by the same senior attending physician, and the injections were administered in collaboration with the same professional nurse.

Surgery and follow-up

All patients underwent 23G pars plana vitrectomy under local anesthesia, including vitreous resection, retinal tear management, subretinal fluid drainage, laser photocoagulation, and tamponade (silicone oil or gas). Silicone oil removal combined with cataract surgery was performed 3 months later. Postoperative follow-up was scheduled at 6 months to record BCVA, fundus findings, and complications.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was conducted using SPSS 26.0 software. Post-hoc power analysis was performed using GPower 3.1 software for between-group comparisons of key CEUS parameters and for the multivariable regression model. For the measurement data, normality tests were first performed. Data that met the normal distribution were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (x̄ ± s), and comparisons between groups were conducted using t-tests or analysis of variance; data that did not meet the normal distribution were expressed as median (interquartile range) [M(Q1, Q3)], and comparisons between groups were conducted using non-parametric tests. Count data were expressed as the number of cases (percentage), and comparisons between groups were conducted using chi-square tests or Fisher’s exact tests. Based on clinical relevance and univariate screening, a pre-specified adjusted binary logistic regression model was constructed with visual improvement as the binary dependent variable. Independent variables included AT, TTP, PI, TIC-AUC, AS, plus key clinical confounders: RD duration, macular status, and tamponade type. Pearson or Spearman correlation analysis was used to evaluate the correlation between CEUS parameters and postoperative visual recovery; multiple regression analysis was used to screen independent predictors; Robust standard errors and bootstrap resampling (1,000 replicates) was used to examine the stability of the model and the robustness of regression coefficients; ROC curves were used to evaluate the efficacy of the prediction model. All statistical tests were two-tailed, and P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Patient characteristics and CEUS parameters by group

Patients were divided into two groups based on 6-month follow-up results: an improvement group (LogMAR reduction ≥ 0.2) (Group 1, n = 39) and a non-improvement group (LogMAR reduction < 0.2) (Group 2, n = 13). A LogMAR reduction ≥ 0.2 was defined as clinically significant visual improvement. This threshold was selected according to the test–retest variability of ETDRS visual acuity, as a change of 0.2 logMAR exceeds measurement error and represents a genuine functional change. Baseline characteristics are shown in Table 1. Groups were well balanced for age, gender, BMI, BP, BG, hypertension/diabetes history, duration, and control. The improvement group had more macula-on cases and shorter RD duration (both P < 0.05). Tamponade type was balanced.

Pre-specified multivariable regression and bootstrap validation

To identify independent predictors of 6-month visual improvement after retinal detachment and validate model robustness, a pre-specified multivariable logistic regression model was constructed, with 6-month visual improvement status (1 = improved, 0 = no significant improvement) as the dependent variable and two clinically relevant candidates—PI (a continuous metric of retinal blood perfusion) and macular status (a categorical variable: 1 = macula-on, 0 = macula-off)—as independent variables. A total of 52 eligible patients were included (39 with improved vision [75.0%], 13 without improvement [25.0%]), with no missing data for the candidate variables.

Multivariable binary logistic regression analysis was performed to evaluate the association between visual improvement at 6 months and potential predictors, including CEUS parameters and ocular clinical variables. (Table 3). Two variables emerged as significant independent predictors of visual improvement: PI and macular status. PI was significantly associated with visual improvement (β = 0.801, SE = 0.328, Wald χ² =5.969, p = 0.015), with an odds ratio (OR) of 2.227 (95% confidence interval [CI]: 1.172–4.234). This indicates that each unit increase in PI was associated with a more than twofold increase in the odds of visual improvement. Macular status also showed a strong and significant predictive effect (β = 3.404, SE = 1.491, Wald χ²=5.211, p = 0.022), with an OR of 30.072 (95% CI: 1.618–558.831), suggesting that a favorable macular status substantially increased the likelihood of visual improvement at 6 months. By contrast, arrival time (AT, p = 0.273) and area under the curve (TIC-AUC, p = 0.297) were not significantly associated with visual improvement in this model.

The significant asymptotic P value confirms marked differences in PI between eyes with and without visual improvement.

Bootstrap validation and model stability analysis

To assess the stability of regression coefficients and model performance across potential sampling variations, 1000 bootstrap resamples (with replacement, n = 52 per resample) were performed (Fig. 4). The distribution of bootstrap-estimated coefficients confirmed the robustness of both predictors (Fig. 4A, C). For PI, the mean bootstrap coefficient was 1.620 (SD = 0.341), with a 95% bootstrap CI of 0.945–2.266 that did not include zero—consistent with the initial model’s significant association. For macular status, the mean bootstrap coefficient was 0.890 (SD = 0.317), with a 95% bootstrap CI of 0.225–1.490 (also excluding zero), further validating its independent predictive role. Box plots of bootstrap coefficients (Fig. 4C) showed narrow interquartile ranges (IQRs) for both variables, with no overlap between the IQRs and the “no-effect” threshold (y = 0), confirming minimal coefficient variability across resamples. Bootstrap validation also confirmed stable model performance (Fig. 4B). The mean bootstrap-estimated accuracy was 0.873 (SD = 0.051), with a 95% bootstrap CI of 0.769–0.962, indicating minimal variation in predictive accuracy across resamples. The mean bootstrap ROC-AUC was 0.926 (SD = 0.037), with a 95% bootstrap CI of 0.839–0.987—indicating consistent excellent discriminative ability, even when accounting for sampling variability. Convergence analysis (Fig. 4D) showed that cumulative means of PI coefficients, macular status coefficients, and accuracy stabilized after approximately 500 resamples, with no further substantial changes as the number of resamples increased to 1000. This confirmed that 1000 resamples were sufficient to achieve reliable and stable estimates, ruling out potential bias from insufficient resampling.

Clinical interpretation

The consistent associations observed in both the initial model and bootstrap validation highlight the clinical relevance of PI and macular status for prognostic assessment. PI, as a quantitative measure of retinal perfusion, likely reflects the integrity of the retinal microcirculation—an essential determinant of post-treatment visual recovery. Macula-on status, a well-established clinical predictor, aligns with prior literature indicating that macular detachment at presentation prolongs photoreceptor damage and impairs visual outcomes. Together, these two variables provide complementary prognostic information, supporting their utility in clinical decision-making for retinal detachment patients.

Post-hoc power analysis

To validate the statistical reliability of associations between the pre-specified predictors (PI and macular status) and 6-month visual improvement, post-hoc power analysis (Fig. 5) was conducted using the observed effect sizes and current sample size (n = 52). For the continuous PI variable, a large between-group effect was observed (Cohen’s d = 1.650), yielding excellent post-hoc power of 94.7% for univariate t-test and 99.9% for multivariable logistic regression—confirming robust detection of PI’s association with visual improvement. For categorical macular status, a moderate effect (Cramér’s V = 0.280) resulted in strong power of 100.0% for univariate chi-square test and 79.3% for adjusted logistic regression (near the 80% adequacy threshold). Sample size requirement analysis further showed the current n = 52 exceeds the 46-patient threshold needed to detect macular status’ moderate effect (80% power) and far exceeds the 12-patient requirement for PI’s large effect. Convergence testing confirmed power estimates stabilized after 30–40 patients, validating the current sample’s efficiency and adequacy for reliable statistical inference.

Discussion

In the present study, patients were stratified into visual improvement and non-improvement groups according to 6-month postoperative LogMAR changes, and baseline characteristics as well as CEUS parameters were compared between groups. No significant intergroup differences were observed in age, BMI, blood pressure, fasting blood glucose, lipid profile, history or duration of hypertension and diabetes, or metabolic control status. These findings indicate that systemic microangiopathy related to hypertension or diabetes was well balanced between groups, reducing confounding effects on the association of CEUS parameters with postoperative visual recovery. Other general characteristics were also comparable between groups, implying that these variables were not major determinants of visual improvement. Standardized ROI size and placement, coupled with excellent intra- and interobserver reproducibility (ICC > 0.85), guarantee the reliability of CEUS quantitative measurements and support their clinical utility for prognostic evaluation.

Significant between-group differences were detected in CEUS parameters including AT, PI, and TIC-AUC. Notably, PI exhibited the most robust significance, with a markedly higher median value in the improvement group than in the non-improvement group. Multivariable logistic regression further confirmed PI as the only independent CEUS predictor for 6-month visual improvement. The model explained 34.7% of the variance in visual improvement and exhibited acceptable statistical performance. Higher PI values were significantly associated with higher odds of visual improvement, consistent with univariate comparisons. As a quantitative marker of tissue microperfusion reflecting the maximum microbubble concentration, PI directly reflects retinal microcirculation integrity and is therefore closely associated with postoperative visual recovery.

TIC-AUC also showed significant differences between the two groups, with higher values in the improvement group, but it was not retained in the final regression model, indicating that its predictive power may be partially overlapping with PI or less independent than PI in predicting visual improvement. Notably, TTP showed a borderline significant trend (p = 0.059), with longer TTP in the non-improvement group. This borderline trend may have physiological significance: prolonged TTP indicates delayed microbubble arrival and accumulation in the detached retina, which likely reflects impaired microvascular patency, reduced perfusion reserve, or subtle ischemia that precedes overt functional damage. Such mild but persistent microcirculatory disturbance may limit postoperative photoreceptor recovery and result in suboptimal visual gain, even in the absence of statistical significance at the current sample size. This trend supports the concept that retinal perfusion dynamics are closely linked to functional outcomes and deserves verification in larger-scale studies. In contrast, the AS of TIC showed no significant between-group difference (p = 0.100). The lack of significance for AS is biologically and technically plausible. First, AS primarily reflects the rate of microbubble influx rather than the total magnitude of tissue perfusion, which may be less critical for long-term retinal functional recovery than the overall perfusion volume represented by PI. Second, in detached retina, variable retinal folding, localized edema, and small ROI positioning may introduce minor heterogeneity in signal rise dynamics, weakening the independent prognostic performance of AS. Third, unlike PI, which represents stable maximal perfusion signal, AS is a transient kinetic parameter more susceptible to intraocular signal noise and patient movement. Therefore, while AS describes early perfusion dynamics, it does not provide independent prognostic information beyond PI in this cohort. These observations underscore that among CEUS-derived perfusion metrics, PI is the most robust and biologically consistent indicator for predicting visual recovery after RD surgery.

When compared with previous research, our findings align with some studies that have emphasized the role of CEUS parameters in assessing disease progression and treatment response14. For instance, previous studies have confirmed the potential of CEUS in evaluating blood perfusion and therapeutic effects of various diseases (such as breast cancer, liver cancer and kidney diseases)14,15. In the field of ophthalmology, although the application of CEUS is relatively limited, its advantages in evaluating microvascular blood perfusion have been widely recognized in other areas16,17.

This study expands current knowledge by validating PI as a robust independent predictor of visual recovery after RD surgery. Unlike previous studies that analyzed single CEUS metrics, our multivariable logistic regression and bootstrap validation clearly distinguish PI as the most reliable prognostic indicator, independent of TIC-AUC and other perfusion parameters. These findings refine the understanding of CEUS application in predicting functional outcomes after retinal reattachment surgery.

Theoretically, these findings support the role of CEUS in assessing ocular pathophysiology and predicting surgical outcomes. Visual recovery after RD relies not only on successful anatomical reattachment but also on the restoration of retinal microcirculation18. As a direct measure of maximum microbubble concentration, PI reflects the adequacy of retinal microperfusion, which is essential for photoreceptor survival and functional recovery. Clinically, identification of PI as an independent predictor provides a practical tool for preoperative risk stratification. Preoperative CEUS-derived PI offers an objective, quantitative marker to predict postoperative visual recovery. Patients with higher PI values tend to have better visual prognosis, allowing clinicians to formulate individualized follow-up and rehabilitation strategies. This objective stratification helps optimize clinical decision-making and improve patient counseling with evidence-based prognostic information. This study has several limitations that should be acknowledged. First, this is a single-center study with a relatively small sample (n = 52) and uneven group allocation (39 in the improvement group versus 13 in the non-improvement group), which may compromise statistical power and limit the external generalizability of the predictive model. The moderate R² value (34.7%) further indicates that additional predictive factors remain to be identified in larger populations. Second, the follow-up period was limited to 6 months. A longer follow-up duration (at least 12 months) is necessary to evaluate the stability of visual recovery and the late impact of retinal microperfusion on long-term functional outcomes. For these reasons, larger sample sizes and longer follow-up are necessary to draw definitive conclusions regarding the prognostic value of preoperative CEUS parameters. The exclusion of TIC-AUC from the final regression model may also be related to the limited sample size, and confirmation in a larger cohort is warranted. Although excellent reproducibility was observed in a subset of patients, full reproducibility in a larger multi-center setting remains to be confirmed.

Future studies should enroll a larger, multicenter patient cohort to improve statistical power, balance group distribution, and validate the independent prognostic role of PI. Extended long-term follow-up (≥ 12 months) is indispensable to establish a definitive conclusion about the association between preoperative CEUS parameters and sustained visual recovery after retinal detachment surgery. Longitudinal observation will also enable evaluation of the stability of retinal microcirculatory recovery and late-onset complications that may influence final visual prognosis. Furthermore, integration of genetic, systemic metabolic, and lifestyle factors into multivariate predictive models may improve prognostic accuracy and translational clinical utility. Collectively, these efforts will help establish a reliable, CEUS-based strategy for preoperative risk stratification and postoperative visual outcome prediction in patients undergoing retinal detachment surgery.

Conclusion

Preoperative CEUS parameters, particularly peak intensity (PI), show significant associations with 6-month visual recovery following retinal reattachment surgery. PI is identified as the only independent CEUS-derived predictor in this cohort, explaining 34.7% of the variance in visual improvement. However, given the single-center design, modest sample size, limited explanatory power of the model, lack of external validation, and wide 95% confidence interval for the ROC-AUC, these findings suggest that PI may serve as a candidate prognostic biomarker, but it is not yet ready for routine clinical use as a validated prognostic test. CEUS provides objective, quantitative information on retinal microperfusion that may aid preoperative risk stratification in clinical practice, but its routine clinical application cannot be recommended at this stage. Prospective, multicenter, externally validated studies are essential to confirm the prognostic value of PI and support its translation into clinical care.

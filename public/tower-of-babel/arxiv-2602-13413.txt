Why is Normalization Preferred? A Worst-Case Complexity Theory for Stochastically Preconditioned SGD under Heavy-Tailed Noise
Yuchen Fang, James Demmel, Javad Lavaei

Abstract
We develop a worst-case complexity theory for stochastically preconditioned stochastic gradient descent (SPSGD) and its accelerated variants under heavy-tailed noise, a setting that encompasses widely used adaptive methods such as Adam, RMSProp, and Shampoo. We assume the stochastic gradient noise has a finite p p -th moment for some p ∈ ( 1 , 2 ] p\in(1,2] , and measure convergence after T T iterations. While clipping and normalization are parallel tools for stabilizing training of SGD under heavy-tailed noise, there is a fundamental separation in their worst-case properties in stochastically preconditioned settings. We demonstrate that normalization guarantees convergence to a first-order stationary point at rate 𝒪 ⁡ ( T − p − 1 3 ​ p − 2 ) \mathcal{O}(T^{-\frac{p-1}{3p-2}}) when problem parameters are known, and 𝒪 ⁡ ( T − p − 1 2 ​ p ) \mathcal{O}(T^{-\frac{p-1}{2p}}) when problem parameters are unknown, matching the optimal rates for normalized SGD, respectively. In contrast, we prove that clipping may fail to converge in the worst case due to the statistical dependence between the stochastic preconditioner and the gradient estimates. To enable the analysis, we develop a novel vector-valued Burkholder-type inequality that may be of independent interest. These results provide a theoretical explanation for the empirical preference for normalization over clipping in large-scale model training.

Introduction

We consider stochastic optimization problems of the form

\min_{\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(\boldsymbol{x}):=\mathbb{E}_{\xi\sim\mathcal{P}}[F(\boldsymbol{x},\xi)],

(1)

where
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}

. Stochastic gradient descent (SGD)

\mathcal{O}(T^{-\frac{1}{4}})
for finding first-order stationary points in smooth nonconvex problems.

Building upon SGD, numerous variants have been proposed to accelerate convergence and improve robustness. A classical and widely used extension is momentum SGD (MSGD)

(2)

where

\bar{g}_{k}
is a stochastic gradient at
\boldsymbol{x}_{k}
, and

is the momentum parameter. Both SGD and MSGD enjoy strong theoretical guarantees and empirical success when the gradient noise has bounded variance.

However, the bounded-variance assumption is often violated in modern machine learning. Empirical studies in image classification

3

(
13

Two simple yet widely used techniques for stabilizing SGD under heavy-tailed noise are clipping (ClipSGD)
(
47
)
and normalization (NSGD)
(
7
)

\mathcal{O}(T^{-\frac{p-1}{3p-2}})

Clipping often suffers from practical limitations: (1) determining the optimal clipping threshold requires precise knowledge of algorithmic parameters that are difficult to estimate; and (2) while theory often prescribes large, increasing thresholds, practitioners typically employ small, fixed thresholds
(
48

23

45

44

40

In this work, we show that this gap between clipping and normalization becomes fundamental once we move beyond vanilla SGD to Stochastically Preconditioned SGD (SPSGD) and its accelerated variants, which has the following update rule

\boldsymbol{x}_{k+1}=\boldsymbol{x}_{k}-\eta D_{k}m_{k},\qquad m_{k}=\theta m_{k-1}+(1-\theta)\bar{g}_{k},

The update rule in (

Despite their empirical success, the theoretical understanding of SPSGD remains limited. Existing analyses often either ignore the randomness of

Our contributions are summarized as follows:

1.

SGD and momentum SGD.

Adaptive learning-rate methods

AdaGrad

G_{k}=\sum_{i=0}^{k}\bar{g}_{i}\odot\bar{g}_{i}

v_{k}=\beta v_{k-1}+(1-\beta)\bar{g}_{k}^{2}

\boldsymbol{x}_{k+1}=\boldsymbol{x}_{k}-\frac{\eta}{\sqrt{v_{k}+\epsilon}}\odot\bar{g}_{k}

D_{k}=\operatorname{diag}\!\big(1/\sqrt{v_{k}+\epsilon}\big)

Kronecker-structured preconditioning methods
extend diagonal scaling by employing structured matrix preconditioners based on Kronecker factorizations, allowing richer curvature information to be exploited efficiently.

K-FAC

(
27

Shampoo

(
15

maintains Kronecker-factored second-moment estimates
L_{k}=\beta L_{k-1}+(1-\beta)G_{k}G_{k}^{T}

R_{k}=\beta R_{k-1}+(1-\beta)G_{k}^{T}G_{k}

Using properties of the Kronecker product, this can be equivalently written as
\operatorname{vec}(X_{k+1})=\operatorname{vec}(X_{k})-\eta(L_{k}\otimes R_{k})^{-1/4}\operatorname{vec}(G_{k})

Stochastic second-order methods
incorporate full or approximate curvature information by choosing

Stochastic Newton methods

Quasi-Newton methods

3
Takeaway: A Geometric Perspective

Before presenting our technical analysis, we offer an intuitive geometric explanation for why
step normalization
remains robust under stochastic preconditioning, whereas
step clipping
faces fundamental stability issues.

At each iteration, the stochastic gradient
\bar{g}_{k}
is transformed by a symmetric positive definite (SPD) preconditioner
D_{k}
. This yields a preconditioned direction
D_{k}\bar{g}_{k}
, which can be highly anisotropic and variable in magnitude due to curvature and data-dependent scaling.

Step normalization.

Under step normalization, the preconditioned update is strictly projected onto the surface of a sphere. By enforcing a fixed norm, normalization effectively decouples the update magnitude from the scale of the preconditioner. Geometrically, this constrains the optimization trajectory to a stable manifold, preventing the “magnitude noise” of
D_{k}
from propagating into the update step.

Step clipping.

In contrast, step clipping allows the update to lie either on the boundary or
anywhere within
the interior of a ball of radius
\tau
. Crucially, to achieve optimal convergence rate, theoretical analyses require this threshold
\tau
to grow with the time horizon
T
. Geometrically, this means the update region is an expanding ball. Because clipping retains magnitude information when the gradient is small, the statistical dependence between
D_{k}
and
\bar{g}_{k}
introduces a bias within this expanding interior.

Normalization Ensures Convergence

In this section, we demonstrate that step normalization ensures the convergence of Stochastically Preconditioned SGD (SPSGD) to a first-order stationary point in expectation. Furthermore, we establish that in the worst case—where minimal structural knowledge of the stochastic preconditioner is assumed—the convergence rate matches the optimal rate of Normalized SGD (NSGD).

Assumption 4.4

(Bounded condition number of

The stochastic preconditioner

We justify this assumption from several perspectives. First, imposing

4

4.3
Step normalization is robust

We establish a key robustness property of step normalization: when the gradient estimate is stochastically preconditioned, the accumulated gradient norms remain controlled by a factor of
\sqrt{\kappa_{D}}
relative to the bounds obtained in normalized SGD (NSGD). Formally, we have the following result.

Lemma 4.9
.

Under Assumptions
4.1
,
4.2
and
4.4
, for all
T\geq 1
, let
\Delta\coloneqq f(\boldsymbol{x}_{1})-f_{*}
, we have

\sum_{k=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left[\|\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|\right]\leq\sqrt{\kappa_{D}}\cdot\left[\frac{\Delta}{\eta}+2\sum_{k=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left[\|\varepsilon_{k}\|\right]+\frac{\eta LT}{2}\right],

where
\varepsilon_{k}=m_{k}-\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})
.

This bound is crucial: even when the stochastic preconditioner
D_{k}
is poorly designed, step normalization limits performance degradation to a multiplicative factor of at most
\sqrt{\kappa_{D}}
compared with standard NSGD, without introducing any new error terms. This controlled degradation explains the robustness of normalized updates in stochastically preconditioned settings.

That said, the analysis above is worst-case and assumes no additional structure on
D_{k}
. When the preconditioners are well-constructed (e.g., approximating a reasonable inverse Hessian or covariance structure), algorithm-specific analyses can typically establish substantially tighter bounds than the one presented here.

4.4
Vector-valued Burkholder-type inequality

Given the result of Lemma
4.9
, to complete the analysis, we must bound
\sum_{k=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left[\|\varepsilon_{k}\|\right]
.

By the momentum recursion, we have

\varepsilon_{k}=\theta^{k}\varepsilon_{0}+\sum_{t=1}^{k}\theta^{k-t}s_{t}+(1-\theta)\sum_{t=1}^{k}\theta^{k-t}\delta_{t},

where
s_{t}=\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{t-1})-\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{t})
and
\delta_{t}=\bar{g}_{t}-\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{t})
. Thus

\mathbb{E}[\|\varepsilon_{k}\|]\leq\theta^{k}\mathbb{E}[\|\varepsilon_{0}\|]\\
+\sum_{t=1}^{k}\theta^{k-t}\mathbb{E}[\|s_{t}\|]+(1-\theta)\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\sum_{t=1}^{k}\theta^{k-t}\delta_{t}\right\|\right].

The first two terms are straightforward to bound. However, bounding
\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\sum_{t=1}^{k}\theta^{k-t}\delta_{t}\right\|\right]
might introduce difficulty.
By Hölder’s inequality, we have

\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\sum_{t=1}^{k}\theta^{k-t}\delta_{t}\right\|\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\sum_{t=1}^{k}\theta^{k-t}(\bar{g}_{t}-\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{t}))\right\|\right]\\
\leq\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\sum_{t=1}^{k}\theta^{k-t}\left(\bar{g}_{t}-\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{t})\right)\right\|^{p}\right]\right)^{1/p}.

When
p=2
, this bound is straightforward to evaluate using the martingale property. However, for
p\in(1,2)
, further simplification is nontrivial.
26
treat the cases
p\in(1,2)
and
p=2
separately using different techniques.
17
provide a unified analysis, but with suboptimal constants. To address this, we establish the following vector-valued Burkholder-type inequality, which unifies the analysis for all
p\in(1,2]
while achieving sharper constants. The proof is deferred to Appendix
D
.

Lemma 4.10

(Vector-valued Burkholder-type inequality)
.

Let
X_{k},k=0,1,\dots,T-1
, be random variables with
\mathbb{E}[X_{k}\mid X_{0:k-1}]=\boldsymbol{0}
and
\mathbb{E}[\|X_{k}\|^{p}]<\infty
for any
k\geq 0
and some
p\in(1,2]
. Then

\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\sum_{k=0}^{T-1}X_{k}\right\|^{p}\right]\leq 2^{2-p}\sum_{k=0}^{T-1}\mathbb{E}[\|X_{k}\|^{p}].

Applying the inequality and Assumption
4.4
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\sum_{t=1}^{k}\theta^{k-t}\delta_{t}\right\|\right]

\displaystyle\leq 2^{\frac{2}{p}-1}\left(\sum_{t=1}^{k}\theta^{p(k-t)}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\bar{g}_{t}-\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{t}))\right\|^{p}\right]\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2^{\frac{2}{p}-1}\sigma}{(1-\theta^{p})^{1/p}}.

5
What Might Go Wrong for Clipping?

In this section, we demonstrate the potential failure of step clipping when gradient estimates are stochastically preconditioned. To simplify the exposition, we omit momentum from the gradient estimate; this choice is discussed further in Remark
5.6
.

Recall that for SGD with gradient clipping, we have

\boldsymbol{x}_{k+1}=\boldsymbol{x}_{k}-\eta\min\left\{1,\frac{\tau}{\|\bar{g}_{k}\|}\right\}\bar{g}_{k},

where
\tau
is a clipping threshold that depends on algorithmic parameters
T
and
\sigma
to achieve the optimal convergence rate
(
47
)
.

When the gradient is stochastically preconditioned, two natural choices arise. The first clips the gradient estimate before preconditioning:

\boldsymbol{x}_{k+1}=\boldsymbol{x}_{k}-\eta D_{k}\cdot\min\left\{1,\frac{\tau}{\|\bar{g}_{k}\|}\right\}\bar{g}_{k},

while the second clips the preconditioned gradient:

\boldsymbol{x}_{k+1}=\boldsymbol{x}_{k}-\eta\min\left\{1,\frac{\tau}{\|D_{k}\bar{g}_{k}\|}\right\}D_{k}\bar{g}_{k}.

We refer to these as
Clipping-Then-Preconditioning
and
Preconditioning-Then-Clipping
, respectively.

Both designs have intuitive justifications. The first approach clips the heavy-tailed gradient estimate to remove outliers before using the stabilized estimates to construct
D_{k}
, yielding more robust curvature information. The second accounts for the stochastic dependence between
D_{k}
and
\bar{g}_{k}
: since
D_{k}
may rotate and stretch
\bar{g}_{k}
stochastically, clipping after forming the complete step may produce more stable updates. Several optimizers for large-scale machine learning, such as Sophia
(
23
)
, follow the second principle.

For the analysis of clipping, we impose a slightly stronger assumption on the preconditioning matrix
D_{k}
.

Assumption 5.1

(Uniform Boundedness of
D_{k}
)
.

There exist deterministic constants
m_{D},M_{D}>0
such that
m_{D}\cdot I\preceq D_{k}\preceq M_{D}\cdot I
for
\forall k\leq T
.

This assumption strengthens Assumption
4.4
by requiring that the singular values of
D_{k}
be uniformly bounded by deterministic constants for all iterations. However, even under this stronger assumption, clipping fails to guarantee convergence, as shown below.

We discuss the two clipping variants separately due to differences in analysis. Proofs are presented in Appendix
C
.

5.1
Clipping-Then-Preconditioning

Under this technique, we write
\boldsymbol{x}_{k+1}=\boldsymbol{x}_{k}-\eta D_{k}\widehat{\bar{g}}_{k}
with
\widehat{\bar{g}}_{k}=\min\{1,\tau/\|\bar{g}_{k}\|\}\bar{g}_{k}
. Following standard analysis, we consider the expected reduction per iteration by separating the cases
\|\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|\geq\tau/2
and
\|\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|<\tau/2
.

We first consider
\|\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|\geq\tau/2
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Under Assumptions
4.2
,
4.3
and
5.1
, when
\|\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|\geq\tau/2
, if we select
\eta<\frac{m_{D}}{24L}
and

\tau=\max\left\{2,4\cdot 3^{1/p}\cdot\sigma\left(\frac{M_{D}}{m_{D}}\right)^{\frac{p+1}{2p}},64\sigma\frac{M_{D}}{m_{D}}\right\},

then
we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{k}[f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k+1})]\leq f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})-\frac{1}{12}\eta m_{D}\|\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|.

This result shows that expected reduction is achieved when the clipping threshold
\tau
is relatively small compared to the true gradient, matching the behavior of clipped SGD. However, the learning rate and clipping threshold now depend on
m_{D}
and
M_{D}
, complicating practical implementation.

Next, we consider the case
\|\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|<\tau/2
, where we show that reduction is not guaranteed in the worst case.

Lemma 5.3
.

Under Assumptions
4.2
,
4.3
and
5.1
, when
\|\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|<\tau/2
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{k}[f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k+1})]

\displaystyle\leq f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})-\frac{1}{2}\eta\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|D_{k}^{1/2}\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|^{2}]

\displaystyle\quad-(\eta-L\eta^{2}M_{D})\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})^{T}\mathbb{E}_{k}[D_{k}\epsilon_{k}^{u}]

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}\eta M_{D}\|\epsilon_{k}^{b}\|^{2}+\frac{\eta^{2}L}{2}M_{D}^{2}\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|\epsilon_{k}^{u}\|^{2}],

where
\epsilon_{k}^{u}=\widehat{\bar{g}}_{k}-\mathbb{E}_{k}[\widehat{\bar{g}}_{k}]
and
\epsilon_{k}^{b}=\mathbb{E}_{k}[\widehat{\bar{g}}_{k}]-\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})
.

This result resembles that for ClipSGD
(
47
)
, except for the additional error term
-(\eta-L\eta^{2}M_{D})\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})^{T}\mathbb{E}_{k}[D_{k}\epsilon_{k}^{u}]
. In ClipSGD, this term vanishes because
D_{k}=I
and
\mathbb{E}_{k}[\epsilon_{k}^{u}]=0
. However, it generally does not vanish when
D_{k}
and
\bar{g}_{k}
are dependent, as in SPSGD. Note that

\mathbb{E}_{k}[D_{k}\epsilon_{k}^{u}]=\mathbb{E}_{k}[D_{k}\widehat{\bar{g}}_{k}]-\mathbb{E}_{k}[D_{k}]\mathbb{E}_{k}[\widehat{\bar{g}}_{k}],

this term essentially measures the covariance between the preconditioner and the clipped gradient noise.

Without explicit knowledge of
D_{k}
’s structure, we can only upper bound this error term. Applying the rescaled Young’s inequality yields

\displaystyle-(\eta-L\eta^{2}M_{D})\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})^{T}\mathbb{E}_{k}[D_{k}\epsilon_{k}^{u}]

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\frac{1}{4}(\eta-L\eta^{2}M_{D})\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|D_{k}^{1/2}\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|^{2}]

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+(\eta-L\eta^{2}M_{D})M_{D}\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|\epsilon_{k}^{u}\|^{2}].

(6)

The rescaling ensures that the coefficient of
\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|D_{k}^{1/2}\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|^{2}]
remains negative. The key issue, and the source of clipping’s potential failure, is the
\mathcal{O}(\eta\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|\epsilon_{k}^{u}\|^{2}])
error term. Combining this with Lemma
5.3
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{k}[f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k+1})]

\displaystyle\leq f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})-\frac{1}{4}\eta\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|D_{k}^{1/2}\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|^{2}]

\displaystyle\quad+\eta M_{D}\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|\epsilon_{k}^{u}\|^{2}]+\frac{1}{2}\eta M_{D}\|\epsilon_{k}^{b}\|^{2}.

Comparing this with ClipSGD
(
47
)
, the current error is
\mathcal{O}(\eta\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|\epsilon_{k}^{u}\|^{2}])
rather than
\mathcal{O}(\eta^{2}\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|\epsilon_{k}^{u}\|^{2}])
. By standard analysis, when
\|\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|<\tau/2
, we have
\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|\epsilon_{k}^{u}\|^{2}]\leq 10\tau^{2-p}\sigma^{p}
and
\|\epsilon_{k}^{b}\|^{2}\leq 4\sigma^{2p}\tau^{-2p+2}
. Substituting these bounds yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{k}[f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k+1})]

\displaystyle\leq f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})-\frac{1}{4}\eta m_{D}\|\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+10\eta M_{D}\tau^{2-p}\sigma^{p}

\displaystyle\quad+2\eta M_{D}\sigma^{2p}\tau^{-2p+2}.

To achieve optimal complexity, the clipping threshold must be carefully chosen. Following
47
, we set
\tau=\mathcal{O}(T^{a})
for some
a>0
. However, when
p\in(1,2)
, this yields
\tau^{2-p}\sigma^{p}\to\infty
as
T\to\infty
, indicating that descent is not guaranteed and divergence may occur.

In contrast, for ClipSGD the error term is
\mathcal{O}(\eta^{2}\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|\epsilon_{k}^{u}\|^{2}])
, so we can choose
\eta
sufficiently small such that
\eta^{2}\tau^{2-p}\sigma^{p}\leq\mathcal{O}(\eta\tau^{-2p+2}\sigma^{p})
, making the power of
\tau
negative.

Remark 5.4
.

One might argue that the bound in (
5.1
) is too loose. However, given the dependence between
D_{k}
and
\bar{g}_{k}
and the lack of structural assumptions on
D_{k}
, this is the tightest bound we can obtain without algorithm-specific analysis. The following example demonstrates that the error bound in (
5.1
) can indeed be achieved.

Example 5.5
.

Consider the one-dimensional case. Suppose at iterate
\boldsymbol{x}_{k}
we have
\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})=C
, where
\sigma\gg C>0
is a constant. The gradient estimate follows a discrete distribution taking two values with equal probability:
\bar{g}_{k}=\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})+\sigma
with probability
1/2
and
\bar{g}_{k}=\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})-\sigma
with probability
1/2
. We set
M_{D}-m_{D}=\mathcal{O}(\sigma)
, and design
D_{k}
such that
D_{k}=m_{D}\cdot I
when
\bar{g}_{k}>0
and
D_{k}=M_{D}\cdot I
when
\bar{g}_{k}<0
. Setting
\tau\gg\sigma
, we have

-(\eta-L\eta^{2}M_{D})\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})^{T}\mathbb{E}_{k}[D_{k}\epsilon_{k}^{u}]=\mathcal{O}(\eta\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|\epsilon_{k}^{u}\|^{2}]).

The proof is deferred to Appendix
C.3
.

Remark 5.6
.

We do not combine clipping with momentum, consistent with most standard analyses of gradient clipping under heavy-tailed noise
(
47
;
34
;
36
;
30
)
. While
38
combine clipping with normalization and momentum, such combinations obscure the individual effects of clipping and normalization. We argue that the problem with clipping cannot be resolved by introducing momentum. The issue originates from the dependence between the preconditioner and the gradient estimate, while momentum merely aggregates the randomness of
\bar{g}_{k}
across iterations without removing its dependence on
D_{k}
. Consequently, momentum alone cannot resolve this fundamental issue.

5.2
Preconditioning-Then-Clipping

Under this technique, we write
\boldsymbol{x}_{k+1}=\boldsymbol{x}_{k}-\eta D_{k}\widehat{\bar{g}}_{k}
with
\widehat{\bar{g}}_{k}=\min\{1,\tau/\|D_{k}\bar{g}_{k}\|\}\bar{g}_{k}
. Similar to Section
5.1
, we analyze this by separating cases based on gradient magnitude. Due to the preconditioning matrix in the clipping criterion, we use the threshold
\tau/(2M_{D})
, which reduces to the standard
\tau/2
when
M_{D}=1
.

When the clipping threshold is relatively small, i.e.,
\|\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|\geq\tau/(2M_{D})
, expected descent can still be achieved, provided that the clipping threshold is properly selected. We defer the lemma to Appendix
C.8
. However, as in Section
5.1
, when the threshold is large, descent is not guaranteed.

Lemma 5.7
.

Under Assumptions
4.2
,
4.3
and
5.1
,
when
\|\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|<\frac{\tau}{2M_{D}}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{k}[f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k+1})]

\displaystyle\leq f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})-\frac{1}{4}\eta m_{D}\|\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\eta M_{D}\Upsilon_{1}^{2}\sigma^{2p}\tau^{-2p+2}+\eta M_{D}\Upsilon_{2}\tau^{2-p}\sigma^{p},

where
\Upsilon_{1},\Upsilon_{2}
are constants independent to
\sigma
and
\tau
,
\epsilon_{k}^{u}=\widehat{\bar{g}}_{k}-\mathbb{E}_{k}[\widehat{\bar{g}}_{k}]
, and
\epsilon_{k}^{b}=\mathbb{E}_{k}[\widehat{\bar{g}}_{k}]-\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})
.

The reasoning is identical to that in Section
5.1
: the additional error term
-(\eta-L\eta^{2}M_{D})\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})^{T}\mathbb{E}_{k}[D_{k}\epsilon_{k}^{u}]
appears and cannot be eliminated in general.

This is an expected result, since this additional error term captures the covariance between
D_{k}
and
\bar{g}_{k}
, which comes from the dependence between
D_{k}
and
\bar{g}_{k}
, which generally cannot be overcome by step clipping.

6
Discussion

6.1
Applications to large-scale machine learning

Our theoretical findings provide a novel explanation for the empirical success of modern large-scale optimizers such as LARS
(
43
;
45
)
, LAMB
(
44
)
, and their variants. These methods combine gradient normalization with adaptive (often layer-wise) preconditioning, mirroring the structure analyzed in this work. Since large-scale models exhibit heavy-tailed gradient noise where bounded-variance assumptions fail, our results formalize why these methods scale reliably: step normalization remains robust by removing magnitude information and avoiding dependence-induced errors under stochastic preconditioning.

6.2
Clipping is still important

Our analysis does not dismiss clipping but rather clarifies its differences from normalization under stochastic preconditioning. Clipping remains widely used and important in practice. Moreover, our worst-case analysis does not imply clipping always fails when
D_{k}
depends on
\bar{g}_{k}
—the theory-practice gap mentioned in Section
1
makes real-world performance difficult to predict, and careful tuning may still ensure good behavior.

6.3
Future work

Several promising directions remain for future work. First, characterizing instance-dependent or average-case behavior under realistic distributions could reveal when clipping succeeds empirically. Second, extending the theory to layer-wise normalization and block-structured preconditioners would further bridge the theory-practice gap. Finally, while our analysis considers linear preconditioning, recent stochastic methods introduce nonlinear transformations of gradient information; extending our framework to such settings would better guide optimizer design.

7
Conclusion

This paper provides a worst-case complexity theory explaining a fundamental separation between step clipping and step normalization under stochastic preconditioning and heavy-tailed noise. We show that clipping may fail due to a structural, dependence-induced bias that generally cannot be eliminated by tuning or clipping, while normalization remains robust and achieves the convergence rates that match the optimal rates of NSGD. Our resulabodern large-scale optimizers such as LARS and LAMB.

Lemma C.2
.

Under Assumptions
4.2
,
4.3
, and
5.1
, when
\|\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|<\tau/2
, then we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{k}[f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k+1})]

\displaystyle\leq f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})-\frac{1}{2}\eta\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|D_{k}^{1/2}\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|^{2}]-(\eta-L\eta^{2}M_{D})\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})^{T}\mathbb{E}_{k}[D_{k}\varepsilon_{k}^{u}]

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}\eta M_{D}\|\varepsilon_{k}^{b}\|^{2}+\frac{\eta^{2}L}{2}M_{D}^{2}\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|\varepsilon_{k}^{u}\|^{2}],

Proof.

We denote
\Delta\boldsymbol{x}_{k}=\boldsymbol{x}_{k+1}-\boldsymbol{x}_{k}
, then
the Taylor expansion yields

\displaystyle f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k+1})\leq f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})+\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})^{T}\Delta\boldsymbol{x}_{k}+\frac{L}{2}\|\Delta\boldsymbol{x}_{k}\|^{2}=f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})-\eta\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})^{T}D_{k}\widehat{\bar{g}}_{k}+\frac{\eta^{2}L}{2}\|D_{k}\widehat{\bar{g}}_{k}\|^{2}.

Letting
\varepsilon_{k}=\widehat{\bar{g}}_{k}-\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})

\|D_{k}\|\leq M_{D}

\displaystyle f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k+1})\leq f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})-\left(\eta-\frac{\eta^{2}L}{2}\right)\|D_{k}^{1/2}\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|^{2}-(\eta-L\eta^{2}M_{D})\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})^{T}D_{k}\varepsilon_{k}+\frac{\eta^{2}LM_{D}^{2}}{2}\|\varepsilon_{k}\|^{2}.

We then follow standard analysis and decompose
\varepsilon_{k}=\varepsilon_{k}^{u}+\varepsilon_{k}^{b}
, where
\varepsilon_{k}^{u}=\widehat{\bar{g}}_{k}-\mathbb{E}_{k}[\widehat{\bar{g}}_{k}]
and
\varepsilon_{k}^{b}=\mathbb{E}_{k}[\widehat{\bar{g}}_{k}]-\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})
. Thus,

\displaystyle f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k+1})

\displaystyle\leq f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})-\left(\eta-\frac{\eta^{2}L}{2}\right)\|D_{k}^{1/2}\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|^{2}-(\eta-L\eta^{2}M_{D})\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})^{T}D_{k}\varepsilon_{k}^{u}

\displaystyle\quad-(\eta-L\eta^{2}M_{D})\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})^{T}D_{k}\varepsilon_{k}^{b}+\frac{\eta^{2}L}{2}M_{D}^{2}\|\varepsilon_{k}^{u}+\varepsilon_{k}^{b}\|^{2}.

\displaystyle-\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})^{T}D_{k}\varepsilon_{k}^{b}\leq\frac{1}{2}\|D_{k}^{1/2}\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|D_{k}^{1/2}\varepsilon_{k}^{b}\|^{2},

thus

\displaystyle f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k+1})

\displaystyle\leq f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})-\left(\eta-\frac{\eta^{2}L}{2}\right)\|D_{k}^{1/2}\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|^{2}-(\eta-L\eta^{2}M_{D})\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})^{T}D_{k}\varepsilon_{k}^{u}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}(\eta-L\eta^{2}M_{D})\|D_{k}^{1/2}\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}(\eta-L\eta^{2}M_{D})\|D_{k}^{1/2}\varepsilon_{k}^{b}\|^{2}+\frac{\eta^{2}L}{2}M_{D}^{2}\|\varepsilon_{k}^{u}+\varepsilon_{k}^{b}\|^{2}.

\mathcal{F}_{k-1}
and obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{k}[f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k+1})]

\displaystyle\leq f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})-\frac{1}{2}\eta\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|D_{k}^{1/2}\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|^{2}]-(\eta-L\eta^{2}M_{D})\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})^{T}\mathbb{E}_{k}[D_{k}\varepsilon_{k}^{u}]

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}\eta M_{D}\|\varepsilon_{k}^{b}\|^{2}+\frac{\eta^{2}L}{2}M_{D}^{2}\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|\varepsilon_{k}^{u}\|^{2}],

where we have implicitly assumed that
M_{D}>1
. We also used the fact that
\mathbb{E}[\varepsilon_{k}^{u}]=0
and
\varepsilon_{k}^{b}
is deterministic conditional on
\boldsymbol{x}_{k}
.

∎

Lemma C.3
.

Lemma C.4
.

Under Assumptions
4.2
,
4.3
, and
5.1
, when
\|\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|\geq\tau/2

\eta<\frac{m_{D}}{24L}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{k}[f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k+1})]\leq f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})-\frac{1}{12}\eta m_{D}\|\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|.

Proof.

By the Taylor expansion, we have

\displaystyle f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k+1})\leq f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})+\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})^{T}\Delta\boldsymbol{x}_{k}+\frac{L}{2}\|\Delta\boldsymbol{x}_{k}\|^{2}=f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})-\eta\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})^{T}D_{k}\widehat{\bar{g}}_{k}+\frac{\eta^{2}L}{2}\|D_{k}\widehat{\bar{g}}_{k}\|^{2}.

We first consider the conditional expectation of the
term
\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})^{T}D_{k}\widehat{\bar{g}}_{k}
. We have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\nabla f(x_{k})^{T}D_{k}\widehat{\bar{g}}_{k}\right]=\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\nabla f(x_{k})^{T}D_{k}\bar{g}_{k}\cdot\boldsymbol{1}_{(\|\bar{g}_{k}\|\leq\tau)}\right]+\tau\cdot\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\nabla f(x_{k})^{T}D_{k}\frac{\bar{g}_{k}}{\|\bar{g}_{k}\|}\cdot\boldsymbol{1}_{(\|\bar{g}_{k}\|>\tau)}\right]

We focusing on the first term:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\nabla f(x_{k})^{T}D_{k}\bar{g}_{k}\cdot\boldsymbol{1}_{(\|\bar{g}_{k}\|\leq\tau)}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\nabla f(x_{k})^{T}D_{k}(\nabla f(x_{k})+\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(x_{k}))\cdot\boldsymbol{1}_{(\|\bar{g}_{k}\|\leq\tau)}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\nabla f(x_{k})^{T}D_{k}\nabla f(x_{k})+\nabla f(x_{k})^{T}D_{k}(\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(x_{k}))\cdot\boldsymbol{1}_{(\|\bar{g}_{k}\|\leq\tau)}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\nabla f(x_{k})^{T}D_{k}\nabla f(x_{k})\right]+\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\nabla f(x_{k})^{T}D_{k}(\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(x_{k}))\cdot\boldsymbol{1}_{(\|\bar{g}_{k}\|\leq\tau)}\right]

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\nabla f(x_{k})^{T}D_{k}\nabla f(x_{k})\right]-\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\|D_{k}^{1/2}\nabla f(x_{k})\|\|D_{k}^{1/2}(\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(x_{k}))\|\cdot\boldsymbol{1}_{(\|\bar{g}_{k}\|\leq\tau)}\right]

\displaystyle\quad-\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\|D_{k}^{1/2}\nabla f(x_{k})\|\|D_{k}^{1/2}(\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(x_{k}))\|\cdot\boldsymbol{1}_{(\|\bar{g}_{k}\|\leq\tau,\|D_{k}^{1/2}(\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(x_{k}))\|\leq\sqrt{m_{D}}\cdot\tau/4)}\right]

\displaystyle\quad-\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\|D_{k}^{1/2}\nabla f(x_{k})\|\|D_{k}^{1/2}(\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(x_{k}))\|\cdot\boldsymbol{1}_{(\|\bar{g}_{k}\|\leq\tau,\|D_{k}^{1/2}(\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(x_{k}))\|>\sqrt{m_{D}}\cdot\tau/4)}\right].

\|\bar{g}_{k}\|\leq\tau,\|D_{k}^{1/2}(\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(x_{k}))\|\leq\sqrt{m_{D}}\cdot\tau/4

\|\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|>\tau/2

\|D_{k}^{1/2}\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|^{2}=\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})^{T}D_{k}\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\geq m_{D}\|\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|^{2}\geq\frac{\tau^{2}m_{D}}{4},

which imply

\|D_{k}^{1/2}\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|\geq\frac{\tau\sqrt{m_{D}}}{2},\quad\|D_{k}^{1/2}(\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(x_{k}))\|\leq\sqrt{m_{D}}\cdot\tau/4\leq\frac{1}{2}\|D_{k}^{1/2}\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|.

Therefore we find

\|D_{k}^{1/2}\nabla f(x_{k})\|\|D_{k}^{1/2}(\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(x_{k}))\|\leq\frac{1}{2}\|D_{k}^{1/2}\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|^{2}.

We further have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\|D_{k}^{1/2}\nabla f(x_{k})\|\|D_{k}^{1/2}(\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(x_{k}))\|\cdot\boldsymbol{1}_{(\|\bar{g}_{k}\|\leq\tau,\|D_{k}^{1/2}(\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(x_{k}))\|\leq\sqrt{m_{D}}\cdot\tau/4)}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\|D_{k}^{1/2}\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|^{2}\cdot\boldsymbol{1}_{(\|\bar{g}_{k}\|\leq\tau,\|D_{k}^{1/2}(\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(x_{k}))\|\leq\sqrt{m_{D}}\cdot\tau/4)}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\|D_{k}^{1/2}\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|^{2}\cdot\boldsymbol{1}_{(\|\bar{g}_{k}\|\leq\tau)}\right]

Plugging this into the above formula, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\nabla f(x_{k})^{T}D_{k}\bar{g}_{k}\cdot\boldsymbol{1}_{(\|\bar{g}_{k}\|\leq\tau)}\right]

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\|D_{k}^{1/2}\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}\cdot\boldsymbol{1}_{(\|\bar{g}_{k}\|\leq\tau)}\right]-\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\|D_{k}^{1/2}\nabla f(x_{k})\|\|D_{k}^{1/2}(\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(x_{k}))\|\cdot\boldsymbol{1}_{(\|D_{k}^{1/2}(\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(x_{k}))\|>\sqrt{m_{D}}\cdot\tau/4)}\right]

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\|D_{k}^{1/2}\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}\right]\cdot\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\boldsymbol{1}_{(\|\bar{g}_{k}\|\leq\tau)}\right]-\sqrt{M_{D}}\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|D_{k}^{1/2}(\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(x_{k}))\|\cdot\boldsymbol{1}_{(\|D_{k}^{1/2}(\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(x_{k}))\|>\sqrt{m_{D}}\cdot\tau/4)}]

\displaystyle=\frac{p_{g}}{2}\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|D_{k}^{1/2}\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}]-\sqrt{M_{D}}\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|D_{k}^{1/2}(\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(x_{k}))\|\cdot\boldsymbol{1}_{(\|D_{k}^{1/2}(\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(x_{k}))\|>\sqrt{m_{D}}\cdot\tau/4)}].

Next, we bound
\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|D_{k}^{1/2}(\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(x_{k}))\|\cdot\boldsymbol{1}_{(\|D_{k}^{1/2}(\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(x_{k}))\|>\sqrt{m_{D}}\cdot\tau/4)}]
.

\displaystyle M_{D}^{p/2}\sigma^{p}

\displaystyle\geq M_{D}^{p/2}\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|^{p}]\geq\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|D_{k}^{1/2}(\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k}))\|^{p}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|D_{k}^{1/2}(\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k}))\|\cdot\|D_{k}^{1/2}(\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k}))\|^{p-1}]

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|D_{k}^{1/2}(\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k}))\|\cdot\|D_{k}^{1/2}(\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k}))\|^{p-1}\cdot\boldsymbol{1}_{(\|D_{k}^{1/2}(\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(x_{k}))\|>\sqrt{m_{D}}\cdot\tau/4)}]

Rearranging the terms yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|D_{k}^{1/2}(\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k}))\|\cdot\boldsymbol{1}_{(\|D_{k}^{1/2}(\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(x_{k}))\|>\sqrt{m_{D}}\cdot\tau/4)}]\leq\frac{M_{D}^{p/2}\sigma^{p}}{\left(\sqrt{m_{D}}\cdot\frac{\tau}{4}\right)^{p-1}}.

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{k}[\nabla f(x_{k})^{T}D_{k}\bar{g}_{k}\cdot\boldsymbol{1}_{(\|\bar{g}_{k}\|\leq\tau)}]\geq\frac{p_{g}}{2}\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|D_{k}^{1/2}\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}]-\|\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|\frac{M_{D}^{p/2+1}\sigma^{p}}{\left(\sqrt{m_{D}}\cdot\frac{\tau}{4}\right)^{p-1}}.

\tau\cdot\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\nabla f(x_{k})^{T}D_{k}\frac{\bar{g}_{k}}{\|\bar{g}_{k}\|}\cdot\boldsymbol{1}_{(\|\bar{g}_{k}\|>\tau)}\right]

We first consider the case
\|D_{k}^{1/2}\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|\geq 2\|D_{k}^{1/2}(\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k}))\|
, where we have

\displaystyle\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})^{T}D_{k}\bar{g}_{k}

\displaystyle=\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})^{T}D_{k}\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})+\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})^{T}D_{k}(\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k}))

\displaystyle\geq\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})^{T}D_{k}\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})-\|D_{k}^{1/2}\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|\|D_{k}^{1/2}(\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k}))\|

\displaystyle\geq\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})^{T}D_{k}\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})-\frac{1}{2}\|D_{k}^{1/2}\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\|D_{k}^{1/2}\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|^{2}.

In addition,

\displaystyle\sqrt{m_{D}}\|\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})+\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|

\displaystyle\leq\|D_{k}^{1/2}(\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})+\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k}))\|

\displaystyle\leq\|D_{k}^{1/2}\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|+\|D_{k}^{1/2}(\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k}))\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{3}{2}\|D_{k}^{1/2}\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|.

\displaystyle\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})^{T}D_{k}\frac{\bar{g}_{k}}{\|\bar{g}_{k}\|}

\displaystyle=\frac{\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})^{T}D_{k}(\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})+\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k}))}{\|\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})+\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\sqrt{m_{D}}}{3}\|D_{k}^{1/2}\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|.

\|D_{k}^{1/2}\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|<2\|D_{k}^{1/2}(\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k}))\|
, we have

\displaystyle\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})^{T}D_{k}\frac{\bar{g}_{k}}{\|\bar{g}_{k}\|}

\displaystyle\geq-\|D_{k}^{1/2}\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|\frac{\|D_{k}^{1/2}\bar{g}_{k}\|}{\|\bar{g}_{k}\|}

\displaystyle\geq-\sqrt{M_{D}}\|D_{k}^{1/2}\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{m_{D}}}{3}\|D_{k}^{1/2}\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|-\left(\frac{\sqrt{m_{D}}}{3}+\sqrt{M_{D}}\right)\|D_{k}^{1/2}\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\sqrt{m_{D}}}{3}\|D_{k}^{1/2}\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|-2\left(\frac{\sqrt{m_{D}}}{3}+\sqrt{M_{D}}\right)\|D_{k}^{1/2}(\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k}))\|.

Combining the above formulas, we have

\displaystyle\tau\cdot\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\nabla f(x_{k})^{T}D_{k}\frac{\bar{g}_{k}}{\|\bar{g}_{k}\|}\cdot\boldsymbol{1}_{(\|\bar{g}_{k}\|>\tau)}\right]

\displaystyle\geq\tau\cdot\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\left(\frac{\sqrt{m_{D}}}{3}\|D_{k}^{1/2}\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|-2\left(\frac{\sqrt{m_{D}}}{3}+\sqrt{M_{D}}\right)\|D_{k}^{1/2}(\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k}))\|\right)\cdot\boldsymbol{1}_{(\|\bar{g}_{k}\|>\tau)}\right]

\displaystyle=\tau\frac{\sqrt{m_{D}}}{3}\cdot\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\|D_{k}^{1/2}\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|\cdot\boldsymbol{1}_{(\|\bar{g}_{k}\|>\tau)}\right]

\displaystyle\quad-2\left(\frac{\sqrt{m_{D}}}{3}+\sqrt{M_{D}}\right)\tau\cdot\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\|D_{k}^{1/2}(\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k}))\|\cdot\boldsymbol{1}_{(\|\bar{g}_{k}\|>\tau)}\right]

\displaystyle\geq\tau\frac{\sqrt{m_{D}}}{3}\cdot\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\|D_{k}^{1/2}\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|\right]\cdot\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\boldsymbol{1}_{(\|\bar{g}_{k}\|>\tau)}\right]

\displaystyle\quad-2\tau\left(\frac{\sqrt{m_{D}}}{3}+\sqrt{M_{D}}\right)\cdot\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\|D_{k}^{1/2}(\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k}))\|\cdot\boldsymbol{1}_{(\|\bar{g}_{k}\|>\tau)}\right]

\displaystyle\geq\tau(1-p_{g})\frac{\sqrt{m_{D}}}{3}\cdot\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\|D_{k}^{1/2}\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|\right]-2\tau\left(\frac{\sqrt{m_{D}}}{3}+\sqrt{M_{D}}\right)\cdot\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\|D_{k}^{1/2}(\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k}))\|\right]

\displaystyle\geq\tau(1-p_{g})\frac{\sqrt{m_{D}}}{3}\cdot\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\|D_{k}^{1/2}\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|\right]-2\tau\left(\frac{\sqrt{m_{D}}}{3}+\sqrt{M_{D}}\right)\sqrt{M_{D}}\cdot\sigma.

Combining the above derivations, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{k}[\nabla f(x_{k})^{T}D_{k}\widehat{\bar{g}}_{k}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{k}[\nabla f(x_{k})^{T}D_{k}\bar{g}_{k}\cdot\boldsymbol{1}_{(\|\bar{g}_{k}\|\leq\tau)}]+\tau\cdot\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\nabla f(x_{k})^{T}D_{k}\frac{\bar{g}_{k}}{\|\bar{g}_{k}\|}\cdot\boldsymbol{1}_{(\|\bar{g}_{k}\|>\tau)}\right]

\displaystyle\geq\frac{p_{g}}{2}\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|D_{k}^{1/2}\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}]-\|\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|\frac{M_{D}^{p/2+1}\sigma^{p}}{\left(\sqrt{m_{D}}\cdot\frac{\tau}{4}\right)^{p-1}}

\displaystyle\quad+\tau(1-p_{g})\frac{\sqrt{m_{D}}}{3}\cdot\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\|D_{k}^{1/2}\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|\right]-2\tau\left(\frac{\sqrt{m_{D}}}{3}+\sqrt{M_{D}}\right)\sqrt{M_{D}}\cdot\sigma.

Note that since
\|D_{k}^{1/2}\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|\geq\sqrt{m_{D}}\|\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{k}[\nabla f(x_{k})^{T}D_{k}\widehat{\bar{g}}_{k}]

\displaystyle\geq\frac{p_{g}}{2}\sqrt{m_{D}}\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|D_{k}^{1/2}\nabla f(x_{k})\|\|\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|]-\|\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|\frac{M_{D}^{p/2+1}\sigma^{p}}{\left(\sqrt{m_{D}}\cdot\frac{\tau}{4}\right)^{p-1}}

\displaystyle\quad+\tau(1-p_{g})\frac{\sqrt{m_{D}}}{4}\cdot\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\|D_{k}^{1/2}\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|\right]-2\tau\left(\frac{\sqrt{m_{D}}}{3}+\sqrt{M_{D}}\right)\sqrt{M_{D}}\cdot\sigma.

Since
\|\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|\geq\tau/2
, we further have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{k}[\nabla f(x_{k})^{T}D_{k}\widehat{\bar{g}}_{k}]

\displaystyle\geq\frac{p_{g}}{4}\sqrt{m_{D}}\tau\cdot\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|D_{k}^{1/2}\nabla f(x_{k})\|]-\|\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|\frac{M_{D}^{p/2+1}\sigma^{p}}{\left(\sqrt{m_{D}}\cdot\frac{\tau}{4}\right)^{p-1}}

\displaystyle\quad+\tau(1-p_{g})\frac{\sqrt{m_{D}}}{4}\cdot\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\|D_{k}^{1/2}\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|\right]-2\tau\left(\frac{\sqrt{m_{D}}}{3}+\sqrt{M_{D}}\right)\sqrt{M_{D}}\cdot\sigma

\displaystyle=\tau\frac{\sqrt{m_{D}}}{4}\cdot\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\|D_{k}^{1/2}\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|\right]-\|\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|\frac{M_{D}^{p/2+1}\sigma^{p}}{\left(\sqrt{m_{D}}\cdot\frac{\tau}{4}\right)^{p-1}}-2\tau\left(\frac{\sqrt{m_{D}}}{3}+\sqrt{M_{D}}\right)\sqrt{M_{D}}\cdot\sigma

\displaystyle\geq\tau\frac{m_{D}}{4}\cdot\|\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|-\|\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|\frac{M_{D}^{p/2+1}\sigma^{p}}{\left(\sqrt{m_{D}}\cdot\frac{\tau}{4}\right)^{p-1}}-2\tau\left(\frac{\sqrt{m_{D}}}{3}+\sqrt{M_{D}}\right)\sqrt{M_{D}}\cdot\sigma.

Since we set

\tau\geq\max\left\{2,4\cdot 3^{1/p}\cdot\sigma\left(\frac{M_{D}}{m_{D}}\right)^{\frac{p+1}{2p}},64\sigma\frac{M_{D}}{m_{D}}\right\},

we further have

\frac{M_{D}^{p/2+1}\sigma^{p}}{\left(\sqrt{m_{D}}\cdot\frac{\tau}{4}\right)^{p-1}}\leq\frac{\tau m_{D}}{12},\quad 2\tau\left(\frac{\sqrt{m_{D}}}{3}+\sqrt{M_{D}}\right)\sqrt{M_{D}}\cdot\sigma\leq\frac{\tau^{2}}{24}m_{D}.

These imply

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{k}[\nabla f(x_{k})^{T}D_{k}\widehat{\bar{g}}_{k}]

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\tau m_{D}}{4}\cdot\|\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|-\frac{\tau m_{D}}{12}\|\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|-\frac{\tau^{2}}{24}m_{D}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\tau m_{D}}{4}\cdot\|\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|-\frac{\tau m_{D}}{6}\|\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|

\displaystyle=\frac{\tau m_{D}}{12}\|\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{k}[f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k+1})]

\displaystyle\leq f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})-\eta\mathbb{E}_{k}[\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})^{T}D_{k}\widehat{\bar{g}}_{k}]+\frac{\eta^{2}L}{2}\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|D_{k}\widehat{\bar{g}}_{k}\|^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq-\eta\frac{\tau m_{D}}{12}\|\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|+\frac{\eta^{2}L}{2}\tau^{2}

\displaystyle\leq-\eta\frac{\tau m_{D}}{12}\|\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|+\eta^{2}L\tau\|\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|

\displaystyle\leq-\eta\frac{\tau m_{D}}{24}\|\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|

\displaystyle\leq-\eta\frac{m_{D}}{12}\|\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|.

We thus complete the proof.
∎

Preconditioning-Then-Clipping

Lemma C.5
.

When
\|\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|<\frac{1}{2M_{D}}\tau
, we have
\|\varepsilon_{k}^{b}\|\leq\Upsilon_{1}\sigma^{p}\tau^{1-p}
and
\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|\varepsilon_{k}^{u}\|^{2}]\leq\Upsilon_{2}\tau^{2-p}\sigma^{p}
.
where
\Upsilon_{1}=\frac{M_{D}^{p}}{m_{D}2^{1-p}}
and
\Upsilon_{2}=\frac{9M_{D}^{p}}{m_{D}^{2}}+\left(\frac{3}{2m_{D}}\right)^{2-p}
.

Proof.

By the definition of
\varepsilon_{k}^{b}
, we have

\displaystyle\|\varepsilon_{k}^{b}\|

\displaystyle=\|\mathbb{E}[\widehat{\bar{g}}_{k}-\bar{g}_{k}]\|

\displaystyle=\|\mathbb{E}[(\widehat{\bar{g}}_{k}-\bar{g}_{k})\boldsymbol{1}_{(\|D_{k}\bar{g}_{k}\|\geq\tau)}]\|+\|\mathbb{E}[(\widehat{\bar{g}}_{k}-\bar{g}_{k})\boldsymbol{1}_{(\|D_{k}\bar{g}_{k}\|<\tau)}]\|

\displaystyle=\left\|\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\frac{\tau}{\|D_{k}\bar{g}_{k}\|}\bar{g}_{k}-\bar{g}_{k}\right)\boldsymbol{1}_{(\|D_{k}\bar{g}_{k}\|\geq\tau)}\right]\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\frac{\tau}{\|D_{k}\bar{g}_{k}\|}\bar{g}_{k}-\bar{g}_{k}\right\|\boldsymbol{1}_{(\|D_{k}\bar{g}_{k}\|\geq\tau)}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\|\bar{g}_{k}\|\left(1-\frac{\tau}{\|D_{k}\bar{g}_{k}\|}\right)\boldsymbol{1}_{(\|D_{k}\bar{g}_{k}\|\geq\tau)}\right].

Since
\|\bar{g}_{k}\|\leq\|D_{k}\bar{g}_{k}\|/m_{D}
, we have

\displaystyle\|\varepsilon_{k}^{b}\|

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\|D_{k}\bar{g}_{k}\|}{m_{D}}\left(1-\frac{\tau}{\|D_{k}\bar{g}_{k}\|}\right)\boldsymbol{1}_{(\|D_{k}\bar{g}_{k}\|\geq\tau)}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\frac{\|D_{k}\bar{g}_{k}\|}{m_{D}}-\frac{\tau}{m_{D}}\right)\boldsymbol{1}_{(\|D_{k}\bar{g}_{k}\|\geq\tau)}\right].

Note that
\|D_{k}\bar{g}_{k}\|\leq\|D_{k}(\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k}))\|+\|D_{k}\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|\leq\|D_{k}(\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k}))\|+\tau
, we have

\displaystyle\|\varepsilon_{k}^{b}\|

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\frac{\|D_{k}\bar{g}_{k}\|}{m_{D}}-\frac{\tau}{m_{D}}\right)\boldsymbol{1}_{(\|D_{k}\bar{g}_{k}\|\geq\tau)}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\frac{\|D_{k}(\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k}))\|+\tau}{m_{D}}-\frac{\tau}{m_{D}}\right)\boldsymbol{1}_{(\|D_{k}\bar{g}_{k}\|\geq\tau)}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\|D_{k}(\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k}))\|}{m_{D}}\boldsymbol{1}_{(\|D_{k}\bar{g}_{k}\|\geq\tau)}\right].

Note that
\|D_{k}\bar{g}_{k}\|\geq\tau
implies
\|D_{k}(\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k}))\|\geq\tau/2
, we have

\displaystyle\|\varepsilon_{k}^{b}\|

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\|D_{k}(\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k}))\|}{m_{D}}\boldsymbol{1}_{(\|D_{k}(\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k}))\|\geq\tau/2)}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\|D_{k}(\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k}))\|^{p}}{m_{D}}\|D_{k}(\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k}))\|^{1-p}\boldsymbol{1}_{(\|D_{k}(\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k}))\|\geq\tau/2)}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{M_{D}^{p}\|\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|^{p}}{m_{D}}(\tau/2)^{1-p}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{M_{D}^{p}}{m_{D}2^{1-p}}\sigma^{p}\tau^{1-p}.

Now we turn to
\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|\varepsilon_{k}^{u}\|^{2}]
.

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|\varepsilon_{k}^{u}\|^{2}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|\widehat{\bar{g}}_{k}-\mathbb{E}_{k}[\widehat{\bar{g}}_{k}]\|^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|\widehat{\bar{g}}_{k}-\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|^{2}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|\widehat{\bar{g}}_{k}-\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|^{2}\cdot\boldsymbol{1}_{(\|D_{k}\bar{g}_{k}\|>\tau)}]+\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|\widehat{\bar{g}}_{k}-\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|^{2}\cdot\boldsymbol{1}_{(\|D_{k}\bar{g}_{k}\|\leq\tau)}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\left\|\frac{\tau}{\|D_{k}\bar{g}_{k}\|}\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\right\|^{2}\cdot\boldsymbol{1}_{(\|D_{k}\bar{g}_{k}\|>\tau)}\right]+\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|^{2}\cdot\boldsymbol{1}_{(\|D_{k}\bar{g}_{k}\|\leq\tau)}].

We note that

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{\tau}{\|D_{k}\bar{g}_{k}\|}\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\|\tau\bar{g}_{k}\|}{\|D_{k}\bar{g}_{k}\|}+\|\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|\leq\frac{\tau\|D_{k}\bar{g}_{k}\|}{\|D_{k}\bar{g}_{k}\|m_{D}}+\frac{\tau}{2M_{D}}=\frac{\tau}{m_{D}}+\frac{\tau}{2M_{D}}\leq\frac{3\tau}{2m_{D}},

and

\displaystyle\|\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|\leq\|\bar{g}_{k}\|+\|\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|\leq\frac{\|D_{k}\bar{g}_{k}\|}{m_{D}}+\|\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|\leq\frac{\tau}{m_{D}}+\frac{\tau}{2M_{D}}\leq\frac{3\tau}{2m_{D}}.

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|\varepsilon_{k}^{u}\|^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\left(\frac{3\tau}{2m_{D}}\right)^{2}\cdot\boldsymbol{1}_{(\|D_{k}\bar{g}_{k}\|>\tau)}\right]+\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\left(\frac{3\tau}{2m_{D}}\right)^{2-p}\|\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|^{p}\cdot\boldsymbol{1}_{(\|D_{k}\bar{g}_{k}\|\leq\tau)}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\left(\frac{3\tau}{2m_{D}}\right)^{2}\cdot\boldsymbol{1}_{(\|D_{k}\bar{g}_{k}\|>\tau)}\right]+\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\left(\frac{3\tau}{2m_{D}}\right)^{2-p}\|\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|^{p}\right]

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{3\tau}{2m_{D}}\right)^{2}\cdot\mathbb{E}_{k}[\boldsymbol{1}_{(\|D_{k}\bar{g}_{k}\|>\tau)}]+\left(\frac{3\tau}{2m_{D}}\right)^{2-p}\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|^{p}].

Since
\|D_{k}(\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|\geq\|D_{k}\bar{g}_{k}\|-\|D_{k}\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|\geq\|D_{k}\bar{g}_{k}\|-\|D_{k}\|\frac{\tau}{2M_{D}}\geq\|D_{k}\bar{g}_{k}\|-\frac{\tau}{2}\geq\frac{\tau}{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{k}[\boldsymbol{1}_{(\|D_{k}\bar{g}_{k}\|>\tau)}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{k}[\|D_{k}\bar{g}_{k}\|>\tau]\leq\mathbb{P}_{k}[\|D_{k}(\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|>\tau/2]\leq\frac{\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|D_{k}(\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|^{p}]}{(\tau/2)^{p}}\leq\frac{2^{p}M_{D}^{p}\sigma^{p}}{\tau^{p}}

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|\varepsilon_{k}^{u}\|^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{3\tau}{2m_{D}}\right)^{2}\frac{2^{p}M_{D}^{p}\sigma^{p}}{\tau^{p}}+\left(\frac{3\tau}{2m_{D}}\right)^{2-p}\sigma^{p}=\left[\frac{9M_{D}^{p}}{m_{D}^{2}}+\left(\frac{3}{2m_{D}}\right)^{2-p}\right]\tau^{2-p}\sigma^{p}.

We therefore complete the proof.
∎

Lemma C.6
.

Under Assumptions
4.2
,
4.3
, and
5.1
, when
\|\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|<\frac{\tau}{2M_{D}}

\|\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|>\frac{1}{2M_{D}}\tau
, we have

\|D_{k}^{1/2}\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|^{2}=\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})^{T}D_{k}\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\geq m_{D}\|\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|^{2}\geq\frac{\tau^{2}m_{D}}{4M_{D}},

which imply

\|D_{k}^{1/2}\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|\geq\frac{\tau\sqrt{m_{D}}}{2\sqrt{M_{D}}},\quad\|D_{k}^{1/2}(\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(x_{k}))\|\leq\frac{\tau\sqrt{m_{D}}}{4\sqrt{M_{D}}}\leq\frac{1}{2}\|D_{k}^{1/2}\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|.

Therefore we have

\|D_{k}^{1/2}\nabla f(x_{k})\|\|D_{k}^{1/2}(\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(x_{k}))\|\leq\frac{1}{2}\|D_{k}^{1/2}\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|^{2}.

Further, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|D_{k}^{1/2}\nabla f(x_{k})\|\|D_{k}^{1/2}(\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(x_{k}))\|\cdot\boldsymbol{1}_{(\|D_{k}\bar{g}_{k}\|\leq\tau,\|D_{k}^{1/2}(\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(x_{k}))\|\leq\frac{\tau\sqrt{m_{D}}}{4\sqrt{M_{D}}})}]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|D_{k}^{1/2}\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|^{2}\cdot\boldsymbol{1}_{(\|D_{k}\bar{g}_{k}\|\leq\tau,\|D_{k}^{1/2}(\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(x_{k}))\|\leq\frac{\tau\sqrt{m_{D}}}{4\sqrt{M_{D}}})}]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|D_{k}^{1/2}\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|^{2}\cdot\boldsymbol{1}_{(\|D_{k}\bar{g}_{k}\|\leq\tau)}]

Plugging this into the above formula, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{k}[\nabla f(x_{k})^{T}D_{k}\bar{g}_{k}\cdot\boldsymbol{1}_{(\|D_{k}\bar{g}_{k}\|\leq\tau)}]

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|D_{k}^{1/2}\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}\cdot\boldsymbol{1}_{(\|D_{k}\bar{g}_{k}\|\leq\tau)}]-\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|D_{k}^{1/2}\nabla f(x_{k})\|\|D_{k}^{1/2}(\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(x_{k}))\|\cdot\boldsymbol{1}_{(\|D_{k}^{1/2}(\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(x_{k}))\|>\frac{\tau\sqrt{m_{D}}}{4\sqrt{M_{D}}})}]

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|D_{k}^{1/2}\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}]\cdot\mathbb{E}_{k}[\boldsymbol{1}_{(\|D_{k}\bar{g}_{k}\|\leq\tau)}]-\sqrt{M_{D}}\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|D_{k}^{1/2}(\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(x_{k}))\|\cdot\boldsymbol{1}_{(\|D_{k}^{1/2}(\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(x_{k}))\|>\frac{\tau\sqrt{m_{D}}}{4\sqrt{M_{D}}})}]

\displaystyle=\frac{p_{g}}{2}\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|D_{k}^{1/2}\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}]-\sqrt{M_{D}}\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|D_{k}^{1/2}(\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(x_{k}))\|\cdot\boldsymbol{1}_{(\|D_{k}^{1/2}(\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(x_{k}))\|>\frac{\tau\sqrt{m_{D}}}{4\sqrt{M_{D}}})}].

Now we bound
\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|D_{k}^{1/2}(\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(x_{k}))\|\cdot\boldsymbol{1}_{(\|D_{k}^{1/2}(\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(x_{k}))\|>\frac{\tau\sqrt{m_{D}}}{4\sqrt{M_{D}}})}]
.

\displaystyle M_{D}^{p/2}\sigma^{p}

\displaystyle\geq M_{D}^{p/2}\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|^{p}]\geq\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|D_{k}^{1/2}(\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k}))\|^{p}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|D_{k}^{1/2}(\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k}))\|\cdot\|D_{k}^{1/2}(\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k}))\|^{p-1}]

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|D_{k}^{1/2}(\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k}))\|\cdot\|D_{k}^{1/2}(\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k}))\|^{p-1}\cdot\boldsymbol{1}_{(\|D_{k}^{1/2}(\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(x_{k}))\|>\frac{\tau\sqrt{m_{D}}}{4\sqrt{M_{D}}})}]

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|D_{k}^{1/2}(\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k}))\|\cdot\left(\frac{\tau\sqrt{m_{D}}}{4\sqrt{M_{D}}}\right)^{p-1}\cdot\boldsymbol{1}_{(\|D_{k}^{1/2}(\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(x_{k}))\|>\frac{\tau\sqrt{m_{D}}}{4\sqrt{M_{D}}})}].

Rearranging the terms yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|D_{k}^{1/2}(\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k}))\|\cdot\boldsymbol{1}_{(\|D_{k}^{1/2}(\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(x_{k}))\|>\frac{\tau\sqrt{m_{D}}}{4\sqrt{M_{D}}})}]\leq\frac{M_{D}^{p/2}\sigma^{p}}{\left(\frac{\tau\sqrt{m_{D}}}{4\sqrt{M_{D}}}\right)^{p-1}}.

Thus, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{k}[\nabla f(x_{k})^{T}D_{k}\bar{g}_{k}\cdot\boldsymbol{1}_{(\|D_{k}\bar{g}_{k}\|\leq\tau)}]\geq\frac{p_{g}}{2}\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|D_{k}^{1/2}\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}]-\|\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|\frac{M_{D}^{p/2+1}\sigma^{p}}{\left(\frac{\tau\sqrt{m_{D}}}{4\sqrt{M_{D}}}\right)^{p-1}}.

Next, we examine
\tau\cdot\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\nabla f(x_{k})^{T}D_{k}\frac{\bar{g}_{k}}{\|D_{k}\bar{g}_{k}\|}\cdot\boldsymbol{1}_{(\|D_{k}\bar{g}_{k}\|>\tau)}\right]
.

We first consider the case
\|D_{k}^{1/2}\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|\geq 2\|D_{k}^{1/2}(\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k}))\|
, where we have

\displaystyle\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})^{T}D_{k}\bar{g}_{k}

\displaystyle=\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})^{T}D_{k}\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})+\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})^{T}D_{k}(\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k}))

\displaystyle\geq\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})^{T}D_{k}\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})-\|D_{k}^{1/2}\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|\|D_{k}^{1/2}(\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k}))\|

\displaystyle\geq\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})^{T}D_{k}\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})-\frac{1}{2}\|D_{k}^{1/2}\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\|D_{k}^{1/2}\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|^{2}.

In addition, we have

\displaystyle\|D_{k}^{1/2}(\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})+\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k}))\|

\displaystyle\leq\|D_{k}^{1/2}\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|+\|D_{k}^{1/2}(\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k}))\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{3}{2}\|D_{k}^{1/2}\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|.

Combining them together, we have

\displaystyle\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})^{T}D_{k}\frac{\bar{g}_{k}}{\|D_{k}\bar{g}_{k}\|}

\displaystyle=\frac{\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})^{T}D_{k}(\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})+\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k}))}{\|D_{k}(\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})+\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k}))\|}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})^{T}D_{k}(\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})+\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k}))}{\sqrt{M_{D}}\|D_{k}^{1/2}(\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})+\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k}))\|}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{3\sqrt{M_{D}}}\|D_{k}^{1/2}\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|.

When
\|D_{k}^{1/2}\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|<2\|D_{k}^{1/2}(\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k}))\|
, we have

\displaystyle\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})^{T}D_{k}\frac{\bar{g}_{k}}{\|D_{k}\bar{g}_{k}\|}

\displaystyle\geq-\|D_{k}^{1/2}\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|\frac{\|D_{k}^{1/2}\bar{g}_{k}\|}{\|D_{k}\bar{g}_{k}\|}\geq-\|D_{k}^{1/2}\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|\frac{\|D_{k}\bar{g}_{k}\|}{\sqrt{m_{D}}\|D_{k}\bar{g}_{k}\|}

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{1}{\sqrt{m_{D}}}\|D_{k}^{1/2}\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{3\sqrt{M_{D}}}\|D_{k}^{1/2}\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|-\left(\frac{1}{3\sqrt{M_{D}}}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{m_{D}}}\right)\|D_{k}^{1/2}\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{3\sqrt{M_{D}}}\|D_{k}^{1/2}\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|-2\left(\frac{1}{3\sqrt{M_{D}}}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{m_{D}}}\right)\|D_{k}^{1/2}(\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k}))\|.

Combining the above formulas, we have

\displaystyle\tau\cdot\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\nabla f(x_{k})^{T}D_{k}\frac{\bar{g}_{k}}{\|D_{k}\bar{g}_{k}\|}\cdot\boldsymbol{1}_{(\|D_{k}\bar{g}_{k}\|>\tau)}\right]

\displaystyle\geq\tau\cdot\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\left(\frac{1}{3\sqrt{M_{D}}}\|D_{k}^{1/2}\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|-2\left(\frac{1}{3\sqrt{M_{D}}}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{m_{D}}}\right)\|D_{k}^{1/2}(\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k}))\|\right)\cdot\boldsymbol{1}_{(\|D_{k}\bar{g}_{k}\|>\tau)}\right]

\displaystyle=\tau\frac{1}{3\sqrt{M_{D}}}\cdot\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\|D_{k}^{1/2}\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|\cdot\boldsymbol{1}_{(\|D_{k}\bar{g}_{k}\|>\tau)}\right]

\displaystyle\quad-2\left(\frac{1}{3\sqrt{M_{D}}}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{m_{D}}}\right)\tau\cdot\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\|D_{k}^{1/2}(\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k}))\|\cdot\boldsymbol{1}_{(\|D_{k}\bar{g}_{k}\|>\tau)}\right]

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\tau}{3\sqrt{M_{D}}}\cdot\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\|D_{k}^{1/2}\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|\right]\cdot\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\boldsymbol{1}_{(\|D_{k}\bar{g}_{k}\|>\tau)}\right]

\displaystyle\quad-2\left(\frac{1}{3\sqrt{M_{D}}}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{m_{D}}}\right)\tau\cdot\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\|D_{k}^{1/2}(\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k}))\|\cdot\boldsymbol{1}_{(\|D_{k}\bar{g}_{k}\|>\tau)}\right]

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\tau(1-p_{g})}{3\sqrt{M_{D}}}\cdot\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\|D_{k}^{1/2}\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|\right]-2\tau\left(\frac{1}{3\sqrt{M_{D}}}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{m_{D}}}\right)\cdot\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\|D_{k}^{1/2}(\bar{g}_{k}-\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k}))\|\right]

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\tau(1-p_{g})}{3\sqrt{M_{D}}}\cdot\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\|D_{k}^{1/2}\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|\right]-2\tau\left(\frac{1}{3\sqrt{M_{D}}}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{m_{D}}}\right)\sqrt{M_{D}}\cdot\sigma.

Combining the above derivations, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{k}[\nabla f(x_{k})^{T}D_{k}\widehat{\bar{g}}_{k}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{k}[\nabla f(x_{k})^{T}D_{k}\bar{g}_{k}\cdot\boldsymbol{1}_{(\|D_{k}\bar{g}_{k}\|\leq\tau)}]+\tau\cdot\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\nabla f(x_{k})^{T}D_{k}\frac{\bar{g}_{k}}{\|D_{k}\bar{g}_{k}\|}\cdot\boldsymbol{1}_{(\|D_{k}\bar{g}_{k}\|>\tau)}\right]

\displaystyle\geq\frac{p_{g}}{2}\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|D_{k}^{1/2}\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}]-\|\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|\frac{M_{D}^{p/2+1}\sigma^{p}}{\left(\frac{\tau\sqrt{m_{D}}}{4\sqrt{M_{D}}}\right)^{p-1}}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\tau(1-p_{g})}{3\sqrt{M_{D}}}\cdot\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\|D_{k}^{1/2}\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|\right]-2\tau\left(\frac{1}{3\sqrt{M_{D}}}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{m_{D}}}\right)\sqrt{M_{D}}\cdot\sigma.

Note that since
\|D_{k}^{1/2}\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|\geq\sqrt{m_{D}}\|\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{k}[\nabla f(x_{k})^{T}D_{k}\widehat{\bar{g}}_{k}]

\displaystyle\geq\frac{p_{g}}{2}\sqrt{m_{D}}\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|D_{k}^{1/2}\nabla f(x_{k})\|\|\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|]-\|\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|\frac{M_{D}^{p/2+1}\sigma^{p}}{\left(\frac{\tau\sqrt{m_{D}}}{4\sqrt{M_{D}}}\right)^{p-1}}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\tau(1-p_{g})}{3\sqrt{M_{D}}}\cdot\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\|D_{k}^{1/2}\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|\right]-2\tau\left(\frac{1}{3\sqrt{M_{D}}}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{m_{D}}}\right)\sqrt{M_{D}}\cdot\sigma.

Since
\|\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|\geq\frac{\tau}{2M_{D}}
, we further have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{k}[\nabla f(x_{k})^{T}D_{k}\widehat{\bar{g}}_{k}]

\displaystyle\geq\frac{p_{g}}{4M_{D}}\sqrt{m_{D}}\tau\cdot\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|D_{k}^{1/2}\nabla f(x_{k})\|]-\|\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|\frac{M_{D}^{p/2+1}\sigma^{p}}{\left(\frac{\tau\sqrt{m_{D}}}{4\sqrt{M_{D}}}\right)^{p-1}}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\tau(1-p_{g})}{3\sqrt{M_{D}}}\cdot\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\|D_{k}^{1/2}\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|\right]-2\tau\left(\frac{1}{3\sqrt{M_{D}}}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{m_{D}}}\right)\sqrt{M_{D}}\cdot\sigma

\displaystyle\geq\frac{p_{g}}{4M_{D}}\sqrt{m_{D}}\tau\cdot\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|D_{k}^{1/2}\nabla f(x_{k})\|]-\|\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|\frac{M_{D}^{p/2+1}\sigma^{p}}{\left(\frac{\tau\sqrt{m_{D}}}{4\sqrt{M_{D}}}\right)^{p-1}}

\displaystyle\geq\tau\frac{m_{D}}{4M_{D}}\cdot\|\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|-\|\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|\frac{M_{D}^{p/2+1}\sigma^{p}}{\left(\frac{\tau\sqrt{m_{D}}}{4\sqrt{M_{D}}}\right)^{p-1}}-2\tau\left(\frac{1}{3\sqrt{M_{D}}}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{m_{D}}}\right)\sqrt{M_{D}}\cdot\sigma.

\tau=\max\left\{2,4\cdot 3^{1/p}\cdot\sigma\left(\frac{M_{D}^{1+\frac{3}{2p}}}{m_{D}^{\frac{p+1}{2p}}}\right),64\sigma\frac{M_{D}^{\frac{5}{2}}}{m_{D}^{\frac{3}{2}}}\right\},

\frac{M_{D}^{p/2+1}\sigma^{p}}{\left(\frac{\tau\sqrt{m_{D}}}{4\sqrt{M_{D}}}\right)^{p-1}}\leq\frac{\tau m_{D}}{12M_{D}},\quad 2\tau\left(\frac{1}{3\sqrt{M_{D}}}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{m_{D}}}\right)\sqrt{M_{D}}\cdot\sigma\leq\frac{\tau^{2}m_{D}}{24M_{D}^{2}}.

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{k}[\nabla f(x_{k})^{T}D_{k}\widehat{\bar{g}}_{k}]

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\tau m_{D}}{4M_{D}}\cdot\|\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|-\frac{\tau m_{D}}{12M_{D}}\|\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|-\frac{\tau^{2}m_{D}}{24M_{D}^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\tau m_{D}}{4M_{D}}\cdot\|\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|-\frac{\tau m_{D}}{6M_{D}}\|\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|

\displaystyle=\frac{\tau m_{D}}{12M_{D}}\|\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|.

Plugging the above result to the Taylor expansion, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{k}[f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k+1})]

\displaystyle\leq f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})-\eta\mathbb{E}_{k}[\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})^{T}D_{k}\widehat{\bar{g}}_{k}]+\frac{\eta^{2}L}{2}\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|D_{k}\widehat{\bar{g}}_{k}\|^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq-\eta\frac{\tau m_{D}}{12M_{D}}\|\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|+\frac{\eta^{2}L}{2}\tau^{2}

\displaystyle\leq-\eta\frac{\tau m_{D}}{12M_{D}}\|\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|+\eta^{2}L\tau M_{D}\|\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|

\displaystyle\leq-\eta\frac{\tau m_{D}}{24M_{D}}\|\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|

\displaystyle\leq-\eta\frac{m_{D}}{12M_{D}}\|\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\|.

We thus complete the proof.
∎

Proof of Lemma
D.1

Proof.

Following (Kornilov et al., 2024), we define
g\sim\mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{0},I)
and
y_{j}:=g^{T}X_{j}
, where
g
is independent of
X_{j}
. We need to verify that
y_{j}
defined this way satisfies the conditions in Lemma
D.2
.

We define the sigma algebra
\mathcal{H}_{1}\coloneqq\sigma(y_{j-1},\dots,y_{1})
and
\mathcal{H}_{2}:=\sigma(X_{j-1},\dots,X_{1},g)
. Since
\mathcal{H}_{1}\subset\mathcal{H}_{2}

\mathbb{E}[g^{T}X_{j}|\mathcal{H}_{1}]=\mathbb{E}[\mathbb{E}[g^{T}X_{j}|\mathcal{H}_{2}]|\mathcal{H}_{1}]=\mathbb{E}[g^{T}\mathbb{E}[X_{j}|\mathcal{H}_{2}]|\mathcal{H}_{1}]=0,

where the last equality holds by independence of
X_{j}
and
g
, and the assumption that
\mathbb{E}[X_{j}\mid X_{1:j-1}]=0
.

Next, we verify that
\mathbb{E}[|y_{j}|^{p}]<\infty
. Note that
g^{T}a\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\|a\|^{2})
for any vector
a\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we have

, where we used the
p

g^{T}X_{j}
given
X_{j}
. Taking full expectation, we get

\mathbb{E}[|y_{j}|^{p}]=C(p)\mathbb{E}[\|X_{j}\|^{p}]<\infty.

(8)

We have verified that the sequence
y_{j}
satisfies the conditions in Lemma
D.2
.

Using the
p

given
\sum_{k=0}^{T-1}X_{k}
, we have

C(p)\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\sum_{k=0}^{T-1}X_{k}\right\|^{p}\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{E}\left[\left|g^{T}\sum_{k=0}^{T-1}X_{k}\right|^{p}\mid\sum_{k=0}^{T-1}X_{k}\right]\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\sum_{j=1}^{n}y_{j}\right|^{p}\right]\leq 2^{2-p}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}[|y_{j}|^{p}],

Taking expectation on both sides, we have

\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\sum_{k=0}^{n}X_{k}\right|^{p}\right]\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}X_{k}\right|^{p}\right]+p\mathbb{E}\left[\text{sgn}(\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}X_{k})\left|\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}X_{k}\right|^{p-1}\cdot X_{n}\right]+2^{2-p}\mathbb{E}\left[|X_{n}|^{p}\right].

Using

\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\sum_{k=0}^{n}X_{k}\right|^{p}\right]\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}X_{k}\right|^{p}\right]+2^{2-p}\mathbb{E}\left[|X_{n}|^{p}\right].

We complete the proof by induction.

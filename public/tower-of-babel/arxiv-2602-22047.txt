Stochastic Optimal Control with Side Information and Bayesian Learning
Johannes Milz, Alexander Shapiro, Enlu Zhou

Abstract
We study infinite-horizon stochastic optimal control problems with observable side information: a Markov chain that modulates an unknown context-conditional randomness distribution. Since this distribution is unknown, we propose a Bayesian reformulation based on a parametric density model and posterior predictive dynamics, which yields a Bayesian Bellman equation. We prove posterior consistency under Markov samples and, under correct specification and identifiability, uniform convergence of the Bayesian value function. Finally, we establish Bernstein–von Mises-type asymptotic normality for the data-driven contextual optimal value.



1
Introduction

Contextual optimization addresses decision making under uncertainty with side information (also known as context or covariate) available at the time of decision. In its static (one‑stage) form, the decision maker observes a context and then chooses an action that optimizes the conditional expected performance. To learn the optimal decision rule that maps from an observed context to the optimal action, there are several different paradigms, including decision rule optimization, sequential learning and optimization, and integrated learning and optimization (see the recent paper
[
18
]
for a comprehensive survey).

Contextual optimization has also been studied in multi‑stage settings where a state evolves over time under a policy and the context may shape rewards and transition kernels from stage to stage. Several existing models instantiate dynamic contextual optimization. In the contextual multi‑armed bandit, the learner repeatedly observes a context and selects an arm, receiving bandit feedback while seeking low regret (see, e.g.,
[
13
]
). In contextual Markov decision processes (CMDPs), an episode is governed by an observed, typically static context that parameterizes both transitions and rewards; foundational work established this model and studied planning/learning guarantees, while subsequent results provided regret and sample‑complexity bounds under realizability assumptions and oracle‑based regression
[
12
,
14
,
7
]
. When the context evolves within an episode, Dynamic Contextual MDPs (DCMDPs) allow history‑dependent or time‑varying contexts and analyze algorithm’s regret under additional structure (e.g., logistic DCMDPs)
[
25
]
. A closely related line of research models the context as a finite‑state Markov chain (often called a regime or mode), yielding Markov‑jump/Markov‑switching systems (MJLS) in which mode‑dependent dynamics are controlled via dynamic programming or coupled Riccati equations; classical treatments typically assume the mode transition matrix and noise statistics are known rather than learned from data (see, e.g.,
[
6
,
11
,
9
]
).
A related stream studies multi-period inventory control under Markov-modulated demand,
with known demand distribution driven by a Markov chain
[
4
,
24
,
8
,
1
,
5
,
16
,
19
]
.

In this paper, we introduce a contextual stochastic optimal control problem, in which (i) the context is observable and evolves according to a Markov chain, and (ii) the distribution of the exogenous randomness, conditional on the context, is unknown and must be learned from sequential data. To estimate this conditional distribution, we posit a regression model and learn its parameters with a Bayesian approach, then solve a Bayesian average estimate of the original control problem. Compared to CMDPs and DCMDPs, our formulation departs by imposing Markovian context dynamics (rather than per-episode static or history-dependent context) and our approach hinges on Bayesian learning of the conditional distribution. Unlike classical MJLS, which presumes known context transition probabilities and randomness distributions, we learn the context‑conditional randomness distribution from data and integrate Bayesian learning into decision making. Conceptually, our approach can be viewed as extending the episodic Bayesian optimal control in
[
23
]
to a Markovian contextual setting.

We summarize our contributions as follows:

•

We introduce a new model for stochastic optimal control with side information (observed context) and unknown randomness distribution. It provides a modeling framework for a wide range of application problems. For example, it can address portfolio optimization with side information such as time-varying economy factors.

•

We propose a reformulation of the original problem by incorporating Bayesian learning of the context-conditional randomness distribution. The Bayesian learning approach sequentially updates the posterior distribution with observed context-randomness data, and the Bayesian average estimate of the cost function serves a surrogate of the original (unknown) objective.

•

We theoretically show that the optimal value functions of the reformulated problem converge uniformly to the true value function as the data size increases. We also develop a Bernstein–von Mises-type asymptotic normality result for the data-driven contextual optimal value.

Bayesian reformulation.

\mathsf{p}_{N}(\theta)=\frac{\mathsf{f}_{N}(\theta)p(\theta)}{\int_{\Theta}\mathsf{f}_{N}(\theta)p(\theta)\,\mathrm{d}\theta},\qquad\text{where}\qquad\mathsf{f}_{N}(\theta)\coloneqq f(\xi_{1}|\eta_{1},\theta)\prod_{i=2}^{N}f(\xi_{i}|\eta_{i},\theta)\ \varpi_{\eta_{i-1},\eta_{i}}.

(5)

Consistency

We analyze Bayesian learning and control in the Markovian context setting.
We introduce notation and probability laws necessary for our Bayesian analysis.
Subsequently, we prove posterior consistency with Markov samples and the

3.1
Consistency of Bayesian posterior with Markov samples

It is well known that Bayesian posterior is consistent with independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) samples (see, e.g.,
[
26
]
). In our setting, the data samples
\{(\xi_{i},\eta_{i})\}_{i\geq 1}
are not i.i.d. because
\{\eta_{i}\}_{i\geq 1}
is generated from a Markov chain. Below we show that Bayesian consistency still holds with the Markov samples.

Suppose that the Markov chain (
3
) admits a stationary distribution

\nu_{\eta}
. Then, as noted above, the joint process
\{(\xi_{t},\eta_{t})\}_{t\geq 1}

is a Markov chain with stationary distribution

P^{*}
.
For
\theta\in\Theta
, define the population log-likelihood

\psi(\theta)\coloneqq\mathbb{E}_{P^{*}}\left[\log f(\xi|\eta,\theta)\right]=\sum_{\eta\in\mathcal{H}}\nu_{\eta}(\eta)\int_{\Xi}\log f(\xi|\eta,\theta)\,q(\xi|\eta)\,\mathrm{d}\xi.

(9)

Then, up to an additive constant independent of
\theta
,

-\psi(\theta)
is the conditional Kullback–Leibler (KL) divergence from
q(\cdot|\eta)
to
f(\cdot|\eta,\theta)
averaged under the distribution
\nu_{\eta}
.
Let

\Theta^{*}\coloneqq\mathrm{argmax}_{\theta\in\Theta}\,\psi(\theta)=\mathrm{argmin}_{\theta\in\Theta}\sum_{\eta\in\mathcal{H}}\nu_{\eta}(\eta)\int_{\Xi}q(\xi|\eta)\log\!\left(\frac{q(\xi|\eta)}{f(\xi|\eta,\theta)}\right)\!\mathrm{d}\xi.

(10)

We define the empirical log-likelihood as

\phi_{N}(\theta)\coloneqq\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\log f(\xi_{i}|\eta_{i},\theta).

We now make the following assumptions.

Assumption 3.1
.

(i)
The parameter set
\Theta\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is compact with nonempty interior.

(ii)
The prior density
p(\theta)
is bounded and bounded away from
0
on
\Theta
: there exist constants
c_{1}\geq c_{2}>0
such that

c_{1}\geq p(\theta)\geq c_{2}
for all
\theta\in\Theta
.

(iii)
For every
(\xi,\eta)\in\Xi\times\mathcal{H}
,
f(\xi|\eta,\theta)>0
for all
\theta\in\Theta
.

(iv)

For every
(\xi,\eta)\in\Xi\times\mathcal{H}
,

\theta\mapsto f(\xi|\eta,\theta)
is continuous on
\Theta
.

(v)
The Markov chain
\{\eta_{t}\}_{t\geq 1}
is irreducible and aperiodic.

(vi)

\log f(\xi|\eta,\theta)
is dominated by an integrable function with respect to
P^{*}
.

Now, we demonstrate that the empirical log-likelihood satisfies a uniform law
of large numbers (LLN) under the true Markov data-generating process.

Lemma 3.1
.

Under
3.1
(i,v,vi)
, the following uniform LLN holds:

\lim_{N\to\infty}\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\left|\phi_{N}(\theta)-\psi(\theta)\right|=0,\;\;\mathbb{P}^{*}\text{-almost surely.}

Proof.

3.1
(v) implies
\{\eta_{t}\}_{t\geq 1}
is a (geometrically) ergodic Markov chain, and moreover,
\{(\xi_{t},\eta_{t})\}_{t\geq 1}
is also ergodic. Hence, for each fixed
\theta\in\Theta
, regardless of the initial condition
(\xi_{1},\eta_{1})
,
\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}|\phi_{N}(\theta)-\psi(\theta)|=0
,
\mathbb{P}^{*}
-almost surely. With the dominated integrability of
\log f(\xi|\eta,\theta)
and the compactness of
\Theta
, the LLN holds uniformly over
\Theta
.
∎

3.2
Consistency of value functions

We now turn to consistency of the Bayesian value functions under correct
specification and identifiability of the randomness model.

Definition 3.1
.

It is said that the model is correctly specified if there exists
\theta^{*}\in\Theta
such that
q(\cdot|\eta)=f(\cdot|\eta,\theta^{*})
for all
\eta\in\mathcal{H}
.
It is said that the model is identifiable (at
\theta^{*}
) if

f(\cdot|\eta,\theta^{*})=f(\cdot|\eta,\theta^{\prime})
,
\theta^{\prime}\in\Theta
, implies
\theta^{\prime}=\theta^{*}
.

From now on, we assume that the model is correctly specified and identifiable at
\theta^{*}
.
Recall that
\Theta^{*}=\mathrm{argmax}_{\theta\in\Theta}\,\psi(\theta)=\mathrm{argmax}_{\theta\in\Theta}\mathbb{E}_{P^{*}}[\log f(\xi,\eta|\theta)]
. Since
\mathbb{E}_{P^{*}}[\log f(\xi,\eta|\theta)]
only differs from
\psi(\theta)
by a constant independent of
\theta

Theorem

3.1
implies that the Bayesian posterior
\mathsf{p}_{N}
almost surely converges to a
\theta^{*}
that maximizes
\mathbb{E}_{P^{*}}[\log f(\xi,\eta|\theta)]
, the population log-likelihood of the context-randomness pair

In the correctly specified and identifiable case, we have

Assumption 3.2
.

The model is correctly specified and the true parameter
\theta^{*}\in\mathrm{int}(\Theta)

We demonstrate the asymptotic consistency of the

towards

.

Proposition 3.1
.

If
3.1
and
3.2

We establish

using the following lemma,
which shows that, for each context state

, the posterior

Recall that the cost function

Lemma 3.3
.

If
3.1
and
3.2

then for all

,
\mathbb{P}^{*}

\mathbb{E}_{\theta\sim\mathsf{p}_{N}}\Big[\int_{\Xi}|f(\xi|\eta,\theta)-f(\xi|\eta,\theta^{*})|\,\mathrm{d}\xi\Big]\to 0,\;\;as\;N\to\infty.

Proof.

Fix
\eta\in\mathcal{H}
.
Let
\varepsilon>0

Standard arguments (see, e.g., eqns. (22) and (23), and p. 6 in

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\theta\sim\mathsf{p}_{N}}\Big[\int_{\Xi}|f(\xi|\eta,\theta)-f(\xi|\eta,\theta^{*})|\,\mathrm{d}\xi\Big]\leq\sup_{\theta\in\overline{B}_{\varepsilon}(\theta^{*})}\int_{\Xi}\big|f(\xi|\eta,\theta)-f(\xi|\eta,\theta^{*})\big|\,\mathrm{d}\xi+2\,\mathsf{P}_{N}(\overline{B}_{\varepsilon}(\theta^{*})^{c}).

(13)

Proof of
Proposition

3.1
.

The proof is inspired by those of Propositions 1 and 3 in
[
23
]
.
We define the Bellman operators

\mathcal{T}
,
\mathcal{T}_{N}\colon\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{H})\to\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{H})

by

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} (x,\eta)&\coloneqq\inf_{u\in\mathcal{U}}\;\mathbb{E}_{\xi\sim q(\cdot|\eta)}\Big[c(x,u,\xi)+\gamma\,\mathbb{E}_{\eta^{\prime}|\eta}\big[V(F(x,u,\xi),\eta^{\prime})\big]\Big],\\
[\mathcal{T}_{N}V](x,\eta)&\coloneqq\inf_{u\in\mathcal{U}}\;\mathbb{E}_{\mathsf{p}_{N}|\eta}\Big[c(x,u,\xi)+\gamma\,\mathbb{E}_{\eta^{\prime}|\eta}\big[V(F(x,u,\xi),\eta^{\prime})\big]\Big].\end{aligned}

(14)

Since
\gamma\in(0,1)
,
the Bellman operators
\mathcal{T}
and
\mathcal{T}_{N}

are
\gamma
-contractions. This ensures the standard error bound
(see, e.g., eq. (19) in
[
23

\displaystyle\|V^{*}_{N}-V^{*}\|_{\infty}\leq(1-\gamma)^{-1}\|\mathcal{T}_{N}V^{*}-\mathcal{T}V^{*}\|_{\infty}.

For each fixed

we define

Z_{(x,\eta,u)}(\xi)\coloneqq c(x,u,\xi)+\gamma\mathbb{E}_{\eta^{\prime}|\eta}\big[V^{*}(F(x,u,\xi),\eta^{\prime})\big]

on

Since

are bounded, there exists a constant

for all

\displaystyle\Big|[\mathcal{T}_{N}V^{*}](x,\eta)-[\mathcal{T}V^{*}](x,\eta)\Big|\leq C\ \mathbb{E}_{\theta\sim\mathsf{p}_{N}}\Big[\int_{\Xi}|f(\xi|\eta,\theta)-f(\xi|\eta,\theta^{*})|\,\mathrm{d}\xi\Big].

Combined with

4
Asymptotics of the contextual optimal value

To show the asymptotic convergence rate of the contextual optimal value
V^{*}_{N}
, we develop a Bernstein–von Mises Theorem for the optimal value of the contextual
infinite horizon Bayesian control problem.

4.1
Bernstein–von Mises Limits for Markov chains

Inspired by
[
3
,
10
]
, we formulate
Bernstein–von Mises limits for Markov chains. Acknowledging the fact that the
precise assumptions required to formulate a Bernstein–von Mises theorem
for Markov chains are technical and rather lengthy, we restrict our attention
to two Bernstein–von Mises limits stated without proof. We provide
sufficient technical background to make these formulations meaningful.
In the next section, we establish Bernstein–von Mises-type limits for the
contextual optimal value, assuming that the results stated in this
section apply.
The Bernstein–von Mises theorems in
[
3
,
10
]

apply only to one-dimensional parameter spaces.

Let

Z=\{Z_{t}\}_{t\geq 1}
be a time-homogeneous Markov chain
with measurable state space
\mathcal{Z}

with arbitrary initial distribution.
For each
\theta\in\Theta
, the Markov
chain is specified by transition probabilities

h(z^{\prime}|z,\theta)
for
z,z^{\prime}\in\mathcal{Z}
.
Given observations
Z_{i}
,
i=1,\ldots,N
, and a prior density
r(\theta)

on
\Theta
, the posterior density is given by

\mathsf{r}_{N}(\theta)=\frac{\mathsf{h}_{N}(\theta)r(\theta)}{\int_{\Theta}\mathsf{h}_{N}(\theta)r(\theta)\,\mathrm{d}\theta},\qquad\text{where}\qquad\mathsf{h}_{N}(\theta)\coloneqq\prod_{i=2}^{N}h(Z_{i}|Z_{i-1},\theta),

where we assume that
\int_{\Theta}\mathsf{h}_{N}(\theta)r(\theta)\,\mathrm{d}\theta>0
.

Let
\theta^{*}\in\mathrm{int}(\Theta)
be the true parameter, that is,

Z_{i}|Z_{i-1}=z\sim h(\cdot|z,\theta^{*})
for
i=2,3,\ldots
.
We assume that
\theta\mapsto h(y|z,\theta)
is differentiable at

\theta^{*}
for all
(y,z)\in\mathcal{Z}\times\mathcal{Z}
such that
h(y|z,\theta^{*})>0
.
We define the Fisher information matrix at
\theta^{*}
by

\displaystyle\mathfrak{I}(\theta^{*})\coloneqq\mathbb{E}_{(Z_{1},Z_{2})\sim\mu_{\theta^{*}}(\cdot)h(\cdot|\cdot,\theta^{*})}\big[\nabla_{\theta}\log h(Z_{2}|Z_{1},\theta^{*})\nabla_{\theta}\log h(Z_{2}|Z_{1},\theta^{*})^{\top}\big],

(15)

where the expectation is taken over the joint law of
(Z_{1},Z_{2})
,
and
\mu_{\theta^{*}}
denotes a stationary distribution of the Markov chain under the
true parameter
\theta^{*}
.

We assume that
\mathfrak{I}(\theta^{*})
is invertible, and let
\phi
be the density of

\mathcal{N}(0,\mathfrak{I}(\theta^{*})^{-1})
.
Let
\hat{\theta}_{N}
be the corresponding maximum likelihood estimator, and let

\mathsf{r}_{N}^{*}
denote the posterior density of the local
parameter
\tau=N^{1/2}(\theta-\hat{\theta}_{N})
,
and let
K
be a nonnegative, measurable function.
Under certain regularity
conditions (cf.
[
3
,
10
]
), almost surely

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}K(\tau)\big|\mathsf{r}_{N}^{*}(\tau)-\phi(\tau)\big|\mathrm{d}\tau=0,\;\;\mathfrak{P}^{*}\text{-almost-surely},

(16)

where
\mathfrak{P}^{*}
is the law of

\{Z_{t}\}_{t\geq 1}
under the true parameter

\theta^{*}
.
Following
[
2
, Theorem 2.3]
, under certain assumptions, differentiability and integrability conditions on
g
, a suitable choice of
K
,
the limit in (
16
) implies the Delta Method Bernstein–von Mises limit,

\displaystyle N^{1/2}\big(\mathbb{E}_{\theta\sim\mathsf{r}_{N}}[g(\theta)]-g(\theta^{*})\big)\overset{\mathfrak{P}^{*}}{\rightsquigarrow}\mathcal{N}\big(0,\nabla g(\theta^{*})^{\top}\mathfrak{I}(\theta^{*})^{-1}\nabla g(\theta^{*})\big).

(17)

4.2
Asymptotics of the contextual optimal value

This section establishes Bernstein–von Mises-type asymptotics for the
data-driven contextual optimal value. Our asymptotic results are based on
(
17
).

Note that
\xi_{t}
, conditioned on
\eta_{t}
, has density
f(\cdot|\eta_{t},\theta)

and
\{\eta_{t}\}_{t\geq 1}
evolves according to a (parameter-free)
Markov kernel on
\mathcal{H}
.

Assumption 4.1
.

(i)

There exists
\epsilon>0
such that
B_{\epsilon}(\theta^{*})\subseteq\Theta
,
and for all
(\xi,\eta)\in\Xi\times\mathcal{H}
,

f(\xi|\eta,\cdot)

is continuously differentiable on
B_{\epsilon}(\theta^{*})

(ii)

We have

\sup_{\theta\in\overline{B}_{\epsilon/2}(\theta^{*})}\,\sup_{t\geq 1}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}_{\theta}}\big[\|s(\xi_{t}|\eta_{t},\theta)\|_{2}^{2}\big]<\infty
,
where

(18)

(iii)

The policy
\pi^{*}
of the original problem (
4

We verify
4.1
(i)–(ii) for a regression model.

Example 1
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a finite set of vectors, and
let
g(\eta_{t},\theta)\coloneqq A\eta_{t}+b
with
\theta=(A,b)
.
For a symmetric positive definite matrix
\Sigma
, we consider

\xi_{t}=g(\eta_{t},\theta)+\varepsilon_{t},\;\;\varepsilon_{t}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\Sigma).

(19)

Then
\xi_{t}\sim\mathcal{N}(g(\eta_{t},\theta),\Sigma)
and

f_{t}(\xi_{t}|\eta_{t},\theta)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d/2}|\Sigma|^{1/2}}\exp\big\{-(\xi_{t}-g(\eta_{t},\theta))^{\top}\Sigma^{-1}(\xi_{t}-g(\eta_{t},\theta))/2\big\}.

We define

\ell(\xi,\eta,\theta)\coloneqq\xi-A\eta-b
. We have

\nabla_{b}\log f(\xi|\eta,\theta)=\Sigma^{-1}\ell(\xi,\eta,\theta),\qquad\nabla_{A}\log f(\xi|\eta,\theta)=\Sigma^{-1}\ell(\xi,\eta,\theta)\,\eta^{\top}.

Under
\mathbb{P}_{\theta}
, we have
\xi_{t}=A\eta_{t}+b+\varepsilon_{t}
with

\varepsilon_{t}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\Sigma)
, so

\ell(\xi_{t},\eta_{t},\theta)=\varepsilon_{t}
.
Since
\mathcal{H}
is finite,

4.1
(i)–(ii) is satisfied.
∎

Let
3.1
,
3.2
and
4.1

hold.
For fixed
(x_{1},\eta_{1})\in\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{H}
, we define the optimal
discounted return,
W^{\pi^{*}}(x_{1},\eta_{1})
, and the cumulative score up to time
T
,

S_{\theta,T}
, by

W^{\pi^{*}}(x_{1},\eta_{1})\coloneqq\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}\gamma^{t-1}c\big(x_{t},\pi^{*}(x_{t},\eta_{t}),\xi_{t}\big),\qquad S_{\theta,T}\coloneqq\sum_{t=1}^{T}s(\xi_{t}|\eta_{t},\theta).

(20)

For
(x,\eta,\theta)\in\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{H}\times\Theta
,
and
V\in\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{H})
,
we also define

\displaystyle[\mathcal{T}_{\theta}^{\pi^{*}}V](x,\eta)\coloneqq\mathbb{E}_{\xi\sim f(\cdot|\eta,\theta)}\Big[c(x,\pi^{*}(x,\eta),\xi)+\gamma\,\mathbb{E}_{\eta^{\prime}|\eta}\big[V(F(x,\pi^{*}(x,\eta),\xi),\eta^{\prime})\big]\Big].

Since
\gamma\in(0,1)
,

\mathcal{T}_{\theta}^{\pi^{*}}
is a
\gamma
-contraction operator.
Let
V_{\theta}^{\pi^{*}}
be the unique solution to

V_{\theta}^{\pi^{*}}=\mathcal{T}_{\theta}^{\pi^{*}}V_{\theta}^{\pi^{*}}
.
Then

V_{\theta}^{\pi^{*}}=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}_{\theta}}[W^{\pi^{*}}(x_{1},\eta_{1})]
,
and
V^{*}=V_{\theta^{*}}^{\pi^{*}}
under a correctly specified, identifiable model.

Our next lemma shows that the expected long-run discounted performance under the optimal policy is a differentiable function of the model parameter, and it provides an explicit gradient formula.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
3.1
,
3.2
and
4.1
hold,
and let
(x_{1},\eta_{1})\in\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{H}
.
Then
g(\theta)\coloneqq V_{\theta}^{\pi^{*}}(x_{1},\eta_{1})

is differentiable in a neighborhood of
\theta^{*}
with

\nabla g(\theta)=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}_{\theta}}[\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}\gamma^{t-1}c(x_{t},\pi^{*}(x_{t},\eta_{t}),\xi_{t})S_{\theta,t}]
.

Proof.

We use the identity
g(\theta)=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}_{\theta}}[W^{\pi^{*}}(x_{1},\eta_{1})]
.
For
T\in\mathbb{N}
, we define

\boldsymbol{c}_{t}\coloneqq c(x_{t},\pi^{*}(x_{t},\eta_{t}),\xi_{t}),\quad\boldsymbol{s}_{t}\coloneqq s(\xi_{t}|\eta_{t},\theta),\quad W_{T}(x_{1},\eta_{1})\coloneqq\sum_{t=1}^{T}\gamma^{t-1}\,\boldsymbol{c}_{t},\quad g_{T}(\theta)\coloneqq\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}_{\theta}}[W_{T}(x_{1},\eta_{1})].

We show that
g_{T}
is differentiable on
B_{\epsilon}(\theta^{*})

with gradient
\nabla g_{T}(\theta)=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}_{\theta}}[W_{T}(x_{1},\eta_{1})S_{\theta,T}]
.
Since
\{\eta_{t}\}_{t\geq 1}
is parameter free,
the likelihood of
\{(\xi_{t},\eta_{t})\}_{1\leq t\leq T}
factors as

\prod_{t=1}^{T}f(\xi_{t}|\eta_{t},\theta)
times a factor independent of

\theta
. Hence

\nabla_{\theta}\log\Big(\prod_{t=1}^{T}f(\xi_{t}|\eta_{t},\theta)\Big)=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\nabla_{\theta}\log f(\xi_{t}|\eta_{t},\theta)=S_{\theta,T}.

Because
W_{T}
is bounded,
W_{T}S_{\theta,T}
is integrable.
Applying Theorem 9.56 in
[
20
]
, we obtain

\nabla g_{T}(\theta)=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}_{\theta}}[W_{T}(x_{1},\eta_{1})S_{\theta,T}]
.
We have
\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}_{\theta}}[\boldsymbol{s}_{t}|\eta_{[t]},\xi_{[t-1]}]=0
.
For
t^{\prime}>t
,

\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}_{\theta}}[\boldsymbol{c}_{t}\boldsymbol{s}_{t^{\prime}}]=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}_{\theta}}[\boldsymbol{c}_{t}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}_{\theta}}[\boldsymbol{s}_{t^{\prime}}|\eta_{[t^{\prime}]},\xi_{[t^{\prime}-1]}]]=0
.
We have

\displaystyle\nabla g_{T}(\theta)=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{t^{\prime}=1}^{T}\gamma^{t-1}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}_{\theta}}[\boldsymbol{c}_{t}\boldsymbol{s}_{t^{\prime}}]=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\gamma^{t-1}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}_{\theta}}[\boldsymbol{c}_{t}S_{\theta,t}].

Since
c
is bounded, there exists a constant
C>0

such that
|c(x,u,\xi)|\leq C
for all
(x,u,\xi)\in\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{U}\times\Xi
.
Hence, for all
\theta\in\Theta
,

\left|g(\theta)-g_{T}(\theta)\right|\leq\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}_{\theta}}[\left|W^{\pi^{*}}(x_{1},\eta_{1})-W_{T}(x_{1},\eta_{1})\right|]\leq C(1-\gamma)^{-1}\gamma^{T}
,
implying that
g_{T}\to g
uniformly on
\Theta
.

Next, we show that
\nabla g_{T}
converges to
h
uniformly on
\overline{B}_{\epsilon/2}(\theta^{*})
, where

h(\theta)\coloneqq\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}\gamma^{t-1}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}_{\theta}}[\boldsymbol{c}_{t}S_{\theta,t}]
.
We have

\displaystyle\|\nabla g_{T}(\theta)-h(\theta)\|_{2}\leq C\sum_{t=T+1}^{\infty}\gamma^{t-1}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}_{\theta}}[\|S_{\theta,t}\|_{2}]\leq C\sup_{t\geq 1}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}_{\theta}}\big[\|s(\xi_{t}|\eta_{t},\theta)\|_{2}\big]\sum_{t=T+1}^{\infty}t\gamma^{t-1}.

Hence,
\nabla g_{T}\to h
uniformly on
\overline{B}_{\epsilon/2}(\theta^{*})
,
yielding
\nabla g(\theta)=h(\theta)
.
∎

Remark 3
.

As an alternative to
Lemma

4.1
, the gradient

\nabla g(\theta)=\nabla_{\theta}V_{\theta}^{\pi^{*}}(x_{1},\eta_{1})
can be
characterized via a fixed-point equation.
Specifically, we may apply the implicit function
in
B_{\epsilon/2}(\theta^{*})\times\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{H})
.
to the fixed-point equation

V_{\theta}^{\pi^{*}}=\mathcal{T}_{\theta}^{\pi^{*}}V_{\theta}^{\pi^{*}}
.
Formally, we obtain the linearized fixed-point equation:
for all
(x,\eta,\theta)\in\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{H}\times B_{\epsilon/2}(\theta^{*})
,

\displaystyle\nabla_{\theta}V_{\theta}^{\pi^{*}}(x,\eta)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\xi\sim f(\cdot|\eta,\theta)}\Big[s(\xi|\eta,\theta)\Big(c(x,\pi^{*}(x,\eta),\xi)+\gamma\,\mathbb{E}_{\eta^{\prime}|\eta}\big[V_{\theta}^{\pi^{*}}(F(x,\pi^{*}(x,\eta),\xi),\eta^{\prime})\big]\Big)\Big]

(21)

\displaystyle+\gamma\,\mathbb{E}_{\xi\sim f(\cdot|\eta,\theta)}\Big[\mathbb{E}_{\eta^{\prime}|\eta}\big[\nabla_{\theta}V_{\theta}^{\pi^{*}}(F(x,\pi^{*}(x,\eta),\xi),\eta^{\prime})\big]\Big].

∎

We are ready to formulate a Bernstein–von Mises theorem for the
data-driven contextual optimal values,
showing that for fixed

(x_{1},\eta_{1})\in\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{H}
,

V_{N}^{*}(x_{1},\eta_{1})
is asymptotically normal around the true optimal
value
V^{*}(x_{1},\eta_{1})
.
We define Fisher information matrices

\displaystyle I(\theta;\eta)\coloneqq\mathbb{E}_{\xi\sim f(\cdot|\eta,\theta)}\big[s(\xi|\eta,\theta)s(\xi|\eta,\theta)^{\top}\big]\quad\text{and}\quad I(\theta)\coloneqq\sum_{h\in\mathcal{H}}\nu_{\eta}(h)I(\theta;h).

(22)

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
3.1
,
3.2
and
4.1
hold,
let
(x_{1},\eta_{1})\in\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{H}
be fixed,
let
I(\theta^{*})
be invertible,
and let the asymptotics in (
17
) be satisfied for

g(\theta)=V_{\theta}^{\pi^{*}}(x_{1},\eta_{1})
.
Moreover, suppose that

\displaystyle N^{1/2}\big(V_{N}^{*}(x_{1},\eta_{1})-\mathbb{E}_{\theta\sim\mathsf{p}_{N}}\big[V_{\theta}^{\pi^{*}}(x_{1},\eta_{1})\big]\big)\xrightarrow{\mathbb{P}^{*}}0.

(23)

Then

\displaystyle N^{1/2}\big(V_{N}^{*}(x_{1},\eta_{1})-V^{*}(x_{1},\eta_{1})\big)\overset{\mathbb{P}^{*}}{\rightsquigarrow}\mathcal{N}\Big(0,\,\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}_{\theta^{*}}}[H_{\theta^{*}}(x_{1},\eta_{1})]^{\top}I(\theta^{*})^{-1}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}_{\theta^{*}}}[H_{\theta^{*}}(x_{1},\eta_{1})]\Big).

(24)

where

\displaystyle H_{\theta^{*}}(x_{1},\eta_{1})\coloneqq\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}\gamma^{t-1}\,c(x_{t},\pi^{*}(x_{t},\eta_{t}),\xi_{t})\,S_{\theta^{*},t}.

(25)

Before we establish
Theorem

4.1
, we comment on
its hypotheses and its assertion.
The condition (
23
) ensures
that the asymptotic distribution of

N^{1/2}\big(V_{N}^{*}(x_{1},\eta_{1})-V^{*}(x_{1},\eta_{1})\big)

equals that of

\displaystyle N^{1/2}\big(\mathbb{E}_{\theta\sim\mathsf{p}_{N}}\big[V_{\theta}^{\pi^{*}}(x_{1},\eta_{1})\big]-V^{*}(x_{1},\eta_{1})\big).

(26)

This statement is with respect to the probability law

\mathbb{P}^{*}
of the data-generating process.
Recalling
V^{*}(x_{1},\eta_{1})=V_{\theta^{*}}^{\pi^{*}}(x_{1},\eta_{1})

under a correctly specified, identifiable model,
the condition (
23
) requires
that the error between the Bayesian optimal value,

V_{N}^{*}(x_{1},\eta_{1})
, and the posterior
mean of
\theta\mapsto V_{\theta}^{\pi^{*}}(x_{1},\eta_{1})

is asymptotically negligible at the
N^{-1/2}
scale.
If
\pi_{N}^{*}
is a Bayesian optimal policy, then for
each
(x,\eta)\in\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{H}
,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} V_{N}^{*}(x,\eta)-\mathbb{E}_{\theta\sim\mathsf{p}_{N}}\big[V_{\theta}^{\pi^{*}}(x,\eta)\big]&=\mathbb{E}_{\mathsf{p}_{N}|\eta}\Big[c(x,\pi_{N}^{*}(x,\eta),\xi)+\gamma\,\mathbb{E}_{\eta^{\prime}|\eta}\big[V_{N}^{*}(F(x,\pi_{N}^{*}(x,\eta),\xi),\eta^{\prime})\big]\Big]\\
&\quad-\mathbb{E}_{\mathsf{p}_{N}|\eta}\Big[c(x,\pi^{*}(x,\eta),\xi)+\gamma\,\mathbb{E}_{\eta^{\prime}|\eta}\big[V_{\theta}^{\pi^{*}}(F(x,\pi^{*}(x,\eta),\xi),\eta^{\prime})\big]\Big].\end{aligned}

(27)

Hence
V_{N}^{*}(x,\eta)
differs from

\mathbb{E}_{\theta\sim\mathsf{p}_{N}}\big[V_{\theta}^{\pi^{*}}(x,\eta)\big]
in that: (i) it evaluates the objective under the Bayesian optimal policy
\pi_{N}^{*}
,
whereas the posterior mean

\mathbb{E}_{\theta\sim\mathsf{p}_{N}}[V_{\theta}^{\pi^{*}}(x,\eta)]

uses the true optimal policy
\pi^{*}
, and (ii) it uses
V_{N}^{*}
in
place of
V_{\theta}^{\pi^{*}}
in (
27
). The
asymptotic equivalence in (
23
)
partially resembles of the finite-dimensional, single-stage
stochastic optimization setting; see eq. (5.24) in
[
20
]
. A key difference
here is that (
27
) involves two distinct value functions
on the right-hand side.

Let us compare the asymptotic nature of
Theorem

3.1
with that of
Theorem

4.1
.
The posterior consistency in
Theorem

3.1
is a two-level asymptotic: for

\mathbb{P}^{*}
-almost every realized data path (“outer” randomness), the conditional
posterior law of
\theta_{N}\sim\mathsf{P}_{N}
(“inner” randomness)
concentrates on
\Theta^{*}
.
In contrast,
Theorem

4.1
is a one-level statement
under
\mathbb{P}^{*}
:
V_{N}^{*}(x_{1},\eta_{1})
and

\mathbb{E}_{\theta\sim\mathsf{p}_{N}}[V_{\theta}^{\pi^{*}}(x,\eta)]

already integrate out the posterior uncertainty in
\theta
and, hence, are
functions only of the observed data
\{(\xi_{i},\eta_{i})\}_{i=1}^{N}
.

Theorem

4.1

establishes asymptotics for the Bayesian optimal value,
rather than convergence of the posterior distribution itself.

The asymptotic variance in
Theorem

4.1
involves
\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}_{\theta^{*}}}[H_{\theta^{*}}(x_{1},\eta_{1})]
.
This vector captures the first-order sensitivity of the infinite-horizon discounted cost,
evaluated under the true optimal policy, to perturbations of the
model. Recalling

\nabla g(\theta^{*})=\nabla_{\theta}V_{\theta^{*}}^{\pi^{*}}(x_{1},\eta_{1})=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}_{\theta^{*}}}[H_{\theta^{*}}(x_{1},\eta_{1})]
,

Remark

3
provides an alternative characterization:

\nabla g(\theta)
is the unique solution of the fixed-point equation
(
21
).
Moreover, the matrix
I(\theta^{*})^{-1}
in (
24
) describes how statistical uncertainty
about
\theta
propagates into uncertainty about the optimal value.

Theorem

4.1

yields Bernstein–von Mises asymptotics for each fixed initial condition

(x_{1},\eta_{1})\in\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{H}
; obtaining limits that hold uniformly over

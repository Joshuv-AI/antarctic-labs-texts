Weak quantum hypergroups from finite index C ∗ C^{*} -inclusions
Keshab Chandra Bakshi,

Abstract
We study a finite index inclusion B ⊂ A B\subset A of simple unital C ∗ C^{*} -algebras and construct a canonical completely positive coproduct on the second relative commutant B ′ ∩ A 1 B^{\prime}\cap A_{1} , thereby endowing it with a natural coalgebra structure. Motivated by this construction, we introduce the notion of a weak quantum hypergroup , a generalization of the quantum hypergroups of Chapovsky and Vainerman [Compact quantum hypergroups, J. Operator Theory 41 (1999), no. 2, 261–289]. We show that every finite index inclusion gives rise to such a weak quantum hypergroup, and that the corresponding weak quantum hypergroup possesses a Haar integral. In the irreducible case, this structure satisfies the axioms of a quantum hypergroup in the sense of Chapovsky and Vainerman, while in the depth 2 2 setting our framework yields the associated weak Hopf algebra constructed by Nikshych and Vainerman. These results provide a unified and intrinsically C ∗ C^{*} -algebraic framework for generalized quantum symmetries associated with finite index inclusions.

Introduction

Jones introduced the notion of the index
[M:N]
for a unital inclusion of type
II_{1}
factors
N\subset M
in
[
17
]
. This breakthrough initiated an active field of research in operator algebras, with significant applications to various areas of mathematics and mathematical physics. Later, Kosaki extended the definition of index to inclusions of type
III
factors
[
19
]
. Inclusion of simple
C^{*}
-algebras encompasses both type
II_{1}
and type
III
subfactors. Watatani subsequently developed a very general algebraic approach to index theory in this setting
[
31
]
, defining an index for inclusions of
C^{*}
-algebras possessing a conditional expectation that admits a quasi-basis, a generalization of the Pimsner–Popa basis
[
26
]
. Inclusions of
C^{*}
-algebras play a central role in operator algebra theory, serving as a unifying framework for index theory, quantum symmetries, and tensor categorical structures. Finite index inclusions, in particular, encode rich internal symmetry and give rise to tensor categories of bimodules, which have become a fundamental tool in subfactor theory, fusion categories, and low-dimensional topology; see, for example,
[
5
,
8
,
13
,
15
,
21
,
25
,
27
]
for recent developments. Understanding how such symmetries can be canonically extracted from an inclusion of simple
C^{*}
-algebras, without imposing restrictive assumptions such as irreducibility or depth conditions, is a key motivation for the present work.

For a subfactor
N\subset M
with finite index
[M:N]<\infty
, Jones observed that one can construct another type
II_{1}
factor
M_{1}
, referred to as the basic construction, satisfying
[M_{1}:M]=[M:N]
. This procedure can be iterated to form the fundamental Jones tower:

N\subset M\subset M_{1}\subset M_{2}\subset\cdots\subset M_{k}\subset\cdots.

The existence of this tower was a key ingredient in the proof of the celebrated index rigidity theorem in
[
17
]
.
Since the inception of subfactor theory, the higher relative commutants
N^{\prime}\cap M_{k}
and
M^{\prime}\cap M_{k}
play a crucial role in understanding the subfactors. Ocneanu introduced the notion of a Fourier transform
\mathcal{F}
from
N^{\prime}\cap M_{1}
onto
M^{\prime}\cap M_{2}
, generalizing the classical Fourier transform for finite abelian groups (see also
[
6
,
7
]
). Through this transformation, one obtains a novel multiplication on
N^{\prime}\cap M_{1}
, which extends the classical convolution product. Motivated by these developments, the first-named author together with Gupta introduced in
[
1

a convolution product on the higher relative commutants
B^{\prime}\cap A_{k}
and
A^{\prime}\cap A_{k}
, arising from a unital inclusion of simple unital
C^{*}
-algebras
B\subset A
with a conditional expectation of index-finite type, where

B\subset A\subset A_{1}\subset A_{2}\subset\cdots\subset A_{k}\subset\cdots

denotes Watatani’s tower of
C^{*}
-basic constructions with respect to the unique minimal conditional expectation. In
[

[
14

B^{\prime}\cap A_{1}
using this convolution product, and we prove that the associated coproduct map is completely positive.

Theorem A
(See
3.6
)
Let
B\subset A
be an inclusion of simple unital
C^{*}
-algebras equipped with a conditional expectation of index-finite type. Equip
B^{\prime}\cap A_{1}
with the inner product
\langle x,y\rangle:=\operatorname{tr}(x^{*}y).
Define a linear map

\Delta\colon B^{\prime}\cap A_{1}\longrightarrow(B^{\prime}\cap A_{1})\otimes(B^{\prime}\cap A_{1})

\langle\Delta(x),\,y\otimes z\rangle=\tau^{\frac{1}{2}}\,\langle x,\,y\star z\rangle,\qquad x,y,z\in B^{\prime}\cap A_{1},

Then the map

Hopf algebras and their generalizations play a fundamental role in the theory of subfactors and fusion categories, as they provide algebraic models for quantum symmetries. In the depth 2 setting, finite index subfactors are completely described by finite-dimensional weak Hopf algebras (also called quantum groupoids), as shown by Nikshych and Vainerman in
[
22
]
. In this case, the subfactor can be realized as a crossed product by a weak Hopf algebra, and the associated fusion category of bimodules is equivalent to the representation category of this quantum symmetry object.
For a depth 2 inclusion of simple unital
C^{*}
-algebras with a finite index conditional expectation, a similar structural result has also been recently established (see
[
15
,
24
]
). Beyond depth 2, fusion categories arising from subfactors no longer admit a single Hopf algebra symmetry. Nevertheless, remnants of Hopf algebraic structure persist in the form of bimodule categories, paragroups, and planar algebras.
A quantum hypergroup (in the sense of Chapovsky and Vainerman in
[
10
]
) is a unital
C^{*}
-algebra equipped with a completely positive, coassociative coproduct. The most important examples of such structures arise from double cosets of compact matrix pseudogroups in the sense of Woronowicz (
[
32
]
). Chapovsky and Vainerman further showed in
[
10
]
that quantum hypergroups possess rich additional structure. As a first step toward developing a general framework for arbitrary inclusions of simple unital
C^{*}
-algebras, we introduce the notion of a weak quantum hypergroup by relaxing some of the axioms of a quantum hypergroup. The present work fits naturally into this landscape by introducing weak quantum hypergroups as a further generalization of Hopf and weak Hopf algebra symmetries. Weak quantum hypergroups retain the essential analytic features of quantum symmetries- such as coproducts, Haar integrals, and actions- while replacing multiplicativity by complete positivity. This allows one to associate a canonical symmetry object to arbitrary finite index inclusions of
C^{*}
-algebras, extending the reach of Hopf-algebraic methods to settings where genuine Hopf or weak Hopf algebra symmetries are unavailable. More precisely, we prove the following:

Theorem B:
(See Theorems
4.14
,
4.17
,
4.23
, and
4.24
) Let
B\subset A
be an inclusion of simple unital
C^{*}
-algebras with a conditional expectation of index-finite type. In this paper, we establish the following results:

•

We show that the second relative commutant
B^{\prime}\cap A_{1}
, associated with the basic construction, carries a canonical
weak quantum hypergroup structure
naturally arising from the inclusion
B\subset A
.

•

We prove that, when the inclusion
B\subset A
is irreducible, the weak quantum hypergroup structure on
B^{\prime}\cap A_{1}
upgrades to that of a (genuine) quantum hypergroup.

•

We further show that the weak quantum hypergroup
B^{\prime}\cap A_{1}
admits left- and right-invariant measures, and that it also admits a Haar integral.

The main contribution of this paper is the introduction of a new symmetry object—the weak quantum hypergroup—which arises canonically from a finite index inclusion of simple unital
C^{*}
-algebras. This concept provides a unified operator-algebraic framework that extends and connects previously known quantum symmetry structures, while remaining valid in complete generality. Even though a weak quantum hypergroup does not encode all higher relative commutants
B^{\prime}\cap A_{n}
in the Jones tower, it nevertheless captures, in a very explicit manner, the canonical first layer of the quantum symmetry inherent in the inclusion. Moreover, our construction unifies all known special cases: in the irreducible setting the weak quantum hypergroup satisfies the axioms of a quantum hypergroup in the sense of Chapovsky and Vainerman, while in the depth 2 case it collapses to the weak Hopf algebra of Nikshych and Vainerman.

Lemma 2.1

(

Lemma 2.2

(

Let
B\subset A

-algebras, and let
E_{0}:A\to B

E_{0}(xa)=E_{0}(ax).

The relative commutants
B^{\prime}\cap A_{n}:=\{x\in A_{n}:xb=bx\text{ for all }b\in B\}

(B^{\prime}\cap A_{n-1})\,e_{n}\,(B^{\prime}\cap A_{n-1})=B^{\prime}\cap A_{n}.

Using the minimal conditional expectations, one obtains “Markov-type traces” on these algebras

\mathrm{tr}_{n}:=(E_{0}\circ E_{1}\circ\cdots\circ E_{n})\big|_{B^{\prime}\cap A_{n}}:B^{\prime}\cap A_{n}\to\mathbb{C}

Proposition 2.3

(

For all

\mathrm{tr}_{n}(xe_{n})=\tau\,\mathrm{tr}_{n-1}(x)\text{ for every }x\in B^{\prime}\cap A_{n-1},

\mathrm{tr}_{n}|_{B^{\prime}\cap A_{n-1}}=\mathrm{tr}_{n-1}.

When there is no risk of confusion, we shall drop the subscript and write simply

for notational convenience. If
\{\lambda_{i}:1\leq i\leq n\}\subset A
is a quasi-basis for the minimal conditional expectation
E_{0}
. Then the

E_{A^{\prime}\cap A_{n}}^{B^{\prime}\cap A_{n}}(x)=\tau\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}x\lambda_{i}^{*}\qquad\text{for all }x\in B^{\prime}\cap A_{n}.

The idea of a Fourier transform for paragroups associated with finite depth subfactors was first proposed by Ocneanu

Quantum hypergroups

We now recall some preliminaries on quantum hypergroups, as introduced by Chapovsky and Vainerman
[
10

[

Definition 2.5

(
[
10
]
)
.

A quadruple
(A,\Delta,\varepsilon,\star)
is said to define a quantum hypergroup structure on the
C^{*}
-algebra

(A,\cdot,1,*)
if the following conditions are satisfied:

•

The system
(A,\Delta,\varepsilon,\star)
forms a
*
-coalgebra with counit
\varepsilon
; that is,
\Delta\colon A\to A\otimes A

and
\varepsilon\colon A\to\mathbb{C}
are linear maps, and

\star\colon A\to A
is an antilinear map such that

(i)

(\Delta\otimes\mathrm{id})\circ\Delta=(\mathrm{id}\otimes\Delta)\circ\Delta,

(ii)

(\varepsilon\otimes\mathrm{id})\circ\Delta=(\mathrm{id}\otimes\varepsilon)\circ\Delta=\mathrm{id},

(iii)

\Delta\circ\star=\Pi\circ(\star\otimes\star)\circ\Delta,

(iv)

\star\circ\star=\mathrm{id},

where
\Pi\colon A\otimes A\to A\otimes A
denotes the flip map.

•

The comultiplication
\Delta\colon A\to A\otimes A
is positive.

•

The following compatibility conditions are satisfied for all
a,b\in A
:

(i)

(a\cdot b)^{\star}=a^{\star}\cdot b^{\star},

(ii)

\Delta\circ*=(*\otimes*)\circ\Delta,

(iii)

\varepsilon(a\cdot b)=\varepsilon(a)\varepsilon(b),

(iv)

\Delta(1)=1\otimes 1,

(v)

\star\circ*=*\circ\star.

(
[
10
]
)

Let
(A,\Delta,\varepsilon,\star)

-algebra

\phi\otimes\mbox{id})\circ\Delta(a)=(\mbox{id}\otimes\phi)\circ\Delta(a)=\phi(a),
for all

If

is a quantum hypergroup whose linear span of positive definite elements is
dense in

(see

). It is worth noting that in
[

the authors
further introduce and study the notion of compact quantum hypergroups, the most important examples of such structures arise from

3
Convolution product and coalgebra structure

In this section, we consider the inclusion
B\subset A
of simple unital
C^{*}
-algebras with a conditional expectation of index-finite type and define a coalgebra structure on the second relative commutant
B^{\prime}\cap A_{1}
using the convolution product. We then show that the associated coproduct map is completely positive. An application of this result will be presented in the next section. Finally, we prove that this coalgebra structure coincides, up to isomorphism, with the one introduced by Nikshych and Vainerman (
[
22
,
24
]
) in the case of depth 2 subfactor. This correspondence reinforces the naturality of our construction and highlights its relevance within the broader theory of generalized quantum symmetries arising from subfactor inclusions.

Notation:
Throughout this paper, we work with an inclusion
B\subset A
of simple unital
C^{*}
-algebras with a conditional expectation of index-finite type. Let
\{\lambda_{i}:1\leq i\leq n\}\subset A
be a quasi-basis for the minimal conditional expectation
E_{0}
.

Definition 3.1
.

On the second relative commutant
B^{\prime}\cap A_{1}
, we define the maps
\Delta
and
\varepsilon
as follows:

\Delta:B^{\prime}\cap A_{1}\rightarrow(B^{\prime}\cap A_{1})\otimes(B^{\prime}\cap A_{1}):\quad\big<\Delta(x),y\otimes z\big>=\tau^{\frac{1}{2}}\big<x,y\star z\big>,

\varepsilon:B^{\prime}\cap A_{1}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}:\quad\varepsilon(x)=\tau^{-1}\big<e_{1},x\big>,

for all
x,y,z\in B^{\prime}\cap A_{1}
. Here the inner product on
B^{\prime}\cap A_{1}
is given by
\big<x,y\big>=\mathrm{tr}(x^{*}y)
.

Proposition 3.2
.

(B^{\prime}\cap A_{1},\Delta,\varepsilon)
becomes a coalgebra.

Proof
:
For any
x,y_{1},y_{2},y_{3}\in B^{\prime}\cap A_{1}
, we have the following:

\displaystyle\big<(\Delta\otimes\mathrm{id})\circ\Delta(x),y_{1}\otimes y_{2}\otimes y_{3}\big>

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big<\Delta(x_{(1)}),y_{1}\otimes y_{2}\big>\,\big<x_{(2)},y_{3}\big>

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tau^{\frac{1}{2}}\big<x_{(1)},y_{1}\star y_{2}\big>\,\big<x_{(2)},y_{3}\big>

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tau\big<x,(y_{1}\star y_{2})\star y_{3}\big>.

Similarly, it is easy to verify that,
\big<(\mathrm{id}\otimes\Delta)\circ\Delta(x),y_{1}\otimes y_{2}\otimes y_{3}\big>=\tau\big<x,y_{1}\star(y_{2}\star y_{3})\big>.
By
[
1
]
[Lemma 3.20], we know that the convolution product is associative. Hence we get
(\Delta\otimes\mathrm{id})\circ\Delta=(\mathrm{id}\otimes\Delta)\circ\Delta.
Also we have,

\displaystyle\big<y_{1},(\varepsilon\otimes\mathrm{id})\circ\Delta(x)\big>

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varepsilon(x_{(1)})\big<y_{1},x_{(2)}\big>

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tau^{-\frac{1}{2}}\big<e_{1}\star y_{1},x\big>

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big<y_{1},x\big>.

Moreover, one readily verifies that
\big<y_{1},(\mathrm{id}\otimes\varepsilon)\circ\Delta(x)\big>=\big<y_{1},x\big>.
So we have
(\varepsilon\otimes\mathrm{id})\circ\Delta=(\mathrm{id}\otimes\varepsilon)\circ\Delta=\mathrm{id}.
Hence
(B^{\prime}\cap A_{1},\Delta,\varepsilon)
becomes a coalgebra.

\Box

As a direct consequence of
[
30
]
, we obtain the following:

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
B\subset A
be an inclusion of simple unital
C^{*}
-algebras with a conditional expectation of finite Watatani index. Then a Hopf algebra can be constructed from the coalgebra
(B^{\prime}\cap A_{1},\Delta,\varepsilon)
.

We now aim to show that
\Delta
is a completely positive map. In the framework of a finite index subfactor, this has been established in
[
14
]
, where the proof is given via pictorial arguments. In contrast, in the
C^{*}
-algebraic framework, the main obstacle is that a comparable pictorial description (as in planar algebras) for the Fourier transform and convolution is still not available. To overcome this difficulty, we instead exploit the relationship between quasi-basis and minimal conditional expectation. We begin by establishing the following preliminary results:

Proposition 3.4
.

The
C^{*}
-algebras
(B^{\prime}\cap A_{1})\otimes(B^{\prime}\cap A_{1})
and
(B^{\prime}\cap A_{1})(A_{1}^{\prime}\cap A_{3})
are isomorphic.

Proof
:
Define
T:(B^{\prime}\cap A_{1})\otimes(B^{\prime}\cap A_{1})\to(B^{\prime}\cap A_{1})(A_{1}^{\prime}\cap A_{3})
by

T(x\otimes y)=x\,S_{0}^{+}(y),\qquad x,y\in B^{\prime}\cap A_{1}.

where
S_{0}^{+}
is the shift operator on
B^{\prime}\cap A_{1}
as defined in
[
3
]
. Then clearly
T
is an
*
-algebra homomorphism. We now observe that

\displaystyle\|\sum_{i}x_{i}\otimes y_{i}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big<\sum_{i}x_{i}\otimes y_{i},\sum_{j}x_{j}\otimes y_{j}\big>=\sum_{i,j}\big<x_{i}\otimes y_{i},x_{j}\otimes y_{j}\big>=\sum_{i,j}\big<x_{i},x_{j}\big>\big<y_{i},y_{j}\big>.

Moreover, we have

\displaystyle\|\sum_{i}x_{i}\,S^{+}_{0}(y_{i})\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big<\sum_{i}x_{i}\,S^{+}_{0}(y_{i}),\sum_{j}x_{j}\,S^{+}_{0}(y_{j})\big>

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j}\big<x_{i}\,S^{+}_{0}(y_{i}),x_{j}\,S^{+}_{0}(y_{j})\big>

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j}\mathrm{tr}(S^{+}_{0}(y^{*}_{i})\,x^{*}_{i}x_{j}\,S^{+}_{0}(y_{j})\big>

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j}\mathrm{tr}(S^{+}_{0}(y_{j})S^{+}_{0}(y^{*}_{i})\,x^{*}_{i}x_{j}).

The following observation holds.

\mathrm{tr}(S^{+}_{0}(y_{j}y^{*}_{i})\,x^{*}_{i}x_{j})=E_{0}\circ E_{1}\circ E_{2}\circ E_{3}(S^{+}_{0}(y_{j}y^{*}_{i})\,x^{*}_{i}x_{j})=E_{0}\circ E_{1}(E_{2}\circ E_{3}(S^{+}_{0}(y_{j}y^{*}_{i}))\,x^{*}_{i}x_{j}).

Using
[
3
, Theorem 3.27]
, we know that
S^{+}_{0}(y_{j}y^{*}_{i})\in A^{\prime}_{1}\cap A_{3}

4
Weak quantum Hypergroup structure on the second relative commutant

The primary objective of this section is to demonstrate that, given an inclusion
B\subset A
of simple unital
C^{*}
-algebras equipped with a conditional expectation of index-finite type, the second relative commutant
B^{\prime}\cap A_{1}
naturally acquires a structure, defined below, that we refer to as a
weak quantum hypergroup
. This notion may be viewed as a relaxation of the quantum hypergroup concept introduced by Chapovsky and Vainerman in
[
10
]
. Furthermore, we show that in the case where the inclusion is irreducible, the resulting structure upgrades to a genuine quantum hypergroup. Finally, we show that the weak quantum hypergroup
B^{\prime}\cap A_{1}
admits both left-invariant and right-invariant normalized measures, as well as a Haar integral.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
(A,\cdot,1,*)
be a unital
C^{*}
-algebra. We say that
(A,\Delta,\varepsilon,{\small\texttt{\#}})
defines a
weak quantum hypergroup
structure on
(A,\cdot,1,*)
if the following conditions hold:

•

(A,\Delta,\varepsilon,{\small\texttt{\#}})
is a
#
-coalgebra with a counit
\varepsilon
; that is,
\Delta:A\rightarrow A\otimes A
and
\varepsilon:A\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
are linear maps, and
{\small\texttt{\#}}:A\rightarrow A
is an antilinear map satisfying:

(i)

(\Delta\otimes\mathrm{id})\circ\Delta=(\mathrm{id}\otimes\Delta)\circ\Delta,

(ii)

(\varepsilon\otimes\mathrm{id})\circ\Delta=(\mathrm{id}\otimes\varepsilon)\circ\Delta=\mathrm{id},

(iii)

\Delta\circ{\small\texttt{\#}}=\Pi\circ({\small\texttt{\#}}\otimes{\small\texttt{\#}})\circ\Delta
, where
\Pi:A\otimes A\rightarrow A\otimes A
is the flip map,

(iv)

{\small\texttt{\#}}\circ{\small\texttt{\#}}=\mathrm{id}
.

•

The map
\Delta
is positive.

•

The following identities hold:

(i)

(x\cdot y)^{{\small\texttt{\#}}}=x^{{\small\texttt{\#}}}\cdot y^{{\small\texttt{\#}}},
for all
x,y\in A
,

(ii)

\Delta\circ*=(*\otimes*)\circ\Delta
,

(iii)

{\small\texttt{\#}}\circ*=*\circ{\small\texttt{\#}}
.

•

Weak multiplicativity of the counit
holds, i.e., for all
x,y,z\in A
, we have

\varepsilon(xyz)=\varepsilon(xy_{(1)})\varepsilon(y_{(2)}z)=\varepsilon(xy_{(2)})\varepsilon(y_{(1)}z).

•

Weak comultiplicativity of the unit
holds, i.e.,

(\mathrm{id}\otimes\Delta)\circ\Delta(1)=(\Delta(1)\otimes 1)(1\otimes\Delta(1))=(1\otimes\Delta(1))(\Delta(1)\otimes 1).

As in the case of weak Hopf algebras, we define the following notions for a weak quantum hypergroup.

Definition 4.2
.

For any weak quantum hypergroup
A
, we define the
target
and
source
maps, denoted by
\varepsilon^{t}
and
\varepsilon^{s}
, respectively, on
A
as follows:

\varepsilon^{t}(x)=\varepsilon(1_{(1)}x)\,1_{(2)},\quad\text{for all }x\in A,

\varepsilon^{s}(x)=1_{(1)}\,\varepsilon(x1_{(2)}),\quad\text{for all }x\in A.

Definition 4.3
.

A functional
\phi\in A^{*}
is called a left-invariant measure on
A
if

(\mathrm{id}\otimes\phi)\circ\Delta=(\varepsilon^{t}\otimes\phi)\circ\Delta.

The measure
\phi
is said to be normalized if
(\mathrm{id}\otimes\phi)\circ\Delta(1)=1.

Similarly, a functional
\psi\in A^{*}
is called a right-invariant measure on
A
if

(\psi\otimes\mathrm{id})\circ\Delta=(\psi\otimes\varepsilon^{s})\circ\Delta.

The measure
\psi
is said to be normalized if
(\psi\otimes\mathrm{id})\circ\Delta(1)=1.

Definition 4.4
.

A left integral is an element
x\in A
such that
ax=\varepsilon^{t}(a)x
for all
a\in A.
Similarly, a right integral is an element
y\in A
such that
ya=y\varepsilon^{s}(a)
for all
a\in A.

An element
h\in A
is called a Haar integral if it is both a left and right integral and satisfies
\varepsilon^{t}(h)=\varepsilon^{s}(h)=1.

\displaystyle\tau^{-\frac{5}{2}}\sum_{i}\mathrm{tr}(\lambda_{i}ye_{2}e_{1}\lambda^{*}_{i}e_{1}e_{2}x^{*}e_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tau^{-\frac{3}{2}}\mathrm{tr}(ye_{2}x^{*}e_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tau^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{tr}(yE_{1}(x^{*})).

\Box

Theorem 4.23
.

Let
B\subset A
be an inclusion of simple unital
C^{*}
-algebras with a conditional expectation of index-finite type. Then the weak quantum hypergroup
B^{\prime}\cap A_{1}
, as defined in
4.5
, admits both left-invariant and right-invariant normalized measures.

Proof
:
Consider the functional
\phi(x)=\mathrm{tr}(x).
First we will show that
\phi
is a left-invariant measure, i.e.,
(\mathrm{id}\otimes\phi)\circ\Delta=(\varepsilon^{t}\otimes\phi)\circ\Delta.
For any
y\in B^{\prime}\cap A_{1}
, using
4.21
, we have the following:

\displaystyle\big<y,x_{(1)}\mathrm{tr}(x_{(2)})\big>

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big<x^{*}_{(1)},y^{*}\big>\,\big<x^{*}_{(2)},1\big>

(4.16)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tau^{\frac{1}{2}}\big<x^{*},y^{*}\star 1\big>

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}(y^{*}E_{1}(x))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}(xE_{1}(y^{*})).

Moreover, using
4.8
item
(ii)
together with
4.21
, we have

\displaystyle\big<y,\varepsilon^{t}(x_{(1)})\mathrm{tr}(x_{(2)})\big>

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tau^{-1}\big<y,E_{1}(x_{(1)}e_{1})\big>\,\big<x^{*}_{(2)},1\big>

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tau^{-1}E_{0}\big(E_{1}(y^{*})E_{1}(x_{(1)}e_{1})\big)\,\big<x^{*}_{(2)},1\big>

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tau^{-1}E_{0}\big(E_{1}(x_{(1)}e_{1}E_{1}(y^{*}))\big)\,\big<x^{*}_{(2)},1\big>

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tau^{-1}\big<x^{*}_{(1)},e_{1}E_{1}(y^{*})\big>\,\big<x^{*}_{(2)},1\big>

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tau^{-\frac{1}{2}}\big<x^{*},(e_{1}E_{1}(y^{*}))\star 1\big>

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tau^{-\frac{1}{2}}\,\overline{\big<x,(E_{1}(y)e_{1})\star 1\big>}.

The last equality follows by
Equation

2.2
. Now using
4.8
item
(i)
, we get that

\displaystyle\big<y,\varepsilon^{t}(x_{(1)})\mathrm{tr}(x_{(2)})\big>=\tau^{-1}\overline{\mathrm{tr}\big(E_{1}(E_{1}(y)e_{1})x^{*}\big)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\overline{\mathrm{tr}\big(E_{1}(y)x^{*}\big)}

(4.17)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}(xE_{1}(y^{*})).

Therefore, by
Equation

4.16
and
Equation

4.17
, we conclude that
\phi
is a left-invariant measure. Moreover, it is straightforward to verify that
\phi
is normalized. Now we will show that
\phi
is also right-invariant measure, i.e.,

(\phi\otimes\mathrm{id})\circ\Delta=(\phi\otimes\varepsilon^{s})\circ\Delta.
For any
y\in B^{\prime}\cap A_{1}
, we get the following:

\displaystyle\big<y,\,\mathrm{tr}(x_{(1)})x_{(2)}\big>=\big<x^{*}_{(1)},\,1\big>\,\big<x^{*}_{(2)},y^{*}\big>

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tau^{\frac{1}{2}}\big<x^{*},\,1\star y^{*}\big>.

(4.18)

Using
4.20
and the fact that
r^{+}_{1}
is an involutive, trace preserving antihomomorphism, we deduce that

\displaystyle\big<y,\mathrm{tr}(x_{(1)})\varepsilon^{s}(x_{(2)})\big>

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tau^{-1}\big<x^{*}_{(1)},\,1\big>\,\mathrm{tr}(y^{*}r^{+}_{1}(E_{1}(e_{1}x_{(2)}))\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tau^{-1}\big<x^{*}_{(1)},\,1\big>\,\mathrm{tr}\big(E_{1}(e_{1}x_{(2)})r^{+}_{1}(y^{*})\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tau^{-1}\big<x^{*}_{(1)},\,1\big>\,E_{0}(E_{1}(e_{1}x_{(2)})E_{1}(r^{+}_{1}(y^{*}))\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tau^{-1}\big<x^{*}_{(1)},\,1\big>\,\big<x^{*}_{(2)},\,E_{1}(r^{+}_{1}(y^{*}))e_{1}\big>

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tau^{-\frac{1}{2}}\big<x^{*},\,1\star(E_{1}(r^{+}_{1}(y^{*}))e_{1})\big>.

Now, using
[
4
, Proposition 3.9]
together with the fact that
r_{1}^{+}(e_{1})=e_{1}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\big<y,\mathrm{tr}(x_{(1)})\varepsilon^{s}(x_{(2)})\big>

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tau^{-\frac{1}{2}}\big<r^{+}_{1}(x^{*}),\,\big(e_{1}r^{+}_{1}(E_{1}(r^{+}_{1}(y^{*})))\big)\star 1\big>

(4.19)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tau^{-\frac{1}{2}}\big<r^{+}_{1}(x^{*}),\,\big(e_{1}E_{1}(r^{+}_{1}(y^{*}))\big)\star 1\big>\quad\text{ (by \lx@cref{creftypecap\lx@tilde refnum}{impforrightinvariantmeasure3})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tau^{-\frac{1}{2}}\overline{\big<r^{+}_{1}(x),\,\big(E_{1}(r^{+}_{1}(y))e_{1}\big)\star 1\big>}\quad\text{ (by \lx@cref{creftypecap\lx@tilde refnum}{3Proposition 3.8})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tau^{-1}\overline{\mathrm{tr}\big(E_{1}(E_{1}(r^{+}_{1}(y))e_{1})r^{+}_{1}(x^{*})\big)}\quad\text{ (by \lx@cref{creftypecap\lx@tilde refnum}{impfor e(xyz)} item $(i)$)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}\big(r^{+}_{1}(x)E_{1}(r^{+}_{1}(y^{*}))\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tau^{\frac{1}{2}}\big<r^{+}_{1}(x^{*}),\,r^{+}_{1}(y^{*})\star 1\big>\quad\text{ (by \lx@cref{creftypecap\lx@tilde refnum}{impforinvariantmeasure2})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tau^{\frac{1}{2}}\big<x^{*},\,1\star y^{*}\big>.

Thus, from
Equation

4.18
and
Equation

4.19
, we obtain that
\phi
is also a right-invariant measure. Clearly,
\phi
is a normalized right-invariant measure.

\Box

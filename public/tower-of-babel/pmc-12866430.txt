The dual links of health literacy in driving exercise adherence: the interactive effects of emotional management ability and life satisfaction
Authors: Wen-hao Zhang, Shan-shan Han, Hu Lou, Yu-yan Qian, Bo Li, Ling-Li Xu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveBased on the Health Action Process Approach theory, this study aims to explore how health literacy influences exercise adherence among Chinese university students. It focuses on examining the chain mediating effects of emotional management ability and life satisfaction.MethodThis study used a stratified, cluster, and phased sampling approach to gather demographic information. Data were collected through the Wenjuanxing platform. The survey gathered information on health literacy, emotional management ability, life satisfaction, and exercise adherence among university students. A total of 11,272 valid questionnaires were collected.ResultsHealth literacy directly predicted exercise adherence positively (β = 0.618, P < 0.001). Emotional management Ability (effect size = 0.281, 95% CI = [0.254, 0.308]) and life satisfaction (effect size = 0.054, 95% CI = [0.044, 0.065]) both served as independent mediators. Life satisfaction also emerged as a significant, albeit relatively small, independent mediator. The chain mediation path of health literacy → emotional management ability → life satisfaction → exercise adherence was significant (effect size = 0.061, 95% CI = [0.051, 0.071]).ConclusionHealth literacy not only directly promotes exercise adherence among Chinese university students but also exerts an indirect effect through the chain mechanism of improved emotional management ability and enhanced life satisfaction. This finding validates the progressive cognitive-affective-behavioral pathway in the Health Action Process Approach theory. Future interventions should integrate health education, emotional management ability training, and life satisfaction enhancement strategies. This approach will systematically optimize the long-term maintenance of exercise behaviors.

Introduction

Health literacy is the ability to access, understand, and use health information. It is key to improving public health [1, 2]. However, only about 12% of people globally have high health literacy [3]. In China, by the end of 2022, the level of health literacy among residents was 27.78%, meaning fewer than 3 out of every 10 people have adequate health literacy [4]. Particularly within the university student population, the results of the “8th National Survey on Students’ Physical Fitness and Health” show concerning physical fitness levels. This issue has attracted widespread attention from both academia and society [4]. Low health literacy raises death rates and healthcare use. It also blocks healthy behaviors [5].Health literacy not only concerns an individual’s comprehension of health information, but is also regarded as a key cognitive foundation that drives positive health behaviors.

Exercise adherence refers to an individual’s long-term and regular exercise behavior achieved through emotional and willpower investment [6]. Insufficient exercise is a widespread global issue, with over one-quarter of adults failing to meet recommended exercise levels [7].Exercise participation among Chinese residents has shown improvement. Data from 2020 indicate that only 37.2% of individuals aged seven and above maintained regular exercise [8]. However, tendencies toward exercise withdrawal and avoidance persist among university student populations [9],, and the pressures of modern life further challenge long-term adherence [10]. Regular exercise yields significant health benefits: it not only improves body composition and physical appearance [11], but also enhances metabolic function, optimizes insulin response, and promotes adaptive changes in multiple organ systems through the release of bioactive substances [12, 13].Conversely, insufficient exercise substantially increases the risk of peripheral and neurological diseases [14, 15]. When individuals engage in systematic exercise, improvements in neural function can effectively drive positive changes in exercise behavior [16].

Emotional Management Ability refers to an individual’s overall capacity to effectively identify, understand, regulate, and express emotions [17, 18]. This ability is particularly crucial for university students, who are in a period of rapid psychological and social identity transformation [19–22].Poor emotional management is a core feature of various mental health issues, often manifesting as persistent negative moods and impaired cognitive functions, such as slowed thinking and lack of motivation [23].In severe cases, it may even lead to depression and self-harm tendencies, significantly impacting academic performance and daily life [24]. Therefore, emotional management ability directly influences university students’ academic performance and quality of campus life [25]. Studies indicate that psychological trauma, peer evaluation, and life experiences are key factors influencing this ability [26, 27].Therefore, enhancing emotional management ability is essential for promoting university students’ mental well-being, academic achievement, and social adaptation, and has become a major focus in global psychological research [28].

Life satisfaction serves as a key indicator for assessing individual mental health, reflecting a person’s subjective evaluation of their overall life circumstances [29].Individuals with high life satisfaction generally possess closer social relationships, a stronger tendency for altruistic behavior [30], and greater capacity for building interpersonal networks [31]. On a personal level, they often exhibit stable personalities, sound psychological states, and balanced emotional management skills, thereby constructing a sustainable framework for well-being [32, 33].Conversely, those with low life satisfaction often face interwoven psychological challenges, primarily rooted in deficient emotional management skills. This deficiency hinders the effective mobilization of psychological resources under stress, manifesting behaviorally as restlessness and impaired inhibitory control, and emotionally as persistent depression or anxiety [34]. These symptoms mutually reinforce each other, forming psychological issues that hinder positive adaptation. When prolonged, such conditions may lead to self-isolation or impulsive behaviors, thereby exacerbating mental health risks [35]. Research indicates that individuals with low life satisfaction are more likely to meet clinical criteria for psychological abnormalities and exhibit significantly higher scores for suicidal ideation [30].

The Health Action Process Approach is widely used to explain the dynamic process of health behavior, from motivation formation to long-term practice [36]. Unlike traditional theories, Health Action Process Approach emphasizes that the formation and maintenance of health behavior is a dynamic process, involving two main stages: motivation generation and behavior execution. In the motivational phase, individuals weigh the pros and cons of engaging in health behaviors and form behavioral intentions [37]. After entering the behavioral phase, individuals need to overcome practical obstacles and convert their intentions into sustained exercise behavior [37]. At this stage, emotional management skills are particularly important. Good emotional management not only helps individuals regulate negative emotions but also enhances self-efficacy in the face of challenges, promoting adherence to exercise behavior [38]. Additionally, life satisfaction, as a positive psychological resource, also influences an individual’s behavior maintenance. People with higher life satisfaction are more likely to maintain a positive mindset, increasing their commitment to and persistence in health behaviors [39]. In summary, health literacy may indirectly promotes exercise adherence by improving emotional management and life satisfaction. The Health Action Process Approach theory provides theoretical support for this study and reveals the intrinsic relationship between health literacy, emotional management, life satisfaction, and exercise adherence. This study aims to explore the complex relationship between health literacy and exercise adherence among chinese university students. Based on the provided research background, this study proposes the following hypotheses grounded in the Health Action Process Approach. In this sense, we hypothesize that: (H1) Health literacy has a significant positive effect on exercise adherence. (H2) Emotional management ability has a significant mediating effect between health literacy and exercise adherence. (H3) Life satisfaction has a significant mediating effect between health literacy and exercise adherence. (H4) Emotional management ability and life satisfaction have a significant mediating effect between health literacy and exercise adherence.

Methods

Participants

This study utilizes data from the 2024 “Chinese College Students Physical Activity and Health Longitudinal Survey”. The Chinese College Students Physical Activity and Health Longitudinal Survey aims to collect high-quality individual-level microdata representing physical activity and physical and mental health behaviors among Chinese university students, with the objective of analyzing interdisciplinary issues related to physical activity and health, thereby promoting cross-disciplinary research on university student health. The Chinese College Students Physical Activity and Health Longitudinal Survey has been widely used in analyzing the health status of Chinese university students [40, 41].The study selected participants from regular higher education institutions in central Chinese provinces including Hubei, Henan, and Jiangxi. The list of regular higher education institutions was referenced from the Ministry of Education’s “National List of Regular Higher Education Institutions (as of June 20, 2024).” The exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) questionnaires with unidentifiable school names were removed; (2) questionnaires with at least 21 consecutive identical responses were eliminated; (3) given an average completion time of 6 min and 12 s, questionnaires with completion times in the (0, 0.5%) and (99.5%, 100%) intervals were excluded [42]. The survey was conducted in September 2024 via the Questionnaire Star platform, with electronic questionnaires distributed uniformly by administrative class. A total of 36,756 questionnaires were collected. To ensure the reliability and representativeness of the findings, a stratified, cluster, and multi-stage sampling method was employed during the sampling process. The study ultimately included 11,272 valid cases. Detailed information is presented in Table 1.

Measures

Sociodemographic information

The sociodemographic information used in this study includes gender, grade level, and age.

Health literacy

Health literacy was measured using the Health Literacy Scale-Short Form 9. Health literacy not only includes traditional literacy skills but also encompasses broader abilities such as numeracy, communication skills, and the ability to interpret and critically assess health information. Xiao-nan Sun et al. simplified theHealth Literacy Scale-Short Form 12 to create the 9-item Health Literacy Scale-Short Form 9 version [43]. The simplified scale has good reliability and validity and serves as an effective tool for quickly assessing health literacy in Chinese populations. The items are measured using a four-point scale: “very difficult,” “difficult,” “easy,” and “very easy.” The Health Literacy Scale-Short Form 9 has no ceiling or floor effects, with a Cronbach’s alpha of 0.913 and a split-half reliability of 0.871. Confirmatory factor analysis results show χ²/df = 10.844, goodness of fit index = 0.985, adjusted goodness of fit index = 0.971, normed fit index = 0.986, comparative fit index = 0.987, and root mean square error of approximation = 0.051 [43].

Exercise adherence

The study used the Exercise Adherence Scale developed by Gu Chunqiang at Fujian Normal University. This scale assesses university students’ adherence to physical exercise from three aspects: regularity of exercise, effort invested, and the emotional experience (positive or negative). The scale measures adherence using a five-level scale, ranging from “1” (completely disagree) to “5” (completely agree). Higher scores indicate stronger exercise adherence. The total scale has a Cronbach’s alpha of 0.947, demonstrating good reliability and validity, making it suitable for evaluating exercise adherence in university students [44].

Emotional management

Emotional management ability in this study was measured using the Emotional Intelligence Scale, developed by Mayer and Salovey in 1998, based on emotional intelligence theory. The scale was translated into Chinese by Wang Caikang in 2002 and is used to measure individuals’ abilities to perceive, understand, express, regulate, manage, and utilize emotions in themselves and others. The scale evaluates emotional perception, emotional facilitation of thinking, emotional understanding, and emotional management abilities across four dimensions. It uses a Likert scale format, with 33 items scored on a 5-point scale (1 = strongly disagree, 5 = strongly agree), with a total score range of 33 to 165 [17]. Higher scores indicate better emotional management skills [45]. The scale has been widely used and validated among Chinese university students [46, 47], with a Cronbach’s alpha of 0.84, showing good reliability and validity [48].

Life satisfaction

Life satisfaction was measured using the Satisfaction With Life Scale, developed by American scholars Diener et al. in the 1980s. The scale consists of 5 items, scored on a 7-point scale (0–6), with a total score ranging from 0 to 30. Higher total scores indicate greater life satisfaction, with 20 being the threshold between dissatisfaction and satisfaction [49]. Xiong Chengqing and colleagues conducted both Cronbach’s internal consistency (alpha) and split-half reliability analyses. The results showed a Cronbach’s alpha of 0.78 and split-half reliability of 0.70, indicating good internal consistency and reliability of the life satisfaction scale for measuring the general population’s life satisfaction [50].

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS 26.0 and Excel software. The steps were as follows: (1) Data obtained from Questionnaire Star were pre-processed using Excel software, and missing or problematic data were retested or deleted. (2) Harman’s single-factor test was used to examine common method bias to avoid common method variance issues. (3) Chi-square tests were used to analyse differences in health literacy between students of different sex and grade. Cramer’s V, an effect size measure for the chi-square test, indicates the strength of association between categorical variables on a scale from 0 to 1, where a higher value denotes a stronger relationship. Based on conventional thresholds, a value greater than 0.1 suggests a weak association, greater than 0.3 a moderate one, and greater than 0.5 a strong association [51].ANOVA was used to analyse emotional management ability, psychological resilience, and health literacy. The η² value ranges from 0 to 1; according to Cohen’s criteria, 0.01 represents a small effect, 0.06 represents a medium effect, and 0.14 represents a large effect [52]. (4) Pearson correlation analysis was used to examine the correlations between health literacy, exercise adherence, emotional management ability, and life satisfaction in chinese university students. (5) Mediation analysis was performed using regression analysis, and the PROCESS macro was used for multiple regression analysis, with bootstrapping to test the mediation effects.

In this study, as data were collected using self-reported subjective scales, Harman’s single-factor test was employed to assess common method bias. An exploratory factor analysis including all items from the health literacy, exercise adherence, emotional management ability, and life satisfaction scales was conducted. The results revealed seven factors with eigenvalues greater than 1. The first factor accounted for 40.347% of the variance, which is below the 50% threshold, indicating that common method bias is not a serious concern.

The results of the multiple regression analysis (see Table 4) show that, in predicting emotional management ability, we controlled for sex and grade. Health literacy was a significant predictor (β = 0.483, P < 0.001). For life satisfaction, the model included sex, grade, health literacy, and emotional management ability. Health literacy significantly predicted life satisfaction (β = 0.240, P < 0.001). Emotional management ability was also a significant positive predictor (β = 0.564, P < 0.001). The final model for exercise adherence included all variables. We controlled for sex, grade, health literacy, emotional management ability, and life satisfaction. Health literacy showed significant positive prediction (β = 0.618, P < 0.001). Emotional management ability was also a significant predictor (β = 0.582, P < 0.001). Life satisfaction positively predicted exercise adherence as well (β = 0.225, P < 0.001). This suggests that the direct effects of life satisfaction on exercise adherence and health literacy are weaker than those of the previous paths. In summary, the differences in path effect sizes reflect the hierarchical nature of the mechanisms between variables. Health literacy serves as the core driver. Emotional management ability acts as the key mediator. Life satisfaction constitutes the final link in the indirect transmission process.

Mediation analysis

As shown in Table 5, the 95% confidence interval (CI) for emotional management ability between health literacy and exercise adherence is [0.254, 0.308]. This interval does not include 0. Therefore, emotional management ability mediates the relationship between health literacy and exercise adherence in this study. The 95% CI for life satisfaction between health literacy and exercise adherence is [0.044, 0.065]. This interval also does not include 0, indicating that life satisfaction mediates the relationship between health literacy and exercise adherence. Additionally, the 95% CI for the chain mediation effect of emotional management ability and life satisfaction between health literacy and exercise adherence is [0.051, 0.071]. This suggests the presence of a chain mediation effect.

Discussion

Descriptive results analysis

According to Hypothesis H1, health literacy positively and significantly predicts exercise adherence among college students. This finding aligns with previous empirical research [53]. From a theoretical perspective, Basic Psychological Need Satisfaction is a core construct of Self-Determination Theory. It refers to the emotional memory sedimentation formed through individuals’ past exercise experiences. Furthermore, Basic Psychological Need Satisfaction serves as a deep-seated psychological mechanism driving exercise motivation and sustaining exercise behavior [54]. At the cognitive level, Basic Psychological Need Satisfaction functions as a critical cognitive cue. It influences individuals’ evaluation and decision-making logic regarding exercise behavior [55]. Specifically, when individuals consistently experience competence, autonomy, and relatedness during exercise, they are more likely to internalize exercise as a core practice for enhancing physical-mental vitality and self-development. This internalization fosters stable participation intentions and behavioral drive. Consequently, individuals exhibit sustained engagement and high concentration during exercise [56]. Regarding intervention mechanisms, initial implementation of health literacy enhancement measures should establish a multidimensional support system. This system helps deepen individuals’ recognition of the value of healthy lifestyles. Simultaneously, expanding access to health-related information significantly improves exercise self-efficacy. Throughout this process, individuals gain clear awareness of the health benefits of exercise. Additionally, they acquire knowledge to overcome exercise barriers effectively. This dual effect directly satisfies their basic psychological needs during exercise. Consequently, it strengthens intrinsic motivation and long-term maintenance capacity for exercise adherence.

The positive mediating effect of emotional management ability

According to Hypothesis H2, emotion management ability significantly mediates the relationship between health literacy and exercise adherence. This finding aligns with the Health Action Process Approach theory [36]. Health Action Process Approach theory posits that emotion regulation capacity is a critical factor for behavioral persistence in health contexts. Emotional management ability, as a core construct within this theoretical framework, encompasses three dimensions: accuracy of emotional recognition, flexibility in emotional regulation, and adaptability in emotional expression. It serves as a key psychological resource that enables individuals to maintain goal-directed behavior under stressful conditions [57]. In the context of exercise, the mediating role of emotional management ability is manifested through three interrelated mechanisms. Firstly, when individuals face physiological limits or environmental barriers, a high level of emotional management ability can effectively reduce the arousal of negative emotions through cognitive reappraisal strategies, thereby significantly decreasing the likelihood of impulsive abandonment of exercise [58]. Secondly, this ability reinforces a positive feedback loop between emotion and behavior: individuals who can effectively manage negative emotions such as anxiety and frustration are more likely to derive endogenous rewards from exercise, and this positive experience further enhances their intrinsic motivation to maintain regular exercise [59]. Furthermore, as a critical psychological resilience resource, emotional management ability enhances an individual’s sense of self-regulatory efficacy in complex situations. Research shows that individuals with stronger emotional management ability cope better with exercise-related challenges. Consequently, they maintain greater consistency and frequency in exercise behavior [60]. From a mechanistic perspective, stronger emotional management ability relies on the behavioral inhibition system dominated by the prefrontal cortex [61]. Regular exercise induces controllable physiological stress, which continuously challenges and gradually enhances the functional efficiency of this system [62]. This adaptation process at the physiological level builds and strengthens the capacity to inhibit impulsive responses [63], thereby altering an individual’s perception of exercise challenges and behavioral regulation.Therefore, emotional management ability functions not merely as a psychological mechanism sustaining exercise behavior, but also as a critical mediator through which health literacy influences exercise adherence.

The positive mediating effect of life satisfaction

According to Hypothesis H3, Life satisfaction also plays a mediating role in the relationship between health literacy and exercise adherence, although its effect is relatively modest compared to that of emotional management ability. This finding is comprehensively explained through the two-phase model of the Health Action Process Approach theory.First enters the motivational phase by enhancing health cognition efficacy. Critically, life satisfaction functions not merely as an emotional by-product but as the key psychological mechanism bridging rational cognition to sustained action. From a theoretical perspective, individuals with high health literacy are more likely to develop positive expectations regarding exercise behavior. This cognitive advantage enhances life satisfaction through two interrelated pathways. Firstly, individuals with higher health literacy tend to more accurately comprehend health information, identify disease risks, and make decisions beneficial to their health [64]. This clear understanding and sense of control over their health status significantly reduces health-related anxiety, thereby strengthening their confidence in their physical and life conditions. Secondly, health literacy empowers individuals to actively engage in health management and disease prevention [65]. Secondly, health literacy empowers individuals to actively engage in health management and disease prevention [65]. This proactive stance inherently reflects self-efficacy, thereby strengthening intrinsic achievement and well-being, which may subsequently elevate life satisfaction. Overall, health literacy constructs a cognitive framework for individuals to understand and manage their own health. This framework enables more effective adaptation to health-related challenges in daily life, reduces psychological stress caused by uncertainty, and thereby establishes a solid cognitive foundation for the sustained enhancement of life satisfaction. Health Action Process Approach’s action-phase model, elevated life satisfaction further strengthens behavioral control. When individuals maintain positive evaluations of their overall life state, they more readily integrate exercise into their self-identity. Consequently, they exhibit stronger situational regulation capacity when confronting barriers such as time conflicts or physical fatigue. Thus, the mediating effect of life satisfaction in sustaining exercise behavior is secondary and more limited compared to the powerful role of emotional management ability.

The serial mediation of emotional management ability and life satisfaction

According to Hypothesis H4, health literacy influences college students’ exercise adherence through the sequential mediation of emotion management ability and life satisfaction. Under the framework of the Health Action Process Approach, the influence of health literacy on university students’ exercise adherence demonstrates distinct stage-specific characteristics. In the initial motivation phase, health literacy establishes a cognitive foundation for students to comprehend health-related information. Research indicates that students with higher health literacy can more accurately grasp the intrinsic connection between exercise and physical and mental health, and clearly recognize the impact of emotional states on exercise outcomes. This deeper awareness further motivates them to proactively manage their emotions, thereby fully preparing them for subsequent engagement in regular exercise [66].As individuals progress to the actual action phase, university students often encounter frustrations from unmet expectations or anxiety stemming from academic pressure during exercise. At this stage, emotional management ability plays a critical role: students who effectively employ cognitive reappraisal strategies can reframe frustration as motivation for improvement, while those skilled in diverting attention can better alleviate anxiety [67]. Such successful regulation of negative emotions aligns with the core requirement of coping planning in the Health Action Process Approach theory, enabling individuals to effectively overcome obstacles encountered during exercise and thus ensuring the sustainability of their exercise behavior. Consistent exercise further yields positive outcomes such as enhanced physical fitness, improved mood, and expanded social interactions [67]. These positive experiences collectively contribute to an increase in individuals’ life satisfaction. Upon reaching the maintenance phase, this elevated life satisfaction forms an important positive feedback loop. According to the Health Action Process Approach theory, such positive psychological experiences continually reinforce students’ intrinsic identification with exercise behavior, transforming it from an externally imposed requirement maintained by willpower into a self-driven choice, ultimately leading to the long-term maintenance of exercise adherence [36].

Health literacy, as a cognitive foundation, emotional management as a process-regulating variable, and life satisfaction as a behavioral reinforcement element, collectively form a dynamic and adaptive progressive transmission mechanism under the Health Action Process Approach framework. This mechanism validates the theory’s explanatory power regarding the multi-stage nature of health behaviors. It also provides a layered intervention pathway for exercise promotion in universities.

At the cognitive level, it is essential to strengthen the cognitive construction of the relationship between emotions and health within health literacy. At the skill level, systematic training in emotional management ability strategies is required. At the environmental level, an exercise support system that enhances life satisfaction needs to be established. Ultimately, these efforts aim to improve university students’ exercise adherence systematically.

Limitations

This study has the following limitations: (1) The data rely on self-report questionnaires, which may introduce recall bias. Future research could combine objective measurement tools to enhance reliability and validity [68]. (2) All findings are based on a cross-sectional design, which does not imply causal relationships. Therefore, when describing the predictive roles of variables in the structural equation model, the conclusions mainly rely on theoretical assumptions. Future studies should adopt longitudinal designs to determine the causal relationships between variables [69]0.3)Some of the patterns observed in this study may not fit other populations because of cultural differences [70], [71], [72].

Conclusion

Health literacy not only directly and positively predicts exercise adherence among university students, but it also indirectly enhances exercise behavior. This occurs through the chain mediation pathway of emotional management ability and life satisfaction. Both emotional management ability and life satisfaction play significant mediating roles. This validates the progressive cognitive-affective-behavioral transmission mechanism in the Health Action Process Approach theory. Improving health literacy requires strengthening emotional management ability strategies and life satisfaction interventions. These efforts can systematically optimize the long-term maintenance of exercise behavior among university students.

Sclareol Inhibit Cell Growth and Induces Apoptosis via Activation of p53/p21 Signaling Pathways in Human Oral Squamous Cell Carcinoma Cells
Authors: Anandhi Nallu, Suresh Kathiresan, Theerthu Azhamuthu, Hemavardhani Ramadoss, Nihal Ahamed Abulkalam Asath, Pugazhendhi Ravichandran, Rajeswari Vasu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTOne of the most common prevalent forms of cancer in the world is oral squamous cell carcinoma (OSCC). In the present study, we explore the chemotherapeutic effect of Sclareol on human oral carcinoma KB cells in scientifically found that cellular and molecular alterations. The human oral carcinoma KB cell and African green monkey kidney normal cell line (Vero) were evaluated for a several types of investigations following they had been various concentrations of Sclareol (5–50 µM) and cisplatin (0–10 µM) for 24 h.Additionally, we exhibited that sclareol induces cytotoxicity effect by MTT assay, Reactive oxidative stress (ROS) was measured by DCFH‐DA staining assay, loss of mitochondrial membrane potential (Δψm) was assessed by Rho123 staining method, nuclear condensation, and apoptotic morphological changes was investigated by DAPI and AO/EtBr staining in KB cells. Sclareol promoted oxidative stress via an imbalance in redox status and increased DNA damage in human oral carcinoma KB cells. Besides, Sclareol induces apoptosis by activating dependent p21waf1/cip1, thereby down‐regulating the expression levels of phosphorylation of Rb (ser 780), cyclin D1, and cyclin‐E. Furthermore, it is associated with mitochondrial‐mediated apoptotic and proapoptotic markers, as evidenced by the downregulated Bcl‐2 protein and the upregulation of (Bax, Cytochrome C, Cleaved PARP, Cleaved Caspase and Caspase‐9 &‐3) induced apoptosis signaling through p53 independent pathways. Therefore, our present study findings suggest that Sclareol could be the productive new chemotherapeutic agent for against oral squamous cell carcinoma.Keywords: apoptosis, cytotoxicity, OSCC, oxidative stress, Sclareol

Introduction

Oral squamous cell carcinoma (OSCC) poses a significant health concern, ranking as the sixth most prevalent cancer in developing countries [1]. The global estimates for the GLOBOCAN recommend that in 2020, there were 10.0 million fatalities and 19.3 million new cases of cancer. Moreover, the global demographic projections, by 2040, there would be 2.8 million cancer cases in India, 57.5% over from 2020 statistics [2, 3]. Among the several etiological factors, the uses of tobacco products, alcohol, chewing betel quid, smoking cigarettes, and infection with the human papillomavirus (HPV) were associated with OSCC [4]. Most of the research has been done recently to evaluate the cancer chemoprevention management options [5, 6]. Nowadays, chemoprevention treatment increases the survival rates of OSCC patients by perhaps inhibiting or delaying the growth of new carcinomas.

Cancer is characterized by hallmarks that are present in all types of cancer. Among those, uncontrolled cell proliferation and apoptosis evasion are the key hallmarks contributing to malignancy progression [7]. The production of apoptotic bodies, membrane blebbing, nuclear chromatin decrease, cell shrinkage, and DNA breakage characterize apoptosis [8]. Apoptosis occurs via two mechanisms, the death receptor pathway and the mitochondrial pathway, which both converge at the activation of Caspase‐3 severing cellular targets; apoptosis has been promoted [9, 10]. Cancer progression is connected with the subsequent events, including cellular dissimilarity, dysfunctional apoptotic genes, deregulation of a tumor‐suppressing gene, and extreme proliferation [11].

The role of the tumour suppressor protein p53 in the cellular response to several intracellular and extracellular threats, including DNA damage, oncogenic activation, and microtubule disruption, is increasingly characterized [12]. p21waf1/cip1 is an inhibitor of cyclin‐dependent kinases (CDK), a mediator of p53, and a marker for cell growth. p21 is an appropriate cell cycle movement, cell demise, DNA fixing, senescence, and development [13].

Phytochemicals have been preferable as simple cost and minimal side effects and are known to exhibit several medicinal properties. Sclareol (labd‐14‐ene‐8, 13‐diol), a bioactive labdane type of diterpene, mostly taken from the readily available plant Salvia sclarea (Family Lamiaceae), is an essential oil used in Southeast Asia, like China and the Mediterranean region, for traditional medicine, food, beverage industry, and scent creation [14, 15]. Sclareol has various pharmaceutical activities, including neuroprotective, antioxidant, antifungal, anticancer, antimicrobial, and anti‐inflammatory [16, 17, 18, 19, 20, 21].

Previously, many researchers have explored the various biological mechanisms underlying the down‐regulated proto‐oncogene c‐myc, which interferes with the cell cycle and promotes apoptosis [22]. In addition, the sclareol exhibits potent cytotoxicity against different types of human malignant cell lines, such as colorectal, breast, and leukaemia cancer lines [23, 24, 25]. Therefore, the current study investigates the sclareol has not yet been anticancer properties against in human oral carcinoma KB cells. To do this, we examined the impact of sclareol on cytotoxicity productive, oxidative DNA damage, and importantly, the following biomarkers p51/p21 lead to induced apoptosis and inhibit cell growth in human oral cancer KB cells.

Materials and Methods

Chemicals

Dulbecco's modified Eagle medium (DMEM), Fetal bovine serum (FBS), Phosphate‐buffered saline (PBS), Alamar blue (7‐hydroxy‐3H‐phenoxazine‐3‐one‐10‐oxide), Ethidium bromide (EtBr), Rhodamine 123, Acridine Orange (AO) and 4, 6‐ Diamidino‐2‐phenylindole (DAPI) were procured from Hi‐Media Laboratories Private Limited, Mumbai, India. Sclareol (> 98%), Cisplatin, Acrylamide, 2‐mercaptoethanol, Sodium dodecyl sulphate (SDS), Bovine serum albumin (BSA), and Monoclonal antibodies such as β‐actin, Caspase‐3, Caspase‐9, Cleaved Caspases‐3, Cleaved PARP, Cytochrome‐c, Bax, and Bcl‐2, were purchased from Sigma‐Aldrich, USA. Monoclonal antibodies for Phospho‐Rb (Ser780), Cyclin D1, Cyclin E, p21 waf1/cip1, p53, and secondary conjugated anti‐mouse antibodies were obtained from Santa Cruz Biotechnology. Fisher Inorganic Chennai has bestowed the remaining analytical‐grade chemicals.

Cell Growth and Maintains

The human oral carcinoma cell line (KB), and African green monkey kidney normal cell line (Vero cells) was secured from the National Centre for Cell Science (NCCS), Pune. The cells were maintained with combined DMEM and 10% FBS (Sigma‐Aldrich, St. Louis, MO, USA). Then enhanced with 100 units/mL penicillin G and 100 μg/mL streptomycin as an antibiotic (Hi‐Media, Mumbai, India) at 37°C in a moisturized atmosphere containing 5% CO2.

Drug Preparation

Sclareol was dissolved in 0.05% DMSO at 1 mg/1 mL as a stock solution, and dilutions were DMEM medium to hold the preferred concentration with five 10‐fold dilutions with the new medium before use.

MTT Assay

Cell growth was determined by mitochondrial dehydrogenase activity in living cells using MTT. Because mitochondrial dehydrogenase in living cells converts the yellow tetrazolium salt MTT to the purple MTT formazan, the 3‐(4,5‐dimethylthiazol‐2‐yl)‐2,5‐diphenyltetrazolium bromide (MTT) assay is based on the measurement of mitochondrial activity [26]. The 96‐well plate KB cells and Vero cells were sowed (2 × 104 cells/mL) and treated with different concentrations of sclareol (5–100 μM) and another various concentration of cisplatin (CP) up to (0–15 μM) for 24 h. After treatment, cells were added 10 µL of MTT (5 mg/ml) reagent and incubated at 37°C for 3 h. Then, 100 µL of DMSO was transfused into each well for 30 min incubation; after that, violet crystal formazan. An optimise concentration was determined at a wavelength of 570 nm by using a Multimode reader (Molecular Devices).

Determination of Reactive Oxygen Species Intracellular Levels (ROS)

ROS intracellular changes were measured by the 2, 7‐dichlorofluorescein DCFH‐DA method [27]. A six‐well plate was seeded with KB cells (1 × 105 cells/well); then, cells were treated with sclareol (5, 10, and 20 µM) and cisplatin (3 µM) for 24 h. After treatment completion, the cells were added 5 µl of DCFH‐DA dye for 30 min at 370 C in the dark condition and rinsed off twice with PBS to remove the unnecessary dye. The fluorescence intensity level was assessed at 485 ± 10 and 530 ± 12.5 using a Multimode reader (Molecular Devices), and the photographic image was captured using a (Floid Cell Imaging Station, Life Technologies‐USA).

Measurement of Mitochondrial Membrane Potential Alteration (∆ψ M)

The role of sclareol on the distraction of mitochondrial membrane potential was estimated by Rhodamine‐123, a lipophilic cationic dye [28]. Six‐well plates were seeded with KB cells (2 × 105 cells/well), and the cells were exposed to several concentrations of sclareol (5, 10, and 20 μM) and cisplatin (3 µM) for 24 h. Following the treatment cells were incubated for 30 min at 37°C in a dark environment using by Rh‐123 staining dye. The fluorescence intensity was measured with a Multimode reader at 485 ± 10 and 530 ± 12 nm. Mitochondrial depolarisation patterns of cells were noticed from the fluorescence microscopy (Floid Cell Imaging Station, Life Technologies, USA).

Detection of Apoptotic Cells With Dual Staining (AO/EtBr)

The dual staining (AO/EtBr) method was used to detect necrotic and apoptotic cell morphological changes [29]. The morphological changes in KB cells were treated with distinguished concentrations of sclareol (5, 10, and 20 µM) and cisplatin (3 µM) for 24 h. After incubation, the cells were stained with a 1:1 ratio (2 µL) of AO/EtBr for 30 min at dark conditions. The staining cells were rinsed off with PBS and examined minutely photographic with a fluorescence microscope (Floid Cell Imaging Station). Finally, an action of the total number of cells in the field was used to determine the % of cells presenting apoptotic signals.

DAPI with Nuclear Staining Assay

DAPI staining was employed to evaluate excessive cell morphology and identify nuclear condensation [30]. The KB cells were exposed to varying doses of sclareol (5, 10, and 20 µM) and cisplatin (3 µM) for 24 h. After incubation, cells were stabilized with 4% paraformaldehyde and Triton X‐100 and stained with (1 mg/mL) DAPI solution for 30 min in a dark place. The nuclear damage was analysed by a fluorescence microscope Floid Cell Imaging Station (Life Technologies, USA).

Measurement of DNA Damage By Alkaline Comet Assay

Lorenzo et al. used by the alkaline single‐cell electrophoresis comet assay approach to assess the level of DNA damage [31]. sclareol following treatment (5, 10, and 20 µM) and cisplatin (3 µM) were KB cells for 24 h incubation. The 30% suspended KB cells were mixed with 100 µl of low melting agarose (0.5%), pipetted out, and kept on the pre‐coated slides. The slides were submerged in a cold lysis buffer (pH‐10) and kept at 4°C for 1 h. After cell lysis, these slides were placed in a fresh alkaline buffer (pH‐13), and electrophoresis was performed (22 V and 200 mA) for 20 min. Then, the slides were removed and rinsed off with a neutralized buffer (pH‐7.5) for 2–4 times. Then, they were stained with 0.5 mg/mL of EtBr for 15 min in a dark place. The DNA damage was examined minutely by an epifluorescence microscope (40X) (Nikon, Eclipse Ti2E, Japan), and the comet image was quantified using CASP software.

Immunoblotting Analysis

The immunoblotting examination was performed on KB cells treated with sclareol following different experimental conditions. The 50 µg of protein underwent separation via 10% SDS‐PAGE gel electrophoresis and was subsequently transferred onto a PVDF membrane (Bio‐Rad). The membrane was sealed using TBS with 5% BSA for 2 h and then left with the specific 1° monoclonal antibodies at 4°C. The membranes were washed with TBST buffer. Then, the membrane was treated with peroxidase (2° antibodies) for 2 h at room temperature. The membranes were washed three times with TBST over 10 min [32]. The protein bands were identified using a chemiluminescence kit known as ECL. These protein bands were analysed through densitometry using Image J software to examine the displayed images.

Statistical Analysis

All data were ordered to analyse using in one‐way ANOVA on the SPSS 21.0 software program. DMRT identified the group's mean ± standard deviation (SD) values, and the results were considered as p < 0.05 to test the significance.

Discussion

Natural substances have long been the preferred option for all individuals because they are indispensable to global healthcare and traditional medical systems [33]. The role of bioactive substances in apoptotic induction, cell proliferation, oxidative stress response, and important signaling pathways has been developed recently for the treatment of oral cancer [34]. Phytochemicals that are important include isoprenoids, carotenoids, polyphenols, phytosterols, saponins, and certain polysaccharides. These compounds have potent antiviral, antibacterial, and antioxidant properties [35]. Additionally, they support the regulation of gene transcription, strengthen the body's immunity, and promote gap junction communication [36]. Sclareol (SCL, Labd‐14‐ene‐8, 13‐diol), a diterpene alcohol that is abundant in calyx capitate oil glands, was mostly extracted from Salvia sclarea L. inflorescences [37]. Additionally, Sclareol prevented the development of MMPs induced by UV‐B rays and controlled the degradation of collagen by reducing the protein expression of AP‐1 transcription factors [38].

In this present work, we aim to establish the scientific means that the chemotherapeutic impact of sclareol on human oral carcinoma KB cancer cells undergo apoptosis and cell death. Here, we finding that the treatment of KB cells by sclareol could inhibit their cell viability, ROS generation, cell proliferation, caspase activation and induce apoptosis through p53/p21 dependent and independent signaling pathway. Several researchers have recently reported that sclareol inhibits cell proliferation in regions of the breast, lung, colon, and leukaemia cancer cell lines, but its mechanism is unclear [23, 24, 25]. To the finest of our understanding, this is the first exploration to reveal that sclareol may also prevent the formation of OSCC.

Therefore, this study revealed that the cytoprotective effects of sclareol treatment in KB cell and Vero cells considerably reduce the percentage of cell viability in a time and dose‐dependent manner. We also observed that the inhibitory concentration of sclareol IC50 value (20 µM) responsive dose explored in KB cells for 24 h. Furthermore, we investigated that sclareol treated with African green monkey kidney Normal cell line (Vero), were exposed to low range of toxicity up to 50 µM. In contrast, sclareol in noncancerous human dermal fibroblast line (Hs68) exhibited low toxicity levels without any damage after 24 h [39].

Moreover, sclareol inhibited cell proliferation, ROS generation, DNA damage and induced apoptosis via mitochondrial‐mediated in KB cells. Thus, sclareol would be a novel medicinal therapeutic drug used to treatment of human OSCC in the future. Notably, the higher levels of sclareol were found to have promoted ROS generation, further demonstrating the intimate relationship between ROS production and cell viability and apoptosis [40]. This finding suggested that sclareol may have influenced these processes through the ROS pathway. In addition, the previous research revealed that ROS generation may impact malignant cells' survival and death [41].

The production of ROS is a chemopreventive drug therapy that alters multiple signalling pathways and induces early apoptosis [40]. The significantly enhanced intracellular ROS levels have led to nuclear condensation, DNA damage, and the p53‐activated apoptosis process in numerous cell lines [42]. In this present study, we observed ROS levels by using DCFH‐DA staining methods in KB cells treated with sclareol various concentrations at (5, 10, and 20 µM) and cisplatin (3 µM) compared to untreated control KB cells. A number of apoptosis regulators suppress the loss of mitochondrial integrity generated by ROS production, which occurs primarily in the mitochondria, an important signalling the centre during cell death [43]. Similarly, Sclareol treatment of KB cells was found to induce apoptosis by activating the mitochondrial membrane potential to Sclareol in a dose‐dependent manner. In addition, sclareol inhibits cell cycle arrest in the G1 phase in different cancer cells and widely leads to apoptosis and loss of ΛΨm [44].

Previously, Theerthu et. al., (2024), explored that DAPI, a blue‐fluorescent DNA‐binding dye that distinguishes between viable and impaired cells and ideally combines apoptosis and necrosis, has been employed to evaluate the shape of apoptotic nuclei in KB cell [45]. AO/EtBr and DAPI nuclear morphological alterations, whereas most of the nuclei in the cancer cells treated with sclareol were compressed and broken up, as is typical of cell death. Sclareol‐mediated apoptosis mechanisms take advantage of the apoptotic pathway by producing chronic DNA damage, leading to mitotic failure.

Apoptotic bodies are formed by the combination of chromatin condensation, DNA fragmentation, and plasma membrane blebbing, which are characteristic of apoptotic cell death [46]. The high level of ROS concentrations destroy cells and promote many metabolic alterations, including oxidative DNA damage, which present as single‐strand breaks (SSB) and double‐strand breaks (DSB) and generally develops by modifications of purine and pyrimidine bases [47]. Several researchers has revealed that phytochemicals contributed to cancer cells in activate apoptosis by increasing ROS generation, and oxidative DNA damage [48, 49]. Therefore, we observed enhanced DNA damage in the percentage of DNA fragmentation, tail moment, olive tail movement, and increased nuclear fragmentation in the sclareol‐treated KB cells compared to untreated control cells (Figure 5A, B).

Cancer cells are known to assist from p53 inactivation or mutant p53 expression in conditions such as enhanced proliferation, apoptosis avoidance, and chemoresistance [50]. The p53, the primary regulator of the cell cycle and cell fate, activates p21, an inhibitor of the cell cycle progression, in response to DNA damage. p21 prevents cell cycle progression in the G1‐S phase by blocking cyclin D1 through retinoblastoma protein (Rb) [51].

The p21 protein associates with CDK2, CDK4, and CDK6 to generate an inhibitory complex that inhibits the cell cycle from progressing through the G1‐S phase transition [52]. In addition to possessing constitutively expressed, cyclin E1 and cyclin D1 have been implicated in the aetiology and carcinogenesis of most human tumours. More significantly, they constitute exciting targets for cancer treatment [53]. In our present study, results showed that sclareol significantly decreased the expression of cyclin E, retinoblastoma‐Rb, and cyclin D1 in a dose‐dependent manner in KB cells, providing evidence of sclareol inhibiting cell proliferation and regulating the cell division through activating p21‐dependent apoptotic signaling.

As apoptosis initiating through a p53‐independent or ‐dependent pathway [53]. It is typically challenging to combat cancer cells with mutations in apoptotic markers like p53 or the Bcl family or that alter caspase signalling. Cancer treatment also involves the caspase‐dependent apoptotic cell death mechanism [54]. As our western blotting analysis, Sclareol also significantly enhanced proapoptotic proteins Bax, cytochrome‐C, cleaved PARP, cleaved caspase‐3, caspase‐3 and 9, and downregulate anti‐apoptotic protein Bcl‐2 in KB cells. As a result, Sclareol suggested that formation of ROS, which causes DNA damage, caspase activation, and cytochrome c export into the cytoplasm, and an increased Bax/Bcl‐2 ratio, it may therefore improve the mitochondrial‐mediated intrinsic apoptotic mechanism in KB cells. Consequently, previous reports suggested that sclareol persistently inhibits Bcl‐2 and up‐regulates BAX in osteosarcoma cells. Because sclareol regulates Bcl‐2, it may be able to overcome cancer chemoresistance [24].

Similarly, A high ROS level produces the release of free Cyt‐c into the cytosol, decreased mitochondrial membrane permeability, and caspase‐9‐mediated caspase‐3 activation [55]. In these current, Sclareol treatment with KB cells, indicating that p53 independent pathway is essential for regulation of cell division, cell proliferation suppression, and apoptosis induction. Previous studies have revealed that Sclareol inhibits the cell proliferation of human colon cancer cells in a p53‐independent apoptosis manner [56]. In addition, Resveratrol (Res‐Nano) has suggested that the inhibition of oral cancer stem cell (CSC) growth by deregulating the major apoptosis signaling pathways (Wnt and Hedgehog) through activation of tumour suppressor proteins p53 independent, and p21‐dependent manner [57].

Based on these results, we found that Sclareol inhibit cell growth and induce apoptosis through mediate p53/p21‐dependent and independent signalling and caspases activation by mitochondrial‐mediated pathways. Recently, Bou et al. (2023) have reported that AGA induced apoptosis and cell cycle arrest on p21‐dependent and p53‐independent/dependent pathways might be involved inhibiting in colon cancer cells [58].

Conclusion

In conclusion, our present study suggests that sclareol inhibits cell growth and induces apoptosis by activating p53/p21 signaling pathway in KB cell line. Our results presented strong experimental evidence for sclareol's substantial cytotoxic action on KB cells from human oral cancer. Moreover, the cytotoxicity response was found to be mediated by the disruption of redox homeostasis of ROS generation and altered MMP; therefore, apoptosis was provoked. Furthermore, Sclareol induce apoptosis signaling pathway through enhancing the release of Bax and Cytochrome c from mitochondria, which promotes the activation of Caspase‐9 and Caspase‐3 and downregulation of Rb, cyclin D1, cyclin E through mitochondrial‐mediated intrinsic apoptotic mechanism. More importantly, Sclareol also prevents cell growth by p21/p53 independent and dependent which lead to the apoptosis signaling. The current study proposed that sclareol has anticancer properties and that it may be employed as a novel anticancer agent to treat patients with oral carcinoma.

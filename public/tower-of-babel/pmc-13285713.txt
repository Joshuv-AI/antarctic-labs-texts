Targeting CPR skills dissemination in schools and communities: the adapted Szpilman’s mannikin and AED
Authors: D Szpilman, CO Cardoso, PN Sugimoto, BW Böttiger, NK Nakagawa
Published: 2026

To the Editor,

Training students in basic life support is essential for health education to improve responses in out-of-hospital cardiac arrest.1 The World Restart a Heart and the KIDS SAVE LIVES (KSL) are well-recognized initiatives to raise community awareness, engagement and encouragement to provide cardiopulmonary resuscitation (CPR) prior to emergency service arrival to improve outcomes.1, 2 Among several devices used for simulated trainings, an AED simulator and an intermediate to high-fidelity mannikin have been used for real-time feedback. However, the cost of these tools may not be feasible for CPR-training in large events, low-resource settings, virus pandemic, or very populated areas.

The KSL Brazil project has used a CPR-kit (Fig. 1) composed by the adapted David Szpilman’s mannikin, created in 2018,3 and an illustrated automated external defibrillator (AED) in a cardboard. This CPR-kit allows the sudden cardiac arrest and CPR simulation. Learners can check awareness, call for help, check respiration, perform chest compression (from correct hands placement in the chest using visual location on the cardboard), mouth-to-mouth ventilations and AED use (including pads placement in the chest). The important novelties of the adapted Szpilman’s mannikin are: (1) the up-side down placement of the left and the right plastic bottles to mimic shoulders (Fig. 1F) for checking victim’s responsiveness; (2) the addition of two anatomical markings on the cardboard for hands placement,4 one in the apex of the manubrium and one in the xiphoid process of the sternum (Fig. 1J); and (3) a cut in the illustrated face on the cardboard to simulate chin for opening the airway and mouth-to-mouth ventilation (Fig. 1O). There are some limitations with the adapted Szpilman’s mannikin: the chest compression and ventilation quality are visual assessed. However, compared with manufactured ones, this CPR-kit is great valuable considering democratization of the education process (simplicity to find recycled low-cost materials and easy-to-build, feasibility, skills retention, augmented confidence by repetition, empowerment by doing themselves and allowance to widespread the teaching–learning process).

Construction flowchart of the Szpilman’s manikin and AED for CPR-training.

The initial results of an ongoing work scoring knowledge, skills and attitude using the Szpilman’s CPR-kit are: before CPR-training, 857 students participated in the study (25.3% aged 9–12 years and 74.7% aged 13–20 years). Few would know how to check responsiveness (7.6%), call medical service (30%), know how to check breathing (7.6%), know how to provide chest compressions (2%), and use the AED (6%). After CPR-training, the great majority of students clearly identified the emergence situation and checked local safety (98.5%), assessed responsiveness (98.5%) called for help (97%), assessed breathing (98.5%), performed CPR, and adequately used the AED (95.5%). The Szpilman’s CPR-kit enhances the CPR teaching–learning process in children and adolescents, that may widespread chest compression and ventilation competences to their families, friends, neighbors and other school members. The construction process of the Szpilman’s manikin is detailed in a video tutorial (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=g_P_QQDfQyo).5

Experimental Investigation of Availability in a 4.6 km 4.6\text{\,}\mathrm{k}\mathrm{m} Terrestrial Urban Coherent Free-Space Optical Communications Link
Vincent van Vliet, Menno van den Hout, Kadir Gümüş, Eduward Tangdiongga, and Chigo Okonkwo

Abstract




1
Introduction

Terrestrial
free-space optical (FSO)
communication links have been shown to accommodate multi-terabit-per-second data transmission over multi-kilometre wireless urban channels
[
1
]
. Combining the flexibility of wireless communication systems with the high capacity of fibre-optic transmission technologies,
FSO
communications is attractive for applications such as backhaul for cellular networks, campus connectivity, and disaster recovery
[
2
]
. When operated with commonly used optical frequencies, such as the C-band, mature high-quality fibre-optic components enable seamless integration with existing fibre-optic infrastructure. However, key to the successful adaptation of terrestrial
FSO
communications, even in hybrid systems, is a thorough understanding of the reliability and availability of these communication links. Previous experimental studies provide initial insights
[
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
]
, but were performed over short distances, over brief periods of time, and/or with low data rates.

In this work, we measure the transmission performance of a coherent communication signal over a
4.6\text{\,}\mathrm{km}
urban
FSO
channel for over six days, during which the weather ranges from clear sky to thick fog. We evaluate the outage probability and corresponding availability by analysing the decoded data rate. Furthermore, we correlate the data transmission performance with simultaneous high-speed measurements of the power coupled into the receiver fibre and with visibility measurements from a nearby weather station. We differentiate between fast and slow fades to investigate the respective effects on the outage probability. We find that deep slow fades are the primary contributor to the outage probability, in the worst case completely prohibiting data transmission. We observe a significantly smaller impact from fast fades, achieving
500\text{\,}\mathrm{G}\mathrm{b}\mathrm{/}\mathrm{s}
data transmission with an overall availability of
99\text{\,}\%
outside of deep slow fades.

Fibre-coupled optical terminals, prototypes of commercial devices from the development partner Aircision, are employed to establish the urban
FSO
link of
4.6\text{\,}\mathrm{km}
. Spanning the city of Eindhoven, the Netherlands, the link is part of the permanent Reid Photonloop

Figure

An automated tracking and pointing system in the optical terminals provides continuous optimisation of the free-space-to-fibre coupling. To further mitigate fluctuations in the received optical power, a pre-amplifier
EDFA
was operated in continuous output power mode at the receiver
[
8
]
. A
wavelength selective switch (WSS)
was used to sequentially select one of the three
WDM
channels from the amplified signal, after which it was mixed with a
local oscillator (LO)
in a coherent receiver. The electrical outputs were digitised for
10\text{\,}\mathrm{\SIUnitSymbolMicro}
per capture using a 4-channel
80\text{\,}\mathrm{G}\mathrm{S}\mathrm{/}\mathrm{s}

RTO
, taking 25 captures before switching to the next
WDM
channel. A short duration is required between the measurement of each
WDM
channel to tune the
LO
and
WSS
. Offline
digital signal processing
, mainly consisting of a decision-directed
multiple-input multiple-output
equalizer with an in-loop
blind phase search
algorithm, was used to recover the transmitted signal
[
9
]
. Metrics such as
generalised mutual information
were determined to evaluate the transmission performance, together with a more practical decoding scheme combining
low-density parity-check
codes from the DVB-S2 standard with code-rate puncturing and an additional hard-decision outer code to obtain error-free transmission
[
10
]
.

Simultaneously to data transmission, a fraction of the optical power before the pre-amplifier
EDFA
was tapped to continuously measure the power in the receiver fibre with a
10\text{\,}\mathrm{k}\mathrm{S}\mathrm{/}\mathrm{s}
power meter (PM). In addition, an
optical spectrum analyser (OSA)
was employed to capture the received spectrum. Moreover, a tap after the optical pre-amplifier and an optical switch enabled measurements of the amplified output power and spectrum.

Information on fast fades is extracted from the power meter measurements by fitting it to a log-normal model to calculate the Rytov variance (
\sigma^{2}_{l}
) per minute
[
11
]
. The Rytov variance is a measure of irradiance fluctuations, mainly caused by the atmosphere and the automatic alignment system’s residual tracking error, with a larger Rytov variance corresponding to stronger fluctuations. For irradiance fluctuations induced by atmospheric turbulence,
\sigma^{2}_{l}\leq 1
is typically considered as weak-to-moderate optical turbulence, while
\sigma^{2}_{l}>1
indicates strong irradiance fluctuations
[
12
]
. As the method for determining the Rytov variance requires received signal power, it is not plotted during the deep slow fades mentioned earlier.

The decoded data rate
R_{dec}

RTO

Outside these periods, the vast majority of the captured signals are decoded successfully. By defining the outage probability for a given decoded data rate
R
as
P_{out}=P(R_{dec}\leq R)

Fig.

25.44\text{\,}\%
for
R
=
500\text{\,}\mathrm{G}\mathrm{b}\mathrm{/}\mathrm{s}
, for example. When taking all
RTO
captures into account, the corresponding overall outage probability for the >6 days is
8.37\text{\,}\%
. It becomes evident that deep slow fading is the primary contributor to this when we remove the
RTO

Fig.

R
=
500\text{\,}\mathrm{G}\mathrm{b}\mathrm{/}\mathrm{s}

0.40\text{\,}\%
outside of deep slow fades. Similarly, the overall outage probability drops to
1.06\text{\,}\%
. As expected, corresponding outage probabilities for days without deep slow fades are equal in both figures, for example
0.37\text{\,}\%0.67\text{\,}\%0.57\text{\,}\%
for October 24, 25, and 26, respectively.

These results highlight the two-sided nature of availability and reliability in terrestrial
FSO
communication links. Fast fading increases the outage probability through short outages. These detrimental effects on data transmission performance can be mitigated by using techniques for optimising free-space-to-fibre coupling, such as the automated tracking and pointing system used here, and link adaptation techniques, such as adaptive coding, error correction, retransmission and interleaving. For example, in this work the overall outage probability outside of deep slow fades can be reduced by
43\text{\,}\%
by operating with a code rate of
0.52\text{\,}
instead of
0.87\text{\,}
, at the cost of transmission rate. On the other hand, deep slow fading can cause a complete loss of optical signal power for extended periods, rendering data communication impossible. With limited available transmit power due to eye-safety requirements, it is impossible to mitigate this effect in a single link. This underscores the need for integration of terrestrial
FSO
links in a hybrid network, if high availability is required.

4
Conclusions and outlook

We measured the transmission performance of a coherent communication signal over a
4.6\text{\,}\mathrm{km}
urban
FSO
channel for over six days. We find that deep slow fades are the primary contributor to the outage probability. In the future we plan to expand the Reid Photonloop
FSO
test bed with dedicated instruments for atmospheric measurements to enable more in-depth and longer-term investigation of the urban
FSO
channel and its availability and reliability.

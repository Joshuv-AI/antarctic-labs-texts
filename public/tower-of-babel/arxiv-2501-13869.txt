Abstract
. In this note we show that the support of a locally k k -uniform measure in ℝ n + 1 \mathbb{R}^{n+1} satisfies a kind of unique continuation property. As a consequence, we show that locally uniformly distributed measures satisfy a weaker unique continuation property. This continues work of Kirchheim and Preiss in [ KP02 ] and David, Kenig and Toro in [ DKT01 ] and lends additional evidence to the conjecture proposed by Kowalski and Preiss in [ KP87 ] that each connected component of the support of a locally n n -uniform measure in ℝ n + 1 \mathbb{R}^{n+1} is contained in the zero set of a quadratic polynomial.

Before proving

Proof of
Theorem

1.2
.

We mainly follow the first part of
[
KP02

. The Lebesgue differentiation theorem implies that the limit

K(y)\coloneqq\lim_{r\to 0}\frac{\mu(B(y,r))}{(\mu+{\mathcal{H}}^{k}|_{\operatorname{supp}\mu})(B(y,r))}

exists at
(\mu+{\mathcal{H}}^{k}|_{\operatorname{supp}\mu})
-a.e.
y\in\operatorname{supp}\mu
, and that

(2.1)

\mu(E)=\int_{E}K(y)\,d(\mu+{\mathcal{H}}^{k}|_{\operatorname{supp}\mu})(y)\text{ for any }E\subset B(z_{0},r_{0}),

since
\mu
is (clearly) absolutely continuous with respect to
\mu+{\mathcal{H}}^{k}|_{\operatorname{supp}\mu}
, i.e.,
\mu\ll(\mu+{\mathcal{H}}^{k}|_{\operatorname{supp}\mu})
. Note also that the lower bound in (
1.2
) implies
K(y)>0
for
(\mu+{\mathcal{H}}^{k}|_{\operatorname{supp}\mu})
-a.e.
y\in\operatorname{supp}\mu
.

Fixed a (any) point
p\in\operatorname{supp}\mu
, we define

g_{\mu}(r)\coloneqq\mu(B(p,r))\text{ for }0<r\leq 1,

see (
1.2
). By the flatness assumption
B(z_{0},r_{0})\cap\operatorname{supp}\mu=B(z_{0},r_{0})\cap P
with
k
-plane
P
, for
y\in B(z_{0},r_{0})\cap\operatorname{supp}\mu
we have

K(y)=\lim_{r\to 0}\frac{g_{\mu}(r)}{g_{\mu}(r)+w_{k}r^{k}}\leq 1,

which is independent of
y\in B(z_{0},r_{0})\cap\operatorname{supp}\mu
. Since
K(y)
exists
(\mu+{\mathcal{H}}^{k}|_{\operatorname{supp}\mu})
-a.e., we conclude that the limit
K(y)
exists for all
y\in B(z_{0},r_{0})\cap\operatorname{supp}\mu
. Furthermore, since
K(y)>0
for
(\mu+{\mathcal{H}}^{k}|_{\operatorname{supp}\mu})
-a.e.
y\in\operatorname{supp}\mu
, it follows that
0<K(y)\leq 1
for all
y\in B(z_{0},r_{0})\cap\operatorname{supp}\mu
.

We define (before checking it makes sense)

c\coloneqq\lim_{r\to 0}\frac{g_{\mu}(r)}{w_{k}r^{k}}.

We have that this limit exists by the existence of the limit
K(y)
for all
y\in B(z_{0},r_{0})\cap\operatorname{supp}\mu
and since

\frac{1}{c}=\lim_{r\to 0}\frac{w_{k}r^{k}}{g_{\mu}(r)}=\lim_{r\to 0}\frac{w_{k}r^{k}+g_{\mu}(r)}{g_{\mu}(r)}-1=\frac{1}{K(y)}-1.

Moreover, the fact that
K(y)>0
implies that
c>0
. Furthermore if
c=\infty
then
K(y)=1
for
y\in B(z_{0},r_{0})\cap\operatorname{supp}\mu
and therefore (
2.1
) would give
{\mathcal{H}}^{k}|_{\operatorname{supp}\mu}(E)=0
for any

, but this cannot happen since
B(z_{0},r_{0})\cap\operatorname{supp}\mu=B(z_{0},r_{0})\cap P
.

Since

, we can write
K(y)=\frac{c}{c+1}\in(0,1)
for all
y\in B(z_{0},r_{0})\cap\operatorname{supp}\mu
, and therefore by (
2.1
) we conclude that

(2.2)

\mu(E)=c{\mathcal{H}}^{k}|_{\operatorname{supp}\mu}(E)\text{ for any }E\subset B(z_{0},r_{0}).

The equality (

) in particular implies

for all

Thus, the measure

is a dilation of

Theorem

1.1
applied to the locally
k
-uniform measure
\widetilde{\mu}
.

3.
Proof of
Theorem

1.1
: Strong unique continuation property

In this section we prove the strong continuation-type property in
Theorem

1.1
for locally
k
-uniform measures in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
that is, if the mean curvature vector of the support vanishes at a point, then the whole connected component containing the point must be a
k
-plane.

Let us recall that the mean curvature vector of a
k
-dimensional
C^{2}
-manifold in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
is the trace of the (vector-valued) second fundamental form, divided by
k
. Let
{\mathcal{M}}\cap B(z_{0},r_{0})
, with
z_{0}\in{\mathcal{M}}
and
r_{0}>0
, be a
C^{2}
-manifold of dimension
k
. If
\{\hat{n}_{j}(z)\}_{j=k+1}^{n+1}
is an orthonormal basis of the normal space
N_{\mathcal{M}}(z)
at
z\in{\mathcal{M}}\cap B(z_{0},r_{0})
then, intuitively, the
j
-component of the mean curvature vector is a measure of “flatness” in that it infinitesimally captures how much the measure of
{\mathcal{M}}
changes under perturbations in the direction of
\hat{n}_{j}(z)
.

For our purposes it is enough to work in coordinates, where the definition becomes more concrete. Assume that
{\mathcal{M}}\cap B(z_{0},r_{0})
can be described by a
C^{2}
function
\varphi=(\varphi_{k+1},\ldots,\varphi_{n+1}):{\mathbb{R}}^{k}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1-k}
such that
z=(z^{\prime},\varphi(z^{\prime}))
for
z\in{\mathcal{M}}\cap B(z_{0},r_{0})
, with
z^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{k}
. Note that, in this case, the tangent space
T_{\mathcal{M}}(z)
is

T_{\mathcal{M}}(z)\coloneqq\Span\left(\left\{t_{j}\right\}_{j=1}^{k}\right),\text{ with }t_{j}(z)\coloneqq\frac{e_{j}^{k}+\partial_{x_{j}}\varphi(z^{\prime})}{\sqrt{1+|\partial_{x_{j}}\varphi(z^{\prime})|^{2}}},

and
\{e_{j}^{k}\}_{j=1}^{k}
is the standard orthonormal basis of
{\mathbb{R}}^{k}
. As above,
\{\hat{n}_{j}(z)\}_{j=k+1}^{n+1}
is an orthonormal basis of the normal space
N_{\mathcal{M}}(z)
. Then the vector-valued second fundamental form
\mathrm{I\!I}_{{\mathcal{M}}}(\cdot,\cdot)(z):T_{\mathcal{M}}(z)\times T_{\mathcal{M}}(z)\to N_{\mathcal{M}}(z)
is given by

\mathrm{I\!I}_{{\mathcal{M}}}(u,v)(z)\coloneqq\Pi_{N_{\mathcal{M}}(z)}(u\cdot\nabla v)=\sum_{j=k+1}^{n+1}\left\langle u\cdot\nabla v,\hat{n}_{j}(z)\right\rangle\hat{n}_{j}(z).

(Where the inner product is the Euclidean inner product).

The mean curvature vector is then the trace so we have that

H_{\mathcal{M}}(z)=\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i,j=1}^{k}g^{ij}(z)\sum_{\ell=k+1}^{n+1}\left\langle t_{j}(z)\cdot\nabla t_{i}(z),\hat{n}_{\ell}(z)\right\rangle\hat{n}_{\ell}(z),

where
g^{ij}(z)
is the inverse of the first fundamental form, given by
g_{ij}(z)=\left\langle t_{i}(z),t_{j}(z)\right\rangle
.

In generality, this expression is extremely complicated, but it simplifies greatly if we assume that
T_{{\mathcal{M}}}(0)={\mathbb{R}}^{k}\times\{0\}^{n+1-k}
(that is,
\partial_{x_{i}}\varphi(0)=0
, for every
i=1,\ldots,k
). In this setting we can take
t_{j}(0)=e_{j}
,
j=1,\ldots,k
, and
\hat{n}_{j}(0)=e_{j}
,
j=k+1,\ldots,n+1
(here
\{e_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n+1}
is the standard orthonormal basis of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
). It follows that
g^{ij}(0)=\delta_{ij}
(i.e., is 1 when
i=j
and
0
otherwise). Thus our formula simplifies to

H_{\mathcal{M}}(0)=\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{\ell=k+1}^{n+1}\left\langle e_{i}\cdot\nabla t_{i}(0),e_{\ell}\right\rangle e_{\ell}.

We recall that
\nabla t_{i}(0)
is a matrix and we need only compute its
(\ell,i)
-entry. This is a straightforward computation that gives

(\nabla t_{i}(0))_{\ell,i}=\partial_{x_{i}}\left(\frac{\partial_{x_{i}}\varphi_{\ell}}{\sqrt{1+|\partial_{x_{i}}\varphi|^{2}}}\right)(0)\\
=\partial^{2}_{x_{i}}\varphi_{\ell}(0),

where we have used in the second equality that
\partial_{x_{i}}\varphi(0)=0
for all
i=1\ldots,k
. Thus we have the final form

(3.1)

H_{\mathcal{M}}(0)=\frac{1}{k}\sum_{\ell=k+1}^{n+1}\Delta\varphi_{\ell}(0)e_{\ell}=\frac{1}{k}(0,\Delta\varphi(0)),\text{ if }T_{\mathcal{M}}(0)={\mathbb{R}}^{k}\times\{0\}^{n+1-k}.

b_{z,r}
and its nonnormalized version
\widetilde{b}_{z,r}
as

(3.2a)

\displaystyle b_{z,r}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{k+2}{2w_{k}r^{k+2}}\int_{B(z,r)}\left(r^{2}-|y-z|^{2}\right)(y-z)\,d\mu(y),

(3.2b)

\displaystyle\widetilde{b}_{z,r}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{2w_{k}r^{k+2}}{k+2}b_{z,r}=\int_{B\left(z,r\right)}\left(r^{2}-|y-z|^{2}\right)\left(y-z\right)\,d\mu(y),

and the quadratic form
Q_{z,r}
and its normalized version
\widetilde{Q}_{z,r}
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
as

(3.3a)

\displaystyle Q_{z,r}(x)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{k+2}{w_{k}r^{k+2}}\int_{B(z,r)}\langle x,y-z\rangle^{2}\,d\mu(y),

(3.3b)

\displaystyle\widetilde{Q}_{z,r}(x)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{w_{k}r^{k+2}}{k+2}Q_{z,r}=\int_{B(z,r)}\langle x,y-z\rangle^{2}\,d\mu(y),

all first introduced in
[
KP87

, see also
[
DKT01

. Throughout this section,
b_{z,r}^{j}
and

denote the

) of the vectors
b_{z,r}
and

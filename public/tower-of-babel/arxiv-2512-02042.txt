Abstract
. The purpose of this paper is to investigate the non-constant entire as well as meromorphic solutions of the Fermat-type partial differential-difference equation: ( ∑ j = 1 m ∂ f ⁡ ( z 1 , z 2 , … , z m ) ∂ z j ) m 1 + f m 2 ​ ( z 1 + c 1 , z 2 + c 2 , … , z m + c m ) = 1 , \left(\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{\partial f(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{m})}{\partial z_{j}}\right)^{m_{1}}+f^{m_{2}}(z_{1}+c_{1},z_{2}+c_{2},\ldots,z_{m}+c_{m})=1, where m 1 m_{1} and m 2 m_{2} are positive integers such that m 1 + m 2 > 2 m_{1}+m_{2}>2 and ( c 1 , c 2 , … , c m ) ∈ ℂ m (c_{1},c_{2},\ldots,c_{m})\in\mathbb{C}^{m} . The results of our paper generalize the result of Xu and Wang [ 34 ] from ℂ 2 \mathbb{C}^{2} to ℂ m \mathbb{C}^{m} . Also in the paper we give positive answer of the open problem addressed by Xu and Wang [ 34 ] . Moreover plenty of examples are provided to illustrate our findings.

We define
\mathbb{Z}_{+}=\mathbb{Z}[0,+\infty)=\{n\in\mathbb{Z}:0\leq n<+\infty\}

An exhaustion

We define

\displaystyle\partial_{z_{i}}(f(z))=\frac{\partial f(z)}{\partial z_{i}},\ldots,\partial^{l_{i}}_{z_{i}}(f(z))=\frac{\partial^{l_{i}}f(z)}{\partial z_{i}^{l_{i}}},

where

\partial^{I}(f(z))=\frac{\partial^{|I|}f(z)}{\partial z_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdots\partial z_{m}^{i_{m}}},

. Therefore the zero multiplicity

of

is defined

, where

In other words, we can write

Let

and

Let

\mu_{f_{1}}^{0}=\mu_{g_{1}}^{0}\;\;(a=\infty)
.
For

\displaystyle n_{\nu}(t)=t^{-2(m-1)}\int_{A[t]}\nu\upsilon_{m}^{m-1},

The valence function of

Also we write

An algebraic subset

We define the linear measure

1.1.
Fermat type functional equation in
\mathbb{C}

The following functional equation

\displaystyle f^{n}(z)+g^{n}(z)=1,

(1.1)

where
n
is a positive integer can be regarded as the Fermat diophantine equations
x^{n}+y^{n}=1
over function fields.
In 1927, Montel
[
27
]
proved that the equation (
1.1
) has no transcendental entire solutions for
n\geq 3
.
Also in Theorem 4
[
10
]
, we see that the equation (
1.1
) actually has no non-constant entire solutions when
n>2
.
Gross
[
9
, Theorem 3]
proved that equation (
1.1
) has no non-constant meromorphic solutions when
n>3
.
For
n=2
, Gross
[
10
, Theorem 4]
found that the equation (
1.1
) has entire solutions of the from
f(z)=\sin(h(z))
and
g(z)=\cos(h(z))
, where
h(z)
is an entire function.
For
n=2
and
n=3
, Gross
[
9
, Theorem 1]
and Baker
[
4
, Theorem 1]
respectively obtained the solutions of Eq. (
1.1
) and it specific forms.
In 1970, Yang
[
36
]
investigated the following Fermat-type equation

\displaystyle f^{m_{1}}(z)+g^{m_{2}}(z)=1

(1.2)

and obtained that equation (
1.2
) has no non-constant entire solutions when
\frac{1}{m_{1}}+\frac{1}{m_{2}}<1
(see the proof of Theorem 1). Therefore it is clear that the equation (
1.2
) has no non-constant entire solutions when
m_{1}>2
and
m_{2}>2
. However, for the case when
m_{1}=m_{2}=2
and
g(z)
has a specific relationship with
f(z)
, many authors investigated the existence of solutions of the equation (
1.2
). As a result, successively several research papers were published (see
[
20
]
-
[
24
]
,
[
33
]
,
[
37
]
) and the research in this area is still ongoing.

1.2.
Fermat type functional equation in
\mathbb{C}^{m}

In 2008, Li
[
19
, Theorem 1]
proved that meromorphic solutions of the Fermat-type functional equation
f^{2}+g^{2}=1
in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
must be constant if and only if
\frac{\partial f(z_{1},z_{2})}{\partial z_{2}}
and
\frac{\partial g(z_{1},z_{2})}{\partial z_{1}}
share
0
CM. Moreover if
f(z_{1},z_{2})=\frac{\partial u(z_{1},z_{2})}{\partial z_{1}}
and
g(z_{1},z_{2})=\frac{\partial u(z_{1},z_{2})}{\partial z_{2}}
, then any entire solutions of the partial differential equations:

\left(\frac{\partial u(z_{1},z_{2})}{\partial z_{1}}\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{\partial u(z_{1},z_{2})}{\partial z_{2}}\right)^{2}=1

in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
are necessarily linear (see
[
15
]
). From the fact that the surface
z_{1}^{n}+z_{2}^{n}=1
in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
is a Kobayashi hyperbolic manifold and thus there are no non-constant holomorphic curves
(f_{1},f_{2})
from the complex plane
\mathbb{C}
(and thus
\mathbb{C}^{m}
) to the surface (see
[
31
, page 360]
), one can easily prove that any
entire solutions of the partial differential equations

\left(\frac{\partial u(z_{1},z_{2})}{\partial z_{1}}\right)^{n}+\left(\frac{\partial u(z_{1},z_{2})}{\partial z_{2}}\right)^{n}=1,

where
n\geq 3
must also be linear. The solutions of Fermat-type partial differential equations
was originally investigated by Li
[
18
]
and Saleeby
[
29
]
.

Recently, Fermat-type difference and Fermat-type partial differential-difference equations are the current interests among researchers by utilizing Nevanlinna value distribution theory in several complex variables (see
[
1
]
-
[

]
,

]
,

]
,

]

In 2020, Xu and Wang
[

considered the following partial differential-difference equation:

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial f(z_{1},z_{2})}{\partial z_{1}}+\frac{\partial f(z_{1},z_{2})}{\partial z_{2}}\right)^{m_{1}}+f^{m_{2}}(z_{1}+c_{1},z_{2}+c_{2})=1,

(1.3)

to discuss the existence of finite order transcendental entire solutions, where
m_{1}
and
m_{2}
are positive integers such that
m_{1}+m_{2}>2
. Using the difference Nevanlinna theory for meromorphic functions (see
[

,
6
,
7
,
16

) in the case of higher dimension, Xu and Wang
[

obtained the following result.

Theorem 1.A
.

[
34
, Theorem 1.6]

Let
c=(c_{1},c_{2})\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
and
m_{1},m_{2}
be two distinct positive integers such that
m_{1}+m_{2}>2
. If the equation (
1.3
) satisfies one of the conditions:
(i)

m_{2}>m_{1}

(ii)

m_{1}>m_{2}\geq 2
,
then equation (
1.3
) does not have any finite order transcendental entire solutions.

When
m_{1}=2
and
m_{2}=1
, Xu and Wang
[
34
]
obtained the following result regarding the finite order transcendental entire solutions of the equation (
1.3
).

Theorem 1.B
.

[
34
, see pp.10]

Let
c=(c_{1},c_{2})\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
. If
f(z_{1},z_{2})
is a finite order transcendental entire solution of the following equation:

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial f(z_{1},z_{2})}{\partial z_{1}}+\frac{\partial f(z_{1},z_{2})}{\partial z_{2}}\right)^{2}+f(z_{1}+c_{1},z_{2}+c_{2})=1,

then
f(z_{1},z_{2})
takes the from

\displaystyle f(z_{1},z_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-\frac{1}{4}c_{1}^{2}-\frac{1}{4}z_{1}^{2}+z_{1}\left[G_{2}(z_{2}-z_{1})+a_{3}(z_{2}-z_{1})\right]-c_{1}G(z_{2}-z_{1})-a_{3}c_{1}[z_{2}-z_{1}-(c_{2}-c_{1})]-

\displaystyle\left(G_{2}(z_{2}-z_{1})+a_{3}\left[z_{2}-z_{1}-(c_{2}-c_{1})\right]\right)^{2},

where
G_{2}(s)
is a finite order transcendental entire period function with period
c_{2}-c_{1}
and
a_{3}=\frac{c_{1}}{2(c_{2}-c_{1})}
.

In the same paper, Xu and Wang
[
34
]
imposed the following open problem for further investigation:

Open Problem 1.A
.

Whether there exists the finite order transcendental entire solutions for the equation (
1.3
) in the case
m_{1}>2
and
m_{2}=1
or not?

To our knowledge, Open Problem 1.A remain unresolved up to the present. Consequently our first goal is to give affirmative answer of
the open problem 1.A. In the paper, we consider the following Fermat-type partial differential-difference equation

\displaystyle F^{m_{1}}(z)+f^{m_{2}}(z+c)=1,

(1.4)

where

\displaystyle F(z)=\sideset{}{{}_{j=1}^{m}}{\sum}\frac{\partial f(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{m})}{\partial z_{j}}.

(1.5)

Moreover, for
m_{1}=m_{2}=1
, it is easy to verify that
f(z)=e^{z_{1}+z_{2}+\cdots+z_{m}}+1
is a solution of

F(z)+f(z+c)=1
, where
e^{c_{1}+c_{2}+\cdots+c_{m}}=-m
. Therefore we consider the Eq. (
1.4
) for the existence of solutions for the case when
m_{1}+m_{2}>2
.

Our next goal is to investigate all possible finite-order transcendental entire as well as meromorphic solutions of the Eq. (
1.4
) for all different combinations of
m_{1}
and
m_{2}
. Specifically, we focus on understanding the conditions under which such solutions exist and determining their explicit forms. Furthermore, we extend these findings to higher-dimensional settings
\mathbb{C}^{m}
and explore how the solutions behave when multiple variables are involved.
Now we state our main results.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
c\in\mathbb{C}^{m}
and let
m_{1}
and
m_{2}
be two positive integers such that
m_{1}+m_{2}>2
. Then any finite order entire solutions of the equation (
1.4
) are characterized as follows:

(i)

if
m_{2}\geq m_{1}
, then
m_{1}=m_{2}=2
and
f(z)=\sin(d_{1}z_{1}+d_{2}z_{2}+\ldots+d_{m}z_{m}+P(z))
,
where
d_{1},d_{2},\ldots,d_{m}\in\mathbb{C}
and
P(z)
is a polynomial in
\mathbb{C}^{m}
such that
P(z+c)\equiv P(z)
and

\displaystyle e^{\iota(d_{1}c_{1}+d_{2}c_{2}+\ldots+d_{m}c_{m})}\sideset{}{{}_{j=1}^{m}}{\sum}\left(\frac{\partial P(z)}{\partial z_{j}}+d_{j}\right)=1,

(ii)

if
m_{1}>m_{2}
, then
m_{1}=2
,
m_{2}=1
and

f(z)=1-\left(-(z_{1}+z_{2}+\ldots+z_{m})/2m+g(z)\right)^{2},

where
g(z)=g(z_{2}-z_{1},z_{3}-z_{2},\ldots,z_{m}-z_{m-1})
is an arbitrary finite order transcendental entire function such that

g(z+c)-g(z)=(c_{1}+c_{2}+\ldots+c_{m})/2m
.

1.1
.

Corollary 1.1
.

For
m_{2}=1
and
m_{1}>2
, the Fermat-type partial differential-difference equation (
1.4
) does not have any finite order transcendental entire solutions.

If
c=(0,0,\ldots,0)
, then Theorem
1.1
implies the following corollary.

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
m_{1}
and
m_{2}
be two positive integers such that
m_{1}+m_{2}>2
. Then any non-constant entire solutions of the following equation

\displaystyle F^{m_{1}}(z)+f^{m_{2}}(z)=1

are characterized as follows:

(i)

if
m_{2}\geq m_{1}
, then
m_{1}=m_{2}=2
and
f(z)=\sin(d_{1}z_{1}+d_{2}z_{2}+\ldots+d_{m}z_{m}+P(z))
,

and

is a polynomial in

(ii)

if

m_{1}=2

m_{2}=1

Following examples are provided to illustrate our findings.

Example 1.1
.

Let
f(z_{1},z_{2},z_{3})=\sin(d_{1}z_{1}+d_{2}z_{2}+d_{3}z_{3}+P(z))
, where
d_{1}=d_{2}=d_{3}=\frac{1}{3}

and

Note that

is a solution of the following Fermat type differential-difference equation

\left(\sideset{}{{}_{j=1}^{3}}{\sum}\frac{\partial f\left(z_{1},z_{2},z_{3}\right)}{\partial z_{j}}\right)^{2}+f^{2}(z_{1}+\pi,z_{2},z_{3}-\pi)=1.

Example 1.2
.

Let
f(z_{1},z_{2},z_{3})=1-\left(-\frac{1}{6}(z_{1}+z_{2}+z_{3})+g(z)\right)^{2},

where

and

. Note that

is a transcendental entire function of finite order such that
g(z+c)-g(z)=0=\frac{1}{6}(c_{1}+c_{2}+c_{3})

is a finite order transcendental entire solution of the following Fermat type differential-difference equation

\left(\sideset{}{{}_{j=1}^{3}}{\sum}\frac{\partial f\left(z_{1},z_{2},z_{3}\right)}{\partial z_{j}}\right)^{2}+f\left(z_{1}+1,z_{2},z_{3}-1\right)=1.

.

Auxiliary lemmas

The following lemmas are relevant to this paper and will be used in the subsequent analysis.
First we recall the lemma of logarithmic derivative:

Lemma 2.1
.

[
13
, Lemma 1.37]
Let

be a non-constant meromorphic function in
\mathbb{C}^{m}
and

for all

, where
l(E)<+\infty
.

The following result is known as second main theorem:

Lemma 2.2
.

[
12
, Lemma 1.2]
Let
f
be a non-constant meromorphic function in
\mathbb{C}^{m}
and let
a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{q}
be different points in
\mathbb{C}\cup\{\infty\}
. Then

\displaystyle\parallel(q-2)T(r,f)\leq\sideset{}{{}_{j=1}^{q}}{\sum}\overline{N}(r,a_{j};f)+O(\log(rT(r,f))).

Lemma 2.3
.

[
13
, Theorem 1.26]
Let
f
be non-constant meromorphic function in
\mathbb{C}^{m}
. Assume that

R(z,w)=\frac{A(z,w)}{B(z,w)}
. Then

\displaystyle T\left(r,R_{f}\right)=\max\{p,q\}T(r,f)+O\Big(\sideset{}{{}_{j=0}^{p}}{\sum}T(r,a_{j})+\sideset{}{{}_{j=0}^{q}}{\sum}T(r,b_{j})\Big),

where
R_{f}(z)=R(z,f(z))
and two coprime polynomials
A(z,w)
and
B(z,w)
are given
respectively as follows:
A(z,w)=\sum_{j=0}^{p}a_{j}(z)w^{j}
and
B(z,w)=\sum_{j=0}^{q}b_{j}(z)w^{j}
.

Lemma 2.4
.

[
13
, Lemma 3.2]
Let
f_{j}\not\equiv 0\;(j=1,2,\ldots,n;n\geq 3)
be meromorphic functions in
\mathbb{C}^{m}
such that
f_{1},\ldots,f_{n-1}
are non-constants and
f_{1}+\cdots+f_{n}=1

\displaystyle\parallel\;\;\sideset{}{{}_{j=1}^{n}}{\sum}\Big\{N_{n-1}(r,0;f_{j})+(n-1)\overline{N}(r,f_{j})\Big\}<\lambda T(r,f_{j})+O(\log^{+}(T(r,f_{j}))

.

.

[
7
, Theorem 2.1]
Let
f
be a non-constant meromorphic function in

and let

[

, Theorem 2.2]
Let

with

.

Let

\mathbb{C}^{m}
. Define complex differential-difference polynomials as follows:

\displaystyle P(f(z))=\sum\limits_{\mathbf{p}\in I}a_{\mathbf{p}}(z)f^{p_{0}}(z)\big(\partial^{\mathbf{i}_{1}}(f(z))\big)^{p_{1}}\cdots\big(\partial^{\mathbf{i}_{l}}(f(z))\big)^{p_{l}}f^{p_{l+1}}(z+\hat{q}_{l+1})\cdots f^{p_{l+s}}(z+\hat{q}_{l+s}),

(2.3)

(3.2)

\displaystyle\parallel\;m_{1}T(r,F)

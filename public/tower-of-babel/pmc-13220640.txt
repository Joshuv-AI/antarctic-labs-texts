Development and validation of a metabolic-inflammatory-nutritional prognostic model for esophageal squamous carcinoma
Authors: Ruiqi Chen, Yun Ding, Lilan Zhao, Zhaojun Yu, Juan Zhang, Zijie He, Songyu Cai, Genglin Li, Tianxing Guo, Wenshu Chen, Xiaojie Pan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe role of triglyceride-glucose (TyG) as a metabolic index in the prognosis of radical esophageal cancer remains underexplored. This study evaluated the association between TyG and postoperative prognosis in esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC) and developed a metabolic-inflammatory-nutritional (MIN) scoring model to improve prognostic assessment.Materials and methodsThis study retrospectively analyzed 434 ESCC patients who underwent radical esophagectomy, divided into development and internal validation cohorts. A MIN score was constructed using LASSO-Cox regression, and a nomogram was developed. Performance was evaluated using C-index, ROC curves, calibration, and decision curve analysis.ResultsThe preoperative TyG index was an independent protective factor for overall survival (HR = 0.44, 95% CI: 0.30–0.64, P < 0.001). The MIN score showed good discriminative ability in the derivation cohort, with 1-, 3-, and 5-year AUC values of 0.749, 0.752, and 0.760, respectively, and maintained robust performance in the validation cohort (AUC: 0.747, 0.724, and 0.728). The nomogram incorporating the MIN score, age, sex, and TNM stage achieved excellent discrimination, with C-indices of 0.832 in the derivation cohort and 0.799 in the validation cohort.ConclusionThe preoperative TyG index is a protective prognostic factor in ESCC. The MIN score and nomogram provide a robust tool for individualized prognosis prediction, complementing TNM staging.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12885-026-16007-0.

Introduction

Data from global cancer statistics indicate that esophageal cancer ranks 11th in incidence and 7th in mortality among malignant tumors in the world, with about 50% of cases occurring in China [1–3]. ESCC represents the predominant histological subtype of esophageal cancer in China, accounting for approximately 85% of all cases [4]. Despite significant advancements in surgical interventions and perioperative adjuvant therapies for esophageal cancer in recent years, esophageal cancer remains the fifth leading cause of cancer-related mortality in China [5–9]. The main reasons for the high mortality rate include late diagnosis, early recurrence, and local and distant metastasis [10]. Consequently, there is an urgent clinical need to establish a practical and reliable preoperative predictive index that can both prognosticate outcomes and inform perioperative therapeutic strategies for esophageal cancer patients.

Extensive research has recently focused on the clinical value of preoperative inflammatory and nutritional status in predicting surgical outcomes and survival rates, with particular emphasis on esophageal cancer patients [11, 12]. In 2018, Geng et al. pioneered the development of the Systemic Inflammatory Response Index (SIRI), establishing its clinical application for prognostic prediction in esophageal cancer patients based on the correlation between peripheral inflammatory markers and clinical outcomes [13]. Subsequent studies have consistently demonstrated that various inflammation- and nutrition-based biomarkers, including the systemic immune-inflammation index (SII), prognostic nutritional index (PNI), albumin-to-alkaline phosphatase ratio (AAPR), and integrated inflammation-nutrition score (IINS), serve as independent prognostic factors in esophageal cancer [14–18]. Nevertheless, the prognostic utility of these laboratory-derived indices may be compromised by various clinical and biological confounders, and their clinical significance in cancer prognosis continues to be debated.

Given these considerations, this study hypothesizes that integrating multiple biomarker indices could potentially overcome the limitations associated with individual parameters, thereby enhancing the predictive accuracy of laboratory-based prognostic assessment in cancer patients. Emerging evidence has recently demonstrated the clinical potential of the TyG index, a novel metabolic biomarker, as a robust predictor of recurrence and overall survival in gastric cancer patients [19]. This observation underscores the need for further investigation into the potential prognostic utility of the TyG index in esophageal cancer management. Consequently, this study aimed to examine the association between the TyG index and clinical outcomes in esophageal cancer while developing a novel MIN scoring system incorporating the TyG index with established inflammation and nutrition biomarkers. Furthermore, a comprehensive nomogram was developed by integrating the MIN scoring system with relevant clinicopathological parameters, with the objective of establishing a novel tool for personalized survival prediction in esophageal cancer patients.

Materials and methods

Participant enrollment

This study retrospectively enrolled patients with ESCC who underwent radical McKeown esophagectomy (R0 resection) at the Department of Thoracic Surgery, Provincial Hospital of Fuzhou University between January 2017 and September 2021. Predetermined inclusion and exclusion criteria were applied to ensure cohort homogeneity and data reliability.

Inclusion criteria. (1) pathologically confirmed ESCC; (2) radical McKeown esophagectomy with R0 resection; and (3) completion of preoperative laboratory tests, including complete blood count, fasting glucose, lipid profile, and liver and kidney function, within one week prior to surgery.

Exclusion criteria. (1) incomplete clinical or follow-up data; (2) history of other malignancies, excluding non-melanoma skin cancer or early-stage cervical cancer curatively treated for more than five years; (3) presence of severe hematological disorders, active autoimmune diseases, chronic inflammatory diseases, or acute infections; and (4) long-term use of medications affecting inflammatory, metabolic, or nutritional status, such as glucocorticoids or immunosuppressants.

Data collection and variable definition

This study systematically collected preoperative hematological parameters and relevant clinicopathological data. The hematological profile comprised fasting blood glucose (FBG), absolute lymphocyte count (ALC), absolute neutrophil count (ANC), platelet count, serum albumin concentration and other indicators. Utilizing these fundamental parameters, this study calculated composite indices reflecting metabolic, inflammatory, and nutritional status to enable comprehensive preoperative physiological assessment. The derived indices comprised the following validated biomarkers: albumin-to-alkaline phosphatase ratio (AAPR), albumin-globulin ratio (AGR), albumin-bilirubin (ALBI) grade, neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR), platelet-to-lymphocyte ratio (PLR), lymphocyte-to-monocyte ratio (LMR), platelet-to-hemoglobin ratio (PHR), neutrophil-to-high-density lipoprotein ratio (NHR), systemic inflammatory response index (SIRI), prognostic nutritional index (PNI), systemic immune-inflammation index (SII), controlling nutritional status (CONUT) score, and triglyceride-glucose (TyG) index. Follow-up

Postoperative follow-up for patients who underwent esophagectomy was conducted via outpatient medical records and telephone interviews. Overall survival (OS) was defined as the duration from the date of esophagectomy to the date of death from any cause or the end of the follow-up period, whichever occurred first. The final follow-up was completed by January 2026.

Statistical analysis

To ensure methodological rigor in model development, the overall cohort was randomly divided into a development cohort and an internal validation cohort at a 7:3 ratio. All statistical analyses were performed using R software (version 4.4.2). Continuous variables were presented as mean ± standard deviation or median (interquartile range), and categorical variables as frequency (percentage). Between-group comparisons were performed using the Student’s t-test, Mann–Whitney U test, or chi-squared test, as appropriate.

Prognostic value of the TyG index

The prognostic value of the TyG index was evaluated using time-dependent ROC analysis, with the optimal cutoff determined by the maximum Youden index. Patients were stratified into high- and low-TyG groups, and survival differences were compared using the log-rank test. Univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses were performed to identify independent prognostic factors.

Model development in the development cohort

The TyG index and the remaining 12 candidate inflammatory and nutritional indices were directly entered into the Least Absolute Shrinkage and Selection Operator (LASSO) Cox regression model with 10-fold cross-validation to identify the most robust predictors and construct the Metabolic-Inflammatory-Nutritional (MIN) score. Variables with non-zero coefficients at the optimal λ determined by the one-standard-error criterion (λ.1se) were retained for inclusion in the MIN score. A clinical nomogram was then developed by incorporating the MIN score and significant clinicopathological covariates identified through multivariate Cox regression.

Model validation

The model’s discrimination was evaluated using the concordance index (C-index) and time-dependent receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves. Calibration was assessed by plotting calibration curves. The clinical utility of the nomogram was determined by decision curve analysis (DCA).

For internal validation, the MIN score formula and the nomogram derived from the development cohort were directly applied to the independent validation cohort. The same set of metrics (C-index, time-dependent ROC curves, calibration curves, and DCA) was used to assess the model’s performance in the validation cohort.

Survival outcomes between risk groups were compared using Kaplan-Meier curves with the log-rank test. All statistical tests were two-sided, and a P-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Population characteristics

This study included 434 patients with ESCC, who were randomly divided into a development cohort (n = 304) and a validation cohort (n = 130) in a 7:3 ratio. Baseline characteristics were comparable between the two cohorts (Table 1). No significant differences were observed between the two cohorts in demographic, clinicopathological, or treatment-related variables (all P > 0.1). The final follow-up was completed on January 13, 2026. Median follow-up was 63 months in both cohorts (development: range 0-106; validation: range 0–92). During follow-up, endpoint events (death) occurred in 152 patients (50.0%) in the development cohort and 55 (42.3%) in the validation cohort.

TyG index as an independent prognostic factor in ESCC

Multivariate Cox regression analysis, adjusted for age, sex, hypertension and TNM stage, identified the TyG index as an independent prognostic factor for survival in ESCC (HR = 0.44, 95% CI: 0.30–0.64, P < 0.001; Table 2). ROC curve analysis identified an optimal cutoff value of 8.721 for the TyG index (Youden index = 0.269, AUC = 0.631). Based on this threshold, patients were stratified into high-TyG (≥ 8.721) and low-TyG (< 8.721) groups. Kaplan-Meier survival analysis demonstrated significantly superior overall survival in the high-TyG group compared with the low-TyG group (log-rank P < 0.001; Fig. 1).

Association of inflammatory and nutritional indices with OS

This study comprehensively evaluated the association between 12 inflammatory and nutritional indices with OS (Fig. 2). The analysis revealed that higher values of AGR, LMR, AAPR, and PNI, along with lower ALBI grades, PHR, SII, NHR, NLR, SIRI, PLR, and CONUT scores were significantly associated with longer OS (P < 0.01).

Construction of the MIN score

Correlation analysis (Fig. 3A) revealed strong correlations (|r| > 0.75) among several inflammatory indicators (e.g., NHR, SIRI, NLR, SII) as well as between nutritional indicators PNI and ALBI, suggesting the presence of multicollinearity. To address this, LASSO Cox regression with 10-fold cross-validation was performed (Fig. 3B). Using the one-standard-error criterion (λ.1se), six predictors with non-zero coefficients were selected: AGR, PLR, PHR, PNI, SII, and TyG index. In the development cohort, the MIN score demonstrated significant discriminative ability for prognosis, with AUC for 1-, 3-, and 5-year OS of 0.749, 0.752, and 0.760, respectively (Fig. 3C), and a C-index of 0.704 (95% CI: 0.666–0.743). Based on the optimal cutoff value of -5.802 determined by the maximum Youden index (0.428) at 1 year, patients were stratified into low-MIN (<-5.802) and high-MIN (≥-5.802) groups. Patients who died had significantly higher MIN scores than survivors (Fig. 3D, P < 0.001), and a higher density of death events was observed in the high-MIN group (Fig. 3E). Baseline characteristics (Table 3) showed that patients in the high-MIN group had more advanced TNM stage and significantly worse inflammatory (PLR, PHR, SII), nutritional (AGR, PNI), and metabolic (TyG) profiles (all P < 0.001), along with a higher mortality rate (P < 0.001).

The Schoenfeld residual test confirmed that the MIN score satisfied the proportional hazards assumption of the Cox model (P = 0.318; mean(β) = 1.021, HR = 2.776, Fig. 3F). Internal validation of the MIN score

In the independent validation cohort, the MIN score maintained robust predictive performance, with AUCs for 1-, 3-, and 5-year OS of 0.747, 0.724, and 0.728, respectively (Fig. 4A), and a C-index of 0.691 (95% CI: 0.621–0.761). Applying the same cutoff of -5.802 effectively discriminated between deceased and surviving patients (Fig. 4B), and the high-MIN group had significantly poorer survival than the low-MIN group (P < 0.001, Fig. 4C).

Predictive performance of the MIN score and comparison with TNM staging

The prognostic performance of the MIN score was systematically evaluated and compared with that of the conventional TNM staging system in both the development and internal validation cohorts.

Comparison between the MIN score and TNM stage demonstrated its independent prognostic value. In the development cohort (Fig. 4D and F), the MIN score achieved AUCs of 0.749, 0.752, and 0.760 for 1-, 3-, and 5-year survival, respectively, which were comparable to those of the TNM stage (0.740, 0.739, 0.755). In comparison, the combined model (TNM + MIN) yielded the best predictive performance, with AUCs of 0.818, 0.825, and 0.837. Similar results were observed in the validation cohort (Fig. 4G and I), where the MIN score (AUCs: 0.747, 0.724, 0.728) showed comparable performance to TNM stage (0.732, 0.726, 0.758), and the combined model again achieved the highest AUCs (AUCs: 0.781, 0.775, 0.802).

The additive value of the MIN score was further quantified by reclassification analyses. In the development cohort, the addition of MIN score to TNM stage yielded an NRI of 0.465 (95% CI: 0.254–0.660, P < 0.001) and IDI of 0.116 (95% CI: 0.061–0.177, P < 0.001). These findings were validated in the independent validation cohort (NRI: 0.422, 95% CI: 0.181–0.665, P < 0.001; IDI: 0.092, 95% CI: 0.036–0.145, P = 0.001), confirming the robust additive prognostic value of the MIN score beyond TNM stage alone.

Development and validation of MIN nomogram

Multivariate Cox regression confirmed the MIN score as an independent prognostic factor in ESCC (HR = 3.08, 95% CI: 2.21–4.30, P < 0.001; Table 4). Sensitivity analysis using E-value estimation further supported the robustness of this finding (E-value = 5.16). A nomogram integrating the MIN score with gender, age, and TNM stage was subsequently developed for individualized prognosis (Fig. 5A).

In the independent validation cohort, the nomogram maintained robust predictive performance. The time-dependent C-index for 1- to 5-year OS ranged from 0.759 to 0.821 (mean: 0.799), higher than that of TNM stage (0.708–0.758; mean: 0.734) and the MIN score (0.712–0.769; mean: 0.734) at all time points (Fig. 5C). Discussion

Despite advancements in surgical techniques, perioperative adjuvant therapies, and treatment optimization, significant challenges remain in preoperative prognostic assessment for ESCC patients globally. Recent years have witnessed growing recognition of inflammatory, nutritional, and metabolic biomarkers in cancer prognosis [20–24]. Accumulating evidence has established the prognostic value of SIRI, PNI, and SII in ESCC preoperative assessment [25]. Notably, Zhang et al.’s study of 655 surgically treated ESCC patients demonstrated that elevated SII and reduced PNI were independently associated with poorer postoperative survival, while their combined evaluation significantly enhanced prognostic prediction accuracy [14]. Originally developed for hepatocellular carcinoma prognosis, the ALBI score has demonstrated extended utility in esophageal cancer following its 2022 modification by Japanese researchers. The refined ALBI scoring system effectively predicts esophageal cancer outcomes, with higher ALBI grades showing significant correlations with malnutrition status [26].

The escalating global prevalence of diabetes and obesity has strengthened the established link between metabolic dysregulation and cancer development [27, 28]. Dyslipidemia, in particular, has been strongly associated with elevated cancer risk, reflecting lipids’ crucial roles in cellular homeostasis maintenance, energy provision, membrane phospholipid bilayer stabilization, and intracellular signaling pathways [29, 30]. Notably, similar findings have been reported in gastric cancer [31]. The prognostic significance of TyG in upper gastrointestinal cancers may reflect its role as a surrogate for metabolic reserve, where higher TyG indices correlate with better nutritional status and treatment tolerance [32–36]. Recent studies have highlighted the role of pyroptosis in tumor biology, linking metabolic stress to inflammation and antitumor immunity [37]. Whether the TyG index is associated with this pathway warrants further investigation. Collectively, these findings align with the critical role of metabolic indices in predicting postoperative outcomes, as exemplified by a comprehensive metabolic scoring system incorporating 14 blood-based parameters [38].

Recognizing the multifactorial nature of cancer prognosis, this study developed an integrated MIN scoring system incorporating six validated preoperative hematological indicators: AGR, PNI, PLR, PHR, SII, and TyG index. Indicator selection was guided by LASSO regression with comparative assessment of λ min and λ 1-SE criteria; five of the six indicators were retained under both, demonstrating robustness against penalty intensity. While individual prognostic value of these parameters is established, their synergistic integration represents a novel approach for comprehensive prognostic assessment [15, 23–25, 39, 40]. Notably, certain established single prognostic indicators, such as CONUT, were not selected by LASSO—likely because their prognostic information was captured by other retained nutritional indicators (e.g., PNI, AGR), consistent with studies showing that weighted integration of multiple parameters outperforms single indicators [41, 42]. Additionally, the association between metabolic indicators and cancer prognosis has been highlighted in studies linking peripheral blood parameters to key oncogenic pathways such as PI3K/Akt/mTOR signaling [43, 44], supporting the biological plausibility of our MIN score.

This study demonstrated that the MIN score is an independent prognostic predictor for ESCC, demonstrating predictive accuracy comparable to TNM staging through similar AUC values [45–47]. Importantly, the MIN score shows superior clinical utility compared to the existing IINS score, achieving comparable prognostic accuracy with fewer biomarkers (6 vs. 10) while showing improved predictive performance across 1-, 3-, and 5-year survival intervals [48]. The developed nomogram, as a visual multivariate predictive tool, effectively integrates and quantifies multiple prognostic factors, graphically illustrating their individual impact on prognostic outcomes [49]. This innovative approach enables precise individualized survival probability prediction, offering substantial support for clinical decision-making in ESCC management [50]. Nomograms have become an established tool in prognostic analysis for radically resected esophageal cancer patients [51, 52]. In this study, a novel predictive nomogram was constructed through comprehensive univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses of the MIN score and relevant clinical parameters. The final model, integrating four key prognostic factors (MIN score, gender, age, and TNM stage), exhibited robust performance in risk stratification and prognostic accuracy, providing clinicians with a reliable tool for precise outcome prediction in ESCC management and guiding preoperative nutritional optimization for patients.

The nomogram demonstrated robust predictive performance, with time-dependent C-indices consistently exceeding those of TNM stage and the MIN score alone. However, calibration curves showed modest deviations at 1 and 5 years (MAE: 0.162–0.193 in development, 0.143–0.162 in validation), while optimal calibration was observed at 3 years (MAE ≤ 0.071). This pattern may reflect the inherent complexity of long-term survival prediction and the influence of competing risks [53, 54]. Additionally, DCA for 1-year survival in the validation cohort showed slightly lower net benefit compared with TNM stage within the 0.25–0.45 threshold range, likely due to the limited number of 1-year events (n = 18) and the potential influence of unmeasured perioperative factors on short-term outcomes. Nonetheless, clinical impact curves consistently confirmed favorable risk stratification across all time points, supporting the overall clinical utility of the nomogram.

When interpreting the findings of this study, several limitations warrant careful consideration. First, as a single-center retrospective analysis, potential selection bias cannot be entirely ruled out despite the use of rigorous data collection criteria, which may limit the generalizability of the conclusions to some extent. Second, this study relied on preoperative laboratory measurements obtained at a single time point. Such a “static snapshot” fails to capture the dynamic changes in the host status during the perioperative period, which may themselves carry important prognostic information. This may partly account for the calibration deviations observed in the nomogram when predicting 1-year (short-term) and 5-year (long-term) survival, as reflected by the MAE values. Third, due to the constraints of retrospective data collection, we were unable to systematically obtain accurate data on preoperative percentage weight loss or body mass index (BMI) for all patients. Consequently, these two well-established nutritional assessment parameters were not included in the analysis, potentially introducing unmeasured confounding. Fourth, the limited sample size-particularly the relatively small number of endpoint events occurring early (e.g., within one year) in the validation cohort-may have affected the extreme stability of the model in short-term prediction. This is reflected in the finding that, although the nomogram consistently demonstrated superior discriminative ability (time-dependent C-index), its net benefit for predicting 1-year survival was not consistently superior to TNM stage across certain threshold probability ranges. This suggests that caution should be exercised when applying the nomogram to early clinical decision-making. Fifth, the sample size remained insufficient for certain patient subgroups (e.g., those with very early-stage disease or distant metastasis), limiting our ability to perform in-depth subgroup analyses. Finally, this study focused primarily on the development and validation of a clinical prediction model, while the underlying biological mechanisms linking metabolic dysregulation, systemic inflammation, and nutritional imbalance to poor prognosis in ESCC remain largely unknown.

Based on the findings of this study, several directions warrant further investigation. First, to address the limitations of static measurements, prospective studies incorporating dynamic perioperative monitoring are essential. Exploring trajectories of changes in biomarkers before and after surgery may yield stronger predictive value than single baseline measurements and enable real-time assessment of intervention effects. Second, large-scale, multicenter internal validation is a core priority to address the issues of sample size and early prediction stability. Additionally, the expanded application of the MIN score, such as in predicting response to neoadjuvant therapy, should be explored to broaden its clinical utility. Finally, the biological basis of the MIN score warrants in-depth investigation. Using archived biospecimens to analyze its associations with specific inflammatory cytokines, metabolic products, and tumor microenvironment characteristics would provide a mechanistic foundation for this clinical tool and may identify novel therapeutic targets.

Conclusion

In conclusion, this study suggests that the TyG index may serve as an independent prognostic factor in ESCC. A novel MIN scoring system integrating metabolic, inflammatory, and nutritional parameters was developed and demonstrated prognostic potential. The MIN score may offer complementary information to TNM staging, and the accompanying nomogram may assist in individualized survival prediction for ESCC patients undergoing surgical resection. Prospective studies with larger, multicenter cohorts are needed to validate these findings.

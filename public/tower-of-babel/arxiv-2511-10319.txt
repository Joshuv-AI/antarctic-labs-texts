Combinatorial degree version of a generalized ℤ p \mathbb{Z}_{p} -Tucker’s lemma with a combinatorial proof
Sajal Mukherjee, Pritam Chandra Pramanik

Abstract
Combinatorial analogues of classical Borsuk–Ulam type theorems (e.g., Tucker’s lemma, ℤ p \mathbb{Z}_{p} -Tucker’s lemma etc.) have numerous important applications in combinatorics. In this paper, we formulate a combinatorial degree version of a generalized ℤ p \mathbb{Z}_{p} -Tucker’s lemma. Our proof is purely combinatorial in the sense that it does not involve homology, cohomology or any notions from continuous topology. As the main technical tool to prove the aforementioned degree theorem, we prove a Hopf trace type formula, which is also purely combinatorial and involves no homology. This combinatorial Hopf trace formula is of independent interest. MSC 2020: 57Q70 (primary), 05E45, 55U15.

9
]
for combinatorial proofs of several Borsuk–Ulam type theorems and their applications in combinatorics.

In this paper, we formulate a combinatorial degree version of Ziegler’s

-Tucker’s lemma
[

in a more generalized form and provide a purely combinatorial proof. Here we use the term ‘combinatorial’ in the sense of Ziegler

Let
\mathcal{K}
be a
d
-dimensional simplicial complex. A

is a collection of ordered pairs of simplices of the form
(\alpha,\beta)

Definition 1.1

(
\mathcal{V}
-trajectory)
.

Let
\mathcal{K}
be a
d
-dimensional simplicial complex and
\mathcal{V}
be a discrete vector field on it. Let

\dim(\beta_{0})=\dim(\beta_{r})=q

-trajectory
(or simply

\beta_{0}
to
\beta_{r}

q

(q-1)
dimensional simplices,

P:\beta_{0}^{(q)},\alpha_{1}^{(q-1)},\beta_{1}^{(q)},\ldots,\alpha_{r}^{(q-1)},\beta_{r}^{(q)},

such that, for each
i\in\{1,\ldots,r\}
,
(\alpha_{i},\beta_{i})\in\mathcal{V}
, and
\beta_{i-1}(\neq\beta_{i})\supsetneq\alpha_{i}
.

The faces
\beta_{0}
and
\beta_{r}
are called the

(denoted by
\mathbf{i}(P)
) and

(denoted by
\mathbf{t}(P)
) of the trajectory

The
weight

is defined by,

w(P):=\prod_{i=1}^{r}-[\beta_{i-1},\alpha_{i}][\beta_{i},\alpha_{i}],

where, for any

Trajectory

The faces

(denoted by

) and

The co-trajectory

A

A

Definition 1.6

A

Now we are in a position to state the main results of this paper. Let
p
be a prime, and
\mathcal{S}
be a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-combinatorial sphere, i.e.,
\mathcal{S}
is a combinatorial sphere and the group
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
acts on
\mathcal{S}
freely and simplicially. Let
k
be a non negative integer and
f:\operatorname{Bd}^{k}(\mathcal{S})\rightarrow\mathcal{S}
be a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-equivariant simplicial map, where
\operatorname{Bd}^{k}(\mathcal{S})
is the
k
-th barycentric subdivision of
\mathcal{S}
. Then we have the following.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-combinatorial sphere and
f:\operatorname{Bd}^{k}(\mathcal{S})\rightarrow\mathcal{S}
be a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-equivariant simplicial map, then,

\deg(f)\equiv 1\pmod{p}.

In the above theorem,
\deg(f)
is a direct combinatorial analogue (see Definition
4.6
) of mapping degree in topology.

Now let us state Ziegler’s
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-Tucker’s lemma(see
[
12
]
) in a slightly generalized form. Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
, and
\mathcal{S}_{1},\mathcal{S}_{2},\ldots,\mathcal{S}_{m}
be
m

\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-combinatorial spheres (not necessarily of same dimension). Let
x*\operatorname{Bd}^{k}(\mathcal{S}_{1}*\mathcal{S}_{2}*\ldots\mathcal{S}_{m})
be the cone over
\operatorname{Bd}^{k}(\mathcal{S}_{1}*\mathcal{S}_{2}*\ldots\mathcal{S}_{m})
with the apex

. Then the
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-Tucker’s lemma essentially goes as follows.

Theorem 1.8

(Generalized
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-Tucker’s lemma)
.

For
m\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\mathcal{S}_{1},\mathcal{S}_{2},\ldots,\mathcal{S}_{m}
be
m

\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-combinatorial spheres. Then, there does not exists any simplicial map
f:x*\operatorname{Bd}^{k}(S_{1}*\cdots*S_{m})\rightarrow S_{1}*\cdots*S_{m}
, such that,

f\big|_{\operatorname{Bd}^{k}(S_{1}*\cdots*S_{m})}:\operatorname{Bd}^{k}(S_{1}*\cdots S_{m})\rightarrow S_{1}*\cdots*S_{m}

is
\mathbb{Z}_{p}

In Section
4
(Proposition

, together with Theorem

, lead to the following combinatorial degree version of the generalized

-Tucker’s lemma.

Theorem 1.9

(Combinatorial degree version of generalized
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-Tucker’s lemma)
.

For
m\in\mathbb{N}
,
let
\mathcal{S}_{1},\mathcal{S}_{2},\ldots,\mathcal{S}_{m}
be m
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-combinatorial spheres. Then, for any
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-equivariant simplicial map

f:\operatorname{Bd}^{(k)}(\mathcal{S}_{1}*\mathcal{S}_{2}*\cdots*\mathcal{S}_{m})\rightarrow(\mathcal{S}_{1}*\mathcal{S}_{2}*\cdots*\mathcal{S}_{m}),

\deg(f)\equiv 1\pmod{p}.

In Appendix, we show that Theorem
1.9
follows form Theorem
1.10
.

As the main technical tool to prove our main result (Theorem
1.8
), we develop the following combinatorial version of Hopf trace formula, which is of independent interest.

Theorem 1.10

(Combinatorial Hopf trace formula)
.

Let
\mathcal{K}
be a
d
-dimensional simplicial complex with a gradient vector field
\mathcal{V}
on it. Let
\phi_{\#}:C_{\#}(\mathcal{K},\mathbb{Z})\rightarrow C_{\#}(\mathcal{K},\mathbb{Z})
be a chain map. Then

\sum_{q=0}^{d}(-1)^{q}\operatorname{tr}(\phi_{q})=\sum_{q=0}^{d}(-1)^{q}\sum_{\sigma\in\operatorname{Crit}_{q}^{(\mathcal{V})}(\mathcal{K})}\langle\overleftarrow{\sigma}^{(\mathcal{V})},\phi_{q}(\overrightarrow{\sigma}^{(\mathcal{V})})\rangle,

(1)

where, for any integer
q
, with
0\leq q\leq d
, and any two
q
-chains
c_{1},c_{2}\in C_{q}(\mathcal{K},\mathbb{Z})
, such that
c_{1}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}s_{i}\sigma_{i}
and
c_{1}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}s_{i}^{\prime}\sigma_{i}
, where
\{\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\ldots,\sigma_{n}\}
is the set of all
q
-dimensional simplices of
\mathcal{K}
, the inner product
\langle c_{1},c_{2}\rangle
is defined to be
\sum_{i=1}^{n}s_{i}s_{i}^{\prime}
.

It is worth mentioning that, this is a purely combinatorial result and unlike usual Hopf trace formula, this theorem (Theorem
1.10
) does not involve any homology and holds for any coefficient ring
R
and not just fields (although, throughout this paper we use
\mathbb{Z}
as the coefficient ring, our proof of Theorem
1.10
works for any ring as well).

Preliminaries

A
Directed graph
(or
Digraph
) is an orderd pair
G=(V,E)
, where
V
is a finite nonempty set, and
E\subseteq\{(a,b):a,b\in V\text{ and }a\neq b\}
. The set
V
is called the
vertex set
of the digraph
G
, and the elements of the set
E
are called
directed edges
(or simply
edges
). A
directed cycle
in
G
is a sequence of vertices,
a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{n}
, where all
a_{i}
-s are distinct for
i\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}
, and
a_{n}=a_{1}
, and for each
i\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}
,
(a_{i},a_{i+1})\in E
. A Digraph is said to be
acyclic
if it does not contain any directed cycle. A
topological ordering
of a digraph is a linear ordering of its vertices such that for each directed edge
(a,b)\in E
,
a
comes before
b
in the ordering. We have the following well-known theorem.

Theorem 2.1

(Topological sorting
[
2
]
)
.

Every acyclic digraph has a topological ordering.

An
abstract simplicial complex

\mathcal{K}
(or in short, a
simplicial complex
)is a finite non-empty collection of finite sets with the property that if
\sigma\in\mathcal{K}
and
\tau\subseteq\sigma
, then
\tau\in\mathcal{K}
. Note that, the empty set
\emptyset
always belongs to
\mathcal{K}
.

An element in
\mathcal{K}
is called a
simplex
. The
dimension
of a simplex
\sigma
is defines as
\dim(\sigma):=|\sigma|-1
. If
\dim(\sigma)=q
, we call
\sigma
a
q
-
simplex
. We often denote a
q
-simplex
\sigma
by
\sigma^{(q)}
. The dimension of a simplicial complex
\mathcal{K}
is defined as
\dim(\mathcal{K}):=\max\{\dim(\sigma):\sigma\in\mathcal{K}\}
. For
\tau,\sigma\in\mathcal{K}
, if
\tau\subseteq\sigma
, then
\tau
is called a
face
of
\sigma
. If
\tau
is a face of
\sigma
with
\dim({\tau})=\dim({\sigma})-1
, then
\tau
is said to be a
facet
of
\sigma
. The
vertex set
of
\mathcal{K}
, denoted by
V(\mathcal{K})
, is defined as,
V(\mathcal{K})=\cup_{\sigma\in\mathcal{K}}\sigma
. A simplex in a simplicial complex is said to be
maximal
, if it is not contained in any other simplex. Note that, for a maximal simplex
\sigma\in\mathcal{K}
,
\mathcal{K}\setminus\{\sigma\}
is also a simplicial complex. A simplicial complex is called
pure
, if all of its maximal simplices are of same dimension. A subset
\mathcal{K}^{\prime}
of
\mathcal{K}
is called a
subcomplex
of
\mathcal{K}
if
\mathcal{K}^{\prime}
is also a simplicial complex. For a simplex
\sigma\in\mathcal{K}
, the
closurer of
\sigma
, denoted by
\operatorname{cl}(\sigma)
, is defined as
\operatorname{cl}(\sigma):=\{\tau\in\mathcal{K}:\tau\subseteq\sigma\}
.

For,
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\Delta^{n}
is the simplicial complex which contains all the subsets of a set of cardinality
n
. For some non-negative integer
q
with
0\leq q\leq n
, the
q
-skeleton of
\Delta^{n}
is defined by
\Delta^{n}_{q}:=\{\sigma\in\Delta^{n}:\dim(\sigma)\leq q\}
.
Note that,
\Delta^{n}_{q}
is a subcomplex of
\Delta^{n}
.

Definition 2.2

(Join of two simplicial complexes)
.

Let
\mathcal{K}_{1}
and
\mathcal{K}_{2}
be two simplicial complexes, such that,
V(\mathcal{K}_{1})\cap V(\mathcal{K}_{2})=\emptyset
. Then, the
join
of
\mathcal{K}_{1}
and
\mathcal{K}_{2}
, denoted by

is the simplicial complex defined as,

\mathcal{K}_{1}*\mathcal{K}_{2}:=\{\sigma\sqcup\tau:\text{ where }\sigma\in\mathcal{K}_{1}\text{ and }\tau\in\mathcal{K}_{2}\}.

Note that, if
\dim(\mathcal{K}_{1})=m
and
\dim(\mathcal{K}_{2})=n
, then
\dim(\mathcal{K}_{1}*\mathcal{K}_{2})=m+n+1
.

Definition 2.3

(Cone over a simplicial complex)
.

Let
\mathcal{K}
be a simplicial complex and

. Then, the

over
\mathcal{K}
(with the apex

), is defined to be the simplicial complex

, and denoted by

.

Definition 2.4

(Barycentric subdivision of a simplicial complex)
.

The

, denoted by

, is the simplicial complex whose vertices correspond to the non-empty simplices of
\mathcal{K}
, and simplices of

The

(Simplicial map)

Let

An

Proposition A.1
.

Let
\mathcal{S}

Proof.

We have
\beta=\sum_{i=1}^{n}s_{i}\sigma_{i}

\sigma_{i}

(d-1)

\displaystyle s_{i}\cdot[\sigma_{i},\tau]+s_{j}\cdot[\sigma_{j},\tau]=0

\displaystyle|s_{i}|=|s_{j}|~~\text{(since, $[\sigma_{i},\tau],[\sigma_{i},\tau]\in\pm 1$)}

Now, when
\sigma_{i}
and
\sigma_{j}
are not adjacent, by definition of pseudomanifold, there exists a sequence of maximal simplices

(d-1)

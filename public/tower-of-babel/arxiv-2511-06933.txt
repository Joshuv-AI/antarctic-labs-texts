Transformed Fréchet Means for Robust Estimation in Hadamard Spaces
Christof Schötz

Abstract
We establish finite-sample error bounds in expectation for transformed Fréchet means in Hadamard spaces under minimal assumptions. Transformed Fréchet means provide a unifying framework encompassing classical and robust notions of central tendency in metric spaces. Instead of minimizing squared distances as for the classical 2-Fréchet mean, we consider transformations of the distance that are nondecreasing, convex, and have a concave derivative. This class spans a continuum between median and classical mean. It includes the Fréchet median, power Fréchet means, and the (pseudo-)Huber mean, among others. We obtain the parametric rate of convergence under fewer than two moments and a subclass of estimators exhibits a breakdown point of 1/2. Our results apply in general Hadamard spaces—including infinite-dimensional Hilbert spaces and nonpositively curved geometries—and yield new insights even in Euclidean settings.



1
Introduction

1.1
The Transformed Fréchet Mean

The
transformed Fréchet mean
(or
\tau
-Fréchet mean) provides a unifying framework for classical and robust notions of centrality. Given a metric space
(\mathcal{Q},d)
, a transformation
\tau\colon\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{R}
, and a
\mathcal{Q}
-valued random variable
Y
, it is defined as any element

m\in\argmin_{q\in\mathcal{Q}}\mathbf{E}[\tau(\overline{Yq})]\,,

(1)

where we write
\overline{yq}:=d(y,q)
. Choosing
\tau(x)=x^{2}
yields the classical
2
-Fréchet mean, while
\tau(x)=x
gives the Fréchet median; in Euclidean spaces these reduce to the expectation and the geometric (or spatial) median, respectively.

We consider a large class of transformed Fréchet means where
\tau
is a nondecreasing, convex function with concave derivative. Examples of such transformations include
\tau(x)=x^{\alpha}
with
\alpha\in[1,2]
, the Huber loss
\tau(x)=x^{2}\mathds{1}_{[0,1)}(x)+(2x-1)\mathds{1}_{[1,\infty)}(x)

[
Hub64
]
, the pseudo-Huber loss
\tau(x)=\sqrt{1+x^{2}}

[
Cha+94
]
, and
\tau(x)=\log(\cosh(x))

[
Gre90
]
. The resulting means are in some sense in-between median and expectation and accordingly exhibit robustness to heavy tails and, in some cases, to contamination, as we will show below.

The transformed Fréchet mean
m
is estimated by its empirical version
m_{n}
based on
n\in\mathbb{N}
independent and identically distributed (iid) copies
Y_{1},Y_{2},\dots,Y_{n}
of
Y
, which is

m_{n}\in\argmin_{q\in\mathcal{Q}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\tau(\overline{Y_{i}q})\,.

(2)

We assume
\tau
to be fixed in a given context so that the dependence of
m
and
m_{n}
on
\tau
is not required to be explicit in our notation.

1.2
Hadamard Spaces

Our results are set in the framework of
Hadamard spaces
, that is, geodesic metric spaces (each pair of points is connected by a geodesic) of nonpositive curvature in the sense of Alexandrov (geodesic triangles are at least as “thin” as their Euclidean counterparts). They are also called
global NPC spaces
or
complete
\mathsf{CAT}(0)
spaces
. A Hadamard space
(\mathcal{Q},d)
can be defined as a complete metric space with the following property: For all
y_{0},y_{1}\in\mathcal{Q}
there is a
m\in\mathcal{Q}
such that

\frac{1}{2}\overline{y_{0}q}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\overline{y_{1}q}^{2}-\frac{1}{4}\overline{y_{0}y_{1}}^{2}\geq\overline{qm}^{2}

(3)

for all
q\in\mathcal{Q}
. In this case,
m
is the midpoint between
y_{0}
and
y_{1}
. More details on the geometry of Hadamard spaces can be found in the textbooks
[
BBI01
,
Bač14a
]
.
Prominent examples of Hadamard spaces include:

•

Euclidean and, more generally, Hilbert spaces
[
Stu03
, Prop. 3.5]
;

•

Cartan–Hadamard manifolds, i.e., complete, simply connected Riemannian manifolds with nonpositive sectional curvature
[
Stu03
, Prop. 3.1]
;

•

\mathbb{R}
-trees (also called metric trees), geodesic spaces containing no subset homeomorphic to a circle
[
Eva08
]
;

•

the space of phylogenetic trees with the Billera–Holmes–Vogtmann metric
[
BHV01
]
;

•

the cone of symmetric positive definite matrices with the affine-invariant metric, for which the Fréchet mean coincides with the matrix geometric mean
[
BH06
]
;

•

tangent cones of Hadamard spaces, suitably completed
[
Bač14a
, Thm. 1.2.17]
,
[
BBI01
, Thm. 9.1.44]
.

Hadamard spaces are stable under a variety of natural operations, including closed convex subsets, images under isometries, products,
L^{2}
-spaces of Hadamard-valued functions, and certain gluing constructions
[
Stu03
, Sec. 3]
. Importantly, they are not required to be finite-dimensional (e.g., in the Hausdorff sense) or separable. These examples and closure properties illustrate the broad applicability of the Hadamard space framework.

1.3
Results

1.3.1
Power Fréchet Means

A particularly important subclass of transformed Fréchet means arises when

\tau(x)=x^{\alpha},\qquad\alpha\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}\,,

(4)

in which case
\tau
-Fréchet means are known as
power Fréchet means
or
\alpha
-Fréchet means
.
We restrict
\alpha\in(1,2]
and denote the
\alpha
-Fréchet mean as
m=\argmin_{q\in\mathcal{Q}}\mathbf{E}[\overline{Yq}^{\alpha}]
with its empirical counterpart
m_{n}=\argmin_{q\in\mathcal{Q}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}{\overline{Y_{i}q}^{\alpha}}
. These minimizers are unique if
\mathbf{E}[\overline{Yq}^{\alpha}]<\infty
for one (and hence all)
q\in\mathcal{Q}

[
Sch25
, Corollary 5.8]
.
We desire an upper bound on the expected loss, but at the same time want to show robustness against heavy tails. Here, heavy tails mean that
\mathbf{E}[\overline{Ym}^{2}]=\infty
. Unfortunately, this may entail
\mathbf{E}[\overline{mm_{n}}^{2}]=\infty
, i.e., the square loss typically does not allow for useful convergence rate results. We instead use a loss that weights large values of
\overline{mm_{n}}
only with power
\alpha
while retaining an
L^{2}
-type error for small values of
\overline{mm_{n}}
: We show in
Theorem

4.3
that

\mathbf{E}\!\left[\min\!\left(\overline{mm_{n}}^{2},\overline{mm_{n}}^{\alpha}\right)\right]\leq Cn^{-1}

(5)

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Specifically, there is a constant
c_{\alpha}>0
, depending only on
\alpha
, such that

C=c_{\alpha}\cdot\max(1,\mathsf{med}(\overline{Ym})^{2-\alpha})\cdot\begin{cases}\sigma_{\alpha-1}^{\frac{2-\alpha}{\alpha-1}}\sigma_{2\alpha-2}+n^{-\frac{2-\alpha}{\alpha-1}}\sigma_{\alpha}&\text{if }\alpha\geq\frac{3}{2}\,,\\
\sigma_{2-\alpha}\sigma_{2\alpha-2}+n^{-1}\sigma_{\alpha}&\text{if }\alpha\leq\frac{3}{2}\,,\\
\end{cases}

(6)

where
\sigma_{a}:=\mathbf{E}[\overline{Ym}^{a}]
and
\mathsf{med}(\cdot)
denotes the median of a random variable.

Strikingly, the only assumptions required for this parametric rate are the moment condition
\sigma_{\alpha}<\infty
and the Hadamard structure of
\mathcal{Q}
. Moreover, the highest-order moment
\sigma_{\alpha}
appears in
C
multiplied by a factor that decreases with
n
, so that for sufficiently large samples the risk is controlled primarily by the lower-order moment
\sigma_{2\alpha-2}
or
\sigma_{2-\alpha}
. With a more refined analysis (
Corollary

5.5

(ii)
), we can show that
\sigma_{2\alpha-2}
is always the dominating moment. This highlights the robustness of power Fréchet means, making them particularly attractive in settings with heavy-tailed data.

1.3.2
General Transformation

For general nondecreasing, convex transformations
\tau
with concave derivative
\tau^{\prime}
, our results take the form

\mathbf{E}\!\left[\min(\overline{mm_{n}}^{2},\overline{mm_{n}}^{2}\tau^{\prime\prime}(\overline{mm_{n}}))\right]\leq Cn^{-1}

(7)

for some constant
C\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
. Typically, the second derivative
\tau^{\prime\prime}
is decreasing to
0
so that the term
\overline{mm_{n}}^{2}\tau^{\prime\prime}(\overline{mm_{n}})
grows slower than
\overline{mm_{n}}^{2}
for large values of
\overline{mm_{n}}
. Such risk bounds hold under mild conditions on the transformation
\tau
and can be separated into two cases:

If
\limsup_{x\to\infty}\tau^{\prime}(x)=\infty
, as in the case of
\tau(x)=x^{\alpha}
,
\alpha\in(1,2]
, we roughly require the moment
\mathbf{E}[\tau^{\prime}(\overline{Ym})^{2}/\tau^{\prime\prime}(\overline{Ym})]
to be finite for establishing the parametric rate of convergence (
7
), see
Theorem

5.3
. The aforementioned moment enters the error bound multiplied by a factor decreasing with
n
and the bound is dominated by
\mathbf{E}[\tau^{\prime}(\overline{Ym})^{2}]
for large
n
. Thus, the
\tau
-Fréchet mean is robust to heavy-tailed distributions.

If
\limsup_{x\to\infty}\tau^{\prime}(x)<\infty
and
\tau^{\prime\prime}(x)>0
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, as in the case of the pseudo-Huber loss
\tau(x)=\sqrt{1+x^{2}}
, we require a minimal moment condition
\mathbf{E}[\overline{Ym}^{\gamma}]<\infty
for an arbitrary
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
to establish the parametric rate of convergence (
7
), see
Theorem

7.1
. Furthermore, in this case, the
\tau
-Fréchet mean has a breakdown point of
1/2
, see
Theorem

6.7
; it is robust to heavy-tailed distributions and contaminated data. Additionally, we establish large deviation bounds in
Theorem

6.2
and
Theorem

6.8
.

1.3.3
Median

For the Fréchet median, i.e.,
\tau(x)=x
, we obtain under some conditions

\mathbf{E}\!\left[\min(\overline{mm_{n}}^{2},\overline{mm_{n}})\right]\leq Cn^{-1}

(8)

for all
n
large enough and a suitable
C\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
.
We make a minimal moment requirement
\mathbf{E}[\overline{Ym}^{\gamma}]<\infty
for an arbitrary
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
. Furthermore, we assume that
Y
is not concentrated on a so-called bow tie (
Definition

2.5
). This condition is related to the requirement of the distribution not being concentrated on a line that is common for the spatial median in linear spaces
[
CC14
]
.
Furthermore, we show large deviation bounds for the Fréchet median, see
Theorem

6.10
. In particular, if
r\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
such that
\mathbf{P}\!\left(\overline{Ym}>r\right)<\frac{1}{10}
, then
Corollary

6.11
implies

\mathbf{P}\!\left(\overline{mm_{n}}>6r\right)\leq\left(2\mathbf{P}(\overline{Ym}>r)^{\frac{1}{3}}\right)^{n}\,.

(9)

1.3.4
Fast Rates

Our main results imply
\overline{mm_{n}}\in\mathbf{O}_{\mathbf{P}}(n^{-1/2})
for a large class of
\tau
-Fréchet means. In
Theorem

9.1
, we show that we can obtain faster rates for some transformations
\tau
if
Y
is highly concentrated at
m
. Specifically, for
\tau(x)=x^{\alpha}
,
\alpha\in(1,2]
, we show
\overline{mm_{n}}\in\mathbf{O}_{\mathbf{P}}(n^{-\frac{1}{\beta}})
if there are
c\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
and
\beta\in[\alpha,2]
such that

\mathbf{P}\!\left(\overline{Ym}\leq x\right)\geq cx^{\beta-\alpha}

(10)

for
x
close to
0
.

1.4
Proof Technique

The proofs of these results follow the ideas of algorithm stability, which have been applied in the context of Fréchet means in
[
Esc24
,
BS25
]
. In contrast to chaining-based proofs
[
Sch19
,
ALP20
]
, this allows us to obtain results not cursed by dimension, i.e., we do not require any notion of dimension to be finite or covering numbers to be bounded in some way.

For the algorithm stability proof, we build on two key results from prior work: the quadruple inequality
[
Sch24
]
and the variance inequality
[
Sch25
]
for transformations
\tau
in Hadamard spaces. Both results assume that
\tau
is nondecreasing and convex with a concave derivative. While highly nontrivial, they are essential to our arguments.

Aside from the requirement of these two fundamental properties, our proofs go beyond classical algorithm stability ideas and similar results previously shown for the
2
-Fréchet mean (i.e.,
\tau(x)=x^{2}
)
[
Esc24
,
BS25
]
as the variance inequality for transformations
\tau
has a distribution-dependent factor that poses one of the main technical challenges for deriving results in expectation.

1.5
Related Literature

The
2
-Fréchet mean (also called barycenter or center of mass) was introduced in
[
Fré48
]
; a foundational treatment in Hadamard spaces can be found in
[
Stu03
]
.
State-of-the-art strong laws of large numbers for power Fréchet means (
\tau(x)=x^{\alpha}
) in general metric spaces are derived in
[
Jaf24
]
, while laws of large numbers for transformed Fréchet means were established in
[
Sch22
]
.

For rates of convergence
[
PM19
,
Sch19
,
ALP20
]
use approaches related to chaining
[
Tal21
]
resulting in bounds that are cursed by dimension, meaning that they slow down in infinite dimensions. This is suboptimal as straightforward calculations in Hilbert spaces show rates for the arithmetic mean that do not exhibit this influence of the dimension. While the chaining-based results apply in great generality (apart from the dimension requirement), stricter geometric assumptions allow the construction of a tangent cone with Hilbert space structure, which yields convergence rates for the
2
-Fréchet mean that are not cursed by dimension
[
Le +23
]
. Furthermore, proofs based on algorithm stability
[
Esc24
,
BS25
]
reduce assumptions further while retaining the non-cursed convergence rates for
2
-Fréchet means.

While typically Fréchet means exhibit a parametric rate of convergence, in some settings the geometry of the underlying metric space induces slower rates (smearyness)
[
EH19
]
or a positive probability of perfect estimation with finite samples (stickiness)
[
Lam+23
]
.

The
1
-Fréchet mean or Fréchet median (
\tau(x)=x
), generalizes the notion of spatial median (also called geometric median) in normed spaces. In Euclidean spaces, the spatial median is well understood
[
MNO10
,
MS24
]
and many results extend to Banach spaces
[
Kem87
,
CC14
,
Min15
,
Rom23
]
. Furthermore, the median on Riemannian manifolds is studied in
[
Yan10
]
. Practical computation of medians and means in Hadamard spaces is addressed in
[
Bač14
]
.

In the context of robust statistics in metric spaces, median-of-means estimators were examined in
[
YP23
,
KPB25
]
. Like the transformation-function approach introduced here, these estimators balance between the median and the classical mean. Another example of such a trade-off is the trimmed Fréchet mean
[
OOR25
]
, which has been shown to be minimax optimal under adversarial sample contamination.

Beyond the power Fréchet means, further forms of transformed Fréchet means have been studied:
[
RB23
]
consider convex transformations in a metric tree—a specific type of Hadamard space;
[
LJ25
]
consider the Huber and pseudo-Huber loss on Riemannian manifolds; and
[
BFR26
]
consider Fréchet means with convex transformations in the Wasserstein space. Fundamental properties such as existence and uniqueness of transformed Fréchet means in Hadamard spaces were studied in
[
Sch25
]
.

Beyond the
2
-Fréchet mean, no general rate of convergence results in Hadamard metric spaces with minimal assumptions have been shown and most results in this paper are new even for Euclidean spaces, e.g., the finite sample bound for power Fréchet means.

2
Preliminaries

2.1
Nondecreasing, Convex Functions with Concave Derivative

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be the set of nondecreasing convex functions
\tau\colon\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{R}
that are differentiable on
\mathbb{R}_{>0}
with concave derivative
\tau^{\prime}
. We extend the domain of
\tau^{\prime}
to
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
by setting
\tau^{\prime}(0):=\lim_{x\searrow 0}\tau^{\prime}(x)
, which exists, as
\tau^{\prime}
is nonnegative and nondecreasing.

Requiring differentiability of
\tau
is not restrictive, as this is implied by convexity for Lebesgue almost all
x\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
.
For technical reasons it is often more convenient to work with
\mathcal{S}_{0}^{+}\subset\mathcal{S}
, the subset of strictly increasing functions
\tau\in\mathcal{S}
with
\tau(0)=0
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{0}^{+}

\displaystyle:=\left\{\tau\in\mathcal{S}\,\kern-1.2pt\mathopen{}\left|\vphantom{\tau\in\mathcal{S}}\,\tau(0)=0\text{ and }\forall x\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}\colon\tau^{\prime}(x)>0\right.\mathclose{}\kern-1.2pt\right\}

(11)

\displaystyle=\left\{x\mapsto\tau(x)-\tau(0)\,\kern-1.2pt\mathopen{}\left|\vphantom{x\mapsto\tau(x)-\tau(0)}\,\tau\in\mathcal{S}\right.\mathclose{}\kern-1.2pt\right\}\setminus\left\{x\mapsto 0\right\}\,.

(12)

This is not restrictive, as we essentially only exclude constant functions.
To be able to talk about derivatives of
\tau\in\mathcal{S}
at
0
and second derivatives, let us recall the definition of the one-sided derivatives.

Notation 2.2
.

Let
A\subset\mathbb{R}
and
f\colon A\to\mathbb{R}
. Let
x_{0}\in A
such that there is
\epsilon>0
such that
(x_{0}-\epsilon,x_{0}]\subset A
. Then denote the left derivative of
f
at
x_{0}
as
f^{\ominus}(x_{0}):=\lim_{x\nearrow x_{0}}\frac{f(x)-f(x_{0})}{x-x_{0}}
if the limit exists. Similarly, for
x_{0}\in A
with
\epsilon>0
such that
[x_{0},x_{0}+\epsilon)\subset A
, we denote the right derivative of
f
at
x_{0}
as
f^{\oplus}(x_{0}):=\lim_{x\searrow x_{0}}\frac{f(x)-f(x_{0})}{x-x_{0}}
if the limit exists.

Let us note some basic continuity properties of functions in
\mathcal{S}
and existence of one-sided derivatives. See
[
Sch25
]
for proofs.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\tau\in\mathcal{S}
. Then

(i)

\tau
and
\tau^{\prime}
are continuous and nondecreasing,

(ii)

\tau^{\oplus}(0)
exists and
\tau^{\oplus}(0)=\tau^{\prime}(0)
,

(iii)

\tau^{\prime\oplus}(x)
exists for all
x\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
and
\tau^{\prime\oplus}
is nonincreasing.

Further important properties of the functions
\tau\in\mathcal{S}
are listed in
Section

2.5
and
Section

S2
.

2.2
Geometry

We state some basic definitions regarding geodesics and convexity in metric spaces and define the bow tie set.
Let
(\mathcal{Q},d)
be a nonempty metric space and denote
\overline{qp}:=d(q,p)
for
q,p\in\mathcal{Q}
.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
I\subset\mathbb{R}
be convex.

(i)

A function
\gamma\colon I\to\mathcal{Q}
is called
geodesic
if and only if

\overline{\gamma(r)\gamma(t)}=\overline{\gamma(r)\gamma(s)}+\overline{\gamma(s)\gamma(t)}

(13)

for all
r,s,t\in I
with
r<s<t
.

(ii)

Let
\gamma\colon I\to\mathcal{Q}
be a geodesic. If there is
L\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
such that
\overline{\gamma(s)\gamma(t)}=L|s-t|
for all
s,t\in I
, then the geodesic is said to have
constant speed
. If
L=1
, we call
\gamma
a
unit-speed geodesic
.

(iii)

The metric space
(\mathcal{Q},d)
is called
unique geodesic space
, if and only if each pair of points
(q,p)\in\mathcal{Q}^{2}
is connected by a unique unit-speed geodesic
\gamma_{q\to p}\colon[0,\overline{qp}]\to\mathcal{Q}
so that
\gamma_{q\to p}(0)=q
and
\gamma_{q\to p}(\overline{qp})=p
.

[

]
for the study of the Fréchet median in general Hadamard spaces.

2.3
Basic Setup

Throughout the remaining article, we will assume the following setup without further mentioning it: Let
(\mathcal{Q},d)
be a Hadamard space. For
q,p\in\mathcal{Q}
, we denote
\overline{qp}:=d(q,p)
. This metric space is equipped with its Borel-
\sigma
-algebra. Let
(\Omega,\Sigma_{\Omega},\mathbf{P})
be a probability space. The expectation of measurable functions
X\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
is denoted as
\mathbf{E}[X]
if it exists. Let
Y
be a measurable function
Y\colon\Omega\to\mathcal{Q}
, i.e., a
\mathcal{Q}
-valued random variable. Let the two sets of
n\in\mathbb{N}
samples
Y_{1},Y_{2},\dots,Y_{n}
and
Y_{1}^{\prime},Y_{2}^{\prime},\dots,Y_{n}^{\prime}
be independent and identically distributed copies of
Y
.
Denote the samples with
i
-th position replaced as
Y_{j}^{i}:=Y_{j}
if
i\neq j
and
Y_{i}^{i}:=Y_{i}^{\prime}
.
Let
\tau\in\mathcal{S}_{0}^{+}
. Let
o\in\mathcal{Q}
be an arbitrary reference point. Assume
\mathbf{E}[\tau^{\prime}(\overline{Yo})]<\infty
. Let the population and sample
\tau
-Fréchet means be

m\in\argmin_{q\in\mathcal{Q}}\mathbf{E}\!\left[\tau(\overline{Yq})-\tau(\overline{Yo})\right]\,,\ m_{n}\in\argmin_{q\in\mathcal{Q}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\tau(\overline{Y_{j}q})\,,\ m_{n}^{i}\in\argmin_{q\in\mathcal{Q}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\tau(\overline{Y_{j}^{i}q})\,.

(15)

2.4
Transformed Fréchet Mean

Basic properties of the transformed Fréchet mean were derived in
[
Sch25
]
. We briefly summarize the essential concepts here and refer to
[
Sch25
]
for proofs and further details.

The assumption
\mathbf{E}[\tau^{\prime}(\overline{Yo})]<\infty
implies
\mathbf{E}[|\tau(\overline{Yq})-\tau(\overline{Yp})|]<\infty
for all
q,p\in\mathcal{Q}
. In this case, we define the
\tau
-Fréchet mean set as

M:=\argmin_{q\in\mathcal{Q}}\mathbf{E}\!\left[\tau(\overline{Yq})-\tau(\overline{Yo})\right]\,.

(16)

The set
M
is nonempty, closed, and convex. Thus, if
\mathcal{Q}
is locally compact, then
M
is compact.
Local compactness of
\mathcal{Q}
may not be required for compactness of
M
: By
[
Jaf24
, Example 2.5 and Corollary 3.10]
, if
\tau(x)=x^{\alpha}
with
\alpha\geq 1
, and
\mathcal{Q}
is a separable Hadamard space, then
M
is compact.
The set
M
does not depend on the choice of
o
.
Moreover, if
\mathbf{E}[\tau(\overline{Yo})]
is finite, then
\mathbf{E}[\tau(\overline{Yq})]
is finite for all
q\in\mathcal{Q}
and
M=\argmin_{q\in\mathcal{Q}}\mathbf{E}[\tau(\overline{Yq})]
.

Let
x_{0}:=\inf\{x\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}\mid\tau^{\prime\oplus}(x)=0\}
, with the convention
\inf\emptyset=\infty
. If
m\in M
and
\mathbf{P}(\overline{Ym}<x_{0})>0
, then
M=\{m\}
. Thus, if
\tau^{\prime\oplus}(x)>0
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, then the
\tau
-Fréchet mean is unique. Alternatively, if
\mathcal{Q}
is separable and the support of
Y
is convex, then the
\tau
-Fréchet mean is unique for any
\tau\in\mathcal{S}_{0}^{+}
.

Let the empirical transformed Fréchet mean set be

M_{n}:=\argmin_{q\in\mathcal{Q}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\tau(\overline{Y_{i}q})=\argmin_{q\in\mathcal{Q}}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\tau(\overline{Y_{i}q})-\tau(\overline{Y_{i}o})\right)\,.

(17)

This estimator of
M
satisfies a strong law of large numbers
[
Sch22
,
EJ24
,
Jaf24
]
under a first-moment condition, which in this setting amounts to
\mathbf{E}[\tau^{\prime}(\overline{Yo})]<\infty
for transformed Fréchet means with
\tau\in\mathcal{S}_{0}^{+}
. If
M
is not a singleton, the convergence guaranteed by the strong law is generally one-sided: convergent subsequences of
m_{n}\in M_{n}
have limits in
M
, but not every
m\in M
arises as the limit of an empirical sequence. See
[
BJ25
]
for a relaxation technique that yields convergence in the Hausdorff metric, i.e., two-sided convergence.

2.5
Quadruple Inequality

The first central ingredient for the main proofs is a quadruple inequality, detailed in
[
Sch24
]
.
Quadruple inequalities generalize the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality of Hilbert spaces
\mathcal{H}
with the square transformation, i.e.,

\left\|y-q\right\|^{2}-\left\|y-p\right\|^{2}-\left\|z-q\right\|^{2}+\left\|z-p\right\|^{2}=2\langle p-q,y-z\rangle\leq 2\left\|q-p\right\|\left\|y-z\right\|

(18)

for all
q,p,y,z\in\mathcal{H}
, to Hadamard spaces and transformations in
\mathcal{S}_{0}^{+}
.

Proposition 2.7

(
[
Sch24
, Theorem 1]
)
.

For all
q,p,y,z\in\mathcal{Q}
,

\tau(\overline{yq})-\tau(\overline{yp})-\tau(\overline{zq})+\tau(\overline{zp})\leq 2\,\overline{qp}\,\tau^{\prime}(\overline{yz})\,.

(19)

Proposition

2.7
implies the symmetrized quadruple inequality

\left|\tau(\overline{yq})-\tau(\overline{yp})-\tau(\overline{zq})+\tau(\overline{zp})\right|\leq 2\min\!\left(\overline{qp}\,\tau^{\prime}(\overline{yz}),\,\overline{yz}\,\tau^{\prime}(\overline{qp})\right)

(20)

for all
q,p,y,z\in\mathcal{Q}
.
The constant
2
on the right-hand side of (
19
) can be slightly improved for
\tau(x)=x^{\alpha}
to the optimal constant
2^{2-\alpha}\alpha
:

Proposition 2.8

(
[
Sch19
, Theorem 3]
)
.

Let
\alpha\in[1,2]
.
Then, for all
q,p,y,z\in\mathcal{Q}
,

\overline{yq}^{\alpha}-\overline{yp}^{\alpha}-\overline{zq}^{\alpha}+\overline{zp}^{\alpha}\leq 2^{2-\alpha}\alpha\,\overline{qp}\,\overline{yz}^{\alpha-1}\,.

(21)

3
Algorithm Stability

The convergence rate proofs in this article rely on algorithmic stability. The initial steps are closely related to the arguments from
[
Esc24
]
for M-estimators and
[
BS25
]
for the
2
-Fréchet mean. These steps extend to
\tau
-Fréchet means using the quadruple inequality
Proposition

2.7
and the variance inequality
Proposition

2.9
. Because the lower bound in this variance inequality depends on the underlying distribution, additional arguments are needed to establish convergence rates for
\tau
-Fréchet means. The arguments derived from the classical line of reasoning are presented here; additional techniques that lead to our main results appear in later sections.

Proposition 3.1
.

Use the convention
0^{-1}=\infty
.
Then

\mathbf{E}\!\left[\overline{mm_{n}}^{2}\tau^{\prime\oplus}\!\left(\overline{Ym}+\overline{mm_{n}}\right)\right]\leq\frac{32}{n^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{E}\!\left[\tau^{\prime}(\overline{Y_{i}m})^{2}H_{i}^{-1}\right]\,,

(24)

where

H_{i}:=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\tau^{\prime\oplus}\!\left(\overline{Y_{j}m_{n}}+\overline{m_{n}m_{n}^{i}}\right)\,.

(25)

Before proving
Proposition

3.1
, observe that in the special case
\tau(x)=x^{2}
, we have
\tau^{\prime}(x)=2x
and
\tau^{\prime\oplus}(x)=2
. Hence,
Proposition

3.1
yields

\mathbf{E}\!\left[\overline{mm_{n}}^{2}\right]\leq 32n^{-1}\mathbf{E}\!\left[\overline{Ym}^{2}\right]\,,

(26)

confer
[
BS25
, Theorem 3]
. In the general case, however, the
\tau^{\prime\oplus}
-terms make the results unsatisfactory at this stage. The subsequent sections are devoted to addressing these terms to obtain clean results from
Proposition

3.1
for arbitrary
\tau\in\mathcal{S}_{0}^{+}
.

For the proof of
Proposition

3.1
, first define the
double excess risk
as

V_{n}:=\mathbf{E}\!\left[\tau(\overline{Ym_{n}})-\tau(\overline{Ym})\right]+\mathbf{E}\!\left[\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\tau(\overline{Y_{i}m})-\tau(\overline{Y_{i}m_{n}})\right)\right]\,.

(27)

Lemma 3.2
.

We have

V_{n}\leq\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{E}\!\left[\overline{m_{n}m_{n}^{i}}\,\tau^{\prime}(\overline{Y_{i}Y_{i}^{i}})\right]\,.

(28)

Proof.

As
Y
has the same distribution as
Y_{i}
and
(Y,m_{n})
has the same distribution as
(Y_{i},m_{n}^{i})
, we have

V_{n}=\mathbf{E}\!\left[\tau(\overline{Ym_{n}})-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\tau(\overline{Y_{i}m_{n}})\right]=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{E}\!\left[\tau(\overline{Y_{i}m_{n}^{i}})-\tau(\overline{Y_{i}m_{n}})\right]\,.

(29)

By the quadruple inequality,
Proposition

2.7
, we have

\left(\tau(\overline{Y_{i}m_{n}^{i}})-\tau(\overline{Y_{i}m_{n}})\right)+\left(\tau(\overline{Y_{i}^{i}m_{n}})-\tau(\overline{Y_{i}^{i}m_{n}^{i}})\right)\leq 2\,\overline{m_{n}m_{n}^{i}}\,\tau^{\prime}(\overline{Y_{i}Y_{i}^{i}})\,.

(30)

As
(Y_{i},m_{n},m_{n}^{i})
has the same distribution as
(Y_{i}^{i},m_{n}^{i},m_{n})
, we obtain

2\mathbf{E}[\tau(\overline{Y_{i}m_{n}^{i}})-\tau(\overline{Y_{i}m_{n}})]\leq 2\mathbf{E}\!\left[\overline{m_{n}m_{n}^{i}}\,\tau^{\prime}(\overline{Y_{i}Y_{i}^{i}})\right]\,.

(31)

Combining (
29
) and (
31
) yields the desired result.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

We have

\overline{m_{n}m_{n}^{i}}\,\tilde{H}_{i}\leq\frac{4}{n}\tau^{\prime}(\overline{Y_{i}Y_{i}^{i}})\,.

(32)

where

\tilde{H}_{i}:=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(\tau^{\prime\oplus}\!\left(\overline{Y_{j}m_{n}}+\overline{m_{n}m_{n}^{i}}\right)+\tau^{\prime\oplus}\!\left(\overline{Y_{j}^{i}m_{n}^{i}}+\overline{m_{n}m_{n}^{i}}\right)\right)\,.

(33)

Proof.

The variance inequality
Proposition

2.9
applied to the empirical distributions yields, for
q\in\mathcal{Q}
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\overline{qm_{n}}^{2}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\tau^{\prime\oplus}\!\left(\overline{Y_{j}m_{n}}+\overline{qm_{n}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(\tau(\overline{Y_{j}q})-\tau(\overline{Y_{j}m_{n}})\right)\,,

(34)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\overline{qm_{n}^{i}}^{2}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\tau^{\prime\oplus}\!\left(\overline{Y_{j}^{i}m_{n}^{i}}+\overline{qm_{n}^{i}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(\tau(\overline{Y_{j}^{i}q})-\tau(\overline{Y_{j}^{i}m_{n}^{i}})\right)\,.

(35)

Thus, plugging in
q=m_{n}^{i}
and
q=m_{n}
respectively, adding the two inequalities, and noting
Y_{j}^{i}=Y_{j}
for
i\neq j
, yields

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\overline{m_{n}m_{n}^{i}}^{2}\tilde{H}_{i}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(\tau(\overline{Y_{j}m_{n}^{i}})-\tau(\overline{Y_{j}m_{n}})+\tau(\overline{Y_{j}^{i}m_{n}})-\tau(\overline{Y_{j}^{i}m_{n}^{i}})\right)

(36)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\left(\tau(\overline{Y_{i}m_{n}^{i}})-\tau(\overline{Y_{i}m_{n}})+\tau(\overline{Y_{i}^{i}m_{n}})-\tau(\overline{Y_{i}^{i}m_{n}^{i}})\right)\,.

(37)

Hence, the quadruple inequality,
Proposition

2.7
, implies

\frac{1}{2}\overline{m_{n}m_{n}^{i}}^{2}\tilde{H}_{i}\leq 2\frac{1}{n}\,\overline{m_{n}m_{n}^{i}}\,\tau^{\prime}(\overline{Y_{i}Y_{i}^{i}})\,.

(38)

Rearranging the terms yields the desired result.
∎

Proof of
Proposition

3.1
.

Combining
Lemma

3.2
and
Lemma

3.3
, we obtain

V_{n}\leq\frac{4}{n^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{E}\!\left[\tau^{\prime}(\overline{Y_{i}Y_{i}^{i}})^{2}\tilde{H}_{i}^{-1}\right]

(39)

with
\tilde{H}_{i}
given in (
33
).
With
Lemma

S2.1
and the triangle inequality we get

\tau^{\prime}(\overline{Y_{i}Y_{i}^{i}})^{2}\leq 2\tau^{\prime}(\overline{Y_{i}m})^{2}+2\tau^{\prime}(\overline{Y_{i}^{i}m})^{2}\,.

(40)

As
(\tilde{H}_{i},Y_{i})
has the same distribution as
(\tilde{H}_{i},Y_{i}^{i})
, this yields

\mathbf{E}\!\left[\tau^{\prime}(\overline{Y_{i}Y_{i}^{i}})^{2}\tilde{H}_{i}^{-1}\right]\leq 4\mathbf{E}\!\left[\tau^{\prime}(\overline{Y_{i}m})^{2}\tilde{H}_{i}^{-1}\right]\,.

(41)

Furthermore,
\tilde{H}_{i}\geq H_{i}
. Thus,

V_{n}\leq\frac{16}{n^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{E}\!\left[\tau^{\prime}(\overline{Y_{i}m})^{2}H_{i}^{-1}\right]\,.

(42)

By the minimizing property of
m_{n}
and the variance inequality
Proposition

2.9
,

V_{n}\geq\mathbf{E}\!\left[\tau(\overline{Ym_{n}})-\tau(\overline{Ym})\right]\geq\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{E}\!\left[\overline{mm_{n}}^{2}\tau^{\prime\oplus}\!\left(\overline{Ym}+\overline{mm_{n}}\right)\right]\,.

(43)

Now (
42
) and (
43
) together show the desired result.
∎

4
Power Fréchet Means

In this section, we consider power Fréchet means, i.e.,
\tau
-Fréchet means with
\tau(x)=x^{\alpha}
. We restrict to
\alpha\in(1,2]
, which makes
\tau
nondecreasing and convex with concave derivative allowing us to use the quadruple and variance inequalities. We exclude the case of the Fréchet median,
\alpha=1
, which is special and is treated separately in
Section

8
. We derive convergence rates in expectation with explicit constants, see
Theorem

4.3
and
Remark

S3.8
. We illustrate the result by applying it in the case
\alpha=\frac{3}{2}
in
Corollary

4.5
.

Notation 4.1
.

Let
a\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
. Use the convention
0^{0}:=1
. Define the
a
-moment of
Y
as

\sigma_{a}:=\mathbf{E}\!\left[\overline{Ym}^{a}\right]\,.

(44)

Remark 4.2
.

When we are only interested in whether an
a
-moment is finite or not, the reference point does not matter: Using the triangle inequality and
Lemma

S1.1
, we have

\mathbf{E}\!\left[\overline{Yq}^{a}\right]\leq 2^{\max(0,a-1)}\left(\mathbf{E}\!\left[\overline{Yp}^{a}\right]+\overline{qp}^{a}\right)\qquad\text{for all $q,p\in\mathcal{Q}$.}

(45)

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
\alpha\in(1,2]
and
\tau(x)=x^{\alpha}
.
Let
\chi:=\mathsf{med}(\overline{Ym})
.
Then

\mathbf{E}\!\left[\min\!\left(\chi^{\alpha-2}\overline{mm_{n}}^{2},\overline{mm_{n}}^{\alpha}\right)\right]\leq Cn^{-1}

(46)

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, where

C:=c_{\alpha}\cdot\begin{cases}\sigma_{\alpha-1}^{\frac{2-\alpha}{\alpha-1}}\sigma_{2\alpha-2}+n^{-\frac{2-\alpha}{\alpha-1}}\sigma_{\alpha}&\text{if }\alpha\geq\frac{3}{2}\,,\\
\sigma_{2-\alpha}\sigma_{2\alpha-2}+n^{-1}\sigma_{\alpha}&\text{if }\alpha\leq\frac{3}{2}\\
\end{cases}

(47)

and
c_{\alpha}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
only depends on
\alpha
.

Remark 4.4
.

Even though we bound the
\alpha

5
Tail-Robust Means

In this section, we establish rates of convergence in expectation for
\tau
-Fréchet means with
\tau\in\mathcal{S}_{0}^{+}
and
\limsup_{x\to\infty}\tau^{\prime}(x)=\infty
. The resulting (unique)
\tau
-Fréchet means are robust to heavy-tailed distributions, but not to arbitrary adversarial corruption of data (breakdown point
0
). The complementary case, where
\limsup_{x\to\infty}\tau^{\prime}(x)<\infty
, will be addressed in
Section

7
.

As a moment assumption, we will require conditions similar to
\mathbf{E}[\tau^{\prime}(\overline{Ym})^{2}/\tau^{\prime\oplus}(\overline{Ym})]<\infty
. This is at least as strong as
\mathbf{E}[\tau(\overline{Ym})]<\infty
(see
Lemma

S5.1
), which allows us to define the
\tau
-Fréchet mean as the minimizer of
q\mapsto\mathbf{E}[\tau(\overline{Yq})]
.

We define the notation
\sigma_{f}
for the moment induced by a function
f
, and
\hat{\sigma}_{f}
for its empirical version.

Notation 5.1
.

Let
f\colon\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
be a measurable function.
Define

\sigma_{f}:=\mathbf{E}\!\left[f(\overline{Ym})\right]\,,\qquad\hat{\sigma}_{f}:=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}f(\overline{Y_{j}m})\qquad\text{and}\qquad\hat{\sigma}_{f}^{i}:=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}f(\overline{Y_{j}^{i}m})\,.

(49)

Notation 5.2
.

Define the generalized inverse function of
\tau^{\prime}
as

(\tau^{\prime})^{-1}(z):=\sup\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}\,\kern-1.2pt\mathopen{}\left|\vphantom{x\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}}\,\tau^{\prime}(x)\leq z\right.\mathclose{}\kern-1.2pt\right\}\quad\text{with the convention }\sup\emptyset=0\,.

(50)

Theorem 5.3
.

Assume
\limsup_{x\to\infty}\tau^{\prime}(x)=\infty
.
Denote
g(x):=\tau^{\prime\oplus}(7x)^{-1}
and
h(x):=g((\tau^{\prime})^{-1}(12x))
.
Let
p\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
. Set

\displaystyle S_{p}

\displaystyle:=\max\!\left(\sigma_{g^{p}},2h(\sigma_{\tau^{\prime}})^{p},\mathbf{E}\!\left[h(2\hat{\sigma}_{\tau^{\prime}})^{p}\right]\right)\,,

(51)

\displaystyle V_{n,p}

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{n}\mathbf{E}\!\left[\tau^{\prime}(\overline{Ym})^{2p}g(\overline{Ym})^{p}\right]+\mathbf{E}\!\left[\tau^{\prime}(\overline{Ym})^{2p}h(2n^{-1}\tau^{\prime}(\overline{Ym}))^{p}\right]\,.

(52)

Set
\chi\in\mathsf{med}(\overline{Ym})
.
Let
p,q>1
such that
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1
.
Set

r_{0}:=\max\!\left(\chi,2(\tau^{\prime})^{-1}\!\left(16\sigma_{\tau^{\prime}}\right)\right)\,.

(53)

Then

\mathbf{E}\!\left[\overline{mm_{n}}^{2}\min\!\left(\tau^{\prime\oplus}(2\chi),\tau^{\prime\oplus}(2\,\overline{mm_{n}})\right)\right]\leq\frac{64}{n}\min\!\left(4\sigma_{(\tau^{\prime})^{2}}S_{1}+V_{n,1},\,\frac{4\sigma_{(\tau^{\prime})^{2}}}{\tau^{\prime\oplus}(4r_{0})}+b_{n}\right)\,,

(54)

where

b_{n}:=\left(V_{n,p}+4\sigma_{(\tau^{\prime})^{2p}}S_{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\exp\!\left(-\frac{n}{16}\right)+\frac{2}{n}\left(\frac{\sigma_{(\tau^{\prime})^{2}}}{\sigma_{\tau^{\prime}}^{2}}-1\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\,.

(55)

Remark 5.4
.

Let us be imprecise for the sake of illustrating this result. We approximate
\tau(x)\approx x^{2}\tau^{\prime\oplus}(x)\approx\tau^{\prime}(x)^{2}/\tau^{\prime\oplus}(x)
, which is a valid approximation at least for
\tau(x)=x^{\alpha}
,
\alpha\in(1,2]
. Then we effectively bound a risk of the loss
\tau
applied to
\overline{mm_{n}}
(for large
\overline{mm_{n}}
). One might expect a moment term
\mathbf{E}[\tau(\overline{Ym})]
to come up in such a risk bound. And indeed it does (in the form of functions related to
\tau^{\prime}(x)^{2}/\tau^{\prime\oplus}(x)
). But this moment is multiplied by factors that vanish for
n\to\infty
so that the dominating moment is
\sigma_{(\tau^{\prime})^{2}}=\mathbf{E}[\tau^{\prime}(\overline{Ym})^{2}]
, which is a lower order moment (except when
\tau(x)\approx x^{2}
for large
x
). Thus, not only do
\tau
-Fréchet means require just a
\tau
-moment instead of a second moment for a parametric rate of convergence, the dominating moment in the rate is of the even lower order
(\tau^{\prime})^{2}
.

Denoting
\mathbf{o}(1)
for a term going to zero for
n\to\infty
, we can simplify
Theorem

5.3
by focusing on the dominating terms in the upper bound:

Corollary 5.5
.

Use the setting of
Theorem

5.3
.

(i)

Assume
\mathbf{E}\!\left[\tau^{\prime}(\overline{Ym})^{2}h(\tau^{\prime}(\overline{Ym}))\right]<\infty
.
Assume
\lim_{x\searrow 0}\tau^{\prime\oplus}(x)=\infty
.
Then

\mathbf{E}\!\left[\overline{mm_{n}}^{2}\min\!\left(\tau^{\prime\oplus}(2\chi),\tau^{\prime\oplus}(2\,\overline{mm_{n}})\right)\right]\leq\frac{256}{n}\left(\sigma_{(\tau^{\prime})^{2}}S_{1}+\mathbf{o}(1)\right)\,.

(56)

(ii)

Let
\epsilon>0
.
Assume
\mathbf{E}\!\left[\tau^{\prime}(\overline{Ym})^{2(1+\epsilon)}h(\tau^{\prime}(\overline{Ym}))^{1+\epsilon}\right]<\infty
,
\mathbf{E}\!\left[h(2\hat{\sigma}_{\tau^{\prime}})^{1+\epsilon}\right]<\infty
and
\sigma_{g^{1+\epsilon}}<\infty
.
Then

\mathbf{E}\!\left[\overline{mm_{n}}^{2}\min\!\left(\tau^{\prime\oplus}(2\chi),\tau^{\prime\oplus}(2\,\overline{mm_{n}})\right)\right]\leq\frac{256}{n}\left(\sigma_{(\tau^{\prime})^{2}}\tau^{\prime\oplus}(4r_{0})^{-1}+\mathbf{o}(1)\right)\,.

(57)

is effectively an application of part

Remark 5.7
.

If

and

are subadditive up to a constant, in the sense that

Lemma 5.8

Proof.

(68)

By the minimizing property of

we also have

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\tau(\overline{Y_{i}m})-\tau(\overline{Y_{i}m_{n}})\right)\geq 0\,.

(69)

Using the last two inequalities and the quadruple inequality,

\displaystyle\tau(\overline{mm_{n}})-2\,\overline{mm_{n}}\,\mathbf{E}\!\left[\tau^{\prime}(\overline{Ym})\right]

(71)

Rearranging the terms, yields

\frac{\tau(\overline{mm_{n}})}{\overline{mm_{n}}}\leq 2\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{E}\!\left[\tau^{\prime}(\overline{YY_{i}})|Y_{i}\right]+2\mathbf{E}\!\left[\tau^{\prime}(\overline{Ym})\right]\,.

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4\sigma_{(\tau^{\prime})^{2}}}{\tau^{\prime\oplus}(4r_{0})}+\left(V_{p}+4\sigma_{(\tau^{\prime})^{2p}}S_{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}u_{n}^{\frac{1}{q}}\,.

(106)

\mathbf{E}\!\left[\tau^{\prime}(\overline{Y_{i}m})^{2}H_{i}^{-1}\right]\leq V_{1}+4\sigma_{(\tau^{\prime})^{2}}S_{1}\,.\qed

Set
\chi\in\mathsf{med}(\overline{Ym})

For all

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\min\!\left(\tau^{\prime\oplus}(2\chi),\tau^{\prime\oplus}(2\,\overline{qm})\right)\qquad\text{and}

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\!\left[\tau(\overline{Yq})-\tau(\overline{Ym})\right]

Proof.

The variance inequality

(113)

Thus,

6
Large Deviations

We discuss large deviation bounds for the
\tau
-Fréchet mean
m
assuming
\limsup_{x\to\infty}\tau^{\prime}(x)<\infty
. Examples of such transformations are the identity (yielding the Fréchet median), the Huber loss, and the pseudo-Huber loss. First, we consider deterministic bounds that quantify the maximum distance of
m
from a set with mass
>\frac{1}{2}
. As a corollary, we obtain that such transformed Fréchet means have a breakdown point of
\frac{1}{2}
. Thereafter, we show that the estimator
m_{n}
stays in a bounded region around
m
with high probability. These results will be important for proving rates of convergence in expectation with minimal moment assumptions in
Section

7
and
Section

8
. The proofs for this section can be found in
Section

S5.2
.

6.1
Deterministic Bound

Notation 6.1
.

Denote the diameter of a set
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{Q}
as
\mathsf{diam}(\mathcal{B}):=\sup_{q,p\in\mathcal{B}}\overline{qp}
and the distance from a point
p\in\mathcal{Q}
to the set
\mathcal{B}
as
d(p,\mathcal{B}):=\inf_{q\in\mathcal{B}}\overline{qp}
.

Recall
Definition

2.6
, for the definition of convex sets in Hadamard spaces.

Theorem 6.2
.

Assume
\limsup_{x\to\infty}\tau^{\prime}(x)=:D<\infty
.
Let
\mathcal{B}\subseteq\mathcal{Q}
be a convex and closed set with diameter
\delta:=\mathsf{diam}(\mathcal{B})
. Set
\rho:=\mathbf{P}(Y\in\mathcal{B})
.
Let
R\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
and
\lambda\in(0,1]
such that
\tau(R)\geq\lambda DR
.
Assume
\rho>\frac{1}{1+\lambda}
.
Set
a:=\frac{1-\rho}{\rho}
and

x_{0}:=\frac{\delta}{\lambda-a}\frac{a+\lambda\sqrt{1-\lambda^{2}+a^{2}}}{a+\lambda}\,.

(118)

Then,

d(m,\mathcal{B})^{2}\leq\max\!\left(x_{0}^{2},R^{2}-\delta^{2}\right)\,.

(119)

Remark 6.3
.

(i)

In
Theorem

6.2
, we have

x_{0}\leq\frac{\delta}{\lambda-a}\,.

(120)

(ii)

As
\tau^{\prime}
is nondecreasing and
\limsup_{x\to\infty}\tau^{\prime}(x)=D
, the condition on
R
and
\lambda
can always be fulfilled: For all
\lambda\in[0,1)
there is
R\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
such that
\tau(R)\geq\lambda DR
.
In this case, we have
Dx\geq\tau(x)\geq\lambda Dx
for all
x\geq R
.

Corollary 6.4
.

Let
\tau(x)=x
so that
m
is a Fréchet median.
Let
\mathcal{B}\subseteq\mathcal{Q}
be a convex and closed set with diameter
\delta:=\mathsf{diam}(\mathcal{B})
. Set
\rho:=\mathbf{P}(Y\in\mathcal{B})
.
Assume
\rho>\frac{1}{2}
.
Then

d(m,\mathcal{B})\leq 2\rho\delta\frac{1-\rho}{2\rho-1}\,.

(121)

Definition 6.6
.

Let
\epsilon>0
. An
\epsilon
-contamination
of a probability distribution
P
on
\mathcal{Q}
is any probability distribution
\tilde{P}=\check{P}+\mu
, where
\check{P}
is a measure with
\check{P}(\mathcal{Q})=1-\epsilon
and
\check{P}(B)\leq P(B)
for all measurable sets
B\subset\mathcal{Q}
and
\mu
is a measure with
\mu(\mathcal{Q})=\epsilon
.

Let
\mathcal{P}
be a set of probability distributions. Let
T\colon\mathcal{P}\to\mathcal{Z}
be a statistic with values in the measurable space
(\mathcal{Z},\Sigma_{\mathcal{Z}})
. Let
\delta\colon\mathcal{Z}\times\mathcal{Z}\to[0,\infty]
be a function. The
breakdown point
of
T
at
P\in\mathcal{P}
with respect to
\mathcal{P}
and
\delta
is

\varepsilon(P,\delta,\mathcal{P},T):=\inf\left\{\epsilon>0\,\kern-1.2pt\mathopen{}\left|\vphantom{\epsilon>0}\,\sup\left\{\delta(T(P),T(\tilde{P}))\,\kern-1.2pt\mathopen{}\left|\vphantom{\delta(T(P),T(\tilde{P}))}\,\tilde{P}\in\mathcal{P}\text{ is }\epsilon\text{-contamination of }P\right.\mathclose{}\kern-1.2pt\right\}=\infty\right.\mathclose{}\kern-1.2pt\right\}\,.

(123)

Let
\mathcal{P}_{0}(\mathcal{Q})
be the set of all probability distributions on
\mathcal{Q}
.
For
\tau\in\mathcal{S}_{0}^{+}
, let
\mathcal{P}_{\tau^{\prime}}(\mathcal{Q})
be the set of all
P\in\mathcal{P}_{0}(\mathcal{Q})
such that
\mathbf{E}[\tau^{\prime}(\overline{Yq})]<\infty
for one (and hence all)
q\in\mathcal{Q}
, where
Y\sim P
.

Theorem 6.7
.

Assume
\mathsf{diam}(\mathcal{Q})=\infty
.
For
P\in\mathcal{P}_{\tau^{\prime}}(\mathcal{Q})
, let
M(P)
be the set of
\tau
-Fréchet means of
Y\sim P
.
For
A,B\subset\mathcal{Q}
, define

\delta(A,B):=\sup_{a\in A,b\in B}\overline{ab}\,.

(124)

(i)

Assume
\limsup_{x\to\infty}\tau^{\prime}(x)<\infty
. Then
\mathcal{P}_{\tau^{\prime}}(\mathcal{Q})=\mathcal{P}_{0}(\mathcal{Q})
and

\forall P\in\mathcal{P}_{0}(\mathcal{Q})\colon\varepsilon(P,\delta,\mathcal{P}_{0}(\mathcal{Q}),M)=\frac{1}{2}\,.

(125)

(ii)

Assume
\limsup_{x\to\infty}\tau^{\prime}(x)=\infty
. Then

\forall P\in\mathcal{P}_{\tau^{\prime}}(\mathcal{Q})\colon\varepsilon(P,\delta,\mathcal{P}_{\tau^{\prime}}(\mathcal{Q}),M)=0\,.

(126)

Here, we are interested in convergence rates for the
\tau
-Fréchet mean with
\limsup_{x\to\infty}\tau^{\prime}(x)<\infty
and
\tau^{\prime\oplus}(x)>0
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
. This excludes the standard Huber loss but includes the pseudo-Huber loss.

Theorem 7.1
.

Assume
\limsup_{x\to\infty}\tau^{\prime}(x)<\infty
.
Assume
\tau^{\prime\oplus}(x)>0
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
.
Assume
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
exists with
\mathbf{E}[\overline{Ym}^{\gamma}]<\infty
.
Let
\chi:=\mathsf{med}(\overline{Ym})
.
Then there are
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
and

depending only on
\tau
,
\gamma
, and the distribution of

\mathbf{E}\!\left[\overline{mm_{n}}^{2}\min\!\left(\tau^{\prime\oplus}(2\chi),\tau^{\prime\oplus}(2\,\overline{mm_{n}})\right)\right]\leq Cn^{-1}

(132)

for all
n\geq n_{0}
.

Remark 7.2
.

We need a minimal polynomial moment condition in the form of
\mathbf{E}[\overline{Ym}^{\gamma}]
for an arbitrarily small
\gamma>0
. This is a weak moment condition, but it excludes distributions with
\mathbf{E}[\log(\overline{Ym}+1)]=\infty
.

Set the double excess risk as

V_{n}:=\mathbf{E}\!\left[\tau(\overline{Ym_{n}})-\tau(\overline{Ym})\right]+\mathbf{E}\!\left[\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\tau(\overline{Y_{i}m})-\tau(\overline{Y_{i}m_{n}})\right)\right]\,.

(133)

Proposition 7.3
.

Assume
\limsup_{x\to\infty}\tau^{\prime}(x)<\infty
.
Assume
\tau^{\prime\oplus}(x)>0
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
.
Assume
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
exists with
\mathbf{E}[\overline{Ym}^{\gamma}]<\infty
.
Then there are
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
and
C\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
depending only on
\tau
,
\gamma
, and the distribution of
Y
such that

V_{n}\leq Cn^{-1}

(134)

for all
n\geq n_{0}
.

Lemma 7.4
.

Assume
\limsup_{x\to\infty}\tau^{\prime}(x)<\infty
.
Assume
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
exists with
\mathbf{E}[\overline{Ym}^{\gamma}]<\infty
.
Assume
n_{0}\geq 8\gamma^{-1}+\max(1,\gamma^{-1})
. Then there is
C\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
depending only on
\tau
,
\gamma
, and the distribution of
Y
such that

\mathbf{E}\!\left[\overline{mm_{n}}^{2}\right]\leq C

(135)

for all
n\geq n_{0}
.

Proof.

By
Corollary

6.9
, there is
t_{0}
such that

\mathbf{P}\!\left(\overline{mm_{n}}>t\right)\leq\left(2\mathbf{P}\!\left(\overline{Ym}>\frac{t}{6}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}\right)^{n}\,.

(136)

for all
t\geq t_{0}
.
By Markov’s inequality, we have

\mathbf{P}\!\left(\overline{Ym}>\tilde{t}\right)=\mathbf{P}\!\left(\overline{Ym}^{\gamma}>\tilde{t}^{\gamma}\right)\leq\frac{\mathbf{E}[\overline{Ym}^{\gamma}]}{\tilde{t}^{\gamma}}\,.

(137)

Hence,

\mathbf{P}\!\left(\overline{mm_{n}}>t\right)\leq\left(\tilde{C}t^{-\frac{\gamma}{4}}\right)^{n}

(138)

with
\tilde{C}=2\cdot 6^{\gamma}\mathbf{E}[\overline{Ym}^{\gamma}]^{\frac{1}{4}}
.
Now, let
t_{1}:=\max(t_{0},\tilde{C}^{\frac{4}{\gamma}})
.
Then,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{E}\!\left[\overline{mm_{n}}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}t\mathbf{P}\!\left(\overline{mm_{n}}>t\right)\mathrm{d}t

(139)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t_{1}}t\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{t_{1}}^{\infty}t\left(\tilde{C}t^{-\frac{\gamma}{4}}\right)^{n}\mathrm{d}t\ =\ \frac{1}{2}t_{1}^{2}+\tilde{C}^{n}\int_{t_{1}}^{\infty}t^{1-\frac{\gamma n}{4}}\mathrm{d}t\,.

(140)

As we assume
n_{0}\geq 8\gamma^{-1}+\max(1,\gamma^{-1})
, we have
2-\frac{\gamma n}{4}<0
for
n\geq n_{0}
. Hence,

\displaystyle\int_{t_{1}}^{\infty}t^{1-\frac{\gamma n}{4}}\mathrm{d}t=\left[\frac{1}{2-\frac{\gamma n}{4}}t^{2-\frac{\gamma n}{4}}\right]_{t_{1}}^{\infty}=\frac{1}{\frac{\gamma n}{4}-2}t_{1}^{2-\frac{\gamma n}{4}}\,.

(141)

Together, we obtain

\mathbf{E}\!\left[\overline{mm_{n}}^{2}\right]\leq t_{1}^{2}+2\tilde{C}^{n}\frac{1}{\frac{\gamma n}{4}-2}t_{1}^{2-\frac{\gamma n}{4}}=t_{1}^{2}+\frac{8}{\gamma n-8}\left(\tilde{C}t_{1}^{-\frac{\gamma}{4}}\right)^{n}t_{1}^{2}\,.

(142)

As we chose

and with the condition on
n_{0}
, we have

\mathbf{E}\!\left[\overline{mm_{n}}^{2}\right]\leq\left(\frac{8}{\gamma n-8}+1\right)t_{1}^{2}\leq 9t_{1}^{2}\,.\qed

Lemma 7.5
.

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(\tau^{\prime\oplus}(\overline{Y_{j}m_{n}}+\overline{m_{n}m_{n}^{i}})+\tau^{\prime\oplus}(\overline{Y_{j}^{i}m_{n}^{i}}+\overline{m_{n}^{i}m_{n}})\right)\right)^{-1}

(156)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(\tau^{\prime\oplus}(\overline{Y_{j}m}+2\,\overline{mm_{n}}+\overline{mm_{n}^{i}})+\tau^{\prime\oplus}(\overline{Y_{j}^{i}m}+\overline{mm_{n}}+2\,\overline{mm_{n}^{i}})\right)\right)^{-1}\,.

(157)

Let
r\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
and
\eta\in(0,1)
. Define the events

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle:=\left\{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathds{1}_{[0,r]}(\overline{Y_{j}m})\geq\eta\right\}\,,

\displaystyle A^{i}

\displaystyle:=\left\{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathds{1}_{[0,r]}(\overline{Y_{j}^{i}m})\geq\eta\right\}\,,

(158)

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle:=\left\{\overline{mm_{n}}\leq r\right\}\,,

\displaystyle B^{i}

\displaystyle:=\left\{\overline{mm_{n}^{i}}\leq r\right\}

(159)

and

\Omega^{i}:=A\cap B\cap A^{i}\cap B^{i}\,.

(160)

On
\Omega^{i}
, we have

\tilde{H}_{i}^{-1}\leq\left(2\eta\tau^{\prime\oplus}(4r)\right)^{-1}\,.

(161)

We split
V_{n}
on
\Omega^{i}
as follows

V_{n}\leq\frac{D}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{E}\!\left[\overline{m_{n}m_{n}^{i}}\right]=\frac{D}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{E}\!\left[\overline{m_{n}m_{n}^{i}}\mathds{1}_{\Omega^{i}}\right]+\frac{D}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{E}\!\left[\overline{m_{n}m_{n}^{i}}\mathds{1}_{(\Omega^{i})^{\mathsf{c}}}\right]\,.

(162)

For the first term, we have already shown

\mathbf{E}\!\left[\overline{m_{n}m_{n}^{i}}\mathds{1}_{\Omega^{i}}\right]\leq\frac{4D}{n}\mathbf{E}\!\left[\tilde{H}_{i}^{-1}\mathds{1}_{\Omega^{i}}\right]\leq\frac{2D}{\eta\tau^{\prime\oplus}(4r)n}\,.

(163)

Hence,

\frac{D}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{E}\!\left[\overline{m_{n}m_{n}^{i}}\mathds{1}_{\Omega^{i}}\right]\leq\frac{2D^{2}}{\eta\tau^{\prime\oplus}(4r)n}\,.

(164)

For the second term, we use the triangle inequality and Cauchy-Schwarz and obtain

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\!\left[\overline{m_{n}m_{n}^{i}}\mathds{1}_{(\Omega^{i})^{\mathsf{c}}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbf{E}\!\left[\overline{mm_{n}}\mathds{1}_{(\Omega^{i})^{\mathsf{c}}}\right]+\mathbf{E}\!\left[\overline{mm_{n}^{i}}\mathds{1}_{(\Omega^{i})^{\mathsf{c}}}\right]

(165)

\displaystyle=2\mathbf{E}\!\left[\overline{mm_{n}}\mathds{1}_{(\Omega^{i})^{\mathsf{c}}}\right]

(166)

\displaystyle\leq 2\left(\mathbf{E}\!\left[\overline{mm_{n}}^{2}\right]\mathbf{P}\!\left((\Omega^{i})^{\mathsf{c}}\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,.

(167)

To finish the proof, we need to show that
\mathbf{E}\!\left[\overline{mm_{n}}^{2}\right]
can be bounded by a constant
\tilde{C}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
and the probability decreases exponentially in
n
, i.e.,
\mathbf{P}\!\left((\Omega^{i})^{\mathsf{c}}\right)\leq\exp(-cn)
with
c\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
.
This is proven in
Lemmas

7.4
and
7.5
.

Putting everything together, we get, for
r\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
large enough and a fixed
\eta\in(0,1)
(chosen to satisfy the conditions of
Lemmas

7.4
and
7.5
),

V_{n}\leq\frac{2D^{2}}{\eta\tau^{\prime\oplus}(4r)n}+2\left(\tilde{C}\exp(-cn)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,.

(168)

Thus, there is
C\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
such that

V_{n}\leq Cn^{-1}

(169)

for all
n\geq n_{0}
.
∎

9
Fast Rates

So far, we have used variance inequalities (VIs) that are of order
\overline{qm}^{2}
for points
q
close to the
\tau
-Fréchet mean
m
. This yields the classical parametric rate of convergence. Under certain conditions, the VI can exhibit steeper growth. For example, in the extreme case of

\mathbf{E}[\overline{Yq}^{\alpha}-\overline{Ym}^{\alpha}]=\overline{qm}^{\alpha}
for
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
. If a VI with steeper-than-squared growth holds for
q
close to
m
, we obtain rates of convergence faster than parametric. Let us illustrate this phenomenon here by an alternative version of

:

C\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
such that

\mathbf{E}\!\left[\min\!\left(\overline{mm_{n}}^{\beta},\overline{mm_{n}}^{\alpha}\right)\right]\leq Cn^{-1}

(175)

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

S3.6
, there is

such that

for all

.

2.9
, we have, for all
q\in\mathcal{Q}
,

(177)

For
q\in\mathcal{Q}
with
\overline{qm}\leq\epsilon
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\!\left[(\overline{Ym}+\overline{qm})^{\alpha-2}\right]

\displaystyle\geq\mathbf{E}\!\left[(\overline{Ym}+\overline{qm})^{\alpha-2}\mathds{1}_{[0,\overline{qm}]}(\overline{Ym})\right]

(178)

\displaystyle\geq(2\overline{qm})^{\alpha-2}\mathbf{P}\!\left(\overline{Ym}\leq\overline{qm}\right)

(179)

\displaystyle\geq 2^{\alpha-2}c\,\overline{qm}^{\alpha-2}\overline{qm}^{\beta-\alpha}\,.

(180)

Hence,

\mathbf{E}\!\left[\overline{Yq}^{\alpha}-\overline{Ym}^{\alpha}\right]\geq\alpha(\alpha-1)2^{\alpha-3}c\,\overline{qm}^{\beta}\,.

(181)

Let
\chi=\mathsf{med}(\overline{Ym})
.
Then, for all
q\in\mathcal{Q}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\!\left[(\overline{Ym}+\overline{qm})^{\alpha-2}\right]\geq\frac{1}{2}(\chi+\overline{qm})^{\alpha-2}\geq 2^{\alpha-3}\max(\chi,\overline{qm})^{\alpha-2}\,.

(182)

\mathbf{E}\!\left[\overline{Yq}^{\alpha}-\overline{Ym}^{\alpha}\right]\geq\alpha(\alpha-1)2^{\alpha-4}\min(\chi^{\alpha-2}\overline{qm}^{2},\overline{qm}^{\alpha})\,.

(183)

(184)

for all
q\in\mathcal{Q}
.
Applying this bound to (
176

\mathbf{E}\!\left[\min(\overline{mm_{n}}^{\beta},\overline{mm_{n}}^{\alpha})\right]\leq\tilde{C}c_{0}^{-1}n^{-1}\,.\qed

(185)

Remark 9.2
.

The result (
175
) implies

\overline{mm_{n}}\in\mathbf{O}_{\mathbf{P}}\!\left(n^{-\frac{1}{\beta}}\right)

(186)

which is faster than the parametric rate
\mathbf{O}_{\mathbf{P}}(n^{-1/2})
if
\beta<2
. If
\overline{Ym}
has a density, condition (
174
) implies that the density goes to
\infty
at
0
.

Similar results can be obtained for other transformations
\tau\in\mathcal{S}_{0}^{+}
. We do not extend this discussion further, as we believe that even these faster rates are not optimal for highly concentrated distributions: In the proof of
Proposition

3.1
, we use the VI not only when relating the excess risk to the risk on
\overline{mm_{n}}
, but also when obtaining the bound on
\overline{m_{n}m_{n}^{i}}

\overline{mm_{n}}
and uses the potentially suboptimal square VI for
\overline{m_{n}m_{n}^{i}}
. We conjecture that using the steep VI in both cases leads to even faster rates of order
\mathbf{O}_{\mathbf{P}}(n^{-\frac{1}{2(\beta-1)}})

S4
Chernoff Bound

For reference, we state different versions of the well-known Chernoff bound.

Proposition S4.1

(Multiplicative Chernoff Bound)
.

Let
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
be independent and identically distributed random variables with values in
\{0,1\}
. Set
p:=\mathbf{E}[X_{1}]
.
Then, for any
0<\delta\leq 1
,

\mathbf{P}\!\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}\leq(1-\delta)p\right)\leq\exp\!\left(-\frac{\delta^{2}pn}{2}\right)\,.

(S139)

Proposition S4.2

(Additive Chernoff Bound)
.

Let
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
be independent random variables with values in
\{0,1\}
.
Let
\rho:=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{E}\!\left[X_{i}\right]
.
Then, for all
t\in[0,1]
,

\displaystyle\text{if $t\geq\rho$}\colon\quad\mathbf{P}\!\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}\geq t\right)\leq\exp\!\left(-n\mathrm{K\!L}(t,\rho)\right)\,,

(S140)

\displaystyle\text{if $t\leq\rho$}\colon\quad\mathbf{P}\!\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}\leq t\right)\leq\exp\!\left(-n\mathrm{K\!L}(t,\rho)\right)\,,

(S141)

where

\mathrm{K\!L}(t,p)=(1-t)\log\!\left(\frac{1-t}{1-p}\right)+t\log\!\left(\frac{t}{p}\right)\,.

(S142)

Furthermore,

\exp\!\left(-n\mathrm{K\!L}(t,\rho)\right)\leq\left(2\min\!\left(\rho^{t},(1-\rho)^{1-t}\right)\right)^{n}\,.

(S143)

Proof.

The first part is the standard Chernoff bound. For the second part

\displaystyle\mathrm{K\!L}(t,\rho)

\displaystyle\geq(1-t)\log\!\left(1-t\right)+t\log\!\left(\frac{t}{\rho}\right)

(S144)

\displaystyle\geq-\log(2)-t\log\!\left(\rho\right)\,.

(S145)

Thus,

\exp\!\left(-n\mathrm{K\!L}(t,\rho)\right)\leq\exp\!\left(n\left(\log(2)+t\log(\rho)\right)\right)=\left(2\rho^{t}\right)^{n}\,.

(S146)

Finally, note
\mathrm{K\!L}(t,\rho)=\mathrm{K\!L}(1-t,1-\rho)
.
∎

.

Let

be independent events with the same probability

(S149)

Proof.

Direct consequence of
Proposition

S4.2

Proof of
Theorem

.

Let
q\in\mathcal{Q}
and
p\in\mathcal{B}
its projection onto
\mathcal{B}

\displaystyle\geq\rho\left(\tau\!\left(\sqrt{\overline{qp}^{2}+\delta^{2}}\right)+D(\overline{qp}-\delta)\right)-D\,\overline{qp}

(S163)

\displaystyle\geq D\left(\rho\left(\lambda\sqrt{\overline{qp}^{2}+\delta^{2}}+(\overline{qp}-\delta)\right)-\overline{qp}\right)

(S164)

\displaystyle=D\rho\left(\lambda\sqrt{\overline{qp}^{2}+\delta^{2}}-\frac{1-\rho}{\rho}\overline{qp}-\delta\right)\,.

(S165)

Let

. Define

(S166)

Thus,

(S167)

We have

\displaystyle f^{\prime}(x)=\lambda\frac{x}{\sqrt{x^{2}+\delta^{2}}}-a\,,\qquad\text{and}\qquad f^{\prime\prime}(x)=\lambda\frac{\delta^{2}}{(x^{2}+\delta^{2})^{\frac{3}{2}}}\,.

(S168)

We assume
\rho>\frac{1}{1+\lambda}
and thus
\lambda>a
.
If
\delta=0
,
f
is linear and
f(x)>0
for all
x>0=x_{0}
. If
\delta>0
, then
f
is strictly convex. If
x_{0}
is the largest
x\geq 0
with
f(x)=0
, then
f
is positive for all values larger than
x_{0}
. So, we calculate

\displaystyle\lambda\sqrt{x^{2}+\delta^{2}}-ax-\delta=0

(S169)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\quad

\displaystyle\lambda^{2}x^{2}+\lambda^{2}\delta^{2}=a^{2}x^{2}+\delta^{2}+2a\delta x

(S170)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\quad

\displaystyle(\lambda^{2}-a^{2})x^{2}-2a\delta x-(1-\lambda^{2})\delta^{2}=0

(S171)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\quad

\displaystyle x=\frac{2a\delta\pm\sqrt{(2a\delta)^{2}+4(\lambda^{2}-a^{2})(1-\lambda^{2})\delta^{2}}}{2(\lambda^{2}-a^{2})}\,.

(S172)

The larger root is

\displaystyle x_{0}=\frac{2a\delta+\sqrt{(2a\delta)^{2}+4(\lambda^{2}-a^{2})(1-\lambda^{2})\delta^{2}}}{2(\lambda^{2}-a^{2})}=\delta\frac{a+\lambda\sqrt{1-\lambda^{2}+a^{2}}}{\lambda^{2}-a^{2}}\,.

(S173)

Thus, for all

for all

.
Hence,

is not a
\tau
-Fréchet mean of

. In other words, for the projection

of

onto

(S175)

see

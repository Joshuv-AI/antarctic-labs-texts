Metal polyphenol CA-V nanozymes promote acute wound healing by destroying the ROS-inflammation cascade cycle
Authors: Liuqin Huang, Xinyu Zhao, Shuo Zhang, Yan Xu, Haibai Wei, Meng Zhang, Huimin Feng, Xianwen Wang, Yang Zheng, Xiaoliang Zheng
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Skin ulceration impairs barrier function, and high concentrations of reactive oxygen species (ROS) and inflammatory factors in the wound microenvironment significantly delay the healing process. The effects of current clinical treatment schemes are limited, and a new strategy with both economic benefits and remarkable curative effects is urgently needed. Therefore, we constructed a metal polyphenol nanozyme with multienzyme activity aimed at targeting the key vicious cycle of ROS-induced inflammation and accelerating the early healing of wounds. First, caffeic acid and NH4VO3 were synthesized via a simple hydrothermal method, and then, purified CA-V nanoparticles were obtained via dialysis and freeze-drying. In vitro experiments revealed that it has good SOD-like enzyme and CAT-like activities, can eliminate ROS/RNS, and can promote the migration of fibroblasts to complete epithelization. The results of the oxidative stress model and RNA sequencing revealed that it has excellent antioxidant and antiapoptotic abilities and obviously downregulates TNF-α and IL-17, which are typical inflammatory pathways. In vivo experiments and RNA sequencing in mice also verified that CA-V can slow inflammation, promote angiogenesis and collagen arrangement, and accelerate wound healing. Notably, CA-V has good biosafety in vitro and in vivo. The use of the ROS-inflammatory circulation system as a therapeutic target provides a promising therapeutic strategy for promoting early healing of acute wounds.

Introduction

An acute wound is a type of injury caused by normal skin tissue under the action of injury factors (such as external force/surgery/heat/current/chemicals/low temperature), which leads to destruction of the integrity of the skin [1,2]. As the largest barrier organ of the human body, once the skin is damaged, the original barrier function is limited [3]. In recent years, with the continuous in-depth study of skin tissue, it has been found that the pH of normal human skin is weakly acidic and can resist the invasion of bacteria in vitro [4]. However, after full-thickness skin injury, deep-tissue exposure gradually becomes neutral or weakly alkaline, which results in bacterial invasion. If wound healing does not occur in the early stage, the wound is likely to gradually develop into a chronic refractory wound [5,6].

Acute wound healing often involves four stages: the hemostatic stage, the inflammatory stage, the proliferation stage and the remodeling stage [7,8]. The inflammatory phase is the self-defense phase, in which neutrophils and macrophages destroy bacteria, remove debris, secrete growth factors and proteins, and attract immune system cells to the wound to promote repair [9]. Once the wound is cleared of foreign bodies, it enters the proliferation stage, and new granulation tissue fills the wound bed to form new blood vessels. The edge of the wound shrinks continuously to promote closure; that is, epithelial formation is completed to cover the wound [10,11]. However, owing to the low immunity or improper treatment of patients, some acute wounds are always in the microenvironment of the vicious cycle of ROS and inflammation, which makes it difficult for them to enter the proliferation stage and complete epithelization [12,13]. At present, the curative effect of clinical drugs for wound healing is limited, and some interventions (such as skin grafting and skin flap surgery) cause economic burdens and physical pain to patients [14]. Therefore, there is an urgent need to explore new repair drugs for precise treatment.

Nanozymes constitute a revolutionary concept in the fields of nanoscience and enzymology in recent years [[15], [16], [17]]. It refers to a type of nanomaterial that is not biological but can simulate the catalytic function of natural enzymes [18,19]. Since it was first reported in 2007, the field of nanozymes has developed rapidly, aiming at overcoming the inherent limitations of natural enzymes, such as high cost, poor stability, harsh storage conditions and difficulty in large-scale production [[20], [21], [22]]. Metal‒phenolic network-based nanozymes represent a new branch in this context [23]. Its core is a metal‒polyphenol network (MPN) structure formed by self-assembly of polyphenol compounds (such as tannic acid, gallic acid, epigallocatechin gallate, etc.) and metal ions (such as Fe3+, Cu2+, Mn2+, Zn2+, etc.) through coordination [24,25]. This structure not only endows materials with various enzyme activities but also combines the unique biological functions of polyphenols and metal ions, thus resulting in excellent synergistic effects and broad application prospects [26,27]. Caffeic acid (CA) is a natural polyphenol extracted from coffee [28]. The aromatic core, conjugated double bond and hydroxyl group in its structure endow it with excellent antioxidant properties [29]. Vanadium is an essential metal element in the human body. It has variable oxidation states (especially +4 and + 5 valences), and it has been reported that it can regulate the NF-κB signaling pathway and downregulate apoptosis [30,31]. If they can coordinate to form metal nanozymes, they will have superior antioxidant capacity.

Here, we propose a novel metallic polyphenol nanozyme (CA-V) aimed at destroying the vicious cycle of ROS and inflammation, thus accelerating wound healing. First, caffeic acid and NH4VO3 were synthesized via a simple hydrothermal method, and then, purified CA-V nanoparticles were obtained via freeze‒dried dialysis. In vitro experiments revealed that it has good SOD-like enzyme and CAT-like activities, can eliminate ROS/RNS, and can promote the migration of fibroblasts to complete epithelization. The results of the oxidative stress model and RNA sequencing revealed that it has excellent antioxidant and antiapoptotic abilities and obviously downregulates TNF-α and IL-17, which are typical inflammatory pathways. In vivo experiments and RNA sequencing in mice also verified that CA-V can slow inflammation, promote angiogenesis and collagen arrangement, and accelerate wound healing. Notably, CA-V has good biosafety in vitro and in vivo (Fig. 1). Results and discussion

Synthesis and characterization of CA-V nanozymes

On the basis of previous work, the synthesis method was further optimized [32]. CA and NH4VO3 are still used as reaction raw materials for one-step hydrothermal synthesis, but the molar amount of NH4VO3 should be sufficient to ensure the full reaction between CA and NH4VO3, and the remaining metal ions will be leached through the dialysis membrane, thus achieving the purification of CA-V (Fig. 2A). Similarly, when CA and NH4VO3 solutions are mixed, black substances are formed in the transparent solution, and after continuous stirring, the color gradually intensifies, indicating that new substances are produced. Then, the purified CA-V nanozyme was characterized via dialysis and freeze-drying, and the morphology and size of the CA-V nanozyme were observed. As shown by the TEM images, the CA-V nanozymes were uniform in size, had a uniform dispersion and had stable structures and properties (Fig. 2B). Consistent with the TEM results, DLS revealed that the particle size of the CA-V nanozyme was approximately 4.65 nm (Fig. 2C). The zeta potential of CA-V was measured several times, and the results revealed that the surface of CA-V was positively charged, with an absolute value of approximately 3.05 mV (Fig. 2D). To further verify the synthesis of the CA-V nanozyme, the CA-V nanozyme powder was analyzed via XPS. For example, the total spectrum results revealed that the CA-V nanozyme contained C, N, O, V and other elements, and the peak value was obvious (Fig. 2E). The O 1 s peaks at 526 eV–538 eV were analyzed in detail. The peaks at 529.8 eV, 531.1 eV and 532.9 eV correspond to O-V, O=C and O-C (Fig. S1), respectively. The V 2p peaks at 512 eV–528 eV were subsequently analyzed in detail. There are two main peaks in the V 2p spectrum: V 2p3/2 at 516.6 eV and V 2p1/2 at 523.69 eV. The main valence positions of the V element are +3 and + 4 (Fig. 2F). Compared with the FTIR spectra of CA, the spectra of CA-V show new characteristic peaks at 760 cm−1 and 815 cm−1, which may be caused by V-O symmetric tensile vibration and antisymmetric tensile vibration, further confirming V-OH binding (Fig. 2G). Evaluation of the antioxidant capacity and enzyme-like activity of CA-V

When the skin is damaged, the pH of the exposed deep tissue is neutral or weakly alkaline. At this time, if the wound does not intervene in time, it is very likely that the wound will become a natural Petri dish for bacteria [33,34]. To defend against bacterial invasion, the body quickly recruits immune cells such as neutrophils and macrophages to wounds. These cells produce large quantities of ROS through a process called the "respiratory burst" [35]. An appropriate amount of ROS is beneficial for bacterial clearance and epithelial repair. However, when excessive ROS are produced, the process lasts too long, the antioxidant defense system fails, and the high level of ROS changes from a repairer to a destroyer [36]. Therefore, the removal of ROS in a neutral environment is important for promoting early wound healing [37,38].

To explore whether CA-V nanozymes can scavenge ROS/RNS, ABTS and DPPH probes were used for detection in vitro. The enzyme-like activities were subsequently detected via a SOD enzyme kit and a CAT enzyme kit. DPPH radicals are stable organic radicals. Its ethanol solution is dark purple and has a maximum absorption peak at 517 nm. When antioxidants are mixed with DPPH, the hydrogen atoms or electrons neutralize the free radical properties of DPPH and convert it into a colorless, nonfree form. The color of the DPPH solution (that is, the decrease in absorbance) directly reflects the number of neutralized free radicals, thus indirectly reflecting the activity of antioxidants. The results revealed that the absorbance at 517 nm decreased with increasing CA-V nanozyme concentration. When the concentration of CA-V nanozyme was 25 μg/mL, the scavenging rate was 84.19 % (Fig. 2H–K). The detection principle of ABTS is similar to that of DPPH, but its absorption peak is at 734 nm. The results revealed that with increasing CA-V nanozyme concentration, the color of the ABTS solution gradually changed from blue‒green to transparent. When the concentration of CA-V nanozyme was 12 μg/mL, the scavenging rate was 87.20 % (Fig. 2I–L). Superoxide dismutase (SOD) is the first line of the antioxidant defense system in organisms, and it is one of the most critical lines of defense. It can convert toxic O2- to hydrogen peroxide (H2O2) and oxygen (O2). Moreover, catalase (CAT) catalyzes the decomposition of H2O2 into water and oxygen. Two kits were used to detect whether the CA-V nanozyme had enzyme-like activity, and the results showed that the CA-V nanozyme had excellent SOD-like and CAT-like enzyme activities. When the concentration of CA-V nanozyme was 40 μg/mL, the rate of superoxide anion scavenging reached 89.98 %. When the concentration of CA-V nanozyme was 100 μg/mL, the scavenging rate of H2O2 reached 82.66 %. These effects were concentration dependent (Fig. 2J–M). The above results indicated that the CA-V nanozyme had excellent antioxidant properties in vitro, which provides a theoretical basis for the application of ROS scavenging in vivo.

Evaluation of the biosafety and epithelial ability of CA-V

The ultimate goal of drugs is to treat diseases, and their therapeutic benefits must far outweigh the potential risks [39]. The antioxidant properties of CA-V nanozymes have been verified in vitro, and their biocompatibility must be evaluated before cell experiments and in vivo experiments. We detected the results via a cytotoxicity test (CCK8), a scratch test, mouse blood indices and HE staining of mouse organs.

Three types of wound repair-related cells (HUVECs, L929 cells and RAW264.7 cells) were used to detect cytotoxicity. The CCK-8 results revealed that even when the concentration of CA-V nanozyme was 120 μg/mL, the activity of the three cell lines was still greater than 80 % (Fig. 3A‒C). L929 is a mouse fibroblast line, and a scratch test can be used to simulate the core link of wound repair in vivo. We established three groups: the control group, CA group and CA-V group (the mass concentration of CA-V was 100 μg/mL, and the molar amounts of CA and CA-V were consistent). The results revealed that CA and CA-V not only did not inhibit wound healing but also promoted wound healing, but CA-V had a better effect than CA alone (Fig. 3D and E). Blood routine and common blood biochemical results revealed that there were no obvious abnormalities (Fig. S3–4) in the CA group or CA-V group. In addition, H&E staining of mouse organs revealed that the CA group and CA-V group had no obvious toxicity or side effects (Fig. 3F). These results indicated that CA-V showed good biocompatibility both in vitro and in vivo (2 weeks). Evaluation of the mechanism by which CA-V disrupts the vicious cycle of the ROS-inflammatory cascade in vitro

The vicious cycle of the ROS-inflammatory cascade has always been a major problem in wound healing [40]. In recent years, with increasing understanding of the wound healing mechanism, the vicious cycle of the ROS-inflammatory cascade has gradually gained increasing interest [41]. In the inflammatory phase of wound healing, ROS and inflammation are usually produced to participate in normal physiological reactions [42]. Owing to improper wound treatment or low immunity, excessive ROS are often produced to aggravate inflammation [43]. Continuous inflammation results in the production of more ROS, which promote each other, causing an increase in mitochondrial membrane permeability, the collapse of membrane potential and the dysfunction of organelles, which leads to apoptosis [44,45]. This breaks the normal stage transition of the healing process and eventually leads to tissue destruction and stagnation of healing. By establishing an oxidative stress model in HUVECs, we tested whether CA-V can slow oxidative stress and reduce the degree of apoptosis. These methods include ROS/RNS fluorescence confocal staining, JC-1 fluorescence confocal staining, AM/PI fluorescence confocal staining and the Annexin V-FITC/PI apoptosis flow assay.

The results showed that the fluorescence intensity of the ROS/RNS in the H2O2 group was very obvious, which indicated that the establishment of the oxidative stress model was very successful. With the addition of CA and CA-V, the green fluorescence intensity of ROS/RNS decreased gradually, which indicated that the ROS/RNS produced by H2O2-induced cells was gradually eliminated and that CA-V had the greatest effect (Fig. 4A–B, Figure S5-6). In normal cells, JC-1 dye is inhaled into the mitochondrial matrix, and high concentrations of JC-1 form aggregates, which emit mainly red fluorescence. When excessive ROS damage mitochondria, membrane permeability changes, the potential decreases, the concentration of JC-1 decreases, and JC-1 exists in the form of a monomer, which results in green fluorescence. In the H2O2 group, green fluorescence staining of the cells indicated that the mitochondrial membrane potential was not balanced. Under these conditions, mitochondrial dysfunction occurs in cells, and with the intervention of inflammatory factors, cells gradually undergo apoptosis and necrosis. With the addition of CA and CA-V, the fluorescence of the cells gradually returned to normal green, which indicated that CA and CA-V could maintain the mitochondrial membrane potential and prevent apoptosis, and the CA-V group presented better therapeutic effects than the CA group did (Fig. 4C and D). To further quantify the ratio of CA-V-mediated reduction in apoptosis, we used an Annexin V-FITC/PI flow assay. The apoptosis rate was 81.7 % in the H2O2 group, 46.9 % in the CA group and 18.98 % in the CA-V group. To further explore the mechanism by which CA-V slows oxidative stress, we performed RNA transcriptome sequencing. The overall distribution difference in the DEG cluster thermogram color indicated that after CA-V treatment, there was an obvious difference in gene expression between the treatment group and the model group (Fig. 5A). A volcano map revealed that after CA-V treatment, compared with the H2O2 group, the treatment group presented the upregulation of 2693 genes and the downregulation of 1202 genes (Fig. 5B). GO enrichment analysis revealed that these DEGs were associated mainly with the regulation of the inflammatory response, the inflammatory response and cell proliferation (Fig. 5C). KEGG enrichment analysis revealed that after CA-V treatment, the DEGs were enriched mainly in the IL-17 signaling pathway and the TNF-α signaling pathway (Fig. 5D). The IL-17 signaling pathway and the TNF-α signaling pathway are both typical proinflammatory signals that are beneficial in the early stage but harmful in persistent activation. Continuously high levels of IL-17 lead to excessive and chronic inflammation. Many recruited neutrophils release proteases (such as MMP-8 and MMP-9) and ROS, degrade the extracellular matrix (ECM), destroy new tissues, and hinder granulation tissue formation and reepithelization [46,47]. High concentrations of TNF-α strongly inhibit the proliferation of fibroblasts and keratinocytes, induce their apoptosis, and strongly disrupt the process of granulation tissue formation and reepithelization [48]. Downregulation of the IL-17 signaling pathway and TNF-α signaling pathway is beneficial for wound healing. In addition, GSEA revealed that the P53 signaling pathway, HIPPO signaling pathway and TGF-β signaling pathway, which are key pathways that promote apoptosis, were downregulated after CA-V treatment [[49], [50], [51]]. The downregulation of these signaling pathways further verified the antiapoptotic ability of CA-V (Fig. 5E-G). The above experimental results indicate that CA-V can effectively destroy the vicious cycle of the ROS-inflammatory cascade in in vitro cell experiments and has excellent antiapoptotic ability. Evaluation and mechanism of CA-V in promoting wound healing in vivo

The genetic background of the BALB/c mice is highly consistent, which greatly reduces the individual differences in the experiment. Coupled with a complete immune system, the wound healing model is a very mature, classic and widely used animal model for evaluating wound healing ability. To eliminate differences in sex hormone factors, female mice were selected and randomly divided into control, CA and CA-V groups for follow-up experiments. We evaluated healing according to the wound size, healing rate, and H&E and Masson staining results of the skin samples and analyzed inflammatory factors and angiogenesis via immunohistochemistry.

By observing and calculating the wound healing rate on the 2nd, 4th, 6th and 8th days, the results revealed that the wound size of the control group was still greater on the 8th day, whereas the wound sizes of the CA and CA-V groups were significantly smaller than those of the control group. The healing rate of the control group was 63.72 % on the 8th day, while the healing rate of the CA-V group was the highest, at 91.23 % (Fig. 6A and B). Moreover, to avoid individual differences, the body weight changes of the mice during treatment were recorded, and the results revealed significant differences among the three groups (Fig. 6C). On the eighth day, samples were collected from the skin wounds. H&E staining revealed that the wound beds in the control group were characterized by a lack of abundant new connective tissue (granulation tissue), resulting in wound depression and the infiltration of numerous diffuse inflammatory cells. However, in the CA-V group, granulation tissue full of fibroblasts and blood vessels formed, the epithelization was complete, the epidermis was covered, and no infiltration of inflammatory cells was detected (Fig. 6D). Masson staining revealed that the collagen fibers in the control group were short, slender, disorganized, reticular or fragmented, lacked directionality and were accompanied by the aggregation of inflammatory cells. To further explore the mechanism by which CA-V promotes wound healing in vivo, we detected the levels of inflammatory factors and vascular markers in wound samples via immunohistochemistry. After CA-V treatment, the levels of IL-6 and TNF-α were 39.6 % and 25.8 % of those in the control group, respectively. Compared with the control, CA-V significantly decreased the levels of inflammatory cytokines (Fig. 6F–G, I-J). Moreover, after CA-V treatment, the expression of CD31 was 5.27 times greater than that in the control group. It markedly upregulated the expression of vascular markers and promoted angiogenesis (Fig. 6H and K). The above results show that CA-V can promote ROS clearance, attenuate inflammation, promote angiogenesis and collagen arrangement in mice, and accelerate wound healing.

To further explore the molecular mechanism by which CA-V promotes wound healing in vivo, we selected skin samples from the control group and CA-V group for eukaryotic transcriptome RNA sequencing. The overall color distribution difference in the DEG cluster thermogram indicated that after CA-V treatment, the gene differences were mainly concentrated in the downregulated genes (Fig. 7A). A volcano map revealed that after CA-V treatment, 1034 genes were significantly downregulated, whereas only 69 genes were upregulated (Fig. 7B). GO enrichment analysis revealed that these DEGs were associated mainly with the activity of RNA polymerase II. When the body is injured, RNA polymerase II is activated rapidly, which drives the transcription of related genes and results in the production of many proinflammatory cytokines (such as TNF-α, IL-1β, and IL-6) and chemokines [52]. If the level of RNA polymerase II is low, the inflammatory reaction fails to start, which easily leads to infection. In contrast, if the level of RNA polymerase II is too high, it can lead to chronic inflammation. Therefore, the regulation of RNA polymerase II is crucial (Fig. 7C) [53]. KEGG enrichment analysis revealed that after CA-V treatment, the downregulated DEGs were enriched mainly in the TGF-β signaling pathway and the phosphatidylinositol 3-kinase-protein kinase B (PI3K-AKT) signaling pathway (Fig. 7D). These pathways are classical pathways related to apoptosis and inflammation [54,55]. In addition, GSEA revealed that after CA-V treatment, the inflammatory body signaling pathway was significantly downregulated (Fig. 7E). The oxidative phosphorylation signaling pathway is obviously upregulated, which provides sufficient energy for cell function and promotes the completion of skin epithelization. The upregulation of this pathway in the construction of the skin epithelial structure also verified this result (Fig. 7F and G). Conclusion

In this study, we successfully constructed a novel metallic polyphenol nanozyme (CA-V) aimed at destroying the vicious cycle of ROS and inflammation, thus accelerating wound healing. CA-V nanoparticles synthesized via the hydrothermal method have stable structures and uniform sizes. CA-V has good SOD-like and CAT-like activities, can eliminate ROS/RNS, and can promote the migration of fibroblasts to complete epithelialization. A cellular oxidative stress model and RNA sequencing results revealed that CA-V has excellent antioxidant and antiapoptotic effects and obviously downregulates TNF-α and IL-17, which are typical inflammatory pathways. In vivo experiments and RNA sequencing in mice also verified that CA-V can slow inflammation, promote angiogenesis and collagen arrangement, and accelerate wound healing. Notably, CA-V has good biosafety in vitro and in vivo. The use of the ROS-inflammatory circulation system as a therapeutic target provides a promising therapeutic strategy for promoting early healing of acute wounds.

Experimental methods

Synthesis and characterization of CA-V nanozymes: CA and NH4VO3 were synthesized via a one-step hydrothermal method, and the synthesis method was further optimized on the basis of previous work. The molar amount of NH4VO3 should be excessive enough to ensure the full reaction between CA and NH4VO3, and the remaining metal ions will be leached through the dialysis membrane, thus achieving the purification of CA-V. CA-V was characterized by TEM, FTIR, XPS, XRD, zeta potential and hydrated particle size.

Antioxidant Properties and Enzyme-Like Activity of the CA-V Nanozyme: The ability to scavenge ROS/RNS was first detected via ABTS and DPPH in vitro probe tests, and then, the enzyme-like activity was detected via a SOD enzyme kit and a CAT enzyme kit.

Cell Culture: All the cells were cultured in an incubator containing 5 % CO2 at 37 °C. HUVECs and L929 cells were cultured in DMEM (supplemented with 10 % fetal bovine serum, 100 U/mL penicillin and 100 U/mL streptomycin). RAW 264.7 cells were cultured in macrophage-specific medium. Passaging was carried out according to the cell state.

Establishment of an oxidative stress model in HUVECs: According to the literature and previous experimental results, we used 500 μM H2O2 to induce oxidative stress for 12 h. The same molar amount of CA or CA-V solution (the mass concentration of CA-V was 100 μg/mL) was added according to the different groups. The therapeutic effect of CA-V was verified via ROS/RNS fluorescence confocal staining, JC-1 fluorescence confocal staining, AM/PI fluorescence confocal staining and an Annexin V-FITC/PI apoptosis flow assay.

Establishment of an acute wound model in mice: All animal experiments were approved by the Ethics Committee of Anhui Medical University (no. LLSC20250047). Fifteen 6-week-old male BALB/c mice were randomly divided into three groups, with 5 mice in each group, namely, the control group and the CA and CA-V treatment groups. The back incision was approximately 0.8 cm in diameter, and each group received drug treatment. A uniform administration volume of 200 μL was applied to all the treatment groups. CA-V was administered at a concentration of 500 μg/mL, with the CA control group receiving an equivalent molar amount. The solutions were delivered directly onto the wound surface via a pipette and allowed to absorb fully, as indicated by their dry appearance, prior to being returned to their cages. On the 2nd, 4th, 6th and 8th days, the wound size was observed, and the healing rate was calculated. On the 8th day, the skin samples were collected, healing was evaluated via H&E and Masson staining, and inflammatory factors and angiogenesis were analyzed via immunohistochemistry.

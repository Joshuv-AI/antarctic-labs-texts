Accretion of a Vlasov gas by a Kerr black hole
Patryk Mach, Mehrab Momennia, Olivier Sarbach

Abstract
We investigate the accretion of a collisionless, relativistic kinetic gas by a rotating Kerr black hole, assuming that at infinity the state of the gas is described by a distribution function depending only on the energy of the particles. Neglecting the self-gravity of the gas, we show that relevant physical observables, including the particle current density and the accretion rates associated with the mass, the energy, and the angular momentum, can be expressed in the form of closed integrals that can be evaluated numerically or approximated analytically in the slow-rotation limit. The accretion rates are computed in this manner for both monoenergetic particles and the Maxwell-Jüttner distribution and compared with the corresponding results in the non-rotating case. Whereas it is shown that the angular momentum accretion rate vanishes exactly, it is found that the rotation of the black hole has a small but non-vanishing effect on the mass and the energy accretion rates, which is remarkably well described by an analytic calculation in the slow-rotation approximation to quadratic order in the rotation parameter. The effects of rotation on the morphology of the accretion flow are also analyzed.



I
Introduction

The problem of relativistic Bondi-type accretion of matter on a rotating (Kerr) black hole has a long history, dating back to about 40 years ago. In 1988 Petrich, Shapiro, and Teukolsky obtained an analytic solution in the hydrodynamical regime, assuming a zero-vorticity potential flow and the ultra-hard equation of state
lPsSsT1988
. It was derived by exploiting a correspondence between potential flows with the ultra-hard equation of state and solutions of the massless Klein-Gordon equation, which is known to be separable on the Kerr background spacetime. The solution obtained in
lPsSsT1988
is valid for moving black holes, and it naturally reduces to the standard Bondi problem if the black hole velocity is set to zero. In this case, it has a particular property: in Boyer-Lindquist coordinates, both polar and covariant azimuthal components of the four-velocity vanish. Unfortunately, generalizations of this solution to other (more physically relevant) equations of state have only been achieved at the approximate level
vByP95
;
vP96
. Another approximate solution was derived in
eTpTjM13
, based on the ballistic approximation. For a systematic numerical study of the spherical Bondi-type accretion of an ideal fluid into a Kerr black hole, see
aAeToSdL21
.

In this paper we present an exact stationary solution representing the relativistic Bondi-type accretion of the collisionless (Vlasov) gas in a rotating subextremal Kerr black hole background. By “Bondi-type” accretion we mean an idealized, asymptotically spherical stationary model, in which the accretion flow is sustained by a homogeneous reservoir of gas at infinity.

Within the Newtonian approximation, the accretion of a Vlasov gas was investigated by Novikov and Zel’dovich
ZelNovik-Book
. First steps towards generalization to the relativistic setting were undertaken by Shapiro and Teukolsky
Shapiro-Book
(see also Appendix A in Ref.
sSsT85a
), who computed the accretion rate of a Vlasov gas of monoenergetic particles spherically infalling onto a Schwarzschild black hole. A modern analysis of spherically symmetric steady relativistic accretion of the collisionless Vlasov gas onto a Schwarzschild black hole was presented in
pRoS16
;
pRoS17
by Rioseco and Sarbach. Their construction was later repeated for the Reissner-Nordström spacetime in
aCpM20
(see also
pLjYsX25
), with the aim of mimicking the effects associated with the black hole spin (replaced by the electric charge) in a spherically symmetric spacetime. Stationary spherical accretion flows of the Vlasov gas in various exotic black hole spacetimes were studied in
BardeenBH
;
SchwLikeBH
;
KalbRamondBH
. A generalization of spherical accretion models to asymptotically flat and generic spherically symmetric black hole spacetimes was recently shown in
mMoS25
. For a generalization of the Bondi-type model on the Schwarzschild spacetime, where the boundary conditions are specified at a sphere of finite radius instead of infinity, see
aGetal21
.

At the same time, non-spherical flows in the Schwarzschild spacetime were investigated within the same framework in
cGoS22a
;
cGoS22c
;
sS23
(axially symmetric disks), or
pMoA21a
;
pMoA21b
;
pMaO22b
;
aCpMaO24
(accretion onto moving black holes). A Bondi-type model confined to the equatorial plane of the Kerr spacetime was derived in
aCpMaO22
and
gKpM25
. Analytic kinetic models describing the relaxation (due to phase-space mixing) of macroscopic variables to a stationary state around a Kerr black hole have been described in
pRoS18
;
pRoS24
. For self-gravitating shells or disks made of Vlasov matter around black holes, see
gR93
;
fJ21
;
hA21
;
fJ22
.

A preliminary analysis of the Bondi-type accretion model of the Vlasov gas in the Kerr spacetime (without restrictions to the equatorial plane) was presented in
pLyLxZ23
. Unfortunately, this paper does not discuss the corresponding phase-space limits, nor does it make an attempt to compute total accretion rates.

Bondi-type models are specified by asymptotic conditions. For simplicity, in this paper we focus on a simple gas
wI63
, that is a gas consisting of spinless and uncharged classical point particles of the same mass and consider asymptotic distributions depending only on the energy of the gas particles. In particular examples, we concentrate on monoenergetic particles and the Maxwell-Jüttner distribution. In all cases, we only take into account unbound particle trajectories (extending to infinity).
Particles moving along bound trajectories can still be included in our model, but since we neglect the collisions between the particles of the gas, as well as its self-gravity, they would not affect results concerning unbound trajectories.

We show that the particle number and energy accretion rates decrease with the increasing black hole spin parameter. This stays in agreement with previous results, in particular the model of accretion onto Reissner-Nordström black holes
aCpM20
;
pLjYsX25
and the planar model discussed in
aCpMaO22
. This is also a general feature present in hydrodynamical models, e.g., in
aAeToSdL21
. In contrast to the results of
aCpMaO22
, both accretion rates decrease with increasing asymptotic temperature of the gas. This fact emphasizes the difference between planar and non-planar models, mostly due to their asymptotic properties. Perhaps more surprisingly, we show that the total angular momentum accretion rate vanishes. This stays in agreement with the hydrodynamical model of
lPsSsT1988
, but not with the properties of the planar model
aCpMaO22
.

Our solution is exact in the sense that the relevant observable quantities (such as the components of the particle current density or the components of the energy-momentum tensor) can be expressed in the form of closed integrals. These integrals are then evaluated numerically. As an example, we compute the components of the particle current density, illustrating the morphology of the flow in the vicinity of the black hole.

II.3
Absorbed and scattered trajectories

From the previous discussions, we arrive at the following conclusions, which are important for the remainder of this paper. Future-directed timelike trajectories emanating from infinity are characterized by

Since

, the specification of

. Furthermore, for fixed values of

and

The main challenge of the analysis presented in this paper consists in computing the relevant spacetime observables: the particle current density and the energy-momentum-stress tensor, defined as

n_{\infty}=4\pi m^{3}A\varepsilon_{0}\sqrt{\varepsilon_{0}^{2}-1},

(66)

for the monoenergetic model with
F_{\infty}
given by Eq. (
61
) and

n_{\infty}=4\pi m^{3}A\frac{K_{2}(z)}{z},

(67)

for
F_{\infty}
defined by Eq. (
62
), where
K_{2}
denotes the modified Bessel function of the second kind (see, for instance, Ref.
DLMF
).

The asymptotic energy density is defined as

\varepsilon_{\infty}:=\lim\limits_{r\to\infty}T_{\mu\nu}k^{\mu}k^{\nu}=\lim\limits_{r\to\infty}T_{tt}=4\pi\int\limits_{m}^{\infty}dEE^{2}\sqrt{E^{2}-m^{2}}F_{\infty}(E),

(68)

which leads to

\varepsilon_{\infty}=4\pi m^{4}A\varepsilon_{0}^{2}\sqrt{\varepsilon_{0}^{2}-1},

(69)

for the monoenergetic model and

\varepsilon_{\infty}=4\pi m^{4}A\left[\frac{K_{1}(z)}{z}+3\frac{K_{2}(z)}{z^{2}}\right],

(70)

in the Maxwell-Jüttner case.

IV
Accretion rates

In this section we discuss the accretion rates for our model. For this, we recall that the particle flux through a three-dimensional timelike surface
S
with unit normal vector field
s^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}
is given by

\mathcal{F}[S]=\int\limits_{S}J_{\mu}s^{\mu}\eta_{S},

(71)

with
\eta_{S}
the induced volume form on
S
. Likewise, the energy and angular momentum fluxes through
S
are defined by replacing
J_{\mu}
with
J^{(k)}_{\mu}:=-T_{\mu\nu}k^{\nu}=-T_{\mu t}
and
J^{(\eta)}_{\mu}:=T_{\mu\nu}\eta^{\nu}=T_{\mu\varphi}
, respectively, where
k
and
\eta
refer to the Killing vector fields defined in Eq. (
4
). In order to compute the accretion rate, we consider
S
to be a constant
r
-surface enclosed between two spatial hypersurfaces
t=t_{1}
and
t=t_{2}
, with
t_{2}>t_{1}
. The unit normal in the outward radial direction is

s^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}=Ng^{\mu\nu}(\nabla_{\nu}r)\partial_{\mu}=\frac{N}{\rho^{2}}\left[\Delta\frac{\partial}{\partial r}+2Mr\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+a\frac{\partial}{\partial\varphi}\right]=\frac{N\Delta}{\rho^{2}}\frac{\partial}{\partial r_{\mathrm{BL}}},

(72)

with
N=\rho/\sqrt{\Delta}
a normalization constant. Furthermore, the induced volume element on
S
is found to be

\eta_{S}=\rho\sqrt{\Delta}\sin\vartheta dtd\vartheta d\varphi,

(73)

such that

J_{\mu}s^{\mu}\eta_{S}=\left(\Delta J_{r}+2MrJ_{t}+aJ_{\varphi}\right)dt(\sin\vartheta d\vartheta)d\varphi=\rho^{2}J^{r}dt(\sin\vartheta d\vartheta)d\varphi.

(74)

Using Eqs. (
45c
) and (
45d
) one finds that the contribution of the scattered particles cancel, as expected, and hence

\rho^{2}J^{r}=-\int_{m}^{\infty}dEF_{\infty}(E)\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}d\chi\int\limits_{0}^{Q_{c}}dQQ.

(75)

Inserting into Eq. (
71
) and dividing by
t_{2}-t_{1}
, one finds that the particle accretion rate through the surface
S
is

\mathcal{\dot{N}}=-\pi\int\limits_{m}^{\infty}dEF_{\infty}(E)\int\limits_{0}^{\pi}d\vartheta\sin\vartheta\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}d\chi(Q_{c})^{2}.

(76)

The energy and angular momentum accretion rates can be obtained in the same fashion by inserting the factors
-p_{t}=E
and
p_{\varphi}=Q\cos\chi
under the integral (
75
), which yields

\mathcal{\dot{E}}=-\pi\int\limits_{m}^{\infty}dEEF_{\infty}(E)\int\limits_{0}^{\pi}d\vartheta\sin\vartheta\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}d\chi(Q_{c})^{2},

(77)

and

\mathcal{\dot{J}}=-\frac{2\pi}{3}\int\limits_{m}^{\infty}dEF_{\infty}(E)\int\limits_{0}^{\pi}d\vartheta\sin\vartheta\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}d\chi\cos\chi(Q_{c})^{3}=0,

(78)

where the vanishing of
\mathcal{\dot{J}}
follows directly from the fact that
Q_{c}
is a smooth function of
\sin\chi
, such that the integral over
\chi
in Eq. (
78
) vanishes. Physical implications of this result will be further elaborated in the conclusions. Note that
\mathcal{\dot{N}}
and
\mathcal{\dot{E}}
are independent of
r
. In fact, they are independent of any continuous deformation of the surface
S
, as a consequence of the continuity equations for
J_{\mu}
and
J^{(k)}_{\mu}
and the fact that the gas distribution is stationary.

In the Schwarzschild limit
a=0
,
Q_{c}=L_{c}(E)
is independent of
\chi
and
\vartheta
and the above expressions for the accretion rates further simplify to

\left.\mathcal{\dot{N}}\right|_{a=0}=-4\pi^{2}\int\limits_{m}^{\infty}dEF_{\infty}(E)L_{c}(E)^{2},\qquad\left.\mathcal{\dot{E}}\right|_{a=0}=-4\pi^{2}\int\limits_{m}^{\infty}dEEF_{\infty}(E)L_{c}(E)^{2},

(79)

which is consistent with Eqs. (76) and (77) in Ref.
pRoS16
.

Combining Eqs. (
76
) and (
77
) with the asymptotic expressions in Section
III.5
one obtains

\frac{\dot{\mathcal{N}}}{n_{\infty}}=\frac{\dot{\mathcal{E}}}{\varepsilon_{\infty}}=-\frac{M^{2}}{4\varepsilon_{0}\sqrt{\varepsilon_{0}^{2}-1}}\int\limits_{0}^{\pi}d\vartheta\sin\vartheta\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}d\chi q_{c}(\alpha,\vartheta,\sin\chi,\varepsilon_{0})^{2},

(80)

for the monoenergetic model and

\displaystyle\frac{\dot{\mathcal{N}}}{n_{\infty}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{M^{2}z}{4K_{2}(z)}\int\limits_{1}^{\infty}d\varepsilon\exp(-z\varepsilon)\int\limits_{0}^{\pi}d\vartheta\sin\vartheta\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}d\chi q_{c}(\alpha,\vartheta,\sin\chi,\varepsilon)^{2},

(81a)

\displaystyle\frac{\dot{\mathcal{E}}}{\varepsilon_{\infty}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{M^{2}z}{4\left[K_{1}(z)+3K_{2}(z)/z\right]}\int\limits_{1}^{\infty}d\varepsilon\varepsilon\exp(-z\varepsilon)\int\limits_{0}^{\pi}d\vartheta\sin\vartheta\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}d\chi q_{c}(\alpha,\vartheta,\sin\chi,\varepsilon)^{2},

(81b)

for the Maxwell-Jüttner model, where
Q_{c}=Mmq_{c}
. It is also useful to consider the low and high-temperature limits of the Maxwell-Jüttner model. Using the asymptotic behavior
K_{\nu}(z)\sim\sqrt{\pi/(2z)}e^{-z}
, performing the variable substitution
\varepsilon\mapsto\xi
with
\varepsilon=1+\xi/z
, and taking the limit
z\to\infty
one finds in the low temperature limit

\lim\limits_{z\to\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi z}}\frac{\dot{\mathcal{N}}}{n_{\infty}}=\lim\limits_{z\to\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi z}}\frac{\dot{\mathcal{E}}}{\varepsilon_{\infty}}=-\frac{M^{2}}{4\pi}\int\limits_{0}^{\pi}d\vartheta\sin\vartheta\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}d\chi q_{c}(\alpha,\vartheta,\sin\chi,1)^{2}.

(82)

In the high-temperature limit one uses the substitution
\varepsilon\mapsto\eta:=z\varepsilon
and
K_{\nu}(z)\sim\Gamma(\nu)(2/z)^{\nu}
to obtain

\lim\limits_{z\to 0}\frac{\dot{\mathcal{N}}}{n_{\infty}}=\lim\limits_{z\to 0}\frac{\dot{\mathcal{E}}}{\varepsilon_{\infty}}=-\frac{M^{2}}{4}\int\limits_{0}^{\pi}d\vartheta\sin\vartheta\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}d\chi\zeta_{c}(\alpha,\vartheta,\sin\chi)^{2},

(83)

where

\zeta_{c}(\alpha,\vartheta,\sin\chi):=\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to\infty}\frac{1}{\varepsilon}q_{c}(\alpha,\vartheta,\sin\chi,\varepsilon),

(84)

can be shown to be finite, see Appendix
B
. This relation reduces to
\zeta_{c}=3\sqrt{3}
for the standard Schwarzschild black hole (see Eq. (33) of
mMoS25
and the related discussion).

V
Slow-rotation approximation

Since
Q_{c}=Q_{c}(a,\vartheta,\chi,E)
is only given in parametric form (see Eq. (
129
)), the integrals with respect to
\chi
present in Eqs. (
45
) cannot be computed analytically and one has to resort to numerical methods. Although we shall compute these integrals numerically in the upcoming section, here we circumvent this problem by considering a slowly rotating Kerr black hole and performing an expansion in the rotation parameter, up to quadratic order. The slow-rotation approximation is an important astrophysically motivated case
jFlM2019
, and it provides an effective strategy to overcome difficulties arising in the Kerr background spacetime due to the frame-dragging effect
pPeBlG2013
. In principle, the analytic formulas presented in this section can be extended to higher order of the black hole spin parameter, to derive relations valid for more rapidly rotating black holes. However, as we will see, the quadratic approximation already leads to an accurate description of the accretion process.

Up to the second order in
\alpha
, the critical value
Q_{c}
can be expressed explicitly in terms of
E
,
\chi
, and
\vartheta
in the following way. First, we expand the critical radius

x_{c}=x_{c}^{(0)}+\alpha x_{c}^{(1)}+\alpha^{2}x_{c}^{(2)}+\mathcal{O}(\alpha^{3}),

(85)

where the zeroth order term
x_{c}^{(0)}
is known from the Schwarzschild case, see Eq. (
22
):

x_{c}^{(0)}=\frac{8}{\varepsilon\sqrt{9\varepsilon^{2}-8}+4-3\varepsilon^{2}},\qquad\varepsilon=\frac{E}{m},

(86)

which is a function of energy only. To find the correction terms
x_{c}^{(1)}
and
x_{c}^{(2)}
, we substitute the ansatz (
85
) into the expression for
E_{c}
given in Eq. (
129
) and then solve the equation
E_{c}=E
order by order in
\alpha
. After some manipulations, this yields

\displaystyle x_{c}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2\sqrt{x_{c}^{(0)}}}{x_{c}^{(0)}-6}\sin\vartheta\sin\chi,

(87a)

\displaystyle x_{c}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{4\cos^{2}\vartheta+2x_{c}^{(0)}\sin^{2}\vartheta}{x_{c}^{(0)}\left(x_{c}^{(0)}-6\right)}-3\frac{\left(x_{c}^{(0)}\right)^{2}-9x_{c}^{(0)}+22}{\left(x_{c}^{(0)}-6\right)^{3}}\sin^{2}\vartheta\sin^{2}\chi,

(87b)

and in principle this procedure can be followed to compute higher correction terms in
\alpha
. This provides
x_{c}
as a function of
\alpha
,
E
,
\vartheta
, and
\chi
as an expansion in
\alpha
to the desired order.

Next, we substitute this function into the expression for
Q_{c}
given in Eq. (
129
) and expand the result in powers of
\alpha
. In terms of the dimensionless quantities
q_{c}=Q_{c}/(Mm)
this yields, up to the second order,

q_{c}=q_{c}^{(0)}+\alpha q_{c}^{(1)}+\alpha^{2}q_{c}^{(2)}+\mathcal{O}(\alpha^{3}),

(88)

where

\displaystyle q_{c}^{(0)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{x_{c}^{(0)}}{\sqrt{x_{c}^{(0)}-3}},

(89a)

\displaystyle q_{c}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{q_{c}^{(0)}}{\sqrt{x_{c}^{(0)}}}\sin\vartheta\sin\chi,

(89b)

\displaystyle q_{c}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{q_{c}^{(0)}}{2x_{c}^{(0)}}\left[-\frac{\cos^{2}\vartheta+x_{c}^{(0)}\sin^{2}\vartheta}{x_{c}^{(0)}}+\frac{\left(x_{c}^{(0)}-5\right)\sin^{2}\vartheta\sin^{2}\chi}{x_{c}^{(0)}-6}\right].

(89c)

Note that the zeroth order term again agrees with the value known from the Schwarzschild case, see Eq. (
22
). Together with Eq. (
86
), Eqs. (
88
) and (
89
) provide the desired expansion for
Q_{c}
as a function of
E
,
\chi
, and
\vartheta
.

From these results one easily obtains

\int_{0}^{\pi}d\vartheta\sin\vartheta\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\chi q_{c}^{2}=4\pi\frac{\left(x_{c}^{(0)}\right)^{2}}{x_{c}^{(0)}-3}\left[1-\frac{2\alpha^{2}}{\left(x_{c}^{(0)}\right)^{2}\left(6-x_{c}^{(0)}\right)}+\mathcal{O}(\alpha^{3})\right],

(90)

from which the accretion rates can be computed by means of Eqs. (
80
) and (
81
) for the monoenergetic and the Maxwell-Jüttner case, respectively. In particular, for the Maxwell-Jüttner model, using formulas (
82
) and (
83
), one obtains the particle and the energy accretion rates in the slow-rotation approximation for both the low (
z\to\infty
) and high (
z\to 0
) temperature limits:

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{z\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi z}}\frac{\mathcal{\dot{N}}}{n_{\infty}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{z\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi z}}\frac{\mathcal{\dot{E}}}{\varepsilon_{\infty}}=-(16-\alpha^{2})M^{2}+\mathcal{O}(\alpha^{3}),

(91a)

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{z\rightarrow 0}\frac{\mathcal{\dot{N}}}{n_{\infty}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{z\rightarrow 0}\frac{\mathcal{\dot{E}}}{\varepsilon_{\infty}}=-\pi(27-2\alpha^{2})M^{2}+\mathcal{O}(\alpha^{3}).

(91b)

These expressions show that
\mathcal{\dot{N}}/n_{\infty}
and
\mathcal{\dot{E}}/\varepsilon_{\infty}
scale as
\sqrt{2\pi z}=\sqrt{2\pi m/(k_{\mathrm{B}}T)}
(the thermal wavelength divided by the reduced Compton wavelength of the gas particles) for
z\to\infty
, whereas
\mathcal{\dot{N}}/n_{\infty}=\mathrm{const}=\mathcal{\dot{E}}/\varepsilon_{\infty}
are constant for
z\to 0
. In addition, the nonvanishing values of the rotation parameter reduce both the particle and energy accretion rates with respect to their values for
\alpha=0
, and the first correction term in
\alpha
is of second order. Note that for
\alpha=0
, relations (
91
) reduce to the ones obtained for the Schwarzschild case in
pRoS16
.

On the other hand, one can numerically perform the following integrations over the energy and compute the accretion rates for generic values of
z
up to the second order in the rotation parameter
\alpha
. Using Eq. (
90
), one obtains

\displaystyle\frac{\dot{\mathcal{N}}}{n_{\infty}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\pi zM^{2}}{K_{2}(z)}\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\left(x_{c}^{(0)}\right)^{2}}{x_{c}^{(0)}-3}\left[1-\frac{2\alpha^{2}}{\left(x_{c}^{(0)}\right)^{2}\left(6-x_{c}^{(0)}\right)}\right]e^{-z\varepsilon}d\varepsilon+\mathcal{O}(\alpha^{3}),

(92a)

\displaystyle\frac{\dot{\mathcal{E}}}{\varepsilon_{\infty}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\pi z^{2}M^{2}}{zK_{1}(z)+3K_{2}(z)}\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\left(x_{c}^{(0)}\right)^{2}}{x_{c}^{(0)}-3}\left[1-\frac{2\alpha^{2}}{\left(x_{c}^{(0)}\right)^{2}\left(6-x_{c}^{(0)}\right)}\right]\varepsilon e^{-z\varepsilon}d\varepsilon+\mathcal{O}(\alpha^{3}).

(92b)

Therefore, within the slow-rotation approximation, the original triple integrals appearing in Eqs. (
81
) can be replaced with simpler formulas (
92
). Remarkably, for all numerical examples dicussed in Sec.
VI
, the difference between the results of the slow-rotation approximation and exact numerical values computed according Eqs. (
81
) stays below
4\%
, even for rapidly rotating black holes with rotation parameters up to
\alpha\leq 0.99
.

In a similar manner, one can approximate the integrals appearing in Eqs. (
45
), to compute the particle current and number densities around a slowly rotating Kerr black hole. To this end, we first expand Eq. (
44
) up to the second order in
\alpha
and obtain
Q_{\max}=Mmq_{\max}
with

\displaystyle q_{\max}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{x\sqrt{x\varepsilon^{2}-x+2}}{\sqrt{x-2}}-\frac{\alpha\varepsilon x\sin\vartheta\sin\chi}{x-2}

(93)

\displaystyle-\frac{\alpha^{2}\varepsilon^{2}x}{2(x-2)^{3/2}\sqrt{x\varepsilon^{2}-x+2}}\left(1-\frac{\left(2x\varepsilon^{2}-x+2\right)\left[(x-2)\cos^{2}\vartheta+x\sin^{2}\vartheta\sin^{2}\chi\right]}{x^{2}\varepsilon^{2}}\right)+\mathcal{O}(\alpha^{3}).

Contrary to the exact expressions which always satisfy
q_{\max}\geq q_{c}
with
q_{\max}=q_{c}
only at
x=x_{c}
, when truncating the expansion at the second order, these conditions are no longer guaranteed to hold. In particular, it can occur that
q_{\max}
given by Eq. (
93
) can be smaller than
q_{c}
defined in Eq. (
88
). For this reason, when computing the expressions corresponding to scattered trajectories, we impose an additional condition, enforcing that
q_{\max}>q_{c}
also in the slow-rotation approximation. Specifically, we numerically determine the largest root of the equation
q_{\max}(x)=q_{c}(x)
. Although this root depends on the angle
\chi
, in practice we found that it can be replaced with its maximum value corresponding to
\chi=0
, without introducing significant errors.

In the next section, we apply these conditions and compute the integrals (
45
) in the slow-rotation approximation. We also compute
n/n_{\infty}
and the ratio
\Omega/\Omega_{\mathrm{ZAMO}}
. Then these approximate results are compared with the corresponding exact numerical values.

On the other hand, for the Maxwell-Jüttner model, it is also possible to compute the particle current density in the low temperature limit
z\to\infty
and obtain analytic expressions for the particle density, up to second order in the rotation parameter. To do so, we first calculate the components of the particle current density (
45
) in the limit
z\to\infty

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{z\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi z}}\frac{J_{t}^{\mathrm{(tot)}}}{n_{\infty}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4\pi x\sqrt{2x}}\left[\frac{16x}{x-2}-\left(1+\frac{4(x+2)\cos^{2}\vartheta}{(x-2)^{2}}\right)\alpha^{2}\right]

(94a)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4\pi x}\sqrt{\frac{2}{x}}\left[\frac{4(x-4)x}{x-2}+\left(1+\frac{2(x+2)\cos^{2}\vartheta}{(x-2)^{2}}\right)\alpha^{2}\right]1_{x>x_{c},q_{\max}>q_{c}}+\mathcal{O}(\alpha^{3}),

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{z\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi z}}\frac{J^{r}_{\mathrm{(tot)}}}{n_{\infty}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{M}{4\pi x^{2}}\left[16-\left(1+\frac{16\cos^{2}\vartheta}{x^{2}}\right)\alpha^{2}\right]+\mathcal{O}(\alpha^{3}),

(94b)

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{z\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi z}}\frac{J_{\varphi}^{\mathrm{(tot)}}}{n_{\infty}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{2\sqrt{2x}\sin^{2}\vartheta}{\pi(x-2)^{2}}\alpha(1-1_{x>x_{c},q_{\max}>q_{c}})+\mathcal{O}(\alpha^{3}),

(94c)

where
x_{c}
,
q_{c}
, and

in the characteristic function

Employing Eqs. (

(95)

where the parameters
c_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x(x-2)\left[32(x-1)+4x^{2}(x-2)+x^{4}(x-4)1_{x>x_{c},q_{\max}>q_{c}}\right],

(96a)

\displaystyle c_{1}

\displaystyle=

(96b)

\displaystyle c_{2}

VII
Conclusions

In this work, we obtained new solutions of the Vlasov equation describing the Bondi-type accretion of a collisionless gas onto a rotating Kerr black hole. Deriving such solutions requires good control of the regions in the phase space available for the motion of the gas particles. Results presented in this work are based on a characterization of these regions in terms of specially adapted “momentum” coordinates
E
,
Q
, and
\chi
.
8
8

8

When this work was nearly completed, Ref.
yL25
appeared which treats the Bondi-type accretion problem in a Kerr-Newman black hole and uses a parametrization which, in the Kerr limit, reduces to the same variables
Q
and
\chi
used in this work. However, the author in
yL25
seems to reach different conclusions regarding the accretion rates; in particular, a non-vanishing angular momentum accretion rate is obtained even in the Kerr case.

As particular examples, we considered two models, differing in the assumed asymptotic form of the one-particle distribution function: a monoenergetic distribution and a Maxwell-Jüttner distribution function characterized by constant temperature and constant particle density at infinity. In both cases, the particle and energy accretion rates decrease with the growing black hole spin parameter. Quite surprisingly, the angular momentum accretion accretion rate vanishes for all models. This stays in contrast with the model of thin accretion disks confined to the equatorial plane, investigated in
aCpMaO22
;
gKpM25
, and can be explained by cancellations occurring in the momentum space. In the quasistationary approximation (i.e., with the evolution of the system approximated by a sequence of stationary solutions corresponding to different black hole parameters), such behavior should lead to a decrease in the black hole spin parameter
\alpha
, due to an increase of the black hole mass alone.

Particle and energy accretion rates can also be computed assuming a slow-rotation approximation. Many of the results obtained within the
slow-rotation approximation agree with the exact solution, even for large values of the black hole spin parameters. This applies especially to particle and energy accretion rates, but also for components of the particle current density, except for a region close to the black hole horizon.

Many natural problems associated with the model presented in this paper are left for future studies. They include addressing the questions about the mass inflation at the Cauchy horizon or taking into account the effects of self-gravity of the gas. Another natural generalization involves considering a uniform velocity of the gas at infinity (accretion onto a moving black hole), especially in the case in which the asymptotic velocity of the gas is misaligned with the rotation axis. That, in turn, implies natural questions about drag effects (see, e.g.,
lPsSrSsT1989
;
cDjRmMvC24
).

To proceed, we recall from Section

S_{c}=\left\{\left(\beta,\lambda_{\mathrm{sph}}(\alpha,\beta,x),\varepsilon_{\mathrm{sph}}(\alpha,\beta,x)\right)\colon-1\leq\beta\leq 1,\,x_{\mathrm{ph}}(\alpha,\beta)<x<x_{\mathrm{mb}}(\alpha,\beta)\right\},

(97)

where

v_{1}:=\begin{pmatrix}1\\
\frac{\partial\lambda_{\mathrm{sph}}}{\partial\beta}\\
\frac{\partial\varepsilon_{\mathrm{sph}}}{\partial\beta}\end{pmatrix},\qquad v_{2}:=\begin{pmatrix}0\\

The relation between the variables
(L,L_{z})

\displaystyle\beta

\displaystyle=\frac{qs\sin\vartheta-\alpha\varepsilon\cos^{2}\vartheta}{\sqrt{q^{2}+\alpha^{2}\cos^{2}\vartheta}},

(104)

\displaystyle\lambda

\displaystyle=\sqrt{q^{2}+\alpha^{2}\cos^{2}\vartheta},

(105)

\displaystyle\varepsilon

\displaystyle=\varepsilon.

(106)

Then, the relation between
(Q,\chi)

. In this case, an efficient and accurate numerical evaluation of integrals (
164
) requires dividing the interval
\chi\in[0,2\pi]
into regions in which the integrand is regular and performing the integration in each region separately. If
J_{t}^{\mathrm{(abs)}}
and
J_{t}^{\mathrm{(scat)}}
are not required separately, one can directly integrate the sum
\tilde{J}_{t}^{\mathrm{(abs)}}+\tilde{J}_{t}^{\mathrm{(scat)}}

Appendix E
Explicit forms of integrals with respect to
Q
.

In this appendix, we provide explicitly real expressions for the integrals of the type

\int\frac{QdQ}{\sqrt{R}},\qquad\int\frac{Q^{2}dQ}{\sqrt{R}}.

(166)

They were used to obtain numerical results reported in Section
VI
. We assume that

R=-AQ^{2}+BQ+C=-A(Q-Q_{-})(Q-Q_{+}),

(167)

where the coefficients
A
,
B
, and
C
are given by Eq. (
51
), and the roots
Q_{\pm}
can be expressed by Eq. (
142
). For simplicity, if
Q_{\pm}
are real, we will assume that
Q_{-}\leq Q_{+}
in this appendix. This convention allows us to write the relevant integrals in a consistent and simple way, but the labeling of the roots might be reversed with respect to Eq. (
142
).

Suppose that
A>0
and
Q_{+}>Q_{-}
. The relevant range of
Q
is
Q_{-}\leq Q\leq Q_{+}
. We have

\displaystyle\int_{Q_{-}}^{Q}\frac{qdq}{\sqrt{R}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{A}}\int_{Q_{-}}^{Q}\frac{qdq}{\sqrt{(q-Q_{-})(Q_{+}-q)}}

(168a)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{A}}\left[-\sqrt{(Q-Q_{-})(Q_{+}-Q)}+(Q_{-}+Q_{+})\mathrm{arcsin}\sqrt{\frac{Q-Q_{-}}{Q_{+}-Q_{-}}}\right],

\displaystyle\int_{Q_{-}}^{Q}\frac{q^{2}dq}{\sqrt{R}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{A}}\int_{Q_{-}}^{Q}\frac{q^{2}dq}{\sqrt{(q-Q_{-})(Q_{+}-q)}}

(168b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\sqrt{A}}\left\{-\sqrt{(Q-Q_{-})(Q_{+}-Q)}\left[2Q+3(Q_{-}+Q_{+})\right]\right.

\displaystyle\left.+(3Q_{-}^{2}+2Q_{-}Q_{+}+3Q_{+}^{2})\mathrm{arcsin}\sqrt{\frac{Q-Q_{-}}{Q_{+}-Q_{-}}}\right\}.

For
A<0
and real roots
Q_{\pm}
satisfying
Q_{-}<Q_{+}
, the relevant range is
Q_{+}<Q
or
Q<Q_{-}
. The following forms can be used:

\displaystyle\int_{Q_{+}}^{Q}\frac{qdq}{\sqrt{R}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{-A}}\int_{Q_{+}}^{Q}\frac{qdq}{\sqrt{(q-Q_{-})(q-Q_{+})}}

(169a)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{-A}}\left[\sqrt{(Q-Q_{-})(Q-Q_{+})}+(Q_{-}+Q_{+})\mathrm{arsinh}\sqrt{\frac{Q-Q_{+}}{Q_{+}-Q_{-}}}\right],\quad Q_{+}<Q,

\displaystyle\int_{Q_{+}}^{Q}\frac{q^{2}dq}{\sqrt{R}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{-A}}\int_{Q_{+}}^{Q}\frac{q^{2}dq}{\sqrt{(q-Q_{-})(q-Q_{+})}}

(169b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\sqrt{-A}}\left\{\sqrt{(Q-Q_{-})(Q-Q_{+})}\left[2Q+3(Q_{-}+Q_{+})\right]\right.

\displaystyle\left.+(3Q_{-}^{2}+2Q_{-}Q_{+}+3Q_{+}^{2})\mathrm{arsinh}\sqrt{\frac{Q-Q_{+}}{Q_{+}-Q_{-}}}\right\},\quad Q_{+}<Q,

\displaystyle\int_{Q}^{Q_{-}}\frac{qdq}{\sqrt{R}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{-A}}\int_{Q}^{Q_{-}}\frac{qdq}{\sqrt{(Q_{-}-q)(Q_{+}-q)}}

(169c)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{-A}}\left[-\sqrt{(Q_{-}-Q)(Q_{+}-Q)}+(Q_{-}+Q_{+})\mathrm{artanh}\sqrt{\frac{Q_{-}-Q}{Q_{+}-Q}}\right],\quad Q<Q_{-},

\displaystyle\int_{Q}^{Q_{-}}\frac{q^{2}dq}{\sqrt{R}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{-A}}\int_{Q}^{Q_{-}}\frac{q^{2}dq}{\sqrt{(Q_{-}-q)(Q_{+}-q)}}

(169d)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\sqrt{-A}}\left\{-\sqrt{(Q_{-}-Q)(Q_{+}-Q)}\left[2Q+3(Q_{-}+Q_{+})\right]\right.

\displaystyle\left.+(3Q_{-}^{2}+2Q_{-}Q_{+}+3Q_{+}^{2})\mathrm{artanh}\sqrt{\frac{Q_{-}-Q}{Q_{+}-Q}}\right\},\quad Q<Q_{-}.

The roots
Q_{\pm}
remain real, provided that

E^{2}\rho^{2}+a^{2}m^{2}\sin^{2}\vartheta\sin^{2}\chi-m^{2}\Delta\geq 0,

and
\Delta>0
. As already argued, the first condition is always satisfied for
E>m
and
r>r_{-}
. The second requires
\Delta>0
. For
r<r_{+}
,
A<0
, and complex roots
Q_{\pm}
, one can use the form

\displaystyle\int\frac{qdq}{\sqrt{-Aq^{2}+Bq+C}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{-Aq^{2}+Bq+C}{-A}-\frac{B}{2(-A)^{2/3}}\mathrm{artanh}\left(\frac{-2Aq+B}{2\sqrt{-A}\sqrt{-Aq^{2}+Bq+C}}\right),

(170)

\displaystyle\int\frac{q^{2}dq}{\sqrt{-Aq^{2}+Bq+C}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{(2Aq+3B)\sqrt{-Aq^{2}+Bq+C}}{4A^{2}}

(171)

\displaystyle-\frac{4AC+3B^{2}}{8(-A)^{5/2}}\ln\left[-A^{2}\left(B-2Aq-2\sqrt{-A}\sqrt{-Aq^{2}+Bq+C}\right)\right].

It is easy to check that in this case

\displaystyle-1<\frac{-2Aq+B}{2\sqrt{-A}\sqrt{-Aq^{2}+Bq+C}}<1,

(172)

as required.

There is also the special case with
A=0
. For
B>0
, we denote
Q_{0}=-C/B
and write

\displaystyle\int_{Q_{0}}^{Q}\frac{qdq}{\sqrt{Bq+C}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{B}}\int_{Q_{0}}^{Q}\frac{qdq}{\sqrt{q-Q_{0}}}=\frac{2}{3\sqrt{B}}\sqrt{Q-Q_{0}}(Q+2Q_{0}),

(173)

\displaystyle\int_{Q_{0}}^{Q}\frac{q^{2}dq}{\sqrt{Bq+C}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{B}}\int_{Q_{0}}^{Q}\frac{q^{2}dq}{\sqrt{q-Q_{0}}}=\frac{2}{15\sqrt{B}}\sqrt{Q-Q_{0}}(3Q^{2}+4QQ_{0}+8Q_{0}^{2}).

(174)

For
B<0
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{Q}^{Q_{0}}\frac{qdq}{\sqrt{Bq+C}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{-B}}\int_{Q}^{Q_{0}}\frac{qdq}{\sqrt{Q_{0}-q}}=\frac{2}{3\sqrt{-B}}\sqrt{Q_{0}-Q}(Q+2Q_{0}),

(175)

\displaystyle\int_{Q}^{Q_{0}}\frac{q^{2}dq}{\sqrt{Bq+C}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{-B}}\int_{Q}^{Q_{0}}\frac{q^{2}dq}{\sqrt{Q_{0}-q}}=\frac{2}{15\sqrt{-B}}\sqrt{Q_{0}-Q}(3Q^{2}+4QQ_{0}+8Q_{0}^{2}),

(176)

where again
Q_{0}=-C/B
.

Correspondence: Effects of short‐term tofogliflozin treatment on insulin secretory capacity in type 2 diabetes: Insights and future directions
Authors: Gargi Bisht, Nitin Kumar Indora
Published: 2026

Dear Editor,

We read with great interest the recent article by Miyamoto et al. entitled “Effects of short‐term tofogliflozin treatment on the insulin secretory capacity of people with type 2 diabetes: A randomized controlled trial, the TOP‐ELM study.” 1 The authors should be commended for conducting a well‐designed randomized controlled trial addressing an important and clinically relevant question regarding early pharmacological intervention in type 2 diabetes.

The study provides valuable evidence that short‐term (4‐week) treatment with tofogliflozin results in significant improvements in glycemic parameters comparable to metformin, along with a greater reduction in fatty liver index. However, the lack of significant improvement in insulin secretory capacity at the group level raises important considerations regarding the temporal dynamics of β‐cell recovery.

One notable strength of the study lies in its subgroup analyses, which demonstrated that individuals with poorer baseline glucose tolerance or greater improvements in glucose levels exhibited significant enhancement in insulin secretory capacity. This finding highlights the potential role of glucotoxicity reduction as a key mediator in β‐cell functional recovery. However, further exploration incorporating continuous rather than median‐based stratification of glucose tolerance may provide a more nuanced understanding of this relationship. Previous evidence suggests that early intensive glycemic control can reverse glucotoxic effects and partially restore β‐cell function. 2 , 3

Additionally, the reliance on OGTT‐derived indices such as the disposition index, although practical, may not fully capture intrinsic β‐cell function. The use of gold‐standard techniques, such as hyperglycemic clamps or mixed‐meal tolerance tests, in future studies could strengthen the physiological interpretation of these findings. Furthermore, the relatively short duration of intervention may have limited the ability to detect meaningful changes in β‐cell function, which is known to recover gradually over time. 4

The observed improvement in hepatic steatosis, as indicated by the fatty liver index, is noteworthy. However, given that FLI is an indirect surrogate marker, future studies incorporating imaging modalities such as MRI‐PDFF would provide more robust evidence regarding hepatic fat reduction and its relationship with metabolic improvements. 5

Another important consideration is the relatively low dose of metformin used as a comparator, which may not fully reflect its maximal therapeutic potential. Dose‐escalation strategies or longer follow‐up periods could allow for a more balanced comparison between the two interventions.

In summary, this study contributes important insights into the early metabolic effects of SGLT2 inhibitors, particularly tofogliflozin. Future research focusing on longer treatment durations, precise phenotyping of patients, and integration of advanced metabolic assessment techniques may help clarify the conditions under which early β‐cell recovery can be optimized. Such efforts will be crucial in advancing personalized treatment strategies for individuals with type 2 diabetes.

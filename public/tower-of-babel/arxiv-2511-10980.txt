Flavor changing neutral current

25

1

The absence of charged leptons in the final state allows precise theoretical predictions, in contrast to

30

The decay rates can be enhanced by physics beyond the SM; for example, virtual contributions from heavy particles such as leptoquarks
42

Z^{{}^{\prime}}
boson
18

The experimental signature is also sensitive to new invisible particles, such as invisible light scalars
21
or fermionic dark matter
20

\left(2.9\pm 0.3\right)\times 10^{-5}

The inclusive

The Belle II software framework (

We produce signal simulation samples consisting of individually generated contributions: exclusive

40

The non-resonant

M_{X_{s}}
of the

system be greater than
1.1\penalty\ {\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV\!/}c^{2}}

;

To simulate the

;
31
;
12

b
-quark follows a Gaussian distribution.
The fragmentation of the final-state hadrons is simulated using
Pythia8
.
The invariant mass squared of the neutrino pair,

The long-distance

One
B

, is fully reconstructed in a hadronic decay mode by the full event interpretation (FEI) algorithm
28

The FEI assigns a probability to each
B_{\rm tag}
candidate based on a multivariate classifier, indicating how likely the candidate is to be properly reconstructed in a given mode.
Candidates with a probability greater than 0.001 are selected.
We require
B_{\rm tag}

M_{\textrm{bc}}\equiv\sqrt{\left(\sqrt{s}/2\right)^{2}-\left|p_{B}^{*}c\right|^{2}}/c^{2}>5.27{\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV\!/}c^{2}}

\left|\Delta E\right|\equiv\left|E_{B}^{*}-(\sqrt{s}/2)\right|<0.2\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV}

Here,
\sqrt{s}
,
p_{B}^{*}
, and
E_{B}^{*}
are the SuperKEKB center-of-mass (c.m.) energy, and the momentum and energy of the
B

Particle candidates not used to reconstruct the
B_{\textrm{tag}}
are used to form the
X_{s}
candidate.
We use a sum-of-exclusive decays method to reconstruct the
X_{s}
candidate, which provides high sensitivity to the inclusive decay, despite the presence of two undetected neutrinos.
The
X_{s}
candidate is reconstructed in 30 decay modes:
Kn\pi

(0\leq n\leq 4)
with at most two
\pi^{0}
, and
3Kn\pi

(0\leq n\leq 1)
with at most one
\pi^{0}

K
indicates
K^{\pm}
or
K^{0}_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle S}
.
These decay modes cover 83% of the non-resonant
X_{s}
decay in the signal simulation samples, assuming the
K^{0}_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle L}
contribution is equal to that of
K^{0}_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle S}
.
In total, the reconstructed modes constitute 93% of the
B\rightarrow X_{s}\nu\bar{\nu}
branching fraction in the signal simulation samples.

The
X_{s}
-candidate reconstruction proceeds as follows.
Except for
\pi^{\pm}
from
K^{0}_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle S}
candidates, the distance of closest approach of a track to the interaction point (IP) is required to satisfy
dr<0.5\rm\,cm
in the transverse plane and
\left|dz\right|<2\rm\,cm
along the
z

The numbers of hits in the PXD and CDC must be greater than 0 and 20, respectively.
Kaon and pion candidates are selected using charged particle identification (PID) likelihoods, built using information from the SVD, CDC, TOP, ARICH, ECL, and KLM subsystems.

Photon candidates are reconstructed from ECL clusters not geometrically matched to charged tracks; to guarantee that clusters do not originate from charged particles, their polar angle
\theta
must be within the CDC acceptance,
17^{\circ}<\theta<150^{\circ}
.
The energy of a photon candidate must be greater than 80
\mathrm{\,Me\kern-1.00006ptV}
, 30
\mathrm{\,Me\kern-1.00006ptV}
, or 60
\mathrm{\,Me\kern-1.00006ptV}
for candidates detected in the forward, barrel, and backward regions of the ECL, respectively.

We reconstruct
\pi^{0}
candidates from pairs of photon candidates with invariant mass in the range
120<m_{\gamma\gamma}<145\penalty\ {\mathrm{\,Me\kern-1.00006ptV\!/}c^{2}}
.
The momentum of
\pi^{0}
candidates must be larger than
0.4
{\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV\!/}c}
.
We require the absolute difference in azimuthal angle between the two photons to be less than
1.5

rad
in the laboratory frame. Additionally, the opening angle must be smaller than
1.4\penalty\ \textrm{rad}
.

We reconstruct

candidates from two oppositely charged tracks, for which a vertex fit
32
is performed and required to converge.
The significance of the distance from this vertex to the IP must be larger than 50.
We require the invariant mass of
K^{0}_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle S}
-candidates to be within
10{\mathrm{\,Me\kern-1.00006ptV\!/}c^{2}}
of the nominal

To suppress combinatorial background, the reconstructed invariant mass of the
X_{s}
candidate (
M_{X_{s}}^{\rm reco}

2.0{\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV\!/}c^{2}}

A decay-chain vertex fit
32

X_{s}
candidates in decay modes with two or more charged tracks.
The c.m.-frame momentum of the
X_{s}
must satisfy
0.5<p^{*}_{X_{s}}<2.96{\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV\!/}c}
.
To suppress backgrounds from

candidates with

in the range
[1.84,1.89]
{\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV\!/}c^{2}}
are rejected. The
X_{s}^{0}
flavor is assigned based on the net charge of the associated kaons; if zero, the flavor is undetermined.
Pairs of
X_{s}
and
B_{\textrm{tag}}

The dineutrino system can be mimicked by particles outside the detector acceptance.
Therefore, we require the polar angle of the missing momentum
\vec{p}_{\textrm{miss}}=\vec{p}_{e^{+}e^{-}}-\sum\vec{p}_{\textrm{track}}-\sum\vec{p}_{\gamma}
to be in the detector acceptance,
17^{\circ}<\theta_{\textrm{miss}}<150^{\circ}

\vec{p}_{\textrm{track}}
and
\vec{p}_{\gamma}
are the momenta of charged tracks and photon candidates.

For each combination of
X_{s}
and
B_{\textrm{tag}}
candidates, the tracks or photon candidates not used in the reconstruction of these candidates form the rest-of-event.
The numbers of tracks from the IP,
\pi^{0}
, and
K^{0}_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle S}
candidates in the rest-of-event are required to be zero.
The rest-of-event is allowed to contain photon candidates and tracks with
dr>2\penalty\ \rm\,cm
or
\left|dz\right|>4\penalty\ \rm\,cm
.
We calculate the total lab-frame energy
E_{\textrm{extra}}
as the sum of all photon candidates with energies greater than 100, 60, and 150
\mathrm{\,Me\kern-1.00006ptV}
in the forward, barrel, and backward regions of the ECL, respectively.
E_{\textrm{extra}}
is required to be less than
1.3\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV}

After these selections, the average multiplicity of
B_{\rm tag}X_{s}
candidates per event is 1.71 in simulated signal samples and 1.45 in data.
In each event, we retain only the candidates with the highest
B_{\textrm{tag}}
FEI probability.
After this, the average multiplicity is reduced to 1.06 for simulated signal samples and 1.13 for data.
If multiple signal candidates remain, we select one at random.

The dominant background contributions arise from continuum
e^{+}e^{-}\rightarrow q\bar{q}
processes and non-signal

decays.
To suppress them, we use a boosted decision tree (BDT)

The BDT uses 32 input variables: 26 variables
related to kinematics, the rest-of-event, and event shape, selected for their separation power and good data-simulation agreement, and 6 variables specifically designed to suppress backgrounds from

The list of input variables is provided in the Supplemental Material

Among the 32 BDT-input variables,

provides the largest signal-background discrimination.
We require the BDT output, denoted

and ranging from 0 to 1, to be greater than 0.86.
This criterion is chosen to include the point that maximizes the figure of merit (FOM), defined as
N_{\textrm{sig}}/\sqrt{N_{\textrm{sig}}+N_{\textrm{bkg}}}
where
N_{\textrm{sig}}
and
N_{\textrm{bkg}}
are the expected numbers signal and background events.
The
\mathcal{O}_{\textrm{BDT}}

The expected background yield is 3338 events, including contributions from both
q\bar{q}
and
B\bar{B}

A signal region is defined in

The bin boundaries along the

[0.0,0.6,1.0,2.0]{\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV\!/}c^{2}}

In MR1, MR2, and MR3, the bin boundaries along the
{\mathcal{O}_{\textrm{BDT}}}

These bin boundaries are chosen to maximize sensitivity without introducing fit bias, which is verified by a pseudo-experiment study.
For simplicity, bin indices are assigned from 1 to 15.
The indices range from 1 to 5 in MR1, from 6 to 10 in MR2, and from 11 to 15 in MR3, increasing along the positive
\mathcal{O}_{\textrm{BDT}}

We use the
HistFactory

23
package to construct the probability density function (PDF) as the product of Poisson distributions and auxiliary terms.
The expected numbers of events for the Poisson distributions are determined from simulation and includes correction factors and systematic uncertainties, described below.
We introduce nuisance parameters to the likelihood with Gaussian constraints and parametrize systematic uncertainties as auxiliary terms.
The values of the nuisance parameters and the signal branching fractions are determined by the fit.

Data-driven corrections are applied to the modeling. The
B^{+}\rightarrow K^{+}\nu\bar{\nu}
analysis
7
observed a mismodeling of the photon multiplicity in Belle II.
To address this, we compare the photon multiplicity distributions in data and simulation using
B_{\textrm{tag}}^{\pm}X_{s}^{0}
candidates formed by combining a charged
B_{\textrm{tag}}
candidate and a neutral
X_{s}
candidate.
Correction factors are derived from the ratio of data to simulation.
We reweight simulation samples as a function of the photon multiplicity to correct for the disagreement.
The simulation of the fragmentation of the
X_{s}
system is corrected using results from a measurement of
B\rightarrow X_{s}\gamma

45
in three
M_{X_{s}}
regions:
[1.15,1.5]{\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV\!/}c^{2}}
,
[1.5,2.0]{\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV\!/}c^{2}}
, and
[2.0,2.4]{\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV\!/}c^{2}}
.

K\pi
without
\pi^{0}
,
K\pi
with one
\pi^{0}
,
K2\pi
without
\pi^{0}
,
K2\pi
with one
\pi^{0}
,
K3\pi
without
\pi^{0}
,
K3\pi
with one
\pi^{0}
,
K4\pi

\pi^{0}

2\pi^{0}

The

For the modeling of

B\rightarrow K^{(*)}p\bar{p}

To correct the remaining
B\rightarrow X_{s}n\bar{n}
simulation samples, we reconstruct
B\rightarrow X_{s}p\bar{p}
events in data and use the measured
M_{p\bar{p}}
distribution to reweight the simulated
M_{n\bar{n}}

The kinematics
and branching fractions of
B^{+}\rightarrow K^{+}K^{0}_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle L}K^{0}_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle L}
and

36

26

The modeling of
B\rightarrow K^{*}K^{0}_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle L}K^{0}_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle L}
and the remaining
B\rightarrow X_{s}K^{0}_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle L}K^{0}_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle L}

B\rightarrow K^{*}K^{0}_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle S}K^{0}_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle S}
and
B\rightarrow X_{s}K^{0}_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle S}K^{0}_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle S}

M_{\textrm{bc}}

{\mathcal{O}_{\textrm{BDT}}}

In summary, we search for
B\rightarrow X_{s}\nu\bar{\nu}
decay using
365.4\mbox{\,fb}^{-1}
of
\mathchar 28935\relax{(4S)}
and
42.7\mbox{\,fb}^{-1}
of off-resonance data collected by the Belle II experiment. The analysis reconstructs one
B
meson in a hadronic decay mode and considers 30 exclusive channels in the reconstruction of the
X_{s}
system.
No significant signal is observed, and we set 90% C.L. upper limits on the partial branching fractions of
2.2\times 10^{-5}
for
0.0<M_{X_{s}}<0.6{\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV\!/}c^{2}}
,
9.5\times 10^{-5}
for
0.6<M_{X_{s}}<1.0{\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV\!/}c^{2}}
, and
3.1\times 10^{-4}
for
1.0{\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV\!/}c^{2}}<M_{X_{s}}
.
We calculate the branching fraction for the entire
M_{X_{s}}
region by summing the partial branching fractions to find

\displaystyle{\mathcal{B}}(B\rightarrow X_{s}\nu\bar{\nu})=[8.8^{+8.5}_{-8.2}(\textrm{stat})^{+12.6}_{-10.8}(\textrm{syst})]\times 10^{-5},

and set a corresponding 90% C.L. upper limit

\displaystyle{\mathcal{B}}(B\rightarrow X_{s}\nu\bar{\nu})<3.2\times 10^{-4}.

This branching fraction and upper limit do not include the contribution from long-distance
B^{+}\rightarrow\tau^{+}(\rightarrow X_{s}^{+}\bar{\nu})\nu
decays.
This is the first search for inclusive
B\rightarrow X_{s}\nu\bar{\nu}
decays
and can be used to constrain potential contributions from physics beyond the SM
24
.

Funnel-Based Online Recovery Control for Nonlinear Systems With Unknown Dynamics
Zihao Song, Shirantha Welikala, Panos J. Antsaklis, Hai Lin

Abstract
In this paper, we focus on recovery control of nonlinear systems from attacks or failures. The main challenges of this problem lie in (1) learning the unknown dynamics caused by attacks or failures with formal guarantees, and (2) finding the invariant set of states to formally ensure the state deviations allowed from the nominal trajectory. To solve this problem, we propose to apply the Recurrent Equilibrium Networks (RENs) to learn the unknown dynamics using the data from the real-time system states. The input-output property of this REN model is guaranteed by incremental integral quadratic constraints (IQCs). Then, we propose a funnel-based control method to achieve system recovery from the deviated states. In particular, a sufficient condition for nominal trajectory stabilization is derived together with the invariant funnels along the nominal trajectory. Eventually, the effectiveness of our proposed control method is illustrated by a simulation example of a DC microgrid control application.



I
Introduction

I-A

Background and motivations

Resilience refers to the capability of a natural or engineered system to withstand disturbances, attacks, or failures, recover its functionality with graceful degradation, and adapt to new operating conditions. Resilience becomes a focal point in the design of next-generation complex engineering systems, such as autonomous vehicles, robotic networks, and smart grids, where the systems are open to uncertain dynamic environments and are vulnerable to cyber-physical attacks.

Most of the existing work focuses on fault detection
[
1
]
, fault tolerant control
[
2
]
, or security enhancement
[
3
]
. However, the study of recovery control is still relatively lacking. By recovery control, we mean designing a control mechanism that not only maintains system stability after faults or disturbances, but also actively drives the system states back to their desired operating points or equilibrium conditions after the occurrence of a fault, attack, or disruption.

Recovery control poses significant challenges. First, we need to ensure formal performance during the transition phase, i.e., the sets of allowable state deviations with respect to the nominal trajectory, so that the systems’ stability and safety are not violated while the system is recovering from faults or attacks.
Secondly, system recovery should usually be conducted online, as system performance will be continuously degraded if not properly and quickly handled, which can cause secondary damage
[
4
]
.
Thirdly, recovery control must deal with unknown dynamics as faults, attacks, and topology changes often alter the system dynamics, which may make conventional control schemes difficult to apply.
In this paper, we are, therefore, motivated to develop an online funnel-based control framework for the recovery control of systems with unknown dynamics.

I-B

Related work

From the control theoretical perspective, early efforts for system recovery control mainly focused on robust control approaches, such as
H_{\infty}
control
[
5
,
6
]
, fault-tolerant control (FTC)
[
7
]
and invariant-set theory
[
8
,
9
]
, which ensure stability/performance margins under attacks or faults.
Although these methods can provide strong guarantees, they usually rely on accurate modeling assumptions with the knowledge of structured unknown dynamics.
Besides, these methods are mostly designed offline to withstand attacks or failures but cannot proactively and efficiently handle their impacts.
There have also been studies on resilient consensus
[
10
,
11
]
and safety-guaranteed designs (e.g., control barrier functions (CBFs)
[
12
]
and barrier Lyapunov functions (BLFs)
[
13
]
), but the adaptability to substantial changes in the system dynamics is still limited.

Recent years have seen the development in this domain including reconfiguration-based methods (e.g., active FTC
[
2
,
14
]
, and control reallocation
[
14
,
15
]
), adaptation-based methods (e.g., adaptive control
[
16
]
, switching-based control
[
17
,
18
]
and adaptive model predictive control (AMPC)
[
19
]
), learning-based methods (e.g., reinforcement learning (RL)
[
20
]
, data-driven methods
[
21
,
22
]
and Gaussian process (GP)-based methods
[
23
]
) and game theoretic methods
[
24
,
25
]
.
However, the aforementioned methods may not be directly applicable to scenarios where there exist unknown dynamics in the systems.
If not properly handled, these dynamics may deteriorate system performance or even result in instability of the systems.

Apart from the unknown dynamics, finding the formal guarantees for the allowable state deviations with respect to the nominal trajectory is generally non-trivial.
Even though methods like tube-based MPC
[
26
]
can ensure the invariance of system states around the nominal trajectory under unknown dynamics, they may not be tractable for complex nonlinear systems and lack formal guarantees for stability and transition performance during system recovery.
Compared to the aforementioned methods, funnel-based control
[
27
,
28
]
, as a relatively new method for path planning, appears to be specifically suitable for system recovery control, since multiple constraints such as convergence behaviors, input saturation, and safety constraints can be combined in the solutions and invariant funnels can be systematically derived that allow maximal state deviations with respect to nominal trajectories. However, unknown dynamics are not involved in existing funnel-based control designs
[
27
,
28
]
.

I-C

Our approach and contributions

To handle unknown dynamics, we mainly focus on learning-based approaches, since they are independent of system dynamics, and can be adaptable to system changes, which is well-suited for recovery control and can be easily combined with the proposed funnel-based method.
In this paper, a specific type of neural network called Recurrent Equilibrium Network (REN)
[
29
]
is applied since this type of neural network can not only approximate the unknown dynamics online around the nominal trajectories, but also provide input-output properties of the learned unknown dynamics during the training phase.

Therefore, we apply the RENs to learn the unknown dynamics online and correct the system dynamics, where we require that the states of the system with the learned unknown dynamics are close to the states of the actual system.
Considering the system with the learned unknown dynamics, we propose a funnel-based recovery control framework so that the closed-loop system is both internally and
L_{2}
stable, with invariant funnels obtained along the nominal trajectory.

Based on the above discussions, the main contributions of this paper can be summarized as follows:

1.

A funnel-based method is proposed for system recovery control with unknown dynamics;

2.

The internal and
L_{2}
stability of the closed-loop system under our proposed controller are shown together with the invariant funnel derivation;

3.

Different from the existing work of funnel-based control
[
27
,
28
]
, where external disturbances are norm bounded by
1
, our results remove this assumption to handle recovery control scenarios.

Our main results of funnel-based recovery control are presented in Section
IV

Note that the nonlinear mapping

To achieve recovery control of the system (

However, the definition of the REN model in

A REN model is a combination of a Recurrent Neural Network (RNN)

Consequently, REN models are suitable for our proposed funnel-based recovery control design (introduced in the next section) that requires linearization of the nonlinear system dynamics (

\hat{\mathcal{U}}:=\{\hat{u}_{r}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\ |\ g_{j}(\hat{u}_{r})\leq 0,\ j\in\mathcal{I}_{l_{u}}\}
, respectively, with all the functions
h_{i}
and
g_{j}
the same as the actual system (
2
).

The REN model is trained using the real-time states of the virtual system, and thus, the training of REN is self-supervised and the loss function is selected by minimizing the difference between the actual system states
x(t)
and the virtual system states
\hat{x}(t)
, i.e.,

\displaystyle L_{\mathrm{REN}}:=\frac{1}{nN_{\mathrm{batch}}}\sum_{k=1}^{N_{\mathrm{batch}}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}|x_{ki}(t)-\hat{x}_{ki}(t)|^{2},

(12)

where
x_{ki}(t)
is the
i\textsuperscript{th}
component of the real-time state
x(t)
of the actual system dynamics (
2
) in the
k\textsuperscript{th}
batch, and similarly,
\hat{x}_{ki}(t)
is the
i\textsuperscript{th}
component of the states
\hat{x}(t)
of the virtual system
in the
k\textsuperscript{th}
batch.

IV
Funnel-based Online Recovery Control Design

In this section, we propose our main theoretical results of our funnel-based recovery control design for the system with learned unknown dynamics and derive the invariant funnels for the states of the system under our designed controller.

IV-A

Closed-Loop Dynamics

With the learned unknown dynamics
\hat{\Delta}
using the REN model in (
7
), we can linearize the virtual nonlinear system (
11
) around this nominal trajectory and convert the virtual system (
11
) into the following Lur’e type dynamics
[
34
]
:

\displaystyle\hskip-5.69054pt\begin{cases}\hat{x}(t+1)=A(t)\hat{x}(t)+B(t)\hat{u}_{r}(t)+Ep(t)+\hat{\Delta}(t),\\
p(t)=\phi(t,q(t)),\\
q(t)=C\hat{x}(t)+D\hat{u}_{r}(t)+G\hat{\Delta}(t),\end{cases}

(13)

where
\hat{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\hat{u}_{r}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
are the states and control input of the Lur’e dynamics with the learned unknown dynamics
\hat{\Delta}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, which are the same as those in (
11
).

p\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{p}}
is the nonlinear term to compensate the linearization errors, which is a time-varying function with argument
q\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{q}}
.
This nonlinear function
\phi:\mathbb{N}_{0}\times\mathbb{R}^{n_{q}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n_{p}}
, which is continuously differentiable, captures the nonlinearities and the dominant linearization errors of the virtual system (
11
).

A(t)=\frac{\partial\bar{f}}{\partial\hat{x}}\big|_{(\bar{x},\bar{u}_{r})}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
and
B(t)=\frac{\partial\bar{f}}{\partial\hat{u}_{r}}\big|_{(\bar{x},\bar{u}_{r})}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}
are the first-order approximations of the nonlinear system (
2
) around the nominal trajectory.
The matrices
E\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n_{p}}
,
C\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{q}\times n}
,
D\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{q}\times m}
and
G\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{q}\times n}
are time-invariant selector matrices with
0
’s and
1
’s to organize the nonlinearity of the system.

Remark 5
.

Note that the learned unknown dynamics
\hat{\Delta}
in (
13
) can not only approximate the actual unknown dynamics caused by attacks or failures, but also compensate for small model mismatches, while the dominant linearization errors are directly modeled by the nonlinear term
p(t)
in (
13
).

To design the funnel-based recovery controller for the virtual system (
11
), suppose that a nominal trajectory has been obtained. In particular, the dynamics of the nominal trajectory are as follows:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\bar{x}(t+1)=A(t)\bar{x}(t)+B(t)\bar{u}_{r}(t)+E\bar{p}(t),\\
\bar{p}(t)=\phi(t,\bar{q}(t)),\\
\bar{q}(t)=C\bar{x}(t)+D\bar{u}_{r}(t),\end{cases}

(14)

where
(\bar{x}(\cdot),\bar{u}_{r}(\cdot))
defines the nominal trajectory, which is a solution of the virtual system (
11
) without learned unknown dynamics, i.e.,
\hat{\Delta}\equiv\mathbf{0}
.
More details of designing this trajectory can be found in
[
35
]
.

Define state and control input tracking errors with respect to the nominal trajectory
(\bar{x}(\cdot),\bar{u}_{r}(\cdot))
are
\eta(t):=\hat{x}(t)-\bar{x}(t)
and
\xi(t):=\hat{u}_{r}(t)-\bar{u}_{r}(t)
, respectively.
Thus, the tracking error dynamics of (
13
) with respect to the nominal trajectory
(\bar{x}(\cdot),\bar{u}_{r}(\cdot))
can be obtained by

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\eta(t+1)=A(t)\eta(t)+B(t)\xi(t)+E\tilde{p}(t)+\hat{\Delta}(t),\\
\tilde{p}(t)=\phi(t,q(t))-\phi(t,\bar{q}(t)),\\
\tilde{q}(t)=C\eta(t)+D\xi(t)+G\hat{\Delta}(t),\end{cases}

(15)

where
\tilde{p}(t):=p(t)-\bar{p}(t)
with
\bar{q}(t):=\bar{C}\bar{x}(t)+\bar{D}\bar{u}_{r}(t)
.

Since the function
\phi
is continuously differentiable, they are locally Lipschitz, and the following condition

\displaystyle|p_{t}-\bar{p}_{t}|\leq\gamma_{1t}|q_{t}-\bar{q}_{t}|,\ \ \forall q_{t},\ \bar{q}_{t}\in\mathcal{Q},

(16)

is satisfied for all
t\in\mathcal{I}_{N-1}^{0}
, where
\gamma_{1t}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
is a Lipschitz constant at each time instant and
\mathcal{Q}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n_{q}}
is any compact set. The estimation of
\gamma_{1t}
for all
t\in\mathcal{I}_{N-1}^{0}
can be found in
[
35
]
, which can be solved in real-time.

For tracking error dynamics (
15
), we design the full-state feedback controller as

\displaystyle\xi(t)=K(t)\eta(t),

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\ \

\displaystyle\hat{u}_{r}(t)=\bar{u}_{r}(t)+K(t)(\hat{x}(t)-\bar{x}(t)),

(17)

where
K(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
is the controller gain to be determined.

Remark 6
.

When applied to the actual system (
2
), the same designed controller gains
K(t)
in (
IV-A
) can be used, i.e., the controller applied in actual system is
u_{r}(t)=\bar{u}_{r}(t)+K(t)(x(t)-\bar{x}(t))
. This can also be seen in our proposed control architecture in Fig.
1
, where both sides share the same form of controller.

Under the controller (
IV-A
), the closed-loop tracking error dynamics (
15
) can be derived as:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\eta_{t+1}=(A_{t}+B_{t}K_{t})\eta_{t}+E\tilde{p}_{t}+\hat{\Delta}_{t},\\
\tilde{q}_{t}=(C+DK_{t})\eta_{t}+G\hat{\Delta}_{t},\end{cases}

(18)

with
|\tilde{p}_{t}|\leq\gamma_{1t}|\tilde{q}_{t}|
(from (
16
)). Besides, in the sequel, we determine the controller gain
K(t)
in (
IV-A
) so that the closed-loop system (
18
) is both internally and
L_{2}
stable from the learned unknown dynamics
\hat{\Delta}_{t}
to the tracking error state
\eta_{t}
.

Remark 7
.

With (
18
), we represent the closed-loop nonlinear system with the state and input dependent nonlinear term
\tilde{p}
given in (
15
) as a linear form.
This may be a conservative way to handle the nonlinear system, but it allows us to design a quadratic Lyapunov function with which we can guarantee the invariance and attractive conditions required in the funnel-based recovery control method we propose for the actual nonlinear system (
2
). Besides, the construction of (
18
) enables the analysis using linear approaches, and thus, can be solved efficiently.
These advantages are important for recovery control problem considered in this paper, since to achieve system recovery, we not only need to design the feedback controller (
IV-A
), but also require to provide a formal guarantee of the largest deviation domain for the real-time states.
This also motivates the proposed funnel-based recovery control method shown next.

IV-B

IV-C

Invariant Funnel Synthesis

The basic architecture of the invariant funnels is shown in Fig.
2
, where we require that any states within the invariant funnels will never escape from there. Besides, if at the first way point, our states stay within the funnel created by the designed controller
K_{0}
, then at the next time step, the states will also stay within the funnel created by the controller
K_{1}
at this way point.

Fig. 2
:
The architecture of the invariant funnels, where the funnels are constructed at each way point and the virtual system states
\hat{x}(t)
are close to the nominal state trajectory
\bar{x}(t)
, for all
t\in\mathcal{I}_{N}^{0}
. The last way point at
t=N
is the desired equilibrium point.

To achieve funnel-based recovery control synthesis, in this paper, we define the specific class of invariant funnels as the following ellipsoidal space, i.e.,

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\eta_{t}}:=\Big\{\eta_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\ \Big|\ \eta_{t}^{\top}P_{t}^{-1}\eta_{t}\leq\frac{1}{r_{t}}\Big\},

(26)

where the constant
\frac{1}{r_{t}}
defines the radius of the ellipsoid with
r_{t}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
, for all
t\in\mathcal{I}_{N}^{0}
.

P_{t}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n\times n}
is the matrix obtained by the DMI (
19
) at the time step
t
.
With the time-varying
P_{t}
and
r_{t}
, the funnels constructed by (
26
) is invariant for any solution
\eta(\cdot)
of (
18
), if
\eta_{0}\in\mathcal{E}_{\eta_{0}}
, then
\eta_{t}\in\mathcal{E}_{\eta_{t}}
, for all
t\in\mathcal{I}_{N}^{0}
.

For funnel-based design, apart from control synthesis using the DMI in (
19
), we also need to find the maximum invariant funnels for states and control inputs of the virtual system (
11
) within feasible state and input spaces
\hat{\mathcal{X}}
and
\hat{\mathcal{U}}
, respectively.
In this paper, we use linear polytopes constructed by several planar surfaces to approximate these two spaces, which are obtained by linearization around the nominal trajectories (way points)
\{\bar{x}_{t}\}_{t=0}^{N}
and
\{\bar{u}_{t}\}_{t=0}^{N-1}
, respectively, as follows
[
35
]
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\hat{x}_{t}}=\{x\ |(a_{it}^{x})^{\top}\hat{x}_{t}\leq b_{it}^{x},\ i\in\mathcal{I}_{l_{x}}\},\ \forall t\in\mathcal{I}_{N}^{0}

(27a)

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\hat{u}_{rt}}=\{u\ |(a_{jt}^{u})^{\top}\hat{u}_{rt}\leq b_{jt}^{u},\ j\in\mathcal{I}_{l_{u}}\},\ \forall t\in\mathcal{I}_{N-1}^{0}.

(27b)

In this case, the inclusions
\mathcal{P}_{\hat{x}_{t}}\subseteq\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{P}_{\hat{u}_{rt}}\subseteq\mathcal{U}
hold, since the functions
h_{i}
and
g_{j}
are concave, for all
i\in\mathcal{I}_{l_{x}}
and
j\in\mathcal{I}_{l_{u}}
.

Then, the following lemma can be established to ensure the maximal volume ellipsoid within a linear polytope region.

Lemma 1
.

With
P_{t}
solved by the DMI in (
19
), for the state tracking error
\eta_{t}:=\hat{x}_{t}-\bar{x}_{t}
in (
15
), where the states
\hat{x}_{t}\in\mathcal{P}_{\hat{x}_{t}}
, for all
t\in\mathcal{I}_{N}^{0}
,
the following condition

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}(b_{it}^{x}-(a_{it}^{x})^{\top}\bar{x}_{t})^{2}r_{t}&(a_{it}^{x})^{\top}P_{t}\\
P_{t}a_{it}^{x}&P_{t}\end{bmatrix}\geq 0,\ \forall i\in\mathcal{I}_{l_{x}},

(28)

ensures the maximal volume ellipsoid
\max_{\eta_{t}}\{\mathcal{E}_{\eta_{t}}\}
(
26
) is within the linear polytope
\mathcal{P}_{\hat{x}_{t}}
, i.e.,
\bar{x}_{t}\oplus\max_{\eta_{t}}\{\mathcal{E}_{\eta_{t}}\}\subseteq\mathcal{P}_{\hat{x}_{t}}
, for all
t\in\mathcal{I}_{N}^{0}
.

Proof.

For all state tracking errors
\eta_{t}:=\hat{x}_{t}-\bar{x}_{t}
satisfying the ellipsoidal constraint in (
26
), where the states
\hat{x}_{t}
in this error stay within the polytope
\mathcal{P}_{\hat{x}_{t}}
, we have:

\displaystyle(a_{it}^{x})^{\top}(\bar{x}_{t}+\eta_{t})\leq b_{it}^{x},\ \ \forall\eta_{t}\in\mathcal{E}_{\eta_{t}},

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\

\displaystyle(a_{it}^{x})^{\top}\bar{x}_{t}+\max_{\eta_{t}\in\mathcal{E}_{\eta_{t}}}(a_{it}^{x})^{\top}\eta_{t}\leq b_{it}^{x},

(29)

for all
t\in\mathcal{I}_{N}^{0}
and
i\in\mathcal{I}_{l_{x}}
.

Define
z_{t}:=P_{t}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\eta_{t}
. Then,
\eta_{t}=P_{t}^{\frac{1}{2}}z_{t}
and the ellipsoidal constraint in (
26
) can be equivalently written as
\mathcal{E}_{z_{t}}:=\{z_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\ |\ z_{t}^{\top}z_{t}\leq\frac{1}{r_{t}}\}
. Consequently,

\displaystyle\max_{\eta_{t}\in\mathcal{E}_{\eta_{t}}}(a_{it}^{x})^{\top}\eta_{t}=\max_{z_{t}\in\mathcal{E}_{z_{t}}}(a_{it}^{x})^{\top}P_{t}^{\frac{1}{2}}z_{t},

(30)

for all
t\in\mathcal{I}_{N}^{0}
and
i\in\mathcal{I}_{l_{x}}
.

By applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we have:

\displaystyle\max_{z_{t}\in\mathcal{E}_{z_{t}}}(a_{it}^{x})^{\top}P_{t}^{\frac{1}{2}}z_{t}\leq\max_{z_{t}\in\mathcal{E}_{z_{t}}}|P_{t}^{\frac{1}{2}}a_{it}^{x}||z_{t}|,

(31)

for all
t\in\mathcal{I}_{N}^{0}
and
i\in\mathcal{I}_{l_{x}}
, where the equality condition holds when the vectors
P_{t}^{\frac{1}{2}}a_{it}^{x}
and
z_{t}
are parallel, i.e.,
z_{t}=\mu_{t}P_{t}^{\frac{1}{2}}a_{it}^{x}
with some
\mu_{t}\in\mathbb{R}
, in which
\mu_{t}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{r_{t}}|P_{t}^{\frac{1}{2}}a_{it}^{x}|}
, since
\max_{z_{t}}z_{t}^{\top}z_{t}=\frac{1}{r_{t}}
.

Therefore, from (
31
), we have:

LHS of
(
31
)

\displaystyle=\max_{z_{t}\in\mathcal{E}_{z_{t}}}|P_{t}^{\frac{1}{2}}a_{it}^{x}||z_{t}|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{r_{t}}}|P_{t}^{\frac{1}{2}}a_{it}^{x}|=\frac{1}{\sqrt{r_{t}}}\sqrt{(a_{it}^{x})^{\top}P_{t}a_{it}^{x}},

(32)

for all
t\in\mathcal{I}_{N}^{0}
and
i\in\mathcal{I}_{l_{x}}
.

Substituting this into (
IV-C
), we have:

\displaystyle(a_{it}^{x})^{\top}\bar{x}_{t}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{r_{t}}}\sqrt{(a_{it}^{x})^{\top}P_{t}a_{it}^{x}}\leq b_{it}^{x},

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\ \

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{r_{t}}}\sqrt{(a_{it}^{x})^{\top}P_{t}a_{it}^{x}}\leq b_{it}^{x}-(a_{it}^{x})^{\top}\bar{x}_{t},

(33)

for all
t\in\mathcal{I}_{N}^{0}
and
i\in\mathcal{I}_{l_{x}}
.
Then, squaring both sides and using Schur complement, the above condition is equivalent to that in (
28
). This completes the proof.
∎

With

Proof.

Similar to the proof of Lem.
1
, we still use the state transformation

, for all

Then, the ellipsoid of the control input is
\mathcal{E}_{\xi_{t}}=\{K_{t}\eta_{t}\ |\ \eta_{t}^{\top}P_{t}^{-1}\eta_{t}\leq\frac{1}{r_{t}}\}
, for all

With the transformed state
z_{t}
, the control input
\xi_{t}

\displaystyle\xi_{t}=K_{t}\eta_{t}=M_{t}z_{t},

Using singular value decomposition (SVD) of

\displaystyle M_{t}=U_{rt}\Sigma_{rt}V_{rt}^{\top},

where

To prove (

), we consider two sets

To show this, we discuss both sets separately.

1) For the set

, take any

\displaystyle z_{t}=V_{rt}y_{t}+z_{t}^{\perp},

where

and
z_{t}^{\perp}\in\ker(V_{rt}^{\top})
, i.e.,
V_{rt}^{\top}z_{t}^{\perp}=\mathbf{0}
. Since the columns of
V_{rt}
are orthonormal, the condition
|y_{t}|^{2}\leq|z_{t}|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{r_{t}}
is satisfied.

Now, we have:

\displaystyle M_{t}z_{t}=U_{rt}\Sigma_{rt}V_{rt}^{\top}z_{t}=U_{rt}\Sigma_{rt}y_{t},

(39)

and thus, every element in the set
\mathcal{A}
is
U_{rt}\Sigma_{rt}y_{t}
for some
y_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{c_{t}}
with
|y_{t}|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{r_{t}}
. In other words, the set
\mathcal{A}
is equivalent to the set
\{U_{rt}\Sigma_{rt}y_{t}\ |\ |y_{t}|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{r_{t}},\ y_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{c_{t}}\}
.

2) For the set
\mathcal{B}
, take any
\zeta_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
with
|\zeta_{t}|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{r_{t}}
. Let
\omega_{t}:=U_{rt}^{\top}\zeta_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{c_{t}}
. Then,
|\omega_{t}|^{2}\leq|\zeta_{t}|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{r_{t}}
is satisfied, since
U_{rt}
has orthonormal columns.

With the
M_{t}
matrix in (
37
), we have:

\displaystyle(M_{t}M_{t}^{\top})^{\frac{1}{2}}\zeta_{t}=U_{rt}\Sigma_{rt}U_{rt}^{\top}\zeta_{t}=U_{rt}\Sigma_{rt}\omega_{t},

(40)

and thus, every element in the set
\mathcal{B}
is
U_{rt}\Sigma_{rt}\omega_{t}
for some
\omega_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{c_{t}}
with
|\omega_{t}|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{r_{t}}
. Therefore, the set
\mathcal{B}
is equivalent to the set
\{U_{rt}\Sigma_{rt}\omega_{t}\ |\ |\omega_{t}|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{r_{t}},\ \omega_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{c_{t}}\}
.

Combining the above cases 1) and 2), it is readily shown that the sets
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{B}
. This completes the proof of the selection of control input ellipsoid (
34
).

With the ellipsoidal space of control input (
34
), the proof of the input constraint (
35
) follows a similar procedure as our proof of the state constraint in Thm.
3
, which can be readily shown and omitted here. This completes the proof.

Remark 9
.

In Thm.
3

can be selected satisfying the two cases in (

Now, with the invariant funnels obtained in Thm.
3
and Schur complement, the funnel-based control synthesis problem can be formulated as:

\displaystyle\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}P_{t},\mu_{t}^{P},\mu_{t}^{K},\\

\displaystyle 0<P_{t}\leq\mu_{t}^{P}\mathbf{I},\ \forall t\in\mathcal{I}_{N}^{0},

(50b)

\displaystyle\eqref{Eq:funnel_size_r_t},\ \eqref{Eq:DMI_main_condition},\ \eqref{Eq:state_funnel+state_constraints},\mbox{ and }\eqref{Eq:input_funnel+input_constraints},

\displaystyle P_{0}\geq r_{0}P_{i},\ P_{N}\leq r_{f}P_{f},

(50e)

where the

-gain

,

VI
Conclusion

In this paper, we proposed a funnel-based control method for system recovery with unknown dynamics. By transforming the system dynamics into the Lur’e type dynamics, the Recurrent Equilibrium Network (REN) was applied to learn the unknown dynamics caused by attacks or failures. Considering the system dynamics with this learned unknown dynamics, we designed the funnel-based recovery controller and the internal and
L_{2}
stability of the closed-loop system were proved. Furthermore, the invariant funnels were derived, where the states were ensured to stay within the funnels along the nominal trajectory. Moreover, we provided the online implementation of our proposed funnel-based controller. Simulation results showed the effectiveness of our proposed method.
Future work may consider decentralized funnel-based control design and the learning of REN model, so that the scalability can be improved.

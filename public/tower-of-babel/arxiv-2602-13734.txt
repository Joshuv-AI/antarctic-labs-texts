1.
Introduction

The Cauchy transform on a planar domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}
,

(C_{\Omega}f)(z)=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\Omega}\frac{f(w)}{z-w}\,dA(w),

is a fundamental operator in complex analysis and elliptic PDE. It gives a
canonical solution operator for the
\bar{\partial}
-equation in the sense that

\bar{\partial}\,(C_{\Omega}f)=f\qquad\text{in }\Omega

(in the distributional sense, and pointwise whenever
f
is sufficiently
regular). Consequently, sharp bounds for the operator norm

\|C_{\Omega}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)\to L^{2}(\Omega)}
are closely connected to
quantitative
\bar{\partial}
estimates and to spectral data of associated
elliptic boundary value problems.

On smoothly bounded connected domains one has the spectral lower bound

\|C_{\Omega}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)\to L^{2}(\Omega)}\geq\frac{2}{\sqrt{\lambda_{1}(\Omega)}},

where
\lambda_{1}(\Omega)
denotes the first Dirichlet eigenvalue of
-\Delta

on
\Omega
; see
[
2
]
. More precisely, the
estimate can be expressed in the equivalent form

Proposition 1

(Norm estimate for the Cauchy transform)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a smoothly bounded domain. Then

\left\|\frac{C_{\Omega}^{*}C_{\Omega}}{4}\right\|=\frac{1}{4}\,\|C_{\Omega}\|^{2}\geq\frac{1}{\lambda_{1}(\Omega)},

where
\lambda_{1}(\Omega)
is the smallest positive eigenvalue for the boundary
value problem
(1.2)
in
\Omega
.

For the unit disk
D=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\ |z|<1\}
, Anderson and Hinkkanen computed the
exact
L^{2}
-operator norm. If
j_{0,1}
denotes the smallest positive zero of
the Bessel function
J_{0}
, then

\|C_{D}\|_{L^{2}(D)\to L^{2}(D)}=\frac{2}{j_{0,1}},\qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad\frac{1}{4}\,\|C_{D}\|^{2}=\frac{1}{j_{0,1}^{2}}.

Since
\lambda_{1}(D)=j_{0,1}^{2}
, this shows that equality holds in the disk case:

\frac{1}{4}\,\|C_{D}\|^{2}=\frac{1}{\lambda_{1}(D)}.

We refer to
[
1
]
for the sharp constant and explicit
extremizers. In contrast, for multiply connected domains additional holomorphic
components may influence sharpness.

In this paper we compute the exact
L^{2}
-operator norm of
C_{\Omega}
when

\Omega
is the circular annulus

A(r,R)=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\ r<|z|<R\}.

Our main result identifies the norm with the first mixed eigenvalue of the
Laplacian (Neumann on
|z|=r
, Dirichlet on
|z|=R
).

Theorem 2

(Main result)
.

Let
0<r<R
. Then

\|C_{A(r,R)}\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}=\frac{2}{\sqrt{\mu_{1}^{ND}(r,R)}},

where
\mu_{1}^{ND}(r,R)
is the first eigenvalue of

-\Delta U=\mu U\ \text{in }A(r,R),\qquad\partial_{\nu}U=0\ \text{on }|z|=r,\qquad U=0\ \text{on }|z|=R.

Equivalently,
\mu_{1}^{ND}(r,R)=\kappa_{1}^{2}
, where
\kappa_{1}
is the smallest
positive root of a Bessel determinant (see Theorem
7
).

The proof exploits rotational symmetry: a Fourier decomposition reduces

C_{A(r,R)}
to a family of one-dimensional weighted Hardy-type operators on
radial profiles. A variational argument then yields a Sturm–Liouville problem
whose first eigenvalue controls the sharp operator norm; the Bessel determinant
arises from solving the radial eigenvalue equation explicitly.
The extremizers are given in terms of the corresponding mixed eigenfunction,
revealing a structural distinction between simply and doubly connected domains.

The proof of main results

Lemma 3

(Exact action on a single Fourier mode)
.

Let
A=A(r,R)=\{z:\ r<|z|<R\}
and

(C_{A}f)(z)=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{A}\frac{f(w)}{z-w}\,dA(w).

Fix
m\in\mathbb{Z}
and write
w=\rho e^{i\phi}
,
z=\rho_{0}e^{i\theta}
.
If

f(w)=g(\rho)e^{im\phi},

then
C_{A}f
lies in the
(m-1)
-st angular mode and is given by

(C_{A}f)(\rho_{0}e^{i\theta})=\begin{cases}-2\,\rho_{0}^{m-1}e^{i(m-1)\theta}\displaystyle\int_{\rho_{0}}^{R}g(\rho)\,\rho^{1-m}\,d\rho,&m\geq 1,\\[8.61108pt]
\ \ 2\,\rho_{0}^{m-1}e^{i(m-1)\theta}\displaystyle\int_{r}^{\rho_{0}}g(\rho)\,\rho^{1-m}\,d\rho,&m\leq 0.\end{cases}

Proof.

Use the geometric-series expansions of
(z-w)^{-1}
:

\frac{1}{z-w}=\frac{1}{z}\sum_{n\geq 0}\Big(\frac{w}{z}\Big)^{n},\quad|w|<|z|,\qquad\frac{1}{z-w}=-\frac{1}{w}\sum_{n\geq 0}\Big(\frac{z}{w}\Big)^{n},\quad|w|>|z|.

Insert
w=\rho e^{i\phi}
,
z=\rho_{0}e^{i\theta}
and multiply by
e^{im\phi}
.
Integrating in
\phi
over
[0,2\pi]
kills all terms except the unique one
whose angular frequency is
0
; this occurs only when
n=-m
in the case

|w|<|z|
(hence
m\leq 0
), and only when
n=m-1
in the case
|w|>|z|

(hence
m\geq 1
). The stated formulas follow after the remaining radial
integration.
∎

Lemma 4

(Weighted Hardy reduction for
m\geq 1
)
.

Fix
m\geq 1
and set

(T_{m}g)(\rho):=-2\,\rho^{m-1}\int_{\rho}^{R}g(t)\,t^{1-m}\,dt,\qquad r<\rho<R.

Then
\|C_{A}\|_{H_{m}\to H_{m-1}}=\|T_{m}\|
when both
H_{m},H_{m-1}
are equipped
with the
L^{2}(A)
norm.
Moreover, if
F(\rho):=\int_{\rho}^{R}g(t)\,t^{1-m}\,dt
(so
F(R)=0
and

F^{\prime}(\rho)=-g(\rho)\rho^{1-m}
), then

\frac{\|T_{m}g\|_{L^{2}((r,R),\rho\,d\rho)}^{2}}{\|g\|_{L^{2}((r,R),\rho\,d\rho)}^{2}}=\frac{4\int_{r}^{R}|F(\rho)|^{2}\,\rho^{2m-1}\,d\rho}{\int_{r}^{R}|F^{\prime}(\rho)|^{2}\,\rho^{2m-1}\,d\rho}.

Hence

\|T_{m}\|=\frac{2}{\sqrt{\mu_{m}}},

where
\mu_{m}
is the first eigenvalue of

(1)

-(\rho^{2m-1}F^{\prime}(\rho))^{\prime}=\mu\,\rho^{2m-1}F(\rho),\quad r<\rho<R,\qquad F^{\prime}(r)=0,\ \ F(R)=0.

Proof.

The mode formula in Lemma
3
shows that the restriction
of
C_{A}
to mode
m
is exactly
T_{m}
on the radial profile.
The
L^{2}(A)
norm of
g(\rho)e^{im\theta}
is
2\pi\int_{r}^{R}|g(\rho)|^{2}\rho\,d\rho
,
and similarly for the output mode, so the operator norm reduces to
\|T_{m}\|
.
The Rayleigh quotient in
F
is immediate from
T_{m}g=-2\rho^{m-1}F
and

g=-\rho^{m-1}F^{\prime}
, and the Euler–Lagrange equation yields (
1
)
with the natural boundary condition
F^{\prime}(r)=0
. Here are the details of the last step:

Let
w(\rho):=\rho^{2m-1}
. From the previous step we arrive at the Rayleigh
quotient

\frac{\|T_{m}g\|_{L^{2}((r,R),\rho\,d\rho)}^{2}}{\|g\|_{L^{2}((r,R),\rho\,d\rho)}^{2}}=\frac{4\int_{r}^{R}|F(\rho)|^{2}\,w(\rho)\,d\rho}{\int_{r}^{R}|F^{\prime}(\rho)|^{2}\,w(\rho)\,d\rho}.

Thus maximizing the left-hand side is equivalent to minimizing

\mathcal{R}[F]:=\frac{\int_{r}^{R}|F^{\prime}(\rho)|^{2}\,w(\rho)\,d\rho}{\int_{r}^{R}|F(\rho)|^{2}\,w(\rho)\,d\rho}\quad\text{over }F\not\equiv 0\text{ with }F(R)=0.

Introduce a Lagrange multiplier
\mu
and consider

\mathcal{J}[F]:=\int_{r}^{R}\Big(|F^{\prime}(\rho)|^{2}-\mu\,|F(\rho)|^{2}\Big)\,w(\rho)\,d\rho,\qquad F(R)=0.

Take a variation
F_{\varepsilon}=F+\varepsilon\varphi
with
\varphi(R)=0
.
Then

\left.\frac{d}{d\varepsilon}\right|_{\varepsilon=0}\mathcal{J}[F_{\varepsilon}]=2\int_{r}^{R}\big(F^{\prime}(\rho)\varphi^{\prime}(\rho)-\mu F(\rho)\varphi(\rho)\big)\,w(\rho)\,d\rho.

Integrating by parts gives

2\int_{r}^{R}F^{\prime}\varphi^{\prime}\,w\,d\rho=2\Big[w(\rho)F^{\prime}(\rho)\varphi(\rho)\Big]_{r}^{R}-2\int_{r}^{R}(w(\rho)F^{\prime}(\rho))^{\prime}\,\varphi(\rho)\,d\rho,

hence

\left.\frac{d}{d\varepsilon}\right|_{\varepsilon=0}\mathcal{J}[F_{\varepsilon}]=2\Big[w(\rho)F^{\prime}(\rho)\varphi(\rho)\Big]_{r}^{R}-2\int_{r}^{R}\Big((wF^{\prime})^{\prime}+\mu wF\Big)\varphi(\rho)\,d\rho.

Since
\varphi(R)=0
, the boundary term at
R
vanishes. At
\rho=r
the
value
F(r)
is free, so
\varphi(r)
is arbitrary; therefore the remaining
boundary term forces

w(r)F^{\prime}(r)=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad F^{\prime}(r)=0\qquad(\text{since }w(r)=r^{2m-1}>0).

Because
\varphi
is arbitrary in
(r,R)
, the integral term yields the
Euler–Lagrange equation

(w(\rho)F^{\prime}(\rho))^{\prime}+\mu\,w(\rho)F(\rho)=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad-(\rho^{2m-1}F^{\prime}(\rho))^{\prime}=\mu\,\rho^{2m-1}F(\rho),

together with the boundary conditions
F^{\prime}(r)=0
and
F(R)=0
. This is
exactly the Sturm–Liouville problem (
1
).

∎

Bessel functions and the characteristic equation.

The symbols
J_{k}
and
Y_{k}
denote the Bessel functions of the first and
second kind (Neumann functions) of order
k
. They form a fundamental pair of
linearly independent solutions of Bessel’s differential equation

x^{2}y^{\prime\prime}(x)+xy^{\prime}(x)+\bigl(x^{2}-k^{2}\bigr)y(x)=0.

The function
J_{k}
is the solution that is finite at
x=0
, while
Y_{k}
is
the second independent solution, which is singular at
x=0
for
k\geq 0
.
In the present annulus problem, separation of variables reduces the mixed
boundary-value eigenproblem for the Laplacian to a radial Bessel ODE. Imposing
the Neumann condition at
|z|=r
and the Dirichlet condition at
|z|=R

produces a nontrivial solution precisely when a certain Bessel determinant
vanishes; for the principal mode this takes the form

J_{1}(\kappa r)Y_{0}(\kappa R)-Y_{1}(\kappa r)J_{0}(\kappa R)=0,

and the smallest positive root
\kappa
determines the sharp norm constant via

\|C_{A(r,R)}\|_{2\to 2}=2/\kappa
.

Lemma 5

(Bessel form for
m\geq 1
)
.

Let
m\geq 1
and write
\mu_{m}=\kappa_{m,1}^{2}
with
\kappa_{m,1}>0
.
Then
\kappa_{m,1}
is the smallest positive solution of

(2)

J_{m}(\kappa r)\,Y_{m-1}(\kappa R)-Y_{m}(\kappa r)\,J_{m-1}(\kappa R)=0,

and

\|C_{A}\|_{H_{m}\to H_{m-1}}=\|T_{m}\|=\frac{2}{\kappa_{m,1}}.

In particular, for
m=1
one gets (
2
) as

J_{1}(\kappa r)\,Y_{0}(\kappa R)-Y_{1}(\kappa r)\,J_{0}(\kappa R)=0.

Proof.

Set
U(\rho):=\rho^{m-1}F(\rho)
. Then (
1
) is equivalent to the
Bessel-type equation

U^{\prime\prime}(\rho)+\frac{1}{\rho}U^{\prime}(\rho)+\Big(\kappa^{2}-\frac{(m-1)^{2}}{\rho^{2}}\Big)U(\rho)=0,

so
U(\rho)=aJ_{m-1}(\kappa\rho)+bY_{m-1}(\kappa\rho)
.
The boundary condition
F(R)=0
becomes
U(R)=0
.
The condition
F^{\prime}(r)=0
becomes
rU^{\prime}(r)=(m-1)U(r)
, which is equivalent to

aJ_{m}(\kappa r)+bY_{m}(\kappa r)=0
by the standard identity

xJ^{\prime}_{m-1}(x)-(m-1)J_{m-1}(x)=-xJ_{m}(x)
(and the analogous one for
Y
).
The existence of a nontrivial
(a,b)
gives (
2
).
Finally, Lemma
4
gives
\|T_{m}\|=2/\sqrt{\mu_{m}}=2/\kappa_{m,1}
.
∎

Lemma 6
.

For
m\geq 1
, let
\mu_{m}
be the first eigenvalue of (
1
).
Then
\mu_{1}\leq\mu_{m}
for all
m\geq 1
, and hence

\sup_{m\geq 1}\|C_{A}\|_{H_{m}\to H_{m-1}}=\|C_{A}\|_{H_{1}\to H_{0}}=\frac{2}{\sqrt{\mu_{1}}}=\frac{2}{\kappa_{1,1}}.

Proof.

Rewrite the quotient in Lemma
4
using
U(\rho)=\rho^{m-1}F(\rho)
:
a direct computation gives

\int_{r}^{R}|F^{\prime}(\rho)|^{2}\rho^{2m-1}\,d\rho=\int_{r}^{R}\Big(|U^{\prime}(\rho)|^{2}\rho+\frac{(m-1)^{2}}{\rho}|U(\rho)|^{2}\Big)\,d\rho+(m-1)|U(r)|^{2},

while

\int_{r}^{R}|F(\rho)|^{2}\rho^{2m-1}\,d\rho=\int_{r}^{R}|U(\rho)|^{2}\rho\,d\rho.

Therefore
\mu_{m}
admits the variational characterization

\mu_{m}=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}U\in H^{1}(r,R),\\
U(R)=0,\ U\neq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{\displaystyle\int_{r}^{R}\Big(|U^{\prime}|^{2}\rho+\frac{(m-1)^{2}}{\rho}|U|^{2}\Big)\,d\rho+(m-1)|U(r)|^{2}}{\displaystyle\int_{r}^{R}|U|^{2}\rho\,d\rho}.

For
m=1
the numerator becomes
\int_{r}^{R}|U^{\prime}|^{2}\rho\,d\rho
(no potential term
and no boundary term). Hence for every admissible
U
,

\frac{\int_{r}^{R}\Big(|U^{\prime}|^{2}\rho+\frac{(m-1)^{2}}{\rho}|U|^{2}\Big)\,d\rho+(m-1)|U(r)|^{2}}{\int_{r}^{R}|U|^{2}\rho\,d\rho}\ \geq\ \frac{\int_{r}^{R}|U^{\prime}|^{2}\rho\,d\rho}{\int_{r}^{R}|U|^{2}\rho\,d\rho}.

Taking the infimum over the same class
\{U\in H^{1}:U(R)=0\}
yields
\mu_{m}\geq\mu_{1}
.
∎

Theorem 7

(Sharp
L^{2}
-norm on a circular annulus)
.

Let
0<r<R
,
A=A(r,R)=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\ r<|z|<R\}
, and

(C_{A}f)(z)=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{A}\frac{f(w)}{z-w}\,dA(w),\qquad f\in L^{2}(A).

Then

\|C_{A}\|_{L^{2}(A)\to L^{2}(A)}=\frac{2}{\kappa_{1}},

where
\kappa_{1}>0
is the smallest positive solution of

(3)

J_{1}(\kappa r)\,Y_{0}(\kappa R)-Y_{1}(\kappa r)\,J_{0}(\kappa R)=0.

Proof.

By Lemmas
3
and
4
,

\|C_{A}\|=\sup_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\|C_{A}\|_{H_{m}\to H_{m-1}},\qquad\|C_{A}\|_{H_{m}\to H_{m-1}}=\begin{cases}\|T_{m}\|,&m\geq 1,\\
\|T_{1-m}\|,&m\leq 0,\end{cases}

and for
m\geq 1
,

\|T_{m}\|=\frac{2}{\sqrt{\mu_{m}}},

where
\mu_{m}
is the first eigenvalue of (
1
).
Lemma
6
gives
\mu_{m}\geq\mu_{1}
for all
m\geq 1
, hence the
supremum is attained at
m=1
and

\|C_{A}\|=\|T_{1}\|=\frac{2}{\sqrt{\mu_{1}}}.

Finally, Lemma
5
(with
m=1
) yields
\mu_{1}=\kappa_{1}^{2}
, where

\kappa_{1}
is the smallest positive root of (
3
). Therefore

\|C_{A}\|=2/\kappa_{1}
.

Moreover, if
F>0
is the first eigenfunction of (
1
) for
m=1
and

f(\rho e^{i\theta})=-F^{\prime}(\rho)e^{i\theta}
, then Lemma
3
gives

(C_{A}f)(\rho e^{i\theta})=2F(\rho),

so
f
is an extremizer, see remark below for details.
∎

Remark 8

(An explicit extremizer and the derivative computation)
.

Let
\kappa_{1}>0
be the smallest positive solution of (
3
). Define

F(\rho):=c\Big(Y_{0}(\kappa_{1}R)\,J_{0}(\kappa_{1}\rho)-J_{0}(\kappa_{1}R)\,Y_{0}(\kappa_{1}\rho)\Big),\qquad r<\rho<R,

where
c\neq 0
is chosen so that
F>0
on
(r,R)
. Then
F(R)=0
holds by
construction. Moreover, using the identities
J_{0}^{\prime}(x)=-J_{1}(x)
and

Y_{0}^{\prime}(x)=-Y_{1}(x)
together with the chain rule, we compute

\displaystyle F^{\prime}(\rho)

\displaystyle=c\Big(Y_{0}(\kappa_{1}R)\,\frac{d}{d\rho}J_{0}(\kappa_{1}\rho)-J_{0}(\kappa_{1}R)\,\frac{d}{d\rho}Y_{0}(\kappa_{1}\rho)\Big)

\displaystyle=c\,\kappa_{1}\Big(Y_{0}(\kappa_{1}R)\,J_{0}^{\prime}(\kappa_{1}\rho)-J_{0}(\kappa_{1}R)\,Y_{0}^{\prime}(\kappa_{1}\rho)\Big)

\displaystyle=c\,\kappa_{1}\Big(J_{0}(\kappa_{1}R)\,Y_{1}(\kappa_{1}\rho)-Y_{0}(\kappa_{1}R)\,J_{1}(\kappa_{1}\rho)\Big).

In particular,

F^{\prime}(r)=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad J_{1}(\kappa_{1}r)\,Y_{0}(\kappa_{1}R)-Y_{1}(\kappa_{1}r)\,J_{0}(\kappa_{1}R)=0,

so
\kappa_{1}
is precisely the parameter for which the boundary conditions

F^{\prime}(r)=0
and
F(R)=0
are satisfied. Consequently,

f(\rho e^{i\theta})=-F^{\prime}(\rho)\,e^{i\theta}

is an extremizer for
\|C_{A}\|
in Theorem
7
.

(On the graph of

.

3.
Dirichlet–Cauchy operator and its sharp
L^{2}
norm

Fix an annulus with radii
0<r<R
. For
g\in L^{2}(A)
, let
u
be the (weak) solution of the zero–Dirichlet Poisson problem

(4)

\begin{cases}-\Delta u=g&\text{in }A,\\
u=0&\text{on }\partial A,\end{cases}

and define the
Dirichlet–Cauchy operator
by

(5)

\mathcal{C}_{A}[g](z):=\partial_{z}u(z),\qquad\partial_{z}=\tfrac{1}{2}(\partial_{x}-i\partial_{y}).

Equivalently, in terms of the Green function
G_{A}(\cdot,\tau)
for (
4
),

(6)

u(z)=\int_{A}G_{A}(z,\tau)\,g(\tau)\,dA(\tau),\qquad\mathcal{C}_{A}[g](z)=\int_{A}\partial_{z}G_{A}(z,\tau)\,g(\tau)\,dA(\tau).

Let
\lambda_{1}(A)
be the first Dirichlet eigenvalue of
-\Delta
on
A
and set

(7)

k_{1}:=\sqrt{\lambda_{1}(A)}.

In the planar case,
k_{1}
is characterized as the smallest positive root of the Bessel cross–product equation

(8)

J_{0}(kr)\,Y_{0}(kR)-J_{0}(kR)\,Y_{0}(kr)=0.

Theorem 10

(
[
8
]
)
.

The Dirichlet–Cauchy operator (
5
) extends boundedly on
L^{2}(A)
and satisfies the sharp estimate

(9)

\|\mathcal{C}_{A}\|_{L^{2}(A)\to L^{2}(A)}=\frac{2}{k_{1}}.

Remark 11

(Normalization)
.

The constant stated in
[
8
]
appears as
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}\,k_{1}}
.
This is a normalization effect: in complex notation one has

\Delta=4\,\partial_{z}\partial_{\bar{z}},

so writing the Poisson equation as
u_{z\bar{z}}=g
corresponds to
\Delta u=4g
.
In addition, some authors define the transform as
\partial_{z}u
while others use
2\,\partial_{z}u

(or estimate
\nabla u
instead of
\partial_{z}u
), which rescales the operator norm by fixed factors.
After translating between these conventions, the sharp constants are consistent.

When

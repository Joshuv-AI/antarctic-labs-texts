Closed-form formulas in number-conserved pairing theory
G. J. Fu

Abstract
In this work, I present closed-form formulas for the norm and many-body density matrices between general wave functions with exact particle numbers in pairing theory, using properties of the generalized Kronecker delta. These formulas, expressed as sums of minors and Pfaffians, apply to both even and odd particle-number systems and accommodate pair condensate as well as broken-pair configurations. This formalism directly facilitates applications in the generator coordinate method and symmetry restoration techniques, including angular momentum projection.



In the physics community, pairing theory, such as the Bardeen-Cooper-Schrieffer (BCS) theory and the Hartree-Fock-Bogoliubov (HFB) approach, has been widely used to describe quantum many-body systems.
While BCS and HFB approaches break U(1) symmetry, this violation has a minimal impact in large systems treated within the grand canonical ensemble.
However, for finite-size systems, such as atomic nuclei, ultra-cold atoms, and ultrasmall superconducting grains,
particle-number conservation is crucial for accurately capturing intrinsic properties and quantum phase transitions.

To restore particle-number conservation, one common approach is the number-projected BCS/HFB approach, which involves numerical integration over the gauge angle
Ring and Schuck 2004
;
Dietrich et al. 1964
.
Alternatively, methods that directly preserve an exact particle number, such as the
N
-pair condensate, offer a more straightforward description.
In the
N
-pair condensate formalism, the state is defined as:

\displaystyle\ket{\rm{c}}\equiv\left(\hat{P}^{{}^{\dagger}}\right)^{N}\ket{},

(1)

where
N
is the number of pairs,
\ket{}
is the vacuum state, and
\hat{P}^{{}^{\dagger}}
is the creation operator for a Cooper pair in a canonical basis of dimension
\omega=2\Omega
, defined as

\displaystyle\hat{P}^{{}^{\dagger}}\equiv\sum_{\alpha=1}^{\Omega}v_{\alpha}\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{\alpha}\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{\tilde{\alpha}},

(2)

where
v_{\alpha}
is the pair coefficient.
The energy of the pair condensate can be computed based on recursive formulas derived from the generalized Wick theorem and the commutation relations between Cooper pairs
Talmi 1993
;
Talmi 1971
;
Jin-Quan et al. 1993
;
Chen 1993
;
Sandulescu et al. 2009
;
Jia 2017
;
Jia 2019
;
He et al. 2020
;
Lei et al. 2021
;
Lu et al. 2022
;
Ma et al. 2023
.
Nevertheless, in number-conserved pairing theory, computations are generally more complex than in the standard BCS and HFB approaches.

Restoring good angular momentum in number-conserved pairing theory presents an additional challenge, as both BCS and HFB break rotational symmetry, a key feature of self-organizing systems like atoms and atomic nuclei.
To recover good angular momentum, integration over the Euler angles or the linear algebra projection
Johnson and O’Mara 2017
;
Johnson and Jiao 2019
is required.
For instance, the configuration space is constructed by projecting angular momentum onto two different pair condensates,
\ket{\rm{c}}
and
|{\rm c}^{\prime}\rangle
.
The Hamiltonian and norm matrices are then computed as

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}^{J}_{K^{\prime}K}=

\displaystyle\frac{2J+1}{8\pi^{2}}\int{\rm d}\Omega~D^{J*}_{K^{\prime}K}(\Omega)\langle{\rm c}|\hat{H}\hat{R}(\Omega)|{\rm c}^{\prime}\rangle,

(3)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{N}}^{J}_{K^{\prime}K}=

\displaystyle\frac{2J+1}{8\pi^{2}}\int{\rm d}\Omega~D^{J*}_{K^{\prime}K}(\Omega)\langle{\rm c}|\hat{R}(\Omega)|{\rm c}^{\prime}\rangle,

where
D^{J}_{K^{\prime}K}(\Omega)
is the Wigner-
D
matrix, and
\hat{R}(\Omega)
is the rotation operator.
These considerations further increase the computational complexity.

This work presents closed-form formulas for evaluating norms and matrix elements of many-body operators between wave functions with exact particle numbers, valid for both even and odd particle numbers. The derivations account for broken pairs (i.e., unpaired particles) and configuration mixing between different pair condensates and canonical bases (alternatively, the generator coordinate method).

Starting from Eq. (
1
), I extend the formalism to a more general case of a broken-pair state with
n
particles in a canonical basis:

\displaystyle\ket{\alpha}\equiv\left(\hat{P}^{{}^{\dagger}}\right)^{N}\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{\alpha_{1}}\cdots\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{\alpha_{\nu}}\ket{},

(4)

where
n=2N+\nu
,
and
\nu
is the number of unpaired particles, typically referred to as the seniority number in nuclear physics
Racah 1942
;
Racah 1943
.
Eq. (
4
) accommodates both even and odd particle numbers depending on the parity of
n
or
\nu
.
The canonical basis can be expressed as a unitary transformation of an orthonormal reference basis:

\displaystyle\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{\alpha}=\sum_{i}U_{\alpha i}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{i},

(5)

where
U
is the unitary matrix.
The rotation operator
\hat{R}
transforms the canonical basis as

\displaystyle U~\overset{\hat{R}}{\longrightarrow}~W=UD^{\intercal},

(6)

where
D^{\intercal}
is the transpose of the Wigner-
D
matrix for single-particle states.
Using Eqs. (
2
), (
4
), and (
6
), the norm between two different broken-pair states can be written as

\displaystyle\langle\alpha|\hat{R}|\beta^{\prime}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1}\cdots i_{N}\\
j_{1}\cdots j_{N}\end{subarray}}v_{i_{1}}\cdots v_{i_{N}}v^{\prime}_{j_{1}}\cdots v^{\prime}_{j_{N}}\sum_{k_{1}\cdots k_{n}}\delta_{k_{1}\cdots k_{n}}^{j_{1}\tilde{j}_{1}\cdots j_{N}\tilde{j}_{N}\beta_{1}\cdots\beta_{\nu}}X_{i_{1}k_{1}}X_{\tilde{i}_{1}k_{2}}\cdots X_{i_{N}k_{2N-1}}X_{\tilde{i}_{N}k_{2N}}X_{\alpha_{1}k_{2N+1}}\cdots X_{\alpha_{\nu}k_{n}},

(7)

where
X\equiv U^{*}W^{\prime\intercal}
, and
\delta_{a_{1}\cdots a_{n}}^{b_{1}\cdots b_{n}}
is the generalized Kronecker delta:
\delta_{a_{1}\cdots a_{n}}^{b_{1}\cdots b_{n}}=+1
(
-1
) if
a_{1},\cdots,a_{n}
are distinct and form an even (odd) permutation of
b_{1},\cdots,b_{n}
, and
=0
otherwise.
The summation of the product of the generalized Kronecker delta and the matrix elements of
X
can be replaced with a minor of
X
, leading to a simplified expression for the norm:

\displaystyle\langle\alpha|\hat{R}|\beta^{\prime}\rangle=(N!)^{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\{i_{1}\cdots i_{N}\}\\
\{j_{1}\cdots j_{N}\}\end{subarray}}v_{i_{1}}\cdots v_{i_{N}}v^{\prime}_{j_{1}}\cdots v^{\prime}_{j_{N}}\det\left(X_{\{i\alpha,j\beta\}}\right),

(8)

where
\det\left(X_{\{i\alpha,j\beta\}}\right)
denotes the minor, i.e., the determinant of the submatrix formed by selecting rows
i_{1},\tilde{i}_{1},\cdots,i_{N},\tilde{i}_{N},\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{\nu}
and columns
j_{1},\tilde{j}_{1},\cdots,j_{N},\tilde{j}_{N},\beta_{1},\cdots,\beta_{\nu}
of
X
.
This minor represents the overlap between the canonical bases of the states
\ket{\alpha}
and
\hat{R}\ket{\beta}^{\prime}
.
The summation
\sum_{\{i_{1}\cdots i_{N}\}}
spans all
N
-element combinations from
\{1,\cdots,\Omega\}
,
reflecting the pair correlation.
It is worth noting that, in addition to using the generalized Kronecker delta, Eq. (
8
) (and related formulas) can also be derived using Grassmann algebra
Negele and Orland 1988
;
Stryzhak 2010
.
The details of this alternative derivation are omitted here for brevity.

The norm
\langle\alpha|\hat{R}|\beta^{\prime}\rangle
can be expressed in an alternative form.
To derive this, the Cooper pair from Eq. (
2
) is written as

\displaystyle\hat{P}^{{}^{\dagger}}\equiv\frac{1}{2}\sum_{ij}\mathcal{P}_{ij}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{i}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{j},

(9)

where the matrix
\mathcal{P}=U^{\intercal}\mathcal{V}U
, and

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccccc}0&v_{1}&&&\lx@intercol\hfil\hbox{\multirowsetup \Large 0 }\hfil\lx@intercol\\
-v_{1}&0&&&\\
&&0&v_{2}&\\
&&-v_{2}&0&\\
\lx@intercol\hfil\hbox{\multirowsetup \Large 0 }\hfil\lx@intercol&\ddots&&\\
&&&&&0&v_{\Omega}\\
&&&&&-v_{\Omega}&0\end{array}\right).

This representation corresponds to the inverse process of the spectral theorem
Zumino 1962
.
Under a rotation transformation, the Cooper pair becomes

\displaystyle\hat{P}^{{}^{\dagger}}\overset{\hat{R}}{\longrightarrow}\frac{1}{2}\sum_{ij}\mathcal{Q}_{ij}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{i}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{j},

(18)

where
\mathcal{Q}=W^{\intercal}\mathcal{V}W
.
The norm is then expressed as

\displaystyle\langle\alpha|\hat{R}|\beta^{\prime}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{2N}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1}\cdots i_{2N}\\
j_{1}\cdots j_{2N}\end{subarray}}\mathcal{P}^{*}_{i_{1}i_{2}}\cdots\mathcal{P}^{*}_{i_{2N-1}i_{2N}}\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}_{j_{1}j_{2}}\cdots\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}_{j_{2N-1}j_{2N}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1}\cdots a_{\nu}\\
b_{1}\cdots b_{\nu}\end{subarray}}\delta_{i_{1}\cdots i_{2N}a_{1}\cdots a_{\nu}}^{j_{1}\cdots j_{2N}b_{1}\cdots b_{\nu}}U^{*}_{\alpha_{1}a_{1}}\cdots U^{*}_{\alpha_{\nu}a_{\nu}}W^{\prime}_{\beta_{1}b_{1}}\cdots W^{\prime}_{\beta_{\nu}b_{\nu}}

(19)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(N!)^{2}\sum_{\mathbb{C}_{n}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\{a_{1}\cdots a_{\nu}\}\subseteq\mathbb{C}_{n}\\
\{b_{1}\cdots b_{\nu}\}\subseteq\mathbb{C}_{n}\end{subarray}}{\rm sgn}(ia)~{\rm sgn}(jb)\det\left(U^{*}_{\{\alpha,a\}}\right)\det\left(W^{\prime}_{\{\beta,b\}}\right){\rm pf}\left(\mathcal{P}^{*}_{\{i\}}\right){\rm pf}\left(\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}_{\{j\}}\right),

where
\mathbb{C}_{n}
represents an
n
-element combination from
\{1,\cdots,\omega\}
, and the summation
\sum_{\mathbb{C}_{n}}
runs over all such combinations.
Each combination
\mathbb{C}_{n}
must satisfy

\displaystyle\mathbb{C}_{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{i_{1},\cdots,i_{2N},a_{1},\cdots,a_{\nu}\}

(20)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{j_{1},\cdots,j_{2N},b_{1},\cdots,b_{\nu}\}\subseteq\{1,\cdots,\omega\}.

The term
{\rm sgn}(ia)
denotes the sign of the permutation
(i_{1}\cdots i_{2N}a_{1}\cdots a_{\nu})
,
and
{\rm pf}\left(\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}_{\{j\}}\right)
represents the Pfaffian of the submatrix formed by both the rows and columns indexed by
i_{1},\cdots,i_{2N}
of
\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}
.
It is worth mentioning that in the pioneering work of Ref.
Robledo 2009
, Robledo introduced a promising Pfaffian formula to resolve the sign problem in HFB wave functions.
Following this approach, substantial efforts have been devoted to improving HFB theory calculations using Pfaffian formulas, as demonstrated in Refs
Robledo 2011
;
Bertsch and Robledo 2012
;
Gao et al. 2014
;
Mizusaki et al. 2018
;
Carlsson and Rotureau 2021
.

Next, I present a formula for the matrix element of a many-body operator between broken-pair states.
An
m
-body operator is defined as

\displaystyle\hat{O}_{m}\equiv\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{f_{1}}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{f_{2}}\cdots\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{f_{m}}\hat{a}_{g_{m}}\hat{a}_{g_{m-1}}\cdots\hat{a}_{g_{1}}.

(21)

Using Eqs. (
2
), (
4
), (
6
), and (
21
), the matrix element of the
m
-body operator is expressed in a compact form:

\displaystyle\langle\alpha|\hat{O}_{m}\hat{R}|\beta^{\prime}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\alpha|\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{f_{1}}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{f_{2}}\cdots\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{f_{m}}\hat{\mathbb{1}}~\hat{a}_{g_{m}}\hat{a}_{g_{m-1}}\cdots\hat{a}_{g_{1}}\hat{R}|\beta^{\prime}\rangle

(22)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(n-m)!}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1}\cdots i_{N}\\
j_{1}\cdots j_{N}\end{subarray}}v_{i_{1}}\cdots v_{i_{N}}v^{\prime}_{j_{1}}\cdots v^{\prime}_{j_{N}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1}\cdots a_{m}c_{1}\cdots c_{n-m}\\
b_{1}\cdots b_{m}d_{1}\cdots d_{n-m}\end{subarray}}\delta_{a_{1}\cdots a_{m}c_{1}\cdots c_{n-m}}^{i_{1}\tilde{i}_{1}\cdots i_{N}\tilde{i}_{N}\alpha_{1}\cdots\alpha_{\nu}}~\delta_{b_{1}\cdots b_{m}d_{1}\cdots d_{n-m}}^{j_{1}\tilde{j}_{1}\cdots j_{N}\tilde{j}_{N}\beta_{1}\cdots\beta_{\nu}}

\displaystyle\quad\times U^{*}_{a_{1}f_{1}}\cdots U^{*}_{a_{m}f_{m}}W^{\prime}_{b_{1}g_{1}}\cdots W^{\prime}_{b_{m}g_{m}}X_{c_{1}d_{1}}\cdots X_{c_{n-m}d_{n-m}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(N!)^{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\{i_{1}\cdots i_{N}\}\\
\{j_{1}\cdots j_{N}\}\end{subarray}}v_{i_{1}}\cdots v_{i_{N}}v^{\prime}_{j_{1}}\cdots v^{\prime}_{j_{N}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\{a_{1}\cdots a_{m}\}\\
\{b_{1}\cdots b_{m}\}\end{subarray}}{\rm sgn}(ac)~{\rm sgn}(bd)\det\left(U^{*}_{\{a,f\}}\right)\det\left(W^{\prime}_{\{b,g\}}\right)\det\left(X_{\{c,d\}}\right).

Here, I have used a resolution of the identity in the form of the completeness relation:

\displaystyle\hat{\mathbb{1}}=\sum_{k}\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{i_{1}\cdots i_{k}}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{i_{1}}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{i_{2}}\cdots\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{i_{k}}\ket{}\bra{}\hat{a}_{i_{k}}\hat{a}_{i_{k-1}}\cdots\hat{a}_{i_{1}}.

(23)

Similar to the approach used for the norm, I present an alternative expression for the matrix element of the
m
-body operator. Using Eqs. (
4
), (
6
), (
9
) and (
18
), the matrix element can be simplified further as

\displaystyle\langle\alpha|\hat{O}_{m}\hat{R}|\beta^{\prime}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{2N}(n-m)!}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1}\cdots i_{2N}\\
j_{1}\cdots j_{2N}\end{subarray}}\mathcal{P}^{*}_{i_{1}i_{2}}\cdots\mathcal{P}^{*}_{i_{2N-1}i_{2N}}\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}_{j_{1}j_{2}}\cdots\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}_{j_{2N-1}j_{2N}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1}\cdots a_{\nu}\\
b_{1}\cdots b_{\nu}\end{subarray}}\sum_{d_{1}\cdots d_{n-m}}\delta_{f_{1}\cdots f_{m}d_{1}\cdots d_{n-m}}^{i_{1}\cdots i_{2N}a_{1}\cdots a_{\nu}}~\delta_{g_{1}\cdots g_{m}d_{1}\cdots d_{n-m}}^{j_{1}\cdots j_{2N}b_{1}\cdots b_{\nu}}

(24)

\displaystyle\quad\times~U^{*}_{\alpha_{1}a_{1}}\cdots U^{*}_{\alpha_{\nu}a_{\nu}}W^{\prime}_{\beta_{1}b_{1}}\cdots W^{\prime}_{\beta_{\nu}b_{\nu}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(N!)^{2}\sum_{\mathbb{D}_{n-m}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\{a_{1}\cdots a_{\nu}\}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{n}\\
\{b_{1}\cdots b_{\nu}\}\subseteq\mathbb{G}_{n}\end{subarray}}{\rm sgn}(ia)~{\rm sgn}(jb)~{\rm sgn}(fd)~{\rm sgn}(gd)\det\left(U^{*}_{\{\alpha,a\}}\right)\det\left(W^{\prime}_{\{\beta,b\}}\right){\rm pf}\left(\mathcal{P}^{*}_{\{i\}}\right){\rm pf}\left(\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}_{\{j\}}\right),

where the set
\mathbb{D}_{n-m}
is defined as

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}_{n-m}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{d_{1},\cdots,d_{n-m}\}

\displaystyle\subseteq

\displaystyle\left[\{1,\cdots,\omega\}\setminus\left(\{f_{1},\cdots,f_{m}\}\cup\{g_{1},\cdots,g_{m}\}\right)\right],

and the sets
\mathbb{F}_{n}
and
\mathbb{G}_{n}
are defined as

\displaystyle\mathbb{F}_{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}_{n-m}\cup\{f_{1},\cdots,f_{m}\},

\displaystyle\mathbb{G}_{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}_{n-m}\cup\{g_{1},\cdots,g_{m}\}.

(26)

Eqs. (
24
) and (
19
) are equivalent to Eqs. (
22
) and (
8
), respectively,
with the former being more convenient for use in the variational principle of the canonical basis.

The time complexity of computing the matrix element of a two-body operator, as presented in Eqs. (
22
) and (
24
), is evaluated in the regime where
\omega/2\geq n\gg\nu
.
For a
k\times k
matrix, both the determinant (calculated using the LU method) and the Pfaffian (calculated using the Parlett-Reid algorithm)
Wimmer 2012
;
Parlett and Reid 1970
have a time complexity of
O(k^{3})
.
For on-the-fly computation of Eqs. (
22
) and (
24
), the time complexity is given by
O[\binom{(\omega-\nu)/2}{N}^{2}\binom{n}{2}^{2}n^{3}]
and
O[\binom{\omega-2}{n-2}\binom{n}{\nu}n^{3}]
, respectively.
The time complexity of Eq. (
24
) can be significantly reduced if the Pfaffians are precomputed and stored in DRAM.

To validate the formulas derived above, I apply them to a simple toy model involving an
N
-pair condensate
|{\rm c}\rangle
, as defined in Eq. (
1
), within a doubly degenerate Hartree-Fock basis and the pairing Hamiltonian:

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{\rm p}\equiv\sum_{k}\varepsilon_{k}(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{k}\hat{a}_{k}+\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\tilde{k}}\hat{a}_{\tilde{k}})-\sum_{kl}g_{kl}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{k}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\tilde{k}}\hat{a}_{\tilde{l}}\hat{a}_{l}.

(27)

Using Eqs. (
8
) and (
22
), the expectation values of the norm and Hamiltonian are calculated as

\displaystyle\langle{\rm c}|{\rm c}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(N!)^{2}\sum_{\{i_{1}\cdots i_{N}\}}v^{2}_{i_{1}}\cdots v^{2}_{i_{N}},

(28)

\displaystyle\langle{\rm c}|\hat{H}_{\rm p}|{\rm c}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(N!)^{2}\sum_{\{i_{1}\cdots i_{N-1}\}}v^{2}_{i_{1}}\cdots v^{2}_{N-1}

(29)

\displaystyle\times\sum_{kl\notin\{i_{1}\cdots i_{N-1}\}}v_{k}v_{l}(2\varepsilon_{k}\delta_{kl}-g_{kl}).

For degenerate single-particle states with constant pairing strength (
\varepsilon_{k}\equiv\varepsilon
and
g_{kl}\equiv g
), the
v
values become uniform, and the norm in Eq. (
28
) reaches its maximum value:
(N!)^{2}\binom{\Omega}{N}/\Omega^{N}
.
This value is much smaller than the norm of an
N
-boson condensate (
N!
) due to the Pauli principle, except in the limit
\Omega\rightarrow\infty
, where the norm
(N!)^{2}\binom{\Omega}{N}/\Omega^{N}
approaches
N!
.
Using Eq. (
29
), the energy in this case is
E=2\varepsilon N-gN(\Omega-N+1)
, which agrees with the result obtained from other approaches, such as quasi-spin theory
Ichimura 1968
.

Another application of the present formalism is the study of collective states in rotational nuclei using the angular momentum projected number-conserved BCS (denoted by PNBCS)
Yu et al. 2022
;
et al.
.
As an example, I examine low-lying states of the medium-heavy nucleus
70
Ge, calculated within the
1p_{1/2}1p_{3/2}0f_{5/2}0g_{9/2}
single-particle space using the JUN45 shell-model effective interaction
Honma et al. 2009
.
In the PNBCS, the level energies are obtained by solving the Hill-Wheeler equation:

\sum_{K}{\mathcal{H}}^{J}_{K^{\prime}K}g^{r}_{JK}=\epsilon_{J_{r}}\sum_{K}{\mathcal{N}}^{J}_{K^{\prime}K}g^{r}_{JK},

(30)

where
\epsilon_{J_{r}}
is energy of the
r
-th state with angular momentum
J
, and
g^{r}_{JK}
is the expansion coefficient of the eigenstate.
The Hamiltonian and norm matrices,
{\mathcal{H}}^{J}_{K^{\prime}K}
and
{\mathcal{N}}^{J}_{K^{\prime}K}
, as given in Eq. (
3
), are computed
using a Fortran code developed based on Eqs. (
19
) and (
24
).
For
70
Ge, this computation is approximately four times faster for the one-body density matrix and twice as fast for the two-body interactions compared to a code based on the generalized Wick theorem
Lei et al. 2021
.
The Cooper pairs, defined in Eqs. (
1
) and (
2
), are determined by solving the number-conserved BCS equation
Yu et al. 2022
;
Jia 2017
;
Jia 2019
.
For comparison, the result of the full configuration shell model (SM) is obtained using the Bigstick code
Johnson et al. 2013
;
Johnson et al. 2018
.

compares the excitation energies for the ground-state rotational band, along with the low-lying
\gamma

70
Ge, from experimental data
Gürdal and McCutchan 2016
, the SM, and the PNBCS.
The PNBCS results exhibit reasonable agreement with both the data and the SM results.
The PNBCS calculation suggests that angular momentum projection onto an intrinsic pair condensate with triaxial deformation naturally generates the ground band and the
\gamma

\beta

Notably, the experimental
\gamma

\gamma
-soft configuration, whereas the SM with the JUN45 interaction and the
1p_{1/2}1p_{3/2}0f_{5/2}0g_{9/2}
single-particle space, as well as the PNBCS, predicts a
\gamma
-rigid configuration.
Future work might address this discrepancy by modifying the effective interaction and expanding the single-particle space to include the
0f_{7/2}
orbital.

To summarize, this work presents a novel closed-form formalism for BCS/HFB wave functions that preserve particle number conservation.
The method utilizes the generalized Kronecker delta to simplify calculations, providing compact expressions for norm and many-body density matrices.
The use of the generalized Kronecker delta is conceptually similar to, yet simpler than, approaches based on Grassmann algebra, and is expected to aid in future developments of quantum many-fermion methods.
These advances establish a robust theoretical framework for number-conserved pairing theory, validated through a benchmark calculation with the pairing interaction and an application to low-lying states in
70
Ge using the angular momentum projected number-conserved BCS with the JUN45 effective interaction.
For
70
Ge, the results indicate that the ground and
\gamma
bands correspond to a triaxially-deformed intrinsic configuration, while the
\beta
band arises from axially symmetric deformation.

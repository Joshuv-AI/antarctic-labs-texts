Salmonella bovismorbificans abscess masking a primary testicular tumour in the retroperitoneum – A case report
Authors: Mark Fitzgerald, Sheshang Kamath
Published: 2024

These arise insidiously and can be hard to detect. Salmonella is an uncommon cause of abscess formation and the subtype of bovismorbificans, arising from geckos, only scarcely reported to cause an abscess. Introduction and importance

Testicular cancer can present with a non-gonadal lesions in about 5 % of cases at various sites including the retroperitoneum, mediastinum, sacrococcygeal region and neck [1]. The primary tumour in a non-testicular region can either be related to regressed testicular primary or De novo tumour formation in the midline [1,2]. Lesions within the retroperitoneal are difficult to detect as commonly asymptomatic until the size increased and invasion or displacement of surrounding structures occurs [3]. Additionally the aetiology of these lesions varies from abscess to metastatic deposit and primary cancer [3,4]. Here we report a case in line with the SCARE Criteria [5] of an isolated non-seminomatous germ cell tumour presenting as a retroperitoneal abscess containing Salmonella Bovismorbificans, an organism that rarely causes abscess formation.

Case presentation

A 37-year-old male presented with a three-day history of fevers, right flank pain and sweats suggestive of pyelonephritis. His past medical history was significant only for left testicular trauma about 15 years prior to presenting resulting in testicular atrophy. Investigations prior presentation included an ultrasound of his scrotum demonstrating a small left testis with two focal lesions, right normal and normal testicular tumour markers. On examination he had renal angle tenderness, atrophic nontender left testis, so an abdominal CT scan was performed, mainly to exclude ureteric calculi [Fig. 1]. This demonstrated a rim enhancing 7.7 by 6 by 6.1 cm retroperitoneal lesion with no other focus of malignancy or aetiology of the pain [Fig. 1]. The lesion extended from L1-L3 between the Aorta and Inferior vena cava with several adjacent lymph nodes mildly enlarged. Numerous tumour markers were arranged with Alpha Foetal Protein (AFP) the only marker level elevatated at 1850kIU/L. A CT guided biopsy was arranged but the biopsy was abandoned as repeated biopsy attempts retrieved purulent material, and a drain was placed instead [Fig. 2], At this point it was felt the adjacent nodes were therefore reactive as had no pathologyical features on imaging. Fluid was sent for cytology, with no malignant cells present, and culture with gram staining showing a gram-negative bacillus. Over the coming two weeks the drain was poorly tolerated by the patient, despite appropriate positioning, appropriate antibiotic coverage with ceftriaxone and metronidazole and input from the acute pain service. His AFP level doubled to 3330kiU/L and testicular ultrasound demonstrating atrophic left testis with two focal lesions measure 15 × 11 × 10 mm and 12 × 12 × 10 mm respectively – unchanged from US ten years prior, His right testis was normal. A FDG PET arranged revealed tracer uptake throughout the retroperitoneal abscess and associated abscess drainage tract with non-specific heterogenous photogenic uptake in the left testis. A repeat biopsy of the retroperitoneal lesion was arranged as no other aetiology of the AFP rise was identified and Salmonella infections are reported to elevate AFP levels. Fluid from the lesion was cultured and again grew salmonella whilst histology revealed a viable tumour with features consistent of a yolk sac tumour and seminoma [Fig. 3]. Clinical discussion

Testicular cancers are increasing in incidence [6] and are the most common solid organ tumour in young males and yet they remain relatively rare [7]. In addition to being rare, the majority are of low stage disease confirmed to either the testis or the retroperitoneal space [7], for this reason long term survive is greater than 95 % for low stage tumours [7]. Within the testis tumours usually present with a painless lump but can present with pain if associated with an infection or haemorrhage [7]. About 30 % of primary gonad tumours have spread to the retroperitoneal space at presentation [3]. Majority of testicular cancers are germ cell in nature [8] meaning the aetiology of primary extragonadal presentation, including the retroperitoneum, can be explained by a failure of migration or arrested migration of gonadal tissue [9]. Alternatively the germ cell either reverse migrated after transformation in the testis or the primary tumour within the gonads has regressed [1].

Salmonella, a rod shaped gram negative bacteria, is a common cause of gastroenteritis and systemic infections [10] with geckos in South East Asia and Australia being a natural reservoir of salmonella [11]. The salmonella serotype bovismorbificans is rarely implicated in systemic infections [10] but has been reported to cause an abscess within the pelvis [10] and pancreas [12] and an mycotic aneurysm of the aorta [13]. It has not been specificity described as the cause of a retroperitoneal abscess despite other salmonella species being implicated in abscesses throughout the body [[14], [15], [16]].

In this patient the patient's primary presentation is atypical of a malignant process. He presented with short duration of flank pain and fevers secondary to an abscess caused by salmonella. Abscess within the retroperitoneum are uncommon [4,17] and salmonella bovismorbificans rarely causes abscess with only two abscess described previously [10,12]. Given the scarcity of this presentation the clinical suspicion remained high. It is unclear if the primary tumour within the testis had burnt out as no tumour was present radiographically or histologically within the atrophy testis, it is possible the primary tumour had regressed.

Conclusion

Whilst testicular cancer arising as a primary mass within the retroperitoneum should be suspected when a lesion is found on imaging confusion can arise when the lesion appears as an abscess cavity. Clinical suspicion for malignancy should remain high when retroperitoneal abscesses fail to improve when antibiotic coverage is adequate or when the organism detected is atypical for abscess formation.

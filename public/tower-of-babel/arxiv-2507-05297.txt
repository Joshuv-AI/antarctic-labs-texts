Continuous Classification Aggregation
Zijun Meng

Abstract




Abstract

We prove that any optimal, independent, and zero unanimous fuzzy classification aggregation function of a continuum of individual classifications of
m\geq 3
objects into
2\leq p\leq m
types must be a weighted arithmetic mean. We also provide a characterization for the case when
m=p=2
.

1
Introduction

In parallel with Arrow’s impossibility theorem on aggregating preferences, Maniquet and Mongin (2016) proved in
[
3
]
that an independent and surjective aggregation of crisp classifications of a finite number of individuals satisfying an unanimity condition must be a dictatorship. Fioravanti (2025) studied in
[
2
]
the same problem in a fuzzy setting (in contrary to the crisp setting in
[
3
]
) and proved that an optimal, independent and surjective aggregation of fuzzy (in contrary to crisp) classifications of a finite number of individuals satisfying a weaker unanimity condition must be a weighted arithmetic mean.

A natural question is whether the same is true for a large economy, where the set of individuals is uncountable. The fuzzy setting in Fioravanti (2025),
[
2
]
allowed us to model the set of individuals as an unit interval in a clean way. In this paper, we prove the same result in the large economy setting. Apart from the technical side, we largely apply the ideas in
[
1
]
,
[
2
]
and
[
5
]
.

3
Model

Throughout this paper, we use
\lambda
to denote the Lebesgue measure.

We use the model and assumptions of
[
2
]
except that we change the set of individuals. Let
I=[0,1]
be the set of individuals,
X=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{m}\}
be the set of
m
objects. We
fuzzy classify
each object into one of
p\geq 2
types, that is, we classify by a map
c\colon X\to I^{p}
, where
c\left(x\right)_{t}
is interpreted as the proportion of the object
x
classified into type
t
. We require
\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m}c\left(x_{j}\right)_{t}\geq 1
(this surjectivity condition requires
m\geq p
) and
\sum\limits_{t=1}^{p}c\left(x_{j}\right)_{t}=1
. Denote by
\mathcal{C}
the set of fuzzy classifications and
\mathcal{C}^{I}=\{c^{I}=(c_{i})_{i\in I}\mid c_{i}\in\mathcal{C}\,\forall i\in I\}
be the set of
fuzzy classification profiles
.

A
fuzzy classification aggregation function (FCAF)
is a map
\alpha\colon\mathcal{C}^{I}\to\mathcal{C}
. Let
\mu
be a probability measure on
I
, then
\alpha_{\mu}\colon\mathcal{C}^{I}\to\mathcal{C}
given by
\alpha_{\mu}(c^{I})(x)=\int_{I}c_{i}(x)\mu(di)
is an FCAF, we call it a
weighted arithmetic mean
. (For example, if
w\in C(I,\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0})
is a weight function such that
\int_{I}w(i)\lambda(di)=1
with the corresponding Lebesgue-Stieljes measure
\mu=\lambda_{w}
, where
C(I,\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0})
is the space of nonnegative continuous functionals on
I
, then
\alpha_{\mu}\colon\mathcal{C}^{I}\to\mathcal{C}
given by
\alpha_{\mu}(c^{I})\left(x\right)=\int_{I}w(i)c_{i}(x)\lambda(di)
is a weighted arithmetic mean.) Furthermore, we say that
\alpha_{\lambda}
is an
arithmetic mean
. Also, for any
i\in I
, we say that
\alpha_{\delta_{i}}
is a
degenerated weighted arithmetic mean

Assumption 1
(Optimality)
.
For any fuzzy classification
c^{I}\in\mathcal{C}^{I}
, for any type
1\leq t\leq p
, if
\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m}c_{i}\left(x_{j}\right)_{t}=h
for
\lambda
-a.e.
i\in I
, where
h\geq 1
is a constant, then
\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m}\alpha(c^{I})\left(x_{j}\right)_{t}=h
.

Assumption 2
(Independence)
.
For any fuzzy classifications
c^{I},(c^{\prime})^{I}\in\mathcal{C}^{I}
, for any object
x\in X
, if
c_{i}(x)=c^{\prime}_{i}(x)
for
\lambda
-a.e.
i\in I
, then
\alpha(c^{I})(x)=\alpha((c^{\prime})^{I})(x)
.

A stronger assumption than independence requires the FCAF to be insensitive to labels of objects:

Assumption 2’
(Symmetry)
.
For any fuzzy classifications
c^{I},(c^{\prime})^{I}\in\mathcal{C}^{I}
, for any objects
x,y\in X
, if
c_{i}(x)=c^{\prime}_{i}(y)
for
\lambda
-a.e.
i\in I
, then
\alpha(c^{I})(x)=\alpha((c^{\prime})^{I})(y)
.

Assumption 3
(Zero Unanimity)
.
For any fuzzy classification
c^{I}\in\mathcal{C}^{I}
, for any type
1\leq t\leq p
and object
x\in X
, if
c_{i}\left(x\right)_{t}=0
for
\lambda
-a.e.
i\in I
, then
\alpha\left(c\right)\left(x\right)_{t}=0
.

A stronger assumption than zero unanimity requires the FCAF to be unanimous at every point in
I
:

Assumption 3’
(Unanimity)
.
For any fuzzy classification
c^{I}\in\mathcal{C}^{I}
, for any type
1\leq t\leq p
and object
x\in X
, for any
h\in I
, if
c_{i}\left(x\right)_{t}=h
for
\lambda
-a.e.
i\in I
, then
\alpha(c)(x)_{t}=h
.

An even stronger assumption requires the FCAF to be bounded by the essential upper and lower bounds:

Assumption 3”
(Coherence)
.
For any fuzzy classification
c^{I}\in\mathcal{C}^{I}
, for any type
1\leq t\leq p
and object
x\in X
, we have

\alpha(c)(x)_{t}\in\left[\essinf_{i\in I}c_{i}(x)_{t},\esssup_{i\in I}c_{i}(x)_{t}\right].

Assumption 4
(Non-dictatorship)
.
There is no
i\in I
such that
\alpha(c^{I})=c_{i}
for any
c^{I}\in\mathcal{C}^{I}
.

A stronger assumption than non-dictatorship requires the FCAF to be insensitive to names of individuals:

Assumption 4’
(Anonymity)
.
For any fuzzy classifications
c^{I},(c^{\prime})^{I}\in\mathcal{C}^{I}
, for any
\lambda
-measurable subset
J\subseteq I
such that
J+s\subseteq I
, and that
J
and
J+s
are disjoint, if
c_{j}(x)=c^{\prime}_{j+s}(x)
and
c_{j+s}(x)=c^{\prime}_{j}(x)
for any
j\in J
and
x\in X
, then
\alpha(c^{I})=\alpha((c^{\prime})^{I})
.

The following proposition summarizing the relationships between the assumptions is immediate:

Proposition 1.
The followings are true:

•

Symmetry
\implies
independence;

•

Coherence
\implies
unanimity
\implies
zero unanimity;

•

Anonymity
\implies
non-dictatorship.

Next, we have the independence of the weakest versions of the four assumptions:

Proposition 2.
For each of the assumptions among optimality, independence, zero unanimity and non-dictatorship, there exist an FCAF violating that assumption but satisfying the other three.

Results

To obtain our result, we use versions of the lemmas from
[
1
]
and
[

Lemma 1.
If
\psi\colon[-\epsilon,\epsilon]^{[0,1]}=\{f\colon[0,1]\to[-\epsilon,\epsilon]\}\to\mathbb{R}
is continuous with respect to the metric induced by the supremum norm, and satisfying the Cauchy equation

\psi\left(f+g\right)=\psi\left(f\right)+\psi\left(g\right)\,\forall f,g,f+g\in[-\epsilon,\epsilon]^{[0,1]},

then there exists a continuous continuation
\overline{\psi}\colon C(I)\to\mathbb{R}
of
\psi
(i.e. such that
\overline{\psi}|_{[-\epsilon,\epsilon]^{[0,1]}}=\psi
) satisfying the Cauchy equation on
C(I)
:

\overline{\psi}\left(f+g\right)=\overline{\psi}\left(f\right)+\overline{\psi}\left(g\right)\,\forall f,g\in C(I).

Lemma 2.
Fix
m\geq 3
, a family of continuous maps
\{\alpha_{j}\colon I^{I}\to I\}_{j=1}^{m}
satisfies the following two conditions

\displaystyle\alpha_{1}(0)=\dots=\alpha_{m}(0)=0;

\displaystyle\forall f_{1},\dots,f_{m}\in I^{I},\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m}f_{j}=1\implies\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m}\alpha_{j}\left(f_{j}\right)=1

iff there exists a probability measure
\mu
on
I
such that
\alpha_{j}(f)=\int_{I}fd\mu
for any
1\leq j\leq m
.

Theorem 1.
Let
m\geq 3
. An FCAF
\alpha
satisfies optimality, independence and zero unanimity iff it is a weighted arithmetic mean.

With the previous theorem, we can immediately obtain the following corollaries:

Corollary 1.
Let
m\geq 3
. An FCAF
\alpha
satisfies optimality, independence, zero unanimity and anonymity iff it is a arithmetic mean.

Corollary 2.
Let
m\geq 3
,
\alpha
be an FCAF. Then the following statements are equivalent:

•

\alpha
is a non-degenerate weighted arithmetic mean;

•

\alpha
satisfies optimality, independence, zero unanimity and non-dictatorship;

•

\alpha
satisfies optimality, independence, unanimity and non-dictatorship;

•

\alpha
satisfies optimality, independence, coherence and non-dictatorship;

•

\alpha
satisfies optimality, symmetry, zero unanimity and non-dictatorship;

•

\alpha
satisfies optimality, symmetry, unanimity and non-dictatorship;

•

\alpha
satisfies optimality, symmetry, coherence and non-dictatorship.

Our job would be complete if we can characterize the case when

\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m}c\left(x_{j}\right)_{t}\geq 1

is automatically optimal. We begin with a lemma from
[

A mapping
\alpha\colon([0,1]^{I})^{2}\to[0,1]^{2}

f_{1},f_{2}\in[0,1]^{I}

Proof of Lemma 2

Proof.

The if part is obvious. For the only if part, define

\tilde{I}=\left[-\frac{1}{m},1-\frac{1}{m}\right],\,\tilde{f}_{j}=f_{j}-\frac{1}{m},\,\tilde{\alpha}_{j}(\tilde{f}_{j})=\alpha_{j}\left(\tilde{f}_{j}+\frac{1}{m}\right)=\alpha_{j}\left(f_{j}\right).

Now we have

\left(\tilde{f}_{j}\in\tilde{I}^{I}\,\forall j,\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m}\tilde{f}_{j}=0\right)\implies\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m}\tilde{\alpha}_{j}(\tilde{f}_{j})=1.

In particular,

\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m}\tilde{\alpha}_{j}\left(0\right)=1.

Define
\psi_{j}(\tilde{f})=\tilde{\alpha}_{j}(\tilde{f})-\tilde{\alpha}_{j}\left(0\right)
. Then

\left(\tilde{f}_{j}\in\tilde{I}^{I}\,\forall j,\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m}\tilde{f}_{j}=0\right)\implies\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m}\psi_{j}(\tilde{f}_{j})=0,\psi_{j}\left(0\right)=0\,\forall j.

In particular,

\left(\tilde{f}_{j}\in[-\epsilon,\epsilon]^{I}\,\forall j,\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m}\tilde{f}_{j}=0\right)\implies\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m}\psi_{j}(\tilde{f}_{j})=0,\psi_{j}\left(0\right)=0\,\forall j.

For any
1\leq j<k\leq m
,
g\in[-\epsilon,\epsilon]^{I}
, substituting
\tilde{f}_{j}=g,\tilde{f}_{k}=-g
and
\tilde{f}_{\ell}=0
for all
j\neq\ell\neq k
, we get

\psi_{j}\left(g\right)+\psi_{k}\left(-g\right)=0.

Thus, for any pairwise distinct
j
,
k
,
\ell

\psi_{j}\left(g\right)=-\psi_{k}\left(-g\right)=-\left(-\psi_{\ell}\left(-\left(-g\right)\right)\right)=\psi_{\ell}\left(g\right)

Let
\psi=\psi_{j}|_{[-\epsilon,\epsilon]^{I}}\,\forall j
, then
\psi\left(g\right)+\psi\left(-g\right)=0
. Now, substituting
\tilde{f}_{1}=f,\tilde{f}_{2}=g,\tilde{f}_{3}=-f-g,\tilde{f}_{4}=\dots=\tilde{f}_{m}=0
, we know that
\psi
satisfies the Cauchy functional equation for all
f,g,f+g\in[-\epsilon,\epsilon]^{I}
. By
Lemma 1
, we can uniquely extend
\psi
to
\overline{\psi}\colon C(I,\tilde{I})\to\mathbb{R}
satisfying the Cauchy functional equation for all
f,g\in C(I)
, note that we still have
\overline{\psi}\geq 0
. Now, define
\varphi_{j}(\tilde{f})=\psi_{j}(\tilde{f})-\overline{\psi}(\tilde{f})
for all
\tilde{f}\in\tilde{I}^{I}
, we show that
\varphi_{j}=0
on
\tilde{I}^{I}
for any
j
. Indeed, we know that
\varphi_{j}=0
on
[-\epsilon,\epsilon]^{I}
. From the above, we get

\left(\tilde{f}_{j}\in\tilde{I}^{I}\,\forall j,\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m}\tilde{f}_{j}=0\right)\implies\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m}\psi_{j}(\tilde{f}_{j})=0\implies\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m}\varphi_{j}(\tilde{f}_{j})=-\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m}\overline{\psi}(\tilde{f}_{j})=-\overline{\psi}\left(\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m}\tilde{f}_{j}\right)=-\overline{\psi}\left(0\right)=0.

For any
f\in\tilde{I}^{I}
, since
-f\in\left[-\frac{m-1}{m},\frac{1}{m}\right]^{I}
, there exist

concluding the proof.

A.4
Proof of Theorem 1

Proof.

The if part is obvious. For the only if part, notice that by independence we can decompose
\alpha
into a set

\{\alpha_{x_{j}}\colon\mathcal{C}^{I}|_{x_{j}}\to\mathcal{C}|_{x_{j}}\}_{j=1}^{m}

of
m
maps, each of which restricted on a single object
x_{j}\in X
. By optimality and zero unanimity, for each
1\leq t\leq p
, the family
\{\alpha_{x_{j}}|_{t}\}_{j=1}^{m}
of maps satisfies the conditions of
Lemma 2
, so applying the lemma, it follows that, there is a probability measure
\mu^{t}
on
I
such that

\alpha_{x_{j}}|_{t}(c(x_{j})_{t})=\int_{I}c_{i}(x_{j})_{t}\mu^{t}(di).

Now, it suffices to show that the measures
\mu^{t}
are consistent between types
1\leq t\leq p
. Indeed, for any
1\leq t\leq p
,
\lambda
-measurable
J\subseteq I
, consider

c_{j}^{t}\left(x_{1}\right)=\left(\underbrace{0,\dots,0}_{t-1},1,0,\dots,0\right)\,\forall j\in J,

and

c_{i}^{t}\left(x_{1}\right)=\left(1,0,\dots,0\right)\,\forall i\in I\setminus J.

Then,

\displaystyle\alpha(c^{t})(x_{1})

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{I\setminus J}c_{i}^{t}(x_{1})_{1}\mu^{1}(di),\underbrace{0,\dots,0}_{t-2},\int_{J}c_{j}^{t}(x_{1})_{t}\mu^{t}(dj),0,\dots,0\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{I\setminus J}\mu^{1}(di),\underbrace{0,\dots,0}_{t-2},\int_{J}\mu^{t}(dj),0,\dots,0\right),

so

1=\int_{I\setminus J}\mu^{1}(di)+\int_{J}\mu^{t}(dj).

For any
1\leq t^{\prime}\leq p
, comparing the last equation and the corresponding equation

1=\int_{I\setminus J}\mu^{1}(di)+\int_{J}\mu^{t^{\prime}}(dj),

we obtain

\int_{J}\mu^{t}(dj)=\int_{J}\mu^{t^{\prime}}(dj).

Since this equation is true for any
\lambda
-measurable set
J\subseteq I
, it follows that
\mu^{t}=\mu^{t^{\prime}}
, establishing the consistency.
∎

A.5
Proof of Lemma 3

Proof.

The if part is obvious. For the only if part, from the symmetric and zero unanimous conditions imposed on

, there exists a function

. Now, it is easy to see that

∎

A.6
Proof of Theorem 2

Proof.

The if part is obvious. For the only if part, by zero unanimity and symmetry, for any
1\leq t\leq 2
, the map
\alpha|_{t}
restricted on type
t
satisfies the conditions of
Lemma 3
. Applying
Lemma 3
, there exists a function
h^{t}\colon\left[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right]\to\left[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right]
satisfying
h(\frac{1}{2})=\frac{1}{2}
and
h(-f)=-h(f)
for any
f\in\left[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right]
such that for any
f_{1},f_{2}\in[0,1]^{I}
,

\alpha|_{t}(f_{1},f_{2})=\left(h^{t}\left(f_{1}-\frac{1}{2}\right)+\frac{1}{2},h^{t}\left(f_{2}-\frac{1}{2}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\right).

It is thus sufficient to show the consistency
h^{1}=h^{2}
. To see this note that

\displaystyle h^{1}\left(c^{I}(x_{1})_{1}-\frac{1}{2}\right)+\frac{1}{2}+h^{2}\left(c^{I}(x_{1})_{2}-\frac{1}{2}\right)+\frac{1}{2}=1,

\displaystyle h^{1}\left(c^{I}(x_{1})_{1}-\frac{1}{2}\right)+\frac{1}{2}+h^{1}\left(c^{I}(x_{2})_{1}-\frac{1}{2}\right)+\frac{1}{2}=1.

It then follows from
c^{I}(x_{1})_{2}=c^{I}(x_{2})_{1}
that
h^{1}=h^{2}
, as desired.
∎

Vaisman Solvmanifolds as Finite Quotients of Kodaira-Thurston Nilmanifolds
Lucas H. S. Gomes

Abstract
. We prove that every Vaisman solvmanifold is a finite quotient of a Kodaira–Thurston manifold. More generally, we show that every aspherical compact Vaisman manifold with strongly polycyclic fundamental group is a finite quotient of a Kodaira–Thurston manifold. As consequences, we obtain that every completely solvable solvmanifold admitting a Vaisman structure is a Kodaira–Thurston manifold, that Oeljeklaus–Toma manifolds admit no Vaisman structures (not necessarily left-invariant), and that solvmanifolds does not admit LCK Einstein–Weyl structures.



1.
Introduction

Let
G
be a Lie group and
\Gamma\leq G
be a lattice, i.e.,
\Gamma
is a discrete cocompact subgroup of
G
. The quotient
\Gamma\backslash G
is called a solvmanifold (nilmanifold) if
G
is a solvable (nilpotent) Lie group. A Kodaira-Thurston manifold is a nilmanifold with associated Lie group
\mathbb{R}\times H(1,n)
, where
H(1,n)
is the Heisenberg group of dimension
2n+1
(Definition
2.4
).

A classical problem in the literature is to classify geometric structures in solvmanifolds. One can study the case where the structure is left-invariant with respect to the associated Lie group
G
, or work with the general case when the structure is not necessarily left-invariant. Examples of such approach are given by Hasegawa’s Theorem in
[
10
]
where he shows that every solvmanifold that admits a Kähler structure is the finite quotient of a complex torus. Another example is given by Kasuya in
[
13
]
where he shows that every solvmanifold that admits a Sasakian structure is a finite quotient of a Heisenberg nilmanifold, that is,
G
is the Heisenberg group
H(1,n)
. Both results rely on the properties of the fundamental group of the solvmanifold. In this work, we focus our attention to the Locally Conformal Kähler case.

Let
(M^{n},J,\omega)
be a Hermitian manifold with complex structure
J
and fundamental form
\omega
with
n\geq 4
.
If there exists a closed 1-form
\theta
such that
d\omega=\theta\wedge\omega
, then the triple
(J,\omega,\theta)
is called a Locally Conformally Kähler (LCK) structure on
M
.
We call this structure Vaisman if
\theta
is parallel with respect to the Levi-Civita connection,
\nabla^{g}\theta=0
.

For the general classification of LCK structures on solvmanifolds, we can consider a combination of the cases where some element of the triple is or is not left-invariant. Initially, Ugarte in
[
28
]
showed that, in real dimension at most 6, every nilmanifold with an LCK structure whose complex structure
J
is left-invariant has its associated Lie group
G
isomorphic to
\mathbb{R}\times H(1,n)
. Ugarte then conjectured that such result should also be true in any dimension, which was proven by Sawai in
[
26
]
. Sawai later extended the result in
[
27
]
for the Vaisman case, showing that a completely solvable solvmanifold with a Vaisman structure whose
J
is left-invariant has its associated Lie group
G
isomorphic to
\mathbb{R}\times H(1,n)
.

At this point, one could hope that for
G
nilpotent, even if
J
is not left-invariant it would still be true that
G
should be isomorphic to
\mathbb{R}\times H(1,n)
. Bazzoni showed in
[
3
]
that this is true when the structure is Vaisman. For the case where the structure is in general only LCK, the problem is still open.

When
G
is solvable, even if
(J,\omega,\theta)
is left-invariant and Vaisman, it has been proved that
G
is not isomorphic to
\mathbb{R}\times H(1,n)
in general. Andrada and Origlia in
[
1
]
completely characterized the solvmanifolds in which the LCK structure
(J,\omega,\theta)
is Vaisman and left-invariant. They used such characterization to construct a family of examples in which
G
was not isomorphic to
\mathbb{R}\times H(1,n)
.

Inspired by the previously mentioned works of Hasegawa and Kasuya, we prove an analogue of their results for the case of Vaisman solvmanifolds, extending the works of Bazzoni and Sawai for the case where
J
is not left-invariant, which is the main result of this paper.

Finally, using a topological restriction of LCK Einstein-Weyl manifolds, we can apply the main theorem to show that no solvmanifold can admit a LCK Einstein-Weyl structure, which is done in section 5.

Preliminaries

2.1.
LCK Structures

Let
(M^{n},J,\omega)
be a Hermitian manifold with complex structure
J
and fundamental form
\omega
with real dimension
n\geq 4
. For this work we consider all manifolds to be connected.

Definition 2.1
.

•

If there exists a closed 1-form
\theta
such that
d\omega=\theta\wedge\omega
, then the triple
(J,\omega,\theta)
is called a Locally Conformally Kähler (LCK) structure on
M
.

•

If
\theta
is exact, then the Hermitian metric
g
is conformal to a Kähler one. In this case we call the structure Globally Conformally Kähler (GCK).

•

We call this structure Vaisman if
\theta
is parallel with respect to the Levi-Civita connection,
\nabla^{g}\theta=0
.

We implicitly assume that the LCK structures are not GCK. In this way we exclude the Kähler case, unless explicitly stated. This can be done because of the following fundamental fact.

Theorem 2.1

(Vaisman
[
29
]
)
.

Let
(J,\omega,\theta)
be an LCK structure on a compact manifold
M
. If the LCK structure is not GCK, then
(M,J)
does not admit any Kähler metric.

Vaisman manifolds are closely related to Sasakian manifolds.

Definition 2.2
.

A Sasakian manifold is a Riemmanian manifold
(M,g)
of odd dimension such that the Riemannian cone
(\mathbb{R}^{>0}\times M,dt^{2}+t^{2}g)
has a complex structure such that the metric is Kähler.

In fact, Sasakian manifolds provide a source of examples of Vaisman manifolds by considering the
\mathbb{Z}
action of a holomorphic homothety
h_{\lambda}(t,p):=(\lambda t,p)
, for
\lambda>0
, on
\mathbb{R}^{>0}\times M
. The quotient
\mathbb{Z}\backslash(\mathbb{R}^{>0}\times M)
is naturally endowed with a Vaisman structure.

By using the Structure Theorem in
[
19
,
17
]
, Bazzoni, Marrero and Oprea obtained the following theorem which is key for our proof of Theorem

2.2.
Einstein-Weyl LCK metrics

For a given metric
g
on a smooth manifold
M
one can consider the conformal class
c
defined by
g
, i.e.,

c=\{e^{2f}g\ |\ f\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(M)\}
. We call the pair
(M,c)
a conformal manifold. For a pair
(g,\theta)
of a metric
g
and a 1-form
\theta
, we can define an affine connection on
M
by

\nabla_{X}Y=\nabla^{g}_{X}Y+\theta(X)Y+\theta(Y)X-g(X,Y)\theta^{\#_{g}}.

This affine connection satisfies
\nabla g=-2\theta\otimes g
. One can show that for a conformal transformation
h:=e^{2f}g
, by defining
\theta^{h}:=\theta-2df
, we get that
(h,\theta^{h})
defines the same
\nabla
.
We call
\nabla
a Weyl structure on
M
and
\theta^{g}
the Lee form associated with
g
. We can define its curvature and Ricci curvature by

\displaystyle R^{\nabla}(X,Y)Z

\displaystyle:=\nabla_{[X,Y]}Z-[\nabla_{X},\nabla_{Y}]Z

\displaystyle\Ric^{\nabla}(X,Y)

\displaystyle:=\Tr(Z\mapsto R^{\nabla}(X,Z)Y).

By a choice of
g
in
c
we can define
\Scal_{g}^{\nabla}
by the
g
-trace of the Ricci curvature
\Ric^{\nabla}
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
(M,c)
be a conformal manifold with a Weyl structure
\nabla
, and denote
n:=\dim M
. We say that
\nabla
is Einstein-Weyl if

\Ric^{\nabla}=\frac{1}{n}\Scal_{g}^{\nabla}g-\frac{1}{2}(n-2)d\theta,

for one (hence for all)
g\in c
.

An LCK structure
(\omega,\theta)
on a complex manifold
(M,J)
defines naturally a Weyl structure by considering the pair
(g,-2\theta)
. If the
\nabla
induced by the LCK structure in this way is Einstein-Weyl, we say that
M
is an Einstein-Weyl LCK manifold.

Theorem 2.3

(
[
6
]
)
.

Let
(M,c)
be a compact oriented conformal manifold of dimension
n\geq 3
and let
\nabla
be a Weyl structure on
(M,c)
. Then, there exists a metric
g\in c
, unique up to multiplication by a real number, such that the Lee form
\theta
associated to
g
in the Weyl structure satisfy
\delta^{g}\theta=0
.

The metric of the theorem above is called the Gauduchon metric.

Theorem 2.4

(
[
7
]
)
.

Let
(M,c)
and
\nabla
be as in the previous theorem. Consider the Gauduchon metric
g
and the associated Lee form
\theta
. If
\nabla
is Einstein-Weyl and
\theta\neq 0
, then the Lee field
X=\theta^{\#_{g}}
, the
g
-dual of
\theta
, is a Killing vector field.

If one considers that
\nabla^{g}\theta=\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{L}_{\theta^{\#_{g}}}g+\frac{1}{2}d\theta
for any
1
-form
\theta
, then we obtain the following.

Corollary 2.1
.

If
M
is an Einstein-Weyl LCK manifold, then its Gauduchon metric
g
is a Vaisman metric.

Denoting by
b_{1}(M)
the first Betti number of
M
, we have the following result, which will be used to prove the final application of the main theorem of this work.

Theorem 2.5

(
[
30
]
)
.

Let
M
be a compact Einstein-Weyl LCK manifold. Assume

is not Kähler. Then,
b_{1}(M)=1
.

2.3.
Solvmanifolds

Definition 2.4
.

We define the Heisenberg group
H(1,n)
of dimension
2n+1
to be the following Lie group

H(1,n):=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}1&x_{1}&x_{2}&\cdots&x_{n}&z\\
&1&&&&y_{1}\\
&&\ddots&&&y_{2}\\
&&&\ddots&&\vdots\\
&&&&1&y_{n}\\
&&&&&1\\
\end{pmatrix}\ \middle|\ x_{i},y_{i},z\in\mathbb{R}\right\}

It is a 2-step nilpotent Lie group admitting a lattice. It also has a natural left-invariant Sasakian structure. The group can also be described as
\mathbb{R}^{2n+1}
with the group multiplication

(z,x_{1},y_{1},\dots,x_{n},y_{n})\cdot(z^{\prime},x_{1}^{\prime},y_{1}^{\prime},\dots,x_{n}^{\prime},y_{n}^{\prime}):=(z+z^{\prime}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}y_{i}^{\prime}\,,x_{1}+x_{1}^{\prime},y_{1}+y_{1}^{\prime},\dots,x_{n}+x_{n}^{\prime},y_{n}+y_{n}^{\prime}).

Definition 2.5
.

Let
G
be a solvable Lie group and
\mathfrak{g}
be its Lie algebra. Assume that
G
is connected and simply-connected.

•

A manifold
M
is called a solvmanifold if it is the quotient of
G
by a lattice
\Gamma
, i.e., a discrete cocompact subgroup of
G
, acting on the left on
G
by left multiplication. In this case we write
M=\Gamma\backslash G
.

•

In the case where
G
is nilpotent,
M
is called a nilmanifold.

•

If
G=\mathbb{R}\times H(1,n)
, we call
M
a Kodaira-Thurston manifold.

•

We say that
G
is completely solvable if
ad_{X}
only has real eigeinvalues for all
X\in\mathfrak{g}
. In this case,
M
is called a completely solvable solvmanifold.

Definition 2.6
.

We call a path-connected topological space
X
aspherical if all its homotopy groups
\pi_{p}(X)
of order
p\geq 2
are trivial.

Every solvmanifold is aspherical, since, by our definition, they are given by a smooth covering where the universal covering is homeomorphic to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
(
[
23
, Preliminaries]
).

Theorem 2.6

(Rigidity Theorem)
.

•

[
23
, Theorem 2.11]
Two nilmanifolds with isomorphic fundamental groups have isomorphic associated Lie groups.

•

[
14
]
Two solvmanifolds with isomorphic fundamental group are diffeomorphic.

Recall that the nilradical
N
of a solvable Lie group
G
is the maximal normal connected nilpotent subgroup of
G
.

Theorem 2.7

(Mostow Structure Theorem
[
14
]
)
.

Let
M=\Gamma\backslash G
be a compact solvmanifold. Consider the nilradical
N
of
G
. Then,

(1)

N\Gamma
is a closed subgroup of
G
;

(2)

N\cap\Gamma
is a lattice in
N
;

(3)

N\Gamma\backslash G
is a torus.

Consequently,
M
is fibered over a torus, with a nilmanifold as a fiber,

(N\cap\Gamma)\backslash N\xrightarrow{}M\xrightarrow{}N\Gamma\backslash G

Definition 2.7
.

We say that a group
\Gamma
is strongly polycyclic if it admits a a sequence

\Gamma=\Gamma_{0}\supset\Gamma_{1}\supset\cdots\supset\Gamma_{k}=\{e\}

of subgroups such that
\Gamma_{i}
is normal in
\Gamma_{i-1}
and
\Gamma_{i-1}/\Gamma_{i}
is infinite cyclic.

.

We describe a family of examples given in

In particular, the theorem agrees with the fact that secondary Kodaira surfaces are finite quotients of primary Kodaira surfaces.

For the next application in this section, we consider the Oeljeklaus-Toma (OT) manifolds, constructed originally in

One has

\phi(a_{1},\dots,a_{s}):=\diag(e^{a_{1}},\dots,e^{a_{s}},\exp\sum_{j=1}^{s}{c_{1j}a_{j}},\dots,\exp\sum_{j=1}^{s}{c_{tj}a_{j}})

]
or
[

\Gamma=U\ltimes_{\phi}\mathcal{O}
, where
U\leq\mathbb{R}^{s}
is a lattice of
\mathbb{R}^{s}
and
\mathcal{O}\leq\mathbb{R}^{s}\times\mathbb{C}^{t}
a lattice of
\mathbb{R}^{s}\times\mathbb{C}^{t}
. The quotient
M=\Gamma\backslash G
is a solvmanifold which is not diffeomorphic to a nilmanifold called a OT manifold, and it carries a natural left-invariant non-Vaisman structure. In fact, Kasuya showed that, for
J
left-invariant,

cannot admit any Vaisman structure. We extend this result for any
J
as follows.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
M
be a OT manifold as above. Then,
M
does not admit any Vaisman structure.

Proof.

First observe that
N:=\mathbb{R}^{s}\times\mathbb{C}^{t}
is the nilradical of
G
. Now, suppose that there exists a
\Delta\leq U\ltimes_{\phi}\mathcal{O}
nilpotent sublattice. Notice that
\Delta\cap\mathcal{O}
is a lattice of
N
by the Mostow Structure Theorem, hence
\Delta\cap\mathcal{O}\neq\{0\}
. Given
(a,x)\in G
, we know that
(a,x)^{-1}=(-a,-\phi(a)^{-1}x)
. For a fixed
(a,x)\in G
, define
I(b,y):=(a,x)(b,y)(a,x)^{-1}(b,y)^{-1}=(0,(\phi(a)-\Id)y-(\phi(b)-\Id)x)
. Thus, by considering
(a,x)\in\Delta
and
(0,y)\in\Delta\cap\mathcal{O}
, we get
I^{n}(0,y)=(0,(\phi(a)-\Id)^{n}y)
. Using the fact that the central series of
\Delta
has finite length, we obtain that there exists a
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
(\phi(a)-\Id)^{n}=0
for all

in the projection of
\Delta
on
U
. However,
\phi(a)
is a diagonal matrix, hence such

can only exists if

. This means that
\Delta\leq\mathcal{O}\leq N
. By Mostow Structure Theorem
(N\Delta)\backslash G
has to be a torus, which is impossible since

4.
The case LCK with potential

Let
M
be an LCK manifold with LCK structure
(J,\omega,\theta)
. Defining
d_{\theta}:=d-\theta\wedge
, if there exists a positive smooth function
\psi:M\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
such that
\omega=d_{\theta}d_{\theta}^{c}\psi
we call
(J,\omega,\theta)
an LCK structure with potential, where
d_{\theta}^{c}:=Jd_{\theta}J^{-1}
. The Vaisman structure is a particular case by considering the constant function
\psi=1
. These structures are abundant in a sense, since they are stable under small deformations
[
18
]
. Therefore, any small deformation of a Vaisman manifold gives an example of LCK structure with potential.

In the proof of Theorem
1
, the LCK structure was invoked only to obtain a covering of the manifold with good properties. In fact, if
M
admits a finite covering as in Theorem
2.2
, then the results of Theorem
1
are still true regardless if
M
admits a structure
(J,\omega,\theta)
. Therefore, by deforming the initial complex structure
J
we can actually extend the main result to LCK structures with potential. This can be done for
\dim M\geq 6
due to a result of Ornea and Verbitsky in
[
20
]
. In dimension 4, we check directly using the classification due to Hasegawa below.

Theorem 4.1

(
[
9
]
)
.

Let
(M,J)
be a complex manifold of real dimension
4
. Then,
M
is diffeomorphic to a solvmanifold if, and only if,
(M,J)
is biholomorphic to one of the following classes of complex surfaces:

(1)

Complex tori;

(2)

Hyperelliptic surfaces;

(3)

Inoue surfaces of type
S_{0}
,
S_{+}
and
S_{-}
;

(4)

Primary Kodaira surfaces;

(5)

Secondary Kodaira surfaces.

Each of these complex surfaces is equipped with a left-invariant complex structure as a solvmanifold. In other words, every complex structure in these classes arise from a canonically defined left-invariant complex structure in the associated Lie group.

Therefore, we only need to verify the existence of LCK structures with potential in each of these complex surfaces. We collect the following important results:

•

Complex tori and Hyperelliptic surfaces admits a Kähler structure, thus cannot have a non-GCK structure.

•

Primary Kodaira surfaces are Kodaira-Thurston nilmanifolds. In particular, it admits a Vaisman structure.

•

Secondary Kodaira surfaces are finite quotients of Primary Kodaira surfaces. In particular, they admit a Vaisman structure.

•

The Inoue surfaces of types
S_{0}
,
S_{+}
and
S_{-}
do not admit LCK structures with potential.
[
22
, Corollary 4.13]

We emphasize that given any of these complex surfaces
(M,J)
above, the complex structure uniquely defines the surface inside its class. This means that if one tries to endow
M
with another complex structure
J^{\prime}
, since
M
still has the same diffeomorphism type,
(M,J^{\prime})
is still in the same class. In particular,
J^{\prime}
is also left-invariant. This ensures us that none of the complex surfaces above admits an LCK with potential
(J,\omega,\theta)
for a different
J
than the initial one.

Corollary 4.1
.

Let
M=\Gamma\backslash G
be a solvmanifold. If
M
admits an LCK structure with potential
(J,\omega,\theta)
,
then
M
is diffeomorphic to a finite quotient of a Kodaira-Thurston manifold
N=(\mathbb{Z}\times\Lambda)\backslash(\mathbb{R}\times H_{2n+1})
, for some
\Lambda\leq\Gamma
.

More precisely, we have a smooth finite covering
p:N\to M
in the following diagram.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{R}\times H_{2n+1}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces N}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Gamma\backslash G}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p}

Proof.

Suppose
\dim M\geq 6
. Since
M
admits an LCK structure with potential
(J,\omega,\theta)
, by
[
20
, Theorem 7.3]

M
admits another complex structure
J^{\prime}
such that there exists a Vaisman structure
(J^{\prime},\omega^{\prime},\theta^{\prime})
in
M
. Hence, the conclusion of theorem
1
follows.

For
\dim M=4
, we consider the classification above on complex surfaces diffeomorphic to solvmanifolds. By the results collected above, we saw that the only possible complex surfaces that admit an LCK metric with potential are the primary and secondary Kodaira surfaces, which satisfy the theorem trivially.

5.
Application to Einstein-Weyl LCK case

For the final application of Theorem
1
, we recall the following two results.

Theorem 5.1

(
[
4
,
8
]
)
.

Let
M
be nilmanifold. If
M
admits a Kählerian structure, then
M
is diffeomorphic to a torus.

Theorem 5.2

(
[
15
]
)
.

Let
M=\Gamma\backslash G
be a nilmanifold. Then
H^{p}(M)=H^{p}(\mathfrak{g})
for all
p>0
, where
\mathfrak{g}
is the Lie algebra of
G
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
M
be a solvmanifold. Then,
M
does not admit any Einstein-Weyl LCK structure.

Proof.

Suppose
(J,g_{0},\theta)
is an Einstein-Weyl LCK structure on
M
. Then, by considering the Gauduchon metric
g
,
(M,J,g)
is Vaisman by Corollary
2.1
. Applying the main theorem we obtain that there exists a nilmanifold
N
from
\mathbb{R}\times H(1,n)
which is a finite covering of
M
. We can lift the structure
(J,g,\theta)
to
N
obtaining an Einstein-Weyl LCK structure on
N
. Since Vaisman is a local property,
(N,J,g)
is also a compact Vaisman manifold.

Now, if
N
is a Kähler manifold, by Theorem
5.1
, we have that
N
is diffeomorphic to a torus. But then, by the Rigidity Theorem, this would imply that
\mathbb{R}\times H(1,n)
is isomorphic as a Lie group to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, which is impossible. Hence
N
is not Kähler. Finally, we can apply Theorem
2.5
to obtain that

, however by Theorem
5.2

H^{1}(N)=H^{1}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathfrak{h})
which implies that

, contradiction, since

. Thus,
(J,g_{0},\theta)
cannot be Einstein-Weyl.
∎

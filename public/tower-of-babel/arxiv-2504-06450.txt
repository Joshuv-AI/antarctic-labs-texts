On the Ext Analog of the Euler Characteristic
Benjamin Katz, Andrew J. Soto Levins

Abstract
The Euler form is an Ext analog of the Euler characteristic, and in this paper we study the Euler form and give some applications. The first being a question of Jorgensen, which bounds the projective dimension of a module over a complete intersection by using the vanishing of self extensions. Our second application uses the Euler form to yield a new result involving the vanishing of the higher Herbrand difference. Along the way we translate some of our results to the graded setting.



1
Introduction

Serre’s work on intersection multiplicities is a fundamental topic in commutative algebra. Let
(R,m,k)
be a Noetherian local ring and let
M
and
N
be finitely generated modules. We write
\length(-)
for the length of a module. For a fixed integer
j
, if
\length(\tor_{i}^{R}(M,N))<\infty
for
i\geq j
and if
\tor_{i}^{R}(M,N)=0
for
i\gg 0
, we define the
partial Euler Characteristic

\chi_{j}^{R}(M,N)
to be

\chi_{j}^{R}(M,N)=\sum_{i\geq j}(-1)^{i-j}\length(\tor_{i}^{R}(M,N)).

We write
\chi^{R}(M,N)
for
\chi_{0}^{R}(M,N)
. Recall that
R
is
unramified
if
R
is equicharacteristic or is mixed characteristic and char(
k
)
\not\in m^{2}
. Work of Hochster
[
14
]
and of Lichtenbaum
[
18
]
proves the following.

Theorem 1.1

(Hochster and Lichtenbaum)
.

Let
R
be an unramified regular local ring and let
M
and
N
be finitely generated modules. Let
j\geq 1
be an integer and assume that
\length(\tor_{i}^{R}(M,N))<\infty
for
i\geq j
. Then
\chi_{j}^{R}(M,N)=0
if and only if
\tor_{i}^{R}(M,N)=0
for all
i\geq j
.

For a fixed integer
j
, if
\length(\ext_{R}^{i}(M,N))<\infty
for
i\geq j
and if
\ext_{R}^{i}(M,N)=0
for
i\gg 0
, we define the
partial Euler Form

\xi_{j}^{R}(M,N)
to be

\xi_{j}^{R}(M,N)=\sum_{i\geq j}(-1)^{i-j}\length(\ext_{R}^{i}(M,N)).

We write
\xi^{R}(M,N)
for
\xi_{0}^{R}(M,N)
. The integer
\xi^{R}(M,N)
is called the
Euler Form
and it was first studied in
[
22
]
, also see
[
7
,
20
,
21
]
.

The work in this paper began with the following question: are the partial Euler forms related to the vanishing of Ext in the same way the partial Euler characteristics are related to the vanishing of Tor? The first theorem of this paper is the following. The
grade of
M
against
N
over
R
, written
\grade_{R}(M,N)
, is the index of the first nonzero Ext module of
M
against
N
(see Section
2
for a more detailed definition).

Theorem A

(Lemma
2.5
and Theorem
2.7
)
.

Let
R
be an unramified regular local ring, let
M
and
N
be finitely generated modules with
\length(M\otimes_{R}N)<\infty
and
\dim{M}+\dim{N}<\dim{R}
, and assume
j
is an integer with

1\leq j\leq\grade_{R}M.

Then
\xi_{j}^{R}(M,N)\geq 0
and the following are equivalent.

1.

\xi_{j}^{R}(M,N)=0
.

2.

\grade_{R}(M,N)\geq j
.

3.

\chi_{\pdim_{R}M-j+1}^{R}(M,N)=0
.

This theorem tells us that the partial Euler forms can detect the vanishing of Ext, but not in the same way that the partial Euler characteristics detect the vanishing of Tor. From the equivalence of (1) and (2) and the definition of
\grade_{R}(M,N)
we have
\xi_{j}^{R}(M,N)=0
if and only if
\ext_{R}^{n}(M,N)=0
for
n<j
, whereas
\chi_{j}^{R}(M,N)=0
is equivalent to
\tor_{n}^{R}(M,N)=0
for
n\geq j
. In Example
2.8
we show that the partial Euler forms can be negative, something that cannot happen with the partial Euler characteristics over an unramified regular local ring.

The rest of the paper is devoted to using the Euler form to prove results related to the vanishing of Ext. In Theorem
3.4
we make progress on a question of Jorgensen
[
16
, Question 2.7]
, which asks must
\ext_{R}^{n}(M,M)
be nonzero for
0\leq n\leq\pdim_{R}M
for a nonzero finitely generated module
M
of finite projective dimension over a local complete intersection. In Theorem
4.1
we prove a graded version of a formula of Chan
[
7
, Theorem 5]
. Chan showed that if
M
and
N
are finitely generated modules of finite projective dimension over a local complete intersection
R
with
\length(M\otimes_{R}N)<\infty
, then

\chi^{R}(M,N)=(-1)^{\grade_{R}M}\xi^{R}(M,N).

We end the paper by giving a new case for when the higher Herbrand difference vanishes (see Theorem
5.1
).

The Partial Euler Forms

The goal of this section is to define and study the partial Euler forms. In the proposition below we summarize the facts about the grade of a pair of modules
\grade_{R}(M,N)
that we will use. We denote
\grade_{R}(M,R)
by
\grade_{R}{M}
. The following is
[
4
, Proposition 1.2.10 and Definition 1.2.11]
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
R
be a Noetherian ring and let
M
and
N
be finitely generated modules. Then

\displaystyle\grade_{R}(M,N)=\inf\{n|\ext_{R}^{n}(M,N)\neq 0\}

\displaystyle=\text{ longest $N$ regular sequence in $\ann{M}$}

\displaystyle=\inf\{\depth{N_{p}}|p\in\supp{M}\cap\supp{N}\}.

The following summarizes what is known about the Euler form.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
R
be a local complete intersection and let
M
and
N
be finitely generated modules with
\pdim_{R}M<\infty
,
\pdim_{R}N<\infty
, and
\length(M\otimes_{R}N)<\infty
. Then the following hold.

1.

If
\dim{M}+\dim{N}<\dim{R}
, then
\xi^{R}(M,N)=0
.

2.

If
R
is regular and unramified and
\dim{M}+\dim{N}=\dim{R}
, then

(-1)^{\grade_{R}{M}}\xi^{R}(M,N)>0.

Proof.

In
[
7
, Theorem 5]
Chan proves

(1)

\chi^{R}(M,N)=(-1)^{\grade_{R}{M}}\xi^{R}(M,N).

If
\dim{M}+\dim{N}<\dim{R}
, then
\chi^{R}(M,N)=0
by Serre’s Vanishing Theorem (see
[
10
,
24
]
). Serre’s Positivity Theorem (see
[
26
]
) shows that
\chi^{R}(M,N)>0
if
R
is regular and unramified and
\dim{M}+\dim{N}=\dim{R}
. This and (
1
) proves 2.
∎

Definition 2.3
.

Let
R
be a ring and let
M
and
N
be finitely generated modules. Let
j
be an integer and assume
\length(\ext_{R}^{i}(M,N))<\infty
for
i\leq j
. Define
\overline{\xi}_{j}^{R}(M,N)
to be

\overline{\xi}_{j}^{R}(M,N)=\sum_{i=0}^{j}(-1)^{i}\length(\ext_{R}^{j-i}(M,N)).

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
R
be a local complete intersection and let
M
and
N
be finitely generated modules with
\pdim_{R}M<\infty
,
\pdim_{R}N<\infty
, and
\length(M\otimes_{R}N)<\infty
. If
j
is an integer, then

(-1)^{\grade_{R}{M}+j}\xi_{j}^{R}(M,N)=\chi^{R}(M,N)+(-1)^{\grade_{R}{M}+j}\overline{\xi}_{j-1}^{R}(M,N).

In particular, if
\chi^{R}(M,N)=0
, then
\xi_{j}^{R}(M,N)=\overline{\xi}_{j-1}^{R}(M,N)
.

Proof.

Letting
g=\grade_{R}{M}
, note that
[
7
, Theorem 5]
gives the first equality below

\displaystyle\chi^{R}(M,N)=(-1)^{g}\xi^{R}(M,N)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{g}[(-1)^{j-1}\overline{\xi}_{j-1}^{R}(M,N)+(-1)^{j}\xi_{j}^{R}(M,N)]

\displaystyle=(-1)^{g+j-1}\overline{\xi}_{j-1}^{R}(M,N)+(-1)^{g+j}\xi_{j}^{R}(M,N).\qed

Part 1 of the following gives part of Theorem
A
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
R
be an unramified regular local ring and let
M
and
N
be finitely generated modules with
\length(M\otimes_{R}N)<\infty
. If
j
is an integer with
1\leq j\leq\grade_{R}{M}
, then the following hold.

1.

If
\dim{M}+\dim{N}<\dim{R}
, then
\xi_{j}^{R}(M,N)\geq 0
, and
\xi_{j}^{R}(M,N)=0
if and only if
\grade_{R}(M,N)\geq j
.

2.

If
\dim{M}+\dim{N}=\dim{R}
and
\grade_{R}{M}+j
is even, then
\xi_{j}^{R}(M,N)>0
.

3.

If
\dim{M}+\dim{N}=\dim{R}
,
\grade_{R}{M}+j
is odd, and
\ext_{R}^{j-1}(M,N)=0
, then
\xi_{j}^{R}(M,N)<0
.

Proof.

1. Since
\dim{M}+\dim{N}<\dim{R}
implies
\chi^{R}(M,N)=0
by Serre’s Vanishing Theorem (see
[
26
]
), we have
\xi_{j}^{R}(M,N)=\overline{\xi}_{j-1}^{R}(M,N)
by Lemma
2.4
. Using
[
27
, Lemma 2.6]
, if
j\geq 2
, then
\overline{\xi}_{j-1}^{R}(M,N)\geq 0
, and
\overline{\xi}_{j-1}^{R}(M,N)=0
if and only if
\ext_{R}^{i}(M,N)=0
for
i\leq j-1
. The case
j=1
is clear.

2. Note that
\overline{\xi}_{j-1}^{R}(M,N)\geq 0
for
2\leq j\leq\grade_{R}{M}
by
[
27
, Lemma 2.6]
and we always have
\overline{\xi}_{0}^{R}(M,N)\geq 0
. Lemma
2.4
gives the equality below

\xi_{j}^{R}(M,N)=\chi^{R}(M,N)+\overline{\xi}_{j-1}^{R}(M,N)\geq\chi^{R}(M,N)>0,

and Serre’s Positivity Theorem (see
[
26
]
) gives the second inequality.

3. By Serre’s Positivity Theorem we have
\chi^{R}(M,N)>0
, and so by Lemma
2.4
we have
\overline{\xi}_{j-1}^{R}(M,N)>\xi_{j}^{R}(M,N)
, which implies it is enough to show
\overline{\xi}_{j-1}^{R}(M,N)=0
. Since
\ext_{R}^{j-1}(M,N)=0
and since
j-1\leq\grade_{R}{M}
,
\ext_{R}^{i}(M,N)=0
for
i\leq j-1
by
[
27
, Theorem 2.9]
, and so
\overline{\xi}_{j-1}^{R}(M,N)=0
.
∎

, we first need a lemma.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
(R,m,k)
be an unramified regular local ring, let

and

\length(M\otimes_{R}N)<\infty

Let

,
\chi_{j}^{R}(M,N)=0

3
On a Question of Jorgensen

The goal of this section is to prove Theorem
3.4
. In
[
16
, Question 2.7]
Jorgensen asked if
\ext_{R}^{n}(M,M)
must be nonzero for
0\leq n\leq\pdim_{R}M
for a finitely generated module
M
of finite projective dimension over a local complete intersection. The answer to this question is yes if
R
is regular
[
17
]
or an unramified hypersurface
[
9
, Proposition 5.4]
. Also, in
[
27
, Theorem 2.9]
it was shown that if
M
is a finitely generated module, not necessarily of finite projective dimension, over an unramified hypersurface
R
, then
\ext_{R}^{n}(M,M)\neq 0
for
0\leq n\leq\grade_{R}M
. Note that we always have
\grade_{R}M\leq\pdim_{R}M
, and equality holds when
M
is CM and has finite projective dimension.

Before proving Theorem
3.4
, we first prove three lemmas.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
(R,m,k)
be a Noetherian local ring and let
M
and
N
be finitely generated modules with
\pdim_{R}M<\infty
and
\length(N)<\infty
. If
\grade_{R}M>0
, then
\xi^{R}(M,N)=0
and
\chi^{R}(M,N)=0
.

Proof.

Note that
\grade_{R}M>0
is equivalent to
\ann{M}
containing a nonzerodivisor. We only prove
\xi^{R}(M,N)=0
. To show
\chi^{R}(M,N)=0
is similar. Since
\ann{M}
contains a nonzerodivisor, if
\length(N)=1
, then
[
19
, Theorem 19.8]
gives us the fourth equality below:

\xi^{R}(M,N)=\xi^{R}(M,k)=\sum_{i=0}^{\pdim_{R}M}(-1)^{i}\length(\ext_{R}^{i}(M,k))=\sum_{i=0}^{\pdim_{R}M}(-1)^{i}\beta_{i}(M)=0,

where
\beta_{i}(M)
is the
ith
Betti number of
M
. An induction on
\length(N)
using that
\xi^{R}(M,-)
is additive on short exact sequences finishes the proof.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
R
be a Noetherian local ring with
\dim{R}\leq 2
, and let
M
and
N
be nonzero finitely generated modules with
\pdim_{R}M<\infty
and
\length(N)<\infty
. If
\grade_{R}M>0
, then
\ext_{R}^{n}(M,N)\neq 0
and
\tor_{n}^{R}(M,N)\neq 0
for
0\leq n\leq\pdim_{R}M
.

Proof.

We prove the statement for Ext. The argument for the Tor statement is similar. Since
\Hom_{R}(M,N)\neq 0
and
\ext_{R}^{\pdim_{R}M}(M,N)\neq 0
, we can assume
\pdim_{R}M=\dim{R}=2
. By Lemma
3.1
we have
\xi^{R}(M,N)=0
. If
\ext_{R}^{1}(M,N)=0
, then we would have

\xi^{R}(M,N)=\length(\Hom_{R}(M,N))+\length(\ext_{R}^{2}(M,N))>0,

a contradiction.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
R
be a CM ring, let
M
be a finitely generated module, and choose
p\in\supp{M}
so that
\dim{R/p}=\dim{M}
. Then
\grade_{R}{M}=\grade_{R_{p}}M_{p}
.

Proof.

Notice that

\height{p}=\dim{R_{p}}=\depth{{R_{p}}}=\grade_{R_{p}}M_{p}.

Also, if
q\in\supp{M}
, then

\dim{R}-\height{q}=\dim{R/q}\leq\dim{R/p}=\dim{R}-\height{p}

where the first and second equalities are because CM rings are catenary (
[
19
, Theorem 17.9]
), and so
\height{p}\leq\height{q}
. This tells us that

\grade_{R}{M}=\inf\{\depth{R_{q}}|q\in\supp{M}\}=\inf\{\height{q}|q\in\supp{M}\}=\height{p}.\qed

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
R
be a Noetherian ring and let
M
be a nonzero finitely generated module with
\pdim_{R}M<\infty
. Assume either
R
is local with equidimensional completion or
R
is CM. If
\grade_{R}M\leq 2
, then the following hold

1.

\ext_{R}^{n}(M,M)\neq 0
for
0\leq n\leq\grade_{R}M
.

2.

\tor_{n}^{R}(M,M)\neq 0
for
0\leq n\leq\grade_{R}M
.

Proof.

First assume that
R
is local with equidimensional completion. Since
\widehat{R}
is a faithfully flat extension of
R
, we can assume
R
is complete. Let
p
be a minimal prime of
M
so that
\dim{R/p}=\dim{M}
. Notice that

\displaystyle\grade_{R_{p}}M_{p}\leq\height{\ann_{R_{p}}M_{p}}\leq\dim{R_{p}}=\height{p}

\displaystyle\leq\dim{R}-\dim{R/p}

\displaystyle=\dim{R}-\dim{M}

\displaystyle=\grade_{R}M.

The last equality is by
[
2
, Theorem 3.6]
since
R
is equidimensional and
\pdim_{R}M<\infty
. Since
\grade_{R}M\leq\grade_{R_{p}}M_{p}
holds in general, this shows
\grade_{R}M=\grade_{R_{p}}M_{p}
. Therefore

\dim{R_{p}}=\pdim_{R_{p}}M_{p}=\grade_{R_{p}}M_{p}=\grade_{R}M\leq 2,

and so the theorem follows from Lemma
3.2
.

Now assume
R
is CM. By Lemma
3.3
we have
\grade_{R}{M}=\grade_{R_{p}}M_{p}
where
p
is a minimal prime of
M
with
\dim{R/p}=\dim{M}
, and so the result follows from the local case by localizing at
p
.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

Recall that given a homomorphism
f\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}Q\rightarrow R
of rings, an
R
module
M

lifts
to a
Q

Modules over regular local rings, modules of finite projective dimension over local unramified hypersurfaces, and modules that lift to a regular local ring are Tor rigid, and known cases of Jorgensen’s question use this fact. In fact, in

for

. In general modules of finite projective dimension are not Tor rigid; Heitmann gave the first example in
[

]
.

4
On a Formula of Chan

The goal of this section is to prove Theorem
4.1
. Before doing this, we need to set up some notation. The notation we use here is from
[
23
, Section 6.3]
. Let
R
be a Noetherian
\mathbb{Z}
graded ring with
R_{0}
Artinian and let
M
be a finitely generated graded module. For an integer
k
, the
shift
of
M
by
k
, denoted by
M[k]
, is the graded module where
M[k]_{n}=M_{n+k}
. The
Hilbert polynomial
of
M
, denoted by
P_{M}
, is the polynomial where
P_{M}(n)=\length_{R_{0}}(\oplus_{k\leq n}M_{k})
for
n\gg 0
. The degree of this polynomial is the dimension of
M
. The
ith
derivative of
P_{M}
is denoted by
P_{M}^{(i)}
.

Let
F
be a graded free resolution of
M
with each
F_{i}
finitely generated and
F_{i}=\oplus R[n_{ij}]
. Define
c_{k}
to be

c_{k}=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{i,j}(-1)^{i}n_{ij}^{k}.

The first part of our proof of the following theorem closely follows
[
23
, Theorem 6.3.1]
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
R
be a Noetherian
\mathbb{Z}
graded ring with
R_{0}
Artinian, and let
M
and
N
be finitely generated graded modules with
\length(M\otimes_{R}N)<\infty
and
\pdim_{R}M<\infty
. Then

\chi^{R}(M,N)=(-1)^{\grade_{R}M}\xi^{R}(M,N).

Proof.

Let
F
be a graded free resolution of
M
with each
F_{i}
finitely generated and
F_{i}=\oplus R[n_{ij}]
and let
k_{0}
be the smallest integer
k
so that
c_{k}\neq 0
. Then
k_{0}=\dim{R}-\dim{M}
by the discussion after
[
23
, Theorem 6.3.1]
, and the discussion after the proof of
[
23
, Theorem 6.3.1]
gives the second equality below

\chi^{R}(M,N)=\sum_{i}(-1)^{i}P_{\tor_{i}^{R}(M,N)}=\sum_{k}c_{k}P_{N}^{(k)}=\sum_{k\geq k_{0}}c_{k}P_{N}^{(k)}.

Also, we have
\dim{M}+\dim{N}\leq\dim{R}
by
[
23
, Theorem 6.3.2]
, and so
P_{N}^{(k)}=0
for
k>k_{0}
. This implies
\sum_{k\geq k_{0}}c_{k}P_{N}^{(k)}=c_{k_{0}}P_{N}^{(k_{0})}
, and so

(2)

\chi^{R}(M,N)=c_{k_{0}}P_{N}^{(k_{0})}

Since

\Hom_{R}(R[n],N)\cong N[-n],

\Hom_{R}(F,N)
is a complex where
\Hom_{R}(F,N)_{-i}\cong\oplus N[-n_{ij}]
. This gives the third equality below

\displaystyle\sum_{i}(-1)^{i}P_{\ext_{R}^{i}(M,N)}(n)=\sum_{i}(-1)^{i}P_{H_{-i}(\Hom_{R}(F,N))}(n)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}(-1)^{i}P_{\Hom_{R}(F,N)_{-i}}(n)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j}(-1)^{i}P_{N}(n-n_{ij})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j,k}(-1)^{i}P_{N}^{(k)}(n)\frac{(-n_{ij})^{k}}{k!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}\frac{P_{N}^{(k)}(n)}{k!}(\sum_{i,j}(-1)^{i+k}n_{ij}^{k})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}\frac{P_{N}^{(k)}(n)}{k!}(\sum_{i,j}(-1)^{i}(-1)^{k}n_{ij}^{k})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}(-1)^{k}\frac{P_{N}^{(k)}(n)}{k!}(\sum_{i,j}(-1)^{i}n_{ij}^{k})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}(-1)^{k}c_{k}P_{N}^{(k)}(n)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{k_{0}}c_{k_{0}}P_{N}^{(k_{0})}(n).

The additivity of Hilbert polynomials with respect to exact sequences gives the second equality and the discussion before
[
23
, Theorem 6.3.1]
gives the fourth equality. Therefore

(-1)^{k_{0}}\sum_{i}(-1)^{i}P_{\ext_{R}^{i}(M,N)}(n)=c_{k_{0}}P_{N}^{(k_{0})}(n)

for all
n
, giving the second equality below

(-1)^{k_{0}}\xi^{R}(M,N)=(-1)^{k_{0}}\sum_{i}(-1)^{i}P_{\ext_{R}^{i}(M,N)}=c_{k_{0}}P_{N}^{(k_{0})}.

Since
\grade_{R}M=\dim{R}-\dim{M}
by
[
23
, Theorem 6.3.4]
, this and (
2
) completes the proof.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

The formula in Theorem
4.1
was first proved in
[
7
, Theorem 5]
for local complete intersections when both modules have finite projective dimension. A similar graded result was proved in
[
20
, Corollary 3.11]
when
R
is positively graded,
R_{0}
is a field, and without the finite projective dimension assumption.

The following is the graded version of Theorem
3.4
.

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
R
be a Noetherian
\mathbb{Z}
graded ring with
R_{0}
Artinian and
\dim{R}\leq 2
, and let
M
and
N
be nonzero finite length graded modules with
\pdim_{R}M<\infty
. If
R
has a unique graded maximal ideal, then
\ext_{R}^{n}(M,N)\neq 0
for
0\leq n\leq\pdim_{R}M
.

Proof.

Since

\pdim_{R}M=\sup\{\pdim_{R_{p}}M_{p}|p\in\supp{M}\}\leq\sup\{\dim{R_{p}}|p\in\spec{R}\}\leq\dim{R},

we can assume
\pdim_{R}M=2
. This is because
\Hom_{R}(M,N)\neq 0
, and
\ext_{R}^{\pdim_{R}M}(M,N)\neq 0
by the graded version of Nakayama’s Lemma (see
[
4
, Exercise 1.5.24.a]
). If
\ext_{R}^{1}(M,N)=0
, then we would have
\xi^{R}(M,N)>0
, giving

\chi^{R}(M,N)=(-1)^{\grade_{R}M}\xi^{R}(M,N)=\xi^{R}(M,N)>0.

The first equality is by Theorem
4.1
, and the second equality is because

\grade_{R}M=\dim{R}-\dim{M}

by
[
23
, Theorem 6.3.4]
. But
\chi^{R}(M,N)=0
by
[
23
, Theorem 6.3.2]
, giving a contradiction.
∎

Example 4.4
.

A ring that satisfies the assumptions in Corollary
4.3
is the
\mathbb{Z}
graded ring
R=k[x,y]/(x^{2})
where
k
is a field, the degree of
x
is -1, the degree of
y
is 1. Another example is a quotient of a standard graded polynomial ring over a field by a homogeneous ideal.

5
The Euler Form and The Higher Herbrand Difference

The goal of this section is to give a new case for when the higher Herbrand difference is zero. Before doing this, we need to review some definitions. Let
(R,m,k)
be a Noetherian local ring and let
M
and
N
be finitely generated modules. We write
\nu_{R}(M)
for the minimal number of generators of
M
. The
complexity
of the pair
(M,N)
, denoted by
\cx_{R}(M,N)
, is

\cx_{R}(M,N)=\inf\{b\in\mathbb{N}|\nu_{R}(\ext_{R}^{n}(M,N))\leq an^{b-1}\text{ for some real number }a\text{ and for all }n\gg 0\}.

The
complexity
of
M
is
\cx_{R}M=\cx_{R}(M,k)
and the
plexity
of
M
is
\px_{R}M=\cx_{R}(k,M)
. Let

f_{tor}^{R}(M,N)=\inf\{s|\length(\tor_{i}^{R}(M,N))<\infty\text{ for all }i\geq s\},

and let

f_{ext}^{R}(M,N)=\inf\{s|\length(\ext_{R}^{i}(M,N))<\infty\text{ for all }i\geq s\}.

Let
e
be an nonnegative integer. If
f_{tor}^{R}(M,N)<\infty
, we define
\eta_{e}^{R}(M,N)
to be

\eta_{e}^{R}(M,N)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\sum_{i=f_{tor}^{R}(M,N)}^{n}(-1)^{i}\length(\tor_{i}^{R}(M,N))}{n^{e}}.

If
f_{ext}^{R}(M,N)<\infty
, we define the higher Herbrand difference
h_{e}^{R}(M,N)
to be

h_{e}^{R}(M,N)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\sum_{i=f_{ext}^{R}(M,N)}^{n}(-1)^{i}\length(\ext_{R}^{i}(M,N))}{n^{e}}.

The invariant
\eta_{e}^{R}(M,N)
was defined in
[
8
]
, and it is a generalization of the Euler characteristic and Hochsters Theta Pairing (
[
13
]
). It was used to study the asymptotic behavior of Tor. The invariant
h_{e}^{R}(M,N)
was defined in
[
6
]
, and it is a generalization of the Euler Form and the Herbrand difference (
[
5
]
). It was used to study the asymptotic behavior of Ext.

The following is an Ext version of a theorem of Dao, and our proof mirrors his closely. In
[
8
, Theorem 6.3 and Corollary 8.3]
Dao showed that if
M
and
N
are finitely generated modules over a codimension
r
local complete intersection
R
with
f_{tor}^{R}(M,N)<\infty
and
\eta_{r}^{R}(M,N)=0
and if
n\geq 0
, then

\tor_{n}^{R}(M,N)=\dots=\tor_{n+r-1}(M,N)=0

implies
\tor_{i}^{R}(M,N)=0
for
i\geq n
. If
\length(M\otimes_{R}N)<\infty
also holds, then

\dim{M}+\dim{N}\leq\dim{R}+c-1

implies
\eta_{c}^{R}(M,N)=0
, where
c=\max\{\cx_{R}M,\cx_{R}N\}
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
R
be a local complete intersection with infinite residue field, let
M
and
N
be finitely generated modules with
\length(M\otimes_{R}N)<\infty
, and let
c=\max\{\cx_{R}M,\cx_{R}N\}
. Assume

\dim{M}+\dim{N}\leq\dim{R}+c-1.

Then
h_{c}^{R}(M,N)=0
. In particular, If

\ext_{R}^{n}(M,N)=\dots=\ext_{R}^{n+c-1}(M,N)=0

for some
n>\depth{R}-\depth{M}
, then
\ext_{R}^{i}(M,N)=0
for all
i>\depth{R}-\depth{M}
.

Proof.

If
c>\cx_{R}(M,N)
, then the result follows from
[
6
, Theorem 3.4.1 and Corollary 4.3]
, and so we can assume
c=\cx_{R}(M,N)
. We can also assume
R
is complete. Then by
[
15
, Theorem 1.3]
there exists a complete intersection
Q
and a
Q
regular sequence
x_{1},\dots,x_{c}
that satisfies the following properties

1.

R\cong Q/(x_{1},\dots,x_{c})
.

2.

If
R_{j}=Q/(x_{1},\dots,x_{j})
, then
\cx_{R_{j}}M=\cx_{R}M-(c-j)
.

3.

\cx_{Q}M=0
and
\cx_{Q}N=0
.

Since
\cx_{Q}M=\cx_{Q}N=0
, we have
\pdim_{Q}M<\infty
and
\pdim_{Q}N<\infty
. Also, our assumptions on dimension gives the following

\displaystyle\dim{M}+\dim{N}\leq\dim{R}+c-1=\dim{Q/(x_{1},\dots,x_{c})}+c-1

\displaystyle=\dim{Q}-c+c-1

\displaystyle=\dim{Q}-1

\displaystyle<\dim{Q}.

Proposition
2.2
now gives
\xi^{Q}(M,N)=0
, and
[
6
, Theorem 3.4.3]
gives the first equality below

2^{c}\cdot h_{c}^{R}(M,N)=h_{0}^{Q}(M,N)=\xi^{Q}(M,N)=0.

The second equality is by definition. Therefore the result follows from
[
6
, Theorem 4.2]
.
∎

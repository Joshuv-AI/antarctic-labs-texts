Electric Field Effects on the Optoelectronic Properties of Fluorinated Heptyl-Bicyclohexyl-Biphenyl: A TD-DFT Investigation
Authors: Jalloul Trabelsi, Mounir Esboui
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
In this study, the electronic, optical, and dielectric properties of fluorinated heptyl-bicyclohexyl-biphenyl are investigated using density functional theory (DFT) and time-dependent DFT. The ground-state geometry is optimized and confirmed to be stable by vibrational analysis, while the low-lying excited states are mainly characterized by ππ* transitions localized on the biphenyl core. In addition, a high energy excited state is identified with the largest oscillator strength (f = 0.8994) at 6.57 eV, corresponding to a πσ* intramolecular charge transfer transition from the biphenyl moiety to the bicyclohexyl substituent. External electric fields are applied along the molecular long axis, corresponding to the direction of maximum polarizability, as well as along two perpendicular directions. The strongest effects on absorption spectra, dipole moment, polarizability, and the HOMO–LUMO gap occur when the field aligns with the molecular long axis. In particular, a dramatic polarizability jump from 317 to 2990 Bohr3 accompanies the field-induced closure of the HOMO–LUMO gap, evidencing substantial charge redistribution and enhanced mixing of frontier molecular orbitals. In contrast, the responses induced by perpendicular field orientations remain comparatively weak.

Introduction

Liquid crystals are anisotropic fluids exhibiting an intermediate phase between crystalline solids and isotropic liquids, giving rise to distinctive optical and physical properties. , Among them, fluorinated liquid crystals have been widely investigated because fluorine strongly influences mesomorphic behavior and related physical characteristics. , Specifically, in liquid crystal materials, fluorine improves device stability by reducing interactions with ionic impurities, tuning molecular dipole moments and their orientation, stabilizing conformations, and increasing alkyl chain rigidity. Fluorination modifies thermal transitions, mesophase organization, and dielectric, optical, and viscoelastic anisotropies while maintaining liquid-crystalline order. , Its impact on the electronic and optoelectronic properties of dimeric liquid crystals has also been examined using density functional theory (DFT) methods.

Beyond the field of materials science, fluorination chemistry has advanced in aqueous and biological environments. Recent strategies include hydrophilic building blocks enabling efficient radiofluorination in water and late-stage electrophilic activation methods for the mild fluorination of alkyl phosphonates, broadening access to fluorinated complex molecules.

Understanding these fundamental chemical properties is crucial because the operation of liquid crystal displays relies on the alignment of molecular dipoles under applied electric fields, a process directly governed by molecular polarizability, the ability to develop an induced dipole in response to an external field. , Therefore, dipole moments and polarizabilities are key parameters for understanding the behavior of organic and liquid crystalline materials in advanced applications. Dipole moments reflect charge distribution and influence intermolecular interactions, while polarizability determines the response to electric fields, playing a central role in dielectric, nonlinear optical, and electro-optical devices. −

This fundamental understanding is directly applicable to device engineering, as recent advances in photonic and electro-optical materials have demonstrated that controlling light–matter interactions and polar ordering is essential for improving device performance, as evidenced by enhanced light extraction strategies in deep-ultraviolet optoelectronic systems and the engineered unipolar and bipolar polar textures in nematic fluids enabling miniaturized nonlinear vectorial optics. ,

The present work is part of our continuing effort to understand the response of fluorinated liquid crystals under an external electric field. In a recent study, we reported a time-dependent DFT (TD-DFT) investigation of the electric-field-dependent polarizability and dipole moment in 3,4,5-trifluorobicyclohexyl biphenyl liquid crystals, revealing a pronounced nonlinear response associated with field-induced modifications of the electronic structure. Building on these findings, the present study focuses on fluorinated heptylbicyclohexylbiphenyl and explores how both the strength and orientation of an external electric field influence its optoelectronic properties and anisotropic polarization behavior.

To this end, we perform a comprehensive TD-DFT analysis of the molecule under applied electric fields. The effects of field magnitude and direction are systematically examined through changes in the electronic structure, absorption spectra, dipole moment, polarizability, and the HOMO–LUMO energy gap. Particular attention is given to anisotropic and nonlinear field-induced effects, emphasizing the role of molecular orientation in governing Stark shifts, charge redistribution, and frontier orbital mixing. These results provide deeper insight into the field-tunable behavior of fluorinated liquid crystals and offer useful guidance for the design of advanced electro-optical materials.

Computational Details

All calculations were performed using the Gaussian 09 program package. DFT calculations were carried out employing the Coulomb-attenuating method with the hybrid exchange–correlation functional CAM-B3LYP. − The ground-state geometry of the fluorinated heptyl-bicyclohexyl-biphenyl molecule was fully optimized without any symmetry constraints. The 6-31++G­(d,p) basis set, which includes both polarization and diffuse functions, was used for all atoms. Vibrational frequency calculations were subsequently performed to confirm that the optimized structure corresponds to a true minimum on the potential energy surface, as indicated by the absence of imaginary frequencies. Subsequently, the electronic absorption spectra of the molecule under external electric fields of varying strength and orientation were calculated using TD-DFT at the CAM-B3LYP/6-31++G­(d,p) level. This approach ensures a reliable description of excited states and field-induced effects, particularly for charge transfer and Stark shift phenomena.

As indicated in Table , the lowest singlet excited state (S 1) exhibits a localized ππ* character, with a vertical excitation energy of 5.13 eV in the UV region. Its modest oscillator strength (f = 0.0125) indicates weak absorption intensity. It is worth noting that the three lowest excited states (S 1–S 3) lie very close in energy, within a narrow range of about 0.08 eV, suggesting similar orbital origins and electronic environments. As shown in Figure , the excited configurations reveal that the π orbital (typically HOMO or nearby occupied orbitals) can be localized either across the biphenyl system or confined to a single phenyl ring, while the π* orbital (LUMO or LUMO + n) is generally delocalized over the entire biphenyl moiety. Of particular interest is the strong HOMO → LUMO transition (S 3), which has a relatively high oscillator strength of f = 0.5322, indicating that it corresponds to an optically bright state. States S 2–S 9 largely retain ππ* character with varying oscillator strengths, the largest being that of S 8 (f = 0.2040).

In contrast to these lower-lying ππ* states, S 10 exhibits the highest oscillator strength (f = 0.8994) with a vertical excitation energy of 6.57 eV. This state corresponds to a π → σ* intramolecular charge transfer (ICT) transition (HOMO–2 → LUMO + 4, as shown in Figure ), in which the π orbital is localized on the biphenyl moiety, while the σ* orbital is localized on the bicyclohexyl substituent. This contrasts with the other ICT character observed in this system, where electron delocalization occurs primarily between phenyl groups within the conjugated biphenyl core (Figure ).

The fluorinated heptyl-bicyclohexyl-biphenyl is characterized by a dipole moment μ = 2.56 D and polarizability α = 315.8 Bohr3. In a related study, a series of fluorinated benzoxazole derivatives (F1–F4) was theoretically investigated using DFT calculations to elucidate the influence of fluorine substitution on molecular electronic properties. Moreover, in our previous work, TD-DFT studies on fluorinated liquid crystals C n F2(COX) revealed that substituent variation (O, S, Se) significantly influences polarizability and optical properties, emphasizing the substituent effect. Effect of the Electric Field on Absorption Spectra: Stark Effect

The electronic absorption spectra of the fluorinated heptyl-bicyclohexyl-biphenyl molecule were investigated using TD-DFT calculations to assess the influence of an external electric field and its orientation. In the absence of an external electric field, two main absorption bands are observed in the ultraviolet region, as shown in Figure : a relatively narrow high-energy band extending from 160 to 225 nm and a broader low-energy band spanning from 205 to 280 nm. These absorption features are mainly attributed to ππ* electronic transitions within the biphenyl core, with additional contributions from the fluorinated and alkyl substituents, which enhance excited-state delocalization.

Calculated UV–Vis electronic absorption spectra of fluorinated heptyl-bicyclohexyl-biphenyl under a weak external electric field (0–3 V/nm) applied along the molecular Z axis. Computed at the TD-DFT/CAM-B3LYP/6-31++G­(d,p) level.

To evaluate the field-induced response on the optical properties, an external electric field was applied along the molecular Z axis, as illustrated in Figure . Under these conditions, pronounced modifications in both the positions and intensities of the absorption bands were observed. The absorption intensity decreases rapidly with increasing field strength, and the main absorption band vanishes at an electric field of 3.5 V/nm, indicating substantial excited state stabilization accompanied by a significant reorganization of the electronic structure due to enhanced polarization effects (Figure ).

When the electric field is tilted, forming an angle θ≈π4 with respect to the molecular Z axis (Figure S1), both absorption bands undergo progressive redshifts accompanied by a more gradual decrease in intensity. The first absorption band steadily loses intensity and vanishes only at a higher field strength of 5 V/nm. As shown in Figure , the second band is also strongly attenuated at 5 V/nm, becoming significantly weaker than at 4.5 V/nm. Therefore, compared with the Z-aligned field configuration, a similar weakening of the absorption intensity occurs only at substantially higher field strengths.

This pronounced orientation dependence highlights the anisotropic electronic response of the molecule and confirms that the direction of the applied electric field plays a decisive role in controlling the absorption characteristics through the Stark-induced reordering of the electronic energy levels.

Effect of the Electric Field on Polarizability, Dipole Moment, and HOMO–LUMO Gap

The external electric field was applied successively along the molecular long axis, corresponding to the direction of maximum polarizability, along the two mutually perpendicular axes, and along a direction forming an angle θ≈π4 with the molecular Z axis. The results reveal a strong directional dependence of the molecular response: significant variations in dipole moment and polarizability are observed only when the field is applied along the molecular long axis, whereas negligible changes occur for fields applied along the perpendicular directions, as shown in Figures S2 and S3. Figure S4 presents the evolution of the parallel polarizability α∥, obtained when the electric field is applied along the molecular long axis, the perpendicular polarizability α⊥, corresponding to a field applied orthogonally to this axis, and the polarizability anisotropy defined as Δα = α∥ – α⊥. A pronounced molecular anisotropy is clearly observed over the entire investigated field range.

The significant difference between α∥ and α⊥ demonstrates that the electronic density is considerably more deformable along the conjugated molecular backbone than in the transverse direction. This directional electronic flexibility generates a strong field-induced torque, thereby favoring molecular alignment along the applied electric field. Such anisotropic behavior highlights the dominant role of molecular orientation in governing field-induced electronic effects.

When the electric field is applied along the principal molecular axis, pronounced Stark shifts and a highly nonlinear evolution of both the dipole moment and polarizability are observed. Several distinct response regimes can be clearly distinguished, closely correlated with field-driven electronic density redistribution and modifications of the HOMO–LUMO energy gap.

Aligning an external electric field with the molecular Z-axis induces strong electronic polarization in fluoroheptyl-bicyclohexyl-biphenyl, leading to the formation of well-defined positive and negative charge centers. As illustrated in Figure , this polarization leads to a pronounced enhancement of the dipole moment, which increases from 2.56 D in the field-free case to significantly higher values under the applied electric field.

Calculated electric-field dependence of the dipole moment under an external electric field applied along the molecular Z-axis for the fluorinated heptyl-bicyclohexyl-biphenyl molecule, computed at the TD-DFT/CAM-B3LYP/6-31++G­(d,p) level.

The molecular response to the applied field is markedly nonlinear for both the polarizability and dipole moment. Four distinct regimes can be identified as the field strength increases from 0 to 16 V/nm.

In the first regime, corresponding to weak fields (E < 5 V/nm), as shown in Figure , the polarizability remains nearly constant at approximately 317 Bohr3, while the dipole moment increases linearly with a moderate slope (Figure ). This behavior is consistent with classical polarization theory, where the applied field induces only a limited electronic redistribution without significantly perturbing the molecular electronic structure.

Calculated dependence of the polarizability and energy gap on an external electric field applied along the molecular Z-axis for fluorinated heptyl-bicyclohexyl-biphenyl, computed at the TD-DFT/CAM-B3LYP/6-31++G­(d,p) level.

In the second regime (5–8 V/nm), an abrupt transition occurs: the polarizability increases sharply to about 2990 Bohr3, accompanied by a strong enhancement of the dipole moment. As illustrated in Figure , this transition coincides with the closure of the HOMO–LUMO gap, indicating substantial mixing of the frontier orbitals under the influence of the electric field. The resulting quasi-degeneracy promotes orbital hybridization and pronounced electron density delocalization across the molecular framework, thereby greatly amplifying the electronic response.

In the third regime (9–13 V/nm), the HOMO–LUMO gap reopens to approximately 1.6 eV, the frontier orbitals become energetically separated again, and the polarizability decreases to around 805 Bohr3. This behavior reflects a saturation effect, in which further electron density redistribution becomes energetically constrained. The dipole moment continues to increase in this regime, albeit with a substantially reduced slope.

The reduction of the energy gap between adjacent electronic states under the applied electric field may enhance nonradiative relaxation via internal conversion. According to the energy gap law, the rate of radiationless transitions increases as the energetic separation between states decreases, due to stronger vibronic coupling and enhanced nonadiabatic interactions. Consequently, compression of the excited-state manifold at higher field strengths may facilitate population transfer between states, leading to attenuation of absorption intensity without invoking structural degradation or ionization.

When the electric field is applied along a direction forming an angle θ≈π4 with respect to the molecular Z-axis, a comparably strong electronic polarization is observed. As illustrated in Figure a, the induced dipole moment starts at approximately 1.9 D and subsequently increases with increasing field strength. In the weak-field regime (E < 6.5 V/nm), the polarizability remains nearly constant at about 317 Bohr3, while the dipole moment increases linearly from its initial value, reflecting its progressive enhancement with increasing electric field strength.

Calculated dependence of the dipole moment (a), polarizability, and energy gap (b) on the applied electric field at an angle θ≈π4 for fluorinated heptyl-bicyclohexyl-biphenyl, computed at the TD-DFT/CAM-B3LYP/6-31++G­(d,p) level.

As shown in Figure b, in the second regime (7–12 V/nm), the polarizability rises sharply to approximately 2841 Bohr3, simultaneously with a pronounced increase in the dipole moment and the closure of the HOMO–LUMO gap. In the third regime (13–17 V/nm), the gap reappears at around 0.68 eV, the polarizability decreases to roughly 872 Bohr3, and the growth of the dipole moment becomes progressively weaker, indicating saturation of the field-induced electronic redistribution.

Conclusion

In this work, a comprehensive TD-DFT investigation was carried out to elucidate the electric field-dependent optoelectronic properties of fluorinated heptyl-bicyclohexyl-biphenyl. The results demonstrate that the molecule exhibits a strongly anisotropic and nonlinear response to externally applied electric fields. The absorption spectra, dipole moment, polarizability, and HOMO–LUMO energy gap are all highly sensitive to both the magnitude and orientation of the applied field.

A clear and quantitative correlation has been established between the abrupt enhancement of polarizability and the field-induced closure of the HOMO–LUMO gap, revealing that strong frontier orbital mixing governs the nonlinear electronic response. Distinct polarization regimes were identified, ranging from weak field linear behavior to a high field regime characterized by gap reopening and electronic saturation. In addition, pronounced Stark shifts and field-induced attenuation of absorption bands were observed, reflecting substantial electronic density redistribution under strong polarization conditions.

Beyond these fundamental insights, the present study provides a molecular-level framework for understanding electric field control in fluorinated liquid crystal systems. The strong orientation dependence highlights the decisive role of molecular alignment in tuning the electro-optical behavior, which is central to advanced liquid crystal and photonic device engineering.

Future research directions may include the incorporation of explicit intermolecular interactions and condensed-phase effects to better represent realistic liquid-crystal environments. The extension of the present approach to supramolecular assemblies and mesophase models would allow for the evaluation of collective polarization and local field corrections. Furthermore, nonadiabatic excited-state dynamics simulations under external electric fields could provide deeper insight into field-enhanced internal conversion mechanisms and absorption quenching processes. Finally, systematic investigations of structurally related fluorinated architectures may enable rational optimization of dipole moment anisotropy and polarizability for next-generation electro-optical and nonlinear optical materials.

Preoperative Weight Loss Before Total Joint Arthroplasty Using Novel Glucagon-Like Peptide-1 Agonists
Authors: Michael W. Seward, Emmett J. Cleary, Anthony C. DeNovio, Jessica A. Grimm, Timothy B. Curry, Brian B. Beam, Charles P. Hannon, Matthew P. Abdel
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThis study determined the prevalence of glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1) use before total joint arthroplasty (TJA), measured weight loss of patients on GLP-1s, and evaluated if GLP-1s were associated with outcomes.MethodsWe identified 6482 patients (2990 hips; 3492 knees) who had primary TJAs between 2019 and February 2023. The mean age was 67 years, 50% were female, and 12% had a body mass index ≥40 kg/m2. Univariable and multivariable models evaluated outcomes. Mean follow-up was 3 years.ResultsThe prevalence of GLP-1s before TJA was 3%, increasing from 2% in 2019 to 5% in 2022. The mean preoperative weight change on GLP-1s was −9 pounds (range, −110 to +36 pounds). Among patients taking GLP-1s, 19% lost ≥20 pounds prior to surgery, and the mean body mass index change was −1.4 kg/m2 (range, −16.3 to 6.2 kg/m2). In univariable analyses, GLP-1 use was not associated with revisions (P = .35), reoperations (P = .09), or complications (P = .23). In multivariable analyses, GLP-1s were not associated with revisions (P = .66), reoperations (P = .34), or complications (P = .85). There was no difference in the risk of a 90-day postoperative emergency department visit between those on GLP-1s vs not on those medications (odds ratio, 1.5; P = .07).ConclusionsThe prevalence of preoperative GLP-1s before TJA increased from 2% in 2019 to 5% in 2022, and preoperative weight change on GLP-1s varied considerably. GLP-1s were not associated with decreased postoperative risks but investigational GLP-1s before TJA require further study.Level of EvidenceLevel III.

Introduction

Obesity is associated with increased postoperative risks after total joint arthroplasty (TJA), [1] and by 2029 over 55% of primary total hip arthroplasty (THA) patients and over 69% of primary total knee arthroplasty (TKA) patients are projected to have obesity [2,3]. A systematic review of studies evaluating nonoperative weight loss prior to TJA found that short-term intensive interventions may be associated with substantial weight loss, [4] and novel glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonists (GLP-1s) may give orthopaedic surgeons a new tool to promote weight loss before TJA.

GLP-1s are approved for the management of diabetes and obesity. These medications are associated with weight loss [5], improved glucose control [6], and decreased cardiovascular mortality [7,8] such that a recent survey found up to 12% of American adults have tried a GLP-1 [9]. Although there are more patients undergoing TJA while taking GLP-1s or are under consideration for starting these medications for weight loss as part of preoperative optimization, there is limited research on the impact of these medications on preoperative weight loss and postoperative outcomes [[10], [11], [12]]. More studies are needed to inform perioperative management of these medications and to better counsel patients on the use of these medications leading up to surgery.

Thus, the purpose of this study was to determine the preoperative prevalence of GLP-1 use before TJA, to measure preoperative weight loss on GLP-1s, and to evaluate whether preoperative GLP-1s were associated with postoperative outcomes.

Material and methods

All patients with body mass indices (BMIs) ≥27 kg/m2 at any time between surgery to 2 years before primary unilateral TJA between 2019 and 2023 were identified via our institutional total joint registry. The minimum (BMI) of 27 kg/m2 was aligned with Food and Drug Administration (FDA) indications for GLP-1s. GLP-1s included those FDA-approved for weight loss, FDA-approved for diabetes, and GLP-1s under investigation for weight loss (semaglutide, liraglutide, dulaglutide, exenatide, lixisenatide, efpeglenatide, and tirzepatide). GLP-1 weekly forms were held 7 days before surgery while daily forms were held the day of surgery. Patients who lacked a weight measured at surgery, lacked a weight measured 1 to 24 months before surgery, were under 18 years of age, or had a second TKA or THA during the study period were excluded.

Among 18,649 primary TJAs, 6482 patients were identified with the above criteria. The mean age at the time of TJA was 67 years (range, 18 to 94 years) and 50% were female (Table 1). The mean BMI at surgery was 34 kg/m2 (range, 22 to 74 kg/m2). Among the 6482 surgeries, 46% were primary THAs and 54% were primary TKAs. Prior to 2 years of follow-up, 137 were revised, and 121 patients died. An additional 2728 had ˂2 years of follow-up. The mean follow-up for the remaining 3496 was 3 years (range, 2 to 5 years). Statistical methods

Weight change was defined as the difference between the weight measured 1 to 24 months before surgery and at surgery [13,14]. The GLP-1 cohort (n = 239) was compared to those not on any GLP-1 agonist preoperatively (n = 6243). Univariable models evaluated GLP-1 use, age, sex, BMI at surgery, and Charlson Comorbidity Index (CCI) for the outcomes of complications, revisions, and reoperations. Multivariable models evaluated GLP-1 use, age, sex, BMI at surgery, and CCI for the same outcomes.

Revisions were defined as exchange of any component of the prosthesis [15,16]. Reoperations were defined as any subsequent surgery related to the arthroplasty [15,16]. Complications included deep venous thromboembolism, pulmonary embolism, delayed wound healing or drainage, wound dehiscence, superficial wound infection, and periprosthetic joint infections treated nonoperatively [15,16]. Survivorship analyses using Cox proportional hazard models used all available follow-up for each outcome. Finally, a univariable analysis of 90-day postoperative emergency department (ED) visits was conducted to compare the risk of an ED visit for any reason among patients using GLP-1 agonists compared to those not on GLP-1 agonists. Statistical analyses were performed using SAS Studio (version 3.81; SAS Institute Inc., Cary, NC, USA) and RStudio (version 4.2.2; R Core Team, R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria).

Results

Weight change prior to surgery

Among all patients, including those not on GLP-1s preoperatively, 16% gained ≥5 pounds, 45% maintained weight, 19% lost 5 to <10 pounds, 13% lost 10 to <20 pounds, and 7% lost ≥20 pounds prior to surgery. Among all patients, the mean BMI change was −0.6 kg/m2 (range, −23 to 13 kg/m2), the mean weight change was −4 pounds (range, −149 to 69 pounds), and the median weight change was −3 pounds.

The overall prevalence of GLP-1 use before TJA was 4%, increasing from 2% in 2019 to 5% in 2022. Among patients on GLP-1s (n = 239), 13% gained ≥5 pounds, 30% maintained weight, 18% lost 5 to <10 pounds, 21% lost 10 to <20 pounds, and 19% lost ≥20 pounds prior to surgery. The mean BMI change was −1.4 kg/m2 (range, −16.3 to 6.2 kg/m2) and mean weight change was −9 pounds (range, −110 to 36 pounds). Nearly a quarter of patients preoperatively with severe obesity (BMI >40 kg/m2) on GLP-1s improved their BMI category by surgery (Fig. 1). Half of the patients preoperatively with severe obesity on GLP-1s achieved a weight loss of at least 5% of their body weight by surgery (Fig. 2).Figure 1Changes in BMI category from the preoperative period to surgery.Figure 2Clinically significant preoperative weight change by preoperative BMI.

Revisions

The 5-year survivorship free of any revision for the overall cohort was 94% (95% confidence interval [CI], 91.7 to 95.7%). There were no differences in survivorship free of any revision by GLP-1 use compared to patients not using GLP-1s (Fig. 3). In univariable analyses, GLP-1 use was not associated with an increased or decreased risk of revision (P = .35). BMI at surgery was associated with an increased risk of revision (per 1 kg/m2 increase at surgery; hazard ratio [HR], 1.03; 95% CI, 1.0 to 1.1; P = .02). In multivariable analyses, there were no associations between the risk of revision and GLP-1 use (P = .66). Older age at surgery was associated with a decreased risk of reoperation (HR, 0.9; 95% CI, 0.8 to 1.0; P = .04). In multivariable analyses, there was no association between the risk of reoperation and GLP-1 use (P = .34). Complications

The 5-year survivorship free of any complication for the overall cohort was 94% (95% CI, 91.9 to 94.8%). There were no differences in survivorship free of any complication by GLP-1 use (Fig. 5). Univariable analyses found that GLP-1 use was not associated with the risk of complication (P = .23). BMI at surgery was associated with an increased risk of complication (per 1 kg/m2 increase at surgery; HR, 1.1; 95% CI, 1.0 to 1.1; P < .01). In multivariable analyses, there were no associations between the risk of complication and GLP-1 use (P = .85). Ninety-day ED visits

There was no difference in the risk of a 90-day ED visit by GLP-1 use (odds ratio, 1.5; 95% CI, 1.0 to 2.3; P = .07). There was no difference in the mean number of days from surgery to the first ED visit (P = .31) or the mean number of ED visits within 90 days postoperatively (P = .07). Among patients who had a 90-day ED visit using GLP-1 medications, the mean number of days from surgery to the first ED visit was 32 days (range, 1 to 88 days). Discussion

In this large series of patients, we analyzed the impact of preoperative GLP-1 agonists before TJA. Although the prevalence of GLP-1 use remained low, the preoperative utilization increased from 2% in 2019 to 5% in 2022. Preoperative weight loss on these medications varied considerably but mean weight loss was modest (−9 pounds). While the use of preoperative GLP-1s was not associated with decreased postoperative risks, GLP-1s were not associated with increased odds of 90-day ED visits.

The prevalence of GLP-1 use among patients undergoing TJA in 2022 of 5% was comparable to prior literature demonstrating relatively low utilization. A recent study found that only 3% of eligible patients received GLP-1s between 2021 and 2024 [17]. Previous studies outside of the preoperative period have found GLP-1s were associated with over 20% body weight loss among a third of subjects [18]. Despite some studies showing encouraging results for GLP-1s [10,12,19], we found more modest mean weight loss for patients overall with preoperative GLP-1s. The mean weight change on GLP-1s preoperatively was −9 pounds. This small amount of weight loss on GLP-1s was consistent with a recent real-world study that found substantially lower weight loss than the main phase 3 trials of GLP-1s [20]. That study found a mean body weight reduction on GLP-1s as low as 3.6% due to early discontinuation of GLP-1s and lower maintenance dosages. To our knowledge, this is the first study of preoperative weight loss among patients on GLP-1s undergoing THA or TKA. This study may suggest tempering surgeon expectations for preoperative weight loss for most arthroplasty patients with obesity on GLP-1s compared to prior clinical trials of GLP-1s in the general population.

A study by Keeney et al. [21] found that only weight loss ≥20 pounds among patients with BMI ≥40 kg/m2 undergoing TKA was associated with shorter length of stay and lower odds of discharge to a rehabilitation facility. It is possible that the mean weight loss expected from most patients on GLP-1 agonists preoperatively may not be enough to make clinically significant changes in postoperative outcomes. While the mean weight loss on GLP-1s in our study was likely not clinically significant for TJA, we did find that nearly 1 in 4 patients who preoperatively had severe obesity and took GLP-1s improved their BMI class by at least 1 category by surgery. Similarly, half of the patients who preoperatively had severe obesity and took GLP-1s achieved a weight loss of at least 5% of their body weight by surgery, which is often considered a clinically significant amount of weight loss [22]. More patients lost ≥20 pounds on GLP-1 agonists (19% vs 7% overall) such that future studies should examine predictors of more substantial preoperative weight loss on GLP-1s.

We found that GLP-1 use was not significantly associated with revisions, reoperations, or complications. This differs from two prior studies that found preoperative GLP-1 use was associated with a lower risk of periprosthetic joint infection (PJI), complications, and readmission [23,24]. Those studies both used administrative databases without clinical details such as the amount of weight loss on GLP-1s and could not adjust for factors including BMI at surgery hich may differ by GLP-1 use and affect or confound outcomes. More studies are needed to evaluate the impact of preoperative GLP-1 agonists on weight loss, hemoglobin A1c, and postoperative outcomes.

A prior study found that GLP-1 use before TKA was associated with increased risks of myocardial infarction, acute kidney injury, pneumonia, and hypoglycemic events [25]. Other studies reported aspiration events and concerns about healing complications on GLP-1s [7,26,27]. In contrast, we found that preoperative GLP-1 use was not associated with increased odds of 90-day ED visits which would likely capture many potential adverse events of preoperative GLP-1 use. More research is needed on the potential adverse events associated with GLP-1s.

The study was limited by the novelty of most GLP-1s. Relatively few patients were taking these medications preoperatively even though the prevalence of GLP-1 use increased over the included study period. Additionally, many patients were excluded for missing weight measurements. This may have increased the risk of a type II error. This study considered a preoperative prescription of GLP-1s to be analogous with GLP-1 use, though especially considering potential adverse medication effects, actual GLP-1 use may differ from prescribed use and bias our results to the null hypothesis. The study was completed at 1 high-volume center so results may differ at lower volume centers with higher event rates. There is a potential for selection bias because the study retrospectively inclwuded patients who were felt safe for surgery and ultimately underwent TJA. Therefore, study conclusions may not apply to all patients with end-stage osteoarthritis considering TJA, such as those who may not be safe for surgery with or without weight loss with GLP-1s. Patients with weights recorded between 1 and 24 months before surgery were included. Although this preoperative period is relevant to our clinical experience optimizing patients before surgery, is consistent with several prior weight loss before TJA studies [13,14], and allowed patients adequate time for weight loss, it may introduce heterogeneity due to variation in GLP-1 treatment duration which may affect results. The inclusion of multiple GLP-1 medications and any indication for GLP-1 use also increased study heterogeneity.

Conclusions

The prevalence of preoperative GLP-1 use before TJA increased from 2% in 2019 to 5% in 2022, and preoperative weight change on GLP-1s varied considerably. GLP-1s were not associated with improved postoperative risks but investigational GLP-1s before TJA require further study.

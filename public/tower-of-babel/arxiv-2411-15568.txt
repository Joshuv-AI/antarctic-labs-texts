1.
Introduction

Let
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
denote a field with
q^{m}
elements where
q
is some positive integral power of prime
p
and
m\in\mathbb{N}
. A
primitive
element of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
is a generator of the multiplicative group
\mathbb{F}^{*}_{q^{m}}
. There are
\phi(q^{m}-1)
primitive elements in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
, where
\phi
is the Euler’s totient function. These primitive elements are of paramount importance due to their extensive use in areas like cryptographic schemes, for example in the Diffie-Hellman key exchange protocol, pseudo-random generators etc.

For an element
\epsilon\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
, the elements
\epsilon,\epsilon^{q},\epsilon^{q^{2}},\ldots,\epsilon^{q^{m-1}}
are called conjugates of
\epsilon
with respect to
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Sum of all these conjugates is called the trace of
\epsilon
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and is denoted by
\Tr_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(\epsilon)
. Many researchers worked in the direction of establishing the existence of primitive elements with some prescribed conditions such as traces, which can be studied from articles like
[
3
]
,
[
9
]

[
13
]
, among others. While majority of them focused on finding primitive pairs of the form
(\epsilon,f(\epsilon))
for some function
f(x)
, Cao and Wang
[
3
]
attempted to find primitive functional values
f(\epsilon)
without considering
\epsilon
to be primitive. But there was some error which was later addressed by Booker et al. in
[
1
]
. Here authors have finally investigated the existence of a non-zero element
\epsilon\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
such that
\epsilon+\epsilon^{-1}
is primitive and
\Tr_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(\epsilon)=a
,
\Tr_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(\epsilon^{-1})=b
for some arbitrary member
a,b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Moreover existence of primitive pairs where both components are rational values i.e., of the form
(f(\epsilon),g(\epsilon))
for some rational functions

has not yet been thoroughly investigated. So in this article we establish a sufficient condition for the existence of a non-zero element
\epsilon\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
, which is not necessarily primitive such that
(f(\epsilon),g(\epsilon))
is a primitive pair in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
with two prescribed traces,
\Tr_{{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}/{\mathbb{F}_{q}}}(\epsilon)=a
and
\Tr_{{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}/{\mathbb{F}_{q}}}(\epsilon^{-1})=b
, where
f(x),g(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}(x)
are rational functions with some restrictions and
a,b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

Throughout this article we are using rational functions that are not of the form
f(x)=cx^{j}g^{d}(x)
, where
j
is any integer,
d>1
divides
q^{m}-1
and
c\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{q^{m}}

2.
Preliminaries

Given a finite abelian group
G
, a character
\chi
of
G
is defined as a homomorphism from
G
to the multiplicative group
S^{1}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|=1\}
. The characters of
G
form a group under multiplication called
\mathit{dual\thinspace group}
or
\mathit{character\thinspace group}
of
G
which is denoted by
\widehat{G}
and indeed
\widehat{G}\cong G
. Let
\chi_{1}
denote the trivial character of
G
defined as
\chi_{1}(a)=1
, for all
a\in G
. A character of the group
G
of order
d
is usually denoted by
\chi_{d}
. We also denote all the characters of order
d
by
(d)
.

Corresponding to the two different abelian group structures present in a finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
i.e.,
(\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}},+)
and
(\mathbb{F}^{*}_{q^{m}},\cdot)
, two types of characters can be formed. We refer to them as additive character and multiplicative character denoted normally by
\psi
and
\chi
respectively. Multiplicative characters can be extended from
\mathbb{F}^{*}_{q^{m}}
to
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
by the rule

\chi(0)=\begin{cases}0\,\mbox{ if}\,\chi\neq\chi_{1},\\
1\,\mbox{ if}\,\chi=\chi_{1}.\end{cases}

Definition 2.1
.

(
e
-free element)

For any divisor
e
of
q^{m}-1
, an element
\epsilon\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
is called
e
-free if
\epsilon\neq\beta^{d}
for any
\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
and for any divisor
d
of
e
other than
1
.

An element
\epsilon\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{q^{m}}
is primitive if and only if it is
(q^{m}-1)
-free. The characteristic function for the
e
-free elements of
\mathbb{F}^{*}_{q^{m}}
is defined as follows:

(2.1)

\rho_{e}:\mathbb{F}^{*}_{q^{m}}\to\{0,1\};\epsilon\mapsto\theta(e)\sum_{d|e}\frac{\mu(d)}{\phi(d)}\sum_{(d)}\chi_{d}(\epsilon);

where
\theta(e)=\frac{\phi(e)}{e}
and
\phi,\mu
denote the Euler’s totient function and the Mobius function respectively.

We shall also need characteristic function for prescribed values of trace. For each
a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
the characteristic function for elements
\epsilon\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
such that
\Tr_{{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}/{\mathbb{F}_{q}}}(\epsilon)=a
is defined as follows:

(2.2)

\tau_{a}:\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}\to\{0,1\};\epsilon\mapsto\frac{1}{q}\sum_{\psi\in\widehat{\mathbb{F}_{q}}}\psi(\Tr_{{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}/{\mathbb{F}_{q}}}(\epsilon)-a).

The additive character
\tilde{\psi}(\epsilon)=e^{2{\pi}i\Tr(\epsilon)/p}
, for all
\epsilon\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
, where
\Tr
is the absolute trace function from
\mathbb{F}_{q}
to
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, is called the canonical additive character of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and every additive character of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is of the form
\psi_{\alpha}
for some
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
, where
\psi_{\alpha}(\beta)=\tilde{\psi}(\alpha\beta)
for all
\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
. If we denote
\widehat{\psi}(\epsilon)=\tilde{\psi}(Tr_{{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}/{\mathbb{F}_{q}}}(\epsilon))
, this new canoninal character will be the lift of
\tilde{\psi}
. In particular,
\widehat{\psi}
is the canonical character of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
. Therefore we have

\displaystyle\tau_{a}(\epsilon)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{q}\sum_{u\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\tilde{\psi}(\Tr_{{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}/{\mathbb{F}_{q}}}(u\epsilon)-ua)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{q}\sum_{u\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\widehat{\psi}(u\epsilon)\tilde{\psi}(-ua).

The following lemmas will also be necessary for subsequent discussions.

Lemma 2.1
.

[

]

Let
f(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}(x)
be a rational function. Write
f(x)=\overset{k}{\underset{j=1}{\prod}}{f_{j}(x)}^{r_{j}}
, where
f_{j}(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}[x]
are irreducible polynomials and

are nonzero integers. Let
\chi
be a nontrivial multiplicative character of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
of square free order

which is a divisor of

. Suppose that

is not of the form

for any rational function
g(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}(x)
and

. Then we have,

\bigg|{\underset{\epsilon\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}},f(\epsilon)\neq\infty}{\sum}\chi(f(\epsilon))}\bigg|\leq\bigg(\overset{k}{\underset{j=1}{\sum}}\deg(f_{j})-1\bigg)q^{\frac{m}{2}}.

Lemma 2.2
.

[
5
]

Let
\chi
and
\psi
be two non-trivial multiplicative and additive characters of the field
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
respectively. Let

be rational functions in

, where

and

, for any

and any

, and

is the order of
\chi
.
Then

\left|\underset{\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}\setminus S}{\sum}\chi(f(\alpha))\psi(g(\alpha))\right|\leq[\deg(g_{\infty})\,+\,l_{0}\,+\,l_{1}\,-\,l_{2}\,-\,2]q^{m/2},

where
S
denotes the set of all poles of
f
and
g
,
g_{\infty}
denotes the pole divisor of
g
,
l_{0}
denotes the number of distinct zeroes and poles of
f
in the algebraic closure
\overline{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}
of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
,
l_{1}
denotes the number of distinct poles of
g
(including infinite pole) and
l_{2}
denotes the number of finite poles of
f
, that are also zeroes or poles of
g
.

Lemma 2.3
.

[
12
]

If
\psi
is a non-trivial additive character of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
and
u,v\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
, not both zero, then the Kloosterman sum
K(\psi,u,v)=\underset{\epsilon\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{q^{m}}}{\sum}\psi(u\epsilon+v\epsilon^{-1})
satisfies

.

Using these, we can now proceed to develop a sufficient condition.

3.
A sufficient condition for elements in
\mathcal{A}_{p}^{m}

Let
e_{1},e_{2}
be two divisors of
q^{m}-1
. We denote the number of nonzero element
\epsilon\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
, which is not necessarily primitive, such that
f(\epsilon)
is
e_{1}
-free and
g(\epsilon)

e_{2}
-free with

\Tr_{{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}/{\mathbb{F}_{q}}}(\epsilon)=a
and
\Tr_{{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}/{\mathbb{F}_{q}}}(\epsilon^{-1})=b

a,b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
and

,
m_{2}
and

remain same as defined in the introduction. Now using the characteristic functions (
2.1
), and (

\displaystyle N_{f,g,a,b}(e_{1},e_{2})=

\displaystyle\underset{\epsilon\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}\textbackslash S}{\sum}\rho_{e_{1}}(f(\epsilon))\rho_{e_{2}}(g(\epsilon))\tau_{a}(\epsilon)\tau_{b}(\epsilon^{-1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\theta(e_{1})\theta(e_{2})}{q^{2}}\underset{d_{1}|e_{1},d_{2}|e_{2}}{\sum}\frac{\mu(d_{1})\mu(d_{2})}{\phi(d_{1})\phi(d_{2})}\underset{\epsilon\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}\textbackslash S}{\sum}

\displaystyle\underset{\chi_{d_{1}},\chi_{d_{2}}}{\sum}\chi_{d_{1}}(f(\epsilon))\chi_{d_{2}}(g(\epsilon))\underset{\psi,\psi^{\prime}\in\widehat{\mathbb{F}_{q}}}{\sum}\psi(\Tr(\epsilon)-a)\psi^{\prime}(\Tr(\epsilon^{-1})-b).

Let us choose
u,v\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
such that
\psi(\beta)=\tilde{\psi}(u\beta)
and
\psi^{\prime}(\beta)=\tilde{\psi}(v\beta)
for all
\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
. If
u,v
run through all the elements in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, then
\psi,\psi^{\prime}
run through all the characters in
\widehat{\mathbb{F}_{q}}
. This transforms the above expression into the following form.

\displaystyle N_{f,g,a,b}(e_{1},e_{2})=

\displaystyle\frac{\theta(e_{1})\theta(e_{2})}{q^{2}}\underset{d_{1}|e_{1},d_{2}|e_{2}}{\sum}\frac{\mu(d_{1})\mu(d_{2})}{\phi(d_{1})\phi(d_{2})}\underset{u,v\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}{\sum}\tilde{\psi}(-ua-vb)\underset{\chi_{d_{1}},\chi_{d_{2}}}{\sum}\chi_{f,g}(d_{1},d_{2},u,v);

where,

\displaystyle\chi_{f,g}(d_{1},d_{2},u,v)=

\displaystyle\underset{\epsilon\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}\textbackslash S}{\sum}\chi_{d_{1}}(f(\epsilon))\chi_{d_{2}}(g(\epsilon))\widehat{\psi}(u\epsilon+v\epsilon^{-1}).

Our aim is to estimate a lower bound for the term
\chi_{f,g}(d_{1},d_{2},u,v)

N_{f,g,a,b}(e_{1},e_{2})
. We divide the process into four sub-cases and find a lower bound of
N_{f,g,a,b}(e_{1},e_{2})
in each of these cases, that leads to the formation of a general lower bound for all. We abbreviate
N_{f,g,a,b}(e_{1},e_{2})
as
N_{\text{case number}}
for our convenience.

Case I:

(d_{1},d_{2},u,v)=(1,1,0,0)

In this case we have

\big|\chi_{f,g}(1,1,0,0)\big|\geq q^{m}-(m_{1}+m_{2}+1).

This thereby provides,

\displaystyle N_{I}\geq\frac{\theta(e_{1})\theta(e_{2})}{q^{2}}\{q^{m}-(m_{1}+m_{2}+1)\}.

Case II:

d_{1}=1
(provided
(d_{2},u,v)\neq(1,0,0)
)

Here,

N_{f,g,a,b}(e_{1},e_{2})=\frac{\theta(e_{1})\theta(e_{2})}{q^{2}}\underset{d_{2}|e_{2}}{\sum}\frac{\mu(d_{2})}{\phi(d_{2})}\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}u,v\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\\
(d_{2},u,v)\neq(1,0,0)\end{subarray}}{\sum}\tilde{\psi}(-ua-vb)\underset{\chi_{d_{2}}}{\sum}\chi_{f,g}(1,d_{2},u,v).

When
d_{2}=1
, we use Lemma
2.3
, to arrive at

\big|\chi_{f,g}(1,1,u,v)\big|\leq 2q^{\frac{m}{2}}.

If we consider
d_{2}\neq 1
, the following situations occur, which are tackled using the Lemma
2.1
and Lemma
2.2
.

\displaystyle\big|\chi_{f,g}(1,d_{2},0,0)\big|

\displaystyle\leq(m_{2}-1)q^{\frac{m}{2}},

\displaystyle\big|\chi_{f,g}(1,d_{2},u,0)\big|

\displaystyle\leq(m_{2})q^{\frac{m}{2}},

\displaystyle\big|\chi_{f,g}(1,d_{2},0,v)\big|

\displaystyle\leq(m_{2})q^{\frac{m}{2}},

\displaystyle\big|\chi_{f,g}(1,d_{2},u,v)\big|

The number of cases when

is
(q^{2}-1)
and when

is
(W(e_{2})-1)q^{2}

\displaystyle N_{II}

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{\theta(e_{1})\theta(e_{2})}{q^{2}}q^{\frac{m}{2}}[2(q^{2}-1)+(W(e_{2})-1)\{(q-1)^{2}(m_{2}+2)

\displaystyle+(q-1)(m_{2})+(q-1)m_{2}+(m_{2}-1)\}]

\displaystyle=-\frac{\theta(e_{1})\theta(e_{2})}{q^{2}}q^{\frac{m}{2}}[W(e_{2})\{q^{2}m_{2}+2q^{2}-4q+1\}-q^{2}m_{2}+4q-3].

Case III:

d_{2}=1
(provided
(d_{1},u,v)\neq(1,0,0)
)

Following the same procedure as in Case II, we can show

\displaystyle N_{III}

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{\theta(e_{1})\theta(e_{2})}{q^{2}}q^{\frac{m}{2}}[W(e_{1})\{q^{2}m_{1}+2q^{2}-4q+1\}-q^{2}m_{1}+4q-3].

Case IV:

d_{1}\neq 1,d_{2}\neq 1

In this case
\chi_{f,g}(d_{1},d_{2},u,v)
can be transformed into

\displaystyle\chi_{f,g}(d_{1},d_{2},u,v)

\displaystyle=\underset{\epsilon\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}\textbackslash S}{\sum}\chi_{q^{m}-1}(f^{k_{1}}(\epsilon)g^{k_{2}}(\epsilon))\widehat{\psi}(u\epsilon+v\epsilon^{-1}).

Here, we have considered
\chi_{d_{1}}=\chi^{k_{1}}_{q^{m}-1}
and
\chi_{d_{2}}=\chi^{k_{2}}_{q^{m}-1}
for some multipicative character
\chi_{q^{m}-1}
of order

, where

and

Then again using Lemma
2.1

\displaystyle\big|\chi_{f,g}(d_{1},d_{2},0,0)\big|

\displaystyle\leq(m_{1}+m_{2}-1)q^{\frac{m}{2}},

\displaystyle\big|\chi_{f,g}(d_{1},d_{2},u,0)\big|

\displaystyle\leq(m_{1}+m_{2})q^{\frac{m}{2}},

\displaystyle\big|\chi_{f,g}(d_{1},d_{2},0,v)\big|

\displaystyle\leq(m_{1}+m_{2})q^{\frac{m}{2}},

\displaystyle\big|\chi_{f,g}(d_{1},d_{2},u,v)\big|

\displaystyle N_{IV}

\displaystyle+(m_{1}+m_{2}-1)\}+(m_{1}+m_{2}-1)]

\displaystyle=-\frac{\theta(e_{1})\theta(e_{2})}{q^{2}}q^{\frac{m}{2}}(W(e_{1})-1)(W(e_{2})-1)[q^{2}m_{1}+q^{2}m_{2}+2q^{2}-4q+1].

\displaystyle N_{f,g,a,b}(e_{1},e_{2})

\displaystyle=N_{I}+N_{II}+N_{III}+N_{IV}

\displaystyle N_{f,g,a,b}(e_{1},e_{2})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\theta(e_{1})\theta(e_{2})}{q^{2}}[\{q^{m}-(m_{1}+m_{2}+1)\}-q^{m/2}\{W(e_{1})(q^{2}m_{1}+2q^{2}-4q+1)

, Lemma 3.2], the next lemma follows.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
d|q^{m}-1
and
p
be a prime dividing
q^{m}-1
but not
d
. Then,

\displaystyle\big|N_{f,g,a,b}(pd,d)-\theta(p)N_{f,g,a,b}(d,d)\big|

\displaystyle\leq\theta(p)\theta(d)^{2}(m_{1}+m_{2}+2)q^{\frac{m}{2}}W(d)^{2},

\displaystyle\big|N_{f,g,a,b}(d,pd)-\theta(p_{i})N_{f,g,a,b}(d,d)\big|

\displaystyle\leq\theta(p_{i})\theta(d)^{2}(m_{1}+m_{2}+2)q^{\frac{m}{2}}W(d)^{2}.

Using Lemma
3.1
and Lemma
3.2
, we deduce the following prime sieving inequality.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
d|q^{m}-1
and
\{p_{1},p_{2},...,p_{r}\}
be the collection of all primes dividing
q^{m}-1
but not
d
. Let
l=1-2\underset{i=1}{\overset{r}{\sum}}\frac{1}{p_{i}}
and
L=\frac{2r-1}{l}+2
. Assuming
l>0
, if

(3.3)

\displaystyle q^{m/2-2}>(m_{1}+m_{2})W(d)+(m_{1}+m_{2}+2)W(d)^{2}l;

then
(q,m)\in\mathcal{A}_{p}^{m}
.

4.
Numerical computations

In the following Proposition
4.2
, we will show that there exists element
\epsilon\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
satisfying our desired properties in all but finitely many fields
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. We require the next lemma in order to do this.

Lemma 4.1

(
[
8
]
, Lemma 3.7)
.

For any positive integer
M
and positive real number
\nu
,
W(M)\leq DM^{1/\nu}
, where
D=\underset{i=1}{\overset{t}{\prod}}\frac{2}{p_{i}^{1/\nu}}
and
p_{1},p_{2},\dots,p_{t}
are the primes
\leq 2^{\nu}
that divide
M
.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
q
be a prime power and
m\geq 5
. Then, there exists an element
\epsilon\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
such that
f(\epsilon)
and
g(\epsilon)
both are
(q^{m}-1)
-free i.e., primitive elements of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
with

Tr_{{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}/{\mathbb{F}_{q}}}(\epsilon)=a
and
Tr_{{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}/{\mathbb{F}_{q}}}(\epsilon^{-1})=b
, where
a,b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
and
f(x),g(x)\in\mathcal{R}
, in all but finitely many fields
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
.

Proof:
From our sufficient condition (
3.2
) we have,

q^{\frac{m}{2}-2}>2(m_{1}+m_{2}+2)W(q^{m}-1)^{2}.

Using Lemma
4.1
in the above inequality, we deduce the following

(4.1)

\displaystyle q^{\frac{m}{2}-2}>2(m_{1}+m_{2}+2)D^{2}q^{2m/\nu}.

Now, taking logarithm on both sides, we get

(4.2)

\displaystyle\log q>\frac{\log 2(m_{1}+m_{2}+2)D^{2}}{\frac{m}{2}-2-\frac{2m}{\nu}}.

The right hand side of the above inequality is a decreasing function of
m
, if
\frac{m}{2}-\frac{2m}{\nu}>2
i.e.
\frac{\nu-4}{2\nu}>\frac{2}{m}
, which is valid for
m\geq 5
and holds for suitable values of
\nu
.

Therefore, there exists a number
q_{\circ}
such that Inequality
4.2
holds for
q\geq q_{\circ}
and
m\geq 5
.

Now, for
q<q_{\circ}
, we consider the following equivalent Inequality
4.1
,

(4.3)

\displaystyle m>\frac{\log 2(m_{1}+m_{2}+2)D^{2}+2\log q}{(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{2}{\nu})\log q}.

The denominator of the right hand side is positive if
\nu>4
. Hence corresponding to each
2\leq q<q_{\circ}
there exists a number
m_{q}
such that Inequality
4.3
holds for
m\geq m_{q}
.
Therefore, based on the discussion above, we can conclude that there exists element
\epsilon\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
satisfying our desired properties in all but finitely many fields
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

Now we proceed in a more particular direction considering
m_{1}=3,m_{2}=3
. In this case the sufficient condition takes the form

(4.4)

\displaystyle q^{\frac{m}{2}-2}\geq 2^{2(2+w(q^{m}-1))}.

For
n\geq 3

\displaystyle w(n)\leq\frac{1.385\log n}{\log\log n}.

If we set

\displaystyle(x+1)^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{2}{m}}\geq 2^{4+\frac{2.77\log x}{\log\log x}}.

Let for

, (
4.4

(4.5)

\displaystyle(x+1)^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{2}{m^{\prime}}}\geq 2^{4+\frac{2.77\log x}{\log\log x}}.

Now given an
m^{\prime}

for which (

for all choices of

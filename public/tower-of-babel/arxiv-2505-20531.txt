Factorial growth in perturbation theory, power corrections: precise extraction of quark masses and α s \alpha_{\text{s}}
*

Abstract
These proceedings summarize a newly found connection between the factorial growth of coefficients in perturbative QCD and power corrections to the perturbation series, discussed in refs. [ 1 , 2 , 3 , 4 ] . The improved convergence is shown for three quantities four which four terms in the series are available: the static energy, the quark pole mass, and the polarized Bjorken sum rule. Prospects for determinations of α s \alpha_{\text{s}} with controlled truncation uncertainties are discussed, as was found earlier in quark-mass determinations [ 3 , 5 ] .



Andreas S. Kronfeld

1
Introduction

The strong coupling
\alpha_{\text{s}}
is determined by measuring (or computing nonperturbatively) a physical quantity dominated by a hard scale and analyzing it in the context of perturbation theory: which value
\alpha_{\text{s}}
reproduces the physical quantity at hand?
A general obstacle to success is the truncation of the perturbative series
[
6
,
7
]
, because the perturbative series does not converge.
Perturbation theory develops an asymptotic series,
i.e.
, a series with vanishing radius of convergence.
These proceedings summarize a new yet simple and general way to address this problem
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
]
, with applications to the static energy, the pole mass, and the polarized Bjorken sum rule.

Consider a physical observable with a single hard scale
Q
, and form a dimensionless combination of the observable and powers of
Q
, denoted
\mathscr{R}(Q)
.
Factorization in perturbative QCD then says, for
Q\gg\Lambda
,

\mathscr{R}(Q)=r_{-1}+R(Q)+C_{p}\frac{\Lambda^{p}}{Q^{p}},

(1)

where
\Lambda
is the scale characteristic of QCD,
r_{-1}
is the value in the absence of QCD interactions, and
R(Q)
and

C_{p}\Lambda^{p}/Q^{p}
are called the “perturbation series” and the “power correction”, respectively.
The perturbation series is written as

R(Q)=\sum_{l=0}r_{l}(\mu/Q)\alpha_{\text{s}}(\mu)^{l+1}.

(2)

Here
\mu
is the renormalization scale and also (at least roughly speaking) a separation scale: energy scales above (below)
\mu

are supposed to be described by the perturbation series
R(Q)
(the power correction
C_{p}\Lambda^{p}/Q^{p}
).
The right-hand side of
eq.

1
can be justified in a variety of ways: an operator-product expansion, an effective field theory, study of loop integrands, phenomenological reasoning, etc.
In general, more than one power correction can appear in
eq.

1
, which can also addressed by the ideas summarized in
these proceedings, as discussed briefly below.

The separation into high and low energies (or short and long distances) could be implemented à la Wilson
[
8
]
, but particle physicists most often obtain the perturbative coefficients
r_{l}(\mu/Q)
via dimensional regularization with modified minimal subtraction of ultraviolet divergences (
i.e.
, the
\overline{\rm MS}
scheme).
Then
\mu
does not physically separate scales, rendering the separation between short and long distances (mathematically) ambiguous.
In particular, the coefficients of the asymptotic series, the
r_{l}
in
eq.

2
, grow factorially with
l
, in a way
dictated by the power
p
in
eq.

1
, and the ambiguity is of order
\mathrm{e}^{-p/2\beta_{0}\alpha_{\text{s}}}
.
1
1

1

The
\mu
dependence of
\alpha_{\text{s}}(\mu)
in
eq.

2
is dictated by the QCD beta function,

\mathrm{d}\alpha_{\text{s}}/\mathrm{d}\ln\mu=-2\beta_{0}\alpha_{\text{s}}^{2}-2\beta_{1}\alpha_{\text{s}}^{2}-\cdots
.

The results given below resolve this ambiguity, and similar ambiguities from higher powers, in a specific way.

2
Factorial growth: some background

Factorial growth in asymptotic series is ubiquitous, appearing in quantum mechanics
[
9
,
10
]
, simple
field theories
[
11
]
, and QED
[
12
]
.
A pedagogical example is the integral
[
13
]

Z(\lambda/m^{4})=\frac{m}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\phi\,\mathrm{e}^{-{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}m^{2}\phi^{2}-{\textstyle\frac{1}{4}}\lambda\phi^{4}},

(3)

which admits an asymptotic expansion for small
\alpha\equiv\lambda/m^{4}

Z(\alpha)\approx 1+\sum_{l=0}a_{l}\alpha^{l+1},\quad a_{l}=(-1)^{l+1}\frac{\Gamma(2l+{\textstyle\frac{5}{2}})}{\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma(l+2)},

(4)

obtained by expanding out the exponential
\mathrm{e}^{-{\textstyle\frac{1}{4}}\lambda\phi^{4}}
before integrating over
\phi
.
For large
\lambda
, on the other hand, expanding
\mathrm{e}^{-{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}m^{2}\phi^{2}}
before integrating leads to the convergent expansion

Z=\alpha^{-1/4}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}c_{j}\alpha^{-j/2},\quad c_{j}=\frac{(-1)^{j}}{2\sqrt{\pi}}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{j}{2}+{\textstyle\frac{1}{4}})}{\Gamma(j+1)}.

(5)

Because
eq.

5
is a convergent series, the sum reproduces the exact expression

Z(\alpha)=\frac{\mathrm{e}^{1/8\alpha}}{2\sqrt{\pi\alpha}}K_{1/4}(1/8\alpha)

(6)

with a Bessel function
K_{\nu}
.
A procedure known as Borel summation can be applied to
eq.

4
: because the
a_{l}
alternate in sign, the outcome is
mathematically unambiguous and yields
eq.

6
too.

In a similar vein, it has long been known (see,
e.g.
, ref.
[
14
]
) that the QCD coefficients satisfy

r_{l}\sim R_{0}^{(p)}\left(\frac{2\beta_{0}}{p}\right)^{l}\frac{\Gamma(l+1+pb)}{\Gamma(1+pb)},

(7)

where “
\sim
” means “goes asymptotically as”—in other words, the relation is supposed to hold only for large orders
l\gg 1
.
The arguments of the
\Gamma
(
i.e.
, factorial!) function contain the quantity
b=\beta_{1}/2\beta_{0}^{2}
, where
\beta_{0}
and
\beta_{1}
are the every-scheme coefficients of the beta function.
For
n_{f}=3
flavors,
b=32/81\approx 0.4
.
The
l
-independent factor
R_{0}^{(p)}
in
eq.

7
is the “normalization” or “strength”.
Expressions for the strength exist in the literature
[
15
,
16
,
17
,
18
]
, but the
derivations and/or explicit outcomes are hard (for me) to understand.

For the gluonic energy stored between a static quark and a static antiquark (the “static energy” for short), the first four
coefficients (
l\in\{0,1,2,3\}
) are 1, 1.38, 5.46, and 26.7.
It looks as if the factorial growth appears already for accessible
l
.

3
Factorial growth in QCD: renormalization constraints

Returning to
eqs.

1
and
2
, the physical quantity
\mathscr{R}(Q)
cannot depend on the artificial renormalization scale
\mu
of the
\overline{\rm MS}
scheme.
The
\mu
independence of
\mathscr{R}(Q)
imposes constraints on the
\mu/Q
dependence of the
r_{l}(\mu/Q)
, such that the
\mu
dependence of
\alpha_{\text{s}}(\mu)
is cancelled.
In this way,
r_{l}(\mu/Q)
contains pieces of the form
r_{l-j}\beta_{j}\,(\ln\mu/Q)^{j}
,
1\leq j\leq l
.
Remarkably and crucially, the only source of
Q
dependence (well, when all quarks can be taken massless or decoupled) of
R(Q)
springs from the
(\ln\mu/Q)^{j}
pieces in the
r_{l}(\mu/Q)
.

This renormalization constraints on the
Q
dependence imply a connection between the perturbative series and the power correction.
An observable without the power correction can be obtained from
\mathscr{R}
by defining
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
]

\mathscr{F}^{(p)}(Q)\equiv\frac{1}{pQ^{p-1}}\frac{\mathrm{d}Q^{p}\mathscr{R}}{\mathrm{d}Q}=r_{-1}+F^{(p)}(Q)=r_{-1}+\sum_{k=0}f_{k}^{(p)}(\mu/Q)\alpha_{\text{s}}(\mu)^{k+1},

(8)

where

f^{(p)}_{k}=r_{k}-\frac{2}{p}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}(j+1)\beta_{k-1-j}r_{j}.

(9)

Equation

9
has the structure of a matrix equation
\bm{f}^{(p)}=\mathbf{Q}^{(p)}\cdot\bm{r}
with a lower-diagonal matrix
\mathbf{Q}^{(p)}
.
Even though the matrix and the vectors
\bm{r}
and
\bm{f}^{(p)}
are infinite, the equation can be solved for
\bm{r}
row-by-row.
The solution is

r_{l}=f_{l}^{(p)}+\left(\frac{2\beta_{0}}{p}\right)^{l}\frac{\Gamma(l+1+pb)}{\Gamma(1+pb)}\sum_{k=0}^{l-1}(k+1)\frac{\Gamma(1+pb)}{\Gamma(k+2+pb)}\left(\frac{p}{2\beta_{0}}\right)^{k}f_{k}^{(p)},

(10)

This result shares with
eq.

7
the same growth
(2\beta_{0}/p)^{l}\Gamma(l+1+pb)/\Gamma(1+pb)
, now with an explicit
strength, which is the mildly
l
-dependent sum
\sum_{k=0}^{l-1}\cdots f_{k}^{(p)}
.
A slight difference is the extra term,
f_{l}^{(p)}
, and a major difference is that the relation is “
=
” instead of “
\sim
”.

Equation

10
holds at every order, hence already at low orders.

The coefficients
f_{l}^{(p)}
can grow, just not as fast as the
r_{l}
.
The manipulations in
eqs.

8
,
9
and
10
do not prove this property, but consistency with the methods
[
14
]
yielding
eq.

7
require it.
For example, if there is a second power correction with
p^{\prime}>p
, then the
f_{l}^{(p)}
satisfy a formula similar to
eq.

10
but with
p^{\prime}
replacing
p
.
This growth is slow enough for the sum defining the strength to converge, hence the assertion that the strength depends mildly on
l
.

In practice, the first
L
coefficients
r_{l}
are available in the literature, and just as many
f^{(p)}_{k}
are obtained from them via
eq.

9
.
So how can
eq.

10
yield anything new?
The answer to this question is that for
l<L
,
eq.

10
regenerates the original
r_{l}
, as it must.
For
l\geq L
, however,
eq.

10
provides a compelling approximation, namely

\displaystyle r_{l}\approx R_{l}^{(p)}

\displaystyle\equiv R_{0}^{(p)}\left(\frac{2\beta_{0}}{p}\right)^{l}\frac{\Gamma(l+1+pb)}{\Gamma(1+pb)},\qquad l\geq L,

(11)

\displaystyle R_{0}^{(p)}

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{k=0}^{L-1}(k+1)\frac{\Gamma(1+pb)}{\Gamma(k+2+pb)}\left(\frac{p}{2\beta_{0}}\right)^{k}f_{k}^{(p)},

(12)

with the sum in the strength
R_{0}^{(p)}
stopping at the last known order.
The expression for
R_{0}^{(p)}
in
eq.

12
is the same as derived by Komijani
[
2
]
from asymptotic (
i.e.
,
l\gg L
) considerations.
The new information of the derivation of this section
[
1
]
, beyond
eq.

7

[
14
]
with
strength from
eq.

12

[
2
]
, is that
eq.

11
is a systematic approximation valid at any order.

In general

4.2
Borel summation

For understanding how the two the two sums in
eq.

14
complement each other, it is useful to write

\displaystyle R_{\text{RS}}(Q,\mu)

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{l=0}^{L-1}\left[r_{l}(\mu/Q)-R^{(p)}_{l}(\mu/Q)\right]\alpha_{\text{s}}(\mu)^{l+1},

(16)

\displaystyle R_{\text{B}}(Q,\mu)

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}R^{(p)}_{l}(\mu/Q)\alpha_{\text{s}}(\mu)^{l+1},

(17)

so that

R(Q)=R_{\text{RS}}(Q,\mu)+R_{\text{B}}(Q,\mu).

(18)

The subtracted series
R_{\text{RS}}(Q,\mu)
and the Borel sum
R_{\text{B}}(Q,\mu)
depend on the renormalization scale
\mu
but the
sum does so only because of truncation; see
section

5
.

The sum on the right-hand side of
eq.

17
is formal, so
R_{\text{B}}(Q,\mu)
still requires a definition that can be evaluated.
A simple version of Borel summation is to express
\Gamma(l+1+pb)
via the integral representation
\Gamma(l+1+pb)=\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{l+pb}\mathrm{e}^{-t}\mathrm{d}{t}
, carry out the sum, and then integrate over
t

R_{\text{B}}^{(p)}=\frac{R_{0}^{(p)}\alpha_{\text{s}}}{\Gamma(1+pb)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{2\beta_{0}\alpha_{\text{s}}t}{p}\right)^{l}t^{pb}\mathrm{e}^{-t}\mathrm{d}{t}=\frac{R_{0}^{(p)}\alpha_{\text{s}}}{\Gamma(1+pb)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{pb}\mathrm{e}^{-t}}{1-2\beta_{0}\alpha_{\text{s}}t/p}\mathrm{d}{t};

(19)

see the Appendix of ref.
[
1
]
for details.
There is a simple pole along the integration contour, but it suffices to take the principal part and define

R_{\text{B}}^{(p)}=R_{0}^{(p)}\frac{p}{2\beta_{0}}\mathscr{J}(pb,p/2\beta_{0}\alpha_{\text{s}}),

(20)

where
\mathscr{J}
is an analytic function with a rapidly convergent power series in the second argument,

p/2\beta_{0}\alpha_{\text{s}}

[
3
]
.

Beyond the principal value, one could deform the contour just above or below the real axis, obtaining an additional contribution

-R_{0}^{(p)}\mathrm{e}^{\pm\mathrm{i}pb\pi}\frac{p^{1+pb}}{2^{1+pb}\beta_{0}}\Gamma(-pb)\left[\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-1/2\beta_{0}\alpha_{\text{s}}}}{(\beta_{0}\alpha_{\text{s}})^{b}}\right]^{p}.

(21)

The quantity in brackets is nothing but
\Lambda/Q
, so it can and should be absorbed into the power correction in the final
prescription for factorially summed perturbation theory:

\mathscr{R}(Q)=r_{-1}+R_{\text{RS}}(Q,\mu)+R_{\text{B}}(Q,\mu)+C_{p}\frac{\Lambda^{p}}{Q^{p}}.

(22)

In practice, an
\alpha_{\text{s}}
determination consists of fitting data for
\mathscr{R}
to the expressions on the right-hand side of
eq.

22
with fit parameters
\Lambda_{\overline{\rm MS}}
and
C_{p}
.

5.1
Static energy

The “static energy”, denoted here

5.3

Another quantity with

Outlook

Reference
[
1
]
(and these proceedings) consider the “minimal renormalon subtraction”
(MRS)
[
2
,
3
]
procedure, finding the generalization to any sequence of power corrections,
thereby summing the dominant and subdominant factorial growth of the perturbation series.
The theoretical keystone is simple and basic, following the consequences of physical quantities being independent of the
artificial scale
\mu
in
\overline{\rm MS}
renormalization.
As it it standard in perturbative QCD to sum logarithms, ref.
[
1
]
(and
sections

3
and
4
) provides
formulas to sum factorials, which grow even faster.
The mild variation in
figure

5
when the
\mu
is varied of over the customary range suggests truncation uncertainties can now
be controlled.
Note that based on current understanding “subtraction” is not involved, rather a summation of known contributions is explicitly
carried out.

From n n -systems to Lie and Courant algebroids
Liqiang Cai, Zhuo Chen, Zhixiong Chen, Yanhui Bi ∗

Abstract
. This paper introduces a method for constructing pure algebroids, dull algebroids, and Lie algebroids. The construction relies on what we defined as n n -systems on vector bundles, and we provide explicit computations for all resulting structure maps. Analogously, metric n n -systems defined on metric vector bundles allow us to construct metric algebroids, pre-Courant algebroids, and Courant algebroids.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
The motivation

Lie algebroids, initially introduced by Pradines
[
30
]
, furnish a rigorous framework for describing the infinitesimal structure of Lie groupoids. The reader who wishes to pursue the topic of Lie algebroids and groupoids is referred to Mackenzie’s book
[
28
]
(see also
[
8
,
27
]
) for foundational materials. In short, a Lie algebroid
(A,[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{A},a_{A})
consists of a vector bundle
A
(over a base manifold
M
), a Lie bracket
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{A}
on the section space
\Gamma(A)
, and an anchor map
a_{A}:A\to TM
which are compatible with the Lie bracket.
Furthermore, any Lie algebroid
(A,[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{A},a_{A})
admits an equivalent characterization as homological vector fields of degree 1 on the graded manifold
(M,\wedge^{\bullet}A^{*})
(see
[
33
]
). Thus, Lie algebroids have important research in algebraic geometry
[
4
,
7
,
6
]
and differential geometry
[
29
]
, while also having some applications in analytical mechanics
[
19
,
18
]
.

Courant algebroids, introduced in
[
25
]
, unify the concept of Lie bialgebras and the bracket on
TM\oplus T^{*}M
first discovered by Courant
[
13
]
, where
M
is an arbitrary smooth manifold. Around the same time and developed independently, the Dorfman bracket
[
16
]
, introduced in the context of Hamiltonian structures, is an alternation of the Courant bracket. Roytenberg provided an equivalent definition using the Dorfman bracket
[
32
]
, which gives the relation of Courant algebroids to
L_{\infty}
-algebras
[
31
]
. In this paper, we use the notation
(E,\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle,\circ,a_{E})
to denote a Courant algebroid. In specific, we have a pseudo-Euclidean vector bundle
(E,\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
(over a base manifold
M
), equipped with a vector bundle morphism
a_{E}:E\to TM
, also called the anchor, and a Leibniz algebra structure
\circ
, called the Dorfman bracket, defined on the section space
\Gamma(E)
, subject to specific compatibility conditions. The subject of Courant algebroids has inspired significant research in higher structure
[
BR
]
, Poisson geometry
[
2
,
3
,
11
,
20
,
26
]
and generalize geometry
[
12
,
17
]
, while also having some important applications in 3-dimensional topological field theory
[
21
,
22
,
23
]
, string theory
[
14
]
and double field theory
[
34
]
.

In a prior work
[
5
]
, we presented a construction of split Courant algebroids utilizing Dirac generating operators. While this provides a theoretical framework, many Lie algebroids and Courant algebroids considered in the literature remain purely abstract concepts. Therefore, it is pertinent to seek concrete examples of these algebraic objects where their structures can be explicitly formulated and analyzed. This paper addresses this need by introducing the concept of
n
-systems and demonstrating how these
n
-systems can be used to generate concrete examples of both Lie algebroids and Courant algebroids.

1.2.
Structures and main results of the paper

An anchored bundle, denoted
(A,a_{A})
, is the fundamental object of study in the initial section. It consists of a vector bundle
A
over a base manifold
M
, equipped with a bundle map
a_{A}:A\to TM
, termed the anchor. At this stage,
(A,a_{A})
lacks a Lie structure. We aim to investigate the conditions under which a Lie bracket, denoted by
[\cdot,\cdot]
, can be defined on
\Gamma(A)
, the space of sections of
A
, such that
(A,[\cdot,\cdot],a_{A})
constitutes a Lie algebroid. Our approach involves employing connections on the anchored bundle, drawing inspiration from the role of connections in differential geometry on smooth manifolds (see Definition
2.1
). We denote such a connection on
(A,a_{A})
by
\nabla
, and we seek a bracket on
\Gamma(A)
of the torsion-free form:

(1)

[x,y]^{\nabla}=\nabla_{x}y-\nabla_{y}x,\quad\forall x,y\in\Gamma(A).

Indeed, it can be demonstrated that any Lie algebroid structure compatible with
(A,a_{A})
must adopt this form (see equation
(7)
in Section
1.3
and Proposition
2.2
). In this scenario, the connection
\nabla
is constrained by a condition referred to as the Lie-Bianchi identity (see Proposition
2.7
).

The primary contribution of this paper is the introduction of a method for constructing anchored bundles, connections, and their associated Lie algebroid structures. This construction relies on a specific set of data, termed an “
n
-system” on
A
, denoted by
(\widehat{X}_{1},\ldots,\widehat{X}_{n};\widehat{\xi}^{1},\ldots,\widehat{\xi}^{n})
. Here,
\widehat{X}_{1}
,
\ldots
,
\widehat{X}_{n}
are covariant differential operators acting on
A
, while
\widehat{\xi}^{1}
,
\ldots
,
\widehat{\xi}^{n}
are elements of
\Gamma(A^{*})
, linearly independent over
C^{\infty}(M)
. From a given
n
-system, we define an anchor map
a_{A}:A\to TM
by

a_{A}(y)=\big\langle y\big|\widehat{\xi}^{i}\big\rangle\cdot\widehat{x}_{i},\forall y\in\Gamma(A),

where
\widehat{x}_{i}\in\Gamma(TM)
represents the symbol of
\widehat{X}_{i}
. Simultaneously, a connection
\nabla^{A}
is defined as:

\nabla^{A}_{y}z:=\big\langle y\big|\widehat{\xi}^{i}\big\rangle\cdot\widehat{X}_{i}(z),\hskip 20.00003pt\forall y,z\in\Gamma(A).

Consequently, the bracket operation on
\Gamma(A)
, defining the Lie algebroid structure on
A
, is given explicitly by:

\displaystyle[y,z]_{A}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla^{A}_{y}z-\nabla^{A}_{z}y=\big\langle y\big|\widehat{\xi}^{i}\big\rangle\cdot\widehat{X}_{i}(z)-\big\langle z\big|\widehat{\xi}^{i}\big\rangle\cdot\widehat{X}_{i}(y),\hskip 20.00003pt\forall y,z\in\Gamma(A).

We note that the Lie algebroid structure of
T^{*}M
constructed in
[
15
]
is a special case derived from a particular
1
-system.

The satisfaction of Lie algebroid axioms by the aforementioned structures on
A
requires specific conditions for the said
n
-system. Proposition
2.11
provides a sufficient condition, expressed by Equations (
13
) and (
14
). When these two equations hold, the
n
-system
(\widehat{X}_{1},\ldots,\widehat{X}_{n};\widehat{\xi}^{1},\ldots,\widehat{\xi}^{n})
is termed a “compatible
n
-system”.

In conclusion, any compatible
n
-system gives rise to an associated Lie algebroid with explicitly defined structure maps. Moreover, the language of compatible
n
-systems allows us to characterize certain DG manifolds and Lie pairs, as demonstrated in Sections
2.4
and
2.5
. In Section
2.3
, we further investigate a special type of compatible
n
-system, providing a characterization of its associated Lie algebroid in terms of explicit bases and functions, alongside concrete examples.

Analogously to Lie algebroids, Courant algebroids can also be constructed using a similar method. In the second part of this paper, we will elaborate on this construction, beginning with an arbitrary anchored metric bundle
(E,\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle,a_{E})
over a base manifold
M
. Here,
(E,a_{E})
denotes an anchored bundle equipped with a metric
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
on
E
. We aim to utilize connections on this anchored metric bundle, specifically metric connections, denoted by
\nabla
(see Section
3.1
). These connections are required to be compatible with the metric
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
. Notably, our metric connection is a specific instance of the generalized connections introduced in
[
9
]
.

A key distinction between the Lie bracket and the Courant bracket lies in the skew-symmetry property, which is satisfied by the former but not by the latter. Consequently, a Courant bracket on
\Gamma(E)
cannot be expressed in the form of equation (
1
). Instead, we will demonstrate that the Courant bracket necessarily takes the form:

e_{1}\overset{\nabla}{\circ}e_{2}=\nabla_{e_{1}}e_{2}-\nabla_{e_{2}}e_{1}+\Delta_{e_{2}}e_{1},

where the operator
\Delta:~\Gamma(E)\times\Gamma(E)\rightarrow\Gamma(E)
is determined by
\nabla
according to the relation:

\langle\Delta_{e_{2}}e_{1},e_{3}\rangle:=\langle\nabla_{e_{3}}e_{1},e_{2}\rangle,\quad\forall e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}\in\Gamma(E).

Proposition
3.2
provides a proof of this result. Following this, Section
3.2
examines three specific examples of anchored metric bundles, analyzing their metric, pre-Courant, and Courant algebroid structures.

To construct metric connections and their associated Courant algebroids, we introduce a method analogous to our previous construction of Lie algebroids from
n
-systems for vector bundles. Specifically, given a metric vector bundle
(E,\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
, we define a
metric
n
-system
as a tuple
(\widehat{Z}_{1},\ldots,\widehat{Z}_{n};\widehat{u}^{1},\ldots,\widehat{u}^{n})
, where
\widehat{Z}_{1},\ldots,\widehat{Z}_{n}
are metric-compatible covariant differential operators on
E
, and
\widehat{u}^{1},\ldots,\widehat{u}^{n}
are elements of
\Gamma(E)
that are linearly independent over
C^{\infty}(M)
. From such a metric
n
-system, we construct a metric connection
\nabla
and an operator
\Delta
on
\Gamma(E)
as follows:

\nabla_{e_{1}}e_{2}:=\big\langle e_{1}\big|\widehat{u}^{i}\big\rangle\cdot\widehat{Z}_{i}(e_{2}),\quad\Delta_{e_{2}}e_{1}:=\big\langle\widehat{Z}_{i}(e_{1})\big|e_{2}\big\rangle\cdot\widehat{u}^{i},

for all
e_{1},e_{2}\in\Gamma(E)
. Consequently, we formulate the associated Courant algebroid structure on
E
as follows:

•

The anchor map
a_{E}:E\to TM
is defined by
a_{E}(e)=\big\langle e\big|\widehat{u}^{i}\big\rangle\cdot\widehat{z}_{i}
, and the operator
\D:C^{\infty}(M)\to\Gamma(E)
is given by
\D(f):=\widehat{z}_{i}(f)\cdot\widehat{u}^{i}
, where
\widehat{z}_{i}\in\Gamma(TM)
denotes the symbol of
\widehat{Z}_{i}
;

•

The Dorfman bracket
\overset{\nabla}{\circ}:\Gamma(E)\times\Gamma(E)\to\Gamma(E)
is defined by

e_{1}\overset{\nabla}{\circ}e_{2}=\big\langle e_{1}\big|\widehat{u}^{i}\big\rangle\cdot\widehat{Z}_{i}(e_{2})-\big\langle e_{2}\big|\widehat{u}^{i}\big\rangle\cdot\widehat{Z}_{i}(e_{1})+\big\langle\widehat{Z}_{i}(e_{1})\big|e_{2}\big\rangle\cdot\widehat{u}^{i},

for all
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
and
e,e_{1},e_{2}\in\Gamma(E)
.

To ensure that the aforementioned structures satisfy the axioms of a Courant algebroid, we invoke Proposition
3.13
, which provides a sufficient condition. This condition is articulated through the satisfaction of three equations: (
41
), (
43
), and (
44
). When these three equations hold, the system
(\widehat{Z}_{1},\ldots,\widehat{Z}_{n};\widehat{u}^{1},\ldots,\widehat{u}^{n})
is defined as a compatible metric
n
-system. Consequently, given any such compatible metric
n
-system, we can conclude that there exists an associated Courant algebroid, the structure maps of which can be explicitly formulated.

In Sections
3.4
and
3.5
, we present applications of our findings. Specifically, Section
3.4
focuses on a particular class of compatible metric
n
-systems, characterizing the Courant algebroid structures they induce and providing illustrative examples. Furthermore, Section
3.5
demonstrates the utility of compatible metric

1.3.
List of terminologies and notations

(1)

Throughout this paper, we adopt the Einstein convention, i.e.,
a^{i}b_{i}
implies summation over the index
i
.

(2)

The letter
A
(or
E
) denotes a vector bundle over a smooth manifold
M
.

(3)

We define
\Gamma(\CDO(A))
as the space of covariant differential operators on
A
. Specifically, an element
\widehat{X}\in\Gamma(\CDO(A))
is an
\mathbb{R}
-linear operator
\Gamma(A)\rightarrow\Gamma(A)
, accompanied by some
\widehat{x}\in\Gamma(TM)
, such that for all
y\in\Gamma(A)
and
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
, the condition

\widehat{X}(fy)=f\widehat{X}(y)+\widehat{x}(f)\cdot y

holds.

(4)

The standard pairing
on
A\oplus A^{*}
is defined for all
x+\xi,y+\eta\in\Gamma(A\oplus A^{*})
as

(2)

\displaystyle\langle x+\xi|y+\eta\rangle:=\xi(y)+\eta(x).

(5)

A pure algebroid

(A,[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{A},a_{A})
is composed by a vector bundle
A\rightarrow M
, a bundle map
a_{A}:A\rightarrow TM
known as the anchor map, and a skew-symmetric bracket
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{A}
on
\Gamma(A)
satisfying the Leibniz identity

\displaystyle[x,fy]_{A}=f[x,y]_{A}+a_{A}(x)(f)\cdot y,\qquad\forall x,y\in\Gamma(A),f\in C^{\infty}(M).

(6)

A dull algebroid
, which we defined slightly differently from
[
24
]
(where the skew-symmetric property of
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{A}
is not required), is a pure algebroid
(A,[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{A},a_{A})
that satisfies an additional condition:

(3)

\displaystyle a_{A}([x,y]_{A})=[a_{A}(x),a_{A}(y)],\qquad\forall x,y\in\Gamma(A).

(7)

A Lie algebroid
is a pure algebroid
(A,[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{A},a_{A})
that adheres to the Jacobi identity

\displaystyle[[x,y]_{A},z]_{A}+[[y,z]_{A},x]_{A}+[[z,x]_{A},y]_{A}=0,\qquad\forall x,y,z\in\Gamma(A).

Notably, a Lie algebroid must be a dull algebroid, as the Jacobi identity implies Condition (
3
).

(8)

A metric algebroid

(E,\langle\,\cdot\,|\,\cdot\,\rangle,\circ,a_{E})
, as discussed in
[
35
]
, is a vector bundle
E\rightarrow M
equipped with three structures: a pseudo-metric
\langle\,\cdot\,|\,\cdot\,\rangle
on
\Gamma(E)
, which is a nondegenerate symmetric bilinear form on
\Gamma(E)
; a bilinear operation
\circ
on
\Gamma(E)
known as the Dorfman bracket; and a bundle map
a_{E}:E\rightarrow TM
called the anchor. These structures must satisfy the following axioms: for all
e,h_{1},h_{2}\in\Gamma(E)
,

(i)

a_{E}(e)\langle h_{1}|h_{2}\rangle=\langle e\circ h_{1}|h_{2}\rangle+\langle h_{1}|e\circ h_{2}\rangle
,

(ii)

e\circ e=\frac{1}{2}\D\langle e|e\rangle
,

where
\D:C^{\infty}(M)\rightarrow\Gamma(E)
is determined by
\langle\D(f)|e\rangle=a_{E}(e)(f)
for all
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
. An important equality for metric algebroids, useful in
[
32
]
, is

(4)

\displaystyle\big\langle\D(f)\circ e\big|h\big\rangle=\big(a_{E}\big(e\circ h\big)-[a_{E}(e),a_{E}(h)]\big)(f),

for all
e,h\in\Gamma(E),f\in C^{\infty}(M)
.

(9)

A pre-Courant algebroid
, as defined in
[
34
]
, is a metric algebroid
(E,\langle\,\cdot\,|\,\cdot\,\rangle,\circ,a_{E})
that satisfies an additional condition:

(5)

\displaystyle a_{E}(e_{1}\circ e_{2})=[a_{E}(e_{1}),a_{E}(e_{2})],\qquad\forall e_{1},e_{2}\in\Gamma(E).

According to Equation (
4
), this condition also implies
\D(f)\circ e=0
for all
e\in\Gamma(E)
and
f\in C^{\infty}(M).

(10)

A Courant algebroid
is a metric algebroid
(E,\langle\,\cdot\,|\,\cdot\,\rangle,\circ,a_{E})
that satisfies the Leibniz identity

\displaystyle e_{1}\circ(e_{2}\circ e_{3})=(e_{1}\circ e_{2})\circ e_{3}+e_{2}\circ(e_{1}\circ e_{3}),\qquad\forall e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}\in\Gamma(E).

2.
From connections to Lie algebroids

This section introduces compatible
n
-systems on vector bundles. We present our main findings, which characterize pure algebroids, dull algebroids, and Lie algebroids through connections and
n
-systems.

2.1.
Connections and pure algebroids

We begin by outlining some key concepts and facts that are well established in the literature.
Let
A
be a vector bundle over a base manifold
M
. We also consider a bundle map
a_{A}:A\rightarrow TM
, which we designate as the
anchor
. The combination
(A,a_{A})
constitutes what we call an
anchored (vector) bundle
.

Definition 2.1
.

A
connection
on the anchored bundle
(A,a_{A})
is an operator
\nabla:\Gamma(A)\times\Gamma(A)\rightarrow\Gamma(A)
that satisfies the following two properties:

\displaystyle\nabla_{(fx)}y=f\nabla_{x}y\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\nabla_{x}(fy)=a_{A}(x)(f)\cdot y+f\nabla_{x}y,

where
x,y\in\Gamma(A)
and
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
.

From this Definition, we can observe that any connection
\nabla
defined on the anchored bundle
(A,a_{A})
naturally induces an associated bracket operation on
\Gamma(A)
. This bracket, denoted as

\displaystyle[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]^{\nabla}:\Gamma(A)\times\Gamma(A)\rightarrow\Gamma(A)

is defined by the covariant derivatives in alternating directions

(6)

\displaystyle[x,y]^{\nabla}:=\nabla_{x}y-\nabla_{y}x,\qquad\forall x,y\in\Gamma(A).

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
(A,a_{A})
be an anchored vector bundle. Given a connection
\nabla
on
(A,a_{A})
, a pure algebroid structure
\big(A,[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]^{\nabla},a_{A}\big)
can be constructed, where the bracket
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]^{\nabla}
is defined by Equation (
6
). Conversely, every pure algebroid structure on
(A,a_{A})
arises from an associated connection through this way.

Proof.

We only prove the second statement.
Beginning with an arbitrary affine connection
\nabla^{TM}:\Gamma(TM)\times\Gamma(A)\rightarrow\Gamma(A)
, we can define a corresponding operator
\nabla^{A}:\Gamma(A)\times\Gamma(A)\rightarrow\Gamma(A)
through the following relation:

\displaystyle\nabla^{A}_{x}y:=\nabla^{TM}_{a_{A}(x)}y,\qquad\forall x,y\in\Gamma(A).

To construct the desired connection, we introduce a skew-symmetric and
C^{\infty}(M)
-bilinear operator
K:\Gamma(A)\otimes\Gamma(A)\rightarrow\Gamma(A)
, defined as

\displaystyle K(x,y):=[x,y]_{A}-\nabla^{A}_{x}y+\nabla^{A}_{y}x,\qquad\forall x,y\in\Gamma(A).

Subsequently, we construct an operator
\nabla:\Gamma(A)\times\Gamma(A)\rightarrow\Gamma(A)
by

\displaystyle\nabla_{x}y:=\nabla^{A}_{x}y+\frac{1}{2}K(x,y),\qquad\forall x,y\in\Gamma(A).

One can verify that
\nabla
determines a connection on
(A,a_{A})
and satisfies the relation:

[x,y]_{A}=\nabla_{x}y-\nabla_{y}x=:[x,y]^{\nabla}.

∎

Definition 2.3
.

Two connections
\nabla
and
\nabla^{\prime}
on
\big(A,a_{A}\big)
are called equivalent if there exists a symmetric bundle map
S:A\otimes A\rightarrow A
satisfying

\nabla^{\prime}_{x}y=\nabla_{x}y+S(x,y),\quad\forall x,y\in\Gamma(A).

As a direct consequence of Proposition
2.2
and Definition
2.3
, we can state the following one-to-one correspondence:

Proposition 2.4
.

There exists a canonical one-to-one correspondence between equivalence classes of connections on the anchored bundle
\big(A,a_{A}\big)
and pure algebroid structures underlying
\big(A,a_{A}\big)
.

Let us introduce the curvature form of
\nabla
, which is an operator

R^{\nabla}:\Gamma(A)\wedge\Gamma(A)\rightarrow\Gamma(\CDO(A))

defined as

(7)

\displaystyle R^{\nabla}(x\wedge y)(z):=\nabla_{x}\nabla_{y}z-\nabla_{y}\nabla_{x}z-\nabla_{([x,y]^{\nabla})}z,

for all
x,y,z\in\Gamma(A)
.

According to Equation (
7
), the operator
R^{\nabla}
is
C^{\infty}(M)
-linear in the arguments
x
and
y
. This property results in the following proposition:

Proposition 2.5
.

For a pure algebroid
\big(A,[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]^{\nabla},a_{A}\big)
arising from the connection
\nabla
, the following statements are equivalent:

•

The curvature
R^{\nabla}
is
C^{\infty}(M)
-linear with respect to the argument
z
,

•

\big(A,[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]^{\nabla},a_{A}\big)
is a dull algebroid.

The proof is easy and is therefore omitted. Through direct computation, we obtain another significant property:

Proposition 2.6
.

With the same condition as in Proposition
2.5
,
for a pure algebroid
\big(A,[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]^{\nabla},a_{A}\big)
, the following identity holds:

(8)

\displaystyle R^{\nabla}(x,y)z+R^{\nabla}(y,z)x+R^{\nabla}(z,x)y=[x,[y,z]^{\nabla}]^{\nabla}+[y,[z,x]^{\nabla}]^{\nabla}+[z,[x,y]^{\nabla}]^{\nabla},

where
x,y,z\in\Gamma(A)
.

This identity (
8
) is called the
pure-Bianchi identity
(of a pure algebroid).

Proposition 2.7
.

The following statements are equivalent:

(1)

\big(A,[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]^{\nabla},a_{A}\big)
is a Lie algebroid;

(2)

For all
x,y,z\in\Gamma(A)
, the following identity holds:

(9)

\displaystyle R^{\nabla}(x,y)z+R^{\nabla}(y,z)x+R^{\nabla}(z,x)y=0.

We call (
9
) the
Lie-Bianchi identity
(of a Lie algebroid).

Remark 2.8
.

There is another type of curvature in
[
1
]
. Let
M
be a smooth manifold and

be a vector bundle; a connection

is a degree

2.2.
Lie algebroids from compatible
n

Consider a vector bundle
A

M

\widehat{\xi}^{1}
,
\widehat{\xi}^{2}
,
\ldots
,
\widehat{\xi}^{n}

\widehat{X}_{1}
,
\widehat{X}_{2}
,

,
\widehat{X}_{n}

Definition 2.9
.

The datum

(\widehat{X}_{1},\ldots,\widehat{X}_{n};\widehat{\xi}^{1},\ldots,\widehat{\xi}^{n})

is called an

n
-system
on
A
.

In the subsequent discussion, we consistently represent the symbols of
\widehat{X}_{1},\ldots,\widehat{X}_{n-1}
, and
\widehat{X}_{n}
by the letters
\widehat{x}_{1},\ldots,\widehat{x}_{n-1}
, and
\widehat{x}_{n}
(where
\widehat{x}_{i}\in\Gamma(TM)
for each
i
). Given an
n
-system
(\widehat{X}_{1},\ldots,\widehat{X}_{n};\widehat{\xi}^{1},\ldots,\widehat{\xi}^{n})
, one can define the following structure maps on
\Gamma(A)
, which establish a pure algebroid
(A,[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{A},a_{A})
:

•

The anchor map
a_{A}:A\rightarrow TM
is defined as

(10)

\displaystyle a_{A}(y):=\big\langle y\big|\widehat{\xi}^{i}\big\rangle\cdot\widehat{x}_{i},\hskip 20.00003pt\forall y\in\Gamma(A).

•

The connection
\nabla^{A}:\Gamma(A)\times\Gamma(A)\rightarrow\Gamma(A)
is defined by

(11)

\displaystyle\nabla^{A}_{y}z:=\big\langle y\big|\widehat{\xi}^{i}\big\rangle\cdot\widehat{X}_{i}(z),\hskip 20.00003pt\forall y,z\in\Gamma(A).

For specific examples of
n

The next step is to investigate the conditions under which this pure algebroid can be characterized as either a dull algebroid or a Lie algebroid. The first fact we need is the following proposition.

Proposition 2.10
.

Consider the
n
-system
(\widehat{X}_{1},\ldots,\widehat{X}_{n};\widehat{\xi}^{1},\ldots,\widehat{\xi}^{n})
on

\displaystyle\widehat{X}_{i}(\widehat{\xi}^{j})

\displaystyle\hskip-5.0pt=\hskip-5.0pt

\displaystyle a_{ik}^{j}\widehat{\xi}^{k},

\displaystyle[\widehat{x}_{i},\widehat{x}_{j}]

\displaystyle\hskip-5.0pt=\hskip-5.0pt

\displaystyle(a_{ji}^{k}-a_{ij}^{k})\widehat{x}_{k},

where

. Under these conditions, the triple

Proof.

Based on the definitions and conditions presented above, one verifies the following relation:

\displaystyle a_{A}([x,y]_{A})-[a_{A}(x),a_{A}(y)]=0,\qquad\forall x,y\in\Gamma(A).

In fact, the left hand side of the above equation can be expressed as:

\displaystyle\big\langle x\big|\widehat{\xi}^{i}\big\rangle\cdot\big\langle\widehat{X}_{i}(y)\big|\widehat{\xi}^{j}\big\rangle\cdot\widehat{x}_{j}-\big\langle y\big|\widehat{\xi}^{i}\big\rangle\cdot\big\langle\widehat{X}_{i}(x)\big|\widehat{\xi}^{j}\big\rangle\cdot\widehat{x}_{j}

\displaystyle\quad-\big\langle x\big|\widehat{\xi}^{i}\big\rangle\cdot\big\langle y\big|\widehat{\xi}^{j}\big\rangle\cdot[\widehat{x}_{i},\widehat{x}_{j}]-\big\langle x\big|\widehat{\xi}^{i}\big\rangle\cdot\widehat{x}_{i}\big(\big\langle y\big|\widehat{\xi}^{j}\big\rangle\big)\cdot\widehat{x}_{j}+\big\langle y\big|\widehat{\xi}^{j}\big\rangle\cdot\widehat{x}_{j}\big(\big\langle x\big|\widehat{\xi}^{i}\big\rangle\big)\cdot\widehat{x}_{i}

\displaystyle\hskip-5.0pt=\hskip-5.0pt

\displaystyle-\big\langle x\big|\widehat{\xi}^{i}\big\rangle\cdot\big\langle y\big|\widehat{\xi}^{j}\big\rangle\cdot[\widehat{x}_{i},\widehat{x}_{j}]-\big\langle x\big|\widehat{\xi}^{i}\big\rangle\cdot\big\langle y\big|\widehat{X}_{i}(\widehat{\xi}^{j})\big\rangle\cdot\widehat{x}_{j}+\big\langle y\big|\widehat{\xi}^{i}\big\rangle\cdot\big\langle x\big|\widehat{X}_{i}(\widehat{\xi}^{j})\big\rangle\cdot\widehat{x}_{j}

\displaystyle\hskip-5.0pt=\hskip-5.0pt

\displaystyle-\big\langle x\big|\widehat{\xi}^{i}\big\rangle\cdot\big\langle y\big|\widehat{\xi}^{j}\big\rangle\cdot(a_{ji}^{k}-a_{ij}^{k})\widehat{x}_{k}-\big\langle x\big|\widehat{\xi}^{i}\big\rangle\cdot\big\langle y\big|a_{ik}^{j}\widehat{\xi}^{k}\big\rangle\cdot\widehat{x}_{j}+\big\langle y\big|\widehat{\xi}^{i}\big\rangle\cdot\big\langle x\big|a_{ik}^{j}\widehat{\xi}^{k}\big\rangle\cdot\widehat{x}_{j}

\displaystyle\hskip-5.0pt=\hskip-5.0pt

\displaystyle 0.

∎

Consider a vector bundle

(18)

\displaystyle a_{E}(e_{1})\langle e_{2}|e_{3}\rangle=\big\langle\nabla_{e_{1}}e_{2}\big|e_{3}\big\rangle+\big\langle e_{2}\big|\nabla_{e_{1}}e_{3}\big\rangle,\quad\forall e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}\in\Gamma(E).

The metric connection

[
9

To see the existence of such a connection, one takes an arbitrary

-connection
\nabla^{0}:\Gamma(TM)\times\Gamma(E)\rightarrow\Gamma(E)

H:\Gamma(TM)\otimes\Gamma(E)\rightarrow\Gamma(E)
and

as follows:

\displaystyle\big\langle H(X,e_{1})\big|e_{2}\big\rangle:=X\langle e_{1}|e_{2}\rangle-\big\langle\nabla^{0}_{X}e_{1}\big|e_{2}\big\rangle-\big\langle e_{1}\big|\nabla^{0}_{X}e_{2}\big\rangle,

\displaystyle\nabla^{TM}_{X}e_{1}:=\nabla^{0}_{X}e_{1}+\frac{1}{2}H(X,e_{1}),

where
X\in\Gamma(TM)
and
e_{1},e_{2}\in\Gamma(E)
. One can easily check that

H(X,fe_{1})=H(fX,e_{1})=fH(X,e_{1}),\quad\forall f\in C^{\infty}(M).

Then, the operator
\nabla^{TM}
defines a
TM
-connection on
E
and satisfies the following condition:

\displaystyle X\langle e_{1}|e_{2}\rangle=\big\langle\nabla^{TM}_{X}e_{1}\big|e_{2}\big\rangle+\big\langle e_{1}\big|\nabla^{TM}_{X}e_{2}\big\rangle.

Subsequently, we define a metric connection
\nabla:\Gamma(E)\times\Gamma(E)\rightarrow\Gamma(E)
as the pullback of
\nabla^{TM}
:

\displaystyle\nabla_{e_{1}}e_{2}:=\nabla^{TM}_{a_{E}(e_{1})}e_{2},\quad\forall e_{1},e_{2}\in\Gamma(E).

Then, this connection
\nabla
satisfies the compatibility condition:

\displaystyle\big\langle\nabla_{e_{1}}e_{2}\big|e_{3}\big\rangle+\big\langle e_{2}\big|\nabla_{e_{1}}e_{3}\big\rangle=\big\langle\nabla^{TM}_{a_{E}(e_{1})}e_{2}\big|e_{3}\big\rangle+\big\langle e_{2}\big|\nabla^{TM}_{a_{E}(e_{1})}e_{3}\big\rangle=a_{E}(e_{1})\langle e_{2}|e_{3}\rangle.

Definition 3.1
.

Given a metric connection
\nabla
on an anchored metric bundle
\big(E,\langle\,\cdot\,|\,\cdot\,\rangle,a_{E}\big)
, we defined an associated bracket
\overset{\nabla}{\circ}:~\Gamma(E)\times\Gamma(E)\rightarrow\Gamma(E)
on
\Gamma(E)
through the following relation:

(19)

\displaystyle\langle e_{1}\overset{\nabla}{\circ}e_{2},e_{3}\rangle:=\langle\nabla_{e_{1}}e_{2},e_{3}\rangle-\langle\nabla_{e_{2}}e_{1},e_{3}\rangle+\langle\nabla_{e_{3}}e_{1},e_{2}\rangle.

Furthermore, define an operator
\Delta:~\Gamma(E)\times\Gamma(E)\rightarrow\Gamma(E)
by

(20)

\displaystyle\langle\Delta_{e_{2}}e_{1},e_{3}\rangle:=\langle\nabla_{e_{3}}e_{1},e_{2}\rangle,\quad\forall e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}\in\Gamma(E).

We can easily verify the following identities:

\displaystyle\Delta_{(fe_{2})}e_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f\Delta_{e_{2}}e_{1},

\displaystyle\Delta_{e_{2}}(fe_{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f\Delta_{e_{2}}e_{1}+\langle e_{1}|e_{2}\rangle\cdot\D(f),

\displaystyle\D\langle e_{1}|e_{2}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Delta_{e_{1}}e_{2}+\Delta_{e_{2}}e_{1},

where
\D:C^{\infty}(M)\rightarrow\Gamma(E)
is given by
\langle\D(f)|e\rangle=a_{E}(e)(f)
for all
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
.

The associated bracket
\overset{\nabla}{\circ}
can also be expressed by

(21)

\displaystyle e_{1}\overset{\nabla}{\circ}e_{2}=\nabla_{e_{1}}e_{2}-\nabla_{e_{2}}e_{1}+\Delta_{e_{2}}e_{1}.

Consequently, we obtain the following identities:

\displaystyle a_{E}(e)\langle h_{1}|h_{2}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle e\overset{\nabla}{\circ}h_{1}|h_{2}\rangle+\langle h_{1}|e\overset{\nabla}{\circ}h_{2}\rangle,

\displaystyle e\overset{\nabla}{\circ}e

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\D\langle e|e\rangle.

Thus, the quadruple
\big(E,\langle\,\cdot\,|\,\cdot\,\rangle,\overset{\nabla}{\circ},a_{E}\big)
forms a metric algebroid.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\big(E,\langle\,\cdot\,|\,\cdot\,\rangle,\circ,a_{E}\big)
be a metric algebroid. Then there exists a metric connection
\nabla
on the anchored metric bundle
\big(E,\langle\,\cdot\,|\,\cdot\,\rangle,a_{E}\big)
such that the operation
\circ
is exactly the operation
\overset{\nabla}{\circ}
, as defined in Equation (
19
) or (
21
).

Proof.

Consider an arbitrary metric connection
\nabla^{0}:\Gamma(E)\times\Gamma(E)\rightarrow\Gamma(E)
on the metric vector bundle
E
. Consequently, we have an operator
\overset{\nabla^{0}}{\circ}:\Gamma(E)\times\Gamma(E)\rightarrow\Gamma(E)
defined as follows:

\displaystyle\big\langle e_{1}\overset{\nabla^{0}}{\circ}e_{2}\big|e_{3}\big\rangle:=\big\langle\nabla^{0}_{e_{1}}e_{2}-\nabla^{0}_{e_{2}}e_{1}\big|e_{3}\big\rangle+\big\langle\nabla^{0}_{e_{3}}e_{1}\big|e_{2}\big\rangle,

for all
e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}\in\Gamma(E)
, and we have a metric algebroid
\big(E,\langle\,\cdot\,|\,\cdot\,\rangle,\overset{\nabla^{0}}{\circ},a_{E}\big)
. However,
\overset{\nabla^{0}}{\circ}
is different from the given operator
\circ
. To derive the desired connection
\nabla
, we introduce an operator
K:\Gamma(E)\otimes\Gamma(E)\rightarrow\Gamma(E)
, which is defined by

\displaystyle K(e_{1},e_{2}):=e_{1}\circ e_{2}-e_{1}\overset{\nabla^{0}}{\circ}e_{2},\qquad\forall e_{1},e_{2}\in\Gamma(E).

Through direct calculation, it can be verified that
K
is skew-symmetric and
C^{\infty}(M)
-bilinear, and satisfies the following identity:

\displaystyle\big\langle K(e_{1},e_{2})\big|e_{3}\big\rangle+\big\langle e_{2}\big|K(e_{1},e_{3})\big\rangle=0,\qquad\forall e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}\in\Gamma(E).

Utilizing these properties, we construct the desired connection
\nabla:\Gamma(E)\times\Gamma(E)\rightarrow\Gamma(E)
as

\displaystyle\nabla_{e_{1}}e_{2}:=\nabla^{0}_{e_{1}}e_{2}+\frac{1}{3}K(e_{1},e_{2}),\qquad\forall e_{1},e_{2}\in\Gamma(E).

It can be demonstrated that
\nabla
is indeed a metric connection on
\big(E,\langle\,\cdot\,|\,\cdot\,\rangle,a_{E}\big)
, satisfying the following equation:

\displaystyle\big\langle e_{1}\circ e_{2}\big|e_{3}\big\rangle=\big\langle\nabla_{e_{1}}e_{2}-\nabla_{e_{2}}e_{1}\big|e_{3}\big\rangle+\big\langle\nabla_{e_{3}}e_{1}\big|e_{2}\big\rangle,\qquad\forall e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}\in\Gamma(E).

This completes the proof.
∎

\displaystyle(x+\xi)\overset{\nabla}{\circ}(y+\eta):=\nabla_{x+\xi}(y+\eta)-\nabla_{y+\eta}(x+\xi)+\Delta_{y+\eta}(x+\xi),

where
\Delta:\Gamma(A\oplus A^{*})\times\Gamma(A\oplus A^{*})\rightarrow\Gamma(A\oplus A^{*})
(according to Equation (
20
)) is explicitly expressed by

\displaystyle\big\langle\Delta_{y+\eta}(x+\xi)\big|z+\gamma\big\rangle=\big\langle\nabla_{z+\gamma}(x+\xi)\big|y+\eta\big\rangle=\big\langle\nabla^{AA}_{z}x+\nabla^{A^{*}A}_{\gamma}x\big|\eta\big\rangle+\big\langle\nabla^{AA^{*}}_{z}\xi+\nabla^{A^{*}A^{*}}_{\gamma}\xi\big|y\big\rangle,

for all
x+\xi,y+\eta,z+\gamma\in\Gamma(A\oplus A^{*})
.

Further, the operator
\overset{\nabla}{\circ}
given by Equation (
33
) can be expressed alternatively:

(34)

\displaystyle(x+\xi)\overset{\nabla}{\circ}(y+\eta)=\big([x,y]_{A}+L_{\xi}y-\iota_{\eta}(\mathrm{d}_{*}x)\big)+\big([\xi,\eta]_{*}+L_{x}\eta-\iota_{y}(\mathrm{d}_{A}\xi)\big).

In conclusion,
(A\oplus A^{*},\langle\,\cdot\,|\,\cdot\,\rangle,\overset{\nabla}{\circ},a_{E})
is a metric algebroid. One may ask under what conditions the datum
(A\oplus A^{*},\langle\,\cdot\,|\,\cdot\,\rangle,\overset{\nabla}{\circ},a_{E})
will become a pre-Courant algebroid. Indeed, the answer is merely some system of equations which is not so interesting.
Moreover, suppose that
(A,A^{*})
is a Lie bialgebroid in the sense of
[
26
]
, then
A\oplus A^{*}
is a Courant algebroid and the canonical Dorfman bracket of
A\oplus A^{*}
is exactly defined as above (see
[
13
]
).

Case 2:
E=TM\oplus T^{*}M
.

Let
A=TM
be the tangent Lie algebroid and
A^{*}=T^{*}M
equipped with the trivial Lie algebroid structure. The anchor map
a_{E}:\Gamma(TM\oplus T^{*}M)\rightarrow\Gamma(TM)
is defined by:

\displaystyle a_{E}(X+\alpha)=X,\qquad\forall X+\alpha\in\Gamma(TM\oplus T^{*}M).

Moreover, the Courant bracket (
34
) transforms into the following expression:

(35)

\displaystyle(X+\alpha)\overset{\nabla}{\circ}(Y+\beta)=[X,Y]+L_{X}\beta-\iota_{Y}\mathrm{d}\alpha,

where
\mathrm{d}:\Gamma(\wedge^{\bullet}T^{*}M)\rightarrow\Gamma(\wedge^{\bullet+1}T^{*}M)
is the de Rham differential. Indeed, the quadruple
\big(TM\oplus T^{*}M
,
\langle\,\cdot\,|\,\cdot\,\rangle
,
\overset{\nabla}{\circ}
,
a_{E}\big)
is the first example of Courant algebroids
[
13
]
.

The standard Courant bracket
\overset{\nabla}{\circ}
in (
35
) can be altered through a twisting operation and a differential 3-form
\omega\in\Omega^{3}(M)=\Gamma(\wedge^{3}T^{*}M)
. The modified Courant bracket, denoted
\overset{\omega}{\circ}
, is defined as

(36)

\displaystyle(X+\alpha)\overset{\omega}{\circ}(Y+\beta):=(X+\alpha)\overset{\nabla}{\circ}(Y+\beta)+\omega(X,Y,\,\cdot\,).

Indeed, we can rewrite
\overset{\omega}{\circ}
via a particular type of metric connections on
(TM\oplus T^{*}M,\langle\,\cdot\,|\,\cdot\,\rangle,a_{E})
as follows:

(1)

Consider an arbitrary torsion-free connection
\nabla^{TM}:\Gamma(TM)\times\Gamma(TM)\rightarrow\Gamma(TM)
. This connection induces a corresponding connection
\nabla^{T^{*}M}:\Gamma(TM)\times\Gamma(T^{*}M)\rightarrow\Gamma(T^{*}M)
on the cotangent bundle
T^{*}M
, defined by the following equation:

\langle\nabla^{T^{*}M}_{X}\alpha,Y\rangle=X\langle\alpha,Y\rangle-\langle\alpha,\nabla^{TM}_{X}Y\rangle,\quad\forall X,Y\in\Gamma(TM),\alpha\in\Gamma(T^{*}M).

(2)

Define an associated connection
\nabla:\Gamma(TM\oplus T^{*}M)\times\Gamma(TM\oplus T^{*}M)\rightarrow\Gamma(TM\oplus T^{*}M)
by

(37)

\displaystyle\nabla_{X+\alpha}(Y+\beta)=\nabla^{TM}_{X}Y+\nabla^{T^{*}M}_{X}\beta,\qquad\forall X+\alpha,Y+\beta\in\Gamma(TM\oplus T^{*}M).

Then
\nabla
is a metric connection on
\Gamma(TM\oplus T^{*}M)
.

(3)

Define an
\omega
-twisted connection
\nabla^{\omega}:\Gamma(TM\oplus T^{*}M)\times\Gamma(TM\oplus T^{*}M)\rightarrow\Gamma(TM\oplus T^{*}M)
by

\displaystyle\nabla^{\omega}_{X+\alpha}(Y+\beta)=\nabla_{X+\alpha}(Y+\beta)+\frac{1}{3}\omega(X,Y),\qquad\forall X+\alpha,Y+\beta\in\Gamma(TM\oplus T^{*}M),

where
\nabla
is the metric connection defined in Equation (
37
).
Then
\nabla^{\omega}
is also a metric connection on
\Gamma(TM\oplus T^{*}M)
.

(4)

It can be directly verified that the Courant bracket
\overset{\nabla}{\circ}
from Equation (
35
) is induced by the connection
\nabla
. Similarly, the
\omega
-twisted Courant bracket
\overset{\omega}{\circ}
defined in Equation (
36
) is induced by the modified connection
\nabla^{\omega}
.

Case 3:
E=TM\oplus TM
.

Let
M
be a smooth manifold and
g:TM\otimes TM\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
a pseudo-Riemannian metric. Thus, one can define an induced pseudo-metric

\langle\,\cdot\,|\,\cdot\,\rangle:\big(TM\oplus TM\big)\otimes\big(TM\oplus TM\big)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

by

\displaystyle\langle(X_{1},X_{2})\mid(Y_{1},Y_{2})\rangle:=g(X_{1},Y_{2})+g(X_{2},Y_{1}),\quad\forall(X_{1},X_{2}),(Y_{1},Y_{2})\in\Gamma(TM\oplus TM).

Define the anchor map
\rho:\Gamma(TM\oplus TM)\rightarrow\Gamma(TM)
by

\rho(X_{1},X_{2}):=X_{1}+X_{2}
, for all
(X_{1},X_{2})\in\Gamma(TM\oplus TM)
.
So, the triple
(TM\oplus TM
,
\langle\,\cdot\,|\,\cdot\,\rangle
,
\rho)
is an anchored metric bundle.

Since we have an isomorphism of vector bundles
g^{\sharp}:TM\rightarrow T^{*}M
given by

\big\langle g^{\sharp}(X)\big|Y\big\rangle:=g(X,Y)
,
for all
X,Y\in\Gamma(TM)
, one can identify
T^{*}M
with
TM
and endow
T^{*}M
with a Lie algebroid structure isomorphic to
TM
. Then consider the particular instance of
A=TM
in
Case 1
, and we see that
TM\oplus TM

\cong TM\oplus T^{*}M
has a metric algebroid structure. However,
it can never become a pre-Courant algebroid
. The reason is as follows.

We can first take the Levi-Civita connection
\nabla^{TM}:\Gamma(TM)\times\Gamma(TM)\rightarrow\Gamma(TM)

arising from
g
; hence
\nabla^{TM}
is torsion-free. Then define an associative operator

\Delta^{TM}:\Gamma(TM)\times\Gamma(TM)\rightarrow\Gamma(TM)
by

g\big(\Delta^{TM}_{w}u,v\big):=g\big(\nabla^{TM}_{v}u,w\big),\quad\forall u,v,w\in\Gamma(TM).

In the meantime, we have the associated metric connection in
TM\oplus TM
, i.e.,
\nabla:\Gamma(TM\oplus TM)\times\Gamma(TM\oplus TM)\rightarrow\Gamma(TM\oplus TM)
, given by

\displaystyle\nabla_{(X_{1},X_{2})}(Y_{1},Y_{2}):=\big(\nabla^{TM}_{X_{1}+X_{2}}Y_{1},\nabla^{TM}_{X_{1}+X_{2}}Y_{2}\big),\qquad\forall(X_{1},X_{2}),(Y_{1},Y_{2})\in\Gamma(TM\oplus TM).

As for the Dorfman bracket
\overset{\nabla}{\circ}:~\Gamma(TM\oplus TM)\times\Gamma(TM\oplus TM)\rightarrow\Gamma(TM\oplus TM)

which is generally expressed by

\displaystyle(X_{1},X_{2})\overset{\nabla}{\circ}(Y_{1},Y_{2}):=\nabla_{(X_{1},X_{2})}(Y_{1},Y_{2})-\nabla_{(Y_{1},Y_{2})}(X_{1},X_{2})+\Delta_{(Y_{1},Y_{2})}(X_{1},X_{2}),

for all
(X_{1},X_{2}),(Y_{1},Y_{2})\in\Gamma(TM\oplus TM)
, we can compute the following four special situations:

\displaystyle(X_{1},0)\overset{\nabla}{\circ}(Y_{1},0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big([X_{1},Y_{1}],0\big),

\displaystyle(X_{1},0)\overset{\nabla}{\circ}(0,Y_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(-\nabla^{TM}_{Y_{2}}X_{1}+\Delta^{TM}_{Y_{2}}X_{1},\nabla^{TM}_{X_{1}}Y_{2}+\Delta^{TM}_{Y_{2}}X_{1}\big),

\displaystyle(0,X_{2})\overset{\nabla}{\circ}(Y_{1},0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(\nabla^{TM}_{X_{2}}Y_{1}+\Delta^{TM}_{Y_{1}}X_{2},-\nabla^{TM}_{Y_{1}}X_{2}+\Delta^{TM}_{Y_{1}}X_{2}\big),

\displaystyle(0,X_{2})\overset{\nabla}{\circ}(0,Y_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(0,[X_{2},Y_{2}]\big).

In order that the said metric algebroid
\big(TM\oplus TM
,

\langle\,\cdot\,\mid\,\cdot\,\rangle
,
\overset{\nabla}{\circ}
,
\rho\big)

is a pre-Courant algebroid, we need

\rho((X_{1},0)\overset{\nabla}{\circ}(0,Y_{2}))=[\rho(X_{1},0),\rho(0,Y_{2})]

which is just the condition
\Delta^{TM}_{Y_{2}}X_{1}=0
,
\forall Y_{2},X_{1}\in\Gamma(TM)
, and hence we must have
\nabla^{TM}=0
, a contradiction.

3.3.
Construction of Courant algebroids from compatible metric
n
-systems

In this section, we are about to state our
second main result. Consider a vector bundle
E\rightarrow M
equipped with a pseudo-metric
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
on
E
and suppose that
\widehat{u}^{1},\widehat{u}^{2},\ldots,\widehat{u}^{n}
are elements of
\Gamma(E)
that are linearly independent over
C^{\infty}(M)
. Furthermore, let
\{\widehat{Z}_{1},\widehat{Z}_{2},\ldots,\widehat{Z}_{n}\}\subset\Gamma(\mathrm{CDO}(E))
be covariant differential operators on
E
(which may be linearly dependent over
C^{\infty}(M)
). We consistently represent the symbols of
\{\widehat{Z}_{1},\ldots,\widehat{Z}_{n}\}
by the letters
\{\widehat{z}_{1},\ldots,\widehat{z}_{n}\}
(where
\widehat{z}_{i}\in\Gamma(TM)
for each
i
). Assume that the operators
\widehat{Z}_{i}
, their corresponding symbols
\widehat{z}_{i}
, and the pseudo-metric
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
satisfy the following compatibility condition:

(38)

\displaystyle\big\langle\widehat{Z}_{i}(e_{1})\big|e_{2}\big\rangle+\big\langle e_{1}\big|\widehat{Z}_{i}(e_{2})\big\rangle=\widehat{z}_{i}\langle e_{1}|e_{2}\rangle,\qquad\forall e_{1},e_{2}\in\Gamma(E).

In this part, we suppose that the rank of the vector bundle
E
is at least
n
.

Definition 3.11
.

The datum

(\widehat{Z}_{1},\ldots,\widehat{Z}_{n};\widehat{u}^{1},\ldots,\widehat{u}^{n})

described as above is called a
metric
n
-system
.

Given a metric
n
-system, one can define the following structure maps on
\Gamma(E)
, which constitute a metric algebroid
\big(E
,
\langle\,\cdot\,|\,\cdot\,\rangle
,
\overset{\nabla}{\circ}
,
a_{E}\big)
.

•

The anchor map
a_{E}:E\rightarrow TM
is defined by the expression:

a_{E}(e):=\big\langle e\big|\widehat{u}^{i}\big\rangle\cdot\widehat{z}_{i},\quad\forall e\in\Gamma(E).

The associated operator
\D:C^{\infty}(M)\rightarrow\Gamma(E)
is given by the following formula

\D(f):=\widehat{z}_{i}(f)\cdot\widehat{u}^{i},\hskip 20.00003pt\forall f\in C^{\infty}(M).

•

The associated connection
\nabla:\Gamma(E)\times\Gamma(E)\rightarrow\Gamma(E)
is defined by the expression:

(39)

\displaystyle\nabla_{e_{1}}e_{2}:=\big\langle e_{1}\big|\widehat{u}^{i}\big\rangle\cdot\widehat{Z}_{i}(e_{2}),\qquad\forall e_{1},e_{2}\in\Gamma(E).

The connection
\nabla
is a metric connection because it satisfies Equation (
38
).
The associated operator
\Delta:\Gamma(E)\times\Gamma(E)\rightarrow\Gamma(E)
corresponding to
\nabla
is given by the following formula

\Delta_{e_{2}}e_{1}:=\big\langle\widehat{Z}_{i}(e_{1})\big|e_{2}\big\rangle\cdot\widehat{u}^{i},\hskip 20.00003pt\forall e_{1},e_{2}\in\Gamma(E).

•

The Dorfman bracket

\overset{\nabla}{\circ}:\Gamma(E)\times\Gamma(E)\rightarrow\Gamma(E)
induced by
\nabla
can be expressed by

(40)

\displaystyle e_{1}\overset{\nabla}{\circ}e_{2}

\displaystyle:=\hskip-5.0pt

\displaystyle\nabla_{e_{1}}e_{2}-\nabla_{e_{2}}e_{1}+\Delta_{e_{2}}e_{1}

\displaystyle=\hskip-5.0pt

\displaystyle\big\langle e_{1}\big|\widehat{u}^{i}\big\rangle\cdot\widehat{Z}_{i}(e_{2})-\big\langle e_{2}\big|\widehat{u}^{i}\big\rangle\cdot\widehat{Z}_{i}(e_{1})+\big\langle\widehat{Z}_{i}(e_{1})\big|e_{2}\big\rangle\cdot\widehat{u}^{i},\qquad\forall e_{1},e_{2}\in\Gamma(E).

The next step is to investigate the sufficient conditions under which this metric algebroid can be characterized as either
a pre-Courant algebroid or a Courant algebroid.

Proposition 3.12
.

Suppose that the metric
n
-system
(\widehat{Z}_{1},\ldots,\widehat{Z}_{n};\widehat{u}^{1},\ldots,\widehat{u}^{n})
on
E
satisfies the following conditions:

(41)

\displaystyle\widehat{Z}_{i}(\widehat{u}^{j})

\displaystyle\hskip-5.0pt=\hskip-5.0pt

\displaystyle C_{ik}^{j}\widehat{u}^{k},\qquad\qquad\text{where}\quad C_{ik}^{j}\in C^{\infty}(M),

(42)

\displaystyle[\widehat{z}_{i},\widehat{z}_{j}]

\displaystyle\hskip-5.0pt=\hskip-5.0pt

\displaystyle(C_{ji}^{k}-C_{ij}^{k})\widehat{z}_{k},

(43)

\displaystyle\big\langle\widehat{u}^{i}\big|\widehat{u}^{j}\big\rangle

\displaystyle\hskip-5.0pt=\hskip-5.0pt

\displaystyle 0,

for all
i,j\in\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
. Then the associated metric algebroid
\big(E
,
\langle\,\cdot\,|\,\cdot\,\rangle
,
\overset{\nabla}{\circ}
,
a_{E}\big)
is a pre-Courant algebroid.

Proof.

We only need to verify the relation

\displaystyle a_{E}(e_{1}\overset{\nabla}{\circ}e_{2})-[a_{E}(e_{1}),a_{E}(e_{2})]=0,\qquad\forall e_{1},e_{2}\in\Gamma(E).

In fact, we have

\displaystyle a_{E}(e_{1}\overset{\nabla}{\circ}e_{2})-[a_{E}(e_{1}),a_{E}(e_{2})]

\displaystyle{\mathrel{\to}^{\mbox{\eqref{Def: Dorfman bracket of Courant algebroid in n system}}}}

\displaystyle\big\langle e_{1}\big|\widehat{u}^{i}\big\rangle\cdot\big\langle\widehat{Z}_{i}(e_{2})\big|\widehat{u}^{j}\big\rangle\cdot\widehat{z}_{j}-\big\langle e_{2}\big|\widehat{u}^{i}\big\rangle\cdot\big\langle\widehat{Z}_{i}(e_{1})\big|\widehat{u}^{j}\big\rangle\cdot\widehat{z}_{j}+\big\langle\widehat{Z}_{i}(e_{1})\big|e_{2}\big\rangle\cdot\langle\widehat{u}^{i}|\widehat{u}^{j}\rangle\cdot\widehat{z}_{j}

\displaystyle\quad-\langle e_{1}|\widehat{u}^{i}\rangle\cdot\langle e_{2}|\widehat{u}^{j}\rangle\cdot[\widehat{z}_{i},\widehat{z}_{j}]-\langle e_{1}|\widehat{u}^{i}\rangle\cdot\widehat{z}_{i}\langle e_{2}|\widehat{u}^{i}\rangle\cdot\widehat{z}_{j}+\langle e_{2}|\widehat{u}^{i}\rangle\cdot\widehat{z}_{j}\langle e_{1}|\widehat{u}^{i}\rangle\cdot\widehat{z}_{i}

\displaystyle{\mathrel{\to}^{\mbox{\eqref{Eqn: compatible condition of pseudo-metric and CDO}}}}

\displaystyle-\big\langle e_{1}\big|\widehat{u}^{i}\big\rangle\cdot\big\langle e_{2}\big|\widehat{Z}_{i}(\widehat{u}^{k})\big\rangle\cdot\widehat{z}_{k}+\big\langle e_{2}\big|\widehat{u}^{j}\big\rangle\cdot\big\langle e_{1}\big|\widehat{Z}_{j}(\widehat{u}^{k})\big\rangle\cdot\widehat{z}_{k}

\displaystyle\quad-\langle e_{1}|\widehat{u}^{i}\rangle\cdot\langle e_{2}|\widehat{u}^{j}\rangle\cdot[\widehat{z}_{i},\widehat{z}_{j}]+\big\langle\widehat{Z}_{i}(e_{1})\big|e_{2}\big\rangle\cdot\langle\widehat{u}^{i}|\widehat{u}^{j}\rangle\cdot\widehat{z}_{j}

\displaystyle{\mathrel{\to}^{\mbox{\eqref{Eqn: compatible condition 1 of Courant algebroid in n system}\eqref{Eqn: compatible condition 2 of pre-Courant algebroid in n system}}}}

\displaystyle-\big\langle e_{1}\big|\widehat{u}^{i}\big\rangle\cdot\big\langle e_{2}\big|C_{ij}^{k}\widehat{u}^{j}\big\rangle\cdot\widehat{z}_{k}+\big\langle e_{2}\big|\widehat{u}^{j}\big\rangle\cdot\big\langle e_{1}\big|C_{ji}^{k}\widehat{u}^{i}\big\rangle\cdot\widehat{z}_{k}

\displaystyle\quad-\langle e_{1}|\widehat{u}^{i}\rangle\cdot\langle e_{2}|\widehat{u}^{j}\rangle\cdot(C_{ji}^{k}-C_{ij}^{k})\widehat{z}_{k}+\big\langle\widehat{Z}_{i}(e_{1})\big|e_{2}\big\rangle\cdot\langle\widehat{u}^{i}|\widehat{u}^{j}\rangle\cdot\widehat{z}_{j}

\displaystyle{\mathrel{\to}^{\mbox{\eqref{Eqn: compatible condition 3 of Courant algebroid in n system}}}}

\displaystyle 0.

∎

Proposition 3.13
.

Consider the metric
n
-system
(\widehat{Z}_{1},\ldots,\widehat{Z}_{n};\widehat{u}^{1},\ldots,\widehat{u}^{n})
on
E
, and suppose that it satisfies Conditions (
41
) and (
43
) of the previous proposition, and the following condition:

(44)

\displaystyle[\widehat{Z}_{i},\widehat{Z}_{j}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(C_{ji}^{k}-C_{ij}^{k})\widehat{Z}_{k},

for all
i,j\in\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
.
Under these conditions, the following equation holds:

C^{\nabla}(e_{1},e_{2})e_{3}=\nabla_{(\Delta_{e_{3}}e_{2})}e_{1}=Q(e_{1},e_{2},e_{3})=(\D\langle e_{1}|e_{3}\rangle)\overset{\nabla}{\circ}e_{2}=0,\qquad\forall e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}\in\Gamma(E).

Consequently, the quadruple
\big(E
,
\langle\,\cdot\,|\,\cdot\,\rangle
,
\overset{\nabla}{\circ}
,
a_{E}\big)
constitutes a Courant algebroid.

Proof.

Now, we verify that
\big(E
,
\langle\,\cdot\,|\,\cdot\,\rangle
,
\overset{\nabla}{\circ}
,
a_{E}\big)
is a Courant algebroid.

\bullet

To see
C^{\nabla}(e_{1},e_{2})e_{3}
=0, we use Equations (
41
) and (
44
) to get

\displaystyle C^{\nabla}(e_{1},e_{2})e_{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla_{e_{1}}\nabla_{e_{2}}e_{3}-\nabla_{e_{2}}\nabla_{e_{1}}e_{3}-\nabla_{(e_{1}\overset{\nabla}{\circ}e_{2})}e_{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle e_{1}|\widehat{u}^{i}\rangle\cdot\langle e_{2}|\widehat{u}^{j}\rangle\cdot[\widehat{Z}_{i},\widehat{Z}_{j}](e_{3})

\displaystyle\qquad+\langle e_{1}|\widehat{u}^{i}\rangle\cdot\langle e_{2}|\widehat{Z}_{i}(\widehat{u}^{k})\rangle\cdot\widehat{Z}_{k}(e_{3})-\langle e_{2}|\widehat{u}^{j}\rangle\cdot\langle e_{1}|\widehat{Z}_{j}(\widehat{u}^{k})\rangle\cdot\widehat{Z}_{k}(e_{3})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-\langle\widehat{Z}_{i}(e_{1})|e_{2}\rangle\cdot\langle\widehat{u}^{i}|\widehat{u}^{k}\rangle\cdot\widehat{Z}_{k}(e_{3})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle e_{1}|\widehat{u}^{i}\rangle\cdot\langle e_{2}|\widehat{u}^{j}\rangle\cdot(C_{ji}^{k}-C_{ij}^{k})\widehat{Z}_{k}(e_{3})

\displaystyle\qquad+\langle e_{1}|\widehat{u}^{i}\rangle\cdot\langle e_{2}|C_{ij}^{k}\widehat{u}^{j}\rangle\cdot\widehat{Z}_{k}(e_{3})-\langle e_{2}|\widehat{u}^{j}\rangle\cdot\langle e_{1}|C_{ji}^{k}\widehat{u}^{i}\rangle\cdot\widehat{Z}_{k}(e_{3})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-\langle\widehat{Z}_{i}(e_{1})|e_{2}\rangle\cdot\langle\widehat{u}^{i}|\widehat{u}^{k}\rangle\cdot\widehat{Z}_{k}(e_{3})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

\bullet

By using the above fact
C^{\nabla}(e_{1},e_{2})e_{3}=0
, and Equation (
43
), we have

\displaystyle\nabla_{(\Delta_{e_{3}}e_{2})}e_{1}=\langle\widehat{Z}_{i}(e_{2})|e_{3}\rangle\cdot\langle\widehat{u}^{i}|\widehat{u}^{j}\rangle\cdot\widehat{Z}_{j}(e_{1})=0,

\displaystyle\big\langle Q(e_{1},e_{2},e_{3})\big|t\big\rangle=\big\langle C^{\nabla}(e_{1},t)e_{2}\big|e_{3}\big\rangle+\big\langle C^{\nabla}(e_{2},t)e_{3}\big|e_{1}\big\rangle+\big\langle C^{\nabla}(e_{3},t)e_{1}\big|e_{2}\big\rangle=0.

\bullet

It is left to show that
(\D\langle e_{1}|e_{3}\rangle)\overset{\nabla}{\circ}e_{2}=0
. By applying Equations (
41
), (
42
) and (
44
), we get

\displaystyle(\D\langle e_{1}|e_{3}\rangle)\overset{\nabla}{\circ}e_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\widehat{z}_{j}\big(\langle e_{1}|e_{3}\rangle\big)\cdot\langle\widehat{u}^{j}|\widehat{u}^{i}\rangle\cdot\widehat{Z}_{i}(e_{2})

\displaystyle\qquad+\big\langle e_{2}\big|\widehat{u}^{j}\big\rangle\cdot[\widehat{z}_{i},\widehat{z}_{j}]\big(\langle e_{1}|e_{3}\rangle\big)\cdot\widehat{u}^{i}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-\big\langle e_{2}\big|\widehat{u}^{j}\big\rangle\cdot\widehat{z}_{k}\big(\langle e_{1}|e_{3}\rangle\big)\cdot\widehat{Z}_{j}(\widehat{u}^{k})+\widehat{z}_{k}\big(\langle e_{1}|e_{3}\rangle\big)\cdot\big\langle\widehat{Z}_{i}(\widehat{u}^{k})\big|e_{2}\big\rangle\cdot\widehat{u}^{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\widehat{z}_{j}\big(\langle e_{1}|e_{3}\rangle\big)\cdot\langle\widehat{u}^{j}|\widehat{u}^{i}\rangle\cdot\widehat{Z}_{i}(e_{2})

\displaystyle\qquad+\big\langle e_{2}\big|\widehat{u}^{j}\big\rangle\cdot(C_{ji}^{k}-C_{ij}^{k})\widehat{z}_{k}\big(\langle e_{1}|e_{3}\rangle\big)\cdot\widehat{u}^{i}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-\big\langle e_{2}\big|\widehat{u}^{j}\big\rangle\cdot\widehat{z}_{k}\big(\langle e_{1}|e_{3}\rangle\big)\cdot C_{ji}^{k}\widehat{u}^{i}+\widehat{z}_{k}\big(\langle e_{1}|e_{3}\rangle\big)\cdot\big\langle C_{ij}^{k}\widehat{u}^{j}\big|e_{2}\big\rangle\cdot\widehat{u}^{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

So, the following Leibniz identity holds:

\displaystyle e_{1}\overset{\nabla}{\circ}(e_{2}\overset{\nabla}{\circ}e_{3})-(e_{1}\overset{\nabla}{\circ}e_{2})\overset{\nabla}{\circ}e_{3}-e_{2}\overset{\nabla}{\circ}(e_{1}\overset{\nabla}{\circ}e_{3})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C^{\nabla}(e_{1},e_{2})e_{3}+C^{\nabla}(e_{2},e_{3})e_{1}+C^{\nabla}(e_{3},e_{1})e_{2}

\displaystyle+\nabla_{(\Delta_{e_{3}}e_{2})}e_{1}+\nabla_{(\Delta_{e_{1}}e_{3})}e_{2}+\nabla_{(\Delta_{e_{2}}e_{1})}e_{3}+Q(e_{1},e_{2},e_{3})+(\D\langle e_{1}|e_{3}\rangle)\overset{\nabla}{\circ}e_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

This fact confirms that the metric algebroid
\big(E
,
\langle\,\cdot\,|\,\cdot\,\rangle
,
\overset{\nabla}{\circ}
,
a_{E}\big)
is a Courant algebroid.
∎

Example 3.15

Let
c_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
be a constant. Consider

V(\cong\mathbb{R}^{4})=\Span_{\mathbb{R}}\{w_{1},w_{2},w_{3},w_{4}\}

over

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}0&0&1&0\\
0&0&0&-1\\

Vogel universality and beyond


Abstract
For simple Lie algebras we construct characteristic identities for split (polarized) Casimir operators in the representations T ⊗ Y n T\otimes Y_{n} and T ⊗ Y n ′ T\otimes Y_{n}^{\prime} , where T T is defining (minimal fundamental for exceptional Lie algebras) representation, Y n Y_{n} are Cartan n n -powers of the adjoint representations ad = Y 1 \rm ad=Y_{1} , and Y n ′ Y_{n}^{\prime} are special representations arising in the Clebsch-Gordan decomposition of the totally symmetric part of ad ⊗ n \rm ad^{\otimes n} . By means of these characteristic identities, we derive (for all simple Lie algebras, except 𝔢 8 \mathfrak{e}_{8} ) explicit formulas for invariant projectors onto irreducible subrepresentations appearing in the decomposition of T ⊗ Y n T\otimes Y_{n} . These projectors and characteristic identities are written in the universal form for all simple Lie algebras (except 𝔢 8 \mathfrak{e}_{8} ) in terms of the Vogel parameters. Universal formulas for the dimensions of Casimir subrepresentations arising in decompositions of T ⊗ Y n T\otimes Y_{n} are found. An example of evaluating of universal colour (group) factors for an infinite set of special Feynman ladder diagrams in non-Abelian gauge theories is given.

\begin{array}[]{c}{\rm for}\;\;\mathfrak{so}_{N}:\;\;\;\rho=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{[\frac{N}{2}]}(\frac{N}{2}-i)e^{(i)}\;;\;\;\;\;\;{\rm for}\;\;\mathfrak{sp}_{N=2r}:\;\;\;\rho=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\frac{N}{2}}(\frac{N}{2}-i+1)e^{(i)}\;.\end{array}

(2.36)

Finally, taking the orthonormal metric

(e^{(i)},e^{(j)})=\delta^{ij}

in the root spaces of
\mathfrak{sl}_{N}
and
\mathfrak{so}_{N}
,
we obtain the eigenvalues for the quadratic Casimir
operators (
2.29
) in the
\mathfrak{sl}_{N}
case
(see e.g.
[

\begin{array}[]{c}C_{(2)}([\lambda_{1},...,\lambda_{N-1}])=\sum\limits_{\ell=1}^{N-1}\lambda_{\ell}^{2}-2\sum\limits_{\ell=1}^{N-1}\ell\lambda_{\ell}+(N+1)|\lambda|-\frac{|\lambda|^{2}}{N}=\\[5.69046pt]
=-\sum\limits_{\ell=1}^{k}a_{\ell}^{2}+2\sum\limits_{\ell=1}^{k}\ell a_{\ell}+(N-1)|\lambda|-\frac{|\lambda|^{2}}{N}\;,\end{array}

(2.37)

where
\lambda^{\sf T}=[a_{1},a_{2},...,a_{k}]
,

|\lambda^{\sf T}|=|\lambda|
, and in

\mathfrak{so}_{N}
case:

\mathfrak{so}_{N}:\;\;\;C_{(2)}([\lambda_{1},...,\lambda_{m}])=\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}(\lambda_{\ell}^{2}+\lambda_{\ell}(N-2\ell))=\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}(\lambda_{\ell}^{2}-2\ell\lambda_{\ell})+N|\lambda|\;,

(2.38)

where

. In the case of
\mathfrak{sp}_{N}

\mathfrak{sp}_{N}:\;\;C_{(2)}([\lambda_{1},...,\lambda_{m}])=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}(\lambda_{\ell}^{2}+\lambda_{\ell}(N+2-2\ell))=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}(\lambda_{\ell}^{2}-2\ell\lambda_{\ell})+\frac{1}{2}(N+2)|\lambda|,

(2.39)

and, in the root space of

according to the normalization (
2.32

we choose the orthogonal metric as

(e^{(j)},e^{(\ell)})=\frac{1}{2}\delta^{j\ell}

Recall that, for the Lie algebra

2.2.2
Universal description of Lie algebras
using the split Casimir operator technique

Note that the eigenvalues (
2.28
) can take the same value
for different irreducible representations
T^{(\Lambda)}

in the decomposition (
2.27
). We denote by
a_{i}

the eigenvalues
\hat{c}_{1...k}^{(\Lambda)}

that take mismatched values
(the index
i=1,2,...,M
numerates these eigenvalues).
Then
the
k
-split Casimir operator (
2.26
) in the representation
(
2.27
) satisfies the characteristic identity

\prod_{i=1}^{M}(\hat{C}_{\Lambda_{1},...,\Lambda_{k}}-a_{i})=0\,,

(2.41)

which leads to the definition of projectors

P_{a_{j}}=\prod_{i\neq j}\frac{(\hat{C}_{\Lambda_{1},...,\Lambda_{k}}-a_{i})}{(a_{j}-a_{i})}\;.

(2.42)

The images of these projectors are invariant subspaces

V_{a_{j}}\subset V_{\Lambda_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes V_{\Lambda_{k}}

with dimensions

\dim V_{a_{j}}={\rm Tr}_{1,2,...,k}(P_{a_{j}})\;.

(2.43)

Then, the trace of the
m
-th power
of the
k
-split Casimir operator in the representation
(
2.27
) is defined by the formula

{\rm Tr}_{12...k}(\hat{C}_{\Lambda_{1},...,\Lambda_{k}})^{m}={\rm Tr}_{12...k}\Bigl(\sum_{j=1}^{M}\;a_{j}^{m}\;P_{a_{j}}\Bigr)=\sum_{j=1}^{M}\;a_{j}^{m}\;\dim V_{a_{j}}\;.

(2.44)

As we mentioned above, the invariant subspaces
V_{a_{j}}

can be reducible since
the quadratic Casimir operator (
2.23
) can take
the same eigenvalue (
2.28
)
in different irreducible representations
T^{(\Lambda)}
.
When
we consider the decomposition (
2.27
) of the representation

\rm ad^{\otimes k}

a_{j}=\hat{c}_{1...k}^{(\Lambda)}

are written in the form (
2.33
) and are determined only
by the value (
2.31
) of the quadratic Casimir
operator in the representations
T^{(\Lambda)}
. In this

T^{(\Lambda)}
,
that act in the subspace
V_{a_{j}}
,
as

(associated
with the eigenvalue

), and call the spaces

\begin{array}[]{c}{\rm Tr}_{1}(\hat{C}_{\Box\otimes\rm ad})^{\,L}=\dfrac{I_{2}\,}{\dim\mathfrak{g}}\,{\rm Tr}_{12}(\hat{C}_{\Box\otimes\rm ad})^{\,L}=\dfrac{I_{2}\,}{\dim\mathfrak{g}}\,{\rm Tr}_{12}\Bigl(\hat{C}_{\Box\otimes\rm ad}^{\,L}\sum\limits_{i}P_{a_{i}}^{(1)}\Bigr)=\\[8.5359pt]
=\dfrac{I_{2}\,}{\dim\mathfrak{g}}\,{\rm Tr}_{12}\Bigl(\sum\limits_{i}a_{i}^{\,L}P_{a_{i}}^{(1)}\Bigr)=\dfrac{I_{2}\,}{\dim\mathfrak{g}}\,\sum\limits_{i=1}^{4}\bigl(a_{i}^{\,L}\,\dim V_{a_{i}}^{(1)}\bigr)=\\[8.5359pt]
=I_{2}\,\dfrac{\dim\Box}{\dim\mathfrak{g}}\Bigl((-\frac{1}{2})^{{}^{L}}+(-\frac{\hat{\alpha}}{2})^{{}^{L}}\,\dfrac{(1-\hat{\alpha}-\hat{\beta}^{\prime})(\hat{\gamma}^{\prime}-1)}{\hat{\alpha}\hat{\beta}^{\prime}(\hat{\gamma}^{\prime}-\hat{\alpha})}+(-\frac{\hat{\gamma}^{\prime}}{2})^{{}^{L}}\,\dfrac{(1-\hat{\beta}^{\prime}-\hat{\gamma}^{\prime})(\hat{\alpha}-1)}{\hat{\gamma}^{\prime}\hat{\beta}^{\prime}(\hat{\alpha}-\hat{\gamma}^{\prime})}\Bigr)\;,\end{array}

(4.39)

where
I_{2}
is the unit operator acting
in the second space of the

\rm ad
-representation, while

\dim V_{a_{i}}^{(1)}
and
a_{i}

were defined (as functions of the
Vogel parameters) in (
4.37
) and (
4.38
).
Thus, formula (
4.39
) gives the universal expression
for the colour factors of the
Feynman diagrams (4.30
b
) in gauge theories
with all gauge groups, except the gauge group generated by
the Lie algebra

\mathfrak{e}_{8}
. In the case of
\mathfrak{e}_{8}
,
the defining representation
\Box
coincides with
the
\rm ad
-representation, and the solid lines
in the diagrams (4.26
b
), (4.30
b
)
can be redrawn as wavy lines. In this case, the split Casimir operator

\hat{C}_{\Box\otimes\rm ad}=\hat{C}_{\rm ad\otimes\rm ad}
is investigated in the
framework of the standard Vogel universality
(see e.g.
[
11
]
) and the colour factor for
the diagram (4.30
b
)
is evaluated by the methods considered in
[
8
]
.

5
Conclusion

The main results of this paper are the universal answers given in
(
4.30
)-(
4.33
) for the dimensions
of the representations
\Lambda^{(n)}_{i}
listed in Table
5
and universal formulas
(
4.34
) for the Casimir eigenvalue
c_{(2)}^{(\Box)}
. The representations
\Lambda^{(n)}_{i}
are not
present in the decomposition of
\rm ad^{\otimes k}
. They arise in the decomposition of the tensor products
\Box\otimes Y_{n}

of the defining representation
\Box
and any representation
Y_{n}
,
for example, the defining representation and the

\rm ad
-representation
Y_{1}
. It should be noted that the universality in (
4.30
)-(
4.33
) is not quite explicit. This means that we need to substitute there the universal formulas for
\dim Y_{n}
,

\dim\mathfrak{g}
, and formulas (
4.34
) for the
eigenvalues of the quadratic Casimir

c_{(2)}^{(\Box)}
in the defining representation.
At the end of the paper, we give an example of applying
the obtained results for
evaluating universal colour (group) factors for an infinite
set of Feynman diagrams in non-Abelian gauge theories
with the gauge group generated by any simple Lie algebra
(except
\mathfrak{e}_{8}
).

Now we discuss some promising directions of investigations
that follow from the results obtained in this paper.
First of all, it is clear that
there exists a uniform (valid for all simple Lie algebras) decomposition
of the tensor product
Y_{1}\otimes Y_{n}

(see equations (
2.45
), (
2.46
), (
2.47
) and
(
3.66
)). Thus, one can find
universal formulas for dimensions
of an infinite series of
representations arising in the decomposition of

Y_{1}\otimes Y_{n}
.

By using formulas (
4.9
) and (
4.4
), one can
find universal expressions for values of
the highest Casimir operators in
the defining
\Box
and in

\Lambda_{i}^{(n)}|_{i=1,2,3,4}
representations.

It is not possible
(without introducing an additional discrete parameter
\epsilon
)
to find a single universal formula for the values

c_{(2)}^{(\Box)}
of the second Casimir operator in (
4.34
),
so that this formula will be valid simultaneously
for the classical and exceptional Lie algebras when choosing the
Vogel parameters, as in Table
4
.
Note that formulas in (
4.34
) are related by a permutation of
\hat{\beta}
with
\hat{\gamma}
.
This may indicate that when considering universality beyond of the tensor products
\rm ad^{\otimes k}
, the symmetry under permutations of the Vogel parameters is broken. Apparently, when we select the Vogel parameters
in Table
4
, we need to permute
\hat{\beta}
with
\hat{\gamma}
, either for the Lie algebras of classical series
or for the exceptional Lie algebras. In this case, the value
\hat{\beta}\,(\hat{\gamma})=1/3
for exceptional algebras will coincide with the value
\hat{\beta}\,(\hat{\gamma})=1/3
for the
\mathfrak{sl}_{3}

and
\mathfrak{so}_{8}
algebras, i.e., on the Vogel’s plane
(see
[
4
]
) , not only the point

D_{4}=\mathfrak{so}_{8}
but also the point

A_{2}=\mathfrak{sl}_{3}
will fall on the line
of the exceptional algebras.

It seems that there is a direct generalization
(
q
and
t
deformations) to the case of
simply laced Lie algebras
of all the universal formulas for dimensions
and identities of the type (
3.7
) (following from the
decomposition of tensor products
\Box\otimes Y_{n}
).
In this connection, we should mention recent papers

[
34
,
35
,
36
]

(see also Sect. 6.1 in
[
31
]
for a review
and references therein). Indeed,
considering the example of the
\mathfrak{sl}_{N}
-algebra,
the
q
-deformations of dimensions (
3.5
) are

\begin{array}[]{c}\dim_{q}([2n+1,n^{N-2}])=\dfrac{[N+2n]_{q}\,[N+n-1]_{q}}{[N+2n-1]_{q}\,[n+1]_{q}}\dim_{q}Y_{n}\;,\\[8.5359pt]
\dim_{q}([2n,n+1,n^{N-3}])=\dfrac{[N+n-1]_{q}\,[n]_{q}\,[N-2]_{q}}{[n+1]_{q}\,[N+n-2]_{q}}\dim_{q}Y_{n}\;,\\[8.5359pt]
\dim_{q}([2n-1,(n-1)^{N-2}])=\dfrac{[N+2n-2]_{q}\,[n]_{q}}{[N+n-2]_{q}\,[N+2n-1]_{q}}\dim_{q}Y_{n}\;,\end{array}

(5.1)

where
[A]_{q}=\frac{(q^{A}-q^{-A})}{(q-q^{-1})}

and the
q
-deformation of the first relation in (
3.7
)
is valid in view of the identity

[N]_{q}=\dfrac{[N+2n]_{q}\,[N+n-1]_{q}}{[N+2n-1]_{q}\,[n+1]_{q}}+\dfrac{[N+n-1]_{q}\,[n]_{q}\,[N-2]_{q}}{[n+1]_{q}\,[N+n-2]_{q}}+\dfrac{[N+2n-2]_{q}\,[n]_{q}}{[N+n-2]_{q}\,[N+2n-1]_{q}}\;.

Analogously, the
q
-deformation of the second
relation in (
3.7
)
is valid in view of the remarkable symmetric identity

[N+M]_{q}[M+1]_{q}[N+1]_{q}=[N]_{q}[N+1]_{q}+[N+M+2]_{q}[M]_{q}[N]_{q}+[M]_{q}[M+1]_{q}\;,

where we have to substitute
M\to 2n
and
N\to N-2n
.
Note that there is an arbitrariness in the definition
of the
q
-dimensions of representations, e.g.,
\dim_{q}(\lambda)\to q^{c\cdot|\lambda|}\dim_{q}(\lambda)
, where

c
is arbitrary constant and
|\lambda|
is the number
of cells of the Young diagram
\lambda

(see in this regard formula (4.3.80) in
[
29
]
,
Subsect. 4.3.6).

Sharp large deviation estimates for Gaussian extrema
José M. Zapata

Abstract
We establish sharp large-deviation asymptotic estimates for the maximum order statistic of i.i.d. standard normal random variables on all Borel subsets of the positive real line. This result yields more accurate tail approximations than the classical Gumbel limit. Keywords: Gaussian maxima; extreme value theory; large deviations; Gumbel distribution.



1
Main result

Let
(X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be i.i.d. standard Gaussian random variables, and let

X_{(n)}=\max_{1\leq i\leq n}X_{i}
denote the maximum order statistic. A classical result in extreme value theory asserts that there exist sequences
a_{n}>0
and
b_{n}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

Y_{n}=a_{n}\bigl(X_{(n)}-b_{n}\bigr)\;\xrightarrow{d}\;\Lambda,

where
\Lambda(x)=e^{-e^{x}}
is the standard Gumbel distribution.

The study of appropriate normalizations for maxima dates back to Fisher and Tippett
[
4
]
, who first identified the possible limiting forms of extreme order statistics. Later, Gnedenko
[
5
]
established necessary and sufficient conditions for convergence to the three classical extreme value distributions, with the Gaussian case belonging to the Gumbel domain of attraction. Cramér
[
1
]
subsequently derived explicit asymptotic expressions for the normalizing constants in the Gaussian setting.

Throughout this note, we will consider the standard choice of normalization sequences in the Gaussian case

a_{n}=\sqrt{2\log n},\qquad b_{n}=a_{n}-\frac{\log\!\bigl(\sqrt{2\pi}\,a_{n}\bigr)}{a_{n}}.

While the Gumbel limit accurately captures moderate fluctuations of

X_{(n)}
(for a fixed sample size
n
), it does not describe events lying in the tail of the distribution,
whose probabilities decay much faster than predicted by the Gumbel. Such extreme tail probabilities are of central importance in risk analysis and actuarial sciences, see, e.g.,
[
3
,
6
]
.

In order to provide a more accurate estimates of the such a tail probabilities, in this note we adopt a large deviation theory approach. Large deviation theory concerns the asymptotic behavior of probabilities of rare events and characterizes their decay on an appropriate logarithmic scale, see e.g.
[
2
]
.

We investigate the rescaled normalization

Z_{n}=\frac{a_{n}\bigl(X_{(n)}-b_{n}\bigr)}{\log n}=\frac{X_{(n)}-b_{n}}{a_{n}/2}.

(1)

Our main result establishes a sharp large
deviation estimates of
(Z_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
for all Borel sets on the right tail of the distribution, and reads as follows.

Theorem 1

Let
(Z_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be defined by (
1
). Then, for every Borel set

A\subset[0,\infty)
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{\log n}\,\log\mathbb{P}(Z_{n}\in A)=-\operatorname*{ess\,inf}_{x\in A}\left(x+\frac{x^{2}}{4}\right).

(2)

For a Borel set
A\subset[0,\infty)
and large
n
, the theorem
yields the approximation

\mathbb{P}(Z_{n}\in A)\;\approx\;n^{-I_{A}},\qquad\text{with}\qquad I_{A}=\operatorname*{ess\,inf}_{x\in A}\left(x+\frac{x^{2}}{4}\right).

In Figure
1
, we compare on a logarithmic
scale the true tail probability
\mathbb{P}(Z_{n}>x)
(black curve), the standard Gumbel
approximation (blue curve), and the large-deviation approximation (orange curve). The figure shows that the large-deviation approximation significantly outperforms the standard Gumbel approximation in the tail regime. In addition, for sufficiently large
values of
x
, the true probability becomes so small that numerical evaluation
suffers from underflow and is rounded to zero; as a consequence, the black curve
(representing its logarithm) drops to
-\infty
. In contrast, the
large-deviation approximation continues to provide an accurate description of the tail behavior.

Figure 1:
Comparison of
\mathbb{P}(Z_{n}>x)
on a logarithmic scale: true
probability (black), standard Gumbel approximation (blue), and large-deviation
approximation (orange). For large values of
x
, the true probability becomes
numerically indistinguishable from zero and its logarithm drops to
-\infty
.
The large-deviation approximation accurately captures the extreme tail
behavior, while the Gumbel approximation is only reliable for moderate
deviations.

2
Proof of the main result

Let
(X_{i})_{i\geq 1}
be i.i.d. standard Gaussian random variables and set
X_{(n)}=\max_{1\leq i\leq n}X_{i}
. Let
Z_{n}
denote the normalized maximum defined in (
1
). In this section, we use the following notations

t_{n}(x)=b_{n}+\tfrac{1}{2}a_{n}x,\qquad I(x)=x+\tfrac{x^{2}}{4}.

Let
F_{n}
denote the distribution function and
f_{n}
the density function of
Z_{n}
. It is easy to verify that

F_{n}(x)=\mathbb{P}(Z_{n}\leq x)=\Phi^{n}\bigl(t_{n}(x)\bigr),\qquad f_{n}(x)=\frac{na_{n}}{2}\,\Phi^{\,n-1}\bigl(t_{n}(x)\bigr)\,\phi\bigl(t_{n}(x)\bigr),

(3)

where
\Phi
and
\phi
denote the standard normal distribution and density.

In the following, we prove some preliminary results.

Lemma 2

There exists
t_{0}
such that

0\leq-\log\Phi(t)\leq\frac{2\,\phi(t)}{t},\qquad\mbox{ for all }t>t_{0}.

(4)

Moreover,

1-\Phi(t)^{n}\leq n\,\frac{\phi(t)}{t},\qquad\mbox{ for all }t>0\mbox{ and }n\in\mathbb{N}.

(5)

Proof.
Since
0<\Phi(t)\leq 1
, we trivially have
-\log\Phi(t)\geq 0
.
By the standard upper bound in Mills’ ratio (see e.g.
[
7
, p. 45]
), we have

1-\Phi(t)\leq\frac{\phi(t)}{t},\qquad t>0.

Using the elementary inequality

-\log u\leq 2(1-u)
for
u<1
close to
1
, and that
\Phi(t)\to 1
as
t\to\infty
, we obtain

-\log\Phi(t)=2(1-\Phi(t))\leq\frac{2\,\phi(t)}{t},

for
t
large enough, which proves (
4
).

As for (
5
), let
u\in[0,1]
. Then

1-u^{n}=(1+u+\cdots+u^{n-1})(1-u)\leq n(1-u).

Applying this inequality with
u=\Phi(t)
and using again

1-\Phi(t)\leq\phi(t)/t
yields the result.

Lemma 3

For every
x>0
,

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{\log n}\log\mathbb{P}(Z_{n}>x)\leq-I(x).

Proof.
Fix
x>0
.
Applying (
5
) in Lemma
2
, we obtain

\frac{1}{\log n}\log\mathbb{P}(Z_{n}>x)=\frac{1}{\log n}\log\left(1-\Phi\!\bigl(t_{n}(x)\bigr)^{n}\right)\leq 1+\frac{\log\phi\!\bigl(t_{n}(x)\bigr)}{\log n}-\frac{\log t_{n}(x)}{\log n}.

Since
\log t_{n}(x)/\log n\to 0
as
n\to\infty
and

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log\phi\!\bigl(t_{n}(x)\bigr)}{\log n}=-\left(1+\frac{x}{2}\right)^{2},

it follows that

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{\log n}\log\mathbb{P}(Z_{n}>x)\leq 1-\left(1+\frac{x}{2}\right)^{2}=-x-\frac{x^{2}}{4}=-I(x),

which concludes the proof.

Lemma 4

Let
Z_{n}
be defined as above and let
f_{n}
denote its density.
Then, for any fixed
M>0
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{\log n}\log f_{n}(x)=-I(x),

uniformly on
x\in[0,M]
.

Proof.
Since
a_{n}^{2}=2\log n
, we may write

\frac{1}{\log n}\log f_{n}(x)=\frac{2}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\!\left(\frac{na_{n}}{2}\right)+\frac{2(n-1)}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\Phi\!\bigl(t_{n}(x)\bigr)+\frac{2}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\phi\!\bigl(t_{n}(x)\bigr).

Denote by
T_{i}(n,x)
,
i=1,2,3
, the i-th term in the sum above. We compute the limit of each of these terms and verify that the convergence is uniform in
[0,M]
.

First,

\lim_{n\to\infty}T_{1}(n,x)=1+\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log a_{n}-\log 2}{\log n}=1.

This convergence is obviously uniform since the
term does not depend on
x
.

Second, applying (
4
) in Lemma
2
taking into account that
t_{n}(x)\geq t_{n}(0)=b_{n}\to\infty
, we have

|T_{2}(n,x)|\leq-\frac{2(n-1)}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\Phi(b_{n})\leq\frac{4(n-1)}{a_{n}^{2}}\frac{\phi(b_{n})}{b_{n}}

(6)

for
n
large enough and all
x\geq 0
.
Inspection shows that
n\phi(b_{n})/b_{n}\to 1
as
n\to\infty
. Then, the upper bound in (
6
) converges to
0
as
n\to\infty
. Since it does not depend on
x
the convergence is uniform in
x
.

Third, using that

t_{n}(x)=a_{n}\left(1+\frac{x}{2}\right)-\frac{\log\left(\sqrt{2\pi}a_{n}\right)}{a_{n}},

one gets

\displaystyle T_{3}(n,x)

\displaystyle=-\left(1+\frac{x}{2}\right)^{2}+\frac{2\left(1+\frac{x}{2}\right)\log(\sqrt{2\pi}a_{n})}{a_{n}^{2}}-\frac{\log(2\pi)}{a_{n}^{2}}-\frac{\log^{2}(\sqrt{2\pi}a_{n})}{a_{n}^{4}}.

Then, for
x\in[0,M]
, we have

\left|T_{3}(n,x)+\left(1+\frac{x}{2}\right)^{2}\right|\leq 2\left(1+\frac{M}{2}\right)\frac{\log(\sqrt{2\pi}a_{n})}{a_{n}^{2}}+\frac{\log(2\pi)}{a_{n}^{2}}+\frac{\log^{2}(\sqrt{2\pi}a_{n})}{a_{n}^{4}}.

The right hand side of the above inequality does not depend on
x
and converges to
0
as
n\to\infty
. Hence,

T_{3}(n,x)\longrightarrow-\left(1+\frac{x}{2}\right)^{2},\quad\mbox{ as }n\to\infty

uniformly for
x\in[0,M]
.

Combining the previous limits yields, for every
x\in[0,M]
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{\log n}\log f_{n}(x)=1-\left(1+\frac{x}{2}\right)^{2}=-x-\frac{x^{2}}{4}=-I(x).

The convergence is uniform in the interval
[0,M]
. The proof is complete.

We finally prove Theorem
1
.

Proof.
Fix a Borel set
B\subset[0,\infty)
with positive Lebesgue measure (otherwise the result is trivial). Set
I_{B}:=\essinf_{B}I
.
Given
\varepsilon>0
, the set

B_{\varepsilon}:=\{x\in B\colon I(x)<I_{B}+\varepsilon\}

has positive Lebesgue measure. Take
M>0
large enough so that
[0,M]\cap B_{\varepsilon}
has positive Lebesgue measure.

By the uniform convergence in Lemma
4
, there exists
n_{0}
such that

n^{-I(x)-\varepsilon}\leq f_{n}(x)\leq n^{-I(x)+\varepsilon},\qquad\mbox{ for all }x\in[0,M],\;n\geq n_{0}.

(7)

Next, to establish the existence of the limit in (
2
) and identify its value, we derive matching upper/lower bounds for the corresponding limsup/liminf.

For the limit inferior, we have

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{\log n}\log\mathbb{P}(Z_{n}\in B)

\displaystyle\geq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{\log n}\log\mathbb{P}(Z_{n}\in B_{\varepsilon}\cap[0,M])

\displaystyle=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{\log n}\log\int_{B_{\varepsilon}\cap[0,M]}f_{n}(x)dx

\displaystyle\geq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{\log n}\log\int_{B_{\varepsilon}\cap[0,M]}n^{-I(x)-\varepsilon}dx

\displaystyle\geq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{\log n}\log\int_{B_{\varepsilon}\cap[0,M]}n^{-I_{B}-2\varepsilon}dx

\displaystyle=-I_{B}-2\varepsilon.

As for the limit superior, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(Z_{n}\in B)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}(Z_{n}\in B\cap[0,M])+\mathbb{P}(Z_{n}>M)

\displaystyle\leq 2\max\Big\{\mathbb{P}(Z_{n}\in B\cap[0,M]),\mathbb{P}(Z_{n}>M)\Big\}.

Hence,

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{\log n}\log\mathbb{P}(Z_{n}\in B)

\displaystyle\leq\max\Bigg\{\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{\log n}\log\mathbb{P}(Z_{n}\in B\cap[0,M]),

\displaystyle\hskip 76.82234pt\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{\log n}\log\mathbb{P}(Z_{n}>M)\Bigg\}.

On the one hand, by Lemma
3
, we have

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{\log n}\log\mathbb{P}(Z_{n}>M)\leq-I(M).

On the other hand, we have

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{\log n}\log\mathbb{P}(Z_{n}\in B)

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{\log n}\log\int_{B\cap[0,M]}n^{-I_{B}+\varepsilon}dx

\displaystyle=-I_{B}+\varepsilon.

Putting all together, we have

\displaystyle-I_{B}-2\varepsilon

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{\log n}\log\mathbb{P}(Z_{n}\in B)

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{\log n}\log\mathbb{P}(Z_{n}\in B)

\displaystyle\leq\max\left\{-I_{B}+\varepsilon,-I(M)\right\}.

Letting first
M\uparrow\infty
and then
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
, we obtain (
2
),
which completes the proof.

Remark 5

van der Hofstad and Honnappa
[
8
]
derived sharp large deviation asymptotics for extrema of bivariate Gaussian random vectors. Their main result, however, is fundamentally different from ours (even in the degenerate case of perfect correlation), since the normalization of the maxima differs from those considered here, leading to a different rate function
I(x)
. Moreover, while our result establishes a large deviation principle for all Borel sets of the positive real line, the results in
[
8
]
are restricted to upper unbounded intervals. Finally, the comparison between the resulting large-deviation approximation and the classical Gumbel approximation is not addressed in
[
8
]
.

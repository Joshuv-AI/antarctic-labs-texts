Protocol for using scCURE to construct an immunotherapy outcome prediction model
Authors: Yujun Liu, Xin Zou, Henry HY Tong, Yuan Ji, Lianjiang Tan, Jie Hao
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
SummaryIt is challenging to predict the immunotherapy outcome from the baseline status of patients with cancer. Here, we introduce a protocol for constructing an immunotherapy prediction model utilizing single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq)-based changed and unchanged cell recognition during immunotherapy (scCURE). Initially, we describe the steps for using scCURE to discriminate unchanged cells with similar cellular and molecular functions from whole scRNA-seq data. Subsequently, we demonstrate ways to construct immunotherapy outcome prediction models using either scRNA-seq or bulk RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) data, predicated on the unchanged cells identified by scCURE.For complete details on the use and execution of this protocol, please refer to Zou et al.1Subject areas: Bioinformatics, Cancer, Health Sciences

Before you begin

Overview

scRNA-seq has substantially improved our comprehension of the diverse cellular responses to immunotherapy. Nevertheless, inconsistencies persist in the mechanistic investigations and predictive model construction of immunotherapy, as some studies have reported distinct cell subtypes associated with these two aspects without a clear explanation. It is possible that such inconsistencies may originate from uncharacterized cell-cell heterogeneities. Here, we present a framework for precisely characterizing immunotherapy-related cell heterogeneities using scCURE (scRNA-seq-based Changed and Unchanged cell Recognition during immunotherapy). By comparing the pre- and post-treatment scRNA-seq data using the proposed framework, we can identify the cells that are affected or unaffected by immunotherapy intervention. Then, we demonstrate how to construct an immunotherapy outcome prediction model based on the identified unchanged cells.

scCURE parameter optimization by leave-one-out cross-validation

Timing: ∼13 h

The major parameters of scCURE are the numbers of Gaussian models set for cell clustering. The scRNA-seq dataset should contain samples collected from both pre- and post-treatment samples, and the immunotherapy outcome pathological evaluation result for each sample, i.e., R/NR, should be available. First, scCURE compares the pre-treatment cells from all patients and the post-treatment cells from responders only. The identified unchanged cells from pre-treatment samples are termed as R_like cells. Similarly, NR_like cells are identified by comparing pre-treatment samples and post-treatment cells from non-responders. By doing so, each patient may simultaneously contain R_like and NR_like cells. Subsequently, the ratio of R-like/NR-like is calculated for each patient and used as a prediction score. Leave-one-out cross validation is adopted to calculate the prediction score for each patient, i.e., select one patient as testing sample (Ptest) and the rest as training samples. Finally, the AUC (Area Under The Curve) ROC (Receiver Operating Characteristics) curve is calculated to evaluate the prediction capability of the obtained prediction scores. The number of Gaussian mixture models setting up is designed to maximize the AUC ROC. This analysis step is performed using the MATLAB 2021b software.

5.Fix numbers of Gaussian Mixture models (between 2 and 6) for pre- and post-treatment data, respectively.a.Calculate R_like parameter by comparing pre- and post-treatment responders using scCURE.i.Preprocess the scRNA-seq data from all pre-treatment samples and post-treatment data from responders.ii.Remove the test-sample (Ptest) from post-treatment responder data (if present).).iii.Use scCURE to identify the unchanged cells based on the pre-treatment samples and test-sample-excluded post-treatment responder single cell data and identify R_like cells. Expected outcomes

scCURE elucidates the heterogeneities in cells related to immunotherapy by identifying the changed and unchanged cells. This aids in the construction of a robust prediction model for ICB treatment. For the prediction model, scCURE can isolate the most predictive informative cells from scRNA-seq datasets. By calculating the ratio of R-like/NR-like cells or the average expression of R-like /NR-like cluster markers, we can predict the ICB therapy and prognosis outcomes. As shown in Figure 3, the ratio of R-like/NR-like cells in responders is significantly higher than that of non-responders. Figure 5 reveals that responders had higher CD8-C2-SELL signature scores than non-responders and the signature is associated with longer overall survival (OS). Additionally, we further select phenotype-related genes to construct prediction model for evaluating ICB therapy on bulk RNA-seq datasets. To improve the robustness of predictive model, we ensure that prediction model consisting of phenotype-related genes not only have better predictive performance but are also associated with responders. We therefore merge the phenotype-related module genes from CD8-C2-SELL marker genes by WGCNA analysis. Figure 6 displays that the predictive model has better predictive capacity in four melanoma ICB datasets.

(B) Survival analysis using the top 30 markers of CD8-C2-SELL on TCGA-SKCM data.

Limitations

scCURE depends on the availability of suitable scRNA-seq datasets, including pre- and post-treatment samples and response information, to distinguish between R-like and NR-like cells. Currently, it is necessary to investigate the prediction model and molecular mechanism within the dimension of bulk datasets. Therefore, we propose an approach that not only involves calculating the ratio of R-like to NR-like cells but also integrates the use of bulk RNA-seq datasets to construct a more applicable prediction model. To establish more reliable prediction models, there is an urgent requirement for larger immunotherapy cohorts and an expanded range of immunotherapy datasets.

Troubleshooting

Problem 1

scCURE can identify some cells with dual characteristics that belong to R-like and NR-like cells (related to steps 9).

Potential solution

•The objective of scCURE is to identify changed and unchanged cells, aiding in the construction of a robust prediction model for ICB treatment. Theoretically, unchanged cells are composed of R-like and NR-like cells. However, some unchanged cells exhibit characteristics of both R-like and NR-like cells. Considering this, we propose to remove these dual-characteristic cells and consolidate R-like and NR-like cells. This approach could potentially enhance the accuracy and reliability of the prediction model.

Problem 2

Some samples do not contain both R-like and NR-like cells (related to steps 10).

Potential solution

•While unchanged cells are composed of both R-like and NR-like cells, some pre-treatment samples derived from responders and non-responders contain only one type of cells. When calculating the ratio of R-like/NR-like cells, we suggest removing those samples that contain only one of R-like and NR-like cells. After plotting an R-like/NR-like ratio bar chart, we can estimate which samples are likely to be responders or non-responders. The removed samples are evaluated by the number of R-like or NR-like cells. For example, if the sample does not have NR-like cells, it can be predicted as responders, and vice versa.

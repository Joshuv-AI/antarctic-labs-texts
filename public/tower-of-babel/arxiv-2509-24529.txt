Birth of a gap: Critical phenomena in 2D Coulomb gas
Matthias Allard, Sampad Lahiry

Abstract
We investigate a family of radially symmetric Coulomb gas systems at inverse temperature β = 2 \beta=2 . The family is characterised by the property that the density of the equilibrium measure vanishes on a ring at radius r ∗ r_{*} , which lies strictly inside the droplet. The large n n expansion of the logarithm of the partition function is obtained up to a novel n 1 / 4 n^{1/4} term. We perform a double scaling limit of the correlation kernel at the n 1 / 4 n^{1/4} scale and obtain a new limiting kernel in the bulk, which differs from the well-known Ginibre kernel.

Introduction

The study of two dimensional Coulomb gas is the study of a point process in the complex plane for which the probability measure is given by

d\mu_{n}^{(\beta)}(z)=\frac{1}{Z_{n}^{(\beta)}}\prod_{j>k=1}^{n}|z_{j}-z_{k}|^{\beta}\prod_{j=1}^{n}e^{-\frac{\beta n}{2}Q(z_{j})}dA(z_{j}),

(1.1)

where
dA
is the flat Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{C}
, and
\beta>0
is a parameter known in the physics literature as the inverse temperature.
This models
n
particles in interaction in a 2D plane exhibiting some logarithmic pairwise repulsion. This is best seen when rewriting the measure in the following form

\displaystyle d\mu_{n}^{(\beta)}(z)=\frac{1}{Z_{n}^{(\beta)}}e^{-\frac{\beta}{2}H_{n}(z)}\prod_{j=1}^{n}dA(z_{j}),\quad H_{n}(z):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j,k=1\\
j\neq k\end{subarray}}^{n}\log\frac{1}{|z_{j}-z_{k}|}+n\sum_{j=1}^{n}Q(z_{j}).

(1.2)

The particles are plunged in an external potential, given by the function

\displaystyle Q:\mathbb{C}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}.

(1.3)

To make the model non-trivial, the potential
Q
is generally assumed to have sufficient growth at infinity to counterbalance the repulsion of the particles and confine a macroscopic number—proportional to
n
—of them in a bounded region of the complex plane.

The point like particles
z=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})
can be interpreted as charged particles at any given inverse temperature
\beta>0
. For the particular value of
\beta=2
, they can also be seen as the eigenvalues of a random normal matrix
M
whose distribution is given by

\frac{1}{Z_{n}^{(\beta=2)}}e^{-n\mathrm{Tr}Q(M)}dM,

(1.4)

with
dM
the induced measure on the set of normal matrices.

A wide variety of variations arise depending not only on the choice of the potential or family of potentials, but also on the value of
\beta
, e.g.
BKS23
;
LS18
;
JV23
;
GKL24
;
ACC23
;
DGZ14
;

;
C23
;
B25
;
AFLS25
;
CFTW15
;

.
To make the above idea precise, one finds that as
n\rightarrow\infty
the points distributed according to (
1.1
) accumulate in a 2-dimensional domain known as the droplet
\mathbb{S}
according to its equilibrium measure
\mu_{Q}
. The measure
\mu_{Q}
is characterised by the weighted logarithmic energy problem
ST97
. In particular, for a probability measure, define the weighted logarithmic energy by

I_{Q}[\mu]:=\int_{\mathbb{C}^{2}}\log\frac{1}{|z-w|}d\mu(z)d\mu(w)+\int_{\mathbb{C}}Q(w)d\mu(w).

(1.5)

Then
\mu_{Q}
minimises (
1.5
) over all probability measures supported on the complex plane. From Frostman’s Theorem
ST97
, it is well known that

d\mu_{Q}=\frac{1}{4\pi}\Delta Q.\mathbf{1}_{\mathbb{S}}\,dA,

(1.6)

where
\Delta:=4\partial\overline{\partial}
is the usual Laplacian and
\mathbf{1}_{\mathbb{S}}
is the indicator function of the set
\mathbb{S}
.

In this article, we take
\beta=2
. As a consequence, the point process given in (
1.1
) is determinantal with a kernel
K_{n}
(see (
2.8
) for a precise definition). Further, we consider a model where the potential is radially symmetric and is such that a spectral gap is about to appear in the bulk around a circle of radius
r_{*}>0
and thus the number of components of the droplet increases. A more precise description of the model is given in
subsection 2.1
. In this setting, we derive a large
n
expansion of the partition function
Z_{n}^{(\beta=2)}
. This is of particular interest, as the asymptotic expansion of the partition function captures important information about the system, such as the logarithmic energy, the entropy, some geometric properties and more.
In
ZW06
, Zabrodin and Wiegmann predicted that, for sufficiently regular potentials—namely, those with smooth boundaries and no singularities or critical behavior—the partition function
Z_{n}^{(\beta)}
admits an asymptotic expansion of the following form, as
n\rightarrow\infty
,

\log Z_{n}^{(\beta)}=\mathcal{C}_{0}n^{2}+\mathcal{C}_{1}n\log(n)+\mathcal{C}_{2}n+\mathcal{C}_{3}\log(n)+\mathcal{C}_{4}+O(n^{-1}).

(1.7)

and proposed explicit formulas for the constant
\mathcal{C}_{j}(\beta,Q)
,
(j=0,\dots 4)
. Recently, it was shown in
HM12
;
DGZ14
and
LS18
that, as
n\rightarrow\infty
,

\log Z_{n}^{(\beta)}=-\frac{\beta}{2}n^{2}I_{Q}[\mu_{Q}]+\frac{\beta}{4}n\log(n)-n\left[\mathcal{C}(\beta)+\left(1-\frac{\beta}{4}\right)E_{Q}[\mu_{Q}]\right]+o(n).

(1.8)

where
\mathcal{C}(\beta)
is independent of
Q
and

E_{Q}[\mu_{Q}]:=\int_{\mathbb{C}}\log(\frac{\Delta Q}{4})d\mu_{Q},

(1.9)

is the entropy associated with
\mu_{Q}
. The quantities
\mu_{Q}
and
I_{Q}[\mu_{Q}]
are given in (
1.6
) and (
1.9
) respectively. In the determinantal setting this conjecture has been partially verified in the works of

;

;

On the real line, the vanishing of the equilibrium measure in the interior and multiple cuts have been investigated. In particular, we refer to

In two dimensions, the statistical properties near the bulk and the outer boundary of the droplet are well studied; see, for example,

For soft edges, the authors in

In contrast to

n^{1/4}
(see

n^{1/4}
is inversely proportional to the mean level spacing of the particles in the vicinity of the circle of radius
r_{*}

If one zooms precisely at this scale around
r_{*}

K_{n}
(
2.8
) which differs from the Ginibre kernel governing correlations in the bulk; we refer to
AKM18

; see

.

2
Main results

2.1
Presentation of the model

We consider here the distribution (
1.1
) with a smooth and radially symmetric potential
Q
. We denote

\displaystyle Q(z):=q(\absolutevalue{z}).

(2.1)

To enforce the critical situation of near emergence of a gap, we ask the Laplacian of
Q
to vanish on the circle
\mathbb{T}_{r_{*}}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}\mid\absolutevalue{z}=r_{*}\}
and to be positive everywhere else, i.e.

\displaystyle\Delta Q(r_{*})=0,\quad\Delta Q(r)>0,\quad r\neq r_{*}>0.

(2.2)

As
Q
is smooth, this ensures that
r_{*}
is the global minimum of
r\mapsto\Delta Q(r)
. The radius
r_{*}
is thus a double zero of the function
r\mapsto\Delta Q(r)
and we impose that it is, however, not a triple zero, i.e.

\partial_{r}\Delta Q(r_{*})=0,\qquad\partial^{2}_{r}\Delta Q(r_{*})>0.

(2.3)

This latter condition is crucial as it determines the criticality of the formation of gap phenomenon; see
\lx@sectionsign
above
subsection 2.2
. Additionally, we assume the potential
Q
to have sufficient growth at infinity,

\displaystyle\liminf_{\absolutevalue{z}\to\infty}\frac{Q(z)}{2\log(\abs{z})}>1,

(2.4)

which guarantees
\mu_{Q}
to be compactly supported. The weak subharmonicity condition (
2.2
) ensures that the support of the equilibrium measure
\mu_{Q}
is

\displaystyle\mathbb{S}:=\mathrm{supp}(\mu_{Q})=\mathbb{A}(r_{0},r_{1})\setminus\mathbb{T}_{r_{*}},\quad 0\leqslant r_{0}<r_{*}<r_{1},

(2.5)

where
\mathbb{A}(r_{0},r_{1}):=\{z\in\mathbb{C}\mid r_{0}\leqslant\absolutevalue{z}\leqslant r_{1}\}
.
Figure 1
illustrates the configuration and a simple example of potential verifying all the previous conditions is presented in
subsection 2.3
.

We will further restrict ourselves to the case
\beta=2
. Thus, the density of the point process can be rewritten in the determinantal form

\displaystyle\frac{1}{Z_{n}^{(\beta=2)}}\prod_{j>k=1}^{n}|z_{j}-z_{k}|^{2}\prod_{j=1}^{n}e^{-n\,Q(z_{j})}=\frac{1}{n!}\det\left[K_{n}(z_{j},z_{k})\right]_{j,k=1}^{n},

(2.6)

where the kernel
K_{n}
can be explicitly expressed in terms of the weighted monic orthogonal polynomials

\displaystyle p_{j,n}(z):=z^{j}e^{-\frac{n}{2}Q(z)},\quad j\in\llbracket 0,n-1\rrbracket,

(2.7)

in the following way

K_{n}(z_{1},z_{2})=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\frac{p_{j,n}(z_{1})\overline{p_{j,n}(z_{2})}}{{\norm{p_{j,n}}^{2}}},

(2.8)

with the norm of the polynomials given by

\norm{p_{j,n}}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{C}}\absolutevalue{z}^{2j}e^{-nQ(\absolutevalue{z})}\frac{dA(z)}{\pi}=2\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{2j+1}e^{-nq(r)}dr.

(2.9)

The partition function
Z_{n}^{(\beta=2)}
then reads

Z_{n}:=Z_{n}^{(\beta=2)}=\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}\prod_{j>k=1}^{n}|z_{j}-z_{k}|^{2}\prod_{j=1}^{n}e^{-nQ(z_{j})}dA(z_{j}).

(2.10)

A standard calculation, as shown in
(
F10
, Exercises 15.3, q.1(ii))
, shows that the corresponding
n
-dimensional integral
Z_{n}
factorizes into a product of one-dimensional integrals, which, after taking the logarithm, yields

\displaystyle\log(\frac{Z_n}{(2\pi)^n })=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\log(u_{j}),\quad u_{j}:=\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{2j+1}e^{-nq(r)}dr.

(2.11)

Before stating our main results, we introduce the following constants

\displaystyle\gamma_{Q}

\displaystyle:=\partial^{2}_{r}\Delta Q(r_{*})=q^{(4)}(r_{*})+\frac{6q^{\prime}(r_{*})}{r_{*}^{3}},

\displaystyle\qquad\varkappa_{Q}

\displaystyle:=\frac{r_{*}}{2}\left(\partial_{r}^{2}\Delta Q(r_{*})\right)^{1/4}

(2.12)

as well as the following Pearcey-like integral

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}(\theta):=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-\theta P(y)}dy,\qquad P(y):=\frac{y^{2}}{4}+\frac{y^{3}}{3!}+\frac{y^{4}}{4!},

(2.13)

which appears both in the large
n
expansion of the partition function (cf.
Theorem 2.1
) and in the expression of the local correlation kernel (cf.
Theorem 2.2
and
subsection 2.2
).

Statement of results

The following theorem presents our first main result: a large

Theorem 2.1
.

Let the potential

(2.14)

\displaystyle+n^{1/4}\frac{\varkappa_{Q}}{2}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}x^{2}\log(\frac{x^2}{\sqrt{4\pi}} \mathcal{P}(x^4))dx+o(n^{1/4}),

where

It is interesting to notice that the novel term of order

Corollary 2.4

For the determinantal point process with kernel

\displaystyle\partial_{r}^{j}\Delta Q(r_{*})=0,\quad j=0,\ldots,2m-1,\qquad\partial^{2m}_{r}\Delta Q(r_{*})>0,

\displaystyle\gamma_{Q,m}

\displaystyle:=\partial_{r}^{2m}\Delta Q(r_{*}),

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{m}(\theta):=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-\theta P_{m}(y)}dy,\qquad P_{m}(y):=\sum_{k=2}^{2m+2}\frac{y^{k}}{(2m+2-k)!\,k!}.

\displaystyle K_{*}^{(m)}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2};\vartheta):=\frac{1}{(2m)!}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{i\vartheta\frac{x^{2m+1}}{(2m+1)!}}\frac{\exp\left(-\frac{x^{2m+2}}{2}\left[P_{m}\left(\frac{\xi_{1}}{x}-1\right)+P_{m}\left(\frac{\xi_{2}}{x}-1\right)\right]\right)}{\mathcal{P}_{m}(x^{2m+2})}\absolutevalue{x}^{2m-1}dx.

2.3
Example of potential

We present here a particular choice of potential verifying the conditions presented in

and for which our main results, consequently, apply. A generalisation of the following example is given in (

).

Denoting
\absolutevalue{z}=\sqrt{r}
, for

, the easiest example of potential for which one can observe the emergence of a gap in the droplet, not located at the origin, is

\displaystyle Q(z)=Q_{t}(r):=\frac{r^{3}}{3}-t\frac{r^{2}}{2}+cr,\quad r,t,c>0.

(2.30)

Note that we introduced here a parameter
t
controlling the formation of the gap. The parameter

does not actually play a role as one can push its dependence in the two parameters
t
and

via a simple rescaling
x\mapsto x/c
. Thus, without loss of generality we take

.

Depending on the value of

, the support of the equilibrium measure is either a disk, or a disk encircled by a disjoint annulus (cf.
Figure 4
). There exists a critical value of

for which the annulus starts to separate from the disk. Indeed, the equation

\Delta Q_{t}(r)=Q_{t}^{\prime\prime}(r)+\frac{1}{r}Q_{t}^{\prime}(r)=0,

(2.31)

admits the following solutions

\displaystyle\begin{cases}&r_{*}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}},\quad t=t_{\rm min}:=\sqrt{3}\\
&r_{\pm}:=\frac{t}{3}\pm\sqrt{\frac{1}{3}\left(\frac{t^{2}}{3}-1\right)},\quad t>t_{\rm min}.\end{cases}

(2.32)

Figure 4
:
(Left)

R_{3}
with the exclusion of a circle of radius
r_{*}
.
(Right)

t>t_{\rm min}
: The droplet (blue) is composed of a disk of radius
\mathbb{D}(R_{3})=\{0\leqslant\absolutevalue{z}\leqslant R_{3}\}
and an annulus
\mathbb{A}(R_{2},R_{3})=\{R_{2}\leqslant\absolutevalue{z}\leqslant R_{3}\}
. The Laplacian of the potential,

, is strictly negative inside the annulus
\mathbb{A}(r_{-},r_{+})=\{r_{-}\leqslant\absolutevalue{z}\leqslant r_{+}\}
(orange) and vanishes on the boundaries.

The case
t=t_{\rm min}
corresponds to the setting described in
subsection 2.1
as
r_{*}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}
is then a double zero of
r\mapsto\Delta Q_{t}(r)
. For
t<t_{\rm min}
, the potential
Q_{t}
is strictly subharmonic, and the droplet is a disk. In contrast, for

,

is superharmonic in the annulus whose radii are given by

in (

illustrates the configurations

The study of the emergence of a gap by varying the parameter

then varies with

3
Preliminaries and notations

To set the stage of the Laplace method used to study the asymptotic expansion of the norms (
2.11
), one can rewrite

\frac{\norm{p_{j,n}}^{2}}{2}=u_{j}=\int_{0}^{\infty}re^{-nV_{\tau}(r)}dr

(3.1)

where we define the phase function
V_{\tau}

V_{\tau}(r):=q(r)-2\tau\log(r),\qquad\tau=\frac{j}{n}.

(3.2)

The potential
Q
being subharmonic implies that the function
r\mapsto rq^{\prime}(r)
,
for
0\leqslant\tau\leqslant 1
,
is increasing inside the droplet, i.e. for
r\in[r_{0},r_{1}]
; see
Figure 1
. Therefore,
V_{\tau}
has a unique minimum
r_{\tau}
and the equation
V_{\tau}^{\prime}(r_{\tau})=0
can be rewritten

r_{\tau}q^{\prime}(r_{\tau})=2\tau.

(3.3)

In particular, there exists
\tau_{*}
such that

r_{*}q^{\prime}(r_{*})=2\tau_{*}.

(3.4)

This
\tau_{*}
is special, as the assumption (
2.3
) implies that the first three derivatives of
V_{\tau_{*}}
vanish at
r_{*}
, i.e.

V_{\tau_{*}}^{\prime}(r_{*})=V_{\tau_{*}}^{\prime\prime}(r_{*})=V_{\tau_{*}}^{\prime\prime\prime}(r_{*})=0,\qquad V_{\tau_{*}}^{(4)}(r_{*})>0.

(3.5)

A crucial point of the analysis is to control the difference
r_{\tau}-r_{*}
. To know how
r_{\tau}
varies with
\tau
, one can differentiate the critical point equation (
3.3
) to get the following differential equation

\frac{dr_{\tau}}{d\tau}=\frac{2}{r_{\tau}\Delta Q(r_{\tau})}.

(3.6)

Moreover, by implicit function theorem, one finds

r_{\tau}-r_{*}=\left(\frac{12}{r_{*}\partial^{2}_{r}\Delta Q(r_{*})}\right)^{1/3}\,(\tau-\tau_{*})^{1/3}+o\big((\tau-\tau_{*})^{1/3}\big).

(3.7)

The difference
r_{\tau}-r_{*}
being an implicit function of the linear parameter
\tau
, the key idea is to reverse the problem and consider instead the difference
\tau-\tau_{*}
as an implicit function of the linearly parametrized difference
r_{\tau}-r_{*}=\delta_{r}
, where
\delta_{r}
is the new parameter of the problem. This corresponds to the following change of coordinates

\displaystyle(\tau,r_{\tau})=(\tau_{*},r_{*})+(p(\delta_{r}),\delta_{r})

(3.8)

where the function
p
is implicitly defined by the relation

p(r_{\tau}-r_{*})=\tau-\tau_{*},

(3.9)

and can be explicitly defined in terms of
q^{\prime}
using (
3.3
) and (
3.4
),

p(x):=\frac{1}{2}\big[(x+r_{*})q^{\prime}(x+r_{*})-r_{*}q^{\prime}(r_{*})\big].

(3.10)

The differentiability of
p
then follows from the differentiability of the potential
q
.

In the forthcoming analysis, we will make use of the following derivatives of
V_{\tau}
, which we conveniently express in terms of
\Delta Q
,

\displaystyle V_{\tau}^{\prime\prime}(r)

\displaystyle=\Delta Q(r)-\frac{1}{r}V_{\tau}^{\prime}(r),

(3.11)

\displaystyle V_{\tau}^{\prime\prime\prime}(r)

\displaystyle=\partial_{r}\Delta Q(r)-\frac{1}{r}\Delta Q(r)+\frac{2}{r^{2}}V_{\tau}^{\prime}(r),

\displaystyle V_{\tau}^{(4)}(r)

\displaystyle=\partial_{r}^{2}\Delta Q(r)-\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r}\Delta Q(r)+\frac{3}{r^{2}}\Delta Q(r)-\frac{6}{r^{4}}V_{\tau}^{\prime}(r).

In a first step, in the large
n
asymptotic of the norms
\norm{p_{j,n}}
, the above derivatives will be evaluated at
r_{\tau}
and thus the
\tau
dependence on the RHS of (
3.11
) will only appear through
r_{\tau}
, as
V_{\tau}^{\prime}(r_{\tau})=0
by definition.
In a second step, as we are ultimately interested in the asymptotic of the sum of the norms
\norm{p_{j,n}}
, one needs to determine which indices
j
will yield the main contribution (
\tau=j/n
). We will see that the main contribution comes from
\tau=\tau_{*}
and equivalently from
r_{\tau}=r_{*}
—hence the aforementioned change of coordinates.

In this perspective, we give a double expansion of the derivatives of
V_{\tau}
, depending on how far the argument is from
r_{*}
and how far
\tau
is from
\tau_{*}
. This will be crucial in our analysis.

Lemma 3.1
.

Denoting,
\delta_{r}=r_{\tau}-r_{*}
, the derivatives of
V_{\tau}
admit the following expansions, as
\delta_{r}\to 0
and
\absolutevalue{r-r_*}\to 0
,

\displaystyle V_{\tau}^{(2)}(r)

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma_{Q}}{2}(r-r_{*})^{2}+O\left(\absolutevalue{\delta_r}^{3}\,\absolutevalue{r-r_*}^{2}+\absolutevalue{r-r_*}^{3}\right),

(3.12)

\displaystyle V_{\tau}^{(3)}(r)

\displaystyle=\gamma_{Q}\,(r-r_{*})+O\left(\absolutevalue{\delta_r}^{3}\,\absolutevalue{r-r_*}+\absolutevalue{r-r_*}^{2}\right),

\displaystyle V_{\tau}^{(4)}(r)

\displaystyle=\gamma_{Q}+O\left(\absolutevalue{\delta_r}^{3}+\absolutevalue{r-r_*}\right),

\displaystyle V_{\tau}^{(5)}(r)

\displaystyle=O(1),

with
\gamma_{Q}=\partial^{2}_{r}\Delta Q(r_{*})
.

Proof.

The first step is to proceed with a Taylor expansion around
r=r_{*}
of the RHS of (
3.11
) where remind that, by assumption,

\displaystyle\Delta Q(r_{*})=q^{\prime\prime}(r_{*})+\frac{q^{\prime}(r_{*})}{r_{*}}=0,\quad\partial_{r}\Delta Q(r_{*})=q^{\prime\prime\prime}(r_{*})+\frac{q^{\prime\prime}(r_{*})}{r}-\frac{q^{\prime}(r_{*})}{r^{2}}=0

(3.13)

and the first non-vanishing derivative is given by

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \gamma_{Q}=\partial^{2}_{r}\Delta Q(r_{*})&=q^{(4)}(r_{*})+\frac{q^{(3)}(r_{*})}{r_{*}}-\frac{2q^{\prime\prime}(r_{*})}{r_{*}^{2}}+\frac{2q^{\prime}(r_{*})}{r_{*}^{3}}>0,\end{aligned}

(3.14)

which is equal to (
2.12
), using (
3.13
).
The expansion obtained is a linear function of
\tau
as all the derivatives of
V_{\tau}
are linear functions of
\tau
. One can then simply rewrite
\tau=\tau_{*}+(\tau-\tau_{*})
and find, using (
3.7
), that each term of the Taylor expansion in
r
, admits an expansion in
\tau
around
\tau_{*}
for which the first correction term is cubic in
\delta_{r}=r_{\tau}-r_{*}
. This yields the claim.

∎

Notation 3.2
.

To avoid excessive use of unspecified constants, we write “
A_{n}\lesssim B_{n}
” to
denote that
A_{n}\leqslant CB_{n}
for all large enough
n
, where
C
is some constant independent of
n
.

4
Asymptotic expansion of the norms
\norm{p_{j,n}}

In this section, we study the large
n
asymptotics of the norms
\norm{p_{j,n}}^{2}=2u_{j}
(
2.9
) which appear both in the kernel (
2.8
) and in the expression of the partition function (
2.11
).

While we treat
\tau
as a continuous parameter, we remind that
\tau=j/n
and thus we define

\displaystyle j_{*}:=n\tau_{*},

(4.1)

which is not necessarily an integer. As we will consider two different regimes, depending on the size of the difference
\tau-\tau_{*}
and ultimately of the difference
j-j_{*}
, we introduce the following scalings

[-\ell_{n}^{-},\ell_{n}^{+}]:=n\,p\!\left(\left[-\frac{\log(n)}{n^{1/4}},\frac{\log(n)}{n^{1/4}}\right]\right),

(4.2)

defined implicitly by the map
p
given in (

).

The first regime is for
j
close to
j_{*}
(
\tau
close to
\tau_{*}
) and the corresponding asymptotic of the norms
u_{j}
is given in the following proposition.

Proposition 4.1
.

For
j\in[j_{*}-\ell_{n}^{-},j_{*}+\ell_{n}^{+}]
, the norms
u_{j}
(
2.11
) admit the following asymptotic expansion, as
n\to\infty
,

\displaystyle u_{j}

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{-nV_{\tau}(r_{\tau})}\,r_{\tau}}{n^{1/4}}\left(f_{1}(x)+O\!\left(\frac{1}{n^{1/4}}\right)\right)

(4.3)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{nV_{\tau}^{\prime\prime}(r_{\tau})}}\,r_{\tau}\,e^{-nV_{\tau}(r_{\tau})}\left(\sqrt{\frac{\gamma_{Q}}{2\pi}}\,|x|\,f_{1}(x)+O\!\left(\frac{1}{n^{1/4}}\right)\right),

where
x=n^{1/4}(r_{\tau}-r_{*})
and

f_{1}(x):=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\exp\!\left[-\gamma_{Q}\left(x^{2}\frac{y^{2}}{4}\,+x\frac{y^{3}}{3!}\,+\frac{y^{4}}{4!}\,\right)\right]\,dy.

(4.4)

with
\gamma_{Q}=\partial^{2}_{r}\Delta Q(r_{*})
.

Proof.

Let us proceed with Laplace’s method on the integral

\displaystyle u_{j}=\int_{0}^{\infty}re^{-nV_{\tau}(r)}dr.

(4.5)

Due to the large
n
parameter, the main contribution of the above integral will come from a small interval
[r_{\tau}-\beta_{n},r_{\tau}+\beta_{n}]
centered around the minimum of the phase function
V_{\tau}
, which is
r_{\tau}
. The cut-off scaling
\beta_{n}
will be shortly determined. On this interval, a good approximation of
V_{\tau}
is given by a truncated Taylor expansion. As pointed out in
section 3
, the phase function
V_{\tau}
vanishes up to third order for
(\tau,r_{\tau})=(\tau_{*},r_{*})
. Thus, one needs to Taylor expand
V_{\tau}
around
r=r_{\tau}
up to fifth order, to control the error.

Let us start by decomposing the integral
u_{j}
as follows

u_{j}=I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3},

(4.6)

where

I_{1}=\int_{0}^{r_{\tau}-\beta_{n}}re^{-nV_{\tau}(r)}\,dr,\quad I_{2}=\int_{r_{\tau}-\beta_{n}}^{r_{\tau}+\beta_{n}}re^{-nV_{\tau}(r)}\,dr,\quad I_{3}=\int_{r_{\tau}+\beta_{n}}^{\infty}re^{-nV_{\tau}(r)}\,dr.

(4.7)

We will show that the tail contributions
I_{1}
and
I_{3}
are negligible compared to
I_{2}
.

Regarding the cut-off scaling
\beta_{n}
, it should be slightly larger than the scales of
r-r_{\tau}
that keeps the difference
n(V_{\tau}(r)-V_{\tau}(r_{\tau}))
exactly of order
1
, independently of
n
and
\tau
(in the range considered). More precisely, if one proceeds with the change of variables
r\mapsto r_{\tau}+r/c_{n}
, an appropriate interpolating scale

is given by

c_{n}:=n^{1/2}\absolutevalue{r_\tau-r_*}+n^{1/4},

(4.8)

as can be seen by Taylor expanding
V_{\tau}
around
r_{\tau}
. From there, one finds that a suitable cut-off scale
\beta_{n}
is

\beta_{n}:=\frac{\log(n)}{c_{n}}.

(4.9)

Step 1: Control of the tails.
Let us first deal with the contributions coming from
I_{1}
and
I_{3}
. Since
V_{\tau}
has a unique critical point at
r_{\tau}
, we first estimate the tail contribution for
r\geqslant r_{\tau}+\beta_{n}
and we have

V_{\tau}(r)-V_{\tau}(r_{\tau})\ \geqslant\ V_{\tau}(r_{\tau}+\beta_{n})-V_{\tau}(r_{\tau})\ \geqslant\ C_{1}\beta_{n}^{2}+C_{2}\beta_{n}^{4},

(4.10)

for some positive constants
C_{1},C_{2}
independent of
n
.
Using the definition of
\beta_{n}
from (
4.9
), it follows that

I_{1}\lesssim e^{-nV_{\tau}(r_{\tau})}\,e^{-n\log(n)^{2}},

(4.11)

which is exponentially small in
n
. The notation
\lesssim
is explained in
section 3
. A similar argument for
r\leqslant r_{\tau}-\beta_{n}
yields

I_{3}\lesssim e^{-nV_{\tau}(r_{\tau})}\,e^{-n\log(n)^{2}}.

(4.12)

Thus both
I_{1}
and
I_{3}
are negligible in comparison with
I_{2}
.

Step 2: Local expansion near
r_{\tau}
.

We now focus on
I_{2}
. For
|r-r_{\tau}|\leqslant\beta_{n}
, the Taylor expansion of
V_{\tau}
about
r_{\tau}
up to fifth order gives

\displaystyle V_{\tau}(r)-V_{\tau}(r_{\tau})=\frac{V_{\tau}^{(2)}(r_{\tau})}{2}(r-r_{\tau})^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{V_{\tau}^{(3)}(r_{\tau})}{6}(r-r_{\tau})^{3}+\frac{V_{\tau}^{(4)}(r_{\tau})}{24}(r-r_{\tau})^{4}

(4.13)

\displaystyle+\frac{V_{\tau}^{(5)}(r_{\tau})}{5!}(r-r_{\tau})^{5}+O(\beta_{n}^{6}).

Substituting into
I_{2}
, we obtain

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=e^{-nV_{\tau}(r_{\tau})}\int_{r_{\tau}-\beta_{n}}^{r_{\tau}+\beta_{n}}r\,\exp\!\left[-n\left(\frac{V_{\tau}^{(2)}(r_{\tau})}{2}(r-r_{\tau})^{2}+\frac{V_{\tau}^{(3)}(r_{\tau})}{6}(r-r_{\tau})^{3}\right.\right.

(4.14)

\displaystyle\left.\left.+\frac{V_{\tau}^{(4)}(r_{\tau})}{24}(r-r_{\tau})^{4}+\frac{V_{\tau}^{(5)}(r_{\tau})}{5!}(r-r_{\tau})^{5}+O(\beta_{n}^{6})\right)\right]dr.

Proceeding with the change of variable
r=r_{\tau}+\frac{y}{c_{n}}
, previously discussed, yields

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{-nV_{\tau}(r_{\tau})}}{c_{n}}\int_{-\log(n)}^{\log(n)}\left(r_{\tau}+\frac{y}{c_{n}}\right)\exp\!\left[-n\left(\frac{V_{\tau}^{(2)}(r_{\tau})}{2}\frac{y^{2}}{c_{n}^{2}}+\frac{V_{\tau}^{(3)}(r_{\tau})}{6}\frac{y^{3}}{c_{n}^{3}}\right.\right.

(4.15)

\displaystyle\left.\left.+\frac{V_{\tau}^{(4)}(r_{\tau})}{24}\frac{y^{4}}{c_{n}^{4}}+\frac{V_{\tau}^{(5)}(r_{\tau})}{5!}\frac{y^{5}}{c_{n}^{5}}+O\!\left(\frac{y^{6}}{c_{n}^{6}}\right)\right)\right]dy.

Finally, using the expansion (
3.12
) and rescaling the difference
r_{\tau}-r_{*}
as follows

\delta_{r}=r_{\tau}-r_{*}=\frac{x}{n^{1/4}},

(4.16)

we find

\displaystyle n\left(\frac{V_{\tau}^{(2)}(r_{\tau})}{2}\frac{y^{2}}{c_{n}^{2}}+\frac{V_{\tau}^{(3)}(r_{\tau})}{3!}\frac{y^{3}}{c_{n}^{3}}+\frac{V_{\tau}^{(4)}(r_{\tau})}{4!}\frac{y^{4}}{c_{n}^{4}}+\frac{V_{\tau}^{(5)}(r_{\tau})}{5!}\frac{y^{5}}{c_{n}^{5}}\right)

(4.17)

\displaystyle=\gamma_{Q}\left(\frac{x^{2}}{2(|x|+1)^{2}}\,\frac{y^{2}}{2}+\frac{x}{(|x|+1)^{3}}\,\frac{y^{3}}{3!}+\frac{1}{(|x|+1)^{4}}\,\frac{y^{4}}{4!}\right)+O(n^{-1/4}\log^{5}n)

I_{2}
decays exponentially as

[-\log(n),\log(n)]
to

, incurring only an exponentially small error in

.
Finally, performing the change of variable

u_{j}=\frac{e^{-nV_{\tau}(r_{\tau})}r_{\tau}}{n^{1/4}}\left(f_{1}(x)+\frac{1}{n^{1/4}}f_{2}(x)+O\!\left(\frac{1}{n^{1/2}}\right)\right),

Lemma 5.1

As

\displaystyle T_{1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\left[-nV_{\tau}(r_{\tau})-\frac{1}{2}\log(n)+\frac{1}{2}\log\!\left(\frac{2\pi r_{\tau}}{V^{\prime\prime}(r_{\tau})}\right)\right],

\displaystyle=-n^{2}I_{Q}[\mu_{Q}]-\frac{n\log(n)}{2}+n\left[\frac{\log(\pi/2)}{2}-\frac{1}{2}E_{Q}[\mu_{Q}]\right]+O(1).

6.3
Mean level spacing

Proof of
subsection 2.2
.

We define the

at
r_{*}

R_{n}

where

and

denotes the number of points in a given realization of the point process that fall within the set
\Omega

Since the macroscopic probability density is given by
z\mapsto K_{n}(z,z)/n
, the average number of particle in a disk of radius
R_{n}
centered around
r_{*}
is then
n
times the probability to find at least one particle in this disk, i.e.

\mathbb{E}\left[N_{n}\!\bigl(\mathbb{D}(r_{*},R_{n})\bigr)\right]=\int_{|z-r_{*}|<R_{n}}K_{n}(z,z)\,dA(z).

(6.31)

r_{*}
the first order approximation of the kernel is given by
K_{n}(z,z)=K_{n}(r_{*},r_{*})+o(1)
, as

. The leading term asymptotic of the

is then given by
subsection 2.2
for

, i.e.

\displaystyle K_{n}(r_{*},r_{*})=\sqrt{n}\frac{\varkappa_{Q}}{r_{*}}\rho(0)+o(1),\qquad n\to\infty,

(6.32)

\pi R_{n}^{2}

\mathbb{E}\left[N_{n}\!\bigl(\mathbb{D}(r_{*},R_{n})\bigr)\right]=1=\sqrt{n}\,\frac{\pi\varkappa_{Q}}{r_{*}}\rho(0)\,R_{n}^{2}+o(1),\qquad n\to\infty.

(6.34)

7
Discussion and outlook

In this work, we studied a 2D Coulomb gas at inverse temperature
\beta=2
and considered a family of radially symmetric potentials for which a gap is about to appear inside the droplet—which is either a disk or an annulus—around a circle of radius
r_{*}>0
.
In particular, we studied the impact of this critical phenomena, both on the macroscopic level via the large
n
asymptotic of the partition function, cf.
Theorem 2.1
, and on the microscopic level via the local correlation functions which are controlled, in this case, by a new universal kernel, cf.
Theorem 2.2
.

Several questions remain open. First, in this work, we restricted ourselves to radially symmetric potentials. If the radial symmetry is broken, it remains to be understood whether the universality of the new kernel in (
2.16
) persists. One can conjecture that it does, as long as the gap appears along a smooth enough curve, since the limiting kernel captures only local correlations. Regarding the expansion of the partition function, without radial symmetry, the expansion is expected to still have a term of order
n^{1/4}
, reminiscent of the criticality of the phenomena, but the associated constant is expected to be different.

It would also be of great interest to study the fluctuations of linear statistics of the point process (
1.1
), near the critical radius
r_{*}
. See
F23
for a survey of results in this direction.

Next, in
section 5
, we obtained an expansion of the partition function with an error term of order
o(n^{1/4})
. We conjecture that the subsequent terms are of order
O(\log n)
and
O(1)
, which would be of considerable interest—both intrinsically and for their potential relevance in the computation of fluctuations.

We also note that, as a generalisation of
subsection 2.3
, one can consider a one parameter family of potentials defined by the difference

Q_{t}(z)=Q_{1}(z)-tQ_{2}(z),

(7.1)

with
Q_{1}
and
Q_{2}
strictly subharmonic and such that

\Delta Q_{1}(z)\geqslant\Delta Q_{2}(z),

(7.2)

with equality for
|z|=r_{*}
, while strict inequality for
|z|\neq r_{*}
. As
t
varies in some small interval centered around
t=1
, the number of connected components of the support of the equilibrium measure increases. The present work concerns the case
t=1
. A natural next step is to study the point process in the scaling limit where
\absolutevalue{t-1}=c_{n}
for some sequence
c_{n}\to 0
. We expect in this regime an interpolation between the Ginibre bulk kernel and the critical kernel in (
2.8
). This will be the object of another article.

Finally, our large
n
expansion of the partition function reveals a new term of order
n^{1/4}
. This scale is inversely proportional to the mean level spacing of particles in the vicinity of the critical phenomena—near emergence of a gap—and dependent on the degree of criticality of the potential. We give
subsection 2.2
and
subsection 2.2
regarding the expansion of the partition function and the microscopic correlation functions under higher degree of criticality of the potential.
We plan to return to these questions in future works.

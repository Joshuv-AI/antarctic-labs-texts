γH2AX and p53 Immunohistochemistry predict the incidence risk of esophageal squamous precancerous lesions
Authors: Xiangwen Cheng, Ying Liu, Huanyu Chen, Yaqi Pan, Zhe Hu, Zhen Liu, Fangfang Liu, Wenlei Yang, Chuanhai Guo, Lixin Zhang, Liheng Zhang, Fenglei Li, Jiangang Li, Xinna Liu, Haijun Yang, Muyan Cai, Mengfei Liu, Zhonghu He, Yang Ke
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundCurrent screening practices for esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC) subject to both over- and under-surveillance of precancerous lesions. This study aims to evaluate potential immunohistochemical (IHC) markers associated with the incidence of ESCC and to establish an IHC-based panel for surveillance.MethodsTissue microarrays were constructed using 946 formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded (FFPE) samples from 883 participants with baseline Lugol-unstained lesions (LULs) enrolled in a population-based screening trial. Candidate IHC markers were identified from literature and our previous findings. IHC results (H-scores) were quantified via HALO software, and optimal cut-offs were set by X-tile. Incident esophageal malignancy identified during a median follow-up of 11.76 years served as the outcome. Cox regression models were applied to identify predictive markers.ResultsEight candidate markers were evaluated. After multivariable adjustment and backward elimination, γH2AX and p53 were identified as significant predictors [HR (95% CI) γH2AX =2.91 (1.94–4.34), HR (95% CI) p53 =3.57 (2.40–5.30)]. The IHC score, combining the expression of γH2AX and p53, demonstrated strong predictive accuracy (C-index = 0.705) with AUCs of 0.894 (3-year), 0.909 (5-year), and 0.733 (10-year). Compared with the current surveillance strategy, 5.06% of the previous low-risk group with non-dysplastic LULs changed to the high-risk group (both γH2AX and p53 positive) with a cumulative incidence of HGIN and above (HGINA) of 44.7% (17/38). while 29.17% of individuals in the previous high-risk group with low-grade intraepithelial neoplasia > 1 cm changed to the low-risk group (neither γH2AX nor p53 positive) with no incident of HGINA (0/7).ConclusionsγH2AX, a novel incidence-warning IHC marker for esophageal squamous precancerous lesions, combined with p53, showed robust and durable predictive performance for incident malignancy after one-time screening. Integrating IHC testing could help refine risk stratification and mitigate over- and under-surveillance within current ESCC screening protocol.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12916-026-04902-z.Keywords: Esophageal squamous cell carcinoma, Precancerous lesions, Surveillance, Immunohistochemical markers, Risk stratification

Background

Esophageal cancer (EC) is one of the most common cancer types worldwide, with 510,000 new cases and 445,000 deaths in 2022 [1]. Esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC) is the predominant histologic subtype of EC, accounting for approximately 85% of cases globally with a considerable variation in disease burden across geographic regions [2]. East and central Asia (such as China and Iran) are high risk areas of ESCC [1]. Primary prevention for ESCC is currently difficult to achieve due to obstacles in the understanding of the etiology, thus secondary prevention in terms of early diagnosis and treatment, is the main approach to decrease its mortality [3].

Currently, Lugol’s chromoendoscopy (LCE) with targeted biopsy is the gold-standard technique for detecting early-stage ESCC and its precancerous lesions, which is widely applied in ESCC screening programs [4]. The effectiveness of population-based screening programs relies on two dimensions. The first is the detection and treatment of patients with high-grade intraepithelial neoplasia (HGIN) and early-stage ESCC [3]; the second is to identify precancerous lesions at high risk of developing ESCC and adopt endoscopic surveillance for timely detection of incident malignancies.

According to the current screening guideline in China, endoscopic surveillance is recommended only for patients with dysplasia [5]. Those with low-grade intraepithelial neoplasia (LGIN) are advised to undergo LCE surveillance every 1–3 years, with annual re-examination recommended for individuals with LGIN sized > 1 cm [5]. Individuals with non-dysplastic Lugol unstained lesions (ND-LULs) are excluded from surveillance and instead undergo routine re-examination every 5 years, consistent with the recommendation for the general population in high-risk areas.

However, we have reported that 40%-50% participants who progressed to HGIN and above (HGINA) lesions during a median follow-up of 4.2 years were diagnosed as ND-LUL at baseline endoscopy [6], indicating the current dysplasia-focused surveillance strategy may lead to missed diagnoses of incident malignancies. Moreover, data from a national screening program in China showed that the incidence of HGINA among patients with LGIN was estimated at 1.4–4.5% over 8.5 years of follow-up [7]. The low absolute rates challenge the feasibility and cost-effectiveness of surveillance, making individuals suffer from adverse effect caused by endoscopy with minimal-to-no clinical benefit.

Given the presence of under- and over-surveillance in the current strategy, molecular biomarkers capable of predicting the incidence risk of esophageal squamous lesions are warranted. Several studies have explored the associations of somatic copy number variations, RNA, and serum markers with the incidence risk of esophageal squamous lesions [8–11]. However, most of these biomarkers are costly, technically demanding, or inefficient, limiting their translation into real-world screening programs. In contrast, immunohistochemistry (IHC) markers are technically feasible, relatively cheap, and readily interpretable by pathologists, which is widely implemented in pathology laboratories worldwide [12]. Nevertheless, currently available incidence-warning IHC markers for esophageal squamous precancerous lesions are scarce and their performance in population-level screening modalities is unknown [13].

In the present study, we conducted an empirical study based on a community-based screening trial of individuals with Lugol unstained lesions (LULs) and a median follow-up of 11.76 years. We assessed the performance of IHC markers in stratifying incidence risk, with the aim of enhancing the precision and efficiency of ESCC surveillance after one-time screening.

Methods

Study specimens and tissue microarray (TMA) construction

This study is based on the screening arm of the Endoscopic Screening for Esophageal Cancer in China (ESECC) randomized controlled trial (ClinicalTrials.gov, Number: NCT01688908), the design of which has been described previously [14]. Briefly, this trial was conducted in Hua County, Henan Province, a high-risk area for ESCC in the Taihang mountain region of China. A total of 15,299 participants aged 45 to 69 years in the screening arm underwent the baseline standard LCE between 2012 and 2016. Biopsy specimens from visually detected LUL areas were formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded (FFPE), and independently diagnosed by two experienced pathologists at Anyang Cancer Hospital, with discrepancies resolved by consultation. The baseline pathologic grade was initially categorized as no dysplasia, mild dysplasia, moderate dysplasia, severe dysplasia, carcinoma in situ and ESCC. In this study, mild and moderate dysplasia were defined as LGIN, severe dysplasia and carcinoma in situ were defined as HGIN, as suggested by previous studies [15, 16].

The inclusion criteria of this study were: (1) enrolled in the screening arm of the ESECC trial; and (2) had LULs detected at baseline endoscopy. The exclusion criteria were: (1) diagnosis of HGINA at baseline; (2) biopsy FFPE specimens were unavailable or insufficient for TMA construction.

For TMA construction, an H&E stained slide was first made from each FFPE block to define representative epithelial or dysplastic regions. The representative areas were sampled via a tissue microarray sampling pen and transferred to recipient paraffin blocks. The mapping between core positions on the array and original specimen IDs was recorded. Each specimen could be represented by multiple cores on the TMA. Through sequential fusion and embedding steps, eligible biopsy FFPE blocks were constructed into TMAs containing 5 × 10 cores per block (Additional file 1: Fig. S1).

Screening for candidate IHC markers

A systematic literature search was conducted in PubMed and CNKI [in Chinese] for articles published between July 1, 2014 and December 31, 2024, using the term of “(Esophageal squamous cell carcinoma [Title/Abstract]) AND ((Immunohistochemistry [Title/Abstract]) OR (Immunolabeling [Title/Abstract])) AND ((Progression) OR (Predict))”. A total of 549 and 177 articles was identified in PubMed and CNKI, respectively.

Titles and abstracts of all the identified articles were screened and articles will be initially included in this study if they meet the following eligibility criteria: (1) study subjects were non-cancer individuals rather than ESCC patients, animals or cell lines; (2) immunohistochemistry was performed on baseline endoscopic samples; (3) the outcome of the study was ESCC incidence rather than recurrence; and (4) the design was prospective cohort with follow-up. A total of 1 and 1 articles were acknowledged in PubMed and CNKI, respectively [13, 17]. Subsequently, the full texts were carefully reviewed in detail. After screening and removing a duplicate study, one eligible study remained, reporting EGFR, ANO1, CHK2, and p53 as potential predictive IHC markers [13].

Moreover, based on our prior work [18, 19], markers showing significant somatic mutations, focal amplifications, or homozygous deletions in progressive cases compared with non-progressive ones were considered candidate markers, as such alterations might lead to changes in protein expression, including Cyclin D1, γH2AX, NOTCH1, and p16 (CDKN2A). In total, eight proteins (EGFR, ANO1, CHK2, p53, Cyclin D1, γH2AX, NOTCH1, and p16) were selected as candidate predictive IHC markers.

Immunohistochemistry and scoring criteria

Sections (3 μm-thick) from TMAs were deparaffinized, rehydrated, immersed in 3% hydrogen peroxide solution for 10 min, heated in boiling antigen retrieval solution (citrate buffer or EDTA) for 2.5 min at 1000 W, and cooled for 150 min at room temperature. Between each incubation step, the sections were washed with phosphate buffered saline (PBS, pH 7.4). Then the sections were incubated with primary antibody at 37℃ (Additional file 1: Table S1). Immunostaining was performed using the GT Vision™ Detection System with diaminobenzidine (GK600711, Gene Tech) and subsequently counterstained with hematoxylin. ESCC tissues were used as positive controls and slides without primary antibody served as negative controls. The control slides were prepared separately from the TMA but processed simultaneously under identical IHC staining conditions.

Protein expressions were assessed by HALO image analysis software (Indica Labs, Albuquerque, NM, USA). For each biomarker, the Multiplex IHC algorithm was optimized to segment the nuclei based on the hematoxylin stain, and to classify each cell as positive or negative based on optical density threshold in the relevant cellular compartment (nucleus, cytoplasm, membrane) (details were showed in Additional file 1) [20]. A histoscore (H-score) was calculated using the formula: H-score = 3× % (strong intensity stained cells) + 2× % (moderate intensity stained cells) + 1× % (weak intensity stained cells), giving a 0–300 semi-quantitative value. For tissues with multiple cores on the TMA, the average H-score of all cores was taken as the H-score of the tissue. Optimal cut-off points for high and low expression of each IHC marker were determined using X-tile software (v3.6.1) by comparing the corrected P-values of survival differences.

Outcome assessment

Outcome of the current study was defined as the incidence of HGINA during the follow-up duration.

Participants in the ESECC trial were followed via annual door-to-door active interviews and passive linkage with the local registry data [14]. This follow-up framework was verified with high sensitivity (over 95%) in identifying incident cancer events [21–23]. Additionally, two rounds of endoscopic re-examination were conducted by our team in 2017–2018 and 2023–2024 for subjects detected with LULs at baseline, in which biopsies were obtained from the same position that presented LUL at the index endoscopy, and HGINA detected at re-examination were also considered as outcome events.

Follow-up time was calculated as the duration from the baseline examination to the date of onset of HGINA, death due to any cause, or the administrative censoring date of this study (May 31, 2025), whichever occurred first.

Covariates

Covariates collected in this study included risk factors of ESCC, features of LULs and baseline pathology. Detailed definitions and coding of covariates were described in the Additional file 1.

Risk factors were collected via baseline questionnaire investigation, including age, sex, body mass index (BMI), family history of cancer, speed of eating, ingestion of leftovers, coal stove for heating, fume exposure in kitchen, upper gastrointestinal (UGI) symptoms, cigarette smoking, alcohol drinking and preference for food temperature [14]. Features of LULs contained the size of LULs, mosaic staining, irregular shape and sharp border at baseline. At the index LCE, endoscopic images were regularly captured every 5 cm along the esophagus. For each LUL, close-up images were taken, and retrospectively reviewed by well-trained researchers in our team as previously reported [24].

Statistical analysis

For IHC markers, we first described their H-score distributions and excluded those with extremely skewed patterns (H-score below 10 in > 95% of individuals) [25]. Then Cox regression analysis with cluster-robust variance to adjust intra-case correlations was applied to evaluate predictive IHC markers. Each IHC marker was initially evaluated for its association with the incidence of HGINA after adjusting for covariates. Backward elimination with a threshold of 0.05 was further performed to determine IHC markers retained in the final model. The count of high-expressed molecules among these selected markers were used to construct an integrated IHC score. The Kaplan-Meier curve was employed to present the cumulative incidence of HGINA stratified by the IHC score. The predictive performance of the IHC score was assessed using the concordance index (C-index) and the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC). Net reclassification improvement (NRI) and the reclassification heat map were used to evaluate the reclassification performance of the IHC score compared with the current surveillance strategy.

To address potential heterogeneity in outcome ascertainment, we conducted a subgroup analysis according to the method of outcome detection. Outcome events were categorized as: (1) lesions detected through surveillance endoscopy (re-examination-detected) and (2) lesions identified through annual interview or registry linkage (follow-up-detected). The association between the IHC score and the incidence of HGINA was evaluated separately in each subgroup using multivariable Cox regression models.

All analyses were performed using R software (version 4.3.3), with two-sided P < 0.05 considered statistically significant.

Results

Characteristics of participants and distribution of IHC H-scores

A total of 946 FFPE blocks from 883 participants were included in this study. Over a median follow-up of 11.76 years, 119 tissue samples (12.58%) developed HGINA (incident HGINA), whereas the other 827 (87.42%) remained free of HGINA at the end of the study (non-HGINA). The two groups were well balanced in terms of sex, BMI, family history of esophageal cancer, speed of eating, ingestion of leftovers, fume exposure in kitchen, upper gastrointestinal symptom, cigarette smoking, alcohol drinking and preference for food temperature at baseline investigation, but had significant differences in other risk factors, features of LULs and pathologic grade at baseline (Table 1).

After adjustment for covariates, five IHC markers (Cyclin D1, CHK2, EGFR, γH2AX and p53) remained independently associated with the incidence of HGINA (Table 2). An integrated IHC score was subsequently constructed based on γH2AX and p53, which was stratified into three risk categories: low risk (neither marker highly expressed), moderate risk (one marker highly expressed), and high risk (both markers highly expressed). Compared with the low-risk group, the HRs for incidence of HGINA were 3.35 (95% CI: 2.19–5.12) in the moderate-risk group and 10.30 (95% CI: 6.36–16.68) in the high-risk group (Fig. 3).

Performance of the IHC score

The current surveillance protocol showed a C-index of 0.608 (3-, 5- and 10-year AUCs = 0.676, 0.700 and 0.616) (Fig. 4). In comparison, the IHC score demonstrated superior discriminatory performance with a C-index of 0.705 (P value < 0.001) and time-dependent AUCs at 3, 5 and 10 years equal to 0.894, 0.909 and 0.733, respectively (Fig. 4). Furthermore, integrating the IHC score with current surveillance indicators enhanced predictive accuracy, resulting in a combined C-index of 0.745 and corresponding AUCs of 0.930, 0.928, and 0.773 at the same time points (Fig. 4).

Comparison of time-dependent AUCs for the current surveillance protocol, the IHC score, and their combination

After excluding cases with missing values in IHC scores or size of LULs, a total of 903 tissues were included in the reclassification analysis. The overall NRI was 0.16 (95% CI: 0.03–0.28), indicating a significant improvement in risk stratification. Compared with the current surveillance protocol, 25.03% (188/751) and 10.53% (16/152) tissues diagnosed with ND-LUL and LGIN were upgraded to a higher risk group with cumulative incidences ranged from 17.3% to 43.8%; and 55.92% (85/152) of LGINs were downgraded to lower risk categories, with cumulative incidences from 0% to 18.8% (Fig. 5). Notably, 38 ND-LUL samples were up-classified as high-risk by the IHC score, with cumulative incidence of 44.7%; and 7 LGINs with size > 1 cm were down-classified as low-risk, with no incident cases were identified during the study period (Fig. 5).

Performance of subgroup analysis

Subgroup analysis showed that the method of case detection did not materially influence the predictive value of the IHC score. Among the 119 events, 89 were detected through surveillance endoscopy and 30 were identified via follow-up interview or registry linkage. The association between the IHC score and the incidence of HGINA remained statistically significant in both the re-examination-detected and the follow-up-detected subgroups (Additional file 1: Table S2).

Discussion

The current surveillance for ESCC after one-time screening, which primarily relies on morphological dysplasia and the size of LULs, has been proved to subject to both over- and under- estimation of the incidence risk among precancerous lesions. Reliable biomarkers are urgently needed to supplement existing risk stratification indices and improve the precision of post-screening management. Although several cohort studies have investigated biomarkers predicting the incidence of ESCC [8–11], their translations into real-world screening are scarce to date. This is mainly due to the fact that most proposed biomarkers are expensive to test, require specialized equipment, and involve complex analytical procedures. Unfortunately, most high-risk areas for ESCC are underdeveloped regions with limited medical resources [26], which restricts the applicability of these biomarkers in large-scale screening practice. IHC, a mature technique already implemented in most pathology laboratories, is relatively low-cost and highly feasible, making it particularly suitable for low-resource settings. It may represent the most practical and feasible approach for population-level ESCC screening programs.

In this study, we identified γH2AX as a novel incidence -warning IHC marker for esophageal squamous precancerous lesions. γH2AX is a well-known indicator of DNA damage [27]. DNA damage can induce somatic mutations, copy number alterations (CNAs) and whole-genome duplication (WGD) [28–30]. Our previous studies have shown that somatic mutations (notably sustained TP53 two-hit events), recurrent focal CNAs (e.g., SOX2, EGFR, MYC, CDKN2A), and WGD were detectable at an extremely early stage of ESCC carcinogenesis before the presence of visible dysplasia (ND-LULs), and determined the progression outcome years before endoscopy-detectable malignancy [15, 18]. This study confirms that γH2AX, as a signal of the initiation of these genomic alterations, can serve as an early warning indicator in ND-LULs. TP53, a famous tumor suppressor gene, whose abnormal expression disrupts cell cycle, cell apoptosis, and DNA repair, thereby promoting carcinogenesis, which has been confirmed at both the genomic and protein levels [17, 31, 32]. This finding may not only provide predictive value but also inspire further investigation into the underlying mechanisms of ESCC initiation.

In this study, we established an IHC-based panel, the IHC score, to stratify individuals with precancerous squamous lesions, thereby addressing the issues of over- and under-surveillance in post-screening management. Among the 24 subjects with LGIN and size of LUL > 1 cm (classified as high-risk by the current protocol), our IHC score reclassified 7 cases as low-risk, 9 as moderate-risk, and 8 as high-risk, with corresponding ESCC incidence rate of 0%, 11.1%, and 62.5% during the study period. This demonstrates the panel’s ability to accurately distinguish individuals at genuine high-risk from those not, thereby minimizing unnecessary endoscopies. What’s more, by extending the surveillance population to include individuals with ND-LUL, 38 participants with ND-LUL defined as low-risk by the current protocol were reclassified as high-risk by our IHC score, with an observed incidence of 44.7% during the study period. Thus, utilizing IHC score represents an effective approach to mitigate under surveillance and reduce missed diagnosis.

The IHC score showed robust and durable discriminatory performance, achieving a 5-year AUC of 0.90 and maintaining an AUC above 0.70 throughout 12 years of follow-up. When integrated with the current surveillance protocol, i.e., baseline pathology and size of LULs, the predictive performance was further improved, with a 5-year AUC exceeding 0.92 and a 12-year AUC above than 0.75, indicating a superior and sustained predictive ability.

Based on findings from this study, we propose an updated ESCC surveillance framework integrating IHC score and the current risk stratification indicators. Compared with the currently-recommended protocol, high-risk individuals identified by IHC score should be referred to frequent endoscopic surveillance, even without dysplasia in pathology. LGIN patients classified as low-risk by the IHC score may retain or consider to decrease the frequency of endoscopic surveillance. Such an approach would enable personalized surveillance, ensuring that high-risk individuals receive timely monitoring while those at lower risks are spared from unnecessary procedures.

This study was based on a population-based screening trial that reflected the natural distribution of ESCC risk following one-time endoscopic screening in real-world settings. The findings provide empirical evidence supporting the application of IHC-based markers in population-level screening modalities for ESCC. This study also has limitations. First, although the empirical validation design enhances the reliability of our results, the single-center nature may limit the generalizability of our findings. Second, because the detection time points for re-examination detected HGINAs were artificially moved forward to concentrated fixed dates rather than their actual time of onset, the surveillance interval of the IHC-based risk categories cannot be determined from the current data. Last, it was uncertain whether follow-up-detected cases originated from the exact LUL recorded at baseline, limiting strict lesion-level concordance. Nevertheless, subgroup analysis confirmed our main findings.

Conclusions

In conclusion, this study identified γH2AX as a novel IHC marker predicting the incidence of esophageal squamous precancerous lesions. An IHC-based panel composed of p53 and γH2AX demonstrated a strong and sustained predictive performance with accurate risk stratification for the incidence of esophageal malignancy, with potential advantages in addressing both under- and over-surveillance challenges inherent in current screening practice for ESCC.

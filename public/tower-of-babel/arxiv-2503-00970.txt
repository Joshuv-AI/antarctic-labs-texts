Abstract
. The L p L_{p} Gaussian Minkowski problem for C C -pseudo-cones is studied in this paper, and the existence and uniqueness results are established. This extends our previous work on the Minkowski problem for C C -pseudo-cones with respect to the Gaussian surface area measure ( p = 1 p=1 ) and the Gaussian cone measure ( p = 0 p=0 ).



1.
Introduction

For convex bodies, the Minkowski problem aims to establish necessary and sufficient conditions for a Borel measure on the unit sphere to be the surface area measure. The
L_{p}
surface area measure is a generalization of the classical surface area measure. The
L_{p}
Minkowski problem, which characterizes the
L_{p}
surface area measure, was first introduced by Lutwak
[
22
,
23
]
, extending the classical Brunn-Minkowski theory into a broader framework. When
p=1
, the
L_{p}
Minkowski problem reduces to the classical Minkowski problem. Consequently, many classical results in convex geometry become part of the more general
L_{p}
theory, such as the affine isoperimetric inequality
[
4
,
12
,
26
]
, the Minkowski problem
[
3
,
14
,
18
,
24
,
25
,
40
]
. Notably, for
p\geq 1
, many forms of the
L_{p}
Minkowski problem have been resolved. However, significant open questions remain for the case
p<1
.

A special class of unbounded closed convex sets, known as
C
-pseudo-cones, has recently attracted considerable research interest. Within the copolarity framework,
C
-pseudo-cones are the counterparts of convex bodies with the origin in their interiors
[
2
,
27
,
31
,
37
]
. Let
C\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a pointed (i.e., containing no lines),
n
-dimensional closed convex cone. A
pseudo-cone
is defined as a nonempty closed convex set
K
that does not contain the origin and satisfies
\lambda K\subseteq K
for all
\lambda\geq 1
. The
recession cone
of
K
, denoted by
\operatorname{rec}K
, is given by

\operatorname{rec}K=\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:K+z\subseteq K\}.

A
C
-pseudo-cone
is a pseudo-cone
K
whose recession cone satisfies
\operatorname{rec}K=C
. As
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
tends to infinity, the shape of the
C
-pseudo-cone
K
is strongly governed by the cone
C
.

The
polar cone
of
C
, denoted by
C^{\circ}
, is defined as

C^{\circ}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\langle x,y\rangle\leq 0\text{ for all }y\in C\},

we will write

\Omega_{C^{\circ}}=S^{n-1}\cap\operatorname{int}C^{\circ}.

Unlike convex bodies, the surface area measure of a
C
-pseudo-cone may be infinite. The Minkowski problem for the surface area measure and cone volume measure of
C
-pseudo-cones was first studied by Schneider
[
29
,
30
]
. To generate finite measures, Schneider
[
33
,
32
]
introduced the weighted Minkowski problem, where the weighted functions are homogeneous. Furthermore, Yang, Ye, and Zhu
[
38
]
later explored the
L_{p}
-Brunn-Minkowski theory for
C
-coconvex sets, while Li, Ye, and Zhu
[
19
]
developed dual versions. Further developments in the theory of pseudo-cones can be found in
[
1
,
34
,
36
,
39
]
.

In
[
35
]
, the first author, the second author, and Xu introduced a class of non-homogeneous measures—the Gaussian surface area measure and the Gaussian cone measure for
C
-pseudo-cones, and posed the corresponding Gaussian Minkowski problem. The Gaussian surface area measure of a
C
-pseudo-cone
K
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is defined on
\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
as

S_{\gamma^{n}}(K,\eta)=\frac{1}{(\sqrt{2\pi})^{n}}\int_{\nu_{K}^{-1}(\eta)}e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{2}}d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)

(1.1)

for every Borel set
\eta\subset\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
, where
\nu_{K}
is the outer unit normal vector of
K
. The measure
S_{\gamma^{n}}(K,\cdot)
is obviously finite.

Naturally, for any real number
p
, the
L_{p}
Gaussian surface area measure, as the
L_{p}
generalization of (
1.1
), can be defined as

S_{p,\gamma^{n}}(K,\eta)=\frac{1}{(\sqrt{2\pi})^{n}}\int_{\nu_{K}^{-1}(\eta)}|\langle x,\nu_{K}(x)\rangle|^{1-p}e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{2}}d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x).

(1.2)

In particular, the cases
p=1
and
p=0
correspond to the Gaussian surface area measure and the Gaussian cone measure, respectively, as introduced in our previous work
[
35
]
.

It is worth noting that, in the case of convex bodies, the corresponding Gaussian Minkowski problem was studied in
[
15
]
, while its
L_{p}
version was provided in
[
20
]
. Additional results on the Gaussian Minkowski problem for convex bodies can be found in
[
5
,
8
,
9
,
17
,
21
]
.

In this paper, we consider the (normalized)
L_{p}
Gaussian Minkowski problem for
C
-pseudo-cones. For the existence theorem, we discuss two cases:
p>0
and
p<0
. When
p>0
, the result corresponds to a generalization of the case
p=1
in
[
35
]
, but when
p<0
, the situation is completely different. For
p>0
, the corresponding result is

Theorem 1.1
.

If
p>0
,
for any nonzero finite Borel measure
\mu
on
\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
, there exists a
C
-pseudo-cone
K
such that

\mu=\frac{c}{p}S_{p,\gamma^{n}}(K,\cdot),

where
c=\frac{\int_{\Omega_{C^{\circ}}}\bar{h}_{K}^{p}d\mu}{\gamma^{n}(K)}
.

Here
\gamma^{n}(K)
denotes the
n
-dimensional Gaussian measure of
K
and
\bar{h}_{K}
denotes the absolute support function of
K
.
For the case
p<0
, the existence of solutions to the (normalized)
L_{p}
Gaussian Minkowski problem can be stated as follows.

Theorem 1.2
.

If
p<0
,
for any nonzero finite Borel measure
\mu
on
\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
, there exists a
C
-pseudo-cone
K
such that

\mu=-\frac{c}{p}S_{p,\gamma^{n}}(K,\cdot),

where
c=\frac{\int_{\Omega_{C^{\circ}}}\bar{h}_{K}^{p}d\mu}{\gamma^{n}(K)}
.

For the case
p=0
, the limiting case of
L_{p}
theory, referred to as the Gaussian logarithmic Minkowski problem, has been studied in
[
35
]
.

The class
\mathcal{K}(C,\omega)
consists of
C
-pseudo-cones whose construction is determined by the compact set
\omega\subset\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
. Specifically,
K\in\mathcal{K}(C,\omega)
implies

K=C\cap\bigcap_{u\in\omega}\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\langle x,u\rangle\leq h_{K}(u)\},

where
h_{K}
denotes the support function of
K
. Under volume constraints, elements in
\mathcal{K}(C,\omega)
satisfy the following uniqueness property:

Theorem 1.3
.

For
p\in(0,1]
, let
\omega\subset\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
be a nonempty compact set. If
K,L\in\mathcal{K}(C,\omega)
have the same
L_{p}
Gaussian surface area measure, i.e.,

S_{p,\gamma^{n}}(K,\cdot)=S_{p,\gamma^{n}}(L,\cdot),

and if
\gamma^{n}(K)=\gamma^{n}(L)
, then
K=L
.

For certain directional hyperplanes
H_{t}
, the Gaussian surface area density of
C\cap H_{t}
tends to zero as
t\to 0^{+}
and
t\to+\infty
, where
t
denotes the distance from the origin to the hyperplane. This asymptotic behavior implies that for any cone
C
and any compact set
\omega\subset\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
, one can always construct examples in
\mathcal{K}(C,\omega)
such that the solution to the
L_{p}
Gaussian Minkowski problem loses uniqueness:

Theorem 1.4
.

If
p<n
, for any pointed,
n
-dimensional closed convex cone
C
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and any nonempty compact set
\omega\subset\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
, there exist distinct sets
K,L\in\mathcal{K}(C,\omega)
such that their
L_{p}
Gaussian surface area measures coincide, i.e.,
S_{p,\gamma^{n}}(K,\cdot)=S_{p,\gamma^{n}}(L,\cdot)
, but
K\neq L
.

2.
Preliminaries

Let
\left(\mathbb{R}^{n},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle\right)
denote the
n
-dimensional Euclidean space with the standard inner product. A subset
C\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is called a
cone
if it satisfies
\lambda x\in C
for all
x\in C
and
\lambda\geq 0
. A closed convex set
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is termed a
pseudo-cone
if it satisfies
\lambda x\in K
for all
x\in K
and
\lambda\geq 1
. The recession cone of
K
, characterizing its asymptotic behavior, is defined as
\operatorname{rec}K=\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:K+z\subseteq K\}
. For a pointed closed convex cone
C\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, a
C
-pseudo-cone
refers to a pseudo-cone with
\operatorname{rec}K=C
. The dual cone is given by
C^{\circ}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\langle x,y\rangle\leq 0\ \forall y\in C\}
. Throughout this paper, we assume that any cone
C
is pointed (i.e., it contains no lines) and
n
-dimensional.

Let
S^{n-1}
represent the unit sphere and
B
the open unit ball.
For
u\in S^{n-1}
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
, we define
H(u,t)=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\langle x,u\rangle=t\}
and its associated halfspace

H^{-}(u,t)=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\langle x,u\rangle\leq t\},\quad H^{+}(u,t)=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\langle x,u\rangle\geq t\}.

Fixing a unit vector
\mathfrak{v}\in\operatorname{int}C\cap\operatorname{int}(-C^{\circ})
, we partition
C
via

C^{-}(t)=C\cap H^{-}(\mathfrak{v},t),C^{+}(t)=C\cap H^{+}(\mathfrak{v},t),\quad\text{and}\quad C(t)=C\cap H(\mathfrak{v},t)

for
t>0
. Correspondingly, any
C
-pseudo-cone
K
is decomposed into
K(t)=K\cap C(t)
,
K^{-}(t)=K\cap C^{-}(t)
, and
K^{+}(t)=K\cap C^{+}(t)
.

For a
C
-pseudo-cone
K
, the
support function

h_{K}:C^{\circ}\to\mathbb{R}
, defined as

h_{K}(x)=\sup\{\langle x,y\rangle:y\in K\},

and it is bounded and non-positive. Its absolute support function is given by

\bar{h}_{K}=-h_{K}.

The radial function
\varrho_{K}:\Omega_{C}\to\mathbb{R}
, where
\Omega_{C}=S^{n-1}\cap\operatorname{int}C,
given by

\varrho_{K}(v)=\min\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}:\lambda v\in K\},

generates the radial map
r_{K}=\varrho_{K}(v)v
and radial Gauss map
\alpha_{K}=\nu_{K}\circ r_{K}
, where
\nu_{K}
denotes the outer unit normal on
\partial K
.

Convergence of
C
-pseudo-cones follows Schneider
[
31
]
:

Definition 2.1
.

A sequence
K_{i}
converges to
K
if there exists
t_{0}>0
such that
K_{i}^{-}(t_{0})\neq\emptyset
for all
i
and
K_{i}^{-}(t)\to K^{-}(t)
in Hausdorff metric for
t\geq t_{0}
.

The following selection theorem of
C
-pseudo-cones is essential:

Lemma 2.2
.

For a sequence
K_{i}
with uniform bounds
a<\operatorname{dist}(o,K_{i})<b
, there exists a convergent subsequence
K_{i_{j}}\to K
.

An integral transformation formula for
C
-pseudo-cones states that
[
33
]
:

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
K
be a
C
-pseudo-cone, for any nonnegative and Borel measurable or
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
-integrable function
F:\partial_{i}K\to\mathbb{R}
:

\displaystyle\int_{\partial_{i}K}F(y)d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(y)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{C}}F\left(r_{K}(v)\right)\frac{\varrho_{K}^{n}(v)}{\bar{h}_{K}\left(\alpha_{K}(v)\right)}dv

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{C}}F\left(r_{K}(v)\right)\frac{\varrho_{K}^{n-1}(v)}{\left|\left\langle v,\alpha_{K}(v)\right\rangle\right|}dv,

where
\partial_{i}K:=\partial K\cap\operatorname{int}C
.

In this paper, we define
\Omega:=\Omega_{C^{\circ}}=S^{n-1}\cap\operatorname{int}C^{\circ}
. For any
u\in\Omega
, the support hyperplane of a
C
-pseudo-cone
K
is defined as

H_{K}(u)=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\langle x,u\rangle=h_{K}(u)\right\},\quad H^{-}_{K}(u)=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\langle x,u\rangle\leq h_{K}(u)\right\}.

A
C
-pseudo-cone
K
is
C
-determined
by a compact set
\omega\subset\Omega=S^{n-1}\cap\operatorname{int}C^{\circ}
if
K=C\cap\bigcap_{u\in\omega}H_{K}^{-}(u)
. The collection
\mathcal{K}(C,\omega)
consists of such
C
-pseudo-cones that are
C
-determined by
\omega
.

We denote

K^{(\omega)}=C\cap\bigcap_{u\in\omega}H_{K}^{-}(u)

for a
C
-pseudo-cone
K
.
K^{(\omega)}\in\mathcal{K}(C,\omega)
.

The Wulff shape construction provides critical examples: For compact
\omega\subset\Omega
and positive continuous function
h:\omega\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
, the
Wulff shape

[h]
associated with
(C,\omega,h)
is defined as:

[h]=C\cap\bigcap_{u\in\omega}\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\langle y,u\rangle\leq-h(u)\}

belongs to
\mathcal{K}(C,\omega)
.

The following lemma, established in
[
33
]
, will be used in our analysis:

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
K_{j}
be a sequence of
C
-pseudo-cones converging to
K
, i.e.,
K_{j}\to K
. For any nonempty compact set
\omega\subset\Omega
, then
K_{j}^{(\omega)}
converge to
K^{(\omega)}
.

Let
E
be a convex set in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Its
n
-dimensional Gaussian measure
\gamma^{n}(E)
is given by

\gamma^{n}(E):=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{\frac{n}{2}}}\int_{E}e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{2}}dx.

The Gaussian measure
\gamma^{n}
is log-concave with respect to convex sets, that is:

\gamma^{n}((1-t)K+tL)\geq\gamma^{n}(K)^{1-t}\gamma^{n}(L)^{t}

equality holds if and only if
K=L
.
For further inequalities related to the Gaussian measure, we refer to

[
6
,
7
,
11
,
16
,
17
,
22
,
23
]
. For a comprehensive treatment of the theory of convex bodies, the classical texts
[
10
]
and
[
28
]
are recommended references.

3.
The
L_{p}
Gaussian surface area measure

The primary aim of this section is to establish the
L_{p}
variational formula. For this purpose, we need the following lemma from Schneider
[
33
]
. Let
\Omega:=\Omega_{C^{\circ}}=S^{n-1}\cap\operatorname{int}C^{\circ}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\omega\subset\Omega
be a nonempty compact set, and a
C
-pseudo-cone
K\in\mathcal{K}(C,\omega)
. Given a continuous function
f:\omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, then there exists a constant
\delta>0
which is small enough such that for any
|t|\leq\delta
, the function
h_{t}
defined as

\log h_{t}(u)=\log\bar{h}_{K}(u)+tf(u)+o(t,u),\quad u\in\omega,

(3.1)

where
o(t,\cdot):\omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a continuous function satisfying
\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}o(t,\cdot)/t=0
uniformly on
\omega
. The Wulff shape
\left[h_{t}\right]
associated with
\left(C,\omega,h_{t}\right)
is termed a logarithmic
family of Wulff shapes.

For almost all
v\in\Omega_{C}
, the derivative of the radial function is given by

\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}\varrho_{\left[h_{t}\right]}(v)}{\mathrm{d}t}\right|_{t=0}=\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}\frac{\varrho_{\left[h_{t}\right]}(v)-\varrho_{K}(v)}{t}=f\left(\alpha_{K}(v)\right)\varrho_{K}(v).

(3.2)

The Gaussian covolume
V_{G}(K)
of a
C
-pseudo-cone
K
is given in
[
35
]
by

Definition 3.2
.

V_{G}(K)=\gamma^{n}(C\backslash K).

Recall the
L_{p}
Gaussian surface area measure

S_{p,\gamma^{n}}(K,\cdot)
of a
C
-pseudo-cone
K
is defined as follows:

Definition 3.3
.

For every Borel set
\eta\subset\Omega
,

S_{p,\gamma^{n}}(K,\eta):=\frac{1}{(\sqrt{2\pi})^{n}}\int_{\nu_{K}^{-1}(\eta)}|\langle x,\nu_{K}(x)\rangle|^{1-p}e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{2}}d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)=\int_{\eta}\bar{h}_{K}^{1-p}(u)dS_{\gamma^{n}}(K,u).

The following
L_{p}
variational formula serves as the foundation for solving the
L_{p}
Minkowski problem using variational methods. We begin by proving the variational formula for the Gaussian covolume. The variational approach originated in
[
13
]
.

Lemma 3.4
.

If
K\in\mathcal{K}(C,\omega)
for some nonempty compact set
\omega\subset\Omega
. For
p\neq 0
, let
f:\omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a continuous function, and
\left[\left(\bar{h}_{K}(u)^{p}+tf(u)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\right]
denotes the Wulff shapes associated with
\left(C,\omega,\left(\bar{h}_{K}(u)^{p}+tf(u)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\right)
, then

\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}\frac{V_{G}\left(\left[\left(\bar{h}_{K}(u)^{p}+tf(u)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\right]\right)-V_{G}(K)}{t}=\frac{1}{p}\int_{\omega}f(u)dS_{p,\gamma^{n}}(K,u).

(3.3)

Proof.

For
p\neq 0
, let

h_{t}(u)=\left(\bar{h}_{K}(u)^{p}+tf(u)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

then

\log h_{t}=\log\bar{h}_{K}+\frac{tf}{p\bar{h}_{K}^{p}}+o(t,u),

(3.4)

where
o(t,\cdot):\omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a continuous function satisfying
\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}o(t,\cdot)/t=0
uniformly on
\omega
.

Using polar coordinates, we have

V_{G}\left(\left[h_{t}\right]\right)=\frac{1}{(\sqrt{2\pi})^{n}}\int_{\Omega_{C}}\int_{0}^{\varrho_{\left[h_{t}\right]}(v)}e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{2}}r^{n-1}drdv=\frac{1}{(\sqrt{2\pi})^{n}}\int_{\Omega_{C}}F_{t}(v)dv,

where we denote
F_{t}(v)=\int_{0}^{\varrho_{\left[h_{t}\right]}(v)}e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{2}}r^{n-1}dr
.
Therefore,

\displaystyle\frac{F_{t}(v)-F_{0}(v)}{t}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{t}\int_{\varrho_{K}(v)}^{\varrho_{\left[h_{t}\right]}(v)}e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{2}}r^{n-1}dr

(3.5)

\displaystyle=\frac{\varrho_{\left[h_{t}\right]}(v)-\varrho_{K}(v)}{t}\cdot\frac{1}{\varrho_{\left[h_{t}\right]}(v)-\varrho_{K}(v)}\int_{\varrho_{K}(v)}^{\varrho_{\left[h_{t}\right]}(v)}e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{2}}r^{n-1}dr.

(3.6)

Moreover, the last term converges to
e^{-\frac{\varrho_{K}^{2}}{2}}\varrho_{K}^{n-1}
. By Lemma
3.1
, we have

\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}\varrho_{\left[h_{t}\right]}(v)}{\mathrm{d}t}\right|_{t=0}=\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}\frac{\varrho_{\left[h_{t}\right]}(v)-\varrho_{K}(v)}{t}=\frac{f\left(\alpha_{K}(v)\right)\varrho_{K}(v)}{p\bar{h}_{K}^{p}(\alpha_{K}(v))},

(3.7)

it follows that

\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}\frac{F_{t}(v)-F_{0}(v)}{t}=e^{-\frac{\varrho_{K}^{2}}{2}}\frac{f\left(\alpha_{K}(v)\right)\varrho_{K}^{n}(v)}{p\bar{h}_{K}^{p}(\alpha_{K}(v))}

(3.8)

for almost
v\in\Omega_{C}
. Moreover, for sufficiently small
|t|
, there exists a constant
M
satisfying

\left|\frac{F_{t}(v)-F_{0}(v)}{t}\right|\leq M.

Applying the dominated convergence theorem and Lemma
2.3
, we obtain

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}\frac{V_{G}\left(\left[h_{t}\right]\right)-V_{G}(K)}{t}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(\sqrt{2\pi})^{n}}\int_{\Omega_{C}}\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}\frac{F_{t}(v)-F_{0}(v)}{t}dv

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(\sqrt{2\pi})^{n}}\int_{\Omega_{C}}e^{-\frac{\varrho_{K}^{2}}{2}}\frac{f\left(\alpha_{K}(v)\right)\varrho_{K}^{n}(v)}{p\bar{h}_{K}^{p}(\alpha_{K}(v))}dv

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}f(u)\bar{h}_{K}(u)^{1-p}dS_{\gamma^{n}}(K,u)

\displaystyle=\int_{\omega}f(u)dS_{p,\gamma^{n}}(K,u).

∎

Naturally, the
L_{p}
variational formula for the Gaussian volume is as follows.

Lemma 3.5
.

If
K\in\mathcal{K}(C,\omega)
for some nonempty compact set
\omega\subset\Omega
. For
p\neq 0
, let
f:\omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a continuous function, and
\left[\left(\bar{h}_{K}(u)^{p}+tf(u)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\right]
denotes the Wulff shapes associated with
\left(C,\omega,\left(\bar{h}_{K}(u)^{p}+tf(u)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\right)
, then

\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}\frac{\gamma^{n}\left(\left[\left(\bar{h}_{K}(u)^{p}+tf(u)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\right]\right)-\gamma^{n}(K)}{t}=-\frac{1}{p}\int_{\omega}f(u)dS_{p,\gamma^{n}}(K,u).

(3.9)

Proof.

This follows from Lemma
3.4
and the fact

\gamma^{n}(\left[\left(\bar{h}_{K}(u)^{p}+tf(u)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\right])+V_{G}(\left[\left(\bar{h}_{K}(u)^{p}+tf(u)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\right])=\gamma^{n}(K)+V_{G}(K)=\gamma^{n}(C).

∎

By the definition of
S_{p,\gamma^{n}}K
and the results established in our previous work
[
35
]
regarding the finiteness and weak convergence of the Gaussian surface area measure (
p=1
), we conclude that the
L_{p}
Gaussian surface area measure is also a finite measure. Furthermore, it is weakly convergent in the sense of Definition
2.1
when restricted to the set
\mathcal{K}(C,\omega)
, for some nonempty compact set
\omega\subset\Omega
:

Lemma 3.6
.

For a nonempty compact set
\omega\subset\Omega
, and
K_{j}\in\mathcal{K}(C,\omega)
,
j\in\mathbb{N}
. If
K_{j}\rightarrow K
as
j\rightarrow\infty
, then the measure
S_{p,\gamma^{n}}\left(K_{j},\cdot\right)
converge weakly to
S_{p,\gamma^{n}}\left(K,\cdot\right)
.

Proof.

This follows from the support functions
\bar{h}_{K_{j}}
converge uniformly to
\bar{h}_{K}
and the weak convergence of the Gaussian surface area measure.
∎

4.
Existence of solutions when
p>0

We first need to establish a uniform estimate for the decay properties of the Gaussian measure.

Lemma 4.1
.

\gamma^{n}(\mathbb{R}^{n}-rB)\leq\frac{2n\sqrt{n}}{(2\pi)^{\frac{1}{2}}r}e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{2n}}.

Proof.

If
t>0
,

\displaystyle\gamma^{1}(x>t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{\frac{1}{2}}}\int_{t}^{+\infty}e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{2}}dr

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{\frac{1}{2}}}\int_{t}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{r}\cdot re^{-\frac{r^{2}}{2}}dr

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{\frac{1}{2}}}\int_{t}^{+\infty}-\frac{1}{r}d(e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{2}})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{\frac{1}{2}}}\left(-\frac{1}{r}e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{2}}\Big|_{t}^{+\infty}-\int_{t}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{r^{2}}e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{2}}dr\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{\frac{1}{2}}}\left(\frac{1}{t}e^{-\frac{t^{2}}{2}}-\int_{t}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{r^{2}}e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{2}}dr\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{\frac{1}{2}}}\cdot\frac{1}{t}e^{-\frac{t^{2}}{2}}.

For a ball
rB
of radius
r
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, let
P_{r}
be its inscribed cube. Then, the edge length of
P_{r}
is
\frac{2r}{\sqrt{n}}
. Since
P_{r}\subset rB
, then
\mathbb{R}^{n}-rB\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}-P_{r}
, we conclude

\displaystyle\gamma^{n}(\mathbb{R}^{n}-rB)

\displaystyle\leq\gamma^{n}(\mathbb{R}^{n}-P_{r})

\displaystyle\leq\gamma^{n}(|x_{1}|>\frac{r}{\sqrt{n}})+\gamma^{n}(|x_{2}|>\frac{r}{\sqrt{n}})+\cdots+\gamma^{n}(|x_{n}|>\frac{r}{\sqrt{n}})

\displaystyle=2n\gamma^{1}(x>\frac{r}{\sqrt{n}})

\displaystyle\leq 2n\cdot\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{\frac{1}{2}}}\cdot\frac{1}{\frac{r}{\sqrt{n}}}e^{-\frac{\left(\frac{r}{\sqrt{n}}\right)^{2}}{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{2n\sqrt{n}}{(2\pi)^{\frac{1}{2}}r}e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{2n}}.

∎

Next, we hope to transform the
L_{p}
Gaussian Minkowski problem into an optimization problem. When
p>0
, the method for solving this problem is similar to
[
35
]
, but
with modifications.

We assume
\omega\subset\Omega
is a nonempty compact subset.
For any nonzero finite Borel measure
\mu
on
\omega
,
we define the set
C^{+}(\omega)
as the collection of continuous functions
f:\omega\to(0,\infty)
. If
p>0
, we introduce a functional
I_{\mu}:C^{+}(\omega)\to(0,\infty)
given by

I_{\mu}(f):=\gamma^{n}([f])\int_{\omega}f^{p}d\mu

for each
f\in C^{+}(\omega)
, where
[f]
represents the Wulff shape associated with
(C,\omega,f)
.

Given
p>0
, the following optimization problem is then considered:

\sup\{I_{\mu}(f)=\gamma^{n}([f])\int_{\omega}f^{p}d\mu:f\in C^{+}(\omega)\}.

Obviously, for any
K\in\mathcal{K}(C,\omega)
,
I_{\mu}(\bar{h}_{K})=\gamma^{n}(K)\int_{\omega}\bar{h}_{K}^{p}d\mu>0
.

If
I_{\mu}
attains a maximum at
\bar{h}_{K}
for some
K\in\mathcal{K}(C,\omega)
,
for any continuous function
f:\omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, the function
\left(\bar{h}_{K}^{p}+tf\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}
belongs to
C^{+}(\omega)
for sufficiently small
|t|
. By Lemma
3.5
, we obtain

0=\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}I_{\mu}(\left(\bar{h}_{K}^{p}+tf\right)^{\frac{1}{p}})=\gamma^{n}(K)\int_{\omega}fd\mu-\frac{1}{p}\int_{\omega}fdS_{p,\gamma^{n}}(K,\cdot)\int_{\omega}\bar{h}_{K}^{p}d\mu.

In other words,

\gamma^{n}(K)\int_{\omega}fd\mu=\frac{1}{p}\int_{\omega}fdS_{p,\gamma^{n}}(K,\cdot)\int_{\omega}\bar{h}_{K}^{p}d\mu

holds for all continuous functions
f
on
\omega
. Then we conclude that

\mu=\frac{\int_{\omega}\bar{h}_{K}^{p}d\mu}{p\gamma^{n}(K)}S_{p,\gamma^{n}}(K,\cdot).

(4.1)

Therefore, in what follows, we will show that
I_{\mu}
indeed attains its maximum at support function
\bar{h}_{K}
for some
K\in\mathcal{K}(C,\omega)
. From the preceding discussion, this is equivalent to
K
being a solution to the (normalized)
L_{p}
Gaussian Minkowski problem.

Lemma 4.2
.

If
p>0
, suppose
\omega\subset\Omega
is a nonempty compact subset.
For any nonzero finite Borel measure
\mu
on
\omega
, there exists a
C
-pseudo-cone
K\in\mathcal{K}(C,\omega)
such that

\mu=\frac{\int_{\omega}\bar{h}_{K}^{p}d\mu}{p\gamma^{n}(K)}S_{p,\gamma^{n}}(K,\cdot).

Proof.

For any
f\in C^{+}(\omega)
, we have
\bar{h}_{[f]}\geq f
. Consequently,

I_{\mu}(f)=\gamma^{n}([f])\int_{\omega}f^{p}d\mu\leq\gamma^{n}([f])\int_{\omega}\bar{h}_{[f]}^{p}d\mu=I_{\mu}(\bar{h}_{[f]}).

This shows that the supremum of
I_{\mu}(f)
is attained by the support functions of
\mathcal{K}(C,\omega)
.
Given a sequence
\{\bar{h}_{K_{i}}\}
, where each
K_{i}\in\mathcal{K}(C,\omega)
, satisfying

\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}I_{\mu}(\bar{h}_{K_{i}})=\sup\left\{I_{\mu}(f):f\in C^{+}\left(\omega\right)\right\}.

Define

r_{i}=\min\{r:rB\cap K_{i}\neq\emptyset\},

then we have

\bar{h}_{K_{i}}\leq r_{i}.

and

K_{i}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}-r_{i}B.

If there exists a subsequence, still denoted by
r_{i}
, such that
r_{i}\rightarrow\infty
, this leads to the estimate by Lemma
4.1
:

\displaystyle I_{\mu}(\bar{h}_{K_{i}})

\displaystyle=\gamma^{n}(K_{i})\int_{\omega}\bar{h}_{K_{i}}^{p}d\mu

\displaystyle\leq\gamma^{n}(\mathbb{R}^{n}-r_{i}B)\mu(\omega)r_{i}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c}{r_{i}}e^{-\frac{r_{i}^{2}}{2n}}\cdot r_{i}^{p}\rightarrow 0,

for some constant

\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}I_{\mu}(\bar{h}_{K_{i}})=\sup I_{\mu}
, since
I_{\mu}(\bar{h}_{K})>0
for each

.

Moreover, assume that
r_{i}\rightarrow 0
,
then we have

\gamma^{n}(\mathbb{R}^{n}-r_{i}B)\rightarrow 1,

and through the same estimate, we obtain

\displaystyle I_{\mu}(\bar{h}_{K_{i}})

\displaystyle=\gamma^{n}(K_{i})\int_{\omega}\bar{h}_{K_{i}}^{p}d\mu

\displaystyle\leq\gamma^{n}(\mathbb{R}^{n}-r_{i}B)\mu(\omega)r_{i}^{p}\rightarrow 0.

Similarly, we arrive at a contradiction.

This implies the uniform estimate

, for some constants

and

. From Lemma
2.2
, we can find a subsequence

, such that

for some

. Consequently, we get
\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}I_{\mu}(\bar{h}_{K_{i_{j}}})=I_{\mu}(\bar{h}_{K})=\sup I_{\mu}(f)
. By the preceding discussion (

),
the result is proved.

6.
uniqueness of solution

The Gaussian measure is log-concave with respect to convex sets (in this paper, we focus on a special class of convex sets—
C
-pseudo-cones), that is:

Lemma 6.1
.

For
C
-pseudo-cones
K
and
L
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
0<t<1
,

\gamma^{n}((1-t)K+tL)\geq\gamma^{n}(K)^{1-t}\gamma^{n}(L)^{t}

(6.1)

equality holds if and only if
K=L
.

Similar to the method for obtaining uniqueness in classical convex body theory, the following
L_{p}
“mixed volume” inequality is a necessary technique for establishing the uniqueness of the corresponding
L_{p}
Minkowski problem.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
\omega\subset\Omega
be a nonempty compact set and
p\in(0,1]
, for
K,L\in\mathcal{K}(C,\omega)
, we have

\frac{1}{p\gamma^{n}(K)}\int_{\omega}\bar{h}_{K}^{p}-\bar{h}_{L}^{p}dS_{p,\gamma^{n}}K\geq\log\frac{\gamma^{n}(L)}{\gamma^{n}(K)},

(6.2)

equality holds if and only if
K=L
.

Proof.

If
p\in(0,1]
,
f(t)=t^{p}
is a concave function, consequently, for
t\in[0,1]
,

\left((1-t)\bar{h}_{K}+t\bar{h}_{L}\right)^{p}\geq(1-t)\bar{h}_{K}^{p}+t\bar{h}_{L}^{p}.

In other words,

(1-t)\bar{h}_{K}+t\bar{h}_{L}\geq\left((1-t)\bar{h}_{K}^{p}+t\bar{h}_{L}^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

and

-\left((1-t)\bar{h}_{K}+t\bar{h}_{L}\right)\leq-\left((1-t)\bar{h}_{K}^{p}+t\bar{h}_{L}^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Therefore, we have the set inclusion

\displaystyle[\left((1-t)\bar{h}_{K}^{p}+t\bar{h}_{L}^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\big|_{\omega}]

\displaystyle=C\cap\bigcap_{u\in\omega}\left\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\langle y,u\rangle\leq-\left((1-t)\bar{h}_{K}(u)^{p}+t\bar{h}_{L}(u)^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\right\}

\displaystyle\supset C\cap\bigcap_{u\in\omega}\left\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\langle y,u\rangle\leq-\left((1-t)\bar{h}_{K}(u)+t\bar{h}_{L}(u)\right)\right\}

\displaystyle=[\bar{h}_{K}|_{\omega}+t(\bar{h}_{L}|_{\omega}-\bar{h}_{K}\big|_{\omega})]

\displaystyle\supset C\cap\bigcap_{u\in\Omega}\left\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\langle y,u\rangle\leq-\left((1-t)\bar{h}_{K}(u)+t\bar{h}_{L}(u)\right)\right\}

\displaystyle=(1-t)K+tL.

By the log-concave property in Lemma
6.1
we get

\displaystyle\log\gamma^{n}\left(\left[\left((1-t)\bar{h}_{K}^{p}+t\bar{h}_{L}^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\big|_{\omega}\right]\right)

\displaystyle\geq\log\gamma^{n}\left(\left[\bar{h}_{K}|_{\omega}+t(\bar{h}_{L}|_{\omega}-\bar{h}_{K}|_{\omega})\right]\right)

\displaystyle\geq\log\gamma^{n}((1-t)K+tL)

\displaystyle\geq(1-t)\log\gamma^{n}(K)+t\log\gamma^{n}(L).

Using the
L_{p}
variational formula in Lemma
3.5
, we differentiate both sides of the above inequality at
t=0^{+}
:

-\frac{1}{p\gamma^{n}(K)}\int_{\omega}\bar{h}_{L}^{p}-\bar{h}_{K}^{p}dS_{p,\gamma^{n}}K\geq\log\gamma^{n}(L)-\log\gamma^{n}(K).

Assuming equality holds, it follows that

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{\log\gamma^{n}\left(\left[\left((1-t)\bar{h}_{K}^{p}+t\bar{h}_{L}^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\big|_{\omega}\right]\right)-\log\gamma^{n}(K)}{t}

(6.3)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{\log\gamma^{n}\left([\bar{h}_{K}|_{\omega}+t(\bar{h}_{L}|_{\omega}-\bar{h}_{K}|_{\omega})]\right)-\log\gamma^{n}(K)}{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{\log\gamma^{n}((1-t)K+tL)-\log\gamma^{n}(K)}{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\log\gamma^{n}(L)-\log\gamma^{n}(K).

From the log-concave inequality (
6.1
), the function
k(t)=\log\left(\gamma^{n}((1-t)K+tL)\right)
is concave on the interval
[0,1]
. In the case where equality is achieved,
k^{\prime}(0)=k(1)-k(0)
in (
6.3
), then
k(t)
must be a linear function. As a result, the equality condition in (
6.1
) leads to the conclusion that
K=L
.
∎

Using the
L_{p}
mixed volume inequality, we can derive the
L_{p}
generalization of the uniqueness result for the Gaussian Minkowski problem for
C
-pseudo-cones in
[
35
]
.

Theorem 6.3
.

For
p\in(0,1]
, let
\omega\subset\Omega
be a nonempty compact set. If
K,L\in\mathcal{K}(C,\omega)
have the same
L_{p}
Gaussian surface area measure, i.e.,

S_{p,\gamma^{n}}(K,\cdot)=S_{p,\gamma^{n}}(L,\cdot),

and if
\gamma^{n}(K)=\gamma^{n}(L)
, then
K=L
.

Proof.

Since
\gamma^{n}(K)=\gamma^{n}(L)
, by Lemma
6.2
, we get

\int_{\omega}\bar{h}_{K}^{p}-\bar{h}_{L}^{p}dS_{p,\gamma^{n}}K\geq 0.

Therefore,

\int_{\omega}\bar{h}_{L}^{p}dS_{p,\gamma^{n}}L=\int_{\omega}\bar{h}_{L}^{p}dS_{p,\gamma^{n}}K\leq\int_{\omega}\bar{h}_{K}^{p}dS_{p,\gamma^{n}}K.

By substituting
K
for
L
and vice versa,

\displaystyle\int_{\omega}\bar{h}_{K}^{p}dS_{p,\gamma^{n}}K=\int_{\omega}\bar{h}_{K}^{p}dS_{p,\gamma^{n}}L\leq\int_{\omega}\bar{h}_{L}^{p}dS_{p,\gamma^{n}}L.

Thus,

\int_{\omega}\bar{h}_{L}^{p}dS_{p,\gamma^{n}}L\leq\int_{\omega}\bar{h}_{K}^{p}dS_{p,\gamma^{n}}K\leq\int_{\omega}\bar{h}_{L}^{p}dS_{p,\gamma^{n}}L.

As a result, equality holds in both inequalities. By
Lemma
6.2
, we conclude that
K=L
.
∎

In the absence of a volume constraint, the following lemma extends the non-uniqueness of the Gaussian Minkowski problem to the
L_{p}
setting.

Theorem 6.4
.

If
p<n
, for any pointed,
n
-dimensional closed convex cone
C
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and any nonempty compact set
\omega\subset\Omega
, there exist distinct sets
K,L\in\mathcal{K}(C,\omega)
such that their
L_{p}
Gaussian surface area measures coincide, i.e.,
S_{p,\gamma^{n}}(K,\cdot)=S_{p,\gamma^{n}}(L,\cdot)
, but
K\neq L
.

Proof.

For each
v\in\omega
, due to the rotational invariance of the Gaussian measure, suppose that
v=-e_{n}\in\Omega
. Let
H(t)
denote the intersection
C\cap H(e_{n},t)
, and define

A=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}:(x,1)\in H(1)\}.

For
t>0
, the Gaussian surface area function
s(t)
of
H(t)
(up to a constant) is given by
s(t)=\int_{H(t)}e^{-\frac{|y|^{2}}{2}}d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(y)
.
Let
B_{a}^{n-1}
be a ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
with radius
a
, the Gaussian volume estimate of
tB_{a}^{n-1}
, as
t\rightarrow 0
, we know

\int_{tB_{a}^{n-1}}e^{-\frac{|y|^{2}}{2}}d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(y)\sim t^{n-1},\quad\quad t\rightarrow 0.

(6.4)

Since

\displaystyle\int_{tB_{a}^{n-1}}e^{-\frac{|y|^{2}}{2}}d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(y)=\int_{S^{n-2}}\int_{0}^{at}e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{2}}r^{n-2}drdu=\mathcal{H}^{n-2}(S^{n-2})\int_{0}^{at}e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{2}}r^{n-2}dr,

and

\frac{\int_{0}^{at}e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{2}}r^{n-2}dr}{t^{n-1}}\rightarrow\frac{a^{n-1}}{n-1}\quad\text{as}\quad t\rightarrow 0.

Then we get the estimate (
6.4
). There exists a ball
B_{a}^{n-1}
such that
A\subset B_{a}^{n-1}
, therefore, for
t>0
,

\displaystyle 0<t^{1-p}s(t)

\displaystyle=t^{1-p}\int_{H(t)}e^{-\frac{|y|^{2}}{2}}d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(y)

\displaystyle=t^{1-p}\int_{(x,t)\in H(t)}e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}+t^{2}}{2}}d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)

\displaystyle=t^{1-p}e^{-\frac{t^{2}}{2}}\int_{tA}e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{2}}d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)

\displaystyle\leq t^{1-p}e^{-\frac{t^{2}}{2}}\int_{B_{a}^{n-1}}e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{2}}d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)

\displaystyle\sim t^{1-p}\cdot t^{n-1}=t^{n-p}\quad\text{as}\quad t\rightarrow 0.

If
p<n
, we obtain
t^{1-p}s(t)\rightarrow 0
as
t\rightarrow 0
. If
t\to\infty
, the convergence rate of
s(t)
is determined by
e^{-\frac{t^{2}}{2}}
, and thus
t^{1-p}s(t)\rightarrow 0
. Then there exist positive numbers
t_{1},t_{2}>0
, with
t_{1}\neq t_{2}
, such that
t_{1}^{1-p}s(t_{1})=t_{2}^{1-p}s(t_{2})
.

Define
K=H(t_{1})+C
and
L=H(t_{2})+C
. Both
K
and
L
belong to the class
\mathcal{K}(C,v)\subset\mathcal{K}(C,\omega)
. We then compute

S_{p,\gamma^{n}}(K,v)=\bar{h}_{K}^{1-p}(v)S_{\gamma^{n}}(K,v)=\frac{1}{(\sqrt{2\pi})^{n}}t_{1}^{1-p}s(t_{1})

and similarly,

S_{p,\gamma^{n}}(L,v)=\bar{h}_{L}^{1-p}(v)S_{\gamma^{n}}(L,v)=\frac{1}{(\sqrt{2\pi})^{n}}t_{2}^{1-p}s(t_{2}).

Since the measures
S_{p,\gamma^{n}}(K,\cdot)
and
S_{p,\gamma^{n}}(L,\cdot)
are both supported on
v
, it follows that
S_{p,\gamma^{n}}(K,\cdot)=S_{p,\gamma^{n}}(L,\cdot)
.
This completes the proof of the desired result.
∎

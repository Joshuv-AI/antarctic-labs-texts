Abstract
. This paper focuses on the asymptotic stability of the spectra of generalized indefinite strings (GISs). A unitarily equivalent linear relation is introduced for GISs. It is shown that the solutions of the corresponding differential equations are continuously dependent on distributions and measures under certain conditions. Using these results, the convergence of unitarily equivalent linear relations for GISs is discussed. By the perturbation theory to closed linear relations, an asymptotic stability result concerning the spectra of linear relations for GISs is obtained.

1.
Introduction

A generalized indefinite string (GIS, for short) is a triple
(L,\omega,\nu)
such that
L\in(0,\infty]
,
\omega
is a real-valued distribution in
H_{\rm loc}^{-1}[0,L)
and
\nu
is a non-negative Borel measure on
[0,L)
. Associated with such a triple is the ordinary differential equation of the form

(1.1)

-f^{\prime\prime}=z\omega f+z^{2}\nu f.

on
[0,L)
, where
z
is a complex spectral parameter. The spectral problems of this equation have attracted significant attention from both mathematicians and physicists. Firstly, they constitute a canonical model for operators with a simple spectrum
[
6
,
7
]
. Secondly, they play an important role in the connection with certain completely integrable nonlinear wave equations, such as the Camassa-Holm equation
[
1
]
, where these spectral problems arise as isospectral problems
[
4
,
5
,
12
]
.

J. Eckhardt and his collaborators have done extensive work on GISs
[
6
,
7
,
8
,
9
,
11
]
. They established in
[
6
]
that there exists a homeomorphism between the set of all GISs and the set of all Herglotz-Nevanlinna functions. Very recently, they obtained a couple of Szegő-type theorems for this correspondence in
[
9
]
. Utilizing these results, the stability results for the absolutely continuous spectra of some model examples of GISs are established
[
8
,
10
]
. It is noteworthy that the spectrum of a GIS is defined as the spectrum of its corresponding linear relation and some stability results concerning the spectra of linear relations under certain perturbations have been obtained in
[
16
,
17
]
. The aim of this paper is to discuss the further problem:
What is the behaviour of the spectra of
T
if the distribution
\omega
and Borel measure
\nu
change
?

It is worth mentioning that G. Meng and M. Zhang
[
15
]
studied the dependence of solutions and eigenvalues of second-order linear measure differential equations on measures, obtaining two fundamental results regarding continuity and continuous Fréchet differentiability in measures under the weak
∗
topology and the norm topology of total variations, respectively. Recently, J. Chu and his collaborators
[
2
]
proved that the eigenvalues for periodic generalized Camassa-Holm equations are continuous in potentials under the weak topology and provided some estimates for them.

In the present paper, we consider the regular case of GISs
[
7
]
. Without loss of generality, we assume that
L=1
. Note that the associated relation
T
of a GIS
(1,\omega,\nu)
belongs to the Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}^{2}
. For the definition of
\mathcal{H}
, see (
2.7
). Since
{\mathcal{H}}
depends on
\nu
, we can not directly discuss the relationship between the perturbation of
T
and the variations of
\omega
and
\nu
. The key idea here is to introduce a new Hilbert space
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}
and a new linear relation
\widetilde{T}
, which is unitarily equivalent to
T
under some conditions. We then study the relationship between the perturbation of
\widetilde{T}
and the variations of
\omega
and
\nu
. Together with the stability results concerning the spectra of linear relations in
[
16
,
17
]
, this implies the perturbation results of the spectrum of
\widetilde{T}
. Therefore, the perturbation results of the spectrum of
T
can be obtained since unitarily equivalent relations have the same spectral structure.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we recall some basic concepts and fundamental results on the self-adjoint relation
T
associated with a GIS, and introduce a unitary operator and a unitarily equivalent relation
\widetilde{T}
of
T
. Additionally, the relationship between the generalized convergence and the norm resolvent convergence of relations is established. In Section 3, we provide properties of solutions of the corresponding differential equation and discuss the continuous dependence of solutions. For
n\geq 1
, let
T_{n}
be the self-adjoint relations of
(1,\omega_{n},\nu_{n})
and
\widetilde{T}_{n}
be their unitarily equivalent relations. We prove that
\{\widetilde{T}_{n}\}
converges to
\widetilde{T}
in the sense of norm resolvent convergence when
\{\omega_{n}\}
and
\{\nu_{n}\}
converge to
\omega
and
\nu
in some sense, respectively. By applying the small gap perturbation result for the spectra of closed linear relations in
[
16
]
, we obtain an asymptotic stability result about the spectra of linear relations for GISs on distributions and measures.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we recall some notations and basic concepts, and give some fundamental results about generalized indefinite strings (GISs). In particular, we give some properties of Borel measures and self-adjoint relations associated with GISs.

2.1.
Generalized indefinite strings

Let
L=1
, we consider the regular case of GISs. First we introduce the following spaces

\begin{array}[]{rrll}H_{\rm loc}^{1}[0,1)&:=&\{f\in AC_{\rm loc}[0,1):\,f^{\prime}\in L_{\rm loc}^{2}[0,1)\},\\[3.44444pt]
H^{1}[0,1)&:=&\{f\in H_{\rm loc}^{1}[0,1):\,f,\,f^{\prime}\in L^{2}[0,1)\},\\[3.44444pt]
H_{c}^{1}[0,1)&:=&\{f\in H^{1}[0,1):\,\rm{supp}(f)\mbox{ is compact in }\,[0,1)\}.\\[2.58334pt]
\end{array}

The space of distributions
H_{\rm loc}^{-1}[0,1)
is the topological dual of
H_{c}^{1}[0,1)
. In fact, any
\mathbf{q}\in L_{\rm loc}^{2}[0,1)
corresponds to a distribution

\chi\in H_{\rm loc}^{-1}[0,1)
,
defined by

\chi(h):=-\int_{0}^{1}\mathbf{q}(x)h^{\prime}(x)dx,\qquad h\in H_{c}^{1}[0,1).

Moreover, the mapping
\mathbf{q}\rightarrow\chi

is a one-to-one correspondence between
L_{\rm loc}^{2}[0,1)
and
H_{\rm loc}^{-1}[0,1)
.
The function
\mathbf{q}\in L_{\rm loc}^{2}[0,1)
is referred to as the
normalized anti-derivative
of the distribution
\chi\in H_{\rm loc}^{-1}[0,1)
.
We say that
\chi
is real-valued if
\mathbf{q}
is real-valued almost everywhere on
[0,1)
.

A special class of distributions in
H_{\rm loc}^{-1}[0,1)
consists of all Borel measures on
[0,1)
. When
\chi
is a complex-valued Borel measure on
[0,1)
, the corresponding distribution in
H_{\rm loc}^{-1}[0,1)
is given by the following linear functional

h\mapsto\int_{[0,1)}hd\chi,\qquad h\in H_{c}^{1}[0,1),

whose normalized anti-derivative
\mathbf{q}
is the following left-continuous function

(2.1)

\mathbf{q}(x)=\int_{[0,x)}d\chi,\qquad\mbox{a.e. }x\in[0,1).

Associated with a GIS
(1,\omega,\nu)
is the differential equation

(2.2)

-f^{\prime\prime}=z\omega f+z^{2}\nu f,

where
z
is a complex spectral parameter. This differential equation has to be understood in a weak sense. A
solution
of (
2.2
) is a function
f\in H_{\rm loc}^{1}[0,1)
such that

\Delta_{f}h(0)+\int_{0}^{1}f^{\prime}(x)h^{\prime}(x)dx=z\omega(fh)+z^{2}\int_{[0,1)}f(t)h(t)d\nu(t),

for all
h\in H_{c}^{1}[0,1)
and some constant
\Delta_{f}\in{\mathbb{C}}
. In this case, the constant
\Delta_{f}
is denoted by
f^{\prime}(0-)
via the integration by parts formula.

Let
\mathbf{u}
be the normalized anti-derivatives of the distributions
z\omega+z^{2}\nu
. Then a function
f\in H_{\rm loc}^{1}[0,1)
is a solution of (
2.2
) if and only if

(2.3)

f^{\prime}(x)+\mathbf{u}(x)f(x)=\Delta_{f}+\int_{0}^{x}\mathbf{u}(t)f^{\prime}(t)dt,

for almost everywhere
x\in[0,1)
. Here
\Delta_{f}\in{\mathbb{C}}
is the same as in the definition above.

The Wronski determinant
W(\theta,\phi)
of two solutions
\theta,\phi
of (
2.2
) is defined by

W(\theta,\phi)=\theta(0)\phi^{\prime}(0-)-\theta^{\prime}(0-)\phi(0).

For more details on the Wronski determinant, see
[
6
, Section 3]
.

From now on, for any GIS
(1,\omega,\nu)
, we always assume that
\nu
satisfies the following basic hypothesis unless otherwise stated.

Hypothesis (0).

\nu
is a positive and finite Borel measure on
[0,1)
, and absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure.

By
[
18
, Theorem 3.3.13]
, there exists an almost everywhere positive measurable function
p(x)
such that

(2.4)

\int_{[0,1)}fdv=\int_{0}^{1}(fp)dx,\;f\in L^{1}([0,1);\nu).

The function
p
is called the
Radon-Nikodym derivative
of
\nu
with respect to the Lebesgue measure. Define an operator
P:L^{2}([0,1);\nu)\to L^{2}[0,1)
by

(2.5)

Pf:=f\sqrt{p},\;\;f\in L^{2}([0,1);\nu),

where
p
is the Radon-Nikodym derivative of
\nu
. Obviously, the operator
P
is well-defined by (
2.4
).

Lemma 2.1
.

P
is a unitary operator from
L^{2}([0,1);\nu)
to
L^{2}[0,1)
.

Proof
For any
f\in L^{2}([0,1);\nu)
, by (
2.4
) we have

\|Pf\|_{L^{2}[0,1)}^{2}=\int_{0}^{1}|f\sqrt{p}|^{2}dx=\int_{0}^{1}|f|^{2}pdx=\int_{[0,1)}|f|^{2}dv=\|f\|_{L^{2}([0,1);\nu)}^{2},

where
p
is the Radon-Nikodym derivative of
\nu
. This implies that
P
is an isometry.

For any
h\in L^{2}[0,1)
, let
f:=\frac{h}{\sqrt{p}}
. Again by (
2.4
), we have

\int_{[0,1)}|f|^{2}dv=\int_{[0,1)}\frac{|h|^{2}}{p}dv=\int_{0}^{1}|h|^{2}dx<\infty.

This implies that
f\in L^{2}([0,1);\nu)
, and
Pf=h
. Then
P
is surjective. Thus,
P
is a unitary operator from
L^{2}([0,1);\nu)
to
L^{2}[0,1)
. The proof is complete.

\Box

For
n\geq 1
, we assume that
p
and
p_{n}
are the Radon-Nikodym derivatives of
\nu
and
\nu_{n}
. The following hypothesis on Radon-Nikodym derivatives is central to this paper.

Hypothesis (1).

There exists a non-negative integrable function g on
[0,1)
such that
p_{n}\leq g
, a.e., and
p_{n}\xrightarrow{a.e.}p
as
n\to\infty
.

The following result can be directly derived from Lebesgue’s Dominated Convergence Theorem and
[
18
, Theorem 3.4.6]
. We omit its proof.

Lemma 2.2
.

Assume that Hypothesis
(1)
holds. Then

\int_{0}^{1}\left|\sqrt{p_{n}}-\sqrt{p}\right|^{2}dx\to 0,\;\int_{0}^{1}\left|p_{n}-p\right|dx\to 0\;\;as\;\;n\to\infty.

The following hypothesis on distributions is also central to this paper. First we introduce a definition of the convergence of distributions.

Definition 2.3
.

For
n\geq 1
, let
\omega_{n},\ \omega\in H_{\rm loc}^{-1}[0,L)
. We say that
\omega_{n}
converges to
\omega
in the sense of anti-derivative
L^{2}
convergence if
{\mathbf{w}_{n}}
converges to
{\mathbf{w}}
in
L^{2}[0,1)
, that is,

(2.6)

\int_{0}^{1}|{\mathbf{w}_{n}}-{\mathbf{w}}|^{2}dx\to 0,\;\;\mbox{as}\;\;n\to\infty,

where
{\mathbf{w}}
and
{\mathbf{w}_{n}}
are the anti-derivatives of
\omega
and
\omega_{n}
.

Hypothesis (2).

\omega_{n}
converges to
\omega
in the sense of anti-derivative
L^{2}
convergence.

2.2.
Self-adjoint linear relations associated with generalized indefinite strings

To study the GIS
(1,\omega,\nu)
, we recall from
[
6
]
the following space

H^{\prime}[0,1):=\{f\in H_{\rm loc}^{1}[0,1):\,f^{\prime}\in L^{2}[0,1),\;\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 1^{-}}f(x)=0\}

as well as the linear space

(2.7)

\mathcal{H}:=H^{\prime}_{*}[0,1)\times L^{2}([0,1);\nu).

Here

H^{\prime}_{*}[0,1)=\{f\in H^{\prime}[0,1):\,f(0)=0\}.

Note that
\mathcal{H}
is a Hilbert space equipped with the scalar product

\langle f,g\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}:=\int_{0}^{1}f^{\prime}_{1}(x)g^{\prime}_{1}(x)^{*}dx+\int_{[0,1)}f_{2}(x)g_{2}(x)^{*}d\nu(x).

For any pair
(f,g)\in\mathcal{H}\times\mathcal{H}
, where
f=(f_{1},f_{2})\in\mathcal{H}
and
g=(g_{1},g_{2})\in\mathcal{H}
, we use the following equation

(2.8)

-f^{\prime\prime}_{1}=\omega g_{1}+\nu g_{2},\qquad\nu f_{2}=\nu g_{1},

to define a linear subspace
T
of
\mathcal{H}\times\mathcal{H}
. It is also called a linear relation or a multi-valued linear operator on
\mathcal{H}
and is denoted by
T\in LR(\mathcal{H})
. Moreover,
T
is a self-adjoint linear relation in
\mathcal{H}
; see
[
6
, Theorem 4.1]
.

Let

(2.9)

\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}:=H^{\prime}_{*}[0,1)\times L^{2}[0,1).

Then
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}
is a Hilbert space equipped with the inner product

\langle f,g\rangle_{\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}}:=\int_{0}^{1}f^{\prime}_{1}(x)g^{\prime}_{1}(x)^{*}dx+\int_{0}^{1}f_{2}(x)g_{2}(x)^{*}dx,

where
f=(f_{1},f_{2})\in\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}
and
g=(g_{1},g_{2})\in\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}
. Define the operator
Q:\mathcal{H}\to\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}
by

(2.10)

Qf:=(f_{1},Pf_{2}),\;\;f=(f_{1},f_{2})\in\mathcal{H}.

By Lemma
2.1
, we have the following result.

Lemma 2.4
.

Q
is a unitary operator from
\mathcal{H}
to
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}
.

Let
\widetilde{T}:=QTQ^{-1}=QTQ^{*}
. Then
\widetilde{T}
is a self-adjoint linear relation in
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}
. In particular,
\widetilde{T}
and
T
have the same spectral structure. By
[
6
, Proposition 4.3]
, we obtain the following result.

Lemma 2.5
.

If
z
belongs to the resolvent set
\rho(\widetilde{T})
, then

z(\widetilde{T}-z)^{-1}g(x)=Q\left(\langle g,Q\mathcal{G}(x,\cdot)^{*}\rangle_{\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}1\\
z\\
\end{array}\right)-g_{1}(x)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}1\\
0\\
\end{array}\right)\right),\;x\in[0,1),

for every
g\in\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}
, where the Green’s function
\mathcal{G}
is given by

\mathcal{G}(x,t)=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}1\\
z\\
\end{array}\right)\frac{1}{W(\psi_{1},\psi_{2})}\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\psi_{1}(x)\psi_{2}(t),&t\in[0,x),\\
\psi_{1}(t)\psi_{2}(x)&t\in[x,1),\end{array}\right.

and
\psi_{1},\psi_{2}
are linearly independent solutions of (
2.2
) such that
\psi_{2}(0)=0
,
\psi_{1}\in H^{\prime}[0,1)
and
z\psi_{1}\in L^{2}([0,1);\nu)
.

At the end of this subsection, we present some properties of linear relations
[
14
]
. For two closed linear relations
S,\ T
in a Hilbert space
X
, the
gap
is defined by

\hat{\delta}(S,T):=\max\{\delta(S,T),\,\delta(T,S)\},\vskip-5.69046pt

where

\delta(S,T):=\sup\{{\rm dist}(\varphi,\,T):\,\varphi\in S,\,\|\varphi\|=1\}.

When either
S
or
T
is
\{0\}
, the gap is understood as
0
. Obviously,

\hat{\delta}(S^{-1},T^{-1})=\hat{\delta}(S,T).

Let
T,T_{n}\in LR(X)
be closed. We say that
T_{n}
converges to
T

in the generalized sense
if
\hat{\delta}(T_{n},T)\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. Furthermore, if
\rho(T)\cap\rho(T_{n})\neq\emptyset
for sufficiently large
n
, we say that
T_{n}
converges to
T

in the sense of norm resolvent convergence
if there exists a
z\in\rho(T)\cap\rho(T_{n})
for sufficiently large
n
such that
\|(T_{n}-z)^{-1}-(T-z)^{-1}\|\to 0
as
n\to\infty
.

The following properties about the gap of linear relations were introduced in
[
16
]
. For the concept of the norm of a linear relation, see
[
3
]
.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
S,T\in LR(X)
be closed and
A\in LR(X)
be bounded. If
D(T)\cup D(S)\subset D(A)

and
A(0)\subset T(0)\cap S(0)
, then

\hat{\delta}(S+A,T+A)\leq 2(1+\|A\|^{2})\hat{\delta}(S,T).

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
T\in LR(X)
be closed and
A\in LR(X)
be bounded. If
D(T)\subset D(A)

and
A(0)\subset T(0)
, then

\hat{\delta}(T+A,T)\leq\|A\|.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
T,T_{n}\in LR(X)
be closed and
\rho(T)\cap\rho(T_{n})\neq\emptyset
for sufficiently large
n
. If
T_{n}
converges to
T
in the sense of norm resolvent convergence, then
T_{n}
converges to
T
in the generalized sense.

Proof
Let
z\in\rho(T)\cap\rho(T_{n})
for sufficiently large
n
. It follows from Lemma
2.7
that when
n\to\infty
, we have

\displaystyle\hat{\delta}((T_{n}-z)^{-1},(T-z)^{-1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{\delta}((T_{n}-z)^{-1}-(T-z)^{-1}+(T-z)^{-1},(T-z)^{-1})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|(T_{n}-z)^{-1}-(T-z)^{-1}\|\to 0.

Therefore, by Lemma
2.6
, we obtain

\hat{\delta}(T_{n},T)=\hat{\delta}(T_{n}-z+z,T-z+z)\leq 2(1+|z|^{2})\hat{\delta}(T_{n}-z,T-z)\to 0.

This implies that
T_{n}
converges to
T
in the generalized sense. The proof is complete.

\Box

Lemma 2.9
.

[
16
, Theorem 4.1]

Let
T\in LR(X)
be closed and
\Gamma
be a compact subset of the resolvent set
\rho(T)
. Then there exists a
\delta>0
such that
\Gamma\subset\rho(S)
for any closed relation
S
with
\hat{\delta}(S,T)<\delta

3.
Main results

In this section, we first present some properties of solutions of (
2.2
), including existence and uniqueness, as well as the bounds for these solutions, and their continuous dependence on distributions and measures. Next, we investigate the asymptotic stability of the spectra of generalized indefinite strings, and show that the spectrum of a GIS
(1,\omega,\nu)
is upper semi-continuous with respect to
\omega
and
\nu
in a certain sense.

3.1.
Properties of solutions of the differential equation (2.2)

For
n\geq 1
, let
(1,\omega,\nu),(1,\omega_{n},\nu_{n})
be GISs where
{\mathbf{w}}
and
{\mathbf{w}_{n}}
are the anti-derivatives of
\omega
and
\omega_{n}
. For any
d_{1},d_{2}\in{\mathbb{C}}
, the basic existence and uniqueness results for (
2.2
) with the initial conditions

(3.1)

f(0)=d_{1}\;\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;\;f^{\prime}(0-)=d_{2},

are available; see
[
6
, Lemma 3.2]
. The following result establishes the existence and uniqueness of solutions to initial value problems when the initial condition is specified at the endpoint
1
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Assume that
{\mathbf{w}}\in L^{2}[0,1)
and
\nu
is finite. Then for any
a\in{\mathbb{C}}
, there exists a unique solution
f
of (
2.2
) with the initial conditions

(3.2)

f(1-)=0\;\;\;and\;\;\;f^{\prime}(1-)=a.

Proof
Since
{\mathbf{w}}\in L^{2}[0,1)
and
\nu
is finite, it is easy to obtain that

(3.3)

\int_{0}^{1}|{\mathbf{u}}|^{2}dx<\infty,

where
\mathbf{u}
is the normalized anti-derivatives of
z\omega+z^{2}\nu
. Consequently,

(3.4)

\int_{0}^{1}|{\mathbf{u}}|dx\leq\left(\int_{0}^{1}|{\mathbf{u}}|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}<\infty.

Let
F=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}f\\
f^{\prime}+{\mathbf{u}}f\\
\end{array}\right)
and
R=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}-{\mathbf{u}}&1\\
-{\mathbf{u}}^{2}&{\mathbf{u}}\\
\end{array}\right)
. Then the differential equation (
2.2
) with (
3.2
) is equivalent to the initial value problem

(3.5)

F^{\prime}=RF,\;\;F(1-)=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}0\\
a\\
\end{array}\right).

By (
3.3
) and (
3.4
), we have

(3.6)

\int_{0}^{1}|R|dx=1+2\int_{0}^{1}|{\mathbf{u}}|dx+\int_{0}^{1}|{\mathbf{u}}|^{2}dx<\infty,

where
|R|
is a norm of the matrix
R
. Then there exists a unique solution
F
of (
3.5
) by
[
19
, Theorem 1.5.3]
, which implies that there exists a unique solution
f
of (
2.2
)–(
3.2
). The proof is complete.

\Box

Now, we investigate the bounds for solutions of (
2.2
).

Lemma 3.2
.

For
d_{1},d_{2},a\in{\mathbb{C}}
, let
f
is the solution of (
2.2
) with (
3.1
) or (
3.2
). Assume that
{\mathbf{w}}\in L^{2}[0,1)
and
\nu
is finite. Then
f(x)
is uniformly bounded on
[0,1]
and

(3.7)

\int_{0}^{1}|f^{\prime}(x)|^{2}dx<\infty.

Proof
Let
{\mathbf{u}}
,
F
, and
R
be as in the proof of Lemma
3.1
. By (
3.6
) and
[
19
, Theorem 1.5.1 and Theorem 1.5.2]
, we have that
F
is uniformly bounded on
[0,1]
. This implies that both
f
and
f^{\prime}+{\mathbf{u}}f
are uniformly bounded on
[0,1]
. Consequently, there exists a constant
M
such that
|f(x)|<M
for all
x\in[0.1]
and
\int_{0}^{1}|f^{\prime}+{\mathbf{u}}f|^{2}dx<\infty
. Then we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}|f^{\prime}(x)|^{2}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}|f^{\prime}+{\mathbf{u}}f-{\mathbf{u}}f|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\int_{0}^{1}|f^{\prime}+{\mathbf{u}}f|^{2}dx+2\int_{0}^{1}|{\mathbf{u}}|^{2}|f|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\int_{0}^{1}|f^{\prime}+{\mathbf{u}}f|^{2}dx+2M^{2}\int_{0}^{1}|{\mathbf{u}}|^{2}dx<\infty.

This concludes the proof.

\Box

Next, we show that the solutions of (
2.2
) with (
3.1
) or (
3.2
) are continuously dependent on the distribution
\omega
and the Borel measure
\nu
under certain conditions.

Theorem 3.3
.

For
d_{1},d_{2}\in{\mathbb{C}}
, let
f
be the solution of (
2.2
) with (
3.1
). Assume that Hypotheses
(1)
and
(2)
hold. Then the solutions
f_{n}
of the differential equation

(3.8)

-f_{n}^{\prime\prime}=z\omega_{n}f_{n}+z^{2}\nu_{n}f_{n}

with the initial conditions (
3.1
)
converges to
f
uniformly on
[0,1]
, and

(3.9)

\int_{0}^{1}|f^{\prime}_{n}(x)-f^{\prime}(x)|^{2}dx\to 0\;\;\mbox{as}\;\;n\to\infty.

Proof
Let
{\mathbf{q}}
and
{\mathbf{q}_{n}}
be the anti-derivatives of
\nu
and
\nu_{n}
. By Lemma
2.2
, we have

\displaystyle|{\mathbf{q}_{n}}(x)-{\mathbf{q}}(x)|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\int_{[0,x)}d\nu_{n}-\int_{[0,x)}d\nu\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{x}(p_{n}-p)dt\right|\leq\int_{0}^{1}|p_{n}-p|dt\to 0,\;\;\mbox{as}\;\;n\to\infty.

This implies that

(3.10)

\int_{0}^{1}|{\mathbf{u}_{n}}-{\mathbf{u}}|^{2}dx\to 0,\;\;\mbox{as}\;\;n\to\infty,

and (
3.3
) holds, where
{\mathbf{u}}
and
{\mathbf{u}_{n}}
are the anti-derivatives of
z\omega+z^{2}\nu
and
z\omega_{n}+z^{2}\nu_{n}
. Thus

(3.11)

\int_{0}^{1}|{\mathbf{u}_{n}}|^{2}dx\leq 2\int_{0}^{1}|{\mathbf{u}_{n}}-{\mathbf{u}}|^{2}dx+2\int_{0}^{1}|{\mathbf{u}}|^{2}dx<\infty.

Let
F,R
be as in the proof of Lemma
3.1
and

F_{n}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}f_{n}\\
f^{\prime}_{n}+{\mathbf{u}_{n}}f_{n}\\
\end{array}\right),\;\;\;R_{n}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}-{\mathbf{u}_{n}}&1\\
-{\mathbf{u}_{n}}^{2}&{\mathbf{u}_{n}}\\
\end{array}\right).

Then
F_{n}
is a solution to the initial value problem

F_{n}^{\prime}=R_{n}F_{n},\;\;\;F_{n}(0)=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}d_{1}\\
d_{2}\\
\end{array}\right).

By (
3.3
), (
3.10
) and (
3.11
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}|R_{n}-R|dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\int_{0}^{1}|{\mathbf{u}_{n}}-{\mathbf{u}}|dx+\int_{0}^{1}|{\mathbf{u}_{n}}^{2}-{\mathbf{u}}^{2}|dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\left(\int_{0}^{1}|{\mathbf{u}_{n}}-{\mathbf{u}}|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\left(\int_{0}^{1}|{\mathbf{u}_{n}}-{\mathbf{u}}|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{0}^{1}|{\mathbf{u}_{n}}|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{1}|{\mathbf{u}_{n}}-{\mathbf{u}}|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{0}^{1}|{\mathbf{u}}|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\to 0,\;\;\mbox{as}\;\;n\to\infty.

It follows from
[
19
, Theorem 1.6.3]
that
F_{n}
converges to
F
uniformly on
[0,1]
. Both
f_{n}
and
f^{\prime}_{n}+{\mathbf{u}_{n}}f_{n}
converge to
f
and
f^{\prime}+{\mathbf{u}}f
uniformly on
[0,1]
. Then

(3.12)

\int_{0}^{1}|f^{\prime}_{n}+{\mathbf{u}_{n}}f_{n}-f^{\prime}-{\mathbf{u}}f|^{2}dx\to 0,\;\;\mbox{as}\;\;n\to\infty.

It follows from Lemma
3.2
, (
3.10
) and (
3.11
) that for any
\epsilon>0
, there exist
N,M>0
such that when
n>N
, we have

\int_{0}^{1}|{\mathbf{u}_{n}}-{\mathbf{u}}|^{2}dx<\epsilon,\;\;\;\;|f_{n}(x)-f(x)|^{2}<\epsilon,\;\;x\in[0,1),

and

\int_{0}^{1}|{\mathbf{u}_{n}}|^{2}dx<M,\;\;\;\;|f(x)|^{2}<M,\;\;x\in[0,1).

Hence, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}|{\mathbf{u}_{n}}f_{n}-{\mathbf{u}}f|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\int_{0}^{1}|{\mathbf{u}_{n}}f_{n}-{\mathbf{u}_{n}}f|^{2}dx+2\int_{0}^{1}|{\mathbf{u}_{n}}f-{\mathbf{u}}f|^{2}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\int_{0}^{1}|{\mathbf{u}_{n}}|^{2}|f_{n}-f|^{2}dx+2\int_{0}^{1}|{\mathbf{u}_{n}}-{\mathbf{u}}|^{2}|f|^{2}dx<4M\epsilon,

which yields that

\int_{0}^{1}|{\mathbf{u}_{n}}f_{n}-{\mathbf{u}}f|^{2}dx\to 0,\;\;\mbox{as}\;\;n\to\infty.

This together with (
3.12
) implies that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}|f^{\prime}_{n}(x)-f^{\prime}(x)|^{2}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}|f^{\prime}_{n}(x)+{\mathbf{u}_{n}}f_{n}-{\mathbf{u}_{n}}f_{n}+{\mathbf{u}}f-{\mathbf{u}}f-f^{\prime}(x)|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\int_{0}^{1}|f^{\prime}_{n}+{\mathbf{u}_{n}}f_{n}-f^{\prime}-{\mathbf{u}}f|^{2}dx+2\int_{0}^{1}|{\mathbf{u}_{n}}f_{n}-{\mathbf{u}}f|^{2}dx\to 0.

Then (
3.9
) holds. This concludes the proof.

\Box

3.2.
Asymptotic stability of the spectra of generalized indefinite strings

For
n\geq 1
, let
(1,\omega,\nu)
,
(1,\omega_{n},\nu_{n})
be GISs with the corresponding self-adjoint linear relations
T
and
T_{n}
, and
\mathcal{H}_{n}:=H^{\prime}_{*}[0,1)\times L^{2}([0,1);\nu_{n})
. Define the operator
Q_{n}:\mathcal{H}_{n}\to\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}
by

(3.13)

Q_{n}f:=(f_{1},P_{n}f_{2}),\;\;f=(f_{1},f_{2})\in\mathcal{H}_{n},

where
P_{n}f_{2}=\sqrt{p_{n}}f_{2}
. Set
\widetilde{T}_{n}:=Q_{n}T_{n}Q^{-1}_{n}=Q_{n}T_{n}Q_{n}^{*}
. Then
\widetilde{T}_{n}
is unitarily equivalent to
T_{n}
.

Theorem 3.5
.

Assume that Hypotheses
(1)
and
(2)
hold. Then
\widetilde{T}_{n}
converges to
\widetilde{T}
in the sense of norm resolvent convergence.

Proof
Let
\psi_{i},\,i=1,2
be linearly independent solutions of (
2.2
) such that

(3.14)

\psi_{1}(0)=1,\,\psi^{\prime}_{1}(0-)=d,\,\psi_{2}(0)=0,\,\psi^{\prime}_{2}(0-)=1,

with
\psi_{1}\in H^{\prime}[0,1)
and
z\psi_{1}\in L^{2}([0,1);\nu)
. Then
\psi_{1}(1-)=0
. Assume that
\psi^{\prime}_{1}(1-)=a
.
Let
\phi_{n},\psi_{n,2}
be solutions of (
3.8
) such that

(3.15)

\phi_{n}(1-)=0,\,\phi^{\prime}_{n}(1-)=a,\,\psi_{n,2}(0)=0,\,\psi^{\prime}_{n,2}(0-)=1.

It follows from Remark
3.4
that
\phi_{n}(x)
converges to
\psi_{1}(x)
uniformly on
[0,1]
as
n\to\infty
. Let
\psi_{n,1}:=\frac{\phi_{n}}{\phi_{n}(0)}
. Then
\psi_{n,1}(x)
converges to
\psi_{1}(x)
uniformly on
[0,1]
, and
\psi_{n,1}(1-)=0
,
\psi_{n,1}(0)=1
. Consequently,

W(\psi_{1},\psi_{2})=W(\psi_{n,1},\psi_{n,2})=1,

which implies that
\psi_{n,1}
and
\psi_{n,2}
are linearly independent. It follows from Theorem
3.3
, Lemma
2.2
and Lemma
3.2
, and the integrability of
g
that for any
\epsilon>0
, there exist
N,M>0
such that when
n>N
, we have

\displaystyle|\psi_{n,i}(x)-\psi_{i}(x)|^{2}<\epsilon,\,\,|\psi_{i}(x)|^{2}<M,\,\,|\psi_{n,i}(x)|^{2}<M,\;\;x\in[0,1),

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}|\psi^{\prime}_{n,i}(x)-\psi^{\prime}_{i}(x)|^{2}dx<\epsilon,\,\int_{0}^{1}|\psi^{\prime}_{i}(x)|^{2}dx<M,\,\int_{0}^{1}|\psi^{\prime}_{n,i}(x)|^{2}dx<M,

(3.16)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\left|\sqrt{p_{n}}-\sqrt{p}\right|^{2}dx<\epsilon,\,\int_{0}^{1}g(x)dx<M,

and
\psi_{n,1}\in H^{\prime}[0,1)
and
z\psi_{n,1}\in L^{2}([0,1);\nu)
.

Let
z\in{\mathbb{C}}\backslash{\mathbb{R}}
. Then
z\in\rho(\widetilde{T})\cap\rho(\widetilde{T}_{n})
since
\widetilde{T}
and
\widetilde{T}_{n}
are self-adjoint. Set

f(x,t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\psi_{1}(x)\psi_{2}(t),&t\in[0,x),\\
\psi_{1}(t)\psi_{2}(x),&t\in[x,1),\end{array}\right.\;f_{n}(x,t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\psi_{n,1}(x)\psi_{n,2}(t),&t\in[0,x),\\
\psi_{n,1}(t)\psi_{n,2}(x),&t\in[x,1),\end{array}\right.

and

(3.17)

\mathcal{G}(x,t)=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}f(x,t)\\
zf(x,t)\\
\end{array}\right),\;\;\;\mathcal{G}_{n}(x,t)=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}f_{n}(x,t)\\
zf_{n}(x,t)\\
\end{array}\right).

It is easy to show that
\mathcal{G}(x,\cdot),\mathcal{G}_{n}(x,\cdot)\in\mathcal{H}\cap\mathcal{H}_{n}
for
x\in[0,1)
.
Let
h(x)=\langle g,Q\mathcal{G}(x,\cdot)^{*}\rangle_{\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}}
and
h_{n}(x)=\langle g,Q_{n}\mathcal{G}_{n}(x,\cdot)^{*}\rangle_{\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}}
for
g\in{\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}}
and
x\in[0,1)
. Then when
n>N
, by (
3.2
), we have

(3.18)

\displaystyle|h_{n}(x)|^{2}\leq\|g\|^{2}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}}\|Q_{n}\mathcal{G}_{n}(x,\cdot)^{*}\|^{2}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|g\|^{2}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}}\left(|\psi_{n,1}(x)|^{2}\int_{0}^{x}|\psi_{n,2}^{\prime}(t)|^{2}dt+|\psi_{n,2}(x)|^{2}\int_{x}^{1}|\psi_{n,1}^{\prime}(t)|^{2}dt\right.

\displaystyle\left.+|z|^{2}|\psi_{n,1}(x)|^{2}\int_{0}^{x}p_{n}|\psi_{n,2}(t)|^{2}dt+|z|^{2}|\psi_{n,2}(x)|^{2}\int_{x}^{1}p_{n}|\psi_{n,1}(t)|^{2}dt\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|g\|^{2}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}}\left(2M^{2}+2|z|^{2}M^{2}\int_{0}^{1}gdt\right)\leq\|g\|^{2}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}}(2M^{2}+2|z|^{2}M^{3}),

which implies that
h_{n}\in L^{2}([0,1);\nu)\cap L^{2}([0,1);\nu_{n})
. Similarly, one can show that
h\in L^{2}([0,1);\nu)\cap L^{2}([0,1);\nu_{n})
. When
n>N
, by Lemma
2.5
, we obtain

(3.19)

z(\widetilde{T}_{n}-z)^{-1}g(x)-z(\widetilde{T}-z)^{-1}g(x)=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}h_{n}(x)-h(x)\\
z(P_{n}h_{n}(x)-Ph(x))\\
\end{array}\right).

Then by (
3.2
) and (
3.18
), we have

\displaystyle\|z(\widetilde{T}_{n}-z)^{-1}g(x)-z(\widetilde{T}-z)^{-1}g(x)\|^{2}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}|h^{\prime}_{n}-h^{\prime}|^{2}dx+|z|^{2}\int_{0}^{1}|P_{n}h_{n}-Ph|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}|h^{\prime}_{n}-h^{\prime}|^{2}dx+2|z|^{2}\int_{0}^{1}|P(h_{n}-h)|^{2}dx+2|z|^{2}\int_{0}^{1}|\sqrt{p_{n}}-\sqrt{p}|^{2}|h_{n}|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}|h^{\prime}_{n}-h^{\prime}|^{2}dx+2|z|^{2}\int_{[0,1)}|(h_{n}-h)|^{2}d\nu(x)+\|g\|^{2}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}}(1+|z|^{2}M)4|z|^{2}M^{2}\epsilon.

Note that

|h_{n}(x)-h(x)|^{2}\leq\|g\|^{2}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}}\|Q_{n}\mathcal{G}_{n}(x,\cdot)^{*}-Q\mathcal{G}(x,\cdot)^{*}\|^{2}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}},

and

|h^{\prime}_{n}(x)-h^{\prime}(x)|^{2}\leq\|g\|^{2}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}}\left\|Q_{n}\frac{\partial\mathcal{G}_{n}}{\partial x}(x,\cdot)^{*}-Q\frac{\partial\mathcal{G}}{\partial x}(x,\cdot)^{*}\right\|^{2}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}}.

Then

\displaystyle\|z(\widetilde{T}_{n}-z)^{-1}g(x)-z(\widetilde{T}-z)^{-1}g(x)\|^{2}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|g\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}}\left(2|z|^{2}\int_{[0,1)}\|Q_{n}\mathcal{G}_{n}(x,\cdot)^{*}-Q\mathcal{G}(x,\cdot)^{*}\|^{2}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}}d\nu(x)\right.

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left.\int_{0}^{1}\left\|Q_{n}\frac{\partial\mathcal{G}_{n}}{\partial x}(x,\cdot)^{*}-Q\frac{\partial\mathcal{G}}{\partial x}(x,\cdot)^{*}\right\|^{2}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}}dx+(1+|z|^{2}M)4|z|^{2}M^{2}\epsilon\right).

On the other hand, we have

\displaystyle\|Q_{n}\mathcal{G}_{n}(x,\cdot)^{*}-Q\mathcal{G}(x,\cdot)^{*}\|^{2}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}}=\int_{0}^{1}\left|\frac{\partial f_{n}}{\partial t}-\frac{\partial f}{\partial t}\right|^{2}dt

\displaystyle+|z|^{2}\int_{0}^{1}|P_{n}f_{n}(x,t)-Pf(x,t)|^{2}dt:=I_{1}+I_{2}.

It follows from (
3.2
) that for
n>N
, we obtain

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{x}|\psi_{n,1}(x)\psi^{\prime}_{n,2}(t)-\psi_{1}(x)\psi^{\prime}_{2}(t)|^{2}dt+\int_{x}^{1}|\psi^{\prime}_{n,1}(t)\psi_{n,2}(x)-\psi^{\prime}_{1}(t)\psi_{2}(x)|^{2}dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2|\psi_{n,1}(x)-\psi_{1}(x)|^{2}\int_{0}^{x}|\psi^{\prime}_{n,2}(t)|^{2}dt+2|\psi_{1}(x)|^{2}\int_{0}^{x}|\psi^{\prime}_{n,2}(t)-\psi^{\prime}_{2}(t)|^{2}dt

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle 2|\psi_{n,2}(x)-\psi_{2}(x)|^{2}\int_{x}^{1}|\psi^{\prime}_{n,1}(t)|^{2}dt+2|\psi_{2}(x)|^{2}\int_{x}^{1}|\psi^{\prime}_{n,1}(t)-\psi^{\prime}_{1}(t)|^{2}dt

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle 8M\epsilon.

Similarly, we have

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2|z|^{2}\int_{0}^{1}|\sqrt{p_{n}}-\sqrt{p}|^{2}|f_{n}|^{2}dt+2|z|^{2}\int_{[0,1)}|f_{n}-f|^{2}d\nu(t)

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle 4|z|^{2}M(M+4K)\epsilon,

where
K:=\nu([0,1))
. This implies that

(3.21)

\int_{[0,1)}\|Q_{n}\mathcal{G}_{n}(x,\cdot)^{*}-Q\mathcal{G}(x,\cdot)^{*}\|^{2}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}}d\nu(x)<4MK(2+|z|^{2}M+4|z|^{2}K)\epsilon.

Note that

\displaystyle\left\|Q_{n}\frac{\partial\mathcal{G}_{n}}{\partial x}(x,\cdot)^{*}-Q\frac{\partial\mathcal{G}}{\partial x}(x,\cdot)^{*}\right\|^{2}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}}=\int_{0}^{1}\left|\frac{\partial^{2}f_{n}}{\partial x\partial t}-\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial x\partial t}\right|^{2}dt

\displaystyle+|z|^{2}\int_{0}^{1}\left|P_{n}\frac{\partial f_{n}}{\partial x}-P\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}\right|^{2}dt:=J_{1}+J_{2}.

Again by (
3.2
), we obtain

\displaystyle J_{1}<2M(|\psi^{\prime}_{n,1}(x)-\psi^{\prime}_{1}(x)|^{2}+|\psi^{\prime}_{n,2}(x)-\psi^{\prime}_{2}(x)|^{2})+2\epsilon(|\psi^{\prime}_{1}(x)|^{2}+|\psi^{\prime}_{2}(x)|^{2}),

and

\displaystyle J_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2|z|^{2}\int_{0}^{1}|\sqrt{p_{n}}-\sqrt{p}|^{2}\left|\frac{\partial f_{n}}{\partial x}\right|^{2}dt+2|z|^{2}\int_{[0,1)}\left|\frac{\partial f_{n}}{\partial x}-\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}\right|^{2}d\nu(t)

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle 4|z|^{2}MK(|\psi^{\prime}_{n,1}(x)-\psi^{\prime}_{1}(x)|^{2}+|\psi^{\prime}_{n,2}(x)-\psi^{\prime}_{2}(x)|^{2})

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle 4|z|^{2}K\epsilon(|\psi^{\prime}_{1}(x)|^{2}+|\psi^{\prime}_{2}(x)|^{2})+2|z|^{2}M\epsilon(|\psi^{\prime}_{n,1}(x)|^{2}+|\psi^{\prime}_{n,2}(x)|^{2}).

Thus

(3.22)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\left\|Q_{n}\frac{\partial\mathcal{G}_{n}}{\partial x}(x,\cdot)^{*}-Q\frac{\partial\mathcal{G}}{\partial x}(x,\cdot)^{*}\right\|^{2}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}}dx

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle 2M(1+2|z|^{2}K)\int_{0}^{1}(|\psi^{\prime}_{n,1}(x)-\psi^{\prime}_{1}(x)|^{2}+|\psi^{\prime}_{n,2}(x)-\psi^{\prime}_{2}(x)|^{2})dx

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle 2|z|^{2}M\epsilon\int_{0}^{1}(|\psi^{\prime}_{n,1}(x)|^{2}+|\psi^{\prime}_{n,2}(x)|^{2})dx

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle 2\epsilon(1+2|z|^{2}K)\int_{0}^{1}(|\psi^{\prime}_{1}(x)|^{2}+|\psi^{\prime}_{2}(x)|^{2})dx

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle 4M(2+|z|^{2}M+4|z|^{2}K)\epsilon.

It follows from (
3.2
)-(
3.22
) that when
n>N
, we have

\displaystyle\|z(\widetilde{T}_{n}-z)^{-1}-z(\widetilde{T}-z)^{-1}\|^{2}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}}

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle 8M\epsilon+8|z|^{2}M(M+4K)\epsilon+4|z|^{4}M(M^{2}+2MK+8K^{2})\epsilon.

This yields that
\|(\widetilde{T}_{n}-z)^{-1}-(\widetilde{T}-z)^{-1}\|_{\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
, that is,
\widetilde{T}_{n}
converges to
\widetilde{T}
in the sense of norm resolvent convergence. The proof is complete.

\Box

Remark 3.6
.

By Lemma
2.8
and Theorem
3.5
, we obtain that
\widetilde{T}_{n}
converges to
\widetilde{T}
in the generalized sense.

Since
T
and
T_{n}
are unitarily equivalent to
\widetilde{T}
and
\widetilde{T}_{n}
, the following result is deduced from Lemma
2.9
and Remark
3.6
.

Corollary 3.7
.

Assume that Hypotheses
(1)
and
(2)
hold. If
\Gamma
is a compact subset of the resolvent set
\rho(T)
, then
\Gamma\subset\rho(T_{n})
for sufficiently large
n
.

The following result shows that the spectrum of a GIS is upper semi-continnous on
\omega
and
\nu
under Hypotheses (1) and (2). We use the notation in
[
14
, p. 208]
as follows

\varrho(\sigma(T_{n}),\sigma(T)):=\sup_{\lambda\in\sigma(T_{n})}{\rm dist}(\lambda,\sigma(T)).

Theorem 3.8
.

Assume that Hypotheses
(1)
and
(2)
hold. Then
\varrho(\sigma(T_{n}),\sigma(T))\to 0
as
n\to\infty
.

Proof
For any
\epsilon>0
, let

\Gamma:=\{\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}:{\rm dist}(\lambda,\sigma(T))\geq\varepsilon\}.

Then
\Gamma
is a compact subset of
\rho(T)
. It follows from Corollary
3.7
that there exist a
N>0
such that when
n>N
, we have
\Gamma\subset\rho(T_{n})
. It follows that

\sigma(T_{n})\subset\{\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}:{\rm dist}(\lambda,\sigma(T))<\varepsilon\}.

Thus we have
\varrho(\sigma(T_{n}),\sigma(T))\leq\epsilon
, which implies that
\varrho(\sigma(T_{n}),\sigma(T))\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. This concludes the proof.

\Box

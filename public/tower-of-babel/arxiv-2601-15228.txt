De Sitter Momentum Space
Nathan Belrhali K, Arthur Poisson K, Sébastien Renaux-Petel K, Denis Werth K,L,F

Abstract
We construct a natural and nonperturbative momentum space for quantum field theory on ( d + 1 ) (d+1) -dimensional de Sitter (dS) spacetime in the Poincaré slicing, adapted to early Universe cosmology. In particular, we identify the dS frequency as the unitary-representation label of the dS \mathrm{dS} isometry group SO ⁡ ( 1 , d + 1 ) \mathrm{SO}(1,d+1) . By diagonalizing the quadratic Casimir together with spatial translations, we provide a harmonic expansion of operators in what we call the Kontorovitch-Lebedev-Fourier (KLF) space. This momentum space shares many structural properties with its Minkowski counterpart, for instance: equations of motion reduce to algebraic equations, and the quadratic dynamics provides a simple propagator analogous to flat space. We reformulate the perturbative computation of in-in correlators in KLF momentum space, showing from first principles how time integrals turn into frequency-space integrals over meromorphic functions. We show how our construction streamlines computations, naturally accommodates the contributions from principal and complementary series in the Källén-Lehmann spectral decomposition of composite operators, and leads to an inversion formula for the spectral density.



Introduction.

Among the three maximally symmetric solutions of the vacuum Einstein equations, de Sitter (dS) spacetime occupies a distinguished role: it describes both the early inflationary Universe and its late-time accelerated expansion. Yet despite this privileged status, quantum field theory (QFT) in dS remains strikingly incomplete and poorly understood.

Unlike anti-de Sitter space, dS admits no holographic dual, obstructing a nonperturbative definition of its quantum dynamics, let alone rendering its strongly coupled regime inaccessible
Strominger 2001
;
Witten 2001
. The absence of a global timelike Killing vector prohibits a conserved notion of energy, and continuous particle production obstructs a conventional
S
-matrix interpretation, reflecting the absence of asymptotic future states (see
Marolf et al. 2013
;
Melville and Pimentel 2024a
;
Donath and Pajer 2024
;
Melville and Pimentel 2024b
for proposals). These conceptual limitations are mirrored by severe technical obstacles for perturbation theory. Late-time correlators are difficult to compute and their analytic structure is not yet fully understood, especially at the loop level (see e.g.
Di Pietro et al. 2022
;
Xianyu and Zhang 2023
;
Chakraborty and Stout 2024
;
Qin and Xianyu 2023
;
Qin and Xianyu 2024
for recent progress). In the conformal-time and spatial-momentum representation, the conventional computation of cosmological correlators parallels that of flat-space scattering amplitudes in
configuration space
, involving integrals over products of Bessel-type special functions
Chen et al. 2017
. Just as flat-space momentum space reorganizes these expressions into simple rational propagators, it is natural to seek an analogous momentum-space formulation for dS correlators, dual to position space.

These difficulties share a common origin: the usual Fourier transform in time and space does not diagonalize the dS isometries. As a result, symmetry constraints, dynamical evolution and analytic structure of observables remain entangled.

In this letter, we show that many of these obstacles are artifacts of representation. We construct a nonperturbative momentum-space formulation of QFT in dS, adapted to its symmetries. We call this space, dual to position-space Poincaré coordinates
(\tau,\bm{x})
and labeled by
(\mu,\bm{k})
, the
Kontorovich-Lebedev-Fourier
(KLF) space, where the unitary-representation label
\mu
provides the appropriate quantum number for the dS frequency.

Of course, a dS momentum space for global coordinates in Euclidean signature, after complexifying (global) time, dates back to many earlier works
Higuchi 1987
;
Bros 1991
;
Bros and Moschella 1996
;
Marolf and Morrison 2010
;
Marolf and Morrison 2011
;
Higuchi et al. 2011
. This formulation provides a nonperturbative definition of QFT free of infrared divergences, with a uniquely defined (Euclidean) Bunch-Davies vacuum.
There, the theory is defined on the
(d+1)
-dimensional sphere and decomposes into spherical harmonics. However, its compactness obscures the dynamics, which can only be accessed via subtle analytic continuation.

By contrast, in the KLF momentum space, dS symmetries, dynamics, and analyticity become simultaneously transparent, equations of motion reduce to algebraic relations, and the Feynman propagator takes the same form as in flat space. We show that perturbative calculations of newly defined (amputated) Schwinger-Keldysch (SK) correlators are free of nested time integrals and instead take the form of spectral integrals over frequencies. The resulting integrands being universally meromorphic, this enables correlators to be evaluated by simply collecting residues.
We also derive an inversion formula for the spectral density and show how our group-theoretical construction streamlines and gives a transparent physical meaning to otherwise complicated computations.
We provide extensive details and applications in a companion paper
Belrhali et al. 2026
.

dS spacetime.

For the sake of generality and to allow the future use of dimensional regularization, throughout this letter, we consider
(d+1)
-dimensional dS spacetime
\mathrm{dS}_{d+1}
, defined as an hyperboloid embedded in
(d+2)
-dimensional Minkowski spacetime
\mathbb{M}^{1,d+1}
:

\eta_{AB}X^{A}X^{B}=H^{-2}\,,

(1)

where
A,B=0,1,\ldots,d+1
and
H^{-1}
is the dS curvature radius. For cosmological purposes, it is enough to restrict our attention to the half of
\mathrm{dS}
space covered by Poincaré coordinates, such that
X^{0}=(2H\tau)^{-1}(\tau^{2}-\bm{x}^{2}-1)\,,X^{i}=-(H\tau)^{-1}x^{i}\,,X^{d+1}=(2H\tau)^{-1}(\bm{x}^{2}-\tau^{2}-1)
, where
-\infty<\tau<0
is conformal time and
x^{i}
,
i=1,\ldots,d
are spatial coordinates. With these, the metric induced on the hyperboloid (
1
) takes the cosmological form
\mathrm{d}s^{2}=(H\tau)^{-2}\left(-\mathrm{d}\tau^{2}+\mathrm{d}\bm{x}^{2}\right)
, which describes an accelerating spacetime with constant Hubble rate
H
and scale factor
-(H\tau)^{-1}
. As can be read off from the definition (
1
),
\mathrm{dS}_{d+1}
is left invariant by the
(d+2)
-dimensional Lorentz group
\mathrm{SO}(1,d+1)
. Instead, constant-
\tau
spatial hypersurfaces are
d
-dimensional Euclidean planes
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, which are only invariant under the action of the Euclidean group
\mathbb{E}_{d}\subset\mathrm{SO}(1,d+1)
, composed of translations and rotations in
d
dimensions.

Hilbert space of QFT in dS.

The generators of the symmetry group
\mathrm{SO}(1,d+1)
are realized as Hermitian operators acting on the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
. Therefore, the latter decomposes into Unitary Irreducible Representations (UIRs) of
\mathrm{SO}(1,d+1)
:
\mathcal{H}=\bigoplus_{\mu}\mathcal{V}_{(\mu)}
where
\mu
is an abstract label of the UIRs and
\mathcal{V}_{(\mu)}
are the corresponding invariant subspaces
Bargmann and Wigner 1948
. The UIRs are classified according to the eigenvalues
M^{2}_{(\mu)}
of the quadratic Casimir operator
\mathcal{C}=-\frac{1}{2}J^{AB}J_{AB}
, where
J_{AB}=-J_{BA}
are the generators of the
(d+2)
-dimensional Lorentz algebra. The Casimir acts as a multiple of the identity when restricted to each
\mathcal{V}_{(\mu)}
,
\mathcal{C}=\bigoplus_{\mu}M^{2}_{(\mu)}\mathds{1}^{(\mu)}
, where the eigenvalues
\Delta(d-\Delta)+S(S+d-2)
generally depend on a complex conformal dimension
\Delta
and a representation
S
of the rotation subgroup
\mathrm{SO}(d)
(see
Sun 2025
for a review). For simplicity, we only consider scalar representations here and set
S=0
. With the parametrization
\Delta=\tfrac{d}{2}+i\mu
, the Casimir eigenvalues of interest read
M_{(\mu)}^{2}=\mu^{2}+\tfrac{d^{2}}{4}
, whose form makes manifest the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
shadow symmetry
\mu\leftrightarrow-\mu
. As the generators are Hermitian, the Casimir eigenvalues are real, implying that the parameter
\mu
is either real or purely imaginary. Discarding the ever-present trivial representation, the possible scalar UIRs of
\mathrm{SO}(1,d+1)
fall into three categories: (i) the principal series
\mathcal{P}_{(\mu)}
with
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
, (ii) the complementary series
\mathcal{C}_{(\mu)}
with
i\mu\in[-\tfrac{d}{2},\tfrac{d}{2}]
, and (iii) the type-I exceptional series
\mathcal{E}_{(\mu)}
with
i\mu=\tfrac{d}{2}+k
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
. The free bulk theory whose single-particle Hilbert space realizes these representations corresponds, respectively, to heavy fields
m>\tfrac{d}{2}H
, light fields
m\leq\tfrac{d}{2}H
, and scalar fields with extended shift symmetries, see e.g.
Bonifacio et al. 2019
for the latter aspect.

To determine a basis of the Hilbert space, we follow a standard procedure: we identify the largest set of commuting generators and diagonalize them in a common orthogonal basis together with the Casimir operator. To make contact with flat space and cosmological observables, we do so in a way that takes advantage of the
\mathbb{E}_{d}
symmetry of spacelike slices. To this end, it is convenient to define the following change of basis in the Lie algebra:

\displaystyle D=J_{0,d+1}

\displaystyle,\quad M_{ij}=J_{ij}\,,

(2)

\displaystyle P_{i}=J_{d+1,i}+J_{0,i}

\displaystyle,\quad K_{i}=J_{d+1,i}-J_{0,i}\,.

The generators
D,P_{i},K_{i}
and
M_{ij}
correspond to dilatation, spatial translations, special conformal transformations (sometimes referred to as dS boosts), and spatial rotations, respectively.
Diagonalizing the translation operators yields the following momentum basis for the Hilbert space:

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}\ket{\mu,\bm{k}}=M_{(\mu)}^{2}\ket{\mu,\bm{k}}\,,\quad P_{i}\ket{\mu,\bm{k}}=k_{i}\ket{\mu,\bm{k}}\,,

(3)

which are identified with single-particle states that can be constructed from the vacuum.

Kontorovich-Lebedev-Fourier space.

The central quantities of interest are correlation functions evaluated in a given state. In inflationary cosmology, one is interested in the Bunch-Davies vacuum state
\ket{\Omega}
, defined by the absence of particles in the asymptotic past
\tau\to-\infty
. In the SK (in-in) formalism, this is implemented by performing a path-integral on a first branch from
-\infty(1-i\epsilon)
to late times, then on a second branch backwards to
-\infty(1+i\epsilon)
in the complex time plane
Weinberg 2005
. As usual in QFT, this path-integral is performed over field configurations that are square-integrable. It is therefore essential to characterize this functional space. For this, let us introduce the following wavefunction:

\Phi^{(\mu)}_{\bm{k},\mathcal{O}}(\tau,\bm{x})\equiv\bra{\Omega}\mathcal{O}(\tau,\bm{x})\ket{\mu,\bm{k}}\,,

(4)

where
\mathcal{O}(\tau,\bm{x})
stands for any local Hermitian operator. Using that the action of the generators (
2
) on
\mathcal{O}
is given by the differential operator associated with the corresponding Killing vectors, one deduces that the Casimir acts on
\mathcal{O}
as the Laplace-Beltrami operator on
\mathrm{dS}_{d+1}
:

\displaystyle[\mathcal{C},\mathcal{O}(\tau,\bm{x})]=H^{-2}\Box_{\mathrm{dS}}\mathcal{O}(\tau,\bm{x})

(5)

\displaystyle=-\left(\tau^{2}\partial_{\tau}^{2}-(d-1)\tau\partial_{\tau}-\tau^{2}\partial_{i}^{2}\right)\mathcal{O}(\tau,\bm{x})\,.

This, together with the definition of the momentum basis (
3
), implies that the wavefunction (
4
) is an eigenfunction of the Killing vectors associated with
\mathcal{C}
and translations
P_{i}
. However, these operators are not self-adjoint on the tilted axis of the SK path-integral contour. Instead, they are self-adjoint once the two SK branches are Wick-rotated to the imaginary axis:

\int\displaylimits_{-\infty(1\mp i\epsilon)}^{0}\mathrm{d}\tau f(\tau)=\mp i\int\displaylimits_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}zf(e^{\pm i\frac{\pi}{2}}z)\,,

(6)

which maps
\mathrm{dS}_{d+1}
geometry to the one of
d+1
Euclidean anti-de Sitter
\mathrm{EAdS}_{d+1}

(see, e.g.
Anninos et al. 2015
;
Sleight and Taronna 2021
;
Di Pietro et al. 2022
;
Loparco et al. 2023
;
Chowdhury et al. 2025
for relevant works in that context). It follows that the analytic continuation of the wavefunction satisfies the differential equations:

\displaystyle H^{-2}\Box_{\mathrm{EAdS}}\Phi_{\bm{k},\mathcal{O}}^{(\mu)}(z,\bm{x})

\displaystyle=-M_{(\mu)}^{2}\Phi_{\bm{k},\mathcal{O}}^{(\mu)}(z,\bm{x})\,,

(7a)

\displaystyle\partial_{i}\Phi_{\bm{k},\mathcal{O}}^{(\mu)}(z,\bm{x})

\displaystyle=-ik_{i}\Phi_{\bm{k},\mathcal{O}}^{(\mu)}(z,\bm{x})\,,

(7b)

with
H^{-2}\Box_{\mathrm{EAdS}}\equiv\left(z^{2}\partial_{z}^{2}-(d-1)z\partial_{z}+z^{2}\partial_{i}^{2}\right)
. The set of equations (
7a
)-(
7b
) define a well-posed Sturm-Liouville problem on
L^{2}\left[\mathrm{EAdS}_{d+1},\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}^{d}\bm{x}\sqrt{-g}\right]
, whose solution reads
\Phi^{(\mu)}_{\bm{k},\mathcal{O}}(z,\bm{x})=c_{\mathcal{O}}(\mu)\Phi^{(\mu)}_{\bm{k}}(z,\bm{x})
, where
c_{\mathcal{O}}(\mu)
is a theory-dependent normalization, and

\Phi^{(\mu)}_{\bm{k}}(z,\bm{x})=\frac{H^{\frac{d+1}{2}}}{\sqrt{\pi}}e^{-i\bm{k}\cdot\bm{x}}z^{\frac{d}{2}}K_{i\mu}(kz)\,,

(8)

where
K_{i\mu}(kz)
is the modified Bessel function of second type, which is real. Since
\Box_{\mathrm{EAdS}}
and
\partial_{i}
are self-adjoint, the functions (
8
) form an orthonormal set.

Importantly, we now show that the harmonic functions (
8
) for
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
, corresponding to
\mathcal{O}
being free fields within the principal series, constitute a complete basis of
L^{2}\left[\mathrm{EAdS}_{d+1},\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}^{d}\bm{x}\sqrt{-g}\right]
. For this, let us introduce the shifted Euclidean Casimir operator in spatial Fourier space

\Delta_{z}=z^{d+1}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}z}\left(\frac{1}{z^{d-1}}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}z}\right)-(kz)^{2}+\frac{d^{2}}{4}\,,

(9)

which is self-adjoint on
L^{2}[\mathbb{R}_{+},\mathrm{d}z/z^{d+1}]
. For
\lambda
complex, we now define the Green function
G^{\lambda}(z,z^{\prime})
, such that
(\Delta_{z}-\lambda)G^{\lambda}(z,z^{\prime})=z^{\prime d+1}\,\delta(z-z^{\prime})
, and with vanishing boundary conditions appropriate for
L^{2}
. It can be built from the homogeneous solutions
z^{\frac{d}{2}}K_{\sqrt{\lambda}}(kz)
and
z^{\frac{d}{2}}I_{\pm\sqrt{\lambda}}(kz)
, where
I
is the modified Bessel function of first type, and reads

G^{\lambda}(z,z^{\prime})=-(zz^{\prime})^{\frac{d}{2}}\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}I_{\sqrt{\lambda}}(kz)K_{\sqrt{\lambda}}(kz^{\prime})&\text{for}\;z<z^{\prime}\\
I_{\sqrt{\lambda}}(kz^{\prime})K_{\sqrt{\lambda}}(kz)&\text{for}\;z^{\prime}<z\end{array}\right.\,.

(10)

Since this is an analytic function of the parameter
\lambda
in the cut plane
\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{R}_{-}
, it can be rewritten as a dispersive integral over its discontinuity, yielding:

G^{\lambda}(z,z^{\prime})=\int\displaylimits^{0}_{-\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}\lambda^{\prime}}{2i\pi}\frac{\text{Disc}_{\lambda^{\prime}}(G^{\lambda^{\prime}}(z,z^{\prime}))}{\lambda^{\prime}-\lambda}\,.

(11)

The discontinuity can be deduced from Eq. (
10
) using the connection formula among Bessel functions
DLMF
. After changing the variable
\lambda=-\mu^{2}
, one finds

G^{\lambda}(z,z^{\prime})=-\frac{(zz^{\prime})^{\frac{d}{2}}}{\pi}\int\displaylimits^{\infty}_{-\infty}\mathrm{d}\mu\,\mathcal{N}_{\mu}\frac{K_{i\mu}(kz)K_{i\mu}(kz^{\prime})}{\mu^{2}-\mu^{2}_{\lambda}}
, where we define
\mathcal{N}_{\mu}\equiv\frac{\mu}{\pi}\sinh(\pi\mu)
which identifies to the de Sitter density of states. Acting with
\Delta_{z}-\lambda
on this expression, one deduces

\frac{(zz^{\prime})^{\frac{d}{2}}}{\pi}\int\displaylimits_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\mu\,\mathcal{N}_{\mu}\,K_{i\mu}(kz)K_{i\mu}(kz^{\prime})=z^{d+1}\delta(z-z^{\prime})\,.

(12)

This shows that the functions
z^{\frac{d}{2}}K_{i\mu}(kz)
, for
\mu
real, form a complete basis of
L^{2}\left[\mathbb{R}_{+},\mathrm{d}z/z^{d+1}\right]
, whose decomposition is known to mathematicians as the Kontorovich-Lebedev transform
Kontorovich and Lebedev 1938
;
Lebedev 1946
.

The spatial component of the harmonic functions (
8
) being the usual
d
-dimensional Fourier plane waves, this motivates introducing a
(d+1)
-dimensional momentum space that we call the Kontorovich-Lebedev-Fourier (KLF) space. Any square-integrable function on
\mathrm{EAdS}_{d+1}
admits the following decomposition:

\displaystyle f(z,\bm{x})

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathrm{KLF}}\;\Phi^{(\mu)}_{\bm{k}}(z,\bm{x})\;f^{(\mu)}_{\bm{k}}\;,

(13)

\displaystyle f^{(\mu)}_{\bm{k}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathrm{EAdS}}\left[\Phi^{(\mu)}_{\bm{k}}(z,\bm{x})\right]^{*}\,f(z,\bm{x})\,,

where the
\mu
-integration range spans the real axis only (i.e. includes only the principal series), and the
\mathrm{EAdS}
integration is over the entire spacetime:

\displaystyle\int_{\mathrm{KLF}}

\displaystyle\equiv\int\displaylimits_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}\mu\,\mathcal{N}_{\mu}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{d}\bm{k}}{(2\pi)^{d}}\,,

(14)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathrm{EAdS}}

\displaystyle\equiv\int\displaylimits_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}z}{(Hz)^{d+1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathrm{d}^{d}\bm{x}\,.

The corresponding harmonic expansion (
13
) provides the
\mathrm{dS}
counterpart of the
(d+1)
-momentum Fourier expansion in Minkowski spacetime, where the harmonic function (
8
) is the dS analogue of the usual plane wave
\exp{[i(\omega t-\bm{k}\cdot\bm{x})]}/\sqrt{2\omega}
.
The reader may wonder why the KLF harmonic functions (
8
) are not themselves square-integrable. This is in fact not different from plane waves not being square-integrable either and still be the correct basis for the Fourier transform. This situation arises when the operator to be diagonalized is unbounded with a continuous spectrum, in which case the proper mathematical structure is not the Hilbert space but the rigged Hilbert space
Gel’fand and Vilenkin 1964
;
Maurin 1968
. As a result, as usual in QFT and harmonic analysis, the KLF expansion should be understood in a distributional sense, and hence can be extended beyond square-integrable functions. In particular, the KLF transform of generic harmonic functions (
8
), not necessarily in the principal series, can be defined following the generalized orthogonality relation:

\displaystyle\int_{\mathrm{EAdS}}\left[\Phi^{(\mu)}_{\bm{k}}(z,\bm{x})\right]^{*}\Phi^{(\alpha)}_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}(z,\bm{x})

(15)

\displaystyle=\frac{(2\pi)^{d}}{\mathcal{N}_{\mu}}\delta^{(d)}(\bm{k}-\bm{k}^{\prime})\hat{\delta}_{\alpha}(\mu)\,.

Here, the distribution
\hat{\delta}_{\alpha}(\mu)
, for any
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
, acts in KLF space as

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{d}\mu\;\hat{\delta}_{\alpha}(\mu)f^{(\mu)}_{\bm{k}}=\frac{1}{2}(f^{(\alpha)}_{\bm{k}}+f^{(-\alpha)}_{\bm{k}})
, where
f^{(\alpha)}_{\bm{k}}
is defined as the analytical continuation of the KLF function
f^{(\mu)}_{\bm{k}}
, and the symmetrization enforces the shadow symmetry. For
\alpha
real, this reduces to
\hat{\delta}_{\alpha}(\mu)=|\alpha|\delta(\mu^{2}-\alpha^{2})
. This is a key aspect of our formalism: all computations can be carried out first for fields in the principal series, free of any difficulty like infrared divergences; more general physical results then simply follow by analytical continuation.
In what follows, for notational simplicity, we will write the KLF delta function as
\delta(^{\mu\,\mu^{\prime}}_{\bm{k}\,\bm{k}^{\prime}})\equiv(2\pi)^{d}\delta^{(d)}(\bm{k}-\bm{k}^{\prime})\;\mathcal{N}_{\mu}^{-1}\,\hat{\delta}_{\mu^{\prime}}(\mu)
.

KLF correlators.

KLF-space correlators are defined for real
\mu_{i}
as:

\mathcal{G}_{{\sf{a}}_{n}\ldots{\sf{a}}_{n}}\left({}^{\mu_{1}...\mu_{n}}_{\bm{k}_{1}...\bm{k}_{n}}\right)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{(2\pi)^{d}}{\mathcal{N}_{\mu_{i}}}\frac{\delta Z[J_{+},J_{-}]}{\delta J_{{\sf{a}}_{i},\bm{k}_{i}}^{\mu_{i}}}\Bigg\rvert_{J_{\pm}=0}\,,

(16)

where the functional generator reads

Z[J_{+},J_{-}]\equiv\int\mathcal{D}\varphi_{\pm}e^{iS_{+}-iS_{-}+\int_{\mathrm{KLF}}(\varphi_{+}J_{+}+\varphi_{-}J_{-})}\,,

(17)

and the integral runs over
L^{2}\left[\mathrm{EAdS}_{d+1},\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}^{d}\bm{x}\sqrt{-g}\right]
functions. Here, as usual in the SK formalism, the action integrals are taken over two in-in branches with integration axis that are suitably tilted in the time complex plane:

S_{\pm}[\varphi]=\hskip-11.38092pt\int\displaylimits^{0}_{-\infty(1\mp i\epsilon)}\hskip-11.38092pt\mathrm{d}\tau\mathrm{d}^{d}\bm{x}\sqrt{-g}\,\mathcal{L}(\varphi,\partial_{\mu}\varphi)\;,

(18)

and the two branches are sewn together at an arbitrary late time
\varphi_{+}(\tau_{0})=\varphi_{-}(\tau_{0})
. The functional generator can be explicitly evaluated for a Gaussian action

S_{0}=\frac{1}{2}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}\tau\,\mathrm{d}^{d}\bm{x}}{(-H\tau)^{d-1}}\left[\left(\partial_{\tau}\varphi\right)^{2}-\left(\partial_{i}\varphi\right)^{2}-\frac{m_{\varphi}^{2}}{H^{2}\tau^{2}}\varphi^{2}\right]\,,

(19)

which, after analytically continuing to
\mathrm{EAdS}
and decomposing onto the KLF harmonic basis, yields

\pm iS_{0,\pm}[\varphi]=\frac{H^{2}e^{\pm\frac{i(d-1)\pi}{2}}}{2}\int_{\mathrm{KLF}}\varphi^{(\mu)}_{\bm{k}}(\mu^{2}-\mu_{\varphi}^{2})\varphi^{(\mu)}_{-\bm{k}}\,,

(20)

where
\mu_{\varphi}^{2}=m_{\varphi}^{2}/H^{2}-d^{2}/4
.
Gluing both SK branches, one can complete the square in the path-integral (
17
) following
Weinberg 2005
, to find the four KLF-space propagators:

\bm{\mathcal{G}}_{\mu_{\varphi}}^{\prime}(\mu)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\frac{e^{-\frac{i\pi(d+1)}{2}}}{(\mu^{2}-\mu_{\varphi}^{2})_{i\epsilon}}&\frac{\hat{\delta}_{\mu_{\varphi}}(\mu)}{\mathcal{N}_{\mu}}\\
\frac{\hat{\delta}_{\mu_{\varphi}}(\mu)}{\mathcal{N}_{\mu}}&\frac{e^{+\frac{i\pi(d+1)}{2}}}{(\mu^{2}-\mu_{\varphi}^{2})_{-i\epsilon}}\end{array}\right)\,,

(21)

where we defined the reduced propagator matrix as
\bm{\mathcal{G}}_{\mu_{\varphi}}\left({}^{\mu\,\mu^{\prime}}_{\bm{k}\,\bm{k}^{\prime}}\right)\equiv H^{-2}\delta(^{\mu\,\mu^{\prime}}_{\bm{k}\,-\bm{k}^{\prime}})\bm{\mathcal{G}}_{\mu_{\varphi}}^{\prime}(\mu)
.
In (
21
), the suitable
i\epsilon
prescription that encodes particle production reads
Melville and Pimentel 2024b
;
Werth 2024

\frac{1}{(\mu^{2}-\mu_{\varphi}^{2})_{i\epsilon}}\equiv\frac{1}{2\sinh(\pi\mu_{\varphi})}\left[\frac{e^{+\pi\mu_{\varphi}}}{\mu^{2}-\mu_{\varphi}^{2}+i\epsilon}-\frac{e^{-\pi\mu_{\varphi}}}{\mu^{2}-\mu_{\varphi}^{2}-i\epsilon}\right]\,.

(22)

The diagonal terms in (
21
) are the equivalent of the Minkowski Feynman propagators in momentum space,
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}(p)=\pm i/(-p^{2}-m_{\varphi}^{2}\pm i\epsilon)
. Following the usual path-integral derivation, it is straightforward to write Feynman rules for KLF correlators. Each vertex comes with a SK
\pm
index, propagators are given by (
21
), and to give a simple example, polynomial interactions
\mathcal{L}=-\lambda\phi^{n}/n!

Spectral density and inversion formula.

At the nonperturbative level, the Wightman two-point function of any scalar operator
\mathcal{O}
can be expressed in the Källén-Lehmann spectral representation
Bros 1991
;
Bros and Moschella 1996
;
Di Pietro et al. 2022
;
Hogervorst et al. 2023
;
Loparco et al. 2023
by inserting the resolution of the identity in the momentum space basis

\displaystyle\bra{\Omega}\mathcal{O}_{\pm}(z_{1},\bm{x}_{1})\mathcal{O}_{\mp}(z_{2},\bm{x}_{2})\ket{\Omega}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{P}\oplus\mathcal{C}}\mathrm{d}\mu\frac{\mathrm{d}^{d}\bm{k}}{(2\pi)^{d}}\rho^{\mathcal{P},\mathcal{C}}_{\mathcal{O}}(\mu)\Phi^{(\mu)}_{\bm{k}}(z_{1},\bm{x}_{1})\Phi^{(\mu)}_{-\bm{k}}(z_{2},\bm{x}_{2})\,,

where the spectral density, which reads
\rho^{\mathcal{P}}_{\mathcal{O}}(\mu)=\mathcal{N}_{\mu}|c_{\mathcal{O}}(\mu)|^{2}

The decomposition (

Conclusion and outlook.

In this letter, we have introduced a novel, fully nonperturbative momentum-space formulation of QFT in the Poincaré patch of dS spacetime, dual to configuration space. This framework enables a reformulation of QFT directly in this space, conveniently defined on the EAdS SK double-branch path-integral contour. It also provides a straightforward correspondence between cosmological and amputated KLF correlators.

This new language makes several key features manifest: (i) the nonconservation of the dS frequency, (ii) the natural emergence of the Bessel-type dS harmonic function for the time/frequency dual correspondence, and (iii) a reorganization of perturbative calculations wherein time ordering is replaced by spectral integration. As a result, the underlying structure of cosmological correlators becomes transparent, and the latter are cast in a form that facilitates contact with flat space. At the nonperturbative level, we further show that the KLF transform directly isolates the spectral density of local operators.

There are many questions directly spurred by our foundational construction. For example, it is well motivated to ask to what extent KLF space can be generalized or deformed to accommodate controlled (and perturbative) symmetry breaking of the dS isometries. A convenient byproduct of our construction is that frequency integration appears naturally and spectral density can be readily extracted. It would be very interesting to use this tool to dress propagators and perform correlator resummation. We also leave for future work the possibility to connect to flat-space amplitudes, where energy conservation emerges from spectral integration, and to define an
S
-matrix from KLF amputated correlators. These and related lines of investigation hold the promise of fundamental new insights into understanding QFT in dS.

Local-available quantum correlation swapping in one-parameter X states Preprint: SB/F/497-25
Hermann L. Albrecht Q.

Abstract
Although introduced originally for entanglement, quantum repeaters and swapping protocols have been analyzed for other quantum correlations (QC), such as quantum discord. Introduced by Mundarain and Ladrón de Guevara, local-available quantum correlations (LAQC) are a promising yet understudied quantum correlation. Recently, Bellorin et al. obtained exact analytical results for the LAQC quantifier of general 2-qubit X states. Starting from those previous results, we analyzed the LAQC swapping for five families of one-parameter 2-qubit X states. As expected, we find that if the initial state and the one used for the projective measurement are correlated, the final state will have non-zero LAQC.

Although the entanglement swapping protocol allows one to redistribute this quantum correlation to non-interacting subsystems, it comes with a cost. The maximal amount of entanglement that one can redistribute depends on the initial states and the projective measurement involved. In 2014, Muñoz, Grüning, and Roa
Roa et al. 2014
analyzed this scheme for X states, obtaining threshold values for the initial states’ entanglement so that the outcome states are not separable.

In 2015, Xie et al.
Xie et al. 2014
extended the concept of entanglement swapping to involve other quantum correlations, therefore deemed as Quantum Correlation Swapping (QCS). Using the same basic protocol described above, the authors studied the redistribution of quantum discord, measurement-induced disturbance
Luo 2008a
, and ameliorated measurement-induced disturbance
Girolami et al. 2011
;
Wu et al. 2009
. While using Werner states as their initial 2-qubit states in this first study, they later extended their analysis to include Bell-Diagonal states
Xie et al. 2019
. In it, the authors focused on analyzing quantum discord and local quantum uncertainty
Girolami et al. 2013
as the quantum correlations they are swapping. More recently, Xie at al.
Xie et al. 2025
have extended their analysis to include X states.

Since one can apply the QCS scheme to various quantum correlations, we analyze the behavior of local-available quantum correlations (LAQC)
Mundarain and de Guevara 2015
. Introduced in 2015 by Mundarain and Ladrón de Guevara, LAQC has yet to become a more commonly studied quantum resource, despite recent results showing them to be more robust than entanglement when subjected to Markovian decoherence
Albrecht Q. et al. 2018
;
Bellorin and Albrecht Q. 2021
. Contrary to what one can achieve for quantum discord, where only approximate analytical results exist, an exact result for the LAQC quantifier for general 2-qubit X states was derived
Albrecht Q. et al. 2022
;
Bellorin et al. 2022
. Therefore, we have focused on studying LAQC swapping by considering relevant one-parameter 2-qubit X states as initial states.

I
One-parameter 2-qubit X states

The widely-studied family of 2-qubit states known as X states
Yu and Eberly 2007
;
Quesada et al. 2012
due to the shape of their density matrix

\displaystyle\rho^{AB}_{X}=\matrixquantity(\lx@physics@matrix
a & 0 & 0 & r\exp^{i\chi} \\
0 & b & s\exp^{i\xi} & 0 \\
0 & s\exp^{-i\xi} & c& 0 \\
r\exp^{-i\chi}& 0 & 0 & d\endlx@physics@matrix)

(1)

is a representative class of 2-qubit states since any arbitrary
\rho_{AB}
can be transformed into a
\rho^{X}
while preserving its entanglement
Mendonça et al. 2014
;
Hedemann 2018
. Although the above density matrix has seven independent real parameters, Zhou et al.

established that applying local unitary transformations allows one to remove the two phase parameters
\chi
and
\xi

1

\displaystyle\rho_{X}=\matrixquantity(\lx@physics@matrix
a & 0 & 0 & r \\
0 & b & s & 0 \\
0 & s & c& 0 \\
r& 0 & 0 & d\endlx@physics@matrix)

(2)

where

\displaystyle a,b,c,d\geq 0,

(3a)

\displaystyle a+b+c+d=1,

(3b)

\displaystyle s\leq\sqrt{bc},\quad\mbox{and}\quad r\leq\sqrt{ad},

(3c)

so that
\rho^{X}
is hermitian, semi-positive definite, and
\Tr\quantity(\rho^X)=1
.

Since the well-known Pauli matrices,

\sigma_{1}=\matrixquantity(\lx@physics@matrix\pmat{1}\endlx@physics@matrix),\quad\sigma_{2}=0&-\matrixquantity(\lx@physics@matrix\pmat{2}\endlx@physics@matrix)\\
i&0,\quad\sigma_{3}=\matrixquantity(\lx@physics@matrix\pmat{3}\endlx@physics@matrix),

(4)

along with the identity matrix
\mathbbm{1}_{2}
, form a complete basis of
2\times 2
matrices, we can relay on them to represent any
\rho_{AB}
. This representation is the Fano form
Fano 1983
of 2-qubit states

\rho^{AB}=\frac{1}{4}\,\sum_{i,j=0}^{3}T_{ij}\sigma_{i}\otimes\sigma_{j},

(5)

where

\sigma_{0}=\mathbbm{1}_{2}=\matrixquantity(\lx@physics@matrix\imat{2}\endlx@physics@matrix)

(6)

and

T_{ij}=\Tr\quantity[\qty(\sigma_i\otimes\sigma_j)\rho^{AB}].

(7)

One can use (
5
) to parametrize (
2
) using
\quantity{T_{30}, T_{03}, T_{11}, T_{22}, T_{33}}
. Usually labeled as the Bloch parameters and denoted as
\quantity{x_3,y_3,T_1,T_2,T_3}
in the literature, these are related to the previous ones in (
2
) by

\begin{aligned} x_{3}&=a+b-c-d,\\
y_{3}&=a-b+c-d,\end{aligned}\quad\mbox{}\quad\begin{aligned} T_{1}&=2(s+r),\\
T_{2}&=2(s-r),\\
T_{3}&=a-b-c+d.\end{aligned}

(8)

The first subset of one-parameter 2-qubit X states analyzed in this article is the highly symmetrical Werner states
Werner 1989
, defined as bipartite states invariant under local unitary transformations. That is, states
\rho_{w}
that satisfy

\rho_{w}=\quantity(\mathbbm{U}_A\otimes\mathbbm{U}_B)\,\rho_{w}\,\quantity(\mathbbm{U}_A\otimes\mathbbm{U}_B)^{\dagger},

(9)

where
\mathbbm{U}_{A},\mathbbm{U}_{B}\in U(2)
. Such 2-qubit states can be written as

\rho_{w}=z\outerproduct{\Psi^-}{\Psi^-}+\frac{1-z}{4}\,\mathbbm{1}_{4},

(10)

where
\ket{\psi^-}
is one of the four maximally entangled 2-qubit states known as Bell states:

\displaystyle\ket{\psi^{\pm}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\,}}\,\quantity(\ket{01}\pm\ket{10}),

(11a)

\displaystyle\ket{\phi^{\pm}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\,}}\,\quantity(\ket{00}\pm\ket{11}).

(11b)

The Bloch parameters of
\rho_{w}
(
10
) are

x_{3}=y_{3}=0\quad\mbox{and}\quad T_{1}=T_{2}=T_{3}=-z,

(12)

.

In 2010, Al-Qasimi and James

introduced the
\alpha

\beta

As the authors analyzed the relationship between discord, entanglement, and linear entropy for 2-qubit systems, they found that those states are related to upper and lower bounds for the quantum discord

;
Henderson and Vedral 2001

For
\alpha
-states, which are related to the upper bound, the corresponding density matrix is

\rho_{\alpha}=\frac{1}{2}\,\matrixquantity(\lx@physics@matrix
\alpha& 0 &0 & \alpha\\
0 & 1-\alpha& 0 &0\\
0 & 0 & 1-\alpha&0\\
\alpha& 0 &0 &\alpha\endlx@physics@matrix),

(13)

where

\begin{split}\rho_{\alpha}=&\alpha\outerproduct{\phi^+}{\phi^+}\\
&\quad\mbox{}\quad+\frac{1-\alpha}{2}\,\quantity(\dyad{\psi^+} +\dyad{\psi^-}).\end{split}

(14)

Its Bloch parameters are

x_{3}=y_{3}=0,\quad T_{1}=\alpha=-T_{2},\quad T_{3}=2\alpha-1.

(15)

For
\beta

\rho_{\beta}=\frac{1}{2}\,\matrixquantity(\lx@physics@matrix
\beta& 0 &0 & \beta\\

\beta& 0 &0 & \beta\endlx@physics@matrix),

(16)

(18)

From the above expression, it is straightforward to realize that, for
\beta=0

\beta=1

On the other hand, since one can consider 2-qubit Werner states as a statistical mixture of a maximally entangled state (
11

\displaystyle x_{3}=y_{3}=1-F,

\displaystyle T_{1}=T_{2}=-F,

\displaystyle T_{3}=1-2F.

Finally, Munro et al.

II
Local-available quantum correlations of 2-qubit X states

In 2015, Mundarain and Ladrón de Guevara introduced local-available quantum correlations (LAQC)
Mundarain and de Guevara 2015
, a quantum correlation defined in terms of two mutually unbiased bases (MUB)
Schwinger 1960
. Those MUBs are, on the one hand, the optimal computational basis, defined as minimizing classical correlations, and, on the other hand, their complementary basis, given as the one that maximizes the LAQC quantifier.

Classical states are diagonal on a local basis, so one can define a classical state related to a given
\rho^{AB}
by choosing the original local computational basis to write it as

\Pi_{\rho^{AB}}=\sum_{i}R_{i}\outerproduct{i\,i}{i\,i},

(25)

where

R_{i}\equiv\expectationvalue{\rho^{AB}}{i\,i}.

(26)

Since this is only the classical state in a particular local basis, one can generalize this concept using the computational basis that results from applying local
U(2)
transformations to the original local computational bases. Given the two local computational bases,
\quantity{\ket{0}^{(I)},\ket{1}^{(I)}}
, with
I=A,B
, one can define new ones by having the following unitary operators acting on them

\mathbbm{U}_{I}=\matrixquantity[\lx@physics@matrix\cos\qty(\frac{\theta_I}{2}) & \sin\qty(\frac{\theta_I}{2}) \\
\sin\qty(\frac{\theta_I}{2}) \vb{e}^{i\phi_I} & -\cos\qty(\frac{\theta_I}{2}) \vb{e}^{i\phi_I}\endlx@physics@matrix]\;\;\in\;U(2),

(27)

where

0\leq\theta_{I}\leq\pi,\quad 0\leq\phi_{I}\leq 2\pi.

(28)

This parametrization leads to a set of classical states
\quantity{ \Pi_{\rho^{AB}} \qty(\theta_I,\phi_I)}
related to
\rho^{AB}
, given by

\Pi_{\rho^{AB}}\quantity(\theta_I,\phi_I)=\sum_{i}R_{i_{A},i_{B}}\quantity(\theta_I,\phi_I)\outerproduct{\widetilde{i\,i}}{\widetilde{i\,i}},

(29)

where

\displaystyle R_{i_{A},i_{B}}\quantity(\theta_I,\phi_I)

\displaystyle=\expectationvalue{\mathbbm{U}_{A\otimes{}B}^\dagger\rho^{AB}\mathbbm{U}_{A\otimes{}B}}{i\,i}

\displaystyle=\expectationvalue{\rho^{AB}}{\widetilde{i\,i}},

(30a)

\displaystyle\mathbbm{U}_{A\otimes{}B}

\displaystyle=\mathbbm{U}_{A}\otimes\mathbbm{U}_{B},\quad\mathbbm{U}_{I}\in U(2).

(30b)

One can write the mutual information of a given
\rho^{AB}
as

I\quantity(\rho^{AB})=\sum_{i,j}R_{i_{A},j_{B}}\log_{2}\quantity[\frac{R_{i_A,j_B}}{R_{i_A}R_{j_B}}],

(31)

where

\displaystyle R_{i_{A},j_{B}}

\displaystyle=\expectationvalue{\rho^{AB}}{i_A\,j_B}

(32a)

\displaystyle R_{i_{A}}

\displaystyle=\expectationvalue{\rho^I}{i_I},

(32b)

and
\rho^{I}
, with
I=A,B
, is the reduced operator corresponding to the
I
subsystem. Therefore, for a classical state
\Pi_{\rho^{AB}}
(
29
), the mutual information depends on the local parameters
\theta_{I}
and
\phi_{I}
:

I\quantity[\Pi_{\rho^{AB}}\qty(\theta_I,\phi_I)]=\sum_{i}R_{i}\quantity(\theta_I,\phi_I)\log_{2}\quantity[\frac{R_i\qty(\theta_I,\phi_I)}{R_{i_A} R_{i_B}}].

(33)

With this in mind, one can define the optimal computational basis as minimizing the above expression, which characterizes the classical correlation quantifier,

\mathcal{C}\quantity(\rho^{AB})=\min_{\theta_{I},\phi_{I}}\quantity\Big{I\qty[\Pi_{\rho^{AB}}\qty(\theta_I,\phi_I)]}.

(34)

Given the optimal computational basis, one must determine its complementary one. To do so, one starts by writing
\rho^{AB}
in the newly found optimal computational basis

\tilde{\rho}^{AB}=\mathbbm{U}_{A\otimes{}B}\;\rho^{AB}\;\mathbbm{U}_{A\otimes{}B}^{\dagger}.

(35)

In the above expression,
\mathbbm{U}_{A\otimes{}B}=\mathbbm{U}_{A\otimes{}B}\quantity(\theta_I,\phi_I)
is given in terms of the angles
\theta_{A},\theta_{B},\phi_{A}
, and
\phi_{B}
that relates the original computational basis with the optimal one. Either by considering the associated complex Hadamard matrix
Bengtsson 2007
;
Durt et al. 2010
or a basis defined by the eigenvectors of
\sigma_{\perp}
, that is, the Pauli matrix vector
\overrightarrow{{\bf\it\sigma}}
projected in a direction perpendicular to the
\sigma_{\hat{\mathbf{n}}}
that specifies the optimal computational basis, one has to define the complementary basis. To do so, one determines classical states in this new basis

\hat{\Pi}_{\rho^{AB}}\quantity(\Phi_I)=\sum_{i}\hat{R}_{i_{A},i_{B}}\quantity(\Phi_A,\Phi_B)\outerproduct{\widehat{i\,i}}{\widehat{i\,i}},

(36)

where

\displaystyle\hat{R}_{i_{A},i_{B}}\quantity(\Phi_A,\Phi_B)

\displaystyle=\expectationvalue{\hat{\mathbbm{U}}_{A\otimes{}B}^\dagger\,\rho^{AB}\,\hat{\mathbbm{U}}_{A\otimes{}B}}{\widetilde{i\,i}}

\displaystyle=\expectationvalue{\rho^{AB}}{\widehat{i\,i}},

(37a)

\displaystyle\hat{\mathbbm{U}}_{I}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\,}}\;\matrixquantity(\lx@physics@matrix 1 & 1 \\
\vb{e}^{i\Phi_I} & -\vb{e}^{i\Phi_I}\endlx@physics@matrix)\;\;\in{}U(2),

(37b)

with
0\leq\Phi_{I}\leq 2\pi
and
I=A,B
.

As in Eq. (
33
), one calculates the quantum mutual information for this classical state using Eq. (
31
):

I\quantity[\tilde{\Pi}_{\rho^{AB}}\qty(\Phi_A,\Phi_B)]=\sum_{i}\hat{R}_{i_{A},i_{B}}\log_{2}\quantity[\frac{\hat{R}_{i_A,i_B}}{\hat{R}_{i_A} \hat{R}_{i_B}}].

(38)

Mundarain and Ladrón de Guevara defined the LAQC quantifier
\mathcal{L}(\rho^{AB})
as the maximum of the above-written mutual information:

\mathcal{L}\quantity(\rho^{AB})=\max_{\Phi_{A},\Phi_{B}}\quantity{I\qty[\tilde{\Pi}_{\rho^{AB}}\qty(\Phi_A,\Phi_B)] }.

(39)

Contrary to other quantum correlations, such as quantum discord
Ollivier and Zurek 2001
;
Henderson and Vedral 2001
, where exact analytical expressions are only possible
Girolami and Adesso 2011
for a reduced set of 2-qubit states like Bell-Diagonal states
Luo 2008b
, here is one such result for the LAQC quantifier for 2-qubit X states
Albrecht Q. et al. 2022
;
Bellorin et al. 2022
. By defining the following functions in terms of the Bloch parameters and diagonal elements of
\rho_{X}
(
2
),

\displaystyle g_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1+T_{1}}{2}\log_{2}\quantity(1+T_1)+\frac{1-T_{1}}{2}\log_{2}\quantity(1-T_1),

(40a)

\displaystyle g_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1+T_{2}}{2}\log_{2}\quantity(1+T_2)+\frac{1-T_{2}}{2}\log_{2}\quantity(1-T_2),

(40b)

\displaystyle g_{3}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\Bigg\{a\,\log_{2}\quantity[\frac{1+x_3+y_3+T_3}{(1+x_3)(1+y_3)}]

\displaystyle\quad\mbox{{\color[rgb]{1,1,1}$\Bigg|$}}\quad+b\,\log_{2}\quantity[\frac{1+x_3-y_3-T_3}{(1+x_3)(1-y_3)}]

\displaystyle\quad\mbox{{\color[rgb]{1,1,1}$\Bigg|$}}\quad+c\,\log_{2}\quantity[\frac{1-x_3+y_3-T_3}{(1-x_3)(1+y_3)}]

\displaystyle\quad\mbox{ }\quad+d\,\log_{2}\quantity[\frac{1-x_3-y_3+T_3}{(1-x_3)(1-y_3)}]\Bigg\},

(40c)

where
a,b,c
, and
d
are the diagonal elements of
\rho_{X}
, the LAQC quantifier for 2-qubit X states is

\mathcal{L}\quantity(\rho_X)=\max\quantity\Big{g_1(T_1),g_2(T_2),g_3(x_3,y_3,T_3)}.

(41)

With this result, one can directly compute the LAQC quantifiers for the chosen one-parameter X states discussed in the previous section. For Werner states (
10

\mathcal{L}\quantity(\rho_w)=\frac{1+z}{2}\log_{2}\quantity(1+z)+\frac{1-z}{2}\log_{2}\quantity(1-z).

(42)

One can consider the concurrence
Wootters 1998
as a measure of entanglement. In such a case, we have the well-known result

C(\rho_{w})=\max\quantity{0\qc\frac{1}{2}\,\qty(3z-1)}.

(43)

IV
Summary and Conclusions

We have successfully applied the standard quantum correlation swapping (QCS) scheme to the redistribution of Local-Available Quantum Correlations (LAQC). Using 2-qubit X states as initial states, we derived a general expression of the resulting state after performing a projective (von Neumann) measurement on two of the subsystems. To illustrate this general result, we analyzed five families of one-parameter X states. Starting with the highly symmetrical Werner states, we also included two families of Bell-Diagonal states known as
\alpha
and
\beta
states. Furthermore, we included a one-parameter mixed state involving a basis element and maximally entangled mixed states as examples of X states with non-maximally mixed marginals. In this last category, we also analyzed the maximally entangled 2-qubit mixed states introduced by Munro et al.
Munro et al. 2001
.

We compared our results for the LAQC quantifier with the redistribution of entanglement by analyzing the concurrence of the final state. As expected from previous results regarding this quantum correlation, LAQC does not disappear if the initial states and the one used in the projective measurement are correlated. This feature exhibited by LAQC, which entanglement does not have, opens the possibility for this quantum correlation to be considered a genuine resource in quantum information technology.

Noninteracting tight-binding models for Fock parafermions
Edward McCann

Abstract
By mapping itinerant spin- 1 / 2 1/2 fermions to four-state Fock parafermions, we construct noninteracting tight-binding models for Fock parafermions in one dimension. They have single-particle real energy spectra consisting of a sum of single-particle energy levels each multiplied by a parafermionic occupation number. The single-particle levels may be determined by diagonalizing a square matrix whose order scales linearly with system size. These levels are the same as those of noninteracting fermionic models, as we show explicitly for the Rice-Mele model and the Su-Schrieffer-Heeger model. We show that the thermodynamic distribution function for the occupation numbers of noninteracting four-state parafermions is consistent with the mapping to spin- 1 / 2 1/2 fermions. We apply the mapping to create a parafermionic counterpart of the Kitaev superconducting chain and show that the ground state in the topological phase is fourfold degenerate, with each ground state distinguished by the fourfold parafermionic occupation numbers of Majorana edge modes.

\displaystyle H

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j}^{L}u_{j}\hat{N}_{j}+\sum_{j}^{L-1}t_{j}\big[c_{j}^{\dagger}(1-c_{j}^{\dagger}c_{j}+c_{j}^{\dagger 2}c_{j}^{2})(1-c_{j+1}^{\dagger}c_{j+1}+c_{j+1}^{\dagger 2}c_{j+1}^{2})c_{j+1}(-i)^{\hat{N}_{j}}+2c_{j}^{\dagger 2}c_{j+1}^{2}(-1)^{\hat{N}_{j}}+\mathrm{H.c.}\big],

\displaystyle c_{j}^{\dagger}|n_{1},\ldots,n_{j},\ldots n_{L}\rangle

\displaystyle(-i)^{\sum_{\ell<j}n_{\ell}}|n_{1},\ldots,n_{j}+1,\ldots n_{L}\rangle,

\displaystyle c_{j}|n_{1},\ldots,n_{j},\ldots n_{L}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i^{\sum_{\ell<j}n_{\ell}}|n_{1},\ldots,n_{j}-1,\ldots n_{L}\rangle,

\displaystyle\hat{N}_{j}|n_{1},\ldots,n_{j},\ldots n_{L}\rangle=n_{j}|n_{1},\ldots,n_{j},\ldots n_{L}\rangle,

\displaystyle\hat{N}_{j}=c_{j}^{\dagger}c_{j}+c_{j}^{\dagger 2}c_{j}^{2}+c_{j}^{\dagger 3}c_{j}^{3}.

\displaystyle c_{\ell}^{\dagger}c_{j}^{\dagger}=ic_{j}^{\dagger}c_{\ell}^{\dagger};\quad c_{\ell}c_{j}=ic_{j}c_{\ell};\quad c_{\ell}c_{j}^{\dagger}=-ic_{j}^{\dagger}c_{\ell}\quad\mathrm{for}\,\,\ell<j.

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{\sum_{\ell<j}(\hat{n}_{\ell\uparrow}+\hat{n}_{\ell\downarrow})}c_{j}^{2},

\displaystyle c_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-i)^{\sum_{\ell<j}\hat{n}_{\ell\uparrow}}[(-1)^{\sum_{\ell<j}\hat{n}_{\ell\downarrow}}f_{j\uparrow}+f_{j\uparrow}^{\dagger}f_{j\downarrow}],

IV
Parafermion distribution function

Given a single-particle energy spectrum, one would often like to know the occupation of the levels as a function of temperature
T
and chemical potential
\mu
.
Here, we show that the mapping to spin-
1/2
fermions is consistent with the thermodynamic distribution function by considering the mean occupation number of a single-particle level
\langle n_{\ell}\rangle
for four-state parafermions.
The grand canonical partition function
Z
may be represented as
Z=\prod_{\ell}Z_{\ell}
where the partition function for a given single-particle level is
Z_{\ell}=\sum_{n_{\ell}}\exp[\beta(\mu-\epsilon_{\ell})n_{\ell}]
, and
\beta=1/k_{\mathrm{B}}T
is inverse temperature
statphys
;

.
For four-state parafermions,

\displaystyle Z_{\ell}=1+x+x^{2}+x^{3};\qquad x=e^{\beta(\mu-\epsilon_{\ell})}.

(19)

The mean occupation number is given by

\displaystyle\langle n_{\ell}\rangle=\frac{1}{\beta}\frac{\partial\ln Z_{\ell}}{\partial\mu}=\frac{3+2x^{-1}+x^{-2}}{(1+x^{-1})(1+x^{-2})}.

(20)

This may be expressed as a sum of two fermionic terms,

\displaystyle\langle n_{\ell}\rangle=\langle n_{\ell\uparrow}\rangle+2\langle n_{\ell\downarrow}\rangle,

(21)

in agreement with the expectations of the mapping from parafermions to spin-1/2 fermions, Eq. (
11
).
Here,

\displaystyle\langle n_{\ell\uparrow}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{e^{\beta(\epsilon_{\ell}-\mu)}+1},

(22)

\displaystyle\langle n_{\ell\downarrow}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{e^{2\beta(\epsilon_{\ell}-\mu)}+1},

(23)

where the down spin component
\langle n_{\ell\downarrow}\rangle
has a Fermi-Dirac distribution, but at a lower effective temperature of
T/2
.
At low temperature,
\langle n_{\ell}\rangle\approx 3
for
\epsilon_{\ell}\ll\mu
, and
\langle n_{\ell}\rangle\approx 0
for
\epsilon_{\ell}\gg\mu

.

The variance

\displaystyle\langle(\Delta n_{\ell})^{2}\rangle=\langle n_{\ell\uparrow}\rangle(1-\langle n_{\ell\uparrow}\rangle)+4\langle n_{\ell\downarrow}\rangle(1-\langle n_{\ell\downarrow}\rangle).

(24)

As with the Fermi-Dirac distribution,

VI
Conclusions

We have described a generic tight-binding model and a topological superconductor model for four-state Fock parafermions and shown they are solvable models with a single-particle spectrum of real energies, Eqs. (
2
) and (
31
). This is achieved using a mapping from four-state parafermions to spin-
1/2
fermions, and, for the examples considered, the single-particle energy levels may be determined by solving a spin-
1/2
tight-binding model.
We note two points: The first is that the gauge transformation (
14
) and (
15
) removes string-related phases from the spin-
1/2
representations with nearest-neighbor hopping
asboth16
that depend on the occupancy of the other spin component, and such phases cannot be gauged away when considering longer-range hoppings. Including them would generally give a nonlocal parafermion Hamiltonian, unlike the examples described here, although this would not preclude a single-particle solution.
The second point is that the thermodynamic distribution function for noninteracting four-state parafermions (
21
), determined independently from the mapping (
6
) and (
7
), is consistent with the mapping, but yields a down spin component with a Fermi-Dirac distribution (
23
) at a lower effective temperature
T/2
.
Finally, we speculate that it may be possible to generalize the mapping to find solvable models with single-particle spectra for higher-state parafermions. The mapping may also provide an avenue for experimental simulation of parafermionic systems.

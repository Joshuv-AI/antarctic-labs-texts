1
Introduction and results

This paper continues our study
[
13
]
of Dirichlet spaces over planar
domains. Such spaces arise naturally in operator theory, planar potential
theory, and Teichmüller theory. Here we pass from the quadratic Dirichlet
space to the
p
-Dirichlet scale,
1<p<\infty
.

Throughout the paper,
\Gamma
is a rectifiable Jordan curve. Its
critical Besov space
B_{p}(\Gamma)=B_{p,p}^{1/p}(\Gamma)
consists, modulo
additive constants, of all measurable functions
u:\Gamma\to\mathbb{C}
such
that

\|u\|_{B_{p}(\Gamma)}^{p}=\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\iint_{\Gamma\times\Gamma}\frac{|u(z_{1})-u(z_{2})|^{p}}{|z_{1}-z_{2}|^{2}}|dz_{1}||dz_{2}|<\infty.

By the Jordan curve theorem,
\Gamma
separates the plane into the bounded
component
\Omega_{i}
and the unbounded component
\Omega_{e}
. For
p>1
, let

\mathcal{D}_{p}(\Omega_{i})
be the space of harmonic functions
U_{i}
on

\Omega_{i}
with finite
p
-Dirichlet energy

D_{p}(U_{i})=\frac{1}{2\pi}\iint_{\Omega_{i}}|\nabla U_{i}(w)|^{p}\lambda_{\Omega_{i}}^{2-p}(w)dA(w)<\infty.

(1.1)

Here,
dA
is planar area measure and

\lambda_{\Omega_{i}}(\varphi(z))=\frac{1}{(1-|z|^{2})|\varphi^{\prime}(z)|},

where
\varphi:\mathbb{D}_{i}\to\Omega_{i}
is conformal from the unit disk
\mathbb{D}_{i}
onto
\Omega_{i}
. For a
complex-valued function
U
and
w=u+iv
, we use the Hilbert–Schmidt norm

|\nabla U|=\bigl(|U_{u}|^{2}+|U_{v}|^{2}\bigr)^{1/2}=\bigl(2|U_{w}|^{2}+2|U_{\bar{w}}|^{2}\bigr)^{1/2}.

(1.2)

In particular,
|\nabla U|=|U_{w}|+|U_{\bar{w}}|
when
U
is
real-valued, whereas
|\nabla U|=\sqrt{2}\,|U^{\prime}|
when
U
is holomorphic.

The
p
-Dirichlet energy is invariant under conformal and
anticonformal bijections. Indeed, let
\Phi:\Omega_{1}\to\Omega_{2}
be such a
bijection and define
\sigma_{\Phi}=|\Phi_{z}|
in the conformal case and

\sigma_{\Phi}=|\Phi_{\bar{z}}|
in the anticonformal case. Under the change of
variables
w=\Phi(z)
,

dA(w)=\sigma_{\Phi}(z)^{2}dA(z),\qquad\lambda_{\Omega_{1}}(z)=\lambda_{\Omega_{2}}(\Phi(z))\sigma_{\Phi}(z).

For a harmonic function
U
on
\Omega_{2}
, the chain rule gives

|\nabla(U\circ\Phi)(z)|=\sigma_{\Phi}(z)|\nabla U(\Phi(z))|.

Combining these identities and applying the change of variables gives

D_{p}(U\circ\Phi)=D_{p}(U)
.

A function
U_{i}\in\mathcal{D}_{p}(\Omega_{i})
has conformally
nontangential boundary values
u
at arclength-almost every point of
\Gamma
.
Moreover, it is recovered from
u
by the Poisson formula

U_{i}=P(u\circ\varphi)\circ\varphi^{-1},

where
P
is the classical Poisson operator on
\mathbb{D}_{i}
. We call

D_{p}(U_{i})
the interior
p
-Dirichlet energy of
u
and write it as

\|u\|_{i}^{p}
.

For completeness, Peller’s result (
[
11
]
) asserts that
\mathcal{D}_{p}(\mathbb{D}_{i})\subset\text{VMO}(\mathbb{D}_{i})
for
p>1
. Since the latter space is included in the Hardy space of harmonic functions
h^{2}(\mathbb{D}_{i})
,
U_{i}\circ\varphi\;(\in\mathcal{D}_{p}(\mathbb{D}_{i}))
has angular limits almost everywhere on the unit circle
\mathbb{S}
. The F. and M. Riesz
theorem for rectifiable Jordan curves states that harmonic measure and arclength measure are mutually absolutely continuous. It therefore transfers the almost-everywhere
statement between
\mathbb{S}
and
\Gamma
.

To define
\mathcal{D}_{p}(\Omega_{e})
, regard
\infty
as an interior
point. After translating and scaling if necessary, assume that

0\in\Omega_{i}\subset\mathbb{D}_{i}
and set
\iota(z)=1/\bar{z}
. Then
\iota
maps
\Omega_{e}

onto a bounded Jordan domain
\widetilde{\Omega}_{i}
and sends
\infty
to
0
.
We call
U_{e}
harmonic on
\Omega_{e}\cup\{\infty\}
when

\widetilde{U}_{i}:=U_{e}\circ\iota
extends harmonically across
0
, and we set

U_{e}(\infty)=\widetilde{U}_{i}(0)
. Thus

\mathcal{D}_{p}(\Omega_{e})=\{\widetilde{U}_{i}\circ\iota:\widetilde{U}_{i}\in\mathcal{D}_{p}(\widetilde{\Omega}_{i})\}.

Its energy is

D_{p}(U_{e})=\frac{1}{2\pi}\iint_{\Omega_{e}}|\nabla U_{e}(w)|^{p}\lambda_{\Omega_{e}}^{2-p}(w)\,dA(w)=D_{p}(\widetilde{U}_{i}),

where the point
\infty
is omitted from the Euclidean integral and the
last equality follows from anticonformal invariance.

If
\widetilde{\varphi}:\mathbb{D}_{i}\to\widetilde{\Omega}_{i}
is
conformal, then

U_{e}=P(u\circ\iota\circ\widetilde{\varphi})\circ\widetilde{\varphi}^{-1}\circ\iota.

We call
D_{p}(U_{e})
the exterior
p
-Dirichlet energy of
u
and denote
it by
\|u\|_{e}^{p}
.

For
p=2
and
\Gamma=\mathbb{S}
, Douglas proved that the boundary
space of
\mathcal{D}_{2}(\mathbb{D}_{i})
is
B_{2}(\mathbb{S})
and that

\frac{1}{2\pi}\iint_{\mathbb{D}_{i}}|\nabla U_{i}(w)|^{2}\,dA(w)=\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\iint_{\mathbb{S}\times\mathbb{S}}\frac{|u(z_{1})-u(z_{2})|^{2}}{|z_{1}-z_{2}|^{2}}|dz_{1}||dz_{2}|.

This is the Douglas formula, introduced in Douglas’s solution of the
Plateau problem
[
3
]
. It also gives

\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\iint_{\mathbb{S}\times\mathbb{S}}\frac{|u(z_{1})-u(z_{2})|^{2}}{|z_{1}-z_{2}|^{2}}\,|dz_{1}|\,|dz_{2}|=\frac{1}{2\pi}\iint_{\mathbb{D}_{e}}|\nabla U_{e}(w)|^{2}\,dA(w).

Thus the same boundary seminorm has three equal representations:

\|u\|_{i}=\|u\|_{B_{2}(\mathbb{S})}=\|u\|_{e}.

In
[
13
]
we identified the rectifiable Jordan curves for which
these three seminorms are equivalent:

\|\cdot\|_{i}\simeq\|\cdot\|_{B_{2}(\Gamma)}\simeq\|\cdot\|_{e}

holds if and only if
\Gamma
is a chord-arc curve. Here and below,

A\simeq B
means that
C^{-1}A\leq B\leq CA
for a constant independent of
the function under consideration.

Recall that a rectifiable Jordan curve is chord-arc (or

K
-chord-arc) if the shorter of the two subarcs joining any

z_{1},z_{2}\in\Gamma
has length at most
K|z_{1}-z_{2}|
.

For every
p>1
, it is known that

\|\cdot\|_{i}\simeq\|\cdot\|_{e}\simeq\|\cdot\|_{B_{p}(\Gamma)}

on a chord-arc curve; see
[
7
]
; that is, there
is a constant
C>0
for which the following inequalities hold:

(a)

\|\cdot\|_{B_{p}(\Gamma)}\leq C\|\cdot\|_{i}
and
\|\cdot\|_{B_{p}(\Gamma)}\leq C\|\cdot\|_{e}
;

(b)

\|\cdot\|_{i}\leq C\|\cdot\|_{B_{p}(\Gamma)}
and
\|\cdot\|_{e}\leq C\|\cdot\|_{B_{p}(\Gamma)}
.

More precisely, the interior and
exterior energies are equivalent if and only if the curve
\Gamma
is a quasicircle, by the critical
composition theorem
[
1
, Theorem 12]
and conformal and anticonformal invariances of
p
-Dirichlet energies. Thus, if
\Gamma
is a rectifiable quasicircle then one inequality in (a) (also in (b)) implies the other with a different constant
C
.

We investigate the converses to these estimates.

Theorem 1

(Main Theorem)
.

Let
\Gamma
be a rectifiable quasicircle. Then the following statements
hold.

(1)

If
(a) holds for some
p>1
, then
\Gamma
is a chord-arc curve.

(2)

There exists a non-chord-arc rectifiable quasicircle
\Gamma
for which (b) holds for every
p>1
.

Remark 2
.

For a harmonic function
U
on one side
\Omega=\{\Omega_{i},\Omega_{e}\}
, define the weighted energy for
1<p<\infty
,
0<s<1
, as

\mathcal{E}_{s,p;\Omega}(U):=\left(\iint_{\Omega}|\nabla U(z)|^{p}\lambda_{\Omega}(z)^{1-(1-s)p}dA(z)\right)^{1/p}.

The
p
-Dirichlet spaces
\mathcal{D}_{p}(\Omega)
are a special case of Besov-Sobolev spaces

\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega):=\{U:\ U\text{ harmonic in }\Omega,\ \mathcal{E}_{s,p;\Omega}(U)<\infty\},

understood modulo constants.
Indeed, the
p
-Dirichlet spaces correspond to the case
s=1/p
and these are precisely the conformally invariant members of the
Besov–Sobolev scale. Whenever
the corresponding trace estimate holds, the trace operator is first defined
on smooth functions by restriction to
\Gamma
and then extended to

\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega)
by completion. If
\Gamma=\partial\Omega
is a rectifiable curve we equip boundary measurable functions
u:\Gamma\to\mathbb{C}
with the
homogeneous Douglas-type Besov seminorm

\|u\|_{B_{p,p}^{s}(\Gamma)}:=\left(\iint_{\Gamma\times\Gamma}\frac{|u(z)-u(\zeta)|^{p}}{|z-\zeta|^{1+sp}}|dz||d\zeta|\right)^{1/p}<\infty.

and denote by
B_{p,p}^{s}(\Gamma)
the space of measurable functions for which
this seminorm is finite, understood modulo constants.

We announce here that
suppose
\Gamma
is a rectifiable quasicircle then if
\Gamma
is chord-arc then

\|\operatorname{Tr}_{\Gamma}U\|_{B^{s}_{p,p}(\Gamma)}\lesssim\mathcal{E}_{s,p;\Omega_{i}}(U),\qquad\|\operatorname{Tr}_{\Gamma}U\|_{B^{s}_{p,p}(\Gamma)}\lesssim\mathcal{E}_{s,p;\Omega_{e}}(U),

(1.3)

and conversely, the validity of either one of these inequalities implies
\Gamma
is chord-arc. This, in particular, shows that the converse assertion in part (1) of Main Theorem
is not a consequence of the conformal invariance peculiar to the critical
space
\mathcal{D}_{p}(\Omega)
. Rather, it is a geometric property shared by
the entire Besov–Sobolev scale considered above. A detailed proof,
together with a careful treatment of the more precise relationship between
B_{p,p}^{s}(\Gamma)
and
\text{Tr}_{\Gamma}\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s}(\Omega)
, will appear in a
forthcoming paper.

The paper is organized as follows.
Section
2
gives a characterization of Ahlfors-regular curves.
Section
3
uses this characterization to prove part (1) for
2\leq p<\infty
, while Section
4
treats
1<p<2
. Section
5
constructs
the example in part (2).

2
Ahlfors-regular curves

We begin with the definition of Ahlfors regularity.

Definition
.

Let
\Gamma
be a rectifiable curve in the plane. We call
\Gamma

M
-regular if, for every
z\in\mathbb{C}
and
r>0
,

\mathrm{length}(\Gamma\cap B(z,r))\leq Mr,

where
B(z,r)
stands for the open disk centered at
z
and of radius
r
.

Theorem 3
.

Let
\Gamma
be a rectifiable curve in the plane. The following
statements are equivalent.

(i)

\Gamma
is an
M
-regular curve;

(ii)

There exists
C>0
such that for every
w\notin\Gamma
,

\int_{\Gamma}\frac{|dz|}{|z-w|^{2}}\leq\frac{C}{d(w,\Gamma)},

where
d(w,\Gamma)
denotes the distance from
w
to
\Gamma
.

A proof of
\rm(ii)\Rightarrow\rm(i)
was given in

[
14
]
, where the result is attributed to Y. Meyer; see also
[
13
]
.
We give a different proof, based on porosity and inspired by
[
2
]
.
The elementary implication
\rm(i)\Rightarrow\rm(ii)
is proved in

[
13
,
2
]
.

Definition
.

A compact set
K\subset\mathbb{C}
is porous if there
exist constants
0<c<1
and
r_{0}>0
such that, for every
z\in K
and every

0<r\leq r_{0}
, the disk
B(z,r)
contains a disk
B(z^{\prime},cr)
disjoint from
K
.

Lemma 4
.

If
\Gamma
satisfies
(ii) and is porous, then it
satisfies (i).

Proof.

It is enough to consider disks
B(z,r)
with
z\in\Gamma
and

0<r\leq r_{0}
; larger disks are handled using the finite length of

\Gamma
. Let

B(z^{\prime},cr)\subset B(z,r)\setminus\Gamma

be the porous hole. For every
w\in\Gamma\cap B(z,r)
, we have

|w-z^{\prime}|\leq 2r
, while
d(z^{\prime},\Gamma)\geq cr
. Hence

\displaystyle{\rm length}(\Gamma\cap B(z,r))\leq 4r^{2}\int_{\Gamma\cap B(z,r)}\frac{|dw|}{|w-z^{\prime}|^{2}}\leq\frac{4Cr^{2}}{d(z^{\prime},\Gamma)}\leq\frac{4C}{c}\,r.

This proves (i).
∎

Proof of
(ii)
\Rightarrow
(i)
.

For convenience, we use the
\ell^{\infty}
-norm

\|x+iy\|_{\infty}=\max\{|x|,|y|\},

so that
B_{\infty}(z_{0},r)
is the square centered at
z_{0}
with side
length
2r
. Since the Euclidean and
\ell^{\infty}
-norms are
equivalent, this change affects only the constants.

By Lemma
4
, it is enough to prove that condition (ii)
implies that
\Gamma
is porous. Consider the closed square

\overline{B_{\infty}(z_{0},r)}
, and choose

\zeta\in\overline{B_{\infty}(z_{0},r)}
such that

d(\zeta,\Gamma)=\max\bigl\{d(z,\Gamma):z\in\overline{B_{\infty}(z_{0},r)}\bigr\}.

There is a square contained in
B_{\infty}(z_{0},r)
, having
\zeta
as
a vertex and side length
d(\zeta,\Gamma)
, whose interior is disjoint
from
\Gamma
. Let
z_{0}^{\prime}
be the center of this square. Thus the

B_{\infty}\left(z_{0}^{\prime},\frac{d(\zeta,\Gamma)}{2}\right).

If

\delta=d(z_{0}^{\prime},\Gamma),

then

\frac{1}{2}d(\zeta,\Gamma)\leq\delta\leq\frac{3}{2}d(\zeta,\Gamma).

(2.1)

By construction, every closed subsquare of

\overline{B_{\infty}(z_{0},r)}
with side length
2\delta
meets

\Gamma
. To prove porosity, it remains to show that

d(\zeta,\Gamma)
is bounded below by a constant multiple of
r
.
In view of (
2.1
), it is enough to prove the corresponding lower
bound for
\delta
.

Condition (ii), applied at
z_{0}^{\prime}
, gives

\int_{\Gamma}\frac{|dz|}{|z-z_{0}^{\prime}|^{2}}\leq\frac{C}{\delta}.

We next obtain a lower bound for this integral. By translating and
rescaling, we may assume that the square under consideration is

B_{\infty}(0,1)
. We may also assume that

\operatorname{diam}(\Gamma)\geq 3
.

The square
B_{\infty}(0,1)
contains a square
\mathcal{C}
of side
length
1
having
z_{0}^{\prime}
as a vertex. We define the
heart
of
a square
B_{\infty}(\xi,\rho)
to be the concentric square

B_{\infty}(\xi,\rho/3)
. If
\Gamma
meets the heart of

B_{\infty}(\xi,\rho)
, then

{\rm length}\bigl(\Gamma\cap B_{\infty}(\xi,\rho)\bigr)\geq\frac{2\rho}{3}.

Divide
\mathcal{C}
into
N^{2}
subsquares of side length
1/N
,
where
N
is chosen so that

\frac{1}{3N}\geq 2\delta\geq\frac{1}{3(N+1)}.

(2.2)

The right-hand inequality in (

) shows that the Euclidean

of each subsquare is less than

,
provided that
\delta<1/12
. If
\delta\geq 1/12
, the desired lower

(
2.2
) shows that the side length of each subsquare is at least

.

Let
\mathcal{C}_{j}
be one of these subsquares whose distance from

is at least
6\delta
. The preceding observations imply that

\Gamma
meets the heart of
\mathcal{C}_{j}

. It follows that

Summing over all such subsquares

Therefore

Here

(

\Omega=\Omega_{i}
and
\delta(x)=\operatorname{dist}(x,\Gamma)
for
x\in\Omega
.

We use the bounded-turning characterization of quasicircles: a Jordan curve
\Gamma
is
a
K
-quasicircle if, for every
a,b\in\Gamma
, one of the two subarcs
joining
a
to
b
has diameter at most
K|a-b|
.

We prove part (1) of Theorem
1
for
1<p<2
using the
interior inequality in (a), bounded turning, and the almost-Dirichlet
principle in Lemma
7
. It suffices to prove that there is a
constant
M
, independent of
x\in\Gamma
and
0<r<\operatorname{diam}(\Gamma)
, such
that

{\rm length}(\Gamma\cap B(x,r))\leq Mr.

(4.1)

Indeed, this implies Ahlfors regularity. If

B(y,r)\cap\Gamma\neq\varnothing
, choose
x\in B(y,r)\cap\Gamma
; then

\Gamma\cap B(y,r)\subset\Gamma\cap B(x,2r)
, and (
4.1
) gives

{\rm length}(\Gamma\cap B(y,r))\leq 2Mr.

The case
B(y,r)\cap\Gamma=\varnothing
is trivial. Thus
\Gamma
is
Ahlfors-regular and, being a quasicircle, is chord-arc.

For
x\in\Gamma
and
r>0
, write

L_{x}(r)=\operatorname{length}(\Gamma\cap B(x,r))
.

Lemma 5
.

Let
\Gamma
be a rectifiable
K
-quasicircle of length
\ell
. For
every
x\in\Gamma
and
R>0
, the set
\Gamma\cap B(x,R)
is contained in
the union of two
subarcs
\alpha_{+}
and
\alpha_{-}
issuing from
x
, such that

\alpha_{+}\cup\alpha_{-}\subset B(x,2KR)

and

{\rm length}(\alpha_{+})+{\rm length}(\alpha_{-})\leq 2L_{x}(2KR).

(4.2)

Proof.

Choose an arclength parametrization

\gamma:[0,\ell]\longrightarrow\Gamma,\qquad\gamma(0)=\gamma(\ell)=x,

which is one-to-one on
[0,\ell)
. For
s\in(0,\ell)
, the two arcs joining

x
and
\gamma(s)
are
\gamma([0,s])
and
\gamma([s,\ell]).

If
\gamma(s)\in B(x,R)
, then at least one of these two
arcs has diameter at most
K|x-\gamma(s)|<KR
, so this arc is contained in

B(x,KR)
.

The parameters
s
for which
\operatorname{diam}(\gamma([0,s]))<KR
form an
initial interval; its closure determines
\alpha_{+}
. Define
\alpha_{-}

similarly from the opposite orientation. Both arcs lie in
B(x,2KR)
, and
the preceding paragraph shows that their union contains

\Gamma\cap B(x,R)
. Since each
\alpha_{\pm}
is contained in

\Gamma\cap B(x,2KR)
,

{\rm length}(\alpha_{\pm})\leq L_{x}(2KR).

Adding the two estimates proves (
4.2
).
∎

We use the following tubular-neighborhood estimate

[
10
, Lemma 4.2]
: if
\alpha
is a rectifiable arc, then

\bigl|\{w:\operatorname{dist}(w,\alpha)<t\}\bigr|\leq C\bigl(t\ell(\alpha)+t^{2}\bigr)

(4.3)

where
C
is universal. This can also be seen directly:
choose at most
\lfloor\ell(\alpha)/t\rfloor+2
points with successive
arclength separation at most
t
. Disks of radius
2t
centered at these
points cover the
t
-neighborhood and yield (
4.3
).

Lemma 6
.

Let
1<p<2
and let
\Gamma
be a rectifiable
K
-quasicircle. Then,
for every
x\in\Gamma
and
r>0
,

\int_{\Omega\cap B(x,2r)}\delta(w)^{p-2}\,dA(w)\leq C_{p}\bigl(r^{p-1}L_{x}(8Kr)+r^{p}\bigr).

(4.4)

Proof.

For
x\in\Gamma
and
0<t\leq 2r
, define

E_{t}=\{w\in\Omega\cap B(x,2r):\delta(w)<t\}.

If
w\in E_{t}
, choose
\xi\in\Gamma
with

\delta(w)=|\xi-w|
. Then

|\xi-x|\leq|\xi-w|+|w-x|=\delta(w)+|w-x|<t+2r\leq 4r.

Lemma
5
, applied with
R=4r
, shows that all such points
\xi
are
contained in two arcs
\alpha_{+}
and
\alpha_{-}
lying in

B(x,8Kr)
with

{\rm length}(\alpha_{+})+{\rm length}(\alpha_{-})\leq 2L_{x}(8Kr).

Consequently,
E_{t}
lies in the
t
-neighborhood of

\alpha_{+}\cup\alpha_{-}
. By (
4.3
),

|E_{t}|\leq C\bigl(tL_{x}(8Kr)+t^{2}\bigr),\qquad 0<t\leq 2r.

(4.5)

Every
w\in B(x,2r)
satisfies
\delta(w)\leq|w-x|<2r
. Put

t_{k}=2^{1-k}r
and decompose
\Omega\cap B(x,2r)
into the layers

D_{k}=\{w:t_{k+1}\leq\delta(w)<t_{k}\},\qquad k=0,1,2,\cdots.

Since
D_{k}\subset E_{t_{k}}
and, because
p-2<0
,

\delta(w)^{p-2}\leq t_{k+1}^{p-2}=2^{2-p}t_{k}^{p-2}\quad\text{on }D_{k},

estimate (
4.5
) gives

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega\cap B(x,2r)}\delta(w)^{p-2}\,dA(w)

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}t_{k}^{p-2}\bigl(t_{k}L_{x}(8Kr)+t_{k}^{2}\bigr)

\displaystyle=C_{p}\left(L_{x}(8Kr)\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}t_{k}^{p-1}+\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}t_{k}^{p}\right).

Both geometric series converge; their sums are bounded by constant
multiples of
r^{p-1}
and
r^{p}
, respectively. This proves (
4.4
).
∎

For
p\neq 2
, the harmonic extension need not minimize the weighted

p
-energy, but it satisfies an almost-Dirichlet principle. Namely, if
U

is the harmonic extension of
u
and
V\in C(\overline{\Omega})\cap C^{1}(\Omega)
is any extension of the same boundary data, then

\iint_{\Omega}|\nabla U(z)|^{p}\delta(z)^{p-2}dA(z)\leq C\iint_{\Omega}|\nabla V(z)|^{p}\delta(z)^{p-2}dA(z)

with a constant depending only on
p
. The disk version
is the periodic counterpart of
[
9
, Theorem 1.11]
.

Lemma 7
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded Jordan domain and
1<p<\infty
. Then the
almost-Dirichlet principle holds for
\mathcal{D}_{p}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

On
\mathbb{D}
, the principle reads

\iint_{\mathbb{D}}|\nabla U(z)|^{p}(1-|z|)^{p-2}dA(z)\leq C\iint_{\mathbb{D}}|\nabla V(z)|^{p}(1-|z|)^{p-2}dA(z)

(4.6)

where
U
is harmonic and
V
has the same boundary trace. Let

\varphi:\mathbb{D}\to\Omega
be conformal. Under
w=\varphi(z)
, the
weighted integral transforms as

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}}|\nabla(F\circ\varphi)(z)|^{p}(1-|z|)^{p-2}\,dA(z)

\displaystyle\qquad=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla F(w)|^{p}\bigl((1-|\varphi^{-1}(w)|)|\varphi^{\prime}(\varphi^{-1}(w))|\bigr)^{p-2}\,dA(w).

Koebe’s theorem gives

(1-|z|)|\varphi^{\prime}(z)|\simeq\delta(\varphi(z)),

with absolute comparison constants. Applying the disk principle to

U\circ\varphi
and
V\circ\varphi
, and then using this comparison, yields

\iint_{\Omega}|\nabla U(w)|^{p}\delta(w)^{p-2}\,dA(w)\leq C_{p}\iint_{\Omega}|\nabla V(w)|^{p}\delta(w)^{p-2}\,dA(w).

(4.7)

This proves the assertion.
∎

We now prove part (1) of Theorem
1
for
1<p<2
in two
steps.

Step 1: establish a recurrence for
L_{x}(r)/r
.

Fix
x\in\Gamma
and
r>0
. Choose a real-valued function

\chi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(B(0,2))
such that
\chi\equiv 1
on
B(0,1)
, and define

\displaystyle F_{1}(z)

\displaystyle=\chi\left(\frac{z-x}{r}\right)\frac{\operatorname{Re}(z-x)}{r},

(4.8)

\displaystyle F_{2}(z)

\displaystyle=\chi\left(\frac{z-x}{r}\right)\frac{\operatorname{Im}(z-x)}{r}.

Then

|\nabla F_{j}|\leq\frac{C_{\chi}}{r},\qquad\operatorname{supp}(\nabla F_{j})\subset B(x,2r),\qquad j=1,2.

(4.9)

Let
u_{j}=F_{j}|_{\Gamma}
. Since
\chi=1
on
B(0,1)
, for

z,\zeta\in\Gamma\cap B(x,r)
we have

u_{1}(z)-u_{1}(\zeta)=\frac{\operatorname{Re}(z-\zeta)}{r},\qquad u_{2}(z)-u_{2}(\zeta)=\frac{\operatorname{Im}(z-\zeta)}{r}.

Using

|\operatorname{Re}(z-\zeta)|^{p}+|\operatorname{Im}(z-\zeta)|^{p}\geq c_{p}|z-\zeta|^{p}

and restricting both Besov integrals to

(\Gamma\cap B(x,r))^{2}
, we obtain

\lVert u_{1}\rVert_{B_{p}(\Gamma)}^{p}+\lVert u_{2}\rVert_{B_{p}(\Gamma)}^{p}\geq c_{p}r^{-p}\int_{\Gamma\cap B(x,r)}\int_{\Gamma\cap B(x,r)}|z-\zeta|^{p-2}\,|dz|\,|d\zeta|.

(4.10)

Since
|z-\zeta|\leq 2r
and
p-2<0
,

|z-\zeta|^{p-2}\geq(2r)^{p-2}.

This is precisely where
p<2
is used. Thus (
4.10
) implies

\lVert u_{1}\rVert_{B_{p}(\Gamma)}^{p}+\lVert u_{2}\rVert_{B_{p}(\Gamma)}^{p}\geq c_{p}\left(\frac{L_{x}(r)}{r}\right)^{2}.

(4.11)

On the other hand, Lemma
7
, (
4.9
),
Lemma
6
, and Koebe’s comparison between the hyperbolic and distance
weights give

\lVert u_{1}\rVert_{i}^{p}+\lVert u_{2}\rVert_{i}^{p}\leq Cr^{-p}\int_{\Omega\cap B(x,2r)}\delta(w)^{p-2}\,dA(w)\leq C\left(1+\frac{L_{x}(8Kr)}{r}\right).

(4.12)

By the interior inequality in (a), with constant
C_{0}
,

\lVert u_{1}\rVert_{B_{p}(\Gamma)}^{p}+\lVert u_{2}\rVert_{B_{p}(\Gamma)}^{p}\leq C_{0}^{p}(\lVert u_{1}\rVert_{i}^{p}+\lVert u_{2}\rVert_{i}^{p}).

Combining this estimate with (
4.11
) and (
4.12
)
yields

\left(\frac{L_{x}(r)}{r}\right)^{2}\leq C_{1}\left(1+\frac{L_{x}(8Kr)}{r}\right),

(4.13)

where
C_{1}
depends only on
p
,
C_{0}
,
K
, and the fixed cutoff
\chi
.

Step 2: iterate to prove
L_{x}(r)\lesssim r
.

Put

A_{x}(r)=\frac{L_{x}(r)}{r}.

Since

\frac{L_{x}(8Kr)}{r}=8KA_{x}(8Kr),

we may absorb
8K
into the constant in (
4.13
) and obtain

A_{x}(r)^{2}\leq C_{2}\bigl(1+A_{x}(8Kr)\bigr).

(4.14)

After increasing a constant
B\geq 1
if necessary,

A_{x}(r)\leq B\max\{1,A_{x}(8Kr)^{1/2}\}.

(4.15)

Let
D=\operatorname{diam}(\Gamma)
and
L=\operatorname{length}(\Gamma)
.
If
r\geq D
, then
A_{x}(r)\leq L/D
. If
0<r<D
, let
N
be the least
positive integer such that
(8K)^{N}r\geq D
. Applying
(
4.15
) successively at the scales

r,8Kr,\ldots,(8K)^{N-1}r
gives

\displaystyle A_{x}(r)

\displaystyle\leq B^{1+1/2+\cdots+1/2^{N-1}}\max\{1,A_{x}((8K)^{N}r)^{1/2^{N}}\}

\displaystyle\leq B^{2}\max\left\{1,\frac{L}{D}\right\}.

(4.16)

Indeed, the geometric sum in the exponent is less than
2
, and

A_{x}((8K)^{N}r)=\frac{L_{x}((8K)^{N}r)}{(8K)^{N}r}\leq\frac{L}{D}.

Thus
L_{x}(r)\leq Mr
with
M
independent of
x
and
r
. This
completes the proof for
1<p<2
.

5
Part
(2)
of Theorem
1

We now construct the example in part (2) of Theorem
1
.
Let
\varphi:\mathbb{D}_{i}\to\Omega_{i}
be a Riemann map. The classical
rectifiability criterion states that
\Gamma=\partial\Omega_{i}
is rectifiable
if and only if
\varphi^{\prime}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{D}_{i})
; see
[
12
, Chapter 3]
.

Definition
.

A rectifiable Jordan domain
\Omega_{i}
is a Smirnov domain if

\varphi^{\prime}
is outer in
H^{1}(\mathbb{D}_{i})
; equivalently,

\log|\varphi^{\prime}(z)|=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{S}}\frac{1-|z|^{2}}{|\zeta-z|^{2}}\log|\varphi^{\prime}(\zeta)|\,|d\zeta|,\qquad z\in\mathbb{D}_{i}.

(5.1)

Lavrentiev proved that chord-arc domains are Smirnov domains

[
6
]
; more generally, a Jordan domain with Ahlfors-regular boundary is
Smirnov
[
15
]
. On the other hand, the constructions in
[
4
,
5
]

give a noncircular quasidisk
\Omega_{i}
with rectifiable boundary for which
the derivative of a Riemann map is a nonconstant singular inner function:

|\varphi^{\prime}(\zeta)|=1\quad\text{\rm for almost\;all }\,\zeta\in\mathbb{S},\quad(0<)|\varphi^{\prime}(z)|<1\quad\text{\rm for}\,z\in\mathbb{D}_{i}.

(5.2)

Consequently, harmonic measure at
\varphi(0)
equals normalized
arclength measure. Since a function that is both inner and outer is constant
up to a unimodular factor, (
5.2
) and the noncircularity of
\Omega_{i}

show that (
5.1
) fails. In particular,
\Gamma
cannot be chord-arc.

Because
|\varphi^{\prime}|=1
almost everywhere on
\mathbb{S}
, the boundary
map
t\mapsto\varphi(e^{it})
is an arclength parametrization and

\operatorname{length}(\Gamma)=2\pi
. Moreover, the shorter parameter arc
gives

|\varphi(e^{it})-\varphi(e^{is})|\leq\pi\left|\sin\frac{t-s}{2}\right|=\frac{\pi}{2}|e^{it}-e^{is}|.

(5.3)

It follows that

\displaystyle\|u\|_{B_{p}(\Gamma)}^{p}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{|u\circ\varphi(e^{it})-u\circ\varphi(e^{is})|^{p}}{|\varphi(e^{it})-\varphi(e^{is})|^{2}}\,dt\,ds

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\pi^{4}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{|u\circ\varphi(e^{it})-u\circ\varphi(e^{is})|^{p}}{|e^{it}-e^{is}|^{2}}\,dt\,ds

\displaystyle=\frac{4}{\pi^{2}}\|u\circ\varphi\|_{B_{p}(\mathbb{S})}^{p}.

The unit-circle trace theorem (the periodic form of

[
9
, Theorems 1.3 and 1.9]
) asserts
\|u\circ\varphi\|_{i}\simeq\|u\circ\varphi\|_{B_{p}(\mathbb{S})}
. Conformal invariance then yields

\|u\|_{i}\lesssim\|u\circ\varphi\|_{B_{p}(\mathbb{S})}\lesssim\|u\|_{B_{p}(\Gamma)}.

Finally, because
\Gamma
is a quasicircle, the interior and exterior

p
-Dirichlet energies are equivalent. Hence

\|u\|_{e}\lesssim\|u\|_{i}\lesssim\|u\|_{B_{p}(\Gamma)}.

Thus both inequalities in (b) hold for every
p>1
, while
\Gamma

is not chord-arc. This proves part (2).

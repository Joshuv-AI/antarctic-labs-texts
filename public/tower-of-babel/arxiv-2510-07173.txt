NurseLLM : The First Specialized Language Model for Nursing
Md Tawkat Islam Khondaker Julia Harrington Shady Shehata

Abstract
Recent advancements in large language models (LLMs) have significantly transformed medical systems. However, their potential within specialized domains such as nursing remains largely underexplored. In this work, we introduce NurseLLM , the first nursing-specialized LLM tailored for multiple choice question-answering (MCQ) tasks. We develop a multi-stage data generation pipeline to build the first large scale nursing MCQ dataset to train LLMs on a broad spectrum of nursing topics. We further introduce multiple nursing benchmarks to enable rigorous evaluation. Our extensive experiments demonstrate that NurseLLM outperforms SoTA general-purpose and medical-specialized LLMs of comparable size on different benchmarks, underscoring the importance of a specialized LLM for the nursing domain. Finally, we explore the role of reasoning and multi-agent collaboration systems in nursing, highlighting their promise for future research and applications.

Related Works

Currently, there are no LLMs developed specifically for nursing; most nursing applications rely on generic or medical LLMs. While LLMs have shown promise in optimizing nursing workflows, personalizing care, and improving healthcare accessibility
Zhou et al. 2024
, existing research highlights significant limitations. Studies report that general LLMs, including
ChatGPT-3.5
, demonstrate indecisiveness in clinical assessments, suggest unnecessary diagnostic tests, and misunderstand nursing concepts, leading to a tendency for over-triage compared to human clinicians
Saban and Dubovi 2024
. Moreover, these models depend heavily on pre-existing data, which may become outdated without continual updates, compromising their reliability and potentially propagating obsolete or inaccurate information
Huang 2023
;
Zhou et al. 2024
. Additionally, the lack of transparency in training data and model processes raises concerns regarding accuracy, safety, and usability in clinical practice
Glauberman et al. 2023
;
Gonzalez 2024
. Issues such as implicit bias and data-shift can further exacerbate inequities in care, emphasizing the urgent need for nursing-specific LLMs optimized through rigorous research to support clinical decision-making effectively
Zhou et al. 2024
;
Saban and Dubovi 2024
.

LLMs in Medical Application.
In recent years, researchers have developed a diverse range of LLMs
Christophe et al. 2024
;
Ankit Pal 2024
;
MedAlpaca 2024
focusing on medical and clinical settings. Examples of such LLMs include BioMistral
Labrak et al. 2024
, BioMedGPT
Zhang et al. 2023
, Meditron
Chen et al. 2023b
. A number of medical LLMs
Gururajan et al. 2024
;
Yu et al. 2025
have also been created on the top of a generalized LLM like LLaMA
Dubey et al. 2024
. More recently, inspired by the strong performance of the reasoning LLMs, especially in medical applications
Qin et al. 2024
,
Chen et al. 2024b
develop HuatuoGPT-o1, providing valuable insights for enhancing the complex medical reasoning capabilities of LLMs.

LLMs are commonly benchmarked on the existing MCQ datasets on medical knowledge like MedQA
Jin et al. 2021
, MedMCQA
Pal et al. 2022
, and MMLU-Medical
Hendrycks et al. 2021
. Recently,
Zuo et al. 2025
introduce more challenging benchmark MedXpertQA to evaluate expert-level and advanced reasoning capabilities of LLMs spanning across
11
body systems. However, recent studies
Chen et al. 2024a
;
Liu et al. 2024
;
Jeong et al. 2024
expose a critical gap of medical LLMs on the clinical domain.

Despite recent advancements in medical LLMs, existing research has largely overlooked the development and evaluation of LLMs specifically tailored to the nursing domain. This gap in the literature motivates us to develop
NurseLLM
and introduce novel benchmarks specifically curated to address the unique needs and challenges within nursing practice.

3.1
Topic Collection

In order to generate diverse NCLEX question-answer pairs, we first produced a comprehensive and exhaustive taxonomy of NCLEX topics. Two
Registered Nurses with NCLEX expertise defined the high-level classification of
specializations
based on the NCLEX questions available in books and web corpora. Each specialization is further categorized into
domains
based on the expertise required for a nurse to be classified into that respective specialization. Then, analyzing the questions, they created the
topic
list for each domain and diversified the topics into different
concepts
. As a result, we created a comprehensive and diverse list of
7

Specializations
,
60

Domains
,
232

Topics
, and
1,830

Concepts
. A sample of this taxonomy is presented in Figure
2
. We share the full taxonomy in Appendix
F
.

3.2
Synthetic NCLEX QA Generation

Figure 3:

Prompt template used for synthetic data generation.

After creating the diverse list of concepts, we prompt GPT-4o to generate corresponding NCLEX MCQ question-answer (QA) from those concepts. The prompt to generate QA is given in Figure
3
. Similar to the real NCLEX exam, we also generate the rationale that justifies the correct answer. We use this rationale later to train
NurseLLM
with chain-of-thought (CoT)
Wei et al. 2022

C

GPT4o-Test Benchmark Generation

Along with generating the synthetic data for training, we also generate a synthetic benchmark for testing the models. For this purpose, we generate one NCLEX QA per concept with GPT-4o and use these
1,830
samples as
GPT4o-Test
benchmark.

3.3
Benchmarks Creation

To rigorously test LLMs on the nursing tasks, we develop multiple NCLEX-based benchmarks. First, we develop a human-labeled gold NCLEX benchmark comprising
1,726
NCLEX questions (
NCLEX-Test
). Our Registered Nurses manually create these
1,726
NCLEX questions based on NCLEX expertise and knowledge gained from experience as educators. They further verified the correct answer as well as the rationales that justify the answer. We use this NCLEX-Test as our prime benchmark to test LLMs.

We further employ GPT-4o to extract nursing exclusive questions from MultiMedQA
Singhal et al. 2023
. MultiMedQA is a collection consisting of multiple generic medical QA datasets. We prompt GPT-4o to identify the QA samples exclusively relevant to nursing topics (prompt for this topic modeling is presented in Appendix
A
). In this way, we mine a subset of MultiMedQA, namely
MultiNurseQA
to evaluate LLMs on the nursing-related questions.

Therefore, as summarized in Section
4.3
, we develop three distinct benchmark datasets: NCLEX-Test, GPT4o-Test, MultiNurseQA, and in conjunction with MultiMedQA, we provide a comprehensive and rigorous framework for evaluating LLMs across both nursing-specific and general medical domains.

3.4
Decontamination

On one hand, it is important to decontaminate the trainings samples from the test set to ensure the generalization of
NurseLLM
, on the other hand, the generated training samples need to be unique to ensure diversity. However, comparing each question against a large-scale dataset is computationally expensive. Hence, to make the decontamination process efficient, we devise a two-step filtration.
(i)
First we decontaminate each generated sample against the whole
GPT4o-Test
and
NCLEX-Test
benchmarks to make sure that there is no overlapping between the train and the test datasets.
(ii)
If a sample passes the first step, then we compare this sample against the pool of the same concept, meaning that we compare this sample against the already generated training samples assigned to the same concept. Since each concept is unique, samples from different concepts will likely also be diverse. Therefore, we can avoid comparing a sample against a pool of samples from different concepts. Following
Taori et al. 2023
, we use the ROUGE-L score for the sample comparison. We apply the threshold of
0.5
and filter out any sample that exceeds this score.

Finetuning LLMs on new data can cause catastrophic forgetting

6.2
NurseLLM
as Multi-Agent System

Figure 7:

Workflow of our multi-agent collaborative system.

The exploration of multi-agent systems (MAS) has gained increasing attraction among researchers
Hu et al. 2024
;
Ke et al. 2025
. Inspired by
Kim et al. 2024
, we deploy
NurseLLM
as a multi-agent collaborative system (Figure
7
) to study its capability in the nursing domain. The workflow of our MAS is designed as follows:
(1)
upon receiving a nursing question an
Orchestrator
LLM first determines the difficulty of the question.
(2)
If the difficulty is determined as easy, the question is then routed to a single LLM. Otherwise, the question is routed to a group of LLMs dynamically assigned by the
Orchestrator
, where each LLM is an
Expert
in different nursing specializations. The
Experts
then involve in a multi-round collaborative debate. Finally, a
Moderator
analyzes discussions among the
Experts
and provides the final answer as the verdict. We present responses from MAS in Appendix
E
.

We evaluate the performance of MAS on NCLEX-Test benchmark. We find that MAS exhibits
79.87
% accuracy improving over SoTA
NurseLLM
by
3.62
%. This finding demonstrates that we can further improve the capability of nursing LLMs by deploying in a multi-agent collaborative system. We provide runtime comparison across different model configurations in Appendix
B
.

9
Conclusion

In this work, we introduce
NurseLLM
, the first LLM specifically tailored for nursing question-answering. We design a multi-stage data generation pipeline to construct the first large-scale, diverse nursing QA dataset. We further develop three rigorous NCLEX-style benchmarks for the comprehensive evaluation of LLMS on the nursing domain. Our extensive experiments demonstrate that
NurseLLM
significantly outperforms strong generalized and medical LLMs of comparable size, underscoring the necessity of domain specialization in the nursing field. Finally, we explore the potential of reasoning-driven and multi-agent collaborative approaches within the nursing domain, showcasing their potential for advancing intelligent systems in nursing applications.

10
Limitations and Ethics Statement

10.1
Limitations

Data Generation Timeline.
In this work, we use
GPT-4o
version from May 2024. Since the model can be updated on a regular interval, the prompting strategy and the data generation pipeline discussed in Section
3
should be treated accordingly, since the model’s responses can change over time
Chen et al. 2023a
.

Data Quality.
We develop an automated data generation pipeline to create a large scale diverse nursing-focused QA dataset. (Section
3
). Although this automated pipeline makes the overall data generation process scalable, it comes at the risk of allowing low-quality data in the corpus. Hence, we suggest human inspection to remove any sort of potential vulnerability and maintain a standard quality of the corpus.

Model Responses.
We show that
NurseLLM
exhibits impressive performance on NCLEX questions compared to the other similar baselines. However, looking at the results (Section
5
), we believe there is still room for improvement for the models in terms of accuracy. This also indicates, LLM can produce inaccurate responses while answering to the nursing questions. Given the sensitiveness of the field, we recommend that
NurseLLM
should be couched with caution before deployment.

Model Evaluation.
We only use open-sourced models while conducting evaluation on our NCLEX benchmarks. As discussed in Section
4
, we omit evaluating closed-source LLMs since (i) the size of the closed-source models is not comparable to our baselines, and (ii) we do not intend to expose the NCLEX-Test benchmark to closed-source LLMs due to the proprietary reasons. However, we aim to release our synthetic test benchmarks as well as the training dataset upon acceptance.

10.2
Ethics Statement

Data Collection.
As mentioned in Section
3
, we create a large-scale training dataset from a wide range of diverse nursing topics. We share the complete topic list in Appendix
F
to facilitate the NCLEX samples generation.

Intended Use of
NurseLLM
.
The application of
NurseLLM
is to solve NCLEX-style nursing MCQ question while also providing corresponding rationale. We aim to help researchers build nursing-specialized LLMs.
NurseLLM
can also be regarded as a promising baseline to develop more robust and effective LLMs suited for nursing applications.

Potential Misuse and Bias.

Our NCLEX corpus and
NurseLLM
model can potentially be misused to generate inaccurate and biased clinical data. For these reasons, we recommend that
NurseLLM
not be used in applications without careful prior consideration of potential misuse and bias.

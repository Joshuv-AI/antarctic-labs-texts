Abstract
. We show that for any clopen collection 𝔛 \mathfrak{X} of subgroups of G G with finite Weyl groups the category of G G -spectra with geometric isotropy in 𝔛 \mathfrak{X} is equivalent to the category of equivariant sheaves over 𝔛 \mathfrak{X} . This gives an algebraic model of injective dimension at most the rank of G G .

1.C.
Ingredients

In Part 1, our task is to understand the category of sheaves over

\mathfrak{X}

In the short Part 2, we then introduce the notion of component

Proof :
Since all the terms are acyclic by Lemma

3.3
, we need only verify the cube of sheaves is exact, and
this can be done stalkwise. If
x\in\mathfrak{D}_{a}
then

(\mathcal{R}_{A})_{x}=0
if
a_{0}>a
, so we only need to consider
\mathcal{R}_{A}
for

A\subseteq[a]
. Furthermore, if
a\not\in A
the map

(\mathcal{R}_{A})_{x}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}(\mathcal{R}_{Aa})_{x}.

This means that the entire limit is from
\emptyset\longrightarrow A=\{a\}
, where we evidently
have the isomorphism

\mathbb{Q}=(\mathbb{Q}_{\mathfrak{X}})_{x}\longrightarrow(R_{\{a\}})_{x}=(\mathbb{Q}_{a})_{x}=\mathbb{Q}.

∎

Corollary 3.6
.

The homotopy limit of the punctured cube
R_{\bullet}
of
rings is its limit

\Gamma(\mathbb{Q}_{\mathfrak{X}})\simeq\mathop{\mathop{\mathrm{holim}}\limits_{\leftarrow}}\nolimits_{A\neq\emptyset}R_{A},

the ring of global sections of the constant sheaf.

Proof :
Taking global sections, we have a spectral sequence for calculating
the cohomology of the homotopy pullback sheaf. Since the sheaves

\mathbb{Q}_{\mathfrak{X}}
and
\mathcal{R}_{A}
are soft, the spectral sequence
collapses to the complex of global sections. Since
H^{0}(\mathfrak{X},\mathcal{R}_{A})=R_{A}
this is the augmented adelic complex. Since the
constant sheaf is soft, the complex is exact.
∎

Warning 3.7
.

The categories of modules over the sheaf
\mathcal{R}_{A}
and the
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra

R_{A}
will generally not be equivalent. For example
\mathcal{R}_{0}
is the constant sheaf over the
discrete space
\mathfrak{D}_{0}
so its module category is the product

\prod_{d\in\mathfrak{D}_{0}}(\mathbb{Q}\mbox{-mod})
. On the other hand the category of
modules over
R_{0}=\prod_{d\in\mathfrak{D}_{0}}\mathbb{Q}
is much larger (for example it includes the module
\prod\mathbb{Q}/\bigoplus\mathbb{Q}
, which has trivial stalks).

4.
Adelic description of sheaves

For scattered Stone spaces
\mathfrak{X}
, one may show directly that the
abelian category of sheaves over
\mathfrak{X}
is equivalent to the category
of modules over the ring of global sections
\Gamma(\mathbb{Q}_{\mathfrak{X}})
. This
just amounts to saying the reconstruction of a sheaf from an étale space only involves constructions that can be expressed using idempotents in the ring.

The present section gives an alternative and less direct approach that
parallels the construction of the algebraic model of
G
-spectra over

\mathfrak{X}
in Part 3.
In Sections
2
and
3
we defined
punctured cubes of rings, one in
the category of vector spaces and one in the category of sheaves over

\mathfrak{X}
. The first gives a model for modules over
\Gamma(\mathbb{Q}_{\mathfrak{X}})
in
terms of modules over diagrams of simpler rings. The second
gives a model for sheaves over
\mathfrak{X}
in terms of modules over diagrams
of sheaves of rings. These two diagram categories are different but equivalent.

4.A.
The category
\mathcal{A}(\mathfrak{X})

In Section

we introduced the punctured cubical diagram
\{R_{A}\}_{A\neq\emptyset}
of rings, and Corollary

shows that we have

\Gamma(\mathbb{Q}_{\mathfrak{X}})=\mathop{\mathop{\mathrm{lim}}\limits_{\leftarrow}}\nolimits_{A}R_{A}\simeq\mathop{\mathop{\mathrm{holim}}\limits_{\leftarrow}}\nolimits_{A}R_{A}.

We now form a related abelian category,

that will be used
in the comparison. We follow
[

]

Definition 4.1
.

The
standard model
on

is the
category

of cocartesian

-modules.

In more detail, an

Lemma 4.2

The inclusion

Since localization and tensor products commute with colimits,

∎

On the other hand, the diagram

5.
Dimension 1

The 1-dimensional case is very attractive because we can be completely
explicit, but most of the interesting features already appear.

5.A.
The context

We suppose the topological space
\mathfrak{X}
is the one-point compactification of a
countably infinite set
\mathcal{F}

we write

pure strata

In this case there are no fewer than five distinct points of view on

geometric isotropy in

model, the complete model and the category of modules over the ring of

\mathbb{Q}

mentioning all five is that they do embody

significant changes and are only equivalences because of special
algebraic features of this case.

5.D.

It is both instructive and reassuring to make the homological algebra

Lemma 5.7

Lemma 5.8

All sheaves are soft.

Proof :

Part II
Component structures and equivariant sheaves

6.
Component structures on Stone spaces

The structures arising from rational
G
-spectra consist of

equivariant
sheaves. In Part 1 we described sheaves over Stone spaces
and in Part 2 we describe the equivariance. In effect this consists of
the action of a finite group on each stalk of the sheaf.

6.A.
Component structures

The notion of
component structure codifies the structure required of this collection
of finite groups
\mathcal{W}_{x}
.

Definition 6.1
.

For a Stone space
\mathfrak{X}
, a
component structure
consists of a finite
group
\mathcal{W}_{x}
for each point
x\in\mathfrak{X}
, and for each
x
there is
a clopen neighbourhood
U_{x}
of
x
with a homomorphism
i^{x}_{y}:\mathcal{W}_{y}\longrightarrow\mathcal{W}_{x}
for all
y\in U_{x}
. These are required to be transitive in
the sense that if
z\in U_{y}\cap U_{x}
and
y\in U_{x}
, we have

i_{z}^{x}=i_{y}^{x}i_{z}^{y}
.

Example 6.2
.

If
H
is a compact Lie group the space
\Phi H
of conjugacy
classes of closed subgroups with finite Weyl group is a Stone
space, as is any compact subset.

By the upper semicontinuity of normalizers
[
7
, 10.3]
,
every subgroup
K\in\Phi H
has a neighbourhood
U_{K}
so that
if
L\subseteq K
and

(L)\in U_{K}
then
N_{G}(L)\subseteq N_{G}(K)
.

We take the component structure in which
\mathcal{W}_{K}=\pi_{0}(W_{G}(K))
. The
component structure is given by using the neighbourhoods
U_{K}
and
the inclusion induces a map

N_{G}(L)/L\longrightarrow N_{G}(K)/L\longrightarrow N_{G}(K)/K,

and applying
\pi_{0}
we obtain the component structure. This is independent of choice of
representatives; indeed, by the Montgomery-Zippin Theorem two
representatives of the conjugacy class
L
lying in
K
will
be conjugate by an element of
N_{G}(K)
. This means the map

\mathcal{W}_{L}=N_{G}(L)/L\longrightarrow N_{G}(K)/L\longrightarrow N_{G}(K)/K=\mathcal{W}_{K}

depends only on the conjugacy classes
(L)
and
(K)
.

Since the maps are induced by inclusions of normalizers, the
transitivity condition is automatic.

When component structures are used for sheaves of rational vector
spaces we may package the relevant structure in a more familiar way,
making sense of the idea that

\mathcal{W}=\coprod_{x}\mathcal{W}_{x}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{X}

has local properties dual to that of an étale space.

Lemma 6.3

A component structure
\mathcal{W}
on
\mathfrak{X}
gives rise to a sheaf of group
rings
\mathbb{Q}\mathcal{W}
on
\mathfrak{X}

x

\mathbb{Q}\mathcal{W}_{x}
and the
germ-spreading map

\mathbb{Q}\mathcal{W}_{x}\longrightarrow\mathbb{Q}\overline{\mathcal{W}_{y}}\cong(\mathbb{Q}\mathcal{W}_{y})^{K(x,y)}\longrightarrow\mathbb{Q}\mathcal{W}_{y},

\overline{\mathcal{W}_{y}}=\mathrm{im}(\mathcal{W}_{y}\longrightarrow\mathcal{W}_{x})

We have given four equivalent abelian categories: the standard model,

\mathfrak{X}
(codified as étale spaces over

\mathfrak{X}

in sheaves,

Given a component structure

of
\mathcal{W}
-equivariant objects in each of these cases. Finally, we will observe that the

Suppose

is a Stone space of finite Cantor-Bendixson rank with

\mathcal{W}

•

When considering a finite group

We now have three different but equivalent abelian categories of equivariant

Part III
Algebraic models of Weyl-finite rational
G
-spectra

Blocks of subgroups with finite Weyl groups are particularly simple, but there are
still issues to be dealt with that have not arisen for tori or in low
dimension.

As in other cases, the framework of the proof is given by two adelic pullback
cubes, one in topology and one in algebra. The one in topology shows
one can assemble the category of
G
-spectra from simple pieces,
and the one in algebra shows that one can assemble the algebraic
model from simple pieces. We need only check that the simple pieces
in topology and algebra are equivalent. For blocks with finite Weyl
groups we saw in Part 1 that
the adelic cube describes how to build a sheaf on a filtered space
from its stalks when the pure strata are discrete and in Part 2 we
upgraded this to take account of the component structure. We
described the algebra of this in detail, and in Section

show that the pullback cube can be realised in homotopy theory where
it is also a pullback. Parts 1 and 2 showed that the entire algebraic
model can be recovered from the stalks and the component structure.
In Part 3, we show the same is true on the topological side.

in degree 0. By Shipley’s Theorem and the formality of DGAs with
homotopy in degree 0, this completes the argument.

9.A.
Stalkwise

Working with singleton isotropy is straightforward.

Lemma 9.1
.

If
R^{top}_{K}
is a ring
G
-spectrum with geometric isotropy concentrated
at
K
where
W_{G}(K)
is finite
then the category of
R^{top}_{K}
-modules is generated by
G/K_{+}\wedge R^{top}_{K}
.

Proof :
By construction, the category of
R^{top}_{K}
-module
G
-spectra
is generated by the objects

G/H_{+}\wedge R^{top}_{K}
. It remains to show that
each of these can be built from the one with
H=K
.

First, by geometric isotropy, the only terms
G/H_{+}\wedge R^{top}_{K}
which are non-trivial
are those with
K
conjugate to a subgroup of
H
. Suppose then that
K\subseteq H
.

The composite
H/H_{+}\longrightarrow H/K_{+}\longrightarrow H/H_{+}
is multiplication by the
Burnside ring element
[H/K]
. When applied to an object with
geometric isotropy
K
this is multiplication by
|H/K^{K}|=W_{H}(K)
;
since it is non-zero it is a unit in
\mathbb{Q}
.
∎

9.B.
Vertexwise

We now pick a non-empty height flag
A=\{a_{0}>a_{1}>\cdots>a_{s}\}
and
assemble the contributions to the category of modules over
R^{top}_{A}
.

Lemma 9.2
.

The category of
R^{top}_{A}
-module
G
-spectra
is generated by the image of the single object

R^{top}_{A}[G/\bullet_{+}]:=\prod_{\mathrm{ht}(H_{0})=a_{0}}\mathcal{I}^{-1}_{H_{0}|a_{1}}\prod_{\mathrm{ht}(H_{1})=a_{1}}\mathcal{I}^{-1}_{H_{1}|a_{2}}\cdots\prod_{\mathrm{ht}(H_{s-1})=a_{s-1}}\mathcal{I}^{-1}_{H_{s-1}|a_{s}}\prod_{\mathrm{ht}(H_{s})=a_{s}}R^{top}_{H_{s}}\wedge(G/H_{s})_{+}.

Proof :
We have argued in Lemma
9.1
that there
G/H_{+}\wedge R^{top}_{K}
is either trivial (if

K
is not subconjugate to
H
) or a retract of
G/K_{+}\wedge R^{top}_{K}
. Applying this at each point gives the required argument.
∎

Now we have a single generator, it is natural to consider the
endomorphism ring spectrum

\mathcal{E}_{A}=\mathrm{Hom}_{R^{top}_{A}}(R^{top}_{A}[G/\bullet_{+}],R^{top}_{A}[G/\bullet_{+}]).

Proposition 9.3
.

\pi^{G}_{*}(\mathcal{E}_{A})=R_{A}[\mathcal{W}].

Proof :
There are formal calculations we apply iteratively.

Argument 1: we make an
argument of the following form, for suitable
G
-spectra
P_{i}

[\prod_{i}P_{i},\prod_{j}P_{j}]^{G}=\prod_{j}[\prod_{i}P_{i},P_{j}]^{G}=\prod_{j}[P_{j},P_{j}]^{G};

the first equality is the universal property of the product. The
second involves showing
[\prod_{i\neq j}P_{i},P_{j}]^{G}=0
.

Argument 2: we make an
argument of the following form, for suitable
G
-spectra
P_{i}

[\prod_{i}\mathcal{I}^{-1}_{i}P_{i},\prod_{j}\mathcal{I}^{-1}_{j}P_{j}]^{G}=\prod_{j}[\prod_{i}\mathcal{I}^{-1}_{i}P_{i},\mathcal{I}^{-1}_{j}P_{j}]^{G}=\prod_{j}\mathcal{I}^{-1}_{j}[P_{j},P_{j}]^{G};

the first equality is the universal property of the product. The
second begins as before by showing
[\prod_{i\neq j}\mathcal{I}^{-1}_{i}P_{i},\mathcal{I}^{-1}_{j}P_{j}]^{G}=0
, as in Argument 1, and then

[\mathcal{I}^{-1}_{j}P_{j},\mathcal{I}^{-1}_{j}P_{j}]^{G}=[P_{j},\mathcal{I}^{-1}_{j}P_{j}]^{G}=\mathcal{I}^{-1}_{j}[P_{j},P_{j}]^{G}.

Here the first step is the universal property of the localization

\mathcal{I}^{-1}_{j}
and the second is a smallness statement for
P_{j}
.

When
A
is of length 0, we need only apply Argument 1 with

P_{a}=R_{a}
. The justification is that since all the terms are of the
same height
a_{0}
, there is a neighbourhood of each element
j
not
containing any
i
and hence
[\prod_{i}P_{i},P_{j}]^{G}=0
. This justifiation
applies in all cases where we use this argument.

When
A=(a_{0}>a_{1})
we have

\begin{array}[]{rcl}[\prod_{i_{0}}\mathcal{I}^{-1}_{i_{0}}\prod_{i_{1}}R_{i_{1}},\prod_{j_{0}}\mathcal{I}^{-1}_{j_{0}}\prod_{j_{1}}R_{j_{1}}]^{G}&=&\prod_{j_{0}}[\prod_{i_{0}}\mathcal{I}^{-1}_{i_{0}}\prod_{i_{1}}R_{i_{1}},\mathcal{I}^{-1}_{j_{0}}\prod_{j_{1}}R_{j_{1}}]^{G}\\
&=&\prod_{j_{0}}[\prod_{i_{1}}R_{i_{1}},\mathcal{I}^{-1}_{j_{0}}\prod_{j_{1}}R_{j_{1}}]^{G}\\
&=&\prod_{j_{0}}\mathcal{I}^{-1}_{j_{0}}[\prod_{i_{1}}R_{i_{1}},\prod_{j_{1}}R_{j_{1}}]^{G}\\
&=&\prod_{j_{0}}\mathcal{I}^{-1}_{j_{0}}\prod_{j_{1}}[\prod_{i_{1}}R_{i_{1}},R_{j_{1}}]^{G}\\
&=&\prod_{j_{0}}\mathcal{I}^{-1}_{j_{0}}\prod_{j_{1}}[R_{j_{1}},R_{j_{1}}]^{G}\end{array}

We note here that the smallness of
\prod_{i_{1}}R_{i_{1}}
involves
working with maps of modules over that ring.

∎

Lemma 9.4
.

There is an equivalence of categories

R^{top}_{A}\mbox{-mod-$G$-spectra}\simeq R_{A}[\mathcal{W}]\mbox{-mod}.

Proof :
Since the category of

\tilde{R}^{top}_{A}
-modules is generated by
\tilde{R}^{top}_{A}[G/\bullet_{+}]
,
Morita theory gives an equivalence between
\tilde{R}^{top}_{A}
-modules in
G
-spectra
and modules over the endomorphism ring spectrum
\mathcal{E}_{A}

By Proposition
9.3
,
\pi^{G}_{*}(\mathcal{E})=R_{A}[\mathcal{W}]
; by Shipley’s
Theorem this is equivalent to modules over a DGA. Since its homotopy
groups are entirely in degree 0, it is formal and equivalent
to the ring
R_{A}[\mathcal{W}]
.
∎

9.C.
Assembly

It remains only to assemble the appropriate conclusion to the
equivalences we have obtained at each vertex.

Theorem 9.5
.

There is an equivalence

\mbox{$G$-{\bf spectra}}\langle\mathfrak{X}\rangle\simeq DG-\mathcal{A}(\mathfrak{X},\mathcal{W})

Proof :
By Corollary
8.4
, the sphere
S^{0}_{\mathfrak{X}}
is the homotopy pullback
of the diagram
R^{top}_{\bullet}
, so that the category of

G
-spectra over
\mathfrak{X}
is
equivalent to the homotopical standard model (cocartesian diagrams of
modules over
R^{top}_{\bullet}
).

By Lemma
9.4

each of the terms
R^{top}_{A}
-modules is equivalent to the
corresponding diagram of modules over
R_{A}[\mathcal{W}]
.

Finally from Lemma
7.3

in Part 2, this is equivalent to the diagram of modules over

R_{\mathfrak{X}}[\mathcal{W}]
-modules, and by Subsection

diagram of
\mathcal{W}
-equivariant sheaves over

.
∎

10.
Examples of Weyl-finite components

We will arrange the examples by dimension, which here refers to the Cantor-Bendixson
rank of the block (all blocks for one dimensional groups are one dimensional, but there are
many other examples). Many such examples were studied in
[
16
]
. We will use the contents of

[
18
]
to describe the general situation: the only purpose of
this section is to demonstrate that examples of Weyl-finite blocks are both
ubiquitous and under full calculational control.

10.A.
1-dimensional examples

Suppose we have a group
G
with identity component a torus
T
and
component group
W=\pi_{0}(G)
. If the action of

W
on
H_{1}(G;\mathbb{Q})
gives a non-trivial simple representation then
the block of full subgroups is a 1-dimensional Weyl-finite block.

Examples of this type can be easily constructed from the integral
representation theory of
W
. If
\Lambda_{0}
is a
non-trivial integral representation of
W
with

\Lambda_{0}\otimes\mathbb{Q}
simple we may form
G=T\rtimes W
, where
T
is the
Pontrjagin dual of
\Lambda^{0}=\mathrm{Hom}(\Lambda_{0},\mathbb{Z})
. If
H^{2}(W;T)\cong H^{3}(W;\Lambda_{0})
is
non-zero there are also non-split forms. A little more generality can be
obtained by making the identity component
\Sigma\times_{Z}T
for a
semisimple group
G
, but for definiteness we can restrict attention
to
G=T\rtimes W
.

The full subgroups of
G
(those meeting all components of
G
) form a
block. For each
W
-submodule
\Lambda^{\prime}
of
\Lambda^{0}
, there are finitely
many conjugacy classes (in bijection to
H^{2}(W;\Lambda^{\prime})
), so
altogether there is a countably infinite set
\mathfrak{D}_{0}
of finite
subgroups and
G
is the only height 1 full subgroup.

The first few naturally occurring examples to come to mind are
as follows.

Example 10.1
.

(a) The group
G=O(2)
with
W
of order 2 give the block
consisting of
G
and the dihedral subgroups, with

\Lambda_{0}=\tilde{\mathbb{Z}}
. With the same
W
and
\Lambda_{0}
, we may also take

G=SO(3),Pin(2)
or
SU(2)
.

(b) Take
G
to be the normalizer of the maximal torus in
SU(3)

and
W
to be the Weyl group
\Sigma_{3}
. In this case
\Lambda_{0}=H_{1}(ST(3))

is the reduced regular representation of
\Sigma_{3}
. Again with same

W
and
\Lambda_{0}
we may take
G=SU(3)
or
PSU(3)
.

10.B.
2 -dimensional Weyl-finite blocks

Similar constructions give examples of higher dimensions. The simplest
case arise from an integral representation

\Lambda_{0}=\Lambda_{0}^{\prime}\oplus\Lambda_{0}^{\prime\prime}
of
W
where

\Lambda_{0}^{\prime}\otimes\mathbb{Q}
and
\Lambda_{0}^{\prime\prime}\otimes\mathbb{Q}
are non-trivial and
simple. We may again take
T
Pontrjagin dual to
\Lambda^{0}=\mathrm{Hom}(\Lambda_{0},\mathbb{Z})
,
and take the split extension
G=T\rtimes W
.

The situation is simplest if the representations
\Lambda_{0}^{\prime}\otimes\mathbb{Q}
and

\Lambda_{0}^{\prime\prime}\otimes\mathbb{Q}
are distinct. In this case, sub-representations of

\Lambda_{0}
are products of subrepresentations of
\Lambda_{0}^{\prime}
and

\Lambda_{0}^{\prime\prime}
:

W-\mathrm{Sub}(\Lambda_{0})=W\!-\!\mathrm{Sub}(\Lambda_{0}^{\prime})\times W\!-\!\mathrm{Sub}(\Lambda_{0}^{\prime\prime}).

Since the cohomology of
(\Lambda^{\prime})^{S}\oplus(\Lambda^{\prime\prime})^{S}
is the
sum of the two factors, the space of full subgroups of
G
is the product of those of

G^{\prime}=\Lambda_{0}^{\prime}\rtimes W
and
G^{\prime\prime}=\Lambda_{0}^{\prime\prime}\rtimes W
.

10.C.
An unmixed 2-dimensional Weyl-finite block

Finally, we consider the first example where the two rational
representations are isomorphic to illustrate the additional complexity.
We take
W
to be of order 2, and
\Lambda_{0}=\tilde{\mathbb{Z}}\oplus\tilde{\mathbb{Z}}
.

Up to group isomorphism, there are only two toral groups with this data:
one is the split extension and the other is a non-split
extension (there are three non-split extensions, but they are
isomorphic as groups).

Because
W
acts as negation on
\Lambda^{0}
, there is no restriction
on the lattice
\Lambda^{S}
. Furthermore
W
acts as negation on
\Lambda^{S}
and

H^{2}(W;\Lambda^{S})=0
. If
G
is a split extension then since
any full subgroup is also a split
extension, and the subgroups are in bijective correspondence to
subgroups
S
of
T^{2}
.

Now consider the space
\mathrm{Sub}(T^{2})
.
There is one 2-dimensional group. Then the 1-dimensional groups
S

correspond to 1-dimensional lattices
\Lambda^{S}
.
We may think of the 1-dimensional lattices as lying over

\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{Q})
with
S
mapping to
\Lambda^{S}\otimes\mathbb{Q}
. The fibres of
this map can be identified with the positive integers, as discussed at
the end of
[
18
, Remark 6.7]
.
The 0-dimensional groups
F
correspond to 2-dimensional lattices

\Lambda^{F}
.

10.D.
The space of full subgroups of the non-split extension

If
G
is a non-split extension then one of the three elements of

T^{2}
of order 2 is distinguished, because it is a square of an
element outside
T^{2}
. Writing
x_{e}
for the distinguished element,
the full subgroups are in bijective correspondence to
subgroups of
T^{2}
containing
x_{0}
. The corresponding subgroups of

\Lambda^{S}
are precisely those lying in the index 2 sublattice

\Lambda^{e}\subseteq\Lambda^{0}
corresponding to
x_{e}
.
Because
W
acts as negation, there is no further restriction on

\Lambda^{S}
.

The space of subgroups can be described exactly as in the split case
but with
\Lambda^{0}
replaced by
\Lambda^{e}
.

10.E.
Component structure

The Weyl group of a subgroup
S
is the
dual of
\Lambda^{S}/2\Lambda^{S}
, which is
C_{2}\times C_{2}
for
finite subgroups,
C_{2}
for 1-dimensional subgroups and is trivial for

G
. This also gives the component structure since if
F\subset S
we
find
\Lambda^{F}\supset\Lambda^{S}
, and there is a map

(\Lambda^{F}/2\Lambda^{F})\longleftarrow(\Lambda^{S}/2\Lambda^{S})
whose dual is the
map in the component structure.

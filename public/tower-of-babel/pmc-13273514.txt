Differential response of soil microbial communities involved in N-cycling to short-term N input depends on the history of N application
Authors: Yehua Yang, Yanling Liu, Han Xiong, Xingcheng Huang, Yarong Zhang, Huaqing Zhu, Huan Yang, Tengxia He, Yu Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
SummaryThis study focuses on investigating the response mechanism of nitrogen cycling microorganisms to nitrogen addition gradients in yellow soil with and without long-term nitrogen application, in order to optimize nitrogen fertilizer management strategies in yellow soil areas. The results showed that nitrogen addition significantly affected the alpha diversity index, gene relative abundance, and species relative abundance of nitrogen cycling microorganisms in soils with a history of long-term nitrogen application. Among the treatments, high nitrogen addition contributed the most to differences in community structure. In contrast, nitrogen cycling microorganisms in soils without long-term nitrogen application exhibited weaker responses to short-term nitrogen addition. Redundancy analysis indicates that differences in soil chemical properties are important drivers of differences in soil nitrogen cycling microbial community composition, and long-term nitrogen application history determines the specificity of microbial responses to short-term nitrogen gradients.Subject areas: Microbiology, Soil biology, Soil chemistry, Soil science

Introduction

Soil nitrogen cycling is the core process involved in maintaining ecosystem productivity, and its regulatory mechanism directly affects agricultural environmental health and sustainability.1 In this process, microbial communities play the role of invisible engineers, driving the transformation, fixation, and utilization of nitrogen through complex metabolic networks (Gelaye and Getahun, 2024).2 Research has shown that soil microorganisms are highly sensitive to environmental changes, especially short-term nutrient fluctuations. Dynamic changes in functional microbial community structure often occur before changes in soil physical and chemical properties and are key biological indicators for characterizing soil environmental status.3 The analysis of microbial community structure acts as an early warning system for evaluating the benefits and potential ecological risks of nitrogen fertilizer application and helps guide agricultural environmental management.

Currently, significant progress has been made in the study of the effects of nitrogen fertilizer application on soil microbial community structure and function, but different studies have shown a certain degree of heterogeneity. The results of a cucumber nitrogen application experiment revealed that the addition of medium to high levels of nitrogen significantly increased the relative abundance of bacterial functional groups related to nitrogen cycling.4 Research on ginseng rhizosphere soil has shown that high nitrogen levels significantly increase the abundance of microbial genes involved in the nitrification process of ginseng rhizosphere soil.5 Research on the rhizosphere soil of goji berries has shown that high nitrogen levels significantly decrease the diversity and stability of bacterial communities, whereas low nitrogen levels increase the relative abundance of ammonia-oxidizing bacteria, especially Nitroso spiral bacteria.6 Research on long-term nitrogen application in grasslands worldwide has shown that nitrogen addition has little effect on the relative abundance of nitrogen-fixing bacteria and ammonia-oxidizing archaea in soil but significantly increases the abundance and alpha diversity of ammonia-oxidizing bacteria.7 Paddy soil, the abundance of ammonia-oxidizing archaea significantly decreased with increasing nitrogen levels, whereas the abundance of ammonia-oxidizing bacteria decreased. High nitrogen fertilizer input increases the complexity of the microbial network in paddy soil.8 The microbial community structure of soil nitrogen cycling is influenced by factors such as the nitrogen fertilizer application rate, crop type, and rhizosphere selection. In addition, factors such as fertilizer type,9 climatic conditions,10 planting system,11 and fertilization timing12 can affect soil nitrogen cycling microorganisms. Further research has indicated that nitrogen addition alters the microbial community structure of soil nitrogen cycling by inducing changes in soil available nitrogen content.13 More research suggests that induced changes in soil pH are the most important factors associated with changes in soil microbial abundance and nitrogen cycling-related genes.14,15 In summary, although any independent study can reveal the dynamic changes and driving factors of soil nitrogen cycling microorganisms to some extent, the results have limitations in cross-regional applications. Therefore, on the basis of regional heterogeneity, studying the effects of nitrogen fertilizer application on soil microorganisms involved in nitrogen cycling according to local conditions has irreplaceable value.

Yellow soil, as an important agricultural soil type in southern China, is currently the focus of research on the inhibitory effect of nitrogen enrichment on microbial biomass. The response of soil microorganisms involved in nitrogen cycling to short-term nitrogen fertilizer addition under long-term nitrogen application is still unclear, and the dynamic adaptation strategies of these microorganism communities have been under researched. In addition, the interaction effect between soil physicochemical properties and the microbial community structure further increases the complexity of the response mechanism. This study aims to clarify the response characteristics of nitrogen cycling microbial community structure in yellow soil to changes in nitrogen management measures under different nitrogen management, which helps to understand the ecological adaptability and functional stability of soil microorganisms, and provides theoretical basis for optimizing nitrogen fertilizer management and mitigating soil degradation.

Results and conclusion

Effect of nitrogen application on the alpha diversity index of soil microorganisms involved in nitrogen cycling

The effects of nitrogen fertilizer application on the alpha diversity index of soil microorganisms involved in nitrogen cycling are shown in Figure 1. The Chao index did not significantly differ among treatment, whereas the Shannon index, Simpson index, and Pielou-e index exhibited varying degrees of variability among treatment. The Shannon index of the S1_N2 was significantly greater than that of the other treatments, and the Simpson index was significantly lower than that of the other treatment. There was no significant difference in the Shannon index, Simpson index, or Pielou-e index between the S1_CK and S1_N0 treatments of the S1 group, whereas there was no significant difference in the Shannon index, Simpson index, or Pielou-e index among the treatments of the S2 group.

Effect of nitrogen application on the beta diversity of soil microorganisms involved in nitrogen cycling

The community compositions of the different treatments were analyzed using principal coordinate analysis (PCoA) and nonmetric multidimensional scale analysis (Figure 2). The results of principal component analysis revealed that the species composition of the S2 group treatment was relatively similar, with S1_CK and S1_N0 being similar and S1_N1 and S1_N2 being clustered separately. The explanatory power of the first and second principal component axes for species composition variation was 73.51% and 13.47%, respectively, totaling 86.98% (Figure 2A). The results of nonmetric multidimensional scaling analysis differed somewhat from those of principal component analysis, but the overall trend was the same. However, the degree of similarity between S2_N2, S2_CK, and S2_N0 was decreased compared with that reported by the PCoA. The species composition of each treatment in the S1 group was relatively independent, but the similarity between S1_CK and S1_N0 was high (Figure 2B).

In brief long-term nitrogen application history and short-term gradient nitrogen application synergistically alter soil pH and nitrogen availability; reconstruct the microecological system of soil nitrogen cycling microorganisms; and induce significant differences in their diversity, community structure composition, and functional gene abundance. The differential response patterns of soil microbial community structure and function to short-term gradient nitrogen applications depend primarily on the soil background properties shaped by long-term nitrogen application. This discovery provides an important theoretical basis for understanding the impact of agricultural management measures on soil microbial-mediated nitrogen cycling processes and has guiding significance for optimizing nitrogen fertilizer management and maintaining soil ecological functions.

Discussion

Application of nitrogen affects the diversity of soil microbes involved in nitrogen cycling

The soil microbial alpha diversity index reflects the heterogeneity of microbial communities and is an important indicator for evaluating soil ecological health, functional stability, and environmental disturbance impacts.16,17 In this study, from the perspective of intra group differences, there was no significant difference in the alpha diversity index among the different treatments of the tested soil S2, but there was a significant difference among the treatments of the tested soil S1 (Figure 1). The differences in the various treatments of the tested soil S1 are mainly manifested in the differences between the S1_N2 treatment and other treatments S1_CK, S1_N0, and S1_N1 (except for the Chao index). The Shannon index and evenness index of the S1_N2 treatment (short-term nitrogen application rate 2.5 times the field recommended amount) were significantly higher than those of the S1_CK, S1_N0, and S1_N1 treatments, while the Simpson index showed the opposite trend. This phenomenon may be related to the “dose-response” of nitrogen addition. In the short term, the large amount of nitrogen application in S1_N2 treatment leads to abundant soil nitrogen, which promotes the rapid reproduction of some nitrogen cycling microorganisms, thereby increasing the Shannon index and Pielou-e index, and reducing the Simpson index.18 The large number of unique species processed by S1_N2 (Figure 3) and the relatively high abundance of other species (Figure 4) also indirectly confirm our hypothesis. It is possible that the nitrogen application rate of S1_N2 treatment may not have reached the level of excessive harm, leading to inconsistencies with other research conclusions.19,20 In contrast, S2 soil may have formed a stable nitrogen utilization strategy in its microbial community due to being in a nutrient balanced environment for a long time. It is rapidly utilized by microorganisms after nitrogen addition. When nitrogen is added within a certain range, the growth rate of nitrogen cycling microorganisms remains synchronized, resulting in no significant changes in diversity index among the treatments.21

The application of nitrogen affects the relative abundance of soil microorganisms involved in nitrogen cycling

The response of soil microorganisms involved in nitrogen cycling in response to nitrogen fertilizers plays a crucial role in maintaining the stability of nutrient cycling functions in complex dynamic soil environments.22 The results of PCoA and nonmetric multidimensional scaling analysis (NMDS) revealed that the similarity of the microbial community structure between the S1 group treatments was high, and the same was true for the S2 group. However, the S1_N1 and S1_N2 within the S2 group were relatively independent (Figure 2). The attribution of differences in soil nitrogen cycling microbial community composition can be elucidated from the dual dimensions of species composition and gene expression. From the perspective of species composition, there are differences between groups and within groups (Figure 4) Among the top 10 species in terms of relative abundance, four species in the S1 group (Nitrosopumilales’archaeon, Luteitalea_sp., Nitrososphaeraceae_archaeon, Luteitalea_pratensis) showed a significant decrease in relative abundance compared to the S2 group treatment, while two species (Gaiella_occulta, Candidatus-Gaiellasilicea_maunaloa) showed a significant increase in relative abundance. From the perspective of fully determined species functions, it to some extent indicates that long-term nitrogen application can enhance nitrification potential, denitrification potential, and organic nitrogen metabolism capacity.23 The results of this study differ from the conclusions of Ge et al.,24 mainly due to the different physical and chemical backgrounds of the tested soils. Specifically, the object of this study is acidic yellow soil, while Ge et al.'s24 research is based on soils with different pH levels worldwide. Differences in soil basic conditions may be the main reason for inconsistent conclusions. From the perspective of the group, there were significant differences in the relative abundance of species among the S1 group treatments of the tested soil, while the S2 group showed relatively small differences (Figure 4). Specifically, in the nitrogen application treatment of the S1 group (S1_N1, S1_N2) Candidatus_Rokubacteria_bacterium、Luteitalea_sp.、Nitrospira_sp.、Candidatus_Gaiellasilicea_maunaloa、Luteitalea_pratensis the relative abundance of certain species, such as Candidatus-Rokubacteria-bacterium and Luteitalea_sp., decreased or increased to varying degrees compared to the non nitrogen treatment (S1_CK, S1_N0). The occurrence of this phenomenon in the same tested soil may be the result of the combined effect of nitrogen fertilizer application gradients.25 Significant changes were observed in the soil microbial species composition of S1_N2 treated with high nitrogen application, and some microorganisms were able to reproduce and grow in this environment, thereby affecting the relative abundance of microorganisms.26

Transient microorganisms refer to microorganisms that can quickly appear and reproduce under specific environmental conditions. These microorganisms are usually very sensitive to changes in the environment, and once the environment becomes unfavorable, they quickly disappear.27 In this study, the abundance and quantity of transient species in the S1_N2 treatment were the greatest among those in all the treatments (Figure 5), indicating that compared with those in the other treatments, the microorganisms in the S1_N2 were more sensitive to environmental changes. In addition, the S1_N2 dominated in terms of the variation in the nitrogen cycling microbial community structure, and the significant fluctuations in its relative abundance were the key factors leading to the expansion of intergroup differences. LEfSe analysis further revealed that the contribution of characteristic species to intergroup differences in both bacterial and fungal communities was significantly greater in the S1 group than in the S2 group (Figure 6).

The study also found that the variation pattern of gene relative abundance between treatments is highly similar to species composition, with significant differences between groups, and intra group variation mainly concentrated among the treatments within the S1 group (Figure 7). Further analysis of the nitrogen metabolism pathway of ko00910 showed that the relative abundance of key enzyme genes in this pathway also followed the above pattern among different groups (Figure 8). Among them, the overall characteristics of the nitrogen metabolism pathway in S1_N2 treatment were significantly different from those in other treatments. It should be emphasized that this is a short-term soil column experiment, and the changes in relative abundance of species and genes only reflect the dynamic balance of community structure, and cannot directly infer the intensity changes of specific nitrogen transformation processes.28 In subsequent research, the quantitative response mechanism of key nitrogen cycling microbial communities should be thoroughly analyzed to reveal the ultimate ecological outcome of fertilization management.

Changes in soil properties are important factors affecting the community structure of soil microbes involved in nitrogen cycling

The RDA results showed that soil properties are the key driving force for the differentiation of soil nitrogen cycling microbial community structure (Figure 9), which is consistent with most research conclusions.29 But in terms of specific influencing factors, it differs from other research results.30 In this study, pH, OM, TN, NH4-N, and NO3-N are the results of the combined effects of long-term and short-term fertilization. Meanwhile, the tested soil is yellow soil, which is inherently acidic and has low nutrient activity, making the mechanism of nutrient transformation more complex. Further in-depth research is needed to combine multiple factors. It is worth noting that although short-term fertilization has a relatively small impact on soil pH, it significantly increases soil ammonium nitrogen, nitrate nitrogen, and organic matter content, leading to a decrease in the similarity of nitrogen cycling microbial community structure between S1_N2 treatment and other treatments. This phenomenon is similar to the research results of Ren et al.10 The correlation analysis between species and treatments further confirms the unique impact of S1_N2 treatment on community structure. The results showed that there were significant differences in the response of the top 10 species with relative abundance to different treatments: the dominant species with the top 6 abundance were positively correlated with pH and S2 group treatments, while they were significantly negatively correlated with S1_N2 treatments. This suggests that S1_N2 treatment may reshape community structure by inhibiting the growth of dominant species, creating ecological niches for other species. This speculation is corroborated by multiple pieces of evidence such as diversity index and gene abundance, further supporting the potential mechanism of short-term high nitrogen input in regulating microbial community structure.

Limitations of the study

The results of this study only reflect the changes in the relative abundance of nitrogen cycling microorganisms between different treatments under specific environmental conditions, and cannot directly confirm the absolute changes in their numbers; subsequent research can further clarify the dynamic changes in population size through quantitative analysis techniques.

Experimental model and study participant details

The study participants of this study are yellow soil microorganisms, which were sequenced through metagenomic sequencing. The phenotype utilization of nitrogen cycling genes was statistically analyzed based on the KEGG database.

Study area and samlie collection

Soil samples were collected from the long-term fertilizer positioning experimental field (106° 39 ′ 52 ″ E, 26° 29 ′ 49 ″ N) of the Guizhou Provincial Academy of Agricultural Sciences in Huaxi District, Guiyang city, Guizhou Province. Soil samples were collected from the long-term nitrogen fertilizer application area (S1) and the no long-term fertilizer application area (S2) of the Guizhou Farmland Conservation and Agricultural Environment Science Observation Experimental Station under the Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Affairs. The field was established in 1994 and has been planted with corn for a long time. The tested soil type was yellow soil. Its parent materials were the weathering products of Triassic limestone and sandstone shale. On December 14, 2023, the cultivated soil layer of 0–20 cm was collected, and after debris and residual plant roots were removed, it was air dried and finely ground through a 5 mm aperture sieve for later use. The physical and chemical properties of the experimental soil are detailed in below table. The tested nitrogen fertilizer is analytical pure urea (N 46%), the phosphorus fertilizer is analytical pure calcium magnesium phosphate fertilizer (P2O5 12%), and the potassium fertilizer is analytical pure potassium chloride (K2O 60%).

Method details

In this study, different nitrogen fertilizer application gradients were established for both S1 and S2 soils: no fertilizer (CK), no nitrogen fertilizer (N0), conventional nitrogen fertilizer (N1), and double nitrogen fertilizer (N2). The specific fertilizer amounts for each treatment are shown in table below. Set 3 repetitions for each treatment. Using a transparent PVC cylindrical tube as the cultivation device. The space below was used to store the solution. The leak plate was evenly perforated with a diameter of 5 mm, and the bottom plate was sealed. A drainage valve was installed 2 cm from the bottom plate. This experiment was conducted in the greenhouse of the Soil and Fertilizer Research Institute of Guizhou Academy of Agricultural Sciences. Two seasons of cabbage cultivation were carried out, with 2.25 kg of soil per soil column and an average filling height of approximately 35 cm. A layer of cotton paper towel was placed on the leakage plate of the soil column, and 125 g of dry quartz sand with a particle size of 1–2 mm was laid on top of the cotton paper towel. The soil was weighed and mixed evenly with fertilizer before it was filled into a simulated soil column. The soil was watered for the first time until it reached 70% of the maximum field capacity, and uniformly growing cabbage seedlings were selected for transplantation. Each treatment was watered with 300 mL of water for 3–5 days. The experiment started on May 31, 2024, and ended on July 16, 2024. After the experiment was completed, the soil was mixed evenly and sieved through a 2 mm sieve to remove impurities. A portion was stored in a −80°C freezer for the extraction of soil DNA, while the remaining portion was air dried, and soil nutrients were measured.

A Mag Bind Soil DNA Kit was used for sample DNA extraction. After DNA extraction, the DNA concentration and purity were determined, and DNA integrity was determined by 1% agarose gel electrophoresis. DNA fragmentation was performed using Covaris M220, and a fragment of approximately 350 bp was screened to construct a PE library. Quantification and statistical analysis

Using Fastp(https://github.com/OpenGene/fastp, Perform quality cutting on the adapter sequences of reads 3 ′and 5′ for data quality control (version 0.23.0). Using the MEGAHIT software(https://github.com/voutcn/megahit, Version 1.1.2) Splicing and assembling optimized sequences. Select contigs with a size of ≥300 bp from the splicing results as the final assembly result. Using MetaGene(http://metagene.cb.k.u-tokyo.ac.jp/)Predict ORFs for contigs in the splicing results. Select genes with a nucleic acid length greater than or equal to 100bp and use the software Emboss 6.6.0(http://emboss.open-bio.org/, V6.6.0) Translate it into an amino acid sequence. Using CD-HIT(http://www.bioinformatics.org/cd-hit/, Version 4.6.1) Cluster the predicted gene sequences of all samples (parameters: 90% identity, 90% coverage), and select the longest gene as the representative sequence for each category to construct a non redundant gene set. Using SOAPAligner software(http://soap.genomics.org.cn/, version 2.21), Compare the high-quality reads of each sample with the non redundant gene set separately (95% identity), and calculate the abundance information of genes in the corresponding samples.

Using Diamond (http://www.diamondsearch.org/index.php, Version 0.8.35) Align the amino acid sequences of the non redundant gene set with the NR database (BLASTP alignment parameter set with an expected e-value of 1e−5), and obtain species annotations from the corresponding taxonomic information database of the NR database. Then, calculate the abundance of the species using the total abundance of genes corresponding to the species. Simultaneously using Diamond(http://www.diamondsearch.org/index.php, Version 0.8.35) Align the amino acid sequences of the non redundant gene set with the KEGG database (BLASTP alignment parameter set with an expected e-value of 1e−5). Obtain the KEGG function corresponding to the gene. Calculate the abundance of the corresponding functional category using the total abundance of genes corresponding to KO. Select genes and species with an average relative abundance greater than 1% for differential analysis between treatments.

Utilizing the online analysis platform of Shanghai Meiji Biomedical Technology Co., Ltd (https://v.majorbio.com) Perform data analysis using SPSS 20.0 software for statistical analysis. The alpha diversity index, relative species abundance, and gene abundance of nitrogen cycling microorganisms were calculated using one-way ANOVA analysis of variance, while the metabolic pathway differences between groups were analyzed using Student’s t test method. (p < 0.05 is a significant difference, represented by ∗, p < 0.01 is a highly significant difference, represented by ∗∗, p < 0.001 is a highly significant difference, represented by ∗∗∗).

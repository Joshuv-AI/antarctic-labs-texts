Morphisms and comorphisms of sites II Distributors of sites
Olivia Caramello, Axel Osmond

Abstract
We introduce a notion of distributor of sites, involving suited analogs of flatness and cover-preservation, and show that this notion jointly generalizes those of morphism and comorphism of sites. Given two sites, we exhibit an adjunction between the category of distributors of sites between them and the category of geometric morphisms between the associated sheaf topoi; this adjunction restricts to an equivalence between geometric morphisms and continuous distributors of sites. We finally discuss some equipment-like properties of the bicategory of sites and distributors of sites.



Introduction

This work is the second installment in a program focused on the relations between morphisms and comorphisms of sites, and in particular the problem of mixing them altogether inside a single 2-dimensional structure.

In the first part
[
8
]
, we introduced a double category of sites, morphisms and comorphisms, and then we exhibited sheafification as a double functor to the lax quintet double category of topoi. This second part presents a different solution: we show that morphisms and comorphisms can jointly be generalized by a single notion of
distributor of sites
. Those are distributors that combine appropriate analogs of the usual properties of cover-flatness and cover-preservation involved in the definition of morphisms of sites.

A notion of flatness for distributors between categories was first introduced in
[
2
]
; we describe at
definition

2.1.11
a refinement of this notion in the presence of coverages. On the other hand, we introduce at
definition

2.1.7
a condition of
cover-distribution
, expressing a form of joint-preservation of covers along heteromorphisms. This notion happens to be closely related to a notion isolated in
[
10
]
, which was however stated in terms of bidense morphisms.

We then explain in
proposition

2.2.2
how distributors of sites induce geometric morphisms; however, a notable improvement relative to morphisms or comorphisms is that now, given two sites, any geometric morphism between the associated sheaf topoi can be induced by a distributor between those sites – see
proposition

2.2.11
– while one may fail to exhibit a functorial lift if both sites are fixed. This process restricts to an equivalence between geometric morphisms and distributors of sites satisfying a further continuity condition, as stated in
theorem

2.2.19
.

It should be emphasized that the profunctorial aspects here-examined do not fit in the double-categorical framework investigated in our first part
[
8
]
, where the double-categories of interest were of a very different kind from those usually involved in
pro-arrow equipments
.

Although this second part makes no use of any double-categorical technology, we nevertheless describe in our last section some equipment-like properties of the bicategory of sites together with distributors of sites; while not all axioms of equipments are fulfilled – because of the very dichotomy between morphisms and comorphisms amongst other reasons – it makes sense to expect some similarities with the equipment provided by distributors, for instance well behaved gluing as described at
proposition

2.3.5
. A further third part of our program with be devoted to the formal category theory for sites.

1.2
Generalities on distributors

Distributors are relational analog of functors;

Definition 1.2.1
.

A

H:\mathcal{C}\looparrowright\mathcal{D}
is a functor
\mathcal{D}^{\textup{op}}\times\mathcal{C}\rightarrow{\bf Set}
. A
transformation of distributors

is a natural transformation in
{\bf Cat}[\mathcal{D}^{\textup{op}}\times\mathcal{C},{\bf Set}]
.

For a pair
(d,c)
with
d
in
\mathcal{D}
and
c
in
\mathcal{C}
, the elements of
H(d,c)
are called
heteromorphisms from
d
to

and will be denoted as generalized arrows across categories as below

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces d}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces c}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces x}

This terminology echoes to the formal name of

for ordinary arrows inside a category; those are in fact a special case of heteromorphisms, namely those associated to the

\hom_{\mathcal{C}}:\mathcal{C}\looparrowright\mathcal{C}
given by the hom functor
\mathcal{C}[-,-]:\mathcal{C}^{\textup{op}}\times\mathcal{C}\rightarrow{\bf Set}
.

1.2.2
Composition of distributors.

For two distributors
H:\mathcal{C}\looparrowright\mathcal{D}
and
K:\mathcal{D}\looparrowright\mathcal{E}
, one can consider the composite distributor, also known as their
tensor product

K\otimes H:\mathcal{C}\looparrowright\mathcal{E}
, which is computed at a pair
(e,c)
as the coend

(K\otimes H)[e,c]=\int^{d\in\mathcal{D}}H[d,c]\times K[e,d]

The unit for the composition of distributors is given by the homset distributor
\hom_{\mathcal{C}}:\mathcal{C}\looparrowright\mathcal{C}
sending
(c,c^{\prime})
to
\mathcal{C}[c,c^{\prime}]
.

Remark 1.2.3
.

Beware that composition is weak: associativity and unit laws only hold up to canonical invertible 2-cells. For this reason, categories together with distributors and transformations between them actually arrange into a
bicategory
rather than a strict 2-category.

We denote as
{\bf Dist}
the bicategory of category, distributors and transformations between them.

1.2.4
Distributors and free cocompletion.

Recall that for two categories
\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D}
, the category of distributors is in equivalence with the category of cocontinuous functors:

\displaystyle{\bf Dist}[\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D}]

\displaystyle={\bf CAT}[\mathcal{D}^{\textup{op}}\times\mathcal{C},{\bf Set}]

\displaystyle\simeq{\bf CAT}[\mathcal{C},\widehat{\mathcal{D}}]

Remark 1.2.5
.

In the case of a functor

, we saw at
proposition

that the restriction functor expresses at a presheaf

1.2.6
.

The construction of the classifying functor associated to a distributor is pseudofunctorial. For a pair

,

\displaystyle\widehat{K\otimes H}

\displaystyle=\textup{lext}_{K}\circ\widehat{H}

1.2.9

2.2

Now, as in the functorial case, cover-distribution and cover-flatness can be mixed altogether do define an appropriate notion of distributors between sites:

Definition 2.2.1
.

Proposition 2.2.2
.

Let

be a distributor of sites; then
\mathfrak{a}_{K}\textup{lext}_{H}
factorizes through
\mathfrak{a}_{J}
and defines the inverse image part of a geometric morphism
{\bf Sh}(H):\widehat{\mathcal{D}}_{K}\rightarrow\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{J}
as depicted below

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\widehat{\mathcal{C}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\widehat{\mathcal{D}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{J}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\widehat{\mathcal{D}}_{K}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\textup{lext}_{H}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{a}_{J}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{a}_{K}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\bf Sh}(H)^{*}}

Proof.

By
K
-flatness,
\mathfrak{a}_{K}\widehat{H}
is a flat functor
\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\widehat{\mathcal{D}}_{K}
: hence its extension
{\textup{lan}}_{\text{\char 136\relax}_{\mathcal{C}}}\mathfrak{a}_{K}\widehat{H}
is lex, but as a left adjoint,
\mathfrak{a}_{K}
commutes with left Kan extensions, so
{\textup{lan}}_{\text{\char 136\relax}_{\mathcal{C}}}\mathfrak{a}_{K}\widehat{H}\simeq\mathfrak{a}_{K}\textup{lext}_{H}
. But since

-flat and cover-distributing from

, then

is a

factorizes through the sheafification

, in the sense that
\mathfrak{a}_{K}\widehat{H}
inverts the units of the adjunction
\mathfrak{a}_{J}\dashv\mathfrak{i}_{J}
.
∎

Lemma 2.2.3
.

Let
H:(\mathcal{C},J)\looparrowright(\mathcal{D},K)
and

be distributors of sites; then the composite

defines a distributor of sites
(\mathcal{C},J)\looparrowright(\mathcal{D},K)
. Moreover for any site
(\mathcal{C},J)
the unit
\hom_{\mathcal{C}}
is a distributor of sites
(\mathcal{C},J)\looparrowright(\mathcal{C},J)
.

Proof.

We saw at
lemma

2.1.8
that cover-distributing distributors compose and that the unit is cover-distributing. Now suppose that
H
,
G
are distributor of sites; recall that

\widehat{G\otimes H}\simeq\textup{lext}_{G}\widehat{H}

But also recall that
\mathfrak{a}_{L}\textup{lext}_{G}
factorizes through
\mathfrak{a}_{K}
for
G
is a distributor of sites, hence
\mathfrak{a}_{L}\textup{lext}_{G}
inverts the units of the adjunction
\mathfrak{a}_{K}\dashv\mathfrak{i}_{K}
and we have hence

\mathfrak{a}_{L}\textup{lext}_{G}\widehat{H}\simeq\mathfrak{a}_{L}\textup{lext}_{G}\mathfrak{i}_{K}\mathfrak{a}_{K}\widehat{H}

but on one hand,
\mathfrak{a}_{L}\textup{lext}_{G}\mathfrak{i}_{K}
is a lex functor since
G
is
L
-flat, and on the other hand,
\mathfrak{a}_{K}\widehat{H}
is flat since

is

-flat, so this composite is flat, and from

must be

\text{\char 136\relax}_{\mathcal{C}}:\mathcal{C}\hookrightarrow\widehat{\mathcal{C}}

\mathfrak{a}_{J}\text{\char 136\relax}_{\mathcal{C}}

Being closed under composition and unit, we can consider the bicategory
{\bf Sit}^{\musSymbol{0.05em}{0.5ex}{1.35em}{\char 87\relax}}
sites, distributors of sites and transformations.

Theorem 2.2.4
.

The construction above defines a pseudofunctor

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{({\bf Sit}^{\musSymbol{0.05em}{0.5ex}{1.35em}{\char 87\relax}})^{\textup{op}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\bf Top}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\bf Sh}}

Proof.

We have to show this construction is compatible with composition of distributors of sites and extends to transformations between them. Taking a pair of distributors of sites

as in the previous lemma, we have that

\displaystyle{\bf Sh}(G\otimes H)^{*}

\displaystyle\simeq\mathfrak{a}_{L}\textup{lext}_{G\otimes H}\mathfrak{i}_{J}

\displaystyle\simeq\mathfrak{a}_{L}\textup{lext}_{G}\textup{lext}_{H}\mathfrak{i}_{J}

\displaystyle\simeq\mathfrak{a}_{L}\textup{lext}_{G}\mathfrak{i}_{K}\mathfrak{a}_{K}\textup{lext}_{H}\mathfrak{i}_{J}

\displaystyle\simeq{\bf Sh}(G)^{*}{\bf Sh}(H)^{*}

where the third isomorphism comes from the fact that
\mathfrak{a}_{L}\textup{lext}_{G}
inverts

-bidense morphisms. Similarly we saw that

\displaystyle{\bf Sh}(\hom_{\mathcal{C}})^{*}

\displaystyle\simeq\mathfrak{a}_{J}{\textup{lan}}_{\text{\char 136\relax}_{\mathcal{C}}}\text{\char 136\relax}_{\mathcal{C}}

\displaystyle\simeq 1_{\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{J}}

{\bf Sh}(\phi)

.
∎

Definition 2.2.5

Proposition 2.2.6

For a Grothendieck topology

Definition 2.2.7

A distributor

Observe that in particular, for this applies to the case of constant diagrams

For an ordinary coverage

Proposition 2.2.9

Definition 2.2.12

Remark 2.2.13

The condition says that for each

\mathcal{C}

\widehat{H}(c)
is a
K

Remark 2.2.14

For any distributor of sites

, it is true by construction that

lands in

Remark 2.2.15
.

Beware that continuous distributors of sites do not compose inside of
{\bf Sit}^{\musSymbol{0.05em}{0.5ex}{1.35em}{\char 87\relax}}

and

, the composite

may not be continuous; although

, though factorizing through

, needs not again landing in
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{L}
for sheaves are not closed under colimits in
\widehat{\mathcal{D}}
(one has to perform sheafification after computing colimits as presheaves). For this reason the classifying functor of the composite

, which is
\textup{lext}_{G}\widehat{H}
, needs not landing in
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{L}

In a similar way, the unit distributors

needs not be continuous, as its classifying functor is

is subcanonical.

2.2.16
.

Consequently, to define a bicategorical structure for continuous comorphisms, one needs to modify the notion of unit and composition:

-

for a site

-

for two continuous distributors of sites

and

as the continuous distributor whose classifying functor is the sheafification of

2.3
Equipment-like properties of
{\bf Sit}^{\musSymbol{0.05em}{0.5ex}{1.35em}{\char 87\relax}}

Let us conclude this section with a few formal categorical observations. The bicategory
{\bf Dist}
is the ur-example of the notion of
pro-arrow equipment
relative to
{\bf Cat}
, as defined in
[
16
]
. This question also relates to the Yoneda structure of
{\bf Cat}
through the classifying property of the free cocompletion relative to distributors. As we are going to see, some of this structure is inherited by sites, although the dichotomy between morphisms and comorphisms breaks some other parts of this structure.

2.3.1
.

It is well known that the 2-category
{\bf Dist}
is the
Kleisli
2-category of the relative lax-idempotent monad
\widehat{(-)}:{\bf Cat}\rightarrow{\bf CAT}
. Although we are not going to enter in the detail of the technology underlying the notion of Kleisli 2-category of a 2-monad, let us examine how this statement refines for sites. While in absence of topology, the free cocompletion is only lax-idempotent and rises size issues, in the case of small generated sites, we actually can consider a true pseudomonad on the 2-category of sites together with morphisms of sites

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{{\bf Sit}^{\flat}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{{\bf Sit}^{\flat}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{T}}

sending a site
(\mathcal{C},J)
to the canonical site
(\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{J},J_{can})
, which is again small generated. Hence, using that
J
-continuous functors
\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\widehat{\mathcal{D}}_{K}
are the same as morphisms of sites
(\mathcal{C},J)\rightarrow(\widehat{\mathcal{D}}_{K},J_{can})
, we have

\displaystyle{\bf Sit}^{\musSymbol{0.05em}{0.5ex}{1.35em}{\char 87\relax}}_{{\bf Cont}}[(\mathcal{C},J),(\mathcal{D},K)]

\displaystyle\simeq{\bf Cont}_{J}[\mathcal{C},\widehat{\mathcal{D}}_{K}]

\displaystyle\simeq{\bf Sit}^{\flat}[(\mathcal{C},J),(\widehat{\mathcal{D}}_{K},J_{can})]

which is exactly the property of the Kleisli 2-category of the pseudomonad
\mathbb{T}
, for which we can state that

{\bf Sit}^{\musSymbol{0.05em}{0.5ex}{1.35em}{\char 87\relax}}_{{\bf Cont}}\simeq\mathbf{Kl}(\mathbb{T})

(Beware that we have to restrict to continuous distributors as sheaf topoi classify distributor of sites only up to sheafification.) This consideration hints at a possibility to examine analogs of the Yoneda structure of
{\bf Cat}
in the context of sites, which will be the topic of a future work.

2.3.2
Equipments.

The wide inclusion
{\bf Cat}\rightarrow{\bf Dist}
sending
f:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}
to the representable
\mathcal{D}(1,f):\mathcal{C}\looparrowright\mathcal{D}
exhibits
{\bf Dist}
as an
equipment
, in the sense of
[
16
]
. Recall that for
\mathcal{K}
be a bicategory; then a pseudofunctor
(-)_{*}:\mathcal{K}\rightarrow\mathcal{M}
is said to
equip
\mathcal{K}
with proarrows
if

1.

(-)_{*}
is a bijection on objects

2.

(-)_{*}
is locally fully faithful

3.

for any arrow
f
in
\mathcal{K}
,
f_{*}
admits a right adjoint
f^{*}
in
\mathcal{M}
.

The main example is the inclusion
{\bf Cat}\rightarrow{\bf Dist}
sending
f:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}
to the representable distributor
\mathcal{D}[1,f]:\mathcal{C}\looparrowright\mathcal{D}
, which we saw at
1.2.7
to be left adjoint to
\mathcal{D}[f,1]:\mathcal{D}\looparrowright\mathcal{C}
. Similarly, as established in
[
15
]
, there is an equipment
{\bf Top}\rightarrow{\bf Top}_{\bf Lex}^{\textup{co}}
where
{\bf Top}_{\bf Lex}
is the bicategory of topoi with lex functors, sending
f
to its direct image part
f_{*}
: then
f^{*}
, as a left adjoint to

, is right adjoint in
{\bf Top}_{\bf Lex}^{\textup{co}}
.

2.3.3
.

Consisting of a bicategory of distributors, it is natural to expect from
{\bf Sit}^{\musSymbol{0.05em}{0.5ex}{1.35em}{\char 87\relax}}
to retain some of this structure relative to
{\bf Sit}^{\flat}
. Indeed the inclusion
{\bf Sit}^{\flat}\rightarrow{\bf Sit}^{\musSymbol{0.05em}{0.5ex}{1.35em}{\char 87\relax}}
sending a morphism of sites
f:(\mathcal{C},J)\rightarrow(\mathcal{D},K)
to the representable
\mathcal{D}[1,f]:(\mathcal{C},J)\looparrowright(\mathcal{D},K)
is bijective on objects and locally fully faithful; however we see that the third axiom fails: for a morphism of site, there is no reason for the corepresentable
\mathcal{D}[f,1]
to be neither cover-flat nor cover-distributive, for which this right adjoint does not exist in
{\bf Sit}^{\musSymbol{0.05em}{0.5ex}{1.35em}{\char 87\relax}}
. On the other hand, we have a dual embedding
({\bf Sit}^{\sharp})^{\textup{co}}\rightarrow({\bf Sit}^{\musSymbol{0.05em}{0.5ex}{1.35em}{\char 87\relax}})^{\textup{op}}

exists in

{\bf Sit}^{\musSymbol{0.05em}{0.5ex}{1.35em}{\char 87\relax}}
to the pair
({\bf Sit}^{\flat},{\bf Sit}^{\sharp})

Although the third axiom fails, some further requirements examined at
[

]
[Axiom (C)] are still available. It is well known that distributors in
{\bf Cat}
correspond with
codiscrete cofibrations
, given by their gluing. Similarly, it appears that distributors of sites admit gluing and those gluings sit both in
{\bf Sit}^{\flat}
and
{\bf Sit}^{\sharp}

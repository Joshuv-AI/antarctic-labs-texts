The role of michigan oral splint construction aided by OTC drug versus extraction of distoverted maxillary third molars in management of temporomandibular joint disorders: a randomized controlled trial
Authors: Nzar Abdulqadr Muhammad Amin
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
The temporomandibular joint disorders (TMD) involve the mastication muscles, the temporomandibular joint (TMJ), and its related structures. To determine the role of Michigan oral splint aided by OTC drug versus removal of distoverted maxillary 3rd molars in the treatment of TMD. The randomized controlled clinical trial study enrolled 280 patients with confirmed TMD who attended different private/public dental centers, from November 2023 to August 2024. Patients had chief complaints of pain and clicking noise/sounds in their TMJ during mouth opening, chewing, muscle tension in the neck/face region, and pain in the head, neck, and pre-auricular/facial areas. Then, patients were randomly assigned to a control group (n = 140) and a study group (n = 140). Patients in the control group (Y1) underwent extraction of distoverted maxillary only, while patients in the study group (Y2) received Michigan oral splint aided by OTC drugs. Then, the Visual Analogue Pain Rating Scale was used to determine the intensity of pain among patients in both groups. Finally, patients were evaluated for treatment outcomes. Most patients were female (67.5%, n = 186) and had a mean age of 38 ± 8 years. Their pain intensity before treatment was 6.47 ± 1.49, and after treatment, it was 0.0 ± 0.0, indicating that the patients were completely healed after using the selected treatment. The results of the normality tests for pain scores before and after treatment showed strong evidence against the null hypothesis (p = 0.000). Additionally, a significant difference (p ≤ 0.05) between both groups suggests that the Michigan occlusal splint, aided by OTC drugs, had a positive effect on healing the patients. Consequently, the occlusal splint has a significant (p = 0.000) behavioural effect on increasing cognition related to mandibular position and function of the stomatognathic system. Michigan occlusal splint aided by OTC drugs was a superior modality than extraction of distoverted maxillary 3rd molars in treatment of TMD.

Introduction

Temporomandibular disorders (TMD) are a significant public health issue that restricts daily activities and significantly decreases the quality of life, as they cause discomfort in the jaw, head, neck, ears, and eyes, resulting in deviations in mandibular movements (Chisnoiu et al. 2015). Bruxism, clenching, stress, anxiety, occlusal disharmony, and trauma may be allied with TMD (González et al. 2025). Abnormalities of the temporomandibular joint (TMJ) are indicated by a change in the mandibular movement, restriction of mouth opening, pain in the masticatory muscles/pre-auricular area that aggravates by chewing, constrained function, and joint noises (clicking sounds) (Maini and Dua 2023).

Classifications that aid in the clinical diagnosis of TMD include muscular, articular, and mixed (muscular and articular) (Manfredini 2018). Based on Wilkes’ criteria, TMD begins with normal joints, progressing to minor forward displacement and eventually to advanced cases that are accompanied by bone degeneration and severe clinical symptoms (Hegab et al. 2021). More than 90–95% of TMD cases have musculoskeletal pain, TMJ dysfunction, or unknown issues. Accordingly, identification should be precise for the patient and should focus on emphasizing the causes (Garrigós-Pedrón et al. 2019). Better diagnosis can be aided by examining the masticatory muscles, articular disks, and soft tissues of the TMJ, as well as the type of pain and mandibular functional movements. Additionally, a patient's behavioural enquiry should also be considered (Li and Leung 2021).

The manifestation of TMD in women is more than two and a half times that of men. The most affected age groups in women ranged between 40 and 50 years, while in men, it was between 30 and 40 years. The frequency of TMD symptoms is higher in individuals aged 20–40 years than in children and older people (> 60 years) (Dzingutė et al. 2017). Regarding the oral appliance and its effect on muscle activity, it was noted that the oral appliance can reduce nocturnal muscle activity, which is attributed to an automatic response to the presence of a foreign object between the teeth, resulting in avoidance behaviour (Di Palma et al. 2017).

An oral splint is a custom-fabricated hard acrylic tool that fits over the occlusal and incisal surfaces of the maxillary or mandibular teeth. It has been used for the management of bruxism and painful TMD for decades, with the stabilization splint (Michigan splint) being the most commonly used interocclusal appliance worldwide and accepted as the gold standard for splint therapy (Dedema and Türpb 2016). A Michigan splint provides relief by shifting the jaw into a position that encourages relaxation and pressure relief. Additionally, it gives a bite plane that serves as a physical barrier between the upper and lower teeth.

Both oral splints and 3rd molar extraction are plausible treatments for the same patient scenarios, and the evidence comparing them is lacking. The rationale for comparing Michigan oral splint therapy with the extraction of distoverted maxillary 3rd molars is based on the clinical observation that both interventions target two major, but different, contributing factors to TMDs.

The bite surface is designed to have evenly distributed occlusal contacts with the teeth; consequently, the splint shields teeth from damage during teeth grinding or jaw clenching (https://www.proteethguard.com/blog/michigan-splint). Thus, this study aimed to confirm and ensure that Michigan splint therapy has the potential to be a superior modality among other treatment options.

Patient and methods

Study design and setting

This randomized controlled clinical trial study enrolled 280 participants who were presenting at Clinics of undergraduate/postgraduate students at the building of College of Dentistry, University of Sulaimani, with council room of Oral and Maxillofacial Surgery at Shar Teaching Hospital and Smart Health Tower, Sulaimaniyah, Iraq, from November 2023 to August 2024.

Inclusion criteria

Participants aged ≥ 18 years old who reported pain, bruxism, and clicking noise/sounds in their TMJ during mouth opening or chewing action and limited mouth opening, muscle tension in the neck/face region, and pain in the head, neck, and pre-auricular/facial areas.

Exclusion criteria

Participants with osteoarthritis, cervical spondylitis, eye/otolaryngeal issues, migraine, cluster/tension headache, congenital/craniofacial anomaly, toothache, fibromyalgia, neuralgia, inflammation, neoplastic conditions, rheumatoid arthritis, recent facial/cervical trauma, and wearied fixed dental appliances. Patients with TMJ replacement, previous TMJ injection, received treatments for TMD, had complete/removable partial prostheses with distal extensions and those with para-functional oral habits, such as clenching.

Materials and patient preparation

The Michigan stabilization splint (SS) includes occlusal registration using 8.0 µm articulating paper (Bausch, Germany) and bite registration wax (Alu Wax, Holland) (Fig. 1). The occlusal status and premature contacts were evaluated through model analysis. Orthopantomography was counselled for early screening, and cone-beam computed tomography (CBCT, Newtom GO, Italy) was taken in some patients to check the distally tilted maxillary 3rd molars and alterations in the condyle of the mandible/eminence. Study protocol

The number of patients was calculated according to G* Power analysis to be a minimum of 280 individuals. The subjects were diagnosed with TMD (by the same expert dentist to minimize study bias) according to the diagnostic criteria for TMD, and they were allocated into a control group (Y1, n = 140) and a study group (Y2, n = 140) using the block randomization method. Patients in the control group underwent extraction of distoverted maxillary third molars under local anaesthesia. They received sedatives if their pain was severe after extraction, and usually, they recovered after 7 to 10 days. Whereas patients of the study group were advised to wear a Michigan oral splint aided by OTC drugs (ibuprofen, naproxen, and each of paracetamol and voltaren tablets in low doses) regularly at night time for more than 10 h for 12 consecutive weeks, and they were followed up 3 times at 4-week intervals. To determine pain perception before and after treatments, the Visual Analogue Pain Rating Scale (VAS) was used. The VAS is a five-point scale that is used to classify the severity of pain (0 = no pain, 1 = mild pain, 2 = moderate pain, 3 = severe pain, and 4 = very severe pain) (Gururaj et al. 2023) at the end of 4, 8, and 12 weeks. Finally, patients in both groups were evaluated for compliance (primary outcome) and efficacy (secondary outcome) according to Wilkes’ criteria (Garrigós-Pedrón et al. 2019). All patients followed the study thoroughly, with no dropouts due to non-compliance or bias towards either treatment.

Data analysis

Variables had been analyzed using Statistical Package for Social Science (SPSS, IBM Chicago, USA, version 27.0). Wilcoxon W and Mann–Whitney U tests were used for nonparametric data, while Kolmogorov–Smirnov and Shapiro–Wilk tests were applied for parametric variables. A p-value of ≤ 0.05 is considered significant, and p ≤ 0.001 is considered highly significant.

Discussion

TMD is a multipurpose disorder with unknown etiology; however, it is related to numerous factors such as bruxism, uncorrected high dental restorations, and occlusal prematurity (Begum and Hossain 2019). Therefore, this study aimed to provide better treatment modalities for patients with TMD.

In this study, the TMD was most common among middle-aged female patients (38 ± 8.0 years) (67.5%), indicating a direct correlation between the disease and age and gender. These outcomes are aligned with that of Gururaj et al. (Gururaj et al. 2023) who found that most patients were female (60%) with a mean age of 38.45 ± 11.92 years. Similar to our study, Mortazavi et al. (Mortazavi et al. 2010) found that 81% of patients with TMD were females with a mean age of 30.2 years.

Studies have acclaimed a detailed treatment procedure that reduces the load on the TMJ and muscles to achieve the goal of treating TMD cases. The main aim of the treatment is to remove occlusal interferences, chronic myofascial pain, neck/head pain, and joint distress produced by many issues and to lower the psychological burden (Kannan et al. 2021). Occlusal splints are used to manage both muscular and joint disorders, in conjunction with medical management. They are routinely employed in dental clinics to diagnose and treat abnormalities of the masticatory system (Albagieh et al. 2023). This study revealed that treatment of patients with TMD potentially and gradually reduced pain intensity throughout treatment and eliminated it by the end of the study. These outcomes are parallel to those of Malik et al. (Malik and Majeed 2019), who found a significant reduction in pain scores after implementing splint therapy, as well as improved mouth opening in patients with TMD.

In the present study, we observed that Michigan occlusal splint therapy was a more effective treatment modality for TMD, including bruxism, jaw pain, headache, postural imbalances related to TMDs, and a reduced vertical dimension of occlusion, than the extraction of a distoverted maxillary 3rd molar only. These results indicate that the type of intervention received was strongly associated with group allocation, suggesting that treatment modality patterns differed substantially between the two approaches. These findings align with those of Badel et al. (Badel et al. 2013), who found that the Michigan SS produces a behavioural effect of self-awareness (cognition) regarding the position, function, and parafunction of the mandible, as well as a placebo effect. Similarly, Katamish et al. (Katamish and Nasr 2018) stated that the use of repositioning splints is a beneficial tool in managing intra-articular pain and dysfunction, with no risk of irreversible occlusal changes. However, these outcomes contradict the findings of Gururaj et al. (Gururaj et al. 2023), who mentioned that the extraction of supraerupted and/or distoverted maxillary 3rd molars is a prerequisite for treating TMD, resulting in a 96% reduction in TMD compared to the study group that did not undergo extraction.

The limitations of this study include a short follow-up period, potential biases in subjective pain assessment among patients, the inability to measure the degree of inclination, and overeruption of the maxillary 3rd molar. The challenge of gaining patients' trust was another limitation, as they might receive conflicting opinions from other specialists. It was also challenging to educate patients about the need and purpose of the custom-made hard acrylic appliance compared to ready-made soft splints. Lastly, patients had a poor concept of TMD and the various treatment aspects that resulted in delayed outcomes.

Conclusions

The TMJ was most common among middle-aged female patients. The responses to treatments indicated that repositioning Michigan oral/occlusal splint therapy was an effective treatment modality for TMD, including bruxism, jaw pain, headache, and postural imbalances related to TMDs, as well as decreased vertical dimension of occlusion. Occlusal oral splints are superior to physiotherapy (X1) in treating TMDs. This device facilitates the ideal relationship between the teeth, in coordination with the muscles and joints of mastication, to reduce abnormal muscle activity and promote neuromuscular balance. Accordingly, Michigan SS is recommended to prevent potential damage to the dentition, periodontium and the TMJ in early TMD.

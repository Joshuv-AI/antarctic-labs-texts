Protective role of methylcobalamin (Vit B12) and pyridoxine (Vit B6) combination against teratogenic effects induced by lead in chick embryo
Authors: Rabia Idrees, Muhammad Ali Kanwal, Syeda Nadia Ahmad, Khawaja Raees Ahmad, Waheed Ahmad, Nazish Ghazanfar, Iram Inayat, Moattar Khalid, Sadia Suleman
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Environmental contamination through heavy metals poses significant health risks and cause detrimental effects on embryonic development of humans and animals. This study investigates the teratogenic effects induced by lead – one of poisonous heavy metals, and rescuing potential of combined dose of vitamin B6 and vitamin B12 in golden black breed of domestic chicken embryos. Experiments were carried out on 200 fertilized eggs. Eggs were weighed and distributed into four groups (50 eggs each). 1. Control group 0.1 mL of patent drinking water 2. Vit-B6 + Vit-B12 combination group 0.05 mL of 0.1 mg/kg vitamin B6 solution + 0.05 mL of 0.1 mg/kg vitamin B12 solution in patent drinking water. 3. Lead acetate group 0.1 mL of 5 mg/kg lead acetate solution in patent drinking water. 4. Lead acetate and Vit-B6 + Vit-B12 combination group 0.1 mL of solution containing 5 mg/kg lead acetate, 0.1 mg/kg vitamin B6 and 0.1 mg/kg vitamin B12 solution in patent drinking water. Recovery of embryos was performed on 14th day of incubation and embryos were preserved for further studies. Significant teratogenic effects were observed in lead treated group compared to control group. Microcephaly, exopthalomus eye, abnormal beak development, skewed spine, reduced neck length and torticollis were observed in lead treated group. Appendicular abnormalities include forelimb and hind limb amelia, meromelia in hind limbs. In some chick embryos agenesis and irregular development of claws was noted. Mortality rate was higher in lead group compared to control group. This study highlights vitamin B6 and vitamin B12 ameliorative effects against lead induced developmental abnormalities in chick embryo.

Introduction

Among metals whose density is greater than 5 g per cubic centimeter are considered as heavy metals [1], [2]. Heavy metals buildup in soil and plants grown in it when irrigated with untreated sewage water over a long period of time [3]. Industrial effluents contain heavy metals pollutants leading to serious environmental concerns [4]. Heavy metals bioaccumulation due to their non-biodegradable nature have damaging effects on health of humans, animals and plants [5].

Lead is bluish grey colored toxic heavy metal [6]. Although it is widely used in batteries, semiconductors, dyes, glass, ceramics, and as a fuel supplement, its use is being restricted because of toxicity concerns [7]. Studies revealed that lead exposure sparked oxidative stress in tissues and produced reactive oxygen species and reactive nitrogen species in mitochondria [8], [9]. Lead interferes with physiological processes at the molecular level by mimicking ions in proteins and enzymes, causing oxidative stress, DNA damage, mitochondrial malfunction, and epigenetic changes [10]. Ionic mechanism of lead toxicity is activated by lead metallic ions having potential to replace bivalent cations (Ca +2, Mg +2, Fe +2) and monovalent cations (Na +) [11]. Protein kinase C which control neural excitation and memory storage is affected when lead replaces calcium even in picomolar concentration [12].

Lead pollution affects aquatic and avian habitats in addition to human health, leading to bioabsorption and detrimental impacts on fish and birds [6]. Lead does not instantly damage welfare and survival of birds or cause an abrupt failure of biological functions, it raises the mortality rate through anaemia, lethargy, muscle atrophy, loss of fat reserves, balance, and coordination and various components of immune system [13], [14].The elevated level of certain heavy metals in poultry results in oxidative damage [15]. Chickens are less sensitive to endotoxins when exposed to low levels of lead acetate. Poultry's resistance to infectious diseases may be weakened due to lead's ability to deactivate antibodies. Many antioxidant defences are hampered by lead, and low antioxidant levels can harm the neurological system, liver, kidneys, and reproductive system, among other organ systems. Pb poisoning can kill chickens in acute exposure circumstances. The liver accumulates absorbed lead, and exposure to lead can cause histological abnormalities in the liver in chickens. Pb buildup in the liver also results in tiny necrotic foci and localized haemorrhages [16], [17], [18].

Lead exposure in animals has been associated with anomalies of the central nervous system, including brain sizes that are smaller and reduced cognitive function [19]. Due to their sensitivity to lead exposure, birds experience lowered hatchability, immunosuppression, and organ damage [20]. Lead exposure harms chickens by deteriorating bone quality in addition to toxicating organs and immediately reducing physiological characteristics (such as body weight and laying rate) [21]. Liver, kidney, lungs and reproductive system are main organs in cattle and poultry where lead builds up as a result of continuous exposure [11]. Lead may get into poultry feed from environmental sources like lead battery trash and contaminated materials like grains and codmeal [22]. Experiments conducted to unearth the consequences of lead acetate treatment on chick embryo somites during development revealed that chondrogenesis, and myogenesis are halted by it. Changes in morphology and nucleus damage in somite cells was triggered by high concentration of lead acetate after 72 h exposure [23], [24]. Lead exposure during development causes a range of behavioural problems and cognitive deficiencies, such as increased aggressiveness and disruptive behavior [25].

Lead is the cause of many deaths and illnesses worldwide, highlighting the need for individual accountability, legislation enforcement, and more knowledge of lead's impacts [26], [27]. Lead is integrated with minerals as a substituent of calcium inside skeleton [28]. Lead effects on chromosome stability and carcinogenic effects accused it as genotoxic material [29]. It is a metabolic toxin that affects numerous body systems in humans. Its exposure either through oral or nasal route cause damaging effects on soft tissues of brain in humans [8]. Lead has a varied half-life in different organs about 30 days in blood, 40 days in soft tissues, and years in bone [30]. Lead exposure during pregnancy increases the likelihood of miscarriage, and long-term exposure is associated with lower male fertility [31]. The blood-brain barrier is especially vulnerable to lead poisoning in newborns and young children, which can hinder cognitive development [32]. Major macromolecules (lipid, protein, and nucleic acids) as well as the liver, nervous system, kidneys, and DNA are the primary targets of lead poisoning in both humans and animals [33]. Acute Lead exposure can cause kidney damage, brain damage, and gastrointestinal disorders [34]. While, chronic exposure may have negative effects on the kidneys, blood pressure, central nervous system, blood, and vitamin D metabolism [35]. Long-term consequences of lead on the environment and human, animal, and wildlife health could be reduced by using non-lead-based paint, replacing lead shot with nontoxic shot, and other measures [36].

Vitamin B6 holds potential to reduce the teratogenic effects of lead. Vitamin B6 act as an antioxidant by reducing the synthesis of reactive oxygen species [37]. Level of pancreatic glutathione reductase regulated antioxidant mechanism was disturbed in rats in case of vitamin B6 deficiency [38]. Hatching percentage of Japanese quail increase after injection of vitamin B6 at dose of 60 and120 μg/ egg [39]. Hatchability, body weight, and certain blood variables were all enhanced by in ovo pyridoxine injection in broiler eggs [40].

The biologically active form of Vitamin B12 is methylcobalamin. It has a vital role in methionine and S-adenosyl-L-methionine amino acids synthesis. It is crucial for CNS functioning, DNA synthesis, RBC formation and in maintaining integrity of myelin [41].Vitamin B12 exhibit free radical eating features through taking part in homocysteine degradation. High Homocysteine level causes generation of ROS and is one of disease symptoms in diseases such as vascular dementia, mental illness and heart diseases [42], [43], [44], [45], [46]. Vitamin B12 halts this process and converts homocysteine into methionine [47].

Models of ischaemic stroke and epilepsy have demonstrated that supplementation with vitamin B6 and vitamin B12 considerably mitigates neuroinflammation, diminishes oxidative stress, and enhances functional neurological outcomes [48], [49]. Valproic acid induced exencephaly, spina bifida occulta, palate and rib deformities, kidney abnormalities, and foetal weight retardation were all considerably decreased by vitamin B6 + vitamin B12 [50]. These findings support the idea that VB6 and VB12 possess antioxidant features and have potential to reduce teratogenic effects, but their mechanism of action is still debatable.

Material and methods

Dose optimization methodology

Lead acetate solution (5 mg/kg)

Molar mass of lead acetate Pb(CH3COO)2 was calculated to be 325.3 g/mol. The mass of lead acetate needed to achieve concentration of 5 ppm in 50 mL of water was 0.00025. 0.00025 g of lead acetate was precisely measured using a digital balance and dissolved in 50 mL of patent drinking water. The resultant solution, which contained five parts per million of lead acetate, was used as the stock solution.

The desired dose of lead acetate was then prepared according to the equation:C1V1=C2V2where C1C2 represent concentrations, and V1V2 represent the volumes involved in the preparation of the final dose.

Vitamin B6 (Pyridoxine) solution (0.5 mg/kg)

A commercially available Vita−6 pill (50 mg) made by Le Mendoza Pharmaceutical Company (Pvt.) Ltd. with Registration No. 003225 was used to make the vitamin B6 solution. The tablet was finely grinded and a sensitive digital balance was used to weigh 5 mg from resulting powder. Ten millilitres of patent drinking water were added to five milligrams of powder, and the mixture was stirred until the powder was completely dissolved. The content of pyridoxine hydrochloride (vitamin B6) in this 10 mL stock solution was 0.1 mg/kg.

Vitamin B12 (Methylcobalamin) solution (0.5 mg/kg)

A commercially available Mecobal pill (500 µg) made by Nabiqasim Pharmaceutical Company (Pvt. Ltd. with Registration No.021631 was used to make the vitamin B12 solution. The tablet was finely grinded and disslolved in five millilitres of patent drinking and the mixture was stirred until the powder was completely dissolved. The resultant solution contained 0.5 mg/kg methylcobalamin.

The lead acetate dose was optimized based on previous research findings and to ensure induction of teratogenic effects without complete embryonic lethality. The combined dose of Vitamin B6 and VitaminB12 was also optimized at levels which showed improved hatchability, body weight, and other physiological outcomes.

Eggs collection and dose groups

A total of two hundred fertilized Golden Black breed of domestic chicken eggs were collected from nearby Sargodha villages. Eggs were brought in lab within 24 h to ensure optimal freshness. Eggs were weighed and randomly distributed into four groups (50 eggs in each group). Dose administration

Eggs were rinsed and disinfected by using cotton wipe emersed in 70% alcohol and placed in horizontal position. For dose administration small area of egg shell was softened by applying 1 drop of concentrated HCL bit away from center of egg. Disinfected 1 mL insulin syringes were used to inject measured dose into eggs. Melted wax was used to seal injection site on egg shell.

Eggs incubation

The eggs were incubated (at 37 ͦC, 60% humidity) for 14 days in disinfected automatic egg incubator (Nanchang Vena egg incubator, VA−48) having capacity of 50 eggs. Candling was done after every 96 h and eggs showing no development of embryo were discarded.

Embryos recovery

On 14th day of incubation embryos were recovered. The embryos were preserved in fixative (90% alcohol and 10% formaldehyde) for future morphometric studies.

Morphometry

For morphometric studies, embryos were taken out from preservative and weighed one by one using electrical balance. A close examination of the embryos revealed morphological anomalies. Morphometric data like crown-rump length, beak length, eyes length and width and forelimbs length were taken using Vernier Calliper having no zero error. The whole data was saved for statistical analysis.

Photography

Photography was carried out by using 17 MP camera in super-macro mode. Canva software was used for cropping and labelling of photographs.

Data and statistical analysis

Morphometric data was analyzed using ANOVA and ANCOVA tests to calculate the mean variations among every group. IBM SPSS Statistics 23 software was used to apply the tests. Statistical significance was assessed using p value of 0.05

Chicken embryos in Lead treatment group compared to control group disclosed significant terato-morphogenic effects. Reduction in body size and weight, microcephaly and abnormal beak development were prominent morphological abnormalities. Other abnormalities include agnathia, evisceration, reduced down plumage development, skewed spine, reduced neck length and torticollis was observed causing head to bend away from the normal angle. Appendicular anomalies included forelimb and hind limb amelia, meromelia in hind limbs and reduced shank. In some chick embryos agenesis, fused claws and irregular digits development were noted. Lead treated group showed higher mortality rate compared to control group (B, Fig. 1).

Growth, weight, and general body development all increased in chick embryos given a combined dose of vitamins B6 and B12. Extensive feather growth was also noted. (C, Fig. 1). However, chick embryos treated with lead and Vitamin B6 + B12 combined dose showed reduction in size and feather growth. The combined dose of vitamins B6 and B12 also concealed the emergence of other abnormalities. Morphology of embryos in vitamin B6 and B12 treated group was almost similar to control group which support the hypothesis that vitamin B6 and B12 have rescuing effect against heavy metals exposure (D, Fig. 1).

2) Mean crown-rump length:

For the crown-rump length analysis, One-way ANOVA test was applied. A very significant difference (p 0.024) between the groups was found by this test. Post-Hoc evaluation using Tukey's Multiple Range Test (TMRT) indicated significantly shorter lengths (p ≤ 0.003) in the lead-exposed group (34.00 ± 4.51.mm) and the lead + vit. B6 group (48.33 ± 1.76 mm) compared to the control group (46.53 ± 2.53 mm). The difference between the control and vitamin B6 group (47.12 ± 0.58 mm) was not significant (p > 0.007) (Table 1).

3) Mean fronto-occipital length:

To analyze the fronto-occipital length, ANCOVA test was applied with crown-rump mean length as a covariate. This analysis showed that the differences among all groups were significant (p ≤ 0.002). Further investigations applying the multiple comparison Least Significant Difference (LSD) Post Hoc test, indicated significantly shorter fronto-occipital lengths (p ≤ 0.006) in the lead-exposed group (11.16 ± 0.70 mm) compared to the control group (13.51 ± 0.47 mm). The fronto-occipital length was increased in the lead + vit. B6 group (15.97 0.52 mm) in comparison to the lead group. However, the difference between the between the vitamin B6 group (16.14 ± 0.45 mm) and the Pb + vit. B6 group was not significant (p > 0.06) (Table 1).

4) Mean biparietal distance:

ANCOVA test with crown-rump length as a covariate was applied to analyze the mean biparietal distance. The results showed moderate significance (p ≤ 0.018) among the four groups. Post Hoc analysis, specifically LSD-based multiple comparison tests, revealed a significantly shorter mean biparietal distance (5.23 ± 1.65 mm) in the lead-exposed group compared to the control group (10.67 ± 1.13 mm). The group that received pyridoxine (vitamin B6) had a significantly increased biparietal distance (12.15 ± 1.07 mm) in comparison to the lead exposed group. However, the difference between the control and vitamin B6 group (12.15 ± 1.07 mm) was not significant (p > 0.05) (Table 1).

5) Mean eye width:

ANCOVA test with crown-rump as a covariate was applied for eye width analysis. The results were significant (p ≤ 0.15) among the groups. Post Hoc tests showed a decrease in mean eye width from the lead exposed group (6.39 ± 1.32 mm) to the control group (9.84 ± 0.85 mm). The mean values for the lead + Vit. B6 group and the vitamin B6 group were (8.80 ± 0.80 mm) and (10.50 ± 0.79 mm), respectively. the difference between the control and vitamin B6 group (10.50 ± 0.79 mm) was not significant (p > 0.05) (Table 1).

6) Mean eye length:

ANCOVA test was applied to analyze the mean eye length, using crown-rump as a covariate. Between the four groups, the results indicated a significant difference of (p < 0.025). Post Hoc LSD-based comparisons shown significance (p < 0.04) between the lead-exposed group (7.86 ± 0.76 mm) and the lead + Vit. B6 group (10.72 ± 0.57 mm). Between the control group (9.06 ± 0.52 mm) and the vitamin B6 group (11.13 ± 0.49 mm), as well as between the control group and the lead + Vit. B6 group, there was a discernible difference (p < 0.04) (Table 1).

7) Mean eye circumference:

The ANCOVA test revealed that there was a very significant difference in the mean eye circumference between the groups (p ≤ 0.006). A distinction that is significant (p ≤ 0.05) was found between the lead exposed group (22.36 ± 2.05 mm) and the vitamin B6 group (30.59 ± 1.32 mm) in Post-Hoc comparisons. The control group (28.31 ± 1.40 mm) and the lead + Vit. B6 group (24.33 ± 1.53 mm) did not vary significantly (p > 0.05) (Table 1).

8) Mean beak length:

Both upper and lower beak lengths were analyzed using ANCOVA (with crown-rump as a covariate), revealing significant differences (p ≤ 0.02) among all four groups. The Post-Hoc analysis, using the Least Significant Difference for multiple comparisons, indicated a substantial reduction in upper beak length (p ≤ 0.05) from the lead-exposed group. (3.68 ± 0.65 mm) compared to the control group (8.87 ± 0.45 mm) and the lead + Vit. B6 group (8.61 ± 0.49 mm).

In comparison to the lead-exposed group, the lower beak length in the lead + Vit. B6 group was substantially longer. The lead + Vitamin B6 group, the vitamin B6 group (6.42 ± 0.76 mm), and the control group did not show significant (p > 0.016). Similarly, in comparison to the lead-exposed group, the mean lower beak length in the lead + vitamin B6 group was substantially longer (6.42 ± 0.76 mm), and a significant difference (p < 0.03) was seen between the control group, vitamin B6 group (7.42 ± 0.72 mm), and the lead + Vit. B6 group (Table 1).

9) Mean anti-brachium length:

The lengths of both the left and right anti-brachium (arms) were analyzed using an ANCOVA test, with crown-rump length as a covariate. For both the left and right anti-brachium, the results revealed extremely significant differences (p ≤ 0.001) across all the groups. Employing post-hoc analysis and the Least Significant Difference in particular revealed significantly shorter left anti-brachium length (14.68 ± 1.25 mm) in the lead-exposed group compared to the control group (11.40 ± 0.85 mm). Similarly, the right anti-brachium length also showed significantly shorter measurements (p ≤ 0.009) in the lead group (7.15 ± 1.22 mm) compared to the control group (10.94 ± 0.83 mm) (Table 1).

10) Mean brachium length:

Discussion

Discussion

This study reports the teratogenic effects of lead. Lead is now present in Antarctic snow and oceanic crust due to its multipurpose uses and boundless anthropogenic activities [51]. Lead is one of the hazardous heavy metals that effects not only the adult birds but also the developing embryos in eggs [52]. Research findings show that environmental pollution, use of supplements and preservatives, or the technology and equipment utilized during manufacturing of animal feeds make their contamination with heavy metal unavoidable [53]. Investigating the rescuing effects of vitamins (B6&B12) combined dose against teratogenic effects induced by lead is novelty of present study.

According to previous research findings, lead can harm mammals and other species in numerous ways, including cytotoxic, embryotoxic, neurotoxic, and mutagenic effects [54], [55]. Oxidative stress, mitochondrial malfunction, calcium substitution, and epigenetic alteration are the mechanisms underlying lead toxicity [56]. Lead attaches itself to erythrocytes, travels to soft tissues (brain, liver, and kidneys), and builds up in bones, where 70–90% of it is kept until being released over time [57]. It exerts damaging effects on developing fetuses through passing placenta and inhibits essential enzymes, which interferes with heme production and results in anaemia [58].

Even trace lead amounts in feed can have a major impact on broiler growth, including dose-related increases in Pb in the blood, kidney, liver, and tibia of broiler chickens as well as decrease in body weight and delta-aminolevulinic acid dehydratase (ALAD) activity [59] Chicken embryos treated with lead showed reduction in skull size, overall body size, absence of feathers, amelia, micromelia, agnathia, rudimentary upper beak, exposed visceral mass, reduced eye circumference and micropthalmia. Previous research also corroborate these findings as lead exposure even at minute level increased mortality rate and anomalies particularly in skull [60]. Lead affects calcium metabolism, neurotransmitter levels, and leads to apoptosis through epigenetic processes like aberrant methylation and histone alteration. It increases the risk of developmental and cognitive abnormalities by altering gene expression and affecting metastable epialleles [51], [61].

Evisceration and muscular dystrophy as an outcome of lead poisoning can be correlated with previous research on zebrafish where lead treatment disturbed the normal development of muscle myofibrils [62]. Reduced neck length, and skewed spine might be due to effect of lead on chondrocytes maturation and it delays endochondral ossification [63].

Abnormal development of fore and hind limbs, reduced fronto-occipital length and crown rump length revealed that lead might have induced weakness in bones and osteoporosis. It might have affected keratin deposition in claws and collagen synthesis in bones during embryonic development [64]. Research showed that lead acetate treatment halted somite cells development including chondrogenesis, myogenesis and collagen synthesis in developing chick embryo [65]. Lead treatment affected eye formation and caused micropthalmia. Rudimentary upper beak and agnathia was observed which suggest that lead exerted toxic effects on neural crest cells [66].

Vitamin B6 holds vital functions in numerous metabolic pathways with anti-inflammatory and neuro-protective features [67], [68]. Vitamin B6 holds properties of reducing negative effects of lead exposure, supporting the immune system, working as an antioxidant, stimulating weight gain and growth of embryos besides other positive aspects [69]. Pyridoxine shows antioxidant features through directly reacting with reactive oxygen radicals due to the hydroxyl and amine groups on its pyridine ring [70]..In present study Vitamin B6 worked synergistically with Vitamin B12 to ameliorate lead induced teratogenicity by increasing antioxidant levels and improved survival rate of chick embryos.

Vitamin B12 injections during embryonic stage in broilers lead to elevated levels of proteins and glucose [71]. Vitamin B12 supplementation in hens reduced liver fat content, increased albumin content, suppressed production of reactive oxygen species and improved hatachability [72]. It works by directly removing free radicals, indirectly increasing antioxidant enzymes, and blocking inflammatory signals pathways. Embryos treated with Methylcobalamin showed better feather development, enhanced body weight, and fewer morphological abnormalities [73].. Similar results were found in previous research where vitamin B12 reduced oxyfluorofen induced teratogenic effects in 14 day chick embryos [74].

Vitamins like B6 and B12 have been demonstrated in recent studies showing clinical efficacy against toxicity caused by heavy metals. Methylcobalamin has been shown to be useful in mitigating the developmental toxicity induced by lambda-cyhalothrin [75]. While vitamin B6 improved morphological abnormalities in Pb-exposed chick embryos [69]. These water soluble vitamins using their antioxidant properties guard against reactive oxygen species that disturb normal patterns of development and functions of body tissues. In previous studies, decrease in oxidative stress was noted in chick embryos after in ovo administration of vitamin B6 and vitamin B12 combined dose [76]. In present study, chick embryos treated with vitamin B6 & B12 combined dose showed increase in growth, weight, overall development and extensive feather growth.

Conclusion

This study provides insights for further research on exploring teratogenic effects of lead. Vitamin B6 and B12 supplementation can reduce the teratogenic effects of lead in poultry. Lead is used as binding agent in poultry feed and can be harmful when its level exceeds the permissible limits. Ingestion of lead shot pellets can increase lead bio magnification in birds. Furthermore, it is anticipated that including vitamin B6 & B12 in the diet will lower the likelihood of abnormalities developing in people exposed to heavy metals like lead. Results of this research work will play beneficial role in maintaining health standards both in humans and poultry industry.

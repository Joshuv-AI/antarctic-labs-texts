β \beta -Uniform Convexity and Divisible Domains
Amelia Pompilio

Abstract
. Divisible convex sets have long been important in the study of Hilbert geometries. When a divisible convex set is an ellipsoid, the Hilbert geometry it induces is the hyperbolic space. In general, strictly convex divisible domains exhibit negative curvature properties, but only the ellipsoid is a CAT(0) space. The notion of p p -uniform convexity from the theory of Banach spaces has been proposed as a generalization of the Alexandrov-Toponogov comparison theorems to Finsler manifolds. We prove that a natural Finsler metric on a strictly convex divisible domain is β \beta -uniformly convex, where the exact constant β \beta is related to the regularity of the boundary.



1.
Introduction

Divisible convex domains
\Omega\in{\mathbb{R}}{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
have historically been objects of interest due to their rich connections to multiple areas of geometry. They form the most interesting examples of Hilbert geometries and have received thorough surveying as in
[
Ben08
]
and
[
Mar14
]
.

Kelly and Strauss proved that if a Hilbert geometry has determinate Busemann curvature, then it must be an ellipsoid and hence hyperbolic
[
KS58
]
. Further, as Hilbert geometries are Finsler manifolds, they are CAT(
\kappa
) only when
\Omega
is an ellipsoid
[
Oht09
]
. For non-ellipsoid strictly convex divisible domains, there are still hints of negative curvature; for example, the geodesic flow of the Hilbert metric on the quotient
\Gamma\backslash\Omega
is Anosov (see Theorem
2.4
). To understand this behavior, we study the notion of
p
-uniform convexity, a decay condition on the modulus of convexity (see Definition
2.8
). In
[
Oht09
]
, Ohta proposes this notion as a generalization of the CAT(0) condition.

For
x_{0}\in\Omega
, let
M_{\Omega}(x_{0},\cdot)
be the Minkowski functional (see Definition
2.3
). Now we can state our main result:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{RP}^{n}
be a strictly convex divisible domain. Then for any
x_{0}\in\Omega,
the space
\big(\Omega,M(x_{0},\cdot)\big)
is
\beta
-uniformly convex for some
\beta\in[2,\infty)
.

There is a natural Finsler metric
F_{\Omega}(x,\cdot)
on
\Omega
that is the symmetrization of the Minkowski functional
M_{\Omega}(x_{0},\cdot)
, see Definition
2.2
. Due to this fact we get the following corollary of Theorem
1.1
:

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{RP}^{n}
be a strictly convex divisible domain. Then the space
\big(\Omega,F_{\Omega}(x,\cdot)\big)
is
\beta
-uniformly convex for some
\beta\in[2,\infty)
.

The proof of Theorem
1.1
is divided into three cases; one degenerate case, and two cases in which we use geometric constructions to bound the modulus of convexity of
\Omega
from below. All three cases require a series of affine transformations that situate
\Omega
in convenient coordinates for our computations, and these transformations are outlined in Section
3

This paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we briefly introduce the notions of divisible convex sets,
\alpha
-Hölder regularity and
\beta
-convexity, and
p
-uniform convexity. In Section
3
we describe the precise coordinate set-up required for the proof of theorem. In Section
4
we prove Theorem
1.1
and Corollary
1.2
, and in Section
5
we discuss how our results may extend to the Hilbert metric on
\Omega
.

2.
Background

2.1.
Divisible Convex Sets

Definition 2.1

(see e.g.
[
Ben08
]
)
.

A
divisible convex set
is a properly convex open subset
\Omega\subset\mathbb{RP}^{n}
for which there exists a discrete group
\Gamma
of projective transformations which acts cocompactly on
\Omega
.

Any such
\Omega
will come equipped with the Hilbert metric
d_{\Omega}(x,y)
. To define
d_{\Omega}(x,y)
for
x,y\in\Omega
, we consider the line
l
through
x
and
y
and the points
l\cap\partial\Omega=\{a,b\}
. We then have the quadruple of points in the order
a,x,y,b
and compute the quantity

(2.1)

d_{\Omega}(x,y)=\log\bigg(\frac{|ay|\cdot|xb|}{|ax|\cdot|by|}\bigg)

The Hilbert metric comes from a Finsler metric on
\Omega
, see
[
Mar14
]
.

Definition 2.2
.

The natural
Finsler metric
on
\Omega
is defined by

(2.2)

F_{\Omega}(x,v)=\frac{d}{dt}\biggr\rvert_{t=0}d_{\Omega}(x,x+tv)=\frac{|v|}{2}\bigg(\frac{1}{|xp^{-}|}+\frac{1}{|xp^{+}|}\bigg)

where
x\in\Omega
,
v\in T_{x}\Omega
, and
p^{+},p^{-}\in\partial\Omega
are the forward and backward boundary points in the directions of
v
and
-v
.

For the proof of Theorem
1.1
, we consider the Minkowski functional relative to
\Omega
.

Definition 2.3
.

The
Minkowski functional
on
\Omega
is defined by

(2.3)

M_{\Omega}(x_{0},v):=\inf\biggl\{a>0:\frac{1}{a}v\in\Omega\biggr\}

where
x_{0}\in\interior\Omega
and
v\in T_{x_{0}}\Omega
. The Minkowski functional
M_{\Omega}(x_{0},v)
can be thought of as the “forward direction” of the Finsler metric on
\Omega
. Moreover,
F_{\Omega}(x,v)
is the symmetrization of
M_{\Omega}(x_{0},v)
.

2.2.
\alpha
-Hölder regularity and
\beta
-convexity

The
\alpha
-Hölder regularity regularity of
\partial\Omega
is related to the Anosov geodesic flow of the Hilbert metric on
\Gamma\backslash\Omega
. In this section we recall the statement of this fact due to Benoist and define
\beta
-convexity.

Theorem 2.4

(Benoist
[
Ben08
]
)
.

Let
\Gamma
be a torsion-free discrete subgroup which divides some strictly convex open set
\Omega
in
\mathbb{S}^{n}
. Then the geodesic flow
\phi_{t}
of the Hilbert metric on the quotient
\Gamma\backslash\Omega
is Anosov.

As a consequence of Theorem
2.4
, we have the following statement about the regularity of
\partial\Omega
.

Theorem 2.5

(Benoist
[
Ben08
]
)
.

Let
\Omega
be a divisible strictly convex open set in
\mathbb{S}^{n}
. Suppose that
\Omega
is not an ellipsoid. Then there exists a maximal
\alpha\in(1,2)
such that the boundary
\partial\Omega
is
C^{\alpha}
.

We now define
\beta
-convexity.

Definition 2.6

(Guichard
[
Gui05
]
)
.

Let
M
be a hypersurface of class
C^{1}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
M
is called
\beta
-convex
for some real number
\beta\geq 2
if, for all compact
K
in
M
, there exists a strictly positive constant
C_{K}
such that

(2.4)

d(x,T_{y}M)\geq C_{K}d(x,y)^{\beta}\text{ for all }x,y\in K.

Remarks 2.7
.

(1)

Guichard remarks in Section 3.2 of
[
Gui05
]
that Definition
2.6
applies to the boundary
\partial\Omega
, and is equivalent to
\beta
-convexity of
f
if
M
is the graph of a
C^{1}
function
f
.

(2)

The inequality
2.4
implies that

(2.5)

d(x,T_{y}M)\geq C_{K}t^{\beta},

where
t=\|\text{proj}_{T_{y}M}x\|
.

(3)

The constants
\alpha
and
\beta
satisfy the relation

(2.6)

\frac{1}{\alpha}+\frac{1}{\beta}=1

with
\alpha=\beta=2
only when
\Omega
is an ellipse
[
Gui05
]
. In the terminology of
[
BCL94
]
,
\alpha
and
\beta
are
dual indices

2.3.
p-Uniform Convexity

We now define
p
-uniform convexity in the context of normed spaces. Later, this will apply to the asymmetric Minkowski functional
M_{\Omega}(x_{0},\cdot)
.

Definition 2.8

(Suzuki
[
Suz14
]
)
.

For
p\in[2,\infty)
, a Banach space
(X,\|\cdot\|)
is
p
-uniformly convex
if there exists
C>0
satisfying

\delta(\varepsilon)\geq C\varepsilon^{p}

for all
\varepsilon\in[0,2]
where
\delta(\varepsilon)
is the
modulus of convexity
defined by the infimum

\delta(\varepsilon)=\inf\bigg(1-\frac{\|x+y\|}{2}\bigg)

for
\varepsilon\in[0,2],
where the infimum is taken over all
x,y\in X
with
\|x\|,\|y\|\leq 1
, and
\|x-y\|\geq\varepsilon
.

Geometrically,
\delta(\varepsilon)
can be thought of as measuring the rate at which the midpoint of a chord of length
\varepsilon
in the unit ball of
X
approaches the boundary of the ball as
\varepsilon
decreases.

Remarks 2.9
.

(1)

The notion of
p
-uniform convexity comes from the study of Banach spaces (see
[
BCL94
]
), and our definition above is due to Suzuki
[
Suz14
]
.

(2)

We note that
\delta(\varepsilon)
is increasing with
\varepsilon
, and that it is sufficient to consider
x,y\in X
with
\|x\|,\|y\|=1
.

(3)

If
X
is a Hilbert space, then
\delta(\varepsilon)=1-\sqrt{1-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{4}}
, so
X
is 2-uniformly convex.

3.
Coordinate Set-Up

Two cases in the proof of the main theorem require linear transformations in order to put
\Omega
in convenient coordinates. In this section we describe these transformations and prove that the amount they distort
\Omega
is bounded.

Let
\Omega
be strictly convex,
x_{0}\in\Omega
, and
\xi\in\partial\Omega
. We define

T:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{n}

as the composition

T=T_{3}\circ T_{2}\circ T_{1}

where

T_{1}:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{n}

rotates
\Omega
so that
T_{\xi}\Omega
is parallel to
\{x_{2}=\dots=x_{n}=0\}
,

T_{2}:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{n}

simultaneously translates
x_{0}
to the first-coordinate axis and rotates
\xi
into the plane given by the first two coordinates, and

T_{3}:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{n}

is the shear transformation taking
\xi
to the second coordinate axis, defined by

(3.1)

A=\begin{bmatrix}1&-\cot(\psi(\xi))&0\dots 0\\
0&1&\\
\vdots&\vdots&I_{n-2}\\
0&0&\end{bmatrix}

where the function

(3.2)

\psi:\partial\Omega\longrightarrow(0,\pi)

is defined as the composition

\psi=\psi_{2}\circ\psi_{1}

where

\displaystyle\psi_{1}:\partial\Omega

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathbb{S}^{n}\times\mathbb{S}^{n}

\displaystyle\xi

\displaystyle\longmapsto(v(\xi),w(\xi))

and

\displaystyle\psi_{2}:\mathbb{S}^{n}\times\mathbb{S}^{n}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow(0,\pi)

\displaystyle(v(\xi),w(\xi))

\displaystyle\longmapsto\angle_{\xi}(v(\xi),w(\xi))

In the above definition,
v(\xi)
is the unit vector based at
\xi\in\partial\Omega
in the direction of the basepoint
x_{0}\in\Omega
and
w(\xi)
is tangent to
\Omega
at
\xi\in\partial\Omega
.

Claim 3.1
.

For fixed basepoint
x_{0}\in\Omega
, the shear transformation defined by
A
is bounded independently of
\xi
.

Proof.

The angle function
\psi
is continuous on a closed set and thus has a maximum and a minimum. Indeed,
\psi_{1}
is continuous since
\partial\Omega
is
C^{1}
, and thus
\psi=\psi_{2}\circ\psi_{1}
is also continuous.

Moreover,
\psi(\xi)
is bounded away from 0 and
\pi
. We recall that since
\Omega
is strictly convex, it must lie completely on one side of its tangent line at
\xi\in\partial\Omega
. Then, since
x_{0}\in\interior\Omega
and the vectors
v(\xi)
and
w(\xi)
meet at
\xi\in\partial\Omega
, the lines spanned by
v(\xi)
and
w(\xi)
cannot be parallel.
∎

4.
Proof of the main theorem

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
, and consider
x,y\in\Omega
with
M_{\Omega}(x-y)>\varepsilon
.

Case 1

(
\frac{x+y}{2}=x_{0}
)
.

If
\frac{x+y}{2}=x_{0}
, then

1-\frac{M_{\Omega}(x+y)}{2}=1

Then choose
C_{0}=2^{-\beta}
so that

(4.1)

C_{0}\varepsilon^{\beta}=\big(\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\big)^{\beta}\leq 1

as desired.

When
\frac{x+y}{2}\neq x_{0}
, we define
\xi\in\partial\Omega
as the boundary point in the direction of
\frac{x+y}{2}
as seen from
x_{0}
. Using
\xi\in\partial\Omega
, we situate
\Omega
in coordinates as specified in Section
3
. We define
\theta
as the angle between
x-y
and
T_{\xi}\Omega
, and consider the cases where
\theta<\frac{\pi}{4}
and
\theta\geq\frac{\pi}{4}
separately.

Case 2

(
\theta<\frac{\pi}{4}
)
.

Since
\partial\Omega
is
\beta
-convex, we have that
\partial\Omega
is bounded below by
f(t)=C_{K}|t|^{\beta}-h
for some constants
C_{K}
and
h
, with both curves meeting at
\xi=(0,-h)
. We denote by
\delta_{\Omega}(\varepsilon)
the modulus of convexity of
\Omega
, and define
z
and
w
as the vertical projections onto
f(t)
of
x
and
y
, respectively, as shown in Figure
1
. Since
\frac{M_{\Omega}(x+y)}{2}<\frac{M_{f}(z+w)}{2}
, we proceed by computing
\frac{M_{f}(z+w)}{2}
in order to bound
\delta_{\Omega}(\varepsilon)
from below.

Case 2

By definition (see
2.3
)

\frac{M_{f}(z+w)}{2}=\frac{1}{a}

where
a>0
is the scaling factor such that
a(C_{K}|t_{0}|^{\beta}-h)=-h
. Then

1-\frac{M_{f}(z+w)}{2}=1-\frac{1}{a}=1-\frac{C_{K}|t_{0}|^{\beta}-h}{-h}=\frac{C_{K}|t_{0}|^{\beta}}{h}

Next, we use equivalence of norms to put
\frac{C_{K}|t_{0}|^{\beta}}{h}
in terms of
\varepsilon
, where
\|\cdot\|
denotes the Euclidean norm. There is a
c_{1}>0
such that

\displaystyle c_{1}M_{\Omega}(x-y)

\displaystyle\leq\|x-y\|

\displaystyle c_{1}\varepsilon

\displaystyle\leq\|x-y\|

\displaystyle c_{1}\varepsilon\cos\theta

\displaystyle\leq\|x-y\|\cos\theta

\displaystyle c_{1}\varepsilon\cos\theta

\displaystyle\leq 2t_{0}

And thus

(4.2)

\delta_{\Omega}(\varepsilon)>\frac{C_{K}|t_{0}|^{\beta}}{h}\geq\frac{C_{K}(c_{1}\varepsilon\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2})^{\beta}}{h}=C_{1}\varepsilon^{\beta}.

Case 3

(
\theta\geq\frac{\pi}{4}
)
.

We put the piecewise function

(4.3)

f(t)=\begin{cases}-h&\text{if }t\leq 0\\
mt-h&\text{if }t>0,\end{cases}

within
\Omega
as shown in figure
2
.

Figure 2.
Case 3

We compute
\frac{M_{f}(x+y)}{2}
to bound
\delta_{\Omega}(\varepsilon)
from below. Here we consider
a>0
the scaling factor such that
a(\frac{mt_{0}}{2}-h)=-h
. Then

(4.4)

1-\frac{M_{f}(x+y)}{2}=1-\frac{1}{a}=1-\frac{\frac{mt_{0}}{2}-h}{-h}=\frac{mt_{0}}{2h}

Comparing norms, there is
c_{2}>0
such that

\displaystyle c_{2}M_{\Omega}(x-y)

\displaystyle\leq\|x-y\|

\displaystyle c_{2}\varepsilon

\displaystyle\leq t_{0}\sqrt{4+m^{2}}

\displaystyle\frac{c_{2}\varepsilon}{\sqrt{4+m^{2}}}

\displaystyle\leq t_{0}

So

\delta_{\Omega}(\varepsilon)>\frac{mt_{0}}{2h}\geq\frac{mc_{2}\varepsilon}{2h\sqrt{4+m^{2}}}\geq\frac{c_{2}\varepsilon}{h\sqrt{8}}>\frac{c_{2}\varepsilon^{\beta}}{h\sqrt{8}}=C_{2}\varepsilon^{\beta}

where the third inequality is due to the slope
m
having a minimum value of 2. This completes Case 3.

Taking
C:=\min\{C_{0},C_{1},C_{2}\}
, we have

(4.5)

\delta_{\Omega}(\varepsilon)\geq C\varepsilon^{\beta}

for all
\varepsilon\in[0,2]
.
∎

We now use the result in
4.5
to show that Theorem
1.1
holds for the Finsler metric on
\Omega
.

Proof of Corollary
1.2
.

We use the notation

M^{\pm}(v):=M(\pm v)

throughout. There exists an
A>0
such that

(4.6)

\frac{1}{A}M^{-}\leq M^{+}\leq AM^{-}.

Take
x,y\in\Omega
such that
F(x-y)>\varepsilon
. Since

F_{\Omega}(x,v)=\frac{M^{+}_{\Omega}(x,v)+M^{-}_{\Omega}(x,v)}{2},

without loss of generality we have
M^{+}_{\Omega}(x-y)>\varepsilon
, and so
M^{-}_{\Omega}(x-y)>\frac{\varepsilon}{A}
. Then by
4.5
,

(4.7)

1-\frac{M^{+}_{\Omega}(x+y)}{2}\geq C_{+}\varepsilon^{\beta}

and

(4.8)

1-\frac{M^{-}_{\Omega}(x+y)}{2}\geq C_{-}\bigg(\frac{\varepsilon}{A}\bigg)^{\beta}.

Then

(4.9)

1-\frac{F_{\Omega}(x+y)}{2}=1-\frac{\frac{M^{+}_{\Omega}(x+y)+M^{-}_{\Omega}(x+y)}{2}}{2}>\Bigg(\frac{C_{+}+\frac{C_{-}}{A^{\beta}}}{2}\Bigg)\cdot\varepsilon^{\beta}>0

∎

5.
\beta
-Uniform Convexity of the Hilbert metric?

For narrative resolution it would be satisfying to prove that the Hilbert metric
d_{\Omega}(x,y)
on a divisible convex set has
\beta
-uniformly convex behavior. Toward this end, Ohta’s generalization of 2-uniform convexity for a nonlinear metric space
(X,d)
may be useful.

Definition 5.1

(Ohta
[
Oht09
]
)
.

Let
(X,d)
be a nonlinear metric space. Then
(X,d)
is
2-uniformly convex
if for any
x\in X
and any minimal geodesic
\eta:[0,1]\longrightarrow X
, we have

(5.1)

d\bigg(x,\eta\bigg(\frac{1}{2}\bigg)\bigg)^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}d(x,\eta(0))^{2}+\frac{1}{2}d(x,\eta(1))^{2}-\frac{1}{4C^{2}}d(\eta(0),\eta(1))^{2}

Remark 5.2
.

When
C=1
, Equation
5.1
corresponds to the CAT(0) property.

We then have the following question.

Question 5.3
.

Let
\Omega
be a strictly convex divisible domain with the Hilbert metric
d_{\Omega}
. Is
(\Omega,d_{\Omega})

\beta
-uniformly convex?

To answer Question
5.3
, it would likely be necessary to prove that
F_{\Omega}(x_{0},\cdot)
is
uniformly

\beta
-uniformly convex, i.e. that
\delta_{\Omega}(\varepsilon)\geq C\varepsilon^{\beta}
with
C
independent of the choice of basepoint
x_{0}\in\Omega
.

Distal phalanx of the hand’s digit as unusual autologous biological tissue acting as a contaminating foreign body in a combat patient with ballistic pelvic injury: a case report from war in Ukraine
Authors: Igor Lurin, Eduard Khoroshun, Volodymyr Nehoduiko, Serhii Shypilov, Maksym Gorobeiko, Andrii Dinets
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe use of various types of weapons against the Armed Forces of Ukraine in the Russo-Ukrainian war leads to both typical and atypical clinical presentations of ballistic injuries. Pelvic injuries are common and can result in severe consequences if patient evacuation is delayed. Wounded projectiles are often metal fragments, while biological tissues are rarely found. The aim of this report is to demonstrate the example of the management of the combat patient with ballistic injury to the pelvis associated with contamination by an autologous biologic part of phalanx of the hand’s digit of the wounded.Case presentationA 36-year-old male service member of the Armed Forces of Ukraine sustained injuries from a shelling incident involving the deployment of a precision-guided munition (KAB) from an aircraft in Eastern Ukraine. The patient was evacuated to the Role 3 deployed field hospital in Kharkiv. A whole-body CT scan revealed two foreign fragments in the abdomen and retroperitoneal space. Damage control surgery was performed. During the operation, the right psoas major muscle was isolated, and a 50 × 30 mm metallic fragment was found and removed. Additionally, while examining the wound tract in the right inguinal region, the distal phalanx of the patient’s right third hand digit was identified and removed.ConclusionsBallistic injuries in the Russo-Ukrainian war are associated with severe wounds and possible unexpected clinical findings, such as contamination by an autologous biological fragment converted into a projectile. Fast evacuation of the injured person to the appropriate Role of the deployed field facility is associated with good treatment outcomes. Whole-body computed tomography is an important diagnostic tool for evaluating body damage and identifying hidden injuries and fragments of ballistic projectiles. Biological tissue material is an unusual and infrequent foreign body object in the case of ballistic trauma.

Introduction

The ongoing Russia-Ukraine war is associated with various kinds of trauma, which is usually severe [1]. The latter is frequently associated with surgical challenges in identifying the best approach to treat patients under conditions of limited medical resources [2–4]. The military medicine facilities in Ukraine apply various emergency approaches such as damage control resuscitation, damage control surgery, which is in line with international and our own experience from the hybrid period of Russia-Ukraine war (2014–2022) followed by further improvement of military medicine during the full Russian invasion since 2022 [5–11].

There are various kinds of shelling projectiles causing severe injuries, with typical and unusual clinical presentations [1, 12]. The contamination by the biological tissues of victims is less common, and previously described in studies of suicide bombers, however such kind of contamination is not frequent by autologous tissue. The mechanism of injury with unusual shelling projectiles is associated with ricochet phenomena or direct high-energy impact on nearby objects, whereas biological shelling is expected to result from tissue fragments becoming projectiles after the explosion [13]. On the other hand, military surgeons expect the regular metal shrapnel or bullets to be a cause of the ballistic trauma, whereas other kinds of foreign bodies or biological material are less expected [14, 15]. It is worth mentioning that the ballistic injury to the pelvis is also severe due to damage to the genitourinary organs associated with frequent complex fractures of the pelvic bones and injuries to the pelvic organs [16, 17]. Still, there are limited publications on pelvic gunshot injuries, and management of the injuries to the pelvis has not been presented previously in the ongoing war in Ukraine.

The aim of this report is to demonstrate the example of the management of the combat patient with ballistic injury to the pelvis associated with contamination by an autologous biologic part of phalanx of the hand’s digit of the wounded.

Case presentation

A 36-year-old male serviceman of the Armed Forces of Ukraine sustained a shelling injury as a result of the deployment of a precision-guided munition namely “KAB” from an aircraft vehicle in East Ukraine. The patient was evacuated to the medical stabilization point (located 1–3 km from the frontline) within 30 min of the injury, in accordance with the “golden hour” principle. At the stabilization point, the patient was diagnosed with severe pelvic injury and amputation of the distal one-third phalanx of the third digit of the right hand, and the decision was made to evacuate the patient to a higher level of medical care. The patient was transported to the Forward Surgical Team, where a whole body X-ray examination, abdominal and pelvic ultrasound, and primary surgical debridement of the wounds were performed. At admission, the patient was diagnosed with an epibole wound in the suprapubic area measuring 50 × 30 mm with settled edges without signs of bleeding. Considering possible severe pelvic injury and potential damage to genitourinary organs, the patient was evacuated to the Role 3 deployed field hospital in Kharkiv, arriving 3 h after injury. Upon admission to the Role 3, the patient underwent typical examination for combat patients according to previously described protocol and equipment [18, 19]. The blood tests showed mild leukocytosis of 12.2 × 109/L; other tests did not reveal specific changes. The patient was hemodynamically stable. Computed tomography (CT) scan analyses showed two foreign bodies in the retroperitoneal space, indicating shelling fragments after the ballistic injury and suggesting the surgical revision of both the abdominal cavity as well as the retroperitoneal space to the right. A significant defect in the suprapubic area measuring 50 × 30 mm was confirmed by CT findings and required laparotomy (Figs. 1 and 2). At revision of the abdominal cavity, we did not find any other changes like pelvic fractures or damage of magistral vessels; therefore, the paravesical space was revised. The right psoas major muscle was isolated, and a 50 × 30 mm metal fragment was identified and removed. To our surprise, during the revision of the wound canal of the right inguinal region, the distal phalanx of the third digit of the patient’s right hand was found and removed (Fig. 3). The location of the digit’s fragments suggested it was amputated by the larger fragment, and then implanted on its final location with the kinetic energy from the metal fragment. The operation was completed with installation of the drainage tube to the retroperitoneal space to the right. The operation length was 40 min. The 1 g of Cefazolin was administrated after the operation as intramuscular injection. During the post-operative period no signs of infection was detected, so patient did not require further administration of antibiotics. On the 4th day after the injury, another operation was performed under local anesthesia to apply primary delayed sutures to the stump of the third right hand digit of the right hand and the wound in the pubic area. The delayed closure is typical approach for the closing of ballistic -related wounds, accept the area of the face and scrotum. The sutures were removed on the 14th and 8th days from the mentioned locations, respectively. The patient was discharged on the 18th day after the injury.

Discussion

In this case report, we demonstrated a rare case of a combat patient with ballistic injury to the pelvis and contaminated with autologous biological material, which is the distal part of the hand of the wounded. The distal fragment of the patient’s hand was amputated by a high-energy projectile, which had a trajectory through the patient’s right hand toward the injury to the pelvis. The kinetic energy from the projectiles forced the implantation of the amputated part of the patient’s hand digit to the pelvic area, which is a rare phenomenon.

Despite the biological origin, the digit’s fragment was identified preoperatively in the retroperitoneal space on CT scan, prompting careful revision of that anatomic area during the operation. It is worth mentioning that unusual presentation of combat trauma is suspected routinely for all patients [1, 10, 12, 18–22].

The wounds from the atypical projectiles have been shown for the metal fragments or entire items of camping utensils, cutlery knives, forks, stainless steel rods, in both combat and civil trauma relating to the high-energy weapon application. The biological material is usually reported in studies of suicide bombers with a regular presentation of bony fragments turned into projectiles by the blast wave [21, 23, 24]. It is worth mentioning that these unusual objects, including biological material, might be a cause of severe injury, as we have recently demonstrated in the publication of combat patients who were injured by a fork from camping utensils, resulting in low extremity amputation [1]. Our point of view regarding the severity of the trauma due to injury by unusual projectiles supports results from study by Mittal et al. who presented a lethal case of a factory worker after the injury by a stainless steel projectile [15].

Contamination by biological material is common in victims in the case of a suicide bomber body parts (mainly bony fragments) spread after the explosion [21–23, 25]. Apart from the direct impact on the victims’ bodies by nails or bones of the bomber, these studies also suggested a risk of contamination by such infections as viral hepatitis or HIV [21, 22]. In line with our case, other studies also supported the conclusion that biological fragments converted into projectiles due to explosion are associated with more severe ballistic trauma [21–23, 25]. In our opinion, autologous biological material also serves as an aggravating factor, associated with a higher risk of purulent complications if the biological fragment was not identified during the preoperative evaluation of the patient.

In contrast to previous publications showing evacuation problems due to the FPV drones’ activities, this patient was evacuated within a reasonable frame of time from the Forward Surgical Team level to the Role 3 deployed field hospital, and this is considered an important achievement to prevent severe complications (bleeding, critical ischemia, peritonitis, etc.) [26]. The specific features of the tactical conditions must be permanently taken into consideration to provide appropriate medical aid [7, 11, 27–30].

We would also suggest considering a differential approach to identify multiple or single wounded projectiles preoperatively or unexpectedly during the revision. In cases of a metal nature of the wound, it is important to consider surgical magnetic tools when the fragment is located in a dangerous area, and the risk of damaging the magistral vessels during tissue dissection is high [19, 27]. The choice of surgical approach is at the discretion of the surgical team, based on trauma severity, tactical circumstances, and the surgeon’s experience.

Conclusions

Ballistic injuries in the Russo-Ukrainian war are associated with severe wounds and possible unexpected clinical findings, such as contamination by an autologous biological fragment converted into a projectile. Whole-body computed tomography is an important diagnostic tool for evaluating body damage and identifying hidden injuries and fragments of ballistic projectiles. Biological tissue material is an unusual and infrequent foreign body object in the case of ballistic trauma.

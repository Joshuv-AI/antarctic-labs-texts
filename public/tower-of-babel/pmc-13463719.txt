Preoperative superselective transarterial ICG-lipiodol embolization for endophytic renal tumors in robot-assisted partial nephrectomy: a preliminary feasibility report
Authors: Chia-Chih Hsieh, Ting-Yi Chiang, Wen-Hsin Tseng, Chien-Liang Liu, Reng-Hong Wu, Steven K Huang, Allen W Chiu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPartial nephrectomy for small, anatomically complex renal tumors presents significant navigational challenges. This study aims to evaluate the synergistic hemostatic and navigational utility and surgical outcomes of preoperative superselective indocyanine green (ICG) embolization during retroperitoneal robot-assisted partial nephrectomy (RAPN). We retrospectively reviewed 32 patients who underwent retroperitoneal RAPN for small renal masses (< 4 cm). Patients were stratified by preoperative intervention: 9 patients (28.1%) received superselective transarterial delivery of an ICG-lipiodol mixture with coil embolization 3 h prior to surgery, while 23 patients (71.9%) underwent standard RAPN. A uniform main renal artery clamping protocol was maintained across both cohorts to evaluating the combined utility of ICG. Strikingly, the ICG cohort consisted entirely of completely endophytic tumors (E3 component: 100% vs. 13%, p < 0.001). Despite this extreme anatomical complexity, the ICG group demonstrated significantly shorter median operative time (90.0 vs. 100.2 min, p = 0.049) and lower median estimated blood loss (100 vs. 350 mL, p = 0.039). Consequently, the perioperative blood transfusion rate was lower in the ICG group (11.1% vs. 60.9%, p = 0.018). Median warm ischemia time (16.48 vs. 16.78 min, p = 0.900) and postoperative renal function changes at day 1, 6 months, and 1 year were comparable between groups. No gastrointestinal or embolization-related complications were observed. Our preliminary experience demonstrates that preoperative superselective ICG embolization serves as a feasible and safe adjunct during RAPN for completely endophytic tumors. By providing a synergistic combination of direct local hemostasis and real-time fluorescent navigation, this tailored approach facilitates precise tumor localization. While these initial findings suggest potential perioperative advantages, they should be interpreted as exploratory signals awaiting further validation in larger, well-balanced cohorts.Keywords: Renal tumor, Endophytic, Indocyanine green, Superselective embolization, Partial nephrectomy

Introduction

Representing up to 85% of all kidney cancers, clear cell renal cell carcinoma (ccRCC) remains the most prevalent renal malignancy. Worldwide, approximately 80,000 individuals are diagnosed annually, with the demographic predominantly consisting of males aged 60 to 80 years [1]. The severity of the disease is categorized via the tumor-node-metastasis (TNM) staging system. While patients presenting with localized, early-stage lesions (Stage I, defined as tumors smaller than 7 cm) enjoy a robust 5-year survival rate exceeding 90%, the prognosis for advanced Stage IV disease drops drastically to around 40%, even with the advent of modern combinatorial therapies involving immune checkpoint and tyrosine kinase inhibitors [2]. Consequently, early detection coupled with the complete surgical excision of organ-confined masses is paramount.

For the management of small renal masses, partial nephrectomy (PN) serves as the gold standard. Minimally invasive techniques, such as robotic-assisted or laparoscopic approaches, are highly preferred to resect the tumor while maximizing nephron sparing. This preservation is a crucial factor for individuals with a solitary kidney, baseline renal dysfunction, or those facing highly complex clinical scenarios such as synchronous bilateral renal masses [3, 4]. In these challenging contexts, tailored surgical strategies must be meticulously adapted to optimize postoperative renal function without compromising cancer control [4]. Nevertheless, localizing these small tumors intraoperatively presents a substantial technical hurdle. Masses under 4 cm often fail to alter the external renal contour. Furthermore, they can be entirely endophytic or obscured by thick perinephric adipose tissue, which severely restricts both tactile and visual localization during minimally invasive surgery [5]. Even though these small malignancies typically exhibit distinct pathological borders, their minimal footprint makes it exceedingly difficult for surgeons to accurately delineate the tumor-parenchyma interface.

Although intraoperative ultrasound is the conventional tool for real-time localization, its efficacy is highly operator-dependent. It frequently struggles to identify lesions under 2 cm and can be compromised by suboptimal acoustic windows, dense post-radiation scarring, or the deeply embedded nature of the tumors [6, 7]. Such limitations highlight the necessity for more robust intraoperative navigation. Indocyanine green (ICG), a compound with near-infrared fluorescent capabilities, has driven the evolution of fluorescence-guided surgery since the late 2000s [8]. Initially popularized in hepatobiliary procedures to illuminate liver malignancies and satellite nodules [9], ICG application has progressively transitioned into urologic oncology over the last decade. In renal surgery, it is primarily utilized to visualize vascular perfusion and demarcate lesion borders [10, 11]. However, the predominant literature evaluates systemic intravenous ICG injection, which facilitates selective arterial clamping but falls short in providing precise spatial navigation for deeply buried tumors. An advanced alternative involves the preoperative, superselective transarterial administration of an ICG-lipiodol-ethanol mixture directly into the tumor-feeding branches, previously described by Simone et al. [12]. This hybrid technique devascularizes the mass while establishing a durable “fluorescent tattoo,” thereby sustaining real-time visual guidance for entirely endophytic lesions during robot-assisted partial nephrectomy (RAPN) [11].

The primary objective of this study is to report our small preliminary retroperitoneal feasibility experience utilizing superselective transarterial ICG–lipiodol embolization prior to RAPN for clinical T1a renal tumors (< 4 cm). Building upon the foundational concepts described in prior literature, this study focuses on evaluating the technical reproducibility, safety, and workflow exclusively via a retroperitoneal route. While a standard RAPN cohort is included to provide contextual background, the current study design and sample size are intended for descriptive purposes rather than aiming to validate comparative superiority.

Materials and methods

Study population and design

A retrospective evaluation was conducted on a consecutive cohort of 100 patients treated with retroperitoneal robot-assisted partial nephrectomy (RAPN) at our institution between January 2022 and December 2024. For this analysis, the cohort was strictly refined to 32 individuals diagnosed with small renal masses, defined as lesions measuring under 4 cm in maximum diameter. Within this specific subgroup, 9 patients underwent preoperative superselective ICG embolization (Group A), whereas the remaining 23 proceeded directly to RAPN without prior intervention (Group B). Allocation to these clinical pathways was not randomized; rather, it was determined through a shared decision-making process balancing surgeon recommendation with patient preference. To ensure robust outcome evaluation, all included participants maintained a minimum follow-up duration of 12 months with comprehensively documented medical records.

Renal functional preservation was tracked by calculating the estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) via the Modification of Diet in Renal Disease (MDRD) formula. These metrics were recorded at specific milestones: postoperative day 1 (POD1), month 3 (POM3), month 6 (POM6), and year 1 (POY1). Surgical complications were systematically documented and categorized utilizing the standard Clavien-Dindo grading scale.

Preoperative superselective embolization

For participants in Group A, the targeted embolization protocol was executed approximately three hours prior to the robotic resection. The embolization was performed in the interventional radiology suite under local anesthesia. Patients were then transferred to the operating room, where they received general anesthesia only before the RAPN. The timing of both procedures was tightly synchronized through multidisciplinary pre-surgical meetings to prevent operating room delays. Under strict angiographic surveillance via right common femoral artery access, interventional radiologists identified the hypervascular tertiary-order arterial branches feeding the tumor. An emulsion consisting of 25 mg of ICG dissolved in 5 mL of sterile water, mixed with lipiodol at a 1:2 ratio, was delivered superselectively. To maximize the devascularization effect and prevent agent washout, pushable fibered coils were subsequently deployed within these specific feeding arteries. The technical adequacy of the lipiodol deposition within the target mass was immediately verified using cone-beam computed tomography (CBCT). This comprehensive preparatory step aimed to establish an optimized, virtually bloodless surgical field for the ensuing robotic enucleation (Fig. 1).

Surgical procedure

Three high-volume surgeons at our center executed all RAPN operations utilizing an exclusive retroperitoneal approach. Patients were secured in a full flank position with ipsilateral elevation, and the surgical bed was flexed to expand the retroperitoneal workspace between the iliac crest and the 12th rib. Following standard cutaneous preparation spanning from the xiphoid process to the pubic symphysis anteriorly, and to the umbilicus posteriorly, surgical access was established.

A roughly 2-cm incision was made superior to the iliac crest at the midaxillary line to accommodate the camera trocar. Under direct endoscopic vision, two robotic working ports were positioned roughly 7 cm apart—one along the anterior axillary line and another near the intersection of the latissimus dorsi and the 12th rib. A third robotic port was situated 7 cm from the second, and an assistant port was placed superior to the Gibson incision line (Fig. 2).

Surgical position and trocar placement. a showed full flank position with table flexion and ipsilateral side up. b showed trocars placement and landmarks

Upon retroperitoneal entry, the surgical team identified the psoas muscle and ureter before isolating the renal hilum and vascular pedicle. Following adequate nephrolysis, the tumor was spatially mapped using a 10-mm, 0-degree lens equipped with near-infrared fluorescence capabilities (Firefly mode). Intracorporeal ultrasonography served as an essential adjunct to verify the depth of invasion and confirm resection margins (Fig. 3). The ultrasonography was systematically utilized in all cases across both cohorts. However, its primary operational role differed depending on the group. In the standard RAPN cohort, IOUS was the primary modality used to scan the renal surface and blindly search for the endophytic tumor. In contrast, for the ICG cohort, initial macroscopic tumor localization was rapidly achieved via real-time fluorescence; the ultrasonography was subsequently applied directly over the illuminated target strictly to confirm tumor depth and deep margins.

Prior to tumor excision, global ischemia was induced via a Bulldog clamp applied to the main renal artery. Post-resection, meticulous hemostasis and a two-layer renorrhaphy were performed. A 5-mm closed wound vacuum (CWV) drain was left in situ and subsequently removed once daily drainage output fell below 50 mL.

Regarding transfusion practices during the study period, our institution utilized a highly preemptive intraoperative protocol. Rather than relying on a strict postoperative hemoglobin threshold (e.g., Hb < 7.0 g/dL), decisions for intraoperative blood transfusions were driven dynamically by the anesthesiologist based on real-time assessments of acute surgical bleeding and hemodynamic stability. All transfusions recorded in this study were administered intraoperatively.

Statistical methodology

Clinical parameters and perioperative results were summarized utilizing descriptive statistics. Given the small sample size and the skewed distribution of clinical outcomes, non-parametric tests were employed. Continuous variables are reported as medians with interquartile ranges (IQR) and were compared between groups using the Mann-Whitney U test. Categorical variables are reported as frequencies with percentages and were compared using Fisher’s exact test. A two-sided p-value of < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. All analytical procedures were executed using IBM SPSS Statistics, version 27.

Results

Study cohort and baseline demographics

The final analytical cohort consisted of 32 individuals harboring small renal masses, defined strictly as lesions under 4 cm in maximal dimension. These participants were stratified into an intervention arm (comprising 9 subjects who underwent ICG-guided superselective arterial embolization prior to surgery) and a conventional control arm (incorporating 23 subjects proceeding directly to standard RAPN). The baseline clinical and demographic profiles for both groups are detailed in Table 1. Variables including patient age, gender distribution, body mass index, Charlson Comorbidity Index, and initial renal functional indices (hemoglobin, serum creatinine, and estimated glomerular filtration rate) exhibited no statistically significant variances between the two cohorts (all p-values > 0.05). Although the aggregate R.E.N.A.L. nephrometry scores did not differ substantially, the anatomical depth of the lesions presented a striking contrast. Specifically, the entire ICG-treated cohort possessed completely endophytic masses (E3 component: 100%), compared to a mere 13% in the conventional RAPN group (p < 0.001). Due to the temporal sequence of technique adoption, the median surveillance period was significantly briefer for the ICG arm (18.9 months) relative to the control arm (35.2 months, p < 0.001).

Perioperative surgical metrics

Table 2 delineates the perioperative outcomes. Strikingly, even with a markedly higher prevalence of deeply embedded tumors, patients receiving preoperative ICG embolization benefited from a significantly abbreviated total operative duration (median 90 [IQR 70–95] vs. 102 [IQR 74.5–117.5] minutes; p = 0.049). Concurrently, intraoperative hemorrhage was substantially mitigated in the intervention cohort, yielding a significantly lower median estimated blood loss (100 ml, IQR 10–200) compared to the standard approach (350 ml, IQR 150–725, p = 0.039). This superior hemostatic control directly correlated with a drastically reduced perioperative blood transfusion requirement, observed in only 11.1% of the ICG arm versus 60.9% of the control arm (p = 0.018). Conversely, parameters such as average tumor diameter, the magnitude of hemoglobin drop, and warm ischemia time (WIT) remained statistically equivalent across both surgical strategies.

Functional, oncological, and safety outcomes

In terms of renal preservation, fluctuations in eGFR trajectories demonstrated no substantial disparities between the cohorts at postoperative day one, the six-month mark, or the one-year follow-up interval. Oncological efficacy was universally maintained, with negative surgical margins secured in all 32 procedures. Pathological malignancy rates and intermediate-term recurrence events also showed no significant variations. Similarly, hospitalization duration and the incidence of high-grade surgical complications were comparable. Specifically, only a single major adverse event (classified as Clavien-Dindo grade 3 or higher) was recorded throughout the entire study. This isolated incident occurred in a patient from the conventional RAPN group (Group B) who experienced delayed incisional wound dehiscence following hospital discharge. The complication was successfully managed conservatively with targeted wet-to-dry local wound care, culminating in complete resolution and favorable patient recovery.

Discussion

Indocyanine green (ICG) is increasingly utilized in fluorescence-guided surgery across various oncological disciplines. In urology, the hypervascular nature of renal cell carcinoma—driven by the dysregulation of the VHL–HIF–VEGF pathway—provides an ideal substrate for vascular-based fluorescence [13]. While ICG is not tumor-specific, its intravascular distribution allows visualization of tissue perfusion, which may be leveraged in tumors with distinct vascular patterns [14]. Unlike standard intravenous ICG, which washes out within 30 min, our transarterial approach provides durable fluorescence for hours. This prolonged retention relies on a dual mechanism: the highly viscous ICG-lipiodol emulsion causes microvascular entrapment, while fibered coils occlude arterial inflow to prevent hemodynamic washout. The concept of preoperative superselective ICG-lipiodol embolization was first described by Simone et al. in 2018 for totally endophytic masses [12]. Recognizing the pioneering work of prior literature, the current study is strictly positioned as a small preliminary retroperitoneal feasibility experience. While early reports primarily established feasibility and facilitated off-clamp RAPN via a transperitoneal approach, our study provides a distinct perspective through three methodological modifications. First, the incremental value of our report lies primarily in demonstrating the technical viability and safety of this adjunct specifically within the confined working space of the retroperitoneal approach. Second, rather than pursuing an off-clamp strategy, we standardized a main renal artery on-clamp protocol to evaluate the synergistic local hemostatic and fluorescent navigational utility of the technique. Finally, we included a standard RAPN cohort strictly limited to small tumors (< 4 cm). However, rather than aiming to establish definitive evidence of clinical superiority or quantify comparative advantages, this cohort serves to provide essential contextual background for interpreting these exploratory signals.

In the present study, the application of superselective transarterial ICG delivery was associated with shorter median operative time (90.0 vs. 102.0 min, p = 0.049). This efficiency stems from expedited localization: while the standard cohort required time-consuming ultrasound sweeping to locate endophytic masses, ICG provided immediate visual demarcation, reserving ultrasound solely to confirm deep margins. Consequently, the reduced median estimated blood loss (100 vs. 350 mL, p = 0.039) and the requirement for perioperative blood transfusion was lower in the ICG group (11% vs. 61%, p = 0.018). The observed reduction in blood loss in the ICG cohort is multifactorial. While superselective coil embolization inherently provides a profound direct hemostatic effect via local devascularization, the augmented ICG fluorescence plays a crucial synergistic role. For small and completely endophytic tumors lacking surface cues, real-time fluorescent demarcation allows for precise, targeted dissection. This effectively prevents the need for extensive, exploratory nephrolysis, thereby minimizing unnecessary collateral tissue trauma and associated diffuse bleeding.

Regarding the relatively high intraoperative transfusion rate (60.9%) in the standard RAPN group, our review confirmed no baseline differences in preoperative anemia or anticoagulation status. This primarily reflects our institution’s historically conservative practice of administering blood products preemptively during active bleeding to maintain hemodynamic stability, rather than relying on a delayed, restrictive hemoglobin threshold. While this early intervention explains the similar postoperative hemoglobin drop observed between the cohorts—artificially mitigating the decline in the standard group—it inherently introduces measurement bias. Consequently, direct comparisons of transfusion rates between these non-concurrent cohorts should be interpreted with caution. Nevertheless, it is noteworthy that the ICG cohort remarkably reduced the need for such intraoperative transfusions (11.1%), suggesting that direct mechanical devascularization and visually guided dissection effectively establish a stable, nearly bloodless surgical field. These findings suggest that ICG serves as a synergistic navigational adjunct, enhancing intraoperative orientation and more efficient identification of hypervascular tumors, particularly in technically demanding cases. Importantly, WIT and postoperative renal function were comparable between groups, suggesting that the observed surgical benefits did not come at the expense of renal preservation.

Historically, the primary utility of ICG in partial nephrectomy has centered on guiding selective arterial clamping. As a result, comparative studies evaluating ICG-guided superselective clamping (SSC) against standard clamping dominate the current literature. Previous meta-analyses and retrospective cohorts have largely favored ICG-guided SSC, highlighting significant reductions in WIT and superior preservation of short-term renal function [15, 16]. Nevertheless, the perioperative superiority of ICG remains a subject of debate. For instance, a comprehensive single-institution retrospective review (2014–2019) reported that while ICG integration mitigated chronic kidney disease upstaging, it failed to yield significant improvements in other perioperative metrics [17]. Furthermore, beyond the traditional clamp vs. SSC debate, an emerging subset of literature is now shifting focus toward ICG-guided off-clamp (zero-ischemia) techniques to ascertain their distinct clinical benefits [18].

While intraoperative SSC is clinically valuable, dissecting sub-segmental branches for completely endophytic tumors poses exceptional technical challenges and risks unintended parenchymal injury. Therefore, we utilized preoperative superselective ICG embolization as a tailored alternative for these deeply embedded lesions. To minimize ischemic confounding factors and evaluate the synergistic navigational and hemostatic utilities of this approach, we standardized the main artery clamping protocol in all patients. Under this constant ischemia strategy, we found that ICG integration still yielded significant reductions in total operative time and estimated blood loss. The magnitude of these perioperative improvements is particularly striking when considering the anatomical complexity of the lesions: the ICG cohort consisted entirely of completely endophytic tumors (E3 component: 100% vs. 13%, p < 0.001). Conventionally, these deeply embedded lesions lack external visual cues, demanding prolonged dissection and carrying a substantially higher risk of hemorrhage, longer WIT and higher positive surgical margin rate [19]. Crucially, the absence of significant differences in WIT and postoperative eGFR between the cohorts reinforces that these perioperative advantages stem entirely from improved intraoperative visual orientation rather than hemodynamic shifts.

A critical consideration for integrating interventional procedures prior to RAPN is the potential for additive morbidity. While transarterial embolization (TAE) is typically well tolerated—occasionally presenting as a mild post-infarction syndrome involving nausea, pain, and fever—historical data highlight significant risks [20]. For instance, Lammer et al. reported that TAE could lead to erroneous infarction of the bowel, contralateral kidney, or spine, citing a serious morbidity of 9.9% and a mortality of 3.3% in a 121-case series [21]. Utilizing contemporary superselective microcatheter techniques, we observed absolutely zero embolization-specific complications, with no instances of non-target ischemia, necrosis, or infection. This exceptional safety profile was further compounded by our standardized surgical strategy. Consistent with previously published findings—which established the feasibility and safety of the retroperitoneal approach for tumors regardless of their anterior or posterior location [22]—we exclusively utilized retroperitoneal access. By inherently avoiding intra-abdominal mobilization, this approach successfully precluded postoperative ileus and eliminated transperitoneal morbidity across all patients. Ultimately, the overall complication rate was negligible, limited to a single non-ICG patient who developed superficial wound dehiscence attributed to poorly controlled diabetes mellitus, which was readily managed with targeted antibiotic therapy.

Our findings must be interpreted in the context of several limitations. First, the retrospective, non-randomized design introduces substantial selection bias. Allocation to the ICG intervention was primarily based on surgeon recommendation for the most anatomically challenging cases, resulting in a fundamental baseline imbalance (100% versus 13%). As this disparity inherently reflects different surgical indications and case selection, the cohorts are fundamentally non-comparable. Consequently, our between-group comparisons cannot support absolute causal claims regarding operative time, blood loss, transfusion rates, renal function, or oncological outcomes. Concurrently, the shorter follow-up in the ICG cohort reflects its recent temporal adoption. While the surgical team had surpassed the learning curve for standard RAPN, this chronological difference introduces a potential era effect that may confound perioperative comparisons. Given these inherent biases, coupled with only a single recurrence observed overall, definitive oncologic comparisons are precluded. Therefore, our findings should be interpreted cautiously as a descriptive feasibility analysis. Future large-scale comparative studies utilizing propensity score matching and extended follow-up are essential to balance tumor complexity, accurately isolate the ICG intervention effect, and confirm whether immediate hemostatic gains safely translate into sustained oncologic benefits. Second, although we appropriately utilized non-parametric testing to account for the data distribution, the statistically significant differences observed in operative time and estimated blood loss are based on small sample sizes and borderline P values (p = 0.049 and p = 0.039, respectively). As such, these findings must be presented strictly as exploratory signals only, not as robust evidence of improved operative efficiency or hemostasis. Furthermore, the small cohort is likely underpowered to definitively capture subtle long-term disparities in renal function or oncological outcomes. Third, because the ICG cohort exclusively comprised completely endophytic renal tumors, the pronounced navigational benefits observed may not translate equally to superficial or exophytic lesions where visual cues are readily apparent. Fourth, the successful execution of superselective transarterial ICG delivery is highly dependent on the technical proficiency of an experienced interventional radiologist. The intricate nature of catheterizing sub-segmental feeding vessels presents a steep learning curve, which may restrict the widespread generalizability of this approach to high-volume centers equipped with such specialized multidisciplinary expertise. Finally, a formal cost-benefit analysis was beyond the scope of this initial study. While preoperative embolization introduces direct costs for the interventional suite and endovascular materials, we hypothesize that these upfront expenses may be offset by reduced operative times, decreased blood product usage, and avoided hemorrhagic complications. Future health-economic evaluations are essential to determine the real-world cost-effectiveness of this tailored approach.

Conclusion

In conclusion, this study should be strictly interpreted as a preliminary descriptive feasibility report. Our preliminary experience indicates that preoperative superselective ICG-lipiodol embolization is a feasible and safe adjunct for RAPN, particularly in the management of anatomically complex, completely endophytic small renal tumors. Rather than focusing on comparative outcomes, our findings highlight the technique’s practical advantages regarding seamless surgical workflow, exceptional fluorescence durability, and technical reproducibility. The synergistic combination of direct local hemostasis—via embolization—and real-time fluorescent navigational guidance facilitates precise tumor localization and contributes to a stable surgical field. While the standard RAPN cohort provided essential contextual background—revealing potential exploratory signals in operative efficiency and blood loss—fundamental baseline imbalances and small sample sizes preclude any definitive comparative claims. Consequently, this study should be strictly interpreted as a preliminary descriptive feasibility report. Future well-balanced comparative studies are essential to validate these exploratory signals.

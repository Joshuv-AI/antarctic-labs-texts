Two classes of connectivity-related non-Hamiltonian 1-planar perfect graphs
Licheng Zhang, Shengxiang Lv, Yuanqiu Huang

Abstract

The existence of Hamiltonian cycles in 1-planar graphs with higher connectivity has attracted considerable attention. Recently, the authors and Dong proved that 4-connected 1-planar chordal graphs are Hamiltonian-connected. In this paper, we investigate the non-Hamiltonicity of a broader class of graphs, specifically perfect graphs, under the constraint of 1-planarity, with a focus on connectivity of at most 5. We also propose some unsolved problems.

A graph

It is known that bipartite graphs are perfect. The non-Hamiltonian 1-planar perfect graphs with connectivity 5 presented in this paper are bipartite, setting them apart from those in the existing literature, which are neither bipartite nor perfect.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let

be a double-stellating quadrangulation. Then,
Q_{s}
is perfect.

Proof.

Let
S
be the set of all stellating vertices of

. It is clear that
S
forms an independent set in
Q_{s}
. Since

is bipartite, it follows that
Q_{s}
is tripartite, implying
\chi(Q_{s})\leq 3
. Furthermore, since
Q_{s}
contains a triangle, we have
\omega(Q_{s})=3
, thus
\chi(Q_{s})=3
.

Let
H
be an induced subgraph of
Q_{s}
. By the definition of perfect graphs, it suffices to show that
\omega(H)=\chi(H)
.

(1).

If
V(H)=\emptyset
, then
\omega(H)=\chi(H)=0
.

(2).

If
V(H)\neq\emptyset
and
E(H)=\emptyset
, then
\omega(H)=\chi(H)=1
.

(3).

If
V(H)\neq\emptyset
and
E(H)\neq\emptyset
, then
2\leq\omega(H)\leq\chi(H)\leq\chi(Q_{s})=3
.

We can 3-color
H
by first 2-coloring

with red and blue, and then letting

inherit the 2-coloring from
Q
. We color stellating vertices

in
H
in the following order:

(step 1)

if all vertices in
N_{H}(w)
are of a single color (e.g., red), we assign
w
the opposite color (blue).

(step 2)

if
N_{H}(w)
contains both red and blue vertices, we assign
w
a third color.

If all stellating vertices follow the first step,
H
is bipartite, leading to
\omega(H)=\chi(H)=2
.
Otherwise, there exists a stellating vertex
u
with two neighbors
x
and
y
of opposite colors in
H
. These vertices
x
and
y
are consecutive vertices of the face in

that
u
and another stellating vertex have double-stellated. Thus,
u,x,
and
y
induce a triangle in

. In this case,
\omega(H)=\chi(H)=3
.

Therefore,
Q_{s}
is perfect, as desired.
∎

Concluding Remarks

Theorem
1.2
indicates that connectivity of 5 does not guarantee that 1-planar perfect graphs are Hamiltonian. However, the Hamiltonicity of 6-connected and 7-connected 1-planar perfect graphs remains unknown. Thus, we propose the following problem with a stronger condition than that of Problem
1.1
.

Problem 3.1
.

Is every
6
-connected or
7
-connected
1
-planar perfect graph Hamiltonian?

Let
\beta(n)
represent the maximum number of edges in bipartite
1
-planar graphs of given order
n\geq 4
. Karpov
[
9
]
proved that

\beta(n)=\begin{cases}3n-8&\text{ for even }n\neq 6;\\
3n-9&\text{ for odd }n\text{ or }n=6.\end{cases}

This implies that the minimum degree of every
1
-planar bipartite graph is at most 5, resulting in the maximum connectivity of
5
. Thus, if non-Hamiltonian perfect
1
-planar graphs with connectivity
6
or
7
exist, they must be non-bipartite.

Recall that every
1
-planar
graph with
n\geq 3
vertices has at most
4n-8
edges. A simple
1
-planar graph
G
with
n
vertices is called
optimal
if it has exactly
4n-8
edges.
The following theorem is easily derived from existing results.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
G
be an optimal
1
-planar graph with
n
vertices. Then
G
is not chordal.

Proof.

It is known that optimal 1-planar graphs are 4-connected
[
12
]
. Zhang et al.
[
16
]
showed that any
4
-connected chordal
1
-planar graph with
n
vertices is a
4
-tree, having exactly
4n-10
edges. Therefore,
G
cannot be chordal, as desired.
∎

It is intriguing to extend this theorem to the perfection of optimal
1
-planar graphs. Notably,
K_{2,2,2,2}
is the unique optimal
1
-planar graph with
8
vertices, which is perfect by direct verification. However, computer searches reveal no optimal
1
- planar perfect graphs with
10\leq n\leq 20
vertices. We thus propose the following conjecture.

Conjecture 3.1
.

Every optimal
1
-planar graph with at least
10
vertices is not perfect.

Refinement of the Theory and Convergence of the Sinc Convolution Thanks: This work was supported by JSPS Grant-in-Aid for Scientific Research (C) JP23K03218.
Tomoaki Okayama

Abstract
. The Sinc convolution is an approximate formula for indefinite convolutions proposed by Stenger. The formula was derived based on the Sinc indefinite integration formula combined with the single-exponential transformation. Although its efficiency has been confirmed in various fields, several theoretical issues remain unresolved. The first contribution of this study is to resolve those issues by refining the underlying theory of the Sinc convolution. This contribution includes an essential resolution of Stenger’s conjecture. The second contribution of this study is to improve the convergence rate by replacing the single-exponential transformation with the double-exponential transformation. Theoretical analysis and numerical experiments confirm that the modified formula achieves superior convergence compared to Stenger’s original formula.

1.
Introduction

This study is concerned with indefinite convolutions of the form

(1.1)

p(x)=\int_{a}^{x}f(x-t)g(t)\,\mathrm{d}{t},\quad a\leq x\leq b,

and their collocation-type approximations given by

p(x)\approx\sum_{j=1}^{m}p_{j}\omega_{j}(x),

where the collocation points
x_{i}
satisfy
\omega_{j}(x_{i})=\delta_{ij}

(
\delta_{ij}
denotes the Kronecker delta).
Here,
p_{j}
are fixed with respect to
x
,
and
\omega_{j}
are composed of elementary functions.
Such approximations are particularly well-suited for
solving ordinary and partial differential equations whose solution can be
written as convolution-type integral, or
that can be transformed into
convolution-type integral equations
[
19
]
.

Stenger
[
17
]

derived a beautiful approximation formula of such a type
utilizing the method known as “Sinc indefinite integration.”
The derivation of the formula comprises two parts.
The first part is to derive the expression of
p(x)
as

(1.2)

p(x)=\left(F(\mathcal{J})g\right)(x),

where
F(s)=\hat{f}(1/s)
,
\hat{f}
denotes the Laplace transform
of
f
, and
\mathcal{J}
denotes an integral operator defined by

(1.3)

\left(\mathcal{J}g\right)(x)=\int_{a}^{x}g(t)\,\mathrm{d}{t}.

For instance, when
f(x)=x
, its transform yields
F(s)=\hat{f}(1/s)=s^{2}
,
and consequently,
p(x)
can be represented as

(1.4)

\int_{a}^{x}(x-t)g(t)\,\mathrm{d}{t}=\left(\mathcal{J}^{2}g\right)(x).

This expression
\mathcal{J}^{2}g
aligns with the fact
that the left-hand side of (
1.4
) can be written as
a repeated integral of
g
as

\int_{a}^{x}(x-t)g(t)\,\mathrm{d}{t}=\int_{a}^{x}\left(\int_{a}^{t}g(u)\,\mathrm{d}{u}\right)\,\mathrm{d}{t}=\left(\mathcal{J}\left(\mathcal{J}g\right)\right)(x),

and we can interpret (
1.2
) as a general formulation
of an indefinite convolution.
The second part is to approximate
\mathcal{J}
by
\mathcal{J}_{m}^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}}

in the expression (
1.2
) as

p(x)=\left(F(\mathcal{J})g\right)(x)\approx\left(F(\mathcal{J}_{m}^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}})g\right)(x),

where
\mathcal{J}_{m}^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}}
denotes an approximation of
\mathcal{J}

via the Sinc indefinite integration
combined with the single-exponential (SE) transformation
(the definition will be given in (
2.14
)).
Furthermore, he theoretically showed that
the formula may achieve root-exponential convergence:

\OO(\exp(-c\sqrt{m}))
.
Owing to its efficiency, the method has found applications
in diverse fields, such as
the Hilbert transform
[
30
]
,
fractional calculus
[
3
,
4
]
,
Laplace transform inversion
[
21
,
27
]
,
integral equations
[
25
]
,
partial differential equations
[
10
,
20
,
24
,
26
]
, and
Navier–Stokes equations
[
28
]
.

In the theory of the Sinc convolution, however,
there still remain two points to be resolved.
The first point is the assumption on the function
F
.
In Stenger’s derivation of the expression (
1.2
),

F
is assumed to be analytic on the open right half of
the complex plane
\Omega^{+}=\{z\in\mathbb{R}:\operatorname{Re}z>0\}
.
This assumption is not satisfied in the case of several classical examples,
such as
f(x)=\E^{x}

(F(s)=s/(1-s))
.
Furthermore, the restriction of
F
implies that
the spectrum of the operator
\mathcal{J}_{m}^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}}
, denoted by
\sigma(\mathcal{J}_{m}^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}})
,
must lie on
\Omega^{+}
to ensure the well-definedness of
F(\mathcal{J}_{m}^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}})
.
This spectral inclusion,
\sigma(\mathcal{J}_{m}^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}})\subset\Omega^{+}
, is known as
Stenger’s conjecture
[
18
]
,
which has remained open since the inception of the Sinc convolution.
Han and Xu
[
7
]

made substantial progress on the conjecture,
but as remarked at the end of their paper,
the conjecture was not fully proved.
Even if proved,
the case
F(s)=s/(1-s)
remains problematic due to
the singular point on
\Omega^{+}
.

The second point is the assumption on the function

(1.5)

P(v,x)=\int_{a}^{x}f(v+x-t)g(t)\,\mathrm{d}{t},\quad a\leq x\leq b,

where
v\in[0,b-a]
.
In Stenger’s error analysis,

P(v,x)
is assumed to belong to a function space
that is characterized by parameters
\alpha
,
\beta
and
d

(details will be explained in Assumption
4.4
).
The values of these parameters are required for implementation;
however, their explicit determination is challenging
because
P(v,x)
is not a given function.

The first objective of this study is to resolve the two points outlined above.
Regarding the first point, instead of the earlier assumption on
F
,
this study assumes that
there exists a sufficiently large circle
centered at the origin
encompassing all singularities of
\hat{f}
.
This assumption permits singularities on
\Omega^{+}
.
Under the assumption, the expression (
1.2
) is derived.
Furthermore, this study shows that for all sufficiently large
m
,

\sigma(\mathcal{J}_{m}^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}})
is located in some domain on which
F
is analytic,
i.e.,
F(\mathcal{J}_{m}^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}})
is well-defined for all sufficiently large
m
.
This finding provides an alternative resolution to Stenger’s conjecture:
it is not necessary to establish

\sigma(\mathcal{J}_{m}^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}})\subset\Omega^{+}

to ensure the well-definedness of
F(\mathcal{J}_{m}^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}})
.
Regarding the second point, this study provides an error analysis
yielding the convergence rate
\OO(\exp(-c\sqrt{m}))

without making an assumption on the function
P(v,x)
.
This finding is of great practical importance because
it eliminates the need for prior knowledge of
P(v,x)
,
which is unavailable or difficult to ascertain in applied contexts.

The second objective of this study is to enhance the convergence rate
of the Sinc convolution.
Recently,
by replacing the SE transformation
with the double-exponential (DE) transformation,
an improved Sinc indefinite integration has been
developed
[
15
]
.
Based on the result,
if we appropriately define
\mathcal{J}_{m}^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm DE}}}
as an approximation of
\mathcal{J}
,
we naturally derive the improved approximation formula as

p(x)=\left(F(\mathcal{J})g\right)(x)\approx\left(F(\mathcal{J}_{m}^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm DE}}})g\right)(x).

This study also theoretically shows that
the enhanced formula may attain substantially accelerated convergence:

\OO(\exp(-\tilde{c}m/\log m))
.

).
Section

and
\mathcal{J}_{m}^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm DE}}}
.
Section
6

analyzes the properties of the matrices
arising in the Sinc convolution,
thereby addressing the first point (well-definedness of
F(\mathcal{J}_{m}^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}})
).
Section
7
provides the proofs
of the presented convergence theorems,
thereby addressing the second point (assumption on
P(v,x)
).
Section
8
provides numerical illustrations.
Finally,
Section
9
concludes this paper.

2.
Sinc approximation and Sinc indefinite integration

The Sinc approximation is a function approximation formula
expressed as

(2.1)

f(u)\approx\sum_{j=-M}^{N}f(jh)\sinc\left(\frac{u-jh}{h}\right),\quad u\in\mathbb{R},

where
\sinc(x)
denotes the normalized sinc function.
As explained later,
h
,
M
and
N
are suitably selected
depending on a positive integer
n
.
Furthermore, throughout this paper,
m
is set as
m=M+N+1
.

Integrating both sides of (
2.1
),
we derive an approximate formula for the indefinite integral
over the real axis as

(2.2)

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\tau}f(u)\,\mathrm{d}{u}

\displaystyle\approx\sum_{j=-M}^{N}f(jh)\int_{-\infty}^{\tau}\sinc\left(\frac{u-jh}{h}\right)\,\mathrm{d}{u}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=-M}^{N}f(jh)J(j,h)(\tau),\quad\tau\in\mathbb{R},

where
J(j,h)(x)
is defined by

(2.3)

J(j,h)(x)=h\left\{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{\uppi}\Si\left(\frac{\uppi(x-jh)}{h}\right)\right\},

and
\Si(x)
denotes the sine integral
defined by
\Si(x)=\int_{0}^{x}\{(\sin t)/t\}\,\mathrm{d}{t}
.
The approximation formula (
2.2
)
is referred to as the “Sinc indefinite integration.”

2.1.
Original and mat-vec friendly SE-Sinc indefinite integration

For the indefinite integral
over the finite interval
(a,b)
,
Haber
[
6
]
combined the formula (
2.2
)
with the tanh transformation

t=\psi^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}}(u)=\frac{b-a}{2}\tanh\left(\frac{u}{2}\right)+\frac{b+a}{2},

which is also referred to as the single-exponential (SE) transformation.
More precisely, the formula was derived as

(2.4)

\displaystyle\int_{a}^{x}g(t)\,\mathrm{d}{t}

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\phi^{\text{\tiny{\rm SE}}}(x)}g(\psi^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}}(u))(\psi^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}})^{\prime}(u)\,\mathrm{d}{u}

\displaystyle\approx\sum_{j=-M}^{N}g(\psi^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}}(jh))(\psi^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}})^{\prime}(jh)J(j,h)(\phi^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}}(x)),\quad x\in[a,b],

where
\phi^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}}
denotes the inverse function of
\psi^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}}
.
The formula (
2.4
)
is referred to as the original SE-Sinc indefinite integration
in the present paper.
If we define an approximate operator
\mathcal{\tilde{J}}_{m}^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}}
as

(2.5)

\left(\mathcal{\tilde{J}}_{m}^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}}g\right)(x)=\sum_{j=-M}^{N}g(\psi^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}}(jh))(\psi^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}})^{\prime}(jh)J(j,h)(\phi^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}}(x)),

the approximation (
2.4
) can be compactly expressed as

\mathcal{J}g\approx\mathcal{\tilde{J}}_{m}^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}}g
(recall (
1.3
)).
The approximation is highly efficient if

g
is analytic on an eye-shaped domain

\psi^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}}(\mathscr{D}_{d})=\left\{z=\psi^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}}(\zeta):\zeta\in\mathscr{D}_{d}\right\},

where
\mathscr{D}_{d}
is a strip complex domain defined by

\mathscr{D}_{d}=\{\zeta\in\mathbb{C}:\left|\operatorname{Im}\zeta\right|<d\},

for a positive constant
d
.
In fact, the following convergence theorem
was given.

Theorem 2.1

(Okayama et al.
[
13
, Theorem 2.9]
)
.

Assume that
g
is analytic on
\psi^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}}(\mathscr{D}_{d})
for
d\in(0,\uppi)
,
and that there exist positive constants

K
,
\alpha
and
\beta

such that

(2.6)

|g(z)|\leq K|z-a|^{\alpha-1}|b-z|^{\beta-1}

holds for all
z\in\psi^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}}(\mathscr{D}_{d})
.
Let
\mu=\min\{\alpha,\beta\}
,

n
be a positive integer, and
h
be
selected by the formula

(2.7)

h=\sqrt{\frac{\uppi d}{\mu n}}.

Moreover, let
M
and
N
be positive integers defined by

(2.8)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} M&=n,&N&=\left\lceil\frac{\alpha}{\beta}n\right\rceil&&(\text{if}\,\,\,\mu=\alpha)\\
N&=n,&M&=\left\lceil\frac{\beta}{\alpha}n\right\rceil&&(\text{if}\,\,\,\mu=\beta)\end{aligned}\right.

respectively.
Then, there exists a constant
C
independent of
n
such that

\displaystyle\max_{x\in[a,b]}\left|\left(\mathcal{J}g\right)(x)-\left(\mathcal{\tilde{J}}_{m}^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}}g\right)(x)\right|\leq C\exp\left(-\sqrt{\uppi d\mu n}\right).

However,
the approximation formula (
2.4
)
entails a computational limitation
due to the involvement of the special function
\Si(x)

in the basis function
J(j,h)
.
because the basis function
J(j,h)
includes a special function
\Si(x)
.
To circumvent this issue,
Stenger
[
16
,
17
]

derived another approximation formula
whose the basis functions comprise
the normalized sinc function augmented with
two auxiliary functions:

\eta(x)=\frac{x-a}{b-a},\quad\tilde{\eta}(x)=\frac{b-x}{b-a}.

The explicit representation of the basis functions is given by

\displaystyle\omega^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}}_{-M}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\tilde{\eta}(\psi^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}}(-Mh))}\left\{\tilde{\eta}(x)-\sum_{k=-M+1}^{N}\tilde{\eta}(\psi^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}}(kh))\sinc\left(\frac{\phi^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}}(x)-kh}{h}\right)\right\},

\displaystyle\omega^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}}_{j}(x)

\displaystyle=\sinc\left(\frac{\phi^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}}(x)-jh}{h}\right)\quad(j=-M+1,\,-M+2,\,\ldots,\,N-2,\,N-1),

\displaystyle\omega^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}}_{N}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\eta(\psi^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}}(Nh))}\left\{\eta(x)-\sum_{k=-M}^{N-1}\eta(\psi^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}}(kh))\sinc\left(\frac{\phi^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}}(x)-kh}{h}\right)\right\}.

Note that
\omega^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}}_{j}(\psi^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}}(ih))=\delta_{ij}
(
i,j=-M,\,\ldots,\,N
) holds,
where
\delta_{ij}
denotes the Kronecker delta.
Consequently, Stenger’s formula takes the form

(2.9)

\int_{a}^{x}g(t)\,\mathrm{d}t\approx\sum_{i=-M}^{N}\left(h\sum_{j=-M}^{N}\delta_{i-j}^{(-1)}g(\psi^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}}(jh))(\psi^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}})^{\prime}(jh)\right)\omega^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}}_{i}(x),\quad x\in[a,b],

where
\delta_{k}^{(-1)}=(1/2)+\sigma_{k}
, and

\sigma_{k}=\int_{0}^{k}\sinc t\,\mathrm{d}{t}
.
For reference, tabulated values of
\sigma_{k}
are
provided
[
16
, Table 1.10.1]
.

The approximation formula (
2.9
)
admits a representation in matrix-vector form,
requiring the following notations.
Let
\boldsymbol{\omega}^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}}_{m}

be a row vector of order
m
(recall
m=M+N+1
) defined by

(2.10)

\boldsymbol{\omega}^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}}_{m}(x)=\left[\omega^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}}_{-M}(x),\,\omega^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}}_{-M+1}(x),\,\ldots,\,\omega^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}}_{N-1}(x),\,\omega^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}}_{N}(x)\right],

and let
\mathcal{V}^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}}_{m}
be a sampling operator
that maps a function
f
to a column vector of order
m

as

(2.11)

\mathcal{V}^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}}_{m}f=\left[f(\psi^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}}(-Mh)),\,f(\psi^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}}(-(M-1)h)),\,\ldots,\,f(\psi^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}}(Nh))\right]^{\mathrm{T}}.

Note that

\mathcal{V}^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}}_{m}\boldsymbol{\omega}^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}}_{m}=I_{m}
holds,
where
I_{m}
denotes the identity matrix of order
m
.
Let
I^{(-1)}_{m}
be a square matrix of order
m
having

\delta^{(-1)}_{i-j}
as its
(i,j)
th element, and

\displaystyle D^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}}_{m}

\displaystyle=\diag\left[(\psi^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}})^{\prime}(-Mh),\,(\psi^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}})^{\prime}(-(M-1)h),\,\ldots,\,(\psi^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}})^{\prime}(Nh)\right],

(2.12)

\displaystyle A^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}}_{m}

\displaystyle=hI_{m}^{(-1)}D^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}}_{m}.

Then, the approximation (
2.9
) can be expressed as

(2.13)

\int_{a}^{x}g(t)\,\mathrm{d}{t}\approx\boldsymbol{\omega}^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}}_{m}(x)A^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}}_{m}\mathcal{V}^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}}_{m}g,\quad x\in[a,b].

This mat-vec friendly approximation formula
is referred to as the SE-Sinc indefinite integration
in the present paper
(note that the formula (
2.4
)
is referred to as the
original
SE-Sinc indefinite integration).
If we define an approximate operator
\mathcal{J}_{m}^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}}
as

(2.14)

\left(\mathcal{J}_{m}^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}}g\right)(x)=\boldsymbol{\omega}^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}}_{m}(x)A^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}}_{m}\mathcal{V}^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}}_{m}g,

the approximation (
2.13
) can be compactly expressed as

.
Its convergence was analyzed as follows.

Theorem 2.2

(Stenger
[
17
, Theorem 4.9]
)

Assume that
g
is analytic on
\psi^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}}(\mathscr{D}_{d})
for
d\in(0,\uppi)
,
and that there exist positive constants

K
,
\alpha
with
\alpha\leq 1
and
\beta
with
\beta\leq 1

such that (
2.6
)
holds for all
z\in\psi^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}}(\mathscr{D}_{d})
.
Let
\mu=\min\{\alpha,\beta\}
,

n
be a positive integer, and

be
selected by the formula (

).
Moreover, let

and

be positive integers defined by (

).

2.2.
Original and mat-vec friendly DE-Sinc indefinite integration

In the original SE-Sinc indefinite integration (
2.4
),
the SE transformation
x=\psi^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}}(u)
is employed to
map
\mathbb{R}
onto
(a,b)
.
Not only the SE transformation but also
the double-exponential (DE) transformation

x=\psi^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm DE}}}(u)=\frac{b-a}{2}\tanh\left(\frac{\uppi}{2}\sinh u\right)+\frac{b+a}{2}

maps
\mathbb{R}
onto
(a,b)
.
Notably, replacing the SE with the DE transformation

approximation
[

,
12
,
29
]
.
This observation motivated

[
11
]
to derive
another approximation formula as

(2.15)

\displaystyle\int_{a}^{x}g(t)\,\mathrm{d}{t}

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\phi^{\text{\tiny{\rm DE}}}(x)}g(\psi^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm DE}}}(u))(\psi^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm DE}}})^{\prime}(u)\,\mathrm{d}{u}

\displaystyle\approx\sum_{j=-M}^{N}g(\psi^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm DE}}}(jh))(\psi^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm DE}}})^{\prime}(jh)J(j,h)(\phi^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm DE}}}(x)),\quad x\in[a,b],

where
\phi^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm DE}}}
denotes the inverse function of
\psi^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm DE}}}
.
The formula (
2.15
)
is referred to as the original DE-Sinc indefinite integration
in the present paper.
If we define an approximate operator
\mathcal{\tilde{J}}_{m}^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm DE}}}
as

(2.16)

\left(\mathcal{\tilde{J}}_{m}^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm DE}}}g\right)(x)=\sum_{j=-M}^{N}g(\psi^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm DE}}}(jh))(\psi^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm DE}}})^{\prime}(jh)J(j,h)(\phi^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm DE}}}(x)),

the approximation (
2.15
) can be compactly expressed as

\mathcal{J}g\approx\mathcal{\tilde{J}}_{m}^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm DE}}}g
.

replacing
\psi^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}}

)

(Okayama et al.
[
13

Assume that
g
is analytic on
\psi^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm DE}}}(\mathscr{D}_{d})

Furthermore, let

3.1.
Existence of the resolvent of

Let

By the Cauchy formula for repeated integration, it follows

With the standard norm

is a Banach space.
For the space
\mathbf{Z}
,
the resolvent of
\mathcal{J}

exists for all

,
as stated below.

Proposition 3.1
.

For all
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
,

(z-\mathcal{J})^{-1}:\mathbf{Z}\to\mathbf{Z}
exists.

Proof.

We show that the Neumann series

\frac{1}{z}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\mathcal{J}^{k}}{z^{k}}=(z-\mathcal{J})^{-1}

converges for all
z\in\mathbb{C}
excluding the point
z=0
.
Applying (
3.1
), we have

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{J}^{k}g\right\|_{\mathbf{Z}}

\displaystyle=\int_{a}^{b}\left|\int_{a}^{x}\frac{(x-t)^{k-1}}{(k-1)!}g(t)\,\mathrm{d}{t}\right|\,\mathrm{d}{x}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{a}^{b}\left\{\int_{a}^{x}\frac{(x-t)^{k-1}}{(k-1)!}|g(t)|\,\mathrm{d}{t}\right\}\,\mathrm{d}{x}

\displaystyle=\int_{a}^{b}\left\{\int_{t}^{b}\frac{(x-t)^{k-1}}{(k-1)!}\,\mathrm{d}{x}\right\}|g(t)|\,\mathrm{d}{t}

\displaystyle=\int_{a}^{b}\frac{(b-t)^{k}}{k!}|g(t)|\,\mathrm{d}{t}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(b-a)^{k}}{k!}\int_{a}^{b}|g(t)|\,\mathrm{d}{t}

\displaystyle=\frac{(b-a)^{k}}{k!}\|g\|_{\mathbf{Z}},

from which

\|\mathcal{J}^{k}\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{Z},\mathbf{Z})}\leq(b-a)^{k}/k!

follows.
This inequality implies that for
z\neq 0

(3.2)

\left\|\frac{1}{z}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\mathcal{J}^{k}}{z^{k}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{Z},\mathbf{Z})}\leq\frac{1}{|z|}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left\|\frac{\mathcal{J}^{k}}{z^{k}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{Z},\mathbf{Z})}\leq\frac{1}{|z|}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(b-a)^{k}}{|z|^{k}k!}=\frac{1}{|z|}\E^{(b-a)/|z|}<\infty,

which shows the claim.
∎

The argument here can be reproduced even if

is replaced with

.
This result is important for the error analysis,
which is given by the uniform norm on

Proposition 3.2
.

For all

(z-\mathcal{J})^{-1}:\mathbf{W}\to\mathbf{W}

Proof.

Note that
\mathcal{J}
maps
\mathbf{W}
onto

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{J}^{k}g\right\|_{\mathbf{W}}

\displaystyle=\max_{a\leq x\leq b}\left|\int_{a}^{x}\frac{(x-t)^{k-1}}{(k-1)!}g(t)\,\mathrm{d}{t}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\max_{a\leq x\leq b}\left\{\int_{a}^{x}\frac{(x-t)^{k-1}}{(k-1)!}|g(t)|\,\mathrm{d}{t}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\|g\|_{\mathbf{W}}\max_{a\leq x\leq b}\left\{\int_{a}^{x}\frac{(x-t)^{k-1}}{(k-1)!}\,\mathrm{d}{t}\right\}

Then, the claim follows

\tilde{\boldsymbol{c}}_{m}
as

\tilde{\boldsymbol{c}}_{m}=\mathcal{V}^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}}_{m}\tilde{u}
,
then
\tilde{\boldsymbol{c}}_{m}
is a solution of (
6.2
).
Because the solution of (
6.2
) is unique,

\boldsymbol{c}_{m}=\tilde{\boldsymbol{c}}_{m}
holds.
Consequently,
\tilde{u}(\psi^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}}(jh))=c_{j}
holds.
Therefore, the equation
(z-\mathcal{J}_{m}^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}})\tilde{u}=v
can be rewritten as

(6.5)

\tilde{u}(x)=\frac{1}{z}\left\{v(x)+\sum_{k=-M}^{N}\left(h\sum_{j=-M}^{N}\delta^{(-1)}_{k-j}{c}_{j}(\psi^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}})^{\prime}(jh)\right)\omega^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}}_{k}(x)\right\}.

Comparing the right-hand sides of (
6.4
)
and (
6.5
),
we conclude
u=\tilde{u}
, which shows the desired uniqueness.
∎

Using this lemma, we prove Lemma
4.2
as follows.

Proof of Lemma
4.2
.

Let
r
be a positive constant such that
F
has no singular point
inside
\varGamma_{r}
.
Lemma
5.1
shows that
there exists a sufficiently large positive integer
n_{r}
such that
for all
n\geq n_{r}
,

(z-\mathcal{J}_{m}^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}})^{-1}:C([a,b])\to C([a,b])
exists
for all
|z|\geq r
.
According to Lemma
6.1
,

(z-A^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}}_{m})^{-1}:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}

exists if and only if

(z-\mathcal{J}_{m}^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}})^{-1}:C([a,b])\to C([a,b])
exists.
Therefore, for all
n\geq n_{r}
,

(z-A^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}}_{m})^{-1}
exists for all
|z|\geq r
.
Thus, we can consider the Dunford integral along
\varGamma_{r}
as

\frac{1}{2\uppi\I}\oint_{\varGamma_{r}}(z-A^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}}_{m})^{-1}F(z)\,\mathrm{d}{z}=F(A^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}}_{m}),

which shows the existence of
F(A^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}}_{m})
.
This completes the proof.
∎

In the preceding proof, the Dunford integral is used
for showing the well-definedness of
F(A^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}}_{m})
,
but we can show it more directly from the definition of matrix functions.
To this end, let us review the definition of matrix functions.

Definition 6.2

(cf. Higham
[
8
, Definition 1.2]
)
.

Let
f
be defined on the spectrum of
A\in\mathbb{C}^{m\times m}

and let
A
have the Jordan canonical form

\displaystyle Z^{-1}AZ

\displaystyle=J=\diag[J_{1},\,J_{2},\,\ldots,\,J_{q}],

\displaystyle J_{k}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}\lambda_{k}&1&&\\
&\lambda_{k}&\ddots&\\
&&\ddots&1\\
&&&\lambda_{k}\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{C}^{m_{k}\times m_{k}},

where
Z
is nonsingular and
m_{1}+m_{2}+\cdots+m_{q}=m
.
Then,

f(A):=Zf(J)Z^{-1}=Z\diag(f(J_{k}))Z^{-1},

where

f(J_{k}):=\begin{bmatrix}f(\lambda_{k})&f^{\prime}(\lambda_{k})&\cdots&\dfrac{f^{(m_{k}-1)}(\lambda_{k})}{(m_{k}-1)!}\\
&f(\lambda_{k})&\ddots&\vdots\\
&&\ddots&f^{\prime}(\lambda_{k})\\
&&&f(\lambda_{k})\end{bmatrix}.

Recall that
F
is analytic, and accordingly infinitely differentiable,
inside
\varGamma_{r}
.
According to Definition
6.2
,
if the spectrum
\sigma(A^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}}_{m})
is located inside
\varGamma_{r}
,
then
F(A^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}}_{m})
is well-defined,
because
F^{(m_{k}-1)}(\lambda_{k})
exists for any
k
.
In the same way of the proof of Lemma
4.2
provided above,
we can show that
there exists a sufficiently large positive integer
n_{r}
such that
for all
n\geq n_{r}
,

(z-A^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}}_{m})^{-1}
exists for all
|z|\geq r
.
This implies that for all
n\geq n_{r}
,

\sigma(A^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}}_{m})
is located inside
\varGamma_{r}
.
Thus,
F(A^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}}_{m})
is well-defined for all
n\geq n_{r}
.
This completes another proof of the well-definedness of
F(A^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}}_{m})
.

We find further information on the spectrum of
A^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}}_{m}
.
Recall that
\hat{f}(s)
has no singular point outside
\varGamma_{R}

(assumption of Lemma
3.7
),
and accordingly
F(s)=\hat{f}(1/s)
is analytic inside
\varGamma_{r}
,
where
r=1/R
.
Here, note that we can choose
r>0
as small as we want.
Lemma
4.2
shows that
even for arbitrary small
r
,
there exists a sufficiently large positive integer
n_{r}
such that
for all
n\geq n_{r}
,
the spectrum
\sigma(A^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}}_{m})
lies inside
\varGamma_{r}
.
Thus, we arrive at the following result.

Corollary 6.3
.

Let
d
be a positive constant with
d<\uppi
,
let
n
be a positive integer, and let
h
be
selected by the formula (
4.2
).
Moreover, let
M=N=n
,
and let
A^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}}_{m}
be defined by (
2.12
).
Let an arbitrary small positive value
\epsilon
be given.
Then, there exists a positive integer
n_{\epsilon}
such that
for all
n\geq n_{\epsilon}
,
\sigma(A^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}}_{m})
lies inside

\varGamma_{\epsilon}
.

This result shows that
\sigma(A^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}}_{m})
contracts toward the origin
as
n\to\infty
.
Because
\sigma(\mathcal{J}_{m}^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}})=\sigma(A^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}}_{m})
,
this is also true for
\sigma(\mathcal{J}_{m}^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}})
,
i.e.,
\sigma(\mathcal{J}_{m}^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}})\to\{0\}
as

.
This behavior is consistent with Proposition
3.2
,
which says
\sigma(\mathcal{J})=\{0\}
.

7.1.
Proof of convergence in the case of the SE transformation

Proof of Theorem
4.6
.

Let
r
be a positive constant
such that
F
has no singular point inside
\varGamma_{r}
.
According to
Proposition
3.2

and
Lemma
5.1
,
there exists a positive integer
n_{r}
such that
for all
n\geq n_{r}
,

\sigma(\mathcal{J})

and
\sigma(\mathcal{J}_{m}^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}})
locate inside
\varGamma_{r}
.
Then, using the Dunford integral, we have

\displaystyle F(\mathcal{J})g-F(\mathcal{J}_{m}^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}})g

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\uppi\I}\oint_{\varGamma_{r}}F(z)\left\{(z-\mathcal{J})^{-1}-(z-\mathcal{J}_{m}^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}})^{-1}\right\}\,\mathrm{d}{z}\,g

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\uppi\I}\oint_{\varGamma_{r}}F(z)(z-\mathcal{J}_{m}^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}})^{-1}(\mathcal{J}-\mathcal{J}_{m}^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}})(z-\mathcal{J})^{-1}\,\mathrm{d}{z}\,g,

where the second resolvent identity is used at the second equality.
Taking the uniform norm over
[a,b]
, we have

\displaystyle\|F(\mathcal{J})g-F(\mathcal{J}_{m}^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}})g\|_{\mathbf{W}}

\displaystyle\leq\max_{z\in\varGamma_{r}}|F(z)|\cdot\|(z-\mathcal{J}_{m}^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}})^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{W},\mathbf{W})}\|(\mathcal{J}-\mathcal{J}_{m}^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}})(z-\mathcal{J})^{-1}g\|_{\mathbf{W}}\cdot\frac{1}{2\uppi}\oint_{\varGamma_{r}}|\,\mathrm{d}{z}|

\displaystyle=\max_{z\in\varGamma_{r}}|F(z)|\cdot\|(z-\mathcal{J}_{m}^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}})^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{W},\mathbf{W})}\|(\mathcal{J}-\mathcal{J}_{m}^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}})\tilde{g}\|_{\mathbf{W}}\cdot r,

where
\tilde{g}=(z-\mathcal{J})^{-1}g
.
From Proposition
3.5
,

\tilde{g}\in\mathbf{H}^{\infty}(\psi^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}}(\mathscr{D}_{d}))
.
Therefore, by Theorem
2.2
with
\alpha=\beta=1

(alternatively, by Theorem
5.3
),
we have

\|(\mathcal{J}-\mathcal{J}_{m}^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}})\tilde{g}\|_{\mathbf{W}}\leq C\sqrt{n}\exp\left(-\sqrt{\uppi dn}\right)

with a certain constant
C
.
Finally, using (
5.1
),
we obtain the conclusion.
∎

9.
Concluding remarks

For the indefinite convolution (
1.1
),
Stenger
[
17
]

derived an excellent approximation formula (
4.1
).
He also gave the convergence analysis (Theorem
4.5
),
which claims that his formula may attain root-exponential convergence:

\OO(\exp(-c\sqrt{m}))
.
However, the convergence theorem
relies on two nontrivial assumptions.
The first one is Assumption
4.1
.
In this assumption,
\hat{f}
is assumed to be analytic on
\Omega^{+}
,
which is not satisfied in several standard examples,
such as
f(x)=\E^{x}

(\hat{f}(s)=1/(s-1))
.
Furthermore, it is assumed that
A^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}}_{m}
is diagonalizable,
and
\sigma(A^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}}_{m})\subseteq\Omega^{+}
.
The latter assumption (
\sigma(A^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}}_{m})\subseteq\Omega^{+}
)
is known as Stenger’s conjecture,
which has not strictly been proved thus far.
This assumption is made to ensure that
F(A^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}}_{m})
is well-defined.
The second one is Assumption
4.4
.
In this assumption,
P(v,x)
is assumed to belong to

\mathbf{M}_{\alpha,\beta}(\psi^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}}(\mathscr{D}_{d+\epsilon}))
,
and the parameters
\alpha
,
\beta
and
d
are used
for implementation to select
h
by (
2.7
)
and
M
and
N
by (
2.8
).
However, those parameters are not easy to obtain
because
P(v,x)
is not explicitly known.

The first contribution of this paper is to eliminate
the two assumptions. Instead of Assumption
4.1
,
this study makes Assumption
3.9
,
which is more amenable to practical applications.
Under Assumption
3.9
, the well-definedness of
F(A^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}}_{m})

is established (Lemma
4.2
).
This result reveals that Stenger’s conjecture is not required
to prove the well-definedness of
F(A^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}}_{m})
.
On a relevant note, this study reveals that

\sigma(A^{\text{\scriptsize{\rm SE}}}_{m})\to\{0\}
as
m\to\infty
(Corollary
6.3
).
Furthermore, eliminating Assumption
4.4
,
this study establishes Theorem
4.6
.
This elimination is fairly useful for a practical use.

It should be noted that the results of this paper
rely on the finiteness of the interval
[a,b]
,
whereas Stenger’s framework accommodates potentially infinite intervals.
Extension to infinite intervals is one of future subjects.
Furthermore, as for the convergence theorem,
this paper (Theorem
4.6
) assumes that
g
is bounded,
whereas
g
may have integrable singularity
in Stenger’s error analysis (Theorem
4.5
).
Extension to the unbounded case is also one of future subjects.

The second contribution of this paper is to improve
the convergence rate of Stenger’s formula. The idea
is to replace the SE transformation with the DE
transformation, which results in the formula (
4.5
).
Furthemore, this study provides similar theoretical
results to the case of the SE transformation;
Lemma
4.7

and Theorem
4.8
are established.
As a result, it is shown that
the improved formula may attain nearly exponential
convergence:
\OO(\exp(-\tilde{c}m/\log m))
,
which is significantly higher than root-exponential
convergence.
In fact, such an improvement can be observed
in the numerical results of Examples
8.1
–
8.6
.

Numerical results of Examples
8.7
–
8.9

offer preliminary insights into the behavior of
the Sinc convolution in contexts beyond the scope of
Theorems
4.6
and
4.8
.
Notably, effective convergence is still observed in
Examples
8.7
and
8.8
,
suggesting the potential robustness of the method.
A rigorous theoretical framework to explain these
phenomena remains a subject for future work.

As a final remark, Stenger’s beautiful expression of
the indefinite convolution (
1.2
) is valid
regardless of the approximation method for the integral
operator
\mathcal{J}
.
If we apply another method instead of the SE/DE-Sinc
indefinite integration, then we obtain another approximation
formula for (
1.2
).
In fact, for some approximation formulas,
Stenger’s conjecture
was formulated
[
22
,
23
]
,
and significant progress on the conjecture
has been made
[
1
,
5
]
.
The idea of refinement of the Sinc convolution presented in this study
may also be applied to those formulas.

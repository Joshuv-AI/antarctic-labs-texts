Redundancy of Probabilistic Frames and Approximately Dual Probabilistic Frames
Dongwei Chen, Emily J. King, Clayton Shonkwiler

Abstract
. This paper studies the redundancy of probabilistic frames and introduces approximately dual probabilistic frames. The redundancy of a probabilistic frame is defined as the dimension of the kernel of the associated synthesis operator, which turns out to be the semi-Fredholm index of the synthesis operator. For a given non-redundant probabilistic frame, we claim that the canonical dual probabilistic frame is the only dual frame of pushforward type. Furthermore, we show that probabilistic frames with finite redundancy are atomic. In the second part, we introduce approximately dual probabilistic frames and characterize all the approximate duals of pushforward type. We also study the approximately dual frames of perturbed probabilistic frames, and show that if a probability measure is close to a probabilistic dual pair in some sense, then this probability measure is an approximately dual probabilistic frame. In particular, this yields discrete approximate duals of any probabilistic frame.

1.
Introduction

A sequence of vectors
\{{\bf f}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
in a separable Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
is called a
frame
for
\mathcal{H}
if there exist
frame bounds

0<A\leq B<\infty
such that for any
{\bf f}\in\mathcal{H}
,

(1.1)

A\|{\bf f}\|^{2}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}|\left\langle{\bf f},{\bf f}_{i}\right\rangle|^{2}\leq B\|{\bf f}\|^{2}.

Furthermore,
\{{\bf f}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
is called a
tight frame
if
A=B
and a
Parseval frame
if
A=B=1
.

Frames were first introduced by Duffin and Schaeffer to analyze perturbations of Fourier series
[
13
]
, and have since been applied in many areas, such as the Kadison–Singer problem
[
6
]
, time-frequency analysis
[
19
]
, wavelet analysis
[
12
]
, coding theory
[
29
]
, sampling theory
[
16
]
, image processing
[
22
]
, compressed sensing
[
25
]
, and the design of filter banks
[
17
]
.
Frames have been studied using tools from many mathematical areas, including operator theory
[
10
]
, combinatorics
[
4
]
, matroid theory
[
3
]
, and algebraic geometry
[
5
]
. In this paper, we study (probabilistic) frames from the perspective of probability theory and optimal transport
[
14
,
15
,
30
,
31
]
.
For readers interested in general frame theory, see
[
9
]
.

Frames are often used to analyze signals and corresponding
dual frames
to reconstruct signals. A sequence
\{{\bf g}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
in
\mathcal{H}
is called a
dual frame
of the frame
\{{\bf f}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
if

(1.2)

{\bf f}=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left\langle{\bf f},{\bf g}_{i}\right\rangle{\bf f}_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left\langle{\bf f},{\bf f}_{i}\right\rangle{\bf g}_{i},\enskip\text{for any ${\bf f}\in\mathcal{H}$}.

In this case,
\{{\bf g}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
and
\{{\bf f}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
is said to be a
dual pair
. If the frame is not a Reisz basis, then there are infinitely many dual frames and thus infinitely many frame expansions for any
{\bf f}
. One may think of this richness of frame expansions as being a measure of redundancy.

A particular choice of dual frame for
\{{\bf f}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
is the
canonical dual frame

\{{\bf S}^{-1}{\bf f}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
where
{\bf S}
is the
frame operator
:

{\bf S}:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{H},\ \ \ {\bf S}({\bf f})=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left\langle{\bf f},{\bf f}_{i}\right\rangle{\bf f}_{i},\ \text{for any}\ {\bf f}\in\mathcal{H}.

Indeed,
{\bf S}:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
is a bounded, positive, and invertible operator. If
{\bf S}=A{\bf Id}
for some
A>0
, then the canonical dual is simply a scaling of the original frame, allowing for easy reconstruction. Such a frame is
tight
and when
A=1
it is called
Parseval
.

Suppose
\{{\bf y}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
is a finite frame for the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
where
N\geq n
. Letting
\mu_{f}:=\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{{\bf y}_{i}}
be the uniform distribution on
\{{\bf y}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
, the frame definition in (
1.1
) is equivalent to

\frac{A}{N}\|{\bf x}\|^{2}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\left\langle{\bf x},{\bf y}\right\rangle|^{2}d\mu_{f}({\bf y})\leq\frac{B}{N}\|{\bf x}\|^{2},\ \text{for any}\ {\bf x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

which allows one to interpret finite frames as discrete probabilistic measures.
More generally, a probability measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is called a
probabilistic frame
for
\mathbb{R}^{n}
if there exist
0<A\leq B<\infty
such that for any
{\bf x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

A\|{\bf x}\|^{2}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\left\langle{\bf x},{\bf y}\right\rangle|^{2}d\mu({\bf y})\leq B\|{\bf x}\|^{2}.

Furthermore,
\mu
is called a
tight probabilistic frame
if
A=B
and
Parseval
if
A=B=1
.

Probabilistic frames have finite second moments, which puts optimal transport and the
2
-Wasserstein distance in the realm of tools that can be used study probabilistic frames. This is of interest even if one is only interested in finite frames, since
2
-Wasserstein distance can be used to quantify the distance between any two probabilistic frames, including atomic probabilistic frames coming from finite frames of different cardinalities.

Our goal here is to study the redundancy of probabilistic frames and to introduce the notion of approximately dual probabilistic frames. This paper is divided into four parts.

Section

2
gives some mathematical preliminaries on probabilistic frames, optimal transport, and reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces (RKHS).

Section

3
concerns the redundancy of probabilistic frames. The definition is motivated by Riesz bases, which are precisely the frames that yield unique frame expansions and, equivalently, are precisely the frames that have a so-called synthesis operator with trivial kernel. In
[
2
,
28
]
, this motivates the definition of
redundancy
as the dimension of the kernel of the synthesis operator. We extend this approach to probabilistic frames using the appropriate generalization of the synthesis operator.

Typically, the redundancy of a probabilistic frame is infinite. We will show in
Corollary

3.9
that if a probabilistic frame has finite redundancy, it must be atomic. The key to our proof is to show that the range of the analysis operator is an RKHS.

Given a probabilistic frame
\mu
, a measure
\nu
with finite second moment is a
dual probabilistic frame
for
\mu
if there exists a transport coupling
\gamma\in\Gamma(\mu,\nu)
such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\bf x}{\bf y}^{t}d\gamma({\bf x},{\bf y})={\bf Id},

where
{\bf y}^{t}
is the transpose of the vector
{\bf y}
. We show in
Corollary

3.11
that if a probabilistic frame has zero redundancy, the canonical dual is the only dual probabilistic frame of pushforward type, generalizing a well-known result for frames in Hilbert spaces.

Characterizing all the dual probabilistic frames for a given probabilistic frame is important for signal reconstruction. This remains an open question, made more challenging by the fact that the set of dual probabilistic frames is not closed
[
8
]
. Therefore, in
Section

4
, we introduce approximately dual probabilistic frames, which can still yield perfect signal reconstruction: a measure
\nu
with finite second moment is an
approximately dual probabilistic frame
for the probabilistic frame
\mu
if there exists
\gamma\in\Gamma(\mu,\nu)
such that

\left\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\bf x}{\bf y}^{t}d\gamma({\bf x},{\bf y})-{\bf Id}\right\|<1.

We show that approximate duals can still yield perfect reconstruction.
We also characterize all the approximately dual probabilistic frames that are pushforwards of the given probabilistic frame.

In
Section

5
, we study the approximately dual frames of perturbed probabilistic frames. We claim that if a probability measure is close to some probabilistic dual pair in an appropriate sense, then this probability measure is an approximately dual probabilistic frame. As a consequence, we show in
Theorem

5.6
that every probabilistic frame has a
finite, discrete
approximately dual probabalistic frame.

Finally, in
Section

6
we consider a more general notion of dual frames, known as
pseudo-dual probabilistic frames
.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section we give some necessary background on probabilistic frames, optimal transport, and reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces. Throughout the paper, we use
{\bf A}
to denote a real matrix and
A
a real number. Similarly,
{\bf x}
is used to denote a vector in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
while
x
is a point in the real line. In particular,
{\bf 0}
is used to denote the zero vector in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
while
0
is the number zero. We use
{\bf Id}
for the
n\times n
identity matrix,
{\bf 0}_{n\times n}
the
n\times n
zero matrix, and
{\bf A}^{t}
for the transpose of a matrix
{\bf A}
.

2.1.
Probabilistic Frames

Let
\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be the set of Borel probability measures on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subset\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
the set of Borel probability measures with finite second moments:

\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\left\{\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):M_{2}(\mu)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\|{\bf x}\|^{2}d\mu({\bf x})<+\infty\right\}.

Let
B_{r}({\bf x})
be the open ball centered at
{\bf x}
with radius
r>0
. The
support
of
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is defined by

\supp(\mu)=\{{\bf x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\text{for any}\ r>0,\,\mu(B_{r}({\bf x}))>0\}.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a Borel measurable map, then
f_{\#}\mu
is called the
pushforward
of
\mu
by the map
f
, and is defined as

f_{\#}\mu(E):=(\mu\circ f^{-1})(E)=\mu\big(f^{-1}(E)\big)\ \text{for any Borel set}\ E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}.

If
f
is linear and represented by a matrix
{\bf A}
with respect to some basis, then
{\bf A}_{\#}\mu
is used to denote
f_{\#}\mu
.
Inspired by the insight that finite frames induce discrete probability measures, Martin Ehler and Kasso Okoudjou
[
14
,
15
]
proposed the concept of probabilistic frame as defined below.

Definition 2.1
.

\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is called a
probabilistic frame
if there exist
0<A\leq B<\infty
such that for any
{\bf x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

A\|{\bf x}\|^{2}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\left\langle{\bf x},{\bf y}\right\rangle|^{2}d\mu({\bf y})\leq B\|{\bf x}\|^{2}.

A probabilistic frame
\mu
is called
tight
if
A=B
and
Parseval
if
A=B=1
. Moreover,
\mu
is a
Bessel probability measure
if the upper bound (but possibly not the lower) holds.

Clearly if
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
\mu
is Bessel with bound
M_{2}(\mu)
. One can also define the
frame operator

{\bf S}_{\mu}
for a probabilistic frame
\mu
as the matrix

{\bf S}_{\mu}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\bf y}{\bf y}^{t}d\mu({\bf y}),

and probabilistic frames can be characterized by frame operators.

Proposition 2.2

(
[
15
, Theorem 12.1]
,
[
23
, Proposition 3.1]
)
.

Let
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

(1)

\mu
is a probabilistic frame if and only if the frame operator
{\bf S}_{\mu}
is positive definite and if and only if
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\spa\{\supp(\mu)\}=\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

(2)

\mu
is a tight probabilistic frame with bound
A>0
if and only if
{\bf S}_{\mu}=A\ {\bf Id}
.

2.2.
Optimal Transport and Dual Probabilistic Frames

Since probabilistic frames are contained in
\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we can use the
2
-Wasserstein metric
W_{2}(\mu,\nu)
from optimal transport to quantify the distance between two probabilistic frames
\mu
and
\nu
:

W_{2}^{2}(\mu,\nu):=\underset{\gamma\in\Gamma(\mu,\nu)}{\operatorname{inf}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\|{\bf x}-{\bf y}\right\|^{2}\ d\gamma({\bf x},{\bf y}).

Here
\Gamma(\mu,\nu)
is the set of transport couplings with marginals
\mu
and
\nu
, given by

\Gamma(\mu,\nu):=\{\gamma\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}):{\pi_{{x}}}_{\#}\gamma=\mu,\ {\pi_{{y}}}_{\#}\gamma=\nu\},

where
\pi_{{x}}
,
\pi_{{y}}
are the projections on the
{\bf x}
and
{\bf y}
coordinates, that is to say, for any
({\bf x},{\bf y})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\pi_{{x}}({\bf x},{\bf y})={\bf x},\pi_{{y}}({\bf x},{\bf y})={\bf y}
. See
[
18
]
for more details on Wasserstein distance and optimal transport.

Like finite frames, one can reconstruct signals by
dual probabilistic frames
. Dual probabilistic frames are also known as
transport duals
, and it has been shown that every dual probabilistic frame is a probabilistic frame
[
31
]
.

Definition 2.3
.

Suppose
\mu
is a probabilistic frame.
\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a
dual probabilistic frame
of
\mu
if there exists
\gamma\in\Gamma(\mu,\nu)
such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\bf x}{\bf y}^{t}d\gamma({\bf x},{\bf y})={\bf Id}.

One of the dual probabilistic frames of a frame
\mu
is the
canonical dual (probabilistic) frame

{{\bf S}_{\mu}^{-1}}_{\#}\mu
(with bounds
\frac{1}{B}
and
\frac{1}{A}
) with coupling
\gamma:={({\bf Id}\times{\bf S}_{\mu}^{-1})}_{\#}\mu\in\Gamma(\mu,{{\bf S}_{\mu}^{-1}}_{\#}\mu)
.
Then, we have the following reconstruction: for any
{\bf f}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

{\bf f}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\langle{\bf f},{\bf S}_{\mu}^{-1}{\bf x}\right\rangle{\bf x}\ d\mu({\bf x})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\langle{\bf S}_{\mu}^{-1}{\bf f},{\bf x}\right\rangle{\bf x}\ d\mu({\bf x}).

We also need the following gluing lemma, a standard tool in optimal transport, to “glue” two transport couplings together. Similarly to how
\pi_{x}
and
\pi_{y}
were defined, let
\pi_{xy}
and
\pi_{yz}
be the projections on the corresponding coordinates, i.e., for any
({\bf x},{\bf y},{\bf z})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\pi_{x}({\bf x},{\bf y},{\bf z})={\bf x},\ \pi_{y}({\bf x},{\bf y},{\bf z})={\bf y},\ \pi_{xy}({\bf x},{\bf y},{\bf z})=({\bf x},{\bf y}),\ \pi_{yz}({\bf x},{\bf y},{\bf z})=({\bf y},{\bf z}).

Lemma 2.4

(Gluing Lemma
[
18
, p. 59]
)
.

Let
\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\mu_{3}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Suppose
\gamma^{12}\in\Gamma(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})
and
\gamma^{23}\in\Gamma(\mu_{2},\mu_{3})
such that
{\pi_{y}}_{\#}\gamma^{12}=\mu_{2}={\pi_{y}}_{\#}\gamma^{23}
. Then there exists
\gamma^{123}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
{\pi_{xy}}_{\#}\gamma^{123}=\gamma^{12}
and
{\pi_{yz}}_{\#}\gamma^{123}=\gamma^{23}
.

2.3.
Reproducing Kernel Hilbert Spaces

Let
X
be a set,
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{C}
, and
\mathscr{F}(X,\mathbb{F})
the set of functions from
X
to
\mathbb{F}
, which can be seen as a vector space over
\mathbb{F}
with pointwise addition and scalar multiplication. A vector subspace
\mathscr{H}\subset\mathscr{F}(X,\mathbb{F})
is called a
reproducing kernel Hilbert space (RKHS)
on
X
if

•

\mathscr{H}
is endowed with a Hilbert space structure
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
, and

•

for every
x\in X
, the linear evaluation functional
E_{x}:\mathscr{H}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}
, defined by
E_{x}(f)=f(x)
, is bounded.

If
\mathscr{H}
is an RKHS on
X
, then the Riesz representation theorem shows that for each
x\in X
, there exists a unique vector
k_{x}\in\mathscr{H}
such that for any
f\in\mathscr{H}
,

f(x)=E_{x}(f)=\langle f,k_{x}\rangle.

The function
k_{x}
is called the
reproducing kernel at the point
x
, and the function
K:X\times X\rightarrow\mathbb{F}
defined by
K(y,x)=k_{x}(y)
is called the
reproducing kernel
for
\mathscr{H}
.
Indeed,
K
is a
kernel function
, meaning that for any
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and any
n
distinct points
\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}\subset X
, the matrix
(K(x_{i},x_{j}))_{i,j}
is positive semidefinite.
It is well-known that there is a one-to-one correspondence between RKHSs and kernel functions on
X
: By Moore’s theorem
[
24
]
, if
K:X\times X\rightarrow\mathbb{F}
is a kernel function, then there exists a unique RKHS
\mathscr{H}
on
X
such that
K
is the reproducing kernel of
\mathscr{H}
.
We let
\mathscr{H}(K)
denote the unique RKHS with the reproducing kernel
K
.
We refer to
[
26
]
for more details on RKHSs and their applications.

3.
Redundancy of Probabilistic Frames

Every frame has at least one dual frame, the canonical dual, and thus every vector in the underlying Hilbert space has at least one expansion in the form of (
1.2
). This expansion is unique if and only if the frame is additionally a so-called Riesz basis. We can characterize the space of expansions by considering the
synthesis operator

U^{\ast}
of a frame,

U^{\ast}:\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N})\rightarrow\mathcal{H},\quad U^{\ast}\left((\omega_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\omega_{i}{\bf f}_{i},

which by the definition of a frame is bounded with bound
\sqrt{B}
for
B
the upper frame bound. Further (see, e.g.,
[
20
]
), a frame is a Riesz basis if and only if the synthesis operator is injective and if and only if removing any single element results in a collection which is no longer a frame. Inspired by this, the authors in
[
2
]
defined the
redundancy

R(F)
of a frame
F=\{{\bf f}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
in a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
as its excess, which is the greatest number of elements that can be removed yet still leave a frame. They further showed that the excess of a frame is the dimension of the kernel of the associated synthesis operator, that is to say,

R(F)=\sup\{|G|:G\subset F\ \text{and}\ F\backslash G\ \text{is a frame}\}=\Dim(\kernel(U^{*}))=\Dim((\range U)^{\perp}),

where
|G|
is the cardinality of the set
G
and
U^{*}
is the synthesis operator of
F
. Furthermore, it has been shown that given a frame, all of the associated dual frames, approximately dual frames, and pseudo-dual frames have the same redundancy
[
1
]
.

Under this definition, any Riesz basis has zero excess or redundancy. The authors in
[
28
]

then generalized this to define the redundancy of
continuous frames
in a similar way. For a fixed measure space
(X,\mu)
and Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, a measurable map
\Psi:X\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
is called a
continuous frame
for
\mathcal{H}
if there exist
0<A\leq B
such that for any
{\bf y}\in\mathcal{H}
,

A\|{\bf y}\|^{2}\leq\int_{X}|\left\langle{\bf y},\Psi_{x}\right\rangle|^{2}d\mu(x)\leq B\|{\bf y}\|^{2}.

The associated
analysis operator
is given by

U:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow L_{2}(X,\mu)
,
[U{\bf y}](x)=\left\langle{\bf y},\Psi_{x}\right\rangle
, and the synthesis operator is

U^{\ast}:L_{2}(X,\mu)\rightarrow\mathcal{H},\quad U^{\ast}(\omega)=\int_{X}\omega(x)\Psi_{x}d\mu(x),

where the integral is defined weakly, and
U
and
U^{\ast}
are indeed adjoints of each other. We can interpret a Riesz basis
\{{\bf f}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
as being a continuous frame with respect to counting measure on
X=\mathbb{N}
, where
\Dim(\kernel(U^{*}))=0
.
This justifies the definition of redundancy for the continuous frame
\Psi
:

R(\Psi):=\Dim(\kernel U^{\ast})=\Dim((\range U)^{\perp}).

Note that the redundancy of a continuous frame need not be finite. The authors in
[
28
]
also showed that if the redundancy of a continuous frame is finite, the associated measure space
(X,\mu)
must be atomic.

In this section, we quantify the redundancy of probabilistic frames in the same way. Given a probabilistic frame
\mu
, the associated analysis operator
U:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow L^{2}(\mu,\mathbb{R}^{n})
and the synthesis operator
U^{*}:L^{2}(\mu,\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
are given by

U{\bf f}=\langle{\bf f},\cdot\rangle\quad\textrm{and}\quad U^{*}\omega=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\bf x}\omega({\bf x})d\mu({\bf x}),

with
U
and
U^{\ast}
being adjoints and
\kernel U^{*}=(\range U)^{\perp}
. Thus, we give the following definition of redundancy.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\mu
be a probabilistic frame. The
redundancy
of
\mu
is defined as the dimension of kernel of its synthesis operator, which is given by

R_{\mu}:=\Dim(\kernel U^{*})=\Dim((\range U)^{\perp}).

This definition of redundancy also works for Bessel probability measures, since the associated analysis and synthesis operators are well-defined. The following example shows that when
\mu
is finitely and uniformly supported, the redundancy of
\mu
is the excess of the associated finite frame.

Example 3.2
.

Let
\mu=\delta_{1}
be a probabilistic frame for the real line
\mathbb{R}
. Note that
f\in L^{2}(\mu,\mathbb{R})
is really an equivalence class
[f]
, and
g\in[f]
if and only if
f(x)=g(x)
for
\mu
almost all
x\in\mathbb{R}
. Since
\mu=\delta_{1}
, all the functions in
L^{2}(\mu,\mathbb{R})
are totally determined by their function values at
1
. Then,
g\in[f]
if and only if
f(1)=g(1)
. Clearly, the constant function
c=f(1)
is also a representative of the equivalence class
[f]
. Therefore,
L^{2}(\mu,\mathbb{R})\cong\mathbb{R}
.

The synthesis operator
U^{*}:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
of
\mu
is given by

U^{*}\omega=\int_{\mathbb{R}}x\omega d\mu(x)=\omega,\quad\omega\in\mathbb{R}.

Then
\kernel U^{*}=\{\omega\in\mathbb{R}:U^{*}\omega=0\}=\{0\}
. Hence,
R_{\mu}=\Dim(\kernel U^{*})=0
.

More generally, let
\mu=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{x_{i}}
for
\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then
L^{2}(\mu,\mathbb{R}^{n})\cong\ell^{2}(\{1,2,\cdots,N\})
. When
\mu
is uniformly and finitely supported, the synthesis operator of the probabilistic frame
\mu
is the same as the synthesis operator of the finite frame
\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
up to a constant
\frac{1}{N}
. Therefore, the kernels of these two synthesis operators are the same, and thus the redundancy of
\mu
is the excess of the associated finite frame
\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
.

It is not necessarily true that the redundancy is weakly continuous; that is, if
\{\mu_{n}\}
is a sequence of probabilistic frames converging weakly to a probabilistic frame
\mu
, then it does not necessarily hold that
R_{\mu_{n}}\rightarrow R_{\mu}
.
For example, let
\mu=\delta_{1}
and
\mu_{n}=\frac{1}{2}\delta_{1}+\frac{1}{2}\delta_{1-\frac{1}{n+1}}
for each
n
, which are frames for the real line. Then,
R_{\mu_{n}}=1
for each
n
and
R_{\mu}=0
. Note that
\mu_{n}
converges to
\mu=\delta_{1}
weakly, and furthermore,
W_{2}(\mu_{n},\mu)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\frac{1}{n+1}\rightarrow 0
, but
R_{\mu_{n}}
does not converge to
R_{\mu}
. Therefore, if two probabilistic frames are close to each other, their redundancies need not be close to each other.

Balan et al.
[
2
]
showed that given a frame
\{{\bf f}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
with frame operator
\bf S
in a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, the excess of
\{{\bf f}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
is given by

R(\{{\bf f}_{i}\})=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left(1-\langle{\bf f}_{i},{\bf S}^{-1}{\bf f}_{i}\rangle\right).

We then give a similar expression for the redundancy of probabilistic frames.

Lemma 3.3
.

Suppose
\mu
is a probabilistic frame and
\{w_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
an orthonormal basis for
L^{2}(\mu,\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Then the redundancy
R_{\mu}
for
\mu
is given by

R_{\mu}=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left(1-\langle{\bf w}_{i},{\bf S}_{\mu}^{-1}{\bf w}_{i}\rangle\right),

where
{\bf w}_{i}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\bf x}w_{i}({\bf x})d\mu({\bf x})
for each
i
.

Proof.

Let
U:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow L^{2}(\mu,\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
U^{*}:L^{2}(\mu,\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
be the analysis and synthesis operators for
\mu
. If
P
is the orthogonal projection onto
\kernel U^{*}
, then
P=I-U{\bf S}_{\mu}^{-1}U^{*}
. By definition,

\begin{split}R_{\mu}&=\Dim(\kernel U^{*})=\trace(P)=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\langle w_{i},Pw_{i}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mu)}\\
&=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left(1-\langle w_{i},U{\bf S}_{\mu}^{-1}U^{*}w_{i}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mu)}\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left(1-\langle{\bf w}_{i},{\bf S}_{\mu}^{-1}{\bf w}_{i}\rangle\right),\end{split}

where
{\bf w}_{i}=U^{*}w_{i}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\bf x}w_{i}({\bf x})d\mu({\bf x})
for each
i
.
∎

Two frames
\{{{\bf f}_{i}}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
and
\{{{\bf g}_{i}}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
in a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
are said to be equivalent if there exists an invertible bounded linear operator
L
on
\mathcal{H}
such that
{\bf g}_{i}=L{\bf f}_{i}
, for each
i
. Bakić and Berić
[
1
]
showed that if
L
is only bounded, the excess of
\{{L{\bf f}_{i}}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
is not smaller than that of
\{{{\bf f}_{i}}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
, i.e.,
R(\{{{\bf f}_{i}}\}_{i=1}^{\infty})\leq R(\{{L{\bf f}_{i}}\}_{i=1}^{\infty})
, and thus if two frames in
\mathcal{H}
are equivalent, they have the same excess. We show a similar result for equivalent probabilistic frames.

Two probabilistic frames
\mu
and
\nu

equivalent
if there exists an invertible
n\times n
matrix
{\bf A}
such that
\nu={\bf A}_{\#}\mu
. The assumption that
\mathbf{A}
is invertible is essential: if
{\bf A}_{\#}\mu
is a probabilistic frame, then
{\bf A}
must be invertible. To see this, observe that
{\bf A}_{\#}\mu
being a probabilistic frame implies, by
Proposition

2.2
, that
\spa(\supp({\bf A}_{\#}\mu))=\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Since
\supp({\bf A}_{\#}\mu)\subset\range({\bf A})
and
\range({\bf A})
is a subspace, then
\spa(\range({\bf A}))=\mathbb{R}^{n}=\range({\bf A})
. Therefore,
{\bf A}
has full rank and hence is invertible.

Lemma 3.4
.

Suppose
\mu
and
\nu
are equivalent probabilistic frames. Then
\mu
and
\nu
have the same redundancy.

Proof.

Since
\mu
and
\nu
are equivalent, there exists an invertible
n\times n
matrix
{\bf A}
such that
\nu={\bf A}_{\#}\mu
. We claim that
L^{2}(\mu,\mathbb{R}^{n})
is isometrically isomorphic to
L^{2}(\nu,\mathbb{R}^{n})
. We define the isometric isometry
T:L^{2}(\nu,\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow L^{2}(\mu,\mathbb{R}^{n})
as

Tf=f\circ\mathbf{A},\quad f\in L^{2}(\nu,\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Note that
T
is well-defined and preserves norms, since

\|Tf\|_{L^{2}(\mu,\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f(\mathbf{A}{\bf x})|^{2}d\mu({\bf x})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f({\bf y})|^{2}d\nu({\bf y})=\|f\|_{L^{2}(\nu,\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}<\infty.

T
is clearly linear and preserves the inner product: for any
f,g\in L^{2}(\nu,\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

\langle Tf,Tg\rangle_{L^{2}(\mu,\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(\mathbf{Ax})g(\mathbf{Ax})d\mu({\bf x})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f({\bf y})g({\bf y})d\nu({\bf y})=\langle f,g\rangle_{L^{2}(\nu,{\mathbb{R}^{n}})}.

Furthermore,
T
is bijective. If
Tf=0
, then
f(\mathbf{Ax})=0
for
\mu
-a.e.
\bf x
. Since
\mathbf{A}
is invertible, then
f({\bf y})=0
for
\nu
-a.e.
{\bf y}
, so
f=0
in
L^{2}(\nu,\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Hence,
T
is injective. For the surjective part, for any
g\in L^{2}(\mu,\mathbb{R}^{n})
, define
f=g\circ\mathbf{A}^{-1}
. Then
Tf=f\circ\mathbf{A}=g\circ\mathbf{A}^{-1}\circ\mathbf{A}=g
and
f\in L^{2}(\nu,\mathbb{R}^{n})
since
T
is an isometry. Therefore,
T:L^{2}(\nu,\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow L^{2}(\mu,\mathbb{R}^{n})
is an isometric isomorphism.

Now let
U_{\mu}
and
U_{\nu}
be the analysis operators for
\mu
and
\nu
. Since
\mu
is a probabilistic frame, the associated analysis operator
U_{\mu}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow L^{2}(\mu,\mathbb{R}^{n})
is bounded below. Therefore,
U_{\mu}
is injective and its range
\range U_{\mu}
is closed. By the Rank–Nullity theorem,
\Dim(\range U_{\mu})=n
. Similarly, we have
\Dim(\range U_{\nu})=n
. Thus,
\range U_{\mu}
is isomorphic to
\range U_{\nu}
(and isomorphic to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
). Therefore,
\Dim((\range U_{\mu})^{\perp})=\Dim((\range U_{\nu})^{\perp})
, that is to say,
R_{\mu}=R_{\nu}
. Hence,
\mu
and
\nu
have the same redundancy.
∎

Conversely, probabilistic frames can be classified by redundancy. Let
\mathcal{P}_{++}\subset\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be the set of probabilistic frames and define a relation
\sim
on
\mathcal{P}_{++}
as follows:
\mu\sim\nu
if and only if
R_{\mu}=R_{\nu}
. Clearly, the relation
\sim
is an equivalence relation on
\mathcal{P}_{++}
, and thus probabilistic frames can be classified by the value of redundancy. However, if two probabilistic frames have the same redundancy, i.e., they are in the same equivalence class under the relation
\sim
, these two frames are not necessarily equivalent to each other. For example, let
\mu=\frac{1}{2}\delta_{1}+\frac{1}{2}\delta_{2}
and
\nu=\frac{1}{2}\delta_{2}+\frac{1}{2}\delta_{5}
. Then
\mu
and
\nu
are probabilistic frames for the real line and
R_{\mu}=R_{\nu}=1
. However, there is no invertible linear transformation
A
so that
A_{\#}\mu=\nu
, since the matrix for
A
is a
1\times 1
matrix
\begin{bmatrix}a\end{bmatrix}
for some
a\neq 0
and hence
A_{\#}\mu=\frac{1}{2}\delta_{a}+\frac{1}{2}\delta_{2a}
, which cannot equal
\nu=\frac{1}{2}\delta_{2}+\frac{1}{2}\delta_{5}
.

Next, we claim that if the redundancy of a probabilistic frame is finite, then it is the Fredholm index of the synthesis operator. This will follow from the fact that the synthesis operator of a probabilistic frame is surjective.

Lemma 3.5
.

The synthesis operator
U^{*}
of the probabilistic frame
\mu
is surjective, and both
U^{*}
and the analysis operator
U
are compact.

Proof.

For any
{\bf y}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the map
\langle{\bf S}_{\mu}^{-1}{\bf y},\cdot\rangle\in L^{2}(\mu,\mathbb{R}^{n})
and

U^{*}(\langle{\bf S}_{\mu}^{-1}{\bf y},\cdot\rangle)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\bf x}\langle{\bf S}_{\mu}^{-1}{\bf y},{\bf x}\rangle d\mu({\bf x})=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\bf x}{\bf x}^{t}d\mu({\bf x})\right){\bf S}_{\mu}^{-1}{\bf y}={\bf y}.

Therefore, the synthesis operator
U^{*}
is surjective. For compactness,
since the synthesis operator
U^{*}:L^{2}(\mu,\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
has a finite-dimensional range, it is compact, and thus its adjoint operator
U
is also compact.
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
\mu
be a probabilistic frame with finite redundancy
R_{\mu}
. Then the associated synthesis operator
U^{*}
is Fredholm and
R_{\mu}
is the Fredholm index of
U^{*}
.

Proof.

By
Lemma

3.5
, the synthesis operator
U^{*}
is onto and thus
\Dim(\range U^{*})=n
. Since
R_{\mu}=\Dim(\kernel U^{*})
is finite and the synthesis operator
U^{*}
is bounded, we know
U^{*}
is Fredholm and the Fredholm index is given by

\FredholmIndex(U^{*})=\Dim(\kernel U^{*})-\Dim(\mathbb{R}^{n}/\range U^{*})=\Dim(\kernel U^{*})=R_{\mu}.

∎

If the redundancy of the probabilistic frame
\mu
is infinite, i.e.,
R_{\mu}=\Dim(\kernel U^{*})=+\infty
, then one can define the index of the synthesis operator
U^{*}
to be infinity, and in this case, the synthesis operator
U^{*}
is said to be a semi-Fredholm operator, and the redundancy of
\mu
becomes the corresponding semi-Fredholm index.

Next, we show that the range of the analysis operator of a probabilistic frame is an RKHS.

Lemma 3.7
.

If
\mu
is a probabilistic frame, then the range of the analysis operator
U
,
\range U\subset L^{2}(\mu,\mathbb{R}^{n})
, is an RKHS with the underlying set
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and the reproducing kernel
K:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is given by

K({\bf x,y})={\bf x}^{t}{\bf S}_{\mu}^{-1}{\bf y}
for all
{\bf x,y}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Proof.

From the probabilistic frame condition, we know the analysis operator
U
is bounded below and thus has closed range. Therefore,
\range U\subset L^{2}(\mu,\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a Hilbert space with the inner product inherited from
L^{2}(\mu,\mathbb{R}^{n})
. For any
f\in\range U
, there exists an
{\bf x}_{f}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that for any
{\bf y}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

(3.1)

f({\bf y})=\langle{\bf x}_{f},{\bf y}\rangle.

Suppose the lower frame bound for
\mu
is
A>0
. Then for any
{\bf y}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the point evaluation functional
E_{\bf y}
is bounded since

\begin{split}|E_{\bf y}({f})|^{2}=|{f}({\bf y})|^{2}=|\langle{\bf x}_{f},{\bf y}\rangle|^{2}\leq\|{\bf x}_{f}\|^{2}\|{\bf y}\|^{2}&\leq\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\langle{\bf x}_{f},{\bf z}\rangle|^{2}d\mu({\bf z})}{A}\|{\bf y}\|^{2}\\
&=\frac{\|{\bf y}\|^{2}}{A}\|{f}\|_{L^{2}(\mu,\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}.\end{split}

Therefore,
\range U\subset L^{2}(\mu,\mathbb{R}^{n})
is an RKHS with the underlying set
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Now let us find the reproducing kernel
K:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
. Note that for any fixed
{\bf y}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, there exists
k_{\bf y}\in\range U
such that for any
{f}\in\range U
,

{f}({\bf y})=\langle{f},k_{\bf y}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mu,\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Since
k_{\bf y}\in\range U
, there exists
{\bf x}_{\bf y}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
k_{\bf y}({\bf z})=\langle{\bf x}_{\bf y},{\bf z}\rangle
. Then

{f}({\bf y})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f({\bf z})k_{\bf y}({\bf z})d\mu({\bf z})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\langle{\bf x}_{f},{\bf z}\rangle\langle{\bf x}_{\bf y},{\bf z}\rangle d\mu({\bf z})=\langle{\bf x}_{f},{\bf S}_{\mu}{\bf x}_{\bf y}\rangle.

We also know from (
3.1
) that
{f}({\bf y})=\langle{\bf x}_{f},{\bf y}\rangle
.
Since
{\bf x}_{f}
can be any vector in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, this implies that
{\bf y}={\bf S}_{\mu}{\bf x}_{\bf y}
and thus
{\bf x}_{\bf y}={\bf S}_{\mu}^{-1}{\bf y}
. Then,

K({\bf z,y})=k_{\bf y}({\bf z})=\langle{\bf S}_{\mu}^{-1}{\bf y},{\bf z}\rangle
is the desired reproducing kernel.
∎

Based on this RKHS structure and the following proposition, we conclude that the redundancy of a probabilistic frame
\mu
is finite only when
\mu
is atomic.

Proposition 3.8

(Proposition 14 in
[
28
]
)
.

If
(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mu)
is not atomic and
\mathcal{H}_{K}\subset L^{2}(\mu,\mathbb{R}^{n})
is an RKHS, then
\Dim(\mathcal{H}_{K}^{\perp})=\infty
.

Corollary 3.9
.

If the probabilistic frame
\mu
has finite redundancy, then the measure space
(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mu)
is atomic.

Proof.

By
Lemma

3.7
, the range of the analysis operator
\range U\subset L^{2}(\mu,\mathbb{R}^{n})
of
\mu
is an RKHS. Since the redundancy
R_{\mu}=\Dim((\range U)^{\perp})<\infty
,
Proposition

3.8
implies that
(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mu)
is atomic.
∎

Given a probabilistic frame
\mu
, Clare Wickman and Kasso Okoudjou
[
30
]
have shown that for each map
h:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
h_{\#}\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we can define a function
T:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
by

T({\bf x}):={{\bf S}_{\mu}^{-1}}{\bf x}+h({\bf x})-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\langle{\bf S}_{\mu}^{-1}{\bf x},{\bf y}\rangle h({\bf y})d\mu({\bf y})

so that
T_{\#}\mu
is a dual probabilistic frame of
\mu
. We call a probabilistic frame of the form
T_{\#}\mu
a frame of
pushforward type
.

In the following, we give a characterization of dual frames of pushforward type. We also show that if the redundancy of a probabilistic frame is zero, the canonical dual is the only dual frame of pushforward type.

Lemma 3.10
.

Suppose
\mu
is a probabilistic frame and
T_{\#}\mu
is a dual frame of
\mu
where
T:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then
T
must be of the form

T({\bf x})={\bf S}_{\mu}^{-1}{\bf x}+h({\bf x}),

where
h:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
is such that
h_{\#}\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\bf y}h({\bf y})^{t}d\mu({\bf y})={\bf 0}_{n\times n}
.

Proof.

If
T
is of the above type, then
T_{\#}\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Define
\gamma:=({\bf Id},T)_{\#}\mu
. Then
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\bf x}{\bf y}^{t}d\gamma({\bf x,y})={\bf Id}
and thus
T_{\#}\mu
is a dual probabilistic frame of
\mu
.

Conversely, if
T_{\#}\mu
is a dual frame for
\mu
, then
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\bf x}T({\bf x})^{t}d\mu({\bf x})={\bf Id}
. Let
h({\bf x})=T({\bf x})-{{\bf S}_{\mu}^{-1}}{\bf x}
. Then

T({\bf x})={{\bf S}_{\mu}^{-1}}{\bf x}+h({\bf x})

and it is easy to verify that
h_{\#}\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\bf y}h({\bf y})^{t}d\mu({\bf y})={\bf Id-Id}={\bf 0}_{n\times n}.

∎

Corollary 3.11
.

Let
\mu
be a probabilistic frame with zero redundancy and
T_{\#}\mu
a dual probabilistic frame for
\mu
. Then, for any
{\bf x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
T({\bf x})={\bf S}_{\mu}^{-1}{\bf x}.

Proof.

Since
T_{\#}\mu
is a dual frame for
\mu
,
Lemma

3.10
implies that there exists
h:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
h_{\#}\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\bf z}h({\bf z})^{t}d\mu({\bf z})={\bf 0}_{n\times n},

so that
T({\bf x})={\bf S}_{\mu}^{-1}({\bf x})+h({\bf x})
for any
{\bf x}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.

Thus, for any
{\bf y}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\bf z}\langle h({\bf z}),{\bf y}\rangle d\mu({\bf z})={\bf 0}.

This means that
\langle h(\cdot),{\bf y}\rangle\in\kernel U^{*}
for every
{\bf y}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, where
U^{*}:L^{2}(\mu,\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
is the synthesis operator of
\mu
. However,
0=R_{\mu}=\Dim(\kernel U^{*})
, so we know that
\kernel U^{*}=\{0\}
. Hence, the function
\langle h(\cdot),{\bf y}\rangle=0
for all
{\bf y}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, so
h(\cdot)={\bf 0}
. Therefore,
T({\bf x})={\bf S}_{\mu}^{-1}({\bf x})
, as desired.
∎

This generalizes the fact that for frames over separable Hilbert spaces, having zero redundancy and the canonical dual frame being the unique dual frame are equivalent (which are both in turn equivalent to being a Riesz basis). However, this is not necessarily true for probabilistic frames, since there can be dual probabilistic frames that are not the canonical dual and not of pushforward type. For example,
\delta_{1}
is a frame for the real line and the redundancy of
\delta_{1}
is zero. Any probability measure
\mu
with mean value 1 and finite second moment is a dual frame of
\delta_{1}
under the coupling
\gamma=({\bf Id},T)_{\#}\mu\in\Gamma(\mu,\delta_{1})
where
T:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is given by
T(x)=1,x\in\mathbb{R}
. For example, if we define
\mu=\frac{1}{2}\delta_{\frac{1}{2}}+\frac{1}{2}\delta_{\frac{3}{2}}
, then
\mu
is a dual frame of
\delta_{1}
and not the canonical dual frame. Of course, this does not violate
Corollary

3.11
since there is no map
M:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
with
\mu=M_{\#}\delta_{1}=\delta_{M(1)}
.

4.
Approximately Dual Probabilistic Frames

Approximately dual frames were introduced by Ole Christensen and Richard Laugesen in
[
11
]
and further studied in
[
21
]
. It is usually complicated to calculate a dual frame explicitly and people often seek methods to construct approximate duals that still yield perfect reconstruction.

This problem is even more acute for probabilistic frames, since characterizing all the dual probabilistic frames for a given probabilistic frame is still a challenge
[
8
]
. Motivated by this issue, we introduce approximately dual probabilistic frames in this section. In what follows, we interpret
n\times n
matrices
\bf A
as operators and take the operator norm of
\bf A
:

\|{\bf A}\|=\underset{{\bf x}\neq 0}{\text{sup}}\ \frac{\|{\bf Ax}\|}{\|{\bf x}\|}.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\mu
be a probabilistic frame.
\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is an
approximately dual probabilistic frame
of
\mu
if there exists
\gamma\in\Gamma(\mu,\nu)
such that

\Big\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\bf x}{\bf y}^{t}d\gamma({\bf x,y})-{\bf Id}\Big\|<1.

Furthermore,
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\bf x}{\bf y}^{t}d\gamma({\bf x,y})
is called the
mixed frame operator
of
\mu
and
\nu
.

By the symmetry of optimal transport, if
\nu
is an approximately dual probabilistic frame to
\mu
, then
\mu
is also an approximately dual probabilistic frame to
\nu
. We call
\mu
and
\nu
approximately dual pairs, and we will often shorten
approximately dual probabilistic frame
to
approximately dual frame
or
approximate dual
. Recall that given a frame, all the associated dual frames, approximately dual frames, and pseudo-dual frames have the same redundancy
[
1
]
.
However, the redundancies of approximately dual probabilistic frames are not necessarily the same. For example,
\delta_{1}
is a probabilistic frame for the real line, and
\nu=\delta_{\frac{1}{2}}
is an approximate dual of
\delta_{1}
with respect to
\delta_{1}\otimes\nu
. Now define
\eta:=\frac{1}{2}\delta_{\frac{1}{2}}+\frac{1}{2}\delta_{\frac{1}{3}}
. Then,
\eta
is also an approximate dual frame of
\delta_{1}
with respect to
\delta_{1}\otimes\eta
, since

\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}}xzd\delta_{1}(x)d\eta(z)-1\right|=\left|\frac{5}{12}-1\right|=\frac{7}{12}<1.

However,
R_{\nu}=0\neq 1=R_{\eta}
. Similarly, the redundancies of dual probabilistic frames are not necessarily the same. Recall that any probability measure
\mu
with mean value 1 and finite second moment is a dual frame of
\delta_{1}
with respect to the coupling
\gamma=({\bf Id},T)_{\#}\mu\in\Gamma(\mu,\delta_{1})
where
T:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is given by
T(x)=1
. Now define
\mu_{1}:=\frac{1}{2}\delta_{\frac{1}{2}}+\frac{1}{2}\delta_{\frac{3}{2}}
and
\mu_{2}:=\frac{1}{3}\delta_{\frac{1}{2}}+\frac{1}{3}\delta_{1}+\frac{1}{3}\delta_{\frac{3}{2}}
. Note that
\mu_{1}
and
\mu_{2}
are dual probabilistic frames of
\delta_{1}
and
R_{\mu_{1}}\neq R_{\mu_{2}}
, since
R_{\mu_{1}}=1
while
R_{\mu_{2}}=2
.

In
Definition

4.1
we did not require that an approximately dual probabilistic frame actually be a probabilistic frame, though in fact it will be. More than that, there is an uncertainty principle for approximate dual pairs, which was previously studied for probabilistic dual pairs in Corollary 5.4 of
[
8
]
. Furthermore, based on this result, we get the relationship between optimal frame bounds for a probabilistic frame and its approximate duals. The relationship between optimal frame bounds of a frame and its duals in Hilbert spaces has been considered by many authors, for example in
[
27
]
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\mu
be a probabilistic frame with upper frame bound
B>0
. Suppose
\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is an approximate dual of
\mu
with mixed frame operator
\bf A
, then
\nu
is a probabilistic frame with bounds
1/(B\|{\bf A}^{-1}\|^{2})
and
M_{2}(\nu)
.

For any
{\bf f}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we have the following uncertainty principle:

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\langle{\bf A}^{-1}{\bf x},{\bf f}\rangle|^{2}d\mu({\bf x})\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\langle{\bf y},{\bf f}\rangle|^{2}d\nu({\bf y})\geq\|{\bf f}\|^{4}.

Furthermore, if
A_{\mu}
,
B_{\mu}
, and
A_{\nu}
,
B_{\nu}
are optimal frame bounds for
\mu
and
\nu
, then

A_{\nu}\geq\frac{1}{B_{\mu}\|{\bf A}^{-1}\|^{2}}\ \text{and}\ A_{\mu}\geq\frac{1}{B_{\nu}\|{\bf A}^{-1}\|^{2}}.

Proof.

Since
\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is an approximate dual of
\mu
with mixed frame operator
\bf A
, then
\bf A
is invertible since
\left\|{\bf A}-{\bf Id}\right\|<1
, and there exists
\gamma\in\Gamma(\mu,\nu)
such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\bf x}{\bf y}^{t}d\gamma({\bf x,y})={\bf A}.

Then, for any
{\bf f}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\begin{split}\|{\bf f}\|^{4}=|\langle{\bf f},{\bf f}\rangle|^{2}=|\langle{\bf A}^{-1}{\bf A}{\bf f},{\bf f}\rangle|^{2}&=\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\langle{\bf A}^{-1}{\bf x},{\bf f}\rangle\langle{\bf y,f}\rangle d\gamma({\bf x,y})\right|^{2}\\
&\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\langle{\bf A}^{-1}{\bf x},{\bf f}\rangle|^{2}d\mu({\bf x})\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\langle{\bf y},{\bf f}\rangle|^{2}d\nu({\bf y})\\
&\leq B\|({\bf A}^{-1})^{t}{\bf f}\|^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\langle{\bf y},{\bf f}\rangle|^{2}d\nu({\bf y})\\
&\leq B\|{\bf A}^{-1}\|^{2}\|{\bf f}\|^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\langle{\bf y},{\bf f}\rangle|^{2}d\nu({\bf y}),\end{split}

where the first inequality is due to the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality and the second follows from the frame property of
\mu
. Note that we have proved the desired uncertainty principle along the way.

Therefore, for any
{\bf f}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\frac{\|{\bf f}\|^{2}}{B\|{\bf A}^{-1}\|^{2}}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\langle{\bf y},{\bf f}\rangle|^{2}d\nu({\bf y})\leq M_{2}(\nu)\|{\bf f}\|^{2},

which implies that
\nu
is a probabilistic frame with bounds
1/(B\|{\bf A}^{-1}\|^{2})
and
M_{2}(\nu)
.

If
A_{\mu}
,
B_{\mu}
and
A_{\nu}
,
B_{\nu}
are optimal bounds for
\mu
and
\nu
, then the above result gives
A_{\nu}\geq\frac{1}{B_{\mu}\|{\bf A}^{-1}\|^{2}}
, and similarly, for any
{\bf f}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we have

\begin{split}\|{\bf f}\|^{4}=|\langle({\bf A}^{-1})^{t}{\bf A}^{t}{\bf f},{\bf f}\rangle|^{2}&=\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\langle({\bf A}^{-1})^{t}{\bf y},{\bf f}\rangle\langle{\bf x,f}\rangle d\gamma({\bf x,y})\right|^{2}\\
&\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\langle{\bf y},{\bf A}^{-1}{\bf f}\rangle|^{2}d\nu({\bf y})\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\langle{\bf x},{\bf f}\rangle|^{2}d\mu({\bf x})\\
&\leq B_{\nu}\|{\bf A}^{-1}\|^{2}\|{\bf f}\|^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\langle{\bf x},{\bf f}\rangle|^{2}d\mu({\bf x}).\end{split}

Therefore,
\frac{1}{B_{\nu}\|{\bf A}^{-1}\|^{2}}
is a lower frame bound for
\mu
and thus
A_{\mu}\geq\frac{1}{B_{\nu}\|{\bf A}^{-1}\|^{2}}
.
∎

If
\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a dual probabilistic frame of
\mu
, then the mixed frame operator
{\bf A}={\bf Id}
, and thus for any
{\bf f}
in the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, the uncertainty principle is

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\langle{\bf x},{\bf f}\rangle|^{2}d\mu({\bf x})\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\langle{\bf y},{\bf f}\rangle|^{2}d\nu({\bf y})\geq 1.

Furthermore, if
A_{\mu}
,
B_{\mu}
, and
A_{\nu}
,
B_{\nu}
are optimal frame bounds for
\mu
and
\nu
, then

A_{\nu}\geq\frac{1}{B_{\mu}}\ \text{and}\ A_{\mu}\geq\frac{1}{B_{\nu}},

where equality holds if
\nu
is the canonical dual frame of
\mu
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\mu
be a probabilistic frame. The set of approximately dual probabilistic frames of
\mu
is convex.

Proof.

Suppose
\nu_{1}
and
\nu_{2}
are approximately dual probabilistic frames of
\mu
with respect to
\gamma_{1}\in\Gamma(\mu,\nu_{1})
and
\gamma_{2}\in\Gamma(\mu,\nu_{2})
. Let
0\leq w\leq 1
and define
\nu:=w\nu_{1}+(1-w)\nu_{2}
and
\gamma:=w\gamma_{1}+(1-w)\gamma_{2}\in\Gamma(\mu,\nu)
. Then

\begin{split}\Big\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\bf x}{\bf y}^{t}d\gamma({\bf x,y})-{\bf Id}\Big\|&\leq w\Big\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\bf x}{\bf y}^{t}d\gamma_{1}({\bf x,y})-{\bf Id}\Big\|\\
&+(1-w)\Big\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\bf x}{\bf y}^{t}d\gamma_{2}({\bf x,y})-{\bf Id}\Big\|<1.\end{split}

Therefore,
\nu=w\nu_{1}+(1-w)\nu_{2}
is an approximately dual frame of
\mu
.
∎

One might hope that the convexity of the set of approximately dual frames for a given probabilistic frame would allow us to simply find the extreme points and use the Krein–Milman theorem to characterize the set. However, Krein–Milman requires the convex set to additionally be compact, and the set of approximately dual probabilistic frames for a given probabilistic frame is not even weakly closed in
\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. To show this, we return to our favorite example of
\delta_{1}
on the real line. For each
n>0
, define
\nu_{n}=\delta_{\frac{1}{n+1}}
. Then, for any
n>0
,
\nu_{n}
is an approximately dual frame to
\delta_{1}
with respect to the product measure
\delta_{1}\otimes\nu_{n}
, since

\left|\int xyd\delta_{1}(x)d\nu(y)-1\right|=1-\frac{1}{n+1}<1.

Note that
\nu_{n}
converges weakly to
\delta_{0}
. However,
\delta_{0}
is not a probabilistic frame (since
\int_{\mathbb{R}}yyd\delta_{0}(y)=0
) and thus not an approximately dual frame of
\delta_{1}
. Therefore, the set of approximately dual frames for
\delta_{1}
is not weakly closed and thus not weakly compact.

Although it is difficult to characterize all the approximate duals, the following results show that given an approximately dual frame, we can get one dual frame and construct infinitely many approximately dual frames with arbitrary closeness to the perfect reconstruction.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
\mu
be a probabilistic frame. If
\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is an approximate dual of
\mu
and
{\bf A}
is the transpose of its mixed frame operator, then
{{\bf A}^{-1}}_{\#}\nu
is a dual probabilistic frame to
\mu
.

Proof.

Since
\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is an approximately dual frame and
{\bf A}
is the transpose of the mixed frame operator, there exists
\gamma\in\Gamma(\mu,\nu)
such that

{\bf A}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\bf y}{\bf x}^{t}d\gamma({\bf x,y}),

and
\|{\bf A}-{\bf Id}\|<1
. This implies the matrix
{\bf A}
is invertible and so

{\bf Id}={\bf A}^{-1}{\bf A}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\bf A}^{-1}{\bf y}{\bf x}^{t}d\gamma({\bf x,y}).

Now define
\tilde{\gamma}=({\bf Id},{\bf A}^{-1})_{\#}\gamma\in\Gamma(\mu,{{\bf A}^{-1}}_{\#}\nu)
. Then

{\bf Id}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\bf y}{\bf x}^{t}d\tilde{\gamma}({\bf x,y}).

Hence,
{{\bf A}^{-1}}_{\#}\nu
is a dual probabilistic frame to
\mu
with respect to
\tilde{\gamma}\in\Gamma(\mu,{\bf A}^{-1}_{\#}\nu)
.
∎

The following proposition is about the construction of approximately dual frames with arbitrary reconstruction resolution.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
\mu
be a probabilistic frame and let
\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be an approximate dual of
\mu
with respect to the coupling
\gamma\in\Gamma(\mu,\nu)
. Let
{\bf A}
be the transpose of the mixed frame operator. Then

(1)

The dual probabilistic frame
{{\bf A}^{-1}}_{\#}\nu
of
\mu
can be written as

{{\bf A}^{-1}}_{\#}\nu=({\bf Id}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}({\bf Id}-{\bf A})^{k})_{\#}\nu.

(2)

For any fixed
N\in\mathbb{N}
, consider the partial sum

\nu_{N}:=\big(\sum_{k=0}^{N}({\bf Id}-{\bf A})^{k}\big)_{\#}\nu=\big({\bf Id}+\sum_{k=1}^{N}({\bf Id}-{\bf A})^{k}\big)_{\#}\nu.

Then
\nu_{N}
is an approximately dual probabilistic frame to
\mu
with respect to
\tilde{\gamma}_{N}:=\big({\bf Id},{\bf Id}+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{N}({\bf Id}-{\bf A})^{k}\big)_{\#}\gamma\in\Gamma(\mu,\nu_{N})
, and

\Big\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\bf y}{\bf x}^{t}d\tilde{\gamma}_{N}({\bf x,y})-{\bf Id}\Big\|\leq\|{\bf Id}-{\bf A}\|^{N+1}\rightarrow 0\ \text{as $N\rightarrow\infty$}.

Proof.

The inverse of
{\bf A}
can be written as a Neumann series

{{\bf A}^{-1}}=({\bf Id}-({\bf Id}-{\bf A}))^{-1}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}({\bf Id}-{\bf A})^{k}={\bf Id}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}({\bf Id}-{\bf A})^{k}.

Hence, (
1
) follows.

For (
2
), note that
\nu_{N}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\bf y}{\bf x}^{t}d\tilde{\gamma}_{N}({\bf x,y})&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\sum\limits_{k=0}^{N}({\bf Id}-{\bf A})^{k}\right){\bf y}{\bf x}^{t}d\gamma({\bf x,y})\\
&=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{N}({\bf Id}-{\bf A})^{k}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\bf y}{\bf x}^{t}d\gamma({\bf x,y})=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{N}({\bf Id}-{\bf A})^{k}{\bf A}\\
&=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{N}({\bf Id}-{\bf A})^{k}({\bf Id}-({\bf Id}-{\bf A}))={\bf Id}-({\bf Id}-{\bf A})^{N+1}.\end{split}

Since
\|{\bf Id}-{\bf A}\|<1
, we have

\begin{split}\left\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\bf y}{\bf x}^{t}d\tilde{\gamma}_{N}({\bf x,y})-{\bf Id}\right\|&=\left\|({\bf Id}-{\bf A})^{N+1}\right\|\leq\left\|{\bf Id}-{\bf A}\right\|^{N+1}<1.\end{split}

Thus,
\nu_{N}
is an approximately dual probabilistic frame to
\mu
with respect to
\tilde{\gamma}_{N}
, and

\Big\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\bf y}{\bf x}^{t}d\tilde{\gamma}_{N}({\bf x,y})-{\bf Id}\Big\|\leq\|{\bf Id}-{\bf A}\|^{N+1}\rightarrow 0\ \text{as $N\rightarrow\infty$}.

∎

Since we can construct approximately dual frames with arbitrarily small error, we give another alternative definition of approximately dual probabilistic frame. Let
\mu
be a probabilistic frame and
0<\epsilon<1
.
\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is said to be an
approximately dual probabilistic frame
of
\mu
if there exists
\gamma\in\Gamma(\mu,\nu)
such that

\left\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\bf x}{\bf y}^{t}d\gamma({\bf x,y})-{\bf Id}\right\|\leq\epsilon.

Similar to the situation with probabilistic duals (see
Lemma

3.10
), we can characterize all approximately dual frames of pushforward type. We begin with the following lemma.

Lemma 4.6
.

Suppose
\bf A
is any
n\times n
matrix such that
\|{\bf A-Id}\|<1
. Then
({\bf A}^{t}{\bf S}_{\mu}^{-1})_{\#}\mu
is an approximately dual frame of
\mu
.

Proof.

Define
\gamma:=({\bf Id},{\bf A}^{t}{\bf S}_{\mu}^{-1})_{\#}\mu\in\Gamma(\mu,({\bf A}^{t}{\bf S}_{\mu}^{-1})_{\#}\mu)
, then

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\bf x}{\bf y}^{t}d\gamma({\bf x,y})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\bf x}{\bf x}^{t}{\bf S}_{\mu}^{-1}{\bf A}d\mu({\bf x})={\bf S}_{\mu}{\bf S}_{\mu}^{-1}{\bf A}={\bf A}.

Since
\|{\bf A-Id}\|<1
, then
({\bf A}^{t}{\bf S}_{\mu}^{-1})_{\#}\mu
is an approximately dual frame to
\mu
.
∎

We characterize all approximately dual frames of pushforward type as follows.

Corollary 4.7
.

Let
T_{\#}\mu
be an approximately dual frame to
\mu
where
T:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a measurable map. Then for any
\ {\bf x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
T:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
precisely satisfies

T({\bf x})={\bf A}^{t}{\bf S}_{\mu}^{-1}{\bf x}+h({\bf x}),

where
\bf A
is an
n\times n
matrix satisfying
\|{\bf A-Id}\|<1
and
h:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
is such that
h_{\#}\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\bf x}h({\bf x})^{t}d\mu({\bf x})={\bf 0}_{n\times n}
.

Proof.

First note that if
T:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
is of the above type, then
T_{\#}\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Now define
\gamma=({\bf Id},T)_{\#}\mu\in\Gamma(\mu,T_{\#}\mu)
.
Then

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\bf x}{\bf y}^{t}d\gamma({\bf x,y})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\bf x}T({\bf x})^{t}d\mu({\bf x})={\bf S}_{\mu}{\bf S}_{\mu}^{-1}{\bf A}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\bf x}h({\bf x})^{t}d\mu({\bf x})={\bf A}.

Since
\|{\bf A-Id}\|<1
, then
T_{\#}\mu
is an approximately dual frame to
\mu
. Conversely, if
T_{\#}\mu
is an approximate dual to
\mu
with respect to
({\bf Id},T)_{\#}\mu\in\Gamma(\mu,T_{\#}\mu)
, then

\Big\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\bf x}T({\bf x})^{t}d\mu({\bf x})-{\bf{Id}}\Big\|<1.

Now let

{\bf A}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\bf x}T({\bf x})^{t}d\mu({\bf x})
. Note that for any
{\bf x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

T({\bf x})={\bf A}^{t}{\bf S}_{\mu}^{-1}({\bf x})+h({\bf x})
where
h({\bf x})=T({\bf x})-{\bf A}^{t}{\bf S}_{\mu}^{-1}({\bf x})
. Then
h_{\#}\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\bf x}h({\bf x})^{t}d\mu({\bf x})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\bf x}T({\bf x})^{t}d\mu({\bf x})-{\bf A}={\bf A}-{\bf A}={\bf 0}_{n\times n}.

Therefore,
T:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
is of the desired type.
∎

Using a similar argument as in the above proof, we give another characterization.

Corollary 4.8
.

Let
T_{\#}\mu
be an approximately dual frame to
\mu
where
T:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
is measurable. Then for all
\ {\bf x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
the map
T:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
precisely satisfies

T({\bf x})={\bf A}^{t}{\bf S}_{\mu}^{-1}({\bf x})+h({\bf x})-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\langle{\bf S}_{\mu}^{-1}{\bf x},{\bf y}\rangle h({\bf y})d\mu({\bf y}),

where
\bf A
is an
n\times n
matrix satisfying
\|{\bf A-Id}\|<1
and
h:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
is such that
h_{\#}\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Note that not every approximately dual frame is of pushforward type, since one can find a dual frame that is not of the pushforward type. As we’ve already seen,
\mu=\frac{1}{2}\delta_{\frac{1}{2}}+\frac{1}{2}\delta_{\frac{3}{2}}
is a dual frame of
\delta_{1}
under the coupling
\gamma=({\bf Id},T)_{\#}\mu\in\Gamma(\mu,\delta_{1})
where
T(x)=1,x\in\mathbb{R}
even though there does not exist a map
M:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that
\mu=M_{\#}\delta_{1}=\delta_{M(1)}
. We finish this section with the following corollary.

Corollary 4.9
.

Let
\mu
be a probabilistic frame and
\bf A
an
n\times n
matrix such that
\|{\bf A-Id}\|<1
. Suppose
T_{\#}\mu
is a dual probabilistic frame to
\mu
where
T:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
is measurable. Then
({\bf A}^{t}{\bf S}_{\mu}^{-1}-{\bf Id}+{\bf S}_{\mu}T)_{\#}\mu
is an approximately dual frame of
\mu
.

Proof.

Since
T_{\#}\mu
is a dual probabilistic frame to
\mu
, then
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\bf x}T({\bf x})^{t}d\mu({\bf x})={\bf Id}
.
By letting
h({\bf x})=(-{\bf Id}+{\bf S}_{\mu}T){\bf x}
, then
h_{\#}\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\bf x}h({\bf x})^{t}d\mu({\bf x})=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\bf x}{\bf x}^{t}d\mu({\bf x})+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\bf x}T({\bf x})^{t}d\mu({\bf x})\cdot{\bf S}_{\mu}={\bf 0}_{n\times n}.

By
Corollary

4.7
,
({\bf A}^{t}{\bf S}_{\mu}^{-1}-{\bf Id}+{\bf S}_{\mu}T)_{\#}\mu
is an approximate dual of
\mu
.
∎

The space of probabilistic frames is open in the
2
-Wasserstein topology, meaning that small perturbations of probabilistic frames are still probabilistic frames. More precisely:

Proposition 5.1

(
[
7
, Proposition 1.2, Corollary 1.3]
)
.

Let
\mu
be a probabilistic frame with lower frame bound
A>0
. If there exist
\eta\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\gamma\in\Gamma(\eta,\mu)
so that

\lambda:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\|{\bf x}-{\bf y}\|^{2}d\gamma({\bf x},{\bf y})<A,

then
\eta
is a probabilistic frame with bounds
(\sqrt{A}-\sqrt{\lambda})^{2}
and
M_{2}(\eta)
. Furthermore, if
W_{2}(\mu,\eta)<\sqrt{A}
, then
\eta
is a probabilistic frame with bounds
(\sqrt{A}-W_{2}(\mu,\nu))^{2}
and
M_{2}(\eta)
.

Based on this result, we establish the following lemma.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
\mu
be a probabilistic frame with lower frame bound
A>0
. Suppose
\nu
is a dual probabilistic frame to
\mu
with upper frame bound
C>0
with
AC\leq 1
. If there exist
\eta\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\gamma\in\Gamma(\eta,\mu)
such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\|{\bf x}-{\bf y}\|^{2}d\gamma({\bf x},{\bf y})<A,

then
\eta
is an approximately dual probabilistic frame to
\nu
.

Proof.

By
Proposition

5.1
, we know that
\eta
is a probabilistic frame. Since
\nu
is a dual probabilistic frame to
\mu
, then there exists
\gamma^{\prime}\in\Gamma(\mu,\nu)
such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\bf y}{\bf z}^{t}d\gamma^{\prime}({\bf y,z})={\bf Id}.

By
Lemma

2.4
(Gluing Lemma), there exists
\tilde{\pi}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
{\pi_{xy}}_{\#}\tilde{\pi}=\gamma,\ {\pi_{yz}}_{\#}\tilde{\pi}=\gamma^{\prime}
where
\pi_{xy}
and
\pi_{yz}
are projections to
({\bf x},{\bf y})
and
({\bf y},{\bf z})
coordinates. Now let
\tilde{\gamma}:={\pi_{xz}}_{\#}\tilde{\pi}\in\Gamma(\eta,\nu).

Then, for any
{\bf f}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\begin{split}\left\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\bf x}\langle{\bf z},{\bf f}\rangle d\tilde{\gamma}({\bf x,z})-{\bf f}\right\|^{2}&=\left\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\bf x}\langle{\bf z},{\bf f}\rangle d\tilde{\gamma}({\bf x,z})-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\bf y}\langle{\bf z},{\bf f}\rangle d\gamma^{\prime}({\bf y,z})\right\|^{2}\\
&=\left\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}({\bf x-y})\langle{\bf z},{\bf f}\rangle d\tilde{\pi}({\bf x,y,z})\right\|^{2}\\
&\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\|{\bf x-y}\right\|^{2}d\gamma({\bf x,y})\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\langle{\bf z},{\bf f}\rangle|^{2}d\nu({\bf z})\\
&<AC\|{\bf f}\|^{2},\end{split}

where the first inequality is due to the triangle inequality and the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality. Then

\Big\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\bf x}{\bf z}^{t}d\tilde{\gamma}({\bf x,z})-{\bf Id}\Big\|<\sqrt{AC}\leq 1,

so
\eta
is an approximately dual frame to
\nu
with respect to
\tilde{\gamma}\in\Gamma(\eta,\nu)
.
∎

If
\gamma\in\Gamma(\eta,\mu)
in the above lemma is an optimal coupling for
W_{2}(\eta,\mu)
, then we have the following corollary.

Corollary 5.3
.

Let
\mu
be a probabilistic frame with lower bound
A>0
and
\nu
a dual probabilistic frame to
\mu
with upper bound
C>0
so that
AC\leq 1
. If there exist
\eta\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
W_{2}(\eta,\mu)<\sqrt{A},

then
\eta
is an approximately dual frame to
\nu
.

By the above corollary, the following claim is clear.

Corollary 5.4
.

For a fixed probabilistic frame, its dual probabilistic frames are interior points in the set of approximately dual frames in the
2
-Wasserstein topology.

A
and the upper frame bound

for the dual frame need to satisfy

. After all, maybe there are no such dual frames or, even if there are, maybe they are effectively impossible to find. Fortunately, the canonical dual frame satisfies this condition.

.

Let
\mu
be a probabilistic frame with lower frame bound
A>0
. Suppose there exist
\eta\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

{{\bf S}_{\mu}^{-1}}_{\#}\mu

(W_{2}(\eta,\mu)-\sqrt{A_{\eta}})^{2}
. Now let
\nu={{\bf S}_{\mu}^{-1}}_{\#}\mu=\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{N}\delta_{{\bf S}_{\mu}^{-1}{\bf f}_{k}}
be the canonical dual to
\mu
. Then the upper frame bound for
\nu
is given by

C_{N}=\frac{1}{(W_{2}(\eta,\mu)-\sqrt{A_{\eta}})^{2}}<\frac{1}{(\sqrt{A_{\eta}}/2)^{2}}=\frac{1}{A_{\eta}/4}

and

A_{N}C_{N}=\frac{A_{N}}{(W_{2}(\eta,\mu)-\sqrt{A_{\eta}})^{2}}<\frac{A_{\eta}/4}{A_{\eta}/4}=1,

so
\nu
satisfies the hypotheses of
Theorem

5.6
.

By
Theorem

5.6
, absolutely continuous probabilistic frames can have discrete approximately dual frames. Conversely, discrete probabilistic frames can also have approximate dual frames that are absolutely continuous. For example, the standard Gaussian measure
d\mu(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-\frac{(x-1)^{2}}{2}}dx
is a dual probabilistic frame of
\delta_{1}
with respect to the product measure
\mu\otimes\delta_{1}
. Thus,
\mu
is also an approximately dual frame to
\delta_{1}
. This is another example showing that not all the dual frames and approximately dual frames to
\delta_{1}
are of pushforward type.

The above results are based on
Proposition

5.1
. We can obtain other results if probabilistic frames are perturbed in different ways.

Lemma 5.7
.

Let
\mu
be a probabilistic frame and
\nu
a dual probabilistic frame of
\mu
with second moment
M_{2}(\nu)
. Suppose
C>0
,
M_{2}(\nu)C<1
, and there exist
\eta\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\pi\in\Gamma(\eta,\mu)
such that for any
{\bf f}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\left\langle{\bf f},{\bf x-y}\right\rangle|^{2}d\pi({\bf x,y})\leq C\|{\bf f}\|^{2}.

Then
\eta
is a probabilistic frame and
\nu
is an approximately dual frame to
\eta
.

Proof.

Since
\nu
is a dual probabilistic frame to
\mu
, there exists
\gamma\in\Gamma(\mu,\nu)
such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\bf z}{\bf y}^{t}d\gamma({\bf y,z})={\bf Id}.

By
Lemma

2.4
(Gluing Lemma), there exists
\tilde{\pi}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
{\pi_{xy}}_{\#}{\tilde{\pi}}=\pi
and
{\pi_{yz}}_{\#}{\tilde{\pi}}=\gamma
. Now let
\tilde{\gamma}={\pi_{xz}}_{\#}{\tilde{\pi}}\in\Gamma(\eta,\nu)
.
Then, for any
{\bf f}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\begin{split}\left\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\bf z}\langle{\bf x},{\bf f}\rangle d\tilde{\gamma}({\bf x,z})-{\bf f}\right\|^{2}&=\left\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\bf z}\langle{\bf x},{\bf f}\rangle d\tilde{\gamma}({\bf x,z})-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\bf z}\langle{\bf y},{\bf f}\rangle d\gamma({\bf y,z})\right\|^{2}\\
&=\left\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\bf z}\langle{\bf x-y},{\bf f}\rangle d\tilde{\pi}({\bf x,y,z})\right\|^{2}\\
&\leq\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\|{\bf z}\|\ |\langle{\bf x-y},{\bf f}\rangle|d\tilde{\pi}({\bf x,y,z})\right|^{2}\\
&\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\|{\bf z}\|^{2}d\nu({\bf z})\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\langle{\bf x-y},{\bf f}\rangle|^{2}d\pi({\bf x,y})\\
&\leq M_{2}(\nu)C\ \|{\bf f}\|^{2}.\end{split}

where the second inequality follows from the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality. Then

\left\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\bf x}{\bf z}^{t}d\tilde{\gamma}({\bf x,z})-{\bf Id}\right\|=\left\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\bf z}{\bf x}^{t}d\tilde{\gamma}({\bf x,z})-{\bf Id}\right\|\leq\sqrt{M_{2}(\nu)C}<1.

Hence,
\nu
is an approximately dual frame to
\eta
, and therefore also a probabilistic frame by
Lemma

4.2
.
∎

Lemma 5.10
.

Let
\mu
be a probabilistic frame with lower bound
A>0
and
\nu
an approximately dual frame of
\mu
with mixed frame operator
{\bf A}
. If the upper frame bound of
\nu
is
C>0

and there exist
\eta\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\pi\in\Gamma(\eta,\mu)
such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\|{\bf x}-{\bf A^{-1}y}\|^{2}d\pi({\bf x,y})<1/C,

then
\eta
is a probabilistic frame and
\nu
is an approximately dual frame to
\eta
.

Proof.

Since
\nu
is an approximately dual frame to
\mu
with mixed frame operator
{\bf A}
, then there exists
\gamma\in\Gamma(\mu,\nu)
such that

{\bf A}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\bf y}{\bf z}^{t}d\gamma({\bf y,z})

and
\|{\bf A-Id}\|<1
. Therefore,
{\bf A}
is invertible. By
Lemma

2.4
(Gluing Lemma), there exists
\tilde{\pi}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
\pi={\pi_{xy}}_{\#}{\tilde{\pi}}
and
\gamma={\pi_{yz}}_{\#}{\tilde{\pi}}
. Now define
\tilde{\gamma}={\pi_{xz}}_{\#}{\tilde{\pi}}\in\Gamma(\eta,\nu)
.
Then, for any
{\bf f}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\begin{split}\left\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\bf x}\langle{\bf z,f}\rangle d\tilde{\gamma}({\bf x,z})-{\bf f}\right\|^{2}&=\left\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\bf x}\langle{\bf z,f}\rangle d\tilde{\gamma}({\bf x,z})-{\bf A^{-1}Af}\right\|^{2}\\
&=\left\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\bf x}\langle{\bf z,f}\rangle d\tilde{\gamma}({\bf x,z})-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\bf A^{-1}}{\bf y}\langle{\bf z,f}\rangle d\gamma({\bf y,z})\right\|^{2}\\
&=\left\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}({\bf x}-{\bf A^{-1}}{\bf y})\langle{\bf z,f}\rangle d\tilde{\pi}({\bf x,y,z})\right\|^{2}\\
&\leq\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\|{\bf x}-{\bf A^{-1}y}\|\,|\langle{\bf z,f}\rangle|\,d\tilde{\pi}({\bf x,y,z})\right|^{2}\\

&<\|{\bf f}\|^{2},\end{split}

where the second inequality is due to the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality. Therefore,

\left\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\bf x}{\bf z}^{t}d\tilde{\gamma}({\bf x,z})-{\bf Id}\right\|<1.

6.
Pseudo-Dual Probabilistic Frames

In this section, we generalize dual probabilistic frames even further, to pseudo-dual probabilistic frames. Given
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\gamma\in\Gamma(\mu,\nu)
, call the matrix

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\bf x}{\bf y}^{t}d\gamma({\bf x,y})

the
mixed frame operator
of
\mu
and
\nu
. This generalizes the terminology of
Definition

4.1
slightly, in that neither
\mu
nor
\nu
is required to be a probabilistic frame.

Definition 6.1
.

Let
\mu
be a probabilistic frame.
\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is called a
pseudo-dual probabilistic frame
of
\mu
if there exists
\gamma\in\Gamma(\mu,\nu)
such that the associated mixed frame operator is invertible.

Clearly, dual probabilistic frames and approximately dual frames are pseudo-duals. Note that for finite frames
\{{\bf f}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
and
\{{\bf g}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
where
N\geq n
, their mixed operator
\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\bf f}_{i}{\bf g}_{i}^{t}
is not always invertible. For example, when
d=2
and
N=4
, define frames

{\bf f}_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}1\\
0\end{pmatrix},\quad{\bf f}_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
1\end{pmatrix},\quad{\bf f}_{3}=\begin{pmatrix}1\\
0\end{pmatrix},\quad{\bf f}_{4}=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
1\end{pmatrix}

and

{\bf g}_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}1\\
0\end{pmatrix},\quad{\bf g}_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}1\\
0\end{pmatrix},\quad{\bf g}_{3}=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
1\end{pmatrix},\quad{\bf g}_{4}=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
1\end{pmatrix}.

The mixed operator between
\{{\bf f}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{4}
and
\{{\bf g}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{4}
is given by

\sum\limits_{i=1}^{4}{\bf f}_{i}{\bf g}_{i}^{t}=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\
1&1\end{pmatrix}
, which is not invertible. This example shows that a frame and a permutation of that frame need not be pseudo-duals of each other.

Furthermore, arbitrary non-atomic probabilisitic frames are not necessarily pseudo-duals. Let
\mu
be any probabilistic frame on
{\mathbb{R}}
with zero mean and non-zero second moment, like the standard Gaussian measure. Then the mixed frame operator of
\mu
with itself with respect to the product measure
\mu\otimes\mu
is not invertible, since

\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}}xyd\mu(x)d\mu(y)=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}xd\mu(x)\right)^{2}=0.

Similarly to
Lemma

4.2
, we claim that a pseudo-dual probabilistic frame is a probabilistic frame. We also obtain an uncertainty principle and a similar relationship between optimal frame bounds for a probabilistic frame and its pseudo-duals. The proof is omitted since the argument is the same as in
Lemma

4.2
.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
\mu
be a probabilistic frame with upper frame bound
B>0
. Suppose
\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a pseudo-dual probabilistic frame of
\mu
with mixed frame operator
\bf A
, then
\nu
is a probabilistic frame with bounds
1/(B\|{\bf A}^{-1}\|^{2})
and
M_{2}(\nu)
.

For any
{\bf f}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we have the following uncertainty principle:

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\langle{\bf A}^{-1}{\bf x},{\bf f}\rangle|^{2}d\mu({\bf x})\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\langle{\bf y},{\bf f}\rangle|^{2}d\nu({\bf y})\geq\|{\bf f}\|^{4}.

Furthermore, if
A_{\mu}
,
B_{\mu}
, and
A_{\nu}
,
B_{\nu}
are optimal frame bounds for
\mu
and
\nu
, then

A_{\nu}\geq\frac{1}{B_{\mu}\|{\bf A}^{-1}\|^{2}}\ \text{and}\ A_{\mu}\geq\frac{1}{B_{\nu}\|{\bf A}^{-1}\|^{2}}.

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be a pseudo-dual of the probabilistic frame
\mu
. If the matrix
{\bf A}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
is invertible, then
{\bf A}_{\#}\nu
is also a pseudo-dual to
\mu
. If
{\bf A}
is the mixed frame operator of
\mu
and
\nu
, then
{({\bf A}^{-1})^{t}}_{\#}\nu
is a dual probabilistic frame to
\mu
.

Proof.

Clearly,
{\bf A}_{\#}\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Since
\nu
is a pseudo-dual frame of
\mu
, then there exists
\gamma\in\Gamma(\mu,\nu)
such that the mixed frame operator
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\bf x}{\bf y}^{t}d\gamma({\bf x,y})
is invertible. Now define
\tilde{\gamma}:=({\bf Id},{\bf A})_{\#}\gamma\in\Gamma(\mu,{\bf A}_{\#}\nu)
. Then,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\bf x}{\bf y}^{t}d\tilde{\gamma}({\bf x,y})=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\bf x}{\bf y}^{t}d\gamma({\bf x,y})\right){\bf A}^{t},

which is also invertible. Therefore,
{\bf A}_{\#}\nu
is a pseudo-dual probabilistic frame to
\mu
.

If
{\bf A}
is the mixed frame operator of
\mu
and
\nu
, then

{\bf A}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\bf x}{\bf y}^{t}d\gamma({\bf x,y})
. Now define
\gamma^{\prime}:=({\bf Id},({\bf A}^{-1})^{t})_{\#}\gamma\in\Gamma(\mu,({\bf A}^{-1})^{t}_{\#}\nu)
. Then

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\bf x}{\bf y}^{t}d\gamma^{\prime}({\bf x,y})=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\bf x}{\bf y}^{t}d\gamma({\bf x,y})\right){\bf A}^{-1}={\bf A}{\bf A}^{-1}={\bf Id}.

Therefore,
({\bf A}^{-1})^{t}_{\#}\nu
is a dual probabilistic frame to
\mu
with respect to
\gamma^{\prime}
.
∎

We have the following characterization for pseudo-dual probabilistic frames of pushforward type. The proof is similar to
Corollary

4.7
.

Corollary 6.4
.

Suppose
\mu
is a probabilistic frame and
T_{\#}\mu
is a pseudo-dual probabilistic frame to
\mu
where
T:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then for any
{\bf x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

T({\bf x})={\bf A}^{t}{\bf S}_{\mu}^{-1}{\bf x}+h({\bf x}),

where
\bf A
is an invertible
n\times n
matrix and
h:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
is such that
h_{\#}\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\bf x}h({\bf x})^{t}d\mu({\bf x})={\bf 0}_{n\times n}
.

Proof.

If
T
is of the above type, then
T_{\#}\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Now define
\gamma=({\bf Id},T)_{\#}\mu
. Then
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\bf x}{\bf y}^{t}d\gamma({\bf x,y})={\bf A}
, which is invertible. Therefore,
T_{\#}\mu
is a pseudo-dual. Conversely, if
T_{\#}\mu
is a pseudo-dual, then define
{\bf A}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\bf x}T({\bf x})^{t}d\mu({\bf x})
. Clearly, for any
{\bf x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

T({\bf x})={\bf A}^{t}{\bf S}_{\mu}^{-1}({\bf x})+T({\bf x})-{\bf A}^{t}{\bf S}_{\mu}^{-1}({\bf x}).

Now let
h({\bf x})=T({\bf x})-{\bf A}^{t}{\bf S}_{\mu}^{-1}({\bf x})
, then
h_{\#}\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\bf x}h({\bf x})^{t}d\mu({\bf x})={\bf 0}_{n\times n}.

Thus
T
is of the desired type.
∎

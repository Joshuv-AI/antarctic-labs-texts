Cotilting duality for artinian rings
Francesca Mantese, Lorenzo Martini

Abstract
. A classical result due to Morita and Azumaya establishes that given two arbitrary rings, any duality between their finitely generated modules is representable by a faithfully balanced bimodule which is a finitely generated injective cogenerator of both rings and, equivalently, these latter are one-sided artinian. We extend this well-known result to the case of a cotilting bimodule, by analysing the duality it represents in the bounded derived categories of the given rings.

1.
Introduction

It is well known that, in contrast to (additive) equivalences, no duality can affect the whole module categories
S\mathchar 45\textup{Mod}
and
\textup{Mod}\mathchar 45R
over two given rings. Nonetheless, given two subcategories
{}_{S}\mathcal{M}
and
\mathcal{M}_{R}
, the somehow reasonable conditions
{}_{S}S\in{}_{S}\mathcal{M}
and
R_{R}\in\mathcal{M}_{R}
for a duality
F:{}_{S}\mathcal{M}\rightleftarrows\mathcal{M}_{R}:G
are enough to make it representable by a bimodule
{}_{S}U_{R}
; that is, there are natural isomorphisms
F\cong\varDelta_{S}
and
G\cong\varDelta_{R}
, where each
\varDelta
is the contravariant Hom functor of
U
. In particular, the modules involved in such a duality are
U
-reflexive, meaning that for all
M\in\mathcal{M}
the evaluation maps

\displaystyle\omega_{M}\colon M

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\varDelta^{2}(M)

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle\longmapsto\begin{aligned} {}\mkern 3.0mu\widetilde{\mkern-3.0mux}\colon\varDelta(M)&\to U\cr f&\mapsto f(x)\end{aligned}

are isomorphisms. Classical Morita theory of duality characterises the representable dualities among thick subcategories of modules (i.e. subcategories with the three-out-of-two condition on short exact sequences). In details, in a duality as above the involved subcategories are thick if, and only if, the representing bimodule is faithfully balanced and an injective cogenerator on both sides. These bimodules are called Morita bimodules. In general, the structure of the rings admitting a Morita bimodule
U
, yet that of
U
-reflexive modules, is not well understood. Nonetheless, a crucial result by Azumaya and Morita characterises the one-sided artinian rings admitting a Morita bimodule
U
as the rings over which the
U
-reflexive modules are precisely the finitely generated ones. We gather this fact in the following statements.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
3

)
.

Let

be a right artinian ring,

a finitely generated injective cogenerator of
\textup{Mod}\mathchar 45R

(1)

is a Morita bimodule;

(2)

(3)

Theorem 1.2

(
[
3

Let

Corollary 1.3
.

Let

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
R

Morita theory has been generalised in a very broad sense, mostly since the advent of tilting theory in the eighties—nowadays playing a prominent role in distinguished categorical contexts, such as derived and triangulated categories (see e.g.
[
18
,
20
]
). From a module-theoretic point of view, the definition of Morita bimodule—formerly, that of injective cogenerator—is generalised by the notion of
1
-cotilting module (namely, of a module which is injective with respect to the class of modules it cogenerates); in particular, a
cotilting bimodule
is defined as a faithfully balanced bimodule which is
1
-cotiling on both sides. We refer the reader to subsection
2.2
to any detail on cotiling (bi)modules. It is then natural to ask how cotilting bimodules shall generalise Morita bimodules, either by looking at their reflexive modules and understanding whether they represent some duality. In this direction, a vital role is played by the fact that any
1
-cotilting module cogenerates a non-trivial torsion pair, unlike any injective cogenerator. For instance, the Cotilting theorem (see e.g.
[
6
, Theorem 6]
) ensures that a cotilting bimodule
{}_{S}U_{R}
yields two pairs of dualities, via its contravariant
\operatorname{Hom}
and
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}
functors, among suitable subcategories of the kernel of these functors, which indeed form two torsion pairs. Comparing this with Morita duality, one remarkable feature that is missing is a representability criterion; still, the classes involved in both dualities are far from being classified. In fact, representability results extending Morita theory have been proven to be more likely over derived module categories, still by generalised tilting theory, namely by means of distinguished complexes. For instance, in
[
19
]
a notion of cotilting bimodule complex is introduced, and proved to representing—over one-sided coherent rings—derived counterparts of Morita dualities. However, no remarkable feature of Azumaya’s Morita theory seems to be decodable by such a context.

In this paper, we exploit some crucial results achieved in
[
16
,
17
]
concerning dualities in derived categories of modules; we will prove that a cotilting bimodule induces a duality, termed
cotilting duality
, for which an analogue of the aforementioned Azumaya’s theorems on Morita duality holds, when finitely generated modules are replaced by bounded complexes with finitely generated cohomology. In particular, on the one hand we will get a criterion for a one-sided artinian ring to admit a cotilting duality; on the other hand, we will prove a representability theorem, that reflects the necessity of both the rings to be one-sided artinian, in order to their bounded complexes with finitely generated cohomology being dual to each other.

Let us discuss more in detail the background and our results. Recall that, for any ring
R
,
\mathcal{D}^{b}(R)
will denote the bounded derived category of
\textup{Mod}\mathchar 45R

\mathbf{R}\varDelta_{S}:\mathcal{D}^{b}(S^{\rm op})\mathrel{\mathop{\vbox{\hbox to17.07182pt{\rightarrowfill}\hbox to17.07182pt{\leftarrowfill}}}}\mathcal{D}^{b}(R):\mathbf{R}\varDelta_{R}

between the right derived functors of

and

and
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}

whose counit maps

\mathcal{D}

\mathcal{D}
-reflexive as a stalk complex; in
[

-reflexive. Consequently, in the notation we set there are equivalences of triangulated categories

\mathcal{D}_{R}^{\vphantom{b}}\cong\mathcal{D}^{b}_{\mathcal{M}_{R}}(R)\quad\hbox{and}\quad\mathcal{D}_{S^{\rm op}}^{\vphantom{b}}\cong\mathcal{D}^{b}_{{}_{S}\mathcal{M}}(S^{\rm op}),

To motivate our study of cotilting dualities as in the context of Morita theory for artinian rings, let us recall two results from
[
17
]
. In
[
17
, Example 1.3]
it is exhibited a two-sided artinian ring which does not admit a Morita bimodule but admits a cotilting bimodule. In particular, cotiling bimodules strictly generalise Morita bimodules even over artinian rings. In
[
17
, Corollary 3.7]
it is proved that if

is right artinian and

is a cotilting bimodule, then the
\mathcal{D}
-reflexive right

. This expresses how Corollary

This said, we have to face at once a substantial lack of symmetry with respect to the classical case: if

is right artinian and
{}_{S}U_{R}
is a cotilting bimodule, then neither

needs to be left artinian (see Remark

) nor the

-reflexive left

that the occurance of one condition is in fact equivalent to the occurance of the other. Consequently, if

is right artinian and

is left artinian, any cotilting duality between

\mathbf{R}\varDelta_{S}:\mathcal{D}^{b}_{S\mathchar 45\textup{mod}}(S^{\textup{op}})\mathrel{\mathop{\vbox{\hbox to17.07182pt{\rightarrowfill}\hbox to17.07182pt{\leftarrowfill}}}}\mathcal{D}^{b}_{\textup{mod}\mathchar 45R}(R):\mathbf{R}\varDelta_{R}.

Altogether, we need to understand when a right artinian ring admits a cotilting duality. In our first main result, we classify these rings still by means of a finiteness condition on their cotilting modules, namely their product completeness, getting the following incarnation of Corollary
1.3
.

Theorem

(Corollary
3.4
)
.

A right artinian ring
R
has a cotilting duality if and only it admits a finitely generated product complete cotilting module.

Notice that the previous is an effective extension of Morita theory for artinian rings to cotiling dualities. Indeed, the aforementioned one-sided artinian ring provides an instance of a ring without a Morita duality and with a cotilting duality.

Our second main result is the following representability theorem, which embodies Theorems
1.1
and
1.2

Theorem

(Theorem

)
.

Let

\mathbf{R}F:\mathcal{D}^{b}_{S\mathchar 45\textup{mod}}(S^{\rm op})\mathrel{\mathop{\vbox{\hbox to17.07182pt{\rightarrowfill}\hbox to17.07182pt{\leftarrowfill}}}}\mathcal{D}^{b}_{\textup{mod}\mathchar 45R}(R):\mathbf{R}G

(1)

(2)

(2)

Let us briefly motivate the stated hypotheses. They make

-reflexive cohomology modules; in particular, (2) is equivalent to the composition of the right derived functors of

The paper is organised as follows. In Section

2.
Preliminaries

All the rings we consider are associative with
1\neq 0
. Given a ring

\textup{Mod}\mathchar 45R
(resp.

) will denote the category of its right (resp. left)

(resp.

R

\mathcal{D}(R)
(resp.
\mathcal{D}^{b}(R)
) the unbounded (resp. bounded) derived category of
\textup{Mod}\mathchar 45R
. Given a right
R

\displaystyle{}^{\bot_{0}}C

\displaystyle=\{M\in\textup{Mod}\mathchar 45R\mid\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(M,C)=0\},

\displaystyle{}^{\bot_{1}}C

\displaystyle=\{M\in\textup{Mod}\mathchar 45R\mid\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{R}(M,C)=0\},

and we will denote by

2.1.
Reflexivity and
\mathcal{D}
-reflexivity

Given a right adjoint pair of contravariant additive functors
F:\mathcal{A}\rightleftarrows\mathcal{B}:G
between two abelian categories, one defines its
reflexive objects
as those objects whose counit morphisms are isomorphisms. For instance, as mentioned in the introduction, over module categories dualities among thick subcategories are essentially represented by Morita bimodules
U
, and the reflexive modules are
U
-reflexive.

It is proved in
[
13
, Lemma 13.6]
that the right adjunction

F:\mathcal{A}\rightleftarrows\mathcal{B}:G
extends to a right adjunction
\mathbf{R}F:\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathcal{A})\rightleftarrows\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathcal{B}):\mathbf{R}G
, as long as the bounded derived categories of
\mathcal{A,B}
and the right derived functors of
F,G
exist. As shown in
[
16
, Example 2.4]
, one gets two independent notions of reflexivity within the underlying abelian categories, namely when looking either at the objects involved in the adjunction
(F,G)
or at the objects that, as stalk complexes, are involved in the derived adjunction
(\mathbf{R}F,\mathbf{R}G)
. Thus, the latter yet all the reflexive complexes shall be called
\mathcal{D}
-reflexive
instead. In
[
16
, Corollary 3.6]
it is proved that, under certain homological conditions, a complex is
\mathcal{D}
-reflexive if, and only if, its cohomologies are
\mathcal{D}
-reflexive modules. As we will recall in the forthcoming Remark
2.3
, such conditions are fulfilled by the adjunction we are going to focus on in the present paper, namely the one provided by the contravariant Hom functors of a
1
-cotilting bimodule.

2.2.
Cotilting bimodules

Let
R
be a ring and
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Following
[
11
, Definition 15.1]
, a right
R
-module
U
is called
n
-cotilting if

(1)

the injective dimension of
U_{R}
is
\leq n
;

(2)

\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{R}(U^{\alpha},U)=0
, for all
1\leq i\leq n
and every cardinal
\alpha
;

(3)

there exist
r\geq 0
and an exact sequence
0\to U_{r}\to\cdots\to U_{1}\to U_{0}\to Q\to 0
, where each
U_{i}
is a direct summand of a product of copies of
U
, and
Q
is an injective cogenerator of
\textup{Mod}\mathchar 45R
.

Remark 2.1
.

Let us recall further well-known facts on
1
-cotilting modules. Given a ring
R
, a module
U_{R}
is
1
-cotilting if and only if
{}^{\bot_{1}}U=\operatorname{Cogen}U
; if and only if
{}^{\bot_{0}}U\cap{}^{\bot_{1}}U=\{0\}
and conditions (i)–(ii) above, for
n=1
, hold. Recall that a module
U_{R}
satisfying conditions (i)–(ii) is called
partial cotilting
.

In particular, any
1
-cotilting module
U_{R}
gives rise to the torsion pair
({}^{\bot_{0}}U,{}^{\bot_{1}}U)
in
\textup{Mod}\mathchar 45R
, meaning that every right
R
-module
M
fits in a functorial short exact sequence
0\to X\to M\to Y\to 0
, with
\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(X,U)=0
and
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{R}(Y,U)=0
. The torsion radical of the torsion pair, i.e. the functor
M\mapsto X
, is explicitly described as the
reject in
U
, namely

\operatorname{Rej}_{U}(M)=\bigcap\{\operatorname{Ker}f\mid f\in\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(M,U)\},

for all
M_{R}
(notice that
\operatorname{Rej}_{U}(M)=\operatorname{Ker}\omega_{M}
). By dropping any reference to
U_{R}
, we will call the modules in
{}^{\bot_{0}}U
simply the
torsion modules
, whereas the modules in
{}^{\bot_{1}}U
will be called
torsionfree modules
. Moreover, we set

\varDelta_{R}=\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(-,U)\quad\hbox{and}\quad\varGamma_{\!R}=\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{R}(-,U).

A
S
-
R
-bimodule
{}_{S}U_{R}
is called

-cotilting bimodule
if it is faithfully balanced and
{}_{S}U
and

are
n
-cotilting modules. Thus,
0
-cotilting bimodules are precisely the Morita bimodules (see also
[
11
, Remark 15.8]
). Henceforth, we will refer to a
1
-cotilting bimodule just as a

(see Remark
2.3
).

Remark 2.2
.

As cotilting bimodules generalise Morita bimodules, in view of
[
25
, Remark 2.9]
let us survey what happens in the context we are interested in—the artinian setting. Recall that, given any bimodule
{}_{S}M_{R}
, the triangular matrix ring

\varLambda=\bigl(\!\begin{smallmatrix}R&0\cr M&S\end{smallmatrix}\!\bigr)

is a right (left) artinian ring if and only if both
S
and
R
are right (left) artinian rings, and
M_{R}
(
{}_{S}M
) is finitely generated.

Now, on the one hand cotilting bimodules extend Morita bimodules over artinian rings. Indeed, let
D
be a division ring having a division subring
C
such that
\dim({}_{C}D)
is finite but
\dim(D_{C})
is not. By the above, the triangular matrix ring

\varLambda=\bigl(\!\begin{smallmatrix}D&0\cr D&C\end{smallmatrix}\!\bigr)

is a two-sided artinian ring, but it has no Morita bimodules (for
\varLambda\mathchar 45\textup{mod}
and
\textup{mod}\mathchar 45\varLambda
are not dual to each other); nonetheless,
{}_{\varLambda}\varLambda_{\varLambda}
is a cotilting bimodule by
[

.

On the other hand, if

is a right artinian ring and
{}_{S}U_{R}
is a cotilting bimodule, then

needs not to be left artinian. Indeed, let

\varLambda=\bigl(\!\begin{smallmatrix}\mathbb{R}&0\cr\mathbb{R}&\mathbb{Q}\end{smallmatrix}\!\bigr)

then
\varLambda

As announced, given a cotilting bimodule

\varDelta_{R}

\mathbf{R}\varDelta_{S}:\mathcal{D}^{b}(S^{\rm op})\mathrel{\mathop{\vbox{\hbox to17.07182pt{\rightarrowfill}\hbox to17.07182pt{\leftarrowfill}}}}\mathcal{D}^{b}(R):\mathbf{R}\varDelta_{R}

(still implying any reference to

When there is no risk of confusion, we shall denote by

and

(and by

Remark 2.3
.

We are going to deal exclusively with
1
-cotilting bimodules, for the duality they induce fulfils the aforementioned conditions of

, Corollary 3.6]
, so that a complex is
\mathcal{D}
-reflexive if, and only if, its cohomologies are

-reflexive modules. In details, given a cotilting bimodule

, since its injective dimensions are at most

Notice that the previous properties also provide, for any cotilting bimodule, the natural isomorphisms

(see
[

, Proposition 5.4]

-reflexive objects shall also be regarded as the complexes

whose counit map
{}\mkern 3.0mu\widehat{\mkern-3.0mu\omega}_{M}\colon M^{\textstyle\boldsymbol{\cdot}}\to\mathbf{L}\varDelta^{2}(M^{\textstyle\boldsymbol{\cdot}})

As we just said, in a cotilting duality the

-reflexive complexes are precisely the complexes whose cohomologies are

-reflexive modules. We now recall the following useful general result describing the

-reflexive modules.

For the sake of the reader, we recall the notion of product completeness together with some results from

Remark 2.6

Let

-module with

is called:

•

•

\varSigma
-pure-injective

Every endofinite module is product complete (these notions coincide over noetherian rings), and every product complete module is
\varSigma
-pure-injective (the notions are distinct even over

is endofinite if and only if every direct summand of

is product complete, if and only if

is
\varSigma
-pure-injective and
{}_{S}M
is noetherian. Eventually, a finitely generated module

is product complete if and only if

is left coherent and right perfect, and
{}_{S}M
is finitely presented.

In case

is right artinian and

is a cotilting bimodule
{}_{S}U_{R}
, then by Corollary
2.5
it follows that
{}_{S}U
is endofinite; by
[

, Corollary 5.4]
we have that

is left coherent.

Let

A right artinian ring has a cotiling duality if and only if it admits a finitely generated product complete cotilting module.

4.

{\rm(a)\Leftrightarrow(b)}

Let us pass to discuss the representability of a derived duality by a cotilting bimodule, namely how to generalise Azumaya’s Theorem

(i)

(iv)

The

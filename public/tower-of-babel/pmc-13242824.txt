Prognostic Impact of the Systemic Immune-Inflammation Index in Unresectable Hepatocellular Carcinoma Undergoing Second-Line Combination Therapy: A Multicenter Study
Authors: Xiaoshuang Chen, Xin Li, Yu Jiang, Qingyun Xie, Pei Zhang, Xia Zhou, Chunyan Wei, Ping Xie, Lei Cao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
IntroductionPatients with advanced hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) who progress after first-line systemic therapy face a poor prognosis. This study evaluated the prognostic value of the baseline systemic immune-inflammation index (SII) in patients with unresectable HCC receiving second-line regorafenib, immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs), and transarterial chemoembolization (TACE).MethodsThis multicenter, retrospective study included a cohort of 118 patients with unresectable HCC who experienced disease progression on first-line systemic treatment. To avoid the optimism bias and overfitting associated with data-derived binary cut-offs, the baseline SII was log-transformed and rigorously evaluated as a continuous variable. Survival outcomes, including overall survival (OS) and progression-free survival (PFS), were analyzed using multivariate Cox proportional hazards models adjusted for key clinical confounders.ResultsIn the multivariate analysis, the continuous log-transformed SII emerged as an independent prognostic factor. Each 1-unit increase in the log-SII was significantly associated with a 33% increased risk of disease progression (Adjusted Hazard Ratio [HR] = 1.33, 95% CI: 1.06–1.68, P = 0.015) and a 50% increased risk of overall mortality (Adjusted HR = 1.50, 95% CI: 1.08–2.09, P = 0.017). Subgroup analyses confirmed the consistency of these prognostic trends regardless of the regorafenib starting dose or the specific type of ICI administered. Notably, while baseline SII levels were not associated with objective response rates (ORR), they were significantly associated with an increased risk of severe treatment-related adverse events, underscoring its role as both a prognostic biomarker and a potential indicator of treatment tolerance.ConclusionBaseline SII, when evaluated as a continuous variable, is a statistically significant and non-invasive prognostic biomarker for patients with unresectable HCC receiving second-line triple therapy. While its discriminatory ability as a standalone tool is modest, it serves as a useful clinical parameter for risk stratification when integrated with established hepatic and tumor burden indices.

Introduction

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) remains a major global health burden, frequently diagnosed at advanced stages where curative interventions are precluded.1–3 For these patients, systemic therapy constitutes the cornerstone of disease management. In recent years, immunotherapy has fundamentally revolutionized the oncological landscape of HCC, establishing immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) as a leading therapeutic modality across various disease stages.4 Simultaneously, multi-kinase inhibitors, such as regorafenib and sorafenib, remain essential components of the systemic arsenal, with extensive gene-chemical interaction studies continually reaffirming their potential as multi-target therapeutic leads.5 For patients progressing on first-line therapies, the combination of regorafenib, ICIs, and transarterial chemoembolization (TACE) is increasingly adopted. However, the profound heterogeneity in clinical outcomes underscores a critical need for robust risk stratification.

Recent translational and bioinformatics research has identified numerous novel molecular prognostic biomarkers and therapeutic targets for HCC, including specific microRNA axes such as miR-3130-5p/FDX1,6 highly expressed proteins like PRDX6,7 and complex sets of cell cycle-related hub genes.5 While these molecular signatures provide profound mechanistic insights, their clinical translation is often hindered by the necessity for expensive, invasive, and specialized laboratory assays. Consequently, readily available serological and clinical markers—such as the neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR), platelet-to-lymphocyte ratio (PLR), C-reactive protein (CRP), and the albumin-bilirubin (ALBI) grade—are frequently utilized in routine practice. Among these, the Systemic Immune-Inflammation Index (SII) has emerged as a particularly comprehensive index. By integrating peripheral platelet, neutrophil, and lymphocyte counts, SII captures the complex host inflammatory-immune balance more effectively than NLR or PLR alone.8,9 Biologically, platelets can physically shield circulating tumor cells from immune clearance,10 neutrophils drive angiogenesis and remodel the tumor microenvironment,11 and lymphocytes act as central mediators of anti-tumor immunity.12 Recently, Teng et al13 successfully combined SII with the ALBI grade to refine the prognosis of regorafenib-treated HCC patients, highlighting the complementary clinical utility of merging systemic inflammation markers with hepatic reserve indices.

Despite its physiological rationale, the prognostic reliability of SII has yielded conflicting results in the literature. While several cohorts have confirmed its value,14,15 other studies—particularly those evaluating ICI monotherapy—have reported that SII fails to serve as an independent prognostic factor.16 This inconsistency likely stems from two critical methodological limitations in prior research. First, the widespread reliance on data-driven, artificial dichotomization (ie., identifying an “optimal” binary cut-off) fundamentally risks statistical overfitting and discards the biological gradient inherent in inflammatory responses. Second, the profound systemic inflammatory shifts induced by multi-modal combination therapies (TKI plus ICI and TACE) are vastly more complex than those induced by single-agent regimens, uniquely challenging the stability of baseline markers.

To address these methodological gaps and conceptual inconsistencies, this multicenter retrospective study aimed to rigorously evaluate the independent prognostic impact—rather than the predictive treatment response—of baseline SII in patients with unresectable HCC receiving second-line regorafenib-based triple therapy. By evaluating SII exclusively as a continuous variable and comprehensively adjusting for competing clinical confounders, we sought to establish a reliable, non-invasive, and mathematically robust reference for optimal risk stratification.

Materials and Methods

Patients and Study Design

This multicenter retrospective study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and approved by the Medical Ethics Committee of Sichuan Academy of Medical Sciences & Sichuan Provincial People’s Hospital (Approval No.: Ethics Review (Research) 2021 No. 40–2). To ensure maximum transparency and representativeness, the study enrolled patients across five tertiary referral centers in China: Sichuan Provincial People’s Hospital (n=30), Deyang People’s Hospital (n=23), Yibin No. 2 People’s Hospital (n=23), Leshan People’s Hospital (n=23), and The Affiliated Hospital of Southwest Medical University (n=19) (Supplementary Table S1). To minimize selection bias inherent in retrospective designs, standardized screening protocols and uniform data extraction templates were strictly implemented across all participating institutions.

We initially screened 210 patients with unresectable hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) who experienced documented radiological progression after first-line systemic therapy between January 2019 and December 2023. Key exclusion criteria were: (1) Child-Pugh class C liver function; (2) presence of other primary malignancies; (3) lack of complete baseline laboratory data for SII calculation, as missing components (platelet, neutrophil, or lymphocyte counts) fundamentally prevent the evaluation of the primary exposure variable without introducing severe imputation bias; and (4) loss to follow-up or incomplete survival records. After applying these criteria, a final cohort of 118 patients was enrolled for analysis. Independent radiologists, who were entirely blinded to the patients’ clinical data and SII status, performed all imaging reviews to ensure objective assessment.

Treatment Protocol

Prior to enrollment, all patients had experienced disease progression on first-line systemic therapies, which predominantly included targeted agents (eg., sorafenib or lenvatinib) administered either alone or in combination with immune checkpoint inhibitors (eg., atezolizumab plus bevacizumab). For the second-line triple regimen, regorafenib was administered orally. Recognizing the substantial variability in patient tolerance and baseline liver function, the regorafenib starting dose was individualized (80 mg, 120 mg, or 160 mg once daily) for three weeks, followed by a one-week rest period. Concurrent ICIs, primarily PD-1 inhibitors (camrelizumab, sintilimab, or tislelizumab), were administered intravenously at standard recommended doses every 3 weeks.

TACE was performed using a standardized and reproducible protocol across all centers. Following superselective catheterization of the tumor-feeding hepatic arteries via the Seldinger technique, an emulsion of chemotherapeutic agents (oxaliplatin or epirubicin) and lipiodol was injected. This was immediately followed by precise embolization using gelatin sponge particles or polyvinyl alcohol to achieve complete stasis of the targeted tumor arterial flow. Treatment continued until unacceptable toxicity, disease progression, or patient withdrawal.

Data Collection and SII Calculation

Demographic, clinical, and laboratory data were retrieved from electronic medical records. Baseline laboratory parameters—including complete blood counts, liver function tests (ALB, TBIL, ALT, AST), and serum alpha-fetoprotein (AFP)—were measured within one week prior to the initiation of second-line therapy. The systemic immune-inflammation index (SII) was calculated using the formula: SII = (P × N) / L, where P, N, and L represent peripheral platelet, neutrophil, and lymphocyte counts (109/L), respectively. Given the highly skewed distribution of raw SII values, the index was log-transformed (log-SII) for all subsequent survival modeling to robustly satisfy the linearity assumption of the Cox proportional hazards models. Tumor staging was determined according to the Barcelona Clinic Liver Cancer (BCLC) system.

Follow-Up and Endpoints

Patients were monitored every 4 to 8 weeks through physical examination, laboratory testing, and contrast-enhanced imaging (CT or MRI). The primary endpoint was overall survival (OS), defined as the interval from the initiation of second-line therapy to death from any cause or the last follow-up. The secondary endpoint was progression-free survival (PFS), defined as the time from treatment initiation to disease progression or death. Tumor response was evaluated utilizing the modified Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (mRECIST).

Statistical Analysis

Continuous variables are expressed as median (interquartile range, IQR) and were compared using the Mann–Whitney U-test. Categorical variables are presented as frequencies (percentages) and were compared utilizing the Chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test. To strictly avoid optimism bias and the risk of overfitting associated with data-derived cut-off values, the SII was exclusively treated as a continuous variable in all survival analyses. Survival distributions across representative baseline SII percentiles were illustrated using predicted survival curves derived from the partial effects of the Cox models.

Univariate and multivariate Cox proportional hazards regression models were employed to identify independent prognostic factors. To comprehensively address potential treatment heterogeneity and rigorous clinical adjustment, the multivariate model incorporated the log-transformed SII alongside a prespecified, robust set of clinical covariates (including continuous age, PVTT, BCLC stage, ALBI grade, prior ICI use, and log-transformed AFP). Furthermore, extensive subgroup analyses and interaction tests were conducted—and visualized via forest plots—to evaluate the prognostic consistency of log-SII across diverse clinical strata, explicitly addressing variations in regorafenib dose and specific ICI types. All statistical analyses were performed using Python (version 3.9) with the lifelines (version 0.27.1) and pandas packages. A two-sided P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Prognostic Value of Continuous SII for PFS and OS

The median progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS) for the total cohort were 7.50 months and 21.34 months, respectively. When evaluated as a continuous variable in the Cox proportional hazards models, a higher log-transformed SII was associated with decreased survival probabilities. The predicted survival curves (Figure 2A and B) illustrate this continuous risk trajectory across the 25th, 50th, and 75th percentiles of baseline SII, demonstrating a gradual decline in both PFS and OS probabilities as the SII level increases. Subgroup Analyses

Subgroup analyses were conducted to evaluate the consistency of the log-transformed SII as a prognostic indicator across various clinical strata (Figure 3A and B). The hazard ratios for log-SII consistently remained above 1.0 across the majority of the predefined subgroups. To formally assess potential heterogeneity, interaction tests were systematically performed. The prognostic value of log-SII demonstrated statistical consistency across most clinical subgroups, including gender, BCLC stage, PVTT, ALBI grade, tumor size, prior ICI use, and ICI type (all Pinteraction > 0.05). However, statistically significant interaction effects were detected for age in relation to PFS (Pinteraction = 0.035), and for the regorafenib starting dose regarding both PFS (Pinteraction = 0.012) and OS (Pinteraction = 0.005). These findings indicate that while the adverse prognostic trend of elevated baseline SII remains broad, its clinical impact is significantly modulated by patient age and targeted therapy dosage. Discussion

In this multicenter retrospective study, we demonstrated that the baseline systemic immune-inflammation index (SII), when evaluated as a continuous variable, serves as an independent prognostic marker for patients with unresectable HCC undergoing second-line triple therapy with regorafenib, ICIs, and TACE. Our data indicate that higher baseline log-transformed SII levels are robustly associated with accelerated deterioration in both overall survival (OS) and progression-free survival (PFS). By integrating peripheral markers of inflammation and immunity, the SII provides a readily accessible reference for risk stratification in patients whose disease has progressed on first-line therapies.17,18

A major methodological strength of our revised analysis is the strict evaluation of SII as a continuous variable rather than relying on binary categorization. As highlighted in recent biostatistical critiques, many previous biomarker studies have utilized data-driven approaches—such as maximally selected rank statistics—to determine “optimal” cut-off values. While this method maximizes the separation between survival curves, it introduces substantial optimism bias, artificially inflates Type I error rates, and severely limits the external validity of the findings. By employing log-transformed continuous SII in our Cox proportional hazards models, we effectively avoided the severe overfitting risks associated with artificial dichotomization. This approach not only provides a more accurate reflection of the dose-response relationship between systemic inflammation and survival but also ensures the statistical robustness of our conclusions.

Of particular clinical interest is the dissociation observed between systemic inflammation and radiographic tumor response. While the continuous SII was strongly associated with survival durations, logistic regression analysis demonstrated no significant correlation between the log-transformed SII and objective response (OR = 0.999, 95% CI: 0.645–1.545, P = 0.995). This distinction is crucial: it underscores that SII acts strictly as a prognostic biomarker reflecting aggressive underlying tumor biology and the risk of early mortality, rather than a predictive biomarker for specific treatment efficacy or tumor shrinkage.19,20 Patients with pronounced systemic inflammation may achieve initial radiographic stabilization, but their pro-tumorigenic immune milieu predisposes them to rapid subsequent progression.21

Furthermore, evaluating the safety profile is essential for intensive combination regimens. Our analysis identified a significant association between baseline log-SII and the occurrence of Grade 3/4 toxicities (OR = 1.622, P = 0.039). This finding implies that a severe baseline systemic inflammatory burden may diminish physiological tolerance to the regorafenib-based triple therapy. Therefore, for patients presenting with elevated baseline SII, more vigilant monitoring and proactive dose individualized adjustments are strongly recommended to mitigate the risk of severe adverse events.

Consistent with the clinical necessity for dose personalization, our formal interaction analyses revealed significant cross-strata heterogeneity precisely regarding regorafenib dosage (PFS Pinteraction = 0.012; OS Pinteraction = 0.005) and age (PFS Pinteraction = 0.035). This statistical interaction underscores that the prognostic relevance of baseline SII is deeply modulated by therapeutic intensity and age-related immune status, rather than being entirely static. Consequently, integrating baseline SII with subsequent treatment exposure status provides a more refined, dynamic risk stratification framework for personalized treatment planning.

The clinical implications of our findings are particularly relevant in the context of sequential systemic therapy. For HCC patients progressing on initial systemic treatments, the administration of second-line ICIs often represents an ICI rechallenge strategy.22 Recent evidence highlights that ICI rechallenge represents a rational and feasible therapeutic option for selected HCC patients, providing an opportunity to achieve additional clinical benefit after initial resistance or discontinuation.22 Mechanistic investigations suggest that renewed immune activation in this setting may result from tumor microenvironment remodeling, immunogenic cell death, and the reexpression of inhibitory checkpoints such as PD-L1 or CTLA-4. However, because clinical benefits from ICI rechallenge are more frequently observed in patients with preserved liver function, age <60 years, and lower tumor burden, optimal patient selection is paramount. Incorporating the continuous SII into predictive frameworks may help clinicians refine individualized ICI rechallenge strategies and identify patients who are most likely to maintain a durable immune response.22

It must be acknowledged, however, that while SII is a statistically significant prognostic factor, its discriminatory ability as a standalone biomarker is modest.16 Therefore, we do not advocate for the use of SII as an isolated, overwhelmingly powerful clinical tool. Instead, it should be viewed as a useful, readily accessible parameter that provides maximal clinical utility when integrated with established anatomical and hepatic reserve indices. As demonstrated in our multivariate analysis, portal vein tumor thrombosis (PVTT) remains a strong independent predictor of survival. Future prognostic models should aim to combine systemic inflammatory markers with factors such as ALBI grade and PVTT status to achieve superior risk stratification.13

Several limitations of this study warrant consideration. First, the retrospective design introduces inherent selection biases, despite our use of strict inclusion criteria and blinded imaging reviews. Second, the study was conducted in a region where HBV is the predominant etiology for HCC; thus, the findings require validation in Western cohorts where HCV and non-alcoholic steatohepatitis are more prevalent.1,3 Third, we did not perform a direct statistical comparison between SII and other established inflammatory markers, such as the neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR) or platelet-to-lymphocyte ratio (PLR), which remains an area for future investigation.11 Finally, our analysis was restricted to baseline SII, and evaluating the dynamic longitudinal changes of this index during treatment may offer additional prognostic insights.

In conclusion, baseline SII, evaluated as a continuous variable, is a statistically robust and independent prognostic biomarker for patients with unresectable HCC undergoing second-line regorafenib-based triple therapy. It provides valuable insights into patient survival trajectories and potential treatment tolerance. Future large-scale, prospective studies are warranted to validate these findings and to explore the integration of SII into comprehensive, multi-variable prognostic models for advanced HCC.

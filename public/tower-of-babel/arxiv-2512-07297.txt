Quantitative unique continuation for Neumann problem in planar C 1 , α C^{1,\alpha} domains
Yingying Cai, Jiuyi Zhu, Jinping Zhuge

Abstract
. In this paper, we study the quantitative unique continuation property of the second-order elliptic operators under the vanishing Neumann boundary condition over C 1 , α C^{1,\alpha} or convex domains in two dimensions. We establish the optimal estimates of the number of critical points, doubling index and the total length of level curves. The key idea is to reduce the Neumann problem to the Dirichlet problem, which has been understood better, by a classical duality between an A A -harmonic function and its stream function. Keywords: Unique continuation, Neumann problem, stream function, doubling index.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Motivation

The unique continuation properties (UCP) for elliptic equations is a fundamental topic in classical analysis, extensively explored over the past decades. Recent work has increasingly focused on the quantitative UCP, including the explicit bound of vanishing order, doubling index, three-ball (or three-sphere) inequality, propagation of smallness, and quantitative estimate of nodal sets and critical/singular sets, etc, particularly for the second-order elliptic equations with Lipschitz coefficients or the Laplacian eigenfunctions on Riemannian manifolds; see e.g.
[
16
,
14
,
26
,
35
,
28
,
27
,
29
]
and reference therein.
The Lipschitz regularity of the coefficients is a threshold for the UCP in three dimensions or higher, which may fail for general
C^{1-\varepsilon}
coefficients for any
\varepsilon>0

[
36
,
32
]
.

On the other hand, the analysis becomes more complex when studying the quantitative UCP near a nonsmooth boundary under a certain boundary condition (such as Dirichlet or Neumann boundary conditions). The quantitative (or qualitative) UCP may even fail for less regular boundaries; see counterexamples in
[
9
,
19
,
21
]
. We provide a brief review on the developments in this direction. Under the vanishing Dirichlet boundary condition, it is more or less well-known that the almost monotonicity of frequency function (a classical tool to study the UCP) centered on a boundary is valid if the boundary satisfies the so-called
A
-starshape condition (i.e.,
n\cdot A(x-x_{0})\geq 0
; see
[
43
]
for a precise definition). This condition can be achieved for free by a simple change of variables if the boundary is
C^{1,1}
(also see
[
2
]
for results in convex domains). Then
C^{1,\alpha}
(or
C^{1,{\rm Dini}}
) boundaries for the Dirichlet problem were studied in the pioneering work by Adolfsson-Escauriaza
[
1
]
(also see
[
24
]
)
by a refined change of variables that recovers
A
-starshape condition. In fact, it was shown that the doubling index and vanishing order are uniformly bounded on
C^{1,\alpha}
boundary with a vanishing Dirichlet boundary condition. For less regular domains, such as Lipschitz domains, the boundedness of doubling index is still unknown. In
[
26
, Theorem 2.3 and its Remark]
, Lin asked in a Lipschitz domain if a harmonic function is trivial if it vanishes in an open set of the boundary and its normal derivative vanishes in a subset of positive surface measure. Recently, for Laplace operator, Tolsa
[
39
]
showed that on
C^{1}
boundaries the doubling index is bounded a.e. with respect to the surface measure and thus confirmed Lin’s conjecture for
C^{1}
boundaries. Then Logunov-Malinnikova-Nadirashvili-Nazarov
[
30
]
established the optimal estimate of nodal sets with
C^{1}
boundaries (also see
[
15
]
for general elliptic operators). These results were finally extended in
[
43
,
12
]
to a unified class of quasiconvex Lipschitz domains that includes both
C^{1}
and convex domains as proper subsets (also see the results in convex domains
[
2
]
or polytopes
[
13
]
). We also refer to
[
20
,
21
,
22
]
for the quantitative estimate of critical/singular sets in
C^{1,{\rm Dini}}
domains and to
[
34
]
for quantitative results in convex domains.

For the Neumann boundary value problem, the study of UCP has been stuck on
C^{1,1}
boundaries since the work
[
1
]
.
In order to prove the monotonicity of frequency function or Carleman estimate under the vanishing Neumann boundary condition, we need the boundary to be exactly “
A
-flat” (i.e.,
n\cdot A(x-x_{0})=0
), which is much more restrictive than being
A
-starshape. For
C^{1,1}
boundaries, Adolfsson-Escauriaza
[
1
]
constructed a special transformation to “
A
-flatten” the boundary such that the monotonicity formula of frequency function is available. Unfortunately, this particular transformation crucially depends on the
C^{1,1}
regularity of the boundary. For boundaries less regular than
C^{1,1}
, a transformation
A
-flattening the boundary will completely destroy the Lipschitz regularity of coefficients and hence at present there is no known monotonicity formula or Carleman estimate available. We also mention some recent literature on the UCP for Neumann/Robin boundary value problems
[
41
,
11
,
25
]
, and all these works assume at least
C^{1,1}
boundaries.

In this paper, we will establish the quantitative UCP for elliptic equations subject to the vanishing Neumann boundary condition in the planar
C^{1,\alpha}
domains. To the best of our knowledge, this should be the first quantitative study of UCP for Neumann problem with a boundary less regular than
C^{1,1}
. Our main results are restricted in two dimensions as we will employ special techniques available only in two dimensions. The questions on the quantitative UCP in higher dimensions for Neumann problems in less regular domains deserve more explorations.

1.2.
Statement of main results

Consider the second-order elliptic operator defined by
\mathcal{L}=-\nabla\cdot A\nabla
with
A
being a positive-definite matrix in a planar domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
. A function
u\in H^{1}(\Omega)
is called
A
-harmonic in
\Omega
if
\mathcal{L}(u)=0
in the weak sense in
\Omega
. We say
u
satisfies the vanishing Neumann boundary condition on
\Gamma\subset\partial\Omega
if
\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}:=n\cdot A\nabla u=0
in the weak sense on
\Gamma
, where
n
is the outer normal to
\partial\Omega
.
In this paper, we assume that the coefficient matrix
A
satisfies the following standard assumptions:

•

Ellipticity: there exists
\Lambda\geq 1
such that

(1.1)

\Lambda^{-1}\mathbin{I}\leq A(x)\leq\Lambda\mathbin{I}\qquad\text{ for any }x\in{\Omega}.

•

Symmetry:

(1.2)

A^{T}=A.

•

C^{\alpha}
-Hölder continuous, i.e., for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
\gamma\geq 0

(1.3)

|A(x)-A(y)|\leq\gamma|x-y|^{\alpha}\qquad\text{for every }x,y\in\Omega.

It is well-known that the quasiconformal mapping is a standard tool to study the elliptic equation in two dimensions. Actually, even with bounded measurable coefficients, the strong unique continuation property (SUCP) holds at interior points and boundary points (reduced to the interior case); see e.g.
[
37
]
. The problem is that, without any additional regularity or structure, the complex analysis tools alone do not give useful quantitative estimates, due to the degenerate distortion of the quasiconformal mapping (see
[
7
]
and Subsection
2.1
for more details). Therefore, the generality of our elliptic operator
\mathcal{L}
prevents us from using complex analysis tools alone to circumvent the essential difficulties to obtain the (optimal) quantitative estimates.

Our first main result is a quantitative upper bound of the number of critical points, in terms of the doubling index that characterizes the growth rate of a solution.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let

satisfy (
1.1
) - (

\#(\mathcal{C}(u)\cap B_{\theta R})\leq C\log\frac{\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(B_{R}\cap\Omega)}}{\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(B_{\theta R}\cap\Omega)}},

The above estimate (

Nevertheless, the Hausdorff dimension of boundary critical set in this case is still zero; see

1.3.
Main ideas of proof

The main difficulty for the Neumann problem is that we are unable to directly establish the monotonicity formula for the Almgren-type frequency function if the boundary is merely
C^{1,\alpha}
.
The key idea of the paper (for all four theorems) is to reduce the Neumann boundary value problem to the Dirichlet boundary value problem by a classical duality between an
A
-harmonic function and its stream function (a generalization of the conjugate harmonic function), for which we have a better understanding from the previous literature. Precisely, for an
A
-harmonic function
u
in
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
, the stream function is given by

(1.8)

\nabla v=\mathcal{R}A\nabla u,

where
\mathcal{R}
is the matrix for a
90^{\circ}
counter-clockwise rotation in the plane:

\mathcal{R}=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}.

If
A
is symmetric, then
v
satisfies
\nabla\cdot(\widehat{A}\nabla v)=0
and
\widehat{A}=(\det A)^{-1}A
is also symmetric. Moreover, if
u
satisfies the vanishing Neumann boundary condition on
\partial\Omega\cap B_{R}
, then

n^{\perp}\cdot\nabla v=n\cdot A\nabla u=0,\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega\cap B_{R},

where
n^{\perp}=(n_{2},-n_{1})
and
n=(n_{1},n_{2})
. This means that the tangential derivative of
v
vanishes along the boundary
\partial\Omega\cap B_{R}
. Thus
v
remains constant on
\partial\Omega\cap B_{R}
. Without loss of generality, we may assume
v=0
on
\partial\Omega\cap B_{R}
. Therefore, the stream function
v
is
\widehat{A}
-harmonic and satisfies the vanishing Dirichlet boundary condition. The point is that, by the equation (
1.8
), any quantitative estimates we have for
v
, can be carried over from
\nabla v
to
\nabla u
without any essential loss. We emphasize that the duality of boundary conditions between a harmonic function or an incompressible flow and its stream function is classical with many applications in two dimensional PDEs in different contexts (e.g.
[
10
,
33
,
44
]
), particularly in fluid mechanics from which the term “stream function” arises (see e.g.
[
38
]
). Nevertheless, to the best of our knowledge, this is the first time it is used to study the UCP of the Neumann boundary value problem.

Critical points.
After reducing the Neumann boundary value problem to the Dirichlet boundary value problem, we then apply a geometric approach inspired by
[
6
]
to estimate the critical points, which is quite different from the ones in recent literature such as
[
35
,
42
,
22
]
, etc. The key idea of the geometric approach is the following observation: a (interior or boundary) point
x_{0}
is a critical point if and only if it is an intersection of at least two level curves. This fact holds on the boundary for the vanishing Dirichlet boundary condition, but not for the Neumann boundary condition. Fortunately, by (
1.8
), the critical points of
u
(including those on the boundary) are exactly the critical points of its stream function satisfying the vanishing Dirichlet boundary condition.

Doubling index.
Again, the almost monotonicity and the upper bound of doubling index of
u
are reduced to the doubling index of
v
through (
1.8
), while the latter has been studied in
[
1
]
for
C^{1,\alpha}
domains and in
[
43
]
for convex (quasiconvex) domains. Since (
1.8
) only shows the equivalence between gradients, a few transitions are needed to study the relation of the doubling index of
v
to that of
u
.

Length of level curves.
The estimate of the total length of level curves follows the approach recently developed in
[
30
]
and
[
43
]
, based on the almost monotonicity and the upper bound of doubling index. Note that estimate of level curves for
u
cannot be reduced to its stream function
v
, as (
1.8
) does not show the connection between their level curves. Instead, we have to adjust the argument, previously developed for the Dirichlet boundary value problem, to the Neumann boundary value problem, with some modifications. Meanwhile, we also need the result on the optimal estimate of interior nodal curves for the two dimensional elliptic equations in
[
4
]
.

Organizations.

In Section
2
, we recall the quasiconformal mapping applied to the two dimensional elliptic equations and use it to derive a property on the propagation of doubling index from the boundary to the interior. In Section
3
, we prove Theorem
1.1
on the number of critical points. In Section
4
, we establish the almost monotonicity and the upper bound of the doubling index, including the proof of Theorem
1.2
.
In Section
5
, we obtain the length estimate of level curves in Theorem
1.3
and its consequence on the Cauchy uniqueness for Neumann problem. In Section
6
, we prove Theorem
1.4
.

Preliminaries

The application of quasiconformal mapping to the second-order elliptic equation in a plane is classical; see e.g.

2.1.
Quasiconformal mapping

Let
A
be a
2\times 2
real-valued matrix given by

A=\begin{pmatrix}a_{11}&a_{12}\\
a_{21}&a_{22}\end{pmatrix}.

We assume that
A
is bounded measurable and satisfy the ellipticity condition. We do not need the regularity assumption of
A
in this subsection.
Let
u\in H^{1}(\Omega)
be an
A
-harmonic function (i.e.,
\nabla\cdot(A\nabla u)=0
) in
\Omega
. Let
v
be the stream function given by (
1.8
).

We identify the point
(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
as a complex point
z=x_{1}+ix_{2}
, and thus write
u(z)=u(x_{1},x_{2})
. Consider the complex function
f=u+iv
. Then it is straightforward to verify that
f
satisfies the complex Beltrami equation

(2.1)

\partial_{\bar{z}}f=\mu\partial_{z}f+\nu\overline{\partial_{z}f}\quad\text{ in }\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}\simeq\mathbb{R}^{2},

where
\partial_{\bar{z}}f=\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{x_{1}}f+i\partial_{x_{2}}f)
,
\partial_{z}f=\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{x_{1}}f-i\partial_{x_{2}}f)
, and

(2.2)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\mu=\frac{a_{22}-a_{11}-i(a_{12}+a_{21})}{\det(I+A)},\\
&\nu=\frac{1-\text{det}(A)+i(a_{12}-a_{21})}{\det(I+A)}.\end{aligned}\right.

By the ellipticity condition on
A
, we know that
\mu
and
\nu
are bounded measurable and

|\mu|+|\nu|\leq\kappa:=\frac{\Lambda-1}{\Lambda+1}<1.

Hence (
2.1
) can be written as
\partial_{\bar{z}}f=\tilde{\mu}\partial_{z}f
with
|\tilde{\mu}|\leq\kappa<1
. This implies that
f
is a quasiregular mapping.

As an important consequence, we can apply the Stoilow Factorization Theorem
[
7
]
to write
f
as

f(z)=g\circ\chi(z),

where
\chi:\Omega\to\Omega^{\prime}
is a
\Lambda
-quasiconformal mapping and
g
is a holomorphic function. Moreover, by taking the real part of
f
, we get

u=h\circ\chi,

where
h
is a harmonic function on
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

Particularly, we may apply the above argument to the
A
-harmonic functions on the disk
B_{1}
. In fact, by
[
8
]
, if
u
is
A
-harmonic in
B_{1}
, then there exists a
\Lambda
-quasiconformal mapping
\chi:B_{1}\to B_{1}
with
\chi(0)=0
and
\chi(1)=1

such that

, where

is harmonic in
B_{1}
. Moreover, for any

,

(2.3)

C^{-1}|z_{1}-z_{2}|^{1/\sigma}\leq|\chi(z_{1})-\chi(z_{2})|\leq C|z_{1}-z_{2}|^{\sigma},

where
C
and
\sigma\in(0,1)
are positive constants depending only on
\Lambda
. By rescaling, we can obtain similar result for
A
-harmonic functions in
B_{R}
for any

.

Let
w
be
A
-harmonic in
B_{R}
and
w=h\circ\chi
for some
\Lambda
-quasiconformal mapping
\chi:B_{R}\to B_{R}

\mathcal{B}_{r}=\{z\in B_{r}:|\chi(z)|<r\}.

By (
2.3

(2.4)

\mathcal{B}_{R}=B_{R}\quad\text{and}\quad B_{R(\frac{r}{C_{1}R})^{1/\sigma}}\subset\mathcal{B}_{r}\subset B_{R(\frac{C_{1}r}{R})^{\sigma}},\quad\text{for all }\,0<r<R,

C_{1}\geq 1
and

. Then by Hadamard’s three circle theorem, we have the following doubling property in quasi-balls.

Lemma 2.1

(

)

Let

-harmonic function in

. Then there exists

\frac{\lVert w\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{B}_{r})}}{\lVert w\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{B}_{r/2})}}\leq C\frac{\lVert w\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{B}_{R})}}{\lVert w\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{B}_{R/4})}},\quad\text{ for all }\;0<r\leq R.

As a corollary, by the distortion relation (

Corollary 2.2

Let

By (
2.4

(2.8)

B_{R(\frac{r_{1}}{C_{1}R})^{1/\sigma}}\subset\mathcal{B}_{r_{1}}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{B}_{2^{-m}r_{1}}\subset B_{R(\frac{C_{1}2^{-m}r_{1}}{R})^{\sigma}}.

Set

to be the smallest integer such that

(2.9)

R\Big(\frac{C_{1}2^{-m}r_{1}}{R}\Big)^{\sigma}\leq\frac{1}{2}r=\frac{1}{2}R\Big(\frac{r_{1}}{C_{1}R}\Big)^{1/\sigma}.

A simple calculation yields that

m=C+(\log 2)^{-1}(\sigma^{-2}-1)\log\frac{R}{r_{1}}\leq C\Big(1+\log\frac{R}{r}\Big).

Moreover, by (
2.7
), (
2.8
) and (
2.9
), we have

\frac{\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r})}}{\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r/2})}}\leq\bigg(C\frac{\lVert u\rVert_{L^{\infty}(B_{R})}}{\lVert u\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{B}_{R/4})}}\bigg)^{m}.

By noting that
B_{\theta R}\subset\mathcal{B}_{R/4}
for some
\theta\in(0,1/2)
and taking logarithmic to the last inequality, we obtain
(
2.6
) for
r<cR
. Finally, for
cR<r<R
, the desired estimate is trivial by choosing
\theta\leq c/2
.
∎

2.2.
Rigid form of doubling index

If
0\in\overline{\Omega}
and
A(0)=I
, then the doubling index centered at 0 of an
A
-harmonic function
u
is defined by

(2.10)

N_{u}(0,r)=\log\frac{\|u\|_{L^{2}(B_{2r}\cap\Omega)}^{2}}{\|u\|_{L^{2}(B_{r}\cap\Omega)}^{2}}.

The doubling index is a crucial notation in the study of unique continuation due to its monotonicity formula (for the interior case), provided that
A
is Lipschitz. Hence it is useful and convenient to introduce a rigid form of this quantity for general coefficient matrix
A(x)
which is invariant under an affine transformation; see e.g.
[
43
]
for details.
For any
x_{0}\in\overline{\Omega}
, we define

\displaystyle\mu(x_{0},y)

\displaystyle=\frac{(y-x_{0})\cdot A(x_{0})^{-1}A(y)A(x_{0})^{-1}(y-x_{0})}{(y-x_{0})\cdot A(x_{0})^{-1}(y-x_{0})},

\displaystyle E(x_{0},r)

\displaystyle=x_{0}+A^{\frac{1}{2}}(x_{0})(B_{r}(0)),

and

(2.11)

J_{u}(x_{0},r)=|\mathrm{det}A(x_{0})|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\int_{E(x_{0},r)\cap\Omega}\mu(x_{0},y)u^{2}dy.

The doubling index is defined by

N_{u}(x_{0},r)=\log\frac{J_{u}(x_{0},2r)}{J_{u}(x_{0},r)}.\\

Note that if
A(x_{0})=I
, then the above definition is reduced to (
2.10
).

Recall the well-known almost monotonicity of the interior doubling index, i.e., if
u
is
A
-harmonic in
E(x_{0},2r)
, then

\sup_{0<s<r}N_{u}(x_{0},s)\leq CN_{u}(x_{0},r).

Notice that the underlying domain
E_{r}(x_{0})=E(x_{0},r)
of integration in (
2.11
) is an ellipsoid that may vary from point to point for variable coefficients. Nevertheless, they are comparable to the Euclidean balls
B_{r}(x_{0})=B(x_{0},r)
in the sense that

B_{\Lambda^{-1/2}r}(x_{0})\subset E_{r}(x_{0})\subset B_{\Lambda^{1/2}r}(x_{0}).

Without loss of generality, in a small region we may even assume
\Lambda\in[1,2)
by an affine transformation and the Lipschitz continuity of
A(x)
.

2.3.
From boundary doubling index to interior doubling index

For the Neumann boundary value problem, it seems difficult to directly establish the doubling property for the interior points close to the boundary. Our strategy is to pass the estimate of doubling index from the boundary to the interior, by reducing the Neumann boundary value problem to the interior problem, which is possible since Corollary
2.2
does not rely on the regularity of the coefficients.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded Lipschitz domain.
Let
0\in\partial\Omega
and
u
be
A
-harmonic in
B_{R}\cap\Omega
, subject to the vanishing Neumann boundary condition. For
0<R<r_{0}
, there exists a bi-Lipschitz diffeomorphism
F^{-1}:B_{R}\cap\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}:=\{(x_{1},x_{2}):x_{2}>0\}
such that
F^{-1}(0)=0
and
F^{-1}(B_{R}\cap\partial\Omega)\subset\partial\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
. Moreover,
F
locally distorts distances by a bounded factor, i.e.,

C^{-1}|x-y|\leq|F^{-1}(x-y)|\leq C|x-y|

for all
x,y\in B_{R}\cap\Omega
. This particularly implies that

B_{C^{-1}r}\cap\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}\subset F^{-1}(B_{r}\cap\Omega)\subset B_{Cr}\cap\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}.

Define
\tilde{u}=u\circ F
. Then
\tilde{u}
is
\widetilde{A}
-harmonic in
B_{C^{-1}R}\cap\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}\subset F^{-1}(B_{R})\cap{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}
with

\widetilde{A}=|\det\mathcal{J}_{F}|\mathcal{J}_{F}^{-T}(A\circ F)\mathcal{J}_{F}^{-1},

where
\mathcal{J}_{F}
is the Jacobian of
F
. Moreover,
\tilde{u}
satisfies the vanishing Nuemman boundary condition on the flat boundary
F^{-1}(B_{R}\cap\partial\Omega)
.

Next, we extend the function
\tilde{u}
to the entire ball by an even reflection across the flat boundary
\{x_{2}=0\}
, i.e.,
\widetilde{u}(x_{1},-x_{2})=\widetilde{u}(x_{1},x_{2})
for
x_{2}<0
. Meanwhile, we extend the coefficient matrix
\widetilde{A}(x_{1},x_{2})
for
x_{2}<0
by

\widetilde{A}(x_{1},x_{2})=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix}\widetilde{A}(x_{1},-x_{2})\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix}.

With the extended coefficient matrix, still denoted by
\widetilde{A}
, the function
\tilde{u}
(now well-defined on the entire ball
B_{C^{-1}R}
) is
\widetilde{A}
-harmonic and
\widetilde{A}
is bounded measurable and satisfies the ellipticity condition. Observe that in general,
\widetilde{A}
may have a jump (thus discontinuous) across the flat interface
\{x_{2}=0\}
. Nevertheless, Corollary
2.2
applies to
\tilde{u}
, which leads to the following result.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a Lipschitz domain and
0\in\partial\Omega.
Suppose that
u
is
A
-harmonic in
B_{R}(0)\cap\Omega
subject to the vanishing Neumann boundary condition on
B_{R}(0)\cap\partial\Omega.
Then there exist
\theta\in(0,1/2),C>0
and
r_{0}>0
depending on
A
and
\Omega
such that if
0<R<r_{0}
, then for
r\in(0,\Lambda^{-1/2}R)
,

(2.12)

N_{u}(0,r)\leq C\Big(1+\log\frac{R}{r}\Big)\log\frac{\|u\|_{L^{2}(B_{R}\cap\Omega)}}{\|u\|_{L^{2}(B_{\theta R}\cap\Omega)}}.

Proof.

As discussed before, by setting
\tilde{u}=u\circ F
and reflection,
\tilde{u}
is
\widetilde{A}
-harmonic in
B_{C^{-1}R}
. By Corollary
2.2
, we have, for
0<r<\frac{1}{2}C^{-1}R
,

(2.13)

\log\frac{\|\tilde{u}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r})}}{\|\tilde{u}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r/2})}}\leq C\Big(1+\log\frac{R}{r}\Big)\Big(1+\log\frac{\|\tilde{u}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{C^{-1}R/2})}}{\|\tilde{u}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{\theta C^{-1}R/2})}}\Big).

In view of the bi-Lipschitz diffeomorphism
F
, we have

\|\tilde{u}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{C^{-1}r})}\leq\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r}\cap\Omega)}\leq\|\tilde{u}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{Cr})}.

Let
C_{1}
be a constant to be selected.
Hence by using (
2.13
) multiple times, we have

\displaystyle\log\frac{\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{C_{1}r}\cap\Omega)}}{\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r}\cap\Omega)}}

\displaystyle\leq\log\frac{\|\tilde{u}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{C_{1}Cr})}}{\|\tilde{u}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{C^{-1}r})}}\leq\sum_{j=0}^{K}\log\frac{\|\tilde{u}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{2^{j+1}C^{-1}r})}}{\|\tilde{u}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{2^{j}C^{-1}r})}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}\Big(1+\log\frac{R}{r}\Big)\Big(1+\log\frac{\|\tilde{u}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{C^{-1}R/2})}}{\|\tilde{u}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{\theta C^{-1}R/2})}}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}\Big(1+\log\frac{R}{r}\Big)\Big(1+\log\frac{\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R/2}\cap\Omega)}}{\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{\theta C^{-2}R/2}\cap\Omega)}}\Big),

where
K
is the smallest integer such that
2^{K+1}C^{-1}\geq CC_{1}
.

Now, by the elliptic regularity,

J_{u}(0,C_{1}\Lambda^{-1}r)\leq C\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{C_{1}r}\cap\Omega)},

and

J_{u}(0,2\Lambda r)\geq C^{-1}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r}\cap\Omega)},

Choosing
C_{1}=4\Lambda^{2}
and combining the previous estimates, we have

\displaystyle N_{u}(0,2\Lambda r)

\displaystyle=\log\frac{J_{u}(0,4\Lambda r)}{J_{u}(0,2\Lambda r)}\leq C+\log\frac{\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{C_{1}r}\cap\Omega)}}{\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r}\cap\Omega)}}

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(1+\log\frac{R}{r}\Big)\Big(1+\log\frac{\|u\|_{L^{2}(B_{R}\cap\Omega)}}{\|u\|_{L^{2}(B_{\theta C^{-3}R}\cap\Omega)}}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(1+\log\frac{R}{r}\Big)\log\frac{\|u\|_{L^{2}(B_{R}\cap\Omega)}}{\|u\|_{L^{2}(B_{\theta C^{-3}R}\cap\Omega)}},

where in the last inequality the constant 1 is absorbed due to Proposition
A.1
.
After relabeling
\theta C^{-3}
as
\theta
and
2\Lambda r
as
r
, we obtain the desired (
2.12
) for
0<r<cR
for some
c>0
. Finally, the estimate for the range
cR<r<\Lambda^{-1}R
is trivial by requiring
\theta\leq c\Lambda^{-1}
.
∎

The next proposition allows us to pass the boundary doubling index to the interior doubling index. This will be useful when we estimate the length of nodal curves quantitatively.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
\Omega,A
and
u
satisfy the same assumptions as Proposition
2.3
. In addition, assume
A
satisfies (
1.5
). Then for every
x\in B_{\theta R}\cap\Omega
with
d(x):={\rm dist}(x,\partial\Omega)\geq\frac{1}{2}|x|
(i.e.,
x
is in a nontangential cone of the origin) and
r\in(0,cR)
,

(2.14)

N_{u}(x,r)\leq C\Big(1+\log\frac{R}{\max{\{d(x),r\}}}\Big)\log\frac{\|u\|_{L^{2}(B_{R}\cap\Omega)}}{\|u\|_{L^{2}(B_{\theta R}\cap\Omega)}}.

Proof.

First note that
0\in\partial\Omega
and thus
\frac{1}{2}|x|\leq d(x)\leq|x|
. Now we consider three cases.

Case 1:

r\in(4d(x),cR)
. Then

E(0,\Lambda^{-1}r/4)\subset E(x,r)\subset E(x,2r)\subset E(0,4\Lambda r).

Thus, by Proposition
2.3
,

(2.15)

\displaystyle N_{u}(x,r)

\displaystyle\leq\log\frac{J_{u}(0,4\Lambda r)}{J_{u}(0,\Lambda^{-1}r/4)}\leq\sum_{j=0}^{3+[2\log_{2}\Lambda]}N_{u}(0,2^{j-2}\Lambda^{-1}r)

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(1+\log\frac{R}{r}\Big)\log\frac{\|u\|_{L^{2}(B_{R}\cap\Omega)}}{\|u\|_{L^{2}(B_{\theta R}\cap\Omega)}}.

Case 2:

r\in(\Lambda^{-1}d(x)/4,4d(x))
. For convenience, set
R_{x}=4d(x)
. In this case, we apply the argument in Proposition
2.3
, using Corollary
2.2
. In fact, by flatting the boundary and even reflection, as well as Lemma
2.1
, we can show, for any
r\in(\Lambda^{-1}R_{x}/16,R_{x})
,

\displaystyle N_{u}(x,r)

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(1+\log\frac{R_{x}}{r}\Big)\log\frac{\|u\|_{L^{2}(B_{\theta^{-1}\Lambda R_{x}}(x)\cap\Omega)}}{\|u\|_{L^{2}(B_{\Lambda R_{x}}(x)\cap\Omega)}}

\displaystyle\leq C\log\frac{\|u\|_{L^{2}(B_{\theta^{-1}\Lambda R_{x}}(x)\cap\Omega)}}{\|u\|_{L^{2}(B_{\Lambda R_{x}}(x)\cap\Omega)}}

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(1+\log\frac{R}{d(x)}\Big)\log\frac{\|u\|_{L^{2}(B_{R}\cap\Omega)}}{\|u\|_{L^{2}(B_{\theta R}\cap\Omega)}},

where we have used the result from Case 1 for a finite number of times for
4d(x)\leq r\leq 4\theta^{-1}\Lambda^{2}d(x)
.

Case 3:

r\in(0,\Lambda^{-1}d(x)/4)
. In this case, we have
E(x,4r)\subset\Omega
. As a consequence of the interior almost monotonicity of the doubling index for
r\in(0,\Lambda^{-1}d(x)/4)
(see
[
18
]
), we have

N_{u}(x,r)\leq CN_{u}(x,\Lambda^{-1}d(x)/2)\leq C\Big(1+\log\frac{R}{d(x)}\Big)\log\frac{\|u\|_{L^{2}(B_{R}\cap\Omega)}}{\|u\|_{L^{2}(B_{\theta R}\cap\Omega)}}.

where in the last inequality we used the result from the second case when
r=\Lambda^{-1}d(x)/2
. This ends the proof.
∎

Remark 2.5
.

We will not need the full version of the above proposition in this paper. Indeed, we only need the case when
R\simeq d(x)\simeq r
.

3.
Critical points

It has been well-known that in the two dimensional case, the critical set of an
A
-harmonic function consists of discrete points in the interior domains. We refer to the recent work
[
42
,
22
]
containing the estimate of the number of interior critical points for the two dimensional equations. On the other hand, the number of boundary critical points depends essentially on the boundary geometry. In particular, for a Lipschitz boundary, each convex tip will generate a critical point and therefore the number of critical points can be infinity even if the boundary is convex; also see the counterexample in
[
21
]
. Nevertheless, if the boundary is flat enough, say
C^{1,\alpha}
or even
C^{1,{\rm Dini}}
, then we can actually show the number of boundary critical points is finite.

In this paper, we will use an geometric approach to estimate the number the critical points in planar domains, which is inspired by
[
3
,
6
]
. The following lemma is a restatement of
[
3
, Remark 1.2]
for Lipschitz coefficients and
[
6
, Lemma 2.5]
(including its following remark), without referring to the notation of geometric index. We provide an independent and self-contained proof for the reader’s convenience.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
A
be
C^{\alpha}
-Hölder continuous. Let
u
be
A
-harmonic in
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Then
\nabla u(x)=0
for
x\in\Omega
if and only if
x
is an intersection of at least two level curves.

Proof.

The “if” part follows from the implicit function theorem.
We only need to prove the “only if” part. Without loss of generality, assume
u(0)=0,\nabla u(0)=0
and
A(0)=I
. It suffices to show that
0
is an intersection of at least two level curves.

Step 1
: Prove it for harmonic functions. Actually, if
u
is harmonic and
u(0)=0
,
\nabla u(0)=0
, then in polar coordinates,

u(x)=a_{m}r^{m}\cos(m\theta+\varphi_{m})+O(r^{m+1}),

for some
m\geq 2
and
a_{m}\neq 0
. Definitely, this implies that in a neighborhood of
0
, there are exactly
m
nodal curves intersecting at
0
.

Step 2
: A blow up argument for
A
-harmonic functions. It is well-known that in two dimensional case, we have the strong unique continuation at the interior points. This means that there exists a sequence of
r_{k}\to 0
such that
N_{u}(0,r_{k})\leq C
for some constant
C
. For otherwise, the vanishing order will be infinity.

Then define

u_{k}(x)=\frac{u(r_{k}x)}{\|u(r_{k}\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(B_{2})}}.

Since
u_{k}
is uniformly
C^{1,\alpha}
in
B_{3/2}
,
we can find a subsequence of
\{u_{k}\}
, still denoted by
\{u_{k}\}
, such that
u_{k}\to u_{\infty}
strongly in
C^{1}(B_{1})
and
u_{\infty}
is nontrivial. Moreover, it is straightforward to verify that
u_{\infty}(0)=0,\nabla u_{\infty}(0)=0
and
\Delta u_{\infty}=0
in
B_{1}
. By Step 1, we see that
u_{\infty}
has at least two level curves intersecting at
0
. This also implies that
u_{\infty}
at least has 4 disjoint nodal components, i.e., the subdomain of
B_{1}
bounded by the nodal curves and
\partial B_{1}
. The function
u_{\infty}
does not change sign on each nodal component. By the uniform convergence from
u_{k}\to u_{\infty}
, we see that for
k
sufficiently large,
u_{k}
also has at least 4 nodal components, which is equivalent to say that
u(x)
has at least 4 nodal components in
B_{r_{k}}
for
r_{k}
small enough. This implies that
0
must be an intersection of at least two nodal curves of
u
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
v
be the stream function of
u
in
\Omega\cap B_{R}
. Observe that (
1.8
) implies that
|\nabla u|\simeq|\nabla v|
, and the critical points of
u
are exactly the critical points of
v
. Thus it suffices to estimate the number of critical points of
v
in
\overline{\Omega}\cap B_{\theta R}
. The reason that we estimate the number of critical points of
v
instead of
u
is explained in Remark
3.2
.

Recall that
v
satisfies
\nabla\cdot(\widehat{A}\nabla v)=0
in
\Omega\cap B_{R}
with
\widehat{A}=(\det A)^{-1}A
and the vanishing Dirichlet boundary condition
v=0
on
\partial\Omega\cap B_{R}
. If
A
is
C^{\alpha}
-Hölder continuous (resp. Lipschitz), then
\widehat{A}
is also
C^{\alpha}
-Hölder continuous (resp. Lipschitz) with possibly different constants.

Let
B_{R,+}=B_{R}\cap\{x_{2}>0\}
. Since
\partial\Omega
is
C^{1,\alpha}
, there exists a
C^{1,\alpha}
diffeomorphism
F
such that
F:B_{R,+}(0)\to\Omega\cap B_{R}
such that
F(B_{R,+})\subset\Omega\cap B_{R/2}
and
F(B_{R}\cap\{x_{2}=0\})\subset\partial\Omega\cap B_{R}
. Let
\tilde{v}(x)=v\circ F(x)
for
x\in B_{R,+}
. Then
\tilde{v}
is
\widetilde{A}
-harmonic in
B_{R,+}
, where

\widetilde{A}=|\det\mathcal{J}_{F}|\mathcal{J}_{F}^{-T}(\widehat{A}\circ F)\mathcal{J}_{F}^{-1}.

Moreover,
\widetilde{A}
is
C^{\alpha}
continuous and
\tilde{v}=0
on the flat boundary
B_{R}\cap\{x_{2}=0\}
. Due to the bi-
C^{1,\alpha}
diffeomorphism,
\nabla v\circ F(x)=0
if and only if
\nabla\tilde{v}(x)=0
.

For the problem with vanishing Dirichlet boundary condition, we perform an odd reflection and thus define
\tilde{v}
as an
\widetilde{A}
-harmonic function in
B_{R}
, where
\widetilde{A}
is extended to
B_{R}
by

\widetilde{A}(x_{1},x_{2})=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix}\widetilde{A}(x_{1},-x_{2})\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix}\qquad\text{for }x\in B_{R,-}=B_{R}\cap\{x_{2}<0\}.

The key point is that
\tilde{v}
is still
C^{1,\alpha}
in
B_{R}
, though
\widetilde{A}
is not continuous on the interface
\{x_{2}=0\}
. This is because
\tilde{v}
is
C^{1,\alpha}
in
B_{R,+}
up to the boundary
B_{R}\cap\{x_{2}=0\}
with vanishing Dirichlet boundary condition, and an odd reflection does not generate a jump for
\nabla\tilde{v}
on the interface
\{x_{2}=0\}
.

Using quasiconformal mapping, we write
\tilde{v}=w\circ\chi
, where
w
is harmonic in
B_{R/2}
and
\chi:B_{R/2}\to B_{R/2}
satisfying
\chi(0)=0
and
\chi(\partial B_{R/2})=\partial B_{R/2}
. By the classical estimate of critical points for harmonic functions (see e.g.
[
17
]
), we have

\#\{\mathcal{C}(w)\cap B_{cR}\}\leq C\log\frac{\|w\|_{L^{2}(B_{R/2})}}{\|w\|_{L^{2}(B_{R/4})}}.

The critical points of
w
are known as geometric critical points of
\tilde{v}
; see the definition in
[
6
]
. By Lemma
3.1
, each interior critical point of
\tilde{v}
in
B_{\theta R}\setminus\{x_{2}=0\}
will generate a critical point of
w
in
B_{cR}
, where
\theta
is chosen such that
\chi(B_{\theta R})\subset B_{cR}
. In fact, if
x_{0}\in B_{\theta R,+}
is a critical point of
\tilde{v}
, Lemma
3.1
implies that
x_{0}
is an intersection of at least two level curves. Because the quasiconformal mapping
\chi
as a homeomorphism preserves the level curves,
\chi(x_{0})
is an intersection of at least two level curves of
w
. Then by Lemma
3.1
again,
\chi(x_{0})\in B_{cR}
is a critical point of
w
.

Next, we consider the critical points of
\tilde{v}
on the interface
B_{\theta R}\cap\{x_{2}=0\}
. Since
A
is discontinuous on the interface, we cannot apply Lemma
3.1
directly. Instead, we will use the fact that
\tilde{v}=0
on
\{x_{2}=0\}
, which is a level curve. Now if
\nabla\tilde{v}(x_{0})=0
for
x_{0}\in B_{\theta R}\cap\{x_{2}=0\}
, we claim that there exists another level curve touch
x_{0}
from
B_{\theta R,+}
. For otherwise,
\tilde{v}
does not change sign in a neighborhood of
x_{0}
above
\{x_{2}=0\}
. By the Hopf’s lemma, this will implies that
\partial_{2}\tilde{v}(x_{0})\neq 0
, leading to a contradiction. Consequently,
\tilde{v}
has two level curves intersecting at the critical point
x_{0}
on the interface. Then as before, by Lemma
3.1
,
\chi(x_{0})
is an intersection of two level curves of
w
which implies that
\chi(x_{0})
is a critical point of
w
in
B_{cR}
.

It follows that

\#\{\mathcal{C}(\tilde{v})\cap B_{\theta R}\}\leq\#\{\mathcal{C}(w)\cap B_{cR}\}.

Consequently, for some
\theta\in(0,1)
,

(3.1)

\#\{\mathcal{C}(u)\cap B_{\theta R}\}=\#\{\mathcal{C}(v)\cap B_{\theta R}\}\leq\#\{\mathcal{C}(\tilde{v})\cap B_{\theta_{1}R}\}\leq C\log\frac{\|w\|_{L^{2}(B_{R/2})}}{\|w\|_{L^{2}(B_{R/4})}}.

Finally, we only need to bound the right-hand side of (
3.1
) by a doubling quantity in terms of
u
. Actually,

\displaystyle|B_{R/2}|^{-1/2}\|w\|_{L^{2}(B_{R/2})}

\displaystyle\leq\|w\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R/2})}=\|\tilde{v}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R/2})}

\displaystyle\leq CR^{-1}\|\tilde{v}\|_{L^{2}(B_{R,+})}\leq C\|\nabla\tilde{v}\|_{L^{2}(B_{R,+})}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(F(B_{R,+}))}\leq C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(F(B_{R,+}))}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\cap B_{R})},

where we used the fact
|\nabla v|\simeq|\nabla u|
in
\Omega\cap B_{R}
.
Similarly, we can derive

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\cap B_{\theta R})}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\cap B_{\theta R})}\leq CR^{-1}\|\tilde{v}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\cap B_{2\theta R})}

\displaystyle\leq C|B_{R/4}|^{-1/2}\|w\|_{L^{2}(B_{R/4})},

for some
\theta\in(0,1)
small enough (depending only on
A
and
\Omega
). Thus,

\log\frac{\|w\|_{L^{2}(B_{R/2})}}{\|w\|_{L^{2}(B_{R/4})}}\leq C\log\frac{\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\cap B_{R})}}{\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\cap B_{\theta R})}}.

This, together with (
3.1
), finishes the proof.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

In this remark, we explain why we study the critical points of the stream function
v
, instead of the original
A
-harmonic function
u
.
The argument in the above proof does not work well for the boundary critical points of
u
with a vanishing Neumann boundary condition. The reason is that, for the Neumann boundary condition, we perform an even reflection and thus the gradient is discontinuous. Moreover, the boundary itself is not a level curve and there might be only one level curve connecting to a critical point on the boundary. Therefore such critical points are not geometric critical points.

Remark 3.3
.

If the boundary is merely Lipschitz, by argument in the above proof, we can still get the optimal bound of the number of interior critical points, namely,

\#(\{x\in\Omega\cap B_{\theta R}:\nabla u(x)=0\})\leq C\log\frac{\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\cap B_{R})}}{\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\cap B_{\theta R})}}.

However, the argument for the critical points on
\partial\Omega\cap B_{\theta R}
does not work any more, simply because of the failure of Hopf’s lemma on general Lipschitz boundaries. As we have mentioned earlier, in this case the critical points may accumulate on the boundary to an infinite number.

4.
Estimate of doubling index

4.1.
Doubling index of stream functions

In this section, assume either
\Omega
is
C^{1,\alpha}
or convex. Let
A
satisfy (
1.1
), (
1.2
) and (
1.5
).
Suppose
u
is a nontrivial
A
-harmonic function in
B_{R}(0)\cap\Omega
, satisfying the vanishing Neumann boundary condition on
B_{R}(0)\cap\partial\Omega
. The doubling index of the
A
-harmonic functions has been defined in Subsection
2.2
.

Recall that the stream function
v
satisfies the elliptic equation
-\nabla\cdot(\widehat{A}\nabla v)=0
in
\Omega\cap B_{R}
, subject to the vanishing Dirichlet boundary condition, where
\widehat{A}=(\det A)^{-1}A
. Then we can define the doubling index for
v
, similar to
u
, but with
A
replaced by
\widehat{A}
. Precisely, we define the notation
\widehat{\mu},\widehat{E},\widehat{J},\widehat{N}
as follows:

\displaystyle\widehat{\mu}(x_{0},y)

\displaystyle=\frac{(y-x_{0})\cdot\widehat{A}(x_{0})^{-1}\widehat{A}(y)\widehat{A}(x_{0})^{-1}(y-x_{0})}{(y-x_{0})\cdot\widehat{A}(x_{0})^{-1}(y-x_{0})},

\displaystyle\widehat{E}(x_{0},r)

\displaystyle=x_{0}+\widehat{A}^{\frac{1}{2}}(x_{0})(B_{r}(0)),

\displaystyle\widehat{J}_{v}(x_{0},r)

\displaystyle=|\mathrm{det}\widehat{A}(x_{0})|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\int_{\widehat{E}(x_{0},r)\cap\Omega}\widehat{\mu}(x_{0},y)v^{2}dy,

\displaystyle\widehat{N}_{v}(x_{0},r)

\displaystyle=\log\frac{\widehat{J}_{v}(x_{0},2r)}{\widehat{J}_{v}(x_{0},r)}.

Since
\widehat{A}
is just a scalar multiple of
A
, we have the following relations.

\displaystyle\mu(x_{0},y)=

\displaystyle\widehat{\mu}(x_{0},y),

\displaystyle E(x_{0},r)=

\displaystyle\widehat{E}(x_{0},(\det A(x_{0}))^{-1/2}r),

\displaystyle J_{u}(x_{0},r)=

\displaystyle\det A(x_{0})\widehat{J}_{u}(x_{0},(\det A(x_{0}))^{-1/2}r),

\displaystyle\widehat{N}_{u}(x_{0},r)=

\displaystyle N_{u}(x_{0},(\det A(x_{0}))^{-1/2}r).

The above relations show that the coefficient matrices
A
and
\widehat{A}
share the same form of the doubling index, up to a harmless dilation.

Finally we define the gradient doubling index by

N_{\nabla u}(x,r)=\log\frac{\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(E(x,2r)\cap\Omega)}}{\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(E(x,r)\cap\Omega)}}.

The definition for
N_{\nabla v}(x,r)
is defined in the same way.

4.2.
Almost monotonicity

In this subsection, we will establish the almost monotonicity of the boundary doubling index, which is useful in the estimate of level curves. We begin with two lemmas comparing the doubling index with gradient doubling index.

Lemma 4.1
.

For any
x\in\overline{\Omega}\cap B_{R}
with
E_{2r}(x)\subset B_{R}
,

(4.1)

\frac{\|u\|_{L^{2}(E_{2r}(x)\cap\Omega)}}{\|u\|_{L^{2}(E_{r}(x)\cap\Omega)}}\leq C\frac{\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(E_{2r}(x)\cap\Omega)}}{\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(E_{r/2}(x)\cap\Omega)}}.

Proof.

Fix
x\in\overline{\Omega}\cap B_{R}
such that
E_{2r}(x)\subset B_{R}
.
Using the triangle inequality and the elliptic regularity for Neumann boundary value problem, we have

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{2}(E_{2r}(x)\cap\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\|u-u(x)\|_{L^{2}(E_{2r}(x)\cap\Omega)}+|E_{2r}(x)\cap\Omega|^{1/2}|u(x)|

\displaystyle\leq\|u-u(x)\|_{L^{2}(E_{2r}(x)\cap\Omega)}+C\|u\|_{L^{2}(E_{r}(x)\cap\Omega)}.

It follows that

(4.2)

\frac{\|u\|_{L^{2}(E_{2r}(x)\cap\Omega)}}{\|u\|_{L^{2}(E_{r}(x)\cap\Omega)}}\leq\frac{\|u-u(x)\|_{L^{2}(E_{2r}(x)\cap\Omega)}}{\|u\|_{L^{2}(E_{r}(x)\cap\Omega)}}+C.

By the regularity of elliptic equation under Neumann boundary condition, we have

\|u-u(x)\|_{L^{\infty}(E_{r}(x)\cap\Omega)}\leq C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(E_{2r}(x)\cap\Omega)}.

Then, we apply the Poincaré inequality to get

\displaystyle\|u-u(x)\|_{L^{2}(E_{2r}(x)\cap\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\|u-\fint_{E_{r}(x)\cap\Omega}u\|_{L^{2}(E_{2r}(x)\cap\Omega)}+Cr|u(x)-\fint_{E_{r}(x)\cap\Omega}u|

\displaystyle\leq Cr\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(E_{2r}(x)\cap\Omega)}.

On the other hand, the Caccioppoli inequality implies that

\|u\|_{L^{2}(E_{r}(x)\cap\Omega)}\geq cr\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(E_{r/2}(x)\cap\Omega)}.

Taking the last two inequalities into (
4.2
), we obtain

\frac{\|u\|_{L^{2}(E_{2r}(x)\cap\Omega)}}{\|u\|_{L^{2}(E_{r}(x)\cap\Omega)}}\leq C\frac{\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(E_{2r}(x)\cap\Omega)}}{\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(E_{r/2}(x)\cap\Omega)}}+C\leq C\frac{\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(E_{2r}(x)\cap\Omega)}}{\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(E_{r/2}(x)\cap\Omega)}},

as desired, where in the last inequality, the additional constant
C
has been absorbed due to Proposition
A.2
.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
x\in\overline{\Omega}\cap B_{R}
with
E_{2r}(x)\subset B_{R}
, and
u(y)=0
for some
y\in\overline{\Omega}\cap E_{r/2}(x)
. Then,

(4.3)

\frac{\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(E_{2r}(x)\cap\Omega)}}{\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(E_{r}(x)\cap\Omega)}}\leq C\frac{\|u\|_{L^{2}(E_{4r}(x)\cap\Omega)}}{\|u\|_{L^{2}(E_{r}(x)\cap\Omega)}}.

Proof.

This is similar to the proof of Lemma
4.1
by the Caccioppoli inequality and Poincaré inequality.
∎

Now recall the almost monotonicity formula for the elliptic equation with vanishing Dirichlet boundary condition. Particular, consider the stream function
v
, which is
\widehat{A}
-harmonic in
\Omega\cap B_{R}
and
v=0
on
\partial\Omega\cap B_{R}
. By
[

(4.4)

\sup_{0<s<r}\widehat{N}_{v}(x,s)\leq C\widehat{N}_{v}(x,r),

whenever

and
E(x,2r)\subset B_{R}
. By examining the proof in
[

, we notice that the doubling index may be replaced by any
\lambda

Also, notice that the factor 2 in (

The next proposition establishes the almost monotonicity of
\nabla v
, which yields the almost monotonicity of
\nabla u
.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
v
be the stream function of
u
with
v=0
on

and

\sup_{0<s<r}N_{\nabla v}(x,s)\leq CN_{\nabla v}(x,r).

As a corollary, we have

(4.5)

\sup_{0<s<r}N_{\nabla u}(x,s)\leq CN_{\nabla u}(x,r).

Proof.

Let
s\leq r/2
. Then by Lemma
4.2

\sqrt{2}

N_{\nabla v}(x,s)\leq C(\widehat{N}_{v}(x,2s)+\widehat{N}_{v}(x,s))\leq C\log\frac{\widehat{J}_{v}(x,2r)}{\widehat{J}_{v}(x,\sqrt{2}r)}.

By Lemma

\sup_{0<s\leq r/2}N_{\nabla v}(x,s)\leq CN_{\nabla v}(x,r).

Finally, the estimate for the range

∎

The almost monotonicity of

Proposition 4.4

Let

Length of level curves

Throughout this section, we assume

Our aim is to prove an upper bound for the total length of level curves in terms of the doubling index. Since the level set

is exactly the nodal curve of

(i.e.,

), which is still

-harmonic, it is sufficient to consider the nodal curve of

.
The proof follows the line of

5.1.
Domain decomposition and maximal doubling index

Define a standard rectangle by
Q_{0}=[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})\times[-8,8).
Throughout this section, all the boundary rectangles considered are translated and rescaled versions of
Q_{0}
, centered on the boundary
\partial\Omega
. Let
\pi(Q)
be the vertical projection of
Q
on
\mathbb{R}.
For example,
\pi(Q_{0})=[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}).

Denote the side length of
Q_{0}
and
\pi(Q_{0})
by
\ell_{Q_{0}}=\ell(\pi(Q_{0}))=1.

Without loss of generality, we assume
\partial\Omega
is locally given by
x_{2}=\phi(x_{1})
with
\phi(0)=0
,
|\phi^{\prime}|\leq 1
and
\Omega=\{(x_{1},x_{2}):x_{2}>\phi(x_{1})\}
.
Let
Q
be a boundary rectangle centered at
x_{Q}\in\partial\Omega
and
Q^{\prime}=\pi(Q)
.
We partition
Q^{\prime}
into
2^{k}
small equal intervals
\left\{q^{\prime}\right\}
with side length
\ell(q^{\prime})=2^{-k}\ell_{Q}
. For each
q^{\prime}
, there is a unique boundary rectangle
q
such that
\pi(q)=q^{\prime}
and the center of
q
lies on
\partial\Omega\cap Q.
Furthermore, we cover
\Omega\cap\pi^{-1}(q^{\prime})\cap Q\setminus q
by at most
8\times 2^{k}
disjoint squares with side length
2^{-k}\ell_{Q}
. Let
\mathbb{B}_{k}(Q)
be the collection of boundary rectangles and
\mathbb{I}_{k}(Q)
be the collection of interior squares that intersect with
\Omega\cap Q
.

There is a simple fact about the above decomposition :

\min_{q\in\mathbb{I}_{k}(Q)}\text{dist}(q,Q\cap\partial\Omega)\geq 2\ell_{q}.

Next, we define the maximal doubling index of
u
. Let
\theta
be as given in Theorem
1.2

and
S
be the smallest integer such that
2^{S}>\theta^{-1}
.

Let
Q
be a boundary rectangle contained in
B_{\theta R}
. Denote by
\ell_{Q}
the side length of

. We define the maximal doubling index of

in

by

(5.1)

N_{u}^{\partial}(Q)=\sup_{x\in Q\cap\partial\Omega,0<r<\ell_{Q}}\sum_{i=0}^{S-1}N_{u}(x,2^{i}r)=\sup_{x\in Q\cap\partial\Omega,0<r<\ell_{Q}}\log\frac{\int_{E(x,2^{S}r)\cap\Omega}\mu u^{2}}{\int_{E(x,r)\cap\Omega}\mu u^{2}}.

Clearly, if
q\in\mathbb{B}_{k}(Q)
, then
N_{u}^{\partial}(q)\leq N_{u}^{\partial}(Q)
.

Let
Q
be an interior square. Denote by
\ell_{Q}
the side length of
Q
. We define the interior maximal doubling index of

in

N_{u}^{*}(Q)=\sup_{x\in Q,0<r<\ell_{Q}}N_{u}(x,r).

5.2.
Drop of doubling index versus absence of nodal curves

We restrict ourselves in
B_{\theta R}\cap\Omega
. Let

be a boundary rectangle centered at a point on
B_{\theta R}\cap\partial\Omega
and contained in
B_{\theta R}
.
The following property of boundary maximal doubling index is in the same spirit of
[

for Dirichlet problem.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let

and

be given as above. Then there exists

such that if

N_{u}^{\partial}(Q)>N_{0},\quad\text{and}\quad k>k_{0},

then we can find at least one

such that either
N_{u}^{\partial}(q)\leq\frac{1}{2}N_{u}^{\partial}(Q)
or
Z(u)\cap q
is empty.

A basic tool for the proof of Proposition
5.1
is the following version of quantitative Cauchy uniqueness proved in
[
43
]
; also see earlier versions in
[
30
,
39
]
.

Lemma 5.2
.

[
43
, Lemma 4.1]

Let
\Omega
be a Lipschitz domain and
0\in\partial\Omega.
There exists
0<\tau<1
such that if
u
is an
A
-harmonic function in
B_{1}\cap\Omega
satisfying
\lVert u\rVert_{L^{2}(B_{1}\cap\Omega)}=1
and
|u|+|n\cdot A\nabla u|\leq\epsilon\leq 1
on
B_{1}\cap\partial\Omega,
then

\lVert u\rVert_{L^{\infty}(B_{1/2}\cap\Omega)}\leq C\epsilon^{\tau}.

Proof of Proposition
5.1
.

We prove by contradiction. Assume for any
q\in\mathbb{B}_{k}(Q),N_{u}^{*}(q)>\frac{1}{2}N
and
q\cap Z(u)\neq\varnothing.

Let
x_{Q}
be the center of
Q
on
\partial\Omega\cap B_{\theta R}
.
Put
N=N_{u}^{\partial}(Q)
and
M=J^{\frac{1}{2}}_{u}(x_{Q},2^{S-2}\ell_{Q})=(\int_{E(x_{Q},2^{S-2}\ell_{Q})\cap\Omega}\mu u^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}.
Recall that our assumption
\Lambda\in[1,2)
implies
B(x,\sqrt{2}r)\supset E(x,r)\supset B(x,\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{2}r)
.

By Proposition
4.4
, for any
q\in\mathbb{B}_{k}(Q),
we have

N_{u}^{\partial}(q)\leq C\sup_{x\in q\cap\partial\Omega}\sum_{i=0}^{S-1}N_{u}(x,2^{i}\ell_{q})\leq CS\sup_{x\in q\cap\partial\Omega}N_{u}(x,2^{S-1}\ell_{q}).

It follows that for any
q\in\mathbb{B}_{k}(Q),
there exists
x_{q}\in q\,\cap\partial\Omega
such that

CSN_{u}(x_{q},2^{S-1}\ell_{q})\geq N_{u}^{\partial}(q)\geq\frac{N}{2}.

Therefore, by Proposition
4.4
again and for
k_{0}
sufficiently large, we deduce that

(5.2)

\displaystyle\log\frac{J_{u}(x_{q},2^{S-3}\ell_{Q})}{J_{u}(x_{q},2^{S-1}\ell_{q})}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i=0}^{k-3}N_{u}(x_{q},2^{i+S-1}\ell_{q})

\displaystyle\geq C^{-1}(k-2)N_{u}(x_{q},2^{S-1}\ell_{q})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{(k-2)N}{2CS}\geq c_{1}kN,

for some constant
c_{1}>0
.

Since
J_{u}(x_{q},2^{S-3}\ell_{Q})\leq J_{u}(x_{Q},2^{S-2}\ell_{Q})\leq M^{2}
, we obtain from (
5.2
) that

J_{u}(x_{q},2^{S-1}\ell_{q})\leq e^{-c_{1}kN}M^{2}.

Hence, by the elliptic regularity for the Neumman boundary value problem, we obtain from the last inequality that

\sup_{q\cap\partial\Omega}|u|\leq\frac{C}{\ell_{q}}J_{u}(x_{q},2^{S-1}\ell_{q})^{1/2}\leq C\ell_{q}^{-1}Me^{-c_{1}kN/2}.

As this estimate holds for every
q\in\mathbb{B}_{k}(Q)
, we then have

\sup_{Q\cap\partial\Omega}|u|\leq C\ell_{Q}^{-1}2^{k}Me^{-c_{1}kN/2},

where we also used the fact
\ell_{Q}=2^{k}\ell_{q}.

Now, applying Lemma
5.2
to
u
with vanishing Neumann boundary conditions in
B_{\frac{1}{2}\ell_{Q}}(x_{Q})
,
we can obtain that

\sup_{E(x_{Q},\frac{1}{4}\ell_{Q})\cap\Omega}|u|\leq CM\ell_{Q}^{-1}2^{k\tau}e^{-\tau c_{1}kN/2},

which yields

(5.3)

J_{u}(x_{Q},\frac{1}{4}\ell_{Q})\leq CM^{2}2^{2k\tau}e^{-\tau c_{1}kN}.

On the other hand, by the definition of the boundary maximal doubling index of
Q
, and taking
r=\frac{1}{4}\ell_{Q}
, we get

\log\frac{J_{u}(x_{Q},2^{S-2}\ell_{Q})}{J_{u}(x_{Q},\frac{1}{4}\ell_{Q})}=\log\frac{M^{2}}{J_{u}(x_{Q},\frac{1}{4}\ell_{Q})}\leq N_{u}^{\partial}(Q)=N,

which results in

(5.4)

J_{u}(x_{Q},\frac{1}{4}\ell_{Q})\geq M^{2}e^{-N}.

Combining (
5.3
) and (
5.4
), we obtain

5.3.
Total length of level curves

Next, we derive
the sharp interior estimate of nodal curves based on

.

Lemma 5.4
.

If

is a square and

is

-harmonic in

\mathcal{H}^{1}(Z(u)\cap q)\leq CN_{u}^{*}(q)\ell_{q}.

Proof.

This estimate is a corollary of
[

. In order to apply the theorem, we convert the equation into a form satisfying the desired assumptions. Since

is Lipschitz, we can write the equation for

\text{tr}(A\nabla^{2}u)+\text{div}(A)\cdot\nabla u=0.

Dividing the equation by
\sqrt{\det(A)}
and letting
A_{1}=A/\sqrt{\det(A)}
, we get

\text{tr}(A_{1}\nabla^{2}u)+b\cdot\nabla u=0,

where
b=\text{div}(A)/\sqrt{\det(A)}
. Now clearly
\det(A_{1})=1
and
b\in L^{\infty}(4\Lambda q)
.

Hence, by
[
4
, Theorem 1]
,

(5.9)

\mathcal{H}^{1}(Z(u)\cap q)\leq C\ell_{q}\Big(1+\log\frac{\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(\frac{7}{4}q)}}{\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(\frac{5}{4}q)}}\Big).

Without loss of generality, let us assume
Z(u)\cap q
is not empty, otherwise there is nothing to prove. This means that there exists
x_{0}\in q
such that
u(x_{0})=0
.
By the elliptic regularity estimate, we have

(5.10)

\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(\frac{7}{4}q)}\leq C\ell_{q}\bigg(\fint_{2q}u^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}.

Using the fact

, the elliptic regularity and Poincaré inequality, we have

(5.11)

\bigg(\fint_{q}u^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\leq C\ell_{q}^{-1}\bigg(\fint_{\frac{5}{4}q}|\nabla u|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\leq C\ell_{q}^{-1}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(\frac{5}{4}q)}.

Inserting (
5.10
) and (
5.11
) into (

), we obtain

\mathcal{H}^{1}(Z(u)\cap q)\leq C\ell_{q}\Big(1+\log\frac{\|u\|_{L^{2}(2q)}}{\|u\|_{L^{2}(q)}}\Big)\leq C\ell_{q}(1+N_{u}^{*}(q))\leq C\ell_{q}N_{u}^{*}(q),

in view of the definition of

.
∎

The following is the estimate of the number of level points on

Proof.

First of all, by Theorem

Appendix A
A universal lower bound of doubling index

We show that if

satisfying the Neumann boundary condition on

and

Proposition A.1
.

Let
\Omega
be

and

be

-harmonic in

(depending only on

and

Proof.

It suffices to prove (

).
Without loss of generality, assume

. We first prove

\int_{B_{r}\cap\Omega}u^{2}\leq C\int_{(B_{2r}\setminus B_{r})\cap\Omega}u^{2}.

By the maximal principle, the maximum of

is attained on

It follows that

where in the last inequality, we have used the local

A.2
).

Note that (
A.2
) yields

\int_{B_{r}\cap\Omega}\mu u^{2}\leq C\int_{(B_{2r}\setminus B_{r})\cap\Omega}\mu u^{2}.

Finally, by a hole-filling argument, we have

J(0,r)\leq\frac{C}{1+C}J(0,2r)=(1-\delta)J(0,2r),

∎

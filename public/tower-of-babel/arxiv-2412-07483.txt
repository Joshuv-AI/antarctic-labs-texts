Lines, twisted cubics on cubic fourfolds, and the monodromy of the Voisin map
Franco Giovenzana, Luca Giovenzana

Abstract




Abstract.
For a general cubic fourfold
Y
with associated Fano variety of lines
F
, we show that the monodromy group of the finite degree 16 rational Voisin self-map
\psi\colon F\dashrightarrow F
is maximal.
To achieve this, we investigate the intriguing interplay between
\psi
and the fixed locus of the antisymplectic involution on the LLSvS variety
Z
, examined via the degree 6 Voisin map
F\times F\dashrightarrow Z
.

Keywords.
Irreducible symplectic varieties, cubic fourfolds

1. Introduction

Let
Y
be a smooth cubic fourfold and
F
be its (Fano) variety of lines. The rich geometry of these varieties has attracted great attention from the mathematical community for several reasons. Notably, the Fano variety
F
is one of the earliest examples of a locally complete family of projective hyperkähler manifolds. It is deformation equivalent to the Hilbert square of a K3 surface, and it has a natural polarisation of degree 6 and divisibility 2; see
[
BD85
]
. The duo
Y-F
between a Fano variety and a hyperkähler manifold has inspired numerous other constructions.

One distinguishing feature of
F
is the degree 16 rational Voisin self-map

\psi\colon F\dashrightarrow F
. Remarkably,

\psi
is the only known finite, non-birational self-map defined on a locally complete family of projective hyperkähler manifolds. Finite maps such as
\psi
are particularly subtle to study as, unlike birational maps, they cannot be detected through their action on the second cohomology group and lack a comprehensive classification result like the Hodge Torelli theorem that would allow precise control over their behaviour. Since its construction, see
[
Voi04
]
, this map has continued to attract significant interest in the mathematical community, as demonstrated by the numerous works on the subject
[
Ame09
,
GK23
,
GK25
,
GK24
]
.

In this paper we focus on the monodromy of the Voisin map
\psi
. The monodromy group is a discrete invariant that encodes the symmetries and intrinsic geometry of finite maps. Determining the monodromy group of branched coverings is a classical problem, originating with Jordan in the 1870s. This topic was revitalised by Harris, who provided a modern framework by proving that the monodromy and Galois groups coincide, and who developed tools to establish when the monodromy group is maximal. Significant progress was made by Vakil, who introduced innovative techniques to study monodromy in Schubert problems on Grassmannians; see
[
Vak06
]
.

Building on these developments, we take a step further by investigating linear spaces on a cubic hypersurface of dimension 4. Our main result shows that given a general cubic fourfold, the monodromy group of the Voisin map
\psi
is maximal, meaning that it is the full symmetric group on 16 elements.
To achieve this, we leverage another Voisin map involving twisted cubics on the cubic fourfold, which unveils a fascinating connection with the fixed locus of the natural anti-symplectic involution acting on the LLSvS variety.

We now introduce the necessary notation and recall some useful results in order to state our main theorems.
Lines on a cubic fourfold fall into two cases: for a general line
L
, the linear space

\Lambda_{L}:=\bigcapop\displaylimits_{p\in L}T_{p}Y

is 2-dimensional, and in this case the line is said to be of
type I
. For special lines, called of
type II
, the dimension of
\Lambda_{L}
is 3; see Proposition
2.1
and Definition
2.2
for more details.
For the general line we have
\Lambda_{L}\cap Y=2L+R
for a line
R\in F
, and one sets
\psi(L):=R
. This map has been studied in detail in
[
Ame09
]
, its indeterminacy locus consists of the lines of type
\operatorname{II}
, which for a general cubic fourfold form a smooth surface
S_{\operatorname{II}}
. Blowing up
S_{\operatorname{II}}
provides a resolution of the indeterminacy

\scriptstyle{{\widehat{\psi}}}
\textstyle{F\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\textstyle{F\hbox to0.0pt{.\hss}}

Gounelas and Kouvidakis recently computed that the restriction of
{\widehat{\psi}}
to the exceptional divisor

of the blow-up morphism, which coincides with the ramification divisor of
{\widehat{\psi}}
, is birational onto the image.

In Section
2
we study the map
{\widehat{\psi}}
by considering the projection of

from a general line

in

. Its resolution is a conic bundle over
\mathbb P^{3}
with discriminant locus a quintic surface

with exactly 16 nodes. These nodes correspond to the preimage of

under
{\widehat{\psi}}
.
Nodal quintic surfaces have been classically studied by Beauville and Catanese

Cat81
]
and more recently studied in
[

BFK+24
,

First we study the singularities of

Theorem 1.1

(see Theorem

)
.

Let

be a general line in the branch divisor of
{\widehat{\psi}}
. Then the quintic surface

has one singularity of type

, corresponding to the unique line

over

In Section

we shift our focus to the geometry of twisted cubics. With any smooth cubic fourfold
Y
not containing a plane, Lehn, Lehn, Sorger, and van Straten associated an

parametrising families of twisted cubics and their flat degenerations. The variety

is equipped with a natural antisymplectic involution

; see
[

]
.
Its fixed locus is a smooth Lagrangian submanifold with two connected components: one is isomorphic to the cubic fourfold

[

]
, and remains somewhat mysterious. Using the degree 6 rational map

We define the variety

(see Theorem

)
.

The variety

In Section

. Despite extensive study and numerous works concerning

, for example about its entropy, see
[

(see Theorem

.

The monodromy group of

is the entire symmetric group on

2. Nodal quintic surfaces with 16 nodes

In this section we recall basic facts about lines on cubic fourfolds and various properties of the Voisin map on
F
. Then we move on to studying degenerations of nodal quintic surfaces with an even set of 16 nodes naturally arising in this context.

Recall that the Gauss map associates to any point of the smooth cubic fourfold
Y\subset{\mathbb P}(V)
its projective tangent space:

\mathscr{G}\colon Y\longrightarrow{\mathbb P}\left(V^{\vee}\right),\quad P\longmapsto T_{P}Y\simeq{\mathbb P}^{4}.

Clemens and Griffiths distinguished lines on cubic hypersurfaces into two types. We recall here the definition for cubic fourfolds; see
[
CG72
]
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Given a line
L
on a smooth cubic fourfold
Y
, either the following equivalent conditions hold:

(1)

N_{L|Y}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{L}^{\oplus 2}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{L}(1)
,

(2)

\mathscr{G}|_{L}\colon L\to\mathscr{G}(L)
is
1:1
,

(3)

\mathscr{G}(L)
is a smooth conic in
{\mathbb P}(V^{\vee})
,

(4)

\bigcapop\displaylimits_{P\in L}T_{P}Y
is isomorphic to
{\mathbb P}^{2}
;

or the following equivalent conditions hold:

(5)

N_{L|Y}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{L}(-1)\oplus\mathcal{O}_{L}(1)^{\oplus 2}
,

(6)

\mathscr{G}|_{L}\colon L\to\mathscr{G}(L)
is
2:1
,

(7)

\mathscr{G}(L)
is a line in
{\mathbb P}(V^{\vee})
,

(8)

\bigcapop\displaylimits_{P\in L}T_{P}Y
is isomorphic to
{\mathbb P}^{3}
.

Definition 2.2

(
cf.

[
CG72
, Definition 6.6]
)
.

Given a line
L
, we say that
L
is a line of type I if the equivalent conditions (
1
)–(
4
) hold, whereas if (
5
)–(
8
) hold, we say that
L
is a line of type II. We set
\Lambda_{L}:=\cap_{P\in L}T_{P}Y
.

We record here elementary observations for future reference.

Remark 2.3
.

If
L\in F
is of type
\operatorname{II}
, then the line
\mathscr{G}(L)\subset{\mathbb P}(V^{\vee})
is the projective dual of
\Lambda_{L}\subset{\mathbb P}(V)

\mathscr{G}(L)
spans a
{\mathbb P}^{2}
in
{\mathbb P}(V^{\vee})

\Lambda_{L}
.

Remark 2.4

(
cf.

[
Huy23

Let

be a smooth cubic fourfold, and let

is of type II if and only if the partial derivatives
\partial_{0}f|_{L},\ldots,\partial_{5}f|_{L}
span a vector space of dimension 2 in

Voisin observed that if

is a line of type I, then

is a cubic curve, which, as it contains

and a residual line

Definition 2.5

The Voisin map

is the rational map defined by mapping a line

3. On a Lagrangian submanifold of
\boldsymbol{Z}
and singular cubic surfaces

In Section
3.1
we recall the construction of the hyperkähler variety

associated with any smooth cubic fourfold

Y
not containing a plane, often referred to as the LLSvS variety, together with some of its basic properties.
It carries the action of an antisymplectic involution which has been studied in
[

,
FMOS26
]
, whose fixed locus consists of two connected components, one isomorphic to the cubic fourfold, which we hence denote by
Y
, and a second one which we denote by

. The goal of this section is to study the less-understood variety

. In order to do that, in Section

we define a subvariety

which is related to

via a rational map

introduced by Voisin which we recall in Section

A key tool in our strategy is to reduce statements to the study of cubic surfaces

which are given by intersecting the cubic

with a projective space

and consider lines and twisted cubics on

. We do so in Section

This analysis provides the key tools to prove the main result of the section, Theorem

3.1. The LLSvS variety
\boldsymbol{Z}

Given a smooth cubic fourfold
Y
not containing any plane, Lehn, Lehn, Sorger, and van Straten constructed in
[

]
a hyperkähler variety

of dimension 8 which parametrises families of twisted cubics on

[

As any twisted cubic has

4. The monodromy group of
\boldsymbol{\psi}
is maximal

In this section we prove that the monodromy group of
{\widehat{\psi}}
is maximal. In order to do so, we use that the ramification of
{\widehat{\psi}}
is simple, as proven in Section
2
and the results of Section

about the variety

.

Given a generically finite dominant morphism
f\colon X\to Y
of degree
d
between irreducible varieties (necessarily of the same dimension), we obtain a degree d extension of function fields

, and taking the Galois closure
K/k(Y)
of this extension, we set
\operatorname{Gal}_{f}=\operatorname{Gal}(K/k(Y))
. This agrees with the usual monodromy group
\operatorname{Mon}_{f}
(see
[
Har79
,
SY25
]
), which is defined as the image in the symmetric group
S_{d}
of the group of deck transformations of the unramified (
i.e.
topological) cover
X\setminus f^{-1}(\operatorname{Branch}(f))\to U
, where
U:=Y\setminus\operatorname{Branch}(f)
.
Recall the following classical results for
f\colon X\to Y
and
U
as above.

Proposition 4.1

(
cf.

[
Har79
, Section II.3, p. 698]
)
.

If there exists a fibre of
f
with a unique point of ramification index
2
and all other points unramified, then
\operatorname{Mon}_{f}\subset S_{d}
contains a transposition.

Let
X^{(s)}_{U}
be the complement of the big diagonal in the fibre product of
X_{U}

s
-times with itself over
U
. In other words, the fibre of the natural morphism
X^{(s)}_{U}\to U
consists
of
s
distinct points in the fibre of
f
.

Lemma 4.2

(
cf.

[
SY25
, Proposition 2]
)
.

The variety
X^{(s)}_{U}
is irreducible if and only if
\operatorname{Mon}_{f}
is an s-transitive subgroup of
S_{d}
.

We now want to apply these results to the resolution of the Voisin map
\widehat{\psi}\colon\widehat{F}\to F
.
For the remainder of the section, let
Y
be a general cubic fourfold,
F
be its Fano variety of lines, and

be the complement of the branch locus of
\widehat{\psi}
.
We recall that

, introduced in Section
3.2
, is mapped onto

under the map
\varphi\colon F\times F\dashrightarrow Z
.
Thanks to Proposition
2.7
, we can readily apply Proposition
4.1
and deduce that the monodromy group
\operatorname{Mon}_{{\widehat{\psi}}}
contains a transposition.
In order to apply Lemma
4.2
, we are led to study the irreducibility of
{\widehat{F}}^{(2)}_{U}
, which is strictly related to the variety
P
. The rest of the section is then devoted to proving the following, which is the crucial ingredient to conclude the maximality of monodromy.

Proposition 4.3
.

The variety

is irreducible.

We postpone the proof and illustrate right away how to get the maximality of monodromy.

Theorem 4.4
.

The monodromy of
{\widehat{\psi}}
is maximal; i.e.
\operatorname{Mon}_{{\widehat{\psi}}}=S_{16}
.

Proof.

From Propositions
2.7
and
4.1
, we deduce that
\operatorname{Mon}_{{\widehat{\psi}}}
contains a transposition, and by Proposition
4.3
and Lemma

, the action of

is

-transitive. Thus
\operatorname{Mon}_{{\widehat{\psi}}}
contains all transpositions: indeed, up to renumbering, we can assume the transposition is
(1,2)\in\operatorname{Mon}_{{\widehat{\psi}}}

such that
\sigma(1)=a
and
\sigma(2)=b
for any

and

and we have

Hence
\operatorname{Mon}_{\widehat{\psi}}
contains any transposition and coincides with the entire symmetric group on 16 elements.
∎

Appendix. Singular cubic surfaces

Singular cubic surfaces of type
\boldsymbol{4A_{1}}

Under a suitable change of coordinate, every cubic surface with four singular points of type
A_{1}
can be expressed as zero locus of the polynomial

\displaystyle t_{0}t_{1}t_{2}+t_{0}t_{1}t_{3}+t_{0}t_{2}t_{3}+t_{1}t_{2}t_{3}.

Its singular locus consists of the coordinate points

\displaystyle p_{0}=(1:0:0:0),\quad p_{1}=(0:1:0:0),\quad p_{2}=(0:0:1:0),\quad p_{3}=(0:0:0:1).

Such a surface can be obtained as the anticanonical model of the blow-up of
\mathbb P^{2}
in a special configuration of points.
Indeed, let
\widetilde{S}
be the blow-up of
\mathbb P^{2}
in the six points given by the intersection of four general lines
L_{0}
,
L_{1}
,
L_{2}
,
L_{3}
.
Then the anticanonical linear system of

maps to a cubic surfaces
S
and contracts the strict transforms of the
L_{i}
to four singular
A_{1}
points.
The exceptional divisors map to lines in a tetrahedron-like arrangement; we refer to them as edges and denote by
E_{ij}
the line connecting the

and the
j^{\mathrm{th}}
coordinate points.
In particular, the edge
E_{ij}
is cut by the equations
(t_{k}=t_{l}=0)
, where
\{i,j,k,l\}=\{0,1,2,3\}
.
Besides the edges, there are three more lines on
S
joining opposite edges; we denote by

the line intersecting the edges
E_{ij}
and
E_{kl}
, where
\{i,j,k,l\}=\{0,1,2,3\}
. These are images of the strict transforms of lines through the blown-up points corresponding to the edges they intersect. For example, the line
J_{01,23}
is the zero locus
(t_{0}+t_{1}=t_{2}+t_{3}=0)
.

Generalised twisted cubics on singular cubic surfaces have been studied in
[
LLS+17
, Section 2]
. Any generalised twisted cubic on an integral normal cubic surface moves in a 2-dimensional linear system and can be described as the image of the pullback of a line in
\mathbb P^{2}
along the diagram of a determinantal representation of
S
. The determinantal representation for the Cayley cubic described above defines

, which contains, as reducible elements of the family, the union of all the edges passing through a fixed vertex of the tetrahedron:

K=[E_{ij}\cup E_{ik}\cup E_{il}]\in Z^{\prime}(S).

Proof of Lemma
3.5
.

The proof is elementary and can be checked directly by the definition of the Voisin map

. For example to compute

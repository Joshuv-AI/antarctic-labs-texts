Nonempty interior of pinned distance and tree sets
Tainara Borges, Benjamin Foster, Yumeng Ou, Eyvindur Palsson

Abstract
. For a compact set E ⊂ ℝ d E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d} , d ≥ 2 d\geq 2 , consider the pinned distance set Δ y ​ ( E ) = { | x − y | : x ∈ E } \Delta^{y}(E)=\{|x-y|:x\in E\} . Peres and Schlag showed that if the Hausdorff dimension of E E is bigger than d + 2 2 \frac{d+2}{2} with d ≥ 3 d\geq 3 , then there exists a point y ∈ E y\in E such that Δ y ​ ( E ) \Delta^{y}(E) has nonempty interior. In this paper we obtain the first non-trivial threshold for this problem in the plane, improving on the Peres–Schlag threshold when d = 3 d=3 , and we extend the results to trees using a novel induction argument.



1.
Introduction and Main results

The Falconer distance problem asks how large the Hausdorff dimension of a compact set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\geq 2
, needs to be to guarantee that its
distance set

\Delta(E)=\{|x-y|:x,y\in E\}

has positive Lebesgue measure. Falconer
[
11
]
showed through a construction that the dimensional threshold
\frac{d}{2}
is the best one can hope for and proved that
\textnormal{dim}_{\mathcal{H}}(E)>\frac{d}{2}+\frac{1}{2}
is sufficient. Bridging this gap, namely solving Falconer’s conjecture that the threshold
\frac{d}{2}
is sufficient, is one of the major open problems at the interface of harmonic analysis and geometric measure theory. Despite much progress in recent years, this conjecture still remains open.

The pinned Falconer distance problem similarly asks how large the Hausdorff dimension of a compact set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\geq 2
, needs to be to guarantee that there exists a point
y\in E
such that the
pinned distance set
at
y
,

\Delta^{y}(E)=\{|x-y|:x\in E\},

has positive Lebesgue measure. Any dimensional threshold for the pinned Falconer distance problem immediately implies that the same dimensional threshold works for the unpinned original problem. This pinned variant was first studied by Peres and Schlag
[
35
]
who obtained a sufficient threshold of
\textnormal{dim}_{\mathcal{H}}(E)>\frac{d}{2}+\frac{1}{2}
. The original unpinned Falconer distance problem saw improvements by Bourgain
[
4
]
, Wolff
[
40
]
and Erdoğan
[
10
]
that left the best threshold at
\textnormal{dim}_{\mathcal{H}}(E)>\frac{d}{2}+\frac{1}{3}
for a while. Liu
[
27
]
connected the main proof techniques for the pinned and unpinned variants and matched the threshold
\textnormal{dim}_{\mathcal{H}}(E)>\frac{d}{2}+\frac{1}{3}
for the pinned problem. Since Liu’s work, all the best thresholds for the pinned and unpinned variants have been the same. With multiple improvements recently
[
8
,
20
,
5
,
6
,
7
]
the best thresholds now stand at
\frac{5}{4}
in the plane and at
\frac{d}{2}+\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{8d+4}
when
d\geq 3
.

One of the motivations behind the Falconer distance problem is a classic result of Steinhaus
[
38
]
, which states that if a set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\geq 1
, has positive Lebesgue measure, then the set
E-E=\{x-y:x,y\in E\}
contains a neighborhood of the origin, which immediately implies that the distance set
\Delta(E)
has positive Lebesgue measure. One can view the Falconer distance problem as reducing the assumptions needed on the set
E
to obtain the conclusion on the distance set. In fact, from the Steinhaus result one not only concludes that the distance set has positive Lebesgue measure but also has nonempty interior. In that spirit, Mattila and Sjölin
[
28
]
showed that a compact set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\geq 2
, satisfying
\textnormal{dim}_{\mathcal{H}}(E)>\frac{d}{2}+\frac{1}{2}
has the property that its distance set
\Delta(E)
has nonempty interior. In the pinned setting Peres and Schlag
[
35
]
showed that if
\textnormal{dim}_{\mathcal{H}}(E)>\frac{d}{2}+1
then there exists a point
y\in E
such that the pinned distance set
\Delta^{y}(E)
has nonempty interior. Despite all the recent progress on the Falconer distance problem and its pinned variant, neither of the results on nonempty interior has been improved since the original papers. Furthermore, observe that unlike when only asking for a positive Lebesgue measure, there is a gap here between the pinned and unpinned results. In the plane when
d=2
one has
\frac{d}{2}+1=2
so the result on nonempty interior for the pinned distance set in
[
35
]
does not provide any information in that case. This leads us to the first main result of this paper, which establishes the first non-trivial threshold for nonempty interior of the pinned distance set in the plane.

Theorem 1
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a compact set with
\textnormal{dim}_{\mathcal{H}}(E)>\frac{7}{4}
. Then there exists
y\in E
such that
\Delta^{y}(E)
contains an interval.

Many further variants of the Falconer distance problem exist. There are dimensional variants that obtain a lower bound on
\textnormal{dim}_{\mathcal{H}}(\Delta(E))
given
\textnormal{dim}_{\mathcal{H}}(E)
, especially in the critical case of
\textnormal{dim}_{\mathcal{H}}(E)=\frac{d}{2}
. See for example the recent works in
[
7
]
and the breakthrough result of Shmerkin and Wang
[
36
]
as well as the references therein. One can look at other notions of distance aside from the classical Euclidean one. Iosevich, Mourgoglou and Taylor
[
25
]
showed that the dimensional threshold
\textnormal{dim}_{\mathcal{H}}(E)>\frac{d}{2}+\frac{1}{2}
for nonempty interior obtained by Mattila and Sjölin extends to a wide range of other metrics. Particularly important to this paper is a result by Iosevich and Liu
[
24
]
in the pinned setting, which recovers the results of Peres and Schlag as well as improving on some dimensional variants and extending to a variety of metrics. Their Fourier analytic techniques rely on local smoothing inequalities and are very different than the projection type methods of Peres and Schlag. The framework of Iosevich and Liu relies on
L^{2}
methods and thus only takes in local smoothing estimates that fit that framework. In this paper we upgrade their framework to handle more general
L^{p}\rightarrow L^{p}
local smoothing estimates, which requires delicate arguments replacing simple
L^{2}
orthogonality. Our next main theorem shows how the threshold for nonempty interior of the pinned distance set changes in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
given different
L^{p}\rightarrow L^{p}
local smoothing estimates. The hypothesis in the theorem that we can control a norm of a
\partial_{t}
derivative will be used in conjuction with one-dimensional Sobolev embedding (in the
t
variable) to prove a continuity result that implies the conclusion on nonempty interior of the pinned distance set.

Theorem 2
.

Let
d\geq 2
and
2<p<\infty
fixed.
Assume that there exists
\eta>0
such that the following local smoothing estimate holds

(1)

\left(\int_{1}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\partial^{\frac{1}{p}+\varepsilon}_{t}A_{t}(f)(x)|^{p}dxdt\right)^{1/p}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\|f\|_{L^{p}_{-\eta+2\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

for every
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small.

Then for every compact set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
\textnormal{dim}_{\mathcal{H}}(E)>d-\eta
, there exists
y\in E
such that
\Delta^{y}(E)
contains an interval. More generally, given two compact sets
E,F
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
satisfying

\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}\textnormal{dim}_{\mathcal{H}}(E)+\frac{1}{p}\textnormal{dim}_{\mathcal{H}}(F)>d-\eta,

there exists a probability measure
\mu_{F}
in
F
such that
\Delta^{y}(E)
contains an interval for
\mu_{F}
-almost every
y\in F
.

If the local smoothing conjecture (see Conjecture
11
) were true in all dimensions, Theorem
2
would yield the dimensional threshold
\frac{d}{2}+1-\frac{1}{2d}
, which would improve on the threshold
\frac{d}{2}+1
obtained by Peres and Schlag in all dimensions. Theorem
1
realizes this optimal threshold when
d=2
. We however note that it is widely believed that the true threshold should be
\frac{d}{2}
, as in the original Falconer distance problem. To further show how robust our result is, we obtain an improvement on the Peres-Schlag dimensional threshold when
d=3
.

Theorem 3
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
be a compact set with
\textnormal{dim}_{\mathcal{H}}(E)>\frac{12}{5}
. Then, there exists
y\in E
such that
\Delta^{y}(E)
contains an interval.

Although we don’t recover the threshold
\frac{7}{3}
, that a solution of the local smoothing conjecture in
\mathbb{R}^{3+1}
would yield, we improve on the Peres-Schlag threshold of
\frac{5}{2}
and make it more than half the way towards the best threshold that our techniques could possibly yield.

Theorem
2
is conditional on given local smoothing estimates. Using the latest results on local smoothing estimates we get the following unconditional corollary to Theorem
2
. In the case
d=2
we use the very recent sharp local smoothing inequalities in
\mathbb{R}^{2+1}
proved in
[
21
]
for the solutions of the wave equation in
\mathbb{R}^{2+1}
or alternatively their extensions to Fourier integral operators in
\mathbb{R}^{2+1}
obtained in
[
13
]
. In the case
d\geq 3
, we use the even more recent results of
[
12
]
.

Corollary 4
.

Let
E,F\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be compact sets satisfying

\begin{cases}\frac{3}{4}\textnormal{dim}_{\mathcal{H}}(E)+\frac{1}{4}\textnormal{dim}_{\mathcal{H}}(F)>\frac{7}{4},\text{ if }d=2,\\
\frac{7}{10}\textnormal{dim}_{\mathcal{H}}(E)+\frac{3}{10}\textnormal{dim}_{\mathcal{H}}(F)>3-\frac{3}{5}=\frac{12}{5},\text{ if }d=3,\text{ or }\\
\frac{1}{p_{d}^{\prime}}\textnormal{dim}_{\mathcal{H}}(E)+\frac{1}{p_{d}}\textnormal{dim}_{\mathcal{H}}(F)>d-\eta(p_{d}),\text{ for }p_{d}=2+\frac{8}{3d-4}\text{ and }\eta(p_{d})=\frac{d-1}{p_{d}}\text{if }d\geq 4.\end{cases}

Then there exists a probability measure
\mu_{F}
in
F
such that
\textnormal{int}(\Delta^{y}(E))\neq\emptyset
for
\mu_{F}
a.e
y\in E
.

Theorems
1
and
3
now follow from Corollary
4
by simply taking
E=F
. To show that Corollary
4
follows from Theorem
2
we show that the operators that arise in our setting fit the setup from
[
21
]
and
[
12
]
in Section
2
. More precisely, Corollary
4
follows from Theorem
2
and Corollaries
14
and
15
in Section
2
.

When
d\geq 4
and if
E=F
Corollary
4
yields a threshold
\frac{d}{2}+\frac{7d-4}{6d}
that matches the Peres-Schlag dimensional threshold when
d=4
and is worse when
d\geq 5
. Our result is more flexible with two different sets,
E
and
F
, and immediately implies the following exceptional set estimates for the bad pins of
E
.

Corollary 5
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a compact set. Then

\textnormal{dim}_{\mathcal{H}}(\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\colon\textnormal{int}(\Delta^{y}(E))=\emptyset\})\leq\begin{cases}7-3\textnormal{dim}_{\mathcal{H}}(E),\text{ if }d=2,\\
8-\frac{7}{3}\textnormal{dim}_{\mathcal{H}}(E),\text{ if }d=3,\\
\frac{3d^{2}+7d-4}{3d-4}-\frac{3d+4}{3d-4}\textnormal{dim}_{\mathcal{H}}(E)\text{ if }d\geq 4.\end{cases}

It is worth comparing the corollary above with the corresponding corollary in Iosevich and Liu paper
[
24
, Corollary 1.4]
,
which gives

\textnormal{dim}_{\mathcal{H}}(\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\colon\text{int}(\Delta^{y}(E))=\emptyset\})\leq\begin{cases}8-2\textnormal{dim}_{\mathcal{H}}(E)\text{ if }d=3,\\
\frac{(d+1)^{2}}{d-1}-\frac{d+1}{d-1}\textnormal{dim}_{\mathcal{H}}(E),\text{ if }d\geq 4,\end{cases}

and does not provide information when
d=2
. Our exceptional set estimates improve upon those of Iosevich–Liu in dimension
d=2
(where they obtain no result) and in dimension
d=3
. For
d\geq 4
, our bound is sharper precisely in the regime
\textnormal{dim}_{\mathcal{H}}(E)>d-3+\frac{4}{d}
.

For a compact set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
one can consider a generalization of the distance set to a tree
\mathcal{T}
, denoted by
\Delta_{\mathcal{T}}(E)
, see precise definition in Section
5
. Bennett, Iosevich and Taylor showed in
[
2
]
for particular trees, called chains, that if
\textnormal{dim}_{\mathcal{H}}(E)>\frac{d+1}{2}
then
\Delta_{\mathcal{T}}(E)
has nonempty interior. This result was extended to all finite trees by Iosevich and Taylor
[
23
]
. In a similar spirit, one can also consider a pinned variant
\Delta_{\mathcal{T},v_{0}}^{x_{0}}(E)
where the tree has the distinguished vertex
v_{0}
pinned at
x_{0}\in E
. The third listed author and Taylor
[
31
]
showed that when
d=2
if
\textnormal{dim}_{\mathcal{H}}(E)>\frac{5}{4}
the Lebesgue measure of
\Delta_{\mathcal{T},v_{0}}^{x_{0}}(E)
is positive, for some
x_{0}\in E
. No results exist in the literature on nonempty interior for pinned trees in any dimension. Our second main result provides the first result in the plane in this direction.

Theorem 6
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a compact set with
\textnormal{dim}_{\mathcal{H}}(E)>7/4
. Let
(\mathcal{T},v_{0})
be any finite tree with
n+1
vertices, including a distinguished pinned vertex
v_{0}
. Then there exists a point
x_{0}\in E
such that
\Delta_{\mathcal{T},v_{0}}^{x_{0}}(E)
has nonempty interior.

The previous results on trees
[
2
,
23
,
31
]
all used inductive strategies, but in the case of nonempty interior showed boundedness of the density as well as lower bounds and then concluded from direct approximation arguments that the density had to be continuous. Even in the most recent paper of Greenleaf, Iosevich and Taylor
[
18
]
, which utilizes the strong microlocal analysis framework developed by the authors in previous papers
[
15
,
16
,
17
]
, they still follow a similar scheme and obtain the same dimensional threshold
\frac{d+1}{2}
. Our approach is built around showing boundedness for derivatives of the densities and utilizing local smoothing estimates. In order to make this work, we need to develop a novel induction strategy that handles all the derivatives appropriately. If implemented naively, taking all the required derivatives would lead to a much worse threshold that in most cases would be trivial. Our methods are robust, as in the case for distances, and extend to higher dimensions and can showcase the reliance on the
L^{p}\rightarrow L^{p}
local smoothing estimates. This is the content of our next main theorem.

Theorem 7
.

Let
d\geq 2
and
2<p<\infty
fixed.
Assume that there exists
\eta>0
such that a local smoothing estimate (
1
) holds for every
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small.
Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a compact set with
\textnormal{dim}_{\mathcal{H}}(E)>d-\eta
. Let
(\mathcal{T},v_{0})
be any finite tree with
n+1
vertices, including a distinguished pinned vertex
v_{0}
. Then there exists a point
x_{0}\in E
such that
\Delta_{\mathcal{T},v_{0}}^{x_{0}}(E)
has nonempty interior.

Using the best-known
L^{p}\rightarrow L^{p}
local smoothing estimates as stated in Corollaries
14
and
15
in Section
2
, coming from
[
21
]
and
[
12
]
, this yields Theorem
6
in the plane and the following estimates in higher dimensions, which are all new in the literature.

Theorem 8
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\geq 3
be a compact set with
\textnormal{dim}_{\mathcal{H}}(E)>\frac{12}{5}
when
d=3
or
\textnormal{dim}_{\mathcal{H}}(E)>\frac{d}{2}+\frac{7d-4}{6d}

. Let
(\mathcal{T},v_{0})

. Then there exists a point

If the local smoothing conjecture (see Conjecture

) were to be true when

at the critical exponent

, the threshold would improve to
\frac{d+2}{2}-\frac{1}{2d}

2.
Tools and Notation

2.1.
Littlewood-Paley pieces and Sobolev spaces

By
P_{j}f
we denote the
j
th Littlewood-Paley projection of
f
, which we sometimes denote as
f^{j}
for short. More explicitly, take
\beta_{0}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R})
to be a nonnegative function such that
\beta_{0}(s)\equiv 1
if
|s|\leq 1
and

\beta_{0}=0
if
|s|\geq 2
. Let
\beta(s):=\beta_{0}(s)-\beta_{0}(2s)
so that
\beta_{j}(s):=\beta(2^{-j}s)
is supported in
\{s\colon 2^{j-1}<|s|<2^{j+1}\}
and

\beta_{0}(s)+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\beta(2^{-j}s)\equiv 1.
Then

(2)

\mathcal{F}(P_{0}f)(\xi)=\beta_{0}(|\xi|)\hat{f}(\xi)\text{ and }\mathcal{F}(P_{j}f)(\xi)=\beta(2^{-j}|\xi|)\hat{f}(\xi)\text{ for all }j\geq 1.

For us,

\mathcal{F}(f)(\xi):=\hat{f}(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x)e^{-2\pi ix\cdot\xi}dx
.

We use the following notation for Sobolev spaces. Given
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
, define

\|f\|_{L^{p}_{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}:=\|(\langle\cdot\rangle^{\gamma}\hat{f})^{\vee}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\text{ where }\langle\xi\rangle:=(1+|\xi|^{2})^{1/2},

and
L_{\gamma}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=\{f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\colon(\langle\cdot\rangle^{\gamma}\hat{f})^{\vee}\in L^{p}\}
.

Remark 9
.

Frequently in the sequel, we will encounter the scenario where a function
\varphi
in a Sobolev space is multiplied by a smooth function that is comparable to
1
on the support of
\varphi
(most often a polynomial factor where
\varphi
is supported on a compact interval away from
0
). We will generally drop such factors without further comment as the Sobolev norm of the product is at most a bounded factor larger than the Sobolev norm of
\varphi
.

When
\gamma>d/p
then every function in
L^{p}_{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
can be modified in a set of measure zero so that the resulting function is Hölder continuous (see
[
39
, Section 2.8.1]
for example, where one just needs to recall that
L^{p}_{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=F_{p,2}^{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
). We will only need this fact in
d=1
, so we state it below for future reference.

Theorem 10
.

Let
1<p<\infty
, and
0<\alpha:=\gamma-1/p<1
. Then every function in
L^{p}_{\gamma}(\mathbb{R})
is
\alpha
-Hölder continuous with

(3)

\|f\|_{C^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R})}=\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}|f(t)|+\sup_{t\neq s}\frac{|f(t)-f(s)|}{|t-s|^{\alpha}}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{p}_{\gamma}(\mathbb{R})}.

If
\varphi
is a function of
t\in\mathbb{R}
we will denote
\partial_{t}^{\gamma}\varphi(t):=(\langle\tau\rangle^{\gamma}\hat{\varphi}(\tau))^{\vee}(t)
so that
\|\varphi\|_{L^{p}_{\gamma}(\mathbb{R})}=\|\partial_{t}^{\gamma}\varphi\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R})}
. Although we use the partial derivative notation to denote this fractional derivative, we will make use of it in the sequel as a way to access
one-dimensional
Sobolev embedding, allowing us to show that certain quantities are continuous.

2.2.
Restricted measures

Let
\mu
be a Borel probability measure supported in a compact set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Given
A\subset E
, with
\mu(A)>0
we will refer to the
restriction
of
\mu
to
A
as the probability measure supported in
A
given by

(4)

\mu_{A}(S)=\frac{\mu(S\cap A)}{\mu(A)},\,\text{ for any Borel set }S\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{d}.

If
\mu(A)=1-\epsilon
, for some
\epsilon\in(0,1)
we will say that
\mu_{A}
is an
(1-\epsilon)
-restriction of
\mu
.

2.3.
Preliminaries in local smoothing

Let
u
be a solution of the Cauchy problem

(5)

\begin{split}\begin{cases}(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)u(x,t)=0,\,(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}\\
u(x,0)=f(x)\\
\partial_{t}u(x,0)=0.\end{cases}\end{split}

For each fixed time
t>0
, by a classical result of Peral
[
34
]
and Miyachi
[
30
]
, in the range
2<p<\infty
we have that

(6)

\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim_{t,p}\|f\|_{L_{s_{p}}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

where
s_{p}=\frac{d-1}{2}-\frac{d-1}{p}
.

Sogge conjectured
[
37
]
that by taking
L^{p}
norms in both space and local time, one has the following local smoothing estimate for the wave equation.

Conjecture 11

(Local smoothing conjecture for the wave equation in
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}

[
37
]
)
.

Let
u
be a solution of the Cauchy problem in (
5
). Then for all
p\geq\frac{2d}{d-1}
and
s_{p}=\frac{d-1}{2}-\frac{d-1}{p}
we have

(7)

\|u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[1,2])}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\|f\|_{L^{p}_{s_{p}-1/p+\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

In other words, by also integrating locally in time, there is almost an extra
1/p
smoothing in comparison to the fixed time estimate in (
6
). In a groundbreaking result by Guth, Wang and Zhang
[
21
]
, the local smoothing conjectured for the wave equation was confirmed for
d=2
. We state it below.

Theorem 12
.

[
21
]

Suppose that
u(x,t)
is a solution of the wave equation in
\mathbb{R}^{2+1}
with initial data
u(x,0)=f
and
\partial_{t}u(x,0)=0
. Then for all
p\geq 4
and
\varepsilon>0
,

(8)

\|u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times[1,2])}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\|f\|_{L^{p}_{1/2-2/p+\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

Recall that a solution of the Cauchy problem in (
5
) can be written in terms of the half-wave propagator as

u(x,t)=e^{it\sqrt{-\Delta}}f(x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{2\pi i(x\cdot\xi+t|\xi|)}\hat{f}(\xi)\,d\xi.

For every
t>0
define the spherical average of
f
at
x
of radius
t>0
as

(9)

A_{t}(f)(x)=\int_{S^{d-1}}f(x-ty)\,d\sigma(y)=f*\sigma_{t}(x),

where
\sigma
is the normalized canonical measure in the unit sphere
S^{d-1}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\colon|x|=1\}
. When written as a Fourier multiplier we have

A_{t}f(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\hat{f}(\xi)\hat{\sigma}(t\xi)e^{2\pi ix\cdot\xi}d\xi.

It is possible to transfer local smoothing estimates for the wave equation to local smoothing estimates for spherical averages. For example, that is shown in
[
1
]
(See Section 2.3 and Proposition 4.4). In what follows, for the reader’s convenience, we’ll explain in more detail how that can be done in our context.

One can write

\hat{\sigma}(\xi)=b_{0}(|\xi|)+\sum_{\pm}b_{\pm}(|\xi|)e^{\pm 2\pi i|\xi|}

where
b_{0}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R})
is supported in
[-1,1]
and each
b_{\pm}
is supported in
|\xi|\geq 1/2
with
b_{\pm}\in S^{-(d-1)/2}(\mathbb{R})
, i.e.,
|\frac{d^{k}(b_{\pm})}{ds^{k}}(s)|\lesssim_{k}(1+|s|)^{-\frac{d-1}{2}-k}
. Therefore, for
j\geq 1
, and
t\in[1,2]

(10)

A_{t}(f^{j})(x)=\sum_{\pm}\int\beta_{j}(|\xi|)b_{\pm}(t|\xi|)\hat{f}(\xi)e^{2\pi i(x\cdot\xi\pm it|\xi|)}d\xi.

Define for
j\geq 0
,

u^{j}_{\pm}(x,t):=\int\beta_{j}(|\xi|)\hat{f}(\xi)e^{2\pi i(x\cdot\xi\pm it|\xi|)}d\xi.

Assume the local smoothing conjecture is true for the wave equation in
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
as in (
7
). Then for
p\geq\frac{2d}{d-1}
,

(11)

\|u^{j}_{\pm}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[1,2])}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}2^{j(\frac{d-1}{2}-\frac{d}{p}+\varepsilon)}\|f^{j}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

We claim that the inequality above implies

(12)

\|A_{t}(f^{j})(x)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[1,2])}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}2^{j(-d/p+\varepsilon)}\|f^{j}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Indeed, note that

(13)

\begin{split}T_{b_{\pm}}(f^{j})(x,t):=&\int\beta_{j}(|\xi|)b_{\pm}(t|\xi|)\hat{f}(\xi)e^{2\pi i(x\cdot\xi\pm it|\xi|)}d\xi\\
=&\int\left(\tilde{\beta}_{j}(|\xi|)b_{\pm}(t|\xi|)\right)\cdot\left(\beta_{j}(|\xi|)\hat{f}(\xi)e^{\pm 2\pi it|\xi|}\right)e^{2\pi ix\cdot\xi}d\xi\\
=&\mathcal{F}^{-1}[\tilde{\beta}_{j}(|\cdot|)\ b_{\pm}(t|\cdot|)]*u^{j}_{\pm}(\cdot,t)(x)\end{split}

where
\tilde{\beta_{j}}:=\beta_{j-1}+\beta_{j}+\beta_{j+1}\equiv 1
in the support of
\beta_{j}
.
From Young’s inequality

\|T_{b_{\pm}}f^{j}(x,t)\|_{L^{p}_{x}}\lesssim\|u^{j}_{\pm}(x,t)\|_{L^{p}_{x}}\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}[\tilde{\beta}_{j}(|\xi|)\ b_{\pm}(t|\xi|)](x)\|_{L^{1}_{x}}.

By a standard integration by parts argument and since
b_{\pm}\in S^{-\frac{d-1}{2}}(\mathbb{R})
we have

\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}[\tilde{\beta}_{j}(|\xi|)\ b_{\pm}(t|\xi|)](x)\|_{L^{1}_{x}}\leq C2^{-j\frac{d-1}{2}},

uniformly for
t\in[1,2]
. By further integrating the inequality above in
t\in[1,2]
and using the local smoothing in (
11
) we get

\sum_{\pm}\|T_{b_{\pm}}f^{j}(x,t)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[1,2])}\lesssim 2^{-j\frac{d-1}{2}}\|f^{j}\|_{L^{p}_{s_{p}-1/p+\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim 2^{j(-d/p+\varepsilon)}\|f^{j}\|_{p},

which leads to the desired inequality (
12
). More generally, for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, one would have,

\left\|\frac{d^{k}}{dt^{k}}A_{t}(f^{j})(x)\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[1,2])}\lesssim_{\varepsilon,k}2^{j(k-d/p+\varepsilon)}\|f^{j}\|_{L^{p}}

which, combined with interpolation, would lead to the estimates in the conjecture below.

Conjecture 13

(
L^{p}\rightarrow L^{p}
local smoothing conjecture for spherical averages)
.

Let
d\geq 2
and
\gamma\geq 0
. For all
p\geq\frac{2d}{d-1}
one has

(14)

\left(\int_{1}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\partial^{\gamma}_{t}A_{t}(f)(x)|^{p}dxdt\right)^{1/p}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\|f\|_{L^{p}_{\gamma-\frac{d}{p}+\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

From Theorem
12
one gets the conjectured local smoothing for spherical averages for
d=2
.

Corollary 14

(
L^{p}\rightarrow L^{p}
local smoothing for spherical averages in
\mathbb{R}^{2+1}
)
.

Let
p\geq 4
and
\gamma\geq 0
. Then

(15)

\left(\int_{1}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\partial^{\gamma}_{t}A_{t}(f)(x)|^{p}dxdt\right)^{1/p}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\|f\|_{L^{p}_{\gamma-\frac{2}{p}+\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

In particular, for all
\varepsilon>0
one has

(16)

\left(\int_{1}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\partial^{1/4+\varepsilon}_{t}A_{t}(f)(x)|^{4}dxdt\right)^{1/4}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\|f\|_{L^{p}_{-\frac{1}{4}+2\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

The local smoothing result above implies that when
d=2
, the hypotheses of Theorem
2
are satisfied with
p=4
and
\eta=1/4
leading to the unconditional Corollary
4
.

For
d\geq 3
, we make use of a recent improvement on local smoothing estimates for the wave equation in
[
12
, Theorem 1.3]
to get the following corollary.

Corollary 15

(
L^{p}\rightarrow L^{p}
local smoothing for spherical averages in
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
)
.

Let
d\geq 3
and
p\geq p_{d}
where
p_{d}=\frac{10}{3}
in
d=3
and
p_{d}=2+\frac{8}{3d-4}
for

.
Then for any
\gamma\geq 0
,

(17)

\left(\int_{1}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\partial^{\gamma}_{t}A_{t}(f)(x)|^{p}dxdt\right)^{1/p}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\|f\|_{L^{p}_{\gamma-\frac{d}{p}+\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R}^{d}}),

In particular, for
p=p_{d}
and
\gamma=1/p_{d}+\varepsilon
,

(18)

\left(\int_{1}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\partial^{\frac{1}{p_{d}}+\varepsilon}_{t}A_{t}(f)(x)|^{p_{d}}dxdt\right)^{1/p_{d}}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\|f\|_{L^{p_{d}}_{-\frac{d-1}{p_{d}}+2\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Observe that for

as in the corollary above one has
d-\frac{d-1}{p_{d}}=\frac{12}{5}
for

and

.

3.
Proof of Theorem
2

Take
E,F
to be compact sets in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
satisfying

\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}\textnormal{dim}_{\mathcal{H}}(E)+\frac{1}{p}\textnormal{dim}_{\mathcal{H}}(F)>d-\eta.

Denoting
s_{E}=\textnormal{dim}_{\mathcal{H}}(E)
, there exists
\mu_{E}
, a Frostman probability measure in
E
, such that

(19)

\mu_{E}(B(x,r))\lesssim_{\varepsilon}r^{s_{E}-\varepsilon}\text{ for all }r>0\text{ and }x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

where
\varepsilon>0
will be chosen sufficiently small. Similarly, let
s_{F}=\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(F)
and take
\mu_{F}
to be a Frostman probability measure in
F
satisfying (
19
) with
E
replaced by
F
.

A standard argument along the lines of
[
3
, Lemma 3]
allows us to assume that all distances from points in
E
to points in
F
live in some compact interval
[a,b]
with
a>0
. Really, what we are doing is finding positive measure subsets of these sets that are separated and working on these subsets instead. The interval
[a,b]
can be decomposed into finitely many intervals at different dyadic scales, and we can apply the argument in each interval separately. So we can assume without loss of generality that
[a,b]=[1,2]
and
dt
is Lebesgue measure in
[1,2]
.

If
\mu_{E}
is a Frostman measure in
E
, one can define a measure
\tilde{\nu}_{E}^{y}
in
\mathbb{R}
as

\int f(t)d\tilde{\nu}^{y}_{E}(t)=\int f(|x-y|)d\mu_{E}(x).

In other words,
\tilde{\nu}_{E}^{y}=(\Phi^{y})_{*}(\mu_{E})
, is the pushforward of the measure
\mu_{E}
through the map
\Phi^{y}:E\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, mapping
x\mapsto|x-y|
.
Note that
\tilde{\nu}^{y}_{E}
is supported in
\Delta^{y}(E)
, which is contained in
[1,2]
by assumption. We want to show that for
\mu_{F}
-almost every
y
,
\tilde{\nu}^{y}_{E}
is absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure in
\mathbb{R}
, and it admits a continuous density. In that case, since
\tilde{\nu}_{E}^{y}
is a probability measure with support in
[1,2]
and continuous density, it follows that its support contains an interval, and so does
\Delta^{y}(E)
.

By the Sobolev embedding in
\mathbb{R}
(Theorem
10
), if
\gamma>1/p
then every element of
L_{\gamma}^{p}(\mathbb{R})
can be modified in a set of measure zero so that the resulting function is bounded and uniformly continuous. Hence, in order to prove Theorem
2
it is enough to show that if
E,\,F
are compact sets in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}\textnormal{dim}_{\mathcal{H}}(E)+\frac{1}{p}\textnormal{dim}_{\mathcal{H}}(F)>d-\eta,

where
\eta
is the assumed local smoothing as in (
1
) then there exist Frostman probability measures
\mu_{E},\,\mu_{F}
in
E,\,F
respectively and
\gamma>1/p
such that

(20)

\left(\int\|\tilde{\nu}^{y}_{E}(t)\|_{L^{p}_{\gamma}(dt)}^{p}d\mu_{F}(y)\right)^{1/p}<\infty.

Such an estimate implies that for
\mu_{F}
a.e.
y
one has that
\|\tilde{\nu}_{E}^{y}\|_{L_{\gamma}^{p}([1,2])}<\infty
, and consequently
\Delta^{y}(E)
has nonempty interior.

We can rewrite (
20
) as a mixed norm estimate of the form

(21)

\|\partial_{t}^{1/p+\varepsilon}\tilde{\nu}_{E}^{y}(t)\|_{L^{p}(d\mu_{F}(y)dt)}<\infty,

for some
\varepsilon>0
(i.e. by taking
\gamma=1/p+\varepsilon
).

Let
\mu_{E}^{\delta}:=\mu_{E}*\rho^{\delta}
where
\{\rho_{\delta}\colon\delta>0\}
is an approximate identity. Namely, choose a nonnegative
C^{\infty}
function
\rho
with
\text{supp}(\rho)\subset B(0,1),\,\int\rho(t)dt=1
and let
\rho_{\delta}(x)=\delta^{-d}\rho(x/\delta)
. Note that for each
\delta>0
one has
\mu_{E}^{\delta}\in C^{\infty}_{c}
and
\|\mu_{E}^{\delta}\|_{L^{1}}\lesssim 1
.

Let
(\tilde{\nu}_{E}^{y})^{\delta}
be the pushforward of
\mu_{E}^{\delta}
through the map
\Phi^{y}
. One can check that for each
\delta>0

(\tilde{\nu}_{E}^{y})^{\delta}(t)=t^{d-1}A_{t}(\mu_{E}^{\delta})(y)

where
A_{t}
denotes the spherical averaging operator in (
9
). Note that by Remark
9
, we will be able to drop the
t^{d-1}
factor in the sequel.

For each
y
, one has
(\tilde{\nu}_{E}^{y})^{\delta}\rightharpoonup\tilde{\nu}_{E}^{y}
in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
.
Moreover, if we recall that
\partial^{\gamma}_{t}\tilde{\nu}_{E}^{y}=(\langle\tau\rangle^{\gamma}(\tilde{\nu}_{E}^{y}\hat{)}(\tau))^{\vee}
understood in a distributional sense, then for all
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}_{+})

(22)

\begin{split}\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\langle\partial^{\gamma}_{t}(\tilde{\nu}_{E}^{y})^{\delta},\varphi\rangle=&\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\int\langle\tau\rangle^{\gamma}\mathcal{F}(\tilde{\nu}_{E}^{y})^{\delta}(\tau)\mathcal{F}^{-1}({\varphi(y,\cdot)})(\tau)d\tau d\mu_{F}(y)\\
=&\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\int\langle\tau\rangle^{\gamma}\mathcal{F}(\tilde{\nu}_{E}^{y})(\tau)\hat{\rho}(\delta\tau)\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\varphi(y,\cdot))(\tau)d\tau d\mu_{F}(y)\\
=&\int\langle\tau\rangle^{\gamma}\mathcal{F}(\tilde{\nu}_{E}^{y})(\tau)\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\varphi(y,\cdot))(\tau)d\tau d\mu_{F}(y)=\langle\partial^{\gamma}_{t}(\tilde{\nu}_{E}^{y}),\varphi\rangle.\end{split}

That is, it holds that

(23)

\partial^{\gamma}_{t}(\tilde{\nu}_{E}^{y})^{\delta}(t)\rightharpoonup\partial^{\gamma}_{t}\tilde{\nu}_{E}^{y}(t)\text{ in }\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}).

The function
t\mapsto t^{d-1}
is smooth and very well behaved away from the origin so by defining

(\nu_{E}^{y})^{\delta}(t)=A_{t}(\mu_{E}^{\delta})(y)
we still have weak convergence to a finite nonzero Borel measure
\nu_{E}^{y}
supported in
\Delta^{y}(E)
(and there is also weak convergence for the fractional
\gamma
derivatives).

Suppose that we prove that

(24)

\|\partial_{t}^{1/p+\varepsilon}(\nu_{E}^{y})^{\delta_{1}}(t)\|_{L^{p}(\mu_{F}^{\delta_{2}}\times dt)}\lesssim 1,

with implicit constant independent of
\delta_{1},\delta_{2}>0
. Then, sending
\delta_{2}
to
0
we get

(25)

\|\partial_{t}^{1/p+\varepsilon}(\nu_{E}^{y})^{\delta_{1}}(t)\|_{L^{p}(d\mu_{F}\times dt)}\lesssim 1,

uniformly in
\delta_{1}>0
. Then by using that
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R})
is dense in
L^{p}(d\mu_{F}dt)
and the norm in
L^{p}(d\mu_{F}dt)
is realized by dualization against Schwartz functions (see
[
29
, Page 270]
) combined with the weak convergence in (
23
) we recover the desired estimate (
21
) where
\delta_{1},\delta_{2}
are dropped.

To keep the notation lighter, we will drop the mollifications in the arguments below. Still, the reader should keep in mind that we can assume that a measure
\mu
was replaced with their smooth mollifications
\mu^{\delta}
, and if there is more than one compact set involved say
E_{1},\,E_{2},\,\dots,\,E_{m}
one could replace
\mu_{E_{i}}
with
\mu_{E_{i}}^{\delta_{i}}
. The bounds we prove will be independent on
\delta_{i}
s so limiting arguments similar to the one above apply.

We now prove (
21
) with a duality strategy inspired by
[
22
, Theorem 4.1]
.

Let
P_{j}
be the
j
th Littlewood-Paley projection piece of
f
, as in (
2
). By duality, it is enough to show that for any
g\in L^{p^{\prime}}(d\mu_{F}dt)
with
\|g\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(d\mu_{F}\times dt)}=1
one has that

(26)

\left|\int_{1}^{2}\int\partial_{t}^{1/p+\varepsilon}\nu_{E}^{y}(t)g(y,t)d\mu_{F}(y)dt\right|\lesssim 1,

with implicit constant independent of
g
. Decomposing into Littlewood-Paley pieces, we have

(27)

\begin{split}\int_{1}^{2}\int&\partial_{t}^{1/p+\varepsilon}\nu_{E}^{y}(t)g(y,t)d\mu_{F}(y)dt\\
=&\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\int_{1}^{2}\int\partial_{t}^{1/p+\varepsilon}A_{t}(P_{j}\mu_{E})(y)g_{t}(y)d\mu_{F}(y)dt,\end{split}

where we used the notation
g_{t}(y)=g(y,t)
. By Plancherel, we can replace the measure function
g_{t}\mu_{F}
with a Littlewood piece as well, say
\tilde{P_{j}}(g_{t}d\mu_{F})
for
\tilde{P}_{j}=\sum_{i=-2}^{2}P_{j+i}
. Next, we do

(28)

\begin{split}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}&\int_{1}^{2}\int\partial_{t}^{1/p+\varepsilon}A_{t}(P_{j}\mu_{E})(y)\tilde{P}_{j}(g_{t}d\mu_{F})(y)dydt\\
\lesssim&\sum_{j\geq 0}\|\partial_{t}^{1/p+\varepsilon}A_{t}(P_{j}\mu_{E})(y)\|_{L^{p}(dydt)}\|\tilde{P}_{j}(g_{t}\mu_{F})(y)\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(dydt)}\\
\lesssim&\sum_{j\geq 0}\|\mu_{E}^{j}\|_{L^{p}_{-\eta+2\varepsilon}}\|\tilde{P}_{j}(g_{t}\mu_{F})(y)\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(dydt)}\end{split}

where we used the assumed local smoothing (
1
).

Next, we will need some standard estimates. First, we recall the following lemma from
[
24
, Lemma 2.4]
that will be used multiple times in this paper.

Lemma 16

(
[
24
]
)
.

Let
1\leq p\leq\infty
,
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
and
\mu
Frostman measure in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
satisfying
\mu(B(x,r))\leq C_{\varepsilon}r^{s-\varepsilon}
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
r>0
. Then, for all
j\geq 0
and
\mu^{j}=P_{j}(\mu)
,

\|\mu^{j}\|_{L^{p}_{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}2^{j(\gamma+(d-s+\varepsilon)\frac{1}{p^{\prime}})}.

Next, we have the following lemma.

Lemma 17
.

For all
1\leq p\leq\infty
and
\mu
as in Lemma
16
, one has for
h(y)\in L^{p}(d\mu)
that for all
j\geq 0
,

(29)

\|P_{j}(\mu h)\|_{L^{p}(dy)}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}2^{j(d-s+\varepsilon)\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\|h\|_{L^{p}(d\mu)}.

Proof.

We check the Lemma for
p=1
and
p=\infty
and then use interpolation.
For
p=1
, by recalling the definition of
P_{j}
in Section
2
we have

(30)

\int|P_{j}(\mu h)(y)|dy\leq\int\int|2^{jd}\check{\beta}(2^{j}(y-x))h(x)|d\mu(x)dy\leq\|\check{\beta}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\int|h(x)|d\mu(x)\lesssim\|h\|_{L^{1}(\mu)}

where in the second inequality, we used Fubini’s Theorem to integrate first in
y
. For
p=\infty
, we have

|P_{j}(\mu h)(y)|\leq 2^{jd}\int|\check{\beta}(2^{j}(y-x))||h(x)|d\mu(x).

From the fast decay of
\check{\beta}
the main contribution comes from
|x-y|\leq 2^{-j}
and

(31)

\begin{split}2^{jd}\int_{x\colon|x-y|\leq 2^{-j}}|h(x)|d\mu(x)\lesssim 2^{jd}\mu(B(y,2^{-j}))\|h\|_{L^{\infty}(\mu)}\lesssim 2^{j(d-s+\varepsilon)}\|h\|_{L^{\infty}(\mu)}.\end{split}

∎

As an immediate consequence, one has

(32)

\|P_{j}(g_{t}\mu)(y)\|_{L^{q}(dydt)}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}2^{j(d-s+\varepsilon)\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}\|g\|_{L^{q}(d\mu\times dt)}

(also see
[
22
, page 78]
).

We remark that if
\mu
is a compactly supported finite measure satisfying
\mu(B(x,r))\leq Cr^{s_{\mu}}
for all
r>0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, then for any
\delta>0
, it is also true for some
C^{\prime}>C
that
\mu^{\delta}(B(x,r))\leq C^{\prime}r^{s_{\mu}}
where
C^{\prime}
is independent of
\delta>0
, so there is no problem in replacing
\mu
by its mollifications in the lemmas above.

Going back to (
28
), we will have

(33)

\begin{split}\sum_{j\geq 0}\|\mu_{E}^{j}\|_{L^{p}_{-\eta+2\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|\tilde{P}_{j}(g_{t}\mu_{F})(y)\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(dydt)}\lesssim\sum_{j\geq 0}2^{j(-\eta+(d-s_{E})\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}+(d-s_{F})\frac{1}{p}+3\varepsilon)}.\end{split}

Finally, note that the series is summable for some small
\varepsilon>0
since

d-\eta<\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}s_{E}+\frac{1}{p}s_{F}
.

The reader should note that, although we gave our argument for a fixed Frostman probability measure
\mu_{E}
, for
A\subset E,\,\mu_{E}(A)>0
if we replace
\mu_{E}
with the restricted measure
\mu_{A}
as in (
4
) then the argument still holds, with the implicit constant in the estimate (
21
) merely being multiplied by a factor of
\mu_{E}(A)^{-1}
. This simple observation will be used in the proof of the result for trees later.

4.
Proof of Theorem
7
: Toy cases

In this section, we explore a few toy cases of trees to illustrate the proof strategy of Theorem
7
. We start with chains, which are some of the simplest types of trees. Given a compact set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\geq 2
and
k\geq 1
, let us consider the set of
k
chains in
E
given by

C(E,k):=\{(|x_{0}-x_{1}|,|x_{1}-x_{2}|,\dots,|x_{k-1}-x_{k}|)\colon x_{0},x_{1},\dots,x_{k}\text{ distinct points in }E\}

and its pinned (at the starting point) variant

C^{x}(E,k):=\{(|x-x_{1}|,|x_{1}-x_{2}|,\dots,|x_{k-1}-x_{k}|)\colon x_{1},\dots,x_{k}\text{ distinct points in }E\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{k}_{+}.

Instead of restricting ourselves to pins at the starting point of the chain, we can also allow pins in more general locations. Let
0\leq p\leq k
correspond to the location of the pin in the chain.

(34)

\begin{split}C^{x,p}(E,k):=\{(|x_{0}-x_{1}|,&|x_{1}-x_{2}|,\dots,|x_{p-1}-x|,|x-x_{p+1}|,\dots,|x_{k-1}-x_{k}|):\\
&x_{0},x_{1},\dots,x_{p-1},x_{p+1},\dots,x_{k}\text{ distinct points in }E\}.\end{split}

As we will see in the definition in the next section,
C^{x,p}
are special types of pinned trees. In the first two toy cases below, we only explore the case when
p=0
, i.e. the endpoint pin case. In the proof of the general case of Theorem
7
, to be presented in the next section, one will see that the degree of the pinned vertex of the tree plays a key role.

4.1.
Toy case: 2 chains with a pin at an endpoint

We begin by working with a slightly smaller 2-chain set. Given
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
{\rm dim}(E)=:s_{E}
, let
E_{0}
,
E_{1}
,
E_{2}
be pairwise separated subsets of
E
with positive
(s_{E}-\varepsilon)
-dimensional Hausdorff measure. (Such a construction is standard. For example, it can be found in
[
3
, Lemma 3]
.) Then there exist probability measures
\mu_{0}
,
\mu_{1}
,
\mu_{2}
supported on
E_{0}
,
E_{1}
,
E_{2}
respectively satisfying the Frostman condition (
19
). We will be studying the pinned chain set

C^{x}(E_{1},E_{2},2):=\{(|x-x_{1}|,|x_{1}-x_{2}|):\,x_{1}\in E_{1},\,x_{2}\in E_{2}\},\quad x\in E_{0}.

One can then construct a probability measure in
C^{x}(E_{1},E_{2},2)
by taking the pushforward of the measure
\mu_{1}\times\mu_{2}
by the map
\Phi^{x}:E_{1}\times E_{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
\Phi^{x}(x_{1},x_{2})=(|x-x_{1}|,|x_{1}-x_{2}|)
which will be given (actually for the mollifications of
\mu_{1}
,
\mu_{2}
) as

\tilde{\nu}^{x}(t_{1},t_{2})=(t_{1}t_{2})^{d-1}A_{t_{1}}(\mu_{1}A_{t_{2}}\mu_{2})(x)

where

A_{t}(f)(x)
is as in (
9
). Indeed, for any test function
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, by using the change of variables
(y_{1},y_{2})=(x_{1}-x,x_{2}-x_{1})
followed by polar coordinates we get

(35)

\begin{split}\int\varphi(t_{1},t_{2})(\tilde{\nu}^{x})^{\mathbf{\delta}}(t_{1},t_{2})=&\int\int\varphi(|x_{1}-x|,|x_{2}-x_{1}|)\mu_{1}^{\delta_{1}}(x_{1})\mu_{2}^{\delta_{2}}(x_{2})\,dx_{1}dx_{2}\\
=&\int\int\varphi(|y_{1}|,|y_{2}|)\mu_{1}^{\delta_{1}}(y_{1}+x)\mu_{2}^{\delta_{2}}(y_{1}+y_{2}+x)\,dy_{1}dy_{2}\\
=&\int\int\varphi(t_{1},t_{2})(t_{1}t_{2})^{d-1}\cdot\\
&\qquad\int_{S^{d-1}}\int_{S^{d-1}}\mu_{1}^{\delta_{1}}(x+t_{1}\omega_{1})\mu_{2}^{\delta_{2}}(x+t_{1}\omega_{1}+t_{2}\omega_{2})\,d\sigma(\omega_{2})d\sigma(\omega_{1})dt_{1}dt_{2}\\
=&\int\int\varphi(t_{1},t_{2})(t_{1}t_{2})^{d-1}\cdot\\
&\qquad\int_{S^{d-1}}\mu_{1}^{\delta_{1}}(x+t_{1}\omega_{1})A_{t_{2}}(\mu_{2}^{\delta_{2}})(x+t_{1}\omega_{1})\,d\sigma(\omega_{1})dt_{1}dt_{2}\\
=&\int\int\varphi(t_{1},t_{2})(t_{1}t_{2})^{d-1}A_{t_{1}}(\mu_{1}^{\delta_{1}}A_{t_{2}}(\mu_{2}^{\delta_{2}}))(x)\,dt_{1}dt_{2}.\end{split}

Since the factor

is smooth in

and is bounded from above and below, let us drop it as before and redefine

The measure
\nu^{x}
is supported in

for
\mu_{0}
a.e.

, under certain dimensional assumption on the set

For fixed

, we can bound for any

(here

(36)

\begin{split}&|\nu^{x}(t_{1}+h_{1},t_{2}+h_{2})-\nu^{x}(t_{1},t_{2})|\\
\leq&|\nu^{x}(t_{1}+h_{1},t_{2}+h_{2})-\nu^{x}(t_{1},t_{2}+h_{2})|+|\nu^{x}(t_{1},t_{2}+h_{2})-\nu^{x}(t_{1},t_{2})|\\
\lesssim&|h_{1}|^{\gamma-1/p}\|A_{s_{1}}(\mu_{1}A_{t_{2}+h_{2}}\mu_{2})(x)\|_{L^{p}_{\gamma}(ds_{1})}+|h_{2}|^{\gamma-1/p}\|A_{t_{1}}(\mu_{1}A_{s_{2}}\mu_{2})(x)\|_{L^{p}_{\gamma}(ds_{2})},\\
\leq&{|h_{1}|^{\gamma-1/p}\sup_{s_{2}\sim 1}\|\partial_{s_{1}}^{\gamma}A_{s_{1}}(\mu_{1}A_{s_{2}}\mu_{2})(x)\|_{L^{p}(ds_{1})}+|h_{2}|^{\gamma-1/p}\sup_{s_{1}\sim 1}\|\partial_{s_{2}}^{\gamma}A_{s_{1}}(\mu_{1}A_{s_{2}}\mu_{2})(x)\|_{L^{p}(ds_{2})},}\\
\lesssim&{(|h_{1}|^{\gamma-\frac{1}{p}}+|h_{2}|^{\gamma-\frac{1}{p}})\|\partial_{s_{1}}^{\gamma}\partial_{s_{2}}^{\gamma}A_{s_{1}}(\mu_{1}A_{s_{2}}\mu_{2})(x)\|_{L^{p}(ds_{1}ds_{2})}}\end{split}

where from the second to third line in the equation above we are making use of Theorem

to guarantee Hölder continuity for the functions

and

.

Let us suppose temporarily that for any

the right-hand side is bounded by

, call it

, where this holds. Then we have that

for all sufficiently small

and

. Thus, the density

is continuous for all

and consequently

has nonempty interior.

) for
\mu_{0}
a.e.
x
.

.

Let

and

Proof of Proposition

.

As before, we will be working with mollified versions of the measures
\mu_{0},\mu_{1},\mu_{2}
. We still use the same notation for them for the sake of simplicity.

By duality,

\begin{split}\|\partial_{t_{1}}^{\gamma}&\partial_{t_{2}}^{\gamma}\nu^{x}(t_{1},t_{2})\|_{L^{p}(d\mu_{0}dt_{1}dt_{2})}\\
=&\sup_{\|g\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(d\mu_{0}dt_{1}dt_{2})}=1}\left|\int_{1}^{2}\int_{1}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\partial_{t_{1}}^{\gamma}\partial_{t_{2}}^{\gamma}\nu^{x}(t_{1},t_{2})g(x,t_{1},t_{2})\,d\mu_{0}(x)dt_{1}dt_{2}\right|.\end{split}

Fix

with

\|g\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(d\mu_{0}\,dt_{1}\,dt_{2})}=1,

and write

. Decomposing in the

-variable we get:

(37)

\displaystyle\left|\int\partial_{t_{1}}^{\gamma}A_{t_{1}}\bigl(\mu_{1}\,\partial_{t_{2}}^{\gamma}A_{t_{2}}\mu_{2}\bigr)(x)g(x,t_{1},t_{2})\,d\mu_{0}(x)\,dt_{1}\,dt_{2}\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j\geq 0}\left\|\partial_{t_{1}}^{\gamma}A_{t_{1}}P_{j}\bigl(\mu_{1}\,\partial_{t_{2}}^{\gamma}A_{t_{2}}\mu_{2}\bigr)\right\|_{L^{p}(dx\,dt_{1}\,dt_{2})}\left\|\widetilde{P}_{j}(g_{t_{1},t_{2}}\mu_{0})\right\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(dx\,dt_{1}\,dt_{2})}.

To estimate the first factor, we note that by using the local smoothing hypothesis in the variables
(x,t_{1})
with
t_{2}
fixed and then taking

norm in the variable

we get

\left\|\partial_{t_{1}}^{\gamma}A_{t_{1}}P_{j}\bigl(\mu_{1}\,\partial_{t_{2}}^{\gamma}A_{t_{2}}\mu_{2}\bigr)\right\|_{L^{p}(dx\,dt_{1}\,dt_{2})}\lesssim 2^{j(-\eta+2\varepsilon)}\left\|P_{j}\bigl(\mu_{1}\,\partial_{t_{2}}^{\gamma}A_{t_{2}}\mu_{2}\bigr)\right\|_{L^{p}(dx\,dt_{2})}.

By Lemma

applied to the measure

and integrated in

,

\left\|P_{j}\bigl(\mu_{1}\,\partial_{t_{2}}^{\gamma}A_{t_{2}}\mu_{2}\bigr)\right\|_{L^{p}(dx\,dt_{2})}\lesssim 2^{j(d-s_{E}+\varepsilon)/p^{\prime}}\left\|\partial_{t_{2}}^{\gamma}A_{t_{2}}\mu_{2}\right\|_{L^{p}(d\mu_{1}\,dt_{2})}.

From the quantitative estimate we proved to get pinned nonempty interior for Falconer pinned distance problem, i.e., estimate (

) in the proof of Theorem

\|\partial_{t_{2}}^{1/p+\varepsilon}A_{t_{2}}\mu_{2}\|_{L^{p}(d\mu_{1}dt_{2})}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}1,

4.2.
Another toy case: 3-chains

In this section, we will give a sketch of the proof of the nonempty interior result for 3-chains which are pinned at a leaf (endpoint). This is a prototypical case that illustrates many of the techniques that we will need when dealing with the most general case of arbitrary trees. We will focus on showcasing the inductive scheme, i.e. on how one can apply the 2-chain result proved in the previous subsection to the 3-chain case.

Similarly to the 2-chain case, one starts with finding four pairwise separated subsets

4.3.
The final toy case: star graphs

We will say a graph
G
is a star graph if it is a tree and there exists a distinguished vertex
v
such that every edge in

has
v
as one of its endpoints. Let

denote a tree with

is the distinguished vertex (and also the point at which we pin).

We start by taking

with positive

-dimensional Hausdorff measure. Construct for each

).

Fix

and let

. Then the relevant density to consider (from Lemma
21
in the next section) is

\nu^{x}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{k})=\prod_{i=1}^{k}A_{t_{i}}(\mu_{i})(x).

When

(where

)), this is easily seen to be continuous for

a.e.

(which may also depend on

) such that for

\left\|\prod_{i=1}^{k}\partial_{t_{i}}^{\gamma}A_{t_{i}}(\mu_{i})(x)\right\|_{L^{p}(d\tilde{\mu}_{0}(x)\,d\mathbf{t})}\leq C_{k},

\displaystyle\bigg(\Phi_{H_{1},{v_{0}}}^{x_{0}}(x_{1,1},\ldots,x_{1,n_{1}}),\Phi_{H_{2},{v_{0}}}^{x_{0}}(x_{2,1},\ldots,x_{2,n_{2}}),\ldots,\Phi_{H_{m},{v_{0}}}^{x_{0}}(x_{m,1},\ldots,x_{m,n_{m}})\bigg)

If
m=1
, then we let
H^{\prime}
be the subgraph induced by deleting
v_{0}
and let
v_{1}
be the unique vertex in
\mathcal{T}
such that
(v_{0},v_{1})
is an edge. In this case, we define its graph map as

\Phi_{\mathcal{T}^{n},v_{0}}^{x_{0}}(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n})=(|x_{0}-x_{1}|,\Phi_{H^{\prime},{v_{1}}}^{x_{1}}(x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}))

That finishes the inductive definition of the graph map.

Let
\nu_{\mathcal{T},v_{0}}^{x_{0}}=(\Phi_{\mathcal{T},v_{0}}^{x_{0}})_{*}(\prod_{j=1}^{n}\mu)
be the pushforward of the measure
\prod_{j=1}^{n}\mu
by the map
\Phi^{x_{0}}_{\mathcal{T},v_{0}}
. Note that
\nu^{x_{0}}_{\mathcal{T},v_{0}}
is a probability measure supported in
\Delta_{\mathcal{T}^{n},v_{0}}^{x_{0}}(E)
.

We want to express the density
\nu_{\mathcal{T},v_{0}}^{x_{0}}
in terms of the densities
\nu_{H_{l},v_{0}}^{x_{0}}
(case
m\geq 2
) and terms of
\nu_{H^{\prime},v_{1}}^{x_{1}}
(case
m=1
), which is accomplished by the following lemma.

Lemma 21
.

Denote
\mathbf{t}=(\mathbf{t}_{1},\mathbf{t}_{2},\dots,\mathbf{t}_{m})
and
\mathbf{t}_{l}=(t_{l,1},\dots,t_{l,n_{l}})
. If
m\geq 2
then one has

\nu^{x_{0}}_{\mathcal{T},v_{0}}(\mathbf{t})=\prod_{l=1}^{m}\nu^{x_{0}}_{H_{l},v_{0}}(\mathbf{t_{l}}).

If
m=1
, then

\nu_{\mathcal{T},{v_{0}}}^{x_{0}}(t_{1},\dots,t_{n})=A_{t_{1}}(\mu(\cdot)\nu_{H^{\prime},v_{1}}^{(\cdot)}(t_{2},t_{3},\dots,t_{n}))(x_{0}).

Proof of Lemma
21
.

Let
\phi
be a smooth test function. We recall that each measure
\mu
has been mollified, though we suppress this from the notation. Then we have

(41)

\displaystyle F(\phi):=

\displaystyle\int\phi(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})\nu^{x_{0}}_{\mathcal{T},v_{0}}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})dt_{1}\dots dt_{n}

(42)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\phi(\Phi_{\mathcal{T},v_{0}}^{x_{0}}(x_{1,1},\ldots,x_{m,n_{m}}))\prod_{l=1}^{m}\prod_{j=1}^{n_{l}}\mu(x_{l,j})\,d\mathbf{x}

(43)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\phi(\Phi_{\mathcal{T},v_{0}}^{x_{0}}(\mathbf{x}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{x}_{m}))\prod_{l=1}^{m}(\prod_{j=1}^{n_{l}}\mu)(\mathbf{x}_{l})\,d\mathbf{x}

where
\mathbf{x}_{l}=(x_{l,1},x_{l,2},\dots,x_{l,n_{l}})
.
By construction every one of the
n
components of the mapping
\Phi_{\mathcal{T},v_{0}}^{x_{0}}
is of the form
|x_{0}-x_{l,1}|
or
|x_{l,j}-x_{l,k}|
where
(v_{l,j},v_{l,k})\in\mathcal{E}_{l}^{\prime}
. We change variables
y_{l,1}=x_{0}-x_{l,1}
and
y_{l,j,k}=x_{l,j}-x_{l,k}
whenever
(x_{l,j},x_{l,k})\in\mathcal{E}_{l}^{\prime}
. For each
l
, since
|\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{l}|=n_{l}-1
we can relabel the variables
\{y_{l,j,k}\colon(v_{l,j},v_{l,k})\in\mathcal{E}_{l}^{\prime}\}
as a
\{y_{l,p}\colon 2\leq p\leq n_{l}\}
and let
(y_{l,1}\cdots y_{l,n_{l}})=\Psi_{l}(x_{l,1},\cdots,x_{l,n_{l}})
, or in shorter notation
\mathbf{y}_{l}=\Psi_{l}(\mathbf{x}_{l})
. The coordinate change map is then

\mathbf{y}=(\mathbf{y}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{y}_{m})=(\Psi_{1}(\mathbf{x}_{1}),\dots,\Psi_{m}(\mathbf{x}_{m}))=:\Psi(\mathbf{x}).

Additionally, notice that we have

\Phi_{\mathcal{T},v_{0}}^{x_{0}}(\mathbf{x}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{x}_{m})=\Big(\Phi_{H_{1},v_{0}}^{x_{0}}(\mathbf{x}_{1}),\Phi_{H_{2},v_{0}}^{x_{0}}(\mathbf{x}_{2}),\ldots,\Phi_{H_{m},v_{0}}^{x_{0}}(\mathbf{x}_{m})\Big)

by construction. In the new coordinate system, the argument of
\phi
is very simple:

(44)

\Phi_{\mathcal{T},v_{0}}^{x_{0}}(\Psi^{-1}(\mathbf{y}))=(|\mathbf{y_{1}}|,|\mathbf{y_{2}}|,\ldots,|\mathbf{y_{m}}|),

where we are abusing notation to mean
|\mathbf{y_{l}}|=(|y_{l,1}|,\ldots,|y_{l,n_{l}}|).
In fact, we can even notice that the affine map
\Psi^{-1}
can be represented with its linear part in a block matrix form, where the
l
th block is exactly the linear part of the map
\Psi_{l}^{-1}
arising as a coordinate change
\mathbf{x}_{l}=\Psi_{l}^{-1}(\mathbf{y}_{l})
when computing
\nu_{H_{l},v_{0}}^{x_{0}}
; we leave this as an exercise for the reader to verify.

The Jacobian determinant of the change of variables splits

J(\mathbf{y}):=|\det(\Psi^{-1})(\mathbf{y})|=\prod_{l=1}^{m}|\det(\Psi_{l}^{-1})(\mathbf{y}_{l})|=:\prod_{l=1}^{m}J_{l}(\mathbf{y}_{l}).

Moreover each
\Psi_{l}^{-1}
is affine with a linear part being a triangular matrix with 1’s in the diagonal (so determinant 1), which gives
J_{l}\equiv 1
. So after the change of variables one has

F(\phi)=\int\phi(|\mathbf{y}_{1}|,\dots,|\mathbf{y}_{m}|)\prod_{l=1}^{m}\left[(\prod_{j=1}^{n_{l}}\mu)(\Psi_{l}^{-1}(\mathbf{y}_{l}))\,\right]d\mathbf{y}

We then change each of the new variables
y_{l,j}
in polar coordinates
y_{l,j}=t_{l,j}\omega_{l,j}
for
1\leq j\leq n_{l}
, where the
\omega_{l,j}
’s are unit vectors. Thus, we can split the spherical integrals into an
m
-fold product, i.e.

F(\phi)=\int_{\mathbf{t}}\phi(\mathbf{t})\prod_{l=1}^{m}(t_{l,1}\dots t_{l,n_{l}})^{d-1}\left(\int_{(S^{d-1})^{n_{l}}}(\prod_{j=1}^{n_{l}}\mu)(\Psi_{l}^{-1}(t_{l,1}\omega_{l,1},\dots t_{l,n_{l}}\omega_{l,n_{l}}))\,d\sigma^{n_{l}}(\mathbf{\omega_{l}})\right)\,d\mathbf{t}.

Notice that because of the block structure of the coordinate change, the
l
th term in the product above is the density corresponding to the subtree
H_{l}

since that would be the expression obtained for
\nu_{\mathcal{T},v_{0}}^{x_{0}}
if this procedure was performed for
H_{l}
instead of
\mathcal{T}
, so we can say that for each
l=1,\ldots,m

(t_{l,1}\dots t_{l,n_{l}})^{d-1}\int_{(S^{d-1})^{n_{l}}}\prod_{j=1}^{n_{l}}\mu(\Psi_{l}^{-1}(t_{l,1}\omega_{l,1},\dots t_{l,n_{l}}\omega_{l,n_{l}}))\,d\sigma^{n_{l}}(\mathbf{\omega_{l}})=\nu_{H_{l},v_{0}}^{x_{0}}(\mathbf{t}_{l})

We will also alternatively denote
\nu_{H_{l},v_{0}}^{x_{0}}(\mathbf{t}_{l})=:A^{(H_{l},v_{0})}_{t_{l,1},\ldots,t_{l,n_{l}}}(\mu,\ldots,\mu)(x_{0})
.
Thus, we have that

\nu_{\mathcal{T},v_{0}}^{x}(\mathbf{t})=\prod_{l=1}^{m}\nu_{H_{l},v_{0}}^{x}(\mathbf{t_{l}})=\prod_{l=1}^{m}A^{(H_{l},v_{0})}_{t_{l,1},\ldots,t_{l,n_{l}}}(\mu,\ldots,\mu)(x).

We now turn to the case where
m=1
. Consider the tree
H^{\prime}
obtained by deleting
v_{0}
from
H:=\mathcal{T}
. Put an orientation on the edges of
H^{\prime}
via the rule that an edge’s direction goes from
v_{i}
to
v_{j}
if and only if
v_{j}
is farther from
v_{0}
than
v_{i}
is (where distance is measured in the sense of the walk with the fewest edges). Form a matrix
M_{H^{\prime}}
whose
(i,j)
th entry is

(M_{H^{\prime}})_{i,j}=\delta_{ij}-\text{Edge}(j,i),

where
\text{Edge}(j,i)
is
1
if there is a directed edge from
v_{j}
to
v_{i}
and
0
otherwise. By relabeling the vertices if needed, we may assume
M_{H^{\prime}}
is a lower triangular matrix with 1s along the diagonal, one entry with value
-1
in each row (except the first row), and all other entries are 0. If we let
Abs
denote the map which replaces each entry of a vector with its absolute value, then by construction,
M_{H^{\prime}}
induces the graph map in the sense that

\Phi_{H^{\prime},v_{1}}^{x_{1}}(x_{2},\ldots,x_{n})=\text{Abs}(M_{H^{\prime}}(x_{2},\ldots,x_{n})-\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}}x_{1}e_{j}),

where
\mathcal{J}
is the set of all indices
j
of vertices for which there is an edge from
v_{1}
to
v_{j}
. Similarly, we have that

\Phi_{H,v_{0}}^{x_{0}}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=\text{Abs}(M_{H}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})-x_{0}e_{1}).

By construction (and using the fact that
v_{0}
is a leaf), we have that
M_{H}
takes the form

\begin{bmatrix}1&0&\cdots&0\\
-1&M_{H^{\prime},(1,1)}&\cdots&M_{H^{\prime},(1,n-1)}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&M_{H^{\prime},(n-1,1)}&\cdots&M_{H^{\prime},(n-1,n-1)}\end{bmatrix}

We consider how to invert this matrix using row operations. First, the first row can be added to the second row (and no other rows, since
v_{0}
is a leaf). Then, the second row can be added to all rows below it which have a
-1
entry in the second column, and the procedure is iterated. Thus, the inverse takes the form

\begin{bmatrix}1&0&\cdots&0\\
1&M_{H^{\prime},(1,1)}^{-1}&\cdots&M_{H^{\prime},(1,n-1)}^{-1}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
1&M_{H^{\prime},(n-1,1)}^{-1}&\cdots&M_{H^{\prime},(n-1,n-1)}^{-1}\end{bmatrix}

In particular, notice that
M_{H}^{-1}(x_{0}e_{1})=x_{0}\mathbf{1}
, where
\mathbf{1}
is the vector whose entries are all equal to 1. With this established, we return to computing the density. Changing variables via
\mathbf{y}=M_{H}(\mathbf{x})-x_{0}e_{1}
(we also write
\mathbf{y}=(y_{1},\mathbf{y^{\prime}})
for convenience and let
t_{j}=|y_{j}|
) leads to the formula

F(\phi)=\int_{\mathbf{y}}\phi(\mathbf{t})\mu(y_{1}+x_{0})(\prod_{j=2}^{n}\mu)(M_{H}^{-1}(\mathbf{y})+x_{0}\mathbf{1})\,d\mathbf{y}.

The form of the inverse
M_{H}^{-1}
guarantees that a
x_{0}+y_{1}
summand appears in each argument of a
\mu
factor. Going to polar coordinates
y_{i}=t_{i}\omega_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq n
, we deduce immediately the desired form

\nu_{\mathcal{T},{v_{0}}}^{x_{0}}(\mathbf{t})=A_{t_{1}}(\mu(\cdot)\nu_{H^{\prime},v_{1}}^{(\cdot)}(t_{2},t_{3},\dots,t_{n}))(x_{0}).

∎

Note that for the tree given in Example
1
, by using

\Phi^{x_{0}}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4})=(|x_{1}-x_{0}|,|x_{2}-x_{0}|,|x_{3}-x_{2}|,|x_{4}-x_{0}|)

we can explicitly compute

\nu^{x}_{\mathcal{T},v_{0}}(t_{1},t_{2},t_{3},t_{4})=A_{t_{1}}(\mu)(x)A_{t_{2}}(\mu A_{t_{3}}(\mu))(x)A_{t_{4}}(\mu)(x),

which is a product of 1-chain operators (coming from
v_{1}
and
v_{4}
) and a 2-chain operator pinned at
v_{0}
, coming from the subtree spanned by
v_{0},v_{2},
and
v_{3}
.

We are finally ready to state our main technical result, which will imply the pinned nonempty interior result for trees. Recall the definition of
(1-\epsilon)
-restriction of a measure in Subsection
2.2
. In the proposition below we will use
A_{\mathbf{t}}^{(\mathcal{T}^{j},v_{0})}(\mu,\mu,\dots,\mu)(x)

as an alternative notation for
\nu_{\mathcal{T}^{j},v_{0}}^{x}(\mathbf{t})
, which is supported in
\Delta_{\mathcal{T}^{j},v_{0}}^{x}(E)
.

Proposition 22
.

Let
d\geq 2
and fix a Borel set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Assume that the local smoothing estimate (
1
) holds for some
p\in(2,\infty)
and
\eta>0
, and that
\textnormal{dim}_{\mathcal{H}}(E)>d-\eta
. For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
and every
\epsilon>0
, there exists
\gamma>\frac{1}{p}
and a sequence of Frostman probability measures
\{\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{n}\}
supported in
E
satisfying
\mu_{n}
is a
(1-\epsilon)
-restriction of
\mu_{1}
and that
\mu_{i^{\prime}}
is a restriction of
\mu_{i}
whenever
i^{\prime}\geq i
, such that for every tree
\mathcal{T}^{k}
with
k+1
vertices with a distinguished vertex
v_{0}
, where
k\leq n
, and such that for every
\tau>0
and every
\tau
-restriction measure
\nu_{n}
of
\mu_{n}
, we have that

\|\partial_{t_{1},\dots,t_{k}}^{\gamma}A_{t_{1},\dots,t_{k}}^{(\mathcal{T}^{k},v_{0})}(\nu_{n},\ldots,\nu_{n})\|_{L^{p}(d\nu_{n}\,dt_{1}\dots dt_{k})}\leq C_{n,\epsilon,\tau,d}

where
\partial_{t_{1},\dots,t_{k}}^{\gamma}
is a shortcut notation for
\partial_{t_{1}}^{\gamma}\partial_{t_{2}}^{\gamma}\dots\partial_{t_{k}}^{\gamma}
.

Proof.

We will proceed by strong induction on
n
: the base case follows by (
20
) and the remark at the end of the proof of Theorem
2
concerning restriction measures, so we will assume that we know the result for all
k\leq n-1
. Fix a tree
\mathcal{T}^{n}
with
n+1
vertices and distinguished vertex
v_{0}
. Let
\mu_{n-1}
be a Frostman probability measure that works with the parameter
\tau/2
for all trees with at most
n
vertices and is a
(1-c_{n}\epsilon)
-restriction of the
\mu_{1}
measure that exists from the base case, where
c_{n}
is sufficiently small. For every tree
\mathcal{T}^{r}
with at most
r\leq n
vertices and a distinguished vertex
v_{0}
, we consider the function

h_{\mathcal{T}^{r},v_{0}}(x)=\|\partial_{t_{1},\dots,t_{r}}^{\gamma}A_{t_{1},\dots,t_{r}}^{(\mathcal{T}^{r},v_{0})}(\mu_{n-1},\ldots,\mu_{n-1})(x)\|_{L^{p}(dt_{1}\dots dt_{r})}.

Thus, since
\mathcal{T}^{r}
has at most
n
vertices, our induction hypothesis guarantees that
h_{\mathcal{T}^{r},v_{0}}\in L^{p}(\mu_{n-1})
. We do this for all such pairs of trees and pins
(\mathcal{T}^{r},v_{0})
where
r\leq n
. Since there are finitely many such pairs we still have

h_{r,v_{0}}(x):=\sum_{(\mathcal{T}^{r},v_{0})}h_{\mathcal{T}^{r},v_{0}}(x)\in L^{p}(\mu_{n-1}).

Hence, for every
\beta>0
, there exists a set
E_{\beta}\subset E
such that
\mu_{n-1}(E_{\beta})>1-\beta
and
|h_{r}(x)|<N_{\beta}
for all
x\in E_{\beta}
and some sufficiently large
N_{\beta}
. We can choose
0<\beta_{n}\ll\epsilon
so that
E_{n}:=E_{\beta_{n}}
is a
(1-\epsilon)
-restriction of
\mu_{1}
. We choose the restricted probability measure
(\mu_{n-1})_{E_{n}}
to be
\mu_{n}
. Notice that by our choice, we will have that for all
(\mathcal{T}^{r},v_{0})
with
r\leq n
,

|h_{\mathcal{T}^{r},v_{0}}(x)|\leq N_{\epsilon,n},\,\text{ for all }x\in\text{supp}(\mu_{n}).

Now, we split into two cases.

Case 1:
v_{0}
is not a leaf.
In particular, deleting the vertex
v_{0}
results in
m\geq 2
new trees. We will consider the pinned trees
H_{1},\cdots,H_{m}
, each with the pin
v_{0}
. Recalling the notation
\mathbf{t}=(t_{1},t_{2},\dots,t_{n})
and
\mathbf{t_{l}}=(t_{l,1},t_{l,2},\dots,t_{l,n_{l}})
. Our goal is to bound

(45)

\|\partial_{\mathbf{t}}^{\gamma}\prod_{l=1}^{m}A^{(H_{l},v_{0})}_{\mathbf{t}_{l}}(\nu_{n},\ldots,\nu_{n})\|_{L^{p}(d\nu_{n}\,d\mathbf{t})}\leq C_{n,d,\varepsilon,\tau}.

where
\nu_{n}
is any
\tau
-restriction of
\mu_{n}
. By Hölder’s inequality, for each
x\in\text{supp}(\nu_{n})\subset E_{n}
, we bound

\displaystyle\|\partial_{\mathbf{t}}^{\gamma}

\displaystyle\prod_{l=1}^{m}A^{(H_{l},v_{0})}_{\mathbf{t}_{l}}(\nu_{n},\ldots,\nu_{n})(x)\|_{L^{p}(d\mathbf{t})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\partial_{\mathbf{t_{1}}}^{\gamma}A^{(H_{1},v_{0})}_{\mathbf{t}_{1}}(\nu_{n},\ldots,\nu_{n})(x)\|_{L^{p}(d\mathbf{t}_{1})}\prod_{l=2}^{m}\|\partial^{\gamma}_{\mathbf{t}_{l}}A^{(H_{l},v_{0})}_{\mathbf{t}_{l}}(\nu_{n},\ldots,\nu_{n})(x)\|_{L^{\infty}(d\mathbf{t}_{l})}.

The first factor is bounded by a constant
C_{n-1,\epsilon,\tau,d}
by the induction hypothesis, where we are using that a
\tau
-restriction of a
(1-\epsilon)
-restriction of
\mu_{1}
is also a
\tau/2
-restriction of
\mu_{1}
. The second factor is bounded by
(N_{\epsilon,n})^{m-1}
, by construction of
\mu_{n}
, which completes this case.

Case 2:
v_{0}
is a leaf.
In this case, let
H^{\prime}
denote the subtree obtained by deleting
v_{0}
from
\mathcal{T}^{n}
and let
v_{1}
denote the unique vertex such that
(v_{0},v_{1})
is an edge in
\mathcal{T}^{n}
. We have the formula

A_{\mathbf{t}}^{(\mathcal{T},v_{0})}(\nu_{n},\ldots,\nu_{n})(x)=A_{t_{1}}(\nu_{n}A^{(H^{\prime},v_{1})}_{t_{2},\ldots,t_{n}}(\nu_{n},\ldots,\nu_{n}))(x),

where once again,
\nu_{n}
is a
\tau
-restriction of
\mu_{n}
.

By duality, there is a function
g
with
\|g\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(d\nu_{n}\,d\mathbf{t})}=1
, where we also use the notation
g_{\mathbf{t}}(y)=g(y,t_{1},\dots,t_{n})
, such that

\|\partial_{\mathbf{t}}^{\gamma}A_{\mathbf{t}}^{(\mathcal{T},v_{0})}(\nu_{n},\ldots,\nu_{n})\|_{L^{p}(d\nu_{n}\,d\mathbf{t})}=\left|\int\partial_{\mathbf{t}}^{\gamma}A_{\mathbf{t}}^{(\mathcal{T},v_{0})}(\nu_{n},\ldots,\nu_{n})(y)g(y,\mathbf{t})\,d\nu_{n}(y)\,d\mathbf{t}\right|.

We will use the notation
\mathbf{t}=(t_{1},\mathbf{t}^{\prime})
. We now take Littlewood-Paley expansions and estimate

\displaystyle\left|\int\partial_{t_{1}}^{\gamma}A_{t_{1}}(\nu_{n}\partial^{\gamma}_{\mathbf{t}^{\prime}}A^{(H^{\prime},v_{1})}_{\mathbf{t}^{\prime}}(\nu_{n},\ldots,\nu_{n})(y)\,g_{\mathbf{t}}(y)\,d\nu_{n}(y)\,dt_{1}\,d\mathbf{t}^{\prime}\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\left|\int_{[1,2]^{n-1}}\int_{1}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\partial^{\gamma}_{t_{1}}A_{t_{1}}(P_{j}(\nu_{n}\partial^{\gamma}_{\mathbf{t}^{\prime}}A_{\mathbf{t}^{\prime}}^{(H^{\prime},v_{1})}(\nu_{n},\ldots,\nu_{n})))(y)g_{\mathbf{t}}(y)\,d\nu_{n}(y)\,dt_{1}\,d\mathbf{t}^{\prime}\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\left|\int_{[1,2]^{n-1}}\int_{1}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\partial^{\gamma}_{t_{1}}A_{t_{1}}(P_{j}(\nu_{n}\partial^{\gamma}_{\mathbf{t}^{\prime}}A_{\mathbf{t}^{\prime}}^{(H^{\prime},v_{1})}(\nu_{n},\ldots,\nu_{n})))(y)\sum_{i=-2}^{2}(P_{j+i}[g_{\mathbf{t}}d\nu_{n}])dy\,dt_{1}\,d\mathbf{t}^{\prime}\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\left(\|\partial^{\gamma}_{t_{1}}A_{t_{1}}(P_{j}(\nu_{n}\partial^{\gamma}_{\mathbf{t}^{\prime}}A_{\mathbf{t}^{\prime}}^{(H^{\prime},v_{1})}(\nu_{n},\ldots,\nu_{n})))\|_{L^{p}(dy\,dt_{1}\,d\mathbf{t}^{\prime})}\sum_{i=-2}^{2}\|P_{j+i}(g_{\mathbf{t}}\nu_{n})\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(dy\,dt_{1}\,d\mathbf{t}^{\prime})}\right).

Applying Lemma
17
with each
\mathbf{t}\in[1,2]^{n}
fixed, let us bound the final factor via

\sum_{i=-2}^{2}\|P_{j+i}(g_{\mathbf{t}}\nu_{n})\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(dydt_{1}d\mathbf{t}^{\prime})}\lesssim 2^{\frac{j}{p}(d-s_{E}+\varepsilon)}\|\|g_{\mathbf{t}}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(d\nu_{n})}\|_{L^{p}(d\mathbf{t})}=2^{\frac{j}{p}(d-s_{E}+\varepsilon)}.

For the first factor, we use local smoothing and Lemma
17
to estimate for
\gamma=1/p+\varepsilon
and for each
\mathbf{t}^{\prime}\in[1,2]^{n-1}
,

\displaystyle\|\partial^{\gamma}_{t_{1}}A_{t_{1}}

\displaystyle(P_{j}(\nu_{n}\partial_{\mathbf{t}^{\prime}}^{\gamma}A_{\mathbf{t}^{\prime}}^{(H^{\prime},v_{1})}(\nu_{n},\ldots,\nu_{n})))\|_{L^{p}(dy\,dt_{1})}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{j(-\eta(p,d)+2\varepsilon)}\|P_{j}(\nu_{n}\partial_{\mathbf{t}^{\prime}}^{\gamma}A_{\mathbf{t}^{\prime}}^{(H^{\prime},v_{1})}(\nu_{n},\ldots,\nu_{n}))\|_{L^{p}(dy)}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{j(-\eta(p,d)+2\varepsilon)}2^{\frac{j}{p^{\prime}}(d-s_{E}+\varepsilon)}\|\partial_{\mathbf{t}^{\prime}}^{\gamma}A_{\mathbf{t}^{\prime}}^{(H^{\prime},v_{1})}(\nu_{n},\ldots,\nu_{n})\|_{L^{p}(d\nu_{n})}.

Hence,

\displaystyle\|\partial^{\gamma}_{t_{1}}A_{t_{1}}

\displaystyle(P_{j}(\nu_{n}\partial_{\mathbf{t}^{\prime}}^{\gamma}A_{\mathbf{t}^{\prime}}^{(H^{\prime},v_{1})}(\nu_{n},\ldots,\nu_{n})))\|_{L^{p}(dy\,d\mathbf{t})}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{j(-\eta(p,d)+2\varepsilon)}2^{\frac{j}{p^{\prime}}(d-s_{E}+\varepsilon)}\|\partial_{\mathbf{t}^{\prime}}^{\gamma}A_{\mathbf{t}^{\prime}}^{(H^{\prime},v_{1})}(\nu_{n},\ldots,\nu_{n})\|_{L^{p}(d\nu_{n}d\mathbf{t})}.

The induction hypothesis implies that

\|\partial_{\mathbf{t}^{\prime}}^{\gamma}A_{\mathbf{t}^{\prime}}^{(H^{\prime},v_{1})}(\nu_{n},\ldots,\nu_{n})\|_{L^{p}(d\nu_{n}d\mathbf{t})}\leq C_{n-1,\epsilon,\tau,d}.

Substituting all the bounds together leads to

\|\partial_{\mathbf{t}}^{\gamma}A_{\mathbf{t}}^{(\mathcal{T},v_{0})}(\nu_{n},\ldots,\nu_{n})\|_{L^{p}(d\nu_{n}\,d\mathbf{t})}\lesssim C_{n-1,\epsilon,\tau,d}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}2^{j(-\eta(p,d)+2\varepsilon)}2^{j(d-s_{E}+\varepsilon)}.

Thus, we deduce that if
\varepsilon
is sufficiently small (depending on
s_{E}
), then the sum converges and

\|\partial_{\mathbf{t}}^{\gamma}A_{\mathbf{t}}^{(\mathcal{T},v_{0})}(\nu_{n},\ldots,\nu_{n})\|_{L^{p}(d\nu_{n}\,d\mathbf{t})}\leq C_{n,\epsilon,\tau,d}

∎

Combining Proposition
22
with the Sobolev embedding theorem implies that measure
\nu^{x_{0}}_{\mathcal{T},v_{0}}
has continuous density. We deduce Theorem
8
as an immediate corollary.

Log-linear Backstepping control on SE 2 ​ ( 3 ) \mathrm{SE}_{2}(3)
Li-Yu Lin, Benjamin Perseghetti, James Goppert

Abstract
Most of the rigid-body systems which evolve on nonlinear Lie groups where Euclidean control designs lose geometric meaning. In this paper, we introduce a log-linear backstepping control law on S ​ E 2 ​ ( 3 ) SE_{2}(3) that preserves full rotational–translational coupling. Leveraging a class of mixed-invariant system, which is a group-affine dynamic model, we derive exact logarithmic error dynamics that are linear in the Lie-algebra. The closed-form expressions for the left- and right-Jacobian inverses of S ​ E 2 ​ ( 3 ) SE_{2}(3) are expressed in the paper, which provides us the exact error dynamics without local approximations. A log-linear backstepping control design ensures the exponentially stable for our error dynamics, since our error dynamics is a block-triangular structure, this allows us to use Linear Matrix Inequality (LMI) formulation or H inf H_{\inf} gain performance design. This work establishes the exact backstepping framework for a class of mixed-invariant system, providing a geometrically consistent foundation for future Unmanned Aerial Vehicle (UAV) and spacecraft control design.



I
Background and Preliminaries

I-A

Lie-Group and Invariant Error Concepts

Rigid-body motion evolves on nonlinear manifolds such as
\mathrm{SO}(3)
or
\mathrm{SE}(3)
, where Euclidean subtraction loses geometric meaning. A Lie group
G
provides a globally valid configuration space with associated Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
(the tangent space at the identity). The exponential and logarithmic maps,

\displaystyle\operatorname{Exp}:\mathfrak{g}\!\to\!G,\qquad\operatorname{Log}:G\!\to\!\mathfrak{g},

(1)

relate algebra elements to finite motions via
X=\operatorname{Exp}(\xi^{\wedge})
.

We will employ the
\mathrm{SE}_{2}(3)
Lie group, whose matrix representation is given by:

X=\begin{bmatrix}R&v&p\\
0&1&0\\
0&0&1\end{bmatrix}\in\mathrm{SE}_{2}(3),

(2)

where
p\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
, inertial-frame velocity
v\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
,
and attitude
R\in\mathrm{SO}(3)
,
This group is capable of representing 3D rigid body rotation and translation. In addition, it can handle translational dynamics as we will show in the following. The associated Lie algebra,
\mathfrak{se}_{2}(3)
, is given by:

[x]^{\wedge}=\begin{bmatrix}[\omega]^{\wedge}&a&v\\
0&1&0\\
0&0&1\end{bmatrix}\in\mathfrak{se}_{2}(3),

(3)

where
x=\begin{bmatrix}v&a&\omega\end{bmatrix}^{T}
is the element in the Lie algebra, and
[\cdot]^{\wedge}
indicates the wedge operator that maps the element from
\mathbb{R}^{9}
to the
\mathfrak{se}_{2}(3)
Lie algebra. Although the angular velocity,
\omega
, cannot be embedded in the
SE_{2}(3)
Lie group, we can consider the dynamics of the angular velocity as a separate sub-system in our application.

For trajectories
X=(p,v,R)^{\wedge}
,
\bar{X}=(\bar{p},\bar{v},\bar{R})^{\wedge}

\in G
, the
left-invariant
error

\displaystyle\eta=\bar{X}^{-1}X,

(4)

is unchanged under
left
multiplication of both
X
and
\bar{X}
by the same group element (a change of body frame), whereas the
right-invariant
error
\eta=X\bar{X}^{-1}
is invariant under right multiplication.

Define the
left-invariant configuration error
between the true and reference states as

\displaystyle\eta_{p}

\displaystyle=\bar{R}^{\top}(p-\bar{p}),

(5)

\displaystyle\eta_{v}

\displaystyle=\bar{R}^{\top}(v-\bar{v}),

\displaystyle\eta_{R}

\displaystyle=\bar{R}^{\top}R\in\mathrm{SO}(3),

which together form the group element
\eta=[(\eta_{p},\eta_{v},\eta_{R})]^{\wedge}\in\mathrm{SE}_{2}(3)

satisfying
\eta=I
when
R=\bar{R}
,
v=\bar{v}
, and
p=\bar{p}
.

For matrices
A,B\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
, the
commutator
(Lie bracket) is

\displaystyle[A,B]\coloneqq AB-BA,

(6)

which satisfies antisymmetry and the Jacobi identity.
The associated
adjoint functions
are

\displaystyle\operatorname{ad}_{A}(B)=[A,B],\qquad\operatorname{Ad}_{[A]^{\vee}}B=A^{-1}BA.

(7)

The logarithmic (Lie algebra) error is defined as

\xi=[\operatorname{Log}(\eta)]^{\vee}\in\mathfrak{se}_{2}(3),

(8)

which provides a minimal coordinate representation of the configuration deviation.

Define the augmented group elements

\displaystyle X=\begin{bmatrix}R&v&p\\[3.0pt]
\mathbf{0}_{1\times 3}&1&0\\
\mathbf{0}_{1\times 3}&0&1\end{bmatrix},\qquad\bar{X}=\begin{bmatrix}\bar{R}&\bar{v}&\bar{p}\\[3.0pt]
\mathbf{0}_{1\times 3}&1&0\\
\mathbf{0}_{1\times 3}&0&1\end{bmatrix},

(9)

where
X

\bar{X}
is the reference trajectory.

Consider a specific class of mixed-invariant system:

\dot{X}=(M-C)X+X(N+C),

(10)

\displaystyle M=\begin{bmatrix}0&g&0\end{bmatrix}^{\wedge},\qquad N=\begin{bmatrix}0&Te_{T}&\omega\end{bmatrix}^{\wedge},

\displaystyle C=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{0}_{3\times 3}&\mathbf{0}_{3\times 3}&\mathbf{0}_{3\times 1}\\
\mathbf{0}_{1\times 3}&\mathbf{0}_{1\times 3}&1\\
\mathbf{0}_{1\times 3}&\mathbf{0}_{1\times 3}&0\end{bmatrix}.

(11)

where
M,N\in\mathfrak{se}_{2}(3)
and the system is
mixed-invariant
. The left-invariant term expresses gravity
g
in the inertial frame, and the right-invariant term expresses thrust input and angular velocity in the body frame, where
e_{T}
denotes the unit vector of thrust input.

The left–invariant group error,
\eta
, between the body position and the reference position is defined as

\displaystyle\eta=\bar{X}^{-1}X\in\mathrm{SE}_{2}(3),

(12)

and the algebraic error,
\xi
, as

\displaystyle\xi=[\operatorname{Log}(\eta)]^{\vee}\in\mathfrak{se}_{2}(3).

(13)

Lemma 1
(Logarithm Error dynamics)

Let
G
be a Lie group with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
of dimension
n
.
Let
\eta=\bar{X}^{-1}X\in G
and define the log–error coordinates

\xi\coloneq[(\operatorname{Log}\eta)]^{\vee}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
For
\bar{N},\bar{M},\tilde{M},\tilde{N}\in\mathfrak{g}
denote their coordinate
vectors by

\displaystyle\bar{n}\coloneq[\bar{N}]^{\vee}=[\mathbf{0},\bar{T}e_{T},\bar{\omega}]^{\top},\bar{m}\coloneq[\bar{M}]^{\vee}=[\mathbf{0},g,0]^{\top},

(14)

\displaystyle\tilde{n}\coloneq[\tilde{N}]^{\vee}=[\mathbf{0},\tilde{T}e_{T},\tilde{\omega}]^{\top},\tilde{m}\coloneq[\tilde{M}]^{\vee}=[\mathbf{0},\tilde{g},0]^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{9}.

Then the algebraic error
\xi
satisfies

\displaystyle\boxed{\dot{\xi}=-\operatorname{ad}_{\bar{n}}\xi+([\xi^{\wedge},C])^{\vee}+J_{r}^{-1}(\xi)\tilde{n}+J_{\ell}^{-1}(\xi)\operatorname{Ad}_{[{\bar{X}^{-1}}]^{\vee}}\tilde{m},}

(15)

where

\displaystyle J_{\ell}(\xi)=\frac{\operatorname{ad}_{\xi}e^{-\operatorname{ad}_{\xi}}}{I-e^{-\operatorname{ad}_{\xi}}},\qquad J_{r}(\xi)=\frac{\operatorname{ad}_{\xi}}{I-e^{-\operatorname{ad}_{\xi}}},

(16)

where
I
is the
9\times 9
identity. Here
\operatorname{ad}_{\bar{n}}
,

J_{\ell}(\xi)
,
J_{r}(\xi)
, and
\operatorname{Ad}_{\bar{X}^{-1}}^{\vee}
are
all linear maps
\mathbb{R}^{n}\!\to\!\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Proof:

From the definition of
\eta
, we have

\displaystyle\dot{\eta}=-\bar{X}^{-1}\dot{\bar{X}}\bar{X}^{-1}{X}+\bar{X}^{-1}\dot{{X}}.

(17)

Substituting the expressions for
\dot{X}
and
\dot{\bar{X}}
gives

\displaystyle\dot{\eta}

\displaystyle=-\bar{X}^{-1}\!\big((M-C)\bar{X}+\bar{X}(N+C)\big)\bar{X}^{-1}{X}

\displaystyle begin{align}3pt]

\displaystyle\quad+\bar{X}^{-1}\!\big((\bar{M}-C){X}+{X}(\bar{N}+C)\big).

Let

\displaystyle\tilde{M}\coloneq\bar{M}-M,\qquad\tilde{N}\coloneq\bar{N}-N,

(18)

then

\displaystyle\dot{\eta}

\displaystyle=X^{-1}\tilde{M}\bar{X}+\eta\bar{N}-N\eta-\eta C+C\eta.

Left–multiplying by
\eta^{-1}
yields

\displaystyle\eta^{-1}\dot{\eta}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Ad}_{\bar{X}^{-1}}\tilde{M}+\bar{N}-\operatorname{Ad}_{\eta^{-1}}N-(I-\operatorname{Ad}_{\eta^{-1}})C.

Applying the vee operator and the right-trivialized differential of the log map,

\displaystyle\dot{\xi}=J_{r}(\xi)(\eta^{-1}\dot{\eta})^{\vee},\qquad J_{r}(\xi)=\frac{\operatorname{ad}_{\xi}}{I-e^{-\operatorname{ad}_{\xi}}},

(19)

with
\operatorname{Ad}_{\eta^{-1}}^{\vee}=e^{-\operatorname{ad}_{\xi}}
and

J_{\ell}(\xi)=\operatorname{Ad}_{\eta^{-1}}^{\vee}J_{r}(\xi)
, gives the stated result.
∎

III
Log-Linear Backstepping Control

The logarithm error dynamics can be seperated as:

\displaystyle\dot{\xi_{p}}

\displaystyle=-[\bar{\omega}]_{\times}\xi_{p}+\xi_{v}+c_{3}\tilde{\omega}+c_{2}e_{T}\tilde{T}+d_{2}\bar{R}\tilde{g}

(35)

\displaystyle\dot{\xi_{v}}

\displaystyle=-[\bar{\omega}]_{\times}\xi_{v}+[-\bar{T}e_{T}]\xi_{r}+c_{4}\tilde{\omega}+c_{1}e_{T}\tilde{T}+d_{1}\bar{R}\tilde{g}

(36)

\displaystyle\dot{\xi_{r}}

\displaystyle=-[\bar{\omega}]_{\times}\xi_{r}+c_{1}\tilde{\omega}

(37)

To reach the desire position and attitude along the thrust input, we design a feedback control with virtual control points,
\xi^{d}_{r}
and
\xi^{d}_{v}
, which represent desired attitude and velocity. We first design
\tilde{\omega}
as:

\displaystyle\tilde{\omega}=c_{1}^{-1}\left([\bar{\omega}]_{\times}\xi_{r}^{d}+\dot{\xi_{r}^{d}}-K_{r}(\xi_{r}-\xi_{r}^{d})\right),

(38)

where
K_{r}
is the control gain. Substitute back to (
37
), we then get:

\displaystyle\dot{\xi_{r}}=-[\bar{\omega}]_{\times}\xi_{r}+[\bar{\omega}]_{\times}\xi_{r}^{d}+\dot{\xi_{r}^{d}}-K_{r}(\xi_{r}-\xi_{r}^{d}),

(39)

let
e_{r}\coloneq\xi_{r}-\xi_{r}^{d}
, the dynamics of the error is:

\displaystyle\dot{e_{r}}=-[\bar{\omega}]_{\times}e_{r}-K_{r}e_{r}.

(40)

To design the velocity controller, we rewrite (
36
) with
e_{r}
:

\displaystyle\dot{\xi_{v}}

\displaystyle=-[\bar{\omega}]_{\times}\xi_{v}+[-\bar{T}e_{T}](\xi_{r}-\xi_{r}^{d}+\xi_{r}^{d})+c_{4}\tilde{\omega}+c_{1}e_{T}\tilde{T}+d_{1}\bar{R}\tilde{g}

(41)

\displaystyle=-[\bar{\omega}]_{\times}\xi_{v}+[-\bar{T}e_{T}]e_{r}+[-\bar{T}e_{T}]e_{r}\xi_{r}^{d}+c_{4}\tilde{\omega}+c_{1}e_{T}\tilde{T}+d_{1}\bar{R}\tilde{g}

The control inputs are
\tilde{T}
and
\xi_{r}^{d}
, therefore, the control is designed as:

\displaystyle[-\bar{T}e_{T}]e_{r}\xi_{r}^{d}+c_{1}e_{T}\tilde{T}=-c_{3}\tilde{\omega}-d_{1}\bar{R}\tilde{g}-K_{v}e_{v}+\dot{\xi_{v}^{d}}-[\bar{\omega}]_{\times}\xi_{v}^{d}

(42)

where
\xi_{v}^{d}
represents the desired error velocity between the vehicle and reference,
K_{v}
represents the velocity control gain, and
e_{v}\coloneq\xi_{v}-\xi_{v}^{d}
.
The dynamics of the error dynamics is:

\displaystyle\dot{e_{v}}=-[\bar{\omega}]_{\times}e_{v}-K_{v}e_{v}+[-\bar{T}e_{T}]_{\times}e_{r}

(43)

To design the position controller, we rewrite (
35
) with the
e_{v}
:

\displaystyle\dot{\xi}_{p}

\displaystyle=-[\bar{\omega}]_{\times}\xi_{p}+\xi_{v}-\xi_{v}^{d}+\xi_{v}^{d}+c_{3}\tilde{\omega}+c_{2}e_{T}\tilde{T}+d_{2}\bar{R}\tilde{g}

(44)

\displaystyle=-[\bar{\omega}]_{\times}\xi_{p}+e_{v}+\xi_{v}^{d}+c_{3}\tilde{\omega}+c_{2}e_{T}\tilde{T}+d_{2}\bar{R}\tilde{g}

We design a feedback linearization control:

\displaystyle\xi_{v}^{d}=-c_{3}\tilde{\omega}-c_{2}e_{T}\tilde{T}-d_{2}\bar{R}\tilde{g}-K_{p}e_{p}

(45)

where
K_{p}
represents the position control gain.
Substitute (
45
) into (
44

\displaystyle\dot{\xi_{p}}=-K_{p}\xi_{p}+e_{v}

(46)

The entire error dynamics are:

\displaystyle\dot{\xi_{p}}

\displaystyle=-[\bar{\omega}]_{\times}\xi_{p}-K_{p}\xi_{p}+e_{v}

(47)

\displaystyle\dot{e_{v}}

\displaystyle=-[\bar{\omega}]_{\times}e_{v}-K_{v}e_{v}+[-\bar{T}e_{T}]_{\times}e_{r}

(48)

\displaystyle\dot{e_{r}}

\displaystyle=-[\bar{\omega}]_{\times}e_{r}-K_{r}e_{r}.

(49)

We could use Linear Matrix Inequalities (LMIs) or

IV
Lyapunov Stability Proof

Theorem IV.1
(Exponential stability using inertial derivatives)

Consider the error dynamics:

\displaystyle\dot{\xi_{p}}

\displaystyle=-[\bar{\omega}]_{\times}\xi_{p}-K_{p}\xi_{p}+e_{v}

(50)

\displaystyle\dot{e_{v}}

\displaystyle=-[\bar{\omega}]_{\times}e_{v}-K_{v}e_{v}+[-\bar{T}e_{T}]_{\times}e_{r}

(51)

\displaystyle\dot{e_{r}}

\displaystyle=-[\bar{\omega}]_{\times}e_{r}-K_{r}e_{r}.

(52)

where
K_{p},K_{v},K_{r}\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}
are symmetric positive-definite gains,

\bar{\omega}(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
is the reference angular velocity, and

\displaystyle B=-\bar{T}[e_{T}]_{\times},

(53)

with
\bar{T}\geq 0
and unit vector
e_{T}
. Assume the state is represented on a single continuous branch of the matrix logarithm. If the gains satisfy

\lambda_{\min}(K_{r})\;>\;\frac{\|B\|^{2}}{2\lambda_{\min}(K_{v})},

(54)

then the equilibrium
(\xi_{p},e_{v},e_{r})=(0,0,0)
is exponentially stable.

Proof:

Choose the standard quadratic Lyapunov function

\displaystyle V(\xi_{p},e_{v},e_{r})=\tfrac{1}{2}\big(\|\xi_{p}\|^{2}+\|e_{v}\|^{2}+\|e_{r}\|^{2}\big).

(55)

Compute the time derivative of
V
along trajectories using the error dynamics:

\displaystyle\dot{V}

\displaystyle=\xi_{p}^{\top}\dot{\xi}_{p}+e_{v}^{\top}\dot{e}_{v}+e_{r}^{\top}\dot{e}_{r}

\displaystyle=\xi_{p}^{\top}\big(-K_{p}\xi_{p}+e_{v}-[\bar{\omega}]_{\times}\xi_{p}\big)

\displaystyle\quad+e_{v}^{\top}\big(-K_{v}e_{v}+Be_{r}-[\bar{\omega}]_{\times}e_{v}\big)

\displaystyle\quad+e_{r}^{\top}\big(-K_{r}e_{r}-[\bar{\omega}]_{\times}e_{r}\big).

Group terms:

\displaystyle\dot{V}

\displaystyle=-\xi_{p}^{\top}K_{p}\xi_{p}+\xi_{p}^{\top}e_{v}-e_{v}^{\top}K_{v}e_{v}+e_{v}^{\top}Be_{r}-e_{r}^{\top}K_{r}e_{r}

(56)

\displaystyle\;+\;\underbrace{\big(-\xi_{p}^{\top}[\bar{\omega}]_{\times}\xi_{p}-e_{v}^{\top}[\bar{\omega}]_{\times}e_{v}-e_{r}^{\top}[\bar{\omega}]_{\times}e_{r}\big)}_{=:S}.

(57)

Now note the skew-symmetry property: for any vector
x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
and skew matrix
S=[\bar{\omega}]_{\times}
,

\displaystyle x^{\top}Sx=0.

(58)

Therefore each term in
S
is zero, so
S=0
. Thus

\displaystyle\dot{V}=-\xi_{p}^{\top}K_{p}\xi_{p}+\xi_{p}^{\top}e_{v}-e_{v}^{\top}K_{v}e_{v}+e_{v}^{\top}Be_{r}-e_{r}^{\top}K_{r}e_{r}.

(59)

The minimum eigenvalues
\kappa_{p}=\lambda_{\min}(K_{p})>0
,
\kappa_{v}=\lambda_{\min}(K_{v})>0
,
\kappa_{r}=\lambda_{\min}(K_{r})>0
.
Bound the cross-terms using Young’s (Cauchy) inequality:

\displaystyle\xi_{p}^{\top}e_{v}\leq\tfrac{\kappa_{p}}{2}\|\xi_{p}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2\kappa_{p}}\|e_{v}\|^{2},

(60)

and

\displaystyle e_{v}^{\top}Be_{r}\leq\tfrac{\kappa_{v}}{2}\|e_{v}\|^{2}+\frac{\|B\|^{2}}{2\kappa_{v}}\|e_{r}\|^{2}.

(61)

Substituting these bounds yields

\displaystyle\dot{V}

\displaystyle\leq-\kappa_{p}\|\xi_{p}\|^{2}+\left(\tfrac{\kappa_{p}}{2}\|\xi_{p}\|^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2\kappa_{p}}\|e_{v}\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\quad-\kappa_{v}\|e_{v}\|^{2}+\left(\tfrac{\kappa_{v}}{2}\|e_{v}\|^{2}+\tfrac{\|B\|^{2}}{2\kappa_{v}}\|e_{r}\|^{2}\right)-\kappa_{r}\|e_{r}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=-\tfrac{\kappa_{p}}{2}\|\xi_{p}\|^{2}-\tfrac{\kappa_{v}}{2}\|e_{v}\|^{2}-\Big(\kappa_{r}-\tfrac{\|B\|^{2}}{2\kappa_{v}}\Big)\|e_{r}\|^{2}.

By assumption
\kappa_{r}>\frac{\|B\|^{2}}{2\kappa_{v}}
, so define

\displaystyle\underline{\alpha}:=\min\Big\{\tfrac{\kappa_{p}}{2},\;\tfrac{\kappa_{v}}{2},\;\kappa_{r}-\tfrac{\|B\|^{2}}{2\kappa_{v}}\Big\}>0.

(62)

Then
\dot{V}\leq-\underline{\alpha}(\|\xi_{p}\|^{2}+\|e_{v}\|^{2}+\|e_{r}\|^{2})=-2\underline{\alpha}V
. Standard comparison yields

\displaystyle V(t)\leq V(0)e^{-2\underline{\alpha}t},

(63)

and therefore there exist constants
c>0,\alpha>0
such that

\displaystyle\|[\xi_{p}(t);\;e_{v}(t);\;e_{r}(t)]\|\leq ce^{-\alpha t}\|[\xi_{p}(0);\;e_{v}(0);\;e_{r}(0)]\|.

(64)

Hence the equilibrium is exponentially stable.
∎

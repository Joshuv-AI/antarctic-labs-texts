Diagnostic value of a multiparametric magnetic resonance imaging-based nomogram incorporating apparent diffusion coefficient in preoperative differentiation of phyllodes tumors from breast cancer: a retrospective study
Authors: Zifan Wei, Hanyan Hu, Yunshu Dai, Yutong Zhang, Yu Liang, Yuanqing Liu, Hao Zhou, Yue Teng, Xinxing Ma
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPreoperative differentiation between phyllodes tumors (PTs) and breast cancers (BCs) remains a clinical challenge due to their overlapping imaging characteristics. The aim of this study was to evaluate the diagnostic performance of multiparametric magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), incorporating morphological, hemodynamic, and apparent diffusion coefficient (ADC) parameters, to develop a nomogram model for differentiating PTs from BCs, and to preliminarily explore the association between ADC parameters and the histologic grade of PTs.MethodsA total of 92 patients (mean age, 46.04 years) with pathologically confirmed PTs (n=31) or BCs (n=61) who underwent 3.0 Tesla (T) breast MRI at the First Affiliated Hospital of Soochow University between June 2018 and June 2025 were retrospectively enrolled. Multiparametric MRI features, including morphological characteristics, hemodynamic parameters, and quantitative diffusion metrics [mean ADC (ADCmean) and relative ADC (rADC)], were assessed for all lesions. Intergroup differences were compared using the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test, independent samples t-test, one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA), Mann-Whitney U test or Kruskal-Wallis H test, Chi-squared test, or Fisher’s exact test, as appropriate. A multivariable logistic regression analysis was performed to identify independent predictors and construct a diagnostic nomogram. Diagnostic performance was evaluated using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis, decision curve analysis (DCA), and calibration curve analysis. Finally, 5-fold internal cross-validation was used to assess the model’s stability.ResultsPTs were characterized predominantly by a lobulated morphology, hyperintensity on T2-weighted imaging (T2WI), and T2 hypointense septations, whereas BCs typically presented as irregular masses with spiculated margins and peritumoral edema. Regarding dynamic contrast-enhanced MRI (DCE-MRI) features, PTs predominantly demonstrated rapid initial enhancement with heterogeneous or dark internal septations, and time-intensity curves (TICs) mainly exhibited Type I/II patterns. PTs demonstrated significantly higher ADCmean, rADC1 (lesion-to-pectoralis muscle ratio), and rADC2 (lesion-to-contralateral gland ratio) values compared with the BCs (all P<0.001). Subgroup analysis revealed a stepwise decrease in ADCmean from benign PTs to borderline/malignant PTs and BCs. The diagnostic nomogram integrating T2WI signal intensity, T2 hypointense septations, TIC pattern, and ADCmean achieved an apparent area under the curve (AUC) of 0.997. Following rigorous internal 5-fold cross-validation with Firth’s penalty to mitigate potential overfitting, the model maintained a robust average AUC of 0.978 [95% confidence interval (CI): 0.951–1.000]. DCA demonstrated the net clinical benefit of the combined model.ConclusionsMultiparametric MRI provides significant diagnostic value in preoperatively differentiating PTs from BCs. Although the subgroup analysis regarding PT histological grading remains preliminary due to limited sample sizes, both ADCmean and rADC offer valuable quantitative insights, with rADC serving as a complementary metric. Crucially, the combined diagnostic nomogram demonstrated robust diagnostic performance despite internal penalization. Pending prospective external validation, this tool may inform individualized diagnosis and optimize surgical decision-making.Keywords: Phyllodes tumor (PT), breast cancer (BC), magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), apparent diffusion coefficient (ADC), nomogram

Introduction

Breast phyllodes tumors (PTs) are rare fibroepithelial neoplasms (1,2) characterized by a biphasic architecture (3). They are classified into distinct histological subtypes (4) with varying risks of recurrence and metastasis (5,6). The surgical management of PTs differs fundamentally from that of breast cancers (BCs) (7,8). However, preoperative differentiation remains a clinical challenge due to their overlapping clinicoradiological features (9), Furthermore, conventional imaging has limited diagnostic efficacy (10-12), and invasive core needle biopsy is subject to inherent sampling errors (13,14). Therefore, precise non-invasive imaging tools are urgently needed to guide surgical planning and prevent detrimental clinical mismanagement (15,16).

Multiparametric magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) is a pivotal tool for evaluating breast lesions (17). Although the apparent diffusion coefficient (ADC) derived from diffusion-weighted imaging (DWI) provides valuable quantitative insights into tumor cellularity (18), absolute ADC values are susceptible to interindividual variability. Calculating the relative ADC (rADC) by normalizing the lesion’s ADC to a stable internal reference (e.g., pectoralis major muscle) effectively mitigates this bias and improves diagnostic stability (19-21). However, most existing studies utilizing these metrics have primarily focused on distinguishing PTs from fibroadenomas (22-24), leaving their utility in differentiating PTs from BCs underexplored.

Relying solely on isolated quantitative metrics (such as ADC or rADC) may still yield insufficient diagnostic accuracy due to the complex heterogeneity of these tumors. A comprehensive approach that integrates functional diffusion metrics with conventional morphological and hemodynamic features could provide a more robust diagnostic solution. Therefore, this study aimed to develop a multiparametric MRI-based nomogram integrating morphological, hemodynamic, and diffusion parameters to preoperatively differentiate PTs from BCs. Additionally, we sought to preliminarily explore whether these diffusion parameters could predict the histological grade of PTs, thereby further facilitating individualized surgical planning. We present this article in accordance with the STARD reporting checklist (available at https://qims.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/qims-2026-0790/rc).

Methods

Study design and population

The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments. This study was approved by the Medical Ethics Committee of The First Affiliated Hospital of Soochow University (approval No. 2025-763). The requirement for written informed consent was waived due to the retrospective nature of the study. We reviewed the clinical and imaging records of 276 female patients with breast lesions who underwent breast MRI at The First Affiliated Hospital of Soochow University before any intervention (biopsy, operation, or neoadjuvant chemotherapy) between June 2018 and June 2025.

The inclusion criteria were as follows: (I) breast MRI performed within 7 days prior to surgery; (II) histopathological confirmation of PTs or BCs based on surgical specimens; and (III) treatment-naïve primary lesions with no history of prior biopsy, surgery, or neoadjuvant chemotherapy. The exclusion criteria were as follows: (I) suboptimal image quality due to significant motion or susceptibility artifacts; (II) concurrent malignancies of other origins; or (III) pregnancy or lactation. Pathological diagnoses were classified according to the 5th edition of the World Health Organization (WHO) Classification of Tumors of the Breast.

Ultimately, a total of 92 patients (mean age, 46.04 years) were enrolled in this study. The flowchart depicting the patient recruitment process employed in this study is presented in Figure 1.

MRI acquisition

All MRI examinations were performed within one week prior to surgery. Images were acquired using a 3.0 Tesla (T) scanner (MAGNETOM Skyra; Siemens Healthineers, Erlangen, Germany) equipped with a dedicated 8-channel phased-array breast coil, with patients in the prone position.

The standardized imaging protocol included the following sequences: (I) axial T1-weighted imaging (T1WI): repetition time (TR)/echo time (TE) =6/2.5 ms, section thickness =1.2 mm, matrix =320×320, field of view (FOV) =350×350 mm2; (II) axial fat-suppressed T2-weighted imaging (T2WI): TR/TE =4,490/53 ms, inversion time (TI) =230 ms, section thickness =4 mm, matrix =320×320, FOV =350×350 mm2; (III) diffusion-weighted imaging (DWI): TR/TE =10,400/53 ms, b-values =0 and 800 s/mm2, section thickness =4 mm, matrix =128×128, FOV =350×350 mm2; (IV) dynamic contrast-enhanced MRI (DCE-MRI): a three-dimensional (3D) volumetric interpolated breath-hold sequence (TR/TE =4.5/1.7 ms, section thickness =1.2 mm, matrix =320×320, FOV =350×350 mm2).

For DCE-MRI, the contrast agent (Gadolinium-DTPA, 0.5 mmol/mL; Bayer Healthcare, Berlin, Germany) was injected intravenously at a dose of 0.1 mmol/kg and a flow rate of 3.0 mL/s, followed by a 15-mL saline flush using an automatic power injector. Five consecutive post-contrast dynamic phases were acquired, starting 60 s after the injection with a temporal resolution of 60 s per phase.

Image analysis

The breast magnetic resonance images were independently reviewed by two fellowship-trained breast radiologists (Z.W. and X.M.), each with more than 5 years of experience in breast imaging diagnosis. Image analysis was performed using a Syngo.Via post-processing workstation (VB40, Siemens Healthineers, Germany) and its integrated MeanCurve analysis module (Version 2.5.1). Both readers were strictly blinded to all clinical information (e.g., patient age, symptoms) and the final histopathological diagnoses during the image evaluation.

Qualitative assessment was conducted in strict accordance with the updated American College of Radiology (ACR) Breast Imaging Reporting and Data System (BI-RADS)® MRI v2025 Manual. The following characteristics were evaluated: (I) morphological features: lesion shape (oval, lobulated, irregular); margin (circumscribed, indistinct, spiculated); T2WI signal intensity (hypointense, isointense, or hyperintense relative to surrounding glandular tissue); T2 hypointense septations (linear hypointense shadows within the mass); cystic necrosis (non-enhancing areas); and peritumoral edema (hyperintense signal surrounding the mass on T2WI). (II) Hemodynamic features: early enhancement rate (EER) classified as fast (>100%), moderate (50–100%), or slow (<50%); internal enhancement (homogeneous, heterogeneous, thick rim enhancement, or dark internal septations); and time-intensity curve (TIC) pattern classified as Type I (persistent, signal increase >10%), Type II (plateau, signal change ±10%), or Type III (washout, signal decrease >10%). (III) DWI features: DWI signal intensity relative to glandular tissue (low, isointense, slightly hyperintense, or hyperintense).

For quantitative assessment, the slice exhibiting the largest cross-sectional area of the solid tumor component was selected based on the second post-contrast dynamic phase. Following the consensus guidelines established by the European Society of Breast Imaging (EUSOBI) International Breast Diffusion-Weighted Imaging working group, circular or oval regions of interest (ROIs, 4–25 mm2) were manually placed on the darkest portion of the corresponding ADC map (derived from b=800 s/mm2). To minimize measurement bias, reference was continuously made to the T2WI and post-contrast T1WI sequences to strictly avoid visible vessels, ducts, cystic spaces, and necrotic areas. The ADC value (10−3 mm2/s) for each lesion was measured independently three times by each observer, and the mean value was recorded as the final lesion ADCmean.

Simultaneously, the ADCmean values of the ipsilateral pectoralis major muscle and the contralateral normal gland were obtained by placing ROIs in homogeneous areas, avoiding fat, fibrosis, and vessels. rADC values were calculated as follows: rADC1 = lesion ADCmean/ipsilateral pectoralis muscle ADCmean; rADC2 = lesion ADCmean/contralateral normal gland ADCmean (both dimensionless).

For qualitative assessments, a consensus was reached through discussion in cases of disagreement. For quantitative measurements, the mean value of the two observers’ results was used for subsequent analyses to minimize interobserver measurement bias. All imaging evaluations were finally correlated with the postoperative histopathological findings derived from routine clinical pathology reports for statistical analysis.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using the software SPSS 24.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA), MedCalc version 18.2 (MedCalc Software, Ostend, Belgium), and R version 4.2.0 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria). Interobserver agreement was assessed using the intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC) for quantitative continuous variables (ADC measurements) and Cohen’s kappa coefficient for qualitative categorical variables (MRI morphological and hemodynamic features). Agreement was interpreted as poor (<0.40), moderate (0.40–0.75), or excellent (> 0.75). Data normality was assessed using the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test. Continuous variables with normal distribution were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD) and compared using the independent samples t-test (for two groups) or one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA; for multiple groups). Non-normally distributed variables were compared using the Mann-Whitney U test or Kruskal-Wallis H test. For subgroup analyses involving three groups (benign PTs, borderline/malignant PTs, and BCs), post hoc pairwise comparisons were subsequently performed. The Bonferroni correction was applied to adjust the P values for multiple comparisons. Categorical variables were summarized as frequencies and percentages and analyzed using the Chi-squared test or Fisher’s exact test. A two-sided P value <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Collinearity among predictors was assessed using the variance inflation factor (VIF), with a VIF >5 indicating substantial multicollinearity. Significant predictors (P<0.05) from the univariate analysis were entered into a multivariable stepwise logistic regression using backward elimination to construct a combined diagnostic model. Since rADC is mathematically derived from ADCmean (resulting in inherent linear dependence), including both would cause severe multicollinearity and violate the assumption of independence in logistic regression. Therefore, ADCmean was preferentially retained as a candidate for the final model because it is a direct, readily available metric that avoids potential measurement errors associated with reference tissue selection, and our prior analyses indicated that rADC offered no statistically significant diagnostic superiority over ADCmean. Based on the multivariable model, a diagnostic nomogram was established to visualize the contribution of each independent predictor, yielding odds ratios (ORs) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs).

Model performance was comprehensively evaluated in terms of discrimination, calibration, and clinical utility. Discrimination was assessed using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis with calculation of the area under the curve (AUC), sensitivity, and specificity, whereas the DeLong test was used to compare the AUCs across models. Calibration was evaluated using the Hosmer-Lemeshow test and calibration curves. Clinical utility was determined via decision curve analysis (DCA) to quantify the net benefit across a range of threshold probabilities. Crucially, to rigorously evaluate model stability and mitigate the risk of overfitting inherent in limited sample sizes, internal validation was performed using a stratified 5-fold cross-validation approach. Furthermore, given the potential for perfect separation observed in subsamples, Firth’s penalized maximum likelihood estimation was applied during cross-validation to prevent regression coefficient inflation and ensure robust predictive performance.

Results

Baseline characteristics and interobserver agreement

A total of 92 patients were included in the final analysis, consisting of 31 patients with PTs (mean age, 39.6±9.4 years) and 61 patients with BCs (mean age, 49.3±11.4 years). The demographic characteristics of the patients in both groups are presented in Table 1. Within the PT group, histological subtypes were classified as follows: benign (n=16; 51.6%), borderline (n=8; 25.8%), and malignant (n=7; 22.6%). Regarding the MRI assessments, the two radiologists demonstrated excellent interobserver agreement, with both ICCs for quantitative measurements and kappa values for qualitative MRI features exceeding 0.75.

Morphological manifestations

Significant differences were observed between the PT and BC groups regarding lesion shape (P<0.001), margin (P=0.001), T2WI signal intensity (P<0.001), T2 hypointense septations (P=0.006), and peritumoral edema (P<0.001). Conversely, no significant difference was found regarding cystic necrosis (P=0.337).

Morphologically, lesions in the PT group predominantly exhibited a lobulated shape (54.8%) with circumscribed (45.2%) or indistinct (48.4%) margins. They typically demonstrated T2WI hyperintensity (87.1%) and frequently presented with T2 hypointense septations (67.7%), whereas peritumoral edema was relatively uncommon (12.9%). In contrast, lesions in the BC group were characterized mainly by an irregular shape (68.9%) with indistinct (44.3%) or spiculated (37.7%) margins, and a higher prevalence of peritumoral edema (62.3%). Although T2WI signal intensity in BCs was variable, cystic necrosis was a common finding observed in both groups.

DCE-MRI manifestations

Significant differences were also observed in internal enhancement characteristics (P=0.013) and TIC patterns (P=0.029); however, no significant difference was found in EER (P=0.573) (Table 1).

In terms of internal enhancement, the PT group primarily exhibited heterogeneous enhancement (54.8%) or dark internal septations (25.8%). In contrast, the BC group frequently demonstrated heterogeneous enhancement (57.4%) or thick rim enhancement (27.9%). Regarding kinetic analysis, the PT group predominantly showed a Type I (persistent) or Type II (plateau) TIC pattern, whereas the BC group mainly presented with a Type II or Type III (washout) pattern. Both groups demonstrated a fast EER in the majority of cases.

DWI/ADC manifestations

Regarding the qualitative DWI assessment, no statistically significant difference was observed in the distribution of signal intensity grades between the two groups (P=0.836), as lesions in both groups typically appeared slightly hyperintense or hyperintense (PTs: 93.5%; BCs: 90.2%) (Table 1).

Quantitative analysis showed that the ADCmean of the PT group was significantly higher than that of the BC group (P<0.001). Similarly, significant differences were observed for both rADC1 and rADC2 (all P<0.001) (Table 1).

To analyze the ADC values, we further divided the PT group into benign PT subgroup and borderline/malignant PT subgroup based on pathological findings, and conducted multiple group comparisons with the BC group. Benign PTs exhibited the highest ADCmean values, followed by borderline/malignant PTs, with BCs showing the lowest values (P<0.001). Specifically, significant subgroup differences were confirmed between benign PTs and BCs, as well as between borderline/malignant PTs and BCs (P<0.001) (Tables 2,3). Furthermore, macro-averaged results indicated that ADCmean demonstrated the best overall diagnostic performance (AUC =0.867), slightly outperforming rADC1 (AUC =0.858) and rADC2 (AUC =0.847). ROC curve analysis demonstrated excellent diagnostic performance for all parameters (Table 4). The optimal cutoff ADCmean in differentiating PTs from BCs was determined to be 1.322×10−3 mm2/s yielding an AUC of 0.975 (95% CI: 0.948–1.000), with a sensitivity of 90.3% and a specificity of 96.7%. The rADC1 achieved the highest diagnostic accuracy (cutoff =0.734; AUC =0.988; 95% CI: 0.974–1.000; sensitivity, 100.0%; specificity, 91.8%), whereas rADC2 showed slightly lower performance (cutoff =0.656; AUC =0.962; 95% CI: 0.930–0.995; sensitivity, 96.8%; specificity, 86.9%). The DeLong test revealed no statistically significant differences among the AUC values of the three parameters (P>0.05).

Construction and evaluation of the combined diagnostic model

Univariate analysis revealed statistically significant differences between the PT and BC groups in lesion shape, margin, T2WI signal intensity, T2 hypointense septations, peritumoral edema, internal enhancement, TIC pattern, ADCmean, rADC1, and rADC2 (all P<0.05; Table 1). Collinearity diagnosis revealed that both rADC1 and rADC2 were multicollinear with ADCmean. As mathematically anticipated and methodologically justified above, rADC parameters were excluded from the multivariable modeling. After excluding rADC1 and rADC2, the VIF values for all remaining candidate parameters were less than 5.0 (range, 1.096–1.818), indicating no residual multicollinearity. Multivariate stepwise logistic regression identified T2WI signal intensity, T2 hypointense septation, TIC pattern, and ADCmean as independent predictors for differentiating PTs from BCs (Table 5). Based on the final multivariable model, a nomogram was developed by assigning a weighted score to each independent predictor (Figure 4). Discussion

To overcome the limitations of relying on single MRI sequences or subjective evaluations, our study systematically evaluated morphological, hemodynamic, and quantitative diffusion metrics to preoperatively differentiate PTs from BCs. We developed a multiparametric MRI-based diagnostic nomogram integrating T2WI signal intensity, T2 hypointense septations, TIC pattern, and ADCmean. This combined model significantly outperformed any single-parameter indicator, exhibiting excellent discrimination and calibration. Furthermore, to our knowledge, this is the first study to incorporate rADC into this specific differentiation, proposing a standardized quantitative approach to mitigate the inherent interindividual variability of absolute ADC measurements. Pending future external validation, this predictive tool holds promise for facilitating precise, individualized preoperative surgical decision-making.

The distinct morphological features observed—lobulated boundaries in PTs versus irregular, spiculated margins in BCs—directly reflect their disparate pathological growth patterns. Specifically, the expansive growth driven by rapid stromal and epithelial proliferation in PTs naturally yields well-defined, coalescent contours (25). Conversely, the typical infiltrative spread and subsequent traction on Cooper’s ligaments in BCs manifest as spiculated margins. Notably, two malignant PTs in our cohort also exhibited spiculated margins, corroborating that localized tissue infiltration can occasionally accompany the malignant progression of PTs(26).

Regarding internal signal characteristics, the characteristic T2WI hyperintensity in PTs is likely attributable to abundant stromal cellularity and increased interstitial water content (27). This contrasts with malignant BCs, where higher cellular density and reduced free water typically result in lower T2WI signals (28,29). Although cystic necrosis offered limited discriminatory value as it occurred frequently in both groups (30), the presence of T2 hypointense septations emerged as a robust hallmark for PTs (31). The prominent manifestation of these septations aligns with prior reports (32), histopathologically corresponding to leaf-like stromal projections that compress adjacent ductal structures into linear, slit-like epithelial clefts (2).

The prominent peritumoral edema characterizing BCs serves as a reliable indicator of malignancy, pathologically stemming from vasogenic interstitial edema secondary to increased microvessel permeability or impaired lymphatic drainage induced by infiltrative tumor growth (3,33). Beyond morphology, DCE-MRI provides vital differential insights by profiling distinct microvascular landscapes (34,35). Although both entities frequently demonstrate heterogeneous enhancement, their underlying mechanisms diverge. In BCs, this pattern typically reflects a dense peripheral microvascular network surrounding a relatively hypovascular, rapidly proliferating core prone to central necrosis (36). Conversely, the heterogeneous perfusion in PTs is driven by disorganized neovascularization and abnormal stromal proliferation. Crucially, the presence of non-enhancing (dark) internal septations on post-contrast images, particularly when spatially matching T2 hypointense septations, acts as a highly specific diagnostic hallmark for PTs.

Hemodynamically, the predominantly progressive or plateauing (Type I/II) TIC profiles in PTs correspond well with the less aggressive microvasculature typical of benign subtypes, which clinically constitute the majority of these tumors (2,30). However, the emergence of Type III (washout) kinetics serves as an indicator of aggressive behavior, specifically mapping to borderline or malignant PTs in concordance with established findings (35). Such kinetic shifts fundamentally reflect underlying variations in tumor vascular maturity (37). Specifically, the overexpression of vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) in aggressive PTs drives the proliferation of tortuous, hyperpermeable vessels, dictating rapid contrast leakage and subsequent washout effects (24). In stark contrast to the variable kinetics of PTs, BCs are intrinsically characterized by high microvessel density and elevated capillary permeability, inherently predisposing them to rapid contrast influx and consistent washout profiles (38).

Quantitative assessment via DWI-derived ADCmean offers crucial imaging evidence for characterizing breast lesions (29,39,40). The characteristically higher ADCmean values in PTs relative to BCs (41,42) are pathologically rooted in the dense cellularity and contracted extracellular spaces of carcinomas, which impede water diffusion, a phenomenon traditionally interpreted as restricted Brownian motion of water molecules, water diffusion (43). However, interpretations of reduced ADC values should be treated with caution. Recent studies have shown that ADC measurements are significantly influenced by T2 relaxation time via the so-called “T2–ADC curve”: tissues with T2 values close to 70 milliseconds consistently yield lower measured ADC values, regardless of their actual water diffusion coefficient (44,45). Notably, Otikovs et al. confirmed a negative correlation between T2 values and ADC values in BC lesions, which contradicts expectations based solely on water diffusion restrictions (46). Therefore, the reduction in ADC in BC likely reflects the combined effects of diffusion restriction, the T2 transmission effect, and perfusion effects, rather than simply reflecting restricted Brownian motion. Since absolute ADC measurements are susceptible to systemic biological variations such as age and hormonal status (47), we incorporated rADC to mitigate this bias. However, given the lack of statistical superiority over ADCmean, the numerically higher diagnostic efficiency of normalizing to the muscle (rADC1) should be interpreted with caution, as it may reflect sampling variation rather than a definitive advantage. Furthermore, the inferior performance of referencing the contralateral gland (rADC2) underscores how anatomical heterogeneity or subclinical fibroglandular changes can obscure true diffusion characteristics (47,48). Additionally, an inverse relationship exists between ADCmean and the histological grade of PTs, highlighting its utility as an auxiliary biomarker for evaluating malignant potential (49). The robust diagnostic performance of ADCmean (43,50) is histopathologically anchored: lower diffusion values directly correspond to the pronounced stromal hypercellularity typical of borderline and malignant PTs (2,51). Although these quantitative metrics excellently discriminated PTs from BCs, they failed to significantly stratify benign from borderline/malignant PTs internally. This intra-group diagnostic bottleneck is primarily attributable to overlapping microstructural features across PT grades. Importantly, given the limited sample size of borderline (n=8) and malignant (n=7) PTs in our cohort, these subgroup analyses should be interpreted as strictly preliminary. Assembling larger, multicenter cohorts remains a persistent clinical challenge but is essential to statistically validate whether diffusion metrics can reliably predict PT histological grades (52).

Ultimately, our multiparametric MRI diagnostic model—integrating morphology, hemodynamics, and diffusion metrics—demonstrated diagnostic performance far superior to any isolated parameter or single-sequence approach, with ADCmean exhibiting the strongest pathological correlation. The robust calibration and discrimination achieved across both internal training and validation sets underscore the model’s reliability, indicating a close alignment between predicted probabilities and actual pathological outcomes.

Nevertheless, several limitations must be acknowledged. First, the small sample size of borderline and malignant PTs constrained our ability to robustly correlate ADCmean across distinct histological grades. Second, utilizing the pectoralis major muscle for rADC1 normalization introduces technical vulnerabilities, in patients with muscle atrophy, partial volume effects from adjacent tissues can compromise measurement precision. Third, the retrospective, single-center design and the absence of an independent external validation cohort inherently introduce selection bias and curtail the immediate generalizability of the nomogram. Fourth, restricting ROI placement to a single-slice two-dimensional (2D) approach rather than whole-tumor 3D volumetric segmentation may inadvertently introduce sampling bias, failing to fully capture the profound intratumoral heterogeneity typical of large PTs. Finally, no independent central pathology review was conducted; reliance on routine clinical reports may introduce minor variations in histological grading. Moving forward, large-scale, multicenter prospective trials incorporating whole-tumor 3D segmentation and artificial intelligence-assisted radiomics are essential to overcome these limitations and externally validate this predictive model.

Conclusions

Multiparametric MRI offers significant diagnostic value for preoperatively differentiating PTs from BCs. Crucially, the proposed multiparametric nomogram, integrating morphological, hemodynamic, and diffusion features, exhibits excellent discrimination, reliable calibration, and favorable clinical utility. Although ADCmean serves as a robust quantitative biomarker for distinguishing PTs from BCs, its role in preliminarily assessing PT histological grade requires further validation. Pending rigorous prospective external validation, this combined imaging tool holds substantial promise for informing individualized diagnosis and optimizing preoperative surgical strategies.

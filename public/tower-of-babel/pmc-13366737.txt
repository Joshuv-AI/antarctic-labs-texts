Comparative effectiveness of the Clutch Cutter versus Dual knife for esophageal endoscopic submucosal dissection by non-expert endoscopists: a single-center retrospective cohort study
Authors: Kei Terasaki, Kyoko Sakai, Yoshimichi Yorioka, Hidetomi Kimura, Shinsaku Fujiishi, Maho Fujinami, Kazuhisa Watanabe, Shohei Amioka, Takahiro Nonaka, Takahiro Miura, Keiichiro Okuda, Hirohisa Oya, Takayuki Katayama, Kohei Fukumoto, Yasuhide Mitsumoto, Chiemi Mizuno, Toshihide Shima, Takeshi Okanoue
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundA Clutch Cutter is a grasping scissor forceps device used for endoscopic submucosal dissection (ESD). This device can grasp and incise the target tissue similar to the action of a biopsy forceps. Therefore, ESD with a Clutch Cutter may be easier and safer than that performed with non-scissor-type knives. This study verified the usefulness of the Clutch Cutter by nonexpert endoscopists during ESD for superficial esophageal cancer.MethodsThis single-center retrospective cohort study enrolled 125 patients who underwent ESD as a first-line therapy for superficial esophageal cancer between January 2016 and June 2023 at Saiseikai Suita Hospital. The patients were divided into groups based on the ESD device: a Clutch Cutter or a non-scissor-type device (dual knife). The clinical characteristics and technical outcomes were compared between the two groups.ResultsAmong one hundred twenty-five patients, seventy-nine patients underwent nonexpert endoscopic treatment, of which twenty-one patients underwent ESD with a Clutch Cutter and fifty-eight patients with a dual knife. There is a significant difference in sex distribution between the two groups, with a higher percentage of males in the dual knife group (94.8%) compared to the Clutch Cutter group (71.4%) (p = 0.01). Patients in the Clutch Cutter group had shorter procedure times (60 min) than those in the dual knife group (93 min) (p = 0.002). However, en bloc and R0 resection rates were not significantly different between the groups. In the multivariate analysis, procedure time (min) was significantly correlated with device type (coefficient 58.86, 95% confidence interval: 26.99—90.72) and tumor size (coefficient 4.9, 95% confidence interval: 3.58—6.21).ConclusionESD for superficial esophageal cancer with the Clutch Cutter significantly shortened procedure time compared to that with the dual knife for nonexpert endoscopists. Therefore, the Clutch Cutter may be a preferred-device for nonexpert endoscopists.

Background

Endoscopic submucosal dissection (ESD) is a minimally invasive treatment for achieving the curative resection of superficial gastrointestinal neoplasms [1, 2]. Initially employed in 2006 to treat early gastric cancer, ESD’s applications expanded in 2008 to treat superficial esophageal cancer. ESD is the standard therapy for superficial esophageal cancer [3, 4], and its efficacy has been reported in several studies [4–7]. However, this procedure requires complex techniques, and is associated with risks of fatal complications, such as perforation and mediastinitis [4, 8, 9].

Several unique ESD devices have been developed [10, 11]; those commonly used include insulated-tip and needle-type knives [12]. However, performing ESD using these devices is technically demanding. The Clutch Cutter (DP2618DT; Fujifilm Medical, Tokyo, Japan) is a grasping scissors forceps device developed by Akahoshi et al. that can grasp and incise the target tissue, similar to biopsy forceps [13] (Fig. 1a, b). ESD with a Clutch Cutter is technically straightforward and safe as the device does not require advanced techniques and the tissue can be regrasped if unintentional grasps take place.Fig. 1a Short-type Clutch Cutter with a 3.5 mm blade. b Overall image of the Clutch Cutter. Several studies have verified the usefulness of Clutch Cutters [14, 15]. For example, Esaki et al. reported the efficacy and safety of Clutch Cutters during ESD for superficial esophageal cancer compared to that of non-scissor-type knives [12]. However, no studies have focused on the use of Clutch Cutters solely by nonexpert endoscopists. This study verified the usefulness of Clutch Cutters during ESD performed by nonexpert endoscopists for treating superficial esophageal cancer.

Methods

Patients

This was a single-center retrospective cohort study conducted at Saiseikai Suita Hospital. We enrolled 125 consecutive patients who underwent endoscopic submucosal dissection (ESD) as first-line therapy for superficial esophageal cancer between January 2016 and June 2023. Patients with circumferential or subcircumferential esophageal cancer were treated initially with chemoradiotherapy or surgery, excluding ESD due to high risk of severe stenosis. Patients with a history of chemoradiation therapy as first-line treatment were excluded from the study. The flowchart of patient enrollment is shown in Fig. 2. This study was approved by the Clinical Ethics Committee on Human Experiments of Saiseikai Suita Hospital (IRB registration number: 2020–12) and conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki (as revised in Fortaleza, Brazil, October 2013). Written informed consent was obtained from all patients prior to their participation in this study.Fig. 2Flowchart of patient enrollment

Flowchart of patient enrollment

Patient characteristics

Patient medical records were retrospectively reviewed for the following data: patient sex, age, use of antithrombotic agents, tumor location, morphology, tumor size, resected specimen size, invasion depth, operator skill, procedure time, resection results (en bloc or R0), and ESD-related complications.

Study design

Two patient groups were created based on the device that had been used during ESD. A scissor-type device, the Clutch Cutter, was used in patients in the Clutch Cutter group, while a non-scissor-type device, a dual knife (KD-650L; Olympus, Tokyo, Japan), was used in patients in the dual knife group. The dual knife was used between January 2016 and March 2019 while the Clutch Cutter was used between April 2019 and June 2023. The clinical characteristics and technical outcomes were compared between the groups.

Participating endoscopists

One expert and five nonexpert endoscopists performed ESD for superficial esophageal cancer. The endoscopist with experience of performing < 50 gastrointestinal ESD procedures and < 15 esophageal ESD procedures was defined as the nonexpert. Meanwhile, the endoscopist with experience of performing > 100 gastrointestinal ESD procedures and > 30 esophageal ESD procedures was defined as the expert.

ESD procedure

ESD was performed using a dual knife or Clutch Cutter via a conventional single-channel endoscope (GIF-Q260J; Olympus, Tokyo, Japan). Circumferential marker dots were created approximately 3 mm from the lesion by injecting hyaluronic acid with small amounts of indigo carmine into the submucosal layer surrounding the lesion. Next, a circumferential mucosal incision was made outside the marked dots, and the dental floss clip method [16–19] was used to create traction. The submucosal dissection was performed as previously described [4].

When a Clutch Cutter was used, the target tissue was marked then grasped and cut using a technique similar to a biopsy technique [12, 15, 20]. Pre-coagulation (forced coagulation; effect 2, 30 W) was used to prevent intraoperative bleeding. Soft coagulation (effect 5, 80 W) was used to achieve hemostasis of active bleeding. All assistants for ESD procedures were nonexperts.

Statistical analysis

Categorical variables are presented as numbers and percentages while continuous variables are presented as mean and standard deviation, or median and range. Fisher’s exact test was used to compare categorical variables. Student’s t-test or Mann- Whitney U test was used to compare continuous variables. To evaluate the correlation between treatment time and device, patient-related factors (age and sex) and tumor-related factors (size, location, invasion depth, and morphology) were adjusted in a multiple regression model. A two-tailed p-value of < 0.05 was considered significant. All statistical analyses were performed using STATA version 12.1 (Stata Corp LP, College Station, TX, USA).

Reporting compliance

This study was designed and reported in accordance with the STROBE guidelines to ensure transparency and completeness in reporting observational research.

Results

One-hundred twenty-five patients (104 males and 21 females) were enrolled in this study, including 67 in the Clutch Cutter group and 58 in the dual knife group. Among them, seventy-nine patients (70 males and 9 females) underwent ESD performed by nonexpert endoscopists. Twenty-one patients underwent ESD with a Clutch Cutter and fifty-eight patients underwent ESD with a dual knife. The clinical characteristics of those patients are shown in Table 1. Significant differences between the two groups were observed for sex in the univariate analysis (p = 0.01). R0 resection was not achieved in two cases in the Clutch Cutter group and one case in the dual knife group because the horizontal margin was positive in all three cases. Two patients in the dual knife group experienced perforation compared to zero patients in the Clutch Cutter group. The perforation site was sutured using clipping in the two cases with perforation. Additionally, no postoperative bleeding was observed in either group. All assistants for ESD in both groups were nonexperts. Discussion

This study investigated the usefulness of a Clutch Cutter during ESD for superficial esophageal cancer. In nonexpert endoscopists, the use of the Clutch Cutter resulted in shorter procedure times than the dual knife, whereas the en bloc resection, R0 resection, perforation, and postoperative bleeding rates did not significantly differ between procedures conducted with the Clutch Cutter or dual knife devices.

Several devices can be used during ESD to treat esophagus, stomach, duodenum, and colon tumors [10, 12, 13, 21–23]. Each device has unique characteristics, but no consensus has been reached regarding the most suitable device for ESD based on tumor type. Devices for ESD are classified into scissor-type and non-scissor-type. Dohi et al. reported the efficacy of a Clutch Cutter, a grasping-type scissor forceps, during ESD for treating early gastric cancer [20]. In addition, Esaki et al. reported the efficacy of a Clutch Cutter during ESD for treating superficial esophageal cancer. According to Esaki et al., using the Clutch Cutter significantly decreased the procedure time with no effects on the en bloc, complete, or curative resection rates compared to non-scissor-type devices [12]. These studies are consistent with the results of the present study, verifying that the Clutch Cutter allows for a shorter ESD procedure than the dual knife, especially for nonexpert endoscopists. This may arise because advanced techniques are not required when performing ESD with a Clutch Cutter, while they are required when using a dual knife. Therefore, using non-scissor-type devices for ESD is often challenging for nonexpert endoscopists. Advanced techniques may lead to serious complications such as perforation. However, the Clutch Cutter can achieve mucosal incision and submucosal dissection by grasping and cutting the target mucosal and submucosal tissues [24]. If the grasp is inappropriate, it can be repeated prior to cutting to avoid miscutting. Additionally, ESD does not differ based on the location of the lesion in the esophagus when a Clutch Cutter device is used. ESD can be accomplished by simply repeating the grasp and cutting from beginning to end. This simple technique may contribute to the reduction of complications including perforation and intraoperative bleeding, which may be life-threatening. Pre-coagulation prior to the mucosal incision and submucosal dissection can be performed using the Clutch Cutter device, which has also been used as hemostatic forceps. Therefore, the frequency of intraoperative bleeding was low among patients in whom the Clutch Cutter device was used in this study, which may have reduced the procedure time. Additionally, Esaki et al. demonstrated the impact of assistant proficiency using the Clutch Cutter technical outcomes, specifically regarding device rotation and adept target tissue grasp [25]. They concluded that ESD procedure durations were notably reduced when performed with expert assistants holding experience assisting and operating in at least 50 ESD procedures, compared to nonexpert assistants with fewer than 10 ESD procedures. Despite all assistants being nonexperts in our study, using a Clutch Cutter still resulted in shorter procedure times than a dual knife. This underscores another aspect of the utility of the Clutch Cutter.

Limitations of the study

This study has several limitations. First, this was a retrospective single-center study with a relatively small sample size, which may limit the robustness of the findings. Second, the use of the dual knife and Clutch Cutter in different time periods introduces a potential temporal bias, including learning curve effects and changes in clinical practice over time.

Third, selection bias cannot be excluded, as device choice was not randomized.

To mitigate these concerns, we performed multivariate analyses and subgroup analyses, including comparisons between expert and nonexpert endoscopists, which consistently demonstrated shorter procedure times and fewer complications with the Clutch Cutter.

Finally, the small number of adverse events limits the precision of safety estimates, and long-term outcomes such as stricture formation and recurrence were not assessed.

Generalizability

The generalizability of our findings may be limited, as this study was conducted in a single high-volume center. However, the inclusion of nonexpert endoscopists suggests that the Clutch Cutter may facilitate safer and more efficient ESD in less experienced operators, which may enhance its applicability in broader clinical settings.

Our findings should be interpreted in the context of nonexpert endoscopists and do not diminish the importance of operator experience, which remains a key determinant of ESD outcomes. While procedures performed by expert endoscopists using conventional devices such as the dual knife may provide a high level of safety and reliability, the Clutch Cutter may serve as a useful adjunct, particularly in the training phase, by offering greater procedural stability and confidence for less experienced operators.

Conclusion

This study revealed that for nonexpert endoscopists, utilizing a Clutch Cutter for ESD in cases of superficial esophageal cancer significantly shortened procedure times compared to using a dual knife. Therefore, the Clutch Cutter may be a recommended device for nonexpert endoscopists. However, randomized controlled trials with larger sample sizes are needed to verify these findings.

Testing the behaviour of exotic matter near wormhole throat in f ⁡ ( R , T ) f(R,T) gravity


Abstract
In this paper, I propose a static wormhole model within modified f ⁡ ( R , T ) f(R,T) gravity where f ⁡ ( R , T ) = R + 2 ​ λ ​ T f(R,T)=R+2\lambda T . The wormhole solutions have been evolved in four cases- three different shape function along with redshift ϕ = ϕ 0 r \phi=\frac{\phi_{0}}{r} and a variable EoS parameter ω ⁡ ( r ) \omega(r) with constant redshift function. I also have explored the energy conditions and the behaviour of exotic matter within the wormhole in all scenarios. Presence of exotic matter violates necessary energy conditions near wormhole throat which gives constraint on modified gravity parameter λ \lambda in all different cases.

2
Wormhole metric and its conditions

The static and spherically symmetric Wormhole (WH) metric is defined as,

ds^{2}=-e^{-2\phi(r)}dt^{2}+\frac{dr^{2}}{1-\frac{b(r)}{r}}+r^{2}d\theta^{2}+r^{2}Sin^{2}\theta d\phi^{2}

(1)

where
\phi(r)
and
b(r)
are the redshift function and the shape fuction respectively which should obey the following conditions:

•

The radial co-ordinate
r
lies within
r_{0}\leq r<\infty
where
r_{0}
is the minimum throat radius.

•

At the throat
r=r_{0}
the shape function will be,

b(r_{0})=r_{0}

(2)

and outside the throat
r>r_{0}
,
1-\frac{b(r)}{r}>0

•

The shape fuction
b(r)
should obey the Flaring out condition i.e.

b^{\prime}(r_{0})<1

(3)

•

For asymptotic flat spacetime the limit
\frac{b(r)}{r}\rightarrow 0
as
r\rightarrow\infty
.

•

The redshift function
\phi(r)
must be non vanishing and finite at the throat.

f(R,T)

The Einstein-Hilbert action of the modified
f(R,T)
theory of gravity, in presence of matter can be written as,

\mathcal{S}=\frac{1}{16\pi G}\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}~f(R,T)+\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}~\mathcal{L}_{m}

(4)

where
R
is the Ricci Scalar,
T
is the trace of energy-momentum tensor,
f(R,T)
is an arbitrary function of
R
and
T
,
g
is the determinant of the metric tensor
g_{\mu\nu}
and G is the Newtonian constant of Gravitation.
2
2

2

We have used natural units,
c=\hbar=1
and considered
8\pi G=1
.
\mathcal{L}_{m}
is the matter Lagrangian and is related to the energy-momentum tensor
T_{\mu\nu}
as,

T_{\mu\nu}=-\frac{2}{\sqrt{-g}}~\frac{\delta}{\delta g^{\mu\nu}}(\sqrt{-g}\mathcal{L}_{m})

(5)

By taking the metric variation of the action (
4
), we find the modified Einstein equation as

f_{R}(R,T)~R_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}f(R,T)~g_{\mu\nu}+(g_{\mu\nu}\Box-\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu})~f_{R}(R,T)=T_{\mu\nu}-f_{T}(R,T)~T_{\mu\nu}-f_{T}(R,T)~\theta_{\mu\nu}

(6)

Here I have taken
\mathcal{L}_{m}=-\rho
, hence
\theta_{\mu\nu}=-2T_{\mu\nu}-\rho g_{\mu\nu}
. Considering
f(R,T)=R+2\lambda T
,
\lambda
is a constant, Eq. (
6
) will be,

R_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}Rg_{\mu\nu}=T_{\mu\nu}+\lambda Tg_{\mu\nu}+2\lambda T{\mu\nu}+2\lambda\rho g_{\mu\nu}

(7)

I have taken an anisotropic fluid for matter content of the form

T{\mu}^{\nu}=diag(-\rho,P_{r},P_{t},P_{t})

(8)

where
\rho(r)
is the energy density,
P_{r}(r)
,
P_{t}(r)
are the radial and lateral pressure respectively. The trace of energy-momentum tensor will be
T=\rho+P_{r}+2P_{t}
.

The field equations for the metric Eq.(
1
) along with Eq.(
8
) are,

\frac{b^{\prime}}{r}=(1+\lambda)\rho-\lambda(P_{r}+2P_{t})

(9)

-\frac{b}{r^{3}}+\frac{2}{r}\phi^{\prime}\left(1-\frac{b}{r}\right)=\lambda\rho+P_{r}(1+3\lambda)+2\lambda P_{t}

(10)

and

\left(1-\frac{b}{r}\right)\left(\phi^{\prime\prime}+\phi^{\prime 2}+\frac{\phi^{\prime}}{r}-\phi^{\prime}\frac{b^{\prime}r-b}{2r(r-b)}-\frac{b^{\prime}r-b}{2r^{2}(r-b)}\right)=\lambda\rho+\lambda P_{r}+P_{t}(1+4\lambda)

(11)

From the above three field equations we can get the energy density, radial pressure and lateral pressure as,

\rho=\frac{b^{\prime}}{r^{2}(1+2\lambda)}+\frac{2\lambda}{(1+4\lambda)(1+2\lambda)}\left(1-\frac{b}{r}\right)\left(\phi^{\prime\prime}+\phi^{\prime 2}-\frac{\phi^{\prime}}{r}-\phi^{\prime}\frac{b^{\prime}r-b}{2r(r-b)}\right)+\frac{6\lambda}{(1+4\lambda)(1+2\lambda)}\frac{\phi^{\prime}}{r}\left(1-\frac{b^{\prime}}{r}\right)

(12)

P_{r}=-\frac{b}{r^{3}(1+2\lambda)}+\frac{1+\lambda}{(1+4\lambda)(1+2\lambda)}\frac{2}{r}\phi^{\prime}\left(1-\frac{b}{r}\right)-\frac{2\lambda}{(1+2\lambda)(1+4\lambda)}\left(1-\frac{b}{r}\right)\left(\phi^{\prime\prime}+\phi^{\prime 2}-\frac{\phi^{\prime}}{r}-\phi^{\prime}\frac{b^{\prime}r-b}{2r(r-b)}\right)

(13)

and,

P_{t}=\frac{b-b^{\prime}r}{2r^{3}(1+2\lambda)}+\frac{1}{1+4\lambda}\left(1-\frac{b}{r}\right)\left(\phi^{\prime\prime}+\phi^{\prime 2}-\frac{\phi^{\prime}}{r}-\phi^{\prime}\frac{b^{\prime}r-b}{2r(r-b)}\right)+\frac{1+\lambda}{(1+4\lambda)(1+2\lambda)}\frac{2}{r}\phi^{\prime}\left(1-\frac{b}{r}\right)

(14)

For constant redshift function
\phi=\phi_{0}
the above three equation reduces to,

\rho=\frac{b^{\prime}}{r^{2}(1+2\lambda)},~~P_{r}=-\frac{b}{r^{3}(1+2\lambda)},~~P_{t}=\frac{b-b^{\prime}r}{2r^{3}(1+2\lambda)}

(15)

4
Energy conditions for different models

Energy conditions are important in order to study the behaviour of matter inside the wormhole. The four fundamental energy conditions in GR are as follows-

•

Null energy condition(NEC) satisfies
\rho+P_{i}\geq 0
and it represents the attractive nature of gravity.

•

Weak energy condition (WEC) satisfies
\rho+P_{i}\geq 0
,
\rho\geq 0
and it additionally ensures the positive behaviour of energy density along with NEC.

•

Strong energy condition (SEC) satisfies
\rho+P_{i}\geq 0,\rho+\Sigma_{i}P_{i}\geq 0
. It originates from attractive nature of gravity and is a result of spherically symmetrical metric.

•

Dominant energy condition (DEC) which is
\rho\geq|P_{i}|
, measures the velocity of energy transfer to the speed of light.

In this section, I have discussed the above mentioned energy conditions for four different cases of matter present at the wormhole.

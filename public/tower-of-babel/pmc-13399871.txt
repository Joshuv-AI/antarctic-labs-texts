Magnetic Resonance Imaging Modified Dixon Fat Quantification Analysis Is a Quantitative and Reliable Method for Evaluating Fatty Atrophy of the Hamstring Muscle Complex After Proximal Avulsion Injury
Authors: John Hanke, Philipp Schenk, Irga Kemal, Markus Heinecke, Philipp Kobbe, Thomas Mendel
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeTo evaluate the reliability and validity of objective fat quantification using the modified Dixon magnetic resonance imaging sequence for assessing fatty degeneration in conservatively treated patients with proximal hamstring avulsion injuries.MethodsMagnetic resonance imaging with modified Dixon sequence was used. The fat percentage was measured in the modified Dixon FatFraction Map by setting a region of interest, whereby 3 methods were used with decreasing accuracy: anatomical outline of the entire bundle region of interest, respective elliptical region of interest of all 3 muscle bellies, and individual elliptical regions of interest of the bundle. In addition, the ischiocrural muscle cross‐sectional area and the Goutallier‐like classification were determined as a measure of atrophy.ResultsMagnetic resonance imaging was performed 35.8 ± 22.43 months after injury. Data from 16 patients (11 women, 5 men) with conservatively treated, unilateral proximal tendon rupture of the ischiocrural muscle (Wood type 3‐5) were included. When determining the cross‐sectional area and the modified Dixon fat content, the inter‐ and intraobserver reliability was good (all ≥ 0.933). In comparison, the reliability values of the Goutallier‐like classification were worse (0.897 and 0.733). The entire bundle and muscle belly region of interest methods for determining the modified Dixon fat content showed comparable results and are therefore suitable for fat quantification. The fat content determined in the anatomical region of interest correlated significantly with the stages of the Goutallier‐like classification (0.823, 95% confidence interval: 0.659, 0.912, P < .001) and the muscle cross‐sectional area (0.363, 95% confidence interval: −0.005, 0.638, P = .041).ConclusionsThe modified Dixon sequence represents a quantitative‐objective measure and is suitable as a measurement tool for fatty atrophy after proximal tendon injuries of the ischiocrural muscles. A simplified region of interest placement approach showed good validity and reproducibility.Level of EvidenceLevel III, retrospective diagnostic comparative study.

As a 2‐joint muscle bundle, the ischiocrural muscles (ICM) fulfill important static and dynamic functions in stance and gait through active hip extension and knee flexion. 1 Proximal avulsion injuries of the ICM are a result of sudden accidental overstretching 2 and can lead to considerable permanent functional impairments. 3 , 4 , 5 Conservative and surgical treatment thereof depends on the extent of the injury, 4 , 5 , 6 with the aim to prevent progressive muscular atrophy or functional restitution of the ICM. Muscular atrophy—characterized by a reduction in muscle cross‐sectional area (CSA) and fatty infiltration into the muscle (myosteatosis) 7 —reflects reduced activation of the affected muscle tissue and is associated with functional impairment. 8

In the clinical context, physical examinations with an assessment of strength development as well as general health questionnaires (e.g., SF12) or specific scores 9 are used to assess injury‐related outcomes. Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) is the diagnostic tool of choice for initial diagnostic confirmation and to assess the course of disease. For the categorical assessment of the extent of injury‐related muscle atrophy, the Goutallier classification, 10 which was primarily developed for the assessment of rotator cuff atrophy in computed tomography and was modified for MRI according to Fuchs et al., 11 can also be applied to the ICM. However, these tools are merely subjective, qualitative assessment measures. Equipment‐based methods such as isokinetic muscle strength measurement 12 and electromyography 13 are also available for the objective assessment of functional deficits. However, these are dependent on qualified personnel and appropriate equipment, which is why they have not become established in everyday clinical practice and are mainly used in the context of scientific research. Therefore, simple, practical, and repeatable instrumental methods that allow quantitative assessment of the extent of atrophy of the ICM after proximal avulsion injuries are required.

The modified Dixon sequence in MRI, used to quantitatively determine organ fat content, 14 has been established and tested for validity in other organ regions. Diagnostic images are generated by exploiting differences in hydrogen signal intensity based on molecular binding, such as in fat or water. It has already been used to analyze the musculoskeletal system, such as for the rotator cuff, 15 vertebral bodies and paravertebral muscles, 16 the pelvis, and the muscles of the thigh and lower leg. 17 , 18 , 19

The purpose of this study was to evaluate the reliability and validity of objective fat quantification using the modified Dixon MRI sequence for assessing fatty degeneration in conservatively treated patients with proximal hamstring avulsion injuries. We hypothesized that quantitative fat fraction analysis using the modified Dixon MRI sequence offers a more reliable and valid measure of fatty muscle degeneration after proximal hamstring avulsion injuries compared with the traditional, categorical Goutallier‐like classification.

METHODS

All patients with a proximal ICM tear with an injury event from 2015 to 2017 were identified via the digital hospital information system and analyzed. We included patients with conservatively treated, unilateral proximal tendon rupture of the ICM (Wood type 3‐5) 20 with no other mobility‐restricting injuries or diseases of the lower extremities and older than 18 years of age at the time of examination. We excluded patients with preexisting relevant functional deficits in stance and gait as a result of fractures, congenital or acquired deformities, underlying neurological diseases, and vertebral nerve disorders; those with previous surgical therapy; and any patients with contraindications for MRI examination. This study was a retrospective cohort study and was approved by the Medical Ethics Committee of the Medical Association of Saxony‐Anhalt, Germany (No. 32/16).

Conservative management consisted of nonsteroidal anti‐inflammatory drug analgesia, compression therapy, weight‐adjusted enoxaparin thromboprophylaxis, and leg immobilization. Clinical reassessment at 1 week was followed by physical therapy referral. Manual therapy, ultrasonography, and lymphatic drainage were initiated within 4 weeks. From week 5, therapy intensified with gait normalization and neuromuscular control. By week 8, thigh muscle strengthening and stretching exercises were added to enhance endurance and gait symmetry. Upon gait normalization, patients commenced individualized sport‐specific movement pattern training.

All patients were examined in the same 3 T MRI scanner (Ingenia model, Philips Healthcare, the Netherlands) in the supine position with feet first. In addition to the integrated posterior coil, an anterior coil was used for the examination. The following MRI protocol was used as standard.

Standard Protocol

The MRI protocol included coronary short Tau inversion recovery sequence, transverse T1w‐TSE, and transverse and sagittal T2w‐TSE sequences. The additional protocol for fat quantification was as follows: 3‐dimensional (3D) Fast Field Echo multiecho sequence with 6 echoes; TR/TE/DeltaTE (ms), 7.7/1.28/1.0; field of view 300 × 442 × 230 mm; acquisition matrix, 152 × 224; voxel (mm), 1.97/1.97/2.00; flip angle of 3°; and layer thickness, 2 mm.

The MRIs were used for a bilateral comparative measurement of the injured and uninjured ICM bundle in axial cross‐sectional images. For this purpose, the ICM bundle was analyzed in a standardized manner at the level of the largest muscle circumference of the recorded axial slices as well as 1 cm above and below, and the values obtained were averaged. The data were measured in 3 layers per thigh (Figure 1). In addition to the relative fat content, the individual CSA of the ICM was defined as an atrophy parameter.

Axial FatFraction Map layers (right and left) with reference line of the corresponding height in the STIR (center). (STIR, short Tau inversion recovery.)

First, the fat content in the ICM was measured in a predefined region of interest (ROI) in the axial fat fraction map of the modified Dixon sequence. For comparison, measurements were performed on both the formerly injured and the uninjured side as follows: ROIBa, muscle bundle anatomical; ROIMe, muscle bellies (individual) elliptical; and ROIBe, muscle bundle elliptical (Figure 2A‐D). The findings were analyzed using the Picture Archiving and Communication System (IMPAX 6.6.1.6014, Agfa Healthcare, Mortsel, Belgium). The ROIs were marked manually using the software function “Freeform” (ROIBa) and “Ellipse” (ROIMe and ROIBe). The ROIs were placed by 2 observers in each case.

Drawing the ROI in the IMPAX 6.6.1.6014 software. (A) Initial image, axial modified Dixon FatFraction Map. (B) ROIBa, ICM, muscle bundles anatomical. (C) ROIMe, muscle bellies elliptical, ICM (M. semimembranosus, M. semitendinosus, M. biceps femoris). (D) ROIBe, muscle bundles elliptical. (ICM, ischiocrural muscle; ROI, region of interest.)

To develop a method that is as time‐saving as possible in a clinical context, yet still reliable and reproducible, we use 3 methods in descending order of complexity. With the ROIBa area, the entire muscle bundle of the ICM was outlined manually in its anatomical shape, leaving out the fatty septa (Figure 2B). This method captures the largest possible muscle area but is technically the most complex. This area is also the basis for determining the CSA of the ICM bundle. In the second, simplified method ROIMe, elliptical ROIs were placed in the 3 muscle bellies of the ICM (M. semimembranosus, M. semitendinosus, M. biceps femoris) as far as possible without exceeding the anatomical limits of the individual muscle (Figure 2C). Therefore, proportionally less muscle tissue was recorded for the measurement. However, other tissue structures are left out. To simplify the measurement process even further, a third method was used to place an elliptical ROIBe including the intermuscular septa and cutting the neurovascular pathways as far as possible so that the outer boundary of the muscle bundle was not exceeded (Figure 2D).

In addition, the injured and uninjured sides were each assessed using the MRI Goutallier‐like classification as a categorical measure of the degree of atrophy. 11 In the axial T1‐weighted sequence, both observers used the extent of the hyperintense parts within the ICM to categorize it into grades as follows: 0, normal muscle without fatty infiltration; 1, low fatty infiltration; 2, fatty infiltration < muscle tissue; 3, fatty infiltration = muscle tissue; 4, fatty infiltration > muscle tissue), which represent the lipomatous areas in this sequence.

All 3 atrophy parameters (Goutallier‐like classification, modified Dixon FatFraction, CSA) were tested with regard to their intra‐ and interobserver reliability. A correlation analysis was then performed between the parameters (Figure 3).

Statistical Analysis

Goutallier‐like classification differences between the uninjured and injured leg were analyzed using the Wilcoxon test. Differences in muscular adiposity (modified Dixon) and area of the anatomical ROI between the injured and uninjured side were analyzed using a general linear model for repeated measures. The values corresponding to the uninjured and injured side were used as repeated measures.

The intra‐ and inter‐rater reliability was calculated using intraclass correlation and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) for absolute agreement and individual measurements. This was performed for the Goutallier‐like classification, the modified Dixon, and the CSA for the complete group and separately for the injured and uninjured sides.

The relationship between the fat content adjusted on the basis of the healthy side (rel modified Dixon) and the CSA (rel CSA) was analyzed using Pearson correlation. Correlations between the Goutallier‐like classification and the CSA and modified Dixon were analyzed using Spearman correlation. The threshold for significance was set at P = .05. SPSS V.29 (IBM SPSS Statistics for Windows, IBM, Armonk, NY) was used for the statistical analyses.

RESULTS

Data from 16 patients (11 women, 5 men) were included. Of these, 7 patients were diagnosed with a partial rupture of the ICM (Wood type 3) and 9 with a complete rupture (Wood type 4 or 5); all patients were treated conservatively. The mean age of the patients was 59.03 ± 5.43 years. The average body mass index was 27.98 ± 5.46. The mean interval between injury and MRI was 35.8 ± 22.43 months.

The muscular fatty degeneration on the uninjured side was rated as 0 in 10 cases (63%) and as 1 in 6 cases (37%) using the Goutallier‐like classification. The injured side (median = 1.5, Q1‐Q3: 0‐2) showed a higher Goutallier‐like classification than the uninjured side (median = 0, Q1‐Q3: 0‐1). The modified Dixon fat distributions for the anatomical ROI ranged from 3% to 46% (13 ± 11%). Muscular adiposity was significantly higher on the injured side. The muscular CSAs of the complete group were between 4 and 36 cm2 (18 ± 8 cm2). Although the CSA on the injured side was smaller on average than that on the injured side, it did not reach statistical significance levels (Table 1).

Reliability Analysis

Goutallier‐Like Classification

Reliability was rated as good for intra‐ and moderate for inter‐reader reliability (RR; see Figure 4). However, its lower limits of 0.529 and 0.316 are considered moderate and poor. On average, the Goutallier‐like classification had the lowest reliability for the uninjured side. This applied to both the intra‐ and inter‐RR of 0.745 and 0.605, respectively. Compared with the modified Dixon FatFraction and the anatomical CSA, the Goutallier‐like classification showed a significantly lower reliability (intra‐RR: 0.897, inter‐RR: 0.733).

Mean values and 95% confidence intervals of the ICCs of the intra‐ and inter‐RR for the respective methods for determining muscular atrophy in patients with ICM tears. Reliability was calculated from the complete cohort (total) as well as separately for the uninjured and injured side. (ICC, intraclass correlation; ICM, ischiocrural muscle; RR, reader reliability.)

modified Dixon FatFraction

The intra‐RR of the different ROIs for the modified Dixon ranged between 0.933 (injured, ROIBe) and 0.972 (total, ROIBa, Table 1). The intra‐RR, therefore, showed very good reliability, regardless of the ROI used or the side analyzed (uninjured or injured). The inter‐RR showed values between 0.869 (uninjured, ROIBa) and 0.991 (total, ROIMe). On average, the modified Dixon showed good to excellent reliability (Figure 4). The CIs of the inter‐RR are somewhat lower than those of the intra‐RR. The lowest 95% CI was found for the inter‐RR with 0.626 for ROIBa of the uninjured side.

CSA

The reliability of the measured CSAs using ROIBa was between 0.960 and 0.984 for the intra‐RR, while the inter‐RR was between 0.954 and 0.965 (Table 2, Figure 4). The reliability of the area measurements carried out can therefore be rated as excellent.

Correlation Analysis

To avoid bias due to anthropometric differences, the values for the 2 quantitative atrophy measures (modified Dixon and CSA) of the uninjured and injured sides were correlated. The correlation between the relative modified Dixon and the relative CSA (n = 16) showed a strong correlation of −0.727 (95% CI: −0.362, −0.899, P < .001) (Figure 5A). The smaller the CSA of the injured side in relation to the healthy side, the greater the extent of fatty muscle atrophy on the injured side.

Graphical comparison of the methods for determining the degree of atrophy with (A) correlation between relative modified Dixon fat fraction and relative CSA, (B) relative modified Dixon fat fraction and the degree of atrophy according to Goutallier, and (C) relative CSA and the degree of atrophy according to Goutallier. (CSA, cross‐sectional area.)

The Spearman correlation of the Goutallier‐like classification of the entire cohort (n = 16) with the rel modified Dixon and with the rel CSA showed significant correlations of 0.823 (95% CI: 0.659, 0.912, P < .001) and −0.363 (95% CI: −0.005, 0.638, P = .041), respectively (Figure 5B,C). For the healthy side, there was a significant correlation between the Goutallier‐like classification and the modified Dixon (r = 0.743, 95% CI: 0.377, 0.908, P < .001) but no correlation with the CSA (P = .837). For the injured side, the Goutallier‐like classification also showed a significant correlation with modified Dixon (r = 0.817, 95% CI: 0.529, 0.936, P < .001), but not with CSA (P = .061).

DISCUSSION

The analysis of the 3 methods of ROI placement for modified Dixon‐based fat quantification in descending order of effort (ROIBa > ROIMe > ROIBe) consistently showed very good values in terms of intra‐ and interobserver reliability with regard to the determination of the relative fat content.

The ICM plays a central role in active hip extension and knee flexion during stance and gait. Proximal avulsion injuries can, therefore, lead to significant and permanent functional impairments. 3 , 4 , 5 Surgical and conservative treatments aim to prevent progressive muscular atrophy and to restore function as far as possible. Measuring the circumference of the thigh in a lateral comparison is a simple method for assessing muscular atrophy and is regularly used for clinical assessment as well as for expert reports. The MRI‐based Goutallier‐like classification allows an image morphological assessment of the degree of atrophy‐related muscular fatty degeneration (myosteatosis) on the basis of signal intensity assessment on T1‐weighted MRI images. 11 However, it only represents an ordinal and subjective assessment scale. Quantitative methods that aim to assess functional parameters, such as isokinetic muscle strength measurement 12 or electromyography, 13 are not established in clinical routines and are mostly used in the context of scientific studies.

The quantitative determination of organ fat content using the modified Dixon sequence in MRI 14 is a method that has already been established and tested for validity in other organ regions. Studies show the proof‐of‐principle of Dixon‐based fat quantification in MRI by means of histopathological examination of muscle biopsies, with a valid correlation between the 2 quantification methods for fat content in muscle. 21 , 22 The advantage of the Dixon‐based method lies in its noninvasive feasibility. However, there are currently no standardized recommendations for its application to the ICM.

The complex manual delineation of the muscle and compartment boundaries (ROIBa) has been used in other studies. 23 ROIBa can also be used to simultaneously determine the anatomical muscle CSA. Alternatively, measurements of fatty muscle infiltration with automated segmentations at different muscle compartments on MRI examinations have also been described. 24 , 25 Comparisons have also shown a good correlation between automated measurements and manually segmented measurements. 26 , 27 In perspective, automated procedures offer the advantage of higher precision and reproducibility. There is also great potential with regard to artificial intelligence. However, this contrasts with the considerable additional costs of purchasing automated segmentation software.

In the present study, 2 less time‐consuming methods for modified Dixon fat quantification were investigated. The placement of a ROIBe (elliptical muscle bundle) is the most time‐effective. However, the area of the ICM cross section captured is smaller. In addition, noncontractile tissue of the ICM bundle was also recorded, which explains the slightly lower measured values. Nevertheless, this method can generate equally valid data as the other 2 methods (ROIBa and ROIMe). In this respect, the manual ROI placement proposed by us appears to be a reliable method that can be used in everyday clinical practice without significant effort.

Other research groups have compared the subjective Goutallier‐like classification with quantitative methods for determining fat content, such as MRI and magnetic resonance spectroscopy, of the rotator cuff, but also of other muscle compartments, such as the paraspinal muscles. A good correlation was found between the methods. 28 , 29 The reliability of the classification is limited by the subjective assessment by the examiner in an ordinal scale, and studies have shown poor interobserver reliability. 30 , 31 This was also confirmed in our study. The objective determination of fat percentage using the modified Dixon fat quantification is superior in terms of reliability and validity. Good reliability was also shown in other studies. 16 , 32

The third parameter used to assess ICM atrophy was the axial ICM CSA, which is automatically calculated and output as part of ROIBa placement. This variable also shows excellent reliability and is therefore superior to the Goutallier‐like classification in terms of scaling and objectivity. However, the more complex freehand placement of an ROIBa is required for an exact CSA determination. In principle, however, an modified Dixon sequence in the MRI is not necessarily required for the CSA measurement alone.

In summary, both the determination of modified Dixon FatFraction and CSA by MRI modified Dixon provide reliable quantitative data for the assessment of ICM atrophy. The repeatability of the measurements in MRI follow‐up examinations allows an assessment of functional restitution.

The quantitative determination of the fat content and the axial CSA of the ICM bundle offer the potential of noninvasive, reliable, and repeatable methods using MRI. All 3 methods of ROI placement presented show consistently comparable reliability. ROIBa also enables the simultaneous measurement of anatomical CSA as a further parameter for muscular trophism; ROIBe allows the measurement of fat content with a technically negligible effort. However, it is not possible to determine the CSA.

The collection of quantitative data distinguishes the method from previous categorical methods such as the Goutallier‐like classification.

Limitations

This study is not without limitations. The small sample size and retrospective nature of the study create a risk for selection bias. The methods investigated only allow a morphological image assessment of atrophy‐related ICM fatty degeneration. A correlation with methods for the direct measurement of functional impairment, such as dynamic strength measurement, should be examined in future studies. Furthermore, no adjustments were made for the covariate body mass index at the time of injury and MRI. Finally, we did not conduct a gender‐specific analysis as the objective of our work was to compare the methods and examine the relationships between the procedures, not to identify differences between the sexes.

CONCLUSIONS

The modified Dixon sequence represents a quantitative‐objective measure and is suitable as a measurement tool for fatty atrophy after proximal tendon injuries of the ICM. A simplified ROI placement approach showed good validity and reproducibility.

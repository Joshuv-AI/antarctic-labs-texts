Experimental investigation of novel 18 multiturn multiplane pulsating heat pipe for methanol
Authors: N. Narendra Babu, G. S. V. L. Narasimham, P. Srikrishna
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
A Pulsating Heat Pipe (PHP) is a wickless meandering capillary tube bent back and forth parallel to itself. In the present work, the possibility of employing a multiturn PHP as a pin-finned heat sink attached to a base plate is considered. Hence all the regions, other than those belonging to evaporators, function as condenser zones. Experimental investigations are carried on PHPs with multiturn, single plane and multiplane configurations. The condenser (or fin portions) of the PHPs is cooled by forced air flow and radiation to the surroundings. Experimental parametric runs are conducted by varying the fill ratio, orientations, and heat loads. Working fluid namely methanol is tested. PHP with 18 turns are tested for fill ratios of 30%, 50% and 70% and five orientations (+ 90°, + 45°, 0°, − 45° and − 90° measured with respect to horizontal). Heat loads are increased in steps of 50 W from 50 to 400 W for testing the PHPs. For different PHP physical geometries, the flow regimes change with respect to heat loads. The optimum angle for best thermal resistance is found to lie between 60 and 70°. Orientation independent operation observed in all the multiplane models and generally, the bottom heating mode (+ 90°) produce the highest heat transfer. During the startup, for lower heat loads, a temperature overshoot is observed before the establishment of pulsations. At higher heat loads, pulsations are set up without appreciable temperature overshoot, following a smooth startup. The results have shown enhancement in fin thermal conductivity compared to solid copper. Introduction

The Pulsating Heat Pipe (PHP) is a passive device which means a device capable of working without the aid of any additional mechanical or electrical power input and hence has a long life span. The working of a conventional heat pipe depends upon the capillary action of a wick, however, the tubing used for PHPs is usually of such small diameter that insertion of a wick becomes impractical. A PHP is a promising two-phase heat transfer device to control heat fluxes1. It is also a complex heat transfer device with strong thermo-hydraulics influencing its performance2. A PHP operates through non-equilibrium two-phase oscillations driven by vapor pressure fluctuations. The success of the device mainly depends on the continuous maintenance or sustenance of these non-equilibrium conditions within the system. Studies have been carried out exclusively towards understanding the flow regimes3.

PHPs are classified into single and multiturn, closed-loop and open-loop. A single loop PHP can be thought of as a building block for multiturn, single plane or multiplane PHPs. The term “capillary” means that the diameter of the tube is small enough for surface tension effect to become prominent4. A PHP contains evaporator, adiabatic and condenser zones. A pulsating heat pipe (PHP) is a wickless meandering capillary tube bent back and forth parallel to itself into many turns (single or multi-turn), These capillary tubes are evacuated initially and partially filled5. When a capillary tube is partially filled, the working fluid will break into liquid slugs and vapour plugs, provided the Bond number criterion is satisfied. Variation in the size of the channels produces variation in the pressure inside the container tubes6. The channel containing the two-phase working fluid with heated portion is referred to as the evaporator and the heat rejection portion is called the condenser7. The externally insulated region between the evaporator and condenser is called the adiabatic region8. The tube is filled with a working fluid such that only the fluid and its vapour phase exist.

Pulsating type physical behaviour is possible for a particular range of diameters. The diameter should satisfy the relation Bo ≤ 2, where Bo is the Bond number. The diameter satisfying the Bond number criterion is termed the critical diameter. Bond numbers less than 2 promote the formation of vapour bubbles and liquid slugs9. A series of alternate bubbles and slugs can more easily produce pulsations upon the application of heat to the evaporator. With diameters larger than critical, it is not possible to produce oscillatory motion of the bubbles and slugs, and this leads to decreased effectiveness of the PHP performance. The critical diameter changes slightly with temperature since the properties of the working fluid depend upon the temperature10.

Under microgravity conditions or where gravity has no component along the axis of the PHP tube, the required diameter is governed by the Weber number criterion. Sharp corners and edges in the channel are to be minimized as much as possible, as this affects normal slug flow in the PHP and tends to change the flow to stratified or annular flow regime. In stratified flow, the fluid flow will not be pulsatile. Also, the gravity driven thermosyphon effect domination leads to fall in heat transfer capability. Hence generally circular cross-sections are preferred for PHP applications11.

Pulsating heat pipes can be classified with respect to various parameters like form factor, spatial orientation of the channels and type of end connection. Mainly there are two types of PHPs; the looped PHP, which has two open ends connected to one another, and the unlooped PHP12, which has two unconnected ends. The number of turns will affect thermal performance by varying the perturbations in each turn. This disturbance in pressure drives the oscillating flow of PHP. As per the literature, a PHP with smaller number of turns will not operate in the top heat modes or horizontal position. Compared to an open loop PHP, a closed loop PHP configuration is likely to get more oscillations because of the possibility of alternate circulations of the working fluid. It may be recalled that the inclination of the PHP varies from Bottom Heat Mode (BHM) to Top Heat Mode (THM). As per the literature, pulsations generally occur from BHM to horizontal position. Therefore, the inclination should be chosen such that it is in the range + 90°to 0°. For fixed temperatures of the evaporator and condenser, their heat transfer capacities depend upon their heat transfer areas13. At low heat flux inputs to evaporator, the fluid may not attain oscillations. On the other hand, for too high a heat flux input to the evaporator, there is the likelihood of dry-out.

In this work a copper tube with an inner diameter of 2 mm is selected with working fluid methanol. In this case 2 mm diameter is slightly more than the critical value but not too far from satisfying the Bond number restriction.

Working of a pulsating heat pipe (PHP)

Heat is supplied to the device in the evaporator portion. Liquid in the form of liquid slugs or liquid film on the internal surface of the evaporator portion evaporates forming a vapour plug or bubble. Due to the increase in specific volume, the vapour plug expands and reaches the condenser portion, pushing the train of slugs and plugs ahead. In the condenser, the vapour volume reduces, and the pressure falls as the vapour transforms into liquid14. The pressure force now acts in the opposite direction, sending the slug-plug train back into the evaporator, wherein the liquid in the slugs or in the liquid film adhering to the wall evaporates creating a higher pressure. In this manner heat is transported from evaporator to condenser in an oscillatory or pulsating phenomenon. Hence the device is called an oscillating or pulsating heat pipe15. For fluid parcels moving between the evaporator and the condenser, if the PHP as a whole is taken as a constant total volume system, state that the overall change in the quality (or dryness fraction) is very small.

Fluid selection and working properties for PHP

The following are the important factors to be considered while selecting the operating fluid in PHP. Larger surface tension enables formation of slugs and plugs even in larger diameter tubes employed for PHPs. In wick-type heat pipes, large surface tension, wettability and low viscosity lead to good pumping action of fluid. Higher latent heat of vapourization results in high liquid conductance and high axial heat transfer. Meting point and critical point should not cross over the operating temperature of the fluid. When the operating range is within these limits, it is possible to obtain high heat transport with a small temperature difference in a PHP. Compatibility and corrosion resistance of the materials and chemical stability, toxicity and flammability of the working fluids are also important factors16. This device supports the possibility to use environmentally safe working fluids. Pressure drop, contact angle and liquid film thickness are important factors governing the working and improvement of the device.

When the capillary tubes are initially evacuated and then partially filled with the working fluid, a train of liquid slugs and vapour plugs is formed inside the PHP3. Differential pressure created in a PHP due to differential heating and cooling between the evaporator and condenser acts as the driving force for producing momentum changes and oscillations in the two-phase fluid. This two-phase oscillatory flow causes efficient heat transmission from evaporator to condenser with very low temperature difference.

The ratio of the volume of the fluid inside the PHP to the total volume is called the fill ratio17. It is widely accepted that the best working occurs in the range of 30% to 70% of the fill ratio. If the fill ratio is less than 20%, due to less liquid, dry-out occurs more readily at the evaporator and if the fill ratio is greater than 80%, the resulting lower degree of freedom may prevent pulsations. Reviews report that PHPs do not operate for higher charging ratios. A PHP is an efficient heat transportation device and is used in a variety of applications in thermal systems. An effective thermal conductance of over \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$1.5\times {10}^{4}$$\end{document} W/m.K is achievable with PHPs. Asymmetry in heating and deployment of valves can promote unidirectional circulation. Use of nano fluids can also increase heat transfer coefficient. Ultrasonic excitation will increase the pulsation amplitude and heat transfer coefficient.

The mode of working depends on the location (orientation) of evaporator and condenser. Bottom Heat Mode (BHM) refers to evaporator below the condenser and the Top Heat Mode (THM) refers to condenser below the evaporator. If the PHP ends are closed at the extreme end points, then it is termed as an Open Loop PHP (OLPHP)18. If both ends are closed the PHP is termed as closed loop PHP (CLPHP). A CLPHP can also be used with check valves. The closed loop PHPs are found to be more efficient than the open loop PHPs. For heat abstraction at the condenser, both water cooling and air cooling methodologies can be adopted.

Experimental work

Constructional details for three-plane, 18-turn PHP

Parametric studies are carried out with various working fluids, fill ratios and orientations. In particular, multi-plane PHP of 18 turns is constructed. The PHP is instrumented to obtain both pressure and temperature data at different locations of the system. In addition, the fin tip and root temperatures are monitored in multi-plane configuration. A study of the start-up characteristics of the PHP is also carried out. The reason behind varying the orientation with multi-plane, PHP is not only to examine the response to change in gravity but also to see if gravity-independent performance can be obtained. Copper tube of outer diameter 3 mm and inner diameter 2 mm is bent back and forth to form 18 turns in three parallel planes (i.e. multiplane, multiturn PHP), the bend radius being 7.5 mm. Two 3-way stop cocks, and a pressure transducer are fitted to the models. One stop clock is for evacuating the system and the other is for charging the working fluid. The vacuumizing and charging port combination features ball valves for their operation. The U-bends of the evaporator are brazed to a bottom copper plate. Cleaning of the PHP during fabrication stage is important to promote wetting of the walls with working fluid and capillary action19. After removing the solid particles from the inner tube, it is cleaned with a chlorinated solvent (trichloro-ethane) and an alkaline solution (sodium hydroxide) for removal of oily substances. Finally, the tube is rinsed with water or methanol for a small amount of time after the valves and ports are fixed.

Heating (evaporator) and cooling (condenser) arrangements

Ohmite make (TA1K0PH8R00KE) thin film resistance heater of 8Ω resistance and power rating of 1000 W (maximum) is attached to the base plate of the multiplane PHP. An axial fan of depth 38 mm and rating 130 cfm at 650 Pa is used to create a forced flow of air over the PHP tubes. The fan is fixed into a square duct of area 80 mm × 80 mm and depth 38 mm and discharges air into the enclosure containing the PHP. It may be noted that barring the evaporator portion, which is heated electrically, the rest of the PHP is exposed to air flow and functions as the condenser (there being no adiabatic sections). A helium leak detector (Pfeiffer ASM 182 TD +) is used to find the leak rate in the PHP. The leak rate is of the order 10–6 Torr-L/s.

The evaporator is heated by the base plate, which in turn is electrically heated. The thin film resistance heater attached to the base plate is connected to a DC (60 V and 40 A maximum) power supply of APLAB make. The heater power is varied in steps of 50 W beginning from 50 up to 400 W. The voltage is measured at the heater leads with a multimeter of accuracy ± 0.1 V. Due to the electrical heating, the boundary condition at the outer surface of the evaporator can be assumed to be constant heat flux. A combined convective and radiative boundary condition applies to condenser (i.e., the rest of the PHP) because the heat dissipation from the condenser takes place partly to the forced air flow and partly to the surrounding surfaces by radiation. Lower heat inputs, though resulting lower heat flux capability of the PHP, are found to provide a stable pulsating thermal behaviour20. Higher heat inputs are found to produce weaker oscillations. Still higher heat inputs (exceeding 400 W) can cause the dry-out of the fluid inside the evaporator zone.

Orientation (Tilt angle) of PHP

The orientation of the PHP can be changed from Bottom Heat Mode (BHM) to Top Heat Mode (THM) as shown in Fig. To change the orientation, a rotary disc mechanism is attached to the heat sink. Using the circular scale marked on the rotary disk for the rotation, the PHP can be clamped at any desired angle.

The PHP model charged with the required working fluid to the required fill ratio are tested in five different orientations, from the Bottom Heat Mode (BHM) (i.e., vertical position with the evaporator zone situated below the condenser) to the Top Heat Mode (THM, that is the reverse of BHM) by changing the angle in steps of 45°. The orientations are designated as: BHM (+ 90°), + 45°, Horizontal (0°), -45° and THM (-90°). To check the working range for each position, the heat input level is varied from minimum to maximum heat load21. It is of course desirable to have a reliable and efficient heat transfer device unaffected by orientation. At every inclination with respect to gravity, the required data (e.g., the average value of the base plate and tube temperature, at each heat input level) is obtained by covering the range from BHM to THM at all five orientations. The step width for orientation change is 45° for this multiplane PHP.

Operating parameters

Vacuum of 0.01 Pa was created inside the PHP using a turbo-molecular pump. For this purpose, the PHP is connected to the turbo-molecular pump via the first stop cock, while the second one is closed. The first stop cock is then closed, and the PHP is charged by connecting the second check valve to the loading copper tube. The charging is accomplished by a graduated syringe into which liquid working medium is filled with another syringe with a hypodermic needle22. The port is then opened, and the required amount of liquid is admitted into the PHP tube depending on the fill ratio. The liquid to be taken into the syringe equals the sum of the charge and the dead volumes in the stop cocks and the pressure transducer. Usually, better heat transfer performance requires fill ratios greater than 30%. In the present set of experiments, the fill ratio is chosen to be 30%, 50% and 70%.

The pressure and temperature data are collected for various orientations and heat fluxes, for different fill ratios and operating fluid is methanol. Type-J (Iron-constantan) thermocouples (accuracy: ± 1 °C as stated by the manufacturer) are embedded in the respective recesses made in the hardware at various locations, in all four models of PHP. The total number of thermocouples for this model is 19, with one reserved for the ambient temperature measurement. After switching on the condenser fan, the interfaced data logger is turned on. The power supply is switched on and the current is adjusted to achieve the desired heat dissipation. The temperature data is recorded for every 1 s for each channel. The startup and the steady state data are recorded. After each test, the heater is turned off for at least 30 min. The PHP is allowed to reach the ambient temperature before the next parameter set is tested. Temperatures are measured in the evaporator and condenser at the locations shown in Figs. A multiplexer (34980A) of 40 channels is used for temperatures logging and 1 Hz sampling rate is applied for each channel. Using pressure transducer (Honeywell make—PX2AG2XX 002BAAAX), the pressure data is measured and recorded once in every 0.05 s (i.e., pressure sampling frequency = 20 Hz). For 18 turn model, sample tests with methanol at 50% fill ratio and all the heat loads are repeated on three successive days to check the repeatability. The ambient temperature recorded during the course of experimentation for the tested conditions is the range of 26 °C to 28 °C.

Initially the condenser fan was switched on, after some time interval the power is supplied to the evaporator to prevent any excessive temperature rise. After this, the interfaced data logger switched to ON position. The electrical power supply is turned to ON position and the desired heat dissipation is maintained by regulating the current supply.

The system is started with the lowest heat input of 50 W in this case of model. It is observed that about 5 min time is sufficient for the system to reach a quasi-steady state after the application of heat. Since the system remains at the ambient temperature prior to the application of the 50 W heat input, a time of 15 min is allowed for the system to reach steady state. Later the heat input is increased in steps of 50 W and for each heat input, a time of 10 min is allowed for the PHP to reach steady state. However, the temperature and pressure data are continuously recorded using the DAQ (Data Acquisition System). For changing the fill ratio or working fluid, the PHP is first completely evacuated by drawing the vacuum and is then charged with the required fluid with the required amount to get the desired fill ratio.

Performance parameters of PHP

The performance of a PHP depends upon several parameters. These are geometrical parameters like tube (or channel) internal diameter, size (i.e., length for a given diameter) of the condenser and the evaporator, and operational parameters like volumetric filling ratio, viscosity of working fluid and thermophysical properties of the working fluid, input heat flux, and orientation of the PHP with respect to gravity24. The oscillatory phenomena might also depend upon inherent disturbances (such as fan flow induced vibration, non-uniformities in temperature of the evaporators, etc.). Increasing the temperature of the heating wall sections significantly increases the total average heat transfer25. Bond number and gravity forces also play an important role in PHP behaviour.

Conclusions

Experimental investigations are carried out on PHP with multi-plane geometrical configurations. For this model the tube inner diameter is 2 mm. The condenser (or fin portion) of the PHP is cooled by forced air flow and radiation to the surrounding surfaces. Parametric runs are conducted by varying fill ratio, orientations, and heat loads. The working fluids namely methanol is employed. PHP with 18 turns are tested for fill ratios of 30%, 50% and 70% and five orientations, namely + 90°, + 45°, 0° (horizontal), − 45° and − 90° with respect to the horizontal. Heat loads are increased in steps of 50 W from 50 to 400 W for testing the PHP.

The major conclusions from the work are as follows:As per Bond criteria, the tube diameter is fixed at 2 mm for methanol. The performance of the multi-plane PHP model is good in both positive and negative angle orientations.Start-up characteristics vary for different heat loads. Sudden start-up at lower heat loads and gradual and smooth start-up for higher heat loads is observed. This variation could be due to differences in void fraction distribution.The succession of events in the evaporator temperature in most of the runs is: (i) a gradual rise, (ii) a temperature overshoot (or horn), (iii) onset of oscillatory behaviour, and (iv) a kind of pseudo steady state. In some of the runs dry-out of the fluid is observed at the end of the heating period. Start-up temperature is lower in BHM, and it increases towards THM. The equivalent thermal conductivity also changes from BHM to THM.Good heat transfer performance is observed between 50° and 70° orientations.

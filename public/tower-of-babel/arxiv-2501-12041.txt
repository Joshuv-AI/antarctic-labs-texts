On the canonical bundle formula and effective birationality for Fano varieties in char p > 0 p>0
Xintong Jiang

Abstract
. In this paper, we give some results on the birational geometry of varieties of Fano type and boundedness problems in positive characteristic, including a result ensuring that boundedness is stable under normalizations, a version of canonical bundle formula which is easier to use, and the effective birationality of certain weak Fano varieties with good singularities, which is predicted by the BAB conjecture.

Divisors, pairs and linear systems

We define
\mathbb{N}=\mathbb{Z}^{\geq 0}
. Divisors refer to Weil divisors, which correspond to reflexive sheaves of rank 1 up to linear equivalence. The notion of Cartier divisors follows the usual definition, which correspond to invertible sheaves or line bundles up to linear equivalence. For a variety

\omega_{X}
denotes to be the lowest cohomology sheaf of its dualizing complex.
\omega_{X}
is a reflexive sheaf, which corresponds to a divisor

up to linear equivalence, which is called the canonical divisor of

For
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{Q},\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}=(\mathbb{Z}-p\mathbb{Z})^{-1}\mathbb{Z}

-divisors are the
\mathbb{F}
-linear combination of divisors and
\mathbb{F}
-linear equivalences between divisors are generated by

-linear combination of linear equivalences. A similar definition applies for Cartier divisors. An
\mathbb{F}
-divisor is
\mathbb{F}
-Cartier if it is
\mathbb{F}
-linear equivalent to an
\mathbb{F}
-Cartier divisor.
(X,B)

is a normal variety and

is a
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor such that

is
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier and

(in coefficients). A sub pair
(X,B)
is called a pair if

. Here,

is called a boundary if
(X,B)
is a pair.

For an
\mathbb{F}
-divisor

, we often denote

. The linear system
|M|=Proj(H^{0}(M))=\{N\sim M,N\geq 0\}
. The
\mathbb{F}
-linear system is defined as

, in particular
|M|_{\mathbb{Q}}=\bigcup\limits_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{1}{m}|mM|
. The base locus

denotes the maximal closed subset of

contained in each

, the stable base locus is defined as
Bs(|M|_{\mathbb{Q}}):=\bigcap\limits_{m\in\mathbb{N}}Bs(|mM|)
. If

,

will define a rational map

, which is determined on

by mapping

F-singularities

Other than singularities from MMP (e.g klt,lc,etc.), there is another system of singularities in positive characteristic, by detecting the wildness of the Frobenius map. The Frobenius
F:X\to X
is identity on the base space and the morphism on the structure sheaf is defined by the inclusion
F^{\sharp}:\mathcal{O}_{X}\to F_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}=\mathcal{O}_{X}^{\frac{1}{p}}
(as X is reduced). The inspiration is the theorem of Kunz
[
Kun69
]
, which says that a local k-ring essentially of finite type is regular if and only if the Frobenius map is free. Later Huneke and Hotchster stated the theory of F-singularities, one can see a detailed note on this topic in
[
Hot22
]
. Here we give the brief definitions:

Definition 2.1

(Map pairs)
.

Let
(X,\Delta)
be a log pair which is
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
-log Gorenstein for simplicity. Assume
(p^{e}-1)(K_{X}+\Delta)
is Cartier, set a certain line bundle:

\mathcal{L}_{e,\Delta}:=\mathcal{O}_{X}((1-p^{e})(K_{X}+\Delta))

Take any open affine chart
U\subset X
, consider

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}om_{\mathcal{O}_{U}}(F^{e}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{U}((p^{e}-1)\Delta),\mathcal{O}_{U})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{H}om_{\mathcal{O}_{U}}(F^{e}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{U}((p^{e}-1)\Delta)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{U}(K_{U}),\mathcal{O}_{U}(K_{U}))

\displaystyle=\mathcal{H}om_{\mathcal{O}_{U}}(F^{e}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{U}((p^{e}-1)\Delta+p^{e}K_{U}),\mathcal{O}_{U}(K_{U}))

\displaystyle=(\mathcal{H}om_{\mathcal{O}_{U}}(\mathcal{O}_{U}((p^{e}-1)\Delta+p^{e}K_{U}),\mathcal{O}_{U}(K_{U})))^{\frac{1}{p^{e}}}

\displaystyle=F^{e}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{U}((1-p^{e})(K_{U}+\Delta))=F^{e}_{*}\mathcal{L}_{e,\Delta}

Hence any section of
\mathcal{L}_{e,\Delta}
will correspond to a map

, i.e. every
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor

such that

and

is effective will correspond to a non-zero map

-divisor

.

(Test ideal and non-F-pure ideal, F-singularities)

Adjunctions and F-adjunctions

Adjunctions are important tools in birational geometry, the first kind of adjunctions is the divisorial adjunction. In characteristic 0, the smooth case of divisorial adjunction is well known as adjunction formulas in complex geometry. Generally speaking, divisorial adjunctions gives a rough relation between the singularities of the ambient variety and that of the divisors:

is again F-pure near

Now come back to our problem, consider
\lceil D\rceil-(\Delta+D^{\prime}_{\epsilon})\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}\epsilon A
, which is ample. Set

to be the (normalized) non-F-regular center of

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\to H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(K_{X}+\lceil D\rceil))\otimes I_{Z})\to H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(K_{X}+\lceil D\rceil))\xrightarrow{\gamma}H^{0}(Z,(K_{X}+\lceil D\rceil)|_{Z})

\displaystyle\to H^{1}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(K_{X}+\lceil D\rceil))\otimes I_{Z})\to\cdots

by the general F-adjunction, by F-version of Nadel vanishing using test ideals

Remark 4.1
.

If
(X,\Delta)
is lc at
x
and not klt at

are in the smooth locus of

and away from the stable base locus of

, then the pair is at best sharply F-pure near

. One can always find

such that

is strongly F-regular at

and

. So there is

is exactly F-pure at

but not F-regular at

and

by the following Lemma 4.2. However, these do not mean that a potentially birational divisor is an F-potentially birational divisor, since the F-pure center of

near

.

Indeed combine the statements above and
[
HMX14
, 7.1]
and some tie breaking arguments, we have if

is an ample
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor with

, then there is a bounded covering family
\{G_{i}\}
of subvarieties of

such that for any

such that
(X,\Delta)
is F-pure at

with a unique F-pure center

containing

.

The following statements are crucial for cutting the F-pure centers.

Lemma 4.2
.

Assume
(X,B)
is a strongly F-regular pair and
x\in X
a closed point. Suppose

is an effective
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
-Cartier
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor on

is sharply F-pure but not strongly F-regular at
x

, where

with the generic point

\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
-Cartier
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor that does not pass through

is sharply F-pure at
\xi
. Moreover, for any
0<\epsilon\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}

(X,B+D+\epsilon D)

Proof.

We may assume

since the problem is local. Consider the map of

-modules:

R_{\xi}\to F^{e}_{*}R_{\xi}(\lceil(p^{e}-1)(B+D)\rceil)\xrightarrow{F^{e}_{*}(i\otimes R_{\xi}(\lceil(p^{e}-1)(B+D)\rceil))}F^{e}_{*}R_{\xi}(\lceil(p^{e}-1)(B+D+\Delta)\rceil)

Where
(p^{e}-1)\Delta
is Cartier. The first morphism splits by sharp F-purity and the second is an isomorphism since

is an isomorphism. By a similar reason for the strongly F-regular pair
(X,B)
, one sees that

passes through
\xi
. If
(X,B+D+\epsilon D)
is sharply F-pure near
\xi
, then by
[
SZ13
, 2.15]
,

is strongly F-regular near
\xi
, which is a contradiction.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Assume
(X,B)
is a strongly F-regular pair and

a general closed point. Suppose

\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
-Cartier
\mathbb{Q}

, with the non-F-regular center

with the generic point

Proof.

Take

is globally generated and has vanished higher cohomologies by Serre’s vanishing. As a result,

Now, since

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
d,I\in\mathbb{N}
be natural numbers where
I
is not a multiple of
p
, then there is

depending only on

, such that if
X
is a strongly F-regular weak Fano variety of dimension
d
and

is a Cartier divisor, then

and
|-mK_{X}|

Proof.

The rough idea of the proof is to make a covering family of F-pure centers and use induction by adjunction formulas.

For the first assertion, one sees that
-IK_{X}
is a nef and big Cartier divisor, hence
vol(-IK_{X})=\frac{(-IK_{X})^{d}}{d!}\geq\frac{1}{d!}
. If
n^{\prime}>2d\sqrt[d]{d!}
, one can see
vol(-n^{\prime}IK_{X})>(2d)^{d}
as desired.

For the second assertion, we only prove the case where
X
is Fano. Suppose
m
is not bounded, where
m
is the smallest natural number such that
|-mK_{X}|
defines a birational map, one can find a sequence of such tuples
(X_{i},m_{i})
where
m_{i}\to\infty
. Keep

as in the first assertion, one sees by Remark 4.1, there is a bounded covering family
\{G_{i}^{s}\}_{s\in S}
of
X_{i}
such that for general points
x,y\in X_{i}
, there is a
0\leq\Delta_{i}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}-(n+1)K_{X_{i}}
such that
(X_{i},\Delta_{i})
is sharply F-pure at
x
with a unique (normalized) F-pure center
G_{i}^{s}
for some
s\in S
and not strongly F-regular at
y
. Set
d_{i}
to be the dimension of general
G_{i}^{s}
, consider the limit inferior of
\{d_{i}\}
, say
k
.

If
k=0
, then consider some
X_{i}
such that
d_{i}=0
, and one sees that
-2(n+1)K_{X_{i}}
is F-potentially birational (c.f.
[
HMX13
, 2.3.4]
), hence
|K_{X_{i}}+\lceil-2(n+1)K_{X_{i}}\rceil|
defines a birational map by Theorem 4.1. Hence

for some big

.

If

. Consider the smallest
l_{i}(=l)
such that
vol(-lnK_{X_{i}}|_{G_{i}})>k^{k}
, then by Lemma 4.3, there is a replacement
\Delta^{\prime}_{i}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}-4lnK_{X_{i}}
of

such that the dimension of the certain F-pure center decreases. As a result,
X
has a bounded covering family
\{G_{i}^{{}^{\prime}s}\}|_{s\in S^{\prime}}
such that for general points

, there is a

such that

is again Cartier, and hence

∎

Convergence to equilibrium for fully discretizations of nonlocal Cahn-Hilliard equation
Danni Zhang, Dongling Wang

Abstract
The study of long-term dynamics for numerical solutions of nonlinear evolution equations, particularly phase field models, has consistently garnered considerable attention. The Cahn-Hilliard (CH) equation is one of the most important phase field models and is widely applied in materials science. In order to more accurately describe the practical phenomena in material microstructural phase transitions, the Nonlocal Cahn-Hilliard (N-CH) equation incorporates a finite range of spatial nonlocal interactions is introduced, which is a generalization of the classic CH equation. However, compared to its classic counterpart, it is very challenging to investigate the long-term asymptotic behavior of solution to the N-CH equation due to the complexity of the nonlocal integral term and the lack of high-order diffusion term. In this paper, we consider first-order and second-order temporal discretization methods for the N-CH equation, respectively, while utilizing a second-order finite difference method for spatial approximation to construct the energy stable fully discrete numerical schemes. Based on energy stability and the Łojasiewicz inequality, we rigorously prove that the numerical solutions of these fully discrete numerical schemes converge to equilibrium as time goes to infinity.

There has been a significant amount of work on the convergence to equilibrium of numerical solutions for the classic CH equation. Merlet and Pierre

For schemes different from the backward Euler method, the study on the convergence to equilibrium can be referred to

It is natural to ask if the sequences generated by the fully discrete numerical schemes for the N-CH equation converge to equilibrium as time approaches infinity. However, the analysis of long-time behavior of numerical solutions to the N-CH equation faces significant challenges, owing to the complexity of the nonlocal integral term and the lack of high-order diffusion term. To date, there has no rigorous numerical analysis results.
Note that when the kernel
J
satisfies the aforementioned conditions, the CH equation can be viewed as a local approximation of the N-CH equation. This observation naturally motivates extending the convergence to equilibrium analysis techniques developed for the discretized classic CH equation to the N-CH equation.
The main contributions of this paper can be summarized as follows:

1.

First-order schemes:
We present a comprehensive analysis of convergence to equilibrium for three energy-stable first-order temporal discretization schemes, including the backward Euler scheme, the convex splitting scheme and stabilized linear semi-implicit (SSI1) scheme, applied to the N-CH equation with an analytic nonlinearity. A second order finite difference method is employed for spatial discretization. For the backward Euler scheme, we first establish the existence and uniqueness of the solution sequence. For all three schemes, we prove discrete mass conservation and energy stability. By combining the energy stability and the Łojasiewicz inequality, we rigorously prove that the sequences generated by these fully discrete schemes converge to equilibrium as time tends to infinity.

2.

Second-order schemes:
We develop and analyze two second-order temporal discretization schemes, including the BDF2 scheme and the linearly implicit (2LI) scheme, both of which are proved to be mass conservative and modified energy stable. Furthermore, we establish the unique solvability of the BDF2 scheme. Again leveraging energy stability and the Łojasiewicz inequality, we prove the convergence of the numerical solution sequences to a steady state as
n\to\infty
.

The paper is organized as follows.
In Section
2
, we introduce the discretizations of the spatial differential operators and the periodic convolution for the N-CH equation.
In Section
3
, we give three first-order fully discrete numerical schemes for the N-CH equation and establish the unique solvability, mass conservation and energy stability. We rigorously prove the sequences generated by the proposed three numerical schemes converge to the equilibrium as time goes to infinity. Two second-order schemes is presented in Section
4
.
Finally, some concluding remarks are given in Section
5
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Guan et al. 2014b

If
\phi,\psi\in\mathcal{C}_{\bar{N}\times\bar{N}}^{per}
. Assume that

3
Convergence to equilibrium for the first-order fully discrete schemes of the N-CH equation

In this section, we mainly consider three energy-stable first-order fully discrete schemes (the backward Euler scheme, convex splitting scheme, stabilized linear semi-implicit (SSI1) scheme)
for the N-CH equation and prove these schemes generate sequences converge to equilibrium as time tends to infinity.
For the first-order backward Euler scheme, we prove the uniquely solvability, mass conservation and energy stability. Subsequently, by leveraging the energy stability and the Łojasiewicz inequality, we give detailed proof for the sequence generates by this scheme converges to a steady state as
n\to\infty
.
For the first-order convex splitting scheme and the SSI1 scheme, we also prove that the corresponding solution sequences converge to equilibrium.

Our proof primarily relies on energy stability and the following Łojasiewicz inequality (
3.1
).

Definition 3.1
.

We say
G\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{M},\mathbb{R})
satisfies the Łojasiewicz inequality at
U^{\ast}\in\mathbb{R}^{M}
if there exists
\nu\in(0,1/2],\gamma_{1}>0
and
\sigma>0
such that for all
V\in\mathbb{R}^{M}
,

\|V-U^{\ast}\|<\sigma\Rightarrow|G(V)-G(U^{\ast})|^{1-\nu}\leq\gamma_{1}\|\nabla G(V)\|,

(3.1)

where
\|\cdot\|
denotes the Euclidean norm in
\mathbb{R}^{M}
and
\nabla G(U)=(\partial_{1}G(U),\cdots,\partial_{M}G(U))^{T},
.

Theorem 3.1

(Łojasiewicz Theorem
Łojasiewicz 1965
)
.

If
G\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{M},\mathbb{R})
is real analytic, thus
G
satisfies the Łojasiewicz inequality at each point
U^{\ast}\in\mathbb{R}^{M}
.

The following theorem is the key to proving convergence to equilibrium.

Theorem 3.2
.

Brachet et al. 2022

If
G\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{M},\mathbb{R})
is real analytic. Consider a bounded sequence
(U^{n})_{n\geq 0}
in
\mathbb{R}^{M}
which satifies the following conditions:

H1:

There exists a constant
c_{2}>0
such that for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

G(U^{n})-G(U^{n+1})\geq c_{2}\|U^{n+1}-U^{n}\|^{2};

H2:

There exists a constant
c_{3}>0
such that for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\|\nabla G(U^{n+1})\|\leq c_{3}\|U^{n+1}-U^{n}\|.

Thus, the whole sequence
(U^{n})_{n\geq 0}
converges in
\mathbb{R}^{M}
.

3.1
The first-order backward Euler scheme

In this subsection, we focus on the fully discrete first-order backward Euler scheme for the N-CH equation. We prove the unique solvability, mass conservation and energy stability of the proposed numerical scheme. Futhermore, we utilize the energy stability, the Łojasiewicz inequality (
3.1
) and Theorem
3.2
to demonstrate that the sequence generated by this scheme converges to an equilibrium as
n\to\infty
.

The fully discrete first-order backward Euler scheme for the N-CH equation is constructed as follows: let
u_{h}^{0}=(u_{ij}^{0})\in\mathcal{C}_{N\times N}^{per}
, and for
n\geq 0
, let
u_{h}^{n+1}=(u_{ij}^{n+1})\in\mathcal{C}_{N\times N}^{per},\;\omega_{h}^{n+1}=(\omega_{ij}^{n+1})\in\mathcal{C}_{N\times N}^{per}
solve

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{lllll}\frac{u_{h}^{n+1}-u_{h}^{n}}{\tau}=\Delta_{h}\omega_{h}^{n+1},\\
\omega_{h}^{n+1}=F^{\prime}(u_{h}^{n+1})+\varepsilon^{2}[J\circledast\mathbf{1}]u_{h}^{n+1}-\varepsilon^{2}[J\circledast u_{h}^{n+1}].\end{array}\right.

The corresponding discrete energy is defined as

E_{h}(u_{h})=h^{2}(F(u_{h})\|\mathbf{1})+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}[J\circledast\mathbf{1}]}{2}\|u_{h}\|_{2}^{2}-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}h^{2}(u_{h}\|[J\circledast u_{h}]),\quad\forall u_{h}\in\mathcal{C}_{N\times N}^{per}.

(3.5)

The following theorem illustrates that the first-order backward Euler scheme (
3.1
) is mass conservative, uniquely solvable and energy stable.

Theorem 3.3
.

The total mass for the scheme (
3.1
) is conserved, i.e.,
(u_{h}^{n+1}-u_{h}^{n}\|\textbf{1})=0
for
n\geq 0
.
Moreover, if the time step size
\tau
satifies

\tau\leq\frac{2\gamma_{0}}{C_{\textrm{J}}\varepsilon^{4}},

(3.6)

where
C_{\textrm{J}}>0
is a constant that depends on kernel function
J
but is independent of
h
. Thus, the first-order backward Euler scheme (
3.1
) is uniquely solvable and energy stable, i.e.,

E_{h}(u_{h}^{n+1})\leq E_{h}(u_{h}^{n}),\quad\forall n\geq 0.

Proof.

Taking the discrete inner product of the first equation in (
3.1
) with
\mathbf{1}
, we obtain
(u_{h}^{n+1}-u_{h}^{n}\|\mathbf{1})=0
, which implies the scheme (
3.1
) is mass conservative.
Next, We prove that the scheme (
3.1
) is uniquely solvable and energy stable.

We define the energy functional
G[z]
on the space
\mathcal{M}_{h}^{\ast}=\{z\in\mathcal{C}_{N\times N}^{per}|\;(z\|\mathbf{1})=(u_{h}^{n}\|\mathbf{1})\}
,

G[z]=\frac{1}{2\tau}\|z-u_{h}^{n}\|_{-1}^{2}+E_{h}(u_{h}).

According to Lemma
2.1
, we have

\displaystyle G[z]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\tau}\|z-u_{h}^{n}\|_{-1}^{2}+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}[J\circledast\mathbf{1}]}{2}\|z\|_{2}^{2}-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}h^{2}}{2}(z\|[J\circledast z])+\frac{1}{4}\|z\|_{4}^{4}-\frac{1}{2}\|z\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}|\Omega|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2\tau}\|z-u_{h}^{n}\|_{-1}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\|z\|_{4}^{4}-\frac{1}{2}\|z\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}|\Omega|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{4}(4\|z\|_{2}^{2}-4|\Omega|)-\frac{1}{2}\|z\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}|\Omega|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\|z\|_{2}^{2}-\frac{3}{4}|\Omega|.

Clearly, the functional
G[z]
is coercive; hence, a minimum of
G[z]
exists. We now prove that
G[z]
is strictly convex for any
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
\psi\in\mathcal{M}_{h}^{0}
, which will imply the uniqueness of the minimizer. Simple calculations yield

\frac{\,\mathrm{d}^{2}G}{\,\mathrm{d}s^{2}}[z+s\psi]\big|_{s=0}=\frac{1}{\tau}\|\psi\|_{-1}^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}[J\circledast\mathbf{1}]\|\psi\|^{2}_{2}-\varepsilon^{2}h^{2}([J\circledast\psi]\|\psi)+3\|z\psi\|_{2}^{2}-\|\psi\|_{2}^{2}.

(3.7)

An application of Lemma
2.2
to the term
([J\circledast\psi]\|\psi)
in (
3.7
) shows

\displaystyle h^{2}([J\circledast\psi]\|\psi)

\displaystyle=-h^{2}([J\circledast\psi]\|\Delta_{h}((-\Delta_{h})^{-1}\psi))

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{\textrm{J}}\tau\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\|\psi\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{1}{2\tau\varepsilon^{2}}\|\nabla_{h}(-\Delta_{h})^{-1}\psi\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{\textrm{J}}\tau\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\|\psi\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{1}{2\tau\varepsilon^{2}}\|\psi\|_{-1}^{2}.

(3.8)

Combining (
3.7
)-(
3.1
) and using the condition that
\tau
satisfies (
3.6
), we obtain,

\displaystyle\frac{\,\mathrm{d}^{2}G}{\,\mathrm{d}s^{2}}[z+s\psi]\big|_{s=0}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2\tau}\|\psi\|_{-1}^{2}+\left(\varepsilon^{2}[J\circledast\mathbf{1}]-\frac{C_{\textrm{J}}\tau\varepsilon^{4}}{2}-1\right)\|\psi\|_{2}^{2}+3\|z\psi\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\tau}\|\psi\|_{-1}^{2}+\left(\gamma_{0}-\frac{C_{\textrm{J}}\tau\varepsilon^{4}}{2}\right)\|\psi\|_{2}^{2}+3\|z\psi\|_{2}^{2}>0,

where we have used the assumption (
2.11
). Therefore, the functional
G[z]
has a unique minimizer
u_{h}^{n+1}
on
\mathcal{M}_{h}^{\ast}
, and this minimizer is also the unique solution of the scheme (
3.1
), and we have,

G[u_{h}^{n+1}]=\frac{1}{2\tau}\|u_{h}^{n+1}-u_{h}^{n}\|_{-1}^{2}+E_{h}(u_{h}^{n+1})\leq G[u_{h}^{n}]=E_{h}(u_{h}^{n}),

(3.9)

this implies that
E_{h}(u_{h}^{n+1})\leq E_{h}(u_{h}^{n})
.
This concludes the proof.
∎

Next, we will use the energy stability, Łojasiewicz inequality (

) and Theorem

to prove that the sequence produced by the fully discrete first-order backward Euler scheme (

Theorem 3.4
.

If
\tau

u_{1}^{n+1}=u_{1}^{n}=u_{1}^{0}

\begin{cases}\frac{\tilde{U}^{n+1}-\tilde{U}^{n}}{\tau}=-\tilde{A}_{h}\tilde{W}^{n+1},\\
\tilde{W}^{n+1}=\tilde{\nabla}\tilde{F}_{M}(\tilde{U}^{n+1})+\varepsilon^{2}\tilde{A_{J}}\tilde{U}^{n+1},\end{cases}

(3.15)

Step 3. The energy
\tilde{\Phi}

When

3.6

\displaystyle\tilde{\Phi}(\tilde{U}^{n})-\tilde{\Phi}(\tilde{U}^{n+1})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2\tau}(\tilde{U}^{n+1}-\tilde{U}^{n})^{T}\tilde{A}_{h}^{-1}(\tilde{U}^{n+1}-\tilde{U}^{n})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2\tau\lambda_{M}}\|\tilde{U}^{n+1}-\tilde{U}^{n}\|^{2},\quad n\geq 0,

(3.16)

where

is the largest eigenvalue of

3.2
The first-order convex splitting scheme

We write the energy

Guan et al. 2014b

3.3
The first-order stabilized linear semi-implicit (SSI1) scheme

In order to ensure that the linear scheme is energy stable, we need to make the following assumption on the nonlinear potential
F
:

Assumption 3.1
.

There exists a constant
\beta>0
, such that

\max_{s\in\mathbb{R}}|F^{\prime\prime}(s)|\leq\beta.

Therefor, for a given
K>1
, we can replace
F(u)=\frac{1}{4}(u^{2}-1)^{2}
by the modified potential
F_{K}

Shen and Yang 2010
,

F_{K}(u)=\begin{cases}\frac{3K^{2}-1}{2}u^{2}-2K^{3}u+\frac{1}{4}(3K^{4}+1),&u>K,\\
\frac{1}{4}(u^{2}-1)^{2},&u\in[-K,K],\\
\frac{3K^{2}-1}{2}u^{2}+2K^{3}u+\frac{1}{4}(3K^{4}+1),&u<-K,\end{cases}

(3.29)

Moreover,

F_{K}^{\prime}(u)=\begin{cases}(3K^{2}-1)u-2K^{3},&u>K,\\
u^{3}-u,&u\in[-K,K],\\
(3K^{2}-1)u+2K^{3},&u<-K.\end{cases}

We note that
F_{K}\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})
and that
F_{K}^{\prime\prime}(s)
satisfies Assumption
3.1
.

The SSI1 scheme of the N-CH equation is established as follows. Let
u_{h}^{0}\in\mathcal{C}_{N\times N}^{per}
, for all
n\geq 0
, find
(u_{h}^{n+1},\omega_{h}^{n+1})\in\mathcal{C}_{N\times N}^{per}\times\mathcal{C}_{N\times N}^{per}
such that

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{lllll}\frac{u_{h}^{n+1}-u_{h}^{n}}{\tau}=\Delta_{h}\omega_{h}^{n+1},\\
\omega_{h}^{n+1}=F_{K}^{\prime}(u_{h}^{n})+S(u_{h}^{n+1}-u_{h}^{n})+\varepsilon^{2}[J\circledast\mathbf{1}]u_{h}^{n+1}-\varepsilon^{2}[J\circledast u_{h}^{n+1}],\\
\end{array}\right.

where
S>0
is a stabilization constant. This is a linear scheme with first order accuracy and has a unique solution. The mass is conserved, which can be verified by taking the discrete
\ell^{2}
inner product of the first equation in (
3.3
) with
\mathbf{1}
.

Define a discrete version of the energy as

(3.33)

Theorem 3.7

4
Convergence to equilibrium for the second-order fully discrete schemes

This section focus on two energy-stable second-order fully discrete schemes for the N-CH equation, including the BDF2 scheme and the linearly implicit (2LI) scheme. First, we construct a fully discrete BDF2 scheme and establish its unique solvable and modified energy stability. Then, by combining the energy stability with the Łojasiewicz inequality (
3.1
) and utilizing Theorem
3.2
, we rigorously prove that the sequence generated by the BDF2 scheme converges to a steady state as time goes to infinity. Finally, we introduce a fully discrete 2LI scheme and prove its discrete modified energy stability. The convergence analysis for this scheme follows similar arguments. Therefore, we only outline the main idea and state the convergence result.

4.1
BDF2 scheme

The BDF2 scheme for the N-CH equation reads: given

\end{array}\right.

We assume that
(u_{h}^{1}\|\mathbf{1})=(u_{h}^{0}\|\mathbf{1})

For notational simplicity, we denote the difference

2.1

\displaystyle G[z]

\tau

3.6

A_{h}^{-1}
. The discrete modified energy is defined by

\widetilde{\Phi}(U,V)=\Phi(U)+\frac{1}{4\tau}V^{T}A_{h}^{-1}V,

(4.12)

where
\Phi(U)
is defined in (
3.12
). We will later show that the modified energy
\widetilde{\Phi}
can be recovered to
\Phi
as
n\to\infty
.

Step 2. The sequence
(U^{n},\delta U^{n})_{n\geq 0}
satisfies the assumptions of Theorem
3.2
in
\mathbb{R}^{2M}
.

We denote
\delta U^{n+1}=U^{n+1}-U^{n}
, and write

3U^{n+1}-4U^{n}+U^{n-1}=2\delta U^{n+1}+(\delta U^{n+1}-\delta U^{n}).

(4.13)

By Theorem
4.11
, when
\tau<\frac{2\gamma_{0}}{C_{\textrm{J}}\varepsilon^{4}}
, we have

\frac{c_{\tau}}{\tau}\left(\|\delta U^{n+1}\|^{2}+(\delta U^{n+1}-\delta U^{n})^{T}A_{h}^{-1}(\delta U^{n+1}-\delta U^{n})\right)+\widetilde{\Phi}(U^{n+1},\delta U^{n+1})\leq\widetilde{\Phi}(U^{n},\delta U^{n}),\quad\forall n\geq 0,

(4.14)

where
c_{\tau}=\min\left(\frac{\gamma_{0}\tau}{2}-\frac{\varepsilon^{4}C_{J}\tau^{2}}{4},\frac{1}{4}\right)>0
. Let
G=\widetilde{\Phi}
. Then the assumption
H1
holds. From inequality (
4.14
), it follows that the sequence
(\widetilde{\Phi}(U^{n},\delta U^{n}))_{n\geq 0}
converges to some
\Phi^{\ast}
and
\delta U^{n}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. This implies that the original energy sequence
(\Phi(U^{n}))_{n\geq 0}
converges to
\Phi^{\ast}
. Therefore, the modified energy
\widetilde{\Phi}
and the original energy
\Phi
share the same limit
\Phi^{\ast}
, meaning the the modified energy is recovered at infinity.
Next, we verity that
\widetilde{\Phi}
satisfies condition
H2
.

By eliminating the intermediate variable
W^{n+1}
from the scheme (
4.11
) and utilizing the identity (
4.13
), we obtain

\|\nabla\Phi(U^{n+1})\|\leq\frac{1}{\tau}\|A_{h}^{-1}\|(\|U^{n+1}-U^{n}\|+\|\delta U^{n+1}-\delta U^{n}\|).

(4.15)

Since
\nabla\widetilde{\Phi}(U,V)=\left(\nabla\Phi(U),\frac{A_{h}^{-1}V}{2\tau}\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{2M}
, we have

\displaystyle\|\nabla\widetilde{\Phi}(U,V)\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|\nabla\Phi(U)\|^{2}+\frac{1}{4\tau^{2}}\|A_{h}^{-1}V\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla\Phi(U)\|^{2}+\frac{\|A_{h}^{-1}\|^{2}}{4\tau^{2}}\|V\|^{2}.

(4.16)

Together with (
4.14
)-(
4.1
), we deduce

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Phi}(U^{n},\delta U^{n})-\widetilde{\Phi}(U^{n+1},\delta U^{n+1})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{c_{\tau}\min\left(1,\frac{1}{\lambda_{M}}\right)}{\tau}(\|U^{n+1}-U^{n}\|^{2}+\|\delta U^{n+1}-\delta U^{n}\|^{2})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\tau c_{\tau}\min\left(1,\frac{1}{\lambda_{M}}\right)}{4\|A_{h}^{-1}\|^{2}}\|\nabla\Phi(U^{n+1})\|^{2}+\frac{c_{\tau}\min\left(1,\frac{1}{\lambda_{M}}\right)}{2\tau}\|U^{n+1}-U^{n}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\tau c_{\tau}\min\left(1,\frac{1}{\lambda_{M}}\right)}{4\|A_{h}^{-1}\|^{2}}\|\nabla\widetilde{\Phi}(U^{n+1},\delta U^{n+1})\|^{2},\quad\forall n\geq 0,

(4.17)

where
\lambda_{M}>0
is the largest eigenvalue of
A_{h}
. This implies that
\widetilde{\Phi}
satisfies assumption
H2
.

Step 3. The modified energy
\widetilde{\Phi}
satisfies the Łojasiewicz inequality in
\mathbb{R}^{2M}
.

From
Step 1
, we have a convergent subsequence
(U^{n_{k}})_{n_{k}\geq 0}
of
(U^{n})_{n\geq 0}
converges to some
U^{\infty}
. Since
\Phi
is real analytic, it satisfies the Łojasiewicz inequality (
3.1
) at
U^{\infty}
. In the following proof, suppose
\|U-U^{\infty}\|<\sigma
and
\|V^{T}A_{h}^{-1}V\|<1
. Using the inequality
(a+b)^{1-\nu}\leq a^{1-\nu}+b^{1-\nu}
for
a,b\geq 0
, we obtain

\displaystyle|\widetilde{\Phi}(U,V)-\widetilde{\Phi}(U^{\infty},\textbf{0})|^{1-\nu}

\displaystyle\leq|\Phi(U)-\Phi^{\ast}|^{1-\nu}+\frac{1}{(4\tau)^{1-\nu}}(V^{T}A_{h}^{-1}V)^{\frac{2(1-\nu)}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\gamma_{1}\|\nabla\Phi(U)\|+\frac{1}{(4\tau)^{1-\nu}}(V^{T}A_{h}^{-1}V)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{2}\max\left\{\gamma_{1},\frac{(4\tau)^{\theta}\lambda_{M}}{2\sqrt{\lambda_{1}}}\right\}\left(\|\nabla\Phi(U)\|^{2}+\frac{1}{4\tau^{2}}\|A_{h}^{-1}V\|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{2}\max\left\{\gamma_{1},\frac{(4\tau)^{\theta}\lambda_{M}}{2\sqrt{\lambda_{1}}}\right\}\|\nabla\widetilde{\Phi}(U,V)\|,

(4.18)

where
\lambda_{1}>0
and
\lambda_{M}>0
are the smallest and largest eigenvalues of the positive definite matrix
A_{h}
. Therefore,
\widetilde{\Phi}
satisfies the Łojasiewicz inequality at
(U^{\infty},\textbf{0})
. Finally, taking the limit in (
4.1
) and invoking Theorem
3.2
completes the proof.
∎

4.2
The second-order linearly implicit (2LI) scheme

We replace the potential function
F
with the modified potential function
F_{K}
defined by (
3.29
), thereby ensuring the energy stability of the linear scheme. The 2LI scheme for the N-CH equation reads: given
u_{h}^{0},u_{h}^{1}\in\mathcal{C}_{N\times N}^{per}
, for all
n\geq 1
, let
u_{h}^{n+1},\omega_{h}^{n+1}\in\mathcal{C}_{N\times N}^{per}
solve

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{lllll}\frac{3u_{h}^{n+1}-4u_{h}^{n}+u_{h}^{n-1}}{2\tau}=\Delta_{h}\omega_{h}^{n+1},\\
\omega_{h}^{n+1}=2F_{K}^{\prime}(u_{h}^{n})-F_{K}^{\prime}(u_{h}^{n-1})+\varepsilon^{2}[J\circledast\mathbf{1}]u_{h}^{n+1}-\varepsilon^{2}[J\circledast u_{h}^{n+1}],\\
\end{array}\right.

where the nonlinearity
F_{K}
satisfies the assumption (
3.1
). The 2LI scheme (
4.2
) is a linear second-order scheme and its solution is unique. Under the condition that
(u_{h}^{1}\|\mathbf{1})=(u_{h}^{0}\|\mathbf{1})
, the 2LI scheme (
4.2
) is mass conservative. Next, we establish its modified energy stability.

Theorem 4.13
.

If
\beta
and
\tau
satisfy:

\beta\leq\frac{\gamma_{0}+1}{3},\;\tau\leq\frac{2(\gamma_{0}+1-3\beta)}{C_{\textrm{J}}\varepsilon^{4}},

(4.22)

where
C_{\textrm{J}}>0
depends on kernel function
J
but is independent of
h
. Then, the 2LI scheme (
4.2
) satisfies the modified energy stability, that is

\widetilde{E}_{K,h}(u_{h}^{n+1},\delta u_{h}^{n+1})\leq\widetilde{E}_{K,h}(u_{h}^{n},\delta u_{h}^{n}),\quad\forall n\geq 0,

(4.23)

where

\widetilde{E}_{K,h}(u_{h},v_{h})=E_{K,h}(u_{h},v_{h})+\frac{\beta}{2}\|v_{h}\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{1}{4\tau}\|v_{h}\|_{-1}^{2}.

Proof.

We Take the discrete
\ell^{2}
inner product of the first equation in (
4.2
) with
(-\Delta_{h})^{-1}\delta u_{h}^{n+1}
and of the second equation with
\delta u_{h}^{n+1}
. Applying the identity (
4.4
) then yields

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\tau}\|\delta u_{h}^{n+1}\|_{-1}^{2}+\frac{1}{2\tau}h^{2}(\delta u_{h}^{n+1}-\delta u_{h}^{n}\|(-\Delta_{h})^{-1}\delta u_{h}^{n+1})+\varepsilon^{2}[J\circledast\mathbf{1}]h^{2}(u_{h}^{n+1}\|\delta u_{h}^{n+1})-\varepsilon^{2}h^{2}([J\circledast u_{h}^{n+1}]\|\delta u_{h}^{n+1})

\displaystyle=h^{2}(2F_{K}^{\prime}(u_{h}^{n})-F_{K}^{\prime}(u_{h}^{n-1})\|u_{h}^{n}-u_{h}^{n+1}).

(4.24)

Since
F_{k}
satisfies assumption (
3.1
), we can use Taylor’s expansion and the mean value theorem to obtain

h^{2}(F_{K}^{\prime}(u_{h}^{n})\|u_{h}^{n}-u_{h}^{n+1})\leq h^{2}(F_{K}(u_{h}^{n})\|\mathbf{1})-h^{2}(F_{K}(u_{h}^{n+1})\|\mathbf{1})+\frac{\beta}{2}\|u_{h}^{n}-u_{h}^{n+1}\|^{2}_{2},

(4.25)

and

h^{2}(F_{K}^{\prime}(u_{h}^{n})-F_{K}^{\prime}(u_{h}^{n-1})\|u_{h}^{n}-u_{h}^{n+1})\leq\frac{\beta}{2}\|u_{h}^{n}-u_{h}^{n-1}\|^{2}_{2}+\frac{\beta}{2}\|u_{h}^{n}-u_{h}^{n+1}\|^{2}_{2}.

(4.26)

Starting from the identity (
4.2
), we combine (
4.25
) and (
4.26
), using (
4.7
) and (
4.1
) to deduce

\frac{3}{4\tau}\|\delta u_{h}^{n+1}\|_{-1}^{2}+\left(\frac{\gamma_{0}+1-3\beta}{2}-\frac{\varepsilon^{4}C_{J}\tau}{4}\right)\|\delta u_{h}^{n+1}\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{1}{4\tau}\|\delta u_{h}^{n+1}-\delta u_{h}^{n}\|_{-1}^{2}+\widetilde{E}_{K,h}(u_{h}^{n+1},\delta u_{h}^{n+1})\leq\widetilde{E}_{K,h}(u_{h}^{n},\delta u_{h}^{n}).

(4.27)

If the condition (
4.22
) holds, we conclude that the 2LI scheme satisfies the energy estimate (
4.23
).
∎

We now prove the convergence to equilibrium for the 2LI scheme (
4.2
).

Theorem 4.14
.

Suppose
\beta<\frac{\gamma_{0}+1}{3},\;\tau<\frac{2(\gamma_{0}+1-3\beta)}{C_{\textrm{J}}\varepsilon^{4}}
and let
(u_{h}^{n},\omega_{h}^{n})_{n\geq 0}
be a sequence which complies with (
4.2
). Then the whole sequence converges to a steady state
(u_{h}^{\infty},\omega_{h}^{\infty})
that is a solution of (
3.10
).

Proof.

Analogously to
Step 2
of Theorem
3.4
, the 2LI scheme (
4.2
) can be rewritten in matrix form: let
U^{0},U^{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{M}
, and for
n=1,2,\cdots
, let
(U^{n+1},W^{n+1})\in\mathbb{R}^{M}\times\mathbb{R}^{M}
solve

\begin{cases}\frac{3U^{n+1}-4U^{n}+U^{n-1}}{2\tau}=-A_{h}W^{n+1},\\
W^{n+1}=\nabla\left(2F_{K,M}(U^{n})-F_{K,M}(U^{n-1})\right)+\varepsilon^{2}A_{J}U^{n+1},\end{cases}

(4.28)

where
F_{K,M}:\mathbb{R}^{M}\to\mathbb{R}
is defined by
F_{K,M}(v_{1},\cdots,v_{M})=\sum_{i=1}^{M}F_{K}(v_{i})
.
We can assume that
A_{h}
is an
M\times M
symmetric positive definite matrix with inverse
A_{h}^{-1}
. The corresponding discrete energy is defined as

\widetilde{\Phi}_{K}(U,V)={\Phi}_{K}(U)+\frac{\beta}{2}\|V\|^{2}+\frac{1}{4\tau}V^{T}A_{h}^{-1}V,

where
{\Phi}_{K}(U)
is defined by (
3.40
) and is semi-algebraic.

By (
4.27
), when
\beta<\frac{\gamma_{0}+1}{3},\;\tau<\frac{2(\gamma_{0}+1-3\beta)}{C_{\textrm{J}}\varepsilon^{4}}
, we have

\frac{c_{\tau}}{\tau}\left(\|U^{n+1}-U^{n}\|^{2}+(\delta U^{n+1}-\delta U^{n})^{T}A_{h}^{-1}(\delta U^{n+1}-\delta U^{n})\right)+\widetilde{\Phi}(U^{n+1},\delta U^{n+1})\leq\widetilde{\Phi}(U^{n},\delta U^{n}),\quad\forall n\geq 0,

(4.29)

where
c_{\tau}=\min\left(\frac{(\gamma_{0}+1-3\beta)\tau}{2}-\frac{\varepsilon^{4}C_{J}\tau^{2}}{4},\frac{1}{4}\right)>0
. Let
G=\widetilde{\Phi}
. Thus, assumption
H1
holds. Furthermore, from inequality (
4.29
), the sequence
(U^{n})_{n\geq 0}
is bounded in
\mathbb{R}^{M}
. Hence, a subsequence
(U^{n_{k}})_{n_{k}\geq 0}
converges to some
U^{\infty}
in
\mathbb{R}^{M}
.

We use the scheme (
4.28
), identity (
4.13
) and assumption (
3.1
) and obtain

\frac{\|A_{h}^{-1}\|}{2\tau}(2\|\delta U^{n+1}\|+\|\delta U^{n+1}-\delta U^{n}\|)\geq\|\nabla\Phi_{K}(U^{n+1})\|-2\beta\|\delta U^{n+1}\|-\beta\|U^{n+1}-U^{n-1}\|.

(4.30)

By the triangle inequality

\|U^{n+1}-U^{n-1}\|=\|2\delta U^{n+1}+(\delta U^{n}-\delta U^{n+1})\|\leq 2\|\delta U^{n+1}\|+\|\delta U^{n+1}-\delta U^{n}\|,

we can obtain

(\|A_{h}^{-1}\|+4\tau\beta)\left(\|\delta U^{n+1}\|+\|\delta U^{n+1}-\delta U^{n}\|\right)\geq\tau\|\nabla\Phi_{K}(U^{n+1})\|.

(4.31)

We note that
\nabla\widetilde{\Phi}_{K}(U,V)=\left(\nabla\Phi_{K}(U),\left(\frac{A_{h}^{-1}}{2\tau}+\beta I\right)V\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{2M}
, and

\displaystyle\|\nabla\widetilde{\Phi}_{K}(U,V)\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|\nabla\Phi_{K}(U)\|^{2}+\left\|\left(\frac{A_{h}^{-1}}{2\tau}+\beta I\right)V\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla\Phi_{K}(U)\|^{2}+\left(\frac{\|A_{h}^{-1}\|}{2\tau}+\beta\right)^{2}\|V\|^{2}.

(4.32)

Combining this inequality with (
4.29
) and (
4.31
), we derive that the sequence
(U^{n},\delta U^{n})_{n\geq 0}
also satisfies condition
H2
. As demonstrated in the
Step 3
of Theorem
4.12
, we obtain the energy
\widetilde{\Phi}_{K}
satisfies the Łojasiewicz inequality near
(U^{\infty},\textbf{0})
. Applying Theorem
3.2
, we conclude the proof.
∎

Remark 4.1
.

For the two energy-stable second-order fully discrete schemes considered above, because the modified energy functions depend on
\tau
, the stability results for first-order schemes in
Merlet and Pierre 2010
,
Alaa and Pierre 2013
cannot be directly applied to analyze the stability of a local minimizer as
\tau\to 0
. A proof of this stability will be provided in our future works.

5
Concluding remarks

In this paper, we investigate three first-order and two second-order fully discrete numerical schemes for the N-CH equation with analytic nonlinearity. The first-order schemes comprise the backward Euler scheme, the convex splitting scheme and the SSI1 scheme, while the second-order schemes consist of the BDF2 scheme and 2LI scheme. A second-order finite difference method is used for spatial discretization. The unique solvability, discrete mass conservation and (modified) energy stability are proved for our proposed numerical schemes. Furthermore, by means of energy stability and the Łojasiewicz inequality, we provide rigorous proofs of converge to equilibrium as time goes to infinity for the sequences generate by our proposed numerical schemes.

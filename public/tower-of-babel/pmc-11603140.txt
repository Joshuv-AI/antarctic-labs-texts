Fault diagnosis of HVCB via the subtraction average based optimizer algorithm optimized multi channel CNN-SABO-SVM network
Authors: Qingjun Song, Jiuxin Wang, Qinghui Song, Kai Li, Wenchao Hao, Haiyan Jiang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
The mechanical fault diagnosis of HVCB is important to ensure the stability of electric power systems. Aiming at the problem of poor diagnostic performance of deep learning methods under limited samples, this paper proposes an HVCB operating mechanism fault diagnosis model (multi-channel CNN-SABO-SVM, MCCSS) based on multimodal data fusion features and Subtraction-Average-Based Optimizer (SABO). This model extracts and fuses features from the input two-dimensional data using a multi-channel CNN network and then uses the multimodal data fusion features to diagnose HVCB faults. Additionally, the SVM is used instead of the Softmax classifier to classify the fused features of vibration and sound, compensating for the poor diagnostic performance and generalization ability of the CNN network in small sample data scenarios. To further enhance the fault diagnosis performance of the SVM, the SABO is introduced for hyperparameter optimization of the SVM classifier. An HVCB fault test platform was established to train and test the model with limited data. The experimental results show that, compared with the multi-channel CNN-SVM and the CNN model based on unimodal signals, the proposed multi-channel CNN-SABO-SVM model improves the accuracy by 2.66% and 10.66%, respectively, and effectively addresses the challenge of circuit breaker fault diagnosis with limited samples.

Introduction

High-voltage circuit breaker (HVCB) is power equipment with protection and control functions in the power system, and its operational reliability is of great significance for ensuring the stable and safe operation of the power system1–3. To ensure the ability of HVCB to break large currents quickly, the operating mechanism of the HVCB needs to transmit high power and heavy loads in a short period. As the operating life of HVCB increases, the mechanical performance of its operating mechanisms will inevitably deteriorate4. According to statistics and surveys of HVCB faults worldwide by the International Council on Large Electric Systems (CIGRE), mechanical faults are the main cause of most faults, such as tripping failures, mechanism jams, and component damage5–8. HVCB failure can expand the range of power system outages, reduce the reliability of power supply, cause damage to other equipment in the system, and even lead to accidents resulting in casualties. With the gradual improvement of the social requirements for power quality, the traditional periodic maintenance method originating from the Soviet Union can no longer meet modern needs. This method has relatively low maintenance efficiency and may cause over-maintenance and under-maintenance issues due to unreasonable maintenance intervals, leading to equipment damage. Currently, machine learning and deep learning methods to achieve rapid and accurate diagnosis of HVCB faults have become a major research direction and a challenge for improving the safety and stability of power systems.

During the operation of HVCB, the large energy stored in the energy storage device is rapidly released in a short period, causing the components of the operating mechanism to collide with each other and generate intense vibrations throughout the operation process. Machine learning diagnosis methods based on vibration signals or combined with other signals have become mainstream research, including support vector machines (SVM)9–11, decision tree models12, and clustering algorithms13. Although existing machine learning methods can recognize faults, the diagnostic accuracy depends on the effective extraction of signal features. Vibration signals have non-stationary characteristics, and the main feature extraction methods currently used are to decompose the vibration signal into multiple modal components using frequency decomposition methods such as empirical mode decomposition14–16 and wavelet transform17–19, followed by entropy feature methods to obtain modal component features. However, these traditional fault diagnosis methods generally have large computational loads, and different entropy calculation methods have different applicability to various signals. The feature vectors obtained after signal decomposition often outnumber the fault types, and excessive features may contain redundant and irrelevant information, making it difficult for feature extraction methods to accurately capture key features related to fault types.

Compared with traditional machine learning methods, deep learning methods can automatically extract fault features from data, reducing reliance on expert experience and signal processing techniques, and thereby decreasing uncertainties brought by human involvement in feature extraction and fault diagnosis20,21. Meanwhile, the powerful feature extraction capabilities of deep learning networks can effectively represent the mapping relationship between signals and health states22. In recent years, some researchers have applied deep learning to fault diagnosis and achieved certain scientific results23–25. Li et al. proposed an IDS-ESN diagnosis model based on D-S evidence theory and ESN network for mechanical fault diagnosis of high-voltage circuit breakers26. Wang et al. proposed an FSTL diagnosis model based on attention mechanisms and transfer learning methods, which can effectively diagnose high-voltage circuit breaker faults with a small number of data samples27. Yang et al. proposed a new RDSCNN network architecture based on residual learning, multi-label attribute learning, and deep separable convolution theory, capable of diagnosing unknown faults under zero-sample conditions28. Xia et al. expanded the original dataset based on a generative adversarial network (GAN) generating virtual training data to improve the detection performance of acoustic event detection systems with fewer training datasets and imbalanced distribution29. Song et al. proposed a CNN-BiLSTM network architecture based on a Bi-directional long and short-term memory network (BiLSTM) for limited training samples for fault diagnosis of bearings30. Although these methods have addressed the issue of deep learning methods being affected by limited sample data to some extent, there is still room for improvement. In the field of fault diagnosis, multi-fault feature fusion is a common method to enhance diagnostic accuracy. Aisha Khan et al. proposed a multi-channel fusion convolutional neural network, which effectively improves the classification accuracy of abnormal patterns31. Samira Zare et al. proposed a fault diagnosis algorithm based on multi-channel convolutional neural networks to detect the operating status of wind turbines, achieving high diagnostic accuracy32. This multi-channel CNN network can extract features from multiple sources simultaneously and analyze data from multiple dimensions, thereby reducing dependence on a single data source and improving the model’s diagnostic performance and generalization ability.

To address the aforementioned issues, this paper proposes an HVCB fault diagnosis model based on multimodal data fusion features and the Subtractive Averaging-Based Optimizer (SABO). The main contributions of this paper are as follows: A HVCB operating mechanism fault diagnosis method based on deep learning and multimodal data fusion features is explored, which provides a new way to solve the problem of low accuracy of deep learning-based circuit breaker fault diagnosis. A new multi-channel CNN-SABO-SVM model is proposed for the diagnosis of mechanical faults of the HVCB operating mechanism under limited data samples, based on the two-dimensional image data of vibroacoustic signals obtained by continuous wavelet transform and Meier spectral methods. The SVM classifier is used instead of the SoftMax classifier of CNN networks, while the SVM is subjected to hyper-parameter optimization using the subtractive averaging optimizer algorithm to further improve the diagnostic performance and generalization ability of the multi-channel CNN-SABO-SVM model.

Basic methods

Continuous wavelet transform

The image data is essentially a multidimensional matrix, compared to one-dimensional (1D) time-domain or frequency-domain signal data, which contains richer feature information and can be used to represent more complex data distributions33. To optimize the application of CNN in HVCB fault diagnosis, considering the excellent performance of CNN in the field of image recognition, this paper converts the breaker vibration-acoustic signals into time-frequency images. Currently, the Wigner-Ville distribution, short-time Fourier transform, and wavelet transform are widely used to obtain time-frequency images of signals34. The vibration signal generated by the operating mechanism of HVCB is a typical non-stationary signal. In practical applications, the Wigner-Ville distribution may cause intrinsic cross-interference terms. The window frequency resolution of the short-time Fourier transform is fixed. The continuous wavelet transform (CWT) overcomes the shortcomings of the above methods, by decomposing the non-stationary signal into a series of superposition of base wavelets through a specific wavelet basis function, thus achieving a more accurate analysis of non-stationary signals.

Mel spectrum

Mel-Frequency Cepstral Coefficients (MFCC) have been widely used in audio recognition and audio categorization tasks, and both have yielded good results36,37. However, due to its compression of the spectrum, it tends to lose the high-frequency information in the spectrum. The audio signals of HVCB faults are mainly distributed in the middle and high-frequency bands, and if MFCC is used to process them, it will inevitably lead to the loss of certain fault information. MFCC is obtained by the discrete cosine transform of the Mel-Frequency Spectral Coefficients (MFSC), so like MFCC, MFSC is also applicable to the processing of audio signals. At the same time, since MFSC does not compress the spectrum, it is more suitable for processing audio signals generated by HVCB because it can retain the middle and high-frequency puzzles as well as provide finer details of the spectrogram.

By omitting the final Discrete Cosine Transform step in the Mel-frequency cepstral coefficients computation, we can obtain the MFSC features of the audio signal. The data information in the audio signals of HVCB is highly nonlinearly correlated, and the Discrete Cosine Transform is a linear transformation that decorrelates the coefficients of the Mel filter bank, so the Discrete Cosine Transform leads to the loss of part of the data information, which often contains Fault Information about the circuit breaker. In summary, we choose MFSC as the classification feature for the audio signals of HVCB. The method for obtaining MFSC features has been detailed in related studies38,39.

Convolutional neural network

Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) is a specialized deep feedforward artificial neural network designed to process data with a grid-like structure40. Convolutional operations are the foundation of the CNN structure and are utilized instead of matrix multiplications to compute the relationship between the inputs and outputs of the network. The commonly used convolutional operations in CNN are 1D convolution and 2D convolution, among which 2D convolution performs excellently in tasks such as image classification and computer vision applications. Support vector machine

SVM is a supervised learning method for small samples, and nonlinear and high dimensional data42. Its basic model is defined as a linear classifier with maximum intervals on the feature space, which can be used to solve regression analysis and binary classification problems. The basic principle is that there exists a sample space composed of N multi-dimensional feature samples, and the sample space is used as the input of the model. By mapping the model input through some nonlinear transformation, a high-dimensional feature space is obtained, which transforms the hypersurface model formed by the multidimensional irregular sample space into a regular hyperplane model after mapping and realizes linear classification.

Fault diagnosis based on multi-channel CNN-SABO-SVM

Multi-channel CNN-SVM

To further improve the diagnostic performance of circuit breaker fault diagnosis using CNN, this paper constructs a multi-channel CNN-SVM (MCCS) model that simultaneously uses the time-frequency spectrogram of vibration signals and the Mel spectrogram of audio signals for diagnosis. In the MCCS model, the multi-channel CNN (MCC) network is responsible for extracting the spatial features of the time-frequency spectrograms and Mel spectrograms, and then fusing the two features. Then, the excellent feature classification performance of SVM is utilized to classify the fused features and make up for the shortcomings of the MCC model. Then, the excellent feature classification performance of SVM is utilized to classify the fused features, and hence, it compensates for the deficiencies of the MCC model. The structure of the MCCS model is shown in Fig. 2. This model mainly consists of a vibroacoustic signal image data input layer, CNN convolution layer, pooling layer, discard layer, fusion layer, output layer, and SVM classifier.

SABO-SVM

Considering the classification performance of SVM is affected by its internal parameters, and if the parameters are not set reasonably, it will often lead to the problem of overfitting in the iteration of SVM. In this paper, we set up an early-stop mechanism in the optimization process of SVM parameters and applied regularization techniques to prevent overfitting problems from occurring during model training44.

This paper proposes a SABO-SVM model optimized using the SABO algorithm, which can adaptively determine hyperparameters of the SVM such as the penalty function \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:C$$\end{document} and the internal parameter \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:g$$\end{document} of the radial basis function. Experiment and dataset

Failure experiment bench

In this paper, the experimental environment is configured as follows: the processor is AMD Ryzen 9-5900HX@3.30 GHz with 32 GB of RAM, the graphics card is NVIDIA GeForce RTX3080 with 16 GB of RAM, and the development tool used is MATLAB R2022b (9.13).

Under normal circumstances, the probability of HVCB failing during its entire life cycle is extremely low, making it difficult to obtain a sufficiently rich and ample variety of fault signals from HVCB. Therefore, it is necessary to construct an HVCB experimental bench in the laboratory to acquire several common fault data types. The bench consists of a 72.5 kV HVCB equipped with a CT126-type spring operating mechanism, an accelerometer and audio sensor, a signal acquisition device, and data analysis software. The signals of the collected conditions are organized, each condition contains 60 sets of data samples, among which each data sample contains 4000 data points. In each experiment, 80% of the data samples from each circuit breaker condition were randomly selected to form the training set, while the remaining data samples constituted the test set. Therefore, the dataset in this study comprises a total of 300 data samples, including 240 training samples and 60 test samples.

For the collected vibration and audio data of the HVCB, the Complex-Morlet wavelet is used as the mother wavelet for the CWT to perform time-frequency analysis on the original 1D vibration signals. Simultaneously, the audio signals were pre-emphasized with a pre-emphasis coefficient of 0.97 before performing a time-frequency analysis. This process converts the 1D time-domain signals into 2D image data that contains both time-domain and frequency-domain information.

Fault diagnosis of HVCB based on multi-channel CNN-SABO-SVM

To validate the effectiveness of the MCCSS diagnostic model, different comparison experiments were set up for comparative analysis. Among them, the first set of experiments compares different feature extraction methods, the second set of experiments compares different several multichannel diagnostic models, the third set of experiments compares single-channel and multichannel diagnostic models, and the last set of experiments compares the MCCSS diagnostic model with several traditional diagnostic methods and more novel diagnostic methods. The CNN model in each comparison model has the same structural parameters as the CNN in the MCCSS model proposed in this paper. In addition, to increase the persuasiveness of the experimental results, the average of the results of the 5-fold crossover experiments is taken for each diagnostic model in this paper to measure the model performance.

Conclusion

We propose a new MCCSS method in this paper, which consists of a feature extraction and feature fusion network containing multiple CNNs, and an improved SVM classifier using the SABO algorithm. The MCCSS method is demonstrated to be effective in realizing fault diagnosis of HVCBs by comparing it with existing machine learning methods and deep learning methods. Secondly, an effective multimodal signal fusion method is explored. Two CNNs with similar structures are used to extract and fuse the features of the input 2D data, and then the SVM optimized by the SABO algorithm is used to classify the fused features, which helps to solve the problems of low efficiency and poor resistance to noise influence of traditional diagnostic methods, and further stabilizes the diagnostic accuracy of the MCCSS model. The experimental comparison results show that the MCCSS model has obvious advantages, whether compared with SVM and ELM, etc., or compared with ESN and MCC-LSTM, etc. However, the MCCSS model still suffers from the problem of decreasing diagnostic accuracy when distinguishing confusing fault signals. In future work, we will conduct research on migration learning, data enhancement techniques, and model noise immunity to further improve the usefulness of MCCSS for different work scenarios and the diagnostic ability for complex faults.

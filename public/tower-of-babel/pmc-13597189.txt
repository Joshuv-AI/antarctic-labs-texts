Papulosquamous secondary syphilis with palmoplantar sparing: A case illustrated
Authors: Varun H., Adarshlata Singh, Bhushan Madke, Naman Kacchawa, Aryan Pal
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Secondary syphilis is a clinical mimic with considerable morphological variability. We describe a man in his early 50s with a generalised papulosquamous eruption, constitutional symptoms and generalised lymphadenopathy, notably without palmoplantar involvement. Syphilis serology was strongly reactive, and histopathology demonstrated a plasma cell-rich superficial and deep perivascular and peri-adnexal infiltrate. This case illustrates the importance of considering secondary syphilis in generalised papulosquamous eruptions despite the absence of classical palmoplantar lesions.

Description

A man in his early 50 s presented with a 3-week history of an asymptomatic generalised eruption associated with fever, malaise and generalised lymphadenopathy. The eruption began over both upper limbs and spread within 7–10 days to the face, trunk and lower limbs. He reported multiple unprotected sexual exposures and recalled a painless penile ulcer approximately 6 months prior that had healed spontaneously.

Examination revealed multiple well-defined hypopigmented-to-pink papules and coalescing minimally scaly plaques over the face, trunk and extremities, with subtle violaceous discolouration of some lesions (Fig. 1). The palms and soles were spared. Initial differential diagnoses included pityriasis lichenoides, psoriasis and disseminated superficial actinic porokeratosis. Secondary syphilis may display marked morphological variability and can mimic non-infectious papulosquamous dermatoses [1]. Palmoplantar involvement, although characteristic, is not obligatory. Plasma cell-rich perivascular and peri-adnexal inflammation provides an important histopathological clue when interpreted alongside serology [2]. The patient received benzathine penicillin G 2.4 million units intramuscularly as a single dose [3,4]. Follow-up was unavailable. This case underscores that secondary syphilis should remain in the differential diagnosis of a generalised papulosquamous eruption even when the palms and soles are spared.

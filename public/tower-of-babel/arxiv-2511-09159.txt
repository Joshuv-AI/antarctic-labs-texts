Rademacher’s Theorem for Calderon-Zygmund-type Spaces
T. Lamby

Abstract
Rademacher’s Theorem is a classical result stating that a Lipschitz function on ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} possesses a total differential almost everywhere. This implies that if f f is a function defined on ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} belonging to the Calderón–Zygmund space T 1 ∞ ​ ( x ) T^{\infty}_{1}(x) for every x ∈ ℝ d x\in\mathbb{R}^{d} , then f ∈ t 1 ∞ ​ ( x ) f\in t^{\infty}_{1}(x) for almost every x ∈ ℝ d x\in\mathbb{R}^{d} . The main purpose of this paper is to extend this property to a broader functional framework. More precisely, we replace the Lipschitz condition by the assumption that f f belongs to a general weighted Calderón–Zygmund space T ϕ p ​ ( x ) T^{p}_{\phi}(x) for x ∈ E x\in E , where E E is a measurable subset of ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} and ϕ \phi is a weight with fractional indices. We then show that, under suitable assumptions, the function belongs to t ϕ p ​ ( x ) t^{p}_{\phi}(x) for almost every x ∈ E x\in E . The result can be obtained either by imposing additional hypotheses or by working within a suitably adapted t ϕ p t^{p}_{\phi} space. Whenever relevant, we also provide counterexamples illustrating the sharpness and the limitations of the statement.

The

r^{-d/p}\|f-P\|_{L^{p}(B(x_{0},r))}\leq C\,r^{u}\quad\forall r>0.

(1)

If, in addition,

r^{-d/p}\|f-P\|_{L^{p}(B(x_{0},r))}=o(r^{u})\text{ when }r\rightarrow 0^{+},

(2)

then
f\in t^{p}_{u}(x_{0})
. They allow to determine the pointwise regularity of functions that are not locally bounded (see for example
[
6
,
16
,
9
]
). Indeed, since the spaces

are decreasing with

h_{p}^{f}(x_{0})=\sup\{u\geq-d/p:f\in T^{p}_{u}(x_{0})\}.

If

is the classical pointwise Hölder space, denoted by
\Lambda^{u}(x_{0})
.

Rademacher’s Theorem is a classical result which states that a Lipschitz function on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
has a total differential almost everywhere. This means that if
f
is a function defined on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
which belongs to the space
T^{\infty}_{1}(x)
for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, then
f\in t^{p}_{1}(x)
for almost every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, where the polynomial involved in the definition of the spaces
t^{p}_{1}(x)
has degree less than or equal to 1.

A. Calderón generalized the differentiability of Sobolev functions by showing that if
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is bounded and open, then every function in
W^{p}_{1}(\Omega)
is differentiable almost everywhere for
p>d

[
5
]
. This result follows naturally from the Lebesgue differentiation theorem and the Sobolev embedding theorem, and Rademacher’s theorem appears as a special case, since every Lipschitz function on
\Omega
lies in
W^{\infty}_{1}(\Omega)
. Rademacher’s Theorem also admits a broader formulation for Lipschitz mappings from Euclidean spaces into arbitrary metric spaces, where differentiability is defined in terms of metric differentials rather than classical derivatives
[
4
]
.

The original idea here was to replace the Lipschitz condition in Rademacher’s Theorem by the assumption that the function belongs to

for

is a measurable subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and to show that the function then belongs to
t^{p}_{\phi}(x)
for almost all
x\in E

(resp.

(resp.

) by replacing

appearing in (
1
) (resp. (
2
)) by a function parameter (see section

version of Rademacher’s Theorem, and this has been established for the spaces

, with

[
17
]
.

Here,
\mathcal{L}

2
Calderón-Zygmund spaces with Function parameter

A crucial aspect in order to refine regularity spaces is the notion of Boyd functions
[
1
,
2
,
7
,
8
,
14
,
10
,
11
]
, which serve as a replacement for the traditional functions
t\mapsto t^{\theta}
typically used in these settings: a function
\phi:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
is a Boyd function if it is continuous,
\phi(1)=1
and

\overline{\phi}(t):=\sup_{s>0}\frac{\phi(st)}{\phi(s)}<\infty,\quad\forall t\in(0,\infty).

The lower and upper Boyd indices of a Boyd function
\phi
are then defined by

\underline{b}(\phi):=\sup_{t<1}\frac{\log\bar{\phi}(t)}{\log t}=\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{\log\bar{\phi}(t)}{\log t}

and

\overline{b}(\phi):=\inf_{t>1}\frac{\log\bar{\phi}(t)}{\log t}=\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\log\bar{\phi}(t)}{\log t},

respectively.

Definition 1
.

Let
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
p\in[1,\infty]
and
\phi\in\mathcal{B}
such that
\underline{b}(\phi)>-\frac{d}{p}
. A function
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
belongs to the space
T^{p}_{\phi}(x_{0})
if there exists a polynomial
P
with degree strictly smaller than
\underline{b}(\phi)
and a constant
C>0
such that

r^{-d/p}\|f-P\|_{L^{p}(B(x_{0},r))}\leq C\,\phi(r)\quad\forall r>0.

(3)

The condition
\underline{b}(\phi)>-\frac{d}{p}
is here to ensure that the spaces are not degenerate. One can easily check that the polynomial appearing in (
3
) is unique. We set

\Lambda^{\phi}(x_{0})=T^{\infty}_{\phi}(x_{0}).

Let
f\in T^{p}_{\phi}(x_{0})
and
P
be the polynomial relating to
f
, then
P
can be written as

P(x)=\sum_{|\alpha|<\underline{b}(\phi)}\frac{D^{\alpha}P(x_{0})}{|\alpha|!}(x-x_{0})^{\alpha}.

We set

|f|_{T^{p}_{\phi}(x_{0})}=\sup_{r>0}\phi(r)^{-1}r^{-d/p}\|f-P\|_{L^{p}(B(x_{0},r))},

and

\|f\|_{T^{p}_{\phi}(x_{0})}=\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\sum_{|\alpha|<\underline{b}(\phi)}\frac{|D^{\alpha}P(x_{0})|}{|\alpha|!}+|f|_{T^{p}_{\phi}(x_{0})}.

Then it is straightforward that
(T^{p}_{\phi}(x_{0}),\|\cdot\|_{T^{p}_{\phi}(x_{0})})
is a Banach space. Let
t^{p}_{\phi,n}(x_{0})
, constitued of functions
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that there exists a polynomial
P
of degree at most
n
such that

r^{-d/p}\|f-P\|_{L^{p}(B(x_{0},r))}=o(\phi(r))\text{\emph{ when }}r\rightarrow 0^{+}.

If
n<\underline{b}(\phi)
, it is easy to prove that
t^{p}_{\phi,n}(x_{0})
is a closed subspace of
T^{p}_{\phi}(x_{0})
with respect to the topology induced by the norm
\|\cdot\|_{T^{p}_{\phi}(x_{0})}
(see
[
12
]
).

Remark 1
.

If
\phi(t)=t^{u}
, with
u\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, then
t^{p}_{\phi,u}(x_{0})
is the usual space
t^{p}_{u}(x_{0})
of
[
17
]
.
Remark that, in this case, the space
T^{p}_{\phi}(x_{0})
involves a polynomial of degree at most
u-1
whereas the space
t^{p}_{u}(x_{0})
involves a polynomial of degree at most
u
, so that
t^{p}_{u}(x_{0})
is not a true subspace of
T^{p}_{\phi}(x_{0})
.

These spaces are used to capture finer notions of regularity. For example (see
[
15
]
), if
B
is a Brownian motion on a probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
, then almost surely and for almost every
t_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
, there exists a constant
C>0
such that

|B(t)-B(t_{0})|\leq C\big(|t-t_{0}|\log\log 1/|t-t_{0}|\big)^{1/2},

(4)

whereas for every
t_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
, the Hölder exponent of
B
at
t_{0}
equals
h(t_{0})=\frac{1}{2}
. Therefore, if
\phi\in\mathcal{B}
is such that
\phi(t)\sim(t\log\log 1/|t|)^{1/2}
for all
t>0
, then
T^{\infty}_{\phi}
is the adapted functional space that take into account the iterated logarithm in the pointwise regularity of
B
, a phenomenon that classical Hölder spaces fail to capture.
In
[
12
]
, they get pointwise estimates for solutions of elliptic partial differential equations in the context of
T^{p}_{\phi}
spaces. They also get a more general version of Whitney’s extension Theorem.

Theorem 1
.

(Generalization of Whitney’s Extension Theorem)

Let
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a closed set,
U=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\mid d(x,E)<1\}
,
p\in[1,\infty]
,
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
\phi\in\mathcal{B}
such that
n<\underline{b}(\phi)
.

If

verifies
\|f\|_{T^{p}_{\phi}(x_{0})}\leq M
for

and for all
x_{0}\in E
, then there exists

such that
F=f
almost everywhere on
E
.Moreover, if
m\in\mathbb{N}
is such that
n<\underline{b}(\phi)\leq\overline{b}(\phi)<m
, then there exists a constant
C>0
such that for all
x\in U
and

satisfying

, we have

|\Delta^{\,m-n}_{h}D^{\alpha}F(x)|\leq C\,\phi(|h|)\,|h|^{-n}

for every multi-index
\alpha
with

.

We generalize here to the spaces
T^{p}_{\phi}(x_{0})
the approach carried out for spaces

and

,

, in
[

This generalization proceeds in two directions: the indices are allowed to be non-integer, and one may also consider more refined regularity spaces, with the flexibility that a weight can exhibit more nuanced behavior than a pure power function; for instance, involving iterated logarithmic terms.

Lemma 1
.

Let

Lemma 2
.

Let
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a measurable set,
p\in[1,\infty]
, and
\phi\in\mathcal{B}
such that
\underline{b}(\phi)>-d/p

, then the function

\xi_{f}:E\rightarrow[0,\infty):x\mapsto\|f\|_{T^{p}_{\phi}(x)}

Proof.

Let
x\in E
. We denote by
P_{x}
the polynomial associated with
f
at
x
, of degree strictly less than
\underline{b}(\phi)
.
It therefore suffices to show that for every multi-index
\alpha
with
|\alpha|<\underline{b}(\phi)
,

x\in E\mapsto D^{\alpha}P_{x}(x)

is measurable.
If
\underline{b}(\phi)\leq 0
, the claim is obvious since

is the zero polynomial for every

. We may thus assume that
\underline{b}(\phi)>0

Let
\varphi
be the function from Lemma
1
for
n=\lfloor\underline{b}(\phi)\rfloor
.

We define, for every

R_{x}=f-P_{x}.

For
\varepsilon>0

\displaystyle D^{\alpha}\left(\varphi_{\varepsilon}\ast f(x)\right)

\displaystyle=D^{\alpha}\left(\varphi_{\varepsilon}\ast P_{x}(x)\right)+D^{\alpha}\left(\varphi_{\varepsilon}\ast R_{x}(x)\right)

\displaystyle=D^{\alpha}P_{x}(x)+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varepsilon^{-d-|\alpha|}(D^{\alpha}\varphi)\left(\frac{y}{\varepsilon}\right)R_{x}(x-y)\,dy.

But, since
|\alpha|<\underline{b}(\phi)

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varepsilon^{-d-|\alpha|}\,(D^{\alpha}\varphi)\left(\frac{y}{\varepsilon}\right)\,R_{x}(x-y)\,dy\right|

\displaystyle\leq C_{\varphi}\,\varepsilon^{-d-|\alpha|}\,\int_{B(0,\varepsilon)}|R_{x}(x-y)|\,dy

\displaystyle=C_{\varphi}\,\varepsilon^{-|\alpha|}\,\varepsilon^{-d}\,\|f-P_{x}\|_{L^{1}(B(x,\varepsilon))}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\varphi}\,\varepsilon^{-|\alpha|}\,C_{d}^{1-1/p}\,\varepsilon^{-d/p}\,\|f-P_{x}\|_{L^{p}(B(x,\varepsilon))}

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime}\,\varepsilon^{-|\alpha|}\,\phi(\varepsilon)

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime\prime}\,\varepsilon^{-|\alpha|}\,\varepsilon^{\underline{b}(\phi)-((\underline{b}(\phi)-|\alpha|)/2)}

\displaystyle=C^{\prime\prime}\varepsilon^{(\underline{b}(\phi)-|\alpha|)/2)}\underset{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}{\longrightarrow}0.

Therefore, for all

Lemma 3
.

Let
p\in[1,\infty)
,
\phi\in\mathcal{B}
such that
\underline{b}(\phi)>0
and
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

r^{-d/p}\|f\|_{L^{p}(B(x,r))}=o(\phi(r))\text{\emph{ when }}r\rightarrow 0^{+}.

Proof.

We may suppose that
E
is bounded and that
f
is with compact support. Since
\underline{b}(\phi)>0
, there exists a constant

such that

\overline{\phi}(r)\leq C_{\star}\,r^{\underline{b}(\phi)-\underline{b}(\phi)/2}\leq C_{\star}\quad\forall r\in(0,1].

Let

, we need to prove that for almost all

r^{-d/p}\|f\|_{L^{p}(B(x,r))}<\varepsilon\,\phi(r)

(5)

for sufficiently small

.

Let
A
be a closed subset of

\mathcal{L}\,(E\backslash A)<\varepsilon.

It is the sufficient to prove the result for almost all

. For
x\in A

\lim_{r\rightarrow 0^{+}}r^{-d}\,\|f\|_{L^{1}(B(x,r))}\leq C_{d}^{1-1/p}\lim_{r\rightarrow 0^{+}}r^{-d/p}\,\|f\|_{L^{p}(B(x,r))}\leq C^{\prime}\,\lim_{r\rightarrow 0^{+}}\phi(r)=0.

Therefore, since almost all points are Lebesgue points,

almost everywhere on
A
.

We define the open set

U=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:d(x,A)<1\}

and for all

, we set

Using classical covering Theorems (see for example Theorem 1.3.1 of

]
), there exists a countable set

such that the family

\left\{\overline{B(s,h(s))}:s\in S\right\}

is disjointed and

\bigcup_{s\in S}\overline{B(s,5h(s))}\supseteq U\backslash A.

We get

\displaystyle\int_{A}\int_{U}\frac{|f(y)|}{|x-y|^{d}\,\phi(|x-y|)}dy\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{A}\int_{U\backslash A}\frac{|f(y)|}{|x-y|^{d}\,\phi(|x-y|)}dy\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{A}\sum_{s\in S}\int_{B(s,5h(s))}\frac{|f(y)|}{|x-y|^{d}\,\phi(|x-y|)}dy\,dx

\displaystyle=\sum_{s\in S}\int_{B(s,5h(s))}|f(y)|\int_{A}\frac{dx}{|x-y|^{d}\,\phi(|x-y|)}dy.

Since
A
is closed, for all
s\in S
, there exists

such that

|s-x_{s}|=d(s,A)=10h(s),

and

B(s,5h(s))\subseteq B(x_{s},|s-x_{s}|+5h(s))=B(x_{s},15h(s)).

Consequently,

\displaystyle(5h(s))^{-d}\,\|f\|_{L^{1}(B(s,5h(s)))}

\displaystyle\leq C_{d}^{1-1/p}\,(5h(s))^{-d/p}\,\|f\|_{L^{p}(B(s,5h(s)))}

\displaystyle\leq C_{d}^{1-1/p}\,(5h(s))^{-d/p}\,\|f\|_{L^{p}(B(x_{s},15h(s)))}

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime}\phi(15h(s))

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime\prime}\phi(5h(s)).

Remark that if
x\in A
and
y\in B(s,5h(s))
, then

|x-y|\geq d(s,A)-5h(s)=5h(s).

Thus, for
y\in B(s,5h(s))
, we get

\displaystyle\int_{A}\frac{dx}{|x-y|^{d}\,\phi(|x-y|)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\int_{|5h(s)|}^{+\infty}\frac{r^{-1}}{\phi(r)}dr

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\,C_{\star}\,(5h(s))^{\underline{b}(\phi)/2}\int_{|5h(s)|}^{+\infty}\frac{r^{-1}}{r^{\underline{b}(\phi)/2}\,\phi(5h(s))}\,dr

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}\,\phi(5h(s))^{-1}.

We obtain

\displaystyle\int_{A}\int_{B(s,5h(s))}\frac{|f(y)|}{|x-y|^{d}\,\phi(|x-y|)}dy\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}\,(\phi(5h(s))^{-1}\int_{B(s,5h(s))}|f(y)|\,dy

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}\,(\phi(5h(s))^{-1}\,(5(h(s))^{d}\,C^{\prime\prime}\,\phi(5h(s))

\displaystyle\leq C_{3}\,h(s)^{d}.

Since family
\left\{\overline{B(s,h(s))}:s\in S\right\}
is disjointed,

\int_{A}\int_{U}\frac{|f(y)|}{|x-y|^{d}\,\phi(|x-y|)}dy\,dx\leq C_{3}\sum_{s\in S}h(s)^{d}\leq C_{3}\sum_{s\in S}h(s)<\infty,

so that

for almost all
x\in A
. Since

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash U}\frac{|f(y)|}{|x-y|^{d}\,\phi(|x-y|)}dy<\infty

for all

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{|f(y)|}{|x-y|^{d}\,\phi(|x-y|)}dy<\infty

for almost all
x\in A
.

r^{-d}\,\|f\|_{L^{1}(B(x,r))}\leq C_{d}^{1-1/p}\,r^{-d/p}\,\|f\|_{L^{p}(B(x,r))}\leq C\phi(r).

Therefore, if

x\in E
.

\displaystyle=\phi(r)\left(\int_{B(x,r)}\frac{|f(y)|^{p}}{r^{d}\,\phi^{p}(r)}\,dy\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq\phi(r)\left(\int_{B(x,r)}\frac{|f(y)|^{p}}{r^{d}\,\phi^{p}(|x-y|)}\,dy\,C_{\star}^{p}\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle<\varepsilon\,\phi(r).

E_{1/m}
and such that for all
|\beta|<n
and for almost all
x\in E_{1/m}
,

D^{\beta}F(x)=D^{\beta}P_{x}(x).

By Theorem
3
,
F\in W^{p}_{n,\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Using Theorem
4
with
m_{1}=m_{2}=n
, it follows that for almost every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, there exists a polynomial
Q_{x}
of degree at most
n
such that

\,r^{-d/p}\,\|F-Q_{x}\|_{L^{p}(B(x,r))}=o(r^{n})\quad\text{when }r\rightarrow 0^{+}.

Moreover, for almost every
x\in E_{1/m}
,

r^{-d/p}\,\|f-F\|_{L^{p}(B(x,r))}\leq C\,r^{n}.

Using Lemma
3
, we get

r^{-d/p}\|f-F\|_{L^{p}(B(x,r))}=o(r^{n})\text{ when }r\rightarrow 0^{+},

Thus, for almost all
x\in E_{1/m}
,

\displaystyle r^{-d/p}\,\|f-Q_{x}\|_{L^{p}(B(x,r))}

\displaystyle\leq r^{-d/p}\,\|f-F\|_{L^{p}(B(x,r))}+r^{-d/p}\,\|F-Q_{x}\|_{L^{p}(B(x,r))}

\displaystyle=o(\phi(r))+o(r^{n+1})

\displaystyle=o(\phi(r))\text{ when }r\rightarrow 0^{+}.

We have thus proved the result for sets
E_{1/m}
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Since a countable union of negligible sets is negligible, we deduce the conclusion for
E
.

\bullet
If
E
is unbounded, it suffices to apply the previous case to the sets

E^{(j)}=E\cap B(0,j),\quad j\in\mathbb{N},

and to conclude again using the fact that a countable union of negligible sets is negligible.
∎

4
Rademacher’s Theorem for Calderon-Zygmund-type Spaces

We suppose that
\phi\in\mathcal{B}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
are such that
n<\underline{b}(\phi)\leq\overline{b}(\phi)<n+1
. For those fractional indices, we can’t easily adapt the proof because of Theorem
4
. The idea to get results is to reduce to the case of integer indices. Before the Theorem, let us remark that if we have some assumption on
f
at a neighborhood of
x_{0}
, then
f\in t^{p}_{\phi,n+1}(x_{0})
.

Remark 2
.

If
f\in C^{n+1}(V)
, where
V
is an open neighborhood of
x_{0}
, then

f\in t^{p}_{\phi,n}(x_{0})\subseteq t^{p}_{\phi,n+1}(x_{0}).

Indeed, there exists
R>0
such that
\overline{B(x_{0},R)}\subseteq V
. If
P
denotes the Taylor polynomial of
f
of order
n
at
x_{0}
, then since
f
is of class
C^{n+1}
on
V
, there exists a constant
C>0
such that

|f(x)-P(x)|\leq C|x-x_{0}|^{n+1}\quad\forall x\in B(x_{0},R).

Thus, for every
r\in(0,R]
, we have

r^{-d/p}\|f-P\|_{L^{p}(B(x_{0},r))}\leq C^{\prime\prime}r^{n+1}.

By assumption, there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that
\overline{b}(\phi)+\varepsilon<n+1
.
It follows that

\phi(r)^{-1}r^{-d/p}\|f\|_{L^{p}(B(x_{0},r))}\leq\phi(r)^{-1}C^{\prime\prime}r^{n+1}\leq\frac{C^{\prime\prime}}{C_{1}}r^{\,n+1-(\overline{b}(\phi)+\varepsilon)}\underset{r\to 0^{+}}{\longrightarrow}0,

and hence
f\in t^{p}_{\phi,n}(x_{0})
.

Remark that,

t^{p}_{\overline{b}(\phi)}(x_{0})\subseteq t^{p}_{\phi,n}(x_{0})\subseteq t^{p}_{\phi,n+1}(x_{0})\subseteq t^{p}_{\underline{b}(\phi)}(x_{0}).

Since the second inclusion is often strict, the best general Rademacher’s Theorem we can get is the following.

Theorem 6
.

(Rademacher’s Theorem for Calderon-Zygmund-type Spaces)

Let
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
measurable,
p\in(1,\infty)
,
\phi\in\mathcal{B}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that
n<\underline{b}(\phi)\leq\overline{b}(\phi)<n+1
. If
f\in T^{p}_{\phi}(x)
for all
x\in E
, then
f\in t^{p}_{\phi,n+1}(x)
for almost all
x\in E
.

Proof.

\bullet
Let us first assume that
E
is bounded. By lemma
2
and Lusin’s Theorem, there exist compacts
E_{1/m}\subseteq E
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
, such that

\mathcal{L}\left(E\backslash E_{1/m}\right)\leq\frac{1}{m}

and such that

\xi_{f}:E_{1/m}\rightarrow[0,\infty):x\mapsto\|f\|_{T^{p}_{\phi}(x)}

is continuous. Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a constant
M=M(m)>0
such that

\|f\|_{T^{p}_{\phi}(x)}\leq M\quad\forall x\in E_{1/m}.

Let
x\in E_{1/m}
, we denote by
P_{x}
the polynomial of degree strictly less than or equal to
n
associated with
f
. Thanks to Whitney’s extension Theorem
1
, there exists an open set
U
containing
E_{1/m}
and a function
F\in C^{n}(U)
such that
F=f
almost everywhere
E_{1/m}
and such that for all
|\beta|\leq n
and for almost all
x\in E_{1/m}
,

D^{\beta}F(x)=D^{\beta}P_{x}(x).

By Theorem
3
,
F\in W^{p}_{n+1,\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Using Theorem
4
with
m_{1}=m_{2}=n+1
, it follows that for almost every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, there exists a polynomial
Q_{x}
of degree at most
n
such that

\,r^{-d/p}\,\|F-Q_{x}\|_{L^{p}(B(x,r))}=o(r^{n+1})\quad\text{when }r\rightarrow 0^{+}.

Moreover, for almost every
x\in E_{1/m}
,

r^{-d/p}\,\|f-F\|_{L^{p}(B(x,r))}\leq C\,\phi(r).

Using Lemma
3
, we get

r^{-d/p}\|f-F\|_{L^{p}(B(x,r))}=o(\phi(r))\text{ when }r\rightarrow 0^{+},

Thus, for almost all
x\in E_{1/m}
,

\displaystyle r^{-d/p}\,\|f-Q_{x}\|_{L^{p}(B(x,r))}

\displaystyle\leq r^{-d/p}\,\|f-F\|_{L^{p}(B(x,r))}+r^{-d/p}\,\|F-Q_{x}\|_{L^{p}(B(x,r))}

\displaystyle=o(\phi(r))+o(r^{n+1})

\displaystyle=o(\phi(r))\text{ when }r\rightarrow 0^{+}.

We have thus proved the result for sets
E_{1/m}
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Since a countable union of negligible sets is negligible, we deduce the conclusion for
E
.

\bullet
If
E
is unbounded, it suffices to apply the previous case to the sets

E^{(j)}=E\cap B(0,j),\quad j\in\mathbb{N},

and to conclude again using the fact that a countable union of negligible sets is negligible.
∎

Example 1
.

Suppose that
\overline{b}(\phi)<h_{p}^{f}(x)
for all
x\in E
, then the statement

f\in T^{p}_{\phi}(x)\quad\forall x\in E\implies f\in t^{p}_{\phi,n}(x)\quad\text{for almost all }x\in E

is always true since, in this case,
f\in t^{p}_{\phi,n}(x)
for all
x\in E
.

Example 2
.

The following observation with Brownian motion led us to conclude that Rademacher’s theorem does not hold for fractional indices in the space
t^{p}_{\phi,0}
(as opposed to
t^{p}_{\phi,1}
). Indeed, using (
4
), it is clear that almost surely and for almost every
t_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
,
B\in T^{p}_{\phi}(t_{0})
, where
\phi(t)\sim(t\log\log 1/|t|)^{1/2}
for all
t>0
but
almost surely and for almost every
t_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
,

\limsup_{r\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{r^{-1/p}(\int_{B(t_{0},r)}|B(t)-B(t_{0})|^{p}dt)^{1/p}}{\sqrt{r\log\log 1/r}}=\sqrt{2}.

5
Conclusion

Let
\phi\in\mathcal{B}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
are such that
n<\underline{b}(\phi)\leq\overline{b}(\phi)<n+1
. On the one hand, we have the inclusions

t^{p}_{\phi,n}(x_{0})\subseteq T^{p}_{\phi}(x_{0})\quad\text{and}\quad t^{p}_{\phi,n}(x_{0})\subseteq t^{p}_{\phi,n+1}(x_{0}),

but in general we do not have the inclusion

t^{p}_{\phi,n+1}(x_{0})\subseteq T^{p}_{\phi}(x_{0}).

On the other hand, our Rademacher’s Theorem
6
states that

f\in T^{p}_{\phi}(x)\,\,\,\,\,\,\forall x\in E\quad\implies\quad f\in t^{p}_{\phi,n+1}(x)\quad\text{for almost all}\ x\in E.

Moreover, Example
1
shows that this latter result is only of pointwise interest when

\overline{b}(\phi)\geq h_{p}^{f}(x)
, since otherwise one already has

f\in t^{p}_{\phi,n}(x)
. Example
2
further illustrates the limitations of our result, and shows that in general one cannot expect
f
to belong almost everywhere to
t^{p}_{\phi,n}(x)
.

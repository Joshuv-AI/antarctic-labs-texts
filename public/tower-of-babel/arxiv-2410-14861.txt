A Struwe-type Decomposition Result for Weighted Critical p p -Laplace Equations Thanks: This work constitutes part of the author’s PhD thesis at McGill University, which was completed with the support of the Canada Graduate Scholarships – Doctoral (CGS D) administered by the Natural Sciences and Engineering Research Council of Canada (NSERC)
Edward Chernysh

Abstract
. We establish Struwe-type decompositions of Palais-Smale sequences for a class of critical p p -Laplace equations of the Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg type in a bounded domain Ω ⊂ ℝ n \Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n} , n ≥ 2 n\geq 2 , containing the origin. In doing so, we highlight important differences introduced by the weights and require new rescaling laws to account for this new framework.



1.
Introduction

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded domain containing the origin and fix a dimension
n\geq 2
. For any
1<p<n
, we let
q
be the critical Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg (henceforth abbreviated CKN) exponent given by the rule

(1.1)

q:=\frac{np}{n-p(1+a-b)},

whilst subject to the constraints

(1.2)

a<\frac{n-p}{p}\quad\text{and}\quad a\leq b<a+1.

In particular, condition (
1.2
) implies that
\max(ap,qb)<n
and
p<q\leq p^{\ast}
where

p^{\ast}:=\frac{np}{n-p}

is the critical Sobolev constant. Therefore, the weight functions
{x}\mapsto\left|x\right|^{-ap}
and
x\mapsto\left|x\right|^{-bq}
both belong to
L^{1}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
Let us also define a homogeneity exponent:

(1.3)

\gamma:=\frac{n-bq}{q}=\frac{n}{q}-b=\frac{n-p(1+a-b)}{p}-b=\frac{n-p(1+a)}{p}.

In this article, we investigate the loss of compactness for Palais-Smale sequences (oft abbreviated
(PS)
-sequences) associated to the critical problem

(A)

\begin{cases}-\operatorname{div}\left(\left|x\right|^{-ap}\left|\nabla u\right|^{p-2}\nabla u\right)=\left|x\right|^{-bq}\left|u\right|^{q-2}u&\text{in }\Omega\\
u\in\mathcal{D}_{a}^{1,p}\left(\Omega,0\right)\end{cases}

where
\mathcal{D}_{a}^{1,p}(\Omega,0)
denotes a homogeneous Sobolev space, with singularity at the origin, obtained via completion of
C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
with respect to a weighted norm. In fact, given an open subset
U
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and singularity
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we define

\mathcal{D}_{a}^{1,p}(U,x_{0})=\mathcal{D}^{1,p}(U,\left|x-x_{0}\right|^{-ap})

(simply written
\mathcal{D}^{1,p}(U)
if
a=0
) as the completion of
C_{c}^{\infty}(U)
under the norm

(1.4)

\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\mathcal{D}_{a}^{1,p}(U,x_{0})}:=\left(\int_{U}\left|\nabla(\cdot)\right|^{p}\left|x-x_{0}\right|^{-ap}\mathrm{d}{x}\right)^{1/p}.

Problem (
A
) is the Euler-Lagrange problem for the Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg (CKN)-inequality (see §
1.1
) in
\Omega
, and may be described via the energy functional
\phi:\mathcal{D}_{a}^{1,p}(\Omega,0)\to\mathbb{R}
, given by

\phi(u):=\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla u\right|^{p}\left|x\right|^{-ap}\mathrm{d}{x}-\int_{\Omega}\left|u\right|^{q}\left|x\right|^{-bq}\mathrm{d}{x}.

Indeed,
u\in\mathcal{D}_{a}^{1,p}(\Omega,0)
solves problem (
A
) provided

\left\langle\phi^{\prime}(u),h\right\rangle=0\qquad\forall h\in\mathcal{D}_{a}^{1,p}(\Omega,0).

Here, with
p^{\prime}:=p/(p-1)
and
\mathcal{D}^{-1,p^{\prime}}_{a}(U,x_{0})
being the topological dual of
\mathcal{D}_{a}^{1,p}(U,x_{0})
, the map
\phi^{\prime}:\mathcal{D}_{a}^{1,p}(\Omega,0)\to\mathcal{D}_{a}^{-1,p^{\prime}}(\Omega,0)
denotes the (Fréchet) derivative of
\phi
.

Formally, in this notation, a
(PS)
-sequence for (
A
) is a sequence
(u_{\alpha})
in
\mathcal{D}_{a}^{1,p}(\Omega,0)
of bounded energy (meaning
\phi(u_{\alpha})
is uniformly bounded in
\alpha
) such that
\phi^{\prime}(u_{\alpha})\to 0
strongly in
\mathcal{D}_{a}^{-1,p^{\prime}}(\Omega,0)
as
\alpha\to\infty
. The purpose of our paper is to establish a Struwe-type decomposition result for the weighted problem (
A
).

Loosely speaking, these decompositions provide an asymptotic expansion of a given
(PS)
-sequence, within the energy space, in terms of perturbed and rescaled solutions to some “limiting” partial differential equations. An important distinction in the general weighted case (i.e.
(a,b)\neq(0,0)
) is the occurrence of this next weighted limiting problem:

(B)

\begin{cases}-\operatorname{div}\left(\left|x\right|^{-ap}\left|\nabla u\right|^{p-2}\nabla u\right)=\left|x\right|^{-bq}\left|u\right|^{q-2}u&\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{n}\\
u\in\mathcal{D}_{a}^{1,p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n},0\right)\end{cases}

whose associated energy functional
\phi_{0,\infty}:\mathcal{D}_{a}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},0)\to\mathbb{R}
is given by

\phi_{0,\infty}(u):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\nabla u\right|^{p}\left|x\right|^{-ap}\mathrm{d}{x}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|u\right|^{q}\left|x\right|^{-bq}\mathrm{d}{x}.

We distinguish two cases:
a\neq b
and
a=b
. The case
a=b\neq 0
is the most challenging and features a new blow-up configuration, which occurs only in this case. However, we shall begin by presenting the case
a\neq b
, which also deviates from the unweighted model case. Formally, when
a\neq b
, the following theorem holds:

Theorem 1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded domain containing the origin and let
(u_{\alpha})
be a Palais-Smale sequence for (
A
). Assume
a\neq b
and let
\gamma>0
be the homogeneity exponent from (
1.3
). Then, there exists a subsequence
(u_{\beta})
of
(u_{\alpha})
along with

(1)

a solution
v_{0}
of (
A
);

(2)

finitely many non-trivial functions
w_{1},\dots,w_{k}
for the weighted limiting problem (
B
) and, for each
j=1,\dots,k
, a sequence
(\lambda_{\beta}^{(j)})
in
(0,\infty
) such that

\lambda_{\beta}^{(j)}\to 0,\quad\text{as }\beta\to\infty.

Furthermore, as
\beta\to\infty
, we obtain

(i)

Decomposition in the Energy Space:

(1.5)

u_{\beta}-v_{0}-\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left(\lambda_{\beta}^{(j)}\right)^{-\gamma}w_{j}\left(\frac{\cdot}{\lambda_{\beta}^{(j)}}\right)\to 0\quad\text{ in }\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{a}(\mathbb{R}^{n},0)

(ii)

Norm Decomposition:

\left\|u_{\beta}\right\|_{\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{a}(\Omega,0)}^{p}\to\left\|v_{0}\right\|_{\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{a}(\Omega,0)}^{p}+\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left\|w_{j}\right\|_{\mathcal{D}_{a}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},0)}^{p}

(iii)

Energy Decomposition:

\phi(u_{\beta})\to\phi(v_{0})+\sum_{j=1}^{k}\phi_{0,\infty}(w_{j})

It should be noted that whenever our
(PS)
-sequence
(u_{\alpha})
is non-negative, the functions
v_{0}
,
w_{j}
are themselves non-negative.

The first result of this type was established by Struwe
[
44
]
in the case
p=2
without weights (i.e. when
a=b=0
). In later years, assuming
a=b=0
, Mercuri-Willem
[
35
]
showed that the decomposition obtained by Struwe
[
44
]
holds for non-negative
(PS)
-sequences and all
p\in(1,n)
.

We remark that in the conclusions of Theorem
1
, all “bubbles” (i.e. the rescaled
w_{j}
’s in (
1.5
)) concentrate at the origin. This stands in sharp contrast to the model case
a=b=0
, considered by Struwe
[
44
]
and Mercuri-Willem
[
35
]
, wherein the bubbles may concentrate at any point of
\overline{\Omega}
. Consequently, when
a<b
, one does not need to impose regularity conditions on
\partial\Omega
.

Deviating strongly from the classical model case, in the limit case
a=b\neq 0
, a
(PS)
-sequence for (
A
) may decompose (at the energy level) in terms of 3 potential limiting problems: the weighted problem (
B
) and problems (
C
), (
D
) which we state after our next main result.

Before stating the theorem in the case
a=b
, we define a key transform
\tau^{-}
in terms of a fixed function
\eta\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n};[0,1])
supported in
B(0,1)
and chosen such that
\eta\equiv 1
on
B(0,1/2)
. Then, given
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\lambda>0
, we define a transformation
\tau^{-}_{y,\alpha}
according to the rule

\tau^{-}_{y,\lambda}h(z):=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\lambda^{-\gamma}h\left(\frac{z-y}{\lambda}\right)&\text{if }a=b=0\\
\displaystyle\left(\frac{\lambda}{\left|y\right|}\right)^{-b}\lambda^{-\gamma}h\left(\frac{z-y}{\lambda}\right)\eta\left(\frac{2(z-y)}{\left|y\right|}\right)&\text{if }a=b\neq 0\,.\end{cases}

We point out the presence of the factor
\left(\frac{\lambda}{\left|y\right|}\right)^{-b}
, a striking difference from the classical unweighted case
a=b=0
. This difference is discussed in more details after the statement of the theorem.

Theorem 2
.

Suppose
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a bounded
C^{1}
-domain containing the origin and let
(u_{\alpha})
be a Palais-Smale sequence for problem (
A
). Assume
a=b
(so that
q=p^{\ast}
) and let
\gamma>0
be the homogeneity exponent from (
1.3
). Then, there exists a subsequence
(u_{\beta})
of
(u_{\alpha})
along with

(1)

A solution
v_{0}
of (
A
).

(2)

Finitely many non-trivial functions
w_{1},\dots,w_{k}
for the weighted limiting problem (
B
) and, for each
j=1,\dots,k
, a sequence
\left(\mu_{\beta}^{(j)}\right)
in
(0,\infty)
such that

\mu_{\beta}^{(j)}\to 0

as
\beta\to\infty
.

(3)

Finitely many non-trivial solutions
v_{1},\dots,v_{l}
for an unweighted limiting problem (
C
) in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
(see below) and, for each
j=1,\dots,l
, sequences
\left(\lambda_{\beta}^{(j)}\right)_{\beta}
in
(0,\infty)
and
\left(y_{\beta}^{(j)}\right)_{\beta}
in
\Omega
satisfying, as
\beta\to\infty
,

\lambda_{\beta}^{(j)}\to 0,\qquad\frac{\left|y_{\beta}^{(j)}\right|}{\lambda_{\beta}^{(j)}}\to\infty\qquad\frac{\operatorname{dist}(y_{\alpha}^{(j)},\partial\Omega)}{\left|\lambda^{(j)}_{\alpha}\right|}\to\infty

(4)

Finitely many non-trivial solutions
v^{\mathbb{H}}_{1},\dots,v^{\mathbb{H}}_{l^{\mathbb{H}}}
for an unweighted limiting problem (
D
) in the half-space
\mathbb{H}
(see below) and, for each
j=1,\dots,l^{\mathbb{H}}
, sequences
(\lambda_{\beta}^{\mathbb{H},(j)})_{\beta}
in
(0,\infty)
and
(y_{\beta}^{\mathbb{H},(j)})_{\beta}
in
\Omega
satisfying, as
\beta\to\infty
,

\lambda_{\beta}^{\mathbb{H},(j)}\to 0,\qquad\frac{\left|y_{\beta}^{\mathbb{H},(j)}\right|}{\lambda_{\beta}^{\mathbb{H},(j)}}\to\infty,\qquad\frac{\operatorname{dist}(y_{\beta}^{\mathbb{H},(j)},\partial\Omega)}{\left|\lambda^{\mathbb{H},(j)}_{\beta}\right|}\text{ is bounded.}

These are such that the following decompositions are true as
\beta\to\infty

(i)

Decomposition in the Energy Space:

u_{\beta}-v_{0}-\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left(\mu_{\beta}^{(j)}\right)^{-\gamma}w_{j}\left(\frac{z}{\mu_{\beta}^{(j)}}\right)-\sum_{j=1}^{l}\left[\tau^{-}_{\lambda_{\beta}^{(j)},y_{\beta}^{(j)}}v_{j}\right]-\sum_{j=1}^{l^{\mathbb{H}}}\left[\tau^{-}_{\lambda_{\beta}^{\mathbb{H},(j)},y_{\beta}^{\mathbb{H},(j)}}V^{\mathbb{H}}_{j}\right]\to 0

in
\mathcal{D}_{a}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},0)
. Here,
V_{j}^{\mathbb{H}}
are translated rotations of
v_{j}^{\mathbb{H}}
.

(ii)

Norm Decomposition:

\left\|u_{\beta}\right\|^{p}_{\mathcal{D}^{1,p}(\Omega,0)}\to\left\|v_{0}\right\|_{\mathcal{D}_{a}^{1,p}(\Omega,0)}+\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left\|w_{j}\right\|_{\mathcal{D}_{a}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},0)}+\sum_{j=1}^{l}\left\|v_{j}\right\|_{\mathcal{D}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\sum_{j=1}^{l^{\mathbb{H}}}\left\|v_{j}^{\mathbb{H}}\right\|_{\mathcal{D}^{1,p}(\mathbb{H})}

(iii)

Energy Decomposition:

\phi(u_{\beta})\to\phi(v_{0})+\sum_{j=1}^{k}\phi_{0,\infty}(w_{j})+\sum_{j=1}^{l}\phi_{\infty}(v_{j})+\sum_{j=1}^{l^{\mathbb{H}}}\phi_{\infty}(v_{j}^{\mathbb{H}})

Remark 1.1
.

It should be noted that whenever our
(PS)
-sequence
(u_{\alpha})
is non-negative the functions
v_{0}
,
w_{j}
,
v_{j}
,
v_{j}^{\mathbb{H}}
are themselves non-negative. Therefore, in this case, thanks to a non-existence result of Mercuri-Willem
[
35
]
, we may rule out the possibility of solutions to the half-space problem (
D
).

As stated before the theorem, we observe three different bubbling configurations involving three limiting problems. First, we have the weighted problem (
B
) that we previously encountered within the
a<b
case. The second, is an entire unweighted analogue given by

(C)

\begin{cases}-\operatorname{div}\left(\left|\nabla u\right|^{p-2}\nabla u\right)=\left|u\right|^{p^{\ast}-2}u&\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{n}\\
u\in\mathcal{D}^{1,p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right),\end{cases}

with associated energy functional

\phi_{\infty}:\mathcal{D}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\supseteq\mathcal{D}^{1,p}(\mathbb{H})\to\mathbb{R},\qquad\phi_{\infty}(u):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\nabla u\right|^{p}\mathrm{d}{x}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|u\right|^{p^{\ast}}\mathrm{d}{x}.

Furthermore, as one cannot consistently rule out “bubbling” near the boundary of
\Omega
, we are forced to also consider the unweighted limiting problem set in the half-space
\mathbb{H}:=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:x_{n}>0\right\}
,

(D)

\begin{cases}-\operatorname{div}\left(\left|\nabla u\right|^{p-2}\nabla u\right)=\left|u\right|^{p^{\ast}-2}u&\text{in }\mathbb{H}\\
u\in\mathcal{D}^{1,p}\left(\mathbb{H}\right).\end{cases}

It is notable that Theorem
2
, in contrast to Theorem
1
and the model case studied by Struwe
[
44
]
and Mercuri-Willem
[
35
]
, may produce bubbles corresponding to three problems, specifically (
B
), (
C
), (
D
). Nevertheless, it is Theorem
2
that generalizes the model case
a=b=0
.

What is more, in Theorem
2
, a striking distinction in our result is that we find bubbles derived from solutions belonging to different energy spaces (i.e. weighted
and
unweighted spaces). Therefore, we require a new transform
\tau_{y,\lambda}^{-}
that “transfers information” from the unweighted space
\mathcal{D}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to its weighted counterpart
\mathcal{D}_{a}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},0)
. In said transform, we introduce a factor which does not appear in the model case: a power of
\lambda/\left|y\right|
accounting for the effect of the weighted measures
\left|x\right|^{-ap}\mathrm{d}{x}
and
\left|x\right|^{-bq}\mathrm{d}{x}
, which are controlled via a cutoff function. Of course, it is important to observe that the
\lambda/\left|y\right|
ratio vanishes when
a=b=0
and the cutoff is unnecessary in this case, at which point we recover the classical transform used by Struwe
[
44
]
and Mercuri-Willem
[
35
]
.

Briefly addressing the proofs of Theorems
1
-
2
, it is instructive to observe that the transformation rules employed within our strategy deviate significantly from those used by Struwe
[
44
]
and Mercuri-Willem
[
35
]
. Speaking informally, in the case
a<b
, we require a transform that accounts for the lack of translation invariance introduced by the aforementioned weighted measures. However, such a transform may seem counter-intuitive since it requires knowing, a priori, that the bubbles concentrate at the origin. We also note that the proof of Theorem
1
coincides with a sub-argument in the
a=b
case and, on that account, constitutes a key facet of the global picture. In addition to this, since when
a=b
we obtain bubbles derived from solutions belonging to distinct energy spaces, we shall also requires a transform
\tau_{y,\lambda}
from the weighted space
\mathcal{D}_{a}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},0)
to
\mathcal{D}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, which acts as a “pseudo-inverse” to
\tau_{y,\lambda}^{-}
appearing in Theorem
2
; both
\tau_{y,\lambda}
and
\tau_{y,\lambda}^{-}
will be discussed in greater depth within §2. We note that
\tau_{y,\lambda}
will also be central in the proof of Theorem
1
, where it is used to rule out bubbling away from the origin.

1.1.
Historical Context and Classifications

Struwe-type decompositions find their roots in the pioneering 1984 work of Michael Struwe
[
44
]
, where the author establishes the contents of Theorem
2
in the case
p=2
without weights (
a=b=0
). Therein, a non-existence result of Pohozaev
[
37
]
was invoked to rule out the possibility of solutions to (
D
). Therefore, in this case, one exclusively encounters solutions to (
C
). Still requiring that
a=b=0
, the result of Struwe was improved by Mercuri-Willem
[
35
]
to include all
1<p<n
, provided the
(PS)
-sequence is non-negative. In this setting, thanks to a non-existence result of Mercuri-Willem
[
35
]
, they once again only produce functions satisfying problem (
C
). We also refer the reader to the work of Alves
[
2
]
.

Results of this type have been used to shed light on existence and multiplicity of solutions, see for instance
[
20
,
15
,
47
,
4
,
16
]
. In addition, we note that decompositions of this type have been established in other contexts. Examples include Hebey-Robert
[
28
]
for Paneitz type operators, Saintier
[
39
]
for
p
-Laplace operators on compact manifolds, El-Hamidi-Vétois
[
23
]
for anisotropic operators, Almaraz
[
1
]
for non-linear boundary conditions and Mazumdar
[
34
]
for polyharmonic operators on compact manifolds.

It should also be noted that, in 1985, Lions
[
32
,
33
]
published works relating to concentration-compactness phenomena in the calculus of variations. In particular, in a spirit similar to Struwe’s result, he established a decomposition with convergence taking place at the level of measures.

We also point out that the stability of the CKN-inequality, which closely relates to quantitative versions of Struwe decompositions, has been recently studied in Ciraolo-Figalli-Maggi
[
14
]
, Figalli-Glaudo
[
25
]
, Deng-Sun-Wei
[
19
]
, and Wei-Wu
[
50
]
.

1.1.1.
Classifications

Our understanding of the loss of compactness phenomenon presented in Theorems
1
,
2
may be improved by characterizing solutions to problems (
B
), (
C
) and (
D
). As such, we provide some historical context and recent results, particularly with regards to classifications of solutions to problem (
B
) and its unweighted analogue (
C
).

Let us first remark that problems (
B
) and (
C
) are intimately related to the Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg (CKN) inequality on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Proven by Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg in
[
7
]
, the CKN-inequality states that there is a best (i.e. minimal) constant
C>0
such that, for any
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, there holds

(1.6)

\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|u(x)\right|^{q}\left|x-x_{0}\right|^{-bq}\mathrm{d}{x}\right)^{1/q}\leq C\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\nabla u(x)\right|^{p}\left|x-x_{0}\right|^{-ap}\mathrm{d}{x}\right)^{1/p}

for all
u\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. When
a=b=0
, this reduces to the Gagliardo-Nirenberg-Sobolev inequality and the best constant is known by works of Aubin
[
3
]
and Talenti
[
46
]
, though computations of this constant date as far back as Rodemich’s seminar
[
38
]
.

By standard techniques in the calculus of variations, a suitable constant multiple of functions achieving equality in (
1.6
) solve problem (
B
). In fact, early classification were for minimizers of the ratio

(1.7)

\dfrac{\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\nabla u(x)\right|^{p}\left|x\right|^{-ap}\mathrm{d}{x}\right)^{1/p}}{\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|u(x)\right|^{q}\left|x\right|^{-bq}\mathrm{d}{x}\right)^{1/q}},\qquad u\in\mathcal{D}_{a}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},0).

In the unweighted case
a=b=0
, Talenti
[
46
]
exhibited a family of (radially symmetric) functions minimizing (
1.7
). For
p=2
and
n\geq 3
, the existence of radially symmetric functions minimizing (
1.7
) was determined by Lieb
[
31
]
when
a=0
. In addition, an explicit form for said best constant was estblished therein. Furthermore, for
p=2
, Chou and Chu
[
12
]
determined the best constant in (
1.6
) when
a\geq 0
and categorizes the functions minimizing (
1.7
) when
a>0
. Considering instead
a<0
, Horiuchi
[
29
]
showed that, when
p=2,n\geq 3
and
a=b<0
, the best constant in (
1.6
) is precisely the Sobolev constant and equality cannot be achieved by any function in
\mathcal{D}_{a}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},x_{0})
. In this same paper, Horiuchi also determined the best constant in (
1.6
) for
1<p<{n}/({1+a-b})
, provided
a\geq 0
and
b<n/q
.

In recent years, positive solutions to problem (
C
) have been fully classified and have the form

(1.8)

x\mapsto\left[\frac{\left(\mu n\left(\frac{n-p}{p-1}\right)^{p-1}\right)^{1/p}}{\mu+\left|x-x_{0}\right|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}}\right]^{\frac{p}{q-p}},\qquad\mu>0,x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

This classification was obtained in the case
p=2
by Caffarelli, Gidas and Spruck
[
6
]
; they showed, via the method of moving planes, that all positive solutions to (
C
) are radial and thus determined an explicit form for said solutions. Furthermore, Damascelli, Merchán, Montoro and Sciunzi
[
17
]
utilized a previous classification due to Guedda and Veron
[
27
]
to extend this result to the range
\frac{2n}{n+2}\leq p<2
. Similarly, Vétois
[
48
]
improved the range to
1<p<2
by exploiting a decay result of Damascelli-Ramaswamy
[
18
]
. Finally, the case
2<p<n
was handled by Sciunzi
[
40
]
using an a-priori estimate of Vétois
[
48
]
. We mention that, for ranges of
p
depending on
n
, this classification is also known for solutions that are
not
assumed to have finite energy (i.e.
u
need not belong to
\mathcal{D}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
a priori), see Catino, Monticelli and Roncoroni
[
8
]
, Ou
[
36
]
and Vétois
[
49
]
for more detail.

Similar classification results are known for the weighted analog of (
C
), i.e. problem (
B
). Indeed, in some cases, positive solutions to problem (
B
) are known to be of the form

(1.9)

x\mapsto\left[\frac{\left(\dfrac{\mu n(n-p(1+a))^{p}}{(n-p(1+a-b))(p-1)^{p-1}}\right)^{1/p}}{\mu+c\left|x\right|^{\frac{(n-p(1+a))}{(n-p(1+a-b))}\frac{p(1+a-b)}{p-1}}}\right]^{\frac{p}{q-p}},\qquad\mu>0

We note that, if
a=b=0
, the weighted measures
\left|x\right|^{-ap}\mathrm{d}{x}
and
\left|x\right|^{-bq}\mathrm{d}{x}
are translation invariant; in particular the functions in (
1.9
) cover all possible solutions of (
B
), up to translation. Put otherwise, in the case
a=b=0
there is no distinction between problems (
B
) and (
C
) so all positive solutions are given by equation (
1.8
); we omit this case from the reminder of our discussion. When
a=0
, generalizing a result of Guedda-Veron
[
27
]
to allow for
b\neq 0
, Ghoussoub and Yuan
[
26
]
showed that every positive radial solutions of (
B
) is of the form (
1.9
). Considering only
p=2
, Dolbeault and Esteban
[
21
]
show that all positive (not a-priori assumed radial) solutions in the non-limiting case
a<b
are as in (
B
), provided either

•

a\geq 0

•

a<0
and
b\leq\gamma\left(\frac{n}{p\sqrt{\gamma^{2}+n-1}}-1\right)
.

Moreover, for dimensions
n\geq 3
, Ciraolo and Corso
[
13
]
, utilizing a-priori asymptotic estimates of Vétois-Shakerian
[
41
]
, demonstrated that this classification extends to the whole range
1<p<n
in the limit case
a=b\geq 0
as well as a wider range of possible values for
a,b
provided
p<n/2
. Building upon the result of Ciraolo-Corso
[
13
]
and again utilizing estimates from Vétois-Shakerian
[
41
]
, Le and Le
[
30
]
showed that, when
a=0
, all positive solutions of (
B
) are radial and therefore classified by Ghoussoub-Yuan
[
26
]
.

We remark that all classification results (for positive solutions) discussed thus far yield radial solutions. However, certain minimizers of (

) are known to break symmetry for some values of
a,b,p
; see
[
9
,
5
,
24
,
43
,
22
,
11
]
for details.

1.2.
Paper structure

We devote the remainder of this paper to the establishment of Theorems
1
-
2
. Firstly, in §2, we introduce some convenient notation and recall important properties of the homogenous weighted Sobolev space
\mathcal{D}_{a}^{1,p}
and Palais-Smale sequences that will be liberally invoked during the proof of our main results. Secondly, in §3, we discuss the new transformation rules in greater detail and, in doing so, we provide explicit bounds for their operator norms. Following this, in §4, we shift our attention to the limiting behaviour of rescaled domains and make precise in what sense domains may converge. Then, in §5, we consider how the new transforms interact with energy functionals associated to problems (
A
)–(
D
). Leveraging our work from the previous sections, we provide results that will be central to the proofs of our main theorems in §6. Specifically, we show how one can extract a solution to the problem in
\Omega
from a given
(PS)
-sequence, which constitutes the first step of each main proof. In addition, still in §6, we demonstrate that, when
a=b
, the new transforms gives rise to new “bubble” terms derived from solutions to three distinct limiting problems, and how one may recover a
(PS)
-sequence for the original problem. Finally, in §7-§8 using these aforementioned lemmas, we provide the proofs of Theorems
1
-
2
by way of an iterative step-by-step procedure.

2.
Homogeneous Sobolev Spaces and Palais-Smale Sequences

In this section, we introduce notation involving weighted homogeneous Sobolev spaces and provide results describing properties of the functions living therein. Recall that, for any open set
U
and
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we let
\mathcal{D}_{a}^{1,p}(U,x_{0})
be the completion of
C_{c}^{\infty}(U)
under the norm (
1.4
). In the same vein, we introduce the following two weighted norms

\left\|u\right\|_{L^{q}_{b}(U,x_{0})}^{q}:=\int_{U}\left|u\right|^{q}\left|x-x_{0}\right|^{-bq}\mathrm{d}{x},\quad\left\|v\right\|_{L^{p}_{a}(U,x_{0})}^{p}:=\int_{U}\left|v\right|^{p}\left|x-x_{0}\right|^{-ap}\mathrm{d}{x}.

The above expressions induce norms on the weighted Lebesgue spaces
L^{q}_{b}(U,x_{0})
and
L^{p}_{a}(U,x_{0})
, consisting (respectively) of those (Lebsegue measurable)
u,v:U\to\mathbb{R}
such that

\int_{U}\left|u\right|^{q}\left|x-x_{0}\right|^{-bq}\mathrm{d}{x}<\infty,\quad\text{and}\quad\int_{U}\left|v\right|^{p}\left|x-x_{0}\right|^{-ap}\mathrm{d}{x}<\infty.

In this notation, an equivalent formulation of the CKN-inequality (
1.6
) is thus given by

(2.1)

C_{a,b}\left\|w\right\|_{L^{q}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{n},x_{0})}^{p}\leq\left\|\nabla w\right\|_{L^{p}_{a}(\mathbb{R}^{n},x_{0})}^{p},\quad\forall w\in\mathcal{D}_{a}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},x_{0})

where
C_{a,b}=C_{a,b}(n,p)
denotes the sharp constant that will henceforth be called a CKN-constant.

Remark 2.1
.

As mentioned in §1, when
a=b=0
, we shall omit the presence of weights and only write
\mathcal{D}^{1,p}(U)
. In this case, with
U=\mathbb{R}^{n}
, available literature includes, for instance, Willem
[
51
, §7.2]
and this homogeneous Sobolev space is precisely

\mathcal{D}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\left\{u\in L^{p^{\ast}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):\nabla u\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\right\}.

For the purposes of this paper, we shall require results from Sobolev theory in the context of the weighted space
\mathcal{D}_{a}^{1,p}(U,x_{0})
, which we recall below. The reader may consult Chernysh
[
10
, Chapter 2]
for more details on these results. Descriptively, every function
u\in\mathcal{D}_{a}^{1,p}(U,x_{0})
belongs to
L^{q}_{b}(U,x_{0})
, whereas
\nabla u
exists in the weak sense on
U\setminus\{x_{0}\}
such that
\nabla u\in L^{p}_{a}(U\setminus\{x_{0}\},x_{0})
. Moreover, when
a\geq 0
, we can assert that
\nabla u
exists in the weak sense on the
whole
of
U
. We further note that weak gradients vanish on level sets. That is, for every
c\in\mathbb{R}
,

\nabla u\equiv 0\text{ almost everywhere on }\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:u(x)=c\right\}.

Following this, we also recall that, under condition (
1.2
),
\mathcal{D}_{a}^{1,p}(U,x_{0})
is always a reflexive Banach space. In fact, every bounded sequence in
\mathcal{D}_{a}^{1,p}(U,x_{0})
admits a subsequence converging weakly and almost everywhere on the set
U
. We also remark that several key results for traditional Sobolev spaces have known analogues in
\mathcal{D}_{a}^{1,p}(U,x_{0})
, e.g. the Rellich-Kondrachov embedding theorem. In particular, the natural embedding
\mathcal{D}_{a}^{1,p}(U,x_{0})\hookrightarrow L_{\text{loc}}^{p}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n},\left|x-x_{0}\right|^{-ap}\big)
is compact.

Denoting by
\mathcal{D}^{-1,p^{\prime}}_{a}(U,x_{0})
, with
p^{\prime}:=p/(p-1)
, the topological dual of
\mathcal{D}_{a}^{1,p}(U,x_{0})
, a Riesz-type representation theorem asserts that every functional
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}_{a}^{-1,p^{\prime}}(U,x_{0})
takes the form

(2.2)

\varphi(u)=\int_{U}\left(\nabla u,\mathbf{g}\right)\left|x-x_{0}\right|^{-ap}\mathrm{d}{x},\qquad\forall u\in\mathcal{D}_{a}^{1,p}(U,x_{0})

for some
\mathbb{R}^{n}
-valued function
\mathbf{g}\in L^{p^{\prime}}_{a}(\mathbb{R}^{n},x_{0})
. Here,
\left(\cdot,\ast\right)
denotes the usual inner-product on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Finally, for simplicity, the topological dual of
\mathcal{D}^{1,p}(U)
is simply denoted
\mathcal{D}^{-1,p^{\prime}}(U)
.

2.1.
Pointwise Convergence of the Gradients

We have noted that bounded sequences in
\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{a}(U,x_{0})
have subsequences that converge weakly and almost everywhere on
U
. In this subsection, we give conditions under which we can find a subsequence whose
gradients
converge pointwise. More specifically, we establish a weighted version of Theorem 3.3 in Mercuri-Willem
[
35
]
.

Let us first define the Lipschitz continuous function
T:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
by the rule

(2.3)

T(x):=\begin{cases}x&\text{if }\left|x\right|\leq 1,\\
\frac{x}{\left|x\right|}&\text{if }\left|x\right|>1.\end{cases}

By considering suitable mollifications of
T
, it is straightforward to see that, given
u\in\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{a}(U,x_{0})
,
T(u)\in\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{a}(U,x_{0})
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
(u_{\alpha})
be a bounded sequence in
\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{a}(U,x_{0})
converging pointwise almost everywhere to
u\in\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{a}(U,x_{0})
as
\alpha\to\infty
. Let
T
be given by (
2.3
) and assume that
(U_{k})
is an increasing sequence of open subsets of
U
such that
\bigcup_{k\geq 1}U_{k}=U
. Assume further that

(2.4)

\lim_{\alpha\to\infty}\int_{U_{k}}\left(\left[\left|\nabla u_{\alpha}\right|^{p-2}\nabla u_{\alpha}-\left|\nabla u\right|^{p-2}\nabla u\right],\nabla\left[T(u_{\alpha}-u)\right]\right)\,\mathrm{d}{\mu}=0

for each
k\geq 1
, where
\mathrm{d}{\mu}=\left|x-x_{0}\right|^{-ap}\mathrm{d}{x}
. Then, there exists a subsequence
(u_{\beta})
such that
\nabla u_{\beta}\to\nabla u
almost everywhere on
U
.

Proof.

We adapt an argument from Szulkin-Willem
[
45
]
, to show that, given
k\in\mathbb{N}
, up to a subsequence,
\nabla u_{\alpha}\to\nabla u
almost everywhere on
U_{k}
.

Fix
k\in\mathbb{N}
and, for each
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}
, consider the set

E^{(k)}_{\alpha}:=\left\{x\in U_{k}:\left|u_{\alpha}(x)-u(x)\right|\leq 1\right\}.

Then, (
2.4
) can be written as

(2.5)

\lim_{\alpha\to\infty}\int_{E^{(k)}_{\alpha}}\left(\left[\left|\nabla u_{\alpha}\right|^{p-2}\nabla u_{\alpha}-\left|\nabla u\right|^{p-2}\nabla u\right],[\nabla u_{\alpha}-\nabla u]\right)\left|x-x_{0}\right|^{-ap}\mathrm{d}{x}=0.

Since the integrand is non-negative and
\mathbf{1}_{E^{(k)}_{\alpha}}(x)\to 1
for almost every
x\in U_{k}
, passing to a subsequence if necessary, we see that

\displaystyle\left(\left[\left|\nabla u_{\alpha}\right|^{p-2}\nabla u_{\alpha}-\left|\nabla u\right|^{p-2}\nabla u\right],[\nabla u_{\alpha}-\nabla u]\right)\to 0

pointwise almost everywhere on
U_{k}
. Appealing classical results (see, for instance, Szulkin-Willem
[
45
, Lemma 2.1]
), it follows that
\nabla u_{\alpha}\to\nabla u
pointwise a.e. on
U_{k}
. Finally, a diagonal argument completes the proof.
∎

Corollary 2.2
.

Let
(u_{\alpha})
be a bounded sequence in
\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{a}(U,x_{0})
converging pointwise almost everywhere to
u\in\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{a}(U,x_{0})
as
\alpha\to\infty
. Under the assumptions of Proposition
2.1
, there exists a subsequence
(u_{\beta})
with the following properties:

(1)

\nabla u_{\beta}\to\nabla u
pointwise almost everywhere on
U
;

(2)

\lim\limits_{\beta\to\infty}\left(\left\|u_{\beta}\right\|^{p}_{\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{a}(U,x_{0})}-\left\|u_{\beta}-u\right\|^{p}_{\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{a}(U,x_{0})}\right)=\left\|u\right\|^{p}_{\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{a}(U,x_{0})}
;

(3)

and

\left|\nabla u_{\beta}\right|^{p-2}\nabla u_{\beta}-\left|\nabla u_{\beta}-\nabla u\right|^{p-2}(\nabla u_{\beta}-\nabla u)\to\left|\nabla u\right|^{p-2}\nabla u

strongly in
L^{\frac{p}{p-1}}(U,\left|x-x_{0}\right|^{-ap})

(4)

and

\left|u_{\beta}\right|^{q-2}u_{\beta}-\left|u_{\beta}-u\right|^{q-2}(u_{\beta}-u)\to\left|u\right|^{q-2}u

strongly in
L^{\frac{q}{q-1}}(U,\left|x-x_{0}\right|^{-bq})
.

Proof.

The first item is the conclusion of Proposition
2.1
whence (2) is a direct consequence of the Brézis-Lieb Lemma. Finally, (3) and (4) follows from straightforward adaptations of Alves
[
2
, Lemma 3]
and Mercuri-Willem
[
35
, Lemma 3.2]
.
∎

2.2.
Palais-Smale Sequences and Energies of Critical Points

Proposition 2.3
.

A Palais-Smale sequence
(u_{\alpha})
for (
A
) is bounded.

Proof.

We follow the argument used in Struwe
[
44
, Lemma 2.3]
.
Let
(u_{\alpha})
be a Palais-Smale sequence for (
A
). A simple calculation shows that, for some
C>0
,

\displaystyle\left\|u_{\alpha}\right\|_{\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{a}(\Omega,0)}^{p}

\displaystyle=p\phi(u_{\alpha})+\frac{p}{q}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\left|u_{\alpha}\right|^{q}}{\left|x\right|^{bq}}\mathrm{d}{x}\leq C+\frac{p}{q}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\left|u_{\alpha}\right|^{q}}{\left|x\right|^{bq}}\mathrm{d}{x}

where we have used that
\phi(u_{\alpha})
is bounded in
\alpha
. Next, observe that

\left(1-\frac{p}{q}\right)\int_{\Omega}\frac{\left|u_{\alpha}\right|^{q}}{\left|x\right|^{bq}}\mathrm{d}{x}=p\phi(u_{\alpha})-\left\langle\phi^{\prime}(u_{\alpha}),u_{\alpha}\right\rangle\leq C+o(1)\left\|u_{\alpha}\right\|_{\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{a}(\Omega,0)}

It then follows that, for a suitable
\widetilde{C}>0
,

\left\|u_{\alpha}\right\|_{\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{a}(\Omega,0)}^{p}\leq C+\frac{p}{q}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\left|u_{\alpha}\right|^{q}}{\left|x\right|^{bq}}\mathrm{d}{x}\\
\leq\widetilde{C}+o(1)\left\|u_{\alpha}\right\|_{\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{a}(\Omega,0)}

whence
(u_{\alpha})
is bounded in
\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{a}(\Omega,0)
.
∎

Using this, we show that (in the limit) Palais-Smale sequences for
\phi
are uniformly bounded from below by zero in energy. More precisely, we have the following:

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
(u_{\alpha})
be a Palais-Smale sequence for (
A
). Then,
(u_{\alpha})
has non-negative limiting energy. More precisely,

\liminf_{\alpha\to\infty}\phi(u_{\alpha})\geq 0.

Proof.

Since
(u_{\alpha})
is bounded and a Palais-Smale sequence, it is readily seen that

\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla u_{\alpha}\right|^{p}\left|x\right|^{-ap}\mathrm{d}{x}-\int_{\Omega}\left|u_{\alpha}\right|^{q}\left|x\right|^{-bq}\mathrm{d}{x}=\left\langle\phi^{\prime}(u_{\alpha}),u_{\alpha}\right\rangle=o(1)

as
\alpha\to\infty
. Consequently, as
\alpha\to\infty
,

\phi(u_{\alpha})=\frac{\left\|\nabla u_{\alpha}\right\|_{L^{p}_{a}(\Omega,0)}^{p}}{p}-\frac{\left\|u_{\alpha}\right\|_{L^{q}_{b}(\Omega,0)}^{q}}{q}=\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}\right)\left\|u_{\alpha}\right\|_{L^{q}_{b}(\Omega,0)}^{q}+o(1)

Passing to the limit inferior, we conclude the proof.
∎

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
C_{a,b}>0
be the CKN-constant, in particular
C_{a,b}>0
is such that (
2.1
) holds. Let
U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an open set and suppose
u\in\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{a}(U,0)
is a non-trivial critical point of
\phi_{0,\infty}
in
U
, i.e.
\phi^{\prime}_{0,\infty}(u,h)=0
for all
h\in\mathcal{D}_{a}^{1,p}(U,0)
. Then,

0<\left(C_{a,b}\right)^{\frac{n}{p(1+a-b)}}\left(\frac{1+a-b}{n}\right)\leq\phi_{0,\infty}(u).

In particular,

(1)

The constant
\left(C_{a,b}\right)^{\frac{n}{p(1+a-b)}}\left(\frac{1+a-b}{n}\right)
is a lower bound for the energy of non-trivial solutions to problems (
A
) and (
B
).

(2)

If
a=b
, then
\left(C_{0,0}\right)^{\frac{n}{p}}\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)
is a lower bound for the energy of non-trivial solutions to problems (
C
) and (
D
).

Proof.

Since
u\in\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{a}(U,0)
is a critical point of
\phi_{0,\infty}
in
U
, after extending
u
by
0
outside of
U
, we have

(2.6)

0=\left\langle\phi^{\prime}_{x_{0},\infty}(u),u\right\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\nabla u\right|^{p}\left|x+x_{0}\right|^{-ap}\mathrm{d}{x}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|u\right|^{q}\left|x+x_{0}\right|^{-bq}\mathrm{d}{x}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\phi_{0,\infty}(u)=\frac{1}{p}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{p}_{a}(\mathbb{R}^{n},-x_{0})}^{p}-\frac{1}{q}\left\|u\right\|^{q}_{L^{q}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{n},-x_{0})}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}\right)\left\|u\right\|^{q}_{L^{q}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{n},-x_{0})}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1+a-b}{n}\right)\left\|u\right\|^{q}_{L^{q}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{n},-x_{0})}

On the other hand, by (
2.1
) and using again (
2.6
),

C_{a,b}\left\|u\right\|_{L^{q}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{n},-x_{0})}^{p}\leq\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{p}_{a}(\mathbb{R}^{n},-x_{0})}^{p}=\left\|u\right\|^{q}_{L^{q}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{n},-x_{0})},

whence

0<\left(C_{a,b}\right)^{q/(q-p)}\leq\left\|u\right\|^{q}_{L^{q}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{n},-x_{0})}.

We conclude that,

\displaystyle\phi_{0,\infty}(u)=\left(\frac{1+a-b}{n}\right)\left\|u\right\|^{q}_{L^{q}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{n},-x_{0})}

\displaystyle\geq\left(C_{a,b}\right)^{q/(q-p)}\left(\frac{1+a-b}{n}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(C_{a,b}\right)^{\frac{n}{p(1+a-b)}}\left(\frac{1+a-b}{n}\right).

∎

3.
Homogeneity and New Rescaling Transformations

Let
u\in\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{a}(\mathbb{R}^{n},0)
be given and fix an arbitrary point
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
along with some scaling factor
\lambda>0
. We consider the following rescaling of
u
:

(3.1)

v(x):=\lambda^{\gamma}u(\lambda(x+x_{0})),

where
\gamma>0
is the homogeneity exponent given by equation (
1.3
). With this rescaling, an easy calculation yields that

\left\|v\right\|_{L^{q}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{n},-x_{0})}=\left\|u\right\|_{L^{q}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{n},0)}\quad\text{and}\quad\left\|\nabla v\right\|_{L^{p}_{a}(\mathbb{R}^{n},-x_{0})}=\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{p}_{a}(\mathbb{R}^{n},0)}

whence it follows that
v\in\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{a}(\mathbb{R}^{n},-x_{0})
.

3.1.
Modifying the Rescaling: A New Transform

Let
u\in\mathcal{D}_{a}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},0)
and consider an arbitrary point
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\left\{0\right\}
along with some
\lambda>0
. We also fix a smooth function
\eta\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
satisfying

(3.2)

\begin{cases}0\leq\eta\leq 1\\
\eta=1&\text{in }B(0,1/2)\\
\eta=0&\text{outside }B(0,1).\end{cases}

Define the transform

\tau_{y,\lambda}:\mathcal{D}_{a}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},0)\to\mathcal{D}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

via the rule

(3.3)

\tau_{y,\lambda}u(x)=\begin{cases}\lambda^{\gamma}u(\lambda x+y)&\text{if }a=b=0\\
\displaystyle\left(\frac{\lambda}{\left|y\right|}\right)^{b}\lambda^{\gamma}u\left(\lambda x+y\right)\eta\left(\frac{2\lambda x}{\left|y\right|}\right)&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

We now examine how this transformation behaves with respect to operator bounds.

Lemma 3.1
.

There exists a constant
C>0
such that, given a point
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\left\{0\right\}
along with some
\lambda>0
, there holds

\displaystyle\left\|\tau_{y,\lambda}u\right\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|u\right\|_{L^{q}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{n},0)}

\displaystyle\left\|\tau_{y,\lambda}u\right\|_{\mathcal{D}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\frac{\lambda}{\left|y\right|}\right)^{b-a}\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{D}_{a}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},0)}

for all
u\in\mathcal{D}_{a}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},0)
.

Proof.

The case
a=b=0
follows directly from homogeneity. Otherwise, we begin with a simple observation. Suppose
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is such that

\left|\frac{2\lambda x}{\left|y\right|}\right|\leq 1\iff\left|\lambda x\right|\leq\frac{1}{2}\left|y\right|.

By the triangle and reverse-triangle inequalities, we then have

\frac{1}{2}\leq\frac{\left|\lambda x+y\right|}{\left|y\right|}\leq\frac{3}{2}.

Therefore, there exists a constant
C=C(ap,bq)>0
independent of
\lambda,y
such that

(3.4)

\left(\frac{\left|\lambda x+y\right|}{\left|y\right|}\right)^{ap}\leq C,\quad\left(\frac{\left|\lambda x+y\right|}{\left|y\right|}\right)^{bq}\leq C

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
{2\lambda\left|x\right|}\leq{\left|y\right|}
. In particular, these last equations hold whenever
{2\lambda x}/{\left|y\right|}
is in the support of
\eta
.

Given
u\in\mathcal{D}_{a}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},0)
, we use inequality (
3.4
) in order to bound the
L^{q}
and
\mathcal{D}^{1,p}
norms of
v:=\tau_{y,\lambda}u
. Since
0\leq\eta\leq 1
, observe that

\displaystyle\left\|v\right\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{q}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|v\right|^{q}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{\lambda}{\left|y\right|}\right)^{bq}\lambda^{\gamma q}\int_{2\lambda\left|x\right|\leq\left|y\right|}\left|u\left(\lambda x+y\right)\right|^{q}\mathrm{d}{x}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{n}\int_{2\lambda\left|x\right|\leq\left|y\right|}\left|u\left(\lambda x+y\right)\right|^{q}\left|y\right|^{-bq}\mathrm{d}{x}

\displaystyle\leq C\lambda^{n}\int_{2\lambda\left|x\right|\leq\left|y\right|}\left|u\left(\lambda x+y\right)\right|^{q}\left|\lambda x+y\right|^{-bq}\mathrm{d}{x}

\displaystyle=C\int_{2\left|z-y\right|\leq\left|y\right|}\left|u\left(z\right)\right|^{q}\left|z\right|^{-bq}\mathrm{d}{z}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|u\right\|_{L^{q}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{n},0)}^{q}

which establishes the first inequality. In order to bound the
\mathcal{D}^{1,p}
-norm of
v
, we will consider two separate terms obtained via the product rule:

\displaystyle\left\|v\right\|_{\mathcal{D}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\nabla v\right|^{p}\mathrm{d}{x}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{p-1}\left(\frac{\lambda}{\left|y\right|}\right)^{p(b-a)}\lambda^{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\eta\left(\frac{2\lambda x}{\left|y\right|}\right)\nabla u(\lambda x+y)\right|^{p}\left|y\right|^{-ap}\mathrm{d}{x}

\displaystyle\quad+2^{p-1+p}\left(\frac{\lambda}{\left|y\right|}\right)^{p}\lambda^{np/q}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|u(\lambda x+y)\nabla\eta\left(\frac{2\lambda x}{\left|y\right|}\right)\right|^{p}\left|y\right|^{-bp}\mathrm{d}{x}

\displaystyle=:I_{1}+I_{2}.

Here, we have used that
p>1
so that convexity implies
(\kappa+\iota)^{p}\leq 2^{p-1}\left(\kappa^{p}+\iota^{p}\right)
for all
\kappa,\iota\geq 0
. We bound
I_{1}
following the procedure used to bound the
L^{q}
-norm of
v
. This yields

I_{1}\leq C2^{p-1}\left(\frac{\lambda}{\left|y\right|}\right)^{p(b-a)}\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{D}_{a}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},0)}^{p}.

For the second term, with
C_{1}>0
such that
2^{1-2p}C_{1}
is an upper bound of
\left|\nabla\eta\right|^{p}
, we have

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\left(\frac{\lambda}{\left|y\right|}\right)^{p}\lambda^{np/q}\int_{2\lambda\left|x\right|\leq\left|y\right|}\left|u(\lambda x+y)\right|^{p}\left|y\right|^{-bp}\mathrm{d}{x}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\left(\frac{\lambda}{\left|y\right|}\right)^{p}\left(\lambda^{n}\int_{2\lambda\left|x\right|\leq\left|y\right|}\left|u(\lambda x+y)\right|^{q}\left|y\right|^{-bq}\mathrm{d}{x}\right)^{p/q}\left(\int_{2\lambda\left|x\right|\leq\left|y\right|}1\mathrm{d}{x}\right)^{p(1+a-b)/n}

by Hölder’s inequality (with Hölder exponent
q/p
and Hölder-conjugate exponent
n/[p(1+a-b)]
). Then, up to a modification of the constant
C>0
, we see that

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\frac{\lambda}{\left|y\right|}\right)^{p(b-a)}\left(\lambda^{n}\int_{2\lambda\left|x\right|\leq\left|y\right|}\left|u(\lambda x+y)\right|^{q}\left|\lambda x+y\right|^{-bq}\mathrm{d}{x}\right)^{p/q}

\displaystyle=C\left(\frac{\lambda}{\left|y\right|}\right)^{p(b-a)}\left(\int_{2\left|z-y\right|\leq\left|y\right|}\left|u(z)\right|^{q}\left|z\right|^{-bq}\mathrm{d}{x}\right)^{p/q}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\frac{\lambda}{\left|y\right|}\right)^{p(b-a)}\left\|u\right\|_{L_{b}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n},0)}^{p}.

Finally, by the CKN inequality (
1.6
), up to another modification of the constant
C>0
, we deduce that

I_{2}\leq C\left(\frac{\lambda}{\left|y\right|}\right)^{p(b-a)}\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{D}_{a}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},0)}^{p}.

Combining this with our inequality for
I_{1}
, we deduce that (possibly adjusting
C>0
again)

\left\|v\right\|_{\mathcal{D}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}\leq C\left(\frac{\lambda}{\left|y\right|}\right)^{p(b-a)}\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{D}_{a}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},0)}^{p}

which completes our proof since
C
is independent of
u,y,\lambda
.
∎

Remark 3.1
.

Inspecting the proof of Lemma
3.1
, it is easy to see that we may instead bound the norms on an arbitrary measurable set
U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Indeed, the very same argument shows that the same constant
C>0
(which is independent of
y,\lambda,U
) satisfies

(3.5)

\displaystyle\left\|\tau_{y,\lambda}u\right\|_{L^{q}(U)}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|u\right\|_{L^{q}_{b}(\lambda U+y,0)}

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla\left[\tau_{y,\lambda}u\right]\right\|_{L^{p}(U)}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\frac{\lambda}{\left|y\right|}\right)^{b-a}\left(\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{p}_{a}(\lambda U+y,0)}+\left\|u\right\|_{L^{q}_{b}(\lambda U+y,0)}\right)

for all
u\in\mathcal{D}_{a}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},0)
.

In line with what we have done thus far, we shall also require a suitable “reverse” transform which will serve as a pseudoinverse for
\tau_{y,\lambda}
. Formally, we define

\tau_{y,\lambda}^{-}:\mathcal{D}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to\mathcal{D}_{a}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},0)

via the rule

(3.6)

\tau_{y,\lambda}^{-}v(z)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\lambda^{-\gamma}v\left(\frac{z-y}{\lambda}\right)&\text{if }a=b=0\\
\displaystyle\left(\frac{\lambda}{\left|y\right|}\right)^{-b}\lambda^{-\gamma}v\left(\frac{z-y}{\lambda}\right)\eta\left(\frac{2(z-y)}{\left|y\right|}\right)&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Obviously, by inspection, it is easy to check that
\tau_{y,\lambda}^{-}
is the proper inverse of
\tau_{y,\lambda}
when
a=b=0
. With this in mind, let us now establish a partial analog of Lemma
3.1
, thereby providing operator bounds related to this pseudoinversion mapping.

Lemma 3.2
.

There exists a constant
C>0
such that, given a point
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\left\{0\right\}
along with some
\lambda>0
, there holds

\displaystyle\left\|\tau^{-}_{y,\lambda}v\right\|_{L^{q}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{n},0)}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|v\right\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\left\|\tau^{-}_{y,\lambda}v\right\|_{\mathcal{D}_{a}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},0)}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\frac{\left|y\right|}{\lambda}\right)^{p(b-a)}\left\|v\right\|_{\mathcal{D}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

for all
v\in\mathcal{D}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Proof.

Once again, the case
a=b=0
is trivial since it follows from homogeneity. Otherwise, as in the proof of Lemma
3.1
with equation (
3.4
), we see that there exists a constant
C>0
independent of
y,\lambda
such that

(3.7)

\left(\frac{\left|\lambda x+y\right|}{\left|y\right|}\right)^{-ap}\leq C,\quad\left(\frac{\left|\lambda x+y\right|}{\left|y\right|}\right)^{-bq}\leq C

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfying
2\lambda\left|x\right|\leq\left|y\right|
. In particular, (
3.7
) is valid for all
x
such that
2\lambda x/\left|y\right|
is in the support of
\eta
.

For an arbitrary
v\in\mathcal{D}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, set
u:=\tau_{y,\lambda}v
. Using that
0\leq\eta\leq 1
we see that

\displaystyle\left\|u\right\|_{L^{q}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{n},0)}^{q}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|u(z)\right|^{q}\left|z\right|^{-bq}\mathrm{d}{z}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{\lambda}{\left|y\right|}\right)^{-bq}\lambda^{-\gamma q}\int_{2\left|z-y\right|\leq\left|y\right|}\left|v\left(\frac{z-y}{\lambda}\right)\right|^{q}\left|z\right|^{-bq}\mathrm{d}{z}

\displaystyle=\int_{2\left|z-y\right|\leq\left|y\right|}\left|v(x)\right|^{q}\left(\frac{\left|\lambda x+y\right|}{\left|y\right|}\right)^{-bq}\mathrm{d}{x}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|v\right\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{q}.

Next, in order to bound the
\mathcal{D}_{a}^{1,p}
norm of
u
, we once again apply the product rule to obtain two terms:

\displaystyle\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{D}_{a}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},0)}^{p}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\nabla u(z)\right|^{p}\left|z\right|^{-ap}\mathrm{d}{z}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{p-1}\left(\frac{\lambda}{\left|y\right|}\right)^{-bp}\lambda^{-n+ap}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\eta\left(\frac{2(z-y)}{\left|y\right|}\right)\nabla v\left(\frac{z-y}{\lambda}\right)\right|^{p}\left|z\right|^{-ap}\mathrm{d}{z}

\displaystyle\quad+2^{p-1+p}\left(\frac{\lambda}{\left|y\right|}\right)^{-bp}\lambda^{-\gamma p}\left|y\right|^{-p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|v\left(\frac{z-y}{\lambda}\right)\nabla\eta\left(\frac{2(z-y)}{\left|y\right|}\right)\right|^{p}\left|z\right|^{-ap}\mathrm{d}{z}

\displaystyle=:I_{1}+I_{2}

where we have used that
(\kappa+\iota)^{p}\leq 2^{p-1}\left(\kappa^{p}+\iota^{p}\right)
for all
\kappa,\iota\geq 0
. Then, after a change of variables, observe that

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{p-1}\left(\frac{\lambda}{\left|y\right|}\right)^{-bp}\lambda^{ap}\int_{2\lambda\left|x\right|\leq\left|y\right|}\left|\nabla v(x)\right|^{p}\left|\lambda x+y\right|^{-ap}\mathrm{d}{z}

\displaystyle\leq C2^{p-1}\left(\frac{\left|y\right|}{\lambda}\right)^{p(b-a)}\left\|v\right\|_{\mathcal{D}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}.

Similarly, with
C_{1}>0
such that
2^{1-2p}C_{1}
is an upper bound of
\left|\nabla\eta\right|^{p}
we see that

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\left(\frac{\lambda}{\left|y\right|}\right)^{-bp}\lambda^{ap+p}\left|y\right|^{-p}\int_{2\lambda\left|x\right|\leq\left|y\right|}\left|v\left(x\right)\right|^{p}\left|\lambda x+y\right|^{-ap}\mathrm{d}{x}

\displaystyle\leq CC_{1}\left(\frac{\lambda}{\left|y\right|}\right)^{p(1+a-b)}\int_{2\lambda\left|x\right|\leq\left|y\right|}\left|v\left(x\right)\right|^{p}\mathrm{d}{x}.

Then, by Hölder’s inequality (with exponent
p^{\ast}/p
and Hölder-conjugate exponent
n/p
),

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle\leq CC_{1}2^{p}\left(\frac{\lambda}{\left|y\right|}\right)^{p(1+a-b)}\left\|v\right\|_{L^{p^{\ast}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}\left(\int_{2\lambda\left|x\right|\leq\left|y\right|}1\right)^{p/n}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\frac{\left|y\right|}{\lambda}\right)^{p(b-a)}\left\|v\right\|_{L^{p^{\ast}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}

up to an adjustment of the constant
C
in the second inequality. We then conclude our proof via an application of the Gagliardo-Nirenberg-Sobolev inequality (i.e. the CKN inequality (
1.6
) with
a=b=0
) and combining our bounds for
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

Let now
U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be Lebesgue measurable. As was noted for the forward transform, we observe that an identical argument exhibits the existence of a constant
C>0
(independent of
y,\lambda,U
) such that

(3.8)

\displaystyle\left\|\tau^{-}_{y,\lambda}v\right\|_{L^{q}_{b}(U,0)}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|v\right\|_{L^{q}([U-y]/\lambda)}

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla\left[\tau^{-}_{y,\lambda}v\right]\right\|_{L^{p}_{a}(U,0)}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\frac{\left|y\right|}{\lambda}\right)^{p(b-a)}\left(\left\|v\right\|_{L^{q}([U-y]/\lambda)}+\left\|\nabla v\right\|_{L^{p}([U-y]/\lambda)}\right)

for all
v\in\mathcal{D}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

3.1.1.
Transformations and the
L^{q}
-norm

As was shown, the transform
\tau_{y,\lambda}
is particularly well behaved with respect to the
L^{q}
-norm. It turns out that when
\left|y\right|/\lambda
is large, the
L^{q}
-norm of the transform remains
almost
unchanged on a set where
\eta
acts as the identity. More precisely, we have the following result:

Lemma 3.3
.

There exists a constant
C>0
such that, for any
u\in\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{a}(\mathbb{R}^{n},0)
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\left\{0\right\}
,
\lambda>0
such that
\left|y\right|\geq 4\lambda
, the remainder
R
in

\left\|\tau_{y,\lambda}u\right\|_{L^{q}(B(0,1))}^{q}=\left\|u\right\|_{L^{q}_{b}(B(y,\lambda),0)}^{q}+R

satisfies

\left|R\right|\leq C\frac{\lambda}{\left|y\right|}\left\|u\right\|_{L^{q}_{b}(B(y,\lambda),0)}^{q}

Before presenting the proof, we recall that Lemma
3.1
and
3.2
relied on
\eta
in order to restrict our support; then, on this set, we utilized uniform bounds. Estimates of this form will be of repeated use and are thus presented independently as follows:

Lemma 3.4
.

There exists a constant
C>0
such that

(a)

Given
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\left\{0\right\}
and
\lambda>0
,

\frac{1}{C}\leq\frac{\left|\lambda x+y\right|}{\left|y\right|}\leq C\quad\text{for all }x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\text{ such that }\left|x\right|\leq\frac{\left|y\right|}{2\lambda}.

(b)

Given
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\left\{0\right\}
,
\lambda>0
and any bounded set
K
such that
\rho:=\sup_{x\in K}\left|x\right|
satisfies
4\lambda\rho\leq\left|y\right|
, there holds

\frac{\left|\left|y\right|^{-ap}-\left|\lambda x+y\right|^{-ap}\right|}{\left|y\right|^{-ap}}\leq C\rho\frac{\lambda}{\left|y\right|},\qquad\frac{\left|\left|y\right|^{-bq}-\left|\lambda x+y\right|^{-bq}\right|}{\left|y\right|^{-bq}}\leq C\rho\frac{\lambda}{\left|y\right|}

for all
x\in K
.

Proof.

The first inequality follows from the reverse and usual triangle inequalities. Next, let
y,\lambda,K,\rho
be as in
(b)
. Given an arbitrary
x\in K
write
z:=\lambda x+y
and observe that

\left|z\right|\leq\left|y\right|+\lambda\left|x\right|\leq\left(1+\frac{\lambda\rho}{\left|y\right|}\right)\left|y\right|.

Using also the reverse triangle inequality, we see that

\left(1-\varepsilon\right)\left|y\right|\leq\left|z\right|\leq\left(1+\varepsilon\right)\left|y\right|

where
\varepsilon:=\lambda\rho/\left|y\right|\leq 1/4
. From this, we further deduce that

\left|\left|y\right|^{-ap}-\left|z\right|^{-ap}\right|\leq\left|1-\left(1+\varepsilon_{\pm}\right)^{-ap}\right|\left|y\right|^{-ap}

where
\varepsilon_{\pm}
is either
\varepsilon
or
-\varepsilon
. It then follows from the Mean Value Theorem that

\left|\left|y\right|^{-ap}-\left|z\right|^{-ap}\right|\leq\left|ap\right|\left(1+\delta\right)^{-ap-1}\varepsilon\left|y\right|^{-ap}

for some
\delta
in
[-\varepsilon,\varepsilon]
. From this last equation and recalling that
\left|\delta\right|\leq\varepsilon\leq 1/4
, we see that there exists a constant
C>0
independent of
y,\lambda
such that

\frac{\left|\left|y\right|^{-ap}-\left|z\right|^{-ap}\right|}{\left|y\right|^{-ap}}\leq C\varepsilon,\qquad\forall z\in\lambda K+y.

Analogously, modifying
C>0
if necessary, we may establish that

\frac{\left|\left|y\right|^{-bq}-\left|z\right|^{-bq}\right|}{\left|y\right|^{-bq}}\leq C\varepsilon,\qquad\forall z\in\lambda K+y.

∎

With this, we are ready to prove Lemma
3.3

Proof of Lemma
3.3
.

Let
u\in\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{a}(\mathbb{R}^{n},0)
,
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\left\{0\right\}
, and
\lambda>0
such that
\left|y\right|\geq 4\lambda
. Properties of
\eta
imply that

\int_{B(0,1)}\left|\tau_{y,\lambda}u\right|^{q}\mathrm{d}{x}=\lambda^{n}\int_{B(0,1)}\left|u\left(\lambda x+y\right)\right|^{q}\left|y\right|^{-bq}\mathrm{d}{x}=\int_{B(y,\lambda)}\left|u\left(z\right)\right|^{q}\left|y\right|^{-bq}\mathrm{d}{z},

where we have used a change of variables in this last equality. Therefore,

\left\|\tau_{y,\lambda}u\right\|_{L^{q}(B(0,1))}^{q}-\left\|u\right\|_{L^{q}_{b}(B(y,\lambda),0)}^{q}=\int_{B(y,\lambda)}\left|u\left(z\right)\right|^{q}\left(\left|y\right|^{-bq}-\left|z\right|^{-bq}\right)\mathrm{d}{z}.

Then, using both parts of Lemma
3.4
, we infer that there is a constant
C>0
independent of
y,\lambda,u
such that

\displaystyle\left|\left\|\tau_{y,\lambda}u\right\|_{L^{q}(B(0,1))}^{q}-\left\|u\right\|_{L^{q}_{b}(B(y,\lambda),0)}^{q}\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{\lambda}{\left|y\right|}\int_{B(y,\lambda)}\left|u(z)\right|^{q}\left|z\right|^{-bq}\mathrm{d}{z}

which concludes our proof.
∎

3.2.
Locality

A useful property of the transforms we introduced is, when
a=b
, the ability to restrict domains. More specifically, we have the following technical result.

Lemma 3.5
.

Given
v\in\mathcal{D}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, if
a=b
then

\left\|\tau^{-}_{y,\lambda}v\right\|_{L_{b}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B(y,\left|y\right|/4))}\to 0,\quad\text{and}\quad\left\|\nabla\left[\tau^{-}_{y,\lambda}v\right]\right\|_{L_{a}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B(y,\left|y\right|/4))}\to 0

as
\left|y\right|/\lambda\to\infty
.

Proof.

This result follows almost immediately from Remark
3.2
. Indeed, there is a constant
C>0
independent of
\lambda,y,v
such that

\left\|\tau^{-}_{y,\lambda}v\right\|_{L^{q}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B(y,\left|y\right|/4))}\leq C\left\|v\right\|_{L^{q}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B\left(0,\frac{\left|y\right|}{4\lambda}\right)\right)}.

Now, since
a=b
we note that the Sobolev exponent is

p^{\ast}=\frac{np}{n-p}=q.

Therefore,
v\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and, as
\left|y\right|/\lambda\to\infty
, the dominated convergence theorem implies that

\left\|\tau^{-}_{y,\lambda}v\right\|_{L^{q}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B(y,\left|y\right|/4))}\leq C\left\|v\right\|_{L^{q}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B\left(0,\frac{\left|y\right|}{4\lambda}\right)\right)}\to 0.

Similarly, since
a=b
, Remark
3.2
allows us to write

\left\|\nabla\left[\tau^{-}_{y,\lambda}v\right]\right\|_{L_{a}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B(y,\left|y\right|/4))}\leq C\left(\left\|v\right\|_{L^{q}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B\left(0,\frac{\left|y\right|}{4\lambda}\right)\right)}+\left\|\nabla v\right\|_{L^{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B\left(0,\frac{\left|y\right|}{4\lambda}\right)\right)}\right)

for some
C>0
independent of
y,\lambda,v
. Once again, the dominated convergence theorem implies that the right-hand side tends to
0
as
\left|y\right|/\lambda\to\infty
.
∎

4.
Convergence of Domains

As in the previous sections, we continue to assume that
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
denotes a bounded domain containing the origin.
Throughout this section, we fix sequences
(y_{\alpha})
in
\overline{\Omega}
and
\lambda_{\alpha}
in
(0,\infty)
satisfying some combination of the following properties:

(P1)

\lambda_{\alpha}\to 0
;

(P2)

The ratio converges in the sense that, for some
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\frac{y_{\alpha}}{\lambda_{\alpha}}\to x_{0}.

(P3)

The ratio diverges:

\frac{\left|y_{\alpha}\right|}{\lambda_{\alpha}}\to\infty,

(P4)

The sequence
(y_{\alpha})
approaches the boundary at rate no less than the rescaling factor; i.e. the following ratio is bounded:

\frac{\operatorname{dist}(y_{\alpha},\partial\Omega)}{\lambda_{\alpha}}.

(P5)

The sequence
(y_{\alpha})
approaches the boundary at a rate slower than the rescaling factor; i.e.

\frac{\operatorname{dist}(y_{\alpha},\partial\Omega)}{\lambda_{\alpha}}\to\infty.

The goal of this section is to study the limiting behaviour of specific sequences of sets. First, we describe the sense in which we will consider the convergence of sets.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
(U_{\alpha})
be a sequence of subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We say that
(U_{\alpha})
converges to
U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
and write
U_{\alpha}\to U
provided

(a)

Any compact subset of
U
is contained in
U_{\alpha}
for all
\alpha
large.

(b)

Any compact subset of
\left(\overline{U}\right)^{\mathtt{C}}
is contained in
\left(\overline{U_{\alpha}}\right)^{\mathtt{C}}\subseteq U_{\alpha}^{\mathtt{C}}
for all
\alpha
large.

Now, suppose condition
(P2)
holds. Then we consider the following deformations of
\Omega
:

\Omega_{\alpha}=\frac{\Omega}{\lambda_{\alpha}}-x_{0}.

Clearly, these sets are approximations of
(\Omega-y_{\alpha})/\lambda_{\alpha}
. Furthermore, we have the following result:

Proposition 4.1
.

Suppose the sequence
(\lambda_{\alpha})
converges. Then, we have
\Omega_{\alpha}\to\Omega_{\infty}
where

\Omega_{\infty}=\begin{cases}\mathbb{R}^{n}&\text{if condition \ref{it:lambda=0} holds, i.e. }\lambda_{\alpha}\to 0\\
\dfrac{\Omega}{\lambda}-x_{0}&\text{if }\lambda_{\alpha}\to\lambda>0.\end{cases}

Proof.

We first treat the case where
\lambda_{\alpha}\to 0
. Since
\Omega
is an open set containing the origin, we have
B(0,\delta)\subseteq\Omega
for some
\delta>0
. It readily follows that

B\left(-x_{0},\frac{\delta}{\lambda_{\alpha}}\right)\subseteq\Omega_{\alpha}

for every index
\alpha
. Then, for an arbitrary compact set
K
, using
\lambda_{\alpha}\to 0
we see that

K\subseteq B\left(-x_{0},\frac{\delta}{\lambda_{\alpha}}\right)\subseteq\Omega_{\alpha}

for all
\alpha
large. Hence,
\Omega_{\alpha}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Let us now assume that we are in the case
\lambda_{\alpha}\to\lambda>0
and fix a compact set
K\subseteq\Omega_{\infty}
. Then,
\lambda(K+x_{0})
is a compact subset of
\Omega
. Since
\Omega
is open, the Lebesgue Number Lemma guarantees the existence of
\varepsilon>0
such that
B(x,\varepsilon)\subseteq\Omega
for all
x\in\lambda(K+x_{0})
. Put otherwise, for all
z\in K
there holds

B\left(\frac{\lambda}{\lambda_{\alpha}}(z+x_{0})-x_{0},\frac{\varepsilon}{\lambda_{\alpha}}\right)\subseteq\Omega_{\alpha}.

On the other hand, since
K+x_{0}
is compact, it is bounded by some
M>0
. Then, for any
z\in K
, if
\alpha
is such that

\left|\lambda_{\alpha}-\lambda\right|<\frac{\varepsilon}{M}

then

\left|z-\left(\frac{\lambda}{\lambda_{\alpha}}(z+x_{0})-x_{0}\right)\right|=\frac{1}{\lambda_{\alpha}}\left|\lambda-\lambda_{\alpha}\right|\left|z+x_{0}\right|<\frac{\varepsilon}{\lambda_{\alpha}}.

Since
\lambda_{\alpha}\to\lambda>0
, it follows that for all
\alpha
sufficiently large we have

K\subseteq B\left(\frac{\lambda}{\lambda_{\alpha}}(z+x_{0})-x_{0},\frac{\varepsilon}{\lambda_{\alpha}}\right)\subseteq\Omega_{\alpha}.

Finally, given a compact set
K\subseteq\left(\overline{\Omega_{\infty}}\right)^{\mathtt{C}}
, the same argument shows that
K\subseteq\overline{\Omega_{\alpha}}^{\mathtt{C}}
for all
\alpha
large. We once again conclude that
\Omega_{\alpha}\to\Omega_{\infty}
.
∎

Next, suppose condition
(P3)
holds. Then we will be interested in the following deformations of
\Omega
:

\Omega_{\alpha}=\frac{\Omega-y_{\alpha}}{\lambda_{\alpha}}.

In this case, if condition
(P1)
holds then we have the following result:

Proposition 4.2
.

Suppose
\Omega
has
C^{1}
boundary. If
(P1)
holds, i.e.
\lambda_{\alpha}\to 0
, then, up to a subsequence,
\Omega_{\alpha}:=(\Omega-y_{\alpha})/\lambda_{\alpha}
tends to a set
\Omega_{\infty}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

(1)

If the distance between
y_{\alpha}
and the boundary tends to
0
no slower than
(\lambda_{\alpha})
, i.e. if condition
(P4)
holds, then
\Omega_{\infty}
is a half-space.

(2)

On the other hand, if condition
(P5)
holds then
\Omega_{\infty}=\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

This result follows from a straightforward adaption of El-Hamidi-Vétois
[
23
, Proposition 2.4]
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Suppose
\Omega_{\alpha}\to\Omega_{\infty}
, with
\Omega_{\infty}
being either
\mathbb{R}^{n}
or a half space. Let
(v_{\alpha})
in
\mathcal{D}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be a sequence converging to some
v\in\mathcal{D}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
pointwise almost everywhere. If
v_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{D}^{1,p}(\Omega_{\alpha})
for each
\alpha
then
v\in\mathcal{D}^{1,p}(\Omega_{\infty})
.

Proof.

If
\Omega_{\infty}=\mathbb{R}^{n}
there is nothing to show. Otherwise, up to a translation, there exists a unit vector
\nu
such that

\Omega_{\infty}=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:x\cdot\nu>0\right\}.

Now, observe that
\phi_{\varepsilon}(x):=v(x-\varepsilon\nu)
converges to
v
in
\mathcal{D}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
as
\varepsilon\searrow 0
by standard results. Therefore, if we can show that
\phi_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{D}^{1,p}(\Omega_{\infty})
for each
\varepsilon>0
then we must have
v\in\mathcal{D}^{1,p}(\Omega_{\infty})
which completes the proof.

To this end, we first show that
v
vanishes outside of
\overline{\Omega_{\infty}}
by showing that
v
vanishes on every compact set
K
such that
K\cap\overline{\Omega_{\infty}}=\varnothing
. Indeed, if
K
is compact and does not intersect
\overline{\Omega_{\infty}}
then, since
\Omega_{\alpha}\to\Omega_{\infty}
, we see that
K\cap\overline{\Omega_{\alpha}}=\varnothing
for all
\alpha
large. Thus,
v_{\alpha}
vanishes on
K
for each
\alpha
large. Since
v_{\alpha}\to v
pointwise almost everywhere, it follows that
v
vanishes on
K
.

Having shown that
v
is supported on
\overline{\Omega_{\infty}}
, it readily follows that, every
\phi_{\varepsilon}
is supported on

\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:x\cdot\nu\geq\varepsilon\right\}.

Now, in order to prove that
\phi_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{D}^{1,p}(\Omega_{\infty})
we show that, for any
\delta>0
, we may find a function in
C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega_{\infty})
whose
\mathcal{D}^{1,p}
-distance to
\phi_{\varepsilon}
is less than
\delta
. With this in mind, let
\delta>0
be given and set
\zeta\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n};[0,1])
to be a smooth function of compact support with
\zeta\equiv 1
on
B(0,1)
. Then, for any
h_{1}>0
, define

\zeta_{h_{1}}(x):=\zeta(h_{1}x)

so that
\phi_{\varepsilon}\zeta_{h_{1}}\in\mathcal{D}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. By the product rule and using that
(\iota+\kappa)^{p}\leq 2^{p-1}(\iota^{p}+\kappa^{p})
for all
\iota,\kappa\geq 0
,

(4.1)

\left\|\phi_{\varepsilon}-\zeta_{h_{1}}\phi_{\varepsilon}\right\|^{p}_{\mathcal{D}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\\
\leq 2^{p-1}\int_{\left|x\right|\geq 1/h_{1}}\left|\nabla\phi_{\varepsilon}\right|^{p}\mathrm{d}{x}+2^{p-1}\int_{\left|x\right|\geq 1/h_{1}}\left|\phi_{\varepsilon}\nabla\zeta_{h_{1}}\right|^{p}\mathrm{d}{x}.

The first term on the right-hand side tends to
0
as
h_{1}\searrow 0
. As for the second term, by Hölder’s inequality with exponent
q/p
and conjugate exponent
n/[p(1+a-b)]
we may write

\displaystyle\int_{\left|x\right|\geq h_{1}}\left|\phi_{\varepsilon}\nabla\zeta_{h_{1}}\right|^{p}\mathrm{d}{x}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{\left|x\right|\geq 1/h_{1}}\left|\phi_{\varepsilon}\right|^{q}\mathrm{d}{x}\right)^{p/q}\left(\int_{\left|x\right|\geq 1/h_{1}}\left|\nabla\zeta_{h_{1}}\right|^{\frac{n}{1+a-b}}\mathrm{d}{x}\right)^{p(1+a-b)/n}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{\left|x\right|\geq 1/h_{1}}\left|\phi_{\varepsilon}\right|^{q}\mathrm{d}{x}\right)^{p/q}\left(h_{1}^{\frac{n}{1+a-b}-n}\int_{\left|y\right|\geq 1}\left|\nabla\zeta\right|^{\frac{n}{1+a-b}}\mathrm{d}{y}\right)^{p(1+a-b)/n}

by a change of variables. Thus, we see that the right-hand side tends to
0
as
h_{1}\searrow 0
. Assembling, we deduce that both terms on the right-hand side of equation (
4.1
) tend to
0
as
h_{1}\searrow 0
. Thus, we may select
h_{1}>0
sufficiently small so that

\left\|\phi_{\varepsilon}-\zeta_{h_{1}}\phi_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{\mathcal{D}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}<\frac{\delta}{2}.

Now, for any
0<h\ll\varepsilon
, observe that the mollification
\left(\zeta_{h_{1}}\phi_{\varepsilon}\right)_{h}
is in
C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega_{\infty})
. Then, by standard mollification results,
we may select
h>0
sufficiently small so that

\left\|\zeta_{h_{1}}\phi_{\varepsilon}-(\zeta_{h_{1}}\phi_{\varepsilon})_{h}\right\|_{\mathcal{D}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}<\frac{\delta}{2}.

By the triangle inequality,
\left\|\phi_{\varepsilon}-(\zeta_{h_{1}}\phi_{\varepsilon})_{h}\right\|_{\mathcal{D}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}<\delta
and we may conclude that
\phi_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{D}^{1,p}(\Omega_{\infty})
, as desired.
∎

5.
Energy and the New Transforms

We continue to assume that
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
denotes a bounded domain containing the origin and fix sequences
(y_{\alpha})
in
\overline{\Omega}
and
\lambda_{\alpha}
in
(0,\infty)
. As in the previous section, the sequences
(y_{\alpha}),(\lambda_{\alpha})
will satisfy some combination of properties
(P1)
-
(P5)
.

In this section, we investigate how the new transforms
\tau_{y,\lambda},\tau_{y,\lambda}^{-}
interact with the energy functionals
\phi
and
\phi_{\infty}
. First, observe that if
a=b=0
, a change of variables shows that

\left\langle\phi_{\infty}^{\prime}(\tau_{y,\lambda}u),h\right\rangle=\left\langle\phi^{\prime}(u),\tau_{y,\lambda}^{-}h\right\rangle.

for any
h\in\mathcal{D}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. More generally, when
a=b
, the difference between terms on either side of the equality will be negligible provided
\left|y\right|/\lambda
is large. This is made precise via the following result:

Lemma 5.1
.

If
a=b
, there exists a constant
C>0
such that the remainder

R:=\left\langle\phi_{\infty}^{\prime}(\tau_{y,\lambda}u),h\right\rangle-\left\langle\phi^{\prime}(u),\tau_{y,\lambda}^{-}h\right\rangle

satisfies

\left|R\right|\leq C\rho\frac{\lambda}{\left|y\right|}\left(\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{D}_{a}^{1,p}(\Omega,0)}^{p-1}\left\|h\right\|_{\mathcal{D}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\left\|u\right\|_{L_{b}^{q}(\Omega,0)}^{q-1}\left\|h\right\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\right)

for any
u\in\mathcal{D}_{a}^{1,p}(\Omega,0)
,
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\left\{0\right\}
,
\lambda>0
and
h\in\mathcal{D}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with support
K
satisfying

(5.1)

\rho:=\sup_{x\in K}\left|x\right|\leq\frac{\left|y\right|}{4\lambda},\qquad K\subseteq\frac{\Omega-y}{\lambda}.

In particular, suppose
\Omega_{\infty}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
is such that
\frac{\Omega-y_{\alpha}}{\lambda_{\alpha}}=:\Omega_{\alpha}\to\Omega_{\infty}
. Let
(u_{\alpha})
be a bounded sequence in
\mathcal{D}_{a}^{1,p}(\Omega,0)
and assume property
(P3)
holds (i.e.
\left|y_{\alpha}\right|/\lambda_{\alpha}\to\infty
). Then, given a fixed compact set
K\subseteq\Omega_{\infty}
,

\left\langle\phi_{\infty}^{\prime}(\tau_{y_{\alpha},\lambda_{\alpha}}u_{\alpha}),h\right\rangle-\left\langle\phi^{\prime}(u_{\alpha}),\tau_{y_{\alpha},\lambda_{\alpha}}^{-}h\right\rangle\to 0

uniformly over
h\in\left\{\mathcal{D}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):\supp(h)\subseteq K\right\}
as
\alpha\to\infty
.

Proof.

Let
u\in\mathcal{D}_{a}^{1,p}(\Omega,0)
,
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\left\{0\right\}
,
\lambda>0
and suppose
h\in\mathcal{D}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
has support
K
satisfying (
5.1
). First, notice that

R=\left\langle\phi_{\infty}^{\prime}(\tau_{y,\lambda}u),h\right\rangle-\left\langle\phi^{\prime}(u),\tau_{y,\lambda}^{-}h\right\rangle

is well defined since
\lambda K+y\subseteq\Omega
implies that
\tau_{y,\lambda}^{-}h\in\mathcal{D}_{a}^{1,p}(\Omega,0)
. Moreover, because
\rho\leq\left|y\right|/(4\lambda)
, any
x\in K=\operatorname{supp}(h)
satisfies

(5.2)

\left|\frac{2\lambda x}{\left|y\right|}\right|\leq\frac{1}{2}\implies\eta\left(\frac{2\lambda x}{\left|y\right|}\right)=1

Using this , we see that

\displaystyle\left\langle\phi_{\infty}^{\prime}(\tau_{y,\lambda}u),h\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\lambda}{\left|y\right|}\right)^{b(p-1)}\lambda^{(\gamma+1)(p-1)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\nabla u(\lambda x+y)\right|^{p-2}\left(\nabla u(\lambda x+y),\nabla h(x)\right)\mathrm{d}{x}

\displaystyle\quad-\left(\frac{\lambda}{\left|y\right|}\right)^{b(q-1)}\lambda^{\gamma(q-1)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|u(\lambda x+y)\right|^{q-2}u(\lambda x+y)h(x)\mathrm{d}{x}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\lambda}{\left|y\right|}\right)^{b(p-1)}\lambda^{(\gamma+1)(p-1)-n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\nabla u(z)\right|^{p-2}\left(\nabla u(z),\nabla h\left(\frac{z-y}{\lambda}\right)\right)\mathrm{d}{z}

\displaystyle\quad-\left(\frac{\lambda}{\left|y\right|}\right)^{b(q-1)}\lambda^{\gamma(q-1)-n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|u(z)\right|^{q-2}u(z)h\left(\frac{z-y}{\lambda}\right)\mathrm{d}{z}.

Using equation (
5.2
), but with
x=(z-y)/\lambda
, yields that

\displaystyle\left\langle\phi_{\infty}^{\prime}(\tau_{y,\lambda}u),h\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\lambda}{\left|y\right|}\right)^{bp}\lambda^{(\gamma+1)p-n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\nabla u(z)\right|^{p-2}\left(\nabla u(z),\nabla\left[\tau^{-}_{y,\lambda}h\right](z)\right)\mathrm{d}{z}

\displaystyle\quad-\left(\frac{\lambda}{\left|y\right|}\right)^{bq}\lambda^{\gamma q-n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|u(z)\right|^{q-2}u(z)\left[\tau^{-}_{y,\lambda}h\right](z)\mathrm{d}{z}

where
u
is extended by
0
outside of
\Omega
. Simplifying and re-arranging, we obtain

\displaystyle\left\langle\phi_{\infty}^{\prime}(\tau_{y,\lambda}u),h\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\lambda}{\left|y\right|}\right)^{p(b-a)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\nabla u(z)\right|^{p-2}\left(\nabla u(z),\nabla\left[\tau^{-}_{y,\lambda}h\right](z)\right)\left|y\right|^{-ap}\mathrm{d}{z}

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|u(z)\right|^{q-2}u(z)\left[\tau^{-}_{y,\lambda}h\right](z)\left|y\right|^{-bq}\mathrm{d}{z}

With this, observe that since
a=b

\displaystyle R:=

\displaystyle\left\langle\phi_{\infty}^{\prime}(\tau_{y,\lambda}u),h\right\rangle-\left\langle\phi^{\prime}(u),\tau_{y,\lambda}^{-}h\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\nabla u(z)\right|^{p-2}\left(\nabla u(z),\nabla\left[\tau^{-}_{y,\lambda}h\right](z)\right)\left(\left|y\right|^{-ap}-\left|z\right|^{-ap}\right)\mathrm{d}{z}

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|u(z)\right|^{q-2}u(z)\left[\tau^{-}_{y,\lambda}h\right](z)\left(\left|y\right|^{-bq}-\left|z\right|^{-bq}\right)\mathrm{d}{z}.

Then, using Lemma
3.4
, we see that there is a constant
C>0
independent of
y,\lambda,u,K
such that

\displaystyle\left|R\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\nabla u(z)\right|^{p-1}\left|\nabla\left[\tau^{-}_{y,\lambda}h\right](z)\right|\left|z\right|^{-ap}\mathrm{d}{z}

\displaystyle\quad+C\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|u(z)\right|^{q-1}\left|\left[\tau^{-}_{y,\lambda}h\right](z)\right|\left|z\right|^{-bq}\mathrm{d}{z}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon\left(\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{D}_{a}^{1,p}(\Omega,0)}^{p-1}\left\|\tau_{y,\lambda}^{-}h\right\|_{\mathcal{D}_{a}^{1,p}(\Omega,0)}+\left\|u\right\|_{L_{b}^{q}(\Omega,0)}^{q-1}\left\|\tau_{y,\lambda}^{-}h\right\|_{L_{b}^{q}(\Omega,0)}\right)

where
\varepsilon:=\lambda\rho/\left|y\right|
and the second inequalities follows from Hölder’s inequality. We note that, for the last line, we have implicitly used that
u
and
\tau_{y,\lambda}^{-}h
are in
\mathcal{D}_{a}^{1,p}(\Omega,0)
and extended by
0
to the rest of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then, by Lemma
3.2
, with a possible modification of the constant
C>0
we deduce that

\left|R\right|\leq C\varepsilon\left(\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{D}_{a}^{1,p}(\Omega,0)}^{p-1}\left\|h\right\|_{\mathcal{D}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\left\|u\right\|_{L_{b}^{q}(\Omega,0)}^{q-1}\left\|h\right\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\right)

where we have once again used that
a=b
. Finally,the second part of the Lemma follows from this last inequality and the CKN-inequality (
1.6
).
∎

In this last lemma, we studied how the composition
\phi_{\infty}^{\prime}\circ\tau_{y,\lambda}
behaves by testing against a fixed function
h
. In a similar vein, with the aims of recovering a
(PS)
-sequence after subtracting bubbles, let us now analyze the behaviour of
\phi_{\infty}^{\prime}
when tested against transformed functions:

Lemma 5.2
.

Suppose
\Omega_{\alpha}=(\Omega-y_{\alpha})/\lambda_{\alpha}
tends to
\Omega_{\infty}
, with
\Omega_{\infty}
being either a half-space of all of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. If
a=b
,
(P3)
holds and
v\in\mathcal{D}^{1,p}(\Omega_{\infty})
is a critical point of
\phi_{\infty}
in
\Omega_{\infty}
, i.e.

\left\langle\phi^{\prime}_{\infty}(v),h\right\rangle=0
for all
h\in\mathcal{D}^{1,p}(\Omega_{\infty}),

then

\left\langle\phi^{\prime}_{\infty}(v),\tau_{y_{\alpha},\lambda_{\alpha}}g\right\rangle=o\left(\left\|g\right\|_{\mathcal{D}_{a}^{1,p}(\Omega,0)}\right)\quad\text{as}\quad\alpha\to\infty.

over
g\in\mathcal{D}_{a}^{1,p}(\Omega,0)
.

Proof.

By way of contradiction, suppose the lemma is false. Then there exists
\varepsilon>0
and a subsequence such that, for each
\alpha
, we may select
g_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{D}_{a}^{1,p}(\Omega,0)
satisfying

\left|\left\langle\phi_{\infty}^{\prime}(v),\tau_{y_{\alpha},\lambda_{\alpha}}g_{\alpha}\right\rangle\right|\geq\varepsilon\left\|g_{\alpha}\right\|_{\mathcal{D}_{a}^{1,p}(\Omega,0)}

Relabeling each
g_{\alpha}
to be the rescaled
g_{\alpha}/\left\|g_{\alpha}\right\|_{\mathcal{D}_{a}^{1,p}(\Omega,0)}
, we obtain

(5.3)

\left|\left\langle\phi_{\infty}^{\prime}(v),\tau_{y_{\alpha},\lambda_{\alpha}}g_{\alpha}\right\rangle\right|\geq\varepsilon

for all
\alpha
where
g_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{D}_{a}^{1,p}(\Omega,0)
has unit norm.

On the other hand, since
a=b
, it follows from Lemma
3.1
that
\tau_{y_{\alpha},\lambda_{\alpha}}g_{\alpha}
is a bounded sequence in
\mathcal{D}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Therefore, a subsequence of
(\tau_{y_{\alpha},\lambda_{\alpha}}g_{\alpha})
converges weakly in
\mathcal{D}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and pointwise almost everywhere to some function
h\in\mathcal{D}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. By Lemma
4.3
, in fact,
h\in\mathcal{D}^{1,p}(\Omega_{\infty})
. Then, by weak convergence and using that
v
is a critical point of
\phi_{\infty}
in
\Omega_{\infty}
, we have

\left\langle\phi_{\infty}^{\prime}(v),\tau_{y_{\alpha},\lambda_{\alpha}}g_{\alpha}\right\rangle\to\left\langle\phi_{\infty}^{\prime}(v),h\right\rangle=0

However, this contradict equation (
5.3
).
∎

5.1.
Locality

Due to the nature of the transform
\tau_{y,\lambda}
, restricting domains may have negligible effect on norms (recall Lemma
3.5
). In relation to the energy functional
\phi_{\infty}
, a similar phenomenon can be observed:

Lemma 5.3
.

Fix
v\in\mathcal{D}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

a=b
and property
(P3)
holds (i.e.
\left|y_{\alpha}\right|/\lambda_{\alpha}\to\infty

\displaystyle R_{g}(y_{\alpha},\lambda_{\alpha}):=\left\langle\phi_{\infty}^{\prime}(v),\tau_{y_{\alpha},\lambda_{\alpha}}g\right\rangle-\Bigg(

\displaystyle\int_{\left|x\right|\leq\frac{\left|y_{\alpha}\right|}{4\lambda_{\alpha}}}\left|\nabla v\right|^{p-2}\left(\nabla v,\nabla\left[\tau_{y_{\alpha},\lambda_{\alpha}}g\right]\right)\left(\frac{\left|\lambda_{\alpha}x+y_{\alpha}\right|}{\left|y_{\alpha}\right|}\right)^{-ap}\mathrm{d}{x}

For any

\displaystyle R_{g}(y_{\alpha},\lambda_{\alpha})

\displaystyle=\int_{\left|x\right|\leq\frac{\left|y_{\alpha}\right|}{4\lambda_{\alpha}}}\left|\nabla v\right|^{p-2}\left(\nabla v,\nabla\left[\tau_{y_{\alpha},\lambda_{\alpha}}g\right]\right)\left(\frac{\left|y_{\alpha}\right|^{-ap}-\left|\lambda_{\alpha}x+y_{\alpha}\right|^{-ap}}{\left|y_{\alpha}\right|^{-ap}}\right)\mathrm{d}{x}

\displaystyle\qquad-\int_{\left|x\right|\leq\frac{\left|y_{\alpha}\right|}{4\lambda_{\alpha}}}\left|v\right|^{q-2}v\left[\tau_{y_{\alpha},\lambda_{\alpha}}g\right]\left(\frac{\left|y_{\alpha}\right|^{-bq}-\left|\lambda_{\alpha}x+y_{\alpha}\right|^{-bq}}{\left|y_{\alpha}\right|^{-bq}}\right)\mathrm{d}{x}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad-\int_{\left|x\right|>\frac{\left|y_{\alpha}\right|}{4\lambda_{\alpha}}}\left|v\right|^{q-2}v\left[\tau_{y_{\alpha},\lambda_{\alpha}}g\right]\mathrm{d}{x}

\displaystyle=:I_{a}^{(1)}+I_{a}^{(2)}-I_{b}^{(1)}-I_{b}^{(2)}.

For all
\alpha
sufficiently large (so that
\left|y_{\alpha}\right|/\lambda_{\alpha}>16
), we may split

\displaystyle I_{a}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=\int_{\left|x\right|\leq\sqrt{\frac{\left|y_{\alpha}\right|}{\lambda_{\alpha}}}}\left|\nabla v\right|^{p-2}\left(\nabla v,\nabla\left[\tau_{y_{\alpha},\lambda_{\alpha}}g\right]\right)\left(\frac{\left|y_{\alpha}\right|^{-ap}-\left|\lambda_{\alpha}x+y_{\alpha}\right|^{-ap}}{\left|y_{\alpha}\right|^{-ap}}\right)\mathrm{d}{x}

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\sqrt{\frac{\left|y_{\alpha}\right|}{\lambda_{\alpha}}}<\left|x\right|\leq\frac{\left|y_{\alpha}\right|}{4\lambda_{\alpha}}}\left|\nabla v\right|^{p-2}\left(\nabla v,\nabla\left[\tau_{y_{\alpha},\lambda_{\alpha}}g\right]\right)\left(\frac{\left|y_{\alpha}\right|^{-ap}-\left|\lambda_{\alpha}x+y_{\alpha}\right|^{-ap}}{\left|y_{\alpha}\right|^{-ap}}\right)\mathrm{d}{x}

\displaystyle=:I_{a}^{(1.1)}+I_{a}^{(1.2)}

It follows from Lemma
3.4
-
(b)
with

that, for a constant

\displaystyle\left|I_{a}^{(1.1)}\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\sqrt{\frac{\left|y_{\alpha}\right|}{\lambda_{\alpha}}}\frac{\lambda_{\alpha}}{\left|y_{\alpha}\right|}\int_{\left|x\right|\leq\sqrt{\frac{\left|y_{\alpha}\right|}{\lambda_{\alpha}}}}\left|\nabla v\right|^{p-1}\left|\nabla\left[\tau_{y_{\alpha},\lambda_{\alpha}}g\right]\right|\mathrm{d}{x}

\displaystyle\leq C\sqrt{\frac{\lambda_{\alpha}}{\left|y_{\alpha}\right|}}\left\|\tau_{y_{\alpha},\lambda_{\alpha}}g\right\|_{\mathcal{D}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\left(\int_{\left|x\right|\leq\sqrt{\frac{\left|y_{\alpha}\right|}{\lambda_{\alpha}}}}\left|\nabla v\right|^{p}\mathrm{d}{x}\right)^{(p-1)/p}.

by Hölder’s inequality. Applying Lemma
3.1
with

is fixed we have, up to a modification of the constant

\left|I_{a}^{(1.1)}\right|\leq C\sqrt{\frac{\lambda_{\alpha}}{\left|y_{\alpha}\right|}}\left\|g\right\|_{\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{a}(\Omega)}=o\left(\left\|g\right\|_{\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{a}(\Omega,0)}\right)

as

. By the same procedure applied to

\displaystyle\left|I_{a}^{(1.2)}\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|g\right\|_{\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{a}(\Omega)}\left(\int_{\sqrt{\frac{\left|y_{\alpha}\right|}{\lambda_{\alpha}}}<\left|x\right|\leq\frac{\left|y_{\alpha}\right|}{4\lambda_{\alpha}}}\left|\nabla v\right|^{p}\mathrm{d}{x}\right)^{(p-1)/p}

\displaystyle=o\left(\left\|g\right\|_{\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{a}(\Omega)}\right)

by the dominated convergence theorem. Combining, we deduce that

(5.4)

I_{a}^{(1)}=o\left(\left\|g\right\|_{\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{a}(\Omega)}\right)

Similarly, using also the CKN inequality (

(5.5)

\left|I_{b}^{(1)}\right|=o\left(\left\|g\right\|_{\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{a}(\Omega)}\right)

, and the CKN inequality (

I_{a}^{(2)}+I_{b}^{(2)}=o\left(\left\|g\right\|_{\mathcal{D}_{a}^{1,p}(\Omega)}\right)\qquad\text{as }\alpha\to\infty.

by the dominated convergence theorem. Combining the last three equations concludes our proof.

Maintaining the notation of the previous section,
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}

y_{\alpha}

6.1.
Extracting a Solution

To establish Theorems
1
-
2
, the first step is to extract a (possibly trivial) solution to (
A
) from a given Palais-Smale sequence. We recall that
(u_{\alpha})
in
\mathcal{D}_{a}^{1,p}(\Omega,0)
is called a
(PS)

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
(u_{\alpha})
in
\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{a}(\Omega,0)
be a Palais-Smale sequence for problem (
A
) such that,

(i)

u_{\alpha}\rightharpoonup u
in

Proof.

Let us define

Using that

is a bounded sequence in

\mathcal{D}_{a}^{1,p}(\Omega,0)

weakly in

With this, let us write

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\alpha}

\displaystyle:=\int_{\Omega}\left(\left[\left|\nabla u_{\alpha}\right|^{p-2}\nabla u_{\alpha}-\left|\nabla u\right|^{p-2}\nabla u\right],\nabla T(u_{\alpha}-u)\right)\left|x\right|^{-ap}\mathrm{d}{x}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\phi^{\prime}(u_{\alpha}),T(u_{\alpha}-u)\right\rangle+\int_{\Omega}\left(\left|u_{\alpha}\right|^{q-2}u_{\alpha},T(u_{\alpha}-u)\right)\left|x\right|^{-bq}\mathrm{d}{x}

(u_{\alpha})
in

\mathcal{D}_{a}^{1,p}(\Omega,0)

(1)

almost everywhere on

(2)

(3)

Conclusion

\displaystyle\phi(u_{\alpha}-u)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p}\left\|u_{\alpha}-u\right\|^{p}_{\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{a}(\Omega,0)}-\frac{1}{q}\left\|u_{\alpha}-u\right\|^{q}_{L^{q}(\Omega,\left|x\right|^{-bq})}

\displaystyle=\phi(u_{\alpha})-\phi(u)+o(1)

\displaystyle\to c-\phi(u).

Next, let

where the second equality holds since
\phi^{\prime}(u_{\alpha})\to 0
strongly in
\mathcal{D}^{-1,p^{\prime}}_{a}(\Omega,0)
. Combining the last two equations, we see tha

as
\alpha\to\infty

strongly in
\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{a}(\Omega,0)

The proof of the main results, namely Theorems

The first handles weighted bubbles and, in the main theorem, will be used provided condition

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\phi^{\prime}(u_{\alpha})\right\|_{\mathcal{D}^{-1,p^{\prime}}_{a}(\Omega,0)}\left\|h_{\alpha}\right\|_{\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{a}(\Omega,0)}

\displaystyle=\left\|\phi^{\prime}(u_{\alpha})\right\|_{\mathcal{D}^{-1,p^{\prime}}_{a}(\Omega,0)}\left\|h\right\|_{\mathcal{D}^{1,p}(B_{k},-x_{0})}.

\phi^{\prime}_{x_{0},\infty}(v_{\alpha})\to 0
strongly in
\mathcal{D}^{-1,p^{\prime}}_{a}(B_{k},-x_{0})
. Using this, we will extract a subsequence such that

pointwise on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, almost everywhere. Let

be a bump function such that

\rho\equiv 1&\text{in }B_{k},\\
\rho\equiv 0&\text{outside }B_{k+1}.\end{cases}

which satisfies

[

]
). Let

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B_{k}}\left(f_{\alpha},\nabla\left[T(v_{\alpha}-v)\right]\right)\left|x+x_{0}\right|^{-ap}\mathrm{d}{x}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(f_{\alpha},\rho\nabla\left[T(v_{\alpha}-v)\right]\right)\left|x+x_{0}\right|^{-ap}\mathrm{d}{x}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\phi^{\prime}_{x_{0},\infty}(v_{\alpha}),\rho T(v_{\alpha}-v)\right\rangle+\underbrace{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|v_{\alpha}\right|^{q-2}v_{\alpha}\rho T(v_{\alpha}-v)\left|x+x_{0}\right|^{-bq}\mathrm{d}{x}}_{=:I_{\alpha}^{(1)}}

\displaystyle\quad-\underbrace{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(f_{\alpha},T(v_{\alpha}-v)\nabla\rho\right)\left|x+x_{0}\right|^{-ap}\mathrm{d}{x}}_{=:I_{\alpha}^{(2)}}-\underbrace{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\nabla v\right|^{p-2}\left(\nabla v,\nabla\left[\rho T(v_{\alpha}-v)\right]\right)\left|x+x_{0}\right|^{-ap}\mathrm{d}{x}}_{=:I_{\alpha}^{(3)}}.

(v_{\alpha})

\frac{x}{\left|x\right|}&\text{if }\left|x\right|>1.\end{cases}

Fix
k\in\mathbb{N}
and let
\rho\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega_{\infty})\subseteq C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be a bump function such that

\begin{cases}0\leq\rho\leq 1&\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{n}\\
\rho\equiv 1&\text{in }B_{k}\\
\rho\equiv 0&\text{outside }B_{k+1}.\end{cases}

Observe that

f_{\alpha}:=\left|\nabla v_{\alpha}\right|^{p-2}\nabla v_{\alpha}-\left|\nabla v\right|^{p-2}\nabla v

is uniformly (in
\alpha
) bounded in
L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, with
p^{\prime}:=p/(p-1)
. Moreover, by a classical result (see, for instance, Szulkin-Willem
[
45
, Lemma 2.1]
and Simon
[
42
, p.210-212]
for the proof), we have that
\left(f_{\alpha},\left[\nabla v_{\alpha}-\nabla v\right]\right)\geq 0
almost everywhere. Therefore,

\displaystyle 0\leq\int_{B_{k}}\left(f_{\alpha},\nabla T(v_{\alpha}-v)\right)\mathrm{d}{x}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(f_{\alpha},\rho\nabla T(v_{\alpha}-v)\right)\mathrm{d}{x}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(f_{\alpha},\nabla\left[\rho T(v_{\alpha}-v)\right]\right)\mathrm{d}{x}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(f_{\alpha},T(v_{\alpha}-v)\nabla\rho\right)\mathrm{d}{x}

\displaystyle=:I_{\alpha}^{(1)}-I^{(2)}_{\alpha}.

We claim that both
I_{\alpha}^{(1)}
and
I_{\alpha}^{(2)}
tend to
0
as
\alpha\to\infty
. First, by Hölder’s inequality,

\displaystyle\left|I_{\alpha}^{(2)}\right|\leq\left\|f_{\alpha}\right\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\nabla\rho\right|^{p}\left|T(v_{\alpha}-v)\right|^{p}\mathrm{d}{x}\right)^{1/p}

Since the right integrand is bounded by a constant multiple of the indicator function of
B_{k+1}
, the dominated convergence theorem (using that
v_{\alpha}\to v
almost everywhere) implies that
I_{\alpha}^{(2)}\to 0
as
\alpha\to\infty
.

Next, in order to estimate
I_{\alpha}^{(1)}
, we first observe that
\rho T(v_{\alpha}-v)\in\mathcal{D}^{1,p}\left(B_{k+1}\right)
and, in fact,
\rho T(v_{\alpha}-v)
is bounded in
\mathcal{D}^{1,p}\left(B_{k+1}\right)
uniformly in
\alpha
. Since
\phi_{\infty}^{\prime}(v_{\alpha})\to 0
strongly in the dual of
\mathcal{D}^{1,p}\left(B_{k+1}\right)
,

\left\langle\phi_{\infty}^{\prime}(v_{\alpha}),\rho T(v_{\alpha}-v)\right\rangle\to 0\quad\text{as }\alpha\to\infty.

Therefore, writing

\displaystyle I_{\alpha}^{(1)}=\left\langle\phi_{\infty}^{\prime}(v_{\alpha}),\rho T(v_{\alpha}-v)\right\rangle

\displaystyle+\underbrace{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|v_{\alpha}\right|^{q-2}v_{\alpha}\rho T(v_{\alpha}-v)\mathrm{d}{x}}_{=:J_{\alpha}^{(1)}}

\displaystyle\quad-\underbrace{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\nabla v\right|^{p-2}\left(\nabla v,\nabla\left[\rho T(v_{\alpha}-v)\right]\right)\mathrm{d}{x}}_{=:J_{\alpha}^{(2)}},

we have

I_{\alpha}^{(1)}=o(1)+J_{\alpha}^{(1)}-J_{\alpha}^{(2)}

as
\alpha\to\infty
. Using Hölder’s inequality, we see that

\left|J_{\alpha}^{(1)}\right|\leq\left\|v_{\alpha}\right\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{q-1}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\rho^{q}(x)\left|T(v_{\alpha}-v)\right|^{q}\mathrm{d}{x}\right)^{1/q}=o(1)

as
\alpha\to\infty
. Here, we have used the dominated convergence theorem and applied the CKN inequality (
1.6
) to deduce that
\left\|v_{\alpha}\right\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
is bounded. On the other hand, as
\alpha\to\infty
,

\displaystyle J_{\alpha}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\nabla v\right|^{p-2}\left(\nabla v,\nabla\left[\rho T(v_{\alpha}-v)\right]\right)\mathrm{d}{x}

\displaystyle=\int_{\left|v_{\alpha}-v\right|\leq 1}\left|\nabla v\right|^{p-2}\left(\nabla v,\rho\nabla(v_{\alpha}-v)\right)\mathrm{d}{x}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\nabla v\right|^{p-2}\left(\nabla v,T(v_{\alpha}-v)\nabla\rho\right)\mathrm{d}{x}

\displaystyle=\int_{\left|v_{\alpha}-v\right|\leq 1}\left|\nabla v\right|^{p-2}\left(\nabla v,\rho\nabla(v_{\alpha}-v)\right)\mathrm{d}{x}+o(1)

where, to deduce the last equality, we have used Hölder’s inequality and the dominated convergence theorem. We then split the right hand-side as follows:

J_{\alpha}^{(2)}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\nabla v\right|^{p-2}\nabla v,\rho\nabla(v_{\alpha}-v)\mathrm{d}{x}-\int_{\left|v_{\alpha}-v\right|>1}\left|\nabla v\right|^{p-2}\left(\nabla v,\rho\nabla(v_{\alpha}-v)\right)\mathrm{d}{x}+o(1).

Note that the first term on the right-hand side tends to
0
as
\alpha\to\infty
. Indeed, this is because
v_{\alpha}\rightharpoonup v
weakly in
\mathcal{D}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
or, equivalently,
\nabla v_{\alpha}\rightharpoonup\nabla v
weakly in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

\displaystyle J_{\alpha}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=-\int_{\left|v_{\alpha}-v\right|>1}\left|\nabla v\right|^{p-2}\left(\nabla v,\rho\nabla(v_{\alpha}-v)\right)\mathrm{d}{x}+o(1)

With another application of Hölder’s inequality we see that,

\displaystyle\left|J_{\alpha}^{(2)}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\left\{\left|v_{\alpha}-v\right|>1\right\}\cap B_{k+1}}\left|\nabla v\right|^{p-1}\left|\nabla(v_{\alpha}-v)\right|\mathrm{d}{x}+o(1)

\displaystyle\leq\left\|v_{\alpha}-v\right\|_{\mathcal{D}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\left(\int_{\left\{\left|v_{\alpha}-v\right|>1\right\}\cap B_{k+1}}\left|\nabla v\right|^{p}\mathrm{d}{x}\right)^{1/p^{\prime}}+o(1)

as
\alpha\to\infty
. Since
v_{\alpha}\to v
pointwise a.e., the dominated convergence theorem ensures that the right hand side tends to
0
as
\alpha\to\infty
. Especially,
J_{\alpha}^{(2)}\to 0
as
\alpha\to\infty
.

Combining our result for
J_{\alpha}^{(1)}
and
J_{\alpha}^{(2)}
, we see that
I_{\alpha}^{(1)}\to 0
as
\alpha\to\infty
. Putting this together with
I_{\alpha}^{(2)}\to 0
as
\alpha\to\infty
, we have shown that

\int_{B_{k}\cap\Omega_{\infty}}\left(f_{\alpha},\nabla T(v_{\alpha}-v)\right)\mathrm{d}{x}\to 0\quad\text{as }\alpha\to\infty

where

f_{\alpha}=\left|\nabla v_{\alpha}\right|^{p-2}\nabla v_{\alpha}-\left|\nabla v\right|^{p-2}\nabla v.

Therefore, by Corollary
2.2
(invoked with
a=b=0
), up to a subsequence, we have the following.

(1)

\nabla v_{\alpha}\to\nabla v
almost everywhere in
\Omega_{\infty}
. Using that
v_{\alpha}
is supported in
\Omega_{\alpha}
,
v_{\alpha}\to v
pointwise almost everywhere and
\Omega_{\alpha}\to\Omega_{\infty}
, it readily follows that
\nabla v_{\alpha}\to\nabla v
almost everywhere in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

(2)

\left\|v\right\|_{\mathcal{D}^{1,p}(\Omega_{\infty})}^{p}=\left\|v_{\alpha}\right\|_{\mathcal{D}^{1,p}(\Omega_{\infty})}^{p}-\left\|v_{\alpha}-v\right\|_{\mathcal{D}^{1,p}(\Omega_{\infty})}^{p}+o(1)
.

(3)

There holds

\left|\nabla v_{\alpha}\right|^{p-2}\nabla v_{\alpha}-\left|\nabla v_{\alpha}-\nabla v\right|^{p-2}\left(\nabla v_{\alpha}-\nabla v\right)\to\left|\nabla v\right|^{p-2}\nabla v.

strongly in
L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega_{\infty})
. Then, since
v\in\mathcal{D}^{1,p}(\Omega_{\infty})
it immediately follows that the convergence remains true in
L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

(4)

Finally,

\left|v_{\alpha}\right|^{q-2}v_{\alpha}-\left|v_{\alpha}-v\right|^{q-2}\left(v_{\alpha}-v\right)\to\left|v\right|^{q-2}v.

strongly in
L^{q/(q-1)}(\Omega_{\infty})
and hence in
L^{q/(q-1)}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Next, fix
h\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega_{\infty})
. Then,
h\in C_{c}^{\infty}(B_{k})
for all
k
large; fix such an index
k
. Since, as stated in equation (
6.12
),
\phi^{\prime}(v_{\alpha})\to 0
strongly in the dual of
\mathcal{D}^{1,p}(B_{k})
, there holds

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\lim_{\alpha\to\infty}\left\langle\phi_{\infty}^{\prime}(v_{\alpha}),h\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\lim_{\alpha\to\infty}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\nabla v_{\alpha}\right|^{p-2}\left(\nabla v_{\alpha},\nabla h\right)\mathrm{d}{x}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|v_{\alpha}\right|^{q-2}v_{\alpha}h\mathrm{d}{x}\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\nabla v\right|^{p-2}\left(\nabla v,\nabla h\right)\mathrm{d}{x}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|v\right|^{q-2}vh\mathrm{d}{x}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\phi_{\infty}^{\prime}(v),h\right\rangle

where the second equality is due to a standard convergence result, using that
v_{\alpha}\to v
and
\nabla v_{\alpha}\to\nabla v
pointwise almost everywhere. Since
h\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega_{\infty})
was arbitrary, we conclude that
\phi_{\infty}^{\prime}(v)=0
in
\Omega_{\infty}
.

Next, we establish the desired properties of the sequence
(w_{\alpha})
. First,
(a)
follows from Lemma
6.4
. By this same lemma, we also see that

\displaystyle\phi_{0,\infty}(w_{\alpha})=\frac{1}{p}\left\|w_{\alpha}\right\|_{\mathcal{D}_{a}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},0)}^{p}-\frac{1}{q}\left\|w_{\alpha}\right\|_{L_{b}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n},0)}

\displaystyle=\phi(u_{\alpha})-\phi_{\infty}(v)+o(1)

\displaystyle=c-\phi_{\infty}(v)+o(1)

as
\alpha\to\infty
. That is, we have
(b)
. Finally, by Lemma
6.5
,

\left\langle\phi_{0,\infty}^{\prime}(w_{\alpha}),g\right\rangle=\left\langle\phi^{\prime}(u_{\alpha}),g\right\rangle-\left\langle\phi_{\infty}^{\prime}(v),\tau_{y_{\alpha},\lambda_{\alpha}}g\right\rangle+o\left(\left\|g\right\|_{\mathcal{D}_{a}^{1,p}(\Omega,0)}\right)

as
\alpha\to\infty
over
g\in\mathcal{D}_{a}^{1,p}(\Omega)
. Then, using that
\phi^{\prime}(u_{\alpha})\to 0
strongly in the dual of
\mathcal{D}_{a}^{1,p}(\Omega)
and applying Lemma
5.2
, we conclude that

\left\langle\phi_{0,\infty}^{\prime}(w_{\alpha}),g\right\rangle=o\left(\left\|g\right\|_{\mathcal{D}_{a}^{1,p}(\Omega,0)}\right)

which gives

.

∎

6.3.
Bubbling

In the iteration lemmas, the terms subtracted from the Palais-Smale sequence
(u_{\alpha})
are referred to as “bubbles”. We claim that, as
\alpha\to\infty
, the bubble terms converge to
0
in suitable senses. Explicitly, we provide two results.

First, in the first iteration result, i.e. Proposition
6.2
, the subtracted terms are

(6.13)

B_{\alpha}(z):=\lambda_{\alpha}^{-\gamma}v\left(\frac{z}{\lambda_{\alpha}}-x_{0}\right).

Now, with condition
(P1)
, we have the following result:

Lemma 6.6
.

Let
v\in\mathcal{D}_{a}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},-x_{0})
and assume condition
(P1)
holds (i.e.
\lambda_{\alpha}\to 0
). Then, up to a subsequence,

(a)

\nabla B_{\alpha}(x)\to 0
pointwise almost everywhere on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

(b)

B_{\alpha}\rightharpoonup 0
weakly in
\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{a}(\mathbb{R}^{n},0)
and pointwise almost everywhere;

as
\alpha\to\infty
. Here,
B_{\alpha}
are given by (
6.13
)

Proof.

Fix
\varepsilon>0
and observe that, after a change of variables,

\int_{B(0,\varepsilon)^{\mathtt{C}}}\left|\nabla B_{\alpha}(z)\right|^{p}\left|z\right|^{-ap}\mathrm{d}{x}=\int_{B\left(-x_{0},\frac{\varepsilon}{\lambda_{\alpha}}\right)^{\mathtt{C}}}\left|\nabla v\left(x\right)\right|^{p}\left|x+x_{0}\right|^{-ap}\mathrm{d}{x}.

The right-hand side of this equation converges to
0
by the monotone convergence theorem. Passing to a subsequence, we infer that
\nabla B_{\alpha}\to 0
pointwise almost everywhere on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B(0,\varepsilon)
. As
\varepsilon>0
was arbitrary, a diagonal argument gives the existence of a subsequence such that
\nabla B_{\alpha}\to 0
almost everywhere on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Since
(\nabla B_{\alpha})
is bounded in
L^{p}_{a}(\mathbb{R}^{n},0)
, a simple change of variables and standard convergence results show that
B_{\alpha}\rightharpoonup 0
in
\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{a}(\mathbb{R}^{n},0)
.

On the other hand, since
(B_{\alpha})
is bounded in
\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{a}(\mathbb{R}^{n},0)
, up to a subsequence, it converges both weakly in
\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{a}(\mathbb{R}^{n},0)
and pointwise almost everywhere to some function in
\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{a}(\mathbb{R}^{n},0)
. By uniqueness of the weak limit, we conclude that
B_{\alpha}\to 0
pointwise almost everywhere.
∎

Next, in Proposition
6.3
, the
bubbles
take the form
\tau^{-}_{y_{\alpha},\lambda_{\alpha}}v
and we obtain a similar convergence result:

Lemma 6.7
.

Let
v\in\mathcal{D}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. If
a=b
and condition
(P1)
holds (i.e.
\lambda_{\alpha}\to 0
) then, up to a subsequence,

(a)

\nabla\left[\tau^{-}_{y_{\alpha},\lambda_{\alpha}}v\right]\to 0
pointwise almost everywhere

(b)

\tau^{-}_{y_{\alpha},\lambda_{\alpha}}v\rightharpoonup 0
weakly in
\mathcal{D}_{a}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},0)
and pointwise almost everywhere.

Proof.

Up to a subseqeunce, we may suppose that
y_{\alpha}\to y
. Given an arbitrary
\varepsilon>0
, observe that for all
\alpha
large

B\left(y_{\alpha},\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)\subset B(y,\varepsilon).

Therefore,

\displaystyle\int_{B(y,\varepsilon)^{\mathtt{C}}}\left|\nabla\left[\tau^{-}_{y_{\alpha},\lambda_{\alpha}}v\right]\right|^{p}\left|z\right|^{-ap}\mathrm{d}{z}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B(y_{\alpha},\varepsilon/2)^{\mathtt{C}}}\left|\nabla\left[\tau^{-}_{y_{\alpha},\lambda_{\alpha}}v\right]\right|^{p}\left|z\right|^{-ap}\mathrm{d}{z}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\left\|v\right\|_{L^{q}\left(B(0,\varepsilon/(2\lambda_{\alpha}))^{\mathtt{C}}\right)}+\left\|\nabla v\right\|_{L^{p}\left(B(0,\varepsilon/(2\lambda_{\alpha}))^{\mathtt{C}}\right)}\right)^{p}

where the second inequality follows from Remark
3.2
and
C>0
is a constant independent of
\alpha
. Since
\lambda_{\alpha}\to 0
, the right-hand side tends to
0
by the dominated convergence theorem as
\alpha\to\infty
. Therefore,

\nabla\left[\tau^{-}_{y_{\alpha},\lambda_{\alpha}}v\right]\to 0\qquad\text{in }L^{p}\left(B(y,\varepsilon)^{\mathtt{C}}\right).

Since
\varepsilon>0
was arbitrary, a diagonal argument shows that, up to a subsequence,
\nabla\left[\tau^{-}_{y_{\alpha},\lambda_{\alpha}}v\right]\to 0
almost everywhere.

By Lemma
3.2
, the sequence
(\tau^{-}_{y_{\alpha},\lambda_{\alpha}}v)
is bounded in
\mathcal{D}_{a}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},0)
. Then, utilizing also that
\nabla\left[\tau^{-}_{y_{\alpha},\lambda_{\alpha}}v\right]\to 0
pointwise almost everywhere, it follows from duality and a standard convergence result that
\nabla\left[\tau^{-}_{y_{\alpha},\lambda_{\alpha}}v\right]\rightharpoonup 0
weakly in
L_{a}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},0)
. Put otherwise,
\tau^{-}_{y_{\alpha},\lambda_{\alpha}}v\rightharpoonup 0
weakly in
\mathcal{D}_{a}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},0)
.

On the other hand, since
\tau^{-}_{y_{\alpha},\lambda_{\alpha}}v
is a bounded sequence in
\mathcal{D}_{a}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},0)
, up to a subsequence we may assume that there is some
w\in\mathcal{D}_{a}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},0)
such that

\bullet

\tau^{-}_{y_{\alpha},\lambda_{\alpha}}v\rightharpoonup w
weakly in
\mathcal{D}_{a}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},0)
, and

\bullet

\tau^{-}_{y_{\alpha},\lambda_{\alpha}}v\to w
pointwise almost everywhere.

Then, by uniqueness of weak limits,
w\equiv 0
.
∎

7.
The Proof When
a<b

We now produce the proof of Theorem
1
, i.e. we establish the main compactness result in the parameter case
a<b
. As previously stated, this setting can only produce weighted bubbles centered at the origin, which may also occur in the limit case
a=b
. For the sake of brevity and clarity, we decompose the proof into several distinct steps.

Step 1
.
Being a Palais-Smale sequence, by Proposition
2.3
,
(u_{\alpha})
is bounded in
\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{a}(\Omega,0)
. Hence, passing to a subsequence,
u_{\alpha}
converges weakly to some
v_{0}\in\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{a}(\Omega,0)
, with pointwise convergence almost everywhere on
\Omega
, as
\alpha\to\infty
. Therefore, by Lemma
6.1
, the sequence
(u_{\alpha}^{1})
defined by
u_{\alpha}^{1}:=u_{\alpha}-v_{0}
satisfies

(i)

\left\|u_{\alpha}^{1}\right\|^{p}_{\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{a}(\Omega,0)}=\left\|u_{\alpha}\right\|^{p}_{\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{a}(\Omega,0)}-\left\|v_{0}\right\|^{p}_{\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{a}(\Omega,0)}+o(1)
;

(ii)

\phi(u_{\alpha}^{1})\to c-\phi(v_{0})
;

(iii)

\phi^{\prime}(u_{\alpha}^{1})\to 0
in
\mathcal{D}^{-1,p^{\prime}}_{a}(\Omega,0)
.

Furthermore, we find that
\phi^{\prime}(v_{0})=0
and
\nabla u_{\alpha}\to\nabla v_{0}
a.e. on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
as
\alpha\to\infty
. Especially,
v_{0}
solves problem (
A
).

Step 2
. If
u_{\alpha}^{1}\to 0
strongly in
L^{q}_{b}(\Omega,0)
then the proof is complete. Indeed,

\displaystyle\lim_{\alpha\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}\left(\left|x\right|^{-ap}\left|\nabla u_{\alpha}^{1}(x)\right|^{p}-\left|x\right|^{-bq}\left|u_{\alpha}^{1}(x)\right|^{q}\right)\mathrm{d}{x}=\lim_{\alpha\to\infty}\left|\left\langle\phi^{\prime}(u_{\alpha}^{1}),u_{\alpha}^{1}\right\rangle\right|\to 0

since
\phi^{\prime}(u_{\alpha}^{1})\to 0
in the dual of
\mathcal{D}_{a}^{1,p}(\Omega)
and the sequence
(u_{\alpha}^{1})
is bounded in
\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{a}(\Omega,0)
. Hence,
\nabla u^{1}_{\alpha}\to 0
strongly in
L^{p}_{a}(\Omega,0)
, i.e.
u_{\alpha}\to v_{0}
strongly in
\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{a}(\Omega,0)
. Thus, the conclusions of our theorem are satisfied for
k=0
since

\phi(v_{0})=\lim_{\alpha\to\infty}\phi(u_{\alpha}).

Step 3
. If we do not satisfy the termination condition of Step 2, then, after passing to a subsequence,

\inf_{\alpha\geqslant 1}\int_{\Omega}\left|u_{\alpha}^{1}\right|^{q}\left|x\right|^{-bq}\mathrm{d}x>\delta

for some
\delta>0
. We may suppose without harm that

(7.1)

0<\delta<\left(\frac{C_{a,b}}{2^{p}}\right)^{\frac{q}{q-p}}.

where
C_{a,b}>0
is such that the CKN inequality (
2.1
) holds.

Consider the family
\left\{Q_{\alpha}\right\}_{\alpha\geqslant 1}
of Lévy concentration functions each defined by

Q_{\alpha}(r):=\sup_{y\in\bar{\Omega}}\int_{B(y,r)}\left|u_{\alpha}^{1}\right|^{q}\left|x\right|^{-bq}\mathrm{d}{x},

where each
(u_{\alpha})
is extended by
0
outside
\Omega
. Note that, by CKN inequality (
1.6
), one has
Q_{\alpha}(r)<\infty
for every

. By continuity of each
Q_{\alpha}
, we may apply the Intermediate Value Theorem to extract a smallest
\lambda_{\alpha}^{1}>0
such that
Q_{\alpha}(\lambda_{\alpha}^{1})=\delta
.

Finally, for each index
\alpha

Q_{\alpha}(\lambda_{\alpha}^{1})=\sup_{y\in\bar{\Omega}}\int_{B(y,\lambda_{\alpha}^{1})}\left|u_{\alpha}^{1}\right|^{q}\left|x\right|^{-bq}\mathrm{d}{x}=\int_{B(y_{\alpha}^{1},\lambda_{\alpha}^{1})}\left|u_{\alpha}^{1}\right|^{q}\left|x\right|^{-bq}\mathrm{d}{x}=\delta.

and

as

.

Step 4
. We show that the sequence of points

{y_{\alpha}^{1}}/{\lambda_{\alpha}^{1}}
is bounded in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. If not, passing to a subsequence we may assume that

\frac{\left|y_{\alpha}^{1}\right|}{\lambda_{\alpha}^{1}}\to\infty,\quad\text{as }\alpha\to\infty,

and that
\left|y_{\alpha}^{1}\right|>4\lambda_{\alpha}^{1}>0
for each

.
Consider the functions

v_{\alpha}(x):=\tau_{y_{\alpha}^{1},\lambda_{\alpha}^{1}}u_{\alpha}^{1}(x)=\left(\frac{\lambda_{\alpha}^{1}}{\left|y_{\alpha}^{1}\right|}\right)^{b}\left(\lambda_{\alpha}^{1}\right)^{\gamma}u_{\alpha}^{1}(\lambda_{\alpha}^{1}x+y_{\alpha}^{1})\eta\left(\frac{2\lambda_{\alpha}^{1}x}{\left|y_{\alpha}^{1}\right|}\right)

with

defined as in equation (

then implies that

with

strongly. Furthermore, by the Gagliardo-Nirenberg-Sobolev inequality, we also obtain

in
L^{p^{\ast}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Using that

. However, Lemma

It is clear that, for each

By homogeneity, we mean that

\left\|v_{\alpha}^{1}\right\|_{\mathcal{D}_{a}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},-x_{0})}=\left\|u_{\alpha}\right\|_{\mathcal{D}_{a}^{1,p}(\Omega,0)},\qquad\left\|v_{\alpha}^{1}\right\|_{L_{b}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n},-x_{0})}=\left\|u_{\alpha}\right\|_{L_{b}^{q}(\Omega,0)}

In particular,

is a bounded sequence in
\mathcal{D}_{a}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},-x_{0})
whence we may assume without loss of generality that

v_{\alpha}^{1}\rightharpoonup v^{1}\text{ weakly in }\mathcal{D}_{a}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},-x_{0}),\quad v_{\alpha}^{1}\to v^{1}\text{ a.e. in }\mathbb{R}^{n}

for some
v^{1}\in\mathcal{D}_{a}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},-x_{0})
. We note that, by a change of variables

(7.3)

\sup_{y\in\overline{\Omega_{\alpha}}}\int_{B(y,1)}\left|v_{\alpha}^{1}(x)\right|^{q}\left|x+x_{0}\right|^{-bq}\mathrm{d}{x}=\sup_{w\in\overline{\Omega}}\int_{B(w,\lambda_{\alpha}^{1})}\left|u_{\alpha}^{1}(z)\right|^{q}\left|z\right|^{-bq}\mathrm{d}{z}=\delta

where the last equality follows from equation (
7.2
).

Step 5
. For a given
h\in\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{a}(\Omega_{\alpha},-x_{0})
, define
h_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{a}(\Omega,0)
via the homogeneous transform:

h_{\alpha}(z):=\left(\lambda_{\alpha}^{1}\right)^{-\gamma}h\left(\frac{z}{\lambda_{\alpha}^{1}}-x_{0}\right).

A change of variables shows that

\left\langle\phi^{\prime}_{x_{0},\infty}(v_{\alpha}^{1}),h\right\rangle=\left\langle\phi^{\prime}(u_{\alpha}^{1}),h_{\alpha}\right\rangle.

Since
\phi^{\prime}(u_{\alpha}^{1})
is a continuous linear functional on
\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{a}(\Omega,0)
, for each
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}
, by a Riesz-type representation result, see (
2.2
), there exist functions
f_{\alpha}^{(1)},\dots,f_{\alpha}^{(n)}\in L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega,\left|x\right|^{-ap})
so that

\left\langle\phi^{\prime}(u_{\alpha}^{1}),h\right\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{\Omega}f^{(i)}_{\alpha}(x)\partial_{i}h(x)\left|x\right|^{-ap}\mathrm{d}{x},\quad\forall h\in\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{a}(\Omega,0).

Of course,
p^{\prime}
denotes the Hölder conjugate exponent to
p
.
Moreover, since
\phi^{\prime}(u_{\alpha}^{1})\to 0
in the strong operator topology,

(7.4)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{\Omega}\left|f^{(i)}_{\alpha}(x)\right|^{p^{\prime}}\left|x\right|^{-ap}\mathrm{d}{x}\to 0,\quad\text{as }\alpha\to\infty.

Therefore, we may write

\displaystyle\left\langle\phi^{\prime}_{x_{0},\infty}(v_{\alpha}^{1}),h\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\phi^{\prime}(u_{\alpha}^{1}),h_{\alpha}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{\Omega}f^{(i)}_{\alpha}(z)\partial_{i}h_{\alpha}(z)\left|z\right|^{-ap}\mathrm{d}{z}

\displaystyle=\left(\lambda_{\alpha}^{1}\right)^{n-ap-(\gamma+1)}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{\Omega_{\alpha}}f^{(i)}_{\alpha}(\lambda_{\alpha}^{1}(x+x_{0}))\partial_{i}h\left(x\right)\left|x+x_{0}\right|^{-ap}\mathrm{d}{x}.

It follows that

(7.5)

\left\langle\phi_{x_{0},\infty}(v_{\alpha}^{1}),h\right\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{\Omega_{\alpha}}g^{(i)}_{\alpha}(x)\partial_{i}h\left(x\right)\left|x+x_{0}\right|^{-ap}\mathrm{d}{x},

where we have defined

g_{\alpha}^{(i)}(x):=\left(\lambda_{\alpha}^{1}\right)^{\frac{n-ap}{p^{\prime}}}f_{\alpha}^{(i)}\left(\lambda_{\alpha}^{1}(x+x_{0})\right).

Furthermore, by another change of variables,

(7.6)

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{\Omega_{\alpha}}\left|g_{\alpha}^{(i)}(x)\right|^{p^{\prime}}\left|x+x_{0}\right|^{-ap}\mathrm{d}{x}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{\Omega}\left|f_{\alpha}^{(i)}(z)\right|^{p^{\prime}}\left|z\right|^{-ap}\mathrm{d}{z}=o(1)

as
\alpha\to\infty
because of (
7.4
).

Step 6
. By way of contradiction, we claim
v^{1}\neq 0
. Assume
v^{1}=0
so that
v_{\alpha}^{1}\rightharpoonup 0
weakly in
\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{a}(\mathbb{R}^{n},-x_{0})
and pointwise a.e. on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
as
\alpha\to\infty
. By the compactness of the embedding
\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{a}(\mathbb{R}^{n},-x_{0})\hookrightarrow L^{p}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\left|x+x_{0}\right|^{-ap})
, we may assume that
v_{\alpha}^{1}\to 0
strongly in
L^{p}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\left|x+x_{0}\right|^{-ap})
whence
(v_{\alpha}^{1})
is bounded in
L^{p}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\left|x+x_{0}\right|^{-ap})
. By Proposition
4.1
,
\Omega_{\alpha}\to\Omega_{\infty}
where

\Omega_{\infty}:=\begin{cases}\mathbb{R}^{n}&\text{if }\lambda^{1}=0,\\
\frac{\Omega}{\lambda^{1}}-x_{0}&\text{if }\lambda^{1}>0,\end{cases}

and let
\mathcal{C}
be a countable cover of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that if
\Lambda\in\mathcal{C}
then either

(1)

\Lambda=B(y,1/2)
for some
y\in\Omega_{\infty}
or

(2)

\Lambda
is compactly contained in the complement of
\overline{\Omega_{\infty}}
.

If
\Lambda\in\mathcal{C}
is compactly contained in the complement of
\overline{\Omega_{\infty}}
, then
\Lambda
and
\Omega_{\alpha}
are disjoint for all
\alpha
large since
\Omega_{\alpha}\to\Omega_{\infty}
. Therefore,
v_{\alpha}^{1}
vanishes on
\Lambda
for all
\alpha
large and, in particular,
\nabla v_{\alpha}^{1}\to 0
in
L^{p}_{a}\left(\Lambda,-x_{0}\right)
.

On the other hand, suppose
\Lambda=B(y,1/2)
for some
y\in\Omega_{\infty}
. Since
\Omega_{\alpha}\to\Omega_{\infty}
,
y\in\Omega_{\alpha}
for all
\alpha
sufficiently large. For any
h\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n};[0,1])
with
\operatorname{supp}(h)\subset B(y,1)
, using Hölder’s inequality with the CKN-inequality (
2.1
) then yields

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}

\displaystyle\left|h\right|^{p}\left|v_{\alpha}^{1}\right|^{q}\left|x+x_{0}\right|^{-bq}\mathrm{d}{x}

\displaystyle=\int_{\operatorname{supp}(h)}\left|v_{\alpha}^{1}h\right|^{p}\left|v_{\alpha}^{1}\right|^{q-p}\left|x+x_{0}\right|^{-bq}\mathrm{d}{x}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{\operatorname{supp}(h)}\left|v_{\alpha}^{1}\right|^{q}\left|x+x_{0}\right|^{-bq}\mathrm{d}{x}\right)^{\frac{q-p}{q}}\left(\int_{\operatorname{supp}(h)}\left|hv_{\alpha}^{1}\right|^{q}\left|x+x_{0}\right|^{-bq}\mathrm{d}{x}\right)^{p/q}

\displaystyle\leq C_{a,b}^{-1}\left(\int_{\operatorname{supp}(h)}\left|v_{\alpha}^{1}\right|^{q}\left|x+x_{0}\right|^{-bq}\mathrm{d}{x}\right)^{\frac{q-p}{q}}\left(\int_{\operatorname{supp}(h)}\left|\nabla(hv_{\alpha}^{1})\right|^{p}\left|x+x_{0}\right|^{-ap}\mathrm{d}{x}\right).

Then, bounding the first integral with equations (
7.3
) and (
7.1
),

(7.7)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|h\right|^{p}\left|v_{\alpha}^{1}\right|^{q}\left|x+x_{0}\right|^{-bq}\mathrm{d}{x}\leq 2^{-p}\int_{\operatorname{supp}(h)}\left|\nabla(hv_{\alpha}^{1})\right|^{p}\left|x+x_{0}\right|^{-ap}\mathrm{d}{x}.

On the other hand, since
v_{\alpha}^{1}\to 0
in
L^{p}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\left|x+x_{0}\right|^{-ap})
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\nabla(hv_{\alpha}^{1})\right|^{p}\left|x+x_{0}\right|^{-ap}\mathrm{d}{x}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{p-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|h\right|^{p}\left|\nabla v_{\alpha}^{1}\right|^{p}\left|x+x_{0}\right|^{-ap}\mathrm{d}{x}+o(1)

\displaystyle=2^{p-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\nabla v_{\alpha}^{1}\right|^{p-2}\left(\nabla v_{\alpha}^{1},\nabla\left[\left|h\right|^{p}v_{\alpha}^{1}\right]\right)\left|x+x_{0}\right|^{-ap}\mathrm{d}{x}+o(1)

\displaystyle=2^{p-1}\left[\left\langle\phi^{\prime}_{x_{0},\infty}(v_{\alpha}^{1}),\left|h\right|^{p}v_{\alpha}^{1}\right\rangle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|v_{\alpha}^{1}\right|^{q}\left|h\right|^{p}\left|x+x_{0}\right|^{-bq}\right]+o(1).

Next, by Step 5 equations (
7.5
)-(
7.6
), we see that as
\alpha\to\infty

\displaystyle\left\langle\phi^{\prime}_{x_{0},\infty}(v_{\alpha}^{1}),\left|h\right|^{p}v_{\alpha}^{1}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{\Omega_{\alpha}}g_{\alpha}^{(i)}\partial_{i}\left(\left|h\right|^{p}v_{\alpha}^{1}\right)\left|x+x_{0}\right|^{-ap}\mathrm{d}{x}=o(1)

where we have used that
\left(\left|h\right|^{p}v_{\alpha}^{1}\right)
is bounded in
\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{a}(\Omega_{\alpha},-x_{0})
. Ergo, concatenating with (
7.7
),

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\nabla(hv_{\alpha}^{1})\right|^{p}\left|x+x_{0}\right|^{-ap}\mathrm{d}{x}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\nabla(hv_{\alpha}^{1})\right|^{p}\left|x+x_{0}\right|^{-ap}\mathrm{d}{x}+o(1).

We deduce that
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\nabla(hv_{\alpha}^{1})\right|^{p}\left|x+x_{0}\right|^{-ap}\mathrm{d}{x}\to 0
. Taking
h\equiv 1
on
\Lambda=B(y,1/2)
, we infer that

\nabla v_{\alpha}^{1}\to 0\text{ in }L^{p}_{a}\left(\Lambda,-x_{0}\right).

Having shown that this convergence holds for all
\Lambda\in\mathcal{C}
, it follows that

\nabla v_{\alpha}^{1}\to 0
in
L^{p}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\left|x+x_{0}\right|^{-ap})
.
Multiplying with a cutoff function and applying the CKN-inequality, this implies that

v_{\alpha}^{1}\to 0

strongly in
L^{q}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\left|x+x_{0}\right|^{-bq})
as
\alpha\to\infty
. However, this contradicts by (
7.2
) since

\displaystyle\int_{B(0,2)}\left|v_{\alpha}^{1}\right|^{q}\left|x+x_{0}\right|^{-bq}\mathrm{d}{x}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{B\left(\frac{y_{\alpha}^{1}}{\lambda_{\alpha}^{1}}-x_{0},1\right)}\left|v_{\alpha}^{1}\right|^{q}\left|x+x_{0}\right|^{-bq}\mathrm{d}{x}

\displaystyle=\int_{B\left(y_{\alpha}^{1},\lambda_{\alpha}^{1}\right)}\left|u_{\alpha}^{1}(z)\right|^{q}\left|z\right|^{-bq}\mathrm{d}{x}

\displaystyle=\delta>0

for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}
sufficiently large.

Step 7
. We claim that
\lambda^{1}=\lim_{\alpha\to\infty}\lambda_{\alpha}^{1}=0
. To this end, we argue by contradiction and assume instead that
\lambda^{1}>0
. Given any
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R}^{n})
, a straightforward change of variables shows that

(7.8)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}

\displaystyle\left(\nabla v_{\alpha}^{1}(x),\varphi(x)\right)\left|x+x_{0}\right|^{-ap}\mathrm{d}{x}

\displaystyle=(\lambda_{\alpha}^{1})^{(\gamma+1)-n+ap}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\nabla u_{\alpha}^{1}(z),\varphi\left(\frac{z}{\lambda_{\alpha}^{1}}-x_{0}\right)\right)\left|z\right|^{-ap}\mathrm{d}{z}.

As the sequence
(\lambda_{\alpha}^{1})
is bounded, there is a compact set
\Lambda
such that, for each
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}
, the function

z\mapsto\varphi\left(\frac{z}{\lambda_{\alpha}^{1}}-x_{0}\right)

is supported on
\Lambda
. Following this, since
\nabla u_{\alpha}^{1}\to 0
pointwise almost everywhere on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and is bounded in
L^{p}_{a}(\mathbb{R}^{n},0)
, standard convergence results imply that

\int_{\varLambda}\left|\nabla u_{\alpha}^{1}(z)\right|\left|z\right|^{-ap}\mathrm{d}{z}\to 0,\quad\text{as }\alpha\to\infty.

Using this and that
\lambda_{\alpha}^{1}\to\lambda_{1}>0
, it follows from (
7.8
) that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\nabla v_{\alpha}^{1}(x),\varphi(x)\right)\left|x+x_{0}\right|^{-ap}\mathrm{d}{x}\to 0,\quad\text{as }\alpha\to\infty.

Since smooth functions of compact support are dense in
L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\left|x+x_{0}\right|^{-ap})
, it readily follows that
\nabla v_{\alpha}^{1}\rightharpoonup 0
weakly in
L^{p}_{a}(\mathbb{R}^{n},-x_{0})
. Put otherwise,
v_{\alpha}^{1}\rightharpoonup 0
in
\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{a}(\mathbb{R}^{n},-x_{0})
which directly contradicts Step 6.

Step 8
. Since
v^{1}\in\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{a}(\mathbb{R}^{n},-x_{0})
and
\Omega_{\alpha}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
, there exists a sequence functions
\psi_{\alpha}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega_{\alpha})
such that
\psi_{\alpha}\to v^{1}
strongly in
\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{a}(\mathbb{R}^{n},-x_{0})
as
\alpha\to\infty
.

Consider the modified sequence given by a homogeneous transform:

\widetilde{\psi}_{\alpha}(z):=\left(\lambda_{\alpha}^{1}\right)^{-\gamma}\psi_{\alpha}\left(\frac{z}{\lambda_{\alpha}^{1}}-x_{0}\right).

Since
\psi_{\alpha}\to v^{1}
strongly in
\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{a}(\mathbb{R}^{n},-x_{0})
, a change of variables (i.e. homogeneity) shows that

\widetilde{\psi}_{\alpha}(\cdot)-\left(\lambda_{\alpha}^{1}\right)^{-\gamma}v^{1}\left(\frac{\cdot}{\lambda_{\alpha}^{1}}-x_{0}\right)\to 0\quad\text{in }\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{a}(\mathbb{R}^{n},0).

Therefore, after an application of Lemma
6.6
(with

), we deduce that

(i)

\nabla\widetilde{\psi}_{\alpha}\to 0
pointwise almost everywhere on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
;

(ii)

\widetilde{\psi}_{\alpha}\rightharpoonup 0
weakly in
\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{a}(\Omega,0)
and pointwise almost everywhere
\alpha\to\infty
.

Step 9
. We outline the iteration process. Applying Proposition

, we see that
v^{1}
solves a translated version of (

u_{\alpha}^{2}(x):=u_{\alpha}^{1}(x)-\left(\lambda_{\alpha}^{(1)}\right)^{-\gamma}v^{1}\left(\frac{x}{\lambda_{\alpha}^{(1)}}-x_{0}^{1}\right),

for

, satisfies

\left\|u_{n}^{2}\right\|_{\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{a}(\mathbb{R}^{n},0)}^{p}=\left\|u_{\alpha}\right\|^{p}_{\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{a}(\Omega,0)}-\left\|v_{0}\right\|^{p}_{{\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{a}(\Omega,0)}}-\left\|v^{1}\right\|_{\mathcal{D}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},-x_{0}^{(1)})}^{p}+o(1)

and

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\phi_{0,\infty}(u_{\alpha}^{2})\to c-\phi(v_{0})-\phi_{x_{0}^{(1)},\infty}(v^{1}),\\
\phi^{\prime}_{0,\infty}(u_{\alpha}^{2})\to 0\text{ in }\mathcal{D}^{-1,p^{\prime}}_{a}(\Omega,0).\end{cases}

Next, we consider the auxiliary sequence

\widetilde{u}_{\alpha}^{2}(x):=u_{\alpha}^{1}(x)-\widetilde{\psi}_{\alpha}(x)

which is bounded in
\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{a}(\Omega,0)
. Because
\psi_{\alpha}\to v^{1}
strongly in
\mathcal{D}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},-x_{0}^{1})
,

\begin{cases}\phi\left(\widetilde{u}^{2}_{\alpha}\right)\to c-\phi(v_{0})-\phi_{x_{0}^{(1)},\infty}(v^{1}),\\
\phi^{\prime}(\widetilde{u}_{\alpha}^{2})\to 0\text{ in }\mathcal{D}^{-1,p^{\prime}}_{a}(\Omega,0).\end{cases}

We may therefore apply Steps 2-9 to this new sequence

, at each stage removing another solution

Step 10
. For each index

solves (

before we reach the termination condition in Step 2.

.

Step 4
. The following dichotomy holds: either

\frac{\left|y_{\alpha}^{1}\right|}{\lambda_{\alpha}^{1}}\to\infty\quad\text{and}\quad\left|y_{\alpha}^{1}\right|>4\lambda_{\alpha}^{1}.

In this case, it is automatic that
\lambda_{\alpha}^{1}\to 0
as
\alpha\to\infty
. Subsequently, we may define

v_{\alpha}^{1}:=\tau_{y_{\alpha}^{1},\lambda_{\alpha}^{1}}u_{\alpha}^{1}\in\mathcal{D}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Then,
v_{\alpha}^{1}\in\mathcal{D}^{1,p}(\Omega_{\alpha})
in
\mathcal{D}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
where

\Omega_{\alpha}:=\frac{\Omega-y_{\alpha}^{1}}{\lambda_{\alpha}^{1}}

and, by Lemma
3.1
, the sequence
(v_{\alpha}^{1})
is bounded in
\mathcal{D}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Therefore, there exists

such that

Furthermore, by Lemma
3.3
and (

\int_{B(0,1)}\left|v_{\alpha}^{1}\right|^{q}\mathrm{d}{x}=\delta+o(1)\qquad\text{as}\quad\alpha\to\infty.

On the other hand, by Remark

and (

\sup_{y\in\overline{\Omega_{\alpha}}}\int_{B(y,1)}\left|v_{\alpha}^{1}\right|^{q}\mathrm{d}{x}\leq C_{\tau}^{q}\sup_{y\in\overline{\Omega}}\int_{B(y,\lambda_{\alpha}^{1})}\left|u_{\alpha}^{1}\right|^{q}\left|z\right|^{-bq}\mathrm{d}{x}=C_{\tau}^{q}\delta

as

(8.3)

\delta+o(1)\leq\sup_{y\in\overline{\Omega_{\alpha}}}\int_{B(y,1)}\left|v_{\alpha}^{1}\right|^{q}\mathrm{d}{x}\leq C_{\tau}^{q}\delta\leq\left(\frac{C_{a,b}}{2^{p}}\right)^{\frac{q}{q-p}}

where, for the last step, we used (
8.2

Step 5
. Fix a compact set
K\subseteq\Omega
and let
(h_{\alpha})
in
\mathcal{D}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be a bounded sequence with

and

for each index
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}
. It follows from Lemma
5.1

(8.4)

\left\langle\phi^{\prime}_{\infty}(v_{\alpha}^{1}),h_{\alpha}\right\rangle=\left\langle\phi^{\prime}(u_{\alpha}^{1}),\tau^{-}_{y_{\alpha}^{1},\lambda_{\alpha}^{1}}h_{\alpha}\right\rangle+o\left(1\right)

as
\alpha\to\infty
. By a Riesz-type representation result, i.e. characterization (
2.2
), we may find, for each
\alpha
, functions
f_{\alpha}^{(1)},\dots,f_{\alpha}^{(n)}
in

such that

\left\langle\phi^{\prime}(u_{\alpha}^{1}),h\right\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{\Omega}f_{\alpha}^{(i)}(z)\partial_{i}h(z)\left|z\right|^{-ap}\mathrm{d}{z},\qquad\forall h\in\mathcal{D}_{a}^{1,p}(\Omega,0).

Moreover, since
\phi^{\prime}(u_{\alpha}^{1})\to 0
strongly in the dual of
\mathcal{D}_{a}^{1,p}(\Omega,0)
,

(8.5)

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{\Omega}\left|f_{\alpha}^{(i)}(z)\right|^{p^{\prime}}\left|z\right|^{-ap}\mathrm{d}{z}\to 0,\quad\text{as }\alpha\to\infty.

Combining (
8.4

\displaystyle\left\langle\phi^{\prime}_{\infty}(v^{1}_{\alpha}),h_{\alpha}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{\Omega}f_{\alpha}^{(i)}(z)\partial_{i}\left[\tau^{-}_{y_{\alpha}^{1},\lambda_{\alpha}^{1}}h_{\alpha}\right](z)\left|z\right|^{-ap}\mathrm{d}{z}+o(1)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{\Omega}f_{\alpha}^{(i)}(z)\partial_{i}\left[\tau^{-}_{y_{\alpha}^{1},\lambda_{\alpha}^{1}}h_{\alpha}\right](z)\left[\left|z\right|^{-ap}-\left|y_{\alpha}^{1}\right|^{-ap}\right]\mathrm{d}{z}

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{\Omega}f_{\alpha}^{(i)}(z)\partial_{i}\left[\tau^{-}_{y_{\alpha}^{1},\lambda_{\alpha}^{1}}h_{\alpha}\right](z)\left|y_{\alpha}^{1}\right|^{-ap}\mathrm{d}{z}+o(1).

Since
h_{\alpha}
is supported in
K
, each integrand is supported in
\lambda_{\alpha}^{1}K+y_{\alpha}^{1}
; then, using Lemma
3.4
, we see that for all
\alpha

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Omega}f_{\alpha}^{(i)}(z)\partial_{i}\left[\tau^{-}_{y_{\alpha}^{1},\lambda_{\alpha}^{1}}h_{\alpha}\right](z)\left[\left|z\right|^{-ap}-\left|y_{\alpha}^{1}\right|^{-ap}\right]\mathrm{d}{z}\right|

Step 6

Since

\alpha

(2)

By Hölder’s inequality with exponents

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|h\right|^{p}\left|v_{\alpha}^{1}\right|^{q}\mathrm{d}{x}

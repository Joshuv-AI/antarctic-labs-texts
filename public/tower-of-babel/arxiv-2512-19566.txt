Ground state solutions to the nonlinear Born-Infeld problem
Bartosz Bieganowski, Norihisa Ikoma, Jarosław Mederski

Abstract
. In the paper we show the existence of ground state solutions to the nonlinear Born-Infeld problem div ⁡ ( ∇ u 1 − | ∇ u | 2 ) + f ⁡ ( u ) = 0 , x ∈ ℝ N \mathrm{div}\,\left(\frac{\nabla u}{\sqrt{1-|\nabla u|^{2}}}\right)+f(u)=0,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{N} in the zero and positive mass cases. Moreover, we find a new proof of the Sobolev-type inequality ∫ ℝ N ( 1 − 1 − | ∇ u | 2 ) ​ 𝑑 x ≥ C N , p ​ ( ∫ ℝ N | u | p ​ 𝑑 x ) N N + p , \int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(1-\sqrt{1-|\nabla u|^{2}}\right)\,dx\geq C_{N,p}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{p}\,dx\right)^{\frac{N}{N+p}}, for p > 2 ∗ p>2^{*} as well as the characterization of the optimal constant C N , p C_{N,p} in terms of the ground state energy level. Previous approaches relied on approximation schemes and/or symmetry assumptions, which typically yield to compact embeddings and may lead to solutions that are not at the ground state energy level. In contrast, neither approximation arguments nor symmetry assumptions are employed in the paper to obtain a ground state solution. Instead, we develop a new direct variational approach based on minimization over a Pohožaev manifold combined with profile decomposition techniques. Finally, we show that nonradial solutions exist whenever N ≥ 4 N\geq 4 ; in particular, this settles a previously open problem in the case N = 5 N=5 . Keywords: Born-Infeld equation, infinite energy problem, ground states, profile decompositions, nonradial solutions. AMS Subject Classification: 35A15, 35J25, 35J93, 35Q75



1.
Introduction

In this paper, we consider the nonlinear problem

(1.1)

\mathrm{div}\,\left(\frac{\nabla u}{\sqrt{1-|\nabla u|^{2}}}\right)+f(u)=0,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

The problem (
1.1
) appears when one considers the electromagnetic theory introduced by Born and Infeld
[
13
,
14
,
15
,
16
]
as a nonlinear alternative to the Maxwell theory. The new electromagnetic theory solves the well-known
infinite energy problem
. Namely, if we consider the electromagnetic field
generated by a point charge in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, and the corresponding scalar field equation in the Maxwell theory

\Delta\phi+\delta_{0}=0,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{3},

then the
fundamental solution

\phi(x)=\frac{C}{|x|}

where
C>0
, has an infinite energy

E_{M}(\phi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla\phi(x)|^{2}\,dx=+\infty.

In the Born-Infeld theory the scalar field equation is governed by the operator
\mathrm{div}\,\left(\frac{\nabla\phi}{\sqrt{1-|\nabla\phi|^{2}}}\right)
. Then the fundamental solution
\phi(x)=\phi(|x|)
to

\mathrm{div}\,\left(\frac{\nabla\phi}{\sqrt{1-|\nabla\phi|^{2}}}\right)+\delta_{0}=0,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{3},

is given by the derivative of its radial profile with some
b>0

\phi^{\prime}(r)=\frac{b^{2}}{\sqrt{r^{4}+b^{2}}}

and its energy

E_{BI}(\phi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left(1-\sqrt{1-|\nabla\phi(x)|^{2}}\right)\,dx=4\pi\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(1-\frac{r^{2}}{\sqrt{r^{4}+b^{2}}}\right)r^{2}\,dr<\infty.

We remark that (
1.1
) has variational structure. In fact, the energy functional associated to (
1.1
) is given by

(1.2)

I(u):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(1-\sqrt{1-|\nabla u|^{2}}\right)\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}F(u)\,dx=\Psi(u)-\Phi(u),

where we set

F(u):=\int_{0}^{u}f(s)\,ds,\quad\Psi(u):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(1-\sqrt{1-|\nabla u|^{2}}\right)\,dx,\quad\Phi(u):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}F(u)\,dx.

Formally, if
\|\nabla u\|_{\infty}=\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}<1
, then

\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{I(u+t\varphi)-I(u)}{h}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\nabla u\cdot\nabla\varphi}{\sqrt{1-|\nabla u|^{2}}}-f(u)\varphi\,dx

and
u
is a weak solution of (
1.1
) provided
u
is a critical point of
I
with
\|\nabla u\|_{\infty}<1
.
However, it should be pointed out that for the well-definedness of
\Psi
, the condition
|\nabla u|\leq 1
for a.e.
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
is required and
it is not possible to find a function space on which
\Psi
becomes of class
{\mathcal{C}}^{1}
.
Therefore, we need to go beyond the smooth critical point theory to treat
I
directly on some function space,
and the nonsmoothness of
\Psi
makes the problem complicated.

The problem

\mathrm{div}\,\left(\frac{\nabla u}{\sqrt{1-|\nabla\phi|^{2}}}\right)+\rho=0

with fixed
\rho
has been studied by many authors; see, for example,

[
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
,
11
,
17
,
20
,
25
,
26
]
.
On the other hand, the literature for (
1.1
) is fragmentary.
In
[
12
]
, the power nonlinearities
f(u)=|u|^{p-2}u
with
p>2^{*}

are studied and the existence of radial positive classical solution and infinitely many radial solutions is proved
through approximations of the differential operator. Furthermore, the following Sobolev type inequality is
established and the existence of optimizers is studied:

(1.3)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(1-\sqrt{1-|\nabla u|^{2}}\right)dx\geq C_{N,p}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{p}\,dx\right)^{\frac{N}{N+p}}.

In
[
1
]
, (
1.1
) is studied for
N\geq 2
and
f(u)=-u+g(u)
by the shooting method,
and the existence of radial positive decreasing solution is found under some assumptions on
g
.
On the other hand,
[
2
]
treats the case
N\geq 3
and
f(u)=|u|^{p-2}u
with
p>2
and
p\neq 2^{*}
where
2^{*}:=2N/(N-2)
.
It is proved that for
p\in(2,2^{*})
, (
1.1
) has no radial solution decaying at infinity, and
for
p>2^{*}
, (
1.1
) has infinitely many radial solutions with infinite energy, that is
\|\nabla u\|_{2}=\infty
.
More general nonlinearities are considered in
[
30
]
for the zero and the positive mass case,
and the existence of at least one radial solutions is established through the mountain pass theorem and a similar approximation in
[
12
]
.
Finally,
[
18
]
develops the monotonicity trick due to
[
31
,
24
,
21
]
for nonsmooth functionals in the setting of
[
32
]
and
the existence of infinitely many radial solutions of (
1.1
) is proved.
In addition, the existence of nonradial solutions is also shown when
N=4
or
N\geq 6
.
We also refer to
[
8
]
for the regularity result of radial local minimizers
and
[
3
]
for the
L^{2}
-normalized solutions.
It is worth mentioning that in all these works, radial symmetry (or some symmetry of functions) plays a crucial role
to overcome the lack of compactness.
Due to the usage of the symmetry,
the existence of nonradial solutions to (
1.1
) when
N=5
is missing in
[
18
]
.

In this paper, we aim to develop a concentration compactness argument or profile decompositions
for the nonsmooth functional corresponding to (
1.1
) and this allows us to discuss
the existence of a ground state solution to (
1.1
), i.e.,
a solution with the least possible energy, which seems to be the most relevant from the physical point of view.
Moreover, the argument can be applied for the existence of nonradial solutions when
N=5
.
For this purpose, we introduce a new direct variational approach based
on minimization over a Pohožaev set, as we shall see later.
Since we study (
1.1
) in the zero and the positive mass case as in
[
30
,
18
]
,
two sets of assumptions are provided. After the existence of ground state solutions,
nonradial solutions are studied.
We start our considerations with the
zero mass case
in the spirit of Berestycki and Lions
[
5
]
.

1.1.
The zero mass case

In this subsection, the results for the zero mass case are stated.
To explain our setting, we first introduce a function space we work on in this case.
The following choice is taken from
[
17
,
18
]
. See
[
8
]
for another choice of function space.

Let
N\geq 3
and choose
j_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\max\{2^{*},N\}<2j_{0}
. Then we introduce the function space

X_{0}:=\left\{u\in L^{2^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\ :\ \|u\|_{X_{0}}:=\|\nabla u\|_{2}+\|\nabla u\|_{2j_{0}}<\infty\right\}.

Thanks to
N<2j_{0}
, it is easily seen from Sobolev’s and the interpolation inequality that

(1.4)

X_{0}\subset W^{1,2^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap{\mathcal{C}}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\quad{\mathcal{C}}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):=\left\{u\in{\mathcal{C}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\ :\ \lim_{|x|\to\infty}u(x)=0\right\}.

Note that if
u\in X_{0}
and
\|\nabla u\|_{\infty}\leq 1
, then the inequality
1-\sqrt{1-t^{2}}\leq t^{2}
in
[0,1]
gives

\Psi(u)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(1-\sqrt{1-|\nabla u|^{2}}\right)\,dx\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}}}\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}dx<\infty.

This observation allows us to define the
domain
of
\Psi
as

D(\Psi):=\left\{u\in X_{0}\ :\ \|\nabla u\|_{\infty}\leq 1\right\}.

Then
\Psi:X_{0}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}
is a convex functional with a convex domain
D(\Psi)
.

Next, to treat
\Phi
in (
1.2
) on
X_{0}
, we introduce the following assumptions on
f
:

(F1)

f\in{\mathcal{C}}^{1}(\mathbb{R})
and
f(0)=0
;

(F2)

\displaystyle\limsup_{s\to 0}\frac{f^{\prime}(s)}{|s|^{2^{*}-2}}=0
;

(F3)

sH^{\prime}(s)>0
for all
s\neq 0
where
H(s):=F(s)+\frac{1}{N}f(s)s
;

(F4)

\displaystyle\lim_{|s|\to\infty}H(s)=\infty
;

(F5)

s\mapsto f(s)s
is nonincreasing in
(-\infty,0)
and nondecreasing in
(0,\infty)
;

(F6)

|F(s)|\lesssim|f(s)s|
for
s\in\mathbb{R}
, that is, there exists a
C\geq 0
such that
|F(s)|\leq C|f(s)s|
for every
s\in\mathbb{R}
.

Remark 1.1
.

(i)

Under (
F1
), condition (
F5
) is equivalent to
0\leq f^{\prime}(s)s^{2}+f(s)s
for every
s\in\mathbb{R}
.
Thus, a direct computation shows that (
F1
) and (
F5
) with an additional condition
sf(s)>0
for each
s\neq 0
imply (
F3
).

(ii)

Condition (
F6
) cannot be derived from (
F1
)–(
F5
) and such an example of
f
is given as follows.
Let
k_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
satisfy
2^{*}<2k_{0}
and consider

F(s):=\log(1+s^{2k_{0}})\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
. Since
f(s)=2k_{0}s^{2k_{0}-1}/(1+s^{2k_{0}})
,
it is immediate to verify (
F1
)–(
F5
), however, (
F6
) is not satisfied.

By (
1.4
), (
F1
) and (
F2
), we may verify
\Phi\in{\mathcal{C}}^{1}(X_{0};\mathbb{R})
and
it allows us to define the notion of a critical point of
I
as in
[
32
]
. Namely,
u\in X_{0}
is said to be a
critical point of
I
if

(1.5)

\Psi(v)-\Psi(u)-\Phi^{\prime}(u)(v-u)\geq 0\quad\text{for all $v\in X_{0}$},

that is,
\Phi^{\prime}(u)\in\partial\Psi(u)
, where
\partial\Psi(u)
denotes the
subdifferential
of a convex functional
\Psi
.

To discuss the existence of ground state solutions of (
1.1
),
we will introduce the Pohožaev set
{\mathcal{M}}_{0}
for (
1.1
). Let

(1.6)

M_{0}(u):=\Psi(u)-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}H(u)\,dx=\Psi(u)-\Phi(u)-\frac{1}{N}\Phi^{\prime}(u)(u)

and define
{\mathcal{M}}_{0}
by

(1.7)

{\mathcal{M}}_{0}:=\left\{u\in X_{0}\setminus\{0\}\ :\ M_{0}(u)=0\right\}.

Since for
u\in X_{0}\setminus D(\Psi)

\Psi(u)=\infty
and
M_{0}(u)=\infty
hold, we easily get
{\mathcal{M}}_{0}\subset D(\Psi)\setminus\{0\}
.
For the semilinear scalar field problems, it is known that each reasonable solution satisfies the Pohožaev identity (for instance, see
[
5
, Proposition 1]
),
and hence any reasonable solution belongs to the Pohožaev set.
A counterpart for (
1.1
) of this fact is obtained in
[
18
, Proposition 2.6]
(see also
Section

2
).
Therefore, we are led to the following minimizing problem:

c_{0}:=\inf_{{\mathcal{M}}_{0}}I.

Remark that if there exists a minimizer
u
corresponding to
c_{0}
and if
u
is a nontrivial solution of (
1.1
),
then
u
has the least energy among all nontrivial solutions of (
1.1
) in
X_{0}
, and hence
u
is a ground state solution of (
1.1
).

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
N\geq 3
and assume (
F1
)–(
F6
). Then the following assertions hold:

(i)

0<c_{0}=c_{0,MP}
holds where
c_{0,MP}
is a mountain pass level given by (
4.3
);

(ii)

every minimizing sequence
(u_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
for
c_{0}
is relatively compact in
X_{0}
up to translations,
and if
\|u_{n}-u_{0}\|_{X_{0}}\to 0
, then
u_{0}\in{\mathcal{M}}_{0}
attains
c_{0}
and is a ground state solution of (
1.1
),
namely
u_{0}
is a classical solution of (
1.1
) with
I(u_{0})=c_{0}
;

(iii)

for each ground state solution
u
of (
1.1
), either
u>0
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
or
u<0
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
holds, and
there exists a ground state solution of (
1.1
) which is radially symmetric.

Remark 1.3
.

By
1.2
(iii), the ground state energy of (
1.1
) is equal to
the
radial
ground state energy, that is, the least energy among all
radial
nontrivial solutions of (
1.1
) in
X_{0}
.
However, a priori, it is not clear whether the ground state energy coincides with the radial one
since we do not impose on
f
the oddness.

When
f(u)=|u|^{p-2}u
with
p>2^{*}
,
as in the seminlinear case (see
[
29
]
), the existence of minimizers corresponding to
c_{0}

implies the Sobolev type inequality (
1.3
) obtained in
[
12
]
,
the expression of the best constant with
c_{0}

and the characterization of every optimizer with the ground state solutions of (
1.1
):

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
N\geq 3
and
p>2^{*}
. Then for any
u\in X_{0}
there holds

(1.8)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(1-\sqrt{1-|\nabla u|^{2}}\right)\,dx\geq\left(\frac{N+p}{Np}\right)N^{\frac{p}{N+p}}\left(\inf_{{\mathcal{M}}_{0}}I\right)^{\frac{p}{N+p}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{p}\,dx\right)^{\frac{N}{N+p}}.

Moreover, every optimizer
v
has a form of
v(x)=\theta u(x/\theta)
,
where
\theta>0
and
u
is a ground state solution of (
1.1
) with
f(u)=|u|^{p-2}u
.

Remark 1.5
.

(1)

A difference between
[
12
, Theorem 1.2]
and
1.4

is in the characterization of optimizer through ground state solutions of (
1.1
) with
f(u)=|u|^{p-2}u
.
In fact, in
[
12
, Theorem 1.2]
it is shown that the constant is achieved by a radial solution of (
1.1
).

(2)

When
p=2^{*}
, we may also obtain a similar inequality to (
1.8
) with the best constant involving
\inf_{{\mathcal{M}}_{0}}I
.
However, in this case, the constant is never achieved as shown in
[
12
]
.

1.2.
Positive mass case

In this case, let
N\geq 1

and suppose that the nonlinearity
f
is of the form
f(s)=-s+g(s)
, where
g\in{\mathcal{C}}^{1}(\mathbb{R})
satisfies the following conditions:

(G1)

g^{\prime}(0)=0=g(0)
;

(G2)

the map
\displaystyle s\mapsto\frac{g(s)}{|s|}
is strictly increasing on
\mathbb{R}
;

(G3)

\displaystyle\lim_{|s|\to\infty}\frac{g(s)}{s}=\infty
.

In this case, choose
j_{0}
such that
2j_{0}>N
and introduce the space

X_{1}:=\{u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\ :\ \nabla u\in L^{2j_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\}

with the norm

\|u\|_{X_{1}}:=\|u\|_{H^{1}}+\|\nabla u\|_{2j_{0}}.

Then, the variational functional
I
is given by (
1.2
),
\Psi
is defined as before with the natural domain

D(\Psi):=\left\{u\in X_{1}\ :\ \|\nabla u\|_{\infty}\leq 1\right\}

and

\Phi(u):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}-\frac{1}{2}u^{2}+G(u)\,dx,\quad G(s):=\int_{0}^{s}g(t)\,dt.

Remark that
\Psi
is a convex functional on a convex domain
D(\Psi)
and
\Phi\in{\mathcal{C}}^{1}(X_{1};\mathbb{R})
.
Therefore, the notation of critical points of
I
on
X_{1}
is defined as in (
1.5
) by changing
X_{0}
to
X_{1}
.
We also introduce the Pohožaev set as

{\mathcal{M}}_{1}:=\left\{u\in X_{1}\setminus\{0\}\ :\ M_{1}(u)=0\right\}\subset D(\Psi)\setminus\{0\},\quad M_{1}(u):=\Psi(u)-\Phi(u)-\frac{1}{N}\Phi^{\prime}(u)(u)

and consider the following minimizing problem:

c_{1}:=\inf_{{\mathcal{M}}_{1}}I.

Then
c_{1}
gives the ground state energy as follows:

Theorem 1.6
.

Suppose
N\geq 1
,
f(s)=-s+g(s)
and (
G1
)–(
G3
). Then

(i)

0<c_{1}=c_{1,MP}
holds where
c_{1,MP}
is a mountain pass level;

(ii)

every minimizing sequence
(u_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
for
c_{0}
is relatively compact in
X_{1}
up to translations,
and if
\|u_{n}-u_{0}\|_{X_{1}}\to 0
, then
u_{1}\in{\mathcal{M}}_{1}
attains
c_{1}
and is a ground state solution of (
1.1
),
namely
u_{0}
is a classical solution of (
1.1
) with
I(u_{0})=c_{1}
;

(iii)

for each ground state solution
u
of (
1.1
), either
u>0
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
or
u<0
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
holds,
and there exists a ground state solution of (
1.1
) which is radially symmetric.

1.3.
Nonradial solutions

Now we show that (
1.1
) also admits nonradial solutions provided
N\geq 4
.
Let
X
denote the space
X_{0}
in the
zero-mass case
, or
X_{1}
in the
positive-mass case
. Similarly, let
{\mathcal{M}}
denote the corresponding Pohožaev set,
{\mathcal{M}}_{0}
or
{\mathcal{M}}_{1}
, respectively.
We treat both cases simultaneously and assume either (
F1
)–(
F6
) or (
G1
)–(
G3
), depending on which case is considered.

The approach here is taken from
[
4
,
6
,
22
,
28
,
29
,
34
]
.
Let
k_{1},k_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
satisfy
k_{1},k_{2}\geq 2
and
N-k_{1}-k_{2}\geq 0

and write
x=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in\mathbb{R}^{k_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{k_{2}}\times\mathbb{R}^{N-k_{1}-k_{2}}

(if
N=k_{1}+k_{2}
, then we write
x=(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{k_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{k_{2}}
).
Define

\displaystyle{\mathcal{O}}

\displaystyle:={\mathcal{O}}(k_{1})\times{\mathcal{O}}(k_{2})\times\{\mathrm{id}\}\subset{\mathcal{O}}(N),

\displaystyle X_{{\mathcal{O}}}

\displaystyle:=\left\{u\in X\ :\ u(\rho x)=u(x)\quad\text{for any $x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}$ and $\rho\in{\mathcal{O}}$}\right\}.

For each
u\in X_{\mathcal{O}}
, we identity
u(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})
with
u(r_{1},r_{2},x_{3})
where
r_{1}=|x_{1}|
and
r_{2}=|x_{2}|
.
Then we define
\tau
by

\tau(r_{1},r_{2},x_{3}):=(r_{2},r_{1},x_{3}),\quad(\tau u)(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}):=-u(\tau(|x_{1}|,|x_{2}|,x_{3}))=-u(|x_{2}|,|x_{1}|,x_{3}).

Remark that
\tau:X_{\mathcal{O}}\to X_{\mathcal{O}}
is an isometry. We also set

X_{\tau}:=\left\{u\in X_{\mathcal{O}}\ :\ \tau u=u\right\}.

Clearly, if
u\in X_{\tau}
is radial, i.e.
u(x)=u(\rho x)
for any
\rho\in{\mathcal{O}}(N)
, then
u\equiv 0
.
Hence
X_{\tau}
does not contain any nontrivial radial functions.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
N\geq 4
and fix
k_{1},k_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
so that
k_{1},k_{2}\geq 2
and
N-k_{1}-k_{2}\geq 0
.
Suppose (
F1
)–(
F6
) (resp. (
G1
)–(
G3
)) and that
f
(reps.
g
) is odd.
Then there is a nontrivial critical point
u_{0}\in{\mathcal{M}}\cap X_{\tau}=:{\mathcal{M}}_{\tau}
such that

I(u_{0})=\inf_{{\mathcal{M}}_{\tau}}I\geq\inf_{{\mathcal{M}}}I>0
. Moreover, when
k_{1}=k_{2}
, then

I(u_{0})=\inf_{{\mathcal{M}}_{\tau}}I>2\inf_{{\mathcal{M}}}I>0.

In particular
u_{0}
is a nonradial classical solution of (
1.1
).

1.4.
Comments

We describe arguments to prove
1.2
and
1.6
and their difficulties.
As explained above, in the literature, the existence of ground state solutions of (
1.1
) with a general nonlinearity
f
is not studied.
As pointed as in
Section

1.1
, it is not clear that radial functions provide the ground state energy of (
1.1
) a priori,
and hence the radial symmetry may not be exploited to prove the existence of ground state solutions for a general nonlinearity
f
.
Thus, we face the lack of compact embedding of
X_{0}
or
X_{1}
, and
to the best of the authors’ knowledge, this paper is the first to deal with the lack of compactness for (
1.1
).
In addition to the lack of compact embedding, the nonsmoothness of functionals
I,M_{0},M_{1}
also makes the analysis complicated and different from
the semilinear case (for instance,
[
5
,
28
,
29
]
).
Thus, to solve difficulties and to prove
1.2
and
1.6
, new ideas or arguments are necessary.

To show the existence of minimizers corresponding to
c_{0}
and
c_{1}
,
consider any minimizing sequence
(u_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset X_{0}
for
c_{0}
(resp.
(u_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset X_{1}
for
c_{1}
).
Since the Pohožaev functionals
M_{0}
and
M_{1}
are different from the semilinear case,
the boundedness of
(u_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in
X_{0}
(resp.
X_{1}
) is an issue.
To overcome this point, in the zero mass case, (
F6
) is used.

2.
Concentration-compactness and a profile decomposition

In this section, preliminary results are stated and proved in the zero mass and positive mass cases.
Throughout this section and
Section

3
, we assume the following:

(2.1)

p_{0}:=\begin{cases}2^{*}&\text{(the zero mass case)},\\
2&\text{(the positive mass case)},\end{cases}\quad X:=\begin{cases}X_{0}&\text{(the zero mass case)},\\
X_{1}&\text{(the positive mass case)}\end{cases}

and set

\|u\|_{X}:=\begin{cases}\|u\|_{X_{0}}&\text{(the zero mass case)},\\
\|u\|_{X_{1}}&\text{(the positive mass case)}.\end{cases}

Then
(X,\|\cdot\|_{X})
is a reflexive Banach space (cf.
[
17
, Proposition 3.3]
) and the following embedding holds
(see (
1.4
) for the definition of
{\mathcal{C}}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
):

(2.2)

X\subset W^{1,p_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap{\mathcal{C}}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

Remark also that
D(\Psi)=\{u\in X\ :\ \|\nabla u\|_{\infty}\leq 1\}
is convex in
X
.
Similarly, put

M(u):=\begin{cases}M_{0}(u)&\text{(the zero mass case)},\\
M_{1}(u)&\text{(the positive mass case)}.\end{cases}

Finally,
the space dimension
N
is assumed to be
N\geq 3
in the zero mass case and
N\geq 1
in the positive mass case.

We first prove that the functional
\Psi
restricted on
D(\Psi)
is continuous:

Lemma 2.1
.

Assume
(u_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset D(\Psi)
,
u\in D(\Psi)
and
\|u_{n}-u\|_{X}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
.
Then
\Psi(u_{n})\to\Psi(u)
as
n\to\infty
.

Proof.

Notice that

(2.3)

\frac{1}{2}t^{2}\leq\sqrt{1-t^{2}}-1\leq t^{2}\quad\text{for any $t\in[-1,1]$}.

Remark also that
\|u_{n}-u\|_{X}\to 0
yields
\|\nabla u_{n}-\nabla u\|_{2}\to 0
.
Then we may find a subsequence
(u_{n_{k}})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
and
w\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that

\nabla u_{n_{k}}(x)\to\nabla u(x)
a.e.
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
|\nabla u_{n_{k}}(x)|+|\nabla u(x)|\leq w(x)
a.e.
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
By (
2.3
) with
|\nabla u(x)|\leq 1
and
|\nabla u_{n_{k}}(x)|\leq 1
due to
u_{n_{k}},u\in D(\Psi)
,
the dominated convergence theorem gives
\Psi(u_{n_{k}})\to\Psi(u)
as
k\to\infty
.
Since the limit is independent of subsequences, we have
\Psi(u_{n})\to\Psi(u)
as
n\to\infty

and this completes the proof.
∎

Next we introduce

\Psi_{0}(u):=\begin{cases}\Psi(u)&\text{(the zero mass case)},\\
\Psi(u)+a\|u\|^{2}_{2}&\text{(the positive mass case)},\end{cases}

where
a\in(0,\infty)
is any fixed number.

Lemma 2.2
.

Suppose that
(u_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset D(\Psi)
satisfies
u_{n}\rightharpoonup u
weakly in
X
and
\Psi_{0}(u_{n})\to\Psi_{0}(u)
.
Then
\|u_{n}-u\|_{X}\to 0
holds.

Proof.

The following argument is essentially contained in
[
17
, Proof of Proposition 3.11]
.
From the uniform convexity of
L^{p}
spaces, it suffices to prove

\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{2j}\to\|\nabla u\|_{2j}
for each
j\in\mathbb{N}
and
\|u_{n}\|_{2}\to\|u\|_{2}
in the positive mass case.
Since
\Psi
is weakly lower semicontinuous due to the convexity, we have

\Psi(u)\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\Psi(u_{n})
.
Moreover, in the positive mass case,

\Psi(u_{n})\to\Psi(u)
and
\|u_{n}\|_{2}\to\|u\|_{2}
hold.
In fact, suppose that there exists
(u_{n_{k}})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
\Psi(u)<\limsup_{k\to\infty}\Psi(u_{n_{k}})
.
By passing to a subsequence if necessary, we may also suppose
\limsup_{k\to\infty}\Psi(u_{n_{k}})=\lim_{k\to\infty}\Psi(u_{n_{k}})
. Then

\displaystyle\Psi_{0}(u)=\Psi(u)+a\|u\|_{2}^{2}<\lim_{k\to\infty}\Psi(u_{n_{k}})+a\liminf_{k\to\infty}\|u_{n_{k}}\|_{2}^{2}=\liminf_{k\to\infty}\Psi_{0}(u_{n_{k}})=\Psi_{0}(u),

which is a contradiction. Hence
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\Psi(u_{n})\leq\Psi(u)
and

\Psi(u_{n})\to\Psi(u)
holds.
In a similarly way,
\|u_{n}\|_{2}\to\|u\|_{2}
can be proved and
\|u_{n}-u\|_{2}\to 0
holds in the positive mass case.

To prove
\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{2j}\to\|\nabla u\|_{2j}
from
\Psi(u_{n})\to\Psi(u)
,
let us recall the following expansion (
[
25
]
)

(2.4)

1-\sqrt{1-t^{2}}=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}b_{j}t^{2j},\quad b_{j}>0.

In particular,

\Psi(u)=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}b_{j}\|\nabla u\|_{2j}^{2j}\quad\text{for any $u\in D(\Psi)$}.

For each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, set

\Psi_{k,1}(u):=\sum_{j=1}^{k}b_{j}\|\nabla u\|_{2j}^{2j},\quad\Psi_{k,2}(u):=\sum_{j=k+1}^{\infty}b_{j}\|\nabla u\|_{2j}^{2j}.

It is not hard to see that
\Psi_{k,1}
and
\Psi_{k,2}
are convex on
D(\Psi)
, and weakly lower semicontinuous.
By utilizing the above argument and
\Psi(u_{n})\to\Psi(u)
,
we obtain
\Psi_{k,1}(u_{n})\to\Psi(u)
and
\Psi_{k,2}(u_{n})\to\Psi(u)
for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
By starting from the case
k=1
,
\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{2j}^{2j}\to\|\nabla u\|_{2j}^{2j}
is obtained for every
j\geq 1
.
This completes the proof.
∎

Finally, we prove that every critical point of

in

becomes a classical solution of (

) and satisfies the Pohožaev identity.
A similar assertion is obtained in
[

for

.
Here our main focus is in the case

. Notice that under (

)–(

) (resp. (

\Phi\in{\mathcal{C}}^{1}(X;\mathbb{R})
due to (

Lemma 2.3
.

Suppose either

and (

)–(

) or else

and (

).
Then each critical point
u
of

in

is a classical solution of (

) and the Pohožaev identity

holds.

Proof.

Let
u\in X
be a critical point of
I
and define

\rho(x):=\begin{cases}f(u(x))&\text{(the zero mass case)},\\
-u(x)+g(u(x))&\text{(the positive mass case)}.\end{cases}

By (
2.2
),
\rho\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap{\mathcal{C}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
holds and by rewriting (
1.5
),

is a (unique) minimizer of

When
N\geq 3
, from
[
18
, Propositions 2.13]
(cf.
[
18
, Proposition 2.10]
),

) and

In particular, by

-\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}a_{ij}(x)\partial_{i}\partial_{j}u=\rho\quad\text{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{N},\quad a_{ij}(x):=\frac{\delta_{ij}}{\sqrt{1-|\nabla u(x)|^{2}}}+\frac{\partial_{i}u(x)\partial_{j}u(x)}{\left(1-|\nabla u(x)|^{2}\right)^{3/2}}

.

We remark that the argument in

]

.

Let

and

be a critical point of

,
and notice that

) and (

).
We follow the argument in

.
Without any changes, the argument in
[
8
, Proposition 2.7]
shows that

(2.6)

\frac{(u^{\prime})^{2}}{\sqrt{1-(u^{\prime})^{2}}}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}),\quad\frac{u^{\prime}\psi^{\prime}}{\sqrt{1-(u^{\prime})^{2}}}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})\quad\text{for each $\psi\in X_{1}$ with $\psi^{\prime}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})$}.

We next prove that

is a weak solution of (

) by a slightly modified argument in
[

, Proof of Theorem 1.4]
.
Let

If

.

In particular,

is a weak solution of (

).
Furthermore, by

, the above equality asserts that

.

In what follows, we may suppose

for each

.
Notice that

,
|E_{2}|<\infty
and

as

by (

).
Let

be large enough so that

and set

\eta_{0}(x):=\frac{1}{|[-n_{0},n_{0}]\cap E_{2}^{c}|}\int_{-\infty}^{x}\bm{1}_{[-n_{0},n_{0}]\cap E_{2}^{c}}(s)\,ds\in W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}).

For
k\geq 2
, introduce

a_{k}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi^{\prime}(s)\bm{1}_{E_{k}^{c}}(s)\,ds=\int_{E_{k}^{c}}\psi^{\prime}(s)\,ds\in\mathbb{R}.

Since

as

, the fact

yields

\lim_{k\to\infty}a_{k}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi^{\prime}\,ds=0.

Define

by

\psi_{k}(x):=\int_{-\infty}^{x}\psi^{\prime}(s)\bm{1}_{E_{k}^{c}}(s)\,ds-a_{k}\eta_{0}(x).

Let

satisfy

. Since
\eta_{0}
and
\psi_{k}
are constant on

the choice of

for every

and

|\psi_{k}^{\prime}(x)|>0

. Furthermore, from

and
|E_{k}|\to 0
with

,
it follows that
\|\psi_{k}-\psi\|_{1}\to 0
.
By
\|u^{\prime}+t\psi_{k}^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\leq 1-1/(2k)
for

,
the dominated convergence theorem yields

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{J(u+t\psi_{k})-J(u)}{t}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{u^{\prime}\psi_{k}^{\prime}}{\sqrt{1-(u^{\prime})^{2}}}\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho\psi_{k}\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{u^{\prime}\left(\psi^{\prime}\bm{1}_{E_{k}^{c}}-a_{k}\eta_{0}^{\prime}\right)}{\sqrt{1-(u^{\prime})^{2}}}\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho\psi_{k}\,dx.

Recalling that
u
is a minimizer of
J
, we observe that for each

and

Letting

leads to

and

.

, Proof of Proposition 2.6]

3.
Concentration-compactness and a profile decomposition

In this section, we establish a profile decomposition for bounded sequences in
X
, which is useful to prove the existence of minimizers of
I
on
{\mathcal{M}}_{i}
.
Recall that in this section, the same notation to
Section

2
is used:
p_{0}
,
X
and
\Psi_{0}
.
For the profile decomposition, several lemmas are necessary. Firstly, a slight extension of
[
18
, Lemma 2.27]
is needed:

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
Q:=[0,1]^{N}
,
(u_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset X
be bounded in
X
and
\zeta\in{\mathcal{C}}(\mathbb{R};[0,\infty))
satisfy

(3.1)

\limsup_{s\to 0}\frac{\zeta(s)}{|s|^{p_{0}}}=0.

Then for every
q\in(p_{0},p^{*}_{0})
and
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
where
1/p_{0}^{*}=(1/p_{0}-1/N)_{+}
,
there exists
C=C(L,\zeta,q,\varepsilon)>0
, where
L:=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|u_{n}\|_{X}
,
such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left|\zeta(u_{n})\right|dx\leq\varepsilon+C\left(\sup_{y\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\int_{y+Q}\left|u_{n}\right|^{q}dx\right)^{(q-p_{0})/q}.

In particular, if

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{y\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\int_{y+Q}|u_{n}|^{q}\,dx=0,

then

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\zeta(u_{n})\,dx\to 0.

Proof.

Let
q\in(p_{0},p_{0}^{*})
and
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
.
From (
2.1
) and (
2.2
),
W^{1,p_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\subset L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
holds.
Since
(u_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is bounded in
X
, it is bounded in
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.
By this bound in
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and (
3.1
), there exists
C=C(L,\zeta,q,\varepsilon)>0
such that

\zeta(u_{n}(x))\leq\varepsilon|u_{n}(x)|^{p_{0}}+C|u_{n}(x)|^{q}\quad\text{for all $x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}$ and $n\in\mathbb{N}$},

which leads to

(3.2)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\zeta(u_{n})\,dx\leq\varepsilon\|u_{n}\|_{p_{0}}^{p_{0}}+C\|u_{n}\|_{q}^{q}\quad\text{for all $n\in\mathbb{N}$}.

Recall
L=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|u_{n}\|_{X}<\infty
and
notice also that
W^{1,p_{0}}(Q)\subset L^{q}(Q)
,
X\subset W^{1,p_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap{\mathcal{C}}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and

\displaystyle\|u_{n}\|_{q}^{q}

\displaystyle=\sum_{y\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\|u_{n}\|_{L^{q}(y+Q)}^{q}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sup_{y\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\|u_{n}\|_{L^{q}(y+Q)}^{q-p_{0}}\right)\sum_{y\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\|u_{n}\|_{L^{q}(y+Q)}^{p_{0}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sup_{y\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\|u_{n}\|_{L^{q}(y+Q)}^{q-p_{0}}\right)\sum_{y\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}C_{N,p_{0},q}\|u_{n}\|_{W^{1,p_{0}}(y+Q)}^{p_{0}}

\displaystyle=C_{N,p_{0},q}\|u_{n}\|_{W^{1,p_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{p_{0}}\sup_{y\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\|u_{n}\|_{L^{q}(y+Q)}^{q-p_{0}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{N,p_{0},q}C_{L,N,p_{0}}\sup_{y\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\|u_{n}\|_{L^{q}(y+Q)}^{q-p_{0}}.

Thus (
3.2
) implies

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\zeta(u_{n})\,dx\leq\left(\sup_{n\geq 1}\|u_{n}\|_{p_{0}}^{p_{0}}\right)\varepsilon+C\sup_{y\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\left(\int_{y+Q}\left|u_{n}\right|^{q}dx\right)^{(q-p_{0})/q}.

Thus we complete the proof.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Suppose that
\zeta:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
is of class
{\mathcal{C}}^{1}
such that
|\zeta^{\prime}(s)|\lesssim|s|^{p_{0}-1}
for
|s|\leq 1
and
\zeta(0)=0
.
Let
(u_{n})\subset X
be a bounded sequence such that
u_{n}(x)\to u_{0}(x)
a.e.
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Then

\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\zeta(u_{n})\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\zeta(u_{n}-u_{0})\,dx\right)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\zeta(u_{0})\,dx.

Proof.

Observe that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\zeta(u_{n})\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\zeta(u_{n}-u_{0})\,dx

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{d}{ds}\zeta(u_{n}-su_{0})\,ds\,dx=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\zeta^{\prime}(u_{n}-su_{0})u_{0}\,dx\,ds

\displaystyle\to\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\zeta^{\prime}(u_{0}-su_{0})u_{0}\,dx\,ds=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{d}{ds}\zeta(u_{0}-su_{0})\,ds\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\zeta(u_{0})\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\zeta(u_{0}-u_{0})\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\zeta(u_{0})\,dx,

where to pass to the limit we used Vitali’s convergence theorem,
X\subset L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, the boundedness of
(u_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in
X
and the estimate

\left|\zeta^{\prime}(u_{n}-su_{0})\right|\lesssim|u_{n}-su_{0}|^{p_{0}-1}
a.e.
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
∎

Finally we prove the profile decomposition.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
(u_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset X
be bounded in
X
.
Then there are

,
(y_{n}^{j})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
\widetilde{u}_{j}\in X
for
0\leq j<K+1
such that
for any
a\in(0,\infty)
in the positive mass case,

\displaystyle u_{n}(\cdot+y_{n}^{j})

\displaystyle\rightharpoonup\widetilde{u}_{j}

\displaystyle\text{for $0\leq j<K+1$},

\displaystyle\widetilde{u}_{j}

\displaystyle\neq 0

\displaystyle\text{for }1\leq j<K+1,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\Psi_{0}(u_{n})

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{j=0}^{K}\Psi_{0}(\widetilde{u}_{j})

and

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\zeta(u_{n})\,dx=\sum_{j=0}^{K}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\zeta(\widetilde{u}_{j})\,dx

for any function
\zeta:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
of class
{\mathcal{C}}^{1}
such that
|\zeta^{\prime}(s)|\lesssim|s|^{p_{0}-1}
for
|s|\leq 1
and

\limsup_{s\to 0}\frac{\zeta(s)}{|s|^{p_{0}}}=0.

Proof.

Fix any
q\in(p_{0},p_{0}^{*})
.
Since
(u_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is bounded in
X
, up to a subsequence, we may assume
u_{n}\rightharpoonup\widetilde{u}_{0}

weakly in
X
,
u_{n}\to u_{0}
a.e.
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and set

If

, then we may put

and

. The convexity of

and

and

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\Psi_{0}(u_{n})\geq\Psi_{0}(\widetilde{u}_{0}),\quad\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\zeta(u_{n})\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\zeta(\widetilde{u}_{0})\,dx+\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\zeta(v^{0}_{n})\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\zeta(\widetilde{u}_{0})\,dx.

Hence the assertions hold.

On the other hand, when

, up to a subsequence,
we may choose

It is easily seen that

weakly in

v^{0}_{n}(\cdot+y_{n}^{1})\to\widetilde{u}_{1}
a.e. on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
Clearly,

We set

v^{1}_{n}:=v^{0}_{n}-\widetilde{u}_{1}(\cdot-y^{1}_{n})=u_{n}-\sum_{j=0}^{1}\widetilde{u}_{j}(\cdot-y^{j}_{n}),\quad d_{1}:=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\sup_{y\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\int_{y+Q}|v^{1}_{n}|^{q}\,dx.

Notice that
Section

3
gives

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\zeta(u_{n})\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\zeta(\widetilde{u}_{0})\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\zeta(v^{0}_{n})\,dx+o(1)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\zeta(\widetilde{u}_{0})\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\zeta(\widetilde{u}_{1})\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\zeta(v^{1}_{n})\,dx+o(1).

When

,
Section

3
implies

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\zeta(u_{n})\,dx=\sum_{j=0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\zeta(\widetilde{u}_{j})\,dx.

For the assertion on
\Psi_{0}
, let

be arbitrary and for a domain

write

\Psi_{0,\Omega}(u):=\begin{cases}\int_{\Omega}1-\sqrt{1-|\nabla u|^{2}}\,dx&\text{(the zero mass case)},\\
\int_{\Omega}1-\sqrt{1-|\nabla u|^{2}}+au^{2}\,dx&\text{(the positive mass case)}.\end{cases}

Since
y^{0}_{n}=0
,
|y_{n}^{1}|\to\infty
and

weakly in

, we obtain

Letting

Now let us consider the case

After taking a subsequence if necessary, we may choose

Then

\mathbb{R}^{N}

We define

Section

3

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\zeta(v_{n}^{1})\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\zeta(\widetilde{u}_{2})\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\zeta(v_{n}^{2})\,dx+o(1),\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\zeta(u_{n})\,dx=\sum_{j=0}^{2}\zeta(\widetilde{u}_{j})\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\zeta(v_{n}^{2})\,dx+o(1).

On the other hand, if

By setting

we repeat the same procedure. Thus, may define

(d_{k})
,
(v^{k}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

To apply

Thus,

By
Section

3
, (

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\zeta(u_{n})\,dx=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\zeta(\widetilde{u}_{j})\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\zeta(v^{k}_{n})\,dx+o(1),

\varepsilon>0

and

for each

For the assertion on

\frac{d}{d\theta}I(u_{\theta})=N\theta^{N-1}\left(\Psi(u)-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}H(\theta u)\,dx\right)

and therefore we have the following formula

\frac{d^{2}}{d\theta^{2}}I(u_{\theta})=N(N-1)\theta^{N-2}\left(\Psi(u)-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}H(\theta u)\,dx\right)-N\theta^{N-2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}H^{\prime}(\theta u)(\theta u)\,dx.

Hence

L_{1}:=\sup\left\{\left|\frac{d^{2}}{d\theta^{2}}I(u_{\theta})\right|\ :\ \frac{1}{2}\leq\theta\leq 2,\ u\in\overline{B_{X}(u_{0},r_{0})}\cap D(\Psi)\right\}<\infty.

From
dI(u_{\theta})/d\theta|_{\theta=\theta(u)}=0
,
\theta(u_{0})=1

and the continuity of
\theta(\cdot)
, it follows that for
u\in\overline{B_{X_{0}}(u_{0},r_{0})}\cap D(\Psi)
,

(4.4)

\frac{1}{2}\leq\theta(u)\leq 2,\quad\left|I(u_{\theta(u)})-I(u)\right|\leq\left|\int_{\theta(u)}^{1}\frac{d}{d\theta}I(u_{\theta})\,d\theta\right|\leq\frac{L_{1}}{2}|1-\theta(u)|^{2}.

To prove that
u_{0}
is a critical point of
I
, let
v\in D(\Psi)
be any point: otherwise, (
1.5
) holds trivially.
For
t\in(0,1)
, we introduce

v_{t}:=(1-t)u_{0}+tv\in D(\Psi).

The convexity of
\Psi
implies that for any
t\in(0,1]
,

(4.5)

\displaystyle\Psi(v_{t})-\Psi(u_{0})-\Phi^{\prime}(u_{0})(v_{t}-u_{0})\leq t\left\{\Psi(v)-\Psi(u_{0})-\Phi^{\prime}(u_{0})(v-u_{0})\right\}.

Let
(t_{n})\subset(0,1)
satisfy
t_{n}\to 0
. We first consider the case
\Psi(u_{0})\leq\Psi(v_{t_{n}})
for all
n
.
Then it is clear that
v_{t_{n}}\to u_{0}
in
X_{0}
and from the convexity of
\Psi
,

(4.6)

\displaystyle 0\leq\Psi(v_{t_{n}})-\Psi(u_{0})\leq(1-t_{n})\Psi(u_{0})+t_{n}\Psi(v)-\Psi(u_{0})=t_{n}\left(\Psi(v)-\Psi(u_{0})\right).

To simplify notations we put

\theta_{n}:=\theta(v_{t_{n}}),\quad w_{n}:=(v_{t_{n}})_{\theta_{n}}=\theta_{n}v_{t_{n}}\left(\frac{\cdot}{\theta_{n}}\right)\in{\mathcal{M}}_{0}.

From
\|v_{t_{n}}-u_{0}\|_{X_{0}}=O(t_{n})
and (
4.6
)
with
\Theta\in{\mathcal{C}}^{1}((0,\infty)\times X_{0}\setminus\{0\})
and
\Theta(\Psi(u),u)=\theta(u)
for

, we get

(4.7)

|\theta_{n}-1|=|\Theta(\Psi(v_{t_{n}}),v_{t_{n}})-\Theta(\Psi(u_{0}),u_{0})|=O(t_{n}).

Using the fact that
\Phi\in{\mathcal{C}}^{1}(X;\mathbb{R})
and (
4.5
), we infer that

(4.8)

\displaystyle I(v_{t_{n}})-I(u_{0})

\displaystyle=\Psi(v_{t_{n}})-\Psi(u_{0})-(\Phi(v_{t_{n}})-\Phi(u_{0}))

\displaystyle=\Psi(v_{t_{n}})-\Psi(u_{0})-\Phi^{\prime}(u_{0})(v_{t_{n}}-u_{0})+o(t_{n})

\displaystyle\leq t_{n}\left\{\Psi(v)-\Psi(u_{0})-\Phi^{\prime}(u_{0})(v-u_{0})\right\}+o(t_{n}).

By (
4.4
) and (
4.7
) with
v_{t_{n}}\in\overline{B_{X_{0}}(u_{0},r_{0})}\cap D(\Psi)
,

\displaystyle I(w_{n})-I(v_{t_{n}})\leq\frac{L_{1}}{2}|\theta_{n}-1|^{2}=O(t_{n}^{2})

and recalling that
u_{0}
minimizes
I
on
{\mathcal{M}}_{0}
, we see from (
4.8
) that

0\leq I(w_{n})-I(u_{0})\leq I(v_{t_{n}})-I(u_{0})+O(t_{n}^{2})\leq t_{n}\left\{\Psi(v)-\Psi(u_{0})-\Phi^{\prime}(u_{0})(v-u_{0})\right\}+o(t_{n}).

Dividing the above inequality by
t_{n}
and sending
t_{n}\to 0
imply (
1.5
) under
\Psi(u_{0})\leq\Psi(v_{t_{n}})
for each
n
.

If there exists a subsequence
(n_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
\Psi(u_{0})\leq\Psi(v_{t_{n_{k}}})
for all
k
,
then the previous argument is still valid.
Therefore, we may assume
\Psi(u_{0})>\Psi(v_{t_{n}})
for all
n
. We will use the same notation as in the previous case. If

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{|\theta_{n}-1|}{t_{n}}<\infty,

then (
4.7
) holds in this case and the previous argument can be exploited to obtain

\Psi(v)-\Psi(u_{0})-\Phi^{\prime}(u_{0})(v-u)\geq 0.

Consequently, we may assume that

(4.9)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{|\theta_{n}-1|}{t_{n}}=\infty.

In what follows, we shall show that (
4.9
) does not occur,
which implies that the desired inequality is satisfied by any
v\in D(\Psi)
and this completes the proof.
For this purpose, we next show
\theta_{n}\in(0,1)
for all sufficiently large
n
. Indeed, suppose that there exists
(\theta_{n_{k}})
such that

n_{k}\to\infty
and
\theta_{n_{k}}\geq 1
. By
\|v_{t_{n_{k}}}-u_{0}\|_{X_{0}}=ct_{n_{k}}
and

0=\Psi(v_{t_{n_{k}}})-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}H(\theta_{n_{k}}v_{t_{n_{k}}})\,dx,

it follows from the facts
u_{0}\in{\mathcal{M}}_{0}
and
\Psi(u_{0})>\Psi(v_{t_{n}})
in view of the assumption that for some

, which is independent of

,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}H(u_{0})\,dx=\Psi(u_{0})>\Psi(v_{t_{n_{k}}})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}H(\theta_{n_{k}}v_{t_{n_{k}}})\,dx\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}H(\theta_{n_{k}}u_{0})\,dx-C_{0}t_{n_{k}}.

Thus, we may find
\tau_{k}\in(0,1)
such that

(4.10)

C_{0}t_{n_{k}}\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}H(\theta_{n_{k}}u_{0})-H(u_{0})\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}H^{\prime}\left(\left(1+\tau_{k}(\theta_{n_{k}}-1)\right)u_{0}\right)u_{0}\,dx\left(\theta_{n_{k}}-1\right).

However, since
\theta_{n}=\theta(v_{t_{n}})\to 1
and (
F3
) lead to

\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}H^{\prime}\left(\left(1+\tau_{k}(\theta_{n_{k}}-1)\right)u_{0}\right)u_{0}\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}H^{\prime}(u_{0})u_{0}\,dx>0,

(
4.10
) contradicts (
4.9
). Therefore,
\theta_{n}\in(0,1)
holds for all sufficiently large
n
.

Since
\theta_{n}<1
and
I(u)=\frac{1}{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(u)u\,dx
for
u\in{\mathcal{M}}_{0}
, (
F5
) and the fact
w_{n}\in{\mathcal{M}}_{0}
give

\displaystyle I(u_{0})\leq I(w_{n})

\displaystyle=\frac{\theta_{n}^{N}}{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(\theta_{n}v_{t_{n}})\theta_{n}v_{t_{n}}\,dx

(4.11)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\theta_{n}^{N}}{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(v_{t_{n}})v_{t_{n}}\,dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(v_{t_{n}})v_{t_{n}}\,dx-(1-\theta_{n}^{N})\frac{1}{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(v_{t_{n}})v_{t_{n}}\,dx.

Observe that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1-\theta_{n}^{N}}{t_{n}}\geq\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1-\theta_{n}}{t_{n}}=\infty

and that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(v_{t_{n}})v_{t_{n}}\,dx\to\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(u_{0})u_{0}\,dx=NI(u_{0})=Nc_{0}>0.

Moreover by
\|v_{t_{n}}-u_{0}\|_{X_{0}}=t_{n}\|v-u_{0}\|_{X_{0}}=O(t_{n})
and
the fact that
X_{0}\ni u\mapsto\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(u)u\,dx\in\mathbb{R}
is of class
{\mathcal{C}}^{1}
, for some
C_{1}>0
,

\left|NI(u_{0})-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(v_{t_{n}})v_{t_{n}}\,dx\right|=\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(u_{0})u_{0}\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(v_{t_{n}})v_{t_{n}}\,dx\right|\leq C_{1}t_{n}.

However this contradicts (
4.11
). Indeed, from (
4.11
) we obtain

I(u_{0})-\frac{1}{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(v_{t_{n}})v_{t_{n}}\,dx\leq-(1-\theta_{n}^{N})\frac{1}{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(v_{t_{n}})v_{t_{n}}\,dx

or equivalently

(1-\theta_{n}^{N})\frac{1}{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(v_{t_{n}})v_{t_{n}}\,dx\leq\frac{1}{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(v_{t_{n}})v_{t_{n}}\,dx-I(u_{0})\leq\frac{C_{1}}{N}t_{n}.

This yields the following contradiction:

0<\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(u_{0})u_{0}\,dx=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(v_{t_{n}})v_{t_{n}}\,dx\leq C_{1}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{t_{n}}{1-\theta_{n}^{N}}=0.

Thus (
4.9
) does not occur and
u_{0}
is a critical point of
I
.
∎

4.4.
Existence of a minimizer and other properties of minimizers

To prove
1.2
, from
Sections

2
,
4.2
and
4.3
, it suffices to prove that

.
We will show the existence of a minimizer by a direct minimization technique on
{\mathcal{M}}_{0}
and the profile decomposition.

Proof of
1.2
.

Let
(u_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset{\mathcal{M}}_{0}
be a minimizing sequence, namely

. Note that for

we have

Thus,
(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(u_{n})u_{n}\,dx)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is bounded. From (
F6
),
(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}F(u_{n})\,dx)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is bounded and
by
I(u_{n})\to c_{0}
,
(\Psi(u_{n}))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is also bounded.
Hence
(u_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is bounded in
X_{0}
via (
2.4
). From
Section

3
with
p_{0}=2^{*}
,
there exist
K\in\{0,1,2,\dots\}\cup\{\infty\}
,
(y_{n}^{j})_{n}
and
\widetilde{u}_{j}
such that

(4.12)

\displaystyle u_{n}(\cdot+y_{n}^{j})\rightharpoonup\widetilde{u}_{j},\quad u_{n}(x+y_{n}^{j})\to\widetilde{u}_{j}(x)\quad\text{a.e. $\mathbb{R}^{N}$},

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\Psi(u_{n})\geq\sum_{j=0}^{K}\Psi(\widetilde{u}_{j}),

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}F(u_{n})\,dx=\sum_{j=0}^{K}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}F(\widetilde{u}_{j})\,dx,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(u_{n})u_{n}\,dx=\sum_{j=0}^{K}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(\widetilde{u}_{j})\widetilde{u}_{j}\,dx.

If
K=0
and
\widetilde{u}_{0}=0
, then

and

or
K=0
and
\widetilde{u}_{0}\not\equiv 0
.

Observe that
{\mathcal{M}}_{0}\subset D(\Psi)\setminus\{0\}
due to
M_{0}(u)=\infty

,
hence
u_{n}\in D(\Psi)\setminus\{0\}
.
Since

is convex and closed, it is also weakly closed thanks to Mazur’s theorem, which gives

.

To treat the cases
K\geq 1
and

with

simultaneously, set

.
Remark that

For

, let
\theta_{j}>0
be the unique number so that

.
Suppose that for all

\theta_{j}>1
. By (
4.2
) and (
4.12

\displaystyle 0<\sum_{j\in J}\Psi(\widetilde{u}_{j})\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}\Psi(u_{n})=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}H(u_{n})\,dx

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in J}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}H(\widetilde{u}_{j})\,dx

\displaystyle<\sum_{j\in J}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}H(\theta_{j}\widetilde{u}_{j})\,dx=\sum_{j\in J}\Psi(\widetilde{u}_{j}),

which is a contradiction. Hence there exists

such that
\theta_{j_{0}}\leq 1
. Then, Fatou’s lemma and (
F5
) yield

(4.14)

\displaystyle c_{0}=\lim_{n\to\infty}I(u_{n})

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(u_{n})u_{n}\,dx=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(u_{n}(\cdot+y_{n}^{j_{0}}))u_{n}(\cdot+y_{n}^{j_{0}})\,dx

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(\widetilde{u}_{j_{0}})\widetilde{u}_{j_{0}}\,dx\geq\frac{1}{N}\theta_{j_{0}}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(\theta_{j_{0}}\widetilde{u}_{j_{0}})\theta_{j_{0}}\widetilde{u}_{j_{0}}\,dx=I(m(\widetilde{u}_{j_{0}}))\geq c_{0}.

Thus
\theta_{j_{0}}=1
,
\widetilde{u}_{j_{0}}\in{\mathcal{M}}_{0}
,
\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(u_{n})u_{n}\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(\widetilde{u}_{j_{0}})\widetilde{u}_{j_{0}}\,dx

and
I(\widetilde{u}_{j_{0}})=c_{0}
. Moreover, since
f(s)s>0
for
s\neq 0
in view of (
F3
) and (
F5
),
by (
4.12
),
\widetilde{u}_{j}=0
for each

,
J=\{j_{0}\}
and
\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}F(u_{n})\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}F(\widetilde{u}_{j_{0}})\,dx
.
Furthermore, by

,
we have
\Psi(u_{n})\to\Psi(\widetilde{u}_{j_{0}})
, and
Section

2

\|u_{n}(\cdot+y_{n}^{j_{0}})-\widetilde{u}_{j_{0}}\|_{X_{0}}\to 0
.
Thanks to

4.3

is a critical point of

and

2

\widetilde{u}_{j_{0}}
is a classical solution of (
1.1
).

We next prove that all minimizers have a constant sign.

u\in{\mathcal{M}}_{0}
be any minimizer corresponding to
c_{0}
.
We argue by contradiction and suppose
u_{\pm}\not\equiv 0

From

2

4.1

\theta_{+}

4.5.
Sobolev-type inequality

Proof of
1.4
.

Consider the case
f(u)=|u|^{p-2}u
with
p>2^{*}
.
Fix any
u\in D(\Psi)\setminus\{0\}
.
Then, from above considerations, it is clear that
I(m(u))\geq c_{0}
.
Recalling that
m(u)=u_{\theta(u)}\in{\mathcal{M}}_{0}
, where
\theta=\theta(u)=\Theta(\Psi(u),u)
, we get

(4.15)

\displaystyle c_{0}\leq I(u_{\theta})=\frac{\theta^{N}}{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f\left(\theta u\right)\theta u\,dx=\frac{\theta^{N+p}}{N}\|u\|_{p}^{p}.

Notice that the equality happens if and only if
m(u)=u_{\theta}
is the minimizer on
{\mathcal{M}}_{0}
.
Since
F(u)=\frac{1}{p}|u|^{p}
,
H(u)=\left(\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{N}\right)|u|^{p}=\frac{N+p}{Np}|u|^{p}
and
M_{0}(m(u))=0
, it follows that

\Psi(u)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{N+p}{Np}|\theta u|^{p}\,dx=\frac{N+p}{Np}\theta^{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{p}\,dx

and that

\theta=\left(\frac{Np}{N+p}\Psi(u)\right)^{1/p}\frac{1}{\|u\|_{p}}.

Thus, from (
4.15
) we obtain

\displaystyle c_{0}\leq\frac{\theta^{N+p}}{N}\|u\|_{p}^{p}=\frac{1}{N}\left(\frac{Np}{N+p}\Psi(u)\right)^{(N+p)/p}\frac{1}{\|u\|^{N}_{p}}

and

\left(\frac{N+p}{Np}\right)^{N+p}N^{p}c_{0}^{p}\|u\|_{p}^{Np}\leq\Psi(u)^{N+p}.

Furthermore, the equality holds if and only if
m(u)=u_{\theta}
is the minimizer of
\inf_{{\mathcal{M}}_{0}}I
,
and the proof is completed.
∎

5.1.
The projection into the Pohožaev set

We will show that there exists a continuous projection from
D(\Psi)\setminus\{0\}
onto
{\mathcal{M}}_{1}
.
To this end, we first prove

Lemma 5.1
.

For every
\alpha>0
and
u\in X_{1}\setminus\{0\}
there is a unique
\Theta=\Theta(\alpha,u)>0
such that

\alpha=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}-\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{N}\right)\Theta^{2}u^{2}+G(\Theta u)+\frac{1}{N}g(\Theta u)\Theta u\,dx.

Furthermore, the map
(0,\infty)\times X_{1}\setminus\{0\}\ni(\alpha,u)\mapsto\Theta(\alpha,u)\in(0,\infty)
is of class
{\mathcal{C}}^{1}
.

Proof.

Set

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}(u,\theta)

\displaystyle:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}-\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{N}\right)\theta^{2}u^{2}+G(\theta u)+\frac{1}{N}g(\theta u)\theta u\,dx,

\displaystyle J(\alpha,u,\theta)

\displaystyle:=\alpha-{\mathcal{H}}(u,\theta)\in{\mathcal{C}}^{1}\left((0,\infty)\times X_{1}\setminus\{0\}\times(0,\infty);\mathbb{R}\right).

From
X_{1}\subset L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, (
G1
) and (
G3
), it follows that for each fixed
u\in X_{1}\setminus\{0\}
,

(5.1)

\lim_{\theta\to 0}\left\|\frac{G(\theta u)}{\theta^{2}u^{2}}+\frac{1}{N}\frac{g(\theta u)}{\theta u}\right\|_{\infty}=0,\quad\lim_{\theta\to\infty}{\mathcal{H}}(u,\theta)=\infty.

Furthermore, by (
G2
), for each
s\neq 0
,

(5.2)

G(s)=\int_{0}^{s}g(\tau)\,d\tau=\int_{0}^{s}\frac{g(\tau)}{\tau}\cdot\tau\,d\tau<\frac{g(s)}{s}\int_{0}^{s}\tau\,d\tau=\frac{1}{2}g(s)s.

For any
u\in X_{1}\setminus\{0\}
and
\theta\in(0,\infty)
with
{\mathcal{H}}(u,\theta)\geq 0
, notice that (
5.2
) and (
G2
) yield

(5.3)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}{\mathcal{H}}(u,\theta)

or

and the strict sign property follows from the same reasoning as in the zero mass case.

The existence of a radial minimizer follows as in the proof of

.
Indeed, let

be any minimizer and write
v:=u^{*}
when

is positive,
and
v:=-u^{*}
when
u
is negative. Then we may verify

,
by

5.1
, there exists

such that

.
Then we may infer from
Section

5.2
and

for any

that

0<c_{1}\leq I(v_{\theta})=\frac{\theta^{N}}{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(\theta v)\theta v\,dx\leq\frac{1}{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(v)v\,dx=\frac{1}{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(u)u\,dx=I(u)=c_{1}.

∎

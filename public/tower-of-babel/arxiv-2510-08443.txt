Surface finite element approximation of parabolic SPDEs with Whittle–Matérn noise
Øyvind S. Auestad, Geir-Arne Fuglstad, Annika Lang

Abstract
. We propose and analyse a new type of fully discrete surface finite element approximation of a class of linear parabolic stochastic evolution equations with additive noise. Our discretization uses a surface finite element approximation of the noise, and is tailored for equations with noise having covariance operator defined by (negative powers of) elliptic operators, like Whittle–Matérn random fields. We derive strong and pathwise convergence rates of our approximation, and verify these by numerical experiments.

1.
Introduction

We study linear parabolic stochastic evolution equations of the form

(1.1)

\displaystyle du(t)=-A_{1}u(t)\,dt+A_{2}^{-\gamma}\,dW(t),\quad u(0)=\xi,\quad t\in[0,T],

where

a parameter,

\displaystyle A_{j}=\alpha_{j}-\nabla_{\Gamma}\cdot\mathcal{A}_{j}\nabla_{\Gamma}+b_{j}\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma},\quad j=1,2,

) has additive noise with Whittle–Matérn type covariance, and is popular in spatial statistics. In this paper we propose and analyse a fully discrete surface finite element approximation of (

Numerical approximations of solutions to stochastic partial differential equations (SPDEs) have been studied mainly for equations posed on Euclidean domains over the last three decades. Important problems in the climate and environmental sciences and biology warrant the development and analysis of numerical approximations of SPDEs defined on domains like Earth or the surface of a cell. The literature on surface finite element approximations of parabolic SPDEs is scarce. On the other hand, spectral approximations of SPDEs on the sphere have been studied exhaustively (see, e.g.,
[

\displaystyle\leq(\|A_{2}^{-\gamma+d/4-1/2+2\epsilon}\|_{L(H)}

\displaystyle\qquad+\|A_{2}^{d/4-1/2+2\epsilon}(A_{2}^{-\gamma}-A_{2,h}^{-\gamma}\pi_{h})\|_{L(H)})Ch^{2}.

The first term above is bounded since
\gamma>d/4-1/2+2\epsilon
. The second term is bounded as shown above. Combining the estimates above finishes the estimate of
(ii)
and the proof.
∎

We are now ready to prove Theorem
3.1
.

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Our approximate mild solution (
3.3
) can be extended from discrete times to all times
t\geq 0
as

(4.2)

\displaystyle u_{h,\Delta t}(t):=S_{h,\Delta t}(t)\pi_{h}\xi+\int_{0}^{t}S_{h,\Delta t}(t-s)Q_{k}^{-\gamma}(A_{2,h})\pi_{h}\tau_{h}\,dW.

We decompose the error as follows

\displaystyle u(t)-u_{h,\Delta t}(t)

\displaystyle=(S_{1}(t)-S_{h,\Delta t}(t)\pi_{h})\xi

\displaystyle\qquad+\bigg(\int_{0}^{t}S_{1}(t-s)A_{2}^{-\gamma}\,dW-\int_{0}^{t}S_{h,\Delta t}(t-s)A_{2,h}^{-\gamma}\pi_{h}\tau_{h}\,dW\bigg)

\displaystyle\qquad+\bigg(\int_{0}^{t}S_{h,\Delta t}(t-s)A_{2,h}^{-\gamma}\pi_{h}\tau_{h}\,dW-\int_{0}^{t}S_{h,\Delta t}(t-s)Q_{k}^{-\gamma}(A_{2,h})\pi_{h}\tau_{h}\,dW\bigg)

\displaystyle=:(i)+(ii)+(iii).

We have,

\displaystyle\|(i)\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;H)}\leq Ce^{c\lambda t}t^{-\theta/2+\rho/2}(h^{\theta}+\Delta t^{\theta/2})\|(\lambda+A_{1})^{\rho/2}\xi\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;H)},

for any
\theta\in[0,2]
and
\rho\in[-1,\theta]\cap[-2+\theta,\theta]
, by Lemma
4.1
. For
(ii)
, we use Lemma
4.4
.

Finally for
(iii)
, we have using the Burkholder–Davis–Gundy inequality (Theorem 4.36 in
[
10
]
), Lemma
4.2
,
2.8
and
A.1

\displaystyle\|\int_{0}^{t}S_{h,\Delta t}(t-s)(A_{2,h}^{-\gamma}-Q_{k}^{-\gamma}(A_{2,h}))\pi_{h}\tau_{h}\,dW\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;H)}^{p}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq C_{p}\bigg(\int_{0}^{t}\|S_{h,\Delta t}(t-s)(A_{2,h}^{-\gamma}-Q_{k}^{-\gamma}(A_{2,h}))\pi_{h}\tau_{h}\|_{L_{2}(H)}^{2}\,ds\bigg)^{p/2}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq C_{p}\bigg(\int_{0}^{t}e^{2c(\lambda-\delta)s}\|(A_{2,h}^{-\gamma}-Q_{k}^{-\gamma}(A_{2,h}))\pi_{h}\tau_{h}\|_{L_{2}(H)}^{2}\,ds\bigg)^{p/2}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq C_{p}e^{pc\lambda t}\bigg(\int_{0}^{t}e^{-2c\delta s}\|(A_{2,h}^{-\gamma}-Q_{k}^{-\gamma}(A_{2,h}))\|_{L(H)}^{2}\|\pi_{h}\tau_{h}\|_{L_{2}(H)}^{2}\,ds\bigg)^{p/2}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq C_{p}e^{pc\lambda t}(e^{-\frac{\pi^{2}}{2k}}N_{h}^{1/2})^{p},

Using the bound on

in terms of

which follows from

, this term is bounded by

In total, we get,

\displaystyle\|u(t)-u_{h,\Delta t}(t)\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;H)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{p,\theta}e^{c\lambda t}(h^{\theta}+\Delta t^{\theta/2})(1+t^{-\theta/2+\rho/2}\|(\lambda+A_{1})^{\rho/2}\xi\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;H)}),

for any
\theta\in[0,2\gamma+1-d/2)\cap[0,2]
, and
\rho\in[-1,\theta]\cap[-2+\theta,\theta]
.
∎

In order to rewrite (
3.3
) as a system of equations for the coefficients of
\iota_{h}^{-1}u_{h,\Delta t}
in the nodal basis, the following two lemmas are key.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
B_{h}\in L(H,\iota_{h}V_{h})
. Then,

\displaystyle\|B_{h}\|_{L_{2}(H)}\leq\|B_{h}\|_{L(H)}N_{h}^{1/2}.

Proof.

Let
\{e_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
be an
H
-orthonormal basis of
H
, and let
\{e_{j,h}\}_{j=1}^{N_{h}}
be an
H
-orthonormal basis of
\iota_{h}V_{h}
. We have,

\displaystyle\|B_{h}\|_{L_{2}(H)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}(B_{h}e_{j},B_{h}e_{j})_{H}=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\sum_{k=1}^{N_{h}}(B_{h}e_{j},e_{k,h})_{H}(B_{h}e_{j},e_{k,h})_{H}=\sum_{k=1}^{N_{h}}\|B_{h}^{*}e_{k,h}\|_{H}^{2},

and using that
\|B_{h}^{*}\|_{L(H)}=\|B_{h}\|_{L(H)}
gives the estimate.
∎

Lemma A.2
.

Let
W
be a cylindrical Wiener process on
H
. Then
\iota_{h}^{-1}\pi_{h}\tau_{h}W
is a cylindrical Wiener process on
V_{h}
, in the sense that,

\displaystyle\iota_{h}^{-1}\pi_{h}\tau_{h}W(t)=\sum_{j=1}^{N_{h}}\beta_{j}(t)e_{j,h},\quad P\text{-a.s.}

where
\beta_{j}
are independent scalar Brownian motions, and
\{e_{j,h}\}_{j=1}^{N_{h}}
is an
L^{2}(\Gamma_{h})
-orthonormal basis of
V_{h}
.

Proof.

We have that
\iota_{h}^{-1}\pi_{h}\tau_{h}\in L_{2}(H,V_{h})
(where
V_{h}
has the
L^{2}(\Gamma_{h})
-norm) since it has finite rank. It follows that
\iota_{h}^{-1}\pi_{h}\tau_{h}W(t)\in V_{h}

P
-a.s. (see, e.g., Section 4.2.1 in
[
10
]
). Therefore, for any
L^{2}(\Gamma_{h})
-orthonormal basis
\{e_{j,h}\}_{j=1}^{N_{h}}
of
V_{h}
we must have

\displaystyle\iota_{h}^{-1}\pi_{h}\tau_{h}W(t)=\sum_{j=1}^{N_{h}}(\iota_{h}^{-1}\pi_{h}\tau_{h}W(t),e_{j,h})_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{h})}e_{j,h},\quad P\text{-a.s.}

To check that the law of
(\iota_{h}^{-1}\pi_{h}\tau_{h}W(t),e_{j,h})_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{h})}
are those of independent scalar Brownian motions for each
j
, we note that

\displaystyle(\iota_{h}^{-1}\pi_{h}\tau_{h}W(t),e_{j,h})_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{h})}=(\iota_{h}^{-1}\tau_{h}W(t),e_{j,h})_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{h})}=(W(t),(\iota_{h}\delta_{h}^{-1/2})\iota_{h}e_{j,h})_{H}.

Now the result follows since
\{(\iota_{h}\delta_{h}^{-1/2})\iota_{h}e_{j,h}\}_{j=1}^{N_{h}}
defines an
H
-orthonormal basis of
\iota_{h}V_{h}
.
∎

To rewrite (
3.3
) as an equation involving the basis coefficients of
\iota_{h}^{-1}u_{h,\Delta t}

(A.1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&(\iota_{h}^{-1}(I+\Delta tA_{1,h})u_{h,\Delta t}(t_{n+1}),\varphi_{j})_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{h})}\\

the nodal basis of

. The following lemma is useful for setting up the system above.

Lemma A.3

We have

\displaystyle\iota_{h}^{-1}Q_{k}^{-\gamma}(A_{2,h})\pi_{h}\tau_{h}(W(t_{n+1})-W(t_{n}))=\sum_{j=1}^{N_{h}}\theta_{j}\varphi_{j},\quad P\text{-a.s.}

\displaystyle\theta=\begin{cases}\Delta t^{1/2}\frac{k\sin(\pi\gamma)}{\pi}\sum_{l=-M}^{N}e^{(1-\gamma)y_{l}}(e^{y_{l}}M_{h}+K_{h})^{-1}M_{h}^{1/2}\varrho_{h}^{n},\quad&\gamma\in(0,1),\\

Set for ease of notation,

For the expression for

\displaystyle\qquad=\begin{cases}M_{h}\Delta t^{1/2}\frac{k\sin(\pi\gamma)}{\pi}\sum_{j=-M}^{N}e^{(1-\gamma)y_{j}}(e^{y_{j}}M_{h}+K_{h})^{-1}M_{h}^{1/2}\varrho_{h}^{n},\quad&\gamma\in(0,1),\\

Local well-posedness of strong solutions to the non-isentropic compressible primitive equations with vertical diffusion
Rupert Klein, Jinkai Li, Xin Liu, Edriss S. Titi

Abstract
. Due to the absence of dynamical equation in the vertical momentum component of the primitive equations (PEs) of atmospheric dynamics, the vertical component of the velocity can be recovered only from the information on the other physical quantities, while utilizing the hydrostatic balance. This causes one spatial derivative loss while leads to a stronger nonlinearity, comparing to the classic compressible Navier-Stokes equations. As a result, the mathematical analysis on the compressible primitive equations is mathematically more challenging than that on the compressible Navier-Stokes equations. In this paper, we consider the initial-boundary value problem to the non-isentropic compressible primitive equations with only vertical diffusion for the temperature, but without gravity. Local existence and uniqueness as well as the continuous dependence on the initial data of strong solutions are established for any suitably regular initial data. The initial velocity and pressure are assumed here to be of one order derivative higher regularity than that of the initial density.

The compressible primitive equations, formally obtained as the hydrostatic approximation of the compressible Navier-Stokes equations by replacing the vertical momentum balance equation with the hydrostatic balance
equation, are the fundamental equations for modern meteorological study (see, e.g.,
[

the vertical scale of the atmosphere is significantly smaller than the planetary horizontal scale; (ii) the balance of gravity and pressure dominates the dynamic in the vertical direction; (iii) the vertical velocity is
usually hard to observe in reality. Rigorous justifications of the hydrostatic approximation of hydrodynamic equations can be found in
[

2

In this work, our goal is to investigate the fundamental problem of the well-posedness of solutions to the compressible primitive equations with non-trivial entropy. In particular, we consider the non-isentropic compressible primitive equations with only vertical diffusion for the temperature:

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\rho+\mathrm{div}_{h}\,(\rho v)+\partial_{z}(\rho w)=0,\\

\partial_{z}p=0,\\

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathbb{S}_{h}=\mu(\nabla_{h}v+\nabla_{h}^{\top}v)+\lambda\mathrm{div}_{h}\,v\mathbb{I}_{2},\quad p=R\rho\theta,

5.2.
Estimates on the linear system

Let
\displaystyle\mathbb{T}^{3}
be the three dimensional torus. For positive time
\displaystyle T
denote
\displaystyle Q_{T}:=\mathbb{T}^{3}\times(0,T)
.
Throughout this subsection, the following facts will be used without further mentions

\|\nabla^{2}u\|_{2}^{2}=\|\Delta u\|_{2}^{2},\quad\|\nabla\Delta u\|_{2}^{2}=\|\nabla^{3}u\|_{2}^{2},\quad\|\nabla^{4}u\|_{2}^{2}=\|\Delta^{2}u\|_{2}^{2},\quad\forall f\in H^{4}(\mathbb{T}^{3}),

which can be verified directly by integrating by parts, with the help of density arguments if necessary.

We start with a spatially periodic initial boundary value problem which, under some symmetry assumptions, is equivalent to the
linear system that we required.

Proposition 5.2
.

Given
\displaystyle u_{0}\in H^{3}(\mathbb{T}^{3})

\displaystyle f\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}))

\displaystyle a,b\in L^{\infty}(Q_{T})\cap L^{4}(0,T;H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})

\displaystyle a\geq\underline{a}
and
\displaystyle b\geq 0

\displaystyle\underline{a}
. Assume
that

\displaystyle\displaystyle u\text{ is periodic in }x,y,z,\quad u|_{t=0}=u_{0}.

Then, there is a positive constant
\displaystyle C
depending only on
\displaystyle\underline{a}
, such that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|u\|_{2}^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}\|u\|_{H^{1}}^{2}dt\leq C\left(\|u_{0}\|_{2}^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}\|f\|_{2}^{2}dt\right)e^{C\left(T+\int_{0}^{T}\|(\Delta a,\Delta b)\|_{2}^{4}dt\right)},

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}\|\Delta u\|_{2}^{2}dt\leq C\left(\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{2}^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}\|f\|_{2}^{2}dt\right)e^{C\int_{0}^{T}\|\Delta b\|_{2}^{4}dt},

and

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|D^{k}u\|_{2}^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}\|D^{k+1}u\|_{2}^{2}dt\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle\times e^{C\int_{0}^{T}\|(\Delta a,\Delta b)\|_{2}^{4}dt},\quad k=2,3.

Moreover, the following estimates hold

) with

from (

).
The proof is complete.
∎

Corollary 5.3
.

Assume that all the conditions of Proposition

hold. Then, the following estimate holds

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|u\|_{\mathscr{Z}_{T}}^{2}\leq C\mathcal{K}_{T}(a,b)\left(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{3}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}^{2}+\|f\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}))}^{2}\right),

(5.18)

Proposition 5.4
.

Given
\displaystyle u_{0i}\in H^{3}(\mathbb{T}^{3})
,
\displaystyle f_{i}\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}))
, and

\displaystyle b_{i}\geq 0
for some positive number
\displaystyle\underline{a}
, for
\displaystyle i=1,2
. Assume

\displaystyle u_{i}\in\mathscr{Z}_{T}
is a solution to

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{t}u_{i}-a_{i}\Delta u_{i}-b_{i}\nabla_{h}\text{div}_{h}u_{i}=f_{i},\\
u_{i}\text{ is periodic in }x,y,z,\\
u_{i}|_{t=0}=u_{0i},\end{array}\right.

for
\displaystyle i=1,2
. Denote

Then, there is a positive constant
\displaystyle C
depending only on
\displaystyle\underline{a}
, such that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|u\|_{\mathcal{Z}_{T}}^{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle C\mathcal{K}_{T}(a_{1},b_{1})\mathcal{K}_{T}(a_{2},b_{2})\left(\|u_{02}\|_{H^{3}}^{2}+\|f_{2}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{2})}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\times\left(\|(\Delta a,\Delta b)\|_{L^{4}(0,T;L^{2})}^{2}+\|(a,b)\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{T})}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\displaystyle+C\mathcal{K}_{T}(a_{1},b_{1})\left(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{3}}^{2}+\|f\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{2})}^{2}\right),

where

Proof.

5.3
, it holds that

\|u_{i}\|_{\mathcal{Z}_{T}}^{2}\leq C\left(\|u_{0i}\|_{H^{3}}^{2}+\|f_{i}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{2})}^{2}\right)\mathcal{K}_{T}{(a_{i},b_{i})},

(5.19)

for
\displaystyle i=1,2
.

\displaystyle\displaystyle u|_{t=0}=u_{0},

Corollary 5.5
.

Given
\displaystyle v_{0i}\in H^{3}(\mathcal{O})
,
\displaystyle f_{i}\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{2}(\mathcal{O}))
, and
\displaystyle\sigma_{i}\in L^{\infty}(\mathcal{O}\times(0,T))\cap L^{4}(0,T;H^{2}(\mathcal{O}))
, satisfying
\displaystyle\sigma\geq 0.5\underline{\sigma}
for some positive number
\displaystyle\underline{\sigma}
, and

\partial_{z}v_{0i}|_{z=0,1}=\partial_{z}f_{i}|_{z=0,1}=0,\quad\partial_{z}\sigma_{i}|_{z=0,1}=0,\quad i=1,2.

(5.21)

For
\displaystyle i=1,2
, assume that
\displaystyle V_{i}\in\mathscr{Y}_{T}
satisfies

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{t}V_{i}-\mu\sigma_{i}\Delta V_{i}-(\mu+\lambda)\sigma_{i}\nabla_{h}\text{div}_{h}V_{i}=f_{i},\\
V_{i}\text{ is periodic in }x,y,\quad\partial_{z}V_{i}|_{z=0,1}=0,\\

Denote

Then, there is a positive constant
\displaystyle C
depending only on
\displaystyle\mu,\lambda,
and
\displaystyle\underline{\sigma}

and

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|V\|_{\mathscr{Y}_{T}}^{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle C\mathcal{L}_{T}(\sigma_{1})\mathcal{L}_{T}(\sigma_{2})\left(\|v_{02}\|_{H^{3}}^{2}+\|f_{2}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{2})}^{2}\right)

where

Proof.

For
\displaystyle i=1,2
, extend
\displaystyle v_{0i},\sigma_{i},
and
\displaystyle f_{i}
periodically and evenly with respect to
\displaystyle z
, and denote, respectively, by
\displaystyle v_{0i}^{\text{ext}},\sigma_{i}^{\text{ext}},
and
\displaystyle f_{i}^{\text{ext}}
the corresponding functions after extension. Then, by (
5.21
), one can verify that
\displaystyle v_{0i}^{\text{ext}}\in H^{3}(\mathbb{T}^{3}),\sigma_{i}^{\text{ext}}\in L^{4}(0,T;H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}))\cap L^{\infty}(Q_{T}),
and
\displaystyle f_{i}^{\text{ext}}\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}))
. Denote by

\displaystyle V_{i}^{\text{ext}}
as the unique solution to

\displaystyle\displaystyle\partial_{t}V_{i}^{\text{ext}}-\mu\sigma_{i}^{\text{ext}}\Delta V_{i}^{\text{ext}}-(\mu+\lambda)\sigma_{i}^{\text{ext}}\nabla_{h}\text{div}_{h}V_{i}^{\text{ext}}=f_{i}^{\text{ext}},

5.3

5.4

5.3.
Solvability of the nonlinear system

Based on the results obtained in the previous two subsections, we are now at the position to prove the local existence to the original nonlinear system. We employ the contracting mapping principle.
Let
\displaystyle M_{0}
and
\displaystyle T_{0}
be two positive constants to be determined later and denote

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathscr{B}_{0}:=\Big\{

\displaystyle\displaystyle(v,\sigma,p)\Big|\|(v,\sigma,p)\|_{\mathscr{Y}_{T_{0}}\times\mathscr{Y}_{T_{0}}\times\mathscr{X}_{T_{0}}}\leq M_{0},(v,\sigma,p)|_{t=0}=(v_{0},\sigma_{0},p_{0})\Big\}.

Recalling the definition of the mapping
\displaystyle\mathfrak{F}
, it suffices to show the existence of fixed point in
\displaystyle\mathscr{B}_{0}
to
\displaystyle\mathfrak{F}
, which is guaranteed by the following proposition.

Proposition 5.7
.

Let
\displaystyle\mathfrak{F}
be the mapping defined by (
5.1
). Then, there are two positive constants
\displaystyle M_{0}\geq 1
and
\displaystyle T_{0}\leq 1
depending only on
\displaystyle\|(v_{0},\sigma_{0})\|_{H^{3}(\mathcal{O})}+\|p_{0}\|_{H^{3}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}
,
\displaystyle\underline{\sigma},\underline{p}
, and the parameters
\displaystyle\mu,\lambda,\nu,\gamma,\epsilon
, such that
\displaystyle\mathfrak{F}
is a contracting mapping from
\displaystyle\mathscr{B}_{0}
to itself and, thus, there is a unique fixed point
\displaystyle(v_{\text{fix}},v_{\text{fix}},p_{\text{fix}})
to
\displaystyle\mathfrak{F}
in
\displaystyle\mathscr{B}_{0}
, which by the definition of
\displaystyle\mathfrak{F}
, is a solution to system (
2.7
)–(
2.10
),
subject to (
1.18
)–(
1.20
).

Proof.

Denote
\displaystyle X_{0}=\|(u_{0},\sigma_{0})\|_{H^{3}(\mathcal{O})}+\|p_{0}\|_{H^{3}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}
and let
\displaystyle M_{0}
be a constant to be determined later.
Throughout the proof of this proposition, we use
\displaystyle C_{M_{0}}
and
\displaystyle C_{X_{0}}
to denote general constants depending on
\displaystyle M_{0}
and
\displaystyle X_{0}
, respectively, which may depend also on
\displaystyle\mu,\lambda,\nu,\gamma,\epsilon,\underline{\sigma}
, and
\displaystyle\underline{p}
, while we use
\displaystyle C
to denote a general constant depending only on
\displaystyle\mu,\lambda,\nu,\gamma,\epsilon,\underline{\sigma}
, and
\displaystyle\underline{p}
.

By the Sobolev and Hölder inequalities, it holds for any
\displaystyle t\in(0,T_{0})
that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\|\sigma-\sigma_{0}\right\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\|\int_{0}^{t}\partial_{t}\sigma ds\right\|_{\infty}\leq\int_{0}^{T_{0}}\|\partial_{t}\sigma\|_{\infty}dt\leq C\int_{0}^{T_{0}}\|\partial_{t}\sigma\|_{H^{2}}dt

(5.23)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle CT_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{0}^{T_{0}}\|\partial_{t}\sigma\|_{H^{2}}^{2}dt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq CM_{0}T_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq 0.5\underline{\sigma},

and similarly

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\|p-p_{0}\right\|_{\infty}\leq 0.5\underline{p},

for any
\displaystyle(v,\sigma,p)\in\mathscr{B}_{0}
, by choosing
\displaystyle T_{0}
sufficiently small depending only on
\displaystyle M_{0}
,
\displaystyle\underline{\sigma}
, and
\displaystyle\underline{p}
. Thanks to this,
recalling that
\displaystyle\sigma_{0}\geq\underline{\sigma}
and
\displaystyle p\geq\underline{p}
, and by the Sobolev embedding inequality, it follows that

0.5\underline{\sigma}\leq\sigma\leq 0.5\underline{\sigma}+\|\sigma_{0}\|_{\infty}\leq C_{X_{0}},\quad p\geq 0.5\underline{p},\quad\forall(v,\sigma,p)\in\mathscr{B}_{0}.

(5.24)

Then, recalling the expression of
\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{T_{0}}(\sigma)
in (
5.22
), one deduces

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{T_{0}}(\sigma)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle Ce^{C\left(T_{0}+\int_{0}^{T_{0}}\|\Delta\sigma\|_{2}^{4}dt\right)}\|\sigma\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{O}\times(0,T_{0}))}

(5.25)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle Ce^{C\left(1+M_{0}^{4}T_{0}\right)}C_{X_{0}}\leq C_{X_{0}},\quad\forall(v,\sigma,p)\in\mathscr{B}_{0},

by choosing
\displaystyle T_{0}
sufficiently small such that
\displaystyle M_{0}^{4}T_{0}\leq 1
.

For any
\displaystyle(v_{i},\sigma_{i},p_{i})\in\mathscr{B}_{0}
,
\displaystyle i=1,2
, noticing that
\displaystyle(\sigma_{1}-\sigma_{2})|_{t=0}=0
, it follows in the same way as (
5.23
) that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|\sigma_{1}-\sigma_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{O}\times(0,T_{0}))}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle CT_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{0}^{T_{0}}\|\partial_{t}(\sigma_{1}-\sigma_{2})\|_{H^{2}}^{2}dt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

(5.26)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle CT_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\sigma_{1}-\sigma_{2}\|_{\mathscr{Y}_{T_{0}}}.

By Proposition
5.1
and the Hölder inequality, one concludes that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{2}\|N_{j}(v_{1},\sigma_{1},p_{1})-N_{j}(v_{2},\sigma_{2},p_{2})\|_{L^{2}(0,T_{0};H^{2}(\mathcal{O}))}

(5.27)

\displaystyle\displaystyle+\|N_{3}(v_{1},p_{1})-N_{3}(v_{2},p_{2})\|_{L^{2}(0,T_{0};H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2}))}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle T_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{2}\|N_{j}(v_{1},\sigma_{1},p_{1})-N_{j}(v_{2},\sigma_{2},p_{2})\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T_{0};H^{2}(\mathcal{O}))}

\displaystyle\displaystyle+T_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|N_{3}(v_{1},p_{1})-N_{3}(v_{2},p_{2})\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T_{0};H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2}))}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle C_{M_{0}}T_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\|(v_{1}-v_{2},\sigma_{1}-\sigma_{2})\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T_{0};H^{3}(\mathcal{O}))}+\|p_{1}-p_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T_{0};H^{3}(\mathbb{T}^{2}))}\right)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle C_{M_{0}}T_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|(v_{1}-v_{2},\sigma_{1}-\sigma_{2},p_{1}-p_{2})\|_{\mathscr{Y}_{T_{0}}\times\mathscr{Y}_{T_{0}}\times\mathscr{X}_{T_{0}}},

and

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{2}\|N_{j}(v,\sigma,p)\|_{L^{2}(0,T_{0};H^{2}(\mathcal{O}))}+\|N_{3}(v,p)\|_{L^{2}(0,T_{0};H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2}))}

(5.28)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle T_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{2}\|N_{j}(v,\sigma,p)\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T_{0};H^{2})}+T_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|N_{3}(v,p)\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T_{0};H^{2})}\leq C_{M_{0}}T_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}},

for any
\displaystyle(v,\sigma,p),(v_{i},\sigma_{i},p_{i})\in\mathscr{B}_{0}
,
\displaystyle i=1,2
.

For
\displaystyle(v,\sigma,p)\in\mathscr{B}_{0}
, denote

\displaystyle(V,\Sigma,P)=\mathfrak{F}(v,\sigma,p)
which, by the definition of
\displaystyle\mathfrak{F}
, is a solution to
(
5.2
)–(
5.4
), subject to (
5.5
)–(
5.6

Recalling (
5.24
), (
5.25
), and (
5.28
),
it follows from Corollary
5.5
, Corollary
5.6
, and the standard parabolic estimates that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|\mathfrak{F}(v,\sigma,p)\|_{\mathscr{Y}_{T_{0}}\times\mathscr{Y}_{T_{0}}\times\mathscr{X}_{T_{0}}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|(V,\Sigma,P)\|_{\mathscr{Y}_{T_{0}}\times\mathscr{Y}_{T_{0}}\times\mathscr{X}_{T_{0}}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle C\mathcal{L}_{T_{0}}(\sigma)\left(\|v_{0}\|_{H^{3}(\mathcal{O})}+\|N_{1}(v,\sigma,p)\|_{L^{2}(0,T_{0};H^{2}(\mathcal{O}))}\right)

\displaystyle\displaystyle+C\mathcal{L}_{T_{0}}(\sigma)\left(\|\sigma_{0}\|_{H^{3}(\mathcal{O})}+\|N_{2}(v,\sigma,p)\|_{L^{2}(0,T_{0};H^{2}(\mathcal{O}))}\right)

\displaystyle\displaystyle+C(\|p_{0}\|_{H^{3}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}+\|N_{3}(v,p)\|_{L^{2}(0,T_{0};H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2}))})

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle C_{X_{0}}\left(X_{0}+C_{M_{0}}T_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)\leq C_{X_{0}},

by choosing
\displaystyle T_{0}
sufficiently small such that
\displaystyle C_{M_{0}}T_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq 1
. Thanks to this, by choosing
\displaystyle M_{0}\geq C_{X_{0}}
,
\displaystyle\mathfrak{F}
is a mapping from
\displaystyle\mathscr{B}_{0}
to itself.

For
\displaystyle(v_{i},\sigma_{i},p_{i})\in\mathscr{B}_{0}
, denote
\displaystyle(V_{i},\Sigma_{i},P_{i})=\mathfrak{F}(v_{i},\sigma_{i},p_{i})
,
\displaystyle i=1,2

\displaystyle(V,\Sigma,P)=(V_{1}-V_{2},\Sigma_{1}-\Sigma_{2},P_{1}-P_{2})

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|\mathfrak{F}(v_{1},\sigma_{1},p_{1})-\mathfrak{F}(v_{2},\sigma_{2},p_{2})\|_{\mathscr{Y}_{T_{0}}\times\mathscr{Y}_{T_{0}}\times\mathscr{X}_{T_{0}}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|V_{1}-V_{2}\|_{\mathscr{Y}_{T_{0}}}+\|\Sigma_{1}-\Sigma_{2}\|_{\mathscr{Y}_{T_{0}}}+\|p_{1}-p_{2}\|_{\mathscr{X}_{T_{0}}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle C\mathcal{L}_{T_{0}}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\sigma_{1})\mathcal{L}_{T_{0}}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\sigma_{2})(\|v_{0}\|_{H^{3}}+\|N_{1}(v_{2},\sigma_{2},p_{2})\|_{L^{2}(0,T_{0};H^{2})}+\|\sigma_{0}\|_{H^{3}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle+\|N_{2}(v_{2},\sigma_{2},p_{2})\|_{L^{2}(0,T_{0};H^{2})})(\|\Delta(\sigma_{1}-\sigma_{2})\|_{L^{2}(0,T_{0};L^{2})}+\|\sigma_{1}-\sigma_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{O}\times(0,T_{0}))})

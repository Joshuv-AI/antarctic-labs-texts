(Iso)spin from Isospin in Top-Down Holography
Marcelo Oyarzo

Abstract




Abstract

Motivated by the spin from isospin mechanism of Jackiw-Rebbi-Hasenfratz-’t Hooft, we study two SU(2) gauged supergravity solutions of the form
M_{d}\times\text{S}^{2}
containing non-Abelian hedgehog monopole on the 2-sphere. Due to the presence of the monopole, the SO(3) isometry group of the 2-sphere is not a symmetry of the configuration. Instead, a diagonal combination of the SU(2) gauge and the SO(3) isometry of the 2-sphere is the true symmetry of the configuration. Uplifting the solutions to Type II, the gauge-isometry diagonal symmetry becomes a diagonal combination between the SO(3) symmetry of the 2-sphere and a SU(2) symmetry of a 3-sphere used to uplift the configuration. One of the uplifts is supersymmetric and corresponds to the I-brane theory on a 2-sphere. The second background is a deformation of AdS
{}_{5}\times\text{S}^{5}
and is not supersymmetric. We study dilaton fluctuations on the later geometry. Due to the diagonal symmetry, the fluctuations show angular momentum mixing between the SU(2) and SO(3) spins, mimicking the spin from isospin mechanism.

2.1
SU(2) Meron Field

In Yang-Mills theory, a Meron gauge field
[
11
,
12
]
is defined as

A=\lambda U^{-1}\mathrm{d}U.

(2.1)

Although this configuration is proportional to a pure gauge one, it is in fact the simplest intrinsically non-Abelian configuration. In fact, its field strength,
F=\mathrm{d}A+gA\wedge A
with
g
the gauge coupling, is non-zero
2
2

2

We are in conventions where the coupling constant appears in the definition of the field strength instead of as a coefficient on the kinetic term. A similar, and more detailed, discussion in the other convention can be found in
[
4
]
.

F=\lambda\left(1-g\lambda\,\right)\mathrm{d}U^{-1}\wedge\mathrm{d}U,

(2.2)

so that this configuration is only pure gauge if
\lambda=g^{-1}
, as expected. In what follows we focus on SU(2) gauge theories, so that
U\in
SU(2), and on spacetimes solutions of SU(2) gauged supergravities containing a 2-sphere, that is

\mathrm{d}s^{2}=g_{ab}\mathrm{d}x^{a}\mathrm{d}x^{b}+f(x)\mathrm{d}s^{2}(\text{S}^{2})\,.

(2.3)

To parametrize the 2-sphere, it is convenient to use the embedding coordinates
\mu^{i}
, with
i=1,2,3
, satisfying
\mu^{i}\mu^{i}=1
, so that
\mathrm{d}s^{2}(\text{S}^{2})=\mathrm{d}\mu^{i}\mathrm{d}\mu^{i}
. This allows us to choose the following hedgehog configuration for the SU(2) Meron
3
3

3

A more general ansatz can be chosen, but this is enough for our discussion.

U=2\mu^{i}T^{i},

(2.4)

where the SU(2) generators are
T^{i}=-\frac{i}{2}\sigma^{i}
, with
\sigma^{i}
the Pauli matrices, which sets the structure constants to

. With this ansatz, the gauge field and the field strength take the form

A^{i}=-2\lambda\epsilon_{ijk}\mu^{j}\mathrm{d}\mu^{k},\quad F^{i}=-4\lambda(1-g\lambda)\mu^{i}\text{vol}(\text{S}^{2}).

(2.5)

At this stage, it is already possible to see the mixing of the spacetime SO(3) with the gauge SU(2) symmetries. It is clear from (
2.3
) that the spacetime under consideration is invariant under the SO(3) isometry group of the 2-sphere. However, the field strength (
2.5
) is not SO(3) invariant due to factor of
\mu^{i}
. Interestingly, under a simultaneous SO(3) and SU(2) action, the field strength is indeed invariant, revealing the true symmetry of the system. This SO(3) invariance up to a SU(2) transformation was discussed in
[
15
,
19
]
for a different type of hedgehog configuration. There it was argued that since the conserved charge is the sum of spin
\vec{J}
and isospin
\vec{T}

\vec{L}_{\text{total}}=\vec{J}+\vec{T},

(2.6)

one should classify quantum fluctuations with respect to the quantum numbers of this symmetry. Hence, a naive SO(3) boson could actually have fermionic quantum numbers depending on its SU(2) representation.
The proportionality factor depends on the specific theory under consideration. It is important to also remark that the components of the Meron gauge field (
2.5
) are 1-forms dual to the
\text{S}^{2}
Killing vectors.

In the following sections, we will work on gauged supergravities where the equation of motion of the non-Abelian gauge field reduces to

\mathrm{d}\star F+g\left[A,\star F\right]=0\quad\Rightarrow\quad\lambda=\frac{1}{2g}

(2.7)

Below, we focus on lifts to 10D of SU(2) gauged supergravities on geometries that contain a 3-sphere. We show below that in this lifts the same isometry mixing occurs (
2.6
), with the gauge symmetry replaced by the isometries of the internal manifold used to lift the configuration. Before discussing two solutions explicitly, we discuss some general aspects of the 10D geometries resulting from these lifts.

2.2
Uplifts using 3-spheres

Let us consider a pseudo-Rimemannian manifold

\displaystyle\hat{M}=M\ltimes[\text{S}^{2}\ltimes(\text{S}^{3}\times\dots\times\text{S}^{3})]

The manifold

to write the 3-spheres using the left-invariant Maurer-Cartan 1-forms
\omega_{\alpha}=g_{\alpha}^{-1}\mathrm{d}g_{\alpha}

\mathrm{d}\omega^{i}=-\frac{1}{2}\epsilon_{ijk}\omega^{j}\wedge\omega^{k},

(2.8)

for any
\alpha

4
4

4

Generically, the uplifted SU(2) gauge fields appear as a fiber over the 3-sphere by means of the Maurer-Cartan forms as
\omega^{i}-gA^{i}
. Since the gauge field (
2.5
), together with

, what enters the 10D metric is the combination
\omega^{i}+\epsilon_{ijl}\mu^{j}d\mu^{k}

\mathrm{d}s^{2}=\mathrm{d}s^{2}(M)+H_{0}\,\mathrm{d}s^{2}(\text{S}^{2})+\frac{1}{4}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{n}H_{\alpha}\sum_{i=1}^{3}(\omega_{\alpha}^{i}+A^{i})^{2}

2.2.1
Uplifted Diagonal Isometry

As stated before, the one forms
A^{i}=\epsilon_{ijk}\mu^{j}\mathrm{d}\mu^{k}
are dual to the Killing vectors
(J^{i})
on the 2-sphere

(J^{i})^{b}=\epsilon_{ijk}\mu^{j}\partial_{a}\mu^{k}{\bar{\gamma}}^{ab},

(2.10)

where
a=1,2
are coordinates along the 2-sphere and
{\bar{\gamma}}^{ab}
is inverse metric of the unit-radius 2-sphere. Then, the one forms
A^{i}
satisfy

\mathscr{L}_{J^{i}}A^{j}=-\epsilon_{ijk}A^{k}.

(2.11)

On the other hand, on each 3-sphere (without the fibration) there is a SU(2)
{}_{L}\times
SU(2)
R
isometry, generated by the left (
L^{i}
) and right (
R^{i}
) Killing vectors, which satisfy
5
5

5

The
L/R
vectors are dual to the
R/L
invariant forms.

\left[L^{i},L^{j}\right]=-\epsilon_{ijk}L^{k},\quad\left[R^{i},R^{j}\right]=\epsilon_{ijk}R^{k}.

(2.12)

While the
R/L
are invariant under the
R/L
action, they are charged under the opposite one. For our purposes, we only require

\mathscr{L}_{R^{i}}\omega^{j}=\epsilon_{ijk}\,\omega^{k}.

(2.13)

At this point we note that this action is of the same form as (
2.11
), which is the key element for the existence of the diagonal symmetry. Consider the vector

K^{i}=-J^{i}+\sum_{\alpha}R_{\alpha}^{i},

(2.14)

where we have restored the index
\alpha
labelling the different 3-spheres. We now compute the Lie derivative of the metric (
2.9
) along
K^{i}

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}_{K^{i}}\mathrm{d}s^{2}

\displaystyle=\mathscr{L}_{K^{i}}\mathrm{d}s^{2}(M)+\mathscr{L}_{K^{i}}H_{0}\,\mathrm{d}s^{2}(\text{S}^{2})+H_{0}\mathscr{L}_{K^{i}}\mathrm{d}s^{2}(\text{S}^{2})+\frac{1}{4}\sum_{\alpha}\mathscr{L}_{K^{i}}H_{\alpha}\sum_{i}(\omega_{\alpha}^{i}+A^{i})^{2}+

(2.15)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha}H_{\alpha}\sum_{i}(\omega_{\alpha}^{i}+A^{i})\otimes\mathscr{L}_{K^{i}}(\omega_{\alpha}^{i}+A^{i})\,.

Since the Lie derivative only has support on the 2-sphere and on the 3-spheres

\mathscr{L}_{K^{i}}\mathrm{d}s^{2}(M)=\mathscr{L}_{K^{i}}H_{0}=\mathscr{L}_{K^{i}}H_{\alpha}=0\,.

(2.16)

On the other hand, the action of the Lie derivative is linear in the vector fields
\mathscr{L}_{X+Y}Z=\mathscr{L}_{X}Z+\mathscr{L}_{Y}Z
, we have that on the metric on the 2-sphere

\mathscr{L}_{K^{i}}\mathrm{d}s^{2}(\text{S}^{2})=\mathscr{L}_{-J^{i}+\sum_{\alpha}R^{i}_{\alpha}}\mathrm{d}s^{2}(\text{S}^{2})=\mathscr{L}_{-J^{i}}\mathrm{d}s^{2}(\text{S}^{2})=0\,.

(2.17)

Finally, the action on the fibered 3-spheres is

\mathscr{L}_{K^{j}}(\omega_{\alpha}^{i}+A^{i})=\sum_{\beta}\mathscr{L}_{R^{j}_{(\beta)}}\omega_{\alpha}^{i}+\mathscr{L}_{-J^{j}}A^{i}=\epsilon_{jil}(\omega_{\alpha}^{l}+A^{l}),

(2.18)

from where the Lie derivative of the metric (
2.15
)

\mathscr{L}_{K^{j}}\mathrm{d}s^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha}H_{\alpha}\sum_{i}(\omega_{\alpha}^{i}+A^{i})\otimes\epsilon_{jil}(\omega_{\alpha}^{l}+A^{l})=0\,,

(2.19)

Hence,
K^{i}
is a Killing vector of the fibered metric (
2.9
). Let us remark that the key element for this result is that the actions of
J^{i}
and
R^{i}
on their dual forms is the same, (
2.11
) and (
2.13
) respectively, which allows one to obtain (
2.18
). Also, it is direct to check that

\left[K^{i},K^{j}\right]=\epsilon_{ijk}K^{k}

(2.20)

so that it closes as an SU(2) algebra.

With this, the complete set of isometries of the spacetime (
2.9
) is

\mathrm{Isom}(M)\times\underbrace{\mathrm{SU}(2)\times\dots\times\mathrm{SU}(2)}_{n-\mathrm{times}}\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_{D}

(2.21)

where, the
n
copies of SU(2) factors correspond to the SU(2)
L
of each 3-sphere which is not broken by the fibrations (as
\omega^{i}
invariant under
L^{i}
). It is also possible to show that this is the case by following Appendix C of

When lifting to Type II, we need to ensure that the dilaton and the NS-NS and RR fluxes are also invariant under the (
2.21
) for it to be a symmetry of the full configuration. For the dilaton we just require that it does not depend on the 2- and 3-spheres. In order to construct

-forms which are invariant under

we can use the invariant tensors

and

\displaystyle=C_{\ell;\hat{m_{L}},\hat{m_{R}}}e^{\mathrm{i}(\hat{m_{L}}\tau+\hat{m_{R}}\varphi)}\cos^{\hat{m_{L}}+\hat{m_{R}}}\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)\sin^{\hat{m_{L}}-\hat{m_{R}}}\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)P_{\frac{\hat{\ell}}{2}-\hat{m_{L}}}^{(\hat{m_{L}}-\hat{m_{R}},\hat{m_{L}}+\hat{m_{R}})}(\cos\theta)\,,

\displaystyle C_{\ell;m_{1},m_{2}}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{\ell+1}{2\pi^{2}}}\sqrt{\frac{(\frac{\ell}{2}+m_{1})!(\frac{\ell}{2}-m_{1})!}{(\frac{\ell}{2}+m_{2})!(\frac{\ell}{2}-m_{2})!}}\,.

(5.7)

where
P_{n}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(x)
are the Jacobi polynomials satisfying

\displaystyle(1-x^{2})\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}P_{n}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(x)}{\mathrm{d}x^{2}}+(\beta-\alpha-(\alpha+\beta+2)x)\frac{\mathrm{d}P_{n}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(x)}{\mathrm{d}x}+n(n+\alpha+\beta+1)P_{n}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(x)

\displaystyle=0\,.

(5.8)

Each family of functions defined in (
5.7
) satisfies

\displaystyle L^{i}L^{i}Y^{\ell}_{m_{L},m_{R}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\ell(\ell+2)}{4}Y^{\ell}_{m_{L},m_{R}}\,,

(5.9)

\displaystyle\hat{L}^{i}\hat{L}^{i}\hat{Y}^{\ell}_{\hat{m}_{L},\hat{m}_{R}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\hat{\ell}(\hat{\ell}+2)}{4}Y^{\hat{\ell}}_{\hat{m}_{L},\hat{m}_{R}}\,.

(5.10)

Regularity of the solutions require

\displaystyle\ell

\displaystyle=0,1,2,3,\dots

(5.11)

\displaystyle m_{L},m_{R}

\displaystyle\in\left\{-\frac{\ell}{2}+n:n=0,1,2,\dots,\ell\right\}

(5.12)

With this, we now proceed to the study of eigenfunctions of the Laplacian on M
7
.

5.2.1
Zero modes on the spheres

The simplest case corresponds to
\Phi_{\text{M}_{7}}=\Phi_{\text{M}_{7}}(\xi)
. In this case, the equation for the dilaton reduces to

\frac{1}{\cos^{3}(\xi)\sin(\xi)}\partial_{\xi}\left(\cos^{3}(\xi)\sin(\xi)\partial_{\xi}\Phi\right)=-M^{2}\Phi_{\text{M}_{7}},

(5.13)

with solution

\displaystyle\Phi_{\text{M}_{7}}

\displaystyle=\frac{c_{1}}{z}\,{}_{2}F_{1}\left(-\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{4+M^{2}},\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{4+M^{2}};0;\cos^{2}(\xi)\right)

(5.14)

\displaystyle+c_{2}\,{}_{2}F_{1}\left(1-\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{4+M^{2}},1+\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{4+M^{2}};2;\cos^{2}(\xi)\right),

were
{}_{2}F_{1}(a,b;c;z)
is the ordinary hypergeometric function. Regularity of the solution at
\xi=0
and
\xi=\pi/2
requires

c_{1}=0,\quad M^{2}=4(p^{2}-1),\quad\text{with }p=1,2,3,...

(5.15)

The mode
p=0
is excluded since
{}_{2}F_{1}\left(1,1;2;z\right)=-\log(1-z)/z
, which is singular at
z=1
(here
z=\cos^{2}(\xi)
so that
z=1
is
\xi=\pi/2
).

Having determined
M^{2}
, we can explicitly check that the BF bound is satisfied since
M^{2}>0
for any
p
.

5.2.2
Spherical Harmonics on the squashed 3-sphere

The next simplest subsector is to allow the dilaton to depend on
(\xi,\theta,\varphi,\tau)
. Since we are not exciting modes on the 3-sphere
(\theta_{2},\varphi_{2},\psi)
, effectively
K^{i}=\tilde{L}^{i}
and
W=-\partial_{\tau}
. The equation now reads

\Lambda^{-2}\tilde{L}^{i}\tilde{L}^{i}\Phi+\left(\frac{4}{\sin^{2}(\xi)}-\Lambda^{-2}\right)\partial^{2}_{\tau}\Phi+\frac{1}{\cos^{3}(\xi)\sin(\xi)}\partial_{\xi}\left(\cos^{3}(\xi)\sin(\xi)\partial_{\xi}\Phi\right)=-M^{2}\Phi_{\text{M}_{7}}

(5.16)

The first two terms correspond to the Laplacian on the squashed 3-sphere. We can then use a decomposition in spherical harmonics

\Phi=h(\xi)Y^{\hat{\ell}}_{\hat{m}_{L},\hat{m}_{R}}(\theta,\varphi,\tau),

(5.17)

which leads to the following equation for
h(\xi)

-\frac{\Lambda^{-2}}{4}\hat{\ell}(\hat{\ell}+2)h-\left(\frac{4}{\sin^{2}(\xi)}-\Lambda^{-2}\right)\hat{m}^{2}_{R}h+\frac{1}{\cos^{3}(\xi)\sin(\xi)}\partial_{\xi}\left(\cos^{3}(\xi)\sin(\xi)\partial_{\xi}h\right)=-M^{2}h

(5.18)

with solution

\displaystyle h(\xi)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\hat{m}_{R}}(1-z)^{-\hat{m}_{R}}{}_{2}F_{1}\left(1-\hat{m}_{R}-p,1-\hat{m}_{R}+p;2;\cos^{2}(\xi)\right),

(5.19)

\displaystyle M^{2}

\displaystyle=4(p^{2}-1)+\frac{1}{\Lambda^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{4}\hat{\ell}(\hat{\ell}+2)-\hat{m}_{R}^{2}\right),\quad p\geq|\hat{m}_{R}|+1,

where the condition
p\geq|\hat{m}_{R}|+1
is imposed for regularity at the boundary. Again, all these fluctuations are above the BF bound.

5.2.3
Solutions with angular momentum coupling

We now study solutions with non-trivial dependencies on the two 3-spheres. A general solution of the eigenvalue problem (
5.3
) is a linear combination of products of the functions in (
5.7
), with coefficients depending on
\xi
. For simplicity, let us consider a set of solutions of (
5.3
) that depend on all the coordinates of the internal space but are annihilated by the operators

K^{i}K^{i}\Phi=0,\qquad W\Phi=0\,.

(5.20)

In the quantum mechanical problem, this is equivalent to finding a set of pairs of states of quantum numbers
\ket{\ell,m}\otimes\ket{\hat{\ell},\hat{m}}
such that the total angular momentum of the system (the
K^{i}
quantum numbers) are
\ket{k=0,m_{k}=0}
.

It is possible to find a set of functions satisfying (
5.20
) labelled by
\ell,m_{R},\hat{m}_{L}
. Explicitly, they are given by

\displaystyle\Phi_{0}^{\ell,m_{R},\hat{m}_{L}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=-\ell/2}^{\ell/2}e^{-\frac{\mathrm{i}\pi m_{R}}{2}}Y^{\ell}_{n,m_{R}}(\theta_{2},\varphi_{2},\psi)Y^{\ell}_{\hat{m}_{L},n}(\theta,\varphi,\tau)\,.

(5.21)

These functions also satisfy

\displaystyle L^{i}L^{i}\Phi_{0}^{\ell,m_{R},\hat{m}_{L}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\ell(\ell+2)}{4}\Phi_{0}^{\ell,m_{R},\hat{m}_{L}}\,,

(5.22)

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau}^{2}\Phi_{0}^{\ell,m_{R},\hat{m}_{L}}

\displaystyle=-\hat{m}_{L}^{2}\Phi_{0}^{\ell,m_{R},\hat{m}_{L}}\,.

(5.23)

Let us consider
\Phi_{M_{7}}=h(\xi)\Phi_{0}^{\ell,m_{R},\hat{m}_{L}}
. Then, the eigenvalue problem for the M
7
Laplacian reads

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\cos^{3}\xi\sin\xi}\partial_{\xi}(\cos^{3}\xi\sin\xi\partial_{\xi}h)-\frac{4\hat{m}_{L}^{2}}{\sin^{2}\xi}h-\frac{\ell(\ell+2)}{\cos^{2}\xi}h

\displaystyle=-M^{2}h\,.

(5.24)

The solution of this equation is given by

\displaystyle h(\xi)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sin^{2\hat{m}_{L}}(\xi)}\left[\frac{C_{1}}{\cos^{2+\ell}(\xi)}\,_{2}F_{1}\left(a-1-\ell,b-1-\ell;-\ell;\cos^{2}\xi\right)+\right.

\displaystyle\hskip 40.00006pt\hskip 20.00003pt\left.\phantom{\frac{1}{2}}+C_{2}\cos^{\ell}(\xi)\,_{2}F_{1}\left(a,b;\ell+2;\cos^{2}\xi\right)\right]

(5.25)

\displaystyle a

\displaystyle\equiv 1+\frac{\ell}{2}-\sqrt{1+\frac{M^{2}}{2}}-2\hat{m}_{L}\,,

(5.26)

\displaystyle b

\displaystyle\equiv 1+\frac{\ell}{2}+\sqrt{1+\frac{M^{2}}{2}}-2\hat{m}_{L}\,.

(5.27)

where
C_{1},C_{2}
are integration constants. Let us analyse the regularity at
\xi=0,\frac{\pi}{2}
. Let
\Gamma(x)

be the Euler Gamma function. For any
{}_{2}F_{1}(a,b,c;\cos^{2}\xi)
, the expansion closed to
\xi=0
goes as

{}_{2}F_{1}\left(a,b;c;\cos^{2}\xi\right)

\displaystyle\simeq\Gamma(c)\left(\frac{\pi}{90\sin(\pi(a+b-c))\Gamma(1+a+b-c)\Gamma(-a+c)\Gamma(-b+c)}+\mathcal{O}(\xi^{2})\right)\,,

\displaystyle\text{for }-a-b+c>0\,.

(5.28)

where
\mathcal{O}(\xi^{2})
are subleading terms that do not contain
\Gamma(c)
. The hypergeometric function controlled by
C_{1}
in (
5.28
) has coefficient
c=-\ell
, thus, this branch is ill defined at
\xi=0
. We set
C_{1}=0
. The branch controlled by
C_{2}
has the following expansion

\displaystyle\xi^{-2\hat{m}_{1}}

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\pi\Gamma(2+\ell)}{\Gamma(-1+a+b-\ell)\Gamma(2-a+\ell)\Gamma(2-b+\ell)}+\mathcal{O}(\xi^{2})\right)-

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt-\xi^{2\hat{m}_{1}}\left(\frac{\pi\Gamma(2+\ell)}{\Gamma(a)\Gamma(b)\Gamma(3-a-b+\ell)}+\mathcal{O}(\xi^{2})\right)

Since
\hat{m}_{1}\in\{-\ell/2,\dots,\ell/2\}
, let us split the analysis in three cases:

•

For
\hat{m}_{1}>0
, then regularity can be
obtained by imposing

\displaystyle 2-a+\ell

\displaystyle=-p\,,\qquad p=0,1,2,\dots

(5.29)

which is solved by the quantization condition

\displaystyle M^{2}

\displaystyle=\ell^{2}+4\ell(1+p+\hat{m}_{1})+4[\hat{m}_{1}^{2}+2\hat{m}_{1}(1+p)+(2+p)p]\,.

(5.30)

•

For
\hat{m}_{1}<0
, regularity at
\xi=0
is achieved by imposing
a=-p
, which leads to the quantization condition

\displaystyle M^{2}

\displaystyle=\ell^{2}+4\ell(1-\hat{m}_{1}+p)+4(\hat{m}_{1}^{2}-2\hat{m}_{1}(1+p)+p(2+p))\,,\qquad\hat{m}_{1}<0\,.

•

For
\hat{m}_{1}=0
(which is only possible for even
\ell
) a logarithmic branch appears, which can be eliminated by imposing

\displaystyle M^{2}

\displaystyle=\ell^{2}+4\ell(1+p)+4p(p+2)\,,

leading to constant behaviour near
\xi=0
.

Regularity at
\xi=\pi/2
does not requires any extra condition. In all the cases discussed above we have
M^{2}>0
, hence, we are always above the BF bound.

6
Conclusion

Motivated by the global-gauge symmetry mixing produced by the presence of non-Abelian hedgehog monopoles in 4D SU(2) gauge theories discussed in
[
19
,
15
]
, in this paper we reviewed a particular SU(2) non-Abelian monopole configuration known as Meron gauge fields. We showed that for this configuration, the argument of
[
19
,
15
]
applies, and also reviewed generalities of uplifts of this type of gauge fields using 3-spheres. At this level, the global-gauge symmetry mixing becomes a diagonal combination of the isometries of the 2-sphere where the non-Abelian monopole is valued, and the 3-sphere used to lift the configuration.

We studied two examples of Type II configurations that have the features described above. The first case, corresponds to a deformation of the 3D theory known as I-branes. The background corresponds to placing the theory on a 2-sphere with a Meron monopole, and it was obtained in
[
5
]
at the level of gauged supergravity. The background corresponds to a 2-dimensional black hole studied in
[
25
,
21
,
30
]
and an 8-dimensional internal manifold, formed by a 2-sphere fibered over two 3-spheres by means of the Meron gauge field. For vanishing black hole mass the configuration is supersymmetric. We find that the Killing spinor is charged under the diagonal symmetry.

The second configuration is new and it corresponds to a solution of 5D SU(2)
\times
U(1) gauged supergravity. The metric is a direct product of the form AdS
{}_{3}\times\text{S}^{2}
, while the SU(2) gauge field is a Meron and there is an Abelian monopole for the U(1) gauge field. When uplifted to Type IIB using
[
23
]
, the background corresponds to a deformation of AdS
{}_{5}\times\text{S}^{5}
, where the D3-branes are wrapping a 2-sphere. In this case, the lower dimensional 2-sphere and one of the
\text{S}^{5}
U(1) directions can be though as (locally) a squashed 3-sphere whose fiber shrinks. There is a second 3-sphere which the squashed one is fibered over. The diagonal symmetry in this case mixes the SU(2)
L
of the squashed 3-sphere with the SU(2)
R
of second 3-sphere. This AdS
3
configuration is not supersymmetric for any range of the parameters.

Finally, we studied dilaton fluctuations on the uplift of the AdS
{}_{3}\times\text{S}^{2}
background. The equation of motion for the fluctuation reduced to an eigenvalue equation for the Laplacian on AdS
3
and M
7
, where M
7
corresponds to the 2-sphere fiber over the 5-sphere used to uplift the geometry to 10-dimensions. We find that for all fluctuations, the effective AdS
3
mass is above the Breitenlohner-Freedman bound, so that the non-supersymmetric background is stable with respect to dilaton fluctuations. On M
7
, the eigenfunctions are labeled by quantum numbers of the global symmetries of the manifold. We find fluctuations which show angular momentum mixing between the SU(2) spins of two 3-spheres inside M
7
, mimicking the spin from isospin mechanism of
[
19
,
15
]
.

Going back to the AdS
{}_{3}\times\text{S}^{2}
solution, from the dual 2D CFT perspective, the diagonal SU(2) symmetry between the SO(3) global and SU(2) gauge symmetries corresponds to an symmetry of the internal space. This is to be compared with
[
19
,
15
]
where the symmetry being mixed is a global (Lorentz) symmetry. Due to this, the diagonal symmetry does not produce a shift of the spin, in the sense of representations of the Lorentz group, but rather a shift of the SO(3) isospin, so that we instead have an “isospin from isospin” mechanism. Furthermore, in
[
19
,
15
]
the global symmetry is mixed with a gauge one, whereas here the internal symmetry is mixed with a second internal symmetry. In any case, these results serve as a first approach to obtaining a background which truly realises the results of
[
19
,
15
]
.

From here, there are several interesting directions to explore

This shows the universality of this phenomena. It would be interesting to study this uplift as a non-supersymmetric reduction of 4D

One possibility to obtain a solution realising a diagonal symmetry between a Lorentz symmetry and a internal symmetry, is to interpret the AdS

\mathrm{d}s^{2}_{5D}=e^{2f(r)}\mathrm{d}x^{2}_{1,1}+e^{2g(r)}\mathrm{d}s^{2}(\text{S}^{2})+\mathrm{d}r^{2}.

(6.1)

If this solution exists, from the point of view of a 4D observer, there would really be a spin from isospin effect. A more general profile for the SU(2) and U(1) gauge fields and a non-zero dilaton might be necessary.

The key ingredient in realizing the diagonal symmetry is that the manifolds
\text{S}^{2}

A possible generalization is to consider manifolds that share a common subalgebra of their isometry groups, allowing one to construct 1-forms whose Lie derivatives along this subalgebra close among themselves. In principle, this would allow the construction of manifolds with non-trivial fibrations that can serve as internal spaces for solutions of 10- or 11-dimensional supergravity.

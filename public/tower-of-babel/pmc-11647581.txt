Bochdalek hernia-induced ventricular tachycardia
Authors: Giulia Bruno, James Shand, Jonathan Dodd
Published: 2024

Summary

This case describes the first case of ventricular arrhythmia secondary to Bochdalek hernia and introduces a new example of large masses, such as hernias, that can cause cardiac compression and result in arrhythmogenic phenomena. Cases of left atrium compression and atrial fibrillation have been documented.1,2 While we cannot be certain that in this instance the non-sustained ventricular tachycardia (NSVT) originated from the area of compression, this seems likely, given normal left ventricular function, absence of documented coronary artery disease, and event resolution upon hernia repair. Cardiac compression should therefore be included in the differential diagnosis of both atrial and ventricular arrhythmias. Early recognition and prompt treatment of reversible causes should indeed always be sought.

Case description

A 78-year-old man presented with a 6-month history of dyspnoea. His background included recurrent urinary tract infections and multiple episodes of syncope, for which an implantable loop recorder (ILR) was inserted.

The absent breath sounds and dullness were noted at the left lung base.

The patient was haemodynamically stable and afebrile.

Interrogation of the ILR demonstrated episodes of NSVT (Figure 1A). Coronary angiography was performed showing normal coronary arteries. Transthoracic echocardiography showed normal left ventricular function.

Non-sustained ventricular tachycardia on implantable loop recorder (A). Left basal changes on chest X-ray (B). Kidney herniation on computed tomography (C). Left ventricular compression on computed tomography (D).

Laboratory testing revealed no signs of infection.

Chest X-ray showed possible left pleural effusion with lower lobe collapse (Figure 1B).

Computed tomography revealed Bochdalek hernia with herniation of the left kidney and peri-renal fat. The proximal left ureter was pinched at the neck of the hernia with resultant hydronephrosis (Figure 1C). The herniated structures led to the compression of the left ventricle and possibly acted as a trigger for the onset of ventricular tachycardia (Figure 1D).

The patient underwent surgical diaphragmatic hernia repair. No further VT episodes were detected on ILR in the 12 months post-surgery. The patient remained asymptomatic during the post-surgery follow-up (12 months).

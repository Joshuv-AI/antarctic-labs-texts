Universal approximation property of ODENet and ResNet with a single activation function
Masato Kimura, Kazunori Matsui, Yosuke Mizuno

Abstract
We study a universal approximation property of ODENet and ResNet. The ODENet is a map from an initial value to the final value of an ODE system in a finite interval. It is considered a mathematical model of a ResNet-type deep learning system. We consider dynamical systems with vector fields given by a single composition of the activation function and an affine mapping, which is the most common choice of the ODENet or ResNet vector field in actual machine learning systems. We show that such an ODENet and ResNet with a restricted vector field can uniformly approximate ODENet with a general vector field.



Keywords:
Deep neural network, ODENet, ResNet, Universal approximation property

Introduction

Neural networks have significantly impacted computer vision, natural language processing, and learning methods
[
24
]
. Historically, these networks have been seen as models inspired by the human brain and eye
[
9
,
20
]
. A neural network, with the input layer as layer
0
, the output layer as layer
L
, and the dimensions of each layer as
N\in\mathbb{N}
, is represented by the following equations:

\left\{\begin{aligned} x^{(l+1)}&=f^{(l)}(x^{(l)})&(l=0,1,\ldots,L-1),\\
x^{(0)}&=\xi\in D,&\end{aligned}\right.

where
f^{(l)}:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
is a bounded closed subset.
The network’s input and output are
\xi
and
x^{(L)}
, respectively.
Neural networks with a large number of layers are called deep networks and perform better than single-hidden-layer neural networks for large-scale and high-dimensional challenges. Notable models like AlexNet
[
16
]
and GoogLeNet
[
26
]
contain many layers.

However, if the depth is overly increased, the accuracy might stagnate or degrade
[
13
]
. Additionally, gradient vanishing or exploding issues may arise in deeper networks, making these networks harder to train
[
3
,
10
]
. To address these issues, the authors in
[
14
]
recommended using residual learning to facilitate the training of considerably deeper networks than previously used. Such a network is called a residual network or ResNet, which has the form:

\left\{\begin{aligned} x^{(l+1)}&=x^{(l)}+f^{(l)}(x^{(l)})&(l=0,1,\ldots,L-1),\\
x^{(0)}&=\xi\in D.&\end{aligned}\right.

(1.1)

ResNet can be viewed as an explicit discretization of a system of
ordinary differential equations
[
5
]
.
Choosing a small
h>0
and denoting
f^{(\ell)}/h
again by
f^{(\ell)}

in (
1.1
), we obtain

\left\{\begin{aligned} x^{(l+1)}&=x^{(l)}+hf^{(l)}(x^{(l)})&(l=0,1,\ldots,L-1),\\
x^{(0)}&=\xi\in D,&\end{aligned}\right.

(1.2)

which is the explicit Euler scheme for the following
initial value problem of a system of ODEs.
Putting
x(t):=x^{(l)}
and
f(x,t):=f^{(l)}(x)
, where
t=hl
,
T=hL
and
f:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{N}
, and taking the limit of (
1.2
) as
h
approaches zero, we formally obtain the following initial value problem of a system of ODEs:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}x^{\prime}(t)=f(x(t),t)\hskip 9.24994pt(0\leq t\leq T),\\
x(0)=\xi\in D.\end{array}\right.

In the context of machine learning,
it is called a neural ordinary differential equation (NODE)
[
5
]
.
We call the function
D\ni\xi\mapsto x(T)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
an ODENet with a vector field
f
. The utilization of NODEs is significant in invertible neural network architectures for constructing more expressive generative models and continuous normalizing flows, facilitating advanced generative modeling and density estimation tasks
[
8
,
11
,
21
]
(cf.
[
12
]
). For a comprehensive overview, we refer to
[
15
]
. We restrict the function
f
to the following form:

f(x,t)=\alpha(t)\odot\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$}(\beta(t)x+\gamma(t)),

(1.5)

where
\alpha:[0,T]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{N}
is a weight vector,
\beta:[0,T]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}
is a weight matrix, and
\gamma:[0,T]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{N}
is a bias vector. The operator
\odot
denotes the Hadamard product (element-wise product) of two vectors defined by (
2.1
). The function
\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$}:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
is defined by

\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$}(x):=\left(\sigma(x_{1}),\sigma(x_{2}),\cdots,\sigma(x_{N})\right)^{\top},

(1.6)

where
\sigma:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is called an activation function. The activation function is, for example, the sigmoid function
\sigma(x)=(1+e^{-x})^{-1}
, the hyperbolic tangent function
\sigma(x)=\tanh(x)
, and the rectified linear unit (ReLU) function
\sigma(x)=\max\{0,x\}
.

It has been shown in
[
18
,
23
]
that an ODENet has universal approximation property (UAP) in the
L^{p}
-norm, and in particular,
[
18
]
also proves UAP in the sup-norm in the one-dimensional case. The UAP in the sup-norm is shown in
[
27
]
under the assumption that the activation function is a solution of a quadratic differential equation (and the target function is monotone and analytic), and in
[
1
]
for the case where the layer width is
2N
.
Additionally,
[
28
]
shows that an ODENet with a vector field given by a linear combination of activation functions (Remark
3.4
) has UAP (cf.
[
7
,
29
]
).

From the general discussion on the explicit Euler method, if ODENet has UAP, then ResNet also has UAP
[
1
,
18
,
23
,
29
]
. In
[
19
]
, it is shown that a ResNet with the ReLU activation function, where the dimensions of the input layer and output one are
N
and
1
, respectively, alternating layers of
1
and
N
dimensions, has UAP in the
L^{1}
-norm.

In this paper, we establish the conditions for UAP in the sup-norm for the ODENet with respect to any non-polynomial continuous activation function that satisfies a Lipschitz condition. Here, the layer width is
N
.
Specifically, we prove that if
\sigma
has UAP and satisfies a Lipschitz condition, then the ODENet with respect to any function satisfying the Lipschitz condition can be approximated by the ODENet with respect to a function of type (
1.5
). Additionally, we also demonstrate that ResNet has UAP.
The organization of this paper is as follows. Section
2
defines the notation and function spaces used in this paper, with particular emphasis on the definitions of ODENet, ResNet, and the universal approximation property. Section
3
introduces the main theorems. In Section
4
, we prepare the necessary theorems and lemmas for proving the main theorems, and in Section
5
, we provide the proofs of the main theorems.

2
ODENet and universal approximation property

For
a=(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{N})^{\top},b=(b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{N})^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, the Hadamard product of
a
and
b
is defined by

a\odot b:=\left(a_{1}b_{1},a_{2}b_{2},\cdots,a_{N}b_{N}\right)^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

(2.1)

For a function
f:\mathbb{R}^{N}\times[0,T]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{N}
, we define its Lipschitz constant by

\displaystyle\text{\rm Lip}(f)\coloneqq\sup\left\{\frac{|f(z,t)-f(\zeta,t)|}{|z-\zeta|}\middle|\;z,\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{N},\;z\neq\zeta,\;t\in[0,T]\right\},

and, if
\text{\rm Lip}(f)<\infty
, we say that
f
satisfies a Lipschitz condition with respect to
x
.

We define a solution to the system of ODEs (
1
) as

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} x(t)&=\xi+\int^{t}_{0}f(x(s),s)ds\hskip 9.24994pt(0\leq t\leq T),\\
x&\in C^{0}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{N}).\end{aligned}\right.

(2.2)

Unless otherwise specified, we assume that

\displaystyle f:\mathbb{R}^{N}\times[0,T]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{N}\mbox{ satisfies a Lipschitz condition with respect to }x

(2.3)

and

\displaystyle f\in L^{\infty}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times[0,T]).

(2.4)

Although we do not assume continuity of
f
with respect to time, there exists a unique solution to (
1
) by Theorem
4.1
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a bounded closed subset. We define ODENet and ResNet as follows.

Definition 2.1

(ODENet)
.

For the system of ODEs (
1
), we call
S_{f}(t)\xi\coloneqq x(t)\hskip 9.24994pt(0\leq t\leq T)
a “flow” associated with the vector field
f
and call
S_{f}(T):\xi\mapsto x(T)
ODENet with respect to
f
.

Definition 2.2

(ResNet)
.

For a sequence of functions
f_{R}=\{f^{(l)}_{R}:\mathbb{R}^{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{N}\}_{l=0}^{L-1}
, we define mappings
S^{res}_{f_{R}}(l):D\ni\xi\mapsto x^{(l)}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, for
l\in 0,\ldots,L
, by the following recurrence formula

\left\{\begin{aligned} x^{(l+1)}&=x^{(l)}+f^{(l)}_{R}(x^{(l)}),&(l=0,1,\ldots,L-1),\\
x^{(0)}&=\xi\in D,\end{aligned}\right.

(2.5)

In particular, we call
S^{res}_{f_{R}}(L)
ResNet with respect to
f_{R}
.

Definition 2.3

(Universal approximation property for the activation function
\sigma
)
.

For
\sigma:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, consider the set

S:=\left\{G:D\to\mathbb{R}\left|\;G(\xi)=\sum_{l=1}^{L}\alpha_{l}\sigma(\beta_{l}\cdot\xi+\gamma_{l}),L\in\mathbb{N},\alpha_{l},\gamma_{l}\in\mathbb{R},\beta_{l}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\right.\right\}.

Suppose that
S
is dense in
C^{0}(D)
. In other words, given
F\in C^{0}(D)
and
\epsilon>0
, there exists a function
G\in S
such that

|G(\xi)-F(\xi)|<\epsilon

for all
\xi\in D
. Then, we say that
\sigma
has a universal approximation property (UAP) on
D
.

In this paper, we assume that
\sigma\in C^{0}(\mathbb{R})
satisfying the following condition:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\sigma\mbox{ has UAP on all bounded closed subsets and
satisfies the Lipschitz condition with respect to }x.\end{aligned}

(2.6)

A non-polynomial function has UAP on all bounded closed subsets
[
17
,
25
]
(cf.
[
6
]
).
For example, the truncated power function
x\mapsto x_{+}^{k}
, defined by

x_{+}^{k}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}x^{k}&x>0\\
0&x\leq 0\end{array}\right.\hskip 9.24994pt(k\in\mathbb{N}),

the ReLU function
x\mapsto x^{1}_{+}
, the softplus function
x\mapsto\log(1+e^{x})
, the sigmoid function
x\mapsto(1+e^{-x})^{-1}
, and the hyperbolic tangent function
x\mapsto\tanh(x)
have UAP on all bounded closed subsets.

We introduce the notations used in this work. We define the following sets:

\displaystyle{\mathcal{F}}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left\{f\in C^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times[0,T];\mathbb{R}^{N})\left|\;\text{\rm Lip}(f)<\infty\right.\right\},

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left\{h:\mathbb{R}^{N}\times[0,T]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{N}\left|\begin{array}[]{ll}h(x,t)=\alpha(t)\odot\boldsymbol{\sigma}(\beta(t)x+\gamma(t))\text{ for all }(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times[0,T],\\
\text{where }\alpha,\gamma\in C^{0}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{N}),\beta\in C^{0}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{N\times N})\end{array}\right.\right\}\subset{\mathcal{F}},

\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}({\mathcal{F}})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left\{S_{f}(T)\left|f\in{\mathcal{F}}\right.\right\}\subset C^{0}(D;\mathbb{R}^{N}),

\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}({\mathcal{H}})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left\{S_{h}(T)\left|h\in{\mathcal{H}}\right.\right\}\subset{\mathcal{S}}({\mathcal{F}}),

\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}^{res}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left\{S^{res}_{f_{R}}(L)\middle|\;\begin{array}[]{ll}f_{R}=\{f^{(l)}_{R}:\mathbb{R}^{N}\ni x\mapsto\alpha_{l}\odot\boldsymbol{\sigma}(\beta_{l}x+\gamma_{l})\}_{l=0}^{L-1},L\in\mathbb{N},\\
\{\alpha_{l}\}^{L-1}_{l=0},\{\gamma_{l}\}^{L-1}_{l=0}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N},\{\beta_{l}\}^{L-1}_{l=0}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}\end{array}\right\}.

Let
\eta\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
satisfy that

\displaystyle\eta(x)\geq 0~(x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}),\hskip 9.24994pt\eta(x)=0~(x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},\;|x|\geq 1),\hskip 9.24994pt\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\eta(x)dx=1.

For
\alpha,\gamma\in L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
\beta\in L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{N\times N})
, and
\delta>0
, we put

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{c}\tilde{\alpha}(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\alpha(t)\hskip 9.24994pt(0\leq t\leq T)\\
0\hskip 9.24994pt(t<0,T<t)\end{array}\right.,\hskip 9.24994pt\tilde{\beta}(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\beta(t)\hskip 9.24994pt(0\leq t\leq T)\\
0\hskip 9.24994pt(t<0,T<t)\end{array}\right.,\hskip 9.24994pt\tilde{\gamma}(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\gamma(t)\hskip 9.24994pt(0\leq t\leq T)\\
0\hskip 9.24994pt(t<0,T<t)\end{array}\right.\end{array}

and
\alpha_{\delta}\coloneqq\eta_{\delta}\ast\tilde{\alpha},\;\beta_{\delta}\coloneqq\eta_{\delta}\ast\tilde{\beta},\;\gamma_{\delta}\coloneqq\eta_{\delta}\ast\tilde{\gamma}
,
where
\eta_{\delta}(x)=\frac{1}{\delta}\eta\left(\frac{x}{\delta}\right)
and
\ast
is the convolution product.

3
Main theorems

Theorem 3.1

(UAP for ODENet)
.

It holds that

\displaystyle\overline{{\mathcal{S}}({\mathcal{F}})}^{C^{0}(D;\mathbb{R}^{N})}=\overline{{\mathcal{S}}({\mathcal{H}})}^{C^{0}(D;\mathbb{R}^{N})},

i.e., for given
F\in\overline{{\mathcal{S}}({\mathcal{F}})}^{C^{0}(D;\mathbb{R}^{N})}
and
\epsilon>0
, there exists
h\in{\mathcal{H}}
such that

\displaystyle\|F-S_{h}(T)\|_{C^{0}(D;\mathbb{R}^{N})}<\epsilon.

Theorem 3.2
.

It holds that

\displaystyle\overline{{\mathcal{S}}({\mathcal{F}})}^{L^{p}(D;\mathbb{R}^{N})}=\overline{{\mathcal{S}}({\mathcal{H}})}^{L^{p}(D;\mathbb{R}^{N})},

i.e., for given
F\in\overline{{\mathcal{S}}({\mathcal{F}})}^{L^{p}(D;\mathbb{R}^{N})}
and
\epsilon>0
, there exists
h\in{\mathcal{H}}
such that

\displaystyle\|F-S_{h}(T)\|_{L^{p}(D;\mathbb{R}^{N})}<\epsilon.

Remark 3.3
.

In the case where
N\geq 2
and
D
is an open cube, since all measure-preserving maps are included in
\overline{{\mathcal{S}}({\mathcal{F}})}^{L^{p}(D;\mathbb{R}^{N})}

[
4
, Corollary 1.1]
, it follows that all measure-preserving maps are contained in
\overline{{\mathcal{S}}({\mathcal{H}})}^{L^{p}(D;\mathbb{R}^{N})}
.

Remark 3.4
.

Theorem
3.1
claims that
S_{f}
can be approximated by
S_{\alpha\odot\sigma(\beta\cdot x+\gamma)}
, not by

S_{\sum_{l=1}^{L}\alpha_{l}\odot\sigma(\beta_{l}\cdot x+\gamma_{l})}
. Since
\sigma
has UAP,
f
can be approximated by
\sum_{l=1}^{L}\alpha_{l}\odot\sigma(\beta_{l}\cdot x+\gamma_{l})
(cf. Lemma
4.4
), one expects that
S_{f}
can be approximated by
S_{\sum_{l=1}^{L}\alpha_{l}\odot\sigma(\beta_{l}\cdot x+\gamma_{l})}
(cf.
[
28
]
). On the other hand,
f
cannot be approximated by
\alpha\odot\sigma(\beta\cdot x+\gamma)
in general. Thus, Theorem
3.1
is not obvious.

Here we show that, in general, any
F\in C^{0}(D;\mathbb{R}^{N})
cannot be approximated by
S_{f}(T)
.

Proposition 3.5

(
[
30
]
)
.

Let
N=1,D=[-1,1]
, and let
F:D\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
satisfy

\displaystyle F(\xi)=-\xi\hskip 9.24994pt(\xi\in D).

Then, we have
\|F-S_{f}(T)\|_{C^{0}(D;\mathbb{R})}\geq 1
for all
f\in{\mathcal{F}}
.

Proof.

Suppose, for the sake of contradiction, that there exists
f\in{\mathcal{F}}
such that
|S_{f}(T)-F|_{C^{0}(D)}<1
. Let
x_{\pm}(t)=(S_{f}(t))(\pm 1)
(in the same order of composition). Then we have
x_{\pm}(0)=\pm 1
and

\displaystyle x_{+}(T)

\displaystyle<|x_{+}(T)-F(1)|+F(1)<1-1=0,

\displaystyle x_{-}(T)

\displaystyle>-|x_{-}(T)-F(-1)|+F(-1)>-1+1=0.

By the intermediate value theorem, there exists
t^{*}\in(0,T)
such that
x_{-}(t^{*})=x_{+}(t^{*})
. This contradicts the uniqueness of the solution to (
1
) (see Theorem
4.1
).
∎

By Theorem
3.1
, we also obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 3.6
.

For all
f\in{\mathcal{F}}
and
\epsilon>0
, there exists
s^{res}\in{\mathcal{S}}^{res}
such that

\displaystyle\|S_{f}(T)-s^{res}\|_{C^{0}(D;\mathbb{R}^{N})}<\epsilon.

4
Preliminary results

We prepare some theorems and lemmas to prove the main theorems.

Theorem 4.1

(Existence and uniqueness of solutions)
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{N}\times[0,T]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{N}
be measurable and satisfy (
2.3
). If there exists
\xi_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
such that
f(\xi_{0},\cdot)\in L^{\infty}(0,T)
, then there exists a unique solution
x(t)
to (
2.2
). In particular, if
f
satisfies (
2.3
) and (
2.4
), then there exist a unique solution
x(t)
to (
2.2
).

Proof.

We prove Theorem
4.1
using Picard’s successive approximation method.
Let
F_{0}=\|f(\xi_{0},\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T)}
. First, we show the existence of a solution
x(t)
. Consider the following sequence of functions:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}x_{0}(t)\coloneqq\xi_{0}\hskip 9.24994pt(0\leq t\leq T)\\
{\displaystyle x_{n}(t)\coloneqq\xi_{0}+\int^{t}_{0}f(x_{n-1}(s),s)ds\hskip 9.24994pt(0\leq t\leq T)\hskip 9.24994pt(n=1,2,\ldots).}\end{array}\right.

The sequence
\{x_{n}\}^{\infty}_{n=0}\subset C^{0}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{N})
satisfies, for all
n=1,2,\ldots
and
0\leq t\leq T
,

\displaystyle|x_{n}(t)-x_{n-1}(t)|\leq\frac{F_{0}\text{\rm Lip}(f)^{n-1}}{n!}t^{n},

(4.3)

which can be proved by induction. Hence, it holds that for all
m\geq l\geq 1
,

\displaystyle|x_{m}(t)-x_{l}(t)|

\displaystyle\leq\sum^{m}_{j=l+1}|x_{j}(t)-x_{j-1}(t)|\leq\frac{F_{0}}{\text{\rm Lip}(f)}\sum^{m}_{j=l+1}\frac{\text{\rm Lip}(f)^{j}t^{j}}{j!}\leq\frac{F_{0}}{\text{\rm Lip}(f)}\sum^{\infty}_{j=l+1}\frac{(\text{\rm Lip}(f)T)^{j}}{j!}\rightarrow 0

as
l\rightarrow\infty
.
Therefore,
\{x_{n}\}^{\infty}_{n=1}
is a Cauchy sequence in
C^{0}([0,T])
and there exists
x\in C^{0}([0,T])
such that

\displaystyle x_{n}\rightrightarrows x\hskip 9.24994pt\text{on}\hskip 9.24994pt[0,T]\hskip 9.24994pt\text{as}\hskip 9.24994ptn\rightarrow\infty.

Since we have that

\displaystyle\left|\int^{t}_{0}f(x_{n}(s),s)ds-\int^{t}_{0}f(x(s),s)ds\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int^{t}_{0}|f(x_{n}(s),s)-f(x(s),s)|ds\leq\text{\rm Lip}(f)\int^{t}_{0}|x_{n}(s)-x(s)|ds

\displaystyle\leq\text{\rm Lip}(f)T\|x_{n}-x\|_{C^{0}([0,T])}\rightarrow 0

as
n\rightarrow\infty
.
we obtain that
x(t)=\xi+\int^{t}_{0}f(x(s),s)ds\hskip 9.24994pt(0\leq t\leq T)
.

Second, we show the uniqueness of
x
using contradiction. Suppose that there exist two solutions
x,\bar{x}
satisfying
x\neq\bar{x}
. Then, it holds that

\displaystyle|x(t)-\bar{x}(t)|

\displaystyle\leq\int^{t}_{0}|f(x(s),s)-f(\bar{x}(s),s)|ds\leq\text{\rm Lip}(f)\int^{t}_{0}|x(s)-\bar{x}(s)|ds.

By Gronwall’s inequality, we have that

\displaystyle|x(t)-\bar{x}(t)|\leq 0\cdot e^{\text{\rm Lip}(f)t}=0.

It means
x=\bar{x}
. But this contradicts the assumption. Therefore, the solution to (
2.2
) is unique.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Under the assumption of Theorem
4.1
, we have that

\displaystyle|S_{f}(t)\xi_{0}-\xi_{0}|

\displaystyle\leq M\hskip 9.24994pt(0\leq t\leq T),

where
M=F_{0}Te^{\text{\rm Lip}(f)T}
and
F_{0}=\|f(\xi_{0},\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T)}
.

Proof.

Let
x(t)=S_{f}(t)\xi~(0\leq t\leq T)
. It holds that

\displaystyle|x(t)-\xi_{0}|

\displaystyle\leq\int^{t}_{0}|f(x(s),s)-f(\xi_{0},s)|ds+\int^{t}_{0}|f(\xi_{0},s)|ds\leq\text{\rm Lip}(f)\int^{t}_{0}|x(s)-\xi_{0}|ds+\int^{t}_{0}F_{0}ds

\displaystyle\leq\text{\rm Lip}(f)\int^{t}_{0}|x(s)-\xi_{0}|ds+F_{0}T.

By Gronwall’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle|x(t)-\xi_{0}|\leq F_{0}Te^{\text{\rm Lip}(f)t}\leq F_{0}Te^{\text{\rm Lip}(f)T}.

∎

By (
2.3
) and (
2.4
),
f(\cdot,t)
is continuous on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
for all
0\leq t\leq T
, and
f\in L^{\infty}(0,T;C^{0}(D))
.
By Lemma
4.2
, it holds that for all
\xi\in D
,

\displaystyle|S_{f}(t)\xi-\xi|\leq\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;C^{0}(D))}Te^{\text{\rm Lip}(f)T}.

Hence, if we define
E=\{\bar{\xi}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}~|\;|\bar{\xi}-\xi|\leq\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;C^{0}(D))}Te^{\text{\rm Lip}(f)T},\xi\in D\}
, then it holds that

\displaystyle S_{f}(t)\xi\in E\hskip 9.24994pt(\xi\in D,\;0\leq t\leq T).

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
f,g:\mathbb{R}^{N}\times[0,T]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{N}
satisfy (
2.3
) and (
2.4
). Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a bounded closed subset and satisfy that

\displaystyle E\supset\{S_{g}(t)\xi\;|\;\xi\in D,\;0\leq t\leq T\}.

Then, we have

\displaystyle\|S_{f}(t)-S_{g}(t)\|_{C^{0}(D)}\leq\|f-g\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;C^{0}(E))}Te^{\text{\rm Lip}(f)T}\hskip 9.24994pt(0\leq t\leq T).

Proof.

Let
x_{f}(t)=S_{f}(t)\xi
and
x_{g}(t)=S_{g}(t)\xi\;\left(0\leq t\leq T,\;\xi\in D\right)
. Then, it holds that for all
0\leq t\leq T
and
\xi\in D
,

\displaystyle|x_{f}(t)-x_{g}(t)|

\displaystyle=\left|\int^{t}_{0}f(x_{f}(s),s)-g(x_{g}(s),s)ds\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int^{t}_{0}|f(x_{f}(s),s)-f(x_{g}(s),s)|ds+\int^{t}_{0}|f(x_{g}(s),s)-g(x_{g}(s),s)|ds

\displaystyle|x_{f}(t)-x_{g}(t)|\leq\|f-g\|_{L^{1}(0,T;C^{0}(E))}e^{\text{\rm Lip}(f)t}\leq\|f-g\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;C^{0}(E))}Te^{\text{\rm Lip}(f)T},

which holds for all

and implies the conclusion.

Lemma 4.6
.

Assume that there exists

such that

We prove

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}S_{g}(t_{\ast})\bar{\xi}\in\partial B_{a}\\

\displaystyle|x(t)-\bar{x}(t)|

\displaystyle=\left|\xi+\int^{t}_{0}f(x(s))ds-\bar{\xi}-\int^{t}_{0}g(\bar{x}(s))ds\right|

Lemma 4.7
.

For

,
\beta\in L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{N\times N})
, and

, we define

\displaystyle\coloneqq\alpha_{\delta}(t)\odot\boldsymbol{\sigma}(\beta_{\delta}(t)x(t)+\gamma_{\delta}(t)).

\displaystyle\leq M_{1}\hskip 9.24994pt(\xi\in D,0\leq t\leq T),

\displaystyle|\boldsymbol{\sigma}(\beta_{\delta}(t)S_{h_{\delta}}(t)\xi+\gamma_{\delta}(t))|

First,

\displaystyle|\alpha_{\delta}(t)|

\displaystyle\leq|(\eta_{\delta}\ast\tilde{\alpha})(t)|\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}\eta_{\delta}(t-s)|\tilde{\alpha}(t)|ds\leq\|\alpha\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{N})}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\eta_{\delta}(\tau)d\tau=\|\alpha\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{N})}.

Similarly, we have
|\beta_{\delta}(t)|\leq\|\beta\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{N\times N})}
and
|\gamma_{\delta}(t)|\leq\|\gamma\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{N})}
. Let

be fixed and let

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} |x_{\delta}(t)-\xi|&=\left|\int^{t}_{0}\alpha_{\delta}(s)\odot\boldsymbol{\sigma}(\beta_{\delta}(t)x_{\delta}(s)+\gamma_{\delta}(s))ds\right|\leq\|\alpha\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{N})}\int^{t}_{0}|\boldsymbol{\sigma}(\beta_{\delta}(t)x_{\delta}(s)+\gamma_{\delta}(s))|ds\\
&\leq\|\alpha\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{N})}\int^{t}_{0}|\boldsymbol{\sigma}(\beta_{\delta}(t)x_{\delta}(s)+\gamma_{\delta}(s))-\boldsymbol{\sigma}(\beta_{\delta}(t)\xi+\gamma_{\delta}(s))|ds\\
&\hskip 9.24994pt~+\|\alpha\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{N})}\int^{t}_{0}|\boldsymbol{\sigma}(\beta_{\delta}(t)\xi+\gamma_{\delta}(s))|ds\\
&\leq\|\alpha\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{N})}\text{\rm Lip}(\sigma)\|\beta\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{N\times N})}\int^{t}_{0}|x_{\delta}(s)-\xi|ds\\
&\hskip 9.24994pt~+\|\alpha\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{N})}\int^{t}_{0}|\boldsymbol{\sigma}(\beta_{\delta}(t)\xi+\gamma_{\delta}(s))|ds.\end{aligned}

(4.6)

Therefore, it holds that

\displaystyle\|S_{h_{L}}(t)-S_{h}(t)\|_{C^{0}(D)}<\frac{\epsilon}{3}\hskip 9.24994pt(0\leq t\leq T).

Proof of Theorem
3.6
.

By Theorem
3.1
, there exist
\alpha,\gamma\in C^{0}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
\beta\in C^{0}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{N\times N})
such that

\displaystyle\|S_{f}(T)-S_{h}(T)\|_{C^{0}(D;\mathbb{R}^{N})}<\frac{\epsilon}{2}

where
h(x,t)=\alpha(t)\odot\boldsymbol{\sigma}(\beta(t)x+\gamma(t))

L\in\mathbb{N}

\alpha_{l}=\alpha(t_{l})
,
\beta_{l}=\beta(t_{l})
,
\gamma_{l}=\gamma(t_{l})
,

, and
z^{(l)}(\xi)\coloneqq x(t_{l};\xi)-x^{(l)}(\xi)
, where
x(t;\xi)=S_{h}(t)\xi
and
x^{(l)}=S^{res}_{\{\Delta th^{(l)}\}_{l=0}^{L-1}}(l)
. It holds that for all
l=0,1,\ldots,L-1,

\displaystyle|z^{(l+1)}(\xi)|

\displaystyle=\left|\left(x(t_{l};\xi)+\int^{t_{l+1}}_{t_{l}}h(x(s;\xi),s)ds\right)-\left(x^{(l)}(\xi)+\Delta th^{(l)}(x^{(l)}(\xi))\right)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|z^{(l)}(\xi)+\int^{t_{l+1}}_{t_{l}}\left(h(x(s;\xi),s)-h(x^{(l)}(\xi),t_{l})\right)ds\right|

\displaystyle\leq|z^{(l)}(\xi)|+\int^{t_{l+1}}_{t_{l}}|h(x(s;\xi),s)-h(x(t_{l};\xi),t_{l})|ds+\int^{t_{l+1}}_{t_{l}}|h(x(t_{l};\xi),t_{l})-h(x^{(l)}(\xi),t_{l})|ds.

2.6
), we obtain

\displaystyle|h(x(t_{l};\xi),t_{l})-h(x^{(l)}(\xi),t_{l})|

\displaystyle=|\alpha(t_{l})\odot\{\boldsymbol{\sigma}(\beta(t_{l})x(t_{l};\xi)+\gamma(t_{l}))-\boldsymbol{\sigma}(\beta(t_{l})x^{(l)}(\xi)+\gamma(t_{l}))\}|

\displaystyle\leq M_{\alpha}|\boldsymbol{\sigma}(\beta(t_{l})x(t_{l};\xi)+\gamma(t_{l}))-\boldsymbol{\sigma}(\beta(t_{l})x^{(l)}(\xi)+\gamma(t_{l}))|

\displaystyle\leq M_{\alpha}\text{\rm Lip}(\sigma)|(\beta(t_{l})x(t_{l};\xi)+\gamma(t_{l}))-(\beta(t_{l})x^{(l)}(\xi)+\gamma(t_{l}))|

\displaystyle\leq C|x(t_{l};\xi)-x^{(l)}(\xi)|=C|z^{(l)}(\xi)|,

where

M_{\beta}=\|\beta\|_{C^{0}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{N\times N})}
. By Lemma
5.1
, there exists
L\in\mathbb{N}

\displaystyle|h(x(s;\xi),s)-h(x(t_{l};\xi),t_{l})|<\epsilon^{\prime}\hskip 9.24994pt(t_{l}\leq s\leq t_{l+1},\;l=0,1,\ldots,L-1),

\displaystyle|z^{(l+1)}(\xi)|

\displaystyle|z^{(l)}(\xi)|

\displaystyle\leq(1+C\Delta t)^{l}|z^{(0)}|+\frac{(1+C\Delta t)^{l}\Delta t\epsilon^{\prime}}{C\Delta t}=\left(1+\frac{CT}{L}\right)^{L}\left\{|z^{(0)}|+\frac{\epsilon^{\prime}}{C}\right\}.

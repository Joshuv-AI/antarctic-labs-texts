Abstract
. We study some combinatorial statistics defined on the set N ​ C ( mton ) ​ ( n ) NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(n) of monotonically ordered non-crossing partitions of { 1 , … , n } \{1,\ldots,n\} , and on the set N ​ C 2 ( mton ) ​ ( 2 ​ n ) NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}_{2}(2n) of monotonically ordered non-crossing pair-partitions of { 1 , … , 2 ​ n } \{1,\ldots,2n\} . Unlike in the analogous results known for unordered non-crossing partitions, the computations of expectations and variances for natural block-counting statistics on N ​ C ( mton ) ​ ( n ) NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(n) and for the expectation of the area statistic on N ​ C 2 ( mton ) ​ ( 2 ​ n ) NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}_{2}(2n) turn out to yield a logarithmic regime. An important role in our study is played by a nice tree structure on the disjoint union of the N ​ C ( mton ) ​ ( n ) NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(n) ’s, which we use to streamline our arguments. As an illustration of how these ideas can be applied to calculations of cumulants in monotone probability, we discuss some combinatorial aspects of the monotonic Poisson process.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
NC(n)

and
NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(n)
.
The
NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}
-tree structure.

We will use the customary notation
NC(n)
for the set of non-crossing partitions
of
\{1,\ldots,n\}
. The elements of
NC(n)
are thus of the form

\pi=\{V_{1},\ldots,V_{k}\}
, where
V_{1},\ldots,V_{k}
(the
blocks
of the
partition
\pi
) are non-empty pairwise disjoint subsets of
\{1,\ldots,n\}
,
with
V_{1}\cup\cdots\cup V_{k}=\{1,\ldots,n\}
, and where it is not possible
to find
a_{1}<a_{2}<a_{3}<a_{4}
in
\{1,\ldots,n\}
such that

a_{1},a_{3}\in V_{i}
and
a_{2},a_{4}\in V_{j}
for some
i\neq j
. The
NC(n)
’s are one
of the many combinatorial structures counted by Catalan numbers. For a quick introduction
to some of their basic properties, one can e.g. consult
[
10
, Lecture 9]
.

This paper is concerned with sets of

monotonically ordered
non-crossing partitions.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
n
be a positive integer.

1^{o}
Let
\pi=\{V_{1},\ldots,V_{k}\}\in NC(n)
. One has a nesting relation
among the blocks of
\pi
, where “
V_{i}
nested inside
V_{j}
” means, by definition,
that
\min(V_{i})>\min(V_{j})
and
\max(V_{i})<\max(V_{j})
. We will
use the term
monotonic ordering of
\pi
to refer to a bijection

u:\pi\to\{1,\ldots,k\}
having the property that

\bigl[\mbox{$V_{i}$ nested inside $V_{j}$}\bigr]\,\Rightarrow\,\bigl[\,u(V_{i})>u(V_{j})\,\bigr].

2^{o}
We denote

NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(n):=\bigl\{(\pi,u)\mid\pi\in NC(n)\mbox{ and $u$ is a monotonic ordering of }\pi\bigr\}
.

Remark 1.2
.

For
(\pi,u)\in NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(n)
, one can think of
u
as “a possible history
of how
\pi
was created, via successive insertions of intervals”. In order to
clarify what this means, let us write explicitly
\pi=\{V_{1},\ldots,V_{k}\}

and let us note that the block
V_{j_{o}}:=u^{-1}(k)\in\pi
is an interval of

\{1,\ldots,n\}
.
(If not, then there would exist
i\neq j_{o}
in
\{1,\ldots,k\}
such that

V_{i}
is nested inside
V_{j_{o}}
, hence such that
u(V_{i})>u(V_{j_{o}})=k
,
in contradiction with
u(V_{i})\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
.) One can view the
insertion of the interval
V_{j_{o}}
as the last step in a
k
-step process which
created
\pi
, via successive insertions of intervals which eventually became the
blocks
u^{-1}(1),\ldots,u^{-1}(k)
, in this order.
For illustration, Figure 1 shows how this process works to create a monotonically
ordered partition with
4
blocks in
NC(9)
.

On a heuristic level, one could say that the relation between a
(\pi,u)\in NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(n)

and the underlying
\pi\in NC(n)
is akin to the relation one has, in the representation
theory of symmetric groups, between a standard Young tableau and the underlying Young diagram
– the tableau records a possible history of how the Young diagram was created, via addition
of boxes. From this perspective, the
NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}
-tree that we will next look at is an analogue
of the tree of monotonic paths of the well-known Young graph (as presented, for instance, in
Section 3.1 of the monograph
[
5
]
).

Remark 1.3
.

(The
NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}
-tree.)

In the considerations of the present paper, a crucial role will be played
by a tree structure with vertex set

NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}:=\sqcup_{n=1}^{\infty}NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(n)
(disjoint union), where the
unique element of
NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(1)
serves as root and where, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
the elements of
NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(n)
are at distance
n-1
from the root.
The formal description of the edges of this
NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}
-tree is given in
Section
2.1
below. Roughly speaking, it is based on the
interpretation of every
(\pi,u)\in NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(n)
as possible history for
how the underlying
\pi=\{V_{1},\ldots,V_{k}\}\in NC(n)
was created,
but where we amend the
k

Remark

, and make it become an
n

(use the number
n

number

\pi
).

Remark 1.4
.

(Homogeneity property of the
NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}
-tree.)

One has that:

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\mbox{for every $n\in\mathbb{N}$, every
$(\pi,u)\in NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(n)$}\\
\mbox{has precisely $n+2$ children in the $NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}$-tree.}\end{array}\right.

This is a fact that is easy to verify (cf. Remark
2.5
below),
but nevertheless has great benefits towards the study of block-counting
statistics on
NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(n)
, because it provides recursions satisfied by
such statistics. A number of results obtained in this way are described
in the next subsection.

We note that, as a byproduct, the homogeneity from (
1.1
) can be used
as an entry point towards some non-commutative probability considerations. It is
relevant to mention here that
NC(n)
and
NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(n)
play crucial roles in the
combinatorics of two brands of independence for non-commutative random variables,

free independence
and respectively
monotonic independence
. In particular,
each of these of two brands of independence uses a notion of
cumulants
for the
non-commutative random variables that are considered, where the cumulants of free
probability are defined (see e.g.
[
10
, Lecture 11]
) with the help of the

NC(n)
’s, while the cumulants of monotonic probability are defined (cf.
[
6
]
)
with the help of the
NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(n)
’s.
The homogeneity (
1.1
) can be used in order to obtain recursion formulas for
monotonic cumulants. The present paper does not aim to detail this direction,
but there is one immediate example that felt worth describing,
concerning the recursion for the monotonic cumulants of the so-called “monotonic Poisson
process” – cf. Section
5
below, where we succinctly review the monotonic
Poisson process, and use (
1.1
) to retrieve some results from
[
4
]
about
the cumulants of the said process.

1.2.
Block-counting statistics
on
NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(n)
.

For motivation, we mention that there exists a fair amount of research literature
(see e.g.
[
1
]
,
[
11
]
, or the more recent presentation made in

[
7
]
) which is
dedicated to the study of block-statistics on
NC(n)
, with
NC(n)
viewed as
a uniform probability space. The calculations made in this regime often yield
quantities that, asymptotically, grow linearly with
n
. For instance if

X_{n}:NC(n)\to\mathbb{N}
is the random variable that counts blocks,

X_{n}(\pi):=|\pi|=k
for
\pi=\{V_{1},\ldots,V_{k}\}\in NC(n)
, then
the expectation and variance of
X_{n}
are found to be

(1.2)

E[X_{n}]=\frac{n+1}{2}\ \mbox{ and }\ \mathrm{Var}[X_{n}]=\frac{n^{2}-1}{4(2n-1)}\sim\frac{n}{8}

(see e.g.
[
7
, Theorem 2.1]
). Another relevant result is that upon
fixing an
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
and considering the random variables

X_{n}^{(\ell)}:NC(n)\to\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
which are defined by

X_{n}^{(\ell)}(\pi):=\ \vline\,\{V\in\pi\mid\,|V|=\ell\}\ \vline\ ,\ \ \pi\in NC(n),

one gets that
E[X_{n}^{(\ell)}]\sim n/2^{\ell+1}
for
n\to\infty

(see e.g.
[
11
, Corollary 4.3]
).

In the present paper we look at the analogous block-counting random variables,
in the parallel world of
NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(n)
.

Remark and Notation 1.5
.

1^{o}
For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we view
NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(n)
as a uniform probability space,
where every
(\pi,u)\in NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(n)
has probability
1/|NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(n)|
.
The latter cardinality is
|NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(n)|=(n+1)!/2
,
as had been noted in the literature related to monotonic probability
(cf.
[
2
, Proposition 3.4]
), and can also be easily derived from the
homogeneity property (
1.1
) of the
NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}
-tree.

2^{o}
For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we let
Y_{n}:NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(n)\to\mathbb{N}
be the
random variable defined by

(1.3)

Y_{n}(\pi,u):=|\pi|\mbox{ (number of blocks of $\pi$),}\ \ (\pi,u)\in NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(n).

The next theorem gives the expectation and variance of
Y_{n}
. Unlike the formulas
(
1.2
) concerning the
X_{n}
’s, the formulas for the
Y_{n}
’s show a
logarithmic regime. In the statements of the latter formulas we will use the
standard notation

H_{n}:=1+\frac{1}{2}+\cdots+\frac{1}{n},\ \mbox{ ($n$-th harmonic number, for $n\in\mathbb{N}$),}

and
\gamma:=\lim_{n\to\infty}H_{n}-\ln n
(the Euler-Mascheroni constant).

Theorem 1.6
.

Let the random variables
Y_{n}:NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(n)\to\mathbb{N}
be
as defined in (
1.3
).

1^{o}
One has

E[\,Y_{n}\,]=n-H_{n}+\frac{3}{2}-\frac{1}{n+1},\ \ n\geq 2.

As a consequence, it follows that

(1.4)

E[\,Y_{n}\,]\approx(n-\ln n)+\bigl(\,\frac{3}{2}-\gamma\,\bigr)\mbox{ for }n\to\infty,

where the meaning of “
\approx
” in (
1.4
) is that the
difference of the quantities on its two sides goes to
0
for
n\to\infty
.

2^{o}
One has
\mathrm{Var}[Y_{n}]=H_{n}-H_{n}^{(2)}-\frac{(n-1)^{2}}{4(n+1)^{2}},\ \ n\geq 2,

where
H_{n}^{(2)}:=1+\frac{1}{2^{2}}+\cdots+\frac{1}{n^{2}}.

As a consequence, it follows that

(1.5)

\mathrm{Var}[Y_{n}]\approx\ln n-\bigl(\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}+\frac{1}{4}-\gamma\bigr),\mbox{ for $n\to\infty$}.

Suppose next that, for randomly chosen
(\pi,u)\in NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(n)
,
one tallies the blocks of
\pi
according to their cardinality. The next
theorem says that, for large
n
, one should expect
\pi
to have a lot
of blocks of size
1
(about
n-2\,\ln n
of them), a fairly significant
number of blocks of size
2
(about
\ln n
of them), and very few blocks of
size
\geq 3
.

Theorem 1.7
.

For every
n,\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, let
Y_{n}^{(\ell)}:NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(n)\to\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}

be defined by

(1.6)

Y_{n}^{(\ell)}(\pi,u):=\ \vline\,\{V\in\pi\mid\,|V|=\ell\}\ \vline\ ,\ \ (\pi,u)\in NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(n).

There exist constants
c_{1},c_{2},\ldots,c_{\ell},\ldots\in\mathbb{R}
such that:

(1.7)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\lim_{n\to\infty}E[Y_{n}^{(1)}]-(n-2\,\ln n)=c_{1},\ \lim_{n\to\infty}E[Y_{n}^{(2)}]-\ln n=c_{2},\\
\mathrm{and}\ \lim_{n\to\infty}E[Y_{n}^{(\ell)}]=c_{\ell},\ \ \forall\,\ell\geq 3.\end{array}\right.

Remark 1.8
.

1^{o}
Proposition
4.6
below gives the precise values

c_{1}=\frac{10}{3}-2\gamma
and
c_{2}=\gamma-\frac{17}{8}
.
The values of
c_{\ell}
for
\ell\geq 3
can also be explicitly computed,
but there doesn’t appear to be some nice general formula for them (see discussion
in Remark
4.7
.2 below).

2^{o}
In the setting of the preceding theorem, one can in fact bundle together all
the random variables
Y_{n}^{(\ell)}
with
\ell\geq 3
into

Y_{n}^{(\geq 3)}:NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(n)\to\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
, where

(1.8)

Y_{n}^{(\geq 3)}(\pi,u):=\ \vline\,\{V\in\pi\mid\,|V|\geq 3\}\ \vline\ ,\ \ (\pi,u)\in NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(n).

It is still the case that the expectations

E[\,Y_{n}^{(\geq 3)}\,]
have a finite limit for
n\to\infty
.
Indeed, by writing
Y_{n}^{(\geq 3)}=Y_{n}-Y_{n}^{(1)}-Y_{n}^{(2)}
,
with
Y_{n}
picked from Theorem
1.6
, one immediately finds that

\lim_{n\to\infty}E[\,Y_{n}^{(\geq 3)}\,]=(\frac{3}{2}-\gamma)-c_{1}-c_{2}=\frac{7}{24}
.

Remark 1.9
.

We reiterate the fact that the formulas announced in Theorems
1.6
and

1.7
are derived by starting from the homogeneity property (
1.1
)
of the
NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}
-tree. As an intermediate between the observation from
(
1.1
) and explicit formulas for expectations and variances, we will put
into evidence two kinds of recursions that may be satisfied by a sequence of random
variables
(Z_{n}:NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(n)\to\mathbb{R})_{n=1}^{\infty}
.

– The recursion of the first kind uses for input a tuple

\underline{r}=(r_{1},\ldots,r_{k})\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
, for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
. We will introduce it
in Section
2.2
, then in Section
4
we will show how
this kind of recursion is used to approach the random variables
Y_{n}
and

Y_{n}^{(\ell)}
from Theorems
1.6
and
1.7
(both these theorems
are consequences of the same general result about the recursion of the first kind,
given in Theorem
3.2
).

– The recursion of the second kind uses for input a triple

(\alpha,\beta;q)
, with
\alpha,\beta,q\in\mathbb{Z}
.
We will introduce it in Section
2.3
, and we will discuss
it further in Section
6
. This recursion of the second kind
can be applied to the statistics counting outer blocks of
\pi
, in
connection to a randomly picked
(\pi,u)\in NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(n)
. A block
V

of a partition
\pi\in NC(n)
is said to be
outer
when it
is not possible to find
W\in\pi
such that
V
is nested
inside
W
. If
Z_{n}:NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(n)\to\mathbb{N}
is defined by putting

Z_{n}(\pi,u):=\ \vline\,\{V\in\pi\mid\,V\mbox{ is outer}\}\ \vline\ ,

then (as we will see in Section
6
, by invoking the recursion of
the second kind) one has the neat formula

E[Z_{n}]=(2n+1)/3,\ \ \forall\,n\in\mathbb{N}.

1.3.
The

NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}_{2}
-tree,
and some block-counting statistics on

NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}_{2}(2n)
.

In this paper we will also look at
some combinatorial statistics on monotonically ordered non-crossing

pair-partitions
. For motivation we mention that
the sets of unordered non-crossing pair-partitions,

NC_{2}(2n):=\bigl\{\pi=\{V_{1},\ldots,V_{n}\}\in NC(2n)\ \vline\ \ |V_{1}|=\cdots=|V_{n}|=2\bigr\},

play an important role in the combinatorics of free probability,
primarily due to their occurrence in the free
Central Limit Theorem (see e.g.
[
10
, Lecture 8]
).
This carries through to monotonic probability, where the sets

(1.9)

NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}_{2}(2n):=\Bigl\{(\pi,u)\begin{array}[]{lr}\vline&\pi\in NC_{2}(2n)\mbox{ and }u:\pi\to\{1,\ldots,n\},\\
\vline&\mbox{monotonic ordering}\end{array}\Bigr\}

appear in the combinatorial description of the monotonic
Central Limit Theorem
[
9
,
6
]
.

Towards studying combinatorial statistics on the
NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}_{2}(2n)
’s, one
can pursue a methodology similar to the one described in
Remark
1.9
in connection to the
NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(n)
’s. This is because
the disjoint union
NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}_{2}:=\sqcup_{n=1}^{\infty}NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}_{2}(2n)
bears
a natural structure of rooted tree analogous to (and in fact easier to spot than)
the one of the
NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}
-tree of Remark
1.3
. The unique pairing in

NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}_{2}(2)
serves as root for the
NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}_{2}
-tree and, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
the elements of
NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}_{2}(2n)
are at distance
n-1
from the root. Moreover,
the
NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}_{2}
-tree has a homogeneity property analogous to the one recorded in
(
1.1
), which now goes like this:

(1.10)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\mbox{for every $n\in\mathbb{N}$, every
$(\pi,u)\in NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}_{2}(2n)$}\\
\mbox{has precisely $2n+1$ children in the $NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}_{2}$-tree.}\end{array}\right.

We note that, as an immediate consequence of (
1.10
), one has

(1.11)

|\,NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}_{2}(2n)\,|=(2n-1)!!,\ \ \forall\,n\in\mathbb{N}

(showing, in particular, how normalizations have to be done in calculations
with uniform random variables on
NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}_{2}(2n)
).

Two interesting block-counting statistics that can be considered on

NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}_{2}(2n)
are

(1.12)

\mathrm{Out}_{n}:NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}_{2}(2n)\to\mathbb{R}\ \mbox{ and }\ \mathrm{Int}_{n}:NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}_{2}(2n)\to\mathbb{R},

counting the
outer pairs
and respectively the

interval pairs
of
\pi
, for a random
(\pi,u)\in NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}_{2}(2n)
.
Such pairs represent the top and bottom of the natural nesting structure among
the pairs of
\pi
: a pair
V\in\pi
is outer when it is not nested inside
any other pair, and is an
interval pair
when it is of the form

V=\{m,m+1\}
for some
1\leq m\leq 2n-1
(which is equivalent to
requiring that no
U\in\pi
is nested inside
V
). In Section
7

of the paper we show how the recursion “of the second kind” from
Remark
1.9
can be adapted to the setting of the

NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}_{2}
-tree, and used to address the random variables
\mathrm{Out}_{n},\mathrm{Int}_{n}

from (
1.12
). In particular, one gets nice explicit formulas for
their expectations, as follows.

Theorem 1.10
.

Let
\mathrm{Int}_{n},\mathrm{Out}_{n}:NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}_{2}(2n)\to\mathbb{R}
be as defined above.

1^{o}
For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, one has that

E[\,\mathrm{Int}_{n}\,]=(2n+1)/3
.

2^{o}
One has

(1.13)

E[\,\mathrm{Out}_{n}\,]=\frac{2^{n}\cdot n!}{(2n-1)!!}-1,\ \ n\in\mathbb{N},

with the consequence that

E[\,\mathrm{Out}_{n}\,]\sim\sqrt{\pi n}
for
n\to\infty
.

1.4.
Another combinatorial statistic
on
NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}_{2}(2n)
: area.

An interesting statistic, specific to non-crossing pair-partitions, is the one
which measures the
area
under a
\pi\in NC_{2}(2n)
, when
\pi
is viewed
in its incarnation as a Dyck lattice path (see e.g.
[
10
, Lecture 2]
)
or, in calculus language, when
\pi
is identified to a piecewise linear function

f_{\pi}:[0,2n]\to[0,\infty)
. To be precise,
the function
f_{\pi}
is determined by the following requirements:

(1.14)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\mbox{$\to$ $f_{\pi}$ is continuous on $[0,2n]$,
and has $f_{\pi}(0)=0$;}\\
\mbox{$\to$ $f_{\pi}$ is linear on every interval
$[m-1,m]$, $1\leq m\leq 2n$;}\\
\mbox{$\to$ For every $V=\{i,j\}\in\pi$, with $i<j$, the slope
of $f_{\pi}\mid[i-1,i]$}\\
\mbox{ \hskip 8.5359pt is equal to $1$ and the slope of
$f_{\pi}\mid[j-1,j]$ is equal to $-1$.}\end{array}\right.

The recipe used to define
f_{\pi}
in (
1.14
) has the
immediate consequences that
f_{\pi}(t)\geq 0
for every
t\in[0,2n]

and that
f_{\pi}(2n)=0
. It is natural to put

\mathrm{area}(\pi):=\int_{0}^{2n}f_{\pi}(t)\,dt\in[0,\infty)

(where the latter quantity is easily seen, in fact, to always be a positive
integer).

f_{\pi}

looks like, for an example of pair-partition
\pi\in NC_{2}(14)
.

.

The expectation of the random variable

An interesting aspect of the formula

1.5.
Organization of the paper.

Besides the Introduction, the paper has 7 sections.

– Section
2
gives the precise description of the
NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}
-tree,
and of the two kinds of recursions mentioned in Remark
1.9
.

– Sections
3
and
4
deal with the recursion of the
first kind for sequences of random variables on the
NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(n)
’s. The main
point here is that, by relying on the
NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}
-tree structure, one can convert
the said kind of recursion into a recursion satisfied by a “combinatorial version”
of the Laplace transforms of the random variables under consideration. The precise
description of how this goes is given in Theorem
3.2
, which is then used in
Section
4
in order to prove the Theorems
1.6
and
1.7

stated above.

– In Section
5
we make a concise review of what are monotonic cumulants,
and we show how calculations from Section
4.1
can be viewed as giving
information on the so-called “monotonic Poisson process”.

– In Section
6
we return to the main line of the paper, and we pursue
a development analogous to the one from Sections
3
and
4
,
in connection to the
NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}
-recursion property of the second kind. The resulting
recursion for combinatorial Laplace transforms is stated in Theorem
6.1
,
and its application to the counting of outer blocks is in Corollary
6.3
.

– In Section
7
we switch to looking at sequences of random
variables on the
NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}_{2}(2n)
’s. We examine how the formulas obtained in
Section
6
about the recursion of the second kind can be adjusted
to the
NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}_{2}
-setting, and we use that in order to prove Theorem
1.10
.

– In the final Section
8
we discuss the “area” statistic for
monotonically ordered non-crossing pair-partitions, and we give the proof
of Theorem
1.11
.

2.
The
NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}
-tree and some recursions related to it

2.1.
Description of the
NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}
-tree.

In this subsection we show how
NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}:=\sqcup_{n=1}^{\infty}NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(n)
can be
organized as set of vertices of a rooted tree, in the way anticipated in
Remark
1.3
. In order to indicate the edges of this tree we will explain,
in Definition
2.2
, how one assigns a parent
\mathfrak{p}(\pi,u)\in NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(n-1)

to a
(\pi,u)\in NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(n)
for
n\geq 2
. First, some preliminary notation.

Notation 2.1
.

1^{o}
For every
(\pi,u)\in\sqcup_{n=1}^{\infty}NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(n)
, we will use the
notation “
J(\pi,u)
” for the block of
\pi
to which
u
assigns its maximal value,

u\bigl(\,J(\pi,u)\,\bigr)=|\pi|
.
As noticed in Remark
1.2
,
J
is sure to be an
interval-block
of
\pi
.

2^{o}
A
restrict-and-relabel
notation: for
\pi\in NC(n)
and

\emptyset\neq W\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}
, we let
\pi_{W}
denote
the partition of
\{1,\ldots,|W|\}
which is obtained by restricting

\pi
to
W
and then re-denoting the elements of
W
as
1,\ldots,|W|

in increasing order. It is immediate that the non-crossing property
is preserved by this operation, thus
\pi_{W}\in NC(\,|W|\,)
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
(\pi,u)\in NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(n)
, where
n\geq 2
. We will construct a

(\rho,v)\in NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(n-1)
, which will be called
the parent
of

(\pi,u)
and will be denoted as
\mathfrak{p}(\pi,u)
.
Towards that end, we consider the block
J(\pi,u)\in\pi
from Notation
2.1
.1,
and we let
m:=\max\bigl(\,J(\pi,u)\,\bigr)\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
.
The “
\rho
” part in the desired
(\rho,v)
is defined as

(2.1)

\rho:=\pi_{\{1,\ldots,n\}\setminus\{m\}}\in NC(n-1)

(using the “restrict-and-relabel” Notation
2.1
.2).
For the “
v
” part in
(\rho,v)
we start from use the monotonic ordering

u:\pi\to\{1,\ldots,|\pi|\}
, and we go as follows.

– Case 1:
|J(\pi,u)|>1
. In this case we have
|\rho|=|\pi|
, and
we can write
\rho=\{\phi(V)\mid V\in\pi\}
, where
\phi
is
the unique increasing bijection from

\{1,\ldots,n\}\setminus\{m\}
onto
\{1,\ldots,n-1\}
.
We define
v:\rho\to\{1,\ldots,|\rho|\}

by putting
v\bigl(\,\phi(V)\,\bigr):=u(V)
for every
V\in\pi
.

– Case 2:
|J(\pi,u)|=1
, and hence
J(\pi,u)=\{m\}
.
Here the set-difference
\{1,\ldots,n\}\setminus\{m\}

completely eliminates the block
J(\pi,u)
, and thus

|\rho|=|\pi|-1
. But we still use a procedure similar to the one
described in Case 1: for the same
\phi
as considered there, we now write

\rho
as
\bigl\{\phi(V)\mid V\in\pi,\,V\neq\{m\},\,\bigr\}
,
and we define the ordering
v:\rho\to\{1,\ldots,|\rho|\}

by putting
v\bigl(\,\phi(V)\,\bigr):=u(V)
for every

V\in\pi
,
V\neq\{m\}
.

Example 2.3
.

1^{o}
Say that
n=6
,

\pi=\bigl\{\,\{1,2,6\},\,\{3\},\,\{4,5\}\,\bigr\}
,
and we consider the monotonic ordering
u:\pi\to\{1,2,3\}
defined by

u\bigl(\,\{1,2,6\}\,\bigr)=1,\ u\bigl(\,\{3\}\,\bigr)=2,\ u\bigl(\,\{4,5\}\,\bigr)=3,

with
J(\pi,u)=\{4,5\}
and
m:=\max\bigl(\,J(\pi,u)\,\bigr)=5
.
Then
\mathfrak{p}(\pi,u)=(\rho,v)\in NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(5)
, where

\rho=\pi_{\{1,\ldots,6\}\setminus\{5\}}=\bigl\{\,\{1,2,5\},\,\{3\},\,\{4\}\,\bigr\}\in NC(5),

and the ordering
v
of the blocks of
\rho
is:

v\bigl(\,\{1,2,5\}\,\bigr)=1,\ v\bigl(\,\{3\}\,\bigr)=2,\ v\bigl(\,\{4\}\,\bigr)=3.

2^{o}
Suppose we look at the same
\pi\in NC(6)
as in
1^{o}
above,
but considered now with the monotonic ordering
u^{\prime}:\pi\to\{1,2,3\}

defined by

u^{\prime}\bigl(\,\{1,2,6\}\,\bigr)=1,\ u^{\prime}\bigl(\,\{4,5\}\,\bigr)=2,\ u^{\prime}\bigl(\,\{3\}\,\bigr)=3,

with
J(\pi,u^{\prime})=\{3\}
and

m^{\prime}:=\max\bigl(\,J(\pi,u^{\prime})\,\bigr)=3
.
Then
\mathfrak{p}(\pi,u^{\prime})=(\rho^{\prime},v^{\prime})\in NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(5)
, where

\rho^{\prime}=\pi_{\{1,\ldots,6\}\setminus\{3\}}=\bigl\{\,\{1,2,5\},\,\{3,4\}\,\bigr\}\in NC(5),

and the ordering
v^{\prime}
of the blocks of
\rho^{\prime}
is:

v^{\prime}\bigl(\,\{1,2,5\}\,\bigr)=1,\ v^{\prime}\bigl(\,\{3,4\}\,\bigr)=2.

Notation 2.4
.

For
n\geq 2
in
\mathbb{N}
and
(\rho,v)\in NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(n-1)
we will denote

(2.2)

C(\rho,v):=\{(\pi,u)\mid\mathfrak{p}(\pi,u)=(\rho,v)\}\subseteq NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(n);

that is,
C(\rho,v)
is the
set of children
of
(\rho,v)

in the
NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}
-tree.

Remark 2.5
.

(Homogeneity property of the
NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}
-tree.)

Let
n\geq 2
be in
\mathbb{N}
and let
(\rho,v)\in NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(n-1)
.
Then
|C(\rho,v)|=n+1
. More precisely, one can write

(2.3)

C(\rho,v)=\bigl\{(\pi_{1},u_{1}),\ldots,(\pi_{n},u_{n}),(\pi_{n+1},u_{n+1})\bigr\},

where:

– For every
1\leq m\leq n
, the partition
\pi_{m}\in NC(n)
is obtained by
inserting a singleton block in the picture of
\rho
, with the added singleton
placed on position
m
, and where this singleton block
\{m\}
gets
to have maximal label in the monotonic ordering
u_{m}
of the blocks of
\pi_{m}

– that is, one has
J(\pi_{m},u_{m})=\{m\}
.

– The partition
\pi_{n+1}
is obtained by considering the interval-block

J(\rho,v)=\{p,\ldots,q\}
with maximal
v
-label,
and by “elongating”
\{p,\ldots,q\}
to become the block with maximal label
in the monotonic ordering
u_{n+1}
of
\pi_{n+1}
– that is, one has

(2.4)

J(\pi_{n+1},u_{n+1})=\{p,\ldots,q,q+1\}\in\pi_{n+1}.

2.2.
The
NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}
-recursion of the first kind.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
(Z_{n}:NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(n)\to\mathbb{R})_{n=1}^{\infty}
be real random variables, let

k\in\mathbb{N}
, and let
\underline{r}=(r_{1},\ldots,r_{k})\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
. We will say
that the sequence of
Z_{n}
’s is
recursive of the first kind

with input
\underline{r}
to mean that the following condition is fulfilled:

(2.5)

\begin{array}[]{lll}\vline&\mbox{For every $n\geq 2$ and $(\pi,u)\in NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(n)$,}&\vline\\
\vline&\mbox{with $\mathfrak{p}(\pi,u)=(\rho,v)\in NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(n-1)$, one has:}&\vline\\
\vline&&\vline\\
\vline&\begin{array}[]{cc}\mbox{$\ $}\ Z_{n}(\pi,u)=Z_{n-1}(\rho,v)+r_{1},&\mbox{ if $|J(\pi,u)|=1$,}\\
\mbox{$\ $}\ \cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots&\cdots\cdots\cdots\\
\mbox{$\ $}\ Z_{n}(\pi,u)=Z_{n-1}(\rho,v)+r_{k},&\mbox{ if $|J(\pi,u)|=k$,}\\
\mbox{$\ $}&\\
\mbox{and }Z_{n}(\pi,u)=Z_{n-1}(\rho,v),&\mbox{ if $|J(\pi,u)|>k$.}\end{array}&\vline\end{array}

Remark 2.7
.

1^{o}
The way to think about the recursive condition stated in (
2.5
)
is that we generally want to have

Z_{n}(\pi,u)=Z_{n-1}\bigl(\,\mathfrak{p}(\pi,u)\,\bigr)
,
but we allow exceptions when the special block
J(\pi,u)\in\pi

has small size – namely, if
|J(\pi,u)|=j\leq k
then

Z_{n}(\pi,u)=Z_{n-1}\bigl(\,\mathfrak{p}(\pi,u)\,\bigr)+r_{j}
.

2^{o}
In the setting of Definition
2.6
, it is immediate
that if the input tuple
\underline{r}
is given and if
Z_{1}
is given, then all the

Z_{n}
’s are uniquely determined. However,
the way we would like to use this definition goes, in some sense, in reverse:
given a sequence of
Z_{n}
’s which we deem to be of interest, we want to see if
it wouldn’t be possible to fit a
k
and a

\underline{r}=(r_{1},\ldots,r_{k})
such that the
Z_{n}
’s are recursive of the
first kind with input
\underline{r}
.
This turns out to work for a number of natural examples of block-counting random
variables, as shown below.

Note that in the above mentioned process of fitting an input vector

\underline{r}=(r_{1},\ldots,r_{k})
, there is some freedom in the choice of
k
:
we can always replace
k
with an
\ell>k
, and replace the input tuple

\underline{r}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
with
(r_{1},\ldots,r_{k},0,\ldots 0)\in\mathbb{R}^{\ell}
.

Example 2.8
.

The block-counting random variables
Y_{n}
introduced in Notation
1.5

satisfy the condition (
2.5
), with
k=1
and
\underline{r}=(1)\in\mathbb{R}^{1}
.
This amounts to the fact that, for every
n\geq 2
and
(\pi,u)\in NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(n)

with parent
\mathfrak{p}(\pi,u)=:(\rho,v)\in NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(n-1)
, one has:

(2.6)

|\pi|=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}|\rho|+1,&\mbox{if $|J(\pi,u)|=1$,}\\
|\rho|,&\mbox{if $|J(\pi,u)|>1$.}\end{array}\right.

For the verification of (
2.6
), we check the two possible cases: if

|J(\pi,u)|=1
then
\pi
was obtained by appending a singleton block to
\rho
,
and thus
|\pi|=|\rho|+1
; while if
|J(\pi,u)|\geq 2
then
\pi
was
obtained by elongating the block
J(\rho,v)
of
\rho
, and thus
|\pi|=|\rho|
.

Example 2.9
.

Let us fix an
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
and consider the random variables

\bigl(\,Y_{n}^{(\ell)}\,\bigr)_{n=1}^{\infty}
which were introduced in
Equation (
1.6
) of Theorem
1.7
, counting blocks of cardinality
\ell
.
We claim that the
Y_{n}^{(\ell)}
’s satisfy the condition (
2.5
), with

k=\ell+1
and
\underline{r}=(0,\ldots,0,1,-1)\in\mathbb{R}^{\ell+1}
. This amounts
to the fact that for
n\geq 2
and
(\pi,u)\in NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(n)

with parent
\mathfrak{p}(\pi,u)=:(\rho,v)\in NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(n-1)
, one has:

(2.7)

Y_{n}^{(\ell)}(\pi,u)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}Y_{n}^{(\ell)}(\rho,v)+1,&\mbox{if $|J(\pi,u)|=\ell$,}\\
Y_{n}^{(\ell)}(\rho,v)-1,&\mbox{if $|J(\pi,u)|=\ell+1$,}\\
Y_{n}^{(\ell)}(\rho,v),&\mbox{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

For the verification of (
2.7
), we check the three possible cases listed there.
We will do the verification under the assumption that
\ell\geq 2
(and leave it as
exercise to the reader to supply the slight adjustments needed for
\ell=1
).

Case 1:

|J(\pi,u)|=\ell
. Since
\ell\geq 2
, it must be that
J(\pi,u)

was obtained by elongating the block
J(\rho,v)
of
\rho
, which had

|J(\rho,v)|=\ell-1
. So
\pi
indeed has one more block of cardinality
\ell

than
\rho
did – this is precisely the block
J(\pi,u)
.

Case 2:

|J(\pi,u)|=\ell+1
. This is similar to Case 1, but
where we now have that
|J(\rho,v)|=\ell\neq|J(\pi,u)|\neq\ell
;
hence in this case
\pi
has one less block of cardinality
\ell
than
\rho
did.

Case 3:

|J(\pi,u)|\not\in\{\ell,\ell+1\}
. In this case, there are no blocks
of cardinality
\ell
that would either appear or disappear in the process of passing
from
(\rho,v)
to
(\pi,u)
, hence
\pi
has the same number of blocks of cardinality

\ell
as
\rho
(as claimed in (
2.7
)).

Example 2.10
.

Consider the random variables

\bigl(\,Y_{n}^{(\geq 3)}\,\bigr)_{n=1}^{\infty}
which were introduced in
Equation (
1.8
) of Remark
1.8
, counting all the blocks which do not have
cardinality
1
or
2
. The
Y_{n}^{(\geq 3)}
’s satisfy the condition (
2.5
),
with
k=3
and
\underline{r}=(0,0,1)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
. The verification of this claim is similar to
the one shown in the preceding example, and is left to the reader.

2.3.
The
NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}
-recursion of the second kind.

Definition 2.11
.

Let
\alpha,\beta,q\in\mathbb{Z}
be given. We will say that a sequence of
random variables
(Z_{n}:NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(n)\to\mathbb{Z})_{n=1}^{\infty}
is

recursive of the second kind
with input
(\alpha,\beta;q)
when it has
the following property: for every
n\geq 2
and
(\rho,v)\in NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(n-1)
,
the set
C(\rho,v)
of children of
(\rho,v)

has a subset
C_{o}(\rho,v)
such that:

(2.8)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathrm{(1)}&\mbox{If $(\pi,u)\in C_{o}(\rho,v)$, then
$Z_{n}(\pi,u)=Z_{n-1}(\rho,v)+\alpha$;}\\
\mathrm{(2)}&\mbox{if $(\pi,u)\in C(\rho,v)\setminus C_{o}(\rho,v)$, then
$Z_{n}(\pi,u)=Z_{n-1}(\rho,v)+\beta$;}\\
\mbox{$\ $}\mathrm{and}&\\
\mathrm{(3)}&\mbox{$|C_{o}(\rho,v)|=Z_{n-1}(\rho,v)+q$.}\end{array}\right.

Example 2.12
.

For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\mathrm{Out}_{n}:NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(n)\to\mathbb{N}
be defined by

(2.9)

\mathrm{Out}_{n}(\pi,u):=\ \vline\,\{V\in\pi\mid\,V\mbox{ is outer}\}\ \vline\ ,\ \ (\pi,u)\in NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(n),

where recall that a block
V\in\pi
is said to be
outer

when there is no
W\in\pi
with
\min(W)<\min(V)
and
\max(W)>\max(V)
.
We will verify that the sequence of random variables
(\mathrm{Out}_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}

is recursive of the second kind, with parameters
\alpha=1
,
\beta=0
and
q=1
.
Towards that end, we fix an
n\geq 2
and a
(\rho,v)\in NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(n-1)
,
and we consider the set
C(\rho,v)
of children of
(\rho,v)
in the

NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}
-tree. We want to find a a subset
C_{o}(\rho,v)\subseteq C(\rho,v)

such that the requirements from (
2.8
) are fulfilled, i.e. such that:

(2.10)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathrm{(1)}&\mbox{If $(\pi,u)\in C_{o}(\rho,v)$, then
$\mathrm{Out}_{n}(\pi,u)=\mathrm{Out}_{n-1}(\rho,v)+1$;}\\
\mathrm{(2)}&\mbox{if $(\pi,u)\in C(\rho,v)\setminus C_{o}(\rho,v)$, then
$\mathrm{Out}_{n}(\pi,u)=\mathrm{Out}_{n-1}(\rho,v)$;}\\
\mbox{$\ $}\mathrm{and}&\\
\mathrm{(3)}&\mbox{$|C_{o}(\rho,v)|=\mathrm{Out}_{n-1}(\rho,v)+1$.}\end{array}\right.

In order to find
C_{o}(\rho,v)
, we resort to the explicit description of

C(\rho,v)
, as given in Equation (
2.3
) of Remark
2.5
:

C(\rho,v)=\{(\pi_{1},u_{1}),\ldots,(\pi_{n},u_{n}),(\pi_{n+1},u_{n+1})\},

where for
1\leq m\leq n
we have
J(\pi_{m},u_{m})=\{m\}
, while for
m=n+1
we
get the block
J(\pi_{n+1},u_{n+1})
of
\pi_{n+1}
by elongating (that is, by
appending one more element to) the interval-block
J(\rho,v)
of
\rho
.

Now, by using the non-crossing property, it is easy to see that the outer blocks of
\rho

can be organized in a list
W_{1},\ldots,W_{s}
such that

\min(W_{1})=1,\ \min(W_{2})=\max(W_{1})+1,\ldots,\min(W_{s})=\max(W_{s-1})+1,\ \max(W_{s})=n-1.

(These blocks form, in some sense, an “outer frame” for
\rho
.)
Referring to the explicit description of
C(\rho,v)
reviewed above,
we observe that for
1\leq m\leq n+1
we have:

\mathrm{Out}_{n}(\pi_{m},u_{m})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathrm{Out}_{n-1}(\rho,v)+1,&\mbox{ if }m\in\{\min(W_{1}),\ldots,\min(W_{s}),n\},\\
\mathrm{Out}_{n-1}(\rho,v),&\mbox{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

This is because, when passing from
\rho
to a
\pi_{m}
, the outer blocks of

\rho
always become outer blocks of
\pi_{m}
, and there is one possible additional outer
block of
\pi_{m}
showing up – this occurs when
1\leq m\leq n
and the singleton block added
to
\rho
in order to obtain
\pi_{m}
is placed “in between” two consecutive outer blocks

W_{i-1}
and
W_{i}
of
\rho
(also allowing here the possibility that the new singleton
block is placed to the left of
W_{1}
or to the right of
W_{s}
).

So then, the subset
C_{o}(\rho,v)\subseteq C(\rho,v)
defined as

C_{o}(\rho,v):=\Bigl\{\pi_{m}\mid m\in\{\min(W_{1}),\ldots,\min(W_{s}),n\}\,\Bigr\}

satisfies the requirements (1) and (2) in (
2.10
). Finally, we observe that
this choice of
C_{o}(\rho,v)
also fulfills the requirement (3) in (
2.10
),
since
|C_{o}(\rho,v)|=s+1=\mathrm{Out}_{n-1}(\rho,v)+1
.

3.
Laplace transforms for the

NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}
-recursion of the first kind

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and let
Z_{n}:NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(n)\to\mathbb{R}
be a random variable, considered
with respect to uniform distribution on
NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(n)
.
The Laplace transform of
Z_{n}
is the complex function

\lambda\mapsto E[e^{\lambda Z_{n}}]=\frac{1}{|NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(n)|}\sum_{(\pi,u)\in NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(n)}e^{\lambda Z_{n}(\pi,u)},\ \ \lambda\in\mathbb{C}.

For the purpose of this paper it is convenient that in the latter equation
we restrict
\lambda
to
\mathbb{R}
, put
t=e^{\lambda}\in(0,\infty)
,
and set aside the normalization constant
|NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(n)|
. This leads
to the following version of the Laplace transform, which is defined on
(0,\infty)

and, in our examples of interest where
Z_{n}
takes
values in
\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
, is a polynomial function.

Definition and Remark 3.1
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and let
Z_{n}:NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(n)\to\mathbb{R}
be a random variable, considered
with respect to uniform distribution on
NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(n)
. We will use the term

combinatorial Laplace transform of
Z_{n}
for the function

L_{n}:(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
defined by

(3.1)

L_{n}(t)=\sum_{(\pi,u)\in NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(n)}t^{Z_{n}(\pi,u)},\ \ t>0.

We note that
L_{n}
contains significant information about the random variable
Z_{n}
;
in particular, direct calculation shows that the expectation and variance of
Z_{n}
are
retrieved as

(3.2)

E[Z_{n}]=\frac{L_{n}^{\prime}(1)}{L_{n}(1)}\ \mbox{ and }\mathrm{Var}[Z_{n}]=E[Z_{n}^{2}]-\bigl(\,E[Z_{n}]\,\bigr)^{2}=\frac{L_{n}^{\prime\prime}(1)+L_{n}^{\prime}(1)}{L_{n}(1)}-\Bigl(\,\frac{L_{n}^{\prime}(1)}{L_{n}(1)}\,\Bigr)^{2},

where the
L_{n}(1)
in the denominators keeps track of normalizations,

L_{n}(1)=|NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(n)|=(n+1)!/2
.

The next theorem says that if a sequence of random variables

(Z_{n}:NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(n)\to\mathbb{R})_{n=1}^{\infty}
is recursive of the first kind (in the sense
of Definition
2.6
), then the combinatorial
Laplace transforms
L(Z_{n})
obey a tractable recursion formula.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
(Z_{n}:NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(n)\to\mathbb{R})_{n=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence of real random
variables which is recursive of the first kind with input

\underline{r}=(r_{1},\ldots,r_{k})\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
, where
k\geq 2
.
For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
L_{n}:(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
be defined as in
Equation (
3.1
). Then one has:

(3.3)

L_{n}(t)=(1+nt^{r_{1}})L_{n-1}(t)+\sum_{j=2}^{k}(n-j+1)\,(t^{r_{j}}-1)\,t^{r_{1}+\cdots+r_{j-1}}\,L_{n-j}(t),

holding for
n>k
in
\mathbb{N}
and
t\in(0,\infty)
.

Towards the proof of Theorem
3.2
, we first prove two lemmas.

Lemma 3.3
.

Consider the framework and notation of Theorem
3.2
. Let

\ell\in\{2,\ldots,k\}
, let
m
be an integer such that
m\geq\ell
,
and let
\mathfrak{p}^{(\ell-1)}:NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(m)\to NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(m-\ell+1)
be the
map obtained by iterating
\ell-1
times the parent map
\mathfrak{p}
of the

NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}
-tree.

1^{o}
Let
(\rho,v)\in NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(m)
be such that

|J(\rho,v)|=\ell
, and let
(\sigma,w):=\mathfrak{p}^{(\ell-1)}(\rho,v)\in NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(m-\ell+1)
.
One has that

(3.4)

Z_{m}(\rho,v)=Z_{m-\ell+1}(\sigma,w)+(r_{2}+\cdots+r_{\ell}).

2^{o}
The map
\mathfrak{p}^{(\ell-1)}
establishes a bijection between

\{(\rho,v)\in NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(m)\mid\ |J(\rho,v)|=\ell\}
and

\{(\sigma,w)\in NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(m-\ell+1)\mid\ |J(\sigma,w)|=1\}
.

Proof.

1^{o}
Let us write explicitly how the
\ell-1
iterations
of the parent map apply to
(\rho,v)
, in order to reach the partition

(\sigma,w)
. We thus denote:

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vline&(\rho_{0},v_{0}):=(\rho,v),\ \ (\rho_{1},v_{1}):=\mathfrak{p}(\rho_{0},v_{0})\in NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(m-1),\\
\vline&\\
\vline&(\rho_{2},v_{2}):=\mathfrak{p}(\rho_{1},v_{1})\in NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(m-2),\ldots,(\rho_{\ell-1},v_{\ell-1}):=\mathfrak{p}(\rho_{\ell-2},v_{\ell-2}),\\
\vline&\\
\vline&\mbox{$\ $ to the effect that }(\sigma,w)=(\rho_{\ell-1},v_{\ell-1})\in NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(m-\ell+1).\end{array}

From the definition of the parent map we infer that for every

1\leq i\leq\ell-1
we have

J(\rho_{i},v_{i})\subseteq J(\rho_{i-1},v_{i-1}),\mbox{ with }|J(\rho_{i},v_{i})|\,=\,|J(\rho_{i-1},v_{i-1})|-1;

this implies that

(3.5)

|J(\rho_{i},v_{i})|=\ell-i,\ \ \forall\,0\leq i\leq\ell-1.

The latter information about the cardinalities
|J(\rho_{i},v_{i})|

can be plugged into the recursive condition (
2.5
) satisfied by the

Z_{n}
’s, in order to get that:

(3.6)

\begin{array}[]{llcc}\vline&Z_{m}(\rho_{0},v_{0})&=&Z_{m-1}(\rho_{1},v_{1})+r_{\ell},\\
\vline&Z_{m-1}(\rho_{1},v_{1})&=&Z_{m-2}(\rho_{2},v_{2})+r_{\ell-1},\\
\vline&\cdots\cdots\mbox{$\ $}\cdots\cdots\mbox{$\ $}\cdots\cdots&\cdots&\cdots\cdots\mbox{$\ $}\cdots\cdots\mbox{$\ $}\cdots\cdots\\
\vline&Z_{m-\ell+2}(\rho_{\ell-2},v_{\ell-2})&=&Z_{m-\ell+1}(\rho_{\ell-1},v_{\ell-1})+r_{2}.\end{array}

Adding together all the equations listed in (
3.6
) then leads to

Z_{m}(\rho_{0},v_{0})=Z_{m-\ell+1}(\rho_{\ell-1},v_{\ell-1})+(r_{2}+\cdots+r_{\ell}),

which is precisely the required formula (
3.4
).

2^{o}
Let
(\rho,v)\in NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(m)
be such that
|J(\rho,v)|=\ell
, and
let
(\sigma,w):=\mathfrak{p}^{(\ell-1)}(\rho,v)\in NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(m-\ell+1)
.
Upon reviewing the argument that proved
1^{o}
above, we see that
the Equation (
3.5
) found there gives in particular

|J(\sigma,w)|\,=\,|J(\rho_{\ell-1},v_{\ell-1})|=\ell-(\ell-1)=1.

Thus the map
\mathfrak{p}^{(\ell-1)}
does indeed send the set

\{(\rho,v)\in NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(m)\mid\ |J(\rho,v)|=\ell\}
into

\{(\sigma,w)\in NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(m-\ell+1)\mid\ |J(\sigma,w)|=1\}
.

On the other hand, let us consider the map

\begin{array}[]{rcl}Q^{(\ell-1)}:\{(\sigma,w)\in NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(m-\ell+1)\mid\ |J(\sigma,w)|=1\}&\longrightarrow&NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(m),\\
(\sigma,w)&\mapsto&(\rho,v),\end{array}

where
(\rho,v)
is obtained by elongating the singleton block
J(\sigma,w)

to an interval block of cardinality
\ell
, and by keeping the ordering of blocks
(thus the elongated block of size
\ell
is the one which becomes
J(\rho,v)
).
The reader should have no difficulty
in checking that the map
Q^{(\ell-1)}
so defined is an inverse for the
map from
\{(\rho,v)\in NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(m)\mid\ |J(\rho,v)|=\ell\}
to

\{(\sigma,w)\in NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(m-\ell+1)\mid\ |J(\sigma,w)|=1\}
which
is induced by
\mathfrak{p}^{(\ell-1)}
.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Consider the framework and notation of Theorem
3.2
. For every integer

m\geq 2
and
t\in(0,\infty)
, one has that

(3.7)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(\rho,v)\in NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(m)\\
\mathrm{with}\ |J(\rho,v)|=1\end{subarray}}t^{Z_{m}(\rho,v)}=m\,t^{r_{1}}\,L_{m-1}(t).

Proof.

On the left-hand side of (
3.7
)
we group the terms according to what is the parent of
(\rho,v)

in the
NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}
-tree, and this converts the quantity indicated there into:

(3.8)

\sum_{(\sigma,w)\in NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(m-1)}\Bigl[\,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(\rho,v)\in NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(m)\\
\mathrm{with}\ \mathfrak{p}(\rho,v)=(\sigma,w)\\
\mathrm{and\ with}\ |J(\rho,v)|=1\end{subarray}}t^{Z_{m}(\rho,v)}\,\Bigr].

Let us now focus on an ordered partition

(\sigma,w)\in NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(m-1)
, for which we examine the inner sum
corresponding to
(\sigma,w)
in (
3.8
). In view of the
explicit description of the children of
(\sigma,w)
in the
NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}
-tree
observed in Remark
2.5
, we can list

\bigl\{(\rho,v)\in NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(m)\mid\mathfrak{p}(\rho,v)=(\sigma,w)\ \mathrm{and}\ |J(\rho,v)|=1\bigr\}=\{(\rho_{1},v_{1}),\ldots,(\rho_{m},v_{m})\},

where, for every
1\leq i\leq m
,

(\rho_{i},v_{i})
is the unique ordered partition in
NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(m)
such that

\mathfrak{p}(\rho_{i},v_{i})=(\sigma,w)
and
J(\rho_{i},v_{i})=\{i\}
.
Since the recursive condition (
2.5
) satisfied by the

Z_{n}
’s assures us that

Z_{m}(\rho_{i},v_{i})=Z_{m-1}(\sigma,w)+r_{1}
,

1\leq i\leq m
we infer that

(3.9)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(\rho,v)\in NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(m)\\
\mathrm{with}\ \mathfrak{p}(\rho,v)=(\sigma,w)\\
\mathrm{and\ with}\ |J(\rho,v)|=1\end{subarray}}t^{Z_{m}(\rho,v)}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}t^{Z_{m}(\rho_{i},v_{i})}=m\,t^{Z_{m-1}(\sigma,w)+r_{1}}.

Equation (
3.9
) holds for every
(\sigma,w)\in NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(m-1)
.
This allows us to plug the right-hand side of (
3.9
) as inner sum in
(
3.8
), in order to conclude that

\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\mbox{quantity}\\
\mbox{from (\ref{eqn:34b})}\end{array}\right)=\sum_{(\sigma,w)\in NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(m-1)}\Bigl[\,m\,t^{r_{1}}\,t^{Z_{m-1}(\sigma,w)}\,\Bigr]=m\,t^{r_{1}}\,L_{m-1}(t),

as claimed in (
3.7
).
∎

3.5
.

Proof of Theorem
3.2
.

We fix for the proof an
n>k
in
\mathbb{N}
and a
t\in(0,\infty)
for which
we will verify that (
3.3
) holds.
In the sum over
(\pi,u)\in NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(n)
which defines
L_{n}(t)
, we
organize the terms according to what is the parent of
(\pi,u)
in the

NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}
-tree:

(3.10)

L_{n}(t)=\sum_{(\rho,v)\in NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(n-1)}\Bigl[\,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(\pi,u)\in NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(n)\\
\mathrm{with}\ \mathfrak{p}(\pi,u)=(\rho,v)\end{subarray}}t^{Z_{n}(\pi,u)}\,\Bigr].

Let us fix for the moment an ordered partition
(\rho,v)\in NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(n-1)
, for which
we examine the inner sum corresponding to
(\rho,v)
in (
3.10
).
This sum is indexed by the set

C(\rho,v)=\{(\pi_{1},u_{1}),\ldots,(\pi_{n+1},u_{n+1})\}

of children of
(\rho,v)
in the
NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}
-tree,
with the
(\pi_{m},u_{m})
’s explicitly described in the paragraph following to
Equation (
2.3
) of Remark
2.5
. By invoking the recursive
property (
2.5
) in connection to the explicit description of
C(\rho,v)

reviewed above, we find that for
1\leq m\leq n

Z_{n}(\pi_{m},u_{m})=Z_{n-1}(\rho,v)+r_{1}

(since
(\pi_{m},u_{m})
is obtained by inserting a singleton block in
(\rho,v)

The remaining value
Z_{n}(\pi_{n+1},u_{n+1})

Z_{n-1}(\rho,v)
in
a way which depends on what is the cardinality of the block

J(\pi_{n+1},u_{n+1})\in\pi_{n+1}

The latter cardinality is just
1+|J(\rho,v)|

(since
(\pi_{n+1},u_{n+1})

in order to make it become

2.5

thus gives the following possibilities
(*)
and
(**)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(*)&\mbox{if }1+|J(\rho,v)|>k,\mbox{ then }Z_{n}(\pi_{n+1},u_{n+1})=Z_{n-1}(\rho,v);\\

(**)&\mbox{if }1+|J(\rho,v)|=j\in\{2,\ldots,k\},\mbox{ then }Z_{n}(\pi_{n+1},u_{n+1})=Z_{n-1}(\rho,v)+r_{j}.\end{array}\right.

Returning to the evaluation of the inner sum which corresponds to

(\rho,v)
in (

), we see that this sum is

\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{n+1}t^{Z_{n}(\pi_{m},u_{m})}

equal to

We now unfix the
(\rho,v)
that was considered throughout the
preceding paragraph, and plug the result of that discussion into the sum over

(\rho,v)\in NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(n-1)
appearing in (
3.10
). Then Equation (
3.10
)
takes the following form:

(3.11)

L_{n}(t)=\sum_{(\rho,v)\in NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(n-1)}\Bigl[\,n\,t^{Z_{n-1}(\rho,v)+r_{1}}+t^{Z_{n-1}(\rho,v)}\,\Bigr]

+\sum_{j=2}^{k}\,\Bigl[\,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(\rho,v)\in NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(n-1)\\
\mathrm{with}\ |J(\rho,v)|=j-1\end{subarray}}t^{Z_{n-1}(\rho,v)+r_{j}}-t^{Z_{n-1}(\rho,v)}\,\Bigr].

From here we continue as follows.

Claim 1.
The first sum listed in (
3.11
) is equal to

(n\,t^{r_{1}}+1)\,L_{n-1}(t).

Verification of Claim 1.
The sum in question is

\sum_{(\rho,v)\in NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(n-1)}(n\,t^{r_{1}}+1)t^{Z_{n-1}(\rho,v)},

and, in view of how
L_{n-1}
is defined, this is indeed

(n\,t^{r_{1}}+1)\,L_{n-1}(t)
.

Claim 2.
The second sum listed in (
3.11
) is equal to

\sum_{j=2}^{k}\,(n-j+1)\,(t^{r_{j}}-1)\,t^{r_{1}+\cdots+r_{j-1}}\,L_{n-j}(t).

Verification of Claim 2.

The sum under consideration can be written as:

\sum_{j=2}^{k}(t^{r_{j}}-1)\,\Bigl[\,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(\rho,v)\in NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(n-1)\\
\mathrm{with}\ |J(\rho,v)|=j-1\end{subarray}}t^{Z_{n-1}(\rho,v)}\,\Bigr],

hence it suffices to verify that we have equalities

(3.12)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(\rho,v)\in NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(n-1)\\
\mathrm{with}\ |J(\rho,v)|=j-1\end{subarray}}t^{Z_{n-1}(\rho,v)}=(n-j+1)\,t^{r_{1}+\cdots+r_{j-1}}\,\,L_{n-j}(t),

holding for every
j\in\{2,\ldots,k\}
. The case
j=2
of (

)
says that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(\rho,v)\in NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(n-1)\\
\mathrm{with}\ |J(\rho,v)|=1\end{subarray}}t^{Z_{n-1}(\rho,v)}=(n-1)\,t^{r_{1}}\,\,L_{n-2}(t),

which is just Lemma
3.4
, used for

. For a
j\in\{3,\ldots,k\}
,

) goes as follows:

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(\rho,v)\in NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(n-1)\\
\mathrm{with}\ |J(\rho,v)|=j-1\end{subarray}}t^{Z_{n-1}(\sigma,w)}

4.1.
Block-counting random variables – proof of Theorem
1.6

In this subsection we consider the random variables

(Y_{n}:NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(n)\to\mathbb{R})_{n=1}^{\infty}
introduced in Notation
1.5

with
Y_{n}(\pi,u):=|\pi|

Notation and Remark 4.1

For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the combinatorial Laplace transform of
Y_{n}
(defined as
in Equation (
3.1
)) will be denoted as
B_{n}

B_{n}(t):=\sum_{(\pi,u)\in NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(n)}t^{|\pi|},\ \ t\in(0,\infty).

Clearly, every

is a polynomial function
without constant term. For example, direct inspection shows that

B_{1}(t)=t

B_{3}(t)=6t^{3}+5t^{2}+t

Lemma 4.2
.

The polynomials
B_{n}
introduced above satisfy the recursion

(4.2)

B_{n}(t)=(1+nt)\,B_{n-1}(t),\ \ n\geq 2,

and are consequently given by the formula

B_{n}(t)=t(1+2t)\cdots(1+nt),\ \mbox{ for $n\geq 2$ in $\mathbb{N}$ and $t\in(0,\infty)$.}

Proof.

We saw in Example
2.8
that the
Y_{n}
are recursive of
the first kind with input vector
(1)\in\mathbb{R}^{1}
. We use here that, equivalently,
they are recursive of the first kind with input vector

3.3
) of Theorem
3.2
in connection to the latter input

This gives the formula (
4.2
), where for

. Equation (

) follows from (

)

∎

, it is easy to find the explicit

, to get

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}(Y_{n})

\displaystyle=\Bigl(\frac{1}{3}+\cdots+\frac{1}{n+1}\Bigr)-\Bigl(\frac{1}{3^{2}}+\cdots+\frac{1}{(n+1)^{2}}\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\Bigl(H_{n+1}-1-\frac{1}{2}\Bigr)-\Bigl(H_{n+1}^{(2)}-1-\frac{1}{4}\Bigr)=H_{n+1}-H_{n+1}^{(2)}-\frac{1}{4},

as required.
Finally, in order to obtain the asymptotics stated in (
1.5
),
we write

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}(Y_{n})-\ln n

\displaystyle=\bigl(H_{n}+\frac{1}{n+1}\bigr)-H_{n+1}^{(2)}-\frac{1}{4}-\ln n

\displaystyle=(H_{n}-\ln n)-H_{n+1}^{(2)}-\frac{1}{4}+\frac{1}{n+1}.

The latter quantity converges to
\gamma-\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}-\frac{1}{4}+0
,
which gives the required
\approx
formula.

\square

5.
An aside to Section
4.1
:
moments of the monotonic Poisson process

In this section we do a brief detour from the main line of the paper, to
point out that the considerations on the block-counting statistic from
Section
4.1
can be cast in a non-commutative probability
setting, where they become considerations on
monotonic cumulants
.
In order to review the latter notion,
we will use (as commonly done by people working in this area) a simplified
algebraic version for what is a probability distribution on the real line.
We want to look at such distributions where every polynomial is integrable,
which makes it convenient to go with the following definition.

Definition 5.1
.

By
algebraic distribution
we will mean a linear functional

\mu:\mathbb{C}[X]\to\mathbb{C}
with the property that
\mu(1)=1
. For such a
functional, it is customary that the number
\mu(X)
is called

expectation
of
\mu
; and in general, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the number

\mu(X^{n})\in\mathbb{C}
will be referred to as the
moment of order
n
of
\mu
.

Remark and Definition 5.2
.

Let
\mu:\mathbb{C}[X]\to\mathbb{C}
be an algebraic distribution. There exists a
sequence of complex numbers
(\,c_{n}(\mu)\,)_{n=1}^{\infty}
, uniquely determined,
such that

(5.1)

\mu(X^{n})=\sum_{(\pi,u)\in NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(n)}\,\Bigl[\,\frac{1}{|\pi|!}\cdot\,\prod_{V\in\pi}c_{|V|}(\mu)\,\Bigr],\ \forall\,n\in\mathbb{N}.

The numbers
c_{n}(\mu)
are called
monotonic cumulants
of
\mu
, and
Equation (
5.1
) goes under the name of
“moment-cumulant formula” (for monotonic cumulants).
Upon grouping terms on the right-hand side, we can also write it in the form:

(5.2)

\mu(X^{n})=\sum_{\pi\in NC(n)}\,\Bigl[\,\frac{\mathrm{monord}(\pi)}{|\pi|!}\cdot\,\prod_{V\in\pi}c_{|V|}(\mu)\,\Bigr],\ \,n\in\mathbb{N},

where
\mathrm{monord}(\pi)
stands for the number of monotonic orderings of

\pi\in NC(n)
. The ratio
\mathrm{monord}(\pi)/|\pi|!
can be thought of as
the probability that a randomly picked ordering of the blocks of
\pi
is monotonic. This
ratio is equal to
1
for an interval partition (when every
V\in\pi
is a
sub-interval of
\{1,\ldots,n\}
), but is
<1
when nestings
among the blocks of
\pi
impose restrictions on what orderings of
\pi
are monotonic.

The existence of a sequence
(\,c_{n}(\mu)\,)_{n=1}^{\infty}
,
uniquely determined, such that Equation (
5.1
) holds, is easily seen by isolating
in the sum on the right-hand side the unique term which has

\pi=\bigl\{\,\{1,\ldots,n\}\,\bigr\}=:1_{n}
(the partition with only one block).
That special term is equal to
c_{n}(\mu)
, which allows us to re-write (
5.2
)
in the form

(5.3)

c_{n}(\mu)=\mu(X^{n})-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\pi\in NC(n),\\
\pi\neq 1_{n}\end{subarray}}\Bigl[\,\frac{\mathrm{monord}(\pi)}{|\pi|!}\cdot\,\prod_{V\in\pi}c_{|V|}(\mu)\,\Bigr],

and then use induction in order to write an explicit formula for
c_{n}(\mu)
in terms
of the moments
\mu(X^{k}),\ 1\leq k\leq n
.
For instance, for the first few values of
n
we get:

\mu(X)=c_{1}(\mu),\ \mu(X^{2})=c_{2}(\mu)+\bigl(\,c_{1}(\mu)\,\bigr)^{2},\ \mu(X^{3})=c_{3}(\mu)+\frac{5}{2}c_{1}(\mu)\,c_{2}(\mu)+\bigl(\,c_{1}(\mu)\,\bigr)^{3},

which gives

c_{1}(\mu)=\mu(X),\ c_{2}(\mu)=\mu(X^{2})-\bigl(\,\mu(X)\,\bigr)^{2},\ c_{3}(\mu)=\mu(X^{3})-\frac{5}{2}\mu(X)\,\mu(X^{2})+\frac{3}{2}\bigl(\,\mu(X)\,\bigr)^{3}.

Another easily derived consequence of (
5.2
) and (
5.3
) is this:
for every sequence of complex numbers
(\alpha_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
there
exists an algebraic distribution
\mu
, uniquely determined, such that

c_{n}(\mu)=\alpha_{n}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. In other words, it is possible to
define an algebraic distribution
\mu
via a prescription of what are the monotonic
cumulants
c_{n}(\mu)
. This can in particular be used (following a well-established
pattern from other brands of non-commutative probability) in order to pinpoint the
monotonic version of Poisson distribution, as follows.

Notation 5.3
.

For every
\alpha\in[0,\infty)
we will let
\nu_{\alpha}:\mathbb{C}[X]\to\mathbb{C}
be the
algebraic distribution defined via the requirement that it has monotonic cumulants

(5.4)

c_{n}(\nu_{\alpha})=\alpha,\ \ \forall\,n\in\mathbb{N}.

The family of distributions
\bigl(\,\nu_{\alpha}\,\bigr)_{\alpha\in[0,\infty)}

goes under the name of
monotonic Poisson process
.

In
[
4
]
, Belton gave a nice description of the moments of the distributions

\nu_{\alpha}
, in terms of a double-array of integers
J_{k}^{(n)}
(not depending on

\alpha
) which satisfy a simple recursive relation. We repeat this here, and observe
how the said recursive relation can be read by looking at the
NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}
-tree.

Proposition 5.4
.

(
[
4
, Section 2]
.)
The moments of the monotone Poisson process are

(5.5)

\nu_{\alpha}(X^{n})=\sum_{k=1}^{n}J_{k}^{(n)}\cdot\frac{\alpha^{k}}{k!},\ \ \forall\,\alpha\in[0,\infty),\,n\in\mathbb{N},

with the double array
\bigl\{J_{k}^{(n)}\mid n,k\in\mathbb{N},\,n\geq k\bigr\}

defined by initializing

(5.6)

J_{1}^{(n)}=1\mbox{ and }J_{n}^{(n)}=n!\,,\ \ \forall\,n\in\mathbb{N}

(in particular
J_{1}^{(1)}=1
,
J_{1}^{(2)}=1
,
J_{2}^{(2)}=2
),
and then by requiring that

(5.7)

J_{k}^{(n)}=J_{k}^{(n-1)}+nJ_{k-1}^{(n-1)},\ \mbox{ for all $n\geq 3$ and $2\leq k\leq n-1$.}

Proof.

The uniqueness of the numbers
J_{k}^{(n)}

5.6

Then for every
\alpha\in[0,\infty)

5.1

\nu_{\alpha}

\displaystyle\nu_{\alpha}(X^{n})

according to whether

of

where

and where for every

We leave it as a straightforward exercise to the reader to use the description
of the parent map
\mathfrak{p}
of the
NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}
-tree (cf. Definition
2.2
) in order to
check that, for every
1\leq m\leq n

\mathfrak{p}

By putting these things together we find that

(\pi,u)\in NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(n)
.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
\alpha,\beta,q\in\mathbb{Z}
and let
(Z_{n}:NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(n)\to\mathbb{R})_{n=1}^{\infty}
be
a sequence of random variables which is recursive of the second kind with input

(\alpha,\beta,q)
, in the sense of Definition
2.11
. For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
let
L_{n}:(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
be the combinatorial Laplace transform of
Z_{n}
, defined
by Equation (
3.1
) of Definition
3.1
. Then one has the recursion:

(6.1)

L_{n}(t)=\Bigl(q\,t^{\alpha}+((n+1)-q)\,t^{\beta}\Bigr)\cdot L_{n-1}(t)+\bigl(t^{\alpha+1}-t^{\beta+1}\bigr)\cdot L_{n-1}^{\prime}(t),

holding for
n\geq 2
and
t\in(0,\infty)
.

Proof.

Consider an
n\geq 2
in
\mathbb{N}
and a
t\in(0,\infty)
, for which we will verify
(
6.1
). Following the same idea as for the proof of Theorem
3.2
,
we organize the sum defining
L_{n}(t)
as

(6.2)

L_{n}(t)=\sum_{(\rho,v)\in NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(n-1)}\Bigl[\,\sum_{(\pi,u)\in C(\rho,v)}t^{Z_{n}(\pi,u)}\,\Bigr],

where
C(\rho,v)
denotes the set of children of
(\rho,v)
in the
NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}
-tree.

Let us fix for the moment a
(\rho,v)\in NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(n-1)
,
and examine the inner sum corresponding to
(\rho,v)
in (
6.2
).
We break this sum into two by using the set
C_{o}(\rho,v)\subseteq C(\rho,v)

provided by the definition of the recursion of the second kind (cf. (
2.8
) there),
and get:

\displaystyle\sum_{(\pi,u)\in C(\rho,v)}t^{Z_{n}(\pi,u)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{(\pi,u)\in C_{o}(\rho,v)}t^{Z_{n}(\pi,u)}+\sum_{(\pi,u)\in C(\rho,v)\setminus C_{o}(\rho,v)}t^{Z_{n}(\pi,u)}

\displaystyle=|C_{o}(\rho,v)|\cdot t^{Z_{n-1}(\rho,v)+\alpha}+|C(\rho,v)\setminus C_{o}(\rho,v)|\cdot t^{Z_{n-1}(\rho,v)+\beta}

\displaystyle=(Z_{n-1}(\rho,v)+q)\cdot t^{Z_{n-1}(\rho,v)+\alpha}+((n+1)-Z_{n-1}(\rho,v)-q)\cdot t^{Z_{n-1}(\rho,v)+\beta}

\displaystyle=t^{Z_{n-1}(\rho,v)}\,\Bigl(\,(t^{\alpha}-t^{\beta})\cdot Z_{n-1}(\rho,v)+\bigl(\,qt^{\alpha}+((n+1)-q)t^{\beta}\,\bigr)\,\Bigr).

Returning now to Equation (
6.2
), we see that the calculation shown in the
preceding paragraph decomposes
L_{n}(t)=\Sigma^{\prime}+\Sigma^{\prime\prime}
, where

\displaystyle\Sigma^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\sum_{(\rho,v)\in NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(n-1)}\,t^{Z_{n-1}(\rho,v)}\cdot\bigl(t^{\alpha}-t^{\beta}\bigr)\,Z_{n-1}(\rho,v)

\displaystyle=\bigl(t^{\alpha+1}-t^{\beta+1}\bigr)\cdot\sum_{(\rho,v)\in NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(n-1)}\,Z_{n-1}(\rho,v)\,t^{Z_{n-1}(\rho,v)-1}

\displaystyle=\bigl(t^{\alpha+1}-t^{\beta+1}\bigr)\cdot L_{n-1}^{\prime}(t),

and

\displaystyle\Sigma^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle=\sum_{(\rho,v)\in NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(n-1)}\,t^{Z_{n-1}(\rho,v)}\cdot\bigl(q\,t^{\alpha}+((n+1)-q)\,t^{\beta})

\displaystyle=\bigl(q\,t^{\alpha}+((n+1)-q)\,t^{\beta})\cdot L_{n-1}(t).

The expression
\Sigma^{\prime}+\Sigma^{\prime\prime}
gives the right-hand side of
the required formula (
6.1
).
∎

Corollary 6.2
.

In the framework and notation of Theorem
6.1
, the sequence of expectations

\bigl(\,E[Z_{n}]\,\bigr)_{n=1}^{\infty}
satisfies the recursion

(6.3)

E[Z_{n}]=\frac{(n+1)+(\alpha-\beta)}{n+1}\cdot E[Z_{n-1}]+\frac{\alpha q+\beta((n+1)-q)}{n+1},\ \ n\geq 2.

Proof.

Differentiating both sides of Equation (
6.1
) and then evaluating them
at
t=1
gives

(6.4)

L_{n}^{\prime}(1)=\bigl(\,\alpha q+\beta((n+1)-q)\,\bigr)\cdot\frac{n!}{2}+\bigl(\,(n+1)+(\alpha-\beta)\,\bigr)\cdot L_{n-1}^{\prime}(1).

We know (as noted in Remark
3.1
) that in (
6.4
) we can substitute

L_{n}^{\prime}(1)=E[Z_{n}]\cdot(n+1)!/2
and
L_{n-1}^{\prime}(1)=E[Z_{n-1}]\cdot n!/2
;
doing so leads to the formula (
6.3
) stated in the corollary.
∎

Corollary 6.3
.

Let
\bigl(\,\mathrm{Out}_{n}:NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(n)\to\mathbb{N}\,\bigr)_{n=1}^{\infty}
be the
sequence of random variables considered in Example
2.12
, where

\mathrm{Out}_{n}(\pi,u)
counts the outer blocks of
\pi
, for
(\pi,u)\in NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(n)
.
One has that

(6.5)

E[\mathrm{Out}_{n}]=\frac{2n+1}{3},\ \ n\in\mathbb{N}.

Proof.

We know from Example
2.12
that the sequence of
\mathrm{Out}_{n}
’s
is recursive of the second kind, with parameters
\alpha=1
,
\beta=0
and
q=1
.
We can thus use the formula (
6.3
) provided by the preceding corollary.
This says, for the case at hand, that we have

(6.6)

E[\mathrm{Out}_{n}]=\bigl(1+\frac{1}{n+1}\bigr)\cdot E[\mathrm{Out}_{n-1}]+\frac{1}{n+1},\ \ n\geq 2.

From (
6.6
), an easy induction (initialized with
E[\mathrm{Out}_{1}]=1
) gives
the formula (
6.5
).
∎

7.
The
NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}_{2}
-tree, and
some combinatorial statistics related to it

We now turn our attention to sets
of monotonically ordered non-crossing
pair-partitions
,

NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}_{2}(2n):=\Bigl\{(\pi,u)\begin{array}[]{lr}\vline&\pi\in NC_{2}(2n)\mbox{ and }u:\pi\to\{1,\ldots,n\},\\
\vline&\mbox{monotonic ordering}\end{array}\Bigr\},\ \ n\in\mathbb{N}.

In this section we follow up on the discussion around these sets
made in Subsection
1.3
of the Introduction, and we start with
the fact that one has a natural tree structure on the union

NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}_{2}:=\sqcup_{n=1}^{\infty}NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}_{2}(2n)
.

7.1.
Description of the
NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}_{2}
-tree.

Remark 7.1
.

Since
NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}_{2}(2n)\subseteq NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(2n)
, we can and will continue to
refer to Notation
2.1
.1 and write “
J(\pi,u)
”
for the block of a pair-partition
\pi\in NC_{2}(2n)
which has the maximal
label in the monotone ordering
u
of
\pi
. The specifics of the
(\pi,u)

considered here ensure that
|J(\pi,u)|=2
and
u(\,J(\pi,u)\,)=n
.
Since we additionally know (cf. Remark
1.2
) that
J(\pi,u)
is
a sub-interval of
\{1,\ldots,2n\}
, we can actually be certain to have here

J(\pi,u)=\{m,m+1\}
for some
1\leq m\leq 2n-1
.

2^{o}
We will also continue to use the “restrict-and-relabel” operation

\pi\mapsto\pi_{W}
introduced in Notation
2.1
.2, making sure
that we only apply it to situations where both
\pi
and
\pi_{W}
belong to

NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}_{2}
.

3^{o}
Similarly to the development shown for the
NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}
-tree in Section
2.1
,
we will describe the edges of the
NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}_{2}
-tree by indicating how one finds the

NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}_{2}
-parent of a
(\pi,u)\in NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}_{2}(2n)
, for
n\geq 2
. In order to distinguish
from the similar terminology introduced in Definition
2.2
, we will refer to the

NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}_{2}
-parent of such a
(\pi,u)
by denoting it as
\mathfrak{pp}(\pi,u)
. This
notation fits with the fact that, if
(\pi,u)
was to be viewed as vertex of the
NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}
-tree,
then
\mathfrak{pp}(\pi,u)\in NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}_{2}(2n-2)
would come out as “the grandparent” (parent of
the parent) of
(\pi,u)
in that context.

Definition 7.2
.

Let
(\pi,u)
be a monotonically ordered pair-partition in
NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}_{2}(2n)
, where

n\geq 2
. Consider the pair
J(\pi,u)=\{m,m+1\}\in\pi
, and consider
the restrict-and-relabel partition

\rho:=\pi_{\{1,\ldots,2n\}\setminus\{m,m+1\}}\in NC_{2}(2n-2).

It is easily checked that the monotonic order
u:\pi\to\{1,\ldots,n\}

induces a monotonic order
v:\rho\to\{1,\ldots,n-1\}
, defined by the formula:

(7.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}v\bigl(\{p,q\}\bigr):=u\Bigl(\,\bigl\{\phi(p),\phi(q)\bigr\}\,\Bigr)\mbox{ for $\{p,q\}\in\rho$, where $\phi$ is the unique}\\
\mbox{increasing bijection from $\{1,\ldots,2n-2\}$
onto $\{1,\ldots,2n\}\setminus\{m,m+1\}$. }\end{array}\right.

The monotonically ordered pair-partition
(\rho,v)\in NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(2n-2)

obtained in this way will be called
the pair-parent
of
(\pi,u)

and will be denoted as
\mathfrak{pp}(\pi,u)
.

.

one easily sees that:

(7.2)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\mbox{for every $1\leq m\leq 2n-1$ there exists a
$(\pi_{m},u_{m})\in C^{(\mathrm{pair})}(\rho,v)$,}\\
\mbox{uniquely determined, such that }J(\pi_{m},u_{m})=\{m,m+1\}.\end{array}\right.

As a consequence of (
7.2
), it follows that
|C^{(\mathrm{pair})}(\rho,v)|=2n-1
.

2^{o}
For the record, we note here that the homogeneity property (
1.10
) stated
in Subsection
1.3
of the Introduction is an immediate consequence of
(
7.2
), and same for the further consequence stated in (
1.11
), that

|NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}_{2}(2n)|=(2n-1)!!
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

7.2.
NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}_{2}
-analogue for the recursive
pattern “of the second kind”.

For a random
(\pi,u)\in NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}_{2}(2n)
, the statistic which counts the
blocks of
\pi
is trivial, and same is the case for the statistics which count
blocks tallied by size. It is, however, non-trivial to examine the statistic which
counts the outer blocks of
\pi
. In what follows we will see how this can be done
by adjusting to
NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}_{2}
-setting the discussion around the recursive condition of
the second kind which was introduced in Subsection
2.3
in connection
to the full
NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}
-tree. For starters, here is the adjusted version of that notion.

Definition 7.5
.

Let
\alpha,\beta,q\in\mathbb{Z}
be given. We will say that a sequence
of random variables
(Z_{n}:NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}_{2}(2n)\to\mathbb{R})_{n=1}^{\infty}
is

pair-recursive of the second kind
with input
(\alpha,\beta;q)
when
it has the following property: for every
n\geq 2
and
(\rho,v)\in NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}_{2}(2n-2)
,
the set
C^{(\mathrm{pair})}(\rho,v)
of children of
(\rho,v)
in the
NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}_{2}
-tree
has a subset
C^{(\mathrm{pair})}_{o}(\rho,v)
such that:

(7.3)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathrm{(1)}&\mbox{If $(\pi,u)\in C^{(\mathrm{pair})}_{o}(\rho,v)$, then
$Z_{n}(\pi,u)=Z_{n-1}(\rho,v)+\alpha$,}\\
\mathrm{(2)}&\mbox{If $(\pi,u)\in C^{(\mathrm{pair})}(\rho,v)\setminus C^{(\mathrm{pair})}_{o}(\rho,v)$,
then $Z_{n}(\pi,u)=Z_{n-1}(\rho,v)+\beta$,}\\
\mbox{$\ $}\mathrm{and}&\\
\mathrm{(3)}&\mbox{$|C^{(\mathrm{pair})}_{o}(\rho,v)|=Z_{n-1}(\rho,v)+q$.}\end{array}\right.

Remark and Notation 7.6
.

In analogy with what we saw in the full
NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}
-setting, the recursion
property from Definition
7.5
entails a recursion for the
combinatorial Laplace transforms of the random variables in question. For any

n\in\mathbb{N}
and
Z_{n}:NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}_{2}(2n)\to\mathbb{R}
, the combinatorial Laplace
transform of
Z_{n}
is the function
L_{n}:(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
defined by a
recipe similar to the one used in the preceding sections,

(7.4)

L_{n}(t)=\sum_{(\pi,u)\in NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}_{2}(2n)}t^{Z_{n}(\pi,u)},\ \ t>0.

When
Z_{n}
is viewed as a random variable with respect to the uniform distribution
on
NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}_{2}(2n)
, the function
L_{n}
can be used towards computing moments of
Z_{n}
,
and in particular the expectation of
Z_{n}
is retrieved via the formula

(7.5)

E[Z_{n}]=\frac{L_{n}^{\prime}(1)}{L_{n}(1)}=\frac{L_{n}^{\prime}(1)}{(2n-1)!!}

(similar to the one from the parallel discussion about random variables
on
NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(n)
, but where we now use the normalization constant

L_{n}(1)=|NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}_{2}(2n)|=(2n-1)!!
).

The next theorem and corollary give the
NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}_{2}
-analogue for the Theorem
6.1

and Corollary
6.2
of the preceding section.
The proofs are mutatis mutandis repetitions of arguments already shown in
Section
6
, and are left to the reader.

Theorem 7.7
.

Let
\alpha,\beta,q\in\mathbb{Z}
and let
(Z_{n}:NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}_{2}(2n)\to\mathbb{R})_{n=1}^{\infty}
be
a sequence of random variables which is pair-recursive of the second kind with input

(\alpha,\beta;q)
. For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
L_{n}:(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
be the
combinatorial Laplace transform of
Z_{n}
. Then one has the recursion:

(7.6)

L_{n}(t)=\Bigl(q\,t^{\alpha}+((2n-1)-q)\,t^{\beta}\Bigr)\cdot L_{n-1}(t)+\bigl(t^{\alpha+1}-t^{\beta+1}\bigr)\cdot L_{n-1}^{\prime}(t),

holding for
n\geq 2
and
t\in(0,\infty)
.

\square

Corollary 7.8
.

In the framework and notation of Theorem
7.7
, the sequence of expectations

\bigl(\,E[Z_{n}]\,\bigr)_{n=1}^{\infty}
satisfies the recursion

(7.7)

E[Z_{n}]=\frac{(2n-1)+(\alpha-\beta)}{2n-1}\cdot E[Z_{n-1}]+\frac{q\,\alpha+((2n-1)-q)\,\beta}{2n-1},\ \ n\geq 2.

\square

8.
Expectation of the “area” statistic on

NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}_{2}(2n)

In this section we consider the framework from Section
1.4
of
the Introduction, and we give the proof of Theorem
1.11
, concerning the
expectation of the area-measuring random variable
A_{n}:NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}_{2}(2n)\to\mathbb{R}
.
Recall that the latter random variable was introduced by putting

A_{n}(\pi,u):=\mathrm{area}(\pi)
, the area in between the horizontal axis and
the Dyck path naturally associated to
\pi
. In the next lemma we give an alternate
description for what is
\mathrm{area}(\pi)
, which does not refer to Dyck paths.

Lemma 8.1
.

Let
n
be in
\mathbb{N}
. One has that:

(\,\diamondsuit_{n}\,)\hskip 56.9055pt\mathrm{area}(\pi)=\sum_{V\in\pi}\max(V)-\min(V),\ \ \forall\,\pi\in NC_{2}(2n).\hskip 56.9055pt\mbox{$\ $}

Proof.

By induction on
n
. The statement
(\,\diamondsuit_{1}\,)

clearly holds, since the unique pair-partition
\pi=\{V_{1}\}\in NC_{2}(2)
has

\mathrm{area}(\pi)=1=\max(V_{1})-\min(V_{1})
. For the rest of the proof we
fix an
n\geq 1
, we assume that the statement
(\,\diamondsuit_{m}\,)
was
proved for every
1\leq m\leq n
, and we verify that

(\,\diamondsuit_{n+1}\,)
holds as well. We divide the verification into
two cases.

Case 1. Consider a
\pi\in NC_{2}(2n+2)
such that
\{1,2n+2\}
is a pair
of
\pi
.

In this case, we let
\pi_{o}\in NC_{2}(2n)
be the relabeled restriction of

\pi
to
\{2,3,\ldots,2n+1\}
. If we write explicitly

\pi=\{V_{1},\ldots,V_{n},V_{n+1}\}
with
V_{n+1}=\{1,2n+2\}
, then

\pi_{o}
can be written as
\{V_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,V_{n}^{\prime}\}
with

V_{j}^{\prime}
obtained by translating
V_{j}
by 1 to the left (and thus having

\max(V_{j}^{\prime})-\min(V_{j}^{\prime})=\max(V_{j})-\min(V_{j})
), for
1\leq j\leq n
.
On the right-hand side of the formula
(\,\diamondsuit_{n+1}\,)
we get:

\sum_{V\in\pi}\max(V)-\min(V)=\bigl(\,\sum_{j=1}^{n}\max(V_{j})-\min(V_{j})\,\bigr)+\bigl(\,(2n+2)-1\,\bigr)

=\bigl(\,\sum_{j=1}^{n}\max(V_{j}^{\prime})-\min(V_{j}^{\prime})\,\bigr)+(2n+1)=\mathrm{area}(\pi_{o})+(2n+1),

where at the third equality sign we invoked the induction hypothesis.

In order to complete the verification of this case, we are left to check that

\mathrm{area}(\pi)=\mathrm{area}(\pi_{o})+(2n+1)
. But this is immediately seen by
looking at the Dyck paths associated to
\pi
and
\pi_{o}
.
Indeed, the path for
\pi
can be obtained by taking the path for
\pi_{o}
and placing
it on top of the trapezoid with vertices at the points
(0,0)
,

(1,1),(2n+1,1)

and
(2n+2,0)
of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, where the latter trapezoid has area equal to
2n+1
.

Case 2. Consider a
\pi\in NC_{2}(2n+2)
where
1
is paired
with
2k
for some
1\leq k\leq n
.

In this case, we let
\pi_{1}\in NC_{2}(2k)
and
\pi_{2}\in NC_{2}(\,2(n+1-k)\,)

be the relabeled restrictions of
\pi
to
\{1,\ldots,2k\}
and to

\{2k+1,\ldots,2n+2\}
, respectively. Upon looking at the pictures of the
Dyck paths associated to
\pi,\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}
, one immediately sees that

(8.1)

\mathrm{area}(\pi)=\mathrm{area}(\pi_{1})+\mathrm{area}(\pi_{2}).

The induction hypothesis applies to both
\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}
, hence
(
8.1
) can be continued with

=\Bigl(\,\sum_{V^{\prime}\in\pi_{1}}\max(V^{\prime})-\min(V^{\prime})\,\Bigr)+\Bigl(\,\sum_{V^{\prime\prime}\in\pi_{2}}\max(V^{\prime\prime})-\min(V^{\prime\prime})\,\Bigr),

which is immediately seen to just be

\sum_{V\in\pi}\max(V)-\min(V)
, as required.
∎

In order to approach the area-measuring random variables
A_{n}
by using
the
NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}_{2}
-tree, we prove another lemma, as follows.

Lemma 8.2
.

Let
n\geq 2
be in
\mathbb{N}
, let
(\rho,v)
be in
NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}_{2}(2n-2)
,
and consider the set
C^{(\mathrm{pair})}(\rho,v)\subseteq NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}_{2}(2n)
consisting
of children of
(\rho,v)
in the
NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}_{2}
-tree
(cf. Notation and Remark
7.4
). One has that

(8.2)

\sum_{(\pi,u)\in C^{(\mathrm{pair})}(\rho,v)}A_{n}(\pi,u)=(2n-1)+(2n+1)\cdot A_{n-1}(\rho,v).

Proof.

As observed in Notation and Remark
7.4
, we can spell out

C^{(\mathrm{pair})}(\rho,v)=\bigl\{(\pi_{1},u_{1}),\ldots,(\pi_{2n-1},u_{2n-1})\bigr\},

where for every
1\leq m\leq 2n-1
the ordered partition

(\pi_{m},u_{m})\in NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}_{2}(2n)
is uniquely determined by the requirements

\mathfrak{pp}(\pi_{m},u_{m})=(\rho,v)
and
J(\pi_{m},u_{m})=\{m,m+1\}
.
For further processing, let us fix an explicit writing
\rho=\{V_{1},\ldots,V_{n-1}\}
.
Then for every
1\leq m\leq 2n-1
we get an explicit writing

\pi_{m}=\bigl\{V_{1}^{(m)},\ldots,V_{n-1}^{(m)},\,\{m,m+1\}\,\bigr\},

where
V_{1}^{(m)},\ldots,V_{n-1}^{(m)}
, are the images of

V_{1},\ldots,V_{n-1}
, respectively, under the unique order-preserving bijection
from
\{1,\ldots,2n-2\}
onto
\{1,\ldots,2n\}\setminus\{m,m+1\}
.
This gives:

\sum_{(\pi,u)\in C^{(\mathrm{pair})}(\rho,v)}A_{n}(\pi,u)=\sum_{m=1}^{2n-1}\mathrm{area}(\pi_{m})\hskip 128.0374pt\mbox{$\ $}

\hskip 56.9055pt=\sum_{m=1}^{2n-1}\Bigl(\,1+\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\max(V_{j}^{(m)})-\min(V_{j}^{(m)})\,\Bigr)\ \mbox{ (by Lemma \ref{lemma:81}) }

(8.3)

=(2n-1)+\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\Bigl(\,\sum_{m=1}^{2n-1}\max(V_{j}^{(m)})-\min(V_{j}^{(m)})\,\Bigr).

Let us now fix for the moment a
j\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}
, and let us examine the
inside sum over
m
in (
8.3
). For every
1\leq m\leq 2n-1
, the relation
between the pair
V_{j}^{(m)}\in\pi_{m}
and the pair
V_{j}\in\pi
forces the quantity

\max(V_{j}^{(m)})-\min(V_{j}^{(m)})
to be equal to

(8.4)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\bigl[\,\max(V_{j})-\min(V_{j})\,\bigr]+2,\mbox{ if $\min(V_{j})\leq m-2$ and $\max(V_{j})\geq m-1$,}\\
\\
\bigl[\,\max(V_{j})-\min(V_{j})\,\bigr],\ \mbox{ otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

Summing over
m
in (
8.4
) thus gives

\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{2n-1}\max(V_{j}^{(m)})-\min(V_{j}^{(m)})

\displaystyle=(2n-1)\cdot\bigl[\,\max(V_{j})-\min(V_{j})\,\bigr]+\sum_{m=\min(V_{j})+2}^{\max(V_{j})+1}2

\displaystyle=(2n-1)\cdot\bigl[\,\max(V_{j})-\min(V_{j})\,\bigr]+(\max(V_{j})-\min(V_{j}))\cdot 2

\displaystyle=(2n+1)\cdot\bigl[\,\max(V_{j})-\min(V_{j})\,\bigr],

where at the second equality sign we observed that the summation range for

m

Proof of Theorem
1.11
.

For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let us put

(8.5)

S_{n}:=\sum_{(\pi,u)\in NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}(n)}A_{n}(\pi,u).

We claim that the numbers
S_{n}
satisfy the following recursion:

(8.6)

S_{n}=(2n+1)\,S_{n-1}+(2n-1)!!\,,\ \ \forall\,n\geq 2.

In order to prove (
8.6
), we do the usual trick of grouping the
terms on the right-hand side of (
8.5
) according to what is the
parent of
(\pi,u)
in the
NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}_{2}
-tree:

\displaystyle S_{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{(\rho,v)\in NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}_{2}(n-1)}\,\Bigl(\sum_{(\pi,u)\in C^{(\mathrm{pair})}(\rho,v)}A_{n}(\pi,u)\,\Bigr),

\displaystyle=\sum_{(\rho,v)\in NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}_{2}(n-1)}\,\Bigl((2n-1)+(2n+1)\cdot A_{n-1}(\rho,v)\,\Bigr)\mbox{ (by Lemma \ref{lemma:82})}

\displaystyle=(2n-1)\cdot|\,NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}_{2}(n-1)\,|+(2n+1)\cdot\sum_{(\rho,v)\in NC^{\mathrm{(mton)}}_{2}(n-1)}A_{n-1}(\rho,v)

\displaystyle=(2n-1)\cdot(2n-3)!!+(2n+1)\cdot S_{n-1},

which gives the right-hand side of (
8.6
).

From (
8.6
), an easy induction on
n
yields the exact formula

(8.7)

S_{n}=(2n+1)!!\,\Bigl(\,\frac{1}{3}+\frac{1}{5}+\cdots+\frac{1}{2n+1}\,\Bigr),\ \ n\geq 1\text{.}

Hence the formula for the expectation of
A_{n}
becomes

E[\,A_{n}\,]=\frac{S_{n}}{(2n-1)!!}=\Bigl(\,(2n+1)!!\,\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{2k+1}\,\Bigr)/(2n-1)!!=(2n+1)\,\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{2k+1},

as claimed in Equation (
1.15
) from the statement of the theorem.

In order to verify that
E[\,A_{n}\,]\sim n\,\ln n
,
we rewrite the above formula as

(8.8)

E[\,A_{n}\,]=\bigl(n+\frac{1}{2}\bigr)\,\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{k+\frac{1}{2}},

where the sum on the right-hand side is bounded above by
\sum_{k=1}^{n}1/k=H_{n}

and bounded below by
\sum_{k=1}^{n}1/(k+1)>H_{n}-1
. This leads to the inequalities

\frac{n\,(H_{n}-1)}{n\,\ln n}\leq\frac{E[\,A_{n}\,]}{n\,\ln n}\leq\frac{(n+1)\,H_{n}}{n\,\ln n},\ \ n\geq 2,

and implies via squeeze that

\lim_{n\to\infty}E[\,A_{n}\,]/(n\,\ln n)=1
.

\square

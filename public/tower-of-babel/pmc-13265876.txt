Comment on “Dietary dimethylglycine alleviates cyclic heat stress-induced jejunal injury in yellow-feathered broilers by inhibiting the TLR4/NF-κB signaling pathway and restoring MUC2 expression”
Authors: Deepti M Sati, Vanashri Dhananjay Shinde, Chhaya Agarwal
Published: 2026

Dear Editor,

We read with great interest the recent study evaluating the protective effects of dietary dimethylglycine (DMG) against cyclic heat stress-induced jejunal injury in yellow-feathered broilers (Shao et al., 2026). The integration of animal and primary epithelial cell experiments provides important mechanistic insight into nutritional modulation of intestinal inflammation during thermal stress. Nevertheless, several aspects merit further consideration to strengthen the translational interpretation of these findings.

The study primarily attributes the protective effects of DMG to suppression of the Toll-like receptor 4 (TLR4)/nuclear factor-kappa B (NF-κB) signaling pathway and restoration of mucin 2 (MUC2) expression. However, the experimental design did not clarify whether reduced inflammatory signaling resulted directly from epithelial modulation or indirectly from improved cellular resilience under thermal stress. Rectal temperature remained elevated throughout most of the experimental period despite DMG supplementation, suggesting that systemic heat burden persisted (Zeng et al., 2023). Under these conditions, oxidative stress, mitochondrial dysfunction, and intestinal hypoxia may independently contribute to cytokine release and epithelial disruption. The absence of oxidative stress markers, apoptosis-related proteins, or tight-junction assessments limits mechanistic discrimination between anti-inflammatory and cytoprotective actions of DMG.

Another consideration relates to the interpretation of restored MUC2 expression as evidence of improved barrier function. Although MUC2 is a critical component of the intestinal mucus layer, goblet cell quantification and direct permeability measurements were not performed. Histological improvement therefore may not fully reflect functional recovery of mucosal defense. Furthermore, intestinal microbiota composition was not evaluated despite previous evidence suggesting that DMG can influence microbial metabolism during heat stress. Because microbial endotoxins are major activators of TLR4 signaling, microbiome-associated effects could have contributed substantially to the observed reduction in inflammatory cytokines (Jialal and Rajamani, 2014). Clarifying this interaction would improve understanding of whether DMG acts primarily through host signaling pathways or through modulation of luminal microbial ecology.

The interpretation of growth performance outcomes also requires caution. Several productive indices, including body weight, average daily gain, and feed conversion ratio, demonstrated only borderline statistical significance. While molecular and histological findings were convincing, the clinical relevance for commercial poultry production remains uncertain without validation in larger flocks and prolonged heat exposure models. Biomarker improvement alone may not necessarily translate into economically meaningful production benefits under field conditions (De Costa, 2018).

Despite these considerations, the study offers valuable evidence supporting DMG as a potential nutritional strategy against heat stress-associated intestinal injury and expands current understanding of inflammatory signaling within the broiler jejunum.

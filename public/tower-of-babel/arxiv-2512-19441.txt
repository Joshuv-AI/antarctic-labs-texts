Fourier dimension of imaginary Gaussian Multiplicative Chaos
Benjamin Bonnefont, Hermanni Rajamäki, Vincent Vargas

Abstract
. We study the Fourier coefficients of imaginary Gaussian multiplicative chaos (GMC) on the unit circle. Under the subcritical phase β ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) \beta\in(0,1) , we show that the Fourier dimension is 1 − β 2 1-\beta^{2} and prove a central limit theorem for the rescaled coefficients.



1.
Introduction

Gaussian multiplicative chaos (GMC) is a theory of random measures obtained by exponentiating log-correlated Gaussian fields. It was introduced by Kahane
[
10
]
, following earlier ideas of Mandelbrot
[
16
]
on turbulence and Høegh-Krohn
[
8
]
in quantum field theory. Over the last decades, GMC has become a central object in probability and mathematical physics, with connections to turbulence, finance, conformal field theory, random matrix theory and related areas. Since log-correlated fields are random distributions rather than functions, the exponential must be defined through a regularization and renormalization procedure, which yields a canonical multifractal object. Understanding its basic properties remains challenging in general dimensions.

In this article we focus on the one-dimensional setting and consider GMC on the unit circle
\mathbb{T}
. In this case, GMC exhibits
numerous symmetries that one can exploit in its study. The underlying field is the trace of the two-dimensional Gaussian free field (GFF) on
\mathbb{T}
with vanishing mean. More precisely, let
(X_{\theta})_{\theta\in\mathbb{T}}
be the centered Gaussian field with covariance

\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[X_{\theta}X_{\theta^{\prime}}}}\right]=\log\frac{1}{\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|e^{i\theta}-e^{i\theta^{\prime}}}}\right|},

understood as a random distribution on
\mathbb{T}
. Given a mollification
X^{(\varepsilon)}
of
X
and a parameter
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
, the GMC measure is defined by the limit

\mathrm{M}_{\gamma}\coloneqq\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\,\mathrm{M}_{\gamma}^{(\varepsilon)},\qquad\mathrm{M}_{\gamma}^{(\varepsilon)}(d\theta)\coloneqq\exp\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\gamma X^{(\varepsilon)}_{\theta}-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[X^{(\varepsilon)2}_{\theta}}}\right]}}\right)\,\,\mathrm{d}\theta,

whenever the limit exists (typically in probability in the space of Radon measures).
In the present setting, the limit yields a non-trivial random measure for
|\gamma|<\sqrt{2}
, the so-called subcritical phase. Moreover, it is almost surely supported on a set of Hausdorff dimension
1-\gamma^{2}/2
. For
|\gamma|\geq\sqrt{2}
, the limit degenerates (one obtains the zero measure) and a different renormalization is needed, see for instance
[
10
]
and the references therein.

One may further generalize the construction to complex parameters
\gamma=\alpha+\mathrm{i}\beta\in\mathbb{C}
. The range of parameters for which it is non-trivial, in the one-dimensional setting considered here, is given by the subcritical domain

\mathcal{D}\coloneqq\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\,|\gamma|<1\,}}\right\}\;\cup\;\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\,|\alpha|\in(1,\sqrt{2})\ \text{and}\ |\alpha|+|\beta|<\sqrt{2}\,}}\right\}.

A detailed discussion in the general case can be found in
[
12
]
.
In particular,
\mathcal{D}
contains purely imaginary parameters
\gamma=\mathrm{i}\beta
with
|\beta|<1
. In this regime, the limit

\mathrm{M}_{\mathrm{i}\beta}\coloneqq\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\,\mathrm{M}_{\mathrm{i}\beta}^{(\varepsilon)}

takes place in the space

H^{s}(\mathbb{T})\coloneqq\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\varphi\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T}):\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\|\varphi}}\right\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}(1+n^{2})^{s}\,|\widehat{\varphi}(n)|^{2}<\infty}}\right\}.

for any
s<-\beta^{2}/2
where
\widehat{\varphi}(n)\coloneqq\int_{\mathbb{T}}e^{\mathrm{i}n\theta}\,\varphi(d\theta)
is the Fourier coefficient of
\varphi
and the integral is understood in the sense of the action of the distribution
\varphi
on the smooth function
\theta\mapsto e^{\mathrm{i}n\theta}
. The imaginary case differs markedly from the real one. Although
\mathrm{M}_{\mathrm{i}\beta}
is no longer a positive measure (it is a complex-valued object), its total mass has finite moments of every order. Beyond this integrability property, the geometry also changes: while real GMC typically exhibits multifractal behavior
[
3
]
, imaginary chaos is monofractal
[
1
]
. For further properties and physical motivation, we refer to the foundational account
[
9
]
and to
[
2
]
for results on absolute continuity. In our setting, the relevant moments can be written as Coulomb-gas integrals on the circle,
which can be analyzed through Jack-polynomial expansions as already explored in the physics literature,
see e.g.
[
5
]
.

Since
\mathrm{M}_{\mathrm{i}\beta}
lives on the circle, it is natural to investigate its Fourier analytic properties. In the real case, this direction was initiated in
[
7
]
, where it was proved that the Fourier coefficients of
\mathrm{M}_{\gamma}
tend to
0
almost surely and sharp decay exponents were conjectured. These conjectures were recently established in elegant works for closely related multiplicative cascade models in
[
4
]
and for GMC in
[
14
]
, the first in a series on the harmonic analysis of multiplicative chaos.

The goal of the present paper is to continue this harmonic analysis in the purely imaginary setting. We set

c_{n}\coloneqq\widehat{\mathrm{M}_{\mathrm{i}\beta}}(n)=\int_{\mathbb{T}}e^{\mathrm{i}n\theta}\,\mathrm{M}_{\mathrm{i}\beta}(d\theta).

The Fourier dimension quantifies the decay of the Fourier coefficients of
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T})
. It is given by

\dim_{F}(\varphi)\coloneqq\sup\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\,s\in[0,1]:|\widehat{\varphi}(n)|^{2}=O(|n|^{-s})\text{ as }|n|\to\infty}}\right\}.

Our first result identifies the Fourier dimension of
\mathrm{M}_{\mathrm{i}\beta}
.

Theorem 1.1

(Fourier dimension)
.

For
\beta\in(0,1)
, the Fourier dimension of
\mathrm{M}_{\mathrm{i}\beta}
is
1-\beta^{2}
almost surely.

An upper bound of the Fourier dimension follows from the regularity results obtained in
[
9
]
. The authors prove that almost surely
\mathrm{M}_{\mathrm{i}\beta}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{T})
for
s<-\beta^{2}/2
and
\mathrm{M}_{\mathrm{i}\beta}\notin H^{s}(\mathbb{T})
for
s>-\beta^{2}/2
, which implies
\dim_{F}(\mathrm{M}_{\mathrm{i}\beta})\leq 1-\beta^{2}
. The content of Theorem
1.1
is to establish the lower bound.

The second result is the convergence in law of the rescaled Fourier coefficients. Here, we denote
\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{C}}(0,\sigma^{2})
the Gaussian complex random variable such that

\mathbb{E}\,\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{C}}(0,\sigma^{2})^{2}=0\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathbb{E}\,|\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{C}}(0,\sigma^{2})|^{2}=\sigma^{2}.

Theorem 1.2

(CLT for the rescaled coefficients)
.

The following convergence in distribution holds:

n^{\frac{1-\beta^{2}}{2}}\,c_{n}\longrightarrow\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{C}}(0,\kappa(\beta)),\qquad\text{ as }n\rightarrow\infty,

where
\kappa(\beta)=4\pi\Gamma(1-\beta^{2})\sin(\frac{\pi\beta^{2}}{2})
. Additionally, for any fixed integer
k\geq 0
, the rescaled process converges

n^{\frac{1-\beta^{2}}{2}}\,(c_{n},\dots,c_{n+k})\longrightarrow(\mathcal{N}^{0}_{\mathbb{C}}(0,\kappa(\beta)),\dots,\mathcal{N}^{k}_{\mathbb{C}}(0,\kappa(\beta))),\qquad\text{ as }n\rightarrow\infty,

where the Gaussian random variables are independent.

As a consequence, we obtain the following result.

Corollary 1.3

(Convergence toward a complex white noise)
.

Let
s<-\frac{1}{2}
.
We have the following convergence in distribution in
H^{s}(\mathbb{T})
:

n^{\frac{1-\beta^{2}}{2}}\,e^{\mathrm{i}n\theta}\,\mathrm{M}_{\mathrm{i}\beta}\longrightarrow W,\qquad\text{ as }n\rightarrow\infty,

where
W
is a complex white noise of intensity measure
\kappa(\beta)\,\mathrm{d}\theta
.

Motivated by the computation of the correlation dimension as performed in
[
11
,
7
]
, we propose the following conjecture

Conjecture 1.4
.

For
\gamma=\alpha+\mathrm{i}\beta\in\mathcal{D}
, the Fourier dimension of
\mathrm{M}_{\gamma}
is given by

\mathrm{dim}_{F}(\mathrm{M}_{\gamma})=\mathrm{dim}_{F}(\mathrm{M}_{\alpha})-\beta^{2}.

Strategy.

We proceed by the method of moments. The first ingredient is the rotational invariance of the field, which implies that each Fourier mode
c_{n}
is isotropic in the complex plane. As a consequence, to identify the limiting law of
c_{n}
it is enough to control the absolute moments.
The second ingredient is that these moments admit an explicit integral representation and such integrals can be analyzed by doing an expansion in the basis of Jack symmetric polynomials. This yields exact identities expressing the moments in terms of sums indexed by partitions. A key point in our approach is that the asymptotic study of these sums relies on the arithmetic of Jack polynomials, in particular on Pieri formulas and explicit evaluations. Finally, asymptotics of these expressions provide the moment limits, from which the Gaussian fluctuations and the joint convergence follow.

2.
The moments of
c_{n}

For complex-valued random variables, the convergence in distribution is a consequence of the convergence of the mixed moments

(2.1)

\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[Z_{n}^{p}\overline{Z_{n}}^{q}}}\right]\longrightarrow\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[Z^{p}\overline{Z}^{q}}}\right]\qquad\text{ for }p,q\in\mathbb{N},

as soon as the law of
Z
is characterized by its moments. This is the case for the complex Gaussian distribution
\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{C}}
. Here, an additional symmetry greatly simplifies matters, since the law of
c_{n}

is isotropic for all
n>0
. Recall that a complex-valued random variable
Z
is isotropic if for every
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
,
Z
has the same law as
e^{\mathrm{i}\alpha}Z
. It is equivalent to the two following properties:

(i)

\theta
is uniformly distributed on
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[0,2\pi}}\right]
,

(ii)

\theta
is independent from
R
.

where
(R,\theta)
is the polar decomposition of
Z
.
For isotropic random variables, the convergence in distribution is equivalent to the convergence in distribution of the modulus
R
. Indeed, suppose that
f
is continuous and bounded on
\mathbb{C}
and let
g(r)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{|z|=r}f(z)\,\mathrm{d}z
, then

\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[f(Z_{n})}}\right]=\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[f(R_{n}e^{\mathrm{i}\theta_{n}})}}\right]=\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[f(R_{n}e^{\mathrm{i}\theta_{n}})\middle|R_{n}}}\right]}}\right]=\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[g(R_{n})}}\right]\to\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[g(R)}}\right]=\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[f(Z)}}\right].

The isotropy of
c_{n}
is a consequence of the invariance by rotation of the field
X
:

e^{\mathrm{i}\alpha}c_{n}=\int_{0}^{2\pi}e^{\mathrm{i}n\theta}\,e^{\mathrm{i}\beta X(\theta-\frac{\alpha}{n})+\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[X(\theta-\frac{\alpha}{n})^{2}}}\right]}\,\,\mathrm{d}\theta\overset{\mathrm{(d)}}{=}c_{n}.

Therefore, to prove convergence in law of the rescaled Fourier coefficient to
\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{C}}(0,\kappa(\beta))
,
it suffices to show that, for each
N
,

n^{(1-\beta^{2})N}\mathbb{E}|c_{n}|^{2N}\longrightarrow N!\,\kappa(\beta)^{N},\quad\text{as }n\to\infty.

On the circle, the
2N
-th moment of
c_{n}
is explicitly given by the Coulomb-gas type integral

(2.2)

\mathbb{E}|c_{n}|^{2N}=\int_{[0,2\pi]^{2N}}e^{\mathrm{i}n\sum_{i=1}^{N}(\theta_{i}-\theta_{i}^{\prime})}\;\dfrac{\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq N}|e^{\mathrm{i}\theta_{i}}-e^{\mathrm{i}\theta_{j}}|^{\beta^{2}}\,|e^{\mathrm{i}\theta^{\prime}_{i}}-e^{\mathrm{i}\theta^{\prime}_{j}}|^{\beta^{2}}}{\prod_{1\leq i,j\leq N}|e^{\mathrm{i}\theta_{i}}-e^{\mathrm{i}\theta^{\prime}_{j}}|^{\beta^{2}}}\,\,\mathrm{d}\theta\,\,\mathrm{d}\theta^{\prime}.

The factors
\prod_{i<j}|e^{\mathrm{i}\theta_{i}}-e^{\mathrm{i}\theta_{j}}|^{\beta^{2}}
induce a natural scalar product, which we study in the next section. The asymptotic behavior of this integral is studied when
n=0
but in the regime
N\rightarrow\infty
in
[
13
]
and subsequent papers.

2.1.
Selberg inner product and Jack polynomials

From now on, let us denote
\gamma=\beta^{2}/2
1
1

1

There is a conflict notation with the intermittency parameter
\gamma
but as we now work in the imaginary case with
\mathrm{i}\beta
, it should cause no harm.
. On
\mathbb{T}^{N}
, the Selberg inner product is defined as

\langle f,g\rangle_{\gamma}\;=\;\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{N}}\int_{[0,2\pi]^{N}}f(e^{\mathrm{i}\theta})\;\overline{g(e^{\mathrm{i}\theta})}\,\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq N}|e^{\mathrm{i}\theta_{i}}-e^{\mathrm{i}\theta_{j}}|^{2\gamma}\,\,\mathrm{d}\theta,

where
f(e^{\mathrm{i}\theta})=f(e^{\mathrm{i}\theta_{1}},\cdots,e^{\mathrm{i}\theta_{N}})
.
There is a natural orthogonal basis of symmetric and homogeneous polynomials
P_{\lambda}^{(1/\gamma)}
, called the Jack polynomials, which are indexed by integer partitions
\lambda=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\lambda_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{N}\geq 0}}\right)
of length smaller than
N
. The definitions and properties of partitions used throughout the paper are gathered in appendix
A
. The orthogonality of the polynomials reads

(2.3)

\int_{[0,2\pi]^{N}}P_{\lambda}^{(1/\gamma)}(e^{\mathrm{i}\theta})\,P_{\mu}^{(1/\gamma)}(e^{-\mathrm{i}\theta})\,\prod_{i<j}|e^{\mathrm{i}\theta_{i}}-e^{\mathrm{i}\theta_{j}}|^{2\gamma}\,\,\mathrm{d}\theta=(2\pi)^{N}\,\delta_{\lambda\mu}\,\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\|P_{\lambda}^{(1/\gamma)}}}\right\|_{\gamma}^{2}.

For an extensive review of Jack polynomials, we encourage the reader to consult Macdonald’s book
[
15
, Chapter VI]
and for its connection with the Selberg integral, we refer to
[
6
]
.

We gather here the principal facts about Jack polynomials required for the present work. To lighten the notation, we omit the superscript
(1/\gamma)
in what follows. First, their norm is given by

(2.4)

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\|P_{\lambda}}}\right\|_{\gamma}^{2}=\frac{\Gamma(1+N\gamma)}{\Gamma(1+\gamma)^{N}}\;\frac{c^{\prime}_{\lambda}}{[1+(N-1)\gamma]_{\lambda}}\,P_{\lambda}(1^{N}).

The generalized Pochhammer symbol being defined by

[b]_{\lambda}=\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{N}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(b+(1-i)\gamma}}\right)_{\lambda_{i}},\qquad(b)_{k}=b(b+1)\cdots(b+k-1),

and

(2.5)

c_{\lambda}=\prod_{s\in\lambda}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(a_{\lambda}(s)+\gamma\,l_{\lambda}(s)+\gamma}}\right),\qquad c^{\prime}_{\lambda}=\prod_{s\in\lambda}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(a_{\lambda}(s)+\gamma\,l_{\lambda}(s)+1}}\right),

where the products in
c_{\lambda}
and
c^{\prime}_{\lambda}
are taken over boxes
s
of the Young diagram of
\lambda
and involve the arm- and leg-lengths
a_{\lambda}(s)
and
l_{\lambda}(s)
(see Appendix
A
for a precise definition). The value at
1=(1,\cdots,1)
of the polynomials is given by

P_{\lambda}(1)=\frac{\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[N\gamma}}\right]_{\lambda}}{c_{\lambda}}.

Stanley’s Cauchy identity.
The following identity will play a key role in what follows:

(2.6)

\prod_{i,j}(1-x_{i}y_{j})^{-\gamma}=\sum_{\lambda}\frac{c_{\lambda}(\gamma)}{c^{\prime}_{\lambda}(\gamma)}\,P_{\lambda}(x)\,P_{\lambda}(y).

Shift property.
One last property is

(2.7)

(x_{1}\cdots x_{N})^{n}\,P_{\lambda}(x)=P_{\lambda+n}(x),

where
\lambda+n
is obtained by adding
n
to each part of
\lambda
, in other words
\lambda+n=(\lambda_{1}+n,\dots,\lambda_{N}+n)
.

And as a consequence,

P_{\lambda}(1)=P_{\lambda+n}(1)=\frac{\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[N\gamma}}\right]_{\lambda+n}}{c_{\lambda+n}}.

2.2.
Jack expansion of the moments

Applying (
2.6
) to
x_{i}=e^{\mathrm{i}\theta_{i}}
and
y_{j}=e^{-\mathrm{i}\theta^{\prime}_{j}}
, and using the fact that Jack coefficients are real, hence
\overline{P_{\lambda}(e^{\mathrm{i}\theta})}=P_{\lambda}(e^{-\mathrm{i}\theta})
on the unit circle, we may formally rewrite the denominator in the integral representation as

(2.8)

\displaystyle\prod_{i,j}|e^{\mathrm{i}\theta_{i}}-e^{\mathrm{i}\theta^{\prime}_{j}}|^{-2\gamma}

\displaystyle=\prod_{i,j}(1-e^{\mathrm{i}\theta_{i}}e^{-\mathrm{i}\theta^{\prime}_{j}})^{-\gamma}\,\prod_{i,j}(1-e^{-\mathrm{i}\theta_{i}}e^{\mathrm{i}\theta_{j}^{\prime}})^{-\gamma}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda,\nu}\frac{c_{\lambda}}{c^{\prime}_{\lambda}}\,\frac{c_{\nu}}{c^{\prime}_{\nu}}P_{\lambda}(e^{\mathrm{i}\theta})\,P_{\lambda}(e^{-\mathrm{i}\theta^{\prime}})P_{\nu}(e^{-\mathrm{i}\theta})\,P_{\nu}(e^{\mathrm{i}\theta^{\prime}}).

Combining (
2.2
) with (
2.8
) yields the double series expansion

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|c_{n}|^{2N}

\displaystyle=\int_{[0,2\pi]^{2N}}e^{\mathrm{i}n\sum_{i}(\theta_{i}-\theta_{i}^{\prime})}\prod_{i<j}|e^{\mathrm{i}\theta_{i}}-e^{\mathrm{i}\theta_{j}}|^{2\gamma}\,|e^{\mathrm{i}\theta^{\prime}_{i}}-e^{\mathrm{i}\theta^{\prime}_{j}}|^{2\gamma}\,\prod_{i,j}|e^{\mathrm{i}\theta_{i}}-e^{\mathrm{i}\theta^{\prime}_{j}}|^{-2\gamma}\,\,\mathrm{d}\theta\,\,\mathrm{d}\theta^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda,\nu}\frac{c_{\lambda}}{c^{\prime}_{\lambda}}\frac{c_{\nu}}{c^{\prime}_{\nu}}\int_{[0,2\pi]^{2N}}e^{\mathrm{i}n\sum_{i}(\theta_{i}-\theta_{i}^{\prime})}\,P_{\lambda}(e^{\mathrm{i}\theta})\,P_{\lambda}(e^{-\mathrm{i}\theta^{\prime}})\;P_{\nu}(e^{-\mathrm{i}\theta})\;P_{\nu}(e^{\mathrm{i}\theta^{\prime}})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\prod_{i<j}|e^{\mathrm{i}\theta_{i}}-e^{\mathrm{i}\theta_{j}}|^{2\gamma}\,|e^{\mathrm{i}\theta^{\prime}_{i}}-e^{\mathrm{i}\theta^{\prime}_{j}}|^{2\gamma}\,\,\mathrm{d}\theta\,\,\mathrm{d}\theta^{\prime}

A rigorous justification is provided in Appendix
B
.

Now, the shift property (
2.7
) together with the orthogonality (
2.3
) reduces the expression above to a the simple positive series:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|c_{n}|^{2N}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda,\nu}\frac{c_{\lambda}}{c^{\prime}_{\lambda}}\frac{c_{\nu}}{c^{\prime}_{\nu}}\,\int P_{\lambda+n}(e^{\mathrm{i}\theta})\,P_{\lambda+n}(e^{-\mathrm{i}\theta^{\prime}})\,P_{\nu}(e^{-\mathrm{i}\theta})\,P_{\nu}(e^{\mathrm{i}\theta^{\prime}})\prod_{i<j}|e^{\mathrm{i}\theta_{i}}-e^{\mathrm{i}\theta_{j}}|^{2\gamma}\,|e^{\mathrm{i}\theta^{\prime}_{i}}-e^{\mathrm{i}\theta^{\prime}_{j}}|^{2\gamma}\,\,\,\mathrm{d}\theta\,\,\mathrm{d}\theta^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda,\nu}\frac{c_{\lambda}}{c^{\prime}_{\lambda}}\frac{c_{\nu}}{c^{\prime}_{\nu}}\,\Bigg|\int_{[0,2\pi]^{N}}P_{\lambda+n}(e^{\mathrm{i}\theta})\,P_{\nu}(e^{-\mathrm{i}\theta})\prod_{i<j}|e^{\mathrm{i}\theta_{i}}-e^{\mathrm{i}\theta_{j}}|^{2\gamma}\,\,\mathrm{d}\theta\Bigg|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda}\frac{c_{\lambda}}{c^{\prime}_{\lambda}}\frac{c_{\lambda+n}}{c^{\prime}_{\lambda+n}}\,(2\pi)^{2N}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\|P_{\lambda+n}}}\right\|_{\gamma}^{4}.

The norm given in (
2.4
) and the value at
1
given in (
2.1
) yields

(2.9)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|c_{n}|^{2N}

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{2N}\frac{\Gamma(1+N\gamma)^{2}}{\Gamma(1+\gamma)^{2N}}\sum_{\lambda}\frac{c_{\lambda}}{c^{\prime}_{\lambda}}\frac{c_{\lambda+n}}{c^{\prime}_{\lambda+n}}\frac{c^{{}^{\prime}2}_{\lambda+n}}{[1+(N-1)\gamma]_{\lambda+n}^{2}}\frac{\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[N\gamma}}\right]_{\lambda}}{c_{\lambda}}\frac{\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[N\gamma}}\right]_{\lambda+n}}{c_{\lambda+n}}

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{2N}\frac{\Gamma(1+N\gamma)^{2}}{\Gamma(1+\gamma)^{2N}}\sum_{\lambda}\frac{c^{\prime}_{\lambda+n}}{c^{\prime}_{\lambda}}\frac{\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[N\gamma}}\right]_{\lambda}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[N\gamma}}\right]_{\lambda+n}}{\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[1+(N-1)\gamma}}\right]_{\lambda+n}^{2}}.

Adding a rectangle.
The Young diagram of
\lambda+n
is obtained from that of
\lambda
by attaching a
N\times n
rectangle on the left. This yields the explicit product formulas:

(2.11)

c_{\lambda+n}=c_{\lambda}\,\prod_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\Gamma(\gamma(N-i+1)+\lambda_{i}+n)}{\Gamma(\gamma(N-i+1)+\lambda_{i})},

(2.12)

c^{\prime}_{\lambda+n}=c^{\prime}_{\lambda}\,\prod_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\Gamma(\gamma(N-i+1)+\lambda_{i}+n+1-\gamma)}{\Gamma(\gamma(N-i+1)+\lambda_{i}+1-\gamma)}.

Moreover, the generalized Pochhammer symbol admits a representation in terms of
\Gamma

[b]^{(\gamma)}_{\lambda}=\prod_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\Gamma\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(b-(i-1)\gamma+\lambda_{i}}}\right)}{\Gamma\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(b-(i-1)\gamma}}\right)}.

Thus the second term in the series (
2.10
) is equal to

(2.13)

\displaystyle\frac{[N\gamma]_{\lambda}\,[N\gamma]_{\lambda+n}}{[1+(N-1)\gamma]^{2}_{\lambda+n}}

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\Gamma((N-i+1)\gamma+\lambda_{i})}{\Gamma((N-i+1)\gamma)}\;\prod_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\Gamma((N-i+1)\gamma+\lambda_{i}+n)}{\Gamma((N-i+1)\gamma)}

(2.14)

\displaystyle\;\times\prod_{i=1}^{N}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{\Gamma(1+(N-i)\gamma+\lambda_{i}+n)}{\Gamma(1+(N-i)\gamma)}}}\right)^{-2}.

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|c_{n}|^{2N}

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{2N}\frac{\Gamma(1+N\gamma)^{2}}{\Gamma(1+\gamma)^{2N}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{(N-1)!\,\gamma^{N-1}}{\Gamma(N\gamma)}}}\right)^{2}

where we used
\Gamma(z+1)=z\Gamma(z)
in the second line.

In the next subsection, we estimate the asymptotic behavior of this last expression.

2.3.
Asymptotics of the moments

It will be more convenient to write the above summand by introducing

F(x)=\frac{\Gamma(x)}{\Gamma(x+1-\gamma)},

G_{\lambda}(n)=\prod_{i=1}^{N}F\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left((N-i+1)\gamma+\lambda_{i}}}\right)F\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left((N-i+1)\gamma+\lambda_{i}+n}}\right),

S(n)=\sum_{\lambda}G_{\lambda}(n).

This yields the following expression for the moment:

\mathbb{E}|c_{n}|^{2N}=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{2\pi}{\Gamma(\gamma)}}}\right)^{2N}(N!)^{2}\,S(n).

Proposition 2.1
.

As
n
tends to infinity,

S(n)\sim\frac{1}{N!}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{x^{1-\gamma}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(1+x}}\right)^{1-\gamma}}}}\right)^{N}\,\frac{1}{n^{N(1-2\gamma)}}.

Proof.

Lower bound.

Using Wendel’s inequality
F(x)>x^{\gamma-1}
valid for all
x>0
, one gets the lower bound

\displaystyle S(n)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{\lambda}\prod_{i=1}^{N}\frac{1}{\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(N-i+1}}\right)\gamma+\lambda_{i}}}\right)^{1-\gamma}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(N-i+1}}\right)\gamma+\lambda_{i}+n}}\right)^{1-\gamma}}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{\lambda_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{N}\geq 0}\prod_{i=1}^{N}\frac{1}{\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(N\gamma+\lambda_{i}}}\right)^{1-\gamma}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(N\gamma+\lambda_{i}+n}}\right)^{1-\gamma}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{N!}\sum_{\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{N}\geq 0}\prod_{i=1}^{N}\frac{1}{\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(N\gamma+\lambda_{i}}}\right)^{1-\gamma}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(N\gamma+\lambda_{i}+n}}\right)^{1-\gamma}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N!}\;\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(N\gamma+k}}\right)^{1-\gamma}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(N\gamma+k+n}}\right)^{1-\gamma}}}}\right)^{N}.

To analyze the series above, it is convenient to factor out the scaling in
n
and then recognize a Riemann sum:

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(N\gamma+k}}\right)^{1-\gamma}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(N\gamma+k+n}}\right)^{1-\gamma}}=\frac{1}{n^{1-2\gamma}}\;\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{N\gamma+k}{n}}}\right)^{1-\gamma}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(1+\frac{N\gamma+k}{n}}}\right)^{1-\gamma}}.

Since the function
x\mapsto x^{-(1-\gamma)}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(1+x}}\right)^{-(1-\gamma)}
is decreasing and integrable on
(0,\infty)
, a series-integral comparison yields

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(N\gamma+k}}\right)^{1-\gamma}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(N\gamma+k+n}}\right)^{1-\gamma}}\sim\frac{1}{n^{1-2\gamma}}\;\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{x^{1-\gamma}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(1+x}}\right)^{1-\gamma}},\qquad\text{as }n\to\infty.

In particular, we obtain the lower bound

(2.15)

\liminf_{n\to\infty}n^{N(1-2\gamma)}\,S(n)\geq\frac{1}{N!}\;\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{x^{1-\gamma}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(1+x}}\right)^{1-\gamma}}}}\right)^{N}.

Upper bound.

The upper bound needs a bit more care. Split the sum the following way

S(n)\;=\;S_{1}^{\varepsilon}(n)\;+\;S_{2}^{\varepsilon}(n)\;+\;S_{3}^{\varepsilon}(n),

where
S_{1}^{\varepsilon}(n)
sums over strictly decreasing partitions with
\lambda_{1}>\cdots>\lambda_{N}>\varepsilon n
,
S_{2}^{\varepsilon}(n)
collects those with at least one equality
\lambda_{i}=\lambda_{i+1}
and
\lambda_{N}>\varepsilon n
, and
S_{3}^{\varepsilon}(n)
sums over
\lambda
such that
\lambda_{N}\leq\varepsilon n
. We prove that
S_{2}^{\varepsilon}(n)
and
S_{3}^{\varepsilon}(n)
are negligible and
S_{1}^{\varepsilon}(n)
produces the correct leading order.

For the second term, note that
\lambda_{i}=\lambda_{j}
implies that all the
\lambda_{k}
between
i
and
j
are equal. If
\Lambda_{i}=\{\lambda\in\mathrm{P}_{n}\text{ such that }\lambda_{i}=\lambda_{i+1}\}
, one gets the following upper bound

\displaystyle S_{2}^{\varepsilon}(n)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{i}}G_{\lambda}(n).

And the terms can be controlled using the elementary bound
F(x)\leq C\,x^{\gamma-1}
, valid for
x\geq\gamma
:

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{i}}G_{\lambda}(n)

\displaystyle\leq C^{2N}\sum_{\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{i},\lambda_{i+2},\ldots,\lambda_{N}\geq 0}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\prod_{k\neq i,i+1}\frac{1}{\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\gamma+\lambda_{k}}}\right)^{1-\gamma}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\gamma+\lambda_{k}+n}}\right)^{1-\gamma}}}}\right)\frac{1}{\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\gamma+\lambda_{i}}}\right)^{2-2\gamma}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\gamma+\lambda_{i}+n}}\right)^{2-2\gamma}}

\displaystyle\leq C^{2N}\frac{1}{n^{(N-2)(1-2\gamma)}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{\gamma+k}{n}}}\right)^{1-\gamma}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(1+\frac{\gamma+k}{n}}}\right)^{1-\gamma}}}}\right)^{N-2}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\gamma+k}}\right)^{2-2\gamma}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\gamma+k+n}}\right)^{2-2\gamma}}.

Since
\gamma<\frac{1}{2}
, the last series is

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\gamma+k}}\right)^{2-2\gamma}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\gamma+k+n}}\right)^{2-2\gamma}}\leq\frac{1}{n^{2-2\gamma}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\gamma+k}}\right)^{2-2\gamma}}.

Hence the whole contribution is
o\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(n^{-N(1-2\gamma)}}}\right)
as
n\to\infty
and
summing over
i=1,\ldots,N-1
yields
S_{2}(n)=o\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(n^{-N(1-2\gamma)}}}\right)
.
Using the same upper bound, one gets

\displaystyle S_{3}^{\varepsilon}(n)

\displaystyle\leq C^{2N}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{N}\geq 0\\
\lambda_{N}<\varepsilon n\end{subarray}}\prod_{i=1}^{N}\frac{1}{\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\gamma+\lambda_{i}}}\right)^{1-\gamma}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\gamma+\lambda_{i}+n}}\right)^{1-\gamma}}

\displaystyle\leq C^{2N}\,\frac{1}{n^{N(1-2\gamma)}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{\gamma+k}{n}}}\right)^{1-\gamma}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(1+\frac{\gamma+k}{n}}}\right)^{1-\gamma}}}}\right)^{N-1}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor\varepsilon n\rfloor}\frac{1}{\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{\gamma+k}{n}}}\right)^{1-\gamma}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(1+\frac{\gamma+k}{n}}}\right)^{1-\gamma}}}}\right).

Passing to the limit
n\to\infty
, the last factor converges to

\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}x^{-(1-\gamma)}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(1+x}}\right)^{-(1-\gamma)}\,\mathrm{d}x
, which vanishes as
\varepsilon\to 0
.
Hence,

\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0}\limsup\limits_{n\to\infty}n^{N(1-2\gamma)}S_{3}^{\varepsilon}(n)=0.

Turning to the main term
S_{1}^{\varepsilon}(n)
, we use the existence of a constant
B(A)
such that
F(x)\leq\frac{B(A)}{x^{1-\gamma}}
for
x>A
, with
B(A)\to 1
as
A\to\infty
. Thus

\displaystyle S_{1}^{\varepsilon}(n)

\displaystyle\leq B(\varepsilon n)^{2N}\sum_{\lambda_{1}>,\ldots,\lambda_{N}>\varepsilon n}\prod_{i=1}^{N}\frac{1}{\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\gamma+\lambda_{i}}}\right)^{1-\gamma}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\gamma+\lambda_{i}+n}}\right)^{1-\gamma}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{B(\varepsilon n)^{2N}}{N!}\sum_{\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{N}\geq 0}\prod_{i=1}^{N}\frac{1}{\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\gamma+\lambda_{i}}}\right)^{1-\gamma}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\gamma+\lambda_{i}+n}}\right)^{1-\gamma}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{B(\varepsilon n)^{2N}}{N!}\,\frac{1}{n^{N(1-2\gamma)}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{\gamma+k}{n}}}\right)^{1-\gamma}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(1+\frac{\gamma+k}{n}}}\right)^{1-\gamma}}}}\right)^{N},

which, as
n\to\infty
, is equivalent to

\frac{1}{N!}\,n^{-N(1-2\gamma)}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{-(1-\gamma)}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(1+x}}\right)^{-(1-\gamma)}\,\mathrm{d}x}}\right)^{N}
.
Combining the three pieces with (
2.15
), we find

(2.16)

\lim_{n\to\infty}n^{N(1-2\gamma)}\,S(n)=\frac{1}{N!}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{x^{1-\gamma}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(1+x}}\right)^{1-\gamma}}}}\right)^{N}.\\

∎

Applying the change of variables
t=\dfrac{x}{1+x}
to (
2.16
) yields

\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{x^{1-\gamma}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(1+x}}\right)^{1-\gamma}}=\int_{0}^{1}t^{\gamma-1}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(1-t}}\right)^{-2\gamma}\,\mathrm{d}t=B\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\gamma,\,1-2\gamma}}\right)=\frac{\Gamma\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\gamma}}\right)\,\Gamma\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(1-2\gamma}}\right)}{\Gamma\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(1-\gamma}}\right)}.

Recalling that

\mathbb{E}|c_{n}|^{2N}=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{2\pi}{\Gamma\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\gamma}}\right)}}}\right)^{2N}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(N!}}\right)^{2}\,S(n),

we obtain the asymptotic

\mathbb{E}|c_{n}|^{2N}\;\sim\;\frac{N!\,C_{\gamma}(N)}{n^{N(1-2\gamma)}}\quad\text{with}\quad C_{\gamma}(N)=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{2\pi}{\Gamma\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\gamma}}\right)}}}\right)^{2N}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{\Gamma\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\gamma}}\right)\,\Gamma\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(1-2\gamma}}\right)}{\Gamma\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(1-\gamma}}\right)}}}\right)^{N}.

And using Euler’s reflection formula
\Gamma\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(z}}\right)\Gamma\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(1-z}}\right)=\dfrac{\pi}{\sin\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\pi z}}\right)}
,
this constant can be rewritten as

C_{\gamma}(N)=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(4\pi\,\Gamma\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(1-2\gamma}}\right)\,\sin\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\pi\gamma}}\right)}}\right)^{N}=\kappa(\beta)^{N}.

Remark 2.2
.

It is instructive to verify the case
N=1
directly:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|c_{n}|^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{[0,2\pi]^{2}}\frac{e^{\mathrm{i}n\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\theta-\theta^{\prime}}}\right)}}{|e^{\mathrm{i}\theta}-e^{\mathrm{i}\theta^{\prime}}|^{2\gamma}}\,\,\mathrm{d}\theta\,\,\mathrm{d}\theta^{\prime}

\displaystyle=2\pi\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{e^{\mathrm{i}nu}}{\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|e^{\mathrm{i}u}-1}}\right|^{2\gamma}}\,\mathrm{d}u

\displaystyle\sim\frac{2\pi}{n^{1-2\gamma}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{e^{\mathrm{i}t}}{|t|^{2\gamma}}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\frac{4\pi\,\Gamma\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(1-2\gamma}}\right)\,\sin{\pi\gamma}}{n^{1-2\gamma}},

which matches the general expression above.

Therefore, upon defining
Z_{n}\coloneqq n^{\tfrac{1}{2}-\gamma}\,c_{n}
, we have established the convergence of even moments

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|Z_{n}}}\right|^{2N}\;=\;\mathbb{E}|\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{C}}(0,\kappa(\beta))|^{2N},

and, by isotropy of
c_{n}
, this proves the first part of Theorem
1.2
. Theorem
1.1
is then a consequence of the convergence of the moments and the Borel-Cantelli lemma.

Convergence of the process

We now turn to the study of mixed moments of the Fourier coefficients

M_{n}(\ell,m)\coloneqq\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\prod_{j=0}^{k}c_{n+j}^{\ell_{j}}\,\overline{c_{n+j}}^{m_{j}}}}\right].

We first treat the modulus case and then address the general mixed moment in Section
3.5
.
This analysis requires additional properties of Jack polynomials, which we now collect.

3.1.
Preliminary results

Pieri formula.

The Pieri formula gives the explicit decomposition of the product
e_{p}P_{\lambda}
on the Jack polynomial basis, where
e_{p}
is the elementary symmetric polynomial of degree
p
. It takes the following form

(3.1)

e_{p}\,P_{\mu}=\sum_{\tau\;:\;\tau/\mu\text{ vertical strip }p}\psi^{\prime}_{\tau/\mu}\,P_{\tau},

If
\tau
and
\mu
are partitions such that
\tau\supset\mu
, let
C_{\tau/\mu}
(resp.
R_{\tau/\mu}
) denote the union of the columns (resp. rows) that intersect
\tau-\mu
.
The coefficient
\psi^{\prime}_{\tau/\mu}
, which we call the
Pieri coefficient
, is given by

(3.2)

\psi^{\prime}_{\tau/\mu}\,=\prod_{s\in C_{\tau/\mu}-R_{\tau/\mu}}\frac{b_{\tau}(s)}{b_{\mu}(s)},

where

(3.3)

b_{\lambda}(s)=\frac{c_{\lambda}(s)}{c^{\prime}_{\lambda}(s)}=\frac{a_{\lambda}(s)+\gamma\,l_{\lambda}(s)+\gamma}{a_{\lambda}(s)+\gamma\,l_{\lambda}(s)+1}.

There are in fact four Pieri formulas but we will only need this particular one in the sequel. For more details on those formulas, one can refer to
[
15
, p340]
.

Definition 3.1
.

If
\lambda
is a partition, let
\Delta\lambda_{i}=\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{i+1}
, with the convention
\lambda_{N+1}=0
. The gap of the partition is defined as

\mathrm{gap}(\lambda)=\min_{i\leq N}\Delta\lambda_{i}.

Definition 3.2
.

For
N_{1},\cdots,N_{k}\in\mathbb{N}
, an
(N_{1},\dots,N_{k})
-shape
of height
N

\sigma
is a vector

\sigma=(\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{N})\in\{0,1,\dots,k\}^{N}
such that

|\{i:\;\sigma_{i}=r\}|=N_{r},\qquad r=1,\dots,k.

Given such
\sigma
and a partition
\lambda

satisfying
\mathrm{gap}(\lambda)\geq k
, we can
define a new partition
\nu=\lambda+\sigma
by

\nu_{i}\coloneqq\lambda_{i}+\sigma_{i},\qquad i=1,\dots,N.

In this case, we say that the skew diagram
\nu/\lambda

has shape
\sigma
. And
\nu
is obtained from
\lambda
by
adding an
(N_{1},\dots,N_{k})
-shape means that

\nu=\lambda+\sigma
for some
(N_{1},\dots,N_{k})
-shape
\sigma
.

Figure 1.
An example of partition
\nu=\lambda+\sigma
obtained by adding
\sigma
of shape
N_{1}=3
and
N_{2}=2
.

The two following lemmas give the estimates of the Pieri coefficients in the regime where the spacings between the parts of
\lambda
become large

(3.4)

\mathrm{gap}(\lambda)\longrightarrow+\infty.

We call this regime the
large gap regime
.

Lemma 3.3
.

If
\sigma
is a shape of height
N
, the Pieri coefficients satisfy the following properties:

\psi^{\prime}_{\lambda+n+\sigma/\lambda+n}=\psi^{\prime}_{\lambda+\sigma/\lambda},

and

\psi^{\prime}_{\lambda+\sigma/\lambda}=1+O\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{1}{\mathrm{gap}(\lambda)}}}\right).

Proof.

The first equality is a consequence of the shift property (
2.7
). For the second one, we use the formulas (
3.2
) and (
3.3
) to get

\psi^{\prime}_{\lambda+\sigma/\lambda}\,=\prod_{s\in C_{\lambda+\sigma/\lambda}-R_{\lambda+\sigma/\lambda}}\frac{c_{\lambda+\sigma}(s)}{c^{\prime}_{\lambda+\sigma}(s)}\,\frac{c^{\prime}_{\lambda}(s)}{c_{\lambda}(s)}.

First, note that the product has at most
(N-1)|\sigma|
terms. So at fixed shape
\sigma
, it is sufficient to prove that each term is
1+O\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{1}{\mathrm{gap}(\lambda)}}}\right)
. And

\displaystyle\frac{c_{\lambda+\sigma}(s)}{c^{\prime}_{\lambda+\sigma}(s)}\,\frac{c^{\prime}_{\lambda}(s)}{c_{\lambda}(s)}

\displaystyle=\frac{a_{\lambda+\sigma}(s)+\gamma l_{\lambda+\sigma}(s)+\gamma}{a_{\lambda+\sigma}(s)+\gamma l_{\lambda+\sigma}(s)+1}\;\frac{a_{\lambda}(s)+\gamma l_{\lambda}(s)+1}{a_{\lambda}(s)+\gamma l_{\lambda}(s)+\gamma}

\displaystyle=\frac{a_{\lambda}(s)+\gamma(l_{\lambda}(s)+1)+\gamma}{a_{\lambda}(s)+\gamma(l_{\lambda}(s)+1)+1}\;\frac{a_{\lambda}(s)+\gamma l_{\lambda}(s)+1}{a_{\lambda}(s)+\gamma l_{\lambda}(s)+\gamma}

\displaystyle=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(1-\frac{1-\gamma}{a_{\lambda}(s)+\gamma(l_{\lambda}(s)+1)+1}}}\right)\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(1+\frac{1-\gamma}{a_{\lambda}(s)+\gamma l_{\lambda}(s)+\gamma}}}\right)

\displaystyle=1+O\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{1}{\mathrm{gap}(\lambda)}}}\right).

∎

Similarly, in the large gap regime, one has the following equivalent for ratios of
c
’s.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\sigma
be a fixed shape, then we have

\frac{c^{\prime}_{\lambda+\sigma}}{c^{\prime}_{\lambda}}\,\frac{c_{\lambda}}{c_{\lambda+\sigma}}=1+O\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{1}{\mathrm{gap}(\lambda)}}}\right).

Proof.

By decomposing the shape
0\subset\sigma^{(1)}\subset\cdots\subset\sigma^{(|\sigma|)}=\sigma
, one sees that it is sufficient to prove the result for a shape
\sigma
of size
1
,
ie
just adding one square at a row
i\in[N]
. In this particular case, the only affected cells in the new coefficient
c_{\lambda+\sigma}
are those in
C_{\lambda+\sigma/\lambda}\cup R_{\lambda+\sigma/\lambda}
, which are those in row
i
and above the extra cell. This way,

\displaystyle\frac{c_{\lambda+\sigma}}{c_{\lambda}}

\displaystyle=\prod_{s\in C_{\lambda+\sigma/\lambda}\cup R_{\lambda+\sigma/\lambda}}\frac{c_{\lambda+\sigma}(s)}{c_{\lambda}(s)}

\displaystyle=\gamma\;\prod_{s\in C_{\lambda+\sigma/\lambda}-\{\sigma\}}\frac{c_{\lambda+\sigma}(s)}{c_{\lambda}(s)}\;\prod_{s\in R_{\lambda+\sigma/\lambda}-\{\sigma\}}\frac{c_{\lambda+\sigma}(s)}{c_{\lambda}(s)}

\displaystyle=\gamma\;\prod_{s\in C_{\lambda+\sigma/\lambda}-\{\sigma\}}\frac{c_{\lambda}(s)+\gamma}{c_{\lambda}(s)}\;\prod_{s\in R_{\lambda+\sigma/\lambda}-\{\sigma\}}\frac{c_{\lambda}(s)+1}{c_{\lambda}(s)}

\displaystyle=\gamma\;\prod_{s\in C_{\lambda+\sigma/\lambda}-\{\sigma\}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(1+O\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{1}{\mathrm{gap}(\lambda)}}}\right)}}\right)\;\prod_{s\in R_{\lambda+\sigma/\lambda}-\{\sigma\}}\frac{c_{\lambda}(s)+1}{c_{\lambda}(s)}.

The last term requires a bit more care due to the hook-lengths. We cut the row
i
in parts with fixed hook-length:

\lambda_{i}+1:
1\;\cdots\;\lambda_{N}
\lambda_{N}\!+\!1\;\cdots\;\lambda_{N-1}
\cdots
\lambda_{i+1}\;\!+\!1\cdots\;\lambda_{i}
\sigma

One ends up with

\displaystyle\prod_{s\in R_{\lambda+\sigma/\lambda}-\{\sigma\}}\frac{c_{\lambda}(s)+1}{c_{\lambda}(s)}

\displaystyle=\prod_{k=0}^{N-i}\prod_{j=\lambda_{N-k+1}+1}^{\lambda_{N-k}}\frac{\lambda_{i}-j+\gamma(N-i-k)+\gamma+1}{\lambda_{i}-j+\gamma(N-i-k)+\gamma}

\displaystyle=\prod_{k=0}^{N-i}\frac{\lambda_{i}-(\lambda_{N-k+1}+1)+\gamma(N-i-k)+\gamma+1}{\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{N-k}+\gamma(N-i-k)+\gamma}.

Therefore

\displaystyle\frac{c_{\lambda+\sigma}}{c_{\lambda}}\,\frac{c^{\prime}_{\lambda}}{c^{\prime}_{\lambda+\sigma}}

\displaystyle=\gamma\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(1+O\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{1}{\mathrm{gap}(\lambda)}}}\right)}}\right)\times

\displaystyle\prod_{k=0}^{N-i}\frac{\lambda_{i}-(\lambda_{N-k+1}+1)+\gamma(N-i-k)+\gamma+1}{\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{N-k}+\gamma(N-i-k)+\gamma}\frac{\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{N-k}+\gamma(N-i-k)+1}{\lambda_{i}-(\lambda_{N-k+1}+1)+\gamma(N-i-k)+1+1}

\displaystyle=\gamma\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(1+O\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{1}{\mathrm{gap}(\lambda)}}}\right)}}\right)\,\frac{1}{\gamma}\,\frac{\Delta\lambda_{i}+\gamma}{\Delta\lambda_{i}+1}

\displaystyle\prod_{k=0}^{N-i-1}\frac{\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{N-k+1}+\gamma(N-i-k)+\gamma}{\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{N-k+1}+\gamma(N-i-k)+1}\;\frac{\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{N-k}+\gamma(N-i-k)+1}{\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{N-k}+\gamma(N-i-k)+\gamma}

\displaystyle=1+O\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{1}{\mathrm{gap}(\lambda)}}}\right).

∎

We will need the following result, which gives asymptotic equivalents for the series appearing later on..

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
G_{\lambda}(n)=\prod_{i=1}^{N}F((N-i+1)\gamma+\lambda_{i})F((N-i+1)\gamma+\lambda_{i}+n)
and
a_{\lambda}(n)
be a bounded sequence such that

a_{\lambda}(n)\rightarrow 1,\quad\text{ as }\mathrm{gap}(\lambda)\rightarrow\infty,\quad\text{uniformly in }n.

Then,

\sum_{\lambda}a_{\lambda}(n)G_{\lambda}(n)\underset{n\to\infty}{\sim}\sum_{\lambda}G_{\lambda}(n).

Proof.

Simply write

\displaystyle\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\sum_{\lambda}a_{\lambda}(n)G_{\lambda}(n)-\sum_{\lambda}G_{\lambda}(n)}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\exists i:\Delta\lambda_{i}\leq n\varepsilon}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|a_{\lambda}(n)-1}}\right|G_{\lambda}(n)+\sum_{\mathrm{gap}(\lambda)>n\varepsilon}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|a_{\lambda}(n)-1}}\right|G_{\lambda}(n)

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\,\sum_{\exists i:\Delta\lambda_{i}\leq n\varepsilon}G_{\lambda}(n)+\sup\limits_{\mathrm{gap}(\lambda)>n\varepsilon}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|a_{\lambda}(n)-1}}\right|\,\sum_{\lambda}G_{\lambda}(n)

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}\,n^{-N(1-2\gamma)}\int_{\exists i:\Delta x_{i}<\varepsilon}\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{\prod_{j}x_{j}^{1-\gamma}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(1+x_{j}}}\right)^{1-\gamma}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\sup\limits_{\mathrm{gap}(\lambda)>n\varepsilon}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|a_{\lambda}(n)-1}}\right|\,\sum_{\lambda}G_{\lambda}(n),

and taking
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
yields, as
n\rightarrow+\infty
,

\sum_{\lambda}a_{\lambda}(n)G_{\lambda}(n)=\sum_{\lambda}G_{\lambda}(n)+o\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{1}{n^{N(1-2\gamma)}}}}\right).

∎

Finally, for notational convenience, we will use the following

(3.5)

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\|P_{\lambda}}}\right\|_{\gamma}^{2}=K(N,\gamma)\,C(N,\gamma)\,\frac{c^{\prime}_{\lambda}}{c_{\lambda}}\,\prod_{i=1}^{N}\mathrm{F}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left((N-i+1)\gamma+\lambda_{i}}}\right),

with
K(N,\gamma)=\frac{\Gamma(1+N\gamma)}{\Gamma(1+\gamma)^{N}}
and

C(N,\gamma)=\frac{\gamma^{N-1}(N-1)!}{\Gamma(N\gamma)}
.

3.2.
Joint convergence :
k=1

Fix
N\geq 1
and
0\leq p\leq N
and consider

\mathbb{E}|c_{n}|^{2(N-p)}|c_{n+1}|^{2p}.

The same arguments as in section
2.2
lead to the following expression for the joint moment:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|c_{n}|^{2(N-p)}|c_{n+1}|^{2p}

\displaystyle=\int_{[0,2\pi]^{2N}}e^{\mathrm{i}n\sum_{i\leq N}(\theta_{i}-\theta_{i}^{\prime})}e^{\mathrm{i}\sum_{i\leq p}(\theta_{i}-\theta_{i}^{\prime})}\prod_{i<j}|e^{\mathrm{i}\theta_{i}}-e^{\mathrm{i}\theta_{j}}|^{2\gamma}\,|e^{\mathrm{i}\theta^{\prime}_{i}}-e^{\mathrm{i}\theta^{\prime}_{j}}|^{2\gamma}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\prod_{i,j}|e^{\mathrm{i}\theta_{i}}-e^{\mathrm{i}\theta^{\prime}_{j}}|^{-2\gamma}\,\,\mathrm{d}\theta\,\,\mathrm{d}\theta^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda,\nu}\frac{c_{\lambda}}{c^{\prime}_{\lambda}}\frac{c_{\nu}}{c^{\prime}_{\nu}}\,\int_{[0,2\pi]^{2N}}e^{\mathrm{i}\sum_{i\leq p}\theta_{i}}P_{\lambda+n}(e^{\mathrm{i}\theta})\,e^{-\mathrm{i}\sum_{i\leq p}\theta_{i}^{\prime}}P_{\lambda+n}(e^{-\mathrm{i}\theta^{\prime}})\,P_{\nu}(e^{-\mathrm{i}\theta})\,P_{\nu}(e^{\mathrm{i}\theta^{\prime}})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\prod_{i<j}|e^{\mathrm{i}\theta_{i}}-e^{\mathrm{i}\theta_{j}}|^{2\gamma}\,|e^{\mathrm{i}\theta^{\prime}_{i}}-e^{\mathrm{i}\theta^{\prime}_{j}}|^{2\gamma}\,\,\,\mathrm{d}\theta\,\,\mathrm{d}\theta^{\prime}

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{2N}\sum_{\lambda,\nu}\frac{c_{\lambda}}{c^{\prime}_{\lambda}}\frac{c_{\nu}}{c^{\prime}_{\nu}}\,\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\langle x_{1}\cdots x_{p}\,P_{\lambda+n},\,P_{\nu}}}\right\rangle_{\gamma}}}\right|^{2},

where
x_{1},\dots,x_{p}
are the
p

where

is the lexicographic order. Now, let

4.
Convergence towards complex white noise

We now prove Theorem
1.3
. Fix
s<-\frac{1}{2}
and choose
s^{\prime}
such that
s<s^{\prime}<-\frac{1}{2}
.
Define the rescaled random distributions

X_{n}\coloneqq n^{\frac{1-\beta^{2}}{2}}\,e^{\mathrm{i}n\theta}\,\mathrm{M}_{\mathrm{i}\beta}\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T}),

whose Fourier coefficients are

\widehat{X_{n}}(k)=\langle X_{n},e^{\mathrm{i}k\cdot}\rangle=n^{\frac{1-\beta^{2}}{2}}\,c_{n+k}.\\

Tightness in
H^{s}

We claim that

(4.1)

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\,\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\|X_{n}}}\right\|_{H^{s^{\prime}}}^{2}}}\right]<+\infty.

Indeed,

\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\|X_{n}}}\right\|_{H^{s^{\prime}}}^{2}}}\right]=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(1+k^{2}}}\right)^{s^{\prime}}\,\mathbb{E}|\widehat{X_{n}}(k)|^{2}=n^{1-\beta^{2}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(1+k^{2}}}\right)^{s^{\prime}}\,\mathbb{E}|c_{n+k}|^{2}.

Using the fact that for some
C>0
, one has

\mathbb{E}|c_{m}|^{2}\leq C\;(1+|m|)^{-(1-\beta^{2})},\qquad m\in\mathbb{Z},

and changing variables
m=n+k
gives

\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\|X_{n}}}\right\|_{H^{s^{\prime}}}^{2}}}\right]\leq C\;n^{1-\beta^{2}}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}(1+(m-n)^{2})^{s^{\prime}}(1+|m|)^{-(1-\beta^{2})}.

Splitting the sum into
|m|\geq n/2
and
|m|<n/2
yields, for some constant
C_{1}>0
,

\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\|X_{n}}}\right\|_{H^{s^{\prime}}}^{2}}}\right]\leq C_{1}\;\bigg(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}(1+j^{2})^{s^{\prime}}+n^{1+2s^{\prime}}\bigg),

which is uniformly bounded since
s^{\prime}<-1/2
. This proves (
4.1
).
Since the embedding
H^{s^{\prime}}(\mathbb{T})\hookrightarrow H^{s}(\mathbb{T})
is compact for
s<s^{\prime}
,
we conclude that
(X_{n})
is tight in
H^{s}(\mathbb{T})
.

Identification of the limit.

Let
\phi(\theta)=\sum_{|k|\leq K}\widehat{\phi}(k)e^{\mathrm{i}k\theta}
be a trigonometric polynomial, one has

\langle X_{n},\phi\rangle=n^{\frac{1-\beta^{2}}{2}}\sum_{|k|\leq K}\widehat{\phi}(k)\,c_{n+k}.

By the (process) statement in Theorem
1.2
, we have the convergence in distribution

n^{\frac{1-\beta^{2}}{2}}(c_{n-K},\ldots,c_{n+K})\longrightarrow(\xi_{-K},\ldots,\xi_{K}),

where
(\xi_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
are i.i.d. complex Gaussians
\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{C}}(0,\kappa(\beta))
.
Therefore, in distribution,

\langle X_{n},\phi\rangle\longrightarrow\sum_{|k|\leq K}\widehat{\phi}(k)\,\xi_{k}.

Define the limiting random distribution

W\coloneqq\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\xi_{k}\,e^{\mathrm{i}k\theta},

which lives in
H^{s}(\mathbb{T})
since

\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\|W}}\right\|_{H^{s}}^{2}}}\right]=\kappa(\beta)\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}(1+k^{2})^{s}<+\infty.

We have
\langle X_{n},\phi\rangle\longrightarrow\langle W,\phi\rangle
in distribution.

Combining these two results yields Theorem
1.3
.

Partitions and Young diagrams

A partition is a finite non-increasing sequence of nonnegative integers

\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots)
with
\lambda_{i}=0
for
i
large enough.
Its size is
|\lambda|\coloneqq\sum_{i\geq 1}\lambda_{i}
, and its length

\ell(\lambda)
is the number of positive parts. In the present work, we identify partitions of length
\ell(\lambda)\leq N
with non-increasing sequences of
N

\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{N})
.

The Young diagram of
\lambda
is the set of boxes

\{(i,j)\in\mathbb{N}^{2}:1\leq i\leq\ell(\lambda)\text{ and }1\leq j\leq\lambda_{i}\},

with rows indexed from top to bottom and columns from left to right.
The conjugate partition
\lambda^{\prime}
is defined by
\lambda^{\prime}_{j}=|\{\,i:\lambda_{i}\geq j\,\}|
and corresponds to reflecting the diagram across the main diagonal.

For a cell
s=(i,j)
in the diagram, the arm-length and leg-length are defined by

a_{\lambda}(s)\coloneqq\lambda_{i}-j,\qquad l_{\lambda}(s)\coloneqq\lambda^{\prime}_{j}-i.

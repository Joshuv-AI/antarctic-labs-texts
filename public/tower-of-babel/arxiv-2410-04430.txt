Global Coherence in Quantum Discord as a Resource
Chellasamy Jebarathinam, Huan-Yu Ku, Hao-Chung Cheng, Hsi-Sheng Goan

Abstract
This work addresses which aspect of bipartite coherence in quantum discord is essential for genuinely quantum correlation . To this end, global coherence of bipartite states is defined as a form of bipartite coherence that is not local coherence in either of the subsystems or in both subsystems alone, and we identify it as being indicated by a witness of discord. With global coherence as a resource , any local operations of the form Φ A ⊗ Φ B \Phi_{A}\otimes\Phi_{B} , which may create coherence locally, are free operations . This implies that with global coherence as a resource for operational tasks, any local operations can be freely used, but require classical randomness not to be freely available. Using this identification, we demonstrate that global coherence is a necessary resource for the task of semi-device-independent steerability of quantum discord. On the other hand, for the task of remote state preparation using quantum discord in two-qubit states, a necessary and sufficient quantum resource is identified by invoking a witness of global coherence .

Preliminaries

2.1
Quantum discord

Quantum discord captures a different form of quantumness in bipartite states that goes beyond quantum correlation due to entanglement
[
9
,
10
]
.
In the following, we present the definition of quantum discord from Alice to Bob
D^{\rightarrow}(\rho_{AB})
. Before that, we introduce the definition of the classical correlation
C^{\rightarrow}(\rho_{AB})
used in defining quantum discord. It is defined with respect to a measurement described by a positive operator-valued measure (POVM),
\{M^{A}_{i}\}
, performed on subsystem
A
. In such an asymmetric measurement scenario, we denote the probability of obtaining the outcome
i
on the measured subsystem as
p_{i}=\tr\{\rho_{AB}M^{A}_{i}\otimes\mathbb{I}^{B}\}
and the corresponding post-measurement state for the subsystem
B
as
\rho^{B}_{i}=\frac{1}{p_{i}}\tr_{A}\{\rho_{AB}M^{A}_{i}\otimes\mathbb{I}_{B}\}
. Classical correlation
C^{\rightarrow}(\rho_{AB})
for local measurements in subsystem
A
is defined as the optimal amount of correlation in the bipartite state as

C^{\rightarrow}(\rho_{AB}):=S(\rho_{B})-\min_{\{M^{A}_{i}\}}\left(\sum_{i}p_{i}S(\rho^{B}_{i})\right),

(1)

where
S(\rho)
is the Von Neumann entropy of a quantum state
\rho
, defined as
S(\rho)=-\tr{\rho\log_2\rho}
.
Quantum discord is then defined as

\displaystyle D^{\rightarrow}(\rho_{AB}):=I(\rho_{AB})-C^{\rightarrow}(\rho_{AB}).

(2)

Here
I(\rho_{AB})=S(\rho_{A})+S(\rho_{B})-S(\rho_{AB})

is the quantum mutual information and can be interpreted as the total correlations in
\rho_{AB}
.

The quantum discord from Alice to Bob
D^{\rightarrow}(\rho_{AB})
vanishes if and only if
\rho_{AB}
can be decomposed in classical-quantum (CQ) form,

\displaystyle\rho_{\texttt{CQ}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}q_{i}\ket{i}\bra{i}^{A}\otimes\rho_{i}^{B},

(3)

where
\{\ket{i}\}
forms an orthonormal basis in Alice’s Hilbert space,
\rho_{i}^{B}
are any quantum states on Bob’s Hilbert space, and
q_{i}
’s are probabilities. On the other hand, quantum discord from Bob to Alice
D^{\leftarrow}(\rho_{AB})
is defined with respect to local quantum measurements on Bob’s subsystem.
D^{\leftarrow}(\rho_{AB})
vanishes for a given
\rho_{AB}
if and only if it is a quantum-classical (QC) state which has a form as in Eq. (
3
) with the subsystems
A
and
B
permuted. The states with zero discord both ways are called a classically correlated (CC) state, given by

\rho_{\rm CC}=\sum_{i,j}q_{ij}\outerproduct{i}{i}^{A}\otimes\outerproduct{j}{j}^{B},

(4)

where
\{\ket{j}\}
forms an orthonormal basis on Bob’s Hilbert space and
q_{ij}
’s are probabilities. The states that can be decomposed in the above form are incoherent with respect to the product basis
\{\ket{i}^{A}\otimes\ket{j}^{B}\}
.
Any bipartite state that is not a CC state is called a
discordant state
since it has a nonzero discord in at least one way.

Entanglement as a resource in quantum information science satisfies the requirement that the quantum resource cannot be generated locally from free states
[
16
]
. However, quantum discord as a resource does not satisfy this requirement, as quantum discord can be created locally from a CC state
[
26
,
29
,
30
,
25
,
22
]
.
Specifically, let
\Phi_{A}\otimes\Phi_{B}
be a local operation, where
\Phi_{A}
and
\Phi_{B}
are completely positive trace-preserving (CPTP) maps on subsystems
A
and
B
, respectively. Then, there are discordant states that can be created via such local operations on a CC state given by (
4
), with
q_{ij}=\delta_{ij}\tilde{q}_{i}
, i.e., the transformed state
(\Phi_{A}\otimes\Phi_{B})\rho_{\rm CC}
has a non-zero discord for a suitable choice of local operations. Such discordant states can always be written as
[
30
]

\rho_{AB}=\sum^{d_{\lambda}-1}_{\lambda=0}q_{\lambda}\rho^{A}_{\lambda}\otimes\rho^{B}_{\lambda},

(5)

with
d_{\lambda}\leq d_{\min}
, where
d_{\min}:=\min\{d_{A},d_{B}\}

[
30
]
, for a suitable choice of local operations.

To give an example of a local creation of discord, consider the two-qubit CC state given by

\rho_{AB}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\outerproduct{00}{00}+\outerproduct{11}{11}\right).

(6)

By applying the local operation
\Phi\otimes\Phi
, defined through
\Phi(\rho)=\outerproduct{0}{0}\rho\outerproduct{0}{0}+\outerproduct{+}{1}\rho\outerproduct{1}{+}
, where
\rho
is a qubit state, to both qubits, we get the discordant state given by,

\rho_{AB}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\outerproduct{00}{00}+\outerproduct{++}{++}\right).

(7)

To verify the discordant states that cannot be generated by a local operation as mentioned above, we introduce the witness of discord
[
26
]
, which is the rank of the correlation matrix of the given density matrix. This witness implies the discordant states, which do not have a local creation of the discord, as mentioned above. To introduce the witness, let us introduce the following decomposition of bipartite states
[
26
]
.
Given a bipartite state
\rho_{AB}
, it can always be decomposed as a sum of arbitrary bases of Hermitian operators
\{A_{i}\}
and
\{B_{i}\}
as

\rho_{AB}=\sum^{d^{2}_{A}}_{n=1}\sum^{d^{2}_{B}}_{m=1}r_{nm}A_{n}\otimes B_{m},

(8)

where
d_{A}
(
d_{B}
) is the dimension of the Hilbert space
A

(
B
). Using this representation, the correlation matrix
R=(r_{nm})
is defined, which can be rewritten using its singular value decomposition and cast in a diagonal representation as

\rho_{AB}=\sum^{L_{R}}_{n=1}c_{n}S_{n}\otimes F_{n}.

(9)

Here,
L_{R}
is the rank of
R
and quantifies how many product operators are needed to represent
\rho_{AB}
.
The value of
L_{R}
can be used to witness the presence of nonzero quantum discord
[
26
]
. For any CQ or QC state,
L_{R}
is upper bounded by the minimum dimension of the subsystems

d_{\min}

[
22
]
. On the other hand, in general,
the correlation rank is bounded by the square of
d_{\min}
:

L_{R}\leq d^{2}_{\min}
. Therefore, states with
L_{R}>d_{\min}
will necessarily be discordant since they are neither a CQ state nor a QC state. Note that, as shown in
[
30
]
, for quantum states whose discord can be created by local operations,
L_{R}\leq d_{\min}
.

2.2
Semi-device-independent steering

Let us consider a steering scenario where the steering party Alice performs a set of black-box (uncharacterized) measurements labeled
x
and obtains an outcome
a
to produce a set of conditional states on Bob’s side. This is a one-sided device-independent (1SDI) scenario, where Alice’s side is device-independent, i.e., her measurements are uncharacterized, and Bob’s side is trusted to perform state tomography or characterized measurements
[
31
]
. We consider the 1SDI scenario where Bob performs a set of characterized measurements labeled
y
on the conditional states prepared on his side and obtains an outcome
b
. In this context, a bipartite correlation
P(a,b|x,y)
, which is described by a set of conditional joint probabilities
\{p(a,b|x,y)\}_{a,x,b,y}
, is produced.

Quantum steering is demonstrated if the correlation
P(a,b|x,y)
does not have a
local hidden variable
-
local hidden state
(LHV-LHS) model
[
28
]
. In other words, quantum steering certifies entanglement in a 1SDI way
[
32
]
. Quantum steering can be witnessed through the violation of a steering inequality
[
33
]
. As an illustration of this, consider the two-setting steering scenario in which Alice performs two dichotomic black-box measurements, denoted by
A_{x}
, with
x=0,1
, and Bob performs two dichotomic qubit measurements. For this scenario, the linear steering inequality is given by

\braket{A_{0}\otimes B_{0}}+\braket{A_{1}\otimes B_{1}}\leq\sqrt{2},

(10)

where
B_{y}
, with
y=0,1
, are two of the three Pauli observables
\sigma_{i}
, with
i=x,y
and
z
and
\braket{A_{x}\otimes B_{y}}=\tr\{\rho_{AB}A_{x}\otimes B_{y}\}

[
33
]
. As an example of the violation of this inequality, for measurements
A_{0}=B_{0}=\sigma_{x}
and
A_{1}=B_{1}=\sigma_{z}
, the maximally entangled state of two qubits
\ket{\Phi^{+}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\ket{00}+\ket{11})
gives rise to the maximal violation, that is,
\braket{A_{0}\otimes B_{0}}+\braket{A_{1}\otimes B_{1}}=2>\sqrt{2}
.

One-sided semi-device-independent scenario
[
27
]
where the local Hilbert-space dimension
d_{A}
of the shared bipartite state
\rho_{AB}
in
\mathbb{C}^{d_{A}}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d_{B}}
producing the correlation
\{p(a,b|x,y)\}_{a,x,b,y}

\lambda

d_{\lambda}\leq d_{A}

Let us now consider a one-sided semi-device-independent (1SSDI) scenario, where the Hilbert space dimension of the steering party, say Alice, is trusted, as in Fig.
1
. This scenario has been considered here to define superunsteerability
[
36
]
. Suppose that an unsteerable bipartite correlation, which has an LHV-LHS model, is produced using a quantum state in
\mathbb{C}^{d_{A}}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d_{B}}
for the measurements described by
\{M^{A}_{a|x}\}_{a,x}
and
\{M^{B}_{b|y}\}_{b,y}
, which are a
POVM, i.e., for each
x
,
M^{A}_{a|x}\geq 0
and
\sum_{a}M^{A}_{a|x}=\mathbbm{1}_{d_{A}}
, where
\mathbbm{1}_{d_{A}}
is the identity operator acting on
\mathbb{C}^{d_{A}}
and similar conditions hold for Bob’s measurements for each
y
. Then, the LHV-LHS model of the correlation can be decomposed as follows:

p(a,b|x,y)=\sum^{d_{\lambda}-1}_{\lambda=0}p_{\lambda}p(a|x,\lambda)p(b|y;\rho_{\lambda})\quad\forall a,x,b,y,

(11)

where
d_{\lambda}
denotes the dimension of the finite shared randomness
\lambda
used to simulate the correlation.

\{p(a|x,\lambda)\}_{a,x}
in Eq. (
11
) is the set of arbitrary probability distributions
p(a|x,\lambda)
conditioned on shared randomness/hidden variable
\lambda
occurring with probability
p_{\lambda}
;
\sum^{d_{\lambda}-1}_{\lambda=0}p_{\lambda}=1
. On the other hand,
\{p(b|y;\rho_{\lambda})\}_{b,y}
is the set of quantum probability distributions
p(b|y;\rho_{\lambda})=\tr\{M^{B}_{b|y}\rho_{\lambda}\}
, arising from some LHS
\rho_{\lambda}
, which satisfies
\sum_{a,\lambda}p_{\lambda}p(a|x,\lambda)\rho_{\lambda}=\rho_{B}
, where
\rho_{B}
is the reduced state of
\rho_{AB}
producing the correlation.
Superunsteerability is defined as follows.

Definition 1
.

Suppose an unsteerable correlation, which has an LHV-LHS model as in Eq. (
11
), is produced in a 1SSDI scenario, as in Fig.
1
. Then, superunsteerability is demonstrated if and only if
d_{\lambda}
in Eq. (
11
) satisfies
d_{\lambda}>d_{A}
for all such LHV-LHS models.

Superunsteerability has been invoked to provide an operational characterization of the quantumness of unsteerable bipartite states
[
37
,
36
,
35
]
. Here, the quantumness arises because superunsteerability requires quantum discord in the given unsteerable state.

More specifically, superunsteerability witnesses
SDI steerability of discordant states
[
27
,
38
]
.
SDI steering can be defined as follows.

Definition 2
.

In a 1SSDI scenario, as in Fig.
1
, SDI steering is demonstrated if a superunsteerable correlation, which implies superunsteerability, or a steerable correlation, which does not have an LHV-LHS model, is produced.

As an illustration, consider the Werner state given by

\rho_{W}=p\outerproduct{\Phi^+}{\Phi^+}+(1-p)\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{2}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{2}}{4},

(12)

where
0\leq p\leq 1
.
\rho_{W}
is entangled if and only if
p>1/3
; at the same time, it has non-zero discord for any
p>0
. On the other hand, in the two-setting steering scenario,
\rho_{W}
has steerability for
p>1/\sqrt{2}
, while it has superunsteerability for
0<p\leq 1/\sqrt{2}

[
39
,
35
]
. Thus,
\rho_{W}
has SDI steerability for any
p>0
.

In the context of a 1SSDI scenario, as shown in Fig.
1
, we have the following assumption.

Observation 1
.

In the given 1SSDI scenario, to observe SDI steerability of discordant states that are not steerable in the 1SDI context, shared randomness
\lambda
is not freely available.

This assumption follows because the set of nonsuperunsteerable correlations is non-convex
[
36
,
35
,
27
]
. If shared randomness is freely available in the context of a 1SSDI scenario, then the correlations that have an LHV-LHS model do not certify SDI steerability. Thus, entanglement is required to demonstrate SDI steering if shared randomness is freely available.

3
Global coherence

The quantum coherence of a state represents superposition with respect to a fixed basis. Different measures of quantum coherence have been studied extensively in the literature
[
3
]
.
For a single system, fixing a reference basis
\{\ket{i}\}
, a state
\rho
of the system is said to have vanishing coherence if it is diagonal in the reference basis, i.e.,

\rho=\sum_{i}p_{i}\outerproduct{i}{i},

(13)

with probabilities
p_{i}
.
In
[
1
]
, the relative entropy of coherence was introduced as a coherence measure, analogous to the relative entropy of entanglement, which quantifies entanglement
[
20
]
.
The quantum relative entropy of a state
\rho
with respect to a state
\sigma
is defined as

S(\rho||\sigma)\equiv-\tr{\rho\log_2\sigma}-S(\rho)
, where

S(\rho)=-\tr{\rho\log_2\rho}
.
The relative entropy of coherence
{\cal C}^{r}(\rho)
of a state
\rho
with respect to a basis is then defined as

{\cal C}^{r}(\rho)\equiv\min_{\sigma\in{\cal I}}S(\rho||\sigma),

(14)

where
{\cal I}
is the set of totally incoherent (diagonal) states in that basis.

Moving to the bipartite systems, fixing a reference product basis
\{\ket{i}^{A}\otimes\ket{j}^{B}\}
, a bipartite state is incoherent (IC) if it can be written as follows
[
40
,
6
]
:

\rho^{IC}_{AB}=\sum_{k}p_{k}\tau^{A}_{k}\otimes\upsilon^{B}_{k},

(15)

where
p_{k}
’s are probabilities,
\tau^{A}_{k}
and
\upsilon^{B}_{k}
are incoherent states on subsystems
A
and
B
, respectively,
i.e.,
\tau^{A}_{k}=\sum_{i}p^{\prime}_{ik}\outerproduct{i}{i}
and
\upsilon^{B}_{k}=\sum_{j}p^{\prime\prime}_{jk}\outerproduct{j}{j}
for probabilities
p^{\prime}_{ik}
and
p^{\prime\prime}_{jk}
. Otherwise, it is said to have
bipartite coherence
. The relative entropy of coherence can also be used to quantify bipartite coherence
[
41
]
.
The set of incoherent states forms a subset of all separable states. As in the case of incoherent operations, which are free operations in a resource theory of coherence for single systems
[
1
]
, incoherent operations on bipartite states, described by a CPTP map, transform any incoherent bipartite state into another incoherent bipartite state.
These incoherent operations are defined globally, and they do not define the free operations in the resource theory of coherence in bipartite scenarios. The free operations of coherence in bipartite scenarios were studied in
[
42
]
.

Quantumness due to a nonzero discord has been studied using the notion of quantum coherence
[
15
,
43
]
. Any incoherent bipartite state has zero discord in both ways; on the other hand, a nonzero discord in one way or both ways always implies bipartite coherence. More specifically, any discordant state has bipartite coherence in a basis-free form
[
44
]
, because it is not a CC state (
4
) with respect to any product basis.

As argued in
[
30
]
, the quantumness of any discordant state that has a decomposition as in Eq. (
5
) is due to local coherence in the subsystem
A
or
B
or both simultaneously.
We now obtain the following observation to identify all bipartite states that have bipartite coherence locally.

Observation 2
.

All discordant states in
\mathbb{C}^{d_{A}}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d_{B}}
, which have a decomposition as in Eq. (
5
) with
d_{\lambda}\leq d_{\min}
, form a strict subset of all discordant states in
\mathbb{C}^{d_{A}}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d_{B}}
with
L_{R}\leq d_{\min}
.

To prove the above observation, note that if
d_{A}\neq d_{B}
, the set of separable states in
\mathbb{C}^{d_{A}}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d_{B}}
, which have a decomposition as in Eq. (
5
), forms a subset of all separable states in
\mathbb{C}^{d_{A}}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d_{B}}
with
L_{R}\leq d_{\min}

[
30
]
. This can be seen as follows. If
d_{A}=d_{B}=d
, all states with
L_{R}\leq d_{\min}
can be decomposed as in Eq. (
5
) with
d_{\min}=d
.
On the other hand, if
d_{A}<d_{B}
or
d_{A}>d_{B}
, there are discordant states with
L_{R}\leq d_{\min}
that do not have a local creation of discord
[
30
]
. We will give an example of such a state. To this end, denote by

\ket{\theta,\phi}:=\cos\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)\ket{0}+\exp(i \phi)\sin\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)\ket{1}\,,

(16)

an arbitrary pure state in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
, then the set
{\mathcal{W}=\{\ket{0,0},\ket{\frac{2\pi}{3},0},\ket{\frac{2\pi}{3},\pi}\}}
can be used to define a QC state in
\mathbb{C}^{2}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{3}
as follows:

\rho_{AB}=\frac{1}{3}\sum_{\lambda=0}^{2}W_{\lambda}\otimes\outerproduct{\lambda}{\lambda}\,,

(17)

with
{W_{\lambda}=\ket{w_{\lambda}}\bra{w_{\lambda}}}
and
{\ket{w_{\lambda}}\in\mathcal{W}}
. The above discordant state does not have a local creation of discord
[
22
]
, yet it has
L_{R}=2
since it has
D^{\leftarrow}(\rho_{AB})=0
.

We now make the following observation.

Observation 3
.

All discordant states in
\mathbb{C}^{d_{A}}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d_{B}}
with
L_{R}\leq d_{\min}
, which do not have a decomposition as in Eq. (
5
) with
d_{\lambda}\leq d_{\min}
, is a CQ state if
d_{A}>d_{B}
or a QC state if
d_{A}<d_{B}
. For such a CQ (QC) state, the dimension of the support of its reduced state
\rho_{A}
(
\rho_{B}
) is greater than that of
\rho_{B}
(
\rho_{A}
).

This observation follows because if the state is neither a CQ state nor a QC state, it has a local creation of discord as shown in
[
30
]
or
L_{R}>d_{\min}
. On the other hand, if the dimension of the support of the reduced state
\rho_{A}
(
\rho_{B}
) of the CQ (QC) state mentioned in the above observation is not greater than that of
\rho_{B}
(
\rho_{A}
), it has a decomposition as in Eq. (
5
) with
d_{\lambda}\leq d_{\min}
.

We have now obtained the following observation.

Observation 4
.

For any given separable state in
\mathbb{C}^{d_{A}}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d_{B}}
with
L_{R}\leq d_{\min}
, its bipartite coherence is locally present in a subsystem or in both subsystems.

The above observation follows because the bipartite coherence of any CQ or QC state is contained locally in the subsystem
B
or
A
, respectively.
On the other hand, all other states with
L_{R}\leq d_{\min}
, which have nonzero discord in both ways, can be created by a local operation on a CC state
[
30
]
.
Using Obs.
4
, we can define the global coherence of bipartite states as follows.

Definition 3
.

A bipartite state,
\rho_{AB}
in
\mathbb{C}^{d_{A}}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d_{B}}
, has global coherence if and only if it is neither a CQ state nor a QC state and its discord cannot be generated by a local operation of the form
\Phi_{A}\otimes\Phi_{B}
on a CC state given by Eq. (
4
) with
q_{ij}=\delta_{ij}\tilde{q}_{i}
.

Note that all entangled states have global coherence because they are not separable to have bipartite coherence locally to any of the subsystems or both subsystems. At the same time, global coherence goes beyond entanglement. For example, the Werner state
\rho_{W}
given by Eq. (
12
), which is entangled if and only if
p>1/3
, has
L_{R}>2
for any
p>0
. Thus,
\rho_{W}
has global coherence for any
p>0
.

In the case of global coherence as a resource, the free states are all incoherent bipartite states and bipartite coherent states whose coherence is present locally, i.e. states with
L_{R}\leq d_{\min}
, and the free operations are given by any local operation of the form
\Phi_{A}\otimes\Phi_{B}
, where
\Phi
is any CPTP map because such operations cannot create the resource of global coherence from any free state. To illustrate how the free operations of global coherence cannot generate a resource state from a free state, we have already seen an example in which, applying the local operation
\Phi\otimes\Phi
, to both qubits of the CC state given by Eq. (
6
), we get the discordant state given by Eq. (
7
), which, however, does not have global coherence.
On the other hand, a globally coherent state can be generated locally from the CC state (
6
) by applying a local operation of the form given by
\sum_{\lambda}p_{\lambda}\Phi_{\lambda}\otimes\Phi_{\lambda}
(each
\Phi_{\lambda}
is a CPTP map and
p_{\lambda}\geq 0
, with
\sum_{\lambda}p_{\lambda}=1
).
Note that such local operations and shared randomness are free operations in the case of entanglement as a resource. For comparison of free operations of global coherence in quantum discord with that of entanglement theory, please refer to Eq. (
18
) in
[
16
]
. For the resource theory of discord, free operations have also been studied in
[
45
,
46
]
. These free operations are more restrictive than those of global coherence, since the former should not create quantum discord locally. Like in the case of quantum discord, the free states of global coherence of quantum discord are also nonconvex.

Based on any contractive pseudo-distance measure
[
47
]
, one can construct a measure of global coherence. Given a bipartite state
\rho_{AB}
, a contractive pseudo-distance measure of global coherence
{\cal G}^{\delta}(\rho_{AB})
is defined as

{\cal G}^{\delta}(\rho_{AB})=\min_{\chi_{AB}\in\mathcal{F}_{G}}\delta(\rho_{AB},\chi_{AB}),

(18)

where
\mathcal{F}_{G}
denotes the set of free states in the resource theory of global coherence and
\delta
is the pseudo-distance measure required to be contractive under quantum operations, i.e.,

\delta(\Phi[\rho],\Phi[\zeta])\leq\delta(\rho,\zeta),

(19)

where
\Phi
is any CPTP map. This implies that
{\cal G}^{\delta}
does not increase under any local operation of the form
\Phi_{A}\otimes\Phi_{B}
, which is a free operation in the case of global coherence as a resource.

We proceed to study the relationship between the resource frameworks of global coherence of bipartite and single-partite states.
To this end, we consider the protocol for converting the coherence of a single system into discord of a bipartite system under a bipartite incoherent operation, as studied in
[
23
]
.
Suppose a coherent state
\rho_{A}
is coupled to an initially incoherent ancilla
\tau_{B}
and an incoherent operation
\Lambda_{IC}
is performed on the joint state
\rho_{AB}=\rho_{A}\otimes\tau_{B}
. Then, there exists a bipartite incoherent operation
\Lambda_{IC}
for which coherence can be converted into entanglement as shown in
[
6
]
. At the same time, discord without entanglement can also be generated
[
23
]
, as in the case of the conversion of coherence into entanglement using a bipartite incoherent operation.
For instance, consider that the bipartite incoherent operation
\varepsilon(\rho_{AB})=pU_{\mathrm{CX}}\rho_{AB}U_{\mathrm{CX}}+(1-p)\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{4}}{4}
, where
U_{\mathrm{CX}}
is the controlled-NOT gate with
U_{\mathrm{CX}}(\ket{i}\otimes\ket{j})=\ket{i}\otimes\ket{i\oplus j}
. If this incoherent operation is performed on the joint state
\rho_{AB}=\outerproduct{+}{+}\otimes\outerproduct{0}{0}
, then the joint state is transformed into a Werner state
\rho_{W}
. Note that in this context, discord without entanglement is generated by tuning
0<p\leq 1/3
.

There is also a bound on the amount of discord generated in the aforementioned protocol to convert coherence into discord
[
23
]
. To state this bound,
let
{\cal C}^{\delta}
denote a contractive pseudo-distance measure of coherence of a single-partite state
\rho
given by

{\cal C}^{\delta}(\rho)=\min_{\sigma\in\cal{I}}\delta(\rho,\sigma),

(20)

such as in Eq. (
14
), and
{\cal D}^{\delta}
denote the contractive pseudo-distance measure of discord, i.e.,

{\cal D}^{\delta}(\rho_{AB})=\min_{\sigma_{AB}\in\mathcal{C}}\delta(\rho_{AB},\sigma_{AB}),

(21)

where
\mathcal{C}
denotes the set of CC states given by Eq. (
4
).
Then, the amount of discord generated satisfies the following inequality:

{\cal D}^{\delta}(\Lambda_{IC}(\rho_{A}\otimes\tau_{B}))\leq{\cal C}^{\delta}(\rho_{A}),

(22)

as shown in
[
23
]
.

Suppose
\Lambda_{IC}(\rho_{A}\otimes\tau_{B})
is a separable state and has
{\cal D}^{\delta}>0
. Then this discordant state necessarily has
{\cal G}^{\delta}>0
. This follows because the coherence of the subsystem must be consumed to create the nonzero discord, as was also demonstrated in Result
2
in
[
23
]
. Next, note that

{\cal G}^{\delta}(\rho_{AB})\leq{\cal D}^{\delta}(\rho_{AB}),

(23)

which follows due to the contractivity of
\delta
, and there are discordant states for which
{\cal D}^{\delta}(\rho_{AB})>0
, but
{\cal G}^{\delta}(\rho_{AB})=0
.
Combining Eqs. (
22
) and (
23
), we have now obtained the following relationship between
{\cal G}^{\delta}
and
{\cal C}^{\delta}
.

Observation 5
.

For any contractive distance
\delta
, the amount of global coherence created between a state
\rho_{A}
and an incoherent ancilla
\tau_{B}
by an incoherent operation
\Lambda_{IC}
satisfies the inequality:

{\cal G}^{\delta}(\Lambda_{IC}(\rho_{A}\otimes\tau_{B}))\leq{\cal C}^{\delta}(\rho_{A}).

(24)

Here, we have derived the inequality in Eq. (
24
) to establish a strong link between the resource frameworks of global coherence of bipartite and single-partite states, extending the result of
[
6
]
, which demonstrated a strong link between the resource frameworks of entanglement and coherence in single systems. The inequality in Eq. (
22
) is only quantitative to capture genuinely quantum correlation generated; on the other hand, our inequality in Eq. (
24
) captures both qualitative and quantitative aspects of genuinely quantum correlation generated in the protocol.

4.1
Semi-device-independent (SDI) steering

Here we demonstrate that global coherence is required to demonstrate SDI steerability in the context of a 1SSDI scenario, as in Fig.
1
. To this end, in the following, we prove two lemmas on superunsteerability to obtain our main result.

Lemma 1
.

If a separable state in
\mathbb{C}^{d_{A}}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d_{B}}
has a decomposition as in Eq. (
5
), it cannot be used to demonstrate superunsteerability.

Proof.

Consider any correlation arising from a separable state of the form (
5
) in the context of a 1SSDI scenario, as in Fig.
1
. It is readily seen that any such correlation admits the following decomposition:

p(a,b|x,y)=\sum^{d_{\lambda}-1}_{\lambda=0}p_{\lambda}p(a|x,\lambda)p(b|y,\lambda),

(25)

with
d_{\lambda}\leq d_{\min}
. Here
p(a|x,\lambda)=\tr\left\{M^{A}_{a|x}\rho^{A}_{\lambda}\right\}
and
p(b|y,\lambda)=\tr\left\{M^{B}_{b|y}\rho^{B}_{\lambda}\right\}
. From the above decomposition, it then follows that any separable state, which has a decomposition as in Eq. (
5
), cannot be used to demonstrate superunsteerability, as it has an LHV-LHS. ∎

As we have noted before, the set of separable states in
\mathbb{C}^{d_{A}}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d_{B}}
, which have a decomposition as in Eq. (
5
), forms a subset of all separable states in
\mathbb{C}^{d_{A}}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d_{B}}
with
L_{R}\leq d_{\min}

[
30
,
22
]
. Thus, there are discordant states that do not have a decomposition as in Eq. (
5
); at the same time, they do not have global coherence. Such states have the form of a CQ or QC state. For any of these states, it is not immediately apparent from their CQ or QC form whether they can be used to demonstrate superunsteerability or not. For example, consider any correlation arising from the QC state given by Eq. (
17
). It can be seen that there exists a correlation arising from such a QC state that can be decomposed as in Eq. (
25
), but with
d_{\lambda}=3
, which may not be the minimal dimension required to simulate the correlation. Thus, from this decomposition of the correlation, it is impossible to immediately conclude whether the correlation is superunsteerable or not. To demonstrate that any CQ or QC state cannot be used to demonstrate superunsteerability, we make the following observation.

Observation 6
.

Suppose that any discordant state in
\mathbb{C}^{d_{A}}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d_{B}}
has no global coherence, i.e.,
L_{R}\leq d_{\min}
. Its nonzero discord is due to a nonvanishing classical correlation
C^{\rightarrow}
as given in Eq. (
2
) or
C^{\leftarrow}
, i.e., the presence of correlation in only a single basis, assisted with local coherence in a subsystem or both subsystems.

The above observation follows because such discordant states can be produced from a CC state (
4
) by locally generating coherence, or they contain coherence locally as a CQ or QC state. Therefore, the discord of these states implies the presence of correlation in only a single basis.
Whereas, for discordant states with
L_{R}>d_{\min}
, their nonzero discord signifies a form of genuinely quantum correlation. Such a form of genuinely quantum correlation indicates the presence of correlations in more than one basis, just as in the case of entangled states.
As an illustration of correlation in more than one basis, note that a Bell state of a two-qubit system exhibits correlations in both bases of the Pauli matrices
\sigma_{x}
and
\sigma_{z}
, implying steerability; at the same time, all discordant states that belong to the two-qubit Bell-diagonal states have correlations in more than one basis
[
48
]
, implying that they have SDI steerability
[
38
]
.

We now proceed to obtain the following lemma using Obs.
6
.

Lemma 2
.

Suppose that any given discordant state in
\mathbb{C}^{d_{A}}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d_{B}}
does not have a decomposition as in Eq. (
5
), but it has
L_{R}\leq d_{\min}
. Then, it cannot be used to demonstrate superunsteerability.

Proof.

As noted in Obs.
3
, any given discordant state in
\mathbb{C}^{d_{A}}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d_{B}}
that does not have a decomposition as in Eq. (
5
) is a CQ state if
d_{A}>d_{B}
or a QC state if
d_{A}<d_{B}
.
Since any CQ or QC state has no global coherence and has only correlation in a single basis, as noted in Obs.
6
, any correlation arising from this state can be reproduced by a suitable CC state. We show this by first considering any QC state in
\mathbb{C}^{d_{A}}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d_{B}}
, with
d_{A}<d_{B}
, that is discordant given by

\rho_{\rm QC}=\sum_{i}q_{i}\rho^{A}_{i}\otimes\outerproduct{i}{i}^{B},

(26)

as mentioned in Obs.
3
.
The QC state given by Eq. (
26
) is not a CC state because it has a nonzero discord. An example of such a QC state is given by Eq. (
17
).

The QC state (
26
) can be transformed into a CC state, while retaining the same amount of the classical correlation
C^{\rightarrow}(\rho_{\rm QC})
, by applying a suitable decohering map on subsystem
A
. To see this, consider the decohering map,

\Phi_{A}=\sum_{i}K_{i}(\cdot)K^{\dagger}_{i}
, with Kraus operators
K_{i}
satisfying
\sum_{i}K^{\dagger}_{i}K_{i}=\mathbbm{1}_{d_{A}}
and
K_{i}=\outerproduct{\chi_i}{\phi_i}
, where
\{\ket{\phi_{i}}\}
is a basis that gives rise to the classical correlation
C^{\rightarrow}(\rho_{\rm QC})
with projective measurement and
\{\ket{\chi_{i}}\}
is any orthogonal basis. Applying this map on
\rho_{\rm QC}
, we get the state,
\rho_{\rm CC}
, as follows:

\rho_{\rm CC}=\sum_{i}(K_{i}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{d_{B}})\rho_{\rm QC}(K^{\dagger}_{i}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{d_{B}}),

(27)

which has the same amount of the classical correlation as that of
\rho_{\rm QC}
, and at the same time, it has vanishing quantum discord from
A
to
B
because it is the CC state.

Now, consider any correlation that arises from
\rho_{\rm QC}
given by

\displaystyle p(a,b|x,y)=\tr\left\{\rho_{\rm QC}M^{A}_{a|x}\otimes M^{B}_{b|y}\right\},\forall a,x,b,y,

(28)

for the POVM on Alice’s and Bob’s sides given by
\{M^{A}_{a|x}\}_{a,x}
and
\{M^{B}_{b|y}\}_{b,y}
, respectively. Any such correlation can be reproduced by the state
\rho_{\rm CC}
in Eq. (
27
) for the new measurement operators on Alice’s side given by
\{\sum_{i}K^{\dagger}_{i}M^{A}_{a|x}K_{i}\}_{a,x}
and the same measurements on Bob’s side. To see this, for such measurements, consider the correlation,
\{p^{\prime}(a,b|x,y)\}_{a,x,b,y}
, arising from the CC state,
\rho_{\rm CC}
in Eq. (
27
), given by

\displaystyle p^{\prime}(a,b|x,y)

\displaystyle=\tr\left\{\rho_{\rm CC}\sum_{i}K^{\dagger}_{i}M^{A}_{a|x}K_{i}\otimes M^{B}_{b|y}\right\},

(29)

for all
a,x,b,y
. Now, substituting
\rho_{\rm CC}
in Eq. (
27
) in the above equation, we obtain

p^{\prime}(a,b|x,y)=\tr\{\sum_{i}(K_{i}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{d_{B}})\rho_{QC}(K^{\dagger}_{i}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{d_{B}})\\
\times\sum_{i^{\prime}}K^{\dagger}_{i^{\prime}}M^{A}_{a|x}K_{i^{\prime}}\otimes M^{B}_{b|y}\Bigg\}.

(30)

Now, using the invariance of the trace under cyclic permutations of the operators inside the trace, we have

p^{\prime}(a,b|x,y)\\
=\tr\left\{\rho_{QC}\sum_{i,i^{\prime}}K^{\dagger}_{i^{\prime}}K_{i}M^{A}_{a|x}K^{\dagger}_{i}K_{i^{\prime}}\otimes M^{B}_{b|y}\right\}.

(31)

Note that
K_{i}
as defined above satisfy the orthogonality condition
K^{\dagger}_{i}K_{i^{\prime}}=K_{i}K^{\dagger}_{i^{\prime}}=0
, for
i\neq i^{\prime}
. Using this condition and
\sum_{i}K^{\dagger}_{i}K_{i}=\mathbbm{1}_{d_{A}}
in the above equation, we obtain,

\displaystyle p^{\prime}(a,b|x,y)=

\displaystyle\tr\left\{\rho_{\rm QC}M^{A}_{a|x}\otimes M^{B}_{b|y}\right\},

(32)

for all
a,x,b,y
, which is the same correlation as in Eq. (
28
). This implies that the correlation has an LHV-LHS model with
d_{\lambda}\leq d_{\min}
, as it can be reproduced by the CC state. Similarly, this statement also holds in the case of the CQ states, which do not have a decomposition as in Eq. (
5
).
∎

We have now obtained the following main result of this paper.

Theorem 1
.

For any given bipartite state in
\mathbb{C}^{d_{A}}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d_{B}}
, global coherence is required to demonstrate SDI steering.

Proof.

To prove the statement of this theorem, we first note that all bipartite states that exhibit SDI steering can be classified into the following three categories.

1.

All states that exhibit standard steering. Such states are necessarily entangled
[
28
,
32
]
and have SDI steerability since standard steering also implies SDI steering by Definition
2
.

2.

There are entangled states that do have standard steerability, but exhibit SDI steering
[
36
,
27
]
.

3.

There are separable states that exhibit SDI steering
[
36
,
27
]
.

We proceed to prove the theorem using this classification.
Because entanglement implies global coherence, in the case of the first two categories of states, global coherence is necessary for SDI steering.
On the other hand, Lemma
1
and Lemma
2
imply that any separable state with
L_{R}\leq d_{\min}
cannot be used to demonstrate superunsteerability. Thus, global coherence is required to demonstrate SDI steering using separable states, since
L_{R}>d_{\min}
implies global coherence by Definition
3
and superunsteerability witnesses SDI steering by Definition
2
. Since we have now arrived at the conclusion that global coherence is required for SDI steering in all of the three categories of states that exhibit SDI steering, we have proved the statement of the theorem.
∎

4.2
Remote state preparation (RSP)

We consider the quantum information protocol,
RSP
[
49
,
50
,
51
]
using two-qubit systems. In this context, in
[
13
]
, it was proposed that quantum discord captures the resourcefulness of two-qubit states for RSP. We review this result as follows.
If Alice and Bob share a maximally entangled state of a two-qubit system, as a resource in the RSP protocol, then RSP can be achieved perfectly
[
58
]
. Instead, if they use a more general resource state, such as a mixed state of two-qubit systems, RSP could occur imperfectly. In this context, to quantify the performance of the RSP protocol, a figure of merit, called RSP-fidelity, was introduced in
[
13
]
. Let Alice use a shared two-qubit state to remotely prepare a state with the Bloch vector
\vec{s}
in the plane orthogonal to the direction
\vec{\beta}
on Bob’s side. Since Alice uses a mixed resource state, she remotely prepares a state with the Bloch vector
\vec{r}
instead of
\vec{s}
. To define the RSP-fidelity, the authors of
[
13
]
first defined the pay-off function as
{\cal P}:=(\vec{r}\cdot\vec{s})^{2}
. Next, for the given
\vec{s}
and
\vec{\beta}

{\cal P}_{\max}
, which is the payoff optimized overall local measurements on Alice’s side, the RSP-fidelity
\mathcal{F}
is finally defined to be
\mathcal{F}(\rho_{AB}):=\min_{\vec{\beta}}{\cal P}^{\rm ave}_{\max}
, where
{\cal P}^{\rm ave}_{\max}
is the optimal payoff averaged over all states
\vec{s}
in the given plane of the Bloch sphere, and
{\cal P}^{\rm ave}_{\max}
minimized over all directions
\vec{\beta}
.

Any two-qubit state, up to local unitary transformations, can be expressed as
[
59
]
:

\zeta_{AB}=\frac{1}{4}\Bigg(\mathbbm{1}_{2}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{2}+\vec{a}\cdot\vec{\sigma}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{2}+\mathbbm{1}_{2}\otimes\vec{b}\cdot\vec{\sigma}+\sum^{3}_{i=1}c_{i}\sigma_{i}\otimes\sigma_{i}\Bigg),

(33)

where
\mathbbm{1}_{2}
is the
2\times 2
identity matrix,
\sigma_{i}

i=1,2
and
3
are the three Pauli matrices, respectively,
\vec{\sigma}
is the vector of these Pauli matrices and
\{\vec{a},\vec{b},\vec{c}\}\in\mathbf{R}^{3}

.
For two-qubit states,
\zeta_{AB}

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(\zeta_{AB})=\frac{1}{2}(c^{2}_{2}+c^{2}_{3}).

5
Conclusions and Discussions

In this work, we have been motivated to study
which aspect of bipartite coherence
implies
genuinely quantum correlation
in discord. To this end, we have defined
global coherence
of bipartite states, which is a specific form of bipartite coherence that is not local coherence in any of the subsystems or both subsystems alone. Global coherence of separable states is equivalent to the witness of discord by the rank of the correlation matrix of the density matrix introduced in
[
26
]
. In case of
global coherence as a resource
,
any local operations
of the form
\Phi_{A}\otimes\Phi_{A}
, which may create coherence locally, are
free operations
. To rigorously formulate global coherence, we pointed out a strong link between resource frameworks of global coherence in bipartite and single-partite states, just as the strong link between entanglement and coherence shown in
[
6
]
.

We have then addressed the question of whether
genuinely quantum correlation
in discord, as indicated by
global coherence
, is required for
operational tasks
. To this end, we have considered the task of
SDI steering
for which shared randomness is not freely available. We have then shown that any discordant state, which does not have global coherence, cannot be used to demonstrate
superunsteerability
. Thus, we have established that global coherence is necessary for SDI steering tasks.

The result obtained in Theorem
1
also holds for superlocality
[
55
,
56
]
in the context of SDI Bell scenarios.
We now address the improvements achieved in this result of the present investigation over the previous research in
[
22
,
56
,
35
,
27
]
.
In
[
22
]
,
genuinely quantum correlation
in separable states was already proposed to be in discord with
L_{R}>d_{\min}
, as they cannot be created via local operations. On the other hand, we have identified that such genuinely quantum correlation is a specific form of bipartite coherence that is not local to any of the subsystems or both of the subsystems and acts as a necessary resource for superunsteerability or superlocality.
In
[
27
]
, the question of whether the one-wayness property of quantum discord can be used to demonstrate the asymmetry of superunsteerability was explored. Using Theorem
1
, it follows that all one-way discordant states cannot be used to demonstrate superunsteerability. Thus, the occurrence of superunsteerability asymmetrically remains to be studied.
On the other hand, in
[
56
,
35
]
, two-way discord as a necessary resource for superlocality was studied, and it remained unknown whether every two-way discordant state can be used to demonstrate superlocality. From Theorem
1
, it follows that a two-way discord is not sufficient for superlocality. In addition to this, we have also identified the necessary condition on two-way discordant states to demonstrate superlocality.

Finally, to answer the aforementioned question of whether
global coherence
is required for
operational tasks
in the context of quantum information processing using separable states, we have considered the quantum communication task of
RSP
using two-qubit correlations
[
13
,
25
]
. We have then identified the necessary and sufficient quantum resource for this task by the witness of global coherence called “Mermin strength”.

Taking quantum discord as a resource for quantum information processing based on previous proposals, such as in
[
13
]
, may be misleading due to the local creation of the resource. Although in
[
13
]
quantum discord was proposed as a resource for RSP, the experimental results of
[
13
]
are impressive and are not misleading from the perspective of the present work: the states chosen in the experiments are a separable state having superunsteerability and an entangled state, and indeed it is very surprising that the separable state outperforms the entangled state, as shown in the experiments of
[
13
]
.

In contrast to the experimental demonstration in
[
13
]
, with a general framework for the RSP in
[
57
]
, it was shown that separability can never outperform entanglement in the quantum communication task. In this framework, a different version of RSP-fidelity based on linear fidelity and general decoding operations on Bob’s side was studied. In showing the superiority of entanglement over separability in the RSP, an assumption of one-way local operation and classical communication native to entanglement theory is utilized. This assumption and the RSP-fidelity based on linear fidelity
convexifies
the quantum resource for the RSP. This convexification of the quantum resource implies that all separable states are free resources for the RSP protocol. Even with this demonstration of
[
57
]
, the experimental results of
[
13
]
are still surprising, as these two RSP protocols using the two-qubit states are in two different resource-theoretic frameworks: the latter is a nonconvex resource theory, whereas the former is a convex resource theory. For the practical implementation of RSP, the RSP protocol in
[
13
]
seems more relevant than the protocol in
[
57
]
, which shows the superiority of entanglement over separability.

Appendix A
Schrödinger strength

Similarly to the quantification of standard steerability based on the steering cost
[
39
]
, a quantifier termed
Schrödinger strength
was introduced in
[
54
]
, which can be used to quantify SDI steering
[
38
]
. To introduce these two quantifiers,
consider
n
-setting steering scenario to observe a box
P(a,b|x,y)
, where
x

\in

\{0,1,2,...,n-1\}
and
y

\in

\{0,1,2,...,n-1\}
. In this scenario, any correlation
P(a,b|x,y)
can be decomposed into a convex mixture of a steerable part and an unsteerable part,

P(a,b|x,y)=pP_{S}(a,b|x,y)+(1-p)P_{US}(a,b|x,y),

(44)

where
P_{S}(a,b|x,y)
is an extremal steerable correlation and
P_{US}(a,b|x,y)
is an unsteerable correlation which may be superunsteerable;
0\leq p\leq 1
. In the given scenario, an extremal steerable correlation cannot be expressed as a convex mixture of other correlations. Thus, it maximally violates the steering inequality.
The weight of the extremal steerable correlation,
P_{S}(a,b|x,y)
, minimized over all possible decompositions of the form (
44
) is called the steering cost of the correlation
P(a,b|x,y)
providing a measure of quantum steering
[
39
]
.

On the other hand, the weight of the extremal steerable correlation,
P_{S}(a,b|x,y)
, in Eq. (
44
) maximized over all possible decompositions is called the
Schrödinger strength
(SS) of the correlation
P(a,b|x,y)
:

SS_{n}\Big(P(a,b|x,y)\Big):=\max_{\text{decompositions}}p.

(45)

Schrödinger strength as defined above has the following properties:

•

0\leq SS_{n}\leq 1
(since
p
is a valid probability,
0\leq p\leq 1
).

•

SS_{n}=0
for all correlations that have a decomposition as in Eq. (
11
) with
d_{\lambda}\leq d_{A}
.

•

SS_{n}>0
implies that the correlation does not have a decomposition as in Eq. (
11
) with
d_{\lambda}\leq d_{A}
.

•

SS_{n}
has nonconvexity, i.e., there exist correlations

P_{1}(a,b|x,y)
and
P_{2}(a,b|x,y)
such that

SS_{n}\left(pP_{1}(a,b|x,y)+(1-p)P_{2}(a,b|x,y)\right)\\
\geq pSS_{n}\left(P_{1}(a,b|x,y))+(1-p)SS_{n}(P_{2}(a,b|x,y)\right),

(46)

for
0\leq p\leq 1
.

In
[
54

, the Schrödinger strengths of two-qubit Bell-diagonal states have been studied in two- and three-setting scenarios.
The two-qubit Bell diagonal states

Lemma 5

For any two-qubit CQ state as in Eq. (

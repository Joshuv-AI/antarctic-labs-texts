1.
Introduction

The theory of dilation is a fundamental result established by Sz.-Nagy
[
14
]
, which asserts that any contraction on a Hilbert space can be dilated to an isometry acting on a larger Hilbert space. This result laid the foundation for a deeper understanding of operator theory, providing a powerful tool for studying contractions through their isometric extensions. Building on Sz.-Nagy’s foundational work, Ando
[
1
]
significantly advanced the theory by constructing isometric dilations for pairs of commuting contractions. That is, for any pair of commuting contractions
(T_{1},T_{2})
on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H},
then there exists a pair of commuting isometries
(V_{1},V_{2})
on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{K}\supseteq\mathcal{H}
such that

T_{1}^{n_{1}}T_{2}^{n_{2}}=P_{\mathcal{H}}V_{1}^{n_{1}}V_{2}^{n_{2}}|_{\mathcal{H}}\quad\quad\mbox{for all}\quad\quad(n_{1},n_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{2},

where
P_{\mathcal{H}}

denotes the orthogonal projection onto
{\mathcal{H}}.

Ando’s theorem thus confirms that every pair of commuting contractions can be dilated to a pair of commuting isometries, providing a complete solution in the two-variable case. Parrott
[
15
]
gave a counterexample showing that for
n\geq 3,

n
-tuples of commuting contractions do not generally admit isometric dilations. This striking limitation highlights the intrinsic difficulties of multivariable dilation theory and motivates a more nuanced investigation into the specific conditions under which such dilations might still be possible.

In recent years, a growing body of literature has explored the interplay between dilation and positivity, revealing that certain classes of operator tuples under specific positivity assumptions can indeed possess isometric dilations. See
[
2
,
4
,
5
,
8
,
13
]
for
more details in the polydisc setup. The main motivation behind our interest comes from a recent work by Das and Sarkar
[
5
]
, they obtained an explicit isometric dilation of pair of commuting contractions in the following theorem:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(T_{1},T_{2})
be a pair of commuting contractions on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}.
Let
T_{1}
be pure and dim
\mathcal{D}_{T_{j}}<\infty,j=1,2.
Then there exist an isometry
\Pi:\mathcal{H}\to H^{2}_{\mathcal{D}_{T_{1}}}(\mathbb{D})
and an inner function
\psi\in H^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{D}_{T_{1}})}(\mathbb{D})
such that

\Pi T_{1}^{*}=M_{z}^{*}\Pi\quad\quad\mbox{and}\quad\quad\Pi T_{2}^{*}=M_{\psi}^{*}\Pi,

where
\mathcal{D}_{T_{j}}=\overline{ran}(I_{\mathcal{H}}-T_{j}T^{*}_{j})
for
j=1,2.

Operator and function theories are closely intertwined and have long been regarded as well-established fields with significant connections to a wide range of disciplines. These areas continue to advance, both by addressing traditional problems and by extending classical ideas to more generalized frameworks. One such extension is the concept of Hilbert
C^{*}
-modules, developed through
C^{*}
-correspondences and covariant representations.

Cuntz
[
3
]
studied a
C^{*}
-algebra, known as Cuntz algebra, generated by isometries
V_{1},\dots,V_{n}

(n\geq 2)
on a Hilbert space with
V_{i}
’s have orthogonal range. Wold decomposition for two isometries with orthogonal range was first introduced by Frazho
[
7
]
. Popescu
[
18
]
extended this decomposition to the case of an infinite sequence of isometries with orthogonal final spaces, and explored a minimal isometric dilation for an infinite sequence of noncommuting contractive operators on a Hilbert space. Pimsner
[
17
]
generalized the construction of Cuntz algebras with the help of the notion of isometric representations of
C^{*}
-correspondences. Muhly and Solel
[
11
]
discussed the Wold decomposition for the isometric covariant representations of
C^{*}
-correspondences based on
[
18
]
.

Muhly and Solel
[
12
]
presented isometric dilation for completely contractive covariant representations of
C^{*}
-correspondences. Solel
[
24
]
proved that every completely contractive representations of product system of
C^{*}
-correspondences over the semigroup
\mathbb{N}_{0}^{2}
can be dilated to isometric representations. Solel
[
25
]
studied completely contractive representations of product system of
C^{*}
-correspondences over
\mathbb{N}_{0}^{k},
and gave a necessary and sufficient condition for such a representation to have a regular isometric dilation. In
[
22
]
Skalski established some sufficient conditions for the existence of a not necessarily regular isometric dilation of a completely contractive representation of the product system and discussed the difference between regular and
*
-regular dilations. He also analysed the case of product systems associated with higher-rank graphs. The main purpose of this paper is to follow this direction along
[
5
]
and to investigate which type of representation dilates completely contractive covariant representations of product system.

\displaystyle\|(I_{E^{\otimes n}}\otimes\widetilde{T}_{m}^{*})(\xi_{n}\otimes\widetilde{T}(\xi\otimes h))\|

Theorem 2.4
.

Assume
\mathbb{E}
to be a product system over
\mathbb{N}^{2}_{0}
. Let
(\sigma,T^{(1)},T^{(2)})
be a completely contractive covariant representation of
\mathbb{E}
on
\mathcal{H}
such that
(\sigma,T^{(1)})
is pure and
\dim(\mathcal{D}_{*,T^{(1)}}\oplus(E_{1}\otimes\mathcal{D}_{*,T^{(2)}}))
=
\dim((E_{2}\otimes\mathcal{D}_{*,T^{(1)}})\oplus\mathcal{D}_{*,T^{(2)}})<\infty.
Then there exist an isometry
\Pi_{1}:\mathcal{H}\longrightarrow\mathcal{F}(E_{1})\otimes\mathcal{D}_{*,T^{(1)}}
and a completely contractive bi-module map
{\Theta}:E_{2}\to B(\mathcal{D}_{*,T^{(1)}},F(E_{1})\otimes\mathcal{D}_{*,T^{(1)}})
such that

\displaystyle\Pi_{1}\sigma(a)=\rho(a)\Pi_{1},(I_{E_{1}}\otimes\Pi_{1})\widetilde{T}^{(1)*}=\widetilde{S}^{(1)*}\Pi_{1}\>\>\mbox{and}\>\>{(I_{E_{2}}\otimes\Pi_{1})\widetilde{T}^{(2)}}^{*}=\widetilde{M}^{*}_{\Theta}\Pi_{1},

for all
a\in\mathcal{M}.

Proof.

Let
(\sigma,T^{(1)})
be a pure completely contractive covariant representation of
E_{1}
on
\mathcal{H},
then from Theorem
1.6
, there exists an isometry
\Pi_{1}:\mathcal{H}\longrightarrow\mathcal{F}(E_{1})\otimes\mathcal{D}_{*,T^{(1)}}
defined by

\Pi_{1}h=\sum_{k\geq 0}(I_{E_{1}^{\otimes k}}\otimes\Delta_{*}(T^{(1)}))\widetilde{T}^{(1)^{*}}_{k}h,\>\>\>h\in\mathcal{H}

such that

\displaystyle\Pi_{1}\sigma(a)=\rho(a)\Pi_{1}\>\>\mbox{and}\>\>(I_{E_{1}}\otimes\Pi_{1})\widetilde{T}^{(1)*}=\widetilde{S}^{(1)*}\Pi_{1},

where
(\rho,S^{(1)})
is an induced representation of
E_{1}
induced by
\sigma|_{\mathcal{D}_{*,T^{(1)}}}.
We only need to show that
{(I_{E_{2}}\otimes\Pi_{1})\widetilde{T}^{(2)}}^{*}=\widetilde{M}^{*}_{\Theta}\Pi_{1}.

Define a completely contractive bi-module map
{\Theta}:E_{2}\to B(\mathcal{D}_{*,T^{(1)}},F(E_{1})\otimes\mathcal{D}_{*,T^{(1)}})
by
\Theta(\xi)h=\widetilde{\Theta}(\xi\otimes h)
where
\xi\in E_{2},h\in\mathcal{D}_{*,T^{(1)}}
and
\widetilde{\Theta}=A^{*}\oplus\bigoplus_{m=0}^{\infty}(I_{E_{1}^{\otimes m+1}}\otimes C^{*})(I_{E_{1}}\otimes\widetilde{D}_{m}^{*})B^{*}.
Define a corresponding contractive linear map
\widetilde{M}_{\Theta}:E_{2}\otimes F(E_{1})\otimes\mathcal{D}_{*,T^{(1)}}\longrightarrow F(E_{1})\otimes\mathcal{D}_{*,T^{(1)}}
by

\widetilde{M}_{\Theta}=\widetilde{S}^{(1)}_{n}(I_{E_{1}^{\otimes n}}\otimes\widetilde{\Theta})(t_{2,1}^{(1,n)}\otimes I_{\mathcal{D}_{*,T^{(1)}}}),

and it satisfies
\widetilde{M}_{\Theta}(\phi_{2}(a)\otimes I_{F(E_{1})\otimes\mathcal{D}_{*,T^{(1)}}})=\rho(a)\widetilde{M}_{\Theta}
for
a\in\mathcal{M}.
For
\eta_{n}\in{E_{2}}\otimes E_{1}^{\otimes n}\otimes\mathcal{D}_{*,T^{(1)}},h\in\mathcal{H}
and from Lemma
2.1
we have

\displaystyle\langle\widetilde{M}^{*}_{\Theta}\Pi_{1}h,\eta_{n}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(I_{E_{1}^{\otimes k}}\otimes\Delta_{*}(T^{(1)}))\widetilde{T}^{(1)^{*}}_{k}h,\widetilde{S}_{n}^{(1)}(I_{E_{1}^{\otimes n}}\otimes\widetilde{\Theta})(t_{2,1}^{(1,n)}\otimes I_{\mathcal{D}_{*,T^{(1)}}})\eta_{n}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle(I_{E_{1}^{\otimes n}}\otimes\Delta_{*}(T^{(1)}))\widetilde{T}^{(1)^{*}}_{n}h,(I_{E_{1}^{\otimes n}}\otimes A^{*})(t_{2,1}^{(1,n)}\otimes I_{\mathcal{D}_{*,T^{(1)}}})\eta_{n}\rangle

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\langle(I_{E_{1}^{\otimes n+m+1}}\otimes\Delta_{*}(T^{(1)}))\widetilde{T}^{(1)^{*}}_{n+m+1}h,

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad(I_{E_{1}^{\otimes n}}\otimes(I_{E_{1}}\otimes(I_{E_{1}^{\otimes m}}\otimes C^{*})\widetilde{D}_{m}^{*})B^{*})(t_{2,1}^{(1,n)}\otimes I_{\mathcal{D}_{*,T^{(1)}}})\eta_{n}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle(I_{E_{1}^{\otimes n}}\otimes A\Delta_{*}(T^{(1)}))\widetilde{T}^{(1)^{*}}_{n}h,(t_{2,1}^{(1,n)}\otimes I_{\mathcal{D}_{*,T^{(1)}}})\eta_{n}\rangle

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\langle(I_{E_{1}^{\otimes n}}\otimes(B(I_{E_{1}}\otimes\widetilde{D}_{m})))(I_{E_{1}^{\otimes n+m+1}}\otimes C\Delta_{*}(T^{(1)}))\widetilde{T}^{(1)^{*}}_{n+m+1}h,

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad(t_{2,1}^{(1,n)}\otimes I_{\mathcal{D}_{*,T^{(1)}}})\eta_{n}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\big(I_{E_{1}^{\otimes n}}\otimes A\Delta_{*}(T^{(1)})+\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}(I_{E_{1}^{\otimes n}}\otimes(B(I_{E_{1}}\otimes\widetilde{D}_{m})))

\displaystyle\quad\quad(I_{E_{1}^{\otimes n+m+1}}\otimes C\Delta_{*}(T^{(1)}))\widetilde{T}^{(1)^{*}}_{m+1})\big)\widetilde{T}^{(1)^{*}}_{n}h,(t_{2,1}^{(1,n)}\otimes I_{\mathcal{D}_{*,T^{(1)}}})\eta_{n}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle(I_{E_{1}^{\otimes n}}\otimes\big(A\Delta_{*}(T^{(1)})+\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}B(I_{E_{1}}\otimes\widetilde{D}_{m})(I_{E_{1}^{\otimes m+1}}\otimes C\Delta_{*}(T^{(1)}))\widetilde{T}^{(1)^{*}}_{m+1})\big)\widetilde{T}^{(1)^{*}}_{n}h,

\displaystyle\quad\quad(t_{2,1}^{(1,n)}\otimes I_{\mathcal{D}_{*,T^{(1)}}})\eta_{n}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle(I_{E_{1}^{\otimes n}}\otimes(I_{E_{2}}\otimes\Delta_{*}(T^{(1)}))\widetilde{T}^{(2)^{*}}\big)\widetilde{T}^{(1)^{*}}_{n}h,(t_{2,1}^{(1,n)}\otimes I_{\mathcal{D}_{*,T^{(1)}}})\eta_{n}\rangle.

On the other hand, since

\displaystyle\langle{(I_{E_{2}}\otimes\Pi_{1})\widetilde{T}^{(2)}}^{*}h,\eta_{n}\rangle=\langle{(I_{E_{2}}\otimes\sum_{k\geq 0}(I_{E_{1}^{\otimes k}}\otimes\Delta_{*}(T^{(1)}))\widetilde{T}^{(1)^{*}}_{k})\widetilde{T}^{(2)}}^{*}h,\eta_{n}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle{(I_{E_{2}}\otimes(I_{E_{1}^{\otimes n}}\otimes\Delta_{*}(T^{(1)}))\widetilde{T}^{(1)^{*}}_{n})\widetilde{T}^{(2)}}^{*}h,\eta_{n}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle h,{\widetilde{T}^{(2)}(I_{E_{2}}\otimes\widetilde{T}^{(1)}_{n}(I_{E_{1}^{\otimes n}}\otimes\Delta_{*}(T^{(1)})))}\eta_{n}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle h,{\widetilde{T}^{(1)}_{n}(I_{E_{1}^{\otimes n}}\otimes\widetilde{T}^{(2)}(I_{E_{2}}\otimes\Delta_{*}(T^{(1)})))}(t_{2,1}^{(1,n)}\otimes I_{\mathcal{D}_{*,T^{(1)}}})\eta_{n}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle(I_{E_{1}^{\otimes n}}\otimes(I_{E_{2}}\otimes\Delta_{*}(T^{(1)}))\widetilde{T}^{(2)^{*}})\widetilde{T}^{(1)^{*}}_{n}h,(t_{2,1}^{(1,n)}\otimes I_{\mathcal{D}_{*,T^{(1)}}})\eta_{n}\rangle=\langle\widetilde{M}^{*}_{\Theta}\Pi_{1}h,\eta_{n}\rangle,

we get
{(I_{E_{2}}\otimes\Pi_{1})\widetilde{T}^{(2)}}^{*}=\widetilde{M}^{*}_{\Theta}\Pi_{1}.
This complets the proof.
∎

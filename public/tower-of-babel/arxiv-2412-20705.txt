Global smooth solutions to the irrotational Euler-Riesz system in three dimensions
Young-Pil Choi, Jinwook Jung, Yoonjung Lee

Abstract
. This paper investigates the global dynamics of the Euler–Riesz system in three dimensions, focusing on the well-posedness and large-time behavior of solutions near equilibrium. The system generalizes classical interactions by incorporating the Riesz interactions ∇ ( − Δ ) − σ / 2 ( ρ − 1 ) \nabla(-\Delta)^{-\sigma/2}(\rho-1) . We show that the system admits a global smooth solution for small irrotational initial perturbations. Specifically, we establish that if the initial data is sufficiently small, the solution remains regular globally in time and decays over time at a rate dependent on σ \sigma .

1.
Introduction

This paper investigates the Euler–Riesz system in three dimensions, described by the equations:

(1.1)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}\rho+\nabla\cdot(\rho u)=0,\quad(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}_{+},\cr&\partial_{t}(\rho u)+\nabla\cdot(\rho u\otimes u)+\nabla(c_{\gamma}\rho^{\gamma})=-\rho\frac{\nabla}{|\nabla|^{\sigma}}(\rho-1)\end{aligned}

with initial data

(\rho(x,0),u(x,0))=(\rho_{0}(x),u_{0}(x)).

Here,
\rho=\rho(x,t)
and
u=u(x,t)
represent the density and velocity of the fluid, respectively, at time
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
and position
x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
.
The pressure term is expressed as
c_{\gamma}\rho^{\gamma}
with a positive constant
c_{\gamma}
and
\gamma>1
. The background state is assumed to be steady and uniform, and for simplicity, it is taken to be unity. The operator
|\nabla|^{\sigma}
is defined by
(-\Delta)^{\sigma/2}
, and this paper focuses on the range
0<\sigma<2
.

The Euler–Riesz system (
1.1
) encompasses not only the classical Coulomb interaction with
\sigma=2
but also the so-called
pure Manev
correction
[
2
]
with
\sigma=1
proposed as a modification of Newtonian gravitation. The Riesz interaction serves as a generalization and has been extensively studied in the physics literature, e.g.,
[
1
,
26
]
. It also appears in the study of equilibrium properties of systems of point particles interacting via Coulomb or Riesz interactions and confined by an external potential
[
24
,
27
,
28
]
.

1.1.
Main result

The objective of the current work is to investigate the global dynamics of the Euler–Riesz system (
1.1
) for
0<\sigma<2
. Specifically, we aim to address the global-in-time existence and uniqueness of regular solutions to the system (
1.1
) near the equilibrium
(\rho,u)=(1,0)
as well as its large-time behaviors. The local-in-time existence and uniqueness of regular solutions to (
1.1
) have been established in
[
3
]
, which also provided sufficient conditions under which finite-time singularity formation can occur.

A significant challenge in proving the global existence of solutions to the system (
1.1
) arises from the absence of a direct dissipation term in the system. This lack of dissipation complicates the analysis since it can lead to the finite-time breakdown of smoothness of solutions. To address this, some approaches have incorporated linear velocity damping. For instance, the global existence and large-time behavior of solutions to the pressureless system with linear damping were investigated in
[
4
]
for small initial perturbations. Additionally, recent work has explored global existence in Besov spaces with critical regularity
[
6
]
.

In a related context, Danchin and Ducomet
[
7
]
demonstrated that for
1\leq\sigma<2
, the system (
1.1
) admits a unique global solution with zero background state under a sufficiently small, regular initial density and a dispersive spectral condition on the initial velocity that causes fluid particles to spread. The complete well-posedness of (
1.1
) for
0<\sigma<1
, including both pressureless and pressure cases, has been recently resolved in
[
5
]
. Further insights into the global dynamics have been obtained from Guo’s work
[
10
]
for
\sigma=2
. The Poisson interaction force introduces oscillatory behavior that results in a linearized system resembling the Klein–Gordon equation with the dispersion relation

p(r)=(1+r^{2})^{1/2}.

Utilizing Shatah’s normal transformation and the Klein–Gordon effect, a global irrotational smooth solution to the 3D Euler–Poisson equations is constructed under small smooth perturbations. This result is complemented by similar studies in
[
20
,
25
]
for the two-dimensional case and
[
11
]
for the one-dimensional case. Guo’s work highlights that dispersion due to the Poisson interaction force can mitigate the finite-time blow-up phenomenon. We also refer to
[
8
,
12
,
13
,
14
,
16
,
17
,
19
]
for the related Euler models.

From this perspective, the Euler system without interaction force presents a completely different scenario in contrast to the Euler–Poisson system. Following Makino’s symmetrization
[
23
]
, the pure Euler system is understood as quadratically nonlinear wave equations with a dispersion relation
p(r)=r
. For these nonlinear wave equations, the lifespan
T_{\varepsilon}
of classical solutions is known to be of the order of
\exp(C/\varepsilon)
for small initial data of size
\varepsilon
. Notably, Sideris
[
29
,
30
]
demonstrated that the lifespan of classical solutions satisfies
T_{\varepsilon}<\exp(C/\varepsilon^{2})
and
T_{\varepsilon}>\exp(C/\varepsilon)
for small smooth perturbation
(\rho_{\varepsilon},u_{\varepsilon})=(\varepsilon n_{0}+1,\varepsilon u_{0})
, where
(n_{0},u_{0})
is compactly supported around the constant state
(\rho,u)=(1,0)
. The sharp asymptotic behavior of
T_{\varepsilon}\sim\exp(C/\varepsilon)
for spherically symmetric Eulerian flows was obtained in
[
9
]
. These results indicate that singularity formation is inevitable in the pure Euler system, even with sufficiently small initial perturbations.

The central question addressed in the present work is whether a global irrotational solution exists for the 3D Euler–Riesz system (
1.1
), extending the work of Guo
[
9
]
. Even though the Riesz interaction
0<\sigma<2
is more singular than the Coulombian force (
\sigma=2
), it is expected to play a significant role in ensuring global-in-time stability near the equilibrium
(\rho,u)=(1,0)
. Throughout this paper, we consider an irrotational initial flow:

\nabla\times u\equiv 0

which is preserved in time. For the convenience of presentation, we set
c_{\gamma}=1/3
and
\gamma=3
(other cases
\gamma\in(1,1+2/3]
can be treated similarly), and then we let
n=\rho-1
to obtain the symmetrized form of (
1.1
):

(1.2)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}n+\nabla\cdot u+\nabla\cdot(nu)=0,\quad(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}_{+},\cr&\partial_{t}u+\nabla n+\nabla(\frac{1}{2}n^{2}+\frac{1}{2}|u|^{2})=-\frac{\nabla}{|\nabla|^{\sigma}}n\end{aligned}

subject to the initial data

(n(x,0),u(x,0))=(n_{0}(x):=\rho_{0}(x)-1,u_{0}(x)),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}.

We study the global well-posedness of the reformulated system (
1.2
) with small initial data, demonstrating that (
1.1
) admits a smooth global solution under a small irrotational initial perturbation. To facilitate our analysis, we introduce the following function spaces: for
s\geq 16

\displaystyle\|f\|_{X}:=\||\nabla|^{-\frac{2-\sigma}{2}}f\|_{H^{2s}}+(1+t)^{\beta_{\sigma}}\|f\|_{W^{s,p}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|f\|_{Y}:=\||\nabla|^{-\frac{2-\sigma}{2}}f\|_{H^{2s}}+\|f\|_{W^{s+6(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}),p^{\prime}}}

where
p^{\prime}
is the Hölder conjugate of
p
. Here,
\beta_{\sigma}
is defined by

(1.3)

\beta_{\sigma}=\begin{cases}3(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})\quad\text{when }0<\sigma\leq 1,\\[2.84526pt]
\frac{8}{3}(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})\quad\text{when }1<\sigma<2,\end{cases}

and
p
is chosen according to

(1.4)

\displaystyle 6<p<\infty\quad\text{when }0<\sigma\leq 1,

\displaystyle 8<p<\infty\quad\text{when }1<\sigma<2.

Then our main result is stated as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

There exists
\varepsilon>0
such that for any initial perturbation
(n_{0}+1,u_{0})
satisfying

\|(n_{0},u_{0})\|_{Y}\leq\varepsilon\quad\text{and}\quad\nabla\times u_{0}=0,

the system (
1.1
) admits a global solution
(n+1,u)
with

\sup_{0\leq t<\infty}\|(n,u)(t)\|_{X}\leq 2\varepsilon.

Moreover, there exists a constant
C>0
independent of
t
such that

(1.5)

\|(n,u)(t)\|_{W_{x}^{s,p}}\leq C(1+t)^{-\beta_{\sigma}},

where
\beta_{\sigma}
and
p
are given as in (
1.3
) and (
1.4
), respectively.

Remark 1.1
.

We provide some remarks on conditions in Theorem
1.1
.

(i)

The lower bound on
p
in (
1.4
) is required to ensure that
(1+t)^{-\beta_{\sigma}}
is integrable in time
t
.

(ii)

The assumption on initial data does not require a neutrality condition.

Remark 1.2
.

The decay rate specified in (
1.5
) exhibits a marginal loss due to
p<\infty
. The critical case
p=\infty
remains uncovered due to technical difficulties, e.g. (
2.3
). Moreover, we would like to emphasize that the decay rate is obtained through linear flow analysis, as detailed in Proposition
3.1
below. Within this context, we believe this rate is optimal within our strategic framework.

Remark 1.3
.

For the case
\sigma=2
, Guo
[
10
]
showed that a global-in-time existence of smooth irrotational solution to (
1.1
) satisfying

\|(n,u)(t)\|_{L^{p}}\leq C(1+t)^{-3(1/2-1/p)}

with
6<p<\infty
. In comparison, our theorem indicates that for
1<\sigma<2
, the solution decays at a rate of
(1+t)^{-8/3(1/2-1/p)}
, which is slower than the rate for
\sigma=2
due to the dispersion relation of the linearized equation having zero Gaussian curvature at some point. Interestingly, for
0<\sigma\leq 1
, the decay rate recovers the same rate as in the case
\sigma=2
.

1.2.
Difficulties and strategy of the proof

The primary challenge in analyzing the Euler–Riesz system arises from the Riesz interaction force, which causes the linearized equation to differ from the Klein–Gordon flow. By differentiating the Euler–Riesz system (
1.2
) with respect to time, we obtain

(1.6)

\partial_{tt}w-\Delta w+|\nabla|^{2-\sigma}w=F(w),

where
w=(n,u)^{T}
and
F
is a vector-valued nonlinear operator. This formulation is due to the curl-free condition. The dispersive characteristics of this equation are given by

(1.7)

p(r)=(r^{2}+r^{2-\sigma})^{1/2}.

It is observed that
p(r)
is non-degenerate when
\sigma=2
, corresponding to the Klein–Gordon flow with a decay rate of
(1+t)^{-3/2}
. In contrast, when
\sigma=0
, the flow is degenerate, leading to a vanishing Gaussian curvature and a slower decay rate of
(1+t)^{-1}
. However, the case for
0<\sigma<2
remains largely unexplored, since the geometric structure of
p(r)
is uniquely determined by
\sigma
. In addition, handling the singularity in the nonlinearity of (
1.6
) is challenging, especially when using the space-time resonance method. In fact, one of the nonlinearities can be viewed as a bilinear operator with a kernel of a fraction form whose denominator is

\Phi=p(|\xi|)-p(|\xi-\eta|)-p(|\eta|).

The value of
\Phi
can be zero over a significant region, and there is no null structure to address this singularity. To deal with these challenges, we need to explore the strict asymptotic behaviors of
p(r)
,
p^{\prime}(r)
, and
p^{\prime\prime}(r)
across low frequencies (
r<1
) and high frequencies (
r\geq 1
), taking into account the value of
\sigma
. Another difficulty is the loss of regularity in the energy estimates. Using standard methods, we encounter terms like
\|\nabla\Lambda^{-\sigma}n\|_{\dot{H}^{2s}}
, which involves a regularity order of
2s+1-\sigma
. It is not feasible to bound this term using the
X
-norm of
n
for
0<\sigma<1
. To overcome this, we need to develop weighted energy estimates that are comparable to classical ones, under a suitable smallness condition on the solution.

Strategy of the proof

We outline the proof of Theorem
1.1
and describe some of the strategies involved. Consider the function:

\alpha(t,x)=\frac{p(|\nabla|)}{|\nabla|}n(t,x)+i\frac{\nabla}{|\nabla|}\cdot u(t,x),

where
p
is given as in (
1.7
).
From (
1.2
), it follows that

(1.8)

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}-ip(|\nabla|))\alpha=\mathcal{Q}(\alpha),

where
\mathcal{Q}(\alpha)
has quadratic forms:

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}(\alpha)

\displaystyle=\sum_{r,l=1}^{2}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{ix\cdot\xi}m_{r,l}(\xi,\eta)\widehat{\alpha_{r}}(\xi-\eta)\widehat{\alpha_{l}}(\eta)\,d\xi d\eta

with

\displaystyle m_{r,l}(\xi,\eta)

\displaystyle=c_{1}p(\xi)r(\xi)r(\xi-\eta)r(\eta)+c_{2}|\xi|r(\xi-\eta)r(\eta).

Here,
p(\xi)=p(|\xi|)
is defined as (
1.7
), and the map
r(x)
is used for
x\mapsto\frac{x}{|x|}
or
x\mapsto\frac{|x|}{p(x)}
, which obeys

\|r(\nabla)f\|_{L^{p}}=\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}\{r\hat{f}\}\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p}}\qquad 1<p<\infty,

and thus it is almost negligible when we estimate it in
L^{p}
spaces. By the Duhamel principle, we have

(1.9)

\alpha(t,x)=e^{itp(|\nabla|)}\alpha(0,x)+\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-\tau)p(|\nabla|)}\mathcal{Q}(\alpha)(\tau,x)\,d\tau.

The main ingredient in establishing energy estimates is
L^{p}
decay estimates.
We need to study the decay estimate of the linear solution to (
1.8
) where
\mathcal{Q}\equiv 0
.
Based on the bootstrapping argument, we deduce a decay rate of the solution to the nonlinear problem (
1.8
) from that of the linear solution in the
L^{p}
sense.
For this, the decay rate is required to be integrable over
\mathbb{R}_{+}
, and the nonlinearity
\mathcal{Q}(\alpha)
could be controlled by terms
\|\alpha\|_{X}^{2}
of at least order
2
. However, due to the lack of regularity for the solution, it is not straightforward to estimate the nonlinear term.

Step 1
(Shatah’s normal transformation).
We apply Shatah’s normal transformation (performing an integration by parts with respect to
t
appropriately) to the Duhamel term. Then it can be expressed as

(1.10)

\text{Duhamel term}=g(t)-e^{itp(|\nabla|)}g(0)+h(t),

where
g
has quadratic forms of
\alpha
(see (
2.10
) below), and
h
is a sum of cubic forms
h_{r,l}
for
r,l=1,2
defined by

(1.11)

\displaystyle h_{r,l}(t,x)

\displaystyle=-2i\int_{0}^{t}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{ix\cdot\xi}e^{i(t-\tau)p(|\nabla|)}\frac{m_{r,l}(\xi,\eta)}{\Phi_{r,l}(\xi,\eta)}\widehat{\alpha_{r}}(\tau,\xi-\eta)\widehat{\mathcal{Q}}(\tau,\eta)\,d\eta d\xi d\tau

with

\Phi_{r,l}(\xi,\eta)=p(\xi)+(-1)^{r}p(\xi-\eta)+(-1)^{l}p(\eta).

Thanks to the symmetry, the argument for
(r,l)=(1,1)
can cover the other cases as well.
It is observed that the phase
\Phi_{1,1}
can be zero on the significant set of

|\eta|^{\frac{2-\sigma}{2}}|\xi-\eta|^{\frac{2-\sigma}{2}}=0

in a stark contrast to the case
\sigma=2
(see Lemma
4.1
). This makes estimating the nonlinear term
h
more challenging in
Step 3
.

Step 2
(Dispersive estimates for the linear system). We analyze the decay estimates for the linear flow generated by the operator
e^{itp(|\nabla|)}
. Precisely, our goal is to establish:

(1.12)

\|e^{itp(|\nabla|)}f\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim(1+t)^{-\beta_{\sigma}}\|f\|_{W^{6(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}),p^{\prime}}}

for
2\leq p<\infty
with
\beta_{\sigma}
given as in (
1.3
).
As mentioned above, the decay rate of the evolution operator
e^{itp(|\nabla|)}
heavily relies on the geometric structure of the dispersive feature
p(r)=(r^{2}+r^{2-\sigma})^{1/2}
, which varies significantly with the parameter
\sigma
.

When
0<\sigma\leq 1
, the dispersion relation
p(r)
is non-degenerate, leading to a decay rate comparable to that of the Klein–Gordon equation. In this regime, the non-degenerate nature of
p(r)
ensures robust dispersive effects, allowing us to derive relatively strong decay estimates for the linear flow. However, for
1<\sigma<2
, the situation becomes more complex due to the presence of a degeneracy point in
p(r)
. This degeneracy results in weak dispersive effects, causing the decay rate to be slower than in the non-degenerate case. Complicating matters further, the dispersion
p(r)
is a non-homogeneous type, making decay estimates less straightforward since
p(r)
exhibits different behaviors at low and high frequencies.

To address these challenges, inspired by
[
17
]
and
[
18
]
, we separate the analysis into low-and high-frequency components. Using the distinct scaling behaviors of
p(r)
for each component, we employ a different approach on
\sigma
, because
p(r)
for
\sigma=0
behaves similarly to the linear wave equation, while the behavior of
p(r)
for
\sigma=2
resembles that of the Klein–Gordon equation. In particular, for
1<\sigma<2
, we obtain a decay rate better than that of the wave flow by isolating the degeneracy point and employing the stationary phase method (see Lemma
3.2
). This careful analysis is essential for understanding the dispersive properties of the system and for establishing the decay estimates necessary for the subsequent steps in the proof.

Step 3
(Control of the singularity of the kernel). We establish the following
L^{p}
decay estimates:

(1.13)

\|\alpha(t)\|_{W^{s,p}}\lesssim(1+t)^{-\beta_{\sigma}}\left(\|\alpha(0)\|_{Y}+\sup_{0<t<T^{\ast}}\|\alpha(t)\|_{X}^{2}+\sup_{0<t<T^{\ast}}\|\alpha(t)\|_{X}^{3}\right).

By applying the decay estimate (
1.12
) to the Duhamel principle expression (
1.9
), we focus on estimating the terms in (
1.10
). For clarity, we specifically consider the cubic term
h
. That is,

\|h\|_{W^{s,p}}\lesssim(1+t)^{-\beta_{\sigma}}\sup_{0<t<T^{\ast}}\|\alpha(t)\|_{X}^{3}.

To achieve this, we employ some bilinear estimates to get
\|\alpha\|_{X}^{3}
. However,
the kernel
\frac{m_{r,l}}{\Phi_{r,l}}
of the bilinear operator for (
1.11
) is not locally bounded due to the singularity of
\frac{1}{\Phi_{r,l}}
as mentioned before.
To overcome this challenge, we introduce the following modified kernel:

\mathfrak{M}_{r,l}=\frac{|\xi|^{\frac{2-\sigma}{2}}|\eta|^{\frac{2-\sigma}{2}}|\xi-\eta|^{\frac{2-\sigma}{2}}}{\langle\xi-\eta\rangle^{2\lambda}\langle\eta\rangle^{2\lambda}\Phi_{r,l}},

where
\lambda>0
is large enough and
\langle\cdot\rangle
denotes the Japanese bracket defined as
\langle x\rangle=(1+x^{2})^{1/2}
.

In this step, it is necessary to employ the negative Sobolev norm
\dot{H}^{-(2-\sigma)/2}
of the solution to incorporate
\mathfrak{M}_{r,l}
as the kernel of the bilinear operator. We then employ bilinear estimates developed by Gusafson–Nakanishi–Tsai
[
15
]
, where the kernel is shown to belong to the spaces
L_{\xi}^{\infty}\dot{H}_{\eta}^{k}
and
L_{\eta}^{\infty}\dot{H}_{\xi}^{k}
. A significant portion of the analysis is dedicated to proving that
\mathfrak{M}_{r,l}\in L_{\xi}^{\infty}\dot{H}_{\eta}^{k}\cap L_{\eta}^{\infty}\dot{H}_{\xi}^{k}
, with the regularity index
k
required to be greater than
1
in order to derive the
L^{p}
decay estimates. The proof requires a deep understanding of the interplay between the angles and magnitudes of
\xi
,
\xi-\eta
, and
\eta
, based on the asymptotic behavior of the relations
p,p^{\prime}
and
p^{\prime\prime}
. Unfortunately, these asymptotic behaviors are not only affected by the value of
\sigma
but also by whether
\xi
lies in the low-frequency region
|\xi|<1
or the high-frequency region
|\xi|\geq 1
. To address these complexities, we divide the analysis into three cases, performing a detailed examination of each scenario by considering the possible configurations of frequency components in both low- and high-frequency regimes.

Step 4
(Time weighted energy estimates).
In this step, we aim to have the a priori estimates for the solution
\alpha
in the Sobolev spaces
H^{2s}
.
The required
\dot{H}^{-(2-\sigma)/2}
estimates for (
1.13
) can be derived since the kernel
m
in
\mathcal{Q}
has a gain in
p(\xi)
or
|\xi|
, which adequately addresses the negative order of the Sobolev index. As a result, we obtain the following bound.

\|\alpha(t)\|_{\dot{H}_{x}^{-\frac{2-\sigma}{2}}}\lesssim\|\alpha(0)\|_{Y}+\sup_{0<t<T^{\ast}}\|\alpha(t)\|_{X}^{2}.

Utilizing the decay estimate (
1.13
), we can further develop the
H^{2s}
estimates as follows:

\|\alpha(t)\|_{H^{2s}}\lesssim\|\alpha(0)\|_{Y}+\sup_{0<t<T^{\ast}}\|\alpha(t)\|_{X}^{3/2}.

This estimate is derived via the application of Grönwall inequality. Specifically, by using the identities

{\rm Re}(\alpha)=\frac{p(|\nabla|)}{|\nabla|}n\quad\text{and}\quad{\rm Im}(\alpha)=\frac{\nabla}{|\nabla|}\cdot u,

we can express the energy functional
\|\alpha\|_{\dot{H}^{2s}}
for the higher order term as

(1.14)

\|(n,u)(t)\|_{\dot{H}^{2s}}^{2}+\|n(t)\|_{\dot{H}^{2s-\frac{\sigma}{2}}}^{2}.

In a rather standard way, the following term associated with the Riesz interaction force appears

(1.15)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla|\nabla|^{2s-\sigma}n\cdot\nabla\left(|\nabla|^{2s-2}\nabla\cdot(nu)\right)dx,

where the regularity order for
n
is approximately
1+2s-\sigma
. This term is manageable when
1\leq\sigma<2
, but for
0<\sigma<1
, the Riesz potential introduces a challenging loss of regularity.

To address this challenge, we employ the modified Sobolev space introduced in
[
4
]
:

\|f\|_{\dot{\mathcal{H}}^{s}}:=\left\|(n+1)^{-\frac{1}{2}}|\nabla|^{s}f\right\|_{L^{2}}\qquad s>0,

which satisfies
\|f\|_{\dot{\mathcal{H}}^{s}}\sim\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}
under the condition that
\sup_{0<t<T^{\ast}}\|n(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}<1/2
. It is noteworthy that Theorem
1.1
does not require any additional assumptions since it inherently ensures the smallness of the global solution. Consequently, we adopt the energy function

\|(n,u)(t)\|_{\dot{H}^{2s}}^{2}+\|n(t)\|_{\dot{\mathcal{H}}^{2s-\frac{\sigma}{2}}}^{2}

in place of (
1.14
), thereby eliminating the problematic term (
1.15
). With strategic cancellations, we successfully close the higher-order estimates.

1.3.
Notation and preliminaries

Throughout this paper, the symbols
C
and
c_{i}
for any
i\geq 1
will represent positive universal constants, which may vary from one occurrence to another. We write
A\lesssim B
to indicate that there exists a constant
C
, independent of time
T
, such that
A\leq CB
, and
A\sim B
if there exists
C>1
such that
A/C\leq B\leq CA
. The phase function
p(r)
is assumed to be radial, and sometimes we abuse the notation
p(r)=p(|r|)
for convenience. Additionally,
\hat{f}
and
\mathcal{F}(f)
stand for the Fourier transform of
f
, and
\mathcal{F}^{-1}
represents the inverse Fourier transform.

The Sobolev space
W^{s,p}
for
1\leq p\leq\infty
consists of functions
f\in L^{p}
such that

\|f\|_{W^{s,p}}:=\sum_{|k|\leq s}\|\partial^{k}f\|_{L^{p}}<\infty.

In particular for
1<p<\infty
, an equivalent norm is given by

\|f\|_{W^{s,p}}\sim\sum_{\alpha\leq s}\||\nabla|^{\alpha}f\|_{L^{p}}

as detailed in
[
33
, Appendix A]
.

The following lemma provides a useful tool that will be frequently used in this paper:

Lemma 1.1
.

The following inequalities hold;

(i)

Let
s>0
,
p\in(1,\infty)

If
f\in W^{s,p}
and
g\in W^{s,p}
,
then we have

(1.16)

\|fg\|_{\dot{W}^{s,p}}\lesssim\|f\|_{\dot{W}^{s,p}}\|g\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\|g\|_{\dot{W}^{s,p}},

and moreover

\|fg\|_{W^{s,p}}\lesssim\|f\|_{W^{s,p}}\|g\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\|g\|_{W^{s,p}}.

(ii)

Let
s>0
. If
f\in\dot{W}^{1,\infty}\cap\dot{H}^{s}
and
g\in L^{\infty}\cap\dot{H}^{s-1}
, then

(1.17)

\|[f,|\nabla|^{s}]g\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}\|g\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\nabla f\|_{L^{\infty}}\|g\|_{\dot{H}^{s-1}}.

We define the Littlewood–Paley operator
P_{N}
by

\mathcal{F}(P_{N}f)=\psi(\xi/N)\hat{f}-\psi(2\xi/N)\hat{f}=:\varphi_{N}(\xi)\hat{f}

for any dyadic number
N>0
.
Here,
\psi(x):\mathbb{R}^{3}\rightarrow[0,1]
is a smooth radial function such that
supp(\psi)\subseteq\{x\,:\,|x|\leq 2\}
and
\psi(x)=1
if
|x|\leq 1
. Additionally, we denote
P_{<N}:=\sum_{k<N}P_{k}
and
P_{\geq N}:=1-P_{<N}
.

The Littlewood–Paley operator
P_{N}
satisfies the well-known Bernstein inequality:

\||\nabla|^{\pm s}P_{N}f\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim N^{\pm s}\|P_{N}f\|_{L^{p}}

for all
s
and
1\leq p\leq\infty
. The Besov space
B_{p,q}^{s}
consists of functions
f\in\mathcal{S}
such that

\|f\|_{B_{p,q}^{s}}:=\left(\|P_{<1}f\|_{L^{p}}^{q}+\sum_{N\geq 1}^{\infty}\|N^{s}P_{N}f\|_{L^{p}}^{q}\right)^{1/q}<\infty.

2.
Normal transformation

To facilitate the analysis of our main system, we introduce a transformation that combines the original variables
n(t,x)
and
u(t,x)
into a single complex-valued function
\alpha(t,x)
. This transformation is designed to simplify the structure of the equations and to make the nonlinear interactions more tractable. By expressing the system in terms of
\alpha(t,x)
, we can more effectively apply analytical techniques such as Fourier analysis and the Duhamel principle.

The transformation is defined as follows:

(2.1)

\alpha(t,x)=\frac{p(|\nabla|)}{|\nabla|}n(t,x)+i\frac{\nabla}{|\nabla|}\cdot u(t,x),

where
p(|\nabla|)=(|\nabla|^{2}+|\nabla|^{2-\sigma})^{1/2}
. Using this formulation, we can express the original variables
n
and
u
in terms of
\alpha
and its complex conjugate
\bar{\alpha}
:

(2.2)

n=\frac{|\nabla|}{p(|\nabla|)}\left(\frac{\alpha+\bar{\alpha}}{2}\right)\quad\text{and}\quad u=\frac{i\nabla}{|\nabla|}\left(\frac{\alpha-\bar{\alpha}}{2}\right).

Substituting these into the system (
1.2
) transforms it into

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}-ip(|\nabla|))\alpha

\displaystyle=-\frac{i}{4}\frac{p(|\nabla|)}{|\nabla|}\nabla\cdot\left(\frac{|\nabla|}{p(|\nabla|)}(\alpha+\bar{\alpha})\frac{\nabla}{|\nabla|}(\alpha-\bar{\alpha})\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{i}{8}|\nabla|\left(\frac{|\nabla|}{p(|\nabla|)}(\alpha+\bar{\alpha})\right)^{2}-\frac{i}{8}|\nabla|\left|\frac{\nabla}{|\nabla|}(\alpha-\bar{\alpha})\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=:\mathcal{Q}(\alpha).

Applying the Fourier transform, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}(\alpha)

\displaystyle=i\sum_{r,l=1}^{2}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{ix\cdot\xi}\left(\frac{(-1)^{l}m_{1}}{4}+\frac{m_{2}}{8}-\frac{(-1)^{l+r}m_{3}}{8}\right)\widehat{\alpha_{r}}(\xi-\eta)\widehat{\alpha_{l}}(\eta)\,d\xi d\eta

where
\alpha_{1}=\alpha
,
\alpha_{2}=\bar{\alpha}
, and

\displaystyle m_{1}=m_{1}(\xi,\eta)

\displaystyle=p(\xi)\frac{|\xi-\eta|}{p(\xi-\eta)}\frac{\xi\cdot\eta}{|\xi||\eta|},

\displaystyle m_{2}=m_{2}(\xi,\eta)

\displaystyle=|\xi|\frac{|\xi-\eta||\eta|}{p(\xi-\eta)p(\eta)},

\displaystyle m_{3}=m_{3}(\xi,\eta)

\displaystyle=|\xi|\frac{(\xi-\eta)\cdot\eta}{|\xi-\eta||\eta|},

with
p(\xi)=p(|\xi|)
. After symmetrizing between
\xi-\eta
and
\eta
, we simplify this as

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}(\alpha)

\displaystyle=\sum_{r,l=1}^{2}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{ix\cdot\xi}m_{r,l}(\xi,\eta)\widehat{\alpha_{r}}(\xi-\eta)\widehat{\alpha_{l}}(\eta)\,d\xi d\eta,

where

\displaystyle m_{r,l}(\xi,\eta)

\displaystyle=c_{1}p(\xi)\frac{|\xi-\eta|}{p(\xi-\eta)}\frac{\xi\cdot\eta}{|\xi||\eta|}+c_{2}p(\xi)\frac{|\eta|}{p(\eta)}\frac{\xi\cdot(\xi-\eta)}{|\xi||\xi-\eta|}+c_{3}|\xi|\frac{(\xi-\eta)\cdot\eta}{|\xi-\eta||\eta|}+c_{4}|\xi|\frac{|\xi-\eta||\eta|}{p(\xi-\eta)p(\eta)}

\displaystyle\sim p(\xi)r(\xi)r(\xi-\eta)r(\eta)+|\xi|r(\xi-\eta)r(\eta)

which led to
m_{r,l}(\xi,\eta)=m_{r,l}(\xi,\xi-\eta)
. Here, a constant
c_{j}
for
j=1,2,3,4
is determined by the value of
(r,l)
as
\pm i/8
or
0
,
but no matter what the value of the constant is.

r(x)
is denoted as functions
x\mapsto\frac{x}{|x|}
or
x\mapsto\frac{|x|}{p(x)}
,
which obeys

(2.3)

\|r(\nabla)f\|_{L^{p}}=\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}\{r\hat{f}\}\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p}},\qquad 1<p<\infty.

By the Duhamel principle, we get

(2.4)

\alpha(t)=e^{itp(|\nabla|)}\alpha(0)+\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-\tau)p(|\nabla|)}\mathcal{Q}(\alpha)(\tau,x)\,d\tau.

For the linear profile

(2.5)

b(t,x)=e^{-itp(|\nabla|)}\alpha(t,x),

this implies

(2.6)

\widehat{b}(t,\xi)=\widehat{\alpha}(0,\xi)+\int_{0}^{t}e^{-i\tau p(\xi)}\widehat{\mathcal{Q}(\alpha)}(\tau,\xi)\,d\tau,

and thus

(2.7)

\partial_{t}\widehat{b}(\xi)=e^{-itp(\xi)}\widehat{\mathcal{Q}(\alpha)}(t,\xi).

Then the Duhamel term in (
2.6
) can be further rewritten as

(2.8)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}e^{-i\tau p(\xi)}\widehat{\mathcal{Q}(\alpha)}(\tau,\xi)\,d\tau

\displaystyle=\sum_{r,l=1}^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{-i\tau p(\xi)}m_{r,l}\widehat{\alpha_{r}}(\xi-\eta)\widehat{\alpha_{l}}(\eta)\,d\eta d\tau

\displaystyle=\sum_{r,l=1}^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{-i\tau\Phi_{r,l}}m_{r,l}\widehat{b_{r}}(\xi-\eta)\widehat{b_{l}}(\eta)\,d\eta d\tau,

where
\Phi_{r,l}=\Phi_{r,l}(\xi,\eta):=p(\xi)+(-1)^{r}p(\xi-\eta)+(-1)^{l}p(\eta)
.
Using the integral by parts, we have

(2.9)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{-i\tau\Phi_{r,l}}m_{r,l}\widehat{b_{r}}(\tau,\xi-\eta)\widehat{b_{l}}(\tau,\eta)\,d\eta d\tau

\displaystyle=i\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\partial_{\tau}\left(e^{-i\tau\Phi_{r,l}}\right)\frac{m_{r,l}}{\Phi_{r,l}}\widehat{b_{r}}(\tau,\xi-\eta)\widehat{b_{l}}(\tau,\eta)\,d\eta d\tau

\displaystyle=i\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{-it\Phi_{r,l}}\frac{m_{r,l}}{\Phi_{r,l}}\widehat{b_{r}}(t,\xi-\eta)\widehat{b_{l}}(t,\eta)\,d\eta

\displaystyle-i\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{m_{r,l}}{\Phi_{r,l}}\widehat{b_{r}}(0,\xi-\eta)\widehat{b_{l}}(0,\eta)\,d\eta

\displaystyle-2i\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{-i\tau\Phi_{r,l}}\frac{m_{r,l}}{\Phi_{r,l}}\widehat{b_{r}}(\tau,\xi-\eta)\widehat{\partial_{\tau}b_{l}}(\tau,\eta)\,d\eta d\tau

with summation over
r,l=1,2
. Here we used for the last term above the symmetry property
m_{r,l}(\xi,\eta)=m_{r,l}(\xi,\xi-\eta)
and
\Phi_{r,l}(\xi,\eta)=\Phi_{r,l}(\xi,\xi-\eta)
.
We combine (
2.8
) and (
2.9
) along with (
2.5
) and (
2.7
) and then we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}e^{-i\tau p(\xi)}\widehat{\mathcal{Q}(\alpha)}(\tau,\xi)\,d\tau

\displaystyle=e^{-itp(\xi)}\hat{g}(t,\xi)-\hat{g}(0,\xi)+e^{-itp(\xi)}\hat{h}(t,\xi),

where

(2.10)

\displaystyle g(t,x)

\displaystyle=i\sum_{r,l=1}^{2}\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{m_{r,l}}{\Phi_{r,l}}\widehat{\alpha_{r}}(t,\xi-\eta)\widehat{\alpha_{l}}(t,\eta)\,d\eta\right),

\displaystyle h(t,x)

\displaystyle=-2i\sum_{r,l=1}^{2}\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{i(t-\tau)p(\xi)}\frac{m_{r,l}}{\Phi_{r,l}}\widehat{\alpha_{r}}(\tau,\xi-\eta)\widehat{\mathcal{Q}}(\tau,\eta)\,d\eta d\tau\right).

From (
2.6
), hence it yields that

(2.11)

\displaystyle\alpha(t,x)=e^{itp(|\nabla|)}\alpha(0,x)+g(t,x)-e^{itp(|\nabla|)}g(0,x)+h(t,x).

3.1.
Linear flow

We investigate time decay estimates for the linear flow

Proposition 3.1

Let

(3.1)

Remark 3.1
.

The case

The case

corresponds to a dispersion relation of homogenous type, where the decay rate of

is known to be

Theorem 3.1

Assume that

If there exist constants

and, moreover, if there exist

such that

(3.2)

then the following estimates hold;

\displaystyle\|e^{itp(|\nabla|)}P_{N}f\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim|t|^{-\frac{3}{2}}N^{(3-\frac{3\alpha_{1}}{2}-\frac{m_{1}-\alpha_{1}}{2})}\|f\|_{L^{1}}\qquad N\geq 1

\displaystyle\|e^{itp(|\nabla|)}P_{N}f\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim|t|^{-\frac{3}{2}}N^{(3-\frac{3\alpha_{2}}{2}-\frac{m_{2}-\alpha_{2}}{2})}\|f\|_{L^{1}}\qquad N<1

For our dispersion relation

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{-1}(e^{itp(|\xi|)}\Psi_{r_{0}})(x)

For

and then by the non-stationary phase method, we deduce

for any interval of
\tilde{r}
.

\frac{d^{k}}{dr^{k}}\tilde{\Psi}_{r_{0}}(r+r_{0})\sim r_{0}^{2-k}\quad\mbox{for $k=0,1$}\quad\mbox{and}\quad 0<r<2\varepsilon,

and (

), we deduce

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{2\varepsilon}e^{itp(r+r_{0})-i(r+r_{0})|x|}\tilde{\Psi}_{r_{0}}(r+r_{0})\bar{Z}((r+r_{0})|x|)\,dr\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{0}^{2\varepsilon}\left(\int_{0}^{r}e^{it\phi_{2}(\tilde{r}+r_{0},|x|,t)}\,d\tilde{r}\right)\,\frac{d}{dr}\left(\tilde{\Psi}_{r_{0}}(r+r_{0})\bar{Z}((r+r_{0})|x|)\right)\,dr\right|

\displaystyle\leq c_{\varepsilon}|t|^{-\frac{1}{3}}\sup_{r<2\varepsilon}\left(\big|\tilde{\Psi}_{r_{0}}^{\prime}(r+r_{0})\bar{Z}((r+r_{0})|x|)\big|+|x|\big|\tilde{\Psi}_{r_{0}}(r+r_{0})\bar{Z}^{\prime}((r+r_{0})|x|)\big|\right)

\displaystyle\leq c_{\varepsilon}|t|^{-\frac{4}{3}}

for

.

which completes the proof of (

). ∎

Proof of Proposition

.

For the proof, we employ an interpolation argument that involves Lemma

for

3.2
for

\|e^{itp(|\nabla|)}P_{N}f\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{2}}

for any

For the case

, we interpolate this result with the estimate given in (
3.9
). Consequently, we have

\|e^{itp(|\nabla|)}P_{N}f\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim|t|^{-3(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})}N^{(3+\frac{3\sigma}{2})(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})}\|f\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}}

for
p\geq 2
.
This implies

(3.11)

\|e^{itp(|\nabla|)}P_{<1}f\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim\sum_{N<1}\|e^{itp(|\nabla|)}P_{N}f\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim|t|^{-3(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})}\|f\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}}.

For
N\geq 1
,
we interpolate between (
3.8
) with
f
replaced by
P_{N}f
and the following estimate

\|e^{itp(|\nabla|)}P_{N}f\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|P_{N}f\|_{L^{2}}.

Thus, we derive the following estimate

\|e^{itp(|\nabla|)}P_{N}f\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim|t|^{-3(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})}\||\nabla|^{\frac{17}{3}(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})}P_{N}f\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}}

for
p\geq 2
.
By using this,
we obtain

(3.12)

\displaystyle\|e^{itp(|\nabla|)}P_{\geq 1}f\|_{L^{p}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{N\geq 1}\|e^{itp(|\nabla|)}P_{N}f\|_{L^{p}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim|t|^{-6(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})}\sum_{N\geq 1}\|P_{N}|\nabla|^{\frac{17}{3}(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})}f\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim|t|^{-6(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})}\|f\|_{W^{\frac{17}{3}(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}),p^{\prime}}}^{2}

since
L^{p^{\prime}}\subset B_{p^{\prime},2}^{0}
for
2\leq p<\infty
.
From the embedding relation
B_{p,2}^{0}\subset L^{p}
for
2\leq p<\infty
, we use (
3.11
) and (
3.12
) to get

(3.13)

\displaystyle\|e^{itp(|\nabla|)}f\|_{L^{p}}^{2}\lesssim\|e^{itp(|\nabla|)}P_{<1}f\|_{L^{p}}^{2}+\|e^{itp(|\nabla|)}P_{\geq 1}f\|_{L^{p}}^{2}\lesssim|t|^{-6(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})}\|f\|_{W^{\frac{17}{3}(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}),p^{\prime}}}

for
t\geq 1
. When
t<1
, we deduce

(3.14)

\|e^{itp(|\nabla|)}f\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim\|e^{itp(|\nabla|)}f\|_{\dot{H}^{3(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})}}\lesssim\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{3(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})}}\lesssim\|f\|_{\dot{W}^{6(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}),p^{\prime}}}

by the Hardy–Littlewood–Sobolev inequality and the Plancherel theorem.
Therefore, a combination of (
3.13
) and (
3.14
) concludes the desired estimate for
1<\sigma<2
.
The case
0<\sigma\leq 1
can be shown in a similar way thanks to Lemma
3.1
. This completes the proof.
∎

3.2.
Decay estimates

This subsection is dedicated to establishing

to (

Then the following estimates were presented in

,

Theorem 3.2
.

Let

By applying the product rule at the frequency level along with Theorem

Lemma 3.3

Let

where

or

In order to employ Theorem
3.2
and Lemma

,

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
0<\sigma<2
and
r,l=1,2

,

\|\mathfrak{M}_{r,l}\|_{M_{\xi,\eta}^{k,\infty}}+\|\mathfrak{M}_{r,l}\|_{M_{\eta,\xi}^{k,\infty}}\lesssim 1

We now establish the

Theorem 3.3

Let

p

Proof of Theorem

From (

\|h(t)\|_{W^{s,p}}\lesssim(1+t)^{-\beta_{\sigma}}\|\alpha\|_{X}^{3},\quad\|g(t)\|_{W^{s,p}}\lesssim(1+t)^{-\beta_{\sigma}}\|\alpha\|_{X}^{2},\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|g(0)\|_{W^{s+6(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}),p^{\prime}}}\lesssim\|\alpha\|_{X}^{2}.

Once these estimates are obtained, it follows from Proposition
3.1
that

\displaystyle\|\alpha(t)\|_{W^{s,p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+t)^{-\beta_{\sigma}}(\|\alpha(0)\|_{W^{s+6(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}),p^{\prime}}}+\|g(0)\|_{W^{s+6(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}),p^{\prime}}})+\|h(t)\|_{W^{s,p}}+\|g(t)\|_{W^{s,p}}

\bullet
Estimates for
h(t)
.
We begin with the expression for

h(t)=-2i\mathcal{F}_{\xi}^{-1}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{i(t-\tau)p(\xi)}\frac{m(\xi,\eta)}{\Phi(\xi,\eta)}\widehat{\alpha}(\xi-\eta)\widehat{\mathcal{Q}(\alpha)}(\tau,\eta)\,d\eta d\tau\right)

We note that

\displaystyle\frac{m(\xi,\eta)}{\Phi(\xi,\eta)}

\displaystyle=c\,\mathfrak{M}(\xi,\eta)(1+|\xi|^{\sigma})^{\frac{1}{2}}r(\xi)\frac{r(\xi-\eta)\langle\xi-\eta\rangle^{2\lambda}}{|\xi-\eta|^{(2-\sigma)/2}}\frac{r(\eta)\langle\eta\rangle^{2\lambda}}{|\eta|^{(2-\sigma)/2}}

\displaystyle+c\,\mathfrak{M}(\xi,\eta)|\xi|^{\frac{\sigma}{2}}\frac{r(\xi-\eta)\langle\xi-\eta\rangle^{2\lambda}}{|\xi-\eta|^{(2-\sigma)/2}}\frac{r(\eta)\langle\eta\rangle^{2\lambda}}{|\eta|^{(2-\sigma)/2}},

and then by using Proposition
3.1

2.3
), and Lemma
3.3
with
k=3/2-\delta
for sufficiently small

in turn, we obtain

\displaystyle\|h(t)\|_{W^{s,p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}(1+t-\tau)^{-\beta_{\sigma}}\left\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}_{\xi}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\langle\xi\rangle^{s+6(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})}\frac{m(\xi,\eta)}{\Phi(\xi,\eta)}\widehat{\alpha}(\tau,\xi-\eta)\widehat{\mathcal{Q}(\alpha)}(\tau,\eta)d\eta\right)\right\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}}d\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}(1+t-\tau)^{-\beta_{\sigma}}\left\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}_{\xi}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\langle\xi\rangle^{s+6(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})}(D_{1}^{\frac{\sigma}{2}}+D_{2}^{\frac{\sigma}{2}})\mathfrak{M}(\xi,\eta)\widehat{A}(\tau,\xi-\eta)\widehat{B}(\tau,\eta)d\eta\right)\right\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}}d\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}(1+t-\tau)^{-\beta_{\sigma}}\left(\|A\|_{H^{s+\frac{\sigma}{2}+6(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})}}\|B\|_{L^{\frac{1}{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}+\frac{\delta}{3}}}}+\|A\|_{L^{\frac{1}{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}+\frac{\delta}{3}}}}\|B\|_{H^{s+\frac{\sigma}{2}+6(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})}}\right)d\tau,

where we let
D_{1}^{\sigma/2}=(1+|\xi|^{\sigma})^{1/2}
,
D_{2}^{\sigma/2}=|\xi|^{\sigma/2}
, and

(3.18)

\widehat{A}(\xi-\eta)=\frac{r(\xi-\eta)\langle\xi-\eta\rangle^{2\lambda}}{|\xi-\eta|^{(2-\sigma)/2}}\widehat{\alpha}(\xi-\eta),\quad\widehat{B}(\eta)=\frac{r(\eta)\langle\eta\rangle^{2\lambda}}{|\eta|^{(2-\sigma)/2}}\widehat{\mathcal{Q}(\alpha)}(\eta).

We observe that for given
\alpha\in X

\displaystyle\|A\|_{H^{s+\frac{\sigma}{2}+6(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})}}=\||\nabla|^{-\frac{2-\sigma}{2}}\alpha\|_{H^{s+\frac{\sigma}{2}+2\lambda+6(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})}}\lesssim\|\alpha\|_{\dot{H}^{-\frac{2-\sigma}{2}}\cap H^{s+\frac{\sigma}{2}+2\lambda+6(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})}}\lesssim\|\alpha\|_{X},

by using the embedding relation
H^{\frac{3}{p}-\delta}\subset\dot{H}^{\frac{3}{p}-\delta}\subset{L^{\frac{1}{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}+\frac{\delta}{3}}}}
.
In a similar way, we have

(3.19)

\displaystyle\|B\|_{H^{s+\frac{\sigma}{2}+6(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})}}\lesssim\||\nabla|^{-\frac{2-\sigma}{2}}\mathcal{Q}(\alpha)\|_{H^{s+\frac{\sigma}{2}+2\lambda+6(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})}},

\displaystyle\|B\|_{L^{\frac{1}{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}+\frac{\delta}{3}}}}\lesssim\||\nabla|^{-\frac{2-\sigma}{2}}\mathcal{Q}(\alpha)\|_{H^{2\lambda+\frac{3}{p}-\delta}}\lesssim\||\nabla|^{-\frac{2-\sigma}{2}}\mathcal{Q}(\alpha)\|_{H^{s+\frac{\sigma}{2}+2\lambda+6(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})}}.

Employing the Plancherel theorem twice, we show for any
\tilde{s}\geq 0
that

\displaystyle\||\nabla|^{-\frac{2-\sigma}{2}}\mathcal{Q}(\alpha)\|_{H^{\tilde{s}}}

\displaystyle\sim\left\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{\langle\eta\rangle^{\tilde{s}}m(\eta,\zeta)}{|\eta|^{(2-\sigma)/2}}\widehat{\alpha}(\eta-\zeta)\widehat{\alpha}(\zeta)\,d\zeta\right\|_{L_{\eta}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\langle\eta\rangle^{\tilde{s}}r(\eta-\zeta)\widehat{\alpha}(\eta-\zeta)r(\zeta)\widehat{\alpha}(\zeta)\,d\zeta\right\|_{L_{\eta}^{2}}

\displaystyle+\left\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\langle\eta\rangle^{\tilde{s}}|\eta|^{\frac{\sigma}{2}}r(\eta-\zeta)\widehat{\alpha}(\eta-\zeta)r(\zeta)\widehat{\alpha}(\zeta)\,d\zeta\right\|_{L_{\eta}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|(r(\nabla)\alpha)(\langle\nabla\rangle^{\tilde{s}}r(\nabla)\alpha)\|_{L^{2}}+\|(\langle\nabla\rangle^{\tilde{s}}r(\nabla)\alpha)(|\nabla|^{\frac{\sigma}{2}}r(\nabla)\alpha)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle+\|(r(\nabla)\alpha)(\langle\nabla\rangle^{\tilde{s}}|\nabla|^{\frac{\sigma}{2}}r(\nabla)\alpha)\|_{L^{2}}

due to the symmetry.

2.3
), and the embedding
\dot{H}^{\frac{3}{p}}\subset L^{\frac{1}{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}}
, we arrive at

(3.20)

\displaystyle\||\nabla|^{-\frac{2-\sigma}{2}}\mathcal{Q}(\alpha)\|_{H^{\tilde{s}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\alpha\|_{L^{p}}\|\alpha\|_{W^{\tilde{s},\frac{1}{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}}}+\|\alpha\|_{W^{\tilde{s},\frac{1}{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}}}\||\nabla|^{\frac{\sigma}{2}}\alpha\|_{L^{p}}+\|\alpha\|_{L^{p}}\||\nabla|^{\frac{\sigma}{2}}\alpha\|_{W^{\tilde{s},\frac{1}{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\alpha\|_{W^{\frac{\sigma}{2},p}}\|\alpha\|_{H^{\tilde{s}+\frac{3}{p}+\frac{\sigma}{2}}}

for any
\tilde{s}\geq 0
. Putting (
3.20
) into (
3.19
) with
\tilde{s}=s+\sigma/2+2\lambda+6(1/2-1/p)
, we deduce

\displaystyle\|A\|_{H^{s+\frac{\sigma}{2}+6(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})}}\|B\|_{L^{\frac{1}{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}+\frac{\delta}{3}}}}+\|A\|_{L^{\frac{1}{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}+\frac{\delta}{3}}}}\|B\|_{H^{s+\frac{\sigma}{2}+6(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})}}\lesssim\|\alpha\|_{W^{s,p}}\|\alpha\|_{X}^{2}\lesssim(1+\tau)^{-\beta_{\sigma}}\|\alpha\|_{X}^{3}

for

(3.21)

s>2\lambda+3-\frac{3}{p}+\sigma.

Consequently, we have

\displaystyle\|h(t)\|_{W^{s,p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\alpha\|_{X}^{3}\left(\int_{0}^{\frac{t}{2}}+\int_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\right)(1+t-\tau)^{-\beta_{\sigma}}(1+\tau)^{-\beta_{\sigma}}\,d\tau\lesssim\left(1+\frac{t}{2}\right)^{-\beta_{\sigma}}\|\alpha\|_{X}^{3}\lesssim(1+t)^{-\beta_{\sigma}}\|\alpha\|_{X}^{3}.

\bullet
Estimates for
g(t)
. We recall the expression for
g
as follows:

g=i\mathcal{F}_{\xi}^{-1}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{m(\xi,\eta)}{\Phi(\xi,\eta)}\widehat{\alpha}(\xi-\eta)\widehat{\alpha}(\eta)\,d\eta\right).

When
|\xi|\leq 1
, we apply the Hardy–Littlewood–Sobolev embedding theorem, the Plancherel theorem and Lemma

\displaystyle\|g\|_{W^{s,p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\langle\xi\rangle^{s}|\xi|^{3(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{m(\xi,\eta)}{\Phi(\xi,\eta)}\widehat{\alpha}(\xi-\eta)\widehat{\alpha}(\eta)\,d\eta\right\|_{L_{\xi}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\||\xi|^{\frac{2-\sigma}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mathfrak{M}(\xi,\eta)\widehat{A}(\xi-\eta)\widehat{A}(\eta)\,d\eta\right\|_{L_{\xi}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\||\nabla|^{\frac{2-\sigma}{2}}A\|_{L^{p}}\|A\|_{L^{\frac{1}{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}+\frac{\delta}{3}}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\alpha\|_{W^{2\lambda,p}}\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\dot{H}^{-\frac{2-\sigma}{2}}\cap H^{2\lambda+\frac{3}{p}-\delta}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\alpha\|_{W^{s,p}}\|\alpha\|_{X},

where
A
is given as in (
3.18
).
Here, for the third and fourth inequalities above, we used

\||\nabla|^{s+3(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})}A\|_{L^{l_{1}}}\lesssim\|\alpha\|_{W^{s,p}}^{1-\nu}\|\alpha\|_{X}^{\nu}.

\displaystyle\|P_{<1}A\|_{L^{l_{2}}}

4.
M_{\xi,\eta}^{k,\infty}\cap M_{\eta,\xi}^{k,\infty}
estimates of the kernel
\mathfrak{M}

Throughout this section, our analysis revolves around a phase function denoted as
\Phi(\xi,\eta):=p(|\xi|)-p(|\xi-\eta|)-p(|\eta|)
, where

p(r)=(r^{2}+r^{2-\sigma})^{1/2}
for
0<\sigma<2
.
We carefully investigate the charateristics of the phase function
\Phi
and demonstrate that the kernel
\mathfrak{M}_{r,l}
belongs to the function spaces
M_{\xi,\eta}^{k,\infty}
and
M_{\eta,\xi}^{k,\infty}
.

4.1.
Estimates of phase function
\Phi

To facilitate the analysis, we introduce the following lemma, a useful tool for obtaining lower bounds of
\Phi
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
0<\sigma<2
,
a=b+c\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
, and
|c|\leq\min\{|a|,|b|\}
.
Then, we have

(4.1)

|p(a)-p(b)-p(c)|\gtrsim\frac{|c|}{1+(|a||c|)^{\sigma}}+|c|(1-\cos[a,c]+1-\cos[a,b])

for
|b|\geq 1
.
Moreover, we obtain

(4.2)

|p(a)-p(b)-p(c)|\gtrsim\frac{|c|^{(2-\sigma)/2}}{(1+|b|^{\sigma})^{1/2}}

for
|c|<1
. Here,
[a,b]
represents the angle between two vectors
a
and
b
.

Proof.

Note that the case
|a|\leq|b|
implies
p(a)\leq p(b)
.
Since
p(r)
is a non-negative and increasing function (e.g. see (
3.3
)),
it follows that

|p(a)-p(b)-p(c)|\geq p(c)=(|c|^{2}+|c|^{2-\sigma})^{1/2}

which implies the desired inequalities.

We now focus on the case
|a|>|b|
. Consider the function

Q(r)=\frac{p(r)}{r}=\left(\frac{1+r^{\sigma}}{r^{\sigma}}\right)^{1/2}.

We observe that
Q
is a decreasing and non-negative function with properties such as

Q(r)>1,\qquad Q^{\prime}(r)=-\frac{\sigma}{2r^{\sigma/2+1}(1+r^{\sigma})^{1/2}}\quad\text{for any}\quad r\geq 0,

Q^{\prime}(1)=-\frac{\sigma}{2\sqrt{2}},\quad\text{and}\quad Q^{\prime}(r)=-\frac{\sigma}{2r^{\sigma+1}}\quad\text{as}\quad r\rightarrow\infty,

where
Q^{\prime}(r)
is an increasing function.
Utilizing the relation
|a|=|b|\cos[a,b]+|c|\cos[a,c]
,
we write

(4.3)

\displaystyle p(b)+p(c)-p(a)

\displaystyle=(|b|+|c|-|a|)Q(a)+|b|(Q(b)-Q(a))+|c|(Q(c)-Q(a))

\displaystyle=\left\{|b|(1-\cos[a,b])+|c|(1-\cos[a,c])\right\}Q(a)

\displaystyle+|b|(Q(b)-Q(a))+|c|(Q(c)-Q(a)).

It is noteworthy that each term on the right-hand side above is positive since
Q
is a decreasing function.

To prove (
4.1
), we first assume
\cos[a,c]\leq 9/10
.
Then, (
4.3
) implies

(4.4)

\displaystyle p(b)+p(c)-p(a)

\displaystyle=|b|(Q(b)-\cos[a,b]Q(a))+|c|(Q(c)-\cos[a,c]Q(a))

\displaystyle\geq|c|(Q(c)-\cos[a,c]Q(a))

\displaystyle\geq\frac{|c|Q(c)}{10}+\frac{9|c|}{10}(Q(c)-Q(a))

\displaystyle\geq\frac{p(c)}{10}

due to the decreasing property of
Q(r)
. Thus, it is clear to deduce (
4.1
) when
\cos[a,c]\leq 9/10
.

Meanwhile, it is observed that
|c|\leq|b|
implies
\cos[a,c]\leq\cos[a,b]
, leading to
|a|\geq 2|c|\cos[a,c]
. When
\cos[a,c]>9/10
, it follows that

|a|\geq\frac{4|c|}{3}.

For
1<|a|<\delta^{-1}
for
\delta>0
small enough, we have

|a|\geq\frac{|a|}{4}+|c|\geq\frac{1}{4}+|c|.

Consequently,

Q(c)-Q(a)=-\int_{|c|}^{|a|}Q^{\prime}(r)\,dr\geq-Q^{\prime}(\delta^{-1})\int_{|c|}^{|a|}\,dr\gtrsim\delta^{\sigma+1}\int_{|c|}^{|c|+\frac{1}{4}}\,dr\gtrsim 1,

yielding

(4.5)

|c|(Q(c)-Q(a))\gtrsim|c|\gtrsim\frac{|c|}{1+|a|^{\sigma}|c|^{\sigma}}.

For
|a|\geq\delta^{-1}
, this can be simplified to
|c|>\delta^{-1}
since
-Q^{\prime}(r)
is decreasing. Subsequently,

Q(c)-Q(a)=-\int_{|c|}^{|a|}Q^{\prime}(r)\,dr\sim\int_{|c|}^{|a|}\frac{\sigma}{r^{\sigma+1}}\,dr=\frac{|a|^{\sigma}-|c|^{\sigma}}{|a|^{\sigma}|c|^{\sigma}}\gtrsim\frac{|a|^{\sigma}}{1+|a|^{\sigma}|c|^{\sigma}}

due to
|a|\geq 4|c|/3
and
|a|>1
. This yields

(4.6)

|c|(Q(c)-Q(a))\gtrsim\frac{|c|}{1+|a|^{\sigma}|c|^{\sigma}}.

By combining (
4.5
) and (
4.6
) along with (
4.3
), we conclude (
4.1
) for
\cos[a,c]>9/10
.

Next, we aim to demonstrate (
4.2
). Similar to the above, it suffices to deal with the case
|b|<|a|
. By utilizing (
4.4
), the case
\cos[a,b]\leq 9/10
can be easily handled as follows:

p(b)+p(c)-p(a)\geq\frac{p(c)}{10}\gtrsim\frac{|c|^{(2-\sigma)/2}}{(1+|b|^{\sigma})^{1/2}}.

When
\cos[a,b]>9/10
, we find

(4.7)

\displaystyle p(b)+p(c)-p(a)

\displaystyle\geq|b|Q(b)+|c|Q(c)-|a|Q(b)

\displaystyle=(|b|+|c|-|a|)Q(b)+|c|(Q(c)-Q(b))

\displaystyle\geq|c|(Q(c)-Q(b))

due to the monotonicity of
Q(r)
and
|a|\leq|b|+|c|
.
If
|c|^{\sigma}<3|b|^{\sigma}/4
, direct computation gives

(4.8)

\displaystyle Q(c)-Q(b)

\displaystyle=\frac{(1+|c|^{\sigma})^{1/2}}{|c|^{\sigma/2}}-\frac{(1+|b|^{\sigma})^{1/2}}{|b|^{\sigma/2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{|b|^{\sigma/2}(1+|c|^{\sigma})^{1/2}-|c|^{\sigma/2}(1+|b|^{\sigma})^{1/2}}{|b|^{\sigma/2}|c|^{\sigma/2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{|b|^{\sigma}-|c|^{\sigma}}{|b|^{\sigma/2}|c|^{\sigma/2}\left(|b|^{\sigma/2}(1+|c|^{\sigma})^{1/2}+|c|^{\sigma/2}(1+|b|^{\sigma})^{1/2}\right)}

\displaystyle>\frac{|b|^{\sigma/2}}{4|c|^{\sigma/2}\left(|b|^{\sigma/2}(1+|c|^{\sigma})^{1/2}+|c|^{\sigma/2}(1+|b|^{\sigma})^{1/2}\right)}

which implies

|c|(Q(c)-Q(b))\gtrsim\frac{|c|^{(2-\sigma)/2}}{(1+|b|^{\sigma})^{1/2}}

whenever
|c|<1
.
Thus, by combining (
4.7
) and (
4.8
), we derive (
4.2
).

Moreover, for
|c|^{\sigma}\geq 3|b|^{\sigma}/4
, we have

|a|\leq|b|+|c|\leq\Big(1+\Big(\frac{4}{3}\Big)^{\frac{1}{\sigma}}\Big)|c|=:\nu|c|.

This leads to

\displaystyle Q(b)-Q(a)=-\int_{|b|}^{|a|}Q^{\prime}(r)\,dr\gtrsim-Q^{\prime}\left(\nu|c|\right)\int_{|b|}^{\frac{4|b|}{3}}\,dr=-\frac{|b|}{3}Q^{\prime}\left(\nu|c|\right)

since
|a|\geq 4|b|/3
when
\cos[a,b]>9/10
. Consequently,

|b|(Q(b)-Q(a))=-\frac{|b|^{2}}{3}Q^{\prime}\left(\nu|c|\right)=\frac{\sigma|b|^{2}}{6\nu^{1+\sigma/2}|c|^{1+\sigma/2}(\nu^{\sigma}|c|^{\sigma}+1)^{1/2}}\gtrsim\frac{|c|^{(2-\sigma)/2}}{(1+|b|^{\sigma})^{1/2}}

for
|b|\geq|c|
. By combining this with (
4.3
), we conclude (
4.2
) as desired.
∎

The following lemma presents useful estimates involving the first-order and second-order derivatives of
\Phi
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
0<\sigma<2
. The following estimates hold;

\displaystyle|\nabla_{\xi}\Phi|

\displaystyle\lesssim\begin{cases}|\eta|+2\sin\frac{[\xi,\xi-\eta]}{2}\hskip 144.0pt\text{if }\displaystyle\min\{|\xi|,|\xi-\eta|\}\geq 1\\
\frac{1}{\min\{|\xi|,|\xi-\eta|\}^{\sigma/2}}+2\max\left\{1,\frac{1}{|\xi-\eta|^{\sigma/2}}\right\}\sin\frac{[\xi,\xi-\eta]}{2}\quad\text{if }\min\{|\xi|,|\xi-\eta|\}<1,\end{cases}

\displaystyle|\nabla_{\eta}\Phi|

\displaystyle\lesssim\begin{cases}|\xi|+2\sin\frac{[\eta,\xi-\eta]}{2}\hskip 144.0pt\text{if }\min\{|\eta|,|\xi-\eta|\}\geq 1\\
\frac{1}{\min\{|\eta|,|\xi-\eta|\}^{\sigma/2}}+2\max\left\{1,\frac{1}{|\xi-\eta|^{\sigma/2}}\right\}\sin\frac{[\eta,\xi-\eta]}{2}\quad\text{if }\min\{|\eta|,|\xi-\eta|\}<1,\end{cases}

\displaystyle|\Delta_{\xi}\Phi|

\displaystyle\lesssim\begin{cases}|\eta|\hskip 85.0pt\text{if }\min\{|\xi|,|\xi-\eta|\}\geq 1\\
\frac{1}{\min\{|\xi|,|\xi-\eta|\}^{1+\sigma/2}}\qquad\text{if }\min\{|\xi|,|\xi-\eta|\}<1,\\
\end{cases}

\displaystyle|\Delta_{\eta}\Phi|

\displaystyle\lesssim\begin{cases}|\xi|\hskip 85.0pt\text{if }\min\{|\eta|,|\xi-\eta|\}\geq 1\\
\frac{1}{\min\{|\eta|,|\xi-\eta|\}^{1+\sigma/2}}\qquad\text{if }\min\{|\eta|,|\xi-\eta|\}<1.\\
\end{cases}

In addition, we have

(4.9)

\displaystyle|\nabla_{\xi}\Phi|\lesssim\frac{|\eta|}{\min\{|\xi|,|\xi-\eta|\}^{1+\sigma/2}}+2\max\left\{1,\frac{1}{|\xi-\eta|^{\sigma/2}}\right\}\sin\frac{[\xi,\xi-\eta]}{2},

\displaystyle|\Delta_{\xi}\Phi|\lesssim\frac{|\eta|}{\min\{|\xi|,|\xi-\eta|\}^{2+\sigma/2}}+\frac{|\eta|^{1+\sigma/2}}{\min\{|\xi|,|\xi-\eta|\}^{2+\sigma}}

for
\min\{|\xi|,|\xi-\eta|\}<1
.

Proof.

We observe that

\displaystyle|\nabla_{\xi}\Phi|

\displaystyle=\left|p^{\prime}(\xi)\frac{\xi}{|\xi|}-p^{\prime}(\xi-\eta)\frac{\xi-\eta}{|\xi-\eta|}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|p^{\prime}(\xi)\frac{\xi}{|\xi|}-p^{\prime}(\xi-\eta)\frac{\xi}{|\xi|}+p^{\prime}(\xi-\eta)\frac{\xi}{|\xi|}-p^{\prime}(\xi-\eta)\frac{\xi-\eta}{|\xi-\eta|}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|p^{\prime}(\xi)-p^{\prime}(\xi-\eta)\right|+|p^{\prime}(\xi-\eta)|\left|\frac{\xi}{|\xi|}-\frac{\xi-\eta}{|\xi-\eta|}\right|.

If
\min\{|\xi|,|\xi-\eta|\}\geq 1
, it follows that

\displaystyle\left|p^{\prime}(\xi)-p^{\prime}(\xi-\eta)\right|\leq\int_{\min\{|\xi|,|\xi-\eta|\}}^{\max\{|\xi|,|\xi-\eta|\}}|p^{\prime\prime}(r)|\,dr\lesssim\int_{\min\{|\xi|,|\xi-\eta|\}}^{\max\{|\xi|,|\xi-\eta|\}}\frac{1}{r}\,dr\lesssim\frac{||\xi|-|\xi-\eta||}{\min\{|\xi|,|\xi-\eta|\}}\leq|\eta|

by utilizing (
3.4
).
When
\min\{|\xi|,|\xi-\eta|\}<1
, we get

\displaystyle|p^{\prime}(\xi)-p^{\prime}(\xi-\eta)|

\displaystyle\leq\left|p^{\prime}(\min\{|\xi|,|\xi-\eta|\})\right|+\left|p^{\prime}(\max\{|\xi|,|\xi-\eta|\})\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\min\{|\xi|,|\xi-\eta|\}^{\sigma/2}}+\max\left\{1,\frac{1}{\max\{|\xi|,|\xi-\eta|\}^{\sigma/2}}\right\}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\min\{|\xi|,|\xi-\eta|\}^{\sigma/2}}

by using (
3.4
) and (
3.5
). Additionally, we have

(4.10)

|p^{\prime}(\xi-\eta)|\lesssim\max\left\{1,\frac{1}{|\xi-\eta|^{\sigma/2}}\right\}.

Since

(4.11)

\left|\frac{\xi}{|\xi|}-\frac{\xi-\eta}{|\xi-\eta|}\right|=2\sin\frac{[\xi,\xi-\eta]}{2},

we deduce

|\nabla_{\xi}\Phi|\lesssim\begin{cases}|\eta|+2\sin\frac{[\xi,\xi-\eta]}{2}\hskip 138.0pt\text{if }\min\{|\xi|,|\xi-\eta|\}\geq 1,\\
\frac{1}{\min\{|\xi|,|\xi-\eta|\}^{\sigma/2}}+2\max\{1,\frac{1}{|\xi-\eta|^{\sigma/2}}\}\sin\frac{[\xi,\xi-\eta]}{2}\quad\text{if }\min\{|\xi|,|\xi-\eta|\}<1.\\
\end{cases}

Next, we observe that

(4.12)

\displaystyle|\Delta_{\xi}\Phi|

\displaystyle=|\Delta_{\xi}p(\xi)-\Delta_{\xi}p(\xi-\eta)|

\displaystyle\leq|p^{\prime\prime}(\xi)-p^{\prime\prime}(\xi-\eta)|+2\left|\frac{p^{\prime}(\xi)}{|\xi|}-\frac{p^{\prime}(\xi-\eta)}{|\xi-\eta|}\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim|p^{\prime\prime}(\xi)-p^{\prime\prime}(\xi-\eta)|+\frac{1}{|\xi|}|p^{\prime}(\xi)-p^{\prime}(\xi-\eta)|+\left|\frac{1}{|\xi|}-\frac{1}{|\xi-\eta|}\right||p^{\prime}(\xi-\eta)|

\displaystyle=:I+II+III.

If
\min\{|\xi|,|\xi-\eta|\}\geq 1
, a similar argument as before gives

I\lesssim\int^{\max\{|\xi|,|\xi-\eta|\}}_{\min\{|\xi|,|\xi-\eta|\}}\frac{1}{r^{2}}\,dr\lesssim\frac{||\xi|-|\xi-\eta||}{|\xi||\xi-\eta|}\lesssim\frac{|\eta|}{\min\{|\xi|,|\xi-\eta|\}^{2}},\quad II\lesssim\frac{|\eta|}{|\xi|}\lesssim|\eta|,

and

III\lesssim\left|\frac{1}{|\xi|}-\frac{1}{|\xi-\eta|}\right||p^{\prime}(\xi-\eta)|\leq\frac{||\xi|-|\xi-\eta||}{|\xi||\xi-\eta|}\leq|\eta|

leading to the desired result for
\min\{|\xi|,|\xi-\eta|\}\geq 1
.

We now consider the case
\min\{|\xi|,|\xi-\eta|\}<1
. It is clear to get

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle\lesssim\left|p^{\prime\prime}(\min\{|\xi|,|\xi-\eta|\})\right|+\left|p^{\prime\prime}(\max\{|\xi|,|\xi-\eta|\})\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\min\{|\xi|,|\xi-\eta|\}^{1+\sigma/2}}+\frac{1}{\max\{|\xi|,|\xi-\eta|\}^{1+\sigma/2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\min\{|\xi|,|\xi-\eta|\}^{1+\sigma/2}},

\displaystyle II

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{|\xi|\min\{|\xi|,|\xi-\eta|\}^{\sigma/2}}\lesssim\frac{1}{\min\{|\xi|,|\xi-\eta|\}^{1+\sigma/2}},

and

III\lesssim\frac{1}{\min\{|\xi|,|\xi-\eta|\}}|p^{\prime}(\xi-\eta)|\lesssim\frac{1}{\min\{|\xi|,|\xi-\eta|\}}\max\{1,\frac{1}{|\xi-\eta|^{\sigma/2}}\}\lesssim\frac{1}{\min\{|\xi|,|\xi-\eta|\}^{1+\sigma/2}}.

Hence, we arrive at the following upper-bound:

|\Delta_{\xi}\Phi|\lesssim\frac{1}{\min\{|\xi|,|\xi-\eta|\}^{1+\sigma/2}}

for
\min\{|\xi|,|\xi-\eta|\}<1
.

In order to derive (
4.9
), if
\max\{|\xi|,|\xi-\eta|\}<1
, we find that

\displaystyle\left|p^{\prime}(\xi)-p^{\prime}(\xi-\eta)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\min\{|\xi|,|\xi-\eta|\}}^{\max\{|\xi|,|\xi-\eta|\}}\frac{1}{r^{1+\sigma/2}}\,dr\leq\frac{||\xi|-|\xi-\eta||}{\min\{|\xi|,|\xi-\eta|\}^{1+\sigma/2}}\leq\frac{|\eta|}{\min\{|\xi|,|\xi-\eta|\}^{1+\sigma/2}}.

If
\max\{|\xi|,|\xi-\eta|\}\geq 1
, then

(4.13)

\displaystyle\left|p^{\prime}(\xi)-p^{\prime}(\xi-\eta)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\min\{|\xi|,|\xi-\eta|\}}^{1}\frac{1}{r^{1+\sigma/2}}\,dr+\int_{1}^{\max\{|\xi|,|\xi-\eta|\}}\frac{1}{r^{1+\sigma}}\,dr

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1-\min\{|\xi|,|\xi-\eta|\}}{\min\{|\xi|,|\xi-\eta|\}^{1+\sigma/2}}+\max\{|\xi|,|\xi-\eta|\}-1

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{||\xi|-|\xi-\eta||}{\min\{|\xi|,|\xi-\eta|\}^{1+\sigma/2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{|\eta|}{\min\{|\xi|,|\xi-\eta|\}^{1+\sigma/2}}

whenever
\min\{|\xi|,|\xi-\eta|\}<1
. By using this along with (
4.10
) and (
4.11
), we deduce that

|\nabla_{\xi}\Phi|\lesssim\frac{|\eta|}{\min\{|\xi|,|\xi-\eta|\}^{1+\sigma/2}}+2\max\{1,\frac{1}{|\xi-\eta|^{\sigma/2}}\}\sin\frac{[\xi,\xi-\eta]}{2}

for
\min\{|\xi|,|\xi-\eta|\}<1
.

Meanwhile, recalling that

\displaystyle|\Delta_{\xi}\Phi|\lesssim I+II+III

from (
4.12
), we estimate

I\lesssim\int_{\min\{|\xi|,|\xi-\eta|\}}^{\max\{|\xi|,|\xi-\eta|\}}\frac{1}{r^{2+\sigma/2}}\,dr\lesssim\frac{||\xi|^{1+\sigma/2}-|\xi-\eta|^{1+\sigma/2}|}{|\xi|^{1+\sigma/2}|\xi-\eta|^{1+\sigma/2}}\lesssim\frac{|\eta|^{1+\sigma/2}}{\min\{|\xi|,|\xi-\eta|\}^{2+\sigma}}

for
\max\{|\xi|,|\xi-\eta|\}<1
, and

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\min\{|\xi|,|\xi-\eta|\}}^{1}\frac{1}{r^{2+\sigma/2}}\,dr+\int_{1}^{\max\{|\xi|,|\xi-\eta|\}}\frac{1}{r^{2}}\,dr

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1-\min\{|\xi|,|\xi-\eta|\}}{\min\{|\xi|,|\xi-\eta|\}^{2+\sigma/2}}+\max\{|\xi|,|\xi-\eta|\}-1

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{|\eta|}{\min\{|\xi|,|\xi-\eta|\}^{2+\sigma/2}}

similarly to (
4.13
) for
\max\{|\xi|,|\xi-\eta|\}\geq 1
.
Moreover, we find

\displaystyle II

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{|\eta|}{\min\{|\xi|,|\xi-\eta|\}^{1+\sigma/2}|\xi|}\lesssim\frac{|\eta|}{\min\{|\xi|,|\xi-\eta|\}^{2+\sigma/2}},

\displaystyle III

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{||\xi|-|\xi-\eta||}{|\xi||\xi-\eta|}\max\{1,\frac{1}{|\xi-\eta|^{\sigma/2}}\}\leq\frac{|\eta|}{\min\{|\xi|,|\xi-\eta|\}^{2+\sigma/2}}.

Combining these, we conclude that

|\Delta_{\xi}\Phi|\lesssim\frac{|\eta|}{\min\{|\xi|,|\xi-\eta|\}^{2+\sigma/2}}+\frac{|\eta|^{1+\sigma/2}}{\min\{|\xi|,|\xi-\eta|\}^{2+\sigma}}.

It can be verified that similar upper bounds for the derivative with respect to
\eta
can be obtained in a similar manner, thereby completing the proof.
∎

4.2.
Proof of Proposition
3.2

In this subsection, we provide the proof of Proposition
3.2
using Lemmas
4.1
and
4.2
, dealing with
\Phi=\Phi_{1,1}
and
\mathfrak{M}=\mathfrak{M}_{1,1}
.

It is worth noting that a similar argument can be applied to
\mathfrak{M}_{1,2}
(respectively
\mathfrak{M}_{2,1}
) by interchanging
\xi
and
\xi-\eta
(respectively
\eta

\mathfrak{M}_{2,2}

since it can be easily verified that
\Phi_{2,2}=p(|\xi|)+p(|\xi-\eta|)+p(|\eta|)

4.1
and
4.2

To demonstrate
\mathfrak{M}\in M_{\xi,\eta}^{k,\infty}\cap M_{\eta,\xi}^{k,\infty}

•

(Case A)
\{|\xi|\leq\frac{|\eta|}{2}\}

•

(Case B)
\{|\eta|\leq\frac{|\xi|}{2}\}
,

•

(Case C)
\{\frac{1}{3}<\frac{|\xi|}{|\eta|}<3\}

Essentially, these three cases respectively correspond to situations where
|\xi-\eta|\sim|\eta|
,
|\xi-\eta|\sim|\xi|
, and
|\eta|\sim|\xi|
.

From Lemma
4.1
, we expect that
\Phi
can be zero when
\min\{|\xi-\eta|,|\eta|\}=0
, leading to a singularity in
\mathfrak{M}
.
Thus, it is crucial to investigate situations where
|\xi-\eta|
or
|\eta|
are small enough.
Utilizing symmetry, we streamline the proof by focusing on (Case B), where
|\eta|
is the smallest among the three cases (proofs for the other cases are provided in Appendix
A
).

Employing a slight abuse of notation, we set
\theta:=[\xi,\eta],\gamma:=[\xi,\xi-\eta]
, and
\pi-\beta:=[\eta,\eta-\xi]
.
We observe the fundamental inequalities:

(4.14)

\frac{|\xi-\eta|}{\sin\theta}=\frac{|\xi|}{\sin\beta}=\frac{|\eta|}{\sin\gamma},\quad 1-\cos y=2\sin^{2}(\frac{y}{2})

for any

and

(4.15)

\frac{x}{2}\leq\sin x\leq x,\quad\frac{x^{2}}{4}\leq\sin^{2}x\leq x^{2}\quad\text{for }0\leq x\leq\frac{\pi}{2}.

In (Case B), we note that
|\eta|
is the smallest value and
|\xi-\eta|\sim|\xi|
. For
\mathfrak{M}\in M_{\eta,\xi}^{k,\infty}
, we further subdivide it into the following cases;

•

(B1)
\min\{|\xi|,|\xi-\eta|\}<1
,

•

(B2)
\min\{|\xi|,|\xi-\eta|\}\geq 1
.

In subcase (B1), we get

|\Phi|\gtrsim\frac{|\eta|^{(2-\sigma)/2}}{\langle\xi\rangle^{\sigma/2}}

by applying (
4.2
) along with
|\xi|\sim|\xi-\eta|
.
By (
4.9
), we obtain

\displaystyle|\nabla_{\xi}\Phi|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{|\eta|}{|\xi|^{1+\sigma/2}}+\max\{1,\frac{1}{|\xi-\eta|^{\sigma/2}}\}\sin\frac{\gamma}{2}\lesssim\frac{|\eta|}{|\xi|^{1+\sigma/2}},

\displaystyle|\Delta_{\xi}\Phi|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{|\eta|}{|\xi|^{2+\sigma/2}}+\frac{|\eta|^{1+\sigma/2}}{|\xi|^{2+\sigma}}=\frac{|\eta|}{|\xi|^{2+\sigma/2}}\left(1+\frac{|\eta|^{\sigma/2}}{|\xi|^{\sigma/2}}\right)\lesssim\frac{|\eta|}{{|\xi|}^{2+\sigma/2}}

since
|\eta|\leq|\xi|\sim|\xi-\eta|
. In the second inequality of the first line above, we used the fact that

(4.16)

\sin\frac{\gamma}{2}\leq\frac{\sin\gamma}{2}=\frac{|\eta|}{2|\xi|}\sin\beta\leq\frac{|\eta|}{|\xi|}

due to
\gamma<\pi/2
. Thus, we have

(4.17)

\displaystyle\left|\nabla_{\xi}(\frac{1}{\Phi})\right|=

\displaystyle\left|-\frac{\nabla_{\xi}\Phi}{\Phi^{2}}\right|\lesssim\frac{\langle\xi\rangle^{\sigma}}{|\xi|^{1+\sigma/2}|\eta|^{1-\sigma}},

\displaystyle\left|\Delta_{\xi}(\frac{1}{\Phi})\right|=

\displaystyle\left|-\frac{\Delta_{\xi}\Phi}{\Phi^{2}}+\frac{2|\nabla_{\xi}\Phi|^{2}}{\Phi^{3}}\right|\lesssim\frac{\langle\xi\rangle^{\sigma}}{|\xi|^{2+\sigma/2}|\eta|^{1-\sigma}}\left(1+\frac{|\eta|^{\sigma/2}\langle\xi\rangle^{\sigma/2}}{|\xi|^{\sigma/2}}\right).

Let us consider

(4.18)

\mathfrak{M}(\xi,\eta)=\frac{|\xi|^{(2-\sigma)/2}|\xi-\eta|^{(2-\sigma)/2}|\eta|^{(2-\sigma)/2}}{\langle\xi-\eta\rangle^{2\lambda}\langle\eta\rangle^{2\lambda}\Phi(\xi,\eta)}=\frac{g(\xi,\eta)}{\Phi(\xi,\eta)},

where we find

|\mathfrak{M}|\lesssim\frac{|\xi|^{2-\sigma}}{\langle\xi\rangle^{2\lambda-\sigma/2}\langle\eta\rangle^{2\lambda}}

due to
|\xi-\eta|\sim|\xi|
. We express

(4.19)

\Delta_{\xi}\mathfrak{M}=g\Delta_{\xi}(\frac{1}{\Phi})+2\nabla_{\xi}g\cdot\nabla_{\xi}(\frac{1}{\Phi})+\frac{\Delta_{\xi}g}{\Phi},

and using (
4.17
), we deduce

\displaystyle|\Delta_{\xi}\mathfrak{M}|\lesssim\frac{1}{|\xi|^{\sigma}\langle\xi\rangle^{2\lambda}\langle\eta\rangle^{2\lambda}}\left(\frac{\langle\xi\rangle^{3\sigma/2}|\eta|^{\sigma}}{|\xi|^{\sigma}}+\frac{\langle\xi\rangle^{\sigma}|\eta|^{\sigma/2}}{|\xi|^{\sigma/2}}+\langle\xi\rangle^{\sigma/2}\right)\lesssim\frac{\langle\xi\rangle^{3\sigma/2}}{|\xi|^{\sigma}\langle\xi\rangle^{2\lambda}\langle\eta\rangle^{2\lambda}}

given that
|\eta|\leq|\xi|
. This implies

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\mathfrak{M}\varphi(\frac{\xi}{N})|^{2}\,d\xi

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\rho\sim N}\frac{\rho^{6-2\sigma}}{\langle\rho\rangle^{4\lambda-\sigma}\langle\eta\rangle^{4\lambda}}\,d\rho\lesssim\frac{N^{7-2\sigma}}{\langle N\rangle^{4\lambda-\sigma}\langle\eta\rangle^{4\lambda}},

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\Delta_{\xi}\mathfrak{M}\varphi(\frac{\xi}{N})|^{2}\,d\xi

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\rho\sim N}\frac{\rho^{2-2\sigma}}{\langle\rho\rangle^{4\lambda-3\sigma}\langle\eta\rangle^{4\lambda}}\,d\rho\lesssim\frac{N^{3-2\sigma}}{\langle N\rangle^{4\lambda-3\sigma}\langle\eta\rangle^{4\lambda}}.

Consequently, we have

\displaystyle\|\mathfrak{M}\varphi_{N}\|_{\dot{H}_{\xi}^{k}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\frac{N^{(7-2\sigma)/2}}{\langle N\rangle^{2\lambda-\sigma/2}\langle\eta\rangle^{2\lambda}}\right)^{1-k/2}\left(\frac{N^{(3-2\sigma)/2}}{\langle N\rangle^{2\lambda-3\sigma/2}\langle\eta\rangle^{2\lambda}}\right)^{k/2}

\displaystyle=\frac{N^{7/2-\sigma-k}}{\langle N\rangle^{2\lambda-\sigma/2-k(\sigma)/2}\langle\eta\rangle^{2\lambda}}\lesssim\frac{1}{\langle N\rangle^{2\lambda-7/2+\sigma/2-k(\sigma-2)/2}}

for
0\leq k\leq 3/2
.
It is clearly summable over

We first provide the

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
0<\sigma<2
. For
T^{\ast}>0
, we have

\|\alpha(t)\|_{\dot{H}_{x}^{-\frac{2-\sigma}{2}}}\lesssim\|\alpha(0)\|_{Y}+\sup_{0<t<T^{\ast}}\|\alpha(t)\|_{X}^{2}.

Proof.

From (
2.4
), we get

(5.1)

\|\alpha(t)\|_{\dot{H}^{-\frac{2-\sigma}{2}}}\lesssim\|\alpha(0)\|_{\dot{H}^{-\frac{2-\sigma}{2}}}+\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\mathcal{Q}(\alpha)(\tau)\right\|_{\dot{H}^{-\frac{2-\sigma}{2}}}d\tau

given the isometric property of the operator
e^{itp(|\nabla|)}
on
\dot{H}^{-(2-\sigma)/2}
.
Recall that

\mathcal{Q}(\alpha)=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{ix\cdot\xi}m_{r,l}(\xi,\eta)\widehat{\alpha_{r}}(\xi-\eta)\widehat{\alpha_{l}}(\eta)\,d\xi d\eta,

where

m_{r,l}\sim p(\xi)r(\xi)r(\xi-\eta)r(\eta)+|\xi|r(\xi-\eta)r(\eta).

Further, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\widehat{\mathcal{Q}(\alpha)}(\xi)}{|\xi|^{(2-\sigma)/2}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{m_{r,l}(\xi,\eta)}{|\xi|^{(2-\sigma)/2}}\widehat{\alpha}_{r}(\xi-\eta)\widehat{\alpha}_{l}(\eta)\,d\eta

\displaystyle\sim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(1+|\xi|^{\sigma})^{\frac{1}{2}}r(\xi)r(\xi-\eta)\widehat{\alpha}_{r}(\xi-\eta)r(\eta)\widehat{\alpha}_{l}(\eta)\,d\eta+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\xi|^{\frac{\sigma}{2}}r(\xi-\eta)\widehat{\alpha}_{r}(\xi-\eta)r(\eta)\widehat{\alpha}_{l}(\eta)\,d\eta.

By using the basic relations

(1+|\xi|^{\sigma})^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq 1+|\xi|^{\frac{\sigma}{2}}\quad\text{and}\quad|\xi|^{\frac{\sigma}{2}}\leq|\xi-\eta|^{\frac{\sigma}{2}}+|\eta|^{\frac{\sigma}{2}},

we deduce that

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{Q}(\alpha)\|_{\dot{H}^{-\frac{2-\sigma}{2}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|(r(\nabla)\alpha_{r})(r(\nabla)\alpha_{l})\right\|_{L^{2}}+\|(r(\nabla)\alpha_{r})(|\nabla|^{\sigma/2}r(\nabla)\alpha_{l})\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\alpha\|_{L^{2}}\|r(\nabla)\alpha\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\alpha\|_{\dot{H}^{\sigma/2}}\|r(\nabla)\alpha\|_{L^{\infty}}

by virtue of (
2.3
), the Plancherel theorem, and symmetry.
By employing the embedding property
W^{\frac{3}{p}+\delta,p}\subset L^{\infty}
for small
\delta>0
, we utilize (
2.3
) to obtain

(5.2)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\mathcal{Q}(\alpha)(\tau)\right\|_{\dot{H}^{-\frac{2-\sigma}{2}}}d\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{0<t<T^{\ast}}(\|\alpha(t)\|_{L_{x}^{2}}+\|\alpha(t)\|_{\dot{H}_{x}^{\sigma/2}})\int_{0}^{t}\|\alpha(\tau)\|_{W^{\frac{3}{p}+\delta,p}}\,d\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{0<t<T^{\ast}}\|\alpha(t)\|_{X}^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{(1+\tau)^{\beta_{\sigma}}}\,d\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{0<t<T^{\ast}}\|\alpha(t)\|_{X}^{2}

if
\beta_{\sigma}>1
and
p<\infty
.
By combining (
5.1
) and (
5.2
), we conclude the desired result.
∎

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{1}{n+1}\nabla\cdot u\left||\nabla|^{2s-\frac{\sigma}{2}}n\right|^{2}dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{1}{(n+1)^{2}}u\cdot\nabla n\left||\nabla|^{2s-\frac{\sigma}{2}}n\right|^{2}dx

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{1}{n+1}|\nabla|^{2s-\frac{\sigma}{2}}n\left[|\nabla|^{2s-\frac{\sigma}{2}},n+1\right]\nabla\cdot u\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla|^{2s-\frac{\sigma}{2}}n|\nabla|^{2s-\frac{\sigma}{2}}\nabla\cdot u\,dx

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{1}{n+1}|\nabla|^{2s-\frac{\sigma}{2}}n\left[|\nabla|^{2s-\frac{\sigma}{2}},u\right]\cdot\nabla n\,dx-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{1}{n+1}u\cdot\nabla\left||\nabla|^{2s-\frac{\sigma}{2}}n\right|^{2}dx

\displaystyle=:\sum_{i=1}^{6}I_{i},

where we easily get

By virtue of (

), it follows that

\displaystyle I_{3}

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{1}{n+1}|\nabla|^{2s-\frac{\sigma}{2}}n\left[|\nabla|^{2s-\frac{\sigma}{2}},n\right]\nabla\cdot u\,dx

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\||\nabla|^{2s-\frac{\sigma}{2}}n\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|[|\nabla|^{2s-\frac{\sigma}{2}},n]\nabla\cdot u\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\nabla w\|_{L^{\infty}}\|w\|_{\dot{H}^{2s-\frac{\sigma}{2}}}^{2},

\displaystyle I_{5}

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{1}{n+1}|\nabla|^{2s-\frac{\sigma}{2}}n[|\nabla|^{2s-\frac{\sigma}{2}},u]\cdot\nabla n\,dx\lesssim\|\nabla w\|_{L^{\infty}}\|w\|_{\dot{H}^{2s-\frac{\sigma}{2}}}^{2}.

For

, we find

\displaystyle I_{6}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla(\frac{1}{n+1})\cdot u\left||\nabla|^{2s-\frac{\sigma}{2}}n\right|^{2}dx+J_{1}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{1}{(n+1)^{2}}\nabla n\cdot u\left||\nabla|^{2s-\frac{\sigma}{2}}n\right|^{2}dx+J_{1}

\displaystyle=-I_{2}+I_{1}.

Finally, we use

to deduce that

(5.8)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|n\|_{\dot{\mathcal{H}}^{2s-\frac{\sigma}{2}}}^{2}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla|\nabla|^{2s-\sigma}n\cdot|\nabla|^{2s}u\,dx\lesssim\|\nabla w\|_{L^{\infty}}\|w\|_{\dot{H}^{2s-\frac{\sigma}{2}}}^{2}.

It follows from (

) and (

5.2.
Proof of Theorem

The local-in-time existence and uniqueness of a solution

can be established through the standard argument detailed in

]
. By Lemma

, Lemma

, and Theorem

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\frac{N^{(5-2\sigma)/2}|\xi|^{(2-\sigma)/2}}{\langle N\rangle^{2\lambda}\langle\xi\rangle^{2\lambda}}\right)^{1-k/2}\left(\frac{N^{3/2}|\xi|^{(2-\sigma)/2}}{\langle N\rangle^{2\lambda}\langle\xi\rangle^{2\lambda}}\right)^{k/2}

which obviously implies
\mathfrak{M}\in M_{\xi,\eta}^{k,\infty}
for

if

followed by

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\mathfrak{M}\varphi(\frac{\xi-\eta}{N})|^{2}\,d\xi

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\rho\sim N}\frac{\rho^{2}|\eta|^{4-2\sigma}}{\langle\rho\rangle^{4\lambda}\langle\eta\rangle^{4\lambda-\sigma}}\,d\rho\lesssim\frac{N^{3}}{\langle N\rangle^{4\lambda}\langle\eta\rangle^{4\lambda-4+\sigma}},

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\Delta_{\xi}\mathfrak{M}\varphi(\frac{\xi-\eta}{N})|^{2}\,d\xi

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\rho\sim N}\frac{|\eta|^{4-2\sigma}}{\rho^{2}\langle\rho\rangle^{4\lambda}\langle\eta\rangle^{4\lambda-3\sigma}}\,d\rho\lesssim\frac{1}{N\langle N\rangle^{4\lambda}\langle\eta\rangle^{4\lambda-4-\sigma}}.

By using an interpolation argument, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\mathfrak{M}\varphi_{N}\|_{\dot{H}_{\xi}^{k}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\frac{N^{3/2}}{\langle N\rangle^{2\lambda}\langle\eta\rangle^{2\lambda-2+\sigma/2}}\right)^{1-k/2}\left(\frac{1}{N^{1/2}\langle N\rangle^{2\lambda}\langle\eta\rangle^{2\lambda-2-\sigma/2}}\right)^{k/2}

\displaystyle=\frac{N^{3/2-k}}{\langle N\rangle^{2\lambda}\langle\eta\rangle^{2\lambda-2-\sigma(k-1)/2}}\lesssim\frac{1}{\langle N\rangle^{2\lambda-3/2+k}}

for

if
\lambda>1+\sigma(k-1)/4

It is summable over
N
when

, thereby establishing
\mathfrak{M}\in M_{\eta,\xi}^{k,\infty}
for
0\leq k\leq 3/2
.

We next consider the case (C2). Applying (

\displaystyle|\Phi|

\displaystyle\gtrsim|\xi-\eta|\left(\frac{1}{1+|\xi|^{\sigma}|\xi-\eta|^{\sigma}}+\gamma^{2}+\theta^{2}\right)\gtrsim|\xi-\eta|\left(\frac{1}{1+|\xi|^{2\sigma}+|\eta|^{2\sigma}}+\beta^{2}\right)=|\xi-\eta|(d^{2}+\beta^{2})

since

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\mathfrak{M}\varphi(\frac{\xi-\eta}{N})|^{2}\,d\xi

and

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\Delta_{\xi}\mathfrak{M}\varphi(\frac{\xi-\eta}{N})|^{2}\,d\xi

Appendix B
Asymptotic behavior of
|p^{\prime\prime}(r)|

In this part, we investigate the asymptotic behavior of
|p^{\prime\prime}(r)|
. Note that

p^{\prime\prime}(r)=\frac{2\sigma(\sigma-1)r^{\sigma}-\sigma(2-\sigma)}{4r^{1+\sigma/2}(r^{\sigma}+1)^{3/2}},

for
0<\sigma<2
. For this, we mainly separate cases
0<\sigma\leq 1
and
1<\sigma<2
.

When
0<\sigma<1
, we note that
p^{\prime\prime}(r)
is negative, and thus

|p^{\prime\prime}(r)|=\frac{2\sigma(1-\sigma)r^{\sigma}+\sigma(2-\sigma)}{4r^{1+\sigma/2}(r^{\sigma}+1)^{3/2}}\sim\begin{cases}r^{-1-\sigma}\qquad\ \ \text{if }r\geq 1\\
r^{-1-\sigma/2}\qquad\text{if }r<1\\
\end{cases}

due to
1+r^{\sigma}\sim r^{\sigma}
when
r\geq 1
and
1+r^{\sigma}\sim 1
when
r<1
.
For the case
\sigma=1
, the expression simplifies to

|p^{\prime\prime}(r)|=\frac{1}{4r^{3/2}(r+1)^{3/2}}\sim\begin{cases}r^{-3}\qquad\ \ \text{if }r\geq 1\\
r^{-3/2}\qquad\text{if }r<1.\\
\end{cases}

In contrast to the case
0<\sigma\leq 1
, the case
1<\sigma<2
introduce a degenerate point at

r_{0}=\left(\frac{2-\sigma}{2(\sigma-1)}\right)^{1/\sigma},

where
p^{\prime\prime}(r_{0})=0
. Then, it can be verified that
p^{\prime\prime}(r)>0
(respectively
p^{\prime\prime}(r)<0
) when
r>r_{0}
(respectively
r<r_{0}
).
When
r<r_{0}
, we let
r^{\sigma}=r_{0}^{\sigma}-c^{\ast}
for some
c^{\ast}>0
. Then, we get

|p^{\prime\prime}(r)|=\frac{\sigma(2-\sigma)-2\sigma(\sigma-1)r^{\sigma}}{4r^{1+\sigma/2}(r^{\sigma}+1)^{3/2}}=\frac{2\sigma(\sigma-1)c^{\ast}}{4r^{1+\sigma/2}(r^{\sigma}+1)^{3/2}}\sim\frac{1}{r^{1+2\sigma}},\qquad 1\leq r<r_{0}

under the condition
1<\sigma\leq 4/3
(i.e.,
r_{0}\geq 1
). Additionally, it follows that

|p^{\prime\prime}(r)|\sim\frac{1}{r^{1+\sigma/2}},\qquad r<1

whenever
1<\sigma<2
.

Similarly for
r>r_{0}
, letting
r^{\sigma}=r_{0}^{\sigma}+c^{\ast}
with some
c^{\ast}>0
yields

|p^{\prime\prime}(r)|=\frac{2\sigma(\sigma-1)r^{\sigma}-\sigma(2-\sigma)}{4r^{1+\sigma/2}(r^{\sigma}+1)^{3/2}}\sim\frac{1}{r^{1+\sigma/2}},\qquad r_{0}<r<1

for
4/3<\sigma<2
which means
r_{0}<1
. Moreover, we obtain

(B.1)

|p^{\prime\prime}(r)|=\frac{2\sigma(\sigma-1)r^{\sigma}-\sigma(2-\sigma)}{4r^{1+\sigma/2}(r^{\sigma}+1)^{3/2}}\sim\frac{1}{r^{1+2\sigma}},\qquad r_{0}\leq 1\leq r

for
1<\sigma\leq 4/3
.
The argument used for (
B.1
) also works for
\sigma>4/3
though, the following approach allows us to require slightly less regularity when
r>1
.
Indeed, when
\sigma>4/3
the condition
r\geq 1
implies

|p^{\prime\prime}(r)|=\frac{2\sigma(\sigma-1)r^{\sigma}-\sigma(2-\sigma)}{4r^{1+\sigma/2}(r^{\sigma}+1)^{3/2}}\gtrsim\frac{\sigma r^{\sigma}\{2(\sigma-1)-(2-\sigma)\}}{4r^{1+2\sigma}}\gtrsim\frac{1}{r^{1+\sigma}}

and

|p^{\prime\prime}(r)|\sim\frac{2\sigma(\sigma-1)r^{\sigma}-\sigma(2-\sigma)}{4r^{1+2\sigma}}\lesssim\frac{1}{r^{1+\sigma}}.

Hence, we have

|p^{\prime\prime}(r)|\sim\frac{1}{r^{1+\sigma}},\qquad r\geq 1

for
4/3<\sigma<2
.

Appendix C
Proof of Lemma
3.3

Here, we provide detailed proof for Lemma
3.3
. We only establish (
3.17
) since the other estimate can be derived through a similar argument. We write
B=B_{\mathfrak{M}}
for simplicity.

By using the Littlewood–Paley theorem, we get

(C.1)

\|\langle\nabla\rangle^{\alpha_{1}}D^{\alpha_{2}}B[f,g]\|_{L^{l_{1}^{\prime}}}^{2}\lesssim\left\|P_{\leq 1}B[f,g]\right\|_{L^{l_{1}^{\prime}}}^{2}+\sum_{N>1}N^{2(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})}\left\|P_{N}B[f,g]\right\|_{L^{l_{1}^{\prime}}}^{2},

where
D^{\alpha_{2}}=\langle|\nabla|^{\alpha_{2}}\rangle
or
D^{\alpha_{2}}=|\nabla|^{\alpha_{2}}
.
The first term can be estimated as follows:

\left\|P_{\leq 1}B[f,g]\right\|_{L^{l_{1}^{\prime}}}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{l_{2}}}\|g\|_{L^{2}}.

For the second term, we observe

\displaystyle\left\|P_{N}B[f,g]\right\|_{L^{l_{1}^{\prime}}}\lesssim\|P_{N}B[P_{\leq\frac{N}{8}}f,g]\|_{L^{l_{1}^{\prime}}}+\sum_{M>\frac{N}{8}}\|P_{N}B[P_{M}f,g]\|_{L^{l_{1}^{\prime}}}.

By using (
3.16
), we deduce

\displaystyle\|P_{N}B[P_{\leq\frac{N}{8}}f,g]\|_{L^{l_{1}^{\prime}}}\lesssim\|B[P_{\leq\frac{N}{8}}f,P_{\frac{N}{8}<\cdot<8N}g]\|_{L^{l_{1}^{\prime}}}\lesssim\|P_{\leq\frac{N}{8}}f\|_{L^{l_{2}}}\|P_{\frac{N}{8}<\cdot<8N}g\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{l_{2}}}\sum_{M\sim N}\|P_{M}g\|_{L^{2}}.

Consequently, we arrive at

\displaystyle\sum_{N>1}N^{2(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})}\|P_{N}B[P_{\leq\frac{N}{8}}f,g]\|_{L^{l_{1}^{\prime}}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{l_{2}}}^{2}\sum_{N>1}\left(\sum_{M\sim N}N^{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}\|P_{M}g\|_{L^{2}}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{l_{2}}}^{2}\sum_{M\gtrsim 1}M^{2(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})}\|P_{M}g\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{l_{2}}}^{2}\|g\|_{\dot{H}^{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}}^{2}.

Meanwhile, we find

\displaystyle\sum_{M>\frac{N}{8}}\|P_{N}B[P_{M}f,g]\|_{L^{l_{1}^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{M>\frac{N}{8}}\|P_{M}f\|_{L^{2}}\|g\|_{L^{l_{2}}}\lesssim\sum_{M>\frac{N}{8}}\frac{1}{M^{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}}\|P_{M}f\|_{\dot{H}^{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}}\|g\|_{L^{l_{2}}}

due to (
3.16
).
Then, it follows that

\displaystyle\sum_{N>1}N^{2(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})}\left(\sum_{M>\frac{N}{8}}\|P_{N}B[P_{M}f,g]\|_{L^{l_{1}^{\prime}}}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{N>1}\left(\sum_{M>\frac{N}{8}}\left(\frac{N}{M}\right)^{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}\|P_{M}f\|_{\dot{H}^{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}}\|g\|_{L^{l_{2}}}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|g\|_{L^{l_{2}}}^{2}\sum_{N>1}\left(\sum_{M>\frac{N}{8}}\left(\frac{N}{M}\right)^{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}\sum_{M>\frac{N}{8}}\left(\frac{N}{M}\right)^{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}\|P_{M}f\|_{\dot{H}^{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\|g\|_{L^{l_{2}}}^{2}\sum_{M>\frac{1}{8}}\sum_{1<N<8M}\left(\frac{N}{M}\right)^{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}\|P_{M}f\|_{\dot{H}^{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|g\|_{L^{l_{2}}}^{2}\sum_{M>\frac{1}{8}}\|P_{M}f\|_{\dot{H}^{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}}^{2}

by the Cauchy–Schwartz inequality and the fact that

\sum_{N<8M}\left(\frac{N}{M}\right)^{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}\lesssim 1.

Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{N>1}N^{2(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})}\left\|P_{N}B[f,g]\right\|_{L^{l_{1}^{\prime}}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{l_{2}}}^{2}\sum_{N\gtrsim 1}\|P_{N}g\|_{\dot{H}^{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}}^{2}+\|g\|_{L^{l_{2}}}^{2}\sum_{M>\frac{1}{8}}\|P_{M}f\|_{\dot{H}^{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{l_{2}}}^{2}\|g\|_{\dot{H}^{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}}^{2}+\|g\|_{L^{l_{2}}}^{2}\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}}^{2}.

Hence combining the estimates for the first and the second terms of (
C.1
), we conclude the desired result.

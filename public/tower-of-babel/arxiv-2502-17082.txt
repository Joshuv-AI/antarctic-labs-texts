On the Global Optimality of Fibonacci Lattices in the Torus
Nicolas Nagel

Abstract
We use linear programming bounds to analyze point sets in the torus with respect to their optimality for problems in discrepancy theory and quasi-Monte Carlo methods. These concepts will be unified by introducing tensor product energies. We show that the canonical 3 3 -point lattice in any dimension is globally optimal among all 3 3 -point sets in the torus with respect to a large class of such energies. This is a new instance of universal optimality, a special phenomenon that is only known for a small class of highly structured point sets. In the case of d = 2 d=2 dimensions it is conjectured that so-called Fibonacci lattices should also be optimal with respect to a large class of potentials. To this end we show that the 5 5 -point Fibonacci lattice is globally optimal for a continuously parametrized class of potentials relevant to the analysis fo the quasi-Monte Carlo method.

Introduction

1.1
Preface on energy

The notion of an energy of a point set is by now a classical one in physics, geometry and coding theory. Simply put, this asks to find a configuration of a given number of points in some space such that the total energy of the system, that is the sum of all the potential interactions between the points, is minimized. The case of the sphere (or more generally of two-point homogeneous spaces
[
54
]
, such as the projective plane) has garnered particular attention over the last couple of decades
[
1
,
2
,
7
,
8
,
9
,

,

,
15
,
16
,
17
,
18
,
19
,
20
,
25
,
30
,
31
,
49
,
56
]
. There, a straight forward notion of energy of a finite point set
X\subseteq S^{d}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
for a potential function
c:(0,2]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is given by

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in X\\
\mathbf{x}\neq\mathbf{y}\end{subarray}}c(\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}\|),

where
\|\cdot\|
denotes the Euclidean norm in
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
. A classical example would be Thomson’s problem
[
2
,
47
,
52
,
56
]
on
S^{2}
with the (scaled) Coulomb potential
c(r)=r^{-1}
. In this particular case, the optimal configurations for
N=1,2,3,4,5,6
and
12
points are known. Much more general, for certain dimensions
d
and certain numbers of points
N
there are point sets that are the minimizers of a large class of potentials simultaneously, a phenomenon known as
universal optimality

[
20
]
.

In this paper we will consider the case of the torus, denoted by
[0,1)^{d}
(in the literature also found as
\mathbb{T}^{d}
) with opposite faces identified. Consequently, functions on
[0,1)^{d}
will be considered as periodic functions, extended to
\mathbb{R}^{d}
in the obvious way if desired. So far, energies on the torus have mostly been studied where the interaction between particles depends on the Euclidean distance between them. In this sense, every point on the torus
[0,1)^{d}
is associated to a corresponding shifted (and possibly skewed) lattice in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and all possible interactions from a given point to all points of the corresponding lattice are considered, see
[

,

,
24
,

,
45
,

,

]
for some references. In this context, tools from complex analysis and modular forms are frequently used.

To motivate the potentials that we will consider we start by discussing some topics concerning uniformly distributed point sets in
[0,1)^{d}
.

1.2
Quasi-Monte Carlo integration, discrepancy and diaphony

Let
p>0
be a parameter. Consider the space
H^{1}_{p}
of
1
-periodic, absolutely continuous functions
f:[0,1)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
with

\|f\|_{H^{1}_{p}}^{2}\coloneqq\left(\int_{0}^{1}f(x)\,\text{d}x\right)^{2}+\frac{1}{p}\int_{0}^{1}f^{\prime}(x)^{2}\,\text{d}x

(
f^{\prime}
denoting the first weak derivative of
f
). This space turns out to be a reproducing kernel Hilbert space with kernel function
[
6
,
36
,
53
]

K_{p}^{1}(x,y)=1+\frac{p}{2}\left(\frac{1}{6}-|x-y|+|x-y|^{2}\right),

that is function evaluation at any point
y\in[0,1)
can be realized via a scalar product with
K(\cdot,y)
in
H^{1}_{p}
:

f(y)=\left(\int_{0}^{1}f(x)\,\text{d}x\right)\left(\int_{0}^{1}K(x,y)\,\text{d}x\right)+\frac{1}{p}\int_{0}^{1}f^{\prime}(x)\frac{\partial}{\partial x}K(x,y)\,\text{d}x

for all
f\in H_{p}^{1}
(in particular, function evaluation is continuous). Taking
d
-fold tensor products
H_{p}^{d}=H_{p}^{1}\otimes...\otimes H_{p}^{1}
gives a reproducing kernel Hilbert space of functions
f:[0,1)^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
(a Sobolev space of periodic functions of dominating mixed smoothness) having the kernel

K_{p}^{d}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})=\prod_{i=1}^{d}K_{p}^{1}(x_{i},y_{i})

for
\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in[0,1)^{d}
. With this at hand we can consider the quasi-Monte Carlo integration algorithm
[
27
,
32
,
37
,
40
]
for
f\in H_{p}^{d}
, that is we approximate

\int_{[0,1)^{d}}f(\mathbf{x})\,\text{d}\mathbf{x}\approx\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in X}f(\mathbf{x})

where
X\subseteq[0,1)^{d}
is a finite set of sample nodes with
\#X=N
. The quality of this approximation depends on the choice of evaluation nodes
X
and can be quantified via the worst case error
[
27
,
41
]

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{\|f\|_{H_{p}^{d}}\leq 1}\left|\int_{[0,1)^{d}}f(\mathbf{x})\,\text{d}\mathbf{x}-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in X}f(\mathbf{x})\right|^{2}=-1+\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in X}K_{p}^{d}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}).

(1.1)

The expression on the right hand side is related to the mainly geometric notion of periodic
L_{2}
-discrepancy on the torus
[
26
,
34
,
39
]

L_{2}^{\text{per}}(X)^{2}\coloneqq-\frac{N^{2}}{3^{d}}+\sum_{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in X}\prod_{i=1}^{d}\left(\frac{1}{2}-|x_{i}-y_{i}|+|x_{i}-y_{i}|^{2}\right)

(unnormalized, as given in
[
34
]
), in fact for
p=6
it only differs from the worst case integration error by a factor of
N^{2}/3^{d}
. It is also similar to the notion of diaphony
[
39
,
57
]

F_{N}(X)^{2}\coloneqq\sum_{\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}-\mathbf{0}}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{d}\max\{1,|m_{i}|\}\right)^{-2}\left|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in X}\exp(2\pi\mathrm{i}\mathbf{m}^{\top}\mathbf{x})\right|^{2}

see
[
34
,
43
]
for details. For small
N
or
d
optimal point configurations minimizing the discrepancy have been determined in
[
42
,
55
]
(note that they use a slightly different notion of discrepancy) and
[
36
]
.

1.3
Tensor product energies

Another interpretation, more akin to the notion of energy, is to observe that minimizing the worst case integration error (
1.1
) is equivalent to minimizing

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in X\\
\mathbf{x}\neq\mathbf{y}\end{subarray}}K_{p}^{d}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in X\\
\mathbf{x}\neq\mathbf{y}\end{subarray}}\prod_{i=1}^{d}\left(1+\frac{p}{2}\left(\frac{1}{6}-|x_{i}-y_{i}|+|x_{i}-y_{i}|^{2}\right)\right).

(1.2)

We can think of every point as a particle whose interaction is given by some potential function and the potential between any two points
\mathbf{x}
and
\mathbf{y}
only depends on

\displaystyle(\min\{|x_{1}-y_{1}|,1-|x_{1}-y_{1}|\},\dots,\min\{|x_{d}-y_{d}|,1-|x_{d}-y_{d}|\})

(1.3)

(since
t^{2}-t=(1-t)^{2}-(1-t)
for
0\leq t\leq 1
). The terms in the components are respectively the geodesic distances between two points in the torus
[0,1)
(similar to (8) in
[
36
]
). Minimizing the worst case integration error is thus equivalent to minimizing this sort of energy given by the kernel function
K_{p}^{d}
.

In the case of
d=2
and
p\in\{1,6\}
the minimizers of (
1.2
) were determined in
[
36
]
for
N\leq 16
points. Notably, it was observed that if
N=F_{\ell}
is a Fibonacci number (initializing
F_{0}=0,F_{1}=1
) in this range, namely
1,2,3,5,8,13
, the algorithm found that the
Fibonacci lattices
given by

\displaystyle\Phi_{\ell}\coloneqq\left\{\left(\frac{m}{F_{\ell}},\left\{\frac{mF_{\ell-1}}{F_{\ell}}\right\}\right):m=0,1,\dots,F_{\ell}-1\right\}

(1.4)

are the global optimizers for the worst case integration error for their respective numbers of points. Prior to that, it was already known that the Fibonacci lattices are at least asymptotically of optimal order, matching (up to a multiplicative constant) the lower bound given by (for
d=2
)

-1+\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in X}K_{p}^{2}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\gtrsim\frac{\log N}{N^{2}},

see
[
12
,
13
,
14
,
28
,
34
,
35
,
44
,
50
]
. In this paper we investigate the question whether Fibonacci lattices are also optimal with respect to a more general notion of energy. The following definition summarizes our setup.

Definition 1
.

Let
c:[0,1)^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a
1
-periodic function in every component. We say that
c
is a
potential
if
c(t_{1},\dots,t_{d})=c(t_{\tau(1)},\dots,t_{\tau(d)})
for any permutation
\tau
of
[d]
and
c(t_{1},t_{2},\dots,t_{d})=c(1-t_{1},t_{2},\dots,t_{d})
for all
\mathbf{t}\in[0,1)^{d}
. For a finite point set
X\subseteq[0,1)^{d}
the
tensor product energy
with respect to the potential
c
is given by

E_{c}(X)\coloneqq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in X\\
\mathbf{x}\neq\mathbf{y}\end{subarray}}c(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}).

The conditions on the potential guarantee that the potential between two point sets only depends on (
1.3
). A special example of a tensor product energy is given by (
1.2
) with (parametrized) potential

\displaystyle c_{p}^{d}(\mathbf{t})=\prod_{i=1}^{d}\left(1+\frac{p}{2}\left(\frac{1}{6}-t_{i}+t_{i}^{2}\right)\right).

(1.5)

This potential tensorizes, hence the name “tensor product potential”, although for the sake of generality we have defined it more broadly.

1.4
Main results

The main contribution of this paper is to show that certain point sets are globally optimal with respect to a wide range of potentials. We start with the following result concerning the
5
-point Fibonacci lattice.

Theorem 2
.

Let
d=2
and let the potential
c=c_{p}^{2}
be given by (
1.5
). Then the
5
-point Fibonacci lattice

\Phi_{5}=\left\{(0,0),\left(\frac{1}{5},\frac{3}{5}\right),\left(\frac{2}{5},\frac{1}{5}\right),\left(\frac{3}{5},\frac{4}{5}\right),\left(\frac{4}{5},\frac{2}{5}\right)\right\}

minimizes
E_{c_{p}^{2}}
among all
5
-point sets in
[0,1)^{2}
for the range
0\leq p\leq 9
.

This generalizes the results from
[
36
]
for
N=5
. The method there is computationally heavy and can only check that
\Phi_{5}
is optimal for any individual
p
. Here however we will get the optimality of an entire range by checking only a few, elementary inequalities.

For the next theorem we need to introduce a certain transformation.

Definition 3
.

For a potential
c:[0,1)^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
define the
arccos-transform

\gamma:[-1,1]^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R},\gamma(s_{1},\dots,s_{d})\coloneqq c\left(\frac{\arccos s_{1}}{2\pi},\dots,\frac{\arccos s_{d}}{2\pi}\right).

Under this transformation we have

E_{c}(X)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in X\\
\mathbf{x}\neq\mathbf{y}\end{subarray}}\gamma\left(\cos(2\pi(x_{1}-y_{1})),\dots,\cos(2\pi(x_{d}-y_{d}))\right).

Theorem 4
.

Let the dimension
d\in\mathbb{N}
be arbitrary and
c:[0,1)^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a potential with the corresponding arccos-transform
\gamma:[-1,1]^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
. Assume that
\gamma
is convex and increasing in the sense that if
\mathbf{s}^{1}\leq\mathbf{s}^{2}
componentwise then
\gamma(\mathbf{s}^{1})\leq\gamma(\mathbf{s}^{2})
. Then

\mathcal{T}^{d}\coloneqq\left\{\frac{1}{3}(k,\dots,k):k=0,1,2\right\}

minimizes
E_{c}
among all
3
-point sets in
[0,1)^{d}
.

This theorem gives the
universal optimality property
for the
3
-point lattice
\mathcal{T}^{d}
in any dimension, meaning that it is the global minimizer among all
3
-point sets for a large, natural class of potentials. Observe that any
3
-point rational lattice (see Definition
8
below) in
[0,1)^{d}
can be transformed into
\mathcal{T}^{d}
under a torus symmetry (coordinate shifts, reflections and permutations) so that we might speak of
the
rational
3
-point lattice. In particular, in
d=2
dimensions,
\mathcal{T}^{2}
is equivalent to the
3
-point Fibonacci lattice
\Phi_{4}
.

Remark 5
.

Note that the case for
N=2
is easily dealt with, since then the point set
\{(0,\dots,0),(1/2,\dots,1/2)\}
is the minimizer for a potential
c
if and only if
c
has a global minimum in
\mathbf{t}=(1/2,\dots,1/2)
.

2
The linear programming bound

Let us state here the main tool for proving lower bounds. This is an adaptation of the method on the sphere, see
[
20
,
25
,
56
]
, to the torus.

Theorem 6
.

Let
c:[0,1)^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a potential. Let
b:[0,1)^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be such that
c(\mathbf{t})\geq b(\mathbf{t})
for all
\mathbf{t}\in[0,1)^{d}
and for its Fourier expansion we have

b(\mathbf{t})=\sum_{\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\hat{b}(\mathbf{m})\exp(2\pi\mathrm{i}\mathbf{m}^{\top}\mathbf{t})

(converging pointwise) with
\hat{b}(\mathbf{m})\geq 0
for all
\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}-\mathbf{0}
. Then

E_{c}(X)\geq N^{2}\hat{b}(\mathbf{0})-Nb(\mathbf{0})

for all
X\subseteq[0,1)^{d}
with
\#X=N
.

A function on the torus
[0,1)^{d}
all of whose Fourier coefficients are nonnegative is also called
positive definite
. The pointwise convergence of
b
will turn out to not be a problem for us since the functions that we will construct further below only us a finite set of indices, that is we only consider trigonometric polynomials.

Proof of Theorem
6
.

Let
b
be as in the assumptions, then

\displaystyle\begin{split}E_{c}(X)&=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in X\\
\mathbf{x}\neq\mathbf{y}\end{subarray}}c(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y})\geq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in X\\
\mathbf{x}\neq\mathbf{y}\end{subarray}}b(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y})=-Nb(\mathbf{0})+\sum_{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in X}b(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y})\\
&=-Nb(\mathbf{0})+\sum_{\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\hat{b}(\mathbf{m})\sum_{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in X}\exp(2\pi\mathrm{i}\mathbf{m}^{\top}(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}))\\
&=-Nb(\mathbf{0})+\sum_{\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\hat{b}(\mathbf{m})\left|\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in X}\exp(2\pi\mathrm{i}\mathbf{m}^{\top}\mathbf{x})\right|^{2}\\
&\geq N^{2}\hat{b}(\mathbf{0})-Nb(\mathbf{0}).\end{split}

(2.1)

∎

For the analysis we will reformulate this statement in terms of
\gamma
. For this we will need the
Chebyshev polynomials

T_{m}(s)\coloneqq\cos(m\arccos s).

Corollary 7
.

Let
c:[0,1)^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a potential with arccos-transform
\gamma:[-1,1]^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
. Let
\beta:[-1,1]^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
fulfill
\gamma(\mathbf{s})\geq\beta(\mathbf{s})
for all
\mathbf{s}\in[-1,1]^{d}
and

\beta(\mathbf{s})=\sum_{\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}}\hat{\beta}(\mathbf{m})\prod_{i=1}^{d}T_{m_{i}}(s_{i})

(converging pointwise) with
\hat{\beta}(\mathbf{m})\geq 0
for all
\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}-\mathbf{0}
. Then

E_{c}(X)\geq N^{2}\hat{\beta}(\mathbf{0})-N\beta(\mathbf{1})

for all
X\subseteq[0,1)^{d},\#X=N
.

Proof.

Let
\beta
be as in the assumption and consider the function
b:[0,1)^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
given by

\displaystyle b(\mathbf{t})=\sum_{\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}2^{-\#\{i\in[d]:m_{i}\neq 0\}}\hat{\beta}(|m_{1}|,\dots,|m_{d}|)\exp(2\pi\mathrm{i}\mathbf{m}^{\top}\mathbf{t}).

(2.2)

We will show that
b
is a valid function fulfilling the requirements of Theorem
6
. Indeed, by definition it is clear that
b
only has nonnegative Fourier coefficients and it remains to check that
c\geq b
. For this note that

\displaystyle b(\mathbf{t})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}}\hat{\beta}(\mathbf{m})\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\underline{\varepsilon}\in\{-1,1\}^{d}\\
\varepsilon_{i}\in\mathfrak{S}(m_{i})\end{subarray}}\prod_{i=1}^{d}\frac{\exp(2\pi\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_{i}m_{i}t_{i})}{\#\mathfrak{S}(m_{i})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}}\hat{\beta}(\mathbf{m})\prod_{i=1}^{d}\left(\frac{1}{\#\mathfrak{S}(m_{i})}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\varepsilon\in\mathfrak{S}(m_{i})\end{subarray}}\exp(2\pi\mathrm{i}\varepsilon m_{i}t_{i})\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}}\hat{\beta}(\mathbf{m})\prod_{i=1}^{d}\cos(2\pi m_{i}t_{i}),

where
\underline{\varepsilon}=(\varepsilon_{1},\dots,\varepsilon_{d})
and

\mathfrak{S}(m)\coloneqq\begin{cases}\{1\}&,m=0\\
\{-1,1\}&,m\neq 0.\end{cases}

In particular we get that

b\left(\frac{\arccos s_{1}}{2\pi},\dots,\frac{\arccos s_{d}}{2\pi}\right)=\sum_{\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}}\hat{\beta}(\mathbf{m})\prod_{i=1}^{d}T_{m_{i}}(s_{i})=\beta(\mathbf{s}),

that is
\beta
is the arccos-transform of
b
. By
\gamma\geq\beta
we thus have
c(\mathbf{t})\geq b(\mathbf{t})
for
\mathbf{t}\in[0,1/2]^{d}
(the range of the function
s\mapsto\arccos(s)/2\pi
). However, by (
1.3
), which by construction is also fulfilled by
b
, the functions
c
and
b
are completely determined by their values on
[0,1/2]^{d}
, so that we even have
c(\mathbf{t})\geq b(\mathbf{t})
for all
\mathbf{t}\in[0,1)^{d}
. We may thus apply Theorem
6
, where it remains to note that
\hat{b}(\mathbf{0})=\hat{\beta}(\mathbf{0})
and
b(\mathbf{0})=\beta(\mathbf{1})
.
∎

Let us finish this section by inspecting when we get equality in the LP-bound. Let us start by the following special class of point sets.

Definition 8
.

A
rational lattice
is a finite set
\Lambda\subseteq[0,1)^{d},\#\Lambda=N
of the form

\left\{\left(\frac{m}{N},\left\{\frac{mh_{2}}{N}\right\},\dots,\left\{\frac{mh_{d}}{N}\right\}\right):m=0,1,\dots,N-1\right\},

where
h_{2},\dots,h_{d}\in\{1,\dots,N-1\}
with
\gcd(h_{2},N)=\dots=\gcd(h_{d},N)=1
. The vector
\frac{1}{N}(1,h_{2},\dots,h_{d})
is called the
generating vector
of
\Lambda
.

Theorem 9
.

Let
\Lambda\subseteq[0,1)^{d}
be a rational lattice of size
\#\Lambda=N<\infty
and generating vector
\frac{1}{N}(1,h_{2},\dots,h_{d})
. Let
c:[0,1)^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a potential with arccos-transform
\gamma:[-1,1]^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
. Let
\beta:[-1,1]^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be such that:

(i)

\gamma(\mathbf{s})\geq\beta(\mathbf{s})
for all
\mathbf{s}\in[-1,1]^{d}
,

(ii)

\beta(\mathbf{s})=\sum_{\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}}\hat{\beta}(\mathbf{m})\prod_{i=1}^{d}T_{m_{i}}(s_{i})
(converging pointwise) with
\hat{\beta}(\mathbf{m})\geq 0
for all
\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}-\mathbf{0}
,

(iii)

\beta(\cos(2\pi s_{1}),\dots,\cos(2\pi s_{d}))=\gamma(\cos(2\pi s_{1}),\dots,\cos(2\pi s_{d}))
for all
\mathbf{s}\in\Lambda-\mathbf{0}
,

(iv)

\hat{\beta}(\mathbf{m})=0
for all
\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}-\mathbf{0}
for which there are signs
(\varepsilon_{1},\dots,\varepsilon_{d})\in\{-1,1\}^{d}
such that
\varepsilon_{1}m_{1}+\varepsilon_{2}m_{2}h_{2}+\dots+\varepsilon_{d}m_{d}h_{d}\equiv 0\mod N
.

Then
E_{c}(X)\geq E_{c}(\Lambda)
for all
X\subseteq[0,1)^{d},\#X=N
.

Proof.

Let
X\subseteq[0,1)^{d},\#X=N
be arbitrary. By Corollary
7
conditions (i) and (ii) give

E_{c}(X)\geq N^{2}\hat{\beta}(\mathbf{0})-N\beta(\mathbf{1}).

Condition (iii) and the lattice structure of
\Lambda
imply that

\displaystyle E_{c}(\Lambda)=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in\Lambda\\
\mathbf{x}\neq\mathbf{y}\end{subarray}}c(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in\Lambda\\
\mathbf{x}\neq\mathbf{y}\end{subarray}}\gamma(\cos(2\pi(x_{1}-y_{1})),\dots,\cos(2\pi(x_{d}-y_{d})))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in\Lambda\\
\mathbf{x}\neq\mathbf{y}\end{subarray}}\beta(\cos(2\pi(x_{1}-y_{1})),\dots,\cos(2\pi(x_{d}-y_{d})))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in\Lambda\\
\mathbf{x}\neq\mathbf{y}\end{subarray}}b(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}),

so that we have equality in the first inequality of (
2.1
). As for the second inequality therein, note that one would need to have

\hat{b}(\mathbf{m})\left|\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in\Lambda}\exp(2\pi\mathrm{i}\mathbf{m}^{\top}\mathbf{x})\right|^{2}=0

for all
\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}-\mathbf{0}
. Thus at least one of
\hat{b}(\mathbf{m})
and

5
Summary and outlook

Motivated by discrepancy theory and related topics we introduced tensor product energies on the torus. We used linear programming methods to show the optimality of certain point sets. To this end we showed that the rational
3
-point lattice in any dimension fulfills a universal optimality statement with respect to monotone and convex potentials (Theorem
4
). In the special case of the two-dimensional torus we showed the optimality of the
5
-point Fibonacci lattice for a continuously parametrized set of potentials related to the worst case error of the quasi-Monte Carlo method over certain Sobolev spaces of smoothness
1
(Theorem
2
).

There are several open question that remain concerning this topic. The main one is whether general Fibonacci lattices also fulfill a universal optimality condition and, if so, if this can also be shown for special point sets in the
d
-dimensional torus. Another question would be to generalize these results to other spaces. Notably, interpreting the
d
-dimensional torus as the
d
-fold product of one-dimensional spheres
S^{1}
, a natural next step would be to consider general product spaces of the form
S^{n_{1}}\times\dots\times S^{n_{d}}
. Further extensions to other non-two-point homogeneous spaces such as Grassmannians
[
22
,
29
]
or discrete spaces
[
4
,

]
are also imaginable.

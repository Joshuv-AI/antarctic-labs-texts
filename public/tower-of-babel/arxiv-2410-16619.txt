A CMC existence result for expanding cosmological spacetimes
Gregory J. Galloway, Eric Ling

Abstract
We establish a new CMC (constant mean curvature) existence result for cosmological spacetimes, by which we mean globally hyperbolic spacetimes with compact Cauchy surfaces. If a cosmological spacetime satisfying the strong energy condition contains an expanding Cauchy surface and is future timelike geodesically complete, then the spacetime contains a CMC Cauchy surface. This result settles, under certain circumstances, a conjecture of the authors and a conjecture of Dilts and Holst. Our proof relies on the construction of barriers in the support sense, and the CMC Cauchy surface is found as the asymptotic limit of mean curvature flow. Analogous results are also obtained in the case of a positive cosmological constant Λ > 0 \Lambda>0 . Lastly, we include some comments concerning the future causal boundary for cosmological spacetimes which pertain to the CMC conjecture of the authors.

Introduction

As is well known, in order to solve the Einstein equations of general relativity, one must first obtain initial data that satisfies the Einstein constraint equations, and then use the Einstein evolution equations to evolve that data.
The standard conformal method for solving the constraint equations involves solving certain equations which decouple when the mean curvature associated to the initial data is assumed to be constant. This assumption then substantially simplifies the problem of solving the constraint equations; see e.g.,
[
23
]
. There are also known advantages for solving the Einstein evolution equations if one works in the CMC gauge, given CMC initial data; see e.g.,
[
2
]
.

One is naturally led to consider the circumstances under which a given spacetime admits a CMC spacelike hypersurface. In
[
9
]
, Dilts and Holst address this issue for the class of what they call
cosmological spacetimes
. These are globally hyperbolic spacetimes
(M,g)
, with compact Cauchy surfaces, that satisfy the strong energy condition,
{\rm Ric}(X,X)\geq 0
for all timelike vectors
X
.
In this setting, they review various results that establish the existence of CMC Cauchy surfaces under various conditions (e.g., as in
[
20
,
4
,
5
]
), as well as examples of spacetimes (suitably global) that fail to have CMC Cauchy surfaces (e.g., as in
[
5
,
8
]
and generalizations thereof
[
25
,
26
]
).

Consideration of these results and examples led them to formulate several conjectures concerning the existence (and nonexistence) of CMC Cauchy surfaces in cosmological spacetimes. Motivated by some of their considerations, in
[
16
]
we obtained the following CMC existence result which relies on a certain spacetime curvature condition.

Theorem 1

(
[
16
]
)
.

Let
(M,g)
be a spacetime with compact Cauchy surfaces. Suppose
(M,g)
is future timelike geodesically complete and has everywhere nonpositive timelike sectional curvatures, i.e.,
K\leq 0
everywhere. Then

(M,g)
contains a CMC Cauchy surface.

Since the sectional curvature condition above implies the strong energy condition, we were naturally led to the following conjecture.

Conjecture 2
.

Let
(M,g)
be a spacetime with compact Cauchy surfaces. If
(M,g)
is future timelike geodesically complete and satisfies the strong energy condition, i.e.,
\emph{Ric}(X,X)\geq 0
for all timelike
X
, then
(M,g)
contains a CMC Cauchy surface.

In this article, we show that the above conjecture holds if we make the additional assumption that the spacetime contains a Cauchy surface with nonnegative mean curvature. (A similar result was obtained previously under special conditions
[
17
]
.) Specifically, our main result is the following.

Theorem 3
.

Let
(M,g)
be a spacetime with compact Cauchy surfaces, and suppose there exists a smooth spacelike Cauchy surface
V
with mean curvature
H\geq 0
. If
(M,g)
is future timelike geodesically complete and satisfies the strong energy condition, i.e.,
{\rm Ric}(X,X)\geq 0
for all timelike
X
, then
(M,g)
contains a CMC Cauchy surface.

This theorem also settles Conjecture 3.7 in
[
9
]
in the case that the spacetime is future timelike geodesically complete.
(In fact, as discussed in Section
3
, the future completeness assumption can be weakened.)
The proof of
Theorem
3

makes use of certain prescribed mean curvature existence results, via mean curvature flow, of Ecker and Huisken
[
12
]
modified to apply to the setting of mean curvature barriers in the support sense (as defined in section
3

In Section 2 we provide the relevant background material concerning mean curvature flow needed for our purposes. The proof of Theorem
3
is presented in Section 3. In Section 4 we discuss an extension of Theorem

to the case of a positive cosmological constant. Section 5 includes some further remarks concerning

Spacetime and mean curvature flow preliminaries

We recall some basic definitions and facts. By a spacetime, we mean an
(n+1)
-dimensional,
n\geq 2
, time-orientable Lorentzian manifold
(M,g)
, which, for the analytic purposes of this paper, we assume smoothness of both the manifold and metric. Classically, a spacetime
(M,g)
is globally hyperbolic if it is strongly causal and all ‘causal diamonds’
J^{+}(p)\cap J^{-}(q)
are compact. A Cauchy (hyper)surface for a spacetime
(M,g)
is a set
S
that is met by every inextendible causal curve exactly once, which implies that
S
is a topological (in fact locally Lipschitz) hypersurface and
M
is homeomorphic to
\mathbb{R}\times S
.
It is a well-known fact that a spacetime
(M,g)
is globally hyperbolic if and only if it contains a Cauchy surface
S
.

Fix a globally hyperbolic spacetime
(M,g)
. Let
F_{0}\colon V\to M
be an embedding such that
V_{0}:=F_{0}(V)
is a smooth compact spacelike Cauchy surface. The following evolution problem defines mean curvature flow with prescribed constant mean curvature
c\in\mathbb{R}
:

\frac{d}{ds}F(x,s)\,=\,[(H-c)\nu](x,s)\quad\text{ and }\quad F(x,0)\,=\,F_{0}(x),

(2.1)

where
\nu(x,s)
is the future-directed unit normal to the smooth spacelike Cauchy surfaces

V_{s}\,=\,\text{Image}\big[F(\cdot,s)\big],

(2.2)

and
H(x,s)
is the mean curvature of
V_{s}
at
F(x,s)
defined with respect to
\nu(x,s)
. By
[
12
, Thm. 4.1]
, a solution to this evolution problem exists at least locally for
s\in[0,s_{0})
; that is, there is a smooth map

F\colon V\times[0,s_{0})\to M

(2.3)

such that

F_{*}(\partial_{s})\,=\,(H-c)\nu\quad\text{ and }\quad F(\cdot,0)\,=\,F_{0}.

(2.4)

Moreover, if the flow
\{V_{s}\}_{s\in[0,s_{0})}
remains in a compact region of the spacetime
M
, then the solution can be extended beyond
s_{0}
.

More generally,

Proposition 4
.

Let
(M,g)
be an
(n+1)
-dimensional spacetime with compact Cauchy surfaces. Let
S
be a fixed Cauchy surface. Consider the sets

S_{\tau}=\{q\in M\mid d(S,q)=\tau\}.

Suppose

Proof.

For the convenience of the reader, we provide a complete proof that

is a Cauchy surface (cf.
[
18
, Lem. 3.15]

S

(M,g)

x\mapsto d(S,x)
is continuous, finite-valued, and for each
q\in S_{\tau}
, there is a unit speed timelike geodesic
\gamma\colon[0,\tau]\to M

to
q
, i.e.,
L(\gamma)=d(S,q)
.

S_{\tau}
is closed by continuity of the distance function. Moreover,
S_{\tau}
is acausal: Clearly
S_{\tau}
is achronal. Let
p,q,\gamma
be as in the first paragraph of this proof. If there is another point
q^{\prime}\in S_{\tau}
with

\lambda

q
to
q^{\prime}
. Consider a convex neighborhood

and

\sigma\subset U

\gamma
from

to
q

[
27

p
to
x
along
\gamma

to

\sigma

to

has no edge points and consequently is a topological hypersurface

27

Now we show that
S_{\tau}
is compact. Let
h
be a complete Riemannian metric on
M
. If
S_{\tau}
is not compact, then there is a sequence of points

such that the

be an
S
-maximizer from
p_{n}\in S
to
q_{n}
, parameterized by
h
-arclength so that

is acausal, each
\gamma_{n}
is a timelike geodesic (when appropriately reparameterized). Let
\widetilde{\gamma}_{n}\colon[0,\infty)\to M
be the future inextendible timelike geodesic extension of
\gamma_{n}
, also parameterized by
h
-arclength. By compactness of

, there is a

such that
p_{n}\to p
(after passing to a subsequence). The limit curve lemma guarantees the existence of a future inextendible causal curve

\gamma
uniformly on compact subsets of
[0,\infty)
. Moreover,
\gamma
is an
S
-maximizer since each
\gamma_{n}
is an
S
-maximizer. (This follows by upper semicontinuity of the Lorentzian length functional.) Therefore, after a reparameterization,
\gamma

. Set

q=\gamma(c),\quad q^{\prime}=\gamma(c+1),\quad q_{n}=\widetilde{\gamma}_{n}(c),\quad q_{n}^{\prime}=\widetilde{\gamma}_{n}(c+1).

Since

If

is closed and acausal, we can apply

Lastly, we show that

4
The case of a positive cosmological constant

In this section we indicate how Theorem
3
extends to the case of a positive cosmological constant.

4.1
Extending Theorem
3
to the case
\Lambda>0

The Einstein equation with a cosmological constant
\Lambda
when solved for the spacetime Ricci tensor becomes

\text{Ric}\,=\,8\pi\left(T-\frac{1}{n-1}\text{tr}_{g}(T)g\right)+\frac{2}{n-1}\Lambda g.

(4.10)

By imposing a standard condition on the energy-momentum tensor
[
22
, Section 4.3]
,
one is led to the following version of the strong energy condition in the case of a positive cosmological constant,

{\rm Ric}(X,X)\geq-n\lambda\,\quad\text{for all unit timelike vectors }X\,,

(4.11)

where
\lambda=\frac{2}{n(n-1)}\Lambda>0
.

In the case of a positive cosmological constant, we have the following analogue of Proposition
4
.

Proposition 6
.

Let
(M,g)
be an
(n+1)
-dimensional spacetime with compact Cauchy surfaces. Fix a Cauchy surface
S
, and consider the sets

S_{\tau}=\{q\in M\mid d(S,q)=\tau\}.

Suppose
(M,g)

). Then, for each
\tau>0
,
S_{\tau}
is a Cauchy surface with mean curvature

Proof.

The proof proceeds just as in the proof of Proposition

In the present situation the inequality (

) becomes,

\frac{d\mathcal{H}}{dr}\leq\lambda-\mathcal{H}^{2},

where
\mathcal{H}=\frac{H}{n}
. The theorem again follows from standard comparison results (see e.g.,
[

[

, Section 2]

As discussed in the next subsection, Theorem 4.1 and the existence part of Theorem 4.2 in

remain valid if the strong energy condition is replaced by the inequality (

).
We thus obtain the following extension of Theorem

3
.

Theorem 7
.

Let
(M,g)
be a spacetime with compact Cauchy surfaces, and suppose there exists a smooth spacelike Cauchy surface with mean curvature
H\geq n\sqrt{\lambda}
. If
(M,g)
is future timelike geodesically complete and the Ricci tensor satisfies the inequality (

4.2
Remarks on extending the results in
[
12
]
to the case
\Lambda>0

One of the assumptions in
[
12
]
is that the spacetime
(M,g)
satisfies the strong energy condition, i.e.,
{\rm Ric}(X,X)\geq 0
for all timelike
X
. As discussed above, when the Einstein equations hold with a positive cosmological constant, the appropriate version of this energy condition is the assumption (
4.11
), where
\lambda>0
.

The work in
[
11
]
in the cosmological setting shows that the existence results in
[
12
]
can be extended to the case when the spacetime satisfies (
4.11
). Moreover, the forcing term
\mathscr{H}
in
[
11
]
can be any smooth function; e.g., it need not be constant as it is in this work nor, more generally, satisfy the monotonicity assumption as in
[
12
]
. In this subsection, we wish to point out how the arguments in
[
12
]
can be easily modified to handle the case of a positive cosmological constant in our setting.

The proof of Theorem 4.1 in
[
12
]
(and hence also the proof of the existence part of Theorem 4.2 in
[
12
]
) relies on the estimates given in Propositions 4.4, 4.6, and 4.7 in
[
12
]
. Specifically, Propositions 4.4 and 4.7 rely on the mean curvature estimate established in 4.6 – this is the only place where the energy condition plays a role.

We show how
[
12
, Prop. 4.6]
generalizes to the case when (
4.11
) is assumed. However, in this new setting, the constant
C
appearing in the statement of
[
12
, Prop. 4.6]
will now also depend on
\lambda
. From Proposition 3.3(ii) and eq. (1) in
[
12
]
, we have the following equation

\left(\frac{d}{ds}-\Delta_{s}\right)(H-\mathscr{H})\,=\,-(H-\mathscr{H})\big(|A|^{2}+\Ric(\nu,\nu)+\langle\nabla\mathscr{H},\nu\rangle\big),

(4.12)

where
\mathscr{H}
is a smooth function on
M
and
\Delta_{s}
is the spatial Laplacian induced from the Riemannian metric on
V_{s}
. (Note there is a typo in the proof of
[
12
, Prop. 4.6]
. A parenthesis is misplaced in the first line of the proof.)

Set
f=H-\mathscr{H}
. Then

\left(\frac{d}{ds}-\Delta_{s}\right)f^{2}\,=\,-2f^{2}\big(|A|^{2}+\Ric(\nu,\nu)+\langle\nabla\mathscr{H},\nu\rangle\big)-2|\nabla_{s}f|^{2}.

Assuming (
4.11
) and the monotonicity assumption as in
[
12
]
,
\langle\nabla\mathscr{H},\nu\rangle\geq 0
(which is trivially satisfied in our situation
\mathscr{H}=c=
const.), we have

\displaystyle\left(\frac{d}{ds}-\Delta_{s}\right)f^{2}\,

\displaystyle\leq\,-2f^{2}|A|^{2}+2n\lambda f^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\,-\frac{2}{n}f^{2}H^{2}+2n\lambda f^{2}\,.

Also,

-f^{2}H^{2}\,=\,-f^{2}(H-\mathscr{H}+\mathscr{H})^{2}\,\leq\,-f^{4}+2|f^{3}\mathscr{H}|\,.

Using the Peter-Paul inequality (
ab\leq\tfrac{a^{2}}{2\varepsilon}+\tfrac{\varepsilon b^{2}}{2}
) twice, we estimate the cubic term:

\displaystyle|f^{3}\mathscr{H}|\,

\displaystyle\leq\,f^{2}\left(\frac{f^{2}}{2\varepsilon_{1}}+\frac{\varepsilon_{1}\mathscr{H}^{2}}{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\,\frac{f^{4}}{2\varepsilon_{1}}+\frac{\varepsilon_{1}}{2}\left(\frac{f^{4}}{2\varepsilon_{2}}+\frac{\varepsilon_{2}\mathscr{H}^{4}}{2}\right).

Therefore

\left(\frac{d}{ds}-\Delta_{s}\right)f^{2}\,\leq\,-\frac{2}{n}f^{4}+\frac{4}{n}\left(\frac{1}{2\varepsilon_{1}}+\frac{\varepsilon_{1}}{4\varepsilon_{2}}\right)f^{4}+n\varepsilon_{1}\varepsilon_{2}\mathscr{H}^{4}+2n\lambda f^{2}.

Using Peter-Paul again, we have

2n\lambda f^{2}\,\leq\,2n\lambda\left(\frac{f^{4}}{2\varepsilon_{3}}+\frac{\varepsilon_{3}}{2}\right).

Therefore

\left(\frac{d}{ds}-\Delta_{s}\right)f^{2}\,\leq\,-\frac{2}{n}f^{4}+\frac{4}{n}\left(\frac{1}{2\varepsilon_{1}}+\frac{\varepsilon_{1}}{4\varepsilon_{2}}\right)f^{4}+n\varepsilon_{1}\varepsilon_{2}\mathscr{H}^{4}+\frac{n\lambda}{\varepsilon_{3}}f^{4}+n\lambda\varepsilon_{3}.

We can choose
\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2},\varepsilon_{3}>0
such that the coefficient in front of
f^{4}
is
\leq-\tfrac{1}{n}
. Hence

\left(\frac{d}{ds}-\Delta_{s}\right)f^{2}\,\leq\,-\frac{1}{n}f^{4}+n\varepsilon_{1}\varepsilon_{2}\mathscr{H}^{4}+n\lambda\varepsilon_{3}.

This inequality is equivalent to the last inequality in the proof of
[
12
, Prop. 4.6]
, where the constant now also depends on
\Lambda
.
The rest of the proof is as in
[
12
]
.

5
Further comments on Conjecture
2

In this last section, we once again assume the strong energy condition. We provide some further comments on Conjecture
2
and its relationship to the future causal boundary.
In section
5.1
we discuss some issues concerning the structure of the future causal boundary of cosmological spacetimes. In
section
5.2
we establish a connection between the future causal boundary being spacelike and the existence of a maximal Cauchy surface. The relationship to the Bartnik splitting conjecture is also discussed.

5.1
Some remarks on the causal boundary of cosmological spacetimes

Conjecture
2
was also motivated in part by the following conjecture
[
5
]
.

Conjecture 8

(Bartnik splitting conjecture)
.

Let
(M,g)
be a spacetime which contains a
compact Cauchy surface and obeys the strong energy condition,

{\rm Ric}(X,X)\geq 0
for all timelike vectors
X
. If
(M,g)
is timelike geodesically complete,
then
(M,g)
splits isometrically into the product

({\mathbb{R}}\times V,-dt^{2}\oplus h)
, where
(V,h)
is a compact Riemannian manifold.

For background and motivation, see e.g.,
[
15
]
.
While Conjecture
8
has been proven subject to various auxilliary conditions,
it remains open in full generality.
However, it is well known that Conjecture
8
holds if one can establish the existence of a CMC Cauchy surface.
In particular, it follows from
Theorem
1
that Conjecture
8
holds under the stronger assumption of nonpositive timelike sectional curvatures.

We make a few remarks about the proof of Theorem
1
. The proof involves the notion of the future causal boundary of a spacetime. Let
(M,g)
be a globally hyperbolic spacetime. Very briefly, the future causal boundary
\mathscr{C}^{+}
consists of the set of all
terminal indecomposable past
sets, or TIPs for short. These are the sets of the form
I^{-}(\gamma)
, where
\gamma
is a future inextendible timelike curve in
M
, with appropriate identifications, when two such curves have the same timelike past.
See e.g.,
[
22
, Section 6.8]
, for a more detailed discussion.

Note that
\mathscr{C}^{+}
consists of a single element if and only if
I^{-}(\gamma)=M
for all future inextendible timelike curves, i.e., if and only if there are no future observer horizons. The timelike sectional curvature condition in Theorem
1
is used to show that
\mathscr{C}^{+}
consists of a single element. This then guarantees the existence of a CMC Cauchy surface: from an observation of Tipler
[
28
]
, it follows that a criterion of Bartnik
[
5
]
for the existence of a CMC Cauchy surface is satisfied.

The authors have previously raised the question
[
15
,
17
]
as to whether one can show that
\mathscr{C}^{+}
consists of a single element if one replaces the timelike sectional curvature assumption in Theorem
1
with the strong energy condition. This of course would settle Conjecture
8
. In fact, the answer is “no”, as we now illustrate with a specific example. Consider the multiply warped product spacetime of the form

M=(0,\infty)\times T^{3},\quad g=-dt^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{3}a_{i}^{2}(t)(dx^{i})^{2}\,,

(5.13)

where the
a_{i}
’s are positive, and the
x^{i}
’s should be viewed as periodic coordinates. (Such spacetimes are sometimes used as spatially homogeneous models in cosmology
[
22
]
.) It is convenient to assume each
x^{i}
lies in an interval,
-b\leq x^{i}\leq b
, with the end points identified.
Basic causal theoretic arguments show that the tori,
V_{t}=\{t\}\times T^{3}
are Cauchy surfaces, and hence that
(M,g)
is globally hyperbolic.

Now consider the following choice for the
a_{i}
’s:

a_{1}=a_{2}=t^{\frac{3}{4}}\,,\;\;a_{3}=t^{\frac{5}{4}}\,.

(5.14)

It is not difficult to show by direct computation that the resulting model satisfies the strong energy condition, in fact it satisfies
{\rm Ric}(X,X)\geq 0
for all vectors
X
. (Alternatively, one could apply
[
10
, Prop. 3.2]
.) As a hint to this, observe that,

{\rm Ric}(e_{0},e_{0})=-\left(\frac{a_{1}^{\prime\prime}}{a_{1}}+\frac{a_{2}^{\prime\prime}}{a_{2}}+\frac{a_{3}^{\prime\prime}}{a_{3}}\right)=-\left(-\frac{3}{16}\frac{1}{t^{2}}-\frac{3}{16}\frac{1}{t^{2}}+\frac{5}{16}\frac{1}{t^{2}}\right)>0\,,

(5.15)

where
e_{0}=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}
. Moreover, it is not difficult to show that
M
is future timelike geodesically complete; this essentially relies on the fact that

\int_{t_{0}}^{\infty}a_{i}dt=\infty
,
i=1,2,3
. (For a general completeness result applicable to multiply warped product spacetimes, see
[
29
, Section 4]
.)

Now we consider the structure of the future causal boundary
\mathscr{C}^{+}
.
Let
t\to\gamma(t)
be the
t
-line centered at
x^{i}=0
,
i=1,2,3
. Let
s\to\eta(s)
be a past directed null geodesic starting at
(t_{0},0,0,0)
on
\gamma
, with
t_{0}>1
. With the choice
a_{3}=t^{\frac{5}{4}}
,
it is not difficult to show that the intersection of every such null geodesic
\eta
with the toroidal time slice
V_{1}=\{t=1\}
is confined to the region
-4<x^{3}<4
. This boils down to the fact that
\int_{1}^{t_{0}}t^{-\frac{5}{4}}dt<4
. Since
\partial I^{-}(\gamma(t_{0}))
is ruled by such geodesics, it follows that
I^{-}(\gamma(t_{0}))
does not meet the region
|x^{3}|\geq 4
. Since
I^{-}(\gamma)=\cup_{t_{0}\in(1,\infty)}I^{-}(\gamma(t_{0}))
, we conclude that
I^{-}(\gamma)\cap V_{1}
does not meet the region
|x^{3}|\geq 4
. In particular
I^{-}(\gamma)\neq M
.
This immediately implies that
\mathscr{C}^{+}
consists of more than one element. However, one can say somewhat more.

From the
x^{3}
-translational symmetry, a similar conclusion to the above holds for each
t
-line centered at
x^{1}=x^{2}=0,x^{3}=\xi
,
-b\leq\xi\leq b
(
b>4
), and, moreover, each such

-line defines a distinct element of
\mathscr{C}^{+}
. It can be further shown that

\mathscr{C}^{+}
consists only of these elements. In fact, much of this follows from a general result of Harris
[
21

, the relevant part of which we now paraphase.

By a general multiply warped product spacetime we mean a spacetime
(M,g)
of the form,

\displaystyle M

\displaystyle=(a,b)\times V_{1}\times\cdots\times V_{m}\,,\text{ and}

\displaystyle g

\displaystyle=-dt^{2}+a_{1}^{2}(t)h_{1}+a_{2}^{2}(t)h_{2}+\cdots+a_{m}^{2}(t)h_{m}\,,

where
(a,b)
is an interval (possibly
a=-\infty
or
b=\infty
), and where
for
i=1,...,m
,

(V_{i},h_{i})
is a Riemannian manifold.

The following is immediate from
[
21
, Prop. 3.5]
.

Proposition 9
.

Let
(M,g)
be a multiply warped product spacetime as above, such that each
V_{i}
is compact. Suppose

(i)

for
i=1,...,k
, and some
c\in(a,b)
,
\int_{c}^{b}a_{i}^{-1}dt=\infty
, and

(ii)

for
i=k+1,...,k_{m}
, and some
c\in(a,b)
,
\int_{c}^{b}a_{i}^{-1}dt<\infty
.

Then the future causal boundary
\mathscr{C}^{+}
(in an appropriate topology) is homeomorphic to
V_{k+1}\times\cdots\times V_{m}
. Moreover,
\mathscr{C}^{+}
is spacelike.

This result applied to our example (
5.13
), (
5.14
) implies that

\mathscr{C}^{+}
is homeomorphic to
S^{1}
, as is somewhat suggested by the discussion above. Furthermore,
\mathscr{C}^{+}
is spacelike.
In general, the future causal boundary being
spacelike
may be taken to mean that no TIP is properly contained in another TIP.

Thus, the simple example presented here, which can be generalized in various directions, shows that, under the assumptions of Conjecture
2
, the future causal boundary need not consist of a single element. One, however, may be inclined to formulate a new question: Under the assumptions of Conjecture
2
, will the future causal boundary always be spacelike? An example showing otherwise could not, of course, be within this class of multiply warped product spacetimes. Moreover, an affirmative answer would settle the Bartnik splitting conjecture. For, as shown in
[
19
]
, in this case the spacetime admits a timelike line (i.e., a globally maximizing inextendible timelike geodesic), and one can apply the Lorentzian splitting theorem under the assumptions of the Bartnik splitting conjecture. In the next section we provide an alternative proof of this fact. We show that if a spacetime satisfies the hypotheses of Conjecture
2
but is also past timelike complete
and has a spacelike future causal boundary, then the spacetime contains a maximal Cauchy surface and consequently splits
[
5
]
.

5.2
Existence of a maximal Cauchy surface from a spacelike causal boundary

Let
(M,g)
be a globally hyperbolic spacetime and let
S
be a (not necessarily smooth) Cauchy surface. A future inextendible timelike geodesic
\gamma\colon[0,a)\to M
, parameterized by proper time, is called a future
S
-ray provided it maximizes the distance to
S
, i.e.,
L(\gamma|_{[0,t]})=d\big(S,\gamma(t)\big)
for all
t\in[0,a)
. A past
S
-ray is defined similarly. If
S
is compact, then it always admits a future and past
S
-ray. If, for some Cauchy surface
S
,
M
contains a future
S
-ray
\gamma
and a past
S
-ray
\eta
such that
I^{-}(\gamma)\cap I^{+}(\eta)\neq\emptyset
, then we say that “
M
satisfies the ray-to-ray condition.”

Theorem 10
.

Suppose
(M,g)
satisfies the assumptions of Conjecture
2
but is also past timelike complete. If
M
satisfies the ray-to-ray condition, then
M
contains a maximal (i.e. mean curvature zero) Cauchy surface.

A spacelike future causal boundary implies the ray-to-ray condition, see
[
19
, Prop. 5.9]
. Thus we have the following corollary.

Corollary 11
.

Suppose
(M,g)
satisfies the assumptions of Conjecture
2
but is also past timelike complete. If
M
contains a spacelike future causal boundary, then
M
contains a maximal Cauchy surface.

10

S

S

Introduction

\mathbb{R}
and
\mathbb{R}_{+}
will stand for the sets of real and positive real numbers, respectively, and
I

[
9

[
3

[
4

3

[
5

, later Daróczy and Páles in
[
6

arrived at the same conclusion with first-order differentiability and without differentiability, respectively, but assuming the equality for all

[
1
]
determined the homogeneous Bajraktarević means that include the means which were introduced by Gini in
[
7

. Losonczi in
[
10

[

[
14

. Using these results, the homogeneous two-variable means were also determined by Losonczi
[

]
,

A characterization of the equality of two-variable Bajraktarević means has been proved in

The purpose of this paper is to consider an analogous problem for an nonsymmetric extension of Bajraktarević means, which was introduced by

2.
Preliminary and auxiliary results

Given a subset
S\subseteq\mathbb{R}
, the smallest convex set containing
S
, which is identical to the smallest interval containing
S
, will be denoted by
\mathop{\hbox{\rm conv}}\nolimits(S)
.
For our definition of generalized Bajraktarević means, we shall need the following lemma about the existence and properties of the left inverse of strictly monotone (but not necessarily continuous) functions.

Lemma 1
.

(
[
8
, Lemma 1]
)

Let
f:I\to\mathbb{R}
be a strictly monotone function. Then there exists a uniquely determined monotone function
f^{(-1)}:\mathop{\hbox{\rm conv}}\nolimits(f(I))\to I
such that
f^{(-1)}
is the left inverse of
f
, i.e.,

\displaystyle(f^{(-1)}\circ f)(x)=x\qquad(x\in I).

(1)

Furthermore,
f^{(-1)}
is monotone in the same sense as
f
, continuous,

\displaystyle(f\circ f^{(-1)})(y)=y\qquad(y\in f(I)),

(2)

and

\displaystyle\liminf_{x\to f^{(-1)}(y)}f(x)\leq y\leq\limsup_{x\to f^{(-1)}(y)}f(x)\qquad(y\in\mathop{\hbox{\rm conv}}\nolimits(f(I))).

(3)

Thus, if
f
is lower (resp. upper) semicontinuous at
f^{(-1)}(y)
, then
f\circ f^{(-1)}(y)\leq y
(resp.
y\leq f\circ f^{(-1)}(y)
).

It is clear from (
1
) and (
2
) that the restriction of
f^{(-1)}
to
f(I)
is the inverse of
f
in the standard sense. Therefore,
f^{(-1)}
is the continuous and monotone extension of the inverse of
f
to the smallest interval containing the range of
f
.

Given a strictly monotone function
f:I\to\mathbb{R}
and an
n
-tuple of positive valued functions
p=(p_{1},\dots,p_{n}):I\to\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}
, we introduce the
n
-variable generalized Bajraktarević mean

A_{f,p}:I^{n}\to I
by the following formula:

\displaystyle A_{f,p}(x):=f^{(-1)}\bigg(\frac{p_{1}(x_{1})f(x_{1})+\dots+p_{n}(x_{n})f(x_{n})}{p_{1}(x_{1})+\dots+p_{n}(x_{n})}\bigg)\qquad(x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in I^{n}),

(4)

and, to simplify the notations, we will use the following definition:

\displaystyle R_{f,p}(x):=\frac{p_{1}(x_{1})f(x_{1})+\dots+p_{n}(x_{n})f(x_{n})}{p_{1}(x_{1})+\dots+p_{n}(x_{n})}.

(5)

Theorem 2
.

(
[
8
, Theorem 2]
)

Let
f:I\to\mathbb{R}
be strictly monotone and
p=(p_{1},\dots,p_{n}):I\to\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}
. Then the function
A_{f,p}:I^{n}\to I
given by
(
4
)
is well-defined and it is a mean, that is,

\displaystyle\min(x)\leq A_{f,p}(x)\leq\max(x)\qquad(x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in I^{n}).

(6)

Theorem 3
.

(
[
8
, Theorem 3]
)

Let
f:I\to\mathbb{R}
be strictly increasing and
p=(p_{1},\dots,p_{n}):I\to\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}
. Then, for all
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in I^{n}
, the equality
y=A_{f,p}(x)
holds if and only if

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}p_{i}(x_{i})(f(z)-f(x_{i}))\begin{cases}<0&\mbox{ for }z\in I,\,z<y,\\[5.69054pt]
>0&\mbox{ for }z\in I,\,z>y.\end{cases}

(7)

If
f
is strictly decreasing, then the inequalities
(
7
)
hold with reversed inequality sign.

Corollary 4
.

(
[
8
, Corollary 4]
)

Let
f:I\to\mathbb{R}
be continuous, strictly monotone, and
p=(p_{1},\dots,p_{n}):I\to\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}
. Then, for all
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in I^{n}
, the value
y=A_{f,p}(x)
is the unique solution of the equation

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}p_{i}(x_{i})(f(y)-f(x_{i}))=0.

(8)

For the formulation of the subsequent results, we define the
diagonal

\mathop{\hbox{\rm diag}}\nolimits(I^{n})
of
I^{n}
by

\displaystyle\mathop{\hbox{\rm diag}}\nolimits(I^{n}):=\{(x,\dots,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid x\in I\}

and, for a function

, its

\displaystyle F^{\Delta}(x):=F(x,\dots,x)\qquad(x\in I).

Given two functions

Given

\displaystyle p_{0}:=p_{1}+\dots+p_{n},\qquad q_{0}:=q_{1}+\dots+q_{n},\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad r_{0}:=\frac{q_{0}}{p_{0}}.

In the next result we point out that the local equality of the
n
-variable Bajraktarević means implies the local equality of the
k
-variable descendant Bajraktarević means for
k\leq n
.

Theorem 9
.

Let
f,g:I\to\mathbb{R}
be strictly monotone and
p=(p_{1},\dots,p_{n})
,
q=(q_{1},\dots,q_{n}):I\to\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}
. Assume that the
n
-variable
A_{f,p}
and
A_{g,q}
are locally (globally) equal on
I^{n}
. Then, for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
i_{1},\dots,i_{k}\in\{1,\dots,n\}
with
i_{1}<\dots<i_{k}
, we have that the
k
-variable means
A_{f,(p_{i_{1}},\dots,p_{i_{k}})}
and
A_{q,(q_{i_{1}},\dots,q_{i_{k}})}
are locally (globally) equal on
I^{k}
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that
f
and
g
are strictly increasing functions. Then
f^{(-1)}
and
g^{(-1)}
are continuous increasing functions.

According to the local equality of
A_{f,p}
and
A_{g,q}
on
I^{n}
, there exists an open set
U\subseteq I^{n}
containing the diagonal of
I^{n}
such that, for all
x\in U
, we have
A_{f,p}(x)=A_{g,q}(x)
.

In what follows, for all
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, let
e_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
denote the
i
th vector of the standard base of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, i.e., let
e_{i}:=(\delta_{i,j})_{j=1}^{n}
, where
\delta_{i,j}
stands for the Kronecker symbol.

Let us now fix
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
i_{1},\dots,i_{k}\in\{1,\dots,n\}
with
i_{1}<\dots<i_{k}
and define the set
V\subseteq I^{k}
by

\displaystyle V:=\bigg\{(y_{1},\dots,y_{k})\in I^{k}\,\bigg|\,\forall\,t\in\!\Big[\min_{1\leq\alpha\leq k}y_{\alpha},\min_{1\leq\alpha\leq k}y_{\alpha}\Big]\colon\,\sum_{j=1}^{n}\Big[t+\sum_{\alpha=1}^{k}\delta_{j,i_{\alpha}}(y_{\alpha}-t)\Big]e_{j}\in U\bigg\}.

The interior of
V
(denoted as
V^{\circ}
) is an open subset of
I^{k}
. To see that
V^{\circ}
contains the diagonal of
I^{k}
, let
y\in I
be fixed. Then the vector

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\big[t+\delta_{j,i_{1}}(y_{1}-t)+\dots+\delta_{j,i_{k}}(y_{k}-t)\big]e_{j}

depends continuously on
(y_{1},\dots,y_{k},t)
and it
tends to the diagonal vector
(y,\dots,y)\in U\subseteq I^{n}
if the variables
y_{1},\dots,y_{k}
, and
t
tend to
y
. In other words, due to the openness of
U
, there exists a positive number
r
such that

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\big[t+\delta_{j,i_{1}}(y_{1}-t)+\dots+\delta_{j,i_{k}}(y_{k}-t)\big]e_{j}\in U\qquad\mbox{for all}\qquad(y_{1},\dots,y_{k},t)\in(y-r,y+r)^{k+1}.

In particular, this inclusion holds if
y-r<\min(y_{1},\dots,y_{k})\leq t\leq\max(y_{1},\dots,y_{k})<y+r
, hence, in this case
(y_{1},\dots,y_{k})\in V
. This shows that
(y,\dots,y)\in I^{k}
is an interior point of
V
and hence
V^{\circ}
is an open subset of
I^{k}
which contains the diagonal of
I^{k}
. In addition, if
A_{f,p}
and
A_{g,q}
are globally equal on
I^{n}
, then
U=I^{n}
and
V=V^{\circ}=I^{k}
.

We are now going to show that the means
A_{f,(p_{i_{1}},\dots,p_{i_{k}})}
and
A_{q,(q_{i_{1}},\dots,q_{i_{k}})}
are equal on
V^{\circ}
. To the contrary, assume that, for some
(y_{1},\dots,y_{k})\in V^{\circ}
, we have

\displaystyle f^{(-1)}\bigg(\frac{p_{i_{1}}(y_{1})f(y_{1})+\dots+p_{i_{k}}(y_{k})f(y_{k})}{p_{i_{1}}(y_{1})+\dots+p_{i_{k}}(y_{k})}\bigg)\neq g^{(-1)}\bigg(\frac{q_{i_{1}}(y_{1})g(y_{1})+\dots+q_{i_{k}}(y_{k})g(y_{k})}{q_{i_{1}}(y_{1})+\dots+q_{i_{k}}(y_{k})}\bigg).

We may assume that

\displaystyle f^{(-1)}\bigg(\frac{p_{i_{1}}(y_{1})f(y_{1})+\dots+p_{i_{k}}(y_{k})f(y_{k})}{p_{i_{1}}(y_{1})+\dots+p_{i_{k}}(y_{k})}\bigg)<g^{(-1)}\bigg(\frac{q_{i_{1}}(y_{1})g(y_{1})+\dots+q_{i_{k}}(y_{k})g(y_{k})}{q_{i_{1}}(y_{1})+\dots+q_{i_{k}}(y_{k})}\bigg).

The set of points where
f
or
g
are discontinuous is countable subset of
I
, therefore, there exists a point
t\in I
, where
f
and
g
are continuous and

\displaystyle f^{(-1)}\bigg(\frac{p_{i_{1}}(y_{1})f(y_{1})+\dots+p_{i_{k}}(y_{k})f(y_{k})}{p_{i_{1}}(y_{1})+\dots+p_{i_{k}}(y_{k})}\bigg)<t<g^{(-1)}\bigg(\frac{q_{i_{1}}(y_{1})g(y_{1})+\dots+q_{i_{k}}(y_{k})g(y_{k})}{q_{i_{1}}(y_{1})+\dots+q_{i_{k}}(y_{k})}\bigg).

(10)

Due to the mean value property of the means (established in Theorem
2
), it follows that

\displaystyle\min(y_{1},\dots,y_{k})<t<\min(y_{1},\dots,y_{k}),

therefore,

\displaystyle x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n}):=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\Big[t+\sum_{\alpha=1}^{k}\delta_{j,i_{\alpha}}(y_{\alpha}-t)\Big]e_{j}\in U,

and hence
A_{f,p}(x)=A_{g,q}(x)
. On the other hand, the inequalities in (
10
) imply that

\displaystyle\frac{p_{i_{1}}(y_{1})f(y_{1})+\dots+p_{i_{k}}(y_{k})f(y_{k})}{p_{i_{1}}(y_{1})+\dots+p_{i_{k}}(y_{k})}<f(t),\qquad g(t)<\frac{q_{i_{1}}(y_{1})g(y_{1})+\dots+q_{i_{k}}(y_{k})g(y_{k})}{q_{i_{1}}(y_{1})+\dots+q_{i_{k}}(y_{k})}.

Rearranging these inequalities, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha=1}^{k}p_{i_{\alpha}}(y_{\alpha})(f(y_{\alpha})-f(t))<0,\qquad\sum_{\alpha=1}^{k}q_{i_{\alpha}}(y_{\alpha})(g(y_{\alpha})-g(t))>0.

Now taking into account the equalities

\displaystyle x_{j}:=\begin{cases}t&\mbox{if }j\not\in\{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}\},\\[2.84526pt]
y_{\alpha}&\mbox{if }j=i_{\alpha}\quad(\alpha\in\{1,\dots,k\}),\end{cases}

(11)

it follows that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}p_{i}(x_{i})(f(x_{i})-f(t))<0,\qquad\sum_{i=1}^{n}q_{i}(x_{i})(g(x_{i})-g(t))>0.

From these inequalities we obtain that

\displaystyle\frac{p_{1}(x_{1})f(x_{1})+\dots+p_{n}(x_{n})f(x_{n})}{p_{1}(x_{1})+\dots+p_{n}(x_{n})}<f(t),\qquad g(t)<\frac{q_{1}(x_{1})g(x_{1})+\dots+q_{n}(x_{n})g(x_{n})}{q_{1}(x_{1})+\dots+q_{n}(x_{n})}.

Finally, using again that
f
and
g
are continuous at the point
t
, we can conclude that

\displaystyle f^{(-1)}\bigg(\frac{p_{1}(x_{1})f(x_{1})+\dots+p_{n}(x_{n})f(x_{n})}{p_{1}(x_{1})+\dots+p_{n}(x_{n})}\bigg)<t<g^{(-1)}\bigg(\frac{q_{1}(x_{1})g(x_{1})+\dots+q_{n}(x_{n})g(x_{n})}{q_{1}(x_{1})+\dots+q_{n}(x_{n})}\bigg),

which contradicts the equality
A_{f,p}(x)=A_{g,q}(x)
.
∎

3.
Reduction of the equality problem

In our first result, with a certain substitution, we reduce the number of unknown functions in the local (global) equality problem of

Theorem 11
.

Let
f,g:I\to\mathbb{R}
be continuous strictly monotone functions,
n\geq 2
and
p=(p_{1},\dots,p_{n}):I\to\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}
,
q=(q_{1},\dots,q_{n}):I\to\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}
. Then the means

and
A_{g,q}
are locally (globally) equal on

if and only if, the means

A_{\mathop{\text{\rm id}}\nolimits,P}
and
A_{h,Q}
are locally (globally) equal on

, where the interval

, the functions

, and

are defined by

\displaystyle J:=f(I),\qquad h:=g\circ f^{-1},\qquad P:=p\circ f^{-1},\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad Q:=q\circ f^{-1}.

(12)

Proof.

Assume that the means
A_{f,p}
and
A_{g,q}
are locally equal on

. Then the equality

\displaystyle f^{-1}\bigg(\frac{p_{1}(x_{1})f(x_{1})+\dots+p_{n}(x_{n})f(x_{n})}{p_{1}(x_{1})+\dots+p_{n}(x_{n})}\bigg)=g^{-1}\bigg(\frac{q_{1}(x_{1})g(x_{1})+\dots+q_{n}(x_{n})g(x_{n})}{q_{1}(x_{1})+\dots+q_{n}(x_{n})}\bigg)

(13)

is satisfied for all
(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in V
, where
V
is an open subset of
I^{n}
containing its diagonal. Consider the map
F:I^{n}\to J^{n}
defined by
F(x_{1},\dots.x_{n}):=(f(x_{1}),\dots,f(x_{n})
. Then
F
is a continuous bijection between
I^{n}
and
J^{n}
whose inverse is also continuous. Therefore,

U:=F(V)
is an open subset of
J^{n}
containing its diagonal. For
(u_{1},\dots,u_{n})\in U
we have that
F^{-1}(u_{1},\dots,u_{n})=(f^{-1}(u_{1}),\dots,f^{-1}(u_{1}))\in V
, therefore, applying (
13
) with

(x_{1},\dots,x_{n}):=(f^{-1}(u_{1}),\dots,f^{-1}(u_{1}))
, we get that

\displaystyle\frac{(p_{1}\circ f^{-1})(u_{1})u_{1}+\dots+(p_{n}\circ f^{-1})(u_{n})u_{n}}{(p_{1}\circ f^{-1})(u_{1})+\dots+(p_{n}\circ f^{-1})(u_{n})}

\displaystyle=(g\circ f^{-1})^{-1}\bigg(\frac{(q_{1}\circ f^{-1})(u_{1})(g\circ f^{-1})(u_{1})+\dots+(q_{n}\circ f^{-1})(u_{n})(g\circ f^{-1})(u_{n})}{(q_{1}\circ f^{-1})(u_{1})+\dots+(q_{n}\circ f^{-1})(u_{n})}\bigg),

holds for all
(u_{1},\dots,u_{n})\in U
.
With the notations introduced in (
12
), the above equality can be rewritten, for all
(u_{1},\dots,u_{n})\in U
, as

\displaystyle\frac{P_{1}(u_{1})u_{1}+\dots+P_{n}(u_{n})u_{n}}{P_{1}(u_{1})+\dots+P_{n}(u_{n})}=h^{-1}\bigg(\frac{Q_{1}(u_{1})h(u_{1})+\dots+Q_{n}(u_{n})h(u_{n})}{Q_{1}(u_{1})+\dots+Q_{n}(u_{n})}\bigg),

(14)

which shows that the means
A_{\mathop{\text{\rm id}}\nolimits,P}
and
A_{h,Q}
are locally equal on
J^{n}
. If, in addition, the means
A_{f,p}
and
A_{g,q}
are globally equal on
I^{n}
, i.e., the equality (
13
) holds on
V=I^{n}
, then
U=J^{n}
, which shows that the means
A_{\mathop{\text{\rm id}}\nolimits,P}
and
A_{h,Q}
are globally equal on
J^{n}
.

The reversed implication can checked similarly, therefore its proof is omitted.
∎

In what follows, we deduce the first-order necessary condition for the equality of
n
-variable generalized Bajraktarević means.

Theorem 12
.

Let
h:J\to\mathbb{R}
a differentiable function with a nonvanishing first derivative, let
n\geq 2
, let
P,Q:J\to\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}
, let
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
and assume that
P_{i}
is continuous on
J
. Assume that the means
A_{\mathop{\text{\rm id}}\nolimits,P}
and
A_{h,Q}
are locally equal on
J^{n}
. Then

\displaystyle\frac{P_{i}}{P_{0}}=\frac{Q_{i}}{Q_{0}},

(15)

where

\displaystyle P_{0}:=P_{1}+\dots+P_{n}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad Q_{0}:=Q_{1}+\dots+Q_{n}.

(16)

Proof.

According to Lemma
7
, the continuity of
P_{i}
implies the continuity of
Q_{i}
. On the other hand, by Theorem
5
, it follows that first-order partial derivatives

\partial_{i}A_{\mathop{\text{\rm id}}\nolimits,P}
and
\partial_{i}A_{h,Q}

exist at the diagonal points of
J^{n}
, and due to the local validity of the equality
A_{\mathop{\text{\rm id}}\nolimits,P}=A_{h,Q}
, they are equal to each other, i.e., (
15
) holds.
∎

Without assuming the differentiability of
h
, for fixed
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
,
u\in J
, we have that

\displaystyle\frac{P_{i}(u)}{P_{0}(u)}=(\partial_{i}A_{h,Q})^{\Delta}(u)=\lim_{v\to u}\frac{1}{v-u}\bigg(h^{-1}\bigg(\frac{(h\cdot Q_{0})(u)-(h\cdot Q_{i})(u)+(h\cdot Q_{i})(v)}{Q_{0}(u)-Q_{i}(u)+Q_{i}(v)}\bigg)-u\bigg).

Could one get the differentiability of
h
from here at a.e. point with a nonvanishing derivative?

Corollary 13
.

Let
h:J\to\mathbb{R}
a differentiable function with a nonvanishing first derivative, let
n\geq 2
, let
P,Q:J\to\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}
and assume that
P
is continuous on
J
. Assume that the means
A_{\mathop{\text{\rm id}}\nolimits,P}
and
A_{h,Q}
are locally equal on
J^{n}
. Then
Q
is also continuous on
J
and there exists a continuous function
r:J\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
such that

\displaystyle Q=rP.

(17)

Furthermore, the equality

\displaystyle h\bigg(\frac{P_{1}(u_{1})u_{1}+\dots+P_{n}(u_{n})u_{n}}{P_{1}(u_{1})+\dots+P_{n}(u_{n})}\bigg)=\frac{(rhP_{1})(u_{1})+\dots+(rhP_{n})(u_{n})}{(rP_{1})(u_{1})+\dots+(rP_{n})(u_{n})}.

(18)

holds locally on
J^{n}
with respect to
u=(u_{1},\dots,u_{n})\in J^{n}
.

Proof.

Define the functions
P_{0}
and
Q_{0}
by (
16
) and let
r:=Q_{0}/P_{0}
. Then, according to Theorem
12
, for all
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, the equality (
15
) holds and
Q_{i}
is continuous. Therefore the equation (
17
) is valid and
r
is also continuous.

Assume that the means
A_{\mathop{\text{\rm id}}\nolimits,P}
and
A_{h,Q}
are equal over an open subset
U\subseteq J^{n}
which contains the diagonal of
J^{n}
. Then (
14
) is valid for all
u=(u_{1},\dots,u_{n})\in U
. Replacing
Q_{i}(u_{i})
by
r(u_{i})P_{i}(u_{i})
and applying the function
h
to this equality side by side, we can conclude that (
18
) is valid for all
u=(u_{1},\dots,u_{n})\in U
, i.e., it holds locally on
J^{n}
.
∎

First we establish a sufficient condition for the equality (
18
).

Theorem 14
.

If there exist four real constants
a,b,c,d
such that, for all
u\in J
,

\displaystyle r(u)=cu+d>0,\qquad h(u)=\frac{au+b}{cu+d}

(19)

and
P:J\to\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}
is arbitrary, then
(
18
)
holds for all
u=(u_{1},\dots,u_{n})\in J^{n}
.

Proof.

Let
u=(u_{1},\dots,u_{n})\in J^{n}
. Then

\displaystyle h\bigg(\frac{P_{1}(u_{1})u_{1}+\dots+P_{n}(u_{n})u_{n}}{P_{1}(u_{1})+\dots+P_{n}(u_{n})}\bigg)=\frac{a\dfrac{P_{1}(u_{1})u_{1}+\dots+P_{n}(u_{n})u_{n}}{P_{1}(u_{1})+\dots+P_{n}(u_{n})}+b}{c\dfrac{P_{1}(u_{1})u_{1}+\dots+P_{n}(u_{n})u_{n}}{P_{1}(u_{1})+\dots+P_{n}(u_{n})}+d}

\displaystyle=\frac{a(P_{1}(u_{1})u_{1}+\dots+P_{n}(u_{n})u_{n})+b(P_{1}(u_{1})+\dots+P_{n}(u_{n}))}{c(P_{1}(u_{1})u_{1}+\dots+P_{n}(u_{n})u_{n})+d(P_{1}(u_{1})+\dots+P_{n}(u_{n}))}

\displaystyle=\frac{(au_{1}+b)P_{1}(u_{1})+\dots+(au_{n}+b)P_{n}(u_{n})}{(cu_{1}+d)P_{1}(u_{1})+\dots+(cu_{n}+d)P_{n}(u_{n})}=\frac{(rhP_{1})(u_{1})+\dots+(rhP_{n})(u_{n})}{(rP_{1})(u_{1})+\dots+(rP_{n})(u_{n})}.

∎

The first main goal of this paper is to show that if the equality (
18
) holds locally in
J^{n}
, then, under some regularity assumptions, the functions
r
and
h
are of the form (
19
) for some real constants
a,b,c,d
. To accomplish this goal, we are going to show that
r
and
h
are twice differentiable functions and

\displaystyle r^{\prime\prime}=0\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad(rh)^{\prime\prime}=0.

The proof of this property will be split in several propositions.

Proposition 15
.

Let
h:J\to\mathbb{R}
be continuously differentiable with a nonvanishing first derivative and let
r:J\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
. Let
P:J\to\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}
,
i,j\in\{1,\dots,n\}
,
i\neq j
and suppose that
P_{i}
and
P_{j}
are continuously differentiable. Assume that
(
18
)
holds locally with respect to
(u_{1},\dots,u_{n})\in J^{n}
. Then
r
is continuously differentiable and

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\Big(P_{i}(u)\cdot(P_{i}(u)+P_{j}(v))+P_{i}^{\prime}(u)\cdot P_{j}(v)\cdot(u-v)\Big)

(20)

\displaystyle\times\Big((h^{\prime}rP_{j})(v)\cdot((rP_{i})(u)+(rP_{j})(v))+(rP_{j})^{\prime}(v)\cdot(rP_{i})(u)\cdot(h(v)-h(u))\Big)

\displaystyle=\Big(P_{j}(v)\cdot(P_{i}(u)+P_{j}(v))+P_{j}^{\prime}(v)\cdot P_{i}(u)\cdot(v-u)\Big)

\displaystyle\times\Big((h^{\prime}rP_{i})(u)\cdot((rP_{i})(u)+(rP_{j})(v))+(rP_{i})^{\prime}(u)\cdot(rP_{j})(v)\cdot(h(u)-h(v))\Big)

is valid locally for
(u,v)\in J^{2}
.

Proof.

The local validity of the equality (
18
) implies that the
n
-variable means
A_{\mathop{\text{\rm id}}\nolimits,P}
and
A_{h,rP}
are locally equal on
J^{n}
. According to Corollary
10
, it follows that two-variable means
A_{\mathop{\text{\rm id}}\nolimits,(P_{i},P_{j})}
and
A_{h,(rP_{i},rP_{j})}
are locally equal on
J^{2}
.
In view of assertion (ii) of Lemma
7
, the continuous differentiability of
P_{i}
implies the continuous differentiability of
rP_{i}
. Therefore, the function
r
must be continuously differentiable. By local equality of these two-variable means, there exists an open subset
V\subseteq J^{2}
containing the diagonal of
J^{2}
such that, for all
(u,v)\in V
, we have

\displaystyle h\bigg(\frac{P_{i}(u)u+P_{j}(v)v}{P_{i}(u)+P_{j}(v)}\bigg)=\frac{(rhP_{i})(u)+(rhP_{j})(v)}{(rP_{i})(u)+(rP_{j})(v)}.

Computing the partial derivatives of this equation side by side with respect to
u
and
v
, for all
(u,v)\in V
, we get

\displaystyle h^{\prime}\bigg(\frac{P_{i}(u)u+P_{j}(v)v}{P_{i}(u)+P_{j}(v)}\bigg)\times\frac{P_{i}(u)\cdot(P_{i}(u)+P_{j}(v))+P_{j}(v)\cdot P_{i}^{\prime}(u)\cdot(u-v)}{(P_{i}(u)+P_{j}(v))^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(h^{\prime}rP_{i})(u)\cdot((rP_{i})(u)+(rP_{j})(v))+(rP_{i})^{\prime}(u)\cdot(rP_{j})(v)\cdot(h(u)-h(v))}{((rP_{i})(u)+(rP_{j})(v))^{2}}

and

\displaystyle h^{\prime}\bigg(\frac{P_{i}(u)u+P_{j}(v)v}{P_{i}(u)+P_{j}(v)}\bigg)\times\frac{P_{j}(v)\cdot(P_{i}(u)+P_{j}(v))+P_{i}(u)\cdot P_{j}^{\prime}(v)\cdot(v-u)}{(P_{i}(u)+P_{j}(v))^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(h^{\prime}rP_{j})(v)\cdot((rP_{i})(u)+(rP_{j})(v))+(rP_{j})^{\prime}(v)\cdot(rP_{i})(u)\cdot(h(v)-h(u))}{((rP_{i})(u)+(rP_{j})(v))^{2}}.

Multiplying the left and right hand sides of the first equality by the right and left hand sides of the second one, after cancellations, the stated equality (
20
) follows.
∎

Proposition 16
.

Let
h:J\to\mathbb{R}
be continuously differentiable with a nonvanishing first derivative and let
r,P_{i},P_{j}:J\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
be continuously differentiable (where
i,j\in\{1,\dots,n\}
and
i\neq j
). Assume that
(
20
)
holds locally with respect to
(u,v)\in J^{2}
. Then
h^{\prime}r^{2}
is a constant function on
J
, hence
h
is twice continuously differentiable on
J
, and the equality

\displaystyle\frac{r(v)-r(u)}{r(u)r(v)(u-v)}=

\displaystyle\frac{(rP_{j}^{\prime})(v)\cdot(P_{i}^{2})(u)+(rP_{i}^{\prime})(u)\cdot(P_{j}^{2})(v)}{(rP_{i})(u)\cdot(rP_{j})(v)\cdot(P_{i}(u)+P_{j}(v))}

(21)

\displaystyle-\frac{(rP_{i})^{\prime}(u)\cdot(rP_{j}^{2})(v)+(rP_{j})^{\prime}(v)\cdot(rP_{i}^{2})(u)}{P_{i}(u)\cdot P_{j}(v)\cdot((rP_{i})(u)+(rP_{j})(v))}\cdot\frac{1}{u-v}\int_{v}^{u}\frac{1}{r^{2}}

\displaystyle+\frac{(rP_{j}^{\prime})(v)\cdot(r^{\prime}P_{i})(u)-(rP_{i}^{\prime})(u)\cdot(r^{\prime}P_{j})(v)}{(P_{i}(u)+P_{j}(v))\cdot((rP_{i})(u)+(rP_{j})(v))}\cdot\int_{v}^{u}\frac{1}{r^{2}}

is valid locally for
(u,v)\in J^{2}
.

Proof.

Let
V\subseteq J^{2}
be an open set containing the diagonal of
J^{2}
where (
20
) holds. Then, rearranging this equality, for all
(u,v)\in V
, we obtain

\displaystyle 0=

\displaystyle P_{i}(u)\cdot P_{j}(v)\cdot(P_{i}(u)+P_{j}(v))\cdot((rP_{i})(u)+(rP_{j})(v))\cdot((h^{\prime}r)(u)-(h^{\prime}r)(v))

\displaystyle+(P_{j}^{\prime}(v)\cdot(h^{\prime}rP_{i}^{2})(u)+P_{i}^{\prime}(u)\cdot(h^{\prime}rP_{j}^{2})(v))\cdot((rP_{i})(u)+(rP_{j})(v))\cdot(v-u)

\displaystyle+((rP_{i})^{\prime}(u)\cdot(rP_{j}^{2})(v)+(rP_{j})^{\prime}(v)\cdot(rP_{i}^{2})(u))\cdot(P_{i}(u)+P_{j}(v))\cdot(h(u)-h(v))

\displaystyle+P_{i}(u)\cdot P_{j}(v)\cdot((rP_{j}^{\prime})(v)\cdot(r^{\prime}P_{i})(u)-(rP_{i}^{\prime})(u)\cdot(r^{\prime}P_{j})(v))\cdot(v-u)\cdot(h(u)-h(v)).

Dividing both sides of this equation by
P_{i}(u)\cdot P_{j}(v)\cdot(P_{i}(u)+P_{j}(v))\cdot((rP_{i})(u)+(rP_{j})(v))\cdot(u-v)
, we get

\displaystyle\frac{(h^{\prime}r)(u)-(h^{\prime}r)(v)}{u-v}=

\displaystyle\frac{P_{j}^{\prime}(v)\cdot(h^{\prime}rP_{i}^{2})(u)+P_{i}^{\prime}(u)\cdot(h^{\prime}rP_{j}^{2})(v)}{P_{i}(u)\cdot P_{j}(v)\cdot(P_{i}(u)+P_{j}(v))}

(22)

\displaystyle-\frac{(rP_{i})^{\prime}(u)\cdot(rP_{j}^{2})(v)+(rP_{j})^{\prime}(v)\cdot(rP_{i}^{2})(u)}{P_{i}(u)\cdot P_{j}(v)\cdot((rP_{i})(u)+(rP_{j})(v))}\cdot\frac{h(u)-h(v)}{u-v}

\displaystyle+\frac{(rP_{j}^{\prime})(v)\cdot(r^{\prime}P_{i})(u)-(rP_{i}^{\prime})(u)\cdot(r^{\prime}P_{j})(v)}{(P_{i}(u)+P_{j}(v))\cdot((rP_{i})(u)+(rP_{j})(v))}\cdot(h(u)-h(v)).

Let
u\in J
be fixed. Observe that the limit of the right hand side as
v\to u
exists. (Here we use that
V
is open and contains the point
(u,u)
.) This shows that
h^{\prime}r
is differentiable and hence
h^{\prime}
is differentiable, too. This proves that
h
is twice differentiable. Upon taking the limit as
v\to u
, we get that the equality

\displaystyle h^{\prime\prime}r+h^{\prime}r^{\prime}=(h^{\prime}r)^{\prime}=

\displaystyle h^{\prime}r\cdot\frac{P_{j}^{\prime}P_{i}^{2}+P_{i}^{\prime}P_{j}^{2}}{P_{i}P_{j}(P_{i}+P_{j})}-h^{\prime}\cdot\frac{(rP_{i})^{\prime}P_{j}^{2}+(rP_{j})^{\prime}P_{i}^{2}}{P_{i}P_{j}(P_{i}+P_{j})}=-h^{\prime}r^{\prime}

is valid over
J
.
Therefore,

\displaystyle h^{\prime\prime}r+2h^{\prime}r^{\prime}=0,

which implies that
h^{\prime}r^{2}
is a constant function on
J
. Using that
r
is continuously differentiable, it follows that
h^{\prime}
is also continuously differentiable and hence
h
is twice continuously differentiable.

Denote
\gamma:=h^{\prime}r^{2}\neq 0
. Then, we get that
h^{\prime}=\gamma/r^{2}
, thus the equality (
22
) can be rewritten as (
21
) for all
(u,v)\in V
.
∎

In what follows, we are going to prove that, for
w\in I
, the
symmetric derivative of
r^{\prime}
at
w
, i.e., the limit

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{r^{\prime}(w+t)-r^{\prime}(w-t)}{2t}

exists and equals

. Then, using the quasi-mean value theorem of Aull
[
2
, Theorem 1]
(see also
[
19
, Theorem 6.3]
) from the theory of symmetric derivatives, it will follow that

is a constant function.

Proposition 17
.

Let
r,P_{i},P_{j}:J\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
be continuously differentiable (where
i,j\in\{1,\dots,n\}
and
i\neq j
) such that
P_{i}(u)\neq P_{j}(u)
for all
u\in J
. Assume that
(
21
)
holds locally with respect to
(u,v)\in J^{2}
. Then
r
is an affine function on
J
, i.e., there exists real constants
c,d
such that
r(u)=cu+d
holds for all
u\in J
.

Proof.

Let
V\subseteq J^{2}
be an open set containing the diagonal of
J^{2}
where (
21
) holds. Multiplying this equality by
((rP_{i})(u)+(rP_{j})(v))
side by side and then rearranging it, for all
(u,v)\in V
, we arrive at the following equation

\displaystyle\frac{P_{i}(u)}{r(v)}

\displaystyle\bigg(\frac{r(u)-r(v)}{u-v}-r^{\prime}(v)\bigg)+\frac{P_{j}(v)}{r(u)}\bigg(\frac{r(u)-r(v)}{u-v}-r^{\prime}(u)\bigg)

(23)

\displaystyle=\bigg(\frac{P_{i}(u)r^{\prime}(v)}{r(v)}+\frac{P_{j}(v)r^{\prime}(u)}{r(u)}+\frac{P_{i}^{\prime}(u)P_{j}^{3}(v)+P_{j}^{\prime}(v)P_{i}^{3}(u)}{(P_{i}(u)+P_{j}(v))P_{i}(u)P_{j}(v)}\bigg)\bigg(\frac{r(u)r(v)}{u-v}\int_{v}^{u}\frac{1}{r^{2}}-1\bigg)

\displaystyle+\frac{P_{i}^{\prime}(u)P_{j}(v)r(u)}{(P_{i}(u)+P_{j}(v))r(v)}\bigg(\frac{(r^{\prime}(v)(u-v)+r(v))r(v)}{u-v}\int_{v}^{u}\frac{1}{r^{2}}-1\bigg)

\displaystyle+\frac{P_{j}^{\prime}(v)P_{i}(u)r(v)}{(P_{i}(u)+P_{j}(v))r(u)}\bigg(\frac{(r^{\prime}(u)(v-u)+r(u))r(u)}{u-v}\int_{v}^{u}\frac{1}{r^{2}}-1\bigg).

Let
w\in J
be fixed and substitute
u:=w+t
and
v:=w-t
into (
23
). First, compute the limit of both sides of this equality as
t\to 0
. By the continuity of the function
r
, we have that

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{1}{2t}\int_{w-t}^{w+t}\frac{1}{r^{2}}=\frac{1}{r(w)^{2}},

(24)

hence both sides of the equality (
23
) tend to zero as
t\to 0
. In what follows, we show that right hand side of the equation (
23
) multiplied by
\frac{1}{2t}
tends to zero as
t\to 0
. For this purpose, we compute the following three limits using (
24
), the equality

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\bigg(\int_{w-t}^{w+t}\frac{1}{r^{2}}\bigg)=\frac{d}{dt}\bigg(\int_{w}^{w+t}\frac{1}{r^{2}}-\int_{w}^{w-t}\frac{1}{r^{2}}\bigg)=\frac{1}{r(w+t)^{2}}+\frac{1}{r(w-t)^{2}},

and L’Hospital’s Rule:

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to 0}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2t}\bigg(\frac{r(w+t)r(w-t)}{2t}\int_{w-t}^{w+t}\frac{1}{r^{2}}-1\bigg)=\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{1}{4t^{2}}\bigg(r(w+t)r(w-t)\int_{w-t}^{w+t}\frac{1}{r^{2}}-2t\bigg)

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{1}{8t}\bigg(\big(r^{\prime}(w+t)r(w-t)-r(w+t)r^{\prime}(w-t)\big)\int_{w-t}^{w+t}\frac{1}{r^{2}}

\displaystyle+r(w+t)r(w-t)\bigg(\frac{1}{r(w+t)^{2}}+\frac{1}{r(w-t)^{2}}\bigg)-2\bigg)

\displaystyle=\frac{r^{\prime}(w)}{4r(w)}-\frac{r^{\prime}(w)}{4r(w)}+\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{1}{8t}\bigg(\frac{r(w-t)}{r(w+t)}+\frac{r(w+t)}{r(w-t)}-2\bigg)

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{(r(w+t)-r(w-t))^{2}}{8tr(w+t)r(w-t)}=\frac{1}{r^{2}(w)}\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{(r(w+t)-r(w-t))^{2}}{8t}=0.

In a similar fashion, we also get

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{1}{2t}\bigg(\frac{(-2tr^{\prime}(w+t)+r(w+t))r(w+t)}{2t}\int_{w-t}^{w+t}\frac{1}{r^{2}}-1\bigg)

\displaystyle=-\lim_{t\to 0}\bigg(\frac{r^{\prime}(w+t)r(w+t)}{2t}\int_{w-t}^{w+t}\frac{1}{r^{2}}\bigg)+\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{1}{4t^{2}}\bigg(r(w+t)^{2}\int_{w-t}^{w+t}\frac{1}{r^{2}}-2t\bigg)

\displaystyle=-\frac{r^{\prime}(w)}{r(w)}+\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{1}{8t}\bigg(2r^{\prime}(w+t)r(w+t)\int_{w-t}^{w+t}\frac{1}{r^{2}}+r(w+t)^{2}\bigg(\frac{1}{r(w+t)^{2}}+\frac{1}{r(w-t)^{2}}\bigg)-2\bigg)

\displaystyle=-\frac{r^{\prime}(w)}{r(w)}+\frac{r^{\prime}(w)}{2r(w)}+\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{r(w+t)-r(w-t)}{2t}\cdot\frac{r(w+t)+r(w-t)}{4r(w-t)^{2}}=-\frac{r^{\prime}(w)}{2r(w)}+\frac{r^{\prime}(w)}{2r(w)}=0

and

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{1}{2t}\bigg(\frac{(2tr^{\prime}(w-t)+r(w-t))r(w-t)}{2t}\int_{w-t}^{w+t}\frac{1}{r^{2}}-1\bigg)

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\to 0}\bigg(\frac{r^{\prime}(w-t)r(w-t)}{2t}\int_{w-t}^{w+t}\frac{1}{r^{2}}\bigg)+\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{1}{4t^{2}}\bigg(r(w-t)^{2}\int_{w-t}^{w+t}\frac{1}{r^{2}}-2t\bigg)

\displaystyle=\frac{r^{\prime}(w)}{r(w)}+\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{1}{8t}\bigg(-2r^{\prime}(w-t)r(w-t)\int_{w-t}^{w+t}\frac{1}{r^{2}}+r(w-t)^{2}\bigg(\frac{1}{r(w+t)^{2}}+\frac{1}{r(w-t)^{2}}\bigg)-2\bigg)

\displaystyle=\frac{r^{\prime}(w)}{r(w)}-\frac{r^{\prime}(w)}{2r(w)}+\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{1}{8t}\frac{r(w-t)^{2}-r(w+t)^{2}}{r(w+t)^{2}}=\frac{r^{\prime}(w)}{r(w)}-\frac{r^{\prime}(w)}{2r(w)}-\frac{r^{\prime}(w)}{2r(w)}=0.

Using the above three equalities and the continuity of
P_{i},P_{i}^{\prime}
and
r
, it follows that the right hand side of the equation (
23
) at
u=w+t
and
v:=w-t
multiplied by
\frac{1}{2t}
tends to zero as
t\to 0
. Therefore, the same property is valid for the left hand side of (

,

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\to 0}\bigg(\frac{P_{i}(w+t)}{r(w-t)}\bigg(\frac{r(w+t)-r(w-t)}{4t^{2}}-\frac{r^{\prime}(w-t)}{2t}\bigg)

\displaystyle+\frac{P_{j}(w-t)}{r(w+t)}\bigg(\frac{r(w+t)-r(w-t)}{4t^{2}}-\frac{r^{\prime}(w+t)}{2t}\bigg)\bigg).

Replacing

-t

-1

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\to 0}\bigg(\frac{P_{i}(w-t)}{r(w+t)}\bigg(\frac{r(w+t)-r(w-t)}{4t^{2}}-\frac{r^{\prime}(w+t)}{2t}\bigg)

\displaystyle+\frac{P_{j}(w+t)}{r(w-t)}\bigg(\frac{r(w+t)-r(w-t)}{4t^{2}}-\frac{r^{\prime}(w-t)}{2t}\bigg)\bigg).

These equalities, with obvious manipulations of the limits, imply that

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\to 0}\bigg(\frac{P_{j}(w-t)}{P_{i}(w+t)}\frac{r(w-t)}{r(w+t)}\bigg(\frac{r(w+t)-r(w-t)}{4t^{2}}-\frac{r^{\prime}(w+t)}{2t}\bigg)

\displaystyle+\frac{r(w+t)-r(w-t)}{4t^{2}}-\frac{r^{\prime}(w-t)}{2t}\bigg)

and

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\to 0}\bigg(\frac{P_{i}(w-t)}{P_{j}(w+t)}\frac{r(w-t)}{r(w+t)}\bigg(\frac{r(w+t)-r(w-t)}{4t^{2}}-\frac{r^{\prime}(w+t)}{2t}\bigg)

\displaystyle+\frac{r(w+t)-r(w-t)}{4t^{2}}-\frac{r^{\prime}(w-t)}{2t}\bigg).

Subtracting the two equalities side by side, we arrive at

\displaystyle 0=\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{r(w-t)}{r(w+t)}\bigg(\frac{P_{j}(w-t)}{P_{i}(w+t)}-\frac{P_{i}(w-t)}{P_{j}(w+t)}\bigg)\bigg(\frac{r(w+t)-r(w-t)}{4t^{2}}-\frac{r^{\prime}(w+t)}{2t}\bigg),

\displaystyle 0=\lim_{t\to 0}\big(P_{j}(w+t)P_{j}(w-t)-P_{i}(w+t)P_{i}(w-t)\big)\bigg(\frac{r(w+t)-r(w-t)}{4t^{2}}-\frac{r^{\prime}(w+t)}{2t}\bigg).

Replacing
t
by
-t
and then multiplying the equality so obtained by
-1
, we also obtain that

\displaystyle 0=\lim_{t\to 0}\big(P_{j}(w+t)P_{j}(w-t)-P_{i}(w+t)P_{i}(w-t)\big)\bigg(\frac{r(w+t)-r(w-t)}{4t^{2}}-\frac{r^{\prime}(w-t)}{2t}\bigg).

Subtracting the second equality from the first one, we conclude that

\displaystyle 0=\lim_{t\to 0}\big(P_{j}(w+t)P_{j}(w-t)-P_{i}(w+t)P_{i}(w-t)\big)\frac{r^{\prime}(w+t)-r^{\prime}(w-t)}{2t}.

therefore, the limit of the second factor in (

Theorem 19

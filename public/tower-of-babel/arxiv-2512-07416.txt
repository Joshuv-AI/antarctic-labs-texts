Harmonic Geometric Polynomials via Geometric Polynomials and Their Applications
Pınar Akkanat, Levent Kargın

Abstract
The aim of this study is to show that harmonic geometric polynomials can be represented in terms of geometric polynomials. This problem was first considered by Keller [ 14 ] ; however, the corresponding coefficients were not fully determined. In the present work, we provide several explicit representations of harmonic geometric polynomials in terms of geometric polynomials. Moreover, several applications of one of these representations are subsequently developed. In particular, we obtain a generalization of the classical identity for the harmonic numbers, compute an integral involving harmonic geometric polynomials and an integral involving products of harmonic geometric and geometric polynomials in terms of Bernoulli numbers. These integral formulas lead to new explicit expressions for Bernoulli numbers. In addition, we give several recurrence relations for harmonic geometric polynomials and evaluate a finite sum involving harmonic numbers and positive powers of integers. MSC 2010: 11B68, 11B83, 33C47. Keywords: Bernoulli numbers, Harmonic numbers, Harmonic geometric polynomials, Geometric polynomials, Semiorthogonal polynomials.

2
Harmonic Geometric Polynomials via Geometric Polynomials

In this section, two representations of HGP in terms of GP are presented.
First one is as follows:

Theorem 1

For positive integer
n,
we have

{}_{H}{w}_{n}(x)=\sum_{l=0}^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\frac{\left(-1\right)^{k+1}}{k}\binom{n}{l}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{n-l}{k}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{k+1}{j+1}{w}_{l+j}(x)\left(\frac{x}{1+x}\right)^{k}.

Proof.
From (
10
), writing derivatives of GP in terms of geometric polynomials
gives that HGP can be written in terms of GP. From (
4
), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{d^{k}}{dx^{k}}w_{n}\left(x\right)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{d^{k}}{dx^{k}}\left(e^{t}-1\right)^{n}x^{n}

\displaystyle=\left(k!\right)^{2}\frac{\left(e^{t}-1\right)^{k}}{k!}\left(\frac{1}{1-x\left(e^{t}-1\right)}\right)^{k+1}

\displaystyle=\left(k!\right)^{2}\left(\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{m}{k}\frac{t^{m}}{m!}\right)\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}w_{n}^{\left(k+1\right)}\left(x\right)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(k!\right)^{2}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{m=k}^{n}\binom{n}{m}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{m}{k}w_{n-m}^{\left(k+1\right)}\left(x\right)\right)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Comparing the coefficients of
\frac{t^{n}}{n!}
gives

\frac{d^{k}}{dx^{k}}w_{n}\left(x\right)=\left(k!\right)^{2}\sum_{m=k}^{n}\binom{n}{m}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{m}{k}w_{n-m}^{\left(k+1\right)}\left(x\right).

(12)

Moreover using the formula
[
19
]

w_{n}^{\left(p+1\right)}\left(x\right)=\frac{1}{p!\left(1+x\right)^{p}}\sum_{k=0}^{p}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{p+1}{k+1}{w}_{n+k}(x)

(13)

we obtain the stated formula.

Remark 1

Boyadzhiev and Dil
[
6
]
attempted to express the derivatives of
geometric polynomials in terms of geometric polynomials, but were unable to
obtain a general solution and instead listed several special cases. Now, with
the help of (
12
) and (
13
), we obtain the following formula:

\frac{d^{k}}{dx^{k}}w_{n}\left(x\right)=\frac{k!}{\left(1+x\right)^{k}}\sum_{m=k}^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{n}{m}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{m}{k}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{k+1}{j+1}{w}_{n-m+j}(x).

For the second representation, we first obtain the generating function of the
HGP

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{}_{H}{w}_{n}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=-\frac{\ln\left(1-x(e^{t}-1)\right)}{1-x(e^{t}-1)},

(14)

which follows from the generating function of Striling numbers of the second kind

{\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=k}^{\infty}}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{n}{k}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\frac{(e^{t}-1)^{k}}{k!}

(15)

and harmonic numbers

{\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=k}^{\infty}}H_{n}t^{n}=\frac{-\ln\left(1-t\right)}{1-t}.\text{ }

(16)

Theorem 2

For positive integer
n,
we have

\text{ }_{H}w_{m}\left(x\right)=\left(1+x-m\right)w_{m-1}\left(x\right)+\left(1+x\right)\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\binom{m}{k}w_{k-1}\left(x\right)w_{m-k}\left(x\right)

(17)

Proof.
Specifically, by using the series expansion of the logarithm function in the
appropriate region, (
14
) can be rewritten as follows:

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}{}_{H}{w}_{k}(x)\frac{t^{k}}{k!}

\displaystyle=-\ln\left(1-x(e^{t}-1)\right)\frac{1}{1-x(e^{t}-1)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\left(x(e^{t}-1)\right)^{n+1}}{n+1}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}{w}_{k}(x)\frac{t^{k}}{k!}.

Employing (
15
) in the equation above gives

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}{}_{H}{w}_{k}(x)\frac{t^{k}}{k!}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}{w}_{k}(x)\frac{t^{k}}{k!}\right)\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\sum_{n=0}^{j}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{j+1}{n+1}n!x^{n+1}\frac{t^{j+1}}{\left(j+1\right)!}\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}x{w}_{k}(x)\frac{t^{k+1}}{k!}.

With the light of
[
11
]

\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{j+1}{n+1}=\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{j}{n+1}\left(n+1\right)+\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{j}{n},

(18)

one can obtain that

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}{}_{H}{w}_{k}(x)\frac{t^{k}}{k!}

\displaystyle=\left(1+x\right)\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}{w}_{k}(x)\frac{t^{k}}{k!}\right)\left(\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}w_{j}\left(x\right)\frac{t^{j+1}}{\left(j+1\right)!}\right)

\displaystyle-x\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}{w}_{k}(x)\frac{t^{k+1}}{k!}.

Therefore, upon performing the required manipulations and comparing the
coefficients of
\frac{t^{k+1}}{k!}
, we obtain the stated result.

Proposition 1

For

Using (

Lemma 1

Theorem 6

For
\left|x\right|<1,\,_{H}A^{\left(p\right)}\left(m;x\right)
can be written as a linear combination of HGP, GP and
the functions
\ln(1-x)
and
\frac{1}{1-x}
. Namely;

{}_{H}A^{\left(p\right)}\left(m;x\right)=\text{ }_{H}A\left(m;x\right)-\text{ }_{H}A_{p}\left(m;x\right)

where

{}_{H}A_{p}\left(m;x\right):=\sum_{n=p+1}^{\infty}H_{n}n^{m}x^{n}=x^{p}\sum_{k=0}^{m}\binom{m}{k}p^{m-k}\text{ }_{H}A\left(k;x\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad\quad+\sum_{k=0}^{m}\binom{m}{k}A\left(k-1;x\right)A^{\left(p-1\right)}\left(m-k;x\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad-\sum_{k=0}^{m}\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}\binom{m}{k}j^{m-k}A^{\left(p-j\right)}\left(k-1;x\right)x^{j}

(36)

with
A\left(-1;x\right)=-\ln\left(1-x\right)/\left(1-x\right).

Proof.
It is obvious that

\text{ }_{H}A^{\left(p\right)}\left(m;x\right)=\text{ }_{H}A\left(m;x\right)-\sum_{n=p+1}^{\infty}H_{n}n^{m}x^{n}.

Accordingly, we need to evaluate
\sum_{n=p+1}^{\infty}H_{n}n^{m}x^{n}
in a
closed form. With some manipulation, one can get that

\displaystyle\sum_{n=p+1}^{\infty}H_{n}n^{m}x^{n}

\displaystyle=x^{p}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}H_{n+p}\left(n+p\right)^{m}x^{n}

\displaystyle=x^{p}\sum_{k=0}^{m}\binom{m}{k}p^{m-k}\mathcal{H}\left(k;x\right)+x^{p}p^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{p}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n+j}x^{n}

\displaystyle+x^{p}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\binom{m}{k}p^{m-k}\sum_{j=1}^{p}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{n^{k}}{n+j}x^{n}.

It is easy to see that

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{x^{n}}{n+j}=-x^{-j}\frac{\ln\left(1-x\right)}{1-x}-\sum_{n=1}^{j}\frac{1}{n}x^{n-j}.

So we need to evaluate the series
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{n^{k}}{n+j}x^{n}.

It is obvious that

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{n^{k}}{n+j}x^{n}

\displaystyle=\left(-1\right)^{k}j^{k}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{n+1}}{n+j+1}

\displaystyle+\sum_{l=1}^{k}\binom{k}{l}\left(-1\right)^{k-l}j^{k-l}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(n+j+1\right)^{l-1}x^{n+1}.

From (
2
) and (
3

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(n+j+1\right)^{l-1}x^{n+1}=x^{-j}A\left(l-1;x\right)-x^{-j}A^{\left(j\right)}\left(l-1;x\right).

Moreover, it is obvious that

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{n+1}}{n+j+1}=-x^{-j}\frac{\ln\left(1-x\right)}{\left(1-x\right)}-\sum_{n=1}^{j}\frac{x^{n-j}}{n}.

Thus we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{n^{k}}{n+j}x^{n}

\displaystyle=\left(-1\right)^{k+1}j^{k}x^{-j}\frac{\ln\left(1-x\right)}{\left(1-x\right)}+\left(-1\right)^{k+1}j^{k}\sum_{n=1}^{j}\frac{x^{n-j}}{n}

\displaystyle+\sum_{l=1}^{k}\binom{k}{l}\left(-1\right)^{k-l}j^{k-l}x^{-j}\left(A\left(l-1;x\right)-A^{\left(j\right)}\left(l-1;x\right)\right).

Using (
2

\sum_{u=0}^{p-1}x^{u}=\frac{1-x^{p}}{1-x}

we derive (

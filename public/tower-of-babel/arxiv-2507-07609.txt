Orbifold Saito theory of A and D type singularities
Alexey Basalaev and Anton Rarovskii

Abstract
. Saito theory associates to an isolated singularity rich structure that plays an important role in mirror symmetry. In this note we construct Saito theory for A and D type Landau–Ginzburg orbifolds. Namely, for the pairs ( f , G ) (f,G) , where f f defines an isolated singularity of A and D type and G G is a group of symmetries of f f . In total we consider five families of such pairs. In particular, we construct the orbifold versions of Brieskorn lattice and the Gauss–Manin connection computing them explicitly for the A and D type Landau–Ginzburg orbifolds.

1.
Introduction

Let
f\in{\mathbb{C}}[x_{1},\dots,x_{N}]
be a polynomial, defining an isolated singularity, i.e. the system of equations
\partial_{x_{1}}f=\partial_{x_{2}}f=\dots=\partial_{x_{N}}f=0
has only isolated solutions in
{\mathbb{C}}^{N}
.
One of the important invariants of the singularity
f
is a
Jacobian algebra

\mathrm{Jac}(f):={\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{C}}^{N},0}/(\partial_{x_{1}}f,\partial_{x_{2}}f,\dots,\partial_{x_{N}}f)
. It is a finite-dimensional Frobenius algebra and we denote
\mu=\text{dim}_{\mathbb{C}}\mathrm{Jac}(f)
.

Fixing a
{\mathbb{C}}
–basis
\{\phi_{\alpha}({\bf x})\}^{\mu-1}_{\alpha=0}
of
\mathrm{Jac}(f)
, associate to
f
the
universal unfolding

F({\bf x},\textbf{s})=f({\bf x})+\sum^{\mu-1}_{\alpha=0}s_{\alpha}\phi_{\alpha}({\bf x}),

where
s_{0},\dots,s_{\mu-1}
are the parameters varying in the
base space

\mathcal{S}
— open neighborhood of
0\in{\mathbb{C}}^{\mu}
.

Let
{\mathcal{H}}_{f}^{(0)}
and
{\mathcal{H}}_{F}^{(0)}
denote the
Brieskorn lattices
of
f
and
F

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}_{f}^{(0)}

\displaystyle:=H^{*}(\Omega^{\bullet}_{{\mathbb{C}}^{N}}\otimes{\mathbb{C}}[[z]],zd+df\wedge),

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}_{F}^{(0)}

\displaystyle:=H^{*}(\Omega^{\bullet}_{{\mathbb{C}}^{N}}\otimes{\mathbb{C}}[[z]]\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathcal{S}},zd+dF\wedge).

These are classical objects developed by
[
SK81
,
SK83
,
SM89
]
.
The cohomology groups above are concentrated at the top grading
N
and
{\mathcal{H}}_{f}^{(0)}
,
{\mathcal{H}}_{F}^{(0)}
are rank
\mu
free
{\mathbb{C}}[[z]]
-modules.
Essentially,
{\mathcal{H}}_{F}^{(0)}
can be viewed as a deformation of
{\mathcal{H}}_{f}^{(0)}
over the base
\mathcal{S}
.

K. Saito associated the connection to the completion of
{\mathcal{H}}_{F}
. Let
{\mathbb{C}}((z))
stand for the Laurent series in
z
. Define

{\mathcal{H}}_{f}:={\mathcal{H}}_{f}^{(0)}\otimes_{{\mathbb{C}}[[z]]}{\mathbb{C}}((z)),\quad{\mathcal{H}}_{F}:={\mathcal{H}}_{F}^{(0)}\otimes_{{\mathbb{C}}[[z]]}{\mathbb{C}}((z)).

With the help of the unfolding
F
, K. Saito introduces the Gauss–Manin connection

\displaystyle\nabla^{F}:\mathcal{TS}\otimes{\mathcal{H}}_{F}\to{\mathcal{H}}_{F},\ \nabla^{F}_{X}\zeta:=\left[(X\cdot\phi+z^{-1}\phi(X\cdot F))dx_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge dx_{N}\right].

where
\zeta=[\phi dx_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge dx_{N}]\in{\mathcal{H}}_{F}
.

Endowed with the higher residue pairing and the primitive form, the pair
({\mathcal{H}}_{F},\nabla^{F})
gives rise to the structure of Dubrovin–Frobenius manifold
[
SaiT
]
,
[
SK83
]
.
Such Dubrovin–Frobenius manifolds played the key role in mirror symmetry conjecture (cf.
[
CR11
]
).

The main goal of our work is to construct an orbifold version of
({\mathcal{H}}_{F},\nabla^{F})
and investigate its properties.

Orbifold theories

Set
G_{f}:=\{\alpha\in({\mathbb{C}}^{\ast})^{N}\ |\ f(\alpha\cdot{\bf x})=f({\bf x})\}
. Let
G\subseteq G_{f}
.
Then the pair
(f,G)
is called
Landau-Ginzburg orbifold
. Investigation of these objects was initiated by physicists in early 90s (cf.
[
IV
]
,
[
V
]
,
[
Witt
]
), and was followed by mathematicians. In
[
BTW1
]
,
[
BT1
]
the authors introduced the algebra
\mathrm{Jac}(f,G)
, which is
G
-equivariant version of Jacobian algebra
\mathrm{Jac}(f)
. In particular, as a vector space,
\mathrm{Jac}(f,G)\cong\oplus_{g\in G}B_{g}
for some vector spaces
B_{g}
. We have
B_{\mathrm{id}}\cong(\mathrm{Jac}(f))^{G}
while the other direct summands are more complicated. In particular, some of
B_{g}
can be zero vector spaces.
However the construction of the orbifold versions of
{\mathcal{H}}_{F}
and
\nabla^{F}
appeared to be much more involved.

Some approaches towards the construction of an orbifold Saito theory appeared in the literature. Junwu Tu introduced in
[
Tu21a
,
Tu21b
]
the categorical approach to equivariant Saito theory using category of
G
-equivariant matrix factorizations and the variations of the semi-infinite Hodge structures (see also
[
AT22
]

[

]

–structure of two orbifold examples.
In our work we are going to use a more classical approach and introduce the orbifold Saito theory for A and D type singularities via the unfoldings
F
.

Main results

Consider the pair
(f,G)
and a miniversal unfolding
F=f+\sum_{\alpha=0}^{\mu-1}s_{\alpha}\phi_{\alpha}
.
Denote by
F^{G}
the
G
-invariant part of
F
. Namely,
F^{G}:=f+\sum_{\beta\in R}s_{\beta}\phi_{\beta}
where
R
is the set of all indices
\beta
, such that
\phi_{\beta}
is
G
–invariant. Such variables
s_{\beta}
can be identified with the
\mathrm{id}
–graded coordinates
v^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{id}}
of
\mathcal{S}_{(f,G)}
. So, we can consider
F^{G}=F^{G}({\bf x},v_{\mathrm{id}}^{\bullet})
.

For any
g\in G
set
I_{g}:=\{k\ |\ g\cdot x_{k}=x_{k}\}
and
I_{g}^{c}:=\{1,2,3\}\backslash I_{g}
. Consider the polynomial

H_{g}^{F}:=\widetilde{m}\det\left(\frac{\partial^{2}F^{G}}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}\right)_{i,j\in I_{g}^{c}}\in{\mathbb{C}}[{\bf x},v_{\mathrm{id}}^{\bullet}]

with
\widetilde{m}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{\ast}
, the certain constant fixed in Section
2.1.1
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
(f,G)
be a Landau-Ginzburg orbifold from Table
1
. Then

(\rm{i})

\nabla^{(f,G)}
is torsion-free and respects the
G
–grading.

(\rm{ii})

for any
[\phi\xi_{\mathrm{id}}]\in{\mathcal{H}}_{(f,G)}^{def}
we have

\Pi_{\mathrm{id}}\cdot\nabla_{\frac{\partial}{\partial v^{\alpha}_{\mathrm{id}}}}^{(f,G)}[\phi\xi_{\mathrm{id}}]=\nabla^{F^{G}}_{\frac{\partial}{\partial v^{\alpha}_{\mathrm{id}}}}[\phi]\xi_{\mathrm{id}}

(\rm{iii})

for any
[\phi\xi_{g}]\in{\mathcal{H}}_{(f,G)}^{def}
we have

\Pi_{g}\cdot\nabla_{\frac{\partial}{\partial v^{\alpha}_{\mathrm{id}}}}^{(f,G)}[\phi\xi_{g}]=\nabla^{F^{G}}_{\frac{\partial}{\partial v^{\alpha}_{\mathrm{id}}}}[\phi]\xi_{g}\ \text{and}\ \Pi_{g}\cdot\nabla_{\frac{\partial}{\partial v^{\alpha}_{g}}}^{(f,G)}[\phi\xi_{\mathrm{id}}]=\nabla^{F^{G}}_{\frac{\partial}{\partial v^{\alpha}_{\mathrm{id}}}}[\phi]\xi_{g}.

(\rm{iv})

for any
[\phi\xi_{g}]\in{\mathcal{H}}_{(f,G)}^{def}
we have

\nabla_{\frac{\partial}{\partial v^{\alpha}_{g}}}^{(f,G)}[\phi\xi_{g}]=c_{g}\nabla_{\frac{\partial}{\partial v^{\alpha}_{\mathrm{id}}}}^{(f,G)}[\phi H_{g}^{F}](\widetilde{v})\xi_{\mathrm{id}}

where
\widetilde{v}=\widetilde{v}(v)
is the certain linear change of the variables, and

c_{g}=\frac{1}{[G]^{2}}(-1)^{\frac{1}{2}(N-N_{g})(N-N_{g}-1)}{\bf e}[-\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{age}(g)]\in{\mathbb{C}}^{\ast}

as in Section
2.1.1
.

Part (ii) of the theorem above is very important for the Dubrovin–Frobenius manifold of an orbifold Saito theory. Such condition was assumed to hold true in
[
BT2
]
in order to construct such Dubrovin–Frobenius manifold axiomatically.

Expanding the theorem above we also compute explicitly the connection
\nabla^{(f,G)}
for Landau–Ginzburg orbifolds from Table
1
.
Our computations show that the connection
\nabla^{(f,G)}
agrees with the product of
\mathrm{Jac}(f,G)
.

The methods, that we develop in this paper can also be used in a wider context. In particular, orbifold equivalence was established by the second author for the series of non–invertible quasihomogeneous singularities (cf.
[
R1
]
). Our approach allows one to define orbifold Saito theory for any such Landau–Ginzburg orbifold.

2.
Brieskorn lattice and Gauss–Manin connection

Consider
{\mathbb{C}}[{\bf x}]:=\mathbb{C}[x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{N}]
a ring of polynomials with complex coefficients. We call a polynomial
f\in{\mathbb{C}}[{\bf x}]

non-degenerate
if
f
defines an isolated singularity at the origin, i.e. the system of equations
\{\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{1}}=\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{2}}=\dots=\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{N}}\ =0\}
has the unique solution
0\in{\mathbb{C}}^{N}
.

Associate to
f
the
Jacobian algebra

\mathrm{Jac}(f):={\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{C}}^{N}}/_{(\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{1}},\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{2}},\dots,\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{N}})}
.
Its dimension
\mu
, called
Milnor number
, is finite if and only if
f
is non-degenerate (see
[
AGV
]
).

The group of
maximal diagonal symmetries
of
f
is defined by:

\displaystyle G_{f}:=\{g\in(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{N}\>|\>f(g\cdot{\bf x})=f({\bf x})\}.

A pair
(f,G)
with
G\subseteq G_{f}
is called a
Landau-Ginzburg orbifold
.

With each element
g\in G_{f}
we can associate the algebra
\mathrm{Jac}(f^{g})
of the polynomial
f^{g}=f|_{\mathrm{Fix}(g)}
where
\mathrm{Fix}(g)=\{{\bf x}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{N}\ |\ g\cdot{\bf x}={\bf x}\}
. If
\mathrm{Fix}(g)=0
, set
f^{g}:=1
and
\mathrm{Jac}(f^{g}):={\mathbb{C}}
.

Proposition 2

(
[
ET1
]
, Prop. 5)
.

If the polynomial
f
is non-degenerate, then
\forall g\in G
such that
\mathrm{Fix}(g)\neq 0
the polynomial
f^{g}
is also non-degenerate.

Orbifold local algebra

We associate with a Landau-Ginzburg orbifold
(f,G)
the algebra
\mathrm{Jac}(f,G)
which is
G

\mathrm{Jac}(f)
.

\mathrm{Jac}(f,G)
is defined either as Hochschild cohomology of the category of

-equivariant matrix factorizations or axiomatically as in
[
BTW1
,
BTW2
]
. Two definitions agree for the polynomials we consider in this text (see
[

[
BT1
]

It was proved in
[

]

(f,G)
from Table

there is a non-degenerate polynomial
\overline{f}\in{\mathbb{C}}[y_{1},y_{2},y_{3}]

(2.1)

\Psi_{\overline{f}}:\mathrm{Jac}(\overline{f})\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathrm{Jac}(f,G).

By the construction we have

2.2.
Elements of Saito Theory

Let
\{\phi_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha=0}^{\mu-1}
be a
{\mathbb{C}}
–basis of
\mathrm{Jac}(f)
. Then we define the
universal unfolding
of
f
to be the following polynomial

F({\bf x},\mathbf{s})=f({\bf x})+\sum_{\alpha=0}^{\mu-1}s_{\alpha}\phi_{\alpha}({\bf x})\in{\mathbb{C}}[{\bf x},\mathbf{s}].

In what follows assume
s_{0},\dots,s_{\mu-1}
to vary in the
base space of the unfolding

\mathcal{S}
— small ball in
{\mathbb{C}}^{\mu}
centered at the origin
0\in{\mathbb{C}}^{\mu}
.

The
Brieskorn lattice
and
completed Brieskorn lattice
of polynomial
f
is a cohomology ring:

{\mathcal{H}}^{(0)}_{f}:=H^{*}(\Omega^{\bullet}_{{\mathbb{C}}^{N}}\otimes{\mathbb{C}}[[z]],zd+df\wedge)\quad{\mathcal{H}}_{f}={\mathcal{H}}^{(0)}_{f}\otimes_{{\mathbb{C}}[[z]]}{\mathbb{C}}((z))

It’s easy to see that

{\mathcal{H}}^{(0)}_{f}\cong(\Omega^{N}_{{\mathbb{C}}^{N}}\otimes{\mathbb{C}}[[z]])/(zd+df).

In the same way, we define the Brieskorn lattices for the universal unfolding that can be seen as a deformation of
{\mathcal{H}}^{(0)}_{f}
:

{\mathcal{H}}^{(0)}_{F}:=H^{*}(\Omega^{\bullet}_{{\mathbb{C}}^{N}}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathcal{S}}\otimes{\mathbb{C}}[[z]],zd+dF),\quad{\mathcal{H}}_{F}:={\mathcal{H}}^{(0)}_{F}\otimes_{{\mathbb{C}}[[z]]}{\mathbb{C}}((z)).

In what follows assume the notation
D_{f}:=zd+df
and
D_{F}:=zd+dF
.
It is well-known that

{\mathcal{H}}_{f}\cong{\mathbb{C}}((z))\otimes\mathrm{Jac}(f)\quad\text{and}\quad{\mathcal{H}}_{F}\cong{\mathbb{C}}((z))\otimes\mathrm{Jac}(f)\otimes\mathcal{O_{S}}.

Gauss-Manin connection
\nabla^{F}
is defined on the chain level by

\nabla^{F}:\mathcal{TS}\otimes\Omega^{\bullet}_{{\mathbb{C}}^{N}}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{S}\otimes{\mathbb{C}}((z))\to\Omega^{\bullet}_{{\mathbb{C}}^{N}}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{S}\otimes{\mathbb{C}}((z))

\nabla^{F}_{X}\left(\alpha d^{m}x\right):=\left(X\cdot\alpha+z^{-1}\alpha(X\cdot F)\right)d^{m}x,\quad m\geq 0

where
\alpha=\alpha({\bf s},{\bf x},z)
. This connection is well–defined on
{\mathcal{H}}_{F}
as well because
[\nabla^{F},D_{F}]=0
.

Remark 6
.

Classical Saito theory contains also such important ingredients as higher residue pairing, good section and primitive form. We do not introduce these objects here because they do not play an important role in the current work.

In order to define the orbifold Gauss–Manin connection we make use of the ‘‘classical’’ Gauss–Manin connection of the orbifold equivalent polynomial.

Fix the universal unfolding
\bar{F}({\bf y},\bar{\textbf{s}},\bar{\textbf{v}})

Extend

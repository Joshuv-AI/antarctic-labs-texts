Holographic free energy and integrated correlators of 𝒩 = 𝟏 ∗ {\cal N}=1^{*} theory
Nakwoo Kim, and Hoseob Shin

Abstract
We consider the 2nd integrated correlators of 𝒩 = 4 {\cal N}=4 , D = 4 D=4 super Yang-Mills theory, especially those which can be associated with 𝒩 = 1 ∗ {\cal N}=1^{*} mass-deformed theories. We provide an analytic derivation of the integrals at supergravity tree level, which has not been available so far. Our result agrees with the previous results from the study of BPS solutions in supergravity and the holographic evaluation of the sphere partition function.

Introduction

A lot of effort has been devoted to thorough understanding of
{\cal N}=4
,
D=4
super Yang-Mills (SYM) theory, especially as the prototype example of AdS/CFT correspondence
[

They in general have non-trivial coordinate dependence in terms of cross ratios, but we are specifically interested in the quantity of 4-point correlators, integrated with an appropriate measure which is consistent with the superconformal invariance. The resulting quantities can be interpreted as certain 4th derivatives of the free energy
\log Z(\tau,\bar{\tau},m)

{\cal N}=4

{\cal N}=2

[

For practical computation, we split the sum into three parts.

Let us now turn to the computation of integrated correlators which are related to the

terms of

\displaystyle J_{i}\equiv\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{tr}\left(Z_{i}^{2}+\bar{Z}_{i}^{2}\right)\,,\qquad K_{i}\equiv-\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{tr}(\chi_{i}\sigma_{2}\chi_{i}+\tilde{\chi}_{i}\sigma_{2}\tilde{\chi}_{i})\,,\qquad{L_{i}\equiv\mathrm{tr}|{Z_{i}}|^{2}}\,.

where

are their superpartner spinors.
The mass-dependent part of the action is,

\displaystyle S_{m}=\sum_{i=1}^{3}\int{{\rm d}}^{4}x\sqrt{g}\left[m_{i}(iJ_{i}+K_{i})+m^{2}_{i}L_{i}\right].

(22)

Since we are interested in the

part of the four-point correlators which contribute to the

order terms of the free energy, we may ignore

terms from now on. Thanks to the

(23)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{1122}\equiv\frac{\partial^{4}F(m_{1},m_{2},m_{3})}{\partial m_{1}^{2}\partial m_{2}^{2}}\bigg|_{m_{i}=0}.

The scalar quadratic operators

4
Discussions

In this paper we have provided a vindication of the supergravity computation for mass-deformed
{\cal N}=4
super Yang-Mills theory on
S^{4}
. The coefficients at quartic order,
{\cal A,B}
in (
5
), have remained mysterious but we have verified them as integrated 4-point correlation functions. We should mention that this is
not
a non-trivial check of AdS/CFT in the usual sense, i.e. comparison of a genuinely quantum field theory calculation against a classical supergravity calculation. For
{\cal N}=2^{*}
, (
7
) is derivable both using supersymmetric localization and classical supergravity, so it is a feat of AdS/CFT. On the other hand, because localization formula is not available for
{\cal N}=1^{*}
, our computation in this paper is based on a supergravity result given in
[
56
]
, compared to another supergravity result obtained in
[
47
]
. The difference between these two works is that the authors of
[
56
]
used Witten diagrams to calculate 4-point functions holographically, while the approach taken in
[
45
,
46
]
etc. is to construct the whole supergravity solutions when one turns on the dual of mass operators. In the former approach one keeps the information of all the subtle functional dependence on cross ratios, but higher-point functions have to be computed one-by-one. In the latter approach the spatial, or cross ratio dependence is swept away, but potentially the on-shell action gives information on all higher-point correlation functions - integrated with appropriate measures - in one go.

Admittedly, the calculation presented in this work looks like quite an inefficient way to arrive at a single number. One might try to do the integral using Mellin transform instead. In fact, while the supergravity level amplitude
{\cal T}^{\rm SG}=-U^{2}\bar{D}_{2422}/8
(
26
) is not everyone’s first guess, in Mellin representation it is probably more appealing aesthetically, being simply
{\cal M}^{\rm SG}=1/(stu)
. Indeed, the authors of
[
2
]
computed the 1st integrated correlators using the second Barnes’ lemma, without much pain. It is straightforward to write down the Mellin form of the integrals we have evaluated in this work, but as it turns out they are not directly amenable to the use of Barnes’ lemma. One might pursue along the line of
[
62
]
to derive more intricate Barnes-like lemmas. Certainly, any other method which streamlines the evaluation of the 2nd integrated correlators will be desirable. For the time being, the method
proposed in this article can be applied to other integrated correlators
in
D=4
conformal field theories. We are currently working on the evaluation of sub-leading terms of integrated correlator given in Eq.(2.17) of
[
2
]
and the result will be reported separately.

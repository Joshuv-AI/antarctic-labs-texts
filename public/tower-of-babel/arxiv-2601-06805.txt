Cancelling second order frequency shifts in Ge hole spin qubits via bichromatic control
Xiangjun Tan, Zhanning Wang, Wenkai Bai, Hanjie Zhu

Abstract
Germanium quantum dot hole spin qubits are compatible with fully electrical control and are progressing toward multi-qubit operations. However, their coherence is limited by charge noise and driving field induced frequency shifts, and the resulting ensemble 1 / f 1/f dephasing. Here we theoretically demonstrate that a bichromatic driving scheme cancels the second order frequency shift from the control field without sacrificing the electric dipole spin resonance (EDSR) rate, and without additional gate design or microwave engineering. Based on this property, we further demonstrate that bichromatic control creates a wide operating window that reduces sensitivity to quasi-static charge noise and thus enhances single qubit gate fidelity. This method provides a low-power route to a stabler frequency operation in germanium hole spin qubits and is readily transferable to other semiconductor spin qubit platforms.

Another advantage of bichromatic driving is the ability to reduce the first order sensitivity of the qubit frequency to gate field fluctuations,
\mathrm{d}\omega_{0}/\mathrm{d}E_{\text{gate}}

Charge noise is often modelled as random telegraph noise (RTN) source from individual traps, whose ensemble produces a

We consider a single charge defect described by screened Thomas-Fermi potential

In the quasi-static limit, the inhomogeneous dephasing time is set by the run-to-run fluctuation of the qubit resonance frequency

The induced frequency offset is
\delta\omega_{c}\equiv\langle\mathbb{2}|V_{\text{TF}}|\mathbb{2}\rangle-\langle\mathbb{1}|V_{\text{ TF}}|\mathbb{1}\rangle

Without drive induced shifts, one estimates

Under bichromatic driving, the resonance acquires an additional second order shift
\delta\omega^{(2)}

T_{2}^{*}=2\pi/(|\delta\omega_{c}+\delta\omega^{(2)}|)

Earlier studies have demonstrated sweet spot where

We emphasize that this does not eliminate dephasing from faster spectral components, rather, it suppresses the dominant quasi-static contribution to the linewidth without degrading the one-photon Rabi rate.

can be tuned to minimize the noise sensitivity of the dressed splitting, thereby realizing a dynamical sweet spot and reducing the dephasing rate without sacrificing the Rabi frequency.

\omega_{1}=\omega_{0}

The non-monotonic dependence of
1/T_{2}^{*}
on
\omega_{2}
reflects the competition between the quasi-static charge noise induced offset
\delta\omega_{c}
and the auxiliary tone induced dispersive longitudinal shift
\delta\omega^{(2)}
.
For small
\omega_{2}
,
\delta\omega_{c}
and
\delta\omega^{(2)}
have the same sign so that
|\delta\omega_{c}+\delta\omega^{(2)}|
increases, leading to a larger dephasing rate.
As
\omega_{2}
is increased further,
\delta\omega^{(2)}
changes sign and a compensation point
\delta\omega_{c}+\delta\omega^{(2)}\approx 0
is reached, where
1/T_{2}^{*}
is minimized (other noise sources then set the residual linewidth).
Beyond this point,
\delta\omega^{(2)}
no longer compensates
\delta\omega_{c}
, so that
|\delta\omega_{c}+\delta\omega^{(2)}|
increases again, producing the rise of
1/T_{2}^{*}
on the other side of the minimum.

In conclusion, we have shown that bichromatic driving suppresses second order frequency shift and extends
T_{2}^{*}
in Ge hole spin qubits.
The scheme requires only minimal changes to standard EDSR techniques and is readily applicable to Si platforms, where the smaller Zeeman splitting permits lower auxiliary frequencies
Huang and Hu 2021
;
Liles et al. 2021
;
Hu et al. 2025
.
It should also generalize to device incorporating strain profiles, where enhanced SOC and larger dipole matrix elements are expected.
While our analysis focuses on the quasi-static limit, extending the Floquet framework can capture richer dynamics such as multiphoton processes, universal dynamical sweet spots, and Raman pathways
Didier et al. 2019
;
Huang et al. 2021
;
Valery et al. 2022
.

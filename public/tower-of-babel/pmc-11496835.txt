A study on aerodynamic pass-by noise based on equivalent source method
Authors: Shumin Zhang, Jiawei Shi, Haoran Wang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractPass-by noise generated by high-speed trains is an important influencing factor on the surrounding environment and is getting more and more attention with the increase of speed. In the past, the aerodynamic pass-by noise prediction was usually conducted with the wind tunnel mode, ignoring the Doppler effect. In this paper, an equivalent source method considering the Doppler effect is proposed to study the aerodynamic pass-by noise. A cylinder case is considered first to verify the effectiveness of the method. Then a DSA 380 pantograph of a Chinese high-speed train is considered. The pantograph is divided into three parts, and each part is equivalent to a monopole or dipole sound source. Results show that the upper part of the pantograph is closer to a dipole source, while the middle and lower parts are closer to a monopole source. When the measurement point is directly above the pantogragh, the contribution of the upper part is the largest, while for both sides measurement points of the pantograph, the contribution of the upper part is the smallest.Keywords: Aerodynamic pass-by noise, Equivalent sound source method, High-speed train pantographSubject terms: Mechanical engineering, Fluid dynamics

Introduction

Noise emitted by high-speed trains is an important factor affecting the surrounding environment1. At present, the highest operating speed in China has reached 350 km/h, and the 400 km/h high-speed train is under development. It is said that the noise level of the 400 km/h high-speed train should not be higher than the noise level at 350 km/h, so the low noise design at the early stage must be effectively implemented.

Many interesting phenomena have been revealed about the external noise generated by high-speed trains thanks to the in-situ tests2–5. The in-situ test about exterior noise can be conducted by a single microphone or the acoustic array measurement. The single microphone measurement positions are usually located at distances of 7.5 and 25 m from the central line of the track, respectively, and both at a height of 3.5 m above the upper surface of the rail6. The result at this point is generally referred to as the far-field pass-by noise. These measurements have helped to understand the exterior noise characteristics and the regression law of the sound pressure level according to the train speed. From the acoustic array measurement, noise can be mapped on the train surface, and the main noise areas at different speeds can be identified, which can provide a reference for noise and vibration control.

According to the field test results, the main noise sources of high-speed trains are wheel-rail noise and aerodynamic noise7. It is generally believed that aerodynamic noise will become the main noise source when the train operation speed exceeds 300 km/h8. In order to meet the requirements of the laws and regulations, aerodynamic noise control has become an important consideration9–11.

An effective tool to study the aerodynamic noise is based on the CFD computations, which has been integrated into many CFD software12,13. However, when using the CFD software, the wind tunnel mode is usually used to simulate the far-field noise; that is, the train and the far-field point keep stationary, and the wind blows towards the train at a certain speed14. Compared with the train passing through the measurement point, this method ignored the Doppler effect. In general, the sliding or overset mesh needs to be used to eliminate this problem. By doing this, the calculation domain is divided into a moving domain and a static domain, and the flow field information between them is transmitted by interpolation through a so-called interface, which may have a negative effect on the simulation accuracy. Based on this issue, Ref15. proposed a method based on the FW-H equations to calculated the aerodynamic pass-by noise, and this method had verified by a moving cylinder.

Another method to simulate the far-field pass-by noise is based on the acoustic array measurement. The sound power of different parts of the train is equivalent to the sound source, and then different far-field pass-by noise prediction models were established, such as PROHV, MAT2S and VAMPASS16,17. However, the Doppler effect considered in VAMPASS model may be too simplified for a high speed train. Ref18. simplified noise sources on a moving train to be a set of incoherent monopole sources so that the Doppler effect can be considered.

Based on the idea of the equivalent source method19, this paper studies another method that can calculate the aerodynamic pass-by noise. Firstly, the sound pressure perceived at a fixed far-field point due to a moving monopole or dipole sound source is recalled, and the principle of equivalence is described. Then, taking a cylinder case as an example, the pass-by noise of the cylinder is calculated and then verified with the results in Ref15. Then, a high-speed pantograph is considered, and the pass-by noise at different far-filed points are predicted and analyzed. In the end, conclusions are given. It can be seen that the studies of this paper can provide an effective tool for the low-noise design at the early stage of the higher-speed train.

A moving high-speed train pantograph

A DSA380 pantograph of the Chinese CRH380AL high-speed train is considered in this section, and its geometry model is shown as Fig. 7. The pantograph is located in the computational domain of 40 × 20 × 10 m, and the distance between the entrance and the pantograph is 10 m. The both sides and upper of the domain are set as symmetry boundaries. The incoming flow velocity of the pantograph is set as 400 km/h (111.11 m/s). A hybrid mesh strategy of trimmed mesh and prism layer mesh is used to discretized the model. The surface mesh size of the pantograph is controlled within a range from 0.5 to 4 mm. A total of 15 fine layers of prism grid meshes are created with an initial height of 0.01 mm and an expansion ratio of 1.2, and the total number of grids is about 120 million. The convergence analysis of the model can be found in Ref20. The grid distribution of the pantograph is shown in Fig. 8.

The pass-by noise time-history results at (30, 0, 0) m radiated by different parts are shown in Fig. 12. It can be seen that the sound pressure reaches its peak at about 0.27s, which is because the sound source is closest to the observer point at that time. The sound pressure radiated by the upper part is significantly higher than those by the middle and lower parts. The overall sound pressure of the upper, middle, and lower parts are 12.6, 2.47, and 4.35 Pa, respectively, and the corresponding contribution of each part to the total pressure are 64.9, 12.7 and 22.4%. Therefore, the contribution of the upper part is the largest at this point.

Aerodynamic pass-by noise time-history results at (30, 0, 0) m radiated by different parts.

The pass-by noise time-history results at (30, 0, 7.5) m radiated by different parts is shown in Fig. 13. Compared with Fig. 12, there is no significant peak at around 0.27 s, which is due to the directivity of the sound source radiation. The sound pressure generated by the middle or lower parts is larger than that generated by the upper part. The over-all sound pressure of the upper, middle, and lower parts are 0.04, 0.57, and 1.02 Pa, respectively. The proportions of relative total sound pressure are 2.3, 35, and 62.7%, and the contribution of the lower part to the total sound pressure at this far-field point is the largest. This is because the upper part is equivalent to a dipole source, and only the lift direction (y-direction) is considered, resulting in the minimum contribution of the upper part when the measuring point is located at the side of the pantograph.

Conclusions

In this paper, an equivalent source method is proposed to calculate the aerodynamic noise of a high-speed train pantograph. A set of measurement points are set on a spherical surface with the pantograph as the center (with radius r), and sound pressure radiated by the pantograph at those points is calculated by STAR-CCM+. The equivalent sound source method is conducted based on the idea of the equivalent sound power of the object.

The effectiveness of the method is validated by a moving cylinder. From the sound pressure spectrum at the far-field point radiated by the cylinder, the peak value is no longer at 480 Hz, but distributed within a frequency band around 480 Hz. According to the directivity of the pantograph, the characteristics of the upper part are much closer to a dipole sound source, while the middle and lower parts are closer to a monopole source. When the measurement point is located at the upper area of the pantograph, the contribution of the sound pressure radiated by the upper part is significantly higher than those in the middle and lower parts. But the contribution of the upper parts becomes the smallest when the measurement point is located on both sides of the pantograph. Therefore, when using this method to calculate the aerodynamic pass-by noise, the directivity of the pantograph must be considered.

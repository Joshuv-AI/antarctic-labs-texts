Scaling limit for the random walk on critical lattice trees
Gérard Ben Arous, Manuel Cabezas, Alexander Fribergh

Abstract
. We prove a scaling limit theorem for the simple random walk on critical lattice trees in ℤ d \mathbb{Z}^{d} , for d ≥ 8 d\geq 8 . The scaling limit is the Brownian motion on the Integrated Super-Brownian Excursion (BISE) which is the same one that we have identified earlier for other simpler models of anomalous diffusion on critical graphs in large enough dimension. The proof of this theorem is based on a combination of the tools of lace-expansion (contained in the articles [ 10 ] and [ 11 ] ), and a new and general convergence theorem.

1.
Introduction

The idea that diffusion is expected to be anomalous on critical graphs goes back at least to Pierre-Gilles de Gennes’ work in 1976 on the “ant in the labyrinth”, i.e., the random walk on critical percolation clusters, which has been followed by a massive effort in the physics literature to gain insight in the matter (see for instance the classical works by
[
37
]
,
[
21
]
, or for a more recent work
[
9
]
). The mathematics literature has also been very active (since the classical work of
[
27
]
proving sub-diffusivity for the case of critical percolation in dimension 2). An important milestone has been reached by Kozma and Nachmias in
[
30
]
, where they proved the Alexander-Orbach conjecture, finally establishing that the spectral dimension of the critical percolation cluster in high enough dimension is
\frac{4}{3}
.

This article is part of our effort to understand in more detail anomalous diffusion on critical graphs in large enough dimension, where the behavior is supposed to be universal (see for instance
[
5
]
,
[
13
]
,
[
22
]
,
[
6
]
or the survey
[
23
]
).

We aim at a detailed result i.e., understanding the full scaling limit of random walks on these critical graphs. The natural candidate as the scaling limit is the so-called
BISE
(the Brownian motion on the Integrated Super-Brownian Excursion, see Section
2
for a formal definition). In our first step
[
8
]
we gave an abstract theorem ensuring that the scaling limit would indeed be the BISE under a set of four conditions (see Section
4
and Section
5
), which we assume would be satisfied in this class of critical random graphs in large enough dimension.

As a first proof of concept, this theorem was applied in
[
7
]
in the context of the simple random walk on the range of critical branching random walks (conditioned on being large). But, in order to deal with more delicate models it appeared that one would have to enlist the heavier tools of the field, i.e. lace expansion, in order to check the four conditions of
[
8
]
.

Our goal is now to show that a similar general result can be applied to the entire class of models that can be analyzed using lace expansion. These models include lattice trees above dimension 8 (see e.g.
[
19
,
16
,
25
]
), critical oriented percolation above dimension 4 (see e.g.
[
24
]
) and critical percolation above dimension 6 (see e.g.
[
20
]
). Our final goal is to understand the case of percolation and oriented percolation, which is the initial question raised by De Gennes, albeit in high dimension. We start this project here with the case of (spread-out) lattice trees. It is important to note here that this article relies indeed on lace-expansion estimates obtained in the two related articles
[
10
]
and
[
11
]
.

In the process of building a proof in the context of lattice trees, we observed that under certain very natural assumptions (which are valid for lattice trees), the four conditions needed for convergence towards the BISE can actually essentially be reduced to three. This represents the main work of this article.

We start by presenting the model of the simple random walk on spread-out lattice trees and stating our main convergence result in this context (see Theorem
1.1
) since it requires less notations than the general case. We then introduce the notations needed to state our new convergence theorem for critical random graphs in general which will lead up to our new conditions for convergence towards the BISE in Section
5
. We then move onto the main proof before applying our results to the lattice tree case in Section
8
.

1.1.
Critical lattice trees

A lattice tree
T
is a finite, connected subgraph of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
containing no cycles. Specifically, we will be considering
spread-out
lattice trees, i.e., subgraphs of
(\mathbb{Z}^{d},E_{L}(\mathbb{Z}^{d}))
, where

E_{L}(\mathbb{Z}^{d}):=\{[x,y]:x,y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},x\neq y,\|x-y\|_{\infty}\leq L\}

is the set of edges of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
whose endpoints are at
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
-distance smaller or equal than some constant
L\in\mathbb{N}
. Let us now define a probability distribution on lattice trees. Let
D(\cdot)
be uniform distribution on a finite box
[-L,L]^{d}\setminus o
, where
o=(0,\dots,0)\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. That is
D(x)=\frac{1}{(2L)^{d}-1}
for
x\in[-L,L]^{d}\setminus o
and
0
otherwise. For a lattice tree
T\ni o
and
z>0
, define the weight of
T
as

(1.1)

W_{z,D}(T):=z^{|E(T)|}\prod_{[x,y]\in E(T)}D(x-y)=\left(\frac{z}{(2L)^{d}-1}\right)^{|E(T)|},

where
E(T)
is the set of edges of
T
and
|E(T)|
is its cardinal. An important observation is that if
T
is an edge-disjoint union of subtrees, then
W_{z,D}(T)
can be factored into a product over the weights of the subtrees. It turns out (see e.g.
[
29
]
) that there exists a critical value
z_{D}
such that
\rho:=\sum_{T\ni o}W_{z_{D},D}(T)<\infty
and
{\bf E}[|\mathcal{T}|]=\infty
, where
{\bf E}
denotes expectation with respect to the probability measure
{\bf P}
defined as
{\bf P}[\mathcal{T}=T]=\rho^{-1}W_{z_{D},D}(T)
. Hereafter we write
W(\cdot)
for the critical weighting
W_{z_{D},D}(\cdot)
and suppose that we are selecting a random tree
\mathcal{T}
according to this critical weighting.

We denote
d_{T}(\cdot,\cdot)
the graph distance of a lattice tree
T
. We can then define the height of a lattice tree
T
as

H(T)=\max\{d_{T}(o,x)\text{ with }x\in T\}.

Let
h>0
and we set
{\bf P}^{h}_{n}[\hskip 2.84544pt\cdot\hskip 2.84544pt]:={\bf P}[\hskip 2.84544pt\cdot\mid H(T)\geq hn]
and denote
T_{n}
a random tree chosen according to that measure.

Given a fixed lattice tree
T
, we are able to define the simple random walk started at
o
on that tree. We denote this process
(X_{k}^{T})_{k\geq 0}
and its corresponding law
P^{T}
.

In this article, we will give scaling limit results on this random walk under the annealed measure
\mathbb{P}_{n}^{h}[\cdot]={\bf E}_{n}^{h}[P^{T_{n}}[\cdot]]
. More precisely, our main result on lattice trees is the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

Fix
d>8
. There exists
L_{0}(d)
such that for all
L\geq L_{0}
there exists constants
C_{1},C_{2}\in(0,\infty)
such that, for each
h>0
,

(n^{-1/2}X^{T_{n}}_{n^{3}t})_{t\geq 0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle n\to\infty}}{{\to}}(C_{1}B^{h\text{-ISE}}_{C_{2}t})_{t\geq 0},

where the convergence is annealed (under
\mathbb{P}^{h}_{n}
) and occurs with the topology of uniform convergence over compact sets of time. The limiting process
B^{h\text{-ISE}}
is the Brownian motion on the
integrated super-Brownian excursion
conditioned to have survived for time at least
h
and is defined in Proposition
2.2
.

1.2.
Simple conditions for the convergence towards the
Brownian motion on the ISE

Let us now discuss our theorem for simple random walks on general critical graphs in high dimensions, without going into full details because the notations needed for the precise statement are quite involved.

In the initial paper
[
8
]
, we established a very general framework for a sequence
G_{n}
of random subgraphs of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, within which one can prove convergence of the simple random walk on the graphs towards the BISE. In the article
[
7
]
, that framework was applied to prove convergence of simple random walks on critical branching random walks in high dimensions towards the BISE.

This type of convergence result is proved by verifying four conditions called
(G)
,
(V)
,
(R)
and
(S)
. Those conditions depend on the choice of certain random points
(V_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}=(V_{i}^{n})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
on the graphs
G_{n}
, which are used to span subtrees from
G_{n}
. More precisely, for each
K\in\mathbb{N}
, we will consider the
skeleton

\mathcal{T}^{(n,K)}
as the minimal subgraph of
G_{n}
containing
o
and
V_{i},i=1,\dots,K
(for more details see Section
4
).

Our main contribution in this article is that, for a specific choice of the random spanning points
(V_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
, one of the four conditions simplifies substantially. Our assumption on the points

(V_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
is that they are chosen
uniform according to edge-volume
, which, simply put, says that the distribution of each
V_{i}
is asymptotically equal to the one given by first choosing an edge uniformly at random, and then to choose one of the end-vertices of the edge, uniformly and independently. The formal statement of the main result can be found at Theorem
5.1
.

Let us describe informally the conditions appearing in this improved theorem.

•

Condition
(G)
: The geometric convergence of the (rescaled) skeletons
\mathcal{T}^{(n,K)}
towards the skeletons taken on the integrated super-Brownian excursion. This condition was proved for lattice trees in
[
10
]
, using the
historical measure-valued process
convergence to the super Brownian Motion obtained in
[
11
]
. We believe that the argument used in
[
10
]
for lattice trees could be carried over to other models commonly studied using lace expansion techniques such as critical percolation.

•

Condition
(R)
: The asymptotic proportionality between the effective electrical resistance between two vertices of
G_{n}
and the graph distance between these two vertices. Since the effective resistance governs the transition probabilities of a simple random walk, this second condition ensures that the random walk has (asymptotically) symmetric transition probabilities. This condition holds trivially for (lattice) trees.

•

Condition
(S)
: For any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a
K
such that for
n
large enough the graphs
G_{n}
(properly rescaled) are within an
\varepsilon
-neighborhood of the skeleton
\mathcal{T}^{(n,K)}
in the Euclidean distance and also in the intrinsic distance (the article
[
10
]
shows why this result holds in the case of lattice trees).

Let
E(G_{n})
denote the set of edges of
G_{n}
. Our main result (expressed in two different ways, corresponding to different conditionings in Theorems
5.1
and
5.2
) is that, for a sequence of random graphs together with random points
(G_{n},(V_{i}^{n})_{i\in\mathbb{N}})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, where the
(V_{i}^{n})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
are chosen according to uniform edge volume, it holds that

(1)

Under conditions
(G)
,
(S)
and
(R)
, we have

(n^{-1/2}X^{G_{n}}_{n\left|E(G_{n})\right|t})_{t\geq 0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle n\to\infty}}{{\to}}(C_{1}B^{\text{ISE}}_{c_{2}t})_{t\geq 0}

where
C_{1},c_{2}
are positive constants.

(2)

If, in addition, there exists
\nu>0
such that
\frac{\left|E(G_{n})\right|}{n^{2}}
converges to
\nu
in probability, we have

(n^{-1/2}X^{G_{n}}_{n^{3}t})_{t\geq 0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle n\to\infty}}{{\to}}(C_{1}B^{\text{ISE}}_{\nu^{-1}c_{2}t})_{t\geq 0},

where the convergences are annealed and occur with the topology of uniform convergence over compact sets of time.

The contribution of this article can be roughly summarized by saying that, when the points
(V_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
are chosen uniform according to edge volume, condition
(V)
can be replaced by a much weaker estimate on the the overall edge volume of the graph. Loosely speaking, Condition
(V)
states that for
K
large enough, the edge volume of
G_{n}
is (asymptotically) distributed evenly over the skeleton
\mathcal{T}^{(n,K)}
and also there exists a constant
\nu>0
such that
\frac{\left|E(G_{n})\right|}{n^{2}}
converges to
\nu
in probability.

1.3.
Organization of the paper

We start by two sections of notations necessary to state the conditions on random graphs that are relevant for proving that the random walk on them scales to the BISE.

In Section
2
, we will introduce the Integrated super-Brownian excursion
ISE
and the Brownian motion on it.

In Section
3
we will show how to span certain subtrees from large random graphs. These subtrees, called the
skeletons
, are finite dimensional and will be used to approximate the original random graphs.

Then, in Section
4
, we introduce all four conditions (
(G)
,
(V)
,
(R)
and
(S)
) from
[
8
]
that will be needed to state our main theorems.

Section
5
contains the rigorous statement of the main theorems of this paper, which give the convergence of the random walks on the graphs to the BISE. As we have mentioned, in this article we prove that, when the spanning points are chosen
according to uniform edge volume
, conditions
(G)
,
(R)
and
(S)
are enough to get convergence towards the BISE (see Theorems
5.1
and
5.2
).

The next sections (Section 6 and Section 7) are devoted to the main proof of this article which states that: when the points
(V_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
are chosen according to uniform edge volume, Condition
(G)
essentially implies Condition
(V)
(see Proposition
7.1
in this article). Condition
(V)
is about understanding the convergence of the uniform measure on
G_{n}
(see Definition
4.4
). In Section
6
we construct an empirical measure in
G_{n}
, which, on the one hand, is a good approximation of the uniform measure in
G_{n}
and, on the other hand, is
finite-dimensional
in the sense that it depends only on geometric information of the skeleton of the graph. In Section
7
we control this empirical measure using Condition
(G)
which allows us to obtain Condition
(V)
.

Finally, in Section
8
, we will apply Theorem
5.1
to the case of critical lattice trees. This proves Theorem
1.1
.

2.
Brownian motion on super-brownian motions

The goal of this section is to introduce the Brownian motion taking values in (the support of) certain versions of the super-Brownian motion. In particular, we will introduce the
Continuum Random Tree
(
CRT
), the
Integrated Super-Brownian Excursion
(
ISE
) and the
Brownian motion on the ISE
(
B^{\text{ISE}}
) and their counterparts obtained by conditioning on height. The presentation is taken from notes of Le Gall (see
[
32
]
).

2.1.
The continuum random tree (CRT) and random trees conditioned on height

Denote by
({\bf e}(t))_{0\leq t\leq 1}
a normalized Brownian excursion. Informally,
({\bf e}(t))_{0\leq t\leq 1}
is just a Brownian path started at the origin and conditioned to stay positive

(0,1)
, and to come back to
0
at time
1
(see e.g. Sections 2.9
and 2.12 of Itô and McKean
[
26
]
for a discussion of the normalized excursion). We
extend
the definition of
{\bf e}
by setting
{\bf e}(t)=0
if
t>1
.

For every
s,t\geq 0
, we set

m_{\bf e}(s,t)=\inf_{r\in[s\wedge t,s\vee t]}{\bf e}(r),

and, the pseudo-metric

d_{\bf e}(s,t)={\bf e}(s)+{\bf e}(t)-2m_{\bf e}(s,t).

We then introduce the equivalence relation

s\sim t
iff
d_{\bf e}(s,t)=0
(or equivalently iff
{\bf e}(s)={\bf e}(t)=m_{\bf e}(s,t)
). Let

T_{\bf e}
be the quotient space

(2.1)

T_{\bf e}=[0,\infty)/\sim.

For any fixed realization
e
of the excursion
\bf e
, the pseudo-metric
d_{e}
becomes a metric in the quotient space
T_{e}
and the metric space
(T_{e},d_{e})
, is a
Real Tree
.
1
1

1

In the sense that
(i)
For
x,y\in T_{e}
, there exists a unique isometric embedding

with
f_{x,y}(0)=a
and
f_{x,y}(d_{e}(x,y))=b
and
(ii)
If

with

.

Definition 2.1
.

The random real tree
T_{\bf e}
is called the Continuum Random Tree (CRT) and will be denoted
(\mathfrak{T},d_{\mathfrak{T}})
. We write
\Xi
to denote its law.

The CRT was initially defined by Aldous

]
with a different formalism,

, up to an unimportant

We can define a natural volume measure on

by projecting the Lebesgue measure on

, i.e., for any open

, we set

\lambda_{\mathfrak{T}}(A)=\text{Leb}\{t\in[0,1],[t]\in A\},

where

denotes the equivalence class of

Let
({\mathbb{e}}^{h}(t))_{t\geq 0}

. More precisely, let

be a standard Brownian motion and

{\mathbb{e}}^{h}(t)=W(t+a)

.

The random real tree

2.2.
The integrated super-Brownian excursion (ISE) and its counterpart conditioned on height

We may consider the
\mathbb{R}^{d}
-valued Gaussian process

(\phi_{\mathfrak{T}}(\sigma),\sigma\in\mathfrak{T})
whose distribution is characterized by

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\phi_{\mathfrak{T}}(\sigma)]=0\;,

\displaystyle{\rm cov}(\phi_{\mathfrak{T}}(\sigma),\phi_{\mathfrak{T}}(\sigma^{\prime}))=d_{\mathfrak{T}}(\text{root},\sigma\wedge\sigma^{\prime})\,{\rm Id}\;,

where
{\rm Id}
denotes the
d
-dimensional identity matrix. This process has a continuous modification (see (8) in
[
32
]
for details).
If we replace
\mathfrak{T}
by
\mathfrak{T}^{h}
in the definition above, we get an
\mathbb{R}^{d}
-valued Gaussian Process
(\phi_{\mathfrak{T}^{h}}(\sigma))_{\sigma\in\mathfrak{T}^{h}}
.

Given
\mathfrak{T}
, we denote
Q_{\mathfrak{T}}
the law of
(\mathfrak{T},(\phi_{\mathfrak{T}}(\sigma),\sigma\in\mathfrak{T}))
and we denote the joint annealed law by

M=\int\Xi(d\mathfrak{T})\,Q_{\mathfrak{T}}.

Definition 2.3
.

The random probability measure
\lambda_{\phi_{\mathfrak{T}}(\mathfrak{T})}
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
defined under
M
by
\lambda_{\phi_{\mathfrak{T}}(\mathfrak{T})}:=\lambda_{\mathfrak{T}}\circ\phi_{\mathfrak{T}}^{-1}
is called

-dimensional ISE (for Integrated Super-Brownian Excursion).

The random measure
ISE was first discussed by Aldous
[
4
]
.
For
d\geq 4
, the topological support of ISE is the range
of
\phi_{\mathfrak{T}}
. Moreover, the measure
\lambda_{\phi_{\mathfrak{T}}(\mathfrak{T})}
should be interpreted as the

\phi_{\mathfrak{T}}(\mathfrak{T})
(see
[
36
]
,
[
35
]
and also
[
15
]
). We will often abuse the terminology and write ISE to mean its topological support.
Using similar definitions, given
\mathfrak{T}^{h}
, we denote

the law of
(\mathfrak{T}^{h},(\phi_{\mathfrak{T}^{h}}(\sigma),\sigma\in T))
and the joint annealed law by

M^{h}=\int\Xi^{h}(d\mathfrak{T}^{h})\,Q_{\mathfrak{T}^{h}}.

Definition 2.4
.

The random measure
\lambda_{\phi_{\mathfrak{T}^{h}}(\mathfrak{T}^{h})}
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
defined under

by
\lambda_{\phi_{\mathfrak{T}^{h}}(\mathfrak{T}^{h})}:=\lambda_{\mathfrak{T}^{h}}\circ\phi_{\mathfrak{T}}^{-1}
is called

Remark 2.1
.

Note that the measure
\lambda_{\phi_{\mathfrak{T}^{h}}}
is not a probability measure in general, but its support is exactly

, see

]
.

2.3.
The Brownian motion on the ISE:
B^{\text{ISE}}

In
[
2
]
, Aldous gave a set of properties defining uniquely a process which corresponds to our intuition of a Brownian motion on
(T,d_{T},\nu)
where
(T,d_{T})
is a real tree and
\nu
will correspond to an invariant measure of said Brownian motion.

The existence of a such process
B^{\text{CRT}}
on the CRT was proved by Krebs
[
31
]
. After, this was generalized using techniques of resistance forms (see
[
28
]
for an introduction on resistance forms). More specifically, it was proved in Section 6 of
[
14
]
that

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
(T,d_{T})
be a compact real tree,
\nu
be a finite Borel measure on
\mathcal{T}
that satisfies
\nu(A)>0
for every non-empty open set
A\subseteq T
, and
(\mathcal{E}_{T},\mathcal{F}_{T})
be the resistance form associated with
(T,d_{T})
. Then
(\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{E}_{T},\mathcal{F}_{T})
is a local, regular Dirichlet form on
L^{2}(T,\nu)
, and the corresponding Markov process
B^{T,\nu}
is the Brownian motion on
(T,d_{T},\nu)
.

It was shown in
[
14
]
that for
d\geq 8
the CRT and the ISE are isometric which allows us to build the
B^{\text{ISE}}
by embedding the
B^{\text{CRT}}
. This is summed up in the following proposition. Let
d_{\phi_{\mathfrak{T}}(\mathfrak{T})}
be a distance defined in
\phi_{\mathfrak{T}}(\mathfrak{T})
as
d_{\phi_{\mathfrak{T}}(\mathfrak{T})}(x,y):=d_{\mathfrak{T}}(\phi^{-1}_{\mathfrak{T}}(x),\phi^{-1}_{\mathfrak{T}}(y))
for all
x,y\in\phi_{\mathfrak{T}}(\mathfrak{T})
.

Proposition 2.2
.

For
\Xi
-a.e.
\mathfrak{T}
, the Brownian motion
B^{\text{CRT}}
on
(\mathfrak{T},d_{\mathfrak{T}},\lambda_{\mathfrak{T}})
exists. Furthermore if
d\geq 8
, for
M
-a.e.
(\mathfrak{T},\phi_{\mathfrak{T}})
, the Brownian motion
B^{\text{ISE}}
on
(\phi_{\mathfrak{T}}(\mathfrak{T}),d_{\phi_{\mathfrak{T}}(\mathfrak{T})},\lambda_{\phi_{\mathfrak{T}}(\mathfrak{T})})
exists and, moreover,
B^{\text{ISE}}=\phi_{\mathfrak{T}}(B^{\text{CRT}})
.

It is straightforward to generalize this statement to prove that
\phi_{{\mathfrak{T}}^{h}}
is injective as long as
d\geq 8
. This allows us to state the following proposition. Let
d_{\phi_{\mathfrak{T}^{h}}(\mathfrak{T}^{h})}
be a distance in
\phi_{\mathfrak{T}^{h}}(\mathfrak{T}^{h})
defined as
d_{\phi_{\mathfrak{T}}(\mathfrak{T})}
but, with
\mathfrak{T}^{h}
in place of
\mathfrak{T}
.

Proposition 2.3
.

For
\Xi^{h}
-a.e.
\mathfrak{T}^{h}
, the Brownian motion
B^{h\text{-CRT}}
on
(\mathfrak{T}^{h},d_{\mathfrak{T}^{h}},\lambda_{\mathfrak{T}^{h}})
exists. Furthermore if

Graph spatial trees and approximations of the ISE and the

We start the section by introducing the concept of graph spatial trees and we will then introduce approximations of the ISE (

(

2.4.1.
Graph spatial trees

Let us now present a notion introduced by Croydon in
[
14
]
.

Definition 2.5
.

A graph spatial tree
(T,d_{T},\phi_{T})
is a triple, where
T
is a (rooted, ordered) tree-graph,
d_{T}
is a metric on
T
determined by edge lengths
(l(e))_{e\in E(T)}
and
\phi_{T}:\overline{T}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a continuous function, where
\overline{T}
is the real-tree naturally associated with
(T,d_{T})
, i.e.,
\overline{T}
is composed of a finite number of line segments with finite edge length
d_{T}
.

We will use the term shape to designate the first component
T
of a graph spatial tree. We say that a graph spatial tree has a non-degenerate shape if all the vertices of
T
have degree 1 or 3.

Given a graph spatial tree
(T,d_{T},\phi_{T})
, we can assign a probability measure
\lambda_{T}
defined as the renormalized Lebesgue measure (so that the
\lambda_{T}
-measure of a line segment in
T
is proportional to its length).

A simple way to construct graph spatial trees

Let
(T,d_{T},\phi_{T})
be a triple where the pair
(T,d_{T})
is a rooted real tree and
\phi_{T}:T\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a continuous embedding and we consider a sequence
(\sigma_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
of points of the real tree
T
. There is a simple way to construct a rooted graph spatial tree from
(T,d_{T},\phi_{T})
with an order on the edges.

Firstly, we introduce some necessary notation. For
x,y\in T
, the unique path between
x
and
y
is denoted
[x,y]
. We define the branching point of
\sigma,\sigma^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime\prime}\in T
as the unique point
b^{T}(\sigma,\sigma^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime\prime})\in T
which is in the triple intersection of the paths
[\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}]
,
[\sigma^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime\prime}]
and
[\sigma^{\prime\prime},\sigma]
.

Fix
K\in\mathbb{N}
. We define the reduced subtree
T(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{K})
to be the graph tree with vertex set

V(T(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{K})):=\{b^{T}(\sigma,\sigma^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime\prime}):\sigma,\sigma^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime\prime}\in\{\text{root},\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{K}\}\},

and graph tree structure induced by the arcs of
T
, so that two elements
\sigma
and
\sigma^{\prime}
of
V(T(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{K}))
are connected by an edge if and only if
\sigma\neq\sigma^{\prime}
and also
[\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}]\cap V(T(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{K}))=\{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}\}
. We set the length of an edge
\{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}\}
to be equal to
d_{T}(\sigma,\sigma^{\prime})
and we extend the distance linearly on that edge. Also, we consider the embedding of
T(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{K})
given by the restriction of
\phi_{T}
to
T(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{K})
. This allows us to view
T(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{K})
as a graph spatial tree.

Next, we will show how to endow
T(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{K})
with an order on the edges. The leaves
\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{K}
are naturally ordered from 1 to
K
and, when
T(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{K})
is non-degenerate, then we can order the remaining branching points in the order you encounter them when moving from the root to
\sigma_{1}
, then continue the labeling as you move from the root to
\sigma_{2}
and so on up to
\sigma_{K}
. This creates an ordering on the vertices. Then the label/order of an edge is that of its endpoint furthest from the root.

This graph spatial tree will be denoted
(T^{K,(\sigma_{i})},d_{T^{K,(\sigma_{i})}},\phi_{T^{K,(\sigma_{i})}})
. The associated normalized probability measure is denoted
\lambda_{\phi_{T^{K,(\sigma_{i})}}(T^{K,(\sigma_{i})})}
. The dependence on
\sigma
will often be dropped in the notation when the context is clear.

The skeleton of a graph

Let
G=(V(G),E(G))
be a graph, where
V(G)\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
containing the origin,
o\in V(G)
. We will have in mind the nearest neighbor case, where
E(G)\subset E(\mathbb{Z}^{d})

such that
\|x-y\|>L
implies
(x,y)\notin E(G)

We are now going to introduce the skeleton of
G
and related notations which are needed to state the four conditions
(G)
,
(R)
,
(V)
and
(S)
for the convergence to the

[

]
.

One of the key notions we will need in this paper is the notion of cut-point.

Definition 3.1
.

We call cut-bond any edge
e\in E(G)
whose removal disconnects G. The endpoints of a cut-bond are called a cut-points.

We denote
V_{\text{cut}}(G)
the set of cut-points of
G
, which we assume to be non-empty.

Let us now consider a sequence
(x_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
of points
V_{\text{cut}}(G)
. Fix
K\in\mathbb{N}
, we construct the graph
G(K)
in the following manner

(1)

the vertices of
G(K)
are the set of all cut-points that lie on a path from the root to an
x_{i}
for
i\leq K
,

(2)

two vertices of
G(K)
are adjacent if there exists a path connecting them which does not use any cut-point.

The new graph
G(K)
will be rooted at
\text{root}^{*}
which is the first cut-point on the path from the origin to
x_{1}
.

The removal of all cut-bonds in
G
results in a with graph several connected components. Those connected components are called bubbles and all cut-points in the same bubble are inter-connected in
G(K)
. This means that
G(K)
is a graph is composed of complete graphs glued together but single edges. This construction can be visualized in Figure 1.

Definition 3.2
.

We will say that a graph
G(K)
is tree-like if it does not contain any subgraph that is a complete graph apart from segments and triangles.

G
is turned into
G(4)
.

Remark 3.1
.

In the case of lattice trees, all edges are cut-bonds and all vertices are cut-points. In particular, the graph
G(K)
is always tree-like.

3.1.
Approximating a tree-like graph by a graph spatial tree

Let us assume that
G(K)
is tree-like. We are now going to perform a technical operation, that will be helpful to complete our proofs. In essence we are trying to build a graph spatial tree that will approximate
G(K)
well.

We want to turn the triangles present in
G(K)
into stars in order to turn out the tree-like graph into a tree, this procedure will add one point for every triangle present in the graph
G(K)
.

Remark 3.2
.

In the case of lattice trees, step 1 and step 2 described below are not necessary since
G(K)
has no actual bubbles. In the end, the graph
\mathcal{T}^{(G,K)}
constructed will simply be the subgraph of
G
composed by the union of the ancestral paths of the points
x_{1},\ldots,x_{K}
.

Step 1: Turning
G(K)
into a tree
\mathcal{T}^{(G,K)}

For every triangle
(x,y),(y,z),(z,x)\in E(G(K))
, we remove the edges
(x,y),(y,z),(z,x)
and we introduce a new vertex
v_{x,y,z}
and new edges
(x,v_{x,y,z})
,
(y,v_{x,y,z})
,
(z,v_{x,y,z})
. We denote
\mathcal{T}^{(G,K)}
, the tree obtained by this construction.

We denote
V(\mathcal{T}^{(G,K)})
the vertices of
\mathcal{T}^{(G,K)}
and
V^{*}(\mathcal{T}^{(G,K)})
the vertices which are not of the form
v_{x,y,z}
(which are actually the vertices of
G(K)
).

Similarly, we denote
E(\mathcal{T}^{(G,K)})
the edges of
\mathcal{T}^{(G,K)}
and
E^{*}(\mathcal{T}^{(G,K)})
the edges which are not of the form
(x,v_{x,y,z}),(y,v_{x,y,z}),(z,v_{x,y,z})
.

Finally, for
x,y\in V(\mathcal{T}^{(G,K)})
, we write
x\sim^{*}y
if there exists no
z\in V^{*}(\mathcal{T}^{(G,K)})
which lies on the path from
x
to
y
. This means that
x
and
y
were neighbours before the star-triangle transformation, or equivalently that they are connected by a bubble.

Since the tree
\mathcal{T}^{(G,K)}
is rooted (at
\text{root}^{*}
) it comes with a natural notion of ancestry. For
x\in\mathcal{T}^{(G,K)}
, we denote
\overrightarrow{\mathcal{T}^{(G,K)}_{x}}
, the set of points of
\mathcal{T}^{(G,K)}
which are descendants of
x
, including
x
.

Remark 3.3
.

In the case where
G
is a (lattice) tree, we have that
V^{*}(\mathcal{T}^{(G,K)})=V(\mathcal{T}^{(G,K)})
,
E^{*}(\mathcal{T}^{(G,K)})=E(\mathcal{T}^{(G,K)})
and
\text{root}^{*}=\text{root}
.

Step 2: Turning
\mathcal{T}^{(G,K)}
into a real tree by adding a metric

The tree
\mathcal{T}^{(G,K)}
comes with a natural metric by setting

(1)

for
(x,y)\in E^{*}(\mathcal{T}^{(G,K)})
, we set
d_{\mathcal{T}^{(G,K)}}(x,y)=d_{G}(x,y)
, where
d_{G}
is the graph distance,

(2)

for any triple of edges
(x,v_{x,y,z}),(y,v_{x,y,z}),(z,v_{x,y,z})
, where
x
is the ancestor of
y
and
z
, we set

d_{\mathcal{T}^{(G,K)}}(x,v_{x,y,z})=\frac{d_{G}(x,y)+d_{G}(x,z)-d_{G}(z,y)}{2},

d_{\mathcal{T}^{(G,K)}}(y,v_{x,y,z})=\frac{d_{G}(x,y)+d_{G}(y,z)-d_{G}(x,z)}{2}

and

d_{\mathcal{T}^{(G,K)}}(z,v_{x,y,z})=\frac{d_{G}(x,z)+d_{G}(y,z)-d_{G}(x,y)}{2}.

Note that this assignment of distances keeps consistency in the sense that

d_{G}(x,y)=d_{\mathcal{T}^{(G,K)}}(x,v_{x,y,z})+d_{\mathcal{T}^{(G,K)}}(y,v_{x,y,z}),

d_{G}(x,z)=d_{\mathcal{T}^{(G,K)}}(x,v_{x,y,z})+d_{\mathcal{T}^{(G,K)}}(z,v_{x,y,z})

and

d_{G}(y,z)=d_{\mathcal{T}^{(G,K)}}(y,v_{x,y,z})+d_{\mathcal{T}^{(G,K)}}(z,v_{x,y,z}).

(3)

the distance grows linearly along an edge.

Our choice for the distances in the second part is arbitrary but it will not have an significant impact on our proof. It can be noted that this distance conserves the distance from
\text{root}^{*}
to any point in
V^{*}(\mathcal{T}^{(G,K)})
.

Step 3: Assigning a spatial location to the points in
\mathcal{T}^{(G,K)}

Finally we want to view our tree as a spatial tree embedded in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, i.e. we want to find an embedding of the edges into
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Any vertex of
V^{*}(\mathcal{T}^{(G,K)})
is assigned its original location in
G
. Moreover the vertices
v_{x,y,z}
are mapped to the barycenter of
x
,
y
and
z
. We write
\phi^{G(K)}
this map.

If
(x,y)\in E(\mathcal{T}^{(G,K)})
, then the point
z
which is at a
d_{\mathcal{T}^{(G,K)}}
-distance
\alpha d_{\mathcal{T}^{(G,K)}}(x,y)
along the edge
(x,y)
is mapped to the point which is at distance
\alpha d_{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}(\phi^{G(K)}(x),\phi^{G(K)}(y))
along the
\mathbb{R}^{d}
-geodesic between
\phi^{G(K)}(x)
and
\phi^{G(K)}(y)
. This extends
\phi^{G(K)}
to a map from
\mathcal{T}^{(G,K)}
to
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

In particular the notation
\phi^{G(K)}(e)
, for
e\in E(\mathcal{T}^{(G,K)})
, corresponds to a segment of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Now if
T=T^{\prime}
, we have a homeomorphism
\Upsilon_{T,T^{\prime}}:\overline{T}\to\overline{T^{\prime}}
such that if
x\in\overline{T}
is at a distance
\alpha\left|e\right|
along the edge
e
, it is mapped to the point
x^{\prime}\in\overline{T^{\prime}}
which is at distance
\alpha\left|e^{\prime}\right|
along the corresponding edge
e^{\prime}
. We then set

d_{2}(T,T^{\prime}):=\sup_{x\in\overline{T}}d_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\psi(x),\psi^{\prime}(\Upsilon_{T,T^{\prime}}(x))).

This yields a metric

(4.4)

D((T,d,\psi),(T^{\prime},d^{\prime},\psi^{\prime})):=(d_{1}(T,T^{\prime})+d_{2}(T,T^{\prime}))\wedge 1

on graph spatial trees with ordered edges. This distance allows us to define our first condition (relevant definitions can be found at Definition
4.1
, Remark
4.1
and Section
2.4
).

Definition 4.2
.

Condition
(G)^{h}_{\sigma_{d},\sigma_{\phi}}
: We say that a sequence of random augmented graphs
(G_{n},(V_{i}^{n})_{i\in\mathbb{N}})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfies condition
(G)_{\sigma_{d},\sigma_{\phi}}^{h}
if

(1)

for all
K\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}{\mathbb{P}}[G^{(n,K)}\text{ is tree-like}]=1,

(2)

there exists
\sigma_{d},\sigma_{\phi}>0
such that for all
K\in\mathbb{N}
, the sequence of graph spatial trees
((T^{(n,K)},n^{-1}d_{G_{n}},n^{-1/2}\phi^{(n,K)}))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges weakly to
(\mathfrak{T}^{h,K},\sigma_{d}d_{\mathfrak{T}^{h,K}},\sigma_{\phi}\sqrt{\sigma_{d}}\phi_{\mathfrak{T}^{h,K}})
in the topology induced by
D
.

Remark 4.2
.

It is very important to stress that in condition
(G)^{h}
, the topology induced by
D
imposes a condition on the convergence of the length of only a finite number of edges (the
2K-1
edges of the asymptotically non-degenerate shape). This is where the distinction between
T^{(n,K)}
and
\mathcal{T}^{(n,K)}
makes a big difference.

Let us also introduce the strengthened version of condition
(G)^{h}
.

Definition 4.3
.

Condition
(G)^{h,+}
: We say that a sequence of random augmented graphs
(G_{n},(V_{i}^{n})_{i\in\mathbb{N}})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfies condition
(G)_{\sigma_{d},\sigma_{\phi},\nu}^{h,+}
if

(1)

for all
K\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}{\mathbb{P}}[G^{(n,K)}\text{ is tree-like}]=1,

(2)

there exists
\sigma_{d},\sigma_{\phi},\nu>0
such that for all
K\in\mathbb{N}
, the sequence of graph spatial trees
((T^{(n,K)},n^{-1}d_{G_{n}}(\cdot,\cdot),n^{-1/2}\phi^{(n,K)},\frac{\left|E(G_{n})\right|}{n^{2}}))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges weakly to
(\mathfrak{T}^{h,K},\sigma_{d}d_{\mathfrak{T}^{h,K}},\sigma_{\phi}\sqrt{\sigma_{d}}\phi_{\mathfrak{T}^{h,K}},\nu\lambda_{\mathfrak{T}^{h}}(\mathfrak{T}^{h}))
with the topology induced by
D
in the first three coordinates and the usual topology in
\mathbb{R}
in the last coordinate.

4.3.
Condition
(V)
: distribution of the volume on the graph

Define
\lambda^{(n,K)}
the Lebesgue measure on the graph spatial tree,
(\mathcal{T}^{(n,K)},d^{(n,K)})
, normalized to have total mass
1
. Recall that
\overrightarrow{\mathcal{T}^{(n,K)}_{x}}
are the descendants of
x
(including
x
itself) in
\mathcal{T}^{(n,K)}
and
\mu^{(n,K)}
was defined in Section
3.2
. Let us introduce the following definition.

Definition 4.4
.

Condition
(V)
:
For each
\varepsilon>0

\lim_{K\to\infty}\limsup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}{\mathbb{P}}\left[\sup_{x\in\mathcal{T}^{(n,K)}}\left|\lambda^{(n,K)}(\overrightarrow{\mathcal{T}_{x}^{(n,K)}})-\mu^{(n,K)}(\overrightarrow{\mathcal{T}^{(n,K)}_{x}})\right|\geq\varepsilon\right]=0.

4.3.1.
Condition
(R)
: the linearity of the resistance

Let
R_{\text{eff}}^{G_{n}}
denote the effective resistance in
G_{n}
. That is, we let each edge of
G_{n}
have unit resistance and, for a pair of vertices
x,y
,
R_{\text{eff}}^{G_{n}}(x,y)
is the electrical resistance between
x
and
y
in the electrical network just described.

Definition 4.5
.

Condition
(R)
: We say that a sequence of random augmented graphs
(G_{n},(V_{i}^{n})_{i\in\mathbb{N}})_{n\in N}
satisfies condition
(R)_{\rho}
if there exists
\rho>0
such that for all
\varepsilon>0
and for all
i\in\mathbb{N}

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\Bigl[\left|\frac{R_{\text{eff}}^{G_{n}}(0,V_{i}^{n})}{d_{G_{n}}(0,V_{i}^{n})}-\rho\right|>\varepsilon\Bigr]=0.

5.
Scaling limit results for simple random walks on critical graphs in high dimensions

In this section, we will state some of the main results of this article, which simplify the conditions under which we can deduce convergence towards the Brownian motion in the ISE.

The article
[
8
]
was dedicated to finding conditions under which simple random walks on critical graphs would converge towards the Brownian motion on the ISE (or minor variants of that process). One of the main results obtained there is the following.

Proposition 5.1
.

Under conditions
(G)^{h}_{\sigma_{d},\sigma_{\phi}}
,
(S)
,
(V)
and
(R)_{\rho}
we have that

(n^{-1/2}X^{G_{n}}_{n\left|E(G_{n})\right|t})_{t\geq 0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle n\to\infty}}{{\to}}(\sqrt{\sigma_{d}}\sigma_{\phi}B^{h\text{-ISE}}_{(\rho\sigma_{d})^{-1}\lambda_{\mathfrak{T}^{h}}(\mathfrak{T}^{h})t})_{t\geq 0},

where the convergence is annealed and occurs with the topology of uniform convergence over compact sets of time.

If, in addition, we also have the control on the cardinality of
G_{n}
, we can get rid of the dependence on
|E(G_{n})|
on the time scaling. The following corollary is a simple consequence of the theorem above.

Corollary 5.1
.

Under conditions
(G)^{h,+}_{\sigma_{d},\sigma_{\phi},\nu}
,
(S)
,
(V)
and
(R)_{\rho}
we have that

(n^{-1/2}X^{G_{n}}_{n^{3}t})_{t\geq 0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle n\to\infty}}{{\to}}(\sqrt{\sigma_{d}}\sigma_{\phi}B^{h\text{-ISE}}_{(\rho\sigma_{d}\nu)^{-1}t})_{t\geq 0},

where the convergence is annealed and occurs with the topology of uniform convergence over compact sets of time.

The conditions
(G)^{h}_{\sigma_{d},\sigma_{\phi}}
,
(S)
,
(V)
and
(R)_{\rho}
are linked to a choice of certain points
(V_{n}^{i})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in our graphs
G_{n}
. One of the main contributions of this article is to show that a specific choice of such points
(V_{n}^{i})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
leads to significant simplifications of those conditions.

5.1.
Simplified scaling limit theorem

For a set
B\subset V(G_{n})
, we denote by
B^{*}
the graph with vertices
B
and with edges
\{[x,y]\in E(G_{n}),\ x,y\in B\}
. Let us start by defining a specific class of points.

Definition 5.1
.

We say that the points
(V_{i}^{n})_{i\in\mathbb{N}},n\in\mathbb{N}
are chosen according to uniform edge-volume if, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
the sequence
(V_{i}^{n})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
is i.i.d. and

\lim_{n\to\infty}{\mathbb{P}}\left[\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}B\subset V(G_{n})\\
B^{*}\text{connected}\end{subarray}}\left|{\mathbb{P}}[V_{1}^{n}\in B\mid G_{n}]-\frac{\left|E(B^{*})\right|}{\left|E(G_{n})\right|}\right|>\varepsilon\right]=0

for all
\varepsilon>0
.

For points chosen in such a way, we do not need to verify condition
(V)
but we only require the much simpler condition that
\frac{|E(G_{n})|}{n^{2}}
converges to
\nu
(this hypothesis is the difference between
(G)^{h}_{\sigma_{d},\sigma_{\phi}}
and
(G)^{h,+}_{\sigma_{d},\sigma_{\phi},\nu}
). Next we present one of the main theorems of this article:

Theorem 5.1
.

Assume the points
(V_{i}^{n})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
are chosen according to uniform edge-volume.

(1)

Under conditions
(G)^{h}_{\sigma_{d},\sigma_{\phi}}
,
(S)
and
(R)_{\rho}
we have that

(n^{-1/2}X^{G_{n}}_{n\left|E(G_{n})\right|t})_{t\geq 0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle n\to\infty}}{{\to}}(\sqrt{\sigma_{d}}\sigma_{\phi}B^{h\text{-ISE}}_{(\rho\sigma_{d})^{-1}\lambda_{\mathfrak{T}^{h}}(\mathfrak{T}^{h})t})_{t\geq 0}.

(2)

Under conditions
(G)^{h,+}_{\sigma_{d},\sigma_{\phi},\nu}
,
(S)
and
(R)_{\rho}
we have that

(n^{-1/2}X^{G_{n}}_{n^{3}t})_{t\geq 0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle n\to\infty}}{{\to}}(\sqrt{\sigma_{d}}\sigma_{\phi}B^{h\text{-ISE}}_{(\nu\rho\sigma_{d})^{-1}t})_{t\geq 0},

where the convergences are annealed and occur with the topology of uniform convergence over compact sets of time.

The theorem stated just above will be used in Section
8
to show that the random walk on critical lattice trees, in high dimensions, converge to the Brownian motion on the ISE.

6.
Construction of an empirical measure to approximate condition
(V)

In this section, we aim to show that either of the conditions,
(G)_{\sigma_{d},\sigma_{\phi}}
or
(G)_{\sigma_{d},\sigma_{\phi}}^{h}
, imply condition
(V)
, when the sequence of points are chosen according to uniform edge volume. For the sake of clarity, we will lead the discussion with condition
(G)_{\sigma_{d},\sigma_{\phi}}
in mind, making the required remarks to deal with condition
(G)^{h}_{\sigma_{d},\sigma_{\phi}}
when necessary.

Condition
(V)
holds exactly for the ISE. That is, recalling from Section
2.4.2
that
\lambda^{(\mathfrak{T},K)}
is the normalized Lebesgue measure in
(\mathfrak{T}^{(K)},d_{\mathfrak{T}^{(K)}})
and letting
\mu^{(\mathfrak{T},K)}
be the measure in
\mathfrak{T}^{(K)}
defined as

\mu^{(\mathfrak{T},K)}(A)=\lambda_{\mathfrak{T}}(\{x\in\mathfrak{T}:\pi^{\mathfrak{T}^{(K)}}(x)\in A\}).

Then, we have that

\lim_{K\to\infty}{\mathbb{P}}\left[\sup_{x\in\mathfrak{T}^{(K)}}\left|\lambda^{(\mathfrak{T},K)}(\overrightarrow{\mathfrak{T}_{x}^{(K)}})-\mu^{(\mathfrak{T},K)}(\overrightarrow{\mathfrak{T}^{(K)}_{x}})\right|\geq\varepsilon\right]=0.

For a proof of the display above, see (
7.12
) in the proof of Proposition
7.1
.

Since condition
(G)_{\sigma_{d},\sigma_{\phi}}
tells us that
G_{n}
and the ISE are similar for
n
large, it is natural to believe that
(V)
should hold. The difficulty is that
(G)_{\sigma_{d},\sigma_{\phi}}
only gives us access to, loosely speaking, finite-dimensional information and and condition
(V)
is a uniform bound on the tree. The challenge will be to approximate condition
(V)
using less information.

For this, we recall that condition
(V)
is a statement on the measure
\mu^{(n,K)}
for large
n
. Our strategy will be to build an “empirical measure”
\hat{\mu}^{(n,K,K_{1},K_{2})}
that approximates
\mu^{(n,K)}
and which is measurable with respect to the shape
\mathcal{T}^{(n,K+K_{1}+K_{2})}
(and we will require no extra information about the volume of
\mathcal{T}^{(n,K)}
). In particular, this measurability condition means that
\hat{\mu}^{(n,K,K_{1},K_{2})}
can be understood for large
n
using condition
(G)_{\sigma_{d},\sigma_{\phi}}
. Our condition
(V)
will then become, up to error terms, a statement on
\hat{\mu}^{(n,K,K_{1},K_{2})}
. Then, by using condition
(G)_{\sigma_{d},\sigma_{\phi}}
, we will turn our discrete problem (linked to
\hat{\mu}^{(n,K,K_{1},K_{2})}
) into a question on the ISE (linked to a measure
\hat{\mu}^{(\mathfrak{T},K,K_{1},K_{2})}
). Finally, by the same argument as in the discrete case, this problem on the ISE will correspond, up to error terms, to proving condition
(V)
for the ISE (which as mentioned previously is trivially true).

6.1.
Building an empirical measure

Let us first describe our strategy for building the empirical measure. We will build the measure
\hat{\mu}^{(n,K,K_{1},K_{2})}
on
\mathcal{T}^{(n,K)}
in two steps.

(1)

First, we will find the location of the atoms of the measure. For this, we will first span a high number
K_{1}
of points chosen according to edge volume and look at their projection onto
\mathcal{T}^{(n,K)}
. This number
K_{1}
is chosen to ensure that, with high probability, most of the edge-volume of
G_{n}
projected onto
\mathcal{T}^{(n,K+K_{1})}
will be located on the newly added branches (
\mathcal{T}^{(n,K+K_{1})}\setminus\mathcal{T}^{(n,K)}
).

(2)

Then, we will find the edge-volume that should be associated to each of the atoms of the measure. This is done by adding a sufficiently high number of points
K_{2}
such that we can do a precise empirical approximation of the edge-volume of
G_{n}
which will be projected onto each one of the atoms of

\hat{\mu}^{(n,K,K_{1},K_{2})}
. This will essentially follow from the law of large numbers.

6.1.1.
Location of the atoms of
\mu^{(n,K,K_{1},K_{2})}

For any
K_{1}>0
, we will consider the tree
\mathcal{T}^{(n,K+K_{1})}
. We introduce the following notations (see Figure
2
):

(1)

y^{(n,K,K_{1})}_{1},\ldots,y^{(n,K,K_{1})}_{l^{(n,K,K_{1})}}
the points of
\mathcal{T}^{(n,K)}
which are of degree
3
in
\mathcal{T}^{(n,K+K_{1})}
but not in
\mathcal{T}^{(n,K)}
. This means that we consider the branching vertices that were added to
\mathcal{T}^{(n,K)}
in order to be able to connect the
K_{1}
points added in
\mathcal{T}^{(n,K+K_{1})}
. We point out that
l^{(n,K,K_{1})}\leq K_{1}
.

(2)

for any
i\leq l^{(n,K,K_{1})}
, we denote
s^{(n,K,K_{1})}_{i}
to be the set of points of
\mathcal{T}^{(n,K+K_{1})}
which are separated from
0
by
y^{(n,K,K_{1})}_{i}
(including
y^{(n,K,K_{1})}_{i}
). It is clear that
s^{(n,K,K_{1})}_{i}
forms a tree which is connected.

(3)

S^{(n,K,K_{1})}
is formed by the union of all
s^{(n,K,K_{1})}_{i}
for
i\leq l^{(n,K,K_{1})}
. This is hence a forest with at most
K_{1}
connected components.

Remark 6.1
.

We can notice that
\{\pi^{(n,K)}(z),\ z\in S^{(n,K,K_{1})}\}=\{y^{(n,K,K_{1})}_{i},\ i\leq l^{(n,K,K_{1})}\}
.

Remark 6.2
.

A similar construction can be made in the limiting setting, i.e. on the continuum random tree
\mathfrak{T}
(and
\mathfrak{T}^{h}
). This will lead to analogous notations,
S^{(K,K_{1})}
,
(y_{i}^{(K,K_{1})})_{i\leq l^{(K,K_{1})}}
and
(s_{i}^{(K,K_{1})})_{i\leq l^{(K,K_{1})}}
.

Those definitions are illustrated in Figure 2.

Figure 2.
Notations associated to the construction of the atoms of the measure
\hat{\mu}^{(n,K,K_{1},K_{2})}

We have already defined the projection of vertices at the beginning of the Section
4
. We will extend this definition to edges in the following way: let
e=[x,y]
be an edge of
E(G_{n})
and
K^{\prime}
any positive integer then

•

if
\pi^{(n,K^{\prime})}(x)=\pi^{(n,K^{\prime})}(y)
then we set
\pi^{(n,K^{\prime})}(e)=\pi^{(n,K^{\prime})}(x)
,

•

if
\pi^{(n,K^{\prime})}(x)\neq\pi^{(n,K^{\prime})}(y)
then if
\pi^{(n,K^{\prime})}(x)
is the ancestor of
\pi^{(n,K^{\prime})}(y)
in
\mathcal{T}^{(n,K^{\prime})}
we set
\pi^{(n,K^{\prime})}(e)=\pi^{(n,K^{\prime})}(x)
and in the other case we set
\pi^{(n,K^{\prime})}(e)=\pi^{(n,K^{\prime})}(y)
.

This means that
\pi^{(n,K^{\prime})}(e)
is the cut-point closest to
e
such that from either endpoint of
e
we have to go through
\pi^{(n,K^{\prime})}(e)
to go to the origin.

Let us start by a technical result.

Lemma 6.1
.

Assume that either of Conditions
(G)_{\sigma_{d},\sigma_{\phi}}
or
(G)_{\sigma_{d},\sigma_{\phi}}^{h}
are verified with points chosen according to uniform edge-volume. Fix
K<\infty
, then for any
M<\infty
there exists
C_{1}(M,K)<\infty
such that for any
K_{1}\geq C_{1}
,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}{\mathbb{P}}[\text{ for all }i\leq M^{1/2},\ \pi^{(n,K+K_{1})}(V^{n}_{K+K_{1}+i})\in S^{(n,K,K_{1})}]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\bigl(1-\frac{1}{M}\bigr)\exp\bigl(-\frac{2}{M^{1/2}}\bigr).

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{T}
denote the normalized CRT, i.e., the one conditioned on having unit volume. Let us introduce the event
A(K,K_{1},L)=\{|S^{(K,K_{1})}|_{d_{\mathfrak{T}}}\geq L|\mathfrak{T}^{(K)}|_{d_{\mathfrak{T}}}\}
, where
|\cdot|_{d_{\mathfrak{T}}}
denotes the length according to
d_{\mathfrak{T}}
. We will start by showing that for any
L<\infty
and
\eta>0
, there exists
K^{\ast}(\eta,L)
such that for all
K_{1}>K^{\ast}(\eta,L)
,

(6.1)

{\mathbb{P}}[A(K,K_{1},L)]>1-\eta.

It is immediate from the line-breaking construction of
\mathfrak{T}
(see Section
) that the total length of
\mathfrak{T}^{(K_{1})}
diverges with
K_{1}
, almost surely.
Also, for fixed
K
, the length of
\mathfrak{T}^{(K)}
is almost surely finite.
Therefore, since
\left|S^{(K,K_{1})}\right|_{d_{\mathfrak{T}}}=\left|{\mathfrak{T}}^{(K_{1})}\right|_{d_{\mathfrak{T}}}-\left|{\mathfrak{T}}^{(K)}\right|_{d_{\mathfrak{T}}}
, it follows that
\left|S^{(K,K_{1})}\right|_{d_{\mathfrak{T}}}
diverges with
K_{1}
(for fixed
K
). Display (
6.1
) follows immediately.

Using the line-breaking construction again, we see that for any
i\geq 1
the point
\pi^{\mathfrak{T}^{(K+K_{1})}}(V_{K+K_{1}+i})
is uniformly distributed on
\mathfrak{T}^{(K+K_{1})}
which by (
6.1
) implies that,

(6.2)

{\mathbb{P}}[\pi^{\mathfrak{T}^{(K+K_{1})}}(V_{K+K_{1}+i})\in S^{(K,K_{1})}\mid A(K,K_{1},L)]\geq\frac{L}{L+1}.

Now, for any
j\geq 1
, let us denote the event

A_{j}(K,K_{1},L):=\bigcap_{1\leq i\leq j}\{\pi^{\mathfrak{T}^{(K+K_{1})}}(V_{K+K_{1}+j})\in S^{(K,K_{1})}\}\cap A(K,K_{1},L),

which we extend by setting
A_{0}(K,K_{1},M):=A(K,K_{1},M)
.

Since
(V_{l})_{l\geq 0}
is an i.i.d. family, we know that
\{\pi^{\mathfrak{T}^{(K+K_{1})}}(V_{K+K_{1}+j+1})\in S^{(K,K_{1})}\}
depends on
(V_{l})_{l\leq K+K_{1}}
(to define
\pi^{\mathfrak{T}^{(K+K_{1})}}
and
S^{(K,K_{1})}
) and on
V_{K+K_{1}+j+1}
, but is independent of
(V_{l})_{K+K_{1}+j\geq l>K+K_{1}}
. On the event
A_{j}(K,K_{1},L)
, we know that we have
|S^{(K,K_{1}+j)}|_{d_{\mathfrak{T}}}\geq L|\mathfrak{T}^{(K)}|_{d_{\mathfrak{T}}}
and this means that the line-breaking construction implies

(6.3)

{\mathbb{P}}[\pi^{\mathfrak{T}^{(K+K_{1})}}(V_{K+K_{1}+j+1})\in S^{(K,K_{1})}\mid A_{j}(K,K_{1},L)]\geq\frac{L}{L+1}.

Hence, (
6.2
) and (
6.3
) imply by induction that

{\mathbb{P}}[\text{ for all }i\leq j,\ \pi^{\mathfrak{T}^{(K+K_{1})}}(V_{K+K_{1}+i})\in S^{(K,K_{1})}\mid A(K,K_{1},L)]\geq\Bigl(\frac{L}{L+1}\Bigr)^{j}.

Applying for the previous equation for
j=M^{1/2}
, we obtain

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}[\text{ for all }i\leq M^{1/2},\ \pi^{\mathfrak{T}^{(K+K_{1})}}(V_{K+K_{1}+i})\in S^{(K,K_{1})}\mid A(K,K_{1},L)]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\bigl(1-\frac{1}{L+1}\bigr)^{M^{1/2}},

and recalling (
6.1
) we obtain

{\mathbb{P}}[\text{ for all }i\leq M^{1/2},\ \pi^{\mathfrak{T}^{(K+K_{1})}}(V_{K+K_{1}+i})\in S^{(K,K_{1})}]\geq\bigl(1-\eta\bigr)\bigl(1-\frac{1}{L+1}\bigr)^{M^{1/2}}.

Since
\eta
and
L
are arbitrary, and the equation above holds for all
K_{1}\geq K^{\ast}(\eta,L)
, it follows that, for any
M\geq 0
,

(6.4)

\liminf_{K_{1}\to\infty}{\mathbb{P}}[\text{ for all }i\leq M^{1/2},\ \pi^{\mathfrak{T}^{(K+K_{1})}}(V_{K+K_{1}+i})\in S^{(K,K_{1})}]=1.

Now we turn our focus to the discrete.
We can notice that the event
\{\text{For all }i\leq M^{1/2},\ \pi^{T^{(n,K+K_{1})}}(V^{n}_{K+K_{1}+i})\in S^{(n,K,K_{1})}\}
is measurable with respect the shape of
T^{(n,K+K_{1}+M^{1/2})}
(recall the definition of shape in Section
4.2
). This leads us to use condition
(G)_{\sigma_{d},\sigma_{\phi}}^{h}
in order to show that, with probability going to 1 as
n
goes to infinity,
T^{(n,K+K_{1}+M^{1/2})}
and
T^{(K+K_{1}+M^{1/2})}
have the same shape, which implies

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}{\mathbb{P}}[\text{ for all }i\leq M^{1/2},\ \pi^{T^{(n,K+K_{1})}}(V^{n}_{K+K_{1}+i})\in S^{(n,K,K_{1})}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}[\text{ for all }i\leq M^{1/2},\ \pi^{\mathfrak{T}^{(K+K_{1})}}(V_{K+K_{1}+i})\in S^{(K,K_{1})}]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\bigl(1-\frac{1}{M}\bigr)\exp\bigl(-\frac{2}{M^{1/2}}\bigr),

where we used the previous equation. This proves the lemma for condition
(G)_{\sigma_{d},\sigma_{\phi}}
.

In order to get the lemma under condition
(G)_{\sigma_{d},\sigma_{\phi}}^{h}
, it suffices to obtain (
6.4
) for
\mathfrak{T}^{h}
, the CRT conditioned on having height larger than
h
, instead of
\mathfrak{T}
. The rest of the proof is completely analogous.
It will be helpful to write
\mathfrak{T}^{h}
as a mixture of CRTs conditioned on a fixed volume and on having height greater than
h
. More precisely, let
\mathfrak{T}_{v}
be the CRT conditioned on having volume exactly equal to
v
. This is the same as conditioning the underlying Brownian excursion to have duration
v
. Moreover,
\mathfrak{T}_{v}
can be obtained from the normalized CRT via scaling, i.e.,
\mathfrak{T}_{v}=(\mathfrak{T},\sqrt{v}d_{\mathfrak{T}},v\lambda_{\mathfrak{T}})
.
Now, let
\mathfrak{T}_{v}^{h}
be
\mathfrak{T}_{v}
conditioned on having height greater than
h
. Since, for any
v\geq 0
,
\mathbb{P}[h(\mathfrak{T}_{v})\geq h]>0
, this conditioning can be defined in the standard way

\mathbb{P}[\mathfrak{T}_{v}^{h}\in A]:=\frac{\mathbb{P}[\mathfrak{T}_{v}\in A,h(\mathfrak{T}_{v})\geq h]}{\mathbb{P}[h(\mathfrak{T}_{v})\geq h]}

for any measurable set
A
. The measurability of
A
means that it is a Borelian subset of the set of ordered trees that can be constructed using a continuous excursion, as in Section
2.1
, endowed with the topology inherited from the uniform convergence of the corresponding excursions.
Let
\nu
be the distribution of the total volume of
\mathfrak{T}^{h}
. The decomposition of
\mathfrak{T}^{h}
we were after is

(6.5)

\mathbb{P}\left[\mathfrak{T}^{h}\in A\right]=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}[\mathfrak{T}_{v}^{h}\in A]\nu(dv),

for all measurable
A
.

It follows from (
6.4
) and the scaling relation between
\mathfrak{T}
and
\mathfrak{T}_{v}
that

(6.6)

\liminf_{K_{1}\to\infty}{\mathbb{P}}[\text{ for all }i\leq M^{1/2},\ \pi^{\mathfrak{T}_{v}^{(K+K_{1})}}(V_{K+K_{1}+i})\in S^{(K,K_{1})}]=1

Also, using that
\mathfrak{T}^{h}_{v}
is defined as
\mathfrak{T}_{v}
conditioned on a positive probability event, we get from the display above that

(6.7)

\liminf_{K_{1}\to\infty}{\mathbb{P}}[\text{ for all }i\leq M^{1/2},\ \pi^{\mathfrak{T}_{v}^{h,K+K_{1}}}(V_{K+K_{1}+i})\in S^{(K,K_{1})}]=1.

Finally, using (
6.5
), we can deduce from the above that

(6.8)

\liminf_{K_{1}\to\infty}{\mathbb{P}}[\text{ for all }i\leq M^{1/2},\ \pi^{\mathfrak{T}^{h,K+K_{1}}}(V_{K+K_{1}+i})\in S^{(K,K_{1})}]=1.

We finish the proof for condition
(G)_{\sigma_{d},\sigma_{\phi}}^{h}
in the same way that we did for condition
(G)_{\sigma_{d},\sigma_{\phi}}
, using the display above instead of (
6.4
).
∎

We will now prove that most of the edge-volume of
G_{n}
projected on
\mathcal{T}^{(n,K+K_{1})}
is projected on
S^{(n,K,K_{1})}
(i.e. on the
K_{1}
last branches).

Lemma 6.2
.

Assume that either of conditions
(G)_{\sigma_{d},\sigma_{\phi}}
or
(G)_{\sigma_{d},\sigma_{\phi}}^{h}
are verified with points chosen according to uniform edge-volume. Fix
K<\infty
, then for any
\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime}>0
, there exists
C_{1}(\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime},K)<\infty
such that for any
K_{1}\geq C_{1}
we have

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}{\mathbb{P}}[\left|\{e\in E(G_{n}),\pi^{(n,K+K_{1})}(e)\in S^{(n,K,K_{1})}\}\right|\geq(1-\varepsilon)|E(G_{n})|]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 1-\varepsilon^{\prime}.

Proof.

Fix
\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime}>0
. Let
B\subset V(G_{n})
and recall from the beginning of Section
5.1
the definition of the subgraph
B^{*}
. Let us denote the event that

(6.9)

A_{\varepsilon}(n):=\Bigl\{\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}B\subset V(G_{n})\\
B^{*}\text{connected}\end{subarray}}\left|{\mathbb{P}}[V_{1}^{n}\in B\mid G_{n}]-\frac{\left|E(B^{*})\right|}{\left|E(G_{n})\right|}\right|<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\Bigr\},

which occurs with high probability for
n
large enough since the
V_{i}^{n}
are chosen according to edge volume.

Recall that the points
V_{i}^{n}
are chosen in an i.i.d. fashion. We denote
\tilde{S}^{(n,K,K_{1})}:=\{x\in G_{n},\ \pi^{(n,K+K_{1})}(x)\in S^{(n,K,K_{1})}\}
. We know that
\tilde{S}^{(n,K,K_{1})}
has at most
K_{1}
connected components, so we can notice that on
A_{\varepsilon/K_{1}}
we have that
\left|{\mathbb{P}}[V_{K+K_{1}+i}^{n}\in\tilde{S}^{(n,K,K_{1})}\mid G_{n}]-\frac{|E[\tilde{S}^{(n,K,K_{1})}]|}{|E(G_{n})|}\right|\leq\varepsilon/2
for any
i\geq 0
.

Hence, on the event
A_{\varepsilon/K_{1}}(n)
(which is measurable with respect to
G_{n}
), we have for any
i\geq 0

{\mathbb{P}}[V^{n}_{K+K_{1}+i}\in\tilde{S}^{(n,K,K_{1})}\mid G_{n},\ (V^{n}_{i})_{i\leq K+K_{1}}]\leq\frac{|E(\tilde{S}^{(n,K,K_{1})})|}{|E(G_{n})|}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2},

and since the points
V^{n}_{K+K_{1}+i}
are chosen independently, we have that, on
A_{\varepsilon/K_{1}}(n)
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}[\text{ for all }i\leq M^{1/2},\ \pi^{T^{(n,K+K_{1})}}(V^{n}_{K+K_{1}+i})\in S^{(n,K,K_{1})}\mid G_{n},\ (V^{n}_{i})_{i\leq K+K_{1}}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Bigl(\frac{|E(\tilde{S}^{(n,K,K_{1})})|}{|E(G_{n})|}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\Bigr)^{M^{1/2}},

by the second point of Remark
6.1
.

Let us denote

B_{\varepsilon}(n):=\left\{\left|\{e\in E(G_{n}),\pi^{(n,K+K_{1})}(e)\in S^{(n,K,K_{1})}\}\right|\leq(1-\varepsilon)\left|E(G_{n})\right|\right\},

then, we can see that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}[\text{ for all }i\leq M^{1/2},\ \pi^{(n,K+K_{1})}(V^{n}_{K+K_{1}+i})\in S^{(n,K,K_{1})}\mid A_{\varepsilon/K_{1}}(n)\cap B_{\varepsilon}(n)]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1-\varepsilon/2)^{M^{1/2}}\leq\exp(-\varepsilon M^{1/2}/2).

Putting together the previous elements, we can see that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left[B_{\varepsilon}(n)\right]\leq

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}[\text{ for some }i\leq M^{1/2},\ \pi^{(n,K+K_{1})}(V^{n}_{K+K_{1}+i})\notin S^{(n,K,K_{1})}]

\displaystyle+{\mathbb{P}}[B_{\varepsilon}(n);\text{ for all }i\leq M^{1/2},\ \pi^{(n,K+K_{1})}(V^{n}_{K+K_{1}+i})\in S^{(n,K,K_{1})}]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}[\text{ for some }i\leq M^{1/2},\ \pi^{(n,K+K_{1})}(V^{n}_{K+K_{1}+i})\notin S^{(n,K,K_{1})}]+{\mathbb{P}}[A_{\varepsilon/K_{1}}(n)^{c}]

\displaystyle+{\mathbb{P}}[B_{\varepsilon}(n)\cap A_{\varepsilon/K_{1}}(n);\text{ for all }i\leq M^{1/2},\ \pi^{(n,K+K_{1})}(V^{n}_{K+K_{1}+i})\in S^{(n,K,K_{1})}]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}[\text{ for some }i\leq M^{1/2},\ \pi^{(n,K+K_{1})}(V^{n}_{K+K_{1}+i})\notin S^{(n,K,K_{1})}]+{\mathbb{P}}[A_{\varepsilon/K_{1}}(n)^{c}]

\displaystyle+{\mathbb{P}}[\text{ for all }i\leq M^{1/2},\ \pi^{(n,K+K_{1})}(V^{n}_{K+K_{1}+i})\in S^{(n,K,K_{1})}|B_{\varepsilon}(n)\cap A_{\varepsilon/K_{1}}(n)]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}[\text{ for some }i\leq M^{1/2},\ \pi^{(n,K+K_{1})}(V^{n}_{K+K_{1}+i})\notin S^{(n,K,K_{1})}]

\displaystyle+{\mathbb{P}}[A_{\varepsilon/K_{1}}(n)]+\exp(-\varepsilon M^{1/2}/2),

where the second term goes to 0 by Definition
5.1
, and then by Lemma
6.1
we know that for any
M
there exists
K_{1}
large enough such that

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}{\mathbb{P}}[B_{\varepsilon}(n)]\leq 1-(1-\frac{1}{M})\exp(-\frac{2}{M^{1/2}})+\exp(-\varepsilon M^{1/2}/2),

and we can see that for any
\varepsilon^{\prime}>0
there exists an
M
large enough (provided we take
K_{1}
large enough) such that right-hand side is lower than
\varepsilon^{\prime}
. This proves the lemma.
∎

6.1.2.
Assigning a weight to each atom of
\hat{\mu}^{(n,K,K_{1},K_{2})}

Our goal in this section is to estimate the amount of mass that should be put to each atom
y^{(n,K,K_{1})}_{i}
of
\hat{\mu}^{(n,K)}
of
\hat{\mu}^{(n,K,K_{1},K_{2})}
. We want to do this using information depending only on
\mathcal{T}^{(n,K^{\prime})}
for some large
K^{\prime}
.

Set

\hat{\mu}^{(n,K,K_{1},K_{2})}(y^{(n,K,K_{1})}_{i})=\frac{\left|\{j\leq K_{2},\ \pi^{(n,K)}(V^{n}_{K+K_{1}+j})=y^{(n,K,K_{1})}_{i}\}\right|}{K_{2}}.

and recall that for any
y\in V^{*}(\mathcal{T}^{(n,K)})

\mu^{(n,K)}(y)=\frac{\left|\{e\in E(G_{n}),\ \pi^{(n,K)}(e)=y\}\right|}{|E(G_{n})|}.

In essence we span
K_{1}
point to create the locations of the atoms and then an extra
K_{2}
points to assign a weight to those atoms.

Lemma 6.3
.

Assume that either of conditions
(G)_{\sigma_{d},\sigma_{\phi}}
or
(G)_{\sigma_{d},\sigma_{\phi}}^{h}
are verified with points chosen according to uniform edge-volume.
For any
K,K_{1}<\infty
and
\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime}>0
, there exists
C_{2}(K,K_{1},\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime})
such that for any
K_{2}\geq C_{2}
we have

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}{\mathbb{P}}\Bigl[\text{ for all $i\leq l^{(K,K_{1})}$},\ \left|\mu^{(n,K)}(y^{(n,K,K_{1})}_{i})-\hat{\mu}^{(n,K,K_{1},K_{2})}(y^{(n,K,K_{1})}_{i})\right|<\frac{\varepsilon}{K_{1}}\Bigr]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 1-\varepsilon^{\prime}.

Proof.

Recall the definition of
A_{\varepsilon}(n)
from (
6.9
).

Denote
\tilde{s}_{i}^{(n,K,K_{1})}:=\{x\in G_{n},\ \pi^{(n,K+K_{1})}(x)\in s_{i}^{(n,K,K_{1})}\}
. Since for all
i\leq l^{(K,K_{1})}
the set
\tilde{s}_{i}^{(n,K,K_{1})}
is connected we know that on
A_{\varepsilon}(n)
we have
\left|{\mathbb{P}}[V_{1}^{n}\in\tilde{s}_{i}^{(n,K,K_{1})}\mid G_{n}]-\frac{\left|E(\tilde{s}_{i}^{(n,K,K_{1})})\right|}{\left|E(G_{n})\right|}\right|<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
. Now, by the second point of Remark
6.1
,
\mu^{(n,K)}(y_{i}^{(n,K,K_{1})})=\frac{\left|E(\tilde{s}_{i}^{(n,K,K_{1})})\right|}{\left|E(G_{n})\right|}
, this means that, on
A_{\varepsilon}(n)
,

\left|{\mathbb{P}}[V_{K+K_{1}+j}^{n}\in\tilde{s}_{i}^{(n,K,K_{1})}\mid G_{n},(V^{n}_{j})_{j\leq K+K_{1}}]-\mu^{(n,K)}(y_{i}^{(n,K,K_{1})})\right|<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}

for any
j\geq 1
.

For any
1\leq i\leq l^{(K,K_{1})}
, let us notice that, conditionally on
G_{n}
and
(V^{n}_{j})_{j\leq K+K_{1}}
, we have that
\hat{\mu}^{(n,K,K_{1},K_{2})}(y_{i}^{(n,K,K_{1})})=\frac{1}{K_{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{K_{2}}B_{i}
, where
B_{i}
are i.i.d. random variables such that
P[B_{i}=1]=1-P[B_{i}=0]={\mathbb{P}}[V_{K+K_{1}+i}\in\tilde{s}_{i}^{(n,K,K_{1})}\mid G_{n},(V^{n}_{j})_{j\leq K+K_{1}}]
(here we used the first point of Remark
6.1
). This means, by Markov’s inequality, for all
i\leq l^{(K,K_{1})}

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\Bigl[\left|\hat{\mu}^{(n,K+K_{1}+K_{2})}(y_{i}^{(n,K,K_{1})})-\mu^{(n,K)}(y_{i}^{(n,K,K_{1})})\right|>\varepsilon\Big|A_{\varepsilon}(n)\Bigr]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\Bigl[\left|\hat{\mu}^{(n,K+K_{1}+K_{2})}(y_{i}^{(n,K,K_{1})})-{\mathbb{P}}[V_{K+K_{1}+i}^{n}\in\tilde{s}_{i}^{(n,K,K_{1})}\mid G_{n},(V^{n}_{j})_{j\leq K+K_{1}}]\right|>\varepsilon/2\Big|A_{\varepsilon}(n)\Bigr]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{4}{\varepsilon^{2}K_{2}}.

We can use the previous computation to see that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\Bigl[\exists i\leq l^{(K,K_{1})},\ \left|\mu^{(n,K)}(y_{i}^{(n,K,K_{1})})-\hat{\mu}^{(n,K,K_{1},K_{2})}(y_{i}^{(n,K,K_{1})})\right|>\frac{\varepsilon}{K_{1}}\Bigr]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\Bigl[\sum_{i=1}^{l^{(K,K_{1})}}{\mathbb{P}}\Bigl[\left|\mu^{(n,K)}(y_{i}^{(n,K,K_{1})})-\hat{\mu}^{(n,K,K_{1},K_{2})}(y_{i}^{(n,K,K_{1})})\right|>\frac{\varepsilon}{K_{1}}\mid A_{\varepsilon}(n)\Bigr]\Bigr]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+{\mathbb{P}}[A_{\varepsilon}(n)^{c}]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\Bigl[\sum_{i=1}^{l^{(K,K_{1})}}\frac{4K_{1}^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}K_{2}}\Bigr]+{\mathbb{P}}[A_{\varepsilon}(n)^{c}]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{4K_{1}^{3}}{\varepsilon^{2}K_{2}}+{\mathbb{P}}[A_{\varepsilon}(n)^{c}],

since
l^{(K,K_{1})}\leq K_{1}
. By taking
n
to infinity the second probability goes to 0 by Definition
5.1
and then we choose
K_{2}
large enough to make this quantity smaller than
\varepsilon^{\prime}
, which proves the lemma.
∎

6.2.
Conclusion

By construction the measure
\hat{\mu}^{(n,K,K_{1},K_{2})}
is measurable with respect to
\mathcal{T}^{(n,K+K_{1}+K_{2})}
and has support on
\mathcal{T}^{(n,K)}
.
Its other key property can be obtained by combining Lemma
6.2
and Lemma
6.3
to prove the following. Let
d_{TV}
denote the total variation distance between measures.

Lemma 6.4
.

Assume that either of conditions
(G)_{\sigma_{d},\sigma_{\phi}}
or
(G)_{\sigma_{d},\sigma_{\phi}}^{h}
are verified with points chosen according to uniform edge-volume.
For any
\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime}>0
and
K<\infty
there exists
C_{1}(\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime},K)<\infty
, such that, for any
K_{1}\geq C_{1}
there exists
C_{2}(\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime},K,K_{1})<\infty
, such that, for any
K_{2}\geq C_{2}
we have that

\limsup_{n\to\infty}{\mathbb{P}}[d_{TV}(\hat{\mu}^{(n,K,K_{1},K_{2})},\mu^{(n,K)})\geq\varepsilon]\leq\varepsilon^{\prime}.

Proof.

Fix
\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime}>0
. Let
A
be a subset of
\mathcal{T}^{(n,K)}
, then

\displaystyle\hat{\mu}^{(n,K,K_{1},K_{2})}(A)=

\displaystyle\hat{\mu}^{(n,K,K_{1},K_{2})}(A\setminus(\cup_{i=1}^{l^{(n,K,K_{1})}}y_{i}^{(n,K,K_{1})}))

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{i=1}^{l^{(n,K,K_{1})}}{\bf 1}_{\{y_{i}^{(n,K,K_{1})}\in A\}}\hat{\mu}^{(n,K,K_{1},K_{2})}(y_{i}^{(n,K,K_{1})}),

with a similar formula holding for
\mu^{(n,K)}
. This means that the total variation

\displaystyle\max_{A\subset\mathcal{T}^{(n,K)}}\left|\hat{\mu}^{(n,K,K_{1},K_{2})}(A)-\mu^{(n,K)}(A)\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\max_{A\subset\mathcal{T}^{(n,K)}}\left|\hat{\mu}^{(n,K,K_{1},K_{2})}(A\setminus(\cup_{i=1}^{l^{(n,K,K_{1})}}y_{i}^{(n,K,K_{1})}))-\mu^{(n,K)}(A\setminus(\cup_{i=1}^{l^{(n,K,K_{1})}}y_{i}^{(n,K,K_{1})}))\right|

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{l^{(n,K,K_{1})}}\left|\hat{\mu}^{(n,K,K_{1},K_{2})}(y_{i}^{(n,K,K_{1})})-\mu^{(n,K)}(y_{i}^{(n,K,K_{1})})\right|.

Firstly, by Lemma
6.3
and the fact that
l^{(n,K,K_{1})}\leq K_{1}
we know that

\limsup_{n\to\infty}{\mathbb{P}}\Bigl[\sum_{i=1}^{l^{(n,K,K_{1})}}\left|\hat{\mu}^{(n,K,K_{1},K_{2})}(y_{i}^{(n,K,K_{1})})-\mu^{(n,K)}(y_{i}^{(n,K,K_{1})})\right|\geq\varepsilon\Bigr]\leq\varepsilon^{\prime},

Secondly, we have
\hat{\mu}^{(n,K,K_{1},K_{2})}(A\setminus(\cup_{i=1}^{l^{(n,K,K_{1})}}y_{i}^{(n,K,K_{1})}))=0
, and by Lemma
6.2
we know that

\limsup_{n\to\infty}{\mathbb{P}}\Bigl[\max_{A\subset\mathcal{T}^{(n,K)}}\mu^{(n,K)}(A\setminus(\cup_{i=1}^{l^{(n,K,K_{1})}}y_{i}^{(n,K,K_{1})}))\geq\varepsilon\Bigr]\leq\varepsilon^{\prime}.

Combining the last three equations we can see that

\limsup_{n\to\infty}{\mathbb{P}}\Bigl[d_{TV}(\hat{\mu}^{(n,K,K_{1},K_{2})},\mu^{(n,K)})\geq 2\varepsilon\Bigr]\leq 2\varepsilon^{\prime},

which implies the result.
∎

.

.

to the continuous setting to get

.

For any
\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime}>0

C_{2}(\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime},K,K_{1})<\infty

7.2.
A preliminary result

By condition
(G)_{\sigma_{d},\sigma_{\phi}}
and the Skorohod representation Theorem, we can assume that, for all

(G_{n},(V^{n}_{i})_{i=1,\dots,K})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
\mathfrak{T}^{(K)}
are defined in a common probability space
(\Omega^{(K)},\mathcal{F}^{(K)},{\mathbf{P}}^{(K)})
and that

(7.1)

(T^{(n,K)},d^{(n,K)},n^{-1/2}\phi_{\mathcal{T}_{n}})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle n\to\infty}}{{\to}}(\mathfrak{T}^{(K)},\sigma_{d}d_{\mathfrak{T}^{(K)}},\sigma_{\phi}\sqrt{\sigma_{d}}\phi_{\mathfrak{T}^{(K)}})\qquad{\mathbf{P}}^{(K)}\text{-a.s.},

in the topology induced by
D
, where
D
is the natural topology on graph spatial trees (see display (
4.4
)).

Similarly, under condition
(G)_{\sigma_{d},\sigma_{\phi}}^{h}

large enough we have that

(7.19)

{\bf P}^{(K)}\left[\max_{i=1,\dots,2K_{3}-1}\left|\lambda^{(\mathfrak{T},K)}(\overrightarrow{\mathfrak{T}}_{a_{i}})-\lambda_{\mathfrak{T}}(\overrightarrow{\mathfrak{T}}_{a_{i}})\right|\geq\varepsilon/3\right]\geq 1-\frac{\eta}{8}.

Let
x\in\mathfrak{T}
, let
i\in\{1,\dots,2K_{3}-1\}
be the index such that
\pi^{\mathfrak{T}^{(K_{3})}}(x)\in[a_{i},b_{i}]
. We will discern according to whether
x\in\mathfrak{T}^{(K_{3})}
or not. Assume
x\in\mathfrak{T}^{(K_{3})}
. We write

(7.20)

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in\mathfrak{T}^{(K_{3})}}\left|\lambda^{(\mathfrak{T},K)}(\overrightarrow{\mathfrak{T}}_{x})-\mu^{(\mathfrak{T},K)}(\overrightarrow{\mathfrak{T}}_{x})\right|\leq\sup_{x\in\mathfrak{T}^{(K_{3})}}\left|\lambda^{(\mathfrak{T},K)}(\overrightarrow{\mathfrak{T}}_{x})-\lambda^{(\mathfrak{T},K)}(\overrightarrow{\mathfrak{T}}_{a_{i}})\right|

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in\mathfrak{T}^{(K_{3})}}\left|\lambda^{(\mathfrak{T},K)}(\overrightarrow{\mathfrak{T}}_{a_{i}})-\mu^{(\mathfrak{T},K)}(\overrightarrow{\mathfrak{T}}_{a_{i}})\right|+\sup_{x\in\mathfrak{T}^{(K_{3})}}\left|\mu^{(\mathfrak{T},K)}(\overrightarrow{\mathfrak{T}}_{a_{i}})-\mu^{(\mathfrak{T},K)}(\overrightarrow{\mathfrak{T}}_{x})\right|.

Moreover, since
x\in\mathfrak{T}^{(K_{3})}
,

\overrightarrow{\mathfrak{T}}_{a_{i}}\setminus\overrightarrow{\mathfrak{T}}_{x}\subseteq\{x:\pi^{\mathfrak{T}^{(K_{3})}}(x)\in[a_{i},b_{i}]\}.

Furthermore, for any
x\in\mathfrak{T}
we have
\overrightarrow{\mathfrak{T}_{x}}\subseteq\overrightarrow{\mathfrak{T}_{a_{i}}}
. Therefore
\left|\lambda^{(\mathfrak{T},K)}(\overrightarrow{\mathfrak{T}}_{x})-\lambda^{(\mathfrak{T},K)}(\overrightarrow{\mathfrak{T}}_{a_{i}})\right|=\lambda^{(\mathfrak{T},K)}(\overrightarrow{\mathfrak{T}_{a_{i}}}\setminus\overrightarrow{\mathfrak{T}}_{x})
and
\left|\mu^{(\mathfrak{T},K)}(\overrightarrow{\mathfrak{T}}_{a_{i}})-\mu^{(\mathfrak{T},K)}(\overrightarrow{\mathfrak{T}}_{x})\right|=\mu^{(\mathfrak{T},K)}(\overrightarrow{\mathfrak{T}_{a_{i}}}\setminus\overrightarrow{\mathfrak{T}}_{x})
.
Hence, from the display above, it follows that

\left|\lambda^{(\mathfrak{T},K)}(\overrightarrow{\mathfrak{T}}_{x})-\lambda^{(\mathfrak{T},K)}(\overrightarrow{\mathfrak{T}}_{a_{i}})\right|\leq\lambda^{(\mathfrak{T},K)}[\{x:\pi^{\mathfrak{T}^{(K_{3})}}(x)\in[a_{i},b_{i}]\}]

and

\left|\mu^{(\mathfrak{T},K)}(\overrightarrow{\mathfrak{T}}_{x})-\mu^{(\mathfrak{T},K)}(\overrightarrow{\mathfrak{T}}_{a_{i}})\right|\leq\mu^{(\mathfrak{T},K)}[\{x:\pi^{\mathfrak{T}^{(K_{3})}}(x)\in[a_{i},b_{i}]\}]

Therefore, from displays (
7.17
) and (
7.18
), we get that the first and last summand of (
7.20
) are smaller than
\varepsilon/3
(outside an event of probability smaller than
3/8\eta
).
Therefore, by (
7.19
),

(7.21)

{\bf P}^{(K)}\left[\sup_{x\in\mathfrak{T}^{(K_{3})}}\left|\lambda^{(\mathfrak{T},K)}(\overrightarrow{\mathfrak{T}}_{x})-\mu^{(\mathfrak{T},K)}(\overrightarrow{\mathfrak{T}}_{x})\right|\leq\varepsilon\right]\geq 1-\eta/2.

If
x\notin\mathfrak{T}^{(K_{3})}
, then, both
\mathfrak{T}^{(K_{3})}_{x}
and
\mathfrak{T}^{(K_{3})}
are contained in
\{x\in\mathfrak{T}:\pi^{\mathfrak{T}^{(K_{3})}}(x)\in[a_{i},b_{i}]\}
. Therefore

\displaystyle\left|\mu^{(\mathfrak{T},K)}(\overrightarrow{\mathfrak{T}}_{x})-\lambda^{(\mathfrak{T},K)}(\overrightarrow{\mathfrak{T}}_{x})\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\lambda^{(\mathfrak{T},K)}(\{x\in\mathfrak{T}:\pi^{\mathfrak{T}^{(K_{3})}}(x)\in[a_{i},b_{i}]\})\vee\mu^{(\mathfrak{T},K)}(\{x\in\mathfrak{T}:\pi^{\mathfrak{T}^{(K_{3})}}(x)\in[a_{i},b_{i}]\}).

Finally, by (
7.17
) and (
7.18
) we have that

{\bf P}^{(K)}\left[\sup_{x\notin\mathfrak{T}^{(K_{3})}}\left|\lambda^{(\mathfrak{T},K)}(\overrightarrow{\mathfrak{T}}_{x})-\mu^{(\mathfrak{T},K)}(\overrightarrow{\mathfrak{T}}_{x})\right|\leq\varepsilon\right]\geq 1-\eta/2.

We have established (
7.14
) which is what we aimed to do.

The proof for (
7.13
) is completely analogous and that finishes the proof.

∎

8.
Application to lattice trees

In this section we apply Theorem
5.1
to the case of lattice trees. We will apply this theorem for points
(V_{n}^{i})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
which are i.i.d. uniformly chosen in the graphs
G_{n}
.

The application of Theorem
5.1
will provide our main result on lattice trees (Theorem
1.1
). For this, we need to show the following:

(1)

the points
(V_{n}^{i})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
are chosen according to uniform edge-volume,

(2)

condition
(G)^{h,+}_{1,\sigma_{0},C_{0}}
is verified, where
C_{0}
is defined above equation (1.3) in
[
10
]
and
\sigma_{0}
is defined below (1.8) in the same article.

(3)

condition
(S)
is verified,

(4)

condition
(R)_{1}
is verified.

The values of
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
appearing in Theorem
1.1
will be

C_{1}=\sigma_{0}\text{ and }C_{2}=C_{0}^{-1}.

The remainder of this section is dedicated to proving the four points listed above.

8.1.
Uniformly chosen points are chosen according to uniform edge-volume

Since
G_{n}
is a lattice tree, and hence a tree, we know that for
B\subset V(G_{n})
such that
B^{*}
is connected we know that
1+\left|E(B^{*})\right|=\left|B\right|
and that
1+\left|E(G_{n})\right|=\left|G_{n}\right|
. In particular

\frac{\left|E(B^{*})\right|}{\left|E(G_{n})\right|}=\frac{1+\left|B\right|}{1+\left|G_{n}\right|}\text{ and }\left|\frac{1+\left|B\right|}{1+\left|G_{n}\right|}-\frac{\left|B\right|}{\left|G_{n}\right|}\right|\leq\frac{1}{\left|G_{n}\right|},

now, since
{\mathbb{P}}[V_{n}^{i}\mid G_{n}]=\frac{\left|B\right|}{\left|G_{n}\right|}
and
\left|G_{n}\right|\geq n
we know that for any
\varepsilon>0

\lim_{n\to\infty}{\mathbb{P}}\Bigl[\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}B\subset V(G_{n})\\
B^{*}\text{connected}\end{subarray}}\left|{\mathbb{P}}[V_{1}\in B\mid G_{n}]-\frac{\left|E(B^{*})\right|}{\left|E(G_{n})\right|}\right|>\varepsilon\Bigr]=0.

8.2.
Graphs are asymptotically tree-like

In several of our conditions, we require that the graphs
\mathcal{T}^{(n,K)}
are asymptotically tree-like with probability going to 1 (see Definition
3.2
). Recalling Remark
3.1
, we see that
\mathcal{T}^{(n,K)}
is always tree-like for lattice trees.

8.3.
Condition
(G)^{h,+}

Considering Remark
3.2
and Remark
3.5
, we can see that the construction of the graph spatial tree
(T^{(n,K)},\sigma_{d}d_{T^{(n,K)}},\sigma_{\phi}\sqrt{\sigma_{d}}\phi_{T^{(n,K)}})
is exactly the same as the construction of
\mathcal{B}_{k}(W)
described in example 17 in the recent article
[
10
]
(in that example the scaling factor
n
was dropped from the notation for simplicity). Also, as pointed out in Remark
2.2
, the corresponding continuous skeletons
\mathcal{B}_{K}(W)
and
\mathfrak{B}^{(K)}
have the same law.

With this in mind, we see that Remark 18 of said article (which uses as a key input the historical
k
-point function for lattice trees from
[
11
]
), shows that Condition
(G)^{h,+}_{1,\sigma_{0},C_{0}}
holds in our context (critical lattice trees conditioned on height, where the skeleton is built using vertices chosen according to the uniform measure).

8.4.
Condition
(S)

In
[
10
]
, Theorem 19 (b) and (c) correspond to the second and third properties of Condition
(S)
. This means Condition
(S)
is verified (since the “tree-like” condition is always verified for lattice trees).

8.5.
Condition
(R)

Since
G_{n}
is a tree, it is clear that
R^{G_{n}}_{\text{eff}}(\cdot,\cdot)=d_{G_{n}}(\cdot,\cdot)
and thus condition
(R)_{1}
is verified.

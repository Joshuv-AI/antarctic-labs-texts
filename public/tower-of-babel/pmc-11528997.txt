The cardio-protective effect of cardiomyopeptidin in critically ill patients with myocardial injury: a retrospective cohort study
Authors: Jingyi Liang, Zengzhu He, Simin Tao, Hongbin Hu, Yuhang Yan, Jinling Huang, Zhenhua Zeng, Zhongqing Chen
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn order to investigate the association between the use of cardiomyopeptidin (CMP) and outcomes in critically ill patients with myocardial injury, we conducted a retrospective cohort study in Nanfang hospital, Southern Medical University. Clinical data of patients with myocardial injury were collected retrospectively and adjusted for possible confounders by propensity score weighting. The main outcome was the in-hospital mortality. Secondary outcomes included the major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE) occurring during the hospital, the individual components of MACEs, changes in high sensitive troponin T (hsTnT) levels, length of stay (LOS) in ICU and hospital. A Cox proportional hazards model was constructed to examine the association between the application of CMP and in-hospital mortality. Logistic regression and linear regression are used to analyze secondary outcomes. In total, 339 critically ill patients with myocardial injury were included in the analysis. After inverse probability of treatment weighting (IPTW), the use of CMP was associated with reduced in-hospital mortality (HR: 0.64, 95%CI: 0.43–0.96, P = 0.03). No significant differences were found in change of hsTnT, the occurrence of MACE and LOS in ICU as well as hospital after IPTW. The association was more significant in the subgroup with hsTnT > 3URL (HR: 0.41, 95%CI: 0.23–0.71, P < 0.001) and APACHE II score < 25 (HR: 0.13, 95%CI: 0.17–0.59, P < 0.001). Cardiomyopeptidin was associated with reduced in-hospital mortality in critically ill patients with myocardial injury. Our study provides initial clinical basis for the application of CMP with cardio-protective effect in critically ill patients. These results need to be confirmed in future randomized controlled trails.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12872-024-04277-0.Keywords: Cardiomyopeptidin, Myocardial injury, Cardio-protective effect, Mortality

Introduction

In recent years, cardiovascular diseases and their complications have posed significant global health challenges [1], with the high incidence of myocardial injury in critically ill patients exacerbating this issue [2]. The Fourth Universal Definition of Myocardial Infarction (2018) introduced the concept of “myocardial injury”, emphasizing the importance of elevated levels of high-sensitive troponin (hsTn) in adults, even in the absence of other clinical evidence of acute myocardial infarction [3]. Research indicates that myocardial injury is not exclusive to organic heart disease, but may occur in patients with sepsis, pulmonary embolism, respiratory failure, and shock in the intensive care unit (ICU) [4]. It has been shown that approximately 50% of critically ill patients admitted to ICU for non-cardiac problems have evidence of myocardial injury and/or myocardial infarction [2], with higher in-hospital mortality rate [2, 5]. There are no recommendations for the management of myocardial injury in critically ill patients based on high-quality clinical evidence, underscore the urgent need for exploring novel therapeutic approaches.

Cardiomyopeptidin, as a peptide active ingredient with amino acid sequence as KGAWSNVLRGMGGAF, derived from the ventricular myocardium of healthy young pigs, has emerged as a potential therapeutic agent for combating myocardial injury treatment and was in clinical use. Preceding animal studies have demonstrated significant cardioprotective effects of CMP, highlighting its potential to repair ischemic, hypoxic, and drug-induced myocardial damage [6, 7]. The mechanism maybe associated with the inhibition the mRNA expression of iNOS and Akr1b4 in cardiomyocytes, the inhibition activity of iNOS and aldosereductase, and the decrease of NO production [7]. Another animal study suggests that cardiomyopeptidin could prevent from apoptosis induced by doxorubicin in H9c2 cells via increasing expression of IGF-1R, thus up-regulation of the antiapoptotic protein Bcl-2 and inhibiting caspase-3 activity [8]. The results of CMP application in some critical care centers in China for myocardial injury patients suggests a promising avenue for improving patient outcomes. However, its efficacy and safety in clinical practice still need to be supported by more extensive and systematic studies.

Our study aims to explore the cardioprotective properties of CMP in critical care patients with myocardial injury. Through a retrospective review of critically ill patients at Nanfang Hospital, Guangzhou, China, we try to compare the outcomes between those treated with or without CMP. Our study seeks to provide a scientific basis for more effective treatment strategies in critical care patients with myocardial injury.

Methods

Study design and study participants

This single-center, retrospective, observational study was conducted and approved by the Ethics Committee of Nanfang Hospital (No. NFEC-2023-060) Patients were enrolled from the department of Critical Care Medicine at Nanfang Hospital, Southern Medical University (Guangdong, China) between Jun 2021 and Feb 2023. Adult patients who had a definite diagnosis of myocardial injury [3] were included.

Patients with myocardial injury were identified and classified according to the Fourth universal definition of myocardial infarction, defined as an elevation of hsTn levels above the 99th percentile, in the absence of other clinical indications of acute myocardial infarction [3]. The identified critical value for high-sensitivity troponin T (hsTnT) is defined as exceeding 0.014 ng/ml, based on the Nanfang Hospital Laboratory criteria. In this study, the diagnosis of myocardial injury was conducted by proficient clinicians who assessed patients for elevated hsTnT level during ICU stay, and also determined whether the observed injury was attributed to an acute myocardial infarction based on the patient’s clinical history.

Patients who were pregnant or breastfeeding, who were admitted for acute myocardial infarction, or whose entire stay lasted for less than 24 h were excluded from the analysis.

Data extraction

The data on the first day of ICU admission and discharge were extracted from medical systems, including age, gender, comorbidities, Acute Physiology and Chronic Health Evaluation II (APACHE II) score, vital sign, arterial blood gas analysis, laboratory values, mechanical ventilation, ECMO, renal replacement therapy, fluid balance. The baseline for hsTnT level was determined using the test result at the first elevation after ICU admission, the last recorded hsTnT result before ICU discharge was documented.

Information on cardiomyopeptidin (Dalian Zhen-ao Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd., Dalian, China) including drug names, dose, route was also collected.

Outcomes

The primary outcome of this study was in-hospital mortality. Secondary outcomes included the adverse cardiovascular events (MACE) occurring during the hospital, which was defined as acute myocardial infarction (MI), heart failure and cardiac death. Evaluation of these MACE was conducted by expert investigators through a comprehensive review of patients’ electronic medical records. Additionally, the individual components of MACEs and other adverse cardiac events such as arrhythmia and cardiac arrest, changes in hsTnT level, length of stay in ICU and hospital were also investigated.

Statistical analyses

In this study, continuous variables were all expressed as median [IQR], and the differences between groups were identified using the Mann–Whitney test due to their non-normal distribution. Categorical variables were presented as percentages and numbers, and comparisons between groups were made using the chi-square or Fisher’s exact test. Missing values in all data were less than 12% (Fig S1). Missing values were imputed by multiple imputation with the chained equations method [9].

To estimate the association between the application of CMP and outcomes among critically ill patients with myocardial injury, propensity score-based inverse probability of treatment weighting (IPTW) and standardization mortality weighting (SMRW) were performed in this study [10].

A Cox proportional hazards model was used to estimate the relationship between the application of CMP and mortality outcomes. The effect of CMP administration on MACEs was assessed using a logistic model. Linear regression was used to evaluate the association between CMP and changes in hsTnT, length of stay in ICU and hospital. The hazard ratios (HR) were calculated using the formula HR = eβi.

Stratification analysis was carried out to investigate whether the association between the application of CMP and in-hospital mortality varied among different subgroups categorized by gender, age, cardiac disease, Covid-19, hsTnT level, APACHE II score and fluid balance.

Statistical analysis was performed using R version 1.3.1093. Statistical significance was set at P < 0.05.

Result

Population and baseline characteristics

During the study period, a total of 795 critically ill patients underwent screening, among whom 558 patients met the diagnostic criteria for myocardial injury. After excluding patients according to the exclusion criteria, 339 critically ill patients were finally included in the analysis (Fig. 1). A total of 172 patients were administered cardiomyopeptidin (CMP group), while 167 patients did not undergo cardiomyopeptidin treatment (non-CMP group) (Fig. 1).

As shown in Table 1 and Table S1, before IPTW and SMRW, CMP and non-CMP groups were appropriately balanced in terms of age, gender, and APACHE II at ICU admission. In CMP group, there was a higher prevalence of diabetes and a greater utilization of CRRT compared to non-CMP group. Additionally, the baseline hsTnT, lactate, and NT-proBNP were significantly elevated in CMP group in comparison to non-CMP group. The median dose of CMP use was 360 mg. Meanwhile, the use of recombinant human BNP (Nesiritide) and doses of norepinephrine were higher in CMP group. Regarding fluid management, a more significantly positive intracardiac volume balance was observed in CMP group during the first three days of ICU admission. Relationship between the application of CMP and outcomes in patients with myocardial injury

As detailed in Table 2, the overall in-hospital mortality among critical ill patients with myocardial injury was 36.0% (122/339). Specifically, the in-hospital mortality of CMP group was 37.2% (64/172), slightly higher than non-CMP group at 34.7% (58/167) without statistically significant difference. The frequency of secondary outcomes was also shown in Table 2. The Cox proportional hazards model was used to examine the in-hospital mortality outcome between the two groups. There was no statistically significant correlation between the application of CMP and in-hospital mortality before propensity score adjustment (HR: 0.73, 95%CI 0.52–1.02, P = 0.07). However, post-adjustment with IPTW (HR: 0.64, 95%CI: 0.43–0.96, P = 0.03) and SMRW (HR: 0.58, 95%CI: 0.38–0.89, P = 0.01) models, significant beneficial effects of CMP use on in-hospital mortality were evident (Table 3 & Table S2). There were no statistically significant differences observed in the adjusted outcomes between the two basic multiple models (Table S2).

Using a logistic regression model, the impact of CMP application on the occurrence of MACE was assessed. The HR was estimated at 0.81 (95%CI 0.39–1.65, P = 0.556) with IPTW and 0.53 (95%CI 0.29–0.97, P = 0.041) with SMRW, indicating a potential protective effect, although statistical significance was reached only with the SMRW model (Table 3 & Table S2). However, no statistically significant value was demonstrated in individual adverse cardiac events. Myocardial infarction and cardiac arrest could not be included in the multiple model due to their low incidence (Table 2). The t-test revealed no statistical difference in the incidence of myocardial infarction and cardiac arrest between the two groups. Furthermore, the adjusted HR using IPTW regarding change of hsTnT was 0.46 (95%CI 0.04–5.01, P = 0.526) for CMP group compared with the control group, not suggesting whether there was any adverse or beneficial impact on this endpoint (Table 3). Similarly, CMP application was not associated with LOS in ICU (10.3d vs. 9.0d, HR 3.92, 95%CI 0.26–60.27, P = 0.327) and hospital (19.6d vs. 20.7d, HR 0.84, 95%CI 0.01–60.08, P = 0.934) (Table 3).

Subgroup analysis

The number of patients in each subgroup is shown in Fig. 3. As shown in Fig. 3, CMP application was associated with reduced in-hospital mortality. The association was more significant in the subgroup with hsTnT > 3URL (HR: 0.41, 95%CI: 0.23–0.71, P < 0.001). Similarly, the predominance of CMP was more pronounced in patients with an APACHE II score < 25 (HR: 0.13, 95%CI 0.17–0.59, P < 0.001) compared with the control group (Fig. 3).

Discussion

Cardiomyopeptidin, as an adjunctive therapeutic agent in the perioperative period of cardiac surgery, was used over-the-counter in ICU patients with myocardial injury. In this retrospective cohort study, the results showed that CMP application related to reduce of in-hospital mortality and the incidence of in-hospital major adverse cardiac events after adjustment. These results could confirm that CMP can benefit patients with myocardial injury in the ICU.

Different from the previous reports about the application of CMP in patients after cardiac surgery, our study explores the possible protective effect of CMP application on patients with myocardial injury after non-cardiac surgery in the ICU. Previous studies have shown that myocardial injury is common in intensive care. Prior to the definition of myocardial injury, researchers had investigated the proportion of patients with elevated troponin in the ICU and found that more than 70% of ICU patients had elevated troponin I and it was an independent risk factor for in-hospital mortality [11, 12]. The occurrence of myocardial injury and its impact on prognosis has received equal attention in some of the ICU-focused populations. A study conducted in the Netherlands revealed that 85% of patients with community-acquired pneumonia experienced myocardial injury while in the ICU [13]. Investigations in populations of septic patients have indicated that the percentage of myocardial injury ranges between 61% and 67% [14, 15], and that the severity of myocardial injury is associated with both 14d mortality and cardiovascular morbidity after discharge [15]. In this study, a total of 823 participants underwent screening, of whom 558 developed myocardial injury, with an incidence rate of 67.8%. This rate is largely in line with previous research. This study also provides data on mortality in patients with myocardial injuries in intensive care medicine.

The use of high-sensitivity troponin T as a diagnostic criterion for myocardial injury in our study has been well established and internationally agreed. The Fourth Universal Definition of Myocardial Infarction (2018) has included high-sensitivity troponin as a biomarker for diagnosing myocardial injury. High-sensitivity troponin T has been shown to identify perioperative myocardial injury in non-cardiac surgery. Additionally, high-sensitivity troponin T is associated with a high risk of perioperative death [16, 17] and major adverse cardiac and cerebrovascular events (MACCE) [18]. Furthermore, myocardial injury identified by high-sensitivity troponin T at presentation in patients with severe trauma has been found to be independently associated with the incidence of acute kidney injury (AKI) [19]. This year, the presence of an elevated high-sensitivity troponin T has also become an important indicator of whether or not a clinical intervention is needed. In this study, CMP group had higher baseline troponin T levels, which is consistent with the drug’s clinical use. Additionally, as supported by previous studies, these patients had more severe myocardial injury and a higher risk of death. Despite the high incidence of myocardial injury in the ICU and its correlation with patient prognosis, there is currently no standardized approach to manage myocardial injury in critically ill patients.

Cardiomyopeptidin is a novel drug developed in China for the adjunctive treatment of perioperative period of cardiac surgery. However, its efficacy in ICU patients is currently unknown. Very importantly, our study preliminarily found that CMP application is related to the reduction of in-hospital mortality and myocardial damage indicators in ICU hospitalized patients. Typically, clinical management involves an observational strategy with continuous troponin monitoring and clinical supervision [13]. Some studies have investigated the efficacy of drugs such as levosimendan [20, 21] and recombinant human brain natriuretic peptide [22] in ICU patients, but there remains a lack of targeted and evidence-based medical data. CMP promotes the repair of myocardial injury caused by ischemia, hypoxia, drug toxicity and other injury factors through direct action on cardiomyocytes [6, 8]. CMP has been demonstrated to exhibit cardioprotective effects in patients undergoing postoperative cardiac surgery. Previous basic experiments have confirmed that CMP application may reduce the activities of creatine phosphokinase and LDH and increased the activities of dehydrogenase of mitochondria in a dose-dependent manner [6–8]. Our study revealed that CMP application reduced in-hospital mortality in ICU patients with myocardial injury, after adjusting for the effects of confounding factors with the use of IPTW. Previous studies have shown that higher levels of high-sensitivity troponin T are associated with increased 30-day mortality in non-cardiac perioperative patients [5]. In present study, hsTnT levels on baseline were significantly higher in CMP group than in non-CMP group, yet in-hospital mortality was not significantly different between the two groups. This may be due to CMP application reduces the risk of death associated with high levels of hsTnT. The mechanism for this could be a result of reduced levels of hsTnT or incidence of MACE. However, it is important to note that due to the limitations of retrospective studies, it was not possible to collect MACE data prospectively. Additionally, hsTnT was not tested multiple times in many patients. Meanwhile, it was discovered that the impact of CMP application on in-hospital mortality was stronger in patients with high-sensitivity troponin T levels that were greatly elevated and APACHE II scores under 25. Possibly implying that CMP application could present a higher efficacy in patients with a more severe myocardial injury but a less severe general condition, this cohort could be a potential focus for future prospective investigations. Subgroup analysis also found that CMP was more effective in non-COVID-19 patients, possibly because COVID-19 patients tend to have more complex and severe conditions, resulting in a lesser impact of CMP on mortality. Furthermore, CMP demonstrated better efficacy in patients without pre-existing heart disease, suggesting that myocardial protection in ICU patients with better baseline cardiac health could have a more significant effect on long-term outcomes. Additionally, CMP appeared to benefit patients without baseline diabetes more; however, we found that there was a high overlap between patients with diabetes and those with cardiac comorbidities or COVID-19, making it difficult to exclude the influence of other factors on the subgroup analysis results. CMP also showed better outcomes in patients who were slightly volume-positive within the first three days, possibly indicating the importance of fluid management during CMP use. CMP has potential as a drug to benefit patients with myocardial injury in the ICU. Further prospective studies are necessary to validate its clinical role.

This study has some limitations. Firstly, there were no disparities in the prevalence of in-hospital MACE and the reduction in high-sensitivity troponin T between the two groups. It remains unclear whether CMP enhances patients’ prognosis by virtue of its cardioprotective properties. Secondly, this study’s sample size was deemed small, thus rendering its findings more appropriate as exploratory outcomes. Thirdly, information regarding in-hospital adverse cardiac events obtained through a retrospective analysis of electronic medical records may be incomplete, thus rendering it impossible to discern whether such events are indeed new. Additionally, more clinically significant outcomes such as adverse cardiac events that occur in distant locations are lacking. Finally, CMP as a peptide extracted from the ventricular muscle of young pigs, its active ingredients are still unclear. The specific mechanism of its cardioprotective effect needs to be verified by more laboratory research.

Conclusion

Cardiomyopeptidin application was associated with reduced in-hospital mortality in critically ill patients with myocardial injury. Our results may provide treatment options for patients with myocardial injury in the ICU. These results need to be confirmed in future randomized controlled trails.

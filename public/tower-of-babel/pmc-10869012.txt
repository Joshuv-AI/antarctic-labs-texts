Comparison of Oral Azathioprine and Oral Mini Pulse Steroid in the Treatment of Vitiligo: An Open-Label Randomized Controlled Trial
Authors: Sugat Jawade, Vikrant Saoji, Bhushan Madke, Adarshlata Singh
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
Background:Vitiligo is a common acquired depigmentory skin disorder supposed to be of autoimmune aetiology. Different immunosuppressants have been tried with varying success. Azathioprine has been less studied in vitiligo.Aims and Objectives:To study the efficacy of oral azathioprine and compare with systemic steroid in the treatment of vitiligo.Materials and Methods:It was an interventional study with multi-armed (three), parallel group, an open-label, randomized controlled trial with allocation ratio of 1:1:1. Patients of vitiligo aged between 18 and 60 years having more than 5% body surface involvement were included in the study. Patients were divided into three groups. Group A—patients received oral azathioprine 50 mg OD daily, group B—patients received combination of oral azathioprine 50 mg OD and PUVASOL and group C—patients received combination of betamethasone oral mini pulse (OMP) and PUVASOL. All the groups were treated for 1 year. Repigmentation was evaluated by vitiligo area severity index (VASI), and stabilization was evaluated by vitiligo disease activity (VIDA).Results:Group A, group B and group C showed 24.24%, 53.24% and 47.28% improvement in VASI score, respectively, at the end of 1 year. Group B and group C showed statistically significant superior repigmentation as compared to azathioprine monotherapy. Though azathioprine and betamethasone showed equivalent efficacy, azathioprine has a better safety profile. Side effects were minimal in azathioprine groups, whereas 50% patients developed various side effects in group C.Conclusion:Azathioprine is safe and effective option in the treatment of vitiligo.

Introduction

Vitiligo is a common acquired depigmentory skin disorder with significant impact on the self-esteem.[1] Depigmented patches in vitiligo result from destruction of melanocytes. Though various hypotheses have been postulated,[2] autoimmunity is believed to be the major pathogenic mechanism as proved by infiltration of CD8 + T cells on immunohistochemistry studies in active vitiligo lesion.[3] Being autoimmune aetiology, immunosuppressant like systemic steroid in the form of oral mini pulse therapy is found effective in the treatment of vitiligo.[4] Because vitiligo follows chronic unpredictable course, steroid needs to be given for long term, which can lead to many side effects.[4] To avoid the potential side effects of long-term steroids, other steroid sparing immunosuppressants have been tried.[56] This study was carried out to study the efficacy of azathioprine in vitiligo.

Materials and Methods

It was interventional study with multi-armed (three), parallel group, an open-label randomized controlled trial (RCT) (equivalence design) with allocation ratio of 1:1:1. This study is conducted in the tertiary care rural hospital (Acharya Vinoba Bhave rural hospital, Sawangi (Meghe), Wardha, Maharashtra) from Feb 2016 to Feb 2020. Study was approved by the institutional ethics committee. Clinically diagnosed cases of vitiligo of all clinical types aged between 18 and 60 years having more than 5% body surface involvement who were willing to participate and sign informed written consent were included into the study. Patients with history of photosensitivity or photoaggravated dermatoses, history of hypertension or diabetes mellitus and other systemic diseases, patients with anaemia, pancytopenia, thrombocytopenia, blood disorder, immuno-compromised patients and patients on other immunosuppressive drugs, pregnant and lactating females were excluded from the study.

The sample size calculation was based on a 95% confidence interval (CI) approach and a desired power of 80%. Considering the dropout rate of 10%, sample size included was 22 in each group.

Patients were enrolled into the three treatment groups A, B and C using simple randomization method in which patients were assigned groups by computer-generated numbers. Equal number of patients was enrolled in each group.

In Group A, patients were treated with tablet azathioprine 50 mg OD. In Group B, patients were treated with the combination of tablet azathioprine 50 mg OD along with PUVASOL. In Group C, patients were treated with the combination of oral mini-pulse of tablet betamethasone 5 mg along with PUVASOL. Patients received oral mini pulse of 5 mg of betamethasone (5 tablets of 1 mg) in the morning for two consecutive days per week. In PUVASOL, patients received tablet trimethoxy psoralen 25 mg after breakfast at 8.00 AM followed by sun exposure (till erythema) after 2 hours of intake (10.00 AM), three times a week on non-consecutive days (alternate days). Duration of therapy in all three groups was for 1 year.

Detailed history regarding duration of disease, disease activity, family history and treatment history was taken. General examinations including measurements of blood pressure and weight and systemic clinical examination were done. Cutaneous examination including the extent of involvement, site of involvement, mucosal involvement and presence of leukotrichia was noted. Blood investigations including blood sugar, complete blood count, liver function test and serum creatinine were carried out.

For safety assessment, patients were asked to follow up on first, second week and thereafter every month. During this clinical examination was carried out, blood investigations were done. Clinical assessment for efficacy was done at 3, 6, 9 and 12 months.

Clinical response was assessed by vitiligo area severity index (VASI).[78]

Efficacy was graded as minimally improved (0–10% improvement), improved (10–25% improvement), much improved (25–50%) and very much improved (>50% improvement).[9] Disease stability was assessed by patient’s own subjective response on assessment scale vitiligo disease activity (VIDA).[10]

Data entry and statistical analysis

Collected data was analysed using SPSS 16.0 version. Descriptive statistics like proportions mean and standard deviation were used, whereas inferential statistics for the comparison of qualitative variables Chi-square test used. The comparison of quantitative data between two variables by using independent t-test (unpaired t-test) and paired t-test was used to pre- and post-comparison and one-way ANOVA (with post hoc Bonferroni correction) was used to compare the continuous variables among more than two groups. The confidence limit for significance was fixed at 95% level with P value < 0.05.

Results

Total 66 patients were recruited into the study out of which ten patients were irregular in follow-up and treatment or side effects and hence considered as drop out. Final analysis of 18 (22 –4) in group A, 20 (22–2) in group B and 18 (22–4) in group C was done. Total numbers of patients available for analysis were 56 which include 30 (53.60%) males and 26 (46.4%) females. Age of the patients ranges from 18 years to 60 years with mean age of 34.14 ± 11.8. All the groups were statistically similar at the baseline.

Side effects

One patient in group A complained of hair loss, but no other haematological abnormalities observed and patient completed the treatment. Another patient in group B developed leukopenia (TLC 2300) at 3 weeks of therapy which was treated symptomatically and recovered within 4 weeks, and the patient was discontinued from the study but included in safety analysis. One patient in group B complained of photosensitivity which was managed by reducing the sun exposure, and the patient was continued in the study.

Out of 38 patients who received azathioprine (groups A and B) only two patients developed side effects (5.56%), photosensitivity in group B was attributed to PUVASOL. In groups C, 10 patients out of 20 developed side effects (50%) as shown in Table 3. Two patients on steroid discontinued treatment, one patient developed hypertension and another patient had persistent raised blood sugar level. Both the patients were discontinued from the study but included in safety analysis. These patients were referred to physician and managed accordingly. Total of three patients were discontinued from the study due to side effects: one from azathioprine group and two from betamethasone group [Table 3].

Discussion

Group A (azathioprine monotherapy) showed 15.49% improvement (improved) at six months and 24.26% at 12 months which is statistically significant [Table 2 and Images 1 and 2]. Comparable results are shown in study done by Madarkar M et al. (2019) in which patients were given higher dose of oral azathioprine 50 mg twice daily for six months with 26.26% improvement in VASI.[11] This indicates even increasing the dose does not affect the response significantly.

In group B (Azathioprine + PUVASOL), VASI improvement was 24.89% and 53.24% at 6 months and one year, respectively [Images 3 and 4]. The results were comparable with that of Radmanesh M et al. study[12] in which patients were treated with the combination of oral azathioprine 50 mg once daily and PUVA therapy on non-consecutive days for 6 months with 58.4% improvement.

Results of combination of betamethasone and PUVASOL were consistent with different studies done in the past.[41314]

In group B and group C, PUVASOL was combined with azathioprine and betamethasone showed better clinical response as that of group C (azathioprine monotherapy) indicating additive benefit of phototherapy.

Synergistic effect of immunosuppression caused by steroid or azathioprine and phototherapy leads to early and better rate of repigmentation as compared to azathioprine alone. Preventing the destruction of melanocytes immunosuppressants stabilizes the disease, and phototherapy by stimulating melanogenesis helps in repigmenting the vitiligo patches[121315161718]. Combination of both (immunosuppressants and phototherapy) gives a good clinical benefit as evident from our study.

Combination of azathioprine and PUVASOL showed slightly better outcome as that of combination of betamethasone and PUVASOL though not statistically significant.

The stability of the disease was assessed by VIDA score. Mean duration for arrest of progression of the disease in azathioprine groups (groups A and B) was 20.5 weeks, and in betamethasone group, it was 16 weeks. At 3 months, betamethasone was superior to azathioprine in stabilization of disease but at six months there was no statistical significant. Betamethasone achieved arrest of disease earlier as compared to azathioprine. Patra S et al. also stated that significant number of patients achieved arrest of disease progression in betamethasone group as compared to azathioprine at initial 2–4 months but at 6 months both therapy was equally effective.[19] Similar findings are shown in the present study.

In the present study, out of 20 patients in group C, two patients discontinued the study because of severe side effects like raised blood sugar level and hypertension. In group C out of 20, ten patients developed side effects (50%). Similar high rate of side effects due to steroids like hirsutism, bloating of abdomen, lethargy, weight gain, cushingoid facies, nausea vomiting, and photosensitivity, striae, insomnia, gastrointestinal distress, headache, glaucoma and amenorrhea are reported by various studies also.[13202122] We have not done bone mineral density measurement in our study but long-term steroid will lead to osteoporosis with severe consequences.[23] High rate of side effects due to steroid limits its uses for long term. In comparison, only three out of 39 patients on azathioprine therapy (6.79%) developed side effects which were manageable indicating better safety profile of azathioprine. Another study mentioned no side effects after 6 months of azathioprine therapy.[11] Vitiligo is a chronic disease and requires long-term treatment for years and using steroid for such a long term can be dangerous. Azathioprine is routinely prescribed for the treatment of rheumatological diseases and inflammatory bowel disease for chronic use. Azathioprine is found to be safe in paediatric atopic dermatitis even in higher doses (1.5–3.5 mg/kg) for longer duration of 5 years.[24] Though children not included in study, vitiligo is common in paediatric population also where azathioprine can be used.[25]

Radmanesh M et al. followed up patients for two years and did not find any risk of malignancy with the combination of azathioprine and PUVA therapy confirming the safety of azathioprine even when use for long term along with phototherapy in vitiligo patients.[12]

Though azathioprine can have serious side effects, they are more likely in higher doses; low dose (50 mg) used in our study is usually safe though need monitoring. TPMT test if available should be done. TPMT test was not done due to financial constrain.

Conclusions

Azathioprine not only arrests the progression of disease but also helps in repigmenting the vitiligo lesions. Azathioprine is effective in the treatment of vitiligo even as a monotherapy and should be combined with phototherapy for better results. It is a safe option for long-term use as compared to oral steroid.

Limitations

Study was done at single centre.

Long-term follow up for assessment of stability and repigmentation beyond the treatment period is not studied.

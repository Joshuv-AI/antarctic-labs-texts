Understanding wetland park feature influence through cross-regional multimodal analysis and interpretable modeling
Authors: Xiaojuan Zheng, Yan Huang, Zhiming Xie, Aimin Zheng
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Wetland parks serve both ecological conservation and social service functions, and the mechanisms through which their spatial characteristics influence public perception have attracted wide attention. Given the ecological and socio-economic differences between China and the United States and the availability of social media data, this study focuses on 147 wetland parks in both countries. It collects image–text reviews and ratings, builds a labeling system covering ecological environment, infrastructure, and user experience, and develops a unified feature framework based on multimodal data. The study extracts text, image, and fused features for predicting sentiment values and scores, and applies the SHAP method to explain feature contributions and their interactions. On this basis, high-contribution features identified by SHAP analysis are selected as optimization variables. A multi-objective optimization model is constructed with scores, sentiment values, and resource costs as objective functions. The NSGA-III algorithm is then applied to obtain Pareto-optimal solutions and explore balanced pathways for enhancing perception under limited resource input. The results show that text features better capture subjective perceptions, while image features reflect objective landscapes. Multimodal fusion outperforms single modalities in predictive performance, enhancing label coverage and model interpretability, with a clear advantage in compensating for the limitations of image features. Perceptions of Chinese wetland parks rely more on the combined influence of ecological and service features, while U.S. wetland parks exhibit a more balanced multi-feature synergy. Further comparison reveals significant differences between the two countries in feature contribution rankings, feature interaction effects, and perception patterns. This study proposes an analytical framework that integrates perception modeling and spatial optimization. It not only reveals the central role of ecological and service features in shaping sentiment values and scores but also verifies balanced pathways between perception improvement and resource control through multi-objective optimization.

Introduction

Wetland parks are important urban spaces that combine ecological conservation with public recreation. They play an crucial role in improving environmental quality, maintaining biodiversity, and providing green open spaces1. Wetland parks perform vital ecological functions through natural regulatory processes such as water filtration and purification2, nutrient absorption and retention3, and carbon sequestration. They participate in the material cycles and energy balance of urban ecosystems4,5. These processes help to alleviate the climatic and environmental problems caused by urbanization. Meanwhile, wetland parks can improve local air quality, mitigate the urban heat island effect. They provide residents with natural recreational spaces and play an important role in ecological tourism and environmental education6.

With the development of wetland parks, there are many studies on their ecological functions, landscape optimization, and social value7. Rational configuration of landscape elements and facility layout helps regulate ecological processes and improve environmental quality. It also enhances visitor experience and satisfaction8,9. These spatial configurations influence people’s perception pathways and ecological cognition, affecting their emotional responses and evaluation behaviors potentially. Although existing studies have explored the value of wetland parks from multiple dimensions, there is still a lack of quantitative analysis. However, research on the synergistic effects among multidimensional features in ecological spaces remains insufficient, and the influence of regional contexts on public perception structures lacks systematic quantitative analysis.

The widespread use of social media has opened up new avenues for ecology research10. People now share their travel experiences in various landscape environments on platforms such as Twitter, Yelp, Ctrip, TripAdvisor, and Dianping11,12. The image-text reviews and scores provide rich information for analyzing public cognitive responses to wetland park features13,14. Compared with traditional questionnaires, social media data offer advantages of wide coverage. They enable the capture of visitors’ real-time emotions and behavioral feedback during their visits15. However, extracting ecology-related features from unstructured content and uncovering their influence on visitor perception remains a challenge.

Sentiment analysis is an important approach to understanding public perception in social media reviews. Dictionary- and rule-based methods are widely applied in social media sentiment analysis. For example, the VADER model can achieve high accuracy in short-text scenarios and is commonly used for sentiment analysis of mobile app reviews and short video platform evaluations16. It can also be combined with classification algorithms such as support vector machines (SVM) to improve performance under imbalanced data conditions17. In the Chinese context, SnowNLP has been widely applied in tourism, energy, and environmental studies, effectively revealing public sentiment tendencies and regional differences on issues such as ice-and-snow tourism, clean heating, and national parks18–20. With the development of deep learning, pretrained models such as BERT have further improved the contextual adaptability and classification accuracy of sentiment recognition21.

Advances in image recognition technology have provided new pathways for extracting environmental features from social media photos. Deep learning methods based on convolutional neural networks can automatically identify landscape elements such as vegetation, water bodies, and buildings22,23. The Google Cloud Vision API has also been widely used for large-scale automatic photo annotation, significantly improving efficiency and accuracy in cultural ecosystem service assessment and environmental perception studies24. In tourism and hospitality research, image recognition has been used to analyze the visual information in user-generated photos, thereby revealing tourist preferences, behavior patterns, and their impact on destination competitiveness25. Meanwhile, the emerging cross-modal model CLIP enables zero-shot recognition in open-category scenarios by jointly learning image and text semantics, allowing for more flexible capture of elements such as cultural symbols, seasonal landscapes, and specific activities26.

When exploring the impact of wetland park features on visitor sentiment and scores, finding the key features is an essential step to understanding the perception process. Traditional analytical methods are mostly based on statistical models, such as linear regression, logistic regression, and analysis of variance27. These methods assume a linear relationship between features and response variables. These methods assess the importance of variables through significance testing28. They are suitable for data contexts with a clear structure and strong independence among variables. However, when dealing with the nonlinear coupling and multivariable interactions commonly found in ecosystems, these methods face limitations29.

With the increasing diversity of ecological perception data, machine learning has become an important tool for feature identification30. Compared with traditional methods, machine learning does not require assumptions about variable relationships. It can accommodate high-dimensional, nonlinear, and interaction-rich data structures31. Typical algorithms such as random forests, support vector machines, and gradient boosting trees have been widely used for the selection and evaluation of environmental features32. These methods offer advantages in handling nonlinear responses and improving prediction accuracy. However, their internal mechanisms are often difficult to interpret, limiting transparent understanding of how features exert their influence.

SHAP (Shapley Additive Explanations) is a game-theory-based interpretability method that provides clear explanations of feature contributions in machine learning models33. This method assigns a specific impact value to each input variable, helping to identify the direction and strength of each feature’s influence within complex models34. When analyzing visitor perception mechanisms, SHAP can reveal the marginal contributions of features within nonlinear structures. This makes black-box models more interpretable35.

This study is based on social media review data from wetland parks in China and the United States. It integrates text and image to construct a labeling system for wetland park features. By integrating multimodal data, this study quantifies the impact of each ecological feature on scores and sentiment values, and uses the SHAP method to explain feature contributions. A multi-objective optimization model is further developed to explore balanced pathways between enhancing perception performance and controlling resource input. A cross-regional comparative analysis is also conducted.

Materials and methods

The overall methodological framework of this study is shown in Fig. 1. The main processes include: (1) Collecting and processing text and image review data, followed by data preprocessing, label mapping, and multimodal fusion to generate a unified label vector; (2) Using the fused label vectors as input, a multimodal feature modeling framework is constructed to predict visitor scores and sentiment values, with multiple model comparisons conducted to identify the optimal model; (3) Implementing SHAP feature contribution analysis based on the optimal model to quantify the influence of different labels on the prediction results; (4) Establishing a multi-objective optimization model based on the SHAP analysis results to simulate optimal feature configurations under limited resource input and conducting comparative analysis.

Study area and data

The data in this study are collected from Ctrip in China and Google Maps in the United States. Ctrip is one of the most popular travel platforms in China36. Data from Ctrip have been widely used in studies on visitor perception analysis, cultural landscape evaluation, and multimodal data mining. Google Maps is one of the most widely used mapping platform. Its review content covers both natural and urban attractions. Google Maps supports image uploads and natural language comments, making it suitable for multimodal analysis37,38.

We collected visitor review data from January 1, 2015, to October 31, 2024, and removed duplicates and invalid reviews. To ensure data adequacy, only wetland parks with more than 30 reviews were retained. A total of 84 wetland parks in China and 63 in the United States were included. The Chinese wetland parks cover a wide range of provinces, with a clear concentration in the eastern coastal region, followed by the central, southwestern, and northeastern areas. They are densely distributed in the Yangtze River Delta, the Pearl River Delta, and the Bohai Rim region. In contrast, wetland parks in the United States are relatively dispersed, with concentrations along the East and West Coasts and additional sites extending into parts of the southern and central regions. A larger number of wetland parks are located in the southeastern coastal region and in California on the West Coast, while additional sites in areas such as Alaska and Hawaii contribute to strong regional representativeness and broad spatial coverage.

An overview of the data obtained from social media platforms is presented in Table 1. Further analysis shows that Chinese visitors express more positive sentiments, with 77.07% concentrated above 0.8 and only 11.54% being negative. Ratings are also dominated by high scores, with 68.48% at 5 and 20.66% at 4. Sentiment among U.S. visitors is more balanced, with 65.49% positive, 30.82% neutral, and only 3.69% negative. Ratings are more concentrated at the highest level, with 76.26% at 5.

There are clear differences between China and the United States in the number of reviews and images, which may lead to sample imbalance in model training. To reduce bias, this study adopts a unified data processing procedure and introduces standardization methods in modeling and comparative analysis to ensure the results of the two countries are comparable.

In addition, differences in rating habits, cultural preferences, and platform mechanisms between China and the United States may affect the absolute comparability of rating values. In this study, however, scores and sentiment values are not used for direct comparison between China and the United States, but rather serve as dependent variables in modeling to analyze the relative influence of features and regional differences. Therefore, this study focuses on the order of feature importance in the models rather than the specific values of scores or sentiment. These differences do not substantially affect the main analytical conclusions.

Label definition

In the data preprocessing stage, text and image data were standardized separately. Text data processing included removing noise characters, converting between simplified and traditional Chinese, filtering stop words, and performing word segmentation. Image data were processed by extracting image feature vectors using the CLIP model, which were then used for subsequent label mapping.

This study constructs a labeling system centered on ecological perception, consisting of three primary-level labels and their corresponding secondary-level labels: Environmental features (water, vegetation, animals, topography), facilities and services (service, accessibility, infrastructure), and visitor experience (comfort, safety, entertainment). The labeling system was developed with reference to the Wetland Park Design Specifications and research findings in ecology and environmental psychology. It was refined through multiple rounds of pilot testing and expert evaluation to ensure semantic clarity and comprehensive representation.

The construction of the labeling system involved both expert input and data-driven processes. Domain experts assigned seed words to each secondary label based on their professional knowledge. The selection of seed words considered both positive and negative emotional expressions. For example, river could be associated with both clear and murky. Based on the word segmentation results of the text reviews, nouns, verbs, and adjectives were extracted. Word frequencies were then calculated, and high-frequency words with a frequency of 3 or more were retained. By calculating the cosine similarity between high-frequency words and the seed words, those with higher similarity were added to the corresponding secondary label vocabulary lists.

For sentiment analysis, Chinese reviews were processed using the SnowNLP tool to calculate sentiment values. English reviews were analyzed using the VADER model for sentiment scoring39. The sentiment values output ranged from [-1, 1] and were linearly normalized to [0, 1]. The normalized values served as a target variable for the model40. These sentiment values are not used to directly compare absolute differences between China and the United States, but rather serve as dependent variables in modeling to analyze feature importance and relative influence.

Multimodal feature modeling

To analyze the impact of wetland park ecological features on visitor ratings and emotional responses, this study establishes a modeling framework based on machine learning methods43. The fused label vector is used as the model input, with visitor ratings and sentiment values as the target variables. The goal is to reveal the nonlinear relationships between multidimensional ecological perception features and visitor evaluations through a data-driven approach. The basic structure of the model is as follows:4\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$y=f(X,\theta )+\varepsilon$$\end{document}

Here, y represents the target variable (visitor rating or sentiment value), X is the fused multimodal label feature matrix, θ denotes the model parameters, and ε is the unobservable error term. This formula is used to represent the nonlinear mapping relationship between input features and output variables.

SHAP-based feature contribution analysis

The SHAP method is based on cooperative game theory. It provides both global and local interpretability by assigning each feature its marginal contribution to the prediction relative to a baseline value. This study applies the SHAP method to analyze the importance of text features, image features, and fused features in predicting wetland park ratings and sentiment values. We first selected the best-performing regression model based on the multimodal feature modeling experimental results. Then, we used this model to compute SHAP values, quantifying the contribution of each feature to the model’s predictions.

Multi-objective optimization method

To further explore the optimal configuration of key features in wetland parks, this study builds a multi-objective optimization model based on the SHAP analysis, with score, sentiment value, and intervention cost as the optimization objectives. The model aims to simulate how to enhance perception outcomes under limited resource input. This provides a more practical reference for ecological interventions in wetland parks.

Priority was given to labels that showed significant influence in both the rating and sentiment dimensions. The NSGA-III algorithm was used for optimization, obtaining a set of non-dominated solutions through Pareto front search to characterize strategies for maximizing perception benefits under different levels of intervention cost.

Text data: In the textual reviews of Chinese wetland parks, infrastructure-related labels have the highest proportion, reflecting visitors’ stronger tendency to evaluate their experiences with services and facilities. Experience-related labels also account for a substantial proportion, indicating that visitors frequently express personal emotions and touring experiences in their reviews. Environment-related labels appear less frequently, suggesting that visitors provide fewer detailed descriptions of natural elements in text and focus more on perceptual experiences and supporting services.

Image data: Image data from wetland parks are more concentrated on environment-related labels, especially water, vegetation, and topography, which show notably higher proportions. This indicates that images offer a clear advantage in presenting natural landscapes. In contrast, infrastructure- and experience-related labels appear less frequently, indicating that images are less effective at conveying visitors’ subjective experiences and are more inclined to depict tangible natural features.

Fused data: The fused data show a relatively balanced distribution between environment- and infrastructure-related labels. The combination of visual and textual features not only strengthens the presentation of natural elements but also broadens the expression of infrastructure- and experience-related labels. In the fused results, the proportions of service and facility labels increase noticeably. This indicates that combining text and image effectively addresses the limitations of single-modality data, enabling a more comprehensive representation of the multidimensional features of wetland parks.

Cross-regional comparison: There are significant differences in label distributions between wetland parks in China and the United States. For environment-related labels, U.S. visitors mention water, vegetation, and topography more frequently in both text and images. This suggests a greater emphasis on the expression of natural features. In contrast, Chinese visitors show a stronger focus on infrastructure. In the text data, labels for facilities and services appear in higher proportions, reflecting visitors’ emphasis on supporting conditions. For experience-related labels, both countries show relatively high proportions in textual reviews.

Correlation analysis of labels

This study employed Spearman’s rank correlation coefficient44 to analyze the correlations among text evaluation labels, image evaluation labels, and fused labels in wetland parks of China and the United States. The Spearman coefficient is a non-parametric measure suitable for assessing the correlation between variables that exhibit a monotonic relationship. Its value ranges from − 1 to 1, where values closer to ± 1 indicate stronger correlation, and values closer to 0 indicate weaker correlation. This analysis allows for the assessment of the labeling system’s design quality. It also helps identify synergistic relationships between labels and validate the system’s effectiveness in representing ecological and service features.

Figure 3 presents the correlation structure of fused labels in wetland parks of China and the United States. It provides a visual representation of the strength and direction of associations among feature labels. As shown in the figure, most labels exhibit weak correlations, indicating that the fused labeling system generally maintains good independence among features. In certain feature combinations, such as vegetation and animal, as well as facility and service, a clear positive correlation is observed. This indicates that these labels frequently co-occur in visitor evaluations, demonstrating a certain degree of synergy. Overall, the fused labeling system exhibits characteristics of overall independence with localized correlations.

Model performance comparison

We developed nine predictive models to forecast scores and sentiment values using text labels, image labels, and fused labels from Chinese wetland parks. Model performance was evaluated by comparing Mean Squared Error (MSE), Mean Absolute Error (MAE), and the coefficient of determination (R2). A comprehensive ranking was then calculated. Discussion

Feature contribution mechanisms in ecological perception pathways

In this study, we analysis multimodal data with the SHAP method to reveal visitors’ perception pathways of various features in wetland parks. The results show that the three categories of labels play different roles in visitor evaluations.

Environmental labels often form visitors’ first visual impression. They are primarily reflected through image data and have a significant intuitive perceptual effect. This visually dominated ecological perception mechanism has been widely found in urban green spaces and forest parks45. Facility labels are more commonly found in text data, reflecting visitors’ concerns about convenience and spatial accessibility. These features often have high frequency and prominence in social media corpora46. In contrast, experience labels more often involve subjective descriptions of emotions and atmosphere. However, their contribution to feature importance is relatively weaker47.

Multimodal fusion improves the completeness of feature recognition. Images can restore the natural state and convey intuitive impressions. Texts record visitors’ subjective feelings and specific opinions. The two modalities complement each other. Together, they can reconstruct visitors’ perception structures, and avoid biases caused by relying on a single modality48. This approach has gained increasing attention in tourism perception modeling and ecological management.

At the perception indicator level, scores are often driven by both landscapes and infrastructure. This reflects an evaluation logic dominated by visibility and functionality. This pattern is consistent with findings from green space research, where visual features and service quality are identified as key components shaping visitor perceptions49. Sentiment values exhibit a more complex generation mechanism. They are influenced by multiple factors, including visual atmosphere, service experience, and spatial perception.

The SHAP method provides clear numerical results and an easy-to-understand way to analyze complex ecosystems with many features. This method not only quantifies the marginal impact of each feature on the model output, but also reveals their relative positions within the overall feature structure. It is suitable for applications involving multimodal, high-dimensional input variables50. This mechanism helps to understand the functional logic of each ecological label in output responses. It also provides quantitative support for subsequent spatial configuration and resource optimization.

Differences in cross-regional perception structures and mechanism explanation

In this study, we found differences in perception pathways between Chinese and U.S. visitors in wetland parks. Differences exist not only in the amount of attention paid to ratings and sentiment, but also in the structural combinations of ecological and service-related features. Visitor perception labels are structured differently across regions. Labels of visitors’ perception are nested in specific regional contexts, social preferences, and usage experiences51.

In the score formation process, Chinese visitors are more sensitive to natural scenery and infrastructure. Visitors make evaluations mainly based on the attributes of water, vegetation, and facilities. It can be seen that visitors are clearly focused on visible ecological attributes and practical functions. Different from Chinese visitors, U.S. visitors are more sensitive to the coordination of service process and spatial use. Visitors make evaluations based on the attributes of service, accessibility, and comfort. It means that visitors from two countries have different focus pathways when forming score judgments. Chinese visitors focus on the spatial elements that can be seen and used. While visitors from U.S. pay more attention to the whole coordination of service and experience52.

The interaction structure between labels can further reveal the organization pattern of visitors’ perception in wetland park. In Chinese wetland parks, there is obvious synergy between ecological and facility labels. It reflects that visitors tend to integrate nature and utilitarian value into a whole during their perception. In contrast, in U.S. wetland parks, different kinds of feature types have relatively balanced effects on visitors’ response. It means that visitors tend to consider multiple features at the same time. The response of visitors is organized by the interaction among multiple factors53.

Spatial optimization approach based on feature contributions

This study examines how labels of ecological features interact to enhance scores and sentiment values. The study is conducted based on multi-objective analysis. The study finds that there are certain significant differences in the interactions of synergistic relationships between labels among wetland parks in different regions. These significant differences present configurations paths for setting ecological features to achieve optimal perception indicators.

For Chinese wetland parks, optimal solutions often include combinations of vegetation and facility. The synergy between labels of these two features keeps improving objectives of score and sentiment value. It is indicated that it is the coordinated relationship between ecological landscape and service function that leads to the optimization results54. Compared to enhance one label alone, this configuration of combination of two labels achieves better results with lower intervention cost.

For wetland parks in U.S., the optimal results depend more on combinations of water, vegetation, and facility. The enhancement of objectives of score and sentiment value is usually promoted by the participation of combinations of three types of features. It indicates that, compared to enhanced localization of prominence of one single feature, coordinated configurations of multiple features lead to better results.

Such differences are not caused by the importance of any single feature label. Instead, they reflect variations in feature synergy mechanisms within ecosystems across different regions. Chinese wetland parks often follow a dual-core structure. Key features take the lead, while other elements provide support, forming a clear path for optimization. U.S. wetland parks show a more balanced pattern, where multiple features work together in a coordinated way. They place greater emphasis on the coordination and stability of the overall system55.

Therefore, in spatial optimization, we should consider regional feature distributions, and adopt different strategies. The results indicate that Chinese wetland parks have considerable potential in the linkage between primary features and supporting elements. This provides insights for optimizing resource allocation, suggesting that enhancing the synergy among different elements can improve overall benefits. In contrast, U.S. wetland parks exhibit a pattern of coexisting multidimensional elements, suggesting that related practices should place greater emphasis on the coordination of the overall structure to reduce the potential impact of local adjustments on system stability.

Conclusion

This study, based on multimodal social media data and the SHAP method, quantitatively analyzes the influence of wetland park ecological features on scores and sentiment values. The results show that water, vegetation, and facility are identified as the top contributing features and serve as the main factors in model prediction. The feature structures of wetland parks differ between the two countries: China relies more on the combined effects of ecological and service elements, while the United States shows a balanced synergy of multiple features.

On this basis, the study introduces a multi-objective optimization model, selects high-contribution features as optimization variables, sets scores, sentiment values, and resource costs as objective functions, and applies the NSGA-III algorithm to obtain Pareto-optimal solutions. The results indicate that different combinations of high-contribution features have significant effects on the optimization outcomes, providing balanced solutions between perception enhancement and cost control. This conclusion offers potential references for regional design and management strategies, but its practical application still requires further validation within specific cultural, policy, and ecological contexts.

This study also has certain limitations. Social media data may be influenced by user group characteristics, leading to biases in representativeness. Although the constructed model performs well for the study objects, its generalizability to other types of ecological spaces remains to be tested. The role of socio-cultural factors in public perception has not yet been fully quantified, which to some extent limits the explanatory depth of cross-regional differences. At the same time, the SHAP interpretive method relies on specific assumptions, which may also affect the applicability of the conclusions. Future studies may incorporate more diverse data sources and methodological tools to further enhance the robustness and generalizability of the conclusions.
